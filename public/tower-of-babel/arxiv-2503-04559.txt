Presymplectic BV-AKSZ for N = 1 N=1 , D = 4 D=4 Supergravity
Maxim Grigoriev, Alexander Mamekin

Abstract
We elaborate on the presymplectic BV-AKSZ approach to supersymmetric systems. In particular, we construct such a formulation for the N = 1 N=1 , D = 4 D=4 supergravity by taking as a target space the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of the super-Poincaré algebra which, as we demonstrate, admits an invariant presymplectic structure of degree 3 3 . This data encodes a full-scale Batalin-Vilkovisky formulation of the system, including a concise form of the BV master action. The important feature of (presymplectic) AKSZ models is that, at least at the level of equations of motion, they can be equivalently reformulated in the spacetime obtained by adding or eliminating contractible dimensions. For instance, the presymplectic AKSZ formulation of gravity can be lifted to the respective “group manifold”, endowing it with the structure of a principle bundle and the Cartan connection therein, at least locally. In particular, the so-called rheonomy conditions emerge as a part of the presymplectic AKSZ equations of motion. The analogous considerations apply to supergravity and its uplift to superspace. We also study general presymplectic BV-AKSZ models related by adding or removing contractible spacetime dimensions in order to systematically relate the spacetime and superspace formulations of the same system within the AKSZ-like framework. These relations are then illustrated using the supersymmetric particle as a toy model.

Introduction

Batalin-Vilkovisky formalism
[

The price to be paid for the universality of this approach is that in applications to concrete local gauge theories the geometrical structures underlying gauge transformations, equations of motion, global symmetries, and so on are somewhat hidden in the BV field-antifield space involving a bunch of auxiliary variables such as ghosts, ghost for ghosts, and their conjugated antifields.

In the case of topological theories the BV formulation can be made very concise and geometrical by employing the celebrated AKSZ

In so doing the BV master action and symplectic structure are encoded in the geometrical structure of the typically finite-dimensional target space while physical fields, ghost fields, antifields, etc. emerge as different components of the inhomogeneous differential forms on the spacetime manifold. Moreover, gauge transformations, equations of motion, etc. acquire a clear geometrical meaning in terms of the space of maps from the source to the target.

2.1
Massless spin
3/2
in Minkowski space

Massless spin-
3/2
is described by a spacetime 1-form with values in Majorana spinors
\psi_{\mu}
(
\overline{\psi_{\mu}}=\psi_{\mu}^{\dagger}\gamma_{0}=\psi_{\mu}^{T}C
). We will use notation
\psi=\psi_{\mu}dx^{\mu}
and assume that
\psi_{\mu}
anticommute, i.e.
\epsilon(\psi_{\mu})=|\psi_{\mu}|=1
, see Appendix
A
where our conventions are summarized. The Lagrangian is given by

L=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\overline{\psi}_{\mu}\gamma_{5}\gamma_{\nu}\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\sigma}.

(2.1)

and its equations of motion are the massless Rarita–Schwinger equations:

\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\gamma_{5}\gamma_{\nu}\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\sigma}=0.

(2.2)

The Lagrangian is invariant under the following gauge transformations:

\psi_{\mu}\to\psi_{\mu}+\partial_{\mu}\varepsilon,

(2.3)

where the Majorana spinor
\varepsilon
is a gauge parameter. Together with the standard gauge-fixing condition
\gamma^{\mu}\psi_{\mu}=0
the equations of motion give the full set of irreducibility conditions:

\partial^{\mu}\psi_{\mu}=0,\qquad\gamma^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}\psi_{\mu}=0,\qquad\Box\psi_{\mu}=0.

(2.4)

The action of the minimal

coupled to Palatini-Cartan-Weyl gravity and can be written as:

(2.5)

where
\gamma_{ab}=\frac{1}{2}[\gamma_{a},\gamma_{b}]
,
R^{ab}=d\omega^{ab}+\omega^{a}_{\kern 4.09723ptc}\omega^{cb}
.

Equations of motion have the following form:

\delta_{\omega}S=0\,\Rightarrow\,R^{a}=T^{a}-\frac{1}{2}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\gamma^{a}\psi=0,

(2.6a)

(2.6c)

where

Action (
2.5

\begin{gathered}\delta e^{a}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\epsilon\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\gamma^{a}\psi,\\

2.3
Presymplectic BV-AKSZ construction

In the context of BV-BRST approach to systems involving physical fermions one has to take care of two degrees: the fermion degree denoted by
\epsilon(\cdot)
and the ghost degree denoted by
\mathrm{gh}(\cdot)
. The total Grassmann degree which determines the graded commutativity is denoted by
|\cdot|
and is given by
|A|=\mathrm{gh}(A)+\epsilon{(A)}\,\,\mathrm{mod}\,\,2
. For instance, for homogeneous functions
A,B
on the graded supermanifold one has

AB=(-1)^{|A||B|}BA
. Unless otherwise specified we assume that the Grassmann degree of the main structures such as BV differential or symplectic structure, are compatible with the ghost degree, e.g.
|Q|=\mathrm{gh}(Q)\,\,\mathrm{mod}\,2
.

Let
(F,\omega^{F},Q)
be a weak presymplectic
Q
-manifold
[
27
]
, i.e. a graded supermanifold equipped with vector field
Q,|Q|=\mathrm{gh}(Q)=1
and presymplectic form
\omega^{F}=d\chi,\mathrm{gh}(\omega^{F})=k
such that,

L_{Q}\omega^{F}=0\,,\qquad i_{Q}i_{Q}(\omega^{F})=0\,.

(2.8)

Note that these relation imply
i_{Q^{2}}\omega^{F}=0
so that
Q
is nilpotent on the symplectic quotient. It follows there exists Hamiltonian
H\in C^{\infty}(F)
such that
3
3

3

For
\mathrm{gh}(\omega^{F})={-1}
the Hamiltonian may exist only locally.

i_{Q}\omega^{F}+dH=0\,.

(2.9)

Now let us consider a real manifold
X
(space-time) of dimension
\dim(X)=\mathrm{gh}(\omega^{F})-1
and its shifted tangent bundle
T[1]X
with coordinates
(x^{\mu},\theta^{\nu})
.
T[1]X
is naturally a
Q
-manifold with the
Q
-structure being the de Rham differential:
\mathrm{d}_{X}=\theta^{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}
.

Fields of the presymplectic BV-AKSZ sigma model
((F,Q,\omega^{F}),(T[1]X,\mathrm{d}_{X}))
are the
ghost-degree preserving supermaps
\sigma:T[1]X\to F
. The (presymplectic) AKSZ action is given by

S[\sigma]=\int_{T[1]X}\left[\sigma^{\ast}(\chi)(d_{X})+\sigma^{\ast}(H)\right]\,,

(2.10)

and its Euler-Lagrange equations take the form

\sigma^{\ast}(\omega^{F}_{AB})\left(d_{X}\sigma^{\ast}(\psi^{B})-\sigma^{\ast}(Q^{B})\right)=0\,,

(2.11)

where
\psi^{A}
are coordinates on
F
and
Q=Q^{A}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}},\,\omega^{F}=\omega^{F}_{AB}d\psi^{B}d\psi^{A}
. Gauge transformations are:

\delta\sigma^{\ast}=\sigma^{\ast}\circ[Q+\mathrm{d}_{X},Y],

(2.12)

where the gauge parameter
Y
is a vertical vector field of ghost degree
-1
on
T[1]X\times F
, which preserves
\omega^{F}
pulled back to
T[1]X\times F
.

Note that if
R=R^{A}\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}$}\kern 1.0pt}
is a regular vector filed on
F
such that
i_{R}\omega^{F}=0
, it generates an algebraic gauge transformation of (
2.10
). In other words, if
\psi^{1}
is a coordinate such that
\omega^{F}_{1A}=0
then the above action does not depend on the field
\psi^{1}
. The BV action extending (
2.10

\widehat{\sigma}
and taking a symplectic quotient of the space of supermaps by the kernel distribution of the degree
-1
presymplectic structure induced by
\omega^{F}
on the space of supermaps, see
[
23
,
25

27
,
28
]
for further details.

The presymplectic BV-AKSZ construction described above admits a straightforward generalisation analogous to that of the usual symplectic AKSZ construction. More precisely in place of
(T[1]X,\mathrm{d}_{X})
one can take a more general
Q
-manifold

2.4
Palatini-Cartan-Weyl gravity as a presymplectic BV-AKSZ

As an example let us recall the presymplectic BV-AKSZ formulation of Einstein gravity.

(3.10)

The presymplectic BV-AKSZ formulation proposed in this work applies, strictly speaking, to the case where the tangent bundle to

3.2
Regularity of the presymplectic structure

To make sure that the above construction gives not only the Lagrangian but also the full-scale BV formulation one needs to check that the presymplectic structure induced on the space of supermaps is regular provided one restricts to admissible supermaps which are supermaps such that their frame field
e_{\mu}^{a}
component is invertible. Another requirement is that the resulting BV master-action is proper, i.e. it takes all the gauge symmetries into account.

To study regularity it is convenient to introduce a graded supermanifold
\bar{F}=Smaps(T_{x}[1]X,F)
of supermaps from
T_{x}[1]X
to
F
, where
x\in X
is a generic point of
X
. In contrast to
Smaps(T[1]X,F)
,
\bar{F}
is finite-dimensional and it is enough to check that the presymplectic structure induced on
\bar{F}
by
\omega^{F}
on

is regular.

Supermaps can be seen as homomorphsims of the respective algebras of functions
\widehat{\sigma}^{*}:\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(F)\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\bar{F}\times T_{x}[1]X)

\bar{F}
are introduced as follows:

\begin{gathered}\widehat{\sigma}^{\ast}(\xi^{a})=\xi^{a}+e^{a}_{\mu}\theta^{\mu}+\frac{1}{2!}{\overset{2}{\xi}}{}^{a}_{\mu\nu}\theta^{\mu}\theta^{\nu}+\frac{1}{3!}{\overset{3}{\xi}}{}^{a}_{\mu\nu\lambda}\theta^{\mu}\theta^{\nu}\theta^{\lambda}+\frac{1}{4!}{\overset{4}{\xi}}{}^{a}_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}\theta^{\mu}\theta^{\nu}\theta^{\lambda}\theta^{\rho},\\
\widehat{\sigma}^{\ast}(\rho^{ab})=\rho^{ab}+\omega^{ab}_{\mu}\theta^{\mu}+\frac{1}{2!}{\overset{2}{\rho}}{}^{ab}_{\mu\nu}\theta^{\mu}\theta^{\nu}+\frac{1}{3!}{\overset{3}{\rho}}{}^{ab}_{\mu\nu\lambda}\theta^{\mu}\theta^{\nu}\theta^{\lambda}+\frac{1}{4!}{\overset{4}{\rho}}{}^{ab}_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}\theta^{\mu}\theta^{\nu}\theta^{\lambda}\theta^{\rho},\\
\widehat{\sigma}^{\ast}(\psi^{\alpha})=\psi^{\alpha}+\psi^{\alpha}_{\mu}\theta^{\mu}+\frac{1}{2!}{\overset{2}{\psi}}{}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}\theta^{\mu}\theta^{\nu}+\frac{1}{3!}{\overset{3}{\psi}}{}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu\lambda}\theta^{\mu}\theta^{\nu}\theta^{\lambda}+\frac{1}{4!}{\overset{4}{\psi}}{}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}\theta^{\mu}\theta^{\nu}\theta^{\lambda}\theta^{\rho}\,.\end{gathered}

(3.11)

In this coordinates the pullback of the evaluation map

\bar{\omega}=\int_{T_{x}[1]X}ev^{*}(\omega^{F})\,,

and by construction is a 2-form on
\bar{F}

T_{x}[1]X
to the entire

so that
ev:\bar{\mathcal{F}}\times T[1]X\to F
,
\bar{\mathcal{F}}
is the space of supermaps from

to
F
. Then the BV action seen as a function on

(3.13)

Note that

We also need to recall how vector fields on

First we verify that the correct spectrum of BV fields and antifields is indeed recovered. We consider the presymplectic structure at generic point

of the body of

where

In order to prove that

defined on
\bar{F}

[

]
to prove the analogous statement in the case of Einstein gravity. Let
\bar{\mathcal{K}}
be the kernel distribution of

and denote by

\bar{\omega}_{p}
in

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists a distribution
\bar{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}\subset\bar{\mathcal{K}}
on
\bar{F}
which is regular and at any point

coincides with
\bar{\mathcal{K}}_{p}

We first check that any vector
\bar{k}_{p}\in\bar{\mathcal{K}}_{p}\subset T_{p}\bar{F}
is the restriction to
p
of a vector field from
\bar{\mathcal{K}}
. To this end let us
decompose

defined in (

) as
\omega^{F}=\omega_{0}^{F}+\omega_{1}^{F}
, where
\omega_{0}^{F}
is the contribution proportional to

(the first two terms) and
\omega_{1}^{F}
is the contribution linear in
\psi^{\alpha}
(the third term). It turns out that
for any

it is easy to find a vector field

on
F
such that

lies in the kernel of
\omega^{F}_{0}
and its prolongation to
\bar{F}
coincides with
\bar{k}_{p}
at
p
. To see this it is enough to consider vectors in the sector of
\xi,\rho
and in the sector of

independently. In the sector of

this follows from the analysis of
[
23
]
. If a vector
\bar{k}_{p}=T^{\alpha}_{a_{1}\ldots a_{k}}\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{a_{1}\ldots a_{k}}^{\alpha}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{a_{1}\ldots a_{k}}^{\alpha}}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{a_{1}\ldots a_{k}}^{\alpha}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{a_{1}\ldots a_{k}}^{\alpha}}$}\kern 1.0pt}\in T_{p}\bar{F}

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

such that it is proportional to

Because the prolongation of a vector field proportional to

one takes the distribution generated by the prolongations of the vector fields in the kernel of

whose restrictions to

to

The regularity then follow from the following general statement:

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a presymplectic manifold,

be a subdistribution of the kernel distribution

of

is regular and

coincides with the kernel of

.

Proof.

In the case of

real this immediately follows from the fact that the rank of

can not drop when moving off the point. If the neighborhood is formal (as in the case at hand) then
the statement is easily seen using local coordinates. Strictly speaking our case is mixed because the body of
\bar{F}
is a real manifold, but the rank is constant on the body of

so only extension in coordinates of nonvanishing degree matters. More precisely, one can perform the analysis for

pulled back to the surface

3.3
Generalization to AdS supergravity

The formulation of the previous Section has a straightforward generalization to the AdS version of the minimal
N=1,D=4
sugra. Recall, that the presymplectic BV-AKSZ formulation of Einstein gravity with nonvanishing cosmological constant is almost identical to that with the vanishing one
[
23
,
21
]
. The only difference is that the target space
Q
-structure is deformed and corresponds to (A)dS algebra seen as a deformation of the Poincaré one. At the same time the presymplectic structure remains unchanged.

It turns out that the situation with the minimal
N=1,D=4
sugra is completely analogous. The only subtlety is that only negative cosmological constants are allowed because of the propertied of spinors in 4 dimensions. More precisely, the respective Lie algebra is
\mathfrak{osp(4|1)}
which is a smooth deformation of
\mathfrak{siso}(1,3)

(3.17)

where

is the rescaled cosmological constant,

|g|^{2}=-\lambda
, and the remaining nontrivial commutators are obtained by complex conjugation. Chevalley-Eilenberg differential for
\mathfrak{osp(4|1)}
reads as

\begin{gathered}Q\xi^{AA^{\prime}}=-\frac{1}{2}(\rho^{A}_{\kern 4.09723ptB}\xi^{B{A^{\prime}}}+\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\rho\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{{A^{\prime}}}_{\kern 4.09723pt{B^{\prime}}}\xi^{{B^{\prime}}A})+2\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{A^{\prime}}\psi^{A},\\

It is clear that at

\mathfrak{siso}(1,3)[1]

The above

H=\frac{i}{4}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\rho\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{A^{\prime}}_{\kern 4.09723ptC^{\prime}}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\rho\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{C^{\prime}B^{\prime}}-\xi_{AB}\rho^{A}_{\kern 4.09723ptC}\rho^{CB})-i\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{A^{\prime}}\psi^{A}(\rho_{A}^{\kern 4.09723ptB}\xi_{BA^{\prime}}-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\rho\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{A^{\prime}}^{\kern 4.09723ptB^{\prime}}\xi_{B^{\prime}A})+\\
+2ig\xi_{AB}\psi^{A}\psi^{B}-2i\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mug\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{A^{\prime}}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{B^{\prime}}+6\lambda\widetilde{\xi}\,.

Now taking

as the source space, where

4
Contractible space-time dimensions

Before discussing the relation between the presymplectic BV-AKSZ formulation of supergravity
and the group manifold approach

let us make some general observations on how the source space of an AKSZ sigma model can be equivalently extended or reduced.

4.1
AKSZ at the level of equations of motion

Quite often it is convenient to formulate a gauge theory in terms of a certain extension of its space-time manifold because this helps to realise symmetries in a manifest and geometrical way. The well-known examples of this is the ambient-space formulation of (A)dS and conformal fields
[
61
,
62
]
, see also
[
63
,
64
,
65
,
66
,
30
,
31
]
. More relevant in the present work are the superspace formulations of supersymmetric systems, where the supersymmery transformation of fields are induced by the super-diffeomophisms of the spacetime manifold, see e.g.
[
54
,
67
,
68
,
69
]
.

A key observation which explains why AKSZ-like models are relevant in relating formulations of the same system in different spacetimes is that adding/removing a space-time dimension in such a way that the system remains essentially equivalent, is a natural geometrical operation
[
4
]
, see also
[
34
,
29
,
70
,
35
]
for earlier related considerations and

[
31
,
13
,
33
,
71
]
for further developments. Let us briefly recall how this works at the level of equations of motion.

An AKSZ model at the level of equations of motion (nonlagrangian AKSZ in what follows) is a pair: the source space
(T[1]X,\mathrm{d}_{X})
and the target
(F,Q)
. In general, one can consider sources which are not of the form
(T[1]X,\mathrm{d}_{X})
but we postpone this discussion for Section
4.3
. This data defines a local BV system whose space of field configurations is
Smaps(T[1]X,F)
, i.e. supermaps from
T[1]X
to
F
. The BV differential therein is a natural lift of
\mathrm{d}_{X}+Q
on
T[1]X\times F
to the space of all (including those not preserving the ghost degree) supermaps.

Given a nonlagrangin AKSZ model one can replace its spacetime
X
with another manifold. It turns out that adding a contractible dimension, i.e. replacing
X
with
X\times\mathbb{R}^{1}
so that the new source is
(T[1]X\times T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1},\mathrm{d}_{X}+\mathrm{d_{T}})
, gives a new AKSZ model which is in a certain sense equivalent to the initial one. This new AKSZ model can be again considered as a gauge field theory on
T[1]X
by identifying its field-antifield space
Smaps(T[1]X\times T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1},F)
as

Smaps(T[1]X\times T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1},F)\cong Smaps(T[1]X,Smaps(T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1},F))\,.

(4.1)

If we allow ourselves to consider AKSZ sigma models with infinite-dimensional target spaces, the above identification defines a new AKSZ sigma model on
T[1]X
whose target is itself a field-antifield space
Smaps(T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1},F)
of the AKSZ sigma model with source
(T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1},\mathrm{d_{T}})

and target
(F,Q)
. Moreover, the new AKSZ sigma model on
T[1]X
is equivalent to the initial one via the elimination of the nonlagrangian version
[
72
,
5
,
73
]
of the generalized auxiliary fields
[
74
]
. Indeed, the initial target space
(F,Q)
is in fact an equivalent reduction of the new target space
(Smaps(T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1},F),Q^{\prime})
, where
Q^{\prime}
is the lift of
\mathrm{d_{T}}+Q
to
(Smaps(T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1},F)
. More precisely, let

be coordinates on
T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1}
and
\psi^{A}
on
F
. A generic supermap can be written as
\psi_{0}(t)+\theta^{\prime}\psi_{1}(t)
so that
\psi_{0}^{A}(t)
and
\psi_{1}^{A}(t)
are local coordinates on
Smaps(T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1},F)

on
Smaps(T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1},F)
reads as:

Q^{\prime}\psi_{0}^{A}(t)=Q^{A}(\psi_{0}(t))\,,\qquad Q^{\prime}\psi_{1}^{A}(t)=\dot{\psi}_{0}(t)+\ldots

(4.2)

where

\psi_{1}^{A}

\psi^{A}(t)
into
\psi_{0}^{A}(0)
and
\dot{\psi}_{0}^{A}(t)
the above relation imply that the surface in
(Smaps(T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1},F),Q^{\prime})
singled out by
\psi_{1}^{A}(t)=0
and
\dot{\psi}_{0}^{A}(t)=0
is an equivalent reduction (in the sense of
Q
-manifolds) and can be identified with
(F,Q)
. This in turn implies that the two AKSZ sigma models over
T[1]X
are equivalent via the elimination of generalized auxiliary fields. In particular, the space of equivalence classes of solutions modulo gauge transformations must be the same. In applications to SUSY systems we also need a version where
t
is Grassmann odd while
\theta^{\prime}
is even. The proof is analogous.

The statement has a straightforward generalization to the case of so called gauge PDEs
[
73
]
. Namely, given a gPDE
(E,Q,T[1]X)
there is a natural gPDE
(E^{\prime},Q^{\prime},T[1]X\times T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1})
whose total space is a product
Q
-manifold
(E,Q)\times(T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1},\mathrm{d_{T}})
considered as a bundle over
T[1]X\times T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1}
. In its turn, it can be seen as a gPDE over
T[1]X

with typical fiber
Smaps(T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1},F)
, where
F
is a typical fiber of
E
. A straightforward generalization of the above argument says that
(E,Q,T[1]X)
can be seen as an equivalent reduction (in the sense of gPDEs, see
[
73
]
) of the extended one.

4.2
Presymplectic AKSZ

The considerations of the previous Subsection can be applied to Lagrangian AKSZ sigma models if we disregard the symplectic structure and consider them at the level of equations of motion. Indeed, for a usual AKSZ sigma model the BV-BRST differential can be defined without using the symplectic structure on the target space.

However, if we are interested in the full-scale Lagrangian AKSZ with the source
(T[1]X,\mathrm{d}_{X})
, the ghost degree of the target space symplectic structure and
\dim{X}
are related as
\mathrm{gh}(\omega^{F})=\dim{X}-1
.
Of course, one can still repeat the AKSZ construction, resulting in the shifted analog of the BV data where the symplectic structure on
Smaps(T[1]X,F)
has degree
\mathrm{gh}(\omega^{F})-\dim{X}
while the analog of the BV-AKSZ action carries degree
\mathrm{gh}(\omega^{F})-\dim{X}+1
. In particular, if
\mathrm{gh}(\omega^{F})=\dim{X}
the shifted AKSZ sigma model defines a Hamiltonian BFV system. Moreover, its associated BV formulation can be immediately constructed as an AKSZ sigma model with
X
replaced with
X\times\mathbb{R}^{1}
(time-line), as was originally observed in
[
34
,
35
]
. The converse is also true, given an AKSZ sigma model on
T[1]X\times T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1}
, its BFV Hamiltonian description is again a BFV-AKSZ sigma model with source
(T[1]X,\mathrm{d}_{X})
, see
[
35
,
6
]
. In a more general setup of not necessarily AKSZ systems analogous relations were thoroughly studied in
[
33
,
75
]
, where the BFV formulation is interpreted as defined on the boundary of the spacetime.

If we now switch to presymplectic AKSZ systems, most of the above remains correct but there are some subtleties related to the regularity of the involved presymplectic structures. If the target space presymplectic structure is regular, the system is equivalent to the standard AKSZ model, at least locally. Indeed, inspecting the construction of
[
27
,
28
]
one finds that the resulting BV formulation is that of the symplectic AKSZ sigma-model whose target is the symplectic quotient of the initial target (we disregard global geometry issues in this discussion). The nontrivial examples of presymplectic BV-AKSZ models arise when the presymplectic structure is not regular but is such that the induced BV symplectic structure is regular provided we exclude the degenerate field configurations. For instance, in the presymplectic BV-AKSZ formulation of gravity-like models
[
23
,
25
,
28
]
among the AKSZ fields one typically has the frame field which should be assumed nondegenerate. We say that presymplectic structure is quasi-regular if the induced presymplectic structure on the space of AKSZ fields is regular for admissible configurations. It is natural to require that addmissible configurations are defined in such a way that a small variation of an admissible configuration is also admissible. Although we formulate these requirements in terms of functional space
Smaps(T[1]X,F)
the role of the base
X
is passive and the analysis boils down to (locally) finite dimensional space
Smaps(T_{x}[1]X,F)
for a generic point
x\in X
.

Just like in the case of usual AKSZ models one can also consider (possibly shifted) presymplectic BV-AKSZ systems with
\mathrm{gh}(\omega^{F})
generic. For instance, for
\mathrm{gh}(\omega^{F})=\dim{X}
the presymplectic AKSZ system defines a local BFV system provided
\omega^{F}
is quasi-regular. Because we have in mind applications to supersymmetric field theories, we generally do not have a natural integration on the source space and hence a natural induced presymplectic structure. However, if we are only interested in the description at the level of equation of motion we can replace presymplectic structure by its kernel distribution
\mathcal{K}
which is by definiton a distribution generated by the vector fields annihilating the presymplectic structure. In contrast to the presymplectic structure,
\mathcal{K}
naturally induces an associated distribution (also called prolongation of
\mathcal{K}
) on the space of AKSZ fields. If the induced distribution is regular one can pass to the quotient space which is naturally a (shifted) local BV system at the level of equations of motion, see
[
76
]
for a systematic exposition. In the case where the induced presymplectic structure is present, this is equivalent to the standard prescription, i.e. when the local BV system arises on the symplectic quotient of the space of (admissible) configurations.

It is natural to address the question of equivalence between a presymplectic AKSZ system and its extension by replacing the presymplectic structures with their associated kernel distributions. Of course, in so doing we forget about Lagranians/symplectic structures and speak about equivalence at the level of equations of motion. More specifically, we define a weak AKSZ system
(F,Q,\mathcal{K},T[1]X)
by taking
(T[1]X,\mathrm{d}_{X})
as a source and a weak
Q
-manifold
(F,Q,\mathcal{K})
as the target. Recall, that a weak
Q
-manifold is an almost
Q
-manifold equipped with an involutive distribution
\mathcal{K}
such that:

L_{Q}\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{K}\,,\qquad Q^{2}\in\mathcal{K}\,.

(4.3)

In addition, the distribution
\bar{\mathcal{K}}
induced by
\mathcal{K}
on admissible configurations from
Smaps(T[1]X,F)
is required to be regular.
The physical interpretation of a weak AKSZ system is that of the BV system on the quotient of
Smaps(T[1]X,F)
by
\bar{\mathcal{K}}
. The BV-BRST differential on the quotient is induced by the lift of
\mathrm{d}_{X}+Q
to
Smaps(T[1]X,F)
. Of course weak AKSZ is just a particular case of so-called weak gauge PDEs introduced recently in
[
76
]
to which we refer for further details and proofs.

Given a weak AKSZ model
(F,Q,\mathcal{K},T[1]X)
let us consider its extension to
T[1]X\times T[1]\mathcal{T}
, where now
T
is either
\mathbb{R}
or
\Pi\mathbb{R}
(i.e. odd line). We also allow
X
to be a supermanifold. Just like above, the extension can be considered as a weak AKSZ model on
T[1]X
with the target being
Smaps(T[1]\mathcal{T},F)
. The target space
Smaps(T[1]\mathcal{T},F)
is naturally an almost
Q
-manifold equipped with the compatible distribution
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
: indeed
Q^{\prime}
is the lift of
Q+\mathrm{d_{T}}
to
Smaps(T[1]\mathcal{T},F)
while
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
is the lift of
\mathcal{K}
. In this way we arrive at the new weak AKSZ model with an infinite-dimensional target
(Smaps(T[1]\mathcal{T},F),Q^{\prime},\mathcal{K}^{\prime})
.

Suppose that for the initial weak AKSZ model
(F,Q,\mathcal{K},T[1]X)
we have a notion of admissible configurations and
\bar{\mathcal{K}}
is regular on the space of admissible configurations. A configuration of the extended weak AKSZ model can be represented as an element of
Smaps(T[1]X\times T[1]\mathcal{T},F)
. We call it admissible if its restriction to any point of the body of
T[1]\mathcal{T}
is admissible as an element of
Smaps(T[1]X,F)
. For instance if
\xi^{a},\mathrm{gh}(\xi^{a})=1
are coordinates on
F
encoding the frame field
e^{a}_{\mu}
and admissible configurations are such that
\widehat{\sigma}^{*}(\xi^{a})=e^{a}_{\mu}(x)\theta^{\mu}+\ldots
with
e^{a}_{\mu}(x)
invertible then the configuration of the extended model is admissible if
e^{a}_{\mu}(x,t)
defined through
\widehat{\sigma}^{*}(\xi^{a})=e^{a}_{\mu}(x,t)\theta^{\mu}+e^{a}_{0}(x,t)\theta^{\prime}+\ldots
is invertible for all
t
(in this example we assumed that
t
is even).
We have the following:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(F,Q,\mathcal{K},T[1]X,\mathrm{d}_{X})
be a weak AKSZ model such that the induced distribution
\bar{\mathcal{K}}
is regular on admissible configurations. Let also
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
be the distribution induced by
\mathcal{K}
on
Smaps(T[1]\mathcal{T},F)
then the distribution
\bar{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}
induced by
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
on
Smaps(T[1]X,Smaps(T[1]\mathcal{T},F))
is regular on admissible configurations.

In other words the extended weak AKSZ model has a natural notion of addmissible configurations and the induced distribution is quasiregular. The proof is based on representing the space of admissible configurations for the extended model as
Smaps(T[1]\mathcal{T},Smaps_{\mathrm{ad}}(T[1]X,F))
, where in the target we take only admissible configurations. As
\bar{\mathcal{K}}
is regular on
Smaps_{\mathrm{ad}}(T[1]X),F)
by assumption, distribution
\bar{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}
is regular as well.

The almost
Q
-structure
Q^{\prime}
on
Smaps(T[1]\mathcal{T},F)
is given by the natural lift of differential
d_{T}+Q
to
Smaps(T[1]\mathcal{T},F)
. If, as before, we use coordinates
\psi^{A}(t,\theta^{\prime})
and assume
t
fermionic, we have
\psi^{A}(t,\theta^{\prime})=\psi^{A}_{0}(\theta^{\prime})+t\psi^{A}_{1}(\theta^{\prime})
. In terms of
\psi^{A}_{0}(\theta^{\prime})
and
\psi^{A}_{1}(\theta^{\prime})
the action of
Q^{\prime}
reads as:

Q^{\prime}\psi^{A}_{0}(\theta^{\prime})=\theta^{\prime}\psi^{A}_{1}(\theta^{\prime})+Q^{A}(\psi_{0}(\theta^{\prime}))\,,\qquad Q^{\prime}\psi^{A}_{1}(\theta^{\prime})=\psi_{1}^{B}(\theta^{\prime})\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial Q^{A}}{\partial\psi^{B}}$}\kern 1.0pt\Big|_{\psi=\psi_{0}(\theta^{\prime})}.

(4.4)

Because functions in
\theta^{\prime}
are polynomials it is clear that all the coefficients in the expansion of
\psi_{1}
in
\theta^{\prime}
and all the coefficients in
\psi_{0}(\theta^{\prime})
save for
\psi_{0}(0)
form contractible pairs. Moreover, because by assumption
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{K}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
is regular one can assume that we work in the adapted coordinate system and hence the elimination can be performed after taking the quotient. After the elimination we are back to the local BV system determined by the initial weak AKSZ model. This shows that the extended weak AKSZ system (seen as a weak AKSZ model with the same source and the target
Smaps(T[1]\mathcal{T},F)
) is equivalent to the initial one as a local gauge theory.

It is worth giving the following corollary of the above considerations. Let
(F,Q,\omega^{F}),(T[1]X,\mathrm{d}_{X})
be a presymplectic BFV-AKSZ sigma model (i.e.
\mathrm{gh}(\omega^{F})=\dim{X}
) and
\omega^{F}
be quasi-regular. Consider the BV-AKSZ model obtained by extending the source
T[1]X
to
T[1](X\times\mathbb{R}^{1})
. It defines a local BV system that is equivalent to the BV reformulation of the initial BFV system encoded in
(F,Q,\omega^{F}),(T[1]X,\mathrm{d}_{X})
. This statement can be considered as an extension of the analogous relation
[
34
,
35
]
known in the context of standard (symplectic) AKSZ models. The proof is based on the fact that for a regular presymplectic structure taking the symplectic quotient of
\bar{F}
and passing to
Smaps(T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1},\bar{F})
commutes.

4.3
Generalization

In fact adding/removing space-time dimension for the AKSZ model is a particular example of a slightly more general equivalence. First of all, we recall that a nonlagragian AKSZ model can be defined for a generic source
Q
-manifold
(\mathscr{X},\delta)
. If in addition
\mathscr{X}
is equipped
with a
\delta
-invariant volume form of degree
-k
and target space
F
is equipped with a symplectic structure of degree
k-1
, the AKSZ construction still produces a local BV system. Of course, this has a straightforward generalisation to the presymplectic case.

More generally, one can replace
(\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathscr{X}),\delta)
with a rather general differential graded commutative algebra (DGCA)
(\mathcal{A},\delta)
. In reasonable cases the space of supermaps from
Spec(\mathcal{A})
to
\mathcal{F}
(we keep using geometrical language and think of the space of super-homomorphsims from
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(F)\to\mathcal{A}
as the space of supermaps from
Spec(\mathcal{A})
to
F
) is a graded supermanifold and
\delta+Q
makes it into a
Q
-manifold, which in turn, defines a BV system. The Lagrangian version is constructed by taking
\mathcal{A}
equipped with a non-degenerate and
\delta
-invariant trace
{\rm Tr}
and
F
with a symplectic structure of a compatible degree. This generalisation was proposed in
[
13
]
, see also
[
14
,
15
,
77
]
.

As we are first of all interested in local field theories we restrict ourselves to the case where
\mathcal{A}
is a module over
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X)
for some smooth (super)manifold
X
which eventually plays the role of the (super)spacetime. In addition we also assume that seen as a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X)
-module,
\mathcal{A}
is a space of sections of a vector bundle over
X
whose typical fiber is a graded commutative algebra
\mathfrak{a}
. Note that
\delta
is a 1st order differential operator on
X
.

The discussed above relations between AKSZ sigma model on
T[1]X
and its extension to
T[1](X\times\mathcal{T})
, where
\mathcal{T}
is either
\mathbb{R}^{1}
or
\Pi\mathbb{R}^{1}
, can be generalised as follows. Suppose that
\mathcal{A}
admits an additional degree such that the expansion of
\delta
into homogeneous components reads as

\delta=\delta_{-1}+\delta_{0}+\delta_{1}+\ldots\,,

(4.5)

and that
\delta_{-1}
is
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X)
-linear and constant rank. It follows, cohomology of
\delta_{-1}
is again a vector bundle and its space of sections is a graded commututative algebra
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
. Moreover, the differential
\delta
induces a differential
\widetilde{\delta}
, making
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
into a differential graded commutative algebra (DGCA). One can derive
\widetilde{\delta}
using the usual spectral sequence arguments or as a version of equivalent reduction for the first-quantized BRST systems, see
[
72
]
for more details. It follows, the generalised AKSZ models with sources
(\mathcal{A},\delta)
and
(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\delta})
are equivalent in the sense of elimination of generalized auxiliary fields.

Let us now turn to Lagrangian systems. Let
(\mathcal{A},\delta)
be equipped with the trace
{\rm Tr}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}
that carries ghost degree
-k
and satisfies
{\rm Tr}(\delta f)=0
. If the target space is equipped with the

Q
-invariant presymplectic structure of degree
k-1
, the space of supermaps
Spec(\mathcal{A})\to F
inherits the degree
-1
presymplectic structure compatible with the total
Q
-structure which the lift of
\delta+Q
to the space of supermaps. Assuming the induced presymplectic structure to be regular and taking a symplectic quotient of the space of supermaps gives a local BV system. In this way we have arrived at the generalisation of the presymplectic BV-AKSZ formulation, which is going to be useful in describing supersymmetric systems. Note that even if the target space presymplectic structure is nondegenreate the presymplectic structure on the space of supermaps can degenerate because of the zero modes of the trace so that even in this case the system is to be interpreted as a presymplectic AKSZ.

Let us present an explicit coordinate form of the main structures. For simplicity let us assume that
\mathcal{A}
is formed by functions on
X
with values in the graded commutative algebra
\mathfrak{a}
which we assume to be a subquotient of the polynomial algebra. Moreover we assume that the trace factorises as
{\rm Tr}{f}=\int d^{n}x{\rm Tr}_{0}(f)
, where
{\rm Tr}_{0}
is the trace on
\mathfrak{a}
. If
e_{i}
denotes a basis in
\mathfrak{a}
a generic section
a\in\mathcal{A}
can be written as
f=f^{i}(x)e_{i}
. The trace defines a graded symmetric bilinear form
k_{ij}={\rm Tr}_{0}(e_{i}e_{j})
. Now, if
\psi^{A}
are coordinates on
F
, a generic supermap
Spec(\mathcal{A})\to F
can be written as

\widehat{\sigma}^{\ast}(\psi^{A})=\psi^{Ai}(x)e_{i}\,,\qquad\mathrm{gh}(\psi^{Ai})=\mathrm{gh}(\psi^{A})-\mathrm{gh}(e_{i})\,,\quad|\psi^{Ai}|=|\psi^{A}|-|e_{i}|\,\,\mathrm{mod}\,2\,.

(4.6)

It is convenient to identify the space of supermaps
Smaps(Spec(\mathcal{A}),F)
with
Smaps(X,\bar{F})
where
\bar{F}
is the space of supermaps from
Spec(\mathfrak{a})
to
F
. Note that
\psi^{Ai}
can be identified with coordinates on
\bar{F}
. In this coordinate system the expression for the induced presympelctic structure at
\widehat{\sigma}
reads as:

\omega^{\bar{F}}_{\widehat{\sigma}}={\rm Tr}_{0}\left(\widehat{\sigma}(\omega^{F}_{AB})d\psi^{Ai}e_{i}d\psi^{Bj}e_{j}\right)\,.

(4.7)

In particular, if
\omega^{F}_{AB}=const
one gets
\omega^{\bar{F}}=k_{ij}\omega^{F}_{AB}d\psi^{Ai}d\psi^{Bj}
. Note that if the basis
e_{i}
splits into
(e_{a},e_{\alpha})
such that
{\rm Tr}_{0}(e_{a}e_{b})=0={\rm Tr}_{0}(e_{\alpha}e_{b})
and

k_{\alpha\beta}\equiv{\rm Tr}_{0}(e_{\alpha}e_{\beta})
is invertible then the linear span of
e_{a}
is an ideal in
\mathfrak{a}
. It is clear that all the fields
\psi^{Ab}
associated to the ideal will be in the kernel of the presymplectic sructure. Of course there can be extra degeneracy originating from the kernel distribution of
\omega^{F}
.

The above considerations suggest that the general situation is well described by the following generalization of presymplectic BV-AKSZ sigma models: the base is a DGCA
(\mathcal{A},\delta)
which is a module over
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X)
and the target is a weak presymplectic
Q
-manifold
(F,Q)
. It follows
\delta
preserves the ideal in
\mathcal{A}
generated by the zero modes of
{\rm Tr}
and hence is well defined on the quotient by the ideal. The quotient is then a DGCA with the nondegenerate trace. In fact, it is enough to require that the image of
\delta^{2}
belongs to the ideal. This is analogous to requiring
i_{Q^{2}}\omega^{F}=0
in the presymplectic context.

We see that there are two alternative approaches to describe a Lagrangian system in terms of the extended (i.e. associated to superspace) source space. The first one is to define the nondegenerate
{\rm Tr}
only on the equivalent reduction of
\mathcal{A}
to the cohomology of
\delta_{-1}
while the second is to define the degenerate trace on
\mathcal{A}
in such a way that the quotient of
\mathcal{A}
by the ideal determined by the trace coincides with the cohomology of
\delta_{-1}
. In the second case, instead of taking the quotient by the ideal one can immediately construct the presymplectic BV formulation on
Smaps(Spec(\mathcal{A}),F)
and then take the symplectic quotient to arrive at the underlying local BV system. We illustrate both approaches in the example of superspace formulation of the supersymmetric particle in Section
6
.

5
Presymplectic BV-AKSZ and rheonomy

5.1
Palatini-Cartan-Weyl gravity

The presymplectic BV-AKSZ formulation of gravity reviewed in Section
2.4
can be lifted to higher-dimensional space-time manifolds if one considers it at the level of equations of motion. More precisely, one reinterprets the presymplectic AKSZ model as a weak AKSZ model for which adding an extra contractible spacetime direction is merely an equivalence.
Of course, if one is only interested in the equations of motion this is almost obvious because the equations of motion can be written in terms of the de Rham differential and the wedge product of spacetime differential forms and hence can be formally considered on any spacetime, see e.g.
[
38
]
. We now uplift the presymplectic-AKSZ formulation described in section
2.4
from
n
-dimensional spacetime to an extended space
P=X\times\mathbb{R}^{n(n-1)/2}\to X
. The second factor will be then identified with the neighborhood of the identity in the Lorentz group
SO(n-1,1)
. The adapted coordinates on
T[1]P
are denoted as
(x^{\mu},\theta^{\mu},x^{i},\theta^{i})
and the de Rham differential as
\mathrm{d_{P}}
.

Let
\sigma:T[1]P\to\mathfrak{iso}(1,n-1)[1]
be a given configuration satisfying the equations of motion. In local coordinate terms it reads as

\sigma^{\ast}(\xi^{a})=\widetilde{e}^{a}=\widetilde{e}^{a}_{\mu}(x^{\nu},x^{i})\theta^{\mu}+\widetilde{e}^{a}_{i}(x^{\nu},x^{i})\theta^{i},\quad\sigma^{\ast}(\rho^{ab})=\widetilde{\omega}^{ab}=\widetilde{\omega}^{ab}_{\mu}(x^{\nu},x^{i})\theta^{\mu}+\widetilde{\omega}^{ab}_{i}(x^{\nu},x^{j})\theta^{i},

(5.1)

and we assume that
(\widetilde{e}^{a},\,\,\widetilde{\omega}^{bc})
define a nondegenerate coframe on
P
. Equations of motion (
2.11
) take the following form:

\mathcal{V}_{abc}(e)\widetilde{R}^{bc}=0,\quad\mathcal{V}_{abc}(e)\widetilde{T}^{c}=0,

(5.2)

where
\widetilde{R}^{ab}=\mathrm{d_{P}}\widetilde{\omega}^{ab}+\widetilde{\omega}^{a}_{\kern 4.09723ptc}\widetilde{\omega}^{cb}
and
\widetilde{T^{a}}=\mathrm{d_{P}}\widetilde{e}^{a}+\widetilde{\omega}^{a}_{\kern 4.09723ptb}\widetilde{e}^{b}
. Expanding 2-forms
\widetilde{R}^{ab}
and
\widetilde{T^{a}}
in the coframe
(\widetilde{e}^{a},\widetilde{\omega}^{ab})
as

\begin{gathered}\widetilde{R}^{ab}=\widetilde{R}^{ab}_{\kern 4.09723pt\kern 4.09723ptcd|}\widetilde{e}^{c}\widetilde{e}^{d}+\widetilde{R}^{ab}_{\kern 4.09723pt\kern 4.09723ptc|de}\widetilde{e}^{c}\widetilde{\omega}^{de}+\widetilde{R}^{ab}_{\kern 4.09723pt\kern 4.09723pt|cdef}\widetilde{\omega}^{cd}\widetilde{\omega}^{ef},\\
\widetilde{T}^{a}=\widetilde{T}^{a}_{\kern 4.09723ptcd|}\widetilde{e}^{c}\widetilde{e}^{d}+\widetilde{T}^{a}_{\kern 4.09723ptc|de}\widetilde{e}^{c}\widetilde{\omega}^{de}+\widetilde{T}^{a}_{\kern 4.09723pt|cdef}\widetilde{\omega}^{cd}\widetilde{\omega}^{ef}\end{gathered}

(5.3)

and substituting them into (
5.2
) one finds:

\widetilde{R}^{ac}_{\kern 4.09723pt\kern 4.09723ptbc}-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{a}_{b}\widetilde{R}^{cd}_{\kern 4.09723pt\kern 4.09723ptcd}=0,\quad\widetilde{T}^{a}_{\kern 4.09723ptcd|}=0,

(5.4a)

\widetilde{R}^{ab}_{\kern 4.09723pt\kern 4.09723ptc|de}=\widetilde{R}^{ab}_{\kern 4.09723pt\kern 4.09723pt|cdef}=\widetilde{T}^{a}_{\kern 4.09723ptc|de}=\widetilde{T}^{a}_{\kern 4.09723pt|cdef}=0\,,

(5.4b)

so that
\widetilde{T}^{a}=0
.

Let us consider a distribution
\mathcal{R}
generated by all the vector fields annihilating
e^{a}
, i.e. vector fields
V
satisfying
i_{V}e^{a}=0
. In fact,
\mathcal{R}
is involutive because
\sigma
is a solution. Indeed, for any
X,Y\in\mathcal{R}
one has:

\begin{gathered}e^{a}([X,Y])=e^{a}(\mathcal{L}_{X}Y)=\mathcal{L}_{X}\left(e^{a}(Y)\right)-(\mathcal{L}_{X}e^{a})(Y)=-i_{Y}(di_{X}+i_{X}d)e^{a}=\\
=-i_{Y}i_{X}(T^{a}-\omega^{a}_{\kern 4.09723ptb}e^{b})=-i_{Y}i_{X}T^{a}=0.\end{gathered}

(5.5)

It follows
P
is foliated by the integral submanifolds of
\mathcal{R}
and hence locally
P
is naturally a fiber bundle over
X
. Note that the bundle structure is determined by the choice of solution
\sigma
.

It is convenient to chose coordinates
x^{\mu},x^{i}
adapted to the bundle structure, i.e.
x^{\mu}
are pullbacks of the coordinates on
X
and
x^{i}
are fiber coordinates. Note that in this coordinate system
e^{a}_{i}=0
.
Now it is clear that equations (
5.4a
) pulled back to any section
X\to P
is precisely the Einstein equation and the zero torsion condition. At the same time equations (
5.4b
) set to zero the remaining components of the curvature and the torsion and are known as the rheonomy conditions in the group manifold approach to (super)gravity
[
37
,
38
]
. The pullback of
\omega^{ab}
to a fiber of
P
gives a nondegenerate flat
so(n-1,1)
-connection and hence one is given with the
so(n-1,1)
action on the fibers. In other words, locally we are dealing with the principle
SO(n-1,1)
-bundle over
X
. The
iso(n-1,1)
-valued 1-form defines a Cartan connection on
P

6
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetric particle and presymplectic BV-AKSZ

In this section we use a toy-model to demonstrate how the presymplectic BV-AKSZ formalism can be employed to reconstruct the superspace Lagrangians and their BV extensions in a geometrical way.

6.1
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymetric mechanics

Our toy-model is the
\mathcal{N}=1
superparticle. The respective SUSY algebra is:

\{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}\}=2\mathcal{P}.

(6.1)

It can be realised via the following vector fields

\mathcal{Q}=\partial_{\eta}+\eta\partial_{t},\quad\mathcal{P}=\partial_{t}

(6.2)

on the superspace
\mathbb{R}^{1|1}
with coordinates
(t,\eta)
. Moreover, on
\mathbb{R}^{1|1}
one also introduces chiral derivative:

\mathcal{D}=\partial_{\eta}-\eta\partial_{t},\quad\{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{D}\}=0.

(6.3)

The action for the particle model can be written in a manifestly supersymmetric way in terms of superfields
X^{a}(t,\eta)
:

S[X]=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1|1}}dtd\eta(\dot{X}_{a}\mathcal{D}X^{a}),

(6.4)

where
\dot{X}^{a}\equiv\partial_{t}X^{a}
. It is invariant under susy transformations

\delta X^{a}(t,\eta)=\varepsilon\mathcal{Q}X^{a}(t,\eta)=\varepsilon(\partial_{\eta}+\eta\partial_{t})X^{a}\,,

(6.5)

where
\varepsilon
is a constant fermionic parameter. In terms of the component fields introduced via

X^{a}(t,\eta)=x^{a}(t)+\eta\lambda^{a}(t),

(6.6)

the above transformations read as:

\delta x^{a}=\varepsilon\lambda^{a},\quad\delta\lambda^{a}=-\varepsilon\dot{x}^{a}\,.

(6.7)

Finally, performing integration over
\eta
one obtains the component action:

S[x,\lambda]=\frac{1}{2}\int dt\left(\dot{x}^{2}+\lambda_{a}\dot{\lambda}^{a}\right)\,.

(6.8)

Further details and examples on supersymmetric (quantum) mechanics can be found in e.g.
[
82
]
.

6.2
AKSZ model for
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetric particle

The supersymmetry invariance of the model from the previous subsection can be gauged by coupling it to the 1d supergravity. The resulting model is usually called
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetric particle and is most naturally constructed in the Hamiltonian BFV formalism by introducing ghosts associated to 1d supersymmetry transfomations. In the Lagrangian picture these ghosts give rise to the einbein and gravitino fields in 1 dimension. Quantization of the fermionic phase-space coordinates gives a spinor representation so that the model indeed describes a spinning particle. The field theoretical interpretation of the quantized model is that it gives a first-quantized description of the massless spinor field in Minkowski space
[
83
,
84
]
.

The phase space of the Hamiltonian constrained system is the super-extension of
T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}
by fermionic coordinates
\lambda^{a}
, equipped with the symplectic structure
dp_{a}dx^{a}-\frac{1}{2}d\lambda_{a}d\lambda^{a}
. The 1st class constraints generating the supersymmetry transformations are

\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}p^{2}\equiv\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}p_{a}p^{a}\,,\qquad(p\cdot\lambda)\,.

(6.9)

Here and below we use
(A\cdot B)\equiv A^{c}B_{c}
to denote the invariant pairing of Minkowski space (co)vectors. Introducing the respective ghost variables
\xi,\epsilon(\xi)=0
and
\psi,\epsilon(\psi)=1
as well as their canonically conjugated ghost momenta
\mathcal{P}_{\xi}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\psi}
, the BRST charge of the system can be written as:

H=-\frac{p^{2}}{2}\xi-\psi(p\cdot\lambda)-\mathcal{P}_{\xi}\psi\psi\,.

(6.10)

Together with the extended symplectic structure
\omega^{F}=d\chi
,
\chi=p_{a}dx^{a}-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{a}d\lambda^{a}+\mathcal{P}_{\xi}d\xi-\mathcal{P}_{\psi}d\psi
these data defines a degree zero symplectic
Q
-manifold
(F,Q,\omega^{F})
, where
Q
is determined by
i_{Q}\omega^{F}+dH=0
and is given explicitly by:

\begin{gathered}Qx^{a}=p^{a}\xi+\psi\lambda^{a},\qquad Qp^{a}=0,\qquad Q\lambda^{a}=-\psi p^{a},\\
Q\xi=\psi\psi,\qquad Q\psi=0,\\
Q\mathcal{P}_{\xi}=\frac{1}{2}p^{2},\qquad Q\mathcal{P}_{\psi}=(\lambda\cdot p)+2\mathcal{P}_{\xi}\psi\,.\end{gathered}

(6.11)

The BV formulation of the system is given by the AKSZ sigma model with source
T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1}
and target
(F,Q,\omega^{F})
.
Let
t,\theta
,
\mathrm{gh}(\theta)=1
denote the source space coordinates so that
\mathrm{d}_{X}=\theta\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}$}\kern 1.0pt}
. Introducing notations for the ghost degree preserving supermaps
\sigma:T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1|0}\to F
via

\begin{gathered}\sigma^{\ast}(p^{a})=p^{a}(t),\quad\sigma^{\ast}(x^{a})=x^{a}(t),\quad\sigma^{\ast}(\lambda^{a})=\lambda^{a}(t),\\
\sigma^{\ast}(\xi)=\theta e(t),\quad\sigma^{\ast}(\psi)=\theta\psi(t),\\
\sigma^{\ast}(\mathcal{P}_{\xi})=\sigma^{\ast}(\mathcal{P}_{\psi})=0\,,\end{gathered}

(6.12)

the AKSZ action takes the following form:

S[x,p,\lambda,e,\psi]=\int dt\left((p\cdot\dot{x})+\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda\cdot\dot{\lambda})-e\frac{p^{2}}{2}-\psi(\lambda\cdot p)\right)\,.

(6.13)

This is of course the usual extended Hamiltonian action for the above constrained system. The einbein
e
and gravitino
\psi
enter the action as Lagrange multipliers associated to the respective constraints.

The initial model considered in the previous subsection is recovered by setting gauge fields
e,\psi
to their background values:

e(t)=1,\quad\psi(t)=0\,.

(6.14)

In particular, the gauge transformations preserving this background are the starting point supersymmetry transformations (
6.7
) rewritten in the 1st order formulation. Indeed, these are given explicitly by

\delta x^{a}(t)=\varepsilon\lambda^{a}(t),\quad\delta p^{a}(t)=0,\quad\delta\lambda^{a}(t)=-\varepsilon p^{a}(t)\,.

(6.15)

Eliminating
p
via its own equations of motion one obtains SUSY transformations
(
6.7
)
. Finally, restricting AKSZ action (
6.14
) to this background gives the standard 1st order reformulation of the superparticle action (
6.8
):

S[x,p,\lambda]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1|0}}dt\left((p\cdot\dot{x})+\frac{1}{2}(\lambda\cdot\dot{\lambda})-\frac{p^{2}}{2}\right).

(6.16)

6.3
Superfield formulation at the level of EOM from AKSZ

We now employ the source space extension described in Section
4.1
. Namely we replace
T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1}

with
T[1]X=T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1|1}=T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1}\times T[1](\Pi\mathbb{R}^{1})
. Denoting the coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{1|1}
by
t,\eta
and their associated fiber coordinates by
\theta
and
\zeta
respectively the de Rham differential
\mathrm{d}_{X}
takes the form:

\mathrm{d}_{X}=\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\zeta\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\,.

(6.17)

Let us also list the ghost degree and the fermionic degree of the coordinates:

\begin{gathered}\mathrm{gh}(t)=0,\quad\epsilon(t)=0,\qquad\mathrm{gh}(\theta)=1,\quad\epsilon(\theta)=0,\\
\mathrm{gh}(\eta)=0,\quad\epsilon(\eta)=1,\qquad\mathrm{gh}(\zeta)=1,\quad\epsilon(\zeta)=1\,.\end{gathered}

(6.18)

For the future convenience we perform the following change of coordinates:

t\to t,\quad\theta\to\theta+\eta\zeta,\quad\eta\to\eta,\quad\zeta\to\zeta\,,

(6.19)

so that in the new coordinates
\mathrm{d}_{X}
takes the following form:

\mathrm{d}_{X}=\theta\partial_{t}+\zeta\mathcal{D}+\zeta^{2}\partial_{\theta},

(6.20)

where
\mathcal{D}
is the chiral derivative defined in (
6.3
). Note that the superspace analog of the
background solution (
6.14
) in these coordinates read as:

\sigma^{*}(\xi)=\theta,\qquad\sigma^{*}(\psi)=\zeta\,.

(6.21)

Indeed, it solves the equations of motion in the sector of

\xi,\psi
-variables and has a
geometrical meaning of a Cartan connection 1-form describing
\mathbb{R}^{1|1}
as a coset of the respective supergroup.

It is easy to see that
(\mathcal{A},\delta)
, where
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1|1})
and
\delta=\mathrm{d}_{X}
, can be equivalently reduced to
(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\delta})
by employing the procedure explained in Section
4.3
. Indeed, taking as a degree homogeneity in
\theta
one finds that the lowest degree term in
\delta
is
\delta_{-1}=\zeta^{2}\partial_{\theta}
and hence
(\mathcal{A},\delta)
equivalently reduces to the cohomology of
\delta_{-1}
. The cohomology can be explicitly identified as functions in
t,\eta,\zeta
modulo the relation
\zeta^{2}=0
and the induced differential is given by
\widetilde{\delta}=\zeta\mathcal{D}
.

In terms of the reduced system the background solution (
6.21
) takes the form:

\sigma^{\ast}(\xi)=0,\quad\sigma^{\ast}(\psi)=\zeta\,.

(6.22)

As before, we also set to zero ghost momenta
\mathcal{P}_{\xi},\mathcal{P}_{\psi}
. The remainning freedom in the ghost degree preserving supermaps
\sigma^{\ast}:C^{\infty}(F)\to\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
is parameterized by:

\begin{gathered}\sigma^{\ast}(x^{a})=X^{a}(t,\eta),\quad\sigma^{\ast}(p^{a})=P^{a}(t,\eta),\quad\sigma^{\ast}(\lambda^{a})=\Lambda^{a}(t,\eta),\,.\end{gathered}

(6.23)

The equations of motion are the
Q
-map condition
\mathrm{d}_{X}\circ\sigma^{*}=\sigma^{*}\circ Q
applied to the remaining coordinates
x,p,\lambda
and are given by:

\begin{gathered}\zeta\mathcal{D}X^{a}(t,\eta)=\zeta\Lambda^{a}(t,\eta),\\
\zeta\mathcal{D}\Lambda^{a}(t,\eta)=-\zeta P^{a}(t,\eta),\\
\zeta\mathcal{D}P^{a}(t,\eta)=0\,.\end{gathered}

(6.24)

They can be equivalently written as

\mathcal{D}\dot{X}^{a}(t,\eta)=0,\quad\Lambda^{a}(t,\eta)=\mathcal{D}X^{a}(t,\eta),\quad P^{a}=-\mathcal{D}\Lambda^{a}(t,\eta)\,.

(6.25)

The first equation is nothing but the equation of motion of the superfield action (
6.4
) while the second and third express
P,\Lambda
in terms of
X
so that
P,\Lambda
are the auxiliary superfields.

Let us finally check that the residual gauge transformation indeed reproduce susy transformations. Recall that gauge parameters are the ghost degree
-1
vertical vector fields on
T[1]X\times F
and hence they can be written as
Y=a(t,\eta)\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}+\alpha(t,\eta)\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi}
. Demanding the corresponding gauge transformation
\delta\sigma=[\mathrm{d}_{X}+Q{,}\,Y]
to preserve background (
6.22
) one gets

a(t,\eta)=b-2\eta\beta\quad\alpha(t,\eta)=\beta,

(6.26)

where
b
(resp.
\beta
) is the constant bosonic (resp. fermionic) parameter. Focusing on the fermionic symmetry and hence setting
b=0
one finds:

\delta X^{a}(t,\eta)=-2\eta\beta P^{a}(t,\eta)+\beta\Lambda^{a}(t,\eta),\quad\delta P^{a}(t,\eta)=0,\quad\delta\Lambda^{a}(t,\eta)=-\beta P^{a}(t,\eta).

(6.27)

Finaly, using (
6.25
) one gets

\delta X^{a}(t,\eta)=\beta(\partial_{\eta}+\eta\partial_{t})X^{a}(t,\eta)\,,

(6.28)

which is exactly (
6.5
).

6.4
Lagrangian superfield formulation via AKSZ

As we discussed in Section
4.3
there can be two equivalent ways to define Lagrangian AKSZ formulation in the case where the source space doesn’t have a natural volume form. It turns out that both work perfectly for the toy-model in question.

The first approach is to equivalently reduce the source space to a DGCA
(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\delta})
that admits an invariant trace. In the case at hand this is precisely the reduced algebra
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}=H^{\bullet}(\delta_{-1})
introduced in the previous subsection. The induced differential therein is
\widetilde{\delta}=\zeta\mathcal{D}
. Identifying
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
as a tensor product of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X)
,
X=\mathbb{R}^{1|1}
and the two-dimensional algebra
\mathfrak{a}=\mathbb{R}[\zeta]/(\zeta^{2})
there is a natural trace operation of ghost degree
-1
given by the tensor product of
\int dtd\eta
on
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{1|1})
and
{\rm Tr}_{0}
on
\mathfrak{a}
defined by

{\rm Tr}_{0}(1)=0,\quad{\rm Tr}_{0}(\zeta)=1.

(6.29)

Equivalently:

{\rm Tr}\left(f_{0}(t,\eta)+\zeta f_{1}(t,\eta)\right)=\int dtd\eta f_{1}(t,\eta)\,.

(6.30)

It easy to see that it is compatible with
\widetilde{\delta}
in the sense that

\int dtd\eta Tr_{0}(\widetilde{\delta}f(t,\eta,\zeta))=0

(6.31)

modulo boundary terms. Let us note that the analogous approach was proposed in
[
85
]
, see also
[
86
]
,
to define a correct inner product in the BFV-BRST quantization of systems with fermionic constraints. In fact in the simplest case of
N=1
spinning particle model the inner product is precisely the above trace tensored
with the usual inner-product for spinors.

Employing notations (
6.23
) for the ghost degree preserving supermaps and setting
\sigma^{*}(\xi),\sigma^{*}(\psi)
to their background values (
6.22
) the AKSZ-like action takes the form:

S[X,P,\Lambda]=\int dtd\eta\left((P\cdot\mathcal{D}X)-\frac{1}{2}(\Lambda\cdot\mathcal{D}\Lambda)-(P\cdot\Lambda)\right).

(6.32)

Eliminating
P
and
\Lambda
from (
6.32
) using their own equations of motion

\frac{\delta S}{\delta P^{a}(t,\eta)}=\mathcal{D}X^{a}-\Lambda^{a}=0,\quad\frac{\delta S}{\delta\Lambda^{a}(t,\eta)}=-\mathcal{D}\Lambda^{a}-P^{a}=0.

(6.33)

one gets

S=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1|1}}dtd\eta(\dot{X}\cdot\mathcal{D}X),\quad\delta X^{a}(t,\eta)=\beta(\partial_{\eta}+\eta\partial_{t})X^{a}(t,\eta)

(6.34)

which coincides with (
6.4
) up to an overall sign.

To obtain the full scale BV-formulation one needs to pass to supermaps
\widehat{\sigma}^{\ast}:C^{\infty}(F)\to\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
, i.e.

\begin{gathered}\widehat{\sigma}^{\ast}(x^{a})=X^{a}(t,\eta)+\zeta\overset{1}{X^{a}}(t,\eta),\qquad\widehat{\sigma}^{\ast}(p^{a})=P^{a}(t,\eta)+\zeta\overset{1}{P^{a}}(t,\eta),\\
\widehat{\sigma}^{\ast}(\lambda^{a})=\Lambda^{a}(t,\eta)+\zeta\overset{1}{\Lambda^{a}}(t,\eta).\end{gathered}

(6.35)

The induced BV symplectic form read as

\omega_{BV}=\int dtd\eta\left(d\overset{1}{P_{a}}(t,\eta)dX^{a}(t,\eta)+dP^{a}(t,\eta)d\overset{1}{X_{a}}-d\overset{1}{\Lambda_{a}}(t,\eta)d\Lambda^{a}(t,\eta)\right)\,.

(6.36)

It follows the BV symplectic structure is nondegenerate and coincides with that of the usual BV extension of the superfield action (
6.32
).

Let us now turn to the second approach. We saw in Section
4.3
that instead of reducing
(\mathcal{A},\delta)
in the first place, one can alternatively introduce a degenerate trace on
\mathcal{A}
in such a way that the induced presymplectic structure on the space of AKSZ fields is degenerate and its symplectic reduction gives the correct BV description. In our toy-model example this is easily done by defining the ghost degree
-1
trace on
\mathcal{A}
as follows

{\rm Tr}_{0}(f(\theta,\zeta))=\int d\theta d\zeta f(\theta,\zeta)\mathbb{Y}^{0|1}\,,\qquad\mathbb{Y}^{0|1}=\theta\delta^{\prime}(\zeta)\,,

(6.37)

where
\delta^{\prime}(\zeta)
denotes the derivative of the Dirac delta-function.
4
4

4

The derivative of the Dirac delta function can be defined in purely algebraic terms by requiring
\int d\zeta\delta^{\prime}(\zeta)f(\zeta)=-f^{\prime}(0)
.
Note that

\mathbb{Y}^{0|1}=\theta\delta^{\prime}(\zeta)
is precisely the picture changing operator from
[
57
]
. It is easy to see, that
{\rm Tr}=\int dtd\eta\otimes{\rm Tr}_{0}
is compatible with
\mathrm{d}_{X}
and that all basis elements proportional to
\theta
or
\zeta^{2}
are in the kernel. Moreover, defining the presymplectic structure on the space of supermaps from
T[1]\mathbb{R}^{1|1}
to
F
using the above trace one finds that all the fields associated to the basis elements proportional to
\theta
or
\zeta^{2}
are in the kernel of the presymplectic structure and hence taking the symplectic quotient gives the same reduced space of supermaps as the reduction of the source to
(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\delta})
. This gives an example of the presymplectic BV-AKSZ system where the presymplectic structure in the target space is nondegenerate while the induced presymplectic structure on the space of supermaps is degenerate thanks to the degenerate trace.

-\frac{i}{2}(\gamma_{5}\gamma_{a})_{\alpha\beta}\psi^{\alpha}(\gamma_{cd})^{\beta}_{\kern 4.09723pt\gamma}\rho^{cd}\psi^{\gamma}d\xi^{a}=-\frac{i}{2}\psi^{\alpha}(\gamma_{5}\gamma_{a}\gamma_{bc})_{\alpha\beta}\rho^{bc}\psi^{\beta}d\xi^{a}

(B.5)

•

The sixth term can be rewritten as:

-2i(\gamma_{5}\gamma_{a})_{\alpha\beta}\psi^{\alpha}d\psi^{\beta}\rho^{a}_{\kern 4.09723ptb}\xi^{b}=2i(\gamma_{5}\gamma_{a})_{\alpha\beta}\rho^{a}_{\kern 4.09723ptb}\xi^{b}d\psi^{\alpha}\psi^{\beta}\,,

(B.6)

so that it cancels one of the terms in (
B.4
).

•

The last term vanishes thanks to Fierz identity (
A.7
).

Collecting summands and taking into account
\psi^{\alpha}\psi^{\beta}=\psi^{(\alpha}\psi^{\beta)}
we get the result:

\begin{gathered}i_{Q}\omega^{F}=-d\left(\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{abcd}\xi^{c}\xi^{d}\rho^{a}_{\kern 4.09723pte}\rho^{eb}\right)+i(\gamma_{5}\gamma_{a}\gamma_{bc})_{(\alpha\beta)}\left(\frac{1}{2}\psi^{\alpha}d\rho^{bc}\psi^{\beta}\xi^{a}+d\psi^{\alpha}\rho^{bc}\psi^{\beta}\xi^{a}-\frac{1}{2}\psi^{\alpha}\rho^{bc}\psi^{\beta}d\xi^{a}\right)=\\

(B.7)

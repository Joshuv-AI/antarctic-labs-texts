Non-linear Quasi-Normal Modes of the Schwarzschild Black Hole from the Penrose Limit
Alex Kehagias, Davide Perrone, Antonio Riotto

Abstract
The Penrose limit connects a plane wave geometry to the photon ring of a black hole, where the quasi-normal modes are located in the eikonal limit. Utilizing this simplification, we analytically extract the quadratic-level non-linearities in the quasi-normal modes of a Schwarzschild black hole for the ( ℓ × ℓ ) → 2 ​ ℓ (\ell\times\ell)\to 2\ell channel. We demonstrate that this result is independent of ℓ \ell and further confirm it through symmetry arguments.

(1)

In this paper we take advantage of the fact that the QNMs, in the limit of large angular momenta (the so-called eikonal limit), may be thought of as degrees of freedom populating the photon ring and slowly leaking out of it

The “near-photon ring” physics can be described by what is known as the Penrose limit which associates a plane wave to a region of spacetime near a null geodesic

Such plane wave geometrically realizes the geometrical optics approximation valid in the large multipole limit.

The Penrose limit considerably simplifies the description of the QNMs

;
Giataganas et al. 2024

\ell

\ell\gg 1

II
The Penrose limit

II.1
Background

The Penrose limit
Penrose
associates to every space-time metric
g_{ab}
, with line element
{\rm d}s^{2}
, and a null geodesic
\gamma
in that space-time, a (limiting) plane wave metric.
The first step is to rewrite the metric in coordinates “adapted” to
\gamma
, which corresponds to an embedding of
\gamma
into a twist-free congruence of null geodesics, given by some coordinates
V
,
Z
, and
\bar{Z}
constant, with the remaining coordinate
U
playing the role of the affine parameter and
\gamma(U)
coinciding with the geodesic at
V=Z=\bar{Z}=0
.
The next step is to perform the change of coordinates

(U,V,Z,\bar{Z})=(u,\lambda^{2}v,\lambda z,\lambda\bar{z})

for some real
\lambda
and to take the limit

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\lambda^{-2}{\rm d}s^{2}={\rm d}s^{2}_{\gamma}.

The resulting metric is the so-called Penrose limit of the initial space-time, which, recast in Brinkmann coordinates, has the plane-parallel (pp) wave form

{\rm d}s^{2}_{\gamma}=2{\rm d}u{\rm d}v+H(u,z,\bar{z}){\rm d}u^{2}-2{\rm d}z{\rm d}\bar{z}.

(2)

The coordinate
u
plays the role of the affine parameter along the geodesic, and the function
H
controls the geodesic deviation properties along the transverse coordinates
z
and
\bar{z}
. For the Schwarzschild black hole of mass
M
and metric (where we have set the Newton constant and the light speed to unity)

\displaystyle{\rm d}s^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(r){\rm d}t^{2}-f^{-1}(r){\rm d}r^{2}-r^{2}({\rm d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta{\rm d}\phi^{2}),

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-2M/r,

(3)

the Penrose limit around the circular photon ring located at
r_{0}=3M
and
\theta=\pi/2
has the metric (
2
) with
(see for instance Refs.
Fransen 2023
;
Giataganas et al. 2024
)

H(z,\bar{z})=-\frac{1}{3M^{2}}(z^{2}+\bar{z}^{2}),

(4)

with
u=-t/3
and
v=-t+3\sqrt{3}M\phi
in terms of Schwarzschild coordinates.

II.2
Linear perturbations

For a generic pp-wave space-time such as the one described by the metric (
2
) and satisfying the condition (where subscripts denote partial derivatives with respect to the corresponding variable, e.g.,
H_{z}=\partial_{z}H,
and similarly for others)

H_{z\bar{z}}=0,

(5)

any metric perturbation
h_{\mu\nu}

that satisfies the linearized Einstein vacuum equations can be expressed in terms of a complex scalar field
\Phi
, the Hertz potential, which satisfies the Klein-Gordon equation

\frac{1}{2}\Box\Phi=-\Phi_{z\bar{z}}-\frac{1}{2}H(z,\bar{z})\Phi_{vv}+\Phi_{uv}=0.

(6)

Indeed, having a solution of the scalar wave equation (
6
), one can construct solutions of spin-two states by using the spin-raising operator. The latter is written in Brinkmann coordinates as
Adamo et al. 2018

\displaystyle R^{+}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}u\,\partial_{\bar{z}}+\mathrm{d}z\,\partial_{v},

\displaystyle R^{-}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}u\,\partial_{z}+\mathrm{d}\bar{z}\,\partial_{v}.

(7)

For the polarization vector

\displaystyle\epsilon=\Big(0,0,\epsilon_{z},\epsilon_{\bar{z}}\Big)=\Big(0,0,\epsilon_{+},\epsilon_{-}\Big),

(8)

where

\displaystyle\epsilon_{+}\epsilon_{-}=0,

(9)

the spin-two field reads

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}\mathrm{d}x^{\nu}=h_{\mu\nu}^{++}\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}\mathrm{d}x^{\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}^{--}\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}\mathrm{d}x^{\nu}

(10)

where

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}^{++}\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}\mathrm{d}x^{\nu}=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{+}\epsilon_{+}R^{+}\Big[R^{+}(\Phi)\Big],

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}^{--}\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}\mathrm{d}x^{\nu}=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{-}\epsilon_{-}R^{-}\Big[R^{-}(\bar{\Phi})\Big].

(11)

Normalizing the polarization vector such that
\epsilon_{+}^{2}=\epsilon_{-}^{2}=1
and in the radiation gauge (setting also the transverse-free and traceless conditions)

\nabla^{\mu}h_{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}=0,\quad h_{v\mu}=0

(12)

the metric perturbed by the spin-two fields
h_{\mu\nu}
is at first-order

\displaystyle{\rm d}s^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2{\rm d}u{\rm d}v+\left[H+\Phi_{\bar{z}\bar{z}}+\bar{\Phi}_{zz}\right]{\rm d}u^{2}+2\Phi_{v\bar{z}}{\rm d}u{\rm d}z+2\bar{\Phi}_{vz}{\rm d}u{\rm d}\bar{z}

(13)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Phi_{vv}{\rm d}z^{2}+\bar{\Phi}_{vv}{\rm d}\bar{z}^{2}-2{\rm d}z{\rm d}\bar{z}.

\displaystyle\Phi_{vv}=(h_{+}-ih_{\times}),\qquad\bar{\Phi}_{vv}=(h_{+}+ih_{\times}).

\displaystyle-C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,\ell^{\mu}n^{\nu}\bar{m}^{\rho}n^{\sigma},

\displaystyle\Psi_{4}=

(22)

The corresponding non-zero spin coefficients in the basis (

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\tau^{(1)}=-\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{vv\bar{z}},\\

III
Second-order equation for the Weyl scalar
\Psi_{0}

For Petrov-type D spacetimes like the Kerr black hole, the first-order
\Psi_{4}^{(1)}
is completely determined by the zeroth-order background (Teukolsky equation) and similarly, the second-order
\Psi_{4}^{(2)}
is determined purely by first-order quantities.
For Petrov-type N, like the pp-wave background we are considering here, this is not true any longer.
In fact, the first-order
\Psi_{4}^{(1)}
is determined now by the other first-order Weyl scalars and similarly the second-order
\Psi_{4}^{(2)}
is determined by the second-order quantities.
In other words, to determine
\Psi_{4}^{(2)}
, we need all the second-order perturbations of the metric.
Therefore, for Petrov-type N spacetimes, it is more convenient to consider
\Psi_{0}
instead.
In order to determine the equation for
\Psi_{0}
, we will need the following two Bianchi identities for Ricci-flat spacetimes

\displaystyle-(\bar{\delta}+\pi-4\alpha)\Psi_{0}+(D-4\rho-2\epsilon)\Psi_{1}+3\kappa\Psi_{2}=0,

(26)

\displaystyle-(\Delta+\mu-4\gamma)\Psi_{0}+(\delta-4\tau-2\beta)\Psi_{1}+3\sigma\Psi_{2}=0,

(27)

where

\displaystyle D=\ell^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu},\qquad\Delta=n^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu},\qquad\delta=m^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu},\qquad\bar{\delta}=\bar{m}^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}.

(28)

Let us now act on Eq. (
26
) with
\delta^{(0)}
and on Eq. (
27
) with
D^{(0)}
and substract them. The result is the equation

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\bigg[\delta^{(0)}(\bar{\delta}+\pi-4\alpha)-D^{(0)}(\Delta+\mu-4\gamma)\bigg]\Psi_{0}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\bigg[\delta^{(0)}(D-4\rho-2\epsilon)-D^{(0)}(\delta-4\tau-2\beta)\bigg]\Psi_{1}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 3\left(\delta^{(0)}\kappa-D^{(0)}\sigma\right)\Psi_{2}=0.

(29)

By the perturbative expansion of the Weyl scalars and the spin coefficients, we find that up to second-order Eq. (
29
) is written as

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\bigg[\delta^{(0)}(\bar{\delta}+\pi-4\alpha)^{(0)}-D^{(0)}(\Delta+\mu-4\gamma)^{(0)}\bigg]\Psi_{0}^{(1)}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\bigg[\delta^{(0)}(\bar{\delta}+\pi-4\alpha)^{(1)}-D^{(0)}(\Delta+\mu-4\gamma)^{(1)}\bigg]\Psi_{0}^{(1)}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\bigg[\delta^{(0)}(\bar{\delta}+\pi-4\alpha)^{(0)}-D^{(0)}(\Delta+\mu-4\gamma)^{(0)}\bigg]\Psi_{0}^{(2)}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\bigg[\delta^{(0)}(D-4\rho-2\epsilon)^{(0)}-D^{(0)}(\delta-4\tau-2\beta)^{(0)}\bigg]\Psi_{1}^{(1)}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\bigg[\delta^{(0)}(D-4\rho-2\epsilon)^{(1)}-D^{(0)}(\delta-4\tau-2\beta)^{(1)}\bigg]\Psi_{1}^{(1)}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 3D^{(0)}\left(\sigma^{(1)}\Psi_{2}^{(1)}\right)=0.

(30)

Note that

\displaystyle\bigg[\delta^{(0)}(D-4\rho-2\epsilon)^{(0)}-D^{(0)}(\delta-4\tau-2\beta)^{(0)}\bigg]\Psi_{1}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\delta^{(0)}D^{(0)}-D^{(0)}\delta^{(0)}\right)\Psi_{1}^{(2)}=0,

(31)

due to the commutation relation

\displaystyle[\delta,D]=(\bar{\alpha}+\beta-\bar{\pi})D+\kappa\Delta-(\bar{\rho}+\epsilon-\bar{\epsilon})\delta-\sigma\bar{\delta},

(32)

so that

\displaystyle\left[\delta^{(0)},D^{(0)}\right]=0.

(33)

Eq. (
30
) can explicitly be written by using Eqs. (
23
), (
24
) and (
25
) and the following explicit form of the differential operators
\delta,D
and
\Delta

\displaystyle\delta^{(0)}=

\displaystyle-\partial_{z},\qquad\delta^{(1)}=-\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{uu}\partial_{\bar{z}},

\displaystyle D^{(0)}=

\displaystyle a\partial_{v},\qquad\Delta^{(0)}=\frac{1}{a}\left(\partial_{u}-\frac{1}{2}H\partial_{v}\right),

\displaystyle\Delta^{(1)}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2a}(\Phi_{\bar{z}\bar{z}}+\bar{\Phi}_{zz})\partial_{v}+\frac{1}{a}(\Phi_{v\bar{z}}\partial_{\bar{z}}+\bar{\Phi}_{vz}\partial_{z}).

(34)

Then it turns out from Eq.(
30
) that the second-order
\Psi_{0}^{(2)}
satisfies the equation

T_{2}\Psi_{0}^{(2)}=S_{2}^{(2)}

(35)

where the differential operator
T_{2}
is given by

\displaystyle T_{2}=-\delta^{(0)}(\bar{\delta}+\pi-4\alpha)^{(0)}+D^{(0)}(\Delta+\mu-4\gamma)^{(0)},

(36)

and the source
S^{(2)}_{\Psi_{0}}
is

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{\Psi_{0}}=

\displaystyle\bigg[\delta^{(0)}(\bar{\delta}+\pi-4\alpha)^{(1)}-D^{(0)}(\Delta+\mu-4\gamma)^{(1)}\bigg]\Psi_{0}^{(1)}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\bigg[\delta^{(0)}(D-4\rho-2\epsilon)^{(1)}-D^{(0)}(\delta-4\tau-2\beta)^{(1)}\bigg]\Psi_{1}^{(1)}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 3D^{(0)}\left(\sigma^{(1)}\Psi_{2}^{(1)}\right)

(37)

We can now use Eqs. (
23
), (
24
) and (
25
) in (
36
) from where we find that

\displaystyle T_{2}\Psi_{0}^{(2)}=\frac{1}{2}\Box\Psi_{0}^{(2)}.

(38)

and calculate the source, which turns out to be

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{\Psi_{0}}=

\displaystyle a^{2}\bigg[-\frac{3}{2}\Phi_{vvv\bar{z}}^{2}+\Phi_{vvv}\Phi_{vvv\bar{z}\bar{z}}+\frac{3}{2}\Phi_{vv\bar{z}\bar{z}}\Phi_{vvvv}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2\Phi_{vv\bar{z}}\Phi_{vvvv\bar{z}}+\frac{1}{4}\Phi_{vv}\Phi_{vvvv\bar{z}\bar{z}}+\frac{1}{4}\bar{\Phi}_{vv}\Phi_{vvvvzz}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Phi_{v\bar{z}\bar{z}}\Phi_{vvvvv}-\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{v\bar{z}}\Phi_{vvvvvv\bar{z}}-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\Phi}_{vz}\Phi_{vvvvvz}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\Phi_{\bar{z}\bar{z}}\Phi_{vvvvvv}+\frac{1}{4}\bar{\Phi}_{zz}\Phi_{vvvvvv}\bigg].

(39)

For the QNMs, we first look for linear solutions to the Eq. (
6
) and demand an outgoing boundary condition for the unstable direction
r-r_{0}=(z+\bar{z})/\sqrt{6}

\theta-\pi/2=i(\bar{z}-z)/r_{0}\sqrt{2}

When dealing only with the fundamental mode, they yield the scalar function to be of the form

\displaystyle\Phi(u,v,z,\bar{z})=Ae^{-iP_{u}u+iP_{v}v+\frac{3}{4}\ell\omega^{2}(1+i)(z^{2}+2iz\bar{z}+\bar{z}^{2})},

(40)

where

P_{u}=\frac{3}{2}\omega(i-1),\qquad P_{v}=\ell\omega\equiv\omega_{\ell},\qquad\omega=\frac{1}{3\sqrt{3}M}.

(41)

When substituting this solution the source in Eq. (

\displaystyle S_{\Psi_{0}}^{(2)}=

\displaystyle-6(1+i)a^{2}\ell^{7}\omega^{8}\Phi^{2}-\frac{3}{4}a^{2}\ell^{7}\omega^{8}\Big[1+3\ell\omega^{2}(z-\bar{z})^{2}\Big]\Phi\bar{\Phi}.

(42)

Then, it is straightforward to verify that the solution to the equation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Box\Psi_{0}^{(2)}=S_{\Psi_{0}}^{(2)},

(43)

is the sum of two functions, one oscillating and decaying, the other only decaying. The oscillating part satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Box\Psi_{0\,{\rm osc}}^{(2)}=-6(1+i)a^{2}\ell^{7}\omega^{8}\Phi^{2},

(44)

and its solution is

\displaystyle\Psi_{0\,{\rm osc}}^{(2)}=2ia^{2}P_{v}^{6}\Phi^{2}.

(45)

Similarly, the the non-oscillating (decaying) part
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Box\Psi_{0\,{\rm dec}}^{(2)}=-\frac{3}{4}a^{2}\ell^{7}\omega^{8}\Big[1+3\ell\omega^{2}(z-\bar{z})^{2}\Big],

(46)

and it is given by

\displaystyle\Psi_{0\,{\rm dec}}^{(2)}=-\frac{1}{4}a^{2}\ell^{6}\omega^{6}\Phi\bar{\Phi}.

(47)

We will not consider further the decaying solution (

IV
Quadratic QNMs for the channel
(\ell\times\ell)\to 2\ell

We are now in the position to calculate the non-linear QNMs of the gravitational waves on the photon ring.
This is not yet the full answer as we will need to propagate it awy from the photon ring at large distances.
Using Eqs. (
19
) and (
21
) we find

\displaystyle\Psi_{0}=\frac{1}{2}a^{2}h_{vv}=-\frac{1}{2}a^{2}P_{v}^{2}h,

(48)

and

\displaystyle h=\frac{2\Psi_{0}}{a^{2}P_{v}^{2}},

(49)

where we have left the arbitrary normalization
a
, to check that the non-linear ratio is independent from it.
From Eqs. (
45
) and (
24
) we obtain

\displaystyle h^{(2)}=

\displaystyle\frac{2\Psi_{0\,{\rm osc}}^{(2)}}{a^{2}(2P_{v})^{2}}=P_{v}^{4}\Phi^{2},

(50)

\displaystyle h^{(1)}=

\displaystyle\frac{2\Psi_{0}^{(1)}}{a^{2}P_{v}^{2}}=P_{v}^{2}\Phi.

(51)

We finally obtain

\frac{h^{(2)}}{\big(h^{(1)}\big)^{2}}=1.

(52)

Notice that we have calculated this ratio in the radiation gauge augmented with the traceless and transverse-free condition, which is the same gauge where the non-linearities are extracted numerically
Loutrel et al. 2021
;
Ripley et al. 2021
.
In this way, the issues about the non gauge-invariance of the Weyl scalars at second-order (see, Appendix
C
and Ref.
Kehagias and Riotto 2025
) are avoided.

We have found that in the Penrose limit of large multipoles the non-linear QNM ratio does not depend on the multipole itself for the process
(\ell\times\ell)\to 2\ell
.
In the followig subsection we offer a simple symmetry argument to explain such a result to explain why
h^{(2)}/(h^{(1)}\big)^{2}
is independent from the multipoles.

IV.1
Symmetry argument

We perform the following change of coordinates

u^{\prime}=u,\,\,v^{\prime}=\lambda^{2}\,v,\,\,z^{\prime}=\lambda\,z,\,\,\bar{z}^{\prime}=\lambda\,\bar{z},

(53)

for which

\Box^{\prime}=\lambda^{-2}\,\Box,

(54)

since
H(z^{\prime},\bar{z}^{\prime})=\lambda^{2}\,H(z,\bar{z})
. Therefore, if
\Phi
is the solution of the equation
\Box\Phi=0
, then

\Phi(u^{\prime},v^{\prime},z^{\prime},\bar{z}^{\prime})=\lambda^{\Delta}\Phi(u,v,z,\bar{z})

(55)

is still a solution of the equation
\Box\Phi=0
for any
\Delta
. From Eq. (
24
) we read off that

\Psi_{0}^{(1)}
has weight
(\Delta-8)
and the second-order source has weight

(2\Delta-14)
. Therefore, assigning a weight
\beta
to
\Psi_{0}^{(2)}
, we have

-2+\beta=2\Delta-14,

(56)

from the equation of motion, from which we read off that
\beta=(2\Delta-12)
. This means that
{\cal R}_{\Psi_{0}}

has weight

(2\Delta-12-2\Delta+8)=-4.

(57)

We now can choose
\lambda=\ell^{1/2}
to cancel the dependence on
\ell
from the function
\Phi
so that
{\cal R}_{\Psi_{0}}
scales like
1/\ell^{2}
. Since
h^{(2)}/(h^{(1)}\big)^{2}
scales like
\ell^{2}{\cal R}_{\Psi_{0}}
, we automatically deduce that the
h^{(2)}/(h^{(1)}\big)^{2}
may not depend on
\ell
in the eikonal limit.

IV.2
An alternative derivation

It is possible to calculate directly the second-order metric perturbations

h_{\mu\nu}^{(2)}
directly from second-order perturbations of the Einstein equations.
For this, let us write the traceless second-order metric in the radiation gauge as

\displaystyle{\rm d}s^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2{\rm d}u{\rm d}v+\left[H+\Phi_{\bar{z}\bar{z}}+\bar{\Phi}_{zz}+h_{uu}^{(2)}\right]{\rm d}u^{2}+\Big(2\Phi_{v\bar{z}}+h_{uz}^{(2)}\Big){\rm d}u{\rm d}z+\Big(2\bar{\Phi}_{vz}+h_{u\bar{z}}^{(2)}\Big){\rm d}u{\rm d}\bar{z}

(58)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Big(\Phi_{vv}+h_{zz}^{(2)}\Big){\rm d}z^{2}+\Big(\bar{\Phi}_{vv}+h_{\bar{z}\bar{z}}^{(2)}\Big){\rm d}\bar{z}^{2}-2{\rm d}z{\rm d}\bar{z}.

In addition, the transversality condition gives the relations

\displaystyle\partial_{\bar{z}}h_{uz}^{(2)}+\partial_{z}h_{u\bar{z}}^{(2)}-\partial_{v}h_{uu}^{(2)}=0,\qquad\partial_{v}h_{uz}^{(2)}=\partial_{\bar{z}}h_{zz}^{(2)},\qquad\partial_{v}h_{u\bar{z}}^{(2)}=\partial_{z}h_{\bar{z}\bar{z}}^{(2)}.

(59)

Then, the second-order vacuum Einstein equations are

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}^{(2)}=0,

(60)

and in particular it turns out that
the equations

\displaystyle R_{zz}^{(2)}=R_{\bar{z}\bar{z}}^{(2)}=0,

(61)

are explicitly given by

\displaystyle\partial_{v}\partial_{z}h_{uz}^{(2)}-\frac{9}{2}\omega^{2}(z^{2}+\bar{z}^{2})\partial_{v}^{2}h_{zz}^{(2)}-\partial_{u}\partial_{v}h_{zz}^{(2)}+3(1+i)\ell^{5}\omega^{6}\Phi^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{3}{4}\ell^{5}\omega^{6}\bigg\{2-2i+3\ell\omega^{2}\left[(z-\bar{z})^{2}-2i(z^{2}-\bar{z}^{2})\right]\bigg\}\Phi\bar{\Phi}=0,

(62)

and its complex conjugate. Using the gauge condition (
59
), we find that (
62
) is written as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Box h_{zz}^{(2)}=3(1+i)\ell^{5}\omega^{6}\Phi^{2}+\frac{3}{4}\ell^{5}\omega^{6}\bigg\{2-2i+3\ell\omega^{2}\left[(z-\bar{z})^{2}-2z\bar{z}-2i(z^{2}-\bar{z}^{2})\right]\bigg\}\Phi\bar{\Phi}.

(63)

The solution of the above equation sourced by the oscillating part of the right-hand source proportional to
\Phi^{2}
is given by

\displaystyle h_{zz}^{(2)}=h_{+}^{(2)}-ih_{\times}^{(2)}=i\ell^{4}\omega^{4}\Phi^{2}.

(64)

By recalling that

\displaystyle h_{zz}^{(1)}=h_{+}^{(1)}-ih_{\times}^{(1)}=\Phi_{vv}=-\ell^{2}\omega^{2}\Phi,

(65)

we find that

\displaystyle\frac{h^{(2)}_{2\ell}}{\big(h^{(1)}_{\ell}\big)^{2}}=1,

(66)

which confirms the result (
52
).

IV.3
From the photon ring to large distances

The last step to find the ratio
{\cal R}_{\ell\times\ell}
is to
match the Penrose limit solutions at first- and second-order to the one at large distances away from the photon ring. This procedure is done in detail in Appendix
A
and we report here only the final result,

\mathcal{R}_{\ell\times\ell}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma_{\ell}}}\cdot\frac{c_{\ell}^{2}}{c_{2\ell}}\frac{h^{(2)}_{2\ell}}{\big(h^{(1)}_{\ell}\big)^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma_{\ell}}}\cdot\frac{c_{\ell}^{2}}{c_{2\ell}},

(67)

where

c_{\ell}={}_{-2}Y_{\ell\ell}\left(\frac{\pi}{2},0\right)=\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{2^{\ell}}\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}\frac{(2\ell)!}{(\ell+2)!(\ell-2)!}}

(68)

and

\gamma_{\ell}=e^{-i\pi/4}\left(2Q_{0}^{\prime\prime}\right)^{1/4},

(69)

with
Schutz and Will 1985

Q_{0}^{\prime\prime}=-\left(1-\frac{r_{s}}{r_{0}}\right)\frac{\ell^{2}}{r_{0}^{4}}\left(6-20\frac{r_{s}}{r}+15\frac{r_{s}^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)

(70)

Here
\lambda=\ell(\ell+1)
, and
r_{0}=3r_{s}/2
is the point of the maximum of the effective potential. In the eikonal limit
|{\cal R}_{\ell\times\ell}|
goes to a plateau whose amplitude is of the order of 0.24.
Indeed, for large
\ell
we have

\displaystyle c_{\ell}\approx\frac{e^{-2/\ell}\ell^{1/4}}{\sqrt{2}\pi^{3/4}},\qquad\gamma_{\ell}\approx\frac{\sqrt{2}\ell^{1/2}}{3\sqrt{3}}e^{-i\pi/4},

(71)

and therefore,
|{\cal R}_{\ell\times\ell}|
asymptotes to

\displaystyle|{\cal R}_{\ell\times\ell}|\approx\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{3}{\pi}\right)^{3/4}\approx 0.24.

(72)

V
Conclusions

In this paper we have calculated the
level of non-linearities in the QNMs for the Schwarzschild black hole in the eikonal limit and at the quadratic level, focussing on the
(\ell\times\ell)\to 2\ell
channel.
We have adopted the Penrose limit which allows to zoom in onto the photon ring where the QNMs are generated.
Our findings can be extended in several directions.
First of all, it will be interesting to consider other channels, e.g. different multipoles as initial states.
Maybe more interestingly, it would be relevant to extend our calculations to the Kerr black holes and confirm that the non-linearities do not scale much with the black hole spin.
We leave such investigations for the next future
Kehagias et al. tion
.

Appendix A
Matching

In this appendix we will study the matching needed to connect the result in the Penrose limit to the asymptotic solutions.
Note that, in order to be consistent with the existing literature on the WKB approximation for the QNMs, we will change the signature used so far from
mostly minus
to
mostly plus
, so that our result can be easily checked.

We will perform the matching of the Hertz potential
\Phi
from the photon ring to large distances in three steps:

•

First we match the angular part with the associated spherical harmonic at
\theta=\pi/2
, which is just proportional to a pure phase
e^{i\ell\phi}
;

•

then, by noticing that the equation of motion for the radial part are the same to that of an harmonic oscillator, we will match within the WKB approximation of an harmonic oscillator at
r=r_{0}
;

•

finally we will match this WKB solution near the lightring with the WKB for the asymptotic behavior.

This procedure will give us a solution of the form

\Phi_{\ell,m,n}=b_{\Phi}\cdot{}_{-2}Y_{\ell,m}\,e^{-i\omega_{\ell,m,n}(t-r)}

(A1)

at large distances away from the photon ring.
The first point can be implemented by recalling that

{}_{-2}Y_{\ell\ell}\left(\frac{\pi}{2},\phi\right)=c_{\ell}\,e^{i\ell\phi},

(A2)

where
c_{\ell}
is given in Eq. (
68
).
The matching to the previous solution requires a constant in front of the asymptotic solution such that

e^{i\ell\phi}\to\frac{1}{c_{\ell}}{}_{-2}Y_{\ell\ell}\left(\frac{\pi}{2},\phi\right),

(A3)

which can then be extended for every value of
\theta
.

The second part is more complex and requires the study of the solution of the equation, (written in real coordinates
x_{1}=(z+\bar{z})/\sqrt{2}
and
x_{2}=(z-\bar{z})/(i\sqrt{2})
),

(2\partial_{u}\partial_{v}-\alpha^{2}(x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2})\partial_{v}^{2}+\partial_{1}^{2}+\partial_{2}^{2})\Phi=S,

(A4)

where
\alpha^{2}=1/3M^{2}
and

.
To familiarize with the form of the equation we recap the homogeneous solution for the equation by writing the ansatz

\Phi=b_{\Phi}\,e^{iP_{v}v+iP_{u}u}\phi_{1}(x_{1})\phi_{2}(x_{2}),

(A5)

and substituting back in the homogeneous equation we get

-2P_{u}P_{v}\phi_{1}(x_{1})\phi_{2}(x_{2})+\alpha^{2}P_{v}^{2}(x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2})\phi_{1}(x_{1})\phi_{2}(x_{2})+\phi_{1}(x_{1})\phi_{2}^{\prime\prime}(x_{2})+\phi_{2}(x_{2})\phi_{1}^{\prime\prime}(x_{1})=0.

(A6)

Splitting the two equations for
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
we get

\displaystyle\phi_{1}^{\prime\prime}(x_{1})+\alpha^{2}P_{v}^{2}\,x_{1}^{2}\phi_{1}(x_{1})=(P_{u}P_{v}+\Delta)\phi_{1}(x_{1}),

(A7)

\displaystyle\phi_{2}^{\prime\prime}(x_{2})-\alpha^{2}P_{v}^{2}\,x_{2}^{2}\phi_{2}(x_{2})=(P_{u}P_{v}-\Delta)\phi_{2}(x_{2}),

(A8)

with

being the splitting constant. We now perform the following change of variables

y_{1}=\gamma\,x_{1},\quad y_{2}=\rho\,x_{2},

(A9)

\gamma=e^{-i\pi/4}\sqrt{2\alpha P_{v}},\quad\rho=\sqrt{2\alpha P_{v}},

(A10)

i\frac{P_{u}P_{v}+\Delta}{2\alpha P_{v}}=-\left(n_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\right),\quad\frac{P_{u}P_{v}-\Delta}{2\alpha P_{v}}=-\left(n_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right).

(A11)

This brings both equations in the form

(A12)

or

{\rm H}_{i}\phi_{i}=E_{i}\phi_{i},

(A13)

where

(A14)

which is the
equation for an harmonic oscillator.
Furthermore to ensure both boundary conditions

\displaystyle e^{i\alpha P_{v}x_{1}^{2}/2},\penalty\ \,\qquad x_{1}\to\pm\infty,

(A15)

\displaystyle e^{-\alpha P_{v}x_{2}^{2}/2},\qquad x_{2}\to\pm\infty,

(A16)

it is necessary to choose the decaying solution for both cases in the variables
y_{i}
, because

e^{-y_{1}^{2}/4}\to e^{-\gamma^{2}x_{1}^{2}/4}=e^{i\alpha P_{v}x_{1}^{2}/2},\qquad e^{-y_{2}^{2}/4}\to e^{-\rho^{2}x_{2}^{2}/4}=e^{-\alpha P_{v}x_{2}^{2}/2}.

(A17)

This condition also fixes the spectrum and the splitting constant
\Delta
. Considering

P_{u}=\alpha\left[i\left(n_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\right)-\left(n_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right],

(A18)

(A19)

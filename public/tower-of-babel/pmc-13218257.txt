Multichannel wavefront manipulation via wavelength-polarization multiplexed terahertz metadevice
Authors: Zhen Yue, Jitao Li, Chenglong Zheng, Qianyun Zhang, Mingyang Chen, Yun Zhang, ZiJie Dai, Xiaoxian Song, Jingjing Zhang, Zetao Xu, Yunxia Ye, Jianquan Yao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryAs the two-dimensional counterpart of metamaterials, metasurfaces have demonstrated exceptional potential in optics due to their remarkable ability to flexibly manipulate electromagnetic waves, offering a platform for multidimensional wavefront manipulation. However, existing multiplexing techniques predominantly focus on a single physical dimension, and multi-parameter joint multiplexing still faces challenges such as limited design degrees of freedom and channel crosstalk. This paper proposes a wavelength-polarization multiplexed terahertz metadevice based on anisotropic meta-atoms, which enables independent wavefront manipulation of three-band responses under orthogonal linear polarizations within a single-layer structure, thereby creating six fully decoupled transmission channels. We designed and numerically validated a dual-polarization triple-frequency decoupled metalens, a dual-polarization achromatic vortex beam generator, and a six-channel holographic display device. This research provides a compact and efficient solution for multidimensional optical field manipulation, with promising applications in terahertz communications, high-capacity information encryption, and holographic displays.

Introduction

The advent of metamaterials has ushered in a distinctive era for the scientific community, enabling the realization of exciting phenomena and novel devices not found in nature, such as negative refraction,1 invisibility cloaks,2 absorption,3 illusions,4 and perfect lenses.5 Metasurfaces, as the two-dimensional counterpart of metamaterials, significantly reduce the volumetric footprint while demonstrating an unprecedented capability to manipulate electromagnetic waves.6,7,8 By ingeniously tailoring the structural parameters at subwavelength scales, the optical response of each unit cell within a metasurface can be independently and fully controlled. Previous work has demonstrated that such artificial two-dimensional materials possess powerful functionalities for manipulating multiple parameters of electromagnetic waves, including amplitude,9 phase,10,11 frequency,12,13 and polarization.14,15,16 These characteristics endow metasurface devices with broad application prospects in fields, such as beam shaping,17,18 multidimensional optical imaging,19,20,21 information encryption,22,23,24,25 and holographic display.26,27,28,29

Polarization multiplexing is a major focus in optical beam multiplexing research because any arbitrary polarization state can be decomposed into a pair of fully decoupled orthogonal components.8,30,31,32,33,34,35 For instance, spin-decoupled metasurfaces enable independent wavefront manipulation for a set of orthogonal circularly polarized (CP) waves.30 Building upon this concept, a chip-integrated metalens architecture was proposed to simultaneously achieve efficient focusing, orthogonal polarization control, and vortex beam generation.31 These implementations require that the meta-atoms constructing the metasurface device satisfy the half-wave plate condition. In practical engineering applications, however, the response of a polarization device to CP wave incidence is non-ideal since it produces the desired cross-polarized component along with an undesired residual co-polarized component. To address this, researchers have proposed the full-phase modulated metasurface to achieve independent wavefront control of both the cross-polarized and co-polarized components under CP wave incidence. Based on such approach, several multi-channel polarization-multiplexed metalenses were designed and fabricated.8 Furthermore, linearly polarized (LP) waves are pivotal for unlocking the considerable potential of polarization imaging, attracting substantial research efforts toward the development of linear polarization multiplexing devices.36,37 Notably, researchers have proposed the integration of multiple LP manipulation channels with diverse spatial phase distributions into a single polarization device.36 Specifically, a single metasurface device can incorporate multiple independent target phase profiles with quantized phase relationships, where these phase distributions exhibit distinctly different information under varying polarization states. The introduction of these polarization multiplexing devices has significantly advanced the development of high-capacity information transmission systems.

On the other hand, wavelength multiplexing has been implemented on metasurfaces as a means of increasing information channel capacity. Design schemes for wavelength multiplexing primarily fall into two categories: spatially interleaving meta-atoms that are specifically designed to exhibit narrowband responses to different wavelengths,38,39 and utilizing the distinct dispersion characteristics exhibited by different wavelengths during diffraction processes.40 However, both methods are unable to enable meta-atoms at specific locations to independently manipulate light of different wavelengths, limiting the development of multifunctionality in polarization devices. Consequently, Ding et al. proposed encoding wavelengths into different polarization states to achieve wavelength multiplexing.41 Additionally, the spectral response of meta-atoms has been engineered to enable advanced optical devices, such as achromatic metalenses42 and metasurfaces with tailored dispersion.43

Despite these significant advances, related theories addressing wavelength-polarization joint multiplexing have rarely been reported.44,45 For instance, Li et al. proposed a polarization-frequency multiplexed terahertz metasurface enabled by vanadium dioxide, achieving six-channel wavefront manipulation for diversified vortex beam generation.44 However, most existing multiplexing strategies focus on a single physical dimension, rely on complex multilayer configurations, or introduce additional material systems that increase fabrication complexity. In contrast, we propose a paradigm for the design of a metadevice capable of wavelength-polarization multiplexing using a single-layer, all-dielectric architecture composed of three distinct types of meta-atoms. This approach not only simplifies the fabrication process but also ensures robust channel decoupling without the need for active tuning elements. Specifically, under illumination by a pair of orthogonal LP waves, the designed metadevice generates corresponding co-polarized waves whose phases can be independently manipulated. The wavefronts of the output beams can be simultaneously and independently manipulated at three distinct wavelengths, demonstrating the device’s ability to establish six fully independent transmission channels. To validate the flexibility and practicality of the proposed design, we developed a functional schematic of the wavelength-polarization multiplexed terahertz metadevice, as illustrated in Figure 1. Under x-polarized (x-P) incidence, the output waves at operating wavelengths λ1 and λ3 are focused at the same focal point, while the output at λ2 is directed to a distinct focal position. Under y-polarized (y-P) incidence, the outputs at λ1 and λ3 are simultaneously focused in a plane located at a distance f3 from the metadevice, whereas the output at λ2 is simultaneously focused at a separate plane positioned at a distance f4. Furthermore, leveraging this design framework, we demonstrated a dual-polarization achromatic vortex beam generator and a six-channel holographic display, both based on a terahertz metadevice. Simulation results are in excellent agreement with the intended designs. We anticipate that the multifunctionality and design flexibility of this metadevice platform will enable transformative advancements across various fields, including information encoding, optical data storage, and image display.Figure 1Operational principle of the designed wavelength-polarization multiplexed terahertz metadevice(A) Results under illumination by x-polarized (x-P) waves containing three distinct wavelengths.(B) Results under illumination by y-polarized (y-P) waves containing three distinct wavelengths.

Method

To achieve multi-channel wavefront manipulation through wavelength-polarization multiplexing, we firstly explore the interaction between anisotropic meta-atoms and incident waves. Typically, the relationship between the output wave (Eout) and the input wave (Ein) of a meta-atom can be described by a 2 × 2 Jones matrix (J) as follows: Eout = J·Ein. In the case of LP incident waves, the Jones matrix corresponding to the meta-atom can be expressed as:(Equation 1)J=[txxtxytyxtyy]=[Axx·eiφxxAxy·eiφxyAyx·eiφyxAyy·eiφyy],where tij, Aij, and φij represent the complex amplitude, absolute amplitude, and phase of the polarization conversion from incident j-polarized beam to output i-polarized beam, respectively. Assuming that the dielectric pillar of the meta-atom remains unrotated, the linear cross-polarization conversion channel will be suppressed, leading to a simplified Jones matrix of the form:(Equation 2)J=[taa00tbb]=[Aaa·eiφaa00Abb·eiφbb].

Here, a and b denote the local coordinates along the long and short axes of the dielectric pillar, respectively. As shown in Equation 2, the meta-atom supports two orthogonally polarized channels under LP incidence, each enabling independent phase manipulation. Building on this, we further explore independent phase control of the output wave for incident waves at different wavelengths. Specially, this study focuses on wavefront manipulation at three operational frequencies: 0.8, 1.0, and 1.2 THz (corresponding to wavelengths of approximately 375, 300 and 250 μm, respectively). Under LP excitation, the output field thereby comprises six distinct transmission channels. As illustrated in Figure 1, the proposed wavelength-polarization multiplexed terahertz metadevice consists of three types of meta-atoms that have a fixed orientation (i.e., they are not rotated). Figure 2A displays the building blocks of the metadevice, which include cylindrical, elliptical, and elliptical cross shaped meta-atoms. Each meta-atom is fabricated from high-resistivity silicon and designed with a height H = 300 μm and period P = 140 μm, arranged periodically on a silicon substrate with thickness T = 700 μm. High-resistivity silicon offers a high refractive index (n = 3.45) and low absorption loss in the terahertz band, making it widely suitable for metadevices in this frequency range. Numerical simulations were conducted using CST Studio Suite. Periodic boundary conditions were applied along the x- and y-directions, while open boundary conditions were set along the z axis. A time-domain solver was employed to compute the transmission properties of the meta-atoms under x- and y-P wave excitations. A systematic parametric sweep revealed that the electromagnetic response varies significantly with geometric parameters. For rectangular meta-atoms, the response depends on the length L and width W; for elliptical units, it is governed by the semi-major axis A and semi-minor axis B; and for the elliptical-cross-shaped type, the response is influenced by parameters A1, A2, and B (see Method S1 in the supplemental information for detailed electromagnetic responses). To reduce computational cost, parameter B is fixed at 10 μm for all elliptical-cross-shaped meta-atoms.Figure 2Schematic diagrams of meta-atoms and design procedure of the metadevice(A) Three types of meta-atoms.(B) Illustration of the design procedure for the wavelength-polarization multiplexed terahertz metadevice.

Then, we proceed to design and realize a wavelength-polarization multiplexed terahertz metadevice capable of independent wavefront manipulation across six channels, leveraging the previously established meta-atom database. As illustrated in Figure 2B, to achieve arbitrary desired wavefront control within each of the six channels, the ideal target phase profiles ψideal(x,y,λ) for both x- and y-P waves are computed at each specific wavelength. Here, (x,y) denotes the position of each meta-atom in the Cartesian coordinate system, and λ represents the operating wavelength. A global phase offset, defined as D = ψideal(x,y,λ)- ψactual(x,y,λ), is introduced, and an optimal value of this offset is determined by sampling within the range [0, π/4] to minimize the discrete phase error, where ψactual(x,y,λ) corresponds to the actual phase available in the database. Subsequently, a discrete particle swarm optimization (DPSO) algorithm is employed for meta-atom selection, where the velocity is updated as follows:(Equation 3)vi+1=w·vi+c1·r1·(pbesti−xi)+c2·r2·(gbesti−xi).

Among these, w represents the inertia weight, c1 and c2 are acceleration coefficients, r1 and r2 are random numbers, pbest denotes the particle’s individual historical best solution, gbest is the global best solution, while v and x represent the velocity and position of the particle, respectively. Meanwhile, owing to the discrete nature of the algorithm, the position update is also discretized:(Equation 4)xi+1=round(xi+vi+1).

Accordingly, the optimal meta-atom structure and corresponding geometric parameters at each position are determined harnessing the PSO algorithm described previously (see Method S6 in the supplemental information for detailed analysis). Finally, based on the optimization results, the meta-atoms are arranged to construct a wavelength-polarization multiplexed terahertz metadevice capable of delivering the desired functionalities.

Results and discussion

Dual-polarization triple-frequency decoupled metalens

This section presents the design methodology and characterizes the operational performance of the metalens illustrated in Figure 1. The device is designed to achieve distinct focal properties under orthogonal polarizations across multiple wavelengths. Specifically, under x-polarized illumination, the metalens is required to focus incident waves at frequencies of 0.8 THz (wavelength λ1 = 375 μm), 1.0 THz (λ2 = 300 μm), and 1.2 THz (λ3 = 250 μm) to focal points located at distances f1, f2, and f1 from the metasurface, respectively. Under y-polarized illumination at the same frequencies, the output waves must be focused at distances f4, f3, and f4, respectively. To realize this multifunctional performance, the target phase profile φpd(x,y) imposed on the metalens must simultaneously satisfy the following set of conditions for each polarization and wavelength combination:(Equation 5)φpd=−2πλd(x2+y2+fpd2−fpd).

Here, let p = x-P and y-P denote the polarization state of the incident wave, and d = 1, 2 and 3 represent the operating wavelengths of 375 μm, 300 μm, and 250 μm, respectively. The variable f denotes the focal length, with the following specific assignments: for p = x-P, fp1 = fp3 = f1 = 5 mm and fp2 = f2 = 7 mm; for p = y-P, fp1 = fp3 = f4 = 7 mm and fp2 = f3 = 5 mm. Following these phase requirements, a metasurface comprising 100 × 100 meta-atoms is designed. Dual-polarization achromatic vortex beam generator

To further validate the design flexibility of the proposed wavelength-polarization multiplexed terahertz metadevice capable of six-channel independent wavefront manipulation, we designed a dual-polarization achromatic vortex beam generator. Under x-P incidence, the device generates focused vortex beams carrying a topological charge of l = 1 at 0.8, 1.0, and 1.2 THz, with identical focal lengths across these frequencies. Under y-P incidence, the output waves form focused vortex beams with a topological charge of l = −1 at the same frequencies, also exhibiting consistent focal lengths. To achieve this functionality, the arrangement of meta-atoms must satisfy the following phase profile condition:(Equation 6)φij=−2πλj(x2+y2+fij2−fij)+li·θ.

Here, i = x- and y-P represents the polarization state of the incident wave, j = 1, 2, and 3 denotes the operating wavelengths of 375 μm, 300 μm, and 250 μm, respectively, and θ indicates the azimuthal angle of each meta-atom relative to the center of the metadevice. For i = x-P, the focal length is set as fij = 5 mm and the topological charge as li = 1; for i = y-P, fij = 7 mm and li = −1. Accordingly, a 100 × 100 array of meta-atoms is utilized as the fundamental building block to realize a dual-polarization achromatic vortex beam generator. This functionality is achieved consistently across the three operational frequencies: 0.8, 1.0, and 1.2 THz.

Six-channel holographic display device

Based on the design framework illustrated in Figure 7, a six-channel holographic display metadevice is designed. The device is intended to achieve the following performance under specified illumination conditions: under x-P wave incidence, the output wave is designed to generate holographic images “U”, “T”, and “H” at operating frequencies of 0.8, 1.0, and 1.2 THz, respectively, with all image planes located 10 mm away from the device; under y-P incidence, the output produces holographic images “1”, “2”, and “C” at the same frequencies, also with an image distance of 10 mm. To realize this functionality, the target phase profile for each meta-atom is first computed using the Gerchberg-Saxton (GS) algorithm. Subsequently, the DPSO algorithm is employed to select optimal meta-atom structures from the pre-established database, thereby constructing the final metadevice capable of delivering the desired multi-channel holographic responses.Figure 7Design framework for the six-channel terahertz metadevice holographic display

Design framework for the six-channel terahertz metadevice holographic display

It should be emphasized that the meta-atom phase distribution for generating the holographic images in Figure 7 is computed based on the Fresnel diffraction formula. The core of the method lies in first defining the target electric field distribution at a plane 10 mm away from the device and then performing a backpropagation calculation utilizing the Fresnel diffraction formula to determine the required phase profile at each spatial position of the metadevice. Subsequently, the plane defined by the actual phase profile of the meta-atoms is used as the initial plane for wavefront propagation. Using the Fresnel diffraction formula in MATLAB, the electric field intensity distribution of the output wave after a 10 mm propagation can be numerically simulated (see Figure 8). As shown in Figure 8A, under x-P wave excitation, the electric field distributions at 10 mm clearly reconstruct the target images “U”, “T”, and “H” at 0.8, 1.0, and 1.2 THz, respectively. Figure 8B presents the corresponding results under y-P illumination: at the same distance, the electric field distributions at the three frequencies successfully form the patterns “1”, “2”, and “C”.Figure 8Electric field intensity distributions on a plane located 10 mm away from the metadevice, calculated using Fresnel diffraction theory(A) Electric field intensity distribution under x-polarized incidence.(B) Electric field intensity distribution under y-polarized incidence.

Discussion

In this work, we have proposed and demonstrated a wavelength-polarization multiplexed terahertz metasurface based on anisotropic meta-atoms, capable of independent wavefront manipulation for three operational bands (0.8, 1.0, and 1.2 THz) under orthogonal linear polarizations within a single-layer architecture, thereby establishing six fully decoupled transmission channels. By systematically designing three types of meta-atoms with distinct geometries and employing the DSPO algorithm for unit selection and arrangement, high integration of multi-dimensional optical field control functionalities has been achieved. We have designed and numerically validated three functional devices: a dual-polarization tri-frequency decoupled metalens, a dual-polarization achromatic vortex beam generator, and a six-channel holographic display. The results demonstrate that the proposed metadevice performs the intended wavefront manipulation under various polarization and wavelength combinations with excellent channel isolation and control accuracy. The metalens achieves distinguishable focal length control across different frequencies and polarizations; the vortex beam generator produces orbital angular momentum beams with high mode purity; and the holographic display successfully reconstructs independent images in multiple channels, fully attesting to the robust multiplexing capability of the design methodology. The primary contribution of this study lies in providing a compact and efficient platform for multi-dimensional wavefront control, breaking through the design limitations of conventional meta-devices in joint wavelength-polarization multiplexing. Furthermore, the proposed design strategy is extendable to other frequency regimes (e.g., infrared and visible light) and provides a foundation for integration with tunable materials. Particularly, future work will explore the incorporation of phase-change materials (e.g., VO2 or GeSbTe) to achieve reconfigurable functionalities, and investigate the metadevice’s potential for dynamic beam steering and multi-channel multiplexing in next-generation terahertz communication systems.

Limitations of the study

Although this study presents progress in wavelength-polarization multiplexed wavefront manipulation based on a single-layer terahertz metasurface, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the metadevice performance is demonstrated only at three discrete frequencies, which may limit the generalizability to broadband or continuous-spectrum operation. Second, potential near-field coupling effects and inter-channel crosstalk were not exhaustively quantified across all polarization-frequency combinations. In addition, all validations were conducted numerically, and experimental verification in real-world environments is required. Future work will focus on the fabrication and experimental characterization of the proposed metadevices to evaluate their practical performance.

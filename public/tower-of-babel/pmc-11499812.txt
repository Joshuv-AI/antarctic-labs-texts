Traditional Chinese Medicine treatment for postoperative axial symptoms of cervical spondylotic myelopathy: a systematic review
Authors: Xuhao Liu, Xiaolong Yang, Yuanwei Dong, Tiannan Liao, Xin Gou, Jinwen Zou, Liangjuan Ren, Ling Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAxial symptoms (AS) represent one of the primary complications after cervical spondylotic myelopathy (CSM) surgery. Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), as a complementary and alternative therapy, is widely used in the treatment of postoperative AS in CSM. However, it lacks standardised treatment protocols, uniform therapeutic criteria, assessment methods and a sufficiently in-depth understanding of its mechanisms of action. These shortcomings impact the credibility of TCM treatment in clinical practice.Methods and analysisWe will conduct comprehensive searches, both manual and electronic, on literature published up to 31 July 2024, and database searches will commence after the publication of this agreement, with an estimated commencement date of 1 October 2024 and the end date of 31 March 2025, without language restrictions. Key databases such as MEDLINE, PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, WHO International Clinical Trial Registration Platform, China National Knowledge Network, China Biomedical Literature Database, China Scientific Journal Database and Wanfang Database will be explored. In addition, we will include resources such as library journals and conference abstracts. After identifying and screening all randomised controlled trials focused on TCM for postoperative AS of CSM, the two investigators will conduct a meta-analysis of the included studies. The results will be summarised as the risk ratio for binary data and the standardised or weighted average difference for continuous data.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval is not required since this review does not involve individual patient data. The review’s findings will provide clinicians with evidence on using TCM treatment for AS post-CSM surgery, disseminated through peer-reviewed publications or conferences.PROSPERO registration numberCRD42024505160.

Introduction

Description of the condition

Cervical spondylosis (CS) is one of the most prevalent musculoskeletal disorders, affecting approximately 30% to 50% of adults who experience neck pain every year.1 The condition has an average annual incidence of 37.2%,2 3 with general practitioners treating an average of seven patients per week.4 The global prevalence and incidence of CS, after age standardisation, are 3.55% and 0.81%, respectively.5 In individuals aged over 55 years, it is estimated that approximately 50% of MRI tests reveal imaging findings consistent with CS, and around 10% exhibit clinical symptoms of cervical spondylotic myelopathy (CSM).6 In the USA, the number of CS patients increased from 3.73 per 100 000 people in 1993 to 7.88 per 100 000 people in 2002, increasing twofold over this period. Additionally, the number of patients undergoing surgery surged by seven times.7 In China, the total incidence of CSM-related inpatients from 1998 to 2009 was 4.04 per 100 000 people.8 In the Netherlands, the rate of patients with CSM undergoing surgery is 1.6 per 100 000.9

CSM is a common neurodegenerative disorder prevalent in the middle-aged and elderly population.10 It is characterised by symptoms such as neuropathic pain, limb weakness, numbness, loss of hand dexterity, gait abnormalities and muscle spasms.11 Severe cases may lead to urinary and faecal incontinence, ultimately resulting in a decline in limb function and independence, adversely affecting the quality of life.12 Clarke believes that the majority of clinically diagnosed patients with CSM require surgical intervention.13 The surgical objectives include decompression of the spinal cord, restoration of cervical lordosis and reconstruction of spinal stability.14 Surgical approaches for CSM vary and can be classified into anterior cervical surgery, posterior cervical surgery and combined anterior–posterior approaches.15

Postoperative follow-ups for CSM reveal that some patients experience discomforts such as neck and shoulder pain, stiffness, soreness and heaviness. In severe cases, these symptoms can significantly impact the patient’s daily life. Kawaguchi et al defined such symptoms as axial symptoms (AS) in 1999.16 These symptoms have a high incidence and prolonged duration and pose challenges in treatment. Once they manifest, they can seriously affect the therapeutic outcomes of surgery.17 A considerable amount of clinical research indicates that the incidence of AS after posterior cervical spine surgery is approximately 37% to 80%,1821 while the occurrence rate of AS following anterior cervical spine surgery is around 20% to 40%.22 23 Therefore, AS represents one of the primary complications after cervical spine surgery.

Why it is important to perform this review

Currently, research on postoperative AS following CSM primarily focuses on understanding the pathogenesis, identifying risk factors and exploring preventive measures. Guilan and Alimohammadi believe that high preoperative anterior spinal canal occupation rate, facet joint damage during surgery, C7 spinous process muscle stop point damage, larger angle of laminar opening, greater postoperative cervical curvature loss and cervical range of motion loss are associated with an increased risk of developing AS.24 Regarding preventive measures, on the one hand, Ruan and Jiang believe that early functional exercise after CSM surgery can help reduce the occurrence of AS.25 On the other hand, He, Lv, et al propose that choosing appropriate modified surgical methods can minimise damage to local anatomical structures during surgery, thereby reducing the occurrence of AS after CSM surgery.26 27 Drug therapy (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, muscle relaxants), physical therapy and Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) treatment are currently the main methods for treating AS after CSM surgery in clinical practice.28 In China, electro-acupuncture, acupuncture, pestle needle, massage, moxibustion, TCM fumigation, Chinese herbal medicine, traditional exercises and other methods have been widely used in the treatment of AS after CSM surgery and have achieved good clinical efficacy.2933 Therefore, Liu et al believe that TCM has a good potential for application in the rehabilitation treatment of AS after CSM surgery.34 However, TCM still lacks standardised clinical treatment guidelines and evidence-based practices specific to postoperative AS after CSM surgery.

To address this gap, we propose a systematic review study to evaluate the effectiveness of TCM treatments for managing postoperative AS following CSM surgery. The objective is to derive reliable conclusions through a thorough evaluation of existing evidence, thereby laying the groundwork for developing evidence-based clinical guidelines for TCM in this area. Using the PICO framework, our research question is: ‘For patients with axial symptoms after cervical spondylotic myelopathy surgery (P), what is the clinical effectiveness and safety (O) of Traditional Chinese Medicine treatment (I) compared with conventional Western medical care (C)?’

Methods

Study registration and design

The systematic review protocol follows the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) standards for reporting systematic review protocols, as detailed in the PRISMA-P checklist.35 This protocol has been prospectively registered in PROSPERO under registration number (No: CRD42024505160). The review will be conducted according to the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions and will adhere to the research guidelines outlined in the PRISMA statement.36

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this research.

Research inclusion criteria

Types of studies

We will include randomised controlled trials (RCTs) investigating TCM interventions for postoperative AS in CSM. This will encompass abstract results from ongoing and completed trials published on clinical trial registration platforms. Eligible studies must provide detailed information on intervention measures, study outcomes and patient characteristics. These studies can be published in peer-reviewed journals, conference proceedings or academic papers, and there are no language restrictions on research reports.

Types of participants

Meeting the diagnostic criteria for postoperative AS in CS proposed by Takeuchi et al,18 individuals will be included regardless of their age, race or background. The diagnostic criteria include:

If a trial did not use the diagnostic criteria for postoperative AS in the CS proposed by Takeuchi et al, but specifies its diagnostic criteria, we will assess whether those criteria align with our inclusion standards. In cases where the information is insufficient, we will reach out to the authors for clarification. If clarification is not obtained, the trial will be excluded from consideration.

Types of interventions

All types of interventions for TCM will be considered, and interventions can include (but not be limited to) the following: acupuncture, electro-acupuncture, Chinese herbal treatment, moxibustion, Tai Ji, Qigong, Chinese herbal bath and massage. The minimum treatment duration for TCM in AS is set at 4 weeks.

All trials related to surgical treatment for AS, trials using Western medicine drugs (such as nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, opioid drugs, acetaminophen, antidepressants and muscle relaxants) to treat AS, trials involving physical therapy for AS and trials involving exercise rehabilitation training for AS will be excluded.

We plan to compare TCM treatment with a placebo control. The placebo control can include either a placebo drug or a sham intervention.

Types of outcome measures

Given the potential differences in the duration and nature of TCM treatment, the first outcome measure was evaluated after 1 week of TCM treatment, which we called the standardised assessment period for primary outcomes.

Primary outcome

Assessment of AS in the CS: The Tsuji scoring system from Keio University in Japan37 is employed to score four symptoms—posterior neck pain, posterior neck stiffness, shoulder pain and shoulder stiffness, with lower scores indicating more severe AS (refer to table 1).

Secondary outcomes

We will assess pain, function and quality of life using a range of instruments, including the visual analogue scale (VAS), the Japanese Orthopaedic Association (JOA) neck pain score, the Neck Disability Index (NDI) and the SF-36 Health Survey questionnaire. However, these specific instruments are examples, and studies using other validated measures of pain, function or quality of life will also be considered. Additionally, each study will report the proportion of follow-up recurrence and adverse events.

Search methods for identification of studies

Data sources

This systematic review aims to retrieve all RCTs of TCM treatments for postoperative AS in CSM up to 31 July 2024, and database searches will commence after the publication of this Agreement, with an estimated commencement date of 1 October 2024 and the end date of 31 March 2025. The search will include both electronic and manual methods, without restrictions on publication status or language. The following databases will be searched: PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, WHO International Clinical Trial Registration Platform, Traditional Chinese Medicine Database, China National Knowledge Network, China Biomedical Literature Database, China Scientific Journal Database and Wanfang Database. Additionally, other sources such as bibliographies of identified publications and meeting minutes will also be searched.

Search strategy

The search strategy will adhere to the PRISMA guidelines. The key search terms used in the strategy are (“axial symptoms” OR “postoperative axial symptoms” OR “post-surgical axial symptoms” OR “postoperative cervical symptoms” OR “surgical neck complications” OR “postoperative spinal symptoms” OR ‘“postoperative neck symptom”) AND (“acupuncture” OR “electro-acupuncture” OR “moxibustion” OR “massage” OR “Traditional Chinese Medicine” OR “Chinese herbal medicine” OR “Chinese herbal treatment” OR “Chinese herbal therapy”) AND (“cervical spondylotic myelopathy” OR “cervical myelopathy” OR “cervical spondylosis” OR “cervical spinal cord compression” OR “cervical cord dysfunction” OR “cervical spine myelopathic conditions” OR “cervical degenerative myelopathy”) AND (“randomised”). The search strategy will be adjusted according to the specific requirements of each database. The search strategy is presented in online supplemental appendix 1.

Data collection and analysis

Selection of studies

The EndNote software (V.X8) will be used for document management and retrieval. Two reviewers (XL and YD) will screen titles and abstracts based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria, evaluating the eligibility of potentially relevant studies’ full texts. Unclear studies will obtain complete copies for further assessment, and excluded literature will be documented with reasons. After preliminary screening, a comprehensive evaluation of included literature’s full texts will be conducted. The full-text screening process will also be performed in duplicate by these two reviewers to ensure accuracy and consistency. Reviewer disagreements will be resolved through discussion or consultation with a third reviewer (XG) if necessary. If needed, efforts will be made to contact trial authors for original data to resolve discrepancies. The main selection process will be presented in a PRISMA flow chart (figure 1).

Data extraction and management

The review team will collectively develop a data extraction table before data extraction. Two independent reviewers (XL and YD) will independently extract data from the included literature and fill in the data extraction table. The collected information will include basic details of the literature, trial features, participant characteristics, intervention measures, control methods, outcome measures, conclusions, adverse events, conflicts of interest, ethical approval and other relevant details. If the data in the literature are incomplete or unclear, the corresponding author will be contacted by email or phone to obtain additional information. Any disagreements between the two reviewers in data extraction will be resolved through discussion and negotiation to reach a consensus. If disagreements persist after discussion and negotiation, a third-party author (XG) will arbitrate to facilitate resolution.

Risk of bias assessments

LR and TL will use the Cochrane Collaboration’s risk of bias 2.0 (RoB 2.0) tool to evaluate experimental studies included in the literature.38 The assessment covered aspects such as sequence generation, allocation concealment, blinding of participants, implementers and outcome assessors, as well as incomplete outcome data, selective outcome reporting and other potential sources of bias. Following the evaluation, trials will be categorised based on low risk of bias (L), unclear risk of bias (U) and high risk of bias (H). In cases of disagreement between the authors, resolution will occur through discussion and negotiation, with possible arbitration by a third-party reviewer (XG) if necessary. If needed, attempts will be made to get it clarified with trial authors. The original text will be used when citing.

Measurements of treatment effect

In this study, the RevMan V.5.3 software will be used for data analysis and quantitative synthesis. Binary data will be analysed using the risk ratio (RR), with a 95% CI calculated. For continuous data without substantial heterogeneity (eg, Tsuji scoring, VAS, JOA, NDI and SF-36), if the measurement methods and units are consistent, the weighted mean difference (WMD) will be employed and a 95% CI will be calculated. If the measurement methods and units differ or if there is significant variation in mean values, the standard mean difference (SMD) will be used, with a calculation of 95% CI.

Unit of analysis issues

Our plan involves conducting a meta-analysis using data from studies by employing a parallel-group design. Only data from the initial phase will be included for randomised crossover trials. In these trials, participants are individually assigned to two intervention groups through random allocation, and individual measurements are collected and analysed for each participant.

Dealing with missing data

If any missing or incomplete data are identified in the included studies, we will make an effort to contact the authors or corresponding authors of the respective studies to obtain the missing raw data. If it is not possible to obtain the missing data, the study will be excluded from the analysis.

Assessment of heterogeneity

Higgins I2 will be used to conduct heterogeneity assessment tests, which quantify the inconsistency among included trials, and the values of 25%, 50% and 75% indicate low, moderate and high heterogeneity, respectively.39 When I2<50%, heterogeneity can be disregarded; when I2>50%, there is clinical, methodological or statistical heterogeneity among the trials, and a meta-regression analysis will be performed (using the Stata V.15.0 software) to identify the sources of heterogeneity. Subsequently, subgroup analyses will be conducted based on the identified sources. If significant statistical heterogeneity is observed, a random-effects model will be used for data synthesis, followed by sensitivity analysis to understand the impact of individual studies on the overall analysis. If substantial unexplained heterogeneity persists among trials, descriptive analysis will be undertaken.

Assessment of reporting biases

If more than 10 trials are included in the meta-analysis, a funnel plot will be employed to assess reporting bias. In the event of detecting asymmetry in the funnel plot, Egger’s test or Begg’s test will be used to analyse the potential reasons for the asymmetry.

Data synthesis

The results will be presented using RR for binary data and the WMD or SMD for continuous data. If I2<50%, a fixed-effects model will be employed for data synthesis. If 50% < I2 < 75%, a random-effects model will be used. If I2>75%, a thorough exploration of potential clinical and methodological factors will be conducted, and descriptive analysis or subgroup analysis will be provided.

Subgroup analysis

TCM encompasses a diverse array of treatment methods, making it a crucial source of heterogeneity in research. Therefore, we will initially calculate the overall effect size for all treatments. Subsequently, based on the diverse interventions in TCM, control groups and varying methodological quality, we will conduct subgroup analyses using a meta-regression approach to explore sources of heterogeneity.

Sensitivity analysis

Through this sensitivity analysis, we aim to evaluate the reliability of this systematic review to determine whether the overall results and conclusions are influenced by different criteria. First, studies with a high risk of bias and small sample trials (<100 participants) will be excluded to validate the consistency of conclusions under different conditions. Excluding small sample size studies is a common approach in sensitivity analyses to address concerns about the precision and reliability of the results, as supported by Higginsand Green, Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions V. 5.1.0.40 41 Subsequently, the impact of different statistical models (fixed-effects model and random-effects model) on the results will be explored.

Grading the quality of evidence

The quality of evidence will be evaluated using the Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluations (GRADE) criteria. This evaluation will consider factors such the as risk of bias, consistency, directness, accuracy and publication bias. Ratings will be assigned as high, moderate, low or very low, according to these criteria.

The GRADE assessments will be performed independently by two reviewers to ensure accuracy and reliability. Any discrepancies between the reviewers’ assessments will be resolved through discussion or consultation with a third reviewer if necessary. This process will help achieve consensus and maintain the integrity of the evaluation.

For detailed methodology, we will follow the GRADE methods and recommendations described in Chapters 14 and 15 of the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions.42 43

Ethics and Dissemination

Ethical approval is not required since this review does not involve individual patient data. The review’s findings will provide clinicians with evidence on using TCM treatment for AS post-CSM surgery, disseminated through peer-reviewed publications or conferences.

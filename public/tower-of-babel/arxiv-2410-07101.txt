On the K K -theory of the p p -adic unit disk
Elden Elmanto, Noah Riggenbach

Abstract
. In this note, we study the p p -complete topological cyclic homology of the affine line relative to a ring A A which is smooth over a perfectoid ring R R . Denoting by N ​ T ​ C ​ ( A , Z p ) NTC(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p}) the spectrum which measures the failure of 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -invariance on A A , we observe a kind of Quillen-Lichtenbaum phenomena for N ​ T ​ C ​ ( A , Z p ) NTC(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p}) — that it is isomorphic to its own K ⁡ ( 1 ) K(1) -localization in a specified range of degrees which depends on the relative dimension of A A . Somewhat surprisingly, this range is better than considerations following from a theorem of Bhatt-Mathew and étale-to-syntomic comparisons. Via the Dundas-Goodwillie-McCarthy theorem, we obtain a description of the algebraic K K -theory of p p -completed affine line over such rings.

Some

p
-torsion free rings

As stated in the introduction, our results on

, where
A

for some rather general

Towards this end let us recall some preliminaries. According to
[
4
]
a
p
-quasisyntomic ring

is said to be

-smooth
if two things happen: first that
{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{A}\{i\}
is Nygaard complete and the fibre of the map
\mathcal{N}^{i}{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{A}\rightarrow\overline{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{A}\{i\}
has
p
-complete tor-amplitude in degrees
\geq i+2
. The notion of
F
-smoothness is a non-noetherian extension of regularity as justified by
[
4
, Theorem 4.15]
; it is also partly inspired by connectivity bounds expected out of the Segal conjecture for
THH
.

The second author has proposed a definition for the dimension of an
F
-smooth ring which interacts well with TC-theoretic considerations. An
F
-smooth ring

has
Nygaard dimension
\leq n
(denoted by
\dim_{\mathcal{N}}A\leq n
)
[
25
, Definition 2.14]
if for all

, we have that

\mathcal{N}^{\geq i}{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{A}\in D^{[0,n]}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

The Quillen-Lichtenbaum estimates that we will produce will depend on the Nygaard dimension of
A
.

The last ingredient we need is a motivic filtration on
K(1)
-local
TC
constructed by H. Kim
[
17
]
. This motivic filtration behaves as follows: for any (animated) ring
A
the natural map
\mathrm{TC}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\rightarrow L_{K(1)}\mathrm{TC}(A)
lifts to a multiplicative, filtered map

F^{\star}_{\mathrm{mot}}\mathrm{TC}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\rightarrow F^{\star}_{\mathrm{mot}}L_{K(1)}\mathrm{TC}(A)

such that, after taking graded pieces, we obtain a multiplicative graded map

\mathbb{Z}_{p}(\star)^{\mathrm{syn}}(A^{\wedge}_{p})[2\star]\rightarrow R\Gamma_{{\acute{e}t}}(A^{\wedge}_{p}[\tfrac{1}{p}];\mathbb{Z}_{p}(\star))[2\star],

which agrees with the étale comparison map in prismatic theory by
[
17
, Corollary 3.36]
. Therefore, we may appeal to
[
4
, Theorem 1.8]
and note that for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and any
k\geq 1
the map on graded pieces

\mathrm{gr}^{n}_{\mathrm{mot}}\mathrm{TC}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})/p^{k}\rightarrow\mathrm{gr}^{n}_{\mathrm{mot}}L_{K(1)}\mathrm{TC}(A)/p^{k}

is
n+1
-truncated. Our argument follows that of
[
9
, Theorem 6.13]
closely.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
A
be a
p
-torsion free F-smooth quasisyntomic ring with finite
\mathcal{N}
-dimension
\dim_{\mathcal{N}}(A)=d
. Then the map

TC(A;\mathbb{F}_{p})\to L_{K(1)}TC(A)/p

is
d
-truncated.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the fibre of the functor
TC(-;\mathbb{F}_{p})\rightarrow L_{K(1)}TC(-)/p
. Noting that this is a morphism of localizing invariants, we see that it admits pushforward along proper morphisms of finite tor-amplitude.

This structure allows us to assume, without loss of generality, that
A
has a
p^{th}
root of unity since if
p=2
then
-1\in A
and if
p
is odd then
\mathcal{F}(A)
is a summand of
\mathcal{F}(A[\zeta_{p}])
for any
p
-complete functor
\mathcal{F}
with transfers (since we are working with mod-
p
coefficients and the extension is coprime to
p
). Consequently we may ignore Tate twists:
R\Gamma_{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(A[1/p];\mu_{p}^{\otimes n})\simeq R\Gamma_{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(A[1/p];\mathbb{F}_{p})
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. We then have that
\tau^{\leq n-1}R\Gamma_{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(A[1/p];\mathbb{F}_{p})\simeq\tau^{\leq n-1}\mathbb{F}_{p}(n)^{\mathrm{syn}}(A)
which does not have any cohomology above degree
d+1
by the assumption on Nygaard dimension. Since this is true for all
n
it follows that
R\Gamma_{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(A[1/p];\mathbb{F}_{p})
has cohomology in degrees
\leq d+1
.

It then follows that the map
\mathbb{F}_{p}(n)^{\mathrm{syn}}(A)\to R\Gamma_{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(A[1/p];\mu_{p}^{\otimes n})
is an equivalence when
n\geq d+2
. Inducing the motivic filtration on
\mathcal{F}
, we get that

\mathrm{gr}^{n}\mathcal{F}(A)\simeq\mathrm{fib}(\mathbb{F}_{p}(n)^{\mathrm{syn}}(A)\to R\Gamma_{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(A[1/p];\mu_{p}^{\otimes n}))[2n].

Consequently

\mathrm{gr}^{n}\mathcal{F}(A;\mathbb{Z}/p^{k})=0\qquad n\geq d+2,

and the highest nonzero homotopy group of
\mathcal{F}(A)
comes from

\displaystyle\mathrm{gr}^{d+1}\mathcal{F}(A;\mathbb{F}_{p})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle H^{d+1}_{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(A[1/p];\mu_{p}^{\otimes d+1})/H^{d+1}(\mathbb{F}_{p}(d+1)(A))[2(d+1)-(d+1)-1]

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle H^{d+1}_{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(A[1/p];\mu_{p}^{\otimes d+1})/H^{d+1}(A;\mathbb{F}_{p}(d+1))[d].

The shift that appears in the last term tells us that the
\mathcal{F}
has highest homotopy group in degree exactly
d
, whence the theorem is proved.
∎

3.1.
Coconnectivity of the Frobenius and the Segal conjecture

In this section we will show that, in certain cases, the Segal conjecture survives taking a polynomial extension. In other words, if the cyclotomic Frobenius is truncated for
\mathrm{THH}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})

\mathrm{THH}(A[t];\mathbb{Z}_{p})

\mathrm{THH}(A[t])\simeq\mathrm{THH}(A)\otimes\mathrm{THH}(\mathbb{S}[t])

[

As a

-equivariant spectrum we have an equivalence

\mathrm{THH}(\mathbb{S}[t])\simeq\mathbb{S}\oplus\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathbb{T}/C_{j}

and the Frobenius is given by the sum of the composites:

(3.3)

\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathbb{T}/C_{j}\simeq\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\left(\mathbb{T}/C_{pj}\right)^{C_{p}}\to\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\left(\mathbb{T}/C_{pj}\right)^{hC_{p}}\to\left(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathbb{T}/C_{pj}\right)^{hC_{p}}\to\left(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathbb{T}/C_{pj}\right)^{tC_{p}}\to\mathrm{THH}(\mathbb{S}[t])^{tC_{p}}.

Combining (

\mathrm{THH}(A[t])\simeq\mathrm{THH}(A)\oplus\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\mathrm{THH}(A)\otimes\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathbb{T}/C_{j}.

3.1

\mathrm{THH}(A[t];\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{tC_{p}}\simeq\mathrm{THH}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{tC_{p}}\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\left(\mathrm{THH}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\otimes\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathbb{T}/C_{j}\right)^{tC_{p}}\right)^{\wedge}_{(p,\xi)}

and on each summand of the source the Frobenius is given by

\mathrm{THH}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\otimes\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathbb{T}/C_{j}\xrightarrow{\phi_{p}\otimes id}\mathrm{THH}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{tC_{p}}\otimes\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathbb{T}/C_{j}

followed by an equivalence
\mathrm{THH}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{tC_{p}}\otimes\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathbb{T}/C_{j}\simeq\left(\mathrm{THH}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\otimes\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathbb{T}/C_{pj}\right)^{tC_{p}}
and then the inclusion of this factor into
\mathrm{THH}(A[t];\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{tC_{p}}
.

We claim that
\left(\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\left(\mathrm{THH}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\otimes\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathbb{T}/C_{j}\right)^{tC_{p}}\right)^{\wedge}_{p}
is already
\xi
-adically complete. For this it is enough to show that each
\left(\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\left(\mathrm{THH}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\otimes\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathbb{T}/C_{j}\right)^{tC_{p}}\right)/p^{n}
is already
\xi
-adically complete, but then by induction it is enough to show that
\left(\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\left(\mathrm{THH}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\otimes\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathbb{T}/C_{j}\right)^{tC_{p}}\right)/p
is
\xi
-adically complete. For this we have that
\phi(\xi):=\xi^{p}+p\delta(\xi)
and so being
\xi
-adially complete is equivalent to being
\phi(\xi)
-adically complete modulo
p
. In fact
\left(\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\left(\mathrm{THH}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\otimes\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathbb{T}/C_{j}\right)^{tC_{p}}\right)/p
is
\phi(\xi)
torsion as a
\mathrm{THH}(R;\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{tC_{p}}\simeq\mathrm{TP}(R;\mathbb{Z}_{p})/\phi(\xi)
-module by
[
6
, Proposition 6.4]
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the fiber of the Frobenius
\phi_{p}:\mathrm{THH}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to\mathrm{THH}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{tC_{p}}
. We then have that the fiber of the Frobenius
\phi_{p}:\mathrm{THH}(A[t];\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to\mathrm{THH}(A[t];\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{tC_{p}}
is given by the
p
-adic completion of the spectrum

\mathcal{F}\oplus\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\left(\mathcal{F}\otimes\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathbb{T}/C_{j}\right)

up to equivalence, because we know that the Frobenius is computed termwise by the commutativity of (
3.4
). By assumption this will not have homotopy above degree
k+1
, and in degree
k+1
it will have bounded
p^{\infty}
torsion. Consequently the
p
-completion will also not have homotopy above degree
k+1
as desired. The statement mod
p
is similar.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The key point of Proposition

, as already observed in
[

]
, is that the

-completion of the big direct sum is
\xi
-adically complete. This lets us compute the fibre on polynomial extensions without additional

-completion, leading to stability of the Segal conjecture under poiynomial extensions.

One consequence of this is the following result.

Corollary 3.4
.

For each

and perfectoid ring

, the Frobenius

is
(d-3)
-truncated. If
R
is
p
-torsion free then the Frobenius mod
p
is still
(d-3)
-truncated.

Proof.

The base case

is
[
6
, Proposition 6.4]
. The induction step is given by the above.
∎

4.
Quillen-Lichtenbaum for NTC

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
. We outline the required steps:

(1)

we prove the result for polynomial rings in equicharacteristic
p
in Lemma
4.1
;

(2)

from which we can deduce the result for polynomial rings over torsion free perfectoid rings in Corollary
4.4
.

(3)

Excision then lets us boostrap the result for polynomial rings over an arbitrary perfectoid ring in Lemma
4.5
;

(4)

Finally we prove the main result in Theorem
4.7
, relying on an étale base change result for the Tate construction in Lemma

.

4.1.
The case of polynomial algebras

First, we work with polynomial algebras over a perfect ring of characteristic
p>0
. We note that for any
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
R
we have that
L_{K(1)}TC(R)\simeq 0
. One way to see this is to observe that
L_{K(1)}TC(R)
is a module over
K(\mathbb{F}_{p};\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and therefore has vanishing
K(1)
-localization.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
R
be a perfect
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra. Then
N\mathrm{TC}_{i}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}])
vanishes for
i\geq d
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
we have that

N\mathrm{TC}^{-}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}])\simeq\Sigma\left(\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\mathrm{THH}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}])^{hC_{j}}\right)^{\wedge}_{(p,u)}

and

N\mathrm{TP}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}])\simeq\Sigma\left(\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\mathrm{THH}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}])^{tC_{j}}\right)^{\wedge}_{(p,\xi)}

where we are replacing
\left(X\otimes(\mathbb{T}/C_{j})_{+}\right)^{h\mathbb{T}}
with
\Sigma X^{hC_{j}}
using the Wirthmüller isomorphism. By
[
24
, Lemma 4.5]
we may replace the
u
-adic completion on the topological negative cyclic homology with the
\xi
-adic filtration, and since
R
is a perfect
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra this amounts to only taking the
p
-adic completion. Thus it is enough to show that the map

\Sigma(\phi_{p}[p]-can):\Sigma\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\mathrm{THH}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}])^{hC_{j}}\to\Sigma\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\mathrm{THH}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}])^{tC_{j}}

is an equivalence in degrees above
d-1
and injective in degree
d-1
. Since
\phi_{p}
multiplies the summand index by
p
and
can
preserves it we have for degree reasons that this map is injective, respectively surjective, whenever
\phi_{p}
is by Lemma
4.2
. Since
\phi_{p}
is
(d-3)
-truncated by Proposition
3.2
the result follows.
∎

We will make use of the following statement in several places going forward, so we include here a proof for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{H_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be two
\mathbb{N}
-indexed families of abelian groups,
f_{i}:G_{i}\to H_{i}
and
g_{i}:G_{i}\to H_{i+1}
be
\mathbb{N}
-indexed group homomorphisms. Suppose that
H_{0}=0
. Define
h:\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}}G_{i}\to\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}}H_{i}
to be the map
g_{i}-f_{i}
on the summand
G_{i}
. Then
h
is injective/surjective/bijective if
g_{i}
is for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

First suppose that
g_{i}
is injective for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
(x_{1},\ldots)\in\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}}G_{i}
be an element of the kernel of
h
. Since we are working with the direct sum, unless
(x_{1},\ldots)=0
there is some
N
large enough so that
x_{N}\neq 0
but
x_{K}=0
for all
K>N
. Note then that
h(x_{1},\ldots)_{N+1}=g_{N}(x_{N})-f_{N+1}(x_{N+1})=g(x_{N})\neq 0
since
g_{N}
is injective. Hence
(x_{1},\ldots)=0
and
h
is injective as desired.

Now suppose that
g_{i}
is surjective for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
and let
(y_{1},\ldots)\in\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}}H_{i}
. Since we are again working with the direct sum, either
(y_{1},\ldots)=0
which is clearly in the image or there is some
N
large enough so that
y_{N}\neq 0
and
y_{K}=0
for all
K>N
. We will define a pre-image
(x_{1},\ldots)
of
(y_{1},\ldots)
inductively starting with
x_{K}=0
for all
K\geq N
and
x_{N-1}
any pre-image of
y_{N}
under
g_{N-1}
. Then define
x_{N-2}=\tilde{y}_{N-1}+\widetilde{f_{N-1}(x_{N-1})}
where the
\tilde{(-)}
denotes choosing a pre-image under
g_{N-2}
. Inductively we can always correct the error introduced by
f_{i}
in this fashion.
∎

We now turn our attention to the case when
R
is
p
-torsion free. In this case we still have the computations

N\mathrm{TC}^{-}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})\simeq\Sigma\left(\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\mathrm{THH}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})^{hC_{j}}\right)^{\wedge}_{\xi}

and

N\mathrm{TP}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})\simeq\Sigma\left(\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\mathrm{THH}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})^{tC_{j}}\right)^{\wedge}_{\xi}

by combining Lemma
3.1
and
[
24
, Lemma 4.5]
. A similar argument as in Lemma
4.1
together with the fact that
R
being
p
-torsion free inductively shows that
\mathrm{THH}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is
p
-torsion free. In turn, this implies that the induced map

\Sigma\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\mathrm{THH}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})^{hC_{j}}\to\Sigma\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\mathrm{THH}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})^{tC_{j}}

is
(d-2)
-truncated. If
\phi_{p}-can
was
\xi
-linear this would then imply that we had the same style of vanishing as in Lemma
4.1
but we know from
[
24
, Theorem 1.2]
that this does not happen.

In order to account for the effect that the
\xi
-adic completion has on the final answer we first restrict our attention to
R
which have a compatible system of
p^{th}
power roots of unity. In other words we are considering
R
which are
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{cycl}:=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\zeta_{p^{\infty}}]^{\wedge}_{p}
-algebras. Recall the notation
\epsilon:=(1,\zeta_{p},\zeta_{p^{2}},\ldots)\in R^{\flat}
. From
[
5
, Example 3.16]
we have that
1+[\epsilon^{{1}/{p}}]+[\epsilon^{{1}/{p}}]^{2}+\ldots+[\epsilon^{{1}/{p}}]^{p-1}
is an orientation of
R
where
[-]:R^{\flat}\to A_{inf}(R)
is the Teichmüller lift; in other words this is the (Frobenius inverse) of the orientation for the prism corresponding to the perfectoid ring
R
and we call it
\xi
. If we let
\mu:=[\epsilon]-1
we then also have that
\tilde{\xi}=\phi(\mu)/\mu
and that
\xi=\mu/\phi^{-1}(\mu)
by
[
5
, Proposition 3.17]
. Then
\phi^{-1}(\mu)u\in\mathrm{TC}^{-}_{2}(R;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is such that
can(\phi^{-1}(\mu)u)=\xi\phi^{-1}(\mu)\sigma=\mu\sigma=\phi_{p}(\phi^{-1}(\mu)u)
in
\mathrm{TP}_{2}(R;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
by
[
6
, Proposition 6.2 and 6.3]
. In particular by
[
22
]
there exists a unique element
\beta\in\mathrm{TC}_{2}(R;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
lifting
\phi^{-1}(\mu)u
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
R
be a
p
-torsion free perfectoid
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{cycl}
-algebra. Then the map

N\mathrm{TC}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})\to N\mathrm{TC}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})[1/\beta]

is
(d-2)
-truncated.

Proof.

Base changing the fiber sequence giving
N\mathrm{TC}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})
shows that the fiber of the map in question is the total fiber of the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{N\mathrm{TC}^{-}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{N\mathrm{TP}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{N\mathrm{TC}^{-}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})[1/\beta]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{N\mathrm{TP}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})[1/\beta]}}

and by a similar argument as in
[
24
, Lemma 4.13]
we may replace inverting
\beta
with inverting
\xi
.

In order to compute the total fiber of the above square we will first compute the vertical fibers. To do this first note that
\mathrm{THH}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})^{hC_{j}}
is a module over
\mathrm{THH}(R;\mathbb{F}_{p})^{hC_{j}}
and similarly
\mathrm{THH}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})^{tC_{j}}
is a module
\mathrm{THH}(R;\mathbb{F}_{p})^{tC_{j}}
. In particular since both
\mathrm{THH}(R;\mathbb{F}_{p})^{hC_{j}}
and
\mathrm{THH}(R;\mathbb{F}_{p})^{tC_{j}}
are
\xi
-power torsion so are
\mathrm{THH}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})^{hC_{j}}
and
\mathrm{THH}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})^{tC_{j}}
. Consequently

\left(\Sigma\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\mathrm{THH}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})^{hC_{j}}\right)[1/\xi]\simeq 0\simeq\left(\Sigma\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\mathrm{THH}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})^{tC_{j}}\right)[1/\xi]

since inverting elements will commute with direct sums and shifts.

Thus by an arithmetic fracture square argument the vertical fibers of the square in question are

\Sigma\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\mathrm{THH}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})^{hC_{j}}\to\Sigma\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\mathrm{THH}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})^{tC_{j}}

which we have already shown to be
(d-2)
-truncated in the paragraph preceding this Lemma.
∎

We are now able to prove the main result for polynomial rings over a
p
-torsion free perfectoid ring.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
R
be a
p
-torsion free perfectoid ring. Then the map

\mathrm{TC}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})\to L_{K(1)}\mathrm{TC}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})

is
\max\{-1,(d-3)\}
-truncated.

Proof.

Note that this statement is fpqc local in
R
since
\mathrm{TC}(-[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})
satisfies fpqc descent and
L_{K(1)}(-)
kills bounded above spectra. In particular we may assume without loss of generality that
R
has a consistent system of
p^{th}
power roots of unity. See the first paragraph of
[
19
, Proposition 5.10]
for the detail of this argument.

We will prove this statement by induction on
d
. For
d=1
note that by
[
19
, Proposition 5.10]
we have that
\mathrm{TC}(R;\mathbb{F}_{p})\to L_{K(1)}\mathrm{TC}(R;\mathbb{F}_{p})
is
(-1)
-truncated. Thus for
\mathrm{TC}(R[t];\mathbb{F}_{p})\to L_{K(1)}\mathrm{TC}(R[t];\mathbb{F}_{p})
to be
d-2=-1
-truncated it is enough to show that
N\mathrm{TC}(R;\mathbb{F}_{p})\to L_{K(1)}N\mathrm{TC}(R)
is
(-1)
-truncated. This is a consequence of
[
24
, Proposition 4.14]
where we identify
N\mathrm{TC}(R;\mathbb{F}_{p})[1/\beta]\simeq L_{K(1)}N\mathrm{TC}(R;\mathbb{F}_{p})
by
[
16
, Theorem 1.3.6, Lemma 1.3.7, and Corollary 1.3.8]
.

Now suppose that for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathrm{TC}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})\to L_{K(1)}\mathrm{TC}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})

\max\{-1,(d-3)\}
-truncated. Then we have that

\mathrm{TC}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d+1}];\mathbb{F}_{p})\simeq\mathrm{TC}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})\oplus N\mathrm{TC}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}];\mathbb{F}_{p})

and so in order to show that the map

\mathrm{TC}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d+1}];\mathbb{F}_{p})\to L_{K(1)}\mathrm{TC}(R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d+1}];\mathbb{F}_{p})

is
\max\{-1,d-2\}
-truncated it is enough to show this on each of the summands. The first summand is the induction hypothesis and the second summand is handled in Lemma
4.3
.
∎

We are now ready to prove the polynomial case for a general perfectoid ring

.

Let

be a perfectoid ring. Then the map

is
\max\{0,d-1\}
-truncated and an isomorphism in degree
\max\{1,d-1\}
.

Proof.

In

, Lemma 7.19]
it is shown that every perfectoid ring

fits into a square

4.2.
Bootstrap to smooth schemes

In this section we will prove the main results. This will follow the proofs of the polynomial cases. We will begin by showing the result in positive characteristic.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
X
be a quasi-compact smooth
k
-scheme of relative dimension
d
,
k
a perfect
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra. Then
NTC_{i}(X)=0
for
i\geq d
.

Proof.

Since
X
is smooth over
k
, each point
x\in X
admits an open neighborhood
U\subseteq X
and an étale morphism
U\to\mathrm{Spec}(k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}])
over
\mathrm{Spec}(k)
. It is then enough to show that each of these schemes
U
has the desired vanishing property since
NTC
is a Zariski sheaf. Note also that we may assume without loss of generality that the
U
are affine.

For a scheme
X
, denote by
\mathcal{F}(X)
the fiber of the cyclotomic Frobenius
\phi_{p}:\mathrm{THH}(X)\to\mathrm{THH}(X)^{tC_{p}}
. By the same argument as in Lemma
4.3
and Proposition
3.2
, it is enough to show that
\tau_{\geq d-2}\mathcal{F}(U)=0
and we have already shown that
\tau_{\geq d-2}\mathcal{F}(k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}])=0
. The result will follow from the fact that
\mathcal{F}(-)
is a coherent étale sheaf, in the sense that

\mathcal{F}(U)\simeq U\otimes_{k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]}\mathcal{F}(k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]).

Coherent étale sheaves are closed under cofibers, so it is enough to show that this is true for
\mathrm{THH}(-)
and
\mathrm{THH}(-)^{tC_{p}}
. That
\mathrm{THH}(-)
is a coherent sheaf is by
[
12
, Lemma 2.4.2]
. Let
f:A\to B
be an étale extension of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebras. It is then enough to show that the map
B\otimes_{A}\mathrm{THH}(A)^{tC_{p}}\to\mathrm{THH}(B)^{tC_{p}}
is an equivalence, where the
A
and
B
module structures come from the ring map
\mathrm{THH}(-)\xrightarrow{\phi_{p}}\mathrm{THH}(-)^{tC_{p}}
. Since we will need this result again in a slightly more general context we will prove this separately in Lemma
4.7
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
4.6
makes use of the following observation, the importance of which the authors learned from Lars Hesselholt; see
[
13
, Propositions 6.2-6.4]
for this argument in a different context.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
f:A\to B
be an étale map of smooth
R
-algebras,
R
a perfectoid ring. Then the natural map

B\otimes_{A}\mathrm{THH}(A)^{tC_{p}}\to\mathrm{THH}(B)^{tC_{p}}

is an equivalence.

Proof.

In this context, let us recall from
[
6

that there is a motivic filtration
\mathrm{Fil}^{\star}_{\mathrm{mot}}\mathrm{THH}(A)^{tC_{p}}
whose graded pieces are given by
\overline{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{A/R}[2i]
(ignoring Breul-Kisin twists in the perfectoid setting). Since
B\otimes_{A}(-)
preserves connectivity and
\overline{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{A/R}
has no cohomology above the relative dimension of
A
over
R
, we have that the motivic filtrations on both the source and target are complete and it is enough to check this isomorphism on associated graded terms. The Breuil-Kisin twists are trivial since we are working over a perfectoid ring and therefore we reduce to showing that
B\otimes_{A}\overline{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{A/R}\to\overline{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{B/R}
is an equivalence which can be proved by reducing to a similar base change formula for the cotangent complex
[
27

or an appeal to
[
8
, Lemma 4.21]
which uses the site-theoretic formalism. Note that the
A

is the correct one to apply this result by
[
8
, Theorem 1.14(2)]
.
∎

We now turn to the

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
X
be a quasi-compact smooth scheme over
\mathrm{Spec}(R)
,
R
a
p
-torsion free perfectoid ring. Then the map

NTC(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})\to L_{K(1)}NTC(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})

is
\max\{-1,(d-2)\}
-truncated.

Proof.

We will proceed in a very similar fashion as in the positive characteristic case. Note that since
L_{K(1)}-
kills bounded above spectra, we get that
L_{K(1)}NTC(-;\mathbb{F}_{p})
is still a Zariski sheaf. Thus again by quasi-compactness and smoothness we may reduce to the case of
U\to\mathrm{Spec}(R[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}])
an étale cover with
U\cong\mathrm{Spec}(A)
. By fpqc descent we may also assume without loss of generality that
R
contains a compatible system of
p^{th}
-power roots of unity.

Since we have a compatible system of
p^{th}
power roots of unity in
R
, the same argument as in Corollary
4.4
shows that the fiber of the map
NTC(U;\mathbb{F}_{p})\to L_{K(1)}NTC(U;\mathbb{F}_{p})
is given by the fiber of the map

\Sigma\left(\bigoplus_{i\geq 1}\mathrm{THH}(A;\mathbb{F}_{p})^{hC_{i}}\right)\to\Sigma\left(\bigoplus_{i\geq 1}\mathrm{THH}(A;\mathbb{F}_{p})^{tC_{i}}\right)

with the map given by
\Sigma(\phi_{p}^{hC_{i}}[p]-can^{hC_{i}})
. By Lemma
4.2
, this map will be (injection / surjection / isomorphism) on
\pi_{*}
provided that
\phi_{p}^{hC_{i}}:\mathrm{THH}(A;\mathbb{F}_{p})^{hC_{i}}\to\mathrm{THH}(A;\mathbb{F}_{p})^{tC_{pi}}
is an (injection / surjection / isomorphism) on
\pi_{*-1}
for all

. Since homotopy orbits preserves coconnectivity of maps it is then enough to show that
\phi_{p}:\mathrm{THH}(A;\mathbb{F}_{p})\to\mathrm{THH}(A;\mathbb{F}_{p})^{tC_{p}}
has fiber concentrated in degrees
\leq d-3
. This either follows by a similar analysis as in Theorem
4.6
or by using
[

.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem

, which we recall the statement here for convinience.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
X

is an
(d-1)
-truncated and an isomorphism in degree

.

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
4.8
, we may reduce to the case of

where

is a smooth
R

R\to R[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]\xrightarrow{f}A

Let

denote the perfecotid ring

, so that as in Lemma

we have a Milnor square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R_{0}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(R_{0}/p)_{perf}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\prime}_{0}}

By

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces NTC(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})}

We now turn our attention to the

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
A
be the
p
-completion of a smooth
R
-algebra,

a perfectoid ring. Suppose that

has relative dimension

-truncated and an equivalence in degree

Contemplate the following commutative cube

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{\geq 0}K(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces TC(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{\geq 0}K(A\langle t\rangle;\mathbb{Z}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces TC(A\langle t\rangle;\mathbb{Z}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(A/\sqrt{(p)};\mathbb{Z}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces TC(A/\sqrt{(p)};\mathbb{Z}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(A/\sqrt{(p)}[t];\mathbb{Z}_{p})}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

where

denotes the radical of the ideal

. The front and back faces are pullback square by

In this Section we will demonstrate the utility of our results by studying the case of curves over perfectoid rings. We will first consider the characteristic
p
case, where we can prove a vanishing result not just for the algebraic
K
-groups but also on the level of syntomic cohomology.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
C
be a smooth curve over
k
, where
k
is a perfect
\mathbb{F}_{p}
algebra. Then
N\mathbb{Z}_{p}(2)^{syn}(C)=0
.

Proof.

Since
N\mathbb{Z}_{p}(2)^{syn}
is a Zariski sheaf we may assume without loss of generality that
C
is affine. It then follows that
N\mathbb{Z}_{p}(2)^{syn}(C)
is concentrated in degrees
[0,3]
. Since
C
is smooth it also follows that
N\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)(C)=0
for all
i\geq 3
. The only possible differential in the spectral spectral sequence
[
6
, Theorem 1.12(5)]
is the differential
H^{0}(N\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1)^{syn}(C))\to H^{3}(N\mathbb{Z}_{p}(2)^{syn}(C))
. Again since
C
is smooth
H^{0}(N\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1)^{syn}(C))=0
, so there are no differentials and each group
NTC_{*}(C)
is given by an extension of the groups
H^{i}(N\mathbb{Z}_{p}(j)(C))
where
2j-i=*
,
0\leq j\leq 2
, and
0\leq i\leq 3
. Thus the cohomology of
N\mathbb{Z}_{p}(2)^{syn}(C)
contributes to
\tau_{\geq 1}NTC(C)
, but this vanishes by Theorem
4.6
.
∎

Consider now
C
the
p
-adic completion of a smooth curve over
R
, where
R
is a general perfectoid ring. We then have that
\tau_{\geq 1}K(C\langle t\rangle,(t);\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\tau_{\geq 1}NTC(C;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\tau_{\geq 1}L_{K(1)}NTC(C;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
by Theorem
1.1
. In the case of
R
a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{cycl}
algebra we may then completely describe the
NK
groups of
C
in terms of more classical invariants.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
C
be the
p
-adic completion of a smooth affine curve over
R
, where
R
is a perfectoid
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{cycl}
-algebra. Then there are isomorphisms

K_{2i-1}(C\langle t\rangle,(t);\mathbb{Z}_{p})\cong H^{0}(C\langle t\rangle;(\mathbb{G}_{m})^{\wedge}_{p})/H^{0}(C;(\mathbb{G}_{m})^{\wedge}_{p})

and

K_{2i}(C\langle t\rangle,(t);\mathbb{Z}_{p})\cong H^{2}(C;N\mathbb{Z}_{p}(2)^{syn})

for all
i\geq 1
.

Proof.

By the discussion preceding the theorem statement we have that
\tau_{\geq 1}K(C\langle t\rangle,(t);\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is the
1
-connective cover of a
L_{K(1)}K(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{cycl})
-algebra, and therefore is
2
-periodic. Thus to prove the result we need to show that the computation works for
i=1
. Since
C
is the
p
-adic completion of a smooth affine curve,
\dim_{\mathcal{N}}(C\langle t\rangle)\leq 2
by the Hodge-Tate comparison theorem. Therefore we have that the complexes
N\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)(C)
have cohomology in degree at most
3
. Thus the only groups that can contribute to
NTC_{1}(C;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
are
H^{1}(C;N\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1)^{syn})
and
H^{3}(C;N\mathbb{Z}_{p}(2)^{syn})
. By
[
2
, Theorem 5.2]
we have that
H^{3}(C;N\mathbb{Z}_{p}(2)^{syn})\to H^{3}((C/\sqrt{p});N\mathbb{Z}_{p}(2)^{syn})
is an isomorphism, and the target vanishes by Lemma
5.2
. Thus the only group which can contribute to
NTC_{1}(C;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is
H^{1}(C;N\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1)^{syn})\cong H^{0}(C\langle t\rangle;(\mathbb{G}_{m})^{\wedge}_{p})/H^{0}(C;(\mathbb{G}_{m})^{\wedge}_{p})
and by using the inclusion map
B\mathbb{G}_{m}\to K(-)
and the determinant map
K(-)\to B\mathbb{G}_{m}(-)
we have that this group does contribute to
NTC_{1}
. Therefore

K_{1}(C\langle t\rangle,(t);\mathbb{Z}_{p})\cong H^{0}(C\langle t\rangle;(\mathbb{G}_{m})^{\wedge}_{p})/H^{0}(C;(\mathbb{G}_{m})^{\wedge}_{p})

as desired.

We now turn our attention to computing
K_{2}(C\langle t\rangle,(t);\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. The syntomic cohomology groups which can contribute to this group are
H^{0}(C;N\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1)^{syn})
and
H^{2}(C;N\mathbb{Z}_{p}(2)^{syn})
when
C
is affine. Since
C
is smooth we have that
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1)^{syn}\simeq(\mathbb{G}_{m})^{\wedge}_{p}[-1]
and so
H^{0}(C;N\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1)^{syn})\cong NH^{-1}(C;(\mathbb{G}_{m})^{\wedge}_{p})\cong 0
since any

-torsion in
\mathbb{G}_{m}(C\langle t\rangle)
must already be in
\mathbb{G}_{m}(C)
. The only possible group which can contribute to
K_{2}(C\langle t\rangle,(t);\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is therefore
H^{2}(C;N\mathbb{Z}_{p}(2)^{syn})
. This group cannot support or receive any nonzero differentials for degree reasons, so we get the desired isomorphism.
∎

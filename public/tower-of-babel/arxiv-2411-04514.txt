Abstract
. For a commutative noetherian ring, we classify all the hereditary cotorsion pairs cogenerated by pure-injective modules of finite injective dimension. The classification is done in terms of integer-valued functions on the spectrum of the ring. Each such function gives rise to local depth conditions which describe the left-hand class in the corresponding cotorsion pair. Furthermore, we show that these cotorsion pairs correspond via an explicit duality to hereditary 𝖳𝗈𝗋 \Tor -pairs generated by modules of finite flat dimension.

1.
Introduction

There have been many successful classification theorems of pure-injective modules over specific rings. However, pure-injective modules over even a well-understood ring, such as a noetherian ring of global dimension greater than one, can have pathological behaviour
[
31
]
. Given that describing them explicitly can be a daunting task, another approach is instead to associate a class of modules to a given collection of pure-injective modules. In this paper, we consider the left-orthogonal with respect to the
\Ext
functor, in particular over commutative noetherian rings.

These classes have several appealing properties. They are known to be closed under direct limits, as well as having good approximation properties; specifically, they are covering classes. In other terminology, these classes are the left constituents of perfect complete cotorsion pairs, a non-trivial consequence of being cogenerated by pure-injective modules, a result which was used to prove affirmatively the long-standing question of whether flat covers necessarily exist
[
9
]
.

Important examples of classes that are
\Ext
-orthogonal to a collection of pure-injective modules are the classes appearing in a
\Tor
-pair.
\Tor
-pairs were introduced as an analogue of cotorsion pairs, that is, pairs of classes which are orthogonal with respect to the
\Tor
functor instead of the
\Ext
functor. Via a Tor-Ext duality induced by the
\mathrm{Hom}
-
\otimes
adjunction, the two classes in a
\Tor
-pair arise as a left-hand class of a complete cotorsion pair cogenerated by the character dual of a module, which is always pure-injective.

Going back to general cotorsion pairs cogenerated by pure-injective modules, we recall that if there is a bound on the injective dimensions of the pure-injective modules and, additionally, one imposes the condition that the left-hand class of the cotorsion pairs is definable, that is, in particular closed under arbitrary products, then these cotorsion pairs are exactly the cotilting cotorsion pairs, see
Proposition

2.7
.
Such cotorsion pairs over commutative noetherian rings
[
1
]
, and even over certain general commutative rings,
[
27
,
26
,
7
,
6
]
have been classified in terms of various ring-theoretic data. In particular, the cotilting cotorsion pairs of cofinite type, which are the ones that appear as a
\Tor
-orthogonal class to a family of strongly finitely presented modules of finite projective dimension, are classified using the spectrum of the ring or, more specifically, in terms of Thomason subsets. Over commutative noetherian rings, all cotilting classes are of cofinite type
[
1
]
. It is also interesting to note that in the characterisations in
[
1
,
27
,
26
]
, the Koszul complex with respect to a finitely generated ideal plays a pivotal role.

In this paper, we are principally interested in commutative noetherian rings. In Theorem
4.17
, we show that the cotorsion pairs cogenerated by pure-injective modules of finite injective dimension are classified by functions from the spectrum to the non-negative integers, which are bounded by the local depth of the prime. The left-hand constituent of the associated cotorsion pair consists precisely of those modules whose local depths are bounded by the corresponding function. As we detail in Corollary
4.20
, our results nicely extend the classification of cotilting cotorsion pairs in the commutative noetherian case, which corresponds to those functions which are in addition order-preserving. We remark that in the follow-up work
[
20
]
, we extend this result to the derived category of quasi-coherent sheaves over a noetherian scheme, where the notion of a
\Tor
-pair is generalised to that of a (pair of) semi-Bousfield classes.

Next, we pay attention to
\Tor
-pairs. It follows that, over any given ring, there is a set of
\Tor
-pairs and the size of this set has a bound that depends on the cardinality of the ring, we recall this in
Corollary

2.3
. By duality, the
\Tor
-orthogonal class to a class of modules of finite flat dimension is also
\Ext
-orthogonal to a class of pure-injective modules of finite injective dimension. As an outcome of our work, we show that in the commutative noetherian setting, the converse is true as well, so that the
\Ext
-orthogonal classes we consider come from a
\Tor
-pair. Thus, our classification in Theorem
4.17
is also a classification of
\Tor
-pairs generated by modules of finite flat dimension. The choice of generators can be made explicit — the function gives rise to a collection of suitable cocycle modules in localised Koszul complexes associated to each prime ideal, see
Proposition

4.13
.

The characterisation of
\Tor
-pairs over a commutative noetherian ring
R
was posed as an open problem in the monograph of Göbel and Trlifaj
[
19
, §16.5, 3.]
. This was motivated by the aforementioned classification of cotilting classes, as well as the fact that if
R
is hereditary then constituents of
\Tor
-pairs coincide with cotilting classes
[
19
, Theorem 16.31]
. In
Section

5
, we show that if
R
is regular then our main classification result covers all hereditary
\Tor
-pairs and all hereditary cotorsion pairs cogenerated by pure-injective modules. On the other hand, in the singular case there are hereditary
\Tor
-pairs which do not fit into our classification, see
Example

4.6
,
Example

4.8
, and
Remark

5.6
. Leaving the noetherian setting for a short while, we will also supplement this in
Example

2.10
by producing a commutative semihereditary ring (in fact, a valuation domain) over which there are
\Tor
-pairs which contain a class which is not a cotilting class.

Additionally, while cotilting classes originate in representation theory, the present work also extends ideas that were studied in commutative algebra and module theory. For example, the large and small restricted flat dimension introduced by Christensen, Frankild and Foxby
[
14
]
can be interpreted in terms of suitable
\Tor
-pairs, and their results on when these two dimension theories coincide can be interpreted as a special case of our classification, see
Section

6
.

We begin this manuscript with some background in the aptly named
Section

2
, which begins over an arbitrary ring before specialising to commutative rings and then to commutative noetherian rings, introducing the machinery we will need at each stage. Next we introduce some preliminary results in
Section

3
on
\Tor
-pairs, cotorsion pairs and pure-injective modules over commutative noetherian rings. The bulk of the main results of this paper are in
Section

4
, which all take place over a commutative noetherian ring, except for some examples.
The main classification is stated in
Theorem

4.17
. Along the way we provide various examples of
\Tor
-pairs which do not fit various hypotheses. We specialise to regular rings in
Section

5
and show that in this setting, our main result gives a classification of all hereditary
\Tor
-pairs. In the final
Section

6
, we show that a special case of our classification can be used to characterise almost Cohen–Macaulay rings in terms of small and large restricted flat dimensions being equal, providing a global version of
[
14
, Theorem 3.2]
. This last argument also employs the recent study of the Govorov-Lazard Theorem in higher dimensions of
[
24
]
.

2.2.
Homological formulae

The
\mathrm{Hom}
-
\otimes
adjunction yields the following natural isomorphism

\Ext_{R}^{i}(A,\mathrm{Hom}_{S}(B,C))\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{S}(\Tor_{i}^{R}(A,B),C)

for any

, where
A\in\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R
,
B\in R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}S
, and

is injective (see
[
19
, Lemma 2.16]
). If
C_{S}
is, in addition, an injective cogenerator then
\Ext_{R}^{n}(A,\mathrm{Hom}_{S}(B,C))=0
if and only if
\Tor_{n}^{R}(A,B)=0
.

Recall that an
R
-module

is
cotorsion
if
\Ext^{1}_{R}(F,M)
vanishes for every
F\in\mathcal{F}_{0}(R)
.

Let
R
and

be rings. Consider the modules
A\in\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R
,

which is flat both as an
R

-module, and

a cotorsion

-module. Then, by the derived

-
\otimes

.

(2.1)

\Ext^{i}_{S}(A\otimes_{R}B,C)\cong\Ext^{i}_{R}(A,\Hom_{S}(B,C))

Indeed, there are natural isomorphisms

\displaystyle\Ext^{i}_{S}(A\otimes_{R}B,C)

\displaystyle\cong\Hom_{\mathsf{D}(S)}(A\otimes_{R}B,C[i])

\displaystyle\cong\Hom_{\mathsf{D}(S)}(A\otimes_{R}^{\mathbf{L}}B,C[i])

\displaystyle\cong\Hom_{\mathsf{D}(R)}(A,\RHom_{S}(B,C)[i])

\displaystyle\cong\Hom_{\mathsf{D}(R)}(A,\Hom_{S}(B,C)[i])

\displaystyle\cong\Ext^{i}_{R}(A,\Hom_{S}(B,C))

where the second isomorphism follows from the flatness of

as an

\otimes

2.3.
Pure-injective modules

We refer the reader to
[
19
, §2.1]
or the monograph
[
34
]
for more details related to this subsection. A
pure embedding
, or
pure monomorphism
of right
R
-modules is a monomorphism
\mu\colon A\to B
which remains exact under the tensor product
(-\otimes_{R}C)
for any
C\in R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}
, or equivalently, the short exact sequence

(2.2)

0\to A\overset{\mu}{\to}B\to B/\mu(A)\to 0

remains exact under
\Hom_{R}(S,-)
for all finitely presented right
R
-modules
S
. If
\mu
is a pure embedding, then the short exact sequence
2.2
is called a
pure-exact sequence
, the epimorphism
B\to B/\mu(A)
in
2.2
is called a
pure epimorphism
, and
\mu(A)
is a
pure submodule
of
B
.

A module
N
is
pure-injective
if the induced map
\Hom_{R}(B,N)\to\Hom_{R}(A,N)
is an epimorphism for any pure embedding
A\to B
. In other words, the pure-injective modules are the modules which are injective with respect to pure monomorphisms. We let
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{I}_{0}
denote the class of pure-injective modules. A fact we will use throughout is that cosyzygies of pure-injective modules are pure-injective,
[
19
, Lemma 6.20]
.

We give some examples of pure-injective modules. For a module
M\in\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R
, we denote the
character module
of

by
M^{+}\vcentcolon=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
. If
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
is a local commutative noetherian ring then we also consider the
Matlis dual

M^{\vee}\vcentcolon=\Hom_{R}(M,E(k))
of an
R
-module

, where

is the injective hull of

are pure-injective

and, for suitable

All pure-injective modules are cotorsion, and in fact a module is flat if and only if

vanishes for every pure-injective module

. Indeed, by

for any left

\Hom_{R}(\varinjlim_{I}M_{i},N)\cong\varprojlim_{I}\Hom_{R}(M_{i},N)

\Ext^{j}_{R}(\varinjlim_{I}M_{i},N)\cong\varprojlim_{I}\Ext^{j}_{R}(M_{i},N)

Let

2.4.
Cotorsion pairs.

A
cotorsion pair
is a pair of classes
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
in
\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R
, such that
\mathcal{A}={}^{\perp_{1}}\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}^{\perp_{1}}
. A cotorsion pair is
hereditary
if moreover
\mathcal{A}={}^{\perp}\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}^{\perp}
, that is, all the higher
\Ext
-groups vanish as well.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a class in
\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R
. The cotorsion pair
generated
by
\mathcal{X}
is the cotorsion pair
({}^{\perp_{1}}(\mathcal{X}^{\perp_{1}}),\mathcal{X}^{\perp_{1}})
. The cotorsion pair
cogenerated
by
\mathcal{X}
is the cotorsion pair
({}^{\perp_{1}}\mathcal{X},({}^{\perp_{1}}\mathcal{X})^{\perp_{1}})
.

The left-hand class in a cotorsion pair is closed under direct sums and extensions, and contains all the projective modules. The right-hand class in a cotorsion pair is closed under products and extensions, and contains all the injective modules. A class
\mathcal{X}
in
\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R
is
resolving
if
\mathcal{X}
is closed under extensions, contains the projectives, and is closed under kernels of epimorphisms. Dually, a class
\mathcal{X}
in
\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R
is
coresolving
if
\mathcal{X}
is closed under extensions, contains the injectives, and is closed under cokernels of monomorphisms.
A cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is hereditary if and only if
\mathcal{A}
is a resolving class if and only if
\mathcal{B}
is a coresolving class.

A cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is
complete
if
\mathcal{A}
is special precovering, or equivalently, if
\mathcal{B}
is special preenveloping, see
[
19
, §5 and §6]
for details.

2.5.
\Tor
-pairs.

A
\Tor
-pair
is a pair of classes
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
with
\mathcal{E}
in
\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R
and
\mathcal{C}
in
R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}
, such that
\mathcal{E}={}^{\top_{1}}\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{E}^{\top_{1}}
. A
\Tor
-pair is
hereditary
if moreover
\mathcal{E}={}^{\top}\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{E}^{\top}
, that is, all the higher
\Tor
-groups vanish as well. In particular,
(\mathcal{F}_{n},\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\top})^{\top}
forms a hereditary
\Tor
-pair for every
n\geq 0
. Indeed, the
n
-th syzygy module
\Omega^{n}(M)
of any left
R
-module
M
belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\top}
by a dimension-shift argument. On the other hand, if
F\in{}^{\top}\Omega^{n}(M)
for any left
R
-module
M
then it is straightforward to verify that
F\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
, establishing that
{}^{\top}((\mathcal{F}_{n})^{\top})=\mathcal{F}_{n}
. Then
(\mathcal{F}_{n},\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\top})^{\top}
forms a
\Tor
-pair, and since
\mathcal{F}_{n}
is a resolving subcategory of
R
-modules, this
\Tor
-pair is clearly hereditary.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a class in
\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R
. The
\Tor
-pair
generated
by
\mathcal{X}
is the
\Tor
-pair of the form
({}^{\top_{1}}(\mathcal{X}^{\top_{1}}),\mathcal{X}^{\top_{1}})^{\top}
. If
\mathcal{X}
is closed under yokes, then the
\Tor
-pair it generates is hereditary. Both classes in a
\Tor
-pair are closed under direct limits, pure submodules, pure epimorphisms and arbitrary direct sums. Over commutative rings, it is clear that if
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
is a
\Tor
-pair, then also
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})^{\top}
is a (distinct)
\Tor
-pair.

2.6.
The lattice of
\Tor
-pairs and cotorsion pairs

The collection of cotorsion pairs in
\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R
is partially ordered by inclusion in their first component, and moreover forms a complete lattice, denoted
L_{\Ext_{1}}
. The cotorsion pairs
(\mathcal{P}_{0},\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R)
and
(\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R,\mathcal{I}_{0})
form the smallest and largest element respectively. In general, the lattice
L_{\Ext_{1}}
is class-sized
[
19
, Example 5.16]
.

Similarly, the collection of
\Tor
-pairs partially ordered by inclusion in their first component form a complete lattice, denoted
L_{\Tor_{1}}
. The
\Tor
-pairs
(\mathcal{F}_{0},R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod})^{\top}
and
(\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R,\mathcal{F}_{0})^{\top}
form the smallest and largest element respectively. The lattice
L_{\Tor_{1}}
can be canonically embedded into
L_{\Ext_{1}}
via the following assignment.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
19
, Lemma 2.16(b), Lemma 5.17]

Let
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
be a
\Tor
-pair.

(1)

There is a cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}^{\perp_{1}})
of right
R
-modules which is cogenerated by
\mathcal{C}^{+}\vcentcolon=\{C^{+}\mid C\in\mathcal{C}\}
.

(2)

There is a cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\perp_{1}})
of left
R
-modules which is cogenerated by
\mathcal{E}^{+}\vcentcolon=\{E^{+}\mid E\in\mathcal{E}\}
.

Moreover, if
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
is hereditary, then so are
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}^{\perp_{1}})
and
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\perp_{1}})
.

In contrast to the case of cotorsion pairs, the lattice of
\Tor
-pairs forms a set.

Corollary 2.3
.

[
19
, Corollary 6.24]

Let
R
be a ring and
\kappa=|R|+\aleph_{0}
. Then
|L_{\Tor_{1}}|\leq 2^{2^{\kappa}}
.

The question remains of whether one can classify all the
\Tor
-pairs over a particular ring; in the case of commutative noetherian rings, this was formulated in
[
19
, §16.5, 3.]
. Some specific examples are known:
\Tor
-pairs over a Dedekind domain have been completely characterised in terms of subsets of the spectrum, and, the
\Tor
-pairs over a right hereditary ring have been classified in terms of resolving subcategories of
\mathsf{mod}\mbox{-}R
, see
[
19
, Theorem 16.29 and 16.31]
. We will show that it is not possible to classify all the
\Tor
-pairs in such a way over an arbitrary commutative noetherian ring, that is, either in terms of the spectrum or in terms of resolving subcategories. Instead, we restrict to a specific collection of
\Tor
-pairs.

2.7.
Hereditary cotorsion pairs cogenerated by pure-injective modules

Let
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{W})
be a hereditary cotorsion pair cogenerated by a class
\mathcal{Q}
of pure-injective
R
-modules, that is,
\mathcal{C}={}^{\perp}\mathcal{Q}
. Then
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{W})
is complete and perfect, the latter meaning that
\mathcal{C}
is closed under direct limits, see
[
19
, §6]
. By
Lemma

2.2
(2)
, a hereditary
\Tor
-pair
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
gives rise to a hereditary cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{W})
cogenerated by pure-injectives via the adjunction formula
\mathcal{C}={}^{\perp_{>0}}(\mathcal{E}^{+})
. This in fact gives a lattice isomorphism between the lattice
L_{\Tor_{1}}
of hereditary
\Tor
-pairs and the sublattice of
L_{\Ext^{1}}
consisting of those hereditary cotorsion pairs which are cogenerated by character dual modules, which in turn embeds into the sublattice of
L_{\Ext^{1}}
consisting of those hereditary cotorsion pairs which are cogenerated by pure-injective modules.

Remark 2.4
.

We do not know whether each hereditary cotorsion pair cogenerated by pure-injective modules is induced by a hereditary
\Tor
-pair in the above sense, or equivalently, is cogenerated by character duals. In
Section

4
, we show that this holds for a commutative noetherian ring when restricted to pure-injective modules of finite injective dimension. In
Section

5
, we show that this covers all cotorsion pairs cogenerated by pure-injective modules provided the ring is, in addition, regular.

Outside of commutative noetherian rings, one could ask the same question for valuation domains. In this case, each pure-injective module is of injective dimension at most one. Bazzoni proved in
[
8
, Theorem 6.11]
that the left-hand class in a cotorsion pair cogenerated by a cotilting module is a class in a
\Tor
-pair, see
Section

2.9
for results on cotilting modules. However, we do not know if this is necessarily the case for an arbitrary pure-injective modules, even of finite injective dimension, or for a cotilting module over more general families of rings.

The question is similar in shape to asking whether each cohomological Bousfield class in the derived module category is a homological Bousfield class. For details, we refer to Stevenson’s paper
[
38
]
, in which he proves that there is a commutative von Neumann regular ring whose derived module category has a cohomological Bousfield class which is not a homological Bousfield class, see
[
38
, Corollary 4.12]
.

2.8.
Generation of
\Tor
-pairs

We call a class
\mathcal{C}
of
R
-modules
definable
if it is closed under products, direct limits, and pure submodules. It is straightforward to see that the
\Tor
-orthogonal of a set of finitely presented modules is a definable class. The converse does not hold, however, the following theorem describes exactly when this happens. An analogous result for cotorsion pairs is known to hold, see
[
37
, Theorem 6.1]
.

Before stating the result, we need to recall the concept of a relative Mittag-Leffler module. Let
M
be a right module over a ring
R
, and let

\mathcal{C}
be a class of left
R
-modules. We say that

is a

\mathcal{C}
-Mittag-Leffler module
if the canonical map

\rho\colon M\otimes_{R}\prod_{i\in I}C_{i}\to\prod_{i\in I}\left(M\otimes_{R}C_{i}\right)

is injective for any family
\{C_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of modules in
\mathcal{C}
.

Theorem 2.5
.

[
21
, Theorem 3.13]

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a class of right
R
-modules and let
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{X}^{\top_{1}}
.Then
\mathcal{C}
is definable if and only if there exists a class of countably presented right
R
-modules
\mathcal{S}
such that

(1)

\mathcal{X}\subseteq\varinjlim\mathcal{S}
,

(2)

\mathcal{S}^{\top_{1}}=\mathcal{C}
, and

(3)

the first syzygy of any module in
\mathcal{S}
is
\mathcal{C}
-Mittag-Leffler.

In particular, if
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
is a (not-necessarily hereditary)
\Tor
-pair such that
\mathcal{C}
is definable, then there exists a set of countably presented modules
\mathcal{S}
which generates the
\Tor
-pair and
\mathcal{E}
coincides with the direct limit of its countably presented constituents.

For the first statement, we comment that the

The second equivalence holds as an

Remark 2.8
.

Bazzoni showed that over a valuation domain, there exist cotilting classes which are not of cofinite type,
[
7
, Proposition 4.5]
. Thus, there exist definable classes in hereditary
\Tor
-pairs generated by modules of bounded flat dimension which are not of cofinite type, even over commutative (semihereditary) rings.

Additionally, as already mentioned in

2.4
, by
[

, Theorem 6.11]
, every cotilting class over a valuation domain is a class in a
\Tor
-pair. As valuation domains have weak global dimension at most one, every
\Tor
-pair is hereditary and generated by modules of flat dimension at most one.

However, the following question remains: Does it follow from
Theorem

2.5
that every cotilting class over any ring is of cocountable type? Here, a cotilting class
\mathcal{C}
is said to be of

if there is a set
\mathcal{S}
of countably presented
R
-modules such that
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{S}^{\top}
. This holds for valuation domains and commutative noetherian rings. What is missing is to show that the left-hand class in a cotorsion pair cogenerated by a cotilting module is a class in a
\Tor
-pair, see
Remark

2.4
.

A
cotilting
\Tor
-pair
is a hereditary
\Tor
-pair
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
generated by a class of modules of finite flat dimension and where the right-hand class is a definable class. By Proposition
2.7
, if a cotilting
\Tor
-pair is generated by a class of modules of flat dimension at most
n
, then it is an
n
-cotilting
\Tor
-pair
.

In

2.16
we shall see another consequence of

2.7
over commutative noetherian rings. Namely, given a cotilting
\Tor
-pair

,

2.7
yields that

is a cotilting class for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)
.

Finally, let us also mention that in
Corollary

4.20
we will show that, over a commutative noetherian ring, a cotilting
\Tor
-pair is a
\Tor
-pair generated by a class of finitely generated modules of finite projective dimension.

Remark 2.9
.

In view of
Proposition

2.7
, the assignment
\mathcal{C}\mapsto(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
constitutes a bijection between

-cotilting

as introduced in
[

, see also
[

for a discussion about
\infty
-cotilting classes relevant to this setting

By
[

, Theorem 16.31]
, over a right hereditary ring all
\Tor
-pairs are classified in terms of resolving subcategories of
\mathsf{mod}\mbox{-}R
. This implies that every class in a

-pair over a commutative hereditary ring is a cotilting class of cofinite type. We show that this is not necessarily the case over semihereditary commutative rings, in particular over a commutative valuation domain. Thus, in this setting, there exist classes in
\Tor

Example 2.10

Let

-module

(iii)

\mathfrak{p}\notin\mathsf{Ass}(\bigoplus^{k-1}_{i=0}\Omega_{-i}(M))

Proof.

(1)
By Proposition
2.12
, if

. Therefore

is an

-module of flat dimension

(2)
Taking duals with respect to an injective module takes any module to a pure-injective module, and flat modules to injective modules. Moreover, both the character module and the Matlis dual are duals with respect to an injective cogenerator, hence they are not only exact functors but also preserve dimensions. That is, the dual of the Koszul resolution of

-projective dimension

For the equalities between the
\Tor
-orthogonals and the left
\Ext

which holds because of the homological formulae given in §

. As these homological formulae also hold for the case of the Matlis dual, we also deduce that

Now we prove the second part of the statement that describes the
\mathrm{Tor}
-orthogonal of

(i)\Leftrightarrow(ii)
Consider the following equivalences.

(2.3)

\displaystyle M\in S_{k}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}};R_{\mathfrak{p}})^{\top}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\Tor^{R}_{i}(S_{k}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}};R_{\mathfrak{p}}),M)=0\text{ for }1\leq i\leq k

(2.4)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\Tor^{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}_{i}(S_{k}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}};R_{\mathfrak{p}}),M_{\mathfrak{p}})=0\text{ for }1\leq i\leq k

(2.5)

Finally, by definition,

(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iii)

If

(3.2)

\Ext_{R}^{i}(M,N)^{S}\cong\Ext_{R}^{i}(M_{S},N)\cong\Ext_{R}^{i}(M,N^{S})\cong\Ext_{R_{S}}^{i}(M_{S},N^{S})

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring and
S
a multiplicative subset of
R
. Consider a hereditary cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{W})
cogenerated by a set
\mathcal{Q}
of pure-injective modules. Then there is a hereditary cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{C}_{S},\mathcal{V})
in
\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R_{S}
cogenerated by the set
\mathcal{Q}^{S}
of pure-injective modules, and moreover
\mathcal{C}_{S}=\mathcal{C}\cap\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R_{S}
.

In addition
(\mathcal{C}_{S})_{(i)}
coincides with
(\mathcal{C}_{(i)})_{S}
for
i\geq 0
.

Proof.

First note that
\mathcal{C}_{S}=\mathcal{C}\cap\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R_{S}
as
\mathcal{C}
is closed under direct limits.

Take
P\in\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R
a pure-injective module, and
M\in\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R
. Then for any
i>0
:

\Ext_{R}^{i}(C_{S},P)\cong\Ext^{i}_{R_{S}}(C_{S},P^{S})\cong\Ext^{i}_{R}(C,P^{S})

by

. Then

if and only if

∎

3.4.
Localisations and
\Tor
-pairs

For further use we record the good behaviour of
\Tor
-pairs with respect to localisation. Recall the definitions before
Proposition

.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring, and let
S
be a multiplicative subset of
R
. Consider a
\Tor
-pair
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
. Then there is a
\Tor
-pair
(\mathcal{E}_{S},\mathcal{C}_{S})^{\top}
in
\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R_{S}
, and moreover
\mathcal{E}_{S}=\mathcal{E}\cap\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R_{S}
and
\mathcal{C}_{S}=\mathcal{C}\cap\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R_{S}

If in addition the

Take

-orthogonal of
\mathcal{C}_{S}
in
\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R_{S}
.
∎

3.5.
Pure-injective modules over commutative noetherian rings

When
R
is a commutative noetherian ring, it is known that every flat cotorsion module is pure-injective
[
43
, Lemma 3.2.3]
. Moreover, by work of Enochs
[
17
]
, or
[
16
, Section 5.3, Theorem 5.3.28]
, every flat cotorsion module is isomorphic to a product
\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)}T_{\mathfrak{p}}
, where
T_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the
\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-completion of a free
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring and
C\in\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R
a cotorsion module. If
\Hom_{R}(R_{\mathfrak{m}},C)=0
for every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)
, then
C=0
.

Proof.

Let
F\overset{\phi}{\to}C\to 0
be a flat cover of
C
. Using
[
16
, Theorem 5.3.28]
, we can explicitly write
F
as
\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)}T_{\mathfrak{p}}
as described above.

First note that if
\mathfrak{m}
is a maximal ideal of
R
and
\Hom_{R}(R_{\mathfrak{m}},C)=0
, then necessarily
\Hom_{R}(M_{\mathfrak{m}},C)=0
for every
R_{\mathfrak{m}}
-module
M_{\mathfrak{m}}
. Therefore, as
T_{\mathfrak{p}}
is an
R_{\mathfrak{m}}
-module for every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
containing
\mathfrak{p}
,
\Hom_{R}(T_{\mathfrak{p}},C)=0
for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)
. As the contravariant
\Hom
functor commutes with coproducts,
\Hom_{R}(\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)}T_{\mathfrak{p}},C)\cong\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)}\Hom_{R}(T_{\mathfrak{p}},C)=0
. Let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle{\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)}T_{\mathfrak{p}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle{\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)}T_{\mathfrak{p}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Coker}(\tau)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

denote the short exact sequence where
\tau
is the natural inclusion of a coproduct into a product. As
\phi\tau=0
,
\phi
factors through
\mathsf{Coker}(\tau)
, that is, there exists a homomorphism
\beta\colon\mathsf{Coker}(\tau)\to C
such that
\beta\pi=\phi
.

As

is a pure monomorphism, it follows that
\mathsf{Coker}(\tau)
is a flat

, there exists a morphism

such that

. Therefore,

, and by the minimality property of the flat cover

is an automorphism. We conclude that

, as desired.

Remark 3.6
.

The noetherianity assumption is required in

. Namely, by work of Trlifaj

]

Proposition 3.7
.

Let

be a commutative noetherian ring. Let

be a pure-injective module and

for every maximal ideal

Proof.

Let
N
be a pure-injective module and
\mathfrak{m}

, there is an isomorphism

\Ext_{R}^{i}(M,N^{\mathfrak{m}})\cong\Ext_{R}^{i}(M,N)^{\mathfrak{m}}.

Thus, it is sufficient to show that

if and only if

.

Remark 3.8
.

The authors do not know whether

holds without the noetherianity assumption. Certainly, if

is commutative and

We also remark that

, if

a contradiction.

Remark 4.7
.

Given a generating set of a
\Tor
-pair, it is not immediately clear whether it will be generated or cogenerated by modules of bounded flat dimension. For example, let

be a local noetherian self-injective ring which is not a field, and consider again the

is closed under injective envelopes, but the flat dimension of

However, clearly this
\Tor

There exist hereditary
\Tor
-pairs which are not generated (or cogenerated) by classes of modules of finite flat dimension, as seen in the following example.

Example 4.8
.

Let

. Moreover,

where
\mathcal{I}

is a hereditary

For

is closed under injective envelopes by

. If
E(M)\in\mathcal{C}

For

. Then it follows trivially that
M\in\mathsf{Sub}(\mathcal{C})
, and
\Omega_{-1}(M)\in\mathcal{C}_{(1)}
follows since
E(M)\in\mathcal{C}
by
Corollary

4.4
and applying
Lemma

3.1
(i)
.

Now suppose that
M\in\mathsf{Sub}(\mathcal{C})
and
\Omega_{-1}(M)\in\mathcal{C}_{(1)}
. Then
E(M)\in\mathcal{C}
by
(i)
, so by
Lemma

3.1
(ii),
M\in\mathcal{C}
.
∎

The following lemma will be used in the proof of
Proposition

4.12
.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local commutative noetherian ring and
N
a pure-injective module of finite injective dimension. Then
\Ext_{R}^{j}(k(\mathfrak{p}),N)=0
for every
j>0pt(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
.

Proof.

In view of the isomorphism
\Ext_{R}^{j}(k(\mathfrak{p}),N)\cong\Ext_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{j}(k(\mathfrak{p}),N^{\mathfrak{p}})
(see
3.2
), it is sufficient to prove the statement when
\mathfrak{p}
is the maximal ideal.

Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}
denote a maximal regular sequence, so
m=0pt(R)
, and let
J\vcentcolon=\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\rangle
. Fix a module
N
of finite injective dimension over
R
. Then, for
j>0pt(R)
,
\Ext_{R}^{j}(R/J,N)=0
as
\mathsf{pd}(R/J)=0pt(R)
, so in particular,
\Ext^{i}_{R}(R/J,\Omega_{-0pt(R)}N)=0
for all
i>0
.

As
J
is generated by a maximal regular sequence,
R/J
is a local ring with zero depth,
\mathfrak{m}
is an associated prime of
R/J
. The module
\Omega_{-0pt(R)}N
is pure-injective of finite injective dimension, so by
Corollary

4.4
,
E(R/J)\in{}^{\perp}\Omega_{-0pt(R)}N
. As
\mathfrak{m}
is an associated prime of
R/J
,
E(k)
is a direct summand of
E(R/J)
, and by
Lemma

4.3
, it follows that
k\in{}^{\perp}\Omega_{-0pt(R)}N
, so
\Ext^{i}_{R}(k,N)=0
for all
i>0ptR
as desired.

∎

The following two propositions provide the two main assignments of the classification
Theorem

4.17
. First, we introduce the following function, which will play an essential role in our classification results.

Definition 4.11
.

We let the
depth function
be the function
0pt\colon\mathsf{Spec}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
assigning to each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
, the depth of the ring localised at
\mathfrak{p}
, i.e.:

0pt(\mathfrak{p})\coloneqq 0pt(R_{\mathfrak{p}})=\inf\{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\mid\Ext_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{i}(k(\mathfrak{p}),R_{\mathfrak{p}})\neq 0\}.

We consider functions on the spectrum
\mathsf{Spec}(R)
with values in non-negative integers
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. For a general function
\phi\colon\mathsf{Spec}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we will write
\phi\leq 0pt
as a shorthand notation for
\phi(\mathfrak{p})\leq 0pt(\mathfrak{p})
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)
.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{W})
be a hereditary cotorsion pair cogenerated by a class
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{P}\mathcal{I}_{0}
. Define a map
\phi\colon\mathsf{Spec}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
by
\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\inf\{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\mid k(\mathfrak{p})\in\mathcal{C}_{(i)}\}
. Then
\phi\leq 0pt
.

Proof.

We have for any
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)

\Ext^{i}_{R}(k(\mathfrak{p}),N)\cong\Ext^{i}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(k(\mathfrak{p}),N^{\mathfrak{p}})

for any pure-injective
R
-module
N
and
i>0
. By
Lemma

4.10
, we know that
\Ext^{j}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(k(\mathfrak{p}),N^{\mathfrak{p}})=0
for every pure-injective
N
of finite injective dimension and every
j>0pt(\mathfrak{p})
. It follows that
k(\mathfrak{p})\in{}^{\perp_{>0pt(\mathfrak{p})}}\mathcal{Q}
, which by definition means that
k(\mathfrak{p})\in\mathcal{C}_{(0pt(\mathfrak{p}))}
so
\phi(\mathfrak{p})\leq 0pt(\mathfrak{p})
, as required.
∎

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
\phi\colon\mathsf{Spec}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
be a function satisfying
\phi\leq 0pt
. Then there is a hereditary
\Tor
-pair
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
generated by a set of modules of finite flat dimension such that
\mathcal{C}=\{M\in\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R\mid 0pt_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq\phi(\mathfrak{p})~\forall\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)\}
.

Moreover,
\mathcal{C}_{(i)}=\{M\in\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R\mid 0pt_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq(\phi(\mathfrak{p})-i)~\forall\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)\}
for any
i\geq 0
. In particular,
k(\mathfrak{p})\in\mathcal{C}_{(i)}
if and only if
\phi(\mathfrak{p})\leq i
.

Proof.

We construct a generating set
\mathcal{S}_{\phi}
for the
\Tor
-pair, consisting of modules of finite flat dimension in
\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R
. For each
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)
we have
\phi(\mathfrak{p})\leq 0pt(\mathfrak{p})
, so
Proposition

2.15
yields that the module
S_{\phi(\mathfrak{p})}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}};R_{\mathfrak{p}})
constructed in
Section

2.10
is of finite flat dimension over
R
for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)
. We let

\mathcal{S}_{\phi}\vcentcolon=\{S_{\phi(\mathfrak{p})}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}};R_{\mathfrak{p}})\}_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)},

and let
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
be the
\Tor
-pair generated by
\mathcal{S}_{\phi}
. Then, again by
Proposition

2.15
, we conclude that the modules in
\mathcal{C}_{\phi}\vcentcolon=\mathcal{S}_{\phi}^{\top}
are of the desired form.

For the moreover statement, let
M\in\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R
and consider a short exact sequence
0\to\Omega_{1}M\to P\to M\to 0
with
P
projective. Then
0pt_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}((\Omega_{1}M)_{\mathfrak{p}})=\min(0pt_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})+1,0pt(\mathfrak{p}))
. Since
P\in\mathcal{C}
, we get
M\in\mathcal{C}_{(1)}\iff\Omega_{1}M\in\mathcal{C}
by
Lemma

3.1
, which yields the desired description of
\mathcal{C}_{(1)}
, then we proceed by induction. The final claim then follows directly as
0pt_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(k(\mathfrak{p}))=0
.
∎

Remark 4.14
.

In the generating set
\mathcal{S}_{\phi}
constructed above, for
n>0
we note that

\mathcal{S}_{\phi}\cap\mathcal{F}_{n}(R)=\{S_{\phi(\mathfrak{p})}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}};R_{\mathfrak{p}})\mid\phi(\mathfrak{p})\leq n\}.

However, for
i\geq 0
, the induced
\Tor
-pairs are generated by the following set of
R
-modules of finite flat dimension,

(\mathcal{E}_{\phi})^{\top_{>i}}=(\mathcal{C}_{\phi})_{(i)}=\{S_{\phi(\mathfrak{p})-i}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}};R_{\mathfrak{p}})\mid\phi(\mathfrak{p})>i\}^{\top}.

In the case that the flat dimensions of the generators are bounded by 1, the class
\mathcal{C}
is closed under submodules. Therefore, we can readily capture these classes in terms of subsets of
\mathsf{Spec}(R)
; this will serve as the base step of the coming induction.
For a subset
X
of
\mathsf{Spec}(R)
, we will denote by
\mathcal{C}_{X}
the class of
R
-modules
\{M\in\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R\mid\mathsf{Ass}(M)\subseteq X\}
.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a class of
R
-modules closed under submodules, extensions, and direct limits. Then there is a subset
X
of
\mathsf{Spec}(R)
such that
\mathcal{C}=\{M\in\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R\mid\mathsf{Ass}(M)\subseteq X\}
.

Proof.

Given
M\in\mathcal{C}
, we have
k(\mathfrak{p})\in\mathcal{C}
for any
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Ass}(M)
as
\mathcal{C}
is closed under submodules and direct limits. Then also
E(k(\mathfrak{p}))\in\mathcal{C}
, because
E(k(\mathfrak{p}))
admits a filtration by coproducts of copies of
k(\mathfrak{p})
by Matlis
[
32
]
. Put
X=\bigcup_{M\in\mathcal{C}}\mathsf{Ass}(M)
, then clearly
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{X}
. To prove the converse, let
N\in\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R
such that
\mathsf{Ass}(N)\subseteq X
. Then the injective envelope
E(N)
of
N
is a coproduct of copies of
E(k(\mathfrak{p}))
with
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Ass}(N)
, again by
[
32
]
. Then
E(N)\in\mathcal{C}
, so that also
N\in\mathcal{C}
.
∎

The following Proposition is the key consequence of
Lemma

4.15
we need in our classification theorem.

Proposition 4.16
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{W})
be a hereditary cotorsion pair cogenerated by a class of pure-injective modules of finite injective dimension. Set
X=\mathsf{Ass}(\mathcal{C})
. Then
\mathsf{Sub}(\mathcal{C})=\{M\in\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R\mid\mathsf{Ass}(M)\subseteq X\}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{C}
is closed under injective envelopes by
Corollary

4.4
, we have
\mathsf{Sub}(\mathcal{C})=\mathsf{Sub}(\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{I}_{0})
, where
\mathcal{I}_{0}
is the class of injective modules. It is straightforward to check that
\mathsf{Sub}(\mathcal{C})
is closed under extensions (see
4.1
). We claim that it is also closed under direct limits. To this aim, by
[
19
, Lemma 2.14]
, it is enough to show that it is closed under direct limits of well-ordered direct systems. By
[
28
, Lemma 3.6]
, the direct limit of any well-ordered direct system of modules from
\mathsf{Sub}(\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{I}_{0})
is a submodule of a direct limit of objects from the class
\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{I}_{0}
which is closed under direct limits. This shows that
\mathsf{Sub}(\mathcal{C})
is also closed by direct limits.

Now the statement of the Proposition follows from
Lemma

4.15
.
∎

Finally, we are ready to state and prove our main theorem.

Theorem 4.17
.

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring. There is a bijective correspondence between the following collections.

(i)

Hereditary
\Tor
-pairs
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
generated by classes of modules of finite flat dimension,

(ii)

Hereditary cotorsion pairs
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{W})
cogenerated by classes of pure-injective modules of finite injective dimension,

(iii)

Functions
\phi\colon\mathsf{Spec}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
\phi\leq 0pt
,

(iv)

Sequences of subsets
X_{0}\subseteq\dots\subseteq X_{n}\subseteq\dots\subset\mathsf{Spec}(R)
such that
\mathsf{Ass}(\Omega_{-n}(R))\subseteq X_{n}
.

The assignments are as follows:

(i)\rightarrow(ii)
: See
Lemma

2.2
.

(iii)\rightarrow(i):

\phi\mapsto(\mathcal{E}_{\phi},\mathcal{C}_{\phi})^{\top}
, where

\mathcal{C}_{\phi}\vcentcolon=\{M\in\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R\mid 0pt_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq\phi(\mathfrak{p})~\forall\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)\}.

(ii)\rightarrow(iii):

\mathcal{C}\mapsto\phi_{\mathcal{C}}
, where

\phi_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathfrak{p})\vcentcolon=\inf\{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\mid k(\mathfrak{p})\in\mathcal{C}_{(i)}\}.

(ii)\rightarrow(iv):

\mathcal{C}\mapsto X_{0}\subseteq\dots\subseteq X_{n}\subseteq\dots\subset\mathsf{Spec}(R)
, where

X_{n}\vcentcolon=\mathsf{Ass}(\mathcal{C}_{(n)}).

Proof.

The assignment
(i)\rightarrow(ii)
of
Lemma

2.2
is clearly injective. The assignments
(iii)\rightarrow(i)
and
(ii)\rightarrow(iii)
are well-defined by
Proposition

4.13
and
Proposition

4.12
, respectively.

There is a straightforward bijection
(iii)\rightarrow(iv)
which assigns to a function
\phi
subsets of
\mathsf{Spec}(R)
of the form
X_{n}\vcentcolon=\bigcup_{0\leq i\leq n}\phi^{-1}(i)
for each
n\geq 0
, as by
Proposition

2.15
,
\phi(\mathfrak{p})\leq 0pt(\mathfrak{p})
if and only if
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Ass}(\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\phi(\mathfrak{p})}\Omega_{-i}(R))
, and by construction
\phi(\mathfrak{p})=\inf\{i\mid\mathfrak{p}\in X_{i}\}
.
In this way,
(ii)\rightarrow(iv)
is identified with
(ii)\rightarrow(iii)
via
Lemma

4.3
.

The composition
(iii)\rightarrow(i)\xhookrightarrow{}(ii)\rightarrow(iii)
is an identity by
Proposition

4.13
. To show that all the involved assignments are bijections, it remains to show that
(ii)\rightarrow(iii)
is injective. Let
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{W})
be a cotorsion pair of the collection
(ii)
and
\phi
the function
\mathsf{Spec}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
associated via
(ii)\rightarrow(iii)
. We shall show that
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{\phi}
.

Using
Corollary

3.9
, we can reduce to the case of
R
local. Then
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}_{\dim(R)}
, which implies
\mathcal{C}_{(\dim(R))}=\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R
.
For each
i\geq 0
, let
X_{i}=\mathsf{Ass}(\mathcal{C}_{(i)})
. Since, by the remarks before
Lemma

3.1
,
(\mathcal{C}_{(i)},\mathcal{W}_{(i)})
is a cotorsion pair cogenerated by pure-injective modules of finite injective dimension we can deduce, from
Proposition

4.16
, that
\mathsf{Sub}(\mathcal{C})_{(i)}=\{M\in\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R\mid\mathsf{Ass}(M)\subseteq X_{i}\}
.
By
Lemma

4.9
, we get the relation
M\in\mathcal{C}_{(i)}
if and only if
\mathsf{Ass}(M)\subseteq X_{i}
and
\Omega_{-1}(M)\in\mathcal{C}_{(i+1)}
. Since
\mathcal{C}_{(\dim(R))}=\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R
, we get by induction that
M\in\mathcal{C}
if and only if
\mathsf{Ass}(\Omega_{-i}(M))\subseteq X_{i}
for all
i\geq 0
. Then
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{\phi}
by the equivalence of
(ii)
and
(iii)
in
Proposition

2.15
:
M\in\mathcal{C}_{\phi}
if and only if
0pt(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq\inf\{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\mid k(\mathfrak{p})\in\mathcal{C}_{(i)}\}
if and only if
\mathfrak{p}\notin\mathsf{Ass}(\Omega_{-i}M)
when
k(\mathfrak{p})\notin\mathcal{C}_{(i)}
if and only if
\mathsf{Ass}(\Omega_{-i}M)\subseteq\mathsf{Ass}(\mathcal{C}_{(i)})
for every

if and only if
M\in\mathcal{C}
.
∎

Remark 4.18
.

It follows that the character duals of the modules in
\mathcal{S}_{\phi}
defined in
Proposition

4.13
are cogenerators of the cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{C}_{\phi},\mathcal{W}_{\phi})
in Theorem
4.17
. However, it also follows that all hereditary cotorsion pairs cogenerated by pure-injective modules of finite injective dimension can be cogenerated instead by modules which are better understood: Matlis duals.

By Proposition
2.15
,
\{S_{\phi(\mathfrak{p})}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}};R_{\mathfrak{p}})^{\vee_{\mathfrak{p}}}\mid S_{\phi(\mathfrak{p})}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}};R_{\mathfrak{p}})\in\mathcal{S}_{\phi}\}
also cogenerate the desired cotorsion pair, where

S_{\phi(\mathfrak{p})}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}};R_{\mathfrak{p}})^{\vee_{\mathfrak{p}}}=\mathrm{Hom}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(S_{\phi(\mathfrak{p})}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}};R_{\mathfrak{p}}),E(R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})).

The following is a restriction of our main theorem to cotilting
\Tor
-pairs. This provides a generalisation of the classification of n-cotilting classes of
[
1
]
to the case when the corresponding resolving subcategory has no uniform bound on projective dimension, which is relevant in the case
\dim(R)=\infty
, see
Remark

2.9
. Note that the bijection
(iii)\leftrightarrow(v)
is precisely
[

, Theorem 1.2]
of Dao and Takahashi.

Remark 4.19
.

Here, we call a function
\phi\colon\mathsf{Spec}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

order-preserving
if
\phi(\mathfrak{q})\leq\phi(\mathfrak{p})
whenever
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. The
grade function
is the function
\mathsf{grade}:\mathsf{Spec}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
defined by the rule
\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\mathsf{grade}_{R}(\mathfrak{p},R)=\inf\{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\mid\Ext_{R}^{i}(R/\mathfrak{p},R)\neq 0\}
, cf. §
2.10
. The relation with the

function used in Theorem
4.17
is given by

, see
[
11
, Proposition 1.2.10]
.
In particular,
\mathsf{grade}
is an order-preserving function such that

. Moreover, we deduce that, for any order-preserving function
\phi:\mathsf{Spec}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

if and only if
\phi\leq\mathsf{grade}
.

Corollary 4.20
.

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring. The correspondence of
Theorem

4.17
restricts to another bijective correspondence between the following collections.

(i)

Hereditary
\Tor
-pairs
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
generated by classes of finitely generated modules of finite projective dimension,

(ii)

Cotilting
\Tor
-pairs
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
,

(iii)

Order-preserving functions
\phi\colon\mathsf{Spec}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
\phi\leq 0pt
,

(iv)

Sequences
X_{0}\subseteq\dots\subseteq X_{n}\subseteq\dots\subset\mathsf{Spec}(R)
of generalisation closed subsets such that
\mathsf{Ass}(\Omega_{-i}(R))\subseteq X_{i}
,

(v)

Resolving subcategories of
\mathsf{mod}\mbox{-}R
consisting of modules of finite projective dimension.

Proof.

The equivalence between
(iii)
and
(iv)
follows directly by restricting the assignment
\phi\mapsto X_{n}\vcentcolon=\bigcup_{0\leq i\leq n}\phi^{-1}(i)
defined in
Theorem

4.17
. It is straightforward to check that
\phi
is order-preserving if and only if each

is generalisation closed for each

.

To a function
\phi\colon\mathsf{Spec}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
as in

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\phi}=\{S_{\phi(\mathfrak{p})}(\mathfrak{p};R)\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)\},

note that
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\phi}
consists of finitely generated modules of finite projective dimension (see

2.13

by Remark
4.19
. Let
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
be the hereditary
\Tor
-pair such that
\mathcal{C}=(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\phi})^{\top}
. We have

\mathcal{C}=\{M\in\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R\mid\mathsf{grade}_{R}(\mathfrak{p},M)\geq\phi(\mathfrak{p}),\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)\}

by
[
26
, Proposition 5.12]
. We claim that this coincides with the
\Tor
-pair associated to
\phi
via
Theorem

4.17
. Indeed, similarly to the discussion above, this follows directly from
[

, Proposition 1.2.10]
and the order-preservation of
\phi
.
Since
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\phi}
consists of finitely generated modules,
\mathcal{C}
is definable, and so
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
is a cotilting
\Tor
-pair. The assignment from
(iii)
to
(v)
is given by taking the smallest resolving subcategory of
\mathsf{mod}\mbox{-}R
which contains
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\phi}
.

If

then we can see that these assignments are bijections directly by
Theorem

2.14
. It remains to explain the case of infinite Krull dimension. Let
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
be a cotilting
\Tor
-pair. For each maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a cotilting class in
\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R_{\mathfrak{m}}
by

and let

be the function associated to

for each maximal ideal

for all inclusions

. This establishes the bijection between items

. Finally, items

and

.

It is natural to ask to what extent is our
Theorem

4.17
broader than the (essentially) known
Corollary

4.20
. In view of the parametrising functions, this is to ask how strong of an assumption it is for a function
\phi\colon\mathsf{Spec}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
bounded by
0pt
to be order-preserving. Using a classical result of Bass, we get the following dichotomy.

Proposition 4.21
.

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

Every function
\phi\colon\mathsf{Spec}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with
\phi\leq 0pt
is order-preserving.

(ii)

\dim(R)\leq 1
.

Proof.

(i)\implies(ii)\colon
Towards contradiction, assume that
\dim(R)>1
. Let
\mathfrak{q}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)
be a prime of height 2. Then a theorem of Bass
[
4
, Proposition 5.2, Corollary 5.3]
provides a non-maximal prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
such that
0pt(\mathfrak{p})=1
. Then the function
\phi
assigning 1 to
\mathfrak{p}
and 0 to all other primes in
\mathsf{Spec}(R)
is bounded by depth and not order-preserving.

(ii)\implies(i)\colon
We have
\phi(\mathfrak{p})\leq 0pt(\mathfrak{p})\leq 0pt(\mathfrak{p})
for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)
. Then
\phi(\mathfrak{p})=0
if
\mathfrak{p}
is a minimal prime and
\phi(\mathfrak{p})\in\{0,1\}
if
\mathfrak{p}
is a maximal prime. Then, clearly,
\phi
is order-preserving.
∎

Remark 4.22
.

The
0pt
function is the largest choice of a parametrising function in
Theorem

4.17
, and as such corresponds to the largest
\Tor
-pair
(\mathcal{E}_{0}pt,\mathcal{C}_{0}pt)^{\top}
in the lattice which is subject to our classification. In another words, the
\Tor
-pair
(\mathcal{E}_{0}pt,\mathcal{C}_{0}pt)^{\top}
is generated by the whole class
\mathcal{F}
consisting of
all
modules of finite flat dimension. In the terminology we will introduce in
Section

6
,
\mathcal{C}_{0}pt
consists precisely of modules of restricted flat dimension zero. If

is Cohen–Macaulay then

is itself an order-preserving function and thus the induced
\Tor
-pair is cotilting. In general though, the
0pt
function may fail to be order-preserving. In fact,
0pt
is order-preserving if and only if
0pt=\mathsf{grade}
if and only if
R
is almost Cohen–Macaulay, see
Section

6
again.

For example, consider a noetherian local ring
R
with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
such that
0pt(\mathfrak{m})=0
. Then the only function associated to a cotilting
\Tor
-pair by
Corollary

4.20
is the constant zero. However, if
\dim(R)>1
then
Proposition

4.21
yields more depth-bounded functions and thus more hereditary
\Tor
-pairs subject to
Theorem

4.17
, including the one corresponding to the
0pt
function itself.

5.
The regular case

Let
R
be a regular ring, that is,
R
is a commutative noetherian ring such that every localisation of
R
at a prime ideal has finite global dimension. We show that
Theorem

4.17
characterises
all
hereditary
\Tor
-pairs over
R
, and in fact, all hereditary cotorsion pairs cogenerated by pure-injective modules. This follows readily if
R
is local, or even when it has finite Krull dimension, but to obtain the general case we need to make the following observation first. Let
\mathsf{mSpec}(R)
denote the set of maximal ideals of
R
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring. Any hereditary cotorsion pair cogenerated by a class
\mathcal{Q}
of pure-injective
R
-modules is cogenerated by the class
\bigcup_{\mathfrak{m}\in\mathsf{mSpec}(R)}\mathcal{Q}^{\mathfrak{m}}
, where
\mathcal{Q}^{\mathfrak{m}}\coloneqq\{N^{\mathfrak{m}}\mid N\in\mathcal{Q}\}
for each maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
.

Proof.

The statement follows directly from
Proposition

3.7
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
R
be a regular ring. Any hereditary cotorsion pair cogenerated by pure-injective modules is cogenerated by pure-injective modules of finite injective dimension.

Proof.

This follows directly from
Proposition

5.1
as
N^{\mathfrak{m}}
is a pure-injective
R
-module whose injective dimension over both
R_{\mathfrak{m}}
and
R
cannot exceed
\dim(R_{\mathfrak{m}})
.
∎

Note that if
R
is regular, then every hereditary
\Tor
-pair is generated by modules of finite flat dimension. Indeed, if a hereditary
\Tor
-pair
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
is generated by a class
\mathcal{G}
of modules, then it is also generated by the class
\bigcup_{\mathfrak{m}\in\mathsf{mSpec}(R)}\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{m}}
, where
\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{m}}=\{G_{\mathfrak{m}}\mid G\in\mathcal{G}\}
. Unlike
Proposition

5.1
, this follows easily by noting that
\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,G)=0
if and only if
0=\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,G)_{\mathfrak{m}}\cong\Tor_{i}^{R_{\mathfrak{m}}}(M,G_{\mathfrak{m}})
for each
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathsf{mSpec}(R)
.

In the regular case, our
Theorem

4.17
now allows the following formulation. Recall that over a regular (or more generally, Cohen–Macaulay) ring
R
, we have
0pt(\mathfrak{p})=\mathsf{grade}(\mathfrak{p})=0pt(\mathfrak{p})
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
R
be a regular commutative noetherian ring. There is a bijective correspondence between the following collections.

(i)

Hereditary
\Tor
-pairs
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
,

(ii)

Hereditary cotorsion pairs
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{W})
cogenerated by classes of pure-injective modules,

(iii)

Functions
\phi\colon\mathsf{Spec}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
\phi\leq 0pt
,

(iv)

Sequences
X_{0}\subseteq\dots\subseteq X_{n}\subseteq\dots\subseteq\mathsf{Spec}(R)
such that
\mathsf{Ass}(\Omega_{-i}(R))\subseteq X_{i}
.

Remark 5.4
.

Note that, even in the regular case above, the theory of pure-injective modules can be far from transparent. In fact, outside of the hereditary case of dimension at most one, such rings always admit a superdecomposable pure-injective module, see
[
31
, Summary 8.71]
.

The collection
(i)
of
Theorem

5.3
has an obvious symmetry: Given a hereditary
\Tor
-pair
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
, also
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})^{\top}
is a hereditary
\Tor
-pair. This is reflected by the corresponding functions in the following way.

Proposition 5.5
.

If
\phi
is the function
\mathsf{Spec}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
associated to
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
in
Theorem

5.3
, then we claim that the function associated to
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})^{\top}
is exactly
\psi(\mathfrak{p})\vcentcolon=0pt(\mathfrak{p})-\phi(\mathfrak{p})
.

Proof.

To see the claim, note first that as
\mathcal{E}
is hereditary, so is
\mathcal{C}
, and by construction
S_{\phi(\mathfrak{p})}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}};R_{\mathfrak{p}})\in\mathcal{E}
. The Koszul complex
K_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}};R_{\mathfrak{p}})
is a projective resolution of
k(\mathfrak{p})
as
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a regular ring by assumption, so
S_{j}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}};R_{\mathfrak{p}})
is a
(0pt(\mathfrak{p})-j)
-syzygy of
k(\mathfrak{p})
for every
0\leq j\leq 0pt(\mathfrak{p})
. In particular,
0pt_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}S_{j}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}};R_{\mathfrak{p}})=0pt(\mathfrak{p})-j
, so it follows from the characterisation in
Proposition

2.15
that
S_{j}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}};R_{\mathfrak{p}})\in\mathcal{C}
for
j\leq 0pt(\mathfrak{p})-\phi(\mathfrak{p})
. So the associated function to the hereditary bounded
\Tor
-pair
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})^{\top}
is exactly
0pt(\mathfrak{p})-\phi(\mathfrak{p})
, as claimed.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring, and let
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
be a hereditary
\Tor
-pair generated by a class of modules of finite flat dimension. If
R
is singular then the opposite
\Tor
-pair
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})^{\top}
is never subject to the classification
Theorem

4.17
. Indeed, towards contradiction assume that both
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})^{\top}
and
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
are generated by modules of finite flat dimension. Passing to a localisation, we can assume that
R
is a local singular ring. Then
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{d}
, where
d=\dim R
. This is a contradiction, as the singularity ensures that there is a module
M
such that
M\in\mathcal{F}^{\top}
but
M\not\in\mathcal{F}
. Indeed, we can choose
M
as a cocycle module in an acyclic, but not contractible, complex of projective
R
-modules (see
[
13
, Theorem 0]
).

For another example, let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal of
R
. Take the syzygy
\Omega_{d}(k(\mathfrak{p}))
where
d=0pt(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
. Then by Ext-Tor duality and
Lemma

4.10
,
\Omega_{d}(k(\mathfrak{p}))\in\mathcal{F}^{\top}
and
k(\mathfrak{p})
is of finite flat dimension in
\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R
if and only if
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is regular.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
R
be a regular commutative noetherian ring. The bijection of
Theorem

5.3
restricts to a bijection between:

(i)

\Tor
-pairs
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
such that both
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{C}
are definable,

(ii)

Functions
\phi\colon\mathsf{Spec}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
satisfying:

•

\phi(\mathfrak{q})=0
for any minimal prime
\mathfrak{q}
, and

•

0\leq\phi(\mathfrak{p})-\phi(\mathfrak{q})\leq 1
for all immediate inclusions
\mathfrak{q}\subsetneq\mathfrak{p}
.

Proof.

First, notice that any function
\phi
as in
(ii)
satisfies
0\leq\phi(\mathfrak{p})\leq 0pt(\mathfrak{p})
, and so corresponds to a hereditary
\Tor
-pair via
Theorem

5.3
. Indeed, given any
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)
, consider a chain of immediate inclusions
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{q}_{0}\subsetneq\mathfrak{q}_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak{q}_{n}=\mathfrak{p}
, where
\mathfrak{q}
is some minimal prime ideal. Then the assumption on
\phi
yields
0=\phi(\mathfrak{q})\leq\phi(\mathfrak{p})\leq n-\phi(\mathfrak{q})=n
, and
n=0pt(\mathfrak{p})
as
R
is catenary.

In view of
Corollary

4.20
and
Proposition

5.5
, it remains to check that the functions of
(ii)
are precisely those functions
\phi\colon\mathsf{Spec}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
of
Theorem

5.3
such that both
\phi
and
\psi=0pt-\phi
are order-preserving. Assume that
\phi
is order-preserving. Then
\psi
is order-preserving precisely if for each immediate inclusion
\mathfrak{q}\subsetneq\mathfrak{p}
of primes we have
\psi(\mathfrak{p})-\psi(\mathfrak{q})\geq 0
. We have

\psi(\mathfrak{p})-\psi(\mathfrak{q})=(0pt(\mathfrak{p})-0pt(\mathfrak{q}))-(\phi(\mathfrak{p})-\phi(\mathfrak{q}))=1-(\phi(\mathfrak{p})-\phi(\mathfrak{q})),

which shows that indeed
\psi(\mathfrak{p})-\psi(\mathfrak{q})\geq 0
if and only if
\phi(\mathfrak{p})-\phi(\mathfrak{q})\leq 1
.
∎

Corollary 5.8
.

[
19
, Theorem 16.31]

Let
R
be a regular commutative noetherian ring of Krull dimension 1 (or, equivalently, a hereditary commutative noetherian ring). Then given any
\Tor
-pair
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})^{\top}
, both
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{C}
are definable, and thus 1-cotilting classes.

Proof.

Let
\phi\colon\mathsf{Spec}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
be a function satisfying
0\leq\phi(\mathfrak{p})\leq 0pt(\mathfrak{p})
. Then the function is determined by values on maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
, with the only possible values being 0 or 1. Such a function automatically satisfies the condition
(ii)
of
Proposition

5.7
.
∎

6.
The restricted flat dimensions

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring. Christensen, Foxby and Frankild introduced the restricted homological dimensions over a commutative noetherian ring in various works, see
[
14
]
and
[
12
, Chapter 5]
. Instead of considering a
\Tor
-pair, their approach involved introducing the
large and small restricted flat dimensions
of a module, denoted
\mathsf{Rfd}
and
\mathsf{rfd}
respectively (also called the large and small restricted
\Tor
-dimensions). They are defined as follows for an
R
-module
M
over a commutative noetherian ring
R
.

\mathsf{Rfd}_{R}(M)\vcentcolon=\sup\{i\geq 0\mid\Tor^{R}_{i}(F,M)\neq 0\text{ for some }F\in\mathcal{F}\}

\mathsf{rfd}_{R}(M)\vcentcolon=\sup\{i\geq 0\mid\Tor^{R}_{i}(F,M)\neq 0\text{ for some }F\in\mathcal{P}^{<\aleph_{0}}\}

We will let
\mathcal{R}\mathcal{F}_{n}
denote the modules of large restricted flat dimension at most
n
and also let
(\mathcal{F}_{(n)},\mathcal{R}\mathcal{F}_{n})^{\top}
be the hereditary
\Tor
-pair, which is generated by
n
-th yokes of modules belonging to
\mathcal{F}
. Note that this
\Tor
-pair corresponds to the function
\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\sup\{0,0pt(\mathfrak{p})-n\}
on
\mathsf{Spec}(R)
via
Theorem

4.17
, see
Proposition

4.13
.

In
[
14
]
, the authors succeed in providing a kind of analogue of the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula for the restricted flat dimensions, see
[
14
, Theorem 2.4(b)]
, which we recover in our main
Theorem

4.17
by setting
\phi\vcentcolon=0pt
.

It is straightforward to see that
\mathsf{rfd}_{R}(M)\leq\mathsf{Rfd}_{R}(M)\leq\mathsf{fd}_{R}(M)
for any
R
-module
M
. However, to determine when each of these inequalities is an equality
is less straightforward, see
[
14
]
for more details. In particular, they show that if
R
is a local ring, then
\mathsf{rfd}_{R}(M)=\mathsf{Rfd}_{R}(M)
for every (finitely generated)
R
-module
M
if and only if
R
is
almost Cohen–Macaulay
, that is if
\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}-0pt(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\leq 1
for every prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
, see
[
14
, Theorem 3.2]
. Note that
R
is almost Cohen–Macaulay if and only if
\mathsf{grade}=0pt
by
[
14
, Lemma 3.1]
.

In the next proposition, we will extend this characterisation to all noetherian rings by combining the original results from
[
14
]
with some newer results from
[
24
]
together with
Proposition

2.7
(which is
[
2
, Theorem 2.3]
). The equivalence of
(iii)
and
(iv)
is generalised in our
Corollary

4.20
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

R
is almost Cohen–Macaulay,

(ii)

\mathsf{rfd}_{R}(M)=\mathsf{Rfd}_{R}(M)
for every (finitely generated)
R
-module
M
,

(iii)

\mathcal{R}\mathcal{F}_{0}
is a definable class, that is,
\mathcal{R}\mathcal{F}_{0}
forms the right-hand class of a cotilting
\Tor
-pair generated by
\mathcal{P}^{<\aleph_{0}}(R)
,

(iv)

\mathcal{F}(R)\subseteq\varinjlim\mathcal{P}^{<\aleph_{0}}(R)
, where the equality holds if and only if
R
is of finite dimension,

(v)

\mathcal{F}_{n}(R)=\varinjlim\mathcal{P}^{<\aleph_{0}}_{n}(R)
for every
n>0
,

(vi)

\mathcal{F}_{n}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})=\varinjlim\mathcal{P}^{<\aleph_{0}}_{n}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
for every
n>0
for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)
.

Proof.

(i)\Leftrightarrow(v)\Leftrightarrow(vi)
is by
[
24
, Theorem B]
and as being almost Cohen–Macaulay is a local condition by definition.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: This holds by
[
14
, Corollary 3.3]
. Alternatively, the equivalence
(i)\Leftrightarrow(ii)
follows from
Theorem

4.17
,
Corollary

4.20
, and the above mentioned fact that
(i)
amounts to
\mathsf{grade}=0pt
. Indeed,
\mathsf{rfd}_{R}=\mathsf{Rfd}_{R}
is equivalent to
\mathcal{R}\mathcal{F}_{n}
being definable by
Corollary

4.20
. By the same result, this amounts to
0pt
being order-preserving, and thus equal to
\mathsf{grade}
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
: By
[
14
, Theorem 3.2]
we can suppose that
\mathsf{rfd}_{R}(M)=\mathsf{Rfd}_{R}(M)
for every
R
-module
M
. In particular,
\mathcal{R}\mathcal{F}_{0}
is closed under products as it is generated as a
\Tor
-orthogonal to a set of finitely presented modules, and such classes are closed under products.

(iii)\Rightarrow(vi)

It follows that for every prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
,
\mathcal{R}\mathcal{F}_{0}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})=\mathcal{R}\mathcal{F}_{0}(R)\cap\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
as
\Tor
commutes with localisations, so
\mathcal{R}\mathcal{F}_{0}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
is a definable class for each prime
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathsf{Spec}(R)
. By
Proposition

2.7
, we conclude that
\mathcal{F}_{n}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})=\varinjlim\mathcal{P}_{n}^{<\aleph_{0}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
for every prime
\mathfrak{p}
where
n\vcentcolon=\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. To see that the statement holds for every
n
, apply
[
24
, Theorem B]
.

(v)\Rightarrow(iv)
: This is clear.

(iv)\Rightarrow(vi)
: Take
F\in\mathcal{F}_{n}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{n}(R)
where
n\vcentcolon=\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. By assumption,
F=\varinjlim M_{i}
, where each
M_{i}
is a finitely presented
R
-module of finite projective dimension. As
F
is an
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module by assumption, and direct limits commute with localisation,
F\cong F\otimes_{R}R_{\mathfrak{p}}=\varinjlim(M_{i}\otimes_{R}R_{\mathfrak{p}})
, where the right-hand side is a direct limit in
\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
of finitely presented
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-modules of projective dimension at most
n
. That the statement holds for all
n
then follows by
[
24
, Theorem B and Subsection 2.13]
.
∎

For the weaker condition that
\mathcal{F}_{n}(R)=\varinjlim\mathcal{P}^{<\aleph_{0}}_{n}(R)
for a particular
n>0
, see
[
24
, Theorem B]
.

Remark 6.2
.

The modules with vanishing restricted flat dimension were also introduced in
[
43
]
with the nomenclature
strongly torsion-free
modules over an arbitrary ring. Additionally, we recover Xu’s characterisation of the strongly torsion-free modules over Gorenstein rings in our
Theorem

4.17
, see
[
43
, Theorem 5.4.8]
.

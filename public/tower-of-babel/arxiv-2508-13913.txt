Xiong Liu, Wenhua Wang

Abstract




Abstract.

Let
(\mathbb{X},d,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space,
L
a non-negative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{X})

satisfying the Davies-Gaffney estimate, and
X(\mathbb{X})
a ball quasi-Banach function space on
\mathbb{X}
satisfying some mild assumptions
with
p\in(0,\infty)
and
s_{0}\in(0,\min\{p,1\}]
.
In this article, the authors study the weak Hardy space
WH_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})
associated with
L
and
X(\mathbb{X})
,
and then give the atomic and molecular decompositions of
WH_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})
.
As applications, the authors establish the boundedness estimate of Schrödinger groups for fractional powers of
L
on
WH_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})
:

\left\|(I+L)^{-\beta/2}e^{i\tau L^{\gamma/2}}f\right\|_{WH_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})}\leq C\left(1+|\tau|\right)^{n(\frac{1}{s_{0}}-\frac{r}{2})}\|f\|_{WH_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})},

where
0<\gamma\neq 1
,
\beta\in[\gamma n(\frac{1}{s_{0}}-\frac{1}{2}),\infty)
,

r\in(0,1]
,
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, and
C>0
is a constant.
Moreover, when
(\mathbb{X},d,\mu)
is an Ahlfors
n
-regular metric measure space and
L
satisfies the Gaussian upper bound estimate,
the authors also obtain the boundedness estimate of imaginary power operators of
L
on
WH_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})
:

\left\|L^{i\tau}f\right\|_{WH_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})}\leq C\left(1+|\tau|\right)^{n(\frac{1}{s_{0}}-\frac{r}{2})}\|f\|_{WH_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})},

where
\alpha>n(\frac{1}{s_{0}}-\frac{1}{2})
,
r\in(\frac{n/s_{0}}{\alpha+n/2},1]
,
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, and
C>0
is a constant. These results are also novelty for strong Hardy spaces
H_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})
. Moreover,
all these results have a wide range of generality and, particularly, even when they are applied to weighted Lebesgue spaces, mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces, Orlicz spaces, variable Lebesgue spaces and Euclidean spaces setting, these results are also new.

1
Introduction

1.1
Background and Motivation

Consider the fractional Laplace operator
(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}
with
\gamma>0
on the Euclidean space
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and the
Schrödinger equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i\partial_{\tau}u+(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}u=0,\\
u(x,0)=f(x).\end{array}\right.

Its any solution
u
satisfies

\|u(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}=\left\|e^{i\tau(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}}f\right\|_{L^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\lesssim(1+|\tau|)^{n|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|}\|f\|_{W^{s,p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})},

where
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<\gamma\neq 1
,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\beta=\gamma n|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|
.
It is well-known that the Schrödinger flow
e^{i\tau(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}}
with
\gamma>0
is a group of isometries on

L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
but is unbounded on every other
L^{p}
space with
p\neq 2
. For
p\neq 2
, it was shown
that for
\beta=\gamma n|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|
, the operator

(I-\Delta)^{-\beta/2}e^{i\tau(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}}
is bounded on
H^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
with
p\in(0,\infty)
(see, for instance,
[
5
]
),
where
H^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
is the classical Hardy space and
H^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})=L^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
if
p\in(1,\infty)
(see, for instance,
[
19
]
).

Let
L
be a non-negative self-adjoint operator
satisfying the Davies-Gaffney estimate on spaces of homogeneous type space.
Since the flows
e^{i\tau L^{\gamma/2}}
are well defined for arbitrary nonnegative self-adjoint operators via spectral calculus, Bui et al.
[
5
]
obtained the sharp estimate for
e^{i\tau L^{\gamma/2}}

on Hardy space
H^{p}_{L}(\mathbb{X})
associated with
L
,
and showed that for any
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\in(0,1]
,

\displaystyle\left\|(I+L)^{-\beta/2}e^{i\tau L^{\gamma/2}}f\right\|_{H^{p}_{L}(\mathbb{X})}\lesssim\left(1+|\tau|\right)^{n|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|}\|f\|_{H^{p}_{L}(\mathbb{X})},

(1.1)

where
0<\gamma\neq 1
and
\beta\in[\gamma n|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|,\infty)
. In the special case of the Hermite operator
L
on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
with
n\geq 2
,
the sharp estimate (
1.1
) on Lebesgue space
L^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
with
p\in(1,\infty)
and Hardy space
H^{p}_{L}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
with
p\in(0,1]
associated with
L
was obtained in
[
8
]
.
For more information about Schrödinger flows beyond the Laplacian case, we refer the
reader to
[
6
,
7
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
.

Moreover, the study for the real-variable theory of (weak) Hardy type spaces
associated with the ball quasi-Banach function space
X
has inspired great interests in recent years
(see, for instance,
[
17
,
28
,
32
,
36
,
37
,
38
,
39
,
40
]
).
In particular, when
L
is a non-negative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

with its heat kernels satisfying the Gaussian upper bound estimate, Lin et al.
[
27
]

introduced the Hardy space
H_{X,L}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
associated with
L
and
X
, and then established the radial and the
non-tangential maximal function characterizations of
H_{X,L}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
.
Meanwhile, Lin et al.
[
29
]
introduced the Hardy space
H_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})
,
associated with
X
and an operator
L
whose heat kernel satisfies the Davies-Gaffney estimate, on spaces of homogeneous type

\mathbb{X}
, and then several characterizations of
H_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})
,
in terms of the atomic and molecular characterizations, the
boundedness of spectral multiplies, and the boundedness estimate of Schrödinger groups
when the operator
L
further satisfies the Gaussian upper bound estimate, are obtained.
Compared with quasi Banach function spaces, ball quasi Banach function spaces contain more function spaces and
hence are more general (see, for instance,
[
27
,
29
,
32
]
).

Let
(\mathbb{X},d,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space,
L
a non-negative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{X})

satisfying the Davies-Gaffney estimate, and
X(\mathbb{X})
a ball quasi-Banach function space on
\mathbb{X}
satisfying some mild assumptions with
p\in(0,\infty)
and
s_{0}\in(0,\min\{p,1\}]
. Motivated by
[
4
,
5
,
8
,
27
,
29
]
,
in this article, we first introduce the weak Hardy space
WH_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})
via the Lusin area function associated with
L
,
and then establish the atomic and the molecular characterizations of
WH_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})
.
As applications, we establish the boundedness estimate of Schrödinger groups for fractional powers of
L
on
WH_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})
:

\left\|(I+L)^{-\beta/2}e^{i\tau L^{\gamma/2}}f\right\|_{WH_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})}\leq C\left(1+|\tau|\right)^{n(\frac{1}{s_{0}}-\frac{r}{2})}\|f\|_{WH_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})},

where
0<\gamma\neq 1
,
\beta\in[\gamma n(\frac{1}{s_{0}}-\frac{1}{2}),\infty)
,

r\in(0,1]
,
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, and
C>0
is a constant.
Moreover, when
(\mathbb{X},d,\mu)
is an Ahlfors
n
-regular metric measure space,
L
satisfies the Gaussian upper bound estimate, and the kernels of the spectral multiplier operators
F(L)
satisfy an appropriate weighted
L^{2}
estimate,
we obtain the boundedness estimate of imaginary power operators of
L
on
WH_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})
:

\left\|L^{i\tau}f\right\|_{WH_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})}\leq C\left(1+|\tau|\right)^{n(\frac{1}{s_{0}}-\frac{r}{2})}\|f\|_{WH_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})},

where
\alpha>n(\frac{1}{s_{0}}-\frac{1}{2})
,
r\in(\frac{n/s_{0}}{\alpha+n/2},1]
,
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, and
C>0
is a constant.
Moreover, these results also novelty for strong Hardy spaces
H_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})
.
All these results have a wide range of generality and, particularly, even when they are applied to weighted Lebesgue spaces, mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces, Orlicz spaces, variable Lebesgue spaces and Euclidean spaces setting, these results are also new.

1.2
Main Results and Organization

In this subsection, we will state the main
results and the organization of this paper.
To state the main results of this article, now we describe the concept of spaces of homogeneous type initially introduced by
Coifman and Weiss
[
14
]
. Let
(\mathbb{X},d,\mu)
be a metric measure space endowed with a distance
d
and a non-negative Borel measure
\mu

satisfy the following doubling condition: there exists a constant
C_{1}\in(0,\infty)
such that, for any
x\in\mathbb{X}
and
r\in(0,\infty)
,

V(x,2r)\leq C_{1}V(x,r)<\infty,

(1.2)

where
V(x,r):=\mu(B(x,r))
and
B(x,r):=\{y\in\mathbb{X}:d(x,y)<r\}
. Moreover, notice that the doubling property (
1.2
) implies that
the following strong homogeneity property that, for some positive constants
C_{2}
and
n
,

V(x,\lambda r)\leq C_{2}\lambda^{n}V(x,r)

(1.3)

uniformly holds true for any
\lambda\in[1,\infty)
,
x\in\mathbb{X}
and
r\in(0,\infty)
. Further implies that there exist constants

C_{3}\in(0,\infty)
and
n_{0}\in[0,n]
such that, for any
x,y\in\mathbb{X}
and
r\in(0,\infty)
,

V(x,r)\leq C_{3}\left[1+\frac{d(x,y)}{r}\right]^{n_{0}}V(y,r).

The symbol
\mathfrak{U}(\mathbb{X})
denotes the set of all
\mu
-measurable functions on
\mathbb{X}
.
Next, we recall the concept of ball (quasi-)Banach function spaces on
\mathbb{X}
(see
[
32
, Definition 2.2 and (2.3)]
for the case of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
).

Definition 1.1
.

A quasi-normed linear space
X(\mathbb{X})\subset\mathfrak{U}(\mathbb{X})
, equipped with a
quasi-norm

\|\cdot\|_{X(\mathbb{X})}
which makes sense for all functions in
\mathfrak{U}(\mathbb{X})
, is called a
ball quasi-Banach function space
(for short, BQBF space) on
\mathbb{X}
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

for any
f\in\mathfrak{U}(\mathbb{X})
,
\|f\|_{X(\mathbb{X})}=0
if and only if
f=0

\mu
-almost everywhere;

(ii)

for any
f,g\in\mathfrak{U}(\mathbb{X})
,
|g|\leq|f|

\mu
-almost everywhere implies that

\|g\|_{X(\mathbb{X})}\leq\|f\|_{X(\mathbb{X})}
;

(iii)

for any
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathfrak{U}(\mathbb{X})
and
f\in\mathfrak{U}(\mathbb{X})
,

0\leq f_{k}\uparrow f

\mu
-almost everywhere as
k\rightarrow\infty
implies that
\|f_{k}\|_{X(\mathbb{X})}\uparrow\|f\|_{X(\mathbb{X})}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
;

(iv)

for any ball
B\subset\mathbb{X}
,
\mathbf{1}_{B}\in X(\mathbb{X})
,
where
\mathbf{1}_{B}
is defined as the
characteristic function
of
B
.

A quasi-normed linear space
X(\mathbb{X})\subset\mathfrak{U}(\mathbb{X})
is called a
ball Banach function space
(for short, BBF space) on
\mathbb{X}
if it satisfies (i)-(iv) and

(v)

for any ball
B
of
\mathbb{X}
, there exists a positive constant
C
, depending only on
B
, such that, for any
f\in X(\mathbb{X})
,

\int_{B}|f(x)|d\mu(x)\leq C\|f\|_{X(\mathbb{X})}.

The
associate space

X^{\prime}(\mathbb{X})
of a BBF space
X(\mathbb{X})
is defined by setting

X^{\prime}(\mathbb{X}):=\{f\in\mathfrak{U}(\mathbb{X}):\|f\|_{X^{\prime}(\mathbb{X})}<\infty\},

where, for any
f\in\mathfrak{U}(\mathbb{X})
,

\|f\|_{X^{\prime}(\mathbb{X})}:=\sup\left\{\|fg\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{X})}:g\in X(\mathbb{X}),\|g\|_{X(\mathbb{X})}=1\right\}

(see, for instance,
[
3
, Definition 2.1]
).

The following concept of the convexification of a BQBF space can be found in

[
32
, Definition 2.6]
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
and
X(\mathbb{X})
be a BQBF space. The
p
-convexification

X^{p}(\mathbb{X})
of
X(\mathbb{X})
is defined by setting

X^{p}(\mathbb{X}):=\left\{f\in\mathfrak{U}(\mathbb{X}):\|f\|_{X^{p}(\mathbb{X})}:=\left\||f|^{p}\right\|^{\frac{1}{p}}_{X(\mathbb{X})}<\infty\right\}.

Recall that the
Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator

\mathcal{M}
on
\mathbb{X}
is defined by setting, for any

f\in\mathfrak{U}(\mathbb{X})
and
x\in\mathbb{X}
,

\mathcal{M}(f)(x):=\sup_{B\ni x}\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}|f(y)|\,d\mu(y),

where the supremum is taken over all the balls
B
containing
x
.

Moreover, we also need the following two key assumptions on BQBF spaces.

Assumption 1.3
.

Let
X(\mathbb{X})
be a BQBF space. Assume that there exists a positive constant
p
such
that, for any given
t\in(0,p)
and
u\in(1,\infty)
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
such that, for any

\{f_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathfrak{U}(\mathbb{X})
,

\left\|\left\{\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\mathcal{M}\left(f_{j}\right)\right]^{u}\right\}^{\frac{1}{u}}\right\|_{X^{\frac{1}{t}}(\mathbb{X})}\leq C\left\|\left(\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left|f_{j}\right|^{u}\right)^{\frac{1}{u}}\right\|_{X^{\frac{1}{t}}(\mathbb{X})}.

Assumption 1.4
.

Let
X(\mathbb{X})
be a BQBF space. Assume that there exist constants
s_{0}\in(0,\infty)
and
q_{0}\in(s_{0},\infty)
such
that
X^{\frac{1}{s_{0}}}(\mathbb{X})
is a BBF space and the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator
\mathcal{M}
is
bounded on the
\frac{1}{(q_{0}/s_{0})^{\prime}}
-convexification of the associate space
(X^{\frac{1}{s_{0}}})^{\prime}(\mathbb{X})
,
where
\frac{1}{(q_{0}/s_{0})^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{q_{0}/s_{0}}=1
.

Let
L
be a non-negative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{X})
. In this article, we always make the
following assumption on the operator
L
.

Assumption 1.5
.

The semigroup
\{e^{-tL}\}_{t\in(0,\infty)}
, generated by
-L
, satisfies the
Davies-Gaffney
estimate
, that is, there exist positive constants
C
and
c
such that, for any closed sets
E,F\subset\mathbb{X}

and any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{X})
with
{\rm{\,supp\,}}(f)\subset E
,

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-tL}(f)\right\|_{L^{2}(F)}\leq C\exp\left\{-\frac{[\mathrm{dist}(E,F)]^{2}}{ct}\right\}\|f\|_{L^{2}(E)},

(1.4)

where
\mathrm{dist}(E,F):=\inf\{d(x,y):\ x\in E,\,y\in F\}
.

Let the weak Hardy spaces
WH_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})
,
WH^{M}_{X,L,\mathrm{atom}}(\mathbb{X})
, and
WH^{M,\varepsilon}_{X,L,\mathrm{mol}}(\mathbb{X})
associated with
L
and
X

be as in Definitions
2.6
,
2.9
, and
2.8
, respectively. Then we have the following atomic and molecular characterizations of
WH_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
X(\mathbb{X})
be a
{\rm{BQBF}}
space satisfying both Assumptions
1.3
and
1.4
for some

p\in(0,\infty)
,
s_{0}\in(0,\min\{p,1\}]
, and
q_{0}\in(s_{0},2]
.
Assume that
L
is a non-negative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{X})

satisfying the Davies-Gaffney estimate (
1.4
). Let
M\in(\frac{n}{2}[\frac{1}{s_{0}}-\frac{1}{2}],\infty)\cap{\mathbb{N}}

and
\varepsilon\in(\frac{n}{s_{0}},\infty)
.
Then we have

WH_{X,L}(\mathbb{X})=WH^{M}_{X,L,\mathrm{atom}}(\mathbb{X})=WH^{M,\varepsilon}_{X,L,\mathrm{mol}}(\mathbb{X})

with equivalent quasi-norms.

.

To describe the details, we begin with recalling the definitions of imaginary power operators.
We assume that

such that, for any

Let us recall the definitions of both Orlicz functions and Orlicz spaces (see e.g.

\Phi

\Phi(t)>0

t\in(0,\infty)
, and

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\Phi(t)=\infty
. For any

, an Orlicz function
\Phi
is said to be of lower (resp., upper) type
p
if
there exists a positive constant
C_{(p)}
, depending on
p
, such that, for any
t\in(0,\infty)

and
s\in(0,1)
(resp.,
s\in[1,\infty)

\Phi(st)\leq C_{(p)}s^{p}\Phi(t).

In what follows, for any given
s\in(0,\infty)
, let
\Phi_{s}(t):=\Phi(t^{s})
for any
t\in(0,\infty)
.
Now, we introduce the concept of the Orlicz spaces on
\mathbb{X}
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\Phi
be an Orlicz function of both lower type

and upper

is defined to be the set of all the functions

such that

\|f\|_{L^{\Phi}(\mathbb{X})}:=\inf\left\{\lambda\in(0,\infty):\int_{\mathbb{X}}\Phi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\right)\,d\mu(x)\leq 1\right\}<\infty.

Remark 5.2
.

Notice that

is a quasi-Banach space, and a BQBF space (see e.g.
[
35
, Section 8.3]
).

In what follows, we always assume that the Orlicz function
\Phi
satisfies lower type
p_{\Phi}^{-}\in(0,\infty)
and upper
type
p_{\Phi}^{+}\in[p_{\Phi}^{-},\infty)
. When

, then

as in Definition
2.4
is reduced to the weak Orlicz space
WL^{\Phi}(\mathbb{X})
, we denote

WH_{L,X}(\mathbb{X})
,
WH^{M}_{X,L,\mathrm{atom}}(\mathbb{X})

,
WH^{\Phi,M}_{L,\mathrm{atom}}(\mathbb{X})

5.2
Variable Lebesgue Spaces

In this subsection, we recall the definition of variable Lebesgue spaces and refer the readers to
[
16
,
18
]

for more details. In what following, we say that a
\mu
-measurable function
p(\cdot):\mathbb{X}\rightarrow(0,\infty]
is a variable
exponent. For any variable exponent
p(\cdot)
, let
\widetilde{p}^{-}
denote its essential infimum and
\widetilde{p}^{+}

its essential supremum.

Definition 5.9
.

Let
p(\cdot)
be a variable exponent with
0<\widetilde{p}^{-}\leq\widetilde{p}^{+}\leq\infty
, and

\mathbb{X}_{\infty}:=\{x\in\mathbb{X}:p(x)=\infty\}.

The variable Lebesgue space
L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{X})
is defined to be the set of all the functions

f\in\mathfrak{U}(\mathbb{X})
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{X})}:=\inf\left\{\lambda\in(0,\infty):\rho_{p(\cdot)}(\frac{f}{\lambda})\leq 1\right\}<\infty,

where, for any
f\in\mathfrak{U}(X)
,

\rho_{p(\cdot)}(f):=\int_{\mathbb{X}\backslash\mathbb{X}_{\infty}}|f(x)|^{p(x)}\,d\mu(x)+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}_{\infty})}.

Let
p(\cdot)
be as the above definition. Observe that
L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{X})

is a quasi-Banach function
space and hence a BQBF space (see e.g.
[
39
, Remark 2.7(iv)]
). If

X(\mathbb{X})=L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{X})
, then
WX(\mathbb{X})
as in Definition
2.4
becomes the variable weak Lebesgue
space
WL^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{X})
.
Furthermore, we denote

WH_{L,X}(\mathbb{X})
,
WH^{M}_{X,L,\mathrm{atom}}(\mathbb{X})
, and

WH^{M,\varepsilon}_{X,L,\mathrm{mol}}(\mathbb{X})
by
WH^{p(\cdot)}_{L}(\mathbb{X})
,
WH^{p(\cdot),M}_{L,\mathrm{atom}}(\mathbb{X})
, and

WH^{p(\cdot),M,\varepsilon}_{L,\mathrm{mol}}(\mathbb{X})
, respectively.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{X}
be the fixed point. Recall that a variable exponent
p(\cdot)
, with
p^{+}<\infty
, is said to be globally log-Hölder continuous if there exist constants

C\in(0,\infty)

and
p_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}
such that, for any
x,y\in\mathbb{X}
,

|p(x)-p(y)|\leq C\frac{1}{\log(e+\frac{1}{d(x,\,y)})}

and

|p(x)-p_{\infty}|\leq C\frac{1}{\log(e+d(x,\,x_{0}))}.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
L
be a non-negative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{X})

satisfying the Davies-Gaffney estimate (
1.4
), and
p(\cdot):\mathbb{X}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be
log-Hölder continuous. Assume that
0<\widetilde{p}^{-}\leq\widetilde{p}^{+}<2
,

and

,

, and

coincide with equivalent quasi-norms.

Proof.

Take
p:=\widetilde{p}^{-}
,
q_{0}:=2
, and
s_{0}\in(0,\,\min\{1,\widetilde{p}^{-}\})
such that
M\in(\frac{n}{2}[\frac{1}{\min\{1,\widetilde{p}^{-}\}}-\frac{1}{2}],\infty)\cap\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon\in(\frac{n}{s_{0}},\infty)
.
From
[
41
, Theorem 2.7]
, we know that the BQBF space

satisfies Assumption
1.3
for above exponents.
Moreover, by
[

, Lemma 2.9]
, we obtain that

where, for any

Therefore, by the assumption that

and
[

1.4
for above exponents. Therefore, all the assumption of Theorem
1.6
are satisfied, which implies the desired result. This completes the proof of Theorem
5.10

∎

5.3
Weighted Lebesgue Spaces

In this subsection, we recall the concept of weights on
\mathbb{X}
(see e.g.

[
33
, Chapter I]
). Let
\mathfrak{B}
denote the set of all balls of
\mathbb{X}
. A locally integrable
function

is called an
\mathbf{A}_{p}(\mathbb{X})
-weight with

[w]_{\mathbf{A}_{p}(\mathbb{X})}:=\sup_{B\in\mathfrak{B}}\mu(B)^{-p}\|w\|_{L^{1}(B)}\left\|w^{-1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{1}{p-1}}(B)}<\infty,

where

For any

q_{w}
of

is defined by setting

\displaystyle q_{w}:=\inf\left\{p\in[1,\infty):w\in\mathbf{A}_{p}(\mathbb{X})\right\}.

(5.1)

Define the space:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{X})}:=\left\{f\in\mathfrak{U}(\mathbb{X}):\|f\|_{L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{X})}:\left(\int_{\mathbb{X}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\right)^{1/p}<\infty\right\}.

(5.2)

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
and
w\in\mathbf{A}_{\infty}(\mathbb{X})
. The weighted Lebesgue space

L_{w}^{p}(\mathbb{X})
is defined as
in (
5.2
). Observe that

is a BQBF space (see e.g.
[
32
, Section 7.1]
),
but not necessarily a quasi-Banach function space (see e.g.
[
37
, Remark
5.22(ii)]
on the Euclidean space case). If
X(\mathbb{X})=L_{w}^{p}(\mathbb{X})
, then

WX(\mathbb{X})
as in Definition
2.4
becomes the weighted weak Lebesgue space

WL_{w}^{p}(\mathbb{X})
.
Furthermore, we denote

WH_{L,X}(\mathbb{X})
,
WH^{M}_{X,L,\mathrm{atom}}(\mathbb{X})
, and

WH^{M,\varepsilon}_{X,L,\mathrm{mol}}(\mathbb{X})
by
WH^{p}_{w,L}(\mathbb{X})
,
WH^{p,M}_{w,L,\mathrm{atom}}(\mathbb{X})
, and

WH^{p,M,\varepsilon}_{w,L,\mathrm{mol}}(\mathbb{X})
, respectively.
The following
theorem is an application of the results obtained in the above sections to weighted
Lebesgue spaces.

.

Let
L

5.4
Mixed-Norm Lebesgue Spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

In this subsection, we apply ball quasi-Banach spaces to mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces when
\mathbb{X}:={{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
. The mixed-norm Lebesgue space
L^{\vec{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
was studied by Benedek and Panzone
[
2
]
in 1961,
which can be traced back to Hörmander
[
21
]
. Particularly, in order to meet the requirements arising in the study of the
boundedness of operators,
PDE
s, and some other analysis fields, the real-variable theory of mixed-norm function spaces has rapidly been developed in recent years (see e.g.
[
25
]
).

Definition 5.22
.

Let
\vec{p}:=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in(0,\infty]^{n}
. The mixed-norm Lebesgue space
L^{\vec{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

is defined to be the set of all the measurable functions
f
on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}

such that

\|f\|_{L^{\vec{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}:=\left\{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\cdots\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left|f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\right|^{p_{1}}\,dx_{1}\right]^{\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}}\cdots\,dx_{n}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p_{n}}}<\infty

with the usual modifications made when
p_{i}=\infty
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Moreover, let

\displaystyle\widetilde{p}^{-}:=\min\left\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\right\}\ \ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ \ \widetilde{p}^{+}:=\max\left\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\right\}.

(5.3)

Remark 5.23
.

Let
\vec{p}:=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in(0,\infty]^{n}

and both
\widetilde{p}^{-}
and
\widetilde{p}^{+}
be the same as in (
5.3
). By
Definition
2.4
, we can conclude that
L^{\vec{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
is a ball quasi-Banach space, but it is worth pointing
out that
L^{\vec{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
may not be a quasi-Banach function space (see e.g.
[
40
, Remark 7.21]
).

Theorem 5.24
.

Let
\vec{p}:=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in(0,\infty]^{n}
,
\widetilde{p}^{-}
and
\widetilde{p}^{+}
be the same as in (
5.3
),
and
L
is a non-negative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

satisfying the Davies-Gaffney estimate (
1.4
).
Assume that
0<\widetilde{p}^{-}\leq\widetilde{p}^{+}<2
,

M\in(\frac{n}{2}[\frac{1}{\min\{1,\widetilde{p}^{-}}\}-\frac{1}{2}],\infty)\cap\mathbb{N}
,
and
\varepsilon\in(\frac{n}{\min\{1,\widetilde{p}^{-}\}},\infty)
. Then the spaces

WH^{\vec{p}}_{L}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
,
WH^{\vec{p},M}_{L,\mathrm{atom}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, and

WH^{\vec{p},M,\varepsilon}_{L,\mathrm{mol}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

coincide with equivalent quasi-norms.

Proof.

Take
p:=\widetilde{p}^{-}
,
q_{0}:=2
, and
s_{0}\in(0,\,\min\{1,\widetilde{p}^{-}\})
such that
M\in(\frac{n}{2}[\frac{1}{\min\{1,\widetilde{p}^{-}\}}-\frac{1}{2}],\infty)\cap\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon\in(\frac{n}{s_{0}},\infty)
.
From
[
41
, Theorem 2.7]
, we know that the BQBF space
X(\mathbb{X})=L^{\vec{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
satisfies Assumption
1.3
for above exponents.
Moreover, by
[
41
, Lemma 2.9]
, we obtain that

\left[\left(\left[L^{\vec{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\right]^{\frac{1}{s_{0}}}\right)^{{}^{\prime}}\right]^{\frac{1}{(2/s_{0})^{{}^{\prime}}}}=L^{\frac{(\vec{p}/s_{0})^{{}^{\prime}}}{(2/s_{0})^{{}^{\prime}}}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}),

where, for any
i\in[1,n]\cap{\mathbb{N}}
,
\frac{1}{(p_{i}/s_{0})^{{}^{\prime}}}+\frac{1}{p_{i}/s_{0}}=1
.
Therefore, by the assumption that
0<\widetilde{p}^{-}\leq\widetilde{p}^{+}<2
and
[
41
, Lemma 2.5]
, we obtain that the BQBF space
X(\mathbb{X})=L^{\vec{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
satisfies Assumption
1.4
for above exponents. Therefore, the space
L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{X})
satisfies all the assumption of Theorem
1.6
, which implies the desired result. This completes the proof of Theorem
5.10
.

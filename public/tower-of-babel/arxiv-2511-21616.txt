Dissipative solutions to Stochastic 3D Euler equations
Umberto Pappalettera, Francesco Triggiano

Abstract
. We construct probabilistically strong solutions to the three-dimensional Euler equations perturbed by additive noise that are ℙ \mathbb{P} -almost surely continuous in time, Hölder in space, and satisfy the local energy inequality up to an arbitrarily large stopping time.



1.
Introduction

In this work, we consider the stochastic Euler equations on the three-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{3}:=\mathbb{R}^{3}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{3}
driven by additive noise

(SE)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}du+\mathord{{\rm div}}(u\otimes u)\,dt+\nabla p\,dt=Q^{1/2}dW,\\
\mathord{{\rm div}}(u)=0.\end{cases}

Euler equations govern the evolution of an ideal fluid and constitute one of the most important models in fluid dynamics.
The presence of a random external force in (
SE
) can help represent many phenomena perturbing the dynamics that are neglected by the deterministic equation, such as endogenous microscopic thermal effects or exogenous influences of the environment on the fluid.
First investigations on (
SE
) date back to the works
[
27
,
22
]
, where local existence of smooth solutions is proved (see also
[
1
]
for other results in dimension two).
Measure-valued solutions satisfying weak-strong uniqueness and a version of the energy inequality have been constructed in
[
2
]
, building upon results by DiPerna and Majda
[
14
]
for the deterministic Euler equations
Q=0
.
Whether solutions are unique or not remained an open problem until the work
[
18
]
by Hofmanová, Zhu, and Zhu, adapting for the first time the convex integration scheme developed by De Lellis and Székelyhidi Jr. in
[
24
]
to the stochastic case, and obtaining non-uniqueness in law for (
SE
).
After
[
18
]
made clear that convex integration techniques can be applied to stochastic equations (let us also mention
[
8
]
in this regard), a sequence of papers has appeared
[
19
,
26
,
23
,
25
]
extending the works
[
12
,
13
,
3
,
5
,
20
,
6
]
on the deterministic Euler equations.
In particular, for every
\vartheta\in(0,1/3)
, Lü, Lü, and Zhu proved in
[
25
]
existence and non-uniqueness of solutions of (
SE
) with regularity
u\in C([0,\infty),C^{\vartheta}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
almost surely, which are strictly dissipative up to an arbitrary large stopping time
\mathfrak{t}
in the sense that
u
satisfies almost surely the energy inequality

(1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|u(t\wedge\mathfrak{t})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u(s\wedge\mathfrak{t})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\int_{s\wedge\mathfrak{t}}^{t\wedge\mathfrak{t}}\langle u(r),Q^{1/2}dW_{r}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\mbox{Tr}(Q)(t\wedge\mathfrak{t}-s\wedge\mathfrak{t}),

for every
s<t
, and the inequality is strict if
s<\mathfrak{t}
.
In the present work we investigate solutions of (
SE
) that satisfy almost surely the
local energy inequality
up to time
\mathfrak{t}
:

(LEI)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}d|u|^{2}+\mathord{{\rm div}}\left(\left(\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2}+p\right)u\right)dt-\mathfrak{q}\,dt-u\cdot Q^{1/2}dW\leq 0,

where
\mathfrak{q}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}|Q^{1/2}e_{k}|^{2}
for a CONS
\{e_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of the space of
L^{2}_{x}
solenoidal vector fields,
d|u|^{2}
and
dW
are Itō stochastic differentials, and the equation is meant as an almost sure identity in the sense of distributions.
Notice that (
1.1
) can be obtained from (
LEI
) by space integration.

The importance of a local form of energy inequality towards partial regularity results for
suitable
weak solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations is known since the works of Scheffer
[
30
]
and Caffarelli, Kohn, and Niremberg
[
7
]
. Similar results are available for the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations, see
[
29
,
16
]
.
The local energy balance (
LEI
) is a natural condition to impose also on the stochastic Euler equations (
SE
), since any physically relevant
L^{3}_{t,x}
zero-viscosity limit of suitable solutions of the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations must inherit this property.
It describes the local change in kinetic energy due to spatial roughness of
u
: indeed, the equality in (
LEI
) holds for smooth solutions
u
, and in general the inequality corresponds to no local creation of kinetic energy due to irregularities of
u
.
We refer to the celebrated work of Duchon and Robert
[
15
]
for more details.

In the deterministic setting
Q=0
, existence and non-uniqueness of solutions to the system (
SE
)-(
LEI
) have been shown in the series of papers
[
11
,
21
,
10
,
17
]
.
The purpose of the present work is to provide similar results in the stochastic case
Q\neq 0
.
More specifically, we will prove the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathbb{F},\{\mathbb{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
be a stochastic basis with right-continuous and complete filtration supporting a sufficiently smooth-in-space
Q
-Wiener process
Q^{1/2}W
with zero space average and null divergence.
Let be given
T>0
,
\varkappa\in(0,1)
,
\alpha\in(0,1/7)
and
E\in C^{1}_{loc}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R})
.
Then there exist two
\{\mathbb{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
-progressively measurable stochastic processes

(1.2)

\displaystyle u:\Omega\to C([0,\infty),C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})),\qquad p:\Omega\to C([0,\infty),C^{2\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathbb{R})),

such that
(u,p)
is
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely a solution of (
SE
) on the time interval
[0,\infty)
in analytically weak sense, and a stopping time
\mathfrak{t}
satisfying
\mathbb{P}\{\mathfrak{t}\geq T\}\geq\varkappa
such that the local energy equality

(LEE)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}d|u|^{2}+\mathord{{\rm div}}\left(\left(\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2}+p\right)u\right)dt-\mathfrak{q}\,dt-u\cdot Q^{1/2}dW=-E^{\prime}dt

holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely in the sense of distributions on the time interval
[0,\mathfrak{t}]
. In particular, (
LEI
) holds strictly if
E^{\prime}>0
.
Finally, non-uniqueness in law for
(u,p)
holds within this class.

2.
Preliminaries and main iterative proposition

2.1.
Reformulation of the problem without local martingales

It is convenient to rewrite (
SE
)-(
LEE
) without the local martingale terms
Q^{1/2}dW
and
u\cdot Q^{1/2}dW
. To attain this, we reformulate the problem using the so-called Da Prato-Debussche trick, namely we decompose solutions of (
SE
) as
u=v+z
, where
z:=Q^{1/2}W
and
v
solves the random PDE

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v+\mathord{{\rm div}}\big((v+z)\otimes(v+z)\big)+\nabla p=0,\\
\mathord{{\rm div}}(v)=0.\end{cases}

A local energy inequality can be derived for
v
as follows. First, rewrite the stochastic differential

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}d|v|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}d|u-z|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}d|u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}d|z|^{2}-d(u\cdot z).

Since by assumption
z
is spatially smooth enough, Itō Formula gives

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}d|z|^{2}=z\cdot dz+\mathfrak{q}\,dt,\quad d(u\cdot z)=du\cdot z+u\cdot dz+2\mathfrak{q}\,dt,

so that the local energy equality (
LEE
) is equivalent to

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}+v\cdot\nabla p+v\cdot\mathord{{\rm div}}\big((v+z)\otimes(v+z)\big)=-E^{\prime}.

We refer to
[
29
]
for additional details.
Therefore, solutions
(u,p)
of (
SE
)-(
LEE
) are equivalent to solutions of the following system:

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v+\mathord{{\rm div}}\big((v+z)\otimes(v+z)\big)+\nabla p=0,\\
\mathord{{\rm div}}(v)=0,\\
\partial_{t}\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}+v\cdot\nabla p+v\cdot\mathord{{\rm div}}\big((v+z)\otimes(v+z)\big)=-E^{\prime}.\end{cases}

2.2.
Notation

Let
\mathbb{N}
denote the set of natural numbers, including
0
in our convention, and let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
. For a multi-index
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
of order
|\mathbf{r}|:=r_{1}+r_{2}+\dots+r_{n}
and a smooth function
f:\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
defined on a smooth domain
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
without boundary, we denote
D^{\mathbf{r}}f:=\partial_{1}^{r_{1}}\partial_{2}^{r_{2}}\dots\partial_{n}^{r_{n}}f
.
With a slight abuse of notation, in the following we identify functions defined on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{3}
as periodic functions defined on
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, and let us define the following Hölder seminorms:

(2.6)

[f]_{N}:=\max\limits_{|\mathbf{r}|=N}\|D^{\mathbf{r}}f\|_{C^{0}},\quad[f]_{N+\delta}:=\max\limits_{|\mathbf{r}|=N}\sup\limits_{x\neq y}\frac{|D^{\mathbf{r}}f(x)-D^{\mathbf{r}}f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\delta}}.

Then, the Hölder norms are defined as

(2.7)

\|f\|_{N}:=\max_{j=0,\dots,N}\{[f]_{j}\},\quad\|f\|_{N+\delta}:=\max\{\|f\|_{N},[f]_{N+\delta}\}.

Furthermore, in order to simplify the statement of many propositions here contained, we adopt the notational convention
\|f\|_{M}\equiv 0
whenever the index
M<0
.

2.3.
Preparation of the convex integration scheme

The proof of
1.1
relies on a convex integration scheme, mostly inspired by the works
[
21
,
10
,
25
]
.
Let us introduce various parameters that will allow to estimate all the objects appearing in the upcoming sections. Define for
q\in\mathbb{N}
:

(2.8)

\lambda_{q}:=\lfloor a^{b^{q}}\rfloor,\quad\overline{\delta}_{q}:=\lambda_{q}^{-2\alpha},\quad\delta_{q}:=\begin{cases}\lambda_{0}^{\gamma},\,&q=0,\\
\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}\overline{\delta}_{q}\lambda_{1}^{2\alpha},\,&q\geq 1,\end{cases}

where
a>2
will be very large,
b>1
will be chosen close to 1 and
\alpha\in(0,1/7)
indicates the Hölder regularity of the solution identified by the iterative procedure.

Let also also denote
N_{1}=n_{0}+5
, where the parameter
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
will be chosen in the following sections and will depend on the value
b
. In particular,
n_{0}
gets larger as
b
is closer to
1
.
We assume that the
Q
-Wiener process
z
takes values
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely in the Sobolev space
H^{2+N_{1}}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
with zero mean and divergence.

Let us also introduce
0<\delta\ll 1
sufficiently small such that

(2.9)

\frac{1/2+\delta}{1/2-\delta}<\frac{1-4\alpha}{3\alpha}\wedge\frac{4}{3},

and
L>1
such that the following stopping time

(2.10)

\mathfrak{t}:=\inf\left\{t\geq 0:\|z\|_{C^{1/2-\delta}([0,t],C_{x}^{N_{1}})}\geq L\right\}\wedge 2T

is larger than
T
with probability greater than
\varkappa\in(0,1)
given by the statement of
1.1
.
Notice that is always possible to find such
L
since
H^{2+N_{1}}\subset C^{N_{1}}
by Sobolev embedding.
In this way, we have a
\mathbb{P}
-almost sure control over the size of
\|z_{t}\|_{N_{1}}
on the time interval
t\in[0,\mathfrak{t}]
.
However, since
z
is temporally rough, we need to introduce a time mollification

(2.11)

z_{q}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}r_{i_{q}}(t-s)z(s)\,ds,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}

where
z(-s):=z(0)
for all
s>0
,
r
is a smooth time mollifier with support contained in
[0,1]
, and the parameter
i_{q}
is defined as

(2.12)

i_{q}:=\begin{cases}\lambda_{0}^{m_{5}}\lambda_{1}^{m_{6}},\,&q=0,\\
\lambda_{q}^{m_{1}}\lambda_{q+1}^{m_{2}}\delta_{q}^{m_{3}}\delta_{q+1}^{m_{4}},\,&q\geq 1,\end{cases}

where
(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},m_{4})=\left(1/2-\delta\right)^{-1}\left(0,0,-2,7/2\right)
and
(m_{5},m_{6})=(7\alpha/(1-2\delta),-m_{5}).

The choice of the asymmetric mollifier guarantees that
z_{q}
remains adapted to the filtration
\{\mathbb{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
, extended to negative times identically equal to
\mathbb{F}_{0}
. Hereafter, we will always adopt this convention when referring to the filtration
\{\mathbb{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
.

2.4.
Induction scheme

The main idea of
[
21
]
is to obtain a solution of Euler equations satisfying the local energy inequality as a limit of smooth solutions of approximating systems, which include a Reynolds stress tensor
R
in the momentum equation and a flux current
\varphi
in the local energy inequality, and boil down to the Euler system when
R=0
and
\phi=0
.
In this sense, the size of the
errors

R
and
\phi
give an indication of “how far” a solution of the approximating system is from being an exact solution of the Euler system.

In this work, the approximating system to (
2.5
) is slightly more involved, since we need to keep track of the random process
z
affecting the dynamics.
Throughout this work we shall deal with
probabilistically strong
solutions, i.e. our objects will be defined on the same stochastic basis
(\Omega,\mathbb{F},(\mathbb{F}_{t}),\mathbb{P})
supporting the Wiener process
z
.

Definition 2.1
.

A tuple of
\{\mathbb{F}_{t}\}
-progressively measurable smooth tensors
(v,p,R,\phi,z)
is a dissipative Euler-Reynolds flow, with energy loss
E\in C^{1}_{b}
, if it satisfies the following system

(2.13)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v+\mathord{{\rm div}}\big((v+z)\otimes(v+z)\big)+\nabla p=\mathord{{\rm div}}(R),\\
\mathord{{\rm div}}(v)=0,\\
\partial_{t}\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}+v\cdot\nabla p+v\cdot\mathord{{\rm div}}\big((v+z)\otimes(v+z)\big)=-E^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}D_{t}(\Tr(R))+\mathord{{\rm div}}(Rv)+R:\nabla z^{T}+\mathord{{\rm div}}(\phi),\end{cases}

where
D_{t}:=\partial_{t}+(v+z)\cdot\nabla
denotes the advective derivative.

We point out that our approximating Euler-Reynolds flow will solve (
2.13
) (in an analytically strong sense) also for negative times
t<0
, as we work with one-sided time mollifications in order to preserve adaptedness to the filtration
\{\mathbb{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
. We shall impose zero external noise for negative times, so that the process
(v,p,R,\phi,z)
will be deterministic for negative times (and thus progressively measurable with respect to the filtration
\{\mathbb{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
).

As usual in convex integration schemes, we set up an iterative procedure to modify a given dissipative Euler-Reynolds flow
(v_{q},p_{q},R_{q},\phi_{q},z_{q})
,
q\in\mathbb{N}
, in such a way to identify a new dissipative Euler-Reynolds flow
(v_{q+1},p_{q+1},R_{q+1},\phi_{q+1},z_{q+1})
whose errors satisfy
\|R_{q+1}\|_{0}\ll\|R_{q}\|_{0}
and
\|\phi_{q+1}\|_{0}\ll\|\phi_{q}\|_{0}
.

Lemma 2.2

(Iteration Lemma)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1/7)
and
E\in C^{1}_{b}((-\infty,2T],\mathbb{R})
. Then there exist parameters
a,b
as above,
C_{v},\overline{M}\in(1,\infty)
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that the following holds.

Assume that
(v_{q},p_{q},R_{q},\phi_{q},z_{q})
,
q\in\mathbb{N}
, is a
\{\mathbb{F}_{t}\}
-progressively measurable dissipative Euler-Reynolds flow solving (
2.13
) on the time interval
(-\infty,\mathfrak{t}]
and such that
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely the following estimates hold for all
t\leq\mathfrak{t}
:

(2.14)

\displaystyle\|v_{q}(t)\|_{N}

\displaystyle\leq C_{v}\overline{M}\lambda_{q}^{N}\delta_{q}^{1/2},

\displaystyle N\in\{1,2,\dots,n_{0}+3\},

(2.15)

\displaystyle\|p_{q}(t)\|_{N}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{M}\lambda_{q}^{N}\delta_{q},

\displaystyle\|D_{t,q}p_{q}(t)\|_{N-2}\leq\overline{M}\lambda_{q}^{N-1}\delta_{q}^{3/2},

\displaystyle N\in\{1,2,\dots,n_{0}+3\},

(2.16)

\displaystyle\|R_{q}(t)\|_{N}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{M}\lambda_{q}^{N-\gamma}\delta_{q+1},

\displaystyle\|D_{t,q}R_{q}(t)\|_{N-2}\leq\overline{M}\lambda_{q}^{N-1-\gamma}\delta_{q+1}\delta_{q}^{1/2},

\displaystyle N\in\{0,1,\dots,n_{0}+3\},

(2.17)

\displaystyle\|\phi_{q}(t)\|_{N}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{M}\lambda_{q}^{N-3\gamma/2}\delta_{q+1}^{\frac{3}{2}},

\displaystyle\|D_{t,q}\phi_{q}(t)\|_{N-1}\leq\overline{M}\lambda_{q}^{N-3\gamma/2}\delta_{q+1}^{\frac{3}{2}}\delta_{q}^{1/2},

\displaystyle N\in\{0,1,2\},

where
\gamma:=(b-1)^{2}
and
D_{t,q}:=\partial_{t}+(v_{q}+z_{q})\cdot\nabla
.

Then, there exists a new tuple
(v_{q+1},p_{q+1},R_{q+1},\phi_{q+1},z_{q+1})
of
\{\mathbb{F}_{t}\}
-progressively measurable tensors which solves (
2.13
), such that the estimates (
2.14
), (
2.15
), (
2.16
), (
2.17
) hold with
q
replaced by
q+1
and, in addition,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely for every
t\leq\mathfrak{t}
:

(2.18)

\displaystyle\|v_{q+1}-v_{q}\|_{0}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{q+1}}\|v_{q+1}-v_{q}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{v}\overline{M}\delta_{q+1}^{1/2}\lambda_{q}^{-\gamma/2},

(2.19)

\displaystyle\|p_{q+1}-p_{q}\|_{0}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{q+1}}\|p_{q+1}-p_{q}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{M}\delta_{q+1}.

We point out that, by definition of
\|\cdot\|_{-1}
and
\|\cdot\|_{-2}
, estimates (
2.15
), (
2.16
) and (
2.17
) on the advective derivatives are void in these specific cases. Notice also that
\mathfrak{t}\leq 2T

\mathbb{P}
-almost surely by definition, so that the system (
2.13
) is meaningful on the time interval
(-\infty,\mathfrak{t}]
.

Since
\delta_{q}\to 0
exponentially fast, the estimates above imply
\|R_{q}\|_{0},\|\phi_{q}\|_{0}\to 0
as
q\to\infty
.
Moreover, (
2.18
) and (
2.19
) on the increments and interpolation guarantee convergence
v_{q}\to v
and
p_{q}\to p
in a space of continuous-in-time, Hölder-in-space functions.
In fact, given the previous lemma, we can prove
1.1
arguing as follows.

Proof of
1.1
.

Step 1: Initialization
.
Let
E\in C^{1}_{loc}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R})
be as in the statement of the theorem. Then we can find an extension of
E
to negative times, still denoted
E
with a little abuse of notation, such that
C^{1}_{b}((-\infty,2T],\mathbb{R})
.
Consider the following dissipative Euler-Reynolds flow at step
q=0
:

(2.20)

(z_{0},v_{0},p_{0},R_{0},\phi_{0}):=\left(z_{0},0,\frac{|z_{0}|^{2}}{3},z_{0}\mathring{\otimes}z_{0}+\frac{2E}{3}\mathrm{Id},-\frac{|z_{0}|^{2}}{2}z_{0}\right),

where
\mathring{\otimes}

Using
\mathord{{\rm div}}(z_{0})=0
and the algebraic identity
z_{0}\otimes z_{0}:\nabla z_{0}^{T}=\mathord{{\rm div}}(z_{0}|z_{0}|^{2}/2)

2.13
) for every

.
Moreover, on the time interval

, the following estimates on the Reynolds stress are valid for every

(2.21)

\displaystyle\|R_{0}(t)\|_{N}

\displaystyle\leq L^{2}+|E(t)|\leq\lambda_{0}^{N-\gamma}(L^{2}+|E(t)|)\lambda_{0}^{\gamma},

(2.22)

\displaystyle\|D_{t,0}R_{0}(t)\|_{N-2}

\displaystyle\leq i_{0}^{-1/2-\delta}L^{2}+L^{3}+|E^{\prime}(t)|\leq\lambda_{0}^{N-1-\gamma}(L^{3}+|E^{\prime}(t)|)\lambda_{0}^{3\gamma/2},

,

\displaystyle\|\phi_{0}(t)\|_{N}\leq L^{3}\leq L^{3}\lambda_{0}^{N-3\gamma/2}\lambda_{0}^{3\gamma/2},\quad\|D_{t,0}\phi_{0}(t)\|_{N-1}\leq i_{0}^{-1/2-\delta}L^{3}+L^{4}\leq L^{4}\lambda_{0}^{N-3\gamma/2}\lambda_{0}^{2\gamma},

\overline{M}\in(1,\infty)
, depending only on

and

2.14
), (
2.15
), (
2.16
), (
2.17
) hold true
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely on the time interval
(-\infty,\mathfrak{t}]
.

Step 2: Construction of
(u_{t\wedge\mathfrak{t}},p_{t\wedge\mathfrak{t}})
.
We iteratively apply

2.2
, starting from the initial tuple
(z_{0},v_{0},p_{0},R_{0},\phi_{0})
defined in Step 1.
We obtain a sequence of dissipative Euler-Reynolds flows,

, and by (

converges

.
Similarly, by (

\mathbb{P}

.
Moreover, by construction

converges
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely to
z
uniformly on the time interval
[0,\mathfrak{t}]
, while
\|R_{q}\|_{0}
and
\|\phi_{q}\|_{0}

) and (

).
Notice that the tuple
(v,p,z)

is so, by
Lemma

2.2
. Thus, we have built a solution of the system (
2.5
) on the time interval
[0,\mathfrak{t}]
. By the discussion in
Section

(u,p)
with

solves (
SE
) and (

) on the same time interval, and has the regularity stated in

Step 2: Continuation of
(u,p)
.
The proof is similar to
[
25

.
Let
\tilde{W}_{t}:=W_{t+\mathfrak{t}}-W_{\mathfrak{t}}
be a cylindrical Wiener process on the filtration
\tilde{\mathbb{F}}_{t}:=\sigma(\{\tilde{W}_{s}\}_{s\leq t},u_{\mathfrak{t}},p_{\mathfrak{t}})

[

with initial condtion
u^{in}:=u_{\mathfrak{t}}\in C^{\beta}_{x}

\mathbb{P}
-almost surely. This produces at least two distinct solutions

The pressure fields

Then the glued processes

3.
Construction of the velocity perturbation

This section explains the construction of the perturbation

, where
(v_{q},p_{q},R_{q},\phi_{q},z_{q})
is a solution to (
2.13
). In particular,
w
is obtained by adapting the construction introduced in
[

]
to our stochastic framework.

3.1.
Mikado flows

The perturbation
w
is built by combining highly oscillating vector fields modulated by scalar amplitudes, that will be chosen to cancel out
R_{q}
and
\phi_{q}
through the following two geometric lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}^{3}
denote the space of
3\times 3
symmetric matrices, and consider a family of vectors
\mathcal{F_{R}}:=\{k_{i}\}_{i=1}^{6}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{3}
and a constant
C>0
such that

(3.1)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{6}k_{i}\otimes k_{i}=C\mathrm{Id},\quad\text{and }\{k_{i}\otimes k_{i}\}\text{ is a basis of }\mathcal{S}^{3}.

Then, there exists a positive constants
N_{0}=N_{0}(\mathcal{F_{R}})
such that for any
N\leq N_{0}
we can find functions
\{\Gamma_{k_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{6}\subset\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(S_{N},(0,\infty))
, where
S_{N}:=\{K\in\mathcal{S}^{3}:|K-Id|_{\infty}\leq N\}
, such that for every
K\in S_{N}
it holds

(3.2)

K=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{6}\Gamma_{k_{i}}^{2}(K)k_{i}\otimes k_{i}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F_{\phi}}:=\{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{3}
is an orthogonal frame and define

(3.3)

\displaystyle k_{4}:=-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}k_{i}.

Then, for any
N_{0}>0
, there exist real-valued affine functions
\{\Lambda_{k_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{4}
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that their restrictions to
\mathcal{V}_{N_{0}}:=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|u|\leq N_{0}\}
are of class
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{V}_{N_{0}},[N_{0},\infty))
and for every
u\in\mathcal{V}_{N_{0}}
it holds

(3.4)

u=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{4}\Lambda_{k_{i}}(u)k_{i}.

As in
[
10
]
, these two lemmas allow to find 27 disjoint families of directions
\{\mathcal{F}^{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}_{3}}
, indexed by equivalence classes
j\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}_{3}
(triples of integers modulo
3
), such that any
\mathcal{F}^{j}
can be decomposed as
\mathcal{F}^{j}=\mathcal{F}^{j}_{R}\cup\mathcal{F}^{j}_{\phi}
, where
\mathcal{F}^{j}_{R}
and
\mathcal{F}^{j}_{\phi}
satisfy the assumptions of
Lemmas

3.1
and
3.2
, respectively.
For any direction
f\in\mathcal{F}:=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}_{3}}\mathcal{F}^{j}
let us define

(3.5)

l_{f}:=\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}:x-\sigma f\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{3}\text{ for some }\sigma\in\mathbb{R}\},

which is nothing but the the line in direction
f
passing through the origin, but seen as subset of
\mathbb{T}^{3}
.
Moreover, le
\eta
be a positive constant and
\psi_{f}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathbb{R})
be a function such that:

(3.6)

\displaystyle\supp(\psi_{f})\subset B\left(l_{f},\frac{\eta}{10}\right),\quad f\cdot\nabla\psi_{f}=0,

namely with support concentrated in the pipe of radius
\eta/10
around the line
l_{f}
, and invariant by translations in the direction
f
. We assume the following renormalization properties:

(3.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi_{f}dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi_{f}^{3}dx=0,\quad\fint_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi_{f}^{2}dx=1,

\displaystyle\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{F}_{R};

(3.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi_{f}dx=0,\quad\fint_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi^{3}_{f}dx=1,

\displaystyle\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{F}_{\phi}.

3.2.
Partition of unity and shifts

In the following we denote
Q(x,r):=\{y\in\mathbb{T}^{3}:|y-x|_{\infty}\leq r\}
the hypercube in
\mathbb{T}^{3}
of points whose box distance from
x
is at most
r
.
Let
\chi_{0}:\mathbb{T}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\theta_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be nonnegative smooth functions and suppose
\supp(\chi_{0})\subset Q\left(0,9\pi/8\right)
with
\chi_{0}\equiv 1\,\mbox{on}\,Q\left(0,7\pi/8\right)
, and similarly
\supp(\theta_{0})\subset[-1/8,9/8]
with
\theta_{0}\equiv 1\,\mbox{on}\,[1/8,7/8]

For every

and
m\in\mathbb{Z}

\chi_{n}(x):=\chi_{0}(x-2\pi n)
and
\theta_{m}(t)=\theta_{0}(t-m)
.
The functions
\chi_{0},\theta_{0}
can be chosen in such a way that

(3.9)

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\chi_{n}^{6}(x)=1,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{T}^{3};\qquad\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\theta_{m}^{6}(t)=1,\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

Next, let
\nu:=\frac{1-7\alpha}{3\alpha}
such that
(7+\nu)\alpha<1
and
(4+2\nu)\alpha-1<-3\alpha
. Then, we introduce the parameters

(3.10)

\epsilon:=\begin{cases}C_{\epsilon}\lambda_{0}^{h_{5}}\lambda_{1}^{h_{6}},\,&q=0,\\
C_{\epsilon}\lambda_{q}^{h_{1}}\lambda_{q+1}^{h_{2}}\delta_{q}^{h_{3}}\delta_{q+1}^{h_{4}},\,&q\geq 1,\end{cases}\qquad C_{\epsilon}:=(C_{v}\overline{M}+L)^{-1}\min\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{\inf_{j}N_{0}(\mathcal{F}_{R}^{j})}{6}\right),

which represents the size of the temporale partition, and

(3.11)

\mu:=\begin{cases}\lambda_{0}^{u_{5}}\lambda_{1}^{u_{6}},\,&q=0\\
\lambda_{q}^{u_{1}}\lambda_{q+1}^{u_{2}}\delta_{q}^{u_{3}}\delta_{q+1}^{u_{4}},\,&q\geq 1\end{cases},

which represents instead the size of the spatial partition, where the exponents

\displaystyle h_{1}=u_{1}=-1,\quad h_{2}=u_{2}=0,\quad h_{3}=u_{3}=-(2+\nu),\quad u_{4}=-u_{3},\quad h_{4}=\frac{3+2\nu}{2},

\displaystyle h_{5}=u_{5}=-1/2,\quad h_{6}=u_{6}=-1/2.

Indeed, given index sets

(3.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle:=\{(m,n,f)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}^{3}\times\mathcal{F}:f\in\mathcal{F}^{n}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{R}

\displaystyle:=\{I\in\mathcal{I}:f\in\mathcal{F}_{R}^{n}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\phi}

\displaystyle:=\{I\in\mathcal{I}:f\in\mathcal{F}_{\phi}^{n}\},

then let us introduce

(3.14)

\chi_{I}(x):=\begin{cases}\chi_{n}^{2}(\frac{x}{\mu}),\quad I\in\mathcal{I}_{\phi},\\
\chi_{n}^{3}(\frac{x}{\mu}),\quad I\in\mathcal{I}_{R},\end{cases}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\theta_{I}(t):=\begin{cases}\theta_{m}^{2}(\frac{t}{\epsilon}),\quad I\in\mathcal{I}_{\phi},\\
\theta_{m}^{3}(\frac{t}{\epsilon}),\quad I\in\mathcal{I}_{R}.\end{cases}

Given a radial smooth function
\mathfrak{m}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
supported in
B(0,2)
and such that
\mathfrak{m}\equiv 1
on
\overline{B(0,1)}
, consider the standard Littlewood-Paley operators acting on Fourier transforms as follows:

(3.15)

\mathcal{F}[\mathbf{P}_{\leq 2^{j}}f](\xi):=\mathfrak{m}(\xi\,2^{-j})\mathcal{F}[f](\xi),\quad\mathcal{F}[\mathbf{P}_{>2^{j}}f](\xi):=[1-\mathfrak{m}(\xi\,2^{-j})]\mathcal{F}[f](\xi),

The symbol
\mathcal{F}
here indicated the Fourier transform and should not be confused with the set of directions introduced in
Section

3.1
.
Given the vector
(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}):=(-1,0,-\frac{4+\nu}{2},\frac{4+\nu}{2}),(e_{5},e_{6}):=(-1/2,-1/2)
, let us introduce the frequency cutoff parameter

(3.16)

\displaystyle l:=\begin{cases}\lambda_{0}^{e_{5}}\lambda_{1}^{e_{6}},\,&q=0\\
\lambda_{q}^{e_{1}}\lambda_{q+1}^{e_{2}}\delta_{q}^{e_{3}}\delta_{q+1}^{e_{4}},\quad&q\geq 1\\
\end{cases},

and denote

(3.17)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\leq l^{-1}}:=\mathbf{P}_{\leq 2^{S}},\quad S:=\sup\{j\in\mathbb{Z}\,:\,2^{j}\leq l^{-1}\}.

Incidentally, we also define here the Littlewood-Paley operator projecting on the frequency shell of size approximately equal to

(3.18)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{2^{j}}f:=\mathbf{P}_{>2^{j-1}}-\mathbf{P}_{>2^{j}},

that will be useful later.
Then we define the spatial regularization of our velocity fields in the following way:

(3.19)

v_{l}:=\mathbf{P}_{\leq l^{-1}}[v_{q}],\quad z_{l}:=\mathbf{P}_{\leq l^{-1}}[z_{q}],\quad u_{l}:=v_{l}+z_{l}.

Denote

the advective derivative with respect to the velocity field

.
Finally, for any

, let us consider the flow
\xi_{I}
solving

(3.20)

\begin{cases}D_{t,l}\xi_{I}(t,x)=0,\\
\xi_{I}(\epsilon(m-1/8),x)=x.\end{cases}

3.3.
Perturbation of the velocity

The principal part of the perturbation

w_{o}(t,x):=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}\theta_{I}(t)\chi_{I}(\xi_{I}(t,x))a_{I}(t,x)\nabla\xi_{I}(t,x)^{-1}f_{I}\psi_{I}(\lambda_{q+1}\xi_{I}(t,x))=:\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}w_{I}^{o}(t,x),

The amplitudes

(i.e.

for some

For every

The proof of the existence of such a family of shifts is a simple adaptation of
[

As in
[

]

where (

(3.24)

\displaystyle w_{o}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\setminus\{0\}}b_{m,k}e^{i\lambda_{q+1}\xi_{m}(x,t)\cdot k},

(3.25)

\displaystyle w_{o}\otimes w_{o}

\displaystyle=c_{0}+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\setminus\{0\}}c_{m,k}e^{i\lambda_{q+1}\xi_{m}(x,t)\cdot k},

(3.26)

\displaystyle\frac{|w_{o}|^{2}}{2}w_{o}

\displaystyle=d_{0}+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\setminus\{0\}}d_{m,k}e^{i\lambda_{q+1}\xi_{m}(x,t)\cdot k},

where the coefficients

are given by

(3.27)

\displaystyle b_{m,k}=\theta_{m}(t)\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{m}}\chi_{I}(\xi_{I}(t,x))a_{I}(x,t)\overline{f_{I}}(x,t)\,\mathring{b}_{I,k},

with

whereas the zero modes

\displaystyle c_{0}=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}\theta^{2}_{I}(t)\chi_{I}^{2}(\xi(x,t))a_{I}^{2}(t,x)\left(\overline{f_{I}}\otimes\overline{f_{I}}\right)(t,x)\fint_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi_{I}(t,y)^{2}dy,

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\theta_{m}^{6}(t)\chi_{n}^{6}(\xi_{m}(x,t))\left[\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{m,n,R}}a_{I}^{2}(t,x)\nabla\xi_{I}^{-1}(t,x)(f_{I}\otimes f_{I})\nabla\xi_{I}^{-T}(t,x)+M_{m,n}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\theta_{m}^{6}(t)\chi_{n}^{6}(\xi_{m}(x,t))\left[\rho\nabla\xi_{I}^{-1}\left(\nabla\xi_{I}\nabla\xi_{I}^{T}-\frac{\nabla\xi_{I}(R_{l}-M_{m,n})\nabla\xi_{I}^{T}}{\rho}\right)\nabla\xi_{I}^{-T}+M_{m,n}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\theta_{m}^{6}(t)\chi_{n}^{6}(\xi_{m}(x,t))\left[\rho\mathrm{Id}-R_{l}\right]=\rho\mathrm{Id}-R_{l},

where the second to last equality comes from

under the additional assumption

(3.46)

\displaystyle\epsilon\|u_{l}(t)\|_{1}\leq\frac{\inf_{j}N_{0}(\mathcal{F}_{R}^{j})}{6}.

3.5.
Estimates on the velocity perturbation

With the construction above, we are able to control the size of the perturbation

, as stated in the following key result. Since its proof is highly technical and involved, the reader could skip it at a first reading without losing much insight on the remainder of this work.
In brief, the main takeaway of this result is that taking the advective derivative

effectively “costs” a factor
\epsilon^{-1}
, whereas a space derivative “costs” a factor

Proposition 3.3
.

For
s\in\{0,1,2\}
,
N\leq n_{0}+3
and for every

, it holds

(3.47)

\displaystyle\epsilon^{s}\|D_{t,l}^{s}w_{o}(t)\|_{N}\lesssim\lambda_{q+1}^{N}\rho^{1/2},

(3.48)

\displaystyle\epsilon^{s}\|D_{t,l}^{s}w_{c}(t)\|_{N}\lesssim(\mu\lambda_{q+1})^{-1}\lambda_{q+1}^{N}\rho^{1/2},

and moreover there exists a finite constant
C_{2}
, independent on
C_{v}
,
\overline{M}
and

, such that

(3.49)

\|w(t)\|_{N}\leq C_{2}\,\lambda_{q+1}^{N}\rho^{1/2}.

In addition, for all
N\leq n_{0}+3
and

, we have

(3.50)

\displaystyle\|u_{q+1}(t)-u_{q}(t)\|_{N}\lesssim\,\lambda_{q+1}^{N}\rho^{1/2},

(3.51)

\displaystyle\|u_{q+1}(t)-u_{l}(t)\|_{N}\lesssim\,\lambda_{q+1}^{N}\rho^{1/2}.

3.5.1.
Proof of

: Preliminary estimates

We report some estimates that will be helpful in the proof of

Lemma 3.4
.

For any

and

\displaystyle\|z_{l}(t)-z_{q+1}(t)\|_{N}\vee\|z_{q}(t)-z_{q+1}(t)\|_{N}\lesssim L\,i_{q}^{1/2-\delta};

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}z_{q}(t)\|_{N}\lesssim L\,i_{q}^{-1/2-\delta}.

\displaystyle\|D_{t,q+1}(z_{q}-z_{l})(t)\|_{N}

∎

Lemma 3.6
.

For
N\leq n_{0}+1
and

\displaystyle\|D_{t,l}(u_{q}-u_{l})(t)\|_{N}+\|D_{t,l}(v_{q}-v_{l})(t)\|_{N}\lesssim l^{1-N}\lambda_{q}^{2}\delta_{q}.

Proof.

First of all, we have that

Lemma 3.7
.

For any

and

\|D_{t,l}\nabla v_{l}(t)\|_{N}+\|D_{t,l}\nabla u_{l}(t)\|_{N}\lesssim l^{-N}\lambda^{2}_{q}\delta_{q}.

Proof.

Since it holds

D_{t,l}(\nabla u_{l})=\nabla(\partial_{t}u_{l}+u_{l}\cdot\nabla u_{l})-(\nabla u_{l})^{2}=\nabla\left(\mathord{{\rm div}}(R_{l}+R_{comm})-\nabla p_{l}+\partial_{t}z_{l}\right)-(\nabla u_{l})^{2},

∎

4.
Construction of the new Reynolds stress and pressure

Based on the previous section, we have defined
v_{q+1}=v_{q}+w_{o}+w_{c}
. Now, we focus on identifying the new Reynolds stress
R_{q+1}
and pressure
p_{q+1}
.
Using
v_{q+1}=v_{q}+w_{o}+w_{c}
and the equation
\partial_{t}v_{q}+\mathord{{\rm div}}\big(u_{q}\otimes u_{q}\big)+\nabla p_{q}=\mathord{{\rm div}}\big(R_{q}\big)
,
we obtain that any putative
R_{q+1}
and
p_{q+1}
satisfying the momentum equation in (
2.13
) must necessarily satisfy also

(4.1)

\displaystyle-\nabla p_{q+1}+\mathord{{\rm div}}(R_{q+1})

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}v_{q+1}+\mathord{{\rm div}}\big(u_{q+1}\otimes u_{q+1}\big)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}w+\mathord{{\rm div}}(w_{o}\otimes w_{o}+R_{l})-\nabla p_{q}+\mathord{{\rm div}}(w_{c}\otimes w+w_{o}\otimes w_{c})

\displaystyle+u_{l}\cdot\nabla w+w\cdot\nabla u_{l}+\mathord{{\rm div}}\big((v_{q}-v_{l})\odot w+R_{q}-R_{l}\big)

\displaystyle+\mathord{{\rm div}}\big(w\odot(z_{q+1}-z_{l})+u_{q}\odot\overline{z}+\overline{z}\otimes\overline{z}\big),

where
\odot
is the symmetric tensor product,
\overline{z}:=z_{q+1}-z_{q}
and
z_{l}
is given by (
3.19
).
Now, we can exploit (
3.25
) and (
3.45
) to get (hereafter the symbol
\mathring{\odot}
stands for the traceless symmetric product)

(4.2)

\displaystyle-\nabla p_{q+1}+\mathord{{\rm div}}(R_{q+1})

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}w+u_{l}\cdot\nabla w+\mathord{{\rm div}}\Big(w_{o}\otimes w_{o}-c_{0}\Big)+w\cdot\nabla u_{l}

\displaystyle+\mathord{{\rm div}}\Big(w_{c}\otimes w+w_{o}\otimes w_{c}+w\odot(v_{q}-v_{l})+R_{q}-R_{l}\Big)

\displaystyle+\mathord{{\rm div}}\Big(w\,\mathring{\odot}\,(z_{q+1}-z_{l})+u_{q}\,\mathring{\odot}\,\overline{z}+\overline{z}\mathring{\otimes}\overline{z}\Big)

\displaystyle+\nabla\Big(\frac{2}{3}w\cdot(z_{q+1}-z_{l})+\frac{2}{3}u_{q}\cdot\overline{z}+\frac{|\overline{z}|^{2}}{3}\Big)-\nabla p_{q}.

Notice that the last line in the expression above is a gradient; hence, we define:

(4.3)

\displaystyle\overline{p}:=p_{q+1}-p_{q}:=-\Big(\frac{2}{3}w\cdot(z_{q+1}-z_{l})+\frac{2}{3}u_{q}\cdot\overline{z}+\frac{|\overline{z}|^{2}}{3}\Big).

Therefore, in order to enforce (
4.2
) we can define
R_{q+1}
as follows:

(4.4)

\displaystyle R_{q+1}

\displaystyle:=R_{A}+R_{T}+R_{S}+\frac{2}{3}f(t)\mathrm{Id},

where
f
is an arbitrary space-independent scalar function and

(4.5)

\displaystyle R_{A}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{R}\Big[\partial_{t}w+u_{l}\cdot\nabla w+w\cdot\nabla u_{l}+\mathord{{\rm div}}\Big(w_{o}\otimes w_{o}-c_{0}\Big)\Big];

(4.6)

\displaystyle R_{T}

\displaystyle:=w_{c}\otimes w+w_{o}\otimes w_{c}+w\odot(v_{q}-v_{l})+R_{q}-R_{l};

(4.7)

\displaystyle R_{S}

\displaystyle:=w\,\mathring{\odot}\,(z_{q+1}-z_{l})+u_{q}\,\mathring{\odot}\,\overline{z}+\overline{z}\mathring{\otimes}\overline{z}.

In (
4.5
) above, the operator
\mathcal{R}
denotes the anti-divergence operator (cf. for instance
[
12
, Definition 4.2]
), which to a given smooth periodic vector field
v
associates a field
\mathcal{R}v
taking values in the space of symmetric and traceless matrices and satisfying
\mathord{{\rm div}}\mathcal{R}v=v-\fint_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}v
.
In fact, the
antidivergence stress

R_{A}
denotes the part of the Reynolds stress
R_{q+1}
containing all the terms that we will estimate through properties of the the antidivergence operator
\mathcal{R}
.
Similarly, the
trace stress

R_{T}
contains all the contributions of terms having trace (except for the term
\frac{2}{3}f(t)\mathrm{Id}
), and the
stochastic stress

R_{S}
contains all the terms with an explicit dependence of the stochastic process
z
.
Our particular
f
entering in the convex integration scheme will be defined by (
5.20
) below.
It will depend on the flux current
\phi
and it will serve the purpose of canceling out the space average of several terms appearing in the equation (
5.3
) for the new current
\phi_{q+1}
.
In passing, we record the following expression for the trace of
R_{q+1}
, that we shall use later:

(4.8)

\displaystyle\Tr\big(R_{q+1}\big)=\Tr\big(R_{q}-R_{l}\big)+|w_{c}|^{2}+2w_{c}\cdot w_{o}+2(v_{q}-v_{l})\cdot w+2f(t).

4.1.
Estimates on the Reynolds stress

We denote
D_{t,q+1}:=\partial_{t}+u_{q+1}\cdot\nabla
. Our main proposition on the Reynolds stress is the following: morally speaking, taking either a space derivative or and advective derivative of the new Reynolds stress
R_{q+1}
“costs” a factor
\lambda_{q+1}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

There exists
b_{0}:=b_{0}(\alpha)>1
such that, for any

, there are parameters
\gamma:=(b-1)^{2}
,

and

sufficiently large with the following property.
For every

,

, and

the following estimates hold:

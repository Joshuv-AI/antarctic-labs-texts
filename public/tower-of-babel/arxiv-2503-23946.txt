Inflated G G -Extensions for Algebraic Number Fields
M Krithika, VIT University, Chennai

Abstract
In 2018, Legrand and Paran proved a weaker form of the Inverse Galois Problem for all Hilbertian fields and all finite groups : that is, there exist possibly non-Galois extensions over given Hilbertian base field with given finite group as the group of field automorphisms fixing the base field. For 𝐐 \mathbf{Q} it was proved earlier by M. Fried. In this paper our objective is how big the degree of such extension can be compared to the order of the automorphism group. A special case of our result shows that if the Inverse Galois problem for 𝐐 \mathbf{Q} has a solution for a finite group G G , say of order n n , then there exist algebraic number fields of degree n ​ m nm , for any m ≥ 3 m\geq 3 with the same automorphism group G G .



Keywords: Galois extension; Inverse Galois Problem; inflated extensions

1
Introduction

In field theory, two opposite extreme examples of extension fields are well-known. By extreme we mean in terms of the number of automorphism of an extension
K
fixing the base field
F
element-wise. For any Galois extension
K/F
the order of the automorphism group is equal to the degree of the extension
K
over
F
which is the maximum possible. On the other hand
K=\mathbf{Q}(2^{1/n})
, with
n
an odd number, is an extension of degree
n
over
\mathbf{Q}
, with the barest minimum number of
automorphisms, namely just the trivial automorphism. These two extremes apart we note that

K=\mathbf{Q}(2^{1/4})
is of degree
4
over
\mathbf{Q}
with exactly two automorphisms.
The focus of our investigation being the relationship between the degree of
K/F
and
and the order of
\mathrm{Aut}\,(K/F)
,
we are naturally led to the following definition.

Definition 1

For a finite separable extension
K/F
, the ratio
[K:F]/|\mathrm{Aut}\,(K/F)|

is called the inflation index. When a finite group
G
is the
group of automorphisms of a field
K
over
F
we say

K
is an inflated
G
-extension of
F
.

Here are some simple properties of this inflation index:

1.

The inflation index is a positive integer.
(This follows from Lemma
1
in Section 2 below.)

2.

If
K/F
is a Galois extension, then the inflation index is
1
.
(By abuse of terminology we call this an
uninflated extension.)

3.

Inflation index of
K
over
F
is equal to the number of
distinct

extension fields of
F

inside an algebraic closure of
{F}

that are isomorphic to
K
over
F
.

4.

The higher the inflation index the poorer
the extension is with respect to the number of automorphisms.

In some sense inflation index is a numerical measure of the degree of
departure from being a Galois extension.
The extension
\mathbf{Q}(2^{1/n})
of
\mathbf{Q}
mentioned above, under our new terminology, is an inflated
G
-extension with

(i)

inflation index
n
for
G
trivial, in case
n
is odd.

(ii)

inflation index
n/2
for
G
the cyclic group of order
2
, in case
n
is even.

The celebrated unsolved inverse Galois problem can be stated as asking if uninflated

G
-extensions exist over a given field
F
.
When we ask the weaker question of existence of inflated
G
-extensions, a result of Legrand and Paran
[
5
]
gives affirmative answer
over all Hilbertian fields
F
and all finite groups
G
. There has been solutions
for other cases by earlier authors to this problem. See
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
6
]
. As their aim was on realizing every finite group as
the automorphism group, the question
of comparing the degree of the extension to the order of the group
was not considered.
But looking at the construction of Legrand and Paran it is not hard to see that their solution has inflation index
3k
where
k
is the index of
G
as an embedded subgroup of some
S_{n}
.

In our earlier work
[
4
]
we concentrated on finding the number of roots of an irreducible polynomial that belong to the field generated by a single root, a study initiated by
Perlis
[
7
]
.
(His work seems to have gone largely unnoticed). We had shown for all number fields
F

and numbers
n,r
with
r|n
(except the case
n=2r
), there exists an algebraic number

\alpha
of relative degree
n
over
F
, with exactly
r
of its
F
-conjugates
lying in
F(\alpha)
.
This number
r
is actually the order of the group of automorphisms of

F(\alpha)
over
F
.
After becoming aware of the work of Legrand and Paran few months back,
we were led to
pursue the following question:

For a given finite group
G
of order
n
, and a number field
F
,
do there exist inflated
G
-extensions over
F
for any given inflation index?

Combining their result with the method used in our work (
[
4
]
)
we have now arrived at a positive answer to this question for inflation index
\geq 4
,
and a little more.
The precise result is below:

Main Theorem (Inflating an Uninflated or a Pre-inflated Extension)
: Let
F
be an algebraic number field and
G
be a finite group of order
n
.
Assume there exists a field
K
that is an inflated
G
-extension of
F
with
inflation index
k\geq 1
. That is,
\mathrm{Aut}\,(K/F)\cong G
and
[K:F]=k\times|G|=kn
.
Then we have

(a)

for any given
m\geq 4
there exist infinitely many inflated
G
-extensions with inflation index
mk
.

(b)

in case
F=\mathbf{Q}
, there also exist infinitely many inflated
G
-extensions with inflation index
3k
.

(c)

in case
G
is an abelian group and
F=\mathbf{Q}
,
there also exist infinitely many inflated
G
-extensions with inflation index
2k
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we collect all the preliminaries needed to prove
the main theorem. Section 3 gives the proof of our Main Theorem spread over three subsections.
The final section provides illustrative examples.

We want to emphasize that proof of our Main Theorem needs no previous knowledge of our paper mentioned above or other papers cited here. Except for using a result of Hilbert from 19th century
on the existence of
S_{n}
and
A_{n}
Galois extensions of number fields our solution needs no prerequisites.

Preliminaries

As we deal with inflated extensions the following folklore lemma in field theory needs to be
singled out as it will be used repeatedly. This is Lemma 2.1 in
[
5
]
and in
[

Lemma 1

Let
L/F
be a finite separable extension with
\tilde{L}/F
any Galois extension
of
F
containing
L
(not necessarily the Galois closure of
L
) with Galois group

G

\mathrm{Aut}\,(L/F)\cong N_{G}(H)/H
where
H\subset G
is the subgroup with
L
as its fixed field.
Further, the number of distinct conjugates of
L/F
is the index of
N_{G}(H)
in
G
.

It is well-known that for the compositum of two linearly disjoint Galois extensions, the Galois
group is the direct product of those individual Galois groups. The following is a very useful
generalization for automorphism groups when the extensions under discussion are not Galois.

Lemma 2

Let
E_{1},E_{2}
be any two Galois extensions over a field

over

.
Let their Galois groups be
G_{1},G_{2}
respectively with
H_{1},H_{2}
their subgroups.
Let

and

be the fixed fields associated to
H_{1},H_{2}
respectively. Let
N_{1},N_{2}
be normalizers of
H_{1},H_{2}
respectively in
G_{1},G_{2}
.
Then for the compositum
K_{1}K_{2}
we have

(i)

K_{1}K_{2}
is the fixed field of
H_{1}\oplus H_{2}

(ii)

\mathrm{Aut}\,(K_{1}K_{2}/F)\cong\mathrm{Aut}\,(K_{1}/F)\oplus\mathrm{Aut}\,(K_{2}/F)
.

Proof: (i) is obvious.
Statement (ii) can be proved by applying Lemma
1
for
L=E_{1}E_{2}
,
by noting
\mathrm{Gal}\,(E_{1}E_{2}/F)\cong G_{1}\oplus G_{2}

and

N_{G_{1}\oplus G_{2}}(H_{1}\oplus H_{2})=N_{G_{1}}(H_{1})\oplus N_{G_{2}}(H_{2})

F
K_{1}
K_{2}
E_{1}
K_{1}K_{2}
E_{1}E_{2}
E_{2}
H_{1}
H_{1}\oplus H_{2}
H_{2}

Our Main Theorem stated for a general finite group is very easy for the case
the group is trivial, and possibly is well-known without our jargon. Nevertheless
considering the crucial role it plays in this work we state and prove it here:

Lemma 3

For every positive integer
m\geq 3
and any number field
F
there exist
infinitely many extensions
K

of degree
m
over
F
with
\mathrm{Aut}\,(K/F)
trivial.
That is, inflated extensions for trivial group exists over all algebraic number
fields and for all inflation indices
\geq 3
.

Proof:
Clearly
m=2
needs to be avoided as quadratic extensions for number fields
are always uninflated (that is, Galois).

This is readily found inside the Galois extensions
L_{m}/F

with Galois group the alternating group
A_{m}
(or the symmetric group
S_{m}
)
which by results of Hilbert, are infinitely many.
Now look at the subgroup
A_{m-1}
(or respectively
S_{m-1}
) embedded
as isotropy of one element for the natural action of
A_{m}
(or
S_{m}
) on
m
letters.
The fixed field
K_{m}
for such a subgroup will do. As
A_{m-1}
(or
S_{m-1}
) is a maximal subgroup
of index
m
in
A_{m}
(respectively in
S_{m}
) its normalizer is seen to be itself.
Now we can apply Lemma
1
and conclude that the only automorphism of
F_{m}
over
F
is the identity map.
(Note actually that in this case we can say a little more: there are no intermediate fields
between
F_{m}
and
F
).

3
Proof of Main Results

3.1
Proof of Part (a):

First we prove under the additional hypothesis that
K/F
is Galois.
So we are in the cases with
m\geq 4
,
k=1
. Let us consider the Galois extension

L_{m}
over
F
guaranteed by Hilbert
with the alternating group
A_{m}
as its Galois group.
We claim this can be chosen to be linearly disjoint with
K
over
F
. For this purpose
it suffices to show
L_{m}\cap K=F
, as
L_{m}
is Galois over
F
.
When
m\geq 5
, as
A_{m}
is simple no intermediate field in
L_{m}
could be Galois.
Consequently the intersection

L_{m}\cap K
can only
be either
L_{m}
or
F
. For the case
m=4
, as
A_{4}
admits
a normal subgroup of index 3, and intersection could be that cubic Galois extension.
The availability of infinitely many linearly disjoint
A_{m}
-extensions of
F

ensures we can choose
L_{m}
to be linearly disjoint with
K
.
Now let
F_{m}\subset L_{m}
be the fixed field for the subgroup

A_{m-1}\subset A_{m}
. This being a maximal subgroup, its normalizer is itself
and now it follows from Lemma
1
that
\mathrm{Aut}\,(L_{m}/F)
is trivial.

Now consider the compositum
K_{m}:=KF_{m}
and regard it as a subfield of
the bigger compositum
KL_{m}
. This
K_{m}
is the fixed field for the subgroup

A_{m-1}\oplus\{e\}
of
A_{m}\oplus G
, in the Galois extension
KL_{m}/F
.
By appealing to Lemma
2
we can now see that
\mathrm{Aut}\,(K_{m})\cong(A_{m-1}\oplus G)/(A_{m-1}\oplus\{e\})\cong G
, Now by linear disjointness again the degree of the extension
K_{m}
over
F
is the product of degrees of
F_{m}
and
K
which is
mn
as required. Thus
K_{m}/F
is an
inflated
G
-extension with inflation index
m
.

This proves part (a) of our Main Theorem in the special case when
K/F
is a Galois extension.
Now we go to the general case with inflation index
k>1
,
So
K
is
not
Galois over
F
. Let
\tilde{K}
be its Galois closure over
F
. We apply Lemma
2
with
E_{1}=L_{m},E_{2}=\tilde{K}

H_{1}=A_{m-1}\cong\mathrm{Gal}\,(L_{m}/F_{m}),\ H_{2}=\mathrm{Gal}\,(\tilde{K}/K)
which yields

\mathrm{Aut}\,(KF_{m}/F)\cong\mathrm{Aut}\,(F_{m}/F)\oplus\mathrm{Aut}\,(K/F)=\{e\}\oplus G\cong G,

and the degree
KF_{m}=mk|G|=mkn
, completing the proof of part (a) in all cases.

3.2
Proof of Part (b),
m=3

As in the proof of part (a) it suffices to prove only for the case

K/\mathbf{Q}
is Galois, i.e.
k=1
.
By hypothesis we are given a Galois extension
K/\mathbf{Q}
with Galois group
G
for some finite
group
G
of order
n
. We will show how to construct extensions
K_{3}
over
\mathbf{Q}
of degree
3n
with
\mathrm{Aut}\,(K_{3}/F)\cong G
.

Let the discriminant of the Galois extension
K/\mathbf{Q}
be
\Delta
.

Case (i)
3\not|\Delta
: Then choose a prime
p
not dividing
\Delta
.
In the cubic extension
F_{3}/\mathbf{Q}
given by a root of
x^{3}-p
, the only ramified primes are
3 and
p
and so its Galois closure
L_{3}
and
K
are linearly disjoint.
As
F_{3}/\mathbf{Q}
has no non-trivial automorphism we see that
for the compositum
K_{3}=KF_{3}
has degree
3n
and its automorphism group is
\{e\}\oplus G

as subgroup of the Galois group
S_{3}\oplus G
, and so isomorphic to
G
. So
K_{3}/F
is an inflated
G
-extension with inflation index
3
.

Case (ii)
3\mid\Delta
: For primes
p>\Delta
we consider the
two Eisenstein polynomials
f(x)=x^{3}\pm p\Delta x+p\Delta
whose discriminants are

\mp 4p^{3}\Delta^{3}-27p^{2}\Delta^{2}=\mp p^{2}\Delta^{2}(4p\Delta\pm 27)
. One of these two
polynomials will have negative discriminant (hence a non-square in
\mathbf{Q}
) depending
on the sign of
\Delta
. For that choice the Galois group will be
S_{3}.

Let
F_{3}/\mathbf{Q}
be obtained by adjoining one root of this cubic. We need
L_{3}
to be linearly
disjoint with
K
over
\mathbf{Q}
. But
K\cap L_{3}
should be a Galois extension.
So the possibilities for this intersection are

\mathbf{Q},L_{3}
, or its unique quadratic subfield.
To show linear disjointness it suffices to show that the above intersection does not contain
the unique quadratic subfield of
L_{3}
.

That quadratic extension inside
L_{3}/\mathbf{Q}
is obtained by adjoining the
square root of
4p\Delta-27
. We need to choose a prime
p>\Delta
such that
4p\Delta-27\neq 3^{k}
for some
k\geq 0
then there exist a new prime which divides the discriminant and not dividing
\Delta
will not ramify in
K
so they are linearly disjoint. It suffices to show for large
p

4p\Delta-27
is not a power of 3.

We claim that such primes are at most finitely many. So assume
4p\Delta+27=3^{k}
for some
k
, equivalently
4p\Delta=3^{k}-27
(
k=0,1,2,3
is not possible). We have
4p\Delta=27(3^{(k-3)}-1)
since
\gcd(27,4p)=1

implies
\Delta
to be a multiple of
27
, but not a multiple of
higher power of
3
. We denote by
\Delta^{\prime}:=\Delta/27
, an integer coprime to 3.
We have
4p\Delta^{\prime}=3^{k-3}-1
and so
4p\Delta^{\prime}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
so
p\Delta^{\prime}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
. Since
\Delta^{\prime}
is fixed this cannot be true for all primes, proving our claim and thereby giving
us linear disjointness as desired.

Hence the compositum
K_{3}=KF_{3}
has degree
3n
and its automorphism group is
\{e\}\oplus G
as subgroup of the Galois group
S_{3}\oplus G
and is isomorphic to
G
.

3.3
Proof of Part (c),
m=2

Let
C_{n}
denote the cyclic group of order
n
written multiplicatively with
g
a generator. Let
D_{n}
denote the dihedral group of order
2n

whose elements may be listed conventionally as below:

D_{n}=\{1,x,x^{2},\cdots,x^{n-1},y,yx,\cdots,yx^{n-1}\}

Lemma 4

There exist Galois extension of
\mathbf{Q}
with Galois group
C_{n}\times D_{n}
.

Proof:

We assume the well-known result that Galois extension over
\mathbf{Q}
with
D_{n}
as Galois group exists.
Let
K_{1}
be such an extension, with discriminant
\Delta
.

Any
p^{\rm th}
-cyclotomic extension with
p
not dividing
\Delta
will be linearly disjoint with
K_{1}
.
Choose
p
such that
p\equiv 1\pmod{n}
and
p>\Delta
this is possible (by Dirichlet’s Theorem on arithmetic progression). This
p^{th}
cyclotomic extension will contain a cyclic extension
K_{2}
of degree
n
over
\mathbf{Q}
again linearly disjoint over
\mathbf{Q}
. Now the compositum
K=K_{1}K_{2}
over
\mathbf{Q}
has Galois group
D_{n}\times C_{n}
.

Before proving part (c) of our Main theorem for general abelian group
we do it for the special case of cyclic groups as below:

Lemma 5

With
K
as above a Galois extension of
\mathbf{Q}
with
D_{n}\times C_{n}
as Galois group for
H\subset D_{n}\times C_{n}
defined as
H=\{(x^{i},g^{i})\mid i=0,1,2,\cdots,(n-1)\}
the fixed field
L
of
H
,
L\subset K
is an inflated
C_{n}
-extension with inflation index
2
.

Proof:

Given
H=\{(x^{i},g^{i})\mid i=0,1,2,\cdots,(n-1)\}
we can see that

N(H)=\{(x^{i},g^{j})\mid i,j=0,1,2,\cdots,(n-1)\}
so
N(H)\cong C_{n}\oplus C_{n}\subset D_{n}\oplus C_{n}

Thus
N(H)/H\cong C_{n}
and the index of
H
in
D_{n}\oplus C_{n}
is
2n^{2}/n=2n
. So the degree of the fixed field for
H
is twice the order of its automorphism group thereby
proving the Lemma.

Let
G
be any finite abelian group we now
need to show inflated
G
-extension with inflated index
2
exists.
This will be done by some kind of induction on the number of cyclic factors in the decomposition
of the abelian group. The Lemma proved just now starts the induction.
Using the structure theorem
G
can be expressed as
G\cong C_{r}\oplus A
,
for some cyclic group
C_{r}
and some abelian group
A
.

Let
K/\mathbf{Q}
be an extension of degree
2r
with automorphism group cyclic of order
r
just constructed above, with
\widetilde{K}
its Galois closure. Now for the abelian group
A
again using Dirichlet theorem, we can find Galois extension
L/\mathbf{Q}
with
\mathrm{Gal}\,(L/\mathbf{Q})\cong A
and
L
linearly disjoint with
\widetilde{K}
. Then for the compositum,

\mathrm{Aut}\,(KL/\mathbf{Q})\cong\mathrm{Aut}\,(K/\mathbf{Q})\oplus\mathrm{Aut}\,(L/\mathbf{Q})\cong C_{r}\oplus A

And the degree is computed as twice the order of
G
.

[KL:\mathbf{Q}]=[K:\mathbf{Q}][L:\mathbf{Q}]=2r\times|A|=2|C_{r}\oplus A|=2|G|

Thus we have proved all the parts of our Main Theorem.

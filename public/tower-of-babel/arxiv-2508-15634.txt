Abstract
. We discuss the notion of submanifolds with boundary with intrinsic C 1 C^{1} regularity in sub-Riemannian Heisenberg groups and we provide some examples. Eventually, we present a Stokes’ Theorem for such submanifolds involving the integration of Rumin’s differential forms in Heisenberg groups.

1.
Introduction

In this note we summarize some of the results of
[
4
]
concerning, first, the notion of submanifolds with boundary with intrinsic
C^{1}
regularity (
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular) in sub-Riemannian Heisenberg groups and, second, the validity of a Stokes-type theorem for the integration of Heisenberg differential forms in the Rumin’s complex. One of our goals is also that of adding to the current literature some examples of
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifolds with boundary in Heisenberg groups.

Recall that, given an integer
n\geq 1
, the
Heisenberg group

\mathbb{H}^{n}
is the connected, simply connected, step 2 nilpotent Lie group associated with the Lie algebra linearly generated by elements
X_{1},\dots,X_{n},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n},T
where the only non-trivial commutation relations are given by
[X_{j},Y_{j}]=T
for every
j=1,\dots,n
.
In exponential coordinates one can identify
\mathbb{H}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}_{y}\times\mathbb{R}_{t}
so that the left-invariant vector fields read as

X_{j}=\partial_{x_{j}}-\frac{y_{j}}{2}\partial_{t},\qquad Y_{j}=\partial_{y_{j}}+\frac{x_{j}}{2}\partial_{t},\qquad T=\partial_{t}.

For
\lambda>0
the
dilations

\delta_{\lambda}(x,y,t)=(\lambda x,\lambda y,\lambda^{2}t)
define a one-parameter family of group isomorphisms. We fix a left-invariant, 1-homogeneous (with respect to dilations) and rotationally invariant (i.e., invariant with respect to rotations fixing the
t
-axis) distance
d
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
(e.g. the Carnot-Carathéodory one) and for
m\geq 1
we define the spherical Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{S}^{m}
. The Hausdorff dimension of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is
Q:=2n+2
.

In Section
2
we introduce
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifolds in Heisenberg groups with and without boundary (Definitions
1
and
2
) and we provide a few examples. Observe (Remark
4
) that it is not possible to produce smooth and
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifolds with boundary with the topology of the disk.
We begin with the most elementary examples, vertical planes (Example
3
), and then move on to less trivial cases (Examples
5
and
6
), which are compact
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifolds with boundary obtained, respectively, by lifting and rotating an horizontal curve. In Section
3
we introduce the Rumin’s complex of differential forms in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
and show that Rumin’s forms can be integrated on
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifolds, eventually stating and discussing the proof of an Heisenberg version of Stokes’ Theorem, Theorem
7
.

3.
Rumin’s complex and Stokes’ Theorem in Heisenberg groups

M. Rumin introduced in
[
15
,
16
]
a complex of differential forms in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
:

0\xrightarrow{d}\Omega^{0}_{\mathbb{H}}\xrightarrow{d}\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}\dots\xrightarrow{d}\Omega^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}\xrightarrow{D}\Omega^{n+1}_{\mathbb{H}}\xrightarrow{d}\dots\xrightarrow{d}\Omega^{2n+1}_{\mathbb{H}}\xrightarrow{d}0.

The are many evidences (see for instance
[
12
,
10
,
11
,
5
,
8
]
) that Rumin’s complex is the correct object to consider in sub-Riemannian Heisenberg groups; let us recall that the Rumin’s complex has the same cohomology as the classical De Rham’s complex.

The precise introduction of the Rumin’s complex goes beyond the scopes of this note: although the expert reader will recognize that the following presentation is an incorrect oversimplification, let us note that

•

for
0\leq k\leq 2n+1
, the space
\Omega^{k}_{\mathbb{H}}
of
Heisenberg
k
-forms
is a linear subspace of the space of smooth differential
k
-forms in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
,

•

for
k\neq n
,
d:\Omega^{k}_{\mathbb{H}}\to\Omega^{k+1}_{\mathbb{H}}
is the usual exterior differentiation operator,

•

for
k=n
, the operator
D:\Omega^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}\to\Omega^{n+1}_{\mathbb{H}}
is a suitable second-order differential operator.

For our scopes, it is important to notice that the structure of
D
is

D\omega=d(\omega+\upsilon_{\omega}),\qquad\omega\in\Omega^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}

where
\upsilon_{\omega}
is a suitable
n
-form, depending on the first-order derivatives of
\omega
, which is “vertical”, in the sense that it annihilates horizontal
n
-vectors.

We denote by
\mathcal{D}^{k}_{\mathbb{H}}
the subspace of
\Omega^{k}_{\mathbb{H}}
of Rumin’s
k
-forms with compact support; let us introduce an integration theory for Rumin’s
k
-forms
\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{k}_{\mathbb{H}}
on an oriented
k
-dimensional
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifold
S
. This is easy when
k\leq n
, because
S
is a submanifold with classical
C^{1}
regularity and one can define
{\int_{S}\omega}
by classical integration. When
k\geq n+1
,
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifolds might be fractals and clearly one cannot build upon classical integration; nonetheless, when
S
is both
C^{1}
- and
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular, then (see
[
2
,
3
,
17
]
) there exists a constant
C=C(n,k,d)
such that the classical integral of
\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{k}_{\mathbb{H}}
on
S
satisfies

\int_{S}\omega=C\int_{S}\langle\omega|t^{\mathbb{H}}_{S}\rangle d\mathcal{S}^{k+1},

where
\mathcal{S}^{h}
denotes the
h
-dimensional spherical Hausdorff measure. This suggests to define

\int_{S}\omega\coloneqq C\int_{S}\langle\omega|t^{\mathbb{H}}_{S}\rangle d\mathcal{S}^{k+1}

for every oriented
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifold
S
and every
\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{k}_{\mathbb{H}}
.

We are finally ready to state our version of Stokes’ Theorem.

Theorem 7
.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
be a
k
-dimensional oriented
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifold with boundary and
\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{k-1}_{\mathbb{H}}
. Then

\int_{S}d_{c}\omega=\int_{\partial S}\omega,

where
d_{c}=d
if
k\neq n+1
and
d_{c}=D
if
k=n+1
.

When
k\leq n
, Theorem
7
is a direct consequence of the classical Stokes’ Theorem. Therefore, Theorem
7
amounts to the study of the case
k\geq n+1
; here, one reasons by approximation and starts by observing that

•

when
k\geq n+2
and
S
is also
C^{1}
-regular, Theorem
7
follows directly from the classical Stokes’ Theorem;

•

when
k=n+1
and
S
is also
C^{1}
-regular, Theorem
7
follows from the classical Stokes’ Theorem upon observing that

\int_{S}D\omega=\int_{S}d(\omega+\upsilon_{\omega})=\int_{\partial S}\omega+\upsilon_{\omega}=\int_{\partial S}\omega,

the last equality following from the “verticality” property of
\upsilon_{\omega}
and the fact that the tangent space to
\partial S
is horizontal.

Let us provide a sketch of the proof of Theorem
7
by studying separately the cases
k\geq n+2
and
k=n+1
. Details can be found in
[
4
]
.

Sketch of the proof of Theorem
7
, case
k\geq n+2
.

The proof works by approximating
S
(in
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
) by a sequence
(S_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of

(1)

\int_{S}d_{c}\omega=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{S_{j}}d_{c}\omega=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{\partial S_{j}}\omega=\int_{\partial S}\omega.

This can be done (locally, which is enough) exploiting the following result (see
[
4
]

Proposition 8
.

Assume that
k\geq n+2
and consider a
k
-dimensional
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifold with boundary
S\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}
, let
h\coloneqq 2n+1-k
be the codimension of

. Then, for every

there exist a neighbourhood
U\ni p
and functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{h+1}\in C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}(U)
such that

(i)

\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f_{1},\dots,\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f_{h+1}
are linearly independent in
U

(ii)

U\cap S=\{q\in U:f_{1}(q)=\dots=f_{h}(q)=0,f_{h+1}(q)>0\},

(iii)

U\cap\partial S=\{q\in U:f_{1}(q)=\dots=f_{h}(q)=f_{h+1}(q)=0\}.

Moreover, the functions
f_{h},\dots,f_{h+1}
can be chosen to be of class

on
U\setminus\{q\in U:f_{1}(q)=\dots=f_{h}(q)=0\}
.

The sequence
(S_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
needed for implementing the argument in (
1

S_{j}\coloneqq\{q\in U:f_{1}(q)=1/j,\ f_{2}(q)=\dots=f_{h}(q)=0,\ f_{h+1}(q)>0\}
.
∎

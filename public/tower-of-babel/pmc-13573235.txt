Fabrication of a carbon paste electrode based on a zirconium cobalt zinc nickel oxide nanocomposite for the voltammetric detection of rutoside
Authors: Bindu A. G., Ramesh S. Bhat
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Herein, the electrochemical investigation of rutoside (RU) using a creatine-functionalized zirconium cobalt zinc nickel oxide nanocomposite-based carbon paste electrode (CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE) was carried out. The synthesis of ZrCoZnNiONC was performed using the combustion technique, and its characterisation was performed using different techniques for surface, morphological, crystal size, phase, and elemental composition analyses. The NCs were modified using amino acid-derived creatine to attain enhanced electrocatalytic behaviour towards proton and electron transfer, deprotonation, and a lower detection range. The electrochemical methods of CV (cyclic voltammetry), DPV (differential pulse voltammetry), and LSV (linear sweep voltammetry), and EIS (electrochemical impedance spectroscopy) were conducted to achieve limits of detection of 4 nM, 2 nM, and 4 nM, respectively, and the selectivity, stability, sensitivity, repeatability, and reproducibility of CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE towards RU detection were determined. The ability to detect the presence of rutoside in a lemon sample was considered for evaluating the practical applicability of CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE in food products.

Introduction

Nanocomposites (NCs) have earned an important place as advanced materials due to their unique characteristics and multifunctional properties. They are prepared by combining various metal nitrates/sulphates, resulting in enhanced nanoscale products with physical, chemical, electrical, mechanical, thermal, optical, magnetic, conductivity, and catalytic properties superior to those of individual nanoparticles; these characteristics make them particularly promising in numerous technological and scientific fields. They are materials with extensive applications in catalysis, energy storage devices, supercapacitors, batteries, photovoltaic systems, environmental remediation, food packaging, biomedical engineering, coatings, aerospace, electronics, and optoelectronic devices. This interaction provides significantly enhanced performance of NCs, making them suitable for future functional devices.1–4

Nickel oxide (NiO) is a p-type semiconductor with good electrical conductivity, redox activity, chemical stability, catalytic activity, and large surface area with abundant active sites that facilitate efficient electron–proton transport characteristics.5,6 Zinc oxide (ZnO) is an n-type semiconductor with a band gap of approximately 3.37 eV and exhibits good charge transport capacity, binding energy, and stability.7,8 Cobalt oxide (Co3O4) is an important transition metal oxide due to its outstanding catalytic and electrochemical properties. The coexistence of its oxidation states helps in redox reactions, resulting in rapid charge transfer and superior electrocatalytic activity. Co3O4 exhibits chemical stability, magnetic resistance, and semiconductor properties.9,10 Zirconium oxide (ZrO2) exhibits thermal stability, mechanical strength, corrosion resistance, conductivity, and chemical inertness. It exhibits many phases depending on temperature. The physicochemical properties are the main highlight, which strongly depend on crystal structure, particle size, and synthesis conditions.11,12

The electrochemical behaviour of transition metal oxide NCs is strongly influenced by their morphology, crystallinity, particle size, and synthesis route. There are several NC preparation methods, including the hydrothermal, solvothermal, sol–gel, co-precipitation, microwave-assisted, spray pyrolysis, sonochemical, chemical vapour deposition, microemulsion, wet chemical, and green synthesis methods.13–17 Among them, combustion synthesis has gained considerable attention due to its simplicity, rapid reaction rate, and low processing cost, and the materials thus formed will have minimal impurity, highly crystallinity and uniform particle distribution.18

Rutin (quercetin-3-O-rutinoside) is a flavonoid glycoside, also known as vitamin P and rutoside, with the molecular formula C27H30O16. It comprises a flavonol quercetin attached to the rutinose disaccharide present in fruits, vegetables, buckwheat, blackcurrant, rose hip, tobacco, medicinal plants, and various dietary supplements. It has scientific interest due to its broad spectrum of pharmacological activities, including antidiabetic, anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, antioxidant, antihypertensive, cardioprotective, neuroprotective, vasoprotective, antiulcer, and anticancer activities. In addition, it is present in pharmaceutical formulations, nutraceuticals, cosmetics, beverages, and functional food products owing to its health-promoting benefits.19–23

Many analytical methods based on ultraviolet-visible spectrophotometry, liquid chromatography, laser-induced fluorescence, high-performance liquid chromatography, electrophoresis, flow injection analysis, chemiluminescence, and amperometry have been developed for its quantitative determination. However, the analytical performance of these techniques is limited as they require sophisticated instrumentation, complex sample preparation, highly skilled personnel, and lengthy analysis times and have high operational costs. Thus, our voltammetry-based approach integrates simplicity, low cost, rapid response, sensitivity, portability, and environmentally friendly operation, offering excellent analytical performance for evaluating oxidation–reduction reactions, proton–electron transfer kinetics, and catalytic efficiency. These advantages make this approach suitable for pharmaceutical analysis, food quality monitoring and environmental, biomedical, and agricultural applications.24–28 Compared with different electrode materials, carbon-based electrodes have gained substantial attention due to their electrical conductivity, broad potential window, stability, low background current, mechanical strength, and ease of fabrication.29

In the present work, the electrochemical investigation of RU was conducted using the synthesised ZrCoZnNiONC. ZrCoZnNiONC was synthesised using the simple combustion method, and it was characterized using X-ray diffraction (XRD), energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX), field emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM), and Raman spectroscopy to investigate its morphology, structure, elemental composition, crystal size, and vibrational modes. The nanocomposite was functionalised with creatine, and CF-ZrCoZnNiONC was incorporated onto the surface of a carbon paste electrode to observe the catalytic performance. The electrochemical behaviour was investigated systematically using the techniques of cyclic voltammetry (CV), differential pulse voltammetry (DPV), linear sweep voltammetry (LSV), and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) using 0.1 M PB (phosphate buffer solution) of pH 6.2 within a PD (potential difference) range of −0.1 to 0.7 V at an SR of 0.1 V s−1, and various parameters, including pH, scan rate, and concentration, interference, repeatability, reproducibility, stability, and real-sample analysis were evaluated for assessing the systematic performance of CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE. The developed CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE platform demonstrated enhanced electron-transfer kinetics, conductivity, catalytic activity, sensitivity, selectivity, and stability for the determination of rutoside in lemon-containing food samples.

Experimental

Materials and methods

The zirconium cobalt zinc nickel oxide nanocomposite (ZrCoZnNiONC) was prepared using the sulphates of zirconium, cobalt, zinc and nickel (precursors) and glycine (fuel). The fabrication of an electrode using carbon powder and silicone oil as the base materials for an electrochemical study. Rutoside was the analyte, creatine was the modifier, potassium ferrocyanide was used as the redox probe, and the supporting electrolyte was potassium chloride. The phosphate buffer (PB) solution was prepared by dissolving disodium hydrogen phosphate and monosodium hydrogen phosphate in water and adjusting the pH to the desired value. The reagents were analytical grade chemicals with a purity of 99% and were utilized without further purification. The experimental solutions were formulated using double-distilled water, and the process was carried out at 30 °C under laboratory conditions.

ZrCoZnNiONC was synthesized through the combustion approach using a hot air oven and a muffle furnace. The nanocomposite was characterized using FESEM at NITK, India, and EDX and XRD at NITTE University, India, for the validation of its morphology, phase, and composition. A CHI-6048E workstation (USA) was utilised for electrochemical studies using a three-electrode system consisting of a carbon paste electrode as the working electrode, a standard calomel electrode as the reference electrode, and a platinum wire as the counter electrode. The EIS, CV, DPV, and LSV techniques were employed for the systematic analysis of the fabricated electrode.

Zirconium cobalt zinc nickel oxide nanocomposite preparation

The zirconium sulphate, cobalt sulphate, zinc sulphate, and nickel sulphate as precursors were taken in equal proportions and combined with glycine fuel in a precursor-to-fuel stoichiometric ratio of 2 : 1 to produce a homogeneous blend. The prepared mixture sample was heated inside the muffle furnace at 800 °C for an hour for the combustion of the sample exothermically by self-ignition to produce a nanopowder. The froths were cleaned with ethanol and double-distilled water. The final product formed was subjected to dehydration in a hot air oven to form the zirconium cobalt zinc nickel oxide nanocomposite (ZrCoZnNiONC).30

Fabrication of the creatine-functionalized zirconium cobalt zinc nickel oxide carbon paste electrode (CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE)

The fabrication of the bare carbon paste electrode was carried out by incorporating graphite powder (0.24 g) and silicon oil in a proportion of 70 : 30 in a Teflon cavity for the formation of a homogeneous mixture, followed by surface smoothening with soft paper on the electrode surface. The zirconium cobalt zinc nickel oxide nanocomposite (0.05 g) was incorporated into the carbon paste electrode to modify the electrode surface, named as the zirconium cobalt zinc nickel oxide nanocomposite carbon paste electrode (ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE). ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE was functionalized with the amino acid-derived compound creatine via 10 cycles of polymerisation by CV to obtain the creatine-functionalized zirconium cobalt zinc nickel oxide nanocomposite carbon paste electrode (CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE). The functionalization, followed by the incorporation of nanocomposites, promotes the electrocatalytic behaviour, thereby enhancing the voltammetric performance.31

The structural morphology of ZrCoZnNiONC was analysed by FESEM, and the images are shown in Fig. 1(a). The NC exhibits a cubic, grain structure with distinct, well-defined grain boundaries and a uniform grain arrangement. The uniformity of the texture specifies the structural homogeneity of the NC. The presence of voids is due to the release of gas during combustion.32

The individual elemental compositions of ZrCoZnNiONC are confirmed by EDX, as shown in Fig. 1(b). The nanocomposites consist of 24.13% zirconium, 14.98% cobalt, 17.91% zinc, 11.76% nickel, and 30.31% oxygen by weight percentage, corresponding to 12.71% zirconium, 18.63% cobalt, 15.67% zinc, 14.45% nickel, and 38.54% oxygen by atomic percentage. EIS analysis

Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was employed to examine the characteristics of charge transfer, conductivity, and resistivity of BCE and CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE in 1.0 mM K4[Fe(CN)6] and 0.1 M KCl. The impedance response was interpreted using Nyquist and Bode plots along with an equivalent electrical circuit to evaluate the interfacial electron-transfer behaviour of both electrodes. The Nyquist plots presented in Fig. 2(a) exhibit a semicircular nature in the high-frequency region, followed by a linear portion at lower frequencies. The corresponding semicircle corresponds to the charge-transfer resistance (Rct), while the linear segment represents the diffusion-controlled transport of the electroactive species. BCE displays a comparatively larger semicircle, indicating a higher charge-transfer resistance and less kinetics in the electron-transfer system. After modification with CF-ZrCoZnNiONC, the diameter of the semicircle decreases markedly, confirming a substantial reduction in Rct. This behaviour demonstrates increased conductivity, electron transport, and charge-transfer capability, which collectively contribute to superior electrocatalytic activity.

The frequency-dependent electrochemical behaviour of both electrodes was further examined using the Bode magnitude and phase plots shown in Fig. 2(c) and (d), respectively. The region of high frequencies specifies the phase angle approaching 0°, representing the dominance of solution resistance (Rs). As the frequency decreases, the phase angle gradually shifts towards 90°, indicating that the capacitive response is associated with the electrical double layer (Cdl). CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE exhibits a phase shift towards lower frequencies together with an enhanced capacitive response, suggesting increased double-layer capacitance, greater surface activity, and more efficient electron-transfer processes. Functionalization provides additional electroactive sites and facilitates rapid charge transport.41

The impedance data, comprising the solution double-layer capacitance (Cdl), inductance (L), charge-transfer resistance (Rct), resistance (Rs), and constant phase element (Q), match the equivalent circuit presented in Fig. 2(b) and are reported in Table 1. An analysis of the fitted parameters reveals that the modified electrode possesses lower Rs and Rct values together with an increased Cdl compared with the bare electrode. These findings confirm that surface modification significantly reduces the resistance to electron transfer while improving the conductivity and charge-storage characteristics. Consequently, CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE exhibits enhanced electrochemical performance, making it a suitable electrode material for sensitive and efficient voltammetric sensing applications.

Optimisation of the nanocomposite dosage

The amount of the nanocomposite integrated onto the electrode surface was optimized for achieving enhanced electrochemical response, selectivity and electron–proton transfer. The response towards the optimisation process was investigated using K4[Fe(CN)6] in KCl as the analyte and supporting electrolyte, respectively, within a PD range of −0.2 to 0.5 V to realize broad and strong peaks. The effects of the variation in the amount of nanocomposites on BCE is demonstrated in Fig. 2(e), with curves of (i) 0.02 grams, (ii) 0.05 grams, and (iii) 0.08 grams. Curve (ii) exhibits high-intensity redox peaks, indicating that 0.05 g was the optimum nanocomposite dosage. Therefore, this dosage was selected for subsequent voltammetric analyses.

Optimisation of the potential difference

The optimisation of the potential difference (PD) for the detection of RU was examined based on the electrochemical response of CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE in terms of peak current, sensitivity, and selectivity in the presence of RU. The investigation was carried out in the presence of RU in PB of pH 6.2 and 0.1 V s−1. The initial peaks at −0.2 to 0.8 V PD produced a low peak current. In the range of −0.1 to 0.7 V, an enhanced peak current is observed. The range between −0.3 and 0.6 V shows a sudden reduction in the peak. The observations in the PD range of −0.1 to 0.7 V show ideal, sharp, narrow, and high-intensity peaks. Thus, this range was suitable for assessing the electrochemical performance of CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE towards the detection of RU.42

Creatine polymerisation on ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE surface

The electrocatalytic behaviour enhancement through electrochemical polymerisation of creatine on ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE was studied in a cell containing creatine in a KCl solution within a PD range of −0.2 to 0.8 V and SR of 0.1 V s−1 through the CV technique to obtain CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE. Optimization was achieved by varying the number of electropolymerization cycles, as shown in curves 5(i), 10(ii), 15(iii), and 20(iv). The variation in the number of polymerization cycles directly influenced the peak response, thereby facilitating the optimization of the fabricated electrode, as shown in Fig. 2(f). The optimisation results specify the expected enhancement in the creatine polymer film formation on the surface of ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE, with film development leading to an increase in peak currents, conductivity, surface area, and active sites. Thus, the film generated at 10 polymerisation cycles (curve (ii)) affords CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE, which achieves significant improvements in the response of the electrode surface compared to that of the non-polymerized electrode surface. Hence, the variation in the CV cycles of polymerisation determines the electrocatalytic response through polymerisation for voltammetric applications.

Effect of the electroactive surface area of the electrode

The electroactive surface area of BCE, ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE, and CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE contributing to catalytic current was analysed using the CV, DPV, and LSV techniques. The estimation of K4[Fe(CN)6] and KCl within a PD range of −0.4 to 0.8 V at 0.1 V s−1 SR was carried out by CV (Fig. 3(a)), DPV (Fig. 3(b)), and LSV (Fig. 3(c)). BCE (curve (i)) displays fewer active sites on the surface, while the functionalization of creatine results in an enhancement in active sites in CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE (curve (ii)). CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE exhibits higher Ip and lower Ep, indicating better electrocatalytic performance compared to that of BCE. Using the Randles–Sevcik equation, the active surface area of BCE was found to be 0.031 cm2, 0.027 cm2, and 0.028 cm2 and that of CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE was found to be 0.052 cm2, 0.039 cm2, and 0.047 cm2 using the CV, DPV and LSV techniques, respectively. Thus, functionalization, along with the modification of the electrode surface, increases the surface area, catalytic performance, and electrochemical performance for effective voltammetry.43

Interference response of CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE

The sensitivity of CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE for RU was investigated via an interference study using metal ions and ascorbic acid by observing the potential or current response. The investigation was carried out in 0.1 M PB at pH 6.2 from −0.1 to 0.7 V at an SR of 0.1 V s−1 by CV, LSV, and DPV, as shown in Fig. 7(d). The different electrochemical responses are of the potential interferents: ascorbic acid (AA), calcium (Ca), hydroquinone (HQ), potassium (K), sodium (Na), and riboflavin (RB). The outcome signifies a ±5.0% error by current or potential deviations, specifying a trend towards resistance to interference. The peak signals of 0.7 for CV, 1.9 for DPV, and 1.4 for LSV by the fabricated CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE display an interference pattern that is acceptable for the determination of RU in the presence of electroactive species.

Reproducibility, repeatability, and stability of CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE

The reproducibility, repeatability, and stability of CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE were estimated for the RU analysis by CV, LSV, and DPV using PB at pH 6.2 within a PD of −0.1 to 0.7 V at an SR of 0.1 V s−1. Repeatability was demonstrated using individual solutions of PB placed on a fixed electrode. The obtained relative standard deviations (RSD) were 1.3% for CV, 1.9% for DPV, and 1.5% for LSV, indicating the repeatability of these voltammetric techniques. Reproducibility was estimated using CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE with a constant PB. The RSD values of 1.7% for CV, 2.0% for DPV, and 1.9% for LSV specify the reproducibility of the electrode. The stability of CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE was studied by observing the electrode response over several measurements. The peak exhibits its 4.1% CV, 5.1% DPV, and 4.6% LSV upon its comeback, specifying the stability of the electrode. These results validate the stability, electrocatalytic activity, and proton–electron transfer ability of CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE towards RU detection.47

Real sample analysis

The practical application of CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE on real samples was investigated using lemon utilizing PB at pH 6.2, within a PD of −0.1 to 0.7 V at an SR of 0.1 V s−1. The lemon extract preparation involved turning it to liquid, followed by purification and standard addition, and it was then investigated using DW by DPV. The concentration of 0.2 µM was used for sample detection. The electrochemical response of RU recovery increased from 95.2% to 99.5%. The outcome specifies the recovery rate of the RU in lemon detection in food preparations towards CF-ZrCoZnNiONC-CPE.

Conclusion

In the present study, a novel ZrCoZnNiONC nanocomposite-based creatine-modified electrode was developed for the detection of rutoside. ZrCoZnNiONC was synthesised via the combustion approach and characterised in terms of its size, crystallinity, yield, elemental composition, and particle morphology. An improvement in the electrochemical behaviour was noticed by the modification of the electrode surface using amino acid-derived creatine functionalized NC, resulting in enhanced conductivity, catalytic behaviour, active surface area, and electron and proton charge transfer kinetics. The voltammetry techniques of EIS, CV, DPV, and LSV conducted at pH 6.2 confirmed the optimization of various parameters, a diffusion-controlled mechanism of proton-electron transfer, and LODs of 4 nM, 2 nM, and 4 nM as per CV, LSV and DPV, respectively. The entire study demonstrates the sensitivity and linear response of the fabricated electrode towards electron–proton transfer, along with excellent repeatability, selectivity, reproducibility, and stability for RU detection, and its application in food samples was demonstrated by RU detection in lemon samples.

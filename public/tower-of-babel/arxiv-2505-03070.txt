Selmer stability in families of congruent Galois representations
Anwesh Ray

Abstract
. In this article, we study the variation of Selmer groups in families of modular Galois representations that are congruent modulo a fixed prime p ≥ 5 p\geq 5 . Motivated by analogies with Goldfeld’s conjecture on ranks in quadratic twist families of elliptic curves, we investigate the stability of Selmer groups defined over ℚ \mathbb{Q} via Greenberg’s local conditions under congruences of residual Galois representations. Let X X be a positive real number. Fix a residual representation ρ ¯ \bar{\rho} and a corresponding modular form f f of weight 2 2 and optimal level. We count the number of level-raising modular forms g g of weight 2 2 that are congruent to f f modulo p p , with level N g ≤ X N_{g}\leq X , such that the p p -rank of the Selmer groups of g g equals that of f f . Under some mild assumptions on ρ ¯ \bar{\rho} , we prove that this count grows at least as fast as X ​ ( log ⁡ X ) α − 1 X(\log X)^{\alpha-1} as X → ∞ X\to\infty , for an explicit constant α > 0 \alpha>0 . The main result is a partial generalization of theorems of Ono and Skinner on rank-zero quadratic twists to the setting of modular forms and Selmer groups.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and background

The questions studied in this article are motivated by a conjecture of Goldfeld
[
5
]
, which concerns the distribution of Mordell–Weil ranks in families of quadratic twists of a fixed elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
. Given an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
, one can consider its quadratic twists
E_{d}
, indexed by square-free integers
d
. Goldfeld conjectured that, asymptotically, 50 percent of these twists should have rank 0 and 50 percent should have rank 1, with 0 percent having rank greater than 1. This conjecture is informed by analogies with random matrix theory, the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, and probabilistic models for Selmer groups. An important feature of the quadratic twist family is that the mod-2 Galois representation remains fixed: for all
d
, one has an isomorphism
E_{d}[2]\simeq E[2]
of Galois modules. This
mod-2 congruence
implies that the 2-Selmer groups of
E_{d}
and
E
are closely related—a theme that plays a central role in the work of Klagsbrun–Mazur–Rubin
[
9
]
, Kriz–Li
[
10
]
, and Smith
[
22
,
23
]
. It is natural to study generalizations of this theme to
p
congruences between modular Galois representations. We shall work with primes
p\geq 5
. Level–raising results of Diamond–Taylor
[
3
]
provide us with parameterizations of families of
p
-congruent Galois representations. Congruences have also played a significant role in Iwasawa theory. For a prime
p
, two elliptic curves
E
and
E^{\prime}
over
\mathbb{Q}
are said to be
p
-congruent
if there exists a Galois-module isomorphism
E[p]\simeq E^{\prime}[p]
. Greenberg and Vatsal
[
6
]
showed that for such
p
-congruent curves, the Iwasawa invariants of their
p
-primary Selmer groups over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
are explicitly related.

1.2.
Main result

In this article, we study the behavior of Selmer groups in
p
-congruent families of modular Galois representations, with particular emphasis on their stability under congruences. We consider Selmer groups defined over
\mathbb{Q}
by Greenberg’s local conditions and investigate analogues of phenomena observed in the context of elliptic curves, aiming to understand how congruences between Galois representations influence the global structure of Selmer groups. The Selmer groups considered are closely related to Bloch–Kato Selmer groups, however are not the same.

We fix a prime
p\geq 5
, a
p
-adic field
\mathcal{K}
with valuation ring
\mathcal{O}
, uniformizer
\varpi
, and residue field
\kappa
. Denote by
\chi
, the
p
-adic cyclotomic character. Let

\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\kappa)

be a surjective, odd Galois representation with determinant
\bar{\chi}
, the mod-
p
reduction of
\chi
. Let
N_{\bar{\rho}}
be the prime to
p
part of the Artin conductor of
\bar{\rho}
. By results of Khare and Wintenberger
[
7
,
8
]
, there exists a weight 2 Hecke eigenform
f
of trivial nebentypus and optimal level
N_{f}=N_{\bar{\rho}}
such that
\bar{\rho}_{f}=\bar{\rho}
. Associated with
f
is a modular Galois representation
\rho_{f}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal{K}}(V_{f})\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{K})
, where
V_{f}\simeq\mathcal{K}^{2}
is the underlying vector space. There exists a Galois stable lattice
T_{f}\subset V_{f}
. We set
A_{f}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=V_{f}/T_{f}\simeq(\mathcal{K}/\mathcal{O})^{2}
. Denote by
\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A_{f}/\mathbb{Q})
the Selmer group of
f
over
\mathbb{Q}
defined by Greenberg local conditions. This is a cofinitely generated
\varpi
-primary
\mathcal{O}
-module, which contains the
\varpi
-primary Bloch–Kato Selmer group as a finite index submodule. For
X>0
, let
\mathcal{S}_{f}(X)
denote the set of natural numbers
N\leq X
such that
N=N_{g}
for some Hecke eigencuspform
g
of weight
2
satisfying:

•

\bar{\rho}_{g}\simeq\bar{\rho}_{f}
,

•

p\nmid N_{g}
,

•

\dim\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A_{g}/\mathbb{Q})[\varpi]=\dim\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A_{f}/\mathbb{Q})[\varpi]
.

Define
N_{f}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\#\mathcal{S}_{f}(X)
.

Theorem A
.

With respect to notation as above, assume that no prime
\ell\equiv\pm 1\pmod{p}
divides
N_{\bar{\rho}}
. Then,

N_{f}(X)\gg X(\log X)^{\alpha-1},

where
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{p-3}{(p-1)^{2}}
.

This theorem may be viewed as an analogue, in the setting of modular forms and Galois representations, of classical results related to Goldfeld’s conjecture. For an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
, let
N_{E}^{0}(X)
denote the number of squarefree integers
d
with
|d|<X
for which the quadratic twist
E^{(d)}
has analytic rank 0. Ono and Skinner
[
15
]
showed that
N_{E}^{0}(X)\gg\frac{X}{\log X}
. Later, Ono
[
16
]
refined this result: if
E(\mathbb{Q})[2]=0
, then there exists an explicit constant
\alpha(E)\in(0,1)
such that

N_{E}^{0}(X)\gg\frac{X}{(\log X)^{1-\alpha(E)}}.

For
2
-Selmer groups of elliptic curves in quadratic twist families of elliptic curves over number fields, results similar to the Theorem above have been proven by Mazur and Rubin (see
[
12
, Theorem 1.4]
).

Modular Galois representations and level raising

Let

Assumption 2.1

(iii)

2.4.
Selmer groups

Associated to a Hecke eigenform
f
, there are two natural Selmer groups attached to its
p
-adic Galois representation. The first is defined using Greenberg’s local condition at
p
, together with the usual unramified conditions away from
p
. This Selmer group is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, and will differ from its Iwasawa theoretic counterpart, which is defined over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. We will refer to this as the
\mathbb{Q}
-Greenberg Selmer group
of
f
. The second is the
Bloch–Kato Selmer group
, which is defined via the framework of
p
-adic Hodge theory.

Since
f
is
p
-ordinary,
V_{f}
sits in a short exact sequence of
\mathcal{K}[\operatorname{G}_{p}]
-modules,

0\rightarrow V_{f}^{+}\xrightarrow{\iota}V_{f}\xrightarrow{\pi}V_{f}^{-}\rightarrow 0,

where
V_{f}^{\pm}
are one-dimensional as
\mathcal{K}
vector spaces. The action of
\operatorname{I}_{p}
on
V_{f}^{+}
is via
\chi_{p}^{k-1}
, where
k
is the weight of
f
. On the other hand, the
\operatorname{G}_{p}
action on the quotient
V_{f}^{-}
is unramified. Setting

T_{f}^{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=T_{f}\cap V_{f}^{+}\text{ and }T_{f}^{-}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\pi(T_{f})

one arrives at an exact sequence of
\mathcal{O}[\operatorname{G}_{p}]
-modules

0\rightarrow T_{f}^{+}\rightarrow T_{f}\rightarrow T_{f}^{-}\rightarrow 0.

Likewise, we set
A_{f}^{\pm}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=V_{f}^{\pm}/T_{f}^{\pm}
and obtain the exact sequence

0\rightarrow A_{f}^{+}\rightarrow A_{f}\rightarrow A_{f}^{-}\rightarrow 0.

Given a rational prime
\ell\neq p
, define
H^{1}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},A_{f})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},A_{f})
. Define

H^{1}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},A_{f})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left\{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},A_{f})\rightarrow H^{1}(\operatorname{I}_{p},A_{f}^{-})\right\}.

(Greenberg Selmer group over
\mathbb{Q}
)
.

With respect to notation above, the Greenberg Selmer group associated to the

is defined as follows:

\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A_{f}/\mathbb{Q})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left\{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},A_{f})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\ell}\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},A_{f})}{H^{1}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},A_{f})}\right\}.

Another natural Selmer group to consider was introduced by Bloch and Kato

[

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A_{f}/\mathbb{Q})[\varpi]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},A_{f})[\varpi]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{\ell}\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},A_{f})[\varpi]}{H^{1}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},A_{f})[\varpi]}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}

The map
h
decomposes into a direct sum
h=\bigoplus_{\ell}h_{\ell}
, where

h_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},A_{f}[\varpi])}{H^{1}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},A_{f}[\varpi])}\longrightarrow\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},A_{f})[\varpi]}{H^{1}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},A_{f})[\varpi]}.

We set
\beta_{\ell}(A_{f})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\dim_{\kappa}\operatorname{ker}h_{\ell}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

The following assertions hold:

(1)

Suppose
\ell\neq p
, then there is a natural injection

(2.3)

\operatorname{ker}h_{\ell}\hookrightarrow\left(\frac{H^{0}(\operatorname{I}_{\ell},A_{f})}{\varpi H^{0}(\operatorname{I}_{\ell},A_{f})}\right)^{\operatorname{G}_{\ell}/\operatorname{I}_{\ell}}.

(2)

Let
\ell\neq p
and suppose that
\ell\nmid N_{\bar{\rho}}
(equivalently,
\bar{\rho}
is unramified at
\ell
). Then,
\beta_{\ell}(A_{f})=0
.

(3)

One has that
\beta_{p}(A_{f})=0
.

Proof.

Let us begin by proving part (1). Consider the commutative diagram

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},A_{f}[\varpi])}{H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},A_{f}[\varpi])}&\xrightarrow{h_{\ell}}&\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},A_{f})[\varpi]}{H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},A_{f})[\varpi]}\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
H^{1}(\operatorname{I}_{\ell},A_{f}[\varpi])^{\operatorname{G}_{\ell}/\operatorname{I}_{\ell}}&\xrightarrow{g_{\ell}}&H^{1}(\operatorname{I}_{\ell},A_{f})^{\operatorname{G}_{\ell}/\operatorname{I}_{\ell}}[\varpi].\end{array}

The vertical maps are injective, and therefore,
\operatorname{ker}h_{\ell}
injects into
\operatorname{ker}g_{\ell}
. From the Kummer sequence,

\operatorname{ker}g_{\ell}\simeq\left(\frac{H^{0}(\operatorname{I}_{\ell},A_{f})}{\varpi H^{0}(\operatorname{I}_{\ell},A_{f})}\right)^{\operatorname{G}_{\ell}/\operatorname{I}_{\ell}},

from which the result follows.

Next, we prove (2). Since the action of
\operatorname{I}_{\ell}
on
A_{f}
is trivial, we find that
H^{0}(\operatorname{I}_{\ell},A_{f})=A_{f}
. Since
A_{f}
is
\varpi
-divisible,
A_{f}/\varpi A_{f}=0
and thus part (2) follows from (1).

In order to prove part (3), it suffices to show that the map

H^{1}(\operatorname{I}_{p},A_{f}^{-}[\varpi])\rightarrow H^{1}(\operatorname{I}_{p},A_{f}^{-})

is injective. By the Kummer sequence, its kernel is isomorphic to
\frac{H^{0}(\operatorname{I}_{p},A_{f}^{-})}{\varpi H^{0}(\operatorname{I}_{p},A_{f}^{-})}
. Since the action of
\operatorname{I}_{p}
on
A_{f}^{-}
is trivial, and
A_{f}^{-}
is
\varpi
-divisible, we deduce that
h_{p}
is injective. Consequently
\beta_{p}(A_{f})=0
, proving (3).∎

Proposition 2.8
.

With the notation as above, one has

\dim_{\kappa}\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A_{f}/\mathbb{Q})[\varpi]\leq\dim_{\kappa}\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A_{f}[\varpi]/\mathbb{Q})+\sum_{\ell}\beta_{\ell}(A_{f}).

Proof.

In (
2.2
), let
h^{\prime}
denote the restriction of
h
to the image of
\Phi
. Applying the snake lemma yields an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\ker\alpha\longrightarrow\ker g\longrightarrow\ker h^{\prime}\longrightarrow\operatorname{coker}\alpha\longrightarrow\operatorname{coker}g.

Since
A_{f}[\varpi]
is irreducible as a
\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
-module, it follows that
H^{0}(\mathbb{Q},A_{f}[\varpi])=0
. Thus, the Kummer sequence shows that
g
is an isomorphism. In particular,
\alpha
is injective and
\operatorname{coker}\alpha
injects into
\ker h
.

There is therefore a left exact sequence

(2.4)

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A_{f}[\varpi]/\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A_{f}/\mathbb{Q})[\varpi]\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\ell}\ker h_{\ell},

from which the claimed inequality follows.
∎

A similar strategy yields lower bounds for the dimension of
\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A_{f}/\mathbb{Q})[\varpi]
over
\kappa
. Let
S
denote the set of primes dividing
N_{f}p
. For each
\ell\in S
, define the local condition
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=H^{1}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},A_{f}[\varpi])
, so that the associated Selmer group is given by

H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q},A_{f}[\varpi])=\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A_{f}[\varpi]/\mathbb{Q}).

One defines the dual Selmer group by setting

\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr},\perp}(A_{f}[\varpi]^{*}/\mathbb{Q})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}^{\perp}}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q},A_{f}[\varpi]^{*}),

as in (
2.1
). While the result that follows is not used in the proof of the main theorem, it is of independent interest.

Lemma 2.9
.

With respect to the above notation,

\begin{split}&\dim_{\kappa}\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A_{f}/\mathbb{Q})[\varpi]\geq\sum_{\ell\in S}\beta_{\ell}(A_{f})+\dim_{\kappa}H^{1}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},A_{f}[\varpi])\\
-&\dim_{\kappa}H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},A_{f}[\varpi])-\dim_{\kappa}H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},A_{f}[\varpi])+\dim_{\kappa}\Sh_{S}^{2}(A_{f}[\varpi]).\end{split}

Proof.

From (
2.2
) and the arguments in the proof of Proposition
2.8
, there is a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A_{f}[\varpi]/\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A_{f}/\mathbb{Q})[\varpi]\longrightarrow\ker h^{\prime}\rightarrow 0.

From the Poitou–Tate sequence (cf.
[
24
, p. 555, line 7]
), we find that

\dim_{\kappa}\operatorname{ker}h\leq\dim_{\kappa}\operatorname{ker}h^{\prime}+\dim_{\kappa}\operatorname{Sel}_{Gr,\perp}(A_{f}[\varpi]^{*}/\mathbb{Q})-\dim_{\kappa}\Sh_{S}^{2}(A_{f}[\varpi]).

Thus, one finds that

(2.5)

\begin{split}&\dim_{\kappa}\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A_{f}/\mathbb{Q})[\varpi]=\dim_{\kappa}\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A_{f}[\varpi]/\mathbb{Q})+\dim_{\kappa}\ker h^{\prime}\\
\geq&\dim_{\kappa}\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A_{f}[\varpi]/\mathbb{Q})-\dim_{\kappa}\operatorname{Sel}_{Gr,\perp}(A_{f}[\varpi]^{*}/\mathbb{Q})+\sum_{\ell\in S}\dim_{\kappa}\operatorname{ker}h_{\ell}+\dim_{\kappa}\Sh_{S}^{2}(A_{f}[\varpi]).\end{split}

It follows from Wiles’ formula
[
13
, Theorem 8.7.9]
that

(2.6)

\begin{split}&\dim_{\kappa}\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A_{f}[\varpi]/\mathbb{Q})-\dim_{\kappa}\operatorname{Sel}_{Gr,\perp}(A_{f}[\varpi]^{*}/\mathbb{Q})\\
=&\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}H^{0}(\mathbb{Q},A_{f}[\varpi])-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}H^{0}(\mathbb{Q},A_{f}[\varpi]^{*})+\sum_{\ell\in S\cup\{\infty\}}\left(\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathcal{L}_{\ell}-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},A_{f}[\varpi])\right)\\
=&\dim_{\kappa}H^{1}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},A_{f}[\varpi])-\dim_{\kappa}H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},A_{f}[\varpi])-\dim_{\kappa}H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},A_{f}[\varpi]).\end{split}

Combining (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we arrive at our result.
∎

3.
Main results

In this section, we fix a prime
p\geq 5
, a
p
-adic field
\mathcal{K}
with valuation ring
\mathcal{O}
, uniformizer
\varpi
and residue field
\kappa
. We also fix a surjective and odd Galois representation

\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\kappa)

with
\operatorname{det}\bar{\rho}=\bar{\chi}
.
As is well known, the results of Khare and Wintenberger imply that there exists a Hecke eigencuspform form
f
of weight
2
, trivial nebentype and optimal level
N_{f}=N_{\bar{\rho}}
such that
\bar{\rho}_{f}=\bar{\rho}
.

Let
\Omega_{\bar{\rho}}
be the set of prime numbers
\ell
such that

(1)

\ell\nmid N_{\bar{\rho}}p
,

(2)

\ell\not\equiv\pm 1\mod{p}
,

(3)

\bar{\rho}_{f}(\sigma_{\ell})=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}-\ell&0\\
0&-1\\
\end{array}}\right)
for a suitable choice of basis.

Remark 3.1
.

It follows from an easy application of the Chebotarev density theorem that if
p\geq 5
and
\bar{\rho}_{f}
is surjective, then
\Omega_{\bar{\rho}}
has positive density. In fact, according to
[
17
, Proposition 5.2]
its density is equal to

(3.1)

\mathfrak{d}(\Omega_{\bar{\rho}})=\frac{(p-3)}{(p-1)^{2}}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
g
be a
p
-ordinary modular form of weight
2
and trivial nebentypus such that
\bar{\rho}_{g}\simeq\bar{\rho}_{f}\simeq\bar{\rho}
. Suppose that
\ell\mid N_{g}
is a prime lying in
\Omega_{\bar{\rho}}
. Then

\beta_{\ell}(A_{f})=0\quad\text{and}\quad\beta_{\ell}(A_{g})=0.

Proof.

Since
\ell\nmid N_{\bar{\rho}}
, the vanishing
\beta_{\ell}(A_{f})=0
follows from part (2) of Lemma
2.7
. For the vanishing of
\beta_{\ell}(A_{g})
, part (1) of the same lemma reduces the problem to showing that

\left(\frac{H^{0}(\mathrm{I}_{\ell},A_{g})}{\varpi H^{0}(\mathrm{I}_{\ell},A_{g})}\right)^{\mathrm{G}_{\ell}/\mathrm{I}_{\ell}}=0.

It follows from
[
17
, Lemma 4.4]
that
\rho_{g}
factors through the maximal pro-
p
tame inertia quotient of
\mathrm{G}_{\ell}
, generated by
\sigma_{\ell}
and
\tau_{\ell}
with the relation
\sigma_{\ell}\tau_{\ell}\sigma_{\ell}^{-1}=\tau_{\ell}^{\ell}
. Here,
\sigma_{\ell}
is a lift of Frobenius and
\tau_{\ell}
is a generator of the tame inertia. Further, there exist
x\equiv-1\pmod{\varpi}
and
y\equiv 0\pmod{\varpi}
such that

\rho_{g}(\sigma_{\ell})=\begin{pmatrix}\ell x&0\\
0&x\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{g}(\tau_{\ell})=\begin{pmatrix}1&y\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Since
\rho_{g}
is ramified at
\ell
, one must have
y\neq 0
. Write
y=\varpi^{n}u
with
u\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}
. It follows that

A_{g}^{\mathrm{I}_{\ell}}=\left(\mathcal{K}/\mathcal{O}\right)\cdot e_{1}\oplus\left(\varpi^{-n}\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{O}\right)\cdot e_{2}.

Thus,

\frac{H^{0}(\mathrm{I}_{\ell},A_{g})}{\varpi H^{0}(\mathrm{I}_{\ell},A_{g})}\simeq\kappa\cdot e_{2},

as a
\mathrm{G}_{\ell}
-module. The element
\sigma_{\ell}
acts on
\kappa\cdot e_{2}
via multiplication by
x\bmod\varpi
, which is
-1
by assumption. Since
p\neq 2
, this action is nontrivial, and so the invariants under
\mathrm{G}_{\ell}/\mathrm{I}_{\ell}
vanish:

\left(\frac{H^{0}(\mathrm{I}_{\ell},A_{g})}{\varpi H^{0}(\mathrm{I}_{\ell},A_{g})}\right)^{\mathrm{G}_{\ell}/\mathrm{I}_{\ell}}=0.

Hence,
\beta_{\ell}(A_{g})=0
, as desired.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
g
be a
p
-ordinary modular form of weight
2
such that
\bar{\rho}_{g}\simeq\bar{\rho}_{f}\simeq\bar{\rho}
. Assume that
p\nmid N_{g}
. Let
\ell
be a prime dividing
N_{\bar{\rho}}
such that
\ell\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{p}
.
Then

\beta_{\ell}(A_{f})=0\quad\text{and}\quad\beta_{\ell}(A_{g})=0.

Proof.

Let
h
denote
f
or
g
. Assume by way of contradiction that
\beta_{\ell}(A_{h})>0
. By Part (1) of Lemma
2.7
we have
H^{0}\left(I_{\ell},A_{h}\right)\neq 0
, hence
H^{0}\left(I_{\ell},A_{h}[\varpi]\right)\neq 0
. Since
\det\bar{\rho}
is the mod
p
cyclotomic character and
\ell\neq p
, we find that
\operatorname{det}(\bar{\rho}_{|\operatorname{I}_{\ell}})=1
. Consequently, there exists a basis
(\bar{e_{1}},\bar{e_{2}})
with respect to which the restriction
\bar{\rho}|_{\mathrm{I}_{\ell}}
takes the form

\bar{\rho}|_{\mathrm{I}_{\ell}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&*\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},

so that
\bar{\rho}
factors through the maximal pro-
p
tame inertia quotient of
\mathrm{G}_{\ell}
, generated by
\sigma_{\ell}
and
\tau_{\ell}
(as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
).
Since
\ell\mid N_{\bar{\rho}}
, the representation
\bar{\rho}
is ramified at
\ell
. Therefore there exists
y\neq 0
for which
\bar{\rho}(\tau_{\ell})=\begin{pmatrix}1&y\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.
We also write
\bar{\rho}(\sigma_{\ell})=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
. From the relation
\sigma_{\ell}\tau_{\ell}\sigma_{\ell}^{-1}=\tau_{\ell}^{\ell}
, we find that

\bar{\rho}(\sigma_{\ell})\bar{\rho}(\tau_{\ell})=\begin{pmatrix}a&ay+b\\
c&cy+d\end{pmatrix},

and

\bar{\rho}(\tau_{\ell})^{\ell}\bar{\rho}(\sigma_{\ell})=\begin{pmatrix}1&\ell y\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a+c\ell y&b+d\ell y\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}.

Equating these matrices, we obtain
c=0
and
a=d\ell
.
Hence,

(3.2)

\bar{\rho}(\sigma_{\ell})=\begin{pmatrix}d\ell&b\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}.

Note that
\det\bar{\rho}(\sigma_{\ell})=\bar{\chi}(\sigma_{\ell})=\ell
, and thus we conclude that
d^{2}\ell=\ell
, and consequently,
d=\pm 1
.

Since
\bar{\rho}\left(\tau_{\ell}\right)-1=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}0&y\\
0&0\end{array}\right)
, we see that
\rho\left(\tau_{\ell}\right)
has Smith Normal Form
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&w\end{array}\right)
for some
w\in(\varpi)
. If
w=0
then
A_{h}^{I_{\ell}}/\varpi A_{h}^{I_{\ell}}=0
, contradicting
\beta_{\ell}\left(A_{h}\right)>0
. Thus
w\neq 0
, giving
A_{h}^{I_{\ell}}=\left(\varpi^{-N}\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{O}\right)\cdot\gamma
for some
N>0
and some
\gamma
which is necessarily congruent to
\bar{e}_{1}
modulo
\varpi
. Since
\ell\neq\pm 1(\bmod\varpi)
, we see from (3.2) that the action of
\sigma_{\ell}-1
on
A_{h}^{I_{\ell}}/\varpi A_{h}^{I_{\ell}}
is invertible, hence
\left(A_{h}^{I_{\ell}}/\varpi A_{h}^{I_{\ell}}\right)^{G_{\ell}/I_{\ell}}=0
. Part (1) of Lemma
2.7
gives
\beta_{\ell}\left(A_{h}\right)=0
. This is a contradiction and the proof is complete. ∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
g
be a
p
-ordinary modular form of weight
2
such that
\bar{\rho}_{g}\simeq\bar{\rho}_{f}\simeq\bar{\rho}
. Assume the following:

(1)

No prime
\ell\equiv\pm 1\pmod{p}
divides
N_{\bar{\rho}}
.

(2)

Every prime dividing
N_{g}/N_{\bar{\rho}}
lies in
\Omega_{\bar{\rho}}
.

Then

\dim_{\kappa}\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(A_{f}/\mathbb{Q})[\varpi]=\dim_{\kappa}\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(A_{g}/\mathbb{Q})[\varpi].

Proof.

Fix
h\in\{f,g\}
. We claim that

\dim_{\kappa}\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(A_{h}/\mathbb{Q})[\varpi]=\dim_{\kappa}\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(A_{h}[\varpi]/\mathbb{Q}).

By the exactness of the sequence

(3.3)

0\to\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(A_{h}[\varpi]/\mathbb{Q})\to\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(A_{h}/\mathbb{Q})[\varpi]\to\bigoplus_{\ell}\operatorname{ker}h_{\ell},

we have the inequality

\dim_{\kappa}\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(A_{h}[\varpi]/\mathbb{Q})\leq\dim_{\kappa}\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(A_{h}/\mathbb{Q})[\varpi],

and further,

\dim_{\kappa}\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(A_{h}/\mathbb{Q})[\varpi]\leq\dim_{\kappa}\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(A_{h}[\varpi]/\mathbb{Q})+\sum_{\ell}\beta_{\ell}(A_{h}).

It therefore suffices to show that
\beta_{\ell}(A_{h})=0
for all primes
\ell
.

By Lemma
2.7
, we have
\beta_{\ell}(A_{h})=0
for
\ell=p
and for all
\ell\nmid N_{h}
. For the remaining primes
\ell\mid N_{h}
, there are two cases:

•

If
\ell\mid(N_{h}/N_{\bar{\rho}})
, then
\ell\in\Omega_{\bar{\rho}}
by assumption. Lemma
3.2
then implies
\beta_{\ell}(A_{h})=0
.

•

If
\ell\mid N_{\bar{\rho}}
, then
\ell\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{p}
by assumption. Lemma
3.3
applies, and again we conclude
\beta_{\ell}(A_{h})=0
.

Hence, we obtain the desired equality:

(3.4)

\dim_{\kappa}\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(A_{h}/\mathbb{Q})[\varpi]=\dim_{\kappa}\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(A_{h}[\varpi]/\mathbb{Q}).

The inertia group at
p
acts on
A_{h}[\varpi]
via
\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\bar{\chi}&\ast\\
0&1\\
\end{array}}\right)
and
A_{h}[\varpi]^{+}
is the
1
-dimensional submodule on which
\operatorname{I}_{p}
acts via the mod-
p
cyclotomic character and
A_{h}[\varpi]^{-}
is the unique quotient on which
\operatorname{I}_{p}
acts trivially. Thus
A_{h}[\varpi]
determines the
1
-dimensional modules
A_{h}[\varpi]^{\pm}
.
Since
\bar{\rho}_{f}\simeq\bar{\rho}_{g}
, we deduce that

\dim_{\kappa}\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(A_{f}[\varpi]/\mathbb{Q})=\dim_{\kappa}\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(A_{g}[\varpi]/\mathbb{Q}).

From (
3.4
) it follows that

\dim_{\kappa}\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(A_{f}/\mathbb{Q})[\varpi]=\dim_{\kappa}\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(A_{g}/\mathbb{Q})[\varpi].

This completes the proof.
∎

Before proving our main theorem, we recall the following well known result of Serre.

Theorem 3.5

(Serre)
.

Let
\Omega
be a set of primes with Dirichlet density
\delta>0
. For
Y>0
, let
M_{\Omega}(Y)
denote the number of squarefree natural numbers
M\leq Y
divisible only by primes in
\Omega
. Then

M_{\Omega}(Y)\gg Y(\log Y)^{\delta-1}.

Proof.

This follows from
[
20
, Theorem 2.4]
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
A
.

Let
\Omega\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Omega_{\bar{\rho}}
, and consider any
M\in M_{\Omega}(Y)
. Set
N\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=MN_{\bar{\rho}}
. Then
N\in\mathcal{S}(\bar{\rho})
, and by Theorem
2.4
, there exists a Hecke eigencuspform
g
such that
\bar{\rho}_{g}\simeq\bar{\rho}_{f}
and
N=N_{g}
. By Proposition
3.4
, we have

\dim_{\kappa}\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A_{f}/\mathbb{Q})[\varpi]=\dim_{\kappa}\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A_{g}/\mathbb{Q})[\varpi].

Thus,
N\in\mathcal{S}_{f}(X)
, and hence

N_{f}(X)\geq M_{\Omega}(X/N_{\bar{\rho}}).

Applying Theorem
3.5
, we obtain

N_{f}(X)\gg X(\log X)^{\alpha-1},

where
\alpha=\mathfrak{d}(\Omega)=\frac{p-3}{(p-1)^{2}}
by (
3.1
).
∎

Cauchy identities for Grothendieck polynomials and a dual RSK correspondence through pipe dreams Thanks: The author was partially supported by NSF awards DMS-2231565 and DMS-1945212.
Hugh Dennin

Abstract
. The Cauchy identity gives a recipe for decomposing a double Grothendieck polynomial 𝔊 w ( β ) ​ ( x , y ) \mathfrak{G}^{(\beta)}_{w}(x;y) as a sum of products 𝔊 v ( β ) ​ ( x ) ​ 𝔊 u ( β ) ​ ( y ) \mathfrak{G}^{(\beta)}_{v}(x)\mathfrak{G}^{(\beta)}_{u}(y) of single Grothendieck polynomials. Combinatorially, this identity suggests the existence of a weight-preserving bijection between certain families of diagrams called pipe dreams. In this paper, we provide such a bijection using an algorithm called pipe dream rectification. In turn, rectification is built from a new class of flow operators which themselves exhibit a surprising symmetry. Finally, we examine other applications of rectification including an insertion algorithm on pipe dreams which recovers a variant of the dual RSK correspondence.



1.
Introduction

Write
S_{\infty}
for the set of permutations of
\mathbb{N}
with finite support.
Each permutation
w\in S_{\infty}
has an associated
Schubert polynomial

\mathfrak{S}_{w}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{i}]_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, first defined by Lascoux and Schützenberger in
[
10
]
.
When
w\in S_{n}
, the Schubert polynomial
\mathfrak{S}_{w}(x)
represents the class of a corresponding Schubert variety
X_{w}
inside the cohomology ring of the complete flag variety
\Fl(n)
.

Double Schubert polynomials

\mathfrak{S}_{w}(x;y)\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{i};y_{j}]_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}
were introduced in
[
9
]
as a two-alphabet generalization of Schubert polynomials.
Double Schubert polynomials are related to (single) Schubert polynomials by the Cauchy identity.

Theorem 1.1

(Cauchy identity for
\mathfrak{S}_{w}
)
.

Let
w\in S_{\infty}
.
Then

\mathfrak{S}_{w}(x;y)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,v\in S_{\infty}\\
w\doteq u^{-1}v\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{v}(x)\mathfrak{S}_{u}(y),

where the notation
w\doteq u^{-1}v
indicates that both
w=u^{-1}v
and
\ell(w)=\ell(u)+\ell(v)
.

In the
K
-theory of
\Fl(n)
, the class of the structure sheaf of a Schubert variety is instead represented by a
Grothendieck polynomial

\mathfrak{G}_{w}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{i}]_{i\in\mathbb{N}}

[
11
,
12
]
.
The Grothendieck polynomials we will consider
\mathfrak{G}_{w}^{(\beta)}(x)
are a variant introduced in
[
4
]
with an additional homogenizing
\beta
-parameter.
We can recover
\mathfrak{S}_{w}(x)
and
\mathfrak{G}_{w}(x)
from
\mathfrak{G}_{w}^{(\beta)}(x)
by specializing
\beta=0
and
\beta=-1
, respectively.

As with Schubert polynomials, we have a notion of
double Grothendieck polynomials

\mathfrak{G}_{w}^{(\beta)}(x;y)\in\mathbb{Z}[\beta][x_{i};y_{j}]_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Grothendieck polynomials also satisfy a Cauchy identity.

Theorem 1.2

(Cauchy identity for
\mathfrak{G}_{w}^{(\beta)}

[
4
]
)
.

Let
w\in S_{\infty}
.
Then

\mathfrak{G}_{w}^{(\beta)}(x;y)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,v\in S_{\infty}\\
w=u^{-1}*v\end{subarray}}\beta^{\ell(u)+\ell(v)-\ell(w)}\mathfrak{G}_{v}^{(\beta)}(x)\mathfrak{G}_{u}^{(\beta)}(y),

where
u^{-1}*v
denotes the product of
u^{-1}
and
v
in the Demazure algebra.

Schubert and Grothendieck polynomials can be viewed as generating functions for certain collections of wiring diagrams called
pipe dreams
; see
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
8
]
.
Their doubled variants have a similar interpretation in terms of
super pipe dreams
—pipe dreams with a coloring of their crossings using black and red.

Equipped with this viewpoint, the Cauchy identities (Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
) become combinatorial identities of pipe dreams, so a natural question is to ask for a weight preserving bijection that realizes them.
In practice, we would like an algorithm that takes a super pipe dream for
w
as input and produces two pipe dreams corresponding to a decomposition of
w
into a pair
u
and
v
.

The main goal of this paper is to provide such an algorithm, which we call pipe dream
rectification
.
Rectification will be constructed from a new class of operators on super pipe dreams that
flow
crosses between columns and rows.
We will then examine some further applications of rectification, including an insertion algorithm on pipe dreams which recovers a variant of the
dual Robinson–Schensted–Knuth
correspondence.

This paper is organized as follows:
in Sections
2
and
3
, we recall the necessary background on permutations and pipe dreams and give our definition of a super pipe dream.
In Section
4
, we define the flow operators
Y_{j}^{+}
and
Y^{+}
which advance red crosses in a super pipe dream to the right by column, as well as their counterparts
X_{i}^{+}
and
X^{+}
acting on black crosses.
We observe a surprising symmetry between the flow operators
X^{+}
and
Y^{+}
(Proposition
5.1
) in Section
5
.

Rectification is defined in Section
6
, where it is used to give a new combinatorial proof of the Cauchy identities for Schubert and Grothendieck polynomials.
Further applications of flowing and rectification are explored in Section
7
.
These include bijective proofs of a derivative formula for Grothendieck polynomials first observed in
[
15
]
(Section
7.1
) and a restricted descent Pieri rule (Section
7.2
), the latter of which will lead to a new insertion algorithm on pipe dreams.
We also derive an identity for Stanley symmetric functions (Section
7.3
) and a recurrence for double Grothendieck polynomials using Bergeron–Sottile operators generalizing that of
[
14
]
(Section
7.4
).

Finally, in Section
8
we obtain our variant of dual RSK by applying the insertion algorithm rectification to reduced pipe dreams for Grassmannian permutations, which are known to be in 1-to-1 correspondence with semi-standard Young tableaux.
We will see that the “insertion tableau” we obtain through our variant of dual RSK agrees with the insertion tableau of the usual dual RSK correspondence (Proposition
8.8
).

2.
Permutations

We use the notation
\mathbb{N}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{1,2,\dots\}
and
[n]\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{1,\dots,n\}
.

Write
S_{n}
for the group of permutations on
\mathbb{N}
with
support

\{i\mid w(i)\neq i\}
contained in
[n]
, and set
S_{\infty}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}S_{n}
.
We will often write permutations in
S_{\infty}
using one-line notation.
For example,
1423
denotes the permutation
w\in S_{4}
with
1\mapsto 1
and
2\mapsto 4\mapsto 3\mapsto 2
.

For
i\in\mathbb{N}
, write
s_{i}\in S_{\mathbb{N}}
for the simple transposition that swaps
i
and
i+1
.
The simple transpositions
s_{1},\dots,s_{n-1}
generate
S_{n}
under the relations

\displaystyle s_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle s_{i}s_{j}

\displaystyle=s_{j}s_{i}\quad\text{(for $\mathinner{\!\left\lvert i-j\right\rvert}>1$)},

\displaystyle s_{i}s_{i+1}s_{i}

\displaystyle=s_{i+1}s_{i}s_{i+1}.

A
word
for
w\in S_{\infty}
is a sequence
\mathbf{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})
of positive integers such that
w=s_{a_{1}}\cdots s_{a_{r}}
.
The minimum length
\ell(w)
among words for
w
is called the
length
of
w
, and words for
w
attaining this length are called
reduced
.

The
code
of
w
is the sequence
\code(w)=(c_{1},c_{2},\dots)\in\mathbb{N}^{\infty}
determined by
c_{i}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\{j>i\mid w(j)<w(i)\}\right\rvert}
.
This is a refinement of the length of
w
in the sense that
\ell(w)=\sum_{i}c_{i}
.
Codes give a bijection between
S_{\infty}
and the subset of
\mathbb{N}^{\infty}
of sequences with finitely many nonzero terms.

The
descent set
of
w
is the collection of indices
\Des(w)=\{i\mid w(i)>w(i+1)\}
.
Notice that
i\in\Des(w)
if and only if
\ell(ws_{i})=\ell(w)-1
.

The
Demazure algebra
(or
0
-Hecke algebra
) is the algebra
H
generated by elements
\{e_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\}
subject to the relations

\displaystyle e_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=e_{i},

\displaystyle e_{i}e_{j}

\displaystyle=e_{j}e_{i}\quad\text{ (for $\mathinner{\!\left\lvert i-j\right\rvert}>1$)},

\displaystyle e_{i}e_{i+1}e_{i}

\displaystyle=e_{i+1}e_{i}e_{i+1}.

There is an isomorphism of the underlying abelian group of the group ring
\mathbb{Z}[S_{\infty}]
with that of
H
given by sending the basis element
w\in S_{\infty}
to the element
\partial(\mathbf{a})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{\ell}}\in H
, where
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell})
is any reduced word for
w
.
By abuse of notation, we will identify
w
with the element
\partial(\mathbf{a})
.
If
u,v\in S_{\infty}
, then we write
u*v
to distinguish the product of
u
and
v
in
H
from their usual product
uv
in
\mathbb{Z}[S_{\infty}]
.

3.1.
Pipe dreams

Visualize
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
as an infinite playing board of square tiles.
A position
(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
will refer to the tile in row
i
and column
j
, with row indices increasing downwards and column indices increasing rightwards.

Definition 3.1

(
[
3
,
1
,
8
]
)
.

A
(single) pipe dream

P
is any finite subset of the half-space

\mathcal{H}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\mid i+j-1\geq 1\}.

We regard
P
as a placement of finitely many black checkers in the region
\mathcal{H}
, with each position being occupied by at most one checker.

Write
\PD
for the set of all pipe dreams.
The terminology for a pipe dream
P
comes from the more typical visualization of
P
as a tiling of
\mathcal{H}_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\mid i+j-1\geq 0\}
using
cross
,
bump
, and
elbow
tiles

whose locations are given by
P
,
\mathcal{H}\smallsetminus P
, and
\mathcal{H}_{0}\smallsetminus\mathcal{H}
, respectively.
Such a tiling depicts a configuration of countably many pipes winding in the plane.
Our visualization using checkers is more in line with the original
RC-graph
terminology for
P
.
See Figure
1
for an example of both visualizations.

Each pipe dream
P
has a
word

\mathbf{a}(P)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{p})
obtained by reading off the values
i+j-1
of each
(i,j)\in P
from right-to-left, beginning with the top row and moving down

[
1
,
3
,
4
]
.
The
permutation
of
P
is the permutation
\partial(P)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\partial(\mathbf{a}(P))
(see Figure
1
).

Say that
P\in\PD
is
reduced
if
\mathbf{a}(P)
is a reduced word.
Write
\PD_{0}
for the set of all reduced pipe dreams.
If
P\in\PD(w)
, then
P
is reduced if and only if
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert P\right\rvert}=\ell(w)
.

For each
w\in S_{\infty}
, write
\PD(w)
for the set of pipe dreams with permutation
w
.
There is a natural bijection
\PD(w)\to\PD(w^{-1})
given by sending a pipe dream
P
to its
transpose

P^{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{(j,i)\mid(i,j)\in P\}.

A pipe dream
P\in\PD
is
ordinary
if
P\subseteq\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
and we write
\PD^{+}
for the collection of these pipe dreams.
Our conventions differ from the standard ones in that what we call ordinary pipe dreams are just pipe dreams elsewhere (e.g.
[
1
,
8
]
).

Throughout this paper, we will freely allow decoration combos on sets of pipe dreams.
For instance,
\PD_{0}^{+}(w)
is understood to mean the family
\PD_{0}\cap\PD^{+}\cap\PD(w)
of reduced, ordinary pipe dreams with permutation
w
.

There is dually a notion of a

In this section, we will define a collection of operators on super pipe dreams which will “flow” red checkers between columns while preserving the row distribution of black checkers, and vice versa.
These operators will be the fundamental building block which allows us to separate a super pipe dream into two single pipe dreams.

4.1.
Flowing red checkers

We begin by defining, for each
j\in\mathbb{Z}

Y^{+}_{j}
that takes a super pipe dream
\mathbf{P}
with no red checkers in column
j+1

j

j+1

Proposition 4.1
.

Let

and

There exists a bijection

6.
Pipe dream rectification and the Cauchy identities

In this section, we provide an algorithm that takes a super pipe dream
\mathbf{W}\in\SPD(w)
and separates it into two single pipe dreams
V\in\PD(v)
and
U\in\PD(u)
for a decomposition
w=u^{-1}*v
.
We call this process pipe dream
rectification
.
We will see that rectification preserves weights in such a way to give a bijective proof of the Cauchy identities for Schubert and Grothendieck polynomials (Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
).

6.1.
Pipe dream rectification

We begin by giving the definition of pipe dream rectification.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
w\in S_{\infty}
.
There is a bijection

\Rect\colon\SPD(w)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\coprod_{w=u^{-1}*v}\PD(v)\times\PD(u)

which preserves weights in the sense that if
(V,U)=\Rect(\mathbf{W})
, then
\wt(\mathbf{W})=\beta^{\ell(u)+\ell(v)-\ell(w)}\wt(V)\wt(U^{\dagger})
.
We refer to
\Rect(\mathbf{W})
as the rectification of
\mathbf{W}
.

As a reminder, we identify
U
with the super pipe dream
(U,\varnothing)
, so in the above proposition
U^{\dagger}=(U,\varnothing)^{\dagger}=(\varnothing,U^{t})
.

Proof.

Assume that
w\in S_{n}
and that the black (resp. red) checkers in
\mathbf{W}
are in rows at or below
i_{0}
(resp. columns at or right of
j_{0}
).
For each
m\geq 0
, the red checkers in
(Y^{+})^{m}\mathbf{W}
get flowed into the region
\{(i,j)\mid 1\leq i+j-1<n,i\geq i_{0}+m\}
where as the black checkers remain in the region
\{(i,j)\mid 1\leq i+j-1<n,j\leq j_{0}\}
.

Picking
m\geq 0
large enough so that these regions are disjoint, we reach a super pipe dream
\mathbf{W}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(Y^{+})^{m}\mathbf{W}
where the red checkers are positioned northeast of the black checkers.
In this case,
\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{W}^{\prime})=\mathbf{a}(W^{\prime}_{y})\mathbf{a}(W^{\prime}_{x})
, so we get a decomposition
w=u^{-1}*v
where
u^{-1}=\partial(W^{\prime}_{y})
and
v=\partial(W^{\prime}_{x})
.

Take
\Rect(\mathbf{W})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(U,V)
where
V=\mathbf{W}^{\prime}_{x}\in\PD(v)
and
U^{t}=\sigma^{m}(\mathbf{W}^{\prime}_{y})\in\PD(u^{-1})
(see Figure
12
).
This is well-defined regardless of which

we picked, since applying

any further to

.

\wt(\mathbf{W})=\beta^{\ell(u)+\ell(v)-\ell(w)}\wt(V)\wt(U^{\dagger})

Proposition 6.6
.

and
\mathbf{W}\in\SPD(w)
.

Conversely, if

\ell(w)=\ell(u)+\ell(v)=|V|+|U|=|W_{x}|+|W_{y}|\geq|W_{x}\cup W_{y}|\geq\ell(w)

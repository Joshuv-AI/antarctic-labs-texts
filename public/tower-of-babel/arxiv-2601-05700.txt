Existence of nontrival n n -harmonic maps via min-max methods
Dorian Martino, Katarzyna Mazowiecka, Armin Schikorra

Abstract
. For any n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 and any closed manifold 𝒩 {\mathcal{N}} with π n + k ​ ( 𝒩 ) ≠ { 0 } \pi_{n+k}({\mathcal{N}})\neq\{0\} for some k ≥ 0 k\geq 0 , we establish the existence of nontrivial n n -harmonic maps from 𝕊 n {\mathbb{S}}^{n} into 𝒩 {\mathcal{N}} . When k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 , these maps naturally appear as bubbling limits of p p -harmonic maps with p > n p>n , obtained by min-max constructions in the limit p → n + p\to n^{+} .

1.
Introduction

In this note, we study the existence of nontrivial
n
-harmonic maps from the sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
into a smooth, compact manifold
{\mathcal{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
without boundary. These maps are critical points of the
n
-energy

\mathcal{E}_{n}(u)\coloneqq\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{n},\quad u\colon{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\to{\mathcal{N}},

i.e., distributional solutions to

(1.1)

-{\rm div}(|\nabla u|^{n-2}\nabla u)=|\nabla u|^{n-2}A(u)[\nabla u,\nabla u].

Here
A
denotes the second fundamental form. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 3
. If
\pi_{n+k}({\mathcal{N}})\neq\{0\}
for some
k\geq 0
, then there exists a nontrivial,
C^{1,\alpha}
-regular
n
-harmonic map from
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
into
{\mathcal{N}}
, where
\alpha
is a small positive number.

Throughout the paper we assume
n\geq 3
. The two-dimensional case can be treated by the same arguments with minor modifications; since the corresponding results for harmonic maps are already well known (see
[
9
]
and references therein), we do not include them here.

The existence result in this theorem is in line with general expectations in the theory of harmonic maps. In the case
k=0
, that is, when
\pi_{n}({\mathcal{N}})\neq\{0\}
, it follows from the classical Sacks–Uhlenbeck theory
[
18
]
, since
\pi_{n}({\mathcal{N}})
is generated by homotopy classes for which the
n
-energy admits a minimizer. The corresponding result for the
n
-harmonic maps was proved by
[
17
,
8
]
. See also
[
13
]
for related minimization results in a fractional framework.

When
\pi_{n}({\mathcal{N}})=\{0\}
but
\pi_{n+k}({\mathcal{N}})\ni\alpha\neq 0
, one is naturally lead to seek critical points of the
n
-energy via a
\min
-
\max
construction of the form

\inf_{\Phi\in\alpha}\ \max_{t\in[-1,1]^{k}}\ \mathcal{E}_{n}\big(\Phi(\cdot,t)\big).

This is the equivalent approach to Birkhoff’s min-max construction of nontrivial closed geodesics on manifolds diffeomorphic to the sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
. A direct implementation of this strategy at the critical exponent
n
is, however, obstructed by the failure of the Palais–Smale condition in
W^{1,n}
.

In the recent work
[
3
]
, this difficulty is overcome by developing a refined analytical framework, leading to a proof of the theorem
Theorem

1.1
in dimension
n=3
, and in dimensions
n\geq 4
in the case of manifolds which are spheres or Lie groups. The present work provides an alternative approach, which applies to all dimensions
n\geq 3
and to arbitrary smooth closed target manifolds
{\mathcal{N}}
.

The starting point is classical. For
p>n
, we consider the perturbed energy

\mathcal{E}_{p}(u)\coloneqq\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{p},\quad u\colon{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\to{\mathcal{N}}.

For
p>n
, the functional satisfies the Palais–Smale condition, allowing one to construct non-trivial critical points of the associated Euler–Lagrange equation

(1.2)

-{\rm div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=|\nabla u|^{p-2}A(u)[\nabla u,\nabla u].

These are obtained through the min-max scheme

\beta_{p}\coloneqq\min_{\Phi\in\alpha}\ \max_{t\in[-1,1]^{k}}\ \mathcal{E}_{p}\big(\Phi(\cdot,t)\big).

Provided that
\liminf_{p\to n}\beta_{p}>0
,
Theorem

1.1
follows from a one-bubble compactness result: for any sequence
p\to n^{+}
,
p>n
and any corresponding sequence of
p
-harmonic maps
u_{p}\in W^{1,p}({\mathbb{S}}^{n},{\mathcal{N}})
, one of the following holds, up to a subsequence. Either
u_{p}
converges strongly in
W^{1,n+\sigma}
for some
\sigma>0
, meaning that the limit is a nontrivial
n
-harmonic map; or there exists at least one nontrivial bubble, which, after rescaling, is a nontrivial
n
-harmonic map.

Outline.
Since the case
k=0
is known (see for instance
[
20
,
21
]
or
[
8
, Theorem 0]
), we focus on the case
k\geq 1
. That is, throughout the remainder of this article we assume
\pi_{n+k}(\mathcal{N})\neq\{0\}
. In
Section

2
we establish the validity of the min-max construction for
p
-harmonic maps and
p>n
.
Section

3
explains the main analytical tool of the paper. There we prove an
\varepsilon
-regularity result for
p
-harmonic maps under a uniform
p
-energy bound. Instead of deriving higher
second-order
regularity, we obtain a fractional gain in the differentiability, showing that solutions belong locally to
W^{1+\delta,p}
for some
\delta>0
. This fractional improvement is sufficient to ensure the compactness needed in the bubbling analysis. The argument relies on the contemporary regularity theory for the
p
-Laplacian combined with the classical stability estimate in the spirit of Iwaniec–Sbordone
[
7
]
, which provides a uniform control of the
(n+\sigma)
-energy for some
\sigma>0
.

Finally, in
Section

4
we combine the previous results to prove the main theorem. Using the compactness provided by the fractional regularity improvement and a one-bubble extraction argument, we show the existence of a nontrivial
n
-harmonic map.

Notation.
Throughout the paper,
B(x,r)
denotes the (open) ball of radius
r>0
centred at
x
. When the ambient space is
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, this is the Euclidean ball; when the ambient space is
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
, it is the geodesic ball. The meaning will always be clear from the context.

2.
Preliminaries: Existence of
p
-harmonic maps

Let
\alpha\in\pi_{n+k}(\mathcal{N})
be nontrivial. We identify
{\mathbb{S}}^{n+k}\simeq{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\times[-1,1]^{k}
, with the usual identification on
\partial[-1,1]^{k}
, and consider for
p>n

(2.1)

\inf_{\Phi\in\alpha}\ \max_{t\in[-1,1]^{k}}\ \mathcal{E}_{p}(\Phi(\cdot,t))=\inf_{\Phi\in\alpha}\ \max_{t\in[-1,1]^{k}}\ \int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}|\nabla\Phi(x,t)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\eqqcolon\beta_{p}.

We need the following.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\beta_{p}
be the value defined in (
2.1
). We have

\liminf_{p\to n^{+}}\beta_{p}>0.

Proof.

By Hölder inequality it suffices to show

|{\mathbb{S}}^{n}|^{1-\frac{p}{n}}(\beta_{p})^{\frac{n}{p}}\geq\inf_{\Phi\in\alpha}\max_{t\in[-1,1]^{k}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}|\nabla\Phi(x,t)|^{n}\,\mathrm{d}x>0.

Assume on the contrary that for a suitably small number
\varepsilon>0
(chosen below) there exists a smooth
\Phi\colon{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\times[-1,1]^{k}\to{\mathcal{N}}
for which we have

(2.2)

\max_{t\in[-1,1]^{k}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}|\nabla\Phi(x,t)|^{n}\,\mathrm{d}x<\varepsilon.

For each
t\in[-1,1]^{k}
, let
\Phi^{h}(\cdot,t)
denote the harmonic extension of
\Phi(\cdot,t)\colon{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\to{\mathcal{N}}
to a map
\Phi^{h}(\cdot,t)\colon{B}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
If
P(x,\xi)=\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{\omega_{n}|x-\xi|^{n+1}}
is the Poisson kernel on the ball
B^{n+1}
, then we have

\Phi^{h}(x,t)=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}P(x,\xi)\Phi(\xi,t)\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Since we have
\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}P(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi=1
, the following holds for any
\zeta\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n}

\begin{split}{\rm dist\,}(\Phi^{h}(x,t),{\mathcal{N}})&\leq\left|\Phi^{h}(x,t)-\Phi(\zeta,t)\right|\leq\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}P(x,\xi)\left|\Phi(\xi,t)-\Phi(\zeta,t)\right|\,\mathrm{d}\xi.\end{split}

Multiplying both sides by
P(x,\zeta)
and integrating over
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
with respect to
\zeta
we obtain

(2.3)

\begin{split}{\rm dist\,}(\Phi^{h}(x,t),{\mathcal{N}})&\leq\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}P(x,\xi)P(x,\zeta)\left|\Phi(\xi,t)-\Phi(\zeta,t)\right|\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}\zeta.\end{split}

If
|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}
, then we have
|x-\xi|\geq\frac{1}{2}
for any
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
and thus, it holds

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}|P(x,\xi)|^{n^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\precsim\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{|x-\xi|^{n+1}}\right)^{n^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\precsim 1.

Hence, for
|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we have, using Hölder and Poincaré inequality

\begin{split}{\rm dist\,}(\Phi^{h}(x,t),{\mathcal{N}})&\leq\|P(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{n^{\prime}}({\mathbb{S}}^{n})}^{2}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}\left|\Phi(\xi,t)-\Phi(\zeta,t)\right|^{n}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}\zeta\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\\
&\precsim\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}|\nabla\Phi(x,t)|^{n}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\precsim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}.\end{split}

If
|x|\geq\frac{1}{2}
, then we let
r=1-|x|
and
X=\frac{x}{|x|}
and we write

A(X,r,0)\coloneqq B(X,r),\quad A(X,r,k)\coloneqq B(X,2^{k}r)\setminus B(X,2^{k-1}r)\text{ for }k\geq 1.

Let us denote by
\theta={\rm dist\,}_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}(\frac{x}{|x|},\xi)
. Then, we have

|x-\xi|^{2}=|x|^{2}+1-2|x|\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\xi=|x|^{2}+1-2|x|\cos\theta=(1-|x|)^{2}+2|x|(1-\cos\theta).

For every
\theta\in[0,1]
we have the elementary inequality
\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\theta^{2}\leq 1-\cos\theta
, thus

|x-\xi|^{2}\succsim(1-|x|)^{2}+|x|\theta^{2}\succsim(1-|x|)^{2}+\theta^{2}.

Hence, for
\xi\in A(X,r,k)
we have
\theta\approx 2^{k}r
and
|x-\xi|\succsim 2^{k}r
. Thus, from (
2.3
) we get for
|x|\geq\frac{1}{2}

\begin{split}{\rm dist\,}(\Phi^{h}(x,t),{\mathcal{N}})&\precsim\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}}\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}}\int_{A(X,k,r)}\int_{A(X,\ell,r)}\left|\Phi(\xi,t)-\Phi(\zeta,t)\right|\frac{r}{(2^{k}r)^{n+1}}\frac{r}{(2^{\ell}r)^{n+1}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}\zeta\\
&\precsim\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}}\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}}2^{-k}2^{-\ell}\rule[2.60138pt]{8.67204pt}{0.65062pt}\kern-11.56346pt\displaystyle\int_{A(X,k,r)}\rule[2.60138pt]{8.67204pt}{0.65062pt}\kern-11.56346pt\displaystyle\int_{A(X,\ell,r)}\left|\Phi(\xi,t)-\Phi(\zeta,t)\right|\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}\zeta\\
&\precsim\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}}\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}}2^{-k}2^{-\ell}\left(\rule[2.60138pt]{8.67204pt}{0.65062pt}\kern-11.56346pt\displaystyle\int_{A(X,k,r)}\rule[2.60138pt]{8.67204pt}{0.65062pt}\kern-11.56346pt\displaystyle\int_{A(X,\ell,r)}\left|\Phi(\xi,t)-\Phi(\zeta,t)\right|^{n}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}\zeta\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\\
&\precsim\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}}\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}}2^{-k}2^{-\ell}\|\nabla\Phi(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{n}({\mathbb{S}}^{n})}\precsim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}.\end{split}

Thus,
\left\|{\rm dist\,}(\Phi^{h},{\mathcal{N}})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{n+1}\times[-1,1]^{k})}\precsim\varepsilon^{1/n}
.

Let
\pi_{{\mathcal{N}}}\colon B_{\delta}({\mathcal{N}})\to{\mathcal{N}}
denote the nearest point projection. We then define

\Psi\coloneqq\pi_{{\mathcal{N}}}\circ\Phi^{h}\colon{B}^{n+1}\times[-1,1]^{k}\to{\mathcal{N}}.

By construction,
\Psi
is continuous and satisfies

\Psi\rvert_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\times[-1,1]^{k}}=\Phi.

Hence, we have
\left[\Psi\rvert_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\times[-1,1]^{k}}\right]=[\Phi]
in
\pi_{k+n}({\mathcal{N}})
. On the other hand, the map
\Psi\rvert_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\times[-1,1]^{k}}
is contractible, therefore
\left[\Psi\rvert_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\times[-1,1]^{k}}\right]=[\Phi]=0
. This is a contradiction with the assumption that
\alpha=[\Phi]
is nontrivial.
∎

Since
p>n
the functional
\mathcal{E}_{p}
satisfies the Palais–Smale condition. In view of
Lemma

2.1
, we may apply the mountain-pass lemma (see, e.g.,
[
19
, Theorem 6.1]
) which yields the existence of a nontrivial critical point realizing the min-max value
\beta_{p}
.
Since
p>n
, the resulting
p
-harmonic map
u_{p}
is
Hölder continuous
by the Morrey–Sobolev embedding. Moreover, following the arguments of
[
5
, Section 3]
(see also
[
11
, Theorem 1.3]
) one concludes that
u\in C^{1,\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
.
We have thus established the following result.

Theorem 2.2
.

If
\pi_{n+k}({\mathcal{N}})\neq\{0\}
for some
k\geq 1
, then for any
p>n
there exists a
p
-harmonic map
u_{p}\in W^{1,p}\cap C^{1,\alpha}({\mathbb{S}}^{n},{\mathcal{N}})
, i.e., a solution to (
1.2
), and we have

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}|\nabla u_{p}|^{p}=\beta_{p}=\min_{\Phi\in\alpha}\ \max_{t\in[-1,1]^{k}}\ \mathcal{E}_{p}\big(\Phi(\cdot,t)\big).

As a corollary, using a standard covering argument, see, e.g.,
[
18
, Proposition 4.3 and Theorem 4.4]
, we have the following disjunction of cases for the limiting behaviour of
u_{p}
as
p\to n^{+}
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. If
\pi_{n+k}({\mathcal{N}})\neq\{0\}
for some
k\geq 1
, then there exists a sequence
p_{j}\xrightarrow{j\to\infty}n^{+}
and a sequence of
p_{j}
-harmonic map
u\in W^{1,p_{j}}\cap C^{1,\alpha_{j}}({\mathbb{S}}^{n},{\mathcal{N}})
, i.e., solutions to (
1.2
) with

\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}|\nabla u_{p_{j}}|^{p_{j}}>0,

such that one of the following holds.

•

(No bubble) For all
x\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
there exists
\rho_{x}>0
such that

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\int_{B(x,\rho_{x})}|\nabla u_{p_{j}}|^{n}<\varepsilon^{n}.

•

(At least one isolated bubble) There exists at least one
\bar{x}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
and a radius
R>0
with the following property

\forall\rho>0,\qquad\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{B(\bar{x},\rho)}|\nabla u_{p_{j}}|^{n}\geq\varepsilon^{n},

and for any
x\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
,
0<{\rm dist\,}_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}(x,\bar{x})<R
there exists some
\rho_{x}>0
such that

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\int_{B(x,\rho_{x})}|\nabla u_{p_{j}}|^{n}\leq\varepsilon^{n}.

3.
An
\varepsilon
-regularity Theorem

The main technical ingredient of the paper is an
\varepsilon
-regularity theorem: it says that there is a threshold
\varepsilon>0
such that for any (geodesic) ball
B(x,\rho)
where the
n
-energy drops below
\varepsilon
we have strong convergence (up to subsequence).

Theorem 3.1
.

There exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that for each
p_{0}>n
there exist
p_{1}>n
and
t_{0}>0
with the property that for all
p\in[n,p_{1}]
and any
\Lambda>0
the following holds.

Assume
u\in W^{1,p_{0}}(B(x_{0},r),{\mathcal{N}})
is a
p
-harmonic map which satisfies

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{n}(B(x_{0},r))}<\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},r))}\leq\Lambda.

Then it holds

[\nabla u]_{W^{t_{0},p}(B(x_{0},r/2))}\leq C(n,\Lambda,r).

We begin with the following standard consequence of Iwaniec–Sbordone stability result
[
7
, Theorem 4]
for the
p
-Laplace equation.

Lemma 3.2

(Iwaniec–Sbordone a priori estimate)
.

Let
n\geq 3
, fix a compact manifold
{\mathcal{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
and let
A
be its second fundamental form. There exists
\varepsilon>0
, such that for any
p_{0}>n
there exists
p_{1}\in(n,p_{0})
and
\sigma>0
so that the following holds whenever
p\in[n,p_{1}]
.

Assume that
u\in W^{1,p_{0}}(B(x_{0},10r),{\mathcal{N}})
satisfies

(3.1)

{\rm div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=|\nabla u|^{p-2}A(u)[\nabla u,\nabla u]\quad\text{in ${B}(x_{0},10r)$},

and

(3.2)

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{n}(B(x_{0},10r))}<\varepsilon.

Then it holds

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{n+\sigma}(B(x_{0},r))}\leq C({\mathcal{N}},n)\left(1+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},10r))}^{p-1}\right)^{\frac{n+1}{n-1}}.

Proof.

For simplicity of presentation, we assume that
B(x_{0},10r)
is a Euclidean ball
B(0,10)
1
1

1

In the case of a geodesic ball in
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
, one works in normal coordinates; the
n
-Laplacian then has smooth coefficients, and all arguments and estimates carry over unchanged.
.

First we localize. Let
\tilde{\eta}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B(0,2))
such that
\eta\equiv 1
in
B(0,1)
and set

\tilde{u}\coloneqq\eta(u-(u)_{B(0,1)}).

We multiply (
3.1
) with
\eta^{p-1}
and obtain a system valid on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

{\rm div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)\eta^{p-1}=|\nabla u|^{p-2}A(u)[\nabla u,\nabla u]\eta^{p-1}\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

Thus, it holds

(3.3)

\begin{split}{\rm div}(\eta^{p-1}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)&=|\nabla u|^{p-2}A(u)[\nabla u,\nabla u]\eta^{p-1}+(p-1)\eta^{p-2}\nabla\eta|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\\
&=|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{p-2}A(u)[\nabla\tilde{u},\nabla u]+g,\\
\end{split}

where the map
g
verifies the following estimate

(3.4)

\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\precsim\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B(0,2))}^{p-1},

where the constant depends on
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}
, that is on
\mathcal{N}
.

We define the following quantity

F\coloneqq|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{p-2}\nabla\tilde{u}-\eta^{p-1}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u.

Since
p>2
, the map
F
satisfies the following estimate

(3.5)

\|F\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\precsim\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B(0,2))}^{p-2},

where the constant depends on
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}
, that is on
\mathcal{N}
.

Plugging
F
into (
3.3
), we arrive at

(3.6)

\begin{split}{\rm div}(|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{p-2}\nabla\tilde{u})&=|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{p-2}A(u)[\nabla\tilde{u},\nabla u]+g+{\rm div}F.\end{split}

Since by assumption
\tilde{u}\in W^{1,p_{0}}(B(0,3))
, then for some
0<\sigma\leq p_{0}-p
with
\sigma<1
, we can find (see
[
6
, Theorem 8.1]
and
[
7
, Theorem 4]
) via Hodge decomposition
\varphi\in W^{1,\frac{p+\sigma}{1+\sigma}}_{0}(B(0,3))
and
B\in L^{\frac{p+\sigma}{1+\sigma}}(B(0,3))
such that

|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{\sigma}\nabla\tilde{u}=\nabla\varphi+B\quad\text{in $B(0,3)$},

with the estimates

(3.7)

\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{\frac{p+\sigma}{1+\sigma}}(B(0,3))}\precsim\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p+\sigma}(B(0,3))}^{1+\sigma}

and

(3.8)

\|B\|_{L^{\frac{p+\sigma}{1+\sigma}}(B(0,3))}\precsim\sigma\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p+\sigma}(B(0,3))}^{1+\sigma}.

The constants in inequalities (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) are independent of
p
and
\sigma
, provided
p\in[n,n+1]
and
\sigma\in[0,1]
.

Since
{\rm supp\,}\tilde{u}\subset B(0,3)
, we have

\begin{split}&\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p+\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p+\sigma}\\
&=\int_{B(0,3)}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{p-2}\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\varphi+\int_{B(0,3)}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{p-2}\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot B\\
&\leq\left|\int_{B(0,3)}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{p-2}\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\varphi\right|+\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p+\sigma}(B(0,3))}^{p-1}\|B\|_{L^{\frac{p+\sigma}{1+\sigma}}(B(0,3))}\\
&\leq C\left|\int_{B(0,3)}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{p-2}\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\varphi\right|+C\sigma\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p+\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p+\sigma}.\end{split}

The constant
C
is independent of
\sigma
. Thus for
\sigma=(2C)^{-1}
, we can absorb the second term on the right-hand side, and arrive at

(3.9)

\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p+\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p+\sigma}\precsim\left|\int_{B(0,3)}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{p-2}\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\varphi\right|,

with constants depending on
n
, but uniform in
p\in[n,n+1]
.
For the fixed
\sigma=(2C)^{-1}
that is not going to change anymore, let
p_{1}\in(n,n+1)
be so that

\frac{p_{1}+\sigma}{1+\sigma}<n.

This is possible because
n\geq 2
. Then we have

(3.10)

\sup_{p\in[n,p_{1}]}\frac{p+\sigma}{1+\sigma}<n.

We continue with (
3.9
) (where constants are independent of
p\in[n,p_{1}]
), using (
3.6
), we obtain

(3.11)

\begin{split}\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p+\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p+\sigma}\precsim&\left|\int_{B(0,3)}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{p-2}\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\varphi\right|\\
\precsim&\int_{B(0,3)}|\nabla u||\nabla\tilde{u}|^{p-1}\left|\varphi\right|+\int_{B(0,3)}\left|g\right|\left|\varphi\right|+\int_{B(0,3)}\left|F\right|\left|\nabla\varphi\right|.\end{split}

We apply Hölder inequality to the first term with exponents

\frac{1}{n}+\frac{p-1}{p+\sigma}+\frac{1}{q}=1,

where

\frac{1}{q}=1-\frac{p-1}{p+\sigma}-\frac{1}{n}=\frac{1+\sigma}{p+\sigma}-\frac{1}{n}\leq 1.

Hence, we obtain that
q=\left(\frac{p+\sigma}{1+\sigma}\right)^{*}
.
By the Sobolev–Poincaré inequality (which we can apply with constant independent of
p
because (
3.10
) holds) and by (
3.7
) we get

(3.12)

\begin{split}\int_{B(0,3)}|\nabla u||\nabla\tilde{u}|^{p-1}\left|\varphi\right|&\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{n}(B(0,3))}\,\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p+\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p-1}\,\|\varphi\|_{L^{\left(\frac{p+\sigma}{1+\sigma}\right)^{*}}(B(0,3))}\\
&\precsim\|\nabla u\|_{L^{n}(B(0,3))}\,\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p+\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p-1}\,\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{\frac{p+\sigma}{1+\sigma}}(B(0,3))}\\
&\precsim\|\nabla u\|_{L^{n}(B(0,3))}\,\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p+\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p+\sigma}.\end{split}

We also have
\left(\frac{p+\sigma}{1+\sigma}\right)^{*}>p
. Indeed,
the inequality is equivalent to
\frac{p+\sigma}{1+\sigma}>\frac{np}{n+p}
, which in turn is equivalent to

p^{2}+\sigma(n-(n-1)p)>0.

Since
p\geq n\geq 2
, we have
n-(n-1)p\leq 0
, and using
0<\sigma<1
we obtain

p^{2}+\sigma(n-(n-1)p)\geq p^{2}+(n-(n-1)p)=p(p-n)+n+p>0.

This proves the claimed inequality
\left(\frac{p+\sigma}{1+\sigma}\right)^{*}>p
.

Hence, we obtain using (
3.7
) and then (
3.4
)

(3.13)

\begin{split}\int_{B(0,3)}\left|g\right|\left|\varphi\right|&\leq\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B(0,3))}\,\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}(B(0,3))}\\
&\precsim\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B(0,3))}\,\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{\frac{p+\sigma}{1+\sigma}}(B(0,3))}\\
&\precsim\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B(0,2))}^{p-1}\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p+\sigma}(B(0,3))}^{1+\sigma}.\end{split}

To estimate the last term of (
3.11
), we note that since
\sigma<1
we have
\frac{p}{2}\leq\frac{p+\sigma}{1+\sigma}
. Hence, from Hölder inequality, (
3.7
), and (
3.5
) we obtain

(3.14)

\begin{split}\int_{B(0,3)}\left|F\right|\left|\nabla\varphi\right|&\leq\|F\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-2}}(B(0,3))}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{\frac{p}{2}}(B(0,3))}\precsim\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B(0,2))}^{p-2}\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p+\sigma}(B(0,3))}^{1+\sigma}.\end{split}

Combining (
3.11
) with (
3.12
), (
3.13
), and (
3.14
) we obtain

\begin{split}\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p+\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p+\sigma}\precsim\ \|\nabla u\|_{L^{n}(B(0,3))}\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p+\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p+\sigma}+\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B(0,3))}^{p-1}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B(0,3))}^{p-2}\right)\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p+\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1+\sigma}.\end{split}

With the smallness assumption (
3.2
), and Young’s inequality for exponents
\frac{1+\sigma}{p+\sigma}+\frac{p-1}{p+\sigma}=1
with
\delta>0
, we find for all
p\in[n,p_{1}]

\begin{split}\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p+\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p+\sigma}\leq&\ C(n,\mathcal{N})\left(\varepsilon\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p+\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p+\sigma}+\delta\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p+\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p+\sigma}\right)\\
&+C(\delta,n,\mathcal{N})\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B(0,3))}^{p-1}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B(0,3))}^{p-2}\right)^{\frac{p+\sigma}{p-1}}.\end{split}

Now, since
\frac{p+\sigma}{p-1}\leq\frac{n+1}{n-1}
for
p\in[n,p_{1}]
, provided that
\delta
and
\varepsilon
are small enough, we obtain

\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p+\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p+\sigma}\precsim_{\delta,n}\left(1+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B(0,3))}^{p-1}\right)^{\frac{n+1}{n-1}}.

In particular, since
\eta\equiv 1
in
B(0,1)
,

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{n+\sigma}(B(0,1))}^{n+\sigma}\precsim_{\delta,n}\left(1+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B(0,3))}^{p-1}\right)^{\frac{n+1}{n-1}}.

∎

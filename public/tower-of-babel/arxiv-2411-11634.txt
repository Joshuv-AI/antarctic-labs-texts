Endomorphism rings of toroidal solenoids
Maria Sabitova

Abstract
. We study the endomorphism ring End ⁡ ( G A ) \operatorname{End}(G_{A}) of a subgroup G A G_{A} of ℚ n {\mathbb{Q}}^{n} defined by a non-singular n × n n\times n -matrix A A with integer entries. In the case when the characteristic polynomial of A A is irreducible and an extra assumption holds if n n is not prime, we show that End ⁡ ( G A ) \operatorname{End}(G_{A}) is commutative and can be identified with a subring of the number field generated by an eigenvalue of A A . The obtained results can be applied to studying endomorphisms of associated toroidal solenoids and ℤ n {\mathbb{Z}}^{n} -odometers. In particular, we build a connection between toroidal solenoids and 𝒮 {\mathcal{S}} -integer dynamical systems, provide a formula for the number of periodic points of a toroidal solenoid endomorphism, and show that the linear representation group of a ℤ n {\mathbb{Z}}^{n} -odometer is computable.



1.
Introduction

We study the endomorphism ring of a subgroup of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}
defined by a matrix with integer entries. The group arises naturally as the character group of a toroidal solenoid. More precisely, let
A\in\operatorname{M}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})
be a non-singular
n\times n
-matrix with integer entries. Denote

G_{A}=\left\{A^{k}{\bf x}\,|\,{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},\,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\},\quad{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\subseteq G_{A}\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}.

One can readily verify that
G_{A}
is a subgroup of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}
. In
[
S22
]
and
[
S24
]
, we study the classification problem of groups
G_{A}
. In particular, given two matrices
A,B\in\operatorname{M}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})
with integer entries, we answer the question of when the corresponding groups
G_{A}
,
G_{B}
are isomorphic as abstract groups in terms of the matrices
A,B
. We cover the case
n=2
in
[
S22
]
and the case of an arbitrary
n
in
[
S24
]
. Groups
G_{A}
arise in connection with toroidal solenoids. Toroidal solenoids defined by non-singular matrices with integer entries were introduced by M. C. McCord in 1965
[
M65
]
.
A toroidal solenoid
{\mathbb{S}}_{A}
defined by a non-singular
A\in\operatorname{M}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})
is an

n
-dimensional topological abelian group. It is compact, metrizable, and connected, but not locally connected and not path connected. Toroidal solenoids are examples of inverse limit dynamical systems. When
n=1
and
A=d
,
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, solenoids are called
d
-
adic solenoids
or
Vietoris solenoids
. The first examples were studied by L. Vietoris in 1927 for

[
V27
]
and later in 1930 by van Dantzig for an arbitrary

[
D30
]
. It is known that the first Ĉech cohomology group

of

is isomorphic to

, where
A^{t}
is the transpose of
A
.
On the other hand, since
{\mathbb{S}}_{A}
is a compact connected abelian group,
H^{1}({\mathbb{S}}_{A},{\mathbb{Z}})
is isomorphic to the character group

\widehat{{\mathbb{S}}_{A}}
of
{\mathbb{S}}_{A}
. Thus
\widehat{{\mathbb{S}}_{A}}\cong G_{A^{t}}
and, using Pontryagin duality theorem,

{\mathbb{S}}_{A}\cong\widehat{G_{A^{t}}}
as topological groups, where
G_{A^{t}}
is endowed with the discrete topology,
the dual
\widehat{G_{A^{t}}}
is endowed with the compact-open topology, and
{\mathbb{S}}_{A}
is endowed with the topology of an inverse limit. Groups
G_{A}
also arise as the first cohomology groups of constant base
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-odometers

defined by
A^{t}

[
GPS19
]
and as the dimension groups of subshifts of finite type defined by

[
BS24
]
.

In this paper, we study the endomorphism ring
\operatorname{End}(G_{A})
of

for an arbitrary
n
. Based on our work in
[
S22
]
and
[
S24
]
, we give a general criterion for a matrix with rational entries
T\in\operatorname{M}_{n}({\mathbb{Q}})
to define an endomorphism of
G_{A}
, equivalently,
T(G_{A})\subseteq G_{A}
(Theorem
5
) as well as a more concrete description when
n=2
(Section
3.3
). In the case when the characteristic polynomial of
A
is irreducible and an extra assumption holds if

is not prime, we prove that

is either zero or
T
commutes with
A
. Moreover, the eigenvalues of

are elements of the form

, where
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
A

, and

generated by

(Proposition

is a commutative ring, and

is a finitely generated abelian group. As a consequence, considering a toroidal solenoid

as a dynamical system with the automorphism
\sigma
defined by multiplication by
A
, it shows that every homomorphism of
{\mathbb{S}}_{A}
as a topological group is a morphism of the dynamical system

({\mathbb{S}}_{A},\sigma)
. Other noteworthy consequences include the connection between toroidal solenoids and
{\mathcal{S}}
-integer dynamical systems defined in
[
CEW97
]
. It turns out that one can consider a toroidal solenoid as a similar but more general object than an
{\mathcal{S}}
-integer dynamical system. The connection allows us to obtain a formula for the number of periodic points of an endomorphism of
{\mathbb{S}}_{A}
similar to the one in
[
CEW97
]
. The case of
n=2
is covered in
[
HL23
]
for a more general class of toroidal solenoids, and our formula holds in higher-dimensions for
{\mathbb{S}}_{A}
. We also apply our results to
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-odometers. We recover results of
[
CP24
]
in the case when

and generalize them to higher-dimensions. We also discuss the question of
[
CP24
]

3.1.
Localization and easy cases

Let
A\in\operatorname{M}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})
be a non-singular
n\times n
-matrix with integer entries. Denote

(1)

G_{A}=\left\{A^{k}{\bf x}\,|\,{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},\,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\},\quad{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\subseteq G_{A}\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}^{n},

where

is written as a column. Denote by
\operatorname{End}(G_{A})

the endomorphism ring of

, consisting of all (group) homomorphisms
\phi:G_{A}\longrightarrow G_{A}
. Denote

(2)

{\mathcal{R}}={\mathcal{R}}(A)={\mathbb{Z}}\left[\frac{1}{\det A}\right]=\left\{\frac{k}{(\det A)^{l}}\,\,\Big|\,\,k,l\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}.

If

to

is given by a matrix

, there exists

. Note that

.
Let

{\mathcal{P}}={\mathcal{P}}(A)=\{\text{primes }p\in{\mathbb{N}}\text{ dividing }\det A\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}(A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{p\in{\mathcal{P}}\,|\,h_{A}\not\equiv x^{n}\,(\text{mod }p)\right\},

where
h_{A}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
is the characteristic polynomial of
A
.

For a prime
p\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
denote the ring of

p
-adic integers and let
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
denote the field of
p
-adic numbers. Let
\overline{G}_{A,p}=G_{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}
, so that

\overline{G}_{A,p}=\left\{A^{k}{\bf x}\,|\,{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n},\,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\},\quad{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}\subseteq\overline{G}_{A,p}\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n}.

We know that

(3)

\overline{G}_{A,p}\cong{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{t_{p}}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n-t_{p}}

as
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-modules, and

equals the multiplicity of zero in the reduction of

modulo

[

.

Clearly, for

, then

Lemma 1
.

For
T\in\operatorname{M}_{n}({\mathbb{Q}})
, we have that
T\in\operatorname{End}(G_{A})
if and only if
T\in\operatorname{M}_{n}({\mathcal{R}})
and

T\in\operatorname{End}(\overline{G}_{A,p})

for any
p\in{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
T\in\operatorname{M}_{n}({\mathbb{Q}})
.
By above, the conditions are necessary. We now show that they are sufficient.
It follows from
[
F73
, p. 183, Lemma 93.2]
that

(4)

G_{A}=\bigcap_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}}({\mathcal{R}}^{n}\cap\overline{G}_{A,p})

(see also
[

for more detail). Also,
\overline{G}_{A,p}={\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n}
for any prime
p\in{\mathcal{P}}\backslash{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}
by (

). Thus,
T(\overline{G}_{A,p})\subseteq\overline{G}_{A,p}
for any
p\in{\mathcal{P}}\backslash{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}

T\in\operatorname{M}_{n}({\mathcal{R}})
and

T\in\operatorname{End}(\overline{G}_{A,p})

for any
p\in{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}
, then
T\in\operatorname{End}(G_{A})
by (

).
∎

Lemma 2
.

(1)

If
{\mathcal{P}}=\emptyset
, equivalently,
A\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})
, then

\operatorname{End}({G}_{A})=\operatorname{M}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}).

(2)

If

and

, then

\operatorname{End}({G}_{A})=\operatorname{M}_{n}({\mathcal{R}}).

Proof.

Clearly,

if and only if
\det A=\pm 1
if and only if

, and Lemma
2
(1) is clear.
Lemma
2
(2) follows from Lemma
1
.
∎

Thus, by Lemma
2
, for the rest of the paper we assume

and
{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset

6.
Toroidal solenoids

In this section,
we apply our results concerning groups
G_{A}
and their endomorphisms to the case of toroidal solenoids.
Let
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
denote a torus considered as a quotient of

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
by its subgroup
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. A matrix
A\in\operatorname{M}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})
induces a map
A:{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
,

A\left([{\bf x}]\right)=[A{\bf x}]
,
[{\bf x}]\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
,
{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Consider the inverse system
(M_{j},f_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, where
f_{j}:M_{j+1}\longrightarrow M_{j}
,

M_{j}={\mathbb{T}}^{n}
and
f_{j}=A
for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
The inverse limit
{{\mathbb{S}}}_{A}
of the system is called a (
toroidal
)
solenoid
. As a set,
{\mathbb{S}}_{A}
is a subset of

\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}M_{j}
consisting of points
(z_{j})\in\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}M_{j}
such that
z_{j}\in M_{j}
and
f_{j}(z_{j+1})=z_{j}
for
\forall j\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
i.e.
,

(47)

{\mathbb{S}}_{A}=\left\{(z_{j})\in\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\,\,\Big|\,\,z_{j}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n},\,\,A(z_{j+1})=z_{j},\,\,j\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}.

Endowed with the natural group structure and the induced topology from the Tychonoff (product) topology on
\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
,
{\mathbb{S}}_{A}
is an

n
-dimensional topological abelian group. It is compact, metrizable, and connected, but not locally connected and not path connected. The map
\sigma:{\mathbb{S}}_{A}\to{\mathbb{S}}_{A}
induced by multiplication by
A
,
(z_{j})\mapsto(A(z_{j}))
is an automorphism of
{\mathbb{S}}_{A}
as a topological group, and the pair
({\mathbb{S}}_{A},\sigma)
is a dynamical system.
The endomorphism ring
\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{S}}_{A},\sigma)
of the dynamical system
({\mathbb{S}}_{A},\sigma)
consists of
endomorphisms of
{\mathbb{S}}_{A}
as a topological group that commute with
\sigma
. It is known that

\widehat{G_{A^{t}}}\cong{\mathbb{S}}_{A}
. Indeed,
G_{A^{t}}
is a direct limit of groups
(A^{t})^{-j}{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
. Here, each
(A^{t})^{-j}{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and the maps
(A^{t})^{-j}{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\to(A^{t})^{-(j+1)}{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
are inclusions. Applying the functor
\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(-,{\mathbb{T}}^{1})
to the system, we obtain the inverse limit of groups

\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}((A^{t})^{-j}{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},{\mathbb{T}}^{1})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n},{\mathbb{T}}^{1})\cong{\mathbb{T}}^{n}

with the maps
f_{j}
as above that defines
{\mathbb{S}}_{A}
. This gives an isomorphism of topological groups
\widehat{G_{A^{t}}}\cong{\mathbb{S}}_{A}
, where
G_{A^{t}}
is endowed with the discrete topology and
\widehat{G_{A^{t}}}
is endowed with the compact-open topology. Moreover, since
G_{A^{t}}
is a locally compact abelian group, it follows from the Pontryagin duality theorem that the map between the rings
\operatorname{End}(G_{A^{t}})
and
\operatorname{End}(\widehat{G_{A^{t}}})
given by

\phi\mapsto(\chi\mapsto\chi\circ\phi)
,
\phi\in\operatorname{End}(G_{A^{t}})
,
\chi\in\widehat{G_{A^{t}}}
,
is a ring isomorphism between the opposite ring
\operatorname{End}(G_{A^{t}})^{op}
of
\operatorname{End}(G_{A^{t}})
and

\operatorname{End}(\widehat{G_{A^{t}}})
. Thus, we have a ring isomorphism
\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{S}}_{A})\cong\operatorname{End}(G_{A^{t}})^{op}
, under which

\sigma
corresponds to multiplication by
A^{t}
on
G_{A^{t}}
. Therefore,
\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{S}}_{A},\sigma)
is isomorphic to the subring of
\operatorname{End}(G_{A^{t}})^{op}
consisting of
T\in\operatorname{M}_{n}({\mathbb{Q}})\cap\operatorname{End}(G_{A^{t}})
that commute with
A^{t}
. By Corollary
9
, under the assumptions of Proposition
8
,
\operatorname{End}(G_{A^{t}})
is commutative and, in particular, every endomorphism of
G_{A^{t}}
commutes with
A^{t}
. This implies that every endomorphism of
{\mathbb{S}}_{A}
commutes with

\sigma
and hence is an endomorphism of the dynamical system
({\mathbb{S}}_{A},\sigma)
.
Thus, we have the following

Proposition 24
.

Assume
A\in\operatorname{M}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})
is non-singular with an irreducible characteristic polynomial,
{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
, and
there exists a prime
p\in{\mathbb{N}}
with

(n,t_{p})=1
. Then
\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{S}}_{A})
is a commutative ring and, in particular,
\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{S}}_{A},\sigma)=\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{S}}_{A})
.

A numerical method to simulate the stochastic linear-quadratic optimal control problem with control constraint in higher dimensions
Abhishek Chaudhary

Abstract
We propose an implementable numerical scheme for the discretization of linear-quadratic optimal control problems involving SDEs in higher dimensions with control constraint . For time discretization, we employ the implicit Euler scheme, deriving discrete optimality conditions that involve time discretization of a backward stochastic differential equations. We develop a recursive formula to compute conditional expectations in the time discretization of the BSDE whose computation otherwise is the most computationally demanding step. Additionally, we present the error analysis for the rate of convergence. We provide numerical examples to demonstrate the efficiency of our scheme in higher dimensions.



Keywords:
stochastic differential equations; additive noise; Wiener process; quadratic control problem; control constraint; BSDE; Pontryagin’s maximum principle; Euler method; gradient descent method.

Introduction

Optimal control problems are central in many fields such as engineering, economics, and finance, where the objective is to find a control policy that minimizes (or maximizes) a certain cost functional over a given time horizon. In real-world scenarios, systems are often subject to uncertainties that can be modeled using stochastic processes. A powerful mathematical tool to describe such systems is the stochastic differential equation (SDE), which allows us to account for randomness and noise in the system dynamics.

In this work, we focus on a class of optimal control problems where the system is governed by a SDE with additive noise. Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F}=(\mathbb{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
be a stochastic basis. The objective is to find a
\mathbb{F}
-adapted control process
{\bf u}^{*}\equiv\{{\bf u}^{*}(t)=({\bf u}_{i}^{*}(t))_{1\leq\,i\leq\,m}:a_{i}\leq\,{\bf u}_{i}^{*}(t)\leq\,b_{i}\,\forall\,t\in[0,T]\}
that minimizes the expected quadratic cost functional
(\alpha>0)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{x},{\bf u})=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(\left<\mathbf{x}(s),\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}(s)\right>+\alpha\left\langle{\bf u}(s),{\bf u}(s)\right\rangle\right){\rm d}s+\left<\mathbf{x}(T),\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}(T)\right>\right],

(1.1)

subject to the system dynamics described by the following SDE with additive noise

\displaystyle{\rm d}\mathbf{x}(s)

\displaystyle=\big[\mathrm{M}\mathbf{x}(s)+\mathrm{N}{\bf u}(s)\big]{\rm d}s+\mathbf{\sigma}(s){\rm d}\mathbf{w}(s)\quad\forall s\in[0,T],

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}(0)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{x}_{0}.

(1.2)

where
\mathbf{x}:\Omega\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the state variable,
{\bf u}:\Omega\times[0,T]\to\Pi_{i=1}^{m}[a_{i},b_{i}]
is the
control constraint
,
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the initial datum,
\mathbf{\sigma}:\Omega\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}
is the noise intensity matrix, and
\mathbf{w}
is a standard
\mathbb{R}^{k}
-valued Brownian motion. For the full list of assumptions, we refer to Section
2.2
. We refer
“minimize (
1.1
) subject to (
1.2
)”
as our
\mathbf{SLQ}
problem.

1.1
Previous works

In general, optimal control for
\mathbf{SLQ}
lacks an explicit form, necessitating the use of numerical schemes to approximate solutions. To address this, a Partial Differential Equation (PDE) known as the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (HJB) equation, which is satisfied by the value function, can be derived from the dynamic programming principle (see, e.g.,
[
16
]
). A Markov chain approximation method is one such approach, as outlined by
[
8
]
and subsequent works. It is important to note that the derivations for these equations are typically formal, and HJB equations themselves often lack rigorous well-posedness, which limits our understanding of solution properties.

Alternatively, the Pontryagin-type maximum principle offers another approach by transforming the
\mathbf{SLQ}
problem’s solvability into that of forward-backward stochastic differential equations, facilitating numerical computation of optimal controls. Various numerical schemes leveraging the Pontryagin-type maximum principle have been developed, such as those by
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
, and
[
9
]
, among others. In
[
15
]
, a time-implicit discretization for stochastic linear quadratic problems subject to SDE with control-dependence noises is proposed, and the convergence rate of this discretization is proved. However, these works often assume deterministic controls or exclude
control constraint
.

When
control constraints
are present in
\mathbf{SLQ}
problems, Riccati equation-based methods are not applicable any more. This paper aims to bridge this gap by proposing an approximation method specifically for
\mathbf{SLQ}
problems involving
control constraints
, and we establish rates of convergence for the proposed method.

1.2
Our aim and contribution in this work

For the linear-quadratic control problem considered here, the Pontryagin’s maximum principle (PMP) implies that the optimal control
{\bf u}^{*}
can be characterized using the adjoint process
\mathbf{p}
, which satisfies the following backward stochastic differential equation (BSDE):

\displaystyle\begin{cases}{\rm d}\mathbf{p}(s)=-\big[\mathrm{M}\mathbf{p}(s)-\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}^{*}(s)\big]{\rm d}s+\mathbf{z}(s){\rm d}\mathbf{w}(s)&\forall\,s\in[0,T],\\
\mathbf{p}(T)=-\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}^{*}(T),\end{cases}

(1.3)

where
\mathbf{z}
is a process that represents the martingale component of the adjoint process. The optimal control
{\bf u}^{*}
is then given by

\displaystyle{\bf u}^{*}(s)=\mathcal{P}_{ad}[\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathrm{N}^{T}\mathbf{p}(s)]\quad\forall s\in[0,T].

(1.4)

Substituting this expression for
{\bf u}^{*}
into the forward SDE and the adjoint BSDE (
1.3
), one obtains a coupled system of forward-backward SDEs (FBSDEs). The solvability of this FBSDE system is a crucial step in characterizing the optimal control and solving the
\mathbf{SLQ}
problem.

1.2.1
Our contribution

The objective of this paper is to develop an efficient numerical method for solving the
\mathbf{SLQ}
problem with the help of the related FBSDEs (
1.2
)-(
1.4
). In particular, it is well-known that to numerically solve the (high-dimensional) BSDE (
2.4
) requires significant computational resources which practically limit this approval to (small) dimensional
d
, see
[
4
,
5
]
.

The main novelty of our approach lies in the use of a recursive formula (see below (
3.14
)) to compute the approximate solution to the BSDE (
2.4
), which significantly enhances the efficiency of the approximation. This recursive method allows us to iteratively compute the state–control approximation (iterate) even in higher dimensions
d
. We remark that more approximation methods based on regression estimators (statistical learning tools) are computationally expensive and suffer from the curse of dimensionality, see
[
4
,
5
]
. To the author’s best knowledge, this is the first attempt for numerical approximation of stochastic linear quadratic optimal control problems with
control constraints
.

The contributions of this work can be summarized as follows:

•

In analytic setup, first we discuss the existence and uniqueness of optimal pair
(\mathbf{x}^{*},{\bf u}^{*})
to
\mathbf{SLQ}
problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). We then give the proof of the Pontryagin’s maximum principle (Theorem

) for the characterization of the optimal pair
(\mathbf{x}^{*},{\bf u}^{*})
.

•

In the second part, we discretize the optimality system (
1.2
)-(
1.4
) in time using the implicit Euler scheme for SDE (
1.2
) and BSDE (
1.3
), and then derive the corresponding discrete optimality conditions (
3.8
). We already emphasized that the presence of conditional expectations in the discrete optimality system (
3.10
) poses a challenge for direct implementation. To solve this decoupled system (
3.10
), we apply a projected gradient descent method
i.e., Algorithm

, which requires again the computation of conditional expectations

to obtain the gradient of the cost function related to discrete
\mathbf{SLQ}_{h}

•

To avoid costly simulations, we develop a recursive formula (

\mathbf{p}_{h}^{(\ell)}
in the decoupled system (
3.10
). This formula leverages the iterative nature of the projected gradient method, the linearity of the optimality system and the additive nature of noise, enabling efficient approximation of the optimal solution
(\mathbf{x}^{*},{\bf u}^{*})
to the

•

In the end, we provide a comprehensive analysis that leads to the convergence rates; see Theorem

. The error analysis also encompasses a wide range of
\mathbf{SLQ}

\mathbf{SLQ}
problems governed by (infinite-dimensional) stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs). Specifically, the matrix
\mathrm{M}
can be viewed as representing a finite-dimensional approximation of an unbounded operator acting on an infinite-dimensional Sobolev space, see Remark

Notations, assumptions and analytic results

In this section, we present some notations, a list of assumptions and the preliminary results necessary for our analysis.

2.1
Notations

Functional spaces:
Let
H
be a separable Hilbert space. We define
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega\times[0,T];H)
as the space of all
H
-valued predictable and
L^{2}
-integrable functions on
\Omega\times[0,T]
. For brevity, we denote the following spaces:

\displaystyle\mathrm{X}

\displaystyle=L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega\times[0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d}),\qquad{\bf U}=L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega\times[0,T];\mathbb{R}^{m})),

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}=L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega\times[0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}),\qquad{\bf U}_{ad}=\{{\bf u}\in{\bf U}:{\bf u}\in\Pi_{i=1}^{m}[a_{i},b_{i}])\}.

Let
\|\cdot\|_{C_{t}\mathbb{L}_{\omega}^{2}}
denote the following norm on
C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}))
: for all
\Phi\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}))
,

\displaystyle\|\Phi\|_{C_{t}\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\omega}}^{2}=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\Phi(t)\|^{2}\big].

For any
l\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote the
\mathbb{L}^{2}
norm as follows: for all
\mathbf{x}\in L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T];\mathbb{R}^{l})
,

\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathbf{x}(s)\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right].

Let
\left<\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>\right>
denote the inner product on
{\bf U}
, while
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>
denotes the inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{l}
for any
l\in\mathbb{N}
.

Matrix norms:
Let
\mathrm{A}=[a_{ij}]\in\mathbb{R}^{l_{1}\times l_{2}}
be a matrix of order
l_{1}\times l_{2}
. The Frobenius norm of matrix
\mathrm{A}
is defined as:

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{A}\|=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{l_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{l_{2}}a_{ij}^{2}}.

The sup-norm of
\mathrm{A}
is denoted by
\|\mathrm{A}\|_{\infty}
and is defined as:

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{A}\|_{\infty}=\sup_{1\leq i\leq l_{1},1\leq j\leq l_{2}}|a_{ij}|.

Time-discretization:
Consider the time interval
[0,T]
, which we uniformly discretize into
N
time steps. The step size is denoted by
h=\frac{T}{N}
, and the discrete times are given by
t_{n}=nh
for
n=0,1,\ldots,N
. At these discrete time points, we approximate the state and adjoint variables. To facilitate our analysis, we define the following spaces:

\displaystyle{\bf U}_{h}

\displaystyle=\left\{{\bf u}_{h}\in{\bf U}:{\bf u}_{h}(s)={\bf u}_{n}\text{ for }s\in[t_{n},t_{n+1}),n=0,\ldots,N-1\right\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{X}_{h}

\displaystyle=\left\{\mathbf{x}_{h}\in\mathrm{X}:\mathbf{x}_{h}(s)=\mathbf{x}_{n}\text{ for }s\in[t_{n},t_{n+1}),n=0,\ldots,N\right\},

\displaystyle{\bf U}_{ad}^{h}

\displaystyle={\bf U}_{h}\cap{\bf U}_{ad}.

These spaces represent the sets of piecewise constant functions on the time interval
[0,T]
, where the control and state variables take constant values on each sub-interval
[t_{n},t_{n+1})
. The norms associated with these spaces are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\|{\bf u}_{h}\|_{{\bf U}_{h}}=\left(h\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\|{\bf u}_{n}\|^{2}\right)^{1/2},\qquad\|\mathbf{x}_{h}\|_{\mathrm{X}_{h}}=\left(h\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

These norms measure the
L^{2}
norm of the discrete control and state variables over the time interval
[0,T]
. We introduce a projection operator
\Pi_{h}:{\bf U}_{ad}\to{\bf U}_{ad}^{h}
defined as follows: for all
{\bf u}\in{\bf U}_{ad}
,

\displaystyle[\Pi_{h}{\bf u}](\cdot,s)={\bf u}(\cdot,t_{n})\quad\forall\,s\in[t_{n},t_{n+1}),\quad n=0,\ldots,N-1.

This projection effectively maps the continuous control function
{\bf u}_{h}
onto a piecewise constant function over each time interval
[t_{n},t_{n+1})
.

2.3
Analytical results

In this subsection, we present analytic results concerning the existence and uniqueness of solutions to the forward stochastic differential equation (SDE) (

Lemma 2.1

(forward SDE)
.

Let
{\bf u}\in{\bf U}
and
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, there exists a unique solution
\mathbf{x}\in\mathrm{X}
to SDE (
1.2
) such that there exists a positive constant
C_{T}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{x}(s)\|^{2}\right]+\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{x}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\leq C_{T}\left(\|\mathbf{x}_{0}\|^{2}+\|\mathrm{N}{\bf u}(\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|\mathbf{\sigma}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right),

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{x}(s)\|^{2}\big]+\|\mathrm{M}\mathbf{x}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\leq\,C_{T}\big(\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{x}_{0}\|^{2}+\|\mathrm{N}{\bf u}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\sigma\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\big),

(2.2)

and

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathrm{M}\mathbf{x}(s)\|^{2}\big]+\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathrm{M}\mathbf{x}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\leq\,C_{T}(\|\mathrm{M}\mathbf{x}_{0}\|^{2}+\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathrm{N}{\bf u}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|\mathrm{M}\sigma\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}).

(2.3)

Proof.

Existence and uniqueness follow from the standard SDE theory, see
[
1
]
. The proof of estimates is a direct consequence of Itô formula. For completeness of the proof, we give the details. For estimate (
2.1
), we apply Itô’s formula to the function
\mathbf{x}\to\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2}
, to get
\mathbb{P}
-a.s., for all
s\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\left<\mathbf{x}(s),\mathbf{x}(s)\right>=

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{x}_{0}\|^{2}+2\int_{0}^{s}\left<\mathbf{x}(r),\mathrm{M}\mathbf{x}(r)\right>{\rm d}r+2\int_{0}^{s}\left<\mathbf{x}(r),\mathrm{N}{\bf u}(r)\right>{\rm d}r+2\int_{0}^{s}\left<\mathbf{x}(r),\sigma(r)\right>{\rm d}\mathbf{w}(r)

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{s}\mbox{Tr}\big(\sigma(r)\sigma^{T}(r)\big){\rm d}r.

It implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{x}(s)\|^{2}\big]+2\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{s}\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{x}(r)\|^{2}{\rm d}s\bigg]\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{x}_{0}\|^{2}+\mathbb{E}\big[\int_{0}^{s}\|\mathbf{x}(r)\|^{2}{\rm d}r\big]+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{s}\|\mathrm{N}{\bf u}(r)\|^{2}{\rm d}r\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{E}\big[\int_{0}^{s}\|\sigma(r)\|^{2}{\rm d}r\big].

By using Grönswall’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{x}(s)\|^{2}\big]+2\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{x}(r)\|^{2}{\rm d}s\bigg]\leq

\displaystyle\,\,e^{T}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\|\mathbf{x}_{0}\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathrm{N}{\bf u}(r)\|^{2}{\rm d}r+\int_{0}^{T}\|\sigma(r)\|^{2}{\rm d}r\bigg].

Similarly, we can apply Itô to
\mathbf{x}\to\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{x}\|^{2}
and
\mathbf{x}\to\|\mathrm{M}\mathbf{x}\|^{2}
to obtain the estimate (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), respectively.
∎

Next, we present a result related to the backward SDE (
1.3
).

Lemma 2.2

(backward SDE)
.

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\mathrm{X}
. Then, there exists a unique solution
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{z})\in\mathrm{X}\times\mathbf{Z}
to the following BSDE

\displaystyle\begin{cases}{\rm d}\mathbf{p}(s)=-\left[\mathrm{M}\mathbf{p}(s)-\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}(s)\right]{\rm d}s+\mathbf{z}(s){\rm d}\mathbf{w}(s),&\forall\,s\in[0,T],\\
\mathbf{p}(T)=-\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}(T).\end{cases}

(2.4)

Moreover, there exists a positive constant
C_{T}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{p}(s)\|^{2}\right]+\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{p}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\|\mathbf{z}(\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{T}\big(\|\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}(\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}(T)\|^{2}\big]\big),

(2.5)

and

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{p}(s)\|^{2}\big]+\|\mathrm{M}\mathbf{p}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{z}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\leq\,C_{T}\big(\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}(T)\|^{2}\big]+\|\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\big).

(2.6)

Proof.

This existence and uniqueness follow from the standard BSDE theory, see
[
16
,
11
]
. For estimates, one can follow similar lines as done in the proof of Lemma
2.1
.
∎

Corollary 2.1

(Time-regularity)
.

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\mathrm{X}
. Let
\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}]\in\mathrm{X}
be the unique solution to the BSDE (
2.4
). Then there exists a positive constant
C_{T}
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}]-\Pi_{h}\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}]\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{T}h(\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}(T))\|^{2}\big]+\|\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}).

(2.7)

Proof.

For convenience we take
\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}]=\mathbf{p}
. From (
2.4
), we obtain for
t\in[t_{n},t_{n+1}]
,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely,

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{p}(t)-\mathbf{p}(t_{n})\|^{2}\leq C\bigg(\int_{t_{n}}^{t}\|\mathrm{M}\mathbf{p}(s)\|^{2}{\rm d}s+\int_{t_{n}}^{t}\|\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}(s)\|^{2}{\rm d}s+\|\int_{t_{n}}^{t}\mathbf{z}(s){\rm d}\mathbf{w}(s)\|^{2}\bigg).

With the help of Itô isometry, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{{t_{n}}}^{t_{n+1}}\|\mathbf{p}(t)-\mathbf{p}(t_{n})\|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg]\leq\,C\,h\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|\mathrm{M}\mathbf{p}(s)\|^{2}{\rm d}s+\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}(s)\|^{2}{\rm d}s+\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|\mathbf{z}(s)\|^{2}{\rm d}s\bigg].

It implies that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{p}-\Pi_{h}\mathbf{p}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq C\,h\bigg(\|\mathrm{M}\mathbf{p}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\|\mathbf{z}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\bigg).

Now we use (
2.5
)-(
2.6
) to conclude the result.
∎

(2.11)

and

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathrm{M}\mathbf{y}(s)\|^{2}\big]+\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathrm{M}\mathbf{y}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\leq\,C_{T}\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathrm{N}{\bf u}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

(2.12)

Proof.

This proof is similar to Lemma
2.1
.
∎

Lemma 1

(time regularity estimate)
.

Let
{\bf u}\in{\bf U}
. Let
\mathbf{y}\equiv\mathbf{y}[{\bf u}]\in{\mathrm{X}}
be the unique solution to (
1.2
) with initial data
\mathbf{y}(0)=0
. Then the following time-regularity estimate holds:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|\mathrm{M}_{1}(\mathbf{y}(s)-\mathbf{y}(t_{n+1}))\|^{2}{\rm d}s\leq\,C_{T}\,h^{2}\,\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathrm{N}{\bf u}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

(2.13)

Proof.

We prove this result in the following two steps.

Step 1.
For all
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{y}_{t}(t)=\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathrm{M}\mathbf{y}(t)+\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathrm{N}{\bf u}(t).

This implies that

\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{y}_{t}\|\leq\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathrm{M}\mathbf{y}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathrm{N}{\bf u}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

Using the estimate from (
2.12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{y}_{t}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{T}\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathrm{N}{\bf u}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

(2.14)

Step 2.
By applying Hölder’s inequality, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|\mathrm{M}_{1}(\mathbf{y}(s)-\mathbf{y}(t_{n+1}))\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\bigg\|\int_{s}^{t_{n+1}}\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{y}_{t}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\bigg\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\bigg(\int_{s}^{t_{n+1}}\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{y}_{t}(r)\|\,\mathrm{d}r\bigg)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}(t_{n+1}-s)\int_{s}^{t_{n+1}}\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{y}_{t}(r)\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq h^{2}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{y}(r)\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r.

This shows that

\sum_{n=0}^{N}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|\mathrm{M}_{1}(\mathbf{y}(s)-\mathbf{y}(t_{n+1}))\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq h^{2}\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{y}_{t}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

Finally, using the estimate (
2.14
), we obtain the desired result.
∎

The following lemma confirms that for any initial state, there exists a unique optimal pair of state and control processes, ensuring the well-posedness of the
\mathbf{SLQ}
problem.

Theorem 2.1

(Pontryagin’s maximum principle)
.

For any
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists a unique optimal pair
(\mathbf{x}^{*},{\bf u}^{*})\in\mathrm{X}\times{\bf U}_{ad}
to the
\mathbf{SLQ}
problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
). Moreover, there exists a positive constant
C_{T,d}
such that

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\mathrm{B}}\mathbf{x}^{*}(\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\alpha}{\bf u}^{*}(\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\mathbb{E}\big[\|\sqrt{\mathrm{D}}\mathbf{x}^{*}(T)\|^{2}\big]\leq C_{T,d}(\|\mathbf{x}_{0}\|^{2}+\|\sigma\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}).

(2.15)

Moreover, the following optimal equality holds,

\displaystyle{\bf u}^{*}(s)=\mathcal{P}_{ad}[\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathrm{N}^{T}\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}](s)]\quad\forall s\in[0,T],

(2.16)

where
\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}]\in\mathrm{X}
is first solution component to (
2.4
), and integral inequality holds, for every
{\bf u}\in{\bf U}_{ad}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\left<\mathbf{y}[{\bf u}-{\bf u}^{*}](s),\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}^{*}(s)\right>+\alpha\left<{\bf u}(s)-{\bf u}^{*},{\bf u}^{*}(s)\right>{\rm d}s+\left<\mathbf{y}[{\bf u}-{\bf u}^{*}](T),\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}^{*}(T)\right>\bigg]\geq\,0.

(2.17)

Proof.

We prove this result through the following steps:

Step 1.
Define the reduced optimal control
\mathcal{\hat{J}}:{\bf U}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\mathcal{\hat{J}}({\bf u})=\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{x}[{\bf u}],{\bf u}).

It is clear that
\mathcal{\hat{J}}
is Fréchet differentiable on
{\bf U}
and strictly convex due to the quadratic nature of the cost functional. Therefore, there exists a global minimum
{\bf u}^{*}\in{\bf U}_{ad}
such that

\mathcal{\hat{J}}({\bf u}^{*})=\min_{{\bf u}\in{\bf U}_{ad}}\mathcal{\hat{J}}({\bf u}).

Since
{\bf u}^{*}
is a global minimizer for
\mathcal{\hat{J}}
, it follows that for all
{\bf u}\in{\bf U}_{ad}
(see
[
7
, Theorem 1.46]
),

\displaystyle\left<\left\langle\mathcal{\hat{J}}^{\prime}({\bf u}^{*}),{\bf u}-{\bf u}^{*}\right\rangle\right>\geq 0.

(2.18)

Step 2.
The Fréchet derivative of
\mathcal{\hat{J}}
at
{\bf u}^{*}
in the direction
{\bf v}\in{\bf U}
is given by

\left<\left\langle\mathcal{\hat{J}}^{\prime}({\bf u}^{*}),{\bf v}\right\rangle\right>=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\big(\left\langle\mathbf{y}[{\bf v}](s),\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}^{*}(s)\right\rangle+\alpha\left\langle{\bf v}(s),{\bf u}^{*}(s)\right\rangle\big)\,\mathrm{d}s+\left\langle\mathbf{y}[{\bf v}](T),\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}^{*}(T)\right\rangle\bigg].

Using this expression with
{\bf v}={\bf u}-{\bf u}^{*}
, we obtain for all
{\bf u}\in{\bf U}_{ad}

\displaystyle\left<\left\langle\mathcal{\hat{J}}^{\prime}({\bf u}^{*}),{\bf u}-{\bf u}^{*}\right\rangle\right>

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\big(\left\langle\mathbf{y}[{\bf u}-{\bf u}^{*}](s),\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}^{*}(s)\right\rangle+\alpha\left\langle{\bf u}(s)-{\bf u}^{*}(s),{\bf u}^{*}(s)\right\rangle\big)\,\mathrm{d}s+\left\langle\mathbf{y}[{\bf u}-{\bf u}^{*}](T),\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}^{*}(T)\right\rangle\bigg].

(2.19)

Combining (
2.18
) and (
2.19
), we obtain inequality (
2.17
).

Step 3.
Let
{\bf v}\in{\bf U}
be arbitrary. Applying Itô’s formula to the pairing
\left\langle\mathbf{y}[{\bf v}],\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}]\right\rangle
, we get,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathbf{y}[{\bf v}](T),\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}](T)\right\rangle-\left\langle\mathbf{y}[{\bf v}](0),\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}](0)\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\mathbf{y}_{t}[{\bf v}](s),\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}](s)\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\mathbf{y}[{\bf v}](s),\mathrm{M}\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}](s)\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\mathbf{y}[{\bf v}](s),\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}^{*}(s)\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\mathbf{y}[{\bf v}](s),\mathbf{z}(s)\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{w}(s).

Since
\mathbf{y}(0)=0
and
\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}](T)=-\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}^{*}(T)
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\mathbf{y}[{\bf v}](s),\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}^{*}(s)\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\left\langle\mathbf{y}[{\bf v}](T),\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}^{*}(T)\right\rangle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\mathbf{y}_{t}[{\bf v}](s),\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}](s)\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s-\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\mathrm{M}\mathbf{y}[{\bf v}](s),\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}](s)\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\mathbf{y}[{\bf v}](s),\mathbf{z}(s)\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{w}(s).

(2.20)

Since
\mathbf{y}[{\bf v}]
solves equation (
2.8
), we also have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\mathbf{y}_{t}(s),\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}](s)\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\mathrm{M}\mathbf{y}[{\bf v}](s),\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}](s)\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\mathrm{N}{\bf v},\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}]\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s.

(2.21)

Substituting (
2.20
) and (
2.21
) and noting that

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\mathbf{y}[{\bf v}],\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}]\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{w}(s)\bigg]=0,

we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\big(\left\langle\mathbf{y}[{\bf v}](s),\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}^{*}(s)\right\rangle+\alpha\left\langle{\bf v}(s),{\bf u}^{*}(s)\right\rangle\big)\,\mathrm{d}s+\left\langle\mathbf{y}[{\bf v}](T),\mathbf{x}^{*}(T)\right\rangle\bigg]=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle{\bf v},\alpha{\bf u}^{*}-\mathrm{N}^{T}\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}]\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg].

(2.22)

For all
{\bf u}\in{\bf U}_{ad}
, by using (
2.17
) and setting
{\bf v}={\bf u}-{\bf u}^{*}
in (
2.22
), we conclude that

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle{\bf u}(s)-{\bf u}^{*}(s),{\bf u}^{*}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathrm{N}^{T}\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}]\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg]\geq 0.

This implies that

{\bf u}^{*}=\mathcal{P}_{ad}[\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathrm{N}^{T}\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}]].

For the estimate (
2.15
), we can conclude that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{x}^{*},{\bf u}^{*})\leq\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{x}[0],{\bf u}[0])\leq C_{T,d}(\|\mathbf{x}_{0}\|^{2}+\|\sigma\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}).

∎

Remark 2.2
.

In the case of free control,
i.e.
,
{\bf U}_{ad}={\bf U}
, the integral identity (
2.17
) evaluates to zero. By following similar lines, we can prove that for all
{\bf u}\in{\bf U}

\displaystyle\mathcal{\hat{J}}^{\prime}({\bf u})=\alpha{\bf u}-\mathrm{N}^{T}\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}[{\bf u}]].

At the stage of the error analysis, establishing the time regularity of the optimal control is essential. To this end, we present the following corollary. In the proof, the optimality condition (
2.16
) plays a key role in enhancing the time regularity of the optimal control.

Corollary 2.2

(Time-regularity estimate)
.

Let
{\bf u}^{*}\in{\bf U}_{ad}
be a unique optimal control to
\mathbf{SLQ}
problem. Then
{\bf u}^{*}\in{\bf U}_{ad}
satisfies the following time regularity estimate

\displaystyle\|{\bf u}^{*}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\leq\,C_{T,d}\,h\big(\|\mathbf{x}_{0}\|^{2}+\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{x}_{0}\|^{2}+\|\sigma\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\sigma\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\big).

(2.23)

Proof.

Starting from the optimality condition (
2.16
) and using the stability property of the projection operator
\mathcal{P}_{ad}
, we have

\displaystyle\|{\bf u}^{*}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{P}_{ad}\bigg[\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathrm{N}(\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}]-\Pi_{h}\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}])\bigg]\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\mathrm{N}(\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}]-\Pi_{h}\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}])\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

Then, by applying inequality (
2.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|{\bf u}^{*}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\mathrm{N}\|^{2}\|\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}]-\Pi_{h}\mathbf{p}[\mathbf{x}^{*}]\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\mathrm{N}\|^{2}\,hC_{T}\big(\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}^{*}(T)\|^{2}+\|\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\big).

Using inequality (
2.2
), we further obtain

\displaystyle\|{\bf u}^{*}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T,d}h\big(\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{x}^{*}(T)\|^{2}+\|\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{T,d}h\big(\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{x}_{0}\|^{2}+\|\mathrm{N}{\bf u}^{*}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\sigma\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\big).

Finally, applying the
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-bound for the optimal pair (
2.15
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\|{\bf u}^{*}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T,d}h\big(\|\mathbf{x}_{0}\|^{2}+\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{x}_{0}\|^{2}+\|\sigma\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\sigma\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\big).

∎

Remark 2.3
.

Note that by explicitly tracking the constant
C_{T,d}
in the above proof, we obtain

\displaystyle C_{T,d}=C_{T}\|\mathrm{N}\|^{2}\max\{\|\mathrm{D}\|_{\infty},\|\sqrt{\mathrm{B}}\|^{2},\|\mathrm{N}\|^{2}\}.

3
Time discretization and an efficient implementable scheme

For a given discrete control
\{{\bf u}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{N-1}\equiv{\bf u}_{h}\in{\bf U}_{h}
, we approximate the state variable using the implicit Euler scheme. The resulting discrete state variable
\mathbf{x}_{h}\equiv\{\mathbf{x}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{N}\in\mathrm{X}_{h}
is governed by the following difference equation:

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{x}_{n}+h\left[\mathrm{M}\mathbf{x}_{n+1}+\mathrm{N}{\bf u}_{n}\right]+\mathbf{\sigma}(t_{n})\Delta\mathbf{w}_{n},\qquad\mathbf{x}_{0}=\mathbf{x},

where
\Delta\mathbf{w}_{n}=\mathbf{w}_{n+1}-\mathbf{w}_{n}
represents the increment of the Brownian motion over the time step
[t_{n},t_{n+1}]
. Rearranging the above equation to solve for
\mathbf{x}_{n+1}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle(\mathbf{I}-h\mathrm{M})\mathbf{x}_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{x}_{n}+h\mathrm{N}{\bf u}_{n}+\mathbf{\sigma}(t_{n})\Delta\mathbf{w}_{n},

where
\mathbf{I}
is the identity matrix. Finally, solving for
\mathbf{x}_{h}\in\mathrm{X}_{h}
yields:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathbf{x}_{n+1}=(\mathbf{I}-h\mathrm{M})^{-1}\left(\mathbf{x}_{n}+h\mathrm{N}{\bf u}_{n}+\mathbf{\sigma}(t_{n})\Delta\mathbf{w}_{n}\right),&\forall n=0,1,\ldots,N-1,\\
\mathbf{x}_{0}=\mathbf{x}_{0}.\end{cases}

(3.1)

This provides the update rule for the discrete state variable at each time step.

Next, consider a given discrete state variable
\mathbf{x}_{h}\equiv\{\mathbf{x}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}\in\mathrm{X}_{h}
. Using the implicit Euler scheme, we approximate the adjoint variable
\mathbf{p}_{h}\equiv\{\mathbf{p}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{N}\in\mathrm{X}_{h}
, which satisfies the following backward difference equations:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathbf{p}_{n}=(\mathbf{I}-h\mathrm{M})^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{p}_{n+1}-\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}_{n+1}\big|\mathbb{F}_{t_{n}}\right]&\forall\,n=N-1,\ldots,0,\\
\mathbf{p}_{N}=-\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}_{N}.\end{cases}

(3.2)

This equation provides the update rule for the adjoint variable, working backwards from the terminal condition at
n=N
.

We also define the operator
\mathcal{T}_{h}:\mathrm{X}_{h}\to\mathrm{X}_{h}
, which maps the discrete state variable
\mathbf{x}_{h}
to the discrete adjoint variable
\mathbf{p}_{h}
, such that

\mathcal{T}_{h}[\mathbf{x}_{h}]=\mathbf{p}_{h}[\mathbf{x}_{h}],

where
\mathbf{p}_{h}[\mathbf{x}_{h}]
denotes the unique solution to the system of equations given by (
3.2
).

The operators
\mathcal{S}_{h}
and
\mathcal{T}_{h}
thus provide the mappings between the control and state variables, and between the state and adjoint variables, respectively, within the discretized time framework. As a discrete variant of (
2.8
), for any
{\bf u}_{h}\in{\bf U}_{h}
,
\mathbf{y}_{h}[{\bf u}_{h}]\in\mathrm{X}_{h}
denotes the unique solution to the following system of random difference equations

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathbf{y}_{n+1}=(\mathbf{I}-h\mathrm{M})^{-1}\left(\mathbf{y}_{n}+h\mathrm{N}{\bf u}_{n}\right)&\forall n=0,1,\ldots,N-1,\\
\mathbf{y}_{0}=0.\end{cases}

(3.3)

We define
\mathbf{x}_{h}^{\sigma}=\mathbf{x}_{h}[0]
. Note that
\mathbf{x}_{h}[{\bf u}_{h}]=\mathbf{y}_{h}[{\bf u}_{h}]+\mathbf{x}_{h}^{\sigma}
for all
{\bf u}_{h}\in{\bf U}_{h}
.
We now define the operator
\mathcal{S}_{h}:{\bf U}_{h}\to\mathrm{X}_{h}
, which maps the discrete control
{\bf u}_{h}
to the discrete state
\mathbf{x}_{h}
, such that

\mathcal{S}_{h}({\bf u}_{h})=\mathbf{y}_{h}[{\bf u}_{h}],

where
\mathbf{y}_{h}[{\bf u}_{h}]
denotes the unique solution to the system of equations given by (
3.3
). It is easy to verify

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}_{h}({\bf u}_{h})\|_{\mathrm{X}_{h}}^{2}\leq\,T\|\mathrm{N}{\bf u}_{h}\|^{2}_{{\bf U}_{h}}.

(3.4)

3.1
Discrete optimal control problem

Let us define the cost functional as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{h}(\mathbf{x}_{h},{\bf u}_{h}):=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(\left<\mathbf{x}_{h}(s),\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}_{h}(s)\right>+\alpha\left<{\bf u}_{h}(s),{\bf u}_{h}(s)\right>\right)\,{\rm d}s+\left<\mathbf{x}_{h}(T),\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}_{h}(T)\right>\right],

where the goal is to find an optimal pair
(\mathbf{x}_{h}^{*},{\bf u}_{h}^{*})\in\mathrm{X}_{h}\times{\bf U}_{h}
that satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{h}(\mathbf{x}_{h}^{*},{\bf u}_{h}^{*})=\min_{(\mathbf{x}_{h},{\bf u}_{h})\in\mathrm{X}_{h}\times{\bf U}_{ad}^{h}}\mathcal{J}_{h}(\mathbf{x}_{h},{\bf u}_{h}),

(3.5)

subject to the following forward difference equations:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathbf{x}_{n+1}=(\mathbf{I}-h\mathrm{M})^{-1}\left(\mathbf{x}_{n}+h\mathrm{N}{\bf u}_{n}+\mathbf{\sigma}(t_{n})\Delta\mathbf{w}_{n}\right)&\forall n=0,1,\ldots,N-1,\\
\mathbf{x}_{0}=\mathbf{x}_{0}.\end{cases}

(3.6)

We refer this discrete stochastic optimal control problem (

)-(

\mathbf{SLQ}_{h}

Theorem 3.1
.

For each
h>0
, there exists a unique discrete optimal pair

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{B}\mathbf{x}_{h}^{*}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\alpha}{\bf u}_{h}^{*}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\mathbb{E}\big[\|\sqrt{\mathrm{D}}\mathbf{x}_{h}^{*}(T)\|^{2}\big]\leq C_{T}\big(\|\mathbf{x}_{0}\|^{2}+\|\sigma\|_{C_{t}\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\omega}}^{2}\big).

Moreover, the optimality condition is given by:

where
\mathbf{p}_{h}[\mathbf{x}_{h}^{*}]
is the unique solution to the backward difference equations (

). Moreover, the following integral inequality holds: for all
{\bf u}_{h}\in{{\bf U}}_{ad}^{h}

Proof.

. This is discrete variant of Theorem

Remark 3.1
.

By following the same lines as in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we can compute the Fréchet derivative of reduced discrete cost functional
\mathcal{\hat{J}}_{h}({\bf u}_{h})=\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{x}_{h}[{\bf u}_{h}],{\bf u}_{h})
with the help of adjoint operator
\mathcal{T}_{h}
as follows: for all
{\bf u}_{h}\in{\bf U}_{h}

Remark 3.2

(characterization of the optimal pair)
.

By putting together the results in Theorem (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we arrive at the following optimality system.
The optimal pair
(\mathbf{x}_{h}^{*},{\bf u}_{h}^{*})\in\mathrm{X}_{h}\times{\bf U}_{ad}^{h}
is characterized by the following decoupled system of equations

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathbf{x}_{n+1}^{*}=(I-h\mathrm{M})^{-1}\left(\mathbf{x}_{n}^{*}+h\mathrm{N}{\bf u}_{n}^{*}+\mathbf{\sigma}(t_{n})\Delta\mathbf{w}_{n}\right)&\forall\,n=0,1,\ldots,N-1,\\
\mathbf{p}_{n}=(\mathbf{I}-h\mathrm{M})^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{p}_{n+1}-\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}_{n+1}^{*}\big|\mathbb{F}_{t_{n}}\right]&\forall\,n=N-1,\ldots,0,\\
{\bf u}_{h}^{*}=\mathcal{P}_{ad}[\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathrm{N}^{T}\mathbf{p}_{h}[\mathbf{x}_{h}^{*}]],\\
\mathbf{x}^{*}_{0}=\mathbf{x}_{0},\\
\mathbf{p}_{N}=-\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}^{*}_{N}.\end{cases}

(3.10)

Remark 3.3
.

The above system provides a complete characterization of the optimal pair in the discrete setting. The backward recursion for
\mathbf{p}_{n}
is particularly important as it determines the optimal control process through the optimality condition.

From this characterization (
3.10
), this is a decoupled system with conditional expectation whose direct computation is out of reach. To address this issue, we approximate to optimal pair
(\mathbf{x}_{h}^{*},{\bf u}_{h}^{*})
by an
implementable
scheme in the context of a projected gradient descent method which accounts the control constraints as well.

3.2
Projected gradient descent method

By (
3.10
), solving minimization
\mathbf{SLQ}_{h}
problem is equivalent to solving the system of
coupled
forward-backward difference equations (
3.10
). We may exploit the variational character of
\mathbf{SLQ}_{h}
problem to apply the projected gradient descent method (
\mathbf{SLQ}_{h}^{{\rm grad}}
, for short) where approximate iterates of the optimal control
{\bf u}^{*}_{h}
in a convex set of the Hilbert space
{\bf U}_{h}
are obtained.

\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, initial guess
{\bf u}_{h}^{(0)}\in{\bf U}_{ad}^{h}
, and fix
\kappa>0
.

2:

Iterates:
For any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
;

3:

State iterates:
Find the state iterates
\mathbf{x}_{h}^{(\ell)}\in\mathrm{X}_{h}
as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathbf{x}_{n+1}^{(\ell)}=(I-h\mathrm{M})^{-1}\left(\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(\ell)}+h\mathrm{N}{\bf u}_{n}^{(\ell)}+\mathbf{\sigma}(t_{n})\Delta\mathbf{w}_{n}\right)&\forall n=0,1,\ldots,N-1,\\
\mathbf{x}_{0}^{(\ell)}=\mathbf{x}_{0}.\end{cases}

(3.11)

4:

Adjoint iterates:
Find the adjoint iterates
\mathbf{p}_{h}^{(\ell)}\in\mathrm{X}_{h}
as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathbf{p}_{n}^{(\ell)}=h\mathrm{M}\mathbf{p}_{n}^{(\ell)}+\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{p}_{n+1}^{(\ell)}-\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}_{n+1}^{(\ell)}\big|\mathbb{F}_{t_{n}}\right]&\forall n=N-1,\ldots,0,\\
\mathbf{p}_{N}^{(\ell)}=-\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}_{N}^{(\ell)}.\end{cases}

(3.12)

5:

Update iterates:

Update
{\bf u}_{h}^{(\ell+1)}\in{{\bf U}}_{ad}^{h}
by the following formula:

\displaystyle{{\bf u}}_{h}^{(\ell+1)}=\mathcal{P}_{ad}[{\bf u}_{h}^{(\ell)}-\frac{1}{\kappa}(\alpha{\bf u}_{h}^{(\ell)}-\mathrm{N}^{T}\mathbf{p}_{h}^{(\ell)})].

(3.13)

At this form of projected gradient descent method is not
implementable
due to the presence of the conditional expectation. But in next proposition, we will see that this conditional expectation can be avoid by using the iterate nature of gradient descent method and the linearity of the state-adjoint equations, this strategy was before exploited in the case of the stochastic Dirichlet boundary optimal control problem

3.3
Recursive formula

In this subsection, we derive a recursive formula for exact computation of conditional expectation.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathrm{A}_{0}=(\mathbf{I}-h\mathrm{M})^{-1}
, a deterministic constant
{\bf u}_{h}^{(0)}\equiv\mathfrak{c}\in\Pi_{i=1}^{M}[a_{i},b_{i}]
. For any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, in (
3.12
), the adjoint
\mathbf{p}_{n}^{(\ell)}
can be computed by the following recursive formula:

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}_{n}^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{A}_{0}^{N-n}\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(\ell)}-h\sum_{q=n+1}^{N}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{q-n}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{q-n}\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(\ell)}-h\mathrm{A}_{0}^{N-n}\mathrm{D}\sum_{r=n}^{N-1}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{N-r}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{U}_{r,n}^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-h^{2}\sum_{q=n+1}^{N}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{q-n}\mathrm{B}\sum_{r=n}^{q-1}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{q-r}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{U}_{r,n}^{(\ell)}.

(3.14)

where
\mathfrak{U}_{r,n}^{(\ell)}
is defined by the following recursive formula: for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, for all
r\in\{n+1,...,N-1\}
,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely

\displaystyle\mathfrak{U}^{(\ell)}_{r,n}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{ad}\bigg[\big(\mathbf{I}-\frac{\alpha}{\kappa}\big)\mathfrak{U}_{r,n}^{(\ell-1)}-\frac{1}{\kappa}\mathrm{N}^{T}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{N-r}\mathrm{D}\bigg[\mathrm{A}_{0}^{N-n}\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(\ell-1)}+h\sum_{m=n+1}^{N-1}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{N-m}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{U}_{m,n}^{(\ell-1)}\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{h}{\kappa}\mathrm{N}^{T}\sum_{s=r+1}^{N}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{s-r}\mathrm{B}\bigg[\mathrm{A}_{0}^{s-n}\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(\ell-1)}+h\sum_{m=n+1}^{s-1}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{s-m}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{U}_{m,n}^{(\ell-1)}\bigg]\bigg],

(3.15)

with
\mathfrak{U}_{r,n}^{(0)}=\mathfrak{c}\quad\forall\,r\in\{n+1,....,N-1\}
.

Proof.

We give complete the proof in the several steps.

Step 1.
By using the tower property of conditional expectation and linearity of adjoint iterate (
3.12
), for any
n\in\{1,...,N-1\}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}_{n}^{(\ell)}=-\mathbb{E}\big[A_{0}^{N-n}\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}_{N}^{(\ell)}+h\sum_{q=n+1}^{N}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{q-n}\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}_{q}^{(\ell)}\Big|\mathbb{F}_{t_{n}}\big].

Step 2.
By making use of the state iterate (
3.6
) and additive nature of noise coefficients, we can conclude that for all
q>n
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{q}^{(\ell)}=\mathrm{A}_{0}^{q-n}\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(\ell)}+h\sum_{r=n}^{q-1}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{q-r}[\mathrm{N}{\bf u}_{r}^{(\ell)}+\mathbf{\sigma}_{r}\Delta_{r}\mathbf{w}].

It implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbf{x}_{q}^{(\ell)}\big|\mathbb{F}_{n}\big]=\mathrm{A}_{0}^{q-n}\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(\ell)}+h\sum_{r=n}^{q-1}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{q-r}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{U}_{r,n}^{(\ell)},

where
\mathfrak{U}_{r,n}^{(\ell)}=\mathbb{E}[{\bf u}_{r}^{(\ell)}\big|\mathbb{F}_{n}]
.

Step 3.
With the help of the control iterate (
3.13
), we can conclude that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{U}^{(\ell)}_{r,n}=\mathcal{P}_{ad}\bigg[(1-\frac{\alpha}{\kappa})\mathfrak{U}_{r,n}^{(\ell-1)}-\frac{1}{\kappa}\mathrm{N}^{T}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\mathrm{A}_{0}^{N-r}\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}_{N}^{(\ell-1)}+h\sum_{s=r+1}^{N}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{s-r}\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}_{s}^{(\ell-1)}\Big|\mathbb{F}_{n}\bigg]\bigg].

Analogously to Step 2, we know that for all
s\in\{n+2,...,N\}
,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbf{x}_{s}^{(\ell-1)}\big|\mathbb{F}_{n}\big]=\mathrm{A}_{0}^{s-n}\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(\ell-1)}+h\sum_{m=n+1}^{s-1}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{s-m}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{U}_{m,n}^{(\ell-1)}.

From the above identity, we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{U}^{(\ell)}_{r,n}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{ad}\bigg[\big({1}-\frac{\alpha}{\kappa}\big)\mathfrak{U}_{r,n}^{(\ell-1)}-\frac{1}{\kappa}\mathrm{N}^{T}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{N-r}\mathrm{D}\bigg[\mathrm{A}_{0}^{N-n}\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(\ell-1)}+h\sum_{m=n+1}^{N-1}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{N-m}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{U}_{m,n}^{(\ell-1)}\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{h}{\kappa}\mathrm{N}^{T}\sum_{s=r+1}^{N}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{s-r}\mathrm{B}\bigg[\mathrm{A}_{0}^{s-n}\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(\ell-1)}+h\sum_{m=n+1}^{s-1}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{s-m}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{U}_{m,n}^{(\ell-1)}\bigg]\bigg],

and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{U}_{r,n}^{(\ell)}=\mathfrak{c}\qquad\forall\,r\in\{n+1,...,N-1\}.

Step 4.
With the information of Step 1 and Step 2, we can conclude that
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely,

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}_{n}^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{A}_{0}^{N-n}\mathrm{D}\bigg[\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(\ell)}+h\sum_{r=n}^{N-1}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{N-r}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{U}_{r,n}^{(\ell)}\bigg]-h\sum_{q=n+1}^{N}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{q-n}\mathrm{B}\bigg[\mathrm{A}_{0}^{q-n}\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(\ell)}+h\sum_{r=n}^{q-1}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{q-r}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{U}_{r,n}^{(\ell)}\bigg]

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{A}_{0}^{N-n}\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(\ell)}-h\sum_{q=n+1}^{N}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{q-n}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{q-n}\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(\ell)}-h\mathrm{A}_{0}^{N-n}\mathrm{D}\sum_{r=n}^{N-1}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{N-r}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{U}_{r,n}^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-h^{2}\sum_{q=n+1}^{N}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{q-n}\mathrm{B}\sum_{r=n}^{q-1}\mathrm{A}_{0}^{q-r}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{U}_{r,n}^{(\ell)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

4
Error analysis

In this subsection, we discuss the error analysis related to time discretization. Our focus is on estimating the errors introduced when approximating the continuous-time control and state trajectories using their discrete counterparts.

4.1
Error bounds for state and adjoint differential equations

We begin by stating a result that provides an error bound for the state stochastic differential equation (SDE) when discretized.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
{\bf u}_{h}\in{\bf U}_{h}
. Let
\mathbf{y}[{\bf u}_{h}]
and
\mathbf{y}_{h}[{\bf u}_{h}]
be the unique solutions to (
2.8
) and (
3.3
), respectively. Then the following error bounds hold:

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq\,n\leq N}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{y}[{\bf u}_{h}](t_{n})-\mathbf{y}_{h}[{\bf u}_{h}](t_{n})\|^{2}\big]\leq C_{T,d}h^{2}\big\|{\bf u}_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2},

(4.1)

and

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{y}[{\bf u}_{h}]-\mathbf{y}_{h}[{\bf u}_{h}]\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq\,C_{T,d}h^{2}\|{\bf u}_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

(4.2)

Proof.

The proof is standard. For completeness of the proof, we give details here. For simplicity, we denote
\mathbf{y}[{\bf u}_{h}]\equiv\mathbf{y}
. Define the error term for all
0\leq n\leq N
as

e_{n}=\mathbf{y}(t_{n})-\mathbf{y}_{n}.

Then, we have
\mathbb{P}
-a.s., for all
0\leq n\leq N-1
,

e_{n+1}=e_{n}+\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathrm{M}(\mathbf{y}(s)-\mathbf{y}_{n+1})\,\mathrm{d}s.

Taking the inner product with
e_{n+1}
, we obtain

\left\langle e_{n+1}-e_{n},e_{n+1}\right\rangle=-\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\left\langle\mathrm{M}_{1}(\mathbf{y}(s)-\mathbf{y}(t_{n+1})),\mathrm{M}_{1}e_{n+1}\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s-h\|\mathrm{M}_{1}e_{n+1}\|^{2}.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Young’s inequality to the first term on the right-hand side, we have

\left\langle e_{n+1}-e_{n},e_{n+1}\right\rangle\leq-\frac{1}{2}h\|\mathrm{M}_{1}e_{n+1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|\mathrm{M}_{1}(\mathbf{y}(s)-\mathbf{y}(t_{n+1}))\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

This implies

\mathbb{E}\big[\|e_{n+1}\|^{2}\big]+\mathbb{E}\big[\|e_{n}-e_{n+1}\|^{2}\big]+\frac{h}{2}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathrm{M}_{1}e_{n+1}\|^{2}\big]\leq\mathbb{E}\big[\|e_{n}\|^{2}\big]+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|\mathrm{M}_{1}(\mathbf{y}(s)-\mathbf{y}(t_{n+1}))\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg].

By summing over
n
from
0
to
N-1
, we obtain, for all
0\leq n\leq N
,

\mathbb{E}\big[\|e_{n}\|^{2}\big]+\frac{h}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathrm{M}_{1}e_{k}\|^{2}\big]\leq\mathbb{E}\big[\|e_{0}\|^{2}\big]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\|\mathrm{M}_{1}(\mathbf{y}(s)-\mathbf{y}(t_{k+1}))\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg].

Using the time regularity estimate (
2.13
), we obtain

\sup_{1\leq n\leq N}\mathbb{E}\big[\|e_{n}\|^{2}\big]+\frac{h}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathrm{M}_{1}e_{n}\|^{2}\big]\leq C_{T,d}h^{2}\|{\bf u}_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

This yields the estimate (
4.1
). By applying the estimate (
4.1
) and noting that
\|\mathbf{y}_{t}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{T}\|{\bf u}_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}
, we can similarly derive the estimate (
4.2
).
∎

(4.4)

The proof of this result can be shown as follows similar lines as done in the previous Proposition
4.1
with the help of the estimates (

) & (
4.3

4.2
Error estimate for optimal pair

Theorem 4.1

(Rate of convergence for control constraint)
.

Let
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\sigma
satisfies the following estimate

\displaystyle\|\sigma-\Pi_{h}\sigma\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq\,C_{T}\,h.

Let
(\mathbf{x}^{*},{\bf u}^{*})\in\mathrm{X}\times{\bf U}_{ad}
and
(\mathbf{x}_{h}^{*},{\bf u}_{h}^{*})\in\mathrm{X}_{h}\times{\bf U}_{ad}^{h}
be the unique optimal pairs for the continuous
\mathbf{SLQ}
problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) and the discrete
\mathbf{SLQ}_{h}
problem (
3.5
)-(
3.6
), respectively. Then the following error bound holds:

\displaystyle\ \|\sqrt{\alpha}({\bf u}^{*}-{\bf u}_{h}^{*})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\sqrt{\mathrm{B}}(\mathbf{x}^{*}-\mathbf{x}_{h}^{*})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\mathbb{E}\big[\|\sqrt{\mathrm{D}}(\mathbf{x}^{*}(T)-\mathbf{x}_{h}^{*}(T))\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq C_{T,d}\,h^{1/2}(\|\mathbf{x}_{0}\|^{2}+\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{x}_{0}\|^{2}+\|\mathrm{M}\mathbf{x}_{0}\|^{2}+\|\sigma\|_{C_{t}\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|\mathrm{M}\mathbf{\sigma}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|\mathrm{M}_{1}\mathbf{\sigma}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}).

(4.5)

Proof.

We start with the expression for the squared norm of the difference between the optimal control and its approximation:

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\alpha}({\bf u}^{*}-{\bf u}_{h}^{*})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}:=I+J,

where

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\left<\alpha({\bf u}^{*}-{\bf u}_{h}^{*}),\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}-{\bf u}_{h}^{*}\right>{\rm d}s\bigg],

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\left<\alpha({\bf u}^{*}-{\bf u}_{h}^{*}),{\bf u}^{*}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}\right>{\rm d}s\bigg].

Step 1.
In this step, we will estimate the term
I
:

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\left<\alpha{\bf u}^{*},\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}-{\bf u}_{h}^{*}\right>{\rm d}s\bigg]-\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\left<\alpha{\bf u}_{h}^{*},\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}-{\bf u}_{h}^{*}\right>{\rm d}s\bigg]

\displaystyle=K_{1}+K_{2}.

Next, we estimate the term
K_{1}
:

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\left<\alpha{\bf u}^{*},{\bf u}_{h}^{*}-{\bf u}^{*}\right>{\rm d}s\bigg]+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\left<\alpha{\bf u}^{*},\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}-{\bf u}^{*}\right>{\rm d}s\bigg]

\displaystyle=K_{11}+K_{12}.

To estimate
K_{11}
, we proceed as follows: By using the integral identity (
2.17
) and the linearity of the differential equation (
2.8
), we obtain:

\displaystyle K_{11}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\left<\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}^{*}(s),\mathbf{y}[{\bf u}_{h}^{*}-{\bf u}^{*}](s)\right>{\rm d}s\bigg]+\mathbb{E}\big[\left<\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}_{h}^{*}(T),\mathbf{y}[{\bf u}_{h}^{*}-{\bf u}^{*}](T)\right>\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\left<\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}^{*}(s),\mathbf{y}[{\bf u}_{h}^{*}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}](s)\right>{\rm d}s\bigg]+\mathbb{E}\big[\left<\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}^{*}(T),\mathbf{y}[{\bf u}_{h}^{*}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}](T)\right>\big]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\big[\int_{0}^{T}\left<\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}^{*}(s),\mathbf{y}[\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}-{\bf u}^{*}](s)\right>{\rm d}s\bigg]+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left<\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}^{*}(T),\mathbf{y}[\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}-{\bf u}^{*}](T)\right>\big].

This implies that:

\displaystyle K_{11}+K_{12}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left<\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}^{*}(s),\mathbf{y}[{\bf u}_{h}^{*}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}](s)\right>{\rm d}s+\mathbb{E}\left<\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}^{*}(T),\mathbf{y}[{\bf u}_{h}^{*}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}](T)\right>+\mathfrak{E},

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{E}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\left<\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}^{*}(s),\mathbf{y}[\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}-{\bf u}^{*}](s)\right>{\rm d}s\bigg]+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\left<\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}^{*}(T),\mathbf{y}[\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}-{\bf u}^{*}](T)\right>\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\left<\alpha{\bf u}^{*}(s),\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}(s)-{\bf u}^{*}(s)\right>{\rm d}s\bigg].

On the other hand, for the term
K_{2}
, we use the integral identity (
3.9
) to obtain

K_{2}\leq-\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\left<\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}_{h}^{*}(s),\mathbf{y}_{h}[{\bf u}_{h}^{*}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}](s)\right>\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg]-\mathbb{E}\big[\left<\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}_{h}^{*}(T),\mathbf{y}_{h}[{\bf u}_{h}^{*}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}](T)\right>\big].

Now we can conclude that

I=K_{1}+K_{2}\leq I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}+\mathfrak{E},

where the terms are defined as follows:

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left<\mathrm{B}(\mathbf{x}^{*}(s)-\mathbf{x}_{h}^{*}(s)),\mathbf{y}[{\bf u}_{h}](s)\right>\,\mathrm{d}s\right],

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left<\mathrm{B}\mathbf{x}_{h}^{*}(s),\mathbf{y}_{h}[{\bf u}_{h}](s)-\mathbf{y}[{\bf u}_{h}](s)\right>\,\mathrm{d}s\right],

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left<\mathrm{D}(\mathbf{x}^{*}(T)-\mathbf{x}_{h}^{*}(T)),\mathbf{y}[{\bf u}_{h}](T)\right>\right],

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left<\mathrm{D}\mathbf{x}_{h}^{*}(T),\mathbf{y}_{h}[{\bf u}_{h}](T)-\mathbf{y}[{\bf u}_{h}](T)\right>\right],

with

{\bf u}_{h}={\bf u}_{h}^{*}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}.

We will now estimate each term separately.

Step 1(a).
In this step, we will estimate
I_{1}
term as follows:

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\left<\mathrm{B}(\mathbf{x}^{*}(s)-\mathbf{x}^{*}_{h}(s)),\mathbf{x}^{*}(s)-\mathbf{x}^{*}_{h}(s)\right>{\rm d}s\bigg]-\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\left<\mathrm{B}(\mathbf{x}^{*}(s)-\mathbf{x}^{*}_{h}(s)),\mathbf{x}^{\sigma}(s)-\mathbf{x}^{\sigma}_{h}(s)\right>{\rm d}s\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\left<\mathrm{B}(\mathbf{x}^{*}(s)-\mathbf{x}^{*}_{h}(s)),\mathbf{y}[{\bf u}^{*}](s)-\mathbf{y}[\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}]\right>{\rm d}s\bigg]

\displaystyle:=I_{11}+I_{12}+I_{13}.

Make use of Young’s inequality and the estimate (
4.4
), we conclude that for any

Remark 4.2

(Finite dimensional approximation of an unbounded operator)
.

It is worth noting that the constant
C_{T,d}
depends polynomially on the norms of the given matrices, and is independent from the norm of
\mathrm{M}
and
\mathrm{M}_{1}
. Due to this, the error analysis encompasses a wide range of stochastic linear quadratic (SLQ) problems related to stochastic differential equations (SDEs), which result from finite-dimensional approximations of
\mathbf{SLQ}
problems governed by (infinite-dimensional) stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs). In particular, the matrix
\mathrm{M}
can be interpreted as a finite-dimensional approximation of an unbounded operator acting on an infinite-dimensional Sobolev space. For example,
\mathrm{M}
may be related to the discrete Laplacian
\Delta_{h}
on the finite-dimensional space
V_{d}(D)
, consisting of piecewise affine on a finite element mesh contained in
H^{1}(D)
, where
D
is a bounded domain. Similarly,
\mathrm{M}_{1}
can be viewed as a matrix associated with the gradient operator.

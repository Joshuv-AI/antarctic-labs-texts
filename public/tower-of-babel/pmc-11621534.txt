Study on mechanical characteristics of phyllite by conventional triaxial compression under multi-factors coupling action
Authors: Yabin Wu, Dongjie Yang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Phyllite belongs to metamorphic soft rock and is often encountered during tunnel excavation, which brings inconvenience and safety problem to construction. The mechanical characteristics of phyllite are influenced by multiple factors. This study conducted conventional triaxial compression mechanical experiments on phyllite under the multi-factor coupling action, including confining pressure, water content state, and bedding angle, which purpose is to explore the evolution process and change law of phyllite under various influencing factors. The results indicate that the relationship between peak strength, elastic modulus and bedding angles follows a “W” shape (dry state) and a “U” shape (saturated state). The peak strength and elastic modulus of high confining pressure is always higher than that of low confining pressure, which can increase by 20.55%~55.44% (saturated) and 23.29%~102.22% (dry) in the peak strength, and 1.19%~22.40% (saturated) and 2.39%~22.45% (dry) in elastic modulus. The peak strength and elastic modulus of dry condition is generally higher than that of saturated condition. The crack initiation and damage of phyllite are affected by various influencing factors, resulting in the failure modes of phyllite are more complex. The failure modes of phyllite include slip shear failure, compression tensile shear failure, tension failure and composite failure.

Introduction

Phyllite is a metamorphic rock that is often encountered during mining. Due to its main content of minerals such as mica, quartz, and kaolinite, it is prone to alteration, damage, and meeting water expansion. And it has a fine-grained scale layered structure, well-developed bedding, and is prone to weathering when exposed to water1–3. Phyllite has a layered structure, and due to different bedding planes, its load will have significant anisotropy, which has considerable influence on the mechanical properties of phyllite4,5. And phyllite is subject to different pressures at different mine depths. Therefore, phyllite is affected by factors such as bedding angle, water content state and pressure environment, which brings serious safety problems to mine production6,7. It is necessary to study the influencing factors of phyllite to provide reference for solving such problems.

Bedding is a type of structural plane that belongs to the weak plane within a rock mass. Studies have shown that the deformation and strength of layered rock mass are significantly affected by the bedding angle8–11. Study by Wei Wang and others found that as the bedding angle increases, the strength and deformation characteristic curve of the rock sample is U-shaped, and the brittleness index of phyllite samples is lower, and the shear failure is more likely to occur when the bedding angle is 45°8. Xuefeng Si et al. studied the influence of different bedding angles on the mechanical properties and rockburst tendency of phyllite, and analyzed the mechanical characteristics, failure modes, and rockburst tendency grade of phyllite12. The water content state has a significant impact on the mechanical properties of phyllite. Phyllite contains clay minerals, which leads to its characteristics of strong water-bearing and water-saturated, swelling in water, etc., which will be harmful to its mechanical properties13–16. Yang Zhou studied the mechanical properties of phyllite and found that water reduced the elastic modulus and deformation modulus of phyllite by 41.63% ~ 7.38% and 37.44% ~ 43.02%, respectively, and the strength softening coefficient was 0.49 ~ 0.7013. Jianjun Zhao studied the conventional triaxial compression tests of phyllite with different saturation times and found that the mechanical parameter values of phyllite decreased linearly with the saturation time within 60 days of saturation. After 60 days, the mechanical parameter values no longer changed, and the deformation changed from elastic to plastic14. As the mining depth increases, the mechanical properties of rock masses are more complex, especially confining pressures is different17. Confining pressure has a greater influence on the strength of layered rock mass, and the study of the influence law of confining pressure on layered rock mass mainly adopts triaxial compression tests [8–10,18−20]. Chao Pu conducted triaxial compression tests on phyllite under different confining pressures, and the results showed that under low confining pressure conditions, phyllite mainly exhibited tension-shear failure, while high confining pressure transformed into shear failure. The relationship between maximum principal stress and confining pressure was established, and a general angle of shear failure of phyllite was proposed20.

Based on the above research, the change law of mechanical parameters of phyllite under confining pressure, water content state and bedding angle is further analyzed. In this study, conventional triaxial compression tests of phyllite under different influencing factors were carried out to analyze the deformation, strength and failure evolution process of phyllite under different confining pressure, water content state, and bedding angle, and the corresponding relationship between strength, deformation and failure characteristics of phyllite is established, and the deterioration law of phyllite mechanical parameters under the multi-factor coupling action is revealed.

Experimental schemes

Scheme design

The phyllite samples used in the test were all taken from large pieces of phyllite within the same area of the engineering rock mass. According to ISRM international standard, the rock sample is processed into a standard cylinder size of Φ50 mm×100 mm (as shown in Fig. 1), ensuring that the error of the two end faces is controlled within ± 0.05 mm, and the parallelism of the two end faces is controlled within ± 0.25°. The mineral composition of phyllite was tested by XRD and XRF, and the results were 16.44% quartz, 53.88% mica, 22.4% kaolinite and 7.28% other minerals. The water content state of the sample was treated by drying box and vacuum saturator. This experiment designed two types of confining pressures (5 MPa and 15 MPa), two water content states (dry and saturated), and five bedding angles (0 °, 30 °, 45 °, 60 °, 90 °) as three influencing factors. Under these three influencing factors, conventional triaxial compression tests were conducted on phyllite. The detailed scheme is shown in Table 1.

Results and analysis

Analysis of stress-strain curve

The typical stress-strain curves of phyllite were obtained by conventional triaxial tests, as shown in Fig. 3. It can be seen from Fig. 3 that the mechanical properties of phyllite are significantly affected by confining pressure, bedding angle, water content state. From loading to failure of phyllite, there are five basic stages, namely compaction deformation stage, elastic deformation stage, plastic yield stage, failure stage, and residual strength stage21. It can be seen from Fig. 3 that all compaction deformation stages almost coincide, and all curve shapes are similar under different water content state. The linear elastic stage shows the fastest growth at 90 °, while the growth of other bedding angles does not change much. The peak strength is the highest at 90° and the lowest at 30°. The peak strength under dry is higher than that under saturated, and the peak strength under high confining pressure is higher than that under low confining pressure. At failure stage after peak, there was a significant decrease in stress, and the stress decrease value is more under saturated. After the stress decrease, stresses of low confining pressure specimens (except sample at 0°) showed a gentle stage, stresses of specimens at 0° showed a slight increase, and stresses of high confining pressure specimens (except sample at 45°) showed a stepped decrease.

Stress-strain diagram of phyllite.

Peak strength analysis

According to the stress-strain diagram, it can be found that the peak strength of phyllite is significantly affected by factors such as bedding angle, confining pressure and water content state, and the relationship between them is shown in Fig. 4. From the perspective of bedding angle, the relationship between peak strength and bedding angle shows “W” (dry state) and “U” (saturated state) shapes. The peak strength of specimen at 30 ° is the lowest, while the peak strength of specimen at 90 ° is the highest. 45 ° is special and there is a bulge under a dry state. From the perspective of water content state, the peak strength of saturated samples is generally lower than that of dry samples, and the peak strength difference of samples at 45° is the largest, followed by 30° and 90°, with little peak strength difference of samples at 0° and 60°, indicating that the weakening effect of peak strength of phyllite samples is mainly influenced by bedding angle. From the perspective of confining pressure, the peak strength of high confining pressure specimens is higher than that of low confining pressure specimens, which can increase by 20.55%~55.44% (saturated) and 23.29%~102.22% (dry).

Elastic modulus analysis

The elastic modulus of phyllite under various influencing factors is obtained by calculating, and the results are shown in Fig. 5. The relationship between elastic modulus of the phyllite sample and bedding angle shows “W” (dry state) and “U” (saturated state) shapes, and the elastic modulus of the sample at 90° is the highest under both states. The elastic modulus of the sample at 90° is less affected by the water content state, and the elastic modulus is the largest when the confining pressure is only 15 MPa. The elastic modulus of the specimen at 45° is significantly affected by the water content state and decreases after saturation. The elastic modulus of phyllite at 60° is affected by confining pressure and water content state, the elastic modulus is maximum at 15 MPa and the minimum elastic modulus at 5 MPa, and the elastic modulus increases after saturation. In short, the elastic modulus of samples under dry state is generally higher than that under saturated state (except sample at 60°), and the elastic modulus of samples under high confining pressure is generally higher than that under low confining pressure. The elastic modulus of samples under high confining pressure can increase by 1.19%~22.40% (saturated) and 2.39%~22.45% (dry).

Threshold analysis

In order to further analyze the influence of bedding angles on crack, threshold ratio parameters of crack are introduced, which are the ratio of crack initiation threshold to axial peak stress (σci/σf) and crack damage threshold to axial peak stress (σcd/σf) respectively. The threshold ratio parameters under different influencing factors are obtained by calculating, the results are shown in Fig. 8. As can be seen from the Fig. 8, when the confining pressure is 5 MPa, there is a downward “U” shape between threshold ratio and bedding angle. When the confining pressure is 15 MPa, there is a “M” shape between σci/σf and bedding angle, and a “W” shape between σcd/σf and bedding angle. When bedding angles are 30° and 60°, σci/σf is high, while σcd/σf is low. When bedding angles are 0°, 45° and 90°, σci/σf is low, while σcd/σf is high. Therefore, phyllite with 30 ° and 60 ° bedding angles quickly reach the crack damage stage after crack initiation, making it more prone to instability, while phyllite with 0 °, 45 ° and 90 ° bedding angles have a longer crack damage process after crack initiation, making it more prone to stability.

Threshold ratio parameters of dry specimens with low bedding angles (0 °, 30 °, and 45 °) are low, and Threshold ratio parameters of saturated specimens with high bedding angles (60 ° and 90 °) are low. Combined with the failure situation of phyllite, the phyllite samples of low bedding angle are often destroyed interspersed with bedding plane, and the action of water makes each stage reach the critical point more quickly, while the phyllite samples of high bedding angle are often destroyed along the bedding plane, and the action of water makes the phyllite reach the yield to destabilize more quickly. The larger the confining pressure, the larger the threshold ratio parameters of phyllite specimens with 0° and 90° bedding angles, while the smaller the threshold ratio parameters of phyllite specimens with 30°, 45° and 60° bedding angles.

In short, the bedding angle has a significant influence on the threshold ratio parameters, while the influence of water on the threshold ratio parameters is related to the bedding angle, which has a weakening effect at low bedding angles and enhancing effect at high bedding angles.

Failure mode analysis

Under different influencing factors, the fracture mode of samples is different27. Failure modes of samples under different confining pressure, bedding angle and water content states were statistically analyzed, as shown in Table 3. From the perspective of failure modes, the main modes are bedding plane sliding shear failure, compressive tensile shear failure, tensile failure, and composite failure. By comparing the failure modes of typical rock samples, it can be found that due to the existence of weak bedding planes, the failure modes of phyllite are more complex.

When the bedding angle is 0°, the typical failure mode of phyllite is shear failure. The cracks are mainly shear cracks, and the angle between the shear plane and the horizontal plane is about 30°. There is a vertical tensile crack at the bottom of the shear main crack, and it is connected to the main crack, which causes fragment fracture and peeling, and there is also peeling phenomenon around the specimen. Under the dry condition, the range of crack growth downward is small, and some small cracks parallel to the main shear direction are generated and connected with the main crack. Under the saturated condition, the shear crack growth direction is close to the loading direction. When the bedding angle is 30°, the typical failure mode of phyllite is shear slip-tension. Some shear cracks do not directly penetrate but grow in a mirror image during the growth process, with a shear plane angle of approximately 60 ° to the horizontal. From the perspective of the influence of the bedding structural plane, the cracks grow through the bedding plane in an inclined or vertical way, but did not grow along the bedding plane, indicating that there is no direct influence bedding angle (0° and 30°) on failure mode.

When the bedding angles are 45° and 60°, the typical failure mode of phyllite is along bedding shear failure. When the bedding angle is 90°, the typical failure mode of phyllite is along bedding tensile failure. The shear planes generally grow along the bedding plane and penetrate the entire specimen, with an angle close to bedding angle to the horizontal plane. The failure modes and the angle between the shear plane and the horizontal under different water content states and confining pressures are consistent. The direction of crack growth will change when the confining pressure is high for samples with bedding angles of 60° and 90°. From the perspective of the influence of the bedding structural plane, the cracks grow along the bedding plane, indicating that there is direct influence bedding angle (45°, 60° and 90°) on failure mode.

By comparing the above failure modes, it can be found that when the bedding angle is small (0°~30°), the main failure mode is compression shear failure that penetrates the bedding plane, and it is not closely related to the bedding angle. When the bedding angle is medium (45°~60°), the main failure mode is along bedding shear failure, and it is closely related to the bedding angle. When the bedding angle is large (90°), the main failure mode is along bedding tensile failure, and it is closely related to the bedding angle. The water content state and confining pressure have an impact on the angle between the shear plane and the horizontal for phyllite sample with small bedding angles.

Conclusion

This study conducted conventional triaxial compression mechanical tests on phyllite, analyzed the deformation, strength, and failure evolution process of phyllite under different confining pressures, water content state, and bedding angle, revealed the degradation law of mechanical parameters of phyllite under multi factor coupling action, and established the corresponding relationship between strength, deformation and failure characteristics of phyllite under multi factor conditions.

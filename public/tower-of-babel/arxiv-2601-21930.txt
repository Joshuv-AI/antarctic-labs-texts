Entropy production versus memory effects in two-level open quantum systems
G. Théret, D. Sugny and C. L. Latune

Abstract
We compare several definitions of entropy production rate introduced in the literature from a large variety of situations and motivations, and then analyze their relations with memory effects. Considering a relevant experimental example of a qubit interacting with a single bosonic mode playing the role of a finite bath, we show that all definitions of entropy production coincide at weak coupling. In the strong coupling regime, significant discrepancies emerge between the different entropy production rates, although some similarities in the overall behaviour remain. However, surprisingly, two of these definitions—one based on local quantities of the system and the other on non-local quantities—coincide exactly, even in the case of strong coupling. Finally, a high degree of correspondence is observed when memory effects characterized by P-divisibility are compared with the sign of all entropy production rates in the case of weak coupling. Such correspondence degrades at strong coupling, leading us to extend the concept of entropy production to the dynamical map. We show a perfect equivalence between the sign of this enlarged concept of entropy production and P-divisibility, both numerically and analytically, in the case of phase-covariant master equations.



I
Introduction

The concept of entropy production is central to both classical and quantum thermodynamics
Landi and Paternostro 2021
. It is closely related to the irreversibility of the local system dynamics and to the information lost in the bath
Esposito et al. 2010
;
Landi and Paternostro 2021
;
Santos et al. 2018
, and is also associated with the arrow of time
Parrondo et al. 2009
;
Deffner and Lutz 2011
;
Santos et al. 2019
;
Latune et al. 2020
.
From a practical viewpoint, the entropy production also determines and often limits our ability to perform operations and tasks such as cooling a system
Taranto et al. 2023
, extracting work
Uzdin et al. 2015
, erasing information
Van Vu and Saito 2022
;
Rolandi and Perarnau-Llobet 2023
, measuring time
Milburn 2020
;
Pearson et al. 2021
, and controlling fluctuations
Hasegawa 2020
, to mention a few.

More recently, several studies have suggested a close relation between entropy production and memory effects
Mazzola et al. 2012
;
Bhattacharya et al. 2017
;
Marcantoni et al. 2017
;
Popovic et al. 2018
;
Santos et al. 2018
;
Strasberg and Esposito 2019
, often refereed to as non-Markovian effects, in reference to classical dynamics. In Ref.
Strasberg and Esposito 2019
, the authors establish the conditions in classical systems for which a negative entropy production rate implies non-Markovianity, and raises the question of its quantum version. Indeed, one of the difficulties in the quantum case is the non-unique characterization of non-Markovianity, which stems from the inability to extend the classical notion straightforwardly to quantum systems
Rivas et al. 2014
.
For open quantum dynamics, the intuitive relation between entropy production and memory effects can be traced back to a measure of memory effects such as the BLP criterion
Breuer et al. 2009
, which relies on the contractivity of the dynamical map. More precisely, when the map is contractive, i.e. when the trace distance between two arbitrary initial states decreases monotonically in time, there is a monotonic loss of information from the system to the bath, which is interpreted as the absence of memory effect. Otherwise, information flows back from the bath to the system. In Ref.
Mazzola et al. 2012
, the authors provide an upper bound on contractivity in terms of system-bath correlations.

On the other hand, many definitions of entropy production are based on the contractivity of the pseudo-distance (the relative entropy) between an arbitrary initial state and a specific state, such as the steady state (for weak coupling time-independent dissipator)
Spohn 1978
;
Alicki 1979
;
Breuer et al. 2002
, or the instantaneous steady state (for strong coupling and time-dependent dissipator)
Colla and Breuer 2022
, or even the joined uncorrelated state of the system and the initial state of the bath
Esposito et al. 2010
.
This contractivity is analogous to the BLP criterion, and therefore suggests, to explore the precise link between memory effects and entropy production, as studied in
Bhattacharya et al. 2017
;
Marcantoni et al. 2017
;
Popovic et al. 2018
;
Santos et al. 2018
. It can be shown that negative entropy production rates lead to memory effects, and more precisely to Non-Markovianity (defined as non CP-divisibility, see below for a description and
Breuer et al. 2016
). The reverse implication is not true, and potential alternative witnesses of memory effects are introduced such as the purity
Bhattacharya et al. 2017
, and the influence of the form of the instantaneous steady state of the map
Marcantoni et al. 2017
. These previous works also raised the key issue of which definition of entropy production should be used. This problem was studied in
Colla and Breuer 2021
, where two definitions of entropy production, the traditional one
Spohn 1978
;
Alicki 1979
;
Breuer et al. 2002
and the Esposito definition
Esposito et al. 2010
–tailored for strong coupling between system and bath– are compared. Interestingly, the two solutions exhibit very different behaviour, except in the case of very weak coupling and high temperature.

In this paper, we propose to pursue efforts in this direction using a simple but relevant model consisting of a qubit interacting with a single-bosonic-mode bath. Such a model combines strong coupling and finite bath, two characteristics that are currently receiving a lot of attention due to their complexity and close proximity with experimental situations. In the first part of the paper, we compare several definitions of entropy production, ranging from traditional to more recent ones that are specifically designed for strong coupling and finite baths. In a second part, we investigate the relation to memory effects.
The main result is that, at vanishing or small coupling, all definitions of entropy production coincide. However, in the strong coupling regime, important discrepancies emerge, even though the global behaviour remains similar. Furthermore, we observe that two definitions of entropy production exactly coincide. Finally, we show numerically the equivalence between P-divisibility and the sign of the entropy production rate of the dynamical map, which is an extension at the map level of the concept of entropy production. This equivalence is then proven mathematically for a large class of open quantum systems governed by a phase-covariant dynamics
Filippov et al. 2020
;
Théret et al. 2025
.

These results provide a broad physical interpretation of some of the suggested entropy production definitions and of the concept of P-divisibility. More generally, our study establishes a direct equivalence between the presence of memory effects and the sign of an entropy-production-related quantity.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we provide the different definitions of entropy production used in this work. Our illustrative example is introduced in Sec.
III
. A systematic numerical comparison of entropy production rates is proposed in Sec.
IV
. Section
V
focuses on the link between entropy production and memory effects. The analytical proof of the equivalence between P-divisibility and the positivity of the map entropy production is presented in Sec.
VI
. Conclusion and prospective views are given in Sec.
VII
. Additional results are described in the Appendices.

II
Entropy production

In this section, we introduce the main characteristics of the different definitions of entropy production considered in our study. We start with the traditional definition
Spohn 1978
;
Alicki 1979
;
Breuer et al. 2002
, which is valid when the quantum system
\displaystyle A
of interest interacts weakly with a thermal bath
\displaystyle B
at temperature
\displaystyle T_{B}
(inverse temperature
\displaystyle\beta_{B}=1/k_{B}T_{B}
). The entropy production is defined as

\Sigma(t):=\Delta S_{A}-\beta_{B}Q(t),

(1)

where
\displaystyle\Delta S_{A}
is the variation of von Neumann entropy
\displaystyle S_{A}(t):=-{\rm Tr}[\rho_{A}(t)\ln\rho_{A}(t)]
of the system, and
\displaystyle Q(t)
is the heat exchanged with the bath,

Q(t):=\int_{0}^{t}du{\rm Tr}[\dot{\rho}_{A}(u)H_{A}(u)].

(2)

The rate of entropy production is given by

\sigma(t)=\dot{S}_{A}(t)-\beta_{B}\dot{Q}(t),

(3)

where
\displaystyle\dot{S}_{A}(t)
is the time-derivative of von Neumann entropy of the system and
\displaystyle\dot{Q}(t)
is the rate of heat exchanged with the bath.
Interestingly, the definition (
3
) of the entropy production rate
\displaystyle\sigma(t)
, inspired from its classical counterpart, coincides with the formal definition from open quantum system theory
Spohn 1978
;
Breuer et al. 2002
,

\sigma(t)=-\frac{d}{dt}D[\rho_{A}(t)|\rho_{A}^{\text{eq}}],

(4)

where
\displaystyle\rho_{A}^{\text{eq}}
is the equilibrium state of the open dynamics and
\displaystyle D[\sigma|\rho]:={\rm Tr}[\sigma(\ln\sigma-\ln\rho)]
is the relative entropy. To be more precise, the coincidence occurs when
\displaystyle\rho_{A}^{\text{eq}}
is equal to the thermal state at the inverse bath temperature
\displaystyle\beta_{B}
, which is usually the case in the weak coupling regime. Additionally, we can mention that
\displaystyle\sigma(t)
can also be expressed as the amount of information lost in the bath (still in the weak coupling regime),

\sigma(t)=\dot{I}_{A:B}+\frac{d}{dt}D[\rho_{B}(t)|\rho_{B}(0)],

(5)

where
\displaystyle I_{A:B}:=S_{A}(t)+S_{B}(t)-S_{AB}(t)
is the mutual information between
\displaystyle A
and
\displaystyle B
, so that
\displaystyle\dot{I}_{A:B}
corresponds to the rate of creation of correlations between
\displaystyle A
and
\displaystyle B
.

However, in the strong coupling regime, the definition (
4
) is expected to become invalid, partly because the instantaneous thermal state does not always coincide with the instantaneous steady state (when it exists). To go beyond these limitations, many definitions of entropy production were suggested in the literature, depending of the context and the chosen viewpoint. We introduce some of them below. A summary is given in Tab.
1
.

•

We start with one of the most accepted definition, valid in strong coupling thermal baths,
Esposito et al. 2010
:

\sigma^{Es}(t):=\dot{S}_{A}(t)+\beta_{B}\dot{E}_{B}(t),

(6)

where
\displaystyle\dot{E}_{B}(t)={\rm Tr}[\dot{\rho}_{B}(t)H_{B}]
is the rate of the energy of the bath. When the system and the bath are initially uncorrelated, the integral of the rate is guaranteed to be always positive,
\displaystyle\Delta S_{A}+\beta_{B}\Delta E_{B}\geq 0
. However, one limitation is that it only applies when the bath is initially in a thermal state. This implies that the energy variation of the bath is considered to be pure heat.

•

In order to extend to more general situations where the bath can be of arbitrary size and start in an arbitrary state, the following definition was introduced
Elouard and Lombard Latune 2023
,

\sigma^{El}(t):=\dot{S}_{A}(t)+\beta_{B}\dot{E}_{B}^{\text{th}}(t),

(7)

where
\displaystyle\dot{E}_{B}^{\text{th}}(t)={\rm Tr}\left\{H_{B}\frac{d}{dt}w[H_{B},\beta_{B}(t)]\right\}
is the variation rate of the so-called thermal energy of the bath. We introduce the following notation,

w[H_{B},\beta_{B}(t)]:=e^{-\beta_{B}(t)H_{B}}/{\rm Tr}[e^{-\beta_{B}(t)H_{B}}],

(8)

that is used also for system
\displaystyle A
in a straightforward manner. In the above expression, the effective inverse temperature
\displaystyle\beta_{B}(t)
is defined for all times
\displaystyle t
as the inverse temperature of the thermal state of the same entropy as
\displaystyle\rho_{B}(t)
, the state of
\displaystyle B
at time t. In other words,
\displaystyle\beta_{B}(t)
is such that
\displaystyle S\{w[H_{B},\beta_{B}(t)]\}=S[\rho_{B}(t)]
. Note that when
\displaystyle B
is in a thermal state at temperature
\displaystyle T_{B}
, we have
\displaystyle\beta_{B}(t)=(k_{B}T_{B})^{-1}
.

•

Another framework has been also recently introduced which is valid for arbitrary quantum system
\displaystyle B

Colla and Breuer 2022
. The initial state of
\displaystyle B
can be arbitrary as long as not correlated with
\displaystyle A
. However, one technical difficulty is that it is based on an exact master equation describing the local dynamics of
\displaystyle A
. Assume that we do have such a master equation, then it can always be cast into the form,

\dot{\rho}_{A}(t)=-\frac{i}{\hbar}[\tilde{H}_{A}(t),\rho_{A}(t)]+{\cal D}_{t}\rho_{A}(t),

(9)

where
\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{A}(t)
is in general a time-dependent Hamiltonian different from the free Hamiltonian
\displaystyle H_{A}
of
\displaystyle A
, and
\displaystyle{\cal D}_{t}
denotes the dissipator of minimal norm (see
Colla and Breuer 2022
) resulting from the interaction with
\displaystyle B
, which is also time-dependent in general. Then, the entropy production is defined as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sigma^{Co}(t):=

(10)

\displaystyle\displaystyle-{\rm Tr}\Big\{\dot{\rho}_{A}(t)\{\ln\rho_{A}(t)-\ln w[\tilde{H}_{A}(t),\beta_{B}]\}\Big\},

where
\displaystyle\beta_{B}
is the initial inverse temperature of
\displaystyle B
. Note that in general
\displaystyle w[\tilde{H}_{A}(t),\beta_{B}]
is not the instantaneous fixed point of the dynamics, meaning it does not satisfy
\displaystyle{\cal D}_{t}w[\tilde{H}_{A}(t),\beta_{B}]=0
. This is one inherent difficulty of strong coupling and finite baths, i.e. the instantaneous thermal state and the instantaneous fixed point no longer coincide. This observation naturally leads to the last definition of entropy production we will consider in our study.

•

We substitute in Eq. (
10
) the instantaneous thermal state by the instantaneous fixed point of the map:

\sigma^{fp}(t):=-{\rm Tr}\{\dot{\rho}_{A}(t)[\ln\rho_{A}(t)-\ln\rho_{A}^{fp}(t)]\},

(11)

with
\displaystyle\rho_{A}^{fp}(t)
such that
\displaystyle{\cal L}_{t}\rho_{A}^{fp}(t)=0
, where
\displaystyle{\cal L}_{t}
is the generator of the open dynamics followed by
\displaystyle A
(and which will be fully specified in Sec.
III
). The authors of Ref.
Colla and Breuer 2022
actually updated their definition (
10
) in a recent proposal
Colla and Breuer 2024
where they choose a time-dependent effective inverse temperature
\displaystyle\beta_{B}^{\text{eff}}(t)
of
\displaystyle B
such that
\displaystyle w[H_{A}(t),\beta_{B}^{\text{eff}}(t)]
is the instantaneous fixed point, recovering the above definition (
11
) in the case of a quantum harmonic oscillator.

•

Finally, it has also been pointed out that the build up of correlations between system and bath is a central contribution to entropy production
Esposito et al. 2010
;
Santos et al. 2018
;
Landi and Paternostro 2021
, and constitutes a minimal entropy production definition. The correlations between
\displaystyle A
and
\displaystyle B
are defined as

I_{A:B}(t):=S_{A}(t)+S_{B}(t)-S_{AB}(t).

(12)

\displaystyle I_{A:B}(t)
starts at zero since
\displaystyle A
and
\displaystyle B
are assumed to be initially uncorrelated, and then increases with time due to correlations. Since the global evolution of
\displaystyle AB
is unitary, the rate of correlation build-up is

\dot{I}_{A:B}(t)=\dot{S}_{A}(t)+\dot{S}_{B}(t).

(13)

In the following, we include
\displaystyle\dot{I}_{A:B}(t)
in the comparison with the other entropy production definitions.

III
Description of the model system

In this section, we present the setting in which we compare the above definitions of entropy production. We choose a
minimal bath

\displaystyle B
composed only of a single bosonic mode and interacting via the Jaynes-Cumming coupling with a two-level system
\displaystyle A
of free Hamiltonian
\displaystyle H_{A}=\omega_{A}\sigma_{+}\sigma_{-}
. The total Hamiltonian is therefore

H_{AB}=\omega_{A}\sigma_{+}\sigma_{-}+g(\sigma_{+}a+\sigma_{-}a^{\dagger})+\omega_{B}a^{\dagger}a,

(14)

with
\displaystyle\sigma_{+}:=|1\rangle\langle 0|
,
\displaystyle\sigma_{-}:=|0\rangle\langle 1|
, and
\displaystyle|1\rangle
,
\displaystyle|0\rangle
denote respectively the excited and ground states of the Pauli matrix
\displaystyle\sigma_{z}
. We assume that the initial state of
\displaystyle B
is a thermal state at inverse temperature
\displaystyle\beta_{B}
, namely
\displaystyle\rho_{B}(0)=w(H_{B},\beta_{B})
and
\displaystyle H_{B}=\omega_{B}a^{\dagger}a
. Note that the Jaynes-Cumming coupling is usually obtained after the Rotating Wave Approximation (RWA), which is not expected to be valid in strong coupling, precisely the regime we intend to explore. The RWA is only important here for computing the entropy production
\displaystyle\sigma^{Co}
, which requires knowledge of the exact master equation describing the open dynamics. As shown in
Smirne and Vacchini 2010
, this equation can be derived in the case of the Jaynes-Cumming Hamiltonian.

With respect to the strong coupling limitation of the RWA, one can always rewrite the coupling term in the form
\displaystyle g(\sigma_{+}a+\sigma_{-}a)=g\sigma_{x}\frac{a+a^{\dagger}}{2}+g\sigma_{y}\frac{i(a-a^{\dagger})}{2}
, where
\displaystyle\sigma_{x}
and
\displaystyle\sigma_{y}
are the usual Pauli matrices. Then, under these conditions, the reduced dynamics of
\displaystyle A
,

\dot{\rho}_{A}={\rm Tr}_{B}(\dot{\rho}_{AB})=-\frac{i}{\hbar}{\rm Tr}_{B}([H_{AB},\rho_{AB}]),

(15)

can be expressed as an exact master equation
Smirne and Vacchini 2010

\displaystyle\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{A}(t)={\cal L}_{t}\rho_{A}

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\frac{i}{\hbar}[\Omega_{A}(t)\sigma_{+}\sigma_{-},\rho_{A}(t)]

(16)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(t)\left[\sigma_{+}\rho_{A}(t)\sigma_{-}-\frac{1}{2}\{\sigma_{-}\sigma_{+},\rho_{A}(t)\}\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\displaystyle\gamma_{2}(t)\left[\sigma_{-}\rho_{A}(t)\sigma_{+}-\frac{1}{2}\{\sigma_{+}\sigma_{-},\rho_{A}(t)\}\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{3}(t)\left[\sigma_{z}\rho_{A}(t)\sigma_{z}-\rho_{A}(t)\right],

with

\Omega_{A}(t)=-\Im[\frac{\dot{\gamma}(t)}{\gamma(t)}],

(17)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\gamma(t)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-n\beta_{B}\omega_{B}}}{Z}e^{-i\omega_{B}t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\times\left[\cos(\Omega_{n}t/2)-i\frac{\Delta}{\Omega_{n}}\sin(\Omega_{n}t/2)\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\times\left[\cos(\Omega_{n+1}t/2)-i\frac{\Delta}{\Omega_{n+1}}\sin(\Omega_{n+1}t/2)\right],

\Omega_{n}=\sqrt{\Delta^{2}+4g^{2}n},

\gamma_{1}(t)=\frac{\alpha(t)\dot{\beta}(t)-\dot{\alpha}(t)\beta(t)-\dot{\beta}(t)}{\alpha(t)+\beta(t)-1},

(18)

(19)

(20)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\beta(t)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-n\beta_{B}\omega_{B}}}{Z}

and

\gamma_{3}(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\left[\gamma_{1}(t)+\gamma_{2}(t)+2\Re[\frac{\dot{\gamma}(t)}{\gamma(t)}]\right],

with

. Note that the quantity
\displaystyle\beta(t)
defined above and the inverse temperature

Entropy production and memory effects

Memory effects in non-Markovian quantum systems can be characterized in many different ways, and constitute a vast field of research
Rivas et al. 2014
;
Breuer et al. 2016
;
de Vega and Alonso 2017
. The main criteria are CP-divisibility, P-divisibility, and the BLP criterion (see more details in Appendix
D
and
Rivas et al. 2014
;
Breuer et al. 2016
;
de Vega and Alonso 2017
;
Théret et al. 2025
).
For a two-level open system governed by a master equation of the form (
16

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\gamma_{3}(t)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\text{if}~2\Gamma(t)\leq\gamma_{+}(t),~\text{then}~\gamma_{-}(t)^{2}\leq 4\Gamma(t)(\gamma_{+}(t)-\Gamma(t)),

(22)

where
\displaystyle\gamma_{\pm}(t):=\gamma_{1}(t)\pm\gamma_{2}(t)
and
\displaystyle\Gamma(t)=\gamma_{3}(t)+\gamma_{2}(t)/2

VI
Equivalence between P-divisibility and non-negativity of
\displaystyle\sigma_{\text{map}}

Based on the numerical results performed in the case of a qubit interacting with a single bosonic mode, the goal of this section is to prove rigorously the equivalence between P-divisibility and non-negativity of
\displaystyle\sigma_{\text{map}}
. However, in the proof, we consider not only this example, but also a more general two-level open quantum system with phase-covariant dynamics
Filippov et al. 2020
;
Théret et al. 2025
, where we neglect all the nonsecular terms of the master equation. In other words, it means that we consider a master equation of the form (
16
) with arbitrary time-dependent coefficients
\displaystyle\Omega_{A}(t)
and
\displaystyle\gamma_{i}(t)
,
\displaystyle i=1,2,3
. For convenience, we introduce the notations
\displaystyle\gamma_{\pm}(t):=\gamma_{1}(t)\pm\gamma_{2}(t)
, and
\displaystyle\Gamma(t):=\gamma_{3}(t)+\gamma_{+}(t)/2
.

Let
\displaystyle(x(t),y(t),z(t))=(\textrm{Tr}[\rho_{A}\sigma_{x}],\textrm{Tr}[\rho_{A}\sigma_{y}],\textrm{Tr}[\rho_{A}\sigma_{z}])
be the Bloch coordinates of the density operator
\displaystyle\rho_{A}(t)
of the system. We introduce the modulus
\displaystyle r
of the Bloch vector with
\displaystyle r^{2}(t)=x^{2}(t)+y^{2}(t)+z^{2}(t)
. In a specific rotating frame, the dynamics of the Bloch coordinates are governed by the following set of differential equations
Théret et al. 2025
;
Théret and Sugny 2023

\displaystyle\displaystyle\dot{x}=-\Gamma(t)x,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\dot{y}=-\Gamma(t)y,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\dot{z}=\gamma_{-}(t)-\gamma_{+}(t)z.

Starting from Eq. (
11
), straightforward computations show that, for all
\displaystyle t
such that
\displaystyle r(t)\neq 1
,

\sigma^{fp}(t)=-\dot{r}(t)L(r(t))+\dot{z}(t)L(\gamma_{\infty}(t)),

(25)

where
\displaystyle L(x)=\textrm{arctanh}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\ln\!\left(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right)
and
\displaystyle\gamma_{\infty}(t)=\frac{\gamma_{-}(t)}{\gamma_{+}(t)}
.
Note that the entropy might be infinite for pure states
\displaystyle r=1
. Infinite values can be avoided when
\displaystyle\dot{r}_{|r=1}=0
.

From now on we consider only the case where
\displaystyle\gamma_{\infty}(t)
is constant, which is assumed to be positive,

\gamma_{\infty}(t)=z_{\infty}\geq 0.

(26)

The case
\displaystyle\gamma_{\infty}(t)
a negative constant yields the same results. We then get,

\sigma^{fp}=\Gamma\ell^{2}f(r)+\gamma_{+}(z_{\infty}-z)\left(z_{\infty}f(z_{\infty})-zf(r)\right),

(27)

where
\displaystyle f(r)\equiv\frac{1}{r}L(r)=\frac{1}{2r}\ln\!\left(\frac{1+r}{1-r}\right)
is strictly increasing from 1 to
\displaystyle+\infty
, and
\displaystyle\ell(t)^{2}=x(t)^{2}+y(t)^{2}
. As expected we see that
\displaystyle\sigma^{fp}
is invariant under rotations around the
\displaystyle z
-axis and therefore only depends upon
\displaystyle z
and
\displaystyle\ell=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}
.

We now consider
\displaystyle\sigma^{fp}
as a function of the parameters
\displaystyle(\ell,z)
defined within the Bloch ball
\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}=\{(\ell,z)\ :\ \ell^{2}+z^{2}\leq 1\}
. This differs from considering the entropy production obtained by allowing the evolution to run for a time
\displaystyle t
, where only the points
\displaystyle(\ell(t),z(t))
attained by trajectories at time
\displaystyle t
would be taken into account.
According to Eq. (
24
), the map entropy production can be defined as

\sigma_{\rm map}(t)=\inf_{(\ell,z)\in\mathfrak{B}}\sigma^{fp}(t).

We shall prove the following result (see Appendix
A
).

Theorem 1
.

For any
\displaystyle t\geq 0
,
\displaystyle\sigma_{\rm map}(t)\geq 0
if and only if the dynamics is P-divisible at time
\displaystyle t
.

It is already known in full generality that P-divisibility at time
\displaystyle t
implies
\displaystyle\sigma^{fp}(t)\geq 0
for all initial states (see Appendix
F
and
Müller-Hermes and Reeb 2017
). However, the reverse has not been proven. Here we present a direct proof for a class of two-level quantum systems, which has the advantage of identifying trajectories where the non-negativity of
\displaystyle\sigma^{fp}
and therefore P-divisibility might fail to occur.

The structure of the proof is as follows. The mathematical details are given in Appendix
A
. We first argue that in order for
\displaystyle\sigma^{fp}
to be non-negative we must have
\displaystyle\Gamma\geq 0
and
\displaystyle\gamma_{+}\geq 0
, which is also a necessary requirement for P-divisibility. Under these conditions we show that the positivity of
\displaystyle\sigma^{fp}
might fail only inside a subset of
\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}
called
the critical zone

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
.
The proof then goes on by studying the sign of
\displaystyle\sigma^{fp}
along the horizontal slices
\displaystyle z=\rm cste
of
\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}
. It turns out that the sign of
\displaystyle\sigma^{fp}
along these slices is given by the sign of
\displaystyle\dot{r}_{|r=1}
, which is directly related to P-divisibility (see
Théret et al. 2025
).
This eventually proves that
\displaystyle\sigma_{\text{map}}\geq 0\Rightarrow
P-divisibility.
The converse is obtained by directly computing the sign of
\displaystyle\sigma
for a P-divisible dynamical map at time
\displaystyle t
.

VII
Conclusion

In this work, we have established a precise link between two key phenomena in open quantum dynamics, i.e. memory effects and irreversibility.
Irreversibility is quantified by entropy production, which is well understood for systems weakly coupled to an infinite bath, but still present a lack of consensus for strong coupling and finite bath situations. The first part of the paper was therefore dedicated to the comparison between several suggestions of entropy production definitions. The model we consider is a challenging situation combining a minimal bath (a single bosonic mode) with strong coupling. We have shown that all definitions of entropy production considered in this paper coincide at weak coupling, highlighting their relevance, while important discrepancies appear at strong coupling. We have also analytically shown that two definitions coincide exactly (see the proof in Appendix
E
): the widely used definition
\displaystyle\sigma^{Es}
based on the energy variation of the bath
Esposito et al. 2010
, and a definition based on the instantaneous fixed point of the map,
\displaystyle\sigma^{fp}
. This is a surprising coincidence because, conversely to
\displaystyle\sigma^{Es}
,
\displaystyle\sigma^{fp}
depends only on local quantities of the system.

Regarding memory effects, many measures exist, including CP-divisibility, P-divisibility, and the BLP criterion. At weak coupling, they mostly coincide (see Appendix
D
and
Théret et al. 2025
), and we have found a very good correspondence with entropy production. More precisely, the dynamics is P-divisible at time
\displaystyle t
if and only if entropy production is positive for all initial states.

However, as expected, the three figures of merit exhibit significant differences at strong coupling. We find that only P-divisibility shows a close correspondence with the entropy production based on the fixed point
\displaystyle\sigma^{fp}
. Still, the correspondance is not as perfect as for weak coupling and there exist small time intervals on which the dynamics is not P-divisibible but the entropy production is positive for all initial states. A perfect equivalence is obtained by introducing
\displaystyle\sigma_{\text{map}}
, a notion of entropy production at the map level, inspired from the local character of P-divisibility. We show that the dynamics is P-divisible whenever
\displaystyle\sigma_{\text{map}}
is positive. This numerical observation is also confirmed by a rigorous proof valid for a large class of phase-covariant dynamics.

More than indicating that P-divisibility and
\displaystyle\sigma^{fp}
are respectively the most relevant figures of merit for memory effects and irreversibility, this result highlights an important link between irreversibility and lack of memory effects. Conversely, it provides an interpretation of negative entropy production rates in terms of memory effects.

Finally, this paper opens many perspectives, such as whether our conclusions remain valid in other systems and situations, and in particular what happens if the bath is initially out-of-equilibrium. Additionally, it raises the question of how to precisely characterise weak and strong coupling in finite bath situations, and invites further studies on the relation between memory effects and irreversibility in open quantum dynamics, combining the viewpoints of open quantum systems and quantum thermodynamics. It should also be pertinent to study these issues for control quantum systems
Koch 2016
;
Koch et al. 2022
;
Lapert et al. 2013
;
Mukherjee et al. 2013
;
Ansel et al. 2024
.

increases with
\displaystyle\Gamma
, so does
\displaystyle\sigma
.
We conclude that the values of
\displaystyle\sigma
are bounded from below by those of
\displaystyle\sigma_{m}
obtained by taking
\displaystyle\Gamma=\frac{\alpha}{2}\gamma_{+}
.
Hence we restrict to this case, and
\displaystyle\sigma_{m}
has the same sign as

\displaystyle\displaystyle s_{m}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\alpha\ell^{2}}{2r}L(r)+(z_{\infty}-z)\left(L(z_{\infty})-\frac{z}{r}L(r)\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\alpha\frac{r^{2}-z^{2}}{2r}L(r)+(z_{\infty}-z)\left(L(z_{\infty})-\frac{z}{r}L(r)\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{L(r)}{r}\left(\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)z^{2}-z_{\infty}z+\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\frac{L(r)}{r}\frac{\alpha}{2}(r^{2}-1)+(z_{\infty}-z)L(z_{\infty}).

It is easily checked that the coefficient
\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)z^{2}-z_{\infty}z+\frac{\alpha}{2}
is non-negative for all
\displaystyle z
. Moreover, the function
\displaystyle\ell\mapsto\frac{L(r)}{r}\left(\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)z^{2}-z_{\infty}z+\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)

We then consider the second term

A=\frac{L(r)}{r}\frac{\alpha}{2}(r^{2}-1)+(z_{\infty}-z)L(z_{\infty}).

As a function of
\displaystyle\ell

\frac{dA}{d\ell}=\frac{\alpha}{2}\frac{dr}{d\ell}\frac{d}{dr}\left(\frac{L(r)}{r}(r^{2}-1)\right).

We find that
\displaystyle\frac{dA}{d\ell}
has the sign of
\displaystyle B=L(r)-\frac{r}{1+r^{2}}
.
We have

\frac{dB}{dr}=\frac{4r^{2}}{(1-r^{4})(1+r^{2})}\geq 0.

We conclude that the function
\displaystyle B(r)
is strictly increasing from
\displaystyle B(0)=0

\displaystyle\ell\mapsto A(\ell)
is strictly increasing.
We deduce that the function
\displaystyle\ell\mapsto s_{m}(\ell)
is also strictly increasing.
Moreover, we have

Hence, for all
\displaystyle\ell\geq\ell_{\infty}

\displaystyle s_{m}(\ell)\geq 0

\displaystyle\sigma_{\rm map}\geq 0

In this section, we provide a brief overview of the analytical analysis used to determine the sign of the minimum in the definition of map entropy production,
\displaystyle\sigma_{\text{map}}(t)=\min_{\rho_{A}}\Big\{\tilde{\sigma}_{fp}[\rho_{A}]\Big\}

For a state
\displaystyle\rho_{A}
with Bloch coordinates
\displaystyle x
,
\displaystyle y

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle X_{0}<0

\displaystyle X_{0}>0

\displaystyle\gamma_{+}<0

Appendix D
Comparison between the entropy production, CP-divisibility and the BLP criterion

In this section we present some results to highligh the relation between entropy production and CP-divisibility (Fig.
10
) and the BLP criterion (Fig.
11
). We consider the minimum over all initial states of all entropy production definitions.

Firstly, we recall briefly the meaning of these criteria. Memory effect characterizations consider the divisibility properties of the dynamics.
More precisely, the most general dynamics of a quantum system is described by a quantum map
Jagadish and Petruccione 2019
, which are CPTP maps (Completely Positive Trace Preserving maps). A quantum map
\displaystyle\Lambda(t,0)
describing the time evolution of a quantum system from
\displaystyle 0
to
\displaystyle t
, is said to be CP-divisible (P-divisible) if for all
\displaystyle t^{\prime}\in[0;t]
, the intertwined map
\displaystyle\Lambda(t,t^{\prime})
describing the evolution from
\displaystyle t^{\prime}
to
\displaystyle t
is completely positive (positive). We recall here that the dynamics is CP-divisible at time
\displaystyle t
if and only if all decay rates in the master equation are positive at this same instant of time
\displaystyle t

Rivas et al. 2014
;
Breuer et al. 2016
;
de Vega and Alonso 2017
;
Théret et al. 2025
. This criterion is used to obtain the plot in Fig.
10
.

Another well-known memory effect characterization is the BLP criterion
Breuer et al. 2009
, which measures the contractivity of the dynamics, interpreted as a backflow of information. There is no backflow of information (interpreted as the absence of memeory effects) if and only if

\frac{d}{dt}||\Lambda_{t,0}(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})||_{1}\leq 0~~~~\forall t,

(37)

for any states
\displaystyle\rho_{1}
and
\displaystyle\rho_{2}
, where
\displaystyle||X||_{1}:={\rm Tr}[\sqrt{X^{\dagger}X}]
denotes the trace norm, and
\displaystyle\Lambda_{t,0}
is the map describing the open dynamics of the system from 0 to
\displaystyle t
. The BLP criterion (
37
) means that the map
\displaystyle\Lambda_{t,0}
is contractive for arbitrary pair of states. For a qubit whose open dynamics is described by the general master equation (
16
), it is shown in
Théret et al. 2025
that the BLP criterion (
37
) is equivalent at any time
\displaystyle t
to,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(t)+\gamma_{2}(t)\geq 0,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\gamma_{3}(t)+\frac{\gamma_{1}(t)+\gamma_{2}(t)}{2}\geq 0.

(38)

This criterion is used to obtain the plot in Fig.
11

.

\displaystyle\displaystyle\dot{\beta}(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{n-1}\frac{\Delta^{2}-\Omega_{n}^{2}}{\Omega_{n}}\cos\frac{\Omega_{n}t}{2}\sin\frac{\Omega_{n}t}{2},

(52)

from which follows the identities:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\dot{\alpha}(t)=e^{-\omega_{B}\beta_{B}}\dot{\beta}(t)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\alpha(t)\dot{\beta}(t)-\dot{\alpha}(t)\beta(t)=Z^{-1}\dot{\beta}(t)

(53)

with
\displaystyle Z=1/(1-e^{-\omega_{B}\beta_{B}})
and the notation
\displaystyle\beta_{B}=1/k_{B}T_{B}
. This implies

\ln\frac{\gamma_{1}(t)}{\gamma_{2}(t)}=\ln\left(\frac{\alpha(t)\dot{\beta}(t)-\beta(t)\dot{\alpha}(t)-\dot{\beta}(t)}{-\alpha(t)\dot{\beta}(t)+\beta(t)\dot{\alpha}(t)-\dot{\alpha}(t)}\right)=-\omega_{B}\beta_{B},

(54)

leading to

{\rm Tr}[\dot{\rho}_{A}(t)\ln\rho_{A}^{fp}(t)]=-\omega_{B}\beta_{B}\Big[(\dot{\alpha}(t)+\dot{\beta}(t))p_{1}(0)-\dot{\alpha}(t)\Big].

(55)

Using Eq. (
51
) and (
52
), we finally arrive at

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\rm Tr}[\dot{\rho}_{A}(t)\ln\rho_{A}^{fp}(t)]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\omega_{B}\beta_{B}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big[p_{1}(0)(p_{n}+p_{n-1})-p_{n}\Big]

(56)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\times\frac{\Omega_{n}^{2}-\Delta^{2}}{\Omega_{n}}\cos\frac{\Omega_{n}t}{2}\sin\frac{\Omega_{n}t}{2},

which is precisely the expression found above for
\displaystyle\beta_{B}\dot{E}_{B}
.

In conclusion, for
\displaystyle B
initially in a thermal state, we indeed have
\displaystyle\sigma^{Es}=\sigma^{fp}
. Note that the relation (
54
) is only valid for
\displaystyle B
initially in a thermal state, so the hypothesis of initial thermality of
\displaystyle B
is necessary to have the equality
\displaystyle\sigma^{Es}=\sigma^{fp}
(but
\displaystyle A
can be initially in arbitrary state).

E.3
Important consequence

Equation (
54
) reveals that
\displaystyle\gamma_{+}(t)/\gamma_{-}(t)
is indeed constant, which implies that the instantaneous fixed point
\displaystyle\rho_{A}^{fp}(t)
is also constant and actually equal to

\rho_{A}^{fp}=w\left[H_{A},\frac{\omega_{B}}{\omega_{A}}\beta_{B}\right].

(57)

It is worth mentioning that this result is valid only when
\displaystyle B
is initially in a thermal state (at inverse temperature
\displaystyle\beta_{B}
).

Appendix F
P-divisibility implies positivity of
\displaystyle\sigma^{fp}

The entropy production based on the instantaneous fixed point
\displaystyle\rho_{A}^{fp}(t)
is defined as Eq. (
11
),

\sigma^{fp}:=-{\rm Tr}\{\dot{\rho}_{A}(t)[\ln\rho_{A}(t)-\ln\rho_{A}^{fp}(t)]\}.

(58)

Note that when
\displaystyle B
is initially in a thermal state,
\displaystyle\rho_{A}^{fp}(t)
is actually time-independent (see Sec.
E.3
). Introducing
\displaystyle\Lambda(t,0)
, the map describing the evolution of
\displaystyle A
from 0 to
\displaystyle t
, we can rewrite the above expression as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sigma^{fp}

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\lim_{dt\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{dt}\Big\{{\rm Tr}\{\Lambda(t+dt,0)\rho_{A}(0)\Big[\ln\Lambda(t+dt,0)\rho_{A}(0)

(59)

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\ln\rho_{A}^{fp}(t)\Big]-{\rm Tr}\{\rho_{A}(t)\Big[\ln\rho_{A}(t)-\ln\rho_{A}^{fp}(t)\Big]\Big\},

Then, assuming
\displaystyle\Lambda(t,0)
is P-divisible at time
\displaystyle t+dt
, we have,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sigma^{fp}

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\lim_{dt\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{dt}\Big\{{\rm Tr}\{\Lambda(t+dt,t)\rho_{A}(t)\Big[\ln\Lambda(t+dt,t)\rho_{A}(t)

(60)

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\ln\Lambda(t+dt,t)\rho_{A}^{fp}(t)\Big]-{\rm Tr}\{\rho_{A}(t)\Big[\ln\rho_{A}(t)

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\ln\rho_{A}^{fp}(t)\Big]\Big\}\geq 0,

which is always positive by using the contractivity property of the relative entropy under positive maps
Müller-Hermes and Reeb 2017
. Importantly, in the above equation, we have used the fact that
\displaystyle\rho_{A}^{fp}(t)
is the instantaneous fixed point at time
\displaystyle t
so that
\displaystyle\Lambda(t+dt,t)\rho_{A}^{fp}(t)=\rho_{A}^{fp}(t)
.

The above proof can be straightforwardly adapted to the fixed point entropy production extended to the Bloch ball,
\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}^{fp}[\rho_{A}]:=-{\rm Tr}\left\{{\cal L}_{t}\rho_{A}\left[\ln\rho_{A}-\ln\rho_{A}^{fp}(t)\right]\right\}
. This implies that the map entropy production
\displaystyle\sigma_{\text{map}}
is positive for the instant of times for which the map is P-divisible.

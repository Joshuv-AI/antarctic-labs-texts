RAD21 regulation of the enhancer–promoter chromatin loop of RAD51 promotes PARPi resistance in ovarian cancer
Authors: Rui Gou, Xiaohong Chang, Hongyan Cheng, Xue Ye, Ouxuan Liu, Yuexin Hu, Bei Lin, Xiaoping Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAlthough poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase inhibitors (PARPi) have been established to enhance ovarian cancer outcomes, the emergence of drug resistance poses considerable clinical challenges.MethodsIn this study, we constructed a Hi-C atlas to systematically characterize the effect of olaparib on chromatin organization at multiple hierarchical scales, namely, chromosomes, A/B compartments, topologically associating domains, and chromatin loops. To investigate the effects of PARPi on expression of the cohesion subunit RAD21, we established olaparib-resistant ovarian cancer cell line. Furthermore, we examined the effects of RAD21 on the functions of ovarian cancer cells and spheroids based on cell proliferation, apoptosis, and comet assays. In addition, by performing integrated analyses using ChIP-seq datasets, ChIP-qPCR, and chromosome conformation capture assays, we assessed the influence of RAD21 on the enhancer–promoter interactions of a homologous recombination repair gene. Moreover, on the basis of our findings in previous studies using clinical samples, we further evaluated the clinical value of RAD21 in multiple databases.ResultsGenome-wide Hi-C heatmap analysis revealed that olaparib led to a reduction in the genome-wide contact frequency for long distance interactions, altered the degree of chromatin compartmentalization, and promoted compartment switching in ovarian cancer. Differences between the olaparib-treated and control cells with respect to topologically associating domain boundaries and chromatin loops were found to be associated with key cellular functions, such as DNA repair and transcriptional mis-regulation in cancer. Furthermore, PARPi treatment was observed to induce the expression of RAD21, whereas an upregulation of RAD21 promoted proliferation and inhibited apoptosis in ovarian cancer spheroids. Mechanistically, we obtained evidence to indicate that by maintaining enhancer–promoter interactions within chromatin conformation, RAD21 regulates the transcription of RAD51, thereby mediating olaparib resistance in ovarian cancer. The high expression of RAD21 was found to show a significant association with poor overall and progression-free survival in patients with ovarian cancer.ConclusionsOur findings in this study indicate that RAD21 could serve as a potential therapeutic target for overcoming olaparib resistance in ovarian cancer, and provide new insights into the mechanisms underlying the resistance to PARPi from the perspective of chromatin organization.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12967-026-08367-9.

Background

Ovarian cancer is the most lethal malignancy of the female reproductive system [1, 2]. Given its non-specific symptoms and the absence of reliable widely applicable screening modalities, ovarian cancer is frequently diagnosed only at an advanced stage, thereby posing significant challenges with respect to treatment efficacy [3]. Although traditionally, the treatment of ovarian cancer has been largely dependent on a combination of surgery and chemotherapy [4], the advent of the genomic era has revolutionized our understanding of the genomic landscape of ovarian cancer, catalyzing the development of a series of targeted therapies and fundamentally transforming the treatment paradigm. As the most prevalent histological sub-type of epithelial ovarian cancer, high-grade serous ovarian carcinoma is characterized by the highest prevalence of germline alterations in homologous recombination (HR) DNA repair genes, including BRCA1 and BRCA2, with 50% of cases being associated with a deficiency in HR [5, 6]. Poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) enzymes act as sensors of DNA damage and coordinate the repair of single-strand DNA breaks (SSBs). At SSBs sites, PARP1 can, nevertheless, be bound by PARP inhibitors (PARPi), thereby hampering effective repair and ultimately leading to double-strand breaks (DSBs) in DNA [7]. Consequently, the failure of cells with defective DSB repair to effectively resolve the accumulation of DSBs induced by PARPi ultimately results in cell death, a phenomenon also referred to as synthetic lethality. Although the efficacy of PARPi in the treatment of ovarian cancer is encouraging, issues such as drug resistance and loss of efficacy remain as substantial challenges [8, 9]. Consequently, elucidating the mechanisms underlying PARPi resistance and developing effective counter-measures are essential for enhancing clinical benefits.

Cohesin is a member of the chromosome structure maintenance complex family of proteins that are characterized by a core composition of four subunits, namely SMC1, SMC3, RAD21/Scc1, and STAG/SA/Scc3. It has been established to play key roles in regulating chromosome segregation, gene transcription, DNA repair, and the stability of replication forks [10, 11], with spatial transcriptomics analysis indicating that cohesin is enriched at replication origins and functions in the formation or stability of chromatin loops in replication factories [12]. Live-cell imaging technology has confirmed that depletion of RAD21 is associated with significant increases in three-dimensional (3D) distances [13]. We have previously observed a marked upregulation of RAD21 in ovarian cancer and shown this to be correlated with a poor prognosis. The findings of in vivo experiments have indicated that RAD21 promotes tumor growth. Furthermore, by facilitating the repair of DSBs, RAD21 reduces the sensitivity of ovarian cancer cells to PARPi [14]. Recent studies have shown that the formation of DNA damage repair foci is mediated by loop extrusion [15], although whether RAD21 mediates PARPi resistance via this mechanism has yet to be determined.

In this context, we developed olaparib-resistant ovarian cancer cell lines via continuous exposure to olaparib, and found that RAD21 regulates the transcription of the homologous recombination repair (HRR) gene RAD51 by maintaining enhancer–promoter interactions, thereby mediating olaparib resistance in ovarian cancer. In addition, we utilized Hi-C technology to demonstrate the effects of olaparib on the spatial organization of chromatin in ovarian cancer cells. By elucidating the mechanisms underlying olaparib resistance and determining the changes in chromatin organization induced by olaparib, we provide new insights and propose therapeutic strategies that could potentially contribute to overcoming the resistance to PARPi in ovarian cancer.

Materials and methods

Cell culture and reagents

The cell lines OVCAR3 (human ovarian adenocarcinoma cell line NIH: OVCAR-3) and ES-2 (human ovarian clear cell carcinoma cells) used in this study were purchased from the Shanghai Cell Collection Center. Cells were cultured in RPMI1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum at 37 °C within a 5% CO2 atmosphere at saturated humidity. Olaparib was purchased from Beyotime (SC9118; China).

Generation of olaparib-resistant cells

Resistance to olaparib was induced in ovarian cancer cells via a step-wise exposure to increasing concentrations of the olaparib [16, 17]. For both OVCAR3 and ES-2 cells, the initial concentration of olaparib in the culture medium was 1 µM, with the medium being replenished with fresh olaparib-containing medium at 3- or 4-day intervals, and with a doubling of the preceding olaparib concentration every 2 weeks. Having thus exposed the cells for 4 months, we succeeded in establishing olaparib-resistant cells, as confirmed by 7.3- to 8.2-fold increases in the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50 concentration). Thereafter, the resistance of the OVCAR3 OlaR and ES-2 OlaR cells was routinely maintained at 25 and 50 µM olaparib, respectively. Prior to being used for subsequent experiments, cells were cultured in the absence of olaparib for 48 h. For experimental purposes, the cells were exposed to different concentrations of olaparib for 72 h.

Cell transfection

Cells were transfected with small interfering (si) RNAs using liposomes (Lipo 3000 transfection kit; GIBCO, Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) and the siRNAs shown in Table S1. Overexpression plasmids were constructed commercially by the Genechem Company (Shanghai, China), and stable cells were subsequently established via puromycin selection.

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction

Total RNA was obtained using TRIzol reagent (Takara, Dalian, China) and subsequently reverse transcribed to cDNA using a cDNA synthesis kit (11141ES60; Yeasen, Shanghai, China). GAPDH (B661104; BBI, Shanghai, China) was used as the internal reference and the levels of mRNA expression were determined using a SYBR Green PCR kit (11201ES08; Yeasen) in conjunction with the 2−ΔΔCT method. The sequences of the primers used for amplification are shown in Table S2.

Western blot analysis

Protein extracts were prepared from cells using RIPA lysis buffer, subjected to sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis, and transferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride membranes. Having initially blocked the membranes with 5% skimmed milk, they were incubated overnight with the following primary antibodies at 4 °C: anti-GAPDH (1:1500, TA-08; Zhongshan Jinqiao, Beijing, China), anti-RAD21 (1:1000, 27071-1-AP; Proteintech, Wuhan, China), and anti-RAD51 (1:1000, 67024-1-Ig; Proteintech). The following day, the membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:5000, ZB-2305, ZB-2306; Zhongshan Jinqiao) for 2 h at room temperature, and thereafter exposed using an enhanced chemiluminescence reagent.

Cancer cell spheroid formation

Cells were cultured to 80% confluence, followed by synchronization and viability counting, and subsequent preparation of a homogeneous single-cell suspension. To establish ovarian cancer spheroids, 5 × 103 OVCAR3/well or 3 × 103 ES-2/well were seeded in the wells of 96-well ultra-low attachment (ULA) plates (YZ-7007; Corning, Kennebunk, ME, USA) [18, 19], cultured in 100 µL of culture medium, and incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO2. Culture medium (RPMI1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum) was replaced with fresh medium after 48 h. Individual spheroids were collected via manual pipetting, with microscopic images being obtained daily for five consecutive days to monitor integrity and uniformity.

Cell proliferation assay

Cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 5000 cells/well and cultured in media containing olaparib. Following the addition of CCK8 solution (B34302; Bimake, Houston, TX, USA), the cells were incubated for a further 2 h. Spheroids were similarly incubated for 3 h following the addition of CCK-3D solution (C0049S; Beyotime). Cell proliferation was determined based on a measurement of optical density at 450 nm.

Cell apoptosis assay

Cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 5000 cells/well and cultured in media containing olaparib for 72 h followed by incubation with YO-PRO-1 and propidium iodide (PI) dyes (C1075S; Beyotime) at 37℃ for 5 min. Whereas YO-PRO-1 stains the DNA of apoptotic cells, emitting a green fluorescence, the DNA of necrotic cells is stained with both YO-PRO-1 and PI. Cell nuclei were stained with DAPI. Spheroids were cultured for 72 h in 96-well ULA plates and similarly stained. Having carefully transferred the spheroids from the ULA plates to the wells of 96-well plates using a pipette and aspirated the original culture medium, 100 µL of the pre-mixed Beyo3D™ YO-PRO-1/PI working solution (C1363S; Beyotime) was added to each well, followed by incubation in the dark at 37 °C for 15 min. Following staining, the staining solution was aspirated from wells, followed by the addition of phosphate-buffered saline along the inner walls of each well to wash the spheroids once. After replacing with complete culture medium, fluorescence staining was photographed under a fluorescence microscope.

Comet assay

Cells were resuspended in pre-cooled phosphate-buffered saline to give a concentration of 1 × 106 cells/mL. Following the protocol of a DNA damage detection kit (KGA240; KeyGEN BioTECH, Jiangsu, China), 10 µL of cell suspension was mixed with 75 µL of 0.7% low-melting-point agarose. Subsequently, the mixture was pipetted onto pre-coated comet assay slides, and having incubated in lysis buffer at 4 °C for 2 h, the slides were treated with alkaline electrophoresis solution at room temperature for 1 h. The samples were then subjected to electrophoresis at 25 V and 300 mA for 30 min, after which the slides were stained with PI, with the levels of DNA damage being presented as a percentage of the DNA in the tail.

Chromatin-immunoprecipitation–quantitative polymerase chain reaction

Chromatin-immunoprecipitation–quantitative polymerase chain reaction (ChIP-qPCR) analysis was performed according to the instructions of a SimpleChIP plus enzymatic chromatin IP kit (9004 S; CST, USA). Briefly, cells were crosslinked with 1% formaldehyde for 15 min at room temperature, quenched with 0.125 M glycine, and lysed by placing in lysis buffer and sonicating on ice. The lysates thus obtained were subsequently incubated overnight at 4℃ with anti-RAD21 (ab217678; abcam, USA), anti-H3K4Me3 (213224; abcam), and control antibodies followed by immunoprecipitation with ChIP-grade protein G agarose beads. Finally, both beads and input DNA were reverse cross-linked and purified. The sequences of the primers used form amplification are shown in Table S3.

Chromosome conformation capture assay

The procedure for used for sample collection and cross-linking followed the protocol described for the ChIP assay. Chromosome conformation capture (3 C) assays were conducted according to the instructions of a chromosome conformation capture kit (Bes5006; bersinbio, Guangzhou, China). The precipitated pellet was washed and re-suspended in restriction enzyme buffer. DNA was digested overnight at 37 °C with 200 U ScaI restriction enzyme (ER0431; Thermo) followed by incubation at 65 °C for 30 min to inactivate the enzyme. T4 DNA ligase was then added followed by incubation at 16 °C for 2 h to facilitate DNA ligation. Subsequently, DNA extraction and qRT-PCR were performed. The sequences of the primers used for amplification are shown in Table S4.

Hi-C library construction

A Hi-C library was constructed according to the previously established methodologies [20]. Briefly, ovarian cancer cells were cultured for 72 h in medium containing olaparib or DMSO, until reaching a cell count of approximately 2 × 107 per sample. After cross-linking, quenching, and lysing, the DNA was digested using restriction enzymes to generate sticky ends. The label-free ends were then biotinylated, and DNA ligases were used to attach DNA fragments. The purified DNA was then subjected to shearing to yield 300- to 500-bp fragments, with the labeled DNA being captured using streptavidin beads. Hi-C libraries were amplified via PCR and sequenced using the BGI DNBSEQ-T7 platform (PE150 mode) [21].

Hi-C data analysis

For processing the Hi-C data, we used a modified version of the Distiller pipeline. Following pre-processing using Chromap [22], the data were filtered using pairtools, and the construction of multi-resolution interaction matrices and ICE correction were performed using cooler. The correlation between Hi-C libraries prepared from different samples was analysed using HiCRep [23], and we subsequently conducted genome-wide coverage analysis on a 100-kb resolution interaction matrix and interaction attenuation analysis on a 40-kb resolution corrected matrix. On the basis of the results obtained for the 100-kb resolution interaction matrix, we conducted principal component analysis (PCA) to investigate genomic compartments [24], categorizing bins as compartment A or B based on their positive or negative values, respectively. The boundaries of topologically associating domains (TADs) were calculated using the Insulation Score method derived from single chromosome interaction matrices [25], and the TADs of different samples were analysed at a 40-kb resolution. Thereafter, MUSTACHE was applied for loop calling and differential analysis, whereas differential gene expression between samples was assessed by performing Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses.

Database processing

Genetic variation with respect to cohesin subunits was analysed using the cBioPortal database [26], whereas the GENT2 and UALCAN databases were used to evaluate the expression of RAD21 [27, 28], and the Kaplan–Meier plotter was employed to analyse the survival outcomes [29]. Gene perturbation data were obtained from the DepMap database [30], and the relationships between RAD21 and tumor mutational burden (TMB), as well as microsatellite instability (MSI), were examined using TCGAplot [31]. Genes established to be co-expressed with RAD21 were retrieved from the cBioPortal database, and those with a Spearman’s correlation coefficient value exceeding 0.50 were subjected to KEGG enrichment analysis [26, 32]. Targeted regulation of RAD21 was retrieved from the hTFtarget database [33]. The GEPIA3 platform was utilized to validate the correlation between RAD21 and RAD51 [34], and an online ChIP-seq dataset was obtained from the Cistrome DB database [35].

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using SPSS software (version 22.0; IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA). Results are presented as the means ± standard deviation. The statistical significance of differences between two groups was assessed using Student’s t-test, whereas that between multiple groups was evaluated using a one-way ANOVA or the Kruskal–Wallis test. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Olaparib influences the spatial organization of chromatin in ovarian cancer cells

To investigate the organization of chromatin in ovarian cancer cells treated with PARPi, we exposed OVCAR3 cells to 13 µM olaparib for 72 h, using DMSO as a control, and subsequently generated Hi-C maps (Fig. 1A). The genome-wide heatmap of Hi-C revealed that olaparib resulted in a slight reduction in both inter- and intra-chromosomal interactions (Fig. 1B). Taking chromosome 5 as an example, the Hi-C contact matrices revealed a significant reduction in the local Hi-C contacts at peripheral sites of the main diagonal following treatment, whereas the number of short-distance contacts along the main diagonal showed no appreciable change (Fig. 1C). By integrating the interaction attenuation curves across the genome, we observed that there was a reduction in the genome-wide contact frequency specifically with respect to long-distance interactions in olaparib-treated cells (Fig. 1D), thereby indicating that olaparib treatment led to scale-dependent changes in the overall compaction of chromatin.

As fundamental characteristics of the organization of chromatin, chromatin compartments can be classified as compartments A and B, defined by the first principal component (PC1). Notably, compartment A represents an open chromatin region that is rich in intrinsic genes that are actively expressed. The high plasticity feature of compartments facilitates the inter-conversion between compartments A and B to meet the survival and functional demands of cells [24]. Again, taking chromosome 5 as an example, we found that compared with the controls (lower left) treatment with olaparib (upper right) resulted in a diminished intensity of the “checkerboard” pattern, indicating a reduced level of compartmentalization within chromatin (Fig. 1E). Nevertheless, the majority of the compartmentalization remained consistent between the two groups, with 48.67% and 46.29% of the genome comprising compartments A and B, respectively. However, following exposure to olaparib, 1.73% of the genomic regions switched from compartment A to B, whereas for 3.31% of the genomic regions the conversion was in the opposite direction (Fig. 1F). In terms of individual genes, 1.66% of genes switched from compartment A to B in response to olaparib treatment, whereas 3.44% of genes underwent the opposite conversion (Fig. 1G). Saddle plots further revealed reductions in the frequency of both intra-compartment (A–A and B–B) and inter-compartment (A–B) interactions in the olaparib-treated cells (Fig. 1H). Collectively, these findings thus indicate that exposure to olaparib altered the degree of chromatin compartmentalization and led to compartment switching.

Differential genes within TAD boundaries and chromatin loops are associated with cancer

In the Hi-C contact frequency map, TADs are manifested as local chromatin domains with high-frequency internal interactions along the diagonal [36]. We compared the local TAD distribution and identified several differential TAD boundary regions (Fig. 2A). Notably, there was no significant change in TAD boundary strength following olaparib treatment, with strong correlation observed between the two groups (Fig. 2B). In addition, we obtained similar insulation scores for the TAD boundaries of the two groups (Fig. 2C). Although a majority of the TAD boundaries remained stable (boundary number: 3116, 72.92%), 12.15% and 11.91% of the TAD boundaries disappeared or emerged in response to olaparib treatment, respectively (Fig. 2D). GO enrichment analysis indicated that genes located within differential TAD boundaries are associated with biological processes such as DNA repair (Fig. 2E), whereas KEGG enrichment analysis indicated that differential genes are associated with the cell cycle; AMPK, Notch, and p53 signaling pathways; DNA replication; and multiple cancers (Fig. 2F).

TADs comprise folded chromatin loops anchored by the CCCTC-binding factor (CTCF) and cohesin [37]. As a basic feature of eukaryotic genomes, chromatin loops physically orientate regulatory elements close to target genes [38]. Among all the chromosomes analysed, those derived from the olaparib-treated cells were found to be characterized by a larger number of chromatin loops compared with those from the control cells (16540 vs. 15448, respectively). Specifically, whereas 3331 rings were unique to the control cells, 4463 were unique to the olaparib-treated cells. Figure 2G illustrates the distinct chromatin rings observed in the two groups of cells. Subsequently, we conducted GO enrichment analysis, which revealed that genes within the differential chromatin loops are associated with cell–cell signalling (Fig. 2H). In addition, KEGG enrichment analysis indicated that these genes are associated with transcriptional mis-regulation in cancer, as well as the Wnt and Hippo signaling pathways (Fig. 2I). These findings thus indicate that olaparib induce alterations in the organization of chromatin and are involved in essential cellular processes, including DNA repair and transcriptional mis-regulation in cancer.

RAD21 is upregulated in multiple cancers and is correlated with a poor prognosis

As a core component involved in chromatin loop formation, we investigated the genetic variations in cohesin. Among its subunits, RAD21 was notably established to be characterized by the highest frequency of gene amplification (Fig. 3A). In previous studies in which we analysed 130 clinical samples, we observed elevated levels of RAD21 expression in ovarian cancer and established that this was associated with a poor prognosis [14]. By evaluating its pan-cancer expression, we found that RAD21 was markedly overexpressed in malignancies, including but not limited to cervical, endometrial, and ovarian cancers (Fig. 3B). Given the clinical success of PARPi in ovarian cancer and their well-established synthetic lethal mechanism, we sought to define the role of RAD21 in ovarian cancer. Compared with normal ovarian tissues, the levels of RAD21 protein were shown to be significantly elevated in tissues of ovarian cancer (Fig. 3C). In addition, gene perturbation data based on CRISPR or RNAi modification revealed a high degree of cellular dependency on the expression of RAD21 (Fig. 3D). By utilizing a platform encompassing a large sample size, we demonstrated that the overall (OS) and progression-free (PFS) survival of ovarian cancer patients with a high expression of RAD21 were significantly lower than those with a low expression of RAD21 (Fig. 3E, F). This differential survival similarly applied to patients who received platinum-based chemotherapy (Fig. 3G, H). However, although the expression of RAD21 was established to be correlated with TMB and MSI in certain cancer types, similar associations were not observed in ovarian cancer (Fig. 3I, J). KEGG enrichment analysis conducted on the genes co-expressed with RAD21 (Supplementary file 2) revealed that in addition to the cell cycle, these genes are also associated with multiple key signaling pathways, including Hippo and VEGF (Fig. 3K). Among these, the Hippo pathway has been demonstrated to be associated with DNA damage repair and resistance to PARPi [39].

Elevated RAD21 expression in olaparib-resistant ovarian cancer cells

We have previously confirmed that when overexpressed, RAD21 promotes resistance to three types of PARPi, including olaparib, rucaparib, and niraparib [14]. To clarify the reciprocal relationship between RAD21 and PARPi, in the present study, we treated OVCAR3 and ES-2 cells with olaparib at concentrations ranging from 0 to 40 µM for a duration of 72 h, in response to which, we detected no significant changes in the levels of RAD21 expression (Fig. 4A). On the basis of these observations, we subsequently sought to determine whether the pattern of expression would be altered in response to an extension of the culture time. Stable olaparib-resistant ovarian cancer cell lines were generated by exposing these to a gradient of olaparib concentrations, and we found that compared with the parental cell lines, there were increases in the IC50 values for olaparib in both the OVCAR3 OlaR and ES-2 OlaR cell lines (Fig. 4B). Consistently, when exposed to the same concentration of olaparib, the levels of necrosis and apoptosis in OVCAR3 OlaR and ES-2 OlaR cells were significantly lower than those detected in the parental cell lines (Fig. 4C). Accordingly, consistent with our hypothesis, we established that RAD21 was upregulated in both OVCAR3 OlaR and ES-2 OlaR cells (Fig. 4D), and on the basis of this finding, we speculate that olaparib induces the expression of RAD21, which in turn promotes resistance to olaparib.

RAD21 promotes the proliferation and inhibits apoptosis of ovarian cancer spheroids

To determine the function of RAD21, we knocked down and overexpressed RAD21 using siRNA and overexpression plasmids respectively (Fig. 5A). Building upon previous results obtained at the 2D level, we developed 3D ovarian cancer spheroids to more accurately replicate the intricate physiology and morphology of cancerous tissues. ES-2 spheroids appeared compact after only 24 h and became round after 48 h. In contrast, OVCAR3 formed only loose aggregates after 24 h and became round after 48–72 h (Fig. 5B). CCK-3D assays indicated that overexpression of RAD21 promotes the viability of ovarian cancer spheroids (Fig. 5C). Furthermore, using a YO-PRO-1/PI detection kit, we assessed the apoptosis and necrosis of the spheroids, and accordingly found that compared with the control spheroids, overexpression of RAD21 led to a reduced proportion of spheroids stained with YO-PRO-1 and PI, thereby indicating reductions in the levels of necrosis and apoptosis. Conversely, the knockdown RAD21 was found to be associated with elevated levels of necrosis and apoptosis (Fig. 5D). On the basis of these finding, we thus demonstrated that RAD21 plays a role in regulating the proliferation and apoptotic capacity of ovarian cancer spheroids.

In combination with olaparib, inhibition of RAD21 synergistically promotes cell apoptosis via DNA double-strand breaks

Previously, we established that combining RAD21 inhibition with exposure to PARPi is associated with synergistic anti-cancer effects characterized by an increase in the formation of γ-H2AX foci [14]. To further elucidate the mechanisms underlying the involvement of RAD21, we treated cells with olaparib at its IC₅₀ concentration (13.26 µM for OVCAR3 and 18.54 µM for ES-2 cell lines) for 72 h. The results revealed that treatment with olaparib promoted an elevation in the total number of apoptotic and necrotic cells in both OVCAR3 and ES-2. Notably, co-treatment with olaparib and RAD21 siRNA further intensified cellular apoptosis, while the RAD21 overexpression group showed the opposite result (Fig. 6A, B). To evaluate DNA damage, we conducted comet assays, the results of which revealed increases in tail moments in both cell lines treated with olaparib. Furthermore, we detected larger amounts of DNA in tails in response to olaparib following RAD21 inhibition, whereas RAD21 overexpression contributed to the maintenance of DNA integrity and diminished the levels of DNA in tails (Fig. 6C, D). On the basis of these observations, we speculate that RAD21 protects cells from DNA damage and inhibits apoptosis, thereby mediating a resistance to olaparib.

RAD21 regulates the enhancer–promoter interactions of the HR gene

As a pivotal regulator of chromatin organization, RAD21 is essential for modulating gene transcription processes. On the basis of our analysis of ChIP-seq datasets, we identified multiple potential targets of RAD21 (Fig. 7A). An assessment of mRNA expression of all the screened targets revealed that only RAD51 and RAD21 showed consistent patterns of expression (Fig. 7B). Correlation between the expression of RAD21 and RAD51 in ovarian cancer was further validated using the cBioPortal and GEPIA3 databases (Fig. 7C, D), and we also demonstrated that an inhibition of RAD21 resulted in a reduction in the levels of RAD51 mRNA expression, whereas an overexpression of RAD21 had the opposite effects (Fig. 7E), with this trend also being observed at the protein level (Fig. 7F).

To determine the effects of RAD21 on RAD51, using ChIP-seq datasets, we analysed the enriched peaks of sequencing reads for the histone modifications H3K4me3 (enriched at promoters) and H3K27ac (enriched at enhancers), as well as RAD21 (Fig. 7G), with subsequent ChIP-qPCR analysis confirming the enrichment of RAD21 within the promoter region of RAD51 (Fig. 7H). Further ChIP-qPCR employing histone antibodies revealed that overexpression of RAD21 was associated with a significant enhancement of H3K4me3 modification within the promoters of RAD51 (Fig. 7I).

Given that cohesin functions synergistically with CTCF to maintain enhancer–promoter chromatin loops, to further elucidate the mechanisms, we performed 3 C assays to evaluate the frequency of contact between the enhancers and promoters of RAD51. Notably, regardless of whether olaparib was administered, ligation frequencies between enhancer and promoter interactions at RAD51 loci were significantly diminished in response to an inhibition of RAD21 (Fig. 7J). On the basis of these observations, we accordingly propose that RAD21 plays a pivotal role in regulating enhancer–promoter chromatin loops for the HRR gene RAD51. Inhibition of RAD21 disrupts these loops, resulting in the silencing of RAD51.

Discussion

Although PARPi have been demonstrated to have notable efficacy in clinical applications, it has also been established that processes such as HR restoration, the stabilization of DNA replication forks, overexpression of drug efflux protein, and epigenetic modifications can contribute to generating resistance to PARPi [40]. In this regard, in the present study, we sought to characterize the reciprocal relationship between RAD21 and olaparib, and determine the effects of RAD21 on the enhancer–promoter interaction of an HRR gene, thereby clarifying the mechanisms associated with PARPi resistance in ovarian cancer. Furthermore, we examined the influence of olaparib on chromatin organization, thereby providing new insights into the mechanisms underlying this resistance.

Genomic integrity is compromised by both endogenous and exogenous damaging factors [41], and in this context, there has been increasing evidence to indicate the roles played by cohesion, a key chromatin organizer and transcriptional regulator, in the maintenance of genomic integrity [42, 43]. A dysfunction of cohesin has been established to be associated with changes in chromatin accessibility and transcription, which ultimately contribute to the development of cancer [11]. At present, numerous studies have firmly established the critical role of RAD21 in regulating DNA damage response and genome stability. For instance, RAD21 depletion induces massive genome-wide DNA breaks and 147 translocation hotspots genes. It also causes replication stresses induced by excessive DNA replication initiation, ultimately resulting in severe DNA damage [44]. Furthermore, Rad21 heterozygous mutant cells exhibit impaired activation of the DNA damage checkpoint and defective DSB repair [45]. Unlike other subunits that are prone to mutation, RAD21 is frequently overexpressed in different cancers and this has been found to be associated with a diminished sensitivity to ionizing radiation, as well as to treatment with cisplatin [46–48]. The findings of our previous research have revealed that RAD21 is highly expressed in ovarian epithelial malignant tumor tissues and is identified as an independent risk factor influencing the survival prognosis of patients. To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to propose that an overexpression of RAD21 contributes to PARPi resistance and leads to significant reductions in the accumulation of γH2AX [14]. In the present study, with a view toward more realistically simulating the in vivo environment, we developed 2D and 3D cell models, and using these, we demonstrated that in combination with olaparib, an inhibition of RAD21 synergistically promotes cell apoptosis and leads to DNA DSBs. Accordingly, in response to the selection pressure imposed by olaparib, cells characterized by elevated levels of endogenous RAD21 expression may survive, leading to an increase in RAD21 expression following olaparib treatment.

Chromatin is a nucleoprotein polymer comprising DNA wrapped around histones [49]. The recently developed in situ Hi-C technique enables the generation of high-resolution 3D genomic maps, and has contributed to revealing spatiotemporal variability at multiple scales, including chromosomes, A/B compartments, TADs, and chromatin loops [50–52]. The loop extrusion model posits that cohesin is extruded along chromatin to form progressively enlarged rings, and that when this progression is impeded by boundary elements such as CTCF, chromatin loops are formed [53, 54]. Hi-C and genomic analyses have revealed that an upregulation of RAD21 contributes to promoting an extensive extrusion of chromatin and development of a vermicelli-like distribution of cohesin, along with increases in the frequency of chromosomal contact for short distance interactions [55]. Conversely, a depletion of RAD21 has been demonstrated to be associated with significant reductions in TADs and loops, an increase in 3D distances, and the loss of numerous enhancer–promoter interactions [56, 57]. Notably, these effects were found to be more pronounced than those observed following a depletion of CTCF [13, 56]. Evidence to date indicates that loop extrusion serves as a mechanism for the formation of DNA damage repair foci [15]. A depletion of cohesin subunits has been found to coincide with increases in the levels of γH2AX and a DSB-dependent transcriptional downregulation of genes within γH2AX domains [58], thereby tending to indicate that loop structures dependent on cohesin may be intricately linked to DSB repair processes. As a core protein involved in HR, RAD51 plays key roles in identifying homologous sequences and facilitating strand exchange, as well as in protecting and resuming arrested replication forks [59]. Our findings in the present study provide evidence that RAD21 modulates the enrichment of H3K4me3 within the promoter region of RAD51, which may stem from the regulatory influence of RAD21 on the level of H3K4me3 expression and chromatin modification [60]. Furthermore, we employed 3 C technology to reveal that an inhibition of RAD21 disrupts the development of enhancer–promoter chromatin loops, which in turn influences the expression of RAD51 in response to DNA damage. This dysregulation may ultimately lead to the accumulation DSBs and cell apoptosis. These findings accordingly provide new insights into the RAD21-mediated resistance to olaparib from the perspective of chromatin organization.

Given its roles as a physical carrier of DNA and pivotal signaling hub, changes in the organization of chromatin are often associated with cancer and developmental diseases [61, 62]. In this context, with advances in new technologies, chromatin modifiers and reader domains have emerged as potentially valuable targets for drug development [49]. Furthermore, the effects of certain traditional genotoxic anticancer drug on the organization of chromatin have gradually come to light [63]. For example, doxorubicin has been to demonstrated to promote a localized reduction in Hi-C interactions within specific genomic regions containing active promoters, accompanied by the redistribution of RAD21 signals in the vicinity of active and acetylated sites [63]. Furthermore, by enhancing DNA damage repair and regulating gene expression, an increase in accessibility to chromatin has been shown to promote resistance to PARPi [64], although at present, there is limited information regarding the influence of PARPi on the chromatin landscape. Notably, however, Moser et al. have demonstrated that PARPi can induce the release of histones from chromatin, and these authors also found that exposure of HAP1ΔBRCA1Δ53BP1 cells to 1 µM olaparib for 24 h had no appreciable effects regarding the overall conformation of chromatin [65]. However, in the present study, we found that exposure of OVCAR3 cells to olaparib for 72 h was associated with reductions in the frequency of genome-wide contact for long distance interactions and a modification of the degree of chromatin compartmentalization. Notably, these findings are consistent with those of previous studies that have examined effects of the upregulation of RAD21 on the frequency of long-distance contact frequency and compartmentalization [55]. Although a majority of TAD boundaries remain stable, in cancer, changes in TAD boundaries and chromatin loops are associated with DNA repair and transcriptional mis-regulation, which thus indicates that alterations in the organization of chromatin within the human genome may be influenced by both the concentration and duration of treatment. Although the direct effects of olaparib on chromatin organization may be limited, the release of histones and changes in the expression of RAD21 induced by olaparib could indirectly influence chromatin dynamics. Furthermore, the damage to DNA induced by olaparib may also contribute to the observed changes in chromatin organization. Consequently, the prolonged use of olaparib may lead to modifications in the organization of chromatin. Future research involving clinical models of PARPi resistance will contribute to further enhancing our understanding of how PARPi influence the organization of chromatin and, ultimately, the fate of cells.

Although this exploratory study provides a theoretical basis for olaparib resistance, several limitations should be considered. Firstly, given the mechanism of PARPi, employing a homologous recombination deficiency (HRD) cell model appears more appropriate for elucidating acquired resistance. Building upon our prior work, we continued to use OVCAR3 and ES-2 cell lines, both of which have been confirmed as BRCA wild-type cell lines. Although OVCAR3 cells retain the capacity to form RAD51 foci and lack functionally inactivating mutations in HRR genes, this widely used ovarian cancer cell line exhibits the same sensitivity to carboplatin and PARPi comparable to that of cell lines with known BRCA mutations. This paradoxical phenotype can be attributed to functional loss of HRR, as demonstrated by plasmid re-joining assay [66]. Consequently, OVCAR3 has emerged as a valuable model for investigating PARPi resistance [67, 68]. In future studies, we will extend our investigations to BRCA1/2-deficient cell lines and clinical specimens derived from patients with PARPi resistance, to assess the generalizability of our findings. Secondly, although our prior work demonstrated that RAD21 confers resistance to three types of PARPi (olaparib, rucaparib, and niraparib), this study specifically focuses on olaparib, the prototypical and most extensively characterized PARPi. Future investigations incorporating additional PARPi will be essential to validate the broader applicability of our findings. Thirdly, as a core subunit of cohesin, RAD21 has been widely confirmed to be associated with a wide range of DNA damages. The impact of olaparib on chromatin organization has been comprehensively discussed in this article. In our subsequent studies, the role of RAD21 in mediating these alterations will be investigated.

Conclusion

On the basis of our findings in this study, we propose novel mechanisms whereby RAD21 mediates olaparib resistance in ovarian cancer from the perspective of chromatin enhancer–promoter loops. We also highlight the effects of olaparib on the organization of chromatin within the human genome. Overall, we present robust pre-clinical evidence indicating that RAD21 could serve as a biomarker for the responsiveness to PARPi, as well as a potential therapeutic target.

Impact of eccentric and calcified plaque on bioresorbable scaffold and drug-eluting metallic stent expansion characteristics: a comparative intravascular ultrasound study
Authors: Wenbo Sun, Chengpeng Liu, Jiasheng Yin, Chenguang Li, Hao Lu, Juying Qian, Zhiqiang Pei, Li Shen, Junbo Ge
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPrevious studies have demonstrated greater lumen eccentricity and asymmetry with BRS compared to DES, but evidence regarding their expansion performance in complex lesions characterized by eccentric morphology or calcification remains scarce. This study aimed to compare and analyze the acute device performance of bioresorbable scaffolds (BRS) versus drug-eluting metallic stents (DES) using intravascular ultrasound (IVUS), and their expansion characteristics in eccentric and calcified lesions.MethodsIVUS analysis was performed pre- and post-procedure in 184 patients (48 BRS and 136 DES). Plaque eccentricity was quantified during pre-procedural using the plaque eccentricity index (PEI), with lesions stratified by calcification severity (no/mild: ≤90°; moderate/severe: >90° arc), while post-procedural device expansion performance was evaluated through three IVUS parameters including asymmetry index (AI), expansion ratio (ER) and eccentricity index (SEI).ResultsPre-procedural PEI demonstrated predictive value for post-procedural SEI and AI in both groups, yielding Areas Under the Curve (AUCs) of 0.811 and 0.773 for BRS, and 0.747 and 0.713 for DES. At high PEI, BRS showed significantly lower SEI than DES (0.87 vs. 0.91, P < 0.001). Moderate/severe calcification significantly increased AI in both BRS (0.36 vs. 0.32, P = 0.029) and DES (0.34 vs. 0.31, P = 0.025).ConclusionsEccentric distribution of plaque affects symmetry and uniformity of device expansion, and device eccentricity was more affected by the degree of pre-procedural plaque eccentricity in BRS than in DES. Moreover, calcified plaque further exacerbated post-procedural device asymmetry.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-025-05355-7.Keywords: Bioresorbable scaffold, Drug-eluting stent, Intravascular ultrasound, Eccentric plaque, Calcified plaque

Introduction

Bioresorbable scaffolds (BRS) possess biomechanical profiles distinct from permanent metallic drug-eluting stents (DES), primarily characterized by their time-dependent structural degradation. This bioabsorption process ultimately eliminates permanent metallic cage effects, restoring physiological vasomotion and may mitigating late-term flow disturbance artifacts associated with metallic implants [1]. However, several large randomized controlled trials (RCTs) including the ABSORB II and ABSORB Japan studies have demonstrated that BRS exhibits more acute lumen eccentricity and asymmetry than DES [2]– [3]. Such geometric discrepancies in stent deployment have been mechanistically implicated in the pathogenesis of subsequent adverse events, including restenotic processes and thrombotic complications [4]. Furthermore, asymmetry in the acute performance of the device has also been shown to be an independent predictor of the device-oriented composite endpoint (DOCE) after percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) [2, 5].

The eccentric distribution of atherosclerotic plaques along coronary artery walls creates spatial asymmetry that induces morphological alterations in the stent surface, thereby increasing surface irregularity [6]. This geometric disparity not only modifies the stent-vessel wall contact interface but also disrupts pressure distribution patterns, ultimately compromising optimal stent expansion [7]. Moreover, coronary calcification poses significant technical challenges in stent delivery systems, frequently resulting in suboptimal device positioning. The reduced deformability of calcified lesions creates biomechanical resistance that propagates asymmetrical stent expansion during deployment [8]. Nevertheless, comparative analyses of expansion characteristics between BRS and DES under different calcified plaque conditions remain scarce, with particularly limited clinical evidence characterizing device acute expansion performance across varying plaque eccentricity morphologies.

Intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) has been widely used in PCI as an important adjunctive imaging modality to coronary angiography. This technique not only accurately quantifies morphological parameters such as lumen area and lesion length, but also systematically evaluates stent expansion performance using geometric indices such as eccentricity index (SEI), expansion ratio (ER) and asymmetry index (AI) [2]. Compared to optical coherence tomography (OCT), IVUS more accurately characterizes deeper vascular structures due to its superior tissue penetration, which gives it a significant advantage in pre-procedural assessment of plaque eccentricity. In addition, the high sensitivity of IVUS to calcified lesions further enhances its value in coronary intervention [9]– [10].

Therefore, the aim of this study was to compare and analyze the acute device performance of bioresorbable scaffolds and drug-eluting metallic stents after implantation by IVUS, paying particular attention to the effects of the degree of plaque eccentricity and calcification on their expansion characteristics.

Materials and methods

Study design and population

This single-center retrospective cohort study evaluated consecutive patients undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention with either Xinsorb BRS or DES at Zhongshan Hospital, Fudan University (Shanghai, China) between June 2023 and May 2024. Inclusion criteria: (1) evidence of myocardial ischaemia; (2) with pre- and post-procedure IVUS; (3) patients receiving stent-only treatment (either BRS or DES). Exclusion criteria: (1) BRS/DES treated lesions ≧ 2; (2) restenotic lesions; (3) the same lesion containing 2 devices; (4) poor quality images. From an initial screening pool of 260 eligible patients, 184 patients (48 in the BRS-treated group and 136 in the DES-treated group) had pre- and post-procedure IVUS images available for analysis (see Fig. 1 for detailed exclusion cascade).

Study device and procedure

The bioresorbable scaffold (Xinsorb BRS, Shandong Hua’an Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China) used in the study features a fully biodegradable poly-L-lactic acid (PLLA) backbone with 160 μm strut thickness, coated with a drug-eluting layer of racemic poly-D, L-lactic acid (PDLLA). The control group received second-generation drug-eluting metallic alloy stents.

The type of device was selected by the physician and patients in both groups were treated with standard stenting and balloon dilatation. All patients received lifelong low-dose aspirin therapy in addition to clopidogrel, ticagrelor or prasugrel for 12 months after implantation.

Quantitative coronary angiographic analysis

Coronary angiographic images were exported to the AngioPlus Core quantitative flow fraction measurement system (version V2; Pulse Medical Imaging Technology Co., Shanghai, China) for hemodynamic analysis. A key frame demonstrating optimal luminal definition was selected from the angiographic projection with optimal contrast filling, ensuring minimal vessel overlap and foreshortening of the interrogated segments. Following meticulous delineation of the target vessel lumen contour and side branch ostia, with identification of proximal and distal reference segments demonstrating angiographically normal morphology, the following QCA parameters were derived: minimum lumen diameter (MLD), reference lumen diameter (RLD), and maximum diameter stenosis (DS).

Intravascular ultrasound analysis

Intravascular ultrasound imaging was performed using 40 MHz mechanical rotation catheters (Atlantis SR Pro and OptiCross; Boston Scientific, Marlborough, MA) interfaced with the iLab Ultrasound Imaging System (Boston Scientific), utilizing an automatic retraction device at 0.5 mm/s during image acquisition.

Offline IVUS analysis was conducted using the dedicated IvusPlus Core software (version V1; Pulse Medical Imaging Technology Co., Shanghai, China), with automated border detection algorithms. Preprocedural plaque eccentricity was quantified using the plaque eccentricity index (PEI), defined as (maximum wall thickness - minimum wall thickness)/maximum wall thickness [11]. To comprehensively characterize plaque eccentricity throughout the target lesion, serial PEI measurements were obtained at 1-mm intervals (corresponding to 30 image frames), with the mean value calculated as the arithmetic average of all measurements. Simultaneously, frames with extensive calcification compromising accurate media layer delineation were systematically excluded during IVUS analysis to ensure objective quantification of PEI. Post-procedural acute device expansion was evaluated through three quantitative parameters: scaffold/stent asymmetry index (AI), expansion ratio (ER), and eccentricity index (SEI) [2]. AI was defined as 1 - (minimum scaffold/stent diameter/maximum scaffold/stent diameter). The minimum and maximum scaffold/stent diameters correspond to the smallest value of the minimal scaffold/stent diameter and the largest value of the maximal scaffold/stent diameter within the entire stent segment, respectively (Figure 2). ER was defined as (minimum scaffold/stent area/mean reference lumen area)×100%. SEI was defined as minimum scaffold/stent diameter divided by maximum scaffold/stent diameter, the minimum and maximum scaffold/stent diameters represent the orthogonal minimal and maximal diameters within the same cross-sectional plane ༈Figure 2༉. All post-procedural parameters were averaged. These calculated indices are presented in Supplementary Table S1. Additionally, parameters including mean/minimal stent diameter, mean/minimal stent area, stent volume, mean/minimal lumen diameter, and mean/minimal lumen area were automatically generated by the system.

For calcification classification, lesions were categorized into no/mild (Grade 0-I, Calcified Arc < 90°) and moderate/severe (Grade II-IV, Calcified Arc >90°) calcification subgroups according to the modified American College of Cardiology consensus criteria and European Society of Cardiology [12]– [13]. Calcified plaques were defined as regions exhibiting high-intensity white signals with acoustic shadowing on IVUS [14]. Quantitative calcium analysis was performed using a protractor tool centered on the lumen to measure the maximum calcium arc [15], with independent assessments conducted by two blinded interventional cardiologists.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS version 27.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables were assessed for normality using Shapiro-Wilk tests and are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for normally distributed data or median (interquartile range [IQR]) for skewed distributions. Between-group comparisons employed independent Student’s t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests as appropriate. Categorical variables were expressed as percentages, and the chi-squared test was used to compare categorical variables. Multivariate regression analyses were used to predict correlates of stent expansion, and receiver operating characteristics (ROC) curve analysis was then employed to assess model discrimination. A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Comparisons between groups were made using a propensity score matching (PSM) method to control for bias in the baseline information of the two groups. The effect of clinical and procedural characteristics of patients in both groups on outcome was first assessed by logistic regression analysis. The matching process accounted for 23 clinically relevant covariates including: age, sex, history of hypertension, history of diabetes mellitus, history of smoking, dyslipidaemia, previous myocardial infarction, family history of CAD, evidence of myocardial ischaemia, target vessel location, eccentric plaque index, maximum diameter and pressure of the balloon used in the pre-dilatation and post-dilatation procedures, maximum diameter of the deployed scaffold/stent, and total scaffold/stent length. Propensity scores were derived from a non-parsimonious logistic regression model incorporating all baseline covariates. Matching was performed using 1:2 nearest-neighbor methodology with caliper width set at 0.2 SD of the logit propensity score.

Results

Eccentric plaques with device expansion performance

The impact of PEI on device expansion characteristics was systematically evaluated using comprehensive IVUS datasets from all enrolled patients. Receiver operating characteristic curve analysis, based on median values of IVUS-derived parameters (SEI, AI, and ER), was employed to assess PEI’s predictive capacity for device expansion metrics. Specifically, the median distributions of the device expansion parameters were SEI 0.89 (IQR: 0.05), AI 0.34 (IQR: 0.09), and ER of 77% (IQR: 20%) in the BRS group, and the corresponding parameters in the DES group were SEI 0.91 (IQR: 0.04), AI 0.33 (IQR: 0.11), and ER of 78% (IQR: 20%). The results of the study showed that the areas under the ROC curves for SEI, AI and ER were 0.81, 0.77 and 0.66 in the BRS group; and 0.75, 0.71 and 0.53 in the DES group (Fig. 3). Representative IVUS images depicting the impact of plaque eccentricity on BRS implantation are shown in Supplementary Figure S1.

Calcified plaque with device expansion performance

This study demonstrates that calcified plaques exert significant modulation on post-procedural geometric conformation of coronary stents. Quantitative analysis revealed that both BRS and DES exhibited significantly higher AI in lesions with moderate/severe calcification compared to non/mild calcified plaques (BRS median AI: 0.36 vs. 0.32, P = 0.03; DES median AI: 0.34 vs. 0.31, P = 0.03), while SEI and ERs showed no inter-device differences. Detailed data are presented in Supplementary Table S5.

The PSM analysis further demonstrated that in the moderate/severe calcification subgroup, the BRS group exhibited significantly higher stent eccentricity compared to the DES group (SEI: 0.88 vs. 0.91, P = 0.001). Subgroup analyses of non/mild calcification revealed that while BRS maintained more pronounced eccentricity than DES (SEI: 0.89 vs. 0.91, P = 0.04), this discrepancy was less pronounced compared to the moderate/severe calcification subgroup (Supplementary Figure S2). Notably, no significant differences were consistently observed between groups in AI or ER throughout the analyses. Detailed data are presented in Table 5. These findings suggest that vascular calcification significantly influences stent morphology abnormalities, with BRS geometric configurations being more susceptible to mechanical interactions in calcified lesions than DES, resulting in exacerbated eccentricity alterations. The differential biomechanical responses between stent platforms highlight calcification’s critical role in device performance optimization.

Discussion

This study employed IVUS to investigate the impact of plaque eccentricity and calcification on the expansion characteristics of BRS versus DES. The main findings can be summarized as follows: (1) Plaque eccentricity critically determines device expansion symmetry and uniformity; (2) Calcification arc > 90° significantly increases stent asymmetry; and (3) DES shows better acute expansion than BRS in both eccentric and calcified lesions.

Predictive value of PEI for device expansion performance

Post-procedure scaffold/stent expansion ratio, eccentricity index and asymmetry index have been used as metrics to evaluate optimal device expansion, but few studies have focused on the impact of pre-procedural plaque eccentricity index on post-procedural device expansion. Eccentric plaque morphology has been consistently identified as a significant independent predictor of impaired fractional flow reserve (FFR), with accumulating evidence demonstrating that the spatial configuration of atherosclerotic plaques correlates strongly with long-term cardiovascular risk profiles [16]– [17]. Our study provides novel mechanistic evidence demonstrating a direct correlation between pre-procedural PEI and post-implantation device expansion parameters. Plaque eccentricity disrupts laminar flow by displacing blood flow from the vascular axis, thereby exacerbating endothelial shear stress heterogeneity, which induces maladaptive vascular remodeling and creates uneven stress distribution during device deployment—ultimately manifesting as asymmetric expansion and suboptimal strut-vessel wall contact [18]– [19]. Collectively, plaque eccentricity emerges as a critical factors of device symmetry and uniformity. This finding highlights the potential value of PEI integration into multiparameter predictive models; however, its moderate discriminative accuracy precludes its use as an independent decision-making tool. Future studies should investigate combining PEI with other morphological and geometrical plaque characteristics to enhance the prediction of device expansion performance and, ultimately, clinical endpoints.

Notably, our analysis showed that plaque eccentricity had a limited effect on the final device expansion ratio. This finding relates to device-specific biomechanical properties and implantation techniques. While drug-eluting stents (DES) provide better acute expansion in eccentric lesions due to optimized radial strength and conformability, bioresorbable scaffolds (BRS) achieve comparable average expansion ratios—despite some uneven expansion—as their polymer material helps reduce localized stress concentration [20]– [21].

Effect of calcified plaque on device expansion performance

The results demonstrated that calcified plaques significantly impair expansion symmetry in both BRS and DES. Previous reports have consistently shown that calcification causes asymmetric expansion in BRS and DES [22]– [23]. The mechanism lies in the elastic heterogeneity induced by calcium deposits, which stiffen the vessel wall and create uneven stress distribution during stent deployment, leading to asymmetric expansion [24].

Our study focused specifically on patients receiving BRS, unlike Fujino et al. [25] who reported more asymmetric expansion with calcification arc >180°. Since BRS are typically used in milder cases, only 12% of our BRS cohort had calcification arc >180°. Given this small sample, we applied ACC/AHA guidelines using a 90°threshold (none/mild vs. moderate/severe calcification) to minimize bias. This approach confirmed the link between >90°calcification and asymmetric expansion, but the binary classification limited detailed analysis of severe subtypes. Additionally, not using modern IVUS/OCT-based calcium scoring systems [26]– [27] may restrict deeper insight into calcification’s biomechanical effects—a limitation worth noting when interpreting our results.

SEI of BRS vs. DES in eccentric and calcified lesions

While current evidence consistently associates BRS with elevated scaffold eccentricity, our stratified analysis demonstrated PEI-dependent differential outcomes: BRS manifested comparable eccentricity profiles to DES at lower PEI, with divergence emerging exclusively in high-PEI subgroups. The threshold effect reflects both the anatomical complexity of high-PEI lesions (e.g., tortuosity, angulation) and the compositional heterogeneity of plaques, which synergistically challenge BRS conformability and promote asymmetric expansion [3]. Although post-dilation improves initial apposition, BRS-specific limitations—including elastic recoil and degradation-phase integrity loss—restrict sustained symmetrical lumen expansion in such lesions [20].

Notably, although BRS exhibit higher eccentricity compared to DES, the magnitude of this eccentricity becomes more pronounced in patients with more extensive calcified plaques. Current evidence delineates divergent calcium-device interactions: BRS expansion is significantly influenced by plaque composition and morphology, with lower device eccentricity indices significantly correlating with greater calcified plaque area, angle, extent and shallower plaque depth, whereas DES eccentricity remains independent of calcification [28]– [29]. This differential calcium susceptibility between scaffold platforms aligns with our findings, confirming BRS’s heightened biomechanical vulnerability in calcified environments.

Clinically, BRS are seldom used in severely calcified lesions owing to concerns about suboptimal expansion. Their selective application in mild, focal calcifications introduces a inherent selection bias. Therefore, the poor expansion symmetry observed with BRS in calcified plaques likely stems from both intrinsic biomechanical limitations and this clinical practice pattern, where BRS are rarely implanted in complex lesions typically treated with DES. Consequently, our findings regarding BRS in severe calcification—based on limited cases—should be interpreted as exploratory and require validation in prospective studies specifically enrolling complex calcified lesions. Moreover, procedural heterogeneity—specifically, the routine use of higher balloon pressures and more intensive lesion preparation in the BRS group—may have influenced the acute expansion outcomes. Although this reflects contemporary implant technique, it precludes the definitive attribution of the observed differences solely to the device platform.

Clinical implications and future research directions

Based on the findings of this study, we recommend that in clinical practice, new-generation DES should be prioritized in lesions with severe eccentricity or significant calcification to ensure reliable expansion. If a BRS is considered, its use should be strictly restricted to anatomically simple lesions and must be coupled with aggressive lesion preparation and high-pressure post-dilation, ideally under the guidance of intravascular imaging, to optimize implantation outcomes. Future individualized device selection will require predictive models that integrate multidimensional plaque and vessel characteristics.

The acute-phase geometric patterns observed in this study, particularly the asymmetric expansion results of the BRS in eccentric and calcified lesions, suggest potential adverse effects on long-term clinical outcomes. While previous studies found no persistent link between acute malapposition and long-term outcomes in general cohorts [5, 30], our study specifically investigates complex lesions (eccentric/calcified) where the hemodynamic and biomechanical impact of asymmetry is most pronounced. To address this, we have established a systematic three-year clinical follow-up registry. This registry aims to explicitly report the correlation between these endpoint events and morphological parameters across a broader spectrum of lesion morphologies. Furthermore, the mechanisms underlying the association between early and late events, as suggested by previous registry studies [5, 31], warrant further in-depth exploration through dedicated mechanistic investigations.

Limitations

First, the generalizability of our findings may be limited by the single-center design and homogeneous East Asian cohort, whose distinct coronary anatomy (e.g., smaller vessel size) could have specifically influenced the device performance. Therefore, caution is warranted when extrapolating these results to other populations, and broader validation is needed. Second, although propensity score matching balanced the measured covariates, residual confounding inherent to the retrospective design may persist. Third, the broader implications of this study are limited by its exclusive evaluation of the Xinsorb BRS, precluding the extrapolation of findings to other bioresorbable scaffold platforms. Fourth, as a purely acute-phase assessment, the lack of follow-up data herein prevents direct correlation of malapposition with late-term clinical outcomes, a connection that will be addressed by our ongoing registry. Finally, the binary classification of calcification adopted in this study simplifies a complex spectrum. Future studies should employ more sophisticated modern IVUS- and OCT-based calcium scoring systems to more accurately characterize the impact of calcification on tissue structure.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates a direct correlation between preprocedural plaque eccentricity severity and postprocedural device expansion characteristics, as quantified by intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) in patients with coronary artery disease. Although eccentric plaques exert limited influence on overall device expansion ratios, they induce significantly greater asymmetric expansion and morphometric eccentricity of the stent architecture. Such abnormal device morphometry is more pronounced in BRS compared to DES. Furthermore, the presence of intraplaque calcific components potentiates post-interventional asymmetric expansion heterogeneity.

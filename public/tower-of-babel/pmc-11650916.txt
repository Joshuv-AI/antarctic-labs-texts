Introduction: Viruses and Autoimmunity
Authors: RuthMabel Boytz, Evan Navori
Published: 2024

Our immune system is a powerful and exquisitely complex apparatus that protects us from external and internal threats. Its remarkable ability to recognize and destroy almost any conceivable target also means that it must be tightly regulated to avoid targeting our own cells and tissues. When these brakes are removed or altered, the immune system can “go rogue,” leading to autoimmune diseases. The drivers of autoimmunity can include genetic predisposition, environmental factors, and infections by microbes. In the December 2024 issue of the Yale Journal of Biology and Medicine, we present eleven manuscripts that focus on the role of viral infections in autoimmunity.

The COVID-19 pandemic has provided an unprecedented opportunity to study the mechanisms, pathogenesis, and treatment of autoimmune disorders following viral infection. In most cases, SARS-CoV-2, the causative agent of COVID-19, results in acute disease. However, it is increasingly understood that infection can lead to long-term immune dysfunction and dysregulation in some patients. Nyarko et al. provide a review of the pathogenesis of what has been termed post-acute sequelae of SARS-CoV-2 (PASC), or “Long COVID,” for clinicians. A review by Zollner and colleagues focuses on the role that the intestine plays in PASC and highlights the difficulties in establishing a causal link between SARS-CoV-2 infection and immune dysregulation, microvascular damage, and nervous system dysfunction. Fatigue is perhaps the most common symptom associated with PASC, leading Mathew and Nugent to review the pathological outcomes of SARS-CoV-2 infection that may cause reduced exercise tolerance and discuss approaches for rehabilitation.

Establishing a causal effect between a specific viral infection and the subsequent onset of an autoimmune disease is one of the greatest challenges in this field. Because the mechanism by which a virus induces autoimmunity is not understood, it is also difficult to predict which patients are at risk of developing autoimmunity. Two papers in this issue approach this question in relation to the chikungunya virus and the development of arthritic disease. Lozano-Parra et al. present a systematic review of predictive biomarkers for developing arthralgia in patients with chikungunya. An original contribution by Amaral and colleagues demonstrates a correlation between disease severity and joint pain in a cohort of chikungunya arthritis patients. Similarly, Ying’s mini-review investigates the mechanisms underlying viral myocarditis, a condition that exemplifies the immune system’s response to viral infections. Adding complexity to the field of virus-induced autoimmunity is the potential role of endogenous retroviruses, which do not cause a distinct acute infection but already exist in our genomes. Viret and Bynoe discuss the current understanding and need for further investigation of human endogenous retrovirus expression and autoimmunity.

Viruses are continuously emerging into humans from zoonotic reservoirs, a trend that is expected to accelerate with increasing globalization, climate change, and environmental degradation. These emergent, understudied viruses also have the potential to cause autoimmunity. We present two papers, one on Monkeypox (Mpox) from Krishna et al. and one on Alkhumra hemorrhagic fever virus (AHFV) from Srivastava and colleagues, that touch on two emerging viruses.

As the mechanisms and causal relationships of virus-induced autoimmunity are poorly understood, treatment options are severely limited. Nguyen et al. contribute a case report on the use of tocilizumab to successfully treat cystoid macular edema secondary to immune recovery uveitis in a patient with a history of cytomegalovirus retinitis. Finally, Banou et al. review how virus-based genetic therapies for other conditions, specifically ocular diseases, can cause unwanted autoimmune immune responses.

These and other articles are presented in this December 2024 issue of YJBM on Viruses and Autoimmunity. We believe the breadth of works covering PASC, biomarkers, endogenous retroviruses, emerging viruses, and treatments will be thought-provoking for our readers and contribute to the advancement of this field.

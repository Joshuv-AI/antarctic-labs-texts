Long-Time Analysis of Stochastic Heavy Ball Dynamics for Convex Optimization and Monotone Equations
Radu Ioan Bo t ,, Chiara Schindler

Abstract
In a separable real Hilbert space, we study the problem of minimizing a convex function with Lipschitz continuous gradient in the presence of noisy evaluations. To this end, we associate a stochastic Heavy Ball system, incorporating a friction coefficient, with the optimization problem. We establish existence and uniqueness of trajectory solutions for this system. Under a square integrability condition for the diffusion term, we prove almost sure convergence of the trajectory process to an optimal solution, as well as almost sure convergence of its time derivative to zero. Moreover, we derive almost sure and expected convergence rates for the function values along the trajectory towards the infimal value. Finally, we show that the stochastic Heavy Ball system is equivalent to a Su-Boyd-Candès-type system for a suitable choice of the parameter function, and we provide corresponding convergence rate results for the latter. In the second part of this paper, we extend our analysis beyond the optimization framework and investigate a monotone equation induced by a monotone and Lipschitz continuous operator, whose evaluations are assumed to be corrupted by noise. As before, we consider a stochastic Heavy Ball system with a friction coefficient and a correction term, now augmented by an additional component that accounts for the time derivative of the operator. We establish analogous convergence results for both the trajectory process and its time derivative, and derive almost sure as well as expected convergence rates for the decay of the residual and the gap function along the trajectory. As a final result, we show that a particular instance of the stochastic Heavy Ball system for monotone equations is equivalent to a stochastic second-order dynamical system with a vanishing damping term. Remarkably, this system exhibits fast convergence rates for both the residual and gap functions.



Key Words.
stochastic Heavy Ball method, convex optimization, monotone equation, convergence of trajectories, convergence rates, time scaling

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{E}_{\eta}(t_{0},Y(t_{0}),X(t_{0}))-\underbrace{\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(\lambda-\eta)\|X(s)\|^{2}-u(s)ds}_{=:U(t)}

\displaystyle\ +\underbrace{\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|\sigma_{Y}(s)\|^{2}_{HS}ds}_{=:A(t)}+\underbrace{\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\langle\eta(Y(s)-y^{*})+X(s),\sigma_{Y}(s)dW(s)\rangle}_{=:N(t)},

where
A(\cdot)
and
U(\cdot)
are increasing processes fulfilling
U(t_{0})=0=A(t_{0})
a.s. According to Proposition
A.5
,
N(\cdot)
is a martingale, since
(Y,X)\in S^{2}_{\cal H\times\cal H}
and for every
t\geq t_{0}

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|\sigma_{Y}^{*}(s)(\eta(Y(s)-y^{*})+X(s))\|^{2}ds\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}3\|\sigma_{Y}(s)\|^{2}\left(\eta^{2}(\|Y(s)\|^{2}+\|y^{*}\|^{2})+\|X(s)\|^{2}\right)ds\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ 3\mathbb{E}\left(\left(\eta^{2}\sup_{s\in[t_{0},t]}\|Y(s)\|^{2}+\sup_{s\in[t_{0},t]}\|X(s)\|^{2}+\eta^{2}\|y^{*}\|^{2}\right)\right)\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|\sigma_{Y}(s)\|^{2}ds<+\infty.

From the square-integrability of
\sigma_{Y}
, we obtain the existence of
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}A(t)<+\infty
. Therefore, by Theorem
A.6
, the limits
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(t,Y(t),X(t))
and
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}U(t)
exist and are finite a.s.

Taking the expectation in (
2.1
), we obtain, for every
t\geq t_{0}
, that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(t,Y(t),X(t))\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(t_{0},Y(t_{0}),X(t_{0})))+\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|\sigma_{Y}(s)\|^{2}_{HS}ds\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(t_{0},Y(t_{0}),X(t_{0}))+\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}\|\sigma_{Y}(s)\|^{2}_{HS}ds\right)<+\infty,

which means, in particular, that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq t_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left(\|Y(t)-y^{*}\|^{2}\right)<+\infty,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq t_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left(b(t)\left(f(Y(t))-\inf\nolimits_{\mathcal{H}}f\right)\right)<+\infty,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sup_{t\geq t_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left(\|\eta(Y(t)-y^{*})+X(t)\|^{2}\right)<+\infty.

Since, for every
t\geq t_{0}
,

\displaystyle\|X(t)\|^{2}\leq 2\|\eta(Y(t)-y^{*})+X(t)\|^{2}+2\eta^{2}\|Y(t)-y^{*}\|^{2},

we have that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq t_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left(\|X(t)\|^{2}\right)<+\infty.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq t_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left(b(t)\left(f(Y(t))-\inf\nolimits_{\mathcal{H}}f\right)+\|X(t)\|^{2}\right)<+\infty,

in other words,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(b(t)\left(f(Y(t))-\inf\nolimits_{\mathcal{H}}f\right)+\|X(t)\|^{2}\Big)=\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)\ \mbox{as}\ t\to+\infty,

thus proving
(ii)
.

Further, it follows from

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}U(t)<+\infty\ \mbox{a.s.}

that

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}\|X(t)\|^{2}dt<+\infty\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}(-u(t))dt<+\infty\ \mbox{a.s.}

From here, since

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}b(t)\left(\eta-\sup_{t\geq t_{0}}\frac{\dot{b}(t)}{b(t)}\right)(f(Y(t))-\inf\nolimits_{\mathcal{H}}f)dt\leq

\displaystyle\ \int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}(\eta b(t)-\dot{b}(t))(f(Y(t))-\inf\nolimits_{\mathcal{H}}f)dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}(-u(t))<+\infty\ \mbox{a.s.},

it follows

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}b(t)(f(Y(t))-\inf\nolimits_{\mathcal{H}}f)dt<+\infty\ \mbox{a.s.}

Now we choose
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\left(\sup_{t\geq t_{0}}\frac{\dot{b}(t)}{b(t)},\lambda\right),\eta_{1}\neq\eta_{2}
. For every
t\geq t_{0}
it holds

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\eta_{1}}(t,Y(t),X(t))-\mathcal{E}_{\eta_{2}}(t,Y(t),X(t))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lambda(\eta_{1}-\eta_{2})\|Y(t)-y^{*}\|^{2}+(\eta_{1}-\eta_{2})\langle Y(t)-y^{*},X(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=(\eta_{1}-\eta_{2})\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}\|Y(t)-y^{*}\|^{2}+\langle Y(t)-y^{*},X(t)\rangle\right).

Denoting

\displaystyle p(t):=\frac{\lambda}{2}\|Y(t)-y^{*}\|^{2}+\langle Y(t)-y^{*},X(t)\rangle,

it follows from the existence of
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{E}_{\eta_{1}}(t,Y(t),X(t))-\mathcal{E}_{\eta_{2}}(t,Y(t),X(t))
a.s. that also
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}p(t)<+\infty
must exist a.s. Observe that for every
t\geq t_{0}

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(t,Y(t),X(t))=

\displaystyle\ b(t)(f(Y(t))-\inf\nolimits_{\mathcal{H}}f)+\frac{1}{2}\|\eta(Y(t)-y^{*})+X(t)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\eta(\lambda-\eta)\|Y(t)-y^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ b(t)(f(Y(t))-\inf\nolimits_{\mathcal{H}}f)+\frac{1}{2}\|X(t)\|^{2}+\eta p(t).

Denoting

\displaystyle h(t):=b(t)(f(Y(t))-\inf\nolimits_{\mathcal{H}}f)+\frac{1}{2}\|X(t)\|^{2},

it yields that the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}h(t)<+\infty\ \mbox{exists a.s.}

Since

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}h(t)dt=\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}b(t)(f(Y(t))-\inf\nolimits_{\mathcal{H}}f)dt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}\|X(t)\|^{2}dt<+\infty,

we can conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}h(t)=0\ \mbox{a.s.}

Thus,

\displaystyle f(Y(t))-\inf\nolimits_{\mathcal{H}}f=o\left(\tfrac{1}{b(t)}\right)\ \mbox{and}\ \lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|X(t)\|=0\ \mbox{a.s.},

which proves
(i)
.

Let us proceed to the proof of statement
(iii)
. For
y^{*}\in\argmin f
fixed, we have

\displaystyle d(\|Y(t)-y^{*}\|^{2})=2\langle Y(t)-y^{*},X(t)\rangle dt.

Applying Lemma
A.1
, we deduce that the existence of
\lim_{t\to+\infty}p(t)<+\infty
implies that
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|Y(t)-y^{*}\|^{2}<+\infty
also exists. Hence, there exist subsets
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{y*}\subseteq\Omega
of full measure such that

\displaystyle f(Y(\omega,t))-\inf\nolimits_{\mathcal{H}}f=o\left(\tfrac{1}{b(t)}\right)\ \mbox{}\forall\omega\in\Omega_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|Y(\omega,t)-y^{*}\|^{2}<+\infty\ \mbox{}\forall\omega\in\Omega_{y^{*}}.

Before concluding our argument, we must establish the existence of a set of full measure, independent of
y^{*}\argmin f
, that can take the place of
\Omega_{y^{*}}
in the second property. This can be attained using an argument from
[
15
,
10
]
, which relies on the separability assumption on
\mathcal{H}
. Since
\argmin f
is closed, the separability of
\mathcal{H}
ensures the existence of a countable set
S\subseteq\mathcal{H}
that is dense in
\argmin f
. By the countability of
S
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{s\in S}\Omega_{s}\right)=1-\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{s\in S}\Omega_{s}^{c}\right)\geq 1-\sum_{s\in S}\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{s}^{c})=1.

Then,
\mathbb{P}(\bigcap_{s\in S}\Omega_{s}\cap\Omega_{1})=1
.

Now let
\omega\in\bigcap_{s\in S}\Omega_{s}\cap\Omega_{1}
be fixed and take
y^{*}\in\argmin f
. Then, there exists a sequence
(s^{k})_{k\geq 0}\subseteq S
such that
s^{k}\rightarrow y^{*}
as
k\rightarrow+\infty
. For every
k\geq 0
, since
\omega\in\Omega_{s^{k}}
, it holds

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|Y(\omega,t)-s^{k}\|<+\infty\ \mbox{exists.}

Using the triangle inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big|\|Y(\omega,t)-s^{k}\|-\|Y(\omega,t)-y^{*}\|\Big|\leq\|s^{k}-y^{*}\|\quad\forall k\geq 0\ \forall t\geq 0.

Hence, for every
k\geq 0
, it holds

\displaystyle-\|s^{k}-y^{*}\|+\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|Y(\omega,t)-s^{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|Y(\omega,t)-y^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|Y(\omega,t)-y^{*}\|\leq\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|Y(\omega,t)-s^{k}\|+\|s^{k}-y^{*}\|.

Letting
k\rightarrow+\infty
, it follows

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|Y(\omega,t)-y^{*}\|=\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|Y(\omega,t)-s^{k}\|<+\infty.

Recall that there exists a set
\Omega_{\text{cont}}\subseteq\Omega
of full measure such that
Y(\omega,\cdot)
is continuous for every
\omega\in\Omega_{\text{cont}}
. Define
\Omega_{\text{converge}}:=\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{\text{cont}}\cap\left(\bigcap_{s\in S}\Omega_{s}\right)
, which is also a subset of full measure of
\Omega
.

We will use Opial’s Lemma (
[
19
]
) to prove the weak almost sure convergence of the trajectory process to a minimizer of
f
. To this end, we fix
\omega\in\Omega_{\text{converge}}
. From the above, for every
y^{*}\in\argmin f
, the limit
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|Y(\omega,t)-y^{*}\|<+\infty
exists. This verifies the first condition of Opial’s Lemma. Using the continuity of
Y(\omega,\cdot)
, we can also show that
Y(\omega,\cdot)
is bounded. If
\bar{y}(\omega)
is a weak limit point of this trajectory process, then using (i) and the weak lower semicontinuity of
f
, we get that
f(\bar{y}(\omega))=\inf_{\mathcal{H}}f
. Therefore,
\bar{y}(\omega)\in\argmin f
. This shows that the second condition of Opial’s Lemma is also satisfied. In conclusion,
Y(\omega,t)
converges weakly to a minimizer of
f
as
t\to+\infty
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Note that in order to use Itô’s formula in the above proof, we do need to require that, for every
t\geq t_{0}
, the mapping
(y,x)\mapsto\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(y,x)
is twice differentiable in
y
. This is because the correspoding components in
\nabla_{(y,x)}\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(t,y,x)
and
\nabla_{(y,x)}^{2}\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(t,y,x)
are multiplied by
0
in the integral representation of
\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(t,Y(t),X(t))
– in the proof of Itô’s formula, the assumptions are only used for precisely the components that are not multiplied by
0
, so it is sufficient to have the requirements verified only on them.

Depending on the choice for the parameter function
b
, these convergence rates may still leave room for improvement–an issue explored in the theorem below and in the remark immediately following the proof.

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume
\sigma_{Y}
is square integrable and
\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{b(s)}\left(\int_{\frac{s+t_{0}}{2}}^{s}b(r)dr\right)\|\sigma_{Y}(s)\|_{HS}^{2}ds<+\infty
. If
(Y,X)\in S^{2}_{\cal H\times\cal H}
is a strong solution of (
SHBF
), then

\displaystyle f(Y(t))-\inf\nolimits_{\mathcal{H}}f=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\int_{\frac{t+t_{0}}{2}}^{t}b(s)ds}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|X(t)\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{b(t)}{\int_{\frac{t+t_{0}}{2}}^{t}b(s)ds}}\right)\ \mbox{as}\ t\rightarrow+\infty\ \mbox{a.s.}

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle\Phi(t,y,x):=(f(y)-\inf\nolimits_{\mathcal{H}}f)+\frac{1}{2b(t)}\|x\|^{2},

and set

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}(t,y,x):=g(t)\Phi(t,y,x),

where

\displaystyle g(t):=\int_{\frac{t+t_{0}}{2}}^{t}b(s)ds.

Observe that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\widetilde{\Phi}(t,y,x)=-\frac{\dot{b}(t)}{2b(t)}\|x\|^{2}g(t)+\left(b(t)-b\left(\frac{t+t_{0}}{2}\right)\right)\Phi(t,x,y)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dy}\widetilde{\Phi}(t,y,x)=g(t)\nabla f(y)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\widetilde{\Phi}(t,y,x)=\frac{g(t)}{b(t)}x

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\widetilde{\Phi}(t,y,x)=\frac{g(t)}{b(t)}\Id.

By Itô’s formula,

\displaystyle\ d\widetilde{\Phi}(t,Y(t),X(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ g(t)\left(-\frac{\dot{b}(t)}{2b(t)^{2}}\|X(t)\|^{2}+\langle\nabla f(Y(t)),X(t)\rangle+\frac{1}{b(t)}\langle X(t),-\lambda X(t)-b(t)\nabla f(Y(t))\rangle\right)dt

\displaystyle+\left(b(t)-b\left(\frac{t+t_{0}}{2}\right)\right)\Phi(t,X(t),Y(t))dt+\left\langle\frac{g(t)}{b(t)}X(t),\sigma_{Y}(t)dW(t)\right\rangle+\frac{g(t)}{2b(t)}\trace\left(\sigma_{Y}(t)\sigma_{Y}(t)^{*}\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{g(t)}{b(t)}\left(\frac{\dot{b}(t)}{2b(t)}+\lambda\right)\|X(t)\|^{2}dt-b\left(\frac{t+t_{0}}{2}\right)\Phi(t,X(t),Y(t))dt+b(t)\Phi(t,X(t),Y(t))dt

\displaystyle\ +\frac{g(t)}{b(t)}\left\langle\sigma_{Y}(t)^{*}X(t),dW(t)\right\rangle+\frac{g(t)}{2b(t)}\trace\left(\sigma_{Y}(t)\sigma_{Y}(t)^{*}\right)dt\quad\forall t\geq t_{0},

therefore,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}(t,Y(t),X(t))=

\displaystyle\ \widetilde{\Phi}(0,Y(0),X(0))

\displaystyle\ -\underbrace{\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{g(s)}{b(s)}\left(\frac{\dot{b}(s)}{2b(s)}+\lambda\right)\|X(s)\|^{2}+b\left(\frac{s+t_{0}}{2}\right)\Phi(s,X(s),Y(s))ds}_{=:U(t)}

\displaystyle\ +\underbrace{\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{g(s)}{2b(s)}\trace(\sigma_{Y}(s)\sigma_{Y}(s)^{*})+b(s)\Phi(s,X(s),Y(s))ds}_{=:A(t)}

\displaystyle\ +\underbrace{\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{g(s)}{b(s)}\left\langle\sigma_{Y}(s)^{*}X(s),dW(s)\right\rangle}_{=:N(t)}\quad\forall t\geq t_{0}.

A(\cdot)
and
U(\cdot)
are increasing processes fulfilling
U(t_{0})=0=A(t_{0})
a.s. Since
(Y,X)\in S^{2}_{\cal H\times\cal H}
and for every
t\geq t_{0}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\left\|\frac{g(s)}{b(s)}\sigma_{Y}(s)^{*}X(s)\right\|^{2}ds\right)\leq

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\left(\frac{g(s)}{b(s)}\right)^{2}\|\sigma_{Y}(s)\|^{2}\|X(s)\|^{2}ds\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \sup_{t_{0}\leq s\leq t}\left(\frac{g(s)}{b(s)}\right)^{2}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t_{0}\leq s\leq t}\|X(s)\|^{2}\right)\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|\sigma_{Y}(s)\|_{HS}^{2}ds<+\infty,

taking into account Proposition
A.5
,
N(\cdot)
is a martingale. We have seen in the proof of Theorem
2.2
that

\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}b(t)(f(Y(t))-\inf_{\mathcal{H}})dt<+\infty
and

\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}\|X(t)\|^{2}dt<+\infty
, hence
\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}b(t)\Phi(t,Y(t),X(t))dt<+\infty
. This, together with the second integrability assumption in the hypothesis, guarantees the existence of
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}A(t)<+\infty
. Thus, from Theorem
A.6
we obtain that the limit
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\widetilde{\Phi}(t,X(t),Y(t))
exists and is finite a.s. This leads to the desired convergence rates.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

Depending on
b
and under stronger integrability conditions on the diffusion term, the convergence rate results in Theorem
2.4
may offer an improvement over those established in Theorem
2.2
.

(a) For
r>1
, let

\displaystyle b(t):=t^{r}\quad\forall t\geq t_{0}.

For every
s\geq t_{0}
it holds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{b(s)}\int_{\frac{s+t_{0}}{2}}^{s}b(u)du\leq\frac{s}{r+1},

thus, assuming that

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}s\|\sigma_{Y}(s)\|_{HS}^{2}ds<+\infty,

both integrability conditions in Theorem
2.4
are satisfied. Under this assumption, the convergence rates improve to

\displaystyle f(Y(t))-\inf\nolimits_{\mathcal{H}}f=\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{1}{t^{r+1}}\right)\ \mbox{and}\ \|X(t)\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\ \mbox{as}\ t\to+\infty\ \mbox{a.s.}

(b) As a second example, we consider for
r>1
and
t_{0}>0

\displaystyle b(t):=t^{r}\log(t)\quad\forall t\geq t_{0}.

For every
s\geq t_{0}
, it holds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{b(s)}\int_{\frac{s+t_{0}}{2}}^{s}b(u)du=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{s^{r}\log(s)}\int_{\frac{s+t_{0}}{2}}^{s}u^{r}\log(u)du=\frac{1}{s^{r}\log(s)}\left(\frac{u^{r+1}}{r+1}\log(u)\bigg|_{\frac{s+t_{0}}{2}}^{s}-\int_{\frac{s+t_{0}}{2}}^{s}\frac{u^{r}}{r+1}du\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{s^{r}\log(s)}\left(\frac{s^{r+1}}{r+1}\log(s)-\frac{(s+t_{0})^{r+1}}{2^{r+1}(r+1)}\log\left(\frac{s+t_{0}}{2}\right)-\frac{s^{r+1}}{(r+1)^{2}}+\frac{(s+t_{0})^{r+1}}{2^{r+1}(r+1)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ s\ \mbox{for}\ s\ \mbox{large enough.}

This shows that if

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}s\|\sigma_{Y}(s)\|_{HS}^{2}ds<+\infty,

then both integrability conditions in Theorem
2.4
are satisfied. Under this assumption, the convergence rates improve to

\displaystyle f(Y(t))-\inf\nolimits_{\mathcal{H}}f=\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{1}{t^{r+1}\log(t)}\right)\ \mbox{and}\ \|X(t)\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\ \mbox{as}\ t\to+\infty\ \mbox{a.s.}

(c) In contrast, if
b(t)=\exp(t)
, no improvement in the convergence rates can be achieved via Theorem
2.4
. Namely,

\displaystyle f(Y(t))-\inf\nolimits_{\mathcal{H}}f=\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{1}{\exp(t)}\right)\ \mbox{and}\ \|X(t)\|=\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)\ \mbox{as}\ t\to+\infty\ \mbox{a.s.}

This is not surprising, since for every
s\geq t_{0}
we have

\frac{1}{b(s)}\int_{\frac{s+t_{0}}{2}}^{s}b(u)du=1-\exp\left(\frac{t_{0}-s}{2}\right),

and therefore the second integrability assumption does not strengthen the required square integrability of the diffusion term.

2.2
Application to the stochastic Su-Boyd-Candès system with vanishing damping

In the following, we show – via time-scaling arguments – that a particular formulation of the stochastic Heavy Ball system (
SHBF
) is closely related to the stochastic Su–Boyd–Candès system with vanishing damping (
SAVD
). This connection allows us to derive a long-time statements for the latter, not through a technically involved energy-dissipativity argument, but rather by transferring the results of Theorem
2.2
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\alpha>1
. For given
t_{0}\geq 0
and
s_{0}>0
, consider the dynamical systems

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dY(t)=X(t)dt\\
dX(t)=\left(-X(t)-\left(\frac{s_{0}}{\alpha-1}\right)^{2}\exp\left(\frac{2(t-t_{0})}{\alpha-1}\right)\nabla f(Y(t))\right)dt+\sigma_{Y}(t)dW(t)\\
Y(t_{0})=Y_{0},\ X(t_{0})=X_{0}\end{cases}

(SHBFEXP)

on
[t_{0},+\infty)
, and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dZ(s)=Q(s)ds\\
dQ(s)=\left(-\frac{\alpha}{s}Q(s)-\nabla f(Z(s))\right)ds+\sigma_{Z}(s)dW(s)\\
Z(s_{0})=Z_{0},\ Q(s_{0})=Q_{0}\ \end{cases}

(SAVD)

on
[s_{0},+\infty)
. Then, the following statements are true:

(i)

Let
(Y,X)
be a strong solution of (
SHBFEXP
). Then, for

\displaystyle\tau(s):=t_{0}+(\alpha-1)\log\left(\frac{s}{s_{0}}\right)\quad\forall s\geq s_{0},

it holds that
(Z,Q)
, defined by

\displaystyle Z(s):=Y(\tau(s))\quad\mbox{and}\quad Q(s):=\frac{\alpha-1}{s}X(\tau(s)),

is a strong solution of (
SAVD
) with initial conditions
Z(s_{0})=Z_{0}=Y_{0}
and
Q(s_{0})=Q_{0}=\frac{\alpha-1}{s_{0}}X_{0}
, and diffusion term

\displaystyle\sigma_{Z}(s)=\left(\dot{\tau}(s)\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\sigma_{Y}(\tau(s))=\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{s}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\sigma_{Y}\left(\tau(s)\right).

(ii)

Let
(Z,Q)
be a strong solution of (
SAVD
). Then, for

\displaystyle\kappa(t):=s_{0}\exp\left(\frac{t-t_{0}}{\alpha-1}\right)\quad\forall t\geq t_{0},

it holds that
(Y,X)
, defined by

\displaystyle Y(t)=Z(\kappa(t))\quad\mbox{and}\quad X(t)=\frac{s_{0}}{\alpha-1}\exp\left(\frac{t-t_{0}}{\alpha-1}\right)Q(\kappa(t))

is a strong solution of (
SHBFEXP
) with initial conditions
Y(t_{0})Y_{0}=Z_{0}
and
X(t_{0})=X_{0}=\frac{s_{0}}{\alpha-1}Q_{0}
, and diffusion term

\displaystyle\sigma_{Y}(t)=\left(\dot{\kappa}(t)\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\sigma_{Z}(\kappa(t))=\left(\frac{s_{0}}{\alpha-1}\exp\left(\frac{t-t_{0}}{\alpha-1}\right)\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\sigma_{Z}(\kappa(t)).

Proof.

(i) Let
(Y,X)
be a strong solution of (
SHBFEXP
). We denote

\displaystyle b(t):=\left(\frac{s_{0}}{\alpha-1}\right)^{2}\exp\left(\frac{2(t-t_{0})}{\alpha-1}\right).

For
Z(s):=Y(\tau(s))
and
Q(s):=\frac{\alpha-1}{s}X(\tau(s))=\dot{\tau}(s)X(\tau(s))
, it yields, for every
s\geq s_{0}
, that

\displaystyle dZ(s)=dY(\tau(s))\dot{\tau}(s)ds=X(\tau(s))\dot{\tau}(s)ds=Q(s)ds,

and

\displaystyle dQ(s)

\displaystyle=\ddot{\tau}(s)X(\tau(s))ds+\dot{\tau}(s)d(X(\tau(s)))

\displaystyle=\ddot{\tau}(s)X(\tau(s))ds+\dot{\tau}(s)^{2}\big(-X(\tau(s))-b(\tau(s))\nabla f(Y(\tau(s)))\big)ds+\dot{\tau}(s)\sigma_{Y}(\tau(s))dW(\tau(s))

\displaystyle=\ddot{\tau}(s)X(\tau(s))+\dot{\tau}(s)^{2}\big(-X(\tau(s))-b(\tau(s))\nabla f(Y(\tau(s)))\big)ds+\left(\dot{\tau}(s)\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\sigma_{Y}(\tau(s))dW(s)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\ddot{\tau}(s)}{\dot{\tau}(s)}Q(s)-\dot{\tau}(s)Q(s)-b(\tau(s))\dot{\tau}(s)^{2}\nabla f(Z(s))\right)ds+\left(\dot{\tau}(s)\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\sigma_{Y}(\tau(s))dW(s),

where we used
[
18

\displaystyle\ \ddot{\tau}(s)X(\tau(s))ds+(\dot{\tau}(s))^{2}(-\lambda X(\tau(s))-\mu(\tau(s))H(\tau(s))-\mu(\tau(s))V(Y(\tau(s))))ds

\displaystyle\ \ddot{\kappa}(t)Q(\kappa(t))+\dot{\kappa}(t)d(Q(\kappa(t)))

\displaystyle\ \ddot{\kappa}(t)Q(\kappa(t))+(\dot{\kappa}(t))^{2}\left(-\frac{\alpha}{\kappa(t)}Q(\kappa(t))-\beta K(\kappa(t))-\frac{\alpha}{2\kappa(t)}\beta V(Z(\kappa(t)))\right)dt+(\dot{\kappa}(t))^{\frac{3}{2}}\sigma_{Z}(\kappa(t))dW(t)

Remark 3.6
.

The deterministic counterpart of (
SFOGDA
) is the dynamical system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\ddot{Z}(s)+\frac{\alpha}{s}\dot{Z}(s)+\beta\frac{d}{ds}V(Z(s))+\frac{\alpha\beta}{2s}V(Z(s))=0\\
Z(t_{0})=Z_{0}\ \mbox{and}\ \dot{Z}(t_{0})=Q_{0}\end{cases}

which was introduced and studied in
[

]
(in a slightly more general form) as the Fast Optimistic Gradient Descent Ascent (OGDA) dynamical system, in connection with solving monotone equations. The Fast OGDA dynamics achieve the best known convergence rates for monotone equations in the literature and reveal the surprising phenomenon that inertial dynamics with vanishing damping can have an accelerating effect beyond the setting of convex minimization. The same phenomenon has also been observed in
[
9
]
for the explicit discretization of Fast OGDA, which yields an algorithm combining Nesterov momentum with a correction-term evaluation of the operator.

The following lemma plays a crucial in our long-time analysis.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\lambda>0,t_{0}\geq 0
and
\phi:[t_{0},+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
an Itô process with
\phi(t)=\phi(t_{0})+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\psi(s)ds
almost surely such that
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}(\lambda\phi(t)+\psi(t))=\ell\in\mathbb{R}
almost surely. Then,
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\phi(t)=\frac{\ell}{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}
almost surely.

Proof.

Let
\Omega_{1}
be the subset of
\Omega
of measure
1
such that for all
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
it holds
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda\phi(\omega,t)+\psi(\omega,t)=\ell(\omega)
as well as
\phi(\omega,t)=\phi(\omega,t_{0})+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\psi(\omega,s)ds
for all
t\geq t_{0}
. We fix
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
and set
\zeta(\omega,t):=\phi(\omega,t)-\frac{\ell(\omega)}{\lambda}
for all
t\geq t_{0}
. Hence,
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}(\lambda\zeta(\omega,t)+\psi(\omega,t))=0
, and thus, for
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
T_{\varepsilon}\geq t_{0}
such that for all
t\geq T_{\varepsilon}
it holds

\displaystyle|\lambda\zeta(\omega,t)+\psi(\omega,t)|<\varepsilon,

therefore,

\displaystyle|\exp(\lambda t)(\lambda\zeta(\omega,t)+\psi(\omega,t))|<\varepsilon\exp(\lambda t).

Denoting

\displaystyle F(\ell,\phi,t):=\exp(\lambda t)\left(\phi-\frac{\ell}{\lambda}\right),

we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}F(\ell,\phi,t)=\lambda\exp(\lambda t)\left(\phi-\frac{\ell}{\lambda}\right),\quad\frac{d}{d\phi}F(\ell,\phi,t)=\exp(\lambda t),\quad\frac{d}{d\ell}F(\ell,\phi,t)=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\exp(\lambda t).

By Itô’s formula, we have for all
t\geq t_{0}

\displaystyle dF(\ell,\phi(t),t)=\lambda\exp(\lambda t)\left(\phi(t)-\frac{\ell}{\lambda}\right)dt+\exp(\lambda t)d\phi(t)=(\lambda\exp(\lambda t)\zeta(t)+\exp(\lambda t)\psi(t))dt.

From here it follows that for all
t\geq T_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle|\exp(\lambda t)\zeta(\omega,t)-\exp(\lambda T_{\varepsilon})\zeta(\omega,T_{\varepsilon})|=

\displaystyle\ \left|\int_{T_{\varepsilon}}^{t}(\lambda\exp(\lambda s)\zeta(\omega,s)+\exp(\lambda s)\psi(\omega,s))ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \varepsilon\int_{T_{\varepsilon}}^{t}\exp(\lambda s)ds=\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}(\exp(\lambda t)-\exp(\lambda T_{\varepsilon})),

hence

\displaystyle\exp(\lambda t)|\zeta(\omega,t)|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\exp(\lambda t)\zeta(\omega,t)-\exp(\lambda T_{\varepsilon})\zeta(T_{\varepsilon})\right|+\exp(\lambda T_{\varepsilon})|\zeta(\omega,T_{\varepsilon})|

\displaystyle<\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}(\exp(\lambda t)-\exp(\lambda T_{\varepsilon}))+\exp(\lambda T_{\varepsilon})|\zeta(\omega,T_{\varepsilon})|,

and thus

\displaystyle|\zeta(\omega,t)|<\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}+\exp(\lambda(T_{\varepsilon}-t))\left(|\zeta(\omega,T_{\varepsilon})|-\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}\right).

From here it yields

\displaystyle 0\leq\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}|\zeta(\omega,t)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda},

which completes the proof, since
\varepsilon>0
and
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
were chosen arbitrarily.
∎

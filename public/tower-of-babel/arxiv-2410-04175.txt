Einstein Cluster as Central Spiky Distribution of Galactic Dark Matter
Kei-ichi Maeda, Vitor Cardoso, Anzhong Wang

Abstract
Using the Einstein cluster models, we construct a fully relativistic, spherically symmetric, spiky structure of matter distribution near a supermassive black hole. We introduce and discuss three simple toy models, together with a more realistic model, which includes a Hernquist-type distribution with a typical galaxy scale. We find that the innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO) depends on the details of the environment, and lies between the photon radius (at 3 ​ M BH 3M_{\rm BH} ) and the ISCO radius of an isolated black hole of mass M BH M_{\rm BH} (at 6 ​ M BH 6M_{\rm BH} ).



I
Introduction

The nature of dark matter (DM) and its role in standard model of particle physics is one of the most mysterious problems in modern cosmology. We know it exists via its gravitational effects, but we are ignorant of its properties, including mass or couplings to other fields. The range of possibilities is extremely large: DM could be a new fundamental particle, or it could simply be a large amount of tiny primordial black holes (PBHs), or a plethora of new fields, among other possibilities. Despite the numerous searches for specific DM candidates, no compelling candidate has yet been found
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
.

One possible approach to understand the nature and local distribution of DM consists on studying
specific events near a supermassive black hole (SMBH) or intermediate mass black hole (IMBH).
The annihilation of DM particles and consequent sourcing of high luminosity events has been discussed
as a possible smoking gun of DM
[
7
]
. A powerful alternative consists on the observation of gravitational waves, which is to a large extent independent of the strength of the coupling between DM and the standard model of particle physics
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
. In addition, precise monitoring of the
periapsis shift and overall motion of stars nearby BHs could also
reveal some features of DM distribution
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
.

Most DM distributions are studied in a fixed, flat-space background. One of the most popular distributions is the Navarro–Frenk–White (NFW) profile, which is consistent with observations and
N
-body simulations. Other profiles such as Hernquist-type distribution have also been proposed
[
15
]
.
The adiabatic growth of a BH
may induce a spiky, dense structure near BHs
[
7
]
. The relativistic extension of the spiky structure near
SMBH was given by
[
16
]
.
One of the striking conclusions of these works is that the inner boundary of the spiky DM distribution
is below the innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO) radius; in particular, the inner boundary is located at an areal radius
4M_{\rm BH}
for a spherically symmetric system, with
M_{\rm BH}
the BH mass.

The DM distribution close to BHs is particularly interesting: its density in the strong gravity regime can be considerably large, and hence effects on gravitational wave emission can be important. Thus, in the context of observations concerning events in highly dynamical, strong-field gravity, a careful modelling of DM distribution in a fully general-relativistic setting is required.
Especially, when we take into account the self-gravity of the DM distribution, the curved spacetime description is no longer that of a vacuum Kerr geometry.
Fully general relativistic solutions describing spherically symmetric BHs immersed in environments were discussed recently
[
9
]
assuming an Hernquist-type DM distribution.
The DM is distributed all throughout the BH exterior regions in the original work, violating the dominant energy condition is a region close to the horizon. A simple modification of the original profile eliminates this issue
[
17
]
; other analytic form of DM distribution was also given in Ref.
[
18
,
19
]
.

In this note, we provide simple analytic models with spiky DM distribution within the so-called Einstein cluster construction. In fact, although we have DM in mind, our results apply as well for standard, baryonic matter environments. There are several works on DM distribution by use of the Einstein cluster
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
. Here we focus on the possibility that some distributions may yield smaller ISCO radius, below
the ISCO radius of the central BH (
6M_{\rm BH}
).

After we introduce the basic equations for the Einstein cluster in §
II
, we discuss on how to find the ISCO radius of the DM distribution when we take into account self-gravity of DM in §
III
. We then present three toy models
with the ISCO radius smaller than
6M_{\rm BH}
in §
IV
.
In §
V

The summary and discussion follow in §
VI

II
A spherically symmetric Einstein cluster spacetime

We consider a spherically symmetric spacetime, described by the line element

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{1-\frac{2m(r)}{r}}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}\,.

(1)

In vacuum, the general solution is the Schwarzschild spacetime,

\displaystyle m(r)=M_{\rm BH}\,,~~f(r)=f_{0}\left(1-{2M_{\rm BH}\over r}\right)\,,

where
M_{\rm BH}
is the BH mass and
f_{0}
is an integration constant.

In this note, we consider DM distribution outside the
event horizon.
We assume that DM is composed of particles
moving along circular geodesics just as the Einstein cluster. Since the radial component of the pressure is assumed to be zero (
P_{r}=0
) as we will show it explicitly for the present model,
the Einstein equations and energy-momentum conservation give

\displaystyle m^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi r^{2}\rho\,,

(2)

\displaystyle{f^{\prime}\over f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{2m(r)\over r(r-2m(r))}\,,

(3)

\displaystyle P_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{m(r)\over 2(r-2m(r))}\rho\,.

(4)

For circular orbits, the 4-velocity obeys

\displaystyle u_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-E\,,~u^{0}\,=\,{E\over f}\,,

\displaystyle u_{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{z}\,,~u^{\phi}\,=\,{L_{z}\over r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\,,

\displaystyle r^{4}(u^{\theta})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L^{2}-{L_{z}^{2}\over\sin^{2}\theta}\,.

With the normalization
u^{\mu}u_{\mu}=-1
,
we find the radial equation

\displaystyle{f(r)\over 1-{2m(r)\over r}}\left({dr\over d\tau}\right)^{2}=E^{2}-V_{\rm eff}^{2}(r;L)\,,

where the effective potential
V_{\rm eff}^{2}
is defined by

\displaystyle V_{\rm eff}^{2}=f(r)\left(1+{L^{2}\over r^{2}}\right)\,.

(5)

The radius of a circular orbit is given by the equation

\displaystyle{dV_{\rm eff}^{2}\over dr}={2f\over r^{3}(r-2m(r))}\left[m(r)r^{2}-L^{2}(r-3m(r))\right]=0\,,

(6)

where we used Eq. (
3
).

The energy
E
and angular momentum
\,\!\!\!\mbox{ \boldmath$L$}\,(L=|\,\!\!\!\mbox{ \boldmath$L$}|\,,L_{z})

of a particle on a circular orbit in the spacetime (
1
)
are given by

\displaystyle E^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(r)\left(1+{L^{2}\over r^{2}}\right)={r-2m(r)\over r-3m(r)}f(r)\,,

(7)

\displaystyle L_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L\cos\theta\,,

(8)

\displaystyle L^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{m(r)r^{2}\over r-3m(r)}\,.

(9)

Note that since
m(r)>0
and
f>0
,
we find that
r>3m(r)
from the above equations, which means that
photon sphere in the present model does not exist inside the
dark DM distribution.

The energy-momentum tensor of
N
particles is given by

\displaystyle T^{\mu}_{~\nu}=\mu_{0}\sum_{I=1}^{N}\int d\tau_{\rm I}{u_{\rm I}^{\mu}{u_{\rm I}}_{\nu}\over\sqrt{-g}}\delta(x-z_{\rm I})\,,

where
\mu_{0}
is a particle mass and
u_{\rm I}^{\mu}
is 4-velocity of the
I
-th particle
in the spacetime given by the metric (
1
).
The energy density and pressures are given by

\displaystyle\rho(\,\!\!\!\mbox{ \boldmath$r$})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u^{0}u_{0}n(\,\!\!\!\mbox{ \boldmath$r$})={E^{2}\over f}n(\,\!\!\!\mbox{ \boldmath$r$})=\left(1+{L^{2}\over r^{2}}\right)n(\,\!\!\!\mbox{ \boldmath$r$})={r-2m(r)\over r-3m(r)}n(\,\!\!\!\mbox{ \boldmath$r$})\,,

\displaystyle P_{r}(\,\!\!\!\mbox{ \boldmath$r$})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,,

\displaystyle P_{t}(\,\!\!\!\mbox{ \boldmath$r$})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle u^{\phi}u_{\phi}\rangle n(\,\!\!\!\mbox{ \boldmath$r$})={L^{2}\langle\cos^{2}\theta\rangle\over r^{2}}n(\,\!\!\!\mbox{ \boldmath$r$})={L^{2}\over 2r^{2}}n(\,\!\!\!\mbox{ \boldmath$r$})={m(r)\over 2(r-3m(r))}n(\,\!\!\!\mbox{ \boldmath$r$})\,,

where
n(\,\!\!\!\mbox{ \boldmath$r$})
is the unknown distribution function of particles, determined by the energy density
\rho(\,\!\!\!\mbox{ \boldmath$r$})
.

III
ISCO radius and Conditions near ISCO radius

We then look for the radius of the ISCO in the spacetime (
1
), which is obtained
by Eq.(
6
) and

{d^{2}V_{\rm eff}^{2}\over dr^{2}}={2f\over r^{4}(r-2m(r))^{2}}\times\left[m^{\prime}r^{4}-2mr^{2}(r-2m)+L^{2}\left(m^{\prime}r^{2}+3(r-2m)(r-4m)\right)\right]=0\,,

(10)

where we again used Eq. (
3
).
Eliminating
L^{2}
, we find
the stability condition for the circular orbit

\displaystyle r^{2}m^{\prime}(r)+rm(r)-6m^{2}(r)\geq 0\,,

(11)

where the equality corresponds to
the inflection point.
Hence solving the equation

\displaystyle r^{2}m^{\prime}(r)+rm(r)-6m^{2}(r)=0\,,

(12)

we obtain the ISCO radius
r_{\rm I}
as the minimum value of the possible solutions.
We then find the energy
E_{\rm ISCO}
and angular momentum

L_{\rm ISCO}
of a particle at
the ISCO radius
from Eqs. (
7
) and (
9
) by inserting
r=r_{\rm I}
.

Now we consider the behaviour of metric and matter distribution near the ISCO radius.
We assume there is no matter fluid below the ISCO radius
r_{\rm I}
.
We then assume mass function
m(r)
near the ISCO radius as

\displaystyle m(r)=M_{\rm BH}\left[1+\alpha(r-r_{\rm I})^{p}/M_{\rm BH}^{p}\right]~~(r\geq r_{\rm I})\,,

where
\alpha>0
. There is no solution for
p<0
.
In the case of
p=0
, we have to put a mass shell at
r_{\rm I}
and
r_{\rm I}=6m(r_{\rm I})=6(M_{\rm BH}+\alpha)
, which is a trivial case. When
0<p<1
,
m^{\prime}
diverges at
r_{\rm I}
and there is no solution for Eq. (
12
).
For
p>1
, we find
r_{\rm I}=6M_{\rm BH}
because
m^{\prime}(r_{\rm I})=0
.
This gives usual BH ISCO radius.
Note that if the energy density vanishes at the ISCO radius, i.e.,

\rho\propto(r-r_{\rm I})^{\tilde{p}}~(\tilde{p}>0)
, we find
p=\tilde{p}+1>1
.

We find a non-trivial solution when
p=1
.
Eq. (
12
) becomes

\displaystyle\alpha r_{\rm I}^{2}+M_{\rm BH}r_{\rm I}-6M_{\rm BH}^{2}=0\,,

(13)

i.e.,

\displaystyle m(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{\rm BH}+\alpha(r-r_{\rm I})~~(r\geq r_{\rm I})\,,

(14)

\displaystyle r_{\rm I}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{M_{\rm BH}\over 2\alpha}\left(-1+\sqrt{1+24\alpha}\right)\,.

(15)

The relation between the ISCO radius (
r_{\rm I}
) and the parameter
\alpha
for the case of
p=1
.

The condition of
r>3m(r)
gives
\alpha<{1\over 3}
.
We show
r_{\rm I}
in terms of
\alpha
in Fig.

.
In the limit of
\alpha\rightarrow{1\over 3}
, we find

r_{\rm I}\rightarrow 3M_{\rm BH}
, while for
\alpha=0
, we recover
the vacuum BH ISCO radius (
r_{\rm I}\rightarrow 6M_{\rm BH}
).

IV
Simple Toy Models

One interesting question is whether DM can exist inside the vacuum BH ISCO radius
(
<6M_{\rm BH}
). In a realistic DM distribution,
we have to take into account
the initial conditions and diffusion process
of particles in a realistic situation.
However, in this note, we focus solely on the above point concerning existence, and construct simple models with
r_{\rm I}<6M_{\rm BH}
,
giving mass distribution
m(r)
.

In what follows, we shall normalize all quantities by the BH mass and set

M_{\rm BH}=1
.

IV.1
Model I (“Isothermal” distribution)

First we assume the (simplistic) mass function

\displaystyle m(r)=1+\alpha(r-r_{\rm I})\,,

where
0<\alpha<1/3
is a constant.
We find

\displaystyle\rho={m^{\prime}\over 4\pi r^{2}}={\alpha\over 4\pi r^{2}}\,,

an “isothermal”-like distribution. The metric function
f
is easily integrated,

\displaystyle f={c_{0}\over r}\left(r-r_{*}\right)^{1\over 1-2\alpha}~~(r\geq r_{\rm I})\,,

(16)

where

\displaystyle r_{*}\equiv{2(1-\alpha r_{\rm I})\over 1-2\alpha}\,,

and
c_{0}
is an integration constant, fixed by the outer boundary conditions.
Stability condition (
11
) is always satisfied. Interestingly, for
\alpha=1/3
and ISCO radius at
r_{\rm I}=3
(the photon sphere radius), then every point satisfies the inflection condition (
12
). Every point is marginally stable.

Since
m(r)\rightarrow\infty
as
r\rightarrow\infty
, we assume that matter distribution is truncated, and exists only between
r_{\rm I}
and
r_{\rm O}
. The total mass of the system is given by
M=m(r_{\rm O})=1+\alpha(r_{\rm O}-r_{\rm I})
. Outside, the metric is given by the Schwarzschild metric with mass
M
. We can confirm that
the outer radius
r_{\rm O}
is always larger than the ISCO radius of the mass
M
, i.e.,

r_{\rm O}\geq 6M
. The metric is given by

\displaystyle m(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&(r<r_{\rm I})\\
1+\alpha\left(r-r_{\rm I}\right)&(r_{\rm I}<r<r_{\rm O})\,\,,\\
M&(r>r_{\rm O})\\
\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}f_{0}\left(1-{2\over r}\right)&(r<r_{\rm I})\\
{c_{0}\over r}\left(r-r_{*}\right)^{1\over 1-2\alpha}&(r_{\rm I}<r<r_{\rm O})\,\,,\\
1-{2M\over r}&(r>r_{\rm O})\\
\end{array}\right.

where

\displaystyle f_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{r_{\rm O}-2M\over r_{\rm I}-2}\left({r_{\rm I}-r_{*}\over r_{\rm O}-r_{*}}\right)^{1\over 1-2\alpha}\,,

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{r_{\rm O}-2M\over(r_{\rm O}-r_{*})^{1\over 1-2\alpha}}\,.

Note that the tangential pressure is given by

\displaystyle P_{t}={m(r)\rho(r)\over 2(r-2m(r))}.

IV.2
Model II

The distribution above, with a very specific choice of ISCO radius, allows for marginally stable timelike circular geodesics in the entire spacetime. We also find a curious, analytic model that doesn’t fine tune the integration constants, and for which every point in the matter distribution is an inflection point. We have to solve Eq. (
12
) for
r_{\rm I}\leq r\leq r_{\rm O}
.
Imposing
r>3m(r)
, we obtain

\displaystyle m(r)={r\over 3[1+(r/r_{c})^{2}]}\,,

(19)

where

r_{c}
is an integration constant. The density distribution is

\displaystyle\rho={m^{\prime}\over 4\pi r^{2}}={1-(r/r_{c})^{2}\over 12\pi r^{2}[1+(r/r_{c})^{2}]^{2}}\,.

The density vanishes at
r=r_{c}
, thus the outer radius of the distribution must be
r_{\rm O}\leq r_{c}
.
From the stability condition, i.e.,
the outer radius is larger than the ISCO radius of the total mass
M_{\rm O}

(
r_{\rm O}\geq 6M_{\rm O}=6m(r_{\rm O})
), we find

\displaystyle r_{\rm O}\geq{2r_{\rm O}\over[1+(r_{\rm O}/r_{c})^{2}]}\,,

which gives
r_{\rm O}\geq r_{c}
.
As a result, the outer radius must be
r_{\rm O}=r_{c}
.

For the metric function
f
, we obtain

\displaystyle f={c_{0}r^{2}\over 1+3(r/r_{\rm O})^{2}}\,,

with
c_{0}
an integration constant.

Since the outside (
r>r_{\rm O}
) and inside (
r<r_{\rm I}
)
of the Einstein cluster are assumed to be in vacuum, the continuity conditions at
r_{\rm I}
and
r_{\rm O}
yield,

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1&(r<r_{\rm I})\\[5.0pt]
{r\over 3[1+(r/r_{\rm O})^{2}]}&(r_{\rm I}<r<r_{\rm O})\,\,,\\[5.0pt]
M&(r>r_{\rm O})\\
\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}f_{0}\left(1-{2\over r}\right)&(r<r_{\rm I})\\[5.0pt]
{c_{0}r^{2}\over(1+3(r/r_{\rm O})^{2}}&(r_{\rm I}<r<r_{\rm O})\,\,,\\[5.0pt]
1-{2M\over r}&(r>r_{\rm O})\\
\end{array}\right.

where

f_{0}={8\left(1+\beta^{2}\right)\over\left(3+\beta^{2}\right)^{2}}\,,\quad c_{0}={8\over 3r_{0}^{2}}\,.

(22)

Here we set
\beta\equiv r_{\rm O}/r_{\rm I}
.
The tangential pressure is given by

\displaystyle P_{t}={1-(r/r_{\rm O})^{2}\over 24\pi r^{2}(1+3(r/r_{\rm O})^{2})(1+(r/r_{\rm O})^{2})^{2}}\,.

The ISCO radius
r_{\rm I}
is given by
\beta
, from the definition
m(r_{\rm I})=1
,
as

\displaystyle r_{\rm I}=3\left(1+{1\over\beta^{2}}\right)\,.

While the total mass
M
is also described by
\beta
as

\displaystyle M=m(r_{\rm O})={r_{\rm O}\over 6}={\beta r_{\rm I}\over 6}={1+\beta^{2}\over 2\beta}\,.

Since
\beta>1
, we find
3<r_{\rm I}<6
and
1<M<\infty
.

IV.3
Model III

Finally, we find another simple analytic model. We assume the mass function
m(r)
as

\displaystyle m(r)=m_{p}r^{p}~~(r_{\rm I}\leq r\leq r_{\rm O})\,,

(23)

where
m_{p}
and
p(>0)
are some constants.
The continuity of mass function at
r_{\rm I}
is described by
m(r_{\rm I})=m_{p}r_{\rm I}^{p}=1
,
and the condition of the ISCO,

\displaystyle r_{\rm I}^{2}m^{\prime}(r_{\rm I})+r_{rmI}m(r_{\rm I})-6m^{2}(r_{\rm I})=0\,,

gives

\displaystyle(p+1)m_{p}r_{\rm I}^{p+1}-6m_{p}^{2}r_{\rm I}^{2p}=0\,.

From these two conditions,
we find

\displaystyle m_{p}=r_{\rm I}^{-p}\,,~{\rm and}~~p={6-r_{\rm I}\over r_{\rm I}}\,.

The condition of

3<r_{\rm I}<6
, in which we are interested,
gives
0<p<1
. The stability condition (
11
) gives

\displaystyle r\geq\left({6m_{p}\over p+1}\right)^{1\over 1-p}=r_{\rm I}\,,

which is always satisfied.

The metric function should satisfy

\displaystyle{1\over f}{df\over dr}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{2m\over r(r-2m)}={2m_{p}r^{p-2}\over(1-2m_{p}r^{p-1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 1-p}{d\over dr}\ln|(1-2m_{p}r^{p-1})|\,,

which is integrated as

\displaystyle f=c_{0}\left(1-{2m_{p}\over r^{1-p}}\right)^{1\over 1-p}\,,

where
c_{0}
is an integration constant.

With the continuity conditions at
r=r_{\rm I}
and
r_{\rm O}
, we find the metric functions as

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&(r<r_{\rm I})\\
m_{p}r^{p}&(r_{\rm I}<r<r_{\rm O})\,,\\
M&(r>r_{\rm O})\\
\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}f_{0}\left(1-{2\over r}\right)&(2M<r<r_{\rm I})\\
c_{0}\left(1-{2m_{p}\over r^{1-p}}\right)^{1\over 1-p}&(r_{\rm I}<r<r_{\rm O})\,,\\
1-{2M\over r}&(r>r_{\rm O})\\
\end{array}\right.

where

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-{2M\over r_{\rm O}}\right)\left(1-{2m_{p}\over r_{\rm O}^{1-p}}\right)^{-{1\over 1-p}}\,,

\displaystyle f_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\left(1-{2M\over r_{\rm O}}\right)\over\left(1-{2\over r_{\rm I}}\right)}\left[{\left(1-{2m_{p}\over r_{\rm I}^{1-p}}\right)\over\left(1-{2m_{p}\over r_{\rm O}^{1-p}}\right)}\right]^{{1\over 1-p}}\,.

In the present model, we have two
free parameters,
r_{\rm I}
and
r_{\rm O}
, which should satisfy

\displaystyle 3<r_{\rm I}<6\,,

\displaystyle r_{\rm O}>r_{\rm I}\,,

which gaurantees
M>1
.

\alpha
is given by

\displaystyle\alpha=m^{\prime}(r_{\rm I})=pm_{p}r_{\rm I}^{p-1}=pm_{p}^{1\over p}={6-r_{\rm I}\over r_{\rm I}^{2}}\,,

which gives

\displaystyle 0<\alpha<{1\over 3}\,.

The energy density is given by

\displaystyle\rho={m^{\prime}\over 4\pi r^{2}}={6-r_{\rm I}\over 4\pi r_{\rm I}^{p+1}}r^{3-p}\,.

The power exponent of density distribution (
3-p
) is uniform and its value takes between 2 and 3.

This model can be extended to the similar mass function such that

\displaystyle m={m_{p}r^{p}\over 1+(r_{*}/r)^{(1-p)}}\,,

VI
Summary and Remarks

Using the Einstein cluster model,
we discuss general relativistic solutions describing possible BH environments. In particular, we show that nontrivial distributions can occur the (isolated) BH ISCO radius
(
6M_{\rm BH}
). We have presented simple, realistic models with a specific galactic scale, as well as three toy models. The ISCO radius of the matter distribution is found to lie between the photon radius (
3M_{\rm BH}
) and
the BH ISCO radius.

One of the issues that we don’t address is stability of these configurations. There have been discussions regarding the stability of the Einstein clusters
[
26
,
22
]
. The Einstein cluster under our stability condition (
11
)
appears to be metastable in the range of
3m(r)<r<6m(r)
, and it may become unstable when we consider a simple Einstein cluster star
[
26
]
.
However there exists a BH inside the matter distribution in our models, and most of DM distribution satisfies the absolute stability condition (
r>6m(r)
) including the region outside the DM distribution. Therefore, we must carefully reanalyze the stability of such a system.

The presence of matter in such a strong gravity region may be significant for gravitational wave observations, as well as for the DM search experiments, such as those involving particle annihilation or creation. We will leave these important studies, along
with the stability analysis, for future work.

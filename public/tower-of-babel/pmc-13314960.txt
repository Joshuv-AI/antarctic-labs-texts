Chaotic dynamics of Tai Chi public attention revealed by an integrated framework of horizontal visibility graphs, autoencoders, and sparse identification
Authors: Yafeng Kang, Pengchao Li, Lu Tang, Chao Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Understanding the dynamical patterns of public attention towards traditional cultural practices such as Tai Chi is crucial for cultural heritage preservation and promotion strategies. This study reveals the chaotic dynamics underlying Tai Chi public attention through an integrated analytical framework combining Horizontal Visibility Graphs (HVG), Autoencoder neural networks, and Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics (SINDy). Using daily Baidu Index data (2014–2024) from four representative Chinese provinces (Beijing, Shanghai, Guangdong, Henan), we construct HVGs and quantify chaos through degree distribution exponents (λ). Results reveal significant regional heterogeneity: HVG λ ranges from 0.291 (Beijing PC, strong chaos) to 0.437 (Shanghai PC, quasi-periodic), with corresponding maximum Lyapunov exponents of 0.025–0.036 bits/day (Rosenstein method) and 0.016–0.217 bits/day (Wolf method); the consistent positivity across both estimation methods provides cross-validated confirmation of chaotic dynamics. Autoencoder-based dimensionality reduction achieves reconstruction correlations of 0.896–0.918, enabling discrete SINDy to identify sparse governing equations (37–38 active terms from 48 candidates) with normalized root-mean-square errors of 11.7–12.8%. The dynamical conclusions are triangulated across three analytically independent characterizations—HVG topological classification, Lyapunov-based phase space analysis, and SINDy equation structure—which converge on consistent regional rankings, with Beijing exhibiting the strongest chaotic signatures and Henan the most structured quasi-periodic behavior. Phase space analysis confirms diverse attractor geometries: Beijing exhibits space-filling chaotic trajectories while Shanghai displays quasi-periodic structures. Comparative evaluation against ARIMA, VAR, and LSTM baselines within the same latent space confirms that SINDy achieves superior predictive fidelity (NRMSE 17.6–22.4%) while uniquely providing interpretable governing equations and chaos diagnostics inaccessible to alternative approaches. This interdisciplinary framework bridges complex network theory, deep learning, and dynamical systems analysis, offering a rigorous quantitative paradigm for studying the temporal evolution of public attention in cultural phenomena.

Introduction

Understanding the dynamical patterns hidden in complex time series is essential for explaining how social and cultural events evolve over time. Compared with many physical signals, attention-related series are often shaped by intertwined mechanisms—long-term trend shifts, seasonal cycles, media amplification, and collective responses—so their evolution is typically nonlinear and may even exhibit chaotic characteristics. This complexity makes it difficult for traditional linear tools to capture regime changes or to describe the intrinsic rules that generate observed fluctuations1–3, a challenge that has been widely recognized in the study of social dynamics more broadly4. Therefore, methods that can both characterize nonlinear structure and infer plausible dynamical mechanisms from data are increasingly needed.

Against this background, Tai Chi offers a meaningful cultural object whose public attention dynamics can be quantified. Originating in the mid-17th century in Henan Province of China, Tai Chi has gradually become a widely practiced cultural activity. In December 2020, Tai Chi was included in the UNESCO list of representative works of human intangible cultural heritage, highlighting its universal cultural value and attracting more than 100 million practitioners from more than 150 countries around the world5,6. Nevertheless, public attention to Tai Chi is not driven by a single event; it reflects the combined influence of policy initiatives, media coverage, seasonal rhythms, and regional cultural foundations. The resulting daily search-index series thus provides an appropriate empirical basis for investigating how cultural attention evolves under multi-factor interactions and heterogeneous local contexts7.

However, understanding the time-varying dynamics of such cultural phenomena requires the development of appropriate analytical frameworks. While traditional methods like linear regression or Fourier analysis fail to capture the complex and nonlinear nature of public engagement, recent advancements in dynamical systems and data-driven techniques have shown promise in addressing these challenges. For instance, the Horizontal Visibility Graph (HVG) method offers a way to map time series into network structures, enabling an exploration of temporal dependencies and deviations from randomness8–10. More broadly, complex network representations of multivariate time series have demonstrated capacity to reveal dynamical structure across diverse physical and social systems3,11. However, HVG-based methods primarily focus on characterizing the structure of the time series rather than providing explicit models of the underlying dynamics12,13. Similarly, Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics (SINDy) has proven effective in discovering governing equations from time series14–16, but it is limited when dealing with high-dimensional data or when the appropriate state variables are not immediately apparent—a challenge that has motivated recent efforts to combine SINDy with deep learning architectures for automated coordinate discovery17,18. Despite these advances, no existing framework integrates these methods to both characterize and model the complex dynamics underlying phenomena like Tai Chi’s public attention19,20. The resilience and adaptability of such attention dynamics, moreover, remain poorly understood from a dynamical systems perspective21.

To address these limitations, this study proposes an integrated approach that combines the strengths of HVG, SINDy, and autoencoders. By leveraging HVG-based network features, we can capture the temporal structure of Tai Chi’s public attention. Using the SINDy framework, we can identify sparse governing equations that describe the evolution of attention over time, while autoencoders allow us to reduce the dimensionality of the data and uncover latent variables that better represent the underlying dynamics. Our approach thus aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of Tai Chi’s public attention dynamics by combining network analysis, dynamical modeling, and data reduction. Specifically, we apply this integrated method to daily search index data from four Chinese provinces (Beijing, Shanghai, Guangdong, and Henan) over a ten-year period, enabling us to investigate regional variations in the temporal patterns of Tai Chi engagement.

Methods and datasets

This study proposes an integrated HVG–Autoencoder–SINDy framework built around a sequential analytical logic. The HVG transforms each raw time series into a network representation whose degree distribution encodes the temporal correlation structure of the underlying dynamics, and extracts a 12-dimensional topological feature vector per sliding window that captures local dynamical properties across time. Because these feature vectors are high-dimensional and the relevant state variables of the underlying system are not directly observable, an autoencoder is employed to compress them into a low-dimensional latent space, where the learned representation retains the essential dynamical structure while remaining tractable for subsequent equation discovery. SINDy then operates on the latent trajectories to identify sparse governing equations, exploiting the compact and geometrically meaningful coordinates provided by the autoencoder to make reliable sparse regression feasible. Classical chaos analysis tools—Lyapunov exponent estimation, phase space reconstruction, and Poincaré sections—are employed alongside this pipeline to independently validate the dynamical characterization obtained from the HVG analysis. Horizontal visibility graph

The HVG algorithm maps a time series \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\{x_t\}_{t=1}^{N}$$\end{document} to an undirected graph \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G = (V, E)$$\end{document} where each time point corresponds to a node8. Two nodes i and j (with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$i < j$$\end{document}) are connected by an edge if and only if all intermediate values satisfy the horizontal visibility criterion:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$x_k< \min (x_i, x_j), \quad \forall k: i< k < j$$\end{document}This geometric condition ensures that a horizontal line connecting \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(i, x_i)$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(j, x_j)$$\end{document} lies entirely above all intermediate points. The resulting graph inherits dynamical properties of the original time series through its topological structure8.

A fundamental theoretical result establishes that for uncorrelated random time series, the HVG degree distribution follows an exponential decay \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$P(k) = \frac{1}{3}\left( \frac{2}{3}\right) ^{k-2}$$\end{document}, equivalently expressed as:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$P(k) \sim \exp (-\lambda k), \quad \lambda _{\text {random}} = \ln (3/2) \approx 0.405$$\end{document}This universal exponent serves as a reference for chaos detection. Luque et al.8 proved analytically that for i.i.d. random series \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$P(k) = \frac{1}{3}(\frac{2}{3})^{k-2}$$\end{document}, yielding \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _{\text {random}} = \ln (3/2) \approx 0.4055$$\end{document}. The mechanistic interpretation is transparent: chaotic time series possess long-range temporal correlations that elevate the probability of distant time points satisfying the horizontal visibility criterion, thereby increasing the frequency of large-degree nodes and flattening the degree distribution below the random baseline (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda < \ln (3/2)$$\end{document}); conversely, periodic series concentrate visibility connections locally, steepening the distribution (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda > \ln (3/2)$$\end{document}). This boundary has been analytically validated for the logistic map, Lorenz attractor, and fractional Brownian motion, and is now established as a standard criterion for HVG-based dynamical classification22–25.

The parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda$$\end{document} is estimated by applying OLS linear regression in the log-transformed domain: for each empirical degree probability \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$P(k) > 0$$\end{document}, we fit \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\log P(k) = a - \lambda k$$\end{document} via numpy.polyfit(Python package), recovering \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda$$\end{document} as the negative slope. This semi-logarithmic regression is the standard approach for exponential distribution fitting, as it renders the model linear in its parameters and ensures unbiased slope estimation under the exponential decay assumption.

To capture the temporal evolution of network properties, we employ a sliding window approach. For each window of length W, we construct the HVG and extract a 12-dimensional feature vector comprising: degree statistics (mean, standard deviation, maximum, minimum), local clustering coefficient (mean, standard deviation), network density, local \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda$$\end{document} estimate, and time series statistics within the window (mean, standard deviation, skewness, kurtosis). These feature vectors form the input to the subsequent autoencoder.

Sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics

For discrete-time dynamics appropriate to our daily time series, we seek equations of the form:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textbf{z}(t+1) = \textbf{f}(\textbf{z}(t))$$\end{document}We construct a library matrix \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\boldsymbol{\Theta }(\textbf{Z})$$\end{document} containing candidate nonlinear functions evaluated at each time point. Lyapunov exponent estimation

The largest Lyapunov exponent \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _{\max }$$\end{document} quantifies the exponential rate of divergence between nearby trajectories, serving as the definitive chaos indicator: \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _{\max } > 0$$\end{document} implies sensitive dependence on initial conditions (chaos), while \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _{\max } \le 0$$\end{document} indicates regular dynamics29.

We employ the Rosenstein algorithm30, which tracks the average logarithmic divergence of nearest neighbors in reconstructed phase space. For each point \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textbf{y}(t_0)$$\end{document}, we identify its nearest neighbor \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textbf{y}(t_0')$$\end{document} (excluding temporally adjacent points) and track the divergence:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$d_j(i) = \Vert \textbf{y}(t_j + i) - \textbf{y}(t_j' + i)\Vert$$\end{document}The largest Lyapunov exponent is estimated from the slope of the average logarithmic divergence:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\langle \ln d_j(i) \rangle \approx \lambda _{\max } \cdot i \cdot \Delta t + c$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta t$$\end{document} is the sampling interval and c is a constant. We additionally implement the Wolf algorithm29 as a complementary validation method.The Rosenstein method is adopted as the primary estimator throughout this study owing to its ensemble-averaged design, which provides greater statistical robustness for non-stationary series of moderate length; the Wolf algorithm serves as a complementary validation tool with a shorter effective integration window (10 steps vs. 50 steps), making it more sensitive to transient local expansion events.

Phase space reconstruction and Poincaré sections

Phase portraits visualize system trajectories in reconstructed phase space, revealing attractor geometry. Poincaré sections provide dimensional reduction of phase space dynamics by recording successive intersections of trajectories with a hyperplane \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Sigma = \{\textbf{x}: h(\textbf{x}) = c\}$$\end{document}. A functional relationship suggests deterministic dynamics, while scattered distributions indicate stochastic or high-dimensional chaotic behavior.

Datasets and processing

This study utilizes daily search index data for the keyword “Tai Chi” (in Chinese) collected from four Chinese provinces over an 11-year period from January 1, 2014 to December 31, 2024. The four provinces—Beijing, Shanghai, Guangdong, and Henan—were selected to represent distinct regional characteristics: Beijing as the political and cultural capital, Shanghai as the leading economic center, Guangdong as the most populous province with a robust digital economy, and Henan as the birthplace of Tai Chi where Chenjiagou Village has served as the spiritual home of this martial art tradition since the mid-17th century. The dataset comprises two channels for each province: PC-based and mobile-based search indices, reflecting user engagement through different digital platforms. Each time series contains 4018 daily observations, yielding a total of 32,144 data points (4 provinces \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\times$$\end{document} 2 channels \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\times$$\end{document} 4018 days) for analysis.

Prior to analysis, the raw time series underwent preprocessing to mitigate noise while preserving dynamical features. A 7-day centered moving average filter was applied to smooth high-frequency fluctuations attributable to weekly periodicity and measurement noise. Subsequently, each smoothed series was standardized using z-score normalization: \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$x'_t = (x_t - \mu )/\sigma$$\end{document}, where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mu$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma$$\end{document} denote the series mean and standard deviation, respectively. This standardization ensures comparability across provinces and channels with different absolute magnitudes while preserving the relative temporal dynamics essential for HVG construction and dynamical analysis.

To contextualize the performance of the proposed framework, we compare SINDy against three alternative approaches applied within the same autoencoder latent space: univariate ARIMA, multivariate Vector Autoregression (VAR), and Long Short-Term Memory networks (LSTM). All methods receive identical 3-dimensional latent trajectories as input and are evaluated on a common 50-day test period using Mobile channel data from all four provinces, ensuring that performance differences reflect modeling capacity rather than differences in input representation. Results are presented in Fig. 6.

SINDy achieves the lowest one-step-ahead NRMSE across all provinces (Beijing: 20.8%, Guangdong: 22.4%, Henan: 19.9%, Shanghai: 17.6%), outperforming ARIMA (21.1–24.2%), VAR (21.2–24.7%), and LSTM (24.2–31.1%). Notably, LSTM yields the highest prediction errors across all provinces despite its greater model complexity, consistent with the known susceptibility of recurrent networks to overfitting under non-stationary conditions with moderate data volumes. The close performance of ARIMA and VAR confirms that linear multi-dimensional extensions provide limited additional benefit over univariate linear baselines for these chaotic series.

The residual violin plots (Fig. 6e–h) further reveal that SINDy produces the most concentrated residual distributions across all provinces, with narrower interquartile ranges and less extreme outliers compared to the three alternative methods, reflecting more consistent error behavior across the test period. While ARIMA and VAR achieve competitive accuracy on smoother series such as Shanghai, they are structurally incapable of capturing the nonlinear cross-dimensional interactions that SINDy identifies through sparse equation discovery. Beyond predictive performance, SINDy uniquely provides interpretable governing equations whose coefficient structure encodes province-specific dynamical properties, enables chaos detection through Lyapunov analysis, and characterizes attractor geometry—capabilities that neither linear methods nor black-box sequence models can replicate.

Discussion

This study introduces an integrated HVG-SINDy Autoencoder framework that bridges network-based complexity analysis with data-driven dynamical modeling, applied here to characterize the temporal evolution of public attention toward Tai Chi across four Chinese provinces over an 11-year period. The convergence of multiple analytical approaches—visibility graph topology, deep representation learning, and sparse equation discovery—yields a coherent picture of cultural attention as a low-dimensional chaotic system exhibiting pronounced regional specificity.

That the SINDy algorithm successfully identifies sparse governing equations from these latent trajectories validates the premise that cultural attention, despite its apparent complexity, obeys discoverable dynamical laws—a finding that positions such phenomena within the broader class of systems amenable to equation-free modeling approaches developed in physics and engineering contexts. The dynamical conclusions of this study derive their credibility from the convergence of three analytically independent characterizations: the HVG \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda$$\end{document} criterion, grounded in complex network theory and sensitive to the topological structure of temporal correlations8,22; the Rosenstein and Wolf \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _{\max }$$\end{document} estimates, grounded in delay embedding theory32 and ergodic theory33 respectively, and sensitive to the geometric divergence of phase space trajectories; and the SINDy-identified governing equations, whose regional variation in coefficient structure mirrors the dynamical hierarchy revealed by the network and phase space analyses. The parallel training of standard and variational autoencoders further provides an internal cross-check on the latent representation: that both architectures recover provincially coherent clustering without supervisory signal suggests that the learned coordinates reflect genuine dynamical structure rather than architectural artefacts. The mutual consistency of these independent lines of evidence — each rooted in a distinct theoretical tradition yet converging on the same regional hierarchy of chaos strength—constitutes the primary warrant for the dynamical interpretations advanced in this study.

The comparative analysis against ARIMA, VAR, and LSTM further substantiates the framework’s practical value. That SINDy achieves the lowest prediction error across all four provinces despite operating under identical input conditions confirms that sparse nonlinear equation discovery captures genuine dynamical structure rather than merely fitting noise. The counterintuitive underperformance of LSTM relative to simpler linear baselines is itself a dynamically meaningful finding: it reflects the sensitivity of recurrent black-box models to the non-stationarity and moderate effective sample sizes characteristic of decade-long culturalattention series, and serves as a cautionary note for uncritical application of deep sequence models to social time series without prior dynamical characterization.

The central finding that Tai Chi attention dynamics partition into chaotic and quasi-periodic regimes across provinces challenges prevailing assumptions in cultural analytics. Conventional approaches typically treat public attention as either purely stochastic or driven by identifiable external events, yet our results suggest an intermediate regime: deterministic chaos with predictability horizons of 30–70 days. This timescale carries practical implications for cultural heritage management, as it defines the window within which promotional interventions or policy initiatives might achieve predictable outcomes before intrinsic dynamical instabilities dominate. The regional heterogeneity we observe—with Henan displaying quasi-periodic behavior while Beijing exhibits strong chaos—likely reflects fundamental differences in how traditional culture interfaces with modern information ecosystems. In its birthplace, Tai Chi attention appears anchored to stable cultural institutions and practice communities that impose regular temporal structure; in the national capital, attention dynamics are shaped by a more volatile confluence of policy signals, media amplification, and competing cultural narratives.

These regional differences are also broadly consistent with provincial population size and urbanization profiles. Guangdong, as China’s most populous province with a highly developed digital economy, exhibits an intermediate chaos profile that may reflect the superposition of large-scale urban mobile engagement atop a residual base of traditional practice communities. Henan, by contrast, despite its substantial population, maintains a more culturally homogeneous relationship with Tai Chi as its birthplace, which may suppress the disruptive influences that amplify chaos in more cosmopolitan settings. A rigorous demographic analysis of these relationships, however, lies beyond the scope of the present study and represents a valuable direction for future investigation.

The SINDy-identified governing equations merit careful interpretation. The moderate sparsity (30–38 active terms from a 48-term library) indicates that attention dynamics, while reducible to tractable mathematical form, resist extreme simplification. Particularly noteworthy is the inverse relationship between HVG-derived periodicity and trigonometric term strength in the discovered equations: Henan’s quasi-periodic attention patterns arise not from dominant harmonic components but from complex nonlinear interactions among lower-order terms. This counterintuitive finding suggests that cultural rootedness manifests dynamically through intricate feedback mechanisms rather than simple seasonal or cyclical drivers—a distinction with implications for understanding how traditional practices maintain temporal coherence across generations. The stronger trigonometric signatures in Beijing’s equations may instead reflect externally imposed periodicities from institutional calendars, policy cycles, or media scheduling that overlay the underlying chaotic dynamics.

The platform-dependent asymmetry between PC and Mobile channels reveals an unexpected dimension of regional specificity. The pattern across Beijing and Henan—where desktop-based attention exhibits stronger chaos than mobile—aligns with intuitive expectations that deliberate, search-oriented behavior on computers might generate more complex temporal patterns than passive mobile browsing. Guangdong’s platform-invariant dynamics (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _{\text {PC}} = \lambda _{\text {Mobile}} = 0.376$$\end{document}) suggest a third regime in which the platform distinction has effectively dissolved, possibly reflecting the maturation of Guangdong’s mobile ecosystem to a point where mobile search behavior is as deliberate and topically focused as desktop search, consistent with Guangdong’s position as China’s leading province in mobile internet penetration and digital economy development.Shanghai’s reversal of this pattern demands explanation. We hypothesize that Shanghai’s highly developed mobile ecosystem, younger demographic profile, and integration of social media sharing into daily routines may have inverted the typical device-behavior relationship, with mobile platforms serving as primary conduits for active cultural engagement rather than passive consumption. This interpretation, if validated through user-level data, would carry implications for how digital platforms mediate cultural transmission in economically advanced urban centers.

This study has limitations that constrain interpretation. Reliance on Baidu Index data, while providing consistent longitudinal coverage, captures only search-based attention and excludes social media engagement, offline participation, and international interest. The identified equations remain phenomenological: they describe rather than explain, and the coefficients lack direct correspondence to measurable social or cultural processes. The 11-year observation window, though substantial for digital data, may inadequately sample slow dynamical modes or miss regime transitions induced by structural changes in China’s digital landscape. Furthermore, our four-province sample, while spanning diverse regional contexts, cannot establish whether the observed patterns generalize across China’s remaining provinces or to other intangible cultural heritage domains.

From a methodological standpoint, the systematic quantitative discrepancy between Rosenstein and Wolf estimates observed here carries a broader lesson for chaos analysis of social time series: the Wolf method’s sensitivity to transient high-divergence events—precisely the spike-like attention surges characteristic of culturally driven phenomena—renders it susceptible to overestimation of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _{\max }$$\end{document} in the presence of non-stationarity, while the Rosenstein method’s ensemble averaging provides more conservative and interpretable estimates. Future studies applying Lyapunov-based chaos detection to social or cultural attention data are therefore advised to report both estimators and to treat their discrepancy as additional diagnostic information about attractor heterogeneity rather than as mere methodological inconsistency.

The comparative analysis presented here, while demonstrating SINDy’s advantages within the autoencoder latent space, evaluates only one-step-ahead prediction accuracy and residual distributions; multi-step forecast horizons and out-of-distribution generalization remain untested. Furthermore, the LSTM baseline was trained on moderate-length latent trajectories without extensive hyperparameter optimization, and its underperformance relative to linear methods should be interpreted with this constraint in mind rather than as a general indictment of recurrent architectures for cultural attention modeling.

Future extensions should pursue mechanistic grounding by incorporating exogenous covariates–policy interventions, media events, demographic shifts—into the SINDy framework, potentially revealing how external perturbations propagate through attention dynamics. Real-time implementation could enable anomaly detection for cultural heritage monitoring, flagging unusual attention patterns that might signal emerging threats or opportunities. Comparative application to other traditional practices—martial arts, traditional medicine, folk customs—would test whether the chaotic-periodic spectrum and regional heterogeneity observed here represent universal features of cultural attention or Tai Chi-specific phenomena. Finally, integration with agent-based modeling could bridge the gap between our phenomenological equations and the micro-level social.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study demonstrates that the temporal evolution of public attention toward traditional cultural practices like Tai Chi constitutes a complex dynamical system amenable to rigorous mathematical characterization. The HVG-SINDy Autoencoder framework offers a powerful toolkit for uncovering the hidden dynamical structures underlying apparently irregular time series, with implications extending beyond cultural heritage analytics to broader applications in social dynamics, public health surveillance, and complex systems science.The comparative evaluation against ARIMA, VAR, and LSTM baselines further confirms that the SINDy component achieves superior one-step prediction accuracy while simultaneously providing interpretable governing equations and chaos diagnostics that black-box and linear alternatives cannot deliver, establishing the integrated framework as both practically competitive and scientifically more informative than existing approaches.

An integrable bound for semilinear rough partial differential equations with unbounded diffusion coefficients
Alexandra Blessing Neamţu, Mazyar Ghani Varzaneh

Abstract
. This work develops moment bounds for the controlled rough path norm of the solution of semilinear rough partial differential equations. The novel aspects are two-fold: first we consider rough paths of low time regularity γ ∈ ( 1 / 4 , 1 / 2 ) \gamma\in(1/4,1/2) and second treat unbounded diffusion coefficients. To this aim we introduce a suitable notion of a controlled rough path according to a monotone scale of Banach spaces and innovative control functions.



Keywords
: controlled rough paths, rough partial differential equations, moment bounds.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2020)
: 60G22, 60L20, 60L50.

1.
Introduction

We investigate non-autonomous rough partial differential equations given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}{\textnormal{d}}y_{t}=[A(t)y_{t}+F(t,y_{t})]\penalty\ {\textnormal{d}}t+G(t,y_{t})\circ\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{t}\\
y_{0}\in E_{\alpha},\end{cases}

where
(E_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}
is a family of Banach spaces,
\mathbf{X}
is a rough path of regularity
\gamma\in(1/4,1/2)
and the diffusion coefficient
G
is linear and thus unbounded. The precise assumptions on the coefficients
A
,
F
and
G
will be stated in Sections
2
and
3
.

The main goal of this work is to obtain a-priori bounds for the moments of the control rough path norm of the mild solution of (
1.1
). For similar results for rough paths of regularity
\gamma\in(1/3,1/2)
and smooth, i.e. three-times Fréchet differentiable with bounded derivatives and bounded diffusion coefficients
G
, we refer to the recent works
[
GVR25
,
BNS24
,
BNGVS25
]
. Here we go beyond the setting of these works decreasing the regularity of the driving rough path and relaxing the boundedness assumption on
G
. The techniques developed in the previous works
[
GVR25
]
and
[
BNGVS25
]
in order to obtain such integrable bounds are limited to bounded diffusion coefficients
G
. The treatment of the unbounded case was left open in
[
BNGVS25
]
. Another development in comparison to the results in
[
GVR25
,
BNGVS25
]
is given by the spatial loss of regularity of
G
. For rough paths of regularity
\gamma\in(1/3,1/2)
it is well-known according to
[
GH19
,
GHN21
]
that
G
is allowed to use spatial regularity
\sigma<\gamma
.
This is due to the fact that the stochastic convolution increases the spatial regularity of the corresponding rough path by a parameter
\sigma
which is strictly less than the time regularity
\gamma
of the rough path. However, in order to ensure the integrability of the bound for the controlled rough path norm of the solution,
[
GVR25
,
BNGVS25
]
imposed that
\sigma\in[0,\frac{1-\gamma}{2})
. In this work we are able to overcome this limitation and treat the case
\sigma\in[0,\gamma)
as well.

We mention that the techniques developed in this work are also applicable to nonlinear diffusion coefficients
G
that are further assumed to be
{N+1}
-times Fréchet differentiable with bounded derivatives. Here
N=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{\gamma}\right\rfloor
represents the number of iterated integrals that have to be taken into account. For a better comprehension we only focus here on the linear case and work with the mild formulation of (
1.1
). For well-posedness results of semilinear rough stochastic partial differential equations with linear diffusion coefficients based on a variational approach we refer to
[
FNS20
]
.

This manuscript is structured as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the main concepts of controlled rough paths and control functions required in this setting. We specify that we consider
(p,\gamma)
-rough paths
\mathbf{X}
for
p<\gamma
and define suitable controlled rough paths according to
\mathbf{X}
. Our definition is a natural generalization of the controlled rough paths according to a monotone scale of interpolation spaces introduced in
[
GHN21
]
. Here we incorporate in Definition
2.6
more iterated integrals and decrease the spatial regularity of the Gubinelli derivatives and remainders according to the time regularity
\gamma
of the rough path
\mathbf{X}
. The main innovative aspect in comparison to
[
GHN21
]
is the replacement of the corresponding Hölder norms of the rough input
\mathbf{X}
and of the controlled rough path with suitable control functions. These are necessary in order to establish the desired integrable bounds and represent a main technical ingredient of our work. To our best knowledge, this is the first work that introduces controlled rough paths measuring their time regularity in suitable Banach spaces in terms of control functions instead of Hölder continuity. Afterwards we construct a rough integral for such controlled rough paths against
\mathbf{X}
. This is naturally achieved by means of a modified version of the Sewing lemma stated in Theorem
2.11
, keeping track of several parameters reflecting an interplay between the time regularity of
\mathbf{X}
, the loss of spatial regularity
\sigma<\gamma
, the number of iterated integrals and the parameter
p<\gamma
arising in the definition of the
(p,\gamma)
-rough path chosen such that
\sigma<p<\gamma
and of the control function. As already mentioned, the main technical challenge is to incorporate the control functions in our arguments.

Section
3
establishes the well-posedness of (
1.1
) based on the properties of the rough integral introduced in Section
2
. This can be achieved by a standard fixed-point argument in the space of controlled rough paths introduced in Section
2
. We provide the necessary steps required in order to set up this argument relying on the assumption that the diffusion coefficient
G
is linear. As already mentioned, one could incorporate a nonlinear term
G
which is
N+1
Fréchet differentiable with bounded derivatives. In this case, our techniques combined with a standard fixed-point argument as in
[
GH19
,
GHN21
]
entail the local well-posedness of (
1.1
). An additional boundedness assumption on the Fréchet derivatives up to order
N+1
as in
[
HN22
]
will imply the global-in-time existence. Such boundedness assumptions have been relaxed for rough differential equations in
[
LY25
]
. We refrain from providing the details of the fixed-point argument and focus instead on establishing moment bounds for (
1.1
).

Section
4
is inspired by the seminal paper
[
CLL13
]
that establishes the existence of moments of all order for the norm of differential equations driven by Gaussian rough paths. We extend this result in Theorem
4.17
to the in the infinite-dimensional case based on Borell’s inequality and a detailed analysis of the iterated integrals arising in our setting. Moreover we provide suitable estimates for the translated path in terms of the control function introduced in Section
2
. We emphasize that Theorem
4.17
is the first result on integrable bounds for the norm of the mild solution of (
1.1
) in the infinite dimensional setting and in the range of regularity
\gamma\in(1/4,1/2)
. As stated in
[
GH19
]
, moment bounds for the norm of the solution of a rough partial differential equation, are challenging to obtain. This aspect was addressed in
[
GVR25
]
for
\gamma\in(1/3,1/2)
and under the limitations on
G
and
\sigma
mentioned above.

We mention that such integrable bounds turned out to be very useful for the analysis of the long-time behavior of (
1.1
) driven by rough paths of regularity
\gamma\in(1/3,1/2)
by means of the random dynamical systems approach. These were employed in
[
BNS24
]
to study attractors for similar problems, including semilinear partial differential equations with rough boundary noise. Recently,
[
BNGVS25
]
additionally established the existence of moments of all order for the controlled rough path norm of the Jacobian of (
1.1
). This turned out to be a crucial step in the application of the multiplicative ergodic theorem that ensures the existence of Lyapunov exponents for (
1.1
). In conclusion, we believe that our approach can be applied for related dynamical aspects for (
1.1
) as considered in
[
GVR25
,
BNS24
,
BNGVS25
]
.

2.
Controlled rough paths and rough integrals

2.1.
Parabolic evolution families

We consider a family
(E_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}
of interpolation spaces, such that
E_{\beta}\hookrightarrow E_{\alpha}
for
\alpha<\beta
and the following interpolation inequality holds

(2.1)

\displaystyle|x|^{\alpha_{3}-\alpha_{1}}_{E_{\alpha_{2}}}\lesssim|x|^{\alpha_{3}-\alpha_{2}}_{E_{\alpha_{1}}}|x|^{\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}}_{E_{\alpha_{3}}},

for
\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha_{2}\leq\alpha_{3}
,
x\in E_{\alpha_{3}}
. Furthermore, we make some standard assumptions about the linear part of the equation (
1.1
), namely we assume that the non-autonomous operators
(A(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
are sectorial and satisfy a suitable Hölder-continuity in time. These conditions are known as the Kato-Tanabe assumptions. We refer to
[
BNGVS25
]
for more details and further references about this topic.

(A1)

The family
(A(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
consists of closed and densely defined operators
A(t):E_{1}\to E_{0}
on a time independent domain
D(A)=E_{1}
. Furthermore, they have bounded imaginary powers, i.e. there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{|s|\leq 1}\|(-A(t))^{is}\|_{\mathcal{L}(D(A))}\leq C

for every
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
, where
i
denotes the imaginary unit.

(A2)

There exists
\vartheta\in(\pi,\frac{\pi}{2})
and a constant
M
such that
\Sigma_{\vartheta}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\penalty\ :\penalty\ |\arg(z)|<\vartheta\}\subset R(A(t))
where
R(A(t))
denotes the resolvent set of
A(t)
and

\displaystyle\lVert(z-A(t))^{-1}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0})}\leq\frac{M}{1+|z|},

for all
z\in\Sigma_{\vartheta}
and
t\in[0,T]
.

(A3)

There exists a
\varrho\in(0,1]
such that

\displaystyle\lVert A(t)-A(s)\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{0})}\lesssim|t-s|^{\varrho},

for all
s,t\in[0,T]
.

Under these assumptions, we obtain an evolution family which is the non-autonomous equivalent of a
C_{0}
-semigroup.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(A(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
satisfy the Assumptions
(A1)
–
(A3)
. Then there exists a unique parabolic evolution family
(U_{s,t})_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T}
of linear operators
U_{t,s}:E_{0}\to E_{0}
such that the following properties holds:

i)

For all
0\leq r\leq s\leq t\leq T
we have

\displaystyle U_{t,s}U_{s,r}=U_{t,r}

as well as
U_{t,t}={\textnormal{Id}}_{E_{0}}
.

ii)

The mapping
(s,t)\mapsto U_{t,s}
is strongly continuous.

iii)

For
s\leq t
we have the identity

\displaystyle\frac{{\textnormal{d}}}{{\textnormal{d}}t}U_{t,s}=A(t)U_{t,s}.

From now on, we denote
E_{\alpha}:=D((-A(t))^{\alpha})
endowed with the norm
|\cdot|_{E_{\alpha}}:=\|(-A(t))^{\alpha}\cdot\|_{E_{0}}
and assume
(A(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
satisfies Assumption
(A1)
–
(A3)
on
(E_{\alpha},E_{\alpha+1})
for every
\alpha>0
. Then the resulting evolution family satisfies for
t>s
similar estimates as in the autonomous case, i.e. there exists constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|(U_{t,s}-\text{I})x|_{E_{\alpha}}&\leq C_{1}|t-s|^{\sigma_{1}}|x|_{E_{\alpha+\sigma_{1}}},\\
|U_{t,s}x|_{E_{\alpha+\sigma_{2}}}&\leq C_{2}|t-s|^{-\sigma_{2}}|x|_{E_{\alpha}},\end{split}

for
\sigma_{2}\in[k_{-},k_{+}]
and
\sigma_{1}\in[0,1]
, where
k_{-}<k_{+}
are fixed natural numbers and the constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(\alpha,\sigma_{1}),C_{2}=C_{2}(\alpha,\sigma_{2})
may also depend on
k_{-},k_{+}
. We mention that Assumption
(A1)
is necessary in order to ensure that the spaces
E_{\alpha}
are time-independent, see
[
BNGVS25
, Remark 2.5 i)]
.

2.3.
Rough path theory

In this subsection, we provide an overview of rough path theory. For technical reasons, our presentation slightly differs from the literature. In order to fix the ideas we first recall the setting of
[
GHN21
]
of controlled rough paths tailored to parabolic rough PDEs. For
d\geq 1
we consider a
d
-dimensional
\gamma
-Hölder rough path
\textbf{X}=(X,\mathbb{X})
, for
\gamma\in(1/3,1/2)
with
X_{0}=0
.
More precisely, we have for
T>0

\displaystyle X\in C^{\gamma}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})\penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{ and }\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathbb{X}\in C_{2}^{2\gamma}(\Delta_{[0,T]};\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\Delta_{J}\coloneqq\{(s,t)\in J\times J\penalty\ :\penalty\ s\leq t\}
for
J\subset\mathbb{R}
and the connection between
X
and
\mathbb{X}
is given by Chen’s relation

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}_{s,t}-\mathbb{X}_{s,u}-\mathbb{X}_{u,t}=(\delta X)_{s,u}\otimes(\delta X)_{u,t},

for
s\leq u\leq t
, where we write
(\delta X)_{s,u}:=X_{u}-X_{s}
for an arbitrary path. Here, we denote by
C^{\gamma}([0,T])
the space of
\gamma
-Hölder continuous paths, by
C^{2\gamma}_{2}(\Delta_{T})
the space of
2\gamma
-Hölder continuous two-parameter functions and by
\mathscr{C}^{\gamma}([0,T])
the space of
\gamma
-Hölder rough paths
\mathbf{X}=(X,\mathbb{X})
. In this setting, one can define the notion of a controlled rough path according to such a family of function spaces, as introduced in
[
GHN21
]
. We omit the time dependence if it is clear from the context, meaning that we write
C^{\gamma}(E_{\alpha})=C^{\gamma}([0,T];E_{\alpha})
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. We call a pair
(y,y^{\prime})
a controlled rough path if
(y,y^{\prime})\in C(E_{\alpha})\times(C(E_{\alpha-\gamma})\cap C^{\gamma}(E_{\alpha-2\gamma}))^{d}
and the remainder

(2.7)

\displaystyle(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}\mapsto R^{y}_{s,t}:=(\delta y)_{s,t}-y^{\prime}_{s}\circ(\delta X)_{s,t}

belongs to
C^{\gamma}(E_{\alpha-\gamma})\cap C^{2\gamma}(E_{\alpha-2\gamma})
, where
y^{\prime}_{s}\circ(\delta X)_{s,t}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}y_{s}^{i,\prime}(\delta X^{i})_{s,t}
. The component
y^{\prime}
is referred to as Gubinelli derivative of
y
.
The space of controlled rough paths is denoted by
\mathscr{D}^{\gamma}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha}([0,T])
and endowed with the norm
\|\cdot,\cdot\|_{\mathscr{D}^{\gamma}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha}([0,T])}
given by

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|y,y^{\prime}\|_{\mathscr{D}^{\gamma}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha}([0,T])}:=\left\|y\right\|_{\infty,E_{\alpha}}+\|y^{\prime}\|_{\infty,E^{d}_{\alpha-\gamma}}+\left[y^{\prime}\right]_{\gamma,E^{d}_{\alpha-2\gamma}}+\left[R^{y}\right]_{\gamma,E_{\alpha-\gamma}}+\left[R^{y}\right]_{2\gamma,E_{\alpha-2\gamma}}.\end{split}

Here we use for
y^{\prime}=(y^{i,\prime})_{1\leq i\leq d}
the notation
|y^{\prime}_{s}|_{E_{\alpha}^{d}}\coloneqq\sup\limits_{1\leq i\leq d}|y_{s}^{i,\prime}|_{\alpha}
.

In our case we need the following generalizations. First, in order to obtain integrable bounds we have to replace the Hölder norms of the random input by suitable control functions. Therefore we leave the framework of
\gamma
-Hölder rough paths and study
(p,\gamma)
-rough paths as given in Definition
2.4
.
Second, since we allow paths of regularity
\gamma\in(1/4,1/3)
and deal with higher order iterated integrals, we have to decrease the spatial regularity of the controlled rough path in
E_{\alpha}
accordingly. This is specified in Definition
2.6
for paths taking values in
E_{\alpha-\sigma}
for
0\leq\sigma<\gamma
and in Definition
2.14
for paths taking values in
E_{\alpha}
.

Definition 2.4
.

We assume that
d\geq 1
,
0\leq p<\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
and set
N=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{\gamma}\right\rfloor
. Since
\frac{1}{\gamma}
is not an integer we have that
N\gamma<1
. We further let
\mathbf{X}:\Delta_{[0,T]}\longrightarrow T^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a multiplicative functional such that for every
1\leq j\leq N
we have

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&W_{\Pi^{j}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}\colon\Delta_{[0,T]}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\\
&W_{\Pi^{j}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}(s,t)\coloneqq\sup_{\pi\in P[s,t]}\left\{\sum_{k}\frac{\left|(\Pi^{j}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau_{k},\tau_{k+1}}\right|^{\frac{1}{j(\gamma-p)}}}{(\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k})^{\frac{p}{\gamma-p}}}\right\}<\infty.\end{split}

In this case, we say that
\mathbf{X}
is a
(p,\gamma)
-rough path. We also set
W_{\mathbf{X},\gamma,p}(s,t):=\sum_{1\leq j\leq N}W_{\Pi^{j}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}(s,t)
. From the definition, we can easily verify that
W
is a control function, i.e., for
s<u<t
, we have the subadditivity property

W_{\mathbf{X},\gamma,p}(s,u)+W_{\mathbf{X},\gamma,p}(u,t)\leq W_{\mathbf{X},\gamma,p}(s,t).

We say that our
(p,\gamma)
-rough path is continuous if
W_{\mathbf{X},\gamma,p}
, is a continuous function. We also use
\mathscr{C}^{\gamma,p}([0,T])
to denote the space of
(p,\gamma)
-rough paths. We also refer to
\mathbf{X}
as a weakly geometric
(p,\gamma)
-rough path if in addition
\mathbf{X}
takes values in the free nilpotent group of step
N
over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For a similar definition we refer to
[
FV10
, Definition 9.15]
.

We can prove the following simple lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that
\mathbf{X}
is a
(p,\gamma)
-rough path. Then
\mathbf{X}
is also a
(0,\gamma)
-rough path. Moreover, the following inequality for the corresponding control functions holds

\displaystyle W_{\mathbf{X},\gamma,0}^{\gamma}(s,t)\leq(t-s)^{p}W_{\mathbf{X},\gamma,p}^{\gamma-p}(s,t).

Proof.

We recall (
2.9
) and assume that
\pi=\{\tau_{k}\}_{0\leq k\leq m}\in P[s,t]
. Then for
1\leq j\leq N
, it follows using Hölder’s inequality that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{k}\left|(\Pi^{j}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau_{k},\tau_{k+1}}\right|^{\frac{1}{j\gamma}}=\sum_{k}(\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k})^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}\frac{\left|(\Pi^{j}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau_{k},\tau_{k+1}}\right|^{\frac{1}{j\gamma}}}{(\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k})^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}}\\
&\quad\leq\left(\sum_{k}\left(\frac{\left|(\Pi^{j}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau_{k},\tau_{k+1}}\right|^{\frac{1}{j\gamma}}}{(\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k})^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-p}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-p}{\gamma}}\left(\sum_{k}(\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k})\right)^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}\leq(t-s)^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}W_{\Pi^{j}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{\frac{\gamma-p}{\gamma}}(s,t).\end{split}

From this point onward, the rest is straightforward.
∎

Now, given a
(p,\gamma)
-rough path
\mathbf{X}
, we naturally expect to define the spaces of controlled paths with respect to
\mathbf{X}
taking values in
E_{\alpha-\sigma}
for
0\leq\sigma<\gamma
in the spirit of Definition
2.3
. The next definition is a natural generalization of Definition
2.3
where
N=2
and
X\in\mathscr{C}^{\gamma}([0,T])
. Moreover, the Hölder regularities of the Gubinelli derivative and of the remainder specified in Definition
2.3
are now quantified in terms of suitable control functions. We emphasize that the spatial regularity of the Gubinelli derivatives and remainders is successively decreased according to the time regularity
\gamma
of the rough path
\mathbf{X}
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathbf{X}\in\mathscr{C}^{\gamma,p}([0,T])
and
0\leq\sigma<\gamma
. We say that
\boldsymbol{\xi}=(\xi^{i})_{0\leq i<N}
is controlled by
\mathbf{X}
in
\left(L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes j+1},E_{\alpha-j\gamma-\sigma}\right)\right)_{0\leq j<N}
if we have for every
0\leq i\leq j<N
that

\displaystyle\xi^{j}:[0,T]\to L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes j+1},E_{\alpha-j\gamma-\sigma}\right)=L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes j-i},L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes i+1},E_{\alpha-j\gamma-\sigma})\right)\right),

where we use the identification specified in Notation
2.2
(3).
For every
0\leq i,l\leq N
with
l-i>1

(2.11)

\displaystyle\delta\xi^{i}_{s,t}=\sum_{i<j<l}\xi^{j}_{s}\circ(\Pi^{j-i}(\mathbf{X}))_{s,t}+R^{i,l}_{s,t}

such that the following properties hold:

(1)

\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}\left|\xi^{i}_{\tau}\right|_{L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes i+1},E_{\alpha-i\gamma-\sigma}\right)}<\infty
for every
0\leq i<N
.

(2)

For every
0\leq i,l\leq N
with
l-i>1
we assume for every
[s,t]\subseteq[0,T]
that

\displaystyle W_{R^{i,l},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,1}(s,t):=\sup_{\pi\in P[s,t]}\left\{\sum_{k}\frac{\left|R^{i,l}_{\tau_{k},\tau_{k+1}}\right|_{L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes i+1},E_{\alpha-(l-1)\gamma-\sigma}\right)}^{\frac{1}{(l-i-1)(\gamma-p)}}}{(\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k})^{\frac{p}{\gamma-p}}}\right\}<\infty,

\displaystyle W_{R^{i,l},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,2}(s,t):=\sup_{\pi\in P[s,t]}\left\{\sum_{k}\frac{\left|R^{i,l}_{\tau_{k},\tau_{k+1}}\right|_{L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes i+1},E_{\alpha-l\gamma-\sigma}\right)}^{\frac{1}{(l-i)(\gamma-p)}}}{(\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k})^{\frac{p}{\gamma-p}}}\right\}<\infty.

This means that the previous terms are assumed to be control functions reflecting the regularity of the remainders of the controlled rough path, i.e. generalizing the last two terms in (
2.8
) in our setting.
Moreover, in the notation above we emphasize the dependence of the two control functions
W_{\cdot,1}
and
W_{\cdot,2}
of the corresponding remainders
R^{i,l}
on the spatial regularity of the controlled rough path
\alpha-\sigma
, time regularity
\gamma
of
\mathbf{X}
and the parameter
p<\gamma
.

(3)

For every
0\leq i<N
and every
[s,t]\subseteq[0,T]
we define
R^{i,i+1}_{s,t}:=\delta\xi^{i}_{s,t}
and assume that

\displaystyle W_{R^{i,i+1},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p}(s,t):=\sup_{\pi\in P[s,t]}\left\{\sum_{k}\frac{\left|\delta\xi^{i}_{\tau_{k},\tau_{k+1}}\right|_{L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes i+1},E_{\alpha-(i+1)\gamma-\sigma}\right)}^{\frac{1}{\gamma-p}}}{(\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k})^{\frac{p}{\gamma-p}}}\right\}<\infty.

For technical reasons, we adopt the convention that for
i=N-1

(2.12)

\displaystyle W_{R^{N-1,N},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,2}(s,t):=W_{R^{N-1,N},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p}(s,t),

which is a control functions based on our previous assumptions.

We denote by
\mathscr{D}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha-\sigma}\left([0,T]\right)
the space of all such elements equipped with the following norm

(2.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{\mathscr{D}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha-\sigma}\left([0,T]\right)}:=\sum W_{R^{i,i+1},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p}^{\gamma-p}(0,T)\\
&+\quad\sum\left(\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}\left|\xi^{i}_{\tau}\right|_{L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes i+1},E_{\alpha-i\gamma-\sigma}\right)}+W_{R^{i,l},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,1}^{(l-i-1)(\gamma-p)}(0,T)+W_{R^{i,l},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,2}^{(l-i)(\gamma-p)}(0,T)\right),\end{split}

where the summations are taken over all indices
i
and
l
in the range specified above, i.e. according to the restrictions on
i
and
l
that we assumed in (1)–(3).

Remark 2.7
.

(1)

A controlled rough path
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathscr{D}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha-\sigma}\left([0,T]\right)
according to
\mathbf{X}
consists of a sequence
(\xi^{i})_{0\leq i<N}
, where the component
\xi^{i}
is commonly referred to as the
i
-th Gubinelli derivative. Note that (
2.11
) is the generalization of (
2.7
) in our case.
In comparison to Definition
2.3
we assume here that the path component
\xi^{0}
takes values in
E_{\alpha-\sigma}
for
0\leq\sigma<\gamma
. We consider in Definition
2.14
a slightly modified space of controlled rough paths
\mathscr{\tilde{D}}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha}\left([0,T]\right)
where the corresponding path component
\tilde{\xi}^{0}
takes values in
E_{\alpha}
and construct the solution of (
1.1
) as an element of this space.

(2)

The reason why deal with two different spatial regularities
\alpha-\sigma
and
\alpha
is that the stochastic convolution improves the spatial regularity of the controlled rough paths by a parameter
\sigma
which is strictly less than the Hölder regularity of the random input
\gamma
. This is known for
\gamma\in(1/3,1/2)
from
[
GHN21
, Corollary 4.6]
and can be recovered in our setting in Proposition
2.16
.

(3)

The definition of the control functions together with the Minkowski inequality yield that (
2.13
) defines a norm on the space
\mathscr{D}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha-\sigma}\left([0,T]\right)
. In conclusion
\mathscr{D}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha-\sigma}\left([0,T]\right)
endowed with the norm (
2.13
) is a Banach space.

(4)

We decided to emphasize the dependence of the path, Gubinelli derivatives and remainders on the tensor product spaces in Definition
2.6
to keep track of the corresponding index of the tensor product and of the index with which we decrease the spatial regularity. Alternatively one could set for e.g.
E_{\alpha-j\gamma-\sigma}^{d}=L(\mathbb{R}^{d},E_{\alpha-j\gamma-\sigma})
as in
[
GH19
]
and accordingly for the other elements in the space of controlled rough paths.

2.4.
Sewing lemma

We naturally expect to define a rough integral for every
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathscr{D}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha-\sigma}\left([0,T]\right)
against
\mathbf{X}
. The main idea is to apply a variant of the sewing lemma. The main technical aspect is to incorporate the definition of the controlled rough path and the control functions in the arguments of the sewing lemma. Before stating this result, let us first begin with some algebraic identities and introduce further notations.

Definition 2.8
.

We consider an arbitrary time interval
[s,t]\subseteq[0,T]
. For
m\geq 1
we set

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{m}[s,t]\coloneqq\left\{s+\frac{2n+1}{2^{m}}(t-s)\,:\,n=0,\ldots,2^{m-1}-1\right\}.

For
\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{m}[s,t]
with
\tau=s+\frac{2n+1}{2^{m}}(t-s)
we define

\displaystyle\tau_{-}\coloneqq s+\frac{2n}{2^{m}}(t-s)\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{+}\coloneqq s+\frac{2n+2}{2^{m}}(t-s).

Lemma 2.9
.

We assume that
[\tau_{1},\tau_{3}]\subseteq[s,t]
, let
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathscr{D}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha-\sigma}\left([0,T]\right)
and set

\displaystyle\Xi^{\tau_{1},\tau_{3}}_{s,t}\coloneqq\sum_{0\leq j<N}U_{t,\tau_{1}}\xi^{j}_{\tau_{1}}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau_{1},\tau_{3}}.

Then we have for
\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}<\tau_{3}
that

(2.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Xi^{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}_{s,t}+\Xi^{\tau_{2},\tau_{3}}_{s,t}-\Xi^{\tau_{1},\tau_{3}}_{s,t}&=\sum_{0\leq j<N}U_{t,\tau_{1}}R^{j,N}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau_{2},\tau_{3}}\\
&+\sum_{0\leq j<N}(U_{t,\tau_{2}}-U_{t,\tau_{1}})\xi^{j}_{\tau_{2}}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau_{2},\tau_{3}}.\end{split}

Proof.

The proof is rather algebraic. First, we note that by the definition of
\boldsymbol{\xi}
, in particular (
2.11
) and the multiplicity of
\mathbf{X}
, the following identity holds true

(2.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{0\leq j<N}\left(\xi^{j}_{\tau_{1}}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}+\xi^{j}_{\tau_{2}}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau_{2},\tau_{3}}-\xi^{j}_{\tau_{1}}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau_{1},\tau_{3}}\right)\\
&=\sum_{0\leq j<N}R^{j,N}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau_{2},\tau_{3}}.\end{split}

By definition

\displaystyle\Xi^{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}_{s,t}+\Xi^{\tau_{2},\tau_{3}}_{s,t}-\Xi^{\tau_{1},\tau_{3}}_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq j<N}U_{t,\tau_{1}}\left(\xi^{j}_{\tau_{1}}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}+\xi^{j}_{\tau_{2}}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau_{2},\tau_{3}}-\xi^{j}_{\tau_{1}}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau_{1},\tau_{3}}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{0\leq j<N}(U_{t,\tau_{2}}-U_{t,\tau_{1}})\xi^{j}_{\tau_{2}}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau_{2},\tau_{3}}.

Therefore, it is sufficient to apply (
2.15
) to prove the claim.
∎

We are ready to define the integral, which is a first step in developing the solution theory of (
1.1
). The main idea is to modify the sewing lemma accordingly in order to incorporate the control functions specified in Definition
2.6
. This is technically challenging since we cannot work with the Hölder norms of the rough input or of the corresponding controlled rough path, since they prevent us from obtaining a final integrable bound for the norm of the solution of (
1.1
).

We first make the following assumption reflecting an interplay between the regularity of the noise
\gamma
, the loss of spatial regularity
\sigma<\gamma
, the number of iterated integrals
N
and the parameter
p<\gamma
arising in the definition of the control functions. In particular, the following assumption will imply that
\sigma<p<\gamma
.

Assumption 2.10
.

In the following we always assume that

(2.16)

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma+N\gamma}{N+1}<p<\gamma.

For
0\leq i\leq N
we further define

\displaystyle P_{i}:=(i+1)p-i\gamma-\sigma,

which is always positive due to (
2.16
). Moreover, condition (
2.16
) obviously yields that
\sigma<p
.

Now we can state the sewing lemma and define the rough integral for controlled rough paths
\boldsymbol{\xi}
as given in Definition
2.6
against rough paths
\mathbf{X}
. We first recall that the number of iterated integrals
N
and the regularity of the rough path
\gamma
satisfy the following relation
(N+1)\gamma>1
and
N\gamma<1
.

Theorem 2.11
.

We assume that
\mathbf{X}\in\mathscr{C}^{\gamma,p}([0,T])
and let
0\leq\sigma<\gamma
. Let Assumption
2.10
hold and let

\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathscr{D}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha-\sigma}\left([0,T]\right).

For every
[s,t]\subseteq[0,T]
,
m\geq 0
and
0\leq n\leq 2^{m}
, we set

\tau^{n}_{m}:=s+\frac{n}{2^{m}}(t-s).

Then, the following limit exists

(2.17)

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}U_{t,v}\xi_{v}\circ\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{v}:=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\left(\sum_{0\leq n<2^{m}}\sum_{0\leq j<N}U_{t,\tau^{n}_{m}}\xi^{j}_{\tau^{n}_{m}}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau^{n}_{m},\tau^{n+1}_{m}}\right).

Moreover, for
0\leq l\leq N
, we have

(2.18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|\int_{s}^{t}U_{t,v}\xi_{v}\circ\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{v}-\sum_{0\leq j<N}U_{t,s}\xi^{j}_{s}\circ(\Pi^{{j}+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{s,t}\right|_{E_{\alpha-l\gamma}}\lesssim(t-s)^{lp}\times\\
&\sum_{0\leq j<N}W_{\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{(j+1)(\gamma-p)}(s,t)\left((t-s)^{P_{N}+l(\gamma-p)}W_{R^{j,N},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,2}^{(N-j)(\gamma-p)}(s,t)+(t-s)^{P_{j}+l(\gamma-p)}\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}\left|\xi^{j}_{\tau}\right|_{\alpha-j\gamma-\sigma}\right),\end{split}

where
W_{\Pi^{j}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}
is specified in (
2.9
).

Proof.

Throughout the proof we use that
\mathbf{\xi}\in\mathscr{D}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha-\sigma}([0,T])
as specified in Definition
2.6
and
the regularizing effect of parabolic evolution families given in (
2.2
). The idea of the proof is similar to the classical sewing lemma
[
GHN21
, Theorem 4.1]
which involves Hölder norms
and
[
GVR25
, Lemma 2.5]
that replaces the Hölder norms of the rough path
\mathbf{X}
by control functions for rough paths of regularity
\gamma\in(1/3,1/2)
. Here we do not only have to deal with the control functions in (
2.9
) but additionally have to incorporate the norm (
2.13
) defined on the space of controlled rough paths. We divide the proof into four steps for better readability.

Step one:

Let us fix
[s,t]\subseteq[0,T]
. For
m\geq 1
, we define

\Gamma^{m}_{s,t}:=\sum_{0\leq n<2^{m}}\sum_{0\leq j<N}U_{t,\tau^{n}_{m}}\xi^{j}_{\tau^{n}_{m}}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau^{n}_{m},\tau^{n+1}_{m}}.

In particular

\Gamma^{0}_{s,t}=\Xi^{s,t}_{s,t}=\sum_{0\leq j<N}U_{t,s}\xi^{j}_{s}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{s,t}.

Then, from Lemma
2.9
and with some direct computations, we have for
m\geq 1

\displaystyle\Gamma^{m}_{s,t}-\Gamma^{m-1}_{s,t}=\sum_{\tau\in D_{m}[s,t]}\sum_{0\leq j<N}\left(U_{t,\tau_{-}}R^{j,N}_{\tau_{-},\tau}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau,\tau_{+}}+(U_{t,\tau}-U_{t,\tau_{-}})\xi^{j}_{\tau}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau,\tau_{+}}\right).

Our aim is to estimate the following terms for every
l\leq N

(2.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{m\geq 1}\left|\Gamma^{m}_{s,t}-\Gamma^{m-1}_{s,t}\right|_{E_{\alpha-l\gamma}}\leq\underbrace{\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{\tau\in D_{m}[s,t]}\sum_{0\leq j<N}\left|U_{t,\tau_{-}}R^{j,N}_{\tau_{-},\tau}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau,\tau_{+}}\right|_{E_{\alpha-l\gamma}}}_{:=E_{l}(s,t)}\\
&+\underbrace{\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{\tau\in D_{m}[s,t]}\sum_{0\leq j<N}\left|(U_{t,\tau}-U_{t,\tau_{-}})\xi^{j}_{\tau}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau,\tau_{+}}\right|_{E_{\alpha-l\gamma}}}_{:=Q_{l}(s,t)}.\end{split}

Our objective is to estimate
E_{l}(s,t)
and
Q_{l}(s,t)
which will be done in the following steps.

Step two:
We begin with the estimate for
E_{l}(s,t)
.
For
\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{m}[s,t]
, we have
\tau-\tau_{-}=\tau_{+}-\tau=\frac{t-s}{2^{m}}
. Thus for
j<N
we derive based on the regularizing properties of the parabolic evolution family given in (
2.2
) combined with Definition
2.6
and (
2.9
)

(2.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|U_{t,\tau_{-}}R^{j,N}_{\tau_{-},\tau}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau,\tau_{+}}\right|_{E_{\alpha-l\gamma}}\leq\left|U_{t,\tau_{-}}R^{j,N}_{\tau_{-},\tau}\right|_{L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes j+1},E_{\alpha-l\gamma}\right)}(\tau_{+}-\tau)^{p(j+1)}W_{\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{(j+1)(\gamma-p)}(\tau,\tau_{+})\\
&\lesssim(t-\tau_{-})^{\gamma(l-N)-\sigma}(\tau-\tau_{-})^{p(N-j)}(\tau_{+}-\tau)^{p(j+1)}W_{R^{j,N},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,2}^{(N-j)(\gamma-p)}(\tau_{-},\tau)W_{\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{(j+1)(\gamma-p)}(\tau,\tau_{+})\\
&=(t-\tau_{-})^{\gamma(l-N)-\sigma}(\tau-\tau_{-})^{p(N-j)}(\tau_{+}-\tau)^{p(j+1)}\left(W_{R^{j,N},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,2}^{\frac{N-j}{N+1}}(\tau_{-},\tau)W_{\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{\frac{j+1}{N+1}}(\tau,\tau_{+})\right)^{(N+1)(\gamma-p)}\\
&=(t-s)^{P_{N}+l\gamma}(\frac{1}{2^{m}})^{(N+1)p}\Big(\frac{t-\tau_{-}}{t-s}\Big)^{(l-N)\gamma-\sigma}\left(W_{R^{j,N},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,2}^{\frac{N-j}{N+1}}(\tau_{-},\tau)W_{\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{\frac{j+1}{N+1}}(\tau,\tau_{+})\right)^{(N+1)(\gamma-p)}.\end{split}

Since
(N+1)(\gamma-p)<1
, it follows from the Hölder inequality that

(2.21)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{m}[s,t]}\sum_{0\leq j<N}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{m}}\Big)^{(N+1)p}\Big(\frac{t-\tau_{-}}{t-s}\Big)^{(l-N)\gamma-\sigma}\left(W_{R^{j,N},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,2}^{\frac{N-j}{N+1}}(\tau_{-},\tau)W_{\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{\frac{j+1}{N+1}}(\tau,\tau_{+})\right)^{(N+1)(\gamma-p)}\\
&\leq\sum_{0\leq j<N}\Bigg(\Big(\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{m}[s,t]}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{m}}\Big)^{\frac{(N+1)p}{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}}\Big(\frac{t-\tau_{-}}{t-s}\Big)^{\frac{(l-N)\gamma-\sigma}{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}}\Big)^{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}\bigtimes\\
&\quad\Big(\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{m}[s,t]}W_{R^{j,N},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,2}^{\frac{N-j}{N+1}}(\tau_{-},\tau)W_{\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{\frac{j+1}{N+1}}(\tau,\tau_{+})\Big)^{(N+1)(\gamma-p)}\Bigg).\end{split}

Recall that
W_{\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}
and
W_{R^{j,N},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,2}
are assumed to be controlled functions as introduced in Definition
2.4
and Definition
2.6
. Therefore, by using the Hölder inequality again, we have

(2.22)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{m}[s,t]}W_{R^{j,N},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,2}^{\frac{N-j}{N+1}}(\tau_{-},\tau)W_{\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{\frac{j+1}{N+1}}(\tau,\tau_{+})\right)^{(N+1)(\gamma-p)}\\
&\leq\left(\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{m}[s,t]}W_{R^{j,N},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,2}(\tau_{-},\tau)\right)^{(N-j)(\gamma-p)}\left(\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{m}[s,t]}W_{\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}(\tau,\tau_{+})\right)^{(j+1)(\gamma-p)}\\
&\leq W_{R^{j,N},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,2}^{(N-j)(\gamma-p)}(s,t)W_{\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{(j+1)(\gamma-p)}(s,t).\end{split}

Consequently, from (
2.19
), (
2.20
) and (
2.22
) we derive a bound for
E_{l}
as

(2.23)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&E_{l}(s,t)\lesssim(t-s)^{P_{N}+l\gamma}\left(\sum_{0\leq j<N}W_{R^{j,N},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,2}^{(N-j)(\gamma-p)}(s,t)W_{\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{(j+1)(\gamma-p)}(s,t)\right)\times\\
&\left(\sum_{m\geq 1}\left(\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{m}[s,t]}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{m}}\Big)^{\frac{(N+1)p}{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}}\Big(\frac{t-\tau_{-}}{t-s}\Big)^{\frac{(l-N)\gamma-\sigma}{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}}\right)^{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}\right).\end{split}

We must ensure that the last term in the previous formula is finite. Note that for every
\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{m}[s,t]
we can find
0\leq n<2^{m-1}
such that

\frac{t-\tau_{-}}{t-s}=1-\frac{n}{2^{m-1}}.

We further have that
\frac{t-\tau_{-}}{t-s}=1-\frac{n}{2^{m-1}}\geq\frac{1}{2^{m}}.
Recalling that Assumption
2.10
holds, we choose

0<\epsilon<\min\left\{\frac{P_{N}}{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)},\frac{(N+1)\gamma-1}{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}\right\}.

Consequently, for every
\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{m}[s,t]
and
0\leq l<N
we obtain

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{2^{m}}\Big)^{\frac{(N+1)p}{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}}\Big(\frac{t-\tau_{-}}{t-s}\Big)^{\frac{(l-N)\gamma-\sigma}{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{1}{2^{m}}\Big)^{1+\epsilon}\Big(\frac{t-\tau_{-}}{t-s}\Big)^{\frac{(l-N)\gamma+(N+1)p-\sigma}{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}-1-\epsilon}=\Big(\frac{1}{2^{m}}\Big)^{1+\epsilon}\Big(\frac{t-\tau_{-}}{t-s}\Big)^{\frac{P_{N}+l\gamma}{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}-1-\epsilon}.

Thus, keeping in mind the definitions of
P_{N}
and
\tau_{-}
, we further infer that

(2.24)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{m\geq 1}\left(\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{m}[s,t]}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{m}}\Big)^{\frac{(N+1)p}{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}}\Big(\frac{t-\tau_{-}}{t-s}\Big)^{\frac{(l-N)\gamma-\sigma}{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}}\right)^{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}\\
&\hskip 10.00002pt\ignorespaces=\sum_{m\geq 1}\left(\sum_{0\leq n<2^{m-1}}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{m}}\Big)^{\frac{(N+1)p}{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}}\Big(1-\frac{n}{2^{m-1}}\Big)^{\frac{(l-N)\gamma-\sigma}{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}}\right)^{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}\\
&\hskip 20.00003pt\ignorespaces\leq\sum_{m\geq 1}\left(\sum_{0\leq n<2^{m-1}}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{m}}\Big)^{1+\epsilon}\Big(1-\frac{n}{2^{m-1}}\Big)^{\frac{P_{N}+l\gamma}{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}-1-\epsilon}\right)^{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}\\
&\hskip 30.00005pt\ignorespaces\leq\sum_{m\geq 1}\left(\frac{1}{2^{m}}\right)^{\epsilon\left(1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)\right)}\left(\sum_{0\leq n<2^{m-1}}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{m}}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{n}{2^{m-1}}\Big)^{\frac{P_{N}+l\gamma}{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}-1-\epsilon}\right)^{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}\\
&\hskip 40.00006pt\ignorespaces\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{1}(1-x)^{\frac{P_{N}+l\gamma}{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}-1-\epsilon}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)}<\infty.\end{split}

Therefore, from (
2.23
) and (
2.24
), we obtain

(2.25)

\displaystyle E_{l}(s,t)\lesssim(t-s)^{lp}(t-s)^{P_{N}+l(\gamma-p)}\sum_{0\leq j<N}W_{R^{j,N},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,2}^{(N-j)(\gamma-p)}(s,t)W_{\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{(j+1)(\gamma-p)}(s,t).

Step three:
Now, we estimate
Q_{l}(s,t)
given in (
2.19
). Since most of the calculations are similar to the previous step, we do not present all details. Recalling that
\xi^{j}
takes values in
L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes j+1},E_{\alpha-j\gamma-\sigma}\right)
, we have

(2.26)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|(U_{t,\tau}-U_{t,\tau_{-}})\xi^{j}_{\tau}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau,\tau_{+}}\right|_{E_{\alpha-l\gamma}}\\
&\quad\lesssim\left|(U_{t,\tau}-U_{t,\tau_{-}})\xi^{j}_{\tau}\right|_{L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes j+1},E_{\alpha-l\gamma}\right)}(\tau_{+}-\tau)^{p(j+1)}W_{\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{(j+1)(\gamma-p)}(\tau,\tau_{+}).\end{split}

We choose a parameter
\theta
such that

(2.27)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&N\gamma<\theta<1,\\
&1-\gamma+\sigma<\theta.\end{split}

This choice is possible since
N\gamma<1
and
\sigma<\gamma
, and it ensures that the following conditions are satisfied for every
0\leq j<N
and every
0\leq l\leq N
:

•

\alpha-\theta<\alpha-j\gamma-\sigma
,

•

\alpha-\theta<\alpha-l\gamma
.

Then, from (
2.2
), we conclude for this choice of
\theta
that

(2.28)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|(U_{t,\tau}-U_{t,\tau_{-}})\xi^{j}_{\tau}\right|_{L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes j+1},E_{\alpha-l\gamma}\right)}=\left|U_{t,\tau}(I-U_{\tau,\tau_{-}})\xi^{j}_{\tau}\right|_{L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes j+1},E_{\alpha-l\gamma}\right)}\\
&\quad\lesssim\left|U_{t,\tau}\right|_{L(E_{\alpha-\theta},E_{\alpha-l\gamma})}\left|(I-U_{\tau,\tau_{-}})\xi^{j}_{\tau}\right|_{L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes j+1},E_{\alpha-\theta}\right)}\\
&\qquad\lesssim(t-\tau)^{l\gamma-\theta}(\tau-\tau_{-})^{\theta-j\gamma-\sigma}\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}\left|\xi^{j}_{\tau}\right|_{L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes j+1},E_{\alpha-j\gamma-\sigma}\right)}.\end{split}

So, from (
2.26
) and (
2.28
)

(2.29)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&Q_{l}(s,t)=\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{\tau\in D_{m}[s,t]}\sum_{0\leq j<N}\left|(U_{t,\tau}-U_{t,\tau_{-}})\xi^{j}_{\tau}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau,\tau_{+}}\right|_{E_{\alpha-l\gamma}}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{0\leq j<N}(t-s)^{lp+l(\gamma-p)+P_{j}}\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}\left|\xi^{j}_{\tau}\right|_{\alpha-j\gamma-\sigma}\times\\
&\quad\left(\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{\tau\in D_{m}[s,t]}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{m}}\Big)^{P_{j}+\theta}\Big(\frac{t-\tau_{-}}{t-s}\Big)^{l\gamma-\theta}W_{\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{(j+1)(\gamma-p)}(\tau,\tau_{+})\right).\end{split}

Since
(j+1)(\gamma-p)<1
holds for all
0\leq j<N
, and by a similar argument to that used in (
2.21
) and (
2.22
), we conclude that

(2.30)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{\tau\in D_{m}[s,t]}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{m}}\Big)^{P_{j}+\theta}\Big(\frac{t-\tau_{-}}{t-s}\Big)^{l\gamma-\theta}W_{\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{(j+1)(\gamma-p)}(\tau,\tau_{+})\\
&\leq\sum_{m\geq 1}\left(\sum_{\tau\in D_{m}[s,t]}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{m}}\Big)^{\frac{P_{j}+\theta}{1-(j+1)(\gamma-p)}}\Big(\frac{t-\tau_{-}}{t-s}\Big)^{\frac{l\gamma-\theta}{1-(j+1)(\gamma-p)}}\right)^{1-(j+1)(\gamma-p)}\times\\
&\qquad\left(\sum_{\tau\in D_{m}[s,t]}W_{\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}(\tau,\tau_{+})\right)^{(j+1)(\gamma-p)}\\
&\hskip 30.00005pt\ignorespaces\leq W_{\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{(j+1)(\gamma-p)}(s,t)\sum_{m\geq 1}\left(\sum_{\tau\in D_{m}[s,t]}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{m}}\Big)^{\frac{P_{j}+\theta}{1-(j+1)(\gamma-p)}}\Big(\frac{t-\tau_{-}}{t-s}\Big)^{\frac{l\gamma-\theta}{1-(j+1)(\gamma-p)}}\right)^{1-(j+1)(\gamma-p)}.\end{split}

We need to verify that the summation term does not grow large as the size of the partition becomes smaller. The reasoning closely resembles the previous case, i.e., (
2.24
), though a few minor adjustments need to be considered. Our choice of
\theta
in (
2.27
) implies that

(2.31)

\displaystyle\frac{P_{j}+\theta}{1-(j+1)(\gamma-p)}>1.

Thus, for every
\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{m}[s,t]
, since
\frac{1}{2^{m}}\leq\frac{t-\tau_{-}}{t-s}
and
p>\frac{\sigma+N\gamma}{(N+1)}
, we get

(2.32)

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{2^{m}}\Big)^{\frac{P_{j}+\theta}{1-(j+1)(\gamma-p)}}\Big(\frac{t-\tau_{-}}{t-s}\Big)^{\frac{l\gamma-\theta}{1-(j+1)(\gamma-p)}}\leq\Big(\frac{1}{2^{m}}\Big)^{1+\epsilon}\Big(\frac{t-\tau_{-}}{t-s}\Big)^{\frac{P_{j}+l\gamma}{1-(j+1)(\gamma-p)}-1-\epsilon},

where

\displaystyle 0<\epsilon<\min\left\{\frac{P_{N}}{1-(N+1)(\gamma-p)},\min\left\{\frac{P_{j}+\theta}{1-(j+1)(\gamma-p)}-1\right\}_{0\leq j<N}\right\}.

Note that this choice of
\epsilon
is valid due to (
2.31
) and Assumption
2.10
. This implies that for every
0\leq j<N
and every
0\leq l\leq N
we have

\displaystyle\frac{P_{j}+l\gamma}{1-(j+1)(\gamma-p)}-1-\epsilon+1=\frac{P_{j}+l\gamma}{1-(j+1)(\gamma-p)}-\epsilon>0.

Consequently, as in (
2.24
) we derive

(2.33)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{m\geq 1}\left(\sum_{\tau\in D_{m}[s,t]}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{m}}\Big)^{\frac{P_{j}+\theta}{1-(j+1)(\gamma-p)}}\Big(\frac{t-\tau_{-}}{t-s}\Big)^{\frac{l\gamma-\theta}{1-(j+1)(\gamma-p)}}\right)^{1-(j+1)(\gamma-p)}\\
&\quad\lesssim\sum_{m\geq 1}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{m}}\Big)^{\epsilon(1-(j+1)(\gamma-p))}\left(\sum_{0\leq n<2^{m-1}}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{m}}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{n}{2^{m-1}}\Big)^{\frac{P_{j}+l\gamma}{1-(j+1)(\gamma-p)}-1-\epsilon}\right)^{1-(j+1)(\gamma-p)}\\
&\qquad\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{1}(1-x)^{\frac{P_{j}+l\gamma}{1-(j+1)(\gamma-p)}-1-\epsilon}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}x\right)^{1-(j+1)(\gamma-p)}<\infty.\end{split}

Note that the estimate (
2.33
) depends on
j
, but we take the maximal bound over all
0\leq j<N
. Now combining (
2.29
) and (
2.30
) and (
2.33
) we derive

(2.34)

\displaystyle Q_{l}(s,t)\lesssim(t-s)^{lp}\sum_{0\leq j<N}(t-s)^{l(\gamma-p)+P_{j}}W_{\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{(j+1)(\gamma-p)}(s,t)\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}\left|\xi^{j}_{\tau}\right|_{\alpha-j\gamma-\sigma}.

Step four:
Now we have all the estimates required in order to prove our claim. From (
2.19
), (
2.25
) and (
2.34
), for every
0\leq l\leq N
, we conclude

(2.35)

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 1}\left|\Gamma^{m}_{s,t}-\Gamma^{m-1}_{s,t}\right|_{\alpha-l\gamma}<\infty.

Therefore, we can infer that the following limit exists

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}U_{t,v}\xi_{v}\circ\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{v}:=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\left(\sum_{0\leq n<2^{m}}\sum_{0\leq j<N}U_{t,\tau^{n}_{m}}\xi^{j}_{\tau^{n}_{m}}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{\tau^{n}_{m},\tau^{n+1}_{m}}\right)=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\Gamma^{m}_{s,t}.

Furthermore, we have for every
0\leq l\leq N
that

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int_{s}^{t}U_{t,v}\xi_{v}\circ\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{v}-\underbrace{\sum_{0\leq j<N}U_{t,s}\xi^{j}_{s}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{s,t}}_{\Gamma^{0}_{s,t}}\Bigg|_{E_{\alpha-l\gamma}}\leq\sum_{m\geq 1}\left|\Gamma^{m}_{s,t}-\Gamma^{m-1}_{s,t}\right|_{E_{\alpha-l\gamma}}.

Now, from (
2.19
), (
2.25
), and (
2.34
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int_{s}^{t}U_{t,v}\xi_{v}\circ\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{v}-\underbrace{\sum_{0\leq j<N}U_{t,s}\xi^{j}_{s}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{s,t}}_{\Gamma^{0}_{s,t}}\Bigg|_{\alpha-l\gamma}\leq E_{l}(s,t)+Q_{l}(s,t)\lesssim(t-s)^{lp}\times

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq j<N}W_{\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{(j+1)(\gamma-p)}(s,t)\left((t-s)^{P_{N}+l(\gamma-p)}W_{R^{j,N},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,2}^{(N-j)(\gamma-p)}(s,t)+(t-s)^{P_{j}+l(\gamma-p)}\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}\left|\xi^{j}_{\tau}\right|_{\alpha-j\gamma-\sigma}\right).

This proves the statement, i.e., (
2.18
).
∎

Remark 2.13

Moreover, for every
0\leq i,l\leq N
with
l-i>1

(2.40)

\displaystyle\delta\tilde{\xi}^{i}_{s,t}=\sum_{i<j<l}\tilde{\xi}^{j}_{s}\circ(\Pi^{j-i}(\mathbf{X}))_{s,t}+\tilde{R}^{i,l}_{s,t}

such that the following properties hold:

(1)

\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}\left|\tilde{\xi}^{i}_{\tau}\right|_{L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes i},E_{\alpha-i\gamma}\right)}<\infty

(2)

For every
1\leq i,l\leq N
with
l-i>1

\displaystyle\tilde{W}_{\tilde{R}^{i,l},\alpha,\gamma,p,2}(s,t):=\sup_{\pi\in P[s,t]}\left\{\sum_{k}\frac{\left|\tilde{R}^{i,l}_{\tau_{k},\tau_{k+1}}\right|_{L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes i},E_{\alpha-l\gamma}\right)}^{\frac{1}{(l-i)(\gamma-p)}}}{(\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k})^{\frac{p}{\gamma-p}}}\right\}<\infty.

(3)

For every
1\leq i<N
and every

\displaystyle\tilde{W}_{\tilde{R}^{i,i+1},\alpha,\gamma,p}(s,t):=\sup_{\pi\in P[s,t]}\left\{\sum_{k}\frac{\left|\delta\tilde{\xi}^{i}_{\tau_{k},\tau_{k+1}}\right|_{L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes i},E_{\alpha-(i+1)\gamma}\right)}^{\frac{1}{\gamma-p}}}{(\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k})^{\frac{p}{\gamma-p}}}\right\}<\infty.

\displaystyle\tilde{W}_{\tilde{R}^{N-1,N},\alpha,\gamma,p,2}(s,t):=\tilde{W}_{\tilde{R}^{N-1,N},\alpha,\gamma,p}(s,t),

We use

(2.42)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\tilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\|_{\tilde{\mathscr{D}}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha}\left([0,T]\right)}:=\sum\tilde{W}_{\tilde{R}^{i,i+1},\alpha,\gamma,p}^{\gamma-p}(0,T)\\
&+\sum\left(\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}\left|\tilde{\xi}^{i}_{\tau}\right|_{L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes i},E_{\alpha-i\gamma}\right)}+\tilde{W}_{\tilde{R}^{i,l},\alpha,\gamma,p,1}^{(l-i-1)(\gamma-p)}(0,T)+\tilde{W}_{\tilde{R}^{i,l},\alpha,\gamma,p,2}^{(l-i)(\gamma-p)}(0,T)\right),\end{split}

where the summations are taken over all indices

and

Previously, we defined in Theorem
2.11
for every
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathscr{D}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha-\sigma}

\mathbf{X}

2.14

2.1

Proposition 2.15
.

Assume
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathscr{D}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha-\sigma}\left([0,T]\right)
. For
1\leq i<N
, we define

\displaystyle\tilde{\xi}^{i}:=[0,T]\to L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes i},L(\mathbb{R}^{d},E_{\alpha-i\gamma})\right)=L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes i+1},E_{\alpha-i\gamma}\right),

\displaystyle\tilde{\xi}^{i}_{\tau}:=\xi^{i-1}_{\tau}.

For
1\leq i,l\leq N
with
l-i>1
, we set
\tilde{R}^{i,l}(s,t):=R^{i-1,l-1}
.
Then for
[s,t]\subseteq[0,T]
, the following properties hold.

I)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}\left|\tilde{\xi}^{i}_{\tau}\right|_{L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes i},E_{\alpha-i\gamma}\right)}\lesssim|\xi^{i-1}_{s}|_{L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes i},E_{\alpha-(i-1)\gamma-\sigma}\right)}\\
&+\left(W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{\gamma-\sigma}(s,t)+(t-s)^{\gamma-\sigma}+(t-s)^{\frac{p(\gamma-\sigma)}{\gamma}}\right)\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{\mathscr{D}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha-\sigma}\left([0,T]\right)}.\end{split}

II)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{W}_{\tilde{R}^{i,l},\alpha,\gamma,p,1}(s,t)\lesssim(t-s)^{\frac{p(\gamma-\sigma)}{\gamma}}\left(W_{R^{i-1,l-1},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,1}^{(l-i-1)(\gamma-p)}(s,t)\right)^{\frac{\sigma}{\gamma}}\left(W_{R^{i-1,l-1},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,2}^{(l-i)(\gamma-p)}(s,t)\right)^{\frac{\gamma-\sigma}{\gamma}}.\end{split}

III)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{W}_{\tilde{R}^{i,l},\alpha,\gamma,p,2}(s,t)&\lesssim\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}|\xi^{l-1}_{\tau}|_{\alpha-(l-1)\gamma-\sigma}W_{\Pi^{l-i}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{(l-i)(\gamma-p)}(s,t)\\
&+(t-s)^{\frac{p(\gamma-\sigma)}{\gamma}}\left(W_{R^{i-1,l},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,1}^{(l-i)(\gamma-p)}(s,t)\right)^{\frac{\sigma}{\gamma}}\left(W_{R^{i-1,l},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,2}^{(l-i+1)(\gamma-p)}(s,t)\right)^{\frac{\gamma-\sigma}{\gamma}}.\end{split}

IV)

For
1\leq i<N

\displaystyle\tilde{W}_{\tilde{R}^{i,i+1},\alpha,\gamma,p}(s,t)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}|\xi_{\tau}^{i}|_{\alpha-i\gamma-\sigma}W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{\gamma-p}(s,t)

\displaystyle+(t-s)^{\frac{p(\gamma-\sigma)}{\gamma}}\left(W_{R^{i-1,i+1},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,1}^{\gamma-p}(s,t)\right)^{\frac{\sigma}{\gamma}}\left(W_{R^{i-1,i+1},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,2}^{2(\gamma-p)}(s,t)\right)^{\frac{\gamma-\sigma}{\gamma}}.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, we suppress for notational simplicity the tensor component of
\mathbf{\xi}
and only write the index of the interpolation space in order to keep track of the spatial regularity.

We begin with
I)
.
Note that

(2.43)

\displaystyle\delta\xi^{i-1}_{s,t}=\xi^{i}_{s}\circ(\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X}))_{s,t}+R^{i-1,i+1}_{s,t}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\left|\delta{\xi}^{i-1}_{s,t}\right|_{\alpha-i\gamma-\sigma}\lesssim(t-s)^{p}W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{\gamma-p}(s,t)\left|\xi^{i}_{s}\right|_{\alpha-i\gamma-\sigma}+(t-s)^{p}W_{R^{i-1,i+1},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,1}^{\gamma-p}(s,t).

So, by the interpolation inequality (
2.1
) for
\tau\in[s,t]
we have

\displaystyle|\tilde{\xi}^{i}_{\tau}|_{\alpha-i\gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\xi^{i-1}_{s}|_{\alpha-i\gamma+\gamma-\sigma}+\left|\delta{\xi}^{i-1}_{s,\tau}\right|_{\alpha-i\gamma-\sigma}^{\frac{\gamma-\sigma}{\gamma}}\left|\delta{\xi}^{i-1}_{s,\tau}\right|_{\alpha-i\gamma+\gamma-\sigma}^{\frac{\sigma}{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\xi^{i-1}_{s}|_{\alpha-i\gamma+\gamma-\sigma}+\left((t-s)^{\frac{p(\gamma-\sigma)}{\gamma}}W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{\frac{(\gamma-p)(\gamma-\sigma)}{\gamma}}(s,t)+(t-s)^{\frac{p(\gamma-\sigma)}{\gamma}}\right)\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{\mathscr{D}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha-\sigma}\left([0,T]\right)}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\xi^{i-1}_{s}|_{\alpha-i\gamma+\gamma-\sigma}+\left(W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{\gamma-\sigma}(s,t)+(t-s)^{\gamma-\sigma}+(t-s)^{\frac{p(\gamma-\sigma)}{\gamma}}\right)\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{\mathscr{D}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha-\sigma}\left([0,T]\right)}.

We now show
II)
. First, note that for every
\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}
, we have

\tilde{R}^{i,l}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}=R^{i-1,l-1}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}

as specified in Definition
2.14
. Since we want to estimate
\tilde{W}_{\tilde{R}^{i,l},\alpha,\gamma,p,1}
, our strategy is first to estimate
\tilde{R}^{i,l}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}
on an arbitrary interval
[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]
and then extend this result to a partition of
[s,t]
.
Again from the interpolation inequality (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\left|R^{i-1,l-1}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}\right|_{\alpha-l\gamma+\gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|R^{i-1,l-1}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}\right|_{\alpha-l\gamma+2\gamma-\sigma}^{\frac{\sigma}{\gamma}}\left|R^{i-1,l-1}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}\right|_{\alpha-l\gamma+\gamma-\sigma}^{\frac{\gamma-\sigma}{\gamma}}.

Therefore we conclude

(2.44)

\displaystyle\frac{\left|R^{i-1,l-1}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}\right|_{\alpha-l\gamma+\gamma}^{\frac{1}{(l-i-1)(\gamma-p)}}}{(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})^{\frac{p}{\gamma-p}}}\lesssim\left(\frac{\left|R^{i-1,l-1}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}\right|_{\alpha-l\gamma+2\gamma-\sigma}^{\frac{1}{(l-i-1)(\gamma-p)}}}{(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})^{\frac{p}{\gamma-p}}}\right)^{\frac{\sigma}{\gamma}}\left(\frac{\left|R^{i-1,l-1}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}\right|_{\alpha-l\gamma+\gamma-\sigma}^{\frac{1}{(l-i-1)(\gamma-p)}}}{(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})^{\frac{p}{\gamma-p}}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-\sigma}{\gamma}}.

Consequently, for every arbitrary partition
\pi=\{s=\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\ldots<\tau_{m}=t\}
, we derive using the interpolation inequality (
2.1
) and (
2.44
)) that

(2.45)

Now we focus on the rough integral.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let

and assume the setting of Theorem

\displaystyle\tilde{\xi}^{0}_{a}:=\int_{s}^{a}U_{a,v}\xi_{v}\circ\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{v}.

In addition, for

, we set

\displaystyle\tilde{\xi}^{j}_{a}:=\xi^{j-1}_{a}.

Furthermore, for
[a,b]\subseteq[s,t]
and

\displaystyle\tilde{R}^{0,l}_{a,b}:=\delta\tilde{\xi}^{0}_{a,b}-\sum_{0<j<l}\tilde{\xi}^{j}_{a}\circ(\Pi^{j}(\mathbf{X}))_{a,b}.

Similar to Definition

, we define

\displaystyle\tilde{W}_{\tilde{R}^{0,l},\alpha,\gamma,p,1}(s,t):=

\displaystyle\sup_{\pi\in P[s,t]}\left\{\sum_{k}\frac{\left|\tilde{R}^{0,l}_{\tau_{k},\tau_{k+1}}\right|_{E_{\alpha-(l-1)\gamma}}^{\frac{1}{(l-1)(\gamma-p)}}}{(\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k})^{\frac{p}{\gamma-p}}}\right\},\ \tilde{W}_{\tilde{R}^{0,l},\alpha,\gamma,p,2}(s,t):=\sup_{\pi\in P[s,t]}\left\{\sum_{k}\frac{\left|\tilde{R}^{0,l}_{\tau_{k},\tau_{k+1}}\right|_{E_{\alpha-l\gamma}}^{\frac{1}{l(\gamma-p)}}}{(\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k})^{\frac{p}{\gamma-p}}}\right\},

Then we have

(2.50)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\max\left\{\tilde{W}_{\tilde{R}^{0,l},\alpha,\gamma,p,1}(s,t),\tilde{W}_{\tilde{R}^{0,l},\alpha,\gamma,p,2}(s,t),\tilde{W}_{\tilde{R}^{0,1},\alpha,\gamma,p}(s,t)\right\}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{0\leq j<N}\left(W_{\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{(j+1)(\gamma-p)}(s,t)W_{R^{j,N},\alpha-\sigma,\gamma,p,2}^{(N-j)(\gamma-p)}(s,t)+W_{\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{(j+1)(\gamma-p)}(s,t)\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}\left|\xi^{j}_{\tau}\right|_{\alpha-j\gamma-\sigma}\right).\end{split}

Proof.

Throughout the proof, we follow the same convention as in Proposition
2.15
for denoting norms. Specifically, we omit the tensor component and only indicate the spatial regularity of the Banach scale. We assume that
[a,b]\subseteq[s,t]
, and let
\pi=\{s=\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\cdots<\tau_{m}=t\}
be an arbitrary partition of
[s,t]
. Moreover, recalling from Assumption
2.10
we have that for every
0\leq j\leq N

\displaystyle P_{j}=(j+1)p-j\gamma-\sigma>0.

For every
1\leq l\leq N
we have

\displaystyle\delta\tilde{\xi}^{0}_{a,b}=\int_{s}^{b}U_{b,v}\xi_{v}\circ\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{v}-\int_{s}^{a}U_{a,v}\xi_{v}\circ\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{v}

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{s}^{a}(U_{b,v}-U_{a,v})\xi_{v}\circ\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{v}+\int_{a}^{b}U_{b,v}\xi_{v}\circ\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{v}=(U_{b,a}-I)\int_{s}^{a}U_{a,v}\xi_{v}\circ\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{v}+\int_{a}^{b}U_{b,v}\xi_{v}\circ\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{v}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\ignorespaces=\underbrace{(U_{b,a}-I)\int_{s}^{a}U_{a,v}\xi_{v}\circ\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{v}}_{C_{l}(u,b)}+\underbrace{\left(\int_{a}^{b}U_{b,v}\xi_{v}\circ\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{v}-\sum_{0\leq j<N}U_{b,a}\xi^{j}_{a}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{a,b}\right)}_{D_{l}(a,b)}

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt\ignorespaces+\underbrace{\sum_{0\leq j<l-1}\left((U_{b,a}-I)\xi^{j}_{a}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{a,b}\right)}_{F_{l}(a,b)}+U_{b,a}\xi^{l-1}_{a}\circ(\Pi^{l}(\mathbf{X}))_{a,b}

\displaystyle\hskip 40.00006pt\ignorespaces+\underbrace{\sum_{l-1<j<N}U_{b,a}\xi^{j}_{a}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{a,b}}_{H_{l}(a,b)}+\underbrace{\sum_{0\leq j<l-1}\xi^{j}_{a}\circ(\Pi^{j+1}(\mathbf{X}))_{a,b}}_{=\sum_{0<j<l}\tilde{\xi}^{j}_{a}(\Pi^{j}\circ(\mathbf{X}))_{a,b}}.

Consequently, for

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\tilde{R}^{0,l}_{u,b}|_{\alpha-(l-r)\gamma}&\leq|C_{l}(u,b)|_{\alpha-(l-r)\gamma}+|D_{l}(u,b)|_{\alpha-(l-r)\gamma}\\

We now estimate each of these terms separately.

A)

, it follows from Lemma

\displaystyle:=\int_{s}^{a}U_{a,v}\xi_{v}\circ\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{v},\quad j=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\xi}^{j}_{a}

\displaystyle:=\xi^{j-1}_{a},\quad 1\leq j\leq N.

\displaystyle\tilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}=(\tilde{\xi}^{i})_{0\leq i<N}\in\tilde{\mathscr{D}}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha}\left([0,T]\right).

Remark 2.18
.

The dependency of

After defining a suitable integral in our setting, we point out that the existence of a solution of (

Assumption 3.1
.

We assume

(1)

There exists
\delta\in[0,1)
such that

. In particular, we have the following Lipschitz condition:

\displaystyle|F_{t}(z)-F_{t}(y)|_{E_{\alpha-\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq L_{F}|z-y|_{E_{\alpha}},

for all

. In particular, this implies that

|F_{t}(y)|_{E_{\alpha-\delta}}\leq C_{F}(1+|y|_{E_{\alpha}}),

(2)

For
0\leq j<N

G:=[0,T]\times E_{\alpha-j\gamma}\to L\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},E_{\alpha-j\gamma-\sigma}\right)
. For every

,

is a continuous linear map such that

\displaystyle C_{G}:=\max_{0\leq j<N}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|G_{t}(.)\|_{L\left(E_{\alpha-j\gamma},L\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},E_{\alpha-j\gamma-\sigma}\right)\right)}<\infty.

For every

, we assume that the map

\displaystyle G(.,z):E_{\alpha}\to E_{\alpha-\sigma},\quad t\mapsto G(t,z)

is Hölder continuous with parameter

. In particular, there exists a constant

such that for every

and

\displaystyle\left|G_{t}(z)-G_{s}(z)\right|_{E_{\alpha-\sigma}}\leq M_{G}(t-s)^{N\gamma}|z|_{E_{\alpha}},

\displaystyle\left|G_{t}(z)\right|_{E_{\alpha-\sigma}}\leq M_{G}|z|_{E_{\alpha}}.

Definition 3.2
.

Under Assumption
3.1

,

\displaystyle G^{\circ k}_{t}:E_{\alpha-j\gamma}\to L\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes k},E_{\alpha-(k+j-1)\gamma-\sigma}\right),

Remark 3.5
.

1)

Based on the bounds obtained in the previous Lemmas, one can perform a fixed-point argument to prove well-posedness of (
1.1
) for a linear diffusion coefficient
G
.

2)

Provided that
\mathbf{X}
is weakly geometric, one can prove analogue results to Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.4
for a nonlinear term
G
which is
N
times Fréchet differentiable. Moreover, if
G
is assumed to be
N+1
times Fréchet differentiable, then one can prove the local well-posedness of (

) by a fixed-point argument similar to
[

, Theorem 5.1]
. Under additional boundedness assumptions on the

[

]
.

3)

For nonlinear
G
, even in the finite-dimensional case, the assumption that
\mathbf{X}
is weakly geometric is important in guaranteeing the existence and uniqueness of the solution, see
[
FV10
, Theorem 10.14 and Theorem 10.26]
. The main reason is given by the definition of the composition of the rough path with nonlinear function
G
. In this case, the Taylor expansion of
G
allows us to canonically define the new Gubinelli derivatives in terms of the original Gubinelli derivatives of the path. In this context, the weak geometricity of
\mathbf{X}
is important for ensuring certain symmetries in the expansion.

We need the following definition, which we refer to as greedy points. This definition was first introduced in the seminal work
[
CLL13
]
to obtain integrable a priori bounds for rough differential equations.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
I=[a,b]
and
\chi>0
be a given treshold. The sequence of greedy points, denoted by
\{\tau^{I}_{m,\mathbf{X}}(\chi)\}_{m\geq 0}
, is constructed as follows: we set
\tau^{I}_{0,\mathbf{X}}(\chi)=a
, and define subsequent points recursively by

(3.5)

\displaystyle\tau^{I}_{m+1,\mathbf{X}}(\chi)\coloneqq\sup\big\{\tau:\tau^{I}_{m,\mathbf{X}}(\chi)\leq\tau\leq b,\ \ W_{\mathbf{X},\gamma,p}^{\gamma-p}(\tau^{I}_{m,\mathbf{X}}(\chi),\tau)\leq\chi\big\}.

Finally, we define the quantity

(3.6)

\displaystyle\tilde{N}(I,\chi,\mathbf{X})\coloneqq\inf\{m>0:\tau_{m,\mathbf{X}}^{I}(\chi)=b\},

which represents the smallest number
m
such that the sequence reaches the endpoint
b
. Note that, since
W_{\mathbf{X},\gamma,p}
is assumed to be continuous, for every
m<\tilde{N}(I,\chi,\mathbf{X})
, the definition yields

W_{\mathbf{X},\gamma,p}^{\gamma-p}(\tau^{I}_{m-1,\mathbf{X}}(\chi),\tau^{I}_{m,\mathbf{X}}(\chi))=\chi
. Moreover, for
m=\tilde{N}(I,\chi,\mathbf{X})
, we have

W_{\mathbf{X},\gamma,p}^{\gamma-p}(\tau^{I}_{m-1,\mathbf{X}}(\chi),\tau^{I}_{m,\mathbf{X}}(\chi))\leq\chi.

Now, we turn the solution of equation (

we further derive based on Assumption

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}\left|\int_{s}^{\tau}U_{\tau,u}F(u,\tilde{\xi}_{u}^{0})\mathrm{d}u\right|_{E_{\alpha}}&\leq\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}\int_{s}^{\tau}\left|U_{\tau,u}F(u,\tilde{\xi}_{u}^{0})\right|_{E_{\alpha}}\mathrm{d}\tau\\

For

Using again Assumption

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&|\text{I}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})|_{E_{\alpha-l\gamma}}\leq\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\left|U_{\tau_{2},u}F(u,\tilde{\xi}_{u}^{0})\right|_{E_{\alpha-l\gamma}}\mathrm{d}u\lesssim\left(1+\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}|\tilde{\xi}_{\tau}^{0}|_{E_{\alpha}}\right)\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}(\tau_{2}-u)^{-\max\{0,\delta-l\gamma\}}\mathrm{d}u\\
&\lesssim(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})^{1-\max\{0,\delta-l\gamma\}}\left(1+\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}|\tilde{\xi}_{\tau}^{0}|_{E_{\alpha}}\right)=(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})^{l\gamma+\min\{1-l\gamma,1-\delta\}}\left(1+\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}|\tilde{\xi}_{\tau}^{0}|_{E_{\alpha}}\right).\end{split}

This yields that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\frac{|\text{I}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})|_{E_{\alpha-l\gamma}}^{\frac{1}{l(\gamma-p)}}}{(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})^{\frac{p}{\gamma-p}}}\lesssim(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})(t-s)^{\frac{\min\{1-l\gamma,1-\delta\}}{{l(\gamma-p)}}}\left(1+\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}|\tilde{\xi}_{\tau}^{0}|_{E_{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{1}{l(\gamma-p)}}.

Again for
1\leq l\leq N
, it follows from (
2.2
) and (
3.10
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\text{II}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})|_{E_{\alpha-l\gamma}}&\lesssim(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})^{l\gamma}\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}\left|\int_{s}^{\tau}U_{\tau_{1},u}F(u,\tilde{\xi}_{u}^{0})\mathrm{d}u\right|_{E_{\alpha-l\gamma}}\\
&\lesssim(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})^{l\gamma}(t-s)^{\min\{1,1-\delta+l\gamma\}}\left(1+\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}|\tilde{\xi}_{\tau}^{0}|_{E_{\alpha}}\right),\end{split}

which gives

(3.12)

\displaystyle\frac{|\text{II}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})|_{E_{\alpha-l\gamma}}^{\frac{1}{l(\gamma-p)}}}{(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})^{\frac{p}{\gamma-p}}}\lesssim(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})(t-s)^{\frac{\min\{1,1-\delta+l\gamma\}}{{l(\gamma-p)}}}\left(1+\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}|\tilde{\xi}_{\tau}^{0}|_{E_{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{1}{l(\gamma-p)}}.

We can represent the path
Z
as
\mathbf{Z}\in\tilde{\mathscr{D}}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha}([s,t])
in the sense of Definition
2.14
. Therefore, from (
3.8
), (
3.9
), (
3.11
), and (
3.12
), the following estimate holds for every interval
[s,t]\subseteq[0,T]
with
t-s\leq 1
and
{\delta_{1}}:=\min\left\{1-\delta,1-N\gamma\right\}

(3.13)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{Z}\|_{\tilde{\mathscr{D}}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha}([s,t])}\lesssim(t-s)^{\delta_{1}}\left(1+\|\tilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\|_{\tilde{\mathscr{D}}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha}\left([s,t]\right)}\right).

By a simpler argument for the first term in (
3.7
), namely the path

\displaystyle\tau\mapsto Y_{\tau}:=U_{\tau,s}y,

we obtain

(3.14)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\tilde{\mathscr{D}}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha}([s,t])}\lesssim|y|_{E_{\alpha}}.

As mentioned in Remark
3.5
, using a standard fixed-point argument similar to
[
GHN21
, Theorem 5.1]
in the space of controlled rough paths
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\in\tilde{\mathscr{D}}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha}\left([0,T]\right)
, one can show that the solution exists and is unique. We refrain from providing the details of this argument, since based on the previous deliberations, this follows as in
[
GHN21
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Definition 3.9
.

Assume that
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\in\tilde{\mathscr{D}}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha}\left([s,t]\right)
is the unique solution of (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
3.7
with initial datum
y\in E_{\alpha}
. For
\tau\in[s,t]
, we define

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(s,\tau,y):=\tilde{\xi}^{0}_{\tau}.

Note that the uniqueness of solutions yields in particular the flow property. Namely
\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(s,s,y)=y
and for
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in[s,t]
with
\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2}
, we have

(3.15)

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(s,\tau_{2},y)=\phi_{\mathbf{X}}\left(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(s,\tau_{1},y)\right).

Using the mild formulation of (
1.1
) we have

(3.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(s,\tau_{2},y)&=U_{\tau_{2},\tau_{1}}\left(\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(s,\tau_{1},y)\right)+\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}U_{\tau_{2},u}F\left(u,\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(s,u,y)\right)\mathrm{d}u\\
&+\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}U_{\tau_{2},u}G\left(u,\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(s,u,y)\right)\circ\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{u}.\end{split}

By definition, the Gubinelli derivatives are obtained through the composition of the solution and
G
. Therefore, we adopt the convention of using
(\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(s,\tau,y))_{\tau\in[s,t]}
to represent the controlled path, including all its derivatives. Accordingly, we write

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(s,\cdot,y)\|_{\tilde{\mathscr{D}}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha}\left([s,t]\right)}

as part of this convention. We use the same convention for the path component of the rough integral

\displaystyle\tau\mapsto\int_{a}^{\tau}U_{\tau,u}G\left(u,\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(s,u,y)\right)\circ\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{u}.

Now, we can present the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let Assumption
3.1
hold, and assume further the setting of Theorem
2.11
. Additionally,
(\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,\tau,y))_{0\leq\tau\leq T}
is the solution of the rough PDE (
1.1
) on the interval
[0,\tau]
in the sense of Definition
3.9
. Then there exist
\chi
and
L_{2}
with
0<\chi\leq L_{2}<1
, both independent of
\mathbf{X}
(but dependent on
F
and
G
) such that for all intervals
[s,t]\subseteq[0,T]
with
t-s\leq L_{2}
, the following bound holds

(3.17)

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}\left|\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,\tau,y)\right|_{E_{\alpha}}\lesssim\left|\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,s,y)\right|_{E_{\alpha}}P_{1}(\mathbf{X},[s,t],\chi)+P_{2}(\mathbf{X},[s,t],\chi),

where
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are greater than one and

(3.18)

\displaystyle\max\bigg\{P_{1}(\mathbf{X},[s,t],\chi),P_{2}(\mathbf{X},[s,t],\chi)\bigg\}\lesssim\exp\left(\tilde{N}([s,t],\chi,\mathbf{X})\log(3L)\right).

Proof.

The main idea is to estimate the solution over small intervals, which also depend on
\mathbf{X}
, and then apply a concatenation argument, particularly using the flow property, to derive the desired bound over an interval with positive length and independent of
\mathbf{X}
. Let
[s,t]\subseteq[0,T]
with
t-s\leq 1
. This interval will be further refined in the sequel. Let
[a,b]\subseteq[s,t]
be arbitrary and consider the controlled path defined on
[a,b]
given by

(3.19)

\displaystyle\tau\to K_{\tau}:=\int_{a}^{\tau}U_{\tau,u}G\left(u,\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,u,y)\right)\circ\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{u}.

Recalling that
G
is linear and
\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,\cdot,y)
is the solution of (
1.1
), we conclude from Lemma
3.4
that

(3.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|K\|_{\tilde{\mathscr{D}}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha}\left([a,b]\right)}\lesssim|\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,a,y)|_{E_{\alpha}}\\
&\quad+\left[(b-a)^{\beta}+W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{(\gamma-\sigma)}(a,b)+\sum_{0<j\leq N}W_{\Pi^{j}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{j(\gamma-p)}(a,b)\right]\|\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,\cdot,y)\|_{\tilde{\mathscr{D}}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha}\left([a,b]\right)}.\end{split}

Then, (
3.13
), (
3.14
), and (
3.16
) entail that

(3.21)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,\cdot,y)\|_{\tilde{\mathscr{D}}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha}\left([a,b]\right)}\\
&\quad\lesssim|\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,a,y)|_{E_{\alpha}}+(b-a)^{{\delta_{1}}}\left(1+\|\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,\cdot,y)\|_{\tilde{\mathscr{D}}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha}\left([a,b]\right)}\right)+\left\|K\right\|_{\tilde{\mathscr{D}}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha}\left([a,b]\right)},\end{split}

By setting
\delta_{2}:=\min\{\delta_{1},\beta\}
and applying (
3.20
) and (
3.21
), we obtain a deterministic constant
L\geq 1
(i.e., independent of
\mathbf{X}
but dependent on
F
and
G
),
such that the following inequality holds

(3.22)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,\cdot,y)\|_{\tilde{\mathscr{D}}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha}\left([a,b]\right)}\leq L\Bigg[1+|\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,a,y)|_{E_{\alpha}}\\
&+\Big[(b-a)^{\delta_{2}}+W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{(\gamma-\sigma)}(a,b)+\sum_{0<j\leq N}W_{\Pi^{j}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{j(\gamma-p)}(a,b)\Big]\|\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,\cdot,y)\|_{\tilde{\mathscr{D}}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha}\left([a,b]\right)}\Bigg].\end{split}

We recall that

(3.23)

\displaystyle W_{\mathbf{X},\gamma,p}(a,b):=\sum_{0<j\leq N}W_{\Pi^{j}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}(a,b).

We refine the interval
[s,t]
and choose
0<\chi\leq t-s<1
such that

(3.24)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&L(t-s)^{\delta_{2}}\leq\frac{1}{3},\\
&(N+1)L\chi\leq\frac{1}{3}.\end{split}

By choosing this value of
\chi
, we generate the greedy points
\{\tau^{[s,t]}_{m,\mathbf{X}}(\chi)\}_{m\geq 0}
as defined in Definition
3.6
. From Definition
2.10
we know that
\gamma-\sigma>\gamma-p
. Moreover, the definition of greedy points, (
3.23
), and the conditions imposed on
\chi
obviously yield for every
1\leq m\leq\tilde{N}([s,t],\chi,\mathbf{X})
and
0<j\leq N
that

•

W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{(\gamma-\sigma)}\left(\tau^{[s,t]}_{m-1,\mathbf{X}}(\chi),\tau^{[s,t]}_{m,\mathbf{X}}(\chi)\right)\leq\chi
,

•

W_{\Pi^{j}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{j(\gamma-p)}\left(\tau^{[s,t]}_{m-1,\mathbf{X}}(\chi),\tau^{[s,t]}_{m,\mathbf{X}}(\chi)\right)\leq\chi
.

Therefore

\displaystyle L\bigg[\left(\tau^{[s,t]}_{m,\mathbf{X}}(\chi)-\tau^{[s,t]}_{m-1,\mathbf{X}}(\chi)\right)^{\delta_{2}}

\displaystyle+W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{(\gamma-\sigma)}\left(\tau^{[s,t]}_{m-1,\mathbf{X}}(\chi),\tau^{[s,t]}_{m,\mathbf{X}}(\chi)\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{0<j\leq N}W_{\Pi^{j}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{j(\gamma-p)}\left(\tau^{[s,t]}_{m-1,\mathbf{X}}(\chi),\tau^{[s,t]}_{m,\mathbf{X}}(\chi)\right)\bigg]\leq\frac{2}{3}.

From this observation, and by plugging in the interval
[\tau^{[s,t]}_{m-1,\mathbf{X}}(\chi),\tau^{[s,t]}_{m,\mathbf{X}}(\chi)]
for
[a,b]
into (
3.22
) and using (
3.24
), we deduce that for every
1\leq m\leq\tilde{N}([s,t],\chi,\mathbf{X})

(3.25)

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,\cdot,y)\|_{\tilde{\mathscr{D}}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha}\left(\left[\tau^{[s,t]}_{m-1,\mathbf{X}}(\chi),\tau^{[s,t]}_{m,\mathbf{X}}(\chi)\right]\right)}\leq 3L+3L\left|\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,\tau^{[s,t]}_{m-1,\mathbf{X}}(\chi),y)\right|_{E_{\alpha}}.

This in particular yields that

\displaystyle\left|\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,\tau^{[s,t]}_{m,\mathbf{X}}(\chi),y)\right|_{E_{\alpha}}\leq 3L+3L\left|\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,\tau^{[s,t]}_{m-1,\mathbf{X}}(\chi),y)\right|_{E_{\alpha}}.

It is now sufficient to a discrete Gronwall lemma to conclude that

(3.26)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\max_{0\leq m\leq\tilde{N}([s,t],\chi,\mathbf{X})}\left|\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,\tau^{[s,t]}_{m,\mathbf{X}}(\chi),y)\right|_{E_{\alpha}}\\
&\quad\leq\underbrace{\frac{\exp\left(\tilde{N}([s,t],\chi,\mathbf{X})\log(3L)\right)-1}{3L-1}}_{P_{0}(\mathbf{X},[s,t],\chi)}+\left(1+\left|\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,s,y)\right|_{E_{\alpha}}\right)\underbrace{\exp\left(\tilde{N}([s,t],\chi,\mathbf{X})\log(3L)\right)}_{P_{1}(\mathbf{X},[s,t],\chi)}.\end{split}

From (
3.25
) we obtain

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq m\leq\tilde{N}([s,t],\chi,\mathbf{X})}

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,\cdot,y)\|_{\tilde{\mathscr{D}}^{\gamma,p}_{\mathbf{X},\alpha}\left(\left[\tau^{[s,t]}_{m-1,\mathbf{X}}(\chi),\tau^{[s,t]}_{m,\mathbf{X}}(\chi)\right]\right)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq 3L\left(1+\max_{0\leq m\leq\tilde{N}([s,t],\chi,\mathbf{X})}\left|\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,\tau^{[s,t]}_{m,\mathbf{X}}(\chi),y)\right|_{E_{\alpha}}\right).

This combined with equation (
3.26
) implies that

(3.27)

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}\left|\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,\tau,y)\right|_{E_{\alpha}}\lesssim\left|\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(0,s,y)\right|_{E_{\alpha}}P_{1}(\mathbf{X},[s,t],\chi)+\underbrace{1+P_{0}(\mathbf{X},[s,t],\chi)+P_{1}(\mathbf{X},[s,t],\chi)}_{P_{2}(\mathbf{X},[s,t],\chi)}.

The conclusion of our argument is inequality (
3.27
), which holds for every subinterval
[s,t]
with an interval length upper bounded by

\displaystyle L_{2}:=\left(\frac{1}{3L}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sigma_{2}}},

due to (
3.24
). This completes the proof.

∎

4.
Gaussian Rough Paths

All our previous results are entirely deterministic and do not require any probabilistic tools. As standard in the rough path theory, we provide now a probabilistic framework and focus
on Gaussian processes that can be enhanced to a rough path
\mathbf{X}
of regularity
\gamma\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2})
. This level of regularity is of particular interest in the context of fractional Brownian motion with Hurst index
H\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2})
, as it allows for the canonical enhancement of this process to be a rough path.

Let us first introduce an assumption, which is used throughout this section. For more background on Gaussian measures, we refer to
[
Led96
]
.

Assumption 4.1
.

We assume that
X=(X^{k})_{1\leq k\leq d}
is a centered Gaussian process with independent components and continuous trajectories, taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Accordingly, we assume that
(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{H},\mu)
is the abstract Wiener space associated with this process. Here
\mathcal{W}:=C([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathcal{H}\subset\mathcal{W}
is the Cameron–Martin space which is a Hilbert space. Furthermore, we assume that the following statements hold.

(1)

For
\gamma\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2})\setminus\{\frac{1}{3}\}
this process can be enhanced to a weakly geometric
(p,\gamma)
-rough path
\mathbf{X}
. We emphasize the source of randomness writing
\mathbf{X}(\omega)
, where with a slight abuse of notation
\Pi^{1}\mathbf{X}(\omega)=X(\omega)
.

(2)

We assume that on a set of full measure (still denoted by
\mathcal{W}
), the control function

\displaystyle W_{\mathbf{X}(\omega),\gamma,p}\colon\Delta_{[0,T]}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},

which is defend in Definition
2.4
, is continuous for every
\omega\in\mathcal{W}
.

(3)

We assume that there exists
\gamma^{\prime}>{\gamma}
such that
\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}>1
. Moreover, for every
h\in\mathcal{H}

(4.1)

\displaystyle W_{\mathbf{h},\gamma^{\prime},0}(0,T)<\infty.

Here, by
\mathbf{h}
we mean the natural
(p,\gamma)
-rough path, which can be defined as the Young integral of
h
with respect to itself.

(4)

The following uniform inequality holds for every
h\in\mathcal{H}

(4.2)

\displaystyle W_{\mathbf{h},\gamma^{\prime},p}(0,T)\lesssim|h|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{\gamma^{\prime}-p}},

where
|\cdot|_{\mathcal{H}}
denotes the norm on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.

In the following we first revisit Borell’s inequality which is a well-known result in the theory of Gaussian measures.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{H},\mu)
be an abstract Wiener space, and let
A\subset\mathcal{W}
be a measurable Borel set with
\mu(A)>0
. Let
a\in(-\infty,+\infty]
be chosen such that

\mu(A)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{a}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=:\Phi(a).

Let
\mathcal{K}
represent the unit ball in
\mathcal{H}
and let
\mu_{\star}
be the inner measure corresponding to
\mu
. Then, for all
r\geq 0
we have the inequality

\mu_{\star}(A+r\mathcal{K})\coloneqq\mu_{\star}\left\{a+rk:a\in A,k\in\mathcal{K}\right\}\geq\Phi(a+r).

Proof.

[
Led96
, Theorem 4.3.]

∎

A famous example of Gaussian process that satisfy Assumption (
4.1
) is given by fractional Brownian motion. For further examples we refer to
[
BNGVS25
, Section 3.3]
. Furthermore, we point out that Assumption
4.1
enables us to translate any element of
\mathbf{X}
in the direction of
\mathcal{H}
canonically via Young integration. This concept of translation was first introduced in
[
CLL13
]
to study integrable bounds for the solution of rough differential equations. Our main strategy in the infinite-dimensional setting is inspired by
[
CLL13
]
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let Assumption
4.1
hold. For
h\in\mathcal{H}
, the translated path

(4.3)

\displaystyle T_{{h}}(\mathbf{X}):\Delta_{[0,T]}\longrightarrow T^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

is defined as follows.

1)

Level one:

\displaystyle\left(\Pi^{1}(T_{{h}}(\mathbf{X}))\right)_{s,t}=\left(\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X})\right)_{s,t}+\left(\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{h})\right)_{s,t}.

2)

Level two:

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\Pi^{2}(T_{{h}}(\mathbf{X}))\right)_{s,t}=\\
&\underbrace{\left(\Pi^{2}(\mathbf{X})\right)_{s,t}}_{\left(\Forest{[X[X]]}\right)_{s,t}}+\underbrace{\int_{s}^{t}\left(\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X})\right)_{s,u}\otimes\mathrm{d}\left(\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{h})\right)_{u}}_{\left(\Forest{[h[X]]}\right)_{s,t}}+\underbrace{\int_{s}^{t}\left(\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{h})\right)_{s,u}\otimes\mathrm{d}\left(\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X})\right)_{u}}_{\left(\Forest{[X[h]]}\right)_{s,t}}+\underbrace{\left(\Pi^{2}(\mathbf{h})\right)_{s,t}}_{\left(\Forest{[h[h]]}\right)_{s,t}}.\end{split}

3)

Level three:

(4.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\Pi^{3}(T_{{h}}(\mathbf{X}))\right)_{s,t}=\\
&\left(\Forest{[X[X[X]]]}\right)_{s,t}+\left(\Forest{[X[X[h]]]}\right)_{s,t}+\left(\Forest{[X[h[X]]]}\right)_{s,t}+\left(\Forest{[h[X[X]]]}\right)_{s,t}+\left(\Forest{[X[h[h]]]}\right)_{s,t}+\left(\Forest{[h[X[h]]]}\right)_{s,t}+\left(\Forest{[h[h[X]]]}\right)_{s,t}+\left(\Forest{[h[h[h]]]}\right)_{s,t}.\end{split}

The mixture of
\mathbf{h}
and
\mathbf{X}
in the second item is defined using Young integration. In the last item, by the 3-ladder trees, we refer to the integration of the above 2-ladder trees against the root. For example

\displaystyle\left(\Forest{[X[X[h]]]}\right)_{s,t}:=\int_{s}^{t}\left(\Forest{[X[h]]}\right)_{s,u}\otimes\mathrm{d}\left(\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X})\right)_{u},\quad\left(\Forest{[h[h[X]]]}\right)_{s,t}=\int_{s}^{t}\left(\Forest{[h[X]]}\right)_{s,u}\otimes\mathrm{d}\left(\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{h})\right)_{u}.

Except for the rough terms, i.e., those involving only
\mathbf{X}
and assumed to be given, the remaining terms are defined using Young integration and the classical Sewing lemma. We provide more details on the construction of these integrals below.

Remark 4.4
.

For
\gamma\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2})
, the third level in Definition
4.3
can be omitted.

Now, we estimate the translated path in terms of our controlled functions. The results presented below generalize
[
CLL13
, Lemma 3.1]
and
[
GVR25
, Lemma 2.8, Lemma 2.9]
by incorporating the control functions given in Definition
2.4
.

Lemma 4.5
.

The following estimate is valid for all
0\leq p<\gamma<\gamma^{\prime}
and for every interval
[s,t]\subseteq[0,T]

(4.6)

\displaystyle W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X+h}),\gamma,p}(s,t)\lesssim W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}(s,t)+W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{h}),\gamma^{\prime},p}^{\frac{\gamma^{\prime}-p}{\gamma-p}}(s,t).

Proof.

First, note that from the Minkowski inequality, for
[s,t]\subseteq[0,T]
and
0\leq p<\gamma
, we have

(4.7)

\displaystyle W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X+h}),\gamma,p}^{\gamma-p}(s,t)\leq W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{\gamma-p}(s,t)+W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{h}),\gamma^{\prime},p}^{\gamma-p}(s,t).

Since
\gamma^{\prime}>\gamma
, by a standard argument and using Lemma
2.2
, we conclude that

\displaystyle W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{h}),\gamma^{\prime},p}^{\gamma-p}(s,t)\leq W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{h}),\gamma^{\prime},p}^{\gamma^{\prime}-p}(s,t).

This inequality together with (
4.7
) entails (
4.6
).
∎

Now, we obtain the same result for the iterated integrals.

Lemma 4.6
.

The following estimate is valid for all
0\leq p<\gamma
and every interval
[s,t]\subseteq[0,T]

\displaystyle W_{\Pi^{2}(\mathbf{X+h}),\gamma,p}(s,t)\lesssim W_{\Pi^{2}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}(s,t)+W_{\Pi^{2}(\mathbf{h}),\gamma^{\prime},p}^{\frac{\gamma^{\prime}-p}{\gamma-p}}(s,t).

Proof.

We assume that
[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]\subseteq[s,t]
. Note that, by the definition of the translated rough path, we must estimate the terms
\Forest{[X[h]]}
,
\Forest{[h[X]]}
, and
\Forest{[X[X]]}
separately. We only treat the term
\Forest{[h[X]]}
, since the rest follow by similar computations. The assumption
\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}>1
allows us to define the second iterated integral, which is a mixture of
\mathbf{h}
and
\mathbf{X}
. To be more precise, first note that from Lemma
2.5
, we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle W_{\Pi^{2}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,0}(s,t)<\infty.

Now, since
\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}>1
, from classical Young integration, we obtain that

(4.9)

\displaystyle\left|\left(\Forest{[h[X]]}\right)_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}\right|\lesssim W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,0}^{\gamma}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{h}),\gamma^{\prime},0}^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}).

Now from Lemma
2.5
, we conclude that

(4.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\left(\Forest{[h[X]]}\right)_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}\right|&\lesssim(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})^{2p}W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{\gamma-p}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{h}),\gamma^{\prime},p}^{\gamma^{\prime}-p}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\\
&=(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})^{2p}\left(W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{\frac{\gamma-p}{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}-2p}}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{h}),\gamma^{\prime},p}^{\frac{\gamma^{\prime}-p}{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}-2p}}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\right)^{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}-2p}.\end{split}

This in turn yields

\displaystyle W_{\Forest{[h[X]]},\gamma,p}(s,t)\lesssim W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}^{\frac{\gamma-p}{2(\gamma-p)}}(s,t)W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{h}),\gamma^{\prime},p}^{\frac{\gamma^{\prime}-p}{2(\gamma-p)}}(s,t)\leq W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{X}),\gamma,p}(s,t)+W_{\Pi^{1}(\mathbf{h}),\gamma^{\prime},p}^{\frac{\gamma^{\prime}-p}{\gamma-p}}(s,t).

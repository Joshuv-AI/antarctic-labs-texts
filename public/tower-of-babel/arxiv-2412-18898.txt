1.
Introduction

Given two coprime integers
1<c<d
, Sylvester
[
7
]
discovered in 1882 that
cd-c-d
represents the largest number, denoted as
g_{c,d}
, which cannot be expressed as
cx+dy~(x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0})
. Subsequently, the author demonstrated that for any
0\leqslant m\leqslant g_{c,d}
, exactly one of
m
and
g_{c,d}-m
can be expressed in the form
cx+dy~(x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0})
. Consequently, it can be concluded that precisely half of the integers within the range
[0,g_{c,d}]
can be represented in this desired form.

Indeed, Sylvester’s findings represent a significant breakthrough in the field of the diophantine Frobenius problem, marking the first instance of nontrivial solutions
[
6
]
. This problem seeks to find the largest integer
g_{c_{1},...,c_{n}}
not that can be expressed as

c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}x_{n}\quad(x_{1},...,x_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}),

given that
c_{1},...,c_{n}
are positive integers with
\gcd(c_{1},...,c_{n})=1
. There exists an extensive body of literature dedicated to studying the diophantine Frobenius problem. For comprehensive insights and findings on this topic, please refer to Ramírez Alfonsín’s exceptional monograph
[
6
]
.
Motivated by Sylvester’s theorems, Ramírez Alfonsín and Skałba
[
5
]
investigated the Diophantine Frobenius problem restricted to prime numbers. Specifically, let
\pi_{c,d,k}
denote the number of
k
-th powers of primes not exceeding
g_{c,d}
with the form
cx+dy~(x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0})
. Through a highly enlightening argument put forth by Ramírez Alfonsín and Skałba, it has been demonstrated that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
k_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\pi_{c,d,1}\geqslant k_{\varepsilon}\frac{g_{c,d}}{(\log g_{c,d})^{2+\varepsilon}}.

After observing the antisymmetry property of integers in the form of
cx+dy~(x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0})
discovered by Sylvester, they naturally formulated the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1

(Ramírez Alfonsín and Skałba)
.

Let
1<c<d
be two relatively prime integers, then

\pi_{c,d,1}\sim\frac{\pi(g_{c,d})}{2}\quad(\text{as}~c\rightarrow\infty),

where
\pi(t)
is the number of primes up to
t
.

The recent proof of Conjecture
1.1
by Ding, Zhai, and Zhao in
[
1
]
has established its validity. Prior to this, Ding
[
2
]
demonstrated that Conjecture
1.1
holds true for almost all values of
c
and
d
. Remarkably, the complete verification of Conjecture
1.1
relies on the application of the classical Hardy-Littlewood method.

The main result is presented below. Throughout the remainder of this paper, let
k\geqslant 1
be an integer.
For convenience, we write
g
as
g_{c,d}
and
\pi_{c,d}
as
\pi_{c,d,k}.

Now, we shall establish the following theorem to document our findings.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
d>c
are two relatively prime integers with
c
sufficiently large, then we have

\pi_{c,d}\sim\frac{k}{k+1}\frac{g^{1/k}}{\log g},\quad\text{as~}c\rightarrow\infty.

Theorem
1.1
presented herein extends the findings in
[
1
]
to encompass arbitrary positive integers
k
. The proof methodology employed in this study aligns with a recent publication by Ding, Zhai, and Zhao
[
1
]
.

The key input for the proof of Theorem
1.1
is the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
c,d
be as in Theorem
1.1
. Denote

\mathcal{N}:=\#\{n^{k}\leqslant g_{c,d}:n^{k}=cx+dy,~x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}\},

then we have

\mathcal{N}\sim\frac{g^{1/k}}{k+1},\quad\text{as~}c\rightarrow\infty.

The initial investigation will follow the customary and focus on the weighted form associated with Theorem
1.1
, specifically

\displaystyle\psi_{c,d}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{k}\leqslant g\\
n^{k}=cx+dy\\
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}\end{subarray}}\Lambda(n),

where
\Lambda(n)
denotes the von Mangoldt function defined as

\Lambda(n)=\begin{cases}\log p,&\text{if }n=p^{\alpha}~(\alpha>0);\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The proof of Theorem
1.1
will be established using the following weighted formula through a standard transition method.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
c,d
be as in Theorem
1.1
, then we have

\psi_{c,d}\sim\frac{g^{1/k}}{k+1},\quad\text{as~}c\rightarrow\infty.

The proofs heavily rely on the exponential sum estimates in
[
3
]
, the prime number theorem, and the Siegel–Welfisz theorem.

2.
First transformations

We first fix some basic notations to be used frequently. From now on, we write
g
instead of
g_{c,d}
for brevity and
c
is supposed to be sufficiently large. Let
Q
denote a positive integer depending only on
g
which shall be decided later.
Define the major arcs to be

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}(Q)=\bigcup_{1\leqslant q\leqslant Q}\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant a\leqslant q\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}\left\{\alpha:\left|\alpha-\frac{a}{q}\right|\leqslant\frac{Q}{qg}\right\}.

We make a further provision that
Q<(g/2)^{1/3}
so that the above subsets are pairwise disjoint. In fact, suppose that

\left\{\alpha:\left|\alpha-\frac{a}{q}\right|\leqslant\frac{Q}{qg}\right\}\bigcap\left\{\alpha:\left|\alpha-\frac{a^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}\right|\leqslant\frac{Q}{qg}\right\}\neq\emptyset

for some
\frac{a}{q}\neq\frac{a^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}
, then

\frac{2Q}{g}\geqslant\frac{2Q}{qg}\geqslant\left|\frac{a}{q}-\frac{a^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}\right|\geqslant\frac{1}{Q^{2}},

which is certainly a contradiction with the provision that
Q<(g/2)^{1/3}
. In addition, we note that

\mathfrak{M}(Q)\subseteq\left[\frac{1}{Q}-\frac{Q}{qg},1+\frac{Q}{qg}\right]\subseteq\left[\frac{Q}{g},1+\frac{Q}{g}\right].

We can now define the minor arcs to be

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}(Q)=\left[\frac{Q+1}{g},1+\frac{Q+1}{g}\right]\setminus\mathfrak{M}(Q).

For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, let

f(\alpha)=\sum_{0\leqslant n^{k}\leqslant g}\Lambda(n)e(\alpha n^{k})\quad\text{and}\quad h(\alpha)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant x\leqslant d\\
0\leqslant y\leqslant c\end{subarray}}e(\alpha(cx+dy)).

By the orthogonality relation, it is clear that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\psi_{c,d}=\int_{0}^{1}f(\alpha)h(-\alpha)\mathrm{d}\alpha=\int_{\mathfrak{M}(Q)}f(\alpha)h(-\alpha)\mathrm{d}\alpha+\int_{\mathfrak{m}(Q)}f(\alpha)h(-\alpha)\mathrm{d}\alpha.

3.
Estimates of the minor arcs

The aim of this section is to prove the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

For estimates of the minor arcs, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{m}(Q)}f(\alpha)h(-\alpha)d\alpha\ll g^{(1-\rho(k))/k+\varepsilon}\log^{4}g+g^{1/k}(\log g)^{\delta+4}Q^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon},

where
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrarily small.

We need two lemmas listed below.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
k\geq 2
, and
\rho(k)
defined as follows,

\rho(k)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{8},&\text{if}\ \;k=2;\\
\frac{1}{14},&\text{if}\ \;k=3;\\
\frac{2}{3}\times 2^{-k},&\text{if}\ \;k\geq 4.\end{cases}

Then for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
,
we have

\sup_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(Q)}|f(\alpha)|\ll\log^{2}g\big(g^{(1-\rho(k))/k+\varepsilon}+g^{1/k}(\log g)^{\delta}Q^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\big),

where
\delta>0
is an absolute constant
and the implied constant depends at most
on
k
and
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Plugging in the definition of
\Lambda(n)
, we have

f(\alpha)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{p^{m}\leqslant g^{1/k}}\Lambda(p^{m})e(\alpha p^{mk})=\sum_{m\leqslant\frac{\log g}{k\log 2}}\sum_{p\leqslant g^{1/{mk}}}\log p\,e\big(\alpha p^{mk}\big).

For
m\geqslant 2
, it is clear that

\sum_{2\leqslant m\leqslant\frac{\log g}{k\log 2}}\sum_{p\leqslant g^{1/{mk}}}\log p\,e\big(\alpha p^{mk}\big)\ll g^{1/{2k}}\log^{2}g.

Thus

(3.1)

f(\alpha)=\sum_{p\leqslant g^{1/k}}\log g\,e\big(\alpha p^{k}\big)+O(g^{1/{2k}}\log^{2}g).

On applying the partial summation formula, we have

(3.2)

\sum_{p\leqslant g^{1/k}}\log p\,e\big(\alpha p^{k}\big)=\frac{\log g}{k}\sum_{p\leqslant g^{1/k}}e\big(\alpha p^{k}\big)-\int_{2}^{g^{1/k}}\sum_{p\leqslant\gamma}e\big(\alpha p^{k}\big)\frac{\mathrm{d}\gamma}{\gamma}.

By the Dirichlet approximation theorem,
there exist
b\in\mathbb{Z}
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(b,r)=1,\quad 1\leqslant r\leqslant R\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\alpha-\frac{b}{r}\right|\leqslant\frac{1}{rR},

where

R=\begin{cases}g^{3/2k},&\text{if}\ \;k=2;\\
g^{(k-2\rho(k))/(2k-1)},&\text{if}\ \;k\geq 3.\end{cases}

Then by Theorem 3 in
[
3
]
, for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, we conclude

(3.3)

\sum_{g^{1/k}\leqslant p\leqslant 2g^{1/k}}e\big(\alpha p^{k}\big)\ll g^{(1-\rho(k))/k+\varepsilon}+\frac{r^{\varepsilon}g^{1/k}(\log g)^{\delta}}{(r+g|r\alpha-b|)^{1/2}},

where
\delta>0
is an absolute constant.
If
r>Q
, then

(3.4)

\sum_{g^{1/k}\leqslant p\leqslant 2g^{1/k}}e\big(\alpha p^{k}\big)\ll g^{(1-\rho(k))/k+\varepsilon}+g^{1/k}(\log g)^{\delta}Q^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

Similarly, if
1\leqslant r\leqslant Q
and
|\alpha-b/r|>\frac{Q}{rg}
,
then (
3.4
) hold as well. From now on, we assume that

(3.5)

1\leqslant r\leqslant Q\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\alpha-\frac{b}{r}\right|\leqslant\frac{Q}{rg}.

By the Dirichlet approximation theorem, there exist
a\in\mathbb{Z}
and
q\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(3.6)

(a,q)=1,\quad 1\leqslant q\leqslant g^{1/2}\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\alpha-\frac{a}{q}\right|\leq\frac{1}{qg^{1/2}}.

We deduce from (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{b}{r}-\frac{a}{q}\right|\leqslant

\displaystyle\left|\alpha-\frac{b}{r}\right|+\left|\alpha-\frac{a}{q}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{Q}{rg}+\frac{1}{qg^{1/2}}=\frac{1}{rq}\Big(\frac{qQ}{g}+\frac{r}{g^{1/2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{Q}{rqg^{1/2}}<\frac{1}{rq}.

It follows that

(3.7)

\displaystyle a=b\quad\text{and}\quad r=q

We obtain from (
3.3
) and (
3.7
) that

(3.8)

\sum_{g^{1/k}\leqslant p\leqslant 2g^{1/k}}e\big(\alpha p^{k}\big)\ll g^{(1-\rho(k))/k+\varepsilon}+\frac{r^{\varepsilon}g^{1/k}(\log g)^{\delta}}{(q+g|q\alpha-a|)^{1/2}},

Since
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(Q)
, we further obtain

\sum_{g^{1/k}\leqslant p\leqslant 2g^{1/k}}e\big(\alpha p^{k}\big)\ll g^{(1-\rho(k))/k+\varepsilon}+g^{1/k}(\log g)^{\delta}Q^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

Then we deduce that

\begin{split}\sum_{p\leqslant g^{1/k}}e\big(\alpha p^{k}\big)&=\sum_{1\leqslant p\leqslant g^{1/k}/Q}e\big(\alpha p^{k}\big)+\sum_{g^{1/k}/Q<p\leqslant g^{1/k}}e\big(\alpha p^{k}\big)\\
&\ll\frac{g^{1/k}}{Q}+\sum_{1\leqslant\kappa\leqslant\frac{\log Q}{\log 2}}\bigg|\sum_{\frac{g^{1/k}}{2^{\kappa-1}}<p\leqslant\frac{g^{1/k}}{2^{\kappa}}}e\big(\alpha p^{k}\big)\bigg|\\
&\ll g^{(1-\rho(k))/k+\varepsilon}+g^{1/k}(\log g)^{\delta}Q^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}.\end{split}

Employing this estimate, we deduce by (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) that

\sup_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(Q)}|f(\alpha)|\ll g^{(1-\rho(k))/k+\varepsilon}\log^{2}g+g^{\frac{1}{k}}(\log g)^{\delta+2}Q^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

4.
Calculations of the major arcs

We now calculate the integral on the major
arcs.

Proposition 4.1
.

For
Q<c^{1/3}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{M}(Q)}f(\alpha)h(-\alpha)\mathrm{d}\alpha=\mathcal{N}+O\Big(g^{1/k}Q^{2}\exp\big(-\kappa_{1}\sqrt{\log g}\big)+g^{1/k}(\log g)^{3}Q^{-2^{1-k}+\varepsilon}+dQ^{3}\Big),

where
\kappa_{1}>0
is an absolute constant.

For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

F(\alpha):=\sum_{0\leqslant n^{k}\leqslant g}e(\alpha n^{k}).

Lemma 4.1
.

For any real number
\beta
, we have

\int_{-\frac{Q}{g}}^{\frac{Q}{g}}f(\beta)h\left(-\beta\right)\mathrm{d}\beta=\int_{-\frac{Q}{g}}^{\frac{Q}{g}}F(\beta)h\left(-\beta\right)\mathrm{d}\beta+O\left(g^{1/k}Q^{2}\exp\left(-\kappa_{1}\sqrt{\log g}\right)\right),

where
\kappa_{1}>0
is an absolute constant.

Proof.

Let
a_{n}=\Lambda(n)-1
. Then for any real number
\beta
, we have

f(\beta)-F(\beta)=\sum_{0\leqslant n^{k}\leqslant g}a_{n}e\big(\beta n^{k}\big).

By partial summation, we have

\begin{split}\sum_{0\leqslant n^{k}\leqslant g}a_{n}e\big(\beta n^{k}\big)&=\sum_{0\leqslant n\leqslant g^{1/k}}a_{n}e\big(\beta n^{k}\big)\\
&=e\big(\beta g^{k}\big)\sum_{0\leqslant n\leqslant g^{1/k}}a_{n}-2\pi ik\beta\int_{0}^{g^{1/k}}\left(\sum_{0\leqslant n\leqslant t}a_{n}\right)e\big(\beta t^{k}\big)t^{k-1}\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Using the prime number theorem, there exists
some absolute constant
\kappa_{1}>0
so that

\sum_{0\leqslant n\leqslant t}a_{n}=\psi(t)-t\ll t\exp\left(-\kappa_{1}\sqrt{\log t}\right).

We deduce that

f(\beta)=F(\beta)+O\left(g^{1/k}(1+k|\beta|g)\exp\left(-\kappa_{1}\sqrt{\log g}\right)\right).

Then we arrive at

\int_{-\frac{Q}{g}}^{\frac{Q}{g}}f(\beta)h\left(-\beta\right)\mathrm{d}\beta=\int_{-\frac{Q}{g}}^{\frac{Q}{g}}F(\beta)h\left(-\beta\right)\mathrm{d}\beta+\mathcal{R}(\beta),

where the error term
\mathcal{R}(\beta)
can be bounded easily by the trivial estimates as

\begin{split}\mathcal{R}(\beta)&\ll\int_{-\frac{Q}{g}}^{\frac{Q}{g}}g^{1/k}(1+k|\beta|g)\exp\left(-\kappa_{1}\sqrt{\log g}\right)|h(-\beta)|\mathrm{d}\beta\\
&\ll\int_{-\frac{Q}{g}}^{\frac{Q}{g}}g^{1/k}(1+k|\beta|g)\exp\left(-\kappa_{1}\sqrt{\log g}\right)g\mathrm{d}\beta\\
&\ll g^{1+1/k}Q\exp\left(-\kappa_{1}\sqrt{\log g}\right)\int_{-\frac{Q}{g}}^{\frac{Q}{g}}1\mathrm{d}\beta\\
&\ll g^{1/k}Q^{2}\exp\left(-\kappa_{1}\sqrt{\log g}\right).\end{split}

This completes the proof of Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
2\leqslant q\leqslant Q
,
(a,q)=1
and
|\beta|\leqslant Q/(qg)
. For
Q<c^{1/3}
, we have

\displaystyle h\left(-\frac{a}{q}-\beta\right)\ll qd.

Proof.

Recall that

\displaystyle h(-\alpha)=\sum_{x\leqslant d}e(-\alpha cx)\sum_{y\leqslant c}e(-\alpha dy)\ll\min\{d,||c\alpha||^{-1}\}\min\{c,||d\alpha||^{-1}\},

hence we have

\displaystyle h\left(-\frac{a}{q}-\beta\right)\ll\min\left\{d,\left\|c\left(-\frac{a}{q}-\beta\right)\right\|^{-1}\right\}\min\left\{c,\left\|d\left(-\frac{a}{q}-\beta\right)\right\|^{-1}\right\}.

Since
(a,q)=1
, we would have

\displaystyle\left\|c\left(-\frac{a}{q}-\beta\right)\right\|\geqslant\frac{1}{2q}\quad\text{if}\quad q\nmid c

and

\displaystyle\left\|d\left(-\frac{a}{q}-\beta\right)\right\|\geqslant\frac{1}{2q}\quad\text{if}\quad q\nmid d

for
q\geq 2
and
|\beta|\leqslant Q/(qg)
, provided that
Q\leqslant c^{1/3}
.
Recall that
(c,d)=1
, thus at least one of the above
inequalities is admissible. Therefore, for all
2\leqslant q\leqslant Q
,
(a,q)=1

and
|\beta|\leqslant Q/(qg)
we have

\displaystyle h\left(-\frac{a}{q}-\beta\right)\ll qd.

This completes the proof of Lemma
4.2
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

For any real number
\beta
, we have

\mathcal{N}=\int_{-\frac{Q}{g}}^{\frac{Q}{g}}F(\beta)h\left(-\beta\right)\mathrm{d}\beta+O\big(g^{1/k}(\log g)^{3}Q^{-2^{1-k}+\varepsilon}+dQ^{3}\big).

Proof.

By the orthogonality relation, it is clear that

\mathcal{N}=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}F(\alpha)h(-\alpha)\mathrm{d}\alpha=\int_{\mathfrak{M}(Q)}F(\alpha)h(-\alpha)\mathrm{d}\alpha+\int_{\mathfrak{m}(Q)}F(\alpha)h(-\alpha)\mathrm{d}\alpha.

We firstly consider the major arc

\int_{\mathfrak{M}(Q)}F(\alpha)h(-\alpha)\mathrm{d}\alpha=\sum_{1\leqslant q\leqslant Q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant a\leqslant q\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}\int_{-\frac{Q}{qg}}^{\frac{Q}{qg}}F\left(\frac{a}{q}+\beta\right)h\left(-\frac{a}{q}-\beta\right)\mathrm{d}\beta.

The term of the above sum for
a=q=1
equals

\displaystyle\int_{-\frac{Q}{g}}^{\frac{Q}{g}}F\left(1+\beta\right)h\left(-1-\beta\right)\mathrm{d}\beta=\int_{-\frac{Q}{g}}^{\frac{Q}{g}}F\left(\beta\right)h\left(-\beta\right)\mathrm{d}\beta.

The sums for
2\leqslant q\leqslant Q
contributes error terms.
By Lemma
4.2
,
we can conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{2\leqslant q\leqslant Q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant a\leqslant q\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}\int_{-\frac{Q}{qg}}^{\frac{Q}{qg}}F\left(\frac{a}{q}+\beta\right)h\left(-\frac{a}{q}-\beta\right)\mathrm{d}\beta\ll\sum_{2\leqslant q\leqslant Q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant a\leqslant q\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}\int_{-\frac{Q}{qg}}^{\frac{Q}{qg}}gdq~\mathrm{d}\beta\ll dQ^{3}.

It is now time to estimate the minor arc.
Let
T=g^{1/k}
.
By the Dirichlet approximation theorem, there exist
s\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(s,t)=1,\quad 1\leqslant t\leqslant 2T^{k-1}\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\alpha-\frac{s}{t}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2tT^{k-1}}.

Then by Lemma 6.4 in
[
4

\varepsilon>0

(4.1)

F(\alpha)\ll g^{(1-2^{1-k})/k+\varepsilon}+\frac{t^{\varepsilon}g^{1/k}(\log g)}{(t+g|t\alpha-s|)^{2^{1-k}}}.

If
t>Q
, then

(4.2)

F(\alpha)\ll g^{1/k}(\log g)Q^{-2^{1-k}+\varepsilon}.

Similarly, if
1\leqslant t\leqslant Q
and
|\alpha-s/t|>\frac{Q}{tT^{k}}

then (
4.2

(4.3)

1\leqslant t\leqslant Q\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\alpha-\frac{s}{t}\right|\leqslant\frac{Q}{tT^{k}}.

By the Dirichlet approximation theorem, there exist
a\in\mathbb{Z}
and
q\in\mathbb{N}

(4.4)

(a,q)=1,\quad 1\leqslant q\leqslant T^{k/2}\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\alpha-\frac{a}{q}\right|\leq\frac{1}{qT^{k/2}}.

We deduce from (

\displaystyle\left|\frac{s}{t}-\frac{a}{q}\right|\leqslant

\displaystyle s=a\quad\text{and}\quad t=q

Proof of Theorem

.

By the orthogonality relation, we get

\mathcal{N}=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}F(\alpha)h(-\alpha)\mathrm{d}\alpha=\int_{\mathfrak{M}(Q)}F(\alpha)h(-\alpha)\mathrm{d}\alpha+\int_{\mathfrak{m}(Q)}F(\alpha)h(-\alpha)\mathrm{d}\alpha.

We firstly consider the major arc

\int_{\mathfrak{M}(Q)}F(\alpha)h(-\alpha)\mathrm{d}\alpha=\sum_{1\leqslant q\leqslant Q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant a\leqslant q\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}\int_{-\frac{Q}{qg}}^{\frac{Q}{qg}}F\left(\frac{a}{q}+\beta\right)h\left(-\frac{a}{q}-\beta\right)\mathrm{d}\beta.

By Theorem 4.1 of
[

]
we have

F(\alpha)=S(q,a)v(\beta)+O(q^{1/2+\varepsilon}(1+g|\beta|)^{1/2}),

where

Thus

\int_{\mathfrak{M}(Q)}F(\alpha)h(-\alpha)\mathrm{d}\alpha=\sum_{1\leqslant q\leqslant Q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant a\leqslant q\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}S(q,a)\int_{-\frac{Q}{qg}}^{\frac{Q}{qg}}v(\beta)h\left(-\frac{a}{q}-\beta\right)\mathrm{d}\beta+\mathfrak{R}(\beta).

where the error term
\mathfrak{R}(\beta)
can be bounded as

\begin{split}\mathfrak{R}(\beta)&\ll\int_{-\frac{Q}{qg}}^{\frac{Q}{qg}}q^{1/2+\varepsilon}(1+g|\beta|)^{1/2}\left|h\left(-\frac{a}{q}-\beta\right)\right|\mathrm{d}\beta\\
&\ll\int_{-\frac{Q}{qg}}^{\frac{Q}{qg}}q^{1/2+\varepsilon}(1+g|\beta|)^{1/2}g\mathrm{d}\beta\\
&\ll\frac{Q^{3/2}}{q^{1-\varepsilon}}.\end{split}

By using Lemma
4.2
,
we can conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{2\leqslant q\leqslant Q}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant a\leqslant q\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}S(q,a)\int_{-\frac{Q}{qg}}^{\frac{Q}{qg}}v(\beta)h\left(-\frac{a}{q}-\beta\right)\mathrm{d}\beta

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{2\leqslant q\leqslant Q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant a\leqslant q\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}\int_{-\frac{Q}{qg}}^{\frac{Q}{qg}}g^{1/k}dq~\mathrm{d}\beta\ll dQ^{3}g^{1/k-1}.

Let
a=q=1
, we have

(4.7)

\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}F(\alpha)h(-\alpha)\mathrm{d}\alpha=\int_{-\frac{Q}{g}}^{\frac{Q}{g}}v(\beta)h(-\beta)\mathrm{d}\beta+O\left(dQ^{3}g^{1/k-1}+\frac{Q^{3/2}}{q^{1-\varepsilon}}\right).

By writing

(4.8)

\int_{-\frac{Q}{g}}^{\frac{Q}{g}}v(\beta)h(-\beta)\mathrm{d}\beta=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}v(\beta)h(-\beta)\mathrm{d}\beta+\mathfrak{R}_{1}(\beta)+\mathfrak{R}_{2}(\beta),

where

\mathfrak{R}_{1}(\beta)=\int_{\frac{Q}{g}}^{1/2}v(\beta)h(-\beta)\mathrm{d}\beta\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{R}_{2}(\beta)=\int_{-1/2}^{-\frac{Q}{g}}v(\beta)h(-\beta)\mathrm{d}\beta.

Note that

\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}v(\beta)h(-\beta)\mathrm{d}\beta=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant n\leqslant g\\
0\leqslant x\leqslant d,~0\leqslant y\leqslant c\end{subarray}}\frac{n^{1/k-1}}{k}\int_{0}^{1}e\Big(\beta\big(n-cx-dy\big)\Big)\mathrm{d}\beta.

By the orthogonality relation, the above integral

Hence, by the result of Sylverter

as we mentioned at the beginning of the introduction, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant n\leqslant g\\
0\leqslant x\leqslant d,~0\leqslant y\leqslant c\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{1}e\Big(\beta\big(n-cx-dy\big)\Big)\mathrm{d}\beta=\frac{g+1}{2}.

By Lemma
4.4
and partial summations, we have

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant n\leqslant g\\

&\sim\frac{g^{1/k}}{k+1},\quad\text{as}~c\rightarrow\infty.\end{split}

Suppose that

Lemma 5.1
.

For positive integers
c
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq y\leqslant c\\
(y,c)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{y^{1/k}}{\varphi(c)}

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{k+1}c^{1/k}+O(1).

Proof.

By Euler-Maclaurin formula, we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{0<k\leq l}m^{1/k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{l}x^{1/k}dx+\int_{0}^{l}(x-[x]-\frac{1}{2})\frac{1}{k}x^{1/k-1}dx-(l-[l]-\frac{1}{2})f(l)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k}{k+1}l^{1+1/k}+O(l^{1/k}).

Therefore using the well-known identity of Möbius’ function, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq y\leqslant c\\
(y,c)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{y^{1/k}}{\varphi(c)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(c)}\sum_{y\leq c}y^{1/k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|y\\
d|c\end{subarray}}\mu(d)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(c)}\sum_{d|c}\mu(d)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\leq c\\
d|y\end{subarray}}y^{1/k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(c)}\sum_{d|c}\mu(d)\left(\frac{k}{k+1}l^{1+1/k}+O(l^{1/k})\right)d^{1/k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k}{k+1}\frac{c^{1+1/k}}{\varphi(c)}\sum_{d|c}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}+\frac{1}{\varphi(c)}\sum_{d|c}\mu(d)O(c^{1/k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k}{1+k}c^{1/k}+O(1).

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1

1.
Introduction

Hardy’s inequality on the Heisenberg group was first established by Garofalo and Lanconelli in
[
16
]
. They established the following result: Let
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
. Then

(1.1)

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\left(\frac{|z|}{d(\xi)}\right)^{2}\frac{|u|^{2}}{d(\xi)^{2}}\leq\Big(\frac{2}{Q-2}\Big)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}u|^{2}d\xi.

Many generalizations of this inequality were obtained by various authors. For these, we refer to the works in
[
4
,
10
,
24
,
28
]
. However, our literature search for fractional Hardy type inequalities for subdomains, or specifically for the half spaces of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, came to nothing. This work is an attempt in this direction. Unlike the Euclidean case, the non-isotropic guage
d(\xi)
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
forces that the distance function behaves differently for the hyperplanes

\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
and
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}
which are defined later.
Following closely the technique in
[
12
]
, we have been able to establish a fractional Hardy type inequality for the half space
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
, although this approach does not allow us to compute the corresponding sharp constant, in this case. However, we plan to address this in our next work. The corresponding questions for the hyperplane
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}
remain open due to the lack of a closed form of the corresponding distance function for this hyperplane. We come back to this point in a while, and also in the Appendix in Section 4.

To recall , in 1925, G. H. Hardy established a fundamental integral inequality for non-negative measurable functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, known as the “Hardy’s inequality”. The generalization of Hardy’s inequality for the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 2
) is of the form

(1.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{|x|^{p}}dx\leq\left|\frac{p}{n-p}\right|^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}dx,

which holds for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if
1<p<n
and for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
if
p>n
. The constant
|\frac{p}{n-p}|^{p}
was shown to be sharp in
[
15
]
for the case
1<p<n
and in
[
6
, pp. 9]
for the case
p>n
. Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
, with non-empty boundary and let us define the the distance function from the boundary of
\Omega
by
\delta_{\Omega}(x)=\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)=\inf\{|x-y|:y\in\partial\Omega\}
. The following variant of Hardy’s inequality was established by Alano Ancona in
[
5
]
for a sufficiently regular domain
\Omega
:

(1.3)

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{\delta_{\Omega}(x)^{2}}dx\leq C_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}dx\ \ \forall\,u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega).

Later on, this inequality was generalized by several authors for various domains, such as bounded convex domains, bounded domains with smooth or Lipschitz boundaries, the upper half-spaces etc. and the corresponding best constants were also discussed in e.g.,
[
18
,
22
,
26
,
27
]
.

Analogous versions of these inequalities on fractional Sobolev spaces have also been studied for several domains. We refer
[
8
,
11
,
13
,
23
]
for introduction of the fractional Sobolev spaces, its connection with the classical spaces and for the Hardy’s inequality for fractional Sobolev spaces. Let
p>0,

0<s<1
and let
\Omega
be as before a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
. In
[
12
]
, B. Dyda established the fractional Hardy’s inequality

(1.4)

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\delta_{\Omega}(x)^{sp}}dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}dydx\ \ \forall\,u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega),

for the following cases:

(1)
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain and
sp>1
;

(2)
\Omega
is the complement of a bounded Lipschitz domain,
sp\neq 1
and
sp\neq n
;

(3)
\Omega
is a domain above the graph of a Lipschitz function
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
sp\neq 1
;

(4)
\Omega
is the complement of a point, and
sp\neq n
.

Inequality (
1.4
) for the critical case
sp=1
is addressed in
[
1
]
for dimension one and in
[
3
]
for higher dimensions. L. Brasco and E. Cinti established another variant of fractional Hardy’s inequality for convex domain
\Omega\neq\mathbb{R}^{n}
in
[
9
]
without imposing any restriction on the quantity
sp
. Inequality (
1.4
) for the upper half space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}>0\}
and the corresponding sharp constant

(1.5)

C_{n,p,s}:=2\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{\Gamma((1+sp)/2)}{\Gamma((n+sp)/2)}\int_{0}^{1}\left|1-r^{(sp-1)/p}\right|^{p}\frac{dr}{(1-r)^{1+sp}}

was first established by Bogdan and Dyda in
[
7
]
for the case
p=2
under the condition
sp\neq 1
. The results for general
p
were later extended by Frank and Seiringer in
[
14
]
.

In this paper our focus will be on inequality like (
1.4
) for the half spaces in the Heisenberg group. Before going to the main results, let us recall needed background on the Heisenberg group. Let
n\geq 1
. The Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is defined by

\mathbb{H}^{n}:=\{\xi=(z,t)=(x,y,t):z=(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \mathrm{and}\ t\in\mathbb{R}\},

with the following group law:

\xi\circ\xi^{\prime}=(x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}+2\langle y,x^{\prime}\rangle-2\langle x,y^{\prime}\rangle),

where
\xi=(x,y,t)
,
\xi^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the usual Euclidean inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. One can easily check that
0\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
is the identity element and
-\xi=(-z,-t)
is the inverse of
\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
. A basis for the left invariant vector fields is given by

X_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}+2y_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n,

Y_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}-2x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n,

T=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}.

For any function
u
of
C^{1}
class,
\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(u):=\left(X_{1}(u),\ldots,X_{n}(u),Y_{1}(u),\ldots,Y_{n}(u)\right)
is the associated Heisenberg sub-gradient of
u
and we define

|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(u)|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(|X_{i}(u)|^{2}+|Y_{i}(u)|^{2}\right).

For
\xi=(x,y,t)=(z,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
d(\xi)=\left((|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})^{2}+t^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}=\left(|z|^{4}+t^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}
is called the Koranyi-Folland non-isotropic gauge. The left-invariant Haar measure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
. For any measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
, we define by
|A|
, the Lebesgue measure of
A
. The dilations on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
are defined by

D_{r}\xi:=(rx,ry,r^{2}t),\ \ \mathrm{for}\ \xi=(x,y,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\ \mathrm{and}\ r>0.

The determinant of the Jacobian matrix of
D_{r}

is
r^{Q}
, where
Q=2n+2,
is called the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. We define the ball centered at
\xi
with radius
r
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
as

(1.6)

B(\xi,r)=\{\xi^{\prime}=(z^{\prime},t^{\prime}):d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})<r\}.

and the set

(1.7)

B_{z}(\xi,r)=\{\xi^{\prime}=(z^{\prime},t^{\prime}):|z^{\prime}-z|<r\},

is an infinite cylinder of radius
r
, parallel to the
t
direction, whose axis passes through
\xi
.

The distance function for a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
with non-empty boundary is defined by

\delta_{\Omega}(\xi)=\begin{cases}\underset{\xi^{\prime}\in\partial\Omega}{\mathrm{inf}}d(\xi^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})&\text{if $\xi\in\Omega$}\\
0&\text{if $\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\Omega$}.\end{cases}

Let us define the following half spaces in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
:

\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}:=\{\xi=(z,t)=(x,y,t)=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n},t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:x_{1}>0\}

and

\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}:=\{\xi=(z,t)=(x,y,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:t>0\}.

It is easy to see that
\delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}(\xi)=x_{1}
by choosing
\xi^{\prime}=(0,x_{2},\ldots,x_{n},y,t-2x_{1}y_{1})\in\partial\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
, whereas the distance function
\delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}}(\xi)\leq\sqrt{t}
for the half space
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}
. Indeed, the distance from the boundary of
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}
is
\sqrt{t}
for
\xi=(0,0,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}
but the value could be strictly less than
\sqrt{t}
for
\xi=(z,t)
in some subsets of
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}
, see the Appendix. Moreover, we do not know a closed form for the distance function in this case.

The inequality (
1.1
) of Garofalo and Lanconelli in
[
16
]
has been followed by establishing versions of Hardy’s inequalities for subdomains in the Heisenberg group, much like inequalities of the form (
1.3
) for the Euclidean setting. This includes the work in
[
17
]
where the authors prove a version of such inequalities for half spaces
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+},
with a remainder term. Likewise in
[
21
]
, the authors prove a version for half space
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t},
with the difference that the distance function is being replaced by the function
\sqrt{t}.
The work in
[
20
]
, contains a version of these Hardy inequalities for convex domains in
\mathbb{H}^{n},

convexity being defined in the Euclidean sense and the distance function is being replaced by a weighted Euclidean distance.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
s\in(0,1)
and
\alpha\geq 0
. For any open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
, we consider the following fractional Sobolev space

W^{s,p,\alpha}(\Omega):=\left\{f\in L^{p}(\Omega):[f]_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\right\},

the norm is defined by
\lVert f\rVert_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}^{p}=\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+[f]_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}^{p}
. The space
W_{0}^{s,p,\alpha}(\Omega)
is the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
under the norm
\lVert f\rVert_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}
. The space
W_{0}^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
was first introduced in
[
2
]
. The following fractional Hardy type inequality for the function
f\in W_{0}^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
was established in
[
2
]
under the condition
sp+\alpha<Q
together with
sp>2
:

(1.8)

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{d(\xi)^{sp}|z|^{\alpha}}d\xi\leq C_{n,p,s,\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z^{\prime}-z|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi.

The case
\alpha=0
was established in
[
19
]
. Recently, the range of the indices has been improved as a particular case of fractional Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg type inequalities in
[
25
]
, which is as follows:

(i) The inequality (
1.8
) holds for the functions of
W_{0}^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
if, either
sp+\alpha<Q-2
, or
sp+\alpha<Q
with
sp\geq 2
.

(ii) The inequality (
1.8
) holds for the functions of
W_{0}^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\{\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:z=0\})
if
sp+\alpha>Q-2
.

Our main result is the following inequality.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\geq 1
and
s\in(0,1)
be such that
sp>1
and
\alpha\geq(Q-2+sp)/2
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
n
,
p
,
s
and
\alpha
, such that

(1.9)

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{x_{1}^{sp}|z|^{\alpha}}d\xi\leq C\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z^{\prime}-z|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\ \ \forall\,f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}).

As in other Hardy type inequalities on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, here the term
|z|
on the left hand side occurs naturally from the structure of the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

Again using Dyda’s technique, we provide an alternate proof of the improved version of the fractional Hardy-type inequality (
1.8
), which was presented as a particular case of Theorem 1.1 in
[
25
]
. More precisely, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p\geq 1
,
s\in(0,1)
and
\alpha\geq 0
.

(i)
If
sp+\alpha<Q-2
, then (
1.8
) holds for all
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
.

(ii)
If
sp+\alpha>Q-2
, then (
1.8
) holds for all
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\{\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:z=0\})
.

2.
Proof of the fractional Hardy’s inequality in half space

For any fixed
\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
and
R_{1},R_{2}>0
, let us define the set

\Sigma_{R_{1},R_{2}}(\xi)=\{\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})<R_{1}x_{1}^{1/2}|z|^{1/2}\ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ |z^{\prime}-z|<R_{2}x_{1}\}.

Let
\mathscr{D}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
be any open set and
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})
. For
R_{1},R_{2}
and
S>0
we define the following set:

(2.1)

F=F(f,\mathscr{D},R_{1},R_{2},S)=\left\{\xi\in\mathscr{D}:|f(\xi)|^{p}>\frac{2^{p+1}}{Sx_{1}^{Q-1}|z|}\int_{\Sigma_{R_{1},R_{2}}(\xi)\cap\mathscr{D}}|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}d\xi^{\prime}\right\}.

Note that
F\subset\mathrm{supp}\,f
, or in particular,
\mathrm{dist}(F,\partial\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})>0
. The role of the set
\mathscr{D}
is to localise the setting. The following properties of the set
F
are useful to prove our first result.

Property 1
.

Let
\alpha\geq\frac{Q-2+sp}{2}
. Then we have

\int_{\mathscr{D}\setminus F}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{x_{1}^{sp}|z|^{\alpha}}d\xi\leq\frac{2^{p+1}R_{1}^{Q+sp}R_{2}^{\alpha}}{S}\int_{\mathscr{D}\setminus F}\int_{\mathscr{D}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z^{\prime}-z|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi.

Proof.

Let
\xi\in\mathscr{D}\setminus F
. We have

\int_{\mathscr{D}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z^{\prime}-z|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}\geq\int_{\Sigma_{R_{1},R_{2}}(\xi)\cap\mathscr{D}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{(R_{1}x_{1}^{1/2}|z|^{1/2})^{Q+sp}(R_{2}x_{1})^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}\\
\geq\frac{S}{2^{p+1}R_{1}^{Q+sp}R_{2}^{\alpha}}\left(\frac{|z|}{x_{1}}\right)^{\alpha-\frac{Q-2+sp}{2}}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{x_{1}^{sp}|z|^{\alpha}}\geq\frac{S}{2^{p+1}R_{1}^{Q+sp}R_{2}^{\alpha}}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{x_{1}^{sp}|z|^{\alpha}}.

Integration over
\mathscr{D}\setminus F
completes the proof.
∎

Property 2
.

Let
\xi\in F
. For
E\subset\Sigma_{R_{1},R_{2}}(\xi)\cap\mathscr{D}
we define the set

E^{*}=E^{*}(\xi):=\left\{\xi^{\prime}\in E:\frac{1}{2}|f(\xi)|\leq|f(\xi^{\prime})|\leq\frac{3}{2}|f(\xi)|\right\}.

If
|E|\geq Sx_{1}^{Q-1}|z|
, then
|E^{*}|\geq|E|-\frac{1}{2}Sx_{1}^{Q-1}|z|\geq\frac{1}{2}|E|
.

Proof.

It is easy to observe that

E^{*}(\xi)\supseteq\left\{\xi^{\prime}\in E:\frac{||f(\xi)|-|f(\xi^{\prime})||}{|f(\xi)|}\leq\frac{1}{2}\right\}.

Therefore

|E^{*}|\geq|E|-\left|\left\{\xi^{\prime}\in E:\frac{||f(\xi)|-|f(\xi^{\prime})||}{|f(\xi)|}>\frac{1}{2}\right\}\right|\\
\geq|E|-2^{p}\int_{\left\{\xi^{\prime}\in E:\frac{||f(\xi)|-|f(\xi^{\prime})||}{|f(\xi)|}>\frac{1}{2}\right\}}\frac{||f(\xi)|-|f(\xi^{\prime})||^{p}}{|f(\xi)|^{p}}d\xi^{\prime}\\
\geq|E|-\frac{2^{p}}{|f(\xi)|^{p}}\int_{E}|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}d\xi^{\prime}.

The rest of the proof follows from the assumptions on
\xi
and
E.

∎

Property 3
.

Let
E_{1}\subset\mathscr{D}
. If
E_{2}
is any set satisfying the following:
E_{2}\subset\Sigma_{R_{1},R_{2}}(\xi)\cap\mathscr{D}
and
|E_{2}|\geq Sx_{1}^{Q-1}|z|
for all
\xi\in E_{1}
, then

\int_{E_{1}\cap F}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{x_{1}^{sp}|z|^{\alpha}}d\xi\leq 2^{p+1}\frac{|E_{1}|}{|E_{2}|}\frac{\sup\{{x_{1}^{\prime}}^{sp}|z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}:\xi^{\prime}\in E_{2}\}}{\inf\{x_{1}^{sp}|z|^{\alpha}:\xi\in E_{1}\}}\int_{E_{2}}\frac{|f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{{x_{1}^{\prime}}^{sp}|z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}.

Proof.

Assume
E_{1}\cap F\neq\phi
. For any fixed
\eta>1
we pick
\xi_{\eta}\in E_{1}\cap F
such that
\underset{E_{1}\cap F}{\sup}|f|\leq\eta|f(\xi_{\eta})|
. It follows from the definition that
\frac{1}{2}|f(\xi_{\eta})|\leq|f(\xi^{\prime})|
for all
\xi^{\prime}\in E_{2}^{*}(\xi_{\eta})
. Therefore

\int_{E_{1}\cap F}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{x_{1}^{sp}|z|^{\alpha}}d\xi\leq|E_{1}\cap F|\cdot\frac{\underset{E_{1}\cap F}{\sup}|f|^{p}}{\inf\{x_{1}^{sp}|z|^{\alpha}:\xi\in E_{1}\cap F\}}\\
\leq\frac{|E_{1}\cap F|}{|E_{2}^{*}(\xi_{\eta})|}\frac{\sup\{{x_{1}^{\prime}}^{sp}|z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}:\xi^{\prime}\in E_{2}^{*}(\xi_{\eta})\}}{\inf\{x_{1}^{sp}|z|^{\alpha}:\xi\in E_{1}\cap F\}}\cdot\eta^{p}\int_{E_{2}^{*}(\xi_{\eta})}\frac{|f(\xi_{\eta})|^{p}}{{x^{\prime}_{1}}^{sp}|z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}\\
\leq 2^{p+1}\eta^{p}\frac{|E_{1}|}{|E_{2}|}\frac{\sup\{{x_{1}^{\prime}}^{sp}|z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}:\xi^{\prime}\in E_{2}\}}{\inf\{x_{1}^{sp}|z|^{\alpha}:\xi\in E_{1}\}}\int_{E_{2}}\frac{|f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{{x_{1}^{\prime}}^{sp}|z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}.

Here we use the fact that
|E_{2}^{*}(\xi_{\eta})|\geq\frac{1}{2}|E_{2}|
. We conclude the proof by sending
\eta\rightarrow 1
.
∎

To prove our main result, we choose the sets
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
and the quantities
R_{1}
,
R_{2}
and
S
in such a way that they satisfy the assumptions of Property
3
. Property
3
helps us to shift the part of the integral on the left hand side of (
1.9
), which is over
F
to the integral over the complement of
F
. Property
1
connects the integrals of both sides of (
1.9
) over the complement of
F
.

The condition
|E|\geq Sx_{1}^{Q-1}|z|
is crucial in Property
2
and Property
3
. To get this estimate we decompose the domain of integration as in the Dyda’s proof for the half space
\{x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\\
\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{1}>0\}
in the Euclidean case. Dyda used dyadic decomposition for the base and worked with the estimate
|x-y|\leq Rx_{1}
for
x\in E_{1}
and
y\in E_{2}
and
|E_{2}|\geq Sx_{1}^{n}
for some dyadic cubes
E_{1}
and
E_{2}

.

Proof.

\frac{\rho}{2^{j+1}}<x_{1}\leq\frac{\rho}{2^{j}}

\frac{(k-1)\rho}{2^{j+1}}<|z|\leq\sqrt{2n}\frac{k\rho}{2^{j}}

\leq|z^{\prime}-z|^{4}+2(t^{\prime}-t)^{2}+8|z|^{2}|z^{\prime}-z|^{2}\qquad\quad\\
\leq 4n^{2}\left(\frac{\rho}{2^{j}}\right)^{4}+2\left(\frac{k\rho^{2}}{2^{2j}}\right)^{2}+16n\left(\frac{\rho}{2^{j}}\right)^{2}|z|^{2}\\
\leq(4n^{2}+2k^{2})\left(\frac{\rho}{2^{j}}\right)^{4}+16n\left(\frac{\rho}{2^{j}}\right)^{2}|z|^{2}.

and for
k\geq 2

\leq 2^{4}(4n^{2}+8)\left(\frac{\rho}{2^{j+1}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{(k-1)\rho}{2^{j+1}}\right)^{2}+2^{4}\cdot 4n\left(\frac{\rho}{2^{j+1}}\right)^{2}|z|^{2}\leq 2^{4}(4n^{2}+4n+8)x_{1}^{2}|z|^{2}.

Therefore, we have, for any

Lemma 2.2

Let

and
\mathscr{D}=\mathcal{Q}(\rho)
. We consider the set

\\

|E_{1}|=2^{(Q-2)m}|E_{2}|
and

|E_{2}|=(2^{j-m}r)^{Q-2}|B_{2n}(0,2)\setminus B_{2n}(0,1)|\,2^{2j}r^{2}=S(2^{j+1}r)^{Q}>S|z|^{Q}.

Therefore, by Property
6
we get

\int_{J_{j}^{j,\ell}\cap G}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{|z|^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi\leq 2^{p+1}2^{(Q-2)m}\left(\frac{2^{j-m+1}}{2^{j}}\right)^{sp+\alpha}\int_{J_{j-m}^{j,\ell}}\frac{|f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{|z^{\prime}|^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}=\gamma\int_{J_{j-m}^{j,\ell}}\frac{|f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{|z^{\prime}|^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime},

where
\gamma=2^{(Q-2-sp-\alpha)m}2^{p+1+sp+\alpha}
. We choose
m
to be large enough so that
\gamma<1
. Summation over
\ell
gives

(3.7)

\int_{A_{j}\cap G}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{|z|^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi\leq\gamma\int_{A_{j-m}}\frac{|f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{|z^{\prime}|^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}.

Next, we have

\int_{A_{j-m}}\frac{|f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{|z^{\prime}|^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}=\left(\int_{A_{j-m}\setminus G}+\int_{A_{j-m}\cap G}\right)\frac{|f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{|z^{\prime}|^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime},

and we iterate (
3.7
) by replacing
j
with
j-m,j-2m,\cdots,j-k_{j}m
until
0\leq j-k_{j}m<m
. Note that,
k_{j}=k
for
j\in\{km,km+1,\ldots,km+m-1\}
and
\cup_{j=km}^{km+m-1}A_{j-k_{j}m}\subset A
. Therefore we obtain

\int_{B^{C}}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{|z|^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi=\left(\int_{B^{C}\setminus G}+\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}\int_{A_{j}\cap G}\right)\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{|z|^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi\\
\leq\int_{B^{C}\setminus G}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{|z|^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi+\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}-1}\gamma^{k}\int_{A_{j-km}\setminus G}\frac{|f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{|z^{\prime}|^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}+\gamma^{k_{j}}\int_{A_{j-k_{j}m}\setminus G}\frac{|f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{|z^{\prime}|^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}\right)\\
\leq\int_{B^{C}\setminus G}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{|z|^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}\right)\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}\int_{A_{j-km}\setminus G}\frac{|f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{|z^{\prime}|^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}\right)\int_{A\setminus G}\frac{|f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{|z^{\prime}|^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}\\
=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}\right)\int_{B^{C}\setminus G}\frac{|f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{|z^{\prime}|^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}\right)\int_{A\setminus G}\frac{|f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{|z^{\prime}|^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}.

Applying Property
4
, we get

\int_{B^{C}}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{|z|^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\frac{2^{p+1}R^{Q+sp+\alpha}}{S}\int_{B^{C}}\int_{B^{C}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z^{\prime}-z|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\int_{A}\frac{|f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{|z^{\prime}|^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.1.
An estimate of the distance function

Let
\xi=(x,y,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}
and consider the point
\xi^{\prime}=(x,y,0)\in\partial\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}
. Clearly,
d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})=\sqrt{t}
, which says that
\delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}}(\xi)\leq\sqrt{t}
.

Next, for any point
\xi=(x,y,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}
with
x_{i}\neq 0
and
y_{i}\neq 0
,
i=1,2,\cdots,n
, we consider the point
\xi_{0}=\left(x_{1}-t/{4ny_{1}},\ldots,x_{n}-t/{4ny_{n}},y_{1}+t/{4nx_{1}},\ldots,y_{n}+t/{4nx_{n}},0\right)\in\partial\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}.

We have

d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi_{0})^{4}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{t}{4ny_{i}}\right)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{t}{4nx_{i}}\right)^{2}\right)^{2}\\
+\left(-t-2\left\langle y,\left(x_{1}-\frac{t}{4ny_{1}},\cdots,x_{n}-\frac{t}{4ny_{n}}\right)\right\rangle+2\left\langle x,\left(y_{1}+\frac{t}{4nx_{1}},\cdots,y_{n}+\frac{t}{4nx_{n}}\right)\right\rangle\right)^{2}\\
=\left(\frac{t}{4n}\right)^{4}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{x_{i}^{2}}+\frac{1}{y_{i}^{2}}\right)\right)^{2}.

Therefore,

(4.1)

\delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}}(\xi)\leq\frac{t}{4n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{x_{i}^{2}}+\frac{1}{y_{i}^{2}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In particular,
\delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}}(\xi)<\sqrt{t}
, if
\xi
belongs to the set

(4.2)

\Omega=\{\xi=(x,y,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:0<t<1,\ \ |x_{i}|,|y_{i}|>1/2\sqrt{2n},\ \ i=1,\cdots,n\}.

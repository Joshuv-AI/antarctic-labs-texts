First-line third-generation EGFR-TKIs for advanced EGFR-mutated non-small cell lung cancer: a systematic review and network meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials
Authors: Xiaomin Liu, Qingfang Zhao, Wei Sun, Jinglong Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHead‑to‑head comparisons among first‑line third‑generation epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) for EGFR‑mutated unresectable non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) are nearly absent. This network meta‑analysis compares the efficacy and safety of all available agents and provide evidence-based references for clinical decision-making.MethodsWe searched PubMed, EMBASE, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL) from inception to April 2026. Eligible studies were randomized controlled trials (RCTs) enrolling patients with advanced EGFR-mutated NSCLC receiving first-line third-generation EGFR-TKIs compared with first-generation EGFR-TKIs. Two reviewers independently extracted data and assessed risk of bias using the Cochrane RoB 2 tool. The primary efficacy outcome was progression-free survival (PFS). We performed a network meta-analysis (NMA) using a fixed-effect model and ranked treatments using surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA). The protocol was registered with PROSPERO (CRD42022349097).ResultsEleven RCTs comprising 4,663 patients were analyzed. All investigated third-generation agents (aumolertinib, osimertinib, furmonertinib, befotertinib, limertinib, rilertinib, lazertinib, and rezivertinib) demonstrated superior PFS compared to first-generation TKIs. However, no statistically significant PFS differences were observed among the third-generation agents. Similarly, no significant differences were found between third- and first-generation TKIs regarding objective response rate (ORR) or ≥ G3 treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs). Subgroup analyses revealed no significant PFS benefit for befotertinib over first-generation TKIs in the L858R subgroup, nor for befotertinib or furmonertinib in elderly patients.ConclusionsMost third-generation TKIs offer superior PFS compared to first-generation agents, with comparable efficacy observed across the third-generation agents. Safety profiles, specifically regarding high-grade adverse events, appear similar between third- and first-generation TKIs.Keywords: Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), EGFR mutation, third-generation tyrosine kinase inhibitor (third-generation TKI), network meta-analysis (NMA)

Highlight box

Key findings

• Third-generation epidermal growth factor receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitors (EGFR-TKIs) (including aumolertinib, osimertinib, furmonertinib, befotertinib, limertinib, rilertinib, lazertinib, and rezivertinib) showed significantly improved prognosis compared to first-generation agents in patients with unresectable, EGFR-mutant non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), with comparable efficacy and safety among the third-generation drugs.

• Prognosis in the L858R subgroup (befotertinib) and in elderly patients (befotertinib and furmonertinib) did not differ significantly from that with first-generation EGFR-TKIs.

What is known and what is new?

• Third-generation EGFR-TKIs are the standard treatment for unresectable EGFR-mutant NSCLC; however, direct comparative data among these agents are lacking.

• The prognostic is similar across these third-generation inhibitors.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• These data establish third-generation EGFR-TKIs as a preferred first-line option, with treatment decisions individualized based on patient profiles and drug accessibility.

• As multiple agents are now available, future high-quality, large-scale studies are needed to delineate their comparative efficacy and safety in various subgroups to guide personalized therapy.

Introduction

Systemic therapy with epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), either as monotherapy or in combination regimens, represents the standard first-line treatment for patients with advanced or metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) harboring EGFR sensitizing mutations (exon 19 deletion or exon 21 L858R). While first- and second-generation EGFR TKIs provide a median progression-free survival (PFS) of approximately 9 to 14 months (1-6), disease progression inevitably ensues due to acquired resistance, predominantly mediated by the EGFR T790M mutation (7,8). In contrast, third-generation EGFR TKIs are designed to inhibit both EGFR-sensitizing mutations and the T790M resistance allele, demonstrating a superior median PFS of approximately 20 months (9-13).

Beyond clinical trials, real-world evidence has emerged as a valuable complement, reflecting treatment patterns and outcomes in routine practice. A recent real-world study (14) highlighted the evolving landscape of third-generation EGFR-TKI use, with increasing adoption in clinical practice following progression on prior therapies. These findings reinforce the need for systematic comparative analyses—such as the present network meta-analysis (NMA)—to help guide treatment decisions in the absence of direct head-to-head comparisons.

Several NMAs have previously compared first-line EGFR-TKIs, including some focusing on third-generation agents. Nevertheless, important gaps remain. First, prior NMAs often pooled trials with different first-generation comparators (e.g., gefitinib, erlotinib) without accounting for potential differential effects of the control agent, which may introduce bias. Second, recently approved third-generation TKIs (e.g., rezivertinib, limertinib) have not yet been incorporated into a comprehensive synthesis.

To address these gaps, we conducted an updated NMA comparing the efficacy and safety of third-generation EGFR TKIs as first-line therapy for advanced or metastatic EGFR-mutated NSCLC. We present this article in accordance with the PRISMA-NMA reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-1-0317/rc).

Methods

A Bayesian framework was employed to compare the efficacy and safety of third-generation EGFR TKIs. The protocol was registered with PROSPERO (CRD42022349097).

Data sources and search strategy

We systematically searched PubMed, EMBASE, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL) from inception through July 2022. To ensure currency, we conducted a systematic research of the aforementioned databases covering the period from August 2022 to April 2026. The search was restricted to English-language publications. The detailed search strategy is provided in Table S1.

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria: (I) trials enrolling patients with advanced or metastatic NSCLC harboring EGFR exon 19 deletion or the L858R mutations; patients with asymptomatic, stable central nervous system (CNS) metastases not requiring corticosteroid therapy for at least 2 weeks prior to study initiation were eligible; (II) randomized controlled trials (RCTs) comparing a third-generation EGFR TKI with a first- or third- generation EGFR TKI; (III) studies published in English. Exclusion criteria: trials involving patients who had received prior systemic therapy for advanced or metastatic NSCLC were excluded.

Study selection and data extraction

Two investigators (X.L. and Q.Z.) independently screened titles and abstracts, followed by a full-text review to assess eligibility. Data were independently extracted by the same reviewers, covering study characteristics (name, author, year, phase, sample size), interventions, comparators, baseline demographics, and efficacy/safety outcomes. Any discrepancies were resolved by consensus or consultation with a third reviewer (W.S. or J.C.).

Outcomes and subgroup analysis

The primary outcome was PFS. Secondary outcomes included intracranial PFS, objective response rate (ORR), and grade 3 or higher treatment-emergent adverse events (≥ G3 TEAEs). An analysis of PFS across multiple subgroups was performed. Hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were used for time-to-event outcomes (PFS), while odds ratios (ORs) with 95% CIs were used for dichotomous outcomes (ORR and ≥ G3 TEAEs).

Risk of bias assessment

Two reviewers (X.L. and Q.Z.) independently assessed the risk of bias using the Cochrane risk-of-bias tool for randomized trials (RoB 2). The following domains were evaluated: randomization process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, measurement of the outcome, and selection of the reported result. Each study was classified as having a low risk, high risk, or some concerns regarding bias. Disagreements were resolved by a third reviewer (W.S. or J.C.).

Statistical analysis

The NMA was conducted within a Bayesian framework using Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) simulation, implemented via the gemtc package (version 1.0.1) and JAGS (version 4.3.1) in R (version 4.2.1). Due to the limited number of trials—which precluded a robust assessment of between-study heterogeneity—and the similarity of results between models, the fixed-effect model was selected for the primary analysis. A random-effects model was used for sensitivity analyses. Model fit was assessed using the Deviance Information Criterion (DIC). Statistical inconsistency was not assessed due to the absence of closed loops in the network. Convergence was verified using trace plots and Gelman-Rubin diagnostics. Treatment rankings were estimated using the surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA); higher SUCRA values indicate superior efficacy, whereas for safety outcomes, lower values indicate a more favorable profile (i.e., lower toxicity).

Results

Study and patient characteristics

Of 6,165 identified records, 11 phase III RCTs (9-13,15-20) involving 4,663 patients were included (Figure 1). These encompassed 10 distinct treatment regimens: ten trials compared third- vs. first-generation EGFR TKIs, and one compared distinct third-generation agents (osimertinib vs. lazertinib).

Risk of bias assessment

Eight trials demonstrated a low risk of bias. The rilertinib trial was rated as having “some concerns” due to limited clinical data. The ASP8273 and zorifertinib trials were classified as high risk, largely driven by the potential for performance and detection bias inherent to their open-label designs (Figure S1).

Efficacy and safety findings

PFS and intracranial efficacy

Analysis of PFS revealed that all third-generation agents—except ASP8273—conferred a significant survival benefit over first-generation EGFR TKIs. ASP8273 performed inferiorly to the control group. Network rankings indicated that limertinib was most likely to yield the best PFS benefit (SUCRA: 67.7%). In terms of intracranial PFS, furmonertinib, limertinib, and zorifertinib significantly outperformed first-generation agents, with limertinib ranking highest (SUCRA: 89.9%). Conversely, befotertinib and rezivertinib showed no significant intracranial advantage over first-generation controls (Figure 3, Figure S2). Tumor response and safety

No statistically significant differences in ORR were observed between third- and first-generation TKIs, or among third-generation agents, with the exception of limertinib showing superiority over ASP8273. Limertinib ranked first for ORR (SUCRA: 81.1%). Regarding safety, the incidence of ≥ G3 TEAEs was similar across all treatment groups. Subgroup analysis of PFS

We stratified PFS outcomes by mutation type (exon 19 deletion or exon 21 L858R), smoking history (never smokers or former/current smokers), gender (male or female), age (<65 or ≥65 years), race (Asian), CNS metastases (with or without), and comparator drug (gefitinib). Data were synthesized from nine trials; notably, the zorifertinib trial was restricted to the CNS metastases subgroup, and smoking data were unavailable for rezivertinib. Gefitinib was the comparator in seven trials; comparative data for osimertinib were extracted from the FLAURA China (21) and Japan (22) cohorts.

In general, third-generation EGFR TKIs maintained superiority over first-generation agents across all subgroups. However, specific agents lacked statistical significance in certain populations: befotertinib in the L858R mutation subgroup, and both befotertinib and furmonertinib in the elderly patient subgroup (≥65 years). Notably, limertinib was significantly superior to befotertinib in elderly patients.

Discussion

This NMA confirms that, with the exception of ASP8273, third-generation EGFR TKIs provide a significant PFS benefit over first-generation agents. Notably, we observed no statistically significant differences in ORR or ≥ G3 TEAEs among the various third-generation agents themselves.

The superiority of third-generation TKIs is well-established, with phase III trials reporting median PFS of approximately 19–21 vs. 9–14 months for first-generation agents (9-13,15-17). While previous meta-analyses have supported these findings (23,24), the recent approval of multiple novel third-generation agents—particularly those developed in Asia—necessitated an updated comparative evaluation. Our analysis integrates these newer agents to assist clinicians in optimizing treatment selection.

Structural heterogeneity among third-generation EGFR TKIs may lead to differential efficacy across patient subgroups. While the class effect generally favors third-generation agents, our subgroup analysis revealed crucial nuances. Specifically, befotertinib failed to demonstrate a significant PFS advantage over first-generation TKIs in patients with the exon 21 L858R mutations. Similarly, in elderly patients (≥65 years), neither befotertinib nor furmonertinib showed significant superiority over control arms. These observations corroborate findings from the befotertinib phase III trial (15), and the FURLONG study (11) suggesting that age and mutation subtype should be key considerations when prescribing these specific agents.

Regarding safety, the incidence of grade ≥3 TEAEs was generally comparable among third-generation EGFR TKIs, although befotertinib appeared to be associated with a numerically higher rate of such events. This is consistent with Du et al., who reported a higher incidence of grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events with befotertinib (24). The specific safety profiles varied: the most common grade ≥3 adverse events were alanine aminotransferase elevation for aumolertinib and lazertinib; decreased appetite for osimertinib; QT interval prolongation for furmonertinib; thrombocytopenia for befotertinib; and hypokalemia for rilertinib (9-11,15-17). These distinct profiles highlight the need for personalized treatment selection based on patient comorbidities.

Given that gefitinib, erlotinib, and icotinib exhibit similar efficacy (25,26), they were pooled as the control group in our primary analysis. To ensure methodological robustness, we conducted sensitivity analyses restricted to trials using gefitinib as the control, given the potential for heterogeneity among first-generation agents. The results remained consistent with the primary analysis, reinforcing the validity of our findings regarding the comparative efficacy of befotertinib and furmonertinib in specific subgroups (11,15).

The reliability of SUCRA-based rankings is limited by the sparsity and indirect nature of the evidence network. SUCRA rankings should be interpreted cautiously given sparse indirect data. Small differences (e.g., between limertinib and the next-best treatment for PFS) may be unstable and lack clinical relevance. These rankings are exploratory only.

Limitations

This study has limitations. First, results are derived primarily from indirect comparisons; direct head-to-head evidence is limited to osimertinib versus lazertinib. Second, the open-label design of the ASP8273 and zorifertinib trials introduces potential performance and detection bias, although this is inherent to the source data. Third, our strict inclusion of only phase III trials ensures high-quality evidence but excludes potential signals from phase II studies. Fourth, clinical heterogeneity across drugs, populations, and trial designs may limit the generalizability of our findings. But, sensitivity analyses with fixed- and random-effects models yielded highly consistent results, confirming the reliability and robustness of our findings. Finally, a further limitation is the inability to formally assess inconsistency (transitivity). No closed loops existed in the evidence network, precluding global or loop‑specific tests. Although node-splitting may be feasible in some sparse networks, the extreme sparsity here—most comparisons informed by single studies or lacking direct evidence—prevented convergence or interpretable estimates. Thus, the consistency assumption could not be empirically evaluated, and results should be interpreted with caution, especially for comparisons based solely on indirect evidence.

Conclusions

Most third-generation EGFR TKIs offer superior PFS compared to first-generation agents, with comparable efficacy and safety profiles observed within the third-generation agents. These findings support the use of these agents as standard first-line therapy, while highlighting the need to consider specific toxicity profiles and patient subgroups—such as elderly patients or those with L858R mutations—when selecting a specific agent.

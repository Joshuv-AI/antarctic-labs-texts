On jump minimizing liftings for 𝕊 1 \mathbb{S}^{1} -valued maps and connections with Ambrosio-Tortorelli-type Γ \Gamma -limits
Giovanni Bellettini, Roberta Marziani, Riccardo Scala

Abstract
This paper is concerned with the Γ \Gamma -limits of Ambrosio-Tortorelli-type functionals, for maps u u defined on an open bounded set Ω ⊂ ℝ n \Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n} and taking values in the unit circle 𝕊 1 ⊂ ℝ 2 \mathbb{S}^{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2} . Depending on the domain of the functional, two different Γ \Gamma -limits are possible, one of which is nonlocal, and related to the notion of jump minimizing lifting, i.e., a lifting of a map u u whose measure of the jump set is minimal. The latter requires ad hoc compactness results for sequences of liftings which, besides being interesting by themselves, also allow to deduce existence of a jump minimizing lifting.

Jump minimizing liftings,

-convergence,

Further directions and open problems.

Lifting theory is useful in several, apparently unrelated, contexts where
topological singularities
arise, such as screw dislocations in crystals, vortices in superconductors, the non-parametric Plateau problem in codimension-two and optimal transport problems.
This shows similarities between different problems to which a suitable version of the Ambrosio-Tortorelli approximations for
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-valued maps might be addressed.
In the following we briefly review some open problems which will be investigated in future works.

Dislocations and vortices
. Dislocations are line-defects in metals that locally alter the crystalline structure and are the main source of plastic slips. From a mathematical point of view they can be identified with codimension-two singularities.
In particular, in a simplified framework one can consider two-dimensional semi-discrete models for dislocations either of screw or edge type, which correspond to point singularities in a two-dimensional domain (see
[
4
,
25
,
31
,
37
,
26
,
23
]
and references therein). It is well known that models for screw dislocations share similarities with Ginzburg Landau models for superconductors
[
2
]
. In particular, in recent works
[
26
,
23
]
it was proposed a free-discontinuity model for screw dislocations. The latter is given by an energy of Mumford-Shah type for
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-valued maps which penalizes the measure of the jump set. From the analysis pursued in
[
26
,
23
]
it turns out that such a model is equivalent (to the leading order) to the Ginzburg Landau one. This seems to suggest that an Ambrosio Tortorelli approximation for screw dislocations, as well as for vortices in superconductors, is possible. In addition, this offers the possibility to extend the results of
[
26
,
23
]
to the three-dimensional setting (see
[
22
,
21
,
32
]
for models in dimension three).

The Plateau and optimal transport problems
. The Cartesian Plateau problem consists in finding an area-minimizing surface among all Cartesian surfaces spanning an appropriate Jordan space curve. While the codimension-one setting has been exhaustively understood
[
34
]
very few is known in codimension two. This has to do with the fact that the relaxed Cartesian area in codimension larger than one is not subadditive, and thus has no integral representation.
A couple of examples in codimension two where the computation of the relaxed graph area is possible are the vortex map
u_{V}
and the triple junction function
[
1
]
. Both are
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-valued maps with singularities, and the corresponding relaxed area has a nonlocal feature involving liftings, similarly as in (
1.6

[

38
,
9

10
]
and references therein). Also, the singular contribution appearing in the case of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-valued maps is related with optimal transport problems with different cost functions. In the specific case of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-valued Sobolev maps, the singularities should be optimally connected each other, in a similar fashion as for the minimal connection of dipoles in the minimization of the
BV
-seminorm of liftings
[
16

(see also
[
39
,
13
]
). Connecting the singularities in the relaxed Cartesian area is related with finding an optimal Cartesian vertical current filling the holes of the graph of the given map

In the simplified setting of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-constrained relaxation

and that is again related to an optimal transport question on how to connect the singularities of

.
We observe that

(E_{k}^{z})^{t_{k}}=\left\{x\in\Omega:2\pi\Big(z-\frac{1}{2}\Big)-t_{k}<\psi_{k}^{z}(x)=\psi_{k}(x)<2\pi\Big(z-\frac{1}{2}\Big)+t_{k}\right\}\,.

For every
k
let
(F_{k}^{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}
be the
Caccioppoli
partition of
\Omega
defined as

F_{k}^{z}:=\Big\{x\in\Omega:\psi_{k}(x)\in\Big(2\pi\Big(z-\frac{1}{2}\Big)-t_{k},2\pi\Big(z+1-\frac{1}{2}\Big)-t_{k}\Big)\Big\}\,.

Thus
\partial^{*}F_{k}^{z}\subset\partial^{*}(E_{k}^{z})^{t_{k}}\cup\partial^{*}(E_{k}^{z+1})^{t_{k}}
and

\displaystyle\sum_{{z}\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}F_{k}^{z})\leq{2}\sum_{{z}\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}(E_{k}^{z})^{t_{k}})\leq C\,,

and so for each

is a
Caccioppoli partition of
\Omega
, with equibounded (in

) total perimeter.

We finally introduce the map
\widetilde{\psi}_{k}\in SBV^{p}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
as

\widetilde{\psi}_{k}:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(\psi_{k}-(2\pi(z-\frac{1}{2})-t_{k})\Big)\chi_{F_{k}^{z}}-\pi-t_{k}=\psi_{k}-2\pi\sum_{{z}\in\mathbb{Z}}z\chi_{F_{k}^{z}},

(4.48)

which satisfies
-\pi\leq\widetilde{\psi}_{k}\leq\pi
.
Moreover, since
S_{\widetilde{\psi}_{k}}\subseteq S_{\psi_{k}}\cup\bigcup_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}\partial^{*}F_{k}^{z}
, by (
4.47
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widetilde{\psi}_{k}})+\|\widetilde{\psi}_{k}\|_{BV}\leq C,

(4.49)

for all
k\geq 1
. Therefore,
up to a not relabelled subsequence, we can also suppose

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}_{k}\rightarrow\widetilde{\psi}\qquad\text{ in }L^{1}(\Omega){\rm~and}~\text{ weakly}^{*}{\rm~in~}BV(\Omega)

for some
\widetilde{\psi}\in SBV^{p}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
As asserted in (
4.40
), we now want to show that
\widetilde{\psi}=0
a.e. on
\Omega
.
Let us go back to the functions
u_{k}
that, up to subsequences, are converging pointwise a.e. to
u
. Hence for a.e.

we have

u_{k}(x)-u(x)=e^{i\varphi(x)}(e^{i(\varphi_{k}(x)-\varphi(x))}-1)\to 0\,.

This in turn implies that the map

is converging pointwise a.e. to
0
, and by the

.
Then there is

such that for a.e.

and
for all

there is

and so by the dominated convergence theorem we conclude
\widetilde{\psi}=0
.

Step 2: compactness and
lower semicontinuity.

We first observe that
\psi_{k}-\widetilde{\psi}_{k}\in GSBV^{p}(\Omega)

. As a

). For all

in

By Theorem

Hence,

5.1
Some density and approximation results for
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
-valued maps

For

we denote by

its
({n-1})
-dimensional Minkowski content, i.e.,

\mathcal{M}^{n-1}(E)=\lim_{\rho\searrow 0}\frac{|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon\,{\rm dist\ }(x,E)<\rho\}|}{2\rho}\,,

provided the limit exists.

Proposition 5.1

(
Density in
SBV^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{S}}^{1})
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a
bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary,

u\in SBV^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{1})
and let

\widehat{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

be a bounded open set with

\Omega\subset\subset\widehat{\Omega}
. Then
u
has an extension

{\widehat{u}}\in SBV^{2}(\widehat{\Omega};\mathbb{S}^{1})
with

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{u}}\cap\partial\Omega)=0.

Moreover, there exists a sequence
(\widehat{u}_{k})\subset SBV^{2}(\widehat{\Omega};{\mathbb{S}}^{1})
such that:

(i)

\widehat{u}_{k}
converges to
{\widehat{u}}
in
L^{1}(\widehat{\Omega};{\mathbb{S}}^{1})
;

(ii)

\nabla\widehat{u}_{k}
converges to
\nabla{\widehat{u}}
in

L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
;

(iii)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{u}_{k}})=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{u}})
;

(iv)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{u}_{k}}\cap\overline{\Omega})=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{u}}\cap\Omega)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{u})
;

(v)

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\overline{S}_{\widehat{u}_{k}}\setminus S_{\widehat{u}_{k}})=0

and
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{u}_{k}}\cap K)=\mathcal{M}^{{n-1}}(S_{\widehat{u}_{k}}\cap K)
for every compact set
K\subset\widehat{\Omega}
.

Note that (ii)-(iii) guarantee the convergence of
{\rm MS}_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}(\widehat{u}_{k},1)
to

{\rm MS}_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}({\widehat{u}},1)
in
\widehat{\Omega}
.

Proof.

The proof follows by suitably
adapting
[
14
, Lemma 4.11]

and
[
7
, Proposition 5.3]

to the
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
-constrained case. For this reason we give here only the main steps.
According to
Remark
2.8
there exists a
lifting
\varphi\in SBV^{2}(\Omega)
of
u
. Since
\partial\Omega
is Lipschitz, by a standard reflection argument we can construct an extension
\widehat{\varphi}\in SBV^{2}(\widehat{\Omega})
of
\varphi
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{\varphi}}\cap\partial\Omega)=0.

By defining

{\widehat{u}}\colon\widehat{\Omega}\to\mathbb{S}^{1},\qquad{\widehat{u}}:=e^{i\widehat{\varphi}},

we immediately get that
{\widehat{u}}
is
an extension of
u
, and

\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}|\nabla{\widehat{u}}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}|\nabla\widehat{\varphi}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,,\quad\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{u}})\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{\varphi}})\,,

and thus
{\widehat{u}}\in SBV^{2}(\widehat{\Omega};\mathbb{S}^{1})
.
Moreover

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{u}}\cap\partial\Omega)\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{\varphi}}\cap\partial\Omega)=0.

(5.1)

By
[
17
, Lemma 4.3]
we know that, for every integer
k>0
, there exists
\widehat{u}_{k}\in SBV^{2}(\widehat{\Omega};\mathbb{S}^{1})

such that

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}|\nabla\widehat{u}_{k}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{u}_{k}})+k\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}|\widehat{u}_{k}-{\widehat{u}}|\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{w\in SBV^{2}(\widehat{\Omega};\mathbb{S}^{1})}\left(\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}|\nabla w|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{w})+k\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}|w-{\widehat{u}}|\,{\rm d}x\right)\,.

Clearly

\|\widehat{u}_{k}\|_{\infty}=1
and

\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}|\nabla\widehat{u}_{k}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{u}_{k}})+k\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}|\widehat{u}_{k}-{\widehat{u}}|\,{\rm d}x\leq\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}|\nabla{\widehat{u}}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{u}})<+\infty\,.

(5.2)

Thus, in particular,
(\widehat{u}_{k})
converges to
{\widehat{u}}
in

L^{1}(\widehat{\Omega};\mathbb{S}^{1})
as
k\to+\infty
, and
(i)
follows.
By
[
5
]
we also get, up to a not relabelled subsequence,

\nabla\widehat{u}_{k}\rightharpoonup\nabla{\widehat{u}}\quad\text{in
}L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})\,,\qquad\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{u}_{k}})\geq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{u}})\,.

This and (
5.2
) imply

\begin{split}\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}|\nabla{\widehat{u}}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{u}})=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\left(\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}|\nabla\widehat{u}_{k}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{u}_{k}})\right)\,,\end{split}

from which we readily deduce
(ii)
and
(iii)
.
Again by
[
5
]
and (
5.1
) we have

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{u}}\cap\Omega)\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{u}_{k}}\cap\Omega)\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{u}_{k}}\cap\overline{\Omega})\,,

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{u}}\setminus\Omega)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{u}}\setminus\overline{\Omega})\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\widehat{u}_{k}}\setminus\overline{\Omega})\,,

which in turn imply
(iv)
. Moreover,
invoking
[
17

we have

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\overline{S}_{\widehat{u}_{k}}\setminus S_{\widehat{u}_{k}})=0
.
Finally, thanks to the density estimate in
[

and arguing exactly as in

for every compact set

and

5.12
) we find

{\rm MM}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(v_{k},\Omega)=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{k}}|(S_{u})_{\xi_{k}}\cap\overline{\Omega}|+\int_{0}^{+\infty}(s+\varepsilon_{k})e^{-s}\frac{|(S_{u})_{s\varepsilon_{k}+\xi_{k}}\cap\overline{\Omega}|}{2(s\varepsilon_{k}+\xi_{k})}\,{\rm d}s\,.

(5.13)

From (
5.4
) it follows

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\frac{|(S_{u})_{s\varepsilon_{k}+\xi_{k}}\cap\overline{\Omega}|}{2(s\varepsilon_{k}+\xi_{k})}=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{u}\cap\overline{\Omega})\,.

This, together with the fact that
\int_{0}^{+\infty}(s+\varepsilon_{k})e^{-s}\,{\rm d}s\to 1
as
k\to+\infty
,
imply

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}{\rm MM}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(v_{k},\Omega)\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{u}\cap\overline{\Omega})\,.

(5.14)

Eventually, combining (
5.8
), (
5.10
) and (
5.14
)
we infer (
5.3
).
∎

5.3
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Step 1: Lower bound
. Let
\varepsilon_{k}\searrow 0
as
k\to+\infty
.
We have to show that, for every sequence
((u_{k},v_{k}))_{k\geq 1}\subset L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{1})\times L^{1}(\Omega)

converging to
(u,v)
in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{1})\times L^{1}(\Omega)
,

\liminf_{k\to+\infty}{{\rm AT}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}}(u_{k},v_{k})\geq{\rm MS}_{{\rm lift}}(u,v)\,.

(5.15)

We may assume

\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}{{\rm AT}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}}(u_{k},v_{k})\leq C<+\infty

(5.16)

so that
(u_{k},v_{k})\in\mathcal{A}_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}
,
v=1
a.e. in
\Omega

and,
up to a not relabelled subsequence,

\liminf_{k\to+\infty}{{\rm AT}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}}(u_{k},v_{k})=\lim_{k\to+\infty}{{\rm AT}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}}(u_{k},v_{k}),

\liminf_{k\to+\infty}{\rm MM}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(v_{k},\Omega)=\lim_{k\to+\infty}{\rm MM}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(v_{k},\Omega).

From Theorem
2.3

and the decoupling property (
2.2
),
it follows that

\begin{split}&\liminf_{k\to+\infty}{{\rm AT}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}}(u_{k},v_{k})\geq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{u})\,,\\
&\liminf_{{}_{k}\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}v_{k}^{2}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\geq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,,\\
&\liminf_{k\to+\infty}{\rm MM}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(v_{k},\Omega)\geq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{u})\,.\end{split}

In particular, from (
5.16
),
u\in SBV^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{1})
.
Hence it remains to show

\lim_{k\to+\infty}{\rm MM}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(v_{k},\Omega)\geq m_{2}[u].

(5.17)

For every
k\geq 1
we start by
selecting a lifting

\varphi_{k}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
of
u_{k}
.
Since
|\nabla u_{k}|=|\nabla\varphi_{k}|
we have

+\infty>C\geq{{\rm AT}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}}(u_{k},v_{k})=\int_{\Omega}\left(v_{k}^{2}|\nabla\varphi_{k}|^{2}+\varepsilon_{k}|\nabla v_{k}|^{2}+\frac{(v_{k}-1)^{2}}{4\varepsilon_{k}}\right)\,{\rm d}x\,.

Thus, using the coarea formula,

C\geq{\rm MM}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(v_{k},\Omega)\geq\int_{\Omega}(1-v_{k})|\nabla v_{k}|\,{\rm d}x\\
=\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}F_{k}^{t})\,{\rm d}t

(5.18)

for any
k\geq 1
, where
F_{k}^{t}:=\{x\in\Omega:v_{k}(x)\leq t\}
. Let
\eta^{\prime},\eta^{\prime\prime}\in(0,1)
,
\eta^{\prime}<\eta^{\prime\prime}
be fixed.
By (
5.18
) and the mean value theorem
there exists
t(k)\in(\eta^{\prime},\eta^{\prime\prime})

such that

C\geq(1-\eta^{\prime\prime})(\eta^{\prime\prime}-\eta^{\prime})\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}F_{k}^{t(k)})\,.

(5.19)

Moreover

C\geq\int_{\Omega}v_{k}^{2}|\nabla\varphi_{k}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\geq(\eta^{\prime})^{2}\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\Omega\setminus F^{t(k)}_{k}}|\nabla\varphi_{k}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,.

(5.20)

Then, setting

\phi_{k}:=\varphi_{k}\chi_{\Omega\setminus F^{t(k)}_{k}}\in SBV^{2}(\Omega),

we have
S_{\phi_{k}}\subset\partial^{*}F_{k}^{t(k)}
and,
for the absolutely continuous parts of the
gradients,
\nabla\phi_{k}=\nabla\varphi_{k}\chi_{\Omega\setminus F_{k}^{t(k)}}
and so,
from (
5.20
) and (
5.19
),

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{k}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\phi_{k}})\leq C(\eta^{\prime},\eta^{\prime\prime})

(5.21)

for some
C(\eta^{\prime},\eta^{\prime\prime})>0

depending on
\eta^{\prime},\eta^{\prime\prime}
and independent of
k
. Let also

\overline{u}_{k}:=e^{i\phi_{k}}=u_{k}\chi_{\Omega\setminus F_{k}^{t(k)}}+(1,0)\chi_{F_{k}^{t(k)}},

which, by
(
5.19
), are
uniformly bounded in
BV(\Omega;{\mathbb{S}}^{1})
. Since

|F_{k}^{t(k)}|\to 0,

(5.22)

the sequence
(\overline{u}_{k})
weakly star converges to
u

in
BV(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{1})
. Hence, using (
5.21
),
we can apply Theorem
3.3

to the sequence

(\phi_{k})_{k\geq 1}
(with
p=2
) and get, for a not-relabelled subsequence, a
Caccioppoli
partition
(E_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\Omega
, sequences
(d^{(i)}_{k})_{k\geq 1}\subset\mathbb{Z}

for any integer
i\geq 1
and a lifting
\varphi_{\infty}\in GSBV^{2}(\Omega)

of
u
, such that

\begin{split}&\lim_{k\to+\infty}(\phi_{k}(x)-2\pi d^{(i)}_{k})=\varphi_{\infty}(x)\qquad\forall i\in{\mathbb{N}},\text{ for a.e. }x\in E_{i}\,,\\
&\lim_{k\to+\infty}|\phi_{k}(x)-2\pi d^{(i)}_{k}|=+\infty\qquad\forall i\in{\mathbb{N}},\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega\setminus E_{i}.\end{split}

Again, using
(
5.22
),
the same holds for
\varphi_{k}
, i.e.,

\begin{split}&\lim_{k\to+\infty}(\varphi_{k}(x)-2\pi d^{(i)}_{k})=\varphi_{\infty}(x)\qquad\forall i\in{\mathbb{N}},\text{ for a.e. }x\in E_{i}\,,\\
&\lim_{k\to+\infty}|\varphi_{k}(x)-2\pi d^{(i)}_{k}|=+\infty\qquad\forall i\in{\mathbb{N}},\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega\setminus E_{i}.\end{split}

(5.23)

Then (
5.17
) follows if we show

\liminf_{k\to+\infty}{\rm MM}(v_{k},\Omega)\geq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\varphi_{\infty}}),

(5.24)

since, being
\varphi_{\infty}
a lifting of
u
, we have

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\varphi_{\infty}})\geq m_{2}[u].

For any integer
K\geq 1
we consider the truncated function

\varphi_{k}^{i,K}:=((\varphi_{k}-2\pi d_{k}^{(i)})\wedge K)\vee(-K)\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)\,,

(5.25)

and

\phi_{k}^{i,K}:=\varphi_{k}^{i,K}\chi_{\Omega\setminus F_{k}^{t(k)}}.

Since
S_{\phi_{k}^{i,K}}\subset\partial^{*}F_{k}^{t(k)}
and
\text{{\rm\textlbrackdbl}}{\phi_{k}^{i,K}}\text{{\rm\textrbrackdbl}}\leq K
, we have

\begin{split}|D\phi_{k}^{i,K}|(\Omega)&=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{k}|\chi_{\{|\varphi_{k}-2\pi d_{k}^{(i)}|<K\}}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{S_{\phi_{k}^{i,K}}}\text{{\rm\textlbrackdbl}}{\phi_{k}^{i,K}}\text{{\rm\textrbrackdbl}}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{k}|\,{\rm d}x+K\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}F_{k}^{t(k)})\leq C\,.\end{split}

Hence, up to a subsequence (depending on
K
),
(\phi_{k}^{i,K})
converges
to some
\phi^{i,K}_{\infty}
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
.
Moreover
from (
5.23
) it holds
\phi^{i,K}_{\infty}:=(\varphi_{\infty}\wedge K)\vee(-K)
in
E_{i}
and

\Omega\setminus E_{i}=F_{i}^{+}\cup F_{i}^{-}
are such that
\phi^{i,K}_{\infty}=\pm K
in
F_{i}^{\pm}
.
As
|F_{k}^{t(k)}|\to 0
it follows that
\varphi_{k}^{i,K}

converges to
\phi_{\infty}^{i,K}
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
.
Hence the sequence

((\varphi_{k}^{i,K},v_{k}))
converges
to
(\phi_{\infty}^{i,K},1)
in
L^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{1}(\Omega)
and

\begin{split}\int_{A}\left(v_{k}^{2}|\nabla\varphi_{k}^{i,K}|^{2}+\varepsilon_{k}|\nabla v_{k}|^{2}+\frac{(v_{k}-1)^{2}}{4\varepsilon_{k}}\right)\,{\rm d}x\leq C\,,\end{split}

for any open set
A\subset\Omega
, for some

C>0
independent of
k
. Thus, from the decoupling property (
2.2
),

\begin{split}&\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\int_{A}v_{k}^{2}|\nabla\varphi_{k}^{i,K}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\geq\int_{A}|\nabla\phi_{\infty}^{i,K}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,,\\[10.00002pt]
&\liminf_{k\to+\infty}{\rm MM}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(v_{k},A)\geq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\phi_{\infty}^{i,K}}\cap A).\end{split}

(5.26)

In particular
\phi_{\infty}^{i,K}\in SBV^{2}(\Omega)
.
We fix an integer
N\geq 1
and set,
for all
i=1,\dots,N
,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{i}^{K}:=(S_{\phi_{\infty}^{i,K}}\cap\partial^{*}E_{i})\cap\Omega\,,\qquad S^{K}_{i}:=S_{\phi^{i,K}_{\infty}}\setminus\partial^{*}E_{i}\,,

\Sigma^{N,K}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\Sigma_{i}^{K}\,,\qquad S^{N,K}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}S_{i}^{K}\,.

We now argue exactly as in the proof of Lemma
4.2
, so, given
\delta\in(0,1)
, after defining the families of balls
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}_{j}

(
j=1,\dots,2N
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}\cap\mathcal{B}_{j}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(B\cap S_{j}^{K})

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{j}^{K})-\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S^{N,K}\cup\Sigma^{N,K})}{1-\delta}\,,

(5.27)

for all
j=1,\dots,N
, and

\displaystyle\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}\cap\mathcal{B}_{N+h}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(B\cap\Sigma_{h}^{K})\geq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Sigma_{h}^{K})-\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S^{N,K}\cup\Sigma^{N,K})}{1-\delta}\,,

(5.28)

for all
h=1,\dots,N
.
From (
5.26
) we know that, for all
j=1,\dots,N
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to+\infty}{\rm MM}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(v_{k},B)\geq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{j}^{K}\cap B)\,,

(5.29)

for any
B\in\mathcal{B}\cap\mathcal{B}_{j}
, and for all
h=1,\dots,N

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to+\infty}{\rm MM}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(v_{k},B)\geq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Sigma_{h}^{K}\cap B)\,,

(5.30)

for any ball
B\in\mathcal{B}\cap\mathcal{B}_{N+h}
.
So, summing (
5.29
) over all
B\in\mathcal{B}\cap\mathcal{B}_{j}
and (
5.30
) over all
B\in\mathcal{B}\cap\mathcal{B}_{N+h}
, and then over
j,h
respectively, we conclude again, using (
5.27
) and (
5.28
),

\begin{split}\liminf_{k\to+\infty}{\rm MM}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(v_{k},\Omega)&=\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}{\rm MM}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(v_{k},\Omega)\\
&\geq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{j}^{K})+\sum_{h=1}^{N}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Sigma_{h}^{K})-\frac{2\delta N\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S^{N,K}\cup\Sigma^{N,K})}{1-\delta}\end{split}

and by the arbitrariness of

,

\lim_{k\to+\infty}{\rm MM}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(v_{k},\Omega)\geq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S^{N,K}\cup\Sigma^{N,K})\,.

The left hand-side in the above inequality is a limit, hence it does not depend on the subsequence, and so in particular on
K
and
N
.
Letting first
N\rightarrow+\infty
and then

we finally get

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to+\infty}{\rm MM}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(v_{k},\Omega)\geq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\varphi_{\infty}})\,,

and (
5.17
) follows.

Step 2: Upper bound
. Let
\varepsilon_{k}\searrow 0
and

u\in SBV^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{S}}^{1})
. We
have to find a sequence
((u_{k},v_{k}))\subset\mathcal{A}_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}
converging to
(u,1)
in
L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{S}}^{1})\times L^{1}(\Omega)
and

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}{{\rm AT}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}}(u_{k},v_{k})\leq{\rm MS}_{{\rm lift}}(u,1)\,.

(5.31)

We notice that we cannot follow the strategy used in the proof
of the upper bound in Theorem
1.1
, since that construction cannot
ensure the functions
u_{k}
to belong to
W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{1})
.

By Corollary
3.2

we can select a jump minimizing lifting

\varphi\in GSBV^{2}(\Omega)
of
u
,

u=e^{i\varphi}
and

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\varphi})=m_{2}[u].

(5.32)

The main difference with the construction in Theorem
1.1
is that we will modify
u
in a neighbourhood of
S_{\varphi}
instead of
S_{u}
.
We divide the proof into two cases.

Case 1:

\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Let
\widehat{\Omega}
, with
\Omega\subset\subset\widehat{\Omega}\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, be open.
Since
\varphi\in SBV^{2}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, by Proposition
5.2
we can assume, without loss of generality,
that
\varphi\in SBV^{2}(\widehat{\Omega})
with

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\varphi}\cap\partial\Omega)=0\,,\quad\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\overline{S}_{\varphi}\setminus S_{\varphi})=0\,,\quad\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\varphi}\cap K)=\mathcal{M}^{n-1}(S_{\varphi}\cap K)\,,

(5.33)

for every compact set
K\subset\widehat{\Omega}
.
In this way, arguing as in
[
7
,
8
]
we can construct
(\varphi_{k},v_{k})\in W^{1,2}(\widehat{\Omega})\times W^{1,2}(\widehat{\Omega})

such that
(\varphi_{k},v_{k})\to(\varphi,1)
in
L^{1}(\widehat{\Omega})\times L^{1}(\widehat{\Omega})
and

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}{\rm AT}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(\varphi_{k},v_{k})\leq{\rm MS}(\varphi,1)\,.

(5.34)

Next we let
u_{k}:=e^{i\varphi_{k}}\in W^{1,2}(\widehat{\Omega};{\mathbb{S}}^{1})
, so that
(u_{k},v_{k})\to(u,1)
in
L^{1}(\widehat{\Omega};{\mathbb{S}}^{1})\times L^{1}(\widehat{\Omega})
. Moreover, from (
5.32
), (
5.34
),
|\nabla u_{k}|=|\nabla\varphi_{k}|
and
|\nabla u|=|\nabla\varphi|

we get

{\rm AT}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(\varphi_{k},v_{k})={{\rm AT}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}}(u_{k},v_{k})\,,\quad{\rm MS}(\varphi,1)={\rm MS}_{\rm lift}(u,1)

and so

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}{{\rm AT}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}}(u_{k},v_{k})\leq{\rm MS}_{\rm lift}(u,1)\,.

Case 2:

\varphi\notin L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
For
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
N\geq 1
we let
\varphi^{(N)}:=\varphi\wedge N\vee(-N)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Then, as
N\to+\infty
, we have

\varphi^{(N)}\to\varphi\quad\text{ in }L^{1}(\Omega)\,,\quad\nabla\varphi^{(N)}\to\nabla\varphi\quad\text{ in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\,,

so that

u^{(N)}:=e^{i\varphi^{N}}\to e^{i\varphi}=u\quad\text{ in }L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{S}}^{1})\,,\quad\nabla u^{(N)}\to\nabla u\quad\text{ in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})\,.

In addition,

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\varphi^{(N)}})\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\varphi})\,.

This in turn implies

\begin{split}\limsup_{N\to+\infty}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi^{(N)}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\varphi^{(N)}})\right)&\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\varphi})\\
&\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+m_{2}[u].\end{split}

(5.35)

For each
N\geq 1
we can argue as in case 1 and find a sequence
(u_{k}^{(N)},v_{k}^{(N)})\to(u^{(N)},1)
in
L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{S}}^{1})\times L^{1}(\Omega)

satisfying

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}{{\rm AT}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}}(u_{k}^{(N)},v_{k}^{(N)})\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi^{(N)}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\varphi}^{(N)})\,.

(5.36)

Combining (
5.35
) with (
5.36
) we have

\limsup_{N\to+\infty}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}{{\rm AT}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}}(u_{k}^{(N)},v_{k}^{(N)})\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+m_{2}[u].

Now we conclude using
a diagonal argument. Namely, for any fixed
N\geq 1
there exists
k_{N}\geq 1
such that for
k\geq k_{N}
:

•

\|u_{k}^{(N)}-u^{(N)}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{1})}+\|v_{k}^{(N)}-1\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{N}
;

•

{{\rm AT}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}}(u_{k}^{(N)},v_{k}^{(N)})\leq\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi^{(N)}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{\varphi}^{(N)})+\frac{1}{N}
.

Without loss of generality we may assume
k_{N+1}\geq k_{N}
. Now, for every
k\geq 1
, we take
(u_{k},v_{k}):=(u_{k}^{(N)},v_{k}^{(N)})
if
k\in[k_{N},k_{N+1})\cap\mathbb{N}
and the proof is concluded.
∎

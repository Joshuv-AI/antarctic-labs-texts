On directional second-order tangent sets of analytic sets and applications in optimization

Abstract
. In this paper we study directional second-order tangent sets of real and complex analytic sets. 1.
Introduction

Second-order tangent constructions are fundamental tools in variational analysis, optimization, and nonsmooth geometry. They arise naturally in the study of second-order optimality conditions, stability, and sensitivity analysis for constrained problems, particularly when the feasible set lacks smooth manifold structure. A systematic account of second-order tangent sets and their role in vector and scalar optimization can be found in the survey of Giorgi, Jiménez, and Novo
[
3
]
and in the monograph of Khan, Tammer, and Zălinescu
[
6
]
.

For a closed set
X\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}
(
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
), the first-order tangent cone
T_{0}X
describes feasible first-order directions. However, first-order information alone is insufficient to characterize second-order behavior of objective functions or to guarantee quadratic growth of minimizers. This motivates the introduction of directional second-order tangent sets, which encode second-order corrections along a fixed tangent direction
u\in T_{0}X
.

Several notions of second-order tangent sets have been proposed in the literature, differing in their definitions and properties; see
[
3
,
6
]
. In the context of analytic sets, two particularly natural constructions emerge. The
geometric directional second-order tangent set

T^{2}_{0,u}X
is defined via limits of second-order approximations along analytic arcs contained in
X
. This definition captures genuine geometric realizability but is typically non-algebraic and difficult to compute explicitly. On the other hand, the
algebraic directional second-order tangent set

T^{2,a}_{0,u}X
is defined through the initial homogeneous parts of analytic equations defining
X
and their first-order derivatives, making it algebraically tractable.

In contrast with the classical first-order theory, where, in the complex case, geometric and algebraic tangent cones coincide by Whitney’s theorem
[
14
,
15
]
, and in the real case satisfy only inclusion relations
[
7
,
12
]
-the relationship between geometric and algebraic second-order tangent sets is more subtle. One of the main objectives of this paper is to clarify this relationship.

Our first contribution is to show that for any analytic set
X
and any nonzero tangent direction
u
, the inclusion

T^{2}_{0,u}X\subseteq T^{2,a}_{0,u}X

always holds. We then demonstrate, by explicit real and complex analytic examples, that this inclusion is generally strict. These examples highlight the existence of algebraically admissible second-order directions that cannot be realized by analytic arcs in
X
, revealing a fundamental obstruction to purely algebraic second-order analysis.

To overcome this difficulty, we introduce a second-jet framework along fixed tangent directions. We define spaces of second jets of analytic arcs in
X
and in its tangent cone
C_{0}X
, and study the natural mapping between them. Our central result shows that the equality
T^{2}_{0,u}X=T^{2,a}_{0,u}X
holds whenever this second-jet map is surjective. We establish this surjectivity for several important classes of analytic sets, including smooth analytic manifolds, homogeneous analytic cones, hypersurfaces with nondegenerate tangent directions, and analytic complete intersections that are nondegenerate along
u
.

The final part of the paper is devoted to applications in optimization. Building on the framework of Bonnans, Cominetti, and Shapiro
[
1
]
and Rockafellar and Wets
[
13
]
, we derive second-order necessary and sufficient conditions for local optimality of
C^{2}
functions constrained to analytic sets. We show that geometric directional second-order tangent sets naturally enter these conditions and explain why replacing them with their algebraic counterparts may lead to false conclusions. Under the jet-surjectivity conditions identified earlier, however, the two notions coincide, allowing all second-order conditions to be checked algebraically.

Overall, this work provides a unified geometric–algebraic perspective on second-order tangent sets of analytic sets and clarifies their precise role in second-order optimization theory.

Remark 2.5
.

T^{2}_{0,u}X

In fact, let

This is an analytic (indeed algebraic) hypersurface in

.

Let

By definition,

Conversely, for any

Proposition 2.6

(Analytic-arc characterization of

)

Let

be an analytic set with
0\in X
, and let

.
Then for

(1)

(2)

There exist

Assume there exists an analytic curve
\gamma:(0,\varepsilon)\to X

\gamma(t)=t\,u+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}w+o(t^{2})\quad(t\to 0).

Choose any sequence

Conversely, assume

t_{k}\downarrow 0

t_{k}u+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}w_{k}\in X\qquad\forall k.

(2.1)

Consider the set

Since

is analytic and the mapping

By (

(t_{k},w_{k})\to(0,w)

By the Curve Selection Lemma for subanalytic (respectively analytic) sets

[

there exist

In particular,

Algebraic directional second-order tangent sets of analytic sets

In this section we study the second-order tangent set of an analytic set
X

U\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}
at
0\in X
in the direction
u\in\mathbb{K}^{n}

For an analytic function
f
in a neighborhood

at a point
x^{0}\in U

\nabla f(x^{0}):=\big(\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}(x^{0}),\ldots,\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}(x^{0})\big);

its
Hessian matrix
at
x^{0}\in U
is defined by

\nabla^{2}f(x^{0}):=\big(\dfrac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}(x^{0})\big)_{i,j=1,\cdots,n}.

More generally, let
\nabla^{(p)}(x^{0})
denote the
p

f
at
x^{0}
, and for any
y\in\mathbb{K}^{n}

[

]

\nabla^{(p)}(x^{0})[y]^{p}:=\nabla^{p}(x^{0})\underbrace{(y)\ldots(y)}_{\mbox{$p$ times}},

where
[y]^{p}
denote the
p

y\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
, and
\nabla^{(p)}(x^{0})[y]^{p}
the outer product of two arrays
\nabla^{(p)}(x^{0})
and
[y]^{p}
.
For example,

\nabla^{(1)}(x^{0})[y]^{1}=\left<\nabla f(x^{0}),y\right>,\quad f^{(2)}(x^{0})[y]^{2}=\left<\nabla^{2}f(x^{0})y,y\right>.

Then for
h\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
such that
x^{0}+h\in U
, the Taylor’s expansion of
f
at
x^{0}
can be written as

f(x^{0}+h)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{i!}\nabla^{(i)}(x^{0})[h]^{i}.

(2.2)

Let
\mathcal{I}(X,0)
denote the ideal of germs of analytic functions in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
about
0
which vanish on the germ of
X
at
0\in\mathbb{K}^{n}

Definition 2.7
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}
be an analytic set with
0\in X
. The
algebraic second-order tangent set
of
X
at
0
in the direction

is defined by

T^{2,a}_{0,u}X:=\Big\{w\in\mathbb{K}^{n}|\dfrac{1}{2}\left<\nabla f^{[*]}(u),w\right>+f^{[*]+1}(u)=0,\forall f\in\mathcal{I}(X,0)\Big\}.

Note that, if
\nabla f^{[*]}(u)\not=0
,
T^{2,a}_{0,u}X
is a hyperplane in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
.

Remark 2.8
.

T^{2,a}_{0,u}X
is, in general, not a cone in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
.

In fact, let

X:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{K}^{2}:y-x^{2}=0\},\qquad 0=(0,0)\in X,\qquad u:=(1,0).

The ideal
\mathcal{I}(X,0)
is generated by

f(x,y)=y-x^{2}.

Its lowest–degree homogeneous part is

f^{[*]}(x,y)=y,

and the next homogeneous part is

f^{[*]+1}(x,y)=-x^{2}.

We compute

\nabla f^{[*]}(x,y)=(0,1),\qquad\nabla f^{[*]}(u)=(0,1),\qquad f^{[*]+1}(u)=-1.

By definition,

T^{2,a}_{0,u}X=\left\{w\in\mathbb{K}^{2}:\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla f^{[*]}(u),w\rangle+f^{[*]+1}(u)=0\right\}.

Since
\langle(0,1),(w_{1},w_{2})\rangle=w_{2}
, this condition becomes

\frac{1}{2}w_{2}-1=0,\quad\text{that is,}\quad w_{2}=2.

Therefore

T^{2,a}_{0,u}X=\{(w_{1},w_{2})\in\mathbb{K}^{2}:w_{2}=2\}.

2.3.
Distinction of tangent sets

We show in this section that
T^{2}_{0,u}X\subsetneq T^{2,a}_{0,u}X
.

Firstly, we consider the case where
X
is a
real analytic set
. For example, let us consider

X=\bigl\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\ f(x,y,z)=z^{2}-x^{3}y^{3}=0\bigr\},\qquad 0\in X,

and take
u\in T_{0}X\setminus\{0\}
.

The tangent cone of
X
at
0\in X
is

T_{0}X=\{(x_{1},x_{2},0)|x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Hence, we may take
u=(u_{1},u_{2},0)\not=(0,0,0)

Now we compute
T^{2,a}_{0,u}X

For every
f\in\mathcal{I}(X,0)
,

\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla f^{[*]}(u),w\rangle+f^{[*]+1}(u)=0,\qquad\text{and also}\qquad f^{[*]}(u)=0.

Here
\mathcal{I}(X,0)=(f)

f^{[*]}(x,y,z)=z^{2},\qquad f^{[*]+1}\equiv 0,\qquad\nabla(z^{2})=(0,0,2z).

and the constraint
x_{k}\in X
is

\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}c_{k}\Bigr)^{2}=\Bigl(t_{k}u_{1}+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}a_{k}\Bigr)^{3}\Bigl(t_{k}u_{2}+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}b_{k}\Bigr)^{3}.

(2.5)

The left side of (
2.5
) satisfies

\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}c_{k}\Bigr)^{2}\sim\frac{1}{4}t_{k}^{4}c_{k}^{2},

while the right side is at least of order
t_{k}^{6}
whenever
u\neq 0
. Hence
c_{k}\to 0
, and therefore

w_{3}=0\qquad\text{for every }w\in T^{2}_{0,u}X.

We consider four cases.

Case 1:

u_{1}u_{2}>0
.

Then the right-hand side of (
2.5
) has leading term
t_{k}^{6}(u_{1}u_{2})^{3}>0
, and one can satisfy
(
2.5
) by choosing
c_{k}
of order
t_{k}
(so that
z_{k}
is order
t_{k}^{3}
), while still having
c_{k}\to 0
.
The sequences
a_{k},b_{k}
can converge arbitrarily. Thus

u_{1}u_{2}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ T^{2}_{0,u}X=\{(w_{1},w_{2},0):w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Case 2:

u_{1}u_{2}<0
.

Then the leading coefficient on the right-hand side of (
2.5
) is
(u_{1}u_{2})^{3}<0
, while the left-hand side is a square and hence
\geq 0
.
For
k
large the right-hand side stays negative, so equality is impossible. Hence

u_{1}u_{2}<0\ \Longrightarrow\ T^{2}_{0,u}X=\varnothing.

Case 3:

u_{1}=0,\ u_{2}\neq 0
.

Then the right-hand side of (
2.5
) is of order
t_{k}^{9}
if
a_{k}
tends to a nonzero limit (since
x
starts at order
t_{k}^{2}
).
To match the left-hand side, one needs
c_{k}
of order
t_{k}^{5/2}
, which is possible only if the relevant coefficient is nonnegative; this forces

w_{1}u_{2}\geq 0
(and still
w_{3}=0
). Thus

u_{1}=0,\ u_{2}\neq 0\ \Longrightarrow\ T^{2}_{0,u}X=\{(w_{1},w_{2},0):w_{1}u_{2}\geq 0\}.

Case 4:
u_{2}=0,\ u_{1}\neq 0
.

By symmetry,

u_{2}=0,\ u_{1}\neq 0\ \Longrightarrow\ T^{2}_{0,u}X=\{(w_{1},w_{2},0):w_{2}u_{1}\geq 0\}.

In summary, for every
u\in T_{0}X\setminus\{0\}
,
T^{2,a}_{0,u}X=\mathbb{R}^{3},

whereas
T^{2}_{0,u}X
is

\begin{cases}\{(w_{1},w_{2},0):w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{R}\},&\text{if }u_{1}u_{2}>0,\\[5.69054pt]
\varnothing,&\text{if }u_{1}u_{2}<0,\\[5.69054pt]
\{(w_{1},w_{2},0):w_{1}u_{2}\geq 0\},&\text{if }u_{1}=0,\ u_{2}\neq 0,\\[5.69054pt]
\{(w_{1},w_{2},0):w_{2}u_{1}\geq 0\},&\text{if }u_{2}=0,\ u_{1}\neq 0.\end{cases}

Hence we have in the real case

T^{2}_{0,u}X\subsetneq T^{2,a}_{0,u}X.

Now we consider the case where
X
is a
complex analytic set
. For example, let us consider

X=\bigl\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{C}^{3}:\ z^{2}-x^{3}y^{3}=0\bigr\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{3},\qquad 0\in X.

Consider
u\in T_{0}X\setminus\{0\}
, where

T_{0}X=\{(x,y,0)|x,y\in\mathbb{C}\}.

Hence
u=(u_{1},u_{2},0)\in\mathbb{C}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
.

Similar to the real case, we can compute

T^{2,a}_{0,u}X=\mathbb{C}^{3}\qquad\text{for all }u\in T_{0}X\setminus\{0\}.

Now we compute
T^{2}_{0,u}X
. Take
w=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
. We ask whether there exist
t_{k}\to 0
in
\mathbb{C}
and

w_{k}\to w
such that

x_{k}:=t_{k}u+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}w_{k}\in X.

Writing
w_{k}=(a_{k},b_{k},c_{k})
, we have

x_{k}=\Bigl(t_{k}u_{1}+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}a_{k},\ \ t_{k}u_{2}+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}b_{k},\ \ \frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}c_{k}\Bigr),

and the equation
x_{k}\in X
becomes

\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}c_{k}\Bigr)^{2}=\Bigl(t_{k}u_{1}+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}a_{k}\Bigr)^{3}\Bigl(t_{k}u_{2}+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}b_{k}\Bigr)^{3}.

(2.6)

Similar to the real case, we have

w_{3}=0\qquad\text{for all }w\in T^{2}_{0,u}X.

Then the right-hand side of (
2.6
) has leading term

t_{k}^{6}(u_{1}u_{2})^{3}\in\mathbb{C},

and we can always choose

c_{k}=2t_{k}\sqrt{(u_{1}u_{2})^{3}}\,(1+o(1)),

(using any branch of the complex square root) so that (
2.6
) holds.
There is no restriction on the signs of
u_{1},u_{2}
, nor on the values of
w_{1},w_{2}
. Thus, for every
u\neq 0
with
u_{3}=0
,

T^{2}_{0,u}X=\{(w_{1},w_{2},0):\ w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{C}\}.

Therefore, for all
u=(u_{1},u_{2},0)\neq 0
,

T^{2,a}_{0,u}X=\mathbb{C}^{3},\qquad T^{2}_{0,u}X=\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\{0\}.

Hence, in the complex case,
T^{2}_{0,u}X\subsetneq T^{2,a}_{0,u}X.

3.
Second jet spaces and directional second-order tangent sets under the surjectivity of second jet maps

The equality

T^{2}_{0,u}X\;=\;T^{2,a}_{0,u}X

fails precisely when there exist formal second–order directions compatible with the defining equations of
X
that cannot be realized by actual analytic curves in
X
.

In this section we propose some conditions for which these two second-order tangent sets coincide. We show that there are several good enough classes of analytic sets in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
satisfied the proposed conditions.

3.1.
Second jets along a fixed direction

Let
X\subseteq K^{n}
be an analytic set with
0\in X
, and let

u\in T_{0}X\setminus\{0\}.

Consider analytic curves

\gamma(t)=tu+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}w+o(t^{2}),\qquad\gamma(0)=0.

Two such curves are said to
have the same
2
–jet at
0
if their expansions agree up to order
t^{2}
.

Define

J^{2}(X)_{0,u}

to be the set of
2
–jets of analytic curves

\gamma:(\mathbb{K},0)\to(X,0)

satisfying

\gamma^{\prime}(0)=u.

An element of
J^{2}(X)_{0,u}
is represented by a pair
(u,w)
, modulo higher–order terms.

Now we define the second jets on the tangent cone of
X
at
0
. Let
C_{0}X
denote the tangent cone of
X
at
0
, defined by the initial forms of the ideal
\mathcal{I}(X)
.

Define

J^{2}(C_{0}X)_{u}

as the set of
2
–jets of curves
\eta(t)\subseteq C_{0}X
of the form

\eta(t)=tu+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}w+o(t^{2}).

Since
C_{0}X
is homogeneous, these jets are purely algebraic objects determined by the initial equations.

There is a canonical map

\Phi:J^{2}(X)_{0,u}\longrightarrow J^{2}(C_{0}X)_{u}

defined as follows: given an analytic arc
\gamma(t)\subseteq X
, replace each defining equation of
X
by its initial (lowest–degree) part and discard all terms of order
>2
.
Intuitively,
\Phi
forgets higher–order corrections and retains only the algebraic constraints imposed by the tangent cone.

Surjectivity of
\Phi
means that every algebraically admissible second–order jet along
u
is realizable by a genuine analytic curve in
X
. Formally, for every

(u,w)\in J^{2}(C_{0}X)_{u},

there exists an analytic curve
\gamma(t)\subseteq X
such that

\gamma(t)=tu+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}w+o(t^{2}).

3.2.
Representation of algebraic second-order tangent sets via second jets

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}
be an analytic set with
0\in X
, let
C_{0}X
be its algebraic tangent cone at
0
, and fix

u\in T_{0}X\setminus\{0\}.

Then

T^{2,a}_{0,u}X=\bigl\{\,w\in\mathbb{K}^{n}:(u,w)\in J^{2}(C_{0}X)_{u}\,\bigr\}.

Proof.

Recall that
C_{0}X
is defined by the ideal generated by the initial homogeneous parts

f^{[*]}
of all
f\in\mathcal{I}(X,0)
.
By definition,
(u,w)\in J^{2}(C_{0}X)_{u}
if and only if there exists an analytic curve

\eta(t)\subseteq C_{0}X
of the form

\eta(t)=t\,u+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}w+o(t^{2}).

Fix
f\in\mathcal{I}(X,0)
and let
m=\deg f^{[*]}
.
Since
f^{[*]}
is homogeneous of degree
m
, we have

f^{[*]}(\eta(t))=f^{[*]}\!\left(tu+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}w+o(t^{2})\right)=t^{m}f^{[*]}\!\left(u+\frac{1}{2}t\,w+o(t)\right).

Expanding at
u
yields

f^{[*]}(\eta(t))=t^{m}f^{[*]}(u)+t^{m+1}\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla f^{[*]}(u),w\rangle+o(t^{m+1}).

Since
u\in C_{0}X
, we have
f^{[*]}(u)=0
for all
f\in\mathcal{I}(X,0)
. Moreover,
\eta(t)\subseteq C_{0}X
follows that

f^{[*]}(\eta(t))=0
. It is equivalent to

\dfrac{1}{2}\langle\nabla f^{[*]}(u),w\rangle=0,\ \forall f\in\mathcal{I}(X,0).
Hence

(u,w)\in J^{2}(C_{0}X)_{u}\mbox{ if and only if }\langle\nabla f^{[*]}(u),w\rangle=0,\ \forall f\in\mathcal{I}(X,0).

(3.1)

On the other hand, by definition,

T^{2,a}_{0,u}X=\left\{w\in\mathbb{K}^{n}:\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla f^{[*]}(u),w\rangle+f^{[*]+1}(u)=0,\ \forall f\in\mathcal{I}(X,0)\right\}.

We claim that
f^{[*]+1}(u)=0
for all
f\in\mathcal{I}(X,0)
.
Indeed, since
u\in T_{0}X\setminus{\{0\}}\subseteq C_{0}X\setminus{\{0\}}
, by Proposition
2.6
, there exists an analytic arc

\alpha(t)\subseteq X
such that

\alpha(t)=t\,u+o(t),\qquad t\to 0.

Because
f
vanishes identically on
X
, we have
f(\alpha(t))\equiv 0
.
Writing the homogeneous expansion

f(x)=f^{[*]}(x)+f^{[*]+1}(x)+(\text{terms of degree }\geq m+2),

substituting
\alpha(t)
and observing that
f^{[*]}(u)=0
, we obtain

0=f(\alpha(t))=t^{m+1}f^{[*]+1}(u)+o(t^{m+1}).

Dividing by
t^{m+1}
and letting
t\to 0
yields
f^{[*]+1}(u)=0
.

Therefore,

T^{2,a}_{0,u}X=\left\{w\in\mathbb{K}^{n}:\langle\nabla f^{[*]}(u),w\rangle=0,\ \forall f\in\mathcal{I}(X,0)\right\},

which coincides exactly with the characterization of

(u,w)\in J^{2}(C_{0}X)_{u}
given in (
3.1
).

This proves the equality

T^{2,a}_{0,u}X=\{\,w\in\mathbb{K}^{n}:(u,w)\in J^{2}(C_{0}X)_{u}\,\}.

∎

3.3.
Directional second-order tangent sets under surjectivity of second jet spaces

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
X\subseteq K^{n}
be an analytic set with
0\in X
, and fix

u\in T_{0}X\setminus\{0\}.

Let
C_{0}X
denote the tangent cone of
X
at
0
.
Assume that the natural map

\Phi_{u}:J^{2}(X)_{0,u}\longrightarrow J^{2}(C_{0}X)_{u}

from
2
-jets of analytic arcs in
X
tangent to
u
to
2
-jets of arcs in
C_{0}X
tangent to
u
is surjective.
Then

T^{2}_{0,u}X=T^{2,a}_{0,u}X.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.9
, it suffices to show that
T^{2,a}_{0,u}X\subseteq T^{2}_{0,u}X
under surjectivity of
\Phi_{u}
.

Let
w\in T^{2,a}_{0,u}X
. It follows from the representation given in Proposition
3.1
that

(u,w)\in J^{2}(C_{0}X)_{u}.

Since
\Phi_{u}
is surjective, there exists a
2
-jet

such that

\Phi_{u}(j)=(u,w)\in J^{2}(C_{0}X)_{u}.

By definition of
J^{2}(X)_{0,u}
, there exists an analytic arc

\gamma:(\mathbb{K},0)\to(X,0)

with
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=u
and
2
-jet equal to
j
. Hence
\gamma
admits an expansion

\gamma(t)=tu+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}w+O(t^{3}).

Since
\gamma(t)\in X
for all sufficiently small

, the curve characterization of the geometric second-order tangent set

shows that

. Thus,

T^{2,a}_{0,u}X\subseteq T^{2}_{0,u}X.

∎

3.4.
Some classes of analytic sets whose second jet maps are surjective

In this section we give some classes of analytic sets whose second jet maps are surjective. Consequently, the second-order tangent sets of these analytic sets are algebraic.

Proposition 3.3

(Smooth analytic germs)
.

If
X
is a smooth analytic submanifold near

, then for every

Proof.

In suitable analytic coordinates,

Then

Given any
(u,w)\in J^{2}(C_{0}X)_{u}

2
-jet.
∎

Proposition 3.4

We have shown that for every
(u,w)\in J^{2}(C_{0}X)_{u}
there exists

\gamma\subseteq X
with
j^{2}_{0}\gamma=(u,w)
, i.e.
\Phi_{u}
is surjective.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Let

be an analytic sets with

. Let

Then

if one of the following assumptions are satisfied:

(1)

X
is a smooth analytic submanifold near

.

(2)

X
is a (germ of a) homogeneous analytic cone.

(3)

be an analytic hypersurface with
\nabla f^{[*]}(u)\neq 0
.

(4)

X\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}
be a germ at

of an analytic complete intersection which is non-degenerate along the direction

.

Proof.

The proof of this theorem follows from Theorem
3.2
and Propositions
3.3
,
3.4
,
3.5
and
3.6
.
∎

4.
Applications of directional second-order tangent sets in optimization

In this section we give some necessary and sufficient conditions for local optimality problems of
C^{2}
-functions on analytic sets in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
.

Theorem 4.1

(Second-order necessary condition on an analytic set)
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}
(
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
) be a closed analytic set
with
0\in X
, and let
f:\mathbb{K}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{2}
function.
Assume that
0
is a local minimizer of
f
on
X
.
Then

(i)

(first order)

\langle\nabla f(0),u\rangle\geq 0\qquad\forall\,u\in T_{0}X.

(ii)

(second order, directional)
For every
u\in T_{0}X\setminus\{0\}
satisfying

\langle\nabla f(0),u\rangle=0
, one has

\inf_{w\in T^{2}_{0,u}X}\Bigl(\langle u,\nabla^{2}f(0)u\rangle+\langle\nabla f(0),w\rangle\Bigr)\ \geq\ 0.

(4.1)

Proof.

Firstly, we prove (i). Fix
u\in T_{0}X
. By definition, there exist sequences

t_{k}\downarrow 0
,
t_{k}>0
and
u_{k}\to u
such that

x_{k}:=t_{k}u_{k}\in X\qquad(\forall k).

Since
0
is a local minimizer of
f
on
X
, we have
f(x_{k})\geq f(0)
for all
k
large enough.
Hence

0\leq\frac{f(x_{k})-f(0)}{t_{k}}.

Because
f
is
C^{1}
at
0
, we have the first-order expansion

f(t_{k}u_{k})=f(0)+t_{k}\langle\nabla f(0),u_{k}\rangle+o(t_{k})\qquad(k\to\infty).

Substituting into the quotient gives

0\leq\langle\nabla f(0),u_{k}\rangle+o(1).

Letting
k\to\infty
and using
u_{k}\to u
yields

0\leq\langle\nabla f(0),u\rangle,

as claimed.

Now we prove (ii). Let
u\in T_{0}X
with
\langle\nabla f(0),u\rangle=0
, and let

w\in T^{2}_{0,u}X
.
By definition of
T^{2}_{0,u}X
, there exist
t_{k}\downarrow 0
and
w_{k}\to w

such that

x_{k}:=t_{k}u+\tfrac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}w_{k}\in X.

Since
0
is a local minimizer of
f
on
X
, we have
f(x_{k})\geq f(0)
for
k

large.

Using the second-order Taylor expansion of
f
at
0
,

f(x_{k})=f(0)+t_{k}\langle\nabla f(0),u\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}\Bigl(\langle u,\nabla^{2}f(0)u\rangle+\langle\nabla f(0),w_{k}\rangle\Bigr)+o(t_{k}^{2}).

The linear term vanishes by the choice of
u
.
Dividing by
\tfrac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}
and letting
k\to\infty
yields

0\leq\langle u,\nabla^{2}f(0)u\rangle+\langle\nabla f(0),w\rangle.

Taking the infimum over
w\in T^{2}_{0,u}X
gives (
4.1
).
∎

Remark 4.2
.

If one formally replaces
T^{2}_{0,u}X
by
T^{2,a}_{0,u}X
, then (
4.1
) becomes

\inf_{w\in T^{2,a}_{0,u}X}\Bigl(\langle u,\nabla^{2}f(0)u\rangle+\langle\nabla f(0),w\rangle\Bigr)\ \geq\ 0.

(4.2)

In general,

T^{2}_{0,u}X\subsetneq T^{2,a}_{0,u}X
, hence (
4.2
) may hold even when (
4.1
) fails,
yielding false second-order optimality conclusions. This explains the disadvantage of non-algebraicity of
T^{2}_{0,u}X
.

In the following we give a second-order sufficient condition for the local optimality problem with constrains on analytic sets. This sufficient condition is proposed based on the parabolic regularity at the reference point. See
[
13
]
for a discussion on the concept of parabolic regularity. The idea for the sufficiency proposed here is learned from
[
1
]
.

Theorem 4.3

(Second–order sufficiency via
T^{2}_{0,u}X
)
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}
be a closed analytic set and let
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{K}^{n})
.
Assume:

(i)

\langle\nabla f(0),u\rangle\geq 0
for all
u\in T_{0}X
;

(ii)

for every
u\in T_{0}X\setminus\{0\}
with

\langle\nabla f(0),u\rangle=0
,

\inf_{w\in T^{2}_{0,u}X}\Bigl(\langle u,\nabla^{2}f(0)u\rangle+\langle\nabla f(0),w\rangle\Bigr)>0;

(iii)

f+\delta_{X}
is parabolically regular at
0
, i.e.
every sequence
\{x_{k}\}\subseteq X\setminus\{0\}
with
x_{k}\to 0
and

\frac{f(x_{k})-f(0)}{\|x_{k}\|^{2}}\ \text{bounded}

admits a representation

x_{k}=t_{k}u_{k}+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}w_{k}+o(t_{k}^{2}),

where
t_{k}:=\|x_{k}\|\downarrow 0
,
u_{k}:=x_{k}/t_{k}\to u\in T_{0}X
, and every cluster
point
w
of
\{w_{k}\}
belongs to
T^{2}_{0,u}X
.

Then
0
is a strict local minimizer of
f
on
X
and satisfies quadratic growth: there exist

c>0
and
\varepsilon>0
such that

f(x)\geq f(0)+c\|x\|^{2}\qquad\forall x\in X\cap B_{\varepsilon}(0).

Proof.

Since
f\in C^{2}
, for
h\to 0
we have the Taylor expansion

f(h)=f(0)+\langle\nabla f(0),h\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle h,\nabla^{2}f(0)h\rangle+o(\|h\|^{2}).

(4.3)

Assume by contradiction that quadratic growth fails. Then there exists a sequence

\{x_{k}\}\subseteq X\setminus\{0\}
with
x_{k}\to 0
such that

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{f(x_{k})-f(0)}{\|x_{k}\|^{2}}\leq 0.

(4.4)

Define
t_{k}:=\|x_{k}\|\downarrow 0
and
u_{k}:=x_{k}/t_{k}
. Passing to a subsequence, we may assume

u_{k}\to u
for some
u\in T_{0}X
. By parabolic regularity (iii), there exists a bounded
sequence
\{w_{k}\}
such that

x_{k}=t_{k}u_{k}+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}w_{k}+o(t_{k}^{2}),

(4.5)

and every cluster point
w
of
\{w_{k}\}
lies in
T^{2}_{0,u}X
.

Substituting (
4.5
) into (
4.3
) yields

\displaystyle f(x_{k})

\displaystyle=f(0)+t_{k}\langle\nabla f(0),u_{k}\rangle

(4.6)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}\Bigl(\langle u_{k},\nabla^{2}f(0)u_{k}\rangle+\langle\nabla f(0),w_{k}\rangle\Bigr)+o(t_{k}^{2}).

(4.7)

Dividing by
t_{k}^{2}
gives

\frac{f(x_{k})-f(0)}{t_{k}^{2}}=\frac{\langle\nabla f(0),u_{k}\rangle}{t_{k}}+\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\langle u_{k},\nabla^{2}f(0)u_{k}\rangle+\langle\nabla f(0),w_{k}\rangle\Bigr)+o(1).

(4.8)

If
\langle\nabla f(0),u\rangle>0
, then by continuity

\langle\nabla f(0),u_{k}\rangle\geq\eta>0
for
k
large, and the first term on the right–hand side
of (
4.8
) diverges to
+\infty
, contradicting (
4.4
).
Hence

\langle\nabla f(0),u\rangle=0.

By assumption (ii), there exists
\alpha>0
such that

\langle u,\nabla^{2}f(0)u\rangle+\langle\nabla f(0),w\rangle\geq 2\alpha\qquad\forall w\in T^{2}_{0,u}X.

Let
w
be a cluster point of
\{w_{k}\}
; then
w\in T^{2}_{0,u}X
, and passing to a subsequence we
may assume
w_{k}\to w
. Consequently,

\langle u_{k},\nabla^{2}f(0)u_{k}\rangle+\langle\nabla f(0),w_{k}\rangle\to\langle u,\nabla^{2}f(0)u\rangle+\langle\nabla f(0),w\rangle\geq 2\alpha.

Thus, for
k
sufficiently large,

\langle u_{k},\nabla^{2}f(0)u_{k}\rangle+\langle\nabla f(0),w_{k}\rangle\geq\alpha.

Returning to (
4.7
), we obtain for large
k
,

f(x_{k})-f(0)\geq\frac{1}{2}\alpha t_{k}^{2}+o(t_{k}^{2}).

Hence there exists
c>0
such that

f(x_{k})-f(0)\geq c\|x_{k}\|^{2}

for all sufficiently large
k
, contradicting (
4.4
).
Therefore quadratic growth holds, and
0
is a strict local minimizer of
f
on
X
.
∎

Parabolic regularity is a second-order exactness property in variational analysis ensuring that second-order models obtained from difference quotients are realizable by actual nearby points. For a proper lower semicontinuous function
F
, parabolic regularity at a point
x
relative to a subgradient
v\in\partial F(x)
guarantees that the second epi-derivative
d^{2}F(x\mid v)
is attained by recovery sequences, and plays a central role in the validity of second-order necessary and sufficient optimality conditions and quadratic growth; see
[
13
, Definition 13.65]
and
[
1
, Chapter 3]
. In the constrained setting
F=f+\delta_{X}
, where
f\in C^{2}
and
X\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}
is closed, parabolic regularity of
F
reduces to a geometric regularity property of the set
X
, since the smooth part
f
contributes no pathology at second order. In this case, parabolic regularity is governed by the exactness of parabolic (directional) second-order tangent models of
X
, which enter the expression of
d^{2}(f+\delta_{X})
through parabolic second-order tangent sets. This issue is particularly delicate for analytic sets, where geometric second-order tangent sets are defined via limits of curves and need not coincide with their algebraic (jet-defined) counterparts. The equality between geometric and algebraic second-order tangent sets therefore provides a concrete and verifiable sufficient condition for parabolic regularity of
f+\delta_{X}
in singular analytic settings, eliminating the classical gap between second-order necessary and sufficient conditions based on parabolic tangent constructions; see
[
1
]
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}

0\in X

involving

(1)

X
is a smooth analytic submanifold near
0
.

(2)

X

(3)

X\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}
be an analytic hypersurface with
\nabla f^{[*]}(u)\neq 0
.

(4)

X\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}
be a germ at
0
of an analytic complete intersection which is non-degenerate along the direction

.

Proof.

This corollary follows directly from Theorem
3.7
, the second-order necessary condition (Theorem
4.1
) and the second-order sufficient condition (Theorem
4.3
).
∎

Effect of endoscopic submucosal dissection on short-term surgical outcome after radical gastrectomy: a multicenter retrospective study based on the Korean Gastric Cancer Association nationwide survey
Authors: Jinjoo Kim, Ki Bum Park, Han Hong Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeEndoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) is a minimally invasive treatment for early gastric cancer (EGC) with a low risk of lymph node metastasis. However, 17%–21% of patients require additional curative gastrectomy (ACG) due to non-curative factors. Although ESD may induce fibrosis or adhesions that could complicate subsequent surgery, its impact on surgical outcomes after ACG remains unclear. This study aimed to evaluate the impact of prior ESD on postoperative outcomes following ACG.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed national data from the Korean Gastric Cancer Association, including 11,594 patients with EGC who underwent gastrectomy between January 2019 and December 2019. Of these, 913 patients underwent ACG after non-curative ESD, while 10,681 underwent primary gastrectomy. Outcomes were compared between the ACG and surgery-only groups using 1:1 propensity score matching.ResultsA total of 1,826 matched patients were analyzed, with 913 patients in each group. There was no significant difference in severe complication rates (Clavien-Dindo grade ≥III) between the post-ESD group and the surgery-only group (4.4% vs. 5.3%, P = 0.986). Operative time, blood loss, and 30-day mortality were also comparable between groups. Higher body mass index (odds ratio [OR], 1.069; P = 0.043) and total gastrectomy (OR, 2.088; P = 0.006) were associated with an increased risk of severe complications.ConclusionThis study is the first to use national data to evaluate surgical outcomes after ACG following non-curative ESD. The results suggest that prior ESD does not adversely affect postoperative outcomes, providing evidence to guide surgical decision-making in patients with EGC requiring ACG.Keywords: Endoscopic submucosal dissection, Gastrectomy, Stomach neoplasms

INTRODUCTION

Gastric cancer is one of the most prevalent malignancies in Korea, and early detection rates have increased with the implementation of well-established national screening programs [1].

Traditionally, surgical resection with lymph node (LN) dissection has been the mainstay of treatment for gastric cancer. However, with advances in minimally invasive surgery, endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) is commonly regarded as one of the standard treatments for select patients with early gastric cancer (EGC) with a negligible risk of LN metastasis [2,3].

Despite technical improvements in ESD and the validation of risk stratification tools such as the eCura system or elderly criteria, approximately 16%–17% of pathological results from ESD do not fulfill the criteria for curative resection or present lymphovascular invasion or a positive vertical margin, and they are classified as non-curative resection due to the risk of LN metastasis or a remnant tumor. Consequently, additional curative gastrectomy (ACG) with LN dissection is recommended for these patients [3,4,5,6]. However, some physicians and surgeons remain concerned about any negative effects of ESD. ESD could result in technical difficulties during ACG due to an artificial gastric ulcer, local inflammation, fibrosis, and intra-abdominal adhesions, which lead to surgical complications [7]. Nonetheless, few studies have analyzed the surgical outcomes in large populations, including postoperative complications, between ACG after non-curative ESD and standard gastrectomy without ESD.

The aim of this study is to evaluate the effect of ESD on surgical outcomes in patients who underwent ACG after the non-curative ESD procedure using nationwide registry data. Furthermore, we analyzed surgical complications associated with ACG risk factors.

METHODS

Ethics statement

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of The Catholic University of Korea, Seoul St. Mary’s Hospital (No. KC25ZISI0811). This study was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and written informed consent was waived due to its retrospective nature.

Data collection

This study used nationwide survey data collected in 2019 by the Information Committee of the Korean Gastric Cancer Association (KGCA) [8]. Data were collected from 14,076 patients who underwent surgery for gastric cancer between January 2019 and December 2019 at 68 institutions. The Information Committee of KGCA developed a standardized case report form that captured patient demographics, medical history, pathological findings, operative methods, and surgical outcomes. While this study is based on the 2019 Nationwide Database, the clinical trends and results remain consistent with the most recent survey data [9], further supporting the reliability and representativeness of our registry-based analysis.

The KGCA Information Committee reviewed the collected data and filtered out missing or incorrect entries. Regarding any identified inaccuracies, the committee queried representatives of the corresponding institution, and the KGCA registry committee updated the database based on their responses. No preoperative data were collected during the 2019 survey period. Histological data were categorized according to the World Health Organization classification and Lauren classifications [10]. Pathological staging was performed according to the 8th American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) classification [11]. Postoperative complications were defined as any adverse event that developed within 30 days of the operation, including stricture or leakage of the anastomosis site, duodenal stump leakage, intraluminal or intra-abdominal hemorrhage, pancreatic fistula, intra-abdominal abscess or peritoneal fluid collection, wound problems, mechanical ileus, pneumonia, cerebrovascular events, and cardiovascular events. Severity of complications was scored according to the Clavien-Dindo classification (CDC), and severe complications were classified as CDC grade III or above [12].

Criteria for endoscopic submucosal dissection and non-curative resection of endoscopic submucosal dissection

ESD is indicated for EGC that meet criteria minimizing the risk of LN metastasis: (1) well or moderately differentiated tubular or papillary adenocarcinoma, (2) tumor confined to the mucosa endoscopically, (3) endoscopically estimated tumor size ≤2 cm, and (4) no ulcerative findings in the tumor. In accordance with the KGCA guidelines, non-curative ESD was defined based on a pathological diagnosis when resections did not meet all criteria for curative intent. Curative resection requires tumor-free horizontal and vertical margins, absence of lymphovascular invasion, and complete resection of lesions meeting the absolute or expanded indications. These indications included: (1) T1a differentiated-type carcinoma without ulceration, regardless of tumor size; (2) T1a differentiated-type carcinoma with ulceration, and tumor size ≤3 cm; (3) differentiated submucosal (T1b) invasion <500 µm, and tumor size ≤3 cm; and (4) T1a undifferentiated-type carcinoma without ulceration, and tumor size ≤2 cm [3,13].

Statistical analysis

Patients were divided into 2 cohorts: those who underwent ACG following non-curative ESD (post-ESD group) and those who underwent standard gastrectomy without prior ESD (surgery-only group). To minimize potential selection bias and ensure comparability between the groups, propensity score matching (PSM) was conducted in a 1:1 ratio. Matching variables included age, sex, body mass index (BMI), American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status classification, TNM stage, and longitudinal tumor location.

The normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Non-normally distributed variables were analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U-test, whereas normally distributed variables were compared using the independent t-test. Categorical data were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher exact test, as appropriate. Results were reported as medians with interquartile ranges (IQR) for continuous variables and frequencies with percentages for categorical variables. Variables with a P-value <0.05 in univariate analyses were included in a multivariable logistic regression model with stepwise selection to identify independent predictors of severe complications (defined as CDC grade ≥III). All statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics ver. 23.0 (IBM Corp.), and a P-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Patient selection and baseline characteristics

The study design is summarized in Fig. 1. Among the 14,076 patients who underwent gastrectomy for gastric cancer, 1,705 with insufficient data, 528 who received neoadjuvant chemotherapy, and 249 with stage IV gastric cancer were excluded. After exclusion, 11,594 patients were included in the initial analysis. Of these, 913 patients underwent ACG following non-curative ESD, while 10,681 patients underwent standard gastrectomy without ESD. To minimize selection bias, 1:1 PSM was performed, resulting in a final study population of 1,826 patients, of which 913 patients in the post-ESD group and 913 patients in the surgery-only group.

Flow diagram of this study. ACG, additional curative gastrectomy; ESD, endoscopic submucosal dissection.

Baseline clinical characteristics and operative details are shown in Table 1. The median age was 66 years in the post-ESD group and 65 years in the surgery-only group, with predominance of male patients. There were no significant differences in other baseline characteristics between the 2 groups. However, significant differences were noted in the rate of combined resection of adjacent structures (6.2% vs. 8.9%, P = 0.034) and surgical approach. The open approach was more commonly performed in the surgery-only group (9.3% vs. 16.8%, P < 0.001). D2 dissection was performed in 37.5% of the post-ESD group and 51.9% of the surgery-only group (P < 0.001). Nonetheless, there were no significant differences in estimated blood loss (P = 0.209) or operative time (P = 0.065). Among the 138 cases requiring combined resection in both groups, cholecystectomy was the most prevalent procedure (n = 102, 73.9%). Splenectomy was performed in 7 cases (5.1%), primarily for tumors located in the upper third of the stomach. Pathologically confirmed pT4b stage with direct invasion was identified in only 2 patients, both of whom were in the post-ESD group in the matched analysis. These 2 cases required extensive multiorgan resections to achieve oncological radicality; specifically, one patient underwent combined resection of the spleen and pancreas, whereas the other required complex resection involving the pancreas, gallbladder, and mesocolon.

Pathological outcomes are shown in Table 2. TNM stage distribution did not differ. However, the median tumor size was larger in the surgery-only group (2.0 cm [IQR, 1.4–3.0] vs. 2.6 cm [IQR, 1.7–4.0], P < 0.001). Postoperative complications and associated risk factors

Postoperative complications and patient outcomes are summarized in Table 3. Overall complications were 13.7% in the post-ESD group and 15.8% in the surgery-only group (P = 0.235). The ESD procedure did not have a significant impact on the occurrence of severe complications (CDC grade ≥III; 4.4% vs. 5.3%, P = 0.986). Anastomotic leakage was the most common complication in both groups. In the post-ESD group, the second most common complication was intra-abdominal abscess (0.7%), followed by fluid collection (0.5%) and wound complications (0.5%). In the surgery-only group, the most common complications were wounds (0.5%) and pneumonia (0.5%). Also, no differences were observed in 30-day mortality (0.1% vs. 0.7%, P = 0.124) and the hospital length of stay (7.0 days [IQR, 6.0–9.0] vs. 7.0 days [IQR, 6.0–9.0], P = 0.726).

The risk factors for surgical complications were assessed. Univariate analysis revealed that increasing age (P = 0.036), higher BMI (P = 0.023), ASA class IV (P = 0.016), and total gastrectomy (P = 0.002) were significantly associated with complications. Preoperative ESD did not significantly influence complication rates (odds ratio [OR], 1.211; P = 0.383). In multivariate logistic regression analysis, a higher BMI was a significant risk factor for severe complications (OR, 1.069; P = 0.043). Additionally, total gastrectomy was an independent factor associated with severe complications (OR, 2.088; P = 0.006) (Table 4).

DISCUSSION

ACG is generally indicated when non-curative factors are identified pathologically after ESD. These decisions rely on a comprehensive assessment of pathological factors including invasion depth, margin status, lymphovascular involvement, differentiation, and the technical feasibility of re-ESD [3,13]. Nonetheless, some physicians have expressed concerns about the potential negative effects of ESD, which may increase surgical morbidity or mortality after ACG. The use of electrosurgical devices for submucosal dissection may induce inflammatory reactions, leading to artificial ulcers, fibrosis, and intraperitoneal adhesions [7,14]. Some of these unintended consequences, such as prolonged operative time and increased blood loss, could contribute to increased surgical complexity. Moreover, scarring resulting from ESD may affect the extent of resection required for additional gastrectomy [15].

However, findings from this nationwide study demonstrated that perioperative ESD did not adversely affect short-term surgical outcomes, including the prevalence of severe surgical complications, operative time, blood loss, length of hospital stay, or 30-day mortality following curative gastrectomy. In the present study, the frequency of combined resection during gastrectomy was significantly lower in the post-ESD group than that in the surgery-only group (6.2% vs. 8.9%, P = 0.034), whereas the rate of D1+ dissection was significantly higher (62.5% vs. 48.1%, P < 0.001), and the resection extent did not differ significantly (P = 0.235). Interestingly, despite significant differences in the surgical approach and extent of LN dissection between the groups, the surgical approach was not a significant risk factor for severe complications (OR, 1.309; P = 0.358). The higher rate of D1+ dissection in the post-ESD group reflects adherence to surgical guidelines, as D1+ is oncologically sufficient for the pathological T1 status of most post-ESD patients. Conversely, the surgery-only group included more patients with clinically suspected advanced stages necessitating standard D2 dissection. This indicates that the comparable short-term outcomes in the post-ESD group were not skewed by the distribution of surgical methods but rather reflected the inherent safety of ACG.

Regarding the clinical rationale for combined resection, although the registry did not specify the clinical indications, our study suggests that most procedures were performed for non-malignant reasons. Notably, none of the 102 cholecystectomies were pathologically confirmed as T4b, suggesting benign gallbladder disease or prophylactic removal. Similarly, 7 splenectomies were performed as part of splenic-hilum lymphadenectomy for proximal cancers. Consequently, pathologically confirmed pT4b invasion requiring combined resection occurred in only a very limited number of cases (2 patients) from the matched cohort. Both cases in the post-ESD group required extensive multiorgan resections, including the pancreas, mesocolon, or diaphragm, to achieve R0 resection. These findings demonstrate that combined resections in this cohort primarily reflect the surgical necessity or management of staging discrepancies rather than expected tumor invasion. We believe that these strategic surgical decisions resulted in comparable outcomes between the groups, without a significant increase in operative time or blood loss.

A previous study utilizing the same database as the present study reported a severe complication rate (CDC grade ≥III) of 4.9% among the entire cohort of patients with gastric cancer who underwent surgical treatment [16]. In contrast, the severe complication rate in the ACG group following ESD in the current study was 4.4%, suggesting that the rate was not elevated compared with that in the overall cohort. Our findings are consistent with those of previous single-center studies that analyzed the perioperative effects of ESD following ACG. Park et al. [17] reported that despite the anticipated technical difficulties during ACG due to fibrosis and adhesions following ESD, the operative time was shorter in the post-ESD group, and the perioperative morbidity rates did not differ.

Another study also reported that ESD was not significantly associated with postoperative complications, whereas total gastrectomy was identified as a factor associated with increased postoperative morbidity [18]. Similarly, the present study revealed that total gastrectomy was an independent factor associated with an increased risk of severe complications, as shown by multivariate analysis (OR, 2.088; P = 0.006). Furthermore, our findings align with prior research indicating that higher BMI is associated with increased surgical morbidity. Inagawa et al. [19] demonstrated that a larger fat volume is associated with a higher rate of postoperative complications, supporting our multivariate analysis, which identified high BMI as an independent risk factor for severe complications (OR, 1.069; P = 0.043). In this context, although ESD may induce pathological changes due to inflammatory processes that could potentially increase the technical complexity during ACG, these factors did not translate into a clinically significant impact on surgical outcomes.

Currently, ESD is considered a minimally invasive treatment option for EGC with an insignificant risk of LN metastasis. However, approximately 17%–21% of patients generally require additional resection, including ACG [4,20]. The number of ESD procedures performed in Korea has steadily increased over the past decade, especially among the elderly population and patients meeting the extended criteria for ESD [21]. The population eligible for ACG is expected to increase arithmetically. In cases where ACG is required following non-curative ESD, observation might be preferred for patients with high-risk perioperative morbidity, including older patients or those with comorbidities. Univariate analysis conducted in this study also revealed that patients with ASA class IV had a significantly higher risk of developing severe postoperative complications (OR, 8.089; 95% confidence interval, 1.486–44.578; P = 0.016). Old age and comorbidities are well-known risk factors for increased mortality and morbidity after surgery under general anesthesia. Therefore, the preoperative assessment of physiological reserve and a multidisciplinary team approach are necessary for perioperative care [22].

The long-term outcomes of patients requiring ACG who are managed with observation remain insufficient, as these studies are subject to selection bias owing to their retrospective cohort designs. However, a multicenter study conducted in Korea demonstrated that the absence of additional treatment following non-curative ESD was associated with lower disease-specific survival, emphasizing the necessity of performing additional curative resection. Although ESD is performed in patients with a lower risk of LN metastasis, this study found that LN metastasis was confirmed in 9.2% of patients who underwent ACG after non-curative ESD. Recent nationwide evidence indicates that LN metastasis is present in approximately 2.4% of Korean gastric cancer patients even within the absolute indications defined by Japanese guidelines [23]. These data underscore that the risk of LN metastasis cannot be overlooked, providing a strong rationale for considering ACG as a key therapeutic strategy following non-curative resection. Notably, the incidence of local recurrence was more than 5 times higher in patients without additional treatment than in those who underwent curative gastrectomy, especially in cases with high-risk factors such as large tumor size, positive horizontal margin, and lymphovascular invasion [20]. In contrast, Yamaguchi et al. [24] reported that additional gastrectomy following non-curative ESD did not adversely affect postoperative complications or long-term prognosis in patients with EGC compared with standard gastrectomy after PSM analysis. Furthermore, a previous study reported that patients with EGC who underwent ESD experienced concerns about cancer recurrence, which has been identified as an independent risk factor for poor quality of life [25].

Managing patients after non-curative ESD should prioritize oncological safety through an evidence-based surgical approach. While conventional guidelines have limitations, incorporating updated risk stratification models—such as the eCura or eCuraU systems—can still provide a valuable framework for identifying candidates who may benefit from additional gastrectomy. Establishing a more refined therapeutic algorithm that integrates these tools with our findings on LN metastasis risk serves as a critical building block for optimizing curability. Ultimately, proper surgical intervention based on a comprehensive assessment remains the most reliable strategy for ensuring long-term oncological success in at-risk individuals [26,27]. Especially in the context of a rapidly aging population, treatment strategies must carefully balance medical comorbidities with oncologic outcomes to optimize survival in geriatric patients.

This study has some limitations. First, its retrospective design introduces inherent biases, which may have introduced potential biases in data collection. Although we analyzed the data after 1:1 PSM to minimize bias, residual confounding effects cannot be completely excluded. Second, because the KGCA registry primarily focuses on patients undergoing gastrectomy, this cohort may not have included the entire population of patients who underwent ESD. In particular, the registry’s inclusion of only ESD cases within 90 days of surgery, while omitting rare cases with longer intervals, and the absence of detailed perioperative rationales for the initial ESD procedure, represent key limitations of this study. Third, long-term outcome data were not available; therefore, we could not comprehensively evaluate the long-term outcomes of the patients who underwent ACG.

Nevertheless, this is the first study to investigate surgical outcomes after additional gastrectomy in patients who underwent non-curative ESD using national data. It is also one of the largest studies to compare surgical outcomes, demonstrating that previous ESD does not adversely affect surgical outcomes after ACG. We believe that these findings will provide valuable insights for physicians to establish optimal strategies for surgical treatment.

Evaluation of Prevalence of Golden Percentage, Recurring Esthetic Dental (RED) and Tarulatha-Ruchi (TR) Proportion in Dental Students
Authors: Nupur Dhanak, Swati Mittal, Riya Dave, Saloni Kanodia, Shikha Kanodia, Geeta Asthana
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:One of the important concepts in aesthetic dentistry is “the principle of gradation,” where each tooth appears proportionally narrower than its mesial neighbor as the eye moves laterally from the midline. Various mathematical and physiological theories determine the widths of maxillary anterior teeth.Objectives:The study aims to evaluate the prevalence of the golden percentage, the recurring esthetic dental (RED) proportion, and the newly proposed Tarulatha-Ruchi (TR) proportion among dental students from Gujarat state.Materials and Methods:A total of 100 Gujarati students with aesthetically pleasing smiles, ages 18–25 years, were selected. Standard frontal-view photographs were taken with a DSLR camera, displaying maxillary anterior teeth and gingiva in centric occlusion. Adobe Photoshop CS6 was used to measure tooth widths, and the values were analyzed statistically.Results:The exact golden percentage was not followed in any subjects. The percentages for the maxillary central incisor, lateral incisor, and canine were 22%, 16%, and 12%, respectively. The RED proportion did not exist, increasing distally from 69% to 78.5%. For the TR proportion, central incisors did not follow the suggested quotient of 0.3; a quotient of 0.4 for the lateral incisor was present in 99% of subjects; a quotient of 0.3 for the canine was present in 25% of subjects; and a suggested quotient of 0.6 for the combined width of the central and lateral incisors was present in none of the subjects.Conclusion:The golden percentage and TR proportion can be applied if adjusted. RED did not exist among participants. A proportional constant of 0.4 for the lateral incisor by TR proportion can be used to build a missing or peg-shaped lateral incisor.

INTRODUCTION

“De Divina Proportione” - a mathematics book, written in the 15th century by Luca Pacioli and illustrated by Leonardo Da Vinci, refers to golden sections over the face, nature, and its use by artists and architects to create masterpieces.[1] Lombardi was the first to suggest the application of the golden proportion in dentistry. According to this the width of the maxillary lateral incisor should be 62% of the width of the maxillary central incisor and the width of the maxillary canine should be 62% of the width of the lateral incisor when viewed from the frontal aspect.[2] However, studies have shown that in natural dentition golden proportion was hardly found and strict adherence to the golden proportion may limit creativity leading to aesthetic failure.[345]

Snow (1999) suggested the Golden percentage - According to which, the width of the maxillary central incisor, lateral incisor, and canine should be 25%, 15%, and 10% respectively of the inter-canine distance.[6] Some studies proposed recurring esthetic dental proportion (RED) - According to which when viewed from the facial aspect and progressing distally, the ratio of the width of consecutive maxillary teeth remains constant.[7] Recently Tarulatha-Ruchi (TR Proportion, 2021) obtained Proportionality constant for width of central incisor, lateral incisor, canine and combined width of central and lateral incisors.[8]

AIM

The aim of the study is to evaluate the prevalence of Golden percentage, Recurring Esthetic Dental (RED) proportion and newly proposed Tarulatha-Ruchi (TR) proportion among dental students from Gujarat State.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

The study was approved by the institutional ethical committee of Government Dental College and Hospital, Ahmedabad (IEC GDCH/S.7/2022). Using a 95% confidence interval and a 5% allowable error, the sample size was calculated as 77 and rounded to 100. Thus, 100 Gujarati students with aesthetically pleasing smiles, aged 18–25 years, were selected, consisting of 62 females and 38 males. Informed consent was obtained. Inclusion criteria included dentofacial symmetry, good gingival health, healthy upper front teeth, full smile display of maxillary second premolars, absence of spacing, less than 2 mm of maxillary gingival display, and lower lip alignment parallel to maxillary incisal edges. Exclusion criteria encompass missing upper front teeth, restorations, caries, crowns in the upper anterior region, history of orthodontic treatment, malalignments, periodontal defects, and congenital or acquired facial and dental deformities.

For the evaluation of aesthetic proportions, calculations were as follows: the golden percentage was determined by the width of the central incisor/total width of six maxillary anterior × 100, the lateral incisor/total width of six maxillary anterior × 100, and the canine/total width of six maxillary anterior × 100. The RED proportion was calculated as the width of the maxillary lateral incisor/width of the maxillary central incisor = width of the maxillary canine/width of the maxillary lateral incisor. TR proportion for x,y,z and x+y was calculated as x/x+y+z, y/x+y, z/x+y+z and x+y/x+y+z respectively. Digital photographs were taken with a DSLR Canon 80D camera with a 100 mm macro lens at a fixed distance of 30 cm from the fixed under specific settings. Subjects were photographed in a standardized position, and measurements were analyzed using Adobe Photoshop CS6. The mesiodistal width of maxillary anterior teeth was recorded by measuring the maximum distance between the mesial and distal contact points of each tooth [Figure 1].

DISCUSSION

Determining the correct dimensions of teeth is crucial for designing an aesthetically pleasing smile, and various proportions have been proposed for this purpose. Limited data exists regarding the prevalence of any particular proportion in the Gujarati population. This study evaluated local students with pleasing smiles to check the existence of the Golden Percentage, RED proportion, and TR proportion using DSLR cameras with macro lenses from a fixed distance of 30 cm, similar to the technique used by Fernandes et al.[7] In this study (n = 100), the golden percentage was not present in any subjects evaluated. The average width of the maxillary central, lateral, and canine in percentage was found to be 22%, 16%, and 12%, respectively, of the intercanine distance, similar to findings in Bangalore, Arabic, and Jaipur populations.[234]

The RED proportion was not present in any subjects evaluated, with values increasing distally from 69% to 78.5%, within the 60%–80% range proposed by Ward. This discrepancy may be because of Ward et al.’s proportions being computer- and software-based rather than checked in natural dentition.

The TR proportion was proposed in 2021, providing constants for the width of the central incisor, lateral incisor, canine, and combined width of central and lateral incisors. In our study, only the lateral incisor matched their suggested value of 0.4. Limitations include not accounting for individual arch form and tooth height and the small sample size, limiting conclusions for the Gujarati population.

CONCLUSIONS

None of the subjects adhered to the golden percentage or RED proportion, and the TR proportion also showed deviations, with only the lateral incisor approximating the suggested quotient. Variability in central incisor and canine proportions, along with the combined width of central and lateral incisors, underscores the need for region-specific assessments and further research to refine aesthetic principles for diverse populations.

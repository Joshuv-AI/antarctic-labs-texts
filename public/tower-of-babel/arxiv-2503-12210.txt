Infinite-dimensional dynamical instabilities of noncompact stationary Ricci flow solutions
Sigurd B. Angenent, Dan Knopf

Abstract
. Regarding Ricci flow as a dynamical system, we derive sufficient conditions for noncompact stationary (Ricci-flat) solutions to possess infinite-dimensional unstable manifolds, and provide examples satisfying those criteria that have uncountably many unstable perturbations.



1.
Introduction

In this work, we develop a set of tools for investigating the stability or instability of noncompact Ricci-flat manifolds. We are
primarily interested in formulating criteria for instability of noncompact solitons that are asymptotically conical. We prove
results that apply to general noncompact manifolds with bounded geometry and then demonstrate their applicability to noncompact
Ricci-flat manifolds which form a subset of a family of Einstein metrics discovered by Christoph Böhm
[
7
]
.

An approach to DeTurck’s trick

Here, we derive the quasilinear heat equation that results from DeTurck’s trick (See
[
15
]
).

2.1.
Perturbed metrics and connections

Let
g
be a fixed background metric on a manifold
{\mathcal{M}}
, and consider a perturbation of
g
given by

\tilde{g}_{ij}=g_{ij}+h_{ij}.

Except as noted below, we raise and lower indices with
g
, so
\tilde{g}^{ij}=g^{ip}g^{jq}\tilde{g}_{pq}
is in general not the inverse of

\tilde{g}_{ij}
. Because of this, we write the inverse of
\tilde{g}_{ij}
as
G^{ij}
,
i.e.,
by definition, we have

\tilde{g}_{ik}G^{kj}=\delta_{i}^{j}.

For small
h
, the inverse is given by the geometric series

(2.1)

G^{ij}=g^{ij}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{\ell}\,g^{ik_{1}}h^{k_{2}}_{k_{1}}h^{k_{3}}_{k_{2}}\cdots h^{j}_{k_{\ell}}.

This series converges at any point
p\in{\mathcal{M}}
such that
\|\hat{h}(p)\|<1
, where
\|\hat{h}(p)\|
is the operator norm of the linear
transformation
\hat{h}^{i}_{j}=g^{ik}h_{kj}
on the vector space
T_{p}{\mathcal{M}}
with inner product
g(p)
. The convergence is uniform if

\|\hat{h}\|=\sup_{p\in{\mathcal{M}}}\|\hat{h}(p)\|<1
. If, in addition, the covariant derivatives
\nabla^{j}\hat{h}
of order
j\leq N
are
uniformly
\alpha
-Hölder continuous, then the series (
2.1
) converges in
C^{N,\alpha}
.

We denote the Levi-Civita connections of
g
and
\tilde{g}
by
\nabla
and
\tilde{\nabla}
, respectively.

Generic lower order terms

In this section, it will be convenient to let
Q=Q_{jk}\,\mathrm{d}x^{j}\,\mathrm{d}x^{k}
stand for a generic quadratic polynomial tensor in

\nabla h
of the form

(2.2)

Q=Q_{0}(g,h)+Q_{1}(g,h)*\nabla h+Q_{2}(g,h)*(\nabla h\otimes\nabla h),

whose coefficients
Q_{0},Q_{1},Q_{2}
are rational functions of
g,h
defined for
\|h\|<1
that satisfy

Q_{0}(h)=\mathcal{O}(\|h\|^{2}),\quad Q_{1}(h)=\mathcal{O}(\|h\|),\quad Q_{2}(h)=\mathcal{O}(1)\qquad\text{as}\quad\|h\|\to 0.

Below, we allow
Q
to change from line to line.

Our main result in this section is the following version of DeTurck’s trick:

Lemma 2.1
.

There exists a tensor field
Q_{jk}(h,\nabla h)
such that, if
\tilde{g}=g+h
for a fixed background metric
g
, the tensor field
h
is
a solution of

(2.3)

h_{t}=\mathscr{F}[h]

with

(2.4)

\mathscr{F}_{jk}[h]=-2R_{jk}+\Big\{G^{\ell m}(h)\nabla_{\ell}\nabla_{m}h_{jk}+2R_{\ell jk}^{m}h_{m}^{\ell}-R_{j}^{\ell}h_{\ell k}-R_{k}^{m}h_{jm}\Big\}\\
+Q_{jk}(h,\nabla h)

if and only if
\tilde{g}_{jk}
satisfies

(2.5)

\partial_{t}\tilde{g}_{jk}=-2\tilde{R}_{jk}+(\mathcal{L}_{X}\tilde{g})_{jk}.

The vector field
X
is defined as follows: if
\gamma
is the tensor field representing the difference of the Levi–Civita
connections,
i.e.,
if
\tilde{\nabla}_{i}W^{k}-\nabla_{i}W^{k}=\gamma_{ij}^{k}W^{j}
for all vector fields
W
, then
X
is the
contraction
4
4

4

The reader may readily verify that this definition is equivalent to the traditional formula,

X^{k}=G^{ij}(h)G^{k\ell}(h)\Big(\nabla_{i}h_{j\ell}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\ell}h_{ij}\Big).

(2.6)

X^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}G^{ij}\gamma_{ij}^{k}.

We proceed to the derivation of these identities and the proof of the Lemma.

2.2.
The Levi-Civita connections

As noted above, the Levi-Civita connections corresponding to
\tilde{g}
and
g
are
\tilde{\nabla}
and
\nabla
, respectively. The tensor
field
\gamma
is given by

(2.7)

\gamma_{ij}^{k}=\frac{1}{2}G^{k\ell}\bigl\{\nabla_{i}h_{j\ell}+\nabla_{j}h_{i\ell}-\nabla_{\ell}h_{ij}\bigr\}.

A trace simplifies to

(2.8)

\gamma_{ij}^{i}=\frac{1}{2}G^{im}\nabla_{j}\tilde{g}_{im}=\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{j}\log\det\bigl(I+\hat{h}\bigr),

where
\hat{h}=g^{-1}\circ h
, as above.

2.3.
Lie derivative of the perturbed metric

If
W^{i}
is any vector field, then the Lie derivative of the metric
\tilde{g}_{jk}
along
W
is given by

(2.9)

\mathcal{L}_{W}\tilde{g}_{jk}=\tilde{\nabla}_{j}W_{k}+\tilde{\nabla}_{k}W_{j}=\nabla_{j}W_{k}+\nabla_{k}W_{j}+2\gamma_{jk}^{\ell}W_{\ell},

where
W_{j}=\tilde{g}_{ij}W^{i}
. This is the one instance where we use the perturbed metric
\tilde{g}_{ij}
to lower an index.

2.4.
Perturbed curvature tensors

The Riemann tensors of the background and perturbed metrics are related by

(2.10)

\tilde{R}_{ijk}^{\ell}=R_{ijk}^{\ell}+\nabla_{i}\gamma_{jk}^{\ell}-\nabla_{j}\gamma_{ik}^{\ell}+\gamma_{im}^{\ell}\gamma_{jk}^{m}-\gamma_{jm}^{\ell}\gamma_{ik}^{m}.

For the Ricci tensors, we have

\tilde{R}_{jk}=R_{jk}+\nabla_{\ell}\gamma_{jk}^{\ell}-\nabla_{j}\gamma_{\ell k}^{\ell}+\gamma_{\ell m}^{\ell}\gamma_{jk}^{m}-\gamma_{jm}^{\ell}\gamma_{\ell k}^{m}.

The “
\gamma*\gamma
” terms are exactly of the form
Q_{jk}
introduced above, so we have

(2.11)

-2\tilde{R}_{jk}=-2\nabla_{\ell}\gamma^{\ell}_{jk}+2\nabla_{j}\gamma^{\ell}_{\ell k}+Q_{jk}-2R_{jk}.

The first term on the right in (
2.11
) is a divergence and can be rewritten as

\displaystyle-2\nabla_{\ell}\gamma_{jk}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{\ell}\Bigl\{G^{\ell m}\bigl(\nabla_{j}h_{km}+\nabla_{k}h_{jm}-\nabla_{m}h_{jk}\bigr)\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=-G^{\ell m}\bigl(\nabla_{\ell}\nabla_{j}h_{km}+\nabla_{\ell}\nabla_{k}h_{jm}-\nabla_{\ell}\nabla_{m}h_{jk}\bigr)+Q_{jk}

\displaystyle=G^{\ell m}\nabla_{\ell}\nabla_{m}h_{jk}-G^{\ell m}\bigl(\nabla_{\ell}\nabla_{j}h_{km}+\nabla_{\ell}\nabla_{k}h_{jm}\bigr)+Q_{jk}.

The first term in the last line above is an elliptic operator applied to
h_{jk}
.

We can rewrite the second group of terms in the last line above as the Lie derivative of
g
plus lower order terms by switching the order of
derivatives. Namely, we compute that

\displaystyle G^{\ell m}\nabla_{\ell}\nabla_{j}h_{km}

\displaystyle=G^{\ell m}\bigl\{\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}h_{km}-R_{\ell jk}^{p}h_{pm}-R_{\ell jm}^{p}h_{kp}\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{j}\bigl(G^{\ell m}\nabla_{\ell}h_{km}\bigr)-G^{\ell m}R_{\ell jk}^{p}h_{pm}-G^{\ell m}R_{\ell jm}^{p}h_{kp}+Q_{jk}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{j}\bigl(G^{\ell m}\nabla_{\ell}h_{km}\bigr)-g^{\ell m}R_{\ell jk}^{p}h_{pm}-g^{\ell m}R_{\ell jm}^{p}h_{kp}+Q_{jk}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{j}\bigl(G^{\ell m}\nabla_{\ell}h_{km}\bigr)-R_{\ell jk}^{p}h_{p}^{\ell}+R_{j}^{p}h_{kp}+Q_{jk},

and hence obtain

G^{\ell m}\bigl(\nabla_{\ell}\nabla_{j}h_{km}+\nabla_{\ell}\nabla_{k}h_{jm}\bigr)=\nabla_{j}V_{k}+\nabla_{k}V_{j}-2R_{\ell jk}^{p}h_{p}^{\ell}+R_{j}^{p}h_{kp}+R_{k}^{p}h_{jp}+Q_{jk},

where
V
is the covector

V_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}G^{\ell m}\nabla_{\ell}h_{mk}.

Using (
2.9
), we then have

G^{\ell m}\bigl(\nabla_{\ell}\nabla_{j}h_{km}+\nabla_{\ell}\nabla_{k}h_{jm}\bigr)=\mathcal{L}_{V}\tilde{g}_{jk}+2\gamma_{jk}^{\ell}V_{\ell}\\
-2R_{\ell jk}^{p}h_{p}^{\ell}+R_{j}^{p}h_{kp}+R_{k}^{p}h_{jp}+Q_{jk}.

Since
V_{i}=G^{\ell m}\nabla_{\ell}h_{mi}
, the term
\gamma_{jk}^{\ell}V_{\ell}
may be absorbed into
Q_{jk}
, so we find that

(2.12)

G^{\ell m}\bigl(\nabla_{\ell}\nabla_{j}h_{km}+\nabla_{\ell}\nabla_{k}h_{jm}\bigr)=\mathcal{L}_{V}\tilde{g}_{jk}-2R_{\ell jk}^{p}h_{p}^{\ell}+R_{j}^{p}h_{kp}+R_{k}^{p}h_{jp}+Q_{jk}.

Now by (
2.8
), the second term on the right in (
2.11
) is

2\nabla_{j}\gamma_{\ell k}^{\ell}=\nabla_{j}\nabla_{k}\log\det\bigl(I+\hat{h}\bigr)=\nabla_{j}U_{k}+\nabla_{k}U_{j},

where
U
is the covector

U_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{k}\log\det\bigl(I+\hat{h}\bigr).

Therefore, we collect terms and conclude that

-2R_{jk}+\mathcal{L}_{V}\tilde{g}_{jk}-\mathcal{L}_{U}g_{jk}=G^{\ell m}\nabla_{\ell}\nabla_{m}h_{jk}+2R_{\ell jk}^{p}h_{p}^{\ell}-R_{j}^{p}h_{kp}-R_{k}^{p}h_{jp}-2R_{jk}+Q_{jk}.

Choosing
X=(V-U)^{\sharp}
, namely,

X^{k}=G^{ij}\gamma_{ij}^{k}=G^{ij}G^{k\ell}\Big(\nabla_{i}h_{jk}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\ell}h_{ij}\Big),

and noting that
\mathcal{L}_{X}g_{jk}=\mathcal{L}_{X}\tilde{g}_{jk}+Q_{jk}
, we complete the proof of Lemma
2.1
.

3.
Short time existence and smooth well-posedness

3.1.
Analytic semigroups

We write

S^{2,0}{\mathcal{M}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}T^{*}{\mathcal{M}}\otimes_{\rm sym}T^{*}{\mathcal{M}}

for the bundle of symmetric
(2,0)
tensors on
{\mathcal{M}}
.

We assume in this section that
({\mathcal{M}},g)
has bounded geometry.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and
A^{pq}
,
B^{pqr}_{jk}
, and
C^{pq}_{jk}
be tensor fields on
{\mathcal{M}}
with
A\in C^{1}
,
and
B,C\in h^{0,\alpha}

is uniformly elliptic, and that the gradients
\nabla A^{pq},\nabla B^{pqr}_{jk}
are
uniformly bounded. Then the linear operator

\mathscr{L}\colon h^{2,\alpha}(S^{2,0}{\mathcal{M}})\to h^{0,\alpha}(S^{2,0}{\mathcal{M}})

defined by

\mathscr{L}h_{jk}=A^{pq}\nabla_{p}\nabla_{q}h_{jk}+B^{pqr}_{jk}\nabla_{p}h_{qr}+C^{pq}_{jk}h_{pq}

generates of an analytic semigroup
e^{t\mathscr{L}}
on
h^{0,\alpha}(S^{2,0}{\mathcal{M}})

We provide a proof in §
4
. In the remainder of this section, we recall some well-known consequences of
Theorem
3.1
.
In particular, we recall the short-time existence and analytic dependence on initial data for nonlinear parabolic equations that can be proved using analytic semigroup methods. Note that the arguments in the remainder of this section work even if
(\mathcal{M},g)
is not Ricci flat, but merely in
h^{2,\alpha}
.
While this result suffices for our
purposes, we should point out that Miles Simon, using different methods, has significantly relaxed the required initial regularity.
See the recent survey
[
25
]
and the references therein.

For any background metric
g
, the right-hand side of (
2.4
) defines a nonlinear map
\mathscr{F}
on the open subset of

h\in h^{2,\alpha}(S^{2,0}{\mathcal{M}})
for which
g
and

4.2.

. To get a
tensor field on
{\mathcal{M}}
again, we restrict
\resolvent_{a}(\lambda)(\varphi_{a}h_{jk})
to
{\mathcal{B}}_{r}(0)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Identifying
{\mathcal{B}}_{r}(0)

with
{\mathcal{B}}_{a}={\mathcal{B}}_{r}(p_{a})\subset{\mathcal{M}}
via the exponential map, we get a tensor field defined on
{\mathcal{B}}_{a}
. Multiplying it with

\varphi_{a}
we get a compactly supported tensor field in
{\mathcal{B}}_{a}
, which we can extend to the whole manifold by letting it vanish on

{\mathcal{M}}\setminus{\mathcal{B}}_{a}
. In other words, in (
4.7
),

\varphi_{a}(x)\resolvent_{a}(\lambda)\bigl(\varphi_{a}\cdot h_{jk}\bigr)
is an abbreviation for

\varphi_{a}(x)(\exp_{p_{a}})_{*}\biggl\{\resolvent_{a}(\lambda)\Bigl[(\exp_{p_{a}})^{*}\bigl(\varphi_{a}\cdot h_{jk}\bigr)\Bigr]\biggr\}.

4.5.2.
Boundedness of
\mathscr{S}(\lambda)

We verify that
\mathscr{S}(\lambda):h^{0,\alpha}(S^{2,0}{\mathcal{M}})\to h^{2,\alpha}(S^{2,0}{\mathcal{M}})
is uniformly bounded,
i.e.,
there exists
C>0

such that for all
h_{jk}\in h^{0,\alpha}(S^{2,0}{\mathcal{M}})
and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda>\omega
, one has

\|\mathscr{S}(\lambda)h_{jk}\|_{h^{2,\alpha}(S^{2,0}{\mathcal{M}})}\leq C\|h_{jk}\|_{h^{0,\alpha}(S^{2,0}{\mathcal{M}})}.

Proof.

The
h^{2,\alpha}
norm is equivalent to

[h_{jk}]_{2,\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\sup_{a\in{\mathbb{N}}}\bigl\|h_{jk}\big|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{a}}\bigr\|_{h^{2,\alpha}}.

At this point, we recall from (
4.6
) that the intersection of more than
N
balls in the covering

\{{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\}_{a\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is empty. This implies that in the sum that defines
\mathscr{S}(\lambda)h_{jk}(x)
, the only nonzero terms are those for
which
x\in{\mathcal{B}}_{a}
. For any
x\in{\mathcal{M}}
, there are at most
N
such terms. We therefore get

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{S}(\lambda)h_{jk}\|_{2,\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{a\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|(\mathscr{S}(\lambda)h_{jk})\big|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{a}}\|_{2,\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{a\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{N}}}\bigl\|\bigl(\varphi_{b}\resolvent_{b}(\lambda)(\varphi_{b}h_{jk})\bigr)\Big|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{a}}\bigr\|_{2,\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{a\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sum_{{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{b}\neq\varnothing}\bigl\|\bigl(\varphi_{b}\resolvent_{b}(\lambda)(\varphi_{b}h_{jk})\bigr)\Big|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{a}}\bigr\|_{2,\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{a\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sum_{{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{b}\neq\varnothing}\bigl\|\varphi_{b}\resolvent_{b}(\lambda)(\varphi_{b}h_{jk})\bigr\|_{2,\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq NC\sup_{b\in{\mathbb{N}}}\bigl\|\varphi_{b}\resolvent_{b}(\lambda)(\varphi_{b}h_{jk})\bigr\|_{2,\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq NC\sup_{b\in{\mathbb{N}}}\bigl\|\resolvent_{b}(\lambda)(\varphi_{b}h_{jk})\bigr\|_{2,\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq NC\sup_{b\in{\mathbb{N}}}\bigl\|\varphi_{b}h_{jk}\bigr\|_{0,\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq NC\|h_{jk}\|_{0,\alpha}.

∎

4.5.3.
The error in the approximate resolvent

We proceed to show that for any
f_{jk}\in h^{0,\alpha}(S^{2,0}{\mathcal{M}})
, one has

(4.8)

(\lambda-\mathscr{L})\mathscr{S}(\lambda)f_{jk}=f_{jk}-\mathscr{E}(f_{jk})=\bigl(\boldsymbol{1}_{h^{0,\alpha}({\mathcal{M}})}-\mathscr{E}\bigr)(f_{jk}),

where
\mathscr{E}
is the operator

\mathscr{E}(f_{jk})=\sum_{a}[\mathscr{L},\varphi_{a}]\resolvent_{a}(\lambda)(\varphi_{a}\cdot f_{jk}).

Proof.

Let
f_{jk}\in h^{0,\alpha}(S^{2,0}{\mathcal{M}})
be given. Then

\displaystyle(\lambda-\mathscr{L})\mathscr{S}(\lambda)f_{jk}

\displaystyle=(\lambda-\mathscr{L})\sum_{a}\varphi_{a}\resolvent_{a}(\lambda)(\varphi_{a}\cdot f_{jk})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}(\lambda-\mathscr{L})\varphi_{a}\resolvent_{a}(\lambda)(\varphi_{a}\cdot f_{jk})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\varphi_{a}(\lambda-\mathscr{L})\resolvent_{a}(\lambda)(\varphi_{a}\cdot f_{jk})-[\mathscr{L},\varphi_{a}]\resolvent_{a}(\lambda)(\varphi_{a}\cdot f_{jk}).

On the support of
\varphi_{a}
, the operator
\mathscr{L}
coincides with our extended operator
\hat{\mathscr{L}}_{\mathfrak{p}_{a}}
, so that

\varphi_{a}(\lambda-\mathscr{L})\resolvent_{a}(\lambda)=\varphi_{a}
. Hence,

\displaystyle(\lambda-\mathscr{L})\mathscr{S}(\lambda)f_{jk}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\varphi_{a}\cdot(\varphi_{a}\cdot f_{jk})-[\mathscr{L},\varphi_{a}]\resolvent_{a}(\lambda)(\varphi_{a}\cdot f_{jk})

\displaystyle=f_{jk}-\sum_{a}[\mathscr{L},\varphi_{a}]\resolvent_{a}(\lambda)(\varphi_{a}\cdot f_{jk}).

∎

4.5.4.
Estimate for the error term

For all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda>\omega
and all tensor fields
h_{jk}\in h^{0,\alpha}(S^{2,0}{\mathcal{M}})
, we will show that

(4.9)

\|\mathscr{E}h_{jk}\|_{0,\alpha}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{|\lambda|}}\|h_{jk}\|_{0,\alpha}.

Proof.

The key observation here is that the commutator
[\mathscr{L},\varphi_{a}]
is a first-order differential operator: in geodesic coordinates, we
have

\displaystyle[\mathscr{L},\varphi_{a}]f_{jk}

\displaystyle=A^{pq}_{(a)}(x)[\partial_{p}\partial_{q},\varphi_{a}]f_{jk}+\tilde{B}^{pqr}_{jk(a)}[\partial_{r},\varphi_{a}]f_{pq}

\displaystyle=A^{pq}_{(a)}\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi_{a}}{\partial x^{p}\partial x^{q}}f_{jk}+\tilde{B}^{pqr}_{jk(a)}\frac{\partial\varphi_{a}}{\partial x^{r}}f_{pq}+2A^{pq}_{(a)}\frac{\partial\varphi_{a}}{\partial x^{p}}\frac{\partial f_{jk}}{\partial x^{q}}.

This implies that

\|[\mathscr{L},\varphi_{a}]f_{jk}\|_{0,\alpha}\leq C\|f_{jk}\|_{1,\alpha}

and that

\|[\mathscr{L},\varphi_{a}]\resolvent_{a}(\lambda)\varphi_{a}h_{jk}\|_{0,\alpha}\leq C\|\resolvent_{a}(\lambda)\varphi_{a}h_{jk}\|_{1,\alpha}.

By the interpolation estimate (
4.3
), one then has

\|[\mathscr{L},\varphi_{a}]\resolvent_{a}(\lambda)\varphi_{a}h_{jk}\|_{0,\alpha}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{|\lambda|}}\|\varphi_{a}h_{jk}\|_{0,\alpha}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{|\lambda|}}\|h_{jk}\|_{0,\alpha}.

The same reasoning as in §
4.5.2
then leads to the bound (
4.9
) for
\mathscr{E}
.
∎

4.5.5.
Construction of the resolvent

We showed in (
4.9
) that
\|\mathscr{E}\|_{0,\alpha\to 0,\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{2}
if
|\lambda|
is large enough. It follows that
for large
|\lambda|
, the operator
\boldsymbol{1}-\mathscr{E}
on
h^{0,\alpha}(S^{2,0}{\mathcal{M}})
has a bounded inverse given by the geometric
series

\bigl(\boldsymbol{1}-\mathscr{E}\bigr)^{-1}=\boldsymbol{1}+\mathscr{E}+\mathscr{E}^{2}+\mathscr{E}^{3}+\cdots.

Together with (
4.8
), this implies that

(\lambda-\mathscr{L})\mathscr{S}(\lambda)(\boldsymbol{1}-\mathscr{E})^{-1}=\boldsymbol{1},

i.e.,
that
\mathscr{S}(\lambda)(\boldsymbol{1}-\mathscr{E})^{-1}
is a right inverse for
\lambda-\mathscr{L}
.

We have obtained this right inverse for all
\lambda
with sufficiently large
|\lambda|
, and thus there exists
\omega^{\prime}>0
such that

\mathscr{S}(\lambda)(\boldsymbol{1}-\mathscr{E})^{-1}
is a right inverse whenever
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda>\omega^{\prime}
. Moreover, the norm of the right inverse is
bounded by

(4.10)

\|\mathscr{S}(\lambda)(\boldsymbol{1}-\mathscr{E})^{-1}\|_{0,\alpha\to 0,\alpha}\leq\\
\|\mathscr{S}(\lambda)\|_{0,\alpha\to 0,\alpha}\|(\boldsymbol{1}-\mathscr{E})^{-1}\|_{0,\alpha\to 0,\alpha}\leq 2\|\mathscr{S}(\lambda)\|_{0,\alpha\to 0,\alpha}\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda|}.

4.5.6.
Left invertibility

We conclude by showing that the right inverse is also a left inverse,

i.e.,
by showing that
\lambda-\mathscr{L}:h^{2,\alpha}\to h^{0,\alpha}
is injective if
|\lambda|
is sufficiently large.

Suppose that
f_{jk}\in h^{2,\alpha}(S^{2,0}{\mathcal{M}})
satisfies
(\lambda-\mathscr{L})f_{jk}=0
. Then we have

\mathscr{S}(\lambda)(\lambda-\mathscr{L})f_{jk}=0,

and hence

\sum_{a}\varphi_{a}\resolvent_{a}(\lambda)\varphi_{a}(\lambda-\mathscr{L})f_{jk}=0.

Arguing as before, we commute
\varphi_{a}
and
\mathscr{L}
to obtain

\sum_{a}\varphi_{a}\resolvent_{a}(\lambda)(\lambda-\mathscr{L})\varphi_{a}f_{jk}+\varphi_{a}\resolvent_{a}(\lambda)[\mathscr{L},\varphi_{a}]f_{jk}=0.

Using the operator identity
\varphi_{a}\resolvent_{a}(\lambda)(\lambda-\mathscr{L})\varphi_{a}=\varphi_{a}^{2}
,we find that

f_{jk}=\sum_{a}\varphi_{a}\resolvent_{a}(\lambda)[\mathscr{L},\varphi_{a}]f_{jk}.

Once again, we note that
[\mathscr{L},\varphi_{a}]
is a first-order differential operator with
C^{1}
coefficients, so that

[\mathscr{L},\varphi_{a}]:h^{1,\alpha}\to h^{0,\alpha}
is bounded. Therefore, one has

\displaystyle\|f_{jk}\|_{1,\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq N\sup_{a}\|\varphi_{a}\resolvent_{a}(\lambda)[\mathscr{L},\varphi_{a}]f_{jk}\|_{1,\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CN}{\sqrt{|\lambda|}}\sup_{a}\|[\mathscr{L},\varphi_{a}]f_{jk}\|_{0,\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CN}{\sqrt{|\lambda|}}\|f_{jk}\|_{1,\alpha}.

If
|\lambda|>CN
, this implies that
f_{jk}=0
.

We have shown that
\lambda-\mathscr{L}
is injective and has a right inverse for all large enough
|\lambda|
, and that the inverse

(\lambda-\mathscr{L})^{-1}
satisfies the required bound (
4.10
). Therefore,
\mathscr{L}
does indeed generate an analytic
semigroup on
h^{0,\alpha}(S^{2,0}{\mathcal{M}})
. This completes our proof of Theorem
3.1
.

5.
The spectrum of
\Delta_{\ell}
on asymptotically flat manifolds

The Lichnerowicz Laplacian is given by (
1.1
),
i.e.,

\Delta_{\ell}h_{jk}=\Delta_{{\mathcal{M}}}+2R^{q}_{pjk}h_{q}^{p}-R_{j}^{p}h_{pk}-R_{k}^{q}h_{jq},

where

\Delta_{{\mathcal{M}}}h_{jk}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}g^{pq}\nabla_{p}\nabla_{q}h_{jk}

is the “rough Laplacian.”

Lemma 5.1
.

If
|\Rm(\mathfrak{p})|\to 0
as
\mathfrak{p}\to\infty
and
r(\mathfrak{p})\to\infty
as
\mathfrak{p}\to\infty
, which are implied by
Assumption
1.2
, then it is true that:

(1)

\Delta_{\ell}:h^{2,\alpha}\to h^{0,\alpha}
generates an analytic semigroup,

(2)

\sigma(\Delta_{{\mathcal{M}}})=(-\infty,0]
, and

(3)

\sigma_{\rm ess}(\Delta_{\ell})=(-\infty,0]
.

We already proved (1) in Theorem
3.1
. Statement (3) follows from (2) because
\Delta_{\ell}
is a compact perturbation of

\Delta_{{\mathcal{M}}}
. We therefore only have to establish (2), which we proceed to do in the remainder of this section.

Let
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(-\infty,0]
be given. We show below that
\lambda-\Delta_{\mathcal{M}}:h^{2,\alpha}\to h^{0,\alpha}
has a bounded
inverse. If we were considering
\Delta_{\mathcal{M}}
as an unbounded operator in
L^{2}({\mathcal{M}};S^{2,0}{\mathcal{M}})
, then self-adjointness of
\Delta_{\mathcal{M}}

would guarantee that
\lambda-\Delta_{\mathcal{M}}:H^{2}(S^{2,0}{\mathcal{M}})\to L^{2}(S^{2,0}{\mathcal{M}})
is invertible. However, on noncompact manifolds, the spectrum
of an elliptic operator like
\Delta_{\mathcal{M}}
may depend on the particular function space in which the resolvent
(\lambda-\Delta_{\mathcal{M}})^{-1}

should act, and the spectrum of
\Delta_{\mathcal{M}}
can be strictly larger than just
(-\infty,0]
. Our goal in this section is to show that the
asymptotic flatness of
{\mathcal{M}}
rules out this possibility.

5.1.
The resolvent at
\infty

Let
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(-\infty,0]
. Then the operator

\lambda-\Delta_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}:h^{2,\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to h^{0,\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
has a bounded inverse

(\lambda-\Delta_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})^{-1}:h^{0,\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to h^{2,\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

Our assumption that
({\mathcal{M}},g)
is asymptotically flat lets us use the resolvent of the Laplacian on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
to approximate the resolvent of

\lambda-\Delta_{{\mathcal{M}}}
in the regions where the manifold is close to flat.

Let constants
\varepsilon>0
,
R>0
be given. Then there is a compact set
K_{R,\varepsilon}\subset{\mathcal{M}}
such that

\mathrm{inj}(\mathfrak{p})>2R
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in{\mathcal{M}}\setminus K_{R,\varepsilon}
, and such that the components of the metric in geodesic coordinates

\exp_{\mathfrak{p}}:{\mathcal{B}}_{2R}(0)\to{\mathcal{B}}_{2R}(\mathfrak{p})
satisfy

(5.1)

\|g_{pq}-\delta_{pq}\|_{h^{2,\alpha}({\mathcal{B}}_{2R}(0))}\leq\varepsilon.

If
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small, then it follows that the inverse
g^{pq}(x)
also satisfies

(5.2)

\|g^{pq}-\delta_{pq}\|_{h^{2,\alpha}({\mathcal{B}}_{2R}(0))}\leq C\varepsilon.

In particular, the Christoffel symbols of the metric and their derivatives are also small in the sense that

\|\Gamma^{i}_{jk}(x)\|_{h^{1,\alpha}({\mathcal{B}}_{2R}(0))}\leq C\varepsilon.

In geodesic coordinates, the rough Laplacian of a tensor field
h_{jk}
is given by

\Delta_{{\mathcal{M}}}h_{jk}=g^{pq}(x)\partial_{p}\partial_{q}h_{jk}+B^{pqr}_{jk}(x)\partial_{r}h_{pq}(x)+C^{pq}_{jk}(x)h_{pq}(x),

where the coefficients
B,C
are related to the Christoffel symbols via expressions of the form
B=\Gamma
and

C=\partial\Gamma+\Gamma*\Gamma
. This implies that

(5.3)

\|\Delta_{\mathcal{M}}h_{jk}-\Delta_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}h_{jk}\|_{h^{0,\alpha}({\mathcal{B}}_{2R}(0))}\leq C\varepsilon\|h_{jk}\|_{h^{2,\alpha}({\mathcal{B}}_{2R}(0))}

for all
h_{jk}\in h^{2,\alpha}({\mathcal{B}}_{2R}(0))
.

Our bounded geometry assumption together with a standard covering argument now let us construct a locally finite covering as follows. We
choose points
\mathfrak{p}_{a}
for
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

(1)

if
{\mathcal{B}}_{a}^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}B_{R/2}(\mathfrak{p}_{a})
, then
\{{\mathcal{B}}_{a}^{\prime}:a\in\mathbb{N}\}
is an open cover of

\overline{{\mathcal{M}}\setminus K_{R,\varepsilon}}
, and

(2)

if
{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}B_{R}(\mathfrak{p}_{a})
, one has
{\mathcal{B}}_{a_{0}}\cap\cdots\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{a_{N}}=\emptyset
for all

a_{0}<a_{1}<\cdots<a_{N}
.

Choose
\eta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\eta(s)\left\{\;\begin{matrix}=1&\mbox{for }s\leq\frac{1}{2},\\
>0&\mbox{for }s<1,\\
=0&\mbox{for }s\geq 1.\end{matrix}\right.

We define

\psi_{a}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\eta\Big(\frac{d(x,\mathfrak{p}_{a})}{R}\Big)\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\varphi(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\frac{\psi_{a}(x)}{\sum_{b}\psi_{b}(x)},

noting that
\sum_{a}\varphi_{a}(x)=1
on
\bigcup_{a}{\mathcal{B}}_{a}
. Furthermore, because

\overline{{\mathcal{M}}\setminus K_{R,\varepsilon}}\subset\bigcap_{a}{\mathcal{B}}_{a}^{\prime}
, for any
x\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}\setminus K_{R,\varepsilon}}
,
there exists
a\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x\in{\mathcal{B}}_{a}^{\prime}
, and thus we have
\psi_{a}(x)\geq\eta(\frac{1}{2})=1
. Therefore,

\sum_{a}\psi_{a}(x)\geq 1
, and
\varphi
is smooth on
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}\setminus K_{R,\varepsilon}}
.

Define

\lambda(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\sum_{d(p_{a},K_{R,\varepsilon})\geq 2R}\varphi_{a}(x).

Now if
d(x,K_{R,\varepsilon})\geq 3R
, then for any index
a
such that
x\in{\mathcal{B}}_{a}
, one has
d(\mathfrak{p}_{a},K_{R,\varepsilon})\geq 2R
. For
such
x
, it follows that

\sum_{b}\psi_{b}(x)=\sum_{\psi_{b}(x)>0}\psi_{b}(x)=\sum_{{\mathcal{B}}_{b}\ni x}\psi_{b}(x)=\sum_{d(\mathfrak{p}_{b},K_{R,\varepsilon})\geq 2R}\psi_{b}(x),

and hence

\lambda(x)\Big|_{d(x,K_{R,\varepsilon})\geq 3R}=\sum_{d(\mathfrak{p}_{a},K_{R,\varepsilon})\geq 2R}\frac{\psi_{a}(x)}{\sum_{b}\psi_{b}(x)}=\frac{\sum_{d(\mathfrak{p}_{a},K_{R,\varepsilon})\geq 2R}\psi_{a}(x)}{\sum_{b}\psi_{b}(x)}=1.

On the other hand, if
\lambda(x)>0
then there exists
{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\ni x
centered at
\mathfrak{p}_{a}
such that
d(\mathfrak{p}_{a},K_{R,\varepsilon})\geq 2R
,
which implies that
d(x,K_{R,\varepsilon})\geq R
. Now define

\varphi_{0}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}1-\lambda(x).

Then
\varphi_{0}(x)=1
on
x\in K_{R,\varepsilon}
. Let

\mathfrak{A}=\{0\}\cup\{a:d(\mathfrak{p}_{a},K_{R,\varepsilon})\geq 2R\}.

We have shown that
\{\varphi_{a}\colon a\in\mathfrak{A}\}
is a partition of unity. Moreover, the following observation is an immediate consequence of
its construction:

Lemma 5.2
.

There exists a constant
C
depending only on the cutoff function
\eta
, but not
R
or
N
,
such that for all
a\in\mathfrak{A}
, we have

\|\nabla\varphi_{a}\|_{0,\alpha}+\|\nabla^{2}\varphi_{a}\|_{0,\alpha}\leq\frac{C}{R}.

The set

\Omega\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\{x\in\mathcal{M}:\varphi_{0}(x)>0\}

is an open neighborhood of
K_{R,\varepsilon}
with compact closure. We write
\Delta_{\Omega}
for the rough Laplacian restricted to symmetric
tensors on
\Omega
. Using standard
L^{2}
theory, one shows that
\Delta_{\Omega}:H_{0}^{1}(S^{2,0}\Omega)\to H^{-1}(S^{2,0}\Omega)
defines a
self-adjoint operator,

\Delta_{\Omega}:\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\Omega})\cap H_{0}^{1}(S^{2,0}\Omega)\to L^{2}(S^{2,0}\Omega),

with compact resolvent, whose spectrum consists of point spectrum contained in
(-\infty,0]
. The resolvent of
\Delta_{\Omega}
on
\Omega

with Dirichlet boundary conditions is therefore a bounded operator

(\lambda-\Delta_{\Omega})^{-1}:L^{2}(S^{2,0}\Omega)\to\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\Omega})\cap H_{0}^{1}(S^{2,0}\Omega).

Interior Schauder estimates for elliptic equations imply for any open subset
\Omega_{1}
of
{\mathcal{M}}
with
\bar{\Omega}_{1}\subset\Omega
and any

h_{jk}\in h^{0,\alpha}(S^{2,0}\Omega)
, the restriction of
(\lambda-\Delta_{\Omega})^{-1}h_{jk}
to
\Omega_{1}
belongs to
h^{2,\alpha}
.
Furthermore one has the bound

\bigl\|(\lambda-\Delta_{\Omega})^{-1}h_{jk}\bigr\|_{h^{2,\alpha}(\Omega_{1})}\leq C\|h_{jk}\|_{h^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)},

where the constant
C
does not depend on
h_{jk}
.

We consider the following approximate resolvent

\mathscr{S}=\varphi_{0}(\lambda-\Delta_{\Omega})^{-1}\varphi_{0}+\sum_{a\in\mathfrak{A},a>0}\varphi_{a}(\lambda-\Delta_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})^{-1}\varphi_{a},

where the terms are again to be interpreted as in (
4.7
); thus, we multiply a tensor field with
\varphi_{a}
, express it
in geodesic coordinates on
{\mathcal{B}}_{a}
, apply the resolvent of
\Delta_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
or
\Delta_{\Omega}
, and multiply again with
\varphi_{a}
,
resulting in an
h^{2,\alpha}
function on
{\mathcal{M}}
; finally we add these over
a\in\mathfrak{A}
.

From here on
\sum_{a}
will always denote a sum over all
a\in\mathfrak{A}
.

As before, we estimate the error in this near-resolvent. Let
\Delta_{a}=\Delta_{\Omega}
for
a=0
and
\Delta_{a}=\Delta_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
for

a\in\mathfrak{A}
with
a>0
. Then

\displaystyle(\lambda-\Delta_{{\mathcal{M}}})\mathscr{S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}(\lambda-\Delta_{{\mathcal{M}}})\varphi_{a}(\lambda-\Delta_{a})^{-1}\varphi_{a}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\varphi_{a}(\lambda-\Delta_{{\mathcal{M}}})(\lambda-\Delta_{a})^{-1}\varphi_{a}-\sum_{a}[\Delta_{{\mathcal{M}}},\varphi_{a}](\lambda-\Delta_{a})^{-1}\varphi_{a}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{1}+\sum_{a}\varphi_{a}(\Delta_{a}-\Delta_{{\mathcal{M}}})(\lambda-\Delta_{a})^{-1}\varphi_{a}-\sum_{a}[\Delta_{{\mathcal{M}}},\varphi_{a}](\lambda-\Delta_{a})^{-1}\varphi_{a}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{1}+\sum_{a>0}\varphi_{a}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}-\Delta_{{\mathcal{M}}})(\lambda-\Delta_{a})^{-1}\varphi_{a}-\sum_{a}[\Delta_{{\mathcal{M}}},\varphi_{a}](\lambda-\Delta_{a})^{-1}\varphi_{a}.

The term with
a=0
does not appear in the first summation, because when
a=0
, we have
\Delta_{a}=\Delta_{\Omega}=\Delta_{\mathcal{M}}
. We define
errors
\mathscr{E}_{1},\mathscr{E}_{2}
by

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a>0}\varphi_{a}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}-\Delta_{{\mathcal{M}}})(\lambda-\Delta_{a})^{-1}\varphi_{a},

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{2}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{a}[\Delta_{{\mathcal{M}}},\varphi_{a}](\lambda-\Delta_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})^{-1}\varphi_{a}.

We now proceed to estimate those errors.

Multiplication with
\varphi_{a}
is a bounded operator on
h^{0,\alpha}
with

\|\varphi_{a}h_{jk}\|_{0,\alpha}\leq C\|h_{jk}\|_{0,\alpha},

where the constant
C
does not depend on our choice of
R\geq 1
and
\epsilon
. It then follows from (
5.3
) that

\|\varphi_{a}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}-\Delta_{{\mathcal{M}}})(\lambda-\Delta_{{\mathcal{M}}})^{-1}\varphi_{a}h_{jk}\|_{0,\alpha}\leq C\varepsilon\|h_{jk}\|_{0,\alpha}

for all
h_{jk}\in h^{0,\alpha}(S^{2,0}{\mathcal{M}})
. Hence,

\|\mathscr{E}_{1}h_{jk}\|_{0,\alpha}=\Bigl\|\sum_{a>0}\varphi_{a}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}-\Delta_{{\mathcal{M}}})(\lambda-\Delta_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})^{-1}\varphi_{a}\cdot h_{jk}\Bigr\|_{0,\alpha}\leq CN\varepsilon\|h_{jk}\|_{0,\alpha}.

We can therefore make the first error as small as needed by reducing
\varepsilon
.

The second error operator
\mathscr{E}_{2}
contains a commutator, which we now compute. For any
h_{jk}
, we have

[\Delta_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi_{a}]h_{jk}=[g^{pq}\nabla_{p}\nabla_{q},\varphi_{a}]h_{jk}=2g^{pq}(\nabla_{p}\varphi_{a})\nabla_{q}h_{jk}+g^{pq}(\nabla_{p}\nabla_{q}\varphi_{a})h_{jk}.

The bounds from Lemma
5.2
on the derivatives of the cutoff functions
\varphi_{a}
imply that

\|[\Delta_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi_{a}]h_{jk}\|_{0,\alpha}\leq\frac{C}{R}\|h_{jk}\|_{1,\alpha}.

Since
(\lambda-\Delta_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})^{-1}:h^{0,\alpha}\to h^{2,\alpha}\hookrightarrow h^{1,\alpha}
is bounded, we can estimate the second error
operator
\mathscr{E}_{2}
as follows:

\forall h_{jk}\in h^{0,\alpha}(S^{2,0}{\mathcal{M}}):\qquad\|\mathscr{E}_{2}h_{jk}\|_{0,\alpha}\leq\frac{CN}{R}\|h_{jk}\|_{0,\alpha}.

The bounds for
\mathscr{E}_{1}
and
\mathscr{E}_{2}
together tell us that

\|(\lambda-\Delta_{\mathcal{M}})\mathscr{S}-\boldsymbol{1}\|_{h^{0,\alpha}\to h^{0,\alpha}}\leq CN\bigl(\varepsilon+R^{-1}\bigr).

We can make this error as small as we like by choosing
\varepsilon
small and
R
large enough. Therefore,
\mathscr{S}
is a right inverse for

Now we deal with

\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(\Delta_{\ell})=(-\infty,0]
. For
(small)

in the operator norm.

Suppose that
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
is an

eigenvalue of
\Delta_{\ell}
of multiplicity
m
, and choose
\delta>0

\sigma(\Delta_{\ell})\cap B_{\delta}(\lambda)=\{\lambda\}
. Denote the spectral projections of
\Delta_{\ell}
and

P_{0}=\oint_{\partial B_{\delta}(\lambda)}\big(\mu-\Delta_{\ell}\big)^{-1}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{2\pi i}\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad P(\theta)=\oint_{\partial B_{\delta}(\lambda)}\big(\mu-A(\theta)\big)^{-1}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{2\pi i}\,,

\theta^{*}

\{u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\}

\Delta_{\ell}

\big(h_{k},\Delta_{\ell}h_{k}\big)_{L^{2}}>0
. Because the
h_{k}
have mutually disjoint supports, they form an orthogonal sequence. This implies
that
\Delta_{\ell}
has infinitely many positive eigenvalues.
∎

6.6.
Simple eigenvalues

Our second goal in this section is to prove that the eigenvalues of
\Delta_{\ell}
with
\mathfrak{G}
-invariant eigentensors are simple. The proof is in three steps.
We begin with a smoothness criterion.

Lemma 6.2
.

If the
(2,0)
tensor field on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}+1}\times\mathcal{S}^{n_{2}}
given by

h=\eta_{0}(x)(\mathrm{d}x)^{2}+\eta_{1}(x)\hat{g}_{1}+\eta_{2}(x)\hat{g}_{2}

is smooth and
\mathfrak{G}=\mathrm{O}(n_{1}+1)\times\mathrm{O}(n_{2}+1)
-invariant, then

(6.10)

\lim_{x\searrow 0}\frac{\eta_{1}(x)}{x^{2}}=\lim_{x\searrow 0}\eta_{0}(x).

Proof.

Let
X=(x_{0},\dots,x_{n_{1}})
be coordinates on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}+1}
, and set

x=|X|=\sqrt{x_{0}^{2}+\cdots+x_{n_{1}}^{2}}.

The only
\mathrm{SO}(n_{1}+1)
-invariant
(2,0)
tensor fields on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}+1}
are linear combinations of
|\mathrm{d}X|^{2}
and
\langle X,\mathrm{d}X\rangle^{2}
,
i.e.,

h=\phi(x)|\mathrm{d}X|^{2}+\psi(x)\langle X,\mathrm{d}X\rangle^{2}

for smooth even functions
\phi,\psi:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. The Euclidean metric on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}+1}
is

|\mathrm{d}X|^{2}=(\mathrm{d}x)^{2}+x^{2}\hat{g}_{1}.

Furthermore,
x^{2}=|X|^{2}
implies that
x\,\mathrm{d}x=\langle X,\mathrm{d}X\rangle
. Therefore, the tensor
h
may be written as

h=\bigl(\phi(x)+x^{2}\psi(x)\bigr)(\mathrm{d}x)^{2}+x^{2}\phi(x)\hat{g}_{1}.

This implies (
6.10
)
∎

Now let
\lambda_{0}\geq\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots>0
be the sequence of positive eigenvalues for
\Delta_{\ell}
with eigentensors

h_{j}
whose existence we have established above. Our second step is as follows.

Lemma 6.3
.

For all
j\geq 0
, the
\eta_{2}(0)
component of
h_{j}
is never zero.

Proof.

In §
6.2
above, we represented the Lichnerowicz Laplacian as an ordinary differential operator

\Delta_{\ell}\eta=\eta_{xx}+\mathcal{A}(x)\eta_{x}+\mathcal{B}(x)\eta,

where
\mathcal{A}(x)
and
\mathcal{B}(x)
are defined in terms of the warping functions
v_{1}
,
v_{2}
as in (
6.5
) and (
6.6
).
Both
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are singular at the origin, but
x\mathcal{A}(x)
and
x^{2}\mathcal{B}(x)
are real analytic functions of
x^{2}
. Indeed,
the warping functions themselves are real analytic functions, where
v_{1}
is odd with
v_{1}^{\prime}(0)=1
, while
v_{2}
is even.
Thus,

v_{1}(x)=x+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{v_{1}^{(2k+1)}(0)}{(2k+1)!}x^{2k+1}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad v_{2}(x)=v_{2}(0)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{v_{2}^{(2k)}(0)}{(2k)!}x^{2k}.

Hence we have

w_{1,x}=\frac{1}{x}+\mathcal{O}(x),\quad w_{1,xx}=-\frac{1}{x^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(1),\quad w_{2,x}=\mathcal{O}(x),\quad w_{2,xx}=\mathcal{O}(1),

and

e^{-2w_{1}}=\frac{n_{1}-1}{v_{1}^{2}}=\frac{n_{1}-1}{x^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(1),\qquad e^{-2w_{2}}=\frac{n_{2}-1}{v_{2}^{2}}=\mathcal{O}(1),

where
\mathcal{O}(1)
and
\mathcal{O}(x)
represent even and odd power series in
x
, respectively.
We also note that
w_{1,x}w_{2,x}=\mathcal{O}(1)
.

In this way, we get the following expansions for
\mathcal{A}(x)
and
\mathcal{B}(x)
:

x\mathcal{A}(x)=\sum_{a}n_{a}\;xw_{a,x}=n_{1}+\mathcal{O}(x^{2})

and

\displaystyle x^{2}\mathcal{B}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{2}\begin{pmatrix}-2n_{1}w_{1,x}^{2}-2n_{2}w_{2,x}^{2}&-2n_{1}w_{1,xx}&-2n_{2}w_{2,xx}\\
-2w_{1,xx}&2e^{-2w_{1}}-2n_{1}w_{1,x}^{2}&-2n_{2}w_{1,x}w_{2,x}\\
-2w_{2,xx}&-2n_{1}w_{1,x}w_{2,x}&2e^{-2w_{2}}-2n_{2}w_{2,x}^{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}_{0}+\mathcal{O}(x^{2}),

with

\mathcal{B}_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}-2n_{1}&2n_{1}&0\\
2&2(n_{1}-1)-2n_{1}&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-2n_{1}&2n_{1}&0\\
2&-2&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

It follows that
x=0
is a regular singular point for the eigentensor equation

(6.11)

x^{2}\eta^{\prime\prime}(x)+x\mathcal{A}(x)x\eta^{\prime}(x)+x^{2}\mathcal{B}(x)\eta(x)=\lambda x^{2}\eta(x).

Because the coefficients
x\mathcal{A}(x)
and
x^{2}\mathcal{B}(x)
are real analytic functions of
x^{2}
in a neighborhood of the origin of the complex
plane,
x=0
is a regular singular point for the eigentensor equation (
6.11
). One can therefore use the Fröbenius method to find a fundamental set of
solutions. These solutions are of the form

\eta(x)=x^{\alpha}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}x^{2k}\quad\text{ or }\quad\eta(x)=x^{\alpha}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigl\{a_{k}x^{2k}+b_{k}x^{2k}\log x\bigr\},

for suitable characteristic exponents
\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}
, and coefficients
a_{k},b_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
. The second form appears in case two
characteristic exponents differ by an integer.

To find the characteristic exponents, we consider the principal terms of the eigenvalue equation, which are

x^{2}\zeta^{\prime\prime}(x)+n_{1}\zeta^{\prime}(x)+\mathcal{B}_{0}\zeta(x)=0.

The eigenvalues of
\mathcal{B}_{0}
are

\displaystyle\beta_{0}

\displaystyle=-(2n_{1}+1),

\displaystyle\beta_{1}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\beta_{2}

\displaystyle=0,

with respective eigenvectors

\displaystyle\zeta_{0}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}2n_{1}\\
-1\\
0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\\
0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\zeta_{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

To obtain the characteristic exponents, we look for solutions having the form
\zeta(x)=x^{\alpha_{j}}\zeta_{j}
for
\zeta_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.
We find for all
j
that

\alpha_{j}(\alpha_{j}-1)+n_{1}\alpha_{j}+\beta_{j}=0,

i.e.,

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}(\alpha_{0}-1)+n_{1}\alpha_{0}-2(n_{1}+1)=0,

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}(\alpha_{1}-1)+n_{1}\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}(\alpha_{2}-1)+n_{1}\alpha_{2}=0.

Solving the quadratic equations leads to the following six characteristic exponents:

\alpha_{0}=+2,\quad\hat{\alpha}_{0}=-(n_{1}+1),\quad\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=0,\quad\hat{\alpha}_{1}=\hat{\alpha}_{2}=-(n_{1}-1).

The leading terms of the six corresponding fundamental solutions
\zeta_{j}(x),\hat{\zeta}_{j}(x)
are

\displaystyle\zeta_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{2}\zeta_{0}+o(x^{2})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}2n_{1}x^{2}+o(x^{2})\\
-x^{2}+o(x^{2})\\
o(x^{2})\end{pmatrix};\qquad

\displaystyle\hat{\zeta}_{0}(x)=x^{-n_{1}-1}\zeta_{0}+o\bigl(x^{-n_{1}-1}\bigr);

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\zeta_{1}+o(1)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1+o(1)\\
1+o(1)\\
o(1)\end{pmatrix};

\displaystyle\hat{\zeta}_{1}(x)=x^{-n_{1}+1}\zeta_{1}+o\bigl(x^{-n_{1}+1}\bigr);

\displaystyle\zeta_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\zeta_{2}+o(1)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}o(1)\\

\displaystyle\hat{\zeta}_{2}(x)=x^{-n_{1}+1}\zeta_{2}+o\bigl(x^{-n_{1}+1}\bigr).

Since any eigentensor

is a linear combination of these fundamental

is given by one of the expressions above.

The fundamental solutions

are excluded because they possess negative characteristic exponents (remember that

) and
therefore are unbounded at

.

The fundamental solutions
\zeta_{0}(x)
and
\zeta_{1}(x)

.

It follows that the only possible behavior of an eigentensor at

\eta_{2}(0)\neq 0
.
∎

Now our second main result of this section follows as an easy corollary:

Proposition 6.4
.

The eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}>0
of
\Delta_{\ell}
are simple.

Proof.

Let

h_{j}=\eta_{0}(x)(\mathrm{d}x)^{2}+\eta_{1}(x)\hat{g}_{1}+\eta_{2}(x)\hat{g}_{2},\qquad\tilde{h}_{j}=\tilde{\eta}_{0}(x)(\mathrm{d}x)^{2}+\tilde{\eta}_{1}(x)\hat{g}_{1}+\tilde{\eta}_{2}(x)\hat{g}_{2}

for the same eigenvalue

. Then we can see from

that
\eta_{2}(0)\neq 0
and
\tilde{\eta}_{2}(0)\neq 0
. Consider the eigentensor

\hat{h}_{j}=\eta_{2}(0)\tilde{h}_{j}-\tilde{\eta}_{2}(0)h_{j},

which we can write as

\hat{h}=\hat{\eta}_{0}(x)(\mathrm{d}x)^{2}+\hat{\eta}_{1}(x)\hat{g}_{1}+\hat{\eta}_{2}(x)\hat{g}_{2}.

Since
\hat{\eta}_{2}(0)=\eta_{2}(0)\tilde{\eta}_{2}(0)-\tilde{\eta}_{2}(0)\eta_{2}(0)=0
, it follows from Lemma

that

and

are linearly dependent.
∎

7.
Distinct instabilities of the nonlinear system

For every solution
g
in Main Theorem
I
, there exists a DeTurck vector field
X_{g}
. The metric
g(t)
is then a solution of
Ricci–DeTurck flow,

\partial_{t}g=-2\Rc[g]+\mathcal{L}_{X_{g}}g.

A family of diffeomorphisms
\phi_{t}:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
is a DeTurck family for the solution
g(t)
if

\forall p\in{\mathcal{M}},\;t\leq 0:\qquad\frac{\partial\phi_{t}(p)}{\partial t}=X_{g}(\phi_{t}(p)).

Such a family exists with the property that
\phi_{0}(p)=p
for all
p\in{\mathcal{M}}
. The following paragraph shows that we can choose
\phi
so
that
\phi_{t}(p)\rightarrow p
as
t\to-\infty
.

The fact that the metrics
g(t)
converge exponentially to
g
in backward time implies that the DeTurck vector field
X_{g}
decays exponentially
as
t\to-\infty
.
This
implies that the DeTurck diffeomorphisms
\phi_{t}:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
converge at an exponential rate to a fixed map that must itself be a diffeomorphism.
Label it
\phi_{-\infty}:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
, and let
\check{\phi}_{t}=\bigl(\phi_{-\infty}\bigr)^{-1}\circ\phi_{t}
. Then the diffeomorphisms
\check{\phi}_{t}
also
evolve by the DeTurck vector field, and for
t\to-\infty
they converge to the identity map. It follows that

\check{g}(t)=\check{\phi}_{t}^{*}g(t)

is a solution of Hamilton’s Ricci flow with
g(t)\to g
as
t\to-\infty
.

While our construction guarantees that the solutions of Ricci–DeTurck flow in the
N_{\delta}
-dimensional family are distinct, it does
not immediately follow that the corresponding solutions
\check{g}(t)
of Ricci flow are geometrically distinct: imagine that

g_{1}(t),g_{2}(t)
are two solutions of the Ricci–DeTurck flow, and suppose that their corresponding Ricci flows

\check{g}_{1}(t),\check{g}_{2}(t)
are equivalent,
i.e.,
suppose there exists a diffeomorphism
\psi:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
such that

\check{g}_{2}(t)=\psi^{*}[\check{g}_{1}(t)]
for all
t
.

We show below that this cannot happen under the assumption that
the Ricci-DeTurck solutions
g_{1}(t),g_{2}(t)
both are invariant
under the isometry group
\mathfrak{G}
of
({\mathcal{M}},g)
.
This assumption holds in the case of doubly warped solutions emanating from
the Böhm soliton that appear in Main Theorem
II
, for in this case the full isometry group of the Ricci-flat soliton is

\mathfrak{G}=\mathrm{O}(n_{1}+1)\times\mathrm{O}(n_{2}+1)
, and the solutions we construct all have the same symmetry.

By construction, we have
\check{g}_{1,2}(t)\to g
as
t\to-\infty
. This implies that
g=\psi^{*}g
,
i.e.,
that
\psi
belongs
to the isometry group
\mathfrak{G}
of
g
.

Let
\check{\phi}_{t,i}:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
be the DeTurck diffeomorphisms with
\check{\phi}_{t,i}\to\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{M}}
as
t\to-\infty
. Because
the Ricci-flat metric
g
and the DeTurck metrics
g_{i}(t)
are
\mathfrak{G}
-invariant, their DeTurck vector fields and diffeomorphisms
are also
\mathfrak{G}
-invariant. Therefore they commute with the isometry
\psi
, which implies

\check{g}_{2}(t)=\psi^{*}[\check{g}_{1}(t)]=\psi^{*}[\check{\phi}_{t,1}^{*}g_{1}(t)]=\check{\phi}_{t,1}^{*}\bigl[\psi^{*}g_{1}(t)\bigr]=\check{\phi}_{t,1}^{*}\bigl[g_{1}(t)\bigr]=\check{g}_{1}(t).

Therefore, the two solutions of Ricci–DeTurck flow are the same. It follows that if solutions of Ricci–DeTurck flow are pairwise distinct,
then the same is true for the corresponding solutions of Ricci flow.

This argument completes the proof of Main Theorem
II
.

Invariant manifolds

In this appendix, we sketch the proof of Theorem

Let

Assume that

Assuming without loss of generality that
p=0_{E}
and hence that

f(x)=Tx+g(x),

where
g(0_{E})=0_{E}
and
\mathrm{d}g_{0}=0
.
The key step in the proof of Theorem

is then to establish the following result:

Lemma C.1
.

For every
v\in E^{u}
, there exists a unique ancient orbit
(x_{i})_{i\leq 0}
in
\mathcal{U}
such that
\pi^{u}(x_{0})=v

and such that
\|x_{i}\|\leq Ca^{i}
for some
C>0
and all
i\leq 0
.

In this Lemma the map
f
is not assumed to be a local diffeomorphism or even injective. The only assumptions are that
f
be
C^{1}
in
the sense of Fréchet derivatives, and that the spectrum
df_{p}
admit a splitting as described above.

Sketch of proof.

If
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small, then the spectrum
\sigma(T)
does not intersect the annulus

\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:a-\varepsilon\leq|\lambda|\leq a+\varepsilon\}.

We may replace the norm on
E
by an equivalent norm in which

\forall v\in E^{s}:\|Tv\|\leq(a-\varepsilon)\|v\|\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\forall v\in E^{u}:\|Tv\|\geq(a+\varepsilon)\|v\|.

Split
x_{i}=x^{s}_{i}\oplus x^{u}_{i}

is equivalent to the pair

(C.1)

x^{s}_{i}=Tx^{s}_{i-1}+g^{s}(x_{i-1})\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad x^{u}_{i}=Tx^{u}_{i-1}+g^{u}(x_{i-1}).

The first recurrence equation implies that

x^{s}_{i}=T^{N}x^{s}_{i-N}+g^{s}(x^{s}_{i-1})+Tg^{s}(x^{s}_{i-2})+\cdots+T^{N-1}g^{s}(x^{s}_{i-N}).

If we only consider sequences
x_{i}
for which

for some constant

\|T^{N}x^{s}_{i-N}\|\leq(a-\varepsilon)^{N}Ca^{i-N}=Ca^{i}\Bigl(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{a}\Bigr)^{N}\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad N\to\infty.

The first equation in (
C.1
) is therefore equivalent to

(C.2)

x^{s}_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}T^{j}g^{s}(x^{s}_{i-j-1}).

We can rewrite the second recurrence equation in (

T|E^{u}

Iterating this recurrence
i

(C.3)

x^{u}_{i}=T^{-i}x^{u}_{0}-\sum_{j=1}^{i}T^{-j}g^{u}(x_{i+j}).

We are looking for orbits
x_{i}

(C.4)

\displaystyle x^{s}_{i}-\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}T^{j}g^{s}(x^{s}_{i-j-1})

\displaystyle x^{u}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{i}T^{-j}g^{u}(x_{i+j})

Because of the decay condition that we impose on the orbits, we are looking for solutions
(x_{i})_{i\leq 0}

We consider a doubly warped product
{\mathcal{M}}=\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}+1}\times\mathcal{S}^{n_{2}}

Ricci–DeTurck flow. We specialize to Ricci–DeTurck flow of

-invariant metrics.
Accordingly, we proceed to consider the evolution of

(D.1)

\tilde{g}=\mathrm{d}s^{2}+g_{1}+g_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}v_{0}^{2}(\mathrm{d}x^{2})+v_{1}^{2}\,\hat{g}_{1}+v_{2}^{2}\,\hat{g}_{2}

by Ricci–DeTruck flow (
2.5
). As background metric, we choose one of the Ricci-flat Böhm metrics we considered in
[
5
]
,
which we write in this appendix as

(D.2)

g_{\mathrm{B}}=(\mathrm{d}x)^{2}+V_{1}(x)^{2}\hat{g}_{1}+V_{2}(x)^{2}\hat{g}_{2}(x).

Our objective here is to prove:

Lemma D.1
.

If
\big({\mathcal{M}}=\mathbb{R}^{1+n_{1}}\times\mathcal{S}^{n_{2}},\tilde{g}=v_{0}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x^{2}+v_{1}^{2}\,\hat{g}_{1}+v_{2}^{2}\,\hat{g}_{2}\big)
is a doubly warped product

), then the number of

for which

\frac{v_{1}(x,t)}{\sqrt{n_{1}-1}}=\frac{v_{2}(x,t)}{\sqrt{n_{2}-1}}

D.1.
Regularity of the flow near a Böhm soliton

Our short-time existence theorem provides solutions
\tilde{g}(t,\cdot)=g_{\mathrm{B}}+h
where
\epsilon g_{\mathrm{B}}<\tilde{g}<\epsilon^{-1}g_{\mathrm{B}}

for some
\epsilon>0

has the form

with

h_{0}=v_{0}^{2}-1,\qquad h_{1}=v_{1}^{2}-V_{1}^{2},\qquad h_{2}=v_{2}^{2}-V_{2}^{2}.

D.2.
The DeTurck vector field

As in Appendix
A
, we let uppercase Roman indices lie in
\{0,1,\dots,n_{1}+n_{2}\}
, lowercase Romans in
\{1,\dots,n_{1}\}
,
and lowercase Greeks in
\{n_{1}+1,\dots,n_{1}+n_{2}\}

\tilde{g}^{AB}=(\tilde{g}^{-1})^{AB}

) to see

\displaystyle X^{A}

\displaystyle=\tilde{g}^{BC}(\tilde{\Gamma}_{BC}^{A}-\Gamma_{BC}^{A})

\displaystyle=\tilde{g}^{00}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{A}_{00}-\Gamma^{A}_{00})+\tilde{g}^{ij}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{A}_{ij}-\Gamma^{A}_{ij})+\tilde{g}^{\alpha\beta}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{A}_{\alpha\beta}-\Gamma^{A}_{\alpha\beta}).

By the formulas in Appendix
A.1
, this vanishes unless

, whereupon we obtain

,
where

(D.3)

f(s)=v_{0,s}-n_{1}\frac{v_{1,s}}{v_{1}}-n_{2}\frac{v_{2,s}}{v_{2}}=\frac{v_{0,x}}{v_{0}}-n_{1}\frac{v_{1,s}}{v_{1}}-n_{2}\frac{v_{2,s}}{v_{2}}.

D.3.
A simplification of the system

In formula (
A.3
) of Appendix

, we show that

\displaystyle-\widetilde{\Rc}=

\displaystyle\left\{\sum_{a}n_{a}\frac{v_{a,ss}}{v_{a}}\right\}\;v_{0}^{2}\,(\mathrm{d}x)^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{a}\left\{\Bigl(\frac{v_{a,s}}{v_{a}}\Bigr)_{s}-\frac{n_{a}-1}{v_{a}^{2}}+\frac{v_{a,s}}{v_{a}}\sum_{b}n_{b}\frac{v_{b,s}}{v_{b}}\right\}v_{a}^{2}\,\hat{g}_{a},

where

\displaystyle v_{0}\frac{\partial v_{0}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle v_{a}\frac{\partial v_{a}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\left\{\Bigl(\frac{v_{a,s}}{v_{a}}\Bigr)_{s}-\frac{n_{a}-1}{v_{a}^{2}}+\frac{v_{a,s}}{v_{a}}\left(f+\sum_{b}n_{b}\frac{v_{b,s}}{v_{b}}\right)\right\}v_{a}^{2},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial v_{a}}{\partial t}

Proof of Lemma

We wish to apply the Sturmian theorem
[

Therefore, on any time interval

Appendix E
Function spaces

In this appendix, we review properties of little-Hölder spaces on Riemannian manifolds of bounded geometry. For details, we refer
to Aubin’s book
[
6
]
, as well as papers by Hans Triebel
[
27

28
]
.
8
8

8

Note that Triebel treats the
Besov spaces

of which the Hölder spaces are a special case:
h^{k,\alpha}=B^{k+\alpha}_{\infty\infty}
,
provided that
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

E.1.
A covering lemma

Definition E.1
.

The
dimension
of a covering
{\mathcal{B}}_{a}
(
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
) is the smallest
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
{\mathcal{B}}_{a_{0}}\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{a_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{a_{N}}=\varnothing
for all
a_{0}<a_{1}<\cdots<a_{N}\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Lemma E.2
.

Suppose
({\mathcal{M}},g)
has bounded geometry. Then there are
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
r_{0}>0
such that for every
r\in(0,r_{0})
, a sequence

\mathfrak{p}_{a}\in{\mathcal{M}}
exists for which
\{{\mathcal{B}}_{a}:={\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{p}_{a},r)\}_{a\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is an open cover of
{\mathcal{M}}
of dimension at most
N
.

This result appears in
[
27
,
28
]
.
A proof is indicated in
[
6
, Lemma 2.26]
. The reader may also wish to consult
[
18
]
.

The idea behind the proof is to use the Vitali covering lemma to find a sequence
\{\mathfrak{p}_{a}\}_{a\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that
{\mathcal{M}}=\bigcup_{a}{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{p}_{a},r)
, while
the smaller balls
{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{p}_{a},\frac{1}{5}r)
are pairwise disjoint. The curvature bounds on the metric imply that for small enough
r>0

one has

\mathrm{vol}\bigl({\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{p},\tfrac{1}{5}r)\bigr)\geq cr^{n}\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\mathrm{vol}\bigl({\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{p},3r)\bigr)\leq Cr^{n}

for every
p\in{\mathcal{M}}
, where
c,C>0
do not depend on
r
or
p
.

If

{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{p}_{a_{0}},r)\cap\cdots\cap{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{p}_{a_{N}},r)\neq\varnothing,

then
\mathfrak{p}_{a_{1}},\dots,\mathfrak{p}_{a_{N}}\in{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{p}_{a_{0}},2r)
, and thus the disjoint balls
{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{p}_{a_{0}},\frac{1}{5}r)
, …,

{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{p}_{a_{N}},\frac{1}{5}r)
are subsets of
{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{p}_{a_{0}},3r)
.

Comparing the volumes of
\cup_{i=0}^{N}{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{p}_{a_{i}},\frac{1}{5}r)
and
{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{p}_{a_{0}},3r)
we find that

(N+1)cr^{n}\leq Cr^{n}.

This is a contradiction if
N\geq C/c
, so that the intersection of more than
C/c+1
balls
{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{p}_{a},r)
is always empty.

E.2.
Partition of unity adapted to a covering

Let
{\mathcal{B}}_{a}:={\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{p}_{a},r)
be an open cover of a manifold
({\mathcal{M}},g)
of bounded geometry, as in Lemma
E.1
. Then
there exists a set
\{\varphi_{a}\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathcal{M}})\colon a\in\mathrm{N}\}
with
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\varphi_{a}={\mathcal{B}}_{a}
such that

\sum_{a}\varphi_{a}(x)^{2}=1

for all
x\in{\mathcal{M}}
.

Furthermore, we can choose the
\varphi_{a}
so that

(E.1)

r|\nabla\varphi_{a}|+r^{2}|\nabla\nabla\varphi_{a}|+r^{3}|\nabla\nabla\nabla\varphi_{a}|\leq C

for all small
r>0
, and for some constant
C
that does not depend on
r
.

Indeed, choose
\psi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
with
\psi(t)=0
for
t\geq 1
and
\psi(t)>0
for
t<1
. Then let

\phi_{a}(x)=\psi\bigl({d(x,\mathfrak{p}_{a})^{2}}/{r^{2}}\bigr)\text{ for }x\in B,\qquad\phi_{a}(x)=0\text{ elsewhere,}

and define

\varphi_{a}(x)=\frac{\phi_{a}(x)}{\sqrt{\sum_{b}\phi_{b}(x)^{2}}}.

We can estimate the gradient of
\phi_{a}
using

|\nabla d(x,p)^{2}|=2d(x,p),

which implies that

\nabla\phi_{a}=\frac{\psi^{\prime}(d^{2}/r^{2})}{r^{2}}\nabla(d^{2})\quad\implies\quad|\nabla\phi_{a}|=\frac{2d}{r^{2}}|\psi^{\prime}(d^{2}/r^{2})|\leq\frac{C}{r}.

For the Hessian, we have

\nabla\nabla\phi_{a}=\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(d^{2}/r^{2})}{r^{4}}\nabla(d^{2})\otimes\nabla(d^{2})+\frac{\psi^{\prime}(d^{2}/r^{2})}{r^{2}}\nabla\nabla(d^{2})\quad\implies\quad|\nabla\nabla\phi_{a}|\leq\frac{C}{r^{2}}.

Reasoning similarly, one sees that

|\nabla^{3}\phi_{a}|\leq\frac{C}{r^{3}}.

E.3.
Little-Hölder spaces

If
(K,d)
is a compact metric space, one says that a function
f:K\to{\mathbb{R}}
is

Hölder continuous of exponent
\alpha
if

\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{d(x,y)^{\alpha}}<\infty.

The set of
\alpha
-Hölder continuous functions on
K
becomes a Banach space if one gives it the norm

\|f\|_{0,\alpha}:=\sup_{x}|f(x)|+\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{d(x,y)^{\alpha}}.

Recall that smooth functions are
not dense
in
C^{0,\alpha}
. For example, one can verify that the distance in

C^{0,1/2}([-1,1])
between
f(x)=\sqrt{|x|}
and any Lipschitz function
g:[-1,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
is at least
1
.

A function
f:K\to{\mathbb{R}}
is
little-Hölder continuous
if

\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}\epsilon^{-\alpha}\sup\bigl\{|f(x)-f(y)|:d(x,y)<\epsilon\bigr\}=0.

We write
h^{0,\alpha}(K)
for the set of little-Hölder continuous functions on
K
.
h^{0,\alpha}(K)
is a closed
subspace of
C^{0,\alpha}(K)
. If
K={\mathcal{B}}(0,R)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, then it is the closure of
C^{\infty}(K)
in

C^{0,\alpha}(K)
.

E.4.
Little-Hölder sections of a tensor bundle

Let
({\mathcal{M}},g)
be a manifold with bounded geometry, and let
T^{(p,q)}{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
be the bundle of
(p,q)
tensors
on
{\mathcal{M}}
. For simplicity of notation we describe the case of
(0,2)
-tensors (such as the metric and Ricci tensor).

Consider a covering
{\mathcal{B}}_{a}:={\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{p}_{a},r)
as in Lemma
E.1
, and parameterize each
{\mathcal{B}}_{a}
by the exponential map at

\mathfrak{p}_{a}
,
i.e.,
by
\psi_{a}:\mathcal{B}\to{{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{p}_{a},r)}
given by
\psi_{a}(\xi)=\exp\mathfrak{p}_{p_{a}}(r\xi)
. We say that a

(0,2)
-tensor
T
is
Hölder continuous of exponent
\alpha
if its components
T_{jk}
(defined by

T(\xi)=T_{jk}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi^{j}\,\mathrm{d}\xi^{k}
) are Hölder continuous functions in all of the coordinate patches
{\mathcal{B}}_{a}
, and if

\|T\|_{0,\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\sup_{a}\max_{j,k}\|T_{jk}(\xi)\|_{h^{0,\alpha}({\mathcal{B}}_{a})}<\infty.

The quantity
\|T\|_{0,\alpha}
is the
\alpha
-Hölder norm.
Changing the cover
\{{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\}
leads to a different but equivalent norm, provided that
r>0
is small enough.

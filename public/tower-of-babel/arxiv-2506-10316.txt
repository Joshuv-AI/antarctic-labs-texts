Abstract
A search for an invisible particle, X X , with a mass between 0 and 0.96 GeV / c 2 \textrm{GeV}/\textit{c}^{2} , is performed in the process J / ψ → ϕ + X J/\psi\rightarrow\phi+X using ( 8774.0 ± 39.4 ) × 10 6 (8774.0\pm 39.4)\times 10^{6} J / ψ J/\psi events collected with the BESIII detector from 2017 to 2019. The ϕ \phi meson is fully reconstructed and an efficient veto of photons, neutral and charged hadrons up to twice the K L 0 K_{L}^{0} mass is applied to the rest of the events, and the recoil mass against the ϕ \phi is obtained precisely from the kinematic constraint in the event. No significant signal is observed in the investigated region and the upper limit on the inclusive branching fraction of J / ψ → ϕ + X J/\psi\rightarrow\phi+X is determined to be 7.5 × 10 − 8 7.5\times 10^{-8} at 90% confidence level. Upper limits at a 90% confidence level are also given for this branching fraction as a function of the invisible particle mass, varying from 9 × 10 − 9 9\times 10^{-9} to 4 × 10 − 8 4\times 10^{-8} over the investigated mass range. Additionally, a 90% confidence level upper limit on the branching fraction of η → invisible \eta\rightarrow\rm{invisible} is determined to 2.6 × 10 − 5 2.6\times 10^{-5} , which improves the previous best results by more than four times. The analysis technique in this work offers a clean window to search for sub-GeV invisible particles, which can be adapted for other J / ψ J/\psi decays and direct e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} annihilation experiments in future studies, and improve the sensitivity by orders of magnitude.



Despite the strong evidence of dark matter (DM) from astrophysics and cosmology
Corbelli and Salucci 2000
;
Cooper et al. 2010
;
Bertone et al. 2005
,
DM is still not observed in accelerator experiments after decades of searches
Bertone and Hooper 2018
. The light DM in sub-GeV (MeV-GeV) range has been widely investigated in both theoretical and experimental efforts
Krnjaic et al. 2022
in the last decades, including many studies in the intensity-frontier experiments.
The mass of a light DM candidate is much less than Weakly Interacting Massive Particles
Jungman et al. 1996
, which poses new opportunities and challenges in accelerator based searches
Battaglieri et al. 2017
. Due to the weak coupling with standard model (SM) particles and the light mass scale,
light DM candidates in the sub-GeV range, such as dark photons
Holdom 1986
;
Caputo et al. 2021
and axion-like particles (ALPs)
McKay and Munczek 1979
;
Jaeckel and Ringwald 2010
, are difficult to observe at high energy colliders and by traditional direct detection methods
Krnjaic et al. 2022
.
By contrast, the
e^{+}e^{-}
colliders have unique advantages where sub-GeV light DM candidates behave as invisible particles due to their weak coupling with SM particles, and their kinematics can be inferred from the conservation of four-momenta when the remaining particles in an event are reconstructed.

Previous searches associated to sub-GeV invisible particles have been performed using many data samples from high intensity facilities
Ablikim et al. 2024a
;
Adachi et al. 2023
;
Abudinén et al. 2023
;
Adachi et al. 2020
;
Ablikim et al. 2020
;
Ablikim et al. 2023
;
Ablikim et al. 2022a
;
Ablikim et al. 2024b
;
Adachi et al. 2024
;
Cortina Gil et al. 2021
;
Ablikim et al. 2025
.
However, there are fewer searches for sub-GeV invisible particles in the hadronic decays of intermediate states, where the strong interaction dominates.
The hadronic decays of intermediate states provide great potential to search for sub-GeV invisible particles, which
have no direct interactions with SM particles or with a long lifetime, such as a free gluon, ALPs coupling with gluons
Ovchynnikov and Zaporozhchenko 2025
, and other strong interaction induced particles beyond current theoretical predictions.
Searching for sub-GeV invisible particles hadronic decays is therefore of high interest.

is determined to be
7.5\times 10^{-8}
at 90% C.L.
The upper limits on this BF are also calculated as a function of
m_{X}
, which vary from
9\times 10^{-9}
to
4\times 10^{-8}
in the same search region.
Moreover, the 90% C.L. upper limit on the BF of
\eta\rightarrow\rm{invisible}
at is set as
2.6\times 10^{-5}
, which is more stringent than the previous
best results
Ablikim et al. 2013
;
Andreev et al. 2024
.
This study shows the great potential for searching sub-GeV invisible particles in the hadronic decays of
J/\psi
, which could significantly improve the search for sub-GeV invisible particles involving the strong interaction, such as a free gluon, ALPs coupling with gluons, and possible strong interaction induced invisible particles.

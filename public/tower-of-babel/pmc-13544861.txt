Nemaline Myopathy Associated with a NEB Splice-Site Variant: A Rare Case
Authors: Pelin Y Yeşildaş, Betül Özenç
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

Nemaline myopathy (NM) is the most frequently reported structural congenital myopathy and is characterized by rod-like inclusions within skeletal muscle fibers.[1] Its clinical spectrum is highly heterogeneous, ranging from severe neonatal presentations to milder, slowly progressive phenotypes manifesting during childhood or early adulthood.[2]

Multiple genes have been implicated in NM, most commonly the NEB gene, which encodes nebulin, a key sarcomeric protein involved in thin filament regulation and sarcomeric stability.[34] NEB-related NM is typically inherited in an autosomal recessive manner and often presents with slowly progressive proximal weakness and normal or near-normal serum creatine kinase levels.[5]

With the increasing use of next-generation sequencing, the diagnostic yield in congenital myopathies with nonspecific clinical findings has improved substantially.[6] Here, we report a case of delayed-diagnosis NEB-related NM caused by a homozygous splice-site variant.

A 19-year-old female was evaluated for long-standing muscle weakness and exertional difficulty present since early childhood. Motor developmental history revealed that head control was achieved on time, independent sitting occurred at 8 months, and independent walking at 18 months. Since childhood, she had experienced early fatigue and reduced performance compared with her peers during running, stair climbing, and long-distance walking. During adolescence, increasing back pain prompted further evaluation, and thoracic scoliosis was identified. Her parents were first-degree cousins. There was no known family history of neuromuscular disease.

Neurological examination demonstrated normal cognitive function and intact cranial nerves, with no evidence of facial weakness, ptosis, or ophthalmoplegia. Muscle strength testing revealed symmetric proximal muscle weakness: Medical Research Council (MRC) grade 4/5 in the proximal upper extremities and MRC grade 4/5 in the proximal lower extremities. Distal muscle strength was preserved in both upper and lower extremities (MRC 5/5). Axial muscle strength was mildly reduced, particularly in the paravertebral muscles. Muscle tone was mildly decreased. Deep tendon reflexes were normal, and no pathological reflexes were detected. Sensory examination was normal. There was no evidence of bulbar, respiratory, or speech involvement.

Serum creatine kinase levels were within normal limits (190–210 U/L). Needle electromyography demonstrated diffuse mild myopathic motor unit potentials without spontaneous activity. Muscle biopsy was not performed; genetic testing was prioritized based on clinical suspicion. Whole-exome sequencing identified a homozygous c.3255+1G>A splice-site variant in intron 32 of the NEB gene (NM_001164507). The variant was classified as pathogenic according to American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) criteria and had previously been associated with NEB-related NM (OMIM: 256030). Based on the combined clinical, electrophysiological, and genetic findings, a diagnosis of autosomal recessive NM was established. The patient was enrolled in a physiotherapy program and was referred for orthopedic follow-up for scoliosis, annual respiratory assessment, and genetic counseling.

This case represents a mild-to-moderate form of NM associated with a homozygous NEB splice-site mutation, consistent with previously reported nebulin-related phenotypes characterized by childhood-onset proximal weakness, preserved distal strength, and normal serum creatine kinase (CK) levels.[78]

The identified c.3255+1G>A variant disrupts the canonical splice donor site and is predicted to cause exon skipping, leading to reduced functional nebulin. Splice-site variants in NEB are often associated with hypomorphic effects and relatively milder clinical courses, which likely explains the slowly progressive phenotype observed in our patient.[45]

Axial involvement with scoliosis in this case further supports the known vulnerability of postural muscles in nebulin deficiency.[4] Although respiratory impairment is often mild in splice-site variants, progressive axial weakness may secondarily affect respiratory mechanics, warranting long-term surveillance.[89]

Importantly, this case highlights that in patients with mild phenotypes and normal CK levels, muscle biopsy may not always be required when next-generation sequencing provides a clear molecular diagnosis, particularly for large genes such as NEB.[6]

In conclusion, we report a mild-to-moderate NM due to a homozygous NEB splice-site mutation, diagnosed only after years of unrecognized symptoms, underscoring the critical role of molecular testing in unexplained, slowly progressive myopathic presentations.

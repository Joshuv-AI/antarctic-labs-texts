Abstract
Polynomials of degree p − 1 2 \frac{p-1}{2} of range sum p p were determined by the second authors relying on a joint work of the authors by Kiss and Nagy. We prove that for large enough primes there is no polynomial of degree p + 1 2 \frac{p+1}{2} of range sum p p .

Introduction

We consider polynomials and polynomial functions over the field of degree
p
, where
p
is a prime. It is well known that there is a natural bijection between polynomials of degree at most
p-1
and functions from
\mathbb{F}_{p}
to
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. On the other hand,
\mathbb{F}_{p}
can be identified with the following subset
\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
of the integers so these functions can also be considered as functions from
\mathbb{F}_{p}
to
\mathbb{Z}
.

Thus we obtain an identification of the elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
of degree at most
p-1
with functions from
\mathbb{F}_{p}
to
\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
. This allows us to sum the elements of the range of a polynomial from
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
, and obtain a nonnegative integer.
It was proved in
[
9
]
that if
f\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
is a polynomial of range sum
p
, then either
f=1
or
\deg{f}\geq\frac{p-1}{2}
. The second author conjectured that up to affine transformations, the only polynomial with the preceding properties is
x^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+1
. The conjecture is clearly false, which is shown by the polynomial
\frac{p+1}{2}(x^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+1)
. It would have been more plausible to conjecture that there are no further polynomials since both of these two types appear as projection polynomial of the set defined by Lovász and Schrijver. This version of the conjecture was later confirmed.

The lower bound for the degree of polynomials whose range sum is equal to
p
, combined with a lemma from
[
4
]
provide a new proof for Rédei’s result on the number of directions determined by a set of
p
points, which was an important result in finite geometry. The original proof of it is the first instance
of polynomial methods used for discrete geometry problems.
It might be important to note that there were several other proof of Rédei’s theorem, both of combinatorial origin
[
1
]
and one using Fourier techniques
[
5
]
.

Lovász and Schrijver proved that there is a unique set of size
p
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
determining exactly
\frac{p+3}{2}
directions.
A further investigation of the connection between the range sum and the degree of a polynomial was proved in
[
3
]
, where the authors verified that the only possible projection polynomials of degree exactly
\frac{p-1}{2}
of a set of size
p
all are derived from the Legendre symbol if
p
is large enough.
This result gave an alternative proof for the result of Lovász and Schrijver but only for the same set of primes, which are larger than
7.48*10^{6}
. This was further developed by the first author
[
7
]
to primes larger than 23

Another important result in this field was proved by Gács

Theorem 1.1
.

If

\frac{p+1}{2}

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}f(x)=p

In order to complement this theorem we construct polynomials for small primes

showing that polynomials of range sum

\frac{p-1}{2}

Further we construct polynomials of degree

of range sum

, which are not projection polynomials of the sets constructed by Gács.

On the other hand, a straightforward generalization of the examples of polynomials of degree

It would be tempting to conjecture that there is no other such polynomial but this is not the case. Clearly, the range sum of

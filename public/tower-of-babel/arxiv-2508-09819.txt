Sections of Jacobian fibrations over lines
János Kollár and Giulia Saccà

Abstract
. Let | H | |H| be a linear system on a smooth surface S S . We study the cohomology classes of sections of the universal Jacobian over lines in | H | |H| . When S S is a K3 surface, the universal compactified Jacobian is a hyperkähler manifold, and we do not see how to reconcile our results with some of the steps in [BKV25].



August 10, 2026

Let
p:X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
be a Lagrangian fibration of a smooth, hyperkähler variety, and

L\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
a general line. Assume that there is a section

\sigma_{L}:L\to X_{L}:=p^{-1}(L)
.
[
BKV25
]
studies whether
it can be extended to a meromorphic section
\sigma_{P}:{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\dasharrow X
,
possibly after changing the complex structure of
X
.

We show that in many cases the answer is negative; there are topological obstructions. The right setting seems to be universal Jacobians for linear systems of curves. We work over
{\mathbb{C}}
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
S
be a smooth, projective surface
such that
\operatorname{Pic}(S)={\mathbb{Z}}[H]
, where
|H|
is basepoint-free, and members of
|H|
have at worst nodes in codimension 1 on
|H|
.
Let
p:J(S,H)\to|H|
be the universal compactified Jacobian as in
(
4
.2),

L\subset|H|

a general line,
J_{L}:=p^{-1}(L)
, and
g
the genus of the curves in
|H|
. Then

(1.1)

the Mordell-Weil group of
J_{L}\to L
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}^{r-1}
for
r=(H^{2})
.

(1.2)

Let
Z\subset J_{L}
be a section whose cohomology class is
contained in the image of the restriction map

H^{2g}(J(S,H),{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{2g}(J_{L},{\mathbb{Z}})
. Then
Z
is the zero section.

Corollary 2
.

Using the notation of Proposition
1
,
let
\sigma_{L}:L\to J_{L}
be a holomorphic section. Assume that there is some complex structure
\mathchanc{J}
on
X:=J(S,H)
such that

p:(X,\mathchanc{J})\to|H|
is holomorphic, and
\sigma_{L}

extends to a meromorphic section
\sigma:|H|\dasharrow(X,\mathchanc{J})
.
Then
\sigma_{L}
is the zero section.

Proof. Let
Z\subset(X,\mathchanc{J})
be the closure of the image of

\sigma
. Since
X
is smooth, it defines a cohomology class
\eta_{P}\in H^{2g}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
whose restriction to
J_{L}
is
\eta_{L}\in H^{2g}(J_{L},{\mathbb{Z}})
, the
cohomology class of
Z_{L}:=\sigma_{L}(L)
. ∎

Remarks 3
.

If
S
is a K3 surface,
Corollary
2
seems inconsistent with some of the intermediate claims in
[
BKV25
]
, but not with the main theorem.

Other results creating sections using Tate-Shafarevich twists
are discussed in
[
Kol25
]
.
Twists of Lagrangian fibrations are also treated in

[
AR25
,

.

See Proposition

.

Before we start the proof of
Proposition
1
, let us recall some facts about families of Jacobians.

4
Universal Jacobian.

Let
S
be a smooth, projective surface, and
|H|
a basepoint-free linear system on
S
.
We set
d:=\dim|H|
,
r:=(H^{2})
and let
g
denote the genus of the curves in
H
.

Let
|H|^{\circ}\subset|H|
be the open subset parametrizing irreducible curves with at worst nodes.
The
universal compactified Jacobian

[formula]

p^{\circ}:J^{\circ}=J^{\circ}(S,H)\to|H|^{\circ}

has the following properties; see
[
D’S79
]
or
[
FGvS99
, Sec.B]
for details.

For a smooth curve
C\in|H|
, the fiber of
p^{\circ}
over

[C]
is
\operatorname{\mathbf{Jac}}(C)
. If
C
is irreducible but singular,
then the fiber is the compactification of
\operatorname{\mathbf{Jac}}(C)
.
Then
p^{\circ}
is flat, projective, and

J^{\circ}(S,H)
is smooth.

The trivial line bundle on each
C
provides a section
|H|^{\circ}\to J^{\circ}
.

If
\operatorname{Pic}(S)={\mathbb{Z}}[H]
, then
p^{\circ}
extends to a
flat, projective morphism

[formula]

p:J=J(S,H)\to|H|,

but

J(S,H)
is not smooth in general.
However
J(S,H)
is smooth if
S
is a K3 surface and
\operatorname{Pic}(S)={\mathbb{Z}}[H]
by

[
Muk84
]
.

5
Sections over lines.

Assume in addition that
|H|\setminus|H|^{\circ}
has codimension
\geq 2
.

Let

L\subset|H|^{\circ}
be a line and
|H_{L}|
the corresponding pencil.
Assume that it has
r:=(H^{2})
distinct basepoints

\{c_{i}:i=1,\dots,r\}
. Blowing them up we get a basepoint-free pencil

B_{r}S\to L
. The
r
exceptional curves give
r
sections

E_{i}
of
B_{r}S\to L
.

Let
M
be a line bundle on
B_{r}S
such that
(L\cdot F)=0
, where
F
is a general fiber. Restricting
M
to each fiber of defines a section
Z_{M}\subset J_{L}
.

The
Shioda-Tate isomorphism
says that
if
h^{1}(S,{\mathcal{O}}_{S})=0
and all members of
|H_{L}|
are irreducible, then
M\mapsto Z_{M}

gives an isomorphism between the
orthogonal complement of
F
in the Néron-Severi group
\operatorname{NS}(B_{r}S)/\langle F\rangle
,
and the Mordell-Weil group
\operatorname{MW}(J_{L}/L)
of
J_{L}\to L
, see

[
Shi92
,
Tat95
,
Shi99
,
Ogu09
]
.

We will focus on sections constructed from the
exceptional curves
E_{i}
.
For
m_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
sstisfying
\textstyle{\sum}m_{i}=0
, the divisor

\textstyle{\sum}m_{i}E_{i}
gives a section of
J_{L}\to L
.
For
\mathbf{m}:=(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})
let us denote
the corresponding section of
J_{L}\to L
by

[formula]

\begin{array}[]{ll}\sigma_{L}(\mathbf{m})&\mbox{as a morphism $L\to J_{L}$,}\\
Z_{L}(\mathbf{m})&\mbox{as a subvariety of $J_{L}$, and}\\
\eta_{L}(\mathbf{m})&\mbox{as a class in $H^{2g}(J_{L},{\mathbb{Z}})$.}\end{array}

These give a subgroup
\Gamma_{L}\subset\operatorname{MW}(J_{L}/L)
.
Note that
\Gamma_{L}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{r-1}
, and

\Gamma_{L}=\operatorname{MW}(J_{L}/L)
iff
\operatorname{Pic}(S)={\mathbb{Z}}[H]
.

Let
W\subset\operatorname{Grass}({\mathbb{P}}^{1},|H|)
be the open subset parametrizing such pencils.
Over
W
, the

(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})
are determined only up to then action of the monodromy group.
In the universal family over
W
, the monodromy group acts transitively on the basepoints. (In most cases, it is the full symmetric group.)

6
Proof of Proposition
1
.

As we noted in Paragraph
5
,
the Shioda-Tate isomorphism implies (
1
.1).
This was already noted in
[
Shi99
]
.

As in (
5
.1), let

\eta_{L}(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})\in H^{2g}(J_{L},{\mathbb{Z}})
be a cohomology class.
As we move the line
L\subset|H|
in the Grassmannian of lines,
the
m_{i}
get permuted.
By contrast, the elements of the image of

H^{2g}(J,{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{2g}(J_{L},{\mathbb{Z}})

are invariant under monodromy.
Assume now that

[formula]

\eta_{L}(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})=\eta_{L}(m^{\prime}_{1},\dots,m^{\prime}_{r})\quad\mbox{iff}\quad m_{i}=m^{\prime}_{i}\quad\forall i.

Then
\eta_{L}(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})
is monodromy invariant only when

m_{1}=\cdots=m_{r}
, but in
\operatorname{MW}(J_{L}/L)
we have
\sum m_{i}=0
.
This shows (
1
.2), provided (
6
.1) holds.∎

By the Shioda-Tate isomorphism the sections

Z_{L}(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})
are all different from each other.
Thus we are done if the map from sections to homology classes is an injection. This is what we discuss next.

We start with the strongest possible form, but then we prove only a rather special case, which however implies (
6
.1).

Conjecture 7
.

Let
C
be a smooth, projective curve over a field
k
,
and
p:A\to C
a smooth group scheme with irreducible fibers, whose generic fiber
A_{K}
is an Abelian variety. Let
Z_{0}
be the zero section, and assume that
A_{K}
has no Abelian subvarieties defined over
k
.
Then sending a section to its numerical equivalence class

[formula]

\operatorname{MW}(A/C)\ni Z\mapsto[Z]-[Z_{0}]\in N_{1}(A)

is an injective group homomorphism modulo torsion.

8
Special case of Conjecture
7
.

Let
T
be a smooth, projective surface over a field
k
such that
h^{1}(T,{\mathcal{O}}_{T})=0
, and
q:T\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
a morphism whose fibers are irreducible and nodal.
Let
J_{T}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
be the relative Jacobian.
Fix a section
E_{0}
of
q
and let
F
be a general fiber of
q
.

Let
K
be the function field of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and
(T_{K},e_{K})
the generic fiber with the point given by
E_{0}
.
This defines embeddings

[formula]

j_{K}:T_{K}\lhook\joinrel\to\operatorname{\mathbf{Jac}}(T_{K})\quad\mbox{and}\quad j:T\lhook\joinrel\to J_{T}.

Note that pull-back induces an isomorphism

[formula]

j_{K}^{*}:\operatorname{\mathbf{Pic}}^{\circ}\bigl(\operatorname{\mathbf{Jac}}(T_{K})\bigr)\cong\operatorname{\mathbf{Pic}}^{\circ}(T_{K})=\operatorname{\mathbf{Jac}}(T_{K}).

Therefore, given a line bundle
M
on
T
such that
(M\cdot F)=0
,
there is a line bundle
M_{J}
on
J_{T}
such that

[formula]

j^{*}M_{J}=M\quad\mbox{as elements of}\quad\operatorname{NS}(T)/\langle F\rangle.

Let
D
be a divisor on
T
such that
(D\cdot F)_{T}=0
. Then

[formula]

(D\cdot M)_{T}=(D\cdot j^{*}M_{J})_{T}=(j_{*}(D)\cdot M_{J})_{J_{T}}.

The middle number is well defined since
(D\cdot F)_{T}=0
.

(Note that
(D\cdot M)_{T}
is the product used in the construction of the Shioda height pairing
[
Shi92
,
Shi99
]
. We need to lift it to the Jacobian.)

Let now
E_{0},\dots,E_{r}
be sections of
q
.
Then
D_{i}:=E_{i}-E_{0}
are linearly independent elements of the
orthogonal complement of
F
in the Néron-Severi group
\operatorname{NS}(T)/\langle F\rangle
.
By the Hodge index theorem,
the intersection form is negative definite on

\langle D_{1},\dots,D_{r}\rangle
.

Combining with (
8
.4) we conclude that

[formula]

j_{*}:\langle D_{1},\dots,D_{r}\rangle\to H_{2}(J_{T},{\mathbb{Z}})\quad\mbox{is injective.}\quad

This is almost what we want, except that the elements of

j_{*}\langle D_{1},\dots,D_{r}\rangle
are linear combinations of sections
of
T\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, viewed as section of
J_{T}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.
For (
6
.1) we need their sum in the Mordell-Weil group.
That is, if
Z_{1},Z_{2},Z_{3}
are sections
such that
Z_{3}=Z_{1}\oplus Z_{2}
in the Mordell-Weil group, then

[formula]

(Z_{1}\cdot M_{J})+(Z_{2}\cdot M_{J})=(Z_{3}\cdot M_{J})+(Z_{0}\cdot M_{J}).

Let
\tau_{i}:A\to A
denote translation by
Z_{i}
. Then

(Z_{i}\cdot M_{J})=(Z_{0}\cdot\tau_{i}^{*}M_{J})
, hence
(
8
.6) becomes

[formula]

(Z_{0}\cdot\tau_{1}^{*}M_{J})+(Z_{0}\cdot\tau_{2}^{*}M_{J})=(Z_{0}\cdot\tau_{3}^{*}M_{J})+(Z_{0}\cdot M_{J}).

This holds since
\tau_{1}^{*}M_{J}\otimes\tau_{2}^{*}M_{J}\cong M_{J}\otimes\tau_{3}^{*}M_{J}
by the Theorem of the square

[
Mum70
, p.60]
.

The proof also shows the following variant of
Proposition
1
.

Proposition 9
.

Let
S
be a smooth, projective surface such that
h^{1}(S,{\mathcal{O}}_{S})=0
,
and

|H|
a basepoint-free linear system whose members are irreducible and nodal in codimension 1 on
|H|
.

Then the Mordell-Weil group of
p^{\circ}:J^{\circ}(S,H)\to|H|^{\circ}

is isomorphic to
[H]^{\perp}
, the orthogonal complement of

[H]
in
\operatorname{NS}(S)
. ∎

Remark 10
.

In particular, every rational section of

p:J(S,H)\to|H|
is regular.
We do not know whether the same holds for all
Lagrangian fibrations of smooth, hyperkähler varieties.

11
Continuous sections.

Let
\eta\in H_{2}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
be a homology class
such that
c_{1}(H)\cap\eta=0
.
Let
N:=\sum n_{i}N_{i}
be a representative of
\eta
, where the
N_{i}\subset S
are oriented submanifolds. Under suitable transversality assumptions,

C\mapsto{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(N\cap C)
defines a continuous section
\sigma_{N}

of
p:J(S,H)\to|H|
.
If the
N_{i}
are algebraic, then
\sigma_{N}
is an algebraic section.
(There are some problems when a singular point of
C\in|H|
is in
\operatorname{Supp}N
.)

If
S
is a K3 surface, then every homology class is algebraic
on some deformation of
S
, so these continuous sections become homotopic to algebraic sections, after changing the complex structure.

Continuous sections of
J_{L}\to L
come from
homology classes of

H_{2}(B_{r}S,{\mathbb{Z}})\cong H_{2}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})\oplus\langle[E_{1}],\dots,[E_{r}]\rangle.

Since our obstructions are topological, they apply to all
deformations of
J\to{\mathbb{P}}^{d}
, not just to those that come from a deformation of
(S,H)
.
Such an example is given by the O’Grady 10-folds
X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{5}

[
O’G99
]
, which deform to
LSV 10-folds
[
LSV17
]
.

SLC3A1 Mitigates Ferroptosis and Promotes Enzalutamide Resistance in Prostate Cancer
Authors: Peng Liu, Haocheng Zhang, Yuhang Qian, Jiong Zhang, Xiaojun Deng, Bin Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTProstate cancer (PCa) remains a leading cause of cancer‐related mortality in aging male, with resistance to enzalutamide (Enza) representing a major therapeutic challenge. This study identifies SLC3A1 as a key driver of Enza resistance. Through integrative bioinformatics analysis of GEO datasets, we found SLC3A1 significantly upregulated in Enza‐resistant PCa models. WGCNA and single‐cell RNA sequencing confirmed its association with adverse prognosis and activation of key survival pathways. Functional experiments demonstrated that SLC3A1 knockdown reduced AKT phosphorylation, and inhibited colony formation in LNCaP cells. Conversely, SLC3A1 overexpression activated PI3K‐AKT signaling, suppressed ferroptosis and conferred Enza resistance in vitro and in vivo. These results reveal SLC3A1 is a promising therapeutic target for advanced prostate cancer.

Introduction

Prostate cancer (PCa) is the most common form of cancer in men worldwide and ranks as a primary cause of cancer‐related deaths [1]. Due to the lack of specific early symptoms, identifying PCa in its initial stages poses a challenge, often resulting in diagnosis at advanced stages [2]. Consequently, timely detection and intervention play a critical role in the management of PCa patients. Prior research has shown that the utilization of androgen receptor signaling inhibitors (ARSIs), such as abiraterone, enzalutamide (Enza), apalutamide, and darolutamide, in conjunction with androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) can significantly extend the overall survival of individuals with advanced PCa [3, 4, 5, 6]. However, resistance to ARSI therapy inevitably emerges in advanced PCa. Therefore, the imperative need for the development of more efficacious pharmaceutical agents for the management of both androgen‐sensitive and androgen‐insensitive PCa is evident [7, 8]. There is an urgent need to identify targets to overcome ARSI resistance due to limited understanding of hormone insensitivity and drug resistance mechanisms.

The Solute Carrier Family 3 Member 1 (SLC3A1) functions as a cysteine uptake transporter predominantly found in the apical plasma membrane of intestinal mucosal epithelial cells and renal proximal tubules, playing a crucial role in regulating intracellular cysteine levels [9, 10]. Alterations in cysteine metabolism have been linked to various driver oncoproteins, tumor suppressors, and the malignant properties of cancer [11, 12]. Research has demonstrated that SLC3A1 functions as a chaperone, aiding in the creation and movement of functional transporter heteromers to the cell membrane [13]. It partners with SLC7A9 to form a transporter complex that enables a 1:1 exchange of cationic and neutral amino acids, driven by high intracellular levels of neutral amino acids and the conversion of L‐cystine to L‐cysteine [14]. SLC7A9‐SLC3A1 is essential for reabsorbing L‐cystine and dibasic amino acids in the early proximal tubule [15]. SLC7A13 and SLC3A1 work together to transport amino acids in the kidneys, specifically reabsorbing L‐cystine in exchange for L‐glutamate or L‐aspartate [16]. The upregulation of SLC3A1 in breast cancer cells enhances cysteine uptake while concurrently reducing glutathione (GSH) accumulation. This modulation results in a decrease in lipid reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels and the subsequent activation of the AKT signaling pathway, thereby contributing to the progression of breast cancer [17, 18]. Its role in drug resistance, particularly with Enza, is not fully understood.

The present study identifies SLC3A1 as a crucial gene in Enza response for PCa, linking its increased expression to poor prognosis. Upregulation of SLC3A1 activates the AKT pathway, leading to Enza resistance, making it a potential target for therapy in resistant cases.

Materials and Methods

Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) Datasets and DEGs Identification

The study utilized data sourced from the GEO database, with detailed information in Supporting Information S1: Table 1. Differential expressed genes (DEGs) analysis was performed to discern genes showing response to Enza. Genes were categorized as differentially expressed in the GSE51872 [19] dataset and GSE78201 [20] dataset. Details are in the supplement materials.

WGCNA

The WGCNA package in R was used to cluster common modules with a cut‐off of 0.25, followed by identifying modules co‐expressed with phenotypes. Details are in the supplement materials.

Single‐Cell RNA Sequencing (scRNA‐seq) Analysis

GSE215943 [21] validated hub gene expression. Details are in the supplement materials.

Cell Culture and Reagents

LNCaP cells were obtained from the Cell Bank of Culture at the Chinese Academy of Sciences. siRNAs, Enzalutamide (S1250), Erastin (S7242) and Perifosine (S1037) [22] were listed in Supporting Information S1: Table 2. Details are in the supplement materials.

Western Blotting

Cells were lysed with RIPA buffer containing phosphatase and protease inhibitors. Supporting Information S1: Table 3 lists the primary antibodies used, their dilutions, and sources. Details are in the supplement materials.

Colony Formation Assay

Cells were seeded at 10,000 per well in 6 cm dishes and cultured for 14 days in either 1640 medium or medium with 10 µM Enza. Details are in the supplement materials.

In Vivo Experiments

All animal procedures were approved by the animal ethics committee (Shanghai Model Organisms Center Inc.). Male BALB/c nude mice were housed in SPF barrier facilities. Details are in the supplement materials.

Statistics

A two‐tailed independent Student's t‐test was performed to assess statistical significance, with significance levels established at p < 0.05 and non‐significance indicated by n.s.

Results

Enza‐Response Related Genes Identification

To identify candidate genes linked to Enza response in PCa, a GEO database search was conducted. In the GSE51872 dataset, VCaP cells treated with Enza were compared to controls, revealing 153 upregulated and 142 downregulated DEGs with log2 FC > 1 and p < 0.05 (Figure 1A). Similarly, analysis of LNCaP cells in the GSE78201 dataset identified 855 upregulated and 721 downregulated DEGs (Figure 1B). Heatmaps depicted the clustering of these DEGs (Figure 1C–D).

Next, 20,148 cells with a median of 23,952 genes each were examined. “RunPCA” dimensionality reduction revealed 10 clusters at a 0.5 resolution (Figure 4A). A heatmap displayed the top 5 variable genes per cluster (Figure 4B). CellChat visualizations were examined for analysis, with a circular plot using distinct colors for cell populations (Figure 4C). Interaction analysis among cell clusters considered each as both recipient and transmitter, highlighting pathways like WNT, MIF, NOTCH, COLLAGEN, and IGF (Figure 4D).

Therefore, our study aimed to investigate the involvement of SLC3A1 in the acquisition of response to Enza in PCa (Figure 4E–F). To identify key signaling pathways in each cluster, Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA) was conducted on 10 clusters, revealing upregulation of HALLMARK_ANDROGEN_RESPONSE in cluster 0 and HALLMARK_ANGIOGENESIS in cluster 1 (Figure 4I). Cells were then divided into SLC3A1 + and SLC3A1 ‐ groups to explore gene expression differences, with distribution and expression patterns shown in Figure 4J–K. GO enrichment analysis highlighted the inner mitochondrial membrane protein complex, oxidative phosphorylation, and biosynthetic processes (Figure 4L), while KEGG analysis pointed to chemical carcinogenesis−reactive oxygen species and oxidative phosphorylation (Figure 4M). GSEA demonstrated SLC3A1's role in promoting PCa cell survival by upregulating the PCa and PI3K‐AKT pathways in SLC3A1 + cells (Figure 4N).

SLC3A1 Mitigates Ferroptosis and Enhances Enza Resistance

To further explore the role of SLC3A1, we performed immunohistochemical (IHC) staining using PCa samples from the Human Protein Atlas (HPA). The results revealed a higher SLC3A1 expression in PCa tissues (Figure 5A), suggesting a potential role for SLC3A1 in PCa progression. We hypothesized that Enza treatment may upregulate SLC3A1, thereby activating the PI3K‐AKT signaling pathway and promoting PCa cell survival, ultimately leading to Enza resistance. To test this, we knocked down SLC3A1 using siRNA in LNCaP cells under Enza treatment and performed western blot analysis. The results showed a reduction in p‐AKT levels upon SLC3A1 knockdown (Figure 5B). We next conducted colony formation assays to evaluate the relationship between Enza resistance and AKT signaling. The results demonstrated that the Enza resistance mediated by SLC3A1 activation could be attenuated by combining SLC3A1 knockdown with the AKT inhibitor Perifosine treatment (Figure 5C). These findings suggest that SLC3A1‐related pathway is related to Enza resistance.

Based on a previous report that SLC3A1 enhances cysteine uptake and glutathione (GSH) synthesis, thereby reducing lipid ROS levels and inhibiting ferroptosis in breast cancer cells [24], we proposed that SLC3A1 overexpression may also suppress ferroptosis in PCa under Enza treatment. To test this, we overexpressed SLC3A1 in LNCaP cells and examined ferroptosis marker GPX4 under Enza burden. The results indicated that Enza treatment increased ferroptosis, while SLC3A1 overexpression elevated p‐AKT and GPX4 levels, indicating inhibition of cellular ferroptosis (Figure 5D). Besides, since SLC3A1 has been reported to activate AKT signaling and promote nuclear translocation of β‐catenin in the Wnt pathway [18], we examined Wnt/β‐catenin signaling in LNCaP‐oeSLC3A1 cells. The results indicated that Enza‐induced suppression of Wnt/β‐catenin signaling was rescued by SLC3A1 overexpression, and this effect was reversed by Perifosine treatment, supporting the involvement of AKT signaling (Figure 5E). Moreover, to enhance the generalizability of the findings and avoid the single cell line bias, we conducted ferroptosis activator Erastin treatment experiment on 22Rv1 cells under Enza burden. The results showed that Enza treatment combined with Erastin significantly activated ferroptosis in 22Rv1 cells (Figure 5F). Furthermore, the EdU assays and half‐maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) assays indicated an increased resistance to Enza in LNCaP‐oeSLC3A1 cells (Figure 5G–H). Together, our in vitro data indicate that targeting SLC3A1 suppresses AKT activation and mitigates Enza resistance in PCa, highlighting the therapeutic potential of SLC3A1 inhibition in PCa progression.

To validate these findings in vivo, we implanted SLC3A1‐overexpressing LNCaP cells into male BALB/c nude mice. After castration and tumor establishment, mice were treated with Enza to simulate androgen‐independent growth. Tumors overexpressing SLC3A1 exhibited significantly larger volumes under Enza treatment compared with control groups (Figure 5I). Notably, SLC3A1 overexpression counteracted the suppressive effect of Enza on tumor growth, indicating that SLC3A1 confers a survival advantage under Enza treatment (Figure 5J–K). Collectively, these results demonstrate that SLC3A1 promotes Enza resistance in PCa by inhibiting ferroptosis and activating AKT and Wnt/β‐catenin signaling pathways, underscoring its potential as a therapeutic target in advanced disease.

Discussion

Resistance to second‐generation androgen receptor signaling inhibitors represents a primary obstacle in PCa therapy [25]. This study explored the potential mechanism of SLC3A1 mediated Enza resistance in PCa (Figure 5L). Prior studies have established that SLC3A1 facilitates cysteine absorption and influences cellular reactions to the antioxidant N‐acetylcysteine in breast cancer [10]. Previous studies have reported Solute Carrier Family Member genes' roles in PCa and resistance to Enza, such as the up‐regulation of SLC4A4 could mediate P53 lactylation via the NF‐κB/STAT3/SLC4A4 axis [12], SLC3A2 expressed in treatment‐resistant PCa and function as a mediator of ferroptosis [26], SLC16A5 might be a biomarker for PCa determining [27]. Our findings highlight the significant role of SLC3A1 by activation of AKT expression in LNCaP cells, which has never been reported before. Furthermore, earlier investigations have suggested that SLC‐related genes, such as SLC3A1, possess prognostic significance in ccRCC and contribute to the immunological environment [28]. Consequently, it is hypothesized that the increased expression of SLC3A1 could influence PCa proliferation and resistance to Enza. To further investigate this relationship, we employed siRNA to inhibit the expression of the SLC3A1 gene in Enza‐treated LNCaP cells and evaluated its effects on the PI3K‐AKT signaling pathway. The findings of our study indicate that targeting SLC3A1 expression does indeed make the LNCaP cell more susceptible to Enza.

Despite our comprehensive analyses and experimental validation, some limitations remain. The complexity of tissues and organisms means cellular analyses may not fully represent the whole organism. Although we have now validated key findings in 22Rv1 cells, most mechanistic experiments were performed in LNCaP cells. LNCaP is an androgen‐sensitive line that does not fully recapitulate the heterogeneity of castration‐resistant PCa. Further studies using patient‐derived xenograft models are warranted to confirm the function of SLC3A1. Besides, while our ferroptosis data have been validated by functional assays, these experiments remain largely in vitro. Further in vivo validation is need to validate ferroptosis related findings. Moreover, our study did not employ models of acquired Enza resistance. Despite these limitations, our study identifies SLC3A1 as a promising therapeutic target for overcoming Enza resistance in PCa and provide new ideas for future investigations into the interplay between cysteine metabolism and ferroptosis in advanced prostate cancer.

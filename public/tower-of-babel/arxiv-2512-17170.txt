Ethic Duality: A Homological Framework for Primal-Dual Problems
Dmitry Pasechnyuk-Vilensky, Martin Takáč

Abstract
We develop a homological duality framework based on a contravariant functor D = Hom E ⁡ ( − , R ) D=\operatorname{Hom}_{E}(-,R) with dualizing object R R . A morphism is called ethic when it satisfies the canonical double-dual compatibility D 2 ​ ( f ) ​ η = η ​ f D^{2}(f)\eta=\eta f . In the derived setting, the functor RHom E ​ ( − , R ) \mathrm{RHom}_{E}(-,R) produces a graded family of Ext-groups that measure all failures of this compatibility. The first layer Ext 1 \operatorname{Ext}^{1} identifies primal–dual gaps, while higher Ext k \operatorname{Ext}^{k} provide a systematic hierarchy of derived obstructions to exactness. This formulation specializes uniformly across several classical domains. In linear and conic optimization, Farkas- and Slater-type exactness criteria correspond to the vanishing of Ext 1 \operatorname{Ext}^{1} , and integer duality gaps coincide with torsion Ext-classes. In graph theory, Kirchhoff- and Baker–Norine-type dualities arise as instances of ethic exactness. In dynamical systems, the higher derived layers encode nonvanishing persistence phenomena. Additional examples include social-choice configurations, categorical factorization in scattering formalisms, coding-theoretic duality, and Bellman-type recurrences, all appearing as concrete instances of Ext-controlled exactness. All resulting invariants are stable under derived Morita equivalence and depend only on the dualizing pair ( E , R ) (E,R) . The framework therefore provides a substrate-independent criterion for primal–dual exactness and a uniform homological description of its obstructions.



Introduction

Duality appears throughout mathematics in many unrelated guises: topological
duals of Banach and locally convex spaces, Verdier and Grothendieck duality in
geometry, Fenchel conjugation in convex analysis, and primal–dual formulations
in optimization. What these settings share is not a specific construction but a
structural question: under what conditions does a dual assignment recover the
information of the original object, and how can one measure the extent to which
it fails to do so?

In this paper we develop a homological framework that formulates this question
inside an abelian category
\mathcal{E}
equipped with a dualizing object
R
. We study
the contravariant functor

\mathbf{D}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(-,R)

together with its canonical comparison map
\eta:\mathrm{Id}\Rightarrow\mathbf{D}^{2}
.
A morphism
f
is called
ethic
if it satisfies

\mathbf{D}^{2}(f)\,\eta=\eta\,f
, which expresses a strict form of compatibility
with respect to dualization. All deviations from this condition are detected by
the derived dual
\mathbf{R}\!\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(-,R)
: the cohomology groups

H^{k}\mathbf{D}(A)=\operatorname{Ext}^{k}_{\mathcal{E}}(A,R)

form a graded obstruction tower, with
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
representing the primary
obstruction to dual exactness and the higher terms describing the remaining
layers of derived inconsistency. In the derived category
D^{+}(\mathcal{E})
these
obstructions give rise to an ethic
t
–structure whose heart consists of the
objects completely determined by their duals.

This homological formulation accommodates a variety of classical duality
phenomena within a single mechanism. In convex and conic optimization,
vanishing of
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
recovers strong duality, and torsion in

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,\mathbb{Z})
captures algebraic duality gaps in integer programs.
Kirchhoff-type formulas and the Baker–Norine Riemann–Roch theorem in graph
theory arise as instances of ethic exactness. In dynamical contexts, higher

\operatorname{Ext}
–groups describe persistence and hysteresis effects invisible to
underived duality. Further examples in social choice, Hopf-algebraic
factorizations, coding theory, and Bellman-type recurrences show that problems
with very different surface structures admit a common homological treatment.

A central feature of the framework is Morita invariance. All quantities obtained
from
\mathbf{D}
and
\mathbf{R}\!\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(-,R)
depend only on the derived dual pair

(D^{+}(\mathcal{E}),\mathbf{R}\!\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(-,R))
and remain unchanged under derived equivalences.
Thus the behaviour of duality is governed solely by the dualizing structure and
not by the particular algebraic, geometric, or combinatorial realisation of the
objects involved.

The aims of this paper are twofold: to give a unified categorical formulation of
duality based on a dualizing object
R
, and to identify the groups

\operatorname{Ext}^{k}_{\mathcal{E}}(-,R)
as the canonical homological invariants governing both dual
exactness and all degrees of its obstruction across the examples above.

The terminology
ethic
and
aisthetic
is used in the classical Greek
sense. The former, from
ethos
, designates an intrinsic structural
condition expressed by compatibility with the biduality transformation. The
latter, from
aisthēsis
, refers to the corresponding manifest or
expressive aspect appearing in tensorial or profunctorial settings. These terms
are purely structural and carry no interpretative meaning beyond their
categorical definitions.

The interpretation of

Theorem 2.5

(Universal reconciliation of gaps)

In the derived setting, duality gaps are not anomalies but degrees: for each short exact sequence of complexes, the failure of underived exactness appears as

(and higher) of

The passage to the graded setting is justified because

2.2
Finite Symmetry Types

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an abelian
k

k

\mathrm{char}\,k\neq 2

For each pair
X,Y\in\mathcal{E}

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(X,Y)
is a finite–dimensional
k
–vector space and composition is
k
–bilinear
[
48
]

Fix a dualizing object
R\in\mathcal{E}
of finite injective dimension
\operatorname{idim}_{\mathcal{E}}R<\infty
, and set

\mathbf{D}=\mathbf{R}\!\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(-,R)\colon D^{+}(\mathcal{E})^{\mathrm{op}}\to D^{+}(\mathcal{E}),

with canonical unit of biduality
\eta_{X}\colon X\to\mathbf{D}^{\,2}X

[

]
.
We denote by

the group of natural automorphisms of the identity functor.

A natural automorphism
\theta\in\Aut(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{E}})
is ethic if

\mathbf{D}^{\,2}(\theta)\circ\eta=\eta\circ\theta.

We write
\Aut_{\eta}(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{E}})\subseteq\Aut(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{E}})
for this subgroup.
For each
\theta

\eta
–adjoint

\theta^{\dagger}:=\eta^{-1}\circ\mathbf{D}(\theta)\circ\eta,

so that
(\psi\!\circ\!\theta)^{\dagger}=\theta^{\dagger}\!\circ\!\psi^{\dagger}
and
(\theta^{\dagger})^{\dagger}=\theta
by functoriality and naturality of
\eta

[
48
]
.
The bidual conjugation

J(\theta):=\eta^{-1}\circ\mathbf{D}^{\,2}(\theta)\circ\eta

is an involution,
J^{2}=\mathrm{id}
, and
\Aut_{\eta}(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{E}})=\Fix(J)
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Every
\theta\in\Aut(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{E}})
acts on any simple
S\in\mathcal{E}
as
\theta_{S}=\lambda(S)\mathrm{id}_{S}
with
\lambda(S)\in k^{\times}
.
If
S,S^{\prime}
belong to the same Gabriel block, then
\lambda(S)=\lambda(S^{\prime})
.
Hence
\Aut(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{E}})\cong(k^{\times})^{t}
, where

is the number of Gabriel blocks
[
26
]
.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{E}}(S)
is a division
k

is algebraically closed,
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{E}}(S)\simeq k

[
84
]
, hence
\theta_{S}=\lambda(S)\mathrm{id}_{S}
.
Naturality across a non–split extension
0\to S\to E\to S^{\prime}\to 0
forces equal scalars on
S,S^{\prime}
.
By the definition of Gabriel blocks, scalars are constant within each block, yielding
(k^{\times})^{t}
.
∎

Theorem 2.7

(Finite classification of ethic symmetry types)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
and
R
be as above.
Then

\Aut_{\eta}(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{E}})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{t},

where
t
is the number of Gabriel blocks.
Moreover, every
\theta\in\Aut_{\eta}(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{E}})
belongs to exactly one of the following five adjoint–symmetry classes:

(O)

\displaystyle\text{ Orthogonal: }\theta^{\dagger}=\theta,\ \theta^{2}=\mathrm{id};

(Sp)

\displaystyle\text{ Symplectic: }\theta^{\dagger}=-\theta,\ \theta^{2}=\mathrm{id};

(U)

\displaystyle\text{ Unitary: }\theta^{\dagger}=\theta^{-1};

(T)

\displaystyle\text{ Twisted: }\exists\,u\in Z(\operatorname{End}(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{E}})),\ u^{2}=\mathrm{id},\ (u\theta u^{-1})^{\dagger}=\pm(u\theta u^{-1});

(N)

\displaystyle\text{ Neutral: }\theta\text{ central and commuting with all }\eta_{X}.

In Hom–finite scalar situations all five classes reduce to blockwise signs
\lambda_{b}=\pm 1
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.6
one has
\Aut(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{E}})\cong(k^{\times})^{t}
,
\theta\leftrightarrow(\lambda_{b})_{b=1}^{t}
.
Because
\mathbf{D}
is
k
–linear and contravariant,
\mathbf{D}^{\,2}(\lambda_{b}\mathrm{id}_{X})=\lambda_{b}\mathrm{id}_{\mathbf{D}^{\,2}X}
for any object
X
in block
b
.
Thus
J
acts on
(k^{\times})^{t}
by inversion,
J((\lambda_{b})_{b})=(\lambda_{b}^{-1})_{b}
.
The equality
J(\theta)=\theta
implies
\lambda_{b}=\lambda_{b}^{-1}
, hence
\lambda_{b}^{2}=1
for all
b
.
As
\mathrm{char}\,k\neq 2
and
k
is algebraically closed,
\lambda_{b}\in\{\pm 1\}
.
Blocks are independent, giving
\Aut_{\eta}(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{E}})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{t}
.

To obtain the five symmetry classes, analyze relations between
\theta
and
\theta^{\dagger}
.
Since
\theta^{\dagger}=\eta^{-1}\mathbf{D}(\theta)\eta
defines an anti–involution on
\operatorname{End}(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{E}})
, admissible combinations of the two involutions
J
and
\dagger
form a Klein–type lattice.
Up to equivalence under central conjugations this lattice has exactly five distinct fixed configurations:

(O)

\theta^{\dagger}=\theta
,
\theta^{2}=\mathrm{id}
(self–adjoint involution);

(Sp)

\theta^{\dagger}=-\theta
,
\theta^{2}=\mathrm{id}
(skew–adjoint involution);

(U)

\theta^{\dagger}=\theta^{-1}
(adjoint equal to inverse);

(T)

(u\theta u^{-1})^{\dagger}=\pm(u\theta u^{-1})
for a central involution
u
, describing inner adjoint twists;

(N)

\theta
central and commuting with all
\eta_{X}
, corresponding to the intersection of (O) and (U).

No further relations are compatible with both
J^{2}=\mathrm{id}
and
(\dagger)^{2}=\mathrm{id}
: every other composite condition either collapses to one of the above or contradicts involutivity.
Hence precisely these five algebraic types occur.
In the scalar case
\lambda_{b}=\pm 1
, one has
\theta^{\dagger}=\theta^{-1}=\theta
, so all five collapse to the discrete blockwise signs
\{\pm 1\}
, but the classification of forms remains exhaustive.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

If
\mathcal{E}
is indecomposable (
t=1
), then
\Aut_{\eta}(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{E}})=\{\mathrm{id},-\mathrm{id}\}
, and only the orthogonal
(+1)
and symplectic
(-1)
forms appear.

The five ethic symmetry forms (O, Sp, U, T, N) formally resemble the classical orthogonal, symplectic and unitary dualities arising in the Tannaka–Krein setting of representation theory, where duality is monoidal and acts on tensor forms of bilinear pairings.
In the present context no monoidal or representation-theoretic assumptions are made: the duality functor
\mathbf{D}=\mathbf{R}\!\operatorname{Hom}(-,R)
is purely homological, and the adjoint involution
\dagger=\eta^{-1}\mathbf{D}(\,\cdot\,)\eta
derives from the canonical bidual transformation
\eta
.
Thus the classification extends the familiar Tannakian trichotomy to the general functorial environment of Hom–finite abelian categories, where ethic symmetry concerns natural transformations of functors rather than bilinear tensor structures.
This makes the result formally parallel to the Tannakian case but conceptually independent of it.

This follows from the general obstruction–lifting mechanism

Since

Any theorem whose statement depends only on
H^{\ast}\mathbf{D}
, the spectral sequence in Proposition
2.18
, or the heart
\heartsuit_{\eth}
(e.g. ethic time/decay laws, Kirchhoff/Baker–Norine instances, Slater/Farkas collapses) transfers verbatim along Morita equivalences
[
73
,
79
]
. The statement holds for any theorem depending functorially
on
\mathbf{D}
or on its derived cohomology, since these data
are preserved under derived Morita equivalence (F intertwines
the dual functors up to natural isomorphism).

Morita transfer formalizes universality: ethic dual laws are properties of the duality functor, not of the material substrate. Arithmetic, economic, or geometric media are equivalent viewpoints when linked by Morita equivalence.

From the categorical viewpoint this means that all ethic invariants
are functions of the derived dual pair

(D^{+}(\mathcal{E}),\mathbf{R}\!\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(-,R))
up to equivalence;
hence they depend only on the derived Morita class of
(\mathcal{E},R)
.

2.6
Ethic Morse Reduction

We work in an abelian category
\mathcal{E}
with enough injectives
[
84
]
.
Fix a resource object
R\in\mathcal{E}
and the contravariant duality functor

\mathbb{D}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(-,R):\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathsf{Ab},\qquad\mathbf{D}=\mathbf{R}\!\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(-,R):D^{+}(\mathcal{E})^{\mathrm{op}}\to D^{+}(\mathcal{E}),

with unit (evaluation)
\eta_{X}:X\to\mathbb{D}^{2}(X)
and its derived avatar
\eta_{A^{\bullet}}:A^{\bullet}\to\mathbf{D}^{2}(A^{\bullet})
.
We use the classical Morse theory on smooth manifolds
[
59
]
and the discrete Morse theory on finite CW or simplicial complexes
[
25
]
, always through functorial chain models
C_{\ast}(-)
in
\mathcal{E}
. All (co)homology is taken in
\mathcal{E}
or its derived category, and all chain maps are understood up to chain homotopy when indicated.

Let
A\in\mathcal{E}
be equipped with a finite filtration

0=\mathcal{F}_{0}A\subset\mathcal{F}_{1}A\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{F}_{N}A=A,

and denote
\operatorname{gr}_{i}A:=\mathcal{F}_{i}A/\mathcal{F}_{i-1}A
.
A reduction step
(r_{\alpha\beta},j_{\alpha\beta})
of
(A,\mathcal{F}_{\bullet})
from level
\beta
to
\alpha<\beta
is a pair of chain maps

r_{\alpha\beta}:C_{\ast}(\mathcal{F}_{\beta}A)\to C_{\ast}(\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}A),\quad j_{\alpha\beta}:C_{\ast}(\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}A)\hookrightarrow C_{\ast}(\mathcal{F}_{\beta}A)

with
r_{\alpha\beta}\circ j_{\alpha\beta}\simeq\mathrm{id}
and
j_{\alpha\beta}\circ r_{\alpha\beta}\simeq\mathrm{id}
on the image of
j_{\alpha\beta}
.

Definition 2.23

(Ethic reduction)
.

A reduction step
(r_{\alpha\beta},j_{\alpha\beta})
is ethic if

\mathbb{D}^{2}(r_{\alpha\beta})\circ\eta=\eta\circ r_{\alpha\beta},\qquad\mathbb{D}^{2}(j_{\alpha\beta})\circ\eta=\eta\circ j_{\alpha\beta},

and, in the derived setting,

\mathbf{D}^{2}(r_{\alpha\beta})\circ\eta\simeq\eta\circ r_{\alpha\beta}
and
\mathbf{D}^{2}(j_{\alpha\beta})\circ\eta\simeq\eta\circ j_{\alpha\beta}

in
D^{+}(\mathcal{E})
.

Ethicity expresses the naturality of the unit
\eta
along the reduction, the same condition used in §
2.3
.

Let
K
be a finite CW or simplicial complex with cellular chain complex
C_{\ast}(K)\in\mathcal{E}
. A discrete Morse matching
V

[
25
]
is an acyclic partial matching of incident cell pairs
\sigma^{p}\leftrightarrow\tau^{p+1}
, producing a Morse complex
M_{\ast}(K;V)
spanned by unmatched (critical) cells with a chain homotopy equivalence
C_{\ast}(K)\simeq M_{\ast}(K;V)
.

Definition 2.24

(Ethic discrete Morse matching)
.

A matching
V
is ethic if for every matched pair
\sigma^{p}\leftrightarrow\tau^{p+1}
the elementary chain map
m_{\sigma\to\tau}:C_{p}(\sigma)\to C_{p+1}(\tau)
and all compositions along V-paths satisfy

\mathbb{D}^{2}(m)\circ\eta=\eta\circ m\quad\text{in }\mathcal{E},\qquad\mathbf{D}^{2}(m)\circ\eta\simeq\eta\circ m\quad\text{in }D^{+}(\mathcal{E}).

Lemma 2.25

(Functoriality of ethic reductions)
.

Composition of ethic reductions is ethic. If
f:C_{\ast}\to C^{\prime}_{\ast}
commutes with
\eta
, then ethic reductions on
C_{\ast}
and
C^{\prime}_{\ast}
transport along
f
to ethic reductions on mapping cones.

Proof.

Naturality of
\eta
implies stability under composition. Mapping cones respect
\mathbf{D}
and
\eta
by derived functoriality
[
28
]
.
∎

Proposition 2.26

(Ethic Morse equivalence)
.

If
V
is an ethic discrete Morse matching, then the Forman collapse
C_{\ast}(K)\simeq M_{\ast}(K;V)
lifts to

\mathbf{D}(C_{\ast}(K))\simeq\mathbf{D}(M_{\ast}(K;V))\quad\text{in }D^{+}(\mathcal{E}),

inducing
H^{i}\mathbf{D}(C_{\ast}(K))\cong H^{i}\mathbf{D}(M_{\ast}(K;V))
.

Proof.

Forman’s collapse is a zig-zag of elementary chain equivalences
[
25
]
. Each step is ethic, hence commutes with
\eta
; since
\mathbf{D}
is triangulated and contravariant, it preserves these equivalences
[
28
]
.
∎

Theorem 2.27

(Ethic Morse inequalities)
.

Let
V
be an ethic matching on
K
with
c_{i}^{\mathrm{eth}}
critical
i
-cells. Then

c_{i}^{\mathrm{eth}}\geq\operatorname{rank}H_{i}(C_{\ast}(K)),\qquad c_{i}^{\mathrm{eth}}\geq\operatorname{rank}H^{i}(\mathbf{D}(C_{\ast}(K))).

Proof.

The first is Forman’s inequality
[
25
]
; the second follows because
H^{i}\mathbf{D}(C_{\ast}(K))\cong H^{i}\mathbf{D}(M_{\ast}(K;V))
and each
i
-critical cell yields one generator.
∎

Theorem 2.28

(Monotonicity under ethic reductions)
.

If
C_{\ast}\rightsquigarrow C^{\prime}_{\ast}
is ethic, then

\operatorname{rank}H^{i}\mathbf{D}(C^{\prime}_{\ast})\leq\operatorname{rank}H^{i}\mathbf{D}(C_{\ast}),\quad\mathrm{tors}\,H^{1}\mathbf{D}(C^{\prime}_{\ast})\mid\mathrm{tors}\,H^{1}\mathbf{D}(C_{\ast}).

Proof.

By functoriality of
\mathbf{D}
on ethic reductions, the reduced Morse complex has no more generators; torsion divisibility follows from the Smith normal form argument on the dual differentials.
∎

Lemma 2.29

(Lifting ethic matchings)
.

If
V
is ethic on
K
and
\pi:\hat{K}\to K
a normal covering with group
\Gamma
, its lift
\hat{V}
is ethic;
\Gamma
-invariant critical cells of

descend bijectively to those of

.

Theorem 2.30

(Ethic descent along coverings)
.

For a tower of normal coverings
\{\pi_{k}:\hat{K}_{k}\to K\}
with deck groups
\Gamma_{k}
and lifts
V_{k}
,
if the sets of
\Gamma_{k}
-invariant critical cells stabilize, then

H^{>1}\mathbf{D}(C_{\ast}(K))=0,\qquad H^{1}\mathbf{D}(C_{\ast}(K))\cong\big(H^{1}\mathbf{D}(C_{\ast}(\hat{K}_{k}))\big)^{\Gamma_{k}}

for large

.

Proof.

Identical to the derived Galois-descent argument in Section

: stabilized invariants imply collapse of higher derived obstructions, giving the claimed identification of

with invariants.
∎

Theorem 2.31

(Continuous ethic Morse reduction)
.

Let
F:X\to\mathbb{R}
be a Morse function on a smooth manifold
X
with gradient flow
\Phi_{t}
.
If every Morse attachment

satisfies
\mathbb{D}^{2}(\cdot)\eta=\eta(\cdot)
,
then

\mathbf{D}(C_{\ast}(X))\simeq\mathbf{D}(M_{\ast}(X;F)),

and the inequalities above hold with
c_{i}^{\mathrm{eth}}
critical points of index
i

[
59
]
.

Ethicity enriches classical Morse theory by controlling the derived dual groups
H^{\ast}\mathbf{D}
, i.e. the obstructions intrinsic to the duality
\mathbb{D}=\operatorname{Hom}(-,R)
. Without

used throughout the paper.

3
Left-adjoint Duality, or Aistetics

3.1
Co-ethics

Let
(\mathcal{E},R)
be an abelian resource category
with duality functor
\mathbf{D}=\mathbf{R}\!\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(-,R)
.
Define the co–ethic functor as its left adjoint
in the sense of Grothendieck–Verdier duality:

\mathbf{C}\;:=\;R\!\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{E}}(-):D^{+}(\mathcal{E})\longrightarrow D^{+}(\mathcal{E}),

\operatorname{Hom}_{D^{+}(\mathcal{E})}(R\!\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}\!A,B)\;\simeq\;\operatorname{Hom}_{D^{+}(\mathcal{E})}(A,\mathbf{R}\!\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(R,B)).

A morphism
f:A^{\bullet}\to B^{\bullet}
is said to be co–ethic if it
commutes with the coevaluation unit

\epsilon_{A^{\bullet}}:\mathbf{C}^{2}(A^{\bullet})\longrightarrow A^{\bullet},\qquad f\circ\epsilon_{A^{\bullet}}=\epsilon_{B^{\bullet}}\circ\mathbf{C}^{2}(f).

This definition mirrors the ethic condition

\mathbf{D}^{2}(f)\eta=\eta f
but exchanges the roles of evaluation and
coevaluation.
Formally,
(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D})
form an adjoint pair

\mathbf{C}\dashv\mathbf{D}
whose unit and counit correspond to
manifestation and reflection, respectively.

(a)

\mathbf{C}
is exact with respect to distinguished triangles and
preserves coproducts.

(b)

The composition
\mathbf{C}\mathbf{D}
acts as a derived
endomorphism of
D^{+}(\mathcal{E})
expressing the bidual adjunction

(R\!\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}\mathbf{R}\!\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(-,R))
.

(c)

If
R
is flat,
\mathbf{C}
reduces to the ordinary tensor product and
all co–ethic obstructions vanish:
\Tor_{i}^{\mathcal{E}}(R,-)=0
for
i>0
.

Unlike the ethic dual
\mathbf{D}
, which is right derived and
detects failure of exactness through the nonvanishing
\operatorname{Ext}^{k}
,
the co–ethic functor
\mathbf{C}
is left derived and
depends on the flatness of
R
.
For most resource objects
R
used in applications
(integers, cones, Hopf algebras),
R
is not flat,
and the corresponding
\Tor_{k}(R,-)

carry no meaningful new invariants beyond standard homology.
Hence the co–ethic hierarchy

H_{k}\mathbf{C}(A^{\bullet})=\Tor_{k}^{\mathcal{E}}(R,A^{\bullet})

collapses in the same cases where the ethic tower
H^{k}\mathbf{D}
is rich.
In this sense, the co–ethic theory is formally valid but
structurally unproductive:
its obstructions record only the failure of flatness of
R
,
not higher coherence of morphisms.
The ethic dual, being contravariant and right–derived, remains the
informative side of the duality, while co–ethics serves merely as its
tensorial shadow.

3.2
Profunctor Reformulation

Let
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
be abelian categories, and let
K:\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{F}\to\mathsf{Ab}
be an
\mathsf{Ab}
–enriched profunctor, biadditive in each variable. Define the pair of adjoint functors

\mathbf{A}(A)=\int^{E\in\mathcal{E}}K(E,-)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{E}(E,A),\qquad\mathbf{D}(X)=\int_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{F}(X,F)\pitchfork K(-,F),

where
\pitchfork=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{Ab}}(-,-)
and all (co)ends exist under standard smallness hypotheses (cf.
[
48
]
,
[
44
]
).

Lemma 3.1

(Aisthetic adjunction)
.

For every
A\in\mathcal{E}
and
X\in\mathcal{F}
there is a natural isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{A}(A),X)\;\cong\;\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(A,\mathbf{D}(X)).

Proof.

Expanding
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{A}(A),X)
and commuting
\operatorname{Hom}
with the coend gives

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{F}}\!\Big(\!\int^{E}K(E,-)\!\otimes\!\mathcal{E}(E,A),\,X\!\Big)

\displaystyle\cong\;\int_{E}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{F}}(K(E,-)\!\otimes\!\mathcal{E}(E,A),X)

\displaystyle\cong\int_{E}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{Ab}}(\mathcal{E}(E,A),\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{F}}(K(E,-),X)).

Applying the tensor–Hom interchange in
\mathsf{Ab}
and Fubini’s rule for (co)ends (see
[
44
]
) yields

\int_{F}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{Ab}}\!\big(\!\int^{E}\mathcal{E}(E,A)\!\otimes\!K(E,F),\,\mathcal{F}(X,F)\big)\;\cong\;\int_{F}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{Ab}}(K(A,F),\mathcal{F}(X,F)).

By the enriched Yoneda lemma this equals
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(A,\mathbf{D}(X))
, proving the claim.
∎

Corollary 3.2

(Unit and counit)
.

The bijection in Lemma
3.1
is implemented by natural transformations

\eta:\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{E}}\!\Rightarrow\!\mathbf{D}\mathbf{A}
and

\epsilon:\mathbf{A}\mathbf{D}\!\Rightarrow\!\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}}

satisfying the triangle identities, giving
\mathbf{A}\dashv\mathbf{D}
.

Assume that
K
admits a representation on the
\mathcal{E}
–side by the resource object
R
, that is,

K(E,-)\;\cong\;\mathcal{E}(E,R)\otimes_{\Lambda}B(-),\qquad\Lambda:=\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{E}}(R),

(3.1)

for some right
\Lambda

in
\mathcal{F}
(see
[

]
).
Then the aisthetic and dual functors take the explicit forms

\mathbf{A}(A)\;\cong\;B(-)\otimes_{\Lambda}\mathcal{E}(R,A),\qquad\mathbf{D}(X)\;\cong\;\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(B(-),\mathcal{F}(X,-))\circ\mathcal{E}(-,R).

Theorem 3.3

(Derived aisthetic structure)
.

Regard
K
as a bounded complex
K^{\bullet}
and define

\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}(A)=\int^{E}K^{\bullet}(E,-)\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{E}(E,A),\qquad\mathbf{D}^{\bullet}(X)=\int_{F}\mathbf{R}\!\mathcal{F}(X,F)\pitchfork K^{\bullet}(-,F).

There exists a first–quadrant spectral sequence

E_{2}^{p,q}=\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{\mathcal{F}}(H_{q}(K^{\bullet}\otimes A),R)\;\Longrightarrow\;H^{p+q}\mathbf{D}^{\bullet}(A),

and a dual
\Tor
–spectral sequence for
H_{\ast}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}

Proof.

The statement follows from the Grothendieck spectral sequence for the composite of the derived functors
\mathbf{R}\!\operatorname{Hom}
and
\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}
, applied objectwise to

; see
[

.
∎

Theorem 3.4

(Flat–injective regimes)
.

Under representation (
3.1
), if

is flat as a right
\Lambda
–module and

is injective in
\mathcal{E}
, then all higher cohomology and homology vanish:

H^{>0}\mathbf{D}^{\bullet}(X)=0,\qquad H_{>0}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}(A)=0.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
and representation (
3.1
), one has

\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}(A)\simeq B\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{E}(R,A)
and

\mathbf{D}^{\bullet}(X)\simeq\mathbf{R}\!\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(B,\mathcal{F}(X,-))\circ\mathcal{E}(-,R)

Flatness of

Theorem 3.5

(Collapse of Aisthetics)
.

Let representation (
3.1
) hold.
If
R
is not injective in
\mathcal{E}
(so that
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{E}}(-,R)\neq 0
for some object)
and

is not flat as a right
\Lambda

then the derived aisthetic functor coincides with the co–ethic tensor:

\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}(A)\;\simeq\;B(-)\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{E}(R,A),\qquad H_{k}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}(A)\;\cong\;\Tor_{k}^{\Lambda}(B(-),\mathcal{E}(R,A)).

Hence the aisthetic tower
H_{k>0}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}
and the co–ethic tower
coincide on all objects and connecting morphisms, defining the same universal
\delta
–functor.

Proof.

Under (
3.1
) the functor
\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}
is precisely the left–derived tensor
B\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{E}(R,-)
.
If

is not flat,
\Tor_{k}^{\Lambda}(B,-)
is nontrivial; if

is not injective, the ethic dual

\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{E}}^{1}(-,R)\neq 0

For every short exact sequence

in
\mathcal{E}
, the long exact sequence of
\Tor^{\Lambda}
for

gives connecting morphisms identical to those of

, so both families of functors satisfy the same axioms of effaceable

–functors
[

]
.
Therefore
\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}
and the co–ethic tensor are naturally isomorphic, proving the collapse.
∎

(Ethic–aisthetic compatibility)
.

), the induced morphism on

and the resulting diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}(A)}}

The collapse theorem shows that when the resource object

When

3.3

Fix a finite set of coordinates

Theorem 3.10

For every finite Boolean function

Proof.

4
Homotopical Ethics

We now lift the ethic duality condition to the level of homotopy theory.
The purpose of this subsection is to show that the equality

\mathbf{D}^{2}(f)\eta_{A}=\eta_{B}f

is only the first layer of a full hierarchy of higher coherences, and that these coherences form a homotopical structure controlled by a non–abelian central filtration.
The resulting theory extends the homological obstruction tower of Section
2.4
into the realm of stable
\infty
–categories and establishes a precise correspondence between higher ethic coherence and the lower central series of an associated automorphism group.
All homotopical constructions below are understood in the sense of Quillen model categories
[
69
]
or, equivalently, in the framework of stable
\infty
–categories
[
55
]
.

4.1
Central Filtrations

Definition 4.1

(Stable homotopical environment)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an abelian category with enough injectives and

.
Denote by

for the mapping space between

and

in
\Sp(\mathcal{E})

It is a pointed Kan complex satisfying

\pi_{n}\,\Map_{\mathcal{E}}(A,B)\;\cong\;\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathcal{E}}(A,B)\qquad(n\geq 0)

by the classical Whitehead–Quillen comparison between simplicial and derived homotopy groups

[
85
]
,
[
69
]

Hence each mapping space refines the graded
\operatorname{Ext}
–groups by encoding all higher compositions and their coherences.

Definition 4.2

(Space of ethic coherences)

Fix

Definition 4.3

A morphism

Hence:

•

2
–ethic means all such homotopies are themselves coherently connected (no ethic loops);

•

higher

The following lemma shows that this definition is compatible with the derived obstruction theory already developed in the homological setting.

Lemma 4.4

(Homological criterion for
k
–ethicity)

For any

the following are equivalent:

(i)

is
(k{-}1)
–connected;

(ii)

\operatorname{Ext}
–sequences induced by

\leq k

(iii)

(Definition

Proof.

By functoriality of

there is a fiber sequence

S(f)\;\longrightarrow\;\Map_{\mathcal{E}}(A,\mathbf{D}^{2}B)\xrightarrow{(\mathbf{D}^{2}(f))_{\ast}-(f^{\ast})}\Map_{\mathcal{E}}(A,\mathbf{D}^{2}A),

whose long exact sequence on homotopy groups coincides with the long exact sequence of
\operatorname{Ext}
for
\operatorname{Hom}(-,R)

\pi_{i}\Map_{\mathcal{E}}(A,B)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{\mathcal{E}}(A,B)

Hence
\pi_{i}S(f)=0
for

iff all connecting morphisms
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}(B,R)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{i+1}(A,R)

∎

Definition 4.5

(Ethic automorphism group)

Definition 4.6

Following the construction of

Theorem 4.7

(Homotopy–central correspondence)

For every

The mapping space

Differentiating this action gives an action of

\pi_{1}G\curvearrowright\pi_{n+1}\Map_{\mathcal{E}}(A,B)

The inclusion

∎

4.2
Postnikov–Whitehead Tower

The homotopy–coherent duality developed in previous section provides a global description of higher ethic coherences through the mapping spaces

S(f)=\Map_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbf{D}^{2}(f)\!\circ\!\eta_{A},\;\eta_{B}\!\circ\!f).

To obtain a constructive, layerwise description of these coherences, we now introduce the Postnikov–Whitehead tower of
S(f)
.
This tower refines the homological obstruction tower of Section
2.4
, extending it to the full hierarchy of homotopical obstructions inside the stable
\infty
–category
\Sp(\mathcal{E})
.
All references to homotopy theory follow the classical constructions of Whitehead
[
85
]
, Quillen
[
69
]
, and Bousfield–Kan
[
16
]
.

Definition 4.12

(Ethic Postnikov tower)
.

Let
S(f)
be the space of ethic coherences defined above.
A Postnikov tower for
S(f)
is a sequence of morphisms in
\Sp(\mathcal{E})

S(f)\;\longrightarrow\;P_{\leq n}S(f)\;\longrightarrow\;P_{\leq n-1}S(f)\;\longrightarrow\cdots

such that the fiber of
P_{\leq n}S(f)\to P_{\leq n-1}S(f)
is the Eilenberg–Mac Lane object

K(\pi_{n}S(f),n)
.
The groups
\pi_{n}S(f)
are called the ethic homotopy obstructions of
f
.
A morphism
f
is
k
–ethic if
\pi_{i}S(f)=0
for
i<k
.

Lemma 4.13

(Abelian shadow)
.

In the stable
\infty
–category
\Sp(\mathcal{E})
, there are natural identifications

\pi_{1}S(f)\;\cong\;\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{E}}(A,R),\qquad\pi_{2}S(f)\;\longrightarrow\;\operatorname{Ext}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(A,R),

and, in general, canonical morphisms

\pi_{n}S(f)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathcal{E}}(A,R)
for
n>0
compatible with the long exact
\operatorname{Ext}
–sequences.
Thus the homological obstruction tower of Section
2.4

is the abelian projection of the Postnikov filtration of
S(f)
.

Proof.

Since
\pi_{n}\Map_{\mathcal{E}}(A,B)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathcal{E}}(A,B)
, the first equality is immediate.
For
n>1
, the Postnikov truncations
P_{\leq n}S(f)
fit into a fiber sequence

K(\pi_{n+1}S(f),n{+}1)\to S(f)\to P_{\leq n}S(f),

whose associated long exact homotopy sequence coincides with the long exact
\operatorname{Ext}
–sequence obtained from applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(-,R)
to the truncated complex defining
f
.
Compatibility with connecting morphisms follows from the universal property of truncations in
\Sp(\mathcal{E})
.
∎

Theorem 4.14

(Whitehead criterion for ethic contractibility)
.

For each morphism
f:A\to B
in
D^{+}(\mathcal{E})
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

the space
S(f)
is contractible;

(ii)

all Postnikov invariants
k_{i}(S(f))\in H^{i+1}(P_{\leq i-1}S(f);\pi_{i}S(f))
vanish;

(iii)

all homological obstructions
\mathrm{ob}_{i}(A^{\bullet})\in\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{\mathcal{E}}(H^{0}(A^{\bullet}),R)

and their commutators are zero.

Proof.

The equivalence
(i)\Leftrightarrow(ii)
is the classical Whitehead theorem
for simplicial groups
[
85
]
,
[
69
]
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
follows because the abelianization of the Postnikov tower is the homological obstruction tower by Lemma
4.13
.
Conversely, if all
\mathrm{ob}_{i}
vanish but some commutator
[\mathrm{ob}_{i},\mathrm{ob}_{j}]
is nonzero,
the corresponding Postnikov invariant
k_{i+j}
survives, so
S(f)
is not contractible.
∎

Theorem 4.15

(Ethic Postnikov–Whitehead decomposition)
.

Every
S(f)
admits a canonical Postnikov–Whitehead decomposition in
\Sp(\mathcal{E})

whose layers are the abelian groups
\pi_{n}S(f)
and whose connecting morphisms are the Postnikov invariants
k_{n}(S(f))
.
Passing to
\pi_{1}
and abelianizing this tower reproduces the ethic obstruction tower of Section
2.4
.

Proof.

This is the standard Postnikov construction in a stable
\infty
–category
[
55
]
.
Because
\Sp(\mathcal{E})
is stable, each truncation
P_{\leq n}
exists and the successive fibers are Eilenberg–Mac Lane objects

.
Abelianization by
\pi_{1}
recovers the chain of
\operatorname{Ext}^{n}
–groups, giving the desired identification.
∎

Theorem 4.16

(Comparison with the central filtration)
.

Let
G=\Aut_{\eta}(\mathrm{Id})
be the ethic automorphism group of

with lower central series

For each

In particular, the vanishing of all

Proof.

The action of

induces a crossed module

.
By the construction of

Functoriality of the Postnikov invariants under group actions

∎

Theorem 4.17

(Truncation and stabilization)
.

If the canonical map

then the ethic obstruction
\mathrm{ob}_{k+1}(A^{\bullet})

and all Postnikov invariants

Hence

for

The vanishing of

Theorem 4.18

(Postnikov depth and stabilization length)
.

For each

define

(Morita invariance of Postnikov data)

(Completeness of the homotopical heart)

The triple of structures

4.3

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an abelian category with enough injective objects, and let

Let

be the automorphism group of the identity functor of

\gamma_{1}(G)=G,\ \gamma_{i+1}(G)=[G,\gamma_{i}(G)]

Fix also a decreasing sequence of ideals of

each

For a morphism

We shall relate the behaviour of the derived duals of

Definition 4.21
.

For each

and the central filtration

on

Lemma 4.22
.

For each
n
there is a canonical morphism of spectral sequences

\Phi_{n}:E^{p,q}_{r}(I)\longrightarrow F^{p,q}_{r}(\gamma)

natural in

and in morphisms of pairs
(I^{\bullet},\gamma_{\bullet})
, and compatible with the differentials.

Proof.

Each ideal

is

\mathcal{E}/I^{n}\to\mathcal{E}/\gamma_{n}(G)
induces a morphism of exact functors on

and thus by functoriality of derived functors a morphism of their Grothendieck spectral sequences. The differentials commute because the connecting morphisms in the long exact
\operatorname{Ext}

∎

Lemma 4.23
.

Lemma 4.24

Related Works

This subsection surveys several influential reformulations or closely related frameworks for the
P
vs.
NP
question and positions our ethic/homological formulation against them. We focus on (i) scope and exactness (whether the reformulation is strictly equivalent to classical
P{=}NP
or an analogue), (ii) the mathematical language and tools employed, (iii) the operative notion of “resource,” and (iv) known strengths/limitations and barriers. References are provided for canonical statements and proofs.

Descriptive complexity.
Fagin’s theorem identifies
NP
with existential second–order (ESO) logic on finite structures
[
24
]
, while the Immerman–Vardi theorem identifies
P
with first–order logic with a least fixed–point operator (
FO({\rm LFP})
) over ordered structures
[
82
,
36
,
37
]
. Thus, on ordered finite structures,

P=FO({\rm LFP}),\qquad NP=\mathrm{ESO},

and the equality
P{=}NP
becomes an equality of logical expressiveness,
FO({\rm LFP})=\mathrm{ESO}
, in that setting.
Scope.
This is a strict equivalence under the ordering hypothesis.
Tools.
Model theory on finite structures, fixed–point logics.
Resource.
Inductive depth/arity and second–order quantification rather than time per se.
Comparison.
Our formulation is likewise machine–independent and replaces time by an intrinsic invariant; however, we use homological vanishing (
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}=0
after polynomial internal depth) within an abelian/derived setup, whereas descriptive complexity encodes complexity classes as logical fragments. The two perspectives are complementary: both transmute
P{=}NP
into an equality of internal expressive powers (logical vs. derived–functorial).

Karchmer–Wigderson depth duality.
For any Boolean
f
, the minimum formula depth equals the depth of a deterministic Karchmer–Wigderson communication game for
f

[
41
]
.
Scope.
This is an exact duality for depth, not a direct reformulation of
P
vs.
NP
.
Tools.
Communication complexity and formula complexity.
Resource.
Formula/decomposition depth.
Comparison.
In our setting this duality is internalized (ethic internal depth
=
formula/KW depth). It supplies the depth anchor that we then combine with
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
–vanishing and uniform compilation to tie back to
P
.

Proof complexity.
Cook–Reckhow formalized propositional proof systems and related
NP
vs.
coNP
to the existence of polynomially bounded proof systems for TAUT
[
20
]
. In particular,
NP=coNP
iff some p-bounded system for TAUT exists.
Scope.
This yields an exact reformulation for
NP
vs.
coNP
, not directly for
P
vs.
NP
.
Tools.
Propositional proof systems, interpolation, lower bounds; key barriers include Natural Proofs
[
71
]
.
Comparison.
Both proof complexity and our approach operationalize “feasible derivability”; ours expresses feasibility as functorial exactness (
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}=0
) after polynomial internal depth, while proof complexity measures the existence/length of syntactic derivations. Our framework may circumvent some “naturalness” issues since the homological property is not a dense combinatorial predicate over truth tables.

Algebraic complexity and GCT.
Valiant introduced algebraic classes VP and VNP and the permanent vs. determinant paradigm
[
81
]
. The Geometric Complexity Theory (GCT) program of Mulmuley–Sohoni proposes separating these classes via representation–theoretic obstructions over orbit closures
[
60
,
61
]
.
Scope.
These are analogues (algebraic shadows) rather than strict reformulations of Boolean
P
vs.
NP
.
Tools.
Algebraic geometry, invariant theory.
Resource.
Algebraic circuit size/degree, orbit–closure singularities.
Comparison.
Like GCT, our approach relocates complexity into a rich mathematical environment; unlike GCT, our equivalence to Turing
P{=}NP
is proved (via uniformity and compilation assumptions satisfied by the bar/cobar model), while GCT pursues lower bounds in the algebraic setting with independent obstacles.

Circuit/Uniformity characterizations of
P
.
The standard depth–time equivalence asserts that
L\in P
iff
L
has a logspace–uniform family of polynomial–size, polynomial–depth circuits
[
78
,
3
,
65
]
.
Scope.
Exact characterization of
P
.
Resource.
Size and depth of uniform circuits.
Comparison.
These results supply the external bridges used in our equivalence: once ethic depth is polynomial and
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
vanishes at that depth, our compilation yields poly–size, poly–depth uniform circuits, hence membership in
P
.

Our ethic reformulation is closest in spirit to descriptive complexity (both provide machine–independent equalities). It also internalizes the KW depth duality as the intrinsic internal depth and augments it with a derived exactness criterion (
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}=0
) standing in for feasible reconstruction. The circuit characterizations provide the uniform, size/depth bridges back to Turing computation, thereby yielding a strict equivalence with classical
P{=}NP
under the admissible model hypotheses. Compared to GCT and proof complexity, our statement is equivalence, not analogy, and it leverages categorical/derived methods (t-structures, truncations, spectral sequence collapse) where the central obstruction is functorial and geometric rather than syntactic. Finally, known meta–barriers (relativization, algebrization, natural proofs) motivate seeking such non-syntactic invariants; our homological criterion offers exactly that, at the price of developing and verifying admissible uniform models, which we have done constructively in the bar/cobar setting.

6.1.2
Galois Descent

This application demonstrates that the categorical formalism of ethic duality extends to arithmetic and discrete settings, where the ambient category consists of modules over an integral group ring and the homological invariants acquire the interpretation of arithmetic obstructions to Galois descent. In particular,
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
identifies with group cohomology
H^{1}(\Gamma,-)
and measures the failure of global coherence of
\Gamma
–invariant data along towers of normal coverings. The underlying picture connects Bass–Serre theory of groups acting on trees, finite Galois coverings of graphs, and the ethic criterion

Let

Write

with trivial
\Gamma

All

\mathbb{D}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(-,R)
.

(Arithmetic resource category)
.

whose objects are integral
\Gamma

\Gamma

with trivial
\Gamma

Definition 6.23

The unit

Let

be the cellular chain complex of a triangulation of

Define the Morse filtration

For every attachment

Self-adjointness gives

coincides with its double dual

on each chain group.

We now develop the categorical formulation of scattering as an aisthetic–ethic pair and show how the classical Born approximation and the Lippmann–Schwinger equation arise from the same universal aisthetic law. Throughout,

Their homology and cohomology groups,

H_{n}(\mathbf{A}_{W})=\ker\partial_{n}/\mathrm{im}\,\partial_{n+1},\qquad H^{n}(\mathbf{D}_{Q})=\ker\delta^{n}/\mathrm{im}\,\delta^{n-1},

measure successive degrees of non–idempotence of the channel and its reconstruction.

For measurable
f\in A_{n}
,
g\in D^{n}
, define

\langle f,g\rangle=\int f(x_{1:n},y)\,g(x_{1:n},y)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{dW(\,\cdot\,|x_{i})}{dQ(\,\cdot\,|x_{i})}(y)\,P_{X}^{\otimes n}(dx_{1:n})P_{Y}(dy),

whenever the Radon–Nikodym derivatives exist in
L^{\alpha}(P_{Y})
for some
\alpha>1
.
Then
\partial
and
\delta
are formally adjoint up to a defect operator

\mathfrak{o}_{n}:=\partial\delta+\delta\partial
depending on the divergence between
W
and
Q
.

For a block code of length
n
and rate
R
, the average error probability satisfies

P_{e}^{(n)}\;\geq\;\|\mathfrak{o}_{n}\|,

where
\|\cdot\|
is the operator norm in the Bochner
L^{2}
–space; this follows from variational representation of Bayesian error and the Neyman–Pearson lemma
[
66
]
.
The norm
\|\mathfrak{o}_{n}\|
equals zero iff
\mathbf{A}_{W}
and
\mathbf{D}_{Q}
are perfectly adjoint, i.e. the model
Q
matches the true channel
W
.

Theorem 6.61

(Strong sphere–packing as aisthetic–ethic divergence)
.

Let
(X,Y)
be standard Borel spaces and
W:X\rightsquigarrow Y
a channel with finite input alphabet and measurable kernel.
Then there exists
\gamma>0
depending only on
W
such that for any sequence of
(n,R_{n})
–codes with
\liminf R_{n}>C_{\mathrm{sp}}(W)
one has

\liminf_{n\to\infty}P_{e}^{(n)}\;\geq\;1-e^{-\gamma n}.

Equivalently, if the block rate exceeds the sphere–packing threshold, the chain complex
\mathbf{A}_{\bullet}
retains nonzero homology of order
n
with exponential weight
\|\mathfrak{o}_{n}\|\approx e^{-nE_{\mathrm{sp}}(R)}
.

Proof.

By the variational formula for error exponents
[
22
]
the average error satisfies

P_{e}^{(n)}\geq\inf_{Q_{Y}}\exp\big(-nE_{\mathrm{sp}}(R;W,Q_{Y})\big),

where
E_{\mathrm{sp}}
is the sphere–packing exponent.
The same functional appears as the
L^{2}
–norm of
\mathfrak{o}_{n}
computed from the comparison pairing, using Hölder–Orlicz inequalities and the exponential tilt
dW_{\lambda}(y|x)\propto e^{\lambda i(x;y)}dW(y|x)
,
i(x;y)=\log\frac{dW(\cdot|x)}{dQ_{Y}}
.
Non–vanishing of
H_{n}(\mathbf{A}_{W})
with weight
e^{-nE_{\mathrm{sp}}}
corresponds to persistence of error; if
R>C_{\mathrm{sp}}
, these classes remain nonzero for infinitely many
n
, forcing
\liminf P_{e}^{(n)}\geq 1-e^{-\gamma n}
.
See
[
22
]
and
[
27
]
for analytic details.
∎

Theorem 6.62

(Mismatch capacity as cohomological limit)
.

Let
W:X\rightsquigarrow Y
and fix an ethic decoder kernel
Q:Y\rightsquigarrow X
.
Define the generalized mutual information

I_{Q}(X;Y)=\sup_{\lambda>0}\frac{1}{\lambda}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\log\mathbb{E}\!\left(e^{\lambda\,\imath_{Q}(X;Y)}\bigm|X\right)\right],\qquad\imath_{Q}(x;y)=\log\frac{dW(\,\cdot\,|x)}{d\bar{Q}(\,\cdot\,)}(y),

where
\bar{Q}
is any marginal of
Q
on
Y
.
Then the supremum achievable rate with vanishing error under decoder
Q
satisfies

C_{\mathrm{mm}}(W,Q)=\sup_{P_{X}}I_{Q}(X;Y),

and for
R>C_{\mathrm{mm}}(W,Q)
there exists
\eta>0
such that
\liminf_{n}P_{e}^{(n)}\geq 1-e^{-\eta n}
.
Moreover,
R\leq C_{\mathrm{mm}}(W,Q)
iff
\limsup_{n}\frac{1}{n}\log\|H^{1}(\mathbf{D}_{Q}^{(n)})\|\leq 0
.

Proof.

The expression for
C_{\mathrm{mm}}(W,Q)
is the classical GMI formula
[
49
]
,
[
57
]
.
Within the present framework,
\|H^{1}(\mathbf{D}_{Q}^{(n)})\|
measures the residual cohomology of the decoder cobar complex: by the comparison pairing
\|H^{1}\|=\|\mathfrak{o}_{n}\|
.
For
R>C_{\mathrm{mm}}
the exponent of
\|\mathfrak{o}_{n}\|
is strictly positive, giving
\|\mathfrak{o}_{n}\|\geq e^{-\eta n}
and hence the strong converse.
If
R<C_{\mathrm{mm}}
, exponential decay of
\|\mathfrak{o}_{n}\|
implies vanishing
H^{1}
and reliable decoding.
This reproduces the mismatched capacity theorem and its strong converse
[
57
]
,
[
22
]
.
∎

The two theorems express the major limits of coding theory —sphere packing and mismatch capacity — as the persistence of homological or cohomological classes arising from the divergence between the forward (aisthetic) and backward (ethic) kernels.
When
Q=W
, the comparison defect
\mathfrak{o}_{\bullet}
vanishes, the complexes become dual, and the classical Shannon theorem on capacity is recovered
[
27
]
,
[
22
]
.
In general, the non-adjoint pair
(\mathbf{A}_{W},\mathbf{D}_{Q})
quantifies loss of coherence between encoding and decoding; its homological magnitude yields the exponential error exponents known from analytic information theory, but here derived categorically from the structure of measures, kernels, and co/ends.

6.4.5
Bellman Duality

Fix an abelian category
\mathcal{E}
with enough injectives and a distinguished resource object
R\in\mathcal{E}
. Let
\mathbb{D}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(-,R)
and
\mathbf{D}=\mathbf{R}\!\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(-,R)
with unit
\eta:\mathrm{Id}\Rightarrow\mathbb{D}^{2}
. Throughout this subsection the ambient probabilistic or combinatorial data of reinforcement learning is encoded in
\mathcal{E}
; theorems below are purely categorical first, and afterward admit a standard analytical reduction to classical Bellman theory
[
8
,
9
,
68
]
.

Definition 6.63

(Categorical MDP datum)
.

A categorical Markov decision datum is a triple
(S,P,r)
inside
\mathcal{E}
where
S\in\mathcal{E}
is the state object,
P:S\to S
is an action–transition endomorphism (one may think of a Markov kernel or policy–transition operator pushed through a chosen linearization), and
r:S\to R
is a reward morphism. A discount is a scalar
\gamma\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{E}}(R)
with
\|\gamma\|<1
in the analytic reduction; categorically we only require that
\gamma
acts centrally on morphism groups. Define the Bellman morphism
B_{(P,r,\gamma)}:S\to\mathbb{D}(S)
by

B\;:=\;r\;\oplus\;\gamma\cdot\mathbb{D}(P)\circ\mathrm{coev}_{S},

where
\mathrm{coev}_{S}:R\to S\oplus\mathbb{D}(S)
is the canonical coevaluation determined by
\mathbb{D}
(in
D^{+}(\mathcal{E})
this is the unit of the
\mathbf{C}\dashv\mathbf{D}
adjunction when it exists, or the image of
\eta
under the canonical identification
\mathbb{D}(S)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(S,R)
).

The Bellman morphism packages the usual affine operator
v\mapsto r+\gamma Pv
as a single arrow
S\to\mathbb{D}(S)
; it acts covariantly on
r
and contravariantly on
P
via
\mathbb{D}
.

Definition 6.64

(Ethic value object)
.

An object
V\in\mathcal{E}
equipped with a morphism
u:S\to V
is a value presentation for
(S,P,r)
if there exists a morphism
v:V\to R
such that
B
factors as
S\xrightarrow{u}V\xrightarrow{v}R
, i.e.
B=v\circ u
. The pair
(V,u)
is ethicly exact for
(S,P,r)
if the ethic Bellman square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{D}(V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{D}^{2}(S)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{D}^{2}(\mathbb{D}(S))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta_{S}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{D}^{2}(u)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{D}(B)}

commutes in
\mathcal{E}
(or in
D^{+}(\mathcal{E})
after applying a fixed injective resolution).

The square states that evaluating
S
into its double dual via
\eta_{S}
and then reflecting
B
agrees with pushing
S
into
\mathbb{D}(V)
and reflecting twice along
u
. This is the categorical shadow of the Bellman fixed point.

Lemma 6.65

(Ethic Bellman identity)
.

If
(V,u)
is ethicly exact for
(S,P,r)
, then there exists a unique
v:V\to R
such that

\mathbb{D}^{2}(u)\circ\eta_{S}\;=\;\mathbb{D}(v\circ u)\circ\eta_{S}\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad u\;=\;\mathbb{D}(v)\circ\eta_{S}

on the ethic heart of
(\mathcal{E},\mathbb{D})
(i.e. after inverting
\eta
on reflexive objects). Equivalently,
u
is a fixed point of the affine endomorphism
X\mapsto\mathbb{D}(v)\circ\eta_{S}
.

Proof.

Commutativity of the ethic Bellman square gives
\mathbb{D}^{2}(u)\eta_{S}=\mathbb{D}(B)\eta_{S}=\mathbb{D}(v\circ u)\eta_{S}
. On the reflexive subcategory
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{ref}}
the unit
\eta
is an isomorphism, hence
\mathbb{D}^{2}(u)=\mathbb{D}(v\circ u)
. Applying
\mathbb{D}
and using the bidual identification yields
u=\mathbb{D}(v)\circ\eta_{S}
. Uniqueness of
v
follows from the Yoneda identification
\operatorname{Hom}(V,R)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{D}(V),\mathbb{D}(R))
and the faithfulness of
\mathbb{D}
on
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{ref}}
.
∎

Theorem 6.66

(Categorical Bellman equation)
.

Let
(S,P,r)
be a categorical MDP datum. Suppose there exists a value presentation
(V,u)
ethicly exact for
(S,P,r)
. Then there is a unique
v\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(V,R)
such that

u\;=\;\mathbb{D}(r)\circ\eta_{S}\;+\;\gamma\cdot\mathbb{D}(P)\circ u,

i.e.
u
solves the categorical Bellman fixed point equation in
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(S,\mathbb{D}(S))
. Conversely, any
u
satisfying this equation makes
(V,u)
ethicly exact.

Proof.

From Lemma
6.65
we have
u=\mathbb{D}(v)\circ\eta_{S}
. The factorization
B=v\circ u
gives, by definition of
B
,

\mathbb{D}(v)\circ\eta_{S}\;=\;\mathbb{D}(r)\circ\eta_{S}+\gamma\cdot\mathbb{D}(P)\circ\mathbb{D}(v)\circ\eta_{S},

which is the displayed identity. Conversely, if
u
satisfies that identity, then
\mathbb{D}^{2}(u)\eta_{S}=\mathbb{D}(B)\eta_{S}
by functoriality and naturality of
\eta
, so the ethic square commutes.
∎

(Finite-step convergence under nilpotent control)

Remark 6.73

(Comparison with facial reduction and classical RL)

The nilpotent control Theorem

provides a finite-step termination certificate driven by a structural lower central filtration; this is not available in standard facial reduction or in classical Bellman theory, which provide feasibility and contraction but not functorial finite-step stabilization. The obstruction calculus à la Lemma

6.4.6
Sketching

We consider a compact metric space
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a nested sequence of
\varepsilon
–nets

\{S_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
with radii
\varepsilon_{n+1}<\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
.
Each
S_{n}
yields a discrete linear sketch represented by a matrix

A_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r_{n}\times m_{n}}
acting on parameter vectors
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{m_{n}}
.
The ethic module of level
n
is defined by

X_{n}=\operatorname{coker}(A_{n}:\mathbb{Z}^{m_{n}}\!\to\!\mathbb{Z}^{r_{n}}),\qquad G_{n}=I^{n}X/I^{n+1}X\simeq\ker(A_{n+1}\!\to\!A_{n}),

so that
0\to G_{n}\to X_{n+1}\to X_{n}\to 0
is exact.
The ethic dual functor
\mathbf{D}=\mathbf{R}\!\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(-,\mathbb{Z})
produces cohomology groups

H^{k}\mathbf{D}(X_{n})=\operatorname{Ext}^{k}_{\mathbb{Z}}(X_{n},\mathbb{Z})
.
The torsion size

|\operatorname{tors}\,\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(X_{n},\mathbb{Z})|=\det_{\text{SNF}}(A_{n})

quantifies the residual incoherence of the sketch at resolution
\varepsilon_{n}
;
its logarithm
\mathrm{Seth}_{n}=\log|\operatorname{tors}\,\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(X_{n},\mathbb{Z})|
is the ethic entropy.

Lemma 6.75

(Layer control)
.

For each
n
the short exact sequence

0\to G_{n}\to X_{n+1}\to X_{n}\to 0

induces the exact fragment

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(X_{n},\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\alpha_{n}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(X_{n+1},\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(G_{n},\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow 0.

Hence

|\operatorname{tors}\,\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(X_{n+1})|\leq|\operatorname{tors}\,\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(X_{n})|\,|\operatorname{tors}\,\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(G_{n})|.

Proof.

Applying the functor
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(-,\mathbb{Z})
to the sequence and passing to derived functors
yields the long exact sequence

\cdots\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(X_{n})\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(X_{n+1})\to\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(G_{n})\to 0
.
Taking torsion subgroups preserves exactness, whence the inequality
for their cardinalities.
∎

Lemma 6.76

(Matrix estimate for the graded layer)
.

Assume that each
A_{n}
is
w
–sparse and that the update

\Delta A_{n}=A_{n+1}\!\restriction_{S_{n}}-A_{n}

satisfies the columnwise Lipschitz bound

\|\Delta A_{n}\|_{\mathrm{col}}\leq L\,\varepsilon_{n}^{\alpha}
for some

and
\alpha>0
.
Let

be the presentation matrix of

consisting of at most

new rows, each supported on at most

Then

\log|\operatorname{tors}\,\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(G_{n},\mathbb{Z})|\leq r^{\prime}_{n}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\log w+\log(B_{0}+L\,\varepsilon_{n}^{\alpha})\right),

where
B_{0}=\max\|b^{(j)}_{\mathrm{base}}\|_{\infty}
is the maximal absolute base coefficient.

Proof.

The group
\operatorname{tors}\,\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(G_{n},\mathbb{Z})\cong\operatorname{tors}\,\coker(B_{n})
.
For any square submatrix
M
of
B_{n}
, the Smith normal form gives

|\operatorname{tors}\,\coker(B_{n})|\leq\max|\det M|
.
By the Hadamard inequality (
[
33
]
),

|\det M|\leq\prod_{j}\|b^{(j)}\|_{2}\leq(\sqrt{w}\,\|b^{(j)}\|_{\infty})^{r^{\prime}_{n}}
.
The bound on
\|b^{(j)}\|_{\infty}
from the Lipschitz condition
gives the required estimate.
∎

Theorem 6.77

(Fine–grained
\varepsilon
–ethic coherence)
.

Under the hypotheses of Lemmas
6.75
–

,

Consequently
\sum_{n}|\mathrm{Seth}_{n+1}-\mathrm{Seth}_{n}|<\infty
whenever

Proof.

Combine Lemma
6.75

\mathrm{Seth}_{n+1}-\mathrm{Seth}_{n}\leq\log|\operatorname{tors}\,\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(G_{n})|\leq r^{\prime}_{n}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\log w+\log(B_{0}+L\varepsilon_{n}^{\alpha})\right).

to obtain the stated inequality.
Absolute summability follows from the convergence of the weighted series
in the right–hand side.

Corollary 6.78

(Ethic–geometric rate law)
.

Let

with

Discussion

The results of this work provide a unified homological formulation of primal–dual
phenomena in abelian and derived environments. The central structural ingredient is
the contravariant functor

D=\operatorname{Hom}_{E}(-,R),

together with its canonical unit
\eta:\mathrm{Id}\Rightarrow D^{2}
.
An ethic morphism, defined by the commutation condition

D^{2}(f)\,\eta=\eta\,f
, isolates the precise locus at which primal and dual data
agree. Passing to the derived category yields the functor

D=\mathrm{RHom}_{E}(-,R)
, whose cohomology groups

H_{k}^{D}(A)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{k}_{E}(A,R)

form a graded hierarchy of deviations from dual self-consistency. In this view,
primal–dual gaps correspond exactly to
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
, while higher

\operatorname{Ext}^{k}
measure deeper obstructions such as multi-layer memory or
instability. The associated obstruction tower records all failures of dual
agreement and stabilizes precisely when all higher derived obstructions vanish.
The induced ethic
t
-structure identifies a heart of objects recovered perfectly
from their duals; stabilization of the tower is equivalent to membership in this
heart.

A key feature of the framework is Morita invariance. Since all constructions depend
only on the derived dual pair
(D^{+}(E),\mathrm{RHom}_{E}(-,R))
, ethic invariants are
preserved under derived equivalences. This implies that primal–dual laws expressed
in this language do not depend on the specific substrate —arithmetical,
combinatorial, geometric, or semantic —but only on the ambient dualizing object
and its functorial behaviour.

It is instructive to contrast this framework with other general notions of
duality. Classical adjunctions describe pairs of functors but do not quantify
the failure of reflexivity or provide graded obstructions. Monoidal duals
(ev/coev-pairs in compact closed or
*
-autonomous categories) encode an intrinsic
algebraic symmetry but do not attach homological invariants to violations of
coherence. Grothendieck–Verdier and Serre dualities supply powerful geometric
duals, yet their obstruction theories are tied to sheaf-theoretic or cohomological
contexts and are not substrate-independent. Convex duality via Fenchel
conjugation provides primal–dual correspondences but lacks a derived tower
measuring the stratified failure of exactness. In contrast, ethic duality is
purely algebraic, functorial, and universal: the obstruction hierarchy

\{\operatorname{Ext}^{k}_{E}(-,R)\}
provides a canonical quantitative spectrum of
primal–dual inconsistency valid in any abelian category with a dualizing object.

This homological standpoint clarifies diverse duality phenomena. In linear and
conic optimization, strong duality appears exactly as the vanishing of

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
, and algebraic duality gaps coincide with torsion in these
groups. In discrete settings such as graph geometry, Kirchhoff- and
Baker–Norine-type identities manifest as instances of ethic exactness. In
dynamical systems, the derived tower furnishes a quantitative model of persistence
and decay of memory, with
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
representing the minimal
obstruction to dual reconstruction and higher layers encoding long-range effects.

The framework also extends to complexity-theoretic contexts. The homological
translation of Karchmer–Wigderson depth interprets bounded-depth reconciliation of
primal and dual data as the eventual disappearance of low-degree obstructions. Under
standard uniformity assumptions, this yields a categorical restatement of
P
vs

NP
in terms of vanishing of the first derived obstruction.

The applications presented in Section 6 illustrate only the representational
breadth of the framework rather than distinct mechanisms. Across optimization,
arithmetic, graph theory, time–memory models, and semantics, a single principle
governs all examples: primal–dual exactness is equivalent to the disappearance of

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
, and all higher-order effects correspond to the successive
layers of the derived obstruction tower. These observations suggest several natural
directions for future work, including
\infty
-categorical refinements, interactions
with monoidal and
*
-autonomous dualities, and computational uses of homological
certificates.

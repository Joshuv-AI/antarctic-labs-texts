Factorizability of optimal quantum sequence discrimination under maximum-confidence measurements
Donghoon Ha, Jeong San Kim

Abstract
We consider the discrimination of quantum sequences under maximum-confidence measurements and show that the optimal discrimination of a quantum sequence ensemble can always be factorized into that of each individual ensemble. In other words, the optimal quantum sequence discrimination under maximum-confidence measurements can be achieved just by performing a maximum-confidence discrimination independently at each step of the quantum sequence. We also show that the maximum confidence of identifying a quantum sequence is to achieve the maximum confidence of identifying each state comprising the quantum sequence. We further provide a necessary and sufficient condition for the optimal quantum state discrimination under maximum-confidence measurements.



I
Introduction

Quantum state discrimination is one of the fundamental concepts used in various quantum information processing tasks
chef2000
;
berg2007
;
barn20091
;
bae2015
.
In general, we can always discriminate orthogonal quantum states using an appropriate measurement.
On the other hand, no measurement can perfectly discriminate nonorthogonal quantum states.
For these reasons, various state discrimination strategies have been developed to optimally discriminate nonorthogonal quantum states, such as minimum-error discrimination, unambiguous discrimination and maximum-confidence discrimination
hels1969
;
ivan1987
;
diek1988
;
pere1988
;
crok2006
.

When a discrimination process involves multiple quantum state ensembles, the situation becomes significantly more intricate, as it requires identifying not just a single quantum state but a sequence of states where each state is independently prepared from a quantum state ensemble.
Quantum sequence discrimination naturally appears in various quantum information tasks, including multiple-copy state discrimination and quantum change-point detection
higg2011
;
sent2016
;
sent2017
.
Thus, a deeper understanding of quantum sequence discrimination provides valuable insights into the general principles of quantum information processing.

The optimal discrimination of a quantum sequence ensemble can be factorized into that of each individual ensemble.
In other words, the optimal discrimination of quantum sequences can be realized just by performing a quantum state discrimination independently at each step of the quantum sequence.
This factorizability always holds in minimum-error discrimination and optimal unambiguous discrimination
gupt20241
;
gupt20242
.
Recently, a factorizability condition was established in the optimal discrimination of multi-party quantum sequences under local operations and classical communication
ha2025
.

The factorizability of quantum sequence discrimination characterizes the fundamental limits of quantum information processing tasks such as quantum cryptography, quantum teleportation and quantum data hiding
benn1986
;
benn1993
;
divi2002
.
When a quantum information processing task based on quantum state discrimination is independently repeated multiple times, the factorizability inherent in the underlying discrimination strategy implies that quantum memory or collective measurements offer no advantage.
As there exist quantum information processing tasks based on maximum-confidence discrimination
benn1992
;
neve2012
, it is a natural question to ask whether factorizability still holds in the optimal quantum sequence discrimination under maximum-confidence measurements.

Here, we provide an answer to the question by showing that the optimal discrimination of a quantum sequence ensemble under maximum-confidence measurements can always be factorized into that of each individual ensemble.
In other words, the optimal quantum sequence discrimination under maximum-confidence measurements can be achieved just by performing a maximum-confidence discrimination independently at each step of the quantum sequence.
We also show that the maximum confidence of identifying a quantum sequence is to achieve the maximum confidence of identifying each state in the quantum sequence.
Moreover, we establish a necessary and sufficient condition for the optimal quantum state discrimination under maximum-confidence measurements.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Sec.
II.1
, we review the definitions and properties of the confidence of measurement in quantum state discrimination.
In Sec.
II.2
, we provide a necessary and sufficient condition for the optimal quantum state discrimination under maximum-confidence measurements.
In Sec.
III.1
, we provide some properties of the confidence of measurement in quantum sequence discrimination, and show that the maximum confidence of identifying a quantum sequence is to achieve the maximum confidence of identifying each state comprising the quantum sequence.
In Sec.
III.2
, we show that the optimal discrimination of a quantum sequence ensemble under maximum-confidence measurements can always be factorized into that of each individual ensemble.
In Sec.
IV
, we summarize our results, discuss a useful application, and suggest a direction for future research.

II
Maximum-confidence discrimination of quantum states

For a finite-dimensional complex Hilbert
\mathcal{H}
, let
\mathbb{H}
be the set of all Hermitian operators acting on
\mathcal{H}
.
We also denote by
\mathbb{H}_{+}
the set of all positive-semidefinite operators in
\mathbb{H}
, that is,

\mathbb{H}_{+}=\{E\in\mathbb{H}\,|\,\bra{v}E\ket{v}\geqslant 0~\mbox{for all}~\ket{v}\in\mathcal{H}\}.

(1)

A quantum state is described by a density operator
\rho
, that is,
\rho\in\mathbb{H}_{+}
with
\Tr\rho=1
.
A measurement is expressed by a positive operator-valued measure
\{M_{i}\}_{i}
, that is,
\{M_{i}\}_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{H}_{+}
with
\sum_{i}M_{i}=\mathbbm{1}
where
\mathbbm{1}
is the identity operator in
\mathbb{H}
.
For a quantum state
\rho
, performing a measurement
\{M_{i}\}_{i}
gives the measurement outcome corresponding to
M_{i}
with probability
\Tr(\rho M_{i})
.

II.1
Quantum state discrimination and confidence of measurement

Let us consider the situation of discriminating
n
quantum states
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{n}
where the state
\rho_{i}
is prepared with the
nonzero
probability
\eta_{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,n
.
We denote this situation as an ensemble

\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}}

(2)

where
\mathbb{N}_{n}
is the set of all integers from
1
to
n
, that is,

\mathbb{N}_{n}=\{1,\ldots,n\}.

(3)

The state of a quantum system prepared from
\mathcal{E}
is denoted by
\rho_{0}
, that is,

\rho_{0}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}}\eta_{i}\rho_{i}.

(4)

To guess the prepared state from the ensemble
\mathcal{E}
in Eq. (
2
), we apply the decision rule using a measurement

\mathcal{M}=\{M_{?}\}\cup\{M_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}}.

(5)

For each
i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}
, obtaining the measurement outcome corresponding to
M_{i}
leads us to guess the prepared state as
\rho_{i}
.
On the other hand, the measurement outcome corresponding to
M_{?}
is inconclusive: “I don’t know what state is prepared.”
In this case, the average probabilities of correctly guessing the prepared state is

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}}\eta_{i}\Tr(\rho_{i}M_{i}).

(6)

Now, we introduce the confidence of a measurement in quantum state discrimination.

Definition 1
.

For an ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}}
, a measurement
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{?}\}\cup\{M_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}}
and
x\in\mathbb{N}_{n}
, the
confidence
of
\mathcal{M}
to identify
\rho_{x}
is the conditional probability

\Pr(\rho_{x}|M_{x})=\frac{\eta_{x}\Tr(\rho_{x}M_{x})}{\Tr(\rho_{0}M_{x})},

(7)

which is well defined only when

\Tr(\rho_{0}M_{x})>0.

(8)

The
maximum confidence
of measurements to identify
\rho_{x}
is

\mathcal{C}_{x}(\mathcal{E})=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{M}~\mathsf{with}\\
\Tr(\rho_{0}M_{x})>0\end{subarray}}\Pr(\rho_{x}|M_{x}),

(9)

where the maximum is taken over all possible measurements
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{?}\}\cup\{M_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}}
with Eq. (
8
).

Here, we note that the confidence
\Pr(\rho_{x}|M_{x})
in Eq. (
7
) represents the probability that the prepared state is
\rho_{x}
given that the measurement outcome corresponding to
M_{x}
is obtained.
We also note that the maximum confidence
\mathcal{C}_{x}(\mathcal{E})
in Eq. (
9
) is known to be the largest eigenvalue of
\sqrt{\rho_{0}}^{-1}\eta_{x}\rho_{x}\sqrt{\rho_{0}}^{-1}
where
\sqrt{\rho_{0}}
is the positive square root of
\rho_{0}
and
\sqrt{\rho_{0}}^{-1}
is the inverse of
\sqrt{\rho_{0}}
on its support
crok2006
;
supp
.
From this fact, we can easily see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{x}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\{q\in\mathbb{R}\,|\,q\mathbbm{1}-\sqrt{\rho_{0}}^{-1}\eta_{x}\rho_{x}\sqrt{\rho_{0}}^{-1}\in\mathbb{H}_{+}\}

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\{q\in\mathbb{R}\,|\,q\rho_{0}-\eta_{x}\rho_{x}\in\mathbb{H}_{+}\}

where
\mathbb{R}
is the set of all real numbers.

For an ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}}
, the following lemma shows that the maximum confidence
\mathcal{C}_{i}(\mathcal{E})
is bounded below by the nonzero probability
\eta_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}
.

Lemma 1
.

For an ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}}
and
x\in\mathbb{N}_{n}
, we have

\mathcal{C}_{x}(\mathcal{E})=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}E\in\mathbb{H}_{+}\\
\Tr(\rho_{0}E)=1\end{subarray}}\eta_{x}\Tr(\rho_{x}E)\geqslant\eta_{x},

(11)

where
\rho_{0}
is defined in Eq. (
4
) and the maximum is taken over all possible
E\in\mathbb{H}_{+}
with
\Tr(\rho_{0}E)=1
.

Proof.

For a measurement
\{M_{?}\}\cup\{M_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}}
providing
\mathcal{C}_{x}(\mathcal{E})
, we have

\mathcal{C}_{x}(\mathcal{E})=\frac{\eta_{x}\Tr(\rho_{x}M_{x})}{\Tr(\rho_{0}M_{x})}=\eta_{x}\Tr(\rho_{x}E^{\prime})\leqslant\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}E\in\mathbb{H}_{+}\\
\Tr(\rho_{0}E)=1\end{subarray}}\eta_{x}\Tr(\rho_{x}E),

(12)

where the second equality is by letting
E^{\prime}=M_{x}/\Tr(\rho_{0}M_{x})
and the inequality is due to
E^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}_{+}
and
\Tr(\rho_{0}E)=1
.
Therefore, to prove the equality in (
11
), it is enough to show that

\mathcal{C}_{x}(\mathcal{E})\geqslant\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}E\in\mathbb{H}_{+}\\
\Tr(\rho_{0}E)=1\end{subarray}}\eta_{x}\Tr(\rho_{x}E).

(13)

Let
E^{\star}
be an optimal operator providing the maximum in the right-hand side of Inequality (
13
).
We first note that
\Tr E^{\star}
is strictly positive because

\Tr E^{\star}\geqslant\eta_{x}\Tr(\rho_{x}E^{\star})\geqslant\eta_{x}\Tr(\rho_{x}\mathbbm{1})=\eta_{x}>0,

(14)

where the first inequality is due to
\mathbbm{1}-\eta_{x}\rho_{x}\in\mathbb{H}_{+}
and the second inequality is from the optimality of
E^{\star}
among all
E\in\mathbb{H}_{+}
with
\Tr(\rho_{0}E)=1
, whereas
\mathbbm{1}
is one of such operators.
From the positivity of
\Tr E^{\star}
, we can consider the measurement
\{M_{?}\}\cup\{M_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}}
with

M_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}E^{\star}/\Tr E^{\star},&i=x,\\
\mathbbm{1}-E^{\star}/\Tr E^{\star},&i=?,\\
\mathbb{O},&\mathrm{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

(15)

where
\mathbb{O}
is the zero operator in
\mathbb{H}
.
Thus, we have

\mathcal{C}_{x}(\mathcal{E})\geqslant\frac{\eta_{x}\Tr(\rho_{x}M_{x})}{\Tr(\rho_{0}M_{x})}=\frac{\eta_{x}\Tr(\rho_{x}E^{\star})}{\Tr(\rho_{0}E^{\star})}=\eta_{x}\Tr(\rho_{x}E^{\star})=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}E\in\mathbb{H}_{+}\\
\Tr(\rho_{0}E)=1\end{subarray}}\eta_{x}\Tr(\rho_{x}E),

(16)

where the inequality is from the definition of
\mathcal{C}_{x}(\mathcal{E})
in Eq. (
9
), the first equality is by Eq. (
15
), and the last two equalities are from the definition of
E^{\star}
.
Moreover, Inequality (
14
) together with the definition of
E^{\star}
leads us to the inequality in (
11
).∎

II.2
Optimal quantum state discrimination under maximum-confidence measurements

In this subsection, we first introduce the notion of maximum-confidence measurement, and present

Definition 2

The following lemma shows that the support of any Hermitian operator realizing

Lemma 3

III
Maximum-confidence discrimination of quantum sequences

III.1
Quantum sequence discrimination and confidence of measurement

Let us consider the situation of discriminating quantum sequences, each being a sequence of quantum states prepared from
L
quantum state ensembles

\mathcal{E}^{1}=\{\eta_{i}^{1},\rho_{i}^{1}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{n_{1}}},\ldots,\mathcal{E}^{L}=\{\eta_{i}^{L},\rho_{i}^{L}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{n_{L}}},

(49)

where each ensemble
\mathcal{E}^{l}
consists of
n_{l}
states
\rho_{1}^{l},\ldots,\rho_{n_{l}}^{l}
prepared with probabilities
\eta_{1}^{l},\ldots,\eta_{n_{l}}^{l}
, for
l=1,\ldots,L
.
In this situation, the preparation of a quantum sequence proceeds step by step. At the first step, a state
\rho_{c_{1}}^{1}
is prepared from the ensemble
\mathcal{E}^{1}
with the corresponding probability
\eta_{c_{1}}^{1}
.
At each subsequent step
l=2,\ldots,L
, a state
\rho_{c_{l}}^{l}
is prepared from the ensemble
\mathcal{E}^{l}
with the corresponding probability
\eta_{c_{l}}^{l}
. Consequently, the quantum sequence

(\rho_{c_{1}}^{1},\ldots,\rho_{c_{L}}^{L})

(50)

is prepared with the probability

\eta_{c_{1}}^{1}\times\cdots\times\eta_{c_{L}}^{L}.

(51)

By using the notion

\vec{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{L}),

(52)

we denote the Cartesian product of
\mathbb{N}_{n_{1}},\ldots,\mathbb{N}_{n_{L}}
as

\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}=\{(c_{1},\ldots,c_{L})\,|\,c_{1}\in\mathbb{N}_{n_{1}},\ldots,c_{L}\in\mathbb{N}_{n_{L}}\}.

(53)

The quantum sequence in Eq. (
50
), together with its probability in Eq. (
51
), can be considered as the tensor-producted state
\rho_{\vec{c}}
with the joint probability
\eta_{\vec{c}}
,

\eta_{\vec{c}}=\prod_{l=1}^{L}\eta_{c_{l}}^{l},~\rho_{\vec{c}}=\bigotimes_{l=1}^{L}\rho_{c_{l}}^{l},

(54)

for
\vec{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{L})\in\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}
, from the tensor product of the ensembles in Eq. (
49
),

\bigotimes_{l=1}^{L}\mathcal{E}^{l}=\{\eta_{\vec{c}},\rho_{\vec{c}}\}_{\vec{c}\in\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}}.

(55)

Figure
1
illustrates the process of preparing a quantum sequence from the quantum sequence ensemble in Eq. (
55
).

Figure 1:
Quantum sequence ensemble
\bigotimes_{l=1}^{L}\mathcal{E}^{l}=\{\eta_{\vec{c}},\rho_{\vec{c}}\}_{\vec{c}\in\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}}
.
For each
l=1,\ldots,L
, the
l
th state
\rho_{c_{l}}^{l}
is prepared from the ensemble
\mathcal{E}^{l}
with the probability
\eta_{c_{l}}^{l}
, for
c_{l}=1,\ldots,n_{l}
.
The quantum sequence
(\rho_{c_{1}}^{1},\ldots,\rho_{c_{L}}^{L})
can be regarded as the tensor producted state
\rho_{\vec{c}}
with the probability
\eta_{\vec{c}}
, for
\vec{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{L})\in\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}
.

The quantum state of a quantum system from the ensemble in Eq. (
55
) is denoted by
\rho_{0}
, that is,

\rho_{0}=\sum_{\vec{c}\in\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}}\eta_{\vec{c}}\rho_{\vec{c}}.

(56)

From Eq. (
54
), the state
\rho_{0}
in Eq. (
56
) can be rewritten as

\rho_{0}=\!\sum_{c_{1}\in\mathbb{N}_{n_{1}}}\!\cdots\!\sum_{c_{L}\in\mathbb{N}_{n_{L}}}\!\eta_{c_{1}}^{1}\rho_{c_{1}}^{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\eta_{c_{L}}^{L}\rho_{c_{L}}^{L}=\bigotimes_{l=1}^{L}\rho_{0}^{l},

(57)

where
\rho_{0}^{l}
is the quantum state of a quantum system from the ensemble
\mathcal{E}^{l}
, as defined in Eq. (
4
), for
l=1,\ldots,L
.

To discriminate the quantum sequences from the ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\bigotimes_{l=1}^{L}\mathcal{E}^{l}
in Eq. (
55
), we use a measurement

\mathcal{M}=\{M_{?}\}\cup\{M_{\vec{c}}\}_{\vec{c}\in\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}}

(58)

with the decision rule that the measurement outcome corresponding to
M_{\vec{c}}
leads us to guess the prepared quantum sequence as
\rho_{\vec{c}}
, for
\vec{c}\in\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}
, and
M_{?}
provides inconclusive measurement outcomes.
In this case, the average probability of correctly guessing the prepared quantum sequence from
\mathcal{E}
is

\sum_{\vec{c}\in\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}}\eta_{\vec{c}}\Tr(\rho_{\vec{c}}M_{\vec{c}}).

(59)

In particular, when a measurement
\mathcal{M}^{l}=\{M_{?}^{l}\}\cup\{M_{i}^{l}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{n_{l}}}
is independently performed at each step
l=1,\ldots,L
of a quantum sequence from
\mathcal{E}
, the measurement operator
M_{\vec{c}}
in Eq. (
58
) can be represented as

M_{\vec{c}}=M_{c_{1}}^{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes M_{c_{L}}^{L}

(60)

for each
\vec{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{L})\in\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}
, therefore we have

\sum_{\vec{c}\in\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}}\eta_{\vec{c}}\Tr(\rho_{\vec{c}}M_{\vec{c}})=\prod_{l=1}^{L}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{n_{l}}}\eta_{i}^{l}\Tr(\rho_{i}^{l}M_{i}^{l}).

(61)

In other words, Eq. (
59
) can be expressed as the product of average success probabilities for discriminating each ensemble
\mathcal{E}^{l}
with respect to the measurement
\mathcal{M}^{l}
, for
l=1,\ldots,L
.

Now, let us consider the confidence of a measurement
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{?}\}\cup\{M_{\vec{c}}\}_{\vec{c}\in\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}}
in quantum sequence discrimination of the ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\bigotimes_{l=1}^{L}\mathcal{E}^{l}
in Eq. (
55
).
For each
\vec{x}\in\mathbb{N}_{n}
, the
confidence
of
\mathcal{M}
to identify
\rho_{\vec{x}}
is the probability
\Pr(\rho_{\vec{x}}|M_{\vec{x}})
that the prepared quantum sequence is
\rho_{\vec{x}}
given that the measurement outcome corresponding to
M_{\vec{x}}
is obtained, that is,

\Pr(\rho_{\vec{x}}|M_{\vec{x}})=\frac{\eta_{\vec{x}}\Tr(\rho_{\vec{x}}M_{\vec{x}})}{\Tr(\rho_{0}M_{\vec{x}})},

(62)

which is well defined only when

\Tr(\rho_{0}M_{\vec{x}})>0.

(63)

The
maximum confidence
of measurements to identify
\rho_{\vec{x}}
is

\mathcal{C}_{\vec{x}}(\mathcal{E})=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{M}~\mathsf{with}\\
\Tr(\rho_{0}M_{\vec{x}})>0\end{subarray}}\Pr(\rho_{\vec{x}}|M_{\vec{x}}),

(64)

where the maximum is taken over all possible measurements
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{?}\}\cup\{M_{\vec{c}}\}_{\vec{c}\in\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}}
with Eq. (
58
).

The following theorem shows that the maximum confidence of identifying a quantum sequence is to achieve the maximum confidence of identifying each state in the quantum sequence.

Theorem 4
.

For a quantum sequence ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\bigotimes_{l=1}^{L}\mathcal{E}^{l}
in Eq. (
55
) and
\vec{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{L})\in\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}
, we have

\mathcal{C}_{\vec{x}}(\mathcal{E})=\prod_{l=1}^{L}\mathcal{C}_{x_{l}}(\mathcal{E}^{l}),

(65)

where
\mathcal{C}_{x_{l}}(\mathcal{E}^{l})
is the maximum confidence to identify
\rho_{x_{l}}^{l}
from the ensemble
\mathcal{E}^{l}
, as defined in Eq. (
9
), for
l=1,\ldots,L
.

To prove Theorem
4
, we employ the following lemma that provides a expression for the sum and difference between two tensor-product operators.
The proof of Lemma
4
is given in Appendix
B
.

Lemma 4
.

For tensor-producted operators
X=\bigotimes_{l=1}^{L}X_{l}
and

, we have

X+(-1)^{a}Y=\frac{1}{2^{L-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\vec{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{L}\\
\omega_{2}(\vec{b})=a\end{subarray}}\bigotimes_{l=1}^{L}\big[X_{l}+(-1)^{b_{l}}Y_{l}\big]

(66)

for each
a\in\{0,1\}
, where
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{L}
is the Cartesian product of
L
copies of
\{0,1\}
,
b_{l}
is the
l
th entry of
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{L})
, and
\omega_{2}(\vec{b})
is the modulo 2 summation of all entries in
\vec{b}
, that is,

\omega_{2}(\vec{b})=\sum_{l=1}^{L}b_{l}~(\mathrm{mod}~2).

(67)

4
.

We prove Eq. (
65
) by showing that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\vec{x}}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{L}\mathcal{C}_{x_{l}}(\mathcal{E}^{l}),

(68a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\vec{x}}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{L}\mathcal{C}_{x_{l}}(\mathcal{E}^{l}).

(68b)

To show Inequality (
68a
), let
\mathcal{M}^{l}=\{M_{?}^{l}\}\cup\{M_{i}^{l}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{n_{l}}}
be a measurement providing the maximum-confidence
\mathcal{C}_{x_{l}}(\mathcal{E}^{l})
in Eq. (
9
) for each
l=1,\ldots,L
, that is,

\displaystyle\Tr(\rho_{0}^{l}M_{x_{l}}^{l})>0,

(69a)

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{x_{l}}^{l}\Tr(\rho_{x_{l}}^{l}M_{x_{l}}^{l})}{\Tr(\rho_{0}^{l}M_{x_{l}}^{l})}=\mathcal{C}_{x_{l}}(\mathcal{E}^{l}).

(69b)

For the measurement
\{M_{?}\}\cup\{M_{\vec{c}}\}_{\vec{c}\in\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}}
constructed as Eq. (
60
) in terms of
\mathcal{M}^{l}
realizing the maximum-confidence
\mathcal{C}_{x_{l}}(\mathcal{E}^{l})
for
l=1,\ldots,L
, we have

\displaystyle\Tr(\rho_{0}M_{\vec{x}})=\prod_{l=1}^{L}\Tr(\rho_{0}^{l}M_{x_{l}}^{l})>0,

(70a)

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{\vec{x}}\Tr(\rho_{\vec{x}}M_{\vec{x}})}{\Tr(\rho_{0}M_{\vec{x}})}=\prod_{l=1}^{L}\frac{\eta_{x_{l}}^{l}\Tr(\rho_{x_{l}}^{l}M_{x_{l}}^{l})}{\Tr(\rho_{0}^{l}M_{x_{l}}^{l})}=\prod_{l=1}^{L}\mathcal{C}_{x_{l}}(\mathcal{E}^{l}).

(70b)

The first equalities in Eqs. (

) and (

) follow from Eqs. (

) and (

).

) and the second equality in Eq. (

) and Eq. (

), respectively.
Thus, the definition of
\mathcal{C}_{\vec{x}}(\mathcal{E})
in Eq. (
64
) leads us to Inequality (

).

) and (

III.2
Optimal quantum sequence discrimination under maximum-confidence measurements

In this subsection, we present our main result, showing that the optimal quantum sequence discrimination under maximum-confidence measurements can always be achieved just by performing quantum state discrimination independently at each step of the quantum sequence.
To this end, we first introduce the notion of maximum-confidence measurement in quantum sequence discrimination.

For a quantum sequence ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\bigotimes_{l=1}^{L}\mathcal{E}^{l}
in Eq. (
55
) and a measurement
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{?}\}\cup\{M_{\vec{c}}\}_{\vec{c}\in\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}}
,
\mathcal{M}
is a
maximum-confidence measurement
of
\mathcal{E}
if and only if it realizes the maximum confidence
\mathcal{C}_{\vec{c}}(\mathcal{E})
in Eq. (
64
), that is,

\frac{\eta_{\vec{c}}\Tr(\rho_{\vec{c}}M_{\vec{c}})}{\Tr(\rho_{0}M_{i})}=\mathcal{C}_{\vec{c}}(\mathcal{E}),

(72)

for all
\vec{c}\in\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}
with
\Tr(\rho_{0}M_{\vec{c}})>0
, or equivalently,

\Tr[(\mathcal{C}_{\vec{c}}(\mathcal{E})\rho_{0}-\eta_{\vec{c}}\rho_{\vec{c}})M_{\vec{c}}]=0

(73)

for all
\vec{c}\in\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}
.
Similarly to Eq. (
19
), we denote by
\mathbb{M}(\mathcal{E})
the set of all maximum-confidence measurements of
\mathcal{E}
, that is,

\mathbb{M}(\mathcal{E})=\{\mbox{measurement}~\{M_{?}\}\cup\{M_{\vec{c}}\}_{\vec{c}\in\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}}\,|\,M_{\vec{c}}\in\mathbb{M}_{\vec{c}}(\mathcal{E})~\mbox{for all}~\vec{c}\in\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}\}

(74)

where
\mathbb{M}_{\vec{c}}(\mathcal{E})
is the set of all positive-semidefinite operators
M_{\vec{c}}
with Eq. (
73
), that is,

\mathbb{M}_{\vec{c}}(\mathcal{E})=\{E\in\mathbb{H}_{+}\,|\,\Tr[(\mathcal{C}_{\vec{c}}(\mathcal{E})\rho_{0}-\eta_{\vec{c}}\rho_{\vec{c}})E]=0\}.

(75)

The following theorem shows that performing a maximum-confidence measurement independently at each step of the quantum sequence is a maximum-confidence measurement for the quantum sequence.

Theorem 5
.

When
\mathcal{M}^{l}=\{M_{?}^{l}\}\cup\{M_{i}^{l}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{n_{l}}}

\mathcal{E}=\bigotimes_{l=1}^{L}\mathcal{E}^{l}
in Eq. (

).

Proof.

As
\mathcal{M}
is obviously a measurement, it is enough to show the validity of Eq. (

) for all
\vec{c}\in\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}
.

For each
\vec{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{L})\in\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}
, it follows from Theorem
4
and Lemma
4
that

\mathcal{C}_{\vec{x}}(\mathcal{E})\rho_{0}-\eta_{\vec{x}}\rho_{\vec{x}}=\frac{1}{2^{L-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\vec{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{L}\\
\omega_{2}(\vec{b})=1\end{subarray}}\bigotimes_{l=1}^{L}\Big[\mathcal{C}_{x_{l}}(\mathcal{E}^{l})\rho_{0}^{l}+(-1)^{b_{l}}\eta_{x_{l}}^{l}\rho_{x_{l}}^{l}\Big],

(76)

where
b_{l}
is the
l
th entry of
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{L})
.
From Eqs. (
60
) and (
76
), we have

\Tr\big[\big(\mathcal{C}_{\vec{x}}(\mathcal{E})\rho_{0}-\eta_{\vec{x}}\rho_{\vec{x}}\big)M_{\vec{x}}\big]=\frac{1}{2^{L-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\vec{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{L}\\
\omega_{2}(\vec{b})=1\end{subarray}}\bigotimes_{l=1}^{L}\Tr\big[\big(\mathcal{C}_{x_{l}}(\mathcal{E}^{l})\rho_{0}^{l}+(-1)^{b_{l}}\eta_{x_{l}}^{l}\rho_{x_{l}}^{l}\big)M_{x_{l}}\big].

(77)

Since every
\vec{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{L}
with
\omega_{2}(\vec{b})=1

is an element of

Now, let us consider the optimal discrimination of a quantum sequence ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\bigotimes_{l=1}^{L}\mathcal{E}^{l}
under maximum-confidence measurements to achieve

p_{\sf G}(\mathcal{E})=\max_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathbb{M}(\mathcal{E})}\sum_{\vec{c}\in\mathbb{N}_{\vec{n}}}\eta_{\vec{c}}\Tr(\rho_{\vec{c}}M_{\vec{c}})

(78)

where the maximum is taken over all possible maximum-confidence measurements

\mathcal{E}
in Eq. (

From Theorem

in Eq. (

IV
Discussion

We have considered quantum sequence discrimination under maximum-confidence measurements and shown that the optimal discrimination of a quantum sequence ensemble can always be factorized into that of each individual ensemble.
In other words, the optimal quantum sequence discrimination under maximum-confidence measurements can be achieved just by performing a maximum-confidence discrimination independently at each step of the quantum sequence.
We have also shown that the maximum confidence of identifying a quantum sequence is to achieve the maximum confidence of identifying each state in the quantum sequence.
We have further established a necessary and sufficient condition for the optimal quantum state discrimination under maximum-confidence measurements.

We note that our results include the known results on unambiguous discrimination of quantum sequences
gupt20241
;
gupt20242
.
In unambiguous discrimination of quantum states, the states are discriminated without error (i.e., unambiguously) by allowing an inconclusive measurement outcome
ivan1987
;
diek1988
;
pere1988
.
For a given quantum state ensemble, a state can be unambiguously discriminated if and only if the maximum confidence of identifying the state is equal to one
crok2006
.
Therefore, it follows from Theorem
4
that a quantum sequence from a quantum sequence ensemble can be unambiguously discriminated if and only if each state in the quantum sequence can be unambiguously discriminated.

When quantum states can be unambiguously discriminated, maximum-confidence measurements become unambiguous measurements that provide unit confidence for each state.
In this case, the optimal state discrimination under maximum-confidence measurements coincides with the optimal unambiguous discrimination.
Therefore, Theorem
6
ensures that the optimal unambiguous discrimination of a quantum sequence ensemble can always be factorized into that of each individual ensemble when quantum sequences can be unambiguously discriminated.

Our results can be applied to situations involving multiple independent repetitions of quantum information processing tasks based on maximum-confidence measurements, such as quantum cryptography and quantum teleportation
benn1992
;
neve2012
.
In these situations, our results indicate that no advantage can be gained even if the receiver has access to quantum memory and considers performing a collective measurement on the quantum sequence.

As our results show the factorizability of the optimal quantum sequence discrimination under maximum-confidence measurements, it is natural to investigate measurement conditions under which the optimal discrimination of a quantum sequence ensemble can be factorized into that of each individual ensemble.
It is also an interesting direction for future research to investigate factorizability in the confidence of identifying a quantum sequence from a multi-party quantum sequence ensemble.

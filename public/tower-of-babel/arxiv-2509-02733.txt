\displaystyle(UAU^{-1}f|f)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(mf|f)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}|m(\xi)|^{2}|f(\xi)|^{2}\,d\mu\geq 0,\quad\forall f\in D(A).

The Mittag-Leffler function (see e.g.

We have that for

,

and

\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}\left[E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda t^{\alpha})\right]=-\lambda t^{\alpha-m}E_{\alpha,\alpha-m+1}(-\lambda t^{\alpha}),

and

(2.4)

\frac{d}{dt}\left[tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda t^{\alpha})\right]=E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda t^{\alpha}),

and

(2.5)

\frac{d}{dt}\left[t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda t^{\alpha})\right]=t^{\alpha-2}E_{\alpha,\alpha-1}(-\lambda t^{\alpha}).

4.
Weak energy solutions for the semilinear problem

Consider the semilinear problem

(4.1)

\begin{cases}\mathbb{D}_{t}^{\alpha}u\left(x,t\right)+Au\left(x,t\right)=f(u\left(x,t\right)),&\mbox{ in }\;\Omega\times\left(0,T\right),\\
u(\cdot,0)=u_{0},\;\;\partial_{t}u(\cdot,0)=u_{1}&\text{ in }\Omega.\end{cases}

Fix
0<T^{\star}\leq T
. To solve this problem, we introduce the Banach space

X_{s,T^{\star}}=\left\{u\in C\left(\left[0,T^{\star}\right];V_{1/2}\right)\cap C^{1}\left((0,T^{\star}];L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)\right):\left\|u\right\|_{s,T^{\star}}<\infty\right\},

where the norm is defined as (for a properly chosen
s>0,
depending on the
physical parameters)

\left\|u\right\|_{s,T^{\star}}^{2}:=\sup_{t\in\left[0,T^{\star}\right]}\left(\left\|u\left(\cdot,t\right)\right\|_{V_{1/2}}^{2}+t^{2s}\left\|\partial_{t}u\left(\cdot,t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+g_{2-\alpha}\ast\left\|\partial_{t}u\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\left(t\right)\right).

Note that, for the latter,
g_{2-\alpha}\ast\left\|\partial_{t}u\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\left(t\right)\to\left\|\partial_{t}u\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}
as

\alpha\to 2^{-}
,where the limit is also the corresponding kinetic energy associated with a
weak solution in the case of the classical wave equation. This is the reason why we refer to this term as kinetic energy.

Next, define the energy space
\mathcal{B}_{s,T^{\star},R^{\star}}\subset X_{s,T^{\star}},

where

\mathcal{B}_{s,T^{\star},R^{\star}}:=\left\{u\in X_{s,T^{\star}}:\left\|u\right\|_{s,T^{\star}}\leq R^{\star}\text{ and }u\left(0\right)=u_{0},\text{ }\partial_{t}u\left(0\right)=u_{1}\right\}.

Let
\Phi:\mathcal{B}_{s,T,R}\to\mathcal{B}_{s,T,R}
defined by

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Phi\left(v\left(\cdot\right)\right)\left(t\right)=

\displaystyle E_{\alpha,1}(-m(\cdot)t^{\alpha})u_{0}(\cdot)+tE_{\alpha,2}(-m(\cdot)t^{\alpha})u_{1}(\cdot)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}f(v\left(\cdot,\tau\right))(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-m(\cdot)(t-\tau)^{\alpha})d\tau,\quad v\in\mathcal{B}_{s,T,R}.

We say that a function
u
is
local weak energy solution
to (
4.1
) if
\Phi(u(t))=u(t)
.

(
Hf2
)

We assume that there exists
C_{f}>0
such that

(4.3)

f(0)=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad|f^{\prime}(\sigma)|\leq C_{f}|\sigma|^{r-1},\quad r>1,\,\sigma\in\mathbb{R},

and

V_{1/2}\hookrightarrow L^{2r}(\Omega).

Note that (
4.3
) implies that

|f(\sigma)|\leq C_{f}|\sigma|^{r},\quad\sigma\in\mathbb{R}.

The next theorem is the main result of this section. Through the Banach fixed point theorem we establish the existence of local weak solutions of
4.1
(on some interval
[0,T]
). Then we show that the solution can be extended to a larger interval.(
[0,T_{\ast}]
) Finally, we prove the maximal interval
[0,T_{\max})
with the property that either
T_{\max}=\infty
or
T_{\max}<\infty
. In the last case the norm blows-up in the sense of (
4.4
) below.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume (
Hf2
). The problem (
4.1
) has a
unique weak energy solution on

, for all

, where

or

\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{\max}}\left(\left\|u\left(t\right)\right\|_{V_{1/2}}^{2}+t^{2s}\left\|\partial_{t}u\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+g_{2-\alpha}\ast\left\|\partial_{t}u\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\left(t\right)\right)=\infty.

holds.

Proof.

We will the following:

•

(Contraction argument)
\Phi
is a contraction from
\mathcal{B}_{s,T^{\star},R^{\star}}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{s,T^{\star},R^{\star}}
, for some
T^{\star},R^{\star},s>0.

•

(Extension argument) The solution
u
can be extended by continuity from
\left[0,T^{\ast}\right],
for some

to the interval
\left[0,T^{\ast}+\varsigma\right],
for some
\varsigma>0.

•

(Global vs blow-up) Finally, we show that that either

or else
if

, one has (

).

Step 1 (The contraction argument)
. Since

is continuously
differentiable, we have that
\Phi(u(\cdot))(t)
is continuously
differentiable on
(0,T^{\star}]
. We will show that by an appropriate

is a

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{s,T^{\star}}
. The appropriate choice of
T^{\star},R^{\star}>0
will be specified below. We first show that
\Phi
maps
\mathcal{B}_{s,T^{\star},R^{\star}}
into
\mathcal{B}_{s,T^{\star},R^{\star}}
.
Indeed, let

. Then, by
assumption (

) and the fact that
u\in C([0,T^{\ast}],V_{1/2})
,
for every
t\in[0,T^{\star}]
, we have that

\|f(u(\cdot,t))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{f}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{V_{1/2}}^{r},

for some
C_{f}>0
. Using the estimate (
4.5
) we get

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}f(u(\xi,\tau))(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}((-m(\xi)(t-\tau)^{\alpha})d\tau\right\|_{V_{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\|u(\cdot,\tau)\|_{V_{1/2}}^{r}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C(T^{\star})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(R^{\star})^{r}.

Thus, proceeding as the proof of Theorem
3.3
, we get that there
is a constant
C>0
such that for every
t\in[0,T^{\star}]
,

(4.6)

\|\Phi(u)(\cdot,t)\|_{V_{1/2}}\leq C\left(\|u_{0}\|_{V_{1/2}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(T^{\star})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(R^{\star})^{r}\right).

Next, we show that
\Phi(u)\in C([0,T^{\star}];V_{1/2})
. Indeed, since
t\mapsto E_{\alpha,1}(-m(\xi)t^{\alpha})v
is continuous for every
t\in[0,T^{\star}]
and for all
v\in\mathcal{H}
,

|(E_{\alpha,1}(-m(\xi)(t+h)^{\alpha})-E_{\alpha,1}(-m(\xi)t^{\alpha}))(m(\xi))^{1/2}u_{0}(\xi)|^{2}\leq C^{2}|(m(\xi))^{1/2}u_{0}(\xi)|^{2}

and
u_{0}\in V_{1/2}
, it follows from the Dominated Convergence Theorem that the function
E_{\alpha,1}(-m(\cdot)t^{\alpha})u_{0}(\cdot)\in C([0,T^{\star}];V_{1/2})
. On the other hand, we see that
tE_{\alpha,2}(-m(\cdot)t^{\alpha})u_{1}(\cdot)
belongs to
C([0,T^{\star}];V_{1/2})
. The
continuity in
t=0
is direct. Let
t>0
and
h>0
. Using (
2.4
), we get

\displaystyle\left|(m(\xi))^{1/2}\left[(t+h)E_{\alpha,2}(-m(\xi)(t+h)^{\alpha})-tE_{\alpha,2}(-m(\xi)t^{\alpha})\right]\right|

\displaystyle=\left|(m(\xi))^{1/2}\int_{t}^{t+h}E_{\alpha,1}(-m(\xi)\sigma^{\alpha})d\sigma\right|

\displaystyle\leq C(m(\xi))^{-1/2}\int_{t}^{t+h}\frac{m(\xi)\sigma^{\alpha}}{1+m(\xi)\sigma^{\alpha}}\sigma\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cm_{0}^{-1/2}}{\alpha-1}\left(\frac{1}{t^{\alpha-1}}-\frac{1}{(t+h)^{\alpha-1}}\right).

Therefore, we deduce

\displaystyle\|\left[(t+h)E_{\alpha,2}(-m(\cdot)(t+h)^{\alpha})-tE_{\alpha,2}(-m(\cdot)t^{\alpha})\right]u_{1}(\cdot)\|_{V_{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cm_{0}}{\alpha-1}\left(\frac{1}{t^{\alpha-1}}-\frac{1}{(t+h)^{\alpha-1}}\right)\|u_{1}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad h\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Next,

\displaystyle\|\int_{0}^{t+h}(t+h-\tau)^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-m(\cdot)(t+h-\tau)^{\alpha})f(u(\cdot,\tau))d\tau

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-m(\cdot)(t-\tau)^{\alpha})f(u(\cdot,\tau))\;d\tau\|_{V_{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|[(t+h-\tau)^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-m(\cdot)(t+h-\tau)^{\alpha})-(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-m(\cdot)(t-\tau)^{\alpha})]f(u(\cdot,\tau))\|_{V_{1/2}}d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{t+h}\|(t+h-\tau)^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-m(\cdot)(t+h-\tau)^{\alpha})f(u(\cdot,\tau))\|_{V_{1/2}}d\tau

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

Let us see that
I_{1}\rightarrow 0
as
h\rightarrow 0^{+}
. First, setting

Q_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right):=x^{\beta-1}E_{\alpha,\beta}(-m(\cdot)x^{\alpha}).

We need to prove that

(4.7)

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\|\left[Q_{\alpha,\alpha}\left(t+h-\tau\right)-Q_{\alpha,\alpha}\left(t-\tau\right)\right]f(u(\cdot,\tau))\|_{V_{1/2}}=0.

Indeed, since
t\mapsto t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-m(\xi)t^{\alpha})f(u(\xi,t))
is continuous for every
t\in[0,T^{\star}]
,

\displaystyle|(m(\xi))^{1/2}[Q_{\alpha,\alpha}\left(t+h-\tau\right)-Q_{\alpha,\alpha}\left(t-\tau\right)]f(u(\xi,\tau))|

\displaystyle\leq 2C(t-\tau)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}|f(u(\xi,\tau))|,

and
f(u(\cdot,\tau))\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, (
4.7
) follows from
Dominated Convergence Theorem. Moreover, it holds

\displaystyle\|\left[Q_{\alpha,\alpha}\left(t+h-\tau\right)-Q_{\alpha,\alpha}\left(t-\tau\right)\right]f(u(\cdot,\tau))\|_{V_{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(t-\tau)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\|u(\cdot,\tau)\|_{V_{1/2}}^{r}

\displaystyle\leq C(t-\tau)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}(R^{\star})^{r}.

Since
\tau\mapsto C(t-\tau)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}(R^{\star})^{r}
is
integrable, then
I_{1}\rightarrow 0
as
h\rightarrow 0^{+}
by the
Dominated Convergence Theorem. On the other hand, observe that

\|Q_{\alpha,\alpha}\left(t+h-\tau\right)f(u(\cdot,\tau))\|_{V_{1/2}}\leq C(t+h-\tau)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}(R^{\star})^{r}

implies

I_{2}\leq C(R^{\star})^{r}\int_{t}^{t+h}(t+h-\tau)^{\alpha-2}d\tau=C(R^{\star})^{r}h^{\alpha-1}\rightarrow 0,\,\,h\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Thus, the claim
\Phi(u)\in C([0,T^{\star}];V_{1/2})
follows. Similarly,
we have that there is a constant
C>0,
such that for every
t\in[0,T^{\star}]
,

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u)^{\prime}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\|u_{0}\|_{V_{1/2}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(T^{\star})^{\alpha-1}\left\|u\right\|_{C\left(\left[0,T^{\ast}\right];V_{1/2}\right)}^{r}\right)

(4.8)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\|u_{0}\|_{V_{1/2}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(T^{\star})^{\alpha-1}\left(R^{\star}\right)^{r}\right).

From here

\displaystyle t^{s}\|\Phi(u)^{\prime}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(t^{s+\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\|u_{0}\|_{V_{1/2}}+t^{s}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+t^{s}(T^{\star})^{\alpha-1}\left\|u\right\|_{C\left(\left[0,T^{\ast}\right];V_{1/2}\right)}^{r}\right)

(4.9)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left((T^{\star})^{s+\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\|u_{0}\|_{V_{1/2}}+(T^{\star})^{s}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(T^{\star})^{s+\alpha-1}\left(R^{\star}\right)^{r}\right).

Clearly,
\Phi^{\prime}(u)\in C((0,T^{\star}];L^{2}(\Omega))
. Indeed,
since
t\mapsto m(\xi)t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-m(\xi)t^{\alpha})u_{0}(\xi)
is continuous for every
t\in[0,T^{\star}]
,

\displaystyle|m(\xi)(t+h)^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-m(\xi)(t+h)^{\alpha})u_{0}(\xi)-m(\xi)t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-m(\xi)t^{\alpha})u_{0}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}|(m(\xi))^{1/2}u_{0}(\xi)|,

and
u_{0}\in V_{1/2}
, we have that by the Dominated Convergence Theorem
that

m(\cdot)t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-m(\xi)t^{\alpha})u_{0}(\cdot)\in C([0,T^{\star}];L^{2}(\Omega)).

Analogously,
it can be shown that
E_{\alpha,1}(-m(\cdot)t^{\alpha})u_{1}(\cdot)

belongs to
C([0,T^{\star}];L^{2}(\Omega))
. Notice that

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left|\left(Q_{\alpha,\alpha-1}(t+h-\tau)-Q_{\alpha,\alpha-1}(t-\tau)\right)f(u(\xi,\tau))\right|^{2}=0,

for a.e.
t>0,
and

\displaystyle\left|\left(Q_{\alpha,\alpha-1}(t+h-\tau)-Q_{\alpha,\alpha-1}(t-\tau)\right)f(u(\xi,\tau))\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}(t-\tau)^{2(\alpha-2)}|f(u(\xi,\tau))|^{2}\in L^{1}(\Omega).

Owing to (
4.5
), we then have

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left\|[Q_{\alpha,\alpha-1}(t+h-\tau)-Q_{\alpha,\alpha-1}(t-\tau)]f(u(\xi,\tau))\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\left\|[Q_{\alpha,\alpha-1}(t+h-\tau)-Q_{\alpha,\alpha-1}(t-\tau)]f(u(\xi,\tau))\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C(t-\tau)^{\alpha-2}\|u(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{2r}(\Omega)}^{r}\in L^{1}(0,T).

The Dominated Convergence Theorem implies that

J_{1}:=\int_{0}^{t}\left\|[Q_{\alpha,\alpha-1}(t+h-\tau)-Q_{\alpha,\alpha-1}(t-\tau)]f(u(\xi,\tau))\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}d\tau

goes to zero as
h\rightarrow 0^{+}
. As before, we can prove that

J_{2}=\int_{t}^{t+h}\|(t+h-\tau)^{\alpha-2}E_{\alpha,\alpha-1}(-m(\cdot)(t+h-\tau)^{\alpha})f(u(\cdot,\tau))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}d\tau\rightarrow 0,\quad h\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{0}^{t+h}\left(Q_{\alpha,\alpha-1}(t+h-\tau)-Q_{\alpha,\alpha-1}(t-\tau)\right)f(u(\cdot,\tau))d\tau\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=:J_{1}+J_{2}\rightarrow 0,\quad h\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Henceforth,
\Phi^{\prime}(u)\in C((0,T^{\star}];L^{2}(\Omega))
. Next,
by (
4.8
) and using the formula

\int_{0}^{t}g_{2-\alpha}(t-\sigma)\sigma^{p}\,d\sigma=\frac{t^{p+2-\alpha}}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}\mathbf{B}(p+1,2-\alpha),

where
\mathbf{B}(a,b)=\int_{0}^{1}\tau^{a-1}(1-\tau)^{b-1}\,d\tau
is
the standard Beta function for
a,b>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle g_{2-\alpha}\ast\|\partial_{t}\Phi\left(u\right)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}(t)

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\int_{0}^{t}g_{2-\alpha}(t-\sigma)\left[\sigma^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\|u_{0}(\cdot)\|_{V_{1/2}}+\|u_{1}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(T^{\star})^{\alpha-1}(R^{\star})^{r}\right]^{2}d\sigma

\displaystyle=C^{2}\left[\mathbf{B}\left(\alpha-1,2-\alpha\right)\|u_{0}(\cdot)\|_{V_{1/2}}^{2}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\mathbf{B}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2},2-\alpha\right)t^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|u_{0}(\cdot)\|_{V_{1/2}}\left(\|u_{1}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(T^{\star})^{\alpha-1}(R^{\star})^{r}\right)\right.

\displaystyle+\left.t^{2-\alpha}\left(\|u_{1}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+(T^{\star})^{2(\alpha-1)}(R^{\star})^{2r}+\|u_{1}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}(T^{\star})^{\alpha-1}(R^{\star})^{r}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=C^{2}\left(\|u_{0}(\cdot)\|_{V_{1/2}}^{2}+t^{2-\alpha}\|u_{1}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+t^{2-\alpha}(T^{\star})^{2(\alpha-1)}(R^{\star})^{2r}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.t^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|u_{0}(\cdot)\|_{V_{1/2}}\|u_{1}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+t^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|u_{0}(\cdot)\|_{V_{1/2}}(T^{\star})^{\alpha-1}(R^{\star})^{r}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.t^{2-\alpha}\|u_{1}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}(T^{\star})^{\alpha-1}(R^{\star})^{r}\right).

Hence,

\displaystyle g_{2-\alpha}\ast\|\partial_{t}\Phi\left(u\right)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}(t)

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\left(\|u_{0}(\cdot)\|_{V_{1/2}}^{2}+(T^{\star})^{2-\alpha}\|u_{1}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+(T^{\star})^{\alpha}(R^{\star})^{2r}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.(T^{\star})^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|u_{0}(\cdot)\|_{V_{1/2}}\|u_{1}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|u_{0}(\cdot)\|_{V_{1/2}}(T^{\star})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(R^{\star})^{r}\right.

(4.10)

\displaystyle+\left.\|u_{1}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}T^{\star}(R^{\star})^{r}\right).

It also follows from (
4.6
), (
6.4
) and (
4.10
) that

\displaystyle\|\Phi\left(u\right)(\cdot,t)\|_{s,T^{\star}}^{2}=

\displaystyle\|\Phi\left(u\right)(\cdot,t)\|_{V_{1/2}}^{2}+t^{2s}\|\partial_{t}\Phi\left(u\right)(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+g_{2-\alpha}\ast\|\partial_{t}\Phi\left(u\right)(\xi,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{2}\left(2+(T^{\star})^{2s+\alpha-2})\|u_{0}\|_{V_{1/2}}^{2}+(1+(T^{\star})^{2s}+(T^{\star})^{2-\alpha})\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\left((T^{\star})^{\alpha}+(T^{\star})^{2(s+\alpha-1)}\right)(R^{\star})^{2r}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.(1+(T^{\star})^{2s+\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}+(T^{\star})^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}})\|u_{0}\|_{V_{1/2}}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\left(2(T^{\star})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}+(T^{\star})^{2s+\frac{3\alpha}{2}-2}\right)\|u_{0}\|_{V_{1/2}}(R^{\star})^{r}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\left((T^{\star})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}+(T^{\star})^{2s+\alpha-1}+T^{\star}\right)\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}(R^{\star})^{r}\right).

Doing some computations, we arrive to

\displaystyle\|\Phi\left(u\right)(\cdot,t)\|_{s,T^{\star}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left((2+(T^{\star})^{2s+\alpha-2})^{1/2}\|u_{0}\|_{V_{1/2}}+((1+(T^{\star})^{2s}+(T^{\star})^{2-\alpha})^{1/2}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.((T^{\star})^{\alpha}+(T^{\star})^{2(s+\alpha-1)})^{1/2}(R^{\star})^{r}\right).

Moreover, using that
\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}\leq x+y
for all
x,y\geq 0
, we
have that

\displaystyle\|\Phi\left(u\right)(\cdot,t)\|_{s,T^{\star}}\leq

\displaystyle C(\|u_{0}\|_{V_{1/2}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})

\displaystyle+C\left((T^{\star})^{s+\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\|u_{0}\|_{V_{1/2}}+((T^{\star})^{s}+(T^{\star})^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}})\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.((T^{\star})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}+(T^{\star})^{s+\alpha-1})(R^{\star})^{r}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{V_{1/2}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})

\displaystyle+C\max\{(T^{\star})^{s+\frac{\alpha}{2}-1},(T^{\star})^{s}+(T^{\star})^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{V_{1/2}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle+C\left(((T^{\star})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}+(T^{\star})^{s+\alpha-1})(R^{\star})^{r}\right).

Letting now

R^{\star}\geq 3C(\|u_{0}\|_{V_{1/2}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}),

we can find a sufficiently small time
T^{\star}>0
such that

\displaystyle C\max\{(T^{\star})^{s+\frac{\alpha}{2}-1},(T^{\star})^{s}+(T^{\star})^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{V_{1/2}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle+C\left(((T^{\star})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}+(T^{\star})^{s+\alpha-1})(R^{\star})^{r}\right)\leq\frac{2R^{\star}}{3},

that is,

(4.11)

\|\Phi\left(u\right)\|_{s,T^{\star}}\leq R^{\star},

provided that
0<s<1
is such that

s>1-\frac{\alpha}{2}.

In this case, it follows that
\Phi(u)\in\mathcal{B}_{s,T^{\star},R^{\star}},
for all
u\in\mathcal{B}_{s,T^{\star},R^{\star}}
.

Next, we show that by choosing a possibly smaller
T^{\star}>0
,
\Phi
is
a contraction on
\mathcal{B}_{s,T^{\star},R^{\star}}
. Indeed, let
u,v\in\mathcal{B}_{s,T^{\star},R^{\star}}
. Using the mean value theorem
it is easy to show that

(4.12)

\|f(u(\cdot,t))-f(v(\cdot,t))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u(\cdot,t)-v(\cdot,t)\|_{V_{1/2}}\left(\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{V_{1/2}}+\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{V_{1/2}}\right)^{r-1}.

Similarly to the foregoing estimates, we can exploit

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u)\left(t\right)-\Phi(v)\left(t\right)\|_{V_{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\|f(u(\cdot,\tau))-f(v(\cdot,\tau))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\|u(\cdot,\tau)-v(\cdot,\tau)\|_{V_{1/2}}\left(\|u(\cdot,\tau)\|_{V_{1/2}}+\|v(\cdot,\tau)\|_{V_{1/2}}\right)^{r-1}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u-v\|_{C([0,T^{\star}];V_{1/2})}(R^{\star})^{r-1}(T^{\star})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

Analogously, we have

\|\Phi(u)^{\prime}\left(t\right)-\Phi(v)^{\prime}\left(t\right)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u-v\|_{C([0,T^{\star}];V_{1/2})}(R^{\star})^{r-1}(T^{\star})^{\alpha-1},

and

t^{s}\|\Phi(u)^{\prime}\left(t\right)-\Phi(v)^{\prime}\left(t\right)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u-v\|_{C([0,T^{\star}];V_{1/2})}(R^{\star})^{r-1}(T^{\star})^{s+\alpha-1}.

Finally,

g_{2-\alpha}\ast\|(\Phi(u)-\Phi(v))^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}(t)\leq C^{2}\|u-v\|_{C([0,T^{\star}];V_{1/2})}^{2}(R^{\star})^{2(r-1)}(T^{\star})^{\alpha}.

Then, for each
t\in[0,T^{\star}],

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u)\left(t\right)-\Phi(v)\left(t\right)\|_{V_{\gamma}}^{2}+t^{2s}\|\Phi(u)^{\prime}(t)-\Phi(v)^{\prime}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+g_{2-\alpha}\ast\|(\Phi(u)-\Phi(v))^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}(t)

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}(R^{\star})^{2(r-1)}\left(2(T^{\star})^{\alpha}+(T^{\star})^{2(s+\alpha-1)}\right)\|u-v\|_{C([0,T^{\star}];V_{1/2})}^{2}.

Namely, it holds

\|\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\|_{s,T^{\star}}\leq C(R^{\star})^{r-1}\left(2(T^{\star})^{\alpha}+(T^{\star})^{2(s+\alpha-1)}\right)^{1/2}\|u-v\|_{C([0,T^{\star}];V_{1/2})}.

Thus, for a sufficiently small
T^{\ast}\leq 1,
the previous estimate
shows that
\Phi
is a contraction.

Step 2 (The extension argument)
. Let

(4.13)

S_{1}(t)u_{0}(\xi):=E_{\alpha,1}(-m(\xi)t^{\alpha})u_{0}(\xi)\;\mbox{
and }\;S_{2}(t)u_{1}(\xi):=tE_{\alpha,2}(-m(\xi)t^{\alpha})u_{1}(\xi),

and

(4.14)

(S_{3}\ast f(u(\xi,\cdot)))(t):=\int_{0}^{t}f(u(\cdot,\tau))(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}((-m(\cdot)(t-\tau)^{\alpha})d\tau

so that

u(\xi,t)=S_{1}(t)u_{0}(\xi)+S_{2}(t)u_{1}(\xi)+(S_{3}\ast f(u(\xi,\cdot)))(t),\quad\xi\in\Omega.

Let also

(4.15)

S_{1}^{\prime}(t)u_{0}(\cdot):=-m(\cdot)t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,1}(-m(\cdot)t^{\alpha})u_{0}(\cdot)\;\mbox{
and }\;S_{2}^{\prime}(t)u_{1}(\cdot):=E_{\alpha,1}(-m(\cdot)t^{\alpha})u_{1}(\cdot),

and

(4.16)

(S_{3}^{\prime}\ast f(u(\xi,\cdot)))(t):=\int_{0}^{t}f(u(\cdot,\tau))(t-\tau)^{\alpha-2}E_{\alpha,\alpha-1}(-m(\cdot)(t-\tau)^{\alpha})d\tau

so that

\partial_{t}u(\xi,t)=S_{1}^{\prime}(t)u_{0}(\xi)+S_{2}^{\prime}(t)u_{1}(\xi)+(S_{3}^{\prime}\ast f(u(\xi,\cdot)))(t),\quad\xi\in\Omega.

Let
T^{\star}>0
be the time of the contraction argument. Fix
\tau>0

and consider the space

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{s,T^{\star},R}:=

\displaystyle\Big\{v\in C([0,T^{\star}+\tau];V_{1/2})\cap C^{1}((0,T^{\star}+\tau];L^{2}(\Omega))\text{:}

\displaystyle v(\cdot,t)=u(\cdot,t),\;\qquad\forall\;t\in[0,T^{\star}],

\displaystyle\|v(\cdot,t)-u(\cdot,T^{\star})\|_{V_{1/2}}^{2}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(t-T^{\star})^{2s}}\|\partial_{t}v(\cdot,t)-\partial_{t}u(\cdot,T^{\star})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+g_{2-\alpha}\ast\|\partial_{t}v(\cdot,t)-\partial_{t}u(\cdot,T^{\star})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq R^{2},\;\;\forall\;t\in[T^{\star},T^{\star}+\tau]\Big\}.

Define the mapping
\Phi
on
\mathcal{K}_{s,T^{\star},R}
by

(4.17)

\displaystyle\Phi(v)(\cdot,t)=

\displaystyle E_{\alpha,1}(-m(\cdot)t^{\alpha})u_{0}(\cdot)+tE_{\alpha,2}(-m(\cdot)t^{\alpha})u_{1}(\cdot)\

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}f(v(\cdot,\tau))(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-m(\cdot)(t-\tau)^{\alpha})d\tau.

Note that
\mathcal{K}_{s,T^{\star},R}
when endowed with the norm of
\|\cdot\|_{s,T^{\star}}
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{B}_{s,T^{\star},R}
. We show that
\Phi
has a fixed point in
\mathcal{K}_{s,T^{\star},R}
. Since
f
is continuously differentiable, we have that
the mapping
t\mapsto\Phi(v(\cdot))(t)
is continuously differentiable on

[0,T^{\star}+\tau]
. We will show that by properly choosing
\tau,R>0
,
\Phi:\mathcal{K}_{s,T^{\star},R}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}_{s,T^{\star},R}

is a contraction mapping with respect to the metric induced by the norm
\|\cdot\|_{s,T^{\star}}
. The appropriate choice of
\tau,R>0

will be specified below. First, We show that
\Phi
maps
\mathcal{K}_{s,T^{\star},R}
into
\mathcal{K}_{s,T^{\star},R}
. Indeed, let
v\in\mathcal{K}_{s,T^{\star},R}
. If
t\in[0,T^{\star}]
, then
v(\cdot,t)=u(\cdot,t)
. Hence
\Phi(v(\cdot))(t)=\Phi(u(\cdot))(t)=u(\cdot,t)
and there is nothing to prove. If
t\in[T^{\star},T^{\star}+\tau]
, then

\displaystyle\|\Phi(v)(\cdot,t)-u(\cdot,T^{\star})\|_{V_{1/2}}

Consequently,

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}m(\xi)(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-m(\xi)(t-\tau)^{\alpha})f(u(\xi,\tau))\;d\tau\right\|_{L^{l}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq\|f(u)\|_{L^{l}((0,T);L^{2}(\Omega))}+CT\|f(u(\cdot,0))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+CT^{\frac{1}{l}}\|f^{\prime}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}\,\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{\zeta}(0,T;V_{\tilde{\gamma}})}.

Thus, the claim is proved.

Next, we prove the estimated of the second derivative.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}u(\cdot,t)=

\displaystyle m(\cdot)t^{\alpha-2}E_{\alpha,\alpha-1}(-m(\cdot)t^{\alpha})u_{0}(\cdot)-m(\cdot)E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-m(\cdot)t^{\alpha})u_{1}(\cdot)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\alpha-2}E_{\alpha,\alpha-1}(-m(\cdot)(t-\tau)^{\alpha})f^{\prime}(u(\cdot,\tau))u_{\tau}(\cdot,\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle+t^{\alpha-2}E_{\alpha,\alpha-1}(-m(\cdot)t^{\alpha})f(u(\cdot,0)).

By hypothesis we have that
\alpha(1-\theta)-2+\frac{1}{\zeta^{\prime}}>0
. Then

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\alpha-2}E_{\alpha,\alpha-1}(-m(\cdot)(t-\tau)^{\alpha})f^{\prime}(u(\cdot,\tau))u_{\tau}(\cdot,\tau)d\tau\right\|_{V_{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\alpha-2-\alpha\theta}\|f^{\prime}(u(\cdot,\tau))u_{\tau}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\|f^{\prime}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\Omega))}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\alpha-2-\alpha\theta}\|u_{\tau}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{V_{\tilde{\gamma}}}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq CT^{\frac{1}{\zeta^{\prime}}+\alpha(1-\theta)-2}\|f^{\prime}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\Omega))}\,\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{\zeta}((0,T);V_{\tilde{\gamma}})}.

Moreover, proceeding as in the proof of (
5.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{t}^{2}u\right\|_{V_{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(T^{\alpha(\gamma-\theta)-2}\|u_{0}\|_{V_{\gamma}}+T^{\alpha(\tilde{\gamma}-\theta-1)}\|u_{1}\|_{V_{\tilde{\gamma}}}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.CT^{\frac{1}{\zeta^{\prime}}+\alpha(1-\theta)-2}\|f^{\prime}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\Omega))}\,\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{\zeta}((0,T);V_{\tilde{\gamma}})}+T^{\alpha-2-\alpha\theta}\|f(u(\cdot,0))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

The proof is finished.

∎

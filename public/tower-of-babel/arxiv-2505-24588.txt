On compact sets possessing q -convex functions
Thomas Pawlaschyk and Nikolay Shcherbina

Abstract
We show that there exists a q q -convex function in a neighborhood of a compact set K K in a complex manifold ℳ \mathcal{M} if and only if the q q -nucleus of this compact set is empty. The latter can be characterized as the maximal q q -pseudoconcave subset of K K , i.e., a subset of K K containing all other compact q q -pseudoconcave subsets in K K .

Introduction

Plurisubharmonic functions are crucial in the study of functions in several complex variables. They originate from (sub-)harmonic functions and were introduced by Lelong and Oka in the early 1940s. In the smooth case, these were generalized to
q
-convex functions by Rothstein
[
10
]
and Grauert
[
3
]
in the 1950s and, in the upper semi-continuous case, to
q
-plurisubhamonic functions by Hunt and Murray
[
6
]
in 1978. Andreotti and Grauert used them to study cohomology groups of
q
-convex complex manifolds and spaces in the 1960s (cf.
[
5
]
).
Plurisubharmonicity is strongly related to Kobayashi hyperbolicity of bounded sets, since the existence of strictly plurisubharmonic functions guarantees that the Kobayashi pseudo-metric is actually hyperbolic, i.e., a metric
[
7
]
. For this reason, the second named author of this paper examined the question on the existence of strictly plurisubharmonic functions in a open neighborhood of a given compact set
K
in complex manifolds
[
11
]
. It turned out that their existence is closely related to the emptiness of the
nucleus
, a subset of
K
which is constituted by the union of all pseudoconcave sets in
K
.
It is natural to ask whether a similar result transfers to
q
-convexity. In this paper we give necessary and sufficient conditions on the existence of strictly
q
-convex functions (with corners) in a neighborhood of a given compact set
K
. For this, we introduce the notion of the
q
-nucleus
of
K
which is eventually composed by all
q
-pseudoconcave subsets of
K
, i.e., complements of
q
-pseudoconvex sets in the sense of Rothstein
[
10
]
. Our main results states that there exists a
q
-convex function in a neighborhood of
K
if and only if the
q
-nucleus of
K
is empty (see Main Theorem).

The
q
-convexity in terms of spherical hats

For
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
we write
\|z\|_{\infty}:=\max\{|z_{j}|:1\leq j\leq n\}
and
\Delta^{n}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\|z\|_{\infty}<1\}
. Denote also the unit ball by
\mathbb{B}_{1}^{n}(0):=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\|z\|<1\}
, where
\|\cdot\|
is the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Fix integer numbers
k,m\geq 1
and let
0<r,s<1
. An Euclidean
(k,m)
-
Hartogs figure
is a set of the form

H^{k,m}=H^{k,m}_{r,s}:=\{z\in\Delta^{k}\times\Delta^{m}:\|(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})\|_{\infty}<r\ \mbox{or}\ \|(z_{k+1},\ldots,z_{k+m})\|_{\infty}>s\}.

In the following,
\mathcal{M}^{n}=\mathcal{M}
always denotes a complex manifold of dimension
n\geq 2
unless otherwise stated.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
q\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
be an integer number. An open set
\Omega
in
\mathcal{M}
is
q
-pseudoconvex
(in
\mathcal{M}
) if it admits the
Kontinuitätssatz
with respect to
(n-q)
-polydiscs, i.e. for every
(q,n-q)
-Hartogs figure
H^{q,n-q}
and every injective holomorphic map
\Phi:\Delta^{n}\to\mathcal{M}
such that
\Phi(H^{q,n-q})\subset\Omega
we have
\Phi(\Delta^{n})\subset\Omega
.

Remark 2.3
.

Notice that the above described
q
-pseudoconvexity is defined in the sense of Rothstein
[
10
]
. It is equivalent to the
(n{-}q{-}1)
-pseudoconvexity in the sense of Słodkowski
[
12
]
(see
[
8
]
for a list of equivalent notions of
q
-pseudoconvexity). In the case
q=n-1
, the
(n-1)
-pseduoconvexity in the sense of Rothstein is simply the classical pseudoconvexity.

We introduce another notion of generalized pseudoconvexity which is based on half spheres rather than Hartogs figures.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
0<r<1
be a real number and fix some integer number
1\leq k\leq n
. We consider the
spherical hat
defined as

\mathbb{S}_{r}^{k}:=\{z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})\in\mathbb{C}^{k}:\|z\|=1\ \hbox{and}\ \operatorname{Re}(z_{1})\geq r\}

and the
filled (spherical) hat

\widehat{\mathbb{S}}_{r}^{k}:=\{z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})\in\mathbb{C}^{k}:\|z\|\leq 1\ \hbox{and}\ \operatorname{Re}(z_{1})\geq r\}.

A pair of sets
(S^{k},\widehat{S}^{k})=(S,\widehat{S})
in
\mathcal{M}
is said to be a
spherical hat pair of order
k
if there exist a real number
0<r<1
, a filled spherical hat
\widehat{\mathbb{S}}_{r}^{k}
, an open neighborhood
U
of
\widehat{\mathbb{S}}_{r}^{k}\times\overline{\Delta^{n-k}}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and an injective holomorphic map
\Phi:U\to\mathcal{M}
such that

S=S^{k}:=\Phi(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{k}\times\Delta^{n-k})\quad\hbox{and}\quad\widehat{S}=\widehat{S}^{k}:=\Phi\big(\widehat{\mathbb{S}}_{r}^{k}\times\Delta^{n-k}\big).

We also denote by
\hbox{Int}(\widehat{S}^{k})
the image of the interior of
\widehat{\mathbb{S}}_{r}^{k}\times\Delta^{n-k}
by
\Phi
and call it the
filling of

S^{k}
.

Now we compare the two notions of
q
-pseudoconvexity defined above.

.

Let
\Omega

\mathcal{M}
. Then the following properties are equivalent.

1.

\Omega
is
q
-pseudoconvex.

2.

For every spherical hat pair
(S,\widehat{S})

(

\Omega
, but the complement of
\Omega
intersects
\Phi(\hbox{Int}(\widehat{\mathbb{S}}_{r}^{n-q+1}\times\Delta^{q-1}))

-pseudoconvex in

[

A_{t}:=\{(t+i\operatorname{Im}(p_{1}),z^{\prime})\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-q}:t^{2}+(\operatorname{Im}(p_{1}))^{2}+\|z^{\prime}\|^{2}<1\}\times\{p^{\prime\prime}\},

•

•

•

and

Then we can find

for the first time as

. But this contradicts the continuity principle with respect to

-dimensional analytic sets of the

\Omega
has to fulfill property (
2
).

(
2
) implies (
1
).
We prove again by contradiction. Assume that
\Omega
admits property (

-pseudoconvex. Then there is a
(q,n-q)

\Phi(H^{q,n-q}_{r,s})\subset\Omega
, but
\Phi(\Delta^{n})
intersects the complement of

.

Let

. Then, by assumption made on the Hartogs figure,
H_{r,s}^{q,n-q}\subset D
, but
(H_{r,s}^{q,n-q})^{c}\cap D^{c}\neq\emptyset
in

. Now let

This means that the closure of

intersects the boundary of

for the first time when the radius

. This intersection takes place at the boundary part of

in

which lies in

and let

be a domain such that

-coordinates to the

Now we can use the same technique as in the second part of the proof of Proposition 2.1 in

and construct a spherical hat pair
(S^{\prime},\hat{S}^{\prime})
of order

in

, and

-pseudoconvex.

In what follows, we study the relation of
q
-pseudoconvex sets and
q
-convex functions.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
q\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

1.

We say that a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth function
\psi
on
\mathcal{M}
is
weakly
q
-convex
if for every point
p
the Levi form of
\psi
at
p
has at most
q-1
negative eigenvalues. We say it is
q
-convex
if for every point
p
the Levi form of
\psi
at
p
has at most
q-1
non-positive eigenvalues.

2.

Furthermore,
\psi
is
(weakly)
q
-convex with corners
on
\mathcal{M}
if for each point
p
in
\mathcal{M}
there are a neighborhood
U
of
p
and finitely many (weakly)
q
-convex functions
\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{k}
on
\mathcal{M}
such that
\psi=\max\{\psi_{j}:j=1,\ldots,k\}
on
U
.

3.

We say that
\mathcal{M}
is
q
-complete with corners
if
\mathcal{M}
admits a
q
-convex with corners exhaustion function
\psi
, i.e.,
\{\psi<c\}\Subset\mathcal{M}
for every number
c\in\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 2.7
.

1. Counting the eigenvalues of the Levi form, it is immediate that a
q
-convex function is weakly
q
-convex. Furthermore, it is obvious that a weakly
q
-convex function is weakly
q
-convex with corners.

2. Hunt and Murray
[
6
]
proved that a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth function
\psi
defined on an open set
U
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is weakly
q
-convex if and only if it is
(q-1)
-plurisubharmonic
in their sense.

3. If
\mathcal{M}
is compact, there are no
q
-convex functions with corners on
\mathcal{M}
. Indeed, otherwise, if such a function, say
\psi
, exists, it attains a maximum in
\mathcal{M}
at some point
p
. Then there is a
q
-convex function
\psi_{j_{0}}
in a neighborhood
U
of
p
such that
\max\{\psi(z):z\in U\}=\psi_{j_{0}}(p)
. But all the eigenvalues of the Levi form of
\psi_{j_{0}}
at
p
have to be non-positive. This contradicts the definition of the
q
-convexity of
\psi_{j_{0}}
.

We are especially interested in complements of

-pseudoconvex sets.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
A
be a closed set in
\mathcal{M}
. Then
A
is called

-pseudoconcave (in

)
if
\mathcal{M}\setminus A
is

-pseudoconvex.

The following result is due to Słodkowski
[

. We give it here as an example for

-pseudoconcave sets.

Example 2.9
.

If
A
is an analytic subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that its dimension is at least
q
at each point
z\in A
, then
A
is
q
-pseudoconcave in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

The
q
-convex functions admit the local maximum principle on
q
-pseudoconcave sets.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
A
be a closed set in
\mathcal{M}
. Then the following properties are equivalent.

1.

A
is
q
-pseudoconcave in
\mathcal{M}
.

2.

For every
z
in
A
there exists an open neighborhood
V
of
z
in
\mathcal{M}
such that
A\cap V
is
q
-pseudoconcave in
V
.

3.

For every
z
in
A
there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
z
in
\mathcal{M}
such that for every compact set
L\Subset U
and every weakly
q
-convex function
\psi
defined in some neighborhood of

\max\{\psi(z):z\in A\cap L\}=\max\{\psi(z):z\in A\cap\partial L\}.

Notice that we set
\max\{\psi(z):z\in A\cap E\}=-\infty
, if
E
is some closed set in
\mathcal{M}
with
A\cap E=\emptyset
.

Proof.

The proof is based on the corresponding proofs of Theorems 4.2 and 5.1 in Słodkowski
[
12
]
. It can be found in Proposition 3.3 of
[
4
]
.

As an application of the local maximum principle, we derive an example for
q
-pseudoconvex sets using
(n-q)
-convex functions.

Proposition 2.11
.

Assume that an open set
\Omega
in
\mathcal{M}
admits an
(n-q)
-convex exhaustion function. Then
\Omega
is
q
-pseudoconvex.

Proof.

Let
H^{q,n-q}=H^{q,n-q}(r,s)
be a Hartogs figure with
\Phi(H^{q,n-q})\subset\Omega
. Now for
\zeta\in\Delta^{q}
and
s<\sigma<1
consider the polydiscs

A_{\zeta,\sigma}:=\{\zeta\}\times\Delta_{\sigma}^{n-q}.

Then

\displaystyle\Delta^{n}=H^{q,n-q}\cup\bigcup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\|\zeta\|_{\infty}<1}}{{s<\sigma<1}}}A_{\zeta,\sigma},\quad\hbox{and}\quad\partial A_{\zeta,\sigma}:=\{\zeta\}\times\partial\Delta_{\sigma}^{n-q}\ \text{lies in}\ H^{q,n-q}.

(1)

We can shrink
H^{q,n-q}
a little bit in order to get that
\Phi(H^{q,n-q})
lies relatively compact in
\Omega
. Let
\rho:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be an exhaustion function of
\mathcal{M}
. Then we can find a real constant
c
such that
\Phi(H^{q,n-q})
is contained in
\Omega_{c}:=\{z\in\Omega:\rho(z)<c\}
. In particular,
\rho\circ\Phi<c
on
\partial A_{\zeta,\sigma}
for every
|\zeta|<1
and
s<\sigma<1
by the inclusion in (
1
). Now the maximum principle for
q
-convex functions implies that
\rho\circ\Phi<c
on
A_{\zeta,\sigma}
for every
|\zeta|<1
and
s<\sigma<1
. By the identity in (
1
), we conclude that
\rho\circ\Phi<c
on
\Delta^{n}
, which yields
\Phi(\Delta^{n})\subset\Omega_{c}\subset\Omega
. This means that
\Omega
is
q
-pseudoconvex.

We are now able to establish the first criteria for the existence of
q
-convex sets on compacts.

Corollary 2.12
.

Let
K
be a compact
q
-pseudoconcave set in
\mathcal{M}
.

1.

If there exists a
q
-convex function
\psi
with corners in some open neighborhood of
K
, then
K
is empty.

2.

If
\mathcal{M}
is
q
-complete with corners, then
K
is empty. In particular, if
\mathcal{M}
is Stein, then there are no compact
q
-pseudoconcave sets in
\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

1. Assume that there exists a function
\psi
which is
q
-convex with corners on an open neighborhood of
K
and that
K
is non-empty. Then
\psi
admits a maximum at a point
p_{0}
in
K
, i.e.,

\psi(p_{0})=\max\{\psi(z):z\in K\}.

Choose a neighborhood
U
of
p_{0}
so small, that we can assume
U
to be a local chart on
\mathcal{M}
, and so that
\psi=\max\{\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\ldots,\psi_{k}\}
on
U
, where
\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\ldots,\psi_{k}
are
q
-convex functions on
U
. Pick one of these functions, say
\psi_{0}:=\psi_{j_{0}}
for some
j_{0}\in\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
, such that
\psi_{0}(p_{0})=\psi(p_{0})
. Then

\psi_{0}(p_{0})=\max\{\psi_{0}(z):z\in U\cap K\}.

Since
U
is a local chart, and since
q
-convexity is invariant under biholomorphic change of coordinates, we can assume that
U
lies inside the ball
\mathbb{B}_{R}^{n}(0)
with radius
R>0
centered at the origin in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
p_{0}=0
. Notice also that
q
-pseudoconcavity is invariant under biholomorphic changes of coordinates. In particular,
U\cap K
is
q
-pseudoconcave in
U
. Now let
B:=\mathbb{B}_{r}^{n}(0)\Subset U
be a smaller ball lying relatively compact in
U
and choose an
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that
\Psi_{0}:=\psi_{0}-\varepsilon_{0}\|z\|^{2}
is still
q
-convex on
U
. Clearly, since
B
contains
p_{0}=0
, we have

\Psi_{0}(0)=\max\{\Psi_{0}(z):z\in K\cap B\}.

Then, by Proposition
2.10
, we obtain

\psi_{0}(0)=\Psi_{0}(0)\leq\max\{\Psi_{0}(z):z\in K\cap B\}=\max\{\Psi_{0}(z):z\in K\cap\partial B\}

<\max\{\psi_{0}(z):z\in K\cap\partial B\}=\max\{\psi_{0}(z):z\in K\cap B\}=\psi_{0}(0).

This leads to a contradiction on the assumption made on
K
being nonempty. Therefore,
K
must be empty, and we have proven the first part of this proposition.

2. If
\mathcal{M}
is
q
-complete with corners, it admits a
q
-convex with corners exhaustion function. By the first part of this proposition,
K
must be empty. If
\mathcal{M}
is Stein, then it admits a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic (i.e.,
1
-convex) exhaustion function. Every
1
-convex function is automatically
q
-convex. Hence, any compact
q
-pseudoconcave set
K
must be empty.

3
The
q
-nucleus for compact sets

The
q
-pseudoconcavity gives already a condition on the existence of a
q
-convex function. The question remains whether it is possible to reverse statement 1 in Corollary
2.12
. For this, we construct a special kind of subset of a given set
K
, the
q
-nucleus of
K
, which is closely related on the existence of
q
-convex functions near
K
.

Definition 3.1
.

Fix an integer number
q\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
.

1.

Let
K^{\prime\prime}\subset K^{\prime}\subsetneq\mathcal{M}
be two proper compact sets in
\mathcal{M}
. We say that
K^{\prime\prime}

is obtained from
K^{\prime}
by a spherical cut of order
k
if there exists a spherical hat pair
(S,\widehat{S})
of order
k
such that

•

S\subset\mathcal{M}\setminus K^{\prime}
,

•

and
K^{\prime\prime}=K^{\prime}\setminus\hbox{Int}(\widehat{S})
.

2.

For two compacts
K^{\prime\prime}\subset K^{\prime}
we say that
K^{\prime\prime}
is obtained from
K^{\prime}
by a sequence of spherical cuts of order
k
, if there exists a finite sequence
\{K_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
of compact sets such that

•

K_{1}\supset K_{2}\supset\cdots\supset K_{m}
,

•

K_{1}=K^{\prime}
and
K^{\prime\prime}=K_{m}
,

•

and
K_{j+1}
is obtained from
K_{j}
by a spherical cut of order
k
for each
j=1,\ldots,m-1
.

3.

For a proper compact set
K\subsetneq\mathcal{M}
we define
\mathcal{F}_{K}^{q}
to be the set of all compacts
K^{\prime\prime}
which are obtained from
K
by a sequence of spherical cuts of order
n-q+1
. Then we define the
q
-nucleus
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)
of
K
as the intersection of all compacts
K^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{K}^{q}
, i.e.,

\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K):=\bigcap_{K^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{K}^{q}}K^{\prime\prime}.

4.

Otherwise, if
K=\mathcal{M}
, i.e., if
\mathcal{M}
is compact itself, we simply set
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(\mathcal{M}):=\mathcal{M}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Notice that the
q
-nucleus is a compact set, and that the
1
-nucleus corresponds to the
nucleus
introduced in
[
11
]
.

We need the following lemma to derive some important properties of the
q
-nucleus.

Lemma 3.3
.

For a finite family of sets
\{K^{\prime\prime}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\ell}
from
\mathcal{F}^{q}_{K}
, the intersection
\displaystyle\bigcap_{j=1}^{\ell}K^{\prime\prime}_{j}
also lies in
\mathcal{F}^{q}_{K}
.

Proof.

This follows directly from the above definition. For details we refer to Lemma 3.1 in
[
11
]
. Its proof can be transferred word by word to our setting.

Now we can give the relation of
q
-nucleus and
q
-pseudoconcavity.

Proposition 3.4
.

The
q
-nucleus of
K\subsetneq\mathcal{M}
is
q
-pseudoconcave in
\mathcal{M}
. Moreover, it is the maximal
q
-pseudoconcave subset of
K
. In particular, if
K
is
q
-pseudoconcave itself, then
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)=K
.

Proof.

If the
q
-nucleus is empty, there is nothing to show, so we assume it is non-empty. Assume that
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)
is not
q
-pseudoconcave.
According to Proposition
2.5
there exists a spherical hat pair
(S,\widehat{S})
of order
n{-}q{+}1
such that

•

S\cap\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)=\emptyset
,

•

but
\widehat{S}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)\neq\emptyset
.

Pick a point
p\in\widehat{S}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)
. Then, in view of Lemma 3.3 and the definition of the
q
-nucleus, there exists a set
K^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{F}_{K}^{q}
such that

•

S\cap K^{\prime}=\emptyset
,

•

but
\widehat{S}\cap K^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
. More precisely, this intersection contains
p
.

We set
K^{\prime\prime}:=K^{\prime}\setminus\hbox{Int}(\widehat{S})
. It is obvious that
p\notin K^{\prime\prime}
and that
K^{\prime\prime}
is obtained from
K^{\prime}
by a spherical cut of order
n{-}q{+}1
. Since
K^{\prime}
is obtained from
K
by a sequence of spherical cuts of order
n{-}q{+}1
, so is
K^{\prime\prime}
as well. This means that
K^{\prime\prime}
lies in
\mathcal{F}_{K}^{q}
. Hence, by the definition of the
q
-nucleus and the choice of
p
, we have that
p\in\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)\subset K^{\prime\prime}
, so
p\in K^{\prime\prime}
, a contradiction. Therefore,
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)
is
q
-pseudoconcave.

Now assume that there exists a
q
-pseudoconcave set
A\subset K
. Let
K^{\prime\prime}\subsetneq\mathcal{M}
be a compact sets in
\mathcal{M}
such that
K^{\prime\prime}
is obtained from
K
by a spherical cut of order
n{-}q{+}1
, i.e., there exists a spherical hat pair
(S,\widehat{S})
of order
n{-}q{+}1
such that
S\subset\mathcal{M}\setminus K
and
K^{\prime\prime}=K\setminus\hbox{Int}(\widehat{S})
. Since
S\subset\mathcal{M}\setminus K\subset\mathcal{M}\setminus A
and
A
is
q
-pseudoconcave,
\widehat{S}\cap A=\emptyset
due to Proposition
2.5
. Hence,
A
lies in
K^{\prime\prime}
. Similarly, by an inductive argument, we can derive that if
K^{\prime\prime}
is obtained from
K
by a
sequence
of spherical cuts of order
n{-}q{+}1
, then
A\subset K^{\prime\prime}
as well. But this means that
A\subset\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)
according to the definition of the
q
-nucleus of
K
. In this sense, the
q
-nucleus is maximal. It contains all
q
-pseudoconcave subsets of
K
. So if
K
is
q
-pseudoconcave itself, then
K\subset\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)\subset K
. Thus,
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)=K
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{q}(K)
denote the collection of all
q
-pseudoconcave subsets of
K\subsetneq\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{P}_{q}^{*}(K)
the collection of all open
q
-pseudoconvex sets
U
in
\mathcal{M}
such that
K\cap U=\emptyset
. Then

\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)=\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(K)}A,

or, equivalently,

\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)=\bigcap_{U\in\mathcal{P}_{q}^{*}(K)}U.

In particular,
\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)
is
q
-pseudoconvex in
\mathcal{M}
.

The
q
-nucleus is a biholomorphic invariant in the following sense.

Remark 3.6
.

Let
K
be a compact set in
\mathcal{M}
. For a given biholomorphic map
\Phi:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
, where
\mathcal{N}
is another complex manifold, and for a compact set
K
in
\mathcal{M}
, we have that

\Phi(\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K))=\mathfrak{n}_{q}(\Phi(K)).

This immediately follows from Corollary
3.5
and from the fact that an open set
U
in
\mathcal{M}
is
q
-pseudoconvex if and only
\Phi(U)
is
q
-pseudoconvex in
\Phi(\mathcal{M})=\mathcal{N}
.

We give an example of the
q
-nucleus in the projective manifold.

Example 3.7
.

Consider the embedded
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{q}
in
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n}
,

\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{q}=\{[z_{0},\ldots,z_{n}]\in\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n}:z_{q+1}=\ldots=z_{n}=0\}.

By computing the eigenvalues of

\rho([z_{0},\ldots,z_{n}])=\frac{|z_{0}|^{2}+\ldots+|z_{q}|^{2}}{|z_{q+1}|^{2}+\ldots+|z_{n}|^{2}}

using local charts, we find that
\rho
is an
(n-q)
-convex exhaustion function for
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{q}
. By Proposition
2.11
, the set
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{q}
is a
q
-pseudoconcave compact subset of
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Therefore, we can conclude that one has
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{q})=\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{q}
in
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

Together with Corollary
2.12
we obtain the following result. It states that the manifold cannot be too
nice
in the geometric and holomorphic sense and that
K
has to be large enough in order to produce a proper
q
-nucleus.

Corollary 3.8
.

The following two properties hold true:

1.

If
\mathcal{M}
is
q
-complete (such as Stein in the case
q=1
), then
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)
is empty for any compact set
K
in
\mathcal{M}
.

2.

If
K
is contained in a local holomorphic chart of
\mathcal{M}
, then
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)=\emptyset
.

Now we deal with the problem how to construct a
q
-convex function with corners in the neighborhood of
K
in the case when the
q
-nucleus of
K
is empty. First, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
K
be a compact set in
\mathcal{M}
and
V_{1},V_{2}
a pair of open sets in
\mathcal{M}
such that
K
is contained in their union
V_{1}\cup V_{2}
. Assume that there are two (weakly)
q
-convex functions with corners
\varphi_{j}
defined on some neighbourhoods of
\overline{V_{j}}
for
j=1,2
such that
\varphi_{1}>\varphi_{2}
on
\partial V_{2}\cap V_{1}\cap K
and
\varphi_{1}<\varphi_{2}
on
\partial V_{1}\cap V_{2}\cap K
. Define

\Psi:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\varphi_{1}\ \hbox{on}\ V_{1}\setminus V_{2}\\
\varphi_{2}\ \hbox{on}\ V_{2}\setminus V_{1}\\
\max\{\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\}\ \hbox{on}\ V_{1}\cap V_{2}\end{array}\right.

Then there is a neighborhood
W
of
K
in
V_{1}\cup V_{2}
such that
\Psi
is (weakly)
q
-convex with corners on
W
.

Proof.

It is obvious that
\max\{\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\}
is (weakly)
q
-convex with corners on
V_{1}\cap V_{2}
simply by definition. We show that it extends according to the definition of
\Psi
outside
K\cap V_{1}\cap V_{2}
. For this, consider the compact sets
M_{1}:=\partial V_{2}\cap V_{1}\cap K
and
M_{2}:=\partial V_{1}\cap V_{2}\cap K
. By the assumptions made on
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
, there is an open neighborhood
U_{1}
of
M_{1}
in
V_{1}\cup V_{2}
such that
\varphi_{1}>\varphi_{2}
on
U_{1}\cap V_{1}
. Thus,
\max\{\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\}=\varphi_{1}
on
U_{1}\cap V_{1}
and easily extends by
\varphi_{1}
into some open neighborhood
W_{1}
of
K\setminus V_{2}
to a
q
-convex function with corners. By the same arguments, we can extend
\max\{\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\}
by
\varphi_{2}
into some open neighborhood
W_{2}
of
K\setminus V_{1}
. Now denote by
\Psi
the above constructed extension of
\max\{\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\}
into the open neighborhood
W=W_{1}\cup W_{2}\cup(V_{1}\cap V_{2})
of
K
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
(S,\widehat{S})
be a spherical hat pair of order
n-q+1
in
\mathcal{M}
. Then there is a neighborhood
U
of the filled hat
\widehat{S}
and a weakly
q
-convex function
\varphi
on
U\setminus S
such that
\varphi
vanishes on
U\setminus\widehat{S}
and
\varphi
is positive and
q
-convex on the filling
\hbox{Int}(\widehat{S})
.

Proof.

In the case
q=1
, the result is covered by Corollary 2.1 in
[
11
]
, so we continue with the case
q>1
. According to Theorem 2.1 of the same paper there exists a domain
W
in
\mathbb{C}^{n-q+1}
and a smooth plurisubharmonic function
\tilde{\varphi}:W\to\mathbb{R}
such that

i.

\Pi_{-}:=\{z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-q+1})\in\mathbb{C}^{n-q+1}:\operatorname{Re}(z_{1})\leq 0\}\subset W
and
\mathbb{B}_{1}^{n-q+1}(0)\subset W
,

ii.

\tilde{\varphi}=0
on
\Pi_{-}
,

iii.

\tilde{\varphi}>0
and strictly plurisubharmonic on
W\setminus\Pi_{-}
,

Denote by
z^{\prime}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-q+1})\in\mathbb{C}^{n-q+1}
,
z^{\prime\prime}=(z_{n-q+2},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{q-1}
and
1^{\prime}=(1,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{C}^{n-q+1}
. Thus,
z=(z^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. For
r>0
, we define the function
\psi_{r}
as follows,

\psi_{r}(z):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}(1-\|z^{\prime\prime}\|^{2})\tilde{\varphi}\big(z^{\prime}-r\cdot 1^{\prime}\big),&\text{for}\ z\in\operatorname{Int}\big(\widehat{\mathbb{S}}_{r}^{n-q+1}\times\Delta^{q-1}\big)\\
0,&\text{for}\ z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus(\widehat{\mathbb{S}}_{r}^{n-q+1}\times\Delta^{q-1})\end{array}\right\}.

Then
\psi_{r}
has at least
n-q+1
non-negative (in
z^{\prime}
-direction) and, thus, at most
q-1
negative eigenvalues. Hence, it is weakly
q
-convex everywhere where it is defined. Moreover, for every
0<r<1
it fulfills

1.

\psi_{r}\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus(\widehat{\mathbb{S}}_{r}^{n-q+1}\times\Delta^{q-1})
.

2.

\psi_{r}
is positive and
q
-convex on the interior of
\widehat{\mathbb{S}}_{r}^{n-q+1}\times\Delta^{q-1}
.

Now we simply set
\varphi:=\psi_{r}\circ\Phi^{-1}
for the proper choice of
r
, where
\Phi
is the function from Definition
2.4
. It is easy to see that
\varphi
fulfills all the properties posed in the statement.

We are now able to formulate and prove the central result of this paper which is given by the following statement.

Main Theorem.
.

Let
K
be a compact set in
\mathcal{M}
.

1.

If there is a
q
-convex function with corners defined on a neighborhood of
K
, then
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)
is empty. In particular,
K
does not contain any
q
-pseudoconcave subset.

2.

If
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)
is empty, then there exists a (positive)
q
-convex function with corners defined on a neighborhood of
K
.

Proof.

1. Let
\psi
be a
q
-convex function with corners on a neighborhood of
K
. Notice that
K\subsetneq\mathcal{M}
due to point 3 in Remark
2.7
. Assume that
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)
is not empty. Since
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)
lies in
K
, the function
\psi
is also defined in a neighborhood of
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)
.
Since the
q
-nucleus is compact and
q
-pseudoconcave, the first part of Corollary
2.12
applied to
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)
(instead of
K
there) will lead us to a contradiction. This, together with Corollary
3.5
, proves the first part of our Main Theorem.

2. We are able to follow mainly the arguments in the second part of the proof of the Main Theorem in
[
11
]
except for the fact that we have to take maximums instead of sums in the last step. For the readers convenience we shall give a detailed proof here.

Since
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)
is empty, we can find a sequence
K_{1}\supset K_{2}\supset\ldots\supset K_{m-1}\supset K_{m}
such that
K_{1}=K
,
K_{m}=\emptyset
and
K_{j}
is obtained from
K_{j-1}
by a spherical cut of order
n{-}q{+}1
. We proceed inductively in order to construct for each
j=m-1,m-2,\ldots,1
an open neighborhood
U_{j}
of
K_{j}
and a positive
q
-convex function
\Psi_{j}
with corners on
U_{j}
.

We start with the base case. We know that
K_{m}
is obtained from
K_{m-1}
by a spherical cut of order
n-q+1
, i.e., there exists a spherical hat pair
(S,\widehat{S})
of order
n-q+1
such that
S\subset\mathcal{M}\setminus K_{m-1}
and
K_{m}=K_{m-1}\setminus\hbox{Int}(\widehat{S})
. By Proposition
3.10
there exist a neighborhood
U
of the filled hat
\widehat{S}
and a weakly
q
-convex function
\Psi_{m-1}:=\varphi_{m-1}
on
U\setminus S
such that
\varphi_{m-1}
vanishes on
U\setminus\widehat{S}
and is positive and
q
-convex on
U_{m-1}:=\hbox{Int}(\widehat{S})
.

We proceed with the induction step and assume that for some
j\in\{2,3,\ldots,m-1\}
we have already constructed an open neighborhood
U_{j}
of
K_{j}
and a positive
q
-convex function
\Psi_{j}
with corners on
U_{j}
. We set
V_{1}:=U_{j}
. Since
K_{j}
is obtained from
K_{j-1}
by a spherical cut of order
n-q+1
, there exists a spherical hat pair
(S,\widehat{S})
of order
n-q+1
such that
S\subset\mathcal{M}\setminus K_{j-1}
and
K_{j}=K_{j-1}\setminus\hbox{Int}(\widehat{S})
.

By Proposition
3.10
there exist a neighborhood
U
of the filled hat
\widehat{S}
and a weakly
q
-convex function
\Psi_{j-1}:=\varphi_{j-1}
on
U\setminus S
such that
\varphi_{j-1}
vanishes on
U\setminus\widehat{S}
and is positive and
q
-convex on the filling
V_{2}:=\hbox{Int}(\widehat{S})
. Observe that
K_{j-1}
is contained in
V_{1}\cup V_{2}
.

Since
\varphi_{j-1}
vanishes on
\partial V_{2}\cap K_{j-1}
, and since
\Psi_{j}
is positive, we conclude that
\Psi_{j}>c\varphi_{j-1}
on
\partial V_{2}\cap V_{1}\cap K_{j-1}
for every positive constant
c>0
. On the other hand, since
\varphi_{j-1}
positive on
V_{2}
, we can find a constant
c>0
so large, that
\Psi_{j}<c\varphi_{j-1}
on
\partial V_{1}\cap V_{2}\cap K_{j-1}
. Notice that we can shrink
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
so that
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are bounded and
\Psi_{j}
and
\varphi_{j}
are continuous up to the boundary of
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
.

By Lemma
3.9
, we are now able to glue
\Psi_{j}
and
c\varphi_{j-1}
to a positive
q
-convex function with corners
\Psi_{j-1}
on some neighborhood
U_{j-1}
of
K_{j-1}
.

Proceeding in such a manner inductively, we can construct a function
\Psi_{1}
which is
q
-convex with corners on some neighborhood
U_{1}
of
K_{1}=K
, which completes the proof of our Main Theorem.

The Main Theorem implies conditions on the existence of the
q
-nucleus in
q
-convex manifolds.

Remark 3.11
.

If
\mathcal{M}
is a
q
-convex manifold
, i.e., if there exists a
q
-convex exhaustion function defined on
\mathcal{M}\setminus L
for some compact set
L
in
\mathcal{M}
, then the Main Theorem asserts that
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)=\emptyset
for every compact set
K
in
\mathcal{M}\setminus L
. Therefore, if there exists a compact
K
in
\mathcal{M}
such that
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)\neq\emptyset
, then necessarily
K
intersects the boundary of
\mathcal{M}\setminus L
in
\mathcal{M}
. Furthermore, since
\Omega:=\mathcal{M}\setminus L
is
q
-complete, we have that
L
itself is
q
-pseudoconcave, and then
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(L)=L
by Proposition
3.4
.

4
The
q
-nucleus for closed sets

Before we define the
q
-nucleus for closed sets, we need the following monotonicity property of the
q
-nucleus for compact sets. From now on, we always assume that
q\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Given two compact sets
K
,
L
in
\mathcal{M}
with
K\subset L
, we have
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)\subset\mathfrak{n}_{q}(L)
.

Proof.

According to Proposition
3.4
, the
q
-nucleus of
K
is a
q
-pseudoconcave set in
K\subset L
. Since, due to Proposition
3.5
,
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(L)
is maximal and contains all
q
-pseudoconcave sets in
L
, we derive that
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)\subset\mathfrak{n}_{q}(L)
.

Assume from now on that
\mathcal{M}
is locally compact. Let
A
be a closed set in
\mathcal{M}
. Let
\{K_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{L_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
be sequences of compact sets in
\mathcal{M}
with
K_{k}\subset K_{k+1}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathcal{M}=\bigcup_{k}K_{k}
and
L_{m}\subset L_{m+1}
for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathcal{M}=\bigcup_{m}L_{m}
. Then for each
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
m_{\nu},k_{\nu}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
K_{\nu}\subset L_{m_{\nu}}
and
L_{\nu}\subset K_{k_{\nu}}
. By the monotonicity of the
q
-nucleus (Lemma
4.1
), we obtain

\mathfrak{n}_{q}(A\cap K_{\nu})\subset\mathfrak{n}_{q}(A\cap L_{m_{\nu}})\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mathfrak{n}_{q}(A\cap L_{\nu})\subset\mathfrak{n}_{q}(A\cap K_{k_{\nu}}).

It follows that

\overline{\bigcup_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{n}_{q}(A\cap K_{\nu})}\ \subset\ \overline{\bigcup_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{n}_{q}(A\cap L_{m_{\nu}})}\ \subset\ \overline{\bigcup_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{n}_{q}(A\cap L_{\nu})}

\subset\ \overline{\bigcup_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{n}_{q}(A\cap K_{k_{\nu}})}\ \subset\ \overline{\bigcup_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{n}_{q}(A\cap K_{\nu})}.

From this, we derive the identity
\displaystyle\overline{\bigcup_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{n}_{q}(A\cap K_{\nu})}=\overline{\bigcup_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{n}_{q}(A\cap L_{\nu})}
, i.e. the set
\displaystyle\overline{\bigcup_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{n}_{q}(A\cap K_{\nu})}
does not depend on the choice of the exhaustion
\{K_{\nu}\}_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\{K_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an arbitrary sequence of compact sets
\{K_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
K_{k}\subset K_{k+1}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathcal{M}=\bigcup_{k}K_{k}
. Then we define the
q
-nucleus for a closed set
A
in
\mathcal{M}
by

\mathfrak{n}_{q}(A):=\overline{\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{n}_{q}(A\cap K_{k})}.

We give a necessary condition on the existence of
q
-convex functions near closed sets.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
A
be a closed set in
\mathcal{M}
. If there exists a
q
-convex function with corners in an open neighborhood
U
on
A
, then
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(A)
is empty.

Proof.

If there exists a
q
-convex function with corners on a neighborhood
U
of
A
, then
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(A\cap K)
is empty for every compact set
K\subset\mathcal{M}
according to the Main Theorem of this paper. Thus, the
q
-nucleus of
A
has to be empty.

We present a rather obvious example in the Stein case.

Example 4.4
.

If
\mathcal{M}
is Stein and
A
is a properly embedded complex submanifold of
\mathcal{M}
, then, since a Stein manifold always admits a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic (i.e. 1-convex) exhaustion function, we can apply Proposition 4.3 with
\mathcal{M}
on the place of
U
to conclude that the
q
-nucleus of
A
is empty. Recall here, that 1-convex function is automatically
q
-convex.

The next observation follows directly from the definition.

Proposition 4.5
.

The
q
-nucleus of a closed set
A\subsetneq\mathcal{M}
is
q
-pseudoconcave in
\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(A)=\hbox{closure\ of}\ \bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{n}_{q}(A\cap K_{n})
and the closure of the union of arbitrary many
q
-pseudoconcave sets is again
q
-pseudoconcave, the
q
-nucleus of
A
is
q
-pseudoconcave in
A
.

An analog of Corollary
3.5
does not hold for the
q
-nucleus of closed, not necessarily compact sets.

Remark 4.6
.

The
q
-nucleus of a closed set
A
is not necessarily the maximal
q
-pseudoconcave subset of
A
. Indeed, if it would be maximal, then

\bigcup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(A)}P=\mathfrak{n}_{q}(A),

where
\mathcal{P}_{q}(A)
denotes the collection of all
q
-pseudoconcave subsets of
A
. But if we consider the case
A=\mathbb{C}^{n}
in
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the set
A
can be filled by complex submanifolds of dimension
q
. Such manifolds are
q
-pseudoconcave (see Example
2.9
). Hence,
\bigcup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathcal{M})}P=\mathbb{C}^{n}
, whereas
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})=\emptyset
due to the existence of a strictly plurisubmarmonic function on the whole of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, e.g.,
\psi(z)=\|z\|^{2}
.

In fact, the
q
-nucleus of
A
is maximal with respect to
compact

q
-pseudoconcave subset of
A
. So if we denote by
\mathcal{P}^{c}_{q}(\mathcal{M})
the collection of all compact
q
-pseudoconcave subsets of
A
, we have

\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{P}^{c}_{q}(\mathcal{M})}A=\mathfrak{n}_{q}(A).

We end this section with the following remark.

Remark 4.7
.

If
S:=\mathfrak{n}_{n-1}(\mathcal{M})
is neither empty nor the whole of
\mathcal{M}
, it is a proper
(n-1)
-pseudoconcave set in
\mathcal{M}
. Since
(n-1)
-pseudoconcave means pseudoconcave, the set
\mathcal{M}\setminus S
is pseudoconvex, and in this sense,
S
can be considered as an analytic multifunction.

5
Open questions

The second part of our Main Theorem asserts that if the nucleus
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)
of a compact set
K\subset\mathcal{M}
is empty, then there is a
q
-convex function with corners defined on a neighborhood of
K
. In the case
q=1
the main result in
[
11
]
gives a better statement, namely, that if
\mathfrak{n}_{1}(K)=\emptyset
, then there is a smooth
1
-convex function in a neighbourhood of
K
. It is well known, due to the results in
[
2
]
, that for
q>1
, in general, functions with corners can not be approximated by smooth
q
-convex functions. This does not mean, however, that in our case such smooth
q
-convex function (maybe obtained by a completely different method) does not exist. This motivates our first question.

Question 1.
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a complex manifold and
K\subset\mathcal{M}
be a compact set such that for some
q>1
one has
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(K)=\emptyset
. Is it always true that there exists a
smooth

q
-convex function defined in an open neighborhood of
K
?

Our next question is concerned with the
q
-nucleus for closed sets and it asks if the reverse to the statement of Proposition
4.3
is true or not.

Question 2.
.

Let
q\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
and let
A
be a closed set in
\mathcal{M}
. Assume that
\mathfrak{n}_{q}(A)=\emptyset
. Is it always true that there exists a
q
-convex function with corners defined in an open neighborhood of
A
?

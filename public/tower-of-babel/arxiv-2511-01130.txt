Boundary estimates for a fully nonlinear Yamabe problem on Riemannian manifolds
Weisong Dong, Yanyan Li, Luc Nguyen

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the Dirichlet boundary value problem for fully nonlinear Yamabe equations on Riemannian manifolds with boundary. Assuming the existence of a subsolution, we derive a priori boundary second derivative estimates and consequently obtain the existence of a smooth solution. Moreover, with respect to a family of equations interpolating the fully nonlinear Yamabe equation and the classical semi-linear Yamabe equation, our estimates remain uniform. Finally, an example of a C 1 C^{1} solution which is smooth in the interior but not smooth at the boundary is also given. Keywords. Fully nonlinear Yamabe problem, Boundary estimates, Dirichlet problem. Mathematical Subject Classification (2020). 35J60; 35B45; 53C18; 53C21.

Introduction

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
with non-empty smooth boundary
\partial M\neq\emptyset
.
The Schouten tensor of
g
is defined as

A_{g}:=\frac{1}{n-2}\Big(\mathrm{Ric}_{g}-\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\mathrm{R}_{g}g\Big),

where
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}
and
\mathrm{R}_{g}
are respectively the Ricci tensor and the scalar curvature of
the metric
g
. The Schouten tensor plays an important role in conformal geometry due to its appearance in the Ricci decomposition of the Riemann curvature tensor.

Consider the problem of finding on
M
a metric
\tilde{g}
conformal to
g
with a prescribed
symmetric function of the eigenvalues of the Schouten tensor
A_{\tilde{g}}
and a prescibed Dirichlet boundary data
\tilde{g}|_{\partial M}
. More precisely, given a function
\psi>0
defined on
M
and a
Riemannian metric
h
on
\partial M
which is conformal to
g|_{\partial M}
, the induced metric on
\partial M
,
one looks for a metric
\tilde{g}
on
M
conformal to
g
such that
\tilde{g}|_{\partial M}=h
and the Schouten tensor
A_{\tilde{g}}
satisfies

f(\lambda_{\tilde{g}}(A_{\tilde{g}}))=\psi(x)\;\mbox{in}\;M,

(1.1)

where
\lambda_{\tilde{g}}(A_{\tilde{g}})=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
are the eigenvalues of
A_{\tilde{g}}
with respect to
\tilde{g}
and the symmetric function
f
shall be defined later. Note that, under a conformal deformation of metrics
\tilde{g}=e^{-2u}g
, the Schouten tensor transforms according to
the formula

A_{\tilde{g}}=\nabla^{2}u+du\otimes du-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|_{g}^{2}g+A_{g},

where
\nabla
denotes the covariant derivative of
g
. If
h=e^{-2\varphi}g|_{\partial M}
for a function
\varphi\in C^{2}(\partial M)
and
f
is homogeneous of degree one,
the problem is equivalent to finding a function
u
on
M

satisfying

\left\{\begin{aligned} f(\lambda_{g}(A_{\tilde{g}}))=&\;\psi(x)e^{-2u}\;&\mbox{in}\;M,\\
u=&\;\varphi\;&\mbox{on}\;\partial M,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

where
\lambda_{g}(A_{\tilde{g}})=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
are the eigenvalues of
A_{\tilde{g}}
with respect to
g
.

Let us now give our assumptions on the symmetric function
f
, following Caffarelli, Nirenberg and Spruck
[
1

.
Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open convex symmetric cone with vertex at the origin satisfying

\Gamma_{n}\subset\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{1}
, where
\Gamma_{n}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\lambda_{i}>0,\forall 1\leq i\leq n\}

and
\Gamma_{1}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}>0\}
.
Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap C^{0}(\bar{\Gamma})
be a function which is symmetric in
\lambda_{i}

and satisfies
the following structural conditions:

\displaystyle f>0\;\mbox{in}\;\Gamma\;\mbox{and}\;f=0\;\mbox{on}\;\partial\Gamma;

(f1)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}f:=\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}>0,\;\forall\;1\leq i\leq n;

(f2)

\displaystyle f\;\mbox{is concave in}\;\Gamma;

(f3)

\displaystyle f\;\mbox{is homogeneous of degree one, i.e. }\;f(s\lambda)=sf(\lambda),\;\forall\;s>0.

(f4)

We remark that (
f2
) ensures that the equation (
1.2
) is elliptic and
(
f3
) enables us to use Evans-Krylov’s Theorem.

Note that, if we define

e=(1,\ldots,1),

then, by the homogeneity and concavity of
f
,

\sum_{i}\partial_{i}f(\lambda)=f(\lambda)+Df(\lambda)\cdot(e-\lambda)\geq f(e)>0\;\mathrm{in}\;\Gamma.

(f5)

By the symmetry and homogeneity of
f
, we have
Df(e)=\frac{1}{n}f(e)e
. Together with the concavity and once again the homogeneity of
f
, this gives

f(\lambda)\leq f\big(\frac{1}{n}\sigma_{1}(\lambda)e\big)+Df\big(\frac{1}{n}\sigma_{1}(\lambda)e\big)\cdot(\lambda-\frac{1}{n}\sigma_{1}(\lambda)e)=\frac{1}{n}\sigma_{1}(\lambda)f(e).

(f6)

Classical examples satisfying (
f1
)-(
f4
) are
\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}(\lambda)
for
1\leq k\leq n

and the quotient
\Big(\frac{\sigma_{k}(\lambda)}{\sigma_{\ell}(\lambda)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{k-\ell}}
for
1\leq\ell<k\leq n

defined on the open convex symmetric cone
\Gamma_{k}
(see
[
1
]
),
where
\sigma_{k}
is the
k
-th elementary symmetric function

\sigma_{k}(\lambda):=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}}

and

\Gamma_{k}:=\{\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\sigma_{j}(\lambda)>0,\forall 1\leq j\leq k\}.

When
f=\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}
and
\psi=1
, equation (
1.1
) is known as the

(1.3)

where

\Gamma_{t}=\big\{\lambda:t\lambda+(1-t)\sigma_{1}(\lambda)e\in\Gamma\big\}

1.2

1.3

[

is smooth at

\partial M
. In other words, the solution in
[

belongs to
C^{\infty}(M)
.

We shall study a Dirichlet problem with a slightly more general right hand side than that in (
1.3
). For a function
u\in C^{2}(M)
, let

W[u]:=A_{e^{-2u}g}=\nabla^{2}u+du\otimes du-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}g+A_{g},

and let
\lambda_{g}(W[u])=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
denote the eigenvalues of
W[u]
with respect to the metric
g
. When it is clear from the context, we sometimes write
\lambda(W[u])
in place of
\lambda_{g}(W[u])
. Given
0<\psi(x,z)\in C^{\infty}(M\times\mathbb{R})
with
\psi_{z}\leq 0
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\partial M)
,
consider the Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} f_{t}(\lambda_{g}(W[u]))=&\;\psi(x,u),\;&\mbox{in}\;M,\\
u=&\;\varphi,\;&\mbox{on}\;\partial M.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.6)

Note that, by (
f3
), (
f4
) and (
f6
),

f_{t}(\lambda)\geq tf(\lambda)+(1-t)\sigma_{1}(\lambda)f(e)\geq(t+n(1-t))f(\lambda)\geq f(\lambda).

We therefore say
\underline{u}\in C^{2}(M)
is a
subsolution
to (
1.6
) for all
t\in[0,1]
if
\underline{u}
satisfies that

\left\{\begin{aligned} f(\lambda_{g}(W[\underline{u}]))\geq&\;\psi(x,\underline{u}),\;&\mbox{in}\;M,\\
\underline{u}=&\;\varphi,\;&\mbox{on}\;\partial M.\end{aligned}\right.

The following is our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
with non-empty smooth boundary
\partial M\neq\emptyset
. Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open convex symmetric cone with vertex at the origin satisfying

\Gamma_{n}\subset\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{1}
and
f\in C^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap C^{0}(\bar{\Gamma})
be a symmetric function
satisfying (
f1
)-(
f4
). Let
\psi(x,z)\in C^{\infty}(M\times\mathbb{R})
be positive with
\psi_{z}\leq 0
, and suppose that there exists a subsolution
\underline{u}\in C^{4}(M)
to the Dirichlet problem (
1.6
) for all
t\in[0,1]
.
Then, for every
t\in[0,1]
there exists a solution
u_{t}\in C^{\infty}(M)
to the Dirichlet problem (
1.6
) satisfying
u_{t}\geq\underline{u}
.
Moreover,
we have the estimate

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\sup_{M}(|u_{t}|+|\nabla u_{t}|+|\nabla^{2}u_{t}|)\leq C,

where
C
only depends on
\underline{u}
,
(M,g)
,
(f,\Gamma)
,
\psi
and
\varphi
.

Our main contribution concerns the uniformity of our estimates with respect to
t
. Available estimates in the literature for a single pair
(f,\Gamma)
depends on the type of
f
and/or
\Gamma
(see Section
2
for terminology). As
t\nearrow 1^{-}
, the type of
f_{t}
and/or
\Gamma_{t}
may change, and this calls for care when considering uniform estimates in this limit. Our proof uses a particular property of the family
(f_{t},\Gamma_{t})
, namely Lemma
2.2
in Section
2
. If one replaces the family
(f_{t},\Gamma_{t})
by another (suitably continuous) family
(\tilde{f}_{t},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t})
which tends to
(f,\Gamma)
as
t\nearrow 1^{-}
but the property in Lemma
2.2
does not hold for
(\tilde{f}_{t},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t})
, it is not clear if our proof would carry over.

We now give a sketch of the proof of Theorem
1.1
. In the discussion, let
C
denote a positive constant depending only on the known data such as
\underline{u},(M,g),(f,\Gamma),\psi
and
\varphi
, but may be different from lines to lines. In particular, the constants
C
will be independent of
t\in[0,1]
. The existence of a solution to equation (
1.6
) can be proved by the standard degree theory once
a priori
estimates have been established, see
[
19
]
.
To this end, we need
a priori
estimates for
u_{t}
with
u_{t}\geq\underline{u}

solving
(
1.6
)
on
M

up to its second order derivatives, as higher order estimates follow from Evans-Krylov’s Theorem and Schauder theory.
Local interior first and second order derivative estimates has been known. (See also
[
9
,
10
]
for local pointwise second derivative estimates for strong solutions.) Since
u_{t}\geq\underline{u}
in
M
and
u_{t}=\underline{u}
on
\partial M
, we obviously have

\min_{M}u_{t}\geq\min_{M}\underline{u}\;\mbox{and}\;\min_{\partial M}\nabla_{\nu}u_{t}\geq\min_{\partial M}\nabla_{\nu}\underline{u}.

Lemma 3.1 in
[
19
]
yields that
\max_{M}u_{t}\leq C
. Then, Lemma 3.2 and Theorem 3.3 in
[
19
]
ensure that

\sup_{M}(|u_{t}|+|\nabla u_{t}|)\leq C
.
For the estimate of second order derivatives, Theorem 3.4 in
[
19
]
shows that

\sup_{M}|\nabla^{2}u_{t}|\leq C(1+\sup_{\partial M}|\nabla^{2}u_{t}|).

We should point out that all the above mentioned results in
[
19
]
are valid in the present setting
as the proof only used the assumptions (
f1
)-(
f4
). To complete this argument, it therefore remains to establish a boundary second derivative esimate, namely a bound for
\sup_{\partial M}|\nabla^{2}u_{t}|
. Therefore, the proof of Theorem
1.1
reduces to that of the following result.

Proposition 1.2
.

Suppose the assumptions in Theorem
1.1
hold.
Then, for any solution
u_{t}\in C^{3}(M)
to the Dirichlet problem (
1.6
) with
t\in[0,1]
satisfying
u_{t}\geq\underline{u}
, we have

\sup_{\partial M}|\nabla^{2}u_{t}|\leq C,

(1.7)

where
C
only depends on
\underline{u}
,
(M,g)
,
(f,\Gamma)
,
\psi
and
\varphi
.

As pointed out earlier, our main contribution concerns a bound for
\sup_{\partial M}|\nabla^{2}u_{t}|
which is independent of
t\in[0,1]
. The estimation of double tangential derivatives and mixed tangential-normal derivatives follows largely the arguments in
[
18
,
19
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
. We note that
t
-dependent estimate of the double normal derivatives for general
f
for
t<1
was done in
[
43
]
. However, to achieve a uniform estimate as
t\nearrow 1^{-}
requires new ideas.

) and (

). For some
\ell>0
to be specified, let

\displaystyle=[-\ell,\ell]\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

\displaystyle=dt^{2}+h,

.

Let

. For every

, there exists

such that the problem

\begin{cases}\sigma_{k}(\lambda_{g}(W[u]))=\frac{n}{k2^{k}}\tbinom{n-1}{k-1}e^{-2ku},\quad\lambda_{g}(W[u])\in\Gamma_{k}&\text{ in }M_{\ell}\setminus\partial M_{\ell},\\
u=c&\text{ on }\partial M_{\ell},\\
\end{cases}

(1.8)

admits a solution

The failure of smoothness of the solution in Example

is similar to that in

.

does not lie above any smooth subsolution that we are aware of. We also do not know if it can be approximated by a sequence of smooth solutions. We conclude the introduction with two related questions on solutions of (

2.1
Some facts on
(f,\Gamma)
and
(f_{t},\Gamma_{t})

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open convex symmetric cone with vertex at the origin satisfying

\Gamma_{n}\subset\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{1}
and
f\in C^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap C^{0}(\bar{\Gamma})
be a symmetric function. In this subsection, we collect various properties for
(f,\Gamma)
and
(f_{t},\Gamma_{t})
which will be used in the proof of Proposition
1.2
. Most of these properties have appeared elsewhere. We first recall a definition in
[
1
]
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
1
]
)
.

\Gamma
is said to be of type 1 if the positive
\lambda_{i}
axes belong to
\partial\Gamma
; otherwise it
is said to be of type 2.

Denote by
\Gamma^{\prime}
the projection of the cone
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}={\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}
onto
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}

and by
\lambda^{\prime}
the projection of
\lambda\in\Gamma
onto
\Gamma^{\prime}
.
If
\Gamma
is of type 1,
\Gamma^{\prime}
is an open convex symmetric cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
and

\{\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}:\lambda^{\prime}_{\alpha}>0,1\leq\alpha\leq n-1\}\subset\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\{\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}:\lambda^{\prime}_{1}+\cdots+\lambda^{\prime}_{n-1}>0\}.

If
\Gamma
is of type 2,
\Gamma^{\prime}=\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
.

We note that, regardless whether
\Gamma
is of type 1 or of type 2, the cones
\Gamma_{t}
are always of type
2
for
t\in[0,1)
. In fact, we have:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open convex symmetric cone with vertex at the origin satisfying

\Gamma_{n}\subset\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{1}
. For
t\in[0,1)
, then cone
\Gamma_{t}
contains the open ball of radius
\frac{1}{2n}(1-t)
about
(0,\ldots,0,1)
. Moreover, if
f\in C^{\infty}(\Gamma)
is a symmetric function satisfying (
f2
) and (
f4
), then

f_{t}(\lambda)\geq\frac{1}{2}(1-t)f(e)

for any
\lambda
in this ball.

Proof.

For two vectors
\lambda,\tilde{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we write
\lambda\geq\tilde{\lambda}
if
\lambda-\tilde{\lambda}\in\bar{\Gamma}_{n}
. For
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
|v|=1
,
r>0
, we have

(0,\ldots,0,1)+rv\geq(-r,\ldots,-r,1-r)

Thus, we only need to show that
\lambda:=(-\frac{1}{2n}(1-t),\ldots,-\frac{1}{2n}(1-t),1-\frac{1}{2n}(1-t))\in\Gamma_{t}
and
f_{t}(\lambda)\geq\frac{1}{2}(1-t)f(e)
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle t\lambda+(1-t)\sigma_{1}(\lambda)e

\displaystyle=(0,\ldots,0,t)+\frac{(1-t)(n+(n-1)t)}{2n}e

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1-t)(n+(n-1)t)}{2n}e\in\Gamma_{n}\subset\Gamma,

which implies
\lambda\in\Gamma_{t}
and

f_{t}(\lambda)=f(t\lambda+(1-t)\sigma_{1}(\lambda)e)\geq\frac{1}{2}(1-t)f(e).

The proof is complete.
∎

In case
\Gamma
is of type
1
,
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a proper subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
. Let
d_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
denote the distance function to
\partial\Gamma^{\prime}
. For
\lambda_{0}^{\prime}\in\partial\Gamma^{\prime}
, let
N_{\lambda_{0}^{\prime}}(\partial\Gamma^{\prime})
denote the set of unit vectors
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
such that
\Gamma^{\prime}
is contained in the half-space
\{\lambda^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}:\gamma\cdot(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda_{0}^{\prime})>0\}
. We list some properties of

and

which we will use later on.

•

Since
\partial\Gamma^{\prime}
contains the ray
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\lambda_{0}^{\prime}
,
\gamma\cdot\lambda_{0}^{\prime}=0
.

•

It follows that
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\{\lambda^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}:\gamma\cdot\lambda^{\prime}>0\}
.

•

Since
\Gamma^{\prime}\supset\Gamma_{n}^{\prime}
, this further implies that
\gamma_{\alpha}\geq 0
for

.

•

For any ascending
\lambda^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{\prime}
,

d_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(\lambda^{\prime})=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{ascending }\lambda_{0}^{\prime}\in\partial\Gamma^{\prime}\\
\text{descending }\gamma\in N_{\lambda_{0}^{\prime}}(\partial\Gamma^{\prime})\end{subarray}}\gamma\cdot\lambda^{\prime}.

(2.1)

The first three properties are immediate. To see the fourth property, denote the right hand side of (
2.1
) as
\tilde{d}(\lambda^{\prime})
. Note that
\tilde{d}(\lambda^{\prime})
is well-defined since there clearly exists an ascending
\lambda_{0}^{\prime}\in\partial\Gamma^{\prime}
(e.g.
(0,\ldots,0,1)
) and the existence of a descending
\gamma
in
N_{\lambda_{0}^{\prime}}(\partial\Gamma^{\prime})
is given by
[
1
, Lemma 6.1]
. The inequality
\tilde{d}(\lambda^{\prime})\geq d_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(\lambda^{\prime})
is a consequence of the fact that
\Gamma^{\prime}
is supported by the hyperplane through
\lambda_{0}^{\prime}
and normal to
\gamma
. To see the reverse inequality, let
\lambda^{\prime}_{*}\in\partial\Gamma^{\prime}
be such that
d_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(\lambda^{\prime})=|\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda_{*}^{\prime}|
. Let
\lambda^{\prime}_{**}
be an ascending rearrangement of
\lambda_{*}^{\prime}
. By symmetricity of
\Gamma^{\prime}
, we have that
\lambda_{**}^{\prime}\in\partial\Gamma^{\prime}
. Since
\lambda^{\prime}
is ascending, a simple induction argument on
n
gives

\lambda^{\prime}\cdot\lambda_{**}^{\prime}\geq\lambda^{\prime}\cdot\lambda_{*}^{\prime},

which, in view of the fact that
|\lambda_{*}^{\prime}|=|\lambda_{**}^{\prime}|
, implies

|\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda_{**}^{\prime}|\leq|\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda_{*}^{\prime}|.

It follows that
d_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(\lambda^{\prime})=|\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda_{*}^{\prime}|=|\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda_{**}^{\prime}|
. Since any hyperplane at
\lambda_{**}^{\prime}
supporting
\Gamma^{\prime}
must support the ball centered at
\lambda^{\prime}
of radius
|\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda_{**}^{\prime}|
, we see that
N_{\lambda_{**}^{\prime}}(\partial\Gamma^{\prime})
contains a unique element, namely
\gamma_{**}:=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda_{**}^{\prime}}{|\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda_{**}^{\prime}|}
. By
[
1
, Lemma 6.1]
,
\gamma_{**}
is descending. It follows that

\tilde{d}(\lambda^{\prime})\leq\gamma_{**}\cdot\lambda^{\prime}=\gamma_{**}\cdot(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda_{**}^{\prime})=|\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda_{**}^{\prime}|=d_{\Gamma}(\lambda^{\prime}).

We have thus proved (
2.1
).

Definition 2.3

(
[

]

Evidently,

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose
\Gamma
is of type
1
. The following statements hold.

(i)

If

, then every matrix

in

(ii)

The set-valued map
\mathcal{N}_{\infty}
anti-monotone:

\sum_{\alpha,\beta}(N_{\alpha\beta}-K_{\alpha\beta})(B_{\alpha\beta}-C_{\alpha\beta})\leq 0\text{ for all }B,C\in\mathcal{U},N\in\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(B),K\in\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(C).

(iii)

For any

and any set

containing

F_{\infty}(A)=\inf_{B\in\mathcal{V},N\in\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(B)}\Big[F_{\infty}(B)+\sum_{\alpha,\beta}N_{\alpha\beta}(A_{\alpha\beta}-B_{\alpha\beta})\Big].

(2.6)

(iv)

For any compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{U}
, there exists
C=C(\mathcal{K},F_{\infty})
such that
\|N\|\leq C
for every
N\in\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(B),B\in\mathcal{K}
.

Proof.

(i) The positive semi-definiteness of

follows from the monotonicity of

and from applying (

(ii)
From (

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha,\beta}N_{\alpha\beta}(C_{\alpha\beta}-B_{\alpha\beta})\geq F_{\infty}(C)-F_{\infty}(B),

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha,\beta}K_{\alpha\beta}(B_{\alpha\beta}-C_{\alpha\beta})\geq F_{\infty}(B)-F_{\infty}(C).

Summing up, we obtain the anti-monotonicity of

.

(iii) The direction “
\geq
” of (

) follows by taking

, while the direction “
\leq
” of (

) follows from (

(iv) Consider first the case that

and

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose that
(f,\Gamma)
is as in Theorem

.

Proof.

We adapt the proof of
[

. In the proof

denotes a constant which varies from lines to lines but depends only on
\beta,K
and
(f,\Gamma)

For

denote the set of

By Taylor’s theorem and the compactness of

\Big|f_{t}(\mu+rv)-f_{t}(\mu)-r|Df_{t}(\mu)|\nu_{t,\mu}\cdot v\Big|\leq Cr^{2}.

(2.7)

In particular, by (

) and (

,

(2.8)

Now observe that, if

\displaystyle Df_{t}(\lambda)\cdot(\mu-\lambda)

\displaystyle\geq f_{t}(\mu)-f_{t}(\lambda)+\frac{1}{C}\text{ for all }\mu\in K,\lambda\in X_{t,\mu}.

(2.9)

and on the other hand (in view of (

\Big|f_{t}(\mu+r\tilde{v}_{t,\mu,\lambda})-f_{t}(\mu)\Big|\leq Cr^{2}\text{ for all }r\in[0,1],\mu\in K,\lambda\in X_{t,\mu}.

Hence, by the concavity of

\displaystyle Df_{t}(\lambda)\cdot(\mu-\lambda)

\displaystyle\geq Df_{t}(\lambda)\cdot(\mu+r\tilde{v}_{t,\mu,\lambda}-\lambda)+\frac{r}{C}|Df_{t}(\lambda)|

\displaystyle\geq f_{t}(\mu+r\tilde{v}_{t,\mu,\lambda})-f_{t}(\lambda)+\frac{r}{C}|Df_{t}(\lambda)|

\displaystyle\geq f_{t}(\mu)-f_{t}(\lambda)+\frac{r}{C}|Df_{t}(\lambda)|-Cr^{2}\text{ for all }r\in[0,1],\mu\in K,\lambda\in X_{t,\mu}.

Together with (

(2.10)

The conclusion follows from (
2.9
) and (
2.10
).

Suppose that
(f,\Gamma)
,

are as in Theorem

.
Then there exists a constant

such that,
for sufficiently small

,
the function

satisfies

\mathcal{L}_{t}v\leq-\varepsilon\big(1+\mathcal{T}_{t}\big)\;\mbox{in}\;\Omega_{\delta,x_{0}},\;v\geq 0\;\mbox{on}\;\bar{\Omega}_{\delta,x_{0}}\;\mbox{and}\;v|_{\partial M}=0,

where

,

depend only on
\|u_{t}\|_{C^{1}(M)},(M,g)
,
(f,\Gamma)
and

.

Proof.

By a direct calculation, we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{t}(u_{t}-\underline{u})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{t}^{ij}(W_{ij}[u_{t}]-W_{ij}[\underline{u}])

\displaystyle+F_{t}^{ij}\Big(\nabla_{i}\underline{u}\nabla_{j}\underline{u}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\underline{u}|^{2}\delta_{ij}-\nabla_{i}u_{t}\nabla_{j}u_{t}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u_{t}|^{2}\delta_{ij}\Big)

\displaystyle+F_{t}^{ij}\Big(\nabla_{i}u_{t}\nabla_{j}(u_{t}-\underline{u})+\nabla_{j}u_{t}\nabla_{i}(u_{t}-\underline{u})-\sum_{l}\nabla_{l}u_{t}\nabla_{l}(u_{t}-\underline{u})\delta_{ij}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{t}^{ij}(W_{ij}[u_{t}]-W_{ij}[\underline{u}])+F_{t}^{ij}\nabla_{i}(u_{t}-\underline{u})\nabla_{j}(u_{t}-\underline{u})-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla(u_{t}-\underline{u})|^{2}\mathcal{T}_{t}.

Therefore, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{t}\Big((u_{t}-\underline{u})-\frac{(u_{t}-\underline{u})^{2}}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(1-(u_{t}-\underline{u})\Big)\mathcal{L}_{t}(u_{t}-\underline{u})-F_{t}^{ij}\nabla_{i}(u_{t}-\underline{u})\nabla_{j}(u_{t}-\underline{u})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(1-(u_{t}-\underline{u})\Big)F_{t}^{ij}(W_{ij}[u_{t}]-W_{ij}[\underline{u}])-(u_{t}-\underline{u})F_{t}^{ij}\nabla_{i}(u_{t}-\underline{u})\nabla_{j}(u_{t}-\underline{u})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\Big(1-(u_{t}-\underline{u})\Big)|\nabla(u_{t}-\underline{u})|^{2}\mathcal{T}_{t}.

Note that
F_{t}^{ij}(W_{ij}[u_{t}]-W_{ij}[\underline{u}])\leq 0
since

is concave,
\underline{u}\leq u_{t}
and
\psi_{z}\leq 0
, i.e.

0\leq\psi(x,\underline{u})-\psi(x,u_{t})\leq F_{t}(W[\underline{u}])-F_{t}(W[u_{t}])\leq F_{t}^{ij}(W_{ij}[\underline{u}]-W_{ij}[u_{t}]).

Also, in
\Omega_{\delta,x_{0}}

\delta
small enough depending on

and
\underline{u}

Combining the above two facts and that

is positive definite, we then arrive at

\mathcal{L}_{t}\Big((u_{t}-\underline{u})-\frac{(u_{t}-\underline{u})^{2}}{2}\Big)\leq\frac{1}{2}F_{t}^{ij}(W_{ij}[u_{t}]-W_{ij}[\underline{u}]).

Let

so that

.
Due to that

Therefore, in

with
\delta

,

and

, we have

where

and

.

Let

We then have

(2.20)

where for the last inequality we used
[

(or see

, Lemma 1.5]
).
Since

,
there exists a sufficiently small constant

,

,

We continue our argument depending on whether

Hence, combining with (

If

3.1
Double tangential and mixed normal-tangential derivative estimates

Recall the notations

introduced in Subsection

.

Proof of Lemma

.

First, since

, we have

\nabla_{\alpha\beta}(u_{t}-\underline{u})(x_{0})=-\nabla_{n}(u_{t}-\underline{u})(x_{0})\Pi(e_{\alpha},e_{\beta}),\;1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n-1,

where

denotes the second fundamental form of
\partial M
with respect to

. We therefore obtain the estimate for the pure tangential second order derivatives:
|\nabla_{\alpha\beta}u_{t}(x_{0})|\leq C
for
t\in[0,1]
and
1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n-1
.

In the rest of the proof, we show that
|\nabla_{\alpha n}u_{t}(x_{0})|\leq C
for
t\in[0,1]
and
1\leq\alpha\leq n-1
. Given
1\leq\alpha\leq n-1
, define

w=\pm\nabla_{\alpha}(u_{t}-\varphi)-\sum_{\ell<n}|\nabla_{\ell}(u_{t}-\varphi)|^{2},

where
\varphi
is extended to
M
with
\nabla_{n}\varphi=0
on
\partial M
. Note that
w=0
on
\partial M
near
x_{0}
.
By (
2.13
) in Lemma
2.8
,
a direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{t}\sum_{\ell<n}|\nabla_{\ell}(u_{t}-\varphi)|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad=2\sum_{\ell<n}\Big(\nabla_{\ell}(u_{t}-\varphi)\mathcal{L}_{t}\nabla_{\ell}(u_{t}-\varphi)+F_{t}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\ell}(u_{t}-\varphi)\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}(u_{t}-\varphi)\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\geq-C\Big(1+\mathcal{T}_{t}+\sum_{i}\partial_{i}f_{t}(\lambda^{(t)})|\lambda^{(t)}_{i}|\Big)+\sum_{\ell<n}F_{t}^{ij}W_{i\ell}[u_{t}]W_{j\ell}[u_{t}].

Using once again (
2.13
) and appealing to Lemma
2.6
, we deduce that

\mathcal{L}_{t}w\leq C\Big(1+\mathcal{T}_{t}+\sum_{i}\partial_{i}f_{t}(\lambda^{(t)})|\lambda^{(t)}_{i}|\Big)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq r}\partial_{i}f_{t}(\lambda^{(t)})(\lambda^{(t)}_{i})^{2}

for some index
r
.
By Lemma
2.7
and choosing
\varepsilon
small enough, we finally arrive at

\mathcal{L}_{t}w\leq C\big(1+\mathcal{T}_{t}\big).

(3.1)

Now, define
h=w+B\rho^{2}+Av
, where
\rho
and
v
are as in Subsection
2.2
. Note that

,
h|_{\partial M\cap\partial\Omega_{\delta,x_{0}}}\geq 0

and

as long as

large enough depending on
\|u_{t}\|_{C^{1}(M)}
,
\varphi
and
\delta
. By Lemma
2.10
and choosing

, where

depends on
\|u_{t}\|_{C^{1}(M)},(M,g)
,
\underline{u}
,
\varphi
,
\psi
,
B
and
\varepsilon
,
we see that

\mathcal{L}_{t}h\leq(C+CB-A\varepsilon)\big(1+\mathcal{T}_{t}\big)\leq 0.

By maximum principle, we have that

and

which concludes the proof.
∎

3.2
Double normal derivative estimates

As an immediate consequence of Lemma
3.1
and the fact that
\Gamma_{t}\subset\Gamma_{1}
, we have:

Corollary 3.3
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
1.2
, for any point
x_{0}\in\partial M
and any adapted frame

near

as in Subsection

\nabla_{nn}u_{t}(x_{0})\geq-C\text{ for }t\in[0,1].

In view of Corollary

, in order to prove Lemma

independent of

Lemma 3.4
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition

and any adapted frame
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
near
x_{0}
as in Subsection

\nabla_{nn}u_{t}(x_{0})\leq\frac{C}{1-t}\text{ for }t\in[0,1).

Consequently,

|W_{ij}^{t}[u_{t}](x_{0})|\leq C\text{ for all }t\in[0,1]\text{ and }(i,j)\neq(n,n).

Proof.

We use an argument in
[
43
]
with the help of Lemma
2.2
. By this lemma and the Lipschitz continuity of ordered eigenvalues of symmetric matrices (Weyl’s inequality), if a symmetric matrix
B=(b_{ij})
satisfies
b_{nn}=1
and

for

and
f_{t}(\lambda(B)\geq\frac{1-t}{C}

, then (in view of the estimate in Lemma

),

It follows that
W_{nn}[u_{t}](x_{0})\leq\frac{C}{1-t}
and hence
\nabla_{nn}u_{t}(x_{0})\leq\frac{C}{1-t}
. It also follows that
\mathrm{tr}(g^{-1}W[u_{t}])(x_{0})\leq\frac{C}{1-t}
. The last assertion is readily seen from this statement together with the definition of
W^{t}[u_{t}]
and Lemma
3.1
.
∎

We next observe that if
\Gamma

2

. The proof of Lemma
3.4
above can then be applied yielding:

Lemma 3.5
.

If
\Gamma
is of type 2 (in the sense of Definition
2.1
), then Lemma
3.2
holds.

In the rest of this section, we consider the proof of Lemma
3.2
when
\Gamma
is not of type

, i.e.
\Gamma
is of type
1
, and for

close to
1
. In particular,
\Gamma^{\prime}
, the projection of

. Recall that

\displaystyle=\nabla_{n}(u_{t}-\underline{u})\Big(b^{t}_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{n-2+(n-1)t}{2}\nabla_{n}(u_{t}+\underline{u})\delta_{\alpha\beta}\Big)

(3.7)

\displaystyle\qquad-(1-t)\nabla_{nn}(u_{t}-\underline{u})\delta_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha<n}\gamma_{\alpha}b^{t}_{\alpha\alpha},

\displaystyle q^{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{n-2+(n-1)t}{2}\sum_{\alpha<n}\gamma_{\alpha},

\begin{cases}(1-\dot{u}^{2})^{k-1}\Big(\ddot{u}+\frac{n-2k}{2k}(1-\dot{u}^{2})\Big)=\frac{n}{2k}e^{-2ku}&\text{ in }(-\ell,\ell),\\
1-\dot{u}^{2}>0&\text{ in }(-\ell,\ell),\\
u(\pm\ell)=c.\end{cases}

(4.1)

The ODE on the first line of (
4.1
) has a first integral: If we define
H
by

H(x,y)=e^{(2k-n)x}(1-y^{2})^{k}-e^{-nx},

then
H(u,\dot{u})
is constant along a solution.

For any
d\in(-\infty,0)
, let
u_{d}
denote the unique classical solution to the initial value problem

\begin{cases}(1-\dot{u}_{d}^{2})^{k-1}\Big(\ddot{u}_{d}+\frac{n-2k}{2k}(1-\dot{u}_{d}^{2})\Big)=\frac{n}{2k}e^{-2ku},\\
u_{d}(0)=d,\dot{u}_{d}(0)=0\end{cases}

(4.2)

in its maximal interval of existence
(-T_{d},T_{d})
. It is routine to show that
1-\dot{u}_{d}^{2}>0
in
(-T_{d},T_{d})
,
H(u_{d},\dot{u}_{d})=H(d,0)<0
,
T_{d}
is finite and given by

T_{d}=\int_{d}^{-\frac{1}{n}\ln|H(d,0)|}\Big[1-e^{\frac{n-2k}{k}x}\Big(e^{-nx}+H(d,0)\Big)^{\frac{1}{k}}\Big]^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,dx<0,

and

\begin{cases}u_{d}(t)\rightarrow-\frac{1}{n}\ln|H(d,0)|,\\
\dot{u}_{d}(t)\rightarrow\pm 1,\\
\ddot{u}_{d}(t)\rightarrow\infty\end{cases}\text{ as }t\rightarrow\pm T_{d}.

Now, for any given
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
, since the function
x\mapsto H(x,0)
increases from
-\infty
to
0
as
x
increases from
-\infty
to
0
, we can find a unique
d_{c}
such that
-\frac{1}{n}\ln|H(d_{c},0)|=c
. The conclusion follows with
\ell=T_{d_{c}}
and
u=u_{d_{c}}
.
∎

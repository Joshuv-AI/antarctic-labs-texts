Characters and The Generation of Sylow 3-subgroups For Almost Simple Groups Thanks: This work was partially supported by the U.S. National Science Foundation, Award No. DMS-2439897
Eden Ketchum

Abstract
. Given an almost simple group A A , we algorithmically show that the character table of A A determines whether or not the Sylow 3-subgroups of A A are 2-generated. We show this property is equivalent to a condition on involving Galois action on characters in the principal block. This would be a consequence of the Alperin-McKay-Navarro conjecture.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
20C15, 20C33

1.
introduction

A popular topic in the representation theory of finite groups is so called local/global connections, which seek to describe when local properties, such as Sylow subgroup structure, predict global properties, such as aspects of the character table, and vice versa. An example of such a result is
[
RSV20
, Theorem A]
in which Rizo, Schaeffer Fry and Vallejo show that finite groups having a cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroup is equivalent to the group’s character table having a property involving action of a specific Galois automorphism on the principal
p
-block for
p=2,3
. In
[
NRSV21
]
Navarro, Rizo, Schaeffer Fry, and Vallejo characterized when a group has 2-generated Sylow 2-subgroups, again in terms of the action of a specific Galois automorphism on the principal
p
-block. It was also shown by Moretó and Samble in
[
MS22
]
that for certain classes of groups the character table determines whether or not a group has
2
-generated Sylow
p
-subgroups; however, the general case remains open and, further, an algorithm to determine this properties for
p\geq 3
has yet to be determined. For any finite group
G
we let
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G))
denote the set of all hight zero characters in the principal block of
G
, and, if we let
\xi
be a primitive
|G|
th root of unity, we define
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\xi)/\mathbb{Q})
to be the Galois automorphism which fixes
3^{\prime}
roots of unity and raises
3
-power roots to the fourth power. Then the main result of our article is the following:

Theorem A
.

Let
S
be a non-abelian finite simple group, and
A
an almost simple group with
S\leq A\leq\operatorname{Aut}(S)
such that
3
divides the order of
A
. Then, for
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(A)
we have
|P\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Phi(P)|=9
if and only if the number of fixed points in
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(A))
under
\sigma
is in
\{6,9\}
.

We recall Burnside’s Basis Theorem which states that, given a
p
-group
P
, if
|P/\Phi(P)|=p^{a}
, where
\Phi(P)
denotes the Frattini subgroup of
P
, then
a
is the size of a minimal generating set for
P
. Thus, Theorem A states that Sylow
3
-subgroups of
A
have minimal generating set of size
2
if and only if the number of fixed points in
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(A))
under
\sigma
is in
\{6,9\}
.
It is believed that the conclusion of Theorem A will hold for all finite groups as was noted in
[
NRSV21
]
.
The reader should note that Theorem A could be thought of as a “Galois version” of
[
GRSV24
, Theorem 3.3]
. We build off of the arguments in
[
GRSV24
, Section 5]
throughout. Additionally
[
MMSV24
]
studies the action of
\sigma
and is used frequently.
We restrict our attention to almost simple groups because in upcoming work it is foreseen that the reduction theorem for the general case will be similar to the reduction in
[
GRSV24
]
and will require the result on a class of almost simple groups.

The structure of this paper will be as follows. In Section 2 we collect preliminary results and prove Theorem A for almost simple groups of socle isomorphic to an alternating group or sporadic simple group. In Section 3 we classify all almost simple groups with socle isomorphic to a simple group of Lie type that have Sylow
3
-subgroup with minimal generating set of size
2
. In section 4 we conclude the proof of Theorem A.

Preliminaries and initial results

Notes on notation:
Throughout,
\sigma
will denote the Galois automorphism that fixes
3^{\prime}
roots of unity and maps
3
-power roots of unity to their fourth power. For a group
G
we let

denote the number of characters of
3^{\prime}
degree lying in the principal 3-block of

denote the number of
\sigma
-fixed characters of

. To denote the sets of these characters rather than the quantities we use
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G))

We now begin by compiling some results which will be useful throughout.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
N\trianglelefteq G
such that
3\nmid|G/N|
, and let
\theta\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(N))
. Then
\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G)|\theta)
is nonempty.

Proof.

From
[
MMSV24
, Corollary 3.6 (ii)]
we have that there exists
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))
such that
[\chi,\theta^{G}]\neq 0
and
\chi
is
\sigma
-fixed. Using
[
Nav18
, Theorem 5.12]
, we have that
3\nmid\chi(1)
, thus
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G))
as desired.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

(Alperin-Dade) Let
N\trianglelefteq G
such that
3\nmid|G/N|
. Further assume
G=N\mathbf{C}_{G}(P)
for
P\in\mathrm{Syl}_{3}(G)
. Then restriction defined a bijection between
\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G))
and
\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(N))
.

Proof.

The work of
[
Alp76
]
and
[
Dad77
]
give that restriction defines a bijection between
\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))
and
\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(N))
. This and Lemma
2.1
give the result.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
P
and
Q
be nontrivial
p
-groups. Define
R\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=P\rtimes Q
. Then
[R\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Phi(R)]>[Q\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Phi(Q)]
.

Proof.

Let
\{M_{1},...,M_{k}\}
be the set of maximal subgroups of
Q
. Then we have
P\rtimes M_{i}
is a maximal subgroup of
R
for each
1\leq i\leq k
. Thus,
\Phi(R)\leq\bigcap(P\rtimes M_{i})=P\rtimes\Phi(Q)
. Since
Q
is a subgroup of
R
, there must be some maximal subgroup
M<R
with
Q\leq M
. It is clear that
P\nleq M
; therefore, since
P\leq P\rtimes\Phi(Q)
, this gives that
\Phi(R)\leq M\cap(P\rtimes\Phi(Q))<P\rtimes\Phi(Q)
. Thus, we have

[R\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Phi(R)]=[R\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}P\rtimes\Phi(Q)][P\rtimes\Phi(Q)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Phi(R)]=[Q\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Phi(Q)][P\rtimes\Phi(Q)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Phi(R)].

Therefore,
[R\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Phi(R)]>[Q\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Phi(Q)]
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
P
be a
p
-group of the form
C_{p^{a_{1}}}\wr...\wr C_{p^{a_{i}}}
. Then
P
is
i
-generated.

Proof.

It is a well know fact that given
G=H\wr K
we have
G/G^{\prime}\cong H/H^{\prime}\times K/K^{\prime}
(see for example
[
Ro96
, Prob. 1.6.20]
). Then, since
P^{\prime}\leq\Phi(P)
, we have that
[P\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Phi({P})]=[(P/P^{\prime})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Phi(P/P^{\prime})]
and we obtain
|P/\Phi(P)|=3^{i}
. Therefore,
P
is
i
-generated.
∎

.

Let
S
be a sporadic simple group,
{}^{2}F(2)\,^{\prime}
,
G_{2}(3)

n

.
If

.

Proposition 2.6
.

Theorem
A
holds for almost simple groups with socle isomorphic to the alternating group
A_{n}

Proof.

The case of
n\leq 6
was considered in Proposition
2.5
, so we need only consider cases where
n\geq 7
and
\operatorname{Aut}(A_{n})=S_{n}
. Further, note that
A_{n}
and
S_{n}
have the same Sylow
3
-subgroups. If
n=3^{i}
, then the Sylow
3
-subgroups of
S_{n}
are of the form
P_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=C_{3}\wr C_{3}\wr...\wr C_{3}
with
i
copies of
C_{3}
. From Lemma
2.4
we then see that
P_{i}
is
i
-generated. For arbitrary
n
we first take the
3
-adic decomposition
n=a_{0}+3a_{1}+3^{2}a_{2}+...+3^{k}a_{k}
, where each
a_{i}\in\{0,1,2\}
. Then, if
P
is a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
S_{n}
, we have
P\cong\prod P_{i}^{a_{i}}
, where
P_{i}\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(S_{3^{i}})
. From this we have
P/P^{\prime}=\prod P_{i}^{a_{i}}/(P_{i}^{\prime})^{a_{i}}\cong C_{3}^{a_{1}+2a_{2}+...+ka_{k}}
. Therefore, we have that
P^{\prime}=\Phi(P)
and
P/\Phi(P)=C_{3}^{a_{1}+2a_{2}+...+ka_{k}}
. It then follows that
P
is 2-generated if and only if
n\in\{6,7,8,9,10,11\}
. By
[
MMSV24
, Lemma 4.1]

we have that all characters of
A_{n}
are
\sigma
-fixed. We also have that all characters of
S_{n}
are
\sigma
-fixed since they are rational. Thus, it suffices to find all
n
such that
k_{0}(B_{0}(A_{n}))\in\{6,9\}
and
k_{0}(B_{0}(S_{n}))\in\{6,9\}
. This was done in
[
GRSV24
, Section 4]
, which gives the result.
∎

3.
Groups of Lie Type with
2
-generated Sylow
3
-subgroups

The goal of this section is to classify all almost simple groups with socle a simple group of Lie type that have
2
-generated Sylow
3
-subgroups.

Our Setting:
Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive algebraic group defined over the field
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
for some prime
p
. We consider the finite groups of Lie type
G=\mathbf{G}^{F}
for some Steinberg endomorphism
F
. We primarily consider simple groups of the form
S=G/\mathbf{Z}(G)
(Recall that
{}^{2}F(2)\,^{\prime}
was considered in Proposition
2.5
so we may omit it here). Given such a simple group, which we call simple groups of Lie type, we denote
\mathbf{G}_{ad}^{F}
by
\tilde{S}
. As we are considering almost simple groups, we discuss their structure here. Let
S
be a simple group of Lie type. We see that
S\leq\tilde{S}\leq\operatorname{Aut}(S)
where
\tilde{S}
is refereed to as the group of inner-diagonal automorphisms and
\operatorname{Aut}(S)/\tilde{S}
has representatives that are field and graph automorphisms (See
[
MT11
, Theorem 24.24]
). Using
[
GLS98
, Thm. 4.10.2]
, we have that a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
can be decomposed as
(P_{T}\rtimes P_{W})\rtimes\langle\tau_{1},\tau_{2},F_{q_{0}}\rangle
, where
P_{T}
is a Sylow
3
-subgroup of a maximal torus,
T\leq\tilde{S}
;
P_{W}
is isomorphic to a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
N_{\tilde{S}}(T)/T
;
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
are (possibly trivial) graph automorphisms; and
F_{p}
is the standard Frobenius map. We see further that
P_{T}\rtimes P_{W}
is isomorphic to a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
\tilde{S}

We first consider the situation for groups of Lie type defined in characteristic
p=3
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
S
be a simple group of
Lie type defined in characteristic 3 which is not isomorphic to one of the groups discussed in Propositions
2.5
and
2.6
. If
S\leq A\leq\operatorname{Aut}(S)
, then
A
does not have 2-generated Sylow
3
-subgroups.

Proof.

Let
S
be as above and let
P\in\mathrm{Syl}_{3}(S)
. Let
r
denote the rank of
\mathbf{G}
and
q
denote the size of the field over which
S
is defined unless
S
is one of the Ree groups in which case we take
q
such that
S=\,^{2}G_{2}(q^{2})
and
q^{2}=3^{2a+1}
for some
a
. First we claim
|P/\Phi(P)|=9
if and only if
(r,q)\in\{(2,3),(1,9)\}
.
We may assume
S\neq G_{2}(3)
by Proposition
2.5
. Thus, arguing as in the proof of
[
GRSV24
, proposition 5.7]
, we have that
P/P^{\prime}
is elementary abelian. Therefore,
P/P^{\prime}=P/\Phi(P)
and the same proof in loc. cit. gives
|P/P^{\prime}|=9
if and only if
(r,q)\in\{(2,3),(1,9)\}
. Note that all cases with
(r,q)\in\{(2,3),(1,9)\}
give groups isomorphic to groups discussed Proposition
2.5
.

Now assume
S\leq A\leq\operatorname{Aut}(S)
with
3\nmid|A/S|
. Then, arguing as in
[
GRSV24
, Proposition 5.7]
, we see that the Sylow
3
-subgroups of
A
are not
2
-generated.
∎

We now consider groups of Lie type defined in characteristic
p\neq 3
. We begin by classifying when such groups have
2
-generated Sylow
3
-subgroups.
For the remainder of the article, we will denote by
\operatorname{GL}_{n}^{+}(q)
the general linear group over a field of size
q
(similarly
\operatorname{SL}_{n}^{+}(q)
and
\operatorname{PSL}_{n}^{+}(q)
will denote the related special linear and projective special linear groups respectively) and we will denote by
\operatorname{GL}_{n}^{-}(q)
the general unitary group over a field of size
q^{2}
(similarly
\operatorname{SL}_{n}^{-}(q)
and
\operatorname{PSL}_{n}^{-}(q)
will denote the related special unitary and projective special unitary groups respectively).

The following results on these groups will be useful throughout.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
q
be a power of a prime and
\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
with
3|(q-\epsilon)
. Let
\widetilde{P}
be a Sylow 3-subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
. Then
|\widetilde{P}/\Phi(\widetilde{P})|\leq 81
if and only
n\in\{1,2,3,4,5,6,9,10,27\}
. Further, we have that
|\widetilde{P}/\Phi(\widetilde{P})|=9
if and only if
n\in\{2,3\}
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{P}_{0}=C_{3^{a}}
. Then recursively define
\tilde{P}_{i}=\tilde{P}_{i-1}\wr C_{3}
. It follows from Lemma
2.4
that
\tilde{P}_{i}
is
i+1
generated. Let
n=a_{o}+a_{1}3+a_{2}3^{2}+...+a_{t}3^{t}
be the 3-adic decomposition of
n
. Then, from the work of
[
We55
]
, we have that
\tilde{P}\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(\operatorname{GL}^{\epsilon}_{n}(q))
is of the form
\displaystyle{\Pi_{i=0}^{t}\tilde{P}_{i}^{a_{i}}}
, where
\tilde{P}_{i}
is as described above. It follows that
\tilde{P}^{\prime}=\displaystyle{\Pi_{i=0}^{t}\tilde{P}_{i}^{\prime a_{i}}}
, and, arguing as in the above discussion, we obtain
|\tilde{P}/\Phi{(\tilde{P})}|=3^{a_{o}+2a_{1}+3a_{2}...(t+1)a_{t}}
. Therefore, the stated result holds.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\widetilde{P}
be a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
with
3|(q-\epsilon)
, and let
P
be a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
. Then
|\widetilde{P}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Phi(\widetilde{P})|/|P\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Phi(P)|\leq 3
.

Proof.

Let
a
be a generator of a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
C_{q-\epsilon}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
, and choose
\widetilde{P}\in\mathrm{Syl}_{3}(\operatorname{GL}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q))
such that
A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathrm{diag}(a,I_{n-1})\in\widetilde{P}
.
First, we note that
P=\widetilde{P}\cap\operatorname{SL}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
is a Sylow 3-subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
.
Next, we note that, since
\langle A\rangle
forms a complete set of coset representative for
\widetilde{P}/P
, every element of
\widetilde{P}
can be written as an element of
P
multiplied by an element of
\langle A\rangle
. Therefore, if
X
is a minimal generating set of
P
,
X\cup\{A\}
is a generating set of
\widetilde{P}
. This is sufficient for the desired result.∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
P
be a Sylow 3-subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
, where
q
is a power of a prime such that
3|(q-\epsilon)
. If
9\leq|P/\Phi(P)|\leq 27
, then
n\in\{3,4,5,6,9,10\}
. Furthermore,
|P/\Phi(P)|=9
if and only if
n\in\{3,4\}
.

Proof.

First, we consider the case in which
3\nmid n
. By
order considerations we see that we can construct
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(\operatorname{SL}^{\epsilon}_{n}(q))
to be the set

P\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&\det(A)^{-1}\end{pmatrix}|A\in\tilde{P}\right\}

for a fixed
\tilde{P}\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(\operatorname{GL}_{n-1}^{\epsilon}(q))
. Thus, a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
is isomorphic to a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}^{\epsilon}_{n-1}(q)
and the result follows from the proof of Proposition
3.2
.

Now assume
3|n
. Proposition
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
give that the only cases we need to consider are when
n\in\{3,6,9,27\}
. If
n=3^{i}
, let
P_{W}=C_{3}\wr..\wr C_{3}
with
i
copies of
C_{3}
. In this case we see explicitly that
P=C_{3^{a}}^{3^{i}-1}\rtimes P_{W}
, where
3^{a}=(q-\epsilon)_{3}
. Applying Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
2.4
gives
|P/\Phi(P)|>|P_{W}/\Phi(P_{W})|=3^{i}
. Thus, if
n=\{9,27\}
we have that
P
is not
2
-generated and, if
n=27
,
P
is not
3
-generated. If
n=3
, we have that
|P/\Phi(P)|\geq 9
and we can explicitly find an order
2
generating set, which gives
|P/\Phi(P)|=9
. For
n=6
we instead take
P_{W}\cong C_{3}\times C_{3}
and the argument follows similarly to the case of
n=9
above.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
P\in\mathrm{Syl}_{3}(\operatorname{SL}^{\epsilon}_{n}(q))
and let
Q\in\mathrm{Syl}_{3}(\operatorname{PSL}^{\epsilon}_{n}(q))
, where
q
is a power of a prime such that
3|(q-\epsilon)
. Then
|P\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Phi(P)|/|Q\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Phi(Q)|\leq 3
.

Proof.

If
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{SL}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)\rightarrow\operatorname{PSL}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
is the usual projection map, we may choose
Q
such that
Q=\pi(P)\cong P/(P\cap\mathbf{Z}(\operatorname{SL}_{n}(q))
. We also note that, since

\mathbf{Z}(\operatorname{SL}^{\epsilon}_{n}(q))
is cyclic,
P\cap\mathbf{Z}(\operatorname{SL}^{\epsilon}_{n}(q))=\langle A\rangle
for some
A
. Let
\{A_{1},...,A_{n}\}
be a complete set of coset representatives for a minimal generating set of
Q
. Then
\{A_{1},...,A_{n},A\}
must generate
P
. This is sufficient for the desired result.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
q
be a power of a prime that is distinct from 3 and let
Q
be a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
\operatorname{\operatorname{PSL}}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
. If
3|(q-\epsilon)
, then
|Q/\Phi(Q)|=9
if and only if
n=3
or
n=4
. If instead
3|(q+\epsilon)
, then
|Q/\Phi(Q)|=9
if and only if
n\in\{4,5,6,7\}
.

Proof.

Assume
3|(q-\epsilon)
. Let
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{SL}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)\rightarrow\operatorname{PSL}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
be the usual projection map, and let
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(\operatorname{SL}^{\epsilon}_{n}(q))
. Define
Q\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=P/(P\cap\mathbf{Z}(\operatorname{SL}^{\epsilon}_{n}(q))\ \cong\pi(P)\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(\operatorname{PSL}^{\epsilon}_{n}(q))
. In cases where
3\nmid n
we have that
Q\cong P
, thus, from Proposition
3.4
, we have that
Q
is
2
-generated and if and only if
n=4
.

Now assume
3|n
. Using Lemma
3.5
, Proposition
3.4
, and the fact that
|Q/\Phi(Q)|
must be less than
|P/\Phi(P)|
since
Q
is a homomorphic image of
P
, we obtain that the only possible choices of
n
which could give 2-generated Sylow 3-subgroups are
n\in\{3,6,9\}
.

Assume
n=3
. By Proposition
3.4
we see that
P
is
2
-generated, so, since
Q
is the homomorphic image of
P
,
Q
must either be
2
-generated or cyclic. Assume by way of contradiction that
Q
is cyclic.
We have
Q\cong P/Z_{3}
, where
Z_{3}=P\cap\mathbf{Z}(\operatorname{SL}^{\epsilon}_{n}(q))
. Since
Z_{3}\subseteq\mathbf{Z}(P)
, we have that
P/\mathbf{Z}(P)
is a quotient group of
Q
. Thus, if
Q
is cyclic, then
P/\mathbf{Z}(P)
is cyclic. It is a well known fact that
P/\mathbf{Z}(P)
being cyclic implies that
P
is abelian. This contradicts Proposition
3.4
.

Now assume
n\in\{6,9\}
. Then, arguing as in the proof of Proposition
3.4
, we have that
Q=(C_{3^{a}}^{8}\rtimes P_{W})/Z_{3}
, where
Z_{3}
is as above and
P_{W}=C_{3}\times C_{3}
or
P_{W}=C_{3}\wr C_{3}
for
n=6
and
n=9
respectively. We see further that
P\cap\mathbf{Z}(\operatorname{SL}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q))\leq C_{3^{a}}^{8}
and
Q=C_{3^{a}}^{8}/Z_{3}\rtimes P_{W}
. Applying Lemma
2.3
gives
|Q/\Phi(Q)|>9
.

If we instead assume

, then we have

4.
Theorem A for groups of Lie type

We have concluded our classification of almost simple groups with socle a simple group of Lie type that have
2
-generated Sylow
3
-subgroups, and will now classify all almost simple groups
A
, with socle a simple group of Lie type, such that

[

\mathcal{E}(G,s_{i})
are in bijection with the unipotent characters of
\mathbf{C}_{G^{*}}(s_{i})

[
GM20
, Section 1.7]

[\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{*}}(s)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbf{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}^{*}}(s)]

\mathbf{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}^{*}}(s)

\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{*}}(s)
. We note that the principal block is contained in the union of all
\mathcal{E}(G,s)
such that

has
3
-power order by
[
CE04

. Lastly we mention that given a Galois Automorphism

-fixed

We now begin our discussion with some useful Lemmas.

∎

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
S
be a simple group of Lie type defined in characteristic
p\neq 3
such that
S\neq\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q)
and
S\neq\operatorname{PSL}_{3}(q)
. Let
S\leq A\leq\operatorname{Aut}(S)
such that
A/S
has nontrivial Sylow 3-subgroups. Then Theorem A holds for
A
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.10
we have that it is sufficient to show that
k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(A))\geq 10
.
From Proposition
4.4
we have that
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(\tilde{S}))
contains at least
4
unipotent characters that extend to
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
and restrict irreducibly to
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(S))
unless
S=\operatorname{PSL}_{4}^{\epsilon}(q)
with
3|(q-\epsilon)
or
S=\operatorname{PSp}_{4}(2^{a})
. Let
S
be not one of those exceptions. First, we consider the case where
3\nmid|A/(\tilde{S}\cap A)|
. Let
\theta_{1},...,\theta_{4}
be the
4
unipotent characters chosen in Proposition
4.4
restricted to
A\cap\tilde{S}
. By
[
MMSV24
, Lemma 4.7]
we have that each
\theta_{i}
is
\sigma
-fixed. Then, using Lemma
2.1
, we find some
\hat{\theta}_{i}\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(A)|\theta_{i})
for each
\theta_{i}
. Then
[
GRSV24
, Lemma 2.7]
gives that
3|k_{0}(B_{0}(A)|(\theta_{i})_{S})
. This, the fact that
\sigma
has
3
-power order, and the fact
\sigma
permutes the elements of
\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(A)|(\theta_{i})_{S})
gives that
3|k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(A)|(\theta_{i})_{S})
. Therefore, since
\hat{\theta}_{i}\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(A)|(\theta_{i})_{S})
, we have that there are at least 3 characters in
\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(A)|(\theta_{i})_{S})
. Since
(\theta_{1})_{S},(\theta_{2})_{S},(\theta_{3})_{S}
and
(\theta_{4})_{S}
are in distinct
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
-orbits, this gives
k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(A))\geq 12
.

Now assume instead that
3
divides
|A/(\tilde{S}\cap A)|
. Let
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}
and
\theta_{4}
be the
4
unipotent characters in
B_{0}(S)
chosen in Proposition
4.4
and let
\theta=\theta_{i}
for some
i
. Let
P
be a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
A/(A\cap\tilde{S})
. Since
\theta
was chosen to be rational and
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
-invariant, by
[
Nav18
, Corollary 6.6(a)]
we can extend
\theta
to some
\hat{\theta}\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(\tilde{S}P))
. Then Lemma
2.1
gives a character in
\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(A)|\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{S}P\cap A})
. This character must lie in
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(A)|\theta_{S})
. Then, arguing as in the preceding paragraph, we see that
k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(A)|\theta_{S})\geq 3
. Applying this argument to all
4
of the
\theta_{i}
yields
k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(A))\geq 12
as they were chosen in distinct
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
-orbits.

Now let
S=\operatorname{PSL}^{\epsilon}_{4}(q)
or
\operatorname{PSp_{4}(2^{a})}
. Then by Proposition
4.4
we have
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(\tilde{S}))
contains at least
3
rational valued unipotent characters that extend to
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
and restrict irreducibly to
S
. Arguing as in the above cases this gives
k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(A))\geq 9
, thus it is sufficient to find one additional
\sigma
-fixed character. Choose
s\in G^{*}
as in Proposition
4.10
so that the semisimple character
\chi_{s}
is contained in
\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(S))
. By Lemma
4.1
we have that
\chi_{s}
extends to some
\hat{\chi}_{s}\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(\tilde{S}))
. Now let
X
be the preimage in
\tilde{S}A
of a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
\tilde{S}A/\tilde{S}
. Then by
[
Tay18
, Proposition 7.2]
we see that
\hat{\chi}_{s}
is
X
-invariant. In both cases we have
\tilde{S}/\tilde{S}^{\prime}
has
2
-power order. Thus, we have that the determinental order
o(\hat{\chi}_{s})
must be prime to
3
. Thus,
[
Nav18
, Corrollary 6.4]
gives an extension of
\hat{\chi}_{s}
in
X
which must be
\sigma
-fixed. Then applying Lemma
2.1
gives a character above
\chi_{s}
in
\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(A))
, which must be distinct from those which lie above the unipotent characters because non-unipotent characters cannot be conjugate to unipotent characters.
∎

, Proposition

,

4.9
, Proposition

, Proposition

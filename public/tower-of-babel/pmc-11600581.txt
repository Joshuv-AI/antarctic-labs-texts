Normal very long-chain fatty acids level in a patient with peroxisome biogenesis disorders: a case report
Authors: Bita Barazandeh Shirvan, Najmeh Ahangari, Razie Rezaie, Parvaneh Layegh, Ehsan Ghayoor Karimiani, Narges Hashemi, Mehran Beiraghi Toosi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundZellweger spectrum disorders (ZSDs) are a group of peroxisome biogenesis disorders (PBDs) with different variants in the PEX genes. The main biochemical marker for screening peroxisomal disorders is very long-chain fatty acids (VLCFAs). The study reveals a rare case of PBD in the Zellweger spectrum in which she had normal plasma VLCFA levels.Case presentationHere, we report a 10-year-old girl with neurodevelopmental delay, facial dysmorphism, and hearing impairment. A brain magnetic resonance imaging scan was done to determine the reason for the seizures and neurodevelopmental delay. MRI images showed a mild widening in sulci especially in frontal lobes and sylvian fissures with pachygyria in the perisylvian regions. Biochemical analysis was done to detect ZSD. However, plasma VLCFA levels were normal. The diagnosis was made using whole-exome sequencing (WES). A homozygous variant of uncertain significance (VUS) in PEX6 NM_000287.4: c.1992G > C (p. Glu664Asp) was identified which has been confirmed through Sanger sequencing in probands and her parents.ConclusionsAccording to the case report, plasma VLCFA levels can be normal in patients with PBDs in the Zellweger spectrum. Furthermore, we could re-classify the c.1992G > C variant in the PEX6 gene from VUS to likely pathogenic based on clinical manifestations including facial dysmorphism, and hearing impairment.

Background

One of the most common peroxisome biogenesis disorders (PBDs) is Zellweger spectrum disorder (ZSD), caused by mutations in the PEX (peroxisome assembly factor-2, PAF-2) genes [1]. There are two groups of peroxisome biogenesis disorders, including ZSD and rhizomelic chondrodysplasia punctata (RCDP) [2]. Formerly, ZSD was categorized into three groups: infantile refsum disease (IRD), neonatal adrenoleukodystrophy (NALD), and Zellweger syndrome (ZS). Nowadays, all people with moderate to severe clinical manifestations and a defect in one of the PEX genes are referred to as “ZSD.”

[3]. Clinical manifestations of PBDs in the Zellweger spectrum patients include hypotonia, seizures, dysmorphic features, liver disease, retinal degeneration, sensorineural hearing loss, and polycystic kidneys [1, 4]. However, some cases present a milder form of the phenotypes. The biogenesis of peroxisomes relies on various proteins known as peroxins, encoded by the PEX genes [5]. The peroxin protein is necessary for peroxisome membrane assembly, matrix protein import, and peroxisomal division [6]. Peroxisomes play a crucial role in numerous metabolic processes, such as the beta-oxidation of very long chain fatty acids (VLCFA) and pristanic acid, the alpha-oxidation of phytanic acid, the biosynthesis of etherphospholipids, bile acids, docosahexaenoic acid, and the detoxification of glyoxylate [7]. One of the screening tests for peroxisomal disorders is the measurement of peroxisomal metabolites because defects in functions of the peroxisome lead to abnormal metabolite levels [8]. According to studies, plasma concentrations of very long-chain fatty acids (VLCFAs), phytanic and pristanic acid, C27-bile acid intermediates, and pipecolic acid were higher in patients with ZSD [1, 9]. However, there are significant differences between the clinical and biochemical characteristics among patients with PBD [1]. This study describes an Iranian case with PBD in the Zellweger spectrum. Despite having clinical characteristics including neurodevelopmental delay and hearing loss, her VLCFA level was normal. In addition, the patient had a variant uncertain significance (VUS) in the PEX6 gene that was clinically associated with PBDs in the Zellweger spectrum.

Case presentation

We report a 10-year-old girl with PBD in the Zellweger spectrum. She was the product of a consanguineous marriage after one miscarriage. The pedigree of this family is presented in Fig. 1A. She was born in 39 weeks of pregnancy and her Apgar score was 8 in the first minute, her birth weight was 3500 g, her birth length was 50 cm, and her head circumference was 35 cm. At two months old, she experienced her first seizure while having a fever. She subsequently experienced several seizures both with and without a fever. Her seizures were controlled with Topiramate 100 mg. She began to walk when she was two years old. The brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) was done when she was 1 year old. MRI images revealed mild widening in sulci especially in the frontal lobes and sylvian fissures with pachygyria in the perisylvian regions and the corpus callosum was presented and appeared intact. Images of her brain MRI are demonstrated in Fig. 1B. She had a developmental delay, limited speech, and could speak 5 words at 6 years old. She didn’t have nonverbal communication, poor social skills (limited interest in other children), little eye contact, and stereotypic movements which are symptoms of autistic spectrum disorder (ASD). Risperidone 1 mg was prescribed: ½ mg in the morning and 1 mg at night for ASD. Her concentration was reported no improvement with Ritalin 10 mg. At the last visit, she was 10 years old, weighed 52 kg, her speech was regressed, she didn’t have movement disorders and she could not talk. Furthermore, she had minor dysmorphic features including a broad nasal bridge and a high forehead which was an ideal dysmorphic feature of ZS (Fig. 1C). In light of the rising diagnostic suspicion of ZS, a VLCFA test and abdominal ultrasound were requested at the age of 10-years-old. The results of the VLCFA test were in the normal range (Table 1). The abdominal ultrasound showed a slight and diffuse increase in liver echogenicity. Organomegaly wasn’t observed in the abdominal organs. high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) of serum amino acids was normal. Whole exome sequencing (WES) was performed using DNA extracted from the whole blood of the proband at the age of 10-years-old. Genomic DNA was enzymatically fragmented and the targeted enrichment was done using the SureSelect Human All Exon V7 kit (Agilent Technologies,Santa Clara,CA,USA). Libraries were sequenced on the Illumine NovaSeq 6000 platform for 2 × 100 bp reads with an average coverage of 100× (Eurofins Genomic Services,Bengaluru,India). We performed quality control (QC) with FastQC to analyze the sequence read quality. Burrows-Wheeler alignment (BWA) algorithm was used with default parameters for alignment to human genome assembly GRCh37 (hg19). Furthermore, we used Picard and the Genome Analysis Toolkit (GATK) to remove duplicate reads, local realignment, and variant calling, while Annovar32 was employed for annotation. Additionally, variants were classified based on multiple private and public databases including, NCBI 1000 genomes,NHLBI Exome Sequencing Project,Exome Aggregation Consortium,and HGMD®. Finally, we evaluated the pathogenicity of the variants regarding the 2015 American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) guidelines. In silico analysis of WES data revealed a homozygous variant of uncertain significance (VUS) in the PEX6 NM_000287.4: c.1992G > C (p. Glu664Asp). This variant is associated with PBDs in the Zellweger spectrum. This is a very rare variant in the genomic databases such as gnomAD (0.0013%) and ExAC (0.0008%), and with no reported frequency in the 1000 Genomes database. This variant had a CADD score of 20.3. (Request: Chromosome 6, Position 42966627, CADD GRCh38-v1.7). According to the ACMG guidelines, this variant has been classified as PS4 and PM2, which enhances its deleterious effect and increases the pathogenicity score. The Sanger sequencing confirmed the candidate variant in proband and parents (Fig. 1D).

Discussion and conclusions

This report describes the Iranian case with PBD in the Zellweger spectrum and normal plasma VLCFA levels. Our patient had developmental delay, dysmorphic face, autism spectrum disorder, colonic seizures, hearing impairment, hypotonia, and a homozygous variant of uncertain significance in the PEX6 gene. The second most common cause of ZS disorders (approximately 16%) is mutations in the PEX6 gene [10]. According to an earlier study, the PEX6 gene contained 104 variants, including 37 missense, five nonsense, 32 deletions, nine insertions, one indel, and 20 splice site mutations. In addition, One-third of variants are found in exon 1 [10]. In this case, the patient has a missense variant of uncertain significance in exon10, which has been documented in 2 previous publications [11, 12]. For instance, a patient [11] had a compound heterozygous variant, one of which was similar to c.1992G > A variant as our case. This patient exhibited limited speech, mild intellectual disability, and hearing impairment, which parallels our patient’s limited speech, autism spectrum disorder (ASD), and hearing impairment.

Zellweger Spectrum Disorders (ZSDs) are a subgroup of peroxisome biogenesis disorders with moderate to severe clinical features. Patients with early-onset ZSD have severe phenotypes such as failure to thrive, jaundice, hypotonia, dysmorphic features, and high mortality within the first year of life [13].

Moreover, patients with a milder phenotype have more variation in the age of clinical manifestation and range of symptoms [14]. The measurement of plasma VLCFAs (26:0, C26:1, C24:0/C22:0, and C26:0/C22:0) is the primary screening test in patients with clinical manifestations of PBD [15]. For instance, researchers reported a patient from Saudi Arabia with a mutation variant in the PEX6 gene, specific clinical manifestations of ZS, and an increased level of VLCFA C26, VLCFA C24/C22, and VLCFA C26/C22 [16]. Furthermore, the study revealed a PBD-ZSD patient with 2 variants in PEX6 who had elevated VLCFA levels (C26:0 1.79 nmol/mL, normal range of ≤ 1.3 nmol/mL; C26:0/C22:0 = 0.052, normal range of ≤ 0.023) and clinical characteristics. Therefore, they suggested biochemical tests such as VLCFAs were important for the diagnosis [17]. Our patient had a normal range of plasma VLCFA levels, which was contrary to previous findings. Others have already reported normal levels of VLCFA [18]. Additionally, researchers demonstrated that plasma VLCFA levels decreased with age. They described 19 patients with ZSD in an earlier study. Seventeen out of the 19 patients showed higher levels of VLCFAs in plasma at the time of the diagnosis. However, the concentration of VLCFAs decreased with age in a follow-up. Based on this study, the age of our patient may justify the normal range of VLCFA levels [11]. Another study unveiled a case with PBD and two heterozygous variants in PEX6 whose plasma VLCFA levels were within the normal range until the age of 18 months but increased after that. This study revealed that plasma VLCFA levels can increase with age [19]. Hence, the findings of this study were in contradiction with previous research. Consequently, plasma VLCFA levels can fluctuate in patients with PBDs. We need a more comprehensive cohort study to clarify the associations between biochemical tests like VLCFAs and the diagnosis of peroxisome biogenesis disorders. Our study has shown that a patient with clinical and molecular symptoms associated with Peroxisome biogenesis disorder can have normal levels of very long-chain fatty acids (VLCFAs) because the VLCFA levels can vary between normal and abnormal in patients. Additionally, this report highlighted the importance of uncertain significance variants in diagnosis and targeted medical management. According to our findings, a comprehensive clinical evaluation, including imaging studies and biochemical testing, is crucial to correlate the molecular genetic findings with the patient’s phenotype. However, many clinical and actual data are still missing to have a clear phenotype-genotype correlation. For better management of these disorders, a personalized approach is required based on the genetic and metabolic profile of each patient, as well as the evolving understanding of the underlying pathophysiology. Moreover, we have initiated symptomatic therapy and recommended that the parents pursue evaluations and checkups for adrenal function, retinal health, and bone integrity. Furthermore, it is important to take this variant into account if there are any consanguineous marriages among other family members.

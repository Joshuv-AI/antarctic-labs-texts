Functions on products X × Y X\times Y with applications to Ascoli spaces, k ℝ k_{\mathbb{R}} -spaces and s ℝ s_{\mathbb{R}} -spaces
Saak Gabriyelyan, Evgenii Reznichenko

Abstract
We prove that a Tychonoff space X X is (sequentially) Ascoli iff for every compact space K K (resp., for a convergent sequence 𝐬 \mathbf{s} ), each separately continuous k k -continuous function Φ : X × K → ℝ \Phi:X\times K\to\mathbb{R} is continuous. We apply these characterizations to show that an open subspace of a (sequentially) Ascoli space is (sequentially) Ascoli, and that the μ \mu -completion and the Dieudonné completion of a (sequentially) Ascoli space are (sequentially) Ascoli. We give also cover-type characterizations of Ascoli spaces and suggest an easy method of construction of pseudocompact Ascoli spaces which are not k ℝ k_{\mathbb{R}} -spaces and show that each space X X can be closely embedded into such a space. Using a different method we prove Hušek’s theorem: a Tychonoff space Y Y is a locally pseudocompact k ℝ k_{\mathbb{R}} -space iff X × Y X\times Y is a k ℝ k_{\mathbb{R}} -space for each k ℝ k_{\mathbb{R}} -space X X . It is proved that X X is an s ℝ s_{\mathbb{R}} -space iff for every locally compact sequential space K K , each s s -continuous function f : X × K → ℝ f:X\times K\to\mathbb{R} is continuous.

Introduction

All spaces are assumed to be Tychonoff. We denote by
C_{p}(X)
and
C_{k}(X)
the space
C(X)
of all continuous real-valued functions on a space
X
endowed with the pointwise topology or the compact-open topology, respectively. Recall that a space
X
is called a
k
-space
if for each non-closed subset
A\subseteq X
there is a compact subset
K\subseteq X
such that
A\cap K
is not closed in
K
. The space
X
is a
k_{\mathbb{R}}
-space
if every
k
-continuous function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous (recall that
f
is
k
-continuous
if each restriction of
f
to any compact set
K\subseteq X
is continuous). Each
k
-space is a
k_{\mathbb{R}}
-space, but the converse is false in general.
k
-spaces and
k_{\mathbb{R}}
-spaces are widely studied in General Topology (see, for example, the classical book of Engelking
[
8
]
or Michael’s article
[
25
]
). The meaning of
k_{\mathbb{R}}
-spaces is explained by the following characterization (see for example Theorem 5.8.7 of
[
27
]
).

Theorem 1.1

A Tychonoff space
X
is a
k_{\mathbb{R}}
-space if, and only if, the space
C_{k}(X)
is complete.

One of the basic theorems in Analysis is the Ascoli theorem which states that if
X
is a
k
-space, then every compact subset of
C_{k}(X)
is evenly continuous, see Theorem 3.4.20 in
[
8
]
. In
[
28
]
, Noble proved that every
k_{\mathbb{R}}
-space satisfies the conclusion of the Ascoli theorem.
So it is natural to consider the class of Tychonoff spaces which satisfy the conclusion of Ascoli’s theorem. Following
[
3
]
, a Tychonoff space
X
is called an
Ascoli space
if every compact subset
\mathcal{K}
of
C_{k}(X)
is evenly continuous, that is the map
X\times\mathcal{K}\ni(x,f)\mapsto f(x)
is continuous. In other words,
X
is Ascoli if, and only if, the compact-open topology of
C_{k}(X)
is Ascoli in the sense of
[
24
, p.45]
. One can easily show that
X
is Ascoli if, and only if, every compact subset of
C_{k}(X)
is equicontinuous. Recall that a subset
H
of
C(X)
is
equicontinuous
if for every
x\in X
and each
\varepsilon>0
there is an open neighborhood
U
of
x
such that
|f(x^{\prime})-f(x)|<\varepsilon
for all
x^{\prime}\in U
and
f\in H
. A more general class of spaces was introduced in
[
12
]
: a Tychonoff space
X
is said to be
sequentially Ascoli
if every convergent sequence in
C_{k}(X)
is equicontinuous. Clearly, every Ascoli space is sequentially Ascoli, but the converse is not true in general (every non-discrete
P
-space is sequentially Ascoli but not Ascoli, see
[
12
]
). For numerous results concerning the (sequentially) Ascoli property, see
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
.

Analogously to
k_{\mathbb{R}}
-spaces, one can define
s_{\mathbb{R}}
-spaces as follows. A space
X
is called an
s_{\mathbb{R}}
-space
if every sequentially continuous function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous. Recall that a function
f:X\to Y
between topological spaces
X
and
Y
is called
sequentially continuous
or
s
-continuous
if the restriction of
f
onto each convergent sequence in
X
is continuous.
s
-continuous functions and
s
-continuous functionals appear naturally not only in General Topology, but also in Functional Analysis, see for example Mazur
[
23
]
and Wilansky
[
35
]
.
The sequential spaces form the most important subclass of the class of
s_{\mathbb{R}}
-spaces. A space
X
is
sequential
if for each non-closed subset
A\subseteq X
there is a sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq A
converging to some point
a\in\bar{A}\setminus A
. The relationships between the introduced classes of spaces are described in the following diagram in which none of the implications is invertible (see Remarks
4.1
and
4.7
below):

A characterization of
k_{\mathbb{R}}
-spaces is given in Theorem 3.3.5 of Banakh
[
2
]
. Numerous new characterizations of
k_{\mathbb{R}}
-spaces and

-spaces are obtained by Gabriyelyan and Reznichenko
[
18
]
.

The aforementioned results and discussion motivate us to study Ascoli spaces,

-spaces and

-spaces, and relationships between these classes of spaces, in particular, for function spaces.

Now we describe the content of the article.
In Section
2
, to make the article self-contained, we recall other notions and some of necessary results used in the article.
In Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
of Section
3
we prove that
X
is a (sequentially) Ascoli space if, and only if, for every compact space
K
(resp., for a convergent sequence
\mathbf{s}
), each separately continuous

-continuous function
\Phi:X\times K\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous. We apply these characterizations to show that an open subspace of a (sequentially) Ascoli space is Ascoli (Proposition
3.4
), and that the
\mu
-completion and the Dieudonné completion of a (sequentially) Ascoli space are (sequentially) Ascoli (Theorem

). In Theorem
3.5
we give cover-type characterizations of Ascoli spaces. In Proposition

and Theorems
3.12
and

we suggest an easy method of construction of pseudocompact Ascoli spaces which are not

-spaces and show that each space

can be closely embedded into such a space.

In Section

we show that analogous characterizations

-spaces and

-spaces but using only

and Theorem

. These characterizations of (sequentially) Ascoli spaces,

-spaces and

-spaces additionally motivate us to study functions on products

, Cohen proved that

2
Preliminary results

Let
X
be a Tychonoff space. A subset
A
of
X
is called
functionally bounded
if
f(A)
is a bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}
for every
f\in C(X)
. We shall say that
X
is
closely embedded into
a space
Y
if there is an embedding
f:X\to Y
whose image
f(X)
is a closed subspace of
Y
. A base of the compact-open topology of
C_{k}(X)
(resp., a base of the pointwise topology of
C_{p}(X)
) consists of the sets

[K;\varepsilon]=\big\{f\in C(X):f(K)\subseteq(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\big\},

where
K
is a compact (resp., finite) subset of
X
and
\varepsilon>0
. We denote by
\mathcal{K}(X)
,
\mathcal{S}(X)
and
\mathcal{C}(X)
the family of all compact subsets of
X
, the family of all convergent sequences in
X
with their limit points, and the family of all countable subsets of
X
.
We denote by
\mathbf{s}=\{\tfrac{1}{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
the convergent sequence with the topology induced from
\mathbb{R}
.

A space
X
is called a
\mu
-space
if every functionally bounded subset of
X
has compact closure. We denote by
\beta X
,
\upsilon X
and
\mathcal{D}X
the Stone-Čech compactification, the Hewitt realcompactification and the Dieudonné completion of
X
, respectively. We denote by
\mu X
the
\mu
-completion
of
X
, i.e.
\mu X
is the smallest
\mu
-subspace of
\beta X
that contains
X
.
It is well-known that

X\subseteq\mu X\subseteq\mathcal{D}X\subseteq\upsilon X.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a continuous mapping between spaces
X
and
Y
. The
adjoint map

f^{\#}:C_{p}(Y)\to C_{p}(X)
of
f
is defined by
f^{\#}(g)(x):=g\big(f(x)\big)
.
Following Karnik and Willard
[
21
]
, a continuous mapping
p:X\to Y
is called
R
-quotient
(
real-quotient
) if
p
is continuous, onto, and every function
\phi:Y\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous whenever the composition
\phi\circ p
is continuous. Clearly, every quotient mapping as well as each surjective open mapping is
R
-quotient. The following characterization of
R
-quotient mappings is Proposition 0.4.10 of
[
1
]
.

Proposition 2.1
(
[
1
]
)

A continuous surjective mapping
p:X\to Y
is
R
-quotient if, and only if,
p^{\#}\big(C_{p}(Y)\big)
is a closed subspace of
C_{p}(X)
.

We shall use repeatedly the following assertion, see Theorem 4.7 of
[
33
]
, in which
\mathsf{id}_{Z}:Z\to Z
is the identity mapping.

Theorem 2.2
(
[
33
]
)

Let
p:X\to Y
be an
R
-quotient mapping between Tychonoff spaces. Then for every locally compact space
Z
, the product mapping
p\times\mathsf{id}_{Z}:X\times Z\to Y\times Z
is
R
-quotient.

R
-quotient mappings are important for us because of the following results which are considerably used in what follows.

Theorem 2.3
(
[
18
, Theorem 6.1]
)

For a Tychonoff space
X
, the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

X
is a
k_{\mathbb{R}}
-space;

(ii)

X
is an
R
-quotient image of some topological sum of compact spaces;

(iii)

X
is an
R
-quotient image of a
k_{\mathbb{R}}
-space.

Theorem 2.4
(
[
18
, Theorem 7.1]
)

For a Tychonoff space
X
, the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

X
is an
s_{\mathbb{R}}
-space;

(ii)

X
is an
R
-quotient image of a metrizable locally compact space;

(iii)

X
is an
R
-quotient image of an
s_{\mathbb{R}}
-space.

Proposition 2.5
(
[
18
, Proposition 4.4]
)

Let
X=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}X_{\alpha}
be a product of first countable spaces. If
|\mathcal{A}|
is not sequential, then
X
is an
s_{\mathbb{R}}
-space.
Consequently, for every
n\in\omega
, the spaces
\mathbb{R}^{\aleph_{n}}
and
\omega_{1}^{\aleph_{n}}
are
s_{\mathbb{R}}
-spaces.

3
Characterizations of Ascoli spaces

Two characterizations of Ascoli spaces are given in Propositions 5.4 and 5.10 of
[
3
]
, sequentially Ascoli spaces are characterized in Theorem 2.7 of
[
13
]
, and a Banach space characterization of (sequentially) Ascoli spaces is given in Theorem 2.5 of
[
16
]
. Below we give several new characterizations of (sequentially) Ascoli spaces.

Theorem 3.1

A Tychonoff space
X
is Ascoli if, and only if, for every compact space
K
, each separately continuous
k
-continuous function
\Phi:X\times K\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous.

Proof 1

Assume that
X
is an Ascoli space, and let
\Phi:X\times K\to\mathbb{R}
be a separately continuous
k
-continuous function for some compact space
K
. Consider the following mapping
\tilde{\Phi}:K\to C_{k}(X)
defined by
\tilde{\Phi}(f)(x):=\Phi(x,f)
. We claim that
\tilde{\Phi}
is continuous. To this end, fix
f\in K
, and let
W:=\tilde{\Phi}(f)+[A;\varepsilon]
be a standard neighborhood of
\tilde{\Phi}(f)
, where
A
is a compact subset of
X
and
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\Phi
is
k
-continuous, the restriction of
\Phi
onto
A\times K
is continuous. Therefore, for every
a\in A
, there are a neighborhood
U_{a}\subseteq A
of
a
and a neighborhood
V_{a}\subseteq K
of
f
such that
|\Phi(x,g)-\Phi(a,f)|<\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}
for all
x\in U_{a}
and
g\in V_{a}
. Since
A
is compact, there are
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in A
such that
A=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{a_{i}}
. Set
V:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}V_{a_{i}}
, then
V\subseteq K
is a neighborhood of
f
. Now, fix an arbitrary
g\in V
. For every
x\in A
, choose
1\leq i\leq n
such that
x\in U_{a_{i}}
, and then

|\tilde{\Phi}(g)(x)-\tilde{\Phi}(f)(x)|\leq|\Phi(x,g)-\Phi(a_{i},f)|+|\Phi(a_{i},f)-\Phi(x,f)|<\varepsilon,

which means that
\tilde{\Phi}(g)\in W
for every
g\in V
. Thus
\tilde{\Phi}
is continuous at
f
.

Fix arbitrarily
x_{0}\in X
and
f_{0}\in K
. To show that
\Phi
is continuous at
(x_{0},f_{0})
, let
\varepsilon>0
. Set
Q:=\tilde{\Phi}(K)
. Then, by the claim,
Q
is a compact subset of
C_{k}(X)
. As
C_{k}(X)
is Ascoli,
Q
is equicontinuous. Choose a neighborhood
U\subseteq X
of
x_{0}
such that

|\Phi(x,f)-\Phi(x_{0},f)|=|\tilde{\Phi}(f)(x)-\tilde{\Phi}(f)(x_{0})|<\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\quad\mbox{ for every }\;x\in U\;\mbox{ and each }\;f\in K.

Since
\Phi
is separately continuous, there is a neighborhood
O\subseteq K
of
f_{0}
such that
|\Phi(x_{0},f_{0})-\Phi(x_{0},f)|<\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}
for every
f\in O
. Therefore, for each
(x,f)\in U\times O
, we obtain

|\Phi(x,f)-\Phi(x_{0},f_{0})|\leq|\Phi(x,f)-\Phi(x_{0},f)|+|\Phi(x_{0},f)-\Phi(x_{0},f_{0})|<\varepsilon.

Thus
\Phi
is continuous at
(x_{0},f_{0})
.

Conversely, assume that for every compact space
K
, each separately continuous
k
-continuous function
\Phi:X\times K\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous. To prove that
X
is Ascoli, fix a compact subset
K
of
C_{k}(X)
. To show that
K
is equicontinuous, define
\Phi:X\times K\to\mathbb{R}
by
\Phi(x,f):=f(x)
. It is easy to see that the function
\Phi
is separately continuous. We claim that
\Phi
is
k
-continuous. To this end, let
A
be compact subset of
X
. Denote by
R:C_{k}(X)\to C_{k}(A)
the restriction mapping. Since
R
is continuous,
R(K)
is a compact subset of
C_{k}(A)
. As
A
is Ascoli, the mapping
A\times R(K)\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
(x,R(f)):=f(x)=\Phi(x,f)
is evenly continuous. Hence the mapping
\Phi:A\times K\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous. This proves the claim. Thus, by assumption, the function
\Phi
is continuous.

We show that
K
is equicontinuous at an arbitrary point
x_{0}\in X
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. For every
f\in K
, choose a neighborhood
V_{f}\subseteq K
of
f
and a neighborhood

A Tychonoff space

To obtain a full analogy of this result we use Proposition 4.3 from

. Recall that a space

Proposition 4.4
(

]

If a space

A space

if every point of

has a neighborhood which is a pseudocompact space. Clearly, if

is locally pseudocompact, then it is locally functionally bounded. It turns out that both these notions are equivalent.

Lemma 4.5

A space

is locally pseudocompact if, and only if, it is locally functionally bounded.

Proof 14

The necessity is trivial. To prove the sufficiency, assume that
X
is locally functionally bounded. We show that
X
is locally pseudocompact. Let

and let

be a functionally bounded neighborhood of

. Since subsets and closures of functionally bounded sets are functionally bounded in
X
, we assume that
U
is open and check that
\overline{U}
is pseudocompact. Assuming the converse we could find, by Theorem 3.10.22 of

of open in

sets which is discrete in
\overline{U}
. Put
U_{n}=W_{n}\cap U
and choose a point

. As

is discrete in the closed subset
\overline{U}
of

, we obtain that the sequence
\{U_{n}\}_{n\in\omega}
is discrete in

, choose a continuous function

such that

and

. Then the function

is well-defined, continuous and unbounded on

. Thus
\overline{U}
is not functionally bounded in

5
A characterization of
k_{\mathbb{R}}
-spaces

In the next theorem we show that in the definition of
k_{\mathbb{R}}
-spaces one can replace
k
-continuous
functions
on
X
by
k
-continuous
linear maps
defined on some compact sets of measures.
To this end, we need some notations.
Recall (see Corollary 7.6.5(a) of
[
20
]
) that the dual space of
C_{k}(Z)
is the space
M_{c}(Z)
of all regular Borel measures on
Z
with compact support.
A linear form
\chi
on
M_{c}(Z)
is called
\gamma
-continuous
if for every equicontinuous weak
∗
closed disk
D
in
M_{c}(Z)
, the restriction
\chi{\restriction}_{D}
is weak
∗
continuous.

Theorem 5.1

For a Tychonoff space
Z
, the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

Z
is a
k_{\mathbb{R}}
-space;

(ii)

each linear form
\chi
on
M_{c}(Z)
which is continuous on each weak
∗
compact subset of the form

B_{C(K)^{\ast}}=\{\mu\in M_{c}(Z):\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\subseteq K\mbox{ and }\|\mu\|\leq 1\},\;\;\mbox{ where $K\in\mathcal{K}(Z)$, }

(5.1)

is weak
∗
continuous
(
i.e.
\chi\in C(Z)
)
.

Proof 19

Let a linear form
\chi
on
M_{c}(Z)
be continuous on each weak
∗
compact subset of the form (
5.1
). Observe that any equicontinuous weak
∗
closed disk
D
in
M_{c}(Z)
is contained in
U^{\circ}
for some neighborhood
U
of zero in
C_{k}(Z)
. On the other hand, each set of the form
U^{\circ}
is an equicontinuous weak
∗
closed disk. So we can assume that
\chi
is
\gamma
-continuous if and only if
\chi{\restriction}_{U^{\circ}}
is weak
∗
continuous for every
U\in\mathcal{N}_{0}\big(C_{k}(Z)\big)
. Moreover, we can assume additionally that
U
has the form

U=[K;\varepsilon]:=\{f\in C(Z):K\mbox{ is compact in $Z$ and }f(K)\subseteq(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\big\}.

Therefore

U^{\circ}=\{\mu\in M_{c}(Z):\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\subseteq K\mbox{ and }\|\mu\|\leq\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\big\}.

Taking into account the homogeneity of locally convex spaces, we can say the following: A linear form
\chi
on
M_{c}(Z)
is
\gamma
-continuous if, and only if, the restriction
\chi{\restriction}_{B_{C(K)^{\ast}}}
is weak
∗
continuous, where
K\in\mathcal{K}(Z)
and
B_{C(K)^{\ast}}
denotes the closed unit ball of the dual space of the Banach space
C(K)
endowed with the weak
∗
topology.

By Theorem
1.1
,
Z
is a
k_{\mathbb{R}}
-space if and only if the space
C_{k}(Z)
is complete. By Grothendieck’s Completeness Theorem 9.2.2 of
[
20
]
,
C_{k}(Z)
is complete if and only if each
\gamma
-continuous linear form on
C_{k}(Z)^{\prime}=M_{c}(Z)
belongs to
C_{k}(Z)
. Now the theorem follows from the discussion in the previous paragraph.∎

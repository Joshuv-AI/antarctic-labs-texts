The Discriminant of the Characteristic Polynomial of the k k th Fibonacci sequence is not a member of the k k th Lucas sequence
Herbert Batte, Florian Luca

Abstract
Let k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 and { L n ( k ) } n ≥ 2 − k \{L_{n}^{(k)}\}_{n\geq 2-k} be the sequence of k k -generalized Lucas numbers whose first k k terms are 0 , … , 0 , 2 , 1 0,\ldots,0,2,1 and each term afterwards is the sum of the preceding k k terms. In this paper, we show that this sequence does not contain the discriminant of its characteristic polynomial.

Introduction

The study of recurrence sequences plays a fundamental role in number theory. Among the most well-known recurrence relations are those of the Fibonacci sequence and its companion, the sequence of Lucas numbers, both of which have been extensively generalized. Given an integer
k\geq 2
, the
k
-generalized Fibonacci sequence
\{F_{n}^{(k)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, is defined by the recurrence relation

F_{n}^{(k)}=F_{n-1}^{(k)}+F_{n-2}^{(k)}+\cdots+F_{n-k}^{(k)},\quad\text{for all}\ n\geq 2,

with initial values
F_{2-k}^{(k)}=\cdots=F_{-1}^{(k)}=F_{0}^{(k)}=0
and
F_{1}^{(k)}=1
. This sequence extends the classical Fibonacci sequence which occurs when
k=2
and has been studied in various algebraic and combinatorial contexts. A closely related sequence is the sequence of
k
-generalized Lucas numbers
\{L_{n}^{(k)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, which follows the same recurrence

L_{n}^{(k)}=L_{n-1}^{(k)}+L_{n-2}^{(k)}+\cdots+L_{n-k}^{(k)},\quad\text{for all}\ n\geq 2,

but with different initial conditions:
L_{2-k}^{(k)}=\cdots=L_{-1}^{(k)}=0,~L_{0}^{(k)}=2,~L_{1}^{(k)}=1
. The classical Lucas numbers correspond to
k=2
and have also been extensively investigated in number theory, cryptography, and primality testing. The above
k
-generalized versions offer a rich structure for further exploration.

Recently in
[
6
]
, Luca studied the discriminant of the characteristic polynomial of the
k
-generalized Fibonacci sequence, given by

g_{k}(X)=X^{k}-X^{k-1}-\cdots-X-1,

which has the formula

\text{Disc}\left(g_{k}\right)=\left(-1\right)^{\binom{k+1}{2}-1}\left(\dfrac{2^{k+1}k^{k}-(k+1)^{k+1}}{(k-1)^{2}}\right).

He showed that the absolute value
|\text{Disc}(g_{k})|
belongs to the sequence
\{F_{n}^{(k)}\}_{n\geq 0}
only for
k=2,3
. This result naturally raises the question of whether some similar property holds for the sequence of
k
-generalized Lucas numbers, since these two sequences share the same characteristic polynomial.

In this paper, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

There are no integer solutions
n\geq 0
and
k\geq 2
to the Diophantine equation

L_{n}^{(k)}=|{\text{\rm Disc}}(g_{k})|.

Some useful formulas and inequalities involving
L_{n}^{(k)}

In this section we collect some useful formulas and inequalities concerning
k
-generalized Lucas numbers. First, it is known that the formula

\displaystyle L_{n}^{(k)}=3\cdot 2^{n-2}\quad{\text{\rm holds~for}}\quad 2\leq n\leq k.

Furthermore,

L_{k+1}^{(k)}=3\cdot 2^{k-1}-2.

Using induction proves easily that the inequality

L_{n}^{(k)}<3\cdot 2^{n-2}\qquad{\text{\rm holds~for~all}}\qquad n\geq k+1.

The
k
-generalized Lucas numbers can be expressed in terms of the
k
-generalized Fibonacci numbers via the formula

L_{n}^{(k)}=2F_{n+1}^{(k)}-F_{n}^{(k)}.

(2.1)

To prove the above formula, it is enough to first notice that it holds for
n=-(k-2),\ldots,0,1,2
and then to invoke the fact that both sequences
\{L_{n}^{(k)}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
and
\{2F_{n+1}^{(k)}-F_{n}^{(k)}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}

satisfy the same
k
th order linear recurrence to conclude that the above formula holds for all
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Another usefull formula is

L_{n}^{(k)}=2L_{n-1}^{(k)}-L_{n-(k+1)}^{(k)}

for all
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
(to see why it holds, replace the left–hand side above by
L_{n-1}^{(k)}+L_{n-2}^{(k)}+\cdots+L_{n-k}^{(k)}
, cancel a term
L_{n-1}^{(k)}
on both sides of the resulting equation, and recognise that
the resulting formula is just the formula representing
L_{n-1}^{(k)}
as the sum of the previous
k
terms of the sequence). Reducing the above formula modulo
2
we get

L_{n}^{(k)}\equiv L_{n-(k+1)}^{(k)}\pmod{2},

showing that
\{L_{n}^{(k)}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is periodic modulo
2
with period
k+1
. This fact is so useful that we record it as a lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

The sequence
\{L_{n}^{(k)}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is periodic modulo
2
with period
k+1
.

Now let’s recall some more formulas related to the sequence of
k
-generalized Lucas numbers. It’s characteristic polynomial

g_{k}(X)=X^{k}-X^{k-1}-\cdots-X-1,

is irreducible in
\mathbb{Q}[X]
. This polynomial has exactly one real root greater than 1, denoted by
\alpha:=\alpha(k)
, and all its other roots lie inside the unit circle in the complex plane. Further, this real root lies within the range

\displaystyle 2\left(1-2^{-k}\right)<\alpha<2,\qquad{\text{\rm for~all}}\qquad k\geq 2.

(2.2)

If we need to refer to all the roots of
g_{k}(X)
, then we label them as
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}
with the convention that
\alpha:=\alpha_{1}
. For all
n\geq 0
and
k\geq 2
, it was proved in
[
2
]
that the
k
-generalized Lucas numbers are bounded by powers of
\alpha
as

\displaystyle\alpha^{n-1}\leq L_{n}^{(k)}\leq 2\alpha^{n}.

(2.3)

Next, we define the function

f_{k}(x):=\frac{x-1}{2+(k+1)(x-2)},\qquad x\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash\left\{2-{2}/{(k+1)}\right\}.

The Binet formula for the general term of the
k
-generalized Lucas sequence is given by

L_{n}^{(k)}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(2\alpha_{i}-1)f_{k}(\alpha_{i})\alpha_{i}^{n-1},\qquad{\text{\rm for~all}}\quad n\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Since
\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{k}
are inside the unit circle one would guess that only the first term
(2\alpha_{1}-1)f_{k}(\alpha_{1})\alpha_{1}^{n-1}
already approximates
L_{n}^{(k)}
and indeed the approximation

\displaystyle\left|L_{n}^{(k)}-f_{k}(\alpha)(2\alpha-1)\alpha^{n-1}\right|<\frac{3}{2},\qquad{\text{\rm for~all}}\quad n\geq 2-k

(2.4)

appears, for example, in
[
2
]
. This tells us that most of the size of
L_{n}^{(k)}
comes from the dominant term involving the real root
\alpha
, while the other terms contribute very little. A better estimate than (
2.4
) appears in Section 3.3 page 14 of
[
1
]
, but with a more restricted range of
n
in terms of
k
. It states that

\displaystyle\left|f_{k}(\alpha)(2\alpha-1)\alpha^{n-1}-3\cdot 2^{n-2}\right|<3\cdot 2^{n-2}\cdot\frac{36}{2^{k/2}}\qquad{\text{\rm provided}}\qquad n<2^{k/2}.

(2.5)

Another useful combinatorial formula for the
k
th generalized Fibonacci numbers is due to Cooper and Howard in
[
4
]
and states that

\displaystyle F_{n}^{(k)}=2^{n-2}+\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{n+k}{k+1}\right\rfloor-1}C_{n,j}\,2^{n-j(k+1)-2},\quad\text{where}\quad C_{n,j}:=(-1)^{j}\left(\binom{n-jk}{j}-\binom{n-jk-2}{j-2}\right).

(2.6)

In Equation (
2.6
), the assumption that
\displaystyle\binom{n}{m}=0
if either
n<m
or one of
n
or
m
is negative, holds.
Via equation (
2.1
) it yields the following formula for the
k
th generalized Lucas number:

\displaystyle L_{n}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=2F_{n+1}^{(k)}-F_{n}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=2\left(2^{n-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{n+1+k}{k+1}\right\rfloor-1}C_{n+1,j}\,2^{n-j(k+1)-1}\right)-2^{n-2}-\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{n+k}{k+1}\right\rfloor-1}C_{n,j}\,2^{n-j(k+1)-2}

\displaystyle=3\cdot 2^{n-2}+\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{n+1+k}{k+1}\right\rfloor-1}4C_{n+1,j}\,2^{n-j(k+1)-2}-\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{n+k}{k+1}\right\rfloor-1}C_{n,j}\,2^{n-j(k+1)-2}.

(2.7)

The
2
-adic valuation of
L_{n}^{(k)}

Since
\{L_{n}^{(k)}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is periodic modulo
2
with period
k+1
(see Lemma
2.1
), it follows that the
2
-adic valuation of
L_{n}^{(k)}
depends on the residue of
n
modulo
k+1
. So, let us write

n=r+m(k+1)
for some integers
m\geq 0
and
r\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
n=r+m(k+1)
with integers
m\geq 0
and
r\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\}
. The following congruences hold.

(i)

If
r=0
, then

L_{n}^{(k)}\equiv 2(-1)^{m}\pmod{2^{k-2}}.

(3.1)

(ii)

If
r=1
, then

L_{n}^{(k)}\equiv(4m+1)(-1)^{m}\pmod{2^{k-1}}.

(3.2)

(iii)

If
r=2
, then

L_{n}^{(k)}\equiv(4m^{2}+6m+3)(-1)^{m}\pmod{2^{k}}.

(3.3)

(iv)

If
r\geq 3
, then

L_{n}^{(k)}\equiv(-1)^{m}2^{r-2}\left(4\left(\binom{m+r+1}{m}-\binom{m+r-1}{m-2}\right)-\left(\binom{m+r}{m}-\binom{m+r-2}{m-2}\right)\right)\pmod{2^{k+r-2}}.

(3.4)

Proof.

Note that

\left\lfloor\frac{n+1+k}{k+1}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{r+(m+1)(k+1)}{k+1}\right\rfloor=m+1,

while

\left\lfloor\frac{n+k}{k+1}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{(r+k)+m(k+1)}{k+1}\right\rfloor=\left\{\begin{matrix}m+1&{\text{\rm if}}&r\geq 1,\\
m&{\text{\rm if}}&r=0.\end{matrix}\right.

Furthermore, for
j
in the summation ranges in (
2.7
), we have

n-j(k+1)-2=(r-2)+(m-j)(k+1)\geq k+r-2\qquad{\text{\rm except~when}}\qquad j=m.

The case
j=m
happens in the last term of the first summation in (
2.7
) and also in the last term of the second summation but only when
r\geq 1
. So, when
r=0
, assuming also that

m\geq 1
(otherwise
n=0
so
L_{n}^{(k)}=L_{0}^{(k)}=2
and congruence (
3.1
) holds), we have that

\displaystyle L_{n}^{(k)}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 2^{0-2}\cdot 4C_{n+1,m}\pmod{2^{k+0-2}}\equiv(-1)^{m}\left(\binom{n+1-mk}{m}-\binom{n+1-mk-2}{m-2}\right)\pmod{2^{k-2}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle(-1)^{m}\left(\binom{1+m(k+1)-mk}{m}-\binom{1+m(k+1)-mk-2}{m-2}\right)\pmod{2^{k-2}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle(-1)^{m}\left(\binom{m+1}{m}-\binom{m-1}{m-2}\right)\equiv 2(-1)^{m}\pmod{2^{k-2}}.

This proves (i). From now on,
r\geq 1
so both sums appearing in (
2.7
) have the same length
m
. It follows that

L_{n}^{(k)}\equiv 2^{r-2}\left(4C_{n+1,m}-C_{n,m}\right)\pmod{2^{k+r-2}}.

We treat the cases
r=1,~r=2
and
r\geq 3
separately. For
r=1
, if
m=0
then
L_{n}^{(k)}=L_{1}^{(k)}=1
and congruence (
3.2
) holds. For
m\geq 1
, we get

\displaystyle L_{n}^{(k)}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 2(-1)^{m}\left(\binom{n+1-mk}{m}-\binom{n-1-mk}{m}\right)-2^{-1}(-1)^{m}\left(\binom{n-mk}{m}-\binom{n-2-mk}{m-2}\right)\pmod{2^{k-1}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 2(-1)^{m}\left(\binom{2+m(k+1)-mk}{m}-\binom{m(k+1)-mk}{m-2}\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2^{-1}(-1)^{m}\left(\binom{1+m(k+1)-mk}{m}-\binom{-1+m(k+1)-mk}{m-2}\right)\pmod{2^{k-1}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 2(-1)^{m}\left(\binom{m+2}{m}-\binom{m}{m-2}\right)-2^{-1}(-1)^{m}\left(\binom{m+1}{m}-\binom{m-1}{m-2}\right)\pmod{2^{k-1}}.

When
m=1
, we get

-2\binom{3}{1}+2^{-1}\binom{2}{1}\equiv-5\pmod{2^{k-1}}\equiv(-1)^{1}(4\cdot 1+1)\pmod{2^{k-1}},

while when
m\geq 2
we get

\displaystyle L_{n}^{(k)}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 2(-1)^{m}\left(\binom{m+2}{2}-\binom{m}{2}\right)-2^{-1}(-1)^{m}\left(\binom{m+1}{1}-\binom{m-1}{1}\right)\pmod{2^{k-1}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 2(-1)^{m}\left(\frac{(m+2)(m+1)}{2}-\frac{m(m-1)}{2}\right)-(-1)^{m}\pmod{2^{k-1}}\equiv(-1)^{m}(4m+1)\pmod{2^{k-1}}.

This takes care of the congruence (
3.2
) in (ii). For (iii), let
r=2
. When
m=0
, we have
n=2
, so
L_{n}^{(k)}=3=(-1)^{0}(4\cdot 0^{2}+6\cdot 0+3)
. When
m\geq 1
, we get

\displaystyle L_{n}^{(k)}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 2^{2}(-1)^{m}\left(\binom{n+1-mk}{m}-\binom{n-1-mk}{m}\right)-2^{-1}(-1)^{m}\left(\binom{n-mk}{m}-\binom{n-2-mk}{m-2}\right)\pmod{2^{k}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 2(-1)^{m}\left(\binom{3+m(k+1)-mk}{m}-\binom{1+m(k+1)-mk}{m-2}\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle(-1)^{m}\left(\binom{2+m(k+1)-mk}{m}-\binom{m(k+1)-mk}{m-2}\right)\pmod{2^{k}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 4(-1)^{m}\left(\binom{m+3}{m}-\binom{m+1}{m-2}\right)-(-1)^{m}\left(\binom{m+2}{m}-\binom{m}{m-2}\right)\pmod{2^{k}}.

When
m=1
, we get

-4\binom{4}{1}+\binom{3}{1}\equiv-13\pmod{2^{k-1}}\equiv(-1)^{1}(4\cdot 1^{2}+6\cdot 1+3)\pmod{2^{k}},

while when
m\geq 2
we get

\displaystyle L_{n}^{(k)}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 4(-1)^{m}\left(\binom{m+3}{3}-\binom{m+1}{3}\right)-(-1)^{m}\left(\binom{m+2}{2}-\binom{m}{2}\right)\pmod{2^{k}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle(-1)^{m}(4m^{2}+6m+3)\pmod{2^{k}}.

Part (iv) is similar, that is, let
r\geq 3
and
m\geq 0
. Then (
2.7
) gives

\displaystyle L_{n}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3\cdot 2^{n-2}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}4C_{n+1,j}\,2^{n-j(k+1)-2}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}C_{n,j}\,2^{n-j(k+1)-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3\cdot 2^{n-2}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(4C_{n+1,j}-C_{n,j}\right)\,2^{n-j(k+1)-2}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle(-1)^{m}2^{r-2}\left(4\left(\binom{m+r+1}{m}-\binom{m+r-1}{m-2}\right)-\left(\binom{m+r}{m}-\binom{m+r-2}{m-2}\right)\right)\pmod{2^{k+r-2}}.

Linear forms in logarithms

To estimate how small a nonzero linear combination of logarithms of algebraic numbers can be, we apply a result known as a Baker-type lower bound. While several such bounds are known in the literature, we make use of a version due to Matveev, as presented in
[
7
]
. Since in our application we only work with forms in logarithms of positive integers, we bypass the regular prerequisites concerning heights and
present the inequality that interests us.

Theorem 5.1

(Matveev,
[
7
]
)
.

Let
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{t}
be positive integers larger than
1
. Put
\Gamma:=\gamma_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots\gamma_{t}^{b_{t}}-1
and assume
\Gamma\neq 0
. Then

\log|\Gamma|>-1.4\cdot 30^{t+3}\cdot t^{4.5}(1+\log B)A_{1}\cdots A_{t},

where
B\geq\max\{|b_{1}|,\ldots,|b_{t}|\}
and
A_{i}:=\log\gamma_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,t
.

We also need a result due to Bugeaud and Laurent
[
3
, Théorème 3]
):

Lemma 5.1

(Bugeaud and Laurent,
[
3
]
)
.

Let
b_{1},b_{2}
be positive integers and suppose that
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
are multiplicatively independent positive integers. Put

\log A_{i}\geq\max\{\log|\alpha_{i}|,\log p\}
for
i=1,2
, and

b^{\prime}:=\frac{b_{1}}{\log{A_{2}}}+\frac{b_{2}}{\log{A_{1}}}.

Then we have

\nu_{p}(\alpha_{1}^{b_{1}}-\alpha_{2}^{b_{2}})\leq\frac{24pg}{(p-1)(\log p)^{4}}\,B^{2}\,\log{A_{1}}\,\log{A_{2}},

where
g
is the smallest positive integer for which
\alpha_{i}^{g}\equiv 1\pmod{p}

and

B:=\max\left\{\log b^{\prime}+\log\log p+0.4,10,10\log p\right\}.

Bounding

We look at the Diophantine equation

\displaystyle L_{n}^{(k)}=\dfrac{2^{k+1}k^{k}-(k+1)^{k+1}}{(k-1)^{2}},

(6.1)

\displaystyle\alpha^{n-1}\leq L_{n}^{(k)}=\dfrac{2^{k+1}k^{k}-(k+1)^{k+1}}{(k-1)^{2}}<\dfrac{2^{k+1}k^{k}}{(k-1)^{2}}.

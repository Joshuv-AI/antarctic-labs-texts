The Deimginarity Cost of Quantum States
Xian Shi

Abstract
Here we address a task denoted as deimaginarity, which is to transform a state into a real state with the aid of random covariant-free unitary operations. We consider the minimum cost of randomness required for deimaginarity in the scenario of infinite copies and sufficiently small error, and we prove that the deimaginarity cost of a state ρ \rho is equal to its regularized relative entropy of imaginarity, which can be seen as an operational interpretation of the relative entropy of imaginarity.

II.1
The Resource Theory of Imaginarity

Here we consider a quantum system described by
\mathcal{H}
with finite dimensions. We denote
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
as the set of linear operators of
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
as the set of states on
\mathcal{H}.
Before reviewing the resource theory of imaginarity
[
17
]
, we fix an orthonormal reference basis
\{|i\rangle\}_{i=0}^{d-1}
of
\mathcal{H}
with its dimension
d
.

In the imaginarity theory, the real states are called the free states, which is the set
\mathcal{S}_{R}
of a quantum state
\rho
with a real density matrix,

\displaystyle\rho=\sum_{j,k}\rho_{jk}|j\rangle\langle k|,

(1)

here
\rho_{jk}\in\mathbb{R}.
In this manuscript, we denote the set of all real density matrices as
\mathcal{F}.
As all density matrices are Hermitian, a state
\rho\in\mathcal{F}
if and only if
\rho^{T}=\rho.
Due to the definitions of
\mathcal{F},

\mathcal{F}
is convex and affine. Besides, the set
\mathcal{F}
depends on the orthonormal reference basis
\{|i\rangle\}_{i=0}^{d-1}
.

Next we present the formulation of the free operations of this resource theory. In general, quantum channels can be represented by a set of Kraus operations
\{K_{i}\}
with
\sum_{i}K_{i}^{\dagger}K_{i}=\mathbb{I}
. The free operations of imaginarity theory can be specified by the Kraus operations
\{K_{i}\}
with
\langle j|K_{i}|k\rangle\in\mathbb{R}
, here
|j\rangle
and
|k\rangle
takes over all the elements in the basis
\{|i\rangle\}_{i=0}^{d-1}
. Due to the properties of free operations of imaginarity theory, the real operations cannot creat imginarity from real states.

Assume
U
is a unitary operator acting on the system
\mathcal{H}
,
\Lambda(\cdot)=U\cdot U^{\dagger}
is RNG if and only
\Lambda(\cdot)=O\cdot O^{T}
, here
O
is a real orthogonal operator. Let
\Theta(\cdot)=\frac{(\cdot)^{T}+(\cdot)}{2}
. When
\Lambda(\cdot)
satisfies
\Lambda\circ\Theta=\Theta\circ\Lambda
,
\Lambda(\cdot)
is covariant. Next if
\Omega(\cdot)=O(\cdot)O^{T}
is covariant, and
\sigma
is a free state

\displaystyle\Omega(\sigma)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[\Omega(\sigma^{T})+\Omega(\sigma)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega\circ\Theta(\sigma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta\circ\Omega(\sigma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[\Omega(\sigma)^{T}+\Omega(\sigma)],

that is,
\Omega(\sigma)=\Omega(\sigma)^{T}
. Hence,
\Omega(\sigma)
is a free state. That is, a covariant-free unitary operation cannot transform a free state into a resourceful state.

Before we introduce the process of deimaginarity of a generic state, we present the process of deimaginarity of the maximally resourceful state,
|\phi\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle+i|1\rangle)
. The deimaginary process can be achieved by two unitary operators
\mathbb{I}
and
\sigma_{z}
with equal probability,

\displaystyle|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|\longrightarrow\frac{1}{2}(|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|+\sigma_{z}|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|\sigma_{z})=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{I}_{2}.

Note that
\mathbb{I}_{2}\in\mathcal{F}
, and
\Theta(\sigma_{z}\cdot\sigma_{z})=\sigma_{z}\Theta(\cdot)\sigma_{z}.
That is, by the application of two covariant unitary operations with equal probability, we could erase the imaginarity of the maximally resourceful state. The above effects can also be implemented for a
d
dimensional quantum system. Let
\mathcal{H}_{d}
is a
d
-dimensional system, any state
\rho
acting on
\mathcal{H}_{d}
can be decomposed as

\displaystyle\rho=Re(\rho)+iIm(\rho),

here
Re(\rho)=\frac{\rho+\rho^{T}}{2}
is a real symmetric quantum state, and
Im(\rho)=\frac{1}{2i}(\rho-\rho^{T})
is a real anti-symmetric state. Moreover, there exists a real
O

[
35
]
such that

\displaystyle OIm(\rho)O^{T}=\mathrm{0}_{d-2r}\bigoplus_{k=1}^{r}\lambda_{k}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},

where
\lambda_{k}>0.
For a real orthogonal matrix
O
, we have

\displaystyle\Theta(O\cdot O^{T})=O\Theta(\cdot)O^{T},

(2)

Besides, for a state
\rho
of two-dimensional systems, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j,k=1}^{2}\sigma_{x}^{k}\sigma_{z}^{j}\rho{\sigma_{z}^{j}}^{\dagger}{\sigma_{x}^{k}}^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{I}_{2}.

(3)

That is, there always exists an ensemble of covariant-free unitaries to erase the imaginarity of a quantum state.

Next we present the resource theory of imaginarity of the composite systems. Assume
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space with reference orthogonal basis
\{|i\rangle\}
, let
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
be a system composed of
n
duplicates of
\mathcal{H}
with reference orthogonal basis
\{|i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{n}\rangle\}
. A state
\rho
acting on
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
is free in terms of the imaginarity resource if and only if
\rho^{T}=\rho
, here
(\cdot)^{T}
is with respect to the basis
|i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{n}\rangle
. As
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
is a composite space composed of
n
duplicates of
\mathcal{H}
, the transpose operator
(\cdot)^{T}
of
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
can be seen as
(\cdot)^{T}=\otimes_{k}(\cdot)^{T_{k}}
, here
(\cdot)^{T_{k}}

\mathcal{H}_{k}

II.2
Relative Entropy of the Resource Theory of Imaginarity

To quantify the imaginarity of a quantum state, Xue
et

al.
considered a quantifier called the relative entropy of imaginarity (REI)
\mathcal{I}_{r}(\cdot)
of a quantum state
[
28
]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{r}(\rho)=\mathop{\rm min}_{\sigma\in\mathcal{R}}D(\rho||\sigma),

(4)

where the minimum takes over all the real states
\sigma
, and
D(\rho||\sigma)=\mathrm{tr}[\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma)].
Moreover, the REI
\mathcal{I}_{r}(\rho)
can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{r}(\rho)=S(Re(\rho))-S(\rho),

(5)

where
Re(\rho)=\frac{\rho+\rho^{T}}{2}
, and
S(\rho)=-\mathrm{tr}(\rho\log\rho)
. The REI is additive.

Up to now, there exists few results on the REI,
\mathcal{I}_{r}(\cdot)
, here we present a direct operational meaning of the REI.

III
Main Results

Assume
\rho
is a state of a system with finite dimensions. A basic task is to transform a state
\rho
to a real state by applying covariant-free unitary operations randomly. A fundamental problem is to minimize the cost of randomness to make the imaginary state free.

Figure 1:
The task of deimginarity. Here
n
-copies of a state
\rho
is transformed by a set of covariant-free unitaries
\{U_{1},U_{2},\cdots,U_{2^{nR}}\}
. This task requires the final state
\sigma_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}
is real with respect to the reference orthonormal basis up to a small
\epsilon>0
in terms of the
1
-norm. The deimaginarity cost of a state
\rho
with respect to the reference basis is defined as the minimum
R
such that the above task is completed by properly choosing
2^{nR}
covariant-free unitaries and free states
\sigma_{n}
when
n\rightarrow\infty.

Definition 1

Assume
\rho
is a state of
\mathcal{H}
with finite dimensions. A rate
R
is denoted to be achievable to make
\rho
free with respect to the reference basis
\{|i\rangle\}_{i=0}^{d-1}
of
\mathcal{H}
if,
\forall\epsilon>0,
there exists a large
n
such that there exists a free state
\sigma_{n}
and a set of real ortthogonal operators
\{O_{k}\}_{k=1}^{2^{nR}}
with
O_{k}\Theta(\cdot)O_{k}^{\dagger}=\Theta(O_{k}\cdot O_{k}^{\dagger})
such that

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{O}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})-\sigma_{n}\rVert_{1}\leq\epsilon,

here

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{n}(\gamma)=\frac{1}{{2^{nR}}}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{nR}}O_{k}\gamma O_{k}^{\dagger}.

The deimaginarity cost of a state
\rho
with respect to the reference basis
\{|i\rangle\}_{i=0}^{d-1}
is

\displaystyle C_{d}(\rho)=

\displaystyle\inf\{R|\textit{$R$ is achievable to make $\rho$ free w. r. t. $\{|i\rangle\}_{i=0}^{d-1}$}\}.

Here the schematic diagram of the task of deimaginarity cost of a state is plotted in Fig
1
.

The main result of this article is to present the analytical representation of the deimaginarity cost of a state
\rho
,
C_{d}(\rho)
.

Theorem 2

Assume
\rho
is a state acting on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H},
then

\displaystyle C_{d}(\rho)=\mathcal{I}_{r}^{\infty}(\rho)=:\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathcal{I}_{r}(\rho^{\otimes n})

The proof of this theorem is placed in Sec.
VI

IV
Conclusion

In this paper, we considered the task of deimaginarity, and analyzed the minimal cost of randomness needed to make a state real. Particularly, we addressed the transformation task in the scenarios of an asymptotic limit of infinite copies and vanishing error, and we showed the minimum cost of the randomness for a state to make it real is equal to the regularized relative entropy of imaginarity of the state. At last, it would be meaningful to study the task in terms of other resources of some quantum system, such as steering
[
15
,
16
]
and contextuality
[
36
,
37
]
.

VI.1.1
Converse Part

Assume

\displaystyle\lVert\frac{1}{2^{nR}}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{nR}}O_{k}\rho O_{k}^{T}-\sigma_{n}\rVert_{1}\leq\epsilon,

(A.1)

here
O_{k}
satisfies
\Theta(O_{k}\cdot O_{k}^{T})=O_{k}\Theta_{n}(\cdot)O_{k}^{T}.
Next let

,as

,
\Theta(\sigma_{n})=\sigma_{n}
,

\displaystyle\lVert(\Theta\circ\mathcal{O}_{n})(\rho^{\otimes n})-\mathcal{O}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})\rVert_{1}

(A.2)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lVert(\Theta\circ\mathcal{O}_{n})(\rho^{\otimes n})-\sigma_{n}\rVert_{1}+\lVert\mathcal{O}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})-\sigma_{n}\rVert_{1}

(A.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lVert(\Theta\circ\mathcal{O}_{n})(\rho^{\otimes n})-\Theta(\sigma_{n})\rVert_{1}+\lVert\mathcal{O}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})-\sigma_{n}\rVert_{1}

(A.4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\lVert\mathcal{O}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})-\sigma_{n}\rVert_{1}\leq 2\epsilon,

(A.5)

here the first inequality is due to the triangle inequality of the
1
-norm, the equality is due to that
\sigma_{n}
is free, the second inequality is due to the contractive property of the trace-preserving quantum operations under the
1
-norm.

Assume
E
is an ancillary system with dimension

, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}=\frac{1}{2^{nR}}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{nR}}|k\rangle_{E}\otimes O_{k}^{A^{n}},

Next let

is a purification of

let

\displaystyle|\overline{\psi}_{n}\rangle_{EA^{n}Z^{n}}=\mathcal{T}|\psi^{\otimes n}\rangle_{A^{n}Z^{n}},

then

\displaystyle nR=

\displaystyle\log\mathop{\rm Dim}\mathcal{H}_{E}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle S(E)_{\overline{\psi}_{n}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle S(EZ_{n})_{\overline{\psi}_{n}}-S(Z_{n})_{\overline{\psi}_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(\mathcal{O}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n}))-nS(\rho),

(A.6)

here the second inequality is due to the subadditivity of von Neumann entropy
S(\cdot)
, the second equality follows from

\displaystyle S(EZ_{n})_{\overline{{\psi}_{n}}}=

\displaystyle S(A_{n})_{\overline{\psi}_{n}}=S(\mathcal{O}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})),

\displaystyle S(Z_{n})_{\overline{{\psi}_{n}}}=

\displaystyle S(Z^{n})_{\psi^{\otimes n}}=S(\rho^{\otimes n})=nS(\rho).

Next due to the inequality (
A.5
) and the Fannes inequality
[
38
]
, we have

\displaystyle S(\mathcal{O}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n}))\geq S((\Theta\circ\mathcal{O}_{n})(\rho^{\otimes n}))-n\eta(2\epsilon)\log d,

(A.7)

here
\eta(\cdot)
satisfies
\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}\eta(x)=0.
Then due to the concavity of the von Neumann entropy, we have

\displaystyle S(\mathcal{O}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n}))

(A.8)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{nR}}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{nR}}S(\Theta(O_{k}\rho^{\otimes n}O_{k}^{T}))

(A.9)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle S(\Theta(\rho^{\otimes n})),

(A.10)

here the first inequality is due to the concavity of

, the second inequality is due to that

is covariant-free and Lemma

.

Combing (

that is,

\displaystyle R\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathcal{I}_{r}(\rho^{\otimes n})-\eta(2\epsilon)\log d.

(A.12)

As the above inequality holds for all

and arbitrarily small

, we finish the proof of the converse part.

Lemma 7

\displaystyle S(Re(\rho)):=S(\frac{\rho+\rho^{T}}{2})\geq S(\rho).

Chiral effects on radiation and energy loss in quark-gluon plasma
Jeremy Hansen and Kirill Tuchin

Abstract
The emergent axion excitations in the quark-gluon plasma are generated by the sphaleron transitions. Their interactions with the photon and gluon fields are governed by the axion electro- and chromodynamics respectively. We discuss the effect of the emergent axion on the photon production and energy loss in the quark-gluon plasma. In particular, we review the Chiral Cherenkov effect, and the impact of the chiral anomaly on bremsstrahlung.

2
Electromagnetic field of a point charge in chiral matter

In this section we study the electromagnetic fields produced by fast charges in the chiral medium. There are two aspects of these fields that are relevant to the relativistic heavy-ion phenomenology: (i) the electric charges of the incident heavy-ions generate the electromagnetic field in the interaction region and (ii) an electric charge moving through the quark-gluon plasma looses a significant part of its energy by exciting the axion modes in the plasma. While the classical approach is a reasonable approximation in (i), it is too crude for computation of the energy loss in (ii). Nevertheless it provides important insights into the dynamics of the process which will serve us well when we perform a fully quantum calculation in the next section.

3
Chiral Cherenkov radiation and energy loss

3.1
Collisional energy loss in Fermi’s model

A fast charged particle moving through a medium experiences energy loss due to its interaction with medium particles. The classical calculation of the collisional energy loss in the non-chiral medium was first preformed by Fermi
[
30
]
. We generalized the Fermi model to the chiral medium with anomalous response to the magnetic field in
[
31
]
, utilizing the expressions for the electromagnetic field of the previous section. In this section we review the main results of this classical approach.

The energy loss rate can be computed as the flux of the Poynting vector out of a cylinder of radius
a
coaxial with the particle path. For a particle moving with velocity
v

z
-axis the total loss per unit length reads

\displaystyle-\frac{dE}{dz}=2a\real\int_{0}^{\infty}(E_{\phi\omega}B^{*}_{z\omega}-E_{z\omega}B^{*}_{\phi\omega})d\omega\,.

(19)

The real part of the integral arises from the pole in
1/\epsilon
at
\omega=\omega_{p}
as well as from the frequency intervals satisfying
k_{1}^{2}<k_{2}^{2}<0
or
k_{1}^{2}<0<k_{2}^{2}
. Section III of
[
31
]
lists the
corresponding expressions for the integrand of (
19

3.1.1
Ultrarelativistic limit

In the limit

the energy loss reads

\displaystyle-\frac{dE}{dz}=\frac{q^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{\omega_{p}^{2}}{v^{2}}\ln\frac{1.12v}{a\omega_{p}}-\frac{q^{2}}{4\pi}\int_{k_{1}^{2}<0<k_{2}^{2}}\frac{\omega}{v^{2}\epsilon}\frac{(s^{2}-k_{1}^{2})(v^{2}\epsilon-1)+\sigma_{\chi}^{2}}{k_{1}^{2}-k_{2}^{2}}d\omega\,.

(20)

The integration limit simplifies in the ultrarelativistic limit

\omega_{p}^{2}/\sigma_{\chi}<\omega<\gamma^{2}\sigma_{\chi}

\displaystyle-\frac{q^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{1}{2\sigma_{\chi}}\int^{\gamma^{2}\sigma_{\chi}}\left(-\frac{\sigma_{\chi}\omega}{\gamma^{2}}+\sigma_{\chi}^{2}\right)d\omega\,,

\displaystyle-\frac{dE}{dz}=\frac{q^{2}}{4\pi v^{2}}\left(\omega_{p}^{2}\ln\frac{v}{a\omega_{p}}+\frac{1}{4}\gamma^{2}\sigma_{\chi}^{2}\right)\,.

(22)

The first term on the right-hand-side was obtained by Fermi. We observe that the energy loss due to the anomaly, represented by the second term in (

In the non-chiral limit

\displaystyle-\frac{dE}{dz}=\frac{q^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{\omega_{p}^{2}}{v^{2}}K_{0}(a\omega_{p}/v)(a\omega_{p}/v)K_{1}(a\omega_{p}/v)+\frac{q^{2}}{4\pi v^{2}}\int_{s^{2}<0}\omega\left(v^{2}-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)d\omega\,.

Working in the radiation gauge

\displaystyle-\nabla^{2}{\bm A}=-\partial_{t}^{2}{\bm A}+\sigma_{\chi}({\bm\nabla}\times{\bm A})\,.

\displaystyle k^{2}=\omega^{2}-{\bm k}^{2}=-\lambda\sigma_{\chi}|{\bm k}|\,.

, whereas in the left-hand-side

. In the chiral matter, the dispersion relation (

\sigma_{\chi}

The goal of this section is to compute the rate of photon emission due to the finite chiral magnetic conductivity

In summary, the chiral term in the photon dispersion relation generates the spacelike gluon mode

4
Bremsstrahlung in chiral medium

Bremsstrahlung dominates the energy loss in conventional media at high energies. This section deals with the novel contributions to bremsstrahlung that arise in chiral media. In additional to the modification of the gauge boson dispersion relations that is the cause of the Chiral Cherenkov radiation which we discussed in the previous sections, the chiral contributions to bremsstrahlung come from the chiral terms of the gauge boson propagator. In this section we do the calculations in the framework of the axion QED. Most results can be readily generalized to the strong interactions. We divided this section in two parts dealing with the electric and magnetic terms in the modified photon propagator respectively
[

4.1
Photon propagator and gauge symmetry in chiral medium

The classical equation of motion of the photon field is

\displaystyle\left[g^{\mu\nu}\partial^{2}-\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}-\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}b_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\right]A_{\nu}=j^{\mu}\,.

(86)

It is invariant under the gauge transform

\displaystyle A_{\nu}\to A_{\nu}+\partial_{\nu}\chi\,

(87)

where
\chi
is an arbitrary function. In the Lorenz gauge
\partial\cdot A=0
the photon Green’s function obeys the equation

\displaystyle\left[g^{\mu\nu}\partial^{2}-(1-1/\xi)\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}-\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}b_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\right]D_{\nu\lambda}(x)=i\delta^{{{\mu}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.74307pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.74307pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.85834pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.04167pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}_{{\mathchoice{\makebox[4.86232pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.86232pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.95248pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.10892pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\lambda}}}\delta^{4}(x)\,,

(88)

where
\xi
is the gauge parameter. In momentum space (
88
) becomes

\displaystyle\left[-k^{2}g^{\mu\nu}-(1-1/\xi)k^{\mu}k^{\nu}+i\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}b_{\alpha}k_{\beta}\right]D_{\nu\lambda}(k)=i\delta^{{{\mu}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.74307pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.74307pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.85834pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.04167pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}_{{\mathchoice{\makebox[4.86232pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.86232pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.95248pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.10892pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\lambda}}}\,,

(89)

which is solved by
[
67
]

\displaystyle D^{\nu\lambda}(k)=

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{k^{4}+b^{2}k^{2}-(k\cdot b)^{2}}\bigg\{k^{2}g^{\nu\lambda}+b^{\nu}b^{\lambda}+i\epsilon_{\nu\lambda\alpha\beta}b^{\alpha}k^{\beta}

\displaystyle-\frac{(b\cdot k)}{k^{2}}(k^{\nu}b^{\lambda}+k^{\lambda}b^{\nu})+\left[b^{2}\xi-(1-\xi)\left(k^{2}-\frac{(b\cdot k)^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)\right]\frac{k^{\lambda}k^{\nu}}{k^{2}}\bigg\}

(90)

The terms in the second line vanish when
D^{\nu\lambda}
is inserted in the Feynman diagram due to the current conservation. Similar expression was obtained in
[
43
]
.

The wavefunction of a photon with given momentum and polarization at finite
b=(\sigma_{\chi},{\bm 0})
is a plane wave

\displaystyle A^{\mu}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega}}e^{\mu}_{k}e^{-ik\cdot x}\,,

(91)

where
e^{\mu}_{k}=(0,{\bm e}_{k})
is a circular polarization vector satisfying the
the Lorenz gauge
k\cdot e_{k}={\bm k}\cdot{\bm e}_{k}=0
. Once the wave functions (
91
) are chosen, there is no remaining gauge freedom. Only circularly polarized photons solve the equation of motion (
86
).
This stands in stark contrast with the free photon wavefunction which is also given by the plane wave as in (
91
). However, since
k^{2}=0
, there still remains gauge freedom to transform the polarization vector
e^{\mu}\to e^{\mu}+\chi k^{\mu}
without changing the form of the wavefunction or violating the Lorenz gauge condition.

The photon polarization sum is

\displaystyle d^{ij}=\sum_{\text{pol}}e^{i}_{k}e^{*j}_{k}=\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{{\bm k}^{2}}\,.

(92)

There are no remaining gauge freedom to transform it to any other form. In particular
d^{ij}
cannot be replaced with
-g^{\mu\nu}
. As an illustration consider the amplitude
e_{k}\cdot\mathcal{M}
. Let
k
be in the

we can write

\sum_{\text{pol}}|e_{k}\cdot\mathcal{M}|^{2}=|\mathcal{M}_{x}|^{2}+|\mathcal{M}_{y}|^{2}=|\mathcal{M}_{x}|^{2}+|\mathcal{M}_{y}|^{2}+|\mathcal{M}_{z}|^{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{{\bm k}^{2}}|\mathcal{M}_{0}|^{2}\neq-g^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{M}_{\mu}\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\nu}\

since

4.2.3
Discussion

In this subsection we discussed the bremsstrahlung radiation emitted by a fast particle traveling in the chiral medium supporting the chiral magnetic current with constant chiral conductivity
\sigma_{\chi}
. In arriving at our conclusions we made a number of assumptions. First of all, we treated the chiral conductivity
\sigma_{\chi}
as a constant. However, it does evolve in time albeit slowly. Its temporal evolution is characterized by the relaxation time
\tau
, which we assumed to be the large parameter in our calculation. The spatial extent of the domain can be neglected as long as it is larger than photon formation time
t_{f}
which is a very good approximation considering that the formation time must be shorter than the mean free path
\ell
in the Bethe-Heitler limit.

Another critical assumption we made is that photon formation time
t_{f}
is much shorter than the mean-free path
\ell
. As argued in
[
77
]
, the condition
t_{f}\ll\ell
translates into the requirement that
m,\mu\ll E\ll\mu\sqrt{\omega\ell}
. Since
t_{f}
is proportional to
\omega
, this approximation breaks down at high energies where one must take account of the multiple scatterings of the projectile in the medium. The resulting LPM quantum interference effect
[
78
,
79
]
is an important feature of the bremsstrahlung spectrum and must certainly be taken into account at higher energies.

4.3

In the previous subsection we discussed scattering due to the Coulomb part

\displaystyle J^{0}({\bm q})=eZ\,,\qquad{\bm J}({\bm q})=i{\bm q}\times{\gothfamily m}\,.

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{{\bm q}^{2}-\sigma_{\chi}^{2}}\left[i({\gothfamily m}\times{\bm q})^{\ell}+\frac{\sigma_{\chi}}{{\bm q}^{2}}({\gothfamily m}\cdot{\bm q}q^{\ell}-{\bm q}^{2}{\gothfamily m}^{\ell})\right]\,.

5
Summary

We discussed radiation and energy loss processes in the quark-gluon plasma in the framework of axion electro- and chromodynamics. The Chern-Simons term (
1
) stemming from the chiral anomaly
[
21
,
22
]
takes account of the
CP
-odd axion excitations in chiral media. The main novel effect is the Chiral Cherenkov radiation whose magnitude competes with the conventional energy loss mechanisms. We also reviewed the effect of the axion excitations on the bremsstrahlung. The most distinctive features of all these effects is strong dependence on the gauge boson polarization. A comprehensive phenomenological analysis requires incorporation of the novel energy loss channels into the numerical packages describing jets in hot nuclear medium.

The total photon spectrum emitted by the quark-gluon plasma is also sensitive to the chiral effects, especially in the infrared region. Many mechanisms that modify the photon spectrum in chiral media were discussed in the literature. Some of them also requite the presence of the external magnetic field
[
83
,
84
]
, but others do not
[
85
,
86
,
5
]
.

While this review focuses mostly on spatially uniform axion excitations (
{\bm\nabla}\theta=0
), the spatial inhomogeneities are very important in applications to Dirac and Weyl semimetals as they induce the anomalous Hall current. It was shown that Chiral Cherenkov radiation exists in such systems as well
[
33
,
60
]
and in fact can be a source of technologically important polarized THz radiation
[
87
]
. The spatial gradients are also important in the quark-gluon plasma if the size of the
CP
-odd domains is of the order a few fm or less. In this case, one has to impose appropriate boundary conditions on the fields at the domain walls. It was argued in
[
15
]
that the boundary conditions strongly impact the dynamics of the chiral magnetic effect. Optical properties of chiral domains in materials were discussed in
[
88
]
who derived the modified Fresnel equations in axion electrodynamics.

Axion electrodynamics was used to investigate other chirality sensitive processes in the quark-gluon plasma. In
[
89
]
we considered radiation of the Chandrasekhar-Kendall states
[
90
]
by an ultrarelativistic quark scattering off thermal gluons. These topological states are characterized by the magnetic helicity which can spontaneously change due to spatially inhomogeneities in the axion field
[
91
]
.

This review by no means intends to review the entire field of axial electrodynamics, not even as it applies to the quark-gluon plasma. This subject is too large to fit in so few pages. Our hope is to convey to the reader some of the excitement that we have working on these subjects.

Hepcidin agonists grape seed proanthocyanidins and berberine hydrochloride improve immunity and prevention against bacterial infection in grass carp (Ctenopharyngodon idella)
Authors: Penghui Yu, Mengzhen Feng, Jianguo Su, Gailing Yuan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractHepcidin is an antimicrobial peptide and iron-regulating factor that has been shown to play a crucial role in combating bacterial diseases, making it a viable alternative to antibiotics. However, its widespread application is limited due to its susceptibility to degradation in vivo and high production costs. Certain extracts from traditional Chinese medicinal plants exhibit significant potential in treating iron overload conditions. This study aims to identify an effective and practical herbal agent as a hepcidin agonist. The results indicated that grape seed proanthocyanidins (GSP) and berberine hydrochloride (BBR) significantly upregulate the expression levels of hepcidin and ferritin genes in L8824 cells. Additionally, both compounds exhibited excellent in vitro antibacterial activity. Interestingly, the combined application of these two substances yields unexpectedly favorable results. Oral administration of GSP + BBR effectively promoted growth in grass carp, enhanced survival rates, and significantly increased serum innate immunity indicators. Furthermore, it reduced bacterial loads in the head kidney, spleen, hepatopancreas, and intestinal of grass carp following infection. Additionally, it elevated the expression levels of immune genes IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α in the head kidney after infection, and markedly alleviated pathological damage in intestinal tissues. These results demonstrated that GSP and BBR act as hepcidin agonists to enhance the expression of endogenous hepcidin, providing a novel approach for the prevention of bacterial infections in aquaculture.Keywords: Hepcidin, Bacterial diseases, GSP, BBR, Immune response

Introduction

Studies have shown that fish are recognized as a major contributor to protein sources [1]. Grass carp (Ctenopharyngodon idella), herbivorous freshwater fish, is one of the four domestic fishes in China, and it ranks the first in the production yield among freshwater farmed fishes [2]. In recent years, with the increasing development of scale and density of grass carp culture, aquaculture has been seriously affected by diseases, especially bacterial and viral diseases, resulting in high mortality and serious economic losses of farmed fish [3]. For years, antibiotics and other chemicals have been widely used to prevent and treat bacterial diseases [4,5]. However, the overuse of antibiotics can lead to public health problems such as bacterial multi-drug resistance and antibiotic residues [6]. With the continuous improvement of people's quality of life, the requirements for food quality and environmental conditions are becoming more and more stringent. Therefore, it is urgent to find eco-friendly alternatives to antibiotics.

Iron is essential for the reproduction and growth of almost all living organisms, from bacteria to humans, and it plays an important role in cell and tissue function [7,8]. hepcidin is an antibacterial peptide and iron regulator secreted by the fish itself, which has a variety of effects such as enhancing immunity [9]. Our previous study proved that feeding grass carp with hepcidin protein could improve growth performance and immune protection. However, as an antimicrobial protein, hepcidin has disadvantages of easy degradation, unstable properties, high production cost and difficult preservation, which limit its wide application in aquaculture [10]. Therefore, the development of drugs targeting hepcidin has become a hotspot of current research. Some studies have shown that certain herbal extracts have great potential in the treatment of iron overload diseases [11]. Chinese herbal medicine is an ideal class of pure, natural, environmentally friendly green medicine. It is not only widely sources, stable nature, but also low price, and is widely used in the prevention of disease in aquaculture [12].

Further investigation into Chinese herbal medicine has revealed its antibacterial, antiviral, antiparasitic, and antifungal properties [13]. Its affordability, abundance, low toxicity, and minimal side effects make it attractive for aquaculture [14]. Given the limitations on antibiotics in modern farming, Chinese herbal medicine is gaining attention as a feed additive for disease management [15]. Many studies have proven that Chinese herbal medicine can promote fish growth, improve disease resistance and immunity, and improve the quality of fish [16]. In addition, more importantly, Chinese herbal medicine is usually characterized by the use of Chinese herbal medicine formulations, which consist of two or more herbs capable of preventing and treating multiple diseases through potentially synergistic effects [17]. The benefits of combination therapy include greatly reducing the potential for the body to develop drug resistance, improving efficacy and reducing side effects [18]. Therefore, combination therapy is also considered to be an effective approach against drug-resistant strains.

Grape seed proanthocyanidins (GSP) are a class of polyphenolic compounds, which can be found in some plants and their fruits. GSP have a variety of biological activities, including antioxidant, antibacterial and anti-inflammatory functions, and are considered to be promising aquafeed additives [19]. Studies have shown that adding GSP in fish feed helps to improve growth performance, antioxidant capacity and disease resistance of the body [20]. Berberine hydrochloride (BBR) is a plant-based isoquinoline alkaloid, a natural compound isolated mainly from Chinese herbs such as Huang Lian and Berberis, which has antibacterial and anti-inflammatory functions [21]. BBR, as a natural antibacterial substance, has been used in clinical treatment as an over-the-counter drug. In aquaculture, BBR is considered a natural alternative to antibiotics and is used to prevent enteritis and other diseases by combining it with other antimicrobial drugs [22].

A. hydrophila poses a significant threat to grass carp farming and is known for its zoonotic potentia [23]. Currently, the problem of multiple drug resistance of A. hydrophila has become increasingly prominent, which brings potential risks to grass carp breeding industry and human health. This study explores four Chinese herbal medicines to identify hepcidin agonists in grass carp hepatocytes, aiming to discover natural compounds that enhance hepcidin expression. The selected hepcidin herbal agonist will then be tested as a feed additive to evaluate its effects on the growth, immune function, and intestinal health of grass carp, ultimately providing valuable insights for using hepcidin herbal agonists to combat bacterial diseases in aquaculture.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

Experimental animal treatment protocols adhered to the ethical guidelines of animal welfare and were duly authorized by the Animal Committee at Huazhong Agricultural University, with the corresponding ethical approval code being HZAUFI-2024–0008.

Cell and bacteria culture

Grass carp liver cell line (L8824) was cultured in M199 supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS, ExCell Bio, Suzhou, China) and augmented with 200 IU/ml penicillin-streptomycin to ensure sterility and cell viability. The cells were maintained at 28 °C in a humidified atmosphere of 5 % CO2.

A. hydrophila (ATCC 7966) was stored at −80 °C. The preserved strains underwent two consecutive plate activations, and a single colony was inoculated into 50 mL LB liquid medium and cultured for 12 h (180 rpm, 28 °C). The bacterial suspension was centrifuged (6000 g, 10 min) to obtain the precipitate; the supernatant was discarded, and the precipitate was washed three times with a sterile phosphate buffer solution (PBS, pH 7.4). Finally, the bacterial pellet was suspended in a PBS buffer solution, and the viable bacteria were counted using a blood cell counting plate under an optical microscope.

Screening of herbal agonists for hepcidin

We selected four Chinese herbal medicines, namely berberine hydrochloride (BBR) (50 μM), grape seed proanthocyanidins (GSP) (50 μM), rosmarinic acid (RA) (50 μM) and astragalus polysaccharide (APS) (50 μM). Four Chinese herbal medicines were prepared with M199 medium and diluted in four concentration gradients. They were stored at 4 °C. When L8824 cells grew into a monolayer covered state, the dilutions of the four herbs were added to the cell culture dishes at 100 μL/well, and incubation was continued for 6 h in the incubator. After that, the cells were digested with trypsin and collected into 1.5 mL centrifuge tubes for RNA extraction and cDNA synthesis. The mRNA expression of hepcidin and ferritin was detected by qRT-PCR.

Preventive effect of GSP and BBR in L8824 cells infected with A. hydrophila

We evaluated the preventive effect of GSP and BBR by infecting L8824 cells with A. hydrophila. L8824 cells were seed in a 24-well culture plate at a density of 5 × 105 cells per well for 24 h at 28 °C. Subsequently, cells were continued to be cultured with M199 medium containing GSP (10 μM) and BBR (50 μM) for 6 h Then the medium was replaced with fresh serum-free medium and the bacterial suspension was co-incubated with the cells for 2 h Then, cells were washed and fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde for 15 min and stained with 0.05 % (w/v) crystal violet for 30 min, then washed with water, and drained. Finally, the plates were photographed under a light box (Bio-Rad).

Feeding experiment

Healthy grass carp (25.5 g ± 0.5 g) were acquired from the Xingfu Farm (Huanggang, China). Before the experiment, grass carp was fed with commercial feed twice a day after a two-week adaptation period. At the start of the experiment, 360 grass carp were randomly distributed into 12 fiberglass tanks with 30 grass carp per tank. The experiment was divided into four groups, including blank control group, GSP group (300 mg/kg), BBR group (300 mg/kg) and GSP + BBR group (300 mg/kg). Each group contained 90 fish in three replicate experiments (30 × 3). Fish were fed twice daily (at 8:00am and 16:00pm) at 2 % body weight for 28 days. A 10 % water change was performed daily. During the feeding period, the water quality parameters were monitored twice weekly and were as follows: water temperature 27.4–29.4 °C, dissolved oxygen ≥ 6.5 mg/L, pH 7.95 ± 0.09, NH 4-N ≤ 0.05 mg/L.

Cell activity assay

The activity of GSP and BBR to L8824 cell was detected by CCK-8 kit. L8824 cell suspension was added to each well of a 96-well culture plates (100 μL per well) and incubated overnight in a 28 °C incubator. And then, final concentrations of 0 μM, 10 μM, 50 μM, 100 μM, and 200 μM of GSP and BBR were added to 96-well plates, and the cells containing the stimulants were continued to be incubated in the incubator for 3 h, 6 h, 12 h, and 24 h, respectively. Then, cell viability was assessed using a CCK-8 kit according to the manufacturer's instructions. Finally, the OD490 was measured using a microplate reader. The cell survival rate under the action of herbs was calculated based on the formula given below:

Cell survival rate (%) = [(average OD of cell control group - average OD of experimental dosing group) / average OD of cell control group] × 100 %

Growth performance and sample collection

Growth performance was evaluated by several growth parameters at the end of the experimental period. The fish were anesthetized by MS-222. Total length (cm) and body weight (g) were measured at the beginning and at the end of the experiment. Weight gain (WG) (%) = (W2 - W1 / W1) × 100, specific growth rate (SGR) (% day−1) = [ln (W2) - ln (W1) / t] × 100, and condition factor (CF) (%) = (W / L3) × 100 were calculated on the 28 days of the experiment, where W1 and W2 are initial and final weight in grams, n1 and n2 are initial and final number of surviving fish, L is standard length in centimeters, and t is the number of feeding days.

Three fish per replicate were randomly sampled at the end of the trial for analysis of serum biochemical parameters, immune related genes, and intestinal histopathological sections.

The challenge experiment in vivo

In this experiment, healthy grass carp were divided into four groups with triplicates. The feeding regime before challenge were as follows: blank control group, GSP group, BBR group and GSP + BBR group. Each group of fish was fed twice a day for 28 days. After that, grass carp were challenged with A. hydrophila (3 × 107 CFU) by intraperitoneal injection, and the fish were monitored every 24 h for 10 d for clinical signs of disease and survival rate. Tissue bacterial loading assay

Tissue bacterial load was quantified by the dilution coated plate method. At 72 h after challenge, four fish were taken from each group, and intestine, hepatopancreas, spleen, and head kidney were collected and grinded by multisample tissue grinding machine, respectively. These tissue homogenates were used to detect the bacterial load in tissues. The homogenates were diluted using sterile PBS (pH = 7.4), spread on Rimler-Shotts (RS) medium plates for 12–16 h at 28 °C. Colonies of A. hydrophila were counted by two independent investigators.

Serum biochemistry index assay

Blood samples were collected from the caudal vein and placed for 2 h at 25 °C. After centrifugation (3000 rpm/min, 10 min) at 4 °C, the serum samples were collected and stored at −80 °C. The serum biochemical indexes of complement 3 (C3), lysozyme, total superoxide dismutase (TSOD), and Catalase activity (CAT) were examined by the commercial kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China). Sera of four grass carp were taken from each group for detection.

qRT-PCR assay of immune genes and iron metabolism-related genes

The expression levels of immune-related genes IL-1β, IL-6, IL-10, and TNF-α were detected in spleen and head kidney. Total RNA was extracted from with RNAisoPlus kit (Takara, Dalian, China) according to the manufacturer's instructions, followed by DNase I treatment. Total RNA was quantified by absorbance ratio of 260 nm and 280 nm with NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, USA), and the quality was evaluated using 1 % agarose gel electrophoresis. The cDNA was synthesized using the HiScript® II Q Select RT SuperMix (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) according to the manufacturer's instruction for subsequent qPCR. All the cDNA products were diluted to 250 ng/µL, and the qPCR was performed using the AceQ® qPCR SYBR® Green Master Mix (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) on a Roche LightCycle® 480 System (Roche, Basel, Switzerland) according to the manufacturer's instructions. The primers mentioned in previous literatures are shown in Table 1. 18S rRNA was used as the housekeeping gene, and the relative mRNA expression levels were calculated with the (2−ΔΔCT) method [24]. qRT-PCR data were mean ± SD from three biological replicates.

Histopathological examination

The intestinal tissues were dissected and fixed immediately in 10 % neutral buffered formalin for 24 h, dehydrated, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned. Then, 4 μm sectioned samples were mounted on aminopropyl triethoxysilane-coated slides. Following the deparaffinization in xylene, sections were rehydrated, stained with hematoxylin-eosin staining (H&E), and mounted with neutral gum. Images were collected by CaseViewer software.

Statistical analysis

The results were expressed as the means ± standard deviation (SD), and all the statistical analysis were done using SPSS 26.0 package. Statistical significance was assessed using Student's two-tailed t-test (each experimental group relative to the control group). (p < 0.05). The experimental data for each group underwent the KruskalWallis test, followed by Dunn's multiple comparisons (with Bonferroni adjustment) to determine significance (p < 0.05). Different superscript letters within each group (a, b, and c) indicate significant variations. The survival rates were analyzed by Mantel-Cox test; * denotes significant variation.

Results

Identification of GSP and BBR as hepcidin agonists

To identify the Chinese herbs most effective in stimulating hepcidin function in grass carp, we selected four widely available, stable, and cost-effective herbs commonly utilized for disease prevention in aquaculture: berberine hydrochloride (BBR), grape seed proanthocyanidins (GSP), rosmarinic acid (RA), and astragalus polysaccharides (APS). The mRNA expressions of hepcidin and ferritin were assessed via qRT-PCR after a 6-hour stimulation of L8824 cells with these herbal medicines. As shown in Fig 1A and 1B, GSP and BBR significantly enhanced the expression levels of hepcidin and ferritin genes in L8824 cells. The safety profiles of GSP and BBR were determined using the CCK-8 assay (Fig 1C-D). The results showed that GSP and BBR at concentrations of 0 μM, 10 μM, 50 μM, and 100 μM did not affect the viability of L8824 cells, whereas concentrations above 200 μM led to the death of nearly half the cells. The optimal concentration and exposure time for GSP were determined to be 100 μM and 6 h, while for BBR, they were 50 μM and 6 h.

We further investigated the protective effects of GSP and BBR against A. hydrophila infection in L8824 cells. Microscopic observation and crystal violet staining revealed that a significant number of cells in the control group died following the challenge (Fig. 1E). In contrast, a greater number of surviving cells were observed in groups treated with GSP and BBR, with the combination of both showing the most pronounced protective effect. These results indicate that GSP and BBR enhance the ability of L8824 cells to resist A. hydrophila infection, with the combined use of these compounds yielding the best preventive outcome.

GSP and BBR improved the growth indexes of grass carp

To investigate the effects of GSP and BBR on individual growth, we assessed the growth indexes of grass carp after 28 days of feeding with these supplements (Table 2). Compared to the control group, the WG, SGR and CF of the three experimental groups showed notable increases, suggesting that GSP and BBR positively influence grass carp growth. Among all four groups, the GSP + BBR combination yielded the highest WG and SGR, demonstrating the most effective weight gain.

GSP and BBR enhanced the survival rate of grass carp challenged with A. hydrophila

The immune-enhancing effects of GSP and BBR were investigated through oral administration of these compounds to grass carp, followed by a challenge with A. hydrophila. We established specific experimental groups, detailed procedural protocols, and defined sampling time points (Fig. 2A). The results indicated that the survival rates in the GSP + BBR group (43 %) and the GSP group (30 %) were significantly higher than that of the control group (12 %). No significant difference in survival rates was observed between the BBR group and the control group (Fig. 2B). The tissue bacterial load is a critical indicator of the severity of bacterial infection in fish. 72 h post-challenge, tissue samples from the spleen, head kidney, hepatopancreas, and intestine of grass carp were collected and inoculated onto RS solid medium for colony counting. The results showed that the bacterial load in the three experimental groups was significantly reduced compared to the control group (Fig. 2C-F). Notably, the concentrations of A. hydrophila in the spleen, head kidney, hepatopancreas, and intestine were lowest in the GSP + BBR group, correlating with the observed survival rate. These findings strongly support the effectiveness of GSP and BBR in mitigating bacterial infections in grass carp.

GSP and BBR establish serum immune protection status

The serum antioxidant enzyme activity results demonstrated that the activities of T-SOD and CAT in both the GSP and BBR groups were significantly elevated compared to the control group (Fig. 3A-B). Notably, the GSP + BBR group showed the highest levels of T-SOD and CAT among all groups. Furthermore, prior to the challenge, the activities of lysozyme and Complement 3 in the GSP + BBR group were significantly greater than those in the other three groups. After the challenge, while the activities of lysozyme and complement C3 decreased at D30, they rose again at D32, with the GSP + BBR group maintaining significantly higher levels than the other groups (Fig. 3C-D). These findings strongly suggest that the combined oral administration of GSP and BBR effectively enhances the serum immune status of grass carp.

GSP and BBR activated hepcidin to regulate of iron levels

To investigate the effects of oral administration of GSP and BBR on iron metabolism in grass carp, we measured ferritin levels, serum iron content, and the expression of hepcidin and ferroportin. Results indicated that serum ferritin increased while serum iron decreased at D32 across all groups. Compared to the control group, the three treatment groups exhibited higher ferritin and lower serum iron levels at D32, with the GSP + BBR group showing the most pronounced effect (Fig. 4A-B). At D28, hepcidin expression in the GSP + BBR group was significantly elevated compared to the other groups (Fig. 4C), while the expression of ferroportin was lower than in the control group (Fig. 4D). On D32, hepcidin levels in all experimental groups were significantly higher than in the control group, and ferroportin expression remained lower. The results indicate that GSP and BBR regulate hepcidin and ferroportin expression in the hepatopancreas, reducing iron outflow, lowering serum iron levels, and inhibiting bacterial growth by limiting available iron.

GSP and BBR regulated immune gene expression

The mRNA expression of immune-related genes IL-1β, IL-2, IL-6, and TNF-α was assessed by qRT-PCR in the head kidney tissues of grass carp at 1, 3 and 7 days post-challenge (Fig. 5A-D). Compared to the control group, mRNA levels of IL-1β, IL-2, IL-6, and TNF-α were significantly elevated in the GSP, BBR, and GSP + BBR groups at D30 and D32, with the GSP + BBR group showing the highest expression. By D36, the expression levels of immune genes in all groups stabilized. The above results showed that GSP and BBR could improve the pro-inflammatory response of fish to resist the invasion of Aeromonas hydrophila and improve the survival rate.

GSP and BBR protect grass carp from bacterial infection by reducing tissue damage

To assess the extent of intestinal tissue damage in grass carp post-challenge, we collected intestinal samples three days after the challenge for H&E staining. As illustrated in Fig. 6, the control group exhibited significant pathological changes, including a clear separation between the submucosa and the muscular layer of the intestine. In contrast, the GSP + BBR group displayed the healthiest intestinal tissue. Overall, these findings suggest that oral administration of GSP and BBR enhances resistance to bacterial infections and mitigates tissue damage in vivo. Notably, the combination of GSP and BBR exhibited the most significant protective effects.

Discussion

In this study, we investigated two Chinese herbal agents, grape seed proanthocyanidins (GSP) and berberine hydrochloride (BBR), known to enhance hepcidin expression. Compared with the control group, the expression of hepcidin and ferritin increased nearly 5-fold when GSP and BBR treated cells. Hepcidin is a crucial regulator of systemic iron homeostasis. It can bind to ferroportin, causing its ubiquitination degradation and internalization, thus resulting in the decreased concentration of extracellular iron [25]. This mechanism is vital for protecting organisms against infections from ferrophilic and gram-negative bacteria [26]. Previous research indicated that administering exogenous hepcidin to grass carp can lower serum iron levels and enhance survival against Flavobacterium columnare. [27]. Hepcidin-induced hypoferremia serves as a significant host defense mechanism [28]. Our results show that GSP and BBR can significantly improve cell survival after challenge when treated with GSP and BBR. Studies have shown that iron is the main element required for bacterial growth and plays a key role in many fundamental biological processes. In addition, pathogen growth in the host depends heavily on the ability of pathogens to capture and utilize iron. hepcidin limits the bioavailability of iron and effectively inhibit pathogen growth [29]. This explains our experimental results very well. GSP and BBR can increase the expression of hepcidin and fertin, reduce the content of iron, limit the utilization of iron by A. hydrophila, thus improve the survival rate of cells.

One of the regulatory effects of Chinese herbal medicine is to promote growth [30]. Chinese herbal medicine can promote the secretion of digestive enzymes, thus promoting the growth and metabolism of aquatic animals [31]. Recent studies highlighted that Cissus quadrangularis and Eclipta alba in freshwater prawn diets increased digestive enzyme activities and improved growth performance. [32]. Dietary Aloe vera extract improved growth performance of carp and elevated the body weight gain by a fold compared with control diets [33]. Our study confirmed that supplementing GSP in grass carp feed improved both growth indices and immune function, consistent with observations in carp and rainbow trout fed GSP-enhanced diets [34]. Furthermore, the use of BBR in aquaculture has been validated to prevent bacterial diseases, with studies showing its synergistic bactericidal effects with enrofloxacin against various fish pathogens, including A. hydrophila, Vibrio traumaticus and Pseudomonas aeruginosa [35].

Serum biochemical indicators are the best reflection of the animal health [36]. In this study, adding GSP and BBR to grass carp feed significantly increased serum superoxide dismutase levels, an essential antioxidant defense against free radicals that aids in pathogen resistance [27]. GSP, recognized as a potent natural antioxidant, has demonstrated higher efficacy than vitamins C and E [37]. GSP can directly scavenge free radicals produced by the body, and studies have demonstrated this function [38]. Additionally, serum lysozyme levels increased following GSP and BBR treatment, suggesting enhanced immune defenses, as lysozyme hydrolyzes bacterial cell walls, leading to their lysis [39]. Similarly, the content of complement C3 was also significantly increased after feeding GSP and BBR. It is well known that the complement system is an important part of the innate immune system and plays an important role in regulating inflammation and pathogen clearance [40]. Collectively, these results indicate that GSP and BBR supplementation can enhance antioxidant capacity and stimulate beneficial immune responses in fish.

Survival rate is a key indicator of drug efficacy [41]. In the model of A. hydrophila infection in grass carp, the GSP + BBR group showed the highest protection rate and significantly higher survival rate than the control group. Meanwhile, GSP + BBR group had the lowest bacterial load and intestinal tissue damage. This suggests that GSP and BBR can improve the resistance of fish to bacterial infection, thus increasing the survival rate of the organism. The expression of immune-related genes in immune organs further contributes to fish immunity [42]. IL-1β and TNF-α, as early inflammatory factors, activate neutrophils and macrophages, enhancing immune responses [43]. Additionally, IL-2 regulates Th1 cytokines critical for pathogen clearance [44]. In this study, GSP and BBR supplementation upregulated the expression of these immune-related genes in the head kidney and spleen, particularly in the BBR and GSP + BBR groups. Thus, GSP and BBR act as immune stimulants, inducing pro-inflammatory responses and bolstering the inherent immune defenses of grass carp against pathogen invasion.

Conclusion

In summary, our results suggested that GSP and BBR function as hepcidin agonists. Oral administration of GSP and BBR, whether individually or in combination, enhances growth performance and innate immune responses in grass carp. Moreover, GSP and BBR regulate iron metabolism, decreasing the iron bioavailability to bacteria, thereby bolstering the host's defense against infections. Furthermore, combined feeding of GSP and BBR yields the highest survival rate after challenges. Therefore, GSP and BBR are promising candidates for herbal medicines to combat bacterial diseases in aquaculture.

Abstract
Quantum error correction is essential for the development of any scalable quantum computer. In this work we introduce a generalization of a quantum interleaving method for combating clusters of errors in toric quantum error-correcting codes. We present new n n -dimensional toric quantum codes, where n ≥ 5 n\geq 5 , which are featured by lattice codes and apply the proposed quantum interleaving method to such new n n -dimensional toric quantum codes. Through the application of this method to these novel n n -dimensional toric quantum codes we derive new n n -dimensional quantum burst-error-correcting codes. Consequently, n n -dimensional toric quantum codes and burst-error-correcting quantum codes are provided offering both a good code rate and a significant coding gain when it comes to toric quantum codes. Another important consequence from the presented n n -dimensional toric quantum codes is that if the Golomb and Welch conjecture in [ 1 ] regarding the Lee sphere in n n dimensions for the respective close packings holds true, then it follows that these n n -dimensional toric quantum codes are the only possible ones to be obtained from lattice codes. Moreover, such a methodology can be applied for burst error correction in cases involving localized errors, quantum data storage and quantum channels with memory.

The necessary theoretical background related to toric quantum codes can mostly be found in
[
2
,
3
]

]

A stabilizer code

is the simultaneous eigenspace with eigenvalue 1 comprising all the elements of an Abelian subgroup
S
of the Pauli group
P_{n}
, called the

. The elements of the Pauli group on
n

\displaystyle P_{n}=\{\pm I,\pm iI,\pm X,\pm iX,\pm Y,\pm iY,\pm Z,\pm iZ\}^{\otimes n},\ \mathrm{where}

\displaystyle I=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&1\\
\end{array}\right),\ X=\sigma_{x}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\\
\end{array}\right),

Y=\sigma_{y}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-i\\
i&0\\
\end{array}\right)\ \mathrm{and}\ Z=\sigma_{z}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\\
\end{array}\right).

(1)

Thus,
C=\{\mid\psi\rangle\in H^{n}\ \mid\ M\mid\psi\rangle=\ \mid\psi\rangle,\ \forall\ M\in S\}

[
13
]
.

Kitaev’s toric codes form a subclass of stabilizer codes and they are defined in a

square lattice of the torus (Figure 1), where

is a positive integer. Qubits are in one-to-one correspondence with the edges of the square lattice. The parameters of this class of codes are
[[2q^{2},2,q]]

equals the number of edges

(connected sum of
g
tori

The distance is the minimum between the number of edges contained in the smallest homologically nontrivial cycle of the lattice and the number of edges contained in the smallest homologically nontrivial cycle of the dual lattice. Recall that the square lattice is self-dual and a homologically nontrivial cycle is a path of edges in the lattice which cannot be contracted to a face. Therefore the smallest of these two paths corresponds to the orthogonal axes either of the lattice or of the dual lattice. Consequently,
d=q

[
14
]
.

The stabilizer operators are associated with each vertex and each face of the square lattice (lattice) (Figure
1
). Given a vertex

, the vertex operator

is defined by the tensor product of

]

A_{v}=\bigotimes_{j\in E}\sigma_{x}^{\delta(j\in E_{v})}\ \mathrm{and}\ B_{f}=\bigotimes_{j\in E}\sigma_{z}^{\delta(j\in E_{f})},

(2)

where

The toric code consists of the space fixed by the operators

and

C=\{\mid\psi\rangle\in H^{n}\ \mid\ A_{v}\mid\psi\rangle=\ \mid\psi\rangle\ \mathrm{and}\ B_{f}\mid\psi\rangle=\ \mid\psi\rangle,\ \forall\ v,f\}.

(3)

The dimension of

is 4, that is,

qubits.

(4)

Now we show that

]

n

n

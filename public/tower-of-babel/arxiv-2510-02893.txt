A functional analytic theory for differential equations on Banach spaces with slowly evolving parameters
Dirk Doorakkers, Daniele Avitabile, Jan Bouwe van den Berg

Abstract
This paper provides a functional analytic approach to differential equations on Banach space with slowly evolving parameters. We develop a Fenichel-like theory for attracting subsets of critical manifolds via a Lyapunov-Perron method. This functional analytic approach to invariant manifold theory for fast-slow systems of differential equations has not been fully developed before, especially for the case that the fast subsystem lives on an infinite-dimensional Banach space. We provide rigorous functional analytic proofs for both the persistence of attracting critical manifolds as smooth slow manifolds, as well as the validity of slow manifold reduction near slow manifolds. Several aspects of our proofs are new in the literature even for the finite-dimensional case. The theory as developed here provides a rigorous framework that allows one (for example) to derive formal statements on the dynamics of biologically meaningful spatially extended models with slowly varying parameters.

Our theorems and proofs can be considered a generalization of center manifold theory
for equilibria of ODEs. Specifically,
theorem

1
can be seen as
an extension of the Center Manifold Theorem, whilst
theorem

2
can
then be seen as an extension of the concept of Center Manifold Reduction, which is
classically understood as the idea that in the center-stable manifold of a
non-hyperbolic equilibrium (or periodic orbit) all orbits are asymptotically
attracted in forward time to an orbit lying on the center manifold, see for example

[
40
]
. In the following we provide some more perspective on how our proof methods and
results relate to existing literature on Lyapunov-Perron approaches to the Center Manifold Theorem and
singularly perturbed systems of differential equations.

A classical text on proving the Center Manifold Theorem via the Lyapunov-Perron
method is that by Carr
[
8
]
, and many texts on the subject seem to closely
follow his approach, see for example Kelley
[
41
]
, Chapter 6 of Henry

[
31
]
, Sijbrand
[
53
]
, Chow & Lu
[
12

13
]
, Chapter 10
of Hale & Verduyn Lunel
[
28
]
and Chicone & Latushkin
[
10
]
. The
proof relies on constructing a contraction mapping on a space of
parameterizations of manifolds via a variation of constants formula (also referred to in literature as Lyapunov-Perron formula).
Other authors construct this contraction mapping on exponentially weighted spaces of bounded solutions
instead (see for example Vanderbauwhede & Van Gils
[
56
]
or Haragus & Iooss
[
29
]
) but
their construction remains conceptually similar to that of Carr otherwise. A direct
extension of Vanderbauwhede’s theory to perturbations of systems of the form

eq.

4
in the context of fast-slow systems of ODEs can be
found in
[
50
]
.

Lyapunov-Perron approaches for proving the Center Manifold Theorem, as worked out by Kelley

[
41
]
, Carr
[
8
]
and others, are closely related to methods for
integral manifolds developed by Bogolyubov and Mitropolsky and their students in the
Soviet literature. The application of these methods to analyzing periodic solutions of non-linear
differential equations is also known as the Krylov-Bogolyubov-Mitropolsky (KBM)
method for periodic solutions. Expositions on the KBM-method can be found in texts
by Hale
[
26
,
27
]
. Attempts to adapt methods for integral manifolds to the
setting of fast-slow systems of ODEs appear to have been done first by Zadiraka

[
62
]
and Baris
[
3
]
, and this work appears to be very similar to the
methods from Carr
[
8
]
and others mentioned above. Yi
[
60
,
61
]

proved existence of integral manifolds and their properties in a setting that can be
seen as a generalization of that of system
eq.

4
. More
related historical notes and references can be found in
[

,
45
,
51
]
.

We provide a functional analytic theory for perturbations of systems of the type

most of the texts mentioned above, which we believe allows for significantly more

[

and others. Moreover, we do not require a Lipschitz

for existence of slow manifolds in contrast to other

[

,

]
, see also

.
As mentioned before, our constructions appear to be more similar to those used in Chapter 9 of Henry

[

]
, but we still deviate from his approach in certain proof steps, see also

In
sections

6
to
8
we
provide proofs for
theorem

36
(which implies

theorem

2
), and this Theorem can be seen as a generalization of the
classical reduction principle from Pliss (see
[
40
]
). Many texts on center
manifold theory, including those relying on the Lyapunov-Perron method, treat this
reduction principle by first proving the existence of certain invariant
foliations. The existence of a locally smooth reduction map (like
P_{\varepsilon}
of

theorem

2
) is implied by these invariant foliations. See for
example
[
11
]
for a Lyapunov-Perron approach to invariant foliations near
equilibria of evolution equations. We opt for a reverse approach where we prove the
existence of a locally smooth reduction map, and then (as mentioned before) invariant
foliations follow from the reduction map. In the context of singularly perturbed
system of differential equations, we are not aware of any literature that has proven
existence of invariant foliations near slow manifolds in this manner yet. So our
proof approach appears to be a new contribution, even in the case of
finite-dimensional systems. The approach is in many ways close to the approach we
follow for proving the existence of smooth slow manifolds in

sections

3
to
5
.

For neighborhoods of isolated non-hyperbolic points on a critical manifold,
reduction of the dynamics to a finite-dimensional center manifold was partially
worked out in
[
2
]
for some integro-differential equations and PDEs with
slowly varying parameters. Our work here can be considered complementary to
[
2
]

as part of a broader effort towards building a complete theory for constructing relaxation oscillations,
canards and delayed bifurcations in spatially extended systems with slowly varying
parameters. While the setting of system
eq.

1
does not directly apply
to PDEs, the proofs worked out here should provide a big step towards an analogous
theory for PDEs as well.

Our proof methods provide an alternative functional analytic approach to fast-slow systems
on Banach space compared to the more geometric material in texts like
[
4
,
7
,
46
]
.
We believe such a functional analytic approach to have utility for the analysis of biologically meaningful
spatially extended models, such as integro-differential equations with neuroscientific interpretation. We
also foresee that the methods developed in this paper can be of interest to other studies
into the extension of classical theory for fast-slow systems of differential equations, for example in the
case of adding noise to a fast-slow system, or when considering spatially extended systems with slowly
evolving spatial domain.

i=1
we shall also simply denote
L(A,B)
for the space
L^{1}(A,B)
.

Let
U\subseteq A
be an open subset. We denote by
C^{k}(U,B)
the space of all continuous functions
f:U\rightarrow B
that are
k
-times continuously differentiable in the sense of Fréchet. We denote the
k
-th derivative of
f
as
D^{k}f
, and note that then for all
1\leq i\leq k
we have
D^{i}f\in C^{0}(U,L^{i}(A,B))
.

Next, we define the space of bounded continuous functions from
A
to
B
as

BC^{0}(U,B):=\{f\in C^{0}(U,B):\sup_{a\in U}\|f(a)\|<\infty\}.

The space
BC^{0}(U,B)
is a Banach space when equipped with the supremum norm

\|f\|_{BC^{0}(U,B)}:=\|f\|_{\infty}=\sup_{a\in U}\|f(a)\|.

For
k\geq 1
we inductively define

BC^{k}(U,B):=\left\{f\in BC^{k-1}(U,B)\cap C^{k}(U,B):\sup_{a\in U}\|D^{k}f(a)\|<\infty\right\},

and
BC^{k}(U,B)
is a Banach space when equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{BC^{k}(U,B)}:=\|f\|_{BC^{k-1}(U,B)}+\|D^{k}f\|_{\infty},

where

\|D^{k}f\|_{\infty}=\sup_{a\in U}\|D^{k}f(a)\|.

Moreover, we define for
k\geq 1
the spaces

BC^{k-1,1}(U,B):=\left\{f\in BC^{k-1}(U,B):\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\in U\,:\\
a_{1}\neq a_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{\|D^{k-1}f(a_{2})-D^{k-1}f(a_{1})\|}{\|a_{2}-a_{1}\|}<\infty\right\}.

The spaces
BC^{k-1,1}(U,B)
are Banach spaces when equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{BC^{k-1,1}(U,B)}:=\|f\|_{BC^{k-1}(U,B)}+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\in U\,:\\
a_{1}\neq a_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{\|D^{k-1}f(a_{2})-D^{k-1}f(a_{1})\|}{\|a_{2}-a_{1}\|}.

We note that
BC^{k-1,1}(U,B)
is the subspace of functions
f
in
BC^{k-1}(U,B)
for which
D^{k-1}f
is also uniformly Lipschitz.

Let
V\subseteq B
be an open subset. Sometimes we shall also consider the space of
\gamma
-bounded functions over
\mathbb{R}
and into
V
which is defined as

BC_{\gamma}^{0}(\mathbb{R},V):=\{f\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R},V):\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}e^{\gamma|t|}\|f(t)\|<\infty\},

which is a Banach space when equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{\gamma,\infty}:=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}e^{\gamma|t|}\|f(t)\|.

The following Lemma from Henry
[
31
]
shall turn out useful for proofs later on;

Lemma 3
.

Suppose
A,B
are Banach spaces, and let
U\subseteq A
an open subset. For every
k\geq 1
, a closed and bounded ball in
BC^{k-1,1}(U,B)
is a closed and bounded subset of
BC^{0}(U,B)
.

Proof.

See Henry
[
31
]
pages 151 – 152.

If in particular
\overline{U},\overline{V}
are closed subsets of Euclidean space, then we assume the norm on them to be the standard Euclidean norm. In this case we also use single bars
|\,.\,|
to indicate the norm. We shall do this as well when working with the norm on
\mathbb{X}
or with product norms on
\mathbb{X}
times some subset of Euclidean space.

For functions of multiple variables, we denote the uniform Lipschitz constant with respect to one variable as
Lip
subscript the relevant variable. So for example for a function
R:\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{X}
, we have

\text{Lip}_{x}R:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{X},\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\\
a_{1}\neq a_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{|R(a_{2},y)-R(a_{1},y)|}{|a_{2}-a_{1}|}.

3
Existence of slow manifolds

This Section and
section

4
treat theory which implies

theorem

1
from the Introduction for the case
k=1
. We consider the
system of differential equations

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=F(x(t),y(t)),

(5)

\displaystyle\dot{y}(t)

\displaystyle=g(x(t),y(t)),

where
x\in\mathbb{X}
,
y\in\overline{V}
with
V
an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}(n\geq 1)
, and
F\in C^{0}(\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V},\mathbb{X})
,
g\in C^{0}(\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V},\mathbb{R}^{n})
are locally Lipschitz. Also
g(x,y)=0
whenever
y\in\partial V
, and we can write

F(x,y)=A_{0}(y)x+R_{0}(x,y),

with
A_{0}\in C^{0}(\overline{V},L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X}))
.

We are looking for an invariant manifold of system
eq.

5
that can be parameterized over the
y
-variable via a function
h\in BC^{0,1}(\overline{V},\mathbb{X})
. Such a manifold corresponds to a slow manifold in the context of fast-slow systems, see for example
[
31
,
50
,
18
]
.

To find such an invariant manifold, consider a
candidate

\sigma:\overline{V}\to\mathbb{X}
for the map
h
that we are seeking. We can then restrict the second component of the system
eq.

5
to
x=\sigma(y)
and consider a solution
y=\tilde{\psi}(t)
of

\dot{y}=g(\sigma(y),y).

Plugging this into the first component of the system
eq.

5
naturally leads us to study the non-autonomous equation

\dot{x}=A_{0}(\tilde{\psi}(t))x+R_{0}(x,\tilde{\psi}(t)).

To start with the linear part,
for arbitrary
\tilde{\psi}\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R},\overline{V})
, we denote by
T_{0}(t,s;\tilde{\psi})
the process associated to the non-autonomous linear ODE

\dot{x}(t)=A_{0}(\tilde{\psi}(t))x(t).

That is, for each
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{X}
, the set
\{T_{0}(t,s;\tilde{\psi})\xi:t\geq s\}
is the (forward) orbit of this ODE for
x(s)=\xi
. Observe that whilst in general
x(t)\in\mathbb{X}
for all
t\geq s
, in the case
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{R}
we have the explicit expression

T_{0}(t,s;\tilde{\psi})=e^{\int^{t}_{s}A_{0}(\tilde{\psi}(\tilde{s}))\,d\tilde{s}}.

The process on
\mathbb{X}
has the following properties;

Lemma 4
.

For arbitrary
\tilde{\psi}\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R},\overline{V})
, it holds for
t\geq s
, with
s\in\mathbb{R}
, that
T_{0}(t,s;\tilde{\psi})
is a bounded linear operator on
\mathbb{X}
with the properties

1.

T_{0}(s,s;\tilde{\psi})=I_{\mathbb{X}},\,T_{0}(t,r;\tilde{\psi})=T_{0}(t,s;\tilde{\psi})T_{0}(s,r;\tilde{\psi})
for all
t\geq s\geq r
;

2.

(t,s)\mapsto T_{0}(t,s;\tilde{\psi})
is continuous in the operator norm for all
t\geq s
;

3.

\partial_{t}T_{0}(t,s;\tilde{\psi})=A_{0}(\tilde{\psi}(t))T_{0}(t,s;\tilde{\psi})
for
t\geq s
;

4.

\partial_{s}T_{0}(t,s;\tilde{\psi})=-T_{0}(t,s;\tilde{\psi})A_{0}(\tilde{\psi}(s))
for
t\geq s
.

Proof.

These statements can be proven as in Chapter 5 of Pazy
[
47
]
.

Remark 5
.

The family
T_{0}(t,s;\tilde{\psi})
of linear operators with
(t,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
t\geq s
that we call ‘process’ here is also known under several other terms in the literature, such as two-parameter semigroup and Green’s function.

We shall also make the following assumption on the process
\{T_{0}(t,s,\tilde{\psi}):t\geq s\}
, when
\tilde{\psi}
is a solution to the
y
-subsystem of
eq.

5
. The assumption entails exponential stability of the process uniformly for all such
\tilde{\psi}
:

\mathrm{(H1)}

There exist
\mu>0
and
K\geq 1
such that

|T_{0}(t,s;\tilde{\psi})\xi|\leq Ke^{-\mu(t-s)}|\xi|\quad\text{for }\xi\in\mathbb{X},t\geq s,

(6)

for all
\tilde{\psi}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},\overline{V})
that solve the ODE
\dot{y}=g(x(t),y)
for some function
x\in BC^{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{X})
.

Remark 6
.

By applying
corollary

66
from the Appendix (or alternatively combine

lemma

65
with a lemma from Daletskii and Krein

[
16
]
, similarly to what is done in the proof of
corollary

67
), one can show that if
\overline{V}
is bounded and there
exists
\mu>0
such that

\sup\{\real\lambda:y\in\overline{V},\lambda\in\sigma(A(y))\}<-\mu,

then there exists
N_{0}>0
such that if

\sup\{|g(x,y)|:x\in\mathbb{X},y\in\overline{V}\}\leq N_{0},

then the assumption (H1) is satisfied with the same choice of
\mu
. This means
assumption (H1) is satisfied when applying the theory in this Section to the
perturbation of compact critical manifolds of fast-slow systems as we do in
section

9
.

We now make two further assumptions (H2)-(H3) on system
eq.

5
:

\mathrm{(H2)}

There exist constants
M_{0},M_{1}^{x},M_{1}^{y}>0
with
M^{x}_{1}<\mu/K
such that

\sup\left\{|R_{0}(x,y)|:(x,y)\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V}\right\}\leq M_{0},

as well as

|R_{0}(x_{2},y)-R_{0}(x_{1},y)|\leq M_{1}^{x}|x_{2}-x_{1}|

for all
(x_{1},x_{2},y)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V}
, and

|F(x,y_{2})-F(x,y_{1})|\leq M_{1}^{y}|y_{2}-y_{1}|

for all
(x,y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V}\times\overline{V}
;

\mathrm{(H3)}

There exists a constant
N_{1}>0
with
N_{1}<\mu-KM_{1}^{x}
such that

|g(x_{2},y_{2})-g(x_{1},y_{1})|\leq N_{1}|(x_{2},y_{2})-(x_{1},y_{1})|

for all
(x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2})\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V}
.

Note that (H2)-(H3) ensure that solutions to system
eq.

5
exist for all time.

Remark 7
.

The condition
N_{1}<\mu-KM_{1}^{x}
in assumption (H3) on the global Lipschitz constant
N_{1}
for
g
can be interpreted as a timescale separation, as it ensures that the growth rate for
y
in System
eq.

5
is strictly smaller than the rate of decay for
x
.

By the above remark, under assumptions (H1)-(H3) the
y
-variable in system
eq.

5
may be interpreted as slow when compared to the fast
x
-variable, and therefore
we can interpret an invariant manifold of
eq.

5
that is parameterized over the
y
-variable as a slow manifold:

Definition 8
.

If (H1)-(H3) hold, we say that a function
h\in BC^{0,1}(\overline{V},\mathbb{X})
parameterizes a slow manifold of system
eq.

5
if for each
\eta\in\overline{V}
the unique solution
(x(t),y(t))
, with
t\in[s,\infty)
, of system
eq.

5
with initial condition
(x(s),y(s))=(h(\eta),\eta)
has the property that
x(t)=h(y(t))
for all
t\geq s
. The slow manifold itself is then defined as

S_{h}:=\{(h(\eta),\eta):\eta\in\overline{V}\}.

In this Section we aim to prove the following Theorem:

Theorem 9
(Existence of slow manifolds).

Assume that (H1)-(H3) are satisfied for system
eq.

5
. Suppose that there exists
\delta>0
with
KM_{1}^{x}+N_{1}(\delta+1)<\mu
such that

\displaystyle\frac{KM_{1}^{y}}{\mu-KM_{1}^{x}-N_{1}(\delta+1)}<\delta.

Then the following statements hold:

1.

There exists a function
h\in BC^{0,1}(\overline{V},\mathbb{X})
that parameterizes a slow manifold of system
eq.

5
with
\|h\|_{BC^{0,1}(\overline{V},\mathbb{X})}\leq KM_{0}/\mu+\delta
;

2.

Moreover, any solution
(x,y)\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V})
of system
eq.

5
for which

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|x(t)|<\infty,

is contained in the manifold
S_{h}=\{(h(\eta),\eta):\eta\in\overline{V}\}
.

Remark 10
.

The existence of a

as mentioned in

is
guaranteed for sufficiently small

; namely set

\delta:=\frac{2KM^{y}_{1}}{\mu-KM^{x}_{1}},

then for

we have

\frac{KM^{y}_{1}}{\mu-KM^{x}_{1}-N_{1}(\delta+1)}<\frac{2KM^{y}_{1}}{\mu-KM^{x}_{1}}=\delta.

Remark 11
.

By applying to

Lemma 13
.

Under assumption (H3) we have

1.

For every
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\overline{V}
and
\sigma\in BC^{0,1}(\overline{V},\mathbb{X})
, it holds for all

that

|\psi(t;\eta_{1},\sigma)-\psi(t;\eta_{2},\sigma)|\leq|\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}|e^{N_{1}(L_{\sigma}+1)|t|};

2.

For every
\eta\in\overline{V}
and
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in BC^{0,1}(\overline{V},\mathbb{X})
, it holds for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and any
\delta\geq L_{\sigma_{1}}
that

|\psi(t;\eta,\sigma_{2})-\psi(t;\eta,\sigma_{1})|\leq\frac{\|\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}\|_{\infty}}{\delta+1}\left(e^{N_{1}(\delta+1)|t|}-1\right).

Proof.

1.

Set
y_{i}(t)=\psi(t;\eta_{i},\sigma)
for
i=1,2
. Then integration of the IVP gives

y_{2}(t)-y_{1}(t)=\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}+\int_{0}^{t}g(\sigma(y_{2}(s)),y_{2}(s))-g(\sigma(y_{1}(s)),y_{1}(s))\,ds.

This implies

|y_{2}(t)-y_{1}(t)|\leq|\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}|+N_{1}(L_{\sigma}+1)\int_{0}^{t}|y_{2}(s)-y_{1}(s)|\,ds.

An application of Grönwall’s inequality gives

\displaystyle|y_{2}(t)-y_{1}(t)|\leq|\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}|e^{N_{1}(L_{\sigma}+1)|t|}.

2.

Set
y_{i}(t)=\psi(t;\eta,\sigma_{i})
for
i=1,2
.
Then integration of the IVP gives

y_{2}(t)-y_{1}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(\sigma_{2}(y_{2}(s)),y_{2}(s))-g(\sigma_{1}(y_{1}(s)),y_{1}(s))\,ds.

This implies, for
\delta\geq L_{\sigma_{1}}
:

\displaystyle|y_{2}(t)-y_{1}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\text{sign}(t)N_{1}\int_{0}^{t}|\sigma_{2}(y_{2}(s))-\sigma_{1}(y_{1}(s))|+|y_{2}(s)-y_{1}(s)|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\text{sign}(t)N_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\|\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}\|_{\infty}+(\delta+1)|y_{2}(s)-y_{1}(s)|\,ds

\displaystyle=N_{1}\|\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}\|_{\infty}|t|+\text{sign}(t)N_{1}(\delta+1)\int_{0}^{t}|y_{2}(s)-y_{1}(s)|\,ds.

An application of Grönwall’s inequality gives

\displaystyle|y_{2}(t)-y_{1}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq N_{1}\|\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}\|_{\infty}\left(|t|+N_{1}(\delta+1)\int_{0}^{t}se^{N_{1}(\delta+1)|t-s|}\,ds\right)

\displaystyle=\text{sign}(t)N_{1}\|\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}e^{N_{1}(\delta+1)|t-s|}\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{\|\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}\|_{\infty}}{\delta+1}\left(e^{N_{1}(\delta+1)|t|}-1\right).

\dot{x}(t)=F(x(t),\tilde{\psi}(t))

Lemma 14
.

Let
\tilde{\psi}\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R},\overline{V})
and suppose that for some
\mu>0
and
K\geq 1
the process
\{T_{0}(t,s,\tilde{\psi}):t\geq s\}

satisfies the property

|T_{0}(t,s;\tilde{\psi})\xi|\leq Ke^{-\mu(t-s)}|\xi|\quad\text{for }\xi\in\mathbb{X},t\geq s.

Then a function

satisfies the ODE

\dot{x}(t)=F(x(t),\tilde{\psi}(t)),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

(7)

if and only if for all

it holds that

\displaystyle x(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{t}T_{0}(t,s;\tilde{\psi})R_{0}(x(s),\tilde{\psi}(s))\,ds.

(8)

Proof.

Suppose first that equation
eq.

8
holds for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
for some
function

. Then for arbitrary
r\in\mathbb{R}

4

t\geq r

We derive now by Leibniz’ integral rule for
t>r
that
\dot{x}(t)
exists and is given by

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=A_{0}(\tilde{\psi}(t))T_{0}(t,r;\tilde{\psi})x(r)+\partial_{t}\int_{r}^{t}T_{0}(t,s;\tilde{\psi})R_{0}(x(s),\tilde{\psi}(s))\,ds

\displaystyle=A_{0}(\tilde{\psi}(t))\left(T_{0}(t,r;\tilde{\psi})x(r)+\int_{r}^{t}T_{0}(t,s;\tilde{\psi})R_{0}(x(s),\tilde{\psi}(s))\,ds\right)+R_{0}(x(t),\tilde{\psi}(t))

\displaystyle=A_{0}(\tilde{\psi}(t))x(t)+R_{0}(x(t),\tilde{\psi}(t))=F(x(t),\tilde{\psi}(t)).

As

Now suppose that

\displaystyle\dot{x}(s)

\displaystyle=A_{0}(\tilde{\psi}(s))x(s)+R_{0}(x(s),\tilde{\psi}(s)).

Pick
r\in\mathbb{R}
arbitrarily. By reordering terms and applying an integrating factor we derive using

lemma

4
, part 4, that, for all
t\geq r
,

x(t)=T_{0}(t,r;\tilde{\psi})x(r)+\int_{r}^{t}T_{0}(t,s;\tilde{\psi})R_{0}(x(s),\tilde{\psi}(s))\,ds.

As

is a bounded function and
|T_{0}(t,r;\tilde{\psi})x(t)|\leq Ke^{-\mu(t-r)}\|x\|_{\infty}
, it must hold as

x(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}T_{0}(t,s;\tilde{\psi})R_{0}(x(s),\tilde{\psi}(s))\,ds.

The integral condition from
lemma

14
can now be used to prove the
existence and uniqueness of a bounded solution to the ODE
eq.

.

Lemma 15
.

Assume (H2). Let
\tilde{\psi}\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R},\overline{V})
and suppose that for some
\mu>0
and
K\geq 1
the process
\{T_{0}(t,s,\tilde{\psi}):t\geq s\}
associated to
A(\tilde{\psi}(t))
satisfies the property

|T_{0}(t,s;\tilde{\psi})\xi|\leq Ke^{-\mu(t-s)}|\xi|\quad\text{for }\xi\in\mathbb{X},t\geq s.

Then there exists a unique function
\phi({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu};\tilde{\psi})\in BC^{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{X})
that solves the ODE

\dot{x}(t)=F(x(t),\tilde{\psi}(t)).

Proof.

Pick
\tilde{\psi}\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
arbitrarily. By
lemma

14
, a
function
\phi\in BC^{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{X})
solves the ODE if and only if it is a fixed
point of the map
\Phi
defined by

\displaystyle\Phi(\phi;\tilde{\psi})(t):=\int_{-\infty}^{t}T_{0}(t,s;\tilde{\psi})R_{0}(\phi(s),\tilde{\psi}(s))\,ds.

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore it is sufficient to show that there exists a unique function
\phi\in BC^{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{X})
such that
\Phi(\phi;\tilde{\psi})=\phi
.

Observe that by (H1) and (H2)

|\Phi(\phi;\tilde{\psi})(t)|\leq\frac{KM_{0}}{\mu}<\infty,

so this map is well-defined on
BC^{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{X})
, and for
\phi_{2},\phi_{1}\in BC^{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{X})
we have

\displaystyle|\Phi(\phi_{2};\tilde{\psi})(t)-\Phi(\phi_{1};\tilde{\psi})(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{KM_{1}^{x}}{\mu}\|\phi_{2}-\phi_{1}\|_{\infty}.

So the map
\Phi({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},\tilde{\psi})
is a contraction on
BC^{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{X})
for
0<M_{1}^{x}<\mu/K
, and therefore has a unique fixed point in
BC^{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{X})
.

For fixed
\eta\in\overline{V}
and
\sigma\in BC^{0,1}(\overline{V},\mathbb{X})
, we will from here on also denote the process
T_{0}(t,s;\psi(\cdot;\eta,\sigma))
as
T_{0}(t,s;\eta,\sigma)
for convenience. The following Lemma treats the existence and uniqueness of a bounded solution to the ODE
\dot{x}(t)=F(x(t),\psi(t;\eta,\sigma))
, and proves some properties of this solution that we use later on.

Lemma 16
.

Assume (H1)-(H3). For each
\eta\in\overline{V}
and
\sigma\in BC^{0,1}(\overline{V},\mathbb{X})
, there exists a unique function
\phi({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu};\eta,\sigma)\in BC^{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{X})
that solves the ODE

\dot{x}(t)=F(x(t),\psi(t;\eta,\sigma)).

This solution has the following properties;

1.

For each
\eta\in\overline{V}
and
\sigma\in BC^{0,1}(\overline{V},\mathbb{X})
, it holds for all

that

\displaystyle\phi(t;\eta,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{t}T_{0}(t,s;\eta,\sigma)R_{0}(\phi(s;\eta,\sigma),\psi(s;\eta,\sigma))\,ds;

If
\eta_{i}\in\overline{V}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
and
\sigma\in BC^{0,1}(\overline{V},\mathbb{X})

\delta\geq L_{\sigma}

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\left\{e^{-N_{1}(\delta+1)|t|}|\phi(t;\eta_{2},\sigma)-\phi(t;\eta_{1},\sigma)|\right\}

\eta\in\overline{V}
and

for

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\left\{e^{-N_{1}(\delta+1)|t|}|\phi(t;\eta,\sigma_{2})-\phi(t;\eta,\sigma_{1})|\right\}

Proof.

1.

To prove the first statement, apply

and
15
with

.

2.

For the second statement, let

\displaystyle+F(x_{2}(s),y_{2}(s))-F(x_{2}(s),y_{1}(s))\bigg)\,ds.

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-\mu(t-s)}\big(|R_{0}(x_{2}(s),y_{1}(s))-R_{0}(x_{1}(s),y_{1}(s))|

As above inequality holds for all

, by reordering terms we obtain

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-N_{1}(\delta+1)|t|}|x_{2}(t)-x_{1}(t)|\right\}\leq\frac{KM_{1}^{y}|\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}|}{\mu-KM_{1}^{x}-N_{1}(\delta+1)}.

3.

The proof of the third statement is similar to that of the second one, and therefore we will be more brief here. Let
\eta\in\overline{V}
and
\sigma_{i}\in BC^{0,1}(\overline{V},\mathbb{X})
for

\displaystyle=F(x_{2}(t),y_{2}(t))-F(x_{1}(t),y_{1}(t))

\displaystyle=A_{0}(y_{1}(t))u(t)+(R_{0}(x_{2}(t),y_{1}(t))-R_{0}(x_{1}(t),y_{1}(t)))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+(F(x_{2}(t),y_{2}(t))-F(x_{2}(t),y_{1}(t))).

We derive by (H2) the estimate

\displaystyle|u(t)|

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-\mu(t-s)}\left(M_{1}^{x}|u(s)|+M_{1}^{y}|y_{2}(s)-y_{1}(s)|\right)\,ds.

An application of

gives for

\displaystyle e^{-N_{1}(\delta+1)|t|}|u(t)|

As above inequality holds for all

, by reordering terms we obtain

Remark 17
.

The proof approach for items 2 and 3 here deviates from that described in Lemma 9.1.7 of Henry
[
31
]
. Translated to our setting, Henry begins there by applying the Mean Value Theorem in integral form to the right hand side of the differential equation

\partial_{t}(x_{2}(t)-x_{1}(t))=F(x_{2}(t),y_{2}(t))-F(x_{1}(t),y_{1}(t)).

This requires the differentiability of
F
such that
DF
is uniformly bounded in the operator norm, which is not necessary to assume in our approach.

Lemma

16
completes step 2 of the contraction mapping construction, and we are thus ready to move on to step 3. For this, we define for arbitrary
\delta>0
the ball

\mathcal{B}_{\delta}:=\left\{\sigma\in BC^{0,1}(\overline{V},\mathbb{X}):\|\sigma\|_{BC^{0,1}(\overline{V},\mathbb{X})}\leq\frac{KM_{0}}{\mu}+\delta\right\}.

(10)

It follows from
lemma

that
\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
is complete when equipped with the supremum norm. We now define an operator
\Lambda:\mathcal{B}_{\delta}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
via

\Lambda(\sigma)(\eta):=\phi(0;\eta,\sigma),

(11)

and proceed to show that
\Lambda
is a contraction on
(\mathcal{B}_{\delta},\|{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\|_{\infty})

Lemma 18
.

Assume (H1)-(H3). Suppose that
KM_{1}^{x}+N_{1}(\delta+1)<\mu
as well as
\frac{KM_{1}^{y}}{\mu-KM_{1}^{x}-N_{1}(\delta+1)}\leq\delta
. Then the operator
\Lambda
is well-defined.

Proof.

Pick
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
. We have for every
\eta\in\overline{V}
that

\displaystyle|\Lambda(\sigma)(\eta)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{0}|T_{0}(0,s;\eta,\sigma)R_{0}(\phi(s;\eta,\sigma),\psi(s;\eta,\sigma))|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq KM_{0}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{\mu s}\,ds=\frac{KM_{0}}{\mu}.

Now suppose that
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\overline{V}
. We see from

lemma

16
that

\displaystyle|\Lambda(\sigma)(\eta_{2})-\Lambda(\sigma)(\eta_{1})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{KM_{1}^{y}}{\mu-KM_{1}^{x}-N_{1}(\delta+1)}|\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}|.

Combining these estimates we obtain for each
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
that

\|\Lambda(\sigma)\|_{BC^{0,1}(\overline{V},\mathbb{X})}\leq\frac{KM_{0}}{\mu}+\frac{KM_{1}^{y}}{\mu-KM_{1}^{x}-N_{1}(\delta+1)}\leq\frac{KM_{0}}{\mu}+\delta.

Lemma 19
.

Suppose that the assumptions of
lemma

18
are satisfied. Then
the operator
\Lambda
is a contraction on
(\mathcal{B}_{\delta},\|{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\|_{\infty})
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
and
\eta\in\overline{V}
, and
note that as
\|\sigma_{1}\|_{BC^{0,1}(\overline{V},\mathbb{X})}\geq L_{\sigma_{1}}
, we
have
L_{\sigma_{1}}\leq KM_{0}/\mu+\delta
. Then we see from
lemma

16
that

\displaystyle|\Lambda(\sigma_{2})(\eta)-\Lambda(\sigma_{1})(\eta)|\leq\frac{KM_{1}^{y}}{(\delta+1)(\mu-KM_{1}^{x}-N_{1}(\delta+1))}\|\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}\|_{\infty}.

As
\frac{KM_{1}^{y}}{\mu-KM_{1}^{x}-N_{1}(\delta+1)}\leq\delta
by assumption, we have

\frac{KM_{1}^{y}}{(\delta+1)(\mu-KM_{1}^{x}-N_{1}(\delta+1))}\leq\frac{\delta}{\delta+1}<1,

and thus the assertion follows.

We can now deduce the existence of a unique fixed point
h\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
for
\Lambda
, as stated in the following Corollary:

Corollary 20
.

Assume (H1)-(H3). Suppose that
KM_{1}^{x}+N_{1}(\delta+1)<\mu
as well as
\frac{KM_{1}^{y}}{\mu-KM_{1}^{x}-N_{1}(\delta+1)}\leq\delta

\Lambda
has a unique fixed point
h\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
.

Proof.

This now follows from

lemma

19
by applying the Contraction Mapping Theorem.

It remains to prove that the fixed point from

Theorem 21
.

Assume
\sigma\in BC^{0,1}(\overline{V},\mathbb{X})
. Under assumptions (H1)-(H3), the following statements are equivalent:

1.

For all
\eta\in\overline{V}
it holds that

\sigma(\eta)=\int_{-\infty}^{0}T_{0}(0,s;\eta,\sigma)R_{0}(\sigma(\psi(s;\eta,\sigma)),\psi(s;\eta,\sigma))\,ds;

(12)

2.

For all
\eta\in\overline{V}
it holds that

\sigma(\eta)=\Lambda(\sigma)(\eta)=\phi(0;\eta,\sigma);

The function
\sigma
parameterizes a slow manifold of system
eq.

.

Proof.

\mathrm{(1\Rightarrow 2)}

Suppose first that equation
eq.

12

holds for arbitrary
\eta\in\overline{V}

\displaystyle\sigma(\psi(t;\eta,\sigma))

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{0}T_{0}(t,s+t;\eta,\sigma)R_{0}(\sigma(\psi(s+t;\eta,\sigma)),\psi(s+t;\eta,\sigma))\,ds

Recalling that the function

, and

and

\Lambda(\sigma)(\eta)=\phi(0;\eta,\sigma)=\sigma(\eta).\\

\mathrm{(2\Rightarrow 3)}

Secondly, assume that

.
Then we readily check for all

that

\sigma(\psi(t;\eta,\sigma))=\phi(0;\psi(t;\eta,\sigma),\sigma)=\phi(t;\eta,\sigma).

Therefore, for any

parameterizes a slow manifold as was defined in

\mathrm{(3\Rightarrow 1)}

Thirdly, suppose that for arbitrary

\sigma(\eta)=\int_{-\infty}^{0}T_{0}(0,s;\eta,\sigma)R_{0}(\sigma(\psi(s;\eta,\sigma)),\psi(s;\eta,\sigma))\,ds.

We are now able to prove the existence of a slow manifold for system

5
:

Proof of

9
.

By

4
Smoothness of slow manifolds

The purpose of this Section is to show that, under the additional assumptions

′
-(H3)
′
below on system

Theorem 22
(Smoothness of slow manifolds).

Suppose that the hypotheses of

9
hold and additionally

′
-(H3)
′

9

.

We will henceforth assume in this Section that the conditions for

theorem

9
are satisfied, and denote
y(t;\eta):=\psi(t;\eta,h)
and
x(t;\eta):=h(y(t;\eta))
. Now in order to set up a proof for

theorem

22

h
is indeed contained in

BC^{1}(\overline{V},\mathbb{X})

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}w(t;\eta)&=\partial_{\eta}F(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))\\
&=D_{x}F(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))w(t;\eta)+D_{y}F(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))\partial_{\eta}y(t;\eta);\\

(13)

Now let

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}\tilde{w}(t;\eta)&=D_{x}F(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))\,\tilde{w}(t;\eta)\\

\mathbb{X}

. We have the following exponential bound on this process:

Lemma 23
.

Let
T_{h}(t,s;\eta)
be the process associated to
eq.

14

\mathbb{X}
or

. For every
\eta\in\overline{V}
and

\|T_{h}(t,s;\eta)\|\leq Ke^{-(\mu-KM^{x}_{1})(t-s)},

where
\mu>0
and

Proof.

The proof runs indifferently for the case
\mathbb{X}
and
L(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{X})

estimates below are intended to be in the corresponding norms.
Pick
\eta\in\overline{V}
arbitrarily. Observe that

D_{x}F(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))=A_{0}(y(t;\eta))+D_{x}R_{0}(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta)).

Therefore if
\tilde{w}(t;\eta)
is a solution of
eq.

14
, with
t\geq s
for some pick of
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we have by the variation of constants formula that:

\tilde{w}(t;\eta)=T_{0}(t,s;\eta,h)\,\tilde{w}(s;\eta)+\int_{s}^{t}T_{0}(t,r;\eta,h)D_{x}R_{0}(x(r;\eta),y(r;\eta))\,\tilde{w}(r;\eta)\,dr,

which implies

e^{\mu t}\|\tilde{w}(t;\eta)\|\leq Ke^{\mu s}\|\tilde{w}(s;\eta)\|+KM^{x}_{1}\int_{s}^{t}e^{\mu r}\|\tilde{w}(r;\eta)\|\,dr.

An application of Grönwall’s inequality now gives

e^{\mu t}\|\tilde{w}(t;\eta)\|\leq Ke^{\mu s}\|\tilde{w}(s;\eta)\|e^{KM^{x}_{1}(t-s)},

which implies the Lemma.

Now returning to the initial value problem
eq.

13
, by the variation of constants formula we obtain for
t\geq s
that

w(t;\eta)=T_{h}(t,s;\eta)\,w(s;\eta)+\int_{s}^{t}T_{h}(t,r;\eta)D_{y}F(h(y(r;\eta)),y(r;\eta))\,\partial_{\eta}y(r;\eta)\,dr.

Observe that
w(t;\eta)=Dh(y(t;\eta))\partial_{\eta}y(t;\eta)
, and

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\partial_{\eta}y(t;\eta)

\displaystyle=Dg(h(y(t;\eta)),y(t;\eta))\begin{pmatrix}Dh(y(t;\eta))\\
I_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\end{pmatrix}\partial_{\eta}y(t;\eta);

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}y(0;\eta)

\displaystyle=I_{\mathbb{R}^{n}},

thus we see for
t\geq s

\|\partial_{\eta}y(s;\eta)\|\leq e^{N_{1}(\|Dh\|_{\infty}+1)(t-s)}\|\partial_{\eta}y(t;\eta)\|,

and thereby, by applying the result from
lemma

23
:

\|T_{h}(t,s;\eta)w(s;\eta)\|\leq Ke^{-(\mu-KM^{x}_{1}-N_{1}(\|Dh\|_{\infty}+1))(t-s)}\|Dh\|_{\infty}\|\partial_{\eta}y(t;\eta)\|.

Under the assumption
\mu-KM^{x}_{1}-N_{1}(\|Dh\|_{\infty}+1)>0
, boundedness of
Dh
implies then

w(t;\eta)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}T_{h}(t,s;\eta)D_{y}F(h(y(s;\eta)),y(s;\eta))\,\partial_{\eta}y(s;\eta)\,ds,

(15)

and moreover
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}e^{-N_{1}(\|Dh\|_{\infty}+1)|t|}\|w(t;\eta)\|<\infty
. As we have
w(0;\eta)=Dh(\eta)
in
eq.

13

t=0
in this last integral equation gives

Dh(\eta)=\int_{-\infty}^{0}T_{h}(0,s;\eta)D_{y}F(h(y(s;\eta)),y(s;\eta))\,\partial_{\eta}y(s;\eta)\,ds.

Lemma 26

1.

\displaystyle\|z(t;\eta,\tilde{w})\|

We set

\displaystyle v(0)

\displaystyle=0.

\displaystyle e^{-N_{1}(\rho+1)|t|}\|z_{2}(t)-z_{1}(t)\|

\displaystyle\quad=\int^{t}_{-\infty}T_{h}(t,s;\eta_{1})\,\Big(\left(D_{x}F(h(y(s;\eta_{2})),y(s;\eta_{2}))-D_{x}F(h(y(s;\eta_{1})),y(s;\eta_{1}))\right)\tilde{w}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-\left(D_{y}F(h(y(s;\eta_{2})),y(s;\eta_{2}))-D_{y}F(h(y(s;\eta_{1})),y(s;\eta_{1}))\right)\tilde{z}(t)\Big)\,ds.

Now we can use the assumed continuity of

to prove the continuous dependence on

in the

Lemma 32

Assume (H2)

-(H3)

for all

Proof.

Set

\displaystyle x(t;\eta)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}T_{h}(t,s;\eta)R_{h}(x(s;\eta),y(s;\eta))\,ds,

where

Making use of this integral equation for

\displaystyle p(t,\eta,\Delta)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{t}T_{h}(t,s;\eta+\Delta)R_{h}(x(s;\eta+\Delta),y(s;\eta+\Delta))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-T_{h}(t,s;\eta)R_{h}(x(s;\eta),y(s;\eta))

y(t;\eta+\Delta)-y(t;\eta)\end{pmatrix}.

\displaystyle p(t,\eta,\Delta)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left(T_{h}(t,s;\eta+\Delta)-T_{h}(t,s;\eta)\right)F(x(s;\eta+\Delta),y(s;\eta+\Delta))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\partial_{s}\left(T_{h}(t,s;\eta+\Delta)-T_{h}(t,s;\eta)\right)x(s;\eta+\Delta)

where integration by parts was used to obtain the last equality.

By uniform boundedness of

′

for all

and therefore we derive that for

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K\gamma_{1}(\rho+1)}{\mu-KM^{x}_{1}-N_{1}(\rho+1)}|\Delta|

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{KM^{y}_{1}}{\mu-KM^{x}_{1}-N_{1}(\rho+1)}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-N_{1}(\rho+1)|t|}|q(t,\eta,\Delta)|\right\}

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 33
.

Assume (H2)
′
-(H3)
′

. Then for every

for all

Proof.

We first set

\displaystyle\hat{g}(t,\eta,\Delta)

\displaystyle:=g(x(t;\eta+\Delta),y(t;\eta+\Delta))-g(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))

\displaystyle\qquad-Dg(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))\begin{pmatrix}x(t;\eta+\Delta)-x(t;\eta)\\
y(t;\eta+\Delta)-y(t;\eta)\end{pmatrix}.

Integration gives for

:

\displaystyle q(t,\eta,\Delta)

\displaystyle=\text{sign}(t)\int_{0}^{t}g(x(s;\eta+\Delta),y(s;\eta+\Delta))-g(x(s;\eta),y(s;\eta))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-Dg(x(s;\eta),y(s;\eta))\begin{pmatrix}h^{1}(y(s;\eta))\\

Then by adding and subtracting under the integral sign the term

.

.

5
k
-smooth slow manifolds

.
In this Section we show, under some suitable additional conditions on the vector
field, that the slow manifold parameterization
h
from
theorem

9
is actually
k
-times continuously differentiable (
k\geq 1
) with uniformly bounded derivatives up to order
k
. Our proof method of
choice is an induction argument that is set up by considering the
k
-th variational
equation of the system alongside orbits on the slow manifold. We remark that, similar
to what was mentioned in
remark

25
, this set-up
deviates from other texts that construct candidate derivatives for
h
by
differentiating (their version of) the mapping
\Lambda
from
section

3
.

We enunciate below the central result of this section, albeit we only provide here a
sketch of the proof, for conciseness.

Theorem 34
(
k
-smooth slow manifolds).

Assume that the conditions for
theorem

9
are satisfied, and let
h\in BC^{0,1}(\overline{V},\mathbb{X})
be the slow manifold parameterization as described in that Theorem. Suppose that
U
is an open set in
\mathbb{X}
that contains
h(\overline{V})
such that the following assumptions are satisfied for some
k\geq 1
:

1.

F\in C^{k}(U\times\overline{V},\mathbb{X})
,
DF\in BC^{k-1}(U\times\overline{V},L(\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{X}))
and the estimates from (H2)
′
hold;

2.

g\in C^{k}(U\times\overline{V},\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
Dg\in BC^{k-1}(U\times\overline{V},L(\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and there exists a constant
N_{1}>0
with
kN_{1}<\mu-KM^{x}_{1}
such that

\sup\left\{\|Dg(x,y)\|:(x,y)\in U\times\overline{V}\right\}\leq N_{1};

3.

There exists
\rho>0
such that

\frac{KM^{y}_{1}}{\mu-KM^{x}_{1}-kN_{1}(\rho+1)}<k(\rho+1)-1.

Then for the function
h\in BC^{0,1}(\overline{V},\mathbb{X})
it holds moreover that
h\in BC^{k}(\overline{V},\mathbb{X})
.

To prove this Theorem, we use an induction argument. The base case
k=1
was proven
in
section

4
. For the induction step, assume the Theorem holds
up until
k-1
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
. As the induction step can be
carried out in a manner that is mostly analogous to the material from

section

4
, we will be comparatively brief in our description of
the proofs involved.

To set up a strategy for carrying out the induction step, first suppose that indeed
also
h\in BC^{k}(\overline{V},\mathbb{X})
. We denote
y(t;\eta):=\psi(t;\eta,h)

(recall
lemma

13
) and
x(t;\eta):=h(y(t;\eta))
. Then
\partial^{k}_{\eta}x(t;\eta)
satisfies the
k
-th variational equation

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}\partial^{k}_{\eta}x(t;\eta)&=\partial^{k}_{\eta}F(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta));\\
&=DF(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))\,\partial^{k}_{\eta}\begin{pmatrix}x(t;\eta)\\
y(t;\eta)\end{pmatrix}+S^{x}_{k}(t;\eta),\\
\partial^{k}_{\eta}x(0;\eta)&=D^{k}h(\eta),\end{split}

(18)

where the term
S^{x}_{k}(t;\eta)
follows from Faà di Bruno’s formula for the generalized chain rule for derivatives and contains only
\eta
-derivates for
x
and
y
of up to order
k-1
. We observe that

\displaystyle S^{x}_{k}(t;\eta)

\displaystyle=\partial_{\eta}DF(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))\,\partial^{k-1}_{\eta}\begin{pmatrix}x(t;\eta)\\
y(t;\eta)\end{pmatrix}+\partial_{\eta}S^{x}_{k-1}(t;\eta)

(19)

\displaystyle=D^{2}F(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))\,\partial_{\eta}\begin{pmatrix}x(t;\eta)\\
y(t;\eta)\end{pmatrix}\,\partial^{k-1}_{\eta}\begin{pmatrix}x(t;\eta)\\
y(t;\eta)\end{pmatrix}+\partial_{\eta}S^{x}_{k-1}(t;\eta),

where
S^{x}_{1}(t;\eta)=0
.

Moreover

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\partial^{k}_{\eta}y(t;\eta)

\displaystyle=Dg(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))\,\partial^{k}_{\eta}\begin{pmatrix}x(t;\eta)\\
y(t;\eta)\end{pmatrix}+S^{y}_{k}(t;\eta);

\displaystyle\partial^{k}_{\eta}y(0;\eta)

\displaystyle=0,

where again the term
S^{y}_{k}(t;\eta)
follows from Faà di Bruno’s formula and contains only
\eta
-derivates for
x
and
y
of up to order
k-1
. We observe

\displaystyle S^{y}_{k}(t;\eta)

\displaystyle=\partial_{\eta}Dg(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))\,\partial^{k-1}_{\eta}\begin{pmatrix}x(t;\eta)\\
y(t;\eta)\end{pmatrix}+\partial_{\eta}S^{y}_{k-1}(t;\eta)

(20)

\displaystyle=D^{2}g(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))\,\partial_{\eta}\begin{pmatrix}x(t;\eta)\\
y(t;\eta)\end{pmatrix}\,\partial^{k-1}_{\eta}\begin{pmatrix}x(t;\eta)\\
y(t;\eta)\end{pmatrix}+\partial_{\eta}S^{y}_{k-1}(t;\eta).

Therefore we introduce candidates for the
k
-th
\eta
-derivatives of
(t,\eta)\mapsto x(t;\eta)
and
(t,\eta)\mapsto y(t;\eta)
via the non-autonomous linear ODE

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}w_{k}(t;\eta)\\
z_{k}(t;\eta)\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}DF(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))\\
Dg(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}w_{k}(t;\eta)\\
z_{k}(t;\eta)\end{pmatrix}+S_{k}(t;\eta),

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}D_{x}F(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))&D_{y}F(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))\\
D_{x}g(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))&D_{y}g(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}w_{k}(t;\eta)\\
z_{k}(t;\eta)\end{pmatrix}+S_{k}(t;\eta),

where

\displaystyle S_{k}(t;\eta):=\begin{pmatrix}S^{x}_{k}(t;\eta)\\
S^{y}_{k}(t;\eta)\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}D^{2}F(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))\\
D^{2}g(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}w_{1}(t;\eta)\\
z_{1}(t;\eta)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}w_{k-1}(t;\eta)\\
z_{k-1}(t;\eta)\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\partial_{\eta}\begin{pmatrix}S^{x}_{k-1}(t;\eta)\\
S^{y}_{k-1}(t;\eta)\end{pmatrix}.

A solution such that
z_{k}(0;\eta)=0
and
w_{k}({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu};\eta)
stays bounded has to satisfy the integral equations

\displaystyle w_{k}(t;\eta)

\displaystyle=\int^{t}_{-\infty}T_{h}(t,s;\eta)\left(D_{y}F(x(s;\eta),y(s;\eta))z_{k}(s;\eta)+S^{x}_{k}(s;\eta)\right)\,ds;

(21)

\displaystyle z_{k}(t;\eta)

\displaystyle=-\int^{0}_{t}\,Dg(x(s;\eta),y(s;\eta))\begin{pmatrix}w_{k}(s;\eta)\\
z_{k}(s;\eta)\end{pmatrix}+S^{y}_{k}(s;\eta)ds

\displaystyle=-\int^{0}_{t}\,T^{sl}_{h}(t,s;\eta)\left(D_{x}g(x(s;\eta),y(s;\eta))w_{k}(s;\eta)+S^{y}_{k}(s;\eta)\right)\,ds,

where
T_{h}(t,s;\eta)
is the same process as introduced in
lemma

23
and
T^{sl}_{h}(t,s;\eta)
is the reversible process associated to

\partial_{t}\tilde{z}_{k}(t;\eta)=D_{y}g(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))\tilde{z}_{k}(t;\eta).

Note that as
\|D_{y}g(x,y)\|
is bounded by
N_{1}
for
(x,y)\in U\times\overline{V}
, we have the estimate
\|T^{sl}_{h}(t,s;\eta)\|\leq e^{N_{1}|t-s|}
.

We can inductively show that
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}e^{-kN_{1}|t|}\|S_{k}(t;\eta)\|<\infty
, and therefore the existence of a bounded solution
w_{k}({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu};\eta)
with
z_{k}(0;\eta)=0
will be guaranteed by the condition

kN_{1}<\mu-KM^{x}_{1}.

The full proof can be done by setting up a contraction mapping argument that is
analogous to the one presented in
section

4
, whereby the
contraction mapping
\Gamma_{\eta}
itself follows the right-hand side of
eq.

21
, and has as domain the Banach space

BC^{0}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R},L^{k}(\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{X})),\quad\text{with }\gamma:=-kN_{1}(\rho+1).

If
\tilde{w}^{i}_{k}({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu};\eta)\in BC^{0}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R},L^{k}(\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{X}))
, for
i\in\{1,2\}
, then for the solutions
z^{i}_{k}({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu};\eta)
to the IVPs

\partial_{t}z^{i}_{k}(t;\eta)=Dg(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{w}^{i}_{k}(s;\eta)\\
z^{i}_{k}(s;\eta)\end{pmatrix}+S^{y}_{k}(t;\eta);\quad z(0;\eta)=0,

we have for any
\rho>0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
that

\|z_{k}^{2}(t;\eta)-z_{k}^{1}(t;\eta)\|\leq\frac{1}{k(\rho+1)-1}\|\tilde{w}_{k}^{2}({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu};\eta)-\tilde{w}_{k}^{1}({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu};\eta)\|_{-kN_{1}(\rho+1),\infty}e^{kN_{1}(\rho+1)|t|}.

Now if we subsequently set

w^{i}_{k}(t;\eta):=\int^{t}_{-\infty}T_{h}(t,s;\eta)\left(D_{y}F(x(s;\eta),y(s;\eta))z^{i}_{k}(s;\eta)+S^{x}_{k}(s;\eta)\right)\,ds,

we derive that

\displaystyle\|w_{k}^{2}({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu};\eta)-w_{k}^{1}({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu};\eta)\|_{-k(\rho+1)N_{1},\infty}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{1}{k(\rho+1)-1}\cdot\frac{KM^{y}_{1}}{\mu-KM^{x}_{1}-kN_{1}(\rho+1)}\|\tilde{w}_{k}^{2}({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu};\eta)-\tilde{w}_{k}^{1}({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu};\eta)\|_{-kN_{1}(\rho+1),\infty}.

Via arguments analogous to those in
section

4
, we therefore
have that
\Gamma_{\eta}
has a unique fixed point in
BC^{0}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R},L^{k}(\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{X}))
under the assumptions from
theorem

34
.
Moreover,
\Gamma_{\eta}
can also be shown to
depend continuously on
\eta
when considered as a map from
BC^{0}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R},L^{k}(\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{X}))
into
BC^{0}_{\tilde{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R},L^{k}(\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{X}))
, in case we have
\tilde{\gamma}:=-kN_{1}(\tilde{\rho}+1)
for some
\tilde{\rho}>\rho
.

To check that the fixed points
w_{k}({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu};\eta)
of the mappings
\Gamma_{\eta}

(viewed as a family of contraction mappings over
\eta\in\overline{V}
) indeed
provide the
k
-th
\eta
-derivatives for the slow manifold trajectories
x({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu};\eta)
we define, again analogously to arguments in
section

4
,
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
\eta\in\overline{V}
and
\Delta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the quantities

\displaystyle p_{k}(t,\eta,\Delta)

\displaystyle:=\partial^{k-1}_{\eta}x(t;\eta+\Delta)-\partial^{k-1}_{\eta}x(t;\eta)-w_{k}(t;\eta)\Delta,

\displaystyle q_{k}(t,\eta,\Delta)

\displaystyle:=\partial^{k-1}_{\eta}y(t;\eta+\Delta)-\partial^{k-1}_{\eta}y(t;\eta)-z_{k}(t;\eta)\Delta,

where now

Lemma 35
.

\displaystyle=o(|\Delta|),

\displaystyle\|q_{k}({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},\eta,\Delta)\|_{\gamma,\infty}=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}e^{-kN_{1}(\rho+1)|t|}\|q_{k}(t,\eta,\Delta)\|

\displaystyle=o(|\Delta|).

from

\displaystyle\hat{F}_{2}(t,\eta,\Delta)

\displaystyle:=DF(x(t;\eta+\Delta),y(t;\eta+\Delta))-DF(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-D^{2}F(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))\begin{pmatrix}w_{1}(t;\eta)\\
z_{1}(t;\eta)\end{pmatrix}\Delta;

\displaystyle\hat{g}_{2}(t,\eta,\Delta)

\displaystyle:=Dg(x(t;\eta+\Delta),y(t;\eta+\Delta))-Dg(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-D^{2}g(x(t;\eta),y(t;\eta))\begin{pmatrix}w_{1}(t;\eta)\\

\displaystyle\partial_{t}

7
Existence of reduction map

As by the previous Section, to prove existence of the reduction map, without loss of generality we now consider systems of the form

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=F(x(t),y(t)),

(23)

\displaystyle\dot{y}(t)

\displaystyle=g(x(t),y(t)),

where
x\in\mathbb{X}
,
y\in\overline{V}
with
V
an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}(n\geq 1)
,
F\in C^{0}(\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V},\mathbb{X})
,
g\in C^{0}(\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V},\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
F,g
are globally Lipschitz. Also we have
g(x,y)=0
whenever
y\in\partial V
.

Now we introduce the following assumptions (S1)-(S2):

Hypothesis 40
.

For the system
eq.

23
it holds that:

\mathrm{(S1)}

There exist
\mu>0
and
K\geq 1
such that every solution of system
eq.

23
, with
(x,y)(0)=(\xi,\eta)
for some
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V}
, has the property that

|x(t;(\xi,\eta))|\leq Ke^{-\mu(t-s)}|x(s;(\xi,\eta))|

(24)

for all
t\geq s\geq 0
;

\mathrm{(S2)}

There exists
N_{1}>0
, with
KN_{1}<\mu
, such that

|g(x_{2},y_{2})-g(x_{1},y_{1})|\leq N_{1}|(x_{2},y_{2})-(x_{1},y_{1})|

for all
(x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2})\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V}
.

Note that (S1) implies that we have
F(0,y)=0
for all
y\in\overline{V}
, and therefore
\{x=0\}
is invariant under the flow of system
eq.

23
.

The central result of this section is the following theorem:

Theorem 41
(Existence of Reduction Map).

Assume System
eq.

23
satisfies assumptions (S1)-(S2). Then there
exists a map
P\in C^{0}(\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V},\overline{V})
such that for each

(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V}
it holds that

\displaystyle P(x(t;(\xi,\eta)),y(t;(\xi,\eta)))=y(t;(0,P(\xi,\eta))),

\displaystyle\textit{for all $t\geq 0$,}

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}e^{\gamma t}|y(t;(\xi,\eta))-y(t;(0,P(\xi,\eta)))|=0,

\displaystyle\textit{for any $0\leq\gamma<\mu$,}

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\frac{|\eta-P(\xi,\eta)|}{|\xi|}\colon(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V},\xi\neq 0\right\}<\infty.

We shall also refer to a map
P\in C^{0}(\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V},\overline{V})

satisfying the first two properties from this Theorem as a reduction map for system

eq.

23
. The proof of
theorem

41
follows directly from two upcoming
results, namely
corollary

46
and
lemma

47
, which
we now address.

To set up a strategy for the proof of
theorem

41
, first suppose that the Theorem is true.
Because of the first property we need to have

P(x(t;(\xi,\eta)),y(t;(\xi,\eta)))=P(\xi,\eta)+\int_{0}^{t}g(0,P(x(s;(\xi,\eta)),y(s;(\xi,\eta))))\,ds.

Then from the second property we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}e^{\gamma t}\Big|\eta-P(\xi,\eta)+\int_{0}^{t}

\displaystyle g(x(s;(\xi,\eta)),y(s;(\xi,\eta)))

\displaystyle\quad-g(0,P(x(s;(\xi,\eta)),y(s;(\xi,\eta))))\,ds\Big|=0.

This can only possibly happen when

P(\xi,\eta)=\eta+\int_{0}^{\infty}g(x(s;(\xi,\eta)),y(s;(\xi,\eta)))-g(0,P(x(s;(\xi,\eta)),y(s;(\xi,\eta))))\,ds.

Set now
Q(\xi,\eta):=\eta-P(\xi,\eta)=(\pi_{y}-P)(\xi,\eta)
to obtain

Q(\xi,\eta)=\int_{0}^{\infty}g(0,(\pi_{y}-Q)(x(s;(\xi,\eta)),y(s;(\xi,\eta))))-g(x(s;(\xi,\eta)),y(s;(\xi,\eta)))\,ds.

To show existence of a map
Q
which satisfies this integral equation, we set up a contraction mapping argument again. For this we naturally also restrict now to maps satisfying the property
Q(0,\eta)=0
for all
\eta\in\overline{V}
.

Define

\mathcal{E}^{0}:=\left\{Q\in C^{0}(\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V},\overline{V}):Q(0,\cdot)=0;\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V};\\
\xi\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{|Q(\xi,\eta)|}{|\xi|}<\infty\right\}.

This is a Banach space with norm

\|Q\|_{\mathcal{E}}:=\sup\left\{\frac{|Q(\xi,\eta)|}{|\xi|}:(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V},\xi\neq 0\right\},

see also
appendix

C
. Observe that we have for all
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V}
that

Q(\xi,\eta)\leq\|Q\|_{\mathcal{E}}|\xi|.

Remark 42
.

The set-up for this function space was partly inspired by the proofs from Chapter 4 of Chicone
[
9
]
.

We now define a map
\Lambda
on (a subset of)
C^{0}(\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V})
as

\Lambda(Q)(\xi,\eta):=\int_{0}^{\infty}g(0,(\pi_{y}-Q)(x(s;(\xi,\eta)),y(s;(\xi,\eta))))-g(x(s;(\xi,\eta)),y(s;(\xi,\eta)))\,ds.

The following consequence of assumption (S1) prepares us for showing that
\Lambda
is well-defined and a contraction mapping on the Banach space
\mathcal{E}^{0}
.

Lemma 43
.

Under (S1), for every
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V}
and
t\geq 0
we have

\int_{t}^{\infty}|x(s;(\xi,\eta))|\,ds\leq\frac{K}{\mu}|x(t;(\xi,\eta))|

In particular it holds for every
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V}
that

\int_{0}^{\infty}|x(s;(\xi,\eta))|\,ds\leq\frac{K}{\mu}|\xi|.

Proof.

From the inequality
eq.

24
from assumption (S1) we derive immediately that for every
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V}
and
t\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}|x(s;(\xi,\eta))|\,ds\leq Kx(t;(\xi,\eta))\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\mu(s-t)}\,ds=\frac{K}{\mu}|x(t;(\xi,\eta))|.

Setting
t=0
gives the last statement.

We are now ready to firstly show that
\Lambda
is well-defined on
\mathcal{E}^{0}
, and next that it is also a contraction on
\mathcal{E}^{0}
under the assumptions (S1)-(S2).

Lemma 44
.

Under (S1), the mapping
\Lambda
is well-defined on
\mathcal{E}^{0}
, and moreover

\|\Lambda(Q)\|_{\mathcal{E}}\leq\frac{KN_{1}}{\mu}(1+\|Q\|_{\mathcal{E}}).

Proof.

Clearly for every
Q\in\mathcal{E}^{0}
we have
\Lambda(Q)(0,\cdot)=0
. Secondly,
we have for every
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V}
, by applying

43

\displaystyle|\Lambda(Q)(\xi,\eta)|

\displaystyle\leq N_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}|x(s;(\xi,\eta))|+|Q(x(s;(\xi,\eta)),y(s;(\xi,\eta)))|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq N_{1}(1+\|Q\|_{\mathcal{E}})\int_{0}^{\infty}|x(s;(\xi,\eta))|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{KN_{1}}{\mu}(1+\|Q\|_{\mathcal{E}})|\xi|.

And therefore
\|\Lambda(Q)\|_{\mathcal{E}}\leq\frac{KN_{1}}{\mu}(1+\|Q\|_{\mathcal{E}})<\infty
for every
Q\in\mathcal{E}^{0}
.

Thirdly, we still have to show that
\Lambda(Q)
is continuous when
Q\in\mathcal{E}^{0}
. For this, set for arbitrary
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V}
and
t\geq 0
:

q(t;(\xi,\eta)):=Q(x(t;(\xi,\eta)),y(t;(\xi,\eta)),

so that

\displaystyle\Lambda(q(t;{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}))(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=\Lambda(Q(x(t;{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}),y(t;{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu})))(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{\infty}g(0,(\pi_{y}-Q)(x(s;(\xi,\eta)),y(s;(\xi,\eta))))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-g(x(s;(\xi,\eta)),y(s;(\xi,\eta)))\,ds.

Now for each
(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V}
we have

\displaystyle|\Lambda(q(t;{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}))(\xi,\eta)-\Lambda(q(t;{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}))(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})|

(25)

\displaystyle\leq 2N_{1}\int_{t}^{\infty}|x(s;(\xi,\eta))-x(s;(\xi_{0},\eta_{0}))|+|y(s;(\xi,\eta))-y(s;(\xi_{0},\eta_{0}))|

\displaystyle+|q(s;(\xi,\eta))-q(s;(\xi_{0},\eta_{0}))|\,ds.

For
\gamma>N_{1}
we have

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}e^{-\gamma t}|y(t;(\xi,\eta))-y(t;(\xi_{0},\eta_{0}))|=0,

so that

\sup_{t\geq 0}e^{-\gamma t}|y(t;(\xi,\eta))-y(t;(\xi_{0},\eta_{0}))|\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as }(\xi,\eta)\rightarrow(\xi_{0},\eta_{0}).

Analogous statements hold when we replace
y
by
x,q
. Thereby we derive from the inequality
eq.

25
that

\sup_{t\geq 0}e^{-\gamma t}|\Lambda(q(t;{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}))(\xi,\eta)-\Lambda(q(t;{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}))(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})|\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as }(\xi,\eta)\rightarrow(\xi_{0},\eta_{0}).

Observe that
Q(\xi,\eta)=q(0;(\xi,\eta))
, and therefore we must have

|\Lambda(Q)(\xi,\eta)-\Lambda(Q)(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})|\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as }(\xi,\eta)\rightarrow(\xi_{0},\eta_{0}),

which means
\Lambda(Q)
is continuous.

Lemma 45
.

Under hypotheses (S1)–(S2) the mapping
\Lambda
is a contraction on
\mathcal{E}^{0}
.

Proof.

Let
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\mathcal{E}^{0}
, now we obtain by
lemma

43
for each

\displaystyle|\Lambda(Q_{2})(\xi,\eta)-\Lambda(Q_{1})(\xi,\eta)|

\displaystyle\leq N_{1}\|Q_{2}-Q_{1}\|_{\mathcal{E}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|x(s;(\xi,\eta))|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{KN_{1}|\xi|}{\mu}\|Q_{2}-Q_{1}\|_{\mathcal{E}}.

As by assumption (S2) we have

q^{2}(t;\xi,\eta):=Q^{2}(x(t;(\xi,\eta)),y(t;(\xi,\eta)))
set

p^{2}(t;(\xi,\eta)):=-q^{2}(t;(\xi,\eta)),\quad P^{2}(\xi,\eta):=p^{2}(0;(\xi,\eta)).

It remains to show that
P^{2}
is indeed the second derivative of
P
. From the proof
of
lemma

51
we may use that
DP
exists and is continuous,
and that
p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta)):=DP(x(t;(\xi,\eta)),y(t;(\xi,\eta)))
satisfies the ODE

\partial_{t}\,p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta))=D_{y}g(0,p(t;(\xi,\eta)))p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta)).

Now we define
t\geq 0
,
(\xi,\eta)\in U\times\overline{V}
and
\Delta\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\displaystyle a_{2}(t;(\xi,\eta),\Delta):

\displaystyle=p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta)+\Delta)-p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta))-p^{2}(t;(\xi,\eta))\,\Delta.

The quantity
a_{2}(t;(\eta,\xi),\Delta)
has to satisfy the ODE

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\,a_{2}(t;(\xi,\eta),\Delta)

\displaystyle=D_{y}g(0,p(t;(\xi,\eta)+\Delta))p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta)+\Delta)

(27)

\displaystyle-D_{y}g(0,p(t;(\xi,\eta)))p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta))

\displaystyle-D_{yy}g(0,p(t;(\xi,\eta)))p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta))\Delta\,p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta))

\displaystyle-D_{y}g(0,p(t;(\xi,\eta)))p^{2}(t;(\xi,\eta))\Delta.

By setting

\displaystyle\hat{g}_{2}(t;(\xi,\eta),\Delta):

\displaystyle=D_{y}g(0,p(t;(\xi,\eta)+\Delta))-D_{y}g(0,p(t;(\xi,\eta)))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-D_{yy}g(0,p(t;(\xi,\eta)))p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta))\Delta,

we can rewrite the ODE for
a(t;(\xi,\eta),\Delta)
to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\,a_{2}(t;(\xi,\eta),\Delta)=D_{y}g(0,p(t;(\xi,\eta)))a(t;(\eta,\xi),\Delta)+\hat{g}_{2}(t;(\eta,\xi),\Delta)p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta)+\Delta)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+D_{yy}g(0,p(t;(\xi,\eta)))\left(p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta)+\Delta)-p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta))\right)\Delta\,p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta)).

As
D_{yy}g(0,p(t;(\xi,\eta)))p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta))=\partial_{(\xi,\eta)}D_{y}g(0,p(t;(\xi,\eta)))
we have for
\gamma>2N_{1}
:

\sup_{t\geq 0}e^{-\gamma t}\|\hat{g}_{2}(t;(\xi,\eta),\Delta)p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta)+\Delta)\|=o(|\Delta|),

and as by assumption
\sup\{\|D^{2}g(x,y)\|:(x,y)\in U\times\overline{V}\}\leq N_{2}
we also have

\sup_{t\geq 0}e^{-\gamma t}\|D_{yy}g(0,p(t;(\xi,\eta)))\left(p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta)+\Delta)-p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta))\right)\Delta\,p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta))\|=o(|\Delta|).

The relevant solutions to the ODE
eq.

27
satisfy the integral equation

\displaystyle a_{2}(t;(\xi,\eta),\Delta)=\int_{t}^{\infty}Z(t,s;(\xi,\eta))\,\bigg(\hat{g}(s;(\xi,\eta),\Delta)p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta)+\Delta)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+D_{yy}g(0,p(t;(\xi,\eta)))\left(p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta)+\Delta)-p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta))\right)\Delta\,p^{1}(t;(\xi,\eta))\bigg)\,ds,

and therefore

\sup_{t\geq 0}e^{-\gamma t}\|a_{2}(t;(\xi,\eta),\Delta)\|=o(|\Delta|).

As
a_{2}(0;(\xi,\eta),\Delta)=DP((\xi,\eta)+\Delta)-DP(\xi,\eta)-P^{2}(\xi,\eta)\Delta
we in particular have

\left\|DP((\xi,\eta)+\Delta)-DP(\xi,\eta)-P^{2}(\xi,\eta)\Delta\right\|=o(|\Delta|),

and thereby
P^{2}=D^{2}P
, as was desired.

Application to local theory for critical manifolds of fast-slow systems of ODEs on Banach space

In this Section we discuss how the main theorems from the introduction,

theorems

1
and
2
, lead to a local theory for
compact attracting submanifolds of critical manifolds of fast-slow systems of ODEs
where the fast system lives on a Banach space. Our starting point is a fast-slow
system like system
eq.

1
, so of the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dot{x}(t)&=F(x(t),y(t),\varepsilon),\\
\dot{y}(t)&=\varepsilon\cdot g(x(t),y(t),\varepsilon),\end{split}

(28)

where
x\in\mathbb{X}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\varepsilon\geq 0
, and
F\in C^{k}(\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\mathbb{X})
,
g\in C^{k}(\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some
k\geq 2
. The local theory we derive in this Section can be compared to that from Fenichel’s 1979 paper
[
23
,
43
]
, with the generalization that we allow the fast variable
x
to possibly live on an infinite-dimensional space.

Let
C_{0}
denote the critical manifold of system
eq.

28
, so

C_{0}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:F(x,y,0)=0\}.

Suppose that
K_{0}
is a compact
\mu
-attracting submanifold of
C_{0}
. This means that for some compact
K^{y}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and some
h_{0}\in C^{k}(K^{y},\mathbb{X})
we have

K_{0}=\{(h_{0}(y),y)\in\mathbb{X}\times K^{y}\}\subset C_{0},

such that for some
\mu>0
it holds that

\sup\{\real\lambda:y\in K^{y},\lambda\in\sigma(D_{x}F(h_{0}(y),y,0))\}<-\mu.

This last condition is also called the spectral gap assumption.

By applying a coordinate transformation as well as suitable cut-off functions (see

[
55
]
for a detailed explanation on the construction of cut-offs in
Banach space setting) we can find a system that satisfies the assumptions of

theorems

1
and
2
and which is identical (after
coordinate transformation) to system
eq.

28
on some tubular
neighborhood of
K_{0}\times\{0\}
in
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.
Therefore
theorems

1
and
2
can be
applied locally to this tubular neighborhood. In order to translate the spectral gap
assumption to assumption (A1) from the introduction, one needs to apply

corollary

66
from
appendix

B
, see also
remark

6
.
This leads to a Theorem on persistence of
K_{0}
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
:

Theorem 54
(Local existence of smooth compact slow manifolds).

Suppose that
K_{0}
is a compact
\mu
-attracting submanifold of the critical manifold
C_{0}
of system
eq.

28
. Then there exist
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and a function
h\in C^{k}(K^{y}\times[0,\varepsilon_{0}],\mathbb{X})
such that:

1.

For all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
, the submanifold with boundary

K_{\varepsilon}:=\{(h(y,\varepsilon),y)\in\mathbb{X}\times K^{y}\}

is locally invariant under the flow of
eq.

28
;

2.

It holds that
h(\cdot,0)=h_{0}
and
\sup_{y\in K^{y}}\|h(y,\varepsilon)-h(y,0)\|_{\mathbb{X}}\rightarrow 0
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
.

We also denote
h(\cdot,\varepsilon)=:h_{\varepsilon}
, and we observe that the flow on the slow manifolds
K_{\varepsilon}
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dot{x}(t)&=\varepsilon\cdot D_{y}h_{\varepsilon}(y(t))\,g(h_{\varepsilon}(y(t)),y(t),\varepsilon),\\
\dot{y}(t)&=\varepsilon\cdot g(h_{\varepsilon}(y(t)),y(t),\varepsilon),\end{split}

which in the slow timescale
\tau
obtained via the transformation of time
\tau=\varepsilon t
gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dot{x}(\tau)&=D_{y}h_{\varepsilon}(y(\tau))\,g(h_{\varepsilon}(y(\tau)),y(\tau),\varepsilon),\\
\dot{y}(\tau)&=g(h_{\varepsilon}(y(\tau)),y(\tau),\varepsilon).\end{split}

From this last system we see that the flow on
K_{\varepsilon}
is a perturbation of the flow on
K_{0}
that is defined by the differential-algebraic equation

\displaystyle x(\tau)

\displaystyle=h_{0}(y(\tau)),

(29)

\displaystyle\dot{y}(\tau)

\displaystyle=g(x(\tau),y(\tau),0),

which is often referred to as the reduced system in theory for fast-slow systems. This corresponds to results by Fenichel about the existence of slow manifolds and the flow on them.

The second result concerns the existence of stable invariant foliations near

K_{\varepsilon}
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, which tells us that orbits that start
near
S_{\varepsilon}
are locally exponentially attracted to some orbit that lies on

K_{\varepsilon}
. We retrieve this result from
theorem

2
by applying
the Implicit and Inverse Function Theorems to the reduction map described there.

Theorem 55
(Local existence of invariant foliations near compact slow manifolds).

Suppose that
K_{0}
is a compact
\mu
-attracting submanifold of the critical manifold

C_{0}
of system
eq.

28
. Let
\varphi:(t;\hat{x}_{0},\varepsilon)\mapsto\varphi(t;\hat{x}_{0},\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote the flow of system

eq.

28
, and let
h_{\varepsilon}
be as in
theorem

1
.
Then there exist
0<\varepsilon_{1}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, an open set
U\subset\mathbb{X}

such that
0\in U
, and a map
\Phi\in C^{k}(U\times K^{y}\times[0,\varepsilon_{1}],\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

1.

(Invariance of leaves) For each
(x_{0},y_{0},\varepsilon)\in U\times K^{y}\times(0,\varepsilon_{1}]
we have

(x,y)(t;\Phi(x_{0},y_{0},\varepsilon),\varepsilon)\in\Phi(U,y(t;(h_{\varepsilon}(y_{0}),y_{0}),\varepsilon),\varepsilon)

for all
t\geq 0
such that
(x,y)(t;(h_{\varepsilon}(y_{0}),y_{0}),\varepsilon)\in K_{\varepsilon}
;

2.

(Contractivity of leaves) There exists
C\geq 1
such that for every
(\hat{x}_{0},\varepsilon)\in(U\times K^{y})\times(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
we have

\displaystyle\|(x,y)(t;\Phi(x_{0},y_{0},\varepsilon),\varepsilon)-(x,y)(t;(h_{\varepsilon}(y_{0}),y_{0}),\varepsilon)\|_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq Ce^{-\mu t/2}\|\Phi(x_{0},y_{0},\varepsilon)-(h_{\varepsilon}(y_{0}),y_{0})\|_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}

for all
t\geq 0
such that
(x,y)(t;(h_{\varepsilon}(y_{0}),y_{0}),\varepsilon)\in K_{\varepsilon}
.

A
C^{k}
reduction map
P_{\varepsilon}
like in
theorem

2
can for each
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{1}]
be defined on
\Phi(U\times K^{y}\times\{\varepsilon\})
. The connection between
P_{\varepsilon}
and
\Phi
is given by

\Phi(U\times\{y_{0}\}\times\{\varepsilon\})=P^{-1}_{\varepsilon}(h_{\varepsilon}(y_{0}),y_{0})

for each
(h_{\varepsilon}(y_{0}),y_{0})\in K_{\varepsilon}
.

Now set
Q_{\varepsilon}(\xi,\eta):=(\xi,\eta)-P_{\varepsilon}(\xi,\eta)
for
(\xi,\eta)\in\Phi(U\times K^{y}\times\{\varepsilon\})
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle(x,y)(t;(\xi,\eta),\varepsilon)=(x,y)(t;P_{\varepsilon}(\xi,\eta),\varepsilon)+Q_{\varepsilon}((x,y)(t;(\xi,\eta),\varepsilon)),

as long as
(x,y)(t;(h_{\varepsilon}(y_{0}),y_{0}),\varepsilon)\in K_{\varepsilon}
. This can be interpreted in the terminology of matched asymptotic expansions (see for example
[
57
]
). Namely, the terms
(x,y)(t;(h_{\varepsilon}(y_{0}),y_{0}),\varepsilon)
for
y_{0}\in K^{y}
are outer solutions, whilst picking
(h_{\varepsilon}(y_{0}),y_{0})=P_{\varepsilon}(\xi,\eta)
and adding the correction
Q_{\varepsilon}((x,y)(t;(\xi,\eta),\varepsilon))
accounts for matched interior layer solutions for the initial value problem defined by
(x,y)(0)=(\xi,\eta)
. It moreover holds that

\|Q_{\varepsilon}((x,y)(t;(\xi,\eta),\varepsilon))\|_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leq Ce^{-\mu t/2}\|(\xi,\eta)-P_{\varepsilon}(\xi,\eta)\|_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}},

so the interior layer-related correction is exponentially decaying. This way,

theorems

54
and
55
provide a justification for the
method of matched asymptotic expansions in the context of spatially extended systems
with slowly evolving parameters. Further details on this are similar to the
finite-dimensional setting and can be found for example in
[
50
,
17
,
57
]
.

10
Finite-dimensional center-unstable manifolds

Whilst the Theorems from the Introduction concern attracting critical manifolds, the Theorems and proofs in this paper can be generalized to apply to normally hyperbolic critical manifolds with finite-dimensional center-unstable manifolds. Finite-dimensionality of unstable directions occurs in many physical applications, so this is often a relevant restriction. In these generalizations, the slow manifolds from the Theorems from the Introduction are replaced by center-unstable manifolds. Note that as these center-unstable manifolds are finite-dimensional, the dynamics on the center-unstable manifolds can be further characterized by applying the theories of Fenichel. See also Theorem 3.1.4 in
[
43
]
for the finite-dimensional analog of center-unstable manifold existence. In specialized literature on invariant manifold theorems for differential equations, the extension of proofs for Center Manifold Theorems to existence of center-unstable manifolds is a common feature, see for example
[
41
,
32
,
52
,
33
]
.

The details for the extension of the material of
section

3
to cover existence of center-unstable manifolds for differential equations on Banach space with slowly evolving parameters are left for a later text. One needs a generalization of the set-up at the start of
section

3
which accounts for the presence of unstable directions, and this generalized system will involve additional hypotheses. However, results from
lemma

13
onwards as well as the construction of a contraction mapping, whose fixed point is a center-unstable manifold, will be largely similar, albeit with some minor modifications.

In applications (we use the set-up of
section

9
for
eq.

28
), normal hyperbolicity for compact critical manifolds
K_{0}
can be characterized as follows. We define stable and unstable parts of the spectrum of the linearized fast subsystem along
K_{0}
as follows:

\displaystyle\Sigma^{s}(y)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\lambda\in\sigma(D_{x}F(h_{0}(y),y)):\real\lambda<0\right\},

\displaystyle\Sigma^{u}(y)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\lambda\in\sigma(D_{x}F(h_{0}(y),y)):\real\lambda>0\right\},

where
y\in K^{y}
. Now we call
K_{0}
normally hyperbolic (with spectral gap constants
\mu,\nu
) if there exist
\mu,\nu>0
such that

\sup\left\{\real\lambda:y\in K^{y},\lambda\in\Sigma^{s}(y)\right\}<-\mu<0<\nu<\inf\left\{\real\lambda:y\in K^{y},\lambda\in\Sigma^{u}(y)\right\}.

Observe that under the assumption that
\sigma^{u}(y)
is finite-dimensional, the infimum on the right-hand side can be replaced by a minimum.

For each
(h_{0}(y),y)\in K_{0}
the unstable eigenspace is defined via the projection

\Pi^{u}(y):=\int_{\Gamma_{y}}\left(\lambda I_{\mathbb{X}}-D_{x}F(h_{0}(y),y)\right)\,d\lambda,

where
\Gamma_{y}
is a simple, counterclockwise oriented Jordan curve that surrounds
\Sigma^{u}(y)
in the complex plane and that lies entirely to the right of the line
\real\lambda=0
. Now the set

E^{cu}:=\{((h_{0}(y),y),x)\in K_{0}\times\mathbb{X}:x\in\range{\Pi^{u}(y)}\}

is a finite-dimensional vector bundle with base space
K_{0}
(see for example Chapter 2 in
[
18
]
).

Now a claim analogous to
theorem

54
, and of which the proof should go mostly along lines similar to
sections

3
to
5
, is that there exists locally (near
K_{0}
) a center-unstable manifold
W^{cu}_{\text{loc},0}
which is tangent to
E^{cu}
along its base space
K_{0}
. Moreover, the manifold
W^{cu}_{\text{loc},0}
is perturbed regularly for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
. This means that there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that

1.

There exists a
C^{k}
function
h^{cu}:E^{cu}_{\text{loc}}\times[0,\varepsilon_{0}]\rightarrow\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which paramaterizes an invariant manifold

W_{\text{loc},\varepsilon}^{cu}:=\left\{h^{cu}(\hat{x},\varepsilon):\hat{x}\in E^{cu}_{\text{loc}}\right\}.

The center-unstable manifold
W_{\text{loc},\varepsilon}^{cu}
is locally invariant under the flow of
eq.

28
, and
W_{\text{loc},0}^{cu}
is tangent to
E^{cu}
along its base space
K_{0}
;

2.

It holds that

\sup_{\hat{x}\in E^{cu}_{\text{loc}}}\|h^{cu}(\hat{x},\varepsilon)-h^{cu}(\hat{x},0)\|_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\downarrow 0.

Furthermore, a local
C^{0}
reduction map
P_{\varepsilon}
on
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
W_{\text{loc},\varepsilon}^{cu}
as stem exists (for sufficiently small
\varepsilon\geq 0
) analogously to
theorem

55
(but without further smoothness claim). All points which are mapped under
P_{\varepsilon}
to the slow manifold within
W_{\text{loc},\varepsilon}^{cu}
form a
C^{k}
foliated center-stable manifold; proofs for this are mostly analogous to the material from
sections

7
to
8
. This way the classical results from
[
23
]
can be reproduced in the setting that the fast system lives on a Banach space via the functional analytic proof methods from this paper. Future research should flesh out the remaining proof details.

Appendix B

This Appendix treats theory for parameter-dependent uniformly continuous semigroups, and its relationship to results for the two-parameter semigroups that are generated when said parameters may also evolve slowly in time. The main result of this Appendix is
corollary

66
, which gives conditions under which bounded linear operators depending on slowly evolving parameters generate two-parameter semigroups that possess exponential bounds. This is relevant to the application of the main results of this paper, as it allows one to validate assumption (A1) from the Introduction, see also
remark

6
and
section

9
.

Definition 59
.

A semigroup
T:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X})
is a family of bounded linear operators on a Banach space
\mathbb{X}
such that

(i)

T(0)=I_{\mathbb{X}}
;

(ii)

T(t+s)=T(t)T(s)
for all
t,s\geq 0
.

Such a semigroup is moreover called uniformly continuous if

(iii)

The mapping

\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\ni t\mapsto T(t)\in L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X})

is continuous with respect to the operator norm on
L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X})
.

For an operator
A\in L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X})
, the semigroup generated by
A
consists of the family of bounded linear operators on
\mathbb{X}
given by

e^{At}:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{A^{n}t^{n}}{n!},

with
t\geq 0
. Such a semigroup is uniformly continuous and is the fundamental solution (in forward time) to the linear ODE

\dot{x}(t)=A\,x(t),\quad x\in\mathbb{X}.

(30)

We denote by
\sigma(A)
the
spectrum
of
A
, and then
\rho(A):=\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma(A)
is the
resolvent set
of
A
. For each
\lambda\in\rho(A)
, we denote by
R(\lambda,A)
the corresponding
resolvent
which is defined as
R(\lambda,A):=(\lambda I_{\mathbb{X}}-A)^{-1}
.

By the following Lemma, the uniformly continuous semigroups on
\mathbb{X}
are precisely those semigroups that are generated by bounded linear operators on
\mathbb{X}
;

Lemma 60
.

A semigroup
T:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X})
is uniformly continuous if and only if there exists
A\in L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X})
such that
T(t)=e^{At}
for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

See for example Chapter I, Theorem 3.7 in
[
19
]
.

For
A\in L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X})
, if
\mu>0
is chosen such that
\sup\{\real\lambda:\lambda\in\sigma(A)\}<-\mu
, it is well-known that there exists
K\geq 1
such that

|e^{At}\xi|\leq Ke^{-\mu t}|\xi|\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb{X},\,t\geq 0.

This means that the condition
\sup\{\real\lambda:\lambda\in\sigma(A)\}<-\mu
ensures exponential stability (at rate of exponential decay
\mu
) of the origin for the linear ODE
eq.

30
.

Now suppose
A\in C^{0}(\overline{V},L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X}))
is dependent on some parameter
\nu\in\overline{V}
. Then for every
\nu\in\overline{V}
there exists
K_{\nu}\geq 1
, such that

|e^{A(\nu)t}\xi|\leq K_{\nu}e^{-\mu t}\xi\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb{X},\,t\geq 0.

In general it is not guaranteed that
\sup_{\nu\in\overline{V}}K_{\nu}
is finite, however this can be desirable for certain applications, as for example in this article or see
[
44
]
.

The following Lemma provides conditions that guarantee the existence of uniform exponential bounds on families of semigroups generated by parameter-dependent bounded linear operators on
\mathbb{X}
.

Lemma 61
.

Let
A\in C^{0}(\overline{V},L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X}))
and assume there exist
\mu>0,\tilde{K}\geq 1
and
\theta\in(\pi/2,\pi)
such that for all
\nu\in\overline{V}

\Omega_{-\mu,\theta}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\lambda\neq-\mu,|\arg(\lambda+\mu)|<\theta\}\subseteq\rho(A(\nu)),

and

\|R(\lambda,A(\nu))\|\leq\frac{\tilde{K}}{|\lambda+\mu|}\quad\text{for all }\lambda\in\Omega_{-\mu,\theta}.

Then there exists a constant
K\geq 1
, such that for all
\nu\in\overline{V}
the semigroup generated by
A(\nu)\in L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X})
satisfies the bounds

|e^{A(\nu)t}\xi|\leq Ke^{-\mu t}|\xi|,\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb{X},\,t\geq 0.

Proof.

The proof is based on checking that a well-known proof for exponential bounds on analytic semigroups provides uniform bounds if the generators
A(\nu)\in L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X})
are uniformly sectorial for all
\nu\in\overline{V}
, see also Lemma 2.3 in recent work by Latushkin
[
44
]
.

Firstly, note that for each
\nu\in\overline{V}
and
t\geq 0
, the semigroup
e^{A(\nu)t}
satisfies the formula

e^{A(\nu)t}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}R(\lambda,A(\nu))\,e^{\lambda t}\,d\lambda,

where
\Gamma
is any piecewise smooth curve in
\Omega_{-\mu,\theta}
that has a parameterization
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\Omega_{-\mu,\theta}
such that
\arg(\gamma(s)+\mu)\rightarrow\pm\varphi
as
s\rightarrow\pm\infty
for some
\varphi\in(\pi/2,\theta)
.

Now for some
r>0
and
\varphi\in(\pi/2,\theta)
, we consider the curve
\Gamma=\Gamma^{-}_{r,\varphi}\cup\Gamma^{c}_{r,\varphi}\cup\Gamma^{+}_{r,\varphi}
,
where

\Gamma^{\pm}_{r,\varphi}:=\{\lambda\in\Omega_{-\mu,\theta}:\arg(\lambda+\mu)=\pm\varphi,|\lambda+\mu|\geq r\}=\{-\mu+se^{\pm i\varphi}:s\geq r\},

and

\Gamma^{c}_{r,\varphi}:=\{\lambda\in\Omega_{-\mu,\theta}:|\arg(\lambda+\mu)|\leq\varphi,|\lambda+\mu|=r\}=\{-\mu+re^{i\phi}:-\varphi\leq\phi\leq\varphi\}.

We then obtain, for
t>0
, by changing variables according to parameterizations for the pieces
\Gamma^{\pm}_{r,\varphi}
:

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\Gamma^{\pm}_{r,\varphi}}R(\lambda,A(\nu))\,e^{\lambda t}\,d\lambda\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{r}^{\infty}R(-\mu+se^{\pm i\varphi},A(\nu))\,e^{-\mu t+ste^{\pm i\varphi}}\,e^{\pm i\varphi}\,ds\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{K}e^{-\mu t}\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{e^{st\cos\varphi}}{s}\,ds\leq\frac{\tilde{K}e^{rt\cos\varphi}}{-rt\cos\varphi}e^{-\mu t}.

Setting
r=1/t
we get

\left\|\int_{\Gamma^{\pm}_{r,\varphi}}R(\lambda,A(\nu))\,e^{\lambda t}\,d\lambda\right\|\leq\frac{\tilde{K}e^{\cos\varphi}}{-\cos\varphi}e^{-\mu t}.

Similarly, for
t>0
, by changing variables according to a parameterization for
\Gamma^{c}_{r,\varphi}
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\Gamma^{c}_{r,\varphi}}R(\lambda,A(\nu))\,e^{\lambda t}\,d\lambda\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{-\varphi}^{\varphi}R(-\mu+re^{i\phi},A(\nu))\,e^{-\mu t+rte^{i\phi}}\,re^{i\phi}\,d\phi\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{K}e^{-\mu t}\int_{-\varphi}^{\varphi}e^{rt\cos\phi}\,d\phi\leq\tilde{K}2e^{rt}\varphi e^{-\mu t},

and thereby for
r=1/t
:

\left\|\int_{\Gamma^{c}_{r,\varphi}}R(\lambda,A(\nu))\,e^{\lambda t}\,d\lambda\right\|\leq\tilde{K}2e\varphi e^{-\mu t}.

By combining above estimates we now see that for each
t>0
:

\displaystyle\left\|e^{A(\nu)t}\right\|=\left\|\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}R(\lambda,A(\nu))\,e^{\lambda t}\,d\lambda\right\|\leq\frac{\tilde{K}}{\pi}\left(e\varphi-e^{\cos\varphi}\sec\varphi\right)e^{-\mu t}.

As all these estimates are independent of the choice of
\nu
, we see that the Lemma is proven with

K:=\frac{\tilde{K}}{\pi}\left(e\varphi-e^{\cos\varphi}\sec\varphi\right)

for arbitrary choice of
\varphi\in(\pi/2,\theta)
.

Definition 62
.

For
A\in C^{0}(\overline{V},L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X}))
, if the family of operators given by

\{A(\nu):\nu\in\overline{V}\}
satisfies the conditions of the above
lemma

61
, we shall also call such a family uniformly sectorial, or
(\mu,\theta,\tilde{K})
-sectorial uniformly with respect to
\nu\in\overline{V}
.

Remark 63
.

As an example of what can go wrong if the set of operators
A(\nu)\in L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X})
from the above Lemma is not uniformly sectorial, consider the planar ODE system

\displaystyle\dot{x}=A(\nu)x:=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-1\\
\nu^{2}&-1\end{pmatrix}x,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

(31)

with
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
. The eigenvalues of
A(\nu)
are
\lambda_{\pm}=-1\pm\nu i
, and its general solution is given by

x(t)=e^{-t}\left(C_{1}\begin{pmatrix}\cos\nu t\\
\nu\sin\nu t\end{pmatrix}+C_{2}\begin{pmatrix}-\sin\nu t\\
\nu\cos\nu t\end{pmatrix}\right).

Pick
C_{1}=1,\,C_{2}=0
to obtain the solution for initial condition
x_{0}:=x(0)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\end{pmatrix}^{T}
, which for odd
\nu=k\in\mathbb{Z}
leads to

x(\pi/2;x_{0})=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\pm ke^{-\pi/2}\end{pmatrix}.

So we see that, for a diverging sequence of odd
k
, as
|k|\rightarrow\pm\infty
we also have that
|x(\pi/2;x_{0})|\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore the solutions do not have an exponential bound that holds for all
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 64
.

As a second example of what can go wrong if the set of operators
A(\nu)\in L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X})
from the above Lemma is not uniformly sectorial, consider the planar ODE system

\displaystyle\dot{x}=A(\nu)x:=\begin{pmatrix}-1&\nu\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}x,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

(32)

with
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
. The matrix
A(\nu)
has a single eigenvalue
\lambda=-1
with geometric multiplicity one, and its general solution is given by

x(t)=e^{-t}\begin{pmatrix}1&\nu t\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}x_{0},\quad x_{0}=x(0)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Let now
x_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\end{pmatrix}^{T}
, so that
x(1;x(0))=e^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\nu&1\end{pmatrix}^{T}
. We see that
|x(1;x_{0})|\rightarrow\infty
as
|\nu|\rightarrow\infty
, so there cannot exist an exponential bound that is uniform for all
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
.

Observe that

A(\nu)^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-\nu\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},

so that for each
a>-1
we have

\|(0-A(\nu))^{-1}\|\geq\frac{|a\nu|}{|0-a|},

which shows that
A(\nu)
cannot be uniformly sectorial with respect to
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
.

Next we turn our attention to settings in which the parameter
\nu\in\overline{V}

evolves in time. As discussed in
section

3
, if
A\in C^{0}(\overline{V},L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X}))
and
\tilde{\psi}\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R},\overline{V})
,
then we can associate a two-parameter semigroup or process
\{T(t,s;\tilde{\psi}):t\geq s\}
to the time-dependent family of operators

\{A(\tilde{\psi}(t)):t\in\mathbb{R}\}
, which describes the solutions to the
non-autonomous linear ODE

\dot{x}(t)=A(\tilde{\psi}(t))\,x(t).

(33)

In general, even if the family
\{A(\nu):\nu\in\overline{V}\}
is uniformly sectorial, exponential stability of the origin of this ODE is not guaranteed. The following Lemma leads to a Corollary which intuitively states that if
\tilde{\psi}
is changing sufficiently slowly, and
\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V}\ni(x,\nu)\mapsto A(\nu)x
is sufficiently close to a function that is uniformly Lipschitz in
\nu
, then the process
\{T(t,s,\tilde{\psi}):t\geq s\}
has an exponential bound which implies exponential stability of the origin of the ODE
eq.

33
in case the family
\{A(\nu):\nu\in\overline{V}\}
is uniformly sectorial.

Lemma 65
.

For
A\in C^{0}(\overline{V},L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X}))
, consider the family of uniformly continuous semigroups
\{e^{A(\nu)t}:\nu\in\overline{V}\}
, and suppose there exist
\mu>0
and
K\geq 1
such that for every
\nu\in\overline{V}

|e^{A(\nu)t}\xi|\leq Ke^{-\mu t}|\xi|,\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb{X},\,t\geq 0.

For arbitrary
0<\varepsilon<\mu
, choose
l>0
such that
Ke^{-\mu l}\leq e^{-(\mu-\varepsilon)l}
. If for
\tilde{\psi}\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R},\overline{V})
it holds that

\left\|A(\tilde{\psi}(t))-A(\tilde{\psi}(s))\right\|\leq\varepsilon/K\quad\text{as long as }|t-s|\leq l,

then for the process
\{T(t,s;\tilde{\psi}):t\geq s\}
associated to
A(\tilde{\psi}(t))
we have

|T(t,s;\tilde{\psi})\xi|\leq Ke^{-(\mu-\varepsilon)(t-s)}|\xi|,\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb{X},t\geq s.

Proof.

Let
A_{s}:=A(\tilde{\psi}(s))
, then by the variation of constants formula a solution of

\dot{x}(t)=A(\tilde{\psi(t)})x(t)=A_{s}x(t)+(A(\tilde{\psi(t)})-A_{s})x(t)

with
x(s)=\xi
, satisfies the integral equation

\displaystyle x(t)

\displaystyle=e^{A_{s}(t-s)}\xi+e^{A_{s}t}\int_{s}^{t}e^{-A_{s}r}(A(\tilde{\psi}(r))-A_{s})x(r)\,dr.

Therefore, for
t\geq s
:

\displaystyle|x(t)|

\displaystyle\leq Ke^{-\mu(t-s)}|\xi|+Ke^{-\mu t}\int_{s}^{t}e^{-\mu r}\|A(\tilde{\psi}(r))-A_{s}\||x(r)|\,dr.

Then by Grönwall’s inequality we obtain for
t\in[s,s+l]
:

\displaystyle|x(t)|

\displaystyle\leq Ke^{-\mu(t-s)}|\xi|e^{K\int_{s}^{t}\|A(\tilde{\psi}(r))-A_{s}\|\,dr}

\displaystyle\leq Ke^{-(\mu-\varepsilon)(t-s)}|\xi|.

Now for
t\in[s,\infty)
, we can write
t=s+nl+\tau
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}
and
\tau\in[0,l)
. And then

\displaystyle|T(t,s;\tilde{\psi})\xi|

\displaystyle=\left|T(t,s+nl;\tilde{\psi})\prod_{i=1}^{n}T(s+il,s+(i-1)l;\tilde{\psi})\xi\right|

\displaystyle\leq Ke^{-(\mu-\varepsilon)(t-(s+nl))}e^{-(\mu-\varepsilon)nl}|\xi|=Ke^{-(\mu-\varepsilon)(t-s)}|\xi|.

Corollary 66
.

For
A\in C^{0}(\overline{V},L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X})
, let
A(\nu)
with
\nu\in\overline{V}
be a family of bounded linear operators on
\mathbb{X}
that is
(\tilde{\mu},\theta,\tilde{K})
-sectorial uniformly with respect to
\nu\in\overline{V}
, for some triplet of constants
\tilde{\mu}>0,\,\theta\in(\pi/2,\pi)
and
\tilde{K}\geq 1
.

Suppose that for all
\nu\in\overline{V},\,x\in\mathbb{X}
it holds that
A(\nu)x=F(x,\nu)-R(x,\nu)
, with
F,R\in C^{0}(\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V},\mathbb{X})
such that for some
M_{1}^{\nu}>0
:

|F(x,\nu_{2})-F(x,\nu_{1})|\leq M_{1}^{\nu}|\nu_{2}-\nu_{1}|\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{X},\,\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in\overline{V}.

Then for every
0<\mu<\tilde{\mu}
there exist
M_{0},N_{0}>0
and
K\geq 1
such that if

\sup\{|R(x,\nu)|:(x,\nu)\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V}\}\leq M_{0},

as well as

\tilde{\psi}\in\{\psi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},\overline{V}):|\dot{\psi}(t)|\leq N_{0}\quad\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}\},

then for the process
\{T(t,s;\tilde{\psi}):t\geq s\}
associated to
A(\tilde{\psi}(t))
it holds that

|T(t,s;\tilde{\psi})\xi|\leq Ke^{-\mu(t-s)}|\xi|,\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb{X},t\geq s.

Proof.

By
lemma

61
, we have that for the family of uniformly continuous semigroups
\{e^{A(\nu)t}:\nu\in\overline{V}\}
there exists
K\geq 1
such that for every
\nu\in\overline{V}

|e^{A(\nu)t}\xi|\leq Ke^{-\mu t}|\xi|,\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb{X},\,t\geq 0.

For arbitrary
0<\mu<\tilde{\mu}
, pick
l
such that
Ke^{-\tilde{\mu}l}\leq e^{-\mu l}
, and next choose
M_{0},N_{0}>0
sufficiently small such that

K(M_{1}^{\nu}N_{0}l+2M_{0})\leq\tilde{\mu}-\mu.

Then if

\sup\{|R(x,\nu)|:(x,\nu)\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V}\}\leq M_{0},

as well as

\tilde{\psi}\in\{\psi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},\overline{V}):|\dot{\psi}(t)|\leq N_{0}\quad\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}\},

we have for
t\geq s
with
|t-s|\leq l
that

\displaystyle\left\|A(\tilde{\psi}(t))-A(\tilde{\psi}(s))\right\|

\displaystyle\leq M_{1}^{\nu}|\tilde{\psi}(t)-\tilde{\psi}(s)|+2M_{0}

\displaystyle\leq M_{1}^{\nu}N_{0}l+2M_{0}\leq(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)/K.

An application of
lemma

65
now gives the desired result.

The above
corollary

66
is a more general case of a theorem that is analogous to theorems in texts by Coppel
[
15
]
and Henry
[
31
]
:

Corollary 67
.

Let
\mathcal{A}:=\{A(\nu)\in L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X}):\nu\in\overline{V}\}
be a pre-compact subset of
L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X})
, such that for some
\mu>0
it holds that

\sup\{\real\lambda:\nu\in\overline{V},\lambda\in\sigma(A(\nu))\}<-\mu.

Assume also that
A
depends uniformly Lipschitz on
\nu\in\overline{V}
, i.e. there exists
M_{1}^{\nu}
such that for all
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in\overline{V}
it holds that

\|A(\nu_{2})-A(\nu_{1})\|\leq M_{1}^{\nu}|\nu_{2}-\nu_{1}|.

Then there exist
N_{0}>0
and
K\geq 1
such that for every

\tilde{\psi}\in\{\psi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},\overline{V}):|\dot{\psi}(t)|\leq N_{0}\quad\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}\},

the process
\{T(t,s;\tilde{\psi}):t\geq s\}
associated to
A(\tilde{\psi}(t))
satisfies the property

|T(t,s;\tilde{\psi})\xi|\leq Ke^{-\mu(t-s)}|\xi|,\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb{X},t\geq s.

Proof.

As shown for example in Chapter III Lemma 6.2 from Daleckii and Krein
[
16
]
, as
\mathcal{A}
is pre-compact there exist
\varepsilon>0
and
K\geq 1
such that it holds for all
\nu\in\overline{V}
that

|e^{A(\nu)t}\xi|\leq Ke^{-(\mu+\varepsilon)t}|\xi|,\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb{X},\,t\geq 0.

Now pick
l
such that
Ke^{-(\mu+\varepsilon)l}\leq e^{-\mu l}
, and next choose
N_{0}>0
sufficiently small such that
KM_{1}^{\nu}N_{0}l\leq\varepsilon
. Then if

\tilde{\psi}\in\{\psi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},\overline{V}):|\dot{\psi}(t)|\leq N_{0}\quad\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}\},

we have for
t\geq s
with
|t-s|\leq l
that

\displaystyle\left\|A(\tilde{\psi}(t))-A(\tilde{\psi}(s))\right\|\leq M_{1}^{\nu}|\tilde{\psi}(t)-\tilde{\psi}(s)|\leq M_{1}^{\nu}N_{0}l\leq\varepsilon/K.

An application of
lemma

65
lastly gives the desired result.

Appendix C

This appendix proves that the space
\mathcal{E}^{0}
introduced in
section

7
, is a Banach space when equipped with the
\mathcal{E}
-norm. It is partly similar to a proof in Chapter 4 of
[
9
]
.

Lemma 68
.

The space

\mathcal{E}^{0}:=\left\{Q\in C^{0}(\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V},\overline{V}):Q(0,\cdot)=0;\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V};\\
\xi\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{|Q(\xi,\eta)|}{|\xi|}<\infty\right\}.

is a Banach space with norm

\|Q\|_{\mathcal{E}}:=\sup\left\{\frac{|Q(\xi,\eta)|}{|\xi|}:(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V},\xi\neq 0\right\}.

Proof.

We let
(Q_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{E}^{0}
. For each
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V}
we have then that
(Q_{n}(\xi,\eta))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy Sequence in
\overline{V}
, and therefore has a limit in
\overline{V}
that we denote by
Q(\xi,\eta)
. We now need to show that
Q\in\mathcal{E}^{0}
and that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|Q_{n}-Q\|_{\mathcal{E}}=0.

To show the convergence, pick
\varepsilon>0
arbitrarily and
m,n
sufficiently large such that

\|Q_{n}-Q_{m}\|_{\mathcal{E}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

then we have for each
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V}
that

\displaystyle|Q_{n}(\xi,\eta)-Q(\xi,\eta)|

\displaystyle\leq|Q_{n}(\xi,\eta)-Q_{m}(\xi,\eta)|+|Q_{m}(\xi,\eta)-Q(\xi,\eta)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|\xi|+|Q_{m}(\xi,\eta)-Q(\xi,\eta)|.

For sufficiently large
n
we have then for all
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V}
, by letting
m\rightarrow\infty
, that

|Q_{n}(\xi,\eta)-Q(\xi,\eta)|<\varepsilon|\xi|,

and therefore

\|Q_{n}-Q\|_{\mathcal{E}}<\varepsilon.

This proves that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|Q_{n}-Q\|_{\mathcal{E}}=0.

To show
Q\in\mathcal{E}^{0}
, first pick
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\|Q-Q_{n}\|_{\mathcal{E}}<1
. Then we have

\|Q\|_{\mathcal{E}}\leq\|Q-Q_{n}\|_{\mathcal{E}}+\|Q_{n}\|_{\mathcal{E}}<1+\|Q_{n}\|_{\mathcal{E}},

and thereby the
\mathcal{E}
-norm of
Q
is bounded. Note that this also implies
Q(0,\cdot)=0
by definition of the
\mathcal{E}
-norm. To show continuity of
Q
, observe that, for each
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V}
, for
\Delta\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
sufficiently small we have

\displaystyle|Q((\xi,\eta)+\Delta)-Q(\xi,\eta)|

\displaystyle\leq|Q((\xi,\eta)+\Delta)-Q_{n}((\xi,\eta)+\Delta)|

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+|Q_{n}((\xi,\eta)+\Delta)-Q_{n}(\xi,\eta)|+|Q_{n}(\xi,\eta)-Q(\xi,\eta)|.

Now continuity of
Q_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and convergence of
(Q_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in the
\mathcal{E}
-norm to
Q
can be used to show continuity of
Q
via a standard argument. Namely, fix
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{X}\times\overline{V}
, and for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
pick
n\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large and
\delta>0
sufficiently small such that
|\Delta|<\delta
implies both

\|Q_{n}-Q\|_{\mathcal{E}}<\varepsilon/3,\quad|Q_{n}((\xi,\eta)+\Delta)-Q_{n}(\xi,\eta)|<\varepsilon/3.

Then we see that for
\delta>0
sufficiently small,
|\Delta|<\delta
must imply that

|Q((\xi,\eta)+\Delta)-Q(\xi,\eta)|\leq\varepsilon(|\xi|+1).

This implies continuity
Q
, and thereby the proof that
(\mathcal{E}^{0},\|{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\|_{\mathcal{E}})
is a Banach space is completed.

Appendix D

This appendix provides an extension of the Uniform Contraction Mapping Theorem, which we use in
section

4
.

Theorem 69
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
,
\mathbb{Y}
and
\Lambda
be Banach spaces,
U\subset\mathbb{X}
and
V\subset\Lambda
are open subsets. Suppose that
\overline{U}
can be continuously embedded in
\mathbb{Y}
via the operator
J:\overline{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{Y}
. Let
F:\overline{U}\times V\rightarrow\overline{U}
be a uniform contraction, i.e. there exists a single constant
\kappa\in[0,1)
such that for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
it holds that

\|F(x_{2},\lambda)-F(x_{1},\lambda)\|_{\mathbb{X}}\leq\kappa\|x_{2}-x_{1}\|_{\mathbb{X}}\quad\text{for all }x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{X}.

For each
\lambda\in V
we then denote by
g(\lambda)
the unique fixed point of the contraction
x\mapsto F(x,\lambda)
in
\overline{U}
.

Then if
J\circ F:\overline{U}\times\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{Y}
depends continuously on
\lambda\in\Lambda
in the
\|{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\|_{\mathbb{Y}}
-norm, the map
J\circ g:\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{Y}
depends continuously on
\lambda\in\Lambda
in the
\|{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\|_{\mathbb{Y}}
-norm as well.

Proof.

This is a special case of the material in Section 4 of
[
56
]
.

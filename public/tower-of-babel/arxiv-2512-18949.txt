A TraceFEM C 0 C^{0} Interior Penalty Method for the Surface Biharmonic Equation Thanks: The authors were supported in part through the NSF, grant DMS-2309425.
Michael Neilan, Hongzhi Wan

Abstract
. We construct and analyze a TraceFEM discretization for the surface biharmonic problem. The method utilizes standard quadratic Lagrange finite element spaces defined on a three-dimensional background mesh and a symmetric C 0 C^{0} interior penalty formulation posed on a second-order polyhedral approximation of the surface. Stability is achieved through a combination of surface edge penalties and bulk-facet penalization of gradient and Hessian jumps. We prove optimal first-order convergence in a discrete H 2 H^{2} norm and quadratic convergence in the L 2 L^{2} norm.



1.
Introduction

TraceFEM has become a widely used unfitted
method for surface PDEs due to its conceptual simplicity
and robustness with respect to surface geometry.
In contrast to surface finite element methods (SFEMs), which
build discrete spaces directly on a surface triangulation, TraceFEM
employs standard volumetric finite element spaces defined on a fixed
three-dimensional background mesh and poses the discrete formulation
on the induced two-dimensional surface mesh.
This approach enables
the use of conventional finite element spaces, and it is particularly well-suited
for problems involving evolving surfaces as well as problems with coupling conditions
with the surrounding bulk. This framework is now relatively established and has been
successfully applied for second-order elliptic problems, as well
as for the surface Navier-Stokes equations (cf., e.g.,
[
20
,
21
,
5
,
14
]
).

Although several TraceFEM (and SFEM) discretizations have been
developed in the past few decades, substantially fewer
results exist for finite element methods for fourth-order surface PDEs.
Existing contributions include
SFEMs based on
C^{0}
interior penalty (IP) methods
in
[
15
,
18
]
, non-conforming SFEMs using
Zienkiewicz-type elements
[
27
]
, and SFEMs built
on either Ciarlet-Raviart or Hellan–Herrmann–Johnson mixed formulations

[
19
,
1
,
26
,
25
]
.
In contrast, to the best of our knowledge, the only TraceFEM discretization for fourth-order
surface PDEs is given in

[
23
,
2
]
which proposes and
analyzes TraceFEM discretizations for the surface stream function
formulation of the Stokes problem, a fourth-order PDE whose
principal operator is the surface biharmonic.
Their method introduces a Ciarlet-Raviart-type mixed finite
element formulation, where the Laplace-Beltrami of the solution is introduced
as an additional unknown. While this construction is relatively straightforward,
using standard Lagrange finite element spaces, it increases
the number of degrees of freedom and leads to a relatively
large saddle-point system.

In this paper, we introduce and analyze a TraceFEM for the surface biharmonic
problem based on a symmetric
C^{0}
IP formulation
[
3
,
10
]
.
The discrete space is the standard
quadratic Lagrange finite element space defined on a three-dimensional
background mesh, with consistency enforced through edge-based integral terms.
In addition to penalization of (surface) gradients over surface edges, analogous to the Euclidean setting, the proposed method also includes penalization
of the gradient and Hessian operators across facets in the background mesh. We show
that these bulk-penalty terms ensure stability of the method, independent
to how the mesh intersects the surface.
We prove optimal first-order convergence in a discrete

H^{2}
norm and quadratic convergence in the
L^{2}
norm, with both
estimates taking geometric errors into account.

The proposed method offers several advantages, foremost is
its simplicity: similar to
[
23
,
2
]
,
it uses standard Lagrange finite element spaces which
are supported in existing software programs (e.g., NGSolve
[
24
]
).
In addition, the proposed scheme is a primal formulation involving a single unknown.
The resulting algebraic system is symmetric positive definite, in line
with the underlying structure of the elliptic PDE.
As far as we are
aware, this is the first primal TraceFEM for fourth-order surface PDEs.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
Section
2
introduces
the relevant surface differential operators as well as
the surface biharmonic problem. Section

3
describes the surface approximation, triangulations, scaling estimates
and norm equivalences with respect to the exact and discrete surfaces.
In Section
4
, we present the
C^{0}
IP method and establish
coercivity, proving that the discrete problem is well posed.
Section
5
gives the error analysis,
including optimal first-order convergence
in a discrete
H^{2}
norm and second-order
L^{2}
estimates
obtained via a duality argument.
Finally, in Section
6
we perform
some numerical experiments which support
the theoretical results.

2.
The surface biharmonic problem

We assume
\Gamma
is a simply connected compact oriented smooth surface without boundary in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We assume that
\Gamma
is the level set of a smooth function

\phi
, i.e.,
\Gamma=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\ \phi(x)=0\}
.
Let
U_{\delta}(\Gamma)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:{\rm dist}(x,\Gamma)<\delta\}
be a neighborhood
of
\Gamma
with
\delta>0
sufficiently small such that the signed distance function
d
(with
d<0
in the interior of
\Gamma
) is well defined. Set
{\bm{n}}=\nabla d
, a column vector,
so that
{\bm{n}}|_{\Gamma}
is the outward unit normal of
\Gamma
. We let
{\bf P}
be the tangential projection, i.e.,

{\bf P}={\bf I}-{\bm{n}}\otimes{\bm{n}},

where
{\bf I}
denotes the
3\times 3
identity matrix.
The closest point projection
{\bm{p}}:U_{\delta}\to\Gamma
is

{\bm{p}}(x)=x-d(x){\bm{n}}(x).

The Weingarten map is denoted by
{\bf H}=\nabla^{2}d
,
and the principal curvatures of
\Gamma
are denoted by
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
.

For a function
\psi
defined on the surface
\Gamma
, its extension

\psi^{e}:U_{\delta}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is given by

\psi^{e}=\psi\circ{\bm{p}}.

On the other hand, a function
\psi
defined
on some
\tilde{\Gamma}\subset U_{\delta}(\Gamma)
,
can be lifted to
\Gamma
via

\psi^{\ell}\circ{\bm{p}}=\psi\quad\text{on }\tilde{\Gamma}.

We will often drop the subscripts
e
and
\ell
for the extensions and lifts
if the context is clear.

The surface gradient of a scalar function

\psi:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi={\bf P}\nabla\psi^{e}.

For a vector-valued function
{\bm{v}}=[v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}]^{\intercal}
,
the extension is defined componentwise so that
({\bm{v}}^{e})_{j}=v_{j}^{e}
and its
Jacobian
\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{v}}
satisfies
(\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{v}})_{i,j}=\frac{\partial v_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}
for
i,j=1,2,3
. The surface Jacobian and the surface divergence are as follows:

\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bm{v}}={\bf P}\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{v}}^{e}{\bf P},\qquad{\rm div}_{\Gamma}{\bm{v}}={\rm tr}(\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bm{v}}).

Combining the above definitions, we define the projected surface Hessian
\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\psi:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}

and Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
as

\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\psi=\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi,\quad\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi={\rm div}_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi={\rm tr}(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\psi).

Given a source function
f:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}

with vanishing mean value
\int_{\Gamma}f=0
, we consider
the problem of finding
u:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfying
\int_{\Gamma}u=0
such that

(2.1)

\Delta_{\Gamma}^{2}u=f\quad\text{on }\Gamma,

where
\Delta_{\Gamma}^{2}u:=\Delta_{\Gamma}(\Delta_{\Gamma}u)
.

3.
Preliminaries

3.1.
Approximate Geometries and Meshes

Let
\mathcal{T}^{\rm ext}_{h}
be a quasi-uniform mesh of shape-regular simplices of a polygonal domain containing
U_{\delta}(\Gamma)
with
h\approx{\rm diam}(T)\ \forall T\in\mathcal{T}^{ext}
. Let
\phi_{h}
be the piecewise linear Lagrange interpolant
of the level set
\phi
, and let
\Gamma_{h}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\ \phi_{h}(x)=0\}

be a polyhedral approximation to
\Gamma
. By standard approximation
properties of the Lagrange interpolant, we have

(3.1)

\|d\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}\lesssim h^{2},\qquad\|{\bm{n}}-{\bm{n}}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}\lesssim h,

where
{\bm{n}}_{h}
is the outward unit normal of
\Gamma_{h}
. Here, we use the notation
A\lesssim B
to mean
A\leq cB
for some constant
c>0
independent of
h
as well as
A\approx B
to mean
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
. The constant
c
is also
independent on how the surface
\Gamma_{h}
intersects
the background mesh
\mathcal{T}^{\rm ext}_{h}
.
Above and throughout this paper,
we assume
h
is sufficiently small so that
\Gamma_{h}\subset U_{\delta}
, in particular,
the closest point projection is well defined on
\Gamma_{h}
.
We define the tangential projection with respect
to
\Gamma_{h}
as
{\bf P}_{h}={\bf I}-{\bm{n}}_{h}\otimes{\bm{n}}_{h}

and note that, due to (
3.1
),

(3.2)

\|{\bf P}-{\bf P}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}\lesssim h.

We define the active mesh as
the set of simplices in
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm ext}
intersecting the approximate surface
\Gamma_{h}
:

\mathcal{T}_{h}=\{T\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm ext}_{h}:T\cap\Gamma_{h}\neq\varnothing\},\qquad\Omega_{h}=\bigcup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}T.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{h}
denote the set of two-dimensional faces in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, and define
the induced triangulation of
\Gamma_{h}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{h}

\displaystyle=\{K=T\cap\Gamma_{h}:T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}.

The set of one-dimensional edges in
\mathcal{K}_{h}
is denoted by
\mathcal{E}_{h}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{h}

\displaystyle=\{E=\partial K^{+}\cap\partial K^{-}:\ K^{+}\neq K^{-},\ K^{+},K^{-}\in\mathcal{K}_{h}\},

where
\partial K^{\pm}
denote the boundaries of
K^{\pm}
.
If
F=\partial T^{+}\cap\partial T^{-}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
, then we use the notation

{\bm{\nu}}_{F}^{\pm}
for the outward unit normal of
\partial T^{\pm}
restricted to
F
.
Likewise, for
E=\partial K^{+}\cap\partial K^{-}
,
{\bm{\mu}}_{E}^{\pm}
is the unit co-normal of
\partial K^{\pm}

restricted to
E
. We shall use the convention
{\bm{\nu}}_{F}={\bm{\nu}}_{F}^{+}
and
{\bm{\mu}}_{E}=({\bm{\mu}}_{E}^{+}-{\bm{\mu}}^{-}_{E})/(1-{\bm{\mu}}^{+}_{E}\cdot{\bm{\mu}}^{-}_{E})
(cf.
[
15
]
) throughout the paper.
We also let
{\bm{t}}_{E}
denote a unit vector tangent to the edge
E
,
and set
{\bm{\mu}}_{h}:\mathcal{E}_{h}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
{\bm{\mu}}_{h}|_{E}={\bm{\mu}}_{E}

for all
E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
.

Analogous mesh quantities under
the image of the closest point projection
are denoted by the superscript
\ell
. For example,

\mathcal{K}_{h}^{\ell}=\{{\bm{p}}(K):\ K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}\}

is the induced surface triangulation on the exact surface
\Gamma
,
and
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\ell}=\{{\bm{p}}(E):\ E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\}
is the set of edges
of
\mathcal{K}_{h}^{\ell}
. We use the notation
K^{\ell}={\bm{p}}(K)

and
E^{\ell}={\bm{p}}(E)
for
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
and
E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
,
let
{\bm{\mu}}_{E^{\ell}}
be the unit co-normal of
E^{\ell}
,
and set
{\bm{\mu}}_{\ell}:\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\ell}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
{\bm{\mu}}_{\ell}|_{E^{\ell}}={\bm{\mu}}_{E^{\ell}}
.
Note that
{\bm{\mu}}_{E^{\ell}}\neq{\bm{\mu}}^{\ell}_{E}:={\bm{p}}({\bm{\mu}}_{E})
in general.

On an edge
E=\partial K^{+}\cap\partial K^{-}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}

and face
F=\partial T^{+}\cap\partial T^{-}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
, we define the jump
of functions
v
and
w
defined on
\Gamma_{h}
and
\Omega_{h}
respectively as

\displaystyle[v]|_{E}=v^{+}|_{E}-v^{-}|_{E},\qquad[w]|_{F}=w^{+}|_{F}-w^{-}|_{F},

where
v^{\pm}
and
w^{\pm}
are the restrictions of
v
and
w
to
K^{\pm}
and
T^{\pm}
, respectively.
Note that, by the definition of
{\bm{\mu}}_{E}
,
[\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v]\cdot{\bm{\mu}}_{E}|_{E}=\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v^{+}\cdot{\bm{\mu}}_{E}^{+}|_{E}+\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v^{-}\cdot{\bm{\mu}}_{E}^{-}|_{E}
.
The averages of
v
and
w
across
E
and
F
are respectively given by

\displaystyle\{v\}|_{E}=\frac{1}{2}(v^{+}+v^{-})|_{E},\qquad\{w\}_{F}=\frac{1}{2}(w^{+}+w^{-})|_{F}.

Throughout this work, we use the notation

(\cdot,\cdot)_{D}
and
\|\cdot\|_{D}
to denote the
L^{2}
-inner product
and
L^{2}
-norm with respect to the domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\ (d=2,3)
.
For a set of geometric quantities
\mathcal{P}_{h}
, we let
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathcal{P}_{h}}=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{h}}(\cdot,\cdot)_{P}

denote the piecewise
L^{2}
-inner product with respect to
\mathcal{P}_{h}
.

For a scalar-valued function
u:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
its
W^{m,p}
surface Sobolev norm is given by (
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\ 1\leq p<\infty)

\|u\|_{W^{m,p}(\Gamma)}^{p}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\|\nabla_{\Gamma}^{k}u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}^{p}.

For vector-valued function
{\bm{\chi}}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
{\bm{\chi}}=(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\chi_{3})^{\intercal}
, we set

\|{\bm{\chi}}\|_{W^{m,p}(\Gamma)}^{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\|\chi_{j}\|_{W^{m,p}(\Gamma)}^{p}.

We denote the piecewise
W^{m,p}
-space over a
set of geometric entities
\mathcal{P}_{h}
as

W^{m,p}(\mathcal{P}_{h})=\prod_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{h}}W^{m,p}(P),

with corresponding norm

\|\cdot\|_{W^{m,p}(\mathcal{P}_{h})}^{p}=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{h}}\|\cdot\|_{W^{m,p}(P)}^{p}
.
Similarly,
\|\cdot\|_{W^{m,p}(\partial\mathcal{P}_{h})}^{p}=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{h}}\|\cdot\|_{W^{m,p}(\partial P)}^{p}
.
This notation is also adopted for semi-norms, and we use the standard conventions
H^{m}=W^{m,2}
and
W^{0,p}=L^{p}
. Furthermore, in the case
m=0
and
p=2
, we write

\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{P}_{h}}=\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{P}_{h})}

and
\|\cdot\|_{\partial\mathcal{P}_{h}}=\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\partial\mathcal{P}_{h})}

For
\tau>0
and a point
x
on
\Gamma
, we set
D_{\tau}(x)=\{y\in\Gamma:|x-y|<\tau\}
,
and

\mathcal{K}_{\tau,x}=\{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}:K^{\ell}\cap D_{\tau}(x)\neq\varnothing\},\quad\mathcal{T}_{\tau,x}=\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}:T\cap\Gamma_{h}\in\mathcal{K}_{\tau,x}\}.

Note that the surface mesh
\mathcal{K}_{h}
can
have arbitrarily small elements and is generally not
shape regular. Nonetheless, the following result from (cf.
[
4
, (5.1)–(5.4)]
), essentially states that, for a triangle
in
\mathcal{K}_{h}
with a large aspect ratio, there exists
a nearby triangle that is shape-regular.

Lemma 3.1

(Large Intersection Covering Property)
.

There exists a set of points
\mathcal{X}_{h}\subset\Gamma

and constant
c>0
such that

\{\mathcal{K}_{ch,x},x\in\mathcal{X}_{h}\}
and
\{\mathcal{T}_{ch,x},x\in\mathcal{X}_{h}\}
are coverings of
\mathcal{K}_{h}
and
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, respectively, with the following properties for
h
sufficiently small :

•

The number of sets containing a given point
y
is uniformly bounded,

\#\{x\in\mathcal{X}_{h}:y\in\mathcal{T}_{ch,x}\}\lesssim 1\qquad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

•

The number of elements in each set
\mathcal{T}_{ch,x}
is uniformly bounded,

\#\mathcal{T}_{ch,x}\lesssim 1\qquad\forall x\in\mathcal{X}_{h},

and each element in
\mathcal{T}_{ch,x}
shares at least one face with another element in
\mathcal{T}_{ch,x}
.

•

For all
x\in\mathcal{X}_{h}
there exists an element
T_{x}\in\mathcal{T}_{ch,x}
that has a large intersection with
\Gamma_{h}
in the sense that

(3.3)

\displaystyle|T_{x}|

\displaystyle\sim h|T_{x}\cap\Gamma_{h}|=h|K_{x}|\qquad\forall x\in\mathcal{X}_{h}.

3.2.
Norm Correspondences

We set
\mu_{h}={\bm{n}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}(1-d\kappa_{1})(1-d\kappa_{2})
,
and for each
E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
,

\mu_{E}=|({\bf P}-d{\bf H}){\bm{t}}_{E}|
.
We then have the change of variable formulas
[
8
,
6
]

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{h}}v^{e}\mu_{h}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}v\quad\forall v\in L^{1}(\Gamma),\qquad\int_{E}v^{e}\mu_{E}=\int_{E^{\ell}}v\quad\forall v\in L^{1}(E^{\ell}).

Because
|1-\mu_{h}|=\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
and
|1-\mu_{E}|=\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|v^{e}\|_{L^{p}(K)}\approx\|v\|_{L^{p}(K^{\ell})},\qquad\|v^{e}\|_{L^{p}(\partial K)}\approx\|v\|_{L^{p}(\partial K^{\ell})}\qquad\forall v\in W^{1,p}(K^{\ell}).

Likewise, since (cf.
[
7
]
)

(3.5)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v^{e}

\displaystyle={\bf P}_{h}({\bf P}-d{\bf H})(\nabla_{\Gamma}v)^{e},

we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v^{e}\|_{L^{p}(K)}\approx\|\nabla_{\Gamma}v\|_{L^{p}(K^{\ell})},\quad\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v^{e}\|_{L^{p}(\partial K)}\approx\|\nabla_{\Gamma}v\|_{L^{p}(\partial K^{\ell})}\quad\forall v\in W^{2,p}(K^{\ell}).

There also holds
[
15
,
18
]
,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2}v^{e}

\displaystyle={\bf P}_{h}(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}v)^{e}{\bf P}_{h}-d{\bf P}_{h}{\bf H}(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}v)^{e}{\bf P}_{h}-d{\bf P}_{h}(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}v)^{e}{\bf H}{\bf P}_{h}+d^{2}{\bf P}_{h}{\bf H}(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}v)^{e}{\bf H}{\bf P}_{h}

\displaystyle\qquad-({\bf P}_{h}{\bm{n}})\otimes({\bf H}(\nabla_{\Gamma}v)^{e}){\bf P}_{h}-({\bf P}_{h}{\bf H}(\nabla_{\Gamma}v)^{e})\otimes({\bf P}_{h}{\bm{n}})-d{\bf P}_{h}\nabla{\bf H}(\nabla_{\Gamma}v)^{e}{\bf P}_{h},

where for a vector valued function
{\bm{v}}=[v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}]^{\intercal}
,
the quantity
\nabla{\bf H}{\bm{v}}
is a
3\times 3
matrix with entries

(3.7)

\displaystyle(\nabla{\bf H}{\bm{v}})_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\frac{\partial H_{i,k}}{\partial x_{j}}v_{k}\qquad i,j=1,2,3.

It thus follows that, for all
v\in W^{3,p}(K^{\ell})
,

(3.8)

\begin{split}\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2}v^{e}\|_{L^{p}(K)}&\lesssim\|\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}v\|_{L^{p}(K^{\ell})}+h\|\nabla_{\Gamma}v\|_{L^{p}(K^{\ell})},\\
\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2}v^{e}\|_{L^{p}(\partial K)}&\lesssim\|\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}v\|_{L^{p}(\partial K^{\ell})}+h\|\nabla_{\Gamma}v\|_{L^{p}(\partial K^{\ell})},\end{split}

and for
v\in W^{3,p}(K)
,

(3.9)

\begin{split}\|\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}v^{\ell}\|_{L^{p}(K^{\ell})}&\lesssim\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2}v\|_{L^{p}(K)}+h\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{L^{p}(K)},\\
\|\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}v^{\ell}\|_{L^{p}(\partial K^{\ell})}&\lesssim\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2}v\|_{L^{p}(\partial K)}+h\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{L^{p}(\partial K)}.\end{split}

Next, by virtue of
\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v^{e}={\rm tr}(\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2}v^{e})

and
\Delta_{\Gamma}v={\rm tr}(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}v)

we have

(3.10)

\begin{split}\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v^{e}&=(\Delta_{\Gamma}v)^{e}-{\bm{n}}_{h}^{\intercal}(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}v)^{e}{\bm{n}}_{h}\\
&\qquad-2d({\rm tr}({\bf H}(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}v)^{e})-{\bm{n}}_{h}^{\intercal}{\bf H}(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}v)^{e}{\bm{n}}_{h})\\
&\qquad+d^{2}({\rm tr}({\bf H}(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}v)^{e}{\bf H})-{\bm{n}}_{h}^{\intercal}{\bf H}(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}v)^{e}{\bf H}{\bm{n}}_{h})\\
&\qquad+2({\bm{n}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}){\bm{n}}_{h}^{\intercal}{\bf H}(\nabla_{\Gamma}v)^{e}-d{\rm tr}({\bf P}_{h}\nabla{\bf H}(\nabla_{\Gamma}v)^{e}).\end{split}

Therefore

(3.11)

\begin{split}\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v^{e}\|_{L^{p}(K)}&\lesssim\|\Delta_{\Gamma}v\|_{L^{p}(K^{\ell})}+h^{2}\|\nabla^{2}_{\Gamma}v\|_{L^{p}(K^{\ell})}+h\|\nabla_{\Gamma}v\|_{L^{p}(K^{\ell})},\\
\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v^{e}\|_{L^{p}(\partial K)}&\lesssim\|\Delta_{\Gamma}v\|_{L^{p}(\partial K^{\ell})}+h^{2}\|\nabla^{2}_{\Gamma}v\|_{L^{p}(\partial K^{\ell})}+h\|\nabla_{\Gamma}v\|_{L^{p}(\partial K^{\ell})},\end{split}

and

(3.12)

\begin{split}\|\Delta_{\Gamma}v^{\ell}\|_{L^{p}(K^{\ell})}&\lesssim\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{L^{p}(K)}+h^{2}\|\nabla^{2}_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{L^{p}(K)}+h\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{L^{p}(K)},\\
\|\Delta_{\Gamma}v^{\ell}\|_{L^{p}(\partial K^{\ell})}&\lesssim\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{L^{p}(\partial K)}+h^{2}\|\nabla^{2}_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{L^{p}(\partial K)}+h\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{L^{p}(\partial K)}.\end{split}

We end this subsection with
an estimate that relates the
L^{p}
norm
on the bulk domain
to the
L^{p}
norm on the surface.

3.4.
Scaling Estimates I

We will frequently use the following trace inequalities for
v\in W^{2,p}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
(

4.
C^{0}
Interior Penalty Method

We define the following bilinear forms and linear form

(4.1)

\displaystyle A_{h}(v,w)

\displaystyle=a_{h}(v,w)+s_{h}(v,w),

(4.2)

\displaystyle a_{h}(v,w)

\displaystyle=(\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v,\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}w)_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}-(\{\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v\},{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot[\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}w])_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}-({\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot[\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v],\{\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}w\})_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}

(4.3)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\sigma}{h}({\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot[\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v],{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot[\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}w])_{\mathcal{E}_{h}},

(4.4)

\displaystyle s_{h}(v,w)

\displaystyle=\gamma\left(([\nabla v],[\nabla w])_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}+([\nabla^{2}v],[\nabla^{2}w])_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}\right),

(4.5)

\displaystyle l_{h}(v)

\displaystyle=(f_{h},v)_{\Gamma_{h}},

where
f_{h}
is an approximation to
f
defined on
\Gamma_{h}
,
and
\sigma,\gamma>0
are penalty parameters.
The
C^{0}
interior penalty method is to find
u_{h}\in V_{h,0}
such that

(4.6)

\displaystyle A_{h}(u_{h},v)

\displaystyle=l_{h}(v)\qquad\forall v\in V_{h,0}.

Associated to the bilinear forms, we define the (semi)norms

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|v\|_{h}^{2}&=\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+h^{-1}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot[\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v]\|_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}^{2}+\|[\nabla v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}+\|[\nabla^{2}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2},\\
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{h}^{2}&=\|v\|_{h}^{2}+h\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\partial\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}.\end{split}

We also define the analogous bilinear form and linear form defined on the exact surface:

\displaystyle a_{h}^{\ell}(v,w)

\displaystyle=(\Delta_{\Gamma}v,\Delta_{\Gamma}w)_{\mathcal{K}_{h}^{\ell}}-(\{\Delta_{\Gamma}v\},{\bm{\mu}}_{\ell}\cdot[\nabla_{\Gamma}w])_{\mathcal{E}^{\ell}_{h}}-({\bm{\mu}}_{\ell}\cdot[\nabla_{\Gamma}v],\{\Delta_{\Gamma}w\})_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\ell}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\sigma}{h}({\bm{\mu}}_{\ell}\cdot[\nabla_{\Gamma}v],{\bm{\mu}}_{\ell}\cdot[\nabla_{\Gamma}w])_{\mathcal{E}^{\ell}_{h}},

\displaystyle l(v)

\displaystyle=(f,v)_{\Gamma},

along with the corresponding norms:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|v\|_{*,h}^{2}&=\|\Delta_{\Gamma}v\|_{\mathcal{K}^{\ell}_{h}}^{2}+h^{-1}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\ell}\cdot[\nabla_{\Gamma}v]\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\ell}_{h}}^{2}+\|[\nabla v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}+\|[\nabla^{2}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2},\\
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*,h}^{2}&=\|v\|_{*,h}^{2}+h\|\Delta_{\Gamma}v\|_{\partial\mathcal{K}^{\ell}_{h}}^{2}\end{split}

Remark 4.1
.

Note that, just like in the Euclidean setting,
the bilinear form
a_{h}^{\ell}(\cdot,\cdot)
is consistent
with the surface biharmonic operator. In particular,
standard integration-by-parts identities
show that the exact solution to (
2.1
)
satisfies
a_{h}^{\ell}(u,v^{\ell})=l(v^{\ell})
for all
v\in V_{h,0}
provided
u\in H^{3}(\Gamma)
.

4.1.
Scaling Estimates II

The stability and convergence analysis of the
C^{0}
IP method requires a few preliminary results, relating norms between the surface and bulk triangulations as well as equivalence norm estimates on
V_{h}
. First, we have the following Poincare-type inequality for piecewise polynomial spaces.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
w\in\mathbb{P}_{k}^{dc}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
,
and let
\lambda_{\Gamma_{h}}(w)\in\mathbb{R}
denote
the average of
w
over
\Gamma_{h}
.
Then for
h
sufficiently small, there holds

(4.7)

\displaystyle\|w-\lambda_{\Gamma_{h}}(w)\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}w\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+h^{2}\|\nabla w\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+h^{-1}\|[w]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}.

Proof.

The proof is a simple generalization of the one given in
[
4
, Lemma 5.2]
, and is therefore omitted.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

For
v\in V_{h}
and
h
sufficiently small, the following estimate holds:

(4.8)

\displaystyle h\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+\|[\nabla^{2}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}.

Proof.

Taking
k=0
and
w=\nabla^{2}v
in (
4.7
) with
v\in V_{h}
and setting
A=\lambda_{\Gamma_{h}}(\nabla^{2}v)
to be the average of
\nabla^{2}v
, a constant symmetric
3\times 3
matrix, we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}h\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}&\lesssim h\|A\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+h\|\nabla^{2}v-A\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim h\|A\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+\|[\nabla^{2}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2},\end{split}

where we used that
v
is a piecewise quadratic polynomial.
It remains to estimate the first term
h\|A\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}
. Clearly,

(4.10)

\displaystyle h\|A\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\|A\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim h^{2}\|A\|_{\Gamma}^{2}

because
A
is constant and
|\Gamma|\approx|\Gamma_{h}|
.

Claim:

\|A\|_{\Gamma}\lesssim\|{\bf P}:A\|_{\Gamma}
.

Proof of claim:

We show that
A\to\|{\bf P}:A\|_{\Gamma}
is a norm on constant symmetric matrices.
Suppose
\|{\bf P}:A\|_{\Gamma}=0
, which implies
{\bf P}:A=0
on
\Gamma
. Since
\Gamma
is a closed, orientable surface in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, its Gauss map is surjective, and so we can consider
{\bm{n}}={\bm{e}}_{j}\ (j=1,2,3)
, the unit vector whose
j
-th component is one. By considering these three cases we easily conclude
{\rm tr}(A)=0
. Therefore
0={\bf P}:A={\rm tr}(A)-{\bm{n}}^{\intercal}A{\bm{n}}=-{\bm{n}}^{\intercal}A{\bm{n}}
for all
{\bm{n}}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
. As
A
is symmetric, this implies
A=0
, and so
A\to\|{\bf P}:A\|_{\Gamma}
is a norm. By equivalence of norms, we then have
\|A\|_{\Gamma}\lesssim\|{\bf P}:A\|_{\Gamma}
, and the claim is proved.

Continuing from (
4.10
), we apply the claim and (
3.2
) to obtain

\displaystyle h\|A\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\|A\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim h^{2}\|{\bf P}:A\|_{\Gamma}^{2}\lesssim h^{2}\|{\bf P}:A\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\|{\bf P}_{h}:A\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+h^{2}\|({\bf P}-{\bf P}_{h}):A\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\|{\bf P}_{h}:A\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+h^{4}\|A\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}.

We absorb
h^{4}\|A\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}
to the left-hand side for
h
sufficiently small. Consequently,

(4.11)

\displaystyle h\|A\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim h^{2}\|{\bf P}_{h}:A\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}.

Because
{\bm{n}}_{h}
is constant, there holds

\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v={\bf P}_{h}:\nabla^{2}v
.
Therefore, using the boundedness of
{\bf P}_{h}
, (
3.20
), and (
4.7
), it follows that

\displaystyle h\|A\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\|{\bf P}_{h}:\nabla^{2}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+h^{2}\|{\bf P}_{h}:(A-\nabla^{2}v)\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+h\|A-\nabla^{2}v\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+\|[\nabla^{2}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2},

which in combination with (
4.9
) yields the desired inequality (
4.8
).
∎

Lemma 4.4

(Trace Inequality)
.

The following estimate holds

(4.12)

\displaystyle h\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\partial\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+\|[\nabla^{2}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}\leq\|v\|_{h}^{2}\qquad\forall v\in V_{h},

for
h
sufficiently small. Consequently, the norms
\|\cdot\|_{h}
and
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{h}
are equivalent on
V_{h}
.

Proof.

We start by observing that since both
{\bm{n}}_{h}
and
\nabla^{2}v
are piecewise constant functions on
\mathcal{K}_{h}
, so is
\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v
. Then, successively employing (
3.21
), (
3.19
), and applying the large intersection covering property and (
3.22
) to
\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v
yield

(4.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}h\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\partial\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}&\lesssim h^{-1}\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim h^{-1}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{h}}\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h,x}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim h^{-1}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{h}}\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{T_{x}}^{2}+\|[\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+\|[\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}.\end{split}

Next, we note that

\displaystyle\|[\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|{\rm tr}([{\bf P}_{h}\nabla^{2}v])\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}\leq\|[{\bf P}_{h}\nabla^{2}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}.

Therefore, by a standard identity for the jump operator, the continuity of
{\bf P}
, the boundedness of
{\bf P}_{h}
, (
3.2
), and (
3.19
) give us

(4.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|[{\bf P}_{h}\nabla^{2}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}&\lesssim\|[{\bf P}_{h}]\{\nabla^{2}v\}\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}+\|\{{\bf P}_{h}\}[\nabla^{2}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\|[{\bf P}-{\bf P}_{h}]\{\nabla^{2}v\}\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}+\|[\nabla^{2}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim h\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+\|[\nabla^{2}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}.\end{split}

We then use (
4.8
) to obtain

(4.15)

\displaystyle\|[\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+\|[\nabla^{2}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}.

Combining (
4.13
) and (
4.15
) yields the desired result (
4.12
).
∎

4.2.
Coercivity and Continuity

Lemma 4.5

(Coercivity)
.

The discrete bilinear form is coercive with respect to the discrete energy norm, i.e.,

(4.16)

\displaystyle\|v\|_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim A_{h}(v,v)\qquad\forall v\in V_{h},

provided
\sigma
is sufficient large and
\gamma>0
.

Proof.

Starting from the definition of
A_{h}
and applying Cauchy’s inequality with
\varepsilon
, we have

\displaystyle A_{h}(v,v)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}-\varepsilon h\|\{\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v\}\|_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}^{2}+\frac{\sigma-\varepsilon^{-1}}{h}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot[\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v]\|_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}^{2}+\gamma(\|[\nabla v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}+\|[\nabla^{2}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}),

for all
\varepsilon>0
.
Employing (
4.12
), we immediately see that

A_{h}(v,v)\gtrsim\|v\|_{h}^{2}.

when choosing
\varepsilon
small and
\sigma

large enough.
∎

Lemma 4.6

(Boundedness)
.

The discrete bilinear form is bounded in the following sense:

(4.17)

\displaystyle A_{h}(v,w)

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{h}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{h}\qquad\forall v,w\in(H^{3}(\Gamma))^{e}+V_{h}.

Moreover,

(4.18)

\displaystyle a_{h}^{\ell}(v,w)\lesssim{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*,h}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*,h}\qquad\forall v,w\in H^{3}(\Gamma)+V_{h}^{\ell}.

Proof.

The continuity estimates follow from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the definitions
of
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{h}
and
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*,h}
.

∎

Estimating the geometric errors

We introduce the following function spaces and norms

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=\{w\in C^{0}(\Gamma_{h}):w|_{K}\in H^{3}(K)\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}\},

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w^{\ell}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*,2h}\lesssim{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2h}

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

(5.1)

\displaystyle h{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{h}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi^{e}-\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2h}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi-(\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})^{\ell}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*,2h}\lesssim h\|\phi\|_{H^{3}(\Gamma)}+h^{2}\|\phi\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)}.

Let
\phi\in H^{4}(\Gamma)
and note that

due to
the smoothness of
\Gamma
.
Set

.
By repeated use of the trace inequality (

)–(
3.17
) and standard approximation properties of the Lagrange interpolant, we have

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|e\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{2h}\lesssim h^{-3}\|\nabla e\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+h^{-1}\|\nabla^{2}e\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+h\|\nabla^{3}e\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+h^{3}\|\nabla^{4}e\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim h|\phi^{e}|_{H^{3}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}+h^{3}|\phi^{e}|_{H^{4}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}.

Therefore by (
3.13
),
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|e\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2h}\lesssim h\|\phi\|_{H^{3}(\Gamma)}+h^{2}\|\phi\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)}
. Similar arguments,
along with (

), (

), and (

) yield

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|e^{\ell}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*,2h}\lesssim h\|\phi\|_{H^{3}(\Gamma)}+h^{2}\|\phi\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)}
.

Next, we use the inverse inequality (

), the local

-stability of the Lagrange interpolant, and (

) to obtain

\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}({\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim h^{-1}\|\nabla^{2}({\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim h^{-1}\|\nabla^{2}\phi^{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}^{2}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}.

We apply similar arguments in the norm

The estimates (

\displaystyle h{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{h}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi^{e}-\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2h}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi-(\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})^{\ell}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*,2h}

\displaystyle\lesssim h{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{h}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi^{e}-\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2h}

\displaystyle=h{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{h}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi^{e}-{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2h}

5.2.
L^{2}
Estimates

In this section, we prove
that the error
u^{e}-u_{h}

in the
L^{2}
norm converges quadratically
with respect to the mesh parameter.
To show this result, we first
extend the result given in
[
15
, Lemma 3.2]

to the TraceFEM setting.

Lemma 5.4
.

For
{\bm{\chi}}\in[W^{2,1}(\Gamma)]^{3}
and
h
small enough, there holds

(5.3)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Gamma_{h}}({\bf P}_{h}{\bm{n}})\cdot{\bm{\chi}}^{e}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\|{\bm{\chi}}\|_{W^{2,1}(\Gamma)},

(5.4)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Gamma_{h}}({\bf P}{\bm{n}}_{h})\cdot{\bm{\chi}}^{e}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\|{\bm{\chi}}\|_{W^{2,1}(\Gamma)}.

Proof.

First, notice that it is enough to prove (
5.3
) since,
if this inequality holds,
we can use the identity

{\bf P}{\bm{n}}_{h}=(1-{\bm{n}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h})({\bm{n}}+{\bm{n}}_{h})-{\bf P}_{h}{\bm{n}},

Holder’s inequality, (
3.1
), and (
3.4
) to get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Gamma_{h}}({\bf P}{\bm{n}}_{h})\cdot{\bm{\chi}}^{e}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\Gamma_{h}}(1-{\bm{n}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h})({\bm{n}}+{\bm{n}}_{h})\cdot{\bm{\chi}}^{e}-\int_{\Gamma_{h}}({\bf P}_{h}{\bm{n}})\cdot{\bm{\chi}}^{e}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\|{\bm{\chi}}\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}+h^{2}\|{\bm{\chi}}\|_{W^{2,1}(\Gamma)}\lesssim h^{2}\|{\bm{\chi}}\|_{W^{2,1}(\Gamma)},

i.e., (
5.4
) holds.

To begin the proof of (
5.3
),
we set
[\hskip-2.0pt[{\bm{\mu}}_{h}]\hskip-2.0pt]|_{E}:={\bm{\mu}}_{\partial K}^{+}+{\bm{\mu}}_{\partial K}^{-}
to denote
the jump of the co-normal across the edge
E
,
where
E=\partial K^{+}\cap\partial K^{-}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
.
By the divergence theorem, we have

(5.5)

\begin{split}\int_{\Gamma_{h}}({\bf P}_{h}{\bm{n}})\cdot{\bm{\chi}}^{e}&=\int_{\Gamma_{h}}({\bf P}_{h}\nabla d)\cdot{\bm{\chi}}^{e}\\
&=-\int_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}d\,\nabla\cdot({\bf P}_{h}{\bm{\chi}}^{e})+\int_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}d\,[\hskip-2.0pt[{\bm{\mu}}_{h}]\hskip-2.0pt]\cdot{\bm{\chi}}^{e}\\
&=:I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

Estimate

I_{1}
: Using that
{\bm{n}}_{h}
is piecewise constant, we have
\nabla\cdot({\bf P}_{h}{\bm{\chi}}^{e})={\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h}}{\bm{\chi}}^{e}
.
Therefore by (
3.1
), Holder’s inequality, and (
3.6
)
we have

(5.6)

|I_{1}|\lesssim h^{2}\|\nabla\cdot({\bf P}_{h}{\bm{\chi}}^{e})\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{K}_{h})}=h^{2}\|{\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h}}{\bm{\chi}}^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{K}_{h})}\lesssim h^{2}\|{\bm{\chi}}^{e}\|_{W^{1,1}(\mathcal{K}_{h})}\lesssim h^{2}\|{\bm{\chi}}\|_{{W}^{1,1}(\Gamma)}.

Estimate

I_{2}
: Applying Holder’s inequality again yields

(5.7)

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|d\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{E}_{h})}\|[\hskip-2.0pt[{\bm{\mu}}_{h}]\hskip-2.0pt]\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{E}_{h})}\|{\bm{\chi}}^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\partial\mathcal{K}_{h})}\lesssim h^{3}\|{\bm{\chi}}^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\partial\mathcal{K}_{h})},

where we used

\displaystyle\|d\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{E}_{h})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|d\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}\lesssim h^{2},

\displaystyle\|[\hskip-2.0pt[{\bm{\mu}}_{h}]\hskip-2.0pt]\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{E}_{h})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\partial K}^{+}-{\bm{\mu}}_{\partial K^{\ell}}^{+}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{E}_{h})}+\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\partial K}^{-}-{\bm{\mu}}_{\partial K^{\ell}}^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{E}_{h})}\lesssim h.

Thanks to (
3.17
), (
3.15
), and (
3.14
), we have

\displaystyle\|{\bm{\chi}}^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\partial\mathcal{K}_{h})}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-1}\|{\bm{\chi}}^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{F}_{h})}+\|\nabla{\bm{\chi}}^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{F}_{h})}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-2}\|{\bm{\chi}}^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}+h^{-1}\|\nabla{\bm{\chi}}^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}+\|\nabla^{2}{\bm{\chi}}^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-1}\|{\bm{\chi}}\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}+\|{\bm{\chi}}\|_{W^{1,1}(\Gamma)}+h\|{\bm{\chi}}\|_{W^{2,1}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-1}\|{\bm{\chi}}\|_{W^{2,1}(\Gamma)}.

Applying this estimate to (
5.7
)
yields
|I_{2}|\lesssim h^{2}\|{\bm{\chi}}\|_{W^{2,1}(\Gamma)}
.
Combining this inequality with (
5.5
)– (

)
yields (
5.3
), which completes the proof.
∎

Estimate
(

\displaystyle((\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi)^{e},(\Delta_{\Gamma}\phi)^{e}-\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}\phi^{e})_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}

(

(5.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&((\{\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi\})^{e},({\bm{\mu}}_{\ell}\cdot[\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi])^{e}-{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot[\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\phi^{e}])_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}\\

Estimate

I_{1}
: By (

), (

), (
3.14

\displaystyle\|(\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi)^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{h})}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-2}\|(\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi)^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}+h^{-1}\|\nabla(\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi)^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}+\|\nabla^{2}(\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi)^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-1}\|\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}+\|\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi\|_{W^{1,1}(\Gamma)}+h\|\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi\|_{W^{2,1}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-1}\|\psi\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)},

and

\displaystyle\|(\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi)^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{h})}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-2}\|(\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi)^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}+h^{-1}\|\nabla(\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi)^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}+\|\nabla^{2}(\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi)^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-1}\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}+\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi\|_{W^{1,1}(\Gamma)}+h\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi\|_{W^{2,1}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-1}\|\phi\|_{H^{3}(\Gamma)}.

Noting that (cf.
[
18
, (3.9)]
)

\|{\bf P}{\bm{\mu}}_{h}-{\bm{\mu}}_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial K)}\lesssim h^{2},

and so by using

{\bf P}({\bm{\mu}}_{\partial K}^{+}+{\bm{\mu}}_{\partial K}^{-})=({\bf P}{\bm{\mu}}_{\partial K}^{+}-{\bm{\mu}}_{\partial K^{\ell}}^{+})+({\bf P}{\bm{\mu}}_{\partial K}^{-}-{\bm{\mu}}_{\partial K^{\ell}}^{-}),

(
3.1
), and the boundedness of
{\bf H}
, it follows that

(5.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{1}&=((\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi)^{e},({\bm{\mu}}_{\partial K}^{+}+{\bm{\mu}}_{\partial K}^{-})\cdot(d{\bf H}(\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi)^{e}))_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}\\
&\lesssim h^{2}(\|{\bf P}{\bm{\mu}}_{\partial K}^{+}-{\bm{\mu}}_{\partial K^{\ell}}^{+}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{E}_{h})}+\|{\bf P}{\bm{\mu}}_{\partial K}^{-}-{\bm{\mu}}_{\partial K^{\ell}}^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{E}_{h})})\|(\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi)^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{h})}\|(\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi)^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{h})}\\
&\lesssim h^{2}\|\psi\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)}\|\phi\|_{H^{3}(\Gamma)}.\end{split}

Estimate

I_{2}
: Before estimating this term, we set
{\bm{\chi}}:=\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi
and observe that
{\bm{\chi}}
is tangent to
\Gamma
. Thus,
{\bm{\chi}}^{e}\cdot{\bm{n}}=0
in a neighborhood of
\Gamma
. Consequently, the product rule and the identity
{\bf H}=\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{n}}
lead to
0=\nabla({\bm{\chi}}^{e}\cdot{\bm{n}})=(\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{\chi}}^{e})^{\intercal}{\bm{n}}+{\bf H}{\bm{\chi}}^{e}
, which implies

(5.12)

\displaystyle(\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{\chi}}^{e})^{\intercal}{\bm{n}}=-{\bf H}{\bm{\chi}}^{e}.

Based on
[
15
, (C.33)–(C.42)]
,

(5.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{2}&=(({\rm div}_{\Gamma}{\bm{\chi}})^{e},1)_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}-d({\rm tr}({\bf Z}),1)_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}-({\bf P}{\bm{n}}_{h},(\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{\chi}}^{e})^{\intercal}{\bm{n}}_{h})_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}\\
&=:I_{2,1}+I_{2,2}+I_{2,3},\end{split}

where
{\bf Z}=(\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bm{\chi}}{\bf H})^{e}+(\nabla{\bf P}{\bm{\chi}}{\bf H})^{e}
, and
\nabla{\bf P}{\bm{\chi}}
is a
3\times 3
matrix with entries similar to (
3.7
). Since
II_{1},II_{2}
satisfy (cf.
[
15
, (C.43)–(C.46)]
)

(5.14)

\displaystyle I_{2,1}\lesssim h^{2}\|\psi\|_{H^{3}(\Gamma)}\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma)},\qquad I_{2,2}\lesssim h^{2}\|\psi\|_{H^{3}(\Gamma)}\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma)},

we just need to focus on the term
I_{2,3}
. By virtue of (
5.12
), Holder’s inequality, (
5.4
), (
3.16
), and (
3.14
),

\displaystyle I_{2,3}

\displaystyle=-({\bf P}{\bm{n}}_{h},(\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{\chi}}^{e})^{\intercal}({\bm{n}}_{h}-{\bm{n}}))_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}-({\bf P}{\bm{n}}_{h},(\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{\chi}}^{e})^{\intercal}{\bm{n}})_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}

\displaystyle=-({\bf P}{\bm{n}}_{h},(\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{\chi}}^{e})^{\intercal}({\bm{n}}_{h}-{\bm{n}}))_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}+({\bf P}{\bm{n}}_{h},{\bf H}{\bm{\chi}}^{e})_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\bf P}{\bm{n}}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}\|\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{\chi}}^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma_{h})}\|{\bm{n}}_{h}-{\bm{n}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}+h^{2}\|{\bf H}{\bm{\chi}}\|_{W^{2,1}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\|\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{\chi}}^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma_{h})}+h^{2}\|\psi\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)}\|\phi\|_{H^{3}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}(h^{-1}\|\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{\chi}}^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}+\|\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla^{2}{\bm{\chi}}^{e}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})})+h^{2}\|\psi\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)}\|\phi\|_{H^{3}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\|{\bm{\chi}}\|_{W^{1,1}(\Gamma)}+h^{3}\|{\bm{\chi}}\|_{W^{2,1}(\Gamma)}+h^{2}\|\psi\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)}\|\phi\|_{H^{3}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\|\psi\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)}\|\phi\|_{H^{3}(\Gamma)}.

Combining the above inequality with (
5.10
)–(
5.14
) completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

There holds for
v,w\in H^{4}(\Gamma)
,

(5.15)

\displaystyle|a_{h}^{\ell}(v,w)-a_{h}(v^{e},w^{e})|

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\|v\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)}\|w\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)}.

Proof.

All terms in
a_{h}^{\ell}(v,w)-a_{h}(v^{e},w^{e})
can be paired and rewritten as

\displaystyle|a_{h}^{\ell}(v,w)-a_{h}(v^{e},w^{e})|

\displaystyle\leq\left|(\Delta_{\Gamma}v,\Delta_{\Gamma}w)_{\mathcal{K}^{\ell}_{h}}-(\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v^{e},\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}w^{e})_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\left|(\{\Delta_{\Gamma}v\},{\bm{\mu}}_{\ell}\cdot[\nabla_{\Gamma}w])_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\ell}}-(\{\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v^{e}\},{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot[\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}w^{e}])_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\left|(\{\Delta_{\Gamma}w\},{\bm{\mu}}_{\ell}\cdot[\nabla_{\Gamma}v])_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\ell}}-(\{\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}w^{e}\},{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot[\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v^{e}])_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\sigma}{h}\left|({\bm{\mu}}_{\ell}\cdot[\nabla_{\Gamma}v],{\bm{\mu}}_{\ell}\cdot[\nabla_{\Gamma}w])_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\ell}}-({\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot[\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v^{e}],{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot[\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}w^{e}])_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}\right|

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}.

By a change of the integration domain, adding and subtracting terms, we can rewrite
I_{1}
as

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big|((\mu_{h}-1)(\Delta_{\Gamma}v)^{e},(\Delta_{\Gamma}w)^{e})_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big|((\Delta_{\Gamma}v)^{e}-\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v^{e},(\Delta_{\Gamma}w)^{e})_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}\Big|+\Big|((\Delta_{\Gamma}v)^{e},(\Delta_{\Gamma}w)^{e}-\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}w^{e})_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big|-((\Delta_{\Gamma}v)^{e}-\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v^{e},(\Delta_{\Gamma}w)^{e}-\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}w^{e})_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}\Big|.

We then use
|\mu_{h}-1|=\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
, Lemma
5.5
, Lemma
4.9
, and Lemma
4.8
to obtain

I_{1}\lesssim h^{2}\|v\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)}\|w\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)}.

Note that the Sobolev embedding
H^{3}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow C^{1}(\Gamma)
implies
({\bm{\mu}}_{\ell}\cdot[\nabla_{\Gamma}v])^{e}=0
, and therefore the estimates for
I_{2},I_{3}
, and
I_{4}
follow similar arguments.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\phi\in H^{4}(\Gamma)
and let
\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e}\in V_{h,0}
be its interpolant given in Lemma
5.1
. Then there holds

(5.16)

\big|a_{h}(u_{h},\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})-a_{h}^{\ell}(u_{h}^{\ell},(\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})^{\ell})\big|\lesssim\big(h^{2}\|f_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}+h\|f-F_{h}^{\ell}\|_{\Gamma}+h^{2}\|u\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)}\big)\|\phi\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)}.

Proof.

Adding and subtracting terms yield

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h},\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})-a_{h}^{\ell}(u_{h}^{\ell},(\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})^{\ell})

\displaystyle=\big(a_{h}(u_{h},\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e}-\phi^{e})-a_{h}^{\ell}(u_{h}^{\ell},(\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})^{\ell}-\phi)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\big(a_{h}(u_{h}-u^{e},\phi^{e})-a_{h}^{\ell}(u_{h}^{\ell}-u,\phi)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\big(a_{h}(u^{e},\phi^{e})-a_{h}^{\ell}(u,\phi)\big)

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

Note that by the coercivity of the bilinear form
A_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)

and Lemma
4.7
, there holds
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2h}\lesssim\|u_{h}\|_{h}\lesssim\|f_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}
.
It follows from (
4.26
), Lemmas
4.7
–
4.8
, Lemma
5.1
, Corollary
5.3

and (
5.15
) that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim h{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}^{\ell}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*,2h}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})^{\ell}-\phi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*,2h}\lesssim h{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2h}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e}-\phi^{e}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2h}\lesssim h^{2}\|f_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}\|\phi\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)},

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}^{\ell}-u\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*,2h}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*,2h}\lesssim h{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}-u^{e}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2h}\|\phi\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)}\lesssim(h\|f-F_{h}^{\ell}\|_{\Gamma}+h^{2}\|u\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)})\|\phi\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)},

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\|u\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)}\|\phi\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)},

which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
u
be the exact solution, and let
u_{h}\in V_{h,0}
solve the
C^{0}
IP method (
4.6
). If
u\in H^{4}(\Gamma)
, then there holds

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}^{\ell}\|_{\Gamma}\lesssim\|f-F^{\ell}_{h}\|_{\Gamma}+h^{2}\|u\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)}+h^{2}\|f_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}.

Proof.

To prove the estimate we apply a duality argument. For
\psi\in C^{0}(\Gamma)
with
\int_{\Gamma}\psi=0
, let
\phi\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
satisfy
\Delta^{2}_{\Gamma}\phi=\psi
and
\int_{\Gamma}\phi=0
. By elliptic regularity,
\phi\in H^{4}(\Gamma)
with

(5.17)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi\|_{\Gamma}.

Furthermore, by consistency of the
C^{0}
IP method,

(5.18)

\displaystyle a_{h}^{\ell}(v,\phi)=(v,\psi)_{\Gamma}\qquad\forall v\in W^{\ell}

We decompose the error as

(5.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}u-u_{h}^{\ell}&=\Bigg(u-\left(u_{h}^{\ell}-\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\int_{\Gamma}u_{h}^{\ell}\right)\Bigg)+\Bigg(\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{h}|}\int_{\Gamma_{h}}u_{h}-\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\int_{\Gamma}u_{h}^{\ell}\Bigg)\\
&=:I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

Consider
v=\psi=I_{1}
in (
5.18
) and note that
\int_{\Gamma}u_{h}^{\ell}/|\Gamma|
is a constant. Therefore,

(5.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|I_{1}\|_{\Gamma}^{2}&=a_{h}^{\ell}(I_{1},\phi)\\
&=a_{h}^{\ell}(u-u_{h}^{\ell},\phi-(\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})^{\ell})+a_{h}^{\ell}(u-u_{h}^{\ell},(\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})^{\ell})\\
&=:I_{1,1}+I_{1,2}.\end{split}

Due to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, Lemma
4.8
,
Lemma
5.1
, and Corollary
5.3
,

(5.21)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{1,1}&\lesssim{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u-u_{h}^{\ell}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*,2h}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi-(\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})^{\ell}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*,2h}\\
&\lesssim{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u^{e}-u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2h}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi^{e}-\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2h}\\
&\lesssim(h\|f-F_{h}^{\ell}\|_{\Gamma}+h^{2}\|u\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)})\|I_{1}\|_{\Gamma}.\end{split}

By adding and subtracting terms, we rewrite
I_{1,2}
as

(5.22)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{1,2}&=a_{h}^{\ell}(u,(\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})^{\ell})-a_{h}^{\ell}(u_{h}^{\ell},(\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})^{\ell})\\
&=l((\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})^{\ell})-a_{h}^{\ell}(u_{h}^{\ell},(\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})^{\ell})\\
&=l((\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})^{\ell})-l_{h}(\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})+a_{h}(u_{h},\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})-a_{h}^{\ell}(u_{h}^{\ell},(\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})^{\ell})\\
&\qquad+s_{h}(u_{h},\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})\\
&=\Big(l((\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})^{\ell})-l_{h}(\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})\Big)+\Big(a_{h}(u_{h},\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})-a_{h}^{\ell}(u_{h}^{\ell},(\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e})^{\ell})\Big)\\
&\qquad+s_{h}(u_{h}-u^{e},\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e}-\phi^{e})\\
&=:I_{1,2,1}+I_{1,2,2}+I_{1,2,3}.\end{split}

It follows from (
4.27
), (
5.1
), and (
5.17
) that

(5.23)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{1,2,1}\lesssim\|f-F_{h}^{\ell}\|_{\Gamma}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{h}\lesssim\|f-F_{h}^{\ell}\|_{\Gamma}\|\phi\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\|f-F_{h}^{\ell}\|_{\Gamma}\|I_{1}\|_{\Gamma}.\end{split}

Likewise, by (
5.16
) and (
5.17
), we have

(5.24)

\displaystyle I_{1,2,2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(h\|f-F_{h}^{\ell}\|_{\Gamma}+h^{2}\|u\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)})\|I_{1}\|_{\Gamma}.

Thanks to Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
5.2
), (
5.1
), and (
5.17
),

\displaystyle I_{1,2,3}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}-u^{e}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{h}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathring{\pi}_{h}\phi^{e}-\phi^{e}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{h}\lesssim(h\|f-F_{h}^{\ell}\|_{\Gamma}+h^{2}\|u\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)})\|I_{1}\|_{\Gamma}.

Combining (
5.22
)–(
5.24
) leads to

(5.25)

\displaystyle I_{1,2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\|f-F_{h}^{\ell}\|_{\Gamma}+h^{2}\|u\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)})\|I_{1}\|_{\Gamma},

and so by (
5.20
), (
5.21
), and (
5.25
),
we have

(5.26)

\displaystyle\|I_{1}\|_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f-F^{\ell}_{h}\|_{\Gamma}+h^{2}\|u\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)}.

Next, the coercivity of
A_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
(
4.16
),
the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and (
4.19
)
yield
\|u_{h}\|_{h}\lesssim\|f_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}
.
Therefore by
[
4
, (6.39)]
and (
4.19
), we have

(5.27)

\displaystyle\|I_{2}\|_{\Gamma}\lesssim\left|\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{h}|}\int_{\Gamma_{h}}u_{h}-\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\int_{\Gamma}u_{h}^{\ell}\right|\lesssim h^{2}\|u_{h}\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}\lesssim h^{2}\|u_{h}\|_{h}\lesssim h^{2}\|f_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}.

Based on (
5.19
), (
5.26
), and (
5.27
), we conclude that

\|u-u_{h}^{\ell}\|_{\Gamma}\lesssim\|f-F^{\ell}_{h}\|_{\Gamma}+h^{2}\|u\|_{H^{4}(\Gamma)}+h^{2}\|f_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}.

∎

6.
Numerical Examples

In this section, we perform
some simple numerical examples to gauge
the theoretical results and determine whether the derived
error estimates are sharp. In the set of numerical examples,
we take
\Gamma
to be the unit sphere, and take the data
f

such that the exact solution is given by
u=\exp(x+y^{2})\cos(z^{3})
.
The resulting expression for
f
is well-defined on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
and we use this expression in the form
l_{h}(\cdot)
.
In this case, simple arguments show
\|f-F_{h}^{\ell}\|_{\Gamma}\lesssim h^{2}
.
The set of numerical experiments are implemented using
Netgen/NGsolve
[
24
]
with the add-on ngsxfem
[
16
]
, which supports unfitted
finite element discretizations.

In addition, to the
C^{0}
IP method (
4.6
),
we solve the method with different
bilinear forms

to determine the minimal about of TraceFEM stabilizations
to ensure well-posedness and convergence of the scheme.
In particular, we consider three
C^{0}
IP methods: Find

such that

(6.1)

\displaystyle A^{(j)}_{h}(u_{h},v)

\displaystyle:=a_{h}(u_{h},w)+s_{h}^{(j)}(u_{h},v)=l_{h}(v)\qquad\forall v\in V_{h,0},\quad j=0,1,2,

),

) (so that the case

4.6
)), and

\displaystyle s_{h}^{(1)}(v,w)

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{h^{2}}([\nabla v],[\nabla w])_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}+\gamma([\nabla^{2}v],[\nabla^{2}w])_{\mathcal{F}_{h}},

(6.3)

\displaystyle s_{h}^{(2)}(v,w)

\displaystyle=\gamma([\nabla^{2}v],[\nabla^{2}w])_{\mathcal{F}_{h}},

, and Laplace-Beltrami

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
4.7

Step 1:

Let
{\bm{c}}=\lambda_{\Gamma_{h}}(\nabla v)
and write

\displaystyle\|\nabla v\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim\|\nabla v-{\bm{c}}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+\|{\bm{c}}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}.

Applying the same arguments as
in the proof of Lemma
4.3
(also see
[
4
, Lemma 5.3]
) yields

\displaystyle\|{\bm{c}}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h\|{\bm{c}}\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim h\|{\bm{P}_{h}}{\bm{c}}\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim h\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+h\|{\bm{P}_{h}}{\bm{c}}-\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim h\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+\|{\bm{c}}-\nabla v\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}.

Thus we find, using (
4.7
) and the inverse inequality (
3.20
),

(A.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\nabla v\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}&\lesssim\|\nabla v-{\bm{c}}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+h\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim h\|\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\nabla v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+h^{2}\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+h^{-1}\|[\nabla v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}+h\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+h^{-1}\|[\nabla v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}+h\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}.\end{split}

We then conclude from (
4.8
) that

(A.2)

\displaystyle h\|\nabla v\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+\|[\nabla^{2}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}+\|[\nabla v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}+h^{2}\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim\|v\|_{h}^{2}+h^{2}\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}.

Step 2:

We apply (
3.20
), (
4.7
), and (
A.2
) to obtain

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+h\|v\|_{\partial\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim h^{-1}\|v\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+h\|\nabla v\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim\|v\|_{h}^{2}+\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2},

where we used the continuity of
v
on
\Omega_{h}
.

Step 3:

We extend
{\bm{n}}_{h}
by constant on each element
so that for each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
{\bf P}_{h}
is constant and
\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v
is well-defined and is a piecewise polynomial with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. By the inverse equalities (
3.21
), (
3.19
), the large intersection covering property, and (
3.22
), we have

(A.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}h\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\partial\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}&\lesssim h^{-1}\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim h^{-1}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{h}}\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h,x}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim h^{-1}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{h}}\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{T_{x}}^{2}+\|[\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}+h^{2}\|[\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla(\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v){\bm{\nu}}_{F}]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}\\
&=:I+II+III.\end{split}

We will estimate each term separately.

Estimate I:
Let
{\bm{g}}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}^{dc}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
satisfy
{\bm{g}}|_{T}=\frac{1}{|T|}\int_{T}\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. A standard Poincare inequality yields

\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v-{\bm{g}}\|_{T}\lesssim h\|\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla(\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v)\|_{T},

and moreover, the inverse inequality (
3.20
) implies

\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v-{\bm{g}}\|_{K}^{2}\lesssim h\|\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla(\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v)\|_{T}^{2}.

Using these results with the large intersection covering property, we find

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-1}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{h}}(\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v-{\bm{g}}\|_{T_{x}}^{2}+\|{\bm{g}}\|_{T_{x}}^{2})\lesssim h^{-1}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{h}}(\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v-{\bm{g}}\|_{T_{x}}^{2}+h\|{\bm{g}}\|_{K_{x}}^{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-1}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{h}}(\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v-{\bm{g}}\|_{T_{x}}^{2}+h\|{\bm{g}}-\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{K_{x}}^{2}+h\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{K_{x}}^{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim h\|\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla(\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v)\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}.

Based on
[
18
, (2.5)]
and notice that
{\bm{n}}_{h}
is piecewise constant, we have

(A.4)

\displaystyle\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla(\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v)

\displaystyle={\bf P}_{h}\nabla^{2}v.

Therefore, the boundedness of
{\bf P}_{h}
and (
4.8
) lead to

(A.5)

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+\|[\nabla^{2}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}+\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim\|v\|_{h}^{2}+\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}.

Estimate II:
It follows from the similar arguments as in the proof of (
4.14
) as well as (
A.2
) that

(A.6)

\begin{split}II&\lesssim\|[{\bf P}_{h}]\{\nabla v\}\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}+\|\{{\bf P}_{h}\}[\nabla v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\|[{\bf P}_{h}-{\bf P}]\{\nabla v\}\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}+\|[\nabla v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim h\|\nabla v\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+\|[\nabla v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim\|v\|_{h}^{2}+h^{2}\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}.\end{split}

Estimate III:
Using (
A.4
), (
4.14
) and its proof again, also with (
4.8
), we have

(A.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}III&=h^{2}\|[{\bf P}_{h}\nabla^{2}v{\bm{\nu}}_{F}]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim h^{2}(\|[{\bf P}_{h}\nabla^{2}v]\{{\bm{\nu}}_{F}\}\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}+\|\{{\bf P}_{h}\nabla^{2}v\}[{\bm{\nu}}_{F}]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2})\\
&\lesssim h^{2}\|[{\bf P}_{h}\nabla^{2}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}+h^{2}\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim h^{3}\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+h^{2}\|[\nabla^{2}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}+h\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim h^{2}\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+\|[\nabla^{2}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim\|v\|_{h}^{2}.\end{split}

Combining (
A.3
), (
A.5
), (
A.6
), and (
A.7
) yields

(A.8)

h\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\partial\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim\|v\|_{h}^{2}+\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}.

Step 4:

Combining Steps 1–3, we have shown

(A.9)

\displaystyle h\|\nabla v\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+\|v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+h\|v\|_{\partial\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+h\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\partial\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim\|v\|_{h}^{2}+\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}.

We integrate by parts, apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and
the estimate (
A.9
) to obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-(\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v,v)_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}+([\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v]\cdot{\bm{\mu}}_{h},v)_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{h}\big(\|v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}+h^{1/2}\|v\|_{\partial\mathcal{K}_{h}}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{h}^{2}+\|v\|_{h}\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}.

Thus, we have

(A.10)

\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim\|v\|_{h}^{2}.

Step 5:

We use (
3.19
)–(
3.21
) and (
4.8
):

(A.11)

h^{2}\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+h^{3}\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2}v\|_{\partial\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim h\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim h^{2}\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{h}}v\|_{\mathcal{K}_{h}}^{2}+\|[\nabla^{2}v]\|_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim\|v\|_{h}^{2}.

Finally, we combine (
A.9
)–(
A.11
) to complete the proof.

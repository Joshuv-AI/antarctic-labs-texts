Unsupervised cycle-consistent network for 3D pelvic mono- or multimodal deformable image registration
Authors: Xin Zhang, Cong Zhang, Hengxin Du, Yankui Chang, Haotian Wu, Lipeng Jiang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTo develop a novel deformable image registration method aimed at improving the accuracy of multimodal image registration in radiotherapy.MethodsAn unsupervised cycle-consistent network (CycleVOS) was proposed as the deformable image registration model, which integrates a cycle-consistent registration framework, a voxel optimization smooth (VOS) module, and a modality-independent neighbourhood descriptor (MIND) loss function. Two convolutional neural networks (CNNs) were employed to generate spatial deformable grids, ensuring consistency in the deformed images. In this study, the CycleVOS model was evaluated on multiple registration tasks, i.e., CT-CT registration, CT-CBCT registration and CT-MR registration tasks. The datasets consisted of 128 paired CT-MR images, 90 paired CT-CT images and 190 paired CT-CBCT images. Among these, 28 CT-MR, 25 CT-CT, and 40 CT-CBCT pairs were used as test data. The performance of the CycleVOS model was compared with that of the Elastix B-spline method using normalized mutual information (NMI) and the dice similarity coefficient (DSC) as evaluation metrics.ResultsIn terms of the test data, the NMI values obtained with the CycleVOS model and the B-spline method were 0.211 vs. 0.205 for CT-MR images, 0.508 vs. 0.485 for CT-CT images, and 0.404 vs. 0.379 for CT-CBCT images. The DSCs for the CycleVOS model were consistently greater than those for the B-spline method across all propagated contours in CT-CT data (bladder, bone marrow, body, CTV, and PTV) and CT-CBCT data (bladder, spinal cord, left femoral head, right femoral head, and bone marrow). Furthermore, compared with the B-spline method, the CycleVOS model trained on mixed data achieved comparable or superior accuracy.ConclusionsThe proposed CycleVOS model is highly accurate for both mono- and multimodal image registration. Its performance when trained on mixed data suggests that integrating various registration tasks into a single model is a feasible direction for future development.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.

Introduction

Image registration is a crucial task in medical image processing and is aimed at aligning anatomical structures between fixed and moving images [1, 2]. In radiotherapy, it plays an essential role in various clinical applications across both monomodal and multimodal imaging scenarios, such as image fusion, contour propagation, and dose accumulation [3–5]. For instance, effective registration of paired computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance (MR) images can allow the complementary advantages of both modalities to be leveraged, assisting physicians in disease diagnosis and enabling more accurate delineation of clinical target volumes (CTVs) [6, 7]. Similarly, CT and cone-beam CT (CBCT) image registration can be used to propagate the contours from plan CT images to CBCT images, and deformed CT images can be used for dose calculation [8]. The generated deformation vector field (DVF) can also be used for dose accumulation among different fractions during adaptive radiotherapy (ART). Monomodal image registration, such as CT-CT image registration, is mainly used for autosegmentation of targets and organs at risk (OARs), and the core technology of the atlas-based segmentation method is rigid and deformable image registration algorithms [9, 10]. In the field of image-guided radiotherapy, the registration algorithm is an indispensable vital component of the clinical process.

Traditional registration methods typically involve iterative optimization to estimate the DVF and can be categorized into surface-based, point-based, and voxel-based approaches [11]. While well-grounded in theory and highly interpretable, these methods often suffer from prolonged computation times. In recent years, deep learning (DL) has been increasingly applied for medical image analysis [12–15], offering a promising pathway to enhance both the speed and accuracy of image registration [16]. Numerous studies have demonstrated that DL-based registration can match or even surpass conventional techniques in terms of accuracy while significantly accelerating the registration process [17, 18]. Current deep learning registration strategies can be broadly divided into supervised and unsupervised paradigms [19, 20]. Supervised methods rely on ground-truth transformations generated by traditional algorithms, which not only are time-consuming but also inherently limit the model’s accuracy to that of the reference method. In contrast, unsupervised learning eliminates the need for preregistered data or transformation labels. However, many early unsupervised approaches overlooked the inverse consistency property inherent in image transformations [21–24]. Cycle-consistent registration methods address this issue by enforcing a cycle constraint that encourages the deformed image to revert to its original form when it is transformed back [17, 25]. Originally introduced in CycleGAN for unpaired image translation [26], the cycle-consistency concept has been effectively adapted to medical image registration. For example, Tanner et al. [27] applied it to deformable registration between MR and CT images, whereas Kim et al. [25] developed a cycle-consistent network for multiphase liver CT image registration. Kuang et al. [28] further incorporated cycle-consistent training to reduce negative Jacobian determinants in deep registration networks, demonstrating superior performance compared to earlier frameworks such as VoxelMorph [16]. These advances confirm the effectiveness of the cycle-consistent strategy for unsupervised deformable registration.

Nevertheless, multimodal registration presents unique challenges. First, monomodal similarity metrics such as the mean squared error (MSE) are unsuitable, and while mutual information (MI) is applicable, its computational complexity can be prohibitive. The modality-independent neighbourhood descriptor (MIND) has thus been widely adopted as a loss function in such scenarios [17, 18, 29]. Second, image artefacts and low soft-tissue contrast in modalities such as CBCT can lead to implausible DVFs with folding or excessive local variations, ultimately compromising the accuracy of image fusion and contour propagation. Although incorporating spatial gradients of the DVF into the loss function can partially regularize the deformation field, this issue persists in many practical settings. Some researchers have adopted various approaches to mitigate the effects of modality differences. For instance, Huang et al. [30] utilized a pretrained segmentation model to guide ROI-based registration, Boussot et al. [31] designed a generic semantic loss, and Han and Hémon employed synthetic CT generation to bridge modality gaps [32, 33].

In this study, we propose an unsupervised cycle-consistent deformable image registration network that integrates voxel-level optimization smoothing and a MIND-based loss function, eliminating the need for ground-truth transformations. Our model is trained and evaluated using a mixed dataset comprising paired CT-CT, CT-MR, and CT-CBCT images, and its performance across diverse monomodal and multimodal registration tasks in radiotherapy is assessed.

Methods

Datasets

The dataset used in this study consisted of 128 paired CT-MR scans, 90 paired CT-CT scans, and 190 paired CT-CBCT scans. All the data were collected retrospectively with patient informed consent.

The CT-MR dataset included data from 56 cervical, 38 rectal, and 34 prostate cancer patients treated at our hospital between January 2024 and October 2024. Both CT and MR images were acquired using SIEMENS scanners, and paired images were acquired within a 3-day interval. MR imaging was performed to assist physicians in delineating clinical target volumes on the basis of CT images. The in-plane resolution of the CT images was 0.977 mm × 0.977 mm, and the slice thickness was 5 mm. MR images exhibited in-plane resolutions ranging from 0.29 mm × 0.29 mm to 1.188 mm × 1.188 mm, with slice thicknesses between 3 mm and 6 mm. The typical longitudinal coverage of MR images was approximately 170 mm.

The CT-CBCT dataset included data from cervical cancer patients treated on Halcyon machines from June 2021 to October 2021. CT images were acquired using a Philips Brilliance™ Big Bore CT scanner with a bore diameter of 85 cm, featuring in-plane resolutions from 0.962 mm × 0.962 mm to 1.365 mm × 1.365 mm and a slice thickness of 5 mm. CBCT images were obtained from a Halcyon 2.0 system (Varian, USA), with in-plane resolutions between 0.908 mm × 0.908 mm and 1.035 mm × 1.035 mm and a slice thickness of 2 mm. The CBCT scanning range covered approximately 240 mm. CBCT was performed when the patient underwent radiotherapy for the first time normally, and the time interval between paired CT-CBCT images was approximately 3–5 days.

The CT-CT dataset included data from cervical cancer patients who received two-course intensity-modulated radiotherapy (IMRT) between July 2021 and November 2021. Following clinical protocols, the second course of radiotherapy was delivered after 20 fractions, which required a repeat CT scan for re-delineation of target volumes and organs at risk; the time interval between paired CT-CT images was approximately 30 days.

Registration framework and network

The proposed unsupervised cycle-consistent network (CycleVOS) is shown in Fig. 1 and included two convolutional neural networks (CNNs): GA−B and GB−A. The GA−B network was defined to generate a spatial field from image modal A to image modal B, whereas the GB−A network was the opposite. Taking the deformation of image A as an example, the multimodal images (A and B) were first input into the CNN-based registration network (GA−B) to obtain the 3D spatial grid for deformed image (A) It needs to be emphasized that deformed image A had the same mode as image A and was more similar to image (B) Therefore, deformed image A and deformed image B were input into the GA−B network again to obtain reconstructed image A. To guarantee topology preservation between the deformed and fixed images, we added a cycle consistency constraint between the original image and the reconstructed image, such as image A and reconstructed image A.

GA−B and GB−A used the same network structure, which is shown in Fig. 2. The paired image A and image B were the combined inputs to the model, and the output of the model was a deformation field vector with three channels. The network consisted of two downsampling layers and two upsampling layers. Nine ResNet blocks [34] were used to increase the depth of the network and make the model easier to optimize.

Structure of the CNN-based registration network

In this study, the voxel optimization smooth (VOS) method was first proposed to maintain the smooth deformation field. In a previous study [35], a reparametrization trick was attempted, and an “integration layer” was inserted to produce smoother deformation, but model training was difficult. We employed the VOS to eliminate excessive local changes. As illustrated in Fig. 1, the spatial grid used here had dimensions of 360 × 280 × 40 × 3 voxels, with the last channel representing the X, Y, and Z offsets. First, the 360 × 280 × 40 matrix was partitioned into 90 × 70 × 20 segments, each containing 4 × 4 × 2 × 3 voxels. The offsets within each segment were averaged, resulting in a deformation field of size 90 × 70 × 20 × 3 voxels. This field was then upsampled back to the original resolution of 360 × 280 × 40 × 3 voxels to produce the final smooth deformation field.

Data preprocessing

In this study, image registration was preceded by a series of preprocessing steps, including rigid alignment, resampling, cropping, and intensity normalization. First, rigid registration was performed using the ITK framework [36, 37]. For all three datasets (CT-MR, CT-CT, and CT-CBCT), the CT image was designated as the fixed image. All the images were subsequently resampled to a uniform resolution of 1 × 1 × 5 mm³. The images were then cropped by extracting 360 × 280 voxels per slice around the centroid of the skin surface as the image centre. Owing to variations in the slice lengths of the CT, MR, and CBCT images, cropping along the head-foot direction was adapted for each dataset. For the CT-MR dataset, the original MR image had fewer than 40 slices after resampling to a thickness of 5 mm. Therefore, the cropped CT-MR images were further resampled to a consistent size of 360 × 280 × 40 voxels. For the CT-CT dataset, a 360 × 280 × 40 voxel volume was cropped centred on the clinical target volume (CTV). For the CT-CBCT dataset, cropping was performed centred on the CBCT image to obtain a volume of 360 × 280 × 40 voxels. Finally, intensity normalization was applied. CT and CBCT images underwent threshold clipping ([-200, 300] HU) before being normalized to the range [0, 1]. MR images were directly normalized to [0, 1] without thresholding.

Training details

As illustrated in Fig. 1, the cycle consistency constraint was employed in the training of our proposed CycleVOS model, which was realized by a loss function. The loss function consisted of three parts: content loss (Lcont), regularization loss (Lreg) and cycle loss (Lcyc).

The modality-independent neighbourhood descriptor (MIND) was used as the Lcont for the mono- and multimodal registration tasks, and previous studies have validated the accuracy of the MIND loss function [17, 18, 29]. L2 regularization was used as Lreg to smooth the deformation field and prevent unreasonable deformation [38]. The cycle loss enabled the deformed image to be deformed back to the original image. Results

This study implemented rigid and B-spline registration methods based on the ITK framework [39, 40] for comparison with the proposed CycleVOS model. The B-spline registration parameters were configured as follows: the interpolator was set to “BSplineInterpolator”, the optimizer to “AdaptiveStochasticGradientDescent”, the transform to “BSplineTransform”, and the metric to “AdvancedMattesMutualInformation”, with a maximum of 5000 iterations. A multiresolution pyramid strategy was employed across four levels, using an optimization grid resolution of 16.

On the basis of the CycleVOS framework, we trained several models: modelCT−MR using 100 paired CT-MR images, modelCT−CT using 65 paired CT-CT images, modelCT−CBCT using 150 paired CT-CBCT images, modelCT−CT_and_CT−CBCT using both 65 paired CT-CT and 150 paired CT-CBCT images, and modelHybrid incorporating 100 paired CT-MR, 65 paired CT-CT, and 150 paired CT-CBCT images. The training data for modelHybrid_without_VOS was the same as that for modelHybrid, but modelHybrid_without_VOS lacked the VOS module.

The test data included 28 paired CT-MR images, 25 paired CT-CT images, and 40 paired CT-CBCT images. The CT-CT images contained contours of the bladder, bone marrow, body, clinical target volume (CTV) and planning target volume (PTV). The CT-CBCT images contained contours of the bladder, spinal cord, left femoral head, right femoral head and bone marrow. The CT-MR images did not contain contours of any structures.

The NMI values of the different registration methods for the CT-MR, CT-CT and CT-CBCT images are shown in Table 1, and the visualized registration results are shown in Fig. 3. The proposed CycleVOS model achieved better results than the B-spline registration methods did; the NMI values of modelCT−MR and the B-spline registration method were 0.211 and 0.205, respectively, for the CT-MR data; the NMI values of modelCT−CT and the B-spline registration method were 0.508 and 0.485, respectively, for the CT-CT data; and the NMI values of modelCT−CBCT and the B-spline registration method were 0.404 and 0.379, respectively, for the CT-CBCT data. The accuracy of modelHybrid on a certain dataset was slightly decreased compared with that of the model trained with single data, but modelHybrid achieved the best performance on the basis of the analysis of all test data.

Discussion

Traditional iterative optimization-based registration algorithms have been widely adopted in both mono- and multimodal image registration and have been incorporated into several mature open-source tools [36, 39]. However, their iterative nature leads to long computation times. With the advancement of deep learning (DL), image registration techniques have progressed rapidly, especially in the domain of deformable image registration. For instance, Cao et al. [41] applied a CNN model to register 3D MR brain images and achieved higher accuracy than SyN [42] and Demons [43]. Duan et al. [44] proposed a patch-based unsupervised registration model for chest CT and CBCT images, which achieved registration within seconds and outperformed the Demons and B-spline methods. These developments underscore the growing role of DL-based approaches in image registration.

Deformable image registration places high demands on both accuracy and efficiency. Traditional iterative methods exhibit strong generalization but are time-consuming. In contrast, DL-based registration offers fast inference and high accuracy in specific tasks, although it often relies heavily on data and suffers from limited generalizability. In this work, we propose an unsupervised cycle-consistent network (CycleVOS) for 3D pelvic mono- and multimodal deformable image registration. The experimental results demonstrate that compared with the B-spline method, the CycleVOS model achieves higher accuracy in less than one second. Furthermore, when trained on mixed datasets containing CT-MR, CT-CT, and CT-CBCT image pairs, the CycleVOS model attained comparable or superior accuracy to the B-spline approach. This performance can be attributed to the cycle-consistent registration framework, the voxel optimization smoothing (VOS) function, and MIND loss. The cycle-consistent constraint helps preserve topology in deformable registration [25], whereas VOS mitigates excessive local deformation and reduces image blur. MIND loss emphasizes structural similarity, thereby diminishing the influence of intermodal differences.

As shown in Table 1, CT-CT image registration was less challenging than CT-MR and CT-CBCT image registration were. Although MIND was originally introduced for multimodal registration, it also proved effective in monomodal scenarios, indirectly indicating that monomodal registration is generally less difficult than multimodal registration is.

Registration accuracy is also influenced by anatomical differences between moving and fixed images. Yang et al. [18] demonstrated that differences in bladder volume between planning CT and CBCT images correlate with registration accuracy; when bladder volume doubled, the dice similarity coefficient (DSC) decreased from 0.874 to 0.587. In our study, the time intervals for CT-CT and CT-CBCT images were approximately 30 days and 3–5 days, respectively. The rigid registration results in Tables 2 and 3 show that for CT-CT and CT-CBCT images, the DSCs were 0.712 and 0.738 for the bladder and 0.607 and 0.796 for the bone marrow, respectively.

Despite these promising results, our study has several limitations. First, when trained on mixed datasets, the accuracy of the CycleVOS model decreased compared with training on a single data type, particularly for CT-MR image pairs. The development of more effective normalization strategies may improve structural similarity in such cases. Second, the current model was validated only on pelvic datasets; extending it to other anatomical regions represents an important direction for future work.

Conclusions

In this study, we proposed an unsupervised cycle-consistent network (CycleVOS) for 3D pelvic mono- or multimodal deformable image registration and applied the CycleVOS model for mono- and multimodal image registration. The results showed that our proposed CycleVOS model had higher accuracy than the B-spline registration method did. The accuracy of the model trained with mixed data was comparable or superior to that of the B-spline registration method, indicating that integrating the registration algorithm into one model is feasible in the future.

Abstract
The crystal structure of Rb 2 ​ Ru 2 ​ O 7 ⋅ H 2 ​ O \text{Rb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{Ru}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{7}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{7}}\text{}}\!\,{\cdot}\,\!\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O} was determined by three-dimensional electron diffraction from the individual crystallites of a solid-state powder product. Rb 2 ​ Ru 2 ​ O 7 ⋅ H 2 ​ O \text{Rb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{Ru}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{7}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{7}}\text{}}\!\,{\cdot}\,\!\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O} crystallizes in space group C 2/ c ( a = 7.841 ​ ( 3 ) a=7.841(3) Å, b = 12.500 ​ ( 3 ) b=12.500(3) Å, c = 8.392 ​ ( 2 ) c=8.392(2) Å, β = 93.57 ​ ( 4 ) ∘ \beta=93.57(4)^{\circ} , Z=4). The structure contains infinite chains that run normal to the (101) plane and consist of alternating RuO 6 \text{RuO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{6}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{6}}\text{}} octahedra and square-pyramidal RuO 5 \text{RuO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{5}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{5}}\text{}} units connected via shared O–O edges. Magnetic properties were measured on the bulk powder, showing a diamagnetic baseline from 300 to 60 K with a small Curie tail below 55 K. The magnetic moment, calculated from the 1.8 K isotherm, saturates at M = 4.4 × 10 − 3 ​ μ B ​ Ru − 1 M=4.4\times 10^{-3}\,\mu_{\mathrm{B}}\,\mathrm{Ru}^{-1} , much less than would be expected for S = 1 S=1 ruthenium. Bond-valence-sum analysis indicates high-valent Ru, and the near-diamagnetic response is consistent with the edge-sharing Ru–Ru motif, where weak direct Ru–Ru overlap yields a local singlet.

1
Synthetic Procedure

Rubidium carbonate (0.347g, 1.5 mmol) and ruthenium oxide (IV) (0.100g, 0.75 mmol) were ground together with a mortar and pestle until a homogeneous powder with uniform color was obtained. The boiling point of rubidium carbonate is lower than the temperature at which the product is synthesized. For this reason, an excess of rubidium carbonate was used in the synthesis. Synthesis was carried out in an alumina crucible in a box furnace at 1000
∘
C, for 4 hours at ramp rate 5
∘
C min
-1
. The product was as a dark brown powder obtained after air-cooling in the furnace.

2
Crystallographic Details

For the 3D ED experiment, the sample was dispersed dry as received onto a copper-supported holey amorphous carbon TEM grid and loaded at 100 K via a high-tilt Gatan Elsa specimen holder into a Rigaku XtaLAB Synergy-ED electron diffractometer, operated at 200 kV and equipped with a Rigaku HyPix-ED hybrid pixel array area detector. Data were collected on various crystallites as single-rotation scans collecting 0.25
∘
frames using CrysAlisPRO system (CCD 1.171.43.129a 64-bit (release 29-06-2024))
3DED_1
using continuous rotation electron diffraction with a selected area aperture of 2
\mu
m apparent diameter. Further experimental details are provided in Table
2
.
All single crystal component datasets were individually indexed and integrated, prior to the merging of three suitable datasets for scaling, using CrysAlisPRO (version 1.171.44.113a)
3DED_1
; no absorption corrections or outlier rejections were applied. The structure was solved using ShelXT
Sheldrick_2015
and initially refined using Olex2.refine in the kinematic approximation, applying an extinction correction to broadly account for multiple scattering as implemented in Olex2 (version 1.5-ac7-016, compiled 2025.05.29 svn.r8cd99b3d for Rigaku Oxford Diffraction, GUI svn.r7254)
Bourhis_2015
;
Dolomanov_2009

Saha_2022

The resulting atomic coordinates were used as the starting point for a full dynamical refinement against the two highest quality data collections which were individually reduced and scaled without outlier rejection in CrysAlisPRO (version 1.171.44.128a)
3DED_1

All non-H atoms are refined anisotropically and the proton, earlier located in the residual difference map, was refined isotropically, all without the application of any restraints.

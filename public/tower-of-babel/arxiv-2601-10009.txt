Möbius-Type Structures in Non-Orientable Singular Semi-Riemannian Manifolds
Nathalie E. Rieger

Abstract
. Our objective is to illuminate the global structure of non-orientable manifolds with signature-changing metrics. Using explicit constructions based on the topology of the Möbius strip, we produce examples of crosscap manifolds where the gluing junction serves as the locus of signature change. In another set of examples, we convert the Möbius strip into a singular signature-type changing manifold. For these resulting manifolds, we test whether the metric can be expressed as g ~ = g + f ​ V ♭ ⊗ V ♭ \tilde{g}=g+fV^{\flat}\otimes V^{\flat} , with g g a Lorentzian metric and f f a smooth interpolation function between the Lorentzian and Riemannian regions, separated by the signature change hypersurface ℋ \mathcal{H} . Our analysis reveals that the radical of the metric can transition from transverse to tangent at ℋ \mathcal{H} , pseudo-space orientability is obstructed by the Euler characteristic, and pseudo-time orientability may still hold. These examples illustrate subtle obstructions to applying standard transformation prescriptions for signature change and highlight novel phenomena in compact, non-orientable semi-Riemannian manifolds.



1.
Introduction

We explore signature-type changing manifolds where a Riemannian region,
endowed with a positive-definite metric, transitions smoothly into
a Lorentzian region, characterized by exactly one negative eigenvalue
of the metric tensor. Continuity of the eigenvalue spectrum across
the transition forces one eigenvalue to vanish at the hypersurface
of signature change. Consequently, the metric degenerates at this
locus, and its inverse ceases to exist. Thus, any continuous signature-changing
metric is necessarily degenerate at the hypersurface
[
3
]
.
Such metrics are called singular metrics.

Formally, we study a smooth, symmetric
(0,2)
-tensor field
\tilde{g}

on a smooth manifold
M
that becomes degenerate on a smoothly embedded
hypersurface
\mathcal{H}\subset M
. The bilinear type of
\tilde{g}

changes upon crossing
\mathcal{H}
. Of particular interest are the
cases where the radical of
\tilde{g}
at points of
\mathcal{H}

is transverse to the hypersurface.

This framework is of significant physical relevance, particularly in theories of quantum gravity and quantum cosmology. Signature change is a core feature in models relying on Wick rotation (e.g., Euclidean quantum gravity and the no-boundary proposal) and is used to calculate instanton transition rates between different phases of the early universe. Our mathematical study of the geometric constraints imposed by non-orientable topologies is thus motivated by the need for rigorous foundation and analysis of novel phenomena in these physical contexts.

This article builds on the framework developed in
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
,
in particular the Transformation Theorem, which asserts that (locally)
the metric
\tilde{g}
of a transverse signature-changing manifold

(M,\tilde{g})
is equivalent to a metric obtained from some Lorentzian
metric
g
. We also rely on the Transformation Prescription, which
provides a procedure to transform an arbitrary Lorentzian manifold
into a singular signature-changing manifold. We assume the reader’s
familiarity with these results. Our aim here is to extend the study
of manifolds with signature change, with particular emphasis on constructions
that exhibit non-orientable global structures. To illuminate the global structure of non-orientable signature-changing
manifolds, we construct explicit examples modeled on the topology
of the Möbius strip. By gluing diffeomorphic, connected nonempty
boundary components of smooth manifolds with collar neighborhoods,
we obtain new manifolds whose topology resembles that of a crosscap,
with the gluing locus serving as the hypersurface of signature change.

Some of our examples lie outside the class introduced in
[
4
,
5
]
, as they give rise to metrics whose radical is of mixed character, i.e., the radical of the metric transitions from transverse to tangent at
\mathcal{H}
. We then apply the Transformation Theorem
to the Möbius strip, transforming it from a Lorentzian manifold
into a signature-type changing manifold with a transverse radical.

Finally, we investigate whether the resulting metrics can be written
in the form

\tilde{g}=g+f(V^{\flat}\otimes V^{\flat}),

where
f
is a smooth transformation function that interpolates between
the Riemannian and Lorentzian regions,
g
is some Lorentzian metric,
and

denotes the metric dual of a vector field

1.1.
Preliminaries and fundamental definitions

Let
(M,\tilde{g})
be a smooth manifold of dimension
\dim M\geq 2
.
Following
[
7
]
,
we call
(M,\tilde{g})
a
transverse type-changing singular
semi-Riemannian manifold
if it satisfies the following condition.

Condition 1.1
.

The metric
\tilde{g}
is a smooth, symmetric,
(0,2)
-tensor
field on
M
which is degenerate on
\mathcal{H}
. Moreover,
\tilde{g}
is a codimension-
1

transverse type-changing metric
, meaning that

d(\det(\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}))_{q}\neq 0\text{ for all }q\in\mathcal{H},

and for any local coordinate system
\xi=(x^{0},\ldots,x^{n-1})

around
q
. As a consequence, the degeneracy locus

\{q\in M\!\!:\tilde{g}\!\!\mid_{q}is\;degenerate\}=:\mathcal{H}\subset M

is a smoothly embedded hypersurface in
M
. The bilinear type of

\tilde{g}
changes when crossing
\mathcal{H}
.

At each point
q\in\mathcal{H}
, there exists a one-dimensional radical

\textrm{Rad}_{q}\subset T_{q}M
, defined as the subspace

\textrm{Rad}_{q}:=\{w\in T_{q}M\mid\tilde{g}_{q}(w,v)=0\text{ for all }v\in T_{q}M\}.

The radical may be either tangent or transverse to
\mathcal{H}
.

Definition 1.2

(Character of the radical)
.

We say that the radical of a metric is transverse to
\mathcal{H}
if, at every point of

\mathcal{H}
, the radical distribution is not contained in
T\mathcal{H}
. If it is contained in

T\mathcal{H}
at every point, we call it purely tangent. In all remaining cases—when it is transverse at some points and tangent at others—we say that the radical has mixed character; equivalently, it is non-transverse with respect to
\mathcal{H}
.

To focus on the transverse case, we impose the following condition.

Condition 1.3
.

The radical
\textrm{Rad}_{q}
is transverse for all
q\in\mathcal{H}
,
i.e.

\textrm{Rad}_{q}\oplus T_{q}\mathcal{H}=T_{q}M\text{ for all }q\in\mathcal{H}.

Equivalently,
\textrm{Rad}_{q}
is not tangent to
\mathcal{H}

at any point
q
, i.e.,
Rad_{q}\not\subset T_{q}\mathcal{H}
.

1.2.
Summary of main results

The first result concerns the causal structure of the
rotating Minkowski model introduced in Subsection
2.1
. We show that the geometry
forces future-directed causal curves to remain confined to certain “stationary
stripes”, a property that plays a crucial role in understanding the causal
behavior of the crosscap metric studied later.

Proposition 1.8

(Trapping of causal curves in stationary stripes)
.

Let
(M,g)
be the two-dimensional manifold
M=\mathbb{R}^{2}
with coordinates
(t,x)

equipped with the “rotating Minkowski” metric

g=-\cos(2\varphi)\,(dt)^{2}+2\sin(2\varphi)\,dt\,dx+\cos(2\varphi)\,(dx)^{2},\qquad\varphi=\pi x.

For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
define the
stationary stripe

M_{k}:=\{(t,x)\in M:\tfrac{1}{4}(4k-1)<x<\tfrac{1}{4}(4k+1)\}.

Then
\partial_{t}
is a Killing field and is timelike on each
M_{k}
. Moreover,
any future-directed causal curve which meets a stationary stripe
M_{k}
at some
point is entirely contained in
M_{k}
(equivalently: causal curves entering a
stationary stripe cannot leave it).

The second result clarifies the orientability properties of manifolds that
undergo a change of signature. Even when both pseudo-time and pseudo-space
orientability hold, orientability in the usual sense may still fail.

Proposition 1.9

(Orientability)
.

Even if a transverse, signature-type changing manifold
(M,g)
with
a transverse radical is pseudo-time orientable and pseudo-space orientable,
it is not necessarily orientable.

Finally, in Subsection
2.3.1
we analyze in detail the explicit crosscap model
constructed via the adjunction space description in
Equation (
2.9
). The following proposition summarizes the
main structural properties of this metric, including the behavior of the
radical and the precise manner in which signature change occurs.

Proposition 1.10

(Radical and signature change on the crosscap)
.

Let
(C,g)
be the crosscap (see Eq.
2.9
) with metric

g=(1-t^{2})\,dt^{2}+2tx\,dt\,dx+(1-x^{2})\,dx^{2},

and define the degeneracy locus

\mathcal{H}=\{(t,x)\in C:t^{2}+x^{2}=1\}.

Then:

(1)

(C,g)
is a
signature-changing manifold
, with Lorentzian signature in the region
t^{2}+x^{2}<1
and Riemannian signature in
t^{2}+x^{2}>1
.

(2)

The
radical
at each point

is 1-dimensional:

\mathrm{Rad}_{q}=\mathrm{span}\Bigl\{(1,\sqrt{1-t^{2}}/t)^{T}\Bigr\}=\mathrm{span}\Bigl\{(\sqrt{1-x^{2}}/x,1)^{T}\Bigr\},

where
T
denotes the transpose of the matrix with respect to the chosen coordinates.

(3)

The radical is of mixed character with respect to
\mathcal{H}
(

is tangent iff

Consequently,

provides a compact example of a signature-changing manifold that

be obtained from a Lorentzian manifold via the Transformation Prescription.

These results illustrate the range of geometric behaviors that may occur in the
presence of signature change, and they motivate the systematic analysis of
signature-type changing metrics developed in the following section.

2.
Non-orientable models and signature change

Based on the method introduced in
[
4
]
,
any Lorentzian manifold
(M,g)
can be transformed into a signature-changing
manifold with metric
\tilde{g}
by means of the Transformation Prescription (
2.1

line element field
\{V,-V\}
together with a smooth function
f\colon M\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
Ideally, one assumes the existence of a smooth, non-vanishing timelike
vector field
V
. Motivated by this approach, we analyze certain
classes of signature-changing metrics and investigate whether they
can be expressed in the form

(2.1)

\tilde{g}=g+fV^{\flat}\otimes V^{\flat},

where
f
is a smooth transformation function interpolating between
the Lorentzian and Riemannian regions. The hypersurface of signature

\mathcal{H}=f^{-1}(1)

2.1.
The Lorentzian background: rotating Minkowski metric

Let
M=\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the standard topology and the canonical
Minkowski metric

\eta=-(dt)^{2}+(dx)^{2},

where
(t,x)
are Cartesian coordinates and let
E_{0}
,
E_{1}
denote
the Gaussian basis vector fields. We restrict attention to two-dimensional
models, although additional flat directions could be included without
affecting curvature (corresponding to a flat plane with a vanishing
Riemannian curvature tensor).

To describe a rotation of the standard coordinate vector fields
(E_{0},E_{1}
)
clockwise by an angle
\varphi
, we define the new, non-holonomic
basis

F_{0}=-(\sin\varphi)E_{1}+(\cos\varphi)E_{0},\quad F_{1}=(\cos\varphi)E_{1}+(\sin\varphi)E_{0},

where
\varphi
is a smooth function of
(t,x)
. We now require
that the metric

g=g_{00}(dt)^{2}+2g_{01}dtdx+g_{11}(dx)^{2}

satisfies

g(F_{1},F_{1})=-g(F_{0},F_{0})=1,\quad g(F_{0},F_{1})=0.

Solving this nonhomogeneous linear equation system gives
the unique solution

g_{11}=-g_{00}=\cos^{2}\varphi-\sin^{2}\varphi=\cos(2\varphi),\text{ and }g_{01}=2\cos\varphi\sin\varphi=\sin(2\varphi).

Hence the metric takes the form

g=-\cos(2\varphi)(dt)^{2}+2\sin(2\varphi)dtdx+\cos(2\varphi)(dx)^{2},

a smooth Lorentzian metric on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
whose light cones
rotate clockwise by an angle
\varphi
. We refer to this
informally as the “rotating Minkowski” metric. Note, however,
that unlike Minkowski space,
(M,g)
is not flat.

Remark 2.1
.

The same metric can be obtained by starting with a non-symmetric
tensor and extracting its symmetric part. Explicitly, one may set

g=-[(\cos\varphi)dx+(\sin\varphi)dt]\otimes[-(\sin\varphi)dx+(\cos\varphi)dt]

=(\sin\varphi)(\cos\varphi)dx\otimes dx-(\cos^{2}\varphi)dx\otimes dt+(\sin^{2}\varphi)dt\otimes dx-(\sin\varphi)(\cos\varphi)dt\otimes dt,

and then replace
g
by its symmetrization
g_{(ab)}=\frac{1}{2}(g_{ab}+g_{ba})

by producing a new symmetric tensor
g_{(ab)}
from the old one,
based on

\underset{g}{\underbrace{g_{ab}}}=\underset{symmetric}{\underbrace{g_{(ab)}}}+\underset{skew-symmetric}{\underbrace{g_{[ab]}}}=\frac{1}{2}(g_{ab}+g_{ba})+\frac{1}{2}(g_{ab}-g_{ba}).

The determinant of
g
is
\det([g_{\mu\nu}])=-\cos^{2}(2\varphi)-\sin^{2}(2\varphi)=-1
,
which is negative and non-vanishing. Therefore,
g
is Lorentzian.
A natural choice is
\varphi=\pi x
, which yields a metric in which
the light cones rotate clockwise in the
tx
-plane as one moves
in the
x
-direction. At integer values
x=k,k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
the metric reduces to the canonical Minkowski form
g=-1(dt)^{2}+1(dx)^{2}
.

Relative to an initial choice of time orientation, the causal cones
swap past and future orientation at odd integers
x\in\{2k-1:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
,
while they realign with their initial orientation at even integers.
Thus, a full rotation of the light cone (with respect to the basis
vectors
E_{0}
and
E_{1}
) occurs after an additional displacement
of the absolute value
1
along the
x
-direction. Hence, a full
rotation occurs only at
x\in\{2k:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, where the past
and future of the light cones align with their orientation at
x=0
,
i.e. along the
t
-axis. The Lorentzian manifold
(M,g)
is nevertheless
both orientable and time-orientable (see Subsection
2.2.1

for the definition of the global non-vanishing, timelike vector field

V
).

The causal structure can be elucidated by means of the

The metric coefficients are independent of the coordinate

General application of the Transformation Prescription

As established in Subsection

Carrying out the calculation yields

[\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}]=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}f\cdot\cos^{2}(\varphi)-\cos(2\varphi)&(1-\frac{f}{2})\sin(2\varphi)\\
(1-\frac{f}{2})\sin(2\varphi)&f\cdot\sin^{2}(\varphi)+\cos(2\varphi)\end{array}\right).

Remark 2.2
.

To ensure the transversality of

2.3.
Compact models and topological obstructions

It is well known that every non-compact smooth manifold admits a Lorentzian
metric. However, this is not generally the case for compact manifolds.
Moreover, any compact time-oriented Lorentzian manifold necessarily
possesses a non-empty chronology-violating set. In particular, such
manifolds contain closed timelike curves, which are typically regarded
as pathological.

Proof.

3.
Conclusion

In this work, we successfully constructed and analyzed explicit models of
singular semi-Riemannian manifolds
that undergo a signature-type change from Lorentzian to Riemannian geometry, focusing specifically on
non-orientable topologies
rooted in the Möbius strip and the crosscap. Our central finding confirms that such non-orientable manifolds can indeed support signature-changing metrics; however, they introduce novel geometric and topological phenomena that impose subtle but fundamental obstructions to the straightforward application of existing transformation prescriptions.

The analysis of our models yielded three key results:

(1)

Causal trapping in non-compact models (Proposition 1.8):
By applying the transformation prescription to a rotating Lorentzian background on the infinite Möbius strip, we demonstrated the explicit emergence of
causal trapping
at the signature change hypersurface
\mathcal{H}
. This highlights how the choice of the Lorentzian background metric can introduce non-trivial causal pathologies that persist across the transition.

(2)

Topological obstructions to orientability (Proposition 1.9):
In the construction of the compact Möbius strip with boundary, we confirmed the failure of
pseudo-space orientability
. This obstruction is fundamentally linked to the non-zero Euler characteristic (
\chi\neq 0
) of the underlying manifold, indicating that pseudo-orientability conditions, critical in the study of semi-Riemannian geometry, are not trivially satisfied by standard topological orientability.

(3)

The mixed radical (Proposition 1.10):
Most critically, our study of the compact crosscap manifold revealed a novel failure mode for the standard transformation prescription: the
radical of the induced metric at
\mathcal{H}
is of mixed character
(neither purely transverse nor purely tangent). This result directly violates the assumptions made in several previous transformation theorems (e.g., see
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
) and suggests that the construction of a simple, smooth interpolation function
\tilde{g}=g+fV^{b}\otimes V^{b}
is insufficient or impossible for manifolds with this specific topological constraint.

These findings open several avenues for future research. A primary goal is to
develop a generalized transformation prescription
capable of accommodating a mixed or purely tangent radical structure at the signature change locus. Furthermore, the role of the manifold’s Euler characteristic and higher-order topological invariants in constraining the causal structure and metric properties of singular semi-Riemannian manifolds warrants deeper investigation. Finally, since signature change is a core feature in models relying on the
Wick rotation
(e.g., Euclidean quantum gravity and the no-boundary proposal) and is used to calculate
instanton transition rates
, exploring the physical implications of a mixed radical, particularly concerning boundary conditions for quantum fields defined across
\mathcal{H}
, will be essential for advancing our understanding of these spacetime models.

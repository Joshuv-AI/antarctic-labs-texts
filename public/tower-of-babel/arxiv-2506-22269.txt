Density of Traceable Graphs
Michal Dvořák, Dušan Knop, Michal Opler, Jan Pokorný, Ondřej Suchý, Krisztina Szilágyi

Abstract
We establish tight lower and upper bounds on the number of edges in traceable graphs in several classes of dense graphs. A graph is traceable if it has a Hamiltonian path. We show that the bound is: • quadratic for the class of graphs of bounded neighborhood diversity, bounded size of maximum induced matching or bounded cluster vertex deletion number; • n ​ log ⁡ n n\log n for the class of cographs or, more generaly, bounded modular-width, and for the class of bounded distance to cograph; and • sligthly superlinear for the class of bounded shrub-depth.

Introduction

Extremal combinatorics is an area of discrete mathematics concerned with determining the maximum or minimum size of a collection of finite objects that satisfies certain properties. In graph theory, a classical result of Turán
[
15
]
states a tight bound on the number of edges a graph can contain while avoiding the complete graph as a subgraph. An extension of this result is the Erdős–Stone theorem which proves similar tight bounds for non-complete graphs
[
3
]
.

Another direction one can consider is the opposite question:
What is the
minimum
number of edges a graph has to have while possesing a certain property?
As a trivial example, every connected graph has to contain at least
n-1
edges and this bound is tight, i.e., there are connected graphs with
n-1
edges, namely trees. A slightly more interesting notion is the one of
saturation number
introduced by Erdős, Hajnal and Moon in 1964
[
2
]
, which asks, for a given fixed graph
H
and positive integer
n
:
What is the minimum number of edges an
n
-vertex graph can have such that it does not contain
H
but adding any edge to it will create a copy of
H
?
. Since then, there have been many results and generalizations of this notion
[
1
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
.

In our setting, we lay two conflicting properties against each other and ask what is the minimum number of edges an
n
-vertex graph can have. On the one hand, we require that the graph possesses some kind of dense structure. Typically, a class capturing a dense structure of graphs (e.g., bounded neighborhood diversity, see
\Cref
sec:preliminaries for a formal definition) contains cliques and is closed under complementation. In order to avoid graphs with too few edges, we require a conflicting property of the graph – in our case we require that the graph is traceable, i.e., it contains a Hamiltonian path.

Note that we aim for superlinear bounds for the number of edges. It is immediate that a traceable graph contains
\Omega(n)
edges as it contains at least
n-1
edges due to the Hamiltonian path. Interesting question is whether it is necessarily
forced
to contain e.g.
\Omega(n\log n)
or
\Omega(n^{3/2})
edges by employing the dense structure.

As a warmup example, consider the class of
cluster graphs
. A graph is a
cluster graph
if every connected component is a clique. Such class clearly contains the edgeless graph on any number of vertices and it also contains cliques, so the number of edges in such graphs can attain both extreme cases:
0
and
\binom{n}{2}
. However, if we also require the cluster graph to contain a Hamiltonian path, we obtain (up to isomorphism) one graph on
n
vertices with such properties, namely the complete graph.

For a slightly less trivial example, consider the class of cographs. Again, an edgeless graph is a cograph, but if we require a cograph to also contain a Hamiltonian path, it necessarily contains
\Omega(n\log n)
edges (as we prove in
\Cref
thm:lower_bound_cograph). On the other hand, this bound is tight as there exists an infinite family of traceable cographs with
O(n\log n)
edges (
\Cref
thm:upper_bound_cograph).

Preliminaries

We consider simple undirected graphs. A
v_{1}
-
v_{k}
path is a sequence

(v_{1},e_{1},v_{2},\ldots,e_{k-1},v_{k})

where vertices and edges alternate, i.e.,
e_{i}=\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}
. A Hamiltonian path in
G
is a path containing all vertices of the graph. Graph
G
is

if it contains a Hamiltonian path.
A

in
G
is
1
-regular subgraph of
G
. An

matching in
G
is a matching that is also an induced subgraph of
G
. The size of a maximum induced matching of
G
is denoted
\operatorname{mim}(G)
and is equal to the number of edges in the matching. Graph
G
is said to be
H
-free if it does not contain
H
as an induced subgraph. We use the following well-known result of Turán (
[
15
]
).

(Turán
[
15
]
)
.

Any
K_{p+1}
-free graph
G
with
n
vertices has at most
\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\cdot\frac{n^{2}}{2}
edges.

Lemma 2

(Jensen’s Inequality,
[
11
]
)
.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a convex function, then for any
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}:

\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\varphi(x_{i})\geq\ell\cdot\varphi\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}x_{i}}{\ell}\right)
.

The
neighborhood diversity
of a graph
G
, denoted
\operatorname{nd}(G)
, is the smallest integer
d
such that there exists a partition of
V
into
d
sets
V_{1},\ldots,V_{d}
such that for any

either we have for every

.

The following definition is from
[
9
]
.
Consider an algebraic expression
A
that uses the following operations:

[(O1)]

1.

create an isolated vertex;

2.

take the
disjoint union
of graphs
G_{1},G_{2}
, denoted by
G_{1}\oplus G_{2}
, which is the graph with vertex set
V(G_{1})\cup V(G_{2})

E(G_{1})\cup E(G_{2})
;

3.

take the
complete join
of
2
graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, denoted by
G_{1}\otimes G_{2}

V(G_{1})\cup V(G_{2})
and edge set
E(G_{1})\cup E(G_{2})\cup\{\{v,w\}\mid v\in V(G_{1})\wedge w\in V(G_{2})\}

4.

for graphs
G_{1},\ldots,G_{n}
and a pattern graph
G
with vertices

perform the
substitution
of the vertices of
G
by the graphs
G_{1},\ldots,G_{n}
, denoted by
G(G_{1},\ldots,G_{n})
, which is the graph with vertex set
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}V(G_{i})
and edge set
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}E(G_{i})\cup\{\{u,v\}\mid u\in V(G_{i})\wedge v\in V(G_{j})\wedge\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\in E(G)\}

G(G_{1},\ldots,G_{n})
is obtained from
G
by replacing every vertex
v_{i}\in V(G)
with the graph
G_{i}
and adding all edges between vertices of a graph
G_{i}
and the vertices of a graph
G_{j}
whenever
\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\in E(G)
.

The
width
of the expression
A
is the maximum number of vertices of a pattern graph used by any occurrence of the operation
(O
4
)
in

(or
0
if
(O
4
)
does not occur in

of a graph

\operatorname{mw}(G)
, is the smallest integer

such that

can be obtained from such an algebraic expression of width at most

(O
2
)
and
(O

)
can be seen as a special case of
(O
4
)
with graphs
K_{2}
, resp.
\overline{K_{2}}
.
The graphs

which become the subgraphs of
G(G_{1},\ldots,G_{n})
are referred to as
modular partition
of
G(G_{1},\ldots,G_{n})
.

A graph is a

if and only if it can be made by operations
(O

)

(O

)

(O

Theorem 5
.

Let
G
be a traceable graph on
n
vertices and
p=\operatorname{mim}(G)
be the size of a maximum induced matching of
G
. Then
G
contains at least
\frac{1}{8}\cdot\frac{n^{2}}{p}
edges.

Proof.

Let
P
be a Hamiltonian path of
G
.
Consider an auxiliary graph
H
with
V(H)=E(P)
and
\{e,f\}\in E(H)
if and only if
e,f
induce an induced matching in
G
, i.e.,
e\cap f=\emptyset
and
E(G[e\cup f])=\{e,f\}
.
Notice that a
K_{p+1}
in
H
would correspond to an induced matching of size
p+1
in
G
.
Consider the complement
\overline{H}
and notice that each edge of
\overline{H}
corresponds to either two consecutive edges on
P
(these do not form an induced matching) or at least one edge in
E(G)\setminus E(P)
.
Picking any of the edges if there are more of them provides a mapping from
E(\overline{H})\setminus\{\{e,f\}\mid\text{$e$, $f$ are consecutive on $P$}\}
to
E(G)\setminus E(P)
which maps at most
4
edges of
E(\overline{H})
to one edge of
E(G)\setminus E(P)
.

We lower bound
|E(\overline{H})|
.
As
H
is
K_{p+1}
-free, by Turán’s theorem, we have
|E(H)|\leq(1-\frac{1}{p})\frac{n^{2}}{2}
.
Therefore
|E(\overline{H})|\geq\binom{n}{2}-(1-\frac{1}{p})\frac{n^{2}}{2}\geq\frac{n^{2}}{2p}-\frac{n}{2}
.
By the above argumentation,
we have that
\frac{n^{2}}{2p}-\frac{n}{2}-(n-2)\leq|E(\overline{H})|-(n-2)\leq 4(|E(G)|-(n-1))
, which entails
|E(G)|\geq\frac{1}{4}\cdot\left(\frac{n^{2}}{2p}-\frac{n}{2}-(n-2)\right)+n-1\geq\frac{n^{2}}{8p}
which we wanted to show.
∎

Theorem 6
.

For any
p\geq 1
and infinitely many
n
there is a traceable graph
G
with at most
\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{n^{2}}{p}
edges and maximum induced matching of size
p
.

Proof.

For any
n=kp,k\geq 4
we construct such graph. We construct
p
cliques of size
k
and connect them into a “path”. More precisely, let the cliques be
K^{1},K^{2},\ldots,K^{p}
and let
v^{i}_{1},v^{i}_{2}
be two distinct vertices of
K^{i}
. For each
i\in[p-1]
add an edge of the form
\{v^{i}_{2},v^{i+1}_{1}\}
. Denote the resulting graph by
G
. By the pigeonhole principle,
G
cannot contain an induced matching larger than
p
. On the other hand, taking one edge (nonincident to
v^{i}_{1},v^{i}_{2}
) in each clique is an induced matching of size
p
. Note that this is possible as there are
k\geq 4
vertices in each clique. The total number of edges of
G
is at most
p\binom{k}{2}+p-1\leq\frac{1}{2}(pk^{2}-pk)+p\leq\frac{n^{2}}{2p}
.
∎

Theorem 7
.

Let
G
be a traceable graph with neighborhood diversity
\operatorname{nd}(G)
and
n\geq 2
vertices. Then
G
contains at least
\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{n^{2}}{\operatorname{nd}(G)}
edges.

Proof.

First, note that
G
has at least
n-1
edges, and if
n\leq 2\operatorname{nd}(G)
, then

n-1\geq\frac{n}{2}\geq\frac{n\cdot 2\operatorname{nd}(G)}{4\operatorname{nd}(G)}\geq\frac{n^{2}}{4\operatorname{nd}(G)}

and the claim holds.
Therefore we assume that
G
has at least
2\operatorname{nd}(G)
vertices.
Let
G
be a traceable graph with a neighborhood diversity partition of
V(G)
into
d=\operatorname{nd}(G)
sets
V_{1},\ldots,V_{d}
and let
n_{i}
denote the size of
V_{i}
for each
i\in[d]
.
We bound the number of edges incident to each
V_{i}
separately depending on whether
V_{i}
induces a clique or an independent set.
If
V_{i}
induces a clique then there are exactly
\binom{n_{i}}{2}
edges with both endpoints in
V_{i}
.
Otherwise,
V_{i}
induces an independent set and no two of its vertices can appear next to each other in the Hamiltonian path.
Therefore, there are at least
n_{i}-1
vertices outside of
V_{i}
with a neighbor in
V_{i}
.
Due to the properties of neighborhood diversity, all these vertices are in fact adjacent to the whole set
V_{i}
and it follows that
V_{i}
is incident to at least
n_{i}(n_{i}-1)
edges.
Notice that in this second case we may consider each edge twice, once from each endpoint.
Accounting for this overcounting, we obtain

\displaystyle|E(G)|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\text{$V_{i}$ clique}}\binom{n_{i}}{2}+\sum_{\text{$V_{i}$ independent}}\frac{n_{i}(n_{i}-1)}{2}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{n_{i}(n_{i}-1)}{2}\geq

\displaystyle\geq d\cdot\frac{\frac{n}{d}(\frac{n}{d}-1)}{2}=

\displaystyle=\frac{n(n-d)}{2d}\geq

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n^{2}}{4d}.

The second inequality follows by applying Jensen’s inequality to the convex function
x\mapsto\frac{x(x-1)}{2}
. The last inequality follows from the fact that
n-d\geq\frac{n}{2}
because
n\geq 2d
.
∎

Theorem 8
.

For each
d\geq 1
and infinitely many
n
there is a traceable graph on
n
vertices with neighborhood diversity at most
2d
and at most
2\cdot\frac{n^{2}}{d}
edges.

Proof.

For any
k\geq 2
we construct a graph on
n=kd
vertices. Construct
d
cliques
K^{1},K^{2},\ldots,K^{d}
of size
k-1
and
d
independent vertices
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{d}
. The idea is to create a path on
2d
vertices where every second vertex is replaced by a clique on
k-1
vertices. Formally, for each
i\in[d]
connect
v_{i}
to all vertices of
K^{i}
and
K^{i-1}
(if it exists). Let
G
denote the resulting graph. Clearly,
G
has neighborhood diversity at most
2d
as the cliques and independent vertices form a neighborhood diversity partition of the resulting graph. Finally, the resulting graph has
kd
vertices and at most
d\binom{k}{2}+2kd\leq dk^{2}+2n=\frac{n^{2}}{d}+2n\leq 2\frac{n^{2}}{d}
edges.
∎

Theorem 9
.

Let
G
be a traceable cograph. Then
G
contains at least
\frac{1}{4}\cdot n\log_{2}n
edges.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n
. The claim is obvious for
n\leq 4
. Let
G
be traceable cograph on
n\geq 5
vertices. Since
G
contains a Hamiltonian path it is necessarily a join of two smaller cographs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
. Denote
|V(G_{1})|=k
and
|V(G_{2})|=n-k
and assume without loss of generality that
k\leq\frac{n}{2}
.
As
n-k\geq\frac{n}{2}
, we have at least
k(n-k)\geq\frac{nk}{2}
edges between the parts.
We split the Hamiltonian path into
\ell
subpaths
P_{1},\ldots,P_{\ell}
by removing edges with one endpoint in
G_{1}
and other endpoint in
G_{2}
. Clearly
2\leq\ell\leq 2k+1
.
The total number of edges is
|E(G)|\geq\frac{kn}{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|E(P_{i})|
. By the induction hypothesis, since the paths
P_{i}
induce a Hamiltonian path in the subgraph induced by
V(P_{i})
, each segment induces at least
\frac{1}{4}|P_{i}|\log|P_{i}|
edges. Hence

\displaystyle|E(G)|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{kn}{2}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|P_{i}|\log|P_{i}|\geq

\displaystyle\geq\frac{kn}{2}+\frac{1}{4}\ell\frac{n}{\ell}\log\frac{n}{\ell}\geq

\displaystyle\geq\frac{kn}{2}+\frac{1}{4}n\log\left(\frac{n}{2k+1}\right)=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}nk+\frac{1}{4}n\log n-\frac{1}{4}n\log(2k+1)

where the last two inequalities are due to Jensen’s inequality and the fact that
\ell\leq 2k+1
. Since
\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}\log(2k+1)\geq 0
for any
k\geq 1
, the last term is lower bounded by
\frac{1}{4}n\log n
and this finishes the proof.
∎

Theorem 10
.

For infinitely many
n
there is a traceable cograph on
n
vertices with at most
n\log_{2}(n)
edges.

Proof.

For every
k
we construct a traceable cograph with
n=2^{k}-1
vertices and at most
2^{k}k\leq n\log_{2}(n)
edges.
We proceed by induction on
k
. For
k=1
we take
P_{1}
and for
k=2
the path
P_{3}
.

For the inductive step, take a disjoint union of two traceable cographs with
2^{k}-1
vertices and at most
2^{k}k
edges each.
Add a new vertex and perform a join with the union of the two graphs on one side and the new vertex on the other side. The new graph has
2\cdot(2^{k}-1)+1=2^{k+1}-1
vertices. Clearly it is traceable and it has at most
2\cdot 2^{k}k+2(2^{k}-1)\leq 2^{k+1}(k+1)
edges.
∎

Theorem 11
.

Let
G
be a traceable graph and let
d=\operatorname{mw}(G)
be the modular-width of
G
. Then
G
has at least
\frac{1}{4}\cdot n\log_{d}(n)
edges.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n
. The claim is obvious for
n\leq 4
. Let
G
be a graph with modular-width
d
on
n\geq 5
vertices and let
P
be its arbitrary Hamiltonian path.
Let
M_{1},\dots,M_{p}
be a modular partition of
G
where
p\leq d
.
For each
i\in[p]
, we let
n_{i}
denote the size of
M_{i}
and we let
\ell_{i}
be the number of disjoint paths
P^{i}_{1},\dots,P^{i}_{\ell_{i}}
obtained by restricting
P
to the module
M_{i}
.

For each
i\in[p]
, we separately bound the edges with both endpoints inside
M_{i}
and the edges with exactly one endpoint in
M_{i}
.
By the induction hypothesis, each segment
P^{i}_{j}
induces at least
\frac{1}{4}|P^{i}_{j}|\log_{d}|P^{i}_{j}|
edges since it is itself a traceable graph with modular-width at most
d
.
Therefore the number of edges with both endpoints in
M_{i}
is at least

\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}\frac{1}{4}|P^{i}_{j}|\log_{d}|P^{i}_{j}|\geq\frac{1}{4}n_{i}\log_{d}\frac{n_{i}}{\ell_{i}}

where the inequality follows by using Jensen’s inequality on the convex function
x\mapsto x\log_{d}x
.

On the other hand, there are at least
\ell_{i}-1
vertices outside of
M_{i}
with neighbors in
M_{i}
since
P
must contain at least one vertex outside of
M_{i}
between the segments
P^{i}_{j}
and
P^{i}_{j+1}
for each
j\in[\ell_{i}-1]
.
Moreover in the special case when
\ell_{i}=1
, there must still exist at least one such vertex outside of
M_{i}
.
By the properties of modules, these vertices are adjacent to the whole module
M_{i}
and thus, there are at least
n_{i}\ell_{i}^{-}
edges with exactly one endpoint in
M_{i}
where
\ell_{i}^{-}=\max(1,\ell_{i}-1)
.

Summing these together and adjusting for overcounting, we obtain

\displaystyle|E(G)|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{4}n_{i}\log_{d}\frac{n_{i}}{\ell_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{n_{i}\ell_{i}^{-}}{2}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{4}n_{i}\log_{d}\frac{d\cdot n_{i}}{d\cdot\ell_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{n_{i}\ell_{i}^{-}}{2}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{4}n_{i}\log_{d}(d\cdot n_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{4}n_{i}\cdot\left(2\ell_{i}^{-}-\log_{d}(d\ell_{i})\right)

where the second term on the right hand side is always non-negative since
2(x-1)-\log_{d}(dx)\geq 0
for every
x\geq 2
and
2\ell_{i}^{-}-\log_{d}(d\ell_{i})=1\geq 0
in the special case when
\ell_{i}=1
.

Finally, it suffices to apply Jensen’s inequality one more time to obtain

|E(G)|\geq\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{4}n_{i}\log_{d}(d\cdot n_{i})\geq\frac{1}{4}p\cdot\frac{n}{p}\log_{d}\left(\frac{dn}{p}\right)\geq\frac{1}{4}n\log_{d}n,

where the last inequality follows from
p\leq d
.
∎

Theorem 12
.

For each
d\geq 2
and infinitely many
n
there is a traceable graph on
n
vertices with modular width at most
2d
and at most
2n\log_{d}n
edges.

Proof.

For every
k\geq 1
we construct a graph
G_{k}
with
n=\sum_{i=1}^{k}d^{i}
vertices, modular width at most
2d
and at most
2k\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{k}d^{i}\leq 2n\log_{d}n
edges, where the last inequality follows from
\log_{d}\sum_{i=1}^{k}d^{i}\geq\log_{d}d^{k}=k
.

We proceed by induction on
k
.
For
k=1
we take
G_{1}
to be the path on
d
vertices.
Obviously, it has
d
vertices, modular width at most
2d
and
d-1\leq 2\cdot 1\cdot d

Let
k\geq 2
.
We create the graph
G_{k}
by taking a path on
2d
vertices
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{2d}

x_{1},x_{3},x_{5},\ldots,x_{2d-1}

G_{k-1}

Theorem 13
.

Let
m\geq 2,d\geq 1
be given integers. Let
G
be a traceable graph on
n\geq 2
vertices admitting a
(d,m)
-tree-model. Then
G
has at least
\frac{1}{16m}\cdot n^{1+\frac{1}{2^{d}-1}}
edges.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
d
. For
d=1
,
G
has neighborhood diversity at most
m
so, by
\Cref
thm:lower_bound_nd, the graph has at least
\frac{n^{2}}{4m}\geq\frac{n^{2}}{16m}
edges.

For the inductive step, consider the root of the tree-model
r
and let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{s}
be subtrees corresponding to the children of
r
,
s\geq 2
.
Let
P
be a Hamiltonian path of
G
.
Cut the Hamiltonian path
P
into
\ell\geq 2
segments of sizes
n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{\ell}
by cutting an edge if its endpoints belong to different subtrees among
T_{1},\ldots,T_{s}
. Let
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{2\ell}
be the endpoints of the segments. Note that it might happen that (e.g.)
v_{2i-1}=v_{2i}
if the segment consists of a single vertex. Consider the right endpoints of every second segment starting from the first one, i.e., the vertices
X=\{v_{2},v_{6},v_{10},\ldots,\}
.
For
j\in[m]
let
X_{j}
be the set of vertices in
X
with label
j
.
For each
v_{k}\in X_{j}
note that the vertex
v_{k+1}
is, by definition, in a different subtree than
v_{k}
.
Partition
X_{j}
by the subtrees
T_{1},\ldots,T_{s}
in which the vertices occur, i.e.,
X_{j}=X_{j}^{1}\cup\cdots X_{j}^{s}
.
We now distinguish two cases.

Case 1

There is
t\in[s]
such that
|X_{j}^{t}|\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot|X_{j}|
. In this case, since all successors of vertices in
X^{i}_{j}
are in different subtrees, there is a complete bipartite graph between the vertices in
X_{j}^{t}
(one part) and the set of successors of vertices in
X_{j}^{t}
, formally the set
Y=\{v_{k+1}\mid v_{k}\in X_{j}^{t}\}
. Note that since we chose every second segment, we have
Y\cap X_{j}^{t}=\emptyset
, hence there are at least
\frac{|X_{j}|^{2}}{4}
edges.

Case 2

For all
t\in[s]
we have
|X_{j}^{t}|<\frac{1}{2}\cdot|X_{j}|
. In this case we consider the successors
Y
of vertices in whole
X_{j}
, i.e.,
Y=\{v_{k+1}\mid v_{k}\in X_{j}\}
. By the same argument as in the previous case,
Y\cap X_{j}=\emptyset
. Consider a vertex
v\in Y
and suppose that it is in subtree
T_{t}
. Note that less than
\frac{1}{2}\cdot|X_{j}|
vertices are in
T_{t}
, hence there are at least
|X_{j}|-\frac{1}{2}|X_{j}|=\frac{1}{2}|X_{j}|
vertices from
X_{j}
that are not in
T_{t}
. By definition of shrub-depth,
v
is connected to at least this many vertices. Summing for each
v\in Y
we obtain
\frac{|X_{j}|^{2}}{2}
edges in this case.

In both cases we obtain at least
\frac{|X_{j}|^{2}}{4}
edges. By summing over
j
, we obtain at least
\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{|X_{j}|^{2}}{4}\geq m\cdot\frac{1}{4}\cdot\left(\frac{|X|}{m}\right)^{2}=\frac{|X|^{2}}{4m}
edges, where the inequality is due to Jensen’s inequality applied to the convex function
x\mapsto x^{2}
. Since
|X|=\frac{\ell}{2}
we obtain at least
\frac{\ell^{2}}{16m}
edges between different subtrees from
T_{1},\ldots,T_{s}
. We apply induction hypothesis to the
\ell
segments and
d-1
and count the edges inside the subtrees
T_{1},\ldots,T_{s}
. For a segment of length
n_{i}
we have at least
\frac{1}{16m}n_{i}^{1+\frac{1}{2^{d-1}-1}}=\frac{1}{16m}n_{i}^{\frac{2^{d-1}}{2^{d-1}-1}}
edges. The total number of edges is thus at least

\frac{\ell^{2}}{16m}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{1}{16m}n_{i}^{\frac{2^{d-1}}{2^{d-1}-1}}\geq\frac{1}{16m}\left(\ell^{2}+\ell\left(\frac{n}{\ell}\right)^{\frac{2^{d-1}}{2^{d-1}-1}}\right)=\frac{1}{16m}\left(\ell^{2}+\frac{n^{\frac{2^{d-1}}{2^{d-1}-1}}}{\ell^{\frac{1}{2^{d-1}-1}}}\right),

(1)

where we applied the Jensen’s inequality to the convex function
n\mapsto n^{\frac{2^{d-1}}{2^{d-1}-1}}
.
We lower bound the parenthesis as follows. Note that one summand is increasing in
\ell
while the other is decreasing in
\ell
, hence the sum is lower bounded by the value attained by any of them when they are equal, that is, when
\ell^{2}=\frac{n^{\frac{2^{d-1}}{2^{d-1}-1}}}{\ell^{\frac{1}{2^{d-1}-1}}}
, which is precisely when

\ell=n^{\frac{\frac{2^{d-1}}{2^{d-1}-1}}{2+\frac{1}{2^{d-1}-1}}}=n^{\frac{2^{d-1}}{2(2^{d-1}-1)+1}}=n^{\frac{2^{(d-1)}}{2^{d}-1}}.

By plugging back we lower bound the last expression in (
1
) by

\frac{1}{16m}\cdot n^{\frac{2^{d}}{2^{d}-1}}=\frac{1}{16m}\cdot n^{1+\frac{1}{2^{d}-1}}
,
which was to be shown.
∎

Theorem 14
.

For any
d
and infinitely many
n
, there is a traceable graph
G
admitting
(d,2^{d-1})
-tree-model with
n
vertices and at most
2^{O(d)}\cdot n^{1+\frac{1}{2^{d}-1}}
edges.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
d
that for every
n\geq 1
, there is a traceable graph with at least
n
vertices and at most
\gamma_{d}\cdot n^{1+\frac{1}{2^{d}-1}}=\gamma_{d}\cdot n^{2^{d}/(2^{d}-1)}
edges that admits a
(d,2^{d-1})
-tree-model where the constants
\gamma_{d}
are given by the recurrence
\gamma_{d}=4\cdot\gamma_{d-1}+32
with initial condition
\gamma_{1}=1
.
Notice that the bound on the number of edges in the statement is with respect to
n
even though the graph can contain more than
n
vertices.
Therefore, we can restrict such traceable graph to its induced traceable subgraph on exactly
n
vertices without violating the edge bound.
This implies the statement since the recurrence solves to
\gamma_{d}=\frac{1}{12}(35\cdot 4^{d}-128)=2^{O(d)}
.

For
d=1
, the inequality holds since any graph on
n
vertices has less than
n^{2}
edges and we can, thus, simply take a clique which admits a trivial
(1,1)
-tree-model.
Let
d>1
and set
n^{\prime}=\lfloor n^{(2^{d-1}-1)/(2^{d}-1)}\rfloor
.
By induction hypothesis, there is a traceable graph
G^{\prime}
on at least
n^{\prime}
vertices with at most
\gamma_{d-1}\cdot(n^{\prime})^{2^{d-1}/(2^{d-1}-1)}
edges that admits a
(d-1,2^{d-2})
-tree-model
(T^{\prime},S^{\prime})
.
By plugging in the inequality
n^{\prime}\leq n^{(2^{d-1}-1)/(2^{d}-1)}
, we obtain

|E(G^{\prime})|\leq\gamma_{d-1}\cdot(n^{\prime})^{2^{d-1}/(2^{d-1}-1)}\leq\gamma_{d-1}\cdot\left(n^{(2^{d-1}-1)/(2^{d}-1)}\right)^{2^{d-1}/(2^{d-1}-1)}=\gamma_{d-1}\cdot n^{2^{d-1}/(2^{d}-1)}.

(2)

Our goal is to take
\lceil n/n^{\prime}\rceil
many copies of
G^{\prime}
and join them to obtain a traceable graph on at least
n
vertices.
First, we double in size the set of available colours.
In order to simplify the argument, let us work from now on with colours from the set
\{0,1\}\times[2^{d-2}]
instead of the set
[2^{d-1}]
.
We define a signature
S\subseteq(\{0,1\}\times[2^{d-2}])^{2}\times[d]
.
For every
\ell<d
, the signature contains exactly the tuples that appear in
S^{\prime}
, ignoring the first coordinate of the colours, i.e. we have for every
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\{0,1\}
and
\ell<d

((\alpha_{1},i),(\alpha_{2},j),\ell)\in S\iff(i,j,\ell)\in S^{\prime}.

(3)

At the root level, the signature contains exactly all possible pairs of colours with their first coordinate equal to 1.
That is, we additionally let
S
contain the tuples
((1,i),(1,j),d)
for every choice of
i,j\in[2^{d-2}]
.

We now define a
(d-1,2^{d-1})
-tree-model
(T^{\prime\prime},S)
of
G^{\prime}
that allows us to distinguish the endpoints of the Hamiltonian path.
The tree
T^{\prime\prime}
is exactly the same as
T^{\prime}
except for the assignment of colours to its leafs.
Let
f\colon V(G^{\prime})\to[2^{d-2}]
be the assignment of colours to vertices of
G^{\prime}
(as leafs) in
T^{\prime}
.
Then
v\in V(G^{\prime})
receives in
T^{\prime\prime}
the colour
(1,f(v))
if it is one of the two endpoints of the Hamiltonian path, and
(0,f(v))
otherwise.
Observe that
(T^{\prime\prime},S)
is indeed a tree model of
G^{\prime}
by (
3
) since
T^{\prime\prime}
has depth
d-1
.

We now let
G
be the graph defined by a tree model
(T,S)
where
T
consists of a root with
\lceil n/n^{\prime}\rceil
subtrees, each a copy of
T^{\prime\prime}
.
Notice that
G
is traceable graph since the endpoints of all individual Hamiltonian paths in the subtrees are connected at the root level into a clique.
For technical reasons, let us first observe the following upper bound on
\lceil n/n^{\prime}\rceil

\left\lceil\frac{n}{n^{\prime}}\right\rceil\leq 2\cdot\frac{n}{n^{\prime}}\leq 2\cdot\left(\frac{2n}{n^{(2^{d-1}-1)/(2^{d}-1)}}\right)\leq 4\cdot n^{2^{d-1}/(2^{d}-1)}

(4)

where the first inequality holds since
n/n^{\prime}\geq 1
and the second follows from the fact that
n^{(2^{d-1}-1)/(2^{d}-1)}>1
and thus,
n^{\prime}=\lfloor n^{(2^{d-1}-1)/(2^{d}-1)}\rfloor\geq n^{(2^{d-1}-1)/(2^{d}-1)}/2
.

Now we separately bound the number of edges within the copies of
G^{\prime}
and the edges introduced at the root level.
There are
\lceil n/n^{\prime}\rceil
copies of
G^{\prime}
in total, each with at most
\gamma_{d-1}\cdot n^{2^{d-1}/(2^{d}-1)}
edges by (
2
).
The number of edges contained within them is thus at most

\left\lceil\frac{n}{n^{\prime}}\right\rceil\cdot\gamma_{d-1}\cdot n^{2^{d-1}/(2^{d}-1)}\leq 4\cdot n^{2^{d-1}/(2^{d}-1)}\cdot\gamma_{d-1}\cdot n^{2^{d-1}/(2^{d}-1)}=4\cdot\gamma_{d-1}\cdot n^{2^{d}/(2^{d}-1)}

(5)

where the first inequality follows by applying (
4
).

It remains to bound the number of extra edges introduced in the root of
T
.
There are
\lceil n/n^{\prime}\rceil
subtrees, each containing only two leafs coloured by
(1,i)
for some
i\in[2^{d-2}]
.
Thus the number of edges introduced is at most

\binom{2\cdot\lceil n/n^{\prime}\rceil}{2}\leq\frac{4\cdot\lceil n/n^{\prime}\rceil^{2}}{2}\leq 2\cdot\left(4\cdot n^{2^{d-1}/(2^{d}-1)}\right)^{2}=32\cdot\left(n^{2^{d}/(2^{d}-1)}\right)

(6)

where we first applied the inequality
\binom{t}{2}\leq\frac{t^{2}}{2}
, followed by an application of (
4
).

Adding (
5
) and (
6
) together, we obtain the desired bound on the number of edges in
G

|E(G)|\leq(4\cdot\gamma_{d-1}+32)\cdot n^{2^{d}/(2^{d}-1)}=\gamma_{d}\cdot n^{2^{d}/(2^{d}-1)}.\qed

Lemma 15
.

Let
\Pi
be a hereditary graph class. Suppose there are constants
n_{0},D
such that every traceable
G\in\Pi
on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices contains at least
Dn^{2}
edges. For any
\alpha\geq 0
let
\Gamma_{\alpha}
be the class of graphs with distance to
\Pi
at most
\alpha
. Then every traceable
G\in\Gamma_{\alpha}
with
n\geq 2n_{0}\cdot\max\{\alpha,1\}
vertices contains at least
\frac{D}{4}\cdot\frac{n^{2}}{(\alpha+1)}
edges.

Proof.

If
\alpha=0
, then
\Pi=\Gamma_{\alpha}
and if
G
has at least
2n_{0}\geq n_{0}
vertices, then it contains
Dn^{2}
edges, hence it also contains at least
D\frac{n^{2}}{4}
edges.

Suppose that
\alpha\geq 1
. Let
G\in\Gamma_{\alpha}
be a graph with
n\geq 2n_{0}\cdot\alpha
vertices and let
S\subseteq V(G),|S|\leq\alpha
be a modulator to
\Pi
. Let
P
be a Hamiltonian path of
G
. The modulator splits
P
into
\ell\leq\alpha+1
segments
P_{1},\ldots,P_{\ell}
with
n-|S|\geq n-\alpha
vertices in total. Denote
p_{i}=|V(P_{i})|
. Up to reordering, suppose that first
\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell
segments contain at least
n_{0}
vertices. Note that, as the other segments contain at most
n_{0}-1
vertices, we have
\ell^{\prime}\geq 1
as otherwise we would have
n\leq(n_{0}-1)\ell+\alpha\leq(n_{0}-1)(\alpha+1)+\alpha=n_{0}(\alpha+1)-1\leq 2n_{0}\cdot\alpha-1
contradicting that
n\geq 2n_{0}\alpha
. Hence
\ell-\ell^{\prime}\leq\alpha
. Denote
G_{i}=G[V(P_{i})]
. By assumption, if
p_{i}\geq n_{0}
then
|E(G_{i})|\geq Dp_{i}^{2}
. Applying Jensen’s inequality to the convex function
x\mapsto x^{2}
, along with the bound
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell^{\prime}}p_{i}\geq n-\alpha-(\ell-\ell^{\prime})\cdot(n_{0}-1)\geq n-\alpha n_{0}
we obtain the following estimate on the number of edges in
G
:

|E(G)|\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell^{\prime}}|E(G_{i})|\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell^{\prime}}Dp_{i}^{2}\geq D\ell^{\prime}\cdot\frac{(n-\alpha n_{0})^{2}}{(\ell^{\prime})^{2}}\geq D\frac{n^{2}}{4\ell^{\prime}}\geq D\frac{n^{2}}{4(\alpha+1)}.

The fourth inequality follows from the fact that
n-\alpha n_{0}\geq\frac{n}{2}
because
n\geq 2\alpha n_{0}
. Finally, the last inequality follows from the fact that
\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell\leq\alpha+1
and this completes the proof.
∎

We remark that
\Cref
lem:transforming_to_distance_to_pi also works with the function
n\mapsto Dn\log(n)
and the lower bound becomes
\frac{D}{2}n\log\left(\frac{n}{2(\alpha+1)}\right)
for
n\geq 2n_{0}\cdot\max\{\alpha,1\}
.

Theorem 17
.

d\geq 0
and infinitely many

G
on

\operatorname{cvdn}(G)\leq d

Theorem 19
.

\Pi
be the class of cographs. For any given

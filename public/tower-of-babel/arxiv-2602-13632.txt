Ward-Takahashi Identity and Gauge-Invariant Response Theory for Open Quantum Systems
Hongchao Li, Xie-Hang Yu, Masaya Nakagawa, Masahito Ueda

Abstract
We derive the Ward-Takahashi identity and establish the gauge-invariant response theory for open quantum systems described by Lindbladians to show that particle-number conservation is not necessary to satisfy gauge invariance. We construct an observable which can be used to test the gauge invariance in the absence of particle-number conservation. We derive the low-energy collective modes that emerge as a consequence of gauge invariance in open quantum systems, and find that two-body loss induces diffusive modes in dissipative Bardeen-Cooper-Schrieffer (BCS) superconductivity. Possible experimental situations for testing gauge invariance in open quantum systems are also discussed.

Here, it is instructive to revisit the two unphysical features in the BCS theory: non-conservation of the particle number and superposition of different particle-number sectors. In this Letter, we develop a gauge-invariant linear response theory for open quantum systems described by the local Lindblad dynamics. In particular, on the basis of the weak U(1) symmetry of the Lindbladian that commutes with the generator of phase rotation

We use the derived Ward-Takahashi identity to construct a general linear response theory for weak U(1) symmetric Lindbladian. Our result only relies on the symmetry of Lindbladians regardless of the specfic form of interactions and Lindblad operators.

Since there is no particle-number conservation in the Lindblad dynamics with weak U(1) symmetry, the particle number does not serve as an indicator of the gauge invariance. Instead, we employ the Ward-Takahashi identity to construct an observable

Gauge-invariant response theory.—
We now prove the gauge invariance of the current in response to the EM field by applying the Ward-Takahashi identity. The perturbed current is given by

Criterion for gauge invariance.—
Equation (

\displaystyle[\delta(x-x_{1})\tau_{3}C_{\alpha\alpha}(x_{1},x_{2})-\delta(x-x_{2})C_{\alpha\alpha}(x_{1},x_{2})\tau_{3}]

Nambu-Goldstone mode.—
We consider low-energy collective modes of a weak U(1)-symmetric Lindbladian. Conclusion.—
In this Letter, we have developed the gauge-invariant response theory for dissipative fermionic systems in the presence of external EM gauge fields by establishing the Ward-Takahashi identity in open quantum systems and shown that the gauge invariance can be preserved even in the absence of particle-number conservation. We have demonstrated that the minimal condition for gauge invariance is weak U(1) symmetry for a local Lindbladian. We have constructed an observable that can be used to test the gauge-invariant transport theory, which can be detected from measuring the quantities on doubled copies. We have also shown the low-energy collective modes for dissipative BCS superconductivity, where two-body loss induces a diffusive mode for propagation.

Ward-Takahashi Identity in Closed Quantum Systems

We derive the Ward-Takahashi identity in open quantum systems on the basis of weak U(1) symmetry of the Lindbladian that is used in the main text. To set the basis for our discussion, we first review the derivation of the real-space Ward-Takahashi identity in closed quantum systems in the parlance of path integral. Let

i\gamma nU\int\frac{k^{2}dk}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{v_{F}qf(q)}{4\sqrt{3}\Delta^{3}}=iU\gamma n\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{A_{1\boldsymbol{k}}+A_{2\boldsymbol{k}}+A_{3\boldsymbol{k}}+A_{4\boldsymbol{k}}}{4E_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{3}(q^{0}+E_{\boldsymbol{k}}-E_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}})^{2}(q^{0}+E_{\boldsymbol{k}}+E_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}})^{2}},

(S.108)

where

\displaystyle A_{1\boldsymbol{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}(E_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}^{2}\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}+(3\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}-2\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}})(\Delta^{2}+\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}})-(q^{0})^{2}(\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}+2\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}})),

\displaystyle A_{2\boldsymbol{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4q^{0}E_{\boldsymbol{k}}\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}(\Delta^{2}+\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}),

\displaystyle A_{3\boldsymbol{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}(E_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}^{2}-(q^{0})^{2})(\Delta^{2}+\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}),

\displaystyle A_{4\boldsymbol{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{4}(\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}-2\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}})-4q^{0}E_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{3}\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}.

By substituting
E_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}+\Delta^{2}}
, we can rewrite the right-hand side of Eq. (
S.108
) as

iU\gamma n\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{-(q_{0}^{2}(\Delta^{2}(\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}+\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}})+2\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{3}))+4q_{0}\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}E_{\boldsymbol{k}}(\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}-\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}})^{2}-(\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}-\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}})^{2}(\Delta^{2}(\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}-\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}})+2\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{3})}{4E_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{3}(q^{0}+E_{\boldsymbol{k}}-E_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}})^{2}(q^{0}+E_{\boldsymbol{k}}+E_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}})^{2}}.

(S.109)

By taking the average over the angle between
\bm{k}
and
\bm{q}
, we finally arrive at the result

iU\gamma n\int\frac{k^{2}dk}{2\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{3(v_{F}q)^{3}}{32\Delta^{5}}+O(q^{4})\right).

(S.110)

Comparing this result with Eq. (
S.108
), we find

i\gamma nU\frac{v_{F}qf(q)}{4\sqrt{3}\Delta^{3}}=iU\gamma n\frac{3(v_{F}q)^{3}}{32\Delta^{5}}\Rightarrow f(q)=\frac{3\sqrt{3}(v_{F}q)^{2}}{8\Delta^{2}}\propto q^{2}.

(S.111)

This indicates that the correction corresponds to the diffusive mode. One can also show that the sign of the linear term does not influence the final result.

Appendix S4
Weak U(1) Symmetry Breaking

In this section, we consider the Lindbladian that breaks weak U(1) symmetry and reconsider the Ward-Takahashi identity and response to the gauge field. We perform strong U(1) transformation of the action and similarly obtain the Ward-Takahashi identity as

\displaystyle\pm[\delta(x-x_{1})\tau_{3}\langle\Psi_{\pm}(x_{1})\bar{\Psi}_{\pm}(x_{2})\rangle-\delta(x-x_{2})\langle\Psi_{\pm}(x_{1})\bar{\Psi}_{\pm}(x_{2})\rangle\tau_{3}]=\langle\Psi_{\pm}(x_{1})\bar{\Psi}_{\pm}(x_{2})\partial_{\mu}\bar{J}_{d}^{\mu}\rangle,

(S.112)

\displaystyle\pm[\delta(x-x_{1})\tau_{3}\langle\Psi_{\pm}(x_{1})\bar{\Psi}_{\mp}(x_{2})\rangle+\delta(x-x_{2})\langle\Psi_{\pm}(x_{1})\bar{\Psi}_{\mp}(x_{2})\rangle\tau_{3}]=\langle\Psi_{\pm}(x_{1})\bar{\Psi}_{\mp}(x_{2})\partial_{\mu}\bar{J}_{d}^{\mu}\rangle,

(S.113)

where the full current is given by

\bar{J}_{d}^{\mu}:=J_{c}^{\mu}+J_{\rm{wd}}^{\mu}.

(S.114)

Here
J_{c}^{\mu}
is defined in Eq. (
S.23
) and
J_{\rm{wd}}^{\mu}
is defined as

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{J}_{\rm{wd}}=i\gamma\left(\bar{L}_{\boldsymbol{r}-}\frac{\partial L_{\boldsymbol{r}+}}{\partial\theta(x)}+\frac{\partial\bar{L}_{\boldsymbol{r}-}}{\partial\theta(x)}L_{\boldsymbol{r}+}\right)-i\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(\frac{\partial(\bar{L}_{\boldsymbol{r}+}\partial L_{\boldsymbol{r}+})}{\partial\theta(x)}+\frac{\partial(\bar{L}_{\boldsymbol{r}-}\partial L_{\boldsymbol{r}-})}{\partial\theta(x)}\right),\ J_{\rm{wd}}^{0}=0.

(S.115)

Compared with Eq. (
S.24
), there emerges an additional term

\nabla\cdot\bm{J}_{\text{da}}:=-i\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(\frac{\partial(\bar{L}_{\boldsymbol{r}+}\partial L_{\boldsymbol{r}+})}{\partial\theta(x)}+\frac{\partial(\bar{L}_{\boldsymbol{r}-}\partial L_{\boldsymbol{r}-})}{\partial\theta(x)}\right).

(S.116)

In this case, if we perform the gauge transformation
A_{\mu}\to A_{\mu}+\partial_{\mu}\phi
, the perturbed current takes the form of

\displaystyle\delta J_{2}^{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2}\sum_{\alpha}\int dx^{\prime}\text{Tr}\langle\gamma_{\alpha}^{\mu}\Psi_{\alpha}(x)J_{\text{da}}^{\nu}(x^{\prime})\bar{\Psi}_{\alpha}(x)\rangle\partial_{\nu}^{\prime}\phi(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{\alpha}\int dx^{\prime}\text{Tr}\langle\gamma_{\alpha}^{\mu}\Psi_{\alpha}(x)\partial_{\nu}^{\prime}J_{\text{da}}^{\nu}(x^{\prime})\bar{\Psi}_{\alpha}(x)\rangle\phi(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{4}\sum_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}\int dx^{\prime}\text{Tr}\left\langle\gamma_{\alpha}^{\mu}\Psi_{\alpha}(x)\frac{\partial(\bar{L}_{\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}}\partial L_{\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}})}{\partial\theta(x^{\prime})}\bar{\Psi}_{\alpha}(x)\right\rangle\phi(x^{\prime}),

(S.117)

which breaks the gauge invariance. Therefore, the minimal condition for the gauge-invariant transport theory is weak U(1) symmetric Lindbladian.

Finally, we address the issue of an experimental signature of the weak U(1) symmetry of the Lindbladian since
J^{\mu}_{q}:=J^{\mu}_{+}-J^{\mu}_{-}
is not an observable. We here focus on the dynamics of a quantity

\displaystyle O_{N}(t):=\text{Tr}[N\rho(t)N\rho(t)]-\text{Tr}[N^{2}\rho(t)^{2}]=-\frac{1}{2}\langle\langle\rho|(N_{+}-N_{-})^{2}|\rho\rangle\rangle,

(S.118)

where
|\rho\rangle\rangle
is the vectorized density matrix defined by
|\rho\rangle\rangle:=\sum_{i,j}\rho_{ij}|i\rangle|j\rangle
for the density matrix
\rho=\sum_{i,j}\rho_{ij}|i\rangle\langle j|
. If the Lindbladian and the initial density matrix satisfy the weak U(1) symmetry, the quantity
O_{N}(t)
remains invariant in the dynamics. The conservation of the observable
O_{N}(t)
is associated with the equation of continuity of the weak U(1) symmetry since it depends only on
N_{+}-N_{-}
. In the main text, we have directly shown this point.

Let us now consider the dynamics of an observable
O_{N}(t)
in dissipative BCS theory
Yamamoto et al. 2021
, which is described by the action (
S.59
). Since there is no off-diagonal terms in the contour basis, it is consistent to use the time-dependent BCS ansatz:

|\Psi_{\text{BCS}}(t)\rangle=\prod_{\bm{k}}(u_{\bm{k}}(t)+v_{\bm{k}}(t)c_{\bm{k}\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-\bm{k}\downarrow}^{\dagger})|0\rangle,

(S.119)

where the initial condition is given by

u_{\bm{k}}(0)=\sqrt{\frac{E_{\bm{k}}+\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}}{2E_{\bm{k}}}},\ v_{\bm{k}}(0)=\sqrt{\frac{E_{\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}}{2E_{\bm{k}}}},\ E_{\bm{k}}=\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{2}+|\Delta|^{2}},

(S.120)

and the order parameter is

\Delta=-\frac{U_{c}}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}}\langle c_{-\bm{k}\downarrow}c_{\bm{k}\uparrow}\rangle=-\frac{U_{c}}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}}u_{\bm{k}}^{*}(t)v_{\bm{k}}(t).

(S.121)

Recall that
U_{c}:=U+i\gamma/2
is complex-valued. The dynamics of
u_{\bm{k}}
and
v_{\bm{k}}
is governed by

i\partial_{t}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{\bm{k}}(t)\\
v_{\bm{k}}(t)\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}&\Delta^{\ast}\\
\Delta&\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{\bm{k}}(t)\\
v_{\bm{k}}(t)\end{array}\right),

(S.122)

which preserves
|u_{\bm{k}}|^{2}+|v_{\bm{k}}|^{2}=1
. We can simplify the observable
O_{N}(t)
defined in Eq. (
S.118
) as

\displaystyle O_{N}(t)

\displaystyle=|\langle\Psi_{\text{BCS}}|N|\Psi_{\text{BCS}}\rangle|^{2}-\langle\Psi_{\text{BCS}}|N^{2}|\Psi_{\text{BCS}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(2\sum_{\bm{k}}|v_{\bm{k}}|^{2}\right)^{2}-\left(4\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}(\bm{k}^{\prime}\neq\bm{k})}|v_{\bm{k}}|^{2}|v_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}|^{2}+4\sum_{\bm{k}}|v_{\bm{k}}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=4\sum_{\bm{k}}|v_{\bm{k}}|^{4}-4\sum_{\bm{k}}|v_{\bm{k}}|^{2}

\displaystyle=-2N(t)+\sum_{\bm{k}}n_{\bm{k}}^{2},

(S.123)

where we use the relation
N(t)=\sum_{\bm{k}}n_{\bm{k}}=2\sum_{\bm{k}}|v_{\bm{k}}|^{2}
with
n_{\bm{k}}=2|v_{\bm{k}}|^{2}
being the number of particles with a momentum
\bm{k}

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{\text{BCS}}|N^{2}|\Psi_{\text{BCS}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Psi_{\text{BCS}}|N\sum_{\bm{k}}\left[2v_{\bm{k}}c_{\bm{k}\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-\bm{k}\downarrow}^{\dagger}\prod_{\bm{k}^{\prime}\neq\bm{k}}(u_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}+v_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}c_{\bm{k}^{\prime}\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-\bm{k}^{\prime}\downarrow}^{\dagger})\right]|0\rangle

\displaystyle=4\sum_{\bm{k}}|v_{\bm{k}}|^{2}+\langle\Psi_{\text{BCS}}|\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}(\bm{k}^{\prime}\neq\bm{k})}\left[(2v_{\bm{k}}c_{\bm{k}\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-\bm{k}\downarrow}^{\dagger})(2v_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}c_{\bm{k}^{\prime}\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-\bm{k}^{\prime}\downarrow}^{\dagger})\prod_{\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}\neq\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}(u_{\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}+v_{\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}c_{\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}\downarrow}^{\dagger})\right]|0\rangle

\displaystyle=2N(t)+4\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}(\bm{k}^{\prime}\neq\bm{k})}|v_{\bm{k}}|^{2}|v_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}|^{2}.

(S.124)

By using the relation
|u_{\bm{k}}|^{2}+|v_{\bm{k}}|^{2}=1

O_{N}(t)=-4\sum_{\bm{k}}|u_{\bm{k}}|^{2}|v_{\bm{k}}|^{2},

(S.125)

which remains negative until all the particles are lost into the environment. This result indicates that the mean-field theory fails in predicting the dynamics of the quantity

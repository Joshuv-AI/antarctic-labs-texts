Abstract
. Let G G be a finite group and let p p be a prime. In this paper, we prove a strengthened version of Brauer’s height zero conjecture for the principal p p -block of G G that takes the action of a certain group of Galois automorphisms into account. This answers a conjecture recently proposed by Malle, Moretó, Rizo and Schaeffer Fry. We then use this to obtain a structural result which can be seen as a Galois version of the Itô–Michler theorem.

Primary 20C15

1.
Introduction

Many local-global conjectures in the representation theory of finite groups have been generalized to take Galois automorphisms into account. For example, the McKay conjecture, recently proven by Cabanes and Späth in
[
1
]
, received such a generalization in
[
15
]
.

Another instance of this phenomenon is the Brauer Height Zero Conjecture, which was also recently proven in
[
14
]
. The principal block case of this conjecture asserts that if
p
is a prime and
G
is a finite group then all irreducible characters in the principal
p
-block of
G
have
p^{\prime}
-degree if and only if
G
has an abelian Sylow
p
-subgroup.

In
[
13
]
, the authors considered the action of a specific automorphism
\sigma
on the characters of the principal
2
-block of a finite group. In that context, they showed that it sufficed to consider the set of
\sigma
-invariant irreducible characters in the statement of Brauer’s Height Zero Conjecture, for
p=2
.

Let
p
be a prime number and let
\mathbb{Q}^{\operatorname{ab}}
be the maximal abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. Then, we write
\mathcal{J}
to denote the set of elements of order
p
of the group
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{\operatorname{ab}}/\mathbb{Q})
that fix all
p
-power roots of unity. In
[
12
]
, it was shown that, in fact, it sufficed to consider the set of
\mathcal{J}
-invariant characters in the statement of Brauer’s Height Zero Conjecture, provided the group
G
does not contain a certain set of composition factors for specific primes
p
.

In this paper, we remove this restriction on the composition factors and prove the theorem below, which is
[
12
, Conjecture 1]
. In the following,
B_{0}(G)
denotes the principal
p
-block of
G
and
{\operatorname{Irr}}_{\mathcal{J}}(G)
, the set of irreducible complex characters of
G
that are invariant under
\mathcal{J}
.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a finite group, let
p
be a prime number and let
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
. Suppose that the elements in
{\rm Irr}_{\mathcal{J}}(B_{0}(G))
are all of
p^{\prime}
-degree. Then
P
is abelian.

In order to do so, we use many results of
[
12
]
, especially those relying on the Classification of Finite Simple Groups (CFSG). However we also employ substantially different techniques from those of
[
12
]
. In fact, the approach in
[
12
]
, as that in
[
13
]
, went through a Galois version of the Itô–Michler theorem, which was known to have exceptions with our set of Galois automorphisms
\mathcal{J}
.

Our approach now relies more on arguments from
[
11
]
. In particular, we use deep classification theorems on the structure of primitive permutation groups. As a consequence of Theorem A, we are also able to prove the following, which is probably the closest one can get to a Galois version of Itô–Michler.

Theorem B
.

Let
G
be a finite group, with
p
a prime and suppose that
{\operatorname{Irr}}_{\mathcal{J}}(G)\subseteq{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
.
Then,
{\mathbf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(G)={\mathbf{O}}_{p}(G)\times K
, where
K\unlhd G
,
{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(K)
is solvable and
K/{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(K)
is a direct product of non-abelian simple groups of order divisible by
p
, whose
\mathcal{J}
-invariant irreducible characters are all of
p^{\prime}
-degree.

We remark that Theorem B generalizes the main result of
[
5
]
. Unfortunately, as we discuss in Section 4, it appears that the proof in
[
5
]
is not correct.

One consequence of this latter classification which will be of particular use to us is the following variation of

, Lemma 3.4]

Lemma 6
.

Let
p
be prime number and let
H
be a primitive subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}

Now suppose we are in case (2).. Then, we may compute the character tables of the two groups explicitly using GAP and we see that

-invariant) character of degree divisible by

-invariant. Finally, for case (3),

and

is the set of

-invariant.

Next, we state one property of simple groups which will be of fundamental importance to us.

Lemma 7
.

Let
S
be a non-abelian finite simple group and let
p
be an odd prime dividing
|S|
. Then, there exists
1_{S}\neq\alpha\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{\Omega}(B_{0}(S))
of
p^{\prime}
-degree.

Proof.

Suppose first that

. By
[
9

,
3\mid|{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(S))|

is a

|{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime},\Omega}(B_{0}(S))|\equiv|{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(S))|\pmod{3}

-degree and
\Omega
-invariant) there are at least two non-trivial characters in
{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime},\Omega}(B_{0}(S))
. We may thus assume

.

For alternating groups, there is nothing to prove, since every irreducible character is
\Omega
-invariant (as
\Omega
is a
p
-group,

and characters of

have at most

For sporadic groups (and the Tits simple group

that, if

, then there exists a rational character of

[

.

Lemma 9

Let

\theta
is

-invariant and that

Let

To finish off this section, the following allows us to deal with the case of groups with a semisimple minimal normal subgroup which is not simple.

Lemma 10

Let
G
be a finite group and let

We will tackle both the general case and the case where

Suppose, first, that this action is not

3.
Proof of Theorem A

We are now ready to prove Theorem A, which we restate for convenience.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a finite group, let
p
be a prime number and let
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
. Suppose that the elements in
{\rm Irr}_{\Omega}(B_{0}(G))
are all of
p^{\prime}
-degree. Then
P
is abelian.

Proof.

We break the proof down into a series of steps.

Step
0
. We may assume
{\mathbf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(G)=G
and
{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)=1
. Moreover,
if
1<N\lhd G
,
G/N
has abelian Sylow
p
-subgroups. Also,
G
has a unique minimal normal subgroup
N
and
p\neq 2
.

First, suppose
{\mathbf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(G)<G
. If there exists
\theta\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{\Omega}(B_{0}({\mathbf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(G)))
of degree divisible by
p
, then, by Lemma
8
, we could find
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{\Omega}(B_{0}(G)\mid\theta)
. Since
\theta(1)
divides
\chi(1)
,
\chi(1)_{p}>1
, which goes against our hypothesis. Thus,
{\mathbf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(G)
also fulfills our hypothesis, meaning it has abelian Sylow
p
-subgroups by induction. As
[G:{\mathbf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(G)]
is not divisible by
p
, this implies
G
has abelian Sylow
p
-subgroups and we are done.

Now, let
1\neq N\lhd G
. Then,
{\operatorname{Irr}}_{\Omega}(B_{0}(G/N))\subseteq{\operatorname{Irr}}_{\Omega}(B_{0}(G))
, meaning
G/N
also satisfies the hypothesis. By induction, it has abelian Sylow
p
-subgroups. In particular, assume
{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)>1
. Then,
G/{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
has abelian Sylow
p
-subgroups and if
P\in{\operatorname{Syl}}_{p}(G)
, then
P\cong P{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)/{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)\in{\operatorname{Syl}}_{p}(G/{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(G))
, meaning
P
is also abelian, as we wanted to prove.

Finally, let
M,N
be distinct minimal normal subgroups of
G
. Then, the diagonal map composed with the canonical projections gives an embedding of
G
into
G/N\times G/M
. By our previous paragraph, both of these have abelian Sylow
p
-subgroups. Since the Sylow
p
-subgroups of
G
embed into those of
G/N\times G/M
, they are abelian as well. The fact that we can assume
p\neq 2
follows from
[
12
, Theorem 2]
.

Step 1. Let
N
be the unique minimal normal subgroup of
G
. We may assume that
N
is not semisimple; in particular,
{\mathbf{O}}_{p}(G)>1
.

Suppose
N=S_{1}\times\cdots\times S_{t}
is a product of isomorphic non-abelian simple groups of order divisible by
p
, which are transitively permuted by
G
-conjugation. If
t>1
, then the result follows by Step 0 and Lemma
10
. Now, if
t=1
, by the uniqueness of
N=S
,
\mathbf{C}_{G}(S)=1
and
G
is an almost simple group with socle
S
, without proper normal subgroups of
p^{\prime}
-index. Then, the result holds by
[
12
, Theorem 2.17]
.

Step 2. If
N\subseteq\mathbf{Z}(G)
, then we are done.

Suppose that
N\subseteq\mathbf{Z}(G)
(so
|N|=p
). Then,
{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(G/N)=N/N
. Otherwise, if
N\subsetneqq K
is such that
K/N={\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(G/N)
, then
K=XN
, by the Schur–Zassenhaus Theorem. But the centrality of
N
implies
X\unlhd K
, and thus
X
is characteristic in
K
, hence normal in
G
, contradicting
N
being the unique minimal normal subgroup of
G
.

Since
G/N
has abelian Sylow

14
, we have that

1<\mathbf{N}_{G}(P)\cap M=\mathbf{C}_{M}(P)\leq\mathbf{C}_{M}(HM)=M\cap\mathbf{Z}(HM).

We claim that
\mathbf{Z}(HM)
is normal in
G
. Indeed, by induction
G/M={\mathbf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(G/M)={\mathbf{O}}_{p}(G/M)\times K_{1}/M
. Notice that
{\mathbf{O}}_{p}(G/M)\subseteq X/M
and therefore
{\mathbf{O}}_{p}(G/M)\subseteq HM/M
.

Notice that
N\subseteq K_{1}
, and hence
{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(K_{1})=N
. Then
K_{1}/N
is a (possibly empty) direct product of non-abelian simple groups of order divisible by
p
. Also notice that
K_{1}={\mathbf{O}}^{p}(G)
, as
K_{1}={\mathbf{O}}^{p}(K_{1})
and
|G:K_{1}|
is a power of
p
. Since the same is true of
Y
, it follows that
Y=K_{1}
.

Therefore, we conclude that
HM/M={\mathbf{O}}_{p}(G/M)
, by comparing orders. Hence
HM
is normal in
G
, and so is
\mathbf{Z}(HM)
, as wanted. Since
M\cap\mathbf{Z}(HM)>1
, we conclude that
M\subseteq\mathbf{Z}(HM)
. Hence
H
is normal in
HM
and therefore characteristic, so it is normal in
G
. As
{\mathbf{O}}_{p}(G)=1
,
H=1
and
G=Y
, a contradiction to
G
being a counterexample.

Step 4. Final contradiction.

By Steps 2 and 3,
M
is the product of non-abelian simple groups of order not divisible by
p
, transitively permuted by
G
-conjugation. Write
M=S_{1}\times\cdots\times S_{k}
. By Lemma
10
,
k=1
, meaning
M=S
, a non-abelian simple group of
p^{\prime}
-order. In particular,
G/\mathbf{C}_{G}(S)
is an almost simple group with socle
S\mathbf{C}_{G}(S)/\mathbf{C}_{G}(S)\cong S
.

Now,
G/S\mathbf{C}_{G}(S)\cong\frac{G/\mathbf{C}_{G}(S)}{S\mathbf{C}_{G}(S)/\mathbf{C}_{G}(S)}
is solvable by Schreier’s conjecture. Write
W/S={\mathbf{O}}_{p}(G/S)
, and notice that, by induction
G/S=W/S\times K_{1}/S
for some
K_{1}
such that
\frac{K_{1}/S}{{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(K_{1}/S)}
is a product of non-abelian simple groups. Let
L=W\mathbf{C}_{G}(S)
. Then,
G/L
is solvable and hence
K_{1}/(K_{1}\cap L)
is solvable. Thus,
K_{1}=(K_{1}\cap L)N
and we have that
K_{1}/(K_{1}\cap L)
is a
p^{\prime}
-group. But since
G=WK_{1}
, we have that
G=LK_{1}
and hence
G/L\cong K_{1}/(K_{1}\cap L)
is a
p^{\prime}
-group. As
{\mathbf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(G)=G
, we obtain that
G=L
and hence
G/S\mathbf{C}_{G}(S)\cong W/(W\cap S\mathbf{C}_{G}(S))
is a
p
-group.

By the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 2.8]
, if
G\neq S\mathbf{C}_{G}(S)
, since
p
does not divide
|S|
, there exists
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{\Omega}(G/\mathbf{C}_{G}(S))
of degree divisible by
p
, which goes against our hypothesis. Thus,
G=S\mathbf{C}_{G}(S)
. But this is a contradiction, as
S\cap\mathbf{C}_{G}(S)=1
and
|S|
is not divisible by
p
.
∎

Lemma 16
.

Let
G
be a finite group with
p
a prime and suppose that
{\operatorname{Irr}}_{\Omega}(G)\subseteq{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
. Then,
{\mathbf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(G)={\mathbf{O}}_{p}(G)\times K
, where
K\unlhd G
is such that
K/{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(K)
is a (possibly empty) direct product of non-abelian finite simple groups of order divisible by
p
without
\Omega
-invariant characters of degree divisible by
p
.

Proof.

By Lemma
15
, we may write
{\mathbf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(G)={\mathbf{O}}_{p}(G)\times K
where
K/{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(K)
is a direct product of non-abelian finite simple groups of order divisible by
p
. If these groups have an
\Omega
-invariant character of degree divisible by
p
, then so does
K/{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(K)
, and hence, so does
{\mathbf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(G)/{\mathbf{O}}_{p}(G)
. This means that
{\mathbf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(G)
has an
\Omega
-invariant character of degree divisible by
p
and, by Lemma
8
, so does
G
, a contradiction.
∎

Finally, we get the complete proof of Theorem B, which we restate below.

Theorem B
.

Let
G
be a finite group, with
p
a prime and suppose that
{\operatorname{Irr}}_{\Omega}(G)\subseteq{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
.
Then,
{\mathbf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(G)={\mathbf{O}}_{p}(G)\times K
, where
K\unlhd G
,
{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(K)
is solvable and
K/{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(K)
is a (possibly empty) direct product of non-abelian simple groups of order divisible by
p
without
\Omega
-invariant characters of degree divisible by
p
.

Proof.

By what we did before in Lemma
15
and Lemma
16
,
{\mathbf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(G)={\mathbf{O}}_{p}(G)\times Y
,
Y/N
is a direct product of non-abelian finite simple groups of order divisible by
p
without
\Omega
-invariant characters of degree divisible by
p
, where
N={\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(G))
, and
{\mathbf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(G)=XY
, where
X={\mathbf{O}}_{p}(G)N
. Also,
Y/N={\mathbf{O}}^{p}({\mathbf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(G)/N)
, meaning
Y/N
is normal in
G/N
; hence,
Y
is normal in
G
.

All that is left is to show that
{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(Y)
is solvable. In view of that, we may assume
p\neq 2
by the Feit–Thompson theorem. If
{\mathbf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(G)\neq G
, then, by Lemma
8
, we get the desired result by induction. Hence, we may assume
G={\mathbf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(G)
. Along the same lines, as
G/{\mathbf{O}}_{p}(G)\cong Y
, if
Y
had an
\Omega
-invariant character of degree divisible by
p
, so would
G
. Thus,
Y
also satisfies our hypothesis.

Notice that
{\mathbf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(Y)=Y
, since
Y\cong G/{\mathbf{O}}_{p}(G)
and
{\mathbf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(G)=G
. Consequently, if
Y\neq G
, by induction, we get that
{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(Y)
is solvable, which is our desired result. So we may assume
Y=G
. In this situation,
{\mathbf{O}}_{p}(G)=1
,
N={\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
and
G/N
is a direct product of non-abelian simple groups of order divisible by
p
whose
\Omega
-invariant characters are of
p^{\prime}
-degree. Let
R=\mathbf{R}(N)
be the solvable radical of
N
and suppose
R\neq 1
. Then, by induction on
G/R
, we get that
{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(G/R)=N/R
is solvable, meaning so, too, is
N
. Thus, we may assume
R=1
.

Let
M
be a minimal normal subgroup of
G
contained in
N
. By the previous paragraph,
M=S_{1}\times\cdots\times S_{k}
, a product of non-abelian simple groups transitively permuted by
G
-conjugation and of order not divisible by
p
. If
k>1
, then the result follows by Lemma
10
. Thus, we may assume
k=1
. Then,
M=S
is a non-abelian simple group and
G/\mathbf{C}_{G}(S)
is an almost simple group with socle isomorphic to
S
. By the Schreier Conjecture,
G/S\mathbf{C}_{G}(S)
is solvable. In particular, due to the structure of
G
,
|G:S\mathbf{C}_{G}(S)|_{p}=1
. Consequently,
G=S\mathbf{C}_{G}(S)
as
{\mathbf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(G)=G
. But this is impossible, as
p\nmid|S|
and
S
is a quotient of
G
.
∎

Notice how the conclusion of Theorem B fails if one considers only the
\Omega
-invariant characters in the principal block of
G
. A small counterexample is
\mathfrak{S}_{3}
, whose characters in the principal
2
-block are rational and linear, and also
{\mathbf{O}}_{2}(\mathfrak{S}_{3})=1,{\mathbf{O}}^{2^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{S}_{3})=\mathfrak{S}_{3}
; nevertheless, it is not a
2^{\prime}
-group, as would be implied by the theorem.

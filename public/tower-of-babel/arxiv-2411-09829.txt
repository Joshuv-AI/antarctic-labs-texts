Scalar products and Left LCD codes
Nabil Bennenni, André Leroy

Abstract
. In this article, we introduce new scalar products over finite rings via additive isomorphisms. This allows us to define new notions of right (respectively left) orthogonal codes, that are not necessarily linear. This leads to definitions of right (resp. left) dual codes and left LCD codes similar to the classical LCD codes. Furthermore, we provide necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of these codes.



Key words
: Scalar products, right and left dual codes, left-LCD codes.

MSC 2020
: 94B05, 16S50.

1.
Introduction

Linear complementary dual codes (LCD) were initially introduced by Massey
[
4
]
in 1992, with the intention of using them for the so-called two-user binary adder channel. Massey proved that the use of an LCD code solves some of the decodability difficulties. LCD codes play an important role in practical applications in particular, against side-channel and fault injection attacks; for more details cf.
[
1
]
.

In this paper, we focus on the class of left LCD codes (left Complementary Dual codes) which is important because of their connection with quantum error correction and quantum computing
[
5
]
.

Skew triangular matrix rings presented in
[
3
]
gave us the opportunity to create new dot products, which is extremely beneficial for creating left and right dual codes different from the classic dual codes. In addition, we stipulated that the codes must be left-LCD codes (left-linear Complementary Dual codes).

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we define a new product on a matrix ring
M_{n}(R)
via the action of an automorphism
\theta\in Aut(R)
. This was inspired by
[
3
]
where a similar construction was defined on upper triangular matrix rings.
We also introduce new dot products and their left and right orthogonality relations. We characterize self-dual codes with respect to this new dot product. We study the connections between some subsets of
R^{n}
arising from these definitions.
In Section 3, we study a necessary and sufficient condition for a left linear code to be a left LCD code (left linear Complementary Dual codes). We give examples of the best known left LCD codes obtained in this way using
\mathbb{F}_{4}
,
\mathbb{F}_{8}
,
\mathbb{F}_{9}
or
\mathbb{F}_{16}
for
R
and the Frobenius automorphism for
\theta
.

2.
New products Via additive isomorphisms

We will define a new product on a matrix ring
M_{n}(R)
via the action of an automorphism
\theta\in Aut(R)
. Let us start with a very general statement.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
R,+,.
be a ring and
S,+
be an additive, abelian group such that
\varphi:R,+\longrightarrow S,+
is an isomorphism of additive groups. Then the following multiplication
*
gives
S
a ring structure.

For\;s_{1},s_{2}\in S,\;s_{1}*s_{2}=\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(s_{1})\varphi^{-1}(s_{2}))

The map
\varphi:R\rightarrow S
is therefore a ring isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
R,+,.
be a ring and
S,+
be an additive (=abelian) group such that
\varphi:R,+\longrightarrow S,+
is an isomorphism of additive groups. By definition, we have
s_{1}*s_{2}=\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(s_{1})\varphi^{-1}(s_{2})).
We compute

\displaystyle s_{1}*(s_{2}*s_{3})

\displaystyle=\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(s_{1})\varphi^{-1}(s_{2}*s_{3}))

\displaystyle=\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(s_{1})\varphi^{-1}(\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(s_{2})\varphi^{-1}(s_{3}))))

\displaystyle=\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(s_{1})(\varphi^{-1}(s_{2})\varphi^{-1}(s_{3})))

\displaystyle=\varphi((\varphi^{-1}(s_{1})\varphi^{-1}(s_{2}))\varphi^{-1}(s_{3}))

\displaystyle=(s_{1}*s_{2})*s_{3}.

\displaystyle s_{1}*(s_{2}+s_{3})

\displaystyle=\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(s_{1})\varphi^{-1}(s_{2}+s_{3}))

\displaystyle=\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(s_{1})\varphi^{-1}(s_{2})+\varphi^{-1}(s_{1})\varphi^{-1}(s_{3}))

\displaystyle=\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(s_{1})\varphi^{-1}(s_{2}))+\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(s_{1})\varphi^{-1}(s_{3}))

\displaystyle=s_{1}*s_{2}+s_{1}*s_{3}.

\displaystyle s_{1}*\varphi(1_{R})

\displaystyle=\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(s_{1})\varphi^{-1}(\varphi(1)))

\displaystyle=\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(s_{1})1)=s_{1}.

\displaystyle\varphi(r_{1}r_{2})

\displaystyle=\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(s_{1})\varphi^{-1}(s_{2}))

\displaystyle=s_{1}*s_{2}=\varphi(r_{1})\varphi(r_{2}).

∎

Before coming to the main focus of our paper, let us apply this lemma and give an example:

We can apply this lemma to a matrix ring
M_{n}(R)
and an automorphism
\theta
of
R,+

(not necessarily a ring automorphism). We get an additive isomorphism, denoted
\varphi:M_{n}(R)\longrightarrow M_{n}(R)

\varphi(A)_{ij}=\theta^{i-1}(A_{ij})

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\theta\in Aut(R)
, and
\varphi
the map defined above. Then the product
*
defined on
M_{n}(R),+
satisfies, for
A=(a_{ij})
and

(A*B)_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{ik}\theta^{i-k}(b_{kj})

Proof.

For
1\leq i,j\leq n
, we compute:

\displaystyle(A*B)_{ij}=\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(A)\varphi^{-1}(B))_{ij}

\displaystyle=\theta^{i-1}((\varphi^{-1}(A)\varphi^{-1}(B))_{ij})

\displaystyle=\theta^{i-1}(\sum_{k}\varphi^{-1}(A)_{ik}\varphi^{-1}(B)_{kj})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\theta^{i-1}(\theta^{1-i}(A_{ik})\theta^{1-k}(B_{kj}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}A_{ik}\theta^{i-k}(B_{kj}).

This concludes the proof. ∎

In order to introduce a scalar product related to the new product of matrices, we are forced to generalize the above construction as follows.

Let
R
be a ring, and
for any
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
, suppose we have an additive isomorphism

\varphi_{n,k}:M_{n,k}(R),+\longrightarrow M_{n,k}(R),+.

Now, if
A\in M_{n,k}(R)
and
B\in M_{k,l}(R)
we define

(a)

A\circ B=\varphi_{n,l}^{-1}(\varphi_{n,k}(A)\varphi_{k,l}(B))
.

(b)

A*B=\varphi_{n,l}(\varphi_{n,k}^{-1}(A)\varphi_{k,l}^{-1}(B))
.

There are many ways of defining the additive maps
\varphi_{n,k}
in general. For instance, we could use any permutation of the
nk
entries of the matrices in
M_{n,k}(R)
.
In this paper, we will consider an automorphism
\theta
of the ring
R
and construct an additive map
\varphi_{n,k}
as follows:

\varphi_{n,k}(A)_{ij}=(\theta^{i-1}(a_{i,j}))\quad{\rm where}\;\;A=(a_{i,j})\in M_{n,k}(R).

With these notations we have:

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
R
be a ring,
A\in M_{n,k}(R),B\in M_{k,l}(R)
and
\theta\in Aut(R)
. With the above notations we have:

(1)

(A\circ B)_{i,j}=\sum_{r}A_{ir}\theta^{r-i}(B_{r,j})

(2)

(A*B)_{i,j}=\sum_{r}A_{ir}\theta^{i-r}(B_{r,j}).

(3)

If
n=k
the map
\varphi:M_{n}(R),+,.\longrightarrow M_{n}(R),+,*
defined by
\varphi((A_{ij}))=((\theta^{i-1})(A_{ij}))
is a ring homomorphism.

In (1) and (2) above, these products are additive on both variables but are linear only on the left.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle(A\circ B)_{ij}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{n,l}^{-1}((\varphi_{n,k}(A)\varphi_{k,l}(B)))_{ij}

\displaystyle=\theta^{1-i}(\sum_{s}\varphi_{n,k}(A)_{is}\varphi_{kl}(B)_{sj})

\displaystyle=\theta^{1-i}(\sum_{s}\theta^{i-1}(A_{is})\theta^{s-1}(B_{sj}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}A_{is}\theta^{s-i}(B_{sj}).

A similar computation gives the second formula.

(3) is left to the reader.
∎

Let us remark that in the above definitions of the maps
\varphi_{n,k}
the indices
n,k
just fix the size of the matrices we are working with. In the sequel we will just write
\varphi
and drop the indices.

We denote by
R^{n}
the space of rows
M_{1,n}(R)
and by
{}^{n}R
the space of columns
M_{n,1}(R)
. For
x\in R^{n}
, we denote

the transpose of

.
If we consider
\varphi_{1,n}
and
\varphi_{n,1}
,
\circ
and
*
give two scalar products i.e. biadditive maps from

into

. More explicitly we have, for

we have:

a\circ b^{t}=\varphi_{1,1}^{-1}(\varphi_{1,n}(a)\varphi_{n,1}(b^{t}))\quad{\rm and}\quad a*b^{t}=\varphi_{1,1}(\varphi_{1,n}^{-1}(a)\varphi_{n,1}^{-1}(b^{t})).

In general, these scalar products are neither
R
-linear nor symmetric in the sense that, in general, for

a\circ b^{t}\neq b\circ a^{t}
and

Similarly, the orthogonalities for both the operations

are different. Let

we define

{}^{\perp*}C=\{\ x\in R^{n}\mid x*c^{t}=0,\;\forall c\in C\}\quad{\rm and}\quad C^{*\perp}=\{x\in R^{n}\mid c*x^{t}=0,\;\forall c\in C\}\\

{}^{\perp\circ}C=\{\ x\in R^{n}\mid x\circ c^{t}=0,\;\forall c\in C\}\quad{\rm and}\quad C^{\circ\perp}=\{x\in R^{n}\mid c\circ x^{t}=0,\;\forall c\in C\}

3.
Left LCD codes and Applications

In this section, we
fix
\theta\in Aut(R)
. We will use
the
*
multiplication for matrices as defined
in Lemma
2.8
. Analogues of the results that we will obtain are also true for the
\circ
multiplication. We
give the definition of a
*
-LCD code and a necessary
and sufficient condition for a code is a
*
-LCD code.

Definition 3.1
.

A left linear code
C
is
*
-LCD if
C\cap C^{*\bot}=\{0\}

(C\cap^{\perp*}C=\{0\})
.

If
C
is a left linear code, we can define it via a set of row bases
\{c_{1},\dots,c_{k}\}\subseteq R^{n}
. So if
H
is the
k\times n
matrix defined by these vectors, we have that
C=R^{k}H
. Since the map
\varphi
is a bijection, we can also define the code
C
by
C=R^{k}*G
for a matrix
G\in M_{k,n}(R)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring,
y\in R^{k}
and
G\in M_{k,n}(R)
, we have

(y*G)^{t}=\varphi(G)^{t}*\varphi^{-1}(y^{t}).

Proof.

We compute

(y*G)^{t}=(y\varphi(G))^{t}=\varphi(G)^{t}y^{t}=\varphi(G)^{t}\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(y^{t}))\\
=\varphi(G)^{t}*\varphi^{-1}(y^{t})
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
R
be a commutative field and
G\in M_{k,n}(R)
. The code
C=R^{k}*G
is a left *-LCD code if and only if

G*\varphi(G)^{t}\in M_{n}(R)
is invertible.

Proof.

Assume that the matrix
G*\varphi(G)^{t}\in M_{n}(R)
is invertible, and let
mathbf{c}\in C\cap^{\perp,*}C
. There exists
u\in R^{k}
such that
mathbf{c}=u*G
. Since
mathbf{c}\in^{\perp,*}C
, we have that for every
y\in R^{k}
,
mathbf{c}*(y*G)^{t}=0
. We thus get that
0=(u*G)*(y*G)^{t}
. The above lemma then gives

0=u*G*\varphi(G)^{t}*\varphi^{-1}(y^{t})
. Since
y\in R^{k}
, our assumption gives that

u=0
and hence
c=0
.
Conversely, let us show that if
C\cap^{\perp,*}C=\{0\}
then
G*\varphi(G)^{t}
is invertible. Assume to the contrary that
G*\varphi(G)^{t}
is not invertible and let
v\in R^{k}
be such that
v*G*\varphi(G)^{t}=0
.
For any
e=e^{\prime}*G\in C
, we have
v*G*e^{t}=v*G*(e^{\prime}*G)^{t}*=v*G*\varphi(G)^{t}*\varphi^{-1}(e^{\prime t})=0
. We thus have that
v*G\in C\cap^{\perp,*}C
, a contradiction. ∎

we have to use a right linear code

, i.e.
C=HR^{k}

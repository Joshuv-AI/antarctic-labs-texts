Assessment of Oral Health-Related Quality of Life in Surgically Treated Oral Cancer Patients after Prosthetic Rehabilitation
Authors: Rakhi Kumari, Nikita Raman, Amit Kumar, Humaira Jabeen Tamanna, Anjali, Praveen Gangadharappa
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Surgical removal of oral cancer results in tissue abnormalities, deformities, and reduced mobility; these problems can lead to esthetic disturbance, dysphagia, dysarthria, and masticatory dysfunction affecting oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL).Aim:Research was conducted to evaluate OHRQoL in surgically treated oral cancer patients after prosthetic rehabilitation.Methods and Materials:A total of 120 patients who underwent surgical resection for oral cancer were assessed in this research. Two dentists who were skilled maxillofacial prosthetists handled the maxillofacial prosthesis therapy. The Oral Health Impact Profile (OHIP), which consists of seven subscales, was incorporated to evaluate OHRQoL.Results:The overall OHIP score after prosthodontic and endodontic treatment was significantly lower as compared to pre-treatment OHIP score indicating improvement in overall OHRQoL in surgically treated oral cancer patients.Conclusion:There is a significant improvement in OHRQoL in surgically managed patients of oral cancer after prosthetic rehabilitation.

BACKGROUND

Patients who underwent radiotherapy, surgery, or chemotherapy for cancer may experience significant functional, mental side effects as a result of their treatment.[12] Surgical removal of oral cancer results in tissue abnormalities, deformities, and reduced mobility; these problems can lead to esthetic disturbance, dysphagia, dysarthria, and masticatory dysfunction.[34] Patients’ lives are greatly impacted by dysfunctions that arise during oral cancer resection as oral function is connected to many other issues. Specifically, eating and speaking are severely impacted, which lowers self-worth and independence.[45] As previously mentioned, a variety of disruptions brought on by treatment for oral cancer result in a decline in quality of life (QoL). Enhancing QoL needs to be the ultimate therapeutic objective, as it can hold equal significance in curing cancer.[234]

As a result, the study’s main focus is QoL. QoL is a notion that is assessed by looking at people from a social and psychological perspective. This type of evaluation involves assessing pain and discomfort, psychological, social, and functional aspects—all of which are influenced by oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL). Following surgery, the faulty area is rebuilt to enhance function.[34] Furthermore, it has been demonstrated that after maxillary area excision, OHRQoL changes in response to better oral function. In order to achieve adequate esthetics and reinstate the function of the oral cavity from where surgical removal of tissue took place, prosthodontic appliances are typically employed to reconstruct defective areas.[45]

This research was performed to assess OHRQoL in surgically treated oral cancer patients after prosthetic rehabilitation.

METHODS AND MATERIALS

A total of 120 patients who underwent surgical resection for oral cancer were assessed in this research. Information regarding study participants, such as gender, years of age, location of defects in the oral cavity after surgery, and occlusion, was tabulated. The assessment of occlusion was conducted with the Eichner classification.[345]

A total of 120 oral cancer patients who underwent surgical therapy were divided into two categories on the basis of locations of defects after surgery.

Category 1: Maxillary defects (n = 60)

Category 2: Mandibular defects, and oral and tongue defects (n = 60).

Skilled maxillofacial prosthetists handled the maxillofacial prosthesis therapy. One endodontist conducted the endodontic procedures. Oral function was assessed before the implantation of a maxillofacial prosthesis and 1 month following the prosthesis’ final modifications. The Sato method was used to evaluate oral functioning, including masticatory activity, and the swallowing ability score—which consists of the Dysphagia Score and the MTF “(method of food intake, time required for food intake, and consistency of food that can be ingested)” score—was used to assess swallowing function.[123]

Additionally, the assessment for fluency of speech involving pronunciation of 25 monosyllables was used to assess articulatory function. Furthermore, the Oral Health Impact Profile (OHIP), which consists of seven subscales, was used to assess OHRQoL.[234] The patients were audio-recorded as they read out each of the 25 suggested monosyllables on the speech fluency test list. Of 100 monosyllables, those that are commonly used in daily speech are more likely to have articulation difficulties.

Statistical analysis

The data were recorded in an MS Excel sheet and fed in the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) software version 22 for statistical scrutiny. The statistical assessment incorporated for analysis was the Wilcoxon signed-rank statistical test that compared scores of before prosthetic therapy and after therapy. P value ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

It was observed that there was an improvement in masticatory function in patients of surgically managed patients with maxillary defects after prosthetic rehabilitation (P = 0.001). There was also improvement in the activity of mastication in the study participants with discrepancy in the mandible, discrepancy of the floor of the mouth, and discrepancy of tongue; however, the change was non-significant statistically (P = 0.875) [Table 1].

DISCUSSION

Enhancing OHRQoL after cancer-related resection is believed as outcome of achieving adequate enhancement in functioning of the oral cavity. Prostheses fabricated for the oral region have been shown to improve OHRQoL, articulatory function, and swallowing activity, especially after maxillary amputation; however, these outcomes rely on factors, such as radiation treatment history and defect dimension.[67]

In our study, there was an improvement in masticatory function in patients of surgically managed patients with maxillary defects after prosthetic and endodontic rehabilitation (P = 0.001).

The findings of this study are well-supported by findings of other research by Hagio M et al.[1] and Endo N et al.[2]

The overall OHIP score after treatment was significantly lower as compared to pre-treatment OHIP score in both maxillary defects and mandibular defects, oral defects, and tongue defects, indicating improvement in overall OHRQoL in surgically treated cancer patients with maxillary defects and mandibular defects, oral defects, and tongue defects.

The findings of this study are also supported by findings of other research, such as Logemann J et al.[3] and Vero N et al.[4] Less number of researches have examined the outcome of prostheses fabricated for the oral region on OHRQoL, despite a wealth of evidence on their improvement of oral function.

CONCLUSION

There is a significant improvement in OHRQoL in surgically managed patients of oral cancer after prosthetic rehabilitation.

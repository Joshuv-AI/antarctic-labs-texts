Assessing the hepatic and renal parameters of breast cancer patient blood samples before and after chemotherapy
Authors: Ghaith B Heilat, Tahani Alwidyan, Amjad Z Alrosan, Aseel O Rataan, Khaled Alrosan, Rehaf B Ahmad, Kinda R Al-Kammash
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBreast cancer (BC) is the most common malignancy in women, with chemotherapy as a fundamental treatment despite its potential to cause hepatotoxicity and nephrotoxicity. Although routine assessment of organ function is customary, the predictive value of composite liver-kidney biomarkers for post-chemotherapy survival remains inadequately investigated. This retrospective study sought to assess the influence of chemotherapy on hepatic and renal parameters in 132 BC patients treated at King Abdullah University Hospital (KAUH) in Jordan from January 2011 to April 2025, with a particular focus on the prognostic relevance of the Albumin-Creatinine Ratio (ACR), Alkaline Phosphatase-to-Creatinine Ratio (ALP/Cr), and Albumin-Bilirubin (ALBI) score. Liver and kidney function tests were evaluated pre- and post-four chemotherapy cycles utilizing Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Kaplan-Meier survival curves, and Cox proportional hazards regression. Post-treatment analysis demonstrated a moderate yet statistically significant reduction in total protein (p = 0.035) and serum creatinine (p = 0.019). Significantly, whereas routine enzymes (ALT, AST, ALP) exhibited no notable alterations, the composite biomarkers ACR and ALP/Cr ratio demonstrated substantial increases post-treatment (p = 0.036 and p = 0.030, respectively). Using ROC-derived cut-offs, the survival analysis revealed that patients with a low post-treatment ACR (<1045) had significantly longer overall survival (OS) than those in the high-ACR group (8.14 vs. 2.35 years; p = 0.009). Further, a lower post-treatment ALP/Cr ratio (<1491.7) was associated with longer survival (8.94 vs. 6.12 years; p = 0.017). These data suggest that, whereas individual liver and kidney indicators were relatively stable, composite biomarkers—specifically ACR and ALP/Cr—were more susceptible to treatment-induced physiological stress. Thus, these ratios serve as crucial indicators of OS, suggesting that incorporating hepatic and renal evaluations into composite markers provides greater predictive value for monitoring BC patients than standalone organ function tests.Keywords: Breast cancer, Chemotherapy, Albumin-Creatinine Ratio (ACR), ALP/Creatinine ratio, Hepatotoxicity, Nephrotoxicity, Survival analysis

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) accounts for approximately 30% of all female malignancies and remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide [1]. In Jordan, BC is the third leading cause of cancer deaths, following lung and colorectal cancers [2]. Unlike Western populations, Jordanian women are frequently diagnosed at a younger age (typically between 40 and 50 years) and often present with more advanced stages of the disease [3]. This demographic disparity highlights an urgent need for optimized therapeutic monitoring and prognostic tools tailored to this specific population.

Cytotoxic chemotherapy remains the backbone of systemic treatment for BC; however, it is associated with a broad spectrum of adverse events, ranging from immediate symptoms like myelosuppression and emesis to delayed, cumulative organ damage [4], [5]. In this study's context, the primary therapeutic indication was the adjuvant or neoadjuvant treatment of histologically verified breast cancer. The participants primarily received anthracycline-based regimens, most commonly the AC protocol (Doxorubicin at 60 mg/m² and Cyclophosphamide at 600 mg/m²), administered every two to three weeks for four cycles followed by paclitaxel 80 mg/m² weekly for 12 cycles plus trastuzumab. While these agents are standard of care, they are associated with cumulative organ stress. For example, anthracyclines are known to induce oxidative stress in hepatocytes, leading to potential hepatotoxicity, while cyclophosphamide metabolites can cause renal tubular irritation and subclinical nephrotoxicity. The liver and kidneys are particularly vulnerable due to their central roles in drug metabolism and excretion. Chemotherapy-induced hepatotoxicity can manifest as enzyme elevations, steatosis, or sinusoidal obstruction syndrome, affecting up to 56% of patients in some cohorts [9], [10], [6], [7], [8]. Similarly, nephrotoxicity is a well-documented complication, with agents like cisplatin carrying a risk of acute kidney injury in nearly one-third of patients [11].

While routine liver function tests (LFTs) and kidney function tests (KFTs) are standard practice, individual markers such as Alanine Aminotransferase (ALT) or serum creatinine often lack sensitivity or fail to predict long-term survival outcomes effectively [12]. Consequently, there is growing interest in composite biomarkers that reflect multi-organ physiological stress and nutritional status. For instance, the Albumin-to-Creatinine Ratio (ACR) and the Alkaline Phosphatase-to-Creatinine Ratio (ALP/Cr) have emerged as potential indicators of systemic stability. Yet, their prognostic value specifically in BC patients undergoing chemotherapy remains underexplored. Additionally, the Albumin-Bilirubin (ALBI) grade provides a refined assessment of hepatic reserve, reducing the subjectivity inherent in older scoring systems for ascites and encephalopathy.

Despite advances in therapy, approximately 20–30% of BC patients develop metastases, with liver involvement drastically reducing the 5-year survival rate to under 10% [13]. Early identification of patients at risk of poor outcomes is therefore critical. To date, limited research has investigated the utility of liver-kidney composite biomarkers in the Jordanian BC population. This study aims to evaluate changes in hepatic and renal parameters before and after chemotherapy and to determine the prognostic significance of ACR, ALP/Cr, and ALBI scores for overall survival (OS).

Methods

Patients and clinical data

This retrospective cohort study was performed at King Abdullah University Hospital (KAUH), a prominent tertiary care and educational institution in Irbid, Jordan. The study period spanned from January 2011 to April 2025. The study protocol received approval from the KAUH Institutional Review Board (IRB) (Reference No. 8/175/2024). Due to the study's retrospective design and the use of anonymized data from existing medical records, the IRB deemed specific patient consent unnecessary.

Sample size

The medical records of approximately 2500 patients diagnosed with BC and treated at KAUH from January 2011 to April 2025 were initially assessed for eligibility. An exhaustive and methodical data collection strategy was employed to ensure the quality and comprehensiveness of the data for the analysis, thereby bolstering confidence in the study's methodology and conclusions.

Patients qualified for the study if they possessed a histologically verified diagnosis of BC, had received systemic chemotherapy in the neoadjuvant, adjuvant, or palliative settings, and had complete LFT and KFT results accessible both before chemotherapy and following four cycles of chemotherapy. Furthermore, qualifying patients needed sufficient follow-up data to determine their OS status.

Patients were excluded from the final analysis if they met multiple predetermined criteria. This encompassed incomplete or unreliable clinicopathological data, including missing final tumor staging information; unavailable estrogen receptor (ER) or progesterone receptor (PR) test results; or ambiguous Human Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor 2 (HER2) status without confirming fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) testing or with absent FISH results. Patients were excluded if treatment details were either undocumented or incomplete; for instance, if the chemotherapy regimen was not recorded, if the patient had a history of radiation therapy, or if critical information regarding surgical interventions was absent when necessary for staging or outcome evaluation.

Furthermore, patients who failed to complete their prescribed chemotherapy regimen (e.g., those who terminated after two or three cycles for reasons not associated with rapid disease progression or significant toxicity necessitating a treatment alteration) were excluded. Individuals with confounding comorbidities or concurrent treatments that could modify LFT and KFT parameters were excluded, including those with active infections at the time of pre-treatment LFT and KFT evaluation, concurrent malignancies, significant autoimmune disorders, or recent steroid therapy within 4 weeks preceding sample collection. Ultimately, individuals lacking the crucial LFT and KFT data necessary to compute composite biomarkers (such as ACR, ALP/Cr, or ALBI), or those without post-chemotherapy LFT and KFT data, were excluded. These rigorous criteria were implemented to guarantee the reliability and validity of the study's results.

After the stringent application of these inclusion and exclusion criteria, data from 132 patients were deemed complete and appropriate for the final analysis. Four of the 132 patients had incomplete hormonal receptor status (ER/PR/HER2) but were included in the study because all other demographic, liver function, and kidney function data were complete.

Data collection and laboratory measurements

Clinical and demographic data were extracted from electronic medical records, including age, smoking status, tumor histology (type and grade), tumor laterality, and receptor status (ER, PR, and HER2).

Biochemical parameters were recorded at baseline (pre-chemotherapy) and post-treatment. Hepatic parameters included total protein, albumin, total bilirubin, direct bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase (ALP), alanine transaminase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), and gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT). Renal parameters included serum urea, creatinine (Cr), sodium (Na+), and potassium (K+).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 27. The distribution of variables was examined using the Shapiro–Wilk and Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests. Non-normally distributed continuous variables were summarized as medians with interquartile ranges (IQR), while categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. Baseline differences between the low- and high-ACR groups were assessed using the Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables and the Chi-square or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables. Changes in liver and kidney function parameters before and after chemotherapy—including total protein, albumin, bilirubin (total and direct), ALP, ALT, AST, Cr, urea, Na+ , and K+ ; as well as composite indices (ACR, ALBI, and the ALP/creatinine (ALP/Cr) ratios) were evaluated using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test, with Z statistics and p-values reported.

To evaluate whether changes in LFT, KFT, and composite biomarker parameters differed according to chemotherapy regimen, delta values were calculated for each parameter as post-treatment minus pre-treatment values. These changes were compared between patients receiving anthracycline-based chemotherapy, including doxorubicin plus cyclophosphamide, and those receiving anthracycline-based chemotherapy with trastuzumab, including doxorubicin, cyclophosphamide, and trastuzumab, using the Mann–Whitney U test.

OS was defined as the time from diagnosis to death or last follow-up. Kaplan–Meier curves were generated to estimate survival probabilities, and differences between biomarker-defined categories were evaluated using the log-rank (Mantel–Cox) test. Optimal cut-off values for post-treatment ACR, ALP/Cr, and ALBI were identified using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, selecting the thresholds that maximized the Youden Index (J = sensitivity + specificity – 1). These ROC-derived cut-off values were used only for survival stratification and should be distinguished from the descriptive median and interquartile range values.

Prognostic factors were further examined using Cox proportional hazards regression, beginning with univariate analyses and followed by multivariate models incorporating clinically relevant covariates such as metastatic status. Model adequacy was evaluated using omnibus chi-square statistics. Biomarker calculations followed established definitions: ALBI = (log10 bilirubin × 0.66) + (albumin × − 0.085), with bilirubin in μmol/L and albumin in g/L [14], and ACR defined as albumin (g/L) divided by creatinine (mg/dL) [15]. A two-tailed p-value ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

The selection of biomarkers in this study was based on their established clinical and prognostic relevance in assessing organ-specific toxicity and systemic physiological reserve. Conventional liver function parameters, including ALT, AST, ALP, albumin, total protein, and bilirubin, were selected because they are routinely used to assess hepatocellular injury, cholestatic changes, hepatic synthetic function, and bilirubin excretion during cancer therapy. Kidney function parameters, including creatinine, urea, sodium, and potassium, were included because they reflect renal filtration, nitrogenous waste handling, and electrolyte homeostasis, all of which may be affected by chemotherapy.

To complement these individual markers, composite biomarkers were also evaluated. The ALBI score was selected because it provides an objective estimate of hepatic reserve using albumin and bilirubin without relying on subjective clinical variables such as ascites or encephalopathy [16]. The albumin-to-creatinine ratio was included because it integrates nutritional status, systemic inflammation, muscle mass, and renal function, which are important determinants of prognosis in cancer patients [15]. Similarly, the ALP/Cr ratio was evaluated as a composite marker that relates liver and bone turnover activity to renal excretory capacity, potentially providing a more sensitive indicator of subclinical metabolic stress than ALP alone [17].

Results

Participants characteristics

A total of 132 BC patients were included in the analysis and stratified according to their pre-treatment ACR ratio. Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics for the low ACR (< 429.6) and high ACR (≥ 429.6) groups are presented in Table 1. Across all assessed variables, no statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups, indicating that the groups were comparable at baseline. Although the median age was higher in the low ACR group than in the high ACR group (59 vs. 50 years), this difference did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.087). There were likewise no significant differences in smoking status, presence of metastasis, tumor grade, histopathological subtype, tumor laterality, or molecular receptor status (ER, PR, HER2). Surgical approach and chemotherapy regimen also did not differ significantly between groups.

Impact of chemotherapy on LFT and KFT parameters

As shown in Table 2, most LFT and KFT parameters remained stable following chemotherapy; however, a few biomarkers demonstrated statistically significant changes. Among LFT, the median of total protein showed a modest but significant reduction after treatment (from 70.8 to 69.6 g/L, p = 0.035), whereas albumin, bilirubin (total and direct), ALT, AST, and ALP showed no significant differences (p > 0.05). A small but statistically significant decline in serum Cr was also observed (median reduced from 0.057 to 0.055 mg/dL after, p = 0.019). Composite biomarker indices showed the most notable changes. Both the ACR and the ALP/Cr ratios increased significantly post-treatment (ACR: 733.3–766.7; p = 0.036 and ALP/Cr: 1095.5–1150; p = 0.030), changes that appear primarily driven by the reduction in creatinine. In contrast, the ALBI score did not change significantly (p = 0.543), suggesting preserved overall hepatic synthetic and excretory capacity. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that while routine LFT and KFT remained largely unchanged following chemotherapy, composite biomarkers, particularly ACR and ALP/Cr were more responsive to treatment-related physiological changes and may have prognostic relevance.

Survival analysis based on post-treatment liver–kidney composite biomarkers

Using the ROC-derived post-treatment ACR cut-off value of 1045, patients with low post-treatment ACR (< 1045) showed significantly superior OS, with a mean OS of 8.14 (±0.62) years (95% CI: 6.93 – 9.36), compared with 2.35 (±0.62) years (95% CI: 1.86 – 2.84) in the high-ACR group (log-rank p = 0.009) (Fig. 1).

Kaplan–Meier survival curves stratified by post-treatment albumin-creatinine (ACR) category.

A similar pattern was observed using the ROC-derived post-treatment ALP/Cr cut-off value of 1491.7, where patients in the lower ALP/Cr group (< 1491.7) demonstrated a mean OS of 8.94 (± 0.38) years (95% CI: 8.19 – 9.69), significantly longer than the high-ratio group (≥ 1491.7), which had a mean OS of 6.12 (±1.03) years (95% CI: 4.10 – 8.14) (log-rank p = 0.017) (Fig. 2).

Kaplan–Meier survival curves stratified by post-treatment alkaline phosphatase- creatinine (ALP/ Cr) category.

In contrast, the post-treatment ALBI score did not significantly differentiate survival outcomes (p = 0.599), despite numerically lower survival in the high-ALBI group (mean OS 5.98 (± 0.61) vs. 8.06 (± 0.71) years) (Fig. 3). Collectively, these findings indicate that kidney–liver composite biomarkers (ACR and ALP/Cr) are more sensitive predictors of post-chemotherapy prognosis than liver-specific measures alone, such as ALBI. The strong survival divergence observed for ACR and ALP/Cr suggests that multi-organ physiological stress more accurately reflects treatment response and long-term outcomes in BC patients.

Kaplan–Meier survival curves stratified by post-treatment albumin-bilirubin (ALBI) category.

Multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression was performed to assess the combined prognostic contribution of post-treatment ACR, ALP/Cr, ALBI, and metastasis status (Table 4). The overall model was statistically significant (χ² (4) = 12.97, p = 0.011), indicating that these variables collectively improved the prediction of OS. Individually, none of the post-treatment biomarkers reached statistical significance (p > 0.05); however, all demonstrated hazard ratios below one, suggesting a trend toward lower mortality risk in the lower-biomarker categories. Metastasis status approached statistical significance (p = 0.071) and showed the strongest effect among the included covariates. Although these findings do not confirm independent prognostic value for ACR, ALP/Cr, or ALBI in the multivariate setting, the consistent direction of association supports the hypothesis that liver–kidney composite biomarkers may contribute to prognosis. Larger studies with adequate statistical power are needed to further validate their independent predictive utility.

Discussion

In this study, the longitudinal variation in liver and kidney function parameters after chemotherapy in BC patients was assessed, and the prognostic value of composite liver–kidney biomarkers was investigated. Our findings demonstrate that, despite the stable levels of conventional LFTs and KFTs post-chemotherapy, composite indices integrating liver and renal parameters—particularly the ACR and the ALP/Cr — were significantly different and strongly associated with OS. These findings suggest the potential of composite biomarkers for detecting subtle physiological stress across multiple organs that individual biomarkers might miss. Additionally, the findings reinforce the concept that cancer prognosis is driven not only by tumor characteristics but also by systemic host responses involving nutrition, inflammation, muscle mass, hepatic metabolism, and renal function.

The absence of significant changes in serum transaminases (ALT, AST), bilirubin, ALP, and albumin post-chemotherapy indicates that most patients maintained hepatocellular integrity and hepatic synthetic and secretory function. This aligns well with previous data showing that current chemotherapy regimens for BC are generally safe and well tolerated with respect to the liver, especially in patients without pre-existing liver disease [18], [19]. On the other hand, the modest but significant drop in total protein observed in our cohort suggests early alterations in nutritional or inflammatory status, which may precede overt hepatic dysfunction. Hypoproteinemia has been previously reported as a common finding in cancer patients and has been linked to systemic inflammation, tumor burden, and impaired prognosis [20], [21].

Similarly, the small but statistically significant decrease in serum creatinine post-chemotherapy likely reflects changes in muscle mass rather than improved renal function. Serum creatinine is influenced by skeletal muscle turnover, and anticancer-associated or chemotherapy-induced generalized muscle loss and wasting as a source of the artificial lowering of the serum concentration of creatinine in cancer patients, despite the presence and retention of preserved renal reserve [22], [23]. This phenomenon has been well described in oncology populations and underscores the limitation of creatinine as a standalone marker of kidney function in cancer patients [24].

One of the most striking results of this study is the strong association between post-treatment ACR and OS. Patients with lower post-chemotherapy ACR values experienced a marked survival advantage compared with those with elevated ACR. Notably, this effect was observed despite the absence of significant changes in albumin alone, highlighting the added prognostic value of composite indices over individual parameters.

The findings of a previous large-scale oncologic study showed that ACR integrates nutritional status (albumin), muscle mass, renal function (creatinine), and the body’s inflammatory burden. This study, conducted by Zhao et al., involved more than 20,000 patients with solid tumors and found that no significant association was observed between ACR and OS in breast cancer patients [15]. This was attributed to the high molecular heterogeneity of BC (e.g., hormone receptor status, HER2 status, triple-negative disease) as well as the strong influence of obesity, body composition, and metabolic factors on prognosis. Mechanistically, the low albumin represents inflammation-driven hepatocyte reprioritizations of protein production [25], whereas low creatinine indicates sarcopenia—both hallmarks of cancer-associated cachexia and poor prognosis [26], [27].

Beyond oncology, elevated ACRs are also associated with frailty, functional decline, and death in the elderly. This serves as another proof of ACR as a global marker of physiological resilience instead of a marker of individual organ function [28].

The ALP/Cr ratio also proved to be an important prognostic factor in our cohort, with lower post-treatment values associated with longer survival. ALP is a well-established surrogate marker of hepatic cholestasis and bone turnover, both of which are relevant in BC, particularly in the context of occult bone metastasis or chemotherapy-induced bone turnover (C. [17]). When normalized to creatinine, ALP/Cr may better reflect the balance between metabolic burden and physiological reserve.

Previous research has found ALP to be predictive of poor outcomes in BC and other solid tumors, especially when associated with skeletal involvement [29], [30]. Our findings extend this concept by demonstrating that integration with creatinine improves prognostic discrimination, again highlighting the value of multi-organ composite indices.

In contrast to ACR and ALP/Cr, the albumin-bilirubin (ALBI) score did not significantly stratify survival in our cohort. ALBI scoring was first developed to evaluate hepatic reserve in hepatocellular carcinoma and advanced chronic liver disease [16], [31], where bilirubin plays a dominant prognostic value [32]. The lack of relevance of the ALBI score in our study may be attributed to the preserved bilirubin excretion and the low prevalence of advanced liver dysfunction in BC patients [33].

Nevertheless, ALBI has been linked to sarcopenia, frailty, as well as adverse outcomes in non-hepatic patients, which indicates that the prognostic value of ALBI may emerge in more advanced disease cases or in patients with concurrent metabolic dysfunction [14].

Although none of the composite biomarkers reached independent statistical significance in multivariate Cox regression, all hazard ratios consistently favored lower biomarker values, and the overall model was significant. The lack of independent significance may be due to the small sample size and correlations among nutritional, inflammatory, and metastatic variables. Importantly, metastasis status approached statistical significance, reinforcing its dominant role in survival while also highlighting the potential complementary value of biochemical composite indices.

From a clinical perspective, our data support the integration of liver–kidney composite biomarkers into routine cancer evaluation. Compared with complex imaging or molecular assays, the cost and access advantages of the ACR or ALP/Cr ratio could enable early risk adjustment in cancer patients receiving chemotherapy.

Strengths and limitations

This study has several strengths and limitations. Strengths include longitudinal evaluation of liver and kidney biomarkers before and after chemotherapy, incorporation of survival outcomes, and use of biomarkers routinely measured in clinical practice, thereby enhancing the relevance and applicability of the findings in real-world oncology settings. On the other hand, the study is limited by its retrospective, single-center design and the relatively modest sample size, which may have reduced the statistical power of multivariate analyses. A potential consideration in interpreting the ACR results is the role of supportive care medications. Anthracycline-based regimens are highly emetogenic, necessitating the use of antiemetic prophylaxis, including corticosteroids like dexamethasone. While steroids can promote proteolysis (potentially increasing creatinine) and alter albumin synthesis, our post-treatment blood samples were drawn approximately 14–21 days after the fourth chemotherapy cycle. This time frame likely minimizes the acute metabolic influence of these supportive agents. Furthermore, since anti-emetic protocols were standardized across the cohort, any residual effects would be consistent across all patients, suggesting that the observed prognostic value of ACR is more likely to reflect cumulative treatment-induced physiological stress rather than acute pharmacological interference from supportive care. Another limitation is the highly imbalanced distribution of patients according to baseline ACR, with only four patients classified in the low-ACR group; therefore, subgroup comparisons based on baseline ACR should be interpreted cautiously. Future studies involving larger, multicenter cohorts and the inclusion of body composition imaging are needed to confirm and extend these results.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study clearly illustrates that composite liver and kidney indices, particularly ACR and ALP/Cr, which are important for the prognosis of BC patients receiving chemotherapy, provide more than conventional liver or kidney function indices alone. While routine LFTs and KFTs remained largely stable post treatment—indicating preserved organ integrity—significant alterations in composite indices revealed subclinical systemic stress that translated into marked differences in OS.

The high survival ratio between post- and pre-treatment ACR and ALP/Cr levels clearly illustrates the need to combine variables that reflect hepatic synthetic function, renal excretion, muscle mass, and metabolism for the evaluation and risk assessment of malignancies. Moreover, the ALBI did not show a significant impact on discriminating survival differences and may reflect that current liver-specific scoring systems lack sensitivity in noncirrhotic breast cancer patients. Taken together, these data clearly demonstrate the potential utility and efficacy of composite multi-organ indices as cost-effective, feasible prognostic variables for discriminating between poor and favorable outcomes following chemotherapy.

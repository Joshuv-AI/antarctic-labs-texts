Global existence and uniqueness of weak solutions for the MHD equations with large L 3 L^{3} -initial values
Baishun Lai, Ge Tang, Ziying Xu

Abstract
This paper is concerned with the weak solution theory for the MHD system with large L 3 L^{3} -initial data. Due to the fact that the natural boundary condition on the magnetic field H H is the slip boundary condition, the Leray-Schauder fixed-point theorem, which have used to investigate the weak solution theory of the Navier-Stokes system, becomes invalid. To address such difficulty, we will invoke the Leray’s approximation technique and the perturbation theory to seek a global weak solution to the Cauchy problem for MHD equations with large L 3 L^{3} -initial data. Our strategy provides a simple alternative (self-contained) proof of weak L 3 L^{3} -solution theory of incompressible Navier-Stokes system. Moreover, this weak solution is unique under some restrictions. Key words : Global weak L 3 L^{3} -solutions; Leray’s approximation; perturbation theory

Introduction

This paper is concerned with the existence and uniqueness of weak solutions for the three-dimensional incompressible Magnetohydrodynamics (MHD for short) equations in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,+\infty)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}v-\Delta v+v\cdot\nabla v+\nabla\Pi=H\cdot\nabla H,\\
&\partial_{t}H-\Delta H+v\cdot\nabla H=H\cdot\nabla v,\\
&\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits v=\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits H=0,\end{aligned}\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

complemented with the initial condition

v(x,0)=v_{0},\quad H(x,0)=h_{0}\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(1.2)

Here,

magnetic field,

It holds a core position in plasma physics, astrophysics, and geophysics. Conducting fluids not only exhibit fluid characteristics but also carry charges under the influence of magnetic fields, making electromagnetic effects and fluid dynamics closely intertwined in their motion patterns. The MHD equations serve as the fundamental tool for describing this interaction by combining fluid dynamics equations with Maxwell’s equations to trace the changes in fluid motion and the magnetic fields they produce in ionized media.

Notice that, in the case

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}v-\Delta v+v\cdot\nabla v+\nabla p=0,\\
&\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits v=0,\\
&v(x,0)=v_{0},\quad\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\end{aligned}\end{array}\right.

2.1

Let

With this notation, we define

The space

is constituted by all infinitely differentiable functions with compact support in
\Omega
. In particular, we usually write

L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)=\left\{v\in L^{p}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}v\cdot\nabla\varphi\,{\rm d}x=0,\ \mbox{for any}\ \varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)\right\}.

Lemma 2.2

(Fixed point theorem)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space, and define the operator
\mathcal{M}
as

\mathcal{M}(u,v)=\mathcal{B}(u,v)+\mathcal{L}(u)+\mathcal{R}(x,t),\quad u,v\in X,

where
\mathcal{B}:X\times X\to X
is a bounded bilinear operator such that

\|\mathcal{B}(u,v)\|_{X}\leq c_{1}\|u\|_{X}\|v\|_{X},

\mathcal{L}:X\to X
is a bounded linear operator such that

\|\mathcal{L}(u)\|_{X}\leq c_{2}\|u\|_{X},

and
\mathcal{R}(x,t)
is a measurable function on
X
. Moreover,

c_{1},\ c_{2}
and
\|\mathcal{R}\|_{X}
satisfy

(1-c_{2})^{2}>4c_{1}\|\mathcal{R}\|_{X},\quad c_{1}>0,\quad 0\leq c_{2}<1.

(2.5)

Then, the quadratic operator
\mathcal{M}(u,u)
has a fixed point
\bar{u}
such that

\|\bar{u}\|_{X}\leq x_{1}=\frac{1-c_{2}-\sqrt{(c_{2}-1)^{2}-4c_{1}\|\mathcal{R}\|_{X}}}{2c_{1}}.

Proof.

First, notice that the term
\sqrt{(c_{2}-1)^{2}-4c_{1}\|\mathcal{R}_{X}\|}
makes sense due to (
2.5
). Thus, the line
Y_{1}(x)=x
and the parabola
Y_{2}(x)=c_{1}x^{2}+c_{2}x+\|\mathcal{R}\|_{X}
intersect as
x_{1}
and
x_{2}\,(0<x_{1}<x_{2})
with

x_{1}=\frac{1-c_{2}-\sqrt{(c_{2}-1)^{2}-4c_{1}\|\mathcal{R}\|_{X}}}{2c_{1}},\quad x_{2}=\frac{1-c_{2}+\sqrt{(c_{2}-1)^{2}-4c_{1}\|\mathcal{R}\|_{X}}}{2c_{1}}.

On the other hand,
Y^{\prime}_{2}(x)=2c_{1}x+c_{2}>0
when
0<x\leq x_{1}
, thus
Y_{2}(x)\leq Y_{2}(x_{1})=x_{1}
. This implies

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{M}(u,u)\|_{X}&\leq c_{1}\|u\|_{X}^{2}+c_{2}\|u\|_{X}+\|\mathcal{R}\|_{X}\\
&\leq c_{1}x_{1}^{2}+c_{2}x_{1}+\|\mathcal{R}\|_{X}\\
&\leq x_{1},\end{split}

for any
u\in B=\{u:\|u\|_{X}\leq x_{1}\}
. Therefore, the operator
\mathcal{M}(u,u)
maps the ball
B
to itself. Next we claim that the operator
\mathcal{M}(u,u)
is a contractive mapping. As a matter of fact, we see that

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{M}(u_{1},u_{1})-\mathcal{M}(u_{2},u_{2})\|_{X}&=\|\mathcal{B}(u_{1},u_{1})-\mathcal{B}(u_{2},u_{2})+\mathcal{L}(u_{1}-u_{2})\|_{X}\\
&\leq\|\mathcal{B}(u_{1},u_{1}-u_{2})\|_{X}+\|\mathcal{B}(u_{1}-u_{2},u_{2})\|_{X}+\|\mathcal{L}(u_{1}-u_{2})\|_{X}\\
&\leq c_{1}\|u_{1}\|_{X}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{X}+c_{1}\|u_{2}\|_{X}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{X}+c_{2}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{X}\\
&\leq\big[c_{1}(\|u_{1}\|_{X}+\|u_{2}\|_{X})+c_{2}\big]\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{X}\\
&\leq(2c_{1}x_{1}+c_{2})\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{X}\\
&\leq\gamma\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{X},\end{split}

with
\gamma=2c_{1}x_{1}+c_{2}<1
due to the fact

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=2c_{1}x_{1}+c_{2}

\displaystyle=2c_{1}\frac{1-c_{2}-\sqrt{(c_{2}-1)^{2}-4c_{1}\|\mathcal{R}\|_{X}}}{2c_{1}}+c_{2}

\displaystyle=1-\sqrt{(c_{2}-1)^{2}-4c_{1}\|\mathcal{R}\|_{X}}.

Thus,
\mathcal{M}(u,u)
is a contractive mapping on the ball
B
, and consequently it has a fixed point
\bar{u}
on the ball
B
such that

\|\bar{u}\|_{X}\leq x_{1}=\frac{1-c_{2}-\sqrt{(c_{2}-1)^{2}-4c_{1}\|\mathcal{R}\|_{X}}}{2c_{1}},

which finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.3

(
[
27
]
)
.

If
u\in L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),\partial_{t}u\in L^{2}(0,T;{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
with

Lemma 2.4
.

If
g\in L^{2}(0,\infty;\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, then
U(x,t)\triangleq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}W_{(t-s)}(x-y)\mathbb{P}g(y)\,{\rm d}y{\rm d}s\in C(0,\infty;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,\infty;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
and

\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,\infty;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty;\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}.

Here,
W_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{\frac{3}{2}}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}
and
\mathbb{P}
is Leray-Hopf projection operator in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
defined as

(\mathbb{P}f)_{i}=f_{i}+\partial_{i}(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot f,\quad\forall~f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Proof.

First, by the Plancherel equality, one has for any
t\in(0,+\infty)

\displaystyle\|U\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)\xi^{2}}\Big(\hat{g}_{i}-\frac{\xi_{i}\xi_{j}}{|\xi|^{2}}\hat{g}_{j}\Big)\,{\rm d}s\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2(t-s)\xi^{2}}\,{\rm d}s\int_{0}^{t}|\hat{g}|^{2}\,{\rm d}s{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{2\xi^{2}}(1-e^{-2t\xi^{2}})\int_{0}^{t}|\hat{g}|^{2}\,{\rm d}s{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\xi^{-2}|\hat{g}|^{2}\,{\rm d}\xi{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,\infty;\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}.

Here,
\hat{g}
denotes by the Fourier transform of
g
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Therefore,

\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty;\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}.

On the other hand, by using the Plancherel equality again, one has

\displaystyle\|\nabla U\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big|\frac{i\xi}{i\tau+\xi^{2}}|^{2}|\hat{g}\Big|^{2}\,{\rm d}\xi{\rm d}\tau\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\xi^{4}}{\tau^{2}+\xi^{4}}\xi^{-2}|\hat{g}|^{2}\,{\rm d}\xi{\rm d}\tau\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\xi^{-2}|\hat{g}|^{2}\,{\rm d}\xi{\rm d}\tau\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty;\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}.

From this, it is clear that

\|U\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty;\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}.

Next, we claim that
\Delta U\in L^{2}(0,\infty;\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
.
In fact,

\displaystyle\|\Delta U\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}=\int_{0}^{T}\|\Delta U\|^{2}_{H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\sup_{\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\frac{|\langle\Delta U,\varphi\rangle|}{\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}}\Big)^{2}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla U\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\,{\rm d}t\leq\|U\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}.

It is clear that
U
is a solution to the following heat equation in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,+\infty)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u-\Delta u=\mathbb{P}g,\\
&u(x,0)=0,\end{aligned}\end{array}\right.

and we already know that
\Delta U
and
\mathbb{P}g
belong to
L^{2}(0,\infty;H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, from which we can deduce that
\partial_{t}U\in L^{2}(0,\infty;H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
. This, along with the Lemma
2.3
, derives
U\in C(0,\infty;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).

∎

2.2
Several useful inequalities

In this subsection, we introduce some important inequalities which play an important role in the proof of this paper. To begin with, we introduce the well-known Gronwall’s inequality:

Lemma 2.6

(Gronwall’s inequality
[
27
]
)
.

(a)
Let
\eta:[0,T]\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be an absolutely continuous function that satisfies the differential inequality

\eta^{\prime}(t)\leq\phi(t)\eta(t)+\psi(t)

where
\phi
and
\psi
are non-negative integrable functions. Then

\eta(t)\leq\mathrm{e}^{\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)\mathrm{d}s}\left[\eta(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\psi(s)\mathrm{d}s\right]\quad\text{ for all }t\in[0,T].

(b)
Let
\eta:[0,T]\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a continuous function satisfying

\eta(t)\leq a(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)\eta(s)\,{\rm d}s,

where
a,\phi:[0,T]\rightarrow[0,\infty)
are integrable functions and
a
is increasing. Then

\eta(t)\leq a(t)\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)\,{\rm d}s\Big)\quad\text{ for all }t\in[0,T].

Lemma 2.7

(Sobolev-Poincere type inequality)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain and
1\leq p<n
, there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
\Omega
such that for all measurable functions
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, the following holds:

\|u-u_{\Omega}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

where

u_{\Omega}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}u\,{\rm d}x
is the average value of
u
over
\Omega
,
1\leq q\leq\frac{np}{n-p}
.

The following lemma is an analogue of the maximum regularity estimate of the heat operator, called as the Solonnikov coercive estimate which will be often used in the proof of our main results.

Lemma 2.8

(Solonnikov coercive estimates
[
30
]
)
.

Assume
1<s,l<\infty
, then for every
f\in L^{l}(0,T;L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, there exists a unique solution
(u,\nabla p)
of the Stokes system

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+\nabla p=f,\\
\operatorname{div}\,u=0,\\

in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T)
satisfying

Finally, we conclude this subsection by giving the following lemma which plays a fundamental role in estimating the nonlinear term. Its proof is a simple consequence of the Hölder and Sobolev inequalities, we omit it here.

, Hölder’s inequality, yields

\displaystyle\|H_{2}\nabla H_{1}-v_{2}\nabla v_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{4}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\|\nabla v_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\|H_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\|\nabla H_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\Big\|t^{-\frac{5}{8}}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T)}+C\|H_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\Big\|t^{-\frac{5}{8}}\|H_{0}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}T^{\frac{1}{24}}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+C\|H_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}T^{\frac{1}{24}}\|h_{0}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|v_{0}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\|h_{0}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})})T^{\frac{7}{24}},

which further implies by Lemma
2.8

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}v^{2}_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{4}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\|\nabla^{2}v^{2}_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{4}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\|\nabla\Pi^{2}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{4}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|H_{2}\nabla H_{1}-v_{2}\nabla v_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{4}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|v_{0}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\|h_{0}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})})T^{\frac{7}{24}}.

For
v^{3}_{2}
, by using the Lemma
3.1
and Hölder’s inequality, one has

\displaystyle\|H_{1}\nabla H_{2}-v_{1}\nabla v_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\|\nabla v_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\|H_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}|\nabla H_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\|\nabla v_{2}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}T^{\frac{1}{6}}+\|H_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}|\nabla H_{2}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}T^{\frac{1}{6}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|v_{0}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\|h_{0}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})})T^{\frac{2}{3}}.

This, along with Lemma
2.8
, derives,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}v^{3}_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\|\nabla^{2}v^{3}_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\|\nabla\Pi^{3}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|v_{0}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\|h_{0}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})})T^{\frac{2}{3}}.

For
v^{4}_{2}
, by using Hölder’s inequality and (
2.4
), one has

\displaystyle\|H_{1}\nabla H_{1}-v_{1}\nabla v_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{1}\nabla v_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\|H_{1}\nabla H_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\|\nabla v_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\|H_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\|\nabla H_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T)}+c\|h_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})})\|t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|v_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|h_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})})T^{\frac{1}{6}},

which implies,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}v^{4}_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\|\nabla^{2}v^{4}_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\|\nabla\Pi^{4}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|H_{1}\nabla H_{1}-v_{1}\nabla v_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C(\|v_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|h_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})})T^{\frac{1}{6}}.

Combing the above estimates and the Lemma
2.7
, we immediately derive (
3.2
).
∎

3.2
The existence of approximate solutions

In this subsection, we are going to prove the existence of global weak
L^{3}
-solutions of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) which is equivalent to gain the existence of energy weak solutions (usually called as Leray-Hopf weak solution) for (
2.1
).
To achieve this goal, we first smooth the nonlinear term of (
2.1
) to obtain its approximate system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}v_{2}-\Delta v_{2}+\nabla\Pi+(\eta_{\epsilon}\ast v_{2})\cdot\nabla v_{2}+v_{2}\cdot\nabla v_{1}+v_{1}\cdot\nabla v_{2}-(\eta_{\epsilon}\ast H_{2})\cdot\nabla H_{2}\\
&\quad-H_{2}\cdot\nabla H_{1}-H_{1}\cdot\nabla H_{2}+v_{1}\cdot\nabla v_{1}-H_{1}\cdot\nabla H_{1}=0,\\
&\partial_{t}H_{2}-\Delta H_{2}+(\eta_{\epsilon}\ast v_{2})\cdot\nabla H_{2}+v_{2}\cdot\nabla H_{1}+v_{1}\cdot\nabla H_{2}-(\eta_{\epsilon}\ast H_{2})\cdot\nabla v_{2}\\
&\quad-H_{2}\cdot\nabla v_{1}-H_{1}\cdot\nabla v_{2}+v_{1}\cdot\nabla H_{1}-H_{1}\cdot\nabla v_{1}=0,\\
&v_{2}(x,0)=H_{2}(x,0)=0,\end{aligned}\end{array}\right.

(3.3)

where
\eta_{\epsilon}=\epsilon^{-3}\eta\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}\right)
is a standard spatial mollifier with
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\eta dx=1
. We then seek the solution pair
(v_{2\epsilon},H_{2\epsilon})
, usually called the approximate solution, to the above approximate system by using Lemma
2.2
.
Subsequently, the standard energy method shows that the solution pair
(v_{2\epsilon},H_{2\epsilon})
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
uniformly in
\epsilon
. For simplicity, we write the Banach space
X
as

\displaystyle X_{T}=L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))

and define the norm as

\displaystyle\|(v,H)\|_{X_{T}}=\|v\|_{X_{T}}+\|H\|_{X_{T}}.

Here and in what follows, we always suppose
T<\infty
.

By applying Lemma
2.2
, we are now in a position to construct a solution to the approximate system (
3.3
).

Theorem 3.3
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, the approximate system (
3.3
) admits a solution pair
(v_{2\epsilon},H_{2\epsilon})\in X_{T}
for any
T\in(0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\|(v_{2\epsilon},H_{2\epsilon})\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\|(v_{2\epsilon},H_{2\epsilon})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C(\|v_{0}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\|h_{0}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})})\sqrt{T}.

(3.4)

Proof.

The proof consists of two steps: In step 1, we construct a energy weak solution
(v_{2\epsilon},H_{2\epsilon})\in X_{T}
for the approximate system (
3.3
) in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T)
with small
T
; subsequently, in step 2 the standard energy estimate shows the energy weak solution constructed in step 1 can be extended in
(0,T)
for any large
T\in(0,\infty)
.

Step 1:

The approximate system (
3.3
) admits a energy weak solution
(v_{2\epsilon},H_{2\epsilon})\in X_{T}
with small
T
.

To achieve this, we define the bilinear operator in
X_{T}

\mathcal{M}\left((\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{H}_{1}),(\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{H}_{2})\right)=\left(\mathcal{M}_{1}\left((\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{H}_{1}),(\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{H}_{2})\right),\mathcal{M}_{2}\left((\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{H}_{1}),(\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{H}_{2})\right)\right)

for any
(\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{H}_{1}),(\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{H}_{2})\in X_{T}
. Here,
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}
are respectively given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1}\Big((\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{H}_{1}),(\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{H}_{2})\Big)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}W_{(t-s)}\ast\mathbb{P}\Big((\eta_{\epsilon}\ast\tilde{v}_{1})\nabla\tilde{v}_{2}+\tilde{v}_{1}\nabla v_{1}+v_{1}\nabla\tilde{v}_{1}-(\eta_{\epsilon}\ast\tilde{H}_{1})\nabla\tilde{H}_{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\tilde{H}_{1}\nabla H_{1}-H_{1}\nabla\tilde{H}_{1}\Big)\,{\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{t}W_{(t-s)}\ast\mathbb{P}\Big(v_{1}\nabla v_{1}-H_{1}\nabla H_{1}\Big)\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{2}\Big((\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{H}_{1}),(\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{H}_{2})\Big)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}W_{(t-s)}\ast\mathbb{P}\Big((\eta_{\epsilon}\ast\tilde{v}_{1})\nabla\tilde{H}_{2}+\tilde{v}_{1}\nabla H_{1}+v_{1}\nabla\tilde{H}_{1}-(\eta_{\epsilon}\ast\tilde{H}_{1})\nabla\tilde{v}_{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\tilde{H}_{1}\nabla v_{1}-H_{1}\nabla\tilde{v}_{1}\Big)\,{\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{t}W_{(t-s)}\ast\mathbb{P}\Big(v_{1}\nabla H_{1}-H_{1}\nabla v_{1}\Big)\,{\rm d}s.

Take note that proving the existence of a solution to the system (
3.3
) is equivalent to proving the existence of a fixed point for the integral equations

(\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{H}_{2})=\mathcal{M}\Big((\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{H}_{2}),(\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{H}_{2})\Big).

To do this, we need to show by Lemma
2.2

\Big\|\mathcal{M}\Big((\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{H}_{1}),(\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{H}_{2})\Big)\Big\|_{X_{T}}\leq c_{1}\|(\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{H}_{1})\|_{X_{T}}\|(\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{H}_{2})\|_{X_{T}}+c_{2}\|(\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{H}_{1})\|_{X_{T}}+\|\mathcal{R}\|_{X_{T}},\\

4c_{1}\|\mathcal{R}\|_{X_{T}}<(1-c_{2})^{2},

for any
(\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{H}_{1}),(\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{H}_{2})\in X_{T}
. To verify the above conditions, we now estimate each term of
\mathcal{M}\Big((\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{H}_{1}),(\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{H}_{2})\Big)
. For
\int_{0}^{t}W_{(t-s)}\ast\mathbb{P}(\eta_{\epsilon}\ast\tilde{v}_{1})\nabla\tilde{v}_{2}\,{\rm d}s
, by the Hölder’s inequality and classical properties of mollifiers, one has

\displaystyle\|\eta_{\epsilon}\ast\tilde{v}_{1}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\eta_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\tilde{v}_{1}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big|\frac{1}{\epsilon^{3}}\eta\Big(\frac{x}{\epsilon}\Big)\Big|^{2}{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\tilde{v}_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|\tilde{v}_{1}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\eta\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

This, together with the Lemma
2.4
, yields,

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}W_{(t-s)}\ast\mathbb{P}(\eta_{\epsilon}\ast\tilde{v}_{1})\nabla\tilde{v}_{2}\,{\rm d}s\Big\|_{X_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\eta_{\epsilon}\ast\tilde{v}_{1})\nabla\tilde{v}_{2}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(\eta_{\epsilon}\ast\tilde{v}_{1})\otimes\tilde{v}_{2}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|\eta_{\epsilon}\ast\tilde{v}_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\tilde{v}_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\,{\rm d}t\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{T}\epsilon^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|\tilde{v}_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\|\tilde{v}_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{T}\epsilon^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|\tilde{v}_{1}\|_{X_{T}}\|\tilde{v}_{2}\|_{X_{T}}

For
\int_{0}^{t}W_{(t-s)}\ast\mathbb{P}(\tilde{v}_{1}\nabla v_{1})\,{\rm d}s
, by the Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
2.4
, one has,

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}W_{(t-s)}\ast\mathbb{P}(\tilde{v}_{1}\nabla v_{1})\,{\rm d}s\Big\|_{X_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{v}_{1}\nabla v_{1}\|_{(L^{2}(0,T),\dot{H}^{-1})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{1}\otimes\tilde{v}_{1}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{1}\|_{L^{5}(0,T;L^{5}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\|\tilde{v}_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{10}{3}}(0,T;L^{\frac{10}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{1}\|_{L^{5}(0,T;L^{5}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\|\tilde{v}_{1}\|_{X_{T}}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}W_{(t-s)}\ast\mathbb{P}(v_{1}\nabla\tilde{v}_{1})\,{\rm d}s\Big\|_{X_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{1}\nabla\tilde{v}_{1}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{1}\otimes\tilde{v}_{1}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{1}\|_{L^{5}(0,T;L^{5}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\|\tilde{v}_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{10}{3}}(0,T;L^{\frac{10}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{1}\|_{L^{5}(0,T;L^{5}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\|\tilde{v}_{1}\|_{X_{T}}

For
\int_{0}^{t}W_{(t-s)}\ast\mathbb{P}(v_{1}\nabla v_{1})\,{\rm d}s
, by the Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
2.4
, one has

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}W_{(t-s)}\ast\mathbb{P}(v_{1}\nabla v_{1})\,{\rm d}s\Big\|_{X_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{1}\nabla v_{1}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{1}\otimes v_{1}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{1}\|_{L^{4}(0,T;L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{2}

The remaining terms can be estimated through the same process as above. Indeed, summing all of these yields

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{M}_{1}\Big((\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{H}_{1}),(\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{H}_{2})\Big)\Big\|_{X_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{T}\epsilon^{-\frac{3}{2}}\Big(\|\tilde{v}_{1}\|_{X_{T}}\|\tilde{v}_{2}\|_{X_{T}}+\|\tilde{H}_{1}\|_{X_{T}}\|\tilde{H}_{2}\|_{X_{T}}\Big)

\displaystyle+C\|v_{1}\|_{L^{5}(0,T;L^{5}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\|\tilde{v}_{1}\|_{X_{T}}+C\|H_{1}\|_{L^{5}(0,T;L^{5}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\|\tilde{H}_{1}\|_{X_{T}}

\displaystyle+C\|v_{1}\|_{L^{4}(0,T;L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{2}+C\|H_{1}\|_{L^{4}(0,T;L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{2},

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{M}_{2}\Big((\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{H}_{1}),(\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{H}_{2})\Big)\Big\|_{X_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{T}\epsilon^{-\frac{3}{2}}\Big(\|\tilde{v}_{1}\|_{X_{T}}\|\tilde{H}_{2}\|_{X_{T}}+\|\tilde{H}_{1}\|_{X_{T}}\|\tilde{v}_{2}\|_{X_{T}}\Big)

\displaystyle+C\|v_{1}\|_{L^{5}(0,T;L^{5}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\|\tilde{H}_{1}\|_{X_{T}}+C\|H_{1}\|_{L^{5}(0,T;L^{5}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\|\tilde{v}_{1}\|_{X_{T}}

\displaystyle+2C\|v_{1}\|_{L^{4}(0,T;L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\|H_{1}\|_{L^{4}(0,T;L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}.

Combing the above relations, we easily derive that

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{M}\Big((\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{H}_{1}),(\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{H}_{2})\Big)\Big\|_{X_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{T}\epsilon^{-\frac{3}{2}}\Big(\|\tilde{v}_{1}\|_{X_{T}}+\|\tilde{H}_{1}\|_{X_{T}}\Big)\Big(\|\tilde{v}_{2}\|_{X_{T}}+\|\tilde{H}_{2}\|_{X_{T}}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+C\Big(\|v_{1}\|_{L^{5}(0,T;L^{5}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\|H_{1}\|_{L^{5}(0,T;L^{5}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\Big)\Big(\|\tilde{v}_{1}\|_{X_{T}}+\|\tilde{H}_{1}\|_{X_{T}}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+C\Big(\|v_{1}\|_{L^{4}(0,T;L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\|H_{1}\|_{L^{4}(0,T;L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\Big)^{2},

which implies

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{M}\Big((\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{H}_{1}),(\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{H}_{2})\Big)\Big\|_{X_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\|(\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{H}_{1})\|_{X_{T}}\|(\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{H}_{2})\|_{X_{T}}+c_{2}\|(\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{H}_{1})\|_{X_{T}}+\|\mathcal{R}\|_{X_{T}},

where

\displaystyle c_{1}=C\sqrt{T}\epsilon^{-\frac{3}{2}},\quad c_{2}=C\Big(\|v_{1}\|_{L^{5}(0,T;L^{5}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\|H_{1}\|_{L^{5}(0,T;L^{5}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\Big),

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}W_{(t-s)}\ast\mathbb{P}\Big(v_{1}\nabla v_{1}-H_{1}\nabla H_{1}\Big)\,{\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{t}W_{(t-s)}\ast\mathbb{P}\Big(v_{1}\nabla H_{1}-H_{1}\nabla v_{1}\Big)\,{\rm d}s,

with

\|\mathcal{R}\|_{X_{T}}\leq C\|(v_{1},H_{1})\|^{2}_{L^{4}(0,T;L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C\|(v_{1},H_{1})\|^{2}_{L^{4}(0,1;L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}.

Now we take
T_{1}\ll 1
small such that
c_{2}<1
, and derive by a simple calculation

4c_{1}\|\mathcal{R}\|_{X_{T}}<(1-c_{2})^{2}

with

\displaystyle T=\min\Big\{T_{1},\frac{1}{16C^{4}}\epsilon^{3}\Big(\|(v_{1},H_{1})\|_{L^{4}(0,1;L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\Big)^{-4}\Big(1-C\Big(\|(v_{1},H_{1})\|_{L^{5}(0,T_{1};L^{5}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\Big)\Big)^{4}\Big\}.

This, along with Lemma
2.2
, shows that (
3.3
) admits a solution, denoted by
(v_{2\epsilon},H_{2\epsilon})
which belongs to
X_{T}
and satisfies

\|(v_{2\epsilon},H_{2\epsilon})\|_{X_{T}}\leq\frac{1-c_{2}-\sqrt{(c_{2}-1)^{2}-4c_{1}\|\mathcal{R}\|_{X_{T}}}}{2c_{1}}.

Finally, from the previous proof process, it is known that

(f_{1},f_{2})\in L^{2}(0,T;{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),

where

\displaystyle f_{1}=-(\eta_{\epsilon}\ast v_{2\epsilon})\cdot\nabla v_{2\epsilon}-v_{2\epsilon}\cdot\nabla v_{1}-v_{1}\cdot\nabla v_{2\epsilon}+(\eta_{\epsilon}\ast H_{2\epsilon})\cdot\nabla H_{2\epsilon}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~+H_{2\epsilon}\cdot\nabla H_{1}+H_{1}\cdot\nabla H_{2\epsilon}-v_{1}\cdot\nabla v_{1}+H_{1}\cdot\nabla H_{1},

\displaystyle f_{2}=-(\eta_{\epsilon}\ast v_{2\epsilon})\cdot\nabla H_{2\epsilon}-v_{2\epsilon}\cdot\nabla H_{1}-v_{1}\cdot\nabla H_{2\epsilon}+(\eta_{\epsilon}\ast H_{2\epsilon})\cdot\nabla v_{2\epsilon}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~+H_{2\epsilon}\cdot\nabla v_{1}+H_{1}\cdot\nabla v_{2\epsilon}-v_{1}\cdot\nabla H_{1}+H_{1}\cdot\nabla v_{1}.

This, along with the maximal regularity of Stokes operator, yields

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}v_{2\epsilon},\partial_{t}H_{2\epsilon})\in L^{2}(0,T;{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).

Thus, by the Lemma
2.3
, one has
(v_{2\epsilon},H_{2\epsilon})\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
.

Step 2:

The existence time interval of the energy weak solution pair
(v_{2\epsilon},H_{2\epsilon})
constructed in Step 1 can be extended to
(0,\infty)
.
Assume by contradiction that the survival time interval of
(v_{2\epsilon},H_{2\epsilon})
is
(0,T^{*})
with some finite
T^{*}
. First of all, notice that by repeating the proof process of Lemma
3.1
, one has

\|(v_{2\epsilon},H_{2\epsilon})\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\|(v_{2\epsilon},H_{2\epsilon})\|_{L^{2}(0,T^{*};\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C{T^{*}}^{\frac{3}{4}},

with some constant
C
depending only on
\|v_{0}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\|h_{0}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
.
Moreover, Step 1 shows

(v_{2\epsilon},H_{2\epsilon})\in C([0,T^{*}];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).

Therefore,
\|(v_{2\epsilon},H_{2\epsilon})(\cdot,T^{*})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
is well-defined and bounded. Then, we reason as in the proof of Step 1 to conclude that
(v_{2\epsilon},H_{2\epsilon})
can be extended after time
T^{*}
, contradicting the fact that
T^{*}
is a maximum.

Now, we claim

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}v_{2\epsilon},\partial_{t}H_{2\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C(\|v_{0}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\|h_{0}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{3}},T)

(3.5)

for any
T\in(0,+\infty)
.
To derive the desired result, we need to verify that
(\Delta v_{2\epsilon},\Delta H_{2\epsilon})
and each term of
f_{1},f_{2}
are uniformly bounded in
L^{2}(0,T;{H}^{-\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, respectively.
For
\Delta v_{2\epsilon}
, employing the Hölder’s inequality, we can get

\displaystyle\|\Delta v_{2\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

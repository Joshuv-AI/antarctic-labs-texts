Intelligent construction method for rock slope fracture network model based on discrete element and neural network
Authors: Zhe Zhang, Huailin Chen, Honggang Wu, Tao Yang, Bokai Li, Hanqin Teng, Haotian Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
To address the engineering challenge of accurately characterizing complex internal fracture networks in high-steep rock slopes, this study proposes an intelligent Discrete Fracture Network (DFN) construction method integrating field adit data and intelligent algorithms. Taking the Dadu River Chengdu Bank rock slope as a case study, spatial distribution characteristics of adit fractures were obtained through engineering geological surveys. A 3D DFN generation model framework was established using Monte Carlo stochastic principles. Multiple numerical simulation experiments were designed using 3DEC software to generate a data sample library encompassing geometric parameters such as fracture orientation, trace length, and spatial position. A mapping relationship between adit fracture characteristic parameters and DFN model parameters was constructed using Back Propagation (BP) and Cascade Correlation (CC) neural networks. Through model structure optimization and hyperparameter training, optimal prediction models were obtained. Results indicate that the exploration adit fracture image format features a resolution of 600 × 9000 pixels, green color, and 70% transparency for fracture traces. Under a stratified modeling strategy, prediction errors for different lithological units were 18.4% for the four-layer BP network in diorite regions, 8.0% for the three-layer BP network in cataclastic rock regions, and 10.3% for the CC network in granite regions, validating the applicability of the intelligent algorithms. This method achieves high-precision inversion of fracture networks using limited adit data, providing a novel approach for modeling complex rock mass structures. The findings have been applied to the Dadu River Grand Bridge slope engineering project.

Introduction

The stability evaluation of rock slopes remains a critical issue in geotechnical engineering1, fundamentally relying on the precise characterization of complex fracture network spatial distributions2–4. As a key factor controlling rock mass mechanical behavior, the Discrete Fracture Network (DFN) model explicitly characterizes fracture geometry (orientation, trace length, density) and spatial topology, serving as an essential tool for rock mechanics analysis5,6. The 3D reconstruction of DFN directly influences slope stability analysis, seepage simulation, and engineering support design reliability7. Traditional methods primarily rely on 2D statistical inferences from borehole cores or surface exposures to estimate 3D fracture parameters8. However, such stochastic modeling approaches based on simplified assumptions struggle to capture spatial heterogeneity in deep rock mass structures9–11. Particularly in high-steep slope engineering, limited by exploration costs and technical constraints, adit exploration data often become the sole means to obtain deep fracture information. Nevertheless, transforming limited 2D adit observations into high-fidelity 3D DFN models remains a significant technical challenge12,13.

Current DFN modeling research primarily follows two directions: probabilistic statistical parameterization14 and machine learning-based intelligent inversion. In statistical modeling, Robertson’s15 disk-shaped fracture model laid the foundation for discrete fracture geometric characterization. Subsequent studies introduced Fisher distributions to describe fracture orientation clustering and established statistical frameworks for fracture density and trace length power-law distributions. However, these methods rely on isotropic assumptions, failing to represent tectonic-induced fracture orientation zoning15,16. Recent advancements in numerical tools like 3DEC enable Monte Carlo stochastic simulations to model DFN parameter spatial variability17,18. Yet, parameter calibration remains constrained by low-resolution borehole data, leading to scale effects in multi-scale fracture system modeling19.

Machine learning offers new avenues for DFN parameter inversion. Early studies utilized Support Vector Machines (SVMs) to correlate fracture orientation with mechanical parameters, while deep learning demonstrates superior nonlinear mapping capabilities20. For instance, Xue21 integrated whole convolutional neural networks with vectorization algorithms to statistically analyze fracture dimensions. Zhou22 proposed a non-parametric generative adversarial network (GAN)-based model named FracGen, which reconstructs and upscales natural fracture networks from a single outcrop image without explicit parameterization.Zhang23 employed residual U-Net architectures for road fracture recognition, and Cha24 applied convolutional neural networks for concrete fracture detection. Despite progress, limitations persist: (1) Cross-dimensional mapping between 2D adit data and 3D DFN parameters remains unclear, and traditional feature engineering struggles to capture multi-scale fracture correlations25; (2) Existing models lack stratified strategies to address spatial heterogeneity caused by multi-phase tectonic activities9,11.

This study proposes an intelligent DFN construction framework integrating discrete element simulations and hybrid neural networks. Focusing on the Dadu River Grand Bridge slope, multi-source data (adit surveys, numerical experiments) were fused to build a fracture parameter sample library. Key innovations address: (1) inadequate representation of fracture spatial zoning in statistical models26; (2) limited generalization of machine learning models under small-sample conditions27; (3) cross-scale mapping errors from 2D adit data to 3D DFN parameters. A stratified modeling strategy was introduced, constructing differentiated neural networks for lithological units (diorite, cataclastic rock, granite). Engineering applications demonstrate a 18.4–55.6% reduction in parameter inversion errors compared to traditional methods, offering a new pathway for digital reconstruction of complex rock mass structures.

Methods

DFN modeling framework and fracture orientation

The construction of the Discrete Fracture Network (DFN) model is grounded in Monte Carlo stochastic simulation principles, implemented within the 3DEC 7.0(Itasca, http://www.itascacg.com/software/3DEC) discrete element software environment to capture the inherent randomness and anisotropy of fracture systems in rock slopes. The modeling process necessitates the definition of a set of key geometric parameters that collectively govern the spatial distribution and characteristics of the fractures.

The fracture orientation in 3DEC is characterized by dip angle(θ) and dip direction(φ). The spatial distribution of fracture orientations was represented using the widely adopted Fisher distribution model. As demonstrated in Reference, the implementation of the Fisher probability distribution model requires the establishment of a transformed spherical coordinate system through coordinate conversion.

As illustrated in Fig. 1, the original Cartesian coordinate system (OX, OY, OZ) was rotated about the origin to align the axis of the new coordinate system (ox′,oy′,oz′) with the normal vector direction of the mean fracture plane, while maintaining the axis within the original plane. Here, θ0 denotes the mean dip angle of fracture orientations, and φ0 represents the mean dip direction.

The spatial positions of fracture centers are generated using a homogeneous Poisson process29, providing a statistically uniform distribution which, while a simplification, is compatible with 3DEC’s built-in algorithms and practical given the constraints of field data extrapolation. Given the complexity of natural fracture surfaces and limitations in field characterization, fracture geometry is abstracted using a widely-adopted discoidal model30. This simplification effectively captures essential geometric attributes while maintaining computational tractability for large-scale DFN generation.

DFN model construction in 3DEC

The DFN modeling in 3DEC begins with systematic preparatory steps: initializing a pseudorandom seed via a linear congruential generator, defining strict computational domain boundaries to confine fractures, numerically reconstructing field-based adit geometries, and integrating three key fracture distribution characteristics—orientation (Fisher distribution), spatial position (Poisson process), and size scaling (power-law with boundaries)—into a unified generation template.

The DFN model is constructed using a function-driven approach based on three parameters: random seed, domain boundaries, and a predefined template. The process iteratively generates fractures while monitoring intersection traces with simulated adit surfaces. Iteration terminates when the cumulative trace count matches field-observed fracture frequency, ensuring geometric consistency between the model and actual geological conditions.Based on the described construction method, the complete construction flowchart of the DFN model is finally established as shown in Fig. 4.

Stratified DFN modeling strategy and parameterization

To address the significant discrepancy between the single DFN model simulation results and the actual field observations (as illustrated in Fig. 9), a stratified modeling approach was adopted. Based on the engineering geological survey and fracture distribution within the exploration adit, the study area was divided into three distinct lithological units: diorite (0–30 m), cataclastic rock (30–60 m), and granite (60–90 m). Each unit exhibits unique fracture geometric characteristics, necessitating the construction of separate DFN sub-models tailored to their specific properties (Table 2).

Image-based fracture feature extraction and optimization

The rock fracture analysis programme PCAS is used for the extraction of discrete fracture information from the statistical map of each fracture trace. By studying the influence of different image factors (such as pixels, colours, etc.) on the calculation accuracy of the program PCAS to determine the optimal image form, and then realize the effective acquisition of fracture information with the help of the program PCAS.

Image Pixel Resolution: The analysis considered a range of pixel values from 100 × 1500 to 800 × 12,000. As shown in Fig. 13, the relative error in total trace length minimized at 700 × 10,500 pixels, with an average processing time of 30.7 s. However, given the large number of images to process and the marginally higher error at 600 × 9000 pixels (with a significantly reduced processing time of 18.2 s), the latter was selected as the optimal resolution for practical efficiency.

Data sample construction and feature analysis

Each data sample consists of two components: input parameters and output parameters. The input parameters are derived from a comprehensive statistical analysis of the geometric distribution characteristics of exploration adit fractures, focusing on three core aspects: fracture orientation (occurrence), size (length), and spatial location (centroid coordinates). These are further categorized based on whether they describe “whole fractures” or “penetrating fractures” within the adit, utilizing the statistical maps shown in Figs. 11 and 12.

A total of 23 input parameters were defined to quantitatively capture the fracture geometry (see Table 7). These include:

For whole fractures: Parameters describing fracture dip (mean, standard deviation, concentration interval upper limit, number in concentration, normality fit), size (total length, standard deviation, min/max values, fractal dimension and fit), and location (mean and standard deviation of centroid coordinates, concentration upper limit, number in concentration, fractal dimension and fit).

For penetrating fractures: Parameters including mean dip, mean length, mean/min/max centroid coordinates, and total number of penetrating fractures.

Intelligent construction of DFN models based on neural networks

Currently, neural networks have been successfully applied in pattern recognition, signal processing, combinatorial optimisation and data mining31. In this paper, we propose to use the created data samples to establish error back propagation neural network (‘BP’ neural network) and cascade correlation neural network (‘CC’ neural network), so as to establish a one-to-one mapping relationship between the geometric distribution characteristics of the flat hole fracture and the key parameters of the DFN model. A one-to-one mapping between the geometric distribution of the flat hole fractures and the key parameters of the DFN model is established.

Constructing neural network prediction models

The intelligent construction of the DFN model relies on establishing an accurate mapping between the geometric characteristics of adit fractures and the key parameters controlling DFN generation. This was achieved using two types of supervised neural networks: the Back Propagation (BP) neural network and the Cascade Correlation (CC) neural network. Both were designed to predict the six critical DFN parameters—density coefficient k, length index α, minimum and maximum fracture sizes lmin and lmax, and elevation bounds Hbottom and Htop—based on 23 input features that quantitatively describe the fracture geometry from exploration adit traces.

The BP and CC networks were structured and trained with careful attention to hyperparameter optimization and model validation. For the BP network, both three-layer (single hidden layer) and four-layer (two hidden layers) architectures were tested, with the number of hidden neurons determined iteratively within a range of 6 to 29. The CC network was designed to dynamically grow its hidden layer during training, with a maximum of 20 hidden neurons. All models used the hyperbolic tangent (tanh) activation function and were trained to minimize the mean squared error (MSE) between predictions and targets. The dataset of 625 samples per lithological unit was normalized to the range [− 1, 1] and split into training (60%), validation (20%), and testing (20%) subsets. Hyperparameters, including learning rate and structural settings, were optimized via a promotion prediction algorithm and grid search, with optimal configurations for each geological zone provided in Tables 9 and 10.

The trained neural networks demonstrated strong performance, with all six output parameters exhibiting prediction errors under 30% on the test set (Table 11). Stability was confirmed through loss convergence curves without overfitting (Fig. 20). The resulting models were applied to predict DFN parameters tailored to the specific fracture characteristics observed in each lithological segment of the adit, enabling the intelligent and stratified reconstruction of the fracture network.

Neural network parameters and function selection

Prior to model establishment, the optimal ranges for both structural and learning parameters of the neural networks must be determined. A limited number of iterative calculations are then performed within these ranges to select the best parameters.

For the BP network, while increasing hidden layers may reduce error, it can also lead to issues such as gradient dissipation. Therefore, this study adopts two structures: a three-layer (one hidden layer) and a four-layer (two hidden layers) BP network. The number of neurons(hBP) in each hidden layer was experimentally determined within a range of 6 to 29. Too few neurons restrict information flow and degrade performance, whereas too many cause exponential increases in training time, overfitting, and convergence to local minima.

For the CC network, which grows its structure dynamically, only the maximum number of hidden neurons(hmax CC) needs defining. Through simulation, this was set to 20. This approach efficiently identifies optimal configurations for both networks.

Due to the special characteristics of the CC neural network model structure and algorithm, there is no need to set the number of hidden layers and the number of hidden neurons, and it is only necessary to determine the maximum number of hidden neurons hmax CC. After several simulation experiments, hmax CC = 20.

For the determination of the range of values of the learning parameters of the network model, with reference to the results of many simulation experiments and the valuable experience of many scholars, the proposed learning step size εBP = εCC∈(0,0.5], and the learning step increment ∆εBP=∆εCC = 0.01; the output transformation coefficient ∆σ0 = 0.01 in the CC neural network, and the increment η∈(0,1]; weight coefficients g, and the increment ∆η = 0.01.

Next, it is necessary to determine the activation function and loss value function required for the training of the two neural network models, respectively.

Conclusion

This study develops an intelligent method for constructing discrete fracture network (DFN) models for rock slopes, integrating discrete element modeling and neural networks, and demonstrates its application to the Dadu River slope in Chengdu. The key findings are as follows:

The types of key parameters (fracture orientation, fracture size, fracture location and fracture shape) required to construct the DFN model are explored. Based on the program PCAS, the three factors of ‘image pixel’, ‘image fracture colour’ and ‘image fracture transparency’ are studied and analysed, and the optimal flat hole fracture image form is finally determined as follows: image pixel 600*9000, green colour and 70% transparency of the fracture trace.Based on the analysis results of PCAS, the best image of the flat hole was determined from the three aspects of ‘fracture inclination’, ‘fracture length’, ‘fracture centre point coordinates’, and From the perspective of ‘full fracture of flat hole’ and ‘penetrating fracture of flat hole’, we comprehensively extracted the geometric distribution characteristics of flat hole fractures, with a total of 23 kinds of parameters; we created the data samples required for the training of the neural network model in groups, with a total of 625 groups of data samples in each large group, and each group of data samples consisted of 23 kinds of parameters. Limitations and future work

Despite the promising results, this study has several limitations that should be acknowledged:

Reliance on a single exploration adit: The intelligent DFN model was constructed and validated primarily based on fracture data from a single exploration adit. While the stratified modeling strategy alleviates this issue to some extent by considering lithological variations, the representativeness of the model for the entire slope and its generalizability to other slopes with different geological settings require further verification with more field data from multiple boreholes or adits.

Inherent 2D-to-3D ambiguity: The method infers 3D fracture parameters from 2D trace maps obtained in the adit. This process is inherently associated with ambiguity, as different 3D fracture geometries can produce identical 2D traces. Although the neural network learns a mapping based on massive simulations, this fundamental uncertainty remains a challenge in discrete fracture network modeling.

Lack of mechanical calibration: The current model focuses on the geometric accuracy of the fracture network. It has not been calibrated or validated against mechanical responses of the rock mass (e.g., deformation, failure patterns). Future work should integrate the generated DFN into mechanical simulations (e.g., using 3DEC for stress analysis) to compare with field monitoring data, ensuring the model’s effectiveness in stability analysis.

Assumption of Spatially Homogeneous Fracture Distribution: The spatial locations of fracture centers were generated using a homogeneous Poisson process, assuming a random and uniform distribution. This assumption may not capture potential clustering effects of fractures influenced by tectonic activities, which could affect the accuracy of subsequent analyses like seepage or rock block stability.

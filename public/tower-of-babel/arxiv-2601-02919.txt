1.
Introduction

Let
q
be a prime power and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field with
q
elements. We call a polynomial
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
a
permutation polynomial
(PP) of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
when the map
f:a\mapsto f(a)
from
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a bijection. Permutation polynomials garnered significant attention for over a century, dating back to the foundational work of Hermite and Dickson. There has been tremendous progress in finding new classes of permutation polynomials over finite fields in the last two decades.

It is well known that every mapping from
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
can be represented by a unique polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of degree at most

q-1
. So the inverse map of a permutation polynomial is given by a polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of degree at most
q-1
. The unique polynomial
g(x)
such that
f(g(x))=g(f(x))=x
for each
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
is called the
compositional inverse
of
f(x)
.

In 1991, Mullen
[
1
]
posed the problem of efficiently computing the coefficients of the inverse of a PP.
While the existence of such an inverse is guaranteed by the bijective nature of
f(x)
, determining the explicit algebraic form of
g(x)
is a difficult problem. The Lagrange interpolation formula provides a theoretical solution but typically yields a polynomial with all possible terms, offering little insight into the algebraic structure and being highly computationally complex. Explicitly obtaining a PP’s compositional inverse is useful, as many applications require both the PPs and their inverses.
For example, in Block ciphers like the Advanced Encryption Standard (AES), PPs and their inverses plays an essential role. The PPs and their inverses are also useful in public key cryptography and coding theory
[
8
,
9
]
.

In recent years, the compositional inverses of several classes of permutation polynomials have been obtained. Q. Wang
[
23
]
has surveyed the known results on compositional inverses of permutation polynomials over finite fields and summarized the current methods used in their study, including the piecewise method, the decomposition method, the commutative diagram method, the local method, and the experimental method.

2.
Auxiliary results

Throughout the paper we take
q=2^{m}
. Let
{\mbox{Tr}}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\alpha x)=\alpha x+\alpha^{q}x^{q}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[x]
be a linearized polynomial. We can easily see that the mapping from
(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}},+)
to
(\mathbb{F}_{q},+)
given by
{\mbox{Tr}}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\alpha x)
is a surjective homomorphism. Clearly,
\Large({\mbox{Tr}}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\alpha x)\Large)^{q}={\mbox{Tr}}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\alpha x)
for each
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.

Next two Lemmas are used in the sequel.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
be a finite field with
m
an odd number. Then
f(x)=x^{3}
is a permutation polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
, and its inverse
f^{-1}(x)=x^{t}
where
3t\equiv 1\mod{(2^{m}-1)}
.

Proof.

The proof is trivial by noting the fact that
\gcd(3,2^{m}-1)=1
for an odd integer
m
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
q=2^{m}
and
\beta,\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
be such that
\beta\neq c\delta
for any
c\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}
. Then
\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q}\neq 0
.

Proof.

If
\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q}=0
, then we have

\beta\delta^{-1}={(\beta\delta^{-1})}^{q}
. Equivalently, we have
\beta\delta^{-1}=c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
or
\beta=c\delta
, a contradiction.
∎

The following Lemma is crucial in finding the compositional inverses of permutation polynomials in the next Section.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
q=2^{m}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\alpha)=1
. Let
\beta,\delta\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{2}}
be such that
\beta\neq c\delta
for any
c\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}
.
Then

T(x)=\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)
is a permutation polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and its inverse is

T^{-1}(x)=(\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q})^{-1}\{\delta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)x)+\beta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x)\}.

Proof.

We can easily see that
\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)y)+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(y)=y
. We consider the equation
T(x)=y
or equivalently, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)=\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)y)+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(y).

(2.1)

Since
\{1,\alpha\}
is a basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, therefore from equation
2.1
, we get the following system of equations

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)y)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(y)

The above system of equations can be written in matrix form as follows

\begin{bmatrix}\beta&\beta^{q}\\
\delta&\delta^{q}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x\\
x^{q}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)y)\\
\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(y)\end{bmatrix}

Operating
R_{1}\rightarrow\delta^{q}R_{1}
and
R_{2}\rightarrow\beta^{q}R_{2}
, we get

\begin{bmatrix}\beta\delta^{q}&\delta^{q}\beta^{q}\\
\delta\beta^{q}&\beta^{q}\delta^{q}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x\\
x^{q}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\delta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)y)\\
\beta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(y)\end{bmatrix}

Operating
R_{1}\rightarrow R_{1}+R_{2}
, we get

\begin{bmatrix}\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q}&0\\
\delta\beta^{q}&\beta^{q}\delta^{q}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x\\
x^{q}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\delta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)y)+\beta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(y)\\
\beta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(y)\end{bmatrix}

From the above system of equations, we get

(\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q})x=\delta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)y)+\beta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(y)

This implies

T^{-1}(x)=(\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q})^{-1}\{\delta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)x)+\beta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x)\}

∎

The following Lemma is used in the next Section.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
q=2^{m}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
be such that
\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\alpha)=1
. Then

\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta T^{-1}(x))=\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)x)
and

\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta T^{-1}(x))=\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x)
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta T^{-1}(x))=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}[\beta(\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q})^{-1}\{\delta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)x)

\displaystyle+\beta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x)\}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}[(\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q})^{-1}\beta\delta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)x)

\displaystyle+(\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q})^{-1}\beta\beta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q})^{-1}\beta\delta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)x)

\displaystyle+(\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q})^{-1}\beta^{q}\delta\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)x)

\displaystyle+(\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q})^{-1}\beta\beta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x)

\displaystyle+(\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q})^{-1}\beta\beta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q})^{-1}\beta\delta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)x)

\displaystyle+(\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q})^{-1}\beta^{q}\delta\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q})^{-1}(\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q})\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)x)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta T^{-1}(x))=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}[\delta(\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q})^{-1}\{\delta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)x)

\displaystyle+\beta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x)\}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}[(\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q})^{-1}\delta\delta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)x)

\displaystyle+(\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q})^{-1}\delta\beta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q})^{-1}\delta\delta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)x)

\displaystyle+(\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q})^{-1}\delta^{q}\delta\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)x)

\displaystyle+(\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q})^{-1}\delta\beta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x)

\displaystyle+(\beta\delta^{q}+\delta\beta^{q})^{-1}\beta\delta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x)

∎

3.
Compositional Inverses of Permutation Polynomials

In this section we present the compositional inverses of six classes of permutation polynomials of the form
x+\gamma\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(h(x))
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
q=2^{m}
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then the compositional inverse of permutation polynomial

f_{1}(x)=x+\gamma\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x^{3}+x^{q+2})
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
is

x+\gamma(\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x))^{3}
.

Proof.

We take
T(x)=\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)
as defined in Lemma
2.3
and find
f_{1}\circ T(x)=f_{1}(T(x))
.
We have,

\displaystyle f_{1}\circ T(x)=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)+\gamma\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}[\{\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)\}^{3}

\displaystyle+\{\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)\}^{q+2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)+\gamma\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}[\{\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)\}^{2}\{\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)

\displaystyle+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)+(\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x))^{q}\}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)+\gamma\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}[\{\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)\}^{2}\{\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)

\displaystyle+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)+\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)+\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)\}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)+\gamma[\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)]^{3}

(3.1)

For any arbitrary element
y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
,
T(y)=\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta y)+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta y)
is an arbitrary element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.
We consider the equation
f_{1}\circ T(x)=T(y)=\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta y)+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta y)
. Since
\{1,\alpha\}
is basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
comparing each side, we get the following system of equations

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta y)

(3.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta y)+\gamma(\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta y))^{3}

(3.3)

We write the above equations in matrix form as follows

\begin{bmatrix}\beta&\beta^{q}\\
\delta&\delta^{q}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x\\
x^{q}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta y)+\gamma(\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta y))^{3}\\
\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta y)\end{bmatrix}

Operating
R_{1}\rightarrow\delta^{q}R_{1}
and
R_{2}\rightarrow\beta^{q}R_{2}
, we get

\begin{bmatrix}\beta\delta^{q}&\beta^{q}\delta^{q}\\
\beta^{q}\delta&\beta^{q}\delta^{q}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x\\
x^{q}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\delta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta y)+\delta^{q}\gamma(\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta y))^{3}\\
\beta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta y)\end{bmatrix}

Operating
R_{1}\rightarrow R_{1}+R_{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\beta\delta^{q}+\beta^{q}\delta&0\\
\beta^{q}\delta&\beta^{q}\delta^{q}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}x\\
x^{q}\end{bmatrix}=A

(3.4)

where,

\displaystyle A=\begin{bmatrix}\delta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta y)+\delta^{q}\gamma(\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta y))^{3}+\beta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta y)\\
\beta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta y)\end{bmatrix}

From the equation
3.4
, we get

x=(\beta\delta^{q}+\beta^{q}\delta)^{-1}\{\delta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta y)+\delta^{q}\gamma(\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta y))^{3}+\beta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta y)\}

or equivalently, we have

\displaystyle T^{-1}\circ f_{1}^{-1}\circ T(y)=

\displaystyle(\beta\delta^{q}+\beta^{q}\delta)^{-1}\{\delta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta y)+\delta^{q}\gamma(\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta y))^{3}

\displaystyle+\beta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta y)\}

Substituting
y=T^{-1}(x)
in above equation, we get

\displaystyle T^{-1}\circ f_{1}^{-1}(x)=

\displaystyle(\beta\delta^{q}+\beta^{q}\delta)^{-1}\{\delta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta T^{-1}(x))+\delta^{q}\gamma(\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta T^{-1}(x)))^{3}

\displaystyle+\beta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta T^{-1}(x))\}

Now using lemma 2.2, we get

\displaystyle T^{-1}\circ f_{1}^{-1}(x)=

\displaystyle(\beta\delta^{q}+\beta^{q}\delta)^{-1}\{\delta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)x)+\delta^{q}\gamma(\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x))^{3}

\displaystyle+\beta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x)\}

(3.5)

Since
T(x)=\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)
, we see that

\displaystyle f_{1}^{-1}(x)=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}\{\beta(T^{-1}\circ f_{1}^{-1}(x))\}+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}\{\delta(T^{-1}\circ f_{1}^{-1}(x))\}

(3.6)

Next, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}\{\beta(T^{-1}\circ f_{1}^{-1}(x))\}=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}[\beta(\beta\delta^{q}+\beta^{q}\delta)^{-1}\{\delta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)x)

\displaystyle+\delta^{q}\gamma(\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x))^{3}+\beta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x)\}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}[\beta\delta^{q}(\beta\delta^{q}+\beta^{q}\delta)^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)x)

\displaystyle+\beta\delta^{q}(\beta\delta^{q}+\beta^{q}\delta)^{-1}\gamma(\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x))^{3}

\displaystyle+\beta\beta^{q}(\beta\delta^{q}+\beta^{q}\delta)^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)x)+\gamma(\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x))^{3}

(3.7)

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta(T^{-1}\circ f_{1}^{-1}(x)))=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}[\delta(\beta\delta^{q}+\beta^{q}\delta)^{-1}\{\delta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)x)

\displaystyle+\delta^{q}\gamma(\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x))^{3}+\beta^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x)\}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}[\delta\delta^{q}(\beta\delta^{q}+\beta^{q}\delta)^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)x)

\displaystyle+\delta\delta^{q}(\beta\delta^{q}+\beta^{q}\delta)^{-1}\gamma(\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x))^{3}

\displaystyle+\delta\beta^{q}(\beta\delta^{q}+\beta^{q}\delta)^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x)

(3.8)

Now, using equations
3.7
and
3.8
in equation
3.6
, we get

\displaystyle f_{1}^{-1}(x)=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}((1+\alpha)x)+\gamma(\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x))^{3}+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x+\gamma(\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(x))^{3}

∎

\displaystyle+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)\}^{q+2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)+\gamma\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}[(\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)+\{\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)

\displaystyle+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)\}^{2}\{1+\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)+\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)+\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\beta x)

\displaystyle+\alpha^{q}\operatorname{Tr}_{q}^{q^{2}}(\delta x)\}]

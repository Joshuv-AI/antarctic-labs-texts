Minimum and extremal process for a branching random walk outside the boundary case
Xinxin Chen, Haojie Hou

Abstract
This work extends the studies on the minimum and extremal process of a supercritical branching random walk outside the boundary case which cannot be reduced to the boundary case. We study here the situation where the log-generating function explodes at 1 1 and the random walk associated to the spine possesses a stretched exponential tail with exponent b ∈ ( 0 , 1 2 ) b\in(0,\frac{1}{2}) . Under suitable conditions, we confirm the conjecture of Barral, Hu and Madaule [Bernoulli 24(2) 2018 801-841], and obtain the weak convergence for the minimum and the extremal process. We also establish an a.s. infimum result over all infinity rays of this system.

1.1
Background introduction

A branching random walk on the real line
\mathbb{R}
is a discrete-time Markov process defined as follows. Initially, at generation
n=0
, a single particle is located at the origin. At generation
n=1
, this particle dies and produces a random number of offspring, whose positions are given by an i.i.d. copy of a point process
\mathscr{L}
. In generation
n=2
, each particle alive at generation
1
independently reproduces in the same manner as its parent. Specifically, a particle at position
x
generates offspring whose positions are determined by an independent copy of the translated point process
x+\mathscr{L}
. The process then continues inductively in this fashion.

We denote by
\mathbb{P}
the probability measure associated with the branching random walk. Let
\mathbb{T}
be the Galton-Watson tree describing the genealogy of the process, with root denoted by
\varnothing
. For a particle
u\in\mathbb{T}
, let
|u|\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\{0,1,\dots\}
denote its generation and
V(u)\in\mathbb{R}
its spatial position. We assume that the branching random walk is supercritical, i.e.
\mathbb{E}(\#\mathscr{L})>1
, so that it survives with positive probability. We also assume that
\mathbb{E}(\#\mathscr{L})<\infty

1

If
\mathbb{E}(\#\mathscr{L})=\infty
, the system may grow double exponentially.
so that the tree
\mathbb{T}
grows exponentially. For each
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the log-generating function

\phi(\beta):=\log\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{|u|=1}e^{-\beta V(u)}\right]=\log\mathbb{E}\left[\int e^{-\beta x}\mathscr{L}(\mathrm{d}x)\right]\;\in\;(-\infty,\infty].

We also assume that the point process
\mathscr{L}
is not almost surely supported on a deterministic lattice. Then
\phi
is strictly convex on its domain
\text{dom}(\phi)=\{\beta:\phi(\beta)<\infty\}
as long as
\text{dom}(\phi)
is non-trivial.

In this work, we are interested in the minimal position of the system at generation
n

M_{n}:=\inf_{|u|=n}V(u)

with the convention that

It is well known (see, e.g.,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{M_{n}}{n}=v:=-\inf_{\beta>0}\frac{\phi(\beta)}{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}.

Hammersley

(I)

(II)

(III)

In the first two cases (I)-(II), we can make a linear transformation

and see that

1.3
Proof strategies and discussions

Our proof strategy for Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
adapts the framework of
[
6
]
, originally developed by
[
2
]
. The key mechanism driving the weak convergence of
M_{n}
is that, with high probability, there is exactly one large jump whose timing is near
n
. In contrast to the polynomial decay case
[
6
]
, where the jump size scales as
-\frac{n}{(\log n)^{3}}=-n^{1+o(1)}
, the stretched exponential setting necessitates a finer tuning. We ultimately set the jump size to be
-(mn-An^{1-b}\log n)
for appropriate constants
m
and
A
.

We briefly explain here why the regime
b\in[\tfrac{1}{2},1)
is not treated in this work.
Let
\xi_{n}
be a particle attaining the minimum at generation
n
, i.e.,
V(\xi_{n})=M_{n}
,
and let
T\leq n
be the time at which a large jump occurs, with jump size

\zeta:=V(\xi_{T})-V(\xi_{T-1})
.

On the one hand, the second‑order fluctuation of the random walk
V(\xi_{n})-\zeta
is of order
n^{1/2}
. On the other hand, for
b\in(\tfrac{1}{2},1)
we have
n^{1-b}\log n=o(n^{1/2})
.
Consequently, the natural scaling for the large jump would become
-(mn-O(\sqrt{n}))

instead of
-(mn-An^{1-b}\log n)
. This shift shows that the density around the
large jump
\zeta
becomes strongly coupled with the Gaussian fluctuations of the
random walk, thereby changing the nature of the problem.

The borderline case
b=\tfrac{1}{2}
requires a different and more delicate analysis
compared to
b\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
, and is therefore left for future investigation.

Our proof strategy for Theorem
1.5
closely mirrors that developed in
[
4
]
. It rests on three main steps: (i) the branching property implies that the limit
a_{*}
is deterministic; (ii) the positivity
a_{*}>0
is derived from a first-moment method; and (iii) the finiteness
a_{*}<\infty
is obtained via a coupling argument.

(ii)

Combining (
2

2

Lemma 2.2

Assume (
1.6
) holds with
b\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
.

n

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\tau_{\zeta_{n}}^{(2)}\leq n\right)\lesssim n^{2}\zeta_{n}^{2(a+1-b)}e^{-2\lambda\zeta_{n}^{b}}.

(ii) When
n
is large enough, for any

2

2.21

1\leq i\leq p

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(S_{p}-y\in[z,z+1],\min_{i\leq j\leq p}S_{j}\geq y,\tau_{\zeta_{n}}=i,\tau_{\zeta_{n}}^{(2)}>p,E_{i}^{(p)}\right)

On the other hand, on the set

Lemma 2.3

Assume (
1.6
) holds with
b\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
. Then

\displaystyle\sup_{y<n^{(3-2b)/4}}\mathbf{P}(S_{n}-y\in[0,1])\lesssim ne^{-\alpha_{n}}.

(2.31)

Proof:
Noticing that we have the following upper bound:

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(S_{n}-y\in[0,1])

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}(S_{n}\leq y+1,\tau_{\zeta_{n}}>n)+\mathbf{P}\left(\tau_{\zeta_{n}}^{(2)}\leq n\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}(S_{n}-y\in[0,1],\tau_{\zeta_{n}}=i,\tau_{\zeta_{n}}^{(2)}>n)

\displaystyle=:J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}.

(2.32)

We first treat
J_{1}
. For any
y<n^{(3-2b)/4}
, combining Lemma
2.1
(ii) and the inequality that
\theta_{n}n^{(3-2b)/4}\lesssim n^{b-1}n^{(3-2b)/4}=n^{(2b-1)/4}=o(1)
, we have

\displaystyle J_{1}\lesssim e^{-\theta_{n}(mn-y-1)}\leq e^{\theta_{n}-\theta_{n}mn+\theta_{n}n^{(3-2b)/4}}\lesssim e^{-\theta_{n}mn}.

(2.33)

According to the definitions of
\theta_{n}
and
\widehat{\zeta}_{n}
in (
2.3
),

\displaystyle e^{-\theta_{n}mn}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\theta_{n}m(n+1)}=\exp\left\{-\left(\lambda\widehat{\zeta}_{n}^{b-1}-\frac{A_{2}\log\widehat{\zeta}_{n}}{\widehat{\zeta}_{n}}\right)\left(\widehat{\zeta}_{n}+A_{1}n^{1-b}\log n\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{-\lambda\widehat{\zeta}_{n}^{b}-\lambda A_{1}\widehat{\zeta}_{n}^{b-1}n^{1-b}\log n+A_{2}\log\widehat{\zeta}_{n}+o(1)\right\}

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{-\lambda(mn)^{b}+\lambda bA_{1}m^{b-1}\log n-\lambda A_{1}\widehat{\zeta}_{n}^{b-1}n^{1-b}\log n+A_{2}\log\widehat{\zeta}_{n}+o(1)\right\},

(2.34)

where in the last equality we used the Taylor’s expansion
\widehat{\zeta}_{n}^{b}=(mn)^{b}-bA_{1}m^{b-1}\log n+o(1)
. Noticing that

\displaystyle\lambda bA_{1}m^{b-1}\log n-\lambda A_{1}\widehat{\zeta}_{n}^{b-1}n^{1-b}\log n+A_{2}\log\widehat{\zeta}_{n}

(2.35)

\displaystyle=-\left((1-b)\lambda A_{1}m^{b-1}-A_{2}+\varepsilon_{n}\right)\log n

(2.36)

for some
\varepsilon_{n}=o(1)
,
according to our choice of
A_{1}
in (
2.4
) and the definition of
\alpha_{n}
, when
n
is large enough such that
a+(1-b)\lambda A_{1}m^{b-1}-A_{2}+\varepsilon_{n}\geq 3/2
,

\displaystyle e^{-\theta_{n}mn}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\alpha_{n}}\exp\left\{-\left(a+(1-b)\lambda A_{1}m^{b-1}-A_{2}+\varepsilon_{n}\right)\log n\right\}\leq\frac{1}{n^{3/2}}e^{-\alpha_{n}}.

(2.37)

Combining (
2.33
) and (
2.37
), it holds that

\displaystyle J_{1}\lesssim e^{-\alpha_{n}}.

(2.38)

For
J_{2}
, by Lemma
2.2
(i),
J_{2}
is bounded from above by

\displaystyle J_{2}\lesssim n^{2}\zeta_{n}^{2(a+1-b)}e^{-2\lambda\zeta_{n}^{b}}=e^{-\alpha_{n}}e^{-\lambda(mn)^{b}(1+o(1))}\lesssim e^{-\alpha_{n}}.

(2.39)

For
J_{3}
, recall the defintion of
E_{i}^{(n)}
in (
2.22
), then by (
2.30
) with
p=n
, we have

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{n}e^{-\alpha_{n}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left(S_{n}-y\in[0,1],\tau_{\zeta_{n}}=i,\tau_{\zeta_{n}}^{(2)}>n,E_{i}^{(n)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\alpha_{n}}+n\mathbf{E}\left(1_{E_{n}^{(n)}}\mathbf{P}(X+s-y\in[0,1])|_{s=S_{n-1}}\right).

(2.40)

Noticing that on
E_{n}^{(n)}
,
y-S_{n-1}<2n^{(3-2b)/4}-m(n-1)<-(mn-3n^{(3-2b)/4})
for large
n
,
J_{3}
is bounded from above by

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\alpha_{n}}+n\sup_{z<-(mn-3n^{(3-2b)/4})}\mathbf{P}(X\in[z,z+1])

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\alpha_{n}}+n\sup_{z<-(mn-3n^{(3-2b)/4})}\left(|z|^{a}e^{-\lambda|z|^{b}}\right)\asymp ne^{-\alpha_{n}}.

(2.41)

Therefore, combining (
2
), (
2.38
), (
2.39
) and (
2
), we arrive at the desired result.

Lemma 2.4

\displaystyle e^{\alpha_{n}}\mathbf{E}\left(1_{\mathcal{A}}1_{\{S_{p}-y\in[0,p^{(3-2b)/4}]\}}1_{E_{p}^{(p)}}H(S_{p}-y,X_{p})\right)

\displaystyle=\ell_{\infty}m^{a}(1+o(1))\mathbf{E}\left(1_{\mathcal{A}}1_{E_{p}^{(p)}}\int_{S_{p-1}-y+z\in[0,p^{(3-2b)/4}]}H(S_{p-1}+z-y,z)\mathrm{d}z\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-x}(L+1)\sum_{p=2[L^{4/(3-2b)}]}^{\infty}p^{|a|+1}e^{-\lambda(mp)^{b}}\lesssim(L+1)^{-2}e^{-x}.

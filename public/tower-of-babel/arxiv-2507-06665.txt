Distributions based on Stable Mixtures and Gamma-Stable Convolutions
Nomvelo Karabo Sibisi sbsnom005@myuct.ac.za

Abstract
This paper explores mixture distributions induced by a product of the positive stable random variable and a power of another positive random variable. The paper also considers the convolution of the stable density with a gamma density. These two constructs, mixing and convolution, suffice to generate a rich family of distributions. An example is the positive Linnik distribution, which is known to arise from a product involving stable and gamma random variables. We show that gamma-Linnik convolution gives the Mittag-Leffler distribution and the Mittag-Leffler Markov chain associated with the growth of random trees. Building on that, we construct a new family of distributions with explicit densities. A particular choice of parameters for this family yields the Lamperti-type laws associated with occupation times for Markov processes.



Keywords—
convolution, mixing;
gamma, beta, stable, Mittag-Leffler distributions;

Mittag-Leffler Markov chain;
positive Linnik law, Lamperti-type laws

1
Introduction

All results derived in this paper follow from two theorems that involve mixing and convolution of distributions,
with the positive stable distribution as a common factor.

First Theorem:

Let
S_{\alpha;z}\sim F_{\alpha}(\cdot|z)

(0<\alpha<1
,
z>0)

denote a positive stable random variable
where
F_{\alpha}(t|z)

(t>0)
, with density
f_{\alpha}(t|z)
,
is characterised by the Laplace transform

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-xS_{\alpha;z}}\right]

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-xt}\,f_{\alpha}(t|z)\,dt=e^{-zx^{\alpha}}\hskip 18.49988pt(x\geq 0)

(1)

Let
U\sim F
be another positive random variable with probability distribution
F
.

Theorem
1
states that
T_{\alpha,z}=S_{\alpha;z}\,U^{1/\alpha}
has a distribution

H_{\alpha}(\cdot|z)
with explicit density

\displaystyle h_{\alpha}(t|z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{\alpha}(t|zu)\,dF(u)\hskip 9.24994pt(t>0)

(2)

and Laplace transform
\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-xT_{\alpha,z}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-zx^{\alpha}U}\right]

(x\geq 0)
.
The mixture density (
2
) may be regarded as an example of
subordination for distributions on
(0,\infty)
(Feller
[
9
, XIII.7(
e
)]
).

Corollary
1.1
uses a representation due to Pollard
[
30
]
of the stable density

to show that, if
F
has moments
\{\mu_{k}\}
of all order,
then

admits the infinite series form

\displaystyle h_{\alpha}(t|z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-z)^{k}}{k!}\sin(\pi\alpha k)\,\frac{\Gamma(\alpha k+1)}{t^{\alpha k+1}}\;\mu_{k}

(3)

The study of laws induced by
S_{\alpha;z}\,U^{1/\alpha}
is not new. Notably, Pakes
[
24
, (4.4)]
considered
U_{\nu,1}\sim G(\nu,1)
(gamma distribution)
and termed the law of

the generalised positive Linnik law.
Bondesson
[

discussed

in a context that

We will explore various choices of

Second Theorem:

Let the Dirac delta function
\delta_{c}(t)\equiv\delta(t-c)
be the density of a jump at
t=c\geq 0
.
Also let
\rho_{\nu}(t)=t^{\nu-1}/\Gamma(\nu)

(t>0,\nu>0)
.

Theorem
2
states that the convolution
\rho_{\nu}\star f_{\alpha}(\cdot|z)
may be written as

\displaystyle\{\rho_{\nu}\star f_{\alpha}(\cdot|z)\}(t)

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{0}^{t}\rho_{\nu}(t-u)f_{\alpha}(u|z)du

(4)

\displaystyle=z^{\nu/\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{\alpha}(t|zu)\{\rho_{\nu/\alpha}\star\delta_{1}\}(u)\,du

(5)

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{\nu/\alpha}}{\Gamma(\nu/\alpha)}\int_{0}^{1}f_{\alpha}(t|z/u)\,u^{-\nu/\alpha}(1-u)^{\nu/\alpha-1}\,\frac{du}{u}

(6)

The first integral is the standard Laplace convolution form.
The second integral

Dirac delta function

The third integral arises from the change of variable

.

If we write
f_{\alpha}(t)\equiv f_{\alpha}(t|1)
, it is easy to see from (

) that

f_{\alpha}(t|u)\equiv f_{\alpha}(tu^{-1/\alpha})u^{-1/\alpha}
.
Hence, for
z=1
,
\{\rho_{\nu}\star f_{\alpha}(\cdot|1)\}(t)
in the form (
6
)
is equivalent to Ho
et al.

[
15
, Proposition 2.2(
iii
)]
, derived therein by a different approach
with
\rho_{\nu}\star f_{\alpha}
expressed as the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral
I_{+}^{\nu}\,f_{\alpha}
.
It is key to note that, in our case, we regard
\{\rho_{\nu}\star f_{\alpha}(\cdot|z)\}(t)
as a density in two variables
(z,t)
.
Depending on context, this may be a density in
t
given
z
or
vice versa
.

1.1
Novelty and Motivation

The novelty of this paper lies in Theorem
1
and Theorem
2

as well as in bringing them together
to generate a broad family of distributional laws based on
S_{\alpha;z}\,U^{1/\alpha}
for general positive

U\sim F
, with explicit densities in every case,
along with associated infinite series representations.
The highlight is a new family of laws that subsumes several well-studied instances.
At a minimum, the approach of providing explicit densities complements the common approach of specifying equalities in law
of various products of independent random variables without necessarily offering explicit forms of the densities involved.

The inspiration to study the family of laws described by Theorems

and

stems from the Mittag-Leffler
distributions, which arise in diverse areas of application. Prominently, they arise in the study of occupation times for Markov processes.
The Mittag-Leffler distribution, often denoted by
{\rm ML}(\alpha)
, arises as
the limiting distribution for the occupation time of a single-state (Darling and Kac
[
7
]
),
while multi-state cases lead to generalised arcsine laws
(Bertoin
et al.

[
3
]
, Feller
[
9
, XIV.3]
, James
[
16
,
17
]
,
James
et al.

[
19
]
,
Lamperti
[
22
]
, Pitman
[
27
,
28
]
).

In a study of random partitions of the integers,
Pitman
[
27
, Theorem 3.8]
derived the two-parameter Mittag-Leffler distribution
{\rm ML(\alpha,\theta})

(\theta>-\alpha)
as the limiting distribution of the number of partition blocks generated by
the Chinese restaurant process construction (Pitman
[
27
, 3.1]

Poisson-Dirichlet process
{\rm PD(\alpha,\theta})
of Pitman and Yor
[
29
]

{\rm ML(\alpha,\theta})
is also said to be the distribution of the

-diversity of
{\rm PD(\alpha,\theta})

a term inspired by application in ecology, where it refers to species diversity in a local habitat.
A derivation of

due to Janson
[

takes the form of a limiting distribution of a Pólya urn scheme
(also see Flajolet
et al.

[
10

).

In another context,

In the study of stable trees (Goldschmidt and Haas
[

, Rembart and Winkel
[
32
,
33
]
)
and stable graphs (Goldschmidt
et al.

[
11
]
),
the distributions that feature are Beta,
{\rm ML(\alpha,\theta})
,
Dirichlet and Poisson-Dirichlet
{\rm PD(\alpha,\theta})
.
James
[
18
]
, Sénizergues
[
35
]
discussed the appearance of

{\rm ML(\alpha,\theta})
as limit distributions in the growth of random graphs
inspired by the preferential attachment model due to Barabási and Albert
[
2
]
.

Our approach is to ‘stand back’ from the technical detail of application contexts and explore in the abstract distributional laws that obey
Theorems
1
and
2
.
The Mittag-Leffler laws turn out to be a part of that class, as do the “Lamperti-type laws” that were so-named by James
[

2.1
Gamma Distribution

The gamma distribution
G(\nu,\lambda)
with shape and scale parameters
\nu,\lambda>0
and density
\rho_{\gamma,\lambda}(t)
is

\displaystyle dG(t|\nu,\lambda)

\displaystyle\equiv\rho_{\nu,\lambda}(t)\,dt=\frac{\lambda^{\nu}}{\Gamma(\nu)}\,t^{\nu-1}\,e^{-\lambda t}dt\hskip 18.49988pt(t>0)

(7)

For
\lambda=0
, we write the unnormalised power density as
\rho_{\nu}(t)=t^{\nu-1}/\Gamma(\nu)
.
The Laplace-Stieltjes transform of
G(\nu,\lambda)
(or the Laplace transform of its density
\rho_{\nu,\lambda}
) is

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-xt}\,dG(t|\nu,\lambda)\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-xt}\,\rho_{\nu,\lambda}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{\nu}}{(\lambda+x)^{\nu}}

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt(x\geq 0)

(8)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-xt}\,\rho_{\nu}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=x^{-\nu}

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt(x>0)

(9)

We also define
\rho_{\nu=0}(t)=\delta(t)
with Laplace transform 1.

2.2
Beta Distribution

The beta distribution
{\rm Beta}(\alpha,\beta)
with density
{\rm beta}(t|\alpha,\beta)

t\in(0,1)
conditioned on
\alpha,\beta>0
is

\displaystyle d\,{\rm Beta}(t|\alpha,\beta)\equiv{\rm beta}(t|\alpha,\beta)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}\,t^{\alpha-1}(1-t)^{\beta-1}dt

(10)

In anticipation of later discussion, let
B_{\alpha,\beta}\sim{\rm Beta}(\alpha,\beta)
and
U\sim F
be independent random variables
where
F
has a density
f(u)
,
u>0
.
The joint density of
U=u,B_{\alpha,\beta}=v
and the product
U\times B_{\alpha,\beta}=t
is

\displaystyle\Pr(t,u,v)=\Pr(t|u,v)\Pr(u)\Pr(v)=\delta(t-uv)f(u){\rm beta}(v|\alpha,\beta)

is the Dirac delta density centred at
c
.
Hence
\Pr(t,u)=\Pr(t|u)\Pr(u)
is

\displaystyle\Pr(t,u)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\delta(t-uv)f(u){\rm beta}(v|\alpha,\beta)dv=f(u)\times\frac{1}{u}\,{\rm beta}(t/u|\alpha,\beta)\hskip 9.24994pt(u>t)

(11)

\displaystyle\implies\;\Pr(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{\infty}f(u){\rm beta}(t/u|\alpha,\beta)\,\frac{du}{u}=\int_{0}^{1}f(t/u)\,{\rm beta}(u|\alpha,\beta)\,\frac{du}{u}

(12)

The notation
T_{\alpha,\beta}\overset{d}{=}UB_{\alpha,\beta}
,
where
T_{\alpha,\beta}
has the distribution with density (
12
),
states equality in distribution of the random variables without giving explicit forms of associated densities.

Mittag-Leffler Distributions

The 2-parameter Mittag-Leffler distribution

\displaystyle p_{\alpha,\theta}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(1+\theta)}{\Gamma(1+\theta/\alpha)}\,t^{\theta/\alpha}\,p_{\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(1+\theta)}{\Gamma(1+\theta/\alpha)}\,\frac{1}{\alpha}f_{\alpha}(t^{-1/\alpha})\,t^{(\theta-1)/\alpha-1}\hskip 9.24994pt

\displaystyle(\theta>-\alpha)

(18)

is also referred to as the generalised Mittag-Leffler distribution and alternatively

]

It is known (James

]
) that, for

, the Laplace transform of

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-xt}p_{\alpha,\theta}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\Gamma(1+\theta)\,E^{1+\theta/\alpha}_{\alpha,1+\theta}(-x)\hskip 9.24994pt

\displaystyle(\theta>-\alpha)

) being

\displaystyle\mu_{\alpha,\theta;k}\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k}\,dP_{\alpha,\theta}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(1+\theta)\Gamma(1+k+\theta/\alpha)}{\Gamma(1+\theta/\alpha)\Gamma(1+k\alpha+\theta)}\hskip 9.24994pt

\displaystyle(\theta>-\alpha)

2.5
Gamma-Stable Convolution

The convolution
\{\rho_{\nu}\star f_{\alpha}(\cdot|z)\}(u)

of the power density
\rho_{\nu}(u)
and the stable density
f_{\alpha}(u|z)
is

\displaystyle\{\rho_{\nu}\star f_{\alpha}(\cdot|z)\}(u)=\int_{0}^{u}\rho_{\nu}(u-v)f_{\alpha}(v|z)dv=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\nu)}\int_{0}^{u}(u-v)^{\nu-1}f_{\alpha}(v|z)dv

(21)

where
0<\alpha<1,\nu>0,z>0
.
The Laplace transform of
\{\rho_{\nu}\star f_{\alpha}(\cdot|z)\}(u)
over
u
is
x^{-\nu}e^{-zx^{\alpha}}
.

Differentiating the Laplace transform
e^{-zx^{\alpha}}
of

gives

\displaystyle z\alpha x^{\alpha-1}e^{-zx^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-xu}\,uf_{\alpha}(u|z)\,du=z\alpha\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-xu}\{\rho_{1-\alpha}\star f_{\alpha}(\cdot|z)\}(u)\,du

(22)

The second equality arises because
x^{\alpha-1}e^{-zx^{\alpha}}
is the Laplace transform of
\{\rho_{1-\alpha}\star f_{\alpha}(\cdot|z)\}(u)
. Uniqueness of Laplace transforms implies that, for
\nu=1-\alpha

\displaystyle\{\rho_{1-\alpha}\star f_{\alpha}(\cdot|z)\}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{u}(u-v)^{-\alpha}f_{\alpha}(u|z)\,dv=\frac{1}{z\alpha}\,uf_{\alpha}(u|z)

(23)

This well-known property has inspired the study of generalised stable densities

defined by

for

(Jedidi

[

]
, Pakes
[

]

[

]

3
Main Theorems

Theorem 1
.

Consider two independent positive random variables

1.

S_{\alpha;z}\sim F_{\alpha}(z>0)

(0<\alpha<1)
, where
F_{\alpha}(z)
is the
\alpha
-stable distribution with density
f_{\alpha}(\cdot|z)

2.

U\sim F
, where
F
is some probability distribution.

Then the product
T_{\alpha,z}=S_{\alpha;z}\,U^{1/\alpha}
has a distribution
H_{\alpha}(z)
with mixture density

\displaystyle h_{\alpha}(t|z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{\alpha}(t|zu)\,dF(u)

(24)

with Laplace transform

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-xT_{\alpha,z}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-zx^{\alpha}U}\right](x\geq 0)
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

The density of
T_{\alpha,z}=S_{\alpha;z}\,U^{1/\alpha}\equiv t

for independent
S_{\alpha;z}\equiv x
and
U\equiv u
, with
\Pr(S_{\alpha;z}\equiv x)=f_{\alpha}(x|z)
is

\displaystyle\Pr(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\delta(t-xu^{1/\alpha})\,f_{\alpha}(x|z)dx\,dF(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\delta(t-y)\,f_{\alpha}(yu^{-1/\alpha}|z)u^{-1/\alpha}dy\,dF(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{\alpha}(t|zu)f(u)du\equiv h_{\alpha}(t|z)

where we have recalled that
f_{\alpha}(tu^{-1/\alpha}|z)u^{-1/\alpha}\equiv f_{\alpha}(t|zu)
.
The Laplace transform of (
24
) is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-xT_{\alpha,z}}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-xt}\,h_{\alpha}(t|z)\,dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zx^{\alpha}u}\,dF(u)=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-zx^{\alpha}U}\right]\hskip 18.49988pt(x\geq 0)

thereby completing the proof.
∎

Corollary 1.1
.

If

has finite moments

of all order,
then

\displaystyle h_{\alpha}(t|z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-z)^{k}}{k!}\sin(\pi\alpha k)\,\frac{\Gamma(\alpha k+1)}{t^{\alpha k+1}}\;\mu_{k}

.

Using the infinite series representation (

) of
f_{\alpha}(t|zu)
in (

) gives

\displaystyle h_{\alpha}(t|z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}{\rm Im}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-zu)^{k}}{k!}e^{-i\pi\alpha k}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-tv}\,v^{\alpha k}\,dv\right]dF(u)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-z)^{k}}{k!}\sin(\pi\alpha k)\,\frac{\Gamma(\alpha k+1)}{t^{\alpha k+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{k}\,dF(u)

∎

Theorem 2
.

Let
\rho_{\nu}(t)=t^{\nu-1}/\Gamma(\nu)

(\nu>0)
. Then

\displaystyle\{\rho_{\nu}\star f_{\alpha}(\cdot|z)\}(t)

\displaystyle=z^{\nu/\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{\alpha}(t|zu)\,\{\rho_{\nu/\alpha}\star\delta_{1}\}(u)du

(26)

\displaystyle=z^{\nu/\alpha}\int_{1}^{\infty}f_{\alpha}(t|zu)\,\rho_{\nu/\alpha}(u-1)\,du

(27)

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{\nu/\alpha}}{\Gamma(\nu/\alpha)}\int_{0}^{1}f_{\alpha}(t|z/u)\,u^{-\nu/\alpha}(1-u)^{\nu/\alpha-1}\,\frac{du}{u}

Proof of Theorem
2
.

The Laplace transform of

is

while that of the right hand side of (

) is

\displaystyle z^{\nu/\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zux^{\alpha}}\,\{\rho_{\nu/\alpha}\star\delta_{1}\}(u)du

\displaystyle=z^{\nu/\alpha}(zx^{\alpha})^{-\nu/\alpha}e^{-zx^{\alpha}}=x^{-\nu}e^{-zx^{\alpha}}

Hence the validity of (
26
) by uniqueness of Laplace transforms.
Explicitly,
\{\rho_{\nu/\alpha}\star\delta_{1}\}
is

\displaystyle\{\rho_{\nu/\alpha}\star\delta_{1}\}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{u}\rho_{\nu/\alpha}(u-v)\delta(v-1)dv=\rho_{\nu/\alpha}(u-1)\mathbbm{1}_{[1,\infty)}

which leads to (

).
Using
\rho_{\nu/\alpha}(u)=u^{\nu/\alpha-1}/\Gamma(\nu/\alpha)
and

in (

)
gives (

).
∎

4
Generalised Positive Linnik Law

Proposition 1
.

Consider two independent positive random variables

1.

S_{\alpha;z}\sim F_{\alpha}(z)

(0<\alpha<1)
, where
F_{\alpha}(z)
is the

\alpha
-stable distribution, density
f_{\alpha}(\cdot|z)

2.

G_{\gamma,\lambda}\sim G(\gamma>0,z>0)
, where
G(\gamma,\lambda)
is the Gamma distribution.

Then the product
S_{\alpha;z}\,G_{\gamma,\lambda}^{1/\alpha}
has a distribution on
(0,\infty)
with density

\displaystyle\ell_{\alpha}(x|\gamma,\lambda,z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{\alpha}(x|zu)\,dG(u|\gamma,\lambda)=\lambda^{\gamma}\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{\alpha}(x|zu)\,\rho_{\gamma}(u)\,e^{-\lambda u}\,du

(29)

The Laplace transform of

8
Discussion and Conclusion

We have solely used convolution and mixing induced by products of random variables to derive a rich family of distributions.
A common feature is the presence of the positive stable distribution in both constructs.
The highlight of the paper is the derivation of a novel and general family of distributions with explicit integral and series representations for their densities. They include the familiar Mittag-Leffler and Lamperti-type laws as special cases.
We have had no cause to mention the Dirichlet and Poisson-Dirichlet distributions, or random selection metaphors such as
Pólya urns or restaurant seating.

The family of distributions has Laplace transforms

\Gamma(\beta+\theta)\,E^{\gamma+\theta/\alpha}_{\alpha,\beta+\theta}(-zx^{\sigma})
.
This common form of Laplace transforms suggests referring to the whole family of distributions as Mittag-Leffler distributions of various levels of generality.
We have constructed all distributions directly through mixing and convolution without invoking any complex analytic inversion.
Such inversion, where it arises implicitly, is contained in the derivation by Pollard
[
30
]
of representation (
14
)
of the stable distribution.

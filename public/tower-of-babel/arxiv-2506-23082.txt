Hall–Littlewood expansions of chromatic quasisymmetric polynomials using linked rook placements
Jang Soo Kim, Seung Jin Lee, Meesue Yoo

Abstract
. In this work, we obtain a Hall–Littlewood expansion of the chromatic quasisymmetric function arising from a natural unit interval order and describe the coefficients in terms of linked rook placements. Applying the Carlsson–Mellit relation between chromatic quasisymmetric functions and unicellular LLT polynomials, we also obtain a combinatorial description for the coefficients of the unicellular LLT polynomials expanded in terms of the modified transformed Hall–Littlewood polynomials.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 05A15; Secondary: 05A30

1.
Introduction

The main objective of this paper is to provide a combinatorial formula
for the Hall–Littlewood expansion of the chromatic quasisymmetric
function in terms of linked rook placements. We first present the
background and motivation for this work.

The
immanant
of an
n\times n
matrix
A=(a_{i,j})

with respect to a partition
\lambda
of
n

is defined by

\operatorname{Imm}_{\lambda}(A)=\sum_{w\in S_{n}}\chi^{\lambda}(w)\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{i,w(i)},

where
\chi^{\lambda}
denotes the irreducible character of the
symmetric group
S_{n}
associated with
\lambda
. If

\lambda=(1^{n})
, the immanant becomes the determinant. The
Jacobi–Trudi formula expresses the (skew) Schur function

s_{\mu/\nu}
as

s_{\mu/\nu}=\det H_{\mu/\nu},

where

H_{\mu/\nu}=\left(h_{\mu_{i}-\nu_{j}-i+j}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
is the Jacobi–Trudi matrix of complete homogeneous symmetric
functions
h_{\lambda}
. This implies that

\operatorname{Imm}_{\lambda}H_{\mu/\nu}
is
s
-positive when

\lambda=(1^{n})
. Here, for a symmetric function
f
and a
basis
\{g_{\lambda}\}
of the space of symmetric functions, we say
that
f
is
g
-positive
if every coefficient

c_{\lambda}
in the expansion

f=\sum_{\lambda}c_{\lambda}g_{\lambda}
is nonnegative. Goulden and
Jackson
[
GJ92
]
conjectured that

\operatorname{Imm}_{\lambda}H_{\mu/\nu}
is
m
-positive for any

\lambda
, which was proved by Greene
[
Gre92
]
,
where
m_{\lambda}
is the monomial symmetric function. More
generally, Haiman
[
Hai93
]
showed that

\operatorname{Imm}_{\lambda}H_{\mu/\nu}
is
s
-positive for any partition

\lambda
.

In
[
Ste92
]
, Stembridge presented several conjectures concerning
immanants. Stanley and Stembridge
[
SS93
]
rephrased one of
them as follows. For partitions
\mu
and
\nu
with at most

n
parts, let

F_{\mu/\nu}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}s_{\lambda}(\bm{y})\operatorname{Imm}_{\lambda}H_{\mu/\nu}(\bm{x}),

where the sum is over all partitions
\lambda
of
n
, and

\bm{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots)
and
\bm{y}=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots)
are
independent variables. Let

E_{\mu/\nu}^{\theta}=E_{\mu/\nu}^{\theta}(\bm{y})
be the coefficient
of
s_{\theta}(\bm{x})
in
F_{\mu/\nu}(\bm{x},\bm{y})
, that is,

F_{\mu/\nu}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\sum_{\theta\vdash N}E_{\mu/\nu}^{\theta}(\bm{y})s_{\theta}(\bm{x}),

where
N=|\mu|-|\nu|
. The following is a restatement
in
[
SS93
, Conjecture 1.1]

of Stembridge’s conjecture
[
Ste92
]
.

Conjecture 1.1
.

The symmetric function
E_{\mu/\nu}^{\theta}
is
h
-positive.

This conjecture implies Haiman’s result that

\operatorname{Imm}_{\lambda}H_{\mu/\nu}
is
s
-positive. The case

\theta=(N)
is particularly interesting due to its connection
with chromatic symmetric functions introduced by Stanley

[
Sta95
]
. For a graph
G
with a vertex set

[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, the
chromatic symmetric function

X_{G}(\bm{x})
is defined by

X_{G}(\bm{x})=\sum_{\kappa}x_{\kappa(1)}\cdots x_{\kappa(n)},

where the sum is over all proper colorings

\kappa:[n]\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
of
G
. In

[
Sta95
, Conjecture 5.1]
, Stanley reformulated
1.1
for

\theta=(N)
in terms of chromatic symmetric functions
X_{G}(\bm{x})

and elementary symmetric functions
e_{\lambda}
as follows.

Conjecture 1.2
.

If
G
is the incompatibility graph of a natural unit interval
order, then
X_{G}(\bm{x})
is
e
-positive.

This conjecture is known as the
Stanley–Stembridge conjecture
.
In fact, the original conjecture was stated using a broader class of
posets, namely,
(3+1)
-free posets. However, Guay-Paquet

[
GP13
]
showed that the two statements are equivalent.
The Stanley–Stembridge conjecture has been studied extensively by
many researchers. We remark that it was recently proved by Hikita

[
Hik24
]
, and also by Griffin et al.
[
GMR+25
]
.

In the following, we highlight the connection between

1.1
and
1.2
that is described in
[
SS93
]
. Stanley
and Stembridge
[
SS93
, Proposition 4.1]
expressed

E_{\mu/\nu}^{(N)}
in terms of permutations with restricted
positions, which naturally correspond to rook placements on a subset
of the
[n]\times[n]
grid, where
[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. By
Equation (5.1) in
[
SS93
]
, we have

(1.1)

\omega(E^{(N)}_{\mu/\nu})=\sum_{\alpha\vdash n}r_{\gamma,\alpha}\left(\prod_{i\geq 1}m_{i}(\alpha)!\right)m_{\alpha},

where
\omega
is the involution sending
h_{\lambda}
to

m_{\lambda}
,
\gamma
is a certain (Ferrers) board in

[n]\times[n]
that is determined by the partitions
\mu
and

\nu
,
m_{i}(\alpha)
is the multiplicity of
i
in a
partition
\alpha
, and
r_{\gamma,\alpha}
is the number of
ways to place non-attacking rooks in the board
\gamma
whose type
is
\alpha
; see
Sections

2
and
3
for precise
definitions. Since the board
\gamma
is below the main diagonal
of the board
[n]\times[n]
, it can be identified with a Dyck
path. Moreover, it is known that there is a correspondence between
natural unit interval orders and Dyck paths
[
GPMR14
,
SR03
]
, so
we use the notation
X_{\gamma}(\bm{x})
to denote the chromatic
symmetric function indexed by the natural unit interval order
corresponding to the Dyck path
\gamma
.

On the other hand, considering the definition of a chromatic symmetric
function, one can deduce that the chromatic symmetric function

X_{\gamma}(\bm{x})
also satisfies the same formula:

(1.2)

X_{\gamma}(\bm{x})=\sum_{\alpha\vdash n}r_{\gamma,\alpha}\left(\prod_{i\geq 1}m_{i}(\alpha)!\right)m_{\alpha}.

Hence, by (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), we obtain

\omega(E^{(N)}_{\mu/\nu})=X_{\gamma}(\bm{x})
, which explains the
equivalence between
1.1
in the case
\theta=(N)
and

1.2
. The formula (
1.2
) is important because it
connects the two different objects
E^{(N)}_{\mu/\nu}
and

X_{\gamma}(\bm{x})
. The goal of this paper is to find a

q
-analogue of this formula.

There are well-known
q
-analogues of chromatic symmetric
functions and monomial symmetric functions. In
[
SW16
]
, Shareshian
and Wachs introduced the
chromatic quasisymmetric function

X_{G}(\bm{x};q)
for any graph
G
, which reduces to the
original chromatic symmetric function
X_{G}(\bm{x})
when

q=1
. They proved that
X_{G}(\bm{x};q)
is symmetric when

G
is the incomparability graph of a natural unit interval order.
The
Hall–Littlewood polynomials

P_{\mu}(\bm{x};q)
are
symmetric functions, which generalize both Schur functions and
monomial symmetric functions, namely,
P_{\mu}(\bm{x};0)=s_{\mu}(\bm{x})

and
P_{\mu}(\bm{x};1)=m_{\mu}(\bm{x})
.

The main result of this paper is a combinatorial description of the
Hall–Littlewood expansion of
X_{\gamma}(\bm{x};q)
, which gives a
natural
q
-analogue of (
1.2
). To define a

q
-analogue
r_{\gamma,\mu}(q)
of the number

r_{\gamma,\mu}
of rook placements with given conditions, we
introduce the notion of
linked rook placements
. Linked rook
placements are essentially the same as ordinary rook placements, but
they provide a useful visualization to determine the weight of a rook
placement. Our main result is then stated as follows; see

Section

3
for precise definitions.

Theorem 1.3
.

For a Dyck path
\gamma\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
, we have

(1.3)

X_{\gamma}({\bm{x}};q)=\sum_{\mu\vdash n}q^{{\rm area}(\gamma)-n(\mu)}r_{\gamma,\mu}(q)\left(\prod_{i\geq 1}[m_{i}(\mu)]_{q}!\right)P_{\mu}({\bm{x}};q).

In
[
GR86
]
, the
q
-rook polynomial

R_{k}(\gamma;q)
, for a Ferrers board
\gamma
, is defined by

R_{k}(\gamma;q)=\sum_{C}q^{\operatorname{inv}(C)},

where the sum is over the rook placements
C
of
k

non-attacking rooks in
\gamma
and
\operatorname{inv}(C)
is a certain
statistic. Note that when
q=1
, we have
R_{k}(\gamma;1)=r_{\gamma,(2^{k}1^{n-2k})}
.
Moreover, by considering the principal specialization of

X_{\gamma}(\bm{x};q)
, we can derive the following relation

R_{n-k}(\gamma;q)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\vdash n\\
\ell(\mu)=k\end{subarray}}r_{\gamma,\mu}(q),

which implies that
r_{\gamma,\mu}(q)
is a refinement of the original
q
-rook polynomial; see Section
5
for the details.

We prove
1.3
by showing that the right-hand side of
(
1.3
) satisfies the modular laws and a multiplicativity
property in
[
AN21
]
. Note that
q
-rook polynomials and

q
-hit polynomials have already appeared in the study of the
chromatic symmetric functions (cf.
[
AN21
,
CMP23
,
HOY25
]
). Hence the
existence of such a polynomial that refine
q
-rook polynomials
and that satisfy the modular laws may provide a better understanding of
various positivity phenomena in chromatic symmetric functions.

By applying the Carlsson–Mellit relation between the chromatic
symmetric functions and the unicellular LLT polynomials, we also
obtain the following modified Hall–Littlewood expansions of the
unicellular LLT polynomials.

Theorem 1.4
.

For a Dyck path
\gamma\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
, we have

\operatorname{LLT}_{\gamma}({\bm{x}};q)=(1-q)^{n-\ell(\mu)}\sum_{\mu\vdash n}q^{{\rm area}(\gamma)-n(\mu)}r_{\gamma,\mu}(q)\,\omega(H_{\mu}({\bm{x}};q))

and

\operatorname{LLT}_{\gamma}({\bm{x}};q)=(1-q^{-1})^{n-\ell(\mu)}\sum_{\mu\vdash n}r_{\gamma,\mu}(q^{-1})\tilde{H}_{\mu}({\bm{x}};q),

where
H_{\mu}(\bm{x};q)
is the modified Hall–Littlewood polynomial and
\tilde{H}_{\mu}({\bm{x}};q)=q^{n(\mu)}H_{\mu}({\bm{x}};q^{-1})
.

Remark 1.5
.

Recently, Griffin et al.
[
GMR+25
]
found a Macdonald
expansion of
X_{\gamma}({\bm{x}};q)
. As a special case
t=0
,
they obtained its Hall–Littlewood expansion. It would be interesting
to find a connection between our formula and theirs.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section

2
, we provide definitions and classical
background material that we use throughout the paper. In Section

3
, we introduce linked rook placements and extended rook
placements, which are crucial ingredients in describing the
Hall–Littlewood coefficients of the chromatic symmetric functions. In
Section
4
, we prove the main theorem by showing that the
Hall–Littlewood coefficients satisfy the modular laws and a
multiplicativity property, following Abreu–Nigro’s criterion (see Theorem

2.3
). We conclude this paper by presenting some open
problems that require further investigation in Section

5
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce necessary definitions and recall some
known results.

2.1.
Basic definitions

For a positive integer
n
, we use the notation
[n]
to denote
the set
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
and
[n]_{q}
to denote the
q
-integer

[n]_{q}=\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q}=1+q+\cdots+q^{n-1}
. We also define

(a;q)_{n}=(1-a)(1-aq)\cdots(1-aq^{n-1})
,

[n]_{q}!=[1]_{q}[2]_{q}\cdots[n]_{q}
, and

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}_{q}=\frac{[n]_{q}!}{[k]_{q}![n-k]_{q}!}
.

A
partition
is a weakly decreasing sequence

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{\ell})
of positive integers,
called
parts
. The number
\ell
of parts is called the

length
of
\lambda
, denoted by
\ell(\lambda)
. We
identify the partition
\lambda
with its
Young diagram

that consists of
\lambda_{i}
cells in the
i
-th row from the
top for all
i\in[\ell]
. If the sum of the parts of

\lambda
equals
n
, then we denote it by

\lambda\vdash n
. We also consider the partition

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{\ell})
as the infinite sequence

(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)
or a sequence

(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m})
for some
m>\ell
, where

\lambda_{i}=0
if
i>\ell
. The
conjugate
partition of

\lambda
, denoted
\lambda^{\prime}
, is defined as the partition
whose Young diagram is obtained from the Young diagram of

\lambda
by reflecting across the main diagonal. We denote by

\delta_{n}
the staircase partition

(n-1,n-2,\dots,2,1,0)
. For a partition
\lambda
, we
let
m_{i}(\lambda)
be the number of
i
’s in
\lambda

and
n(\lambda)=\sum_{i\geq 1}(i-1)\lambda_{i}
.

Given a partition
\lambda
, a
semistandard Young tableau

of shape
\lambda
is a filling of
\lambda
with elements in

\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that the entries are weakly increasing
along the rows and strictly increasing along the columns, from top to
bottom. For a semistandard Young tableau
T
, we let the

weight
of
T
, denoted by
\text{wt}(T)
, be the
sequence
(\text{wt}_{1}(T),\text{wt}_{2}(T),\dots)
, where

\text{wt}_{i}(T)
is the number of occurrences of
i
’s in

T
. We use
\operatorname{SSYT}(\lambda)
to denote the set of semistandard
Young tableaux of shape
\lambda
. We also use

\operatorname{SSYT}(\lambda,\mu)
to denote the set of semistandard Young
tableaux of shape
\lambda
and weight
\mu
.

A
Dyck path
is a lattice path from
(0,0)
to
(n,n)

consisting of north steps
N=(0,1)
and east steps
E=(1,0)

that never goes below the line
y=x
. Let
\mathcal{D}_{n}
denote the
set of Dyck paths from
(0,0)
to
(n,n)
, and let

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}=\cup_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{D}_{n}
be the set of all Dyck paths.
Given a Dyck path
\gamma\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
, let
a_{i}(\gamma)
, for

1\leq i\leq n
, denote the number of cells that lie below

\gamma
and above the line
y=x
, in the
i
-th row
counted from the left. We define the
area
of
\gamma
,
denoted
\operatorname{area}(\gamma)
, to be the sum of
a_{i}(\gamma)
, for

1\leq i\leq n
.

2.2.
Symmetric functions

Let
\bm{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots)
be an infinite sequence of variables.
We denote by
\Lambda_{q}=\Lambda_{q}(\bm{x})
the algebra of symmetric
functions in the variables
\bm{x}
with coefficients in

\mathbb{Q}(q)
. Each element in
\Lambda_{q}
is a formal power
series
f(\bm{x})
in the variables
\bm{x}
, with a bounded degree,
that is invariant under permutations of the variables. If there is no
possible confusion, we will omit the variables
\bm{x}
and simply
write
f
instead of
f(\bm{x})
.

For a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)
, the

monomial symmetric function

m_{\lambda}
is defined by

m_{\lambda}=\sum_{\nu}\bm{x}^{\nu}
, where the sum is over
all distinct permutations
\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\dots)
of the
infinite sequence
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)
and

\bm{x}^{\nu}=\prod_{i\geq 1}x_{i}^{\nu_{i}}
. The
elementary
symmetric function

e_{\lambda}
is defined by

e_{\lambda}=\prod_{i\geq 1}e_{\lambda_{i}}
, where

e_{0}=1
and

e_{k}=\sum_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}
, for

k\geq 1
.
The
complete homogeneous symmetric function

h_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
is defined by
h_{\lambda}=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}h_{\lambda_{i}}
, where
h_{0}=1
and
h_{k}=\sum_{i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{k}}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}
, for
k\geq 1
.
The
power sum symmetric function

p_{\lambda}
is
defined by
p_{\lambda}=\prod_{i\geq 1}p_{\lambda_{i}}
,
where
p_{0}=1
and
p_{k}=\sum_{i\geq 1}x_{i}^{k}
, for

k\geq 1
.

The
Schur functions

s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
were first
studied by Cauchy in
[
Cau15
]
, where he defined them as a ratio
of alternants: for a partition
\lambda
with at most
n

parts,

(2.1)

s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\frac{\det\left(x_{i}^{\lambda_{j}+n-j}\right)_{i,j=1}^{n}}{\det\left(x_{i}^{n-j}\right)_{i,j=1}^{n}}.

From this definition, we see that
s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
is
a symmetric function in the variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
with
homogeneous degree
|\lambda|
, and that

s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},0)=s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. This
allows us to define the Schur function
s_{\lambda}(\bm{x})
with
infinite variables
\bm{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots)
by

s_{\lambda}(\bm{x})=\lim_{n\to\infty}s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
,
which lies in
\Lambda_{q}
.

Later, an equivalent combinatorial description of
s_{\lambda}(\bm{x})

was found by Kostka
[
Kos82
]
:

(2.2)

s_{\lambda}(\bm{x})=\sum_{T\in\operatorname{SSYT}(\lambda)}\bm{x}^{T}=\sum_{\mu\vdash n}K_{\lambda\mu}m_{\mu}(\bm{x}),

where
\bm{x}^{T}=\prod_{i}x_{i}^{\text{wt}_{i}(T)}
and

K_{\lambda\mu}
is the number of semistandard Young tableaux of
shape
\lambda
and weight
\mu
, called the
Kostka
number
.

Plethysm is a certain type of substitution on symmetric functions. Here, we briefly introduce some plethystic
notations. For a fuller account, we refer the reader to
[
Hag08
,
Mac95
]
.

Let
E=E(t_{1},t_{2},\dots)
be a formal Laurent series in variables

t_{1},t_{2},\dots
with rational coefficients. For any symmetric
function
f\in\Lambda_{q}
, the
plethystic substitution

f[E]
is defined as follows. First, for a power sum symmetric
function
p_{k}
, we define
p_{k}[E]=E(t_{1}^{k},t_{2}^{k},\dots)
;
that is,
p_{k}[E]
is obtained by replacing each
t_{i}
in

E
by
t_{i}^{k}
. For a partition
\lambda
, define

p_{\lambda}[E]=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}p_{\lambda_{i}}[E]
.
Then, for a general
f\in\Lambda_{q}
, we define

f[E]=\sum_{\lambda}c_{\lambda}p_{\lambda}[E],

where the
c_{\lambda}
are the coefficients in the expansion

f=\sum_{\lambda}c_{\lambda}p_{\lambda}
.

Let
X=x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots
. Then for any

f\in\Lambda_{q}(\bm{x})
, we have
f[X]=f(\bm{x})
. Moreover, if

f
is homogeneous of degree
n
, then

(2.3)

f[-X]=(-1)^{n}\omega(f(\bm{x})),

where
\omega
is the involution on
\Lambda_{q}
sending

s_{\lambda}
to
s_{\lambda^{\prime}}
.

2.3.
Hall–Littlewood polynomials

The
Hall–Littlewood polynomials

[
Mac95
, p. 208]
are defined
as follows: for a partition
\mu
with at most
n
parts,

(2.4)

P_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};q)=\frac{1}{\prod_{i\geq 0}[m_{i}(\mu)]_{q}!}\sum_{w\in S_{n}}w\left(x_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\mu_{n}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{x_{i}-qx_{j}}{x_{i}-x_{j}}\right),

where
m_{i}(\mu)
is the number of
i
’s in

\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})
,
S_{n}
is the symmetric group on

[n]
, and
w
is the ring homomorphism with

w(x_{i})=x_{w(i)}
. From the definition, we have

P_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},0)=P_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. Hence, as in the
case of Schur functions, the Hall–Littlewood polynomials

P_{\mu}(\bm{x})
with infinite variables are defined by

P_{\mu}(\bm{x})=\lim_{n\to\infty}P_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
.

Note that by comparing the definition of Schur functions in
(
2.1
) we see that the Schur functions arise from the
Hall–Littlewood polynomials by setting
q=0
:

P_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};0)=s_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}).

Thus, Hall–Littlewood polynomials can be considered as

q
-analogues of Schur functions. Moreover, if
q=1
, then the
right-hand side of (
2.4
) becomes the monomial symmetric
function
m_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. Hence,
P_{\mu}(\bm{x};q)

generalizes both
m_{\lambda}(\bm{x})
and
s_{\lambda}(\bm{x})
.

In terms of the
modified Schur functions

s_{\lambda}[X(1-q)]
,
the Hall–Littlewood polynomials have the following expansion:

P_{\mu}(\bm{x};q)=\sum_{\lambda\vdash|\mu|}\frac{K_{\lambda\mu}(q)}{\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\mu)}(q;q)_{m_{i}(\mu)}}s_{\lambda}[X(1-q)].

Here,
K_{\lambda\mu}(q)
is the
Kostka–Foulkes polynomial

K_{\lambda\mu}(q)=\sum_{T\in\operatorname{SSYT}(\lambda,\mu)}q^{{\rm ch}(T)},

with
{\rm ch}(T)
the
charge
statistic (see
[
Mac95
]
for the details).
Then we can see that the expression
\prod_{i\geq 1}(q;q)_{m_{i}(\mu)}P_{\mu}(\bm{x};q)
has a positive
expansion in terms of
s_{\lambda}[X(1-q)]
, namely,

(2.5)

\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\mu)}(q;q)_{m_{i}(\mu)}P_{\mu}(\bm{x};q)=\sum_{\lambda\vdash|\mu|}K_{\lambda\mu}(q)s_{\lambda}[X(1-q)].

Note that (
2.5
) is equal to the integral form Macdonald polynomial
J_{\mu}(\bm{x};q,t)
, with
q=0
and
t
replaced
by
q
.

We define the
modified Hall–Littlewood polynomials

P_{\mu}(\bm{x};q)
by
replacing the modified Schur function by the Schur function in the
expansion (
2.5
):

H_{\mu}(\bm{x};q)=\sum_{\lambda\vdash|\mu|}K_{\lambda\mu}(q)s_{\lambda}(\bm{x}).

Lastly, we define the
modified transformed Hall–Littlewood polynomials

\tilde{H}_{\mu}(\bm{x};q)
by

\tilde{H}_{\mu}(\bm{x};q)=\sum_{\lambda\vdash|\mu|}\tilde{K}_{\lambda\mu}(q)s_{\lambda}(\bm{x}),

where
\tilde{K}_{\lambda\mu}(q)=q^{n(\mu)}K_{\lambda\mu}(q^{-1})
, with
n(\mu)=\sum_{i\geq 1}(i-1)\mu_{i}
.
Note that, by definition, we have

\tilde{H}_{\mu}(\bm{x};q)=q^{n(\mu)}H_{\mu}(\bm{x};q^{-1}).

2.6.
LLT polynomials

LLT polynomials are a family of symmetric functions introduced by
Lascoux, Leclerc, and Thibon in
[
LLT97
]
, which naturally arise in
the description of the power-sum plethysm operators on symmetric
functions. The original definition of LLT polynomials uses

cospin
statistic of ribbon tableaux, but Haiman and Bylund
found a consistent statistic, called
inv
, defined on

k
-tuples of semistandard Young tableaux of various skew shapes.
The Bylund–Haiman formula is described in
[
HHL+05
]
, where it is
proved that two definitions are equivalent.

Especially when the skew shape consists of single cells, the LLT
polynomials are called
unicellular
. In this case, the diagram
of a tuple of single cells corresponds to a Dyck path
[
CM18
,
Hag08
]
.
Since we only consider the unicellular LLT polynomials in this paper,
we define them over the set of Dyck paths.

Definition 2.4
.

Given a Dyck path
\gamma
, the
unicellular LLT polynomial
indexed by
\gamma
is

\operatorname{LLT}_{\gamma}(\bm{x};q)=\sum_{w\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{n}}q^{\operatorname{inv}(w)}\bm{x}^{w},

where
\operatorname{inv}(w)=|\{(i,j)\,:\,1\leq i<j\leq n,\,i\nless_{\gamma}j,\text{ and }w_{i}>w_{j}\}|
.

Combining the observations from Sections
2.5
and

2.6
, we see that both the unicellular LLT polynomials and
the chromatic quasisymmetric functions can be computed with respect to a Dyck
path
\gamma
, and that the
\operatorname{inv}
and
{\rm asc}

statistics essentially count the same pairs of integers. A subtle discrepancy
between the two definitions lies in the properness of the colorings
used when computing the chromatic quasisymmetric functions, as
opposed to the fact that all possible words of length
n
are
considered when computing
\operatorname{LLT}_{\gamma}(\bm{x};q)
. The precise
relationship between the two has been explained via plethysm by Carlsson and
Mellit in
[
CM18
, Proposition 3.4]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

[
CM18
, Proposition 3.4]

Let
\gamma
be the Dyck path corresponding to

{\rm inc}(P(\bm{m}))
of a natural unit interval order

with

To prove (
4.15
), we first reformulate it.

Fix a partition
\nu\vdash n+k
. Let
L
and
R
be the
coefficients of
P_{\nu}({\bf x};q)
in the left-hand side and
in the right-hand side of (
4.15
), respectively. Then, it

. By definition,

(4.16)

with

[

, (3.2), p. 215]

P_{\mu}(\bm{x};q)P_{(1^{k})}(\bm{x};q)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\vdash n+k\\
\rho/\mu:\text{ vertical strip}\end{subarray}}P_{\rho}(\bm{x};q)\prod_{i\geq 1}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\rho_i'-\rho_{i+1}'}{\rho_i'-\mu_i'}_{q},

(4.17)

R=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\vdash n\\
\nu/\mu:\text{ vertical strip}\end{subarray}}q^{\operatorname{area}(\gamma)-n(\mu)}r_{\gamma,\mu}(q)[k]_{q}!\prod_{i\geq 1}[m_{i}(\mu)]_{q}!\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\nu_i'-\nu_{i+1}'}{\nu_i'-\mu_i'}_{q}.

) and (

4.2
, it suffices to show that

(4.18)

\nu/\mu:\text{ vertical strip}\end{subarray}}q^{n(\nu)-n(\mu)-\binom{k}{2}}r_{\gamma,\mu}(q)[k]_{q}!\prod_{i\geq 1}\frac{[m_{i}(\mu)]_{q}!}{[m_{i}(\nu)]_{q}!}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\nu_i'-\nu_{i+1}'}{\nu_i'-\mu_i'}_{q}.

Observe that if
\nu/\mu
is a vertical strip, both

\mu_{i}^{\prime}-\nu_{i+1}^{\prime}
and
\nu_{i+1}^{\prime}-\mu^{\prime}_{i+1}
are
nonnegative. Since
m_{i}(\nu)=\nu_{i}^{\prime}-\nu_{i+1}^{\prime}
and

(4.19)

m_{i}(\mu)=\mu_{i}^{\prime}-\mu_{i+1}^{\prime}=(\mu_{i}^{\prime}-\nu_{i+1}^{\prime})+(\nu_{i+1}^{\prime}-\mu^{\prime}_{i+1}),

the identity (
4.18
) (and hence also

4.2
) is equivalent to the following
proposition.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
,
\tilde{\gamma}=\gamma+N^{k}E^{k}
, and

\nu\vdash n+k
. Then we have

(4.20)

r_{\tilde{\gamma},\nu}(q)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\vdash n\\
\nu/\mu:\text{ vertical strip}\end{subarray}}q^{n(\nu)-n(\mu)-\binom{k}{2}}r_{\gamma,\mu}(q)\frac{[k]_{q}!}{[\nu_{1}^{\prime}-\mu_{1}^{\prime}]_{q}!}\prod_{i\geq 1}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{m_i(\mu)}{\nu_{i+1}'-\mu_{i+1}'}_{q}.

The rest of this subsection is devoted to proving
4.8
. We
begin with a simple lemma.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots)
and
(b_{1},b_{2},\dots)
be any sequences
of integers with finitely many nonzero elements.
Then

(4.21)

\sum_{1\leq i\leq j}(a_{i}-b_{i})(b_{j}-a_{j+1})=\sum_{i\geq 1}\left(\binom{a_{i}}{2}-\binom{b_{i}}{2}\right)-\binom{\sum_{i\geq 1}(a_{i}-b_{i})}{2}.

Proof.

Let
L
and
R
be the left-hand side and the right-hand
side of (
4.21
), respectively. It suffices to show that

L=R
as formal power series in the variables in

X=\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,b_{1},b_{2},\dots\}
, where we consider each
binomial coefficient
\binom{c}{2}=c(c-1)/2
as a polynomial
in these variables. Then, it is straightforward to check that

[uv]L=[uv]R
for any
u,v\in X
, where
[uv]L
is the
coefficient of
uv
in
L
. More precisely, for every

i
and
j
with
1\leq i<j
, we have

\displaystyle[a_{i}^{2}]L

\displaystyle=[a_{i}^{2}]R=0,

\displaystyle[a_{i}a_{j}]L

\displaystyle=[a_{i}a_{j}]R=-1,

\displaystyle[b_{i}^{2}]L

\displaystyle=[b_{i}^{2}]R=-1,

\displaystyle[b_{i}b_{j}]L

\displaystyle=[b_{i}b_{j}]R=-1,

\displaystyle[a_{i}b_{i}]L

\displaystyle=[a_{i}b_{i}]R=1,

\displaystyle[a_{i}b_{j}]L

\displaystyle=[a_{i}b_{j}]R=1,

\displaystyle[a_{j}b_{i}]L

\displaystyle=[a_{j}b_{i}]R=1.

Hence,
L=R
as formal power series.
∎

We will find a combinatorial interpretation for the summand in
(
4.20
). To this end, we introduce some definitions.

We use the letters
i_{a}
, for integers
i\geq 0
and

a\geq 1
, with the ordering given by
i_{1}>i_{2}>\cdots
, and

i_{a}<j_{b}
for any
i<j
and
a,b\geq 1
. For a partition

\mu
, let

M_{\mu}:=\{i_{a}:1\leq i\leq\mu_{1},1\leq a\leq m_{i}(\mu)\}.

For a word
w=w_{1}\cdots w_{k}
, we write

\operatorname{set}(w):=\{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\}
.

Definition 4.10
.

Suppose that
\nu\vdash n+k
and
\mu\vdash n
are partitions such that

\nu/\mu
is a vertical strip.
We denote by
W(\nu,\mu)
the set of words

w=w_{1}\cdots w_{k}
on the alphabet

M_{\mu}\cup\{0_{1},0_{2},\dots\}
satisfying the following
conditions:

(1)

there are no repeated letters in
w
,

(2)

|\{i_{a}\in\operatorname{set}(w):a\geq 1\}|=\nu^{\prime}_{i+1}-\mu^{\prime}_{i+1}
for all
i\geq 0
,

(3)

the subword of
w
consisting of the letters
0_{a}
for

a\geq 1
is
0_{1}0_{2}\cdots 0_{\nu^{\prime}_{1}-\mu^{\prime}_{1}}
.

For a word
w=w_{1}\cdots w_{k}\in W(\nu,\mu)
and a set

B\subseteq M_{\mu}\cup\{0_{1},0_{2},\dots\}
, we define

\displaystyle f(w)

\displaystyle=|\{(w_{i},w_{j}):1\leq i<j\leq k,\,w_{i}<w_{j}\}|,

\displaystyle f_{\mu}(B)

\displaystyle=|\{(i_{a},j_{b}):i\geq 0,\,j\geq 1,\,i_{a}\in B,\,j_{b}\in M_{\mu}\setminus B,\,i_{a}<j_{b}\}|.

The following lemma shows that the summand in (
4.20
) without
the factor
r_{\gamma,\mu}(q)
is a generating function for the
words in
W(\nu,\mu)
.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\nu\vdash n+k
and
\mu\vdash n
be partitions such
that
\nu/\mu
is a vertical strip. Then

\sum_{w\in W(\nu,\mu)}q^{f_{\mu}(\operatorname{set}(w))+f(w)}=q^{n(\nu)-n(\mu)-\binom{k}{2}}\frac{[k]_{q}!}{[\nu_{1}^{\prime}-\mu_{1}^{\prime}]_{q}!}\prod_{i\geq 1}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{m_i(\mu)}{\nu_{i+1}'-\mu_{i+1}'}_{q}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\{\operatorname{set}(w):w\in W(\nu,\mu)\}
. Equivalently,

\mathcal{B}
is the set of all subsets

B\subseteq M_{\mu}\cup\{0_{1},0_{2},\dots\}
such that

(4.22)

|\{i_{a}\in B:a\geq 1\}|=\nu^{\prime}_{i+1}-\mu^{\prime}_{i+1}
for all
i\geq 0
.

By definition, we have

\sum_{w\in W(\nu,\mu)}q^{f_{\mu}(\operatorname{set}(w))+f(w)}=\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}q^{f_{\mu}(B)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in W(\nu,\mu)\\
\operatorname{set}(w)=B\end{subarray}}q^{f(w)}.

We claim that

(4.23)

\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}q^{f_{\mu}(B)}=q^{\sum_{1\leq i\leq j}(\nu^{\prime}_{i}-\mu^{\prime}_{i})(\mu^{\prime}_{j}-\nu^{\prime}_{j+1})}\prod_{i\geq 1}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{m_i(\mu)}{\nu'_{i+1}-\mu'_{i+1}}_{q},

and that for any
B\in\mathcal{B}
,

(4.24)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in W(\nu,\mu)\\
\operatorname{set}(w)=B\end{subarray}}q^{f(w)}=\frac{[k]_{q}!}{[\nu^{\prime}_{1}-\mu^{\prime}_{1}]_{q}!}.

By
4.9
, the exponent of the power of
q
in the
right-hand side of (
4.23
) is

\sum_{1\leq i\leq j}(\nu^{\prime}_{i}-\mu^{\prime}_{i})(\mu^{\prime}_{j}-\nu^{\prime}_{j+1})=\sum_{i\geq 1}\left(\binom{\nu^{\prime}_{i}}{2}-\binom{\mu^{\prime}_{i}}{2}\right)-\binom{\sum_{i\geq 1}(\nu^{\prime}_{i}-\mu^{\prime}_{i})}{2}=n(\nu)-n(\mu)-\binom{k}{2}.

Thus, it suffices to prove (
4.23
) and (
4.24
).

To prove (
4.23
), suppose
B\in\mathcal{B}
.
Then, by definition,
f_{\mu}(B)
is equal to

|\{(i_{a},j_{b}):0\leq i<j,\,i_{a}\in B,\,j_{b}\in M_{\mu}\setminus B\}|+|\{(i_{a},i_{b}):i\geq 1,\,i_{a}\in B,\,i_{b}\in M_{\mu}\setminus B,\,a>b\}|.

Note that by (
4.19
) and (
4.22
), we have

(4.25)

|\{a:i_{a}\in M_{\mu}\setminus B\}|=\mu^{\prime}_{i}-\nu^{\prime}_{i+1}\quad\mbox{for all $i\geq 1$}.

Therefore, by (
4.22
) and (
4.25
), we always have

(4.26)

|\{(i_{a},j_{b}):0\leq i<j,\,i_{a}\in B,\,j_{b}\in M_{\mu}\setminus B\}|=\sum_{0\leq i<j}(\nu^{\prime}_{i+1}-\mu^{\prime}_{i+1})(\mu^{\prime}_{j}-\nu^{\prime}_{j+1}).

By the well-known property of
q
-binomial coefficients

[
Sta11
, 1.7.1 Proposition]
, we have

\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}q^{|\{(i_{a},i_{b})\,:\,i\geq 1,\,i_{a}\in B,\,i_{b}\in M_{\mu}\setminus B,\,a>b\}|}=\prod_{i\geq 1}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{m_i(\mu)}{\nu'_{i+1}-\mu'_{i+1}}_{q}.

By combining these results, we obtain (
4.23
).

Suppose now that
w\in W(\nu,\mu)
and
\operatorname{set}(w)=B
for some
fixed
B\in\mathcal{B}
. Then
w
is a permutation of the
elements in
B
such that the letters
0_{a}
for
a\geq 1

are arranged as
0_{1}0_{2}\cdots 0_{\nu^{\prime}_{1}-\mu^{\prime}_{1}}
. Thus,
(
4.24
) also follows from
[
Sta11
, 1.7.1 Proposition]
, which
completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
,
\tilde{\gamma}=\gamma+N^{k}E^{k}

\nu\vdash n+k

\phi:\operatorname{LRP}(\tilde{\gamma},\nu)\to\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\vdash n\\
\nu/\mu:\text{ vertical strip}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{LRP}(\gamma,\mu)\times W(\nu,\mu)

such that if
\phi(P)=(Q,w)\in\operatorname{LRP}(\gamma,\mu)\times W(\nu,\mu)
, then

(4.27)

\operatorname{fc}_{\tilde{\gamma}}(P)=\operatorname{fc}_{\gamma}(Q)+f_{\mu}(\operatorname{set}(w))+f(w).

Proof.

Consider
P\in\operatorname{LRP}(\tilde{\gamma},\nu)
. Let

be the

P
to the rectangle

5.
Concluding remarks

Here, we propose some open problems that require further investigation.

5.1.
Direct bijection

The Schur expansion of the chromatic quasisymmetric function is
well-known in terms of
P
-tableaux
[
Gas96
]
: for
\gamma\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
,

(5.1)

X_{\gamma}(\bm{x};q)=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T:P\text{-tableau}\\
{\rm sh}(T)=\lambda\end{subarray}}q^{\operatorname{inv}(T)}\right)s_{\lambda}(\bm{x}).

It is also well known
[
Mac95
, (6.5), p.242]
that the Schur
function has the following Hall–Littlewood expansion:

(5.2)

s_{\lambda}(\bm{x})=\sum_{\mu\vdash n}K_{\lambda\mu}(q)P_{\mu}(\bm{x};q),

where

K_{\lambda\mu}(q)=\sum_{T\in\operatorname{SSYT}(\lambda,\mu)}q^{{\rm ch}(T)}.

Combining (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) gives another Hall–Littlewood expansion of chromatic quasisymmetric functions computed over the
set of pairs of a
P
-tableau and a semistandard Young tableau. It would be interesting to see if there exists any correspondence between the set of pairs of
P
-tableaux and semistandard Young tableaux and
the set of linked rook placements.

5.2.
Refinement of hit polynomials

In
[
HOY25
, Proposition 2.6]
, the principal specialization of the chromatic symmetric function is given as

X_{\gamma}(1,q,\dots,q^{\alpha-1};q)=q^{\operatorname{area}(\gamma)}\prod_{i=1}^{n}[\alpha-a_{i}(\gamma)]_{q}=q^{\operatorname{area}(\gamma)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}R_{n-k}(B_{\gamma};q)[\alpha]_{q}^{\underline{k}},

where
B_{\gamma}
is the Ferrers board with
i
-th column
height
c_{i}=i-1-a_{i}(\gamma)
,
R_{k}(B_{\gamma};q)
is the

k
-th
q
-rook polynomial

[
GR86
]
on the board

B_{\gamma}
, and
[\alpha]_{q}^{\underline{k}}
is the

k
-falling factorial

[\alpha]_{q}[\alpha-1]_{q}\cdots[\alpha-k+1]_{q}
. If we compare the
result of the principal specialization of the Hall–Littlewood
expansion of
X_{\gamma}(\bm{x};q)
in (
3.1
), by noting
the fact
[
Mac95
, Example 1, p. 213]
that

\prod_{i\geq 1}[m_{i}(\mu)]_{q}!P_{\mu}(1,q,\dots,q^{\alpha-1};q)=\frac{q^{n(\mu)}}{(1-q)^{\ell(\mu)}}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{\alpha}(1-q^{i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{\alpha-\ell(\mu)}(1-q^{i})}=q^{n(\mu)}[\alpha]_{q}^{\underline{\ell(\mu)}},

we get

\sum_{k=0}^{n}R_{n-k}(B_{\gamma};q)[\alpha]_{q}^{\underline{k}}=\sum_{\mu\vdash n}r_{\gamma,\mu}(q)[\alpha]_{q}^{\underline{\ell(\mu)}}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\vdash n\\
\ell(\mu)=k\end{subarray}}r_{\gamma,\mu}(q)\right)[\alpha]_{q}^{\underline{k}}.

This shows that

(5.3)

R_{n-k}(B_{\gamma};q)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\vdash n\\
\ell(\mu)=k\end{subarray}}r_{\gamma,\mu}(q).

Thus, we can consider
r_{\gamma,\mu}(q)
as a refinement of the original rook polynomial.
On the other hand, we also have the following identity
[
BHR
]
:

\prod_{i=1}^{n}[\alpha-a_{i}(\gamma)]_{q}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}R_{n-k}(B_{\gamma};q)[\alpha]_{q}^{\underline{k}}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}h_{k}(B_{\gamma};q)\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\alpha+k}{n}_{q},

where
h_{k}(B_{\gamma};q)
is the
k
-th
q
-hit polynomial

[
Dwo98
,
Hag98
]
. Hence, a natural question would be whether one can find
a refinement of the
q
-hit polynomials in a similar vein.

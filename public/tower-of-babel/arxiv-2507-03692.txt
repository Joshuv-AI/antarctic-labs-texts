Abstract
In 2004, Aaronson [ Aar04 ] introduced the complexity class 𝖯𝗈𝗌𝗍𝖡𝖰𝖯 \mathsf{PostBQP} ( 𝖡𝖰𝖯 \mathsf{BQP} with postselection) and showed that it is equal to 𝖯𝖯 \mathsf{PP} . In this paper, we define a new complexity class, 𝖢𝗈𝗋𝗋𝖡𝖰𝖯 \mathsf{CorrBQP} , a modification of 𝖡𝖰𝖯 \mathsf{BQP} which has the power to perform correlated measurements , i.e. measurements that output the same value across a partition of registers. We show that 𝖢𝗈𝗋𝗋𝖡𝖰𝖯 \mathsf{CorrBQP} is exactly equal to 𝖡𝖯𝖯 𝖯𝖯 \mathsf{BPP}^{\mathsf{PP}} , placing it “just above” 𝖯𝖯 \mathsf{PP} . In fact, we show that other metaphysical modifications of 𝖡𝖰𝖯 \mathsf{BQP} , such as 𝖢𝖡𝖰𝖯 \mathsf{CBQP} (i.e. 𝖡𝖰𝖯 \mathsf{BQP} with the ability to clone arbitrary quantum states, introduced in [ BBJ16 , Hir+25 ] ), are also equal to 𝖡𝖯𝖯 𝖯𝖯 \mathsf{BPP}^{\mathsf{PP}} . We show that 𝖢𝗈𝗋𝗋𝖡𝖰𝖯 \mathsf{CorrBQP} is self-low with respect to classical queries. In contrast, if it were self-low under quantum queries, the counting hierarchy ( 𝖢𝖧 \mathsf{CH} ) would collapse to 𝖡𝖯𝖯 𝖯𝖯 \mathsf{BPP}^{\mathsf{PP}} . Finally, we introduce a variant of rational degree that lower-bounds the query complexity of 𝖡𝖯𝖯 𝖯𝖯 \mathsf{BPP}^{\mathsf{PP}} .

1.2
Related Work

The study of modifications of
\mathsf{BQP}
has a long history. To the best of our knowledge, the first investigation of metaphysical quantum computation was through the study closed timelike curves (CTCs)
[
Deu91
]
, whose computational power is equal to
\mathsf{PSPACE}

[
AW08
]
, making it essentially the strongest known modification. For example, it has been shown that CTCs would allow us to clone arbitrary states
[
BWW13
]
.

The power of cloning arbitrary states was described in
[
BBJ16
]
, where it was shown that it can lead to an exponential speed-up in solving any search problem. The complexity class associated with this power,
\mathsf{CBQP}
, was formally studied in
[
Hir+25
]
, where it was shown that its power is equal to
\mathsf{RwBQP}
, and
\mathsf{AdPostBQP}
.

The power of postselection was initialized in
[
Aar04
]
, where it was shown that
\mathsf{PostBQP}=\mathsf{PP}
. Furthermore, it has been shown that it may solve any boolean problem when combined with quantum polynomial advice, formally meaning that
\mathsf{PostBQP/qpoly}=\mathsf{ALL}

[
Aar05
]
. Additionally,
\mathsf{PostBQP}
was considered in the query setting as well, where it was shown that its query complexity is tightly described by the measure
rdeg
, the degree of a rational function
[
MdW14
]
.

Arguably, weakest (currently known) metaphysical ability which adds computational power is non-collapsing measurements, the ability to obtain a measurement sample without altering the state. It was shown that the associated complexity class
\mathsf{PDQP}
sits between
\mathsf{SZK}
and
\mathsf{BPP}^{\mathsf{PP}}

[
Aar+14
]
. However, its power increases significantly when combined with quantum advice, yielding
\mathsf{PDQP/qpoly}=\mathsf{ALL}

[
Aar18
]
, or when combined with
\mathsf{QMA}
, in which case
\mathsf{PDQMA}=\mathsf{NEXP}

[
Aar+24
,
BM24
]
. Additionally, it is interesting to note that
\mathsf{PDQP}
and
\mathsf{AdPostBQP}
gain additional power from the presence of intermediate measurements, without them, these classes collapse to
\mathsf{BQP}
and
\mathsf{PostBQP}
, respectively. In contrast, classes such as
\mathsf{BQP}
or
\mathsf{CorrBQP}
, do not exhibit such behavior.

Lastly, there are several results which consider the space between
\mathsf{PP}
and
\mathsf{PSPACE}
. Specifically, Toda’s theorem shows that
\mathsf{PH}\subseteq\mathsf{P}^{\mathsf{PP}}

[
Tod91
]
. A similar result has been shown for a quantum version of
\mathsf{PH}
, specifically that
\mathsf{QCPH}\subseteq\mathsf{P}^{\mathsf{PP}^{\mathsf{PP}}}

[
Gha+18
]
. Lastly, only few oracle separations are known in this space. Specifically, there exist oracles
O
such that
\mathsf{PP}^{O}\not\subseteq\mathsf{P}^{\mathsf{NP}^{O}}

[
Bei94

Che16a
]
and
\mathsf{\oplus P}^{O}\not\subseteq\mathsf{PP}^{\mathsf{PH}^{O}}

Gre91
]
.

2.3
Complexity Measures

An
n
-variate polynomial is a function
P:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
which may be written as
P(x_{1},..,x_{n})=\sum_{d_{1},..,d_{n}}c_{d_{1}\dots d_{n}}\prod_{i\in[n]}x_{i}^{d_{i}}
. Its degree is
\deg(P)=\max\{\sum_{i\in[n]}d_{i}|c_{d_{1}\dots d_{n}}\neq 0\}
. Without loss of generality, as the domain is
\{0,1\}^{n}
, we may assume
P
is a multilinear polynomial. This means that for all
i
,
d_{i}\in\{0,1\}
. We say a polynomial
P

\epsilon
-approximates a function
f
if for all
x
,
\absolutevalue{P(x) - f(x)}\leq~\epsilon
.

A rational function is a ratio
R=P/Q
of polynomials
P,Q:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
where
\forall x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
,
Q(x)\neq 0
. The degree of a rational function is
\deg(R)=\max\{\deg(P),\deg(Q)\}
. The rational degree of a function
f
, denoted
\text{rdeg}_{\epsilon}(f)
, is the smallest degree over all rational functions
R
which
\epsilon
-approximate
f
.

[
MdW14
]
showed that the query complexity of a
\mathsf{PostBQP}
algorithm computing a function

up to

, is equivalent to the
\text{rdeg}_{\epsilon}(f)
. Formally, they show,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\text{rdeg}_{\epsilon}(f)\leq\text{PostQ}_{\epsilon}(f)\leq\text{rdeg}_{\epsilon}(f)

Specifically, to show that

, they describe an algorithm with postselection which evaluates a rational function
R
.

3
Correlated Measurements

As the best-known upper-bound on
\mathsf{PDQP}
is
\mathsf{BPP}^{\mathsf{PP}}
, it has been an open question whether we may tighten it to
\mathsf{PDQP}\subseteq\mathsf{PP}

[
Aar+14
]
and thereby
\mathsf{SZK}\subseteq\mathsf{PP}
. The direct way of using the result
\mathsf{PostBQP}=\mathsf{PP}
and trying to simulate
\mathsf{PDQP}
using postselection does not work. Given a
\mathsf{PDQP}
algorithm with
P
non-collapsing measurements, one may try to run
P
parallel runs of computation. However, it is difficult to simulate partial measurements across all copies with
equal amplitudes
using postselection.

An attempt to go around this issue was to define an intermediate class
\mathsf{CorrBQP}
which generalizes the definition of
\mathsf{PDQP}
. The main idea is to use the measurement gate defined in
[
MP25
]
. This gate allows one to perform a partial measurement across a group of states which is guaranteed to give us the same result across all of them. We call such action a
correlated measurement
. In this section, we define a computational class with correlated measurements,
\mathsf{CorrBQP}
(Correlated
\mathsf{BQP}
). Showing that
\mathsf{PP}\subseteq\mathsf{CorrBQP}
is straightforward. However, showing the opposite direction appeared difficult due to similar issues encountered in the attempt to show that
\mathsf{PDQP}\subseteq\mathsf{PP}
. Instead, we found that
\mathsf{CorrBQP}
is stronger, specifically that
\mathsf{CorrBQP}=\mathsf{BPP}^{\mathsf{PP}}
.

3.1
Quantum Computation with Correlated Measurements

Before formally defining
\mathsf{CorrBQP}
, let us describe correlated measurements. Suppose we split the state of our system in
k
partitions. A correlated measurement is a partial measurement across a partitioned set of registers such that within each set, the measurement outcome is the same across all of them and their probability is determined by a specific set of registers. Let us formally define such operator.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\rho
be a state over the registers
R
. Suppose we partition the registers
R
into
k
equisized disjoint sets
R_{1},...,R_{k}
where
\forall i,\absolutevalue{R_i}=j
. Additionally, let
M_{a,i}=(\outerproduct{a}{a})^{\otimes i}
.

The
set of valid correlated outputs

V
is the set of strings
a\in\{0,1\}^{j}
such that,

\displaystyle\Tr({M_{a,k}}^\dagger M_{a,k} \rho)>0

(1)

Let
R_{1}
be denoted as a
leader
and let the state over registers
R_{1}
be denoted by
\rho_{1}
. We call
\rho_{1}
a
leader state
. A correlated measurement
C_{\{R_{i}\}_{i\in[k]},R_{1}}
on
\rho
is a measurement described by the collection of measurement operators
\{M_{a}^{*}\}_{a\in V}
where,

\displaystyle M_{a}^{*}=\frac{\Tr({M_{a,1}}^\dagger M_{a,1} \rho_1)}{\Tr({M_{a,k}}^\dagger M_{a,k} \rho)}(\ket{a}\bra{a})^{\otimes k}

We call such operator a valid operator if
V\neq\emptyset
.

First, let us emphasize that such operator would not satisfy the postulates of quantum mechanics. Specifically, the measurement operators do not satisfy the completeness equation,

\displaystyle\sum_{a}{M_{a}^{*}}^{\dagger}M_{a}^{*}\neq I

However, as we are attempting to amplify the power of
\mathsf{BQP}
to a metaphysical regime, such operation is justifiable. Secondly, the operators
\{M_{a}^{*}\}_{a\in V}
are designed in a way such that the probability of measuring
a
across all partitions
\{R_{i}\}_{i\in[k]}
is equal to the probability of measuring
a
in
\rho_{1}
for
a\in V
. Let
p_{a}(b)
be the probability of measuring
a
in the state
b
. Then the probability of output
a^{\otimes k}
from
C_{\{R_{i}\}_{i\in[k]},R_{1}}
is:

\displaystyle p_{a^{\otimes k}}(\rho)=\frac{p_{a}(\rho_{1})}{\sum_{a^{\prime}\in V}p_{a^{\prime}}(\rho_{1})}

We must normalize
p_{a^{\otimes k}}(\rho)
in the case that
V
is a strict subset of
\{0,1\}^{j}
. As is mentioned at the end of Definition
3.1
,
V
cannot be empty as otherwise the correlated measurement is invalid. In the case that
V=\emptyset
, we assume that the computation is invalid
5
5

5

Same is true for the definition of
\mathsf{PostBQP}
wherein it is defined only on outcomes with nonzero probability. If a measurement outcome has zero probability, then the entire circuit is invalid.
. We note that we may also perform partial correlated measurements by applying the operator to a strict subset of registers of the entire state. Additionally, once a correlated measurement is performed over a set of quantum registers, they become classical registers as is the case in standard measurement. Let us formally define the class
\mathsf{CorrBQP}
.

Definition 3.2

(
\mathsf{CorrBQP}
)
.

A language
L
is said to be in
\mathsf{CorrBQP}
if there exists a family of quantum circuits
\{Q_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
composed of Hadamard, Toffoli and valid correlated measurement operators such that:

•

If
x\in L
, the probability that
Q_{n}
outputs
1
to input
\ket{x}
is at least
2/3
.

•

If
x\not\in L
, the probability that
Q_{n}
outputs
1
to input
\ket{x}
is at most
1/3
.

As we are using Hadamard and Toffoli gates, the condition for a valid correlated measurements in Equation
1
becomes,

\displaystyle\Tr({M_{a,k}}^\dagger M_{a,k} \rho)\geq\frac{1}{2^{h}}

where
h
is the number of Hadamard gates used in
Q_{n}
.

3.2
Simulating Non-collapsing Measurements and Postselection

Here we provide proofs that
\mathsf{PDQP}\subseteq\mathsf{CorrBQP}
and
\mathsf{PP}=\mathsf{PostBQP}\subseteq\mathsf{CorrBQP}
, offering intuition for the power of this class.

Let us shortly describe the class
\mathsf{PDQP}
, a formal definition may be found in
[
Aar+14
,
MP25
]
. In summary,
\mathsf{PDQP}
is defined as the class where a polynomial-time Turing Machine is given an input, based on which it creates a quantum circuit. This circuit is given to an oracle which simulates the circuit, obtaining measurement samples which do not collapse the state at each step of the computation. These samples are returned to the TM, which based on them runs a post-processing algorithm and halts.

Lemma 3.3
.

\mathsf{PDQP}\subseteq\mathsf{CorrBQP}

Proof.

Consider an arbitrary
\mathsf{PDQP}
algorithm which runs circuit
Q
which consists of Hadamard, Toffoli and (standard) measurement operators and performs
P
non-collapsing measurements. Then
\mathsf{CorrBQP}
can simulate this algorithm by running
Q_{P}
, a circuit which runs
P
parallel runs of
Q
. Each run
i\in[P]
would stop right before non-collapsing measurements number
i
is performed. Therefore each run
i
represents the state we would be performing a non-collapsing measurement on. On the other hand, suppose we perform a (collapsing) partial measurement after
p
non-collapsing measurements. Then the measurement result must hold for the
P-p
non-collapsing measurements which follow.
Therefore, every time we perform a partial measurement in
Q
,
Q_{P}
performs a correlated measurement over the
P-p
runs. We may set the run
P
to be the leader as all states are exact copies of each other. At the end of
Q_{P}
, we run a quantum circuit which mimics the deterministic Turing Machine which postprocesses the non-collapsing measurement results in
\mathsf{PDQP}
.
∎

\mathsf{AdPDQP}\subseteq\mathsf{CorrBQP}

Lemma 3.4
.

\mathsf{PP}\subseteq\mathsf{CorrBQP}

Proof.

As

3.3
Further Properties of Correlated Measurements

Here we establish some properties of
\mathsf{CorrBQP}
which will be useful later.

Lemma 3.5
.

\mathsf{CorrBQP}
=
\mathsf{coCorrBQP}

Proof.

For any language
L\in\mathsf{CorrBQP}
, let
Q_{n}
be the circuit per Definition
3.2
that recognizes
L
. Then for
\overline{L}
, the complement of
L
, we may decide whether
x\in\overline{L}
by using the same circuit
Q_{n}
and flipping the output register at the end. Therefore
\mathsf{coCorrBQP}\subseteq\mathsf{CorrBQP}
. Showing the opposite direction uses the same argument.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

\mathsf{CorrBQP(2/3,1/3)}
=
\mathsf{CorrBQP(1-2^{-p(n)},2^{-p(n)})}

Proof.

In order to amplify the completeness and soundness gap, we perform
m
computations in parallel and take a majority vote. By the Chernoff Bound
[
AB09
]
, this will decrease the error probability from
1/3
to
2^{-e(m)}
for some increasing function
e(m)
. What remains is setting
m
such that
e(m)\geq p(n)
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

\mathsf{CorrBQP}^{\mathsf{CorrBQP}}_{\text{classical}}=\mathsf{CorrBQP}

Proof.

\mathsf{CorrBQP}\subseteq\mathsf{CorrBQP}^{\mathsf{CorrBQP}}_{\text{classical}}
is straight-forward: The base machine does not make any queries to the oracle.
Let us show the reverse. Let
Q_{n}
be the
\mathsf{CorrBQP}
circuit which includes polynomially many classical oracle calls to
\mathsf{CorrBQP}
. Any classical call denotes that
Q_{n}
has some classical query registers containing
x
and a classical output register
o
. In the simulation using
\mathsf{CorrBQP}
, we copy
x
to a separate register, run a separate
\mathsf{CorrBQP}
subroutine, measure the output at the end and copy the output to
o
. Using Lemma
3.6
, we can amplify the computation to obtain the wrong answer with exponentially low probability of error. Since the number of calls to the oracle is polynomial, the total accumulated error can still be bounded above any desired constant. Furthermore, Lemma
3.5
ensures that running the subroutine is sufficient for computing
any
input. Finally, the resultant circuit
Q_{n}
can also be amplified in order to ensure the error is at most
1/3
.
∎

3.4
\mathsf{CorrBQP}=\mathsf{BPP}^{\mathsf{PP}}

Lemma 3.8
.

\mathsf{BPP}^{\mathsf{PP}}\subseteq\mathsf{CorrBQP}

Proof.

By definition, we have that
\mathsf{BPP}^{\mathsf{PP}}\subseteq\mathsf{CorrBQP}^{\mathsf{PP}}_{\text{classical}}
as we are only adding more power to the machine being run and
\mathsf{BPP}
may only perform classical queries to the
\mathsf{PP}
oracle. By Lemma
3.4
, we have
\mathsf{CorrBQP}^{\mathsf{PP}}_{\text{classical}}\subseteq\mathsf{CorrBQP}^{\mathsf{CorrBQP}}_{\text{classical}}
. Finally, as
\mathsf{CorrBQP}^{\mathsf{CorrBQP}}_{\text{classical}}\subseteq\mathsf{CorrBQP}
by Lemma
3.7
, we obtain the desired result.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

\mathsf{CorrBQP}\subseteq\mathsf{BPP}^{\mathsf{PP}}

Proof.

We extend the argument from
[
Aar+14
]
which showed
\mathsf{PDQP}\subseteq\mathsf{BPP}^{\mathsf{PP}}
for
\mathsf{CorrBQP}
.Let us note that
\mathsf{BPP}^{\mathsf{PP}}=\mathsf{BPP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}
as repeated use of the
\mathsf{PP}
oracle may calculate the result of a problem in
\mathsf{\#P}
.

Let
Q_{n}
be the quantum circuit run. For simplicity, suppose we only perform a single correlated measurement
C_{\{R_{i}\}_{i\in[k]},R_{1}}
where
\forall i,\absolutevalue{R_i}=l
. As all other operations are Hadamard and Toffoli gates, the probability of each measurement output is
\frac{2^{c}}{2^{h}}
where we used
h

. This holds even for probabilities which are conditioned on another measurement output. By the techniques used to show
\mathsf{BQP}\subseteq\mathsf{PP}

[
ADH97
]
, we may ask the
\mathsf{\#P}
oracle to compute these probabilities.

Let
m_{i}
denote the correlated output of the registers in
R_{i}
,
m=m_{1}m_{2}\dots m_{k}
and let
R_{1}
be the registers over the leader state. We shall iteratively obtain
m_{i}(j)
for
j\in[l]
, starting with
j=1
. First, we ask the
\mathsf{\#P}
oracle for the probabilities
(P_{1,1},...,P_{1,k})
where

\displaystyle P_{1,i}=\Pr[R_{i}(1)=1]

Suppose that there exists some
j\in[k]
such that
P_{1,j}\in\{0,1\}
. If that is the case, we know that all valid correlated measurements must begin with the string
P_{1,j}
, meaning the
\mathsf{BPP}
machine may set

for all

. Note that it is not possible to have

as that would imply that there are no valid correlated measurement outputs, contradicting our assumption about the algorithm. On the other hand, suppose that
\forall i\in[k]

. Then the
\mathsf{BPP}
machine flips a coin with bias

. Assuming it gets

\forall i\in[k]

.

Obtaining the remainder of

continues analogously, except the

for

. Formally, we ask for
(P_{j,1},...,P_{(}j,k))

\displaystyle P_{j,i}=\Pr[R_{i}(j)=1\;|\;R_{1}(1)R_{1}(2)\dots R_{k}(j-1)=m_{1}(1)m_{1}(2)\dots m_{k}(j-1)]

The rest of processing

Theorem 3.10
.

\mathsf{CorrBQP}=\mathsf{BPP}^{\mathsf{PP}}

Proof.

By combining Lemmas

3.7
Comments on the Self-low Property

Finally, one may ask themselves why
\mathsf{PostBQP}
is not self-low with respect to classical queries. The main reasoning is due to the fact that
\mathsf{PostBQP}
does not allow for intermediate measurements during its execution. As has been mentioned in prior work
[
Aar04
,
Hir+25
]
, proving the statement via simulation has the issue with correctly processing the error of the output. While amplification may bring the error of a subroutine simulating a query exponentially close to 0, we cannot guarantee it is 0. Therefore performing a query whose input depends on the result of a previous query must run the query in a superposition on both. However, the amplitude of the postselected output may be exponentially larger in the incorrect query than the correct query. This would mean that the incorrect computational path has a much larger amplitude than the correct one, making the simulation fail.

The next question one may ask is whether any of the classes such as
\mathsf{CorrBQP}
or
\mathsf{AdPostBQP}
are self-low with respect to quantum queries. The standard attempt to perform simulations fails due to the
inability of
\mathsf{AdPostBQP}
to defer measurements until the end (if it could,
\mathsf{AdPostBQP}=\mathsf{PostBQP}
). However, if we are simulating a quantum query using a subroutine in superposition, the measurement output probabilities may significantly differ when conditioned on an input, but they must be the same across all of them. Secondly, the same issue which was described for
\mathsf{PostBQP}
above remains for
\mathsf{AdPostBQP}
, meaning that even showing
\mathsf{AdPostBQP}^{\mathsf{PostBQP}}=\mathsf{AdPostBQP}
is tricky.

4
Rational Trees and Postselection

In this section we define the
rational tree degree
measure and establish its relationship with the query complexity of
\mathsf{AdPostBQP}
.

4.1
Definitions

4.1.1
Rational Trees

A rational tree
T_{R}
is a binary tree where each node contains a rational function
R_{i}
. When evaluating such tree on an input
x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, we flip a coin with bias
R_{i}(x)
. Therefore we assume that
R_{i}(x)\in[0,1]
for all inputs. In the case that
R_{i}(x)
is out of this range, we set it either to 1 or 0, whichever is closer. If we are at a leaf node, we output the output value of the coin. Otherwise, we go down the tree. The degree of the tree is the maximum degree of the product of nodes over one path in
T_{R}
.

We note that any such tree may be rewritten as a sum and product of rational functions. We may create such formula by processing each path separately by either adding a
R_{i}
or
1-R_{i}
term to its product at each node and taking a sum over all paths. We say that
T_{R}

\epsilon
-approximates a boolean function if for all
x
,
\absolutevalue{T_R(x) - f(x)}\leq\epsilon
. Let us formally define
trdeg
.

Definition 4.1
.

Consider a function
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
. The rational tree degree up to
\epsilon
error of
f
, denoted by
\text{trdeg}_{\epsilon}(f)
, is the minimum degree among all rational trees
T_{R}
which
\epsilon
-approximate
f
.

4.1.2
Query Complexity of Adaptive Postselection

An
\mathsf{AdPostBQP}
algorithm is one whose postselections may depend on measurement results. Therefore a quantum query algorithm with adaptive postselection is an algorithm whose postselections are randomly determined. Alternatively, one may view it as a tree where each node contains queries and postselections, and we split into two branches when a measurement is performed, each output weighted with their respective success probability.
We denote the adaptive postselection query complexity of
f
as
\text{AdPostQ}_{\epsilon}(f)
.

4.2
Approximate Rational Tree

We show that the degree of a rational tree lower-bounds the query complexity of
\mathsf{AdPostBQP}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

For all
\epsilon\in[0,1/2)
and
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
,

\displaystyle\text{trdeg}_{\epsilon}(f)\leq 2\text{AdPostQ}_{\epsilon}(f)

Proof.

Let
T
denote the number of queries made and
M
the number of intermediate single-qubit measurements. We show that
\text{trdeg}_{\epsilon}(f)\leq 2\text{AdPostQ}_{\epsilon}(f)
via induction on
M
. When
M=0
, the algorithm does not make any intermediate measurements, meaning it behaves as a
\mathsf{PostBQP}
algorithm. Thus, per
[
MdW14
]
, the query complexity of the algorithm may be described using a rational function of degree at most
2T
. Specifically, one may define the polynomials
Q(x)=\Pr[p=1]
and
P(x)=\Pr[p=o=1]
which describe the query algorithm. Here
o
is the output register and
p
is the postselection register. Their ratio
P(x)/Q(x)
equals
\Pr[o=1|p=1]
, the probability of outputting 1 when postselecting on 1. Per
[
Bea+98
]
,
P
and
Q
have degree at most
2T
, meaning that the degree of
P/Q
is at most
2T
.

Assume the statement holds for all integers less than
N>0
. Consider the first partial measurement is performed after
t<T
queries on register
m
. Per the same argument as above, let
Q_{1}(x)=\Pr[p=1]
and
P_{1,i}=\Pr[m=i\wedge p=1]
. Therefore
P_{1,i}/Q_{1}
describes
\Pr[m=i|p=1]
and has degree at most
2t
. At this point, the computation takes one of two paths based on
m
. Per the inductive argument, there exists a rational formula
R_{i}
for each
i
of degree at most
2(T-t)
. Thus the rational formula
R
describing the query algorithm is
R=R_{0}(P_{1,0}/Q_{1})+R_{1}(P_{1,1}/Q_{1})
, may be described as a tree and has degree at most
2T
.
∎

Unfortunately, we are not able to show
\text{AdPostQ}_{\epsilon}(f)\leq\text{trdeg}_{\epsilon}(f)
for
\epsilon\neq 0
. The issue is that the algorithm in
[
MdW14
]
which was used to show
\text{PostQ}_{\epsilon}\leq\text{rdeg}_{\epsilon}
is biased towards outputting the correct answer. This causes an issue when evaluating multiple rational functions as it may add bias towards a path which outputs the incorrect output. Additionally, we may extend prior results of
[
Che16a
]
to show a separation between
rdeg
and
trdeg
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Consider
f^{\prime}=\textup{Ada}_{\textup{AND}_{n},\log n}
. Then
\text{rdeg}_{1/3}(f^{\prime})=\Omega(\textup{poly}(n))
, while
\text{trdeg}_{1/3}(f^{\prime})=O(\log^{3}n)
.

Proof.

By Theorems
2.12
and
2.3
, we know that
\text{PostQ}_{1/3}(f^{\prime})=\textup{poly}(n)
. On the other hand, the algorithm in
[
Aar04
]
which shows that
\mathsf{PostBQP}
can compute majority may also be used to compute
AND
in the same time, namely
O(\log^{2}n)
. Therefore
\mathsf{AdPostBQP}
may run the
\mathsf{PostBQP}
subroutine which computes each level of
\textup{Ada}_{\textup{ADA},d}
. By measuring at the end of each subroutine, we ensure that the we obtain the correct output with high probability.

As
\text{PostQ}_{1/3}(f^{\prime})\leq\text{rdeg}_{1/3}(f^{\prime})
and
\text{trdeg}_{1/3}(f^{\prime})\leq 2\text{AdPostQ}_{1/3}(f^{\prime})
, we are done.
∎

We note that the original result of
[
Che16a
]
showed an oracle separation between
\mathsf{PP}
and
\mathsf{P}^{\mathsf{NP}}
, implying an oracle separation between
\mathsf{PostBQP}
and
\mathsf{AdPostBQP}
.

4.3
Exact Computation

The relationship between
\text{rdeg}_{\epsilon}
and
\text{PostQ}_{\epsilon}
established in
[
MdW14
]
suggests that the rational degree conjecture
8
8

8

The rational degree conjecture is asking whether in the zero-error setting,
\text{rdeg}(f)
is polynomially related to
\widetilde{\text{deg}}(f)
for all boolean functions
f

[
NS92
]
.
may be approached by studying the complexity class
\mathsf{PostEQP}
,
\mathsf{PostBQP}
which outputs the correct answer with certainty. We show that adding adaptivity to
\mathsf{PostEQP}
does not increase its power.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any classical oracle
O
,

\displaystyle\mathsf{PostEQP}^{O}=\mathsf{AdPostEQP}^{O}

Proof.

By definition,
\mathsf{PostEQP}\subseteq\mathsf{AdPostEQP}
. Consider some language
L\in\mathsf{AdPostEQP}
, some input
x
and an
\mathsf{AdPostEQP}
circuit
C
as in Definition
2.6
. We know that if
x\in L
,
C
always outputs 1 and always outputs 0 otherwise. Consider the first measurement
C
performs. We know that regardless of the measurement output, we must always obtain the correct output. Therefore this measurement may be omitted as it does not alter the output probability. As the only difference between
\mathsf{PostEQP}
and
\mathsf{AdPostEQP}
is the ability to perform partial measurements during computation,
\mathsf{AdPostEQP}\subseteq\mathsf{PostEQP}
. Finally, this proof relativizes as any
\mathsf{PostEQP}
circuit may simulate a
\mathsf{AdPostEQP}
circuit and vice-versa.
∎

We find this interesting as the same does not hold in the bounded-error case. Furthermore, when we take the
exact
version of other metaphysical complexity classes we study in this paper and apply the techniques of Theorem
2.8
, we find the relationship below.

\displaystyle\mathsf{EQP}\subseteq\mathsf{RwEQP}\subseteq\mathsf{CEQP}\subseteq\mathsf{AdPostEQP}=\mathsf{PostEQP}

The fact that all classes above
\mathsf{RwEQP}
are not equal is do to the fact that the mitigation protocol which was used to show
\mathsf{PostBQP}\subseteq\mathsf{RwBQP}
is not exact. Additionally, when we consider the zero-error case for the complexity measures associated with
\mathsf{PostBQP}
and
\mathsf{AdPostBQP}
, we find that they are equivalent.

Lemma 4.5
.

For any function
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
,

\displaystyle\text{trdeg}_{0}(f)\leq\text{rdeg}_{0}(f)\leq 2\text{trdeg}_{0}(f)

Proof.

By definition,
\text{trdeg}_{0}(f)\leq\text{rdeg}_{0}(f)
. On the other hand, by
[
MdW14
]
,
\text{rdeg}_{0}(f)\leq 2\text{PostQ}_{0}(f)
. Let us show that
\text{PostQ}_{0}(f)\leq\text{trdeg}_{0}(f)
.

Consider an arbitrary rational tree
T_{R}
which exactly evaluates to
f
. Therefore, given an input
x
, any leaf which has non-zero probability of being evaluated must output
f(x)
. This means that without loss of generality, we may assume that any non-leaf node must also always output
1
or
0
for every
x
as it suffices to describe one path to a leaf which outputs
f(x)
. This may be simulated in
\mathsf{PostBQP}
by evaluating each node using the rational function evaluation algorithm, as described in
[
MdW14
]
, as it always outputs
f(x)
when restricted to exact rational functions.
∎

5
Postselection with Classical Queries

Finally, in order to address Lemma
3.12
, we study the class
\mathsf{PostBQP}_{\textup{classical}}
,
\mathsf{PostBQP}
which may only make classical queries to the oracle. Of course, in the absence of an oracle,
\mathsf{PostBQP}_{\textup{classical}}=\mathsf{PP}
, but in the relativized world, we only know that for every classical oracle
O
,
\mathsf{PostBQP}^{O}_{\textup{classical}}\subseteq\mathsf{PP}^{O}
.

We extend the proof of
[
Aar09
,
Che16
]
which separates
\mathsf{BQP}
from
\mathsf{PostBPP}
to obtain a separation between
\mathsf{BQP}
(with
quantum
query access) and
\mathsf{PostBQP}_{\textup{classical}}
. As
\mathsf{BQP}\subseteq\mathsf{PP}
relativizes, there exists an oracle
O
such that
\mathsf{PP}^{O}\not\subseteq\mathsf{PostBQP}^{O}_{\textup{classical}}
. Our result underscores the necessity to allow computational classes to posses full access to an oracle as even a slight restriction may thwart their computational power. This is interesting to see as
\mathsf{PP}
may only perform classical queries. We begin our proof with the proposition below.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
f:D\rightarrow\{0,1\}
be a function where
D\subseteq\{0,1\}^{n}
. Suppose there are two distributions
\mathcal{D}_{0},\mathcal{D}_{1}
which are
\epsilon
-almost
k
-wise equivalent. If
\epsilon=o(1)
, then there does not exist a
\mathsf{PostBQP}_{\textup{classical}}
algorithm which may compute
f
using
k
queries.

Proof.

Let
M
be some
\mathsf{PostBQP}_{\textup{classical}}
machine which computes
f
, meaning that it is a machine which makes classical queries to the input
x\in D
. Let
\Pi_{p}
and
\Pi_{o}
be the postselection and output projectors, respectively (we assume they are valid projectors for
\mathsf{PostBQP}
, meaning that
\Pi_{o}\leq\Pi_{p}
). Note that these projectors are independent of the input
x
. Let
(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})
be the queried indices of
x
and
Z=(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{k})=(x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{k}})
the outputs to the queries. After these queries, the circuits state
\rho_{Z}
solely depends on
Z
. Given a distribution
D
and
s\in\{p,o\}
, we define
p_{D,s}
to be the probability that when
x
is drawn from
D
, that
\Pi_{s}
succeeds on
\rho_{z}
.

\displaystyle p_{D,s}\coloneqq\E_{x\sim D}\left[\Tr(\Pi_s \rho_{Z(x)} )\right]=\sum_{z\in\{0,1\}^{k}}\Pr_{x\sim D}[Z(x)=z]\Tr(\Pi_s \rho_z)

Therefore the probability of accepting is
a_{D}=\frac{p_{D,o}}{p_{D,p}}
. This means that for
M
to compute
f
, then
a_{D_{0}}\leq 1/3
, while
a_{D_{1}}\geq 2/3
.

As
\mathcal{D}_{0}
and
\mathcal{D}_{1}
are
\epsilon
-almost
k
-wise equivalent,
(1-\epsilon)p_{D_{0},s}\leq p_{D_{1},s}\leq(1+\epsilon)p_{D_{0},s}
, meaning that
1-\epsilon\leq p_{D_{1},s}/p_{D_{0},s}\leq 1+\epsilon
. Therefore,

\displaystyle 1-\epsilon\leq\frac{p_{D_{1},o}/p_{D_{1},p}}{p_{D_{0},o}/p_{D_{0},p}}=\frac{a_{D_{1}}}{a_{D_{0}}}\leq 1+\epsilon

If
M
succeeds, we know that
\frac{a_{D_{1}}}{a_{D_{0}}}\geq 2
, but when
\epsilon=o(1)
, this is false.
∎

This suffices for us to prove our main result.

Theorem 5.2
.

There exists an oracle
O
such that
\mathsf{BQP}^{O}\not\subseteq\mathsf{PostBQP}^{O}_{\textup{Classical}}
.

Proof.

We may combine Theorem
2.10
with Lemma
5.1
in the case that
k=\textup{polylog}(n)=n^{o(1)}
. Then, by standard diagonalization techniques, we obtain the oracle separation.
∎

6
Discussion

There is a variety of open questions in this direction, most of which stem from our lack of understanding of the space between
\mathsf{PP}
and
\mathsf{PSPACE}
. Let us list several of them.

•

Can we obtain an oracle separation between
\mathsf{P}^{\mathsf{PP}}
(or a larger class such as
\mathsf{CorrBQP}
) and
\mathsf{PSPACE}
? Currently the largest class which is separated from
\mathsf{PSPACE}
is
\mathsf{PP}^{\mathsf{PH}}

[
Gre91
]
.

•

Are there other metaphysical modifications of
\mathsf{BQP}
which are equal to different classes between
\mathsf{PP}
and
\mathsf{PSPACE}
, such as
\mathsf{P}^{\mathsf{PP}}
or
\mathsf{BQP}^{\mathsf{PP}}
?

•

Are any of such modifications mentioned above self-low with respect to quantum queries? As mentioned in Lemma
3.12
, this could be used to collapse
\mathsf{CH}
.

•

Is there a statement which would address the power of
\mathsf{PP/poly}
in a similar way to how the Karp-Lipton addresses
\mathsf{P/poly}

[
KL80
]
?

•

Can
\mathsf{CorrBQP}
be used to strengthen the current quantum-classical version of Toda’s theorem,
\mathsf{QCPH}\subseteq\mathsf{P}^{\mathsf{PP}^{\mathsf{PP}}}

[
Gha+18
]
?

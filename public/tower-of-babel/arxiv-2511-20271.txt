A sharp Sobolev inequality on the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg hyperbolic space
B. Devyver, L. Dupaigne, P.D. Thizy

Abstract
. In the Euclidean space 𝐑 d {\mathbf{R}}^{d} , the sharp classical Sobolev inequality is equivalent by conformal invariance to a Sobolev inequality on the hyperbolic space 𝐇 d \mathbf{H}^{d} . This inequality is sharp in dimension d ≥ 4 d\geq 4 , but it is not in dimension d = 3 d=3 (see [ 2 ] and [ 14 ] ). In this article, we investigate a similar phenomenon for the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality and its hyperbolic analogue. In our setting, the condition for improving the inequality reads n ∈ [ 3 , 4 ) n\in[3,4) , where n n is an “effective dimension”.

3.
Conformal changes & proof of Theorem
1.3

3.1.
Conformal changes

We first recall conformal invariance properties of Sobolev inequalities and conformal weighted Laplacians from
[
9
]
. Let us start with some terminology: if
(X,g,\mu)
is a (smooth) weighted Riemannian manifold, and
c:X\to(0,\infty)
is a smooth function, the weighted Riemmannian manifold
(X,c^{-2}g,c^{-n}\mu)
is called
n
-conformal
to
(X,g,\mu)
. It follows from the definitions that
(\mathbf{H},g_{\mathbf{H}},\mu_{\mathbf{H}})
is
n
-conformal to
({\mathbf{B}}^{*},g_{\mathbf{E}},\mu_{\mathbf{E}})
with the conformal change given by the function
c=\frac{1}{\varphi}=\frac{1-\rho^{2}}{2}
. In
[
9
]
, building on previous work from
[
1
]
, a quantity
S(X,g,\mu)
is introduced, which is
n
-conformal
, in the sense that if
(X,c^{-2}g,c^{-n}\mu)
is
n
-conformal to
(X,g,\mu)
, then
s=S(X,g,\mu)
and
\tilde{s}=S(X,c^{-2}g,c^{-n}\mu)
are related through the following Yamabe-type equation:

(27)

-Lv+sv=\tilde{s}v^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}},

where
v=c^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}
and
L
is the weighted Laplacian on
X
. More precisely, for a definition of the latter, if one writes
d\mu=e^{-W}dv_{g}
, where
dv_{g}
denotes the Riemannian volume of
(X,g)
, then one has

L=\Delta_{g}-g(\nabla W,\nabla\cdot)

The quantity
S(X,g,\mu)
could be called, by analogy with the unweighted case, a renormalized
weighted scalar curvature
. Moreover, explicit computations carried out in
[
9
]
show that

(28)

S(\mathbf{E},\mu_{\mathbf{E}},g_{\mathbf{E}})=0,\quad S(\mathbf{H},g_{\mathbf{H}},\mu_{\mathbf{H}})=-\frac{n(n-2)}{4}\alpha^{2}

Hence, in this sense, the weighted manifold
(\mathbf{H},g_{\mathbf{H}},\mu_{\mathbf{H}})
has constant negative weighted scalar curvature. Equivalently to the Yamabe equation (
27
), the operator
-L+S(X,g,\mu)
(a weighted conformal Laplacian) is
n
-conformally invariant, that is: if
\tilde{L}
denotes the weighted Laplacian on
(X,c^{2}g,c^{n}\mu)
, then

(29)

(-L+s)(vw)=v^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}(-\tilde{L}+\tilde{s})(w)

Multiplying (
29
) by
vw
and integrating by parts, denoting
\tilde{g}=c^{-2}g
and
\tilde{\mu}=c^{-n}\mu
, one easily finds that

(30)

\int_{X}\left(|\nabla(vw)|_{g}^{2}+s(vw)^{2}\right)\,d\mu=\int_{X}\left(|\nabla w|_{\tilde{g}}^{2}+\tilde{s}w^{2}\right)\,d\tilde{\mu}

Also, an easy computation shows that with
p=\frac{2n}{n-2}
,

(31)

\int_{X}|vw|^{p}\,d\mu=\int_{X}|w|^{p}\,d\tilde{\mu},

and finally that

\int_{X}(vw)^{2}v^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\,d\mu=\int_{X}w^{2}\,d\tilde{\mu}.

It follows from (
30
) and (
31
) that the Sobolev constants are invariant under
n
-conformal changes, a fact we have already pointed out in the introduction.

Having recalled these facts, we proceed with the proof of Theorem
1.3
, which we split in two parts.

3.2.
Computing
\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{H})

We begin by computing
\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{H})
. Given

, consider the change of function

, where

and

(32)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{H}}|\nabla f|_{g_{\mathbf{H}}}^{2}d\mu_{\mathbf{H}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{H}}|\nabla(g_{0}g)|_{g_{\mathbf{H}}}^{2}d\mu_{\mathbf{H}}=\int_{\mathbf{H}}\left(g_{0}^{2}|\nabla g|_{g_{\mathbf{H}}}^{2}+g_{\mathbf{H}}\left(\nabla g_{0},\nabla(g_{0}g^{2})\right)\right)d\mu_{\mathbf{H}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{H}}g_{0}^{2}|\nabla g|_{g_{\mathbf{H}}}^{2}d\mu_{\mathbf{H}}+\int_{\mathbf{H}}\frac{-L_{\mathbf{H}}g_{0}}{g_{0}}f^{2}d\mu_{\mathbf{H}}

Set

(this is the conformal factor named

Choose

Recalling the expression of

,

A
. Furthermore, expanding the square, we have

|\nabla v|^{2}_{g_{\mathbf{E}}}=|\nabla(g\chi)|^{2}_{g_{\mathbf{E}}}=\chi^{2}|\nabla g|^{2}_{g_{\mathbf{E}}}+2g\chi\nabla g\cdot\nabla\chi+g^{2}|\nabla\chi|^{2}_{g_{\mathbf{E}}},

and a similar formula for
|\nabla w|^{2}_{g_{\mathbf{E}}}
. Hence,

\displaystyle|\nabla v|^{2}_{g_{\mathbf{E}}}+|\nabla w|^{2}_{g_{\mathbf{E}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\nabla g|^{2}_{g_{\mathbf{E}}}+2|g|\cdot|\nabla g\cdot\nabla\chi|+|||\nabla\chi|_{g_{\mathbf{E}}}^{2}||_{\infty}g^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\nabla g|^{2}_{g_{\mathbf{E}}}+|g|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{A}+|||\nabla\chi|^{2}_{g_{\mathbf{E}}}||_{\infty}|g|\cdot|\nabla g|_{g_{\mathbf{E}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\nabla g|^{2}_{g_{\mathbf{E}}}+|g|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{A},

where
\mathbf{1}_{A}
is the characteristic function of the set
A
. Therefore, using again the elementary inequality (
38
), we find that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbf{H}}|\nabla v|^{2}_{g_{\mathbf{E}}}\psi\,d\mu_{\mathbf{E}}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\int_{\mathbf{H}}|\nabla w|^{2}_{g_{\mathbf{E}}}\psi\,d\mu_{\mathbf{E}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbf{H}}|\nabla g|^{2}_{g_{\mathbf{E}}}\psi\,d\mu_{\mathbf{E}}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\int_{A}g^{2}\psi\,d\mu_{\mathbf{E}}\right)^{1/2}

But
\psi\asymp 1
on
A
, therefore the second term on the RHS of the above inequality is
\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbf{H}}g^{2}\,d\mu_{\mathbf{E}}\right)^{1/2}
. Finally, putting all the inequalities together, we conclude that (
34
) holds and this concludes the proof.

4.1.
Green functions and Sobolev constants

In all this subsection, we consider a general situation. We let
\Omega
be a domain in
{\mathbf{R}}^{d}
endowed with a measure
\mu=e^{-W}dx
, and let
-L
be a non-negative, second-order, elliptic operator with smooth coefficients in
\Omega

-L
is symmetric in the weighted Lebesgue space

. Extend

as an unbounded self-adjoint operator in

by the Friedrichs extension method (see
[
20

; we also briefly review the method in the appendix of the present paper). We denote by

the quadratic form associated with

Q_{L}(u,u)=\int_{\Omega}u(-Lu)\,d\mu,\quad\forall u\in\mathrm{Dom}(L)

For instance, if

with
\mathrm{div}_{\mu}
the formal adjoint for the measure

of
\nabla

smooth, symmetric, elliptic and

Q_{L}(u,u)=\int_{\Omega}\left(\langle A\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle+Vu^{2}\right)\,d\mu

The domain of the quadratic form

is denoted
\mathrm{Dom}(Q_{L})
. By construction of the self-adjoint extension of

by the Friedrichs method,

\mathrm{Dom}(Q_{L})=\mathrm{Dom}((-L)^{1/2}).

Moreover,

Q_{L}(u,u)=||(-L)^{1/2}u||_{2}^{2},\quad\forall u\in\mathrm{Dom}(Q_{L}).

We assume that the operator

is

in the sense of criticality theory (see Theorem 13.6 in
[

or Theorem 1.5 in

), which means that there exists a non-negative, non-zero weight function

Q_{L}(u,u)\geq\int_{\Omega}u^{2}w\,d\mu,\quad\forall u\in\mathrm{Dom}(Q_{L})

In particular,

is subcritical provided a Sobolev inequality holds. In what follows, we also let
\{\Omega_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbf{N}}}
be an exhaustion of

by smooth bounded domains. Subcriticality is the key assumption for the existence of positive, minimal Green functions, as the following lemma demonstrates:

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that

is subcritical as indicated above. Then, the following statements hold:

(i)

there exists a nonnegative kernel

which is finite outside of the diagonal

, where

is the positive Green function of
(-L)
on
\Omega_{n}
with Dirichlet boundary conditions.

(ii)

The kernel

defines an integral operator

Gf(x):=\int_{\Omega}G(x,y)f(y)\,d\mu(y)

Moreover, if

is such that

, then

and

.

We have not been able to find this lemma in the literature in this form. Therefore, we give a full proof of it in the Appendix. We also recall the notion of positive solution of

with minimal growth from criticality theory (see

]
):

Definition 4.2

Let

in

For instance, if

Coming back to the case of a general domain

Theorem 4.3

Assume that

is subcritical. Then the Green function

, which is solution of

.

Definition 4.4
.

A family of Green functions

The class

naturally appears because of the following easy fact:

Lemma 4.5
.

Let

be a non-negative second-order, elliptic operator with smooth coefficients. Assume that

has positive minimal Green function

, which is of class

. Then,

C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)\subset\mathrm{Dom}\left((-L)^{-1}\right),

and if

.

Proof.

It follows easily from the facts that the domain of

contains the space of functions

such that

(see Lemma

∎

We now introduce the Sobolev inequality for

it writes:

(39)

C_{S}\left(\int_{\Omega}|f|^{p}\,d\mu\right)^{2/p}\leq Q_{L}(f)=||(-L)^{1/2}f||_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)}^{2},\quad\forall f\in\mathrm{Dom}((-L)^{1/2}),

with

being the best constant. We will need the following technical lemma, which makes the connection between the Green operator and the Sobolev constant for

:

Lemma 4.6
.

Make the same assumptions as in Lemma

and let

be the family of minimal Green functions given by the lemma. Assume in addition that

belong to the class

. Then, the best constant

in Sobolev’s inequality (

is given by the formula:

\frac{1}{C_{S}}=\sup\frac{\langle Gu,u\rangle}{||u||^{2}_{q}},

where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, and the sup is taken over all non-negative, smooth compactly supported functions.

Let us point out that this lemma is related (at least in spirit) to the proof of the main theorem in
[
2
]
.

Proof.

Step 1. Let
C_{S}
denote the best constant in (
39
). We claim that

\frac{1}{C_{S}}=\sup_{u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\langle(-L)^{-1}u,u\rangle}{||u||_{q}^{2}}\,.

Take indeed

and let

; by the Sobolev inequality (
39
) applied to the function

, we have

(40)

{C}||(-L)^{-1/2}u||_{p}^{2}\leq||u||_{2}^{2}

The inequality (
40
) is thus valid for any

in the image

of
(-L)^{1/2}
, and the best constant is equal to
C_{S}
. We claim that

is dense in

; indeed, if

is orthogonal to

, then by definition, for every

\langle v,(-L)^{1/2}w\rangle=0.

Since

is self-adjoint, the above equality implies that

and
(-L)^{1/2}v=0
.
By the Sobolev inequality (
39
),

. Thus,

, and we conclude that

is dense in

.
It follows from this fact and (

\frac{1}{C_{S}}=||(-L)^{-1/2}||_{2\to p}^{2}.

Duality of the Lebesgue spaces and the self-adjointness of

imply that

||(-L)^{-1/2}||_{2\to p}=||(-L)^{-1/2}||_{q\to 2}.

Now, if

\langle(-L)^{-1/2}u,(-L)^{-1/2}u\rangle=\langle(-L)^{-1}u,u\rangle

According to Lemma
4.5

C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)\subset\mathrm{Dom}((-L)^{-1})
, and since

is dense in

, we get

\frac{1}{C_{S}}=\sup_{u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\langle(-L)^{-1}u,u\rangle}{||u||_{q}^{2}}

as claimed. Moreover, according to Lemma

, for

, we have

, hence we obtain that

\frac{1}{C_{S}}=\sup_{u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\langle Gu,u\rangle}{||u||_{q}^{2}}

Step 2. It remains to be shown that the above supremum can be taken over non-negative, smooth and compactly supported functions.

From the fact that
(-L)^{-1}
has a kernel

which is non-negative, we have for any

Gu\leq G|u|.

Moreover, one can approximate

uniformly by non-negative, smooth, compactly supported functions

whose support remains in a fixed compact set, so that

\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}G(x,y)u_{n}(x)u_{n}(y)\,d\mu(x)d\mu(y)\rightarrow\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}G(x,y)|u(x)u(y)|\,d\mu(x)d\mu(y).

This implies that

\sup_{u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\langle Gu,u\rangle}{||u||_{q}^{2}}\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq u\\
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}\end{subarray}}\frac{\langle Gu,u\rangle}{||u||_{q}^{2}}.

The converse inequality being obvious, we finally get

\frac{1}{C_{S}}=\sup_{0\leq u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\langle Gu,u\rangle}{||u||_{q}^{2}},

which concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

\psi(y)=\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}|y|^{-a}|x|^{-a}\Phi(x,y)\left(-\left(\Delta+\frac{c}{|x|^{2}}\right)\psi(x)\right)\,dx

that is, to the fact that

|y|^{-a}|x|^{-a}\Phi(x,y)

is a fundamental solution of the operator
\Delta+\frac{c}{|x|^{2}}
. Let us consider the positive, minimal Green function
g(x,y)
of the operator
\Delta+\frac{c}{|x|^{2}}
in the domain
{\mathbf{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, and let us choose

\Phi(x,y)=|x|^{a}|y|^{a}g(x,y).

Note that
g
is well-defined. Indeed, since
c<\frac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}
, the operator
\Delta+\frac{c}{|x|^{2}}
is subcritical in
{\mathbf{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
. In Appendix B of
[
12
]
, it is proven the existence of a positive function
h:{\mathbf{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\times{\mathbf{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\setminus\{(x,x)\,;\,x\in{\mathbf{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\}
, which solves
\mathscr{L}_{x}h(\cdot,y)=\delta_{y}
,
\mathscr{L}=-\Delta-\frac{c}{|x|^{2}}
in the distribution sense, and with the following asymptotics as zero and at infinity:

(49)

h(x,y)\asymp_{x\to 0}|x|^{-\beta_{-}(c)},\quad h(x,y)\asymp_{x\to\infty}|x|^{-\beta_{+}(c)},

where

\beta_{\pm}(c)=\frac{d-2}{2}\pm\sqrt{\frac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}-c}.

In addition, it is easily checked that the functions
x\mapsto|x|^{-\beta_{\pm}(c)}
are two positive
\mathscr{L}
-harmonic functions on
{\mathbf{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
. Since obviously

\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{|x|^{-\beta_{+}(c)}}{|x|^{-\beta_{-}(c)}}=+\infty,\quad\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{|x|^{-\beta_{+}(c)}}{|x|^{-\beta_{-}(c)}}=0,

it follows from
[
5
, Prop. 6.1]
that
|x|^{-\beta_{-}(c)}
has minimal growth at zero, and
|x|^{-\beta_{+}(c)}
has minimal growth at infinity, for
\mathscr{L}
. Given the asymptotics (
49
) of
h
near zero and near infinity, we conclude that
h(\cdot,y)
has minimal growth for
\mathscr{L}
both at zero and at infinity, or in other words, at infinity in the punctured domain
\Omega={\mathbf{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
. According to the uniqueness of positive minimal Green functions (Theorem
4.3
), one necessarily has
h=g
. Thus, we conclude that the Green function constructed by N. Ghoussoub and F. Robert in
[
12
]
is the same as the one which follows from the construction of Lemma
4.1
. In particular, the estimates proved in
[
12
]
apply to
g
:

(50)

g(x,y)\asymp|x-y|^{2-d}\left(\frac{\max(|x|,|y|)}{\min(|x|,|y|)}\right)^{\beta_{-}(c)}\,.

Now, since by assumption
a<\frac{d-2}{2}
, it follows that
\beta_{-}(c)=a
. This implies easily that
\Phi
satisfies the estimate (
51
). Thus, we have proved the following result:

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\Phi
be the fundamental solution of
L_{\mathbf{E}}
. Then, we have

(51)

\Phi(x,y)\asymp|x-y|^{2-d}\max(|x|,|y|)^{2a}

uniformly in all
x,y\in{\mathbf{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, such that
x\neq y
.

Now consider the restriction of

Proposition 5.5
.

The Green’s function

of

Remark 5.6
.

Note that for the case

,

considered in this paragraph, the coefficients of

are not singular at zero. Thus, the Green function estimates that we obtain hold for

{\mathbf{B}}

Proof.

We start by recalling how
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
is related to

\psi(x)=\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{2}
is such that
g_{\mathbf{H}^{d}}=\psi^{-2}g_{\mathbf{E}}
(with

being the standard Euclidean metric, since

\Delta_{\mathbf{H}^{d}}v+\frac{d(d-2)}{4}v=\psi^{\frac{d+2}{2}}\Delta(\psi^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}v)

Thus, it follows that

(\Delta_{\mathbf{H}^{d}}+\frac{d(d-2)+\lambda}{4})v=\psi^{\frac{d+2}{2}}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(\psi^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}v),

or equivalently

(56)

\psi^{-\frac{d+2}{2}}(\Delta_{\mathbf{H}^{d}}+\frac{d(d-2)+\lambda}{4})(\psi^{\frac{d-2}{2}}w)=\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}w,

Denote by
g_{\lambda}(x,y)
the Green’s function of the operator
\Delta_{\mathbf{H}^{d}}+\frac{d(d-2)+\lambda}{4}
. By definition of
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
, for every test function

, one has

-\varphi(x)=\int_{{\mathbf{B}}}\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(x,y)(\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}\varphi)(y)\,d\mu_{\mathbf{E}}(y).

Writing
\mu_{\mathbf{H}^{d}}=\psi^{-d}\mu_{\mathbf{E}}
,
\varphi=\psi^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\tilde{\varphi}
, and replacing
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}\varphi
using the conformal invariance of equation (
56

-\psi(x)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\tilde{\varphi}(x)=\int_{\mathbf{B}}\psi(y)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(x,y)\left(\Delta_{\mathbf{H}^{d}}+\frac{d(d-2)+\lambda}{4}\right)\tilde{\varphi}(y)\,d\mu_{\mathbf{H}^{d}},

from which it follows that the Green functions

and
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
are related by:

(57)

\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(x,y)=\psi(x)^{\frac{2-d}{2}}\psi(y)^{\frac{2-d}{2}}g_{\lambda}(x,y),

where
\psi(x)=\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{2}
is the conformal factor relating the Euclidean and hyperbolic metrics.
Given a point

in hyperbolic space, let

denote the hyperbolic translation such that

\frac{\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(x,y)}{\mathcal{G}_{0}(x,y)}=\frac{g_{\lambda}(x,y)}{g_{0}(x,y)}=\frac{g_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},0)}{g_{0}(x^{\prime},0)}=\frac{\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},0)}{\mathcal{G}_{0}(x^{\prime},0)},

is Green’s function for the standard Laplacian in the unit ball; the function

\mathcal{G}_{0}(x,0)\asymp|x|^{2-d}\delta(x)

Let us write in
{\mathbf{B}}_{1}

, with
\psi_{\lambda}>0
, so that
\psi_{\lambda}(\cdot,0)
vanishes on

-\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}\psi_{\lambda}(\cdot,0)=\frac{\lambda}{(1-|x|^{2})^{2}}\mathcal{G}_{0}(\cdot,0)\qquad\text{in ${\mathbf{B}}_{1}$}

Since

Near the boundary

\frac{\lambda}{(1-|x|^{2})^{2}}\mathcal{G}_{0}(x,0)\lesssim\delta(x)^{-1}

\psi_{\lambda}(x,0)\lesssim\delta(x)^{\beta_{\lambda}}

Plugging this in (

\frac{\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(x,y)}{\mathcal{G}_{0}(x,y)}=1+\frac{\mathcal{\psi}_{\lambda}(x,y)}{\mathcal{G}_{0}(x,y)}\lesssim 1+\delta(x^{\prime})^{\beta_{\lambda}-1}|x^{\prime}|^{d-2}

It remains to understand the behaviour of

as a function of

and
y

.
Then, either
\delta(y)\leq 10|x-y|

\delta(x)\leq 10|x-y|

(59)

|x^{\prime}|\geq\frac{1}{100}.

Indeed, by definition of
x^{\prime}
and the known formula for the hyperbolic translation

x-y=(1-|y|^{2})\frac{x^{\prime}+|x^{\prime}|^{2}y}{1+2x^{\prime}y+|y|^{2}|x^{\prime}|^{2}}

\delta(x^{\prime})\asymp\frac{\delta(x)\delta(y)}{|x-y|^{2}}

By (

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{0}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\psi(x)^{\frac{2-d}{2}}\psi(y)^{\frac{2-d}{2}}g_{0}(x,y)

Proof of Lemma

Recall that

Recall that

given by (

~
L

E

(-\tilde{L}_{\mathbf{E}}-\lambda V)(r^{a}\delta^{\gamma})=-\left(\gamma(\gamma-1)+\frac{\lambda}{4}\right)\delta^{\gamma-2}+O(\delta^{\gamma-1}).

The equation

\gamma(\gamma-1)+\frac{\lambda}{4}=0

has for solutions

\gamma_{\pm}=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1-\lambda}}{2}.

Note that
\beta_{\lambda}=\gamma_{+}>\gamma_{-}>0
, so the function
r^{a}\delta^{\gamma}
is in some sense an approximate solution of
(-\tilde{L}_{\mathbf{E}}-\lambda V)
which decays the fastest possible at the boundary. The statement of the lemma is that it gives the decay at the boundary of the Green function. In order to make this rigorous, we consider the following functions which will be used as barriers:

\varphi_{\pm}:=r^{a}(\delta^{\beta}_{\lambda}\pm\delta^{\nu}),

where
\nu>\beta
is to be determined shortly. According to (
66
), as
\delta\to 0
, one has

(-\tilde{L}_{\mathbf{E}}-\lambda V)\varphi_{\pm}=O(\delta^{\beta_{\lambda}-1})\mp\left(\nu(\nu-1)+\frac{\lambda}{4}\right)\delta^{\nu-2}+O(\delta^{\nu-1}).

Take
\nu
such that the dominating term of the right-hand side is
\asymp\delta^{\nu-2}
, and such that
\varphi_{\pm}\asymp r^{a}\delta^{\beta_{\lambda}}
as
\delta\to 0
: all this amounts to
\beta_{\lambda}<\nu<\beta_{\lambda}+1
. For such a choice of
\nu
, one has moreover that
\nu(\nu-1)+\frac{\lambda}{4}>0
. Hence,
\varphi_{+}
(resp.
\varphi_{-}
) is a positive subsolution (resp. supersolution) of
(-\tilde{L}_{\mathbf{E}}-\lambda V)
in a neighborhood of
\partial{\mathbf{B}}
. According to the comparison principle stated in
[
15
, Lemma 8]
(whose proof adapts easily to our context), if we can prove that

(67)

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{D_{k}}\frac{\varphi_{\pm}^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\,dx=0,

where
D_{k}:=\{2^{-k-1}\leq\delta<2^{-k}\}
,
k\in{\mathbf{N}}
, then one concludes that there is a constant
C>1
such that any positive solution of
(-\tilde{L}_{\mathbf{E}}-\lambda V)u=0
in a neighborhood of
\partial{\mathbf{B}}
with minimal growth for this equation, satisfies

\frac{1}{C}\varphi_{+}\leq u\leq C\varphi_{-}.

Since

\varphi_{\pm}\asymp\delta^{\beta_{\lambda}}

as
\delta\to 0
, it follows that

u\asymp\delta^{\beta_{\lambda}}

as
\delta\to 0
. In particular, this applies to
u=G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(\cdot,y)
, which by construction of the Green functions does have minimal growth in a neighborhood of
\partial{\mathbf{B}}
. Hence, we get

(68)

G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(x,y)\asymp\delta(x)^{\beta_{\lambda}}

for fixed
y
and
x\to\partial{\mathbf{B}}
. Let us now prove (
67
); using
\varphi_{\pm}\asymp\delta^{\beta_{\lambda}}
as
\delta\to 0
, and taking coordinates
t=\delta(x)
,
\omega=\frac{x}{|x|}
on
D_{k}
, we see that

\int_{D_{k}}\frac{\varphi_{\pm}^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\,dx\asymp\int_{2^{-k-1}}^{2^{-k}}t^{2(\beta_{\lambda}-1)}\,dt\leq\frac{1}{2\beta-1}2^{-k(2\beta_{\lambda}-1)},

which indeed goes to
0
as
k\to\infty
, since by definition of
\beta
,
2\beta_{\lambda}-1=\sqrt{1-\lambda}>0
.

Finally, let us prove that the decay rate (
68
) of
G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(x,y)
for fixed
y
is uniform in
y\in\partial{\mathbf{B}}_{1/4}
. According to (
68
), for every fixed
y\in\partial{\mathbf{B}}_{\eta_{1}}
, there is a constant
C(y)>0
such that for all
x\notin{\mathbf{B}}_{\eta_{2}}
,

C(y)^{-1}\delta(x)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\leq G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(x,y)\leq C(y)\delta(x)^{\beta_{\lambda}}.

Let us fix
y_{0}\in\partial{\mathbf{B}}_{\eta_{1}}
. By the local Harnack inequality,

G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(x,y)\asymp G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(x,y_{0}),\quad y\in\partial{\mathbf{B}}_{\eta_{1}},

uniformly in
x\notin{\mathbf{B}}_{\eta_{2}}
. Using the decay rate (
68
) for
y=y_{0}
shows the result of the Lemma.
∎

Keeping the notation in (
64
), now we look at what happens as
y\to 0
. We have the following lemma:

Lemma 5.10
.

The following estimate holds:

G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(x,y)\asymp 1\,,

uniformly in
y\in{\mathbf{B}}_{\eta_{1}}^{*},\,x\in\partial{\mathbf{B}}_{\eta_{2}}
.

Proof of Lemma
5.10
.

Let us keep the notations of the proof of Lemma
5.9
, in particular the definition of the conjugated operator
\mathscr{L}
. It follows from
[
12
, Theorem 6]
that if
x
is fixed, then the Green function of
\mathscr{L}
at
(x,y)
is comparable to
|y|^{-\beta_{-}}
as
y\to 0
, where

\beta_{-}=\frac{d-2}{2}-\sqrt{\frac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}-c}.

It follows that
G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(x,y)
is comparable to
|y|^{a}\cdot|y|^{-\beta_{-}}
as
y\to 0
. One then computes that (since
a<\frac{d-2}{2}
)

\beta_{-}=a.

Thus,
G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(x,y)
possesses the announced asymptotic for fixed
x
and
y\to 0
. The local Harnack argument already used at the end of the proof of Lemma
5.9
then implies that this behaviour can be made uniform in
x\in\partial{\mathbf{B}}_{\eta_{2}}
. This concludes the proof.∎

Now, we can prove Proposition
5.8
as follows.
By definition of the considered regime (
64
), we want to prove that

G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(x,y)\asymp\delta(x)^{\beta_{\lambda}}.

Fix
x
such that

and let

for

. Then,
\psi
is
L_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}
harmonic in
{\mathbf{B}}_{\eta_{1}}
and, by Lemma

\psi(y)\asymp\delta(x)^{\beta_{\lambda}}

(which applies since

5.4.2.
Regime (ii)

In this paragraph, we consider the following regime

(69)

x\in{\mathbf{B}}_{1}\setminus{\mathbf{B}}_{1-\varepsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad y\in{\mathbf{B}}_{1}\setminus{\mathbf{B}}_{1-\varepsilon}\,,\text{ with }x\neq y\,,

and show the following estimate:

Proposition 5.11
.

There is
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, such that the following estimate holds:

(70)

G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(x,y)\lesssim|x-y|^{2-d}\min\left(1,\left(\frac{\delta(x)\delta(y)}{|x-y|^{2}}\right)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\right)

uniformly
x,y
such that (

Proof of Proposition
5.11
.

Observe that the coefficients of the second order elliptic operators

\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}~=\Delta+\frac{\lambda}{(1-|x|^{2})^{2}}\quad\text{ and }\quad L_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}=|x|^{2(1-\alpha)}\left(\Delta-2a\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\cdot\nabla\right)+\frac{\lambda\alpha^{2}}{(1-|x|^{2\alpha})^{2}}

are equivalent to leading order as
x\to\partial{\mathbf{B}}_{1}
(except a troublesome first order drift appearing in
L_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}
). With this in mind, we want to prove that for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, the corresponding Green’s function of the latter operator on
{\mathbf{B}}_{1}\setminus{\mathbf{B}}_{1-\varepsilon}
can be obtained as a perturbation of the former, paving the way to extending the Green’s function estimate derived in the previous section to the case
\alpha\in(0,1)
in the regime (
ii
). For this, fix
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon^{\prime}
, and denote
\Omega={\mathbf{B}}_{1}\setminus{\mathbf{B}}_{1-\varepsilon^{\prime}}
(which by definition contains
{\mathbf{B}}_{1}\setminus{\mathbf{B}}_{1-\varepsilon}
). Let
\mathcal{G}^{\Omega},G^{\Omega}
denote the respective Green’s functions of
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda},L_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}
on
\Omega
constructed from Lemma
4.1
. From this very construction, since
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
is the Green’s function for the same operator
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
on
{\mathbf{B}}^{*}\supset\Omega
, we get that
0\leq\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}\leq\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
, so that we get from Proposition
5.5
that

(71)

0\leq\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(x,y)\lesssim|x-y|^{2-d}\min\left(1,\left(\frac{\delta(x)\delta(y)}{|x-y|^{2}}\right)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\right)

uniformly in
x,y\in\Omega=\Omega(\varepsilon’)
such that
x\neq y
and in
\varepsilon’>0
. Independently, given
\varepsilon’>0
and
y\in\Omega
,
G^{\Omega}(\cdot,y)
has minimal growth on
\partial\Omega
by construction, and we have that

(72)

0\leq G^{\Omega}(x,y)\lesssim|x-y|^{2-d}\delta(x)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial{\mathbf{B}}_{1-\varepsilon^{\prime}})

uniformly (only) in
x\in\Omega\setminus\{y\}
. To get (
72
), one can apply almost verbatim the proof of Lemma
5.9
in order to get the estimate for
x\to\partial{\mathbf{B}}
, apply the classical Hopf lemma to get the estimate for
x\to\partial{\mathbf{B}}_{1-\varepsilon’}
, and apply classical comparison principles to
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
with smooth coefficients in
\Omega
for the estimate as
x\to y
. The first and main step to get Proposition
5.11
basically reduces to improving (part of) the estimate (
72
) into an estimate which is uniform in
y
in the spirit of (
71
) and it is stated precisely as follows:

Step 1: given any
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
small enough, one has

0<G^{\Omega}(x,y)\lesssim|x-y|^{2-d}\min\left(1,\left(\frac{\delta(x)\delta(y)}{|x-y|^{2}}\right)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\right)

for all
x,y\in\Omega
such that
x\neq y
.

By definition of Green’s functions, given
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
and
y\in\Omega
, we have

\varphi(y)=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(x,y)(-\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}\varphi(x))dx

and

(73)

\varphi(y)=\int_{\Omega}G^{\Omega}(x,y)(-L_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}\varphi(x))d\mu_{\mathbf{E}}(x)\\
=\int_{\Omega}|x|^{-2a}G^{\Omega}(x,y)\left(-\Delta+2a\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\cdot\nabla-\frac{\lambda\alpha^{2}|x|^{-2(1-\alpha)}}{(1-|x|^{2\alpha})^{2}}\right)\varphi(x)dx\,,

so that, letting
c
be the function given by
c(x)=|x|^{-2a}G^{\Omega}(x,y)-\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(x,y)
, we get

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}c(x)(-\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}\varphi(x))dx\\
&\qquad=\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(x,y)+c(x))\left(-2a\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\cdot\nabla+\frac{\lambda\alpha^{2}|x|^{-2(1-\alpha)}}{(1-|x|^{2\alpha})^{2}}-\frac{\lambda}{(1-|x|^{2})^{2}}\right)\varphi(x)dx\,.\end{split}

Integrating by parts, we deduce that
c\in C^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus\{y\})
solves

-\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}c=R(\cdot,y)\quad\text{ weakly in }\Omega

and then also strongly in
\Omega\setminus\{y\}
, where
R(\cdot,y)\in C^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus\{y\})
is given by

R(x,y)=2a\nabla\cdot\left([\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(x,y)+c(x)]\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)+\lambda\bigg(\underset{\lesssim\delta(x)^{-1}}{\underbrace{\frac{\alpha^{2}|x|^{-2(1-\alpha)}}{(1-|x|^{2\alpha})^{2}}-\frac{1}{(1-|x|^{2})^{2}}}}\bigg)(\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(x,y)+c(x))\,.

By (
71
) and (
72
), we get from standard elliptic theory that, for each fixed
y
,

|\nabla\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(\cdot,y)|+|\nabla G^{\Omega}(\cdot,y)|\lesssim|\cdot-y|^{1-d}\delta^{\beta_{\lambda}-1}\text{ in }\Omega\setminus\{y\}\,.

From the definition of
c
, using this gradient estimate and the pointwise estimates (
71
)-(
72
) (using
\beta_{\lambda}>1/2
on
\partial{\mathbf{B}}
) to show that the boundary terms on
\partial(\Omega\setminus\{y\})
do not contribute, we integrate by parts and get on the one hand

(74)

c(x)=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(x,z)R(z,y)\,dz\,,

and on the other hand

(75)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(x,z)R(z,y)\,dz=\int_{\Omega}W(x,z)(\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(z,y)+c(z))\,dz\end{split}

for all
x\neq y
, where

W(x,z)=\left(-2a\nabla_{z}\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(x,z)\cdot\frac{z}{|z|^{2}}\right)+\lambda\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}|z|^{-2(1-\alpha)}}{(1-|z|^{2\alpha})^{2}}-\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}\right)\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(x,z)\,.

Now given
y\in\Omega
, let us introduce the
L^{\infty}
-type weighted space (depending on
y
)
X=X_{y}\subset\left[C^{0}\left(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{y\}\right)\cap L^{1}(\Omega)\right]
given as the completion of
C^{0}\left(\overline{\Omega}\right)
for the norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
given by

\left\|f\right\|_{X}:=\sup_{x\in\Omega\setminus\{y\}}\left|\frac{f(x)}{|x-y|^{2-d}\min\left(1,\left(\frac{\delta(x)\delta(y)}{|x-y|^{2}}\right)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\right)}\right|\,.

By (
72
),
G^{\Omega}(\cdot,y)
belongs to
X
. From now on in this proof, our estimates are uniform in
y\in\Omega
and in
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
small enough. By (
71
), we have now

\left\|\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(\cdot,y)\right\|_{X}\lesssim 1\,,

so that, in order to prove Step 1, it is sufficient to show
\|c\|_{X}\leq 1
for all
y\in\Omega
, for
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
small enough. Then, by taking the norm of (
74
) while using (
75
) with the triangular inequality, the conclusion of the proof of Step 1 reduces to

(76)

\begin{split}&\|c\|_{X}\leq\left\|C_{1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(\cdot,z)}{\delta(z)}(\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(z,y)+c(z))\,dz\right\|_{X}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\left\|C_{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{z}\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(\cdot,z)|(\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(z,y)+c(z))\,dz\right\|_{X}\leq\,\frac{1}{2}\left({1+\|c\|_{X}}\right)\end{split}

for all
y\in\Omega
, for
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
small enough, for some constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
. We start by estimating the first integral term in (
76
):

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{C_{1}}{\delta(z)}\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(x,z)\left(\frac{\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(z,y)+c(z)}{1+\|c\|_{X}}\right)dz

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\int_{\Omega}|x-z|^{2-d}\left(\frac{\delta(x)\delta(z)}{|x-z|^{2}}\right)^{\beta_{\lambda}}|z-y|^{2-d}\left(\frac{\delta(z)\delta(y)}{|z-y|^{2}}\right)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\frac{1}{\delta(z)}dz

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim(\varepsilon^{\prime})^{2\beta_{\lambda}-1}\delta(x)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\delta(y)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\int_{\Omega}|x-z|^{2-d-2\beta}|z-y|^{2-d-2\beta}dz

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim(\varepsilon^{\prime})^{2\beta_{\lambda}-1}\delta(x)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\delta(y)^{\beta_{\lambda}}|x-y|^{4-d-4{\beta_{\lambda}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim(\varepsilon^{\prime})^{2\beta_{\lambda}-1}|x-y|^{2-2\beta_{\lambda}}|x-y|^{2-d}\left(\frac{\delta(x)\delta(y)}{|x-y|^{2}}\right)^{\beta_{\lambda}}

Since
1/2<\beta_{\lambda}<1
, choosing
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
sufficiently small, assuming that
\frac{\delta(x)\delta(y)}{|x-y|^{2}}\leq 4
, the above quantity is bounded by

(77)

\frac{1}{16}|x-y|^{2-d}\left(\frac{\delta(x)\delta(y)}{|x-y|^{2}}\right)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\leq\frac{1}{4}|x-y|^{2-d}\min\left(1,\left(\frac{\delta(x)\delta(y)}{|x-y|^{2}}\right)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\right),

Otherwise when
\frac{\delta(x)\delta(y)}{|x-y|^{2}}>4
, we may always assume by symmetry that
\frac{\delta(x)}{|x-y|}>2
, whence
\frac{3}{2}\delta(x)\geq\delta(y)\geq\frac{1}{2}\delta(x)
and so
\frac{\delta(y)}{|x-y|}>1
. Then, on the ball
B(x,\delta(x)/2)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{[|x-z|\leq\frac{1}{2}\delta(x)]}\frac{C_{1}}{\delta(z)}\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(x,z)\left(\frac{\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(z,y)+c(z)}{1+\|c\|_{X}}\right)dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\delta(x)}\int_{\Omega}|x-z|^{2-d}|y-z|^{2-d}dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\delta(x)}|x-y|^{2-d}|x-y|^{2}\lesssim\delta(x)|x-y|^{2-d}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}|x-y|^{2-d}\min\left(1,\left(\frac{\delta(x)\delta(y)}{|x-y|^{2}}\right)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\right)

if, for the last inequality,
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
is chosen small enough (using
\delta(x)\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}
). By symmetry, the same estimate holds on the ball
B(y,\delta(y)/2)
. In
C:=\Omega\setminus(B(x,\delta(x)/2)\cup B(y,\delta(y)/2)
at last, recalling that
\delta(x)\sim\delta(y),
we have the bound

\displaystyle\int_{C}\frac{C_{1}\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(x,z)}{\delta(z)}\left(\frac{\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(z,y)+c(z)}{1+\|c\|_{X}}\right)dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta(x)^{2\beta_{\lambda}}\int_{C}\delta(z)^{2{\beta_{\lambda}}-1}|x-z|^{2-d-2\beta_{\lambda}}|y-z|^{2-d-2\beta_{\lambda}}dz\,.

Change variable by setting
Z=\frac{z-x}{\delta(x)}
,

Y=\frac{y-x}{\delta(x)}
and
C_{x,y}=\frac{C-x}{\delta(x)}
. Recalling that
d\geq 3
, the right-hand side of the above inequality is bounded above by

\int_{C_{x,y}}\mathtt{d}(Z)^{-1+2{\beta_{\lambda}}}|Z|^{2-d-2{\beta_{\lambda}}}|Y-Z|^{2-d-2{\beta_{\lambda}}}dZ,

where
\mathtt{d}(Z)
is the distance function to the boundary of the ball
{\mathbf{B}}^{x}=\frac{{\mathbf{B}}_{1}-x}{\delta(x)}
. This ball is included in the half-space
[Z_{1}\leq 1]
where
Z_{1}
is the coordinate of
Z
in the direction
e_{1}=x/|x|
and so
\mathtt{d}(Z)\leq 1-Z_{1}\leq 1+|Z|
for

. Using that
{\beta_{\lambda}}>1/2
, that
\frac{3}{2}\geq\frac{\delta(y)}{\delta(x)}\geq\frac{1}{4}
and that
|Y|<\frac{1}{2}
which implies
|Z-Y|\geq\max(1/4,|Z|/2)
for

, we can bound the above integral by a constant multiple of

\int_{[Z_{1}\leq 1]\setminus{\mathbf{B}}(0,1/2)}(1+|Z|)^{-1+2{\beta_{\lambda}}}|Z|^{2-d-2{\beta_{\lambda}}}\max(1,|Z|)^{2-d-2{\beta_{\lambda}}}dZ,

which is a finite integral. Recall that in the considered regime, we have
|x-y|<\frac{1}{2}\delta(x)
and so
|x-y|^{2-d}

\frac{1}{8}|x-y|^{2-d}=\frac{1}{8}|x-y|^{2-d}\min\left(1,\left(\frac{\delta(x)\delta(y)}{|x-y|^{2}}\right)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\right)
in the considered regime. We finally conclude that, in all cases,

\int_{\Omega}\frac{C_{1}}{\delta(z)}\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(x,z)\left(\frac{\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(z,y)+c(z)}{1+\|c\|_{X}}\right)dz\leq\frac{1}{4}|x-y|^{2-d}\min\left(1,\left(\frac{\delta(x)\delta(y)}{|x-y|^{2}}\right)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\right)

It remains to estimate the second integral term in (
76
). To do so, we observe that

|\nabla_{z}\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(x,z)|\lesssim\frac{1}{\delta(z)}|x-z|^{2-d}\min\left(1,\left(\frac{\delta(x)\delta(z)}{|x-z|^{2}}\right)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\right)

if

, as follows from standard elliptic theory applied to the rescaled function
u(Z)=\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(x,z+\delta(z)Z)
,
Z\in{\mathbf{B}}_{1/4}
with (
71
), while

(78)

|\nabla_{z}\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(x,z)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-z|}|x-z|^{2-d}\min\left(1,\left(\frac{\delta(x)\delta(z)}{|x-z|^{2}}\right)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\right)

if

, as follows from standard elliptic theory applied to the rescaled function
v(Z)=\mathcal{G}^{\Omega}(x,z+|x-z|Z)
,
Z\in{\mathbf{B}}_{1/4}
with (
71
). The second integral term in (
76

can therefore be bounded above by the sum of (

) and a constant multiple of

\int_{[|x-z|\leq\frac{1}{2}\delta(z)]}|x-z|^{1-d}|z-y|^{2-d}\min\left(1,\left(\frac{\delta(z)\delta(y)}{|z-y|^{2}}\right)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\right)dz\,.

If
|x-y|\leq\delta(x)/2
, there exists a constant
C_{3}>0
such that the above integral is bounded by

\int_{[|x-z|\leq\frac{1}{2}\delta(z)]}|x-z|^{1-d}|z-y|^{2-d}dz\lesssim\ln\left(\frac{C_{3}\delta(x)}{|x-y|}\right)\lesssim\left(\frac{\delta(x)}{|x-y|}\right)^{2-d},

which is bounded by (

) for small enough

, while if

it is bounded instead by a constant times

\delta(x)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\delta(y)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\int_{[|x-z|\leq\frac{1}{2}\delta(z)]}|x-z|^{1-d}|z-y|^{2-d-2\beta_{\lambda}}dz\lesssim\delta(x)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\delta(y)^{\beta_{\lambda}}|x-y|^{3-d-2\beta_{\lambda}},

which is again bounded by (
77
) for small enough
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
. This concludes the proof of (

) and then that of Step 1.

Step 2: we show the required estimate for
G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}

Consider the function

f=G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}-G^{\Omega}.

By construction of
G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}
, one has
G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}\geq G^{\Omega}
, thus
f
is non-negative. Moreover,
f
is
L_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}
-harmonic in
\Omega
, and non-zero, so by the strong maximum principle,
f
is positive. Since

f\leq G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda},

and
G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(x,\cdot)
is a positive
L_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}
-harmonic function with minimal growth at infinity in
\mathbf{H}
, it follows that the function
f(x,\cdot)
has minimal growth at
\partial{\mathbf{B}}_{1}
. According to Lemma
5.9
the minimal growth for positive
L_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}
-harmonic function is
\simeq\delta(y)^{\beta_{\lambda}}
when
y
approaches
\partial{\mathbf{B}}_{1}
, and thus it follows that there is a constant
C>0
(depending only on the maximum of
f
on
\partial{\mathbf{B}}_{1-\varepsilon}\times\partial{\mathbf{B}}_{1-\varepsilon}
, which is finite by local elliptic regularity) such that, for all
x\in\partial{\mathbf{B}}_{1-\varepsilon}
and
y\in{\mathbf{B}}_{1}\setminus{\mathbf{B}}_{1-\varepsilon}

(79)

f(x,y)\leq C\delta(y)^{\beta_{\lambda}}.

Now fix
y\in{\mathbf{B}}_{1}\setminus{\mathbf{B}}_{1-\varepsilon}
, and consider
u(x)=f(x,y)
, which is a positive
L_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}
-harmonic function in
{\mathbf{B}}_{1}\setminus{\mathbf{B}}_{1-\varepsilon}
. By the same argument as above,
u
has minimal growth as
x
approaches
\partial{\mathbf{B}}_{1}
, and thus there is a constant
C^{\prime}>0
, which only depends on
\max_{\partial{\mathbf{B}}_{1-\varepsilon}}u
, such that

u(x)\leq C^{\prime}\delta(x)^{\beta_{\lambda}},\quad x\in{\mathbf{B}}_{1-\varepsilon}.

But according to (
79
),
\max_{\partial{\mathbf{B}}_{1-\varepsilon}}u
is bounded by
C\delta(y)^{\beta_{\lambda}}
, and we obtain the estimate

f(x,y)\leq C^{\prime\prime}\delta(x)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\delta(y)^{\beta_{\lambda}},\quad\forall x,y\in{\mathbf{B}}_{1}\setminus{\mathbf{B}}_{1-\varepsilon},

where
C^{\prime\prime}
is a positive constant.
Finally, using the estimate on
G^{\Omega}
from Step 1, we get:

(80)

G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(x,y)\lesssim|x-y|^{2-d}\min\left(1,\left(\frac{\delta(x)\delta(y)}{|x-y|^{2}}\right)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\right)+\delta(x)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\delta(y)^{\beta_{\lambda}},\quad\forall x,y\in{\mathbf{B}}_{1}\setminus{\mathbf{B}}_{1-\varepsilon}

This implies the estimate (
70
) for
G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}
: indeed,

•

if
\min\left(1,\left(\frac{\delta(x)\delta(y)}{|x-y|^{2}}\right)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\right)=\left(\frac{\delta(x)\delta(y)}{|x-y|^{2}}\right)^{\beta_{\lambda}}
, then one conclude from (
80
), the fact that
2-d-2\beta_{\lambda}<0
, and
1\lesssim|x-y|^{-1}
that
G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(x,y)\lesssim|x-y|^{2-d}\left(\frac{\delta(x)\delta(y)}{|x-y|^{2}}\right)^{\beta_{\lambda}}
.

•

if
\min\left(1,\left(\frac{\delta(x)\delta(y)}{|x-y|^{2}}\right)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\right)=1
, then the estimate
G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(x,y)\lesssim|x-y|^{2-d}
follows from (
80
), and
\delta(x)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\delta(y)^{\beta_{\lambda}}\lesssim 1\lesssim|x-y|^{2-d}
.

This concludes the proof of Proposition
5.11
. ∎

5.4.3.
Regime (iii)

In this regime, we assume that

(81)

x\in{\mathbf{B}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad y\in{\mathbf{B}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}\,,\text{ with }x\neq y\,.

The estimate for
G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}
in regime (
iii
) follows from:

Proposition 5.12
.

If
\varepsilon>0
is small enough, then the following estimate holds for the Green function
G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}
:

(82)

G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(x,y)\lesssim\Phi(x,y),

uniformly in all
x,y\in{\mathbf{B}}_{\varepsilon}^{*},
such that
x\neq y
.

Proof of Proposition
5.12
.

Denote
G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda,\varepsilon}
the Green function of
L_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}
in
{\mathbf{B}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}
with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Since the potential term
\frac{\lambda\alpha}{(1-\rho^{2})^{2}}
in
L_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}
tends to
\lambda\alpha
as
\rho\to 0
, one sees that, if
\varepsilon
is chosen small enough,

G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda,\varepsilon}(x,y)\leq G_{L_{\mathbf{E}}+\eta,\varepsilon}(x,y),

where
G_{L_{\mathbf{E}}+\eta,\varepsilon}
is by definition the Green function of
L_{\mathbf{E}}+\eta
in
{\mathbf{B}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}
, and
\eta=(1+\varepsilon)\lambda\alpha
. Thus, in order to prove the proposition, we prove as a first step that

(83)

G_{L_{\mathbf{E}}+\eta,\varepsilon}(x,y)\lesssim\Phi(x,y),

which implies that

(84)

G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda,\varepsilon}(x,y)\lesssim\Phi(x,y),\quad x,y\in B_{\varepsilon}^{*},\,x\neq y

We claim that, according to the proof of Step (
ii
) (see also
[
18
]
), in order to show (
83
) for some small enough
\varepsilon>0
, it is enough to show that

(85)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{x,y\in{\mathbf{B}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}}\int_{{\mathbf{B}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}}\frac{\Phi(x,z)\Phi(z,y)}{\Phi(x,y)}\,d\mu_{\mathbf{E}}(z)=0.

In the language of
[
18
]
, (
85
) says that the constant potential is a small perturbation of
L_{\mathbf{E}}
around the origin, which implies that the Green functions of
L_{\mathbf{E}}
and
L_{\mathbf{E}}+\eta
on
{\mathbf{B}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}
are equivalent. For the sake of completeness, let us indicate briefly why showing (
85
) is enough to get (
83
), in the spirit of the proof of Step (
ii
). For a fixed
y\in{\mathbf{B}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}
, denote

c(x)=G_{L_{\mathbf{E}}+\eta,\varepsilon}(x,y)-G_{L_{\mathbf{E}},\varepsilon}(x,y).

Since
G_{L_{\mathbf{E}},\varepsilon}(x,y)\leq\Phi(x,y)
, (
83
) follows from the inequality
c(x)\lesssim\Phi(x,y)
, which, given the two-sided estimates on
\Phi(x,y)
, follows from
||c||_{X}\leq 1
(where the space
X=X_{y}
and the norm
||\cdot||_{X}
are defined as in Step (
ii
)). One easily checks that
c
satisfies

-L_{\mathbf{E}}c=-\eta G_{L_{\mathbf{E}}+\eta}(x,y):=R(x,y),

hence, one finds that

c(x)=\int_{{\mathbf{B}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}}G_{L_{\mathbf{E}},\varepsilon}(x,z)R(z,y)\,d\mu_{\mathbf{E}}(z)

Therefore, proving that
||c||_{X}\leq 1
amounts to finding
\varepsilon>0
small enough, such that

\eta\left\|\int_{{\mathbf{B}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}}G_{L_{\mathbf{E}},\varepsilon}(x,z)(G_{L_{\mathbf{E}},\varepsilon}(z,y)+c(z))\,d\mu_{\mathbf{E}}(z)\right\|_{X}\leq\frac{1}{2}(1+||c||_{X})

Dividing this inequality by
1+||c||_{X}
, we see that it is enough to prove that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{x,y\in{\mathbf{B}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}}\int_{{\mathbf{B}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}}\frac{G_{L_{\mathbf{E}},\varepsilon}(x,z)(G_{L_{\mathbf{E}},\varepsilon}(z,y)+1)}{\Phi(x,y)}\,d\mu_{\mathbf{E}}(z)=0

Given that
G_{L_{\mathbf{E}},\varepsilon}\leq\Phi
and that
\Phi(z,w)\gtrsim 1
for
z,w\in B_{\varepsilon}^{*}
(as follows from the standing assumption that
a<\frac{d-2}{2}
), we see that the above limit being zero is a consequence of (
85
). This completes the explanation that (
85
) implies (
83
). Hence, in what follows, we focus on the proof of (
85
). Writing everything using the Lebesgue measure and the estimates on
\Phi
, we must show that for every
x,y\in{\mathbf{B}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}
,

(86)

\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbf{B}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}}|z|^{n\alpha-d}|x-z|^{2-d}|y-z|^{2-d}\max(|x|,|z|)^{2a}\max(|y|,|z|)^{2a}dz\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\lesssim\Lambda(\varepsilon)|x-y|^{2-d}\max(|x|,|y|)^{2a},\end{split}

where
\Lambda(\varepsilon)
is some function which tends to
0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Letting
x=2\varepsilon x^{\prime}
,
y=2\varepsilon y^{\prime}
and
z=2\varepsilon w
, one has
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in{\mathbf{B}}_{1/2}^{*}
, and we see that the above integral is equal to

\varepsilon^{2-d+2a+\gamma}\int_{{\mathbf{B}}_{1/2}}|w|^{n\alpha-d}|x^{\prime}-w|^{2-d}|y^{\prime}-w|^{2-d}\max(|x^{\prime}|,|w|)^{2a}\max(|y^{\prime}|,|w|)^{2a}dw

where

\gamma=(n\alpha-d)+(2-d)+2a+d=(n\alpha-d)+2+(d-2-\alpha(n-2))=2\alpha>0

using the formula
2a=(d-2)-\alpha(n-2)
.
Thus, in order to prove (
86
), it is enough to prove that for
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in{\mathbf{B}}_{1/2}^{*}
,

\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbf{B}}_{1/2}}|w|^{n\alpha-d}|x^{\prime}-w|^{2-d}|y^{\prime}-w|^{2-d}\max(|x^{\prime}|,|w|)^{2a}\max(|y^{\prime}|,|w|)^{2a}dw\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\lesssim|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{2-d}\max(|x^{\prime}|,|y^{\prime}|)^{2a}\end{split}

But the right-hand side is bounded from below by

\max(|x^{\prime}|,|y^{\prime}|)^{2-d+2a}=\max(|x^{\prime}|,|y^{\prime}|)^{-\alpha(n-2)}\gtrsim 1.

Thus it is enough to prove that, uniformly in
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in{\mathbf{B}}_{1/2}
, we have

\int_{{\mathbf{B}}_{1/2}}|w|^{n\alpha-d}|x^{\prime}-w|^{2-d}|y^{\prime}-w|^{2-d}\max(|x^{\prime}|,|w|)^{2a}\max(|y^{\prime}|,|w|)^{2a}dw\lesssim 1,

which is what we now intend to do. For simplicity, we change back to
x,y,z
instead of
x^{\prime},y^{\prime},w
, and show:

(87)

\int_{{\mathbf{B}}_{1/2}}|z|^{n\alpha-d}|x-z|^{2-d}|y-z|^{2-d}\max(|x|,|z|)^{2a}\max(|y|,|z|)^{2a}dz\lesssim 1,\quad x,y\in{\mathbf{B}}_{1/2}

By symmetry, we may always assume that
|x|\leq|y|
. We split
{\mathbf{B}}_{1/2}
into the union of the three sets
\mathcal{A}=\{z:\;2|z|\geq|y|\}
,
\mathcal{B}=\{z:\;|y|\geq 2|z|\geq|x|\}
and
\mathcal{C}=\{z:\;2|z|\leq|x|\}
. On
\mathcal{A}
, we are left with

\int_{\mathcal{A}}|z|^{\alpha(4-n)+d-4}|x-z|^{2-d}|y-z|^{2-d}dz=\int_{\mathcal{A}}|z|^{-A}|x-z|^{-B}|y-z|^{-C}dz

with
A=-\alpha(4-n)+4-d
,
B=d-2
,
C=d-2
. Clearly, since
\alpha>0
,
n<4
and
d=3
, one has
A<4-d=1<d
, and moreover
\max(A,0)+\max(B,0)+\max(C,0)<4-d+d-2+d-2=d
. Hence, we can apply Lemma
8.3
and deduce that the above integral is bounded.

On the set
\mathcal{B}
, we need to estimate

|y|^{2a}\int_{\mathcal{B}}|z|^{-2(1-\alpha)}|x-z|^{2-d}|y-z|^{2-d}dz

Changing variable
Z=z/|y|
and letting
X=x/|y|
,
Y=y/|y|
, we obtain the upper bound

(88)

|y|^{\alpha(4-n)}\int_{1\geq|Z|\geq|X|}|Z|^{-2(1-\alpha)}|X-Z|^{2-d}|Y-Z|^{2-d}dZ

If
|X|\leq 1/2
, since
|Y|=1
, (
88
) is bounded above by a constant multiple of

|y|^{\alpha(4-n)}\left(\int_{3/4\geq|Z|\geq|X|}|Z|^{-2(1-\alpha)}|X-Z|^{2-d}dZ+\int_{1\geq|Z|\geq 3/4}|Y-Z|^{2-d}dZ\right),

which is bounded thanks to Giraud’s lemma (see for instance Hebey
[
13
, Lemma 7.5]
), while if
|X|>1/2
, (
88
) is bounded by a constant multiple of

|y|^{\alpha(4-n)}\int_{1\geq|Z|\geq 1/2}|X-Z|^{2-d}|Y-Z|^{2-d}dZ,

which is again bounded thanks to Giraud’s lemma.

In order to conclude this step, we deal with the region
\mathcal{C}
. There, we have to estimate

|x|^{2a}|y|^{2a}\int_{\mathcal{C}}|z|^{n\alpha-d}|x-z|^{2-d}|y-z|^{2-d}dz

Since
|y|\geq|x|\geq 2|z|
in
\mathcal{C}
, we obtain the bound

|x|^{2a+2-d}|y|^{2a+2-d}\int_{|x|\geq 2|z|}|z|^{n\alpha-d}dz\lesssim|y|^{\alpha(4-n)}\lesssim 1

This concludes the proof of (
87
), hence of (
84
). It remains to be shown that
G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}
has the required estimate. Arguing as in Step 2 of regime (
ii
), and using that the minimal growth for positive
L_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}
-harmonic function at
0
is
\simeq 1
(by Lemma
5.10
), we obtain:

G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}\lesssim\Phi(x,y)+1,\quad x,y\in{\mathbf{B}}_{\varepsilon},\,x\neq y.

But

\Phi(x,y)\geq\max(|x|,|y|)^{2-d+2a}=\max(|x|,|y|)^{-\alpha(n-2)}\gtrsim 1,\quad x,y\in{\mathbf{B}}_{\varepsilon}^{*},\,x\neq y,

so we can absorb the constant in the right-hand side in
\Phi(x,y)
and get

G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(x,y)\lesssim\Phi(x,y),\quad x,y\in{\mathbf{B}}_{\varepsilon}^{*},\,x\neq y.

The proof of Proposition
5.12
is complete.∎

5.4.4.
Regime (iv)

In this regime, we assume that

(89)

\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\leq|x|\leq 1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\leq|y|\leq 1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,,\text{ with }x\neq y\,,

and prove

Proposition 5.13
.

We have

G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(x,y)\lesssim\Phi(x,y)

uniformly in
x,y
such that (
89
) holds.

Proof of Proposition
5.13
.

To see this, consider the Green’s function
G_{\varepsilon}
of
L_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}
on the annulus
A_{\varepsilon}={\mathbf{B}}_{1-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}}\setminus{\mathbf{B}}_{\frac{\varepsilon}{4}}
with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition. Since
L_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}
is a smooth elliptic operator on

, we may apply more classical Green’s function estimates (see for instance Robert
[
21
]
) to deduce that

0\leq G_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\lesssim|x-y|^{2-d}\lesssim\Phi(x,y)\quad\text{for $x,y\in A_{\varepsilon}$, $x\neq y$.}

It remains to bound
\psi=G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}-G_{\varepsilon}
at points
x\neq y
satisfying (
89
). Fix
y
such that
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\leq|y|\leq 1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
. Observe that for
|x|=\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
,

\psi(x,y)=G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(x,y)\lesssim\Phi(x,y)\lesssim 1
thanks to Proposition
5.8
and the fact
|x-y|\geq\varepsilon/4\gtrsim 1
. Similarly, for

\psi(x,y)=G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(x,y)\lesssim\Phi(x,y)\lesssim 1
. Thus,

is bounded on the boundary of

. Since

is bounded in

, whence bounded by a constant multiple of

5.5.
Extending the Green functions and identities up to zero

The goal of this subsection is twofold: first showing that
\Phi(x,y)
and
G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(x,y)
can be extended continuously at
x
or
y=0
still for
x\neq y
, secondly extending the Green identities (

Φ

) and (

G

H
,
λ

) to test functions which do not necessarily vanish at
0
. Concerning the latter, let us stress that we do not make here any “low dimensional” assumption as we did in Theorems
1.2
or
1.5
: indeed, keeping our overall relations on the parameters, we have no restriction on our dimensional parameters
n\in[d,+\infty)
, while our overall assumption
\alpha>0
is crucially used at several places. Let us introduce the notation for the diagonal:

\mathscr{D}:=\{(x,y)\in{\mathbf{R}}^{d}\,;\,x=y\}\,.

Concerning the extension at
0
, we start with the case of
\Phi
and
G_{\mathbf{H},0}
:

Proposition 5.14
.

The Green functions
\Phi
and
G_{\mathbf{H},0}
extend continuously to
{\mathbf{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbf{R}}^{d}\setminus\mathscr{D}
and
{\mathbf{B}}\times{\mathbf{B}}\setminus\mathscr{D}
respectively.

Proof of Proposition
5.14
.

By symmetry and local uniform continuity in
{\mathbf{B}}
, it is enough to show that,
y
being fixed in
{\mathbf{B}}^{*}
, both
\Phi(x,y)
and
G_{\mathbf{H},0}(x,y)
have a limit as
x\to 0
. Indeed, let
G\in\{\Phi,G_{\mathbf{H},0}\}
and
(x_{n},y_{n})\to(0,y)\neq(0,0)
as
n\to+\infty
. Since

|G(x_{n},y_{n})-G(0,y)|\leq|G(x_{n},y_{n})-G(x_{n},y)|+|G(x_{n},y)-G(0,y)|

and since each function
G_{n}:y\mapsto G(x_{n},y)
is
L
-harmonic for an elliptic operator
L
with smoothly bounded coefficients in a neighborhood of
y\neq 0
, the sequence
(G_{n})
is equicontinuous and so we are left indeed with proving the convergence of the sequence
(G(x_{n},y))
.
Let us thus fix
y\in{\mathbf{B}}^{*}
, and let
0<\varepsilon<|y|
. Let us denote
u(x)=\Phi(x,y)
and
v(x)=G_{\mathbf{H},0}(x,y)
. By definition, these are two
L_{\mathbf{E}}
-harmonic functions in
{\mathbf{B}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}
, and moreover, according to Propositions
5.1
and
5.2
, both
u
and
v
are bounded on
{\mathbf{B}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}
. In the ball
{\mathbf{B}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}
, let us use the spherical coordinates
(\rho,\omega)
,
\rho=r^{\alpha}
, in which the operator
L_{\mathbf{E}}
as the expression (
14
).

In what follows, we denote by
w
one of the two functions
u
or
v
(the argument will be identical in both cases). We thus have to prove that

\lim_{x\to 0}w(x)

exists. Consider
w
as a function of
(\rho,\omega)
, and let
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbf{N}}}
be a Hilbert basis of eigenfunctions for the Laplacian on the sphere
S^{d-1}
(thus,
-\Delta\varphi_{k}=\lambda_{k}\varphi_{k}
, where
0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\lambda_{k}\leq\cdots
is the spectrum of
-\Delta_{S^{d-1}}
, counting multiplicities). Writing
w(\rho,\cdot)
in this basis, we obtain the following decomposition in spherical harmonics:

w(\rho,\omega)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}(\rho)\varphi_{k}(\omega),\quad 0<\rho<\varepsilon^{1/\alpha},\,\omega\in S^{d-1},

where for each
n\in{\mathbf{N}}
, the function
a_{n}
solves the following ODE:

(90)

z^{\prime\prime}+\frac{n-1}{t}z^{\prime}-\frac{\mu_{k}}{t^{2}}z=0,\quad\mu_{k}=\frac{\lambda_{k}}{\alpha^{2}}.

Since
w
is bounded,
\frac{1}{|S^{d-1}|}\int_{S^{d-1}}w^{2}\,d\sigma
is bounded, and using the orthogonality of the Hilbert basis
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbf{N}}}
, we get that each function
a_{k}
is necessarily bounded. Equation (
90
) admits for general solution

C_{1}\rho^{\gamma_{-}(\mu_{k})}+C_{2}\rho^{\gamma_{+}(\mu_{k})},

where

\gamma_{\pm}(s)=-\frac{n-2}{2}\pm\sqrt{\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)^{2}+s}.

Since
n>2
, if the solution is bounded as
\rho\to 0
then necessarily
C_{1}=0
. Thus,

a_{k}(\rho)=c_{k}\rho^{\gamma_{+}(\mu_{k})},\quad c_{k}\in{\mathbf{R}},

where
c_{k}
is a numerical constant such that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}^{2}<\infty
. Elliptic regularity for
L_{\mathbf{E}}
away from the origin implies that actually, the sequence
(c_{k})_{k\in{\mathbf{N}}}
has fast decay, and in particular it is summable. Since

\lim_{\rho\to 0}a_{k}(\rho)=0,\quad k\geq 1,

we get

\lim_{\rho\to 0}w(\rho,\omega)=c_{0}.

This shows that
w
extends by continuity at the origin, and concludes the proof. ∎

We continue with the same result for
G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}
:

Proposition 5.15
.

The Green function
G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}
has a continuous extension to
{\mathbf{B}}\times{\mathbf{B}}\setminus\mathscr{D}
.

Proof of Proposition
5.15
.

We indicate what has to be modified in the proof of Proposition
5.14
so that it applies to the present situation where
\lambda
might be nonzero. Denote as before
w(x)=G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(x,y)
, where
y
is fixed. Write the expansion of
w
in spherical harmonics:

w(\rho,\omega)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}(\rho)\varphi_{k}(\omega),\quad 0<\rho<\varepsilon^{1/\alpha},\,\omega\in S^{d-1},

The difference is now the ODE satisfied by
a_{k}
. Instead of (
90
), we find that
a_{k}
is a solution of

z^{\prime\prime}+\frac{n-1}{t}z^{\prime}-\left(\frac{\mu_{k}}{t^{2}}+\frac{\lambda}{(1-t^{2})^{2}}\right)z=0,\quad\mu_{k}=\frac{\lambda_{k}}{\alpha^{2}}.

The theory of linear ODEs with regular singular points (see Fuchs theorem in
[
26

) implies that a general solution to equation (

) writes for
0<t<\varepsilon
,

C_{1}u_{k,1}(t)+C_{2}u_{k,2}(t),

with
C_{1},C_{2}\in{\mathbf{R}}

u_{k,1}(t)\sim_{t\to 0}t^{\gamma_{-}(\mu_{k})},\quad u_{k,2}(t)\sim_{t\to 0}t^{\gamma_{+}(\mu_{k})},

where

is as in the proof of Proposition
5.14
. The rest of the proof is nearly identical to that of Proposition
5.14
, hence is skipped. ∎

We need now the following technical lemma:

Lemma 5.16
.

Given
r\in(0,+\infty)
, there exists
p>1
such that the family of functions
\left(\Phi(\cdot,y)\right)_{0<|y|\leq r}
is bounded in

. Similarly, given
r\in(0,1)
, there exists
p>1
such that the family
\left(G_{\mathbf{H},\lambda}(\cdot,y)\right)_{0<|y|\leq r}
is bounded in

.

It will be used later combined with the following classical and easy result:

Lemma 5.17
.

Let
\mu
be a nonnegative finite measure on
\Omega
a Borel subset of
{\mathbf{R}}^{d}
. Let
f
be a function and
(f_{k})_{k}
be a sequence of measurable functions on
\Omega
such that
f_{k}\to f

\mu
-almost everywhere. If there exists
p>1
such that
(f_{k})_{k}
is bounded in

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
0\leq A<1
,

\int_{\{\delta(z)\leq\frac{1}{2}\delta(y)\}\cap{\mathbf{B}}_{1}}\delta(z)^{-A}|z-y|^{-B}\,dz\lesssim\delta(y)^{-A-B+d}.

Proof.

We make the change of variables

\{\delta(z)\leq\frac{1}{2}\delta(y)\}\cap{\mathbf{B}}_{1}
into

from
\omega
to the point

of

is fixed and chosen small enough (see later), that is, identifying a point

(-L^{\Omega_{n}}+s)^{-1}f
is extended by
0

\Omega_{m}\setminus\Omega_{n}

x\neq y
in
\Omega

(R_{s}^{\Omega_{n}}(x,y))_{n\geq n_{0}}
is non-decreasing, thus has a positive limit which we call

. By the spectral theorem, since the spectrum of
-L^{\Omega_{n}}+s
is included in
[s,+\infty)

||(-L^{\Omega_{n}}+s)^{-1}||_{L^{2}(\Omega_{n})\to L^{2}(\Omega_{n})}\leq\frac{1}{s}<+\infty.

Thus, if

and

are two

functions, by monotone convergence

Thus
R_{s}
is a kernel which gives rise to an operator bounded on
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Taking
f
and
g
to be characteristic functions of compact sets, this implies that
R_{s}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega\times\Omega)
. Now, dominated convergence shows that for
f\in L^{2}
,
(-L^{\Omega_{n}}+s)^{-1}f=R_{s}^{\Omega_{n}}f
converges weakly in

to
R_{s}f
. Moreover, since by integration by parts,

Q(R_{s}^{\Omega_{n}}f)=\int_{\Omega_{n}}\left((-L)(-L^{\Omega_{n}}+s)^{-1}f\right)(x)\left(R_{s}^{\Omega_{n}}f(x)\right)\,d\mu(x)

(note that
(-L^{\Omega_{n}}+s)^{-1}f
has compact support in
\Omega_{n}
, and lies in fact in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega_{n})
, so the integral defining
Q(R_{s}^{\Omega_{n}}f)
, which a priori is over the whole
\Omega
, is indeed in fact only over
\Omega_{n}
). The operator
(-L)(-L^{\Omega_{n}}+s)^{-1}
having norm bounded by
1
as an operator acting on
L^{2}(\Omega_{n})
, we obtain that for
f\in L^{2}
,
(R_{s}^{\Omega_{n}}f)_{n\in{\mathbf{N}}}
is a bounded sequence in the Hilbert space
(\mathrm{Dom}(Q),||\cdot||)
; one can therefore assume that it has a weakly converging sequence, and since
\mathrm{Dom}(Q)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
is locally compact by Rellich’s theorem, we can conclude that the weak limit has to be equal to
R_{s}f
, which thus belongs to
\mathrm{Dom}(Q)
. Let us define the operator
(-L^{\Omega}+s)^{-1}
by

(-L^{\Omega}+s)^{-1}f:=R_{s}f

According to the above the norm estimate, we get

||R_{s}||_{L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{s}<+\infty,

Since
R_{s}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega\times\Omega)
, this allows one to pass to the limit
n\to\infty
in the equation
(-L+s)R_{s}^{\Omega_{n}}=\delta_{\{x=y\}}
in the distribution sense in
\Omega_{n}\times\Omega_{n}
, and get that
R_{s}
is a fundamental solution of
(-L+s)
in
\Omega\times\Omega
, more precisely: for every functions
\varphi,\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}R_{s}(x,y)((-L+s)\varphi)(y)\psi(x)\,d\mu(x)d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\iint_{\Omega_{n}\times\Omega_{n}}R_{s}^{\Omega_{n}}x,y)

\displaystyle\times((-L+s)\varphi)(x)\psi(y)\,d\mu(x)d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\psi(x)\,d\mu(x).

(using (
126
) to get the last line). Hence
R_{s}
is smooth (and finite) outside the diagonal. Analysis of the singularities of such fundamental solutions gives that the singularity of
R_{s}(x,y)
along the diagonal

is in fact locally integrable in one variable
x
or in
y
only (it is locally
\asymp|x-y|^{2-d}
). Note also that if
f\in L^{2}
, then

solves the equation
(-L+s)g=f
. However, since
\mathrm{ker}(Q)=\{0\}
, the function

(where
(-L+s)^{-1}
is defined by functional calculus) is the only solution of this equation lying in
\mathrm{Dom}(Q)
. Hence,
R_{s}f=(-L+s)^{-1}f
for
f\in L^{2}
, i.e.
(-L^{\Omega}+s)^{-1}=(-L+s)^{-1}
and
R_{s}
is a common kernel.

Step 2: sending

For any fixed

A consequence of Step 4 will be that this limit is actually finite for all

. A quick proof is as follows: by the inequalities of Step 1, one has for every

,

with compact support,

where

and

is the Green kernel of

. Taking the limit as

and then

The RHS is finite since
-L
is subcritical by assumption. Taking for

the characteristic function of a compact set

, we get that

R\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega\times\Omega)
. In particular,

is finite a.e.
(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega

Let

be non-negative. The fact that the kernels

are non-negative and the monotone convergence theorem imply that

\lim_{s\to 0}R_{s}f(x)=Rf(x)\quad\mu-\mathrm{a.e.}\,x\in\Omega.

For an arbitrary

for which

and using the above equation both for

and for

\lim_{s\to 0}R_{s}f(x)=Rf(x)\quad\mu-\mathrm{a.e.}\,x\in\Omega.

(the RHS being finite almost everywhere thanks to the assumption that

be such that

\lim_{s\to 0}||R_{s}f-Rf||_{2}=0.

Recall the isometry

\lim_{s\to 0}||(-L+s)^{-1}g-(-L)^{-1}g||_{2}=0.

Step 4:
R
is equal to

.

Recall that by construction,

is given by the following formula:

Since the quantity
R_{s}^{\Omega_{n}}(x,y)
is non-decreasing in

and non-increasing in

, we can exchange the order of the limits and get

R(x,y)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\lim_{s\to 0}R_{s}^{\Omega_{n}}(x,y)\right),\quad x\neq y.

But

(the last equality is in fact the classical definition of the positive minimal Green function

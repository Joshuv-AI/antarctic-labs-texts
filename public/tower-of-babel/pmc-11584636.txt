Enhancing strategic decision-making in differential games through bifurcation prediction
Authors: Jesús García Pérez, Bogdan Epureanu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Qualitative changes can occur in the dynamics of nonlinear systems even for small parameter variations. Such changes are manifestations of bifurcation in dynamical systems. In the context of differential game theory, bifurcations offer insights into the underlying mechanisms driving strategic interactions and identify transitions between different types of behavior. Such critical transitions are tipping points that can dramatically change the outcomes of the game. This work explores the possibility of predicting such qualitative shifts, including supercritical Hopf bifurcations, before they occur using a data-driven forecasting technique. This concept is demonstrated for an attacker–defender game in a limited resource scenario and for an active cybersecurity defense game. The time histories of the system dynamics as it approaches a bifurcation allow one player to detect the existence of bifurcations. This capability provides that player insights into the dynamics of the game and potential defense mechanisms in resource-constrained scenarios.

Introduction

Game theory and dynamical models are widely used to analyze strategic interactions and complex multi-agent systems. Game theory provides a mathematical foundation for studying decision-making in scenarios where outcomes depend on the actions of all participants1. These concepts have numerous real-world applications, including economics2, traffic flow3, climate4, population dynamics5, and conflict resolution6. In contrast, dynamical models focus on understanding how variables evolve over time and the interactions among different components within a system. The combination of game theory and dynamical models has provided valuable insights into the dynamics and behavior of strategic interactions. For example, game-based approaches have been applied to model swarm behavior in community interactions7. Additionally, they have been utilized to optimize multi-agent actions in the planning process involving human drivers and autonomous vehicles8. In the context of attacker–defender games, the strategies employed by defenders are of utmost importance. The use of multi-agent systems has enabled the design of effective defense strategies to capture attackers9.

An important phenomenon in differential games is bifurcations1. These critical points cause abrupt qualitative changes in a system as variables or parameters are adjusted. Bifurcations lead to various outcomes such as new equilibrium states, complex dynamics, or phase transitions10. In game theory, they offer valuable insights into strategic interactions, revealing how small changes in player strategies can lead to significant shifts in equilibrium outcomes or in the stability of cooperative behavior. Bifurcations identify critical transitions, distinguishing between behaviors like cooperation and defection in different game-theoretic settings. Some studies explore this subject, including works like the classic hawk and dove game11,12, the historical Salami battle explanation with a Hopf bifurcation13, applications to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2 \times 2$$\end{document}2×2 games2, and a game-based model of cybersecurity attacks on computers showing rich phenomena such as Hopf bifurcations14. Predicting these bifurcations could be decisive regarding the outcomes of the game when multiple equilibria and different solutions are involved. However, predicting bifurcation diagrams is a complicated task due to the nonlinear nature of the system and the unavailability of a well calibrated/identified model.

In this context, an innovative technique has been developed to forecast bifurcation diagrams in various domains15. This data-driven approach requires several transients prior to the bifurcation, enabling the prediction of tipping points16. It has successfully been applied to diverse systems, including aeroelastic systems17, evolution of populations18,19, traffic jams20, as well as optimization21. While demonstrating its effectiveness in models, the forecasting technique has also proven its applicability to real-world systems19,22. To the knowledge of the authors, no prior research has been presented concerning the prediction of bifurcations in differential games. Specifically, there is a lack of approaches aimed at forecasting bifurcations in the strategies made by players. This represents an unexplored area of study with significant potential because anticipating bifurcations in game theory would yield valuable insights into the dynamics of strategic decision-making and the pursuit of competitive advantages.

Here, a novel approach for capturing critical transitions and tipping points in differential games is presented, along with its application to an attacker–defender game and to an active cybersecurity game. Our aim is to provide players with the ability to predict game outcomes and make informed decisions to adapt their strategies accordingly. In “Forecasting method” section, we introduce our forecasting technique15 that uses historical system response data to forecast critical transitions and tipping points. We also introduce an extension of the method to capture bifurcations involving non-zero equilibria. In “Results” section, we present the outcomes of the forecasting approach for a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2\times 2$$\end{document}2×2 attacker–defender game scenario and its application in the domain of cybersecurity. In the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2\times 2$$\end{document}2×2 game, the forecasted bifurcation diagrams are compared with the actual game outcomes, assessing the accuracy and effectiveness of our approach. Results demonstrate the capability of our forecasting technique to capture significant changes in equilibria and to identify potential tipping points. Furthermore, we showcase the application of our forecasting approach in the context of cybersecurity games. These examples demonstrate the relevance of predicting critical transitions in attacker–defender scenarios. By forecasting and anticipating such transitions, players can proactively adjust their defensive strategies and effectively mitigate potential cyber threats. This example highlights the benefits of our novel approach in capturing critical transitions in attacker–defender games.

Results

In this section, the forecasting method is applied to predict the bifurcation diagrams of attacker–defender games as explained in “Forecasting method” section, by only using measurements of time histories from the game. Two examples are considered to demonstrate the capability of the approach. First, a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2\times 2$$\end{document}2×2 attacker–defender game using Q-learning dynamics is analyzed in “Attacker–defender Q-learning dynamics” section. Then, a cybersecurity attacker–defender game on a large network is presented in “Active cyber defense” section.

Attacker–defender Q-learning dynamics

In this section, the dynamics of an attacker–defender game are studied, where a limited resource is at stake. The attacker seeks to acquire it while the defender aims to prevent the attacker from doing so. This game involves two players or two large homogeneous populations, each possessing limited resources. The strategies adopted by the players are influenced by the availability of these resources, and the gameplay unfolds iteratively over multiple rounds.

The evolution of strategies is modeled by Q-learning dynamics for both the attacker and defender23. Q-learning is a widely used reinforcement learning algorithm that allows agents to learn optimal strategies through trial and error. By utilizing Q-learning, the players can keep track of the past performance of their actions, and therefore they can adapt their strategies over time based on the outcomes of previous interactions24.

The game considers two possible actions for the players: “fight” for or “abandon” the resource. The choice between these actions depends on the players’ resources and their strategic considerations.

Replicator dynamics, a concept from evolutionary game theory, models the change in proportions of different strategies in a population over time. By incorporating the rationality term, we introduce a parameter that can induce changes in the equilibria and can potentially lead to bifurcations. By tracking the evolution of strategies over multiple instances of the game, one can observe the changes in the players’ behaviors and the emergence of new equilibria. The rationality (or exploration rate) plays a crucial role in driving these bifurcations and changes from stable to unstable equilibria. By quantifying the effects of rationality-driven bifurcations, we gain a better understanding of the dynamics of the game and its implications for the players’ strategies. Furthermore, keeping track of these evolutionary changes allows one player to predict and forecast potential bifurcations in the game by using the proposed approach. This predictive capability provides valuable insights into the stability and resilience of strategies employed by the attacker and defender. Notably, players can adapt their strategies accordingly and potentially gain an advantage over their opponents by anticipating potential changes in the equilibrium points.

A model is not needed to apply the forecasting method. However, data is needed in the form of recoveries. Conclusions

This study introduces an innovative forecasting technique and demonstrates its successful application to a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2 \times 2$$\end{document}2×2 attacker–defender game with Q-learning dynamics and to a cybersecurity attacker–defender system.

By predicting the dynamics of an attacker–defender game in a limited resource scenario, the results demonstrate how Q-learning dynamics, replicator dynamics, and rationality-driven bifurcations can be utilized to forecast and predict the evolution of strategies. The ability to forecast potential bifurcations and changes in equilibrium points empowers players to make informed decisions and adapt their strategies accordingly. The application of forecasting techniques enhances the understanding of game dynamics and contributes to the development of more effective defense mechanisms in resource-constrained scenarios.

The application to a cybersecurity model highlights the applicability of the forecasting technique, enabling the identification of critical transitions when there are only non-zero equilibria. The ability to forecast potential bifurcations and changes in equilibria plays a crucial role in optimizing defense strategies and mitigating risks associated with the complexities introduced by bifurcations.

This study highlights the significance of bifurcations as important indicators of critical transitions in differential games. The use of the forecasting technique allows for the prediction of equilibria by only using data from past histories or multiple instances of the game. The identification of tipping points before the onset of instability enhances our understanding of game dynamics, facilitating the development of proactive strategies.

Future work will focus on advancing this research by validating the forecasting technique with real-world data and assessing its effectiveness in scenarios involving noisy or incomplete data. These efforts aim to enhance the practical relevance and broader applicability of our findings.

Methods for producing a National Infection Prevention and Control Manual in Scotland: 2025 update with a new considered judgement form
Authors: Lorna Gordon, Emma Hooker, Gemma Waugh, Sarah Thirlwell, Sofia French
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryIntroductionThe National Infection Prevention and Control Manual (NIPCM) is an online evidence-based resource developed by Antimicrobial Resistance and Healthcare Associated Infection (ARHAI) Scotland (NHS Scotland). Following a 2022 evaluation and informed by lessons from the COVID-19 pandemic, the NIPCM guidance development process was reviewed to improve transparency and support auditable decision-making.MethodsThe guidance development methodology, inclusive of the recommendation grading system, was assessed as part of an annual review process. Extant infection prevention and control (IPC) or public health guidance development frameworks of relevance were identified and appraised for potential integration into a new considered judgement form (CJF), which was piloted in practice with working groups.ResultsElements from the SIGN 50 and HICPAC methodologies were incorporated into ARHAI Scotland's new CJF with notable addition of a dedicated ‘Expert opinion’ section. The recommendation grading system was altered from a lettered system (A–C) to use of the terms ‘recommendation’ and ‘good practice point’.ConclusionBy clearly documenting decision making, context and expert opinion, the new CJF improves transparency and supports guidance users with interpretation and implementation. The new grading system more adequately conveys the strength of supportive evidence and practical intent of recommendations.

Introduction

The National Infection Prevention and Control Manual (NIPCM) is an online, evidence-based resource developed and updated in real-time by Antimicrobial Resistance and Healthcare Associated Infection (ARHAI) Scotland (NHS Scotland) [1] to support infection prevention and control (IPC) best practice across health and care settings in Scotland. It features chapters on standard and transmission-based precautions, incidents, outbreaks and data exceedances as well as a multitude of IPC resources. A chapter on infection control in the built environment was added in July 2024.

First published in 2012, the NIPCM is maintained by ARHAI Scotland. The NIPCM has been adopted or used to inform IPC manuals in Public Health Wales (2018) [2], NHS England (2022) and the Public Health Agency Northern Ireland (2024) [3,4]. Further detail of the Scottish NIPCM's conception, governance, and editorial independence can be found in a 2020 ‘Infection Prevention in Practice’ publication [5].

The NIPCM guidance development methodology is aligned with that of the Scottish Intercollegiate Guidelines Network (SIGN). At the time of writing there are 48 published SIGN guidelines including ‘SIGN50: a guideline developer's handbook’ [6], last updated in March 2025 and accredited by the National Institute for Clinical Excellence (NICE). SIGN also provide critical appraisal tools for primary research studies and recommend the AGREE II tool for appraising guidance [7].

Following a 2022 evaluation and informed by lessons from the COVID-19 pandemic, the NIPCM guidance development process was reviewed by ARHAI Scotland to improve transparency and support auditable decision-making.

Before the 2022 evaluation, a ‘considered judgement form’ (CJF) was produced for each research question which briefly summarised evidence quality and some key considerations that informed recommendations. However, this did not adequately capture stakeholder expert opinion or clearly document the evidence to decision process.

The need was therefore identified for a more formal, structured and transparent recommendation development process that formally records expert opinion and key decisions made. This process is sometimes referred to as the ‘evidence to decision’ stage of guidance development. Evidence to decision frameworks facilitate presentation of how evidence quantity and quality, alongside contextual factors, have influenced recommendations.

This short report describes ARHAI Scotland's review and update of its NIPCM guidance development process which included development of a novel CJF tailored to IPC guidance development.

Methods

IPC evidence differs from traditional medical evidence as it is largely comprised of observational studies such as outbreak reports with bundled interventions. IPC guidance is therefore often heavily supplemented by expert opinion as objective scientific evidence is not available. For this reason, its development requires a tailored CJF to support decision-making. In 2022, a systematic search for extant IPC and public health guidance development frameworks identified two relevant frameworks, both of which were published in 2019 and based on GRADE methodology: the SIGN50 CJF and the ‘Justification for Choice of Recommendation Strength’ system which was published by the USA Healthcare Infection Control Practices Advisory Committee (HICPAC) [6,8,9].

The annual evaluation of the methodology also prompted revision of the recommendation grading system. The previous Mandatory and Category A–C recommendation grading approach (see Table II) was deemed to be poorly suited to IPC, where high-quality experimental evidence from randomised controlled trials is often unavailable. Use of this approach resulted in most NIPCM recommendations being graded as Category C. Lettered categories did not adequately convey the strength of supportive evidence or practical intent of recommendations. This contrasts with the new grading system which utilises the terms ‘recommendation’ or ‘good practice point’. Results

During piloting of the CJF, working group members initially reported feeling overwhelmed by the volume and length of documents requiring review and comment. Positive feedback was received regarding the detailed content and transparency provided by the expert opinion section of the CJF, which was reported to be beneficial in supporting stakeholder understanding and implementation of guidance in practice.

The updated NIPCM guidance development methodology, including the new CJF, was ratified by the ARHAI Scotland IPC Oversight and Advisory group following a consultation and published online in March 2025 [10]. The CJFs are now published in the NIPCM alongside the literature reviews. Discussion

The new CJF improves transparency by clearly documenting decision making, context and expert opinion, thus supporting guidance users with interpretation and implementation. It also provides a structured framework to guide working group discussions ensuring that all necessary aspects are thoroughly considered, and highlights gaps in the evidence base where further research is needed. ARHAI Scotland are increasingly utilising multi-disciplinary working groups and consultations with external subject matter experts to support CJF development, particularly for complex guidance.

Whilst introduction of the new CJF has had a positive impact in terms of increasing transparency and supporting implementation, it does require additional time and staff resource to complete. This may delay guidance publication and put additional pressure on working group members. ARHAI Scotland have tackled this issue with production of a consultation guide as well as live, collective completion of the CJFs during multi-disciplinary short life working group meetings, a process that was ratified in March 2026.

Overall, the new CJF represents a significant change in how ARHAI Scotland presents evidence and communicates guidance development. Ongoing assessment of the effectiveness and impact of this new approach in 2026, via stakeholder evaluation and feedback, will inform further refinements. Whilst a ‘before and after’ comparative data assessment approach is not feasible, surveys and focus group sessions with working group members and stakeholders will provide valuable qualitative feedback. Assessment will include determination of the impact of the CJFs on adaptation of the NIPCM by the other UK nations. In line with a continued dedication to improve service delivery and transparency, ARHAI Scotland are in the process of developing a streamlined CJF to support rapid guidance production in emergent situations.

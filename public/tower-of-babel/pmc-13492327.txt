Introduction

Both right ventricular (RV) and left ventricular leads have been reported to induce lead-mediated ventricular tachycardia (VT). Involvement of the tip electrode region in the reentry circuit represents a challenging therapeutic target and has been described as a rare or frequently under-recognized indication for catheter ablation (CA) and/or transvenous lead extraction (TLE).1, 2, 3 We present the first case of an innovative hybrid ablation approach, termed LEAD2ER, combining RV lead–mediated radiofrequency (RF) energy delivery followed by TLE for the treatment of VT.

Case report

A 54-year-old man with chronic, postinfarction heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (30%) and a dual-chamber implantable cardioverter-defibrillator was admitted for incessant VT at 140 beats per minute. Urgent coronary angiography showed patent grafts and no need for further revascularization. Urgent electrophysiological (EP) study with 3-dimensional electroanatomic mapping revealed an RV scar around the tip of the RV lead, measuring 1–1.5 cm. Optimal entrainment parameters were found adjacent to the tip of the RV lead. Moreover, during VT, entrainment from the implantable cardioverter-defibrillator RV lead tip confirmed the optimal ablation site, with a postpacing interval equal to the tachycardia cycle length and a paced QRS matching the VT QRS morphology (12/12). CA performed in close proximity to the tip of the RV lead was initially successful, with VT terminating for more than 24 hours (Figure 1A–1C). Recurrence of the same VT at a slower rate (120 beats per minute), which might reflect a nondurable lesion or transient edema of the isthmus or exit zone after the initial CA, led the EP heart team to consider a novel therapeutic approach for RV lead–mediated VT. After shared decision making and urgent transfer, the patient was admitted for a hybrid approach including generator and lead replacement, with TLE preceded by RF energy delivery via the RV lead. The LEAD2ER approach was performed by a hybrid team with monitoring of lead stability, ventricular lead position, and steam pop risk (impedance rise). Based on all parameters, the RV lead was located in the perfect exit site of the VT circuit with a stimulus-to-QRS interval of <80 ms (with 12/12 electrocardiogram). After preparation and generator removal, the proximal end of the RV lead was connected to the tip of the ablation catheter, and RF energy (10–20 W) was delivered 3 times for 30 seconds to achieve complete exit block. After each energy delivery, lead integrity was assessed. During RF energy delivery, VT terminated, and the previously incessant VT was no longer observed or inducible. Exit block from the RV lead was confirmed at maximum output, and the lead was subsequently extracted. A new RV lead was implanted in the RV outflow tract. The stepwise course of the procedure is presented in Figure 1. The patient was discharged after 3 days without pericardial effusion or VT recurrence. A 3-year follow-up was uneventful. Negative programmed ventricular stimulation and an uneventful 3-year follow-up (no recurrence of sustained or incessant slow or rapid VT) further supported procedural success. Therefore, no additional reassurance RF applications were performed after lead extraction to minimize the risk of further complications.

Discussion

This case report demonstrates the successful implementation of a novel hybrid approach—combining ablation and TLE—termed “LEAD2ER” for RV lead–mediated VT.

Various individualized noninvasive and invasive strategies can be applied for patients with RV lead–mediated VT, including device reprogramming, hybrid therapy, CA, needle catheter VT ablation, and TLE.2,4, 5, 6 However, inaccessible sites of the circuit, fibrosis surrounding the lead tip, and the risk of early or late RV lead failure and malfunction make this treatment challenging.4, 5, 6 Our hybrid approach, termed “LEAD2ER,” could be considered a potential option for patients with RV lead–mediated VT, possibly in the future by distal lead tip screwing into inaccessible sites of the VT circuit, either intramurally or deep within scar tissue.

RV lead entrainment requires detailed validation, including connection of the programmer and the lead to the EP system, use of low- and high-output pacing, imaging assessment of scar tissue characteristics, and differentiation of the optimal isthmus and exit sites to ensure efficacy and safety of the hybrid procedure.

Naturally, questions may arise regarding the biophysical safety of the lead–electrode. Available experimental data suggest that the lead is biophysically safe.3 Although evaluation of lead integration/isolation was planned after the procedure, access to a dedicated lead laboratory was not available during the coronavirus disease 2019 pandemic. Further studies are required to assess the potential values of the power selection process, energy delivery, and extraction safety.

Additional techniques using RF energy have been described for transseptal puncture or for left bundle branch area pacing implantation.7, 8, 9, 10 RF energy was delivered using a standard electrosurgical pencil connected to a transseptal Brockenbrough needle or guidewire to facilitate transseptal puncture. Recently, an animal model has confirmed that delivery of RF energy to the pacing lead tip facilitates deep septal penetration for left bundle branch area pacing. The LEAD2ER technique represents an additional hybrid option among available strategies for the management of VT and electrical storm, including endoepicardial ablation approaches, unipolar and bipolar RF CA, cryoablation (including deep cryoablation), pulsed field ablation, and bilateral cardiac sympathetic denervation2,6,11, 12, 13; however, at this stage, it should be considered an experimental therapy reserved for bailout clinical scenarios, with its efficacy and safety profile requiring further investigation. The use of de novo endocardial pacing leads after failed elective catheter-based RF ablation requires further ex vivo and in vivo studies, as well as comprehensive technological and clinical evaluation.

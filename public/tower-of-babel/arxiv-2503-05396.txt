On primes represented by a ​ X 2 + b ​ Y 3 aX^{2}+bY^{3}
Jori Merikoski

Abstract
. Let a , b > 0 a,b>0 be coprime integers. Assuming a conjecture on Hecke eigenvalues along binary cubic forms, we prove an asymptotic formula for the number of primes of the form a ​ x 2 + b ​ y 3 ax^{2}+by^{3} with x ≤ X 1 / 2 x\leq X^{1/2} and y ≤ X 1 / 3 y\leq X^{1/3} . The proof combines sieve methods with the theory of real quadratic fields/indefinite binary quadratic forms, the Weil bound for exponential sums, and spectral methods of GL(2) automorphic forms. We also discuss applications to elliptic curves.



1.
Introduction

The problem of counting prime numbers along thin polynomial sequences has been solved only for a very narrow class of polynomials. Results fall into one of two lineages, one starting from the Friedlander-Iwaniec primes of the form
X^{2}+Y^{4}

[
8
]
and one from the Heath-Brown primes of the form
X^{3}+2Y^{3}

[
18
]
. See
[
7
,
10
,
12
,
19
,
26
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
38
]
and
[
20
,
21
,
27
,
29
]
for their respective descendants. In particular, all known results about prime values of thin polynomials require that the polynomial factorizes in a number field gaining at least one linear variable, for example,
X^{2}+Y^{4}=(X+iY^{2})(X-iY^{2})
in
{\mathbb{Q}}(i)
and
X^{3}+2Y^{3}
is the norm of
X+Y\sqrt[3]{2}
in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[3]{2})
.

We introduce a new approach for counting the prime values of the polynomial
aX^{2}+bY^{3}
that has no obvious factorization. In the absence of a linear variable, we will leverage the quadratic variable. This is a much harder task and our main result is conditional on a hypothesis that we formulate now.

Let
d>0
be a fundamental discriminant and consider the real quadratic field
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})
(see Section
2
for a more detailed discussion). We let
\lambda_{\chi\xi^{\ell}}(n)=\sum_{N_{{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})}(\mathfrak{a})=n}\chi\xi^{\ell}(\mathfrak{a})
denote the Hecke eigenvalue associated to the Größencharakter
\chi\xi^{\ell}
, parametrized by
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and class group characters
\chi\in\widehat{{\mathfrak{C}}(d)}

[
22
,
23
]
. For
\chi\xi^{\ell}=1
it is given by the Dirichlet convolution
\lambda_{1}=1\ast(\tfrac{d}{\cdot})
where
(\tfrac{d}{n})
is the Jacobi symbol. For
T>1
we define the truncated approximation
\lambda_{1}^{\sharp}(n,T)=1\ast((\tfrac{d}{\cdot})\mathbf{1}_{[0,T]})(n)
and denote the error term by
\lambda_{1}^{\flat}(n,T)=\lambda_{1}(n)-\lambda_{1}^{\sharp}(n,T)
. Having no reason to suspect otherwise, for
\chi\xi^{\ell}\neq 1
we expect square-root cancellation along the values of binary cubic forms, and for
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
and
\varepsilon>0
we make the following hypothesis.

Conjecture
\mathrm{C}_{a}(\varepsilon)
.
Let
C(X,Y)=c_{1}X^{3}-c_{2}Y^{3}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X,Y]
for some
c_{1},c_{2}>0
. Then for
\chi\xi^{\ell}\neq 1
and
B_{1},B_{2}>1
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y_{1}\leq B_{1},\,y_{2}\leq B_{2}\\
C(y_{1},y_{2})\equiv 0\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{a})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\chi\xi^{\ell}}(|\tfrac{1}{a}C(y_{1},y_{2})|)

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}\max\{B_{1}^{2},B_{2}^{2},c_{1}^{2},c_{2}^{2},d,|\ell|^{2}\}^{\varepsilon}(B_{1}+B_{2}),

and for some
\eta>0
we have for any
T\in[(B_{1}+B_{2})^{1-\eta},B_{1}+B_{2}]

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y_{1}\leq B_{1},\,y_{2}\leq B_{2}\\
C(y_{1},y_{2})\equiv 0\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{a})\end{subarray}}\lambda^{\flat}_{1}(|\tfrac{1}{a}C(y_{1},y_{2})|,T)

\displaystyle\ll\max\{c_{1}^{2},c_{2}^{2},d,|\ell|^{2}\}^{\eta}(B_{1}^{2}+B_{2}^{2})^{1-\eta}.

For
B_{1}\approx B_{2}
taking
\varepsilon=o(1)
corresponds to square-root cancellation, and
\varepsilon=1/2
corresponds to no cancellation at all. For
\chi\xi^{\ell}=1
we require only a small power-saving. This conjecture serves as a placeholder, we only require such a bound on average over a large family of eigenvalues, see Conjecture
\mathrm{L}_{a,b}(\varepsilon)
in Remark
7.1
. Assuming that this conjecture holds for all
\varepsilon>0
, we can show an asymptotic formula for the number of primes of the form
aX^{2}+bY^{3}
. As usual,
\Lambda(n)
denotes the von Mangoldt function.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
a,b>0
be coprime integers. Assume that Conjecture
\mathrm{C}_{a}(\varepsilon)
holds for all
\varepsilon>0
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{x\leq X^{1/2}}\sum_{y\leq X^{1/3}}\Lambda(ax^{2}+by^{3})=(1+o(1))X^{5/6}.

For a fixed
\varepsilon>0
the error term
o(1)
is replaced by
O(\varepsilon)
(see Theorem
4.1
). We get a correct order lower bound for primes with the fixed value
\varepsilon=1/17
and a lower bound for products of exactly
k\geq 2
primes for any
\varepsilon<1/4
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
a,b>0
be coprime integers. Assuming that Conjecture
\mathrm{C}_{a}(1/17)
holds, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{x\leq X^{1/2}}\sum_{y\leq X^{1/3}}\Lambda(ax^{2}+by^{3})\geq(0.05+o(1))X^{5/6}.

Assuming that Conjecture
\mathrm{C}_{a}(\varepsilon)
holds for some
\varepsilon<1/4
, we have for any
k\geq 2

\displaystyle\sum_{x\leq X^{1/2}}\sum_{y\leq X^{1/3}}(\underbrace{\Lambda\ast\cdots\ast\Lambda}_{k\,\mathrm{times}})(ax^{2}+by^{3})\gg X^{5/6}(\log X)^{k-1}.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
may be adapted to show that, under the same hypothesis, the Möbius function
\mu(n)
has cancellation.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
a,b>0
be coprime integers. Assume that Conjecture
\mathrm{C}_{a}(\varepsilon)
holds for all
\varepsilon>0
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{x\leq X^{1/2}}\sum_{y\leq X^{1/3}}\mu(ax^{2}+by^{3})=o(X^{5/6}).

Unconditionally, for the Liouville function
\lambda
, Teräväinen
[
35
, Theorem 2.12]
has shown that
\lambda(ax^{2}+by^{3})
takes both values
\pm 1
infinitely often.

1.1.
Applications to elliptic curves

A major motivation for studying prime numbers of this form comes from elliptic curves

\displaystyle E_{A,B}:\quad y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B

whose discriminant

\displaystyle\Delta(E_{A,B})=-16(4A^{3}+27B^{2})

controls the places of bad reduction. Therefore, as a special case of Theorem
1.1
with
a=27,b=4
, assuming Conjecture
\mathrm{C}_{27}(\varepsilon)
for all
\varepsilon>0
, we get an asymptotic formula for the number of elliptic curves with exactly one place of bad reduction
p>2
.

It is not clear if the argument can be adapted to study the distribution of the root numbers of the elliptic curves
E_{A,B}
(see
[
39
, proof of Proposition 3.1]
for
4A^{3}+27B^{2}
square-free)

\displaystyle w(E_{A,B})=-\mu(4A^{3}+27B^{2})(\tfrac{6B}{4A^{3}+27B^{2}})w_{2}(E_{A,B}).

The twist by the Jacobi symbol appears surprisingly difficult to accommodate in our approach. Helfgott
[
24
, Corollary 5.2]
has shown that for the family
y^{2}=x(x+A)(x+B)
the root numbers are evenly distributed, by considering essentially
(\tfrac{B}{A})\mu(AB(A-B))
, but for his arguments the Jacobi symbol causes only minor issues.

It should however be possible to consider the root numbers of the quadratic twist family

\displaystyle E^{(B)}_{A,B}:\quad By^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B

for
B
and
4A^{3}+27B^{2}
square-free with
\gcd(B,\Delta(E_{A,B}))=1
, since their root numbers satisfy

\displaystyle w(E^{(B)}_{A,B})=w(E_{A,B})(\tfrac{-B}{4A^{3}+27B^{2}})=-\mu(4A^{3}+27B^{2})(\tfrac{-6}{4A^{3}+27B^{2}})w_{2}(E_{A,B}).

The local root number
w_{2}(E_{A,B})
may be controlled using

after sorting
A
and

1.2.
Overview

We restrict to the case
a=b=1
in this non-rigorous sketch. Replacing the rough cut-offs by smooth weights
f
, we consider counting primes weighted by the sequence

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{n=x^{2}+y^{3}}f(\tfrac{x}{A})f(\tfrac{y}{B}),\quad A\asymp X^{1/2},\,B\asymp X^{1/3}.

By a sieve argument (essentially Heath-Brown’s identity
[
16
]
), the proof reduces to the asymptotic evaluation of three different types of sums

Linear sums (Type I)

\displaystyle\sum_{d\leq D_{1}}\alpha_{d}\sum_{n}a_{dn},

Bilinear sums (Type II)

\displaystyle\hskip-8.0pt\sum_{m\sim X/N}\alpha_{m}\sum_{n}\beta_{n}a_{mn},

Divisor sums (Type I
2
)

\displaystyle\sum_{d\leq D_{2}}\alpha_{d}\sum_{m,n}a_{dmn},

where
\alpha
and
\beta
denote bounded complex coefficients.

1.2.1.
Type I sums

We are able to handle the Type I sums for

\displaystyle D_{1}<X^{5/9}

by applying Poisson summation to the variables
x
and
y
and the Weil bound for exponential sums.

1.2.2.
Type II sums

Assuming conjecture
\mathrm{C}_{1}(\varepsilon)
for some
\varepsilon>0
, we are able to handle Type II sums in the range

\displaystyle X^{1/6}<N<X^{1/3-2\varepsilon/3}.

This estimate for type II sums is based on the following considerations that can be seen as the main idea of this paper. After applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality on
m
and rearranging sums, the task reduces to evaluating a sum of the form

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\sim N}\beta_{n_{1}}\beta_{n_{2}}\sum_{k\asymp NX}\Upsilon_{n_{1},n_{2}}(k)\mathcal{Q}_{n_{1},n_{2}}(k),

where
\Upsilon_{n_{1},n_{2}}(k),\mathcal{Q}_{n_{1},n_{2}}(k)
denote the restricted representations by binary cubic and quadratic forms

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{n_{1},n_{2}}(k)=\sum_{k=n_{1}y_{2}^{3}-n_{2}y_{1}^{3}}f(\tfrac{y_{1}}{B})f(\tfrac{y_{2}}{B})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{Q}_{n_{1},n_{2}}(k)=\sum_{k=n_{2}x_{1}^{2}-n_{1}x_{2}^{2}}f(\tfrac{x_{1}}{A})f(\tfrac{x_{2}}{A}).

Using the classical correspondence between the class group
{\mathfrak{C}}(d)
of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})
and the set of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
-equivalence classes of binary quadratic forms of discriminant
d,
the latter may be expanded (see Lemma
2.1
) using the Hecke characters for the real quadratic field of discriminant
d=4n_{1}n_{2}\asymp N^{2}
. We essentially get

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{n_{1},n_{2}}(k)\approx\frac{1}{h(d)R_{d}}\sum_{|\ell|\ll R_{d}}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{{\mathfrak{C}}(d)}}\chi\xi^{\ell}(\mathfrak{n}_{2})\lambda_{\chi\xi^{\ell}}(k),\quad\mathfrak{n}_{2}^{2}=(4n_{2})\subseteq O_{d}.

Here the class number
h(d)
and the regulator
R_{d}
satisfy the Dirichlet class number formula (
2.1
)

\displaystyle 2h(d)R_{d}=\sqrt{d}L(1,(\tfrac{d}{\cdot}))\approx\sqrt{d}\asymp N,

where
\approx
holds on average over
n_{1},n_{2}
. By the Cohen-Lenstra heuristics, we expect that typically
h(d)\ll 1
and
R_{d}\asymp N
, so that the characters
\xi^{\ell}
are the main culprit for the losses. Corresponding to the fact that a minimal solution to Pell’s equations may be exponentially large, we expect that often
\mathcal{Q}_{n_{1},n_{2}}(k)=0.
More precisely, we have an expansion of the function
\mathcal{Q}_{n_{1},n_{2}}(k)
which has expected density
\approx 1/N
into roughly
N
many harmonics
\lambda_{\chi\xi^{\ell}}(k)
.

Invoking Conjecture
\mathrm{C}_{1}(\varepsilon)
, we obtain for
\chi\xi^{\ell}\neq 1

\displaystyle\sum_{k\asymp NX}\Upsilon_{n_{1},n_{2}}(k)\lambda_{\chi\xi^{\ell}}(k)\ll X^{1/3+2\varepsilon/3},

which is sufficient provided that
N<X^{1/3-2\varepsilon/3}
. We merely require this bound on average over the complete family of
n_{1},n_{2}\sim N
,
\chi\in\widehat{{\mathfrak{C}}(d)}
, and
|\ell|\ll R_{d}
(see Remark
7.1
). It is tempting to think that this problem would be amenable to large sieve techniques but unfortunately, the cubic forms appearing in
\Upsilon_{n_{1},n_{2}}(k)
are entangled with
n_{1},n_{2}
. We mention that similar sums for a fixed discriminant and a fixed binary cubic or quartic form arise naturally in the context of Manin’s conjecture for Châtelet surfaces
[
37
]
.

The task is then to evaluate the main term coming from
\chi\xi^{\ell}=1

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\sim N}\frac{\beta_{n_{1}}\beta_{n_{2}}}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}}L(1,(\tfrac{4n_{1}n_{2}}{\cdot}))}\sum_{k\asymp NX}\Upsilon_{n_{1},n_{2}}(k)\lambda_{1}(k).

It is not obvious how to make the argument unconditional and we have to assume that
\lambda_{1}(k)
may be replaced by the truncated approximation
\lambda^{\sharp}_{1}(k,T)
. For the divisor function along binary cubic forms Greaves has shown an asymptotic formula with a power saving
[
11
]
.

Using Heath-Brown’s large sieve for quadratic characters
[
17
]
, on average over
n_{1},n_{2}
we can replace the factor
L(1,(\tfrac{4n_{1}n_{2}}{\cdot}))^{-1}
by
\sum_{k\leq X^{\varepsilon}}\frac{\mu(k)}{k}(\tfrac{4n_{1}n_{2}}{k})
(see Lemma
3.5
). For the
truncated approximation
\lambda^{\sharp}_{1}(k,T)=\sum_{c\leq T}(\tfrac{4n_{1}n_{2}}{c})
we can evaluate the sum over
y_{1},y_{2}
by applying Poisson summation twice, which produces a count for cubic congruences

(1.1)

\displaystyle\#\{(y_{1},y_{2})\in({\mathbb{Z}}/c{\mathbb{Z}})^{2}:n_{1}y_{2}^{3}\equiv n_{2}y_{1}^{3}\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{c})\}.

Expanding (
1.1
) by cubic Dirichlet characters, the principal characters give the main term. We then need to bound error terms of the form

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{c\leq T}\frac{1}{c}\sum_{k\leq X^{\varepsilon}}\frac{\mu(k)}{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{c})\\
\chi^{3}=\chi_{0}\neq\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}\bigg|\sum_{n\sim N}\beta_{n}\chi(n)(\tfrac{n}{ck})\bigg|^{2}.

This is bounded using the large sieve for sextic characters due to Baier and Young
[
1
]
.

1.2.3.
Type I
2
sums

The obtained Type I and Type II ranges would already be enough for a lower bound of the correct order of magnitude for the number of primes
p=x^{2}+y^{3}
. To show an asymptotic formula, we also need to consider the Type I
2
sums. We are able to handle these for

\displaystyle D_{2}<X^{1/4}

by using the spectral methods of GL(2) automorphic forms. The proof will appear in an upcoming joint work with Grimmelt
[
14
]
, as an application of the
averages over orbits
introduced in
[
13
]
. To sketch the idea in the critical case
d\sim D_{2}
,
m\sim n\sim\sqrt{X/D_{2}}
, note that for a fixed
y
we consider a variant of the divisor problem along a quadratic polynomial
\sum_{x}f(\tfrac{x}{A})d(x^{2}+y^{3})
. Applying Poisson summation on
x
produces the expected main term and an error term with the dual variable of length
H\approx\sqrt{D_{2}}
.

Arguing similarly to Duke, Friedlander, and Iwaniec
[
5
]
, utilizing the factorization
y^{3}=y\cdot y^{2}
, we get a spectral expansion for the error term that is morally of the shape (suppressing the Eisenstein series and other minor details)

\displaystyle\frac{B^{1/2}}{H^{1/2}}\sum_{y}f(\tfrac{y}{B})\sum_{d\sim D_{2}}\alpha_{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\\
|t_{j}|\ll 1\end{subarray}}^{\Gamma_{0}(d)}\lambda_{j}(y)\sum_{h\sim H}\frac{\lambda_{j}(h)}{d^{1/2}}\sum_{z\in\Lambda_{y}}\sum_{\tau}u_{j}(\tau z).

Here
u_{j}(z)
are the (
L^{2}
-normalized) Maass cusp forms for the Hecke congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(d)
and
\lambda_{j}(h)
denote the Hecke eigenvalues. Here
\Lambda_{y}\subseteq\mathbb{H}
is essentially the set Heegner points for the discriminant
y
so that
\#\Lambda_{y}\approx y^{1/2}
, and
\tau
runs over a subset of size
\ll d^{o(1)}
in
\Gamma_{0}(d)\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality with the variables
z,\tau
on the outside (as in
[
5
]
), and using the Rankin-Selberg bound
\sum_{y}f(\tfrac{y}{B})|\lambda_{j}(y)|^{2}\leq(dB)^{o(1)}B
, we would get the range
D_{2}<X^{1/6}
. This turns out to be just barely insufficient for an asymptotic formula for primes. Taking advantage of the average over the orbits
z\in\Lambda_{y}
allows us to essentially save a factor of
(\#\Lambda_{y})^{1/2}\approx X^{1/12}
, which bumps the range up to
D_{2}<X^{1/4}
. We note that any range with
D_{2}>X^{1/6+\eta}
would be sufficient for getting the asymptotic formula – curiously, the prime detecting sieve has a discontinuity at
1/6
in terms of the parameter
\frac{\log D_{2}}{\log X}
, caused by crossing from Type I
2
sums to Type II sums (see
[
6
]
for a detailed discussion of discontinuities in sieve methods).

It seems difficult to improve the error term
o(1)
in Theorem
1.1
. Even if we assumed a much more uniform conjecture with
\max\{\cdots\}^{\varepsilon}
replaced by
(\log\max\{\cdots\})^{O(1)}
, we would only improve the error term
o(1)
to
O(\frac{\log\log X}{\log X})
. We are missing arithmetic information for multiple different types of sums, especially (i) three variables of size
X^{1/3+o(1)}
and (ii) six variables of size
X^{1/6+o(1)}
.

1.2.4.
Generalizations to other sequences

We mainly leverage the fact that the first term is a quadratic monomial, so the discussion, at least in principle, extends primes of the form
X^{2}+y
with
y
weighted by other sequences
\gamma_{y}
. Of particular interest are sequences
\gamma_{y}
with support of size
X^{1/2-\eta}
for some small
\eta>0
. For such a sequence the diagonal terms in the Type II argument are admissible if
N>X^{\eta}
. Therefore, to produce a non-trivial Type II range, we would only require a small amount of saving instead of square-root saving in the corresponding convolution sums along Hecke eigenvalues

(1.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{v_{1},v_{2}}\gamma_{v_{1}}\gamma_{v_{2}}\lambda_{\chi\xi^{\ell}}(n_{1}v_{2}-n_{2}v_{1}).

Furthermore, since
n_{1},n_{2}
are small, the uniformity in the conductor does not seem to be a formidable issue for, say, automorphic techniques. It would therefore be of great interest to obtain bounds for sums of the form (
1.2
), as it would quickly translate to results about primes or at least products of
k\geq 2
primes of the form
x^{2}+y
with the weights
\gamma_{y}
.

2.
Restricted representations by indefinite binary quadratic forms

2.1.
Real quadratic fields

Let
d>0
be a fundamental discriminant, that is,
d\equiv 0,1\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{4})
and
d
is square-free except for a possible factor of
4
or
8
. Consider the real quadratic field
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d}):={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})
and its ring of integers

\displaystyle O_{d}={\mathbb{Z}}[\sqrt{\omega_{d}}],\quad\text{where}\quad\omega_{d}:=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{d},\quad&d\equiv 0\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{4})\\
\tfrac{1}{2}(1+\sqrt{d}),\quad&d\equiv 1\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{4}).\end{cases}

For
z=x+y\sqrt{d},x,y\in{\mathbb{Q}}
we let
z^{\sigma}:=x-y\sqrt{d}
denote the other embedding into
{\mathbb{R}}
. The norm
N_{d}:{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})\to{\mathbb{Q}}
is defined by
N_{d}(z):=zz^{\sigma}=x^{2}-dy^{2}
. The norm is extended to ideals
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq O_{d}
via
N_{d}(\mathfrak{a})=\#O_{d}/\mathfrak{a}
, so that for any principal ideal
\mathfrak{a}=(z)
we have
N_{d}(\mathfrak{a})=|N_{d}(z)|
.

The group of units has rank 1 and it is generated by
-1
and the fundamental unit
\varepsilon_{d}
, which is defined as the smallest element
\varepsilon\in O_{d}
with
\varepsilon>1
and
N_{d}(\varepsilon)=\pm 1
. In other words,
\varepsilon_{d}=\frac{a+b\sqrt{d}}{2}
is the smallest element
>1
such that
(a,b)\in{\mathbb{Z}}
is a solution to the Pell equations
a^{2}-db^{2}=\pm 4
.

We let
\mathcal{I}_{d}
denote the group of fractional ideals and
\mathcal{P}_{d}
denote the subgroup of principal fractional ideals. The ideal class group is then defined as

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{C}}(d):=\mathcal{I}_{d}/\mathcal{P}_{d}.

The class group
{\mathfrak{C}}(d)
is a finite abelian group and the class number is defined as
h(d):=\#{\mathfrak{C}}(d).
The regulator of the number field
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})
is defined by
R_{d}:=\log\varepsilon_{d}.
We then have the
Dirichlet class number formula
for real quadratic fields

(2.1)

\displaystyle 2h(d)R_{d}=\sqrt{d}\,L(1,(\tfrac{d}{\cdot})),\quad L(s,(\tfrac{d}{\cdot})):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(\tfrac{d}{n})n^{-s}

where
(\tfrac{d}{n})
is the primitive real character associated to the fundamental discriminant
d
.

We define the hyperbolic coordinates of any non-zero
z\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})
via

\displaystyle r(z)=\operatorname{sgn}(z)|N_{d}(z)|^{1/2},\quad\alpha(z)=\tfrac{1}{2}\log|z/z^{\sigma}|.

Then analogous to the polar coordinates for imaginary quadratic fields we have for
zz^{\sigma}>0

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=x+y\sqrt{d}=r(z)e^{\alpha(z)},\quad z^{\sigma}=r(z)e^{-\alpha(z)},

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=r(z)\cosh\alpha(z),\quad y=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}r(z)\sinh\alpha(z).

For
zz^{\sigma}<0
we have
z^{\sigma}=-r(z)e^{-\alpha(z)}
and the roles of
\cosh\alpha
and
\sinh\alpha
are swapped.

The Hecke characters
[
22
,
23
]

\{\xi^{\ell}\}_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
are defined for principal ideals
\mathfrak{a}=(z)
by the formula

\displaystyle\xi^{\ell}(\mathfrak{a})=\xi^{\ell}(z):=e(\ell\tfrac{\alpha(z)}{R_{d}}),\quad e(x)=e^{2\pi ix}.

It is quick to check using
\varepsilon_{d}^{\sigma}=1/\varepsilon_{d}
that this definition does not depend on the choice of the generator
z
. The character
\xi^{\ell}
may be extended to a character on all fractional ideals, and the extension is unique up to multiplication by a class group character
\chi\in\widehat{{\mathfrak{C}}(d)}.

We denote

(2.2)

\displaystyle\psi_{\chi\xi^{\ell},s}(\mathfrak{a}):=\xi^{\ell}(\mathfrak{a})\chi(\mathfrak{a})N_{d}(\mathfrak{a})^{-s/2},\quad\psi_{\chi\xi^{\ell}}:=\psi_{\chi\xi^{\ell},0}.

We define the associated Hecke eigenvalues

(2.3)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\chi\xi^{\ell}}(n):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a}\subseteq O_{d}\\
N_{d}(\mathfrak{a})=n\end{subarray}}\psi_{\chi\xi^{\ell}}(\mathfrak{a}).

For
\chi\xi^{\ell}=1
we have the Dirichlet convolution
\lambda_{1}=1\ast(\tfrac{d}{\cdot})
. For any
T>1
, we define the truncated approximation

\displaystyle\lambda^{\sharp}_{1}(n)=\lambda^{\sharp}_{1}(n,T)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c|n\\
c\leq T\end{subarray}}(\tfrac{d}{c})

and set

(2.4)

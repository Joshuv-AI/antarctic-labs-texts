Model Checking Linear Temporal Logic with Standpoint Modalities
Rajab Aghamov, Christel Baier, Toghrul Karimov, Rupak Majumdar, Joël Ouaknine, Jakob Piribauer, Timm Spork

Abstract
Standpoint linear temporal logic ( SLTL \mathrm{SLTL} ) is a recently introduced extension of classical linear temporal logic (LTL) with standpoint modalities. Intuitively, these modalities allow to express that, from agent a a ’s standpoint, it is conceivable that a given formula holds. Besides the standard interpretation of the standpoint modalities we introduce four new semantics, which differ in the information an agent can extract from the history. We provide a general model checking algorithm applicable to SLTL \mathrm{SLTL} under any of the five semantics. Furthermore we analyze the computational complexity of the corresponding model checking problems, obtaining PSPACE-completeness in three cases, which stands in contrast to the known EXPSPACE-completeness of the SLTL \mathrm{SLTL} satisfiability problem.

Introduction

Automated reasoning about the dynamics of scenarios in which multiple agents with access to different information interact is a key problem
in artificial intelligence and formal verification. Epistemic temporal logics are prominent, expressive formalisms to specify properties of such scenarios (see, e.g.,
HalVar-STOC86
;
Halpern-Overview86
;
Reasoning-about-knowledge-MIT-2004
;
MC-LTLK-and-beyond-2024
).
The resulting algorithmic problems, however, often have non-elementary complexity or are even undecidable (see, e.g.,
vanderMeydenS1999
;
Dima2009
;
MC-LTLK-and-beyond-2024
).

Aiming to balance expressiveness and computational tractability,
SL-FOIS21
;
phdGomez
defines
static
standpoint logics
that extend propositional logic with modalities

\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi
expressing that “according to agent
a
, it is conceivable that
\varphi
” and the dual modalities

[\![a]\!]\varphi
expressing that “according to
a
, it is unequivocal that
\varphi
”.
Standpoint logics and their extensions have proven useful to, e.g., reason about inconsistent formalizations of concepts
in the medical domain to align different ontologies and in a forestry application, where different sources disagree about the global extent of forests
SL-FOIS21
;
phdGomez
;
Gomez2022
.

Recently introduced combinations of linear temporal logic (LTL) with standpoint modalities

SLTL-KR23
;
Complexity-SLTL-ECAI24
enables reasoning about dynamical aspects of multi-agent systems.
The focus of
SLTL-KR23
;
Complexity-SLTL-ECAI24

is the satisfiability problem for the resulting
standpoint LTL
(
\mathrm{SLTL}
).
In this paper, we consider the model-checking problem that asks whether all executions of a transition system satisfy a given
\mathrm{SLTL}
-formula. To the best of our knowledge, this problem has not been addressed in the literature.

Whether the formula
\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi
holds after some finite history, i.e., whether it is plausible for agent
a
that property
\varphi
holds on the future execution, depends on
a
’s standpoint
as well as what was observable to
a
from the history.
To illustrate this, consider a situation in which different political agents have different perceptions of how actions taken by the state influence future developments.
These perceptions are the standpoints of the agents. After a series of events, i.e., a history, the agents deem different future developments possible according to their standpoint
as well as the aspects of the history that they are actually aware of. When reasoning about each other’s standpoints, there are different ways in which information might be exchanged between the agents.
For example, in a discussion agents might learn about aspects of the history they were not aware of; or they might completely ignore what other agents have observed in the past.

Besides the semantics for
\mathrm{SLTL}
proposed in
SLTL-KR23
;
Complexity-SLTL-ECAI24
, we introduce four additional semantics that differ in the amount of information agent
a
can access from the history and how information is transferred between agents.
To formalize these semantics, we follow a natural
approach for standpoint logics that
uses separate
transition systems
\mathcal{T}_{a}
describing the executions that are consistent with
a
’s standpoint as in

SLTL-KR23
;
Complexity-SLTL-ECAI24
, together with a main transition system
\mathcal{T}
modeling the actual system.
Unlike
SLTL-KR23
;
Complexity-SLTL-ECAI24
,
we assume the labels of the states in
\mathcal{T}_{a}
to be from
a subset
P_{a}
of the set
P
of atomic propositions of
\mathcal{T}
.
The difference
to the use of indistinguishablity
relations
\sim_{a}
on states of a single transition system for all agents
a\in\mathit{Ag}
, common in epistemic temporal logics,
is mostly
of syntactic nature as we will show by translations of families of
indistinguishablity
relations
(\sim_{a})_{a\in\mathit{Ag}}
on a transition system to
separate transition systems
(\mathcal{T}_{a})_{a\in\mathit{Ag}}
, and vice versa.

Under all five semantics, the intuitive meaning of
\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi
is that
there is a state
s
in
\mathcal{T}_{a}
, which is one of the
potential current states from agent
a
’s view of the history,
and a path
\pi
in
\mathcal{T}_{a}
from
s
such that
\pi
satisfies
\varphi

when agent
a
makes nondeterministic guesses for truth values of
the atomic propositions outside
P_{a}
. Informally, the different semantics are as follows:

– The
step semantics

\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm step}}
agrees with the
semantics proposed
in
SLTL-KR23
;
Complexity-SLTL-ECAI24
.
It assumes that only
the number of steps performed in the past are accessible
to the agents.

– The
pure observation-based semantics

\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm pobs}}

is in the spirit of
the perfect-recall LTLK (LTL extended with knowledge operators) semantics of
MC-LTLK-and-beyond-2024

where
a
can access
exactly the truth values of
the atomic propositions in
P_{a}
from the history.

– The
public-history semantics

\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm public}}
can be seen
as a perfect-recall variant of the LTLK semantics
where all agents have
full access to the history.

– The
decremental semantics

\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm decr}}
is a variant of

\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm pobs}}
where
standpoint subformulas
\langle\!\!\langle b\rangle\!\!\rangle\psi

of a formula
\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi

are interpreted from the view of agent
a
, when
a
knows the transitions
system of
b
, but can access only
the atomic propositions
in
P_{a}\cap P_{b}
to guess what agent
b
knows from the history.

– The
incremental semantics

\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm incr}}
is as
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm decr}}
, but under the assumption that standpoint subformulas
\langle\!\!\langle b\rangle\!\!\rangle\psi

of a standpoint formula
\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi

are interpreted from the view of the coalition
\{a,b\}
, i.e.,
that
a
can access
the atomic propositions
in
P_{a}\cup P_{b}
to guess what agent
b
knows from the history.

The decremental and incremental semantics share ideas of distributed knowledge and the “everybody knows” operator of epistemic logics
Reasoning-about-knowledge-MIT-2004
.

Main contributions.

Besides introducing the four new semantics for
\mathrm{SLTL}
(Section
3
), our main contributions are

– a generic model-checking algorithm that is applicable for all five semantics
(Section
4
)

– complexity-theoretic results for the model checking problem of
\mathrm{SLTL}
under the different semantics (Section
5
).
More precisely we show
PSPACE-completeness for full
\mathrm{SLTL}
under
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm step}}
and
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm public}}
, and for
\mathrm{SLTL}
formulas of alternation depth 1 under
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm pobs}}
,
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm decr}}
and
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm incr}}
. This stands in contrast to the EXPSPACE-completeness of the satisfiability problem for SLTL under the step semantics
Complexity-SLTL-ECAI24
.
Furthermore, our results yield an EXPTIME upper bound for
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm decr}}
. The same holds for
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm pobs}}
under the additional assumption that the
P_{a}
’s are pairwise disjoint.
We show that
\mathrm{SLTL}
under all five semantics can be embedded into LTLK. For the case of
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm incr}}
, the embedding yields an
(N{-}1)
-EXPSPACE upper bound where
N=|\mathit{Ag}|
. For the case of
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm pobs}}
and the
\mathrm{SLTL}
fragment of alternation depth at most
d
, the embedding into LTLK implies

(d{-}1)
-EXPSPACE membership.

While our algorithm relies on similar ideas as the
LTLK model-checking algorithm as in
MC-LTLK-and-beyond-2024

(even for the richer logic CTL*K), it
exploits the simpler nature of

\mathrm{SLTL}
compared to LTLK and generates smaller
history-automata than those that would have been constructed when
applying iteratively
the powerset constructions of
MC-LTLK-and-beyond-2024
.
As such, our algorithm can be seen as an adaption of

MC-LTLK-and-beyond-2024
that takes a more fine-grained approach for the different
\mathrm{SLTL}
semantics resulting in the
different complexity bounds described above.

Omitted proofs and details can be found in the appendix.

Notations for strings.

Given an alphabet
\Sigma
,
we write
\Sigma^{*}
for the set of finite strings over
\Sigma
,

\Sigma^{\omega}
for the set of infinite strings over
\Sigma

and
\Sigma^{\infty}
for
\Sigma^{*}\cup\Sigma^{\omega}
.
As usual,
\Sigma^{+}=\Sigma^{*}\setminus\{\varepsilon\}
where

\varepsilon
denotes the empty string.
Given a (in)finite string

\varsigma=H_{0}\,H_{1}\ldots H_{n}
or

\varsigma=H_{0}\,H_{1}\,\ldots
over
\Sigma
,
let
\mathit{first}(\varsigma)=H_{0}
.
If
\varsigma=H_{0}\ldots H_{n}
is finite then

\mathit{last}(\varsigma)=H_{n}
.
Given
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
we write
\varsigma[i\ldots j]

for the substring
H_{i}\ldots H_{j}
if
i\leqslant j

(and assuming
j\leqslant n
if
\varsigma
is a finite string of length
n
)
and
\varsigma[i\ldots j]=\varepsilon
if
i>j
.
If
i=j
then
\varsigma[i\ldots i]=\varsigma[i]=H_{i}
.
So,
\varsigma[0\ldots j]
denotes the prefix
H_{0}\ldots H_{j}
. If

\varsigma
is infinite then
\varsigma[j\ldots\infty]=H_{j}\,H_{j+1}\,H_{j+2}\ldots
.

If
\Sigma=2^{P}
is the powerset of
P
and
R\subseteq P
then the projection function

|_{R}:(2^{P})^{\infty}\to(2^{R})^{\infty}

is obtained by applying the projection

2^{P}\to 2^{R}
,
H\mapsto H\cap R
,
elementwise, i.e., if
\varsigma=H_{0}\,H_{1}\,H_{2}\ldots
then

\varsigma|_{R}=(H_{0}\cap R)\,(H_{1}\cap R)\,(H_{2}\cap R)\ldots
.

Transition systems.

A transition system is a tuple
\mathcal{T}=(S,\to,\mathit{Init},R,L)
where

S
is a finite state space,
{\to}\,\subseteq\,S\times S
a total transition
relation (where totality means that every state
s
has at least one outgoing transition
s\to s^{\prime}
),
\mathit{Init}\subseteq S
the set of initial states,
R
a finite set of atomic propositions and
L:S\to 2^{R}
the labeling function.
If
\mathit{Init}
is a singeleton, say
\mathit{Init}=\{\mathit{init}\}
, we simply write

\mathcal{T}=(S,\to,\mathit{init},R,L)
.

A path in
\mathcal{T}
is a (in)finite string
\pi=s_{0}\,s_{1}\ldots s_{n}\in S^{+}
or
\pi=s_{0}\,s_{1}\,s_{2}\ldots\in S^{\omega}
such that
s_{i}\to s_{i+1}
for all
i
.
\pi
is initial if
\mathit{first}(\pi)\in\mathit{Init}
.
The trace of
\pi
is

\mathit{trace}(\pi)=L(s_{0})\,L(s_{1})\ldots\in(2^{R})^{+}\cup(2^{R})^{\omega}
.
If
s\in S
then

\mathit{Paths}(\mathcal{T},s)
denotes the set of infinite paths in
\mathcal{T}
starting in
s
and

\mathit{Traces}(\mathcal{T},s)=\{\mathit{trace}(\pi):\pi\in\mathit{Paths}(\mathcal{T},s)\}
.
If
P
is a superset of
R
then

\mathit{Traces}^{P}(\mathcal{T},s)=\bigl\{\rho\in\bigl(2^{P}\bigr)^{\omega}:\rho|_{R}\in\mathit{Traces}(\mathcal{T},s)\bigr\}
.

Thus,
\mathit{Traces}(\mathcal{T},s)\subseteq(2^{R})^{\omega}
, while

\mathit{Traces}^{P}(\mathcal{T},s)\subseteq(2^{P})^{\omega}
.
Moreover,

\mathit{Paths}(\mathcal{T})=\bigcup_{s\in\mathit{Init}}\mathit{Paths}(\mathcal{T},s)
.

\mathit{Traces}(\mathcal{T})
and
\mathit{Traces}^{P}(\mathcal{T})
have the analogous meaning.
If
h\in(2^{P})^{+}
then
\mathit{Reach}(\mathcal{T},h)
denotes the
set of states
s
in
\mathcal{T}
that are reachable from
\mathit{Init}
via a path
\pi

with
\mathit{trace}(\pi)=h|_{R}
.

Linear temporal logic (LTL).

The syntax of LTL over
P
is given by (where
p\in P
):

\begin{array}[]{lclcr}\varphi&::=&\mathit{true}\ \ \big|\ \ p\ \ \big|\ \ \neg\varphi\ \ \big|\ \ \varphi_{1}\wedge\varphi_{2}\ \ \big|\ \bigcirc\varphi\ \ \big|\ \ \varphi_{1}\,\mathrm{U}\,\varphi_{2}\end{array}

Other Boolean connectives are derived as usual, e.g.,

\varphi_{1}\vee\varphi_{2}=\neg(\neg\varphi_{1}\wedge\neg\varphi_{2})
.
The modalities
\Diamond
(eventually) and
\Box

\Diamond\varphi=\mathit{true}\,\mathrm{U}\,\varphi

\Box\varphi=\neg\Diamond\neg\varphi

;
BK08

(\rho,n)\models\varphi
iff
\rho[n\ldots\infty]\models_{\text{\rm\tiny LTL}}\varphi
.

(and later its extension
\mathrm{SLTL}

interpretes formulas over

Then,

(f,h)\models\varphi_{1}\wedge\varphi_{2}

(f,h)\models\varphi_{1}

(f,h)\models\varphi_{2}

(f,h)\models\neg\varphi

iff

,

3
\mathrm{SLTL}
: LTL with standpoint modalities

Standpoint LTL (
\mathrm{SLTL}
)
extends LTL by standpoint modalities
\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi

where
a
is an agent and
\varphi
a formula.

3.1
Syntax

Given a finite set
P
of atomic propositions and
a finite set
\mathit{Ag}
of agents, say
\mathit{Ag}=\{a,b,\ldots\}
,
the syntax of
\mathrm{SLTL}
formulas over
P
and
\mathit{Ag}
for
p\in P
and
a\in\mathit{Ag}
is given by

\begin{array}[]{lclcr}\varphi&::=&\mathit{true}\ \big|\ p\ \big|\ \neg\varphi\ \big|\ \varphi_{1}\wedge\varphi_{2}\ \big|\ \bigcirc\varphi\ \big|\ \varphi_{1}\,\mathrm{U}\,\varphi_{2}\ \big|\ \langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi\end{array}

The intuitive meaning of
\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi

is that from agent
a
’s standpoint it is conceivable that
\varphi

will hold, in the sense that there are indications from
a
’s view
that there is a path
starting in the current state that fulfills
\varphi
.
The dual standpoint modality is defined by

[\![a]\!]\varphi\ =\ \neg\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\neg\varphi
and
has the intuitive meaning that from the standpoint of
a
,

\varphi
is unequivocal.
That is, under
a
’s view
all paths starting in the current state fulfill
\varphi
.

Formulas of the shape
\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi
are
called
standpoint formulas
.
If
\varphi
is a
\mathrm{SLTL}
formula, then
maximal standpoint subformulas
of
\varphi

are subformulas that have the form
\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\phi

and that are not in the scope
of another standpoint operator.
For example,

\varphi=(p\wedge\bigcirc\chi_{1})\vee\psi

with
\chi_{1}=\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle(\langle\!\!\langle b\rangle\!\!\rangle q\,\mathrm{U}\,r)
and

\psi=[\![c]\!]\bigcirc(r\wedge\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle p\,\mathrm{U}\,\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle q)

has two maximal standpoint subformulas, namely

\chi_{1}
and

\chi_{2}=\langle\!\!\langle c\rangle\!\!\rangle\neg\bigcirc(r\wedge\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle p\,\mathrm{U}\,\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle q)
.

The
alternation depth

\mathit{ad}(\varphi)
of
\varphi

is the maximal number of alternations between standpoint modalities
for different agents.
For instance,

\mathit{ad}(\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle(p\wedge\bigcirc[\![b]\!]q))=2
, while

\mathit{ad}(\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle(p\wedge\bigcirc[\![a]\!]q))=\mathit{ad}(\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle p\wedge\bigcirc[\![b]\!]q)=1

if
a\not=b
.
The precise definition is provided in
Definition
B.1
in the appendix.
Each
\mathrm{SLTL}
formula over a singleton agent set
\mathit{Ag}=\{a\}
has
alternation depth at most 1.
For
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathrm{SLTL}_{d}
denote the sublogic of
\mathrm{SLTL}
where all formulas
\varphi
satisfy
\mathit{ad}(\varphi)\leq d
. In particular,
\mathrm{SLTL}_{0}
is LTL.

Remark 3.1
.

The original papers
SLTL-KR23
;
Complexity-SLTL-ECAI24

on standpoint LTL have an additional
type of formula
a\preceq b

where
a,b\in\mathit{Ag}
.
The intuitive semantics of
a\preceq b
is that
the standpoint of
a
is sharper than that of
b
.
In our setting, where we are given
transition systems for
a
and

, formulas

are
either true or false depending on whether the set of traces
of the transition system representing

’s view is contained in

As the trace inclusion problem is PSPACE-complete

like the LTL model-checking problem

the complexity results of Section

are not affected when adding sharpening statements

3.2
Semantics of
\mathrm{SLTL}

We will consider different semantics of the standpoint modality
that differ in what the agents can observe from the history.

\mathrm{SLTL}
structures.

\mathrm{SLTL}
structures are
tuples

\mathfrak{T}=\bigl(\mathcal{T}_{0},(\mathcal{T}_{a})_{a\in\mathit{Ag}}\bigr)
with
\mathcal{T}_{0}=(S_{0},\to_{0},\mathit{init}_{0},P_{0},L_{0})
a
transition system
over the full set
P_{0}=P
of atomic propositions, and

\mathcal{T}_{a}=(S_{a},\to_{a},\mathit{init}_{a},P_{a},L_{a})
transition systems
for the agents
a\in\mathit{Ag}
over some
P_{a}\subseteq P
.
For simplicity, we assume these transition systems to have
unique initial states.

Semantics of the standpoint modalities.

We consider five different semantics for

\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi
, using satisfaction relations

\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm step}}
(step semantics as in
SLTL-KR23
;
Complexity-SLTL-ECAI24
),

\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm pobs}}

(pure observation-based semantics which is essentially the
perfect-recall partial information semantics for LTLK

MC-LTLK-and-beyond-2024

adapted for
\mathrm{SLTL}
structures),

\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm public}}

(public-history semantics which can be seen as a
perfect-recall semantics where agents have full information about the
history),

\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm decr}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle Q}
and
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm incr}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle Q}

(variants of
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm pobs}}
with decremental resp. incremental knowledge)
where
Q\subseteq P
.

We deal here with an interpretation over future-history pairs
(see Section
2
).
Let
\models
be one of the five satisfaction relations.
The semantics of
\mathrm{SLTL}

can be presented in a uniform manner
as shown in Figure
1
.
For the dual standpoint operator we obtain:

(f,h)\models[\![a]\!]\varphi
iff
(f^{\prime},h^{\prime})\models\varphi

for all
h^{\prime}\in(2^{P})^{+}
,
t\in\mathit{Reach}(\mathcal{T}_{a},h^{\prime})

and
f^{\prime}\in\mathit{Traces}^{P}(\mathcal{T}_{a},t)
with

\mathit{last}(h^{\prime})=\mathit{first}(f^{\prime})
and

\mathit{obs}_{a}(h)=\mathit{obs}_{a}(h^{\prime})
.

The five semantics rely on different observation functions

\mathit{obs}_{a}
. In all cases,

\mathit{obs}_{a}
is a projection
\mathit{obs}_{a}:(2^{P})^{+}\to(2^{\mathfrak{O}_{a}})^{+}
,

\mathit{obs}_{a}(h)=h|_{\mathfrak{O}_{a}}

for some
\mathfrak{O}_{a}\subseteq P
.
Intuitively,
\mathfrak{O}_{a}
formalizes which of the propositions are visible to agent
a

in the history. (For the decremental and
incremental semantics,
both
\mathfrak{O}_{a}
and the induced observation function
\mathit{obs}_{a}

do not only depend on
a
, but on the context of the
formula
\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi
as will be explained later.)
Before presenting the specific choices of
\mathfrak{O}_{a}
in the five

\mathrm{SLTL}
semantics, we make some general observations:

Lemma 3.2
.

If
f_{1},f_{2}\in(2^{P})^{\omega}
and
h\in(2^{P})^{+}
with

\mathit{last}(h)=\mathit{first}(f_{1})=\mathit{first}(f_{2})

Step semantics:

The semantics of the standpoint modality

\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi

introduced in

SLTL-KR23
;
Complexity-SLTL-ECAI24

relies on the assumption that the only information that the agents can extract
from the history is the number
of steps that have been performed in the past.
They formulate the semantics in terms of trace-position pairs

(\rho,n)\in(2^{P})^{\omega}\times\mathbb{N}
and define

(\rho,n)\models\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi
iff
(\rho^{\prime},n)\models\varphi

for some
\rho^{\prime}\in\mathit{Traces}(\mathcal{T}_{a})
.
Reformulated to our setting,
the set of propositions visible for agent
a
in the history
is
\mathfrak{O}_{a}=\varnothing
, which yields the
observation function
\mathit{obs}_{a}:(2^{P})^{+}\to\mathbb{N}
,
\mathit{obs}_{a}(h)=|h|
.
An equivalent formulation for future-history pairs is:

(f,h)\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm step}}\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi
iff
there exist a word
h^{\prime}\in(2^{P})^{+}
,
a state

t\in\mathit{Reach}(\mathcal{T}_{a},h^{\prime})

and a trace

f^{\prime}\in\mathit{Traces}^{P}(\mathcal{T}_{a},t)
such that

\mathit{last}(h^{\prime})=\mathit{first}(f^{\prime})
,

|h|=|h^{\prime}|
and
(f^{\prime},h^{\prime})\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm step}}\varphi
.

Pure observation-based semantics:

In the style of the (dual of the) classical K-modality in LTLK

;
HalVar-JCSS89
;
MC-LTLK-and-beyond-2024
(cf. Section
3.3
) we can deal with
the observation function that projects the given history

to the
observations that agent
a
can make when exactly the propositions in
P_{a}

are visible for
a
. That is, for the pure observation-based semantics,
\mathfrak{O}_{a}=P_{a}
. Then,

(f,h)\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm pobs}}\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi
iff
there exist a word
h^{\prime}\in(2^{P})^{+}
,
a state

t\in\mathit{Reach}(\mathcal{T}_{a},h^{\prime})

and a trace

f^{\prime}\in\mathit{Traces}^{P}(\mathcal{T}_{a},t)
such that

\mathit{last}(h^{\prime})=\mathit{first}(f^{\prime})
,

h|_{P_{a}}=h^{\prime}|_{P_{a}}
and
(f^{\prime},h^{\prime})\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm pobs}}\varphi
.

Public-history semantics:

In the public-history semantics all agents
a
have full information
about the history
h
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{O}_{a}=P

and

\mathit{obs}_{a}(h)=h
. Then,

(f,h)\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm public}}\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi
iff
there exist
a state
t\in\mathit{Reach}(\mathcal{T}_{a},h)

and a trace

f^{\prime}\in\mathit{Traces}^{P}(\mathcal{T}_{a},t)
such that

\mathit{last}(h)=\mathit{first}(f^{\prime})
and

(f^{\prime},h)\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm public}}\varphi
.

Decremental semantics:

The decremental semantics is a variant of the pure observation-based
semantics with a different meaning of nested standpoint subformulas:
A standpoint formula
\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi

interpretes
maximal standpoint subformulas
\langle\!\!\langle b\rangle\!\!\rangle\psi
of
\varphi
from the view of
agent
a
. The assumption is that
a

knows agent
b
’s transition system
\mathcal{T}_{b}
and thus
can make the same guesses for the future as
b
, but can access
only the truth values of
joint atomic propositions in
P_{a}^{\prime}\cap P_{b}
from the history to make a guess
what
b
has observed in the past.
Here,
P_{a}^{\prime}\subseteq P_{a}
is the set of atomic propositions accessible
when interpreting
\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi

which can itself be a subformula
of a larger standpoint formula
\langle\!\!\langle c\rangle\!\!\rangle\chi

(in which case
\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi
is interpreted from the view of agent
c

and

P_{a}^{\prime}\subseteq P_{c}\cap P_{a}
).
Formally, we use a parametric satisfaction relation

\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm decr}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle Q}
for
Q\subseteq P
.
The intuitive meaning of
\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi
under
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm decr}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle Q}
is that
a
can extract from the history

h
exactly the truth values of the propositions in

\mathfrak{O}_{a}^{Q}=Q\cap P_{a}
. So,

(f,h)\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm decr}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle Q}\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi
iff
there exist a word
h^{\prime}\in(2^{P})^{+}
,
a state

t\in\mathit{Reach}(\mathcal{T}_{a},h^{\prime})

and a trace

f^{\prime}\in\mathit{Traces}^{P}(\mathcal{T}_{a},t)
such that

\mathit{last}(h^{\prime})=\mathit{first}(f^{\prime})
,

h|_{Q\cap P_{a}}=h^{\prime}|_{Q\cap P_{a}}

and
(f^{\prime},h^{\prime})\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm decr}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle Q\cap P_{a}}\varphi
.
For the satisfaction over a
\mathrm{SLTL}
structure, we start with
Q=P
.

Incremental semantics:

The incremental semantics also relies on a parametric satisfaction relation

\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm incr}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle Q}
where
Q\subseteq P
.
The intuitive meaning of

\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi

under
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm incr}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle Q}

is
that agent
a
can extract from the history
h
exactly the propositions
in
\mathfrak{O}_{a}^{Q}=Q\cup P_{a}
and interpretes
\varphi

over
Q\cup P_{a}
.
Thus, when interpreting nested standpoint subformulas

\langle\!\!\langle b\rangle\!\!\rangle\psi
of
\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi
then agents
a
and
b
build a
coalition to extract the information from the history.
Formally,
(f,h)\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm incr}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle Q}\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi
iff
there exist a word
h^{\prime}\in(2^{P})^{+}
,
a state

t\in\mathit{Reach}(\mathcal{T}_{a},h^{\prime})

and
a trace

f^{\prime}\in\mathit{Traces}^{P}(\mathcal{T}_{a},t)
such that

\mathit{last}(h^{\prime})=\mathit{first}(f^{\prime})
,

h|_{Q\cup P_{a}}=h^{\prime}|_{Q\cup P_{a}}

and
(f^{\prime},h^{\prime})\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm incr}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle Q\cup P_{a}}\varphi
.
For the satisfaction over a
\mathrm{SLTL}
structure, we start with
Q=\varnothing
.

3.3
From pure observation-based
\mathrm{SLTL}
to LTLK

LTLK (also called CKL
m
)

HalVar-STOC86
;
HalVar-JCSS89
;
MC-LTLK-and-beyond-2024

is an extension of LTL by an unary knowledge modality
K_{a}
for
every agent
a\in\mathit{Ag}
and a common knowledge operator
C_{A}
for
coalitions
A\subseteq\mathit{Ag}
.
We drop the latter and deal with LTLK where the grammar
for formulas is the same as for
\mathrm{SLTL}
when

\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi
is replaced with
K_{a}\varphi
.
The alternation depth of LTLK formulas and the sublogics LTLK
d
are defined as for
\mathrm{SLTL}
.

LTLK structures are tuples

(\mathcal{T},(\sim_{a})_{a\in\mathit{Ag}})
where

\mathcal{T}=(S,\to,\mathit{init},R,L)
is a transition system and the

\sim_{a}
,
a\in\mathit{Ag}
, are equivalence relations on
S
. The intended meaning
is that

if agent
a
cannot distinguish the states
s
and
t
.

The perfect-recall semantics of LTLK extends
the standard LTL semantics formulated for path-position pairs
(\pi,n)

consisting of a path
\pi=s_{0}s_{1}s_{2}\ldots\in\mathit{Paths}(\mathcal{T})

and a position
n\in\mathbb{N}

by
(\pi,n)\models_{\text{\rm\tiny LTLK}}K_{a}\varphi
iff

(\pi^{\prime},n)\models_{\text{\rm\tiny LTLK}}\varphi
for all paths

\pi^{\prime}=s_{0}^{\prime}s_{1}^{\prime}s_{2}^{\prime}\ldots\in\mathit{Paths}(\mathcal{T})
with

s_{i}\sim_{a}s_{i}^{\prime}
for
i=0,1,\ldots,n
.
As such, the dual standpoint modality

[\![a]\!]
under the pure observation-based semantics
resembles the
K_{a}
modality of LTLK.
Indeed,
\mathrm{SLTL}
under
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm pobs}}
can be considered
as a special case of LTLK under the perfect-recall semantics:

Lemma 3.8
.

Given a pair
(\mathfrak{T},\phi)
consisting of a
\mathrm{SLTL}
structure

\mathfrak{T}=(\mathcal{T}_{0},(\mathcal{T}_{a})_{a\in\mathit{Ag}})
and a
\mathrm{SLTL}
formula
\phi
,
one can construct in polynomial time
an LTLK structure
\mathfrak{T}_{\text{\rm\tiny LTLK}}=(\mathcal{T}^{\prime},(\sim_{a})_{a\in\mathit{Ag}})

and an LTLK formula
\phi_{\text{\rm\tiny LTLK}}
such that:

(1)

\mathfrak{T}\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm pobs}}\phi
if and only if

\mathfrak{T}_{\text{\rm\tiny LTLK}}\models_{\text{\rm\tiny LTLK}}\phi_{\text{\rm\tiny LTLK}}

(2)

\phi
and
\phi_{\text{\rm\tiny LTLK}}
have the same alternation depth.

Proof sketch.

Assume w.l.o.g. that the initial state labelings in
\mathcal{T}_{0}
and
\mathcal{T}_{a}
are consistent for all

First, for each

\mathcal{T}_{a}
to its completion

by adding new states
\bot_{a}^{Q}
for each

, where the set of atomic propositions in
\mathcal{T}_{a}^{\bot}
is

P_{a}^{\bot}=P_{a}\cup\{\bot_{a}\}
.
The labeling of the original states is unchanged, i.e.,

L_{a}^{\bot}(s)=L_{a}(s)
, while
for the fresh states

.
We then construct the synchronous product
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=S_{0}^{\bot}\times\prod_{a\in\mathit{Ag}}\mathcal{T}_{a}^{\bot}

for each

.
The set of atomic propositions is

L^{\prime}(s,(s_{a})_{a\in\mathit{Ag}})=L_{0}(s)\cup\{\bot_{a}:a\in\mathit{Ag}\cup\{0\},\bot_{a}\in L_{a}^{\bot}(s_{a})\}

The initial state of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
is

\mathit{init}^{\prime}=(\mathit{init}_{0},(\mathit{init}_{a})_{a\in\mathit{Ag}})
, and the transitions are defined synchronously. The equivalence relations
\sim_{a}
on

satisfy

L^{\prime}(\sigma)\cap P_{a}^{\bot}=L^{\prime}(\theta)\cap P_{a}^{\bot}
.
We then inductively
translate
\mathrm{SLTL}
formulas
\varphi
into “equivalent” LTLK formulas
\varphi^{\prime}
:

\varphi^{\prime}=\varphi,\ (\neg\varphi)^{\prime}=\neg\varphi^{\prime},\ (\varphi\wedge\psi)^{\prime}=\varphi^{\prime}\wedge\psi^{\prime},\ (\bigcirc\varphi)^{\prime}=\bigcirc\varphi^{\prime},\ (\varphi_{1}\,\mathrm{U}\,\varphi_{2})^{\prime}=\varphi_{1}^{\prime}\!\!\! \,\mathrm{U}\,\varphi_{2}^{\prime},\ (\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi)^{\prime}=\overline{K}_{a}(\varphi^{\prime}\wedge\Box\neg\bot_{a}).

Finally, we define
\phi_{\text{\rm\tiny LTLK}}=\Box\neg\bot_{0}\to\phi^{\prime}
.
∎

4
Model checking
\mathrm{SLTL}

Let
\models
be one of the five satisfaction relations defined in
Section
3.2
. The task of the
\mathrm{SLTL}
model checking problem
is as follows. Given a
\mathrm{SLTL}
structure

\mathfrak{T}=\bigl(\mathcal{T}_{0},(\mathcal{T}_{a})_{a\in\mathit{Ag}}\bigr)

and an
\mathrm{SLTL}
formula
\phi
, decide whether

\mathfrak{T}\models\phi
.

We present a generic model checking algorithm for the five semantics,
which can be seen as an adaption of the standard CTL*-like model checking
procedure
EmerSistla-CTLstar-MC-InfCon84
;
Emerson-Lei-87

and its variant for LTLK and CTL*K under the perfect-recall semantics

MC-LTLK-and-beyond-2024
.
The essential idea is an inductive approach to compute
deterministic finite automata (DFA)

\mathcal{D}_{\chi}
for all standpoint subformulas
\chi=\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi

of
\phi
.
The automaton for
\chi
, called history-DFA, represents the language
of all histories
h\in(2^{P})^{+}
with

(*,h)\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\chi}\chi

where
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\chi}
stands for one of the satisfaction
relations

\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm step}}
,
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm pobs}}
,
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm public}}
,

\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm decr}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle Q}
or
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm incr}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle Q}

with
Q\in\{Q_{\chi}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\tiny\text{\rm decr}}},Q_{\chi}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\tiny\text{\rm incr}}}\}
as in Remark
3.6
.

The inductive computation corresponds to a bottom-up approach where the innermost standpoint subformulas, i.e.,
subformulas
\chi=\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\psi
where

\psi
is an LTL formula, are treated first.
When computing
\mathcal{D}_{\chi}
for
a subformula
\chi=\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\psi
where
\psi
contains
standpoint modalities,
we may assume that history-DFA
for the maximal standpoint subformulas
\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{k}

of
\psi
have been computed before.

Preprocessing for
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm decr}}
and
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm incr}}
.

When dealing with the decremental and incremental semantics,
our iterative bottom-up approach requires the computation of the relevant parameters
Q\subseteq P
of
the satisfaction relations
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm decr}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle Q}
and
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm incr}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle Q}

over which subformulas of
\phi
are interpreted.
This can be done in time linear in the length of
\phi
by a top-down analysis
of the syntax tree of
\phi

(see Remark
3.6
).
In what follows, we shall write
Q_{\chi}
to denote
the corresponding subset
Q_{\chi}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\tiny\text{\rm decr}}}
or
Q_{\chi}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\tiny\text{\rm incr}}}
, respectively,
of
P
for (an occurrence of) a
subformula
\chi
.

Thus, if
\chi
is
not in the scope of a standpoint modality
or a maximal standpoint subformulas of
\phi
then

Q_{\chi}=P
under the decremental semantics and

Q_{\chi}=\varnothing
under the incremental semantics.
If
\chi=\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\psi
is (an occurrence) of a
standpoint subformula of
\phi
and

\chi^{\prime}
a subformula of
\psi
that – as a subformula of
\psi
– is
not in the scope of a standpoint modality or
a maximal standpoint subformula of
\psi
then

Q_{\chi^{\prime}}=Q_{\chi}\cap P_{a}
for the decremental semantics
and
Q_{\chi^{\prime}}=Q_{\chi}\cup P_{a}
for the incremental semantics.

Observation sets
\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}
.

Our algorithm
uses subsets
\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}\subseteq P
for the standpoint subformulas

\chi=\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi
of
\phi
, whose
definition
depends on the considered semantics:

\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}=\varnothing
for
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm step}}
,

\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}=P_{a}
for
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm pobs}}
,

\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}=P
for
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm public}}
,

\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}=Q_{\chi}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\tiny\text{\rm decr}}}\cap P_{a}
for
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm decr}}

and
\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}=Q_{\chi}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\tiny\text{\rm incr}}}\cup P_{a}
for
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm incr}}
.
Let
\mathit{obs}_{\chi}:(2^{P})^{*}\to(2^{\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}})^{*}

be the induced observation function

\mathit{obs}_{\chi}(h)=h|_{\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}}
.

Transition systems
\mathcal{T}_{a}^{R}
.

From
\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}
we will derive a superset
R=R_{\chi}=P_{a}\cup\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}
of
P_{a}

and switch from
\mathcal{T}_{a}
to a transition system
\mathcal{T}_{a}^{R}
over
R

that behaves as

\mathcal{T}_{a}
, but makes nondeterministic guesses for the truth values
of propositions in
R\setminus P_{a}
for the starting state, as well as
in every step of a computation.
So, the states of
\mathcal{T}_{a}^{R}
are pairs

(s,O)
with
s\in S_{a}
and
O\subseteq R
such that

L_{a}(s)=O\cap P_{a}
.
For the precise definition of
\mathcal{T}_{a}^{R}
, see
Definition
C.1
in the appendix.
The switch from
\mathcal{T}_{a}
to
\mathcal{T}_{a}^{R}

will be needed to deal with the constraints

\mathit{last}(h^{\prime})=\mathit{first}(f^{\prime})
in the definitions of

\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm public}}{}
and
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm incr}}
.

Remark 4.1
.

If
R=P_{a}
then
\mathcal{T}_{a}^{R}
and
\mathcal{T}_{a}
are isomorphic, so the switch from

\mathcal{T}_{a}
to
\mathcal{T}_{a}^{R}
is obsolete. (Note that then
O=L_{a}(s)
for all states
(s,O)
in
\mathcal{T}_{a}^{R}
.)
This applies to
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm step}}
where
\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}=\varnothing
,

\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm pobs}}
where
\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}=P_{a}
,
and
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm decr}}
where

\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}\subseteq P_{a}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\chi
be a standpoint subformula of
\phi
.
A history-DFA for
\chi
is a DFA
\mathcal{D}
over the alphabet
2^{P}

such that

(1)

The language of
\mathcal{D}
is

\{h\in(2^{P})^{+}:(*,h)\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\chi}\chi\}
.

(2)

\mathcal{D}
is
\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}
-deterministic
in the following sense:
Whenever
h_{1},h_{2}\in(2^{P})^{+}
such that

h_{1}|_{\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}}=h_{2}|_{\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}}
then

\delta_{\chi}(\mathit{init}_{\chi},h_{1})=\delta_{\chi}(\mathit{init}_{\chi},h_{2})
, where
\delta_{\chi}
denotes the transition relation of
\mathcal{D}
.

Generic
\mathrm{SLTL}
model checking algorithm

Basis of induction.

In the basis of induction we are given a standpoint formula

\chi=\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi
with
\varphi
an LTL formula.
Let
\mathcal{T}_{\chi}=\mathcal{T}_{a}^{R}
where
R=R_{\chi}=P_{a}\cup\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}
.
We apply a mild variant of standard LTL model checking techniques

VarWolp-LICS86
;
VarWolp-InfComp94
;
BK08
;
CGKPV18

(see Section
C.2
in the appendix)
to compute the set
\mathit{Sat}_{\mathcal{T}_{a}^{R}}(\exists\varphi)

of states
(s,O)
in
\mathcal{T}_{a}^{R}
for which
there is a
f\in\mathit{Traces}^{P}(\mathcal{T}_{a}^{R},(s,O))
with

f\models_{\text{\rm\tiny LTL}}\varphi
.
We then use a powerset construction
applied to
\mathcal{T}_{a}^{R}
(see Definition
4.3
below) to construct an
\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}
-deterministic
history-DFA
\mathcal{D}_{\chi}
.

Step of induction.

Suppose now

\chi=\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi
and
\varphi
has standpoint subformulas.
Let
\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{k}
be the maximal standpoint subformulas of
\varphi
,
say
\chi_{i}=\langle\!\!\langle b_{i}\rangle\!\!\rangle\psi_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,k
, and let

\mathcal{D}_{1}=\mathcal{D}_{\chi_{1}},\ldots,\mathcal{D}_{k}=\mathcal{D}_{\chi_{k}}
be their history-DFAs
over

2^{P}
.
Let
\mathcal{D}_{i}=(X_{i},\delta_{i},\mathit{init}_{i},F_{i})
and
\mathfrak{O}_{i}=\mathfrak{O}_{\chi_{i}}
. Further, let
R_{\chi} =P_{a}\cup\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}
.
We consider the transition system

\mathcal{T}_{\chi}=\mathcal{T}_{a}^{R_{\chi}}\bowtie\mathcal{D}_{1}\bowtie\ldots\bowtie\mathcal{D}_{k}

that is obtained by putting
\mathcal{T}_{a}^{R_{\chi}}
in parallel to
the product of the
\mathcal{D}_{i}
’s.
For this, we introduce pairwise distinct, fresh atomic propositions

p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}
for each of the
\chi_{i}
’s and define the components

\mathcal{T}_{\chi}=(Z_{\chi},\to_{\chi},\mathit{Init}_{\chi},\mathcal{P}_{\chi},L_{\chi})

as follows (where we write
R
instead of
R_{\chi}
).
The state space is

Z_{\chi}\ =\ S_{a}^{R}\times X_{1}\times\ldots\times X_{k}
.
The transition relation satisfies:

((s,O),x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\to_{\chi}((s^{\prime},O^{\prime}),x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{k}^{\prime})

iff there are
H,H^{\prime}\subseteq P
such that

H\cap\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}=H^{\prime}\cap\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}
,

H\cap R=O
,
(s,O)\to_{a}^{R}(s^{\prime},O^{\prime})

and

x_{i}^{\prime}=\delta_{i}(x_{i},H^{\prime})
for
i=1,\ldots,k
.
The set of atomic propositions is
\mathcal{P}_{\chi}=R\cup\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}\}
.
The labeling function
L_{\chi}:Z_{\chi}\to 2^{\mathcal{P}_{\chi}}
is given by

L_{\chi}((s,O),x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\cap R=O

and

p_{i}\in L_{\chi}(s,x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
iff
x_{i}\in F_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,k
.
Lastly,
\mathit{Init}_{\chi}
contains all states

((\mathit{init}_{a},O),x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
such that

(\mathit{init}_{a},O)\in\mathit{Init}_{a}^{R}
(i.e.,
O\cap P_{a}=L_{a}(\mathit{init}_{a})
)
and
there exists
H^{\prime}\subseteq P

with
x_{i}=\delta_{i}(\mathit{init}_{i},H^{\prime})
for
i=1,\ldots,k
and

O\cap\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}=H^{\prime}\cap\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}
.

We now replace
\varphi
with the LTL formula

\varphi^{\prime}\ =\ \varphi[\chi_{1}/p_{1},\ldots,\chi_{k}/p_{k}]

over
\mathcal{P}=P\cup\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}\}
that results
from
\varphi
by syntactically
replacing the maximal standpoint subformulas
\chi_{i}
of
\varphi
with
p_{i}
.
We then apply standard LTL model checking techniques
(see Section
C.2
in the appendix)
to compute
\mathit{Sat}_{\mathcal{T}_{\chi}}(\exists\varphi^{\prime})=\{z\in Z_{\chi}:\exists f\in\mathit{Traces}^{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{T}_{\chi},z)\text{ s.t. }f\models_{\text{\rm\tiny LTL}}\varphi^{\prime}\}
.

Having computed
\mathit{Sat}_{\mathcal{T}_{\chi}}(\exists\varphi^{\prime})
,
the atomic propositions
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}
are no longer needed.
We therefore switch from
\mathcal{T}_{\chi}
to

\mathcal{T}_{\chi}^{\prime}=\mathcal{T}_{\chi}|_{R}

which agrees with
\mathcal{T}_{\chi}
except that
\mathcal{P}_{\chi}^{\prime}=R
and

L_{\chi}^{\prime}(s,x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=L_{\chi}(s,x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\cap R
.
By applying a powerset construction to
\mathcal{T}_{\chi}^{\prime}
we get
an
\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}
-deterministic history DFA
\mathcal{D}_{\chi}

for the language
\{h\in(2^{P})^{+}:(*,h)\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\chi}\chi\}

(see Definition
4.3
below,
applied to
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}_{\chi}^{\prime}
,

\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}
and
the set
U_{\chi}=\mathit{Sat}_{\mathcal{T}_{\chi}}(\exists\varphi^{\prime})
for the declaration
of the final states in
\mathcal{D}_{\chi}
).

Final step.

After treating all maximal standpoint subformulas of
\phi
,
we need to check whether
\phi
holds for all traces
of
\mathcal{T}_{0}
. This is done via similar techniques as in the first part
of the step of induction, i.e., we build the product
\mathcal{T}_{\phi}

of
\mathcal{T}_{0}
with the history-DFAs that have been
constructed for the maximal standpoint subformulas of
\phi
and
replace them with fresh atomic propositions, yielding an LTL formula

\phi^{\prime}
over an extension of
P
. We then apply
standard LTL model checking techniques to check whether all traces
of
\mathcal{T}_{\phi}
satisfy
\phi^{\prime}
.
This can be done using an algorithm that is polynomially space-bounded
in the size of
\mathcal{T}_{\phi}
and the length of
\phi^{\prime}
(and
\phi
).

Computation of history-DFA

The history-DFA
\mathcal{D}_{\chi}

for standpoint subformulas
\chi=\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi

can be obtained by applying a
powerset construction to the transition system

\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}_{\chi}
over
R=R_{\chi}
.
This construction is an adaption of the classical powerset construction
for nondeterministic finite automata and can be seen as a one-agent variant
of the powerset construction introduced by Reif
Reif-JCSS84

for partial-information
two-player games and its variant for CTL*K model checking

MC-LTLK-and-beyond-2024
.
The acceptance condition in
\mathcal{D}_{\chi}
is derived from
the set
U=\mathit{Sat}_{\mathcal{T}}(\exists\varphi)
in the basis of induction and

U=\mathit{Sat}_{\mathcal{T}}(\exists\varphi^{\prime})
in the step of induction.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{T}=(S,\to,\mathit{Init},R,L)

with
R\subseteq P
,

\mathfrak{O}\subseteq P
an observation set,
and
U\subseteq S
.
Then,

\mathit{pow}(\mathcal{T},\mathfrak{O},U)

is the following
DFA
\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{D}},\delta_{\mathcal{D}},\mathit{init}_{\mathcal{D}},F_{\mathcal{D}})
over the alphabet
2^{P}
.
The state space is
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{D}}=2^{S}\cup\{\mathit{init}_{\mathcal{D}}\}
.
The transition function
\delta_{\mathcal{D}}:\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{D}}\times 2^{P}\to\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{D}}
is
given by (where
x\in 2^{S}
):

\delta_{\mathcal{D}}(x,H)
=

\bigr\{s^{\prime}\in S:\text{ there exists }s\in x\text{ with }

s\to s^{\prime}

and

L(s^{\prime})\cap\mathfrak{O}=H\cap\mathfrak{O}\bigr\}

\delta_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathit{init}_{\mathcal{D}},H)
=

\bigl\{s\in\mathit{Init}:L(s)\cap\mathfrak{O}=H\cap\mathfrak{O}\bigr\}

The set of final states is

F_{\mathcal{D}}=\bigl\{x\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{D}}:x\cap U\not=\varnothing\bigr\}
.

For the soundness proof, see
Section
C.3
in the Appendix.

5
Complexity-theoretic results

At first glance the time complexity of
our generic model checking algorithm
is
d
-fold exponential for

\mathrm{SLTL}
formulas
\varphi
where
d
is the maximal nesting depth of standpoint modalities in
\varphi
.
This yields a nonelementary upper bound for the

\mathrm{SLTL}
model checking problem.

For
*\in\{\text{\rm step},\text{\rm pobs},\text{\rm public},\text{\rm decr},\text{\rm incr}\}
, let
\mathrm{SLTL}^{*}
denote
\mathrm{SLTL}
under the semantics w.r.t.
\models_{*}
.
We now investigate the complexity
of the different
\mathrm{SLTL}^{*}
model checking problems.

Bounds on the sizes of history-DFA and
time bounds

Obviously, it suffices to construct the reachable fragment of the
default history-DFA. Furthermore, we may apply a
standard poly-time minimization
algorithm to the default history-DFA. Thus, to establish better complexity
bounds it suffices to provide elementary
bounds on the sizes of minimal history-DFA
for standpoint subformulas.
Let us start with a simple observation on how to exploit the property that
the history-DFA
\mathcal{D}_{\chi}
are
\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}
-deterministic.

Remark 5.1
.

With the notations of the step of induction for
\chi=\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi
,
the history-DFA
\mathcal{D}_{\chi}
for
\chi
is obtained by
(the reachable fragment) of
\mathit{pow}(\mathcal{T}_{\chi}^{\prime},\mathfrak{O}_{\chi},U_{\chi})
where

U_{\chi}=\mathit{Sat}_{\mathcal{T}_{\chi}}(\exists\varphi^{\prime})
.
Recall that
\mathcal{T}_{\chi}^{\prime}
has been defined via a product construction

\mathcal{T}_{a}^{R}\bowtie\mathcal{D}_{1}\bowtie\ldots\bowtie\mathcal{D}_{k}
where the
\mathcal{D}_{j}
’s
are history-DFAs for the maximal subformulas
\chi_{j}=\langle\!\!\langle b_{j}\rangle\!\!\rangle\psi_{j}

of
\varphi
.

Suppose now that
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
such that
\mathcal{D}_{i}

is
\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}
-deterministic.
Then, for all histories
h\in(2^{P})^{+}
and states

((s,O),x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})

in
\mathit{Reach}(\mathcal{T}_{\chi}^{\prime},h)
we have

x_{i}=\delta_{i}(\mathit{init}_{i},h)
.
Hence, if
x
is a reachable state in
\mathcal{D}
,
say
x=\delta_{\mathcal{D}_{\chi}}(\mathit{init}_{\mathcal{D}},h)
, then for all states

((s,O),x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in x
we have

x_{i}=\delta_{i}(\mathit{init}_{i},h)
.
Thus, we may redefine the default history-DFA
\mathcal{D}_{\chi}
as a product

\mathit{pow}(\mathcal{T}_{a}^{R}\bowtie\prod_{j\not=i}\mathcal{D}_{j},\mathfrak{O}_{\chi})\bowtie\mathcal{D}_{i}

rather than a powerset construction of

\mathcal{T}_{a}^{R}\bowtie\mathcal{D}_{1}\bowtie\ldots\bowtie\mathcal{D}_{k}
.
From now on, we write
\mathit{pow}(\mathcal{T},\mathfrak{O})
for the
structure
(\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{D}},\delta_{\mathcal{D}},\mathit{init}_{\mathcal{D}})
defined as in
Definition
4.3
. The acceptance condition

F_{\mathcal{D}_{\chi}}

is (re)defined as the set of all states
(x,x_{i})

in the above product (i.e.,
x
is a subset of the state space of

\mathcal{T}_{a}^{R}\bowtie\prod_{j\not=i}\mathcal{D}_{j}

and
x_{i}
a state in
\mathcal{D}_{i}
) such that

\{(\xi,x_{i}):\xi\in x\}\cap U_{\chi}\neq\varnothing
.
Let
I
denote the set of indices
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
where
the history-DFA for
\chi_{i}=\langle\!\!\langle b_{i}\rangle\!\!\rangle\psi_{i}

is
\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}
-deterministic.
W.l.o.g.
I=\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
.
Then, we can think of
\mathcal{D}_{\chi}
as (the reachable
fragment of) a product

\mathit{pow}(\mathcal{T}_{a}^{R}\bowtie\mathcal{D}_{\ell+1}\bowtie\ldots\bowtie\mathcal{D}_{k},\mathfrak{O}_{\chi})\bowtie\mathcal{D}_{1}\bowtie\ldots\bowtie\mathcal{D}_{\ell}
.

If
\chi=\langle\!\!\langle a\rangle\!\!\rangle\varphi
then

\mathfrak{O}_{\chi_{i}}=\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}

for all maximal standpoint subformulas
\chi_{i}=\langle\!\!\langle b_{i}\rangle\!\!\rangle\psi_{i}

of
\varphi
where
a=b_{i}
.
Thus, by induction on
d=\mathit{ad}(\chi)
,
Remark
5.1

yields that
the size of the reachable fragment of the default
history-DFA for a standpoint formula

\chi
is
d
-fold exponentially bounded. Hence:

Lemma 5.2
.

Under all five semantics,
the time complexity of the
algorithm of Section
4

is at most
d
-fold exponential when
d=\mathit{ad}(\phi)
.

This
d
-fold exponential upper time bound will now be improved
using further consequences of
Remark
5.1
.

We start with
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm step}}
.
Here, we have
\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}=\varnothing
for all
\chi
.
With the notations used in Remark
5.1
,
the history-DFA
\mathcal{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{D}_{k}
are

\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}
-deterministic. Thus, we can think of

\mathcal{D}_{\chi}

As a consequence, the default history-DFA

can be redefined for

(See
Lemma

in
the appendix for details.)

\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm public}}

(where
\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}=P
for all
\chi
) and
for

(where
\mathfrak{O}_{\chi_{i}}\subseteq\mathfrak{O}_{\chi}

for all standpoint subformulas

of
\chi
).
In the case of

there are history-DFA
where the state space
is contained in
(\prod_{b\in\mathit{Ag}(\chi)}2^{S_{b}})\times 2^{P}
.
For
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm decr}}
, we assign history-DFA
\mathcal{D}_{v}
to the nodes
in the syntax tree of

\prod_{w}\mathit{pow}(\mathcal{T}_{a_{w}},\mathfrak{O}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\tiny\text{\rm decr}}}_{w})

ranges
over all nodes in the syntax subtree of

such that the formula given by

.
(See
Lemma

and Lemma

in the appendix.)
For
\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm pobs}}
under the additional assumption
that

for

with

\mathcal{T}_{\chi}

to obtain history-DFA
\mathcal{D}_{\chi}
for

where the state space is contained in

.
(See Lemma

in the appendix.)

With these observations, we obtain (where we assume
the above mentioned simplification of the default-history DFAs):

Lemma 5.3
.

For

Definition B.1

(Alternation depth of
\mathrm{SLTL}

\mathit{ad}(\mathit{true})=\mathit{ad}(p)=0

Lemma B.4

(Cf. Lemma
3.8
)
.

Given a pair
(\mathfrak{T},\phi)
consisting of a
\mathrm{SLTL}
structure

\mathfrak{T}=(\mathcal{T}_{0},(\mathcal{T}_{a})_{a\in\mathit{Ag}})

\mathrm{SLTL}

,

\mathfrak{T}_{\text{\rm\tiny LTLK}}=(\mathcal{T}^{\prime},(\sim_{a})_{a\in\mathit{Ag}})

and an LTLK formula

such that:

(1)

\mathfrak{T}\models_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm pobs}}\phi
if and only if

\mathfrak{T}_{\text{\rm\tiny LTLK}}\models_{\text{\rm\tiny LTLK}}\phi_{\text{\rm\tiny LTLK}}

(2)

\phi
and
\phi_{\text{\rm\tiny LTLK}}
have the same alternation depth.

Proof.

We may assume w.l.o.g. that the labelings of the initial states
in
\mathcal{T}_{0}
and the
\mathcal{T}_{a}
’s are consistent in the sense that

L_{0}(\mathit{init}_{0})\cap P_{a}=L_{a}(\mathit{init}_{a})
for each

.

\mathcal{T}_{a}
for

by
a fresh initial state
\mathit{init}_{a}^{\prime}
with
L_{a}(\mathit{init}_{a}^{\prime})=\varnothing
and

\mathit{init}_{a}^{\prime}\to_{a}\mathit{init}_{a}
and replace the given
\mathrm{SLTL}

\bigcirc\phi

For each

\mathcal{T}_{a}

\mathcal{T}_{a}^{\bot}
which extends

\mathcal{T}_{a}
by states
\bot_{a}^{Q}
for

The state space of
\mathcal{T}_{a}^{\bot}
is

\mathit{init}_{a}
.
The transition relation of
\mathcal{T}_{a}^{\bot}

by:

•

in
\mathcal{T}_{a}

•

The set of atomic propositions in

is

The labeling of the original states is unchanged, i.e.,

\mathrm{SLTL}

Definition C.1

Lemma C.2
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a set of atomic propositions and
R\subseteq\mathcal{P}
.
Given a transition system
\mathcal{T}=(S,\to,\mathit{Init},R,L)
and
an LTL formula
\varphi
over
\mathcal{P}
,
the set

\mathit{Sat}_{\mathcal{T}}(\exists\varphi)\ =\ \bigl\{s\in S:\exists f\in\mathit{Traces}^{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{T},s)\text{ s.t. }f\models_{\text{\rm\tiny LTL}}\varphi\bigr\}

length of
\varphi
and polynomial in the size of
\mathcal{T}
.
Furthermore, the problem to decide whether

is in
\mathit{PSPACE}
.

Proof.

We apply standard techniques to
build a nondeterministic Büchi automaton (NBA)

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{\psi}=(Y,\delta_{\mathcal{A}},\mathit{init}_{\mathcal{A}},F)

for
\psi
over the alphabet
2^{\mathcal{P}}
. That is, the accepted language

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A})
of
\mathcal{A}
are the infinite words
f\in 2^{\mathcal{P}}
such that
f\models_{\text{\rm\tiny LTL}}\psi
.
We now consider the product

\mathcal{T}\bowtie\mathcal{A}=(S^{\prime},\to^{\prime}\nolinebreak,\mathit{Init}^{\prime},Y,L^{\prime})

defined as follows:

•

The state space
S^{\prime}
is
S\times Y
.

•

The transition relation
{\to}^{\prime}\,\subseteq\,(S\times Y)^{2}
is defined
as follows.
If
(s,y),(s^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in S\times Y
then

(s,y)\to^{\prime}(s^{\prime},y^{\prime})
iff
there exists
H\subseteq\mathcal{P}

such that

,
H\cap R=L(s)
and
y^{\prime}\in\delta_{\mathcal{A}}(y,H)
.

•

The labels of the states are mostly irrelevant, we only
need to distinguish states

(s,y)
where
y\in F
from those where
y\notin F
.
Formally, we can deal with the state space of
\mathcal{A}
as atomic propositions
and
the labeling function

given by

•

The set of initial states is

where for

\mathit{Init}_{\mathcal{T}\bowtie\mathcal{A}}(s)\ =\ \bigl\{\ (s,y)\ :\ \exists H\subseteq\mathcal{P}\text{ s.t. }H\cap R=L(s),\ y\in\delta_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathit{init}_{\mathcal{A}},H)\ \bigr\}
.

We then apply standard graph algorithms (backward search from the SCCs)
to determine the set

consisting of all states

(s,y)
in
\mathit{Init}^{\prime}

that have a path visiting

S\times F
infinitely often.
Then,
\mathit{Sat}_{\mathcal{T}}(\exists\varphi)
is the set
of all states

in
\mathcal{T}

with

\mathit{Init}_{\mathcal{T}\bowtie\mathcal{A}}(s)\cap\mathit{Sat}_{\mathcal{T}\bowtie\mathcal{A}}(\exists\Box\Diamond F)\neq\varnothing
.
Thus,
\mathit{Sat}_{\mathcal{T}}(\exists\varphi)

is computable with known techniques
in time exponential in the length of
\varphi
and polynomial in the
size of
\mathcal{T}
.
The polynomial space bound for the decision problem
can be obtained with the same techniques
as for standard automata-based
LTL model checking
[
20
,
21
]
.

D.2

In Lemma

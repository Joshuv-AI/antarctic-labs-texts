Introduction

Let
G
be a finite
simple
graph, i.e.,
G
does not contain loops nor multiple edges. Let
V(G)
and
E(G)
denote the vertex and edge sets of
G
, respectively. Let
\varkappa(G)
denote the
vertex connectivity
of
G
. That is,
\varkappa(G)
is the minimum cardinality of a subset
W\subset V(G)
for which the induced subgraph
G|_{V(G)\setminus W}
is disconnected. The present paper studies the
spectrum
of the vertex connectivity of
G
, when
G
is a
chordal
graph. Precisely, we investigate the set of all possible values that
\varkappa(G)
may take when
G
is a chordal graph on
n
vertices, for a fixed positive integer
n
. This is inspired by the authors’ recent study
[
3
]
of resolution of edge ideals of finite graphs.

A vertex
x\in V(G)
is called
universal
if
\{x,y\}\in E(G)
for all
y\in V(G)
with
y\neq x
. When discussing the vertex connectivity of
G
, one can assume that
G
has no universal vertex. In fact, if
x\in V(G)
is universal, then
\varkappa(G)=\varkappa(G|_{V(G)\setminus\{x\}})+1
.

A
vertex cover
of
G
is a subset
C\subset V(G)
for which
e\cap C\neq\emptyset
for all
e\in E(G)
. Let
\tau_{\max}(G)
denote the maximum size of minimal vertex covers of
G
. It is shown
[
1
, Theorem 2.2]
that
\tau_{\max}(G)\geq\lceil 2\sqrt{n}-2\rceil
, where
n=|V(G)|
.

In combinatorics on finite graphs, invariants
\varkappa(G)
and
\tau_{\max}(G)
appear to be fundamentally distinct. However, our work shows that, in certain restricted settings, the syzygy theory of monomial ideals in commutative algebra establishes an explicit relationship between these invariants.

A chordal graph
G
is called chordal
∗
if
G
has no universal vertex. When
G
is a chordal
∗
graph on
n
vertices, in Theorem
1.1
, we deduce from the aforementioned bound
\tau_{\max}(G^{c})\geq\lceil 2\sqrt{n}-2\rceil
, where
G^{c}
is the complementary graph of
G
, that

(1)

\displaystyle 0\leq\varkappa(G)\leq(n-1)-\lceil 2\sqrt{n}-2\,\rceil.

Furthermore, given an integer
0\leq\varkappa\leq(n-1)-\lceil 2\sqrt{n}-2\,\rceil
, we construct a chordal
∗
graph
G
on
n
vertices satisfying
\varkappa(G)=\varkappa
.

For a more general graph on
n
vertices containing no universal vertices, the vertex connectivity could be bigger than
(n-1)-\lceil 2\sqrt{n}-2\,\rceil
. For example, if
n
is even and if
G_{n}
is a finite graph obtained by removing the edges
\{1,2\},\{3,4\},\ldots,\{n-1,n\}
from the complete graph
K_{n}
on
\{1,\ldots,n\}
, then
\varkappa(G_{n})=n-2
. If
n
is odd and if
H_{n}
is a finite graph by adding the edges
\{2,n\},\{3,n\},\ldots,\{n-1,n\}
to
G_{n-1}
, then
\varkappa(H_{n})=n-3
. Thus, our bound (
1
) is special for chordal graphs.

Our proof of (
1
) is heavily based on the syzygy theory of monomial ideals in commutative algebra. Our technique has its origin in
[
6
,
7
]
, where the connectivity of comparability graphs of distributive lattices and of modular lattices is studied. Furthermore, in
[
7
]
, a short proof of Barnette’s theorem that the connectivity of the graph of a simplicial
(d-1)
-sphere with
d\geq 2
is at least
d
was given.

1.
Connectivity of chordal
∗
graphs

The background of commutative algebra required in the proof of Theorem
1.1
can be found in
[
5
, Chapter 9]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a chordal
\,{}^{*}
graph on
n
vertices and
G^{c}
the complementary graph of
G
. One has

(2)

\displaystyle\varkappa(G)+\tau_{\max}(G^{c})\leq n-1

and

0\leq\varkappa(G)\leq(n-1)-\lceil 2\sqrt{n}-2\,\rceil.

Furthermore, given an integer
0\leq\varkappa\leq(n-1)-\lceil 2\sqrt{n}-2\,\rceil
, there exists a chordal
\,{}^{*}
graph
G
on
n
vertices satisfying
\varkappa(G)=\varkappa
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta(G)
denote the clique complex (
[
5
, p. 155]
) of
G
. Let
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
denote the polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
K
and
I(G^{c})
the edge ideal (
[
5
, p. 156]
) of
G^{c}
. The Stanley–Reisner ring
S/I_{\Delta(G)}
(
[
5
, p. 132]
) of
\Delta(G)
coincides with the quotient ring
S/I(G^{c})
. Since
G
is chordal
∗
, it follows that
G^{c}
possesses no isolated vertex. Hence
\operatorname{proj\,dim}(S/I(G^{c}))\geq\tau_{\max}(G^{c})\geq\lceil 2\sqrt{n}-2\,\rceil
(
[
3
, Corollary 4.2]
). Furthermore, since
S/I(G^{c})
has linear resolution (
[
5
, Theorem 9.2.3]
), it follows that
\operatorname{proj\,dim}(S/I(G^{c}))
is equal to the biggest integer
i
for which
\beta_{i,i+1}(S/I(G^{c}))\neq 0
. Now,
[
7
, Lemma 2.1]
guarantees that
\varkappa(G)
is the biggest integer
i
with
\beta_{n-i,n-i+1}(S/I(G^{c}))=0
. Hence,

(3)

\displaystyle n-\varkappa(G)=\operatorname{proj\,dim}(S/I(G^{c}))+1\geq\tau_{\max}(G^{c})+1\geq\lceil 2\sqrt{n}-2\,\rceil+1,

as desired.

In
[
3
, Theorem 5.5]
, given an integer
\lceil 2\sqrt{n}-2\,\rceil\leq j\leq n-1
, a gapfree chordal graph
\Gamma_{j}
on
n
vertices satisfying
\operatorname{proj\,dim}(S/I(\Gamma_{j}))=j
is constructed. (Recall that a
gapfree
graph is a connected graph
G
for which if
e
and
e^{\prime}
are edges of
G
with
e\cap e^{\prime}=\emptyset
, then there is an edge
f
of
G
with
e\cap f\neq\emptyset
and
e^{\prime}\cap f\neq\emptyset
.) It follows from
[
4
, Corollary 6.9]
that
(\Gamma_{j})^{c}
is a chordal
∗
graph and, by (
3
), one has
\varkappa((\Gamma_{j})^{c})=(n-1)-j
. Thus, given an integer
0\leq\varkappa\leq(n-1)-\lceil 2\sqrt{n}-2\,\rceil
, the chordal
\,{}^{*}
graph
G=(\Gamma_{(n-1)-\varkappa})^{c}
on
n
vertices satisfies
\varkappa(G)=\varkappa
.
∎

It is unclear if the relationship (
2
) between
\tau_{\max}(G^{c})
and
\varkappa(G)
for a chordal
∗
graph
G
appears in the literature on finite graphs.

Example 1.2
.

Let
C_{n}
denote the cycle of length
n\geq 4
. Since
\varkappa(C_{n})=2
and
\tau_{\max}((C_{n})^{c})=n-2
, one has
\varkappa(C_{n})+\tau_{\max}((C_{n})^{c})=n>n-1
.

On the other hand, the
algebraic connectivity
of
G
, denoted by
a(G)
, was first introduced by Fiedler in
[
2
]
and has been much studied. A classical result of Fiedler
[
2
, 4.1]
states that if
G
is not a complete graph, then
a(G)\leq\varkappa(G)
. Thus, as an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
G
be a chordal
\,{}^{*}
graph on
n
vertices. Then,

0\leq a(G)\leq(n-1)-\lceil 2\sqrt{n}-2\rceil.

It would be of interest to find all possible
0\leq a\leq(n-1)-\lceil 2\sqrt{n}-2\rceil
which can be the algebraic connectivity of a chordal
∗
graph on
n
vertices.

2.

It is a reasonable problem to classify all chordal
∗
graphs
G
on
n
vertices satisfying
\varkappa(G)=(n-1)-\lceil 2\sqrt{n}-2\,\rceil
.

Example 2.1
.

Let
n=6
. Then
(n-1)-\lceil 2\sqrt{n}-2\,\rceil=2
. Each chordal
∗
graph
G
of Figure
1
satisfies
\varkappa(G)=2
.

In particular, given an integer
n\geq 2
, a chordal
∗
graphs
G
on
n
vertices satisfying
\varkappa(G)=(n-1)-\lceil 2\sqrt{n}-2\,\rceil
is, in general, not unique. However,

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
n\geq 4
be a perfect square. Then a chordal
∗
graph on
n
vertices satisfying
\varkappa(G)=(n-1)-\lceil 2\sqrt{n}-2\,\rceil
exists uniquely.

Proof.

The existence follows from Theorem
1.1
. A chordal
∗
graph on
4
vertices satisfying
\varkappa(G)=1
is only the path graph on
4
vertices. Suppose that a chordal
∗
graph
G
on
n\geq 9
vertices satisfies
\varkappa(G)=(n-1)-\lceil 2\sqrt{n}-2\,\rceil
. It then follows from (
3
), that
\operatorname{proj\,dim}(S/I(G^{c}))=\tau_{\max}(G)=\lceil 2\sqrt{n}-2\,\rceil
. Now, it is shown
[
3
, Theorem 5.4]
that a finite graph
H
on
n
vertices satisfying
\tau_{\max}(H)=\lceil 2\sqrt{n}-2\,\rceil
exists uniquely.
∎

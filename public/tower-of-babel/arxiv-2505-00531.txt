Superintuitionistic predicate logics of linear frames: undecidability with two individual variables Thanks: The work is supported by the Basic Research Program of the HSE University.
Mikhail Rybakov

Abstract
The paper presents a solution to the long-standing question about the decidability of the two-variable fragment of the superintuitionistic predicate logic 𝐐𝐋𝐂 \mathbf{QLC} defined by the class of linear Kripke frames, which is also the ‘superintuitionistic’ fragment of the modal predicate logic 𝐐𝐒𝟒 ​ .3 \mathbf{QS4.3} , under the Gödel translation. We prove that the fragment is undecidable ( Σ 1 0 \Sigma^{0}_{1} -complete). The result remains true for the positive fragment, even with a single binary predicate letter and an infinite set of unary predicate letters. Also, we prove that the logic defined by ordinal ω \omega as a Kripke frame is not recursively enumerable (even both Σ 1 0 \Sigma^{0}_{1} -hard and Π 1 0 \Pi^{0}_{1} -hard) with the same restrictions on the language. The results remain true if we add also the constant domain condition. The proofs are based on two techniques: a modification of the method proposed by M. Marx and M. Reynolds, which allows us to describe tiling problems using natural numbers rather than pairs of numbers within an enumeration of Cantor’s, and an idea of ‘double labeling’ the elements from the domains, which allows us to use only two individual variables in the proof when applying the former method.



1
Introduction

This paper is mainly devoted to solving the open problem of the decidability of the superintuitionistic predicate logic
\mathbf{QLC}
which can be defined as the logic of linear Kripke frames, or, equivalently, as the logic obtained from the intuitionistic predicate logic
\mathbf{QInt}
by adding formulas of the form
(\varphi\to\psi)\vee(\psi\to\varphi)
as axioms, or, equivalently, as the ‘superintuitionistic’ fragment of the modal predicate logic
\mathbf{QS4.3}
; see
[
8
, Section 6.7]
for details. This logic is very close to the classical predicate logic: it contains a lot of classical principles rejected by
\mathbf{QInt}
and even by
\mathbf{QKC}
, the logic of the weak low of the excluded middle.

It is well known that the classical predicate logic
\mathbf{QCl}
is undecidable
[
6
]
, even in the language containing a single binary predicate letter and three individual variables
[
39
, Section 4.8 (ii)]
(see also
[
26
,
27
]
). Then, we readily obtain that
\mathbf{QLC}
is undecidable in this language, also. At the same time, some quite expressive fragments of
\mathbf{QCl}
are decidable: for example, the monadic fragment, even enriched by equality
[
3
, Chapter 21]
, the two-variable fragment
[
19
,
10
]
, and guarded fragments
[
9
]
; see
[
4
]
for the classical decision problem as well. Nevertheless, non-classical logics are often undecidable even in languages with only monadic predicate letters
[
15
,
18
,
21
,
22
,
25
]
or with only two individual variables
[
14
]
or even both with one-two monadic predicate letters and two individual variables
[
30
,
32
,
33
,
28
]
. There are results that provide us with decidable fragments, but they are based on quite strong restrictions on languages or semantics
[
11
,
12
,
43
,
44
,
25
,
5
,
1
,
36
,
37
]
.

Let us return to
\mathbf{QLC}
.

On the one hand, the mentioned results show us that non-classical logics can be undecidable in languages with only two individual variables. For example, the two-variable fragment of
\mathbf{QKC}
is undecidable even with a single unary predicate letter
[
30
]
. Moreover, there are results providing us with the undecidability of modal predicate logics of linear frames. So, the monadic two-variable fragment of modal predicate logic
\mathbf{QS4.3}
, whose class of Kripke frames is the same as of
\mathbf{QLC}
, is undecidable
[
14
]
, even with two unary predicate letters
[
35
]
.

On the other hand, the methods used in the mentioned papers are not applicable to
\mathbf{QLC}
. The results for
\mathbf{QS4.3}

[
14
,
35
]
are based on the fact that in Kripke semantics the heredity condition is not required for the modal predicate language; but this condition is required in the intuitionistic case. The construction for
\mathbf{QKC}

[
30
]
essencially uses the fact that Kripke frames validating
\mathbf{QKC}
can contain infinite antichains, that is impossible in Kripke frames validating
\mathbf{QLC}
. The techniques presented in the other papers are quite far from being applicable to the two-variable fragment of
\mathbf{QLC}
as well. It is also worth adding that the one-variable fragment of
\mathbf{QLC}
is decidable, that immediately follows from the decidability of the propositional bimodal logic
\mathbf{S5}\times\mathbf{S4.3}

[
7
, Theorem 6.61]
; very ineteresting close results can be found in
[
5
]
.

But the methods that allow us to prove the decidability of the mentioned fragments are not applicable to the two-variable fragment of
\mathbf{QLC}
, too. As a result, the situation with the decidability of the fragment is so unclear that X. Caicedo, G. Metcalfe, R. Rodríguez, and O. Tuyt recently noted that it ‘remains an intriguing open problem’
[
5
]
.

The author was informed about this issue by Dmitry Shkatov in a private conversation.

Here, we fix the situation.

Exactly, we prove that
\mathbf{QLC}
— and even its positive fragment — is undecidable in the language with two individual variables, a single binary predicate letter, and an infinite set of unary predicate letters. Then, we also show that the result remains true for some extensions of
\mathbf{QLC}
, in particular, for
\mathbf{QLC.cd}
, the logic of linear frames with constant domains.
The result was presented in
[
29
]
.

2
Syntax and semantics

We assume that the intuitionistic predicate language
\mathcal{L}
contains countably many individual variables, countably many predicate letters of every arity, the constant
\bot
, the binary connectives
\wedge
,
\vee
,
\to
, the quantifier symbols
\forall
and
\exists
. Formulas in
\mathcal{L}
, or
\mathcal{L}
-formulas
, as well as the symbols
\neg
and
\leftrightarrow
, are defined in the usual way; in particular,
\neg\varphi=\varphi\to\bot

and
\varphi\leftrightarrow\psi=(\varphi\to\psi)\wedge(\psi\to\varphi)
.

A
Kripke frame
is a pair
\mathfrak{F}=\langle W,R\rangle
, where
W
is a non-empty set of
possible worlds
and
R
is a binary
accessibility relation
on
W
. Let, as usual,
R(w)=\{w^{\prime}\in W:wRw^{\prime}\}
; so,
w^{\prime}\in R(w)
mean the same as
wRw^{\prime}
. If
wRw^{\prime}
holds, then we say that
w^{\prime}
is
accessible
from
w
, or that
w

sees

w^{\prime}
.
A Kripke frame
\mathfrak{F}=\langle W,R\rangle
is called
intuitionistic
if
R
is a partial order — i.e., a reflexive, transitive, and antisymmetric binary relation — on
W
. We say that a Kripke frame is
linear
if its accessibility relation is a linear order.

We consider only intuitionistic Kripke frames below.

A
Kripke frame with domains
, or, for short, an
augmented frame
, is a pair
\bm{\mathfrak{F}}=\langle\mathfrak{F},D\rangle
, where
\mathfrak{F}=\langle W,R\rangle
is a Kripke frame and
D
is a
domain function

D\colon W\to\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{D})
associating with every
w\in W
a non-empty subset of a non-empty set
\mathcal{D}
of
individuals
. The set
D(w)
, also denoted by
D_{w}
, is called
the domain of the world
w
. Sets of the form
D_{w}
are also called
local domains
of
\bm{\mathfrak{F}}
and
\mathcal{D}
is called
the global domain
of
\bm{\mathfrak{F}}
; without a loss of generality, we may assume that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{D}&=&\displaystyle\bigcup\limits_{\mathclap{w\in W}}D_{w}.\end{array}

The augmented frame
\bm{\mathfrak{F}}=\langle\mathfrak{F},D\rangle
is also denoted by
\mathfrak{F}_{D}
. We say that
\mathfrak{F}_{D}
is
based on
\mathfrak{F}
, or is
defined over
\mathfrak{F}
.

We say that an augmented frame
\mathfrak{F}_{D}
based on a Kripke frame
\mathfrak{F}=\langle W,R\rangle
satisfies the
expanding domain condition
if, for all
u,w\in W
,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}uRw&\Longrightarrow&D_{u}\subseteq D_{w};\end{array}

(2.1)

then we call
\mathfrak{F}_{D}

augmented frame with expanding domains
or, for short,
e-augmented frame
. We say that
\mathfrak{F}_{D}
satisfies the
locally constant domain condition
if, for all
u,w\in W
,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}uRw&\Longrightarrow&D_{u}=D_{w},\end{array}

(2.2)

and the
globally constant domain condition
if, for all
u,w\in W
,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}D_{u}=D_{w}.\end{array}

(2.3)

If
\mathfrak{F}_{D}
satisfies (
2.2
), then we call it
augmented frame with constant domains
or, for short,
c-augmented frame
. If
\mathfrak{F}_{D}
satisfies (
2.3
) and
\mathcal{D}
is the global domain of
\mathfrak{F}_{D}
, then, following
[
8
]
, we also denote it by
\mathfrak{F}\odot\mathcal{D}
. For convenience, sometimes we write
\langle W,R,D\rangle
for
\langle\mathfrak{F},D\rangle
with
\mathfrak{F}=\langle W,R\rangle
. We assume that all augmented frames satisfy (
2.1
), i.e., are e-augmented frames, below; if an e-augmented frame is based on an intuitionistic Kripke frame, then we call it
intuitionistic augmented frame
.

An
intuitionistic predicate Kripke model
, or simply a
Kripke model
, is a tuple
\mathfrak{M}=\langle\mathfrak{F}_{D},I\rangle
, where
\mathfrak{F}_{D}=\langle W,R,D\rangle
is an intuitionistic augmented frame and
I
is a map, called an
interpretation of predicate letters
, assigning to a world
w\in W
and an
n
-ary predicate letter
P
an
n
-ary relation
I(w,P)
on
D_{w}
and satisfying the following
heredity condition
:
for all
w,w^{\prime}\in W
and every predicate letter
P
,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}wRw^{\prime}&\Longrightarrow&I(w,P)\subseteq I(w^{\prime},P).\end{array}

We also write

\langle W,R,D,I\rangle
for
\langle\mathfrak{F}_{D},I\rangle
below.
For a Kripke model
\mathfrak{M}=\langle\mathfrak{F}_{D},I\rangle
, we say that
\mathfrak{M}
is
based on
\mathfrak{F}_{D}
and is
based on
\mathfrak{F}
.

An
assignment
in a Kripke model
\mathfrak{M}=\langle W,R,D,I\rangle
is a map
g
associating with every variable
x
an element
g(x)
of the global domain of the augmented frame
\langle W,R,D\rangle
. If
g
and
h
are assignments such that
g(y)=h(y)
whenever
y\neq x
, we write
g\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x}}{{=}}h
.

The truth of an
\mathcal{L}
-formula
\varphi
at a world
w
of an intuitionistic predicate Kripke model
\mathfrak{M}=\langle W,R,D,I\rangle
under an assignment
g
is defined recursively:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathfrak{M},w\models^{g}P(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})&\leftrightharpoons&\parbox{235.20581pt}{$\langle g(x_{1}),\ldots,g(x_{n})\rangle\in P^{I,w}$,}\\
\end{array}

where
P
is an
n
-ary predicate letter;

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\parbox{87.63855pt}{{}\hfill\parbox{64.74725pt}{$\mathfrak{M},w\not\models^{g}\bot;$}}\\
\parbox{87.63855pt}{{}\hfill\parbox{64.74725pt}{$\mathfrak{M},w\models^{g}\varphi^{\prime}\wedge\varphi^{\prime\prime}$}}&\leftrightharpoons&\parbox[t]{118.49464pt}{$\mathfrak{M},w\models^{g}\varphi^{\prime}$ and $\mathfrak{M},w\models^{g}\varphi^{\prime\prime}$;}\\
\parbox{87.63855pt}{{}\hfill\parbox{64.74725pt}{$\mathfrak{M},w\models^{g}\varphi^{\prime}\vee\varphi^{\prime\prime}$}}&\leftrightharpoons&\parbox[t]{118.49464pt}{$\mathfrak{M},w\models^{g}\varphi^{\prime}$\hfill or\hfill$\mathfrak{M},w\models^{g}\varphi^{\prime\prime}$;}\\
\parbox{87.63855pt}{{}\hfill\parbox{64.74725pt}{$\mathfrak{M},w\models^{g}\varphi^{\prime}\to\varphi^{\prime\prime}$}}&\leftrightharpoons&\parbox[t]{235.20581pt}{$\mathfrak{M},\parbox{7.42825pt}{$v$}\models^{g}\varphi^{\prime}$ implies $\mathfrak{M},v\models^{g}\varphi^{\prime\prime}$, for every $v\in R(w)$;}\\
\parbox{87.63855pt}{{}\hfill\parbox{64.74725pt}{$\mathfrak{M},w\models^{g}\forall x\,\varphi^{\prime}$}}&\leftrightharpoons&\parbox{235.20581pt}{$\mathfrak{M},\parbox{7.42825pt}{$v$}\models^{h}\varphi^{\prime}$, for every $v\in R(w)$ and every assignment}\\
&&\mbox{\phantom{$\mathfrak{M},w\models^{g^{\prime}}\varphi^{\prime}$, }$h$ such that $h\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x}}{{=}}g$ and $h(x)\in D_{v}$;}\\
\parbox{87.63855pt}{{}\hfill\parbox{64.74725pt}{$\mathfrak{M},w\models^{g}\exists x\,\varphi^{\prime}$}}&\leftrightharpoons&\parbox{235.20581pt}{$\mathfrak{M},w\models^{h}\varphi^{\prime}$, for some assignment $h$ such that $h\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x}}{{=}}g$}\\
&&\mbox{\phantom{$\mathfrak{M},w\models^{g^{\prime}}\varphi^{\prime}$, }and $h(x)\in D_{w}$.}\end{array}

Let
\mathfrak{M}
,
\mathfrak{F}_{D}
,
\mathfrak{F}
, and
\mathscr{C}
be an intuitionistic Kripke model, an intuitionistic augmented frame, an intuitionistic Kripke frame, and a class of intuitionistic augmented frames, respectively,
w
a world of
\mathfrak{M}
, and
\varphi
a formula with free variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
; then define

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\parbox{87.63855pt}{{}\hfill$\mathfrak{M}\models\varphi$}&\leftrightharpoons&\parbox[t]{235.20581pt}{$\mathfrak{M},w\models^{g}\varphi$, for every world $w$ of $\mathfrak{M}$ and every\penalty\ $g$}\\
&&\mbox{\phantom{$\mathfrak{M},w\models^{g}\varphi$, }such that $g(x_{1}),\ldots,g(x_{n})\in D_{w}$;}\\
\parbox{87.63855pt}{{}\hfill$\mathfrak{F}_{D}\models\varphi$}&\leftrightharpoons&\parbox[t]{235.20581pt}{$\parbox{13.78157pt}{$\mathfrak{M}$}\models\varphi$, for every intuitionistic model $\mathfrak{M}$ based on $\mathfrak{F}_{D}$;}\\
\parbox{87.63855pt}{{}\hfill$\mathfrak{F}\models\varphi$}&\leftrightharpoons&\parbox[t]{235.20581pt}{$\parbox{13.78157pt}{$\mathfrak{M}$}\models\varphi$, for every intuitionistic model $\mathfrak{M}$ based on $\mathfrak{F}$;}\\
\parbox{87.63855pt}{{}\hfill$\mathscr{C}\models\varphi$}&\leftrightharpoons&\parbox[t]{195.1634pt}{$\parbox{13.78157pt}{$\mathfrak{F}_{D}$}\models\varphi$, for every $\mathfrak{F}_{D}\in\mathscr{C}$.}\\
\end{array}

If
\mathfrak{S}\models\varphi
, for a structure
\mathfrak{S}
(a model, a frame, etc.), we say that the formula
\varphi
is
true
or
valid
in (on, at)
\mathfrak{S}
; otherwise,
\varphi
is
refuted
in (on, at)
\mathfrak{S}
.
These notions, and the corresponding notations, can be extended to sets of formulas in a natural way: for a set of formulas
X
, define
\mathfrak{S}\models X
as
\mathfrak{S}\models\varphi
, for every
\varphi\in X
.

The intuitionistic predicate logic
\mathbf{QInt}
is the set of
\mathcal{L}
-formulas valid on every intuitionistic Kripke frame; it can also be defined through a Hilbert-style calculus with a finite set of axioms
[
8
,
40
]
. A
superintuitionistic predicate logic
is a set of
\mathcal{L}
-formulas that includes
\mathbf{QInt}
and is closed under Modus Ponens, Substitution, and Generalization. If
L
is a superintuitionistic predicate logic and
\Gamma
is a set of
\mathcal{L}
-formulas, then
L+\Gamma
denotes the smallest superintuitionistic logic containing
L\cup\Gamma
. For a formula
\varphi
, we write
L+\varphi
rather than
L+\{\varphi\}
. If
L
is a propositional superintuitionistic logic, then define
\mathbf{Q}L
by
\mathbf{Q}L=\mathbf{QInt}+L
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a class of intuitionistic augmented frames. Define the
superintuitionistic predicate logic
\mathop{\mbox{{{QSIL}}}}\mathscr{C}
of the class
\mathscr{C}
by

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathop{\mbox{{{QSIL}}}}\mathscr{C}&=&\{\varphi\in\mathcal{L}:\mathscr{C}\models\varphi\}.\end{array}

For a class
\mathscr{C}
of intuitionistic Kripke frames, define

•

\mathop{\mbox{{{aug}}}^{\mathit{e}}_{\mathit{}}}{\mathscr{C}}
be the classes of e-augmented frames based on Kripke frames of
\mathscr{C}
;

•

\mathop{\mbox{{{aug}}}^{\mathit{c}}_{\mathit{}}}{\mathscr{C}}
be the classes of c-augmented frames based on Kripke frames of
\mathscr{C}
,

and let

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathop{\mbox{{{QSIL}}}^{\mathit{e}}_{\mathit{}}}\mathscr{C}&=&\mathop{\mbox{{{QSIL}}}}\mathop{\mbox{{{aug}}}^{\mathit{e}}_{\mathit{}}}\mathscr{C};\\
\mathop{\mbox{{{QSIL}}}^{\mathit{c}}_{\mathit{}}}\mathscr{C}&=&\mathop{\mbox{{{QSIL}}}}\mathop{\mbox{{{aug}}}^{\mathit{c}}_{\mathit{}}}\mathscr{C}.\end{array}

Observe that
\mathop{\mbox{{{QSIL}}}^{\mathit{e}}_{\mathit{}}}\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathop{\mbox{{{QSIL}}}^{\mathit{c}}_{\mathit{}}}\mathscr{C}
.
For an intuitionistic Kripke frame
\mathfrak{F}
, we write
\mathop{\mbox{{{QSIL}}}^{\mathit{e}}_{\mathit{}}}\mathfrak{F}
and
\mathop{\mbox{{{QSIL}}}^{\mathit{c}}_{\mathit{}}}\mathfrak{F}
rather than
\mathop{\mbox{{{QSIL}}}^{\mathit{e}}_{\mathit{}}}\{\mathfrak{F}\}
and
\mathop{\mbox{{{QSIL}}}^{\mathit{c}}_{\mathit{}}}\{\mathfrak{F}\}
, respectively; similarly for augmented frames.

Let
P
be a unary predicate letter and
p
a proposition letter (i.e., nullary predicate letter); the formula
\bm{cd}=\forall x\,(P(x)\vee p)\to\forall x\,P(x)\vee p
is valid on an intuitionistic augmented frame
\mathfrak{F}_{D}
if, and only if,
\mathfrak{F}_{D}
satisfies (
2.2
).
If
L
is a superintuitionistic predicate logic, then
L\mathbf{.cd}
denotes the logic
L+\bm{cd}
.

Here, we are interested in logic
\mathbf{QLC}
— more exactly, in its two-variable fragment — and some its extensions, such as the logics of the frames
\langle\mathds{Z},\leqslant\rangle
and
\langle\mathds{R},\leqslant\rangle
. Logic
\mathbf{QLC}
is a predicate counterpart of the superintuitionistic propositional logic
\mathbf{LC}=\mathbf{Int}+(p\to q)\vee(q\to p)
. It is known that
\mathbf{QLC}
is the logic of the class of linear intuitionistic Kripke frames and even is the logic of the frame
\langle\mathds{Q},\leqslant\rangle

[
8
, Section 6.7]
; also, it is the ‘superintuitionistic’ fragment of the modal predicate logic
\mathbf{QS4.3}
, under the Gödel translation.

3

\mathbf{QLC}
and some its extensions are undecidable (
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-hard)
in the language with two individual variables. To that end, we reduce a

-complete

{\mathit{right}}
,
{\mathit{above}}
,
{\mathit{wall}}
,
{\mathit{floor}}
, and

{\textnormal{{next}}}\colon\mathds{N}\to\mathds{N}
defined by
{\textnormal{{next}}}(k)=k+1
, for every

, — but not quite so that, for example,
the truth of
{\mathit{right}}(x)
alone makes it possible to say something definite about the value
{\textnormal{{right}}}(x)
, and the same for
{\mathit{above}}(x)
and
{\textnormal{{above}}}(x)
. However, in some significant cases, a certain correspondence will be achieved.

Let us explain a technical idea that we shall use for this purpose.

To simulate a
T
-tiling, we need a tool allowing us to distinguish elements in the domains of worlds of Kripke models over linear Kripke frames. To distinguish such an element by means of the modal predicate language, we can use a unary predicate letter, say,
U
: it is possible to make
U(a)
being true at a world but being false at all different worlds, both accessible from it and seeing it. There is a certain difficulty to do the same using the intuitionistic predicate language: if
\mathfrak{M},w\models U(a)
, for some
a
from the domain of a world
w
of a model
\mathfrak{M}
, then also
\mathfrak{M},w^{\prime}\models U(a)
, for every
w^{\prime}
accessible from
w
. To overcome this difficulty, we shall use not one, but two (or more) unary predicate letters for such purposes. Exactly, let
U
and
U^{\prime}
be unary predicate letters;
w^{\prime}
,
w
, and
w^{\prime\prime}
be different worlds of a model
\mathfrak{M}
such that
w^{\prime}
sees
w
and
w
sees
w^{\prime\prime}
;
a
be an element of the domains of the worlds. Let us assume
a
be
distinguished
at
w
by means of
U
and
U^{\prime}
if

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathfrak{M},w^{\prime\prime}\hfill\models U^{\prime}(a)&\mbox{and}&\mathfrak{M},w^{\prime\prime}\hfill\models U(a);\\
\mathfrak{M},w\hfill\not\models U^{\prime}(a)&\mbox{and}&\mathfrak{M},w\hfill\models U(a);\\
\mathfrak{M},w^{\prime}\hfill\not\models U^{\prime}(a)&\mbox{and}&\mathfrak{M},w^{\prime}\hfill\not\models U(a).\\
\end{array}

If the domain of a Kripke model over a Kripke frame validating
\mathbf{QLC}
is
\mathds{N}
, then, distinguishing an element
k
with a pair of certain unary predicate letters, we can achieve that, for exapmle,
{\mathit{right}}(k)
corresponds to
{\textnormal{{right}}}(k)
or that
{\mathit{above}}(k)
corresponds to
{\textnormal{{above}}}(k)
.

9
Conclusion

The undecidability of
\mathbf{QLC}
is proved, and, clearly, the methods used can be applied to other logics. In particular, they can be used in various modal predicate logics, because the modal language is more flexible than intuitionistic. Some results in this direction have already been obtained, and perhaps they will be published soon.
Also, the methods seem working in the predicate counterparts of the basic and formal propositional logics introduced by A. Visser
[
42
]
.

Let us turn to the language used. Two questions naturally arise: Do we need a binary predicate letter in the construction? Do we need infinitely many unary predicate letters in it?

It is known that in many cases the atomic predicate formulas can be simulated by the monadic — modal or superintuitionistic — formulas
[
15

13

21
,

,

,

]

\Diamond(Q_{1}(x)\wedge Q_{2}(y))
in the modal language
[

]
and by

in the intuitionistic
[
34
,
37
]
.

2

In the intuitionistic case, the method can be applied to the positive formulas only; however,
\bot
can be eliminated in ‘natural’ situations
[

]
.
Notice that there are limitations that do not allow the use of such simulations in some cases: in Kripke frames, we need worlds seeing ‘sufficiently many’ worlds (in the modal case) or seeing ‘sufficiently large’ antichains of worlds (in the intuitionistic case). In view of the last requirement, such simulation is not applicable to
\mathbf{QLC}
and its extensions.

However, the binary predicate letter we used corresponds in models, as we have seen, to a quite special relation which can be understood as ‘taking the next element’ in a linearly ordered set: so, in the proof of Lemma

we define

and in the proof of Lemma

Clearly, in a model based on a frame validating

using the binary relation corresponding to

? Of course, this is not hard to do if there is a third individual variable. Moreover, sometimes this problem can be easily solved in the modal case by means of the monadic language with two individual variables
[
28
]
. At the same time, there are results showing us that the monadic fragments of modal predicate logics can be decidable
[
25
,
36
,
37
]
; they are obtained for logics complete with respect to quite simple semantics, and the semantics for
\mathbf{QLC}
does not seem to be too complicated. As a summary, the answer to the first question is unclear to the author.

The situation with the second question is quite similar. On the one hand, to simulate all unary predicate letters in an intuitionistic formula by formulas with a single one

Nevertheless, despite the non-obviousness, these questions do not seem too hopeless, and perhaps they will be the subject of future research by the author.

Another question concerns the logic of
\omega^{\ast}
viewed as a Kripke frame; and the same holds for logics of linear Noetherian orders as well. It seems that we can argue similarly to Section
7

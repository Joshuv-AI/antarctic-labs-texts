Is A 1 A_{1} of type B 2 B_{2} ?
S Launois, I Oppong

Abstract
. By a theorem of Dixmier, primitive quotients of enveloping algebras of finite-dimensional complex nilpotent Lie algebras are isomorphic to Weyl algebras. In view of this result, it is natural to consider simple quotients of positive parts of quantized enveloping algebras (and more generally of uniparameter Quantum Nilpotent Algebras) as quantum analogues of Weyl algebras. In this note, we study the Lie algebra of derivations of the simple quotients of U q + ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔬 5 ) U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5}) of Gelfand-Kirillov dimension 2. For a specific family of such simple quotients, we prove that all derivations are inner (as in the case of Weyl algebras) whereas all other such algebras are quantum Generalized Weyl Algebras over a commutative Laurent polynomial algebra in one variable and have a first Hochschild cohomology group of dimension 1.

1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{K}}
be a field of characteristic
0
and let
q
be a non-zero scalar which is not a root of unity. Weyl algebras are central objects in noncommutative algebra due to their appearences in a range of areas e.g. representation theory of Lie algebras; combinatorics; quantum mechanics;
\mathcal{D}
-modules to name a few. Despite many efforts over the last 60 years, the structure of Weyl algebras is still proving mysterious. For instance, only the automorphism group of the first Weyl algebra
A_{1}({\mathbb{K}})
has been computed (by Dixmier in
[
10
]
) and only the irreducible representations of
A_{1}({\mathbb{K}})
have been classified in a highly technical tour de force by Block
[
6
]
. These two questions remain open for higher Weyl algebras.

Since Weyl algebras are simple, their endomorphisms are injective, and it is an open conjecture of Dixmier
[
10
, Problème 1]
that all endomorphisms are actually automorphisms. This conjecture is (stably) equivalent to the celebrated Jacobian Conjecture (see
[
5
,
24
]
), and it is still open even in the case of the first Weyl algebra
A_{1}({\mathbb{K}})
, to the best of the authors’ knowledge.

Given its central role in noncommutative algebra, it is not surprising that mathematicians have tried to generalise and/or deform Weyl algebras. This led in particular to the notion of Generalized Weyl Algebras (GWAs for short), a class of noncommutative algebras introduced by Bavula in the 1990s (see
[
3
]
). This class of algebras includes in particular two families of (quantum) algebras that are deforming Weyl algebras: quantum Weyl algebras and Weyl-Hayashi algebras. The later appear as simple quotients of the quantum Heisenberg algebra
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})

[
13
]
, and were studied for instance in
[
1
,
14
]
. A well-known theorem of Dixmier
[
11
, Théorème 4.7.9]

shows that primitive quotients of enveloping algebras of nilpotent Lie algebras are isomorphic to Weyl algebras. In many respects, (uniparameter) Quantum Nilpotent Algebras (QNA for short), also known as CGL extensions
[
12
,
20
]
, are behaving very much as enveloping algebras of nilpotent Lie algebras, and indeed include all quantum Schubert cells
U_{q}[w]
which are deformations of the enveloping algebras of
\mathfrak{n}^{+}\cap w(\mathfrak{n}^{-})
, where
\mathfrak{n}^{\pm}
are the nilradicals of the standard opposite Borel subalgebras of a symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and where
w
is an element of the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{g}
.

As such, it is natural to think of primitive/simple quotients of QNAs as “quantum” Weyl algebras. This point of view is reinforced by the fact that primitive quotients of uniparameter QNAs have even Gelfand-Kirillov dimension
[
4
, Theorem 1.6]
. With this idea in mind, Lopes
[
23
]
studied the primitive ideals of
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
coming from the
0
-stratum, that is the primitive ideals that do not contain any
H
-eigenvectors for the natural action by automorphisms of the torus
H:=({\mathbb{K}}^{*})^{n-1}
on
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
. These primitive ideals are all maximal of even Gelfand-Kirillov dimension, and so feature some common properties with Weyl algebras. However, they also feature notable differences: they contain non-trivial units and not all derivations are inner in general. It is worth noting that, in the case of
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
, these primitive quotients are the Weyl-Hayashi algebras and can be presented as GWAs over a (commutative) Laurent polynomial ring
{\mathbb{K}}[h^{\pm 1}]
. The presence of non-trivial units was exploited in
[
16
]
to prove that all endomorphisms of Weyl-Hayashi algebras (and more generally of quantum GWAs over a commutative Laurent polynomial algebra) are automorphisms. In this note, we study the case of the positive part
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
of the quantized enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{so}_{5}
, and this will lead us to a “quantum deformation of
A_{1}({\mathbb{K}})
”. Recently, the authors did study the case of
U_{q}^{+}(G_{2})

[
22
]
to obtain quantum/algebraic deformations of the second Weyl algebra.

In
[
19
]
, primitive ideals of
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
were classified. This led in particular to three non-isomorphic families of simple quotients of
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
of Gelfand-Kirillov dimension 2. Out of these three families, we retrieve the Weyl-Hayashi algebras as a first family, another family of quantum GWAs over a commutative Laurent polynomial ring, and a third family
B_{\alpha,\beta}
, with
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{K}}^{*}
, which cannot be presented as a GWA over a commutative Laurent polynomial ring. This last family can be presented as the
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra generated by
e_{1}
,
e_{2}
,
e_{3}
and
e_{4}
subject to the following relations:

\displaystyle e_{2}e_{1}

\displaystyle=q^{-2}e_{1}e_{2}

\displaystyle e_{4}e_{1}

\displaystyle=q^{2}e_{1}e_{4}-q^{2}e_{2}

\displaystyle e_{4}e_{2}

\displaystyle=q^{-2}e_{2}e_{4}+(1-q^{-2})\beta

and

\frac{q^{6}}{q^{4}-1}e_{2}^{2}=\dfrac{-(q+q^{-1})}{1-q^{-2}}\alpha-\beta e_{1}+e_{1}e_{2}e_{4},

where
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{K}}^{*}
.

Before we go any further, it is worth noting that these relations are just expressing the fact that
B_{\alpha,\beta}
is the quotient of
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
by the maximal ideal
\langle\chi_{1}-\alpha,\chi_{2}-\beta\rangle
of
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
, where
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
are the generators of the center of
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
(see
[
19
, Proposition 2.7]
).

For the first two families of simple quotients of
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
, since they can be presented as quantum GWAs over a Laurent polynomial ring, their endomorphisms were proved to be automorphisms in
[
16
]
, and derivations were computed in
[
15
]
, proving in particular that not all derivations are inner (whereas all derivations of a Weyl algebra are inner).

Our main aim in this note is to show that all derivations of
B_{\alpha,\beta}
when
\alpha\beta\neq 0
are inner. This makes the algebra
B_{\alpha,\beta}
with
\alpha\beta\neq 0
into a quantum deformation of the first Weyl algebra which is simple, has Gelfand-Kirillov dimension 2, has no non-trivial units, and has all its derivations inner. This somehow justifies the claim that “
A_{1}({\mathbb{K}})
is of type
B_{2}
” since none of the primitive quotients of the quantum Heisenberg algebra
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})
have all these properties.

This paper is organised as follows. We recall some basic properties of
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
and
B_{\alpha,\beta}
in Sections
2
and
3
respectively. More importantly, we study Cauchon’s deleting derivation algorithm
[
8
]
for the QNA
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
in Section
2
. This theory allows us to construct an embedding:

B_{\alpha,\beta}\subset R=B_{\alpha,\beta}[e_{4}^{-1}]

and to present
R
as a GWA. This presentation of
R
is obtained through the elements
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
constructed at the first step of the deleting derivation algorithm (DDA for short) for
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
. We then extend any derivation
D
of
B_{\alpha,\beta}
uniquely to a derivation of the GWA
R
. Thanks to a result of Kitchin
[
15
, Proposition 1.3]
, derivations of
R
are known to be the sum of an inner derivation and a scalar derivation (i.e. a derivation that acts on the generators
f_{1},~f_{2},~e_{3}
and
e_{4}
of
R
by multiplication by scalars). Having a perfect description of
D
as a derivation
R
, we then describe it as a derivation of
B_{\alpha,\beta}
by controlling the effect of the DDA on our derivation
D
. Similarly to Weyl algebras, we conclude that every derivation of
B_{\alpha,\beta}
is inner provided
\alpha\beta\neq 0.

2.
Deleting derivation algorithm for the Quantum Nilpotent Algebra
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})

2.1.
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
as a Quantum Nilpotent Algebra

The quantized enveloping algebra
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
is the
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra generated by two indeterminates
E_{1}
and
E_{4}
subject to the quantum Serre relations:

\displaystyle E_{1}^{2}E_{4}-(q^{2}+q^{-2})E_{1}E_{4}E_{1}+E_{4}E_{1}^{2}=0

\displaystyle E_{4}^{3}E_{1}-(q^{2}+1+q^{-2})E_{4}^{2}E_{1}E_{4}+(q^{2}+1+q^{-2})E_{4}E_{1}E_{4}^{2}-E_{1}E_{4}^{3}=0.

To each reduced decomposition of the longest element of the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{so}_{5}
, we can associate a PBW basis for
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
by using Lusztig automorphisms. In the present case, this leads us to introduce the following elements (see
[
9
, Appendix A]
with
w_{0}=s_{\beta}s_{\alpha}s_{\beta}s_{\alpha}
, where the root vectors
E_{\beta}
,
E_{\alpha+\beta}
,
E_{2\alpha+\beta}
and
E_{\alpha}
from
[
9
]
corresponds to our
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
,
E_{3}
and
E_{4}
respectively):

E_{2}:=E_{1}E_{4}-q^{-2}E_{4}E_{1}

and

E_{3}:=\frac{1}{q+q^{-1}}\left(E_{2}E_{4}-E_{4}E_{2}\right).

By results of Levendorskii-Soibelman and Lusztig (see
[
7
, Theorem I.6.8]
), the monomials
E_{1}^{i_{1}}E_{2}^{i_{2}}E_{3}^{i_{3}}E_{4}^{i_{4}}
with
i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
form a PBW basis of
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
, so that
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
can be seen as the
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra generated by
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
,
E_{3}
and
E_{4}
subject to the relations below:

\displaystyle E_{2}E_{1}

\displaystyle=q^{-2}E_{1}E_{2}

\displaystyle E_{4}E_{1}

\displaystyle=q^{2}E_{1}E_{4}-q^{2}E_{2}

\displaystyle E_{3}E_{1}

\displaystyle=E_{1}E_{3}+\dfrac{q-q^{3}}{1+q^{2}}E_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle E_{4}E_{2}

\displaystyle=E_{2}E_{4}-(q+q^{-1})E_{3}

\displaystyle E_{3}E_{2}

\displaystyle=q^{-2}E_{2}E_{3}

\displaystyle E_{4}E_{3}

\displaystyle=q^{-2}E_{3}E_{4}.

This presentation leads to the following presentation of
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
as an iterated Ore extension:

B:=U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})=\mathbb{K}[E_{1}][E_{2};\sigma_{2}][E_{3};\sigma_{3},\delta_{3}][E_{4};\sigma_{4},\delta_{4}],

where
\sigma_{2}
is an automorphism of
\mathbb{K}[E_{1}]
defined by

\sigma_{2}(E_{1})=q^{-2}E_{1};

\sigma_{3}
is an automorphism of
\mathbb{K}[E_{1}][E_{2};\sigma_{2}]
defined by

\sigma_{3}(E_{1})=E_{1},\ \sigma_{3}(E_{2})=q^{-2}E_{2};

\delta_{3}
is a
\sigma_{3}
-derivation of
\mathbb{K}[E_{1}][E_{2};\sigma_{2}]
defined by

\delta_{3}(E_{1})=(q-q^{3})/(1+q^{2})E_{2}^{2},\ \delta_{3}(E_{2})=0;

\sigma_{4}
is an automorphism of
\mathbb{K}[E_{1}][E_{2};\sigma_{2}][E_{3};\sigma_{3},\delta_{3}]
defined by

\sigma_{4}(E_{1})=q^{2}E_{1},\ \sigma_{4}(E_{2})=E_{2},\ \sigma_{4}(E_{3})=q^{-2}E_{3}
and

\delta_{4}
is a
\sigma_{4}
-derivation of

\mathbb{K}[E_{1}][E_{2};\sigma_{2}][E_{3};\sigma_{3},\delta_{3}]
defined by

\delta_{4}(E_{1})=-q^{2}E_{2},\ \delta_{4}(E_{2})=-(q+q^{-1})E_{3},\ \delta_{4}(E_{3})=0.

Note in particular that
B
is a noetherian domain.

This presentation of
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
as an iterated Ore extension makes
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
into a so-called (uniparameter) QNA, (see for instance
[
8
, Proposition 6.1.2 and Lemme 6.2.1]
). At this stage, we do not need to recall the definition of a QNA. However, we point out that, first, this implies that all primes of
B
are completely prime, and then that this will allow us to apply the deleting derivation theory of Cauchon
[
8
]
to
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
with this iterated Ore extension structure. It is worth noting that the iterated Ore extension presentation of
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
used in
[
2
,
19
]
does not allow to apply Cauchon’s deleting derivation theory.

2.2.
Deleting derivation algorithm of
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})

Since the iterated Ore extension
B
is a QNA, similarly to
[
22
, Sec 2.3]
, Cauchon’s theory of deleting derivation algorithm (DDA for short) (see
[
8
]
) can be used to construct the following elements of the skew field of fractions
\mathrm{Frac}(B)
of
B
:

\displaystyle E_{i,5}

\displaystyle:=E_{i}\qquad(i\in[1,4])

\displaystyle E_{1,4}

\displaystyle=E_{1}+p_{1}E_{2}E_{4}^{-1}+p_{2}E_{3}E_{4}^{-2}

\displaystyle E_{2,4}

\displaystyle=E_{2}+p_{3}E_{3}E_{4}^{-1}

\displaystyle E_{3,4}

\displaystyle=E_{4}

\displaystyle E_{4,4}

\displaystyle=E_{4}

\displaystyle E_{1,3}

\displaystyle=E_{1,4}+p_{4}E_{2,4}^{2}E_{3,4}^{-1}

\displaystyle T_{1}:

\displaystyle=E_{1,2}=E_{1,3}

\displaystyle T_{2}:

\displaystyle=E_{2,2}=E_{2,3}=E_{2,4}

\displaystyle T_{3}:

\displaystyle=E_{3,2}=E_{3,3}=E_{3,4}=E_{3}

\displaystyle T_{4}:

\displaystyle=E_{4,2}=E_{4,3}=E_{4,4}=E_{4},

where

\displaystyle p_{1}=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{q^{-2}-1}=\dfrac{q^{2}}{1-q^{2}},

\displaystyle~~~p_{2}=

\displaystyle\dfrac{q^{-1}+q^{-3}}{(1+q^{-2})(1-q^{-2})^{2}}=\dfrac{q^{3}}{(q^{2}-1)^{2}},

\displaystyle p_{3}=

\displaystyle\dfrac{-(q+q^{-1})}{1-q^{-2}}=\dfrac{-(q^{3}+q)}{q^{2}-1},

\displaystyle~~~p_{4}=

\displaystyle\dfrac{q-q^{3}}{(1-q^{-4})(1+q^{2})}=\dfrac{-q^{5}}{(1+q^{2})^{2}}.

Recall from
[
2
, Lemma 3.1]
that the center of
B
is a polynomial algebra
Z(B)={\mathbb{K}}[\chi_{1},\chi_{2}],

where

\chi_{1}:=T_{1}T_{3}=E_{1,4}E_{3,4}+p_{4}E_{2,4}^{2}=E_{1}E_{3}+p_{4}E_{2}^{2}

and

\chi_{2}:=T_{2}T_{4}=E_{2}E_{4}+p_{3}E_{3}.

Note that these two central generators take a simpler form when expressed in the new elements
T_{i}
.

3.
Simple quotients of
U_{q}^{+}(B_{2})

Primitive/maximal ideals of
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
were classified in
[
19
]
, where it is proved (see
[
19
, Propositions 2.6 and 2.7]
) that the only maximal ideals of height 2 are the ideals
\langle\chi_{1}-\alpha,\chi_{2}-\beta\rangle
with
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{K}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
.

Our aim in this note is to compute the derivations of the corresponding simple quotients of
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
. So we set:

B_{\alpha,\beta}:=\frac{B}{\langle\chi_{1}-\alpha,\chi_{2}-\beta\rangle},

where
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{K}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}.

The algebra
B_{\alpha,\beta}
is a simple noetherian domain with Gelfand-Kirillov dimension
2
(
[
19
, Proposition 2.8]
) and its center is reduced to
{\mathbb{K}}
by
[
19
, Proposition 3.7]
. From
[
19
, Observation 3.1]
, we also have that
B_{\alpha,\beta}
is a quantum deformation of the first Weyl algebra
A_{1}({\mathbb{K}})
, hence
B_{\alpha,\beta}
can be thought of as a quantum first Weyl algebra.

In the case where
\alpha
or
\beta
is zero (but not both), then
B_{0,\beta}
and
B_{\alpha,0}
are Generalized Weyl Algebras (GWA) over a commutative Laurent polynomial ring in one variable by
[
19
, Propositions 3.9 and 3.10]
. As such, their derivations were computed in
[
15
]
. Thus, from now on, we focus our attention on the case where
\alpha\beta\neq 0
.

Denote the canonical image of each
E_{i}
by
e_{i}
in
B_{\alpha,\beta}.
We have that
B_{\alpha,\beta}
satisfies the following commutation relations:

\displaystyle e_{2}e_{1}

\displaystyle=q^{-2}e_{1}e_{2}

\displaystyle e_{4}e_{1}

\displaystyle=q^{2}e_{1}e_{4}-q^{2}e_{2}

\displaystyle e_{3}e_{1}

\displaystyle=e_{1}e_{3}+\dfrac{q-q^{3}}{1+q^{2}}e_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle e_{3}e_{2}

\displaystyle=q^{-2}e_{2}e_{3}

\displaystyle e_{4}e_{2}

\displaystyle=e_{2}e_{4}-(q+q^{-1})e_{3}

\displaystyle e_{4}e_{3}

\displaystyle=q^{-2}e_{3}e_{4},

and

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}e_{3}&=k_{1}\beta-k_{1}e_{2}e_{4}\\
e_{2}^{2}&=\alpha k_{2}-\beta k_{1}k_{2}e_{1}+k_{1}k_{2}e_{1}e_{2}e_{4},\end{split}

where
k_{1}=1/p_{3}=\dfrac{1-q^{2}}{q^{3}+q}
and
k_{2}=1/p_{4}=-q^{-5}(1+q^{2})^{2}.

The following relations can easily be checked by induction.

Lemma 3.1
.

For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
:

(1)

e_{3}e_{1}^{i}=e_{1}^{i}e_{3}+d[i]e_{1}^{i-1}e_{2}^{2},

where
d[i]=\dfrac{(q-q^{3})(1-q^{-4i})}{(1-q^{-4})(1+q^{2})}.
Note in particular that
d[0]=0.

(2)

e_{4}e_{1}^{i}=q^{2i}e_{1}^{i}e_{4}-f[i]e_{1}^{i-1}e_{2},

where
f[i]=\dfrac{q^{2i}(1-q^{-4i})}{1-q^{-4}}.
Note in particular that
f[0]=0.

A linear basis of
B_{\alpha,\beta}
was obtained in
[
19
]
. Noting that our generators
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
and
E_{4}
of
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{so}_{5})
correspond to the generators
e_{1}
,
e_{3}
and
e_{2}
of
U_{q^{-1}}^{+}(B_{2})
in
[
19
]
, the linear basis of
B_{\alpha,\beta}
from
[
19
, Proposition 3.2]
can be expressed as follows with the notation of this note.

Proposition 3.2
.

The set
\mathcal{E}=\{e_{1}^{i}e_{4}^{j},e_{1}^{i}e_{2}e_{4}^{j}\mid i,j\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a
\mathbb{K}
-basis of
B_{\alpha,\beta}.

4.
Derivations of
B_{\alpha,\beta}

In this section, we aim to compute the set of
{\mathbb{K}}
-derivations of the algebra
B_{\alpha,\beta}.
Our strategy is to prove that a localization of
B_{\alpha,\beta}
is a quantum GWA. This isomorphism was obtained through the first step of the DDA since this GWA presentation is naturally expressed in terms of the elements produced by the first step of the DDA, that is
E_{1,4}
,
E_{2,4}
,
E_{3}
and
E_{4}
.

Once this isomorphism is established, we can extend any derivation of
B_{\alpha,\beta}
to a derivation of this GWA. It then follows from work of Kitchin
[
15
]
that derivations of a (simple) quantum GWA are the sum of an inner derivation and a central/scalar derivation. To conclude, we will need to control the effect of the deleting derivation algorithm on our derivation.

Unless otherwise stated, we assume that
\alpha\beta\neq 0
.

4.1.
Embedding
B_{\alpha,\beta}
into a quantum GWA

At this stage, we recall that the (quantum) generalized Weyl algebra
{\mathbb{K}}[h^{\pm 1}](\sigma_{q},a)
associated to the automorphism
\sigma_{q}
of
{\mathbb{K}}[h^{\pm 1}]
defined by
\sigma_{q}(h)=q^{2}h
and to a nonzero element
a\in{\mathbb{K}}[h^{\pm 1}]
is the
{\mathbb{K}}[h^{\pm 1}]
-algebra generated by two indeterminates
x
and
y
subject to the relations

yx=a,~xy=\sigma_{q}(a),~xh=q^{2}hx,\mbox{ and }yh=q^{-2}hy.

Observe that
E_{4}\notin\langle\chi_{1}-\alpha,\chi_{2}-\beta\rangle
. Indeed, otherwise,
E_{4}
and
[E_{4},E_{2}]=-(q+q^{-1})E_{3}
would belong to
\langle\chi_{1}-\alpha,\chi_{2}-\beta\rangle
and then
\chi_{2}=E_{2}E_{4}+p_{3}E_{3}
would also belong to
\langle\chi_{1}-\alpha,\chi_{2}-\beta\rangle
, a contradiction since
\beta\neq 0
.

Set
e_{4}:=E_{4}+\langle\chi_{1}-\alpha,\chi_{2}-\beta\rangle\in B_{\alpha,\beta}
. Since the multiplicative system generated by
E_{4}
in
B
satisfies the Ore condition on both side by
[
8
, Lemme 2.1]
, it follows that
\{e_{4}^{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a multiplicative system of regular elements of
B_{\alpha,\beta}
satisfying the Ore condition on both sides.

We set
R:=B_{\alpha,\beta}[e_{4}^{-1}]=\frac{B[E_{4}^{-1}]}{\langle\chi_{1}-\alpha,\chi_{2}-\beta\rangle[E_{4}^{-1}]}
. Our aim in this section is to prove that
R
is a GWA of the form
{\mathbb{K}}[h^{\pm 1}](\sigma_{q},a)
. To prove this, we will make use of the elements created by the first step of the DDA. Namely, recall that the first step of the algorithm leads to the following elements of
\mathrm{Frac}(B)
:

\displaystyle E_{1,4}

\displaystyle=E_{1}+p_{1}E_{2}E_{4}^{-1}+p_{2}E_{3}E_{4}^{-2}\mbox{ and }E_{2,4}=E_{2}+p_{3}E_{3}E_{4}^{-1}.

Recall also that

\chi_{1}=E_{1,4}E_{3}+p_{4}E_{2,4}^{2}\mbox{ and }\chi_{2}=E_{2,4}E_{4}.

This leads us to introduce the following elements of
R
:

f_{1}:=e_{1}+p_{1}e_{2}e_{4}^{-1}+p_{2}e_{3}e_{4}^{-2}

and

f_{2}:=e_{2}+p_{3}e_{3}e_{4}^{-1}.

Clearly,
f_{1},~f_{2},e_{3},e_{4}^{\pm 1}
can be expressed in terms of the generators
e_{1},~e_{2},~e_{3},~e_{4}^{\pm 1}
of
R
.
Note that the converse is also true since
e_{2}=f_{2}-p_{3}e_{3}e_{4}^{-1}
and
e_{1}=f_{1}-p_{1}e_{2}e_{4}^{-1}-p_{2}e_{3}e_{4}^{-2}=f_{1}-p_{1}(f_{2}-p_{3}e_{3}e_{4}^{-1})e_{4}^{-1}-p_{2}e_{3}e_{4}^{-2}
.

At this point, it is worth noting that the fact that
\chi_{2}=\beta
in
B_{\alpha,\beta}
forces
f_{2}
to be invertible in
R
and
e_{4}:=\beta f_{2}^{-1}
. As a consequence,
R
is generated by
f_{1},~f_{2}^{\pm 1}
and
e_{3}
, and one can easily show using various universal properties that

R\simeq\frac{{\mathbb{K}}\langle f_{1},f_{2}^{\pm 1},e_{3}\rangle}{\langle f_{1}f_{2}=q^{2}f_{2}f_{1},~e_{3}f_{2}=q^{-2}f_{2}e_{3},~e_{3}f_{1}=f_{1}e_{3}+\frac{q-q^{3}}{1+q^{2}}f_{2}^{2},~f_{1}e_{3}-\frac{q^{5}}{(1+q^{2})^{2}}f_{2}^{2}=\alpha\rangle}.

We are ready to prove that
R
is indeed a quantum GWA.

Proposition 4.1
.

R
is isomorphic to the GWA
{\mathbb{K}}[h^{\pm 1}](\sigma_{q},\alpha+\frac{q}{(q^{2}+1)^{2}}h^{2})
by an isomorphism that sends
f_{2}
to
h
,
f_{1}
to
x
and
e_{3}
to
y
.

Proof.

By universal property, it is easy to check that there exists an homomorphism

\phi:{\mathbb{K}}[h^{\pm 1}](\sigma_{q},\alpha+\frac{q}{(q^{2}+1)^{2}}h^{2})\rightarrow R

defined by
\phi(h)=f_{2}
,
\phi(x)=f_{1}
and
\phi(y)=e_{3}
. By the above discussion, this homomorphism is surjective. Moreover, the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of the domains
R
and
{\mathbb{K}}[h^{\pm 1}](\sigma_{q},\alpha+\frac{q}{(q^{2}+1)^{2}}h^{2})
is equal to 2. This follows from
[
19
, Proposition 2.8]
for
R
. For the GWA
{\mathbb{K}}[h^{\pm 1}](\sigma_{q},\alpha+\frac{q}{(q^{2}+1)^{2}}h^{2})
, this follows from the fact that it contains the quantum plane
{\mathbb{K}}_{q^{2}}[h,x]
and is contained in the quantum torus
{\mathbb{K}}_{q^{2}}[h^{\pm 1},x^{\pm 1}]
(and both have Gelfand-Kirillov dimension 2). Hence
\phi
is an isomorphism by
[
17
, Proposition 3.15]
.
∎

As a GWA, the algebra
R
has a linear basis formed by the monomials
\{f_{2}^{i}f_{1}^{j}\mid i,j\in\mathbb{N}\}\cup\{f_{2}^{i}e_{3}^{j}\mid i,j\in\mathbb{N}\mbox{ with }j\geq 1\}
. However, it also possesses a linear basis inherited from
B_{\alpha,\beta}
, see Proposition
4.2
.

Proposition 4.2
.

The set
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\{e_{1}^{i}e_{4}^{j},e_{1}^{i}e_{2}e_{4}^{j}\mid(i,j)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}\}
is a
\mathbb{K}
-basis of
R
.

4.2.
Derivations of
B_{\alpha,\beta}
when
\alpha\beta\neq 0

Let Der
(B_{\alpha,\beta})
denote the set of
\mathbb{K}
-derivations of
B_{\alpha,\beta}
and
D\in\text{Der}(B_{\alpha,\beta}).
One can extend
D
to a derivation of the GWA
R
(still denoted by
D
). Derivations of quantum GWA have been described in
[
15
]
and we deduce from the isomorphism
\phi
from Proposition
4.1
the following result:

Lemma 4.3
.

There exist
x\in R
and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{K}}
such that
D=\mathrm{ad}_{x}+\delta_{\lambda}
, where
\delta_{\lambda}\in\mathrm{Der}(R)
is defined by
\delta_{\lambda}(f_{2})=0
,
\delta_{\lambda}(f_{1})=\lambda f_{1}
and
\delta_{\lambda}(e_{3})=-\lambda e_{3}
.

Moreover
\delta_{\lambda}(e_{4})=0
.

Proof.

This follows from
[
15
, Proposition 2.3]
and the fact that
e_{4}=\beta f_{2}^{-2}
.
∎

We are now ready to describe
D
as a derivation of
B_{\alpha,\beta}.

Lemma 4.4
.

(1)

x\in B_{\alpha,\beta}
;

(2)

\lambda=0
;

(3)

D={\rm ad}_{x}.

Proof.

(1) Since
x\in R=B_{\alpha,\beta}[e_{4}^{-1}],
it follows from Proposition
4.2
that one can write

x=\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{1}}a_{i,j}e_{1}^{i}e_{4}^{j}+\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{2}}b_{i,j}e_{1}^{i}e_{2}e_{4}^{j},

where
a_{i,j},b_{i,j}\in{\mathbb{K}}
are families of scalars and,
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are finite subsets of
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}.

Write
x=x_{+}+x_{-},
where

x_{+}=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in I_{1}\\
j\geq 0\end{subarray}}a_{i,j}e_{1}^{i}e_{4}^{j}+\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in I_{2}\\
j\geq 0\end{subarray}}b_{i,j}e_{1}^{i}e_{2}e_{4}^{j}

and

x_{-}=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in I_{1}\\
j<0\end{subarray}}a_{i,j}e_{1}^{i}e_{4}^{j}+\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in I_{2}\\
j<0\end{subarray}}b_{i,j}e_{1}^{i}e_{2}e_{4}^{j}.

Clearly,
x_{+}\in B_{\alpha,\beta}.
We want to show that
x_{-}=0.
Suppose that

x_{-}\neq 0.
Then, there exists a minimum
j_{0}<0
such that
a_{i,j_{0}}\neq 0
or
b_{i,j_{0}}\neq 0
for some
i\geq 0,
and
a_{i,j}=b_{i,j}=0
for all
j<j_{0}.

Write

x_{-}=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in I_{1}\\
j_{0}\leq j<0\end{subarray}}a_{i,j}e_{1}^{i}e_{4}^{j}+\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in I_{2}\\
j_{0}\leq j<0\end{subarray}}b_{i,j}e_{1}^{i}e_{2}e_{4}^{j}.

Now,
D(e_{3})=\text{ad}_{x_{+}}(e_{3})+\text{ad}_{x_{-}}(e_{3})+\delta_{\lambda}(e_{3})\in B_{\alpha,\beta}.
Since
\delta_{\lambda}(e_{3})=-\lambda e_{3}\in B_{\alpha,\beta}
, we obtain that
w:=\text{ad}_{x_{-}}(e_{3})=x_{-}e_{3}-e_{3}x_{-}\in B_{\alpha,\beta}.
This implies that
we_{4}^{-j_{0}-1}\in B_{\alpha,\beta}
, that is:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in I_{1}\\
j_{0}\leq j<0\end{subarray}}a_{i,j}e_{1}^{i}e_{4}^{j}e_{3}e_{4}^{-j_{0}-1}+\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in I_{2}\\
j_{0}\leq j<0\end{subarray}}b_{i,j}e_{1}^{i}e_{2}e_{4}^{j}e_{3}e_{4}^{-j_{0}-1}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in I_{1}\\
j_{0}\leq j<0\end{subarray}}a_{i,j}e_{3}e_{1}^{i}e_{4}^{j-j_{0}-1}-\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in I_{2}\\
j_{0}\leq j<0\end{subarray}}b_{i,j}e_{3}e_{1}^{i}e_{2}e_{4}^{j-j_{0}-1}\in B_{\alpha,\beta}.

Using the relation
e_{3}e_{4}=q^{2}e_{4}e_{3}
, this leads to:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in I_{1}\\
j_{0}\leq j<0\end{subarray}}a_{i,j}q^{-2j}e_{1}^{i}e_{3}e_{4}^{j-j_{0}-1}+\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in I_{2}\\
j_{0}\leq j<0\end{subarray}}b_{i,j}q^{-2j}e_{1}^{i}e_{2}e_{3}e_{4}^{j-j_{0}-1}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in I_{1}\\
j_{0}\leq j<0\end{subarray}}a_{i,j}e_{3}e_{1}^{i}e_{4}^{j-j_{0}-1}-\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in I_{2}\\
j_{0}\leq j<0\end{subarray}}b_{i,j}e_{3}e_{1}^{i}e_{2}e_{4}^{j-j_{0}-1}\in B_{\alpha,\beta}.

The only terms that could not belong to
B_{\alpha,\beta}
in the above sums are those for which the exponents of
e_{4}
is negative. Since
j-j_{0}-1\geq 0
for all
j>j_{0}
, we obtain that:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{1}}a_{i,j_{0}}q^{-2j_{0}}e_{1}^{i}e_{3}e_{4}^{-1}+\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{2}}b_{i,j_{0}}q^{-2j_{0}}e_{1}^{i}e_{2}e_{3}e_{4}^{-1}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{1}}a_{i,j_{0}}e_{3}e_{1}^{i}e_{4}^{-1}-\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{2}}b_{i,j_{0}}e_{3}e_{1}^{i}e_{2}e_{4}^{-1}\in B_{\alpha,\beta}.

This is equivalent to

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{1}}a_{i,j_{0}}q^{-2j_{0}}e_{1}^{i}e_{3}+\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{2}}b_{i,j_{0}}q^{-2j_{0}}e_{1}^{i}e_{2}e_{3}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{1}}a_{i,j_{0}}e_{3}e_{1}^{i}-\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{2}}b_{i,j_{0}}e_{3}e_{1}^{i}e_{2}\in B_{\alpha,\beta}e_{4}.

Since
e_{3}-k_{1}\beta\in B_{\alpha,\beta}e_{4}
by (
3.1
), this leads to

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{1}}a_{i,j_{0}}q^{-2j_{0}}k_{1}\beta e_{1}^{i}+\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{2}}b_{i,j_{0}}q^{-2j_{0}}k_{1}\beta e_{1}^{i}e_{2}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{1}}a_{i,j_{0}}e_{3}e_{1}^{i}-\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{2}}b_{i,j_{0}}e_{3}e_{1}^{i}e_{2}\in B_{\alpha,\beta}e_{4}.

Using the straightening relation between
e_{3}
and
e_{1}^{i}
from Lemma
3.1
and the relation
e_{3}e_{2}=q^{-2}e_{2}e_{3}
, we get:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{1}}a_{i,j_{0}}q^{-2j_{0}}k_{1}\beta e_{1}^{i}+\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{2}}b_{i,j_{0}}q^{-2j_{0}}k_{1}\beta e_{1}^{i}e_{2}-\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{1}}a_{i,j_{0}}e_{1}^{i}e_{3}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{1}}a_{i,j_{0}}d[i]e_{1}^{i-1}e_{2}^{2}-\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{2}}b_{i,j_{0}}q^{-2}e_{1}^{i}e_{2}e_{3}-\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{2}}b_{i,j_{0}}d[i]e_{1}^{i-1}e_{2}^{3}\in B_{\alpha,\beta}e_{4}.

Since
e_{3}-k_{1}\beta\in B_{\alpha,\beta}e_{4}
and
e_{2}^{2}-\alpha k_{2}+\beta k_{1}k_{2}e_{1}\in B_{\alpha,\beta}e_{4}
by (
3.1
), we obtain:

\displaystyle w^{\prime}:=\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{1}}a_{i,j_{0}}q^{-2j_{0}}k_{1}\beta e_{1}^{i}+\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{2}}b_{i,j_{0}}q^{-2j_{0}}k_{1}\beta e_{1}^{i}e_{2}-\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{1}}a_{i,j_{0}}k_{1}\beta e_{1}^{i}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{1}}a_{i,j_{0}}d[i]\alpha k_{2}e_{1}^{i-1}+\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{1}}a_{i,j_{0}}d[i]\beta k_{1}k_{2}e_{1}^{i}-\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{2}}b_{i,j_{0}}q^{-2}k_{1}\beta e_{1}^{i}e_{2}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{2}}b_{i,j_{0}}d[i]\alpha k_{2}e_{1}^{i-1}e_{2}+\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j_{0})\in I_{2}}b_{i,j_{0}}d[i]q^{-2}\beta k_{1}k_{2}e_{1}^{i}e_{2}\in B_{\alpha,\beta}e_{4}.

(Notice that a coefficient
q^{-2}
has appeared in the last sum since we had to use the relation
e_{2}e_{1}=q^{-2}e_{1}e_{2}
.)

Note that all the monomials involved in the above sums are elements of the basis
\mathcal{E}
of
B_{\alpha,\beta}
not involving
e_{4}
. However, for an element to belong to
B_{\alpha,\beta}e_{4}
, it needs to have all the coefficients of the basis elements
e_{1}^{i}
and
e_{1}^{i}e_{2}
to be equal to
0
. So, if
i
is maximum such that
(i,j_{0})\in I_{1}
and
a_{i,j_{0}}\neq 0
, we obtain by inspection of the coefficient of
e_{i}^{i}
in
w^{\prime}
:

a_{i,j_{0}}q^{-2j_{0}}k_{1}\beta-a_{i,j_{0}}k_{1}\beta+a_{i,j_{0}}d[i]\beta k_{1}k_{2}=0.

Since
k_{1}\beta\neq 0
and
d[i]k_{2}=1-q^{-4i}
, this leads to

a_{i,j_{0}}(q^{-2j_{0}}-q^{-4i})=0.

Since
q
is not a root of unity and
j_{0}<0
whereas
i\geq 0
, we conclude that
a_{i,j_{0}}=0
. This is a contradiction, and so all coefficients
a_{i,j_{0}}
are equal to
0
.

By definition of
j_{0}
, this means that there exists
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
b_{i,j_{0}}\neq 0
. Let choose such
i
maximal. Then the coefficients of
e_{1}^{i}e_{2}
in
w^{\prime}
must be equal to
0
for
w^{\prime}
to belong to
B_{\alpha,\beta}e_{4}
. This leads to

b_{i,j_{0}}q^{-2j_{0}}k_{1}\beta-b_{i,j_{0}}q^{-2}k_{1}\beta+b_{i,j_{0}}d[i]q^{-2}\beta k_{1}k_{2}=0.

Since
k_{1}\beta\neq 0
and
d[i]k_{2}=1-q^{-4i}
, this leads to

b_{i,j_{0}}\left(q^{-2j_{0}}-q^{-2}+q^{-2}(1-q^{-4i})\right)=0,

that is

b_{i,j_{0}}(q^{-2j_{0}}-q^{-4i-2})=0.

Again,
q^{-2j_{0}}-q^{-4i-2}\neq 0
since
q
is not a root of unity, and
-2j_{0}>0
while
-4i-2<0
. Hence,
b_{i,j_{0}}=0
, a contradiction with our choice of
j_{0}
.

Hence
x_{-}=0
, as desired.

(2) Recall that
\delta_{\lambda}(e_{3})=-\lambda e_{3}.
Also, from Lemma
4.3
,
\delta_{\lambda}(e_{4})=\delta(e_{2})=0
and

\delta_{\lambda}(f_{1})=\lambda f_{1}
. Recall that
e_{2}=f_{2}-p_{3}e_{3}e_{4}^{-1}
in
R.
Therefore,
\delta_{\lambda}(e_{2})=-p_{3}\lambda e_{3}e_{4}^{-1}
. Now,

D(e_{2})=\text{ad}_{x}(e_{2})+\delta_{\lambda}(e_{2})\in B_{\alpha,\beta}.
Since
\text{ad}_{x}(e_{2})\in B_{\alpha,\beta},
we have that

\delta_{\lambda}(e_{2})=-p_{3}\lambda e_{3}e_{4}^{-1}\in B_{\alpha,\beta}.
This implies that
p_{3}\lambda e_{3}\in B_{\alpha,\beta}e_{4}.
Hence,
p_{3}\lambda k_{1}\beta-p_{3}\lambda k_{1}e_{2}e_{4}\in B_{\alpha,\beta}e_{4}
since
e_{3}=k_{1}\beta-k_{1}e_{2}e_{4}
by (
3.1
). Clearly,
e_{2}e_{4}\in B_{\alpha,\beta}e_{4},
it follows that
p_{3}\lambda k_{1}\beta\in B_{\alpha,\beta}e_{4}.
Since
k_{1},p_{3},\beta\neq 0,
this forces
\lambda=0.
Otherwise, we would have a contradiction given the basis
\mathcal{E}
of
B_{\alpha,\beta}
from Proposition
3.2
.

(3) Since
\lambda=0
, then
\delta_{\lambda}
coincides with the
0
derivation on a generating set for
R
. Hence
\delta_{\lambda}=0
and
D=\mathrm{ad}_{x}
with
x\in B_{\alpha,\beta}
as desired.
∎

4.3.
Summary

We are now ready to state the main result of this note which shows that the simple quotients
B_{\alpha,\beta}
present similarities with the first Weyl algebra when
\alpha\beta\neq 0
. Before we proceed, let us recall that the first Hochschild cohomology group of
B_{\alpha,\beta}
(denoted by
{\rm HH}^{1}(B_{\alpha,\beta})
) is defined by

{\rm HH}^{1}(B_{\alpha,\beta}):=\frac{\text{Der}(B_{\alpha,\beta})}{\text{InnDer}(B_{\alpha,\beta})},

where the subset
\text{InnDer}(B_{\alpha,\beta}):=\{\text{ad}_{x}\mid x\in B_{\alpha,\beta}\}
of
\text{Der}(B_{\alpha,\beta})

is the set of inner derivations of
B_{\alpha,\beta}.

Recall that
{\rm HH}^{1}(B_{\alpha,\beta})
is a free module over
Z(B_{\alpha,\beta})={\mathbb{K}}.

Theorem 4.5
.

Assume that
\alpha,\beta\neq 0
.

(1)

Every derivation of
B_{\alpha,\beta}
is inner and
{\rm HH}^{1}(B_{\alpha,\beta})=0.

(2)

\dim{\rm HH}^{1}(B_{\alpha,0})=\dim{\rm HH}^{1}(B_{0,\beta})=1.

Proof.

The first point was proved in Lemma
4.4
. For the second point, observe that
B_{\alpha,0}
and
B_{0,\beta}
are both quantum GWAs by
[
18
, Propositions 3.9 and 3.10]
and so the result follows from
[
15
, Proposition 1.3]
.
∎

5.
Open questions

As explained in the introduction, simple quotients of (uniparameter) QNAs can be thought of as “algebraic deformation” of Weyl algebras. However, some of them may behave differently to Weyl algebras. For instance, they may contain nontrivial units, and this fact was for instance used to prove that endomorphisms of GWAs over a Laurent polynomial ring in one variable are automorphisms (see
[
16
]
). Closer to the main topic of this paper, some simple quotients have only inner derivations whereas some have larger first Hochschild cohomology group. This leads us to the following natural questions:

Question 5.1
.

Can we classify simple quotients of uniparameter QNAs for which all derivations are inner?

Question 5.2
.

If
A
is a simple quotient of a uniparameter QNA, are all endomorphisms of
A
automorphisms?

General results regarding Question
5.1
have been obtained while preparing this note and will be published in a forthcoming publication
[
21
]
.

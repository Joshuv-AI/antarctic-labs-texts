Cusp forms as p p -adic limits circumventing p p -adic version of the Legendre period relation
P. Guerzhoy

Abstract
. Several authors have recently proved results which express a cusp form as a p p -adic limit of weakly holomorphic modular forms under repeated application of Atkin’s U U -operator. Initially, these results had a deficiency: one could not rule out the possibility when a certain quantity vanishes and the final result fails to be true. Later on, Ahlgren and Samart [ 1 ] found a method to prove that no exceptions happen in the specific case considered by El-Guindy and Ono [ 9 ] . Hanson and Jameson [ 14 ] and (independently) Dicks [ 8 ] generalized this method to finitely many other cases. In this paper, we present a different approach which allows us to prove a similar non-vanishing result for an infinite family of similar cases. Our approach also allows us to return back to the original example considered by El-Guindy and Ono [ 9 ] , where we calculate the (manifestly non-zero) quantity explicitly in terms of Morita’s p p -adic Γ \Gamma -function.

Remark 9
.

These are two different ways to prove the existence of

and
\mu_{p}
: the application of an argument along the lines of

which is applicable whenever

In the setting under consideration in this paper, both are applicable, and we employ the interplay between them.
In particular, the explicit calculation of

becomes available with the specific parametrization of the elliptic curve considered here.

We now want to calculate
\mu_{p}
(that coincides with
\gamma
in Theorem

) such that

\xi(t)+\frac{1}{p}\mu_{p}l(t^{p})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\llbracket t\rrbracket.

The denominator of the coefficient of

in

, and letting

we obtain the condition

(14)

\frac{1}{2}4^{n}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{1}{p(4s+1)}+\mu_{p}\frac{1}{p}4^{s}\frac{1}{4s+1}\binom{2s}{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}.

Let

Obviously,
M
is odd,

, and we conclude that

4^{n-s}=4^{(p^{m+1}M-p^{m}M+1)/4}=2\left(2^{(p-1)/2}\right)^{p^{m}M}\equiv 2\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)\pmod{p^{m+1}}

since

is odd and

2^{(p-1)/2}\equiv\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)\pmod{p}

Thus (

\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)\binom{(p^{m+1}M+1)/2}{(p^{m+1}M+1)/4}\equiv-\mu_{p}\binom{(p^{m}M-1)/2}{(p^{m}M-1)/4}\pmod{p^{m}}

Since we know in advance that
\mu_{p}
in (

(and even

):

\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)\binom{(p^{m+1}+1)/2}{(p^{m+1}+1)/4}\equiv-\mu_{p}\binom{(p^{m}-1)/2}{(p^{m}-1)/4}\pmod{p^{m}}.

We now use the identity

\ord_{p}\binom{(p^{m+1}+1)/2}{(p^{m+1}+1)/4}=\ord_{p}\binom{(p^{m}-1)/2}{(p^{m}-1)/4}=\frac{m}{2}<m.

One can prove this identity with a straightforward argument using the well-known fact that
\ord_{p}(j!)=(j-S_{j})/(p-1)
, where

is the sum of digits of the integer

written in base

.
(It is likely that a similar fact is true with an arbitrary odd

\mu_{p}=-\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)\lim_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{3}{m \rightarrow\infty}{ 2|m}}\left.\binom{(p^{m+1}+1)/2}{(p^{m+1}+1)/4}\right/\binom{(p^{m}-1)/2}{(p^{m}-1)/4}.

We now recall a definition of the Catalan numbers

(15)

C(n)=\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}

to finish our proof of the first claim of Theorem
2
.

Abbreviate
a=(p^{m}-1)/4\in{\mathbb{Z}}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and note that
m\rightarrow\infty
translates to
a\rightarrow-1/4
. Rewrite
\mu_{p}
as

(16)

\mu_{p}=-\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)\lim_{a\rightarrow-1/4}\left.\binom{2pa+(p+1)/2}{pa+(p+1)/4}\right/\binom{2a}{a}\\
=-\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)\lim_{a\rightarrow-1/4}\left.\binom{2pa}{pa}\right/\binom{2a}{a}\frac{(2pa+1)\ldots(2pa+(p+1)/2)}{(pa+1)^{2}\ldots(pa+(p+1)/4)^{2}}.

We calculate the limits of the factors in (

) separately.

Recall that Morita’s
p
-adic
\Gamma
-function extends continuously to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
the function defined by its values on integers

\Gamma_{p}(x)=(-1)^{x}\prod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{3}{1 \leq j < x}{p \nmid j}}j.

An observation credited to L. van Hamme in
[

, Section 7.1.6]
allows us to conclude that

\left.\binom{2pa}{pa}\right/\binom{2a}{a}=\frac{\Gamma_{p}(2pa)}{\Gamma_{p}(pa)^{2}}\ \xrightarrow[a\rightarrow-1/4]{}\ \frac{\Gamma_{p}(-p/2)}{\Gamma_{p}(-p/4)^{2}}.

Since none of the integers
1,2,\ldots,(p+1)/2
is divisible by
p
, and
(p+1)/2
is even,

(2pa+1)\ldots(2pa+(p+1)/2)\\
=\frac{\Gamma_{p}(2pa+1+(p+1)/2)}{\Gamma_{p}(2pa+1)}\ \xrightarrow[a\rightarrow-1/4]{}\ \frac{\Gamma_{p}(3/2)}{\Gamma_{p}(1-p/2)}.

Similarly,

(pa+1)^{2}\ldots(pa+(p+1)/4)^{2}\\
=\frac{\Gamma_{p}(pa+1+(p+1)/4)^{2}}{\Gamma_{p}(pa+1)^{2}}\ \xrightarrow[a\rightarrow-1/4]{}\ \frac{\Gamma_{p}(5/4)^{2}}{\Gamma_{p}(1-p/4)^{2}}.

Collecting the three above observations together, we obtain that

\mu_{p}=-\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)\frac{\Gamma_{p}(-p/2)}{\Gamma_{p}(-p/4)^{2}}\frac{\Gamma_{p}(3/2)}{\Gamma_{p}(1-p/2)}\frac{\Gamma_{p}(1-p/4)^{2}}{\Gamma_{p}(5/4)^{2}}.

Furthermore, since
\Gamma_{p}(1+x)=-\Gamma_{p}(x)
for

and
\Gamma_{p}(1+x)=-x\Gamma_{p}(x)
for

, we conclude the proof of the second equality in Theorem

\mu_{p}=-8\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)\frac{\Gamma_{p}(1/2)}{\Gamma_{p}(1/4)^{2}}.

In order to prove the last equality in Theorem

\Gamma_{p}(1/2)=\begin{cases}(-1)^{(1+h(-p))/2}&\textup{if $p>3$}\\
1&\textup{if $p=3$.}\end{cases}

It immediately follows from the reflection formula for

(see
[

\Gamma_{p}(1/2)\equiv\Gamma_{p}((p+1)/2)=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}((p-1)/2)!=((p-1)/2)!\pmod{p}.

The congruence

((p-1)/2)!\equiv(-1)^{(1+h(-p))/2}\pmod{p}

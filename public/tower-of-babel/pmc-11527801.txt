Successful use of single‐port laparoscopic surgery for ovarian cyst removal on postpartum day two after vaginal delivery
Authors: Zhou Yang, Jun Jiao, Mingbao Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Key Clinical MessageThis report describes a single‐port laparoscopic approach for giant ovarian cystectomy tailored for postpartum patients. For asymptomatic giant ovarian cysts reaching full term, timely postpartum single‐port laparoscopic surgery may confer benefits by utilizing physiological alterations to mitigate risks and enhance surgical outcomes.Keywords: ovarian cyst, puerperium, single‐port laparoscopic surgery, vaginal delivery

A woman aged 25 presented at 39 weeks and 5 days of gestation and delivered vaginally at 40 weeks and 3 days. Initially, an ovarian cyst measuring 5 cm was identified via early pregnancy ultrasound, remaining asymptomatic. Subsequent monitoring revealed an increase in cyst size to approximately 15 cm. Two days post‐delivery, she underwent transumbilical single‐port laparoscopic surgery, with a 2.5 cm incision at the umbilicus providing access for cyst removal. Operative findings included a 15 cm cyst on the left ovary and an enlarged uterus. Approximately 1500 mL of clear fluid was aspirated via a lotus suture, with excision of the cyst wall and suturing of the ovary under direct visualization. Immediate pathology confirmed a serous cyst. The patient had an uneventful recovery and was discharged 48 h post‐surgery. During the puerperal period, the uterus enlarges and the adnexa ascend, facilitating their extraction through the single incision. The relaxation of the abdominal wall allows for easier expansion of the single‐port, aiding in suture placement and enhancing cosmetic outcomes. 1 It has been demonstrated that single‐port laparoscopy can mitigate tumor spillage for giant ovarian tumors and reduce postoperative pain. 1 However, it is crucial to be mindful of the potential complications associated with single‐port laparoscopy, such as postoperative incisional hernia. Haueter et al. 2 reported a four‐fold increase in the risk of incisional hernia following single‐incision laparoscopic cholecystectomy compared to multi‐port laparoscopic prodedures.

Managing ovarian cysts during pregnancy presents potential risks of adverse outcomes, including miscarriage and preterm labor. 3 The enlarged uterus during pregnancy poses challenges, elevating the risks of complications such as ovarian torsion and rupture, which may necessitate emergency interventions if addressed post‐puerperium. For patients with asymptomatic giant ovarian cysts reaching full term, timely single‐port laparoscopic surgeries may offer benefits by leveraging physiological changes to mitigate risks and enhance surgical outcomes. These observations highlight the critical importance of precise timing for surgical interventions in cases of ovarian cysts during pregnancy.

Correlation between MMP-3-1171 5A/6A polymorphism and the risk of Alzheimer’s disease
Authors: Yan Wang, Shaohui Zhu, Yanjiao Ma, Xiaoshan Chen, Minhua Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Previous studies of matrix metalloproteinase-3 (MMP-3) -1171 5A/6A polymorphism in Alzheimer’s disease (AD) have been extensively investigated; however, the results from different studies are inconsistent. This meta-analysis was performed to clarify the link between this variant and AD.Methods:We searched PubMed, Embase, Chinese National Knowledge Infrastructure and Wanfang Database. The associations were calculated as odds ratios with the corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs).Results:7 case-control studies with a total of 1457 cases and 1959 controls were included. Overall, there was no significant association between matrix metalloproteinase-3-1171 5A/6A polymorphism and AD risk in all genetic models (the allele model 5A vs. 6A: odds ratio (OR) = 1.06, 95% CI 0.82–1.36, P = .65; the heterozygote model 5A/6A vs. 6A/6A: OR = 0.98, 95%CI 0.75–1.28, P = .89; the homozygous model 5A/5A vs. 6A/6A: OR = 1.20, 95%CI 0.70–2.07, P = .51; the dominant model 5A/6A + 5A/5A vs. 6A/6A: OR = 1.03, 95%CI 0.76–1.40, P = .85; the recessive model 5A/5A vs. 5A/6A + 6A/6A: OR = 1.24, 95%CI 0.80–1.93, P = .34). In subgroup analysis by ethnicity, no significant difference was detected in both Caucasians and Asians between matrix metalloproteinase-3-1171 5A/6A polymorphism and AD risk. Similar results were obtained in sensitivity analysis.Conclusion:In summary, the present meta-analysis suggests that MMP-3-1171 5A/6A polymorphism may not be associated with genetic susceptibility to AD in the general population. Given the limited sample size and study heterogeneity, findings warrant cautious interpretation and require verification through larger, well-designed trials.

1. Introduction

Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is the most common neurodegenerative disease of the central nervous system characterized by progressive memory loss, confusion and cognitive dysfunction.[1] The main neuropathological hallmarks of AD are the intracellular deposition of neurofibrillary tangles and extracellular amyloid β (Aβ) deposits.[2,3] The etiopathogenesis of AD remains still unclear. Inflammatory processes play a key role in the pathophysiology of AD.[4] One of the mechanisms of AD progression is related to neuroinflammation caused by matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs).

MMPs are zinc-dependent enzymes, which play a crucial role in the degradation of Aβ.[5–7] Recent animal and post-mortem studies indicate that MMPs that are involved in the degradation of connective tissue and extracellular matrix are implicated in the pathogenesis of AD.[8–11] Matrix metalloproteinase-3 (MMP-3), a valuable member of the MMP family, might in particular play a vital role because it activates several latent-type MMPs such as MMP-1, MMP-8, MMP-9 and MMP-13,[6] which in turn are involved in Aβ degradation.[6,10,11] MMP-2 and MMP‑3 are selectively up-regulated by the clioquinol-Cu2+ complex via phosphoinositol 3-kinase/c-Jun N-terminal kinase/extracellular signal-regulated kinase signaling and then directly cleave extracellular Aβ-(1–40/42), accounting for the ~90 % reduction observed. Pharmacological activation of these MMPs thus provides a potent therapeutic route to enhance Aβ clearance in AD.[10]

Published studies suggest that common polymorphisms in the gene encoding MMP-3, in particular the 5A/6A promotor polymorphism, are associated with the risk of AD. However, these studies showed inconsistent results. Currently, meta-analysis of gene-disease associations (including the correlation between AD and genetic factors) has become a key research methodology. Its multiple advantages include enhanced statistical power, resolution of conflicting findings, more precise effect size estimates, identification of heterogeneity sources, and cost-effective research outcomes.[12,13] To better clarify the association between MMP-3-1171 5A/6A polymorphism and risk of AD, we conducted a meta-analysis by collecting and sorting the previously published studies.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Identification and eligibility of relevant studies

A comprehensive systematic literature search was performed in PubMed, Embase, the Chinese National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), and Wanfang Database from their inception to May 17, 2026. The search strategy was developed using a combination of Medical Subject Headings and free-text terms, organized into 2 thematic blocks: MMP-3, AD and polymorphism. Specifically, the search strings included variations of: (“matrix metalloproteinase 3” OR “MMP-3” OR “stromelysin-1”) AND (“Alzheimer disease” OR “Alzheimer’s” OR “AD” OR “senile dementia”). The search was restricted to articles published in English or Chinese. Additionally, the reference lists of retrieved articles and relevant reviews were manually screened to identify potentially missed studies.

2.2. Inclusion and exclusion criteria

All studies included in this meta-analysis must meet the following criteria: comparative case-control studies stating the correlation between the MMP-3-1171 5A/6A variant and AD risk, available genotype and allele data in cases and controls, and sufficient data to determine odds ratios (OR) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). On the contrary, studies without controls, abstracts, review articles, commentaries, unsuitable genotype frequency data, and duplicate publications are excluded.

2.3. Data extraction

Two researchers (Yan Wang and Yanjiao Ma) independently extracted data including year of publication, population characteristics and numbers of genotyped cases and controls. Different ethnicities of participants were categorized as Caucasian and Asian. Any inconsistencies in the data extraction were resolved by discussion among the authors.

2.4. Assessment of study quality and evidence certainty

The methodological quality of these studies was assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale. This scale comprises 3 main domains: Selection (of study participants), Comparability (between groups), and Exposure Assessment (measurement of the exposure factor). These domains encompass a total of 8 items. The Newcastle-Ottawa Scale has a maximum score of 9 points (or stars), with higher scores indicating higher methodological quality. Studies scoring ≥ 7 points were considered high quality, those scoring 4 to 6 points were moderate quality, and those scoring ≤3 points were low quality. The certainty of evidence for all outcome indicators was evaluated using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) methodology. Evidence quality was graded as high, moderate, low, or very low based on consideration of risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and potential publication bias.

2.5. Ethical approval

This meta-analysis used aggregated data from published studies and did not involve direct human subject contact; therefore, ethical approval was waived by the Institutional Review Board of Guangdong Provincial Hospital of Integrated Traditional Chinese and Western Medicine.

2.6. Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was conducted with the software Review Manager (version 5.3) and STATA (version 12.0). ORs with 95% CIs for genotypes and alleles were used to estimate the link between the matrix metalloproteinase-3(MMP-3)-1171 5A/6A polymorphism and AD risk. The resulting ORs were analyzed using 5 genetic models, namely, the allele model (5A vs. 6A), heterozygote model (5A/6A vs. 6A/6A), homozygous model (5A/5A vs. 6A/6A), dominant model (5A/6A + 5A/5A vs. 6A/6A), and recessive model (5A/5A vs. 5A/6A + 6A/6A). Cochran’s Q test and I2 test were used to examine the heterogeneity of the included studies. A random-effects model (DerSimonian-Laird method) was adopted when there was a statistically significant heterogeneity (PQ<.10, I2>50%); otherwise, a fixed-effects model (Mantel-Haenszel method) was used. The Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (HWE) was determined in the control group. To explore the sources of substantial heterogeneity (I2 > 50% in most models), we performed subgroup analysis by ethnicity and conducted a sensitivity analysis by excluding studies with HWE deviation. Additionally, publication bias was assessed using Begg’s test[14] and Egger’s test.[15]

3. Results

3.1. Characteristics of published studies

A systematic literature search was conducted across multiple electronic databases, including PubMed, Embase, CNKI, and Wanfang, as well as relevant registers. The initial search yielded a total of 211 records (PubMed: n = 120; Embase: n = 55; CNKI: n = 34; Wanfang: n = 2; Registers: n = 0). Prior to screening, 19 duplicate records were identified and removed. During the initial screening of titles and abstracts, 158 records were excluded as they did not meet the predefined inclusion criteria. This left 34 reports that were sought for full-text retrieval. Ultimately, 7 eligible case-control studies (1457 cases and 1959 controls)[16–22] that met the inclusion criteria were included in this meta-analysis (Fig. 1), and all studies were published in English. The characteristics of the included studies in the meta-analysis are presented in Table 1. Among all the eligible studies, the subjects in 6 included studies were Caucasian [16,18–22] and the other 1 was Asian.[17] The genotype distributions of all studies in the control group conformed to HWE except for 1 study.[18] Moreover, 5 out of the 7 included studies scored ≥7, indicating high methodological quality.

3.2. Meta-analysis results

To evaluate the MMP-3-1171 5A/6A polymorphism in AD susceptibility, a total of 5 genetic association models were applied. The findings indicated that no statistically significant correlation was observed between the MMP-3-1171 5A/6A polymorphism and the risk of AD in all genetic models (for the allele model 5A vs. 6A: OR = 1.06, 95%CI 0.82–1.36, P = .65; for the heterozygote model 5A/6A vs. 6A/6A: OR = 0.98, 95%CI 0.75–1.28, P = .89; for the homozygous model 5A/5A vs. 6A/6A: OR = 1.20, 95%CI 0.70–2.07, P = .51; for the dominant model 5A/6A + 5A/5A vs. 6A/6A: OR = 1.03, 95%CI 0.76–1.40, P = .85; for the recessive model 5A/5A vs. 5A/6A + 6A/6A: OR = 1.24, 95%CI 0.80–1.93, P = .34) (Table 2, Figures S1-S5). Subgroup analyses by ethnicity further showed that there was also no significant association between MMP-3 -1171 5A/6A polymorphism and susceptibility to AD in both Caucasian and Asian populations (Table 2).

3.3. GRADE evidence quality evaluation

The GRADE approach rated the evidence for the association between MMP‑3 -1171 5A/6A polymorphism and AD risk as very low. Genetic association studies started as low-quality evidence, downgraded due to moderate risk of bias (one study deviated from HWE), substantial heterogeneity (I2 up to 80%), significant publication bias (Egger’s test, P = .017), and moderate imprecision. Subgroup analyses also yielded very low evidence for Caucasian (6 studies, persistent heterogeneity and publication bias) and Asian populations (1 study, limited precision).

3.4. Sensitivity analysis

Sensitivity analysis omitting studies with genotype frequencies deviating from HWE yielded consistent results for the association between the MMP-3 -1171 5A/6A polymorphism and AD (data not displayed).

3.5. Publication bias

Analysis of publication bias was conducted using Begg’s test and Egger’s test. Significant publication bias was observed in the allele model (5A vs. 6A) and the dominant model (5A/6A + 5A/5A vs. 6A/6A) from the outcomes of Egger’s test (P<.05), whereas these models did not show publication bias in Begg’s test (P>.05) (Table 2). On the other hand, the other genetic models did not show any publication bias in both tests (Table 2).

4. Discussion

AD is the most common form of dementia and is considered one of the greatest health challenges worldwide. According to the latest epidemiological report, it is estimated that 4.7 million people aged ≥65 years suffer from AD, and the disease will result in over 6,00,000 deaths, accounting for 32% of all elderly deaths in the United States.[23] By 2050, the number of patients with AD is expected to be 13.8 million, leading to approximately 1.6 million deaths. These deaths will account for 43% of all older adult deaths.[24,25] In both developing and developed countries, cases of AD are likely to increase, and their complications will place a huge mental and financial burden on patients and families.

To date, the etiology and pathogenesis of AD remain unclear. Increasing evidence suggests that genetic abnormalities, including genetic mutations, may confer an increased risk of AD.[26] Identification of the gene that contributes to the development of AD can help us find some effective and preventive interventions to decrease the incidence of the disease. MMP-3 exerts a key role in neuroinflammation and is closely associated with AD pathogenesis. Moreover, studies indicate that Aβ- induced expression of MMP-3 may contribute to the breakdown of the blood-brain barrier and induction of neuroinflammation.[27–29] Hence, MMP-3 gene promoter variants are promising biomarkers associated with AD pathogenesis. It is necessary to investigate the association between AD risk and MMP-3.

Previous studies evaluating the association between MMP-3-1171 5A/6A genetic polymorphism and the risk of AD have produced controversial results. Although studies in Japanese and Slovak populations found no association between this polymorphism and AD,[17,22] MMP-3-1171 5A/6A polymorphism may influence the age at onset of AD in Slovak patients.[22] An association was observed between the MMP-3-1171 5A allele and the 5A/5A genotype and the risk of AD.[16,18,21] Conversely, the association of the MMP-3-1171 6A allele with the risk of AD was also observed.[19,20] To gain a better understanding of the association between AD susceptibility and the MMP-3-1171 5A/6A polymorphism, we conducted a meta-analysis by analyzing previous studies.

In this meta-analysis, 7 studies with a total of 1457 AD cases and 1959 controls on the MMP-3-1171 5A/6A polymorphism were included. As the I2 >50%, the random-effects model was used to calculate the pooled OR in this meta-analysis. These pooled results demonstrated that the MMP-3-1171 5A/6A polymorphism in AD patients did not reveal a significant association in all genetic model. This overall lack of association aligns with the conclusions of some individual studies included in our analysis, such as those in Japanese and Slovak populations [17,22] but contrasts with others that reported significant effects of the 5A allele or 5A/5A genotype in increasing AD risk.[16,18,21] The discrepancy between individual studies and our pooled result highlights the value of meta-analysis in overcoming the limitations of individual studies, such as small sample sizes and potential population stratification. Ethnicity-based subgroup analysis revealed no significant association between the MMP-3 -1171 5A/6A polymorphism and AD risk in either Caucasians or Asians. Moreover, this analysis showed minimal impact on I2 values; for instance, in the Caucasian subgroup, the I2 for 5A vs. 6A remained high at 82% relative to the overall cohort’s I2 of 80%. These results suggest that ethnicity is unlikely to be the primary contributor to heterogeneity.

Assessment of HWE revealed that all studies except 1 in the control group conformed to HWE. Sensitivity analysis eliminating the study with genotype frequencies deviating significantly from HWE yielded consistent findings. Further sensitivity analysis excluding HWE-deviant studies proved more informative. Upon exclusion of that single HWE-violating study, a marked reduction in I2 was observed across all genetic models (e.g., for 5A vs. 6A, I2decreased from 80% to 63%; for 5A/6A vs. 6A/6A, from 52%–0%). These results indicate that methodological factors, particularly genotyping errors or population stratification reflected by HWE deviation, are a major contributor to the observed heterogeneity.

Evidence of publication bias in the allele model and dominant model was observed via Egger’s test, whereas other genetic models showed no significant bias. However, it should be noted that Egger’s regression test and Begg’s test have low ability to bias due to the small number of studies.

There are some limitations in this meta-analysis, so the results should be interpreted with caution. First, the literature searches in this study were limited to English and Chinese articles, which might bias the results. Second, observed heterogeneity may stem from inter-study discrepancies in sample size or related methodological factors. Third, the allele model and dominant model revealed a publishing bias in Egger’s test. Moreover, intergenic interactions and gene-environment interactions were not fully addressed in this study due to the limited amount of currently available data. A further limitation of this meta-analysis is that the protocol was not prospectively registered, which may introduce a risk of reporting bias, although we adhered strictly to the PRISMA 2020 guidelines for transparent reporting. Consistent with these limitations, the GRADE assessment indicated very low-quality evidence for the association between the MMP‑3 -1171 5A/6A polymorphism and AD risk.

In conclusion, our study suggested that the MMP-3-1171 5A/6A polymorphism may not be associated with the risk of AD susceptibility based on the current published studies. However, more studies with large sample sizes, gene-environment interactions, and intergenic interactions are needed to confirm these findings.

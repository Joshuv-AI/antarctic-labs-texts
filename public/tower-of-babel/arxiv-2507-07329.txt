Abstract
. For a fusion category, we prove some new integral properties concerning the dimension of a simple object that generates a Isaacs fusion subcategory. If any such fusion subcategory is Isaacs this implies that 𝒞 \mathcal{C} is a Frobenius type fusion category. A stronger divisibility result is proven for any modular fusion category. This divisibility result implies the converse direction of a Ito-Michler type result for modular fusion categories, see [ Bur25 , Proposition 2.3] .



1.
Introduction

Fusion categories represent a significant advancement in both mathematics and theoretical physics, providing a categorical framework for exploring symmetry and structure across diverse contexts, including quantum field theory, topology, and representation theory. By generalizing classical concepts from group theory, fusion categories address various settings where traditional group symmetries fail to describe the intricacies of complex phenomena.

A central problem in the theory of fusion categories is the generalized Kaplansky’s 6th conjecture, which states that in any spherical fusion category, the dimensions of all simple objects divide the dimension of the category itself.

This conjecture plays a crucial role in the study of fusion categories by offering insights into the structural properties of these mathematical objects. The conjecture is significant because it establishes a deeper connection between fusion categories and representation theory. Moreover, verifying Kaplansky’s sixth conjecture could lead to advancements in understanding the interplay between quantum symmetries and topological features, further enriching the theoretical landscape of both mathematics and physics.

In this paper, we establish new divisibility results for the dimensions of simple objects in fusion categories, generalizing classical results from finite group representation theory. For instance, it is well-known that for a finite group
G
, the dimension of any of its complex irreducible representations divides the index of the center of the group, see
[
Isa76
, Theorem 3.12]
. We show that this property extends to a broad class of fusion categories:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a psudo-unitary ribbon category, and
X
be a simple object of
\mathcal{C}
. Then,

\mathsf{FPdim}(X)\mid\frac{\mathsf{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}{{|{U(\mathcal{C})}|}}.

This result is a special case of a more general divisibility criterion, presented in Theorem
6.4
), involving the dimension of the
center
of a simple object (defined in Equation (
2.15
)).

For modular fusion categories, we prove a stronger constraint:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a modular fusion category, and let
X
be a simple object of
\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathcal{C}={\langle X\rangle}
. Then,

\dim(X)^{2}\mid\frac{\dim\mathcal{C}}{{|{U(\mathcal{C})}|}}.

These theorems arise naturally in the study of
s
-Isaacs fusion categories, a concept introduced in
[
ENO25
]
to investigate integrality properties related to the Liu-Palcoux-Wu invariants and the generalized Kaplansky’s 6th conjecture. This concept builds upon the notion of Isaacs fusion rings as discussed in
[
LPR22
,
LPR23
]
.

Recall
[
ENO25
]
, that a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is called
s
-Isaacs
if for any character
\mu_{j}:{K(\mathcal{C})}\to\mathbb{C}
and any simple object
X\in\mathcal{C}
, the quantity

\lambda_{s}(\mu_{j},X):=(\dim\mathcal{C})^{s}\dim(\mathcal{C}^{j})^{1-s}\frac{\mu_{j}([X])}{\dim(X)}

is an algebraic integer. For the definition of the conjugacy class
\mathcal{C}^{j}
corresponding to the character
\mu_{j}
, see Subsection
2.3
.

The two theorems presented above are particular cases of the following more general results concerning
s
-Isaacs fusion categories.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
s\geq 1/2
be a rational number and
\mathcal{C}
be a spherical fusion category such that
{\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}}
is real non-negative (RN). Suppose that
X
is a simple object such that the fusion subcategory
{\langle X\rangle}
is
s
-Isaacs. Then:

\frac{(\dim\mathcal{C})^{2s}\mathsf{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}{\dim(X)^{2}{|{U(\mathcal{C})}|}}\in\mathbb{A}.

The above theorem follows from a more general version, see Theorem
5.5
, involving the aforementioned notion of the center of a simple object. The definition of the dual space
{\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}}
and the
real non-negative (RN)
condition are described in Section
2
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
s\geq 0
be a rational number and
\mathcal{C}
be a spherical fusion category such that
{\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}}
is real non-negative (RN). Suppose that
X
is a simple object such that the fusion subcategory
{\langle X\rangle}
is
s
-Isaacs. Then:

\frac{(\dim\mathcal{C})^{s}\mathsf{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}{\dim(X){|{U(\mathcal{C})}|}}\in\mathbb{A}.

The above theorem also generalize further (see Theorem
6.3
,) by incorporating
the center
of a simple object. Notably, since every ribbon category is Isaacs (
s
= 0)
[
ENO25
, Proposition 5.2]
, and
{\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}}
is real non-negative (RN) in this case, these results apply broadly to ribbon fusion categories.

1.1.
On the converse part of Ito-Michler

A celebrated result in finite group theory, the Ito-Michler theorem, see
[
Mic86
]
, states that for a finite group
G
and a prime
p
dividing the order of
G
the following are equivalent:

(a)

No irreducible character of
G
has degree divisible by
p
.

(b)

A Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
is both abelian and normal.

In
[
Bur25
]
we explored an analogue of
(a)\implies(b)
for modular categories.
More precisely, we have proved the following proposition:

Proposition 1.5
.

[
Bur25
, Proposition 2.3]

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a weakly integral modular fusion category, and let
p
be a prime divisor of
\mathsf{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})
such that
\mathsf{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})=p^{\alpha}N
with
\alpha\geq 1
and
(p,N)=1
. If
p
does not divides the Frobenius-Perron dimension of any of its simple objects, then
p^{\alpha}
divides
|U(\mathcal{C})|
, the order of the universal grading group of
\mathcal{C}
.

In this paper, we use Theorem
1.2
to demonstrate the other direction,
(b)\implies(a)
. Notably, this direction can be extended to the slightly broader context of braided categories:

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is a weakly integral braided fusion category. If
\mathsf{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})=p^{\alpha}N
with
(p,N)=1
and
p^{\alpha}\mid{|{U(\mathcal{C})}|}
, then
\mathsf{FPdim}(X)^{2}
is not divisible by
p
for any simple object
X
of
\mathcal{C}
.

Note that since
\mathcal{C}
is weakly integral then
\mathsf{FPdim}(X)^{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
for all any
X\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})
, see
[
GN08
, Theorem 3. 10]
.

For modular categories, this yields a full analogue of the Ito-Michler theorem:

Corollary 1.7

(Complete Ito-Michler analogue)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a weakly integral modular fusion category, and let
p
be a prime divisor of
\mathsf{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})
such that
\mathsf{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})=p^{\alpha}N
with
\alpha\geq 1
and
(p,N)=1
. Then
p
does not divides the Frobenius-Perron dimension of any of its simple objects if and only if
p^{\alpha}
divides
|U(\mathcal{C})|
, the order of the universal grading group of
\mathcal{C}
.

.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we work over the base field
\mathbb{C}
. For
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
with
\beta\neq 0
, we write
\beta\mid\alpha
if
\alpha/\beta\in\mathbb{A}
, the ring of algebraic integers.

For a detailed exposition on fusion categories, we recommend the monograph
[
EGNO15
]
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category. We will refer to the maximal pointed fusion subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
as
{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pt}}}
, and let
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})
represent a set of representatives for the isomorphism classes of simple objects within
\mathcal{C}
. The cardinality of
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})
is called the
rank of
\mathcal{C}
, denoted as
\text{rank\,}(\mathcal{C})
. We let
{K(\mathcal{C})}:={K_{0}(\mathcal{C})}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
, where
{K_{0}(\mathcal{C})}
is the Grothendieck ring of
\mathcal{C}
.

2.1.
Pivotal and Spherical Structures

A
pivotal structure
on a rigid monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
is a monoidal isomorphism
j:\text{id}_{\mathcal{C}}\to()^{**}
between monoidal functors. A fusion category with such a structure is called
pivotal
. As in
[
EGNO15
]
, a pivotal fusion category is
spherical
if and only if
\dim(V)=\dim(V^{*})
for all simple objects
V
, where
\dim
denotes the
quantum dimension
of
\mathcal{C}
. A fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is
pseudo-unitary
if
\mathsf{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})=\dim\mathcal{C}
. In this context, according to
[
ENO05
, Proposition 8.23]
, the category
\mathcal{C}
possesses a unique spherical structure, designated as the
canonical spherical structure
, which ensures that the categorical dimensions of all simple objects are positive. Under this spherical structure, the categorical dimension of any object
X
in
\mathcal{C}
coincides with its Frobenius–Perron dimension, i.e.,
\mathsf{FPdim}(X)=\dim(X)
holds for every object
X
. A fusion category is referred to as
weakly integral
if its Frobenius-Perron dimension is an integer. Moreover, it is termed
integral
if the Frobenius-Perron dimension of each object is an integer. By
[
ENO05
, Proposition 8.24]
, weakly integral fusion categories are pseudo-unitary.

2.2.
Drinfeld center and adjunction

For any fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, its Drinfeld center
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
is a braided fusion category
{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
equipped with a forgetful functor
F:{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
, as described in
[
EGNO15
]
.

The forgetful functor
F
has a right adjoint functor denoted as
R:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
, allowing us to define a Hopf comonad
Z:=FR:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
as an end.

Indeed, following
[
Shi16
, Section 2.6]
, it is established that

(2.1)

Z(V)\simeq\int_{X\in\mathcal{C}}X\otimes V\otimes X^{*}

It is known that the object
A:=Z(\mathbf{1})
(the image of the unit object) constitutes a (central) commutative algebra
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
, referred to as
the adjoint algebra
of
\mathcal{C}
.

The vector space
\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}:=\mathrm{{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{1},A)
is called the
the set of central elements
. The vector space
{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}:=\mathrm{{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,\mathbf{1})
is known as
the space of class functions
of
\mathcal{C}
.

For a fusion category over
\mathbb{C}
, according to
[
Shi16
, Example 4.4]
, there is a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra isomorphism
{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}\simeq{K_{0}(\mathcal{C})}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
. Therefore,
{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}
is commutative if and only if
{K_{0}(\mathcal{C})}
is commutative. Both
{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}
and
\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
possess a semisimple
\mathbb{C}
-algebra structure, as described in
[
Shi16
]
. Furthermore,
\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
is a commutative algebra, implying that it decomposes as a direct product of copies of the field
\mathbb{C}
.

Given that
R:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
is a right adjoint to the forgetful functor
F:{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
,
[
Shi16
, Theorem 3.8]
establishes that this adjunction induces an isomorphism of
\mathbb{C}
-algebras:

(2.2)

{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathrm{End}_{{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}}(R(\mathbf{1})),\;\;\chi\mapsto Z(\chi)\circ\delta_{\mathbf{1}}.

where
\delta_{\mathbf{1}}
represents the comultiplication of the Hopf comonad
Z
associated at the unit
\mathbf{1}
of the fusion category
\mathcal{C}
.

Example 2.1
.

If
H
is a semisimple Hopf algebra, then, for
\mathcal{C}=\text{Rep}(H)
, it follows from
[
Shi16
, Subsect. 3.7]
that
{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}\simeq C(H)
, the
character algebra
of
H
. Recall that
C(H)
consists of all linear trace maps
f:H\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
.

2.3.
A bijection on the set of conjugacy class sums

Assume that
\mathcal{C}
is a pivotal fusion category with a commutative Grothendieck ring
K(\mathcal{C})
. Since
{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
is also a fusion category, we can express
R(\mathbf{1})=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i}
as a direct sum of simple objects in
{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
. It is noteworthy that any two distinct simple objects
\mathcal{C}^{i}
are not isomorphic to one another, since from the isomorphism of Equation (
2.2
) the endomorphism ring of
R(\mathbf{1})
is a commutative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra. We refer to,
\mathcal{C}^{0},\dots,\mathcal{C}^{m}
as
the conjugacy classes of
\mathcal{C}
.
Since the unit object
\mathbf{1}_{{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}}
is clearly a sub-object of
R(\mathbf{1})
, we can assume
\mathcal{C}^{0}=\mathbf{1}_{{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}}
.

In
[
Shi16
, Section 5]
, Shimizu also established a canonical pairing between
{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}
and
\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
with values in
\mathbb{C}
, defined by the relation:

(2.3)

f\circ a=\langle f,a\rangle\text{id}_{\mathbf{1}}

for all
f\in{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}
and
a\in\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
.

Let
\tilde{F}_{0},\tilde{F}_{1},\dots,\tilde{F}_{m}\in\mathrm{End}_{{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}}(R(\mathbf{1}))
denote the canonical projections onto each of the conjugacy classes. The corresponding primitive idempotents of
{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}
under the canonical adjunction isomorphism
{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}\simeq\mathrm{End}_{{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}}(R(\mathbf{1}))
from Equation (
2.2
) will be denoted as
F_{0},F_{1},\dots,F_{m}
. This establishes a canonical bijection between the (central) primitive idempotents of
{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}
and the conjugacy classes of
\mathcal{C}
. Let
\mathcal{J}:=\{0,1,\dots,m\}
represent the set of indices corresponding to the central primitive idempotents of
{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}
. For each
F_{j}
with
j\in\mathcal{J}
, the aforementioned bijection assigns a conjugacy class
\mathcal{C}^{j}
. Since
{K(\mathcal{C})}
is commutative, we have
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}{K(\mathcal{C})}=m+1
and
\{F_{j}\}
for a
\mathbb{C}
-linear basis of
{K(\mathcal{C})}
.

2.4.
On the definition of conjugacy class sums

In a pivotal fusion category, Shimizu defines the elements
C_{j}:=\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}^{-1}(F_{j})\in\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
as the
conjugacy class sums
corresponding to the conjugacy class
\mathcal{C}^{j}
. Here,
\lambda\in\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
represents a cointegral of
\mathcal{C}
such that
\langle\lambda,u\rangle=1
(refer to
[
Shi16
, Section 5]
). Moreover, the morphism
u:\mathbf{1}\rightarrow A
represents the unit of the central algebra
A
, and
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
is the
Fourier transform
of
\mathcal{C}
associated with
\lambda
. This is a linear map defined by:

(2.4)

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}:\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}\;\;\text{given by}\;\;a\mapsto\lambda\leftharpoonup\mathcal{S}(a)

where
\mathcal{S}
denotes the antipode.

2.5.
The integral of a fusion category

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category, and let
A=Z(\mathbf{1})
be its adjoint algebra as previously defined. In accordance with
[
Shi16
, Definition 5.7]
, an
integral
in
\mathcal{C}
is represented as a morphism
\Lambda:\mathbf{1}\rightarrow A
in
\mathcal{C}
satisfying

m\circ(\text{id}_{A}\otimes\Lambda)=\varepsilon_{\mathbf{1}}\otimes\Lambda,

where
m:A\otimes A\rightarrow\mathbf{1}
denotes the multiplication of the adjoint algebra
A
. Here,
\varepsilon_{\mathbf{1}}
represents the counit
\varepsilon_{V}:Z(V)\rightarrow V
specialised at
V=\mathbf{1}
, the unit object of the fusion category
\mathcal{C}
.

It is established that the integral of a fusion category is unique up to a scalar. Let
\Lambda\in\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
denote the idempotent integral of
\mathcal{C}
and define
\widehat{\Lambda}:=\dim(\mathcal{C})\Lambda
.

Let
\{V_{0},\dots,V_{m}\}
be a complete set of representatives of the isomorphism classes of simple objects in
\mathcal{C}
. Shimizu provided, in
[
Shi16
, Section 6]
, a construction of the (central) primitive elements
E_{i}\in\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
, corresponding to the irreducible characters
\chi_{i}:=\chi_{V_{i}}
associated to each simple object
V_{i}
of
\mathcal{C}
. This correspondence is characterized by the condition:

\langle\chi_{i},E_{j}\rangle=\delta_{i,j},

for all
i,j
.

Assuming that
{K(\mathcal{C})}
is commutative, note that
[
Bur20
, Lemma 4.1]
, along with Equation (4.7) from the same paper, provides an alternative pair of dual bases for the canonical pairing. More explicitly, it follows that
\{F_{j}\}
and
\{\frac{C_{j}}{\dim(\mathcal{C}^{j})}\}
form another pair of dual bases expressed as:

(2.5)

\langle F_{j},\frac{C_{j^{\prime}}}{\dim(\mathcal{C}^{{j^{\prime}}})}\rangle=\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}

for all
j,j^{\prime}\in\mathcal{J}
.

Without loss of generality, we can set
V_{0}=\mathbf{1}
, the unit of the fusion category
\mathcal{C}
. Consequently, we have
\chi_{0}=\varepsilon_{\mathbf{1}}
, and the associated primitive idempotent is
E_{0}=\Lambda
, as seen in
[
Shi16
, Lemma 6.1]
.

By
[
Bur25
, Lemma 2.1]
we have that

(2.6)

\widehat{\Lambda}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}C_{j}.

for any a pivotal fusion category
\mathcal{C}
with a commutative Grothendieck ring.

2.6.
Orthogonality relations

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category with a commutative Grothendieck ring
{K_{0}(\mathcal{C})}
. Define
{\widehat{\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}}
as the set of all linear characters
\mu:{K(\mathcal{C})}\to\mathbb{C}
. Given that
{K(\mathcal{C})}
is commutative,
{\widehat{\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}}
forms a basis for the linear dual space
{K(\mathcal{C})}^{*}
, denoted by
{\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}}
. Thus, we have
{\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}}:=\mathbb{C}[{\widehat{\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}}]
.

In
{K(\mathcal{C})}
, the orthogonality relations are as follows. The first orthogonality relation from
[
BP24
, Equation (2.5)]
expressed as:

(2.7)

\sum_{i=0}^{m}\mu_{j}([X_{i}])\mu_{k}([X_{i^{*}}])=\delta_{j,k}n_{j},

where
n_{j}
represents the
formal codegree
of
\mu_{j}
, as as described in
[
BP24
, Subsection 2.1]
or
[
Ost15
]
. For
j=k
, this relation simplifies to:

(2.8)

\sum_{i=0}^{m}|\mu_{j}([X_{i}])|^{2}=n_{j}.

The second orthogonality relation in
{K(\mathcal{C})}
, from
[
BP24
, Equation (2.6)]
, (see also
[
Shi16
, Corollary 6.11]
), can be written as:

(2.9)

\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{\dim(\mathcal{C}^{j})}{\dim\mathcal{C}}\mu_{j}([X])\mu_{j}([Y^{*}])=\delta_{[X],[Y]},

for any simple objects
X
and
Y
of
\mathcal{C}
.

2.7.
{\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}}
multiplication

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a pivotal fusion category with Grothendieck group
{K_{0}(\mathcal{C})}
. Recall
{K(\mathcal{C})}={K_{0}(\mathcal{C})}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
. We define a multiplication on
{\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}}
as follows. For two linear characters
\mu_{i},\mu_{j}\in{\widehat{\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}}
, their multiplication is given by:

(2.10)

[\mu_{i}\star\mu_{j}]({\frac{[X]}{\mathsf{FPdim}(X)}}):=\mu_{i}({\frac{[X]}{\mathsf{FPdim}(X)}})\mu_{j}({\frac{[X]}{\mathsf{FPdim}(X)}}),\;\text{for all}\;X\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C}).

This multiplication can be extended linearly to the entire space
{\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}}
, yielding an algebra structure on
{\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}}
.

Additionally, there exists an involution on
\widehat{K(\mathcal{C})}
defined on the basis
{\widehat{\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}}
by
\mu_{j}\mapsto\mu_{j^{\#}}
, where
\mu_{j^{\#}}
is defined by
\mu_{j^{\#}}([X]):=\mu_{j}([X^{*}])
for all
X\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})
. It is also well-known that
\mu_{j}([X^{*}])=\overline{\mu_{j}([X])}
for all
X\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})
.

Without loss of generality, we may suppose that
\mu_{0}:=\mathsf{FPdim}
, i.e
\mu_{0}([X])=\mathsf{FPdim}(X)
, for all
X\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})
. Equation (
2.10
) implies that
\mu_{0}
is the unit of
{\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}}
. Following the terminology in
[
BP24
, Section 2]
,
{\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}}
becomes the dual hypergroup of the abelian normalizable hypergroup
{K(\mathcal{C})}
. Since
{\widehat{\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}}
is a linear basis for
{\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}}
, there exist non-zero scalars
{\widehat{p}}_{k}(i,j)\in\mathbb{C}
such that:

(2.11)

\mu_{i}\star\mu_{j}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}{\widehat{p}}_{k}(i,j)\mu_{k}.

We refer to
{\widehat{p}}_{k}(i,j)
as
dual-structure fusion coefficients.
. A formula for the dual fusion coefficients
{\widehat{p}}_{k}(i,j)
is provided in
[
Bur21
, Proposition 2.1]
. In the fusion category settings, this can be expressed as:

(2.12)

\widehat{p}_{k}(iweaklyintegral,j)=\frac{1}{n_{k}}\bigg(\sum_{X\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}\frac{1}{\mathsf{FPdim}(X)}\mu_{i}([X])\mu_{j}([X])\mu_{k}([X^{*}])\bigg),

where
n_{k}
, as above, it is the formal codegree of
\mu_{k}
. According to
[
Bur20
, Equation (4.7)]
, for any spherical fusion category, we have

(2.13)

n_{j}=\frac{\dim(\mathcal{C})}{\dim(\mathcal{C}^{j})}.

See also
[
Ost15
, Theorem 2.13]
for a related statement.

For a weakly integral fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, by
[
ENO05
, Proposition 8.27]
it follows that
\dim(\mathcal{C}^{j})\in\mathbb{Z}
.

2.8.
Kernels and centers of objects

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category with a commutative Grothendieck ring. For any object
X\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})
, as in
[
Bur16
]
, we define its
kernel
as follows,

(2.14)

\ker_{\mathcal{C}}(X):=\{\psi\in{\widehat{\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}}\;|\;\psi([X])=\mathsf{FPdim}(X)\}.

The
center of an object

X\in\mathcal{C}
is defined as:

(2.15)

Z_{\mathcal{C}}(X)=\{\psi\in{\widehat{\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}}\;|\;|\psi([X])|=\mathsf{FPdim}(X)\}.

Following
[
BP24
, Definition 5.2]
, we can also define the dual notion of kernels as:

(2.16)

\ker_{\widehat{K(\mathcal{C})}}(\psi):=\{X\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})\>|\;\psi(X)=\mathsf{FPdim}(X)\}.

According to
[
BP24
, Lemma 5.3]
, the set
\ker_{\widehat{K(\mathcal{C})}}(\psi)
is closed under tensor products and taking duals, thereby generating a fusion subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
. Additionally, we define the dual notion of the center as:

(2.17)

Z_{{}_{\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}}}(\psi)=\{X\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})\;|\;|\psi(X)|=\mathsf{FPdim}(X)\}.

By
[
BP24
, Lemma 5.3]
, the set
Z_{\widehat{K(\mathcal{C})}}(\psi)
is also closed under tensor products and taking duals, thus generating a fusion subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
.

2.9.
Group-like elements of
{\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}}

Following
[
BP24
, Definition 1.3]
, we say that an algebra morphism
\mu_{j}\in{\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}}
is a
group-like element
if
\mu_{j}\star\mu_{j^{\#}}=\widehat{p}_{0}(j,j^{\#})\mu_{0}
. Evaluating both sides of the above equation at the unit
[\mathbf{1}]
of
{K(\mathcal{C})}
, we find that
\widehat{p}_{0}(j,j^{\#})=1
. Therefore,
\mu_{j}\in{\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}}
is a group-like element of
{\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}}
if and only if

(2.18)

\mu_{j}\star\mu_{j^{\#}}=\mu_{0}.

Remark 2.1
.

Since
{K(\mathcal{C})}
is an real non-negative (RN) hypergroup, Theorem
[
BP24
, Theorem 7.8]
implies that for any fusion category
\mathcal{C}
we have
U(\mathcal{C})\simeq{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}
.

We now prove the following:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category with a commutative Grothendieck ring. With the above notations, we have:

{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}=\bigcap_{X\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}{Z_{\mathcal{C}}(X)}

Proof.

For every
\mu\in{\widehat{\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}}
, as mentioned in
[
BP24
, Lemma 2.21]
, it follows that
\mu\in{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}
if and only if the condition below is satisfied for all
X\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})
:

(2.19)

|\mu([X])|=\mathsf{FPdim}(X).

This establishes that
{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}=\bigcap_{X\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}{Z_{\mathcal{C}}(X)}
.
∎

2.10.

Following the definition from

\omega_{X}:{\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\;\omega_{X}(\mu_{j})=\mu_{j}(\frac{[X]}{\mathsf{FPdim}(X)}).

]

in Equation (

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category with a commutative Grothendieck group. If

is a faithful simple object of

Relation with the adjoint subcategory

Next lemma is an analogue of item e) of

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category such that. (e.g, pseudo-unitary braided, or unitary). Then for any object
X
, we have that:

Z_{\mathcal{C}}(X)\subseteq({\langle X\rangle}_{ad})^{\perp}.

Moreover, suppose that
X
is a simple object of
\mathcal{C}
, then

(2.26)

{Z_{\mathcal{C}}(X)}=({\langle X\rangle}_{ad})^{\perp}.

Proof.

By definition, we have:

{Z_{\mathcal{C}}(X)}=\{\mu\in{\widehat{\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}}\;|\;\big|\mu(X)\big|=\mathsf{FPdim}(X)\}.

Let
\mu\in{Z_{\mathcal{C}}(X)}
. The fusion subcategory
{\langle X\rangle}_{ad}
is generated by
Y\otimes Y^{*}
for
Y
a simple object of
{\langle X\rangle}
. If
Y\in{\langle X\rangle}
, then
Y
is a constituent of some tensor power
X^{\otimes m}
of
X
. Thus, by
[
BP24
, Lemma 2.35]
, for any
\mu\in{Z_{\mathcal{C}}(X)}
, we have
\mu([Y])=\omega\mathsf{FPdim}(Y)
for some root of unity
\omega
. This implies that
\mu([Y\otimes Y^{*}])=\mathsf{FPdim}(Y)^{2}
, showing that
\mu\in({\langle X\rangle}_{ad})^{\perp}
. This proves the inclusion
Z_{\mathcal{C}}(X)\subseteq({\langle X\rangle}_{ad})^{\perp}
.

Now, suppose
X
is simple. If
\mu\in({\langle X\rangle}_{ad})^{\perp}
, then
\mu([X\otimes X^{*}])=\mathsf{FPdim}(X)^{2}
. Since
X
is a simple object we have
\mu([X^{*}])=\overline{\mu([X])}
, and this implies
\big|\mu(X)\big|=\mathsf{FPdim}(X)
, i.e.,
\mu\in{Z_{\mathcal{C}}(X)}
. Thus the converse inclusion also holds when
X
is a simple object.
∎

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category such that
{\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}}

3.
On the size of character classes

Throughout this section, let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category with a commutative Grothendieck ring,
K(\mathcal{C})
. Then as above,
{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}
is a commutative semisimple algebra isomorphic to
{K(\mathcal{C})}\cong K_{0}(\mathcal{C})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
. As above, Let
\mathcal{J}^{\mathcal{C}}:=\{0,\dots,m\}
represent the set of indices corresponding to the conjugacy classes of
\mathcal{C}
. It is clear that the rank of
\mathcal{C}
is
\text{rank\,}(\mathcal{C})=|\mathcal{J}^{\mathcal{C}}|=m+1
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{D}}
is a fusion subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
. Note that the Grothendieck ring
K({\mathcal{D}})
is also commutative, and, as in the case above,
K({\mathcal{D}})\simeq\mathrm{{CF}}({\mathcal{D}})
as algebras. We let
\mathcal{J}^{{\mathcal{D}}}:=\{0,\dots,m^{\prime}\}
represent the set of indices corresponding to the conjugacy classes of
{\mathcal{D}}
. As above, it is clear that the rank of
{\mathcal{D}}
is
\text{rank\,}({\mathcal{D}})=|\mathcal{J}^{\mathcal{D}}|=m^{\prime}+1
.

Let
\{F_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq m}
be the (central) primitive idempotents of
{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}
, and let
\{\mathcal{C}^{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq m}
be their associated conjugacy classes. For each (central) primitive idempotent
F_{j}\in{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}
, let
\mu_{j}:{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
denote its associated linear character. Similarly, let
\{G_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq m^{\prime}}
be the (central) primitive idempotents of
\mathrm{{CF}}({\mathcal{D}})
, with
\{{\mathcal{D}}^{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq m^{\prime}}
being their associated conjugacy classes. Additionally, let
\nu_{t}:{\mathrm{CF}({\mathcal{D}})}\to\mathbb{C}
denote the characters associated with these idempotents.

We say that two characters
\mu_{j},\mu_{j^{\prime}}:{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
are
{\mathcal{D}}
-equivalent
(
\mu_{j}\simeq_{\mathcal{D}}\mu_{j^{\prime}}
) if their restrictions to
\mathrm{{CF}}({\mathcal{D}})
coincide.

The set of equivalence classes is denoted by
\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{C}:{\mathcal{D}})
. There is a natural bijection between this set and the characters of
{\mathrm{CF}({\mathcal{D}})}
.

Moreover, an element
C\in\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{C}:{\mathcal{D}})
consists of all the indices
j
such that the restriction
\mu_{j}\downarrow_{{\mathrm{CF}({\mathcal{D}})}}
corresponds to the same character in
{\mathrm{CF}({\mathcal{D}})}
, denoted as
\rho_{C}:=\nu_{s}
for some
s\leq m^{\prime}
. Thus, we use
\rho_{C}
to represent the common restriction to
{\mathrm{CF}({\mathcal{D}})}
of all characters
\mu_{j}
belonging to some class
C\in\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{C}:{\mathcal{D}})
.

As noted above, the set of character classes of
\mathcal{C}
relative to
{\mathcal{D}}
corresponds bijectively to the characters of
{\mathrm{CF}({\mathcal{D}})}
. Consequently, the central primitive idempotents of
{\mathrm{CF}({\mathcal{D}})}
are also indexed by the classes
C\in\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{C}:{\mathcal{D}})
. We denote the central primitive idempotent associated with a class
C\in\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{C}:{\mathcal{D}})
by
G_{C}\in\mathrm{{CF}}({\mathcal{D}})
. Thus, the elements
G_{C}
for
C\in\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{C}:{\mathcal{D}})
form a linear basis for
\mathrm{{CF}}({\mathcal{D}})
, consisting of the complete set of (central) primitive idempotents of the commutative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
\mathrm{{CF}}({\mathcal{D}})
. For the remainder of this section, we identify the set of indices
\mathcal{J}^{\mathcal{D}}
of the central primitive idempotents of
\mathrm{{CF}}({\mathcal{D}})
with the elements of
\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{C}:{\mathcal{D}})
. For any primitive idempotent
G_{C}
of
{\mathrm{CF}({\mathcal{D}})}
, as described in Subsection
2.3
, let
\mathrm{D}_{C}\in\mathrm{{CE}({\mathcal{D}})}
denote the corresponding conjugacy class sum of
{\mathcal{D}}
associated with

.

In
[
Bur20
, Appendix]
we have constructed two

-algebra maps, denoted by

\pi_{f}:{\mathrm{CF}({\mathcal{D}})}\rightarrow{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}\;\text{and},\;{\pi_{e}}:\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow\mathrm{{CE}({\mathcal{D}})}.

Furthermore, by
[

it is known that

is a monomorphism and

is an epimorphism.

If

3.1.
Main Size Theorem

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a pivotal fusion category with a commutative Grothendieck ring
{K(\mathcal{C})}
, and
{\mathcal{D}}={\langle X\rangle}
with
X\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})
. Then, with the above notations, we have:

(3.7)

\frac{\dim\mathcal{C}}{{n_{dim}}(Z_{\mathcal{C}}(X))}=\frac{\dim{\mathcal{D}}}{|U({\mathcal{D}})|}.

Proof.

It is straightforward to see that
{Z_{\mathcal{C}}(X)}
is a union of character classes
C\in\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{C}:{\mathcal{D}})
. Specifically, if
\mu_{1}\simeq\mu_{2}
and
\mu_{1}\in{Z_{\mathcal{C}}(X)}
, then since
X
is a simple object of
{\mathcal{D}}
, we have
\mu_{2}([X])=\mu_{1}([X])
. Consequently,
|\mu_{2}([X])|=\mathsf{FPdim}(X)
, which implies that
\mu_{2}\in{Z_{\mathcal{C}}(X)}
as well.

Since
{\mathcal{D}}
is a fusion subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
, there is an algebra inclusion
K({\mathcal{D}})\subseteq K(\mathcal{C})
, which gives rise to a well-defined restriction map:

{\mathrm{Res}}:{\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}}\rightarrow{\widehat{{\mathrm{K}}({\mathcal{D}})}},\;\mu\mapsto\mu\big|_{\widehat{{\mathrm{K}}({\mathcal{D}})}}.

Moreover, from the definition of the centers, we have
\mu\in{Z_{\mathcal{C}}(X)}
if and only if
{\mathrm{Res}}(\mu)\in{Z_{\mathcal{D}}(X)}
. Suppose that
{Z_{\mathcal{C}}(X)}=C_{1}\sqcup C_{2}\dots\sqcup C_{r}
with
C_{i}\in\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{C}:{\mathcal{D}})
. Then, we can express the total order as:
{n_{dim}}({Z_{\mathcal{C}}(X)})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}{n_{dim}}(C_{i})
. By Equation (
3.3
), we know that
{n_{dim}}(C_{i})=\frac{\dim\mathcal{C}}{\dim{\mathcal{D}}}
, for all
1\leq i\leq r
. Therefore, we have:
{n_{dim}}({Z_{\mathcal{C}}(X)})=r\frac{\dim\mathcal{C}}{\dim{\mathcal{D}}}
.

On the other hand, note that
r
is the cardinality of the set
Z_{\mathcal{D}}(X)
. By Lemma
2.5
we have
r=|G({\widehat{{\mathrm{K}}({\mathcal{D}})}})|
, and then
r=|U({\mathcal{D}})|
by Remark
2.1
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Note that
n({G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})})=|{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}|
since
{\widehat{h}}_{j}=1
for all
\mu_{j}\in{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}
. For a spherical fusion category, the above Theorem and Equation (
3.5
) gives that:

(3.8)

\frac{\mathsf{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}{n(Z_{\mathcal{C}}(X))}=\frac{\dim\mathcal{C}}{{n_{dim}}(Z_{\mathcal{C}}(X))}=\frac{\dim{\mathcal{D}}}{|U({\mathcal{D}})|}.

4.
Action of dual group-like elements

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category with a commutative Grothendieck group
{K_{0}(\mathcal{C})}
. By
[
BP24
, Corollary 2.39]
, since
{K(\mathcal{C})}
is an abelian real non-negative (RN) hypergroup, it follows that
{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}
is a group. Moreover, for any
\mu\in{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}
, left multiplication by
\mu
permutes the dual basis
{\widehat{\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}}
. Specifically, if
\mu_{i}\in{\widehat{\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}}
and
\mu_{z}\in{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}
then
\mu_{z}\star\mu_{i}=\mu_{j}
for some
\mu_{j}\in{\widehat{\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}}
.

4.1.
Invariance properties under the group action

We begin by establishing several key invariance properties of characters under the
{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}
-action.

Lemma 4.1
.

Under the
{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}
-action on
{\widehat{\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}}
by left multiplication, for any simple object
Y\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})
, the absolute value

\big|\mu_{j}([Y])\big|
is constant on each orbit of this action.

Proof.

Suppose
\mu_{j_{1}}=\mu_{j_{2}}\star\mu_{z}
for some
z\in Z
. Then we compute:

\displaystyle\mu_{j_{2}}([Y])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mu_{j_{1}}\star\mu_{z}]([Y])=\mathsf{FPdim}(Y)[\mu_{j_{1}}\star\mu_{z}](\frac{[Y]}{\mathsf{FPdim}(Y)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{FPdim}(Y)\mu_{j_{1}}(\frac{[Y]}{\mathsf{FPdim}(Y)})\mu_{z}(\frac{[Y]}{\mathsf{FPdim}(Y)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{j_{1}}([Y])\mu_{z}(\frac{[Y]}{\mathsf{FPdim}(Y)}).

Taking absolute values and applying Equation (
2.19
) yields
|\mu_{j_{1}}([Y])|=|\mu_{j_{2}}([Y])|
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Under the
{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}
-action, the formal codegree
n_{j}
is constant on each orbit.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mu_{j}=\mu_{z}\star\mu_{k}
for some
\mu_{z}\in{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}
. Since
|\mu_{z}(Y)|=\mathsf{FPdim}(Y)
for all
Y\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})
we have:

n_{j}=\sum_{Y\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}|\mu_{j}(Y)|^{2}=\sum_{Y\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}|\mu_{k}(Y)|^{2}=n_{k}.

Thus, characters in the same orbit share the same formal codegree.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

For a spherical fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, the dimension
\dim(\mathcal{C}^{j})
is constant on each orbit of the
{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}
-action.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Lemma
4.2
and Equation (
2.13
), which relates formal codegrees to dimensions in the spherical case.
∎

4.2.
Single Generator Subcategory Case
\mathcal{C}={\langle X\rangle}

We now specialize to the case where
\mathcal{C}
is generated by a single simple object
X
.

Definition 4.4
.

A character
\mu\in{\widehat{\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}}
is
non-vanishing
if
\mu([X])\neq 0
. An orbit
\mathcal{O}\subset{\widehat{\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}}
is called a non-vanishing orbit if it consists of non-vanishing characters.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}={\langle X\rangle}
with
X\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})
. Then the
{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}
-action on
{\widehat{\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}}
is free when restricted to any non-vanishing orbit.

Proof.

Suppose
\mu_{z}\star\mu=\mu
for some non-vanishing
\mu\in{\widehat{\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}}
and
\mu_{z}\in{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}
. Evaluating at
{\frac{[X]}{\mathsf{FPdim}(X)}}
gives that
\mu_{z}({\frac{[X]}{\mathsf{FPdim}(X)}})\mu({\frac{[X]}{\mathsf{FPdim}(X)}})=\mu({\frac{[X]}{\mathsf{FPdim}(X)}})
. Since
\mu({\frac{[X]}{\mathsf{FPdim}(X)}})\neq 0
, we conclude
\mu_{z}({\frac{[X]}{\mathsf{FPdim}(X)}})=1
. As
X
is faithful, Lemma
2.3
implies
\mu_{z}=\mu_{1}
, the identity of
{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}
.
∎

5.
Isaacs fusion categories with
s\geq 1/2

In this section we prove some results for
s
-Isaacs fusion categories for a rational number,
s\geq 1/2
. In particular, we prove Theorem
1.3
.

5.1.
Definition and Basic Properties

Let
s\geq 0
be a rational number.
Following
[
ENO25
]
, we have the following definition:

Definition 5.1
.

We say that
\mathcal{C}
is
s
-
Isaacs
if for any linear character
\mu_{j}:{K(\mathcal{C})}\to\mathbb{C}
and any simple object
X\in\mathcal{C}
,

\lambda_{s}(\mu_{j},X):=(\dim\mathcal{C})^{s}\dim(\mathcal{C}^{j})^{1-s}\frac{\mu_{j}([X])}{\dim(X)}\in\mathbb{A}

where
\mathbb{A}
denotes the algebraic integers.

Remark 5.2
.

Since
\dim(\mathcal{C}^{j})
divides
\dim\mathcal{C}
, if
\mathcal{C}
is
s
-Isaacs then it is
t
-Isaacs for any
t>s
. As in
[
ENO25
]
, the
0
-Isaacs property will simply be called the Isaacs property. This was introduced in
[
LPR22
,
LPR23
]
and by
[
ENO25
, Proposition 5.2]
, any ribbon fusion category is Isaacs.

Recall that
\mathcal{C}
is said to be Frobenius type if dimensions of its simple objects divide
\dim\mathcal{C}
.
The Kaplansky 6th conjecture for fusion categories states that any spherical fusion category
is Frobenius type.

Following
[
ENO25
]
, we say that a category
\mathcal{C}
is
s
-Frobenius type
for a
rational number
s\geq 1
if the dimensions of its simple objects divide
(\dim\mathcal{C})^{s}
. Clearly,
s
-Frobenius type implies
t
-Frobenius type for any
t>s
.

5.2.
Single Generator Case

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}={\langle X\rangle}
is a spherical fusion category with a commutative Grothendieck ring generated by a simple object
X
. If
\mathcal{C}
is s-Isaacs (
s\geq 1/2
), then

\frac{(\dim\mathcal{C})^{2s+1}}{|{U(\mathcal{C})}|\dim(X)^{2}}\in\mathbb{A}.

Proof.

Let
\{\mathcal{A}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{r}
be the non-vanishing orbits of
\widehat{K(\mathcal{C})}
under
G(\widehat{K(\mathcal{C})})
-action. Since the action of
{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}
is free on these orbits, it follows that
|\mathcal{A}_{i}|=|{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}|
for all
0\leq i\leq r
.

From Equation (
2.9
) we derive:

(5.1)

\dim\mathcal{C}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\dim(\mathcal{C}^{j})\mu_{j}([X])\mu_{j^{\#}}([X])

Multiplying both sides by
\frac{(\dim\mathcal{C})^{2s}}{\dim(X)^{2}}
we obtain

(5.2)

\frac{(\dim\mathcal{C})^{2s+1}}{{\dim(X)}^{2}}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\dim(\mathcal{C})^{s}\dim(\mathcal{C}^{j})^{1-s}\frac{\mu_{j}([X])}{{\dim(X)}}\dim(\mathcal{C}^{j})^{1-s}\dim(\mathcal{C})^{s}\frac{\mu_{j^{\#}}([X])}{{\dim(X)}}\dim(\mathcal{C}^{j})^{2s-1}

and this can be written as

(5.3)

\frac{(\dim\mathcal{C})^{2s+1}}{{\dim(X)}^{2}}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\lambda_{s}(\mu_{j},X)\lambda_{s}(\mu_{j^{\#}},X)\dim(\mathcal{C}^{j})^{2s-1}

Lemma
4.1
together with Corollary
4.3
imply that each summand

\lambda_{s}(\mu_{j},X)\lambda_{s}(\mu_{j^{\#}},X)\dim(\mathcal{C}^{j})^{2s-1}

is constant within any non-vanishing orbit
\mathcal{A}_{i}
. Thus the above Equation can be rewritten as:

\displaystyle\frac{(\dim\mathcal{C})^{2s+1}}{{\dim(X)}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{r}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{A}_{i}}\lambda_{s}(\mu_{j},X)\lambda_{s}(\mu_{j^{\#}},X)\dim(\mathcal{C}^{j})^{2s-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{r}|\mathcal{A}_{i}|\lambda_{s}(\mu_{j_{i}},X)\lambda_{s}(\mu_{{j_{i}}^{\#}},X)\dim(\mathcal{C}^{{j_{i}}})^{2s-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}|\sum_{i=0}^{r}\lambda_{s}(\mu_{j_{i}},X)\lambda_{s}(\mu_{{j_{i}}^{\#}},X)\dim(\mathcal{C}^{{j_{i}}})^{2s-1},

where
j_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
is arbitrarily chosen. Since
{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}\simeq{U(\mathcal{C})}
by Remark
2.1
we obtain that

\frac{(\dim\mathcal{C})^{2s+1}}{{|{U(\mathcal{C})}|}{\dim(X)}^{2}}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\lambda_{s}(\mu_{j_{i}},X)\lambda_{s}(\mu_{{j_{i}}^{\#}},X)\dim(\mathcal{C}^{{j_{i}}})^{2s-1}.

Since
s\geq 1/2
and
\dim(\mathcal{C}^{j})\in\mathbb{A}
it follows that
\dim(\mathcal{C}^{j})^{2s-1}\in\mathbb{A}
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is s-Isaacs we also have
\lambda_{s}(\mu_{j_{i}},X),\;\lambda_{s}(\mu_{{j_{i}}^{\#}},X)\in\mathbb{A}
and the proof is completed.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is spherical and
s
-Isaacs for
s\geq 1/2
then
\mathcal{C}
is
1+s/2
Frobenius, see
[
ENO25
, Proposition 5.4]
. Note that previous lemma strengthen this result in the case of an Isaacs fusion category generated by a simple object. More precisely, under these hypotheses we derive that:

\frac{(\dim\mathcal{C})^{s+1/2}}{\dim(X)\sqrt{{|{U(\mathcal{C})}|}}}\in\mathbb{A}.

5.3.
General Case for
s\geq 1/2
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
s\geq 1/2
be a rational number and
\mathcal{C}
be a spherical fusion category. Suppose that

such that the fusion subcategory

Let

. Applying Lemma
5.3
for

we have that:

By Equation (

(5.4)

\frac{(\dim{\mathcal{D}})^{2s+1}}{{|U({\mathcal{D}})|}}=\frac{\dim{\mathcal{D}}}{|U({\mathcal{D}})|}(\dim{\mathcal{D}})^{2s}=\frac{\dim\mathcal{C}}{{n_{dim}}(Z_{\mathcal{C}}(X))}(\dim{\mathcal{D}})^{2s}

]
.
∎

Proof.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let

(6.1)

\frac{\dim\mathcal{C}}{|{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}|}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\dim(\mathcal{C}^{j_{i}})\mu_{j_{i}}(X)\overline{\mu_{j_{i}}([X])},

\dim\mathcal{C}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\dim(\mathcal{C}^{j})\big|\mu_{j}([X])\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=

The result follows by dividing both sides by

Lemma 6.2
.

\mathcal{C}={\langle X\rangle}

7.
The modular case

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a premodular fusion category (i.e. braided and spherical). We maintain all previous notation, particularly
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})=\{X_{0},X_{1},\dots,X_{m}\}
for the simple objects, and
d_{i}:=\dim(X_{i})
for their quantum dimensions.

By
[
Shi16
, Example 6.14]
there is
\mathbb{C}
-algebra homomorphism (the Drinfeld map):
{\mathrm{f}_{Q}}:{{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}}\rightarrow\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
given by the following formula:

(7.1)

{\mathrm{f}_{Q}}(\chi_{i})=\sum_{i^{\prime}=0}^{m}\frac{s_{ii^{\prime}}}{d_{i^{\prime}}}E_{i^{\prime}}.

where
S=(s_{ij})
is the
S
-matrix of
\mathcal{C}
.

A premodular fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is called

if its

-matrix is non-degenerated. By
[
DGNO07
, Proposition 3.7]
the S-matrix is non-degenerate if and only if
{\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{C})}=\mathrm{Vec}
.

In the case of a modular tensor category
\mathcal{C}
, the Drinfeld map
f_{Q}:{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
, as discussed in
[
Shi16
, Example 6.14]
, is a an algebra isomorphism. Thus, for any (central) primitive idempotent
F_{j}
of
{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}
, we have that
{\mathrm{f}_{Q}}(F_{j})
is a (central) primitive idempotent of
\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
. Therefore, we have
{\mathrm{f}_{Q}}(F_{j})=E_{i}
for some central primitive idempotent
E_{i}\in\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
corresponding to the simple object
V_{i}\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})
. This yields a bijection
F_{j}\leftrightarrow V_{i}
between the set of (central) primitive idempotents
\{F_{j}\}
of
{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}
and
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})
.

This bijection was also discussed in
[
Bur20
, Section 6]
. It allows us to index the (central) primitive idempotents of
{\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal{C})}
by the set
\mathcal{I}
defined above. With this new indexation, the second item of
[

, Theorem 6.9]
implies that
\dim(\mathcal{C}^{i})=\dim(V_{i})^{2}
for any

.

Moreover, under this new notation, applying
{\mathrm{f}_{Q}}^{-1}
to Equation (

) we have that

(7.2)

\chi_{i}=\sum_{i^{\prime}=0}^{m}\frac{s_{ii^{\prime}}}{d_{i^{\prime}}}F_{i^{\prime}}.

which implies the crucial evaluation formula:

(7.3)

\mu_{i^{\prime}}([\chi_{i}])=\frac{s_{ii^{\prime}}}{d_{i^{\prime}}}.

Proof of Theorem
1.2

Proof.

Using Equation (
7.3
) and the fact that
\dim(\mathcal{C}^{i})=d_{i}^{2}
for all

, we transform Equation (

\frac{\dim\mathcal{C}}{{|{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}|}}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}d_{i}^{2}\mu_{X_{i}}([X])\overline{\mu_{X_{i}}([X])}

and Equation (

(7.5)

\frac{\dim\mathcal{C}}{|{G({\widehat{{K(\mathcal{C})}}})}|\dim(X)^{2}}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\mu_{X}([{X_{i}}])\overline{\mu_{X}([{X_{i}}])}

which shows that

7.2.
Ito-Michler theorem for modular categories.

In this section we prove Theorem
1.6
:

Proof.

For any simple object
X
, consider
\mathcal{D}={\langle X\rangle}
. Then
{\mathcal{D}}
is weakly integral and therefore pseudo-unitary. Since
{\mathcal{D}}
is also braided it is Isaacs and therefore Theorem

Thus we have that
\mathsf{FPdim}(X)^{2}\mid\frac{\mathsf{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})^{2}}{{|{U(\mathcal{C})}|}^{2}}
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is weakly integral it follows that

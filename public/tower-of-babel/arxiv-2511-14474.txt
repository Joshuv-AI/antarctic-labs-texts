1.
Introduction

The approximation property (AP) for groups was introduced by Haagerup and Kraus
[
12
]
. They also showed that the associated reduced group
C^{*}
-algebra has this property if the group is weakly amenable (a weaker notion than that of amenability). Crann and Neufang
[
7
]
(also see
[
22
]
) recently proved that the AP property for groups is equivalent to a non-commutative Fejér theorem for associated
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-dynamical (or
\mathrm{W}^{*}
) systems. They showed that the Fejér type representation of elements of a crossed product
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra forces the acting group to have AP. Now, reduced crossed products arising from a topological dynamical system
(X,\Gamma)
can be viewed as the reduced
C^{*}
-algebra associated with the transformation groupoid
\Gamma\rtimes X
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an étale locally compact second countable Hausdorff groupoid. Let
U=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}:f(\gamma)\neq 0\}
be the open support of
f\in C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{G})
. We identify the reduced groupoid
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{G})
as a subspace of
C_{0}(\mathcal{G})
, via the evaluation map
j
(see Section
2
). One can view
f(\gamma)
, for
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}
, as
\gamma
-th Fourier coefficient of
f
. In this regard, we ask whether
f
can be recovered from its Fourier coefficients.
The third author and Fuller asked the following question in
[
11
]
.

Question 1
.

[
11
, Question 1]

Given
f\in C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{G})
, does there exist a compactly supported function on
U
which approximates
f
in the reduced norm?

In this article, we explore Question
1
for groupoids admitting
Fejér property
.
The following definition is motivated by
[
7
, Subsection 5.2]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a topological étale groupoid. We say
\mathcal{G}
has
Fejér property
if there exists a net
(h_{i})_{i\in I}\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
such that for every
f\in C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{G})
, we have

\|M_{h_{i}}(f)-f\|_{r}\xrightarrow{i\to\infty}0.

Here,
M_{h_{i}}(f)=h_{i}f
is a pointwise product for
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
. Note that it follows from
[
19
, Proposition 3.3]
that each
M_{h_{i}}
is a completely bounded operator. Moreover, if the sequence of multipliers
M_{h_{i}}
is uniformly bounded, i.e.,
\sup_{i}\|M_{h_{i}}\|_{cb}<\infty
then we say
\mathcal{G}
has
bounded Fejér property
.

As mentioned before, for a group
\Gamma
acting, by homeomorphisms, on a locally compact Hausdorff space
X
, the reduced crossed product
C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
can be realized as the reduced groupoid
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma\rtimes X)
associated to the transformation groupoid
\Gamma\rtimes X
(see Example
2.1
for more on this).
Affirmative answers to Question
1
concerning crossed products are known when
\Gamma
is weakly amenable (
[
1
, Theorem 5.6]
), or more generally, when
\Gamma
has AP (see
[
7
,
22
]
). For a Hausdorff, locally compact groupoid
\mathcal{G}
, positive answers to Question
1
are known when
\mathcal{G}
is amenable (see
[
2
, Theorem 4.2]
). Furthermore, if
f\in C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})
has a clopen support, an affirmative answer is given by
[
10
, Lemma 3.8]
.

In this paper, we show that the
Fejér property
is weaker than weak amenability; namely, we establish that weakly amenable groupoids possess the
bounded Fejér property
. This result precisely resonates with the classical case for groups.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is a weakly amenable groupoid, then
\mathcal{G}
has the bounded Fejér property.

It should come as no surprise then that the crossed products of the form
C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
satisfy the above theorem, where
\Gamma
has AP (see Proposition
3.2
). Nonetheless, there are situations where Question
1
has a negative answer (for instance
[
11
, Example 2.4]
), and the landscape for non-amenable groupoids remains largely unexplored. Some progress was made in this direction by considering groupoids with the Rapid decay property in
[
11
]
and for weakly amenable groupoids in
[
18
]
. However, when the groupoid fails to be inner exact, Question
1
can have a negative answer (see
[
11
]
, for example, or
[
13
]
). To recover elements of the groupoid
C^{\ast}
-algebra from the Fourier series, the inner exact condition is mandatory. In our setup, when the groupoid
\mathcal{G}
satisfies the
Fejér property
, we show that it must be inner exact (Theorem
3.9
).

In the setting of crossed product
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
,
\mathcal{A}
being a
\Gamma
-
C^{*}
-algebra and
\Gamma
a discrete group, Choda
[
6
]
demonstrated a bijective correspondence between the subgroups of
\Gamma
and a particular class of intermediate
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras
{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
. An intermediate algebra
{\mathcal{B}}
belongs to this class if, among other requirements, a faithful conditional expectation exists from
{\mathcal{A}}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
onto
{\mathcal{B}}
. Building on Izumi’s earlier work
[
14
]
for finite groups, Cameron and Smith
[
5
]
showed that when
{\mathcal{A}}
is a simple
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and
\Gamma
acts on
{\mathcal{A}}
by outer automorphisms, the assignment
H\mapsto{\mathcal{A}}\rtimes_{r}H
yields a bijection between subgroups
H\subseteq\Gamma
and
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras
{\mathcal{B}}
with
{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
. Analogously, we have established a Galois correspondence for principal étale groupoids that admit the
Fejér property
(see
[
2
, Theorem 4.5]
for Galois correspondence in the amenable setup).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a principal étale groupoid satisfying Fejér property. Let

C_{0}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})\subseteq C_{0}(\mathcal{G})

be an intermediate subalgebra. Then
{\mathcal{B}}=C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{H})
for some open subgroupoid
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{G}
such that
\mathcal{H}^{(0)}=\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
.

In
[
2
, Corollary 5.6]
, the authors have obtained a spectral theorem for bimodules in the crossed product,
C(X)\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
, abelian
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
C(X)
. Specifically, if a discrete group
\Gamma
acts on a compact Hausdorff space
X
, then any norm closed
C(X)
bimodule
M\subset C(X)\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is precisely of the form
M=\overline{\{fu_{g}:f\in C(X),fu_{g}\in M\}}
. In other words, the spectral theorem of bimodules states that one can recover the bimodule elements by examining their Fourier coefficients in the ambient bimodule.
We have generalized this result to the second countable principal étale groupoid admitting the Fejér property.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a principal étale second countable groupoid satisfying the
Fejér property
. Assume
M\subset C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})\subseteq C_{0}(\mathcal{G})
is a closed
C_{0}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})
-bimodule. Then there exists an open set
U
in
\mathcal{G}
such that
M=\overline{C_{c}(U)}^{\mathrm{r}}
.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper,
\mathcal{G}
will denote a second countable locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid. Since we work with the reduced group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra associated with a second countable locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid, we now proceed to give a primer on these and refer the readers to
[
21
]
for more details. We shall follow the following notation. We denote by
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
the set
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}=\{t^{-1}t:t\in\mathcal{G}\}
. The range and source maps from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, denoted respectively by
r
and
s
, are defined by

r(\gamma)=\gamma\gamma^{-1}\text{ and }s(\gamma)=\gamma^{-1}\gamma,~\gamma\in\mathcal{G}.

Note that
\mathcal{G}_{x}=\{t\in\mathcal{G}:s(t)=x\}
is discrete for every
x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
.
A topological groupoid is a groupoid endowed with a topology such that the multiplication and inversion operations are continuous. Moreover,
\mathcal{G}
is said to be an étale groupoid if the range map
r\colon\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is a local homeomorphism. Note that
s
is also a local homeomorphism. Given an étale groupoid
\mathcal{G}
, the collection of all compactly supported functions on
\mathcal{G}
, denoted by
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
has a
\ast
-algebra structure given by the convolution product, namely

\displaystyle f\ast g(\gamma)=\sum_{\alpha\beta=\gamma}f(\alpha)g(\beta)\qquad f,g\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G}),~\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathcal{G},

\displaystyle f^{\ast}(\gamma)=\overline{f(\gamma^{-1})}.

For each
x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, there is a
*
-representation
\pi_{x}:C_{c}(\mathcal{G})\to\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{x}))
defined by

\pi_{x}(f)\delta_{\gamma}=\sum_{t\in G_{r(\gamma)}}f(t)\delta_{t\gamma},~f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G}),~\gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{x}.

The reduced
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra of
\mathcal{G}
, denoted by
C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{G})
, is defined as the completion of

\left(\bigoplus_{x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\pi_{x}\right)C_{c}(\mathcal{G})\subset\bigoplus_{x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{x})).

We will denote the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-norm on
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
by
\|\cdot\|_{r}
and the space of continuous functions vanishing at infinity by
C_{0}(\mathcal{G})
.

More often than not, we shall use the following identification, which is well-known. Let
\mathcal{G}
be a topological étale groupoid and
f\in C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})
. We define the evaluation map
j:C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})\to C_{0}(\mathcal{G})
by

j(f)(g)=\left\langle\delta_{g},\pi_{s(g)}(f)\delta_{s(g)}\right\rangle,~g\in\mathcal{G},

where
\delta_{g}(g)=1
and
\delta_{g}(h)=0
for
h\neq g
,
h\in\mathcal{G}
. Then
j:C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})\to C_{0}(\mathcal{G})
is a norm-decreasing injective linear map. Moreover,
j
is an identity map on
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
. Since
j:C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})\to C_{0}(\mathcal{G})
is injective, we often identify
j(f)
with
f
, hence, regard
f
as
a function on
\mathcal{G}
.
To be pedantic, for
f\in C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{G})
we denote its open support by

\operatorname{supp}(f)=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}:j(f)(\gamma)\neq 0\}.

We suppress
j
after the identification onwards.
Let
\mathcal{G}
be a topological groupoid with unit space
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
. A locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is called
topologically principal
if the set

\{x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}:\operatorname{Iso}(x)=\{x\}\}

is dense in
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, where
\operatorname{Iso}(x):=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}:s(\gamma)=r(\gamma)=x\}
is the isotropy group at
x
.
We end this section with the following well-known example of transformation groupoids.

Example 2.1

(Transformation Groupoids)
.

Let
X
be a locally compact Hausdorff space and assume that a discrete group
\Gamma
is acting on
X
, i.e.,
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
by homeomorphisms.
Then for the transformation groupoid
\Gamma\rtimes X
, we have
s(\gamma,x)=x
and
r(\gamma,x)=\gamma x
.
The multiplication and inverse are given by

(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}x)(\gamma_{2},x)=(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2},x)
and
(\gamma,x)^{-1}=(\gamma^{-1},\gamma x)
for all
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
and
x\in X
.
Then it is well-known that the groupoid
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra
C_{r}^{\ast}(\Gamma\rtimes X)\cong C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
.

3.
Groupoids and Fejér property

We now formalize the Fejér property for étale groupoids, modeled on the Fejér-type approximation for groups established by Crann and Neufang. Note that a function
h\colon\mathcal{G}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is called a completely bounded multiplier if the multiplier map

M_{h}(f)(\gamma)=h(\gamma)\cdot f(\gamma)\qquad\gamma\in\mathcal{G},~f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})

extends to a completely bounded linear map on
C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{G})
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a topological étale groupoid. We say
\mathcal{G}
has
Fejér property
if there exists a net
(h_{i})_{i\in I}\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
such that for every
f\in C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{G})
, we have

\|M_{h_{i}}(f)-f\|_{r}\xrightarrow{i\to\infty}0,

where
M_{h_{i}}\colon C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{G})\to C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{G})
is the multiplier map.
We identify groupoid
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra
C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{G})\subset C_{0}(\mathcal{G})
. Moreover, if the sequence of multipliers
M_{h_{i}}
is uniformly bounded, i.e.,
\sup_{i}\|M_{h_{i}}\|_{cb}<\infty
then we say
\mathcal{G}
has
bounded Fejér property
.

In general, for
h\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
, using
[
19
, Proposition 3.3]
, we see that

\|M_{h}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\sup_{f\in C_{r}^{*}({\mathcal{G}})_{1}}\|M_{h}(f)\|_{r}<\infty

and is a completely bounded linear operator on
C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{G})
.
The following proposition shows that the above notion is aligned with the classical case of groups admitting the approximation property (see
[
7
, Subsection 5.2]
).
The proof is essentially the same as in
[
7
, Theorem 4.10]
. We spell it out nonetheless.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete group with the Approximation Property (AP), and let
X
be a compact Hausdorff
\Gamma
-space. Then the transformation groupoid
\Gamma\rtimes X
has the bounded Fejér property.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma
has AP, using
[
7
, Theorem 4.10]
, we can find a net
h_{i}\in C_{c}(\Gamma)\cap A(\Gamma)
converging to
1
in the weak* topology of
M_{\mathrm{cb}}(A(\Gamma))
such that

(1)

\displaystyle a=\lim_{j}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}h_{j}(\gamma)E(au_{\gamma}^{\ast})u_{\gamma},~a\in C(X)\rtimes_{r}\Gamma.

Here,
u
is the left regular representation of the discrete group
\Gamma
on the crossed product.
By
[
7
, Theorem 5.4]
, the associated net
H_{j}\colon\Gamma\times C(X)\to C(X)
is given by

\displaystyle H_{j}(\gamma,f)=h_{j}(\gamma)f,~\gamma\in\Gamma,~f\in C(X)\,

such that

(2)

\displaystyle M_{H_{j}}\left(\sum_{\gamma}f_{\gamma}u_{\gamma}\right)=\sum_{\gamma}H_{j}(\gamma,f_{\gamma})u_{\gamma}=\sum_{\gamma}h_{j}(\gamma)f_{\gamma}u_{\gamma}\qquad\gamma\in\Gamma,~f_{\gamma}\in C(X)\,.

It follows that from Equation (
1
) that, one has

(3)

\displaystyle a=\lim_{j}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}h_{j}(\gamma)E(au_{\gamma}^{*})u_{\gamma}=\lim_{j}M_{H_{j}}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E(au_{\gamma}^{*})u_{\gamma}\right).

For given
a\sim\sum_{\gamma}f_{\gamma}u_{\gamma}\in C(X)\rtimes\Gamma
, we define
\hat{f}\in C_{c}(\Gamma,C(X))
by

\hat{f}(\gamma)=f_{\gamma}
. We use the identification
C_{c}(\Gamma\rtimes X)\cong C_{c}(\Gamma,C(X))
and for
h_{j}\in C_{c}(\Gamma)\cap A(\Gamma)
, we define
k_{j}\in C_{c}(\Gamma,C(X))
as

\displaystyle k_{j}(\gamma)=h_{j}(\gamma)1_{C(X)}\qquad\gamma\in\Gamma\,.

The corresponding multipliers are defined as

\displaystyle M_{k_{j}}\colon C_{c}(\Gamma,C(X))\to C_{c}(\Gamma,C(X)),

\displaystyle M_{k_{j}}(\hat{f})(\gamma)=k_{j}(\gamma)\hat{f}(\gamma)=h_{j}(\gamma)f_{\gamma}=M_{H_{j}}(\hat{f})(\gamma)\,.

under the identification
C_{r}^{*}(\Gamma\rtimes X)\cong C(X)\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
.
Since
M_{H_{j}}(a)\to a
in norm for all
a\in C(X)\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
, we have
M_{k_{j}}(f)\to f
in
C_{r}^{\ast}(\Gamma\rtimes X)
when
j\to\infty
.
Moreover
\sup_{j}\|M_{h_{j}}\|_{\mathrm{cb}}\leq 1
, gives
\sup_{j}\|M_{k_{j}}\|_{\mathrm{cb}}\leq 1
.
∎

The following example illustrates how the Fejér property behaves under disjoint unions of groupoids. We will use it later in the context of inner exactness.

Example 3.3
.

Let
\Gamma_{1}
, and
\Gamma_{2}
be two discrete countable groups.
Consider the groupoid
\mathcal{G}=\Gamma_{1}\sqcup\Gamma_{2}
, and assume that
{\mathcal{G}}
has the Fejér property, then each
\Gamma_{j}
also has the Fejér property. Indeed, Since
\mathcal{G}
is the disjoint union of
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
, the reduced groupoid
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra decomposes as

\displaystyle C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})\cong C_{r}^{*}(\Gamma_{1})\oplus C_{r}^{*}(\Gamma_{2}).

Moreover, any
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
decomposes as
f=(f_{1},f_{2})
with
f_{j}\in C_{c}(\Gamma_{j})
. Similarly, the net
(h_{i})\subset C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
decomposes as

h_{i}=\bigl(h_{i}^{(1)},h_{i}^{(2)}\bigr),\quad h_{i}^{(j)}\in C_{c}(\Gamma_{j}).

The multiplication operators act as

M_{h_{i}}(f)=\bigl(M_{h_{i}^{(1)}}(f_{1}),\,M_{h_{i}^{(2)}}(f_{2})\bigr)
, where the multiplier
M_{h_{i}^{(j)}}(f_{j})(\gamma)=h_{i}^{(j)}(\gamma)f_{j}(\gamma)
for
\gamma\in\Gamma_{j}
. Now, the norm converges as

\|M_{h_{i}}(f)-f\|_{r,\Gamma_{1}}=\max\bigl(\|M_{h_{i}^{(1)}}(f_{1})-f_{1}\|_{r,\Gamma_{1}},\,\|M_{h_{i}^{(2)}}(f_{2})-f_{2}\|_{r,\Gamma_{2}}\bigr)\to 0.

Thus, for each
j=1,2
,

\|M_{h_{i}^{(j)}}(f_{j})-f_{j}\|_{r,\mathcal{G}}\to 0,\quad\text{for all }f_{j}\in C_{r}^{*}(\Gamma_{j})
.
Moreover, since
M_{h_{i}}
is completely bounded on
C_{r}^{*}({\mathcal{G}})
, and the completely bounded norm satisfies

\|M_{h_{i}}\|_{cb}=\max\left(\|M_{h_{i}^{(1)}}\|_{cb},\,\|M_{h_{i}^{(2)}}\|_{cb}\right),

we see that
(h_{i}^{(j)})
is a Fejér net for
\Gamma_{j}
.

It is natural to ask if
\Gamma\rtimes X
having
Fejér
has any relation with the approximation property of the group
\Gamma
. We show that this is the case if
X
, in addition, admits an invariant measure
\mu
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
X
be a
\Gamma
-space. Assume that
\Gamma\rtimes X
has Fejér property in our sense, and suppose that
\mu
is
\Gamma
-invariant measure on
X
. Then
\Gamma
has property AP.

Proof.

Assume that
\Gamma\rtimes X

has Fejér realized by the net
\{h_{j}\}
. Let
\mu
be a
\Gamma
-invariant measure on
X
.
For each
h_{j}\in C_{c}(\Gamma\rtimes X)
, define
\tilde{h}_{j}\in C_{c}(\Gamma)
by

\tilde{h}_{j}(s)=\int_{X}h_{j}(s,x)d\mu(x),~s\in\Gamma.

Since
\mu
is a
\Gamma
-invariant measure, the map
E_{\mu}:C(X)\rtimes_{r}\Gamma\to C_{r}^{*}(\Gamma)
defined by
f\lambda(s)\to\mu(f)\lambda(s)
is a
\Gamma
-equivariant conditional expectation (see
[
4
, Exercise 4.1.4]
).
Letting
M_{\tilde{h}_{j}}=E_{\mu}\circ M_{h_{j}}
, we see that

\displaystyle\|M_{\tilde{h}_{j}}(a)-a\|_{r}

\displaystyle=\|M_{\tilde{h}_{j}}(a)-E_{\mu}(a)\|_{r}

\displaystyle=\|E_{\mu}\circ M_{h_{j}}(a)-E_{\mu}(a)\|_{r}\leq\|M_{h_{j}}(a)-a\|_{r}\to 0.

Also, note that
\|M_{\tilde{h}_{j}}\|_{cb}\leq\|M_{h_{j}}\|_{cb}
for each
j
. Hence,
\sup_{j}\|M_{\tilde{h}_{j}}\|_{cb}<\infty
. Thus,
\Gamma
has AP.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

We do not know if the assumption that
\mu
is invariant can be removed in general.

We now show that when
{\mathcal{G}}
has the
Fejér property
, the support of an element in
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
is contained in the subalgebra generated by an open set. In particular, the support of an element determines the subalgebra it belongs to. The following proposition makes it precise. The idea of the proof is essentially contained in
[
3
, Theorem]
. We sketch it nonetheless with all its details. Recall that a subset
B
of an étale groupoid is called a bisection if there is an open set
U
containing
B
such that
r:U\to r(U)
and
s:U\to s(U)
are homeomorphisms.

Proposition 3.6
.

Assume that
\mathcal{G}
has the Fejér property. Let
U
be an open subset of
\mathcal{G}
and let
f\in C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{G})
be such that
\operatorname{supp}(f)\subseteq U
. Then,
f\in\overline{C_{c}(U)}^{r}

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{G}
has the
Fejér property
, there exists a net
(h_{i})\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
satisfying the conditions in Definition
1.1
. Hence, it suffices to show that
M_{h_{i}}(f)\in\overline{C_{c}(U)}^{r}
.
Observe that
M_{h_{i}}(f)\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
for
f\in C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{G})
. Since
M_{h_{i}}(f)\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
vanishes off
U
, we can assume that
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
.
Therefore, assume that
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
and we show that
f\in\overline{C_{c}(U)}^{r}
.
Let
E
be a compact subset of
\mathcal{G}
outside of which
f
vanishes.

Since
\mathcal{G}
is étale we may find finitely many open bisections
\{U_{1},U_{2},\ldots U_{n}\}
, whose union contains
E
. Let
\{g_{1}\ldots,g_{n}\}
be a partition of unity for
E
subordinate to the open cover
\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}\}
. That is, each
g_{i}
is a continuous nonnegative real-valued function on
G
,
\operatorname{supp}(g_{i})\subset U_{i}
, and such that

f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}f,

where
g_{i}f
is the pointwise product. Towards this end, it is enough to show that
g_{i}f\in\overline{C_{c}(U)}^{r}
for each
i
. Note that
g_{i}f
vanishes off the bisection
U_{i}\cap U
. Thus, we may assume, without loss of generality, that our originally chosen
f
vanishes outside
some open bisection
U_{0}
, with
U_{0}\subseteq U
.
Set
W=s(U_{0})
, and let
(w_{j})_{j}
be an approximate unit for
C_{0}(W)
contained in
C_{c}(W)
.
Observe that, for each
j
, we have

\displaystyle f\ast w_{j}(g)=f(g)w_{j}(s(g))\qquad g\in\mathcal{G}\,.

Since
s
is a homeomorphism between
U_{0}
and
W
, it follows that

f\ast w_{j}=f(w_{j}\circ s)\to f

uniformly. Since
U_{0}
is a bisection it follows that
f\ast w_{j}\to f
in
\|\cdot\|_{r}
(see
[
21
, Theorem 5.1.11]
). Observe that
f\ast w_{j}
is compactly supported in
U_{0}\subseteq U
. Then it follows that
f\ast w_{j}\in C_{c}(U_{0})\subseteq C_{c}(U)\subseteq\overline{C_{c}(U)}^{r}
, and hence
f\in\overline{C_{c}(U)}^{r}
.
∎

3.1.
Inner exactness

To analyze structural consequences of the Fejér property, we recall the notion of inner exactness for reduced groupoid
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras.
Let
\mathcal{G}
be a topological groupoid with unit space
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
. A subset
U\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is called
invariant
if for all
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}
,

s(\gamma)\in U\implies r(\gamma)\in U,

where
s
and
r
denote the source and range maps, respectively. Equivalently,
U
is invariant if
s^{-1}(U)=r^{-1}(U)
, i.e., every arrow in
\mathcal{G}
with source in
U
also has range in
U
(and vice versa).
Given an invariant subset
U\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, the
reduction
or
restricted subgroupoid

\mathcal{G}|_{U}
is defined as

\mathcal{G}|_{U}:=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}:s(\gamma)\in U\text{ and }r(\gamma)\in U\}.

This is a subgroupoid of
\mathcal{G}
with unit space
U
.
Let
F\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
be a closed invariant subset and
U:=\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus F
.
The inclusion
C_{c}(\mathcal{G}|_{U})\hookrightarrow C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
induces injective
*
-homomorphisms

C^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{F})\to C^{*}(\mathcal{G})\quad\text{and}\quad C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G}|_{F})\to C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G}).

Moreover, the restriction map
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})\to C_{c}(\mathcal{G}|F)
induces surjective
*
-homomorphisms

C^{*}(\mathcal{G})\to C^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{F}),\qquad C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})\to C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G}|_{F}).

Consequently, the following sequence is exact:

0\longrightarrow C^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{U})\longrightarrow C^{*}(\mathcal{G})\longrightarrow C^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{F})\longrightarrow 0.

However, this exactness may fail for the reduced
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra; this can be demonstrated by considering the so-called HLS groupoid (see, for example
[
13
]
).

As mentioned above, there exists a sequence

(4)

0\longrightarrow C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G}|_{U})\xrightarrow{\iota}C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})\xrightarrow{\pi}C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G}|_{F})\longrightarrow 0,

which is not exact in the middle.

Definition 3.7
.

A groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is said to be
inner exact
if the sequence in Equation (
4
) is exact for all closed invariant subsets
F\subseteq X
.

Example 3.8
.

Let
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
be discrete groups, and consider the groupoid

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}=\Gamma_{1}\sqcup\Gamma_{2},

the disjoint union of
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
viewed as groupoids with one object each. Observe that

\mathcal{G}^{(0)}=\{e_{\Gamma_{1}}\}\sqcup\{e_{\Gamma_{2}}\}
, where
e_{\Gamma_{i}}
corresponds to the unit elements of the groups
\Gamma_{i}
. The only open
\mathcal{G}
-invariant subsets of
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
are
\emptyset
,
\{e_{\Gamma_{1}}\}
,
\{e_{\Gamma_{2}}\}
, and
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
.
For each such open invariant subset, the restricted groupoid
\mathcal{G}|_{U}
is either empty or isomorphic to one of the groups
\Gamma_{1}
or
\Gamma_{2}
viewed as groupoids. Moreover, the reduced groupoid
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra decomposes as a direct sum
C_{r}^{*}(\Gamma_{1})\oplus C_{r}^{*}(\Gamma_{2})
. Considering the short sequences corresponding to each invariant open set, one checks that they are all exact. Hence
\mathcal{G}
is inner exact.

We now show that groupoids possessing the
Fejér property
must necessarily be inner exact.

Theorem 3.9
.

If
\mathcal{G}
has
Fejér property
, then
\mathcal{G}
is inner exact.

Proof.

Let
F\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
be a closed invariant subset and
U:=\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus F
. We need to show that the sequence

0\longrightarrow C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G}|_{U})\xrightarrow{\iota}C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})\xrightarrow{{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0.5}\pi}}C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G}|_{F})\longrightarrow 0

is exact. Let
f\in C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})
with
\pi(f)=0
. Then
f|_{\mathcal{G}|_{F}}=0
, and since
U
is invariant, we see that
\operatorname{supp}(f)\subseteq\mathcal{G}|_{U}
. By
Proposition
3.6
, it follows that
f\in\overline{C_{c}(\mathcal{G}|_{U})}^{r}=\operatorname{im}(\iota)
. Therefore,
\ker(p)\subseteq\operatorname{im}(\iota)
. The other inclusion is clear, so the sequence is exact and
\mathcal{G}
is inner exact.
∎

The inner exactness does not imply the Fejér property for groupoids as the following example demonstrates.

Example 3.10
.

Take
\Gamma_{1}=SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\Gamma_{2}=\mathbb{Z}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}=\Gamma_{1}\sqcup\Gamma_{2}

is inner exact but does not have the
Fejér property
by Example
3.3
and Example
3.8
, since having the
Fejér property
for
\mathcal{G}
would imply that
SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
also has the Fejér property. However, for any locally compact group, AP is equivalent to the Fejér property by
[
7
]
. But this is a contradiction since
SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
does not have the Approximation Property (AP) (see
[
8
]
).

3.2.
Weak amenability

It is well-known in the group case that weak amenability implies the approximation property (see
[
12
]
and
[
23
]
). We now show that weak amenability for an étale groupid
{\mathcal{G}}
implies the
bounded Fejér property
. In particular, we prove Theorem
1.2
. Before that, we briefly recall the definition of weak amenability and refer the readers to
[
18
]
for more details.

Given a quasi-invariant probability measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{G}
, a function
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{G},\mu)
is said to be a multiplier of the Fourier algebra if the map
M_{\varphi}:A(\mathcal{G})\to A(\mathcal{G})
given by
M_{\varphi}(f)=\varphi f
is well-defined and bounded (see
[
19
, Subsection 1.3]
for definition of
A(\mathcal{G})
). Moreover, if
M_{\varphi}
is completely bounded, we say that
\varphi
is a completely bounded multiplier. We denote the set of such multipliers by
M_{0}A(\mathcal{G})
, endowed with the completely-bounded (CB)-norm,

\|\varphi\|_{M_{0}A(\mathcal{G})}=\left\|M_{\varphi}\right\|_{\text{cb}}.

A topological étale groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is said to be
(topologically) weakly amenable
if there exists a net
(\varphi_{i})\subset C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
, a constant
C>0
, and a quasi-invariant probability measure with full support
\mu
on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, such that

(i)

\varphi_{i}\rightarrow 1\text{ uniformly on compact subsets of }\mathcal{G}
, and

(ii)

\sup_{i}\|\varphi_{i}\|_{M_{0}A(\mathcal{G})}=C<\infty
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a weakly amenable étale groupoid. Then, there exists a net
(\varphi_{i})\subset C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
, a constant
C>0
, and a quasi-invariant probability measure with full support
\mu
on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, such that
\varphi_{i}\rightarrow 1
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathcal{G}
, and

\sup_{i}\|\varphi_{i}\|_{M_{0}A(\mathcal{G})}=C<\infty
. Since
\varphi_{i}\in M_{0}A(\mathcal{G})\cap C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
,
M_{\varphi_{i}}\colon C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{G})\to C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{G})
defines a completey bounded operator. Moreover, since
\sup_{i}\|\varphi_{i}\|_{M_{0}A(\mathcal{G})}=C<\infty
, we have that
\sup_{i}\|M_{\varphi_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{cb}}<\infty
.
Now for
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
supported in bisection, and using the fact that
\|\cdot\|_{r}\leq\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
(see
[
21
, Lemma 3.2.2]
) for such functions, we have

\displaystyle\|M_{\varphi_{i}}(f)-f\|_{r}

\displaystyle=\|\varphi_{i}f-f\|_{r}

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi_{i}f-f\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq M\|\varphi_{i}-1\|_{\infty,K}\to 0,\qquad\operatorname{supp}(f)\subseteq K

where
M=\sup_{i}\|M_{\varphi_{i}}\|_{cb}
. Now, let
f
be in
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
be given. Suppose
\text{supp}(f)\subseteq L
where
L
is a compact subset of
\mathcal{G}
. Then
L
can be covered by finitely many open bisections
U_{1},\dots,U_{n}
. Choose a partition of unity subordinate to these bisections, i.e., choose
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
in
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
such that
\text{supp}(g_{i})\subset U_{i}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}=1
. Take
f_{i}=fg_{i}
. Then we have
f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}
with
f_{i}\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
with
\text{supp}(f_{i})\subset U_{i}
.
We now see that

\displaystyle\|M_{\varphi_{i}}(f)-f\|_{r}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}M_{\varphi_{i}}(f_{k})-\sum_{k=1}^{n}(f_{k})\right\|_{r}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}(M_{\varphi_{i}}(f_{k})-f_{k})\right\|_{r}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\|M_{\varphi_{i}}(f_{k})-f_{k}\right\|_{r}\rightarrow 0.

Now by a density argument and using the fact that
\sup_{i}\|M_{\varphi_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{cb}}<\infty
, one has for any
f\in C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{G})
,

\|M_{\varphi_{i}}(f)-f\|_{r}\to 0.

Hence
\mathcal{G}
has bounded
Fejér property
. The proof is now complete.
∎

3.3.
Galois Correspondence

In this section, we establish a version of the Galois correspondence in the context of groupoids. The next corollary, which is immediate from Proposition
3.6
, shows that if an element is supported on an open subgroupoid, it lies in the corresponding reduced
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an open subgroupoid of
\mathcal{G}
, where
\mathcal{G}
has the Fejér property. Assume that
f\in C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{G})
is such that
\operatorname{supp}(f)\subseteq\mathcal{H}
. Then
f\in C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
.

Galois correspondences are significant in the analysis of intermediate Cartan embeddings. A primary question concerns whether, for
D\subseteq B\subseteq A
with
D
Cartan in
A
, the subalgebra
D
remains Cartan in an intermediate subalgebra
B
of
A
. When
D\subseteq A
is a Cartan subalgebra of
A
, a groupoid model is associated with this structure (see Kumjian
[
16
]
and Renault-Li
[
17
]
). Specifically, there exists a twist
q\colon\Sigma\to G
such that
D\cong C_{0}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})
and
A\cong C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{G},\Sigma)
.
We now establish a Galois correspondence below.
Nonetheless, it is essential to note that such a Galois correspondence will fail if the action is not free (for instance, see
[
2
, Example 5.1]
), that is, if the groupoid is not principal. Given any open set
U
, we write

A_{U}:=\{f\in C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{G})\subseteq C_{0}(\mathcal{G}):\operatorname{supp}(f)\subset U\}.

We now prove a Galois correspondence between open subgroupoids and intermediate
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebras for principal groupoids with the Fejér property.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a principal étale groupoid satisfying
Fejér property
. Let

C_{0}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})\subseteq C_{0}(\mathcal{G})

be an intermediate subalgebra. Then
{\mathcal{B}}=C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{H})
for some open subgroupoid
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

Define the set

\mathcal{H}=\left\{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}:\exists\,b\in{\mathcal{B}}\text{ such that }b(\gamma)\neq 0\right\}.

Since
{\mathcal{G}}
is a principal étale groupoid, it follows from
[
2
, Theorem 2.3]
that
C_{0}({\mathcal{G}}^{(0)})
is a
C^{*}
-diagonal of
C_{r}^{*}({\mathcal{G}})
. Hence, arguing similarly as in the first part of the proof of
[
2
, Theorem 4.4]
, we see that
\mathcal{H}
is an open subgroupoid of
\mathcal{G}
. Then we have

C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{H})\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq A_{\mathcal{H}}.

Now, using Proposition
3.6
, we have
A_{\mathcal{H}}=C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{H})
. Consequently, we get
{\mathcal{B}}=C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{H})
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Recall that the map
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
defined by
E(f)(x):=f(x)
, for all
x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
extends to a conditional expectation

E:C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})\to C_{0}({\mathcal{G}}^{0})
. Moreover, given an inclusion of unital
C^{*}
-algebras
D\subset A
, we denote by
N(A,D)
the set of all elements in
A
that normalize
D
, i.e.,

N(A,D)=\left\{a\in A:aDa^{*}\cup a^{*}Da\subset D\right\}.

The following lemma constructs normalizers supported on a given bisection, a key step in describing bimodules.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a principal étale groupoid, and
M
be a norm closed
C_{0}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})
-bimodule. Moreover, let
a\in M
be such that
\operatorname{supp}(a)
contains a bisection
B
. Then there exists
m\in M\cap N(C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G}),C_{0}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}))
such that
\operatorname{supp}(m)=B
.

Proof.

Consider
n\in C_{0}(B)
such that
\operatorname{supp}(n)=B
. Then
n
normalizes
C_{0}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})
by
[
20
, Proposition 4.8(ii)]
. Moreover, since
B
is a bisection, the reduced norm
\|\cdot\|_{r}
and the
\sup
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
agree on it (see
[
21
, Corollary 3.3.4]
). Hence
n
can be approximated by elements from
C_{c}(B)
in
\|\cdot\|_{r}
-norm. Consequently,
n\in C_{r}^{*}({\mathcal{G}})
. We now claim that
m=n\ast E(n^{\ast}\ast a)
is the required element. We first show that
m
is supported on the bisection
B
. Indeed, since
E(n^{\ast}\ast a)\in C_{0}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})
and
n\in C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{G})
, using
[
20
, Proposition 4.3]
along with
[
20
, Lemma 4.4]
, we have

\displaystyle(n*E(n^{*}*a))(\gamma)=n(\gamma)E(n^{*}*a)(s(\gamma))=n(\gamma)\overline{n(\gamma)}a(\gamma)\,.

It then follows immediately that the support of
m
is
B
. Using
[
9
, Proposition 3.10]
, we see that
m\in M
. It now follows from
[
20
, Proposition 4.8(i)]
that
m
is a normalizer.
∎

Recall that for any open set
U
, we denote

A_{U}:=\{f\in C_{r}^{\ast}(\mathcal{G})\subseteq C_{0}(\mathcal{G}):\operatorname{supp}(f)\subset U\}.

We now establish a spectral-type theorem for bimodules, showing that closed
C_{0}({\mathcal{G}}^{(0)})
-bimodules correspond exactly to open subsets of the groupoid.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We first show the following. Let
m\in M
be a normalizer of
C_{0}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})
. Then
C_{c}\left(\operatorname{supp}(m)\right)\subset M
.

Since
m
normalizes
C_{0}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})
,
\operatorname{supp}(m)
is a bisection by
[
20
, Proposition 4.8(ii)]
. Consequently, the range map
r:\operatorname{supp}(m)\to\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is a homeomorphism onto its image. Take
f\in C_{c}(\operatorname{supp}(m))
. Following the approach of
[
15
, Theorem 2.1.2]
, define
g\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})
by

g(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{f\left((r|_{\operatorname{supp}(m)})^{-1}(x)\right)}{m\left((r|_{\operatorname{supp}(m)})^{-1}(x)\right)}&\text{if }x\in r(\operatorname{supp}(m)),\\[8.61108pt]
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Note that
f=g\ast m
, and since
M
is a
C_{0}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})
-bimodule, it follows that
f\in M
. This shows that
C_{c}(\text{supp}(m))\subset M
.

For each
a\in M
, define

U_{a}=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}:a(\gamma)\neq 0\}
. Let

U:=\bigcup_{a\in M}U_{a}

Let
B\subseteq U
be a bisection in
\mathcal{G}
. We now show that
C_{c}(B)\subset M
.
Let
f\in C_{c}(B)
and let
K
be a compact subset of
\mathcal{G}
such that
\operatorname{supp}(f)\subset K\subset B
. Observe that for any
\gamma\in K\subset B\subseteq U
, there exists
a_{\gamma}\in M
such that
a_{\gamma}(\gamma)\neq 0
.

Since
\mathcal{G}
is étale and has a basis of open bisections, there exists a bisection
W_{\gamma}
such that
\gamma\in W_{\gamma}\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(a_{\gamma})
. Therefore,

K\subseteq\bigcup_{\gamma\in K}W_{\gamma}.

As
K
is compact, there exist finitely many
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\in K
such that

K\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}W_{\gamma_{i}}.

By Lemma
3.13
, for each
i=1,\ldots,n
, there exists
n_{\gamma_{i}}\in M
(a normalizer) such that
\operatorname{supp}(n_{\gamma_{i}})=W_{\gamma_{i}}
. Let
\{\tilde{f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be a partition of unity for
K
subordinate to the open cover
\{W_{\gamma_{i}}:i=1,\ldots,n\}
. Then, letting
f_{i}=\tilde{f}_{i}f
for each
i=1,2,\ldots,n
, we have

f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}.

As each
f_{i}\in C_{c}(W_{\gamma_{i}})=C_{c}(\operatorname{supp}(n_{\gamma_{i}}))\subset M
, by our first claim, it follows that
f\in M
. This shows that
C_{c}(B)\subset M
.

We now show that
C_{c}(U)\subset M
. Let
f\in C_{c}(U)
. Then
\operatorname{supp}(f)\subset K\subset U
, where
K
is a compact set. For each
\gamma\in K
, there exists an open bisection
U_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
such that
K\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}
and

f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}

with
f_{i}\in C_{c}(U_{i})
. As
U_{i}\subset U
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
, the claim follows. Therefore, we see that

C_{c}(U)\subset M\subset A_{U}.

Since
\mathcal{G}
has the
Fejér property
, it follows from Proposition
3.6
that
A_{U}=\overline{C_{c}(U)}^{\mathrm{r}}
.
Consequently, we conclude that
M=\overline{C_{c}(U)}^{\mathrm{r}}
.
∎

On concentrated vortices of 3D incompressible Euler equations under helical symmetry: with swirl
Guolin Qin, Jie Wan

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the existence of concentrated helical vortices of 3D incompressible Euler equations with swirl. First, without the assumption of the orthogonality condition, we derive a 2D vorticity-stream formulation of 3D incompressible Euler equations under helical symmetry. Then based on this system, we deduce a non-autonomous second order semilinear elliptic equations in divergence form, whose solutions correspond to traveling-rotating invariant helical vortices with non-zero helical swirl. Finally, by using Arnold’s variational method, that is, finding maximizers of a properly defined energy functional over a certain function space and proving the asymptotic behavior of maximizers, we construct families of concentrated traveling-rotating helical vortices of 3D incompressible Euler equations with non-zero helical swirl in infinite cylinders. As parameter ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 , the associated vorticity fields tend asymptotically to a singular helical vortex filament evolved by the binormal curvature flow.



Keywords:
Incompressible Euler equations; Helical symmetry; With swirl; Variational methods; Semilinear elliptic equations.

Primary: 76B47; Secondary: 35J25, 76B03.

1.
Introduction and Main results

In this paper, we study the concentrated helical vortices of an incompressible ideal fluid described by the Euler equations. We are interested in the existence of vorticity field with helical symmetry which is concentrated in a helical tube of radius
\varepsilon\ll 1
around a helical curve in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, when the orthogonality condition fails.
The motion of an inviscid incompressible flow is governed by the following Euler equations

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}+(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}=-\nabla P,\ \ &\text{in}\ \ \Omega\times(0,T),\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0,\ \ &\text{in}\ \ \Omega\times(0,T),\\
\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0,\ \ &\text{on}\ \ \partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
\mathbf{v}(\cdot,0)=\mathbf{v}_{0}(\cdot),\ \ &\text{in}\ \ \Omega,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where the domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
is the velocity field,
P
is the scalar pressure and
\mathbf{n}
is the outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega
. For a solution
\mathbf{v}
of (
1.1
), define the corresponding vorticity field of the fluid

\mathbf{w}:=\nabla\times\mathbf{v}.

Taking curl of the first equation in (
1.1
), we obtain the following equations for vorticity:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\mathbf{w}+(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{w}=(\mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v},\ \ &\text{in}\ \ \Omega\times(0,T),\\
\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0,\ \ &\text{on}\ \ \partial\Omega\times(0,T)\\
\mathbf{w}(\cdot,0)=\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{0}(\cdot),\ \ &\text{in}\ \ \Omega.\end{cases}

(1.2)

1.1.
Backgrounds

The study of the evolution of vorticity fields of (
1.2
) concentrated in a tube with small cross-section around a smooth curve can be traced back to Helmholtz’s classical result in 1858
[
40
]
. Generally speaking, there are two main concerns:

\bullet

How does the curve evolved?

\bullet

How to give a rigorous justification of the relation between vorticity field of (
1.2
) and the curve?

The first question seems to have been well studied. Helmholtz
[
40
]
first found that vorticity fields of (
1.2
) with axial symmetry (called the vortex ring) supported in a toroidal region with small cross-section near a circular curve have an approximately steady form and travel with a large constant velocity along the axis of the ring. Then, Lord Kelvin and Hicks
[
47
,
61
]
further gave a quantitative formula for the velocity of a vortex ring with a small cross-section, which confirmed Helmholtz’s observation. Let
l
be any oriented closed curve with tangent vector field
\mathbf{t}
that encircles the aforementioned vorticity region once and
\sigma
is any surface with
l
as its boundary. Define the circulation of a vortex

\kappa=\oint_{l}\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{t}dl=\iint_{\sigma}\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{n}d\sigma.

(1.3)

It is shown in
[
47
,
61
]
that vortex rings concentrated around a torus with fixed radius
r^{*}
, circulation
\kappa
and a small, nearly singular cross-section of radius
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
would approximately move at the velocity

\frac{\kappa}{4\pi r^{*}}\left(\ln\frac{8r^{*}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{4}\right).

(1.4)

For general curves, the first derivation of the motion law of curves dates back to the work of da Rios in 1906
[
18
]
and Levi-Civita in 1908
[
48
,
49
]
with the help of potential theory. Consider a vortex tube with a small section of radius
\varepsilon
and a fixed circulation
\kappa
, uniformly distributed around an evolving smooth curve
\Gamma(t)
. By using the Biot-Savart law and calculating the leading order of instantaneous velocity of the curve,
[
18
]
indicates that the curve
\Gamma(t)
evolves by its
binormal curvature flow
: let
\Gamma(t)
be parameterized by
\gamma(s,t)
with
s
the arc length parameter, then the motion of
\gamma
would asymptotically obey a law of the form

\partial_{t}\gamma=\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}|\ln\varepsilon|(\partial_{s}\gamma\times\partial_{ss}\gamma)=\frac{\kappa\bar{K}}{4\pi}|\ln\varepsilon|\mathbf{b}_{\gamma(t)},

(1.5)

where
\mathbf{b}_{\gamma(t)}
is the binormal unit vector and
\bar{K}
is its local curvature. We refer interested readers to
[
32
]
for a generalization of (
1.5
). By applying the scale
t=|\ln\varepsilon|^{-1}\tau
, (
1.5
) is equivalent to

\partial_{\tau}\gamma=\frac{\kappa\bar{K}}{4\pi}\mathbf{b}_{\gamma(\tau)}.

(1.6)

(
1.5
) is known by many names, including the vortex filament equation, and the local induction approximation. In subsequent years, the binormal curvature flow was rediscovered several times, see for example Arms and Hama
[
4
]
and Moore and Saffman
[
53
]
. More discussions about (
1.5
) can be seen in the texts
[
52
,
56
,
57
,
58
]
and references
therein.

The second problem, that is, a rigorous mathematical justification of the relation between vorticity field of (
1.2
) and the curve (
1.5
), is still a very concerned problem and has not been completely solved. Jerrard and Seis
[
42
]
first justified da Rios’ computation mathematically under some assumptions on a solution to (
1.2
) which remains suitably concentrated
around an evolved vortex filament. It is shown in
[
42
]
that under certain conditions, if there exists a family of solutions
\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}
to (
1.2
) such that in the sense of distribution,

\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,|\ln\varepsilon|^{-1}\tau)\to\kappa\delta_{\gamma(\tau)}\mathbf{t}_{\gamma(\tau)},\ \ \text{as}\ \varepsilon\to 0,

(1.7)

where
\mathbf{t}_{\gamma(\tau)}
is the tangent unit vector of
\gamma
and
\delta_{\gamma(\tau)}
is the uniform Dirac measure on the curve, then
\gamma(\tau)
satisfies (
1.6
). See also
[
43
]
. On the other hand, for a general curve that solves (
1.5
), finding a family of true solutions of (
1.2
) that satisfy (
1.7
) remains a long-standing open problem, which is commonly known as the
vortex filament conjecture
. Until now, the only rigorous result in favor of the vortex filament conjecture for Euler flows is restricted to flows with several kinds of symmetry structures: translation invariance, axial symmetry and helical symmetry, which correspond to the cases where the curve
\gamma
is a straight line, a traveling circle and a traveling-rotating helix, respectively. In the case of straight lines, one always reduces the problem to the vortex desingularization problem of point vortices in planar Euler equations, which was investigated in much literature, see e.g.
[
7
,
11
,
12
,
21
,
33
,
34
,
38
,
39
,
41
,
59
,
62
]
and the references therein. For traveling circles, Fraenkel
[
28
,
29
]
first provided a constructive proof of the existence of a vortex ring concentrated around a torus with fixed radius
r^{*}
and a small cross-section of radius
\varepsilon>0
, traveling with constant speed
\sim|\ln\varepsilon|
, rigorously varifying the results of Helmholtz
[
40
]
. We refer readers to
[
3
,
15
,
23
,
30
,
31
,
54
,
55
,
65
]
for more results on the existence of vortex rings.

The third particular curve satisfying (
1.5
) is the traveling-rotating helix parameterized as

\gamma(s,\tau)=\left(r_{*}\cos\left(\frac{-s-a_{1}\tau}{\sqrt{h^{2}+r_{*}^{2}}}\right),r_{*}\sin\left(\frac{-s-a_{1}\tau}{\sqrt{h^{2}+r_{*}^{2}}}\right),\frac{hs-b_{1}\tau}{\sqrt{h^{2}+r_{*}^{2}}}\right)^{t},

(1.8)

where
r_{*}>0
is the constant characterizing the distance between a point in
\gamma(\tau)
and the
x_{3}
-axis and
h\neq 0
stands for the pitch of the helix, and

a_{1}=\frac{\kappa h}{4\pi(h^{2}+r_{*}^{2})},\ b_{1}=\frac{\kappa r_{*}^{2}}{4\pi(h^{2}+r_{*}^{2})}.

Many articles concern the global well-posedness of helical solutions of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) under suitable functional spaces, see, e.g.,
[
2
,
5
,
24
,
27
,
37
,
44
,
45
]
. Recently, the vortex filament conjecture was verified to be true for traveling-rotating helices by D
\acute{\text{a}}
vila et al.
[
22
]
, where a family of smooth traveling-rotating helical vortices of (
1.2
) concentrated around a helical filament parameterized by (
1.8
) in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
in the sense of (
1.7
) were constructed via the inner-outer gluing method. More results of traveling-rotating helical vortices of (
1.2
) with small cross-section concentrated around the helix (
1.8
) in infinite cylinders were constructed in
[
13
]
through the Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction method and
[
9
,
14
]
by a variational method. For more results, see, e.g.,
[
36
]
.

We remark that in all existing results
[
8
,
9
,
13
,
14
,
22
,
36
]
, constructions of traveling-rotating invariant helical vortices were proved under the assumption of the
“without swirl” condition
:

\mathbf{v}\cdot\vec{\xi}\equiv 0,

(1.9)

where
\vec{\xi}(x)=(x_{2},-x_{1},h)^{t}.
The assumption (
1.9
) plays an essential role in aforementioned works, which makes it possible to reduce the vorticity equations (
1.2
) to a 2D evolved model for some scalar function
w
and then construct helical vortices by solving an autonomous semilinear elliptic equation in a planar domain, see also subsection 1.2. Note that under (
1.9
), the vorticity field and the velocity field are orthogonal at every point, so (
1.9
) is also called the
orthogonality condition
, see
[
27
]
.
However, the assumption (
1.9
) appears to be artificial as many helical symmetric vorticity fields may not satisfy this condition, such as Beltrami flows, see
[
25
,
26
]
. So here comes a natural question: when (
1.9
) fails, is it possible to construct a family of helical vorticity fields of (
1.2
) which concentrates in a helical tube with small cross-section of radius
\varepsilon
around the helix (
1.8
)?

In fact, there is some evidence suggesting this possibility when the orthogonality condition (
1.9
) is not satisfied. From a physical perspective, it has been formally demonstrated in
[
32
]
that when the velocity field and the vorticity field are not orthogonal, the instantaneous velocity of the vortex tube with cross-section of radius
\varepsilon
still satisfies the vortex filament equation (
1.5
), as referenced in (3.1),
[
32
]
. The key lies in decomposing the vorticity into ”axial vorticity” along the curve and ”transverse vorticity” perpendicular to the curve, and proving formally that the transverse vorticity makes no contribution to the induced velocity. Mathematically, as mentioned earlier
[
42
]
provided sufficient conditions for the curve where the vorticity field concentrates to satisfy the vortex filament equation, as stated in Theorem 2,
[
42
]
. It is noted that the proof of Theorem 2 does not require the assumption of the orthogonality condition, thus making it possible to construct vortex solutions of (
1.2
) concentrated around a curve satisfying (
1.5
) even when the vorticity and velocity fields are not orthogonal. The third argument is that in the study of vortex rings,
[
16
,
63
]
have constructed vortex rings with small cross-section of radius
\varepsilon
concentrating around a traveling circles, and the corresponding velocity field is not orthogonal to the vorticity field. In
[
1
]
, using the critical point theory the author proved the existence of vortex rings satisfying the Beltrami flow condition. Here the Beltrami flow condition means that the vorticity field and the velocity field are parallel everywhere, so the corresponding velocity field must not be orthogonal to the vorticity field. To conclude, it is possible to construct helical vortices of (
1.2
) concentrating around the helix (
1.8
), when (
1.9
) is not valid.

In this paper, without assuming (
1.9
), we will construct helical vortices of (
1.2
) concentrating in a helical tube with cross-section of radius
\varepsilon
around the helix (
1.8
). Our idea is as follows. First, using the helical symmetry structure we derive a 2D vorticity-stream evolved model (
1.17
) of 3D incompressible Euler equations (
1.2
), when the orthogonality condition (
1.9
) does not hold, see Theorem
1.1
. Second, using the rotational invariance of solutions, we deduce a non-autonomous second order semilinear elliptic equations in divergence form, whose solutions correspond to traveling-rotating invariant helical vortices with swirl, see (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) in section 3. Finally, by using Arnold’s variational method, we construct concentrated traveling-rotating invariant helical vortices of 3D incompressible Euler equations with swirl in infinite cylinders. As parameter
\varepsilon\to 0
, the associated vorticity fields
\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}
tend asymptotically to the helix (
1.8
), see Theorem
1.4
.

We conclude this subsection with a brief overview on related
works. Indeed, helical solutions were also constructed for other models, see for example
[
64
]
for the Schrödinger map equations,
[
17
,
20
]
for the Gross-Pitaevskii equation and
[
19
]
for the Ginzburg-Landau equation. Another related topic is the knotted solutions of Euler equations (
1.1
). The possibility of non-trivial steady vortex configurations featuring knots and links was conjectured by Kelvin
[
60
]
. Such solutions were found by Enciso and Peralta-Salas
[
25
,
26
]
. Explicit knotted solutions to the binormal
curvature flow were studied in
[
46
]
.

1.2.
2D vorticity-stream formulation

Now we begin to show our main results. To construct helical vorticity field of three-dimensional Euler equations (
1.2
) without the assumption (
1.9
), the first key step is to reduce the system (
1.2
) to a two-dimensional evolved model by the helical symmetric structure. For any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, let us define the rotation by an angle
\theta
around the
x_{3}
-axis

Q_{\theta}=\begin{pmatrix}R_{\theta}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\ \ \text{with}\ \ R_{\theta}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\
-\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}.

Let
h>0
be a fixed constant. We introduce a group of one-parameter helical transformations
\mathcal{H}_{h}=\{H_{\theta}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}\mid\theta\in\mathbb{R}\}
, where

H_{\theta}(x)=Q_{\theta}(x)+\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\
h\theta\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\cos\theta+x_{2}\sin\theta\\
-x_{1}\sin\theta+x_{2}\cos\theta\\
x_{3}+h\theta\end{pmatrix}.

We can define helical domains as follows. For
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we say that
\Omega
is a helical domain, if
\Omega
is invariant under the action of
\mathcal{H}_{h}
, that is, for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}

H_{\theta}(\Omega):=\{H_{\theta}(x)\mid x\in\Omega\}=\Omega.

(1.10)

Denote
D=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid(x_{1},x_{2},0)\in\Omega\}
the cross-section of
\Omega
.

Following
[
22
,
24
,
27
]
, we can define helical functions and vector fields. We say that
a scalar function
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is helical, if

f(H_{\theta}(x))=f(x),\ \ \forall\,\theta\in\mathbb{R},x\in\Omega,

and a vector field
\mathbf{v}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
is helical, if

\mathbf{v}(H_{\theta}(x))=Q_{\theta}\mathbf{v}(x),\ \ \forall\,\theta\in\mathbb{R},x\in\Omega.

Naturally, a function pair (
\mathbf{v},P
) is called a
helical
solution of (
1.1
) in
\Omega
, if (
\mathbf{v},P
) solves (
1.1
) and both vector field
\mathbf{v}
and scalar function
P
are helical in the above sense.

Denote the field of tangents of symmetry lines of
\mathcal{H}_{h}

\vec{\xi}(x)=(x_{2},-x_{1},h)^{t},

(1.11)

where
\mathbf{v}^{t}
is the transposition of a vector
\mathbf{v}
. For a velocity field
\mathbf{v}
, we define its
helical swirl
as

v_{\vec{\xi}}(x):=\mathbf{v}(x)\cdot\vec{\xi}(x).

(1.12)

As mentioned earlier, it is proved in
[
22
,
24
,
27
]
that if a helical solution (
\mathbf{v},P
) of (
1.1
) satisfies the
orthogonality condition
:

v_{\vec{\xi}}\equiv 0,

(1.13)

then its vorticity field
\mathbf{w}
satisfies

\mathbf{w}=\frac{w_{3}}{h}\overrightarrow{\xi},

(1.14)

where
w_{3}=\partial_{x_{1}}v_{2}-\partial_{x_{2}}v_{1}
, the third component of
\mathbf{w}
, is a helical function. Moreover, if we set
w(x_{1},x_{2},t)=w_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},0,t)
, then
w
satisfies the following 2D evolved vorticity-stream equations:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}w+\nabla^{\perp}\varphi\cdot\nabla w=0,\ \ &\text{in}\ \ D\times(0,T),\\
\mathcal{L}_{K}\varphi=w,\ \ &\text{in}\ \ D\times(0,T),\\
\varphi=0,\ \ &\text{on}\ \ \partial D\times(0,T),\\
w(\cdot,0)=w_{0}(\cdot),\ \ &\text{in}\ \ D,\end{cases}

(1.15)

where
\varphi
is called the stream function,
\mathcal{L}_{K}\varphi:=-\text{div}(K(x_{1},x_{2})\nabla\varphi)
is a second order elliptic operator in divergence form with the coefficient matrix

K(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{1}{h^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}h^{2}+x_{2}^{2}&-x_{1}x_{2}\\
-x_{1}x_{2}&h^{2}+x_{1}^{2}\end{pmatrix},

(1.16)

and
\perp
denotes the clockwise rotation through
\pi/2
, i.e.,
(a,b)^{\perp}=(b,-a)
.

By studying the rotating invariant solutions of (
1.15
), several families of traveling-rotating helical vortices with the assumption (
1.13
) were constructed in
[
8
,
9
,
13
,
14
,
22
,
36
]
. Our first result is that even in the absence of the zero helical swirl condition (
1.13
), the three-dimensional Euler equations (
1.2
) under helical symmetry can still be reduced to a two-dimensional evolved system.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\mathbf{v},P)
be a
helical
solution pair of (
1.1
) in
\Omega
and
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})^{t}
be the corresponding vorticity field of
\mathbf{v}
. Let
v_{\vec{\xi}}
be the helical swirl of
\mathbf{v}
defined by (
1.12
) and
D
be the cross-section of
\Omega
. For
(x_{1},x_{2})\in D
, define
v(x_{1},x_{2},t)=v_{\vec{\xi}}(x_{1},x_{2},0,t)
and
w(x_{1},x_{2},t)=w_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},0,t)
. Then
(v,w)
solves the following evolved system:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v+\nabla^{\perp}\varphi\cdot\nabla v=0,\ \ &\text{in}\ \ D\times(0,T),\\
\partial_{t}w+\nabla^{\perp}\varphi\cdot\nabla w=\partial_{x_{1}}v\partial_{x_{2}}\left(\frac{x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}\varphi+x_{2}\partial_{x_{2}}\varphi}{|{\vec{\xi}}|^{2}}\right)\\
\qquad\qquad-\partial_{x_{2}}v\partial_{x_{1}}\left(\frac{x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}\varphi+x_{2}\partial_{x_{2}}\varphi}{|{\vec{\xi}}|^{2}}\right)+\frac{2v\left(x_{2}\partial_{x_{1}}v-x_{1}\partial_{x_{2}}v\right)}{|{\vec{\xi}}|^{4}},\ \ &\text{in}\ \ D\times(0,T),\\
\mathcal{L}_{K}\varphi=w+\frac{2h^{2}v}{|{\vec{\xi}}|^{4}}+\frac{x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}v+x_{2}\partial_{x_{2}}v}{|{\vec{\xi}}|^{2}},\ \ &\text{in}\ \ D\times(0,T),\\
\varphi=0,\ \ &\text{on}\ \ \partial D\times(0,T),\\
w(\cdot,0)=w_{3}((\cdot,0),0),\ \ v(\cdot,0)=v_{\vec{\xi}}((\cdot,0),0),&\text{in}\ \ D.\end{cases}

(1.17)

Conversely, suppose that
(v,w)
is a solution pair of (
1.17
). Let
\tilde{\mathbf{u}}=(\tilde{u}_{1},\tilde{u}_{2},\tilde{u}_{3})^{t}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{v}}=(\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{v}_{3})^{t}
be vector fields in
D
such that for
(x_{1},x_{2})\in D

\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{u}_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})\\
\tilde{u}_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{|\vec{\xi}|^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}-x_{1}x_{2}&h^{2}+x_{1}^{2}\\
-(h^{2}+x_{2}^{2})&x_{1}x_{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{x_{1}}\varphi(x_{1},x_{2})\\
\partial_{x_{2}}\varphi(x_{1},x_{2})\end{pmatrix},

\tilde{u}_{3}(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{-x_{2}\tilde{u}_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})+x_{1}\tilde{u}_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})}{h},

and
\tilde{\mathbf{v}}(x_{1},x_{2})=\tilde{\mathbf{u}}(x_{1},x_{2})+\frac{v(x_{1},x_{2})\vec{\xi}}{|\vec{\xi}|^{2}}
.
Define for any
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\Omega

\mathbf{v}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=Q_{x_{3}/h}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}\left(R_{-x_{3}/h}(x_{1},x_{2})\right),

and

\mathbf{w}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\frac{w\left(R_{-x_{3}/h}(x_{1},x_{2})\right)}{h}\vec{\xi}+\frac{(\partial_{x_{2}}v\left(R_{-x_{3}/h}(x_{1},x_{2})\right),-\partial_{x_{1}}v\left(R_{-x_{3}/h}(x_{1},x_{2})\right),0)^{t}}{h}.

Then
(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})
is a helical solution pair of
the vorticity equations (
1.2
).

Remark 1.2
.

Theorem
1.1
implies that to find helical solutions of (
1.2
), it suffices to solve solution pairs
(v,w)
of the 2D evolved model (
1.17
). Note that if the orthogonality condition (
1.13
) holds, then
v(x_{1},x_{2},t)\equiv 0
in (
1.17
) and we immediately recover (
1.15
) from (
1.17
). Thus to some extend, system (
1.17
) in Theorem
1.1
generalizes previous 2D classical model (
1.15
) in
[
13
,
22
,
27
]
.

We note that when (
1.13
) holds,
[
27
]
obtained the existence and uniqueness of
L^{\infty}
weak solutions to (
1.15
), which corresponds to Yudovich’s global well-posedness result in the case of the 2-dimensional incompressible Euler equations, see
[
2
,
5
,
42
]
for more results. However when (
1.13
) fails, to our knowledge there is no result to obtain the global well-posedness of solutions to the system (
1.17
), see
[
45
,
51
]
and reference therein.

Remark 1.3
.

Due to the complexity of 3D incompressible Euler equations, many references always use specific symmetries to simplify the original problem, among which axial symmetry is most widely used. There are many results devoted to deducing 3D Euler equations under axial symmetry with and without swirl to 2D models, and we refer readers to Proposition 2.15 in
[
52
]
for the 2D formulation for these cases. The vorticity-stream formulation (
1.17
) plays a parallel role in the context of 3D Euler equations under helical symmetry, just as the Proposition 2.15 in
[
52
]
(take
\nu=0
there) does for 3D Euler equations under axial symmetry. We believe that (
1.17
) will be useful in other related research area of helical solutions to 3D incompressible Euler equations.

1.3.
Traveling-rotating helical vortices with swirl

Based on (
1.17
), we now consider the existence of concentrated helical vortices in infinite cylinders, such that the corresponding support sets of vorticity fields remain invariant in shape as time evolves. Let
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
be an infinite cylinder
B_{R}(0)\times\mathbb{R}
for some
R>0
, which is a helical domain defined by (
1.10
). Let
D
be the cross-section of
\Omega
. Let
\bar{\alpha}>0
be a fixed constant. We study rotating invariant solutions of (

) being of the form

w(x,t)=W(R_{\bar{\alpha}t}x),\ \ \ v(x,t)=V(R_{\bar{\alpha}t}x),\ \ \ \varphi(x,t)=\Phi(R_{\bar{\alpha}t}x).

(1.18)

Substituting (

), we deduce later in section

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{K}\Phi=\frac{2h^{2}F_{1}\left(\Phi-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)}{(h^{2}+|x|^{2})^{2}}+\frac{F_{1}\left(\Phi-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)F_{1}^{\prime}\left(\Phi-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)}{h^{2}+|x|^{2}}-\frac{\bar{\alpha}|x|^{2}F_{1}^{\prime}\left(\Phi-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)}{h^{2}+|x|^{2}}\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+F_{2}\left(\Phi-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right),\ \ &\text{in}\ \ D,\\
\Phi=0,\ \ &\text{on}\ \ \partial D,\\
\end{cases}

(1.19)

for some profile functions
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
. Note that if we impose

, then (

) coincides with the elliptic system considered in
[

,
13
,
14

22

, which corresponds to traveling-rotating helical vortices of (

) with the orthogonality condition (
1.13
).

We observe that if

, then solutions to (

1.17
) with non-trivial

, and thus to traveling-rotating helical vortices of (

) with non-zero helical swirl

. Our second result is the existence of a family of concentrated helical vortices without the assumption (
1.13
) in infinite cylinders, whose vorticity field tends to a helical vortex filament (

) as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
h\not=0
,
\kappa>0
and
r_{*}\in(0,R)
be any given numbers. Let
\gamma(\tau)
be the helix parameterized by equation (
1.8
). Then there exists a small number
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, there exists a helical symmetric solution pair
(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon},P_{\varepsilon})(x,t)
of (
1.1
) such that the support set of
\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}=\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}
is a topological traveling-rotating helical tube that does not change form and concentrates near
\gamma(\tau)
in sense that for all
\tau
,

\text{dist}\left(\partial(supp(\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,|\ln\varepsilon|^{-1}\tau))),\gamma(\tau)\right)\to 0,\ \ \text{as}\ \varepsilon\to 0.

Moreover, the following properties hold:

(1)

The vorticity field
\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}
tends to
\gamma
in distributional sense, i.e.,

\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,|\ln\varepsilon|^{-1}\tau)\to\kappa\delta_{\gamma(\tau)}\mathbf{t}_{\gamma(\tau)},\ \ \text{as}\ \varepsilon\to 0.

(2)

The helical swirl
v_{\vec{\xi},\varepsilon}
defined by (
1.12
) is not vanishing. That is,
v_{\vec{\xi},\varepsilon}\not\equiv 0
.

(3)

Define
\bar{A}_{\varepsilon}=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in D\ |\ (x_{1},x_{2},0)\in supp(\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon})\}
the cross-section of the support of
\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}
. Then there are constants
r_{1},r_{2}>0
independent of
\varepsilon
such that

r_{1}\varepsilon\leq diam(\bar{A}_{\varepsilon})\leq r_{2}\varepsilon.

The idea of proof of Theorem
1.4
is as follows. Let
a>0
and
b\geq 0
be some fixed constants and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Let
\alpha=\frac{\kappa}{4\pi h\sqrt{h^{2}+r_{*}^{2}}}
be a constant dependent of
\kappa,h
and
r_{*}
. We choose
\bar{\alpha}=\alpha|\ln\varepsilon|
and

F_{1}(s)=\varepsilon^{-1}a(s-\mu_{\varepsilon})_{+},\ \ \ F_{2}(s)=\varepsilon^{-2}(b+\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon)\textbf{1}_{\{s-\mu_{\varepsilon}>0\}},\ \ \ s\in\mathbb{R},

(1.20)

in (
1.19
), where
s_{+}:=\max\{s,0\}
,
\textbf{1}_{A_{0}}
the characteristic function of a set
{A_{0}}
, namely
\textbf{1}_{A_{0}}=1
in
{A_{0}}
and
\textbf{1}_{A_{0}}=0
in
{A_{0}}^{c}
, and
\mu_{\varepsilon}
is a proper constant. Indeed,
\mu_{\varepsilon}
is a Lagrange multiplier whose value is determined in Lemma
4.2
.

We prove Theorem
1.4
by constructing solutions to (
1.19
) via Arnold’s variational principle, which is successfully used in the construction of concentrated helical vortices with the assumption (
1.13
) in infinite cylinders, see, e.g.,
[
13
]
. For
\Lambda>0
, we define constraint sets

\mathcal{M}_{\kappa,\Lambda}=\left\{\omega\in L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(D)\ \Big|\ 0\leq\omega\leq\frac{\Lambda}{\varepsilon^{2}},\,\,\,\int_{D}\omega dx=\kappa\right\},

and the energy functional

E_{\varepsilon}(\omega)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}\omega\mathcal{G}_{K}\omega\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}\int_{D}|x|^{2}\omega\mathrm{d}x-\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{D}J_{\varepsilon}(|x|,\varepsilon^{2}\omega(x))\mathrm{d}x,

where
\mathcal{G}_{K}
is the inverse operator of
\mathcal{L}_{K}
defined by (
1.16
),
|x|=\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}
and

J_{\varepsilon}\left(r,s\right)=\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}\left(s-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}^{2}}{4h^{2}a\varepsilon+2a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}.

(1.21)

The reason of the choice of
J(r,s)
in (
1.21
) is shown in section 4. By proving the existence of maximizers of
E_{\varepsilon}
relative to
\mathcal{M}_{\kappa,\Lambda}
and analyzing their asymptotic behavior, we will eventually find a family of concentrated helical vortex solutions of (
1.1
) with non-zero helical swirl.

Remark 1.5
.

The reason of taking
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
in (
1.19
) in the form of (
1.20
) is for simplicity of the proof. Indeed, by a slight modification of arguments in the present paper (see the proof in section 4), one may construct solutions of (
1.19
) with other types of profile functions
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
. For example, for any
p>1
, we can take
F_{1}(s)=\varepsilon^{-1}(s-\mu_{\varepsilon})_{+}^{p+1},\ F_{2}(s)=\varepsilon^{-2}(s-\mu_{\varepsilon})_{+}^{p}
and construct traveling-rotating helical vortices with swirl, which correspond to
C^{1}
classical helical vortex solutions of (
1.1
). Note also that constants
a,b
in (
1.20
) is arbitrary. By choosing different
a
and
b
, we can get different families of concentrated helical vorticity field of (
1.2
) satisfying results in Theorem
1.4
.

Remark 1.6
.

One of the key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.4
in section 4 is the refined decomposition of Green’s functions for the operator
\mathcal{L}_{K}
in the disk obtained in
[
14
]
(see Lemma
4.1
). Recently, similar decomposition of Green’s functions for
\mathcal{L}_{K}
in the whole space was proved in
[
10
]
and helical vortex patches without helical swirl in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
were constructed by a variational method. Therefore, by using decomposition of Green’s functions obtained in
[
10
]
and combining ideas of the proof of Theorem
1.4
, we are able to construct a family of concentrated helical vortices with
non-zero helical swirl
in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Due to the absence of new difficulties, we will omit the statement and proof of this result in this paper.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we derive the 2D vorticity-stream formulation of 3D Euler equations under helical symmetry with swirl and prove Theorem
1.1
. Some basic properties of helical functions and vector fields are proved. In section 3, we show that to find traveling-rotating invariant helical vortices, it is sufficient to solve the semilinear elliptic equations (
1.19
). In section 4, we prove the existence and asymptotic behaviors of maximizers of
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}
in the set

, from which we obtain rotating invariant solution pairs
(v,w)
to equations (
1.17
) and finish the proof of Theorem

.

2.
2D vorticity-stream formulation for helical vortices with swirl

Let us start by giving some notations which are frequently used in the following sections. Let
\Omega

For a general vector field

The following property can be proved directly by using the definitions of helical functions and vector fields, see also
[

Lemma 2.1

(
[

]
)
.

The following claims hold true:

(a).

Let

\nabla_{\vec{\xi}}f\equiv 0.

(b).

Let

\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})^{t}

\nabla_{\vec{\xi}}\mathbf{v}=(v_{2},-v_{1},0)^{t}.

Proof.

Proofs of
(a)
and
(b)
are given in Claims 2.3 and 2.5 in
[
27
]
respectively and we omit them here. ∎

It follows from
[

that if

satisfies (
1.13

satisfies (
1.14
) and (
1.15
). However, in case that
\mathbf{v}
does not satisfy (

) without the assumption (

), we define the
orthogonal vector field
of
\mathbf{v}

\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{v}-\frac{v_{\vec{\xi}}\vec{\xi}}{|\vec{\xi}|^{2}}

(2.1)

and the vorticity field of

\vec{\zeta}=\nabla\times\mathbf{u},

where the helical swirl

We have properties of
\mathbf{u}
,
\mathbf{v}
and the associated vorticity field

as follows.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathbf{v}
be a
C^{1}
vector field which is helical symmetric and divergence-free. Then there hold

(a).

v_{\vec{\xi}}
is helical symmetric.

(b).

\mathbf{u}
is helical symmetric and divergence-free.

(c).

Let
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})^{t}
and
\vec{\zeta}=(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3})^{t}
be the vorticity field of
\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{u}
. Then

\mathbf{w}=\frac{w_{3}}{h}\vec{\xi}+\frac{1}{h}(\partial_{x_{2}}v_{\vec{\xi}},-\partial_{x_{1}}v_{\vec{\xi}},0)^{t},

(2.2)

and

w_{3}=\zeta_{3}-\frac{2h^{2}v_{\vec{\xi}}}{|\vec{\xi}|^{4}}-\frac{x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}v_{\vec{\xi}}+x_{2}\partial_{x_{2}}v_{\vec{\xi}}}{|\vec{\xi}|^{2}}.

(2.3)

Remark 2.3
.

Note that if
\mathbf{v}
satisfies the orthogonality condition, i.e.,
v_{\vec{\xi}}\equiv 0,
then (
2.2
) becomes
\mathbf{w}=\frac{w_{3}}{h}\vec{\xi}
. This coincides with the property (
1.14
) in
[

Proof.

For
(a)
and
(b)
, we refer to the proof of Lemma 2.5 in
[
45

. Indeed, these can be directly verified by Lemma
2.1
and the definition of
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
.

As for
(c)
, we first prove (
2.2
). By the definition of
\mathbf{u}
, we observe that
\mathbf{u}\cdot\vec{\xi}=0
, which implies that

u_{3}=\frac{-x_{2}u_{1}+x_{1}u_{2}}{h}.

(2.4)

We deduce from the above identity and the relation
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{v}-\frac{v_{\vec{\xi}}\vec{\xi}}{|\vec{\xi}|^{2}}
that

\begin{split}v_{3}=&u_{3}+\frac{v_{\vec{\xi}}h}{|\vec{\xi}|^{2}}\\
=&\frac{-x_{2}u_{1}+x_{1}u_{2}}{h}+\frac{v_{\vec{\xi}}h}{|\vec{\xi}|^{2}}\\
=&\frac{1}{h}\left[-x_{2}\left(v_{1}-\frac{x_{2}v_{\vec{\xi}}}{|\vec{\xi}|^{2}}\right)+x_{1}\left(v_{2}+\frac{x_{1}v_{\vec{\xi}}}{|\vec{\xi}|^{2}}\right)\right]+\frac{v_{\vec{\xi}}h}{|\vec{\xi}|^{2}}\\
=&\frac{-x_{2}v_{1}+x_{1}v_{2}}{h}+\frac{v_{\vec{\xi}}}{h}.\end{split}

(2.5)

Since
\mathbf{v}
is helical symmetric, using Lemma
2.1
we find

\partial_{x_{3}}v_{2}=\frac{-x_{2}\partial_{x_{1}}v_{2}+x_{1}\partial_{x_{2}}v_{2}-v_{1}}{h}.

By direct calculations, we deduce from the above equation and (
2.5
) that

\begin{split}w_{1}&=\partial_{x_{2}}v_{3}-\partial_{x_{3}}v_{2}\\
&=\partial_{x_{2}}\left(\frac{-x_{2}v_{1}+x_{1}v_{2}}{h}+\frac{v_{\vec{\xi}}}{h}\right)-\frac{-x_{2}\partial_{x_{1}}v_{2}+x_{1}\partial_{x_{2}}v_{2}-v_{1}}{h}\\
&=\frac{x_{2}}{h}(\partial_{x_{1}}v_{2}-\partial_{x_{2}}v_{1})+\frac{\partial_{x_{2}}v_{\vec{\xi}}}{h}\\
&=\frac{x_{2}}{h}w_{3}+\frac{\partial_{x_{2}}v_{\vec{\xi}}}{h}.\end{split}

Similarly, one has

\begin{split}w_{2}=\frac{-x_{1}}{h}w_{3}-\frac{\partial_{x_{1}}v_{\vec{\xi}}}{h}.\end{split}

(
2.2
) then follows from the above calculations immediately. As for (
2.3
), using
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{v}-\frac{v_{\vec{\xi}}\vec{\xi}}{|\vec{\xi}|^{2}}
and the definition of vorticity field
\vec{\zeta}
and
\mathbf{w}
, one computes directly that (
2.3
) holds. The proof of Lemma

is complete.
∎

From
[
27
]
, we know that for a helical symmetric and divergence-free vector field
\mathbf{v}
satisfying (

\mathbf{w}
is a helical symmetric vector field. While the following lemma shows that, without the assumption (
1.13
),
\mathbf{w}
is still helical.

Lemma 2.4
.

For a
C^{2}
vector field
\mathbf{v}
, let
\mathbf{w}
be the vorticity field of
\mathbf{v}
. If
\mathbf{v}
is helical symmetric and divergence-free, then
\mathbf{w}
is helical symmetric.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
2.1
, it suffices to show that

\nabla_{\vec{\xi}}\mathbf{w}=(w_{2},-w_{1},0)^{t}.

(2.6)

By (
2.2
) and straightforward computations, we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}=\partial_{x_{1}}w_{1}+\partial_{x_{2}}w_{2}+\partial_{x_{3}}w_{3}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x_{1}}\left(\frac{x_{2}w_{3}+\partial_{x_{2}}v_{\vec{\xi}}}{h}\right)+\partial_{x_{1}}\left(\frac{-x_{1}w_{3}+\partial_{x_{1}}v_{\vec{\xi}}}{h}\right)+\partial_{x_{3}}w_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{2}\partial_{x_{1}}w_{3}-x_{1}\partial_{x_{2}}w_{3}+h\partial_{x_{3}}w_{3}}{h}=\frac{\nabla_{\vec{\xi}}w_{3}}{h}.

Thus
\nabla_{\vec{\xi}}w_{3}=0
, which implies that
w_{3}
is a helical symmetric function.

Using (
2.2
) and the fact
\nabla_{\vec{\xi}}w_{3}=\nabla_{\vec{\xi}}v_{\vec{\xi}}=0
, one computes directly that

\displaystyle\nabla_{\vec{\xi}}w_{1}=\frac{1}{h}\left(x_{2}\nabla_{\vec{\xi}}w_{3}+w_{3}\nabla_{\vec{\xi}}x_{2}+\nabla_{\vec{\xi}}\partial_{x_{2}}v_{\vec{\xi}}\right)=\frac{-x_{1}w_{3}-\partial_{x_{1}}v_{\vec{\xi}}}{h}=w_{2}.

Here we have used the identity
\partial_{x_{2}}(\nabla_{\vec{\xi}}v_{\vec{\xi}})=\nabla_{\vec{\xi}}\partial_{x_{2}}v_{\vec{\xi}}+\partial_{x_{1}}v_{\vec{\xi}}
. Similarly, we find

\nabla_{\vec{\xi}}w_{2}=-w_{1}.

Thus (
2.6
) holds and
\mathbf{w}
is helical symmetric.
∎

Based on the above three lemmas, we are able to introduce the “stream function” for a divergence-free helical symmetric vector field
\mathbf{v}
without the assumption (
1.13
). Let
D
be the cross-section of
\Omega
, which is a simply-connected bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial D.
We have

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathbf{v}
be a helical symmetric and divergence-free vector field and
v_{\vec{\xi}}
and
\mathbf{u}
be defined by (
1.12
) and (
2.1
). If in addition

\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0,\ \ \text{on}\ \ \partial\Omega,

where
\mathbf{n}
is the outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega
.
Then there exists the unique function
\varphi:D\mapsto\mathbb{R}
such that

\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},0)\\
u_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},0)\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{|\vec{\xi}|^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}-x_{1}x_{2}&h^{2}+x_{1}^{2}\\
-(h^{2}+x_{2}^{2})&x_{1}x_{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{x_{1}}\varphi(x_{1},x_{2})\\
\partial_{x_{2}}\varphi(x_{1},x_{2})\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ \forall\,(x_{1},x_{2})\in D

(2.7)

and

\varphi=0,\ \ \text{on}\ \ \partial D.

(2.8)

Moreover, let
\vec{\zeta}=(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3})^{t}
be the vorticity of
\mathbf{u}
. Then the following relation holds

\mathcal{L}_{K}\varphi(x_{1},x_{2})=\zeta_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},0),\ \ \ \forall\,(x_{1},x_{2})\in D,

where
\mathcal{L}_{K}\varphi:=-\text{div}(K(x_{1},x_{2})\nabla\varphi)
is a second order elliptic operator in divergence form with the coefficient matrix

K(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{1}{h^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}h^{2}+x_{2}^{2}&-x_{1}x_{2}\\
-x_{1}x_{2}&h^{2}+x_{1}^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{v}
is helical symmetric and divergence-free, from Lemma
2.2
we get that
\mathbf{u}
is also helical symmetric and divergence-free. Using identities
\mathbf{u}\cdot\vec{\xi}=0
and
\nabla_{\vec{\xi}}u_{3}=0

0=\partial_{x_{1}}u_{1}+\partial_{x_{2}}u_{2}+\partial_{x_{3}}u_{3}=\frac{1}{h^{2}}\partial_{x_{1}}\left[(h^{2}+x_{2}^{2})u_{1}-x_{1}x_{2}u_{2}\right]+\frac{1}{h^{2}}\partial_{x_{2}}\left[(h^{2}+x_{1}^{2})u_{2}-x_{1}x_{2}u_{1}\right].

Thus we can define a function
\varphi:D\mapsto\mathbb{R}
such that

\partial_{x_{2}}\varphi(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{1}{h^{2}}\left[(h^{2}+x_{2}^{2})u_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},0)-x_{1}x_{2}u_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},0)\right]

and

\partial_{x_{1}}\varphi(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{1}{h^{2}}\left[(h^{2}+x_{1}^{2})u_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},0)-x_{1}x_{2}u_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},0)\right],

which implies (
2.7
). (
2.9
) follows immediately from (
2.7
) and the definition of
\zeta_{3}
, that is,
\zeta_{3}=\partial_{x_{1}}u_{2}-\partial_{x_{2}}u_{1}
.

Next, we verify the boundary condition for
\varphi

\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})^{t}
be the outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega
at the point
(x_{1},x_{2},0)\in\partial\Omega

\vec{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0
(See (2.62) in
[

n_{3}=\frac{-x_{2}n_{1}+x_{1}n_{2}}{h}.

Therefore, by using (

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{n}=\left(\mathbf{u}+\frac{v_{\vec{\xi}}\vec{\xi}}{|\vec{\xi}|^{2}}\right)\cdot\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}=u_{1}n_{1}+u_{2}n_{2}+\frac{-x_{2}u_{1}+x_{1}u_{2}}{h}\cdot\frac{-x_{2}n_{1}+x_{1}n_{2}}{h}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x_{2}}\varphi n_{1}-\partial_{x_{2}}\varphi n_{2},

which implies that for some constant

\varphi\equiv C\ \ \text{on}\ \ \partial D.

Without loss of generality, we may take
\varphi\equiv 0\ \ \text{on}\ \ \partial D.
The uniqueness of
\varphi
follows from the maximum principle of elliptic equations. The proof is thus finished.
∎

From the definition of helical functions, we get the following property of
\varphi

Lemma 2.6
.

For any helical symmetric function
g:\Omega\mapsto\mathbb{R}
, the following identity holds

\left(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla g\right)(x_{1},x_{2},0)=\nabla^{\perp}\varphi(x_{1},x_{2})\cdot\nabla g(x_{1},x_{2},0),\ \ \ \ \ (x_{1},x_{2})\in D.

Since

\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla g=\left(\mathbf{u}+\frac{v_{\vec{\xi}}\vec{\xi}}{|\vec{\xi}|^{2}}\right)\cdot\nabla g=\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla g.

By
\mathbf{u}\cdot\vec{\xi}=0
and
\nabla_{\vec{\xi}}g=0
, we have

u_{3}=\frac{-x_{2}u_{1}+x_{1}u_{2}}{h},

and

\partial_{x_{3}}g=\frac{-x_{2}\partial_{x_{1}}g+x_{1}\partial_{x_{2}}g}{h}.

Combining the above equalities with (

\begin{split}\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla g=&(u_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},0),u_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},0))\begin{pmatrix}1+\frac{x_{2}^{2}}{h^{2}}&-\frac{x_{1}x_{2}}{h^{2}}\\
-\frac{x_{1}x_{2}}{h^{2}}&1+\frac{x_{1}^{2}}{h^{2}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{x_{1}}g(x_{1},x_{2},0)\\
\partial_{x_{2}}g(x_{1},x_{2},0)\end{pmatrix}\\
=&\frac{1}{|\vec{\xi}|^{2}}\left(\partial_{x_{1}}\varphi(x_{1},x_{2}),\partial_{x_{2}}\varphi(x_{1},x_{2})\right)\begin{pmatrix}-x_{1}x_{2}&-(h^{2}+x_{2}^{2})\\
h^{2}+x_{1}^{2}&x_{1}x_{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1+\frac{x_{2}^{2}}{h^{2}}&-\frac{x_{1}x_{2}}{h^{2}}\\
-\frac{x_{1}x_{2}}{h^{2}}&1+\frac{x_{1}^{2}}{h^{2}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{x_{1}}g(x_{1},x_{2},0)\\
\partial_{x_{2}}g(x_{1},x_{2},0)\end{pmatrix}\\

3.
Elliptic equation for traveling-rotating helical vortices

Let

For a function

\nabla_{x}f(x,t)=&R_{\bar{\alpha}t}(\nabla_{y}F)(R_{\bar{\alpha}t}x).\end{split}

In what follows, we will use change of variable

and still denote
y
as

for simplicity of notations. Substituting (
3.1
) into the first equation of (

\nabla^{\perp}\left(\Phi(x)-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)\cdot\nabla V(x)=0.

(3.2)

So if there exists a function
F_{1}
such that

(3.3)

then (
3.2
) holds.

Substituting (

\nabla^{\perp}\left(\Phi-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)\cdot\nabla W=\partial_{x_{2}}V\partial_{x_{1}}U_{3}-\partial_{x_{1}}V\partial_{x_{2}}U_{3}+\frac{2V\left(x_{2}\partial_{x_{1}}V-x_{1}\partial_{x_{2}}V\right)}{|{\vec{\xi}}|^{4}},

3.3

\begin{split}&\partial_{x_{2}}V\partial_{x_{1}}U_{3}-\partial_{x_{1}}V\partial_{x_{2}}U_{3}\\
=&F_{1}^{\prime}\left(\Phi(x)-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)\left(\nabla^{\perp}\left(\Phi-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)\cdot\nabla U_{3}\right)\\

F_{2}

(3.6)

then (
3.5
) holds.

Now, substituting (
3.1
) into the third equation of (
2.12

\mathcal{L}_{K}\Phi=W+\frac{2h^{2}V}{|{\vec{\xi}}|^{4}}+\frac{x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}V+x_{2}\partial_{x_{2}}V}{|{\vec{\xi}}|^{2}}.

(3.7)

Taking
U_{3}(x)=-\frac{x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}\Phi(x)+x_{2}\partial_{x_{2}}\Phi(x)}{|{\vec{\xi}}|^{2}}
, (
1.11
), (
3.3
) and (

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{K}\Phi(x)&=\frac{2h^{2}F_{1}\left(\Phi(x)-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)}{(h^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})^{2}}+\frac{F_{1}\left(\Phi(x)-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)F_{1}^{\prime}\left(\Phi(x)-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)}{h^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}\\
&\quad-\frac{\bar{\alpha}|x|^{2}F_{1}^{\prime}\left(\Phi(x)-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)}{h^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}+F_{2}\left(\Phi(x)-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right),\ \ \ x\in D.\end{split}

(3.8)

for
\Phi
:

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{K}\Phi=\frac{2h^{2}F_{1}\left(\Phi-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)}{(h^{2}+|x|^{2})^{2}}+\frac{F_{1}\left(\Phi-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)F_{1}^{\prime}\left(\Phi-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)}{h^{2}+|x|^{2}}-\frac{\bar{\alpha}|x|^{2}F_{1}^{\prime}\left(\Phi-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)}{h^{2}+|x|^{2}}+F_{2}\left(\Phi-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right),\ \ &\text{in}\ \ D,\\
\Phi=0,\ \ &\text{on}\ \ \partial D,\\
\end{cases}

(3.9)

where profile functions
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
can be chosen arbitrary. Let
\zeta=\mathcal{L}_{K}\Phi
and
\mathcal{G}_{K}
be the Green’s operator of the elliptic operator
\mathcal{L}_{K}
in
D
with Dirichlet condition (see Lemma
4.1
in section 4). Then
\Phi=\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta
and (
3.9
) is equivalent to the following equations for
\zeta

\begin{split}\zeta=&\frac{2h^{2}F_{1}\left(\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)}{(h^{2}+|x|^{2})^{2}}+\frac{F_{1}\left(\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)F_{1}^{\prime}\left(\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)}{h^{2}+|x|^{2}}\\
&-\frac{\bar{\alpha}|x|^{2}F_{1}^{\prime}\left(\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)}{h^{2}+|x|^{2}}+F_{2}\left(\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right),\ \ \text{in}\ \ D.\end{split}

(3.10)

Solutions of (
3.10

). By constructing families of concentrated solutions to (

and

tends to a helical vortex filament (

\varepsilon\to 0

Construction of traveling-rotating helical vortices in an infinite pipe

As stated above, we will prove the existence of concentrated solutions

4.1.
Green’s function of
\mathcal{L}_{K}

To construct concentrated solutions of (

. Since the Green’s operator
\mathcal{G}_{K}
in (
3.10
) is the same as that in (

for the operator
\mathcal{L}_{K}
in bounded domains, which has been already found in the previous literature
[

Lemma 4.1

(Theorem 1.3,
[
14
]
)
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary. Let
\Gamma(x)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|x|
be the fundamental solution of the Laplacian
-\Delta
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
T_{x}
be a
C^{\infty}
positive-definite matrix-valued function determined by
K
satisfying

T_{x}^{-1}(T_{x}^{-1})^{t}=K(x)\ \ \ \ \ \ \forall x\in U.

(4.1)

Then there exists a function
G_{K}(x,y)
defined on
U\times U
such that for any
f\in L^{q}(U)
with
q>2
, the solution of the following linear problem

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{K}u=-\mathrm{div}(K(x)\nabla u)=f,\ \ &x\in U,\\
u=0,\ \ &x\in\partial U,\end{cases}

(4.2)

can be represented by

\begin{split}u(x)=\mathcal{G}_{K}f(x)=\int_{U}G_{K}(x,y)f(y)\mathrm{d}y,\ \ \ \ \ \ x\in U.\end{split}

(4.3)

Moreover,
G_{K}(x,y)
has the following decomposition

G_{K}(x,y)=\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}+\sqrt{\det K(y)}^{-1}}{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}(x-y)\right)+H_{0}(x,y),

(4.4)

where
H_{0}(x,y)\in C_{loc}^{0,\gamma}(U\times U)
for some
\gamma\in(0,1)
and

H_{0}(x,y)\leq C,\ \ \ \forall\,x,y\in U

for some constant
C
.

4.2.
Variational setting and existence of maximizers

We observe that profile functions
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
in (

) can be arbitrary.
In the following, we will choose

F_{1}(s)=\varepsilon^{-1}a(s-\mu_{\varepsilon})_{+},\ \ \ F_{2}(s)=\varepsilon^{-2}(b+\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon)\textbf{1}_{\{s-\mu_{\varepsilon}>0\}},\ \ \ s\in\mathbb{R},

in (
3.10
), where
0<\varepsilon<1
,

are arbitrary fixed constants,
\bar{\alpha}=\alpha|\ln\varepsilon|
with
\alpha=\frac{\kappa}{4\pi h\sqrt{h^{2}+r_{*}^{2}}}
and
\mu_{\varepsilon}
is a constant to be determined later, see Lemma
4.2
. From (
3.10
), we get equations for
\zeta

\begin{split}\zeta=&\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\frac{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+|x|^{2})}{(h^{2}+|x|^{2})^{2}}\left(\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}-\mu_{\varepsilon}\right)_{+}\\
&+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+|x|^{2}}+b\right)\textbf{1}_{\{\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}-\mu_{\varepsilon}>0\}}\ \ \ \ a.e.\ \text{in}\ D.\end{split}

(4.5)

Note that the case
a=0
corresponds to helical vortices with the assumption (
1.13
), which has been considered in
[
9
,
13
,
14
,
22
]
. To construct helical vortices of (

) without the assumption (

), we focus our attention on the case

Set
|x|=(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Let
i_{\varepsilon}\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
be a function defined by

i_{\varepsilon}\left(r,s\right)=\frac{2h^{2}a\varepsilon s_{+}}{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}}+\frac{a^{2}s_{+}}{h^{2}+r^{2}}+\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon\textbf{1}_{\{s>0\}}}{h^{2}+r^{2}}+b\textbf{1}_{\{s>0\}},\ \ \ \ \ r,s\geq 0.

(4.6)

Then the right hand side of (
4.5
) equals
\varepsilon^{-2}i_{\varepsilon}\left(|x|,\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}-\mu_{\varepsilon}\right)
.

Let
I_{\varepsilon}(r,s)=\int_{0}^{s}i_{\varepsilon}(r,\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau
for
s\geq 0
and
J_{\varepsilon}(r,\cdot)
be the modified conjugate function of
I_{\varepsilon}(r,\cdot)
defined by

J_{\varepsilon}(r,s)=\begin{cases}\sup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}}[st-I_{\varepsilon}(r,t)],\ \ \ &\text{if}\ \ s>0,\\
0,\ \ \ &\text{if}\ \ s=0.\end{cases}

Then direct calculation yields

J_{\varepsilon}\left(r,s\right)=\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}\left(s-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}^{2}}{4h^{2}a\varepsilon+2a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}.

(4.7)

Let
\Lambda>1
. We consider maximization problem of the following functional for
\zeta

E_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}\zeta\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}\int_{D}|x|^{2}\zeta\mathrm{d}x-\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{D}J_{\varepsilon}(|x|,\varepsilon^{2}\zeta(x))\mathrm{d}x,

(4.8)

over the constraint set

\mathcal{M}_{\kappa,\Lambda}=\left\{\zeta\in L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(D)\ \Big|\ 0\leq\zeta\leq\frac{\Lambda}{\varepsilon^{2}},\,\,\,\int_{D}\zeta dx=\kappa\right\}.

(4.9)

The choice of
\Lambda
will be given in Lemma
4.11
.

Our goal is to find maximizers of
E_{\varepsilon}
relative to
\mathcal{M}_{\kappa,\Lambda}
and give their asymptotic behavior as
\varepsilon\to 0
. To begin with, we prove the existence and profiles of maximizers of
E_{\varepsilon}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\Lambda>\max\{\alpha a+b,1+\pi R^{2}/\kappa\}
, there exists
\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
being a maximizer of
E_{\varepsilon}
over
\mathcal{M}_{\kappa,\Lambda}
, i.e.,

E_{\varepsilon}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})=\max_{\zeta\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}}E_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)<+\infty.

(4.10)

Moreover, there exists a Lagrange multiplier
\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
with

\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\geq-\frac{\alpha R^{2}}{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-\frac{(h^{2}+R^{2})^{2}\left(\Lambda-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+R^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+R^{2})},

such that

\begin{split}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=&\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\frac{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}}\psi_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}+\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}+b\right)\textbf{1}_{\{0<\psi_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}<(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}(\Lambda-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b)/(2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2}))\}}\\
&+\frac{\Lambda}{\varepsilon^{2}}\textbf{1}_{\{\psi_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\geq(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}(\Lambda-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b)/(2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2}))\}}\ \ a.e.\ \text{in}\ D,\end{split}

(4.11)

where
r=|x|
and

\psi_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{\bar{\alpha}r^{2}}{2}-\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}.

(4.12)

Proof.

We may take a sequence
\zeta_{j}\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
such that as
j\to+\infty

\begin{split}E_{\varepsilon}(\zeta_{j})&\to\sup\{E_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)~|~\zeta\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\}.\end{split}

Since
\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
is closed in
L^{\infty}(D)
weak star topology and
L^{2}(D)
weak topology, there exists
\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\in L^{\infty}(D)
such that as
j\to+\infty

\zeta_{j}\to\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\ \ \ \ \ \ ~~\text{weakly~in}\ L^{2}(D)\ \text{and~weakly~star~in}\ L^{\infty}(D).

It is easily checked that
\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
. Since the Green’s operator
\mathcal{G}_{K}
is compact from
L^{2}(D)
to
L^{2}(D)
, we have

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{D}{\zeta_{j}\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{j}}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{D}{\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}}\mathrm{d}x.

On the other hand, it follows from
|x|^{2}\in L^{2}(D)
that

\begin{split}\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{D}|x|^{2}\zeta_{j}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{D}|x|^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Moreover, due to the convexity of
J_{\varepsilon}(r,s)
relative to
s
, we infer from the lower semicontinuity of
J_{\varepsilon}(r,s)
that

\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\int_{D}J_{\varepsilon}(r,\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{j}(x))\mathrm{d}x\geq\int_{D}J_{\varepsilon}(r,\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(x))\mathrm{d}x.

To conclude, we have
E_{\varepsilon}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})=\lim_{j\to+\infty}E_{\varepsilon}(\zeta_{j})=\sup_{\zeta\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}}E_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)
. The existence of maximizers of
E_{\varepsilon}
over
\mathcal{M}_{\kappa,\Lambda}
is proved.

We now turn to prove the profile
(
4.11
)
of maximizers
\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
. We may assume
\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\not\equiv 0
, otherwise the conclusion is obtained by letting
\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=0
.
We consider a family of variations of
\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}

\zeta_{(s)}=\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}+s(\zeta-\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}),\ \ \ s\in[0,1],

defined for arbitrary
\zeta\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
. Since
\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
is a maximizer, one computes directly that

\begin{split}0&\geq\frac{d}{ds}E_{\varepsilon}(\zeta_{(s)})|_{s=0^{+}}\\
&=\int_{D}(\zeta-\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})\left(\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{\bar{\alpha}r^{2}}{2}-\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}\right)\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

This implies that for any
\zeta\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
, there holds

\begin{split}\int_{D}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\left(\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{\bar{\alpha}r^{2}}{2}-\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}\right)\mathrm{d}x\\
\geq\int_{D}\zeta\left(\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{\bar{\alpha}r^{2}}{2}-\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}\right)\ \mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

By the classical bathtub principle (see
[
50
]
, §1.14x), we obtain that there exists a Lagrange multiplier
\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
such taht

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{\bar{\alpha}r^{2}}{2}-\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}&\geq\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\ \ \ \text{whenever}\ \zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=\frac{\Lambda}{\varepsilon^{2}},\\
\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{\bar{\alpha}r^{2}}{2}-\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}&=\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\ \ \ \text{whenever}\ 0<\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}<\frac{\Lambda}{\varepsilon^{2}},\\
\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{\bar{\alpha}r^{2}}{2}-\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}&\leq\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\ \ \ \text{whenever}\ \zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=0.\end{split}

(4.13)

Moreover,
\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
satisfies

\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=\inf\left\{t:\left|\left\{\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{\bar{\alpha}r^{2}}{2}-\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}>t\right\}\right|\leq\frac{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}{\Lambda}\right\}\in\mathbb{R}.

(4.14)

Set

\psi_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{\bar{\alpha}r^{2}}{2}-\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}.

Note that from the second equation in (

\{0<\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\leq\varepsilon^{-2}(h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon/(h^{2}+r^{2})+b)\}\subseteq\{\psi_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=0\}
. While on the set

, by properties of Sobolev spaces, one has

0=\mathcal{L}_{K}(\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})-\mathcal{L}_{K}\left(\frac{\bar{\alpha}|x|^{2}}{2}\right)=\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}+\frac{2h^{4}\bar{\alpha}}{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}}>0\ \ \ \text{a.e.}

Thus, we conclude that the Lesbague measure of
\{\psi_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=0\}
is 0, which implies that
|\{0<\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\leq\varepsilon^{-2}(h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon/(h^{2}+r^{2})+b)\}|=|\{\psi_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=0\}|=0

4.11
)

It remains to show that
\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\geq-\frac{\alpha R^{2}}{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-\frac{(h^{2}+R^{2})^{2}\left(\Lambda-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+R^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+R^{2})}

\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}<-\frac{\alpha R^{2}}{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-\frac{(h^{2}+R^{2})^{2}\left(\Lambda-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+R^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+R^{2})}

\left\{\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{\bar{\alpha}r^{2}}{2}-\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}>-\frac{\alpha R^{2}}{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-\frac{(h^{2}+R^{2})^{2}\left(\Lambda-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+R^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+R^{2})}\right\}

On the other hand, it can be seen that for any

\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(x)>0
and

\frac{\bar{\alpha}r^{2}}{2}+\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}\leq\frac{\alpha R^{2}}{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\frac{(h^{2}+R^{2})^{2}\left(\Lambda-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+R^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+R^{2})}.

\left\{\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{\bar{\alpha}r^{2}}{2}-\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}>-\frac{\alpha R^{2}}{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-\frac{(h^{2}+R^{2})^{2}\left(\Lambda-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+R^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+R^{2})}\right\}

equals exactly to

4.3.
lower bounds of

and
\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}

For convenience, we will use
C_{1},C_{2},\cdots
to denote generic positive constants independent of
\varepsilon

\Lambda

(4.15)

Clearly,
Y
is radially symmetric in

Lemma 4.3
.

The maximizers set of

in

. That is,
Y|_{\partial B_{r_{*}}(0)}=\max_{D}Y
. Moreover, up to a rotation the maximizer is unique.

We first give lower bounds of

by choosing proper test functions.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any
z\in D
, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
and
\Lambda
, such that for all
\varepsilon
sufficiently small

E_{\varepsilon}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})\geq\frac{\kappa}{2}Y(z)\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-C.

As a consequence, there holds

E_{\varepsilon}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})\geq\frac{\kappa}{2}Y((r_{*},0))\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-C.

(4.16)

Proof.

For any
z\in D
, we choose a test function
\hat{\zeta}_{\varepsilon}
defined by

\hat{\zeta}_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\textbf{1}_{T_{z}^{-1}B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)+z},

where the matrix
T_{z}
satisfies (
4.1
), and
r_{\varepsilon}>0
satisfies

\pi r_{\varepsilon}^{2}\sqrt{\det K(z)}=\varepsilon^{2}\kappa.

(4.17)

Thus
\hat{\zeta}_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small. Since
\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
is a maximizer, we have
E_{\varepsilon}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})\geq E_{\varepsilon}(\hat{\zeta}_{\varepsilon})
. By Lemma
4.1
, we get

\begin{split}E_{\varepsilon}(\hat{\zeta}_{\varepsilon})=&\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}\int_{D}\hat{\zeta}_{\varepsilon}(x)G_{K}(x,y)\hat{\zeta}_{\varepsilon}(y)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y-{\frac{\alpha}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}}\int_{D}|x|^{2}\hat{\zeta}_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}x-\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{D}J_{\varepsilon}(r,\varepsilon^{2}\hat{\zeta}_{\varepsilon}(x))\mathrm{d}x\\
\geq&\frac{1}{2}\int_{T_{z}^{-1}B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)+z}\int_{T_{z}^{-1}B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)+z}\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}+\sqrt{\det K(y)}^{-1}}{2\varepsilon^{4}}\Gamma\left(\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}(x-y)\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\\
&-{\frac{\alpha}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}}\int_{D}|x|^{2}\hat{\zeta}_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}x-C_{1}.\end{split}

Since
r_{\varepsilon}=O(\varepsilon),
by the positive-definiteness and regularity of
K
, we have

\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}=\sqrt{\det K(z)}^{-1}+O(\varepsilon),\ \ \ \ \forall x\in T_{z}^{-1}B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)+z,

\Gamma\left(\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}(x-y)\right)=\Gamma\left(T_{z}(x-y)\right)+O(\varepsilon),\ \ \ \ \forall x,y\in T_{z}^{-1}B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)+z,\ x\neq y.

Thus one computes directly that

\begin{split}E_{\varepsilon}(\hat{\zeta}_{\varepsilon})\geq&\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{4}}\int_{T_{z}^{-1}B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)+z}\int_{T_{z}^{-1}B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)+z}\left(\sqrt{\det K(z)}^{-1}+O(\varepsilon)\right)\left(\Gamma\left(T_{z}(x-y)\right)+O(\varepsilon)\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\\
&-{\frac{\kappa\alpha(|z|+O(\varepsilon))^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}}-C_{1}\\
\geq&\frac{\sqrt{\det K(z)}^{-1}}{4\pi\varepsilon^{4}}\int_{T_{z}^{-1}B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)+z}\int_{T_{z}^{-1}B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)+z}\ln{\frac{1}{|T_{z}(x-y)|}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y-{\frac{\kappa\alpha|z|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}}-C_{2}\\
=&\frac{\sqrt{\det K(z)}^{-1}}{4\pi\varepsilon^{4}}\int_{B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)}\int_{B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)}\ln{\frac{1}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|}}\cdot\det K(z)\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}-{\frac{\kappa\alpha|z|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}}-C_{2}\\
\geq&\frac{\sqrt{\det K(z)}}{4\pi\varepsilon^{4}}(\pi r_{\varepsilon}^{2})^{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-{\frac{\kappa\alpha|z|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}}-C_{3}\\
=&\frac{\kappa}{2}Y(z)\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-C_{4},\end{split}

where we have used (
4.1
) and (
4.17
). By choosing
z=(r_{*},0)
and using Lemma
4.4
, we get (
4.16
). The proof is thus finished.
∎

Based on the lower bound of
E_{\varepsilon}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})
in Lemma
4.4
, we are able to refine the lower bound of Lagrange multiplier
\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
in Lemma
4.2
.

Lemma 4.5
.

There holds for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small

\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\geq\frac{2}{\kappa}E_{\varepsilon}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})+\frac{\alpha}{2\kappa}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{D}|x|^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x-C,

where
C
is a constant independent of
\varepsilon
and
\Lambda
. As a consequence, it holds

\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\geq Y((r_{*},0))\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\frac{\alpha}{2\kappa}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{D}|x|^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x-C.

Proof.

It follows from the definition of
E_{\varepsilon}
and (
4.13
) that

\displaystyle\quad 2E_{\varepsilon}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})=\int_{D}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x-\alpha\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{D}|x|^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x-2\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{D}J_{\varepsilon}(r,\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(x))\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\psi_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\alpha}{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{D}|x|^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x-2\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{D}J_{\varepsilon}(r,\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(x))+\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\kappa

\displaystyle=\int_{\{\varepsilon^{-2}(h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon/(h^{2}+r^{2})+b)<\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}<\Lambda\varepsilon^{-2}\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\int_{\{\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=\Lambda\varepsilon^{-2}\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\psi_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x-\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{D}\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(x)-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}^{2}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\ \ \ -\frac{\alpha}{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{D}|x|^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x+\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\kappa

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{D}\left(\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}+b\right)\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\int_{\{\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=\Lambda\varepsilon^{-2}\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\left[\psi_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}\left(\Lambda-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ -\frac{\alpha}{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{D}|x|^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x+\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\kappa,

from which we obtain

\displaystyle\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\kappa=2E_{\varepsilon}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})+\frac{\alpha}{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{D}|x|^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x

(4.18)

\displaystyle\ \ \ -\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{D}\left(\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}+b\right)\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\Lambda}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{D}U_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(x)\mathrm{d}x,

where
U_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(x)=\left[\psi_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(x)-\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}\left(\Lambda-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}\right]_{+}
. Note that due to (
4.13
),
U_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(x)=0
for any
x\in\{\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}<\Lambda\varepsilon^{-2}\}
.

In what follows, we will prove that the last two terms in the right hand side of (
4.18
) are uniformly bounded independent of
\varepsilon
and
\Lambda
.

By direct calculations, we get

\begin{split}\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{D}&\left(\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}+b\right)\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}\mathrm{d}x\\
\leq&\int_{D}\left(\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a}{h^{2}+r^{2}}+b\right)\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}}{a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x\leq C\int_{D}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x=C\kappa.\end{split}

This demonstrates the uniform boundedness of the penultimate term on the right side of (
4.18
).

As for the term
\frac{\Lambda}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{D}U_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(x)\mathrm{d}x
, by (
4.12
) and
\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\geq-\frac{\alpha R^{2}}{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-\frac{(h^{2}+R^{2})^{2}\left(\Lambda-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+R^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+R^{2})}
in Lemma
4.2
, we have

\psi_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(x)-\frac{(h^{2}+R^{2})^{2}\left(\Lambda-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+R^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+R^{2})}\leq 0

for
x\in\partial D.
So
U_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\in H^{1}_{0}(D).

Denote
A_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in\Omega\mid U_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(x)>0\}=\{\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=\Lambda\varepsilon^{-2}\}
. Since
|A_{\varepsilon}|\frac{\Lambda}{\varepsilon^{2}}\leq\int_{D}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x=\kappa
, we obtain

|A_{\varepsilon}|\leq\frac{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}{\Lambda}.

(4.19)

In view of (
4.11
), we infer from the definition of
\psi_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
that

\mathcal{L}_{K}\left[\psi_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(x)-\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}\left(\Lambda-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}\right]=\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\frac{\bar{\alpha}\mathcal{L}_{K}|x|^{2}}{2}-\mathcal{L}_{K}\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}\left(\Lambda-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}.

(4.20)

Multiplying
U_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
to both sides of (
4.20
) and integrating by parts, we get

\begin{split}\int_{D}\nabla U_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\cdot(K(x)\nabla U_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})\mathrm{d}x=\frac{\Lambda}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{D}U_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}\int_{D}(\mathcal{L}_{K}|x|^{2})U_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x\\
-\int_{D}\left(\mathcal{L}_{K}\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}\left(\Lambda-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}\right)U_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

(4.21)

Note that
\sup_{x\in D}|\mathcal{L}_{K}|x|^{2}|\leq c
and
\sup_{x\in D}\left|\mathcal{L}_{K}\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}\left(\Lambda-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}\right|\leq c(\Lambda+1)
for some constant
c
independent of
\varepsilon
and
\Lambda
. Thus we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\Lambda}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{D}U_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}\int_{D}(\mathcal{L}_{K}|x|^{2})U_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x-\int_{D}\left(\mathcal{L}_{K}\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}\left(\Lambda-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}\right)U_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C\Lambda}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{D}U_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C\Lambda}{\varepsilon^{2}}|A_{\varepsilon}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{D}U_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C\Lambda}{\varepsilon^{2}}|A_{\varepsilon}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{A_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla U_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C\Lambda}{\varepsilon^{2}}|A_{\varepsilon}|\left(\int_{A_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla U_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\kappa\left(\int_{D}\nabla U_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\cdot(K(x)\nabla U_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}x,

(4.22)

where we have used (
4.19
), the Sobolev embedding
W^{1,1}_{0}(D)\subset L^{2}(D)
and the positive-definiteness of
K
. Note that the constant
C
in (
4.22
) is independent of
\varepsilon
and
\Lambda
.

Combining (
4.21
) and (
4.22
), we conclude that
\frac{\Lambda}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{D}U_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(x)\mathrm{d}x
is uniformly bounded with respect to
\varepsilon
and
\Lambda
. The desired result clearly follows from (
4.18
) and Lemma
4.4
.
∎

4.4.
Location and diameter of
\text{supp}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})

Let
\Lambda>\max\{\alpha a+b,1+\pi R^{2}/\kappa\}
be a fixed number. Now, we estimate the diameter and location of the support set of
\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
.
To this end, we denote
r_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
and
R_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
as

r_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=\inf\{|x|\mid x\in\text{supp}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})\},\ \ \text{and}\ \ R_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=\sup\{|x|\mid x\in\text{supp}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})\}.

(4.23)

So
r_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
and
R_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
describe the lower bound and upper bound of the distance between the origin and
\text{supp}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})
, respectively.
We will prove that, the support set of the maximizers
\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
constructed in Lemma
4.2

must concentrate near some points in
\partial B_{r_{*}}(0)
as
\varepsilon
tends to 0. The steps of proof are as follows: estimates of
\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}r_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
, estimates of
\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}R_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
and estimates of diameter and limiting location of
\text{supp}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})
.

Using the expansion of Green’s function
G_{K}(x,y)
in Lemma
4.1
and the theory of rearrangement function, we have

Lemma 4.6
.

It holds that
\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}r_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=r_{*}
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in(0,1)
be a fixed number. By Lemma
4.2
, for any
x\in{supp}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})
, we have

\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(x)-\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\geq\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}.

Note that

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(x)&=\int_{D}G_{K}(x,y)\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(y)\mathrm{d}y\\
&=\left(\int_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|>\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}+\int_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\right)G_{K}(x,y)\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(y)\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

On the one hand, it follows from Lemma
4.1
that
\max_{x,y\in D}H_{0}(x,y)\leq C
, which implies that
\int_{D}H_{0}(x,y)\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(y)\mathrm{d}y\leq C
. Direct computation yields that

\sqrt{\det K(y)}^{-1}\leq\sqrt{\det K((R,0))}^{-1}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall\ y\in D,

\bigg|\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}(y-x)\bigg|\geq C\varepsilon^{\gamma}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall\ |T_{x}(y-x)|>\varepsilon^{\gamma}.

Thus by the decomposition of
G_{K}
in Lemma
4.1
, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|>\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}G_{K}(x,y)\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(y)\mathrm{d}y\\
&\ \ \leq\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|>\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}+\sqrt{\det K(y)}^{-1}}{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}\left(x-y\right)\right)\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(y)\mathrm{d}y+C\\
&\ \ \leq\frac{\sqrt{\det K((R,0))}^{-1}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|>\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\gamma}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(y)\mathrm{d}y+C\\
&\ \ \leq\frac{\sqrt{\det K((R,0))}^{-1}\kappa\gamma}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+C,\end{split}

(4.24)

where
C
is a constant independent of
\varepsilon
and
\Lambda
.

On the other hand, by the smoothness and positive-definiteness of
K
, it is not hard to get that

\sqrt{\det K(y)}^{-1}\leq\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}+C\varepsilon^{\gamma},\ \ \ \ \forall\ y\in D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\},

\Gamma\left(\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}(x-y)\right)=\Gamma\left(T_{x}(x-y)\right)+O(\varepsilon^{\gamma}),\ \ \ \ \forall\ y\in D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\},y\neq x.

Hence we get

\begin{split}&\int_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}G_{K}(x,y)\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(y)\mathrm{d}y\\
&\ \ \leq\left(\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}+C\varepsilon^{\gamma}\right)\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\Gamma\left(\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}\left(x-y\right)\right)\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(y)\mathrm{d}y+C\\
&\ \ \leq\left(\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}+C\varepsilon^{\gamma}\right)\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\Gamma\left(T_{x}\left(x-y\right)\right)\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(y)\mathrm{d}y+C\\
&=\ \ \left(\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}+C\varepsilon^{\gamma}\right)\int_{|z|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}}\Gamma(z)\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(T_{x}^{-1}z+x)\sqrt{\det K(x)}dz+C.\end{split}

Denote
\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}^{*}(z)=\frac{\Lambda}{\varepsilon^{2}}\textbf{1}_{B_{t_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}}(0)}
the non-negative decreasing rearrangement function of
\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(T_{x}^{-1}z+x)\textbf{1}_{\{|z|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}
. Here we choose
t_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}>0
such that

\int\limits_{|z|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(T_{x}^{-1}z+x)dz=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}^{*}dz=\frac{\pi\Lambda t_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}.

By the rearrangement inequality and the fact
\int\limits_{|z|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(T_{x}^{-1}z+x)dz\leq\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}\kappa
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{|z|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}}\Gamma(z)\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(T_{x}^{-1}z+x)dz\leq&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Gamma(z)\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}^{*}(z)dz\\
\leq&\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{|z|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(T_{x}^{-1}z+x)dz+C(1+\ln\Lambda),\end{split}

from which we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}G_{K}(x,y)\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(y)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\ \ \leq\left(\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}+C\varepsilon^{\gamma}\right)\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\limits_{|z|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(T_{x}^{-1}z+x)\sqrt{\det K(x)}dz+C(1+\ln\Lambda)

\displaystyle\ \ \leq\left(\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}+C\varepsilon^{\gamma}\right)\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}y+C(1+\ln\Lambda)

\displaystyle\ \ \leq\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}y+C(1+\ln\Lambda),

(4.25)

where
C
is a constant independent of
\varepsilon
,
\gamma
and
\Lambda
.
Therefore by Lemma
4.5
, (
4.24
) and (
4.25
), we conclude that for any
x\in supp(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
, there holds

\begin{split}&\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}y+\frac{\sqrt{\det K((R,0))}^{-1}\kappa\gamma}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\\
&\ \ \geq Y((r_{*},0))\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\frac{\alpha}{2\kappa}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{D}|x|^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x-C(1+\ln\Lambda).\end{split}

(4.26)

Dividing both sides of the above inequality by
\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
, we obtain

\displaystyle Y(x)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}y-\alpha|x|^{2}

(4.27)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle Y((r_{*},0))+\frac{\alpha}{2\kappa}\left\{\int_{D}|x|^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x-\kappa|x|^{2}\right\}-\frac{\sqrt{\det K((R,0))}^{-1}\kappa\gamma}{2\pi}-\frac{C(1+\ln\Lambda)}{\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}.

Note that the point
x\in{supp}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})
is arbitrary. So we may take
x=x_{\varepsilon}
be a point such that

|x_{\varepsilon}|\leq r_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}+\varepsilon.

The definition of
r_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
yields

\int_{D}|x_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x\geq\kappa(r_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})^{2}.

Then letting
\varepsilon
tend to
0^{+}
, we deduce from
(
4.27
)
that

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}Y(x_{\varepsilon})\geq Y((r_{*},0))-\sqrt{\det K((R,0))}^{-1}d\gamma/(2\pi).

(4.28)

Hence we get the desired result by letting
\gamma\to 0
and using Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Next, we estimate the moment of inertia
\mathcal{I}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}|x|^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x
. We have

Lemma 4.7
.

There holds

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{I}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})=\frac{1}{2}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{D}|x|^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{\kappa}{2}r_{*}^{2}.

(4.29)

As a consequence, for any
\eta>0
, it holds

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\{x\in D\mid|x|\geq r_{*}+\eta\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x=0.

(4.30)

Proof.

Let
x=x_{\varepsilon}\in D
in
(
4.27
)
, we know that for any
\gamma\in(0,1)

0\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\left[\int_{D}|x|^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x-\kappa(x_{\varepsilon})^{2}\right]\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\left[\int_{D}|x|^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x-\kappa(x_{\varepsilon})^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\sqrt{\det K((R,0))}^{-1}\kappa^{2}\gamma}{\alpha\pi}.

Combining this with Lemma
4.6
, we find

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{D}|x|^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\kappa(x_{\varepsilon})^{2}=\kappa r_{*}^{2}.

Then (
4.30
) follows immediately from
(
4.29
)
and Lemma
4.6
. The proof is hence complete.
∎

Lemma
4.7
implies that the moment of inertia of
\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
will tend to
\kappa r_{*}^{2}/2
and for any
\eta>0
the mass of
\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
on the set
\{x\in D\mid|x|\geq r_{*}+\eta\}
tends to 0 as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. Based on this, we can then get limits of
R_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}.

Lemma 4.8
.

There holds

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}R_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=r_{*}
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}R_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\geq\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}r_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=r_{*}
. Note that for any
x\in D

0<\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}\leq\sqrt{\det K((R,0))}^{-1}<+\infty.

Let
\hat{X}_{\varepsilon}
be a point in
supp(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})
such that

|\hat{X}_{\varepsilon}|\geq R_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}-\varepsilon.

Taking
x=\hat{X}_{\varepsilon}
in (
4.27
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\frac{\sqrt{\det K((R,0))}^{-1}}{2\pi}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{\hat{X}_{\varepsilon}}(y-\hat{X}_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}y\\
&\ \ \geq Y((r_{*},0))+\frac{\alpha}{2}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}(R_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})^{2}+\frac{\alpha r_{*}^{2}}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{\det K((R,0))}^{-1}\kappa\gamma}{2\pi}\\
&\ \ \geq\frac{\kappa\sqrt{\det K((r_{*},0))}^{-1}}{2\pi}-\frac{\sqrt{\det K((R,0))}^{-1}\kappa\gamma}{2\pi}.\end{split}

Hence, we get

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{\hat{X}_{\varepsilon}}(y-\hat{X}_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}y\geq\frac{\kappa\sqrt{\det K((r_{*},0))}^{-1}}{\sqrt{\det K((R,0))}^{-1}}-\kappa\gamma.

So by choose
\gamma
small such that
0<\gamma<\frac{\sqrt{\det K((r_{*},0))}^{-1}}{\sqrt{\det K((R,0))}^{-1}}
, we arrive at

\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{\hat{X}_{\varepsilon}}(y-\hat{X}_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}y\geq C_{0}>0,

which combined with (
4.30
) immediately yields the conclusion of this lemma.

∎

From Lemmas
4.6
and
4.8
, we know that
supp(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})
locates in a narrow annular domain whose centerline is
\partial B_{r_{*}}(0)
. That is, for any
x_{\varepsilon}\in supp(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}|x_{\varepsilon}|=r_{*}.

Based on this, we can further refine the estimates of
supp(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})
. We can prove that, for any
\varepsilon>0
small the diameter of the support set of
\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
is the order of
\varepsilon^{\gamma}
for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,1)
be a fixed number. Then there exists an
\varepsilon_{\Lambda}\in(0,1)
depending on
\Lambda
such that

diam\big({supp}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})\big)\leq 2\varepsilon^{\gamma},\ \ \ \forall\,\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{\Lambda}).

Proof.

Since
\int_{D}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}dx=\kappa,
it suffices to prove that

\int\limits_{B_{\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x)}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}dx>\kappa/2,\ \ \forall\,x\in{supp}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}).

(4.31)

Indeed, for any
x\in{supp}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})
, we infer from
(
4.27
)
that

\begin{split}\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}y\geq&2\pi\sqrt{\det K(x)}\bigg[Y((r_{*},0))+\frac{\alpha}{2\kappa}\left\{\int_{D}|x|^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}dx+\kappa|x|^{2}\right\}\\
&-\frac{\sqrt{\det K((R,0))}^{-1}\kappa\gamma}{2\pi}-\frac{C(1+\ln\Lambda)}{\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\bigg].\end{split}

(4.32)

From Lemmas
4.6
and
4.8
, we know that
|x|\to r_{*}
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. Taking this into (
4.32
), we obtain

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int\limits_{\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}y\geq\kappa-\frac{\sqrt{\det K((r_{*},0))}\kappa\gamma}{\sqrt{\det K((R,0))}}.

(4.33)

From the definition of
T_{x}
, one computes directly that

\{y\mid T_{x}(y-x)|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}\subset B_{\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x).

Thus we get

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int\limits_{B_{\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x)}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}y\geq\kappa-\frac{\sqrt{\det K((r_{*},0))}\kappa\gamma}{\sqrt{\det K((R,0))}},

which implies (
4.31
) by taking
\gamma=\gamma_{0}:=\min\left\{1,\frac{\sqrt{\det K((R,0))}}{4\sqrt{\det K((r_{*},0))}}\right\}>0
. Thus we conclude that
diam\big({supp}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})\big)\leq 2\varepsilon^{\gamma_{0}}
, from which we deduce that

diam\big({supp}(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})\big)\leq\frac{C}{\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}

(4.34)

provided
\varepsilon
is small enough.

Now we go back to
(
4.24
)
and improve estimates of
\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x)|>\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}G_{K}(x,y)\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(y)\mathrm{d}y
. From (
4.34
), we get for any
\gamma\in(0,1)

\begin{split}&\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|>\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}G_{K}(x,y)\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(y)\mathrm{d}y\\
&\ \ \leq\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|>\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}+\sqrt{\det K(y)}^{-1}}{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}\left(x-y\right)\right)\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(y)\mathrm{d}y+C\\
&\ \ \leq\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}\gamma}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|>\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}y+C.\end{split}

Repeating the proof of (
4.26
), we obtain a refined estimate of
(
4.26
)
as follows

\begin{split}&\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}y+\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}\gamma}{2\pi}\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|>\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}y\\
&\ \ \geq Y((r_{*},0))+\frac{\alpha}{2\kappa}\left\{\int_{D}|x|^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}dx+\kappa|x|^{2}\right\}-\frac{C(1+\ln\Lambda)}{\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}},\end{split}

which implies that

\begin{split}&(1-\gamma)\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}y\\
&\ \ \geq 2\pi\sqrt{\det K(x)}\left[Y((r_{*},0))+\frac{\alpha}{2\kappa}\left\{\int_{D}|x|^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}dx+\kappa|x|^{2}\right\}-\frac{C(1+\ln\Lambda)}{\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\right]-\kappa\gamma.\end{split}

(4.35)

Taking the limit inferior to (
4.35
), we get

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}y\geq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\left[2\pi\sqrt{\det K((r_{*},0))}(Y((r_{*},0))+\alpha r_{*}^{2})-\kappa\gamma\right]=\kappa,

which implies (
4.31
) for all
\gamma\in(0,1)
. The proof is thus complete.
∎

4.5.
Vanishment of the vortex patch term in profile (
4.11
)

Having made the above preparations, we are able to show that the “bad” term of
\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
, i.e., the last patch term in (
4.11
), indeed vanishes when choosing the parameter
\Lambda
properly large. This, together with (
4.11
), will guarantee that the maximizers
\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
in Lemma
4.2
is a solution of (
3.10
) with proper profile functions
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
. The key observation is the following estimates of upper bounds of
\psi_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}
.

Lemma 4.10
.

For any
\Lambda>\max\{\alpha a+b,1+\pi R^{2}/\kappa\}
, let
\varepsilon_{\Lambda}
be the small positive constant in Lemma
4.9
. Then there exists an
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\Lambda}\in(0,\varepsilon_{\Lambda})
such that for any
0<\varepsilon<\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\Lambda}
,

||(\psi_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})_{+}||_{L^{\infty}(D)}\leq C(\ln\Lambda+1),

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
\varepsilon
and
\Lambda
.

Proof.

If
x\notin supp\,(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})
, then
(\psi_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})_{+}=0.
For any
x\in supp\,(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})
, by Lemma
4.9
we find

supp\,(\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})\subset B_{2\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x).

Using similar calculations as the proof of (
4.25
), we are able to show

\begin{split}&\ \ \ \int_{D}G_{K}(x,y)\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(y)\mathrm{d}y\\
&\leq\left(\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}+C\varepsilon^{\gamma}\right)\int\limits_{B_{2\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x)}\Gamma\left(\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}\left(x-y\right)\right)\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(y)\mathrm{d}y+C\\
&\leq\left(\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}+C\varepsilon^{\gamma}\right)\int\limits_{B_{2\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x)}\Gamma\left(T_{x}\left(x-y\right)\right)\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(y)\mathrm{d}y+C\\
&=\left(\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}+C\varepsilon^{\gamma}\right)\int_{|T_{x}^{-1}z|\leq 2\varepsilon^{\gamma}}\Gamma(z)\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(T_{x}^{-1}z+x)\sqrt{\det K(x)}dz+C\\
&\leq\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}\kappa}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+C(1+\ln\Lambda),\end{split}

where
C
is a constant independent of
\varepsilon
and
\Lambda
. Thus using the above estimate and Lemmas
4.3
and
4.5
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\ \ \ \psi_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(x)=\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}(x)-\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}-\mu_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\\
&\leq\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}\kappa}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\log{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}-Y((r_{*},0))\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-\frac{\alpha}{2\kappa}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{D}|y|^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}\mathrm{d}y+C(1+\ln\Lambda)\\
&\leq\left(Y(x)-Y((r_{*},0))\right)\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+C(1+\ln\Lambda)\\
&\leq C(1+\ln\Lambda),\end{split}

where the positive number
C
does not depend on
\varepsilon
and
\Lambda
. The proof is thus completed.
∎

Based on Lemma
4.10
, we can finally get that the last term in (
4.11
) indeed vanishes when choosing
\Lambda
large.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\Lambda}
be the small positive constant in Lemma
4.10
. Let
r=|x|
. Then there exists a constant
\Lambda_{0}
sufficiently large independent of
\varepsilon
such that for any
\Lambda\geq\Lambda_{0}
and
0<\varepsilon<\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\Lambda},

\left|\left\{\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=\frac{\Lambda}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\}\right|=0.

As a result, if we choose
\Lambda\geq\Lambda_{0}
and
0<\varepsilon<\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\Lambda}
, then one has

\begin{split}\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\frac{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}}(\psi_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})_{+}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}+b\right)\textbf{1}_{\{\psi_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}>0\}}\ \ a.e.\ \text{in}\ D.\end{split}

(4.36)

Proof.

In view of (
4.13
), we find

(\psi_{\varepsilon,\Lambda})_{+}\geq\frac{(h^{2}+r^{2})^{2}\left(\Lambda-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+r^{2}}-b\right)_{+}}{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}\ \ \ \ \text{on}\ \ \left\{\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=\frac{\Lambda}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\},

from which we conclude that

(\psi_{{}_{\beta,\Lambda}})_{+}\geq C_{1}\Lambda-C_{2}\ \ \ \ \text{on}\ \ \left\{\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=\frac{\Lambda}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\},

(4.37)

for some positive numbers
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
independent of
\varepsilon
and
\Lambda
.
Combining (
4.37
) with Lemma
4.10
, we deduce that on the set
\left\{\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=\frac{\Lambda}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\}

C\ln\Lambda\geq C_{1}\Lambda-C_{3},

which is impossible when
\Lambda
is chosen to be sufficiently large, i.e.,
\Lambda\geq\Lambda_{0}
for a constant
\Lambda_{0}
independent of
\varepsilon
. Hence, we have

\left|\left\{\zeta_{\varepsilon,\Lambda}=\frac{\Lambda}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\}\right|=0,\ \ \ \forall\ \ 0<\varepsilon<\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\Lambda},\ \ \Lambda\geq\Lambda_{0}.

(4.38)

(
4.36
) follows immediately from (
4.38
) and Lemma
4.2
.
The proof is therefore completed.
∎

4.6.
Refined asymptotic estimates of
\zeta_{\varepsilon}

We can further improve results of Lemma
4.9
that the diameter of the support set of
\zeta_{\varepsilon}
is the order of
\varepsilon
and analyze the properties of limiting function of
\zeta_{\varepsilon}
. As a result, we can deduce that the support set
\text{supp}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})
tends asymptotically to an ellipse.

Lemma 4.12
.

There exist
r_{1},r_{2}>0
independent of
\varepsilon
such that for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small,

r_{1}\varepsilon\leq diam(\text{supp}(\zeta_{\varepsilon}))\leq r_{2}\varepsilon.

Proof.

Since
0\leq\zeta_{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{\Lambda_{0}}{\varepsilon^{2}}
, we get

\kappa=\int_{D}\zeta_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{\pi\Lambda_{0}}{\varepsilon^{2}}(diam(\text{supp}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})))^{2},

which shows
diam({supp}(\zeta_{\varepsilon}))\geq r_{1}\varepsilon
for some
r_{1}>0
. It suffices to prove
diam({supp}(\zeta_{\varepsilon}))\leq r_{2}\varepsilon
for some
r_{2}>0
.

We observe that for any
x\in{supp}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})
,

\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x)-\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\geq\mu_{\varepsilon}.

(4.39)

According to Lemma
4.9
, there holds

supp(\zeta_{\varepsilon})\subseteq B_{2\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}(x).

(4.40)

Let
R_{*}>1
to be determined later. On the one hand, we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x)&=\left(\int_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|>R_{*}\varepsilon\}}+\int_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq R_{*}\varepsilon\}}\right)G_{K}(x,y)\zeta_{\varepsilon}(y)\mathrm{d}y\\
&=:B_{1}+B_{2}.\end{split}

By (
4.40
), we get

\begin{split}B_{1}&=\int_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|>R_{*}\varepsilon\}}G_{K}(x,y)\zeta_{\varepsilon}(y)\mathrm{d}y\\
&\leq\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}+O\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)}{2\pi}\ln{\frac{1}{R_{*}\varepsilon}}\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|>R_{*}\varepsilon\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}y+C\\
&\leq\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}}{2\pi}\ln{\frac{1}{R_{*}\varepsilon}}\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|>R_{*}\varepsilon\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}y+C,\end{split}

(4.41)

and

\begin{split}B_{2}&=\int_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq R_{*}\varepsilon\}}G_{K}(x,y)\zeta_{\varepsilon}(y)\mathrm{d}y\\
&\leq\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}+O\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)}{2\pi}\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq R_{*}\varepsilon\}}\left(\ln\frac{1}{|T_{x}(y-x)|}+O\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right)\zeta_{\varepsilon}(y)\mathrm{d}y+C\\
&\leq\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}+O\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)}{2\pi}\int\limits_{|z|\leq R_{*}\varepsilon}\left(\ln\frac{1}{|z|}+O\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right)\zeta_{\varepsilon}\left(T_{x}^{-1}z+x\right)\sqrt{\det K(x)}dz+C\\
&\leq\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}+O\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\limits_{|z|\leq R_{*}\varepsilon}\zeta_{\varepsilon}\left(T_{x}^{-1}z+x\right)\sqrt{\det K(x)}dz+C\\
&\leq\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\limits_{D\cap\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq R_{*}\varepsilon\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}y+C.\end{split}

(4.42)

Taking (
4.41
) and (
4.42
) into (
4.39
), we get

\begin{split}&\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}}{2\pi}\ln{\frac{1}{R_{*}\varepsilon}}\int\limits_{\{|T_{x}(y-x)|>R_{*}\varepsilon\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}y+\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\limits_{\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq R_{*}\varepsilon\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}y\\
&\ \ \geq\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}+\mu_{\varepsilon}-C.\end{split}

(4.43)

On the other hand, by Lemma
4.5
, one has

\mu_{\varepsilon}\geq\left(\frac{\kappa}{2\pi\sqrt{\det K(x)}}-\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\right)\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}-C.

(4.44)

Combining
(
4.43
)
and
(
4.44
)
, we obtain

\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln{\frac{1}{R_{*}\varepsilon}}\int\limits_{\{|T_{x}(y-x)|>R_{*}\varepsilon\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}y+\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\limits_{\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq R_{*}\varepsilon\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}y+C,

which implies that

\int\limits_{\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq R_{*}\varepsilon\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}y\geq\kappa\left(1-\frac{C}{\ln R_{*}}\right).

Taking
R_{*}>1
such that
C(\ln R_{*})^{-1}<1/2
, we obtain

\int\limits_{D\cap{B_{R_{*}\varepsilon}(x)}}\zeta_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}y\geq\int\limits_{\{|T_{x}(y-x)|\leq R_{*}\varepsilon\}}\zeta_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}y>\frac{\kappa}{2}.

Taking
r_{2}=2R_{*}
, we complete the proof of Lemma
4.12
. ∎

Lemma
4.10
implies that diameter of the support set of
\zeta_{\varepsilon}
is the order of
\varepsilon
. By using the scaling method and theory of rearrangement function, we can further analyse the asymptotic properties of the scaled function of
\zeta_{\varepsilon}
.

We define the center of
\zeta_{\varepsilon}

X_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\kappa}\int_{D}x\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x)dx.

(4.45)

It follows from Lemmas
4.6
,
4.8
and
4.9
that
X_{\varepsilon}\to(r_{*},0)
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. Let
g_{\varepsilon}
be the scaled function of
\zeta_{\varepsilon}
defined by

g_{\varepsilon}(x)={\varepsilon^{2}}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}+T^{-1}_{X_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon x),\ \ \ \ \ x\in D_{\varepsilon},

(4.46)

where
T_{X_{\varepsilon}}
is the positive-definite matrix such that (
4.1
) holds, i.e.,

T_{X_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}\left(T_{X_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}\right)^{t}=K\left(X_{\varepsilon}\right),

and
D_{\varepsilon}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid X_{\varepsilon}+T^{-1}_{X_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon x\in D\right\}
. By Lemmas
4.2
,
4.11
and
4.12
,
\int_{D}g_{\varepsilon}(x)dx=\sqrt{\det K(X_{\varepsilon})}^{-1}\kappa
,

and
||g_{\varepsilon}||_{L^{\infty}}\leq\Lambda_{0}
for some constants
R_{1},\Lambda_{0}>0
. Then up to a subsequence, there must be a function
g\in L^{\infty}(B_{R_{1}}(0))
such that as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}

g_{\varepsilon}\to g\ \ \text{ weakly ~in}\ \ L^{2}(B_{R_{1}}(0)).

The following lemma gives the asymptotic behavior of
g_{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Lemma 4.13
.

g
is a radially symmetric nonincreasing function, i.e.,
g(x)=g(|x|)
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Proof.

We denote
g^{\bigtriangleup}_{\varepsilon}
the radially symmetric nonincreasing Lebesgue-rearrangement of
g_{\varepsilon}
centered on 0, i.e.,
g^{\bigtriangleup}_{\varepsilon}
is radially symmetric nonincreasing and for any
\mu>0

\left|\left\{g^{\bigtriangleup}_{\varepsilon}(x)>\mu\right\}\right|=\left|\left\{g_{\varepsilon}(x)>\mu\right\}\right|.

(4.47)

Then
\int_{D}g^{\bigtriangleup}_{\varepsilon}(x)dx=\int_{D}g_{\varepsilon}(x)dx=\sqrt{\det K(X_{\varepsilon})}^{-1}\kappa
,
||g^{\bigtriangleup}_{\varepsilon}||_{L^{\infty}}\leq\Lambda_{0}
and there must be a function
h\in L^{\infty}(B_{R_{1}}(0))
such that as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}

g^{\bigtriangleup}_{\varepsilon}\to h\ \ \text{ weakly ~in}\ \ L^{2}(B_{R_{1}}(0)).

Moreover,
h
is a radially symmetric nonincreasing function.

One the one hand, by virtue of Riesz’ rearrangement inequality, we have

\iint\limits_{B_{R_{1}}(0)\times B_{R_{1}}(0)}\ln\frac{1}{|x-x^{\prime}|}g_{\varepsilon}(x)g_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})dxdx^{\prime}\leq\iint\limits_{B_{R_{1}}(0)\times B_{R_{1}}(0)}\ln\frac{1}{|x-x^{\prime}|}g^{\bigtriangleup}_{\varepsilon}(x)g^{\bigtriangleup}_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})dxdx^{\prime}.

Hence

\iint\limits_{B_{R_{1}}(0)\times B_{R_{1}}(0)}\ln\frac{1}{|x-x^{\prime}|}g(x)g(x^{\prime})dxdx^{\prime}\leq\iint\limits_{B_{R_{1}}(0)\times B_{R_{1}}(0)}\ln\frac{1}{|x-x^{\prime}|}h(x)h(x^{\prime})dxdx^{\prime}.

(4.48)

On the other hand, we define test functions
\tilde{\zeta}_{\varepsilon}

\tilde{\zeta}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\varepsilon^{-2}g^{\bigtriangleup}_{\varepsilon}\left(\varepsilon^{-1}T_{X_{\varepsilon}}(x-X_{\varepsilon})\right)&\text{if}&x\in B_{R_{1}\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}),\\
0&\text{if}&x\in D\backslash B_{R_{1}\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}).\end{array}\right.

Then
\int_{D}\tilde{\zeta}_{\varepsilon}(x)dx=\kappa
and thus
\tilde{\zeta}_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}
. This implies
E_{\varepsilon}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})\geq E_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{\zeta}_{\varepsilon})
.

From (
4.7
), (
4.8
) and Lemma
4.1
, we have

\begin{split}E_{\varepsilon}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})=&\frac{1}{4\pi}\iint\limits_{B_{R_{1}\varepsilon}\times B_{R_{1}\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}+\sqrt{\det K(y)}^{-1}}{2}\ln\frac{1}{\left|\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}\left(x-y\right)\right|}+H_{0}(x,y)\right)\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x)\zeta_{\varepsilon}(y)dxdy\\
&-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}\int_{D}|x|^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x)dx-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{D}J(|x|,\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x))dx.\end{split}

(4.49)

Using transformation of coordinates
x=X_{\varepsilon}+T^{-1}_{X_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon x^{\prime},y=X_{\varepsilon}+T^{-1}_{X_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon y^{\prime}
and Lemma
4.12
, one computes directly that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{4\pi}&\iint\limits_{B_{R_{1}\varepsilon}\times B_{R_{1}\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}+\sqrt{\det K(y)}^{-1}}{2}\ln\frac{1}{\left|\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}\left(x-y\right)\right|}+H_{0}(x,y)\right)\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x)\zeta_{\varepsilon}(y)dxdy\\
=&\frac{\left(\det T_{X_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}\right)^{2}}{4\pi}\iint\limits_{B_{R_{1}}(0)\times B_{R_{1}}(0)}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}+\sqrt{\det K(y)}^{-1}}{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon\left|\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}T_{X_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}\left(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\right)\right|}+H_{0}(x,y)\right)\\
&\cdot g_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})g_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime})dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}\\
=&\frac{\sqrt{\det K(X_{\varepsilon})}}{4\pi}\iint\limits_{B_{R_{1}}(0)\times B_{R_{1}}(0)}\left(\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon\left|\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}T_{X_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}\left(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\right)\right|}+H_{0}(X_{\varepsilon},X_{\varepsilon})\right)g_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})g_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime})dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}+o(1)\\
=&\frac{\sqrt{\det K(X_{\varepsilon})}}{4\pi}\iint\limits_{B_{R_{1}}(0)\times B_{R_{1}}(0)}\ln\frac{1}{\left|\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}T_{X_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}\left(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\right)\right|}g_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})g_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime})dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}\\
&+\frac{\sqrt{\det K(X_{\varepsilon})}}{4\pi}\left(\int_{B_{R_{1}}(0)}g_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}\right)^{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\frac{\sqrt{\det K(X_{\varepsilon})}H_{0}(X_{\varepsilon},X_{\varepsilon})}{4\pi}\left(\int_{B_{R_{1}}(0)}g_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}\right)^{2}+o(1),\end{split}

\begin{split}-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}\int_{D}|x|^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x)dx=-\frac{\bar{\alpha}\kappa}{2}|X_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+O\left(\varepsilon\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right),\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{D}J(|x|,\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x))dx=&\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{D}\frac{(h^{2}+|x|^{2})^{2}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x)-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+|x|^{2}}-b\right)_{+}^{2}}{4h^{2}a\varepsilon+2a^{2}(h^{2}+|x|^{2})}dx\\
=&\det T_{X_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}\cdot\int_{B_{R_{1}}(0)}\frac{(h^{2}+|X_{\varepsilon}|^{2})^{2}\left(g_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})-\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+|X_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}-b\right)_{+}^{2}}{4h^{2}a\varepsilon+2a^{2}(h^{2}+|X_{\varepsilon}|^{2})}dx^{\prime}+O(\varepsilon)\\
=&\frac{\det T_{X_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}\cdot(h^{2}+|X_{\varepsilon}|^{2})^{2}}{4h^{2}a\varepsilon+2a^{2}(h^{2}+|X_{\varepsilon}|^{2})}\int_{B_{R_{1}}(0)}\left(g_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})-b\right)_{+}^{2}dx^{\prime}+O\left(\varepsilon\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right).\end{split}

Taking these into (
4.49
), we get

\begin{split}E_{\varepsilon}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})=&\frac{\sqrt{\det K(X_{\varepsilon})}}{4\pi}\iint\limits_{B_{R_{1}}(0)\times B_{R_{1}}(0)}\ln\frac{1}{\left|\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}T_{X_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}\left(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\right)\right|}g_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})g_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime})dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}\\
&+\frac{\sqrt{\det K(X_{\varepsilon})}}{4\pi}\left(\int_{B_{R_{1}}(0)}g_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}\right)^{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\frac{\sqrt{\det K(X_{\varepsilon})}H_{0}(X_{\varepsilon},X_{\varepsilon})}{4\pi}\left(\int_{B_{R_{1}}(0)}g_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}\right)^{2}\\
&-\frac{\bar{\alpha}\kappa}{2}|X_{\varepsilon}|^{2}-\frac{\det T_{X_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}\cdot(h^{2}+|X_{\varepsilon}|^{2})^{2}}{4h^{2}a\varepsilon+2a^{2}(h^{2}+|X_{\varepsilon}|^{2})}\int_{B_{R_{1}}(0)}\left(g_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})-b\right)_{+}^{2}dx^{\prime}+o(1).\end{split}

Similarly, we have

\begin{split}E_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{\zeta}_{\varepsilon})=&\frac{\sqrt{\det K(X_{\varepsilon})}}{4\pi}\iint\limits_{B_{R_{1}}(0)\times B_{R_{1}}(0)}\ln\frac{1}{\left|\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}T_{X_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}\left(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\right)\right|}g^{\bigtriangleup}_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})g^{\bigtriangleup}_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime})dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}\\
&+\frac{\sqrt{\det K(X_{\varepsilon})}}{4\pi}\left(\int_{B_{R_{1}}(0)}g^{\bigtriangleup}_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}\right)^{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\frac{\sqrt{\det K(X_{\varepsilon})}H_{0}(X_{\varepsilon},X_{\varepsilon})}{4\pi}\left(\int_{B_{R_{1}}(0)}g^{\bigtriangleup}_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}\right)^{2}\\
&-\frac{\bar{\alpha}\kappa}{2}|X_{\varepsilon}|^{2}-\frac{\det T_{X_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}\cdot(h^{2}+|X_{\varepsilon}|^{2})^{2}}{4h^{2}a\varepsilon+2a^{2}(h^{2}+|X_{\varepsilon}|^{2})}\int_{B_{R_{1}}(0)}\left(g^{\bigtriangleup}_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})-b\right)_{+}^{2}dx^{\prime}+o(1).\end{split}

Using (
4.47
) and
E_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{\zeta}_{\varepsilon})\leq E_{\varepsilon}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\iint\limits_{B_{R_{1}}(0)\times B_{R_{1}}(0)}\ln\frac{1}{\left|\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}T_{X_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}\left(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\right)\right|}g^{\bigtriangleup}_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})g^{\bigtriangleup}_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime})dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}\\
\leq&\iint\limits_{B_{R_{1}}(0)\times B_{R_{1}}(0)}\ln\frac{1}{\left|\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}T_{X_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}\left(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\right)\right|}g_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})g_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime})dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}+o(1),\end{split}

where
x=X_{\varepsilon}+T^{-1}_{X_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon x^{\prime},y=X_{\varepsilon}+T^{-1}_{X_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon y^{\prime}
. Passing
\varepsilon
to 0, we conclude that

\iint\limits_{B_{R_{1}}(0)\times B_{R_{1}}(0)}\ln\frac{1}{|x-x^{\prime}|}h(x)h(x^{\prime})dxdx^{\prime}\leq\iint\limits_{B_{R_{1}}(0)\times B_{R_{1}}(0)}\ln\frac{1}{|x-x^{\prime}|}g(x)g(x^{\prime})dxdx^{\prime}.

which together with
(
4.48
)
yield to

\iint\limits_{B_{R_{1}}(0)\times B_{R_{1}}(0)}\ln\frac{1}{|x-x^{\prime}|}g(x)g(x^{\prime})dxdx^{\prime}=\iint\limits_{B_{R_{1}}(0)\times B_{R_{1}}(0)}\ln\frac{1}{|x-x^{\prime}|}h(x)h(x^{\prime})dxdx^{\prime}.

By Lemma 3.2 of
[
6
]
, we know that there exists a translation
\mathcal{T}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\mathcal{T}g=h
. Note also that

\int_{B_{R_{1}}(0)}xg(x)dx=\int_{B_{R_{1}}(0)}xh(x)dx=0.

Thus
\mathcal{T}
is an identity mapping and
g=h
. The proof is thus complete.
∎

Note that
g_{\varepsilon}\to g
weakly in
L^{2}(B_{R_{1}}(0))
, where
g
is radially symmetric. We define
\tilde{\psi_{\varepsilon}}
the scaled function of the stream function
\psi_{\varepsilon}
by

\tilde{\psi_{\varepsilon}}(y)=\psi_{\varepsilon}\left(X_{\varepsilon}+T_{X_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}\varepsilon y\right),\ \ \ y\in D_{\varepsilon}.

(4.50)

From Lemmas
4.2
,
4.12
and the definition of
g_{\varepsilon}
in (
4.46
), we know that
supp((\tilde{\psi_{\varepsilon}})_{+})=supp(g_{\varepsilon})\subseteq B_{R_{1}}(0).

Moreover, one has

\begin{split}-\nabla_{y}&\cdot\left(T_{X_{\varepsilon}}K\left(X_{\varepsilon}+T_{X_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}\varepsilon y\right)T_{X_{\varepsilon}}^{t}\nabla_{y}\tilde{\psi_{\varepsilon}}(y)\right)\\
=&-\varepsilon^{2}\nabla_{x}\cdot\left(K\left(x\right)\nabla_{x}\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)\right)\left(X_{\varepsilon}+T_{X_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}\varepsilon y\right)\\
=&\varepsilon^{2}\zeta_{\varepsilon}\left(X_{\varepsilon}+T_{X_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}\varepsilon y\right)+\frac{\bar{\alpha}\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\mathcal{L}_{K}|x|^{2}\left(X_{\varepsilon}+T_{X_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}\varepsilon y\right)\\
=&g_{\varepsilon}(y)+\frac{\bar{\alpha}\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\mathcal{L}_{K}|x|^{2}\left(X_{\varepsilon}+T_{X_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}\varepsilon y\right)\to g\ \ \text{ weakly ~in}\ \ L^{2}(B_{R_{1}}(0))\ \ \text{as}\ \varepsilon\to 0.\end{split}

By classical regularity estimates for elliptic equations (see
[
35
]
), there exists a function
\psi^{*}\in C^{1,\gamma}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
\gamma\in(0,1)
such that as
\varepsilon\to 0

\tilde{\psi_{\varepsilon}}\to\psi^{*}\ \ \text{in}\ C^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

(4.51)

Moreover,
supp\left(\left(\psi^{*}\right)_{+}\right)\subseteq B_{R_{1}}(0)
and

-\Delta\psi^{*}=g,\ \ \ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}.

Using the moving plane method, we know that
\left(\psi^{*}\right)_{+}
is radially symmetric in
B_{R_{1}}(0)
.

As a result, we have

Lemma 4.14
.

There holds for
i=1,2

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{D}\partial_{x_{i}}(\psi_{\varepsilon}(x))_{+}dx\to 0\ \ \ \text{as}\ \ \varepsilon\to 0.

Proof.

Using variable substitution and (
4.50
), we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{D}\partial_{x_{i}}(\psi_{\varepsilon}(x))_{+}dx=&\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left(T_{X_{\varepsilon}}\right)_{i,j}\int_{B_{R_{1}}(0)}\partial_{y_{j}}(\tilde{\psi}_{\varepsilon}(y))_{+}\cdot\det T_{X_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}dy.\end{split}

Here
\left(T_{X_{\varepsilon}}\right)_{i,j}
stands for row
i
and column
j
of the matrix
T_{X_{\varepsilon}}
. Since (
4.51
) holds and
\left(\psi^{*}\right)_{+}
is radially symmetric, we get

\int_{B_{R_{1}}(0)}\partial_{y_{j}}(\tilde{\psi}_{\varepsilon}(y))_{+}dy\to\int_{B_{R_{1}}(0)}\partial_{y_{j}}(\psi^{*}(y))_{+}dy=0\ \ \ \text{as}\ \ \varepsilon\to 0.

Combining the above two formulas, we complete the proof.

∎

4.7.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

Having made all the preparation, we are now able to give proof of Theorem
1.4
. Let
h>0,\kappa>0,R>0
be fixed numbers. For any
r_{*}\in(0,R)
, let

\alpha=\frac{\kappa}{4\pi h\sqrt{h^{2}+r_{*}^{2}}}.

We first construct a family of vorticity field
\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}
in terms of
\zeta_{\varepsilon}
. Let
a>0,b\geq 0
be two prescribed constants and
\bar{\alpha}=\alpha\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
.
From Lemmas
4.2
and
4.11
, we know that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
all maximizers of
E_{\varepsilon}
over
\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,\kappa}
are of the form (see (
4.36
))

\begin{split}\zeta_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\frac{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+|x|^{2})}{(h^{2}+|x|^{2})^{2}}(\psi_{\varepsilon})_{+}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+|x|^{2}}+b\right)\textbf{1}_{\{\psi_{\varepsilon}>0\}}\ \ a.e.\ \text{in}\ D,\end{split}

where
\psi_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}-\mu_{\varepsilon}
and
\mu_{\varepsilon}
is a Lagrange multiplier determined by
\zeta_{\varepsilon}
. If we denote two functions

F_{1}(s)=\varepsilon^{-1}a(s-\mu_{\varepsilon})_{+},\ \ \ F_{2}(s)=\varepsilon^{-2}(b+\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon)\textbf{1}_{\{s-\mu_{\varepsilon}>0\}},\ \ \ s\in\mathbb{R},

then
\zeta_{\varepsilon}
has the profile

\begin{split}\zeta_{\varepsilon}=&\frac{2h^{2}F_{1}\left(\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)}{(h^{2}+|x|^{2})^{2}}+\frac{F_{1}\left(\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)F_{1}^{\prime}\left(\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)}{h^{2}+|x|^{2}}\\
&-\frac{\bar{\alpha}|x|^{2}F_{1}^{\prime}\left(\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right)}{h^{2}+|x|^{2}}+F_{2}\left(\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}|x|^{2}\right),\ \ \text{in}\ \ D,\end{split}

which coincides with (
3.10
). We define function pairs
(\varPhi_{\varepsilon},V_{\varepsilon},W_{\varepsilon},w_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon},\varphi_{\varepsilon})
in
D
such that
\varPhi_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{G}_{K}\zeta_{\varepsilon}
,
V_{\varepsilon}
satisfies (
3.3
),
W_{\varepsilon}
satisfies (
3.6
) and
(w_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon},\varphi_{\varepsilon})
satisfies (
3.1
). From the deduction of (
3.10
) in section 3, we know that
(w_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})
is a rotating-invariant solution pair of the system (
1.17
) with the angular velocity
\bar{\alpha}
and the stream function
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
.

By Lemmas
4.6
,
4.8
,
4.9
and
4.12
, the support set
\bar{A}_{\varepsilon}
of the maximizer
\zeta_{\varepsilon}
concentrates near
(r_{*},0)
up to a rotation as
\varepsilon\to 0
with
\int_{D}\zeta_{\varepsilon}dx=\kappa
. Define for any
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in B_{R}(0)\times\mathbb{R},t\in\mathbb{R}

\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},t)=\frac{w_{3}^{\varepsilon}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},t)}{h}\vec{\xi}+\frac{1}{h}\left(\partial_{x_{2}}v_{\vec{\xi}}^{\varepsilon}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},t),-\partial_{x_{1}}v_{\vec{\xi}}^{\varepsilon}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},t),0\right)^{t},

(4.52)

where
w_{3}^{\varepsilon}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},t)
is a helical function satisfying

w_{3}^{\varepsilon}(x_{1},x_{2},0,t)=w_{\varepsilon}(x_{1},x_{2},t)

and
v_{\vec{\xi}}^{\varepsilon}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},t)
is a helical function satisfying

v_{\vec{\xi}}^{\varepsilon}(x_{1},x_{2},0,t)=v_{\varepsilon}(x_{1},x_{2},t).

From Theorem
1.1
, we know that
\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}
is a helical vorticity field of (
1.2
) with non-zero helical swirl
v_{\vec{\xi}}^{\varepsilon}
. Note that the support set of
\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}
is a helical domain whose cross-section is
\bar{A}_{\varepsilon}
. Inside this helical region, the helical swirl of
\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}
is non-zero, while outside the helical swirl is identical to zero. Moreover, the cross-section of
supp(\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon})
tends asymptotically to a rotating point vortex
\bar{R}_{\alpha|\ln\varepsilon|}((r_{*},0))
up to a rotation as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Define
P(\tau)
the intersection point of the curve parameterized by (
1.8
) and the
x_{1}Ox_{2}
plane. Taking
s=\frac{b_{1}\tau}{h}
into (
1.8
), one computes directly that
P(\tau)
satisfies a 2D point vortex model

\begin{split}P(\tau)=\bar{R}_{\alpha^{\prime}\tau}((r_{*},0)),\end{split}

where
\alpha^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{h^{2}+r_{*}^{2}}}\left(a_{1}+\frac{b_{1}}{h}\right)=\frac{\kappa}{4\pi h\sqrt{h^{2}+r_{*}^{2}}}=\alpha
. Thus by our construction, we readily check that the support set of
\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}(x_{1},x_{2},0,|\ln\varepsilon|^{-1}\tau)
defined by (
4.52
) concentrates near
P(\tau)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, which implies that, the support set of
\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}
is a topological traveling-rotating helical tube that does not change form and concentrates near
\gamma(\tau)
(
1.8
) in sense that for all
\tau
,

\text{dist}\left(\partial(supp(\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,|\ln\varepsilon|^{-1}\tau))),\gamma(\tau)\right)\to 0,\ \ \text{as}\ \varepsilon\to 0.

It remains to prove that the vorticity field
\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}
tends asymptotically to (
1.8
) in distributional sense, i.e.,
\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,|\ln\varepsilon|^{-1}\tau)\to\kappa\delta_{\gamma(\tau)}\mathbf{t}_{\gamma(\tau)}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Note that (
4.52
) holds. We will give the proof in two steps.

Step 1.
We calculate
\frac{w_{3}^{\varepsilon}}{h}\vec{\xi}
. By (
2.2
), we get

w_{\varepsilon}=\zeta_{\varepsilon}-\frac{2h^{2}v_{\varepsilon}}{|\vec{\xi}|^{4}}-\frac{x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}v_{\varepsilon}+x_{2}\partial_{x_{2}}v_{\varepsilon}}{|\vec{\xi}|^{2}}.

(4.53)

By the definition of the constraint set (
4.9
) and (
4.36
), we have

\kappa=\int_{D}\zeta_{\varepsilon}dx=\int_{D}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\frac{2h^{2}a\varepsilon+a^{2}(h^{2}+|x|^{2})}{(h^{2}+|x|^{2})^{2}}(\psi_{\varepsilon})_{+}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\frac{h^{2}\bar{\alpha}a\varepsilon}{h^{2}+|x|^{2}}+b\right)\textbf{1}_{\{\psi_{\varepsilon}>0\}}dx.

(4.54)

So by (
4.54
), the choice of
F_{1}(\cdot)
and (
3.3
), we obtain

\int_{D}\frac{2h^{2}v_{\varepsilon}}{|\vec{\xi}|^{4}}dx=\int_{D}\frac{2h^{2}a(\psi_{\varepsilon})_{+}}{\varepsilon(h^{2}+|x|^{2})^{2}}dx=O(\varepsilon).

From Lemma
4.14
, we get

\int_{D}\frac{x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}v_{\varepsilon}}{|\vec{\xi}|^{2}}dx=\int_{D}\frac{ax_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}(\psi_{\varepsilon})_{+}}{\varepsilon(h^{2}+|x|^{2})}dx=\frac{aX_{\varepsilon,1}}{\varepsilon(h^{2}+|X_{\varepsilon}|^{2})}\int_{D}\partial_{x_{1}}(\psi_{\varepsilon})_{+}dx+o(1)=o(1).

Similarly, we have
\int_{D}\frac{x_{2}\partial_{x_{2}}v_{\varepsilon}}{|\vec{\xi}|^{2}}dx=o(1).
Taking these into (
4.53
), we have

\int_{D}w_{\varepsilon}dx=\kappa+o(1),

(4.55)

which implies that in distributional sense as
\varepsilon\to 0
,

\frac{w_{3}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,|\ln\varepsilon|^{-1}\tau)}{h}\vec{\xi}\to\kappa\delta_{\gamma(\tau)}\mathbf{t}_{\gamma(\tau)}.

(4.56)

Step 2.
We calculate
\frac{1}{h}\left(\partial_{x_{2}}v_{\vec{\xi}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,|\ln\varepsilon|^{-1}\tau),-\partial_{x_{1}}v_{\vec{\xi}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,|\ln\varepsilon|^{-1}\tau),0\right)^{t}
.
Note that from Lemma
4.14
, one has

\int_{D}\partial_{x_{2}}v_{\varepsilon}dx=\frac{a}{\varepsilon}\int_{D}\partial_{x_{2}}(\psi_{\varepsilon})_{+}dx=o(1),

which implies that
\frac{1}{h}\left(\partial_{x_{2}}v_{\vec{\xi}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,|\ln\varepsilon|^{-1}\tau),0,0\right)^{t}\to 0
in distributional sense as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Similarly, we get
\frac{1}{h}\left(0,-\partial_{x_{1}}v_{\vec{\xi}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,|\ln\varepsilon|^{-1}\tau),0\right)^{t}\to 0
.
So we have as
\varepsilon\to 0
,

\frac{1}{h}\left(\partial_{x_{2}}v_{\vec{\xi}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,|\ln\varepsilon|^{-1}\tau),-\partial_{x_{1}}v_{\vec{\xi}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,|\ln\varepsilon|^{-1}\tau),0\right)^{t}\to 0.

(4.57)

Combining (
4.56
) and (
4.57
), we conclude that in distributional sense

\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,|\ln\varepsilon|^{-1}\tau)\to\kappa\delta_{\gamma(\tau)}\mathbf{t}_{\gamma(\tau)}\ \ \ \text{as}\ \ \varepsilon\to 0.

The proof of Theorem
1.4
is thus complete.

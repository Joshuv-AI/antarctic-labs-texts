Computing Radially-Symmetric Solutions of the Ultra-Relativistic Euler Equations with Entropy-Stable Discontinuous Galerkin Methods
Ferdinand Thein, Hendrik Ranocha

Abstract
The ultra–relativistic Euler equations describe gases in the relativistic case when the thermal energy dominates. These equations for an ideal gas are given in terms of the pressure, the spatial part of the dimensionless four-velocity, and the particle density. Kunik et al. (2024, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2024.113330 ) proposed genuine multi–dimensional benchmark problems for the ultra–relativistic Euler equations. In particular, they compared full two-dimensional discontinuous Galerkin simulations for radially symmetric problems with solutions computed using a specific one-dimensional scheme. Of particular interest in the solutions are the formation of shock waves and a pressure blow-up. In the present work we derive an entropy-stable flux for the ultra–relativistic Euler equations. Therefore, we derive the main field (or entropy variables) and the corresponding potentials. We then present the entropy-stable flux and conclude with simulation results for different test cases both in 2D and in 3D.

2
Conservative Formulations of the Equations

In this section we specify the governing equations precisely and provide necessary details on the conservative, primitive as well as entropy variables.
Here we consider the flat Minkowski metric given by

g_{\alpha\beta}=\begin{cases}+1,&\alpha=\beta=0,\\
-1,&\alpha=\beta=1,2,3,\\
\hphantom{-}0,&\alpha\neq\beta.\end{cases}

(2.1)

As mentioned before we study the ultra-relativistic equations for a perfect fluid in Minkowski space-time
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
,
t=x_{0}
, namely

\sum_{\beta=0}^{3}\frac{\partial T_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial x_{\beta}}=0

(2.2)

with
\alpha,\beta\in\{0,1,2,3\}
and the energy-momentum tensor

T_{\alpha\beta}=-pg_{\alpha\beta}+4pu_{\alpha}u_{\beta}.

(2.3)

Here
p
represents the pressure,
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the spatial part of the four-velocity vector
(u_{0},u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})=(\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}},\mathbf{u})
.
For the ideal ultra-relativistic gas the thermal energy dominates and thus the local energy density
e
at rest is linked to the pressure
p
by

\displaystyle e=3p.

(2.4)

Note that (
2.4
) is universal for an ideal gas in the ultra-relativistic regime.
For the physical background we refer to Weinberg
[
72
, Part I, pp 47-52]
, further details can be found in Kunik
[
35
, Chapter 3.9]
and in the works by Ruggeri and co-authors
[
46
,
57
,
56
]
.

The conservative multi–dimensional
d+1
form of the ultra–relativistic Euler equations (
2.2
) is presented in the following.
We consider either
d=2
or
d=3
space dimensions. Then the unknown quantities
p
and
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

satisfying (
2.2
)
depend on time
t\geq 0
and position
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Putting
\alpha=0
in (
2.2
) gives the conservation of energy

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(3p+4p|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\left(4pu_{k}\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\right)=0,

(2.5)

whereas for
\alpha=j=1,\dots,d
we obtain the conservation of momentum

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(4pu_{j}\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\right)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\left(p\delta_{jk}+4pu_{j}u_{k}\right)=0,\quad j=1,\dots,d.

(2.6)

We want to rewrite the conserved variables
\mathbf{w}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{d},w_{d+1})=(\overline{\mathbf{w}},w_{d+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
,
\overline{\mathbf{w}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

in terms of the primitive variables determined by the velocity
\mathbf{u}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the pressure
p
, i.e.,

\displaystyle w_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=4pu_{j}\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}},\ j=1,\ldots,d,

(2.7a)

\displaystyle w_{d+1}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=p(3+4|\mathbf{u}|^{2}).

(2.7b)

Reversely, the primitive variables can be rewritten in terms of the conserved variables as

\displaystyle u_{j}=\frac{w_{j}}{\sqrt{4p(w_{d+1}+p)}},\ j=1,\ldots,d,

(2.8a)

\displaystyle p=\frac{1}{3}\left(-w_{d+1}+\sqrt{-3|\overline{\mathbf{w}}|^{2}+4w_{d+1}^{2}}\right)\geq 0.

(2.8b)

According to
[
35
]
the system is equipped with a concave physical entropy given by

\displaystyle\eta=p^{\frac{3}{4}}\sqrt{1+\mathbf{u}^{2}}

(2.9)

and the corresponding flux reads

\displaystyle\mathbf{q}=p^{\frac{3}{4}}\mathbf{u}.

(2.10)

The calculations to show concavity for the physical entropy and the relation of the entropy and the entropy flux, i.e.,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}q_{k}^{T}=\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}\eta^{T}\cdot\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{w}}\mathbf{F}_{k}

(2.11)

are given in the Appendix
A
. Here
\mathbf{F}_{k}
denotes the flux of the system in
x_{k}
direction for
k=1,\dots,d
.
The entropy variables which are also known as main field, see
[
57
]
, are given by

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\omega}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\partial_{w_{j}}\eta=\begin{cases}-\dfrac{1}{4}\dfrac{\eta}{p}\dfrac{u_{j}}{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}},\,&j=1,\ldots,d,\\[15.00002pt]
\hphantom{-}\dfrac{1}{4}\dfrac{\eta}{p},\,&j=d+1.\end{cases}

(2.12)

The main field of the present system may be obtained from the main field of the relativistic Euler equations since the ultra–relativistic Euler equations are a subsystem of the latter. We refer the interested reader to
[
56
,
57
]
and to the recent survey
[
71
]
.
To construct the entropy-stable numerical flux, cf.
[
68
,
11
]
, we need to calculate the entropy - flux potential.
The corresponding flux is given by

\displaystyle\psi_{k}=\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\eta\cdot\mathbf{F}_{k}-q_{k}=-\frac{1}{4}\eta\frac{u_{k}}{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\boldsymbol{\psi}=-\frac{1}{4}\eta\frac{\mathbf{u}}{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}}.

(2.13)

3
Derivation of an entropy-conservative numerical flux

In this section we present the detailed derivation of the entropy-conservative numerical flux. Following the seminal works of Tadmor
[
68
,
67
]
, the condition
for an entropy-conservative numerical flux reads

\left\llbracket\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\rrbracket\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_{k}-\left\llbracket\psi_{k}\right\rrbracket=0,

(3.1)

where
\boldsymbol{\omega}
are the entropy variables given by (
2.12
),
\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_{k}
is the numerical flux in
space direction
k
,
\psi_{k}
is the flux potential in direction
k
defined in (
2.13
),
and
\left\llbracket a\right\rrbracket=a^{+}-a^{-}
denotes the jump of a quantity
a
.
The numerical fluxes
\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_{k}
should be consistent approximations of the
fluxes

\mathbf{F}_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}p\delta_{1k}+4pu_{1}u_{k}\\
\vdots\\
p\delta_{dk}+4pu_{d}u_{k}\\[10.00002pt]
4pu_{k}\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}p\delta_{1k}+\dfrac{w_{1}w_{k}}{w_{d+1}+p}\\
\vdots\\
p\delta_{dk}+\dfrac{w_{d}w_{k}}{w_{d+1}+p}\\[10.00002pt]
w_{k}\end{pmatrix}

(3.2)

of the ultra-relativistic Euler equations (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) which form a conservative system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{w}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{k}}\mathbf{F}_{k}(\mathbf{w})=0.

We follow the algorithmic approach of
[
49
]
to
derive an entropy-conservative numerical flux for the ultra-relativistic
Euler equations. Thus, we choose a set of variables and express all
jumps in terms of these variables. We then solve the resulting system
of equations in order to obtain entropy-conservative numerical fluxes which are “affordable” in the sense of
[
29
]
.

To express jumps of general expressions, we use mean values such as the
arithmetic mean

\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{491520}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}a\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{491520}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}=\frac{a^{+}+a^{-}}{2}.

(3.3)

Applying straight forward algebraic manipulations we obtain the analogues of well known rules for differentiation, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left\llbracket ab\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{491520}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}a\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{491520}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}\left\llbracket b\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket a\right\rrbracket\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{546131}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}b\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{546131}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket a^{2}\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=2\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{491520}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}a\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{491520}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}\left\llbracket a\right\rrbracket,

(3.5)

and for positive
a
we also have

\left\llbracket\sqrt{a}\right\rrbracket=\frac{\left\llbracket a\right\rrbracket}{2\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{491520}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}\sqrt{a}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{491520}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}}.

(3.6)

Moreover, we will use

-(a_{-}\sqrt{a_{+}}+a_{+}\sqrt{a_{-}})\left\llbracket a^{-1/2}\right\rrbracket=-(a_{-}+\sqrt{a_{+}}\sqrt{a_{-}}-\sqrt{a_{+}}\sqrt{a_{-}}-a_{+})=\left\llbracket a\right\rrbracket,

(3.7)

i.e.,

\frac{\left\llbracket a^{-1/2}\right\rrbracket}{\left\llbracket a\right\rrbracket}=-\frac{1}{a_{-}\sqrt{a_{+}}+a_{+}\sqrt{a_{-}}}.

(3.8)

Because of the structure of the entropy variables
\boldsymbol{\omega}
, we choose the
variables
u_{i}p^{-1/4}
and
p
to express all jumps. Then, we get

\left\llbracket\omega_{i}\right\rrbracket=-\frac{1}{4}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$[\mkern-3.0mu[$}}}u_{i}p^{-1/4}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$]\mkern-3.0mu]$}}},\quad i\in\{1,\dots,d\}.

(3.9)

The jump of the entropy variable
\omega_{d+1}
is given by

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\omega_{d+1}\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left\llbracket p^{-1/4}\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\right\rrbracket=\frac{1}{4}\left\llbracket\sqrt{p^{-1/2}+\left(|\mathbf{u}|p^{-1/4}\right)^{2}}\right\rrbracket=\frac{1}{4}\frac{\left\llbracket p^{-1/2}+\left(|\mathbf{u}|p^{-1/4}\right)^{2}\right\rrbracket}{2\raisebox{-2.99998pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}\sqrt{p^{-1/2}+\left(|\mathbf{u}|p^{-1/4}\right)^{2}}\raisebox{-2.99998pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}}

(3.10)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\raisebox{-2.99998pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}\sqrt{p^{-1/2}+\left(|\mathbf{u}|p^{-1/4}\right)^{2}}\raisebox{-2.99998pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}}\left(\frac{\left\llbracket p^{-1/2}\right\rrbracket}{\left\llbracket p\right\rrbracket}\left\llbracket p\right\rrbracket+2\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}u_{i}p^{-1/4}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}\left\llbracket u_{i}p^{-1/4}\right\rrbracket\right),

where (
3.6
) has been used. Moreover, the ratio
of the jumps of the pressure terms can be expressed using (
3.8
).
For the jump of the flux potential we get

\left\llbracket\psi_{k}\right\rrbracket=-\frac{1}{4}\left\llbracket p^{3/4}u_{k}\right\rrbracket=-\frac{1}{4}\left\llbracket p\,u_{k}p^{-1/4}\right\rrbracket=-\frac{1}{4}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{491520}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}p\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{491520}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}\left\llbracket u_{k}p^{-1/4}\right\rrbracket-\frac{1}{4}\left\llbracket p\right\rrbracket\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}u_{k}p^{-1/4}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}.

(3.11)

Thus, the condition for the entropy-conservative numerical flux in the present situation reads

\displaystyle 0=\left\llbracket\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\rrbracket\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_{k}-\left\llbracket\psi_{k}\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{1}{4}\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_{k}^{(i)}+\frac{\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}u_{i}p^{-1/4}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}}{4\raisebox{-2.99998pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}\sqrt{p^{-1/2}+\left(|\mathbf{u}|p^{-1/4}\right)^{2}}\raisebox{-2.99998pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}}\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_{k}^{(d+1)}+\frac{1}{4}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{491520}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}p\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{491520}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}\delta_{ik}\right)\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$[\mkern-3.0mu[$}}}u_{i}p^{-1/4}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$]\mkern-3.0mu]$}}}

(3.12)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{8\raisebox{-2.99998pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}\sqrt{p^{-1/2}+\left(|\mathbf{u}|p^{-1/4}\right)^{2}}\raisebox{-2.99998pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}}\frac{\left\llbracket p^{-1/2}\right\rrbracket}{\left\llbracket p\right\rrbracket}\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_{k}^{(d+1)}+\frac{1}{4}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}u_{k}p^{-1/4}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}\right)\left\llbracket p\right\rrbracket.

This equation is satisfied if we choose the numerical flux
\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_{k}
so that
each term in parentheses vanishes. First, we obtain the numerical energy flux

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_{k}^{(d+1)}

\displaystyle=-2\frac{\left\llbracket p\right\rrbracket}{\left\llbracket p^{-1/2}\right\rrbracket}\raisebox{-2.99998pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}p^{-1/4}\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\raisebox{-2.99998pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}u_{k}p^{-1/4}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}

\displaystyle=2\left(p_{-}\sqrt{p_{+}}+p_{+}\sqrt{p_{-}}\right)\raisebox{-2.99998pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}p^{-1/4}\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\raisebox{-2.99998pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}u_{k}p^{-1/4}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}.

This is clearly a consistent approximation of the energy flux

\mathbf{F}_{k}^{(d+1)}=4pu_{k}\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}

into the other term in parentheses, we get

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_{k}^{(i)}+\frac{\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}u_{i}p^{-1/4}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}}{4\raisebox{-2.99998pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}\sqrt{p^{-1/2}+\left(|\mathbf{u}|p^{-1/4}\right)^{2}}\raisebox{-2.99998pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}}\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_{k}^{(d+1)}+\frac{1}{4}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{491520}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}p\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{491520}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}\delta_{ik}

\displaystyle=2\left(p_{-}\sqrt{p_{+}}+p_{+}\sqrt{p_{-}}\right)\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}u_{i}p^{-1/4}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}u_{k}p^{-1/4}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}+\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{491520}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}p\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{491520}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}\delta_{ik}.

This is a consistent approximation of the momentum flux

\mathbf{F}_{k}^{(i)}=4pu_{i}u_{k}+\delta_{ik}p
of the ultra-relativistic Euler equations.
Thus, we have proven

3.1
.

The numerical fluxes

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_{k}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=2\left(p_{-}\sqrt{p_{+}}+p_{+}\sqrt{p_{-}}\right)\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}u_{i}p^{-1/4}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}u_{k}p^{-1/4}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}+\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{491520}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}p\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{491520}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}\delta_{ik},

(3.16)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_{k}^{(d+1)}

\displaystyle=2\left(p_{-}\sqrt{p_{+}}+p_{+}\sqrt{p_{-}}\right)\raisebox{-2.99998pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}p^{-1/4}\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\raisebox{-2.99998pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\{\mkern-6.0mu\{$}}}u_{k}p^{-1/4}\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\resizebox{0}{737586}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\}\mkern-6.0mu\}$}}}

are symmetric, consistent, and entropy-conservative two-point fluxes
for the ultra-relativistic Euler equations (
2.6
) and (
2.5
).

3.2
.

The pressure in the momentum flux of (
3.16
)
is approximated using an arithmetic mean. For the classical compressible
Euler equations, this is required for the stronger version of
kinetic energy preservation
[
30
,
9
]

discussed in
[
50
,
51
]
,
see also
[
53
,
48
]
.
Even without provable entropic properties, kinetic energy-preserving split forms have been widely used for compressible computational fluid dynamics and turbulence simulations because of the increased robustness
[
22
,
60
,
31
]
.

4
Numerical Methods

We want to compare numerical results for radially symmetric solutions of the original multi–dimensional initial-value problem (IVP) for the ultra-relativistic Euler equations (
2.5
)–(
2.6
). To avoid confusion of the pressure in the multi–dimensional case and the pressure in the radially symmetric case we denote the pressure in the multi–dimensional case by
\tilde{p}
from now on.
For
j=1,\dots,d
the IVP reads

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(3\tilde{p}+4\tilde{p}|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\left(4\tilde{p}u_{k}\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\right)&=0,\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(4\tilde{p}u_{j}\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\right)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\left(\tilde{p}\delta_{jk}+4\tilde{p}u_{j}u_{k}\right)&=0,\\
\tilde{p}(0,\mathbf{x})=\tilde{p}_{0}(|\mathbf{x}|),\quad\mathbf{u}(0,\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{u}_{0}(\mathbf{x})&=u_{0}(|\mathbf{x}|)\frac{\mathbf{x}}{|\mathbf{x}|}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.1)

We require radially symmetric solutions of this system, i.e., for
t\geq 0
and
x=|\mathbf{x}|>0
the restrictions for pressure and velocity are given by

\displaystyle\tilde{p}(t,\mathbf{x})=p(t,x)\,,\quad\mathbf{u}(t,\mathbf{x})=\frac{v(t,x)}{\sqrt{1-v(t,x)^{2}}}\cdot\frac{\mathbf{x}}{x}\,.

(4.2)

For
t=0
we obtain given initial data
\tilde{p}(0,\mathbf{x})=p(0,x)=p_{0}(x)
and

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(0,\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{u}_{0}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{v_{0}(x)}{\sqrt{1-v_{0}(x)^{2}}}\cdot\frac{\mathbf{x}}{x}\,,

(4.3)

and for
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we may also put
\mathbf{u}(0,\mathbf{0})=\mathbf{0}
and
v_{0}(0)=0
.

Multi–dimensional discontinuous Galerkin methods.

We use the numerical simulation framework Trixi.jl
[
55
,
59
]
implemented in Julia
[
5
]
to solve the multi–dimensional IVP (
4.1
) using discontinuous Galerkin methods.
Specifically, we use the discontinuous Galerkin spectral element method (DGSEM) based on Gauss-Lobatto-Legendre nodes
[
32
]
.
We use flux differencing based on our newly developed entropy-conservative two-point flux in the volume and a local

Lax-Friedrichs/Rusanov flux at interfaces combined with finite volume subcell shock capturing
[
24
]
.
We use adaptive mesh refinement (AMR) based on p4est
[
6
]
and an adaptation of the indicator of
[
43
]
applied to the variable
\tilde{p}\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}
.
For the self-similar expansion (Example 2 below), we also apply the positivity-preserving limiter of Zhang and Shu for the pressure
[
74
]
.
We use the third-order, four-stage explicit strong stability preserving Runge-Kutta method of
[
33
]
with embedded method of
[
12
]
and adaptive step size controller of
[
52
]
implemented in OrdinaryDiffEq.jl
[
47
]
to integrate the resulting system of ordinary differential equations in time.
All data and the Julia source code required to reproduce the numerical results presented in this paper are available online in our reproducibility repository
[
69
]
.

One-dimensional radially symmetric solver RadSymS.

For a detailed presentation of the scheme
RadSymS
used to compute the solution to the quasi one-dimensional radially symmetric form of the ultra-relativistic Euler equations we refer to
[
36
]
and
[
37
]
.
The solution of the radially symmetric quasi one-dimensional problem satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} \quad\quad\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(x^{d-1}a\right)+\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(x^{d-1}b\right)&=0,\\
\quad\quad\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(x^{d-1}b\right)+\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(x^{d-1}c\right)&=\frac{d-1}{2}x^{d-2}(a-c),\\
\lim\limits_{t\searrow 0}a(t,x)=a_{0}(x),\quad\lim\limits_{t\searrow 0}b(t,x)&=b_{0}(x),\end{aligned}\right.

(4.4)

for
t>0
and
x>0
representing the radial direction.
A detailed presentation can again be found in
[
36
]
and
[
37
]
.
Here
d=2
or
d=3
denotes the space dimension and
c=c(a,b)
is given by

\displaystyle c=c(a,b)=\frac{5}{3}a-\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{4a^{2}-3b^{2}}.

(4.5)

We are looking for weak solutions
a=a(t,x)
,
b=b(t,x)
with
|b|<a
, i.e.

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}x^{d-1}a\,\textup{d}x-x^{d-1}b\,\textup{d}t=0,\,\int_{\partial\Omega}x^{d-1}b\,\textup{d}x-x^{d-1}c\,\textup{d}t=\frac{d-1}{2}\iint_{\Omega}x^{d-2}(a-c)\,\textup{d}t\textup{d}x.

(4.6)

Here
\Omega\subset Q
is a convex domain with piecewise smooth boundary and
Q=\{(t,x)\,|\,t>0,\;x>0\}
, see
[
37
,
36
]
. The variables
(a,b)
are linked to
(p,u)
through a one-to-one transformation on the sets

\mathcal{S}=\{(p,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,|\,p>0\}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{\mathcal{S}}=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,|\,|b|<a\}.

The mapping
\Theta\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{S}\to\tilde{\mathcal{S}}
and its inverse are given by

\displaystyle\Theta(p,u)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}p\left(3+4u^{2}\right)\\
4pu\sqrt{1+u^{2}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a\\
b\end{pmatrix},

(4.7)

\displaystyle\Theta^{-1}(a,b)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{1}{3}\left(\sqrt{4a^{2}-3b^{2}}-a\right)\\
\dfrac{b}{\sqrt{4p(p+a)}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}p\\
u\end{pmatrix}.

(4.8)

Using the transformation (
4.7
), its inverse (
4.8
) and the velocity

\displaystyle v=\frac{u}{\sqrt{1+u^{2}}}\quad\text{with}\;|v|<1,

(4.9)

we replace the state variables
a
and
b
by
p
and
v
, respectively.
We prescribe the initial pressure
p_{0}=p(0,\cdot)
as well as the initial velocity
v_{0}=v(0,\cdot)
.
The variable
v
is chosen, because the restriction
|v|<1
leads to better color plots (the variable
u
is unbounded).

The quasi one-dimensional problem (
4.4
) is approximately solved by the one-dimensional radially symmetric scheme presented in
[
37
,
36
]
with
N=5000
.
Note that for constant pressure
p_{0}>0
and
v_{0}=0
, we obtain a stationary solution.
Such a steady part is contained in the solutions to the examples presented below.

Example 1: Solutions including a shock and a stationary part.

Following
[
41
]
, self-similar solutions can be constructed solving an ODE system in radial direction
x
. These solutions are in particular constant along rays
\xi=x/t
or, equivalently,
\vartheta=t/x
.

We consider constant initial data with pressure
p_{0}=1
and radial velocity
v_{0}\in(-1,0)
.
Due to
[
41
, Section 2.3]
there is a solution
p(t,x)=P(\vartheta)
and
v(t,x)=V(\vartheta)
depending only on
\vartheta=t/x
for
t,x>0
,
with a single straight line shock emanating from the zero point. Let
\tilde{s}\in(0,1)
be the unknown constant shock speed.
Then we put
v_{-}=0
and can find an unknown pressure
p_{-}>0
with

p(t,x)=p_{-}\,,\quad v(t,x)=v_{-}=0\quad\mbox{for\penalty\ }x<\tilde{s}\cdot t.

Due to the Rankine-Hugoniot shock conditions introduced in
[
35
, Section 4.4]
we obtain from
[
35
, page 82]

for a so called 3-shock after a lengthy calculation the algebraic shock conditions

\frac{p_{+}}{p_{-}}=\frac{9\tilde{s}^{2}-1}{3(1-\tilde{s}^{2})},\quad v_{+}=\frac{3}{2}\tilde{s}-\frac{1}{2\tilde{s}}\,,\quad v_{-}=0,

(5.1)

0<p_{+}<p_{-},\quad\frac{1}{3}<\tilde{s}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}.

Here
p_{-},v_{-}
are the values of pressure and velocity left to the 3-shock, and
p_{+},v_{+}
are the values of pressure and velocity right to the 3-shock.
Due to Lai
[
41
, Section 2.3]
the solution
p=P(\vartheta)
,
v=V(\vartheta)
satisfies the IVP of ordinary differential equations

\begin{split}\dot{V}(\vartheta)&=(d-1)\frac{V(V-\vartheta)(1-V^{2})}{3(\vartheta V-1)^{2}-(V-\vartheta)^{2}},\quad V(0)=v_{0}\in(-1,0),\\
\dot{P}({\vartheta})&=(d-1)\frac{4PV(\vartheta V-1)}{3(\vartheta V-1)^{2}-(V-\vartheta)^{2}},\quad P(0)=p_{0}=1.\end{split}

(5.2)

Moreover, it is shown in the appendix of
[
36
]
that Lai’s results guarantee a unique solution for
0\leq\vartheta<\vartheta_{max}

with a certain value
\vartheta_{max}>\sqrt{3}
and a unique value
\sqrt{3}<\tilde{\vartheta}<\sqrt{v_{0}^{2}+3}-v_{0}<3
, see
[
36
, (A.10)]
such that

V(\tilde{\vartheta})=\frac{3}{2\tilde{\vartheta}}-\frac{\tilde{\vartheta}}{2}.

(5.3)

After having determined
\tilde{\vartheta}
we finally obtain

\tilde{s}=\frac{1}{\tilde{\vartheta}},\quad v_{+}=V(\tilde{\vartheta}),\quad p_{+}=P(\tilde{\vartheta}),\quad p_{-}=p_{+}\frac{3(1-\tilde{s}^{2})}{9\tilde{s}^{2}-1}.

(5.4)

For the numerical simulation we prescribe a constant initial pressure
p_{0}=1
and a constant initial velocity
v_{0}=-1/\sqrt{2}
.
This corresponds to the following initial data for the original IVP (
4.1
)

\tilde{p}(0,\mathbf{x})=1\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{u}(0,\mathbf{x})=-\frac{\mathbf{x}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\,,\quad\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}.

The radially symmetric solution of (
4.1
) is determined by the solution of the ODE system (
5.2
) which gives a shock wave moving at constant speed
\tilde{s}
. We want to point out that these ODEs are written in terms of
\vartheta=t/x
. Thus, the initial data

and

for (

and

The ODE system (

For the computation of

6
Conclusion

We have investigated the system of the ultra–relativistic Euler equations in two and three space dimensions. For the first time an entropy-conservative flux has been derived and applied for numerical computations. We studied five different benchmarks in two and three dimensions, where the full-dimensional system is treated using Trixi.jl and the results are compared to numerical reference solutions. It should be emphasized that this is the first time that genuine three-dimensional results for these benchmarks have been obtained and presented.
The obtained numerical results coincide well, although they emphasize the need to reduce the computational complexity for the three-dimensional case. Despite the interest in the particular system under consideration, these benchmarks may serve as challenging benchmarks for other multi–dimensional solvers.

Appendix A
Derivatives and Supplementary Calculations for the Entropy

In this section we want to provide further details on the derivatives. Furthermore, we show the concavity of the entropy and proof relation (
2.11
).
For simplicity, we will express the derivatives in terms of the primitive variables, which is more convenient to work with.

Using (
2.8b
) and (
2.7
) we can calculate the derivatives of the pressure

\displaystyle\partial_{w_{j}}p=\begin{cases}-\dfrac{w_{j}}{w_{d+1}+3p}=-2u_{j}\dfrac{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}}{3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}},\,&j=1,\ldots,d,\\[10.00002pt]
\dfrac{w_{d+1}-p}{w_{d+1}+3p}=\dfrac{1+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}{3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}},\,&j=d+1.\end{cases}

(A.1)

The derivatives of the velocity components
u_{i},i=1,\dots,d
can be obtained using (
2.8a
) and (
2.7
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{w_{j}}u_{i}&=\dfrac{\delta_{ij}}{\sqrt{4p(w_{d+1}+p)}}+\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{u_{i}}{p}\dfrac{w_{j}(w_{d+1}+2p)}{(w_{d+1}+p)(w_{d+1}+3p)}\\
&=\dfrac{\delta_{ij}}{4p\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}}+\dfrac{u_{i}u_{j}}{4p}\dfrac{5+4|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}(3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2})},\,j=1,\ldots,d,\\[15.00002pt]
\partial_{w_{d+1}}u_{i}&=-\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{u_{i}}{p}\dfrac{w_{d+1}+p}{w_{d+1}+3p}=-\dfrac{u_{i}}{p}\dfrac{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}{3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}.\end{split}

(A.2)

We yield for the derivatives of the entropy with respect to the primitive variables

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial p}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4}p^{-1/4}\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}=\frac{3}{4}\frac{\eta}{p},

(A.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial u_{i}}

\displaystyle=p^{3/4}\frac{u_{i}}{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}}=\eta\frac{u_{i}}{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}.

(A.4)

Note that corresponding to the flux potential (
2.13
) we can also calculate the entropy potential. Sometimes it is also called generating potential; we refer to the recent survey
[
71
]
and the references therein.
We obtain for the entropy potential

\displaystyle\varphi=\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}\eta\cdot\mathbf{w}-\eta=-\frac{1}{4}\eta.

(A.5)

Next, we provide the second order derivatives of
\eta
with respect to the conservative variables, i.e.,

\displaystyle\partial_{w_{i}}^{2}\eta

\displaystyle=-\frac{\left(1+u_{i}^{2}\right)\left(3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)+u_{i}^{2}\left(1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)}{16p^{\frac{5}{4}}\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\left(3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)},\;i=1,\dots,d,

(A.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{w_{i}w_{j}}^{2}\eta

\displaystyle=-\frac{u_{i}u_{j}\left(4+3|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)}{16p^{\frac{5}{4}}\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\left(3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)},\;i,j=1,\dots,d,\;i\neq j,

(A.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{w_{i}w_{d+1}}^{2}\eta

\displaystyle=-\frac{u_{i}\left(5+6|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)}{16p^{\frac{5}{4}}\left(3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)},\;i=1,\dots,d,

(A.8)

\displaystyle\partial_{w_{d+1}}^{2}\eta

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\left(1+6|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)}{16p^{\frac{5}{4}}\left(3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)}.

(A.9)

For simplicity, we are going to show the convexity of
\tilde{\eta}=-\eta
and the Hessian can be written as follows

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}^{2}_{\mathbf{w}}\tilde{\eta}=\frac{1}{16p^{\frac{5}{4}}\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}(3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2})}(\mathbf{A}_{1}+\mathbf{A}_{2}+\mathbf{A}_{3})

(A.10)

with

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)\begin{pmatrix}&&0\\
&\mathbf{Id}_{d\times d}&\vdots\\
0&\dots&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathbf{A}_{2}=\left(4+3|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}^{2}&u_{1}u_{2}&\dots&u_{1}u_{d}&0\\
u_{1}u_{2}&u_{2}^{2}&\dots&u_{2}u_{d}&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
u_{1}u_{d}&\dots&\dots&u_{d}^{2}&0\\
0&\dots&\dots&0&0\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad\mathbf{A}_{3}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&\dots&0&u_{1}\left(5+6|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\\
\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&\dots&0&u_{d}\left(5+6|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\\
u_{1}\left(5+6|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}&\dots&u_{d}\left(5+6|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}&\left(1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)\left(1+6|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)\end{pmatrix}

To prove convexity we now want to show that

\mathbf{v}^{T}\ \mathbf{D}^{2}_{\mathbf{w}}\tilde{\eta}\,\mathbf{v}>0,\;\forall\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}.

Therefore, we first note that all elements
(v_{1},\dots,v_{d},0)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
belong to
\ker(\mathbf{A}_{3})
and conversely all elements
(0,\dots,0,v_{d+1})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
belong to
\ker(\mathbf{A}_{1})
as well as to
\ker(\mathbf{A}_{2})
.
Clearly,
\mathbf{A}_{1}>0
for all
(v_{1},\dots,v_{d},0)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
.
For
\mathbf{A}_{2}
we first consider the non–zero block matrix and verify that the rows are pairwise linearly dependent, since the
i
-th row is written as
u_{i}\mathbf{u}
. Thus, we have that
\mathbf{A}_{2}\geq 0
for all
(v_{1},\dots,v_{d},0)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
. For
\mathbf{A}_{3}
we yield when multiplying with elements
\tilde{\mathbf{v}}=(0,\dots,0,v_{d+1})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}

\tilde{\mathbf{v}}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{3}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}=\left(1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)\left(1+6|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)v_{d+1}^{2}>0

and thus
\mathbf{A}_{3}>0
for all
(0,\dots,0,v_{d+1})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
.
Altogether we hence have shown the convexity of
\tilde{\eta}=-\eta
.

We now want to verify relation (
2.11
). Therefore, we first calculate
the derivatives of the entropy flux with respect to the primitive quantities. These are given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial q_{i}}{\partial p}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4}p^{-1/4}u_{i}=\frac{3}{4}\frac{q_{i}}{p},

(A.11)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial q_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}

\displaystyle=p^{3/4}\delta_{ij}.

(A.12)

From these we calculate the derivatives with respect to the conservative variables

\displaystyle\partial_{w_{j}}q_{i}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\delta_{ij}(3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2})-u_{i}u_{j}(1+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2})}{4p^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}(3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2})},\,&j=1,\ldots,d,\\[15.00002pt]
-\dfrac{u_{i}}{4p^{\frac{1}{4}}}\dfrac{1-2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}{3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}},\,&j=d+1.\end{cases}

(A.13)

We further need the Jacobians of the conservative fluxes
\mathbf{F}_{k}
in the normal directions w.r.t.
x_{k}
. To this end we refer to
[
36
]
where the Jacobians are given for any normal direction, see
[
36
, (B.5)]
.
Here the situation simplifies, since we only consider the normal directions
\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{e}_{k},k=1,\dots,d
.
The flux Jacobian is determined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{w}}\mathbf{F}_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}\overline{\mathbf{A}}&\overline{\mathbf{a}}\\
\mathbf{e}_{k}^{T}&0\end{pmatrix}

(A.14)

with

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{A}}

\displaystyle=-2\frac{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}}{3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&\dots&0\\
&\mathbf{u}^{T}&\\
0&\dots&0\end{pmatrix}+\frac{u_{k}}{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}}\mathbf{Id}_{d\times d}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}0&&0\\
\vdots&\mathbf{u}&\vdots\\
0&&0\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle+2\frac{u_{k}}{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\left(3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)}\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}^{2}&u_{1}u_{2}&\dots&u_{1}u_{d}\\
u_{1}u_{2}&u_{2}^{2}&\dots&u_{2}u_{d}\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
u_{1}u_{d}&\dots&\dots&u_{d}^{2}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{a}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}{3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\mathbf{e}_{k}-\frac{4u_{k}}{3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\mathbf{u}.

If (
2.11
) is written component wise we yield

\displaystyle\partial_{w_{i}}q_{k}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\overline{\mathbf{w}}}\eta^{T}\overline{\mathbf{A}}^{(i,\cdot)}+\partial_{w_{d+1}}\eta\delta_{ik},\;i=1,\dots,d,

\displaystyle\partial_{w_{d+1}}q_{k}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\overline{\mathbf{w}}}\eta^{T}\overline{\mathbf{a}}.

Considering
i=1,\dots,d,i\neq k
yield

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{A}}^{(i,\cdot)}

\displaystyle=-2\frac{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}}{3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}u_{i}\mathbf{e}_{k}+\frac{u_{k}}{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}}\mathbf{e}_{i}+2\frac{u_{i}u_{k}}{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\left(3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)}\mathbf{u},

\displaystyle\nabla_{\overline{\mathbf{w}}}\eta^{T}\overline{\mathbf{A}}^{(i,\cdot)}

\displaystyle=-2\frac{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}}{3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}u_{i}\partial_{w_{k}}\eta+\frac{u_{k}}{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}}\partial_{w_{i}}\eta+2\frac{u_{i}u_{k}}{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\left(3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)}\nabla_{\overline{\mathbf{w}}}\eta^{T}\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\frac{\eta}{p}\frac{u_{i}u_{k}\left(1+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)}{\left(1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)\left(3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)}=\partial_{w_{i}}q_{k}.

Now we deal with
i=k=1,\dots,d
and obtain

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{A}}^{(k,\cdot)}

\displaystyle=-2\frac{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}}{3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}u_{k}\mathbf{e}_{k}+\frac{u_{k}}{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}}\mathbf{e}_{k}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}}\mathbf{u}+\frac{2u_{k}^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\left(3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)}\mathbf{u},

\displaystyle\nabla_{\overline{\mathbf{w}}}\eta^{T}\overline{\mathbf{A}}^{(k,\cdot)}

\displaystyle=u_{k}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}}-2\frac{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}}{3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\right)\partial_{w_{k}}\eta+\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}}+\frac{2u_{k}^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\left(3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)}\right)\nabla_{\overline{\mathbf{w}}}\eta^{T}\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\frac{\eta}{p}\frac{1}{\left(1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)\left(3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)}\left(u_{k}^{2}+3|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+2|\mathbf{u}|^{4}+2u_{k}^{2}|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right),

\displaystyle\nabla_{\overline{\mathbf{w}}}\eta^{T}\overline{\mathbf{A}}^{(k,\cdot)}+\partial_{w_{d+1}}\eta

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\frac{\eta}{p}\frac{u_{k}^{2}\left(1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)-\left(3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)}{\left(1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)\left(3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)}=\partial_{w_{k}}q_{k}.

We now consider the final case and get

\displaystyle\nabla_{\overline{\mathbf{w}}}\eta^{T}\overline{\mathbf{a}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}{3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\partial_{w_{k}}\eta-\frac{4u_{k}}{3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}\nabla_{\overline{\mathbf{w}}}\eta^{T}\mathbf{u}=-\frac{1}{4}\frac{\eta}{p}\frac{u_{k}\left(1-2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)}{\left(1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)\left(3+2|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)}=\partial_{w_{d+1}}q_{k}.

Thus, (
2.11
) is verified.

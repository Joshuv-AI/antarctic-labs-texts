LINC01518 predicts poor prognosis of prostate cancer and promotes its progression by regulating hsa-miR-320a/CNKSR2 axis
Authors: Guodong Chen, Zixin Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundProstate cancer (PCa) is the most common malignancy of the male genitourinary system. Understanding the molecular mechanism of PCa and its prognostic markers will assist in the selection of better treatment.AimTo explore the role of LINC01518 in the development of PCa and its prognostic value, and to understand its specific regulatory mechanism.MethodsThe levels of LINC01518, hsa-miR-320a, and CNKSR2 mRNA were detected by RT-qPCR. ROC curve was constructed to evaluate the prognostic value of LINC01518 in PCa. The effects of LINC01518 on the functions of PCa cells were demonstrated by CCK-8, Transwell test, and the detection of apoptotic markers, after LINC01518 knockdown. The interaction between hsa-miR-320a and LINC01518 or CNKSR2 mRNA was examined by constructing luciferase vectors.ResultsLINC01518 was abnormally expressed in PCa cells and tumor tissues, and knockdown of it inhibited the growth of PCa cells. LINC01518 predicted castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC) outcomes in PCa patients with AUC of 0.803, sensitivity and specificity of 75.4% and 74.6%, respectively. Hsa-miR-320a mimics reduced luciferase activity in PCa cells transfected with WT-LINC01518/CNKSR2 plasmids. Knocking down LINC01518 reduced CNKSR2 level, and this regulatory effect disappeared with the inhibition of hsa-miR-320a.ConclusionHigh levels of LINC01518 predicted poor prognosis in PCa patients and promoted CNKSR2 expression by competitively binding hsa-miR-320a, contributing to the progression of PCa.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01458-3.Keywords: Prostate cancer, LINC01518, hsa-miR-320a, CNKSR2, Castration-resistant prostate cancer

Introduction

Prostate cancer (PCa) is a kind of tumor caused by malignant hyperplasia of prostate epithelial cells, which is the most common malignant tumor of the male genitourinary system with high incidence and mortality among males [1]. In 2020, there were 1,414,259 new cases of PCa, and 375,304 death cases from PCa were reported globally. There were approximately 268,490 new cases of PCa and 34,500 deaths from it in the United States in 2022 [2]. Early-stage PCa patients can achieve good treatment results through radical surgery or radical radiotherapy, and even be cured. However, PCa is a very slow-progressing cancer, and in the early stages of it, many patients do not realize that they have the disease, so clinically new cases are generally late in stage and more malignant. Androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) is the main treatment for middle and advanced PCa patients. However, almost all patients will progress to the more aggressive castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC) after treatment [3].

Biomarkers play an important role in the screening, treatment, and prognosis of PCa. PCa runs in families. Genetic analysis showed that men with a family history of PCa have a 2–3 times greater risk of developing PCa than men without that [4]. Gene mutations of BRCA 1/2 greatly increase the probability of PCa in men, and genetic change of BRCA2 is associated with the aggressiveness of PCa [4]. The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) reported mutations or copy number changes in FOXA1, RB1, KMT2D, and other genes in PCa patients [5]. Detection of the expression patterns of these genes can be used to screen for PCa disease and facilitate early intervention. Many long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) have been linked to PCa. The length of lncRNA is greater than 200 nucleotides, and it plays an important regulatory role in cell growth, differentiation, and tumor development [6]. PCA3, a typical lncRNA biomarker in PCa, is highly expressed only in patients diagnosed with PCa, but not in patients with benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) [7]. PCA3 has been approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for use as a urine biomarker test for PCa diagnosis [8]. LncRNA MALAT1 could predict survival in patients with early-stage non-small cell lung cancer and is also associated with the onset and progression of PCa [9]. MALAT1 has been reported to be highly expressed in PCa and can be used as an independent predictor of PCa [10, 11]. MALAT1-derived miniRNA (MD-miniRNA) from plasma showed high diagnostic accuracy for PCa and was able to distinguish well between PCa and BPH patients [12, 13]. LncRNA HOTTIP was identified as an oncogene in PCa, and its high expression was positively correlated with adverse clinicopathological features, including perineural infiltration, higher T stage, seminal vesicle infiltration, etc., and predicted poor overall survival [14].

LncRNA LINC01518 is highly expressed in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), and its knockdown inhibits the tumorigenicity of ESCC cells by up-regulating miR-1-3p to restrain PIK3CA/Akt pathway [15]. In addition, LINC01518 has been identified as a potential prognostic gene for bladder cancer [16]. However, the role of LINC01518 in PCa and its prognostic value have not been explored. In this study, we retrospectively investigated the prognostic value of LINC01518 in PCa and studied the molecular mechanism of its promotion of PCa progression, expecting to provide theoretical support for LINC01518 as a biomarker to evaluate the post-treatment outcome of PCa patients and molecular targeted therapy.

Materials and methods

Subjects

The 217 healthy volunteers and 230 PCa patients included in this study were all from the Affiliated Wuxi People’s Hospital of Nanjing Medical University. Among the PCa patients, 114 volunteers underwent surgical castration and 116 underwent drug castration. They were followed up 1 year after treatment to determine whether they developed castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC). Before the study started, we had received the informed consent signed by the volunteers and obtained the approval of the hospital ethics committee.

Information and blood samples from all volunteers, as well as tumor tissue and para-carcinoma tissue (normal tissue adjacent to tumor tissue) from 114 PCa patients who underwent surgery, were well preserved.

Cell culture

RWPE-1 cells (human normal prostate epithelial cells) were purchased from SUNNCELL company and were cultured using the special medium (SNLM-569) from the same company. LNCaP and PC3 cells (PCa cells) purchased from ATCC were grown in RPMI 1640 medium with 10% FBS and 1% Pen/Strep. All cell lines were cultured in an incubator with 5% CO2 at 37 °C.

RT-qPCR

The mirVana™ miRNA separation Kit (ThermoFisher, America) was used to extract total RNA. MiRNA was reversely transcribed into cDNA using the microRNA reverse transcripti on Kit from the same company, and lncRNA and mRNA were reversely transcribed using the cDNA First Chain Synthesis Kit (Beyotime, China) for RT-qPCR. GAPDH was used as a housekeeping gene of LINC01518, CNKSR2, Ki-67, Bcl-2, and Bax. Hsa-miR-320a level was normalized by U6. Their expression levels were calculated by the formula 2−ΔΔCt. The sequences of primers were listed in Supplemental Table 1.

Prognostic assay

Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve and logistic regression analysis were used to evaluate the prognostic value of LINC01518 in PCa. The SPSS software was used to construct ROC curve of LINC01518 in predicting CRPC and to perform univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses.

Cell transfection

The well-grown PCa cells were pre-inoculated in a 6-well plate (the density was about 25%). Transfection was performed the next day when the cell density was about 30–50%. The small interfering RNA of LINC01518 was synthesized by RIBOBIO Biological Company. The hsa-miR-320a mimics and inhibitor were purchased from MedChemExpress company. 120 µL OPTI-MEM medium and 5 µL RNA solution (or negative control) were added into the sterile 1.5 mL EP tube successively, and then 6 µL Lipofectamine 2000 (Beyotime) was added and gently mixed, sitting for 15 min. The old medium was replaced with serum-free medium (1869 µL/well). The transfection mixture was added to the 6-well plate to culture away from light for 6 h. After transfection, they were replaced with fresh serum-containing medium and continued to culture for 24–48 h for follow-up experiments.

Cell viability assay

PCa cells were inoculated into a 96-well plate with a density of 4 × 104 cells/mL after transfection. After 24 h, the original culture medium was discarded, and 100 µL culture medium containing 10% CCK-8 reagent (Solarbio, China) was added to each well and incubated at 37 °C for 2–4 h. After incubation, the light absorption value at 450 nm was measured by an enzyme-labeled instrument, and the cell viability was calculated according to the calculation formula in the CCK-8 reagent specification.

Migration and invasion assay

An uncoated or Matrigel-coated dual-chamber system (pore size 8 μm, Corning) was used for migration or invasion tests. 100 µL of FBS-free cell suspension (1 × 106 cells/mL) was added to the upper cavity, and 500 µL of medium containing FBS was added to the lower cavity. After 24 h, the migrated and invaded cells were fixed with 10% formaldehyde, stained with 5% crystal violet, and observed under a microscope, to count the number of osmotic cells from five selected fields at random.

Western blotting

Total protein was extracted using RIPA lysate (Solarbio), whose concentration was detected by a BCA kit (Solarbio). SDS-PAGE was performed with 50 µg protein per well. After the isolated proteins were transferred to PVDF membranes, they were incubated in 5% skim milk at room temperature for 2 h. The PVDF membrane was then incubated in primary antibody solution at 4 °C overnight. Next day, the PVDF membrane was incubated in the secondary antibody solution for 1.5 h, and the protein signal was detected by the ECL chemiluminescence detection kit (Absin, China).

Detection of apoptosis rate

The apoptosis rate was detected by an Annexin V-FITC/PI apoptosis detection kit (Absin). The cells were collected in an EP tube and cleaned with pre-cooled PBS buffer. After the supernatant was removed, 300 µL of 1× Binding Buffer and 5 µL of Annexin V-FITC were added and incubated for 15 min at room temperature away from light. Then 5 µL of PI solution was added to detect apoptotic cells using the flow cytometry.

Bioinformatics analysis

The GSE230278, GSE179321, and GSE115414 datasets were derived from NCBI-GEO Database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). The LncRNASNP2 Database (https://guolab.wchscu.cn/lncRNASNP#!/) was used to predict the downstream miRNAs of LINC01518. TargetScan-Human 8.0 Database (https://www.targetscan.org/vert_80/) and starBase Database (https://rnasysu.com/encori/index.php) were used to predict the downstream genes of hsa-miR-320a.

Double-luciferase reporter gene assay

Wild-type and mutant LINC01518/CNKSR2 gene fragments were cloned into double luciferase vectors to construct WT- and MUT-LINC01518/CNKSR2. WT- or MUT-LINC01518/CNKSR2 were co-transfected with miRNA negative control (mim-NC) or hsa-miR-320a mimics (mim-320a). The luciferase activity was then analyzed using the Dual-Luciferase Reporting System (Beyotime).

Statistical analysis

The significance of the association between various clinical factors and LINC01518 levels was analyzed by χ2 test using SSPS software. The difference between the two groups was analyzed by Student’s t-test, and the difference between multiple groups was analyzed by one-way ANOVA, using GraphPad software. All results were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and p < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Clinical features of subjects and LINC01518 expression in PCa

The clinical features of 230 PCa patients were collected and summarized in Table 1. These patients were divided into a high-expression group and a low-expression group based on the LINC01518 level in peripheral blood (bounded by the median). The high expression group of LINC01518 included more patients with higher tumor node metastasis (TNM) grade or higher Gleason score (Table 1). RT-qPCR results displayed that the expression level of LINC01518 was significantly increased in PCa patients’ peripheral blood (compared with healthy volunteers), tumor tissues (compared with para-carcinoma tissues), and PCa cells (compared with normal prostate epithelial cells) (Fig. 1A–C).

LINC01518 is associated with poor prognosis in PCa

RT-qPCR results showed that LINC01518 was highly expressed in blood samples from CRPC patients (Fig. 2A). Then we used the LINC01518 level of peripheral blood to construct a ROC curve for predicting CRPC in PCa patients (Fig. 2B). The results displayed that LINC01518 was a good classifier for the outcome of CRPC in PCa patients with castration treatment (AUC = 0.803, sensitivity = 75.4%, specificity = 74.6%, cutoff value = 2.183). The results of multifactorial logistic regression analysis displayed that LINC01518 was an independent influence on CRPC outcome in PCa patients (p < 0.001), and for every one unit increase in LINC01518 level, the risk of developing CRPC in castration-treated PCa patients will elevate 3.942 times (Table 2). These pieces of evidence suggest that a high level of LINC01518 represented a poor prognosis of PCa.

LINC01518 promotes PCa development

To investigate the function of LINC01518 in PCa, we introduced its small interfering RNA (si-LINC01518) and verified its knockdown efficiency (Fig. 3A). LINC01518 knockdown inhibited PCa cell viability and decreased the expression of proliferation marker Ki-67 (Fig. 3B, C), indicating that LINC01518 contributed to the growth of PCa cells. LINC01518 promoted PCa cells to migrate and invade, transwell assay results showed that the number of migrated and invasive PCa cells with LINC01518 knockdown significantly reduced (Fig. 3D, E). In addition, knocking down LINC01518 reduced the ratio of the expression of anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2 to pro-apoptotic protein Bax (Fig. 3F–H) and increased the apoptosis rate (Fig. 3I), suggesting that LINC01518 played an anti-apoptotic role in PCa.

Molecular mechanism of LINC01518 promoting PCa development

We predicted the downstream miRNAs of LINC01518 using the LncRNASNP2 Database. Hsa-miR-320a was identified in the Venn diagram between these miRNAs and miRNAs with low expression in PCa tumor tissues (compared to benign tissues) (p < 0.001, logFC < − 1) from the GSE230278 dataset (Fig. 4A). We then used the TargetScanHuman 8.0 and starBase Databases to predict the target genes of hsa-miR-320a, in which CNKSR2 was shared with high expression (p < 0.001, logFC > 2) in PCa tumor tissues from the GSE179321 and GSE115414 datasets (Fig. 4B). Our RT-qPCR experiment confirmed the results of bioinformatics analysis, hsa-miR-320a was indeed low-expressed and CNKSR2 was high-expressed, in PCa cells and tumor tissues (Fig. 4D–G). We constructed the luciferase vectors of wild-type (WT)/mutant (MUT) LINC01518 and CNKSR2, respectively. The binding sequences were shown in Fig. 4C. The mimics of hsa-miR-320a decreased luciferase activity in PCa cells transfected with WT-LINC01518/CNKSR2 but did not affect that in cells transfected with mutant vectors (Fig. 4H–K). After the LINC01518 knockdown, the expression of hsa-miR-320a was increased, and the expression of CNKSR2 was decreased (Fig. 4L, N–O). The inhibition of hsa-miR-320a up-regulated the expression level of CNKSR2 (Fig. 4M). After inhibiting hsa-miR-320a, the inhibitory effect of LINC01518 knockdown on CNKSR2 expression almost disappeared (Fig. 4N, O). These results suggest that LINC01518 acted as an competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA) adsorbing hsa-miR-320a to inhibit its suppression of CNKSR2 expression.

Discussion

Given the complexity and heterogeneity of cancer, the exploration of potential biomarkers will be conducive to the early diagnosis and prognosis assessment of cancer, so that patients can receive more accurate treatment. These markers may be proteins, DNA, metabolites, and lncRNA, etc. [17]. A lot of solid evidence showed an important function of lncRNA in PCa progression. LncRNA PVT1 plays a carcinogenic role in PCa. High PVT1 level predicted poorer overall survival and higher tumor stage. Knocking down PVT1 can significantly inhibit the growth of PCa and promote apoptosis in vivo and in vitro [18]. LncRNA PCAT29 plays an opposite role in PCa, inhibiting the carcinogenic phenotype of PCa. Low expression of PCAT29 is associated with poor prognosis [19]. LINC01518 is considered to be a carcinogen of ESCC [15]. We demonstrated that LINC01518 also played a pro-cancer role in PCa and was highly expressed in PCa cells and tissues. Moreover, PCa patients with high peripheral blood LINC01518 expression levels had a significantly higher risk of developing more malignant CRPC after ADT treatment than patients with low levels.

LncRNAs usually act as ceRNAs that compete with mRNA for binding to miRNA, thereby inhibiting the regulation of miRNA in gene expression and thus participating in the PCa process. LncRNA SNHG7 is a carcinogen of PCa, it binds to miR-324-3p as a sponge, thereby releasing the binding of miR-324-3p to WNT2B mRNA, which up-regulated the expression of WNT2B. WNT2B is an important member of the Wnt signaling pathway, and its high expression promotes the epithelial-mesenchymal transformation (EMT) of PCa cells [20]. Wu et al. showed that lncRNA VPS9D1-AS1 up-regulates FGFRL1 expression through competitively sponging miR-187-3p and promotes the malignant phenotype of PCa cells [21]. LncRNA SNHG15, located in the cytoplasm of PCa cells, is a molecular sponge of miR-338-3p. FKBP1A is a target gene of miR-338-3p, and SNHG15 plays a carcinogenic role in PCa by regulating the miR-338-3p/FKBP1A axis [22]. These pieces of evidence suggest that the lncRNA/miRNA/mRNA axis plays a crucial role in the occurrence and development of PCa. In this study, we screened the potential target miRNA of LINC01518, hsa-miR-320a. In breast cancer, increased expression of hsa-miR-320a predicts higher survival rates [23]. Down-regulation of hsa-miR-320a induced by human papillomavirus 16 (HPV16) promotes cervical cancer cells to grow, migrate, and invade [24]. However, the role of hsa-miR-320a in PCa has not been reported. Our study showed that hsa-miR-320a expression was deficient in PCa. LINC01518 can act as a sponge to compete with CNKSR2 mRNA for binding to hsa-miR-320a, thus promoting CNKSR2 expression. A study has shown that ubiquitin ligase Smurf2 could increase CNKSR2 protein level by promoting its stability, thereby contributing to the proliferation of breast cancer cells [25]. Silencing Smurf2 inhibits tumorigenic properties of breast cancer cells by reducing CNKSR2 levels [26]. Our evidence showed that CNKSR2 was abnormally expressed in PCa, and LINC01518 promoted the malignant phenotypes of PCa cells through elevated CNKSR2 level mediated by hsa-miR-320a inhibition.

Drug resistance is a major challenge to be overcome in CRPC therapy. LncRNA plays an important role in CRPC resistance. LncRNA PCBP1-AS1 protects USP22-AR/AR-V7 complexes from ubiquitination degradation. Targeting PCBP1-AS1 significantly increases the sensitivity of PCa tumors to enzalutamide in vivo and in vitro [27]. LncRNA ERVH48-1 is highly expressed in docetaxel-resistant (DR) PCa cells and its overexpression increased the malignant phenotype and tumorigenicity of PCa-DR cells, which is achieved by sponging miR-4784 and activating the Wnt/β-catenin pathway [28]. LINC00184 is considered a potential therapeutic target for PCa-DR patients. LINC00184 could induce upregulation of PD-L1 expression through adsorption of miR-105-5p, promoting DR and immune escape of PCa cells [29]. These findings suggest that the LINC01518/has-miR-320a/CNKSR2 axis may be a valuable potential target for PCa and CRPC therapy.

In summary, we demonstrated the prognostic value of LINC01518 in PCa as an independent risk factor for CRPC. In terms of mechanism, LINC01518 played a carcinogenic role in PCa by regulating the has-miR-320a/CNKSR2 axis in vitro. In fact, the tumor environment in vivo is much more complex than in vitro, and the role of LINC01518/has-miR-320a/CNKSR2 pathway in PCa needs to be verified in vivo experiments. In the future, we will further explore the specific molecular mechanism of PCa pathogenesis by constructing a PCa mouse model. Overall, our study will provide new ideas for the understanding of the pathogenesis of PCa, the selection of molecular targeted therapy for PCa and the evaluation of the efficacy of ADT therapy.

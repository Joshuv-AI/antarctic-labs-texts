Disruption of mgrB gene by ISkpn14 sourced from a blaKPC−2 carrying plasmid mediating polymyxin resistance against carbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae during treatment: study on the underlying mechanisms
Authors: Ziyao Li, Zichen Lei, Xinmeng Liu, Feilong Zhang, Xinrui Yang, Yongli Wu, Chen Li, Jiankang Zhao, Yulin Zhang, Yanning Hua, Binghuai Lu, Bin Cao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCarbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae (CRKP) infections poses global challenges, with limited options available for targeted therapy. Polymyxin was been regarded as one of the most important last-resort antimicrobial agents. Many factors could accelerate the resistance evolution of polymyxin. Insertion sequence (IS) inserted into mgrB is the main polymyxin resistance mechanism in K. pneumoniae. In this study, two CRKPs (KP31157 and KP31311) were isolated from the urine of a patient, shifting from susceptible to resistant as the mgrB inserted by ISkpn14. We intended to explore the origin of the IS and underlying mechanisms resulting in polymyxin resistance.MethodsThe within-host evolution relationship and molecular features of both CRKPs were determined by pulsed-field gel electrophoresis (PFGE) and whole-genome sequencing (WGS). pKP31311_KPC-2 plasmid genome structures contained in the above two CRKPs were aligned with the homologic plasmids, retrieved from the NCBI genome database via comparative genomic analysis. The plasmids encoding ISkpn14 elements flanked by direct repeat (DR) or not were analyzed. The mRNA expression, plasmid curing and in vitro antibiotics inducing experiment were employed to understand the potential mechanism of polymyxin resistance.ResultsBoth strains, sharing homology, exhibited polymyxin resistance due to the insertion of ISkpn14 into the mgrB gene, influenced by minocycline exposure. Minocycline and tigecycline could accelerate polymyxin resistance (P < 0.05), validated by an in vitro induction experiment. The ISkpn14 without DR flanked expressed about 4 times higher than that with DR. The frequency of the mgrB insertion induced by polymyxin was significantly reduced (0 strain detected) after the blaKPC−2-carrying plasmid was eliminated.ConclusionsThis study provides direct experimental evidence that the ISkpn14 element causing mgrB inactivation and polymyxin resistance in K. pneumoniae originates from blaKPC−2-carrying plasmids. Minocycline exposure will accelerate the evolution of polymyxin resistance. Understanding the dynamics of IS transposition and its association with antibiotic exposure is crucial for developing effective strategies to reduce the emergence of polymyxin resistance in CRKP.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12866-024-03572-2.

Introduction

Klebsiella pneumoniae has emerged as a major clinical threat owing to the increasing prevalence of infections caused by carbapenem-resistant K. pneumoniae (CRKP), posing a challenge in treatment with limited antimicrobial options [1]. Polycationic antimicrobial agents, including polymyxin B and polymyxin E (also known as colistin), were the last-resort antibiotics against CRKP [2]. However, the emergence of polymyxin-resistant CRKP (PR-CRKP) has been documented worldwide [2–4]. The resistance mechanisms to polymyxin in K. pneumoniae involve mutation, insertion, loss of two-component system (TCS, PhoP/Q, PmrA/B, and CrrA/B), or plasmid-borne mcr gene [2]. Of them, mgrB gene, encoding MgrB, plays a crucial role in regulating PhoP/Q system, resulting in LPS modification, and thus is often responsible for the acquired polymyxin resistance in K. pneumoniae strains [2, 5–9].

A recent concern with polymyxins usage in CRKP infections is that the resistance mechanisms are activated following administration, resulting in treatment failure, in which the disruption of mgrB by insertion sequence (IS) elements plays a potential role [10]. However, the administration of other antibiotic agents, including tigecycline, might also result in PR-CRKP strains via insertional inactivation of mgrB [11]. The role of the transposition of ISs, e.g. ISkpn14, ISKpn26, IS903B, from blaKPC- or blaNDM-bearing plasmids into chromosomal mgrB has been speculated through genome analysis [10]; however, given the complexities of IS types, the following questions have long remained unelucidated: (1) Sources: where the IS elements integrated into mgrB comes from? (2) Genetic environments: what types of structures of IS facilicate its mobilization for further insertion into mgrB? (3) Activators: what types of antibiotics for CRKP treatment might induce polymyxin resistance mediated by mgrB disruption? (4) Will the plasmids carrying both carbapenemase genes (blaKPC−2 mainly in China) and specific IS elements facilitate the dissemination of carbapenem resistance and polymyxin resistance via IS-meditated mgrB inactivation?

Herein, we reported two polymyxin-sensitive and resistant CRKP strains sharing homology, isolated consecutively from urine specimens of an 83-year-old female with tracheotomy over 2 weeks. The underlying resistance mechanisms were studied via bionomics analysis, plasmid curing and induced resistance experiments with different antibiotics. We tried to answer the above questions using these two polymyxin-sensitive and -resistant CRKP strains, because they by coincidence had the following features:

Materials and methods

PR-CRKP strains

The mgrB-inserting PR-CRKP strains mentioned in our previous study were reviewed [4]. The clinical medical record information of the strains was consulted, and the general information and the antibiotic application record of the patient were summarized.

Furthermore, two K. pneumoniae strains (KP31157 and KP31311) were respectively isolated from the urine of a female patient who was not exposed to polymyxin before July 26th, 2020 to October 2nd, 2020, and identified in our previous surveillance of PR-CRKP [4]. For comparative genomic analysis, the MGH 78578 (NCBI Accession: CP000647) was selected as the reference genome. The K.pneumoniae CDC29, sensitive to all commonly used antibiotics, plasmid-free, and harboring no IS located on chromosome identified via WGS, was selected as the recipient strain for the transformation experiment.

Antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST)

AST was performed using N335 susceptibility cards and Vitek-2 system (bioMérieux, France), as described previously [12], involving amikacin, tobramycin, minocycline, ceftazidime, cefepime, piperacillin/tazobactam, cefoperazone/sulbactam, aztreonam, imipenem, meropenem, levofloxacin, ciprofloxacin, and sulfamethoxazole/trimethoprim (SXT/TMP). The minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) of tigecycline and polymyxin were determined using the microdilution broth method (bio-KONT, Ltd. China). The breakpoints of tigecycline and polymyxin were defined by America Food and Drug Administration (FDA, https://www.fda.gov/) and CLSI (Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute, 2022) [13], with MICs > 8 mg/L and > 2 mg/L indicating resistance, respectively [12]. The production of carbapenemases was determined using mCIM and eCIM [13].

Population analysis profiling (PAP)

To identify polymyxin heteroresistance, the PAP experiment was conducted on the polymyxin-susceptive KP31157 with slight modifications [14]. Briefly, overnight cultures were diluted to 0.5 McFarland standard, streaked on MH agar plates with varying polymyxin concentrations (0–10 mg/L), and colonies were counted after overnight incubation at 37℃.

Pulsed-field gel electrophoresis (PFGE) analysis

Genetic relatedness of K. pneumoniae strains KP31157 and KP31311 was initially determined by XbaI-PFGE, following a modified protocol [15]. Clusters were assigned with a 2-band difference threshold, and the dendrogram was generated using BioNumerics v.7.6 software (Applied Maths, Marcy-l’Étoile, France) [16].

WGS and bioinformatics analysis

K. pneumoniae strains KP31157 and KP31311 underwent complete genome sequencing via Illumina NovaSeq PE150 (for both) and nanopore sequencing on MinION flow cells (for KP31311 only). Raw reads underwent quality filtering and adaptor removal. De novo assembly utilized SOAP de novo version 2.04 [17, 18], SPAdes v3.10.0 [19] AbySS 1.3.7 [20], and Unicycler [21], integrated with CISA 1.3 [22]. Gapclose v1.12 filled assembly gaps. Gene prediction was performed using Prokka v1.13 [23]. The antimicrobial resistance genes, multi-locus sequence types (MLST) and plasmid replicon were analyzed via the CGE server (https://cge.cbs.dtu.dk/services/). IS elements were identified using the ISfinder database [24]. The single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) were called using Snippy 3.2-dev (https://github.com/tseemann/snippy)), and then the SNPs were concatenated and aligned to construct the Maximum-Likelihood phylogenetic tree using RaxML v8.2.4. iTOL (https://itol.embl.de/) was used to graph the RaxML best-trees output.

Comparative genomic analysis

For comparative genomic analysis, the NCBI RefSeq database was searched for intact plasmids with similar sequences to the plasmid pKP31311-KPC-2. The alignment was performed via BLAST + 2.12.0., selecting the top 15 plasmids with the highest match. The BRIG and Snippy were employed for the cluster analysis. The plasmid replicon, antibiotic gene prediction, and IS elements detection were performed using CGE server (https://cge.cbs.dtu.dk/services/) and the ISFinder database, respectively. The geographical distribution of the strains containing the above plasmids was obtained from BioSample.

In vitro evolution of polymyxin resistance

In vitro evolution of polymyxin resistance was performed to investigate the potential mechanism of the shifting of polymyxin-sensitive K. pneumoniae to resistant isolate under antibiotic pressure. As shown in Fig. 1A, the overnight cultures of polymyxin-susceptive CRKP strain KP31157 incubated in Cation-adjusted Muller-Hinton Broth (CAMHB, Becton-Dickinson Company) were 1:100 diluted into fresh medium containing sub-inhibitory concentrations of related antibiotics, namely, minocycline, levofloxacin, polymyxin, ceftazidime-avibactam, meropenem, amikacin and sulfamethoxazole/trimethoprim, then incubated and mixed with 200 rpm shaking at 37℃. After diluted appropriately, spontaneous mutants were screened on Muller-Hinton Agar (MHA, OXOID) plates containing 2 mg/L of polymyxin or not, respectively, to obtain well-separated PR-CRKP colonies, and counted. mRNA expression of ISkpn14 elements flanked by DRs or not

The ISkpn14 sequences with or without DRs flanking were amplificated by PCR and then cloned into the apramycin-resistant vector pDK6 plasmid previously described [25]. The protocol is displayed in Fig. 1B.

Each overnight-cultured K. pneumoniae strains CDC29_DR (CDC29 with pDK6 harboring ISkpn14 with DR flanked), CDC29_NDR (CDC29 with pDK6 harboring ISkpn14 without DR flanked) and CDC29_ pDK6 (CDC29 with an empty vector pDK6) was incubated into 2 tubes of fresh medium containing 30 mg/L apramycin then incubated and mixed with 200 rpm shaking at 37℃ until OD600 reached 0.5, then the 1-mM IPTG (isopropyl-β-D-thiogalactopyranoside) was added in one tube, with the another as the blank control. Then they were cultured at 37℃ with 200 rpm shaking for 3 h. The total RNA was extracted and reverse-transcribed into cDNA. The qPCR was used to quantify gene expression with ropB gene used as the internal control. Primer sequences are listed in Supplementary Table 1 (S1). The fold changes in the expression of these genes were calculated by 2–ΔΔCT as previously described [26, 27]. All tests were repeated in triplicate.

Plasmids curing

Further, to investigate the origination of the ISkpn14 elements inserted into mgrB, the curing experiment of blaKPC−2-carrying plasmid in KP31157, which harbored ISkpn14 without DRs, was performed using sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS) [28]. Briefly, various concentrations of SDS (4%, 3%, 2%, 1%, and 0.5%) were used for plasmid curing. The cultures were incubated overnight with shaking at 27 °C. The culture was streaked onto LB agar, and the blaKPC−2-harboring plasmid cured colonies by using Xpert Carba-R assay (Cepheid, USA).

Statistical analysis

The IBM SPSS Statistics software package 22.0 (SPSS, Inc., Chicago, IL) was used to evaluate the relationship between antibiotic exposure and polymyxin resistance generation via conducting Student t-tests on continuous variables. A P value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

PAP

To rule out strain mixture, PAP tested polymyxin-sensitive KP31157; no growth on MHA (> 2 mg/L polymyxin) confirmed no heteroresistance.

PFGE and WGS analysis

PFGE indicated identical banding patterns (Fig. 2B), affirming strain homology. WGS and SNPs (only 12 SNPs detected) confirmed homogeneity, signifying a common ancestor and shared antibiotic-resistance genes.

Both strains were classified as ST11, harboring the IncFII (pHN7A8), IncR and ColRNAI plasmid, as shown in Table 2. The IncFII (pHN7A8) plasmid hosted the carbapenemase gene blaKPC−2 and other resistance genes, blaCTX−M−65, blaTEM−1B, blaSHV−12, aadA2, rmtB, fosA3, catA2, OqxA and OqxB,, shown in Fig. 2C. The pKP31311_KPC-2 was further analyzed, blaKPC−2, blaCTX−M−65, blaTEM−1B, blaSHV−12, aadA2, rmtB, fosA3 and catA2 were located on plasmid pKP31311_KPC, harboring 15 IS elements including ISkpn14 (1), ISkpn26 (1) and IS26 (9). Comparative genomics revealed the widespread distribution of plasmids resembling pKP31311_KPC-2 (shown in Fig. 2D), indicating its prevalence.

Polymyxin resistance genes

As summarized in Table 2, polymyxin-resistance-related genes were aligned with MGH 78578 via BLAST. The ISkpn14 element was inserted at the +116th nt of mgrB gene in KP31311. No mcr1-10 genes were identified.

Antibiotic induction experiment

The in vitro antibiotic-induced experiments, using minocycline and levofloxacin, were undertaken to confirm the hypothesis that antibiotic exposure might cause the insertional inactivation of the mgrB gene by ISkpn14 element. Minocycline and levofloxacin induction experiments were performed using the KP31157 K. pneumoniae strain, isolated before polymyxin-resistant KP31311. The results in Table 3 revealed a significant difference between the minocycline-treated group and the control group (PKP31157−MIN=0.0144 < 0.05), while levofloxacin inducing did not bring about a significant difference (PKP31157−LEV=0.2202 > 0.05), suggesting minocycline could accelerate the with-host evolution of polymyxin resistance. Furthermore, the polymyxin-resistant clones treated with minocycline were selected and amplified by PCR with the primers mgrB-ext-F/R. The PCR products (approximately 500 bp) were analyzed with agarose gel electrophoresis. We distinguished them as insertion and non-insertion in line with the length of mgrB gene. The fragments > 500 bp in length inserted by IS elements were screened for sanger sequences. Consequently, the insertion was detected to occur in approximately half of the minocycline-treated group (13/30, KP31157-M = 43.33%). The IS types were identified via Sanger sequences. The distribution of IS elements is shown in Fig. 3A. The ISkpn26 (61.54%, 8/13) elements transited most frequently, followed by ISkpn14 (38.46%, 5/13), indicating that minocycline treatment could promote the IS transition. Meanwhile, we are concerned about whether other antibiotics could induce the polymyxin resistance mediated by IS-inserted mgrB gene disruption. Polymyxin, ceftazidime-avibactam, meropenem, and amikacin induction experiments were applied to KP31157. After overnight inducing, the clones on MHA with or without polymyxin were counted with the RRT calculating. The RRTs in polymyxin/tigecycline-treated groups were significantly different from those in the control group (PKP31157−PB=0.0056, PKP31157−TGC=0.0149), while the ceftazidime-avibactam, meropenem, amikacin, and sulfamethoxazole/trimethoprim treated group did not significantly influence the polymyxin resistance (PKP31157−CZA=0.3143, PKP31157−MEM=0.4055, PKP31157−AK=0.4862, PKP31157-SXT/TMP=0.1179). Based on the above results, it was deemed that KP31157 was prone to developing polymyxin resistance after exposure to polymyxin and tigecycline. The resistant clones exposed to polymyxin and tigecycline were amplified by PCR and agarose gel electrophoresis was analyzed, showing 20.00% (6/30) and 56.67% (17/30) mgrB-inserting strains with the tigecycline and polymyxin induction, respectively. The IS types were identified by sanger sequence with the ISkpn26 inserted into mgrB most frequently with the tigecycline (100%, 6/6) and polymyxin (70.59%, 12/17). The ISkpn14 was more prevalent in the polymyxin-treated group (29.41%, 5/17). The results are illustrated in Fig. 3A.

IS location analysis by comparative genomic analysis

We are concerned about where the ISkpn14 inserted into mgrB gene in polymyxin-resistant K. pneumoniae came from.

WGS revealed that KP31311 strains harbored 4 ISkpn14 elements flanked by DRs on the chromosome, and 1 on the IncFII/R plasmid that also carried blaKPC−2 gene, lacking DR flanking at both ends of ISkpn14, as shown in Fig. 3B. Additionally, 3 ISkpn26 elements, consistently inserted into mgrB in the above antibiotic induction experiment, were flanked by DR on the chromosome, while 1 ISkpn26 on the plasmid was not flanked by DR. This suggested a potential correlation between IS insertion into mgrB and the presence or absence of DR.

mRNA expression and induced experiment of ISkpn14 flanked by DR or not

Therefore, to test whether the ISkpn14 element flanked by DR was easily activated or not, the plasmid vectors carrying ISkpn14 with and without DRs flanked were constructed. Afterwards, mRNA expression experiments were conducted on CDC29_DR, CDC29_NDR, and CDC29_pDK6 after 1mM IPTG induction. After 3-hour incubation, the mRNA expression levels were determined. Furthermore, the mRNA expression level of ISkpn14 without DR flanked was higher about 4 folds than that with flanking DRs, as depicted in Fig. 3C, suggesting that the ISkpn14 element without flanking DRs had higher transposable activity than that with.

The CDC29_DR and CDC29_NDR mentioned above were incubated overnight after adding IPTG. Then each was spread on the MH agar with and without polymyxin, respectively. The RRT, calculated and shown in Table 3, indicated accelerated polymyxin resistance in CDC29_NDR compared to CDC29_DR, with statistical significance (p = 0.0005 < 0.05). Then polymyxin-resistant clones were selected and PCR was performed. There were 54.26% of clones having mgrB inserted among CDC29_NDR (51/94) and the mgrB genes in CDC29_DR were all intact (0%, 0/30). The IS-inserting segments were exclusively detected in the CDC29_NDR, hinting the IS without DR flanked was more activated than the IS carrying DR.

Plasmid-cured strain-induced experiment

We tried to explore whether the ISkpn14 element inserted in mgrB gene of KP31157 originated from the blaKPC−2-carrying plasmid or not, therefore, the plasmid curing experiment and subsequent polymyxin-induced experiment were performed. The ISkpn14 element without flanking DRs was only detected on the blaKPC−2-harboring plasmid as mentioned above. Therefore, to confirm its transposable activity, we cured the blaKPC−2-carrying plasmids in KP31157 followed by the polymyxin-induced experiment. The frequency of the emergence of polymyxin resistance in plasmid-cured K. pneumoniae significantly reduced (no isolate detected) compared to the K. pneumoniae strains carrying blaKPC−2-harboring plasmids before curing, demonstrating that the ISkpn14 elements that inserted into mgrB genes of polymyxin-resistant K. pneumoniae strains might originate from blaKPC−2-carrying plasmid.

Discussion

CRKP categorized as “most critical” by WHO (https://www.who.int/news/item/27-02-2017-who-publishes-list-of-bacteria-for-which-new-antibiotics-are-urgently-needed), poses urgent antibiotic challenges, having acquired resistance, including to polymyxins [29]. PR-CRKP pose a significant threat to severely ill patients, including immune deficiencies and metabolic diseases [30].

Tetracycline antibiotics may accelerate the development of polymyxin resistance

The evolution of polymyxin resistance in CRKP may be associated with other antibiotics, such as minocycline, exposure rather than polymyxin. The insertional inactivation of mgrB gene by IS elements was the main mechanism of polymyxin resistance while exposed to polymyxins [10] and tigecycline [11], the transposition of ISs from plasmids to chromosomal mgrB genes due to IS-mediated mgrB gene disruption [5, 31, 32]. However, a previous report showed that 20.1% (3/13) PR-CRKP was inserted by IS without polymyxins exposure [33]. Similarly, our patient had also no polymyxin/tigecycline exposure history, and only minocycline and levofloxacin were administered during polymyxin susceptibility phenotypic shifting. In line with our in vitro experiment, the evolution of polymyxin resistance might be related to minocycline rather than levofloxacin, through accelerating the frequencies of integration inactivation of mgrB by IS element. Tigecycline could induce the emergence of colistin resistance in CRKP, whose mgrB gene was inserted by ISkpn26 during the tigecycline treatment without the source of ISkpn26 clarified [11]. In the current study, the in vitro induced experiment revealed that the ISkpn14 can be inserted into mgrB coming from a blaKPC−2-carrying plasmid under the pressure of minocycline, whereas the exposure to levofloxacin, ceftazidime-avibactam, meropenem, amikacin and sulfamethoxazole/trimethoprim might not accelerate the emergence of polymyxin resistance.

The transposition of ISs from plasmids might preferentially insert into the specific mgrB regions. It was hypothesized that there were some insertion hotspots by IS elements on mgrB. A previous study in Brazil demonstrated that the mgrB was always inserted at the + 69th ~ + 77th nt and + 116th ~ + 118th nt, accounting for 64% (27/42) [34]. The IS102, ISkpn26 and IS903B are mainly inserted into the + 69th ~ + 77th sites of mgrB [34–37]. It was the same as our results, while it was concluded from an epidemic survey or the strains collected after the polymyxin operated in their studies. However, our study emphasized the transposition of IS due to the use of antibiotics except for polymyxin.

ISkpn14 without flanking DR exhibiting enhanced transposition activity

Furthermore, the ISkpn14 not flanked by DRs might transit more efficiently than ISkpn14 flanked. Sequencing revealed 5 ISkpn14 elements in KP31311, with all 4 on the chromosome flanked by DRs and only 1 on plasmid without DRs. DR was taken as the activity marker of IS insertion [38]. Therefore, it is possible that the ISkpn14 elements on chromosome, and that inserted into the mgrB gene, originated from the blaKPC−2-bearing plasmid.

ISkpn14-mediated insertion into mgrB leading to polymyxin resistance derived from blaKPC-carrying plasmid

To prove our hypothesis of blaKPC−2-bearing plasmids as donors for ISkpn14, the plasmid vectors carrying ISkpn14 elements with DR flanked or without were constructed These vectors were then electroporated into a polymyxin-susceptible strain. The polymyxin-induced experiment showed the insertional inactivation of mgrB gene significantly more frequently occurred in the K. pneumoniae strain carrying ISkpn14 without DR than that with DR. This observation aligns with previous reports and suggests that IS elements without DR flanking transfer more efficiently than those with DR-containing elements [39].

Moreover, it was further demonstrated that the ISkpn14 element inserted into mgrB might originate from the IncFII/R plasmid carrying blaKPC−2 through plasmid curing experiments and subsequent induced experiments. The results showed that the number of polymyxin-resistant strains significantly decreased (no polymyxin-resistant strain detected) compared to the blaKPC−2-carrying plasmid mentioned above. Taken together, our results revealed that the IS elements inserted into mgrB originated from the blaKPC−2-carrying plasmid.

Widespread global distribution of these plasmids

Previously, one bioinformatics analysis study showed the relationship between IS types that are frequently inserted into mgrB and the plasmids carrying carbapenemase genes, via blasting the IS sequences online and analyzing IS distribution [10]. ISkpn26 (n = 73/83, 87.9%) and ISkpn14 (n = 53/107, 49.5%) present in plasmids are endemic in China [10]. The mgrB-disrupting related insertion sequences were predominant among K. pneumonia, and plasmids carrying ISkpn14 and ISkpn26 harbored more carbapenemase genes, mainly blaKPC in China. IncR plasmids are the most commonly identified plasmid Inc groups from ISkpn14 carrying plasmids, suggesting these plasmid Inc groups are receptive toward IS element uptake and maintenance [10]. This phenomenon was confirmed in our study that ISkpn14 inserted into mgrB gene was derived from the blaKPC−2-carrying plasmid.

Strengths and limitations

Our study presents a novel investigation into the mechanisms underlying polymyxin resistance in Klebsiella pneumoniae, with a specific focus on the insertional inactivation of the mgrB gene by ISkpn14. Notably, this study firstly provided experimental evidence demonstrating that the insertion sequences originated from plasmids harboring carbapenemase genes, particularly blaKPC−2. Through comprehensive genomic analyses of two consecutively isolated clinical strains, we elucidated the role of antibiotic-induced transposition in the development of polymyxin resistance and its impact on mgrB. Additionally, it is imperative to emphasize that clinical antibiotics should be administered in adequate dosages and for appropriate durations to minimize the emergency of resistance.

Our study is limited by the small sample size, thus the conclusions should be interpreted with caution. Although experimental methods have validated the transposition and resistance mechanisms, the true dynamics within in vivo environments required further investigation.

In summary, this study reported 2 different PR-CRKPs respectively, were recovered from the same patient consecutively. Genetic and molecular analyses identified that the insertion of an ISkpn14 element into the mgrB gene was responsible for the acquisition of polymyxin resistance stemming from exposure to minocycline. It was proved by experiment directly that the ISkpn14 element was traced back to a blaKPC−2-carrying plasmid, suggesting its role in the transition from polymyxin-sensitive to resistant phenotypes. The IS element without direct repeat flanking exhibited higher transposition activity. The study emphasized the importance of careful antibiotic selection and dosage, and close surveillance of polymyxin susceptibility profiles during antibiotic administration.

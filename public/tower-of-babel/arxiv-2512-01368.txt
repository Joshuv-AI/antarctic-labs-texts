On the future cover of a sofic shift
Klaus Thomsen

Abstract
. The paper contains a new proof of the theorem by Krieger which establishes the canonicity of the future cover of a sofic shift. In addition the paper describes a method to produce new canonical covers from a given one, resulting in canonical covers related to, but generally different from the future cover.

1.
Introduction

A cover of a sofic shift
Y
is a subshift of finite type
X
together with a factor map
\pi:X\to Y
from
X
onto
Y
. Every sofic shift has a wealth of different covers, so the least one can do to simplify considerations regarding covers is to identify two covers
\pi:X\to Y
and
\pi^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to Y
of the same sofic shift
Y
when they are isomorphic in the sense that there is a conjugacy
\chi:X\to X^{\prime}
such that
\pi^{\prime}\circ\chi=\pi
. Recall that a subshift of finite type can be realized as the edge shift
X_{G}
of a finite directed graph
G
and that a labeling of the set of edges
E_{G}
is a map
L_{G}:E_{G}\to A
, where
A
is a finite set. If
Y=L_{G}(X_{G})
, where
L_{G}:X_{G}\to Y\subseteq A^{\mathbb{Z}}
is the map obtained by reading the labels of elements in
X_{G}
, we say that
(G,L_{G})
is a presentation of
Y
. Then any cover of
Y
is isomorphic to one coming from a presentation of
Y
, see Theorem 3.2.1 in
[
LM
]
. But even after having reduced covers to labeled graphs, every sofic shift has an uncanny host of different presentations. Fortunately the future cover of Wolfgang Krieger,
[
Kr1
]
, and its associated labeled graph which we denote by
(\mathbb{K}(Y),L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)})
, stands out because it is canonical in the sense that a conjugacy
\psi:Y\to Z
of sofic shifts lifts to a conjugacy
\psi_{\mathbb{K}}:X_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}\to X_{\mathbb{K}(Z)}
such that

\textstyle{X_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{\mathbb{K}}}
\textstyle{X_{\mathbb{K}(Z)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{L_{\mathbb{K}(Z)}}
\textstyle{Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi}
\textstyle{Z}

commutes. Thus the subshift of finite type
X_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}
and the factor map
L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}
are both conjugacy invariants for
Y
. The same is of course also true for any other cover which is canonical in the same way. It must be emphasized that also components of a canonical cover that are not of maximal entropy as well as the wandering parts of the cover, which often arise even when
Y
is mixing, also give rise to conjugacy invariants for
Y
. This observation is the main motivation for the work and the results that are presented below.

We start by giving a new proof of Krieger’s theorem. While his proof is a tour de force in handling sliding block codes, the proof given here is more conceptual and leads to an abstract characterization of the future cover analogous to the abstract characterization of the canonical cover of an irreducible sofic shift known as the minimal right-resolving cover or Fischer cover,
[
F
]
. Alternative proofs of parts of Krieger’s theorem have been given before; first by Boyle, Kitchens and Marcus in
[
BKM
]
and later by Nasu in
[
N
]
. The proof presented here is also different from those.

The main new result of the paper is a theorem which out of a given canonical cover produces a new one. To appreciate this, recall that Krieger proved the uniqueness of the conjugacy
\psi_{\mathbb{K}}
in the diagram above. Without this additional requirement it is obvious that a canonical cover is not unique up to isomorphism; for example the disjoint union of two copies of the future cover will again be canonical in the sense used so far, but not with unique lifts of conjugacies. In the following we shall therefore refer to a construction of covers as
weakly canonical
when it gives lifts of conjugacies, but possibly lifts that are not unique, while a construction which gives rise to unique lifts of conjugacies, as the future cover does, will be called a
strongly canonical
cover. The main new result of the paper, Theorem
4.6
, describes a method to produce a strongly canonical cover out of a given weakly canonical one.

An important point concerning the construction is that it produces new strongly canonical covers of sofic shifts. Up to now only two strongly canonical covers for sofic shifts have been known; at least when we for simplicity ignore the doubling coming from the passage from right-resolving to left-resolving covers that can be achieved by ’time-reversal’. Besides the future cover it is the minimal right-resolving cover, and only for irreducible sofic shifts. It is therefore interesting to compare the strongly canonical covers that can be produced using Theorem
4.6
with the future cover and, in the irreducible case, the minimal right-resolving cover. For this we introduce a method to find the future cover of a sofic shift starting from an arbitrary given presentation. This is based on a variation of the so-called subset construction described in §3.3 of
[
LM
]
. More specifically, it is shown that a restriction to a subset of the vertexes in the subset construction, applied to an arbitrary presentation of the sofic shift, gives rise to a labeled graph whose merged graph is isomorphic to the future cover. Thus the future cover is the factor obtained from a subgraph of the subset construction by identifying vertexes with the same follower set. This fact is used to produce the examples occurring in the paper, and makes it easy to show by examples that the new strongly canonical covers that can be obtained from Theorem
4.6
in general are different both from the future cover, and in the irreducible case, also the minimal right-resolving cover.

2.1.
Setup and notation

Let

A closed subset

The set

with the property that
a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n}=x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}

The symbols

to finite or infinite paths in the natural way. In particular, when

\displaystyle L_{G}(x):=\left(L_{G}(e_{i})\right)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}=\cdots L_{G}(e_{-3})L_{G}(e_{-2})L_{G}(e_{-1})L_{G}(e_{0})L_{G}(e_{1})L_{G}(e_{2})L_{G}(e_{3})\cdots

2.2.
Regular vertexes and rays

Let
(H,L_{H})
be a labeled graph and
Y=L_{H}(X_{H})
the sofic shift it presents. A vertex
v\in V_{H}
is
regular
when there is an element
z\in X_{H}
such that
t_{H}(z_{(-\infty,-1]})=v
and

(1)

\left\{L_{H}(x):\ x\in X_{H}{[0,\infty)},\ s_{H}(x)=v\right\}=\left\{y\in Y[0,\infty):\ L_{H}(z_{(-\infty,-1]})y\in Y\right\}.

For any element
z\in X_{H}
with
t_{H}(z_{(-\infty,-1]})=v
, the inclusion

\left\{L_{H}(x):\ x\in X_{H}{[0,\infty)},\ s_{H}(x)=v\right\}\subseteq\left\{y\in Y[0,\infty):\ L_{H}(z_{(-\infty,-1]})y\in Y\right\}

always holds, so the real condition in (
1
) is that the opposite inclusion,

\left\{L_{H}(x):\ x\in X_{H}{[0,\infty)},\ s_{H}(x)=v\right\}\supseteq\left\{y\in Y[0,\infty):\ L_{H}(z_{(-\infty,-1]})y\in Y\right\},

also holds.

In order to work with regularity of vertexes we introduce the following notation. Given an element
y\in Y
, set

(2)

F(y):=\left\{w\in Y[0,\infty):\ y_{(-\infty,-1]}w\in Y\right\},

and given a vertex
v\in V_{H}
, set

f_{H}(v):=\left\{L_{H}(x):\ x\in X_{H}[0,\infty),\ s_{H}(x)=v\right\}.

Then
v\in V_{H}
is regular when there is an element
z\in X_{H}
such that
t_{H}(z_{(-\infty,-1]})=v
and

(3)

f_{H}(v)=F(L_{H}(z)).

The set
f_{H}(v)
will be called the
follower set
of
v
and
F(y)
the
follower set
of
y
.

.

Set
\gamma^{\prime}:=z_{(-\infty,-1]}\gamma

\gamma^{\prime}=z^{\prime}_{(-\infty,-1]}
for some

with

2.3.
The future cover and Krieger’s theorem

Let
A
be a finite set and

Y\subseteq A^{\mathbb{Z}}
a sofic subshift. To define the future cover of
Y
we use the following notation. When
D\subseteq A^{\mathbb{N}}
is a subset of
A^{\mathbb{N}}
and
a\in A
, we let
\frac{D}{a}
denote the set

\frac{D}{a}:=\left\{x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}\cdots\in A^{\mathbb{N}}:\ x_{0}x_{1}x_{2}\cdots\in D,\ x_{0}=a\right\}=\left\{x\in A^{\mathbb{N}}:\ ax\in D\right\};

i.e.
\frac{D}{a}
is the set of elements of
A^{\mathbb{N}}
that are obtained from the elements of
D
that start with
a
by deleting the first coordinate. More generally, when
w\in A^{m}
, we set

\frac{D}{w}=\left\{x\in A^{\mathbb{N}}:\ wx\in D\right\}.

Let
y\in Y
. Recall from (
2
) that

F(y)=\left\{z_{[0,\infty)}:\ z\in Y,\ z_{j}=y_{j},\ j\leq-1\right\}\subseteq A^{\mathbb{N}}.

Set

V_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}:=\left\{F(y):\ y\in Y\right\}.

This is a finite set because
Y
is sofic.
V_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}
is the set of vertexes in a labeled graph
(\mathbb{K}(Y),L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)})
where there is a labeled arrow

F(y)\overset{a}{\to}F(z)

from
F(y)
to
F(z)
in
\mathbb{K}(Y)
precisely when

F(z)=\frac{F(y)}{a}
.
The labeled graph
(\mathbb{K}(Y),L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)})
is then clearly right-resolving. Since

F(\sigma^{i+1}(y))=\frac{F(\sigma^{i}(y))}{y_{i}},

there is an element
\alpha_{Y}(y)\in X_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}
determined by the two conditions:

a)

s_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}(\alpha_{Y}(y)_{i})=F(\sigma^{i}(y)),\ i\in\mathbb{Z}
, and

b)

L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}(\alpha_{Y}(y))=y
.

The map
\alpha_{Y}:Y\to X_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}
commutes with the shift, i.e.
\alpha_{Y}\circ\sigma=\sigma\circ\alpha_{Y}
, but it is not continuous.

Lemma 2.9
.

L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}(X_{\mathbb{K}(Y)})=Y
.

Proof.

Let
x=(x_{i})_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\in X_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}
and consider an
N\in\mathbb{N}
. By definition
s_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}(x_{-N})=F(y)
for some
y\in Y
and

L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}(x_{-N})L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}(x_{-N+1})L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}(x_{-N+2})\cdots\ \ \in F(y).

In particular,

L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}(x_{-N})L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}(x_{-N+1})\cdots L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}(x_{N}))\in\mathbb{W}(Y)

for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
, implying that
L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}(X_{\mathbb{K}(Y)})\subseteq Y
. The reversed inclusion,
Y\subseteq L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}(X_{\mathbb{K}(Y)})
, follows from the observation b) above.
∎

Given an element

3.1.
A universal property of the future cover

.

Let

be a labeled graph. A subset

of

2.18
can then be improved to the following.

.

Assume that

. It follows that there is a labeled graph homomorphism

. If, furthermore,

onto the hereditary labeled subgraph

By Lemma

and its proof there is a labeled-graph homomorphism

Let

4.1.
Subset constructions

In this section by a
dynamical system
we mean a pair
(X,\phi)
where
X
is a compact metrizable space and
\phi:X\to X
is a homeomorphism of
X
onto itself.

Let
(X,\phi)
be a dynamical system and
d
a metric for the topology of
X
. The set of non-empty compact subsets
K
of
X
can be equipped with a metric, the
Hausdorff metric

d_{Haus}
, defined by

d_{Haus}(K,K^{\prime}):=\max\left\{\sup_{x\in K}\left(\inf_{y\in K^{\prime}}d(x,y)\right),\ \sup_{y\in K^{\prime}}\left(\inf_{x\in K}d(x,y)\right)\right\}.

This turns the set of non-empty compact subsets of
X
into a compact metric space

(X^{\#},d_{Haus}).

See for example Section 2.6 in
[
B
]
.

When
(X,d)
and
(X^{\prime},d^{\prime})
are two compact metric spaces and
\psi:X\to X^{\prime}
is a continuous map we get a continuous map
\psi^{\#}:X^{\#}\to X^{\prime\#}
defined such that

\psi^{\#}(K):=\psi(K)

for
K\in X^{\#}
. In particular, we get from
(X,\phi)
a new dynamical system
(X^{\#},\phi^{\#})
. More generally when
(X,\phi)
and
(Y,\phi^{\prime})
are dynamical systems and
\pi:X\to Y
is a map of dynamical systems in the sense that
\pi\circ\phi=\phi^{\prime}\circ\pi
, the set

X^{\pi}:=\left\{K\in X^{\#}:\ \pi(x)=\pi(y)\ \forall x,y\in K\right\}

is a closed
\phi^{\#}
-invariant subset of
X^{\#}
and
(X^{\pi},\phi^{\#})
is also a dynamical system. When
\pi
is surjective and hence a factor map, then so is
\pi^{\#}:(X^{\pi},\phi^{\#})\to(Y,\phi^{\prime})
.

The following is now a straightforward observation.

5.
Constructing the future cover

In this section we present a method to construct the future cover of a sofic shift from any given presentation of it.

5.1.
On the merging of a labeled graph

Let
(H,L_{H})
be a labeled graph. The
merged graph

([H],L_{[H]})
of
(H,L_{H})
is then defined as follows, cf. page 78 of
[
LM
]
. The set of vertexes
V_{[H]}
is the set

V_{[H]}:=\left\{f_{H}(v):\ v\in V_{H}\right\}

and for
a\in A
there is a labeled arrow
f_{H}(v)\overset{a}{\to}f_{H}(w)
when there is are vertexes
v^{\prime},w^{\prime}\in V_{H}
such that
f_{H}(v^{\prime})=f_{H}(v),\ f_{H}(w^{\prime})=f_{H}(w)
and there is a labeled arrow
v^{\prime}\overset{a}{\to}w^{\prime}
in
H
. When
(H,L_{H})
is right-resolving we can define a map
f_{H}:E_{H}\to E_{[H]}
such that
f_{H}(e)
is the arrow
f_{H}(e)\in E_{[H]}
with the properties

•

s_{[H]}(f_{H}(e))=f_{H}\left(s_{H}(e)\right)
, and

•

L_{[H]}(e)=L_{H}(e)
.

Then
f_{H}:H\to[H]
is a labeled-graphs homomorphism.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume that
(H,L_{H})
is right-resolving. Let
v,w\in V_{H}
. There is a labeled arrow
f_{H}(v)\overset{a}{\to}f_{H}(w)
in
[H]
if and only if there is a labeled arrow
v\overset{a}{\to}u
in
H
such that
f_{H}(u)=f_{H}(w)
.

Proof.

Assume that
f_{H}(v)\overset{a}{\to}f_{H}(w)
in
[H]
and let
v_{1},w_{1}\in V_{H}
be such that
v_{1}\overset{a}{\to}w_{1}
in
H
,
f_{H}(v_{1})=f_{H}(v)
and
f_{H}(w_{1})=f_{H}(w)
. Since
f_{H}(v)=f_{H}(v_{1})
there is an arrow
e
in
E_{H}
labeled
a
which leaves
v
. Set
u:=t_{H}(e)
. By using that
(H,L_{H})
is right-resolving we find then that

f_{H}(u)=\frac{f_{H}(v)}{a}=\frac{f_{H}(v_{1})}{a}=f_{H}(w_{1}).

This establishes the ’only if’ part. The ’if’ part is trivial.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume that
(H,L_{H})
is right-resolving. Then
([H],L_{[H]})
is right-resolving and follower-separated, and there is a labeled-graph homomorphism
f_{H}:H\to[H]
such that
f_{H}:X_{H}\to X_{[H]}
is a factor map.

Proof.

([H],L_{[H]})
is right-resolving by Lemma 3.3.8 of
[
LM
]
. To see that

is surjective, consider a finite path

(17)

f_{H}(v_{1})\overset{a_{1}}{\to}f_{H}(v_{2})\overset{a_{2}}{\to}f_{H}(v_{3})\overset{a_{3}}{\to}\cdots\cdots\overset{a_{n-1}}{\to}f_{H}(v_{n})

in
[H]
. Repeated applications of Lemma
5.1
give a path

v_{1}\overset{a_{1}}{\to}u_{2}\overset{a_{2}}{\to}u_{3}\overset{a_{3}}{\to}\cdots\cdots\overset{a_{n-1}}{\to}u_{n}

in

which is mapped to (

) under

. The surjectivity of

follows therefore from compactness of

. The argument shows also that the follower set of

in
([H],L_{[H]})
is the same as the follower set of

in
(H,L_{H})
; in symbols

f_{[H]}(f_{H}(v_{1}))=f_{H}(v_{1}).

In particular,
([H],L_{[H]})
is follower-separated
∎

We note that the morphism

is injective on

if and only if it is an isomorphism of labeled graphs, and this happens if and only if

5.2.
A cover of the future cover

Let
(G,L_{G})
be a labeled graph and
Y=L_{G}(X_{G})
the sofic shift presented by
(G,L_{G})
. When
D\subseteq V_{G}
and

, set

[D,a]:=\left\{t_{G}(e):\ e\in E_{G},\ s_{G}(e)\in D,\ L_{G}(e)=a\right\};

of non-empty subsets of

of

F(y)=f_{\underline{G}}(D^{y})

Proof.

It follows from the definitions that

\displaystyle f_{\underline{G}}(D^{y})=\left\{L_{\underline{G}}(x):\ x\in X_{\underline{G}}[0,\infty),\ s_{\underline{G}}(x)=D^{y}\right\}

Lemma 5.4
.

Let

. There is a labeled arrow

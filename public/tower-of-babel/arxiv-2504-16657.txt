A new characterization of Sobolev spaces on Lipschitz differentiability spaces
Bang-Xian Han, Zhe-Feng Xu, Zhuonan Zhu

Abstract




We prove a new characterization of metric Sobolev spaces, in the spirit of Brezis–Van Schaftingen–Yung’s asymptotic formula. A new feature of our work is that we do not need Poincaré inequality which is a common tool in the literature. Another new feature is that we find a direct link between Brezis–Van Schaftingen–Yung’s asymptotic formula and Cheeger’s Lipschitz differentiability.

Keywords
: Sobolev space, metric measure space, Lipschitz differentiability space, asymptotic formula

1
Introduction

Recently, Brezis, Van Schaftingen and Yung
[
BVSY21
]
discover an asymptotic formula for Sobolev norms:

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}\mathcal{L}^{2N}(E^{\mathbb{R}^{N}}_{\lambda,u})=\frac{k_{p,N}}{N}\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}},\quad\forall u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(\mathrm{BVY})

where

E^{\mathbb{R}^{N}}_{\lambda,u}=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}:x\neq y,~|u(x)-u(y)|\geq\lambda|x-y|^{\frac{N}{p}+1}\right\},

and
k_{p,N}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|e\cdot w|^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}w
,
e\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
. As a variant of the well-known asymptotic formula of Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu (cf.
[
BBM01
]
), this new formula has received a lot of attention since its discovery and has been generalized to different settings such as ball Banach function spaces
[
DLY+23
]
, Orlicz spaces
[
KMS23
,
IS24
]
, convex bodies
[
GH23
]
, Triebel Lizorkin spaces
[
BSY23
]
, metric measure spaces
[
DLY+22
]
, and one parameter families of operators
[
DM22
]
.

Naturally, one may ask whether this formula is valid on more general spaces or not. A more interesting question is,
once we have such a formula, what can we say about the structure of the underling space?

Inspired by recent papers
[
DMS19
]
and
[
DLY+22
]
, in which the authors proved a characterization of Sobolev spaces in PI spaces (i.e. it is doubling and it satisfies a
p
-Poincaré inequality) in the spirit of Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu’s formula and Brezis–Van Schaftingen–Yung’s formula respectively, we can partially answer the above questions and reads as follows. Denote by
{\rm{lip}}(u)
and
{\rm{Lip}}(u)
the lower and upper pointwise Lipschitz constants. We refer to Section
2
for other missing definitions.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
3.1
, Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
3.6
)
.

Let
p\geq 1
and
(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space equipped with a
\beta
-doubling measure
\mathfrak{m}
. Denote by
N
the doubling dimension
\frac{\log\beta}{\log 2}
. Assume there are constants
b>a>0
, such that the lower and upper density functions satisfy
a\leq\theta_{N}^{-}\leq\theta_{N}^{+}\leq b
. Then there exist universal constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\varliminf_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}(\mathfrak{m}\times\mathfrak{m})({E_{\lambda,u}})\geq C_{1}\int_{X}\frac{\big({\rm{lip}}(u)(x)\big)^{N+p}}{\big({\rm{Lip}}(u)(x)\big)^{N}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x),

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}(\mathfrak{m}\times\mathfrak{m})({E_{\lambda,u}})\leq C_{2}\int_{X}\big({\rm{Lip}}(u)(x)\big)^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x)

for any
u\in{\rm{Lip}}_{b}(X,{\mathrm{d}})
, where

E_{\lambda,u}=\left\{(x,y)\in X\times X:x\neq y,\,|u(x)-u(y)|\geq\lambda\big({\mathrm{d}}(x,y)\big)^{\frac{N}{p}+1}\right\}.

(1.1)

Furthermore, the parameter
N
appearing in (
1.1
) cannot be replaced by any other constant.

In the seminal paper
[
Che99
]
, Cheeger studied the differentiability of Lipschitz function in the setting of metric measure space. In PI spaces, he proved that any Lipschitz function
u
is differentiable and
{\rm{lip}}(u)={\rm{Lip}}(u)
almost everywhere. In the next decade, this “generalized Rademacher’s theorem” was further studied by Keith
[
Kei04
]
and several authors, and was formed into a concept, called
Lipschitz differentiability space
named by Bate
[
Bat15
]
. A characterization of Lipschitz differentiability spaces, obtained by Keith and Bate, tells us that
(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
is a Lipschitz differentiability space if and only if
\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(u)
and
{\rm lip}(u)
are comparable for any Lipschitz function
u
.

Surprisingly, recently Bate, Eriksson-Bique and Soultanis
[
BEBS24
]
prove that a metric measure space
(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
is a Lipschitz differentiability space if and only if for any
u\in{\rm{Lip}}(X,{\mathrm{d}})
,

{\rm{Lip}}(u)={\rm lip}(u)=|{\mathrm{D}}u|_{*}~~~~\mathfrak{m}\text{-a.e.}

where
|{\mathrm{D}}u|_{*}
denotes the minimal
*
-upper gradient of
u
introduced in
[
BEBS24
]
. Applying their theorem to Lipschitz differentiability spaces, we get the following characterization of Sobolev norms. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first asymptotic formula of this type without Poincaré inequality (we refer to
[
DMS19
]
for a characterization of Sobolev spaces in PI spaces in the spirit of the Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu and Nguyen).

Corollary 1.2

(Corollary
3.3
)
.

Let
(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
be a Lipschitz differentiability space. Using
the same notations and assumptions as Theorem
1.1
, we have

\displaystyle\varliminf_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}(\mathfrak{m}\times\mathfrak{m})({E_{\lambda,u}})\geq C_{1}\||{\mathrm{D}}u|_{*}\|_{L^{p}(X,\mathfrak{m})}^{p},

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}(\mathfrak{m}\times\mathfrak{m})({E_{\lambda,u}})\leq C_{2}\||{\mathrm{D}}u|_{*}\|_{L^{p}(X,\mathfrak{m})}^{p}

for any
u\in{\rm{Lip}}_{b}(X,{\mathrm{d}})
.

In
[
Han24
]
, via a metric geometry approach (after Górny
[
Gór22
]
), the first author finds a unified proof to Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu’s asymptotic formulas on some important spaces, including finite dimensional Banach spaces and equi-regular sub-Riemannian manifolds. Concerning the asymptotic formula

(
BVY
)

, such geometric approach still works. We remark that one can also prove more asymptotic formulas, by replacing
E_{\lambda,u}
by
\left\{(x,y)\in X\times X:x\neq y,\,|u(x)-u(y)|\geq\rho_{\lambda}(x,y)\right\}
for suitable mollifiers
\{\rho_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda>0}
.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
3.5
)
.

Let
p>1,N\in\mathbb{N}
and
(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space satisfying the hypothesis in Proposition
3.4
. Then for any
u\in{{\rm{Lip}}_{b}}(X,{\mathrm{d}})
, we have

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}(\mathfrak{m}\times\mathfrak{m})({E_{\lambda,u}})=\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{K_{p,\mathfrak{C}}},

where

\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{K_{p,\mathfrak{C}}}=\int_{X}\int_{S^{\mathfrak{C}}_{1}}\frac{|u_{0,x}(w)|^{p}}{N}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}^{+}_{\mathfrak{C}}(w){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x),

(1.2)

where
S^{\mathfrak{C}}_{1}
is the unit ball centered at the origin in the tangent space
\mathfrak{C}
, and
u_{0,x}
denotes the uniform limit of the rescaling functions
u_{\delta,x}(y):=\frac{u(y)-u(x)}{\delta},\delta>0
.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall some
preliminaries on calculus on metric measure spaces. In Section
3
, we prove our characterization of metric Sobolev spaces.

2
Preliminaries

In this paper,
(X,{\mathrm{d}})
is a Polish space, and
\mathfrak{m}
is a
\sigma
-finite Radon measure on
X
with full support. The triple
(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
is called a metric measure space.

Given a function
u:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the upper pointwise Lipschitz constant Lip
(u)(x):X\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
is defined as

\text{Lip}(u)(x):=\varlimsup_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{y\in B_{r}(x)}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{r}=\varlimsup_{y\rightarrow x}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{{\mathrm{d}}(x,y)},

and the lower pointwise Lipschitz constant lip
(u)(x):X\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
is defined as

\text{lip}(u)(x):=\varliminf_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{y\in B_{r}(x)}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{r},

where
B_{r}(x):=\{y\in X:{\mathrm{d}}(x,y)<r\}
. The (global) Lipschitz constant is defined as

\text{\bf Lip}(u):=\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{{\mathrm{d}}(x,y)}.

If
{\bf Lip}(u)<+\infty
, we call
u
a Lipschitz function and write
u\in\text{Lip}(X,{\mathrm{d}})
. We denote by Lip
{}_{b}(X,{\mathrm{d}})
the collection of Lipschitz functions with bounded support.
The following property provides an alternative definition of
\text{Lip}(u)(x)
and
\text{lip}(u)(x)
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
Kei04
]
)
.

For any
u\in{\rm{Lip}}(X,{\mathrm{d}})
and
r>0
, define

l_{r}u(x)=\inf_{0<s\leq r}\sup_{y\in B_{s}(x)}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{s},

and

L_{r}u(x)=\sup_{0<s\leq r}\sup_{y\in B_{s}(x)}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{s}.

Then for any
x\in X
,

{\rm{lip}}(u)(x)=\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}{l_{r}u(x)}\,\quad{\rm{and}}\,\quad{\rm{Lip}}(u)(x)=\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}{L_{r}u(x)}.

In particular,
{\rm{lip}}(u)
and
{\rm{Lip}}(u)
are Borel measurable.

Definition 2.2

(Doubling measure)
.

We say
\mathfrak{m}
is
\beta
-doubling if there exists a constant
\beta>1
, called doubling constant, such that for any
x\in\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(\mathfrak{m})
and
r>0
, it holds

\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{2r}(x)\big)\leq\beta\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r}(x)\big).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a
\beta
-doubling measure. Then for any
x_{0}\in X
,
r_{0}>0
,
x\in B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
, we have the following sharp estimate:

\frac{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r}(x)\big)}{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})\big)}\geq\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{\frac{\log\beta}{\log 2}},\quad\quad\forall r\in(0,r_{0}).

In particular, the exponent
\frac{\log\beta}{\log 2}
is optimal and we call it doubling dimension.

Definition 2.4

(Density functions)
.

For any
N>0
, the upper and lower density functions
\theta_{N}^{\pm}(x):X\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
are defined as

\theta_{N}^{+}(x):=\varlimsup_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r}(x)\big)}{r^{N}}\quad\text{and}\quad\theta_{N}^{-}(x):=\varliminf_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r}(x)\big)}{r^{N}}.

On a metric measure space, we can also talk about the differentiability of Lipschitz functions. Recall the following definition proposed by Cheeger
[
Che99
]
:

Definition 2.5
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
(X,{\mathrm{d}})
be a metric space. We say a Borel set
U\subset X
and a Lipschitz function
\phi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
form a chart
(U,\phi)
of dimension
n
and that a function
u:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is differentiable at
x_{0}\in U
with respect to
(U,\phi)
if there exists a unique
{\mathrm{d}}_{x_{0}}u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\varlimsup_{x\rightarrow x_{0}}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(x_{0})-{\mathrm{d}}_{x_{0}}u\cdot\big(\phi(x)-\phi(x_{0})\big)\right|}{{\mathrm{d}}(x,x_{0})}=0.

Definition 2.6

(Lipschitz differentiability space)
.

We say a metric measure space
(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
is a Lipschitz differentiability space if there exists a countable decomposition of
X
into charts such that any
u\in\text{Lip}(X,{\mathrm{d}})
is differentiable at almost every point of every chart.

It is proved by Cheeger
[
Che99
]
that PI spaces are Lipschitz differentiability spaces. Furthermore, Lipschitz differentiability can be characterized by the following
``\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits
-
{\rm lip}
condition”, introduced by Keith in
[
Kei04
]
.

Definition 2.7
.

We say a metric measure space
(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
satisfies
\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits
-
{\rm lip}
condition if there exists a countable Borel decomposition
X=\bigcup_{i}X_{i}
and for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\delta_{i}>0
such that for any
u\in\text{Lip}(X,{\mathrm{d}})
, we have

\text{Lip}(u)(x)\leq\delta_{i}\text{lip}(u)(x),\quad\text{for}\,\,\mathfrak{m}\text{-a.e.}~\,x\in X_{i}.

Theorem 2.8

(
[
Bat15
,
Gon12
,
Kei04
]
)
.

If
(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
is a metric measure space equipped with a doubling measure
\mathfrak{m}
, then
(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
is a Lipschitz differentiability space if and only if it satisfies the
\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits
-
{\rm lip}
condition.

Surprisingly, the inequality in
\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits
-
{\rm lip}
condition can be improved to an equality.
For any
u\in\text{Lip}(X,{\mathrm{d}})
, there exists a minimal
*
-upper gradient, denoted by
|{\mathrm{D}}u|_{*}
. We refer to
[
BEBS24
, Section
2
]
(and
[
Sch16
,
CKS16
]
) for details.

Theorem 2.9

(
[
BEBS24
]
)
.

(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
is a Lipschitz differentiability space if and only if for any
u\in{\rm{Lip}}(X,{\mathrm{d}})
,

{\rm{Lip}}(u)={\rm lip}(u)=|{\mathrm{D}}u|_{*},~~\,\,\mathfrak{m}\text{-a.e.}.

Remark 2.10
.

There are some different notions about weak gradient in metric measure setting. In general, we can not replace
|{\mathrm{D}}u|_{*}
by Cheeger’s minimal
p
-weak upper gradient
|{\mathrm{D}}u|_{p}
in the above theorem. Consider a Lipschitz differentiability space
(K,|\cdot|,\mathcal{L}^{n}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{K}$})
, where
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a Cantor set with
\mathcal{L}^{n}(K)>0
. In such a space,
|{\mathrm{D}}u|_{p}=0
for any
u\in{\rm{Lip}}_{b}(K)
and
p\geq 1
. We refer to
[
AGS13
,
HKST15
,
IPS22
]
for more discussions about this topic.

3
Main results

3.1
Lower bound estimate

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
p\geq 1
and
(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space equipped with a
\beta
-doubling measure
\mathfrak{m}
. Assume
\theta_{N}^{-}\geq a
for some
a>0
. Then for any
u\in{\rm{Lip}}_{b}(X,{\mathrm{d}})
, we have

\varliminf_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}(\mathfrak{m}\times\mathfrak{m})({E_{\lambda,u}})\geq C\int_{X}\frac{\big({\rm{lip}}(u)(x)\big)^{N+p}}{\big({\rm{Lip}}(u)(x)\big)^{N}}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x),

where
E_{\lambda,u}
is defined in
(
1.1
)
,
N=\frac{\log\beta}{\log 2}
,
C=C(\beta,a,p)>0
.

Proof.

For any
u\in{\rm{Lip}}_{b}(X,{\mathrm{d}})
, denote

L_{i}=\left\{x\in X:\frac{1}{2^{i+1}}{{\rm{Lip}}(u)}(x)<{\rm{lip}}(u)(x)\leq\frac{1}{2^{i}}{\rm{Lip}}(u)(x)\right\},\quad i\in\mathbb{N},

(3.1)

then
X=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}L_{i}\bigcup\{{\rm{lip}}(u)(x)=0\}
.
By Proposition
2.1
, for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
L_{i}
is Borel. We divide the proof into six steps.

Step 1:

Let
i_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\mathfrak{m}(L_{i_{0}})>0
. Given
0<\epsilon<\min\left\{\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{8T_{i_{0}}}\right)^{\frac{\log\beta}{\log 2}},\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{m}(L_{i_{0}})\right\}
satisfying

\mathfrak{m}\{x\in L_{i_{0}}:{\rm{lip}}(u)(x)>2^{i_{0}+3}\epsilon\}>\frac{2}{3}\mathfrak{m}(L_{i_{0}}),

(3.2)

where
T_{i_{0}}
depends only on
i_{0}
to be determined. We claim:

•

there is
K_{\epsilon}\subseteq L_{i_{0}}
such that

\mathfrak{m}(L_{i_{0}}\setminus K_{\epsilon})<\epsilon,

(3.3)

•

there is
r_{\epsilon}>0
such that for any
0<r\leq r_{\epsilon}
and
x\in K_{\epsilon}
,

{\rm{lip}}(u)(x)-\epsilon\leq\sup_{y\in B_{r}(x)}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{r}<2^{{i_{0}}+1}{\rm{lip}}(u)(x)+\epsilon,

(3.4)

•

for any
x,y\in K_{\epsilon}
with
{\mathrm{d}}(x,y)\leq r_{\epsilon}
,

\left|{\rm{lip}}(u)(x)-{\rm{lip}}(u)(y)\right|<\epsilon,

(3.5)

•

for any
x\in K_{\epsilon}
and
0<r\leq r_{\epsilon}
,

\frac{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r}(x)\cap K_{\epsilon}\big)}{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r}(x)\big)}>1-\epsilon.

(3.6)

Firstly, by Proposition
2.1
,
{\rm{lip}}(u)=\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}{l_{r}u}
pointwisely. By Egoroff’s theorem, there is
K^{1}_{\epsilon}\subseteq L_{i_{0}}
with
\mathfrak{m}\left({L_{i_{0}}\setminus K^{1}_{\epsilon}}\right)<\frac{\epsilon}{4}
such that
l_{r}u
converges to
{\rm{lip}}(u)
uniformly on
K^{1}_{\epsilon}
. So there is
r^{1}_{\epsilon}
such that for any
0<r\leq r^{1}_{\epsilon}
and
x\in K^{1}_{\epsilon}
, it holds

l_{r}u(x)=\inf_{0<s\leq r}\sup_{y\in B_{s}(x)}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{s}\geq{\rm{lip}}(u)(x)-\epsilon,

so that

\sup_{y\in B_{r}(x)}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{r}\geq{\rm{lip}}(u)(x)-\epsilon.

Similarly, there is
K^{2}_{\epsilon}\subseteq L_{i_{0}}
, such that
\mathfrak{m}\left({L_{i_{0}}\setminus K^{2}_{\epsilon}}\right)<\frac{\epsilon}{4}
and
L_{r}u
converges to
{\rm{Lip}}(u)
uniformly on
K^{2}_{\epsilon}
. So there is
r^{2}_{\epsilon}
such that for any
0<r\leq r^{2}_{\epsilon}
and
x\in K^{2}_{\epsilon}
, it holds

L_{r}u(x)=\sup_{0<s\leq r}\sup_{y\in B_{s}(x)}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{s}\leq{\rm{Lip}}(u)(x)+\epsilon.

Note that
x\in L_{i_{0}}
, we also have

\sup_{y\in B_{r}(x)}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{r}\leq{\rm{Lip}}(u)(x)+\epsilon<2^{{i_{0}}+1}{\rm{lip}}(u)(x)+\epsilon.

Next, by Lusin’s theorem, there exists a compact set
K^{3}_{\epsilon}\subseteq L_{i_{0}}
such that
\mathfrak{m}\left(L_{i_{0}}\setminus K^{3}_{\epsilon}\right)<\frac{\epsilon}{4}
and
{\rm{lip}}(u)
is continuous on
K^{3}_{\epsilon}
. By Heine–Cantor theorem, there is
r^{3}_{\epsilon}
such that for any
x,y\in K^{3}_{\epsilon}
with
{\mathrm{d}}(x,y)\leq r^{3}_{\epsilon}
, we have
|{\rm{lip}}(u)(x)-{\rm{lip}}(u)(y)|\leq\epsilon
.

By Lebesgue differentiation theorem, for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\in K^{3}_{\epsilon}
, we have

\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r}(x)\cap K^{3}_{\epsilon}\big)}{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r}(x)\big)}=1.

By Egoroff’s theorem again, there is
K^{4}_{\epsilon}\subseteq K^{3}_{\epsilon}\subseteq L_{i_{0}}
such that
\mathfrak{m}\left(K^{3}_{\epsilon}\setminus K^{4}_{\epsilon}\right)<\frac{\epsilon}{4}
and
\frac{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r}(x)\cap K^{3}_{\epsilon}\big)}{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r}(x)\big)}
converges to
1
uniformly on
K^{4}_{\epsilon}
. Thus there exists
r^{4}_{\epsilon}
such that for any
0<r\leq r^{4}_{\epsilon}
and
x\in K^{4}_{\epsilon}
, it holds

\frac{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r}(x)\cap K^{3}_{\epsilon}\big)}{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r}(x)\big)}\geq 1-\epsilon.

Choosing
r_{\epsilon}=\min\{r^{1}_{\epsilon},r^{2}_{\epsilon},r^{3}_{\epsilon},r^{4}_{\epsilon}\}
,
K_{\epsilon}=K^{1}_{\epsilon}\cap K^{2}_{\epsilon}\cap K^{3}_{\epsilon}\cap K^{4}_{\epsilon}
, we get the claim.

Step 2:

Denote

A_{\epsilon,i_{0}}=\left\{x\in K_{\epsilon}\subset L_{i_{0}}:{\rm{lip}}(u)(x)>2^{{i_{0}}+3}\epsilon\right\}.

(3.7)

We know that
A_{\epsilon,i_{0}}
is non-empty. Indeed, if
A_{\epsilon,i_{0}}
is empty, by the choice of
\epsilon
and (
3.3
), we have

\mathfrak{m}(\left\{x\in K_{\epsilon}\subset L_{i_{0}}:{\rm{lip}}(u)(x)\leq 2^{{i_{0}}+3}\epsilon\right\})=\mathfrak{m}(K_{\epsilon})>\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{m}(L_{i_{0}}),

which contradicts (
3.2
).

Fix
x\in A_{\epsilon,i_{0}}
and
0<r\leq r_{\epsilon}
.
By (
3.4
), there is
\bar{y}_{x,r}\in\overline{B_{\frac{r}{2}}(x)}
such that

\displaystyle|u(x)-u(\bar{y}_{x,r})|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{r}{2}\big({\rm{lip}}(u)(x)-\epsilon\big)\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{{i_{0}}+4}}\right){\rm{lip}}(u)(x)r.

(3.8)

Let
T_{i_{0}}>2
and consider geodesic balls
B_{\frac{r}{T_{i_{0}}}}({\bar{y}_{x,r}})
and
B_{\frac{r}{2T_{i_{0}}}}({\bar{y}_{x,r}})
.
Assume by contradiction that
B_{\frac{r}{T_{i_{0}}}}({\bar{y}_{x,r}})\setminus B_{\frac{r}{2T_{i_{0}}}}({\bar{y}_{x,r}})\subseteq K_{\epsilon}^{c}
. Note
B_{\frac{r}{T_{i_{0}}}}({\bar{y}_{x,r}})\subset B_{r}(x)
, by (
3.6
), we have

\frac{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{\frac{r}{T_{i_{0}}}}({\bar{y}_{x,r}})\setminus B_{\frac{r}{2T_{i_{0}}}}({\bar{y}_{x,r}})\big)}{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r}(x)\big)}\leq\frac{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r}(x)\cap K_{\epsilon}^{c}\big)}{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r}(x)\big)}\leq\epsilon.

However, there is
z\in B_{r}(x)
such that
{\mathrm{d}}(z,{\bar{y}_{x,r}})=\frac{3r}{4T_{i_{0}}}
and

B_{\frac{r}{8T_{i_{0}}}}(z)\subset B_{\frac{r}{T_{i_{0}}}}({\bar{y}_{x,r}})\setminus B_{\frac{r}{2T_{i_{0}}}}({\bar{y}_{x,r}})
. By Lemma
2.3
, we get

\frac{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{\frac{r}{T_{i_{0}}}}({\bar{y}_{x,r}})\setminus B_{\frac{r}{2T_{i_{0}}}}({\bar{y}_{x,r}})\big)}{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r}(x)\big)}\geq\frac{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{\frac{r}{8T_{i_{0}}}}(z)\big)}{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r}(x)\big)}\geq\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{8T_{i_{0}}}\right)^{\frac{\log\beta}{\log 2}}>\epsilon,

which is a contradiction. So there is
y_{x,r}\in B_{\frac{r}{T_{i_{0}}}}({\bar{y}_{x,r}})\setminus B_{\frac{r}{2T_{i_{0}}}}({\bar{y}_{x,r}})\cap K_{\epsilon}
. Then

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in B_{\frac{r}{T_{i_{0}}}}(y_{x,r})}|u(z)-u(y_{x,r})|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{g5.1}}}{{<}}

\displaystyle\frac{r}{T_{i_{0}}}\big(2^{{i_{0}}+1}{\rm{lip}}(u)(y_{x,r})+\epsilon\big)

(3.9)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{g5.2}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\frac{r}{T_{i_{0}}}\big(2^{{i_{0}}+1}{\rm{lip}}(u)(x)+2^{{i_{0}}+1}\epsilon+\epsilon\big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{210}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\frac{2^{{i_{0}}+2}r}{T_{i_{0}}}{\rm{lip}}(u)(x).

Step 3:

In this step we will estimate
|u(y_{x,r})-u({\bar{y}_{x,r}})|
. Denote
y=y_{x,r}
,
\bar{y}={\bar{y}_{x,r}}
, and
s={\mathrm{d}}(y,\bar{y})\in\left[\frac{r}{2T_{i_{0}}},\frac{r}{T_{i_{0}}}\right]
.
By (
3.4
), (
3.5
), (
3.7
) and the fact
{\mathrm{d}}(y,x)<r
,
we have

\displaystyle|u(y)-u(\bar{y})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in B_{s}(y)}{|u(z)-u(y)|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle s\big(2^{{i_{0}}+1}{\rm{lip}}(u)(y)+\epsilon\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle s\big(2^{{i_{0}}+1}{\rm{lip}}(u)(x)+2^{{i_{0}}+1}\epsilon+\epsilon\big)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 2^{i_{0}+2}{\rm{lip}}(u)(x){\mathrm{d}}(y,\overline{y}).

Step 4:

Denote
S_{i_{0}}(x,r)=\left\{z\in B_{r}(x):x\in A_{\epsilon,i_{0}},|u(z)-u(x)|\geq\frac{1}{8}{\rm{lip}}(u)(x){\mathrm{d}}(z,x)\right\}
. We claim, for
T_{i_{0}}
large enough,

B_{\frac{r}{T_{i_{0}}}}(y_{x,r})\subseteq S_{i_{0}}(x,r),~~~~~\forall x\in A_{\epsilon,i_{0}},r<r_{\epsilon}.

(3.10)

Recall that
{\mathrm{d}}(y_{x,r},\bar{y}_{x,r})\leq\frac{r}{T_{i_{0}}}
. By Step 3, we have

\displaystyle|u(y_{x,r})-u(\bar{y}_{x,r})|\leq\frac{2^{i_{0}+2}}{T_{i_{0}}}{\rm{lip}}(u)(x)r.

(3.11)

For
T_{i_{0}}=2^{i_{0}+5}
, we have

|u(y_{x,r})-u(\bar{y}_{x,r})|\leq\frac{1}{8}{\rm{lip}}(u)(x)r.

(3.12)

Thus, by (
3.8
) and (
3.12
),

|u(x)-u(y_{x,r})|\geq|u(x)-u(\bar{y}_{x,r})|-|u(y_{x,r})-u(\bar{y}_{x,r})|>\frac{1}{4}{\rm{lip}}(u)(x)r.

(3.13)

By (
3.9
) and (
3.13
), for any
z\in B_{\frac{r}{T_{i_{0}}}}(y_{x,r})\subset B_{r}(x)
,

|u(z)-u(x)|\geq|u(x)-u(y_{x,r})|-|u(z)-u(y_{x,r})|>\frac{1}{8}{\rm{lip}}(u)(x){\mathrm{d}}(z,x).

(3.14)

Therefore,
B_{\frac{r}{T_{i_{0}}}}(y_{x,r})\subseteq S_{i_{0}}(x,r)
.

Step 5:

Fix
x\in A_{\epsilon,i_{0}}
. Let
\lambda,r>0
be such that
8\lambda r^{N/p}={\mathrm{lip}}({u})(x)
. Denote

\hat{E}_{\lambda,u}(x)=\left\{z\in X:|u(x)-u(z)|\geq\lambda\big({\mathrm{d}}(x,z)\big)^{\frac{N}{p}+1}\right\}.

We can see that

B_{\frac{r}{T_{i_{0}}}}(y_{x,r})\subseteq S_{i_{0}}(x,r)\subseteq\hat{E}_{\lambda,u}(x).

(3.15)

Then by Fatou’s lemma, we have

\displaystyle\varliminf_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}\int_{L_{i_{0}}}\int_{X}\chi_{E_{\lambda,u}}(x,y){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{614}}}{{\geq}}

\displaystyle\varliminf_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}\int_{A_{\epsilon,i_{0}}}\left(\frac{r}{T_{i_{0}}}\right)^{N}\left(\frac{T_{i_{0}}}{r}\right)^{N}\mathfrak{m}\left({B_{\frac{r}{T_{i_{0}}}}(y_{x,r})}\right){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{a}{2^{(i_{0}+5)N}8^{p}}\int_{A_{\epsilon,i_{0}}}|{\mathrm{lip}}({u})(x)|^{p}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x),

where we use
\theta_{N}^{-}\geq a
in the last inequality.

Letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, we obtain

\varliminf_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}\int_{L_{i_{0}}}\int_{X}\chi_{E_{\lambda,u}}(x,y){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x)\geq\frac{a}{2^{(i_{0}+5)N}8^{p}}\int_{L_{i_{0}}}|{\mathrm{lip}}({u})(x)|^{p}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x).

Step 6:

Finally,

\displaystyle\varliminf_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}(\mathfrak{m}\times\mathfrak{m})({E_{\lambda,u}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varliminf_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}\int_{X}\int_{X}\chi_{E_{\lambda,u}}(x,y){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{+\infty}\varliminf_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}\int_{L_{i}}\int_{X}\chi_{E_{\lambda,u}}(x,y){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{+\infty}\int_{L_{i}}\frac{a}{2^{(i+5)N}8^{p}}|{\rm{lip}}(u)(x)|^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{key}}}{{\geq}}

\displaystyle\frac{a}{2^{5N}8^{p}}\int_{X}\frac{\big({\rm{lip}}(u)(x)\big)^{N+p}}{\big({\rm{Lip}}(u)(x)\big)^{N}}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x),

which is the thesis.

∎

3.2
Upper bound estimate

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
p\geq 1
and
(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space equipped with a
\beta
-doubling measure
\mathfrak{m}
. Assume
\theta_{N}^{+}\leq b
for some
b>0
. Then for any
u\in{\rm{Lip}}_{b}(X,{\mathrm{d}})
, we have

\varlimsup_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}(\mathfrak{m}\times\mathfrak{m})({E_{\lambda,u}})\leq 2b\int_{X}\big({\rm{Lip}}(u)(x)\big)^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x),

where
E_{\lambda,u}
is defined in
(
1.1
)
and
N=\frac{\log\beta}{\log 2}
.

Proof.

Note that if
x,y\notin\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits u
, then
(x,y)\notin{E_{\lambda,u}}
. We just need to consider
x\in\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits u
. Denote
\hat{E}_{\lambda,u}(x)=\{y\in X:y\neq x,|u(x)-u(y)|\geq\lambda\big({\mathrm{d}}(x,y)\big)^{\frac{N}{p}+1}\}
. For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for any
y\in B_{\delta}(x)
,

\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{{\mathrm{d}}(x,y)}\leq\text{Lip}(u)(x)+\epsilon.

Note also that there exists
\lambda_{1}
, such that for any
\lambda>\lambda_{1}
,

\left(\frac{\text{Lip}(u)(x)+\epsilon}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}\leq\left(\frac{\text{\bf Lip}(u)+\epsilon}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}<\delta.

So for any
y\in\hat{E}_{\lambda,u}(x)
, we have

\lambda\delta^{\frac{N}{p}}>\text{\bf Lip}(u)+\epsilon\geq\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{{\mathrm{d}}(x,y)}\geq\lambda\big({\mathrm{d}}(x,y)\big)^{\frac{N}{p}}.

Thus

\hat{E}_{\lambda,u}(x)\subset B_{\delta}(x)
and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}\left(\hat{E}_{\lambda,u}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{m}\left(\hat{E}_{\lambda,u}(x)\cap B_{\delta}(x)\right)

(3.16)

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{m}\left(\left\{y\in B_{\delta}(x):\,\text{Lip}(u)(x)+\epsilon\geq\lambda\big({\mathrm{d}}(x,y)\big)^{\frac{N}{p}}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{m}\left(B_{\left(\frac{\text{Lip}(u)(x)+\epsilon}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}}(x)\right).

Since
\mathfrak{m}
is
\beta
-doubling, by Lemma
2.3
, for any
0<r<\left(\frac{{\bf Lip}(u)+\epsilon}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}
, we have

\frac{\mathfrak{m}\left(B_{\left(\frac{{\bf Lip}(u)+\epsilon}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}}(x)\right)}{\lambda^{-p}}\leq\beta^{2}\left(\frac{\left(\frac{{\bf Lip}(u)+\epsilon}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}}{r}\right)^{N}\frac{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r}(x)\big)}{\lambda^{-p}}=\beta^{2}\big({\bf Lip}(u)+\epsilon\big)^{p}\frac{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r}(x)\big)}{r^{N}}.

Letting
r\rightarrow 0^{+}
, we obtain

\lambda^{p}\mathfrak{m}\left(B_{\left(\frac{{\rm Lip}(u)(x)+\epsilon}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}}(x)\right)\leq\beta^{2}\big({\bf Lip}(u)+\epsilon\big)^{p}\varlimsup_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r}(x_{0})\big)}{r^{N}}\leq\beta^{2}\big({\bf Lip}(u)+\epsilon\big)^{p}b.

Therefore, by Fubini’s theorem and Fatou’s lemma, we have

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}(\mathfrak{m}\times\mathfrak{m})({E_{\lambda,u}})

(3.17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}\int_{X}\int_{X}\chi_{E_{\lambda,u}}(x,y){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits u}\varlimsup_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}\mathfrak{m}\left(B_{\left(\frac{\text{Lip}(u)(x)+\epsilon}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}}(x)\right){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2b\int_{X}\big({\rm{Lip}}(u)(x)+\epsilon\big)^{p}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x),

where we use
\theta_{N}^{+}\leq b
in the last inequality.

Letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, then we prove the theorem.
∎

3.3
Asymptotic formula

The following geometric characterizations of finite-dimensional Banach spaces and equi-regular sub-Riemannian manifolds can be find in
[
Han24
, Section 4]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
and
(\mathfrak{C},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{C}},\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{C}})
be one of the following pairs of metric measure spaces:

•

(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
is an
m
-dimensional equi-regular sub-Riemannian manifold with homogeneous dimension
N\geq m
, equipped with the Carnot–Carathéodory metric
{\mathrm{d}}
and the associated Hausdorff measure
\mathfrak{m}=\mathcal{H}^{N}_{\mathrm{d}}
, and
(\mathfrak{C},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{C}},\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{C}})
is the Carnot group
(\mathbb{R}^{m},{\mathrm{d}}_{C},\mathcal{H}^{N}_{{\mathrm{d}}_{C}})
with homogeneous dimension
N
.

•

both
(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
and
(\mathfrak{C},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{C}},\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{C}})
are isometric to an
N
-dimensional Banach space
(\mathbb{R}^{N},||\cdot||,\mathcal{L}^{N})
.

Then we have:

•

(
Tangent space: metric
) For
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\in X
, there is a family of maps
\{\phi_{\delta}\}_{\delta>0}
from
X
to
\mathfrak{C}
satisfying
\phi_{\delta}(x)=0\in\mathfrak{C}
and

\left|\frac{\frac{1}{\delta}{{\mathrm{d}}(y,z)}}{{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{C}}\big(\phi_{\delta}(y),\phi_{\delta}(z)\big)}-1\right|<\eta(\delta),~~~~\forall y,z\in B_{\delta}(x),\,\delta\in(0,1),

(3.18)

where
\eta:(0,1)\rightarrow(0,1)
is an increasing function with
\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}\eta(\delta)=0
.

•

(
Tangent space: measure
) For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that for any
\delta<\delta_{0}
, we have

(1-\epsilon)\delta^{N}\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{C}}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{\phi_{\delta}(B_{\delta}(x))}$}<(\phi_{\delta})_{\sharp}\left(\mathfrak{m}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{B_{\delta}(x)}$}\right)<(1+\epsilon)\delta^{N}\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{C}}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{\phi_{\delta}(B_{\delta}(x))}$}.

(3.19)

•

(
Homogeneity
) For any
\delta>0
, there is a dilation map
D_{\delta}
, which is an isometry between
(\mathfrak{C},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{C}},\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{C}})
and
(\mathfrak{C},\delta^{-1}{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{C}},\delta^{-N}\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{C}})
such that
D_{\delta}(0)=0
and
D_{\delta}\circ D_{\delta^{-1}}=\rm{Id}
. In particular,

(D_{\delta})_{\sharp}\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{C}}=\delta^{-N}\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{C}}

(3.20)

and

\mathfrak{m}^{+}_{\mathfrak{C}}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{S^{\mathfrak{C}}_{\delta}}$}=\delta^{N-1}(D_{\delta})_{\sharp}\left(\mathfrak{m}^{+}_{\mathfrak{C}}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{S^{\mathfrak{C}}_{1}}$}\right),

(3.21)

where
S^{\mathfrak{C}}_{\delta}
is the boundary of the ball centered at the origin in the tangent space
\mathfrak{C}
with radius
\delta
, and
\mathfrak{m}^{+}_{\mathfrak{C}}
is the boundary measure.

•

(
Rademacher’s theorem
) For any
u\in{{\rm{Lip}}_{b}}(X,{\mathrm{d}})
and
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\in X
, there is a unique function
u_{0,x}
on
(\mathfrak{C},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{C}})
, such that the rescaling functions

u_{\delta,x}(y):=\frac{u(y)-u(x)}{\delta},\quad y\in X,~~~\delta>0

converge to
u_{0,x}
in the following sense: there is a function
\alpha(\delta)
satisfying
\alpha(\delta)\searrow 0
as
\delta\searrow 0
such that

\|u_{0,x}\circ\phi_{\delta}-u_{\delta,x}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\delta}(x),\mathfrak{m})}\leq\alpha(\delta),\quad\forall\delta>0.

(3.22)

Then we can prove the following asymptotic formula. We remark that our proof also works for general mollifiers. Interested readers could find more asymptotic formulas by combining our theorems and some carefully selected mollifiers (cf.
[
Han24
, §3.2]
).

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
p>1,N\in\mathbb{N}
and
(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space satisfying the hypothesis in Proposition
3.4
. Then for any
u\in{{\rm{Lip}}_{b}}(X,{\mathrm{d}})
, we have

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}(\mathfrak{m}\times\mathfrak{m})({E_{\lambda,u}})=\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{K_{p,\mathfrak{C}}},

where

\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{K_{p,\mathfrak{C}}}=\int_{X}\int_{S^{\mathfrak{C}}_{1}}\frac{|u_{0,x}(w)|^{p}}{N}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}^{+}_{\mathfrak{C}}(w){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x).

(3.23)

Proof.

For
x\in X
,
\delta<\delta_{0}
and
\lambda>0
, we write

E_{\lambda,u,\delta}(x)=\underbrace{\big\{(x,y)\in E_{\lambda,u}\big\}\cap B_{\delta}(x)}_{E_{\lambda,u,\delta}^{1}(x)}\cup\underbrace{\big\{(x,y)\in E_{\lambda,u}\big\}\cap B^{c}_{\delta}(x)}_{E_{\lambda,u,\delta}^{2}(x)}.

If
y\in E_{\lambda,u,\delta}^{2}(x)
, we have

\lambda\leq\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{\big({\mathrm{d}}(x,y)\big)^{\frac{N}{p}+1}}\leq\frac{\text{\bf Lip}(u)}{\delta^{\frac{N}{p}}}.

So
\mathfrak{m}\big(E_{\lambda,u,\delta}^{2}(x)\big)=0
for large
\lambda
and

\varlimsup_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}\int_{X}\chi_{E_{\lambda,u,\delta}(x)}(y){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)=\varlimsup_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}\underbrace{\int_{X}\chi_{E_{\lambda,u,\delta}^{1}(x)}(y){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)}_{I_{1}(\lambda,u,\delta,x)}.

(3.24)

Concerning
I_{1}(\lambda,u,\delta,x)
we have

\displaystyle I_{1}(\lambda,u,\delta,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}\left({\left\{y\in B_{\delta}(x):|u(x)-u(y)|\geq\lambda\big({\mathrm{d}}(x,y)\big)^{\frac{N}{p}+1}\right\}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}\left({\left\{y\in B_{\delta}(x):|u_{\delta,x}(y)|\geq\frac{\lambda}{\delta}\big({\mathrm{d}}(x,y)\big)^{\frac{N}{p}+1}\right\}}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{1}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}\left({\left\{y\in B_{\delta}(x):|u_{0,x}(\phi_{\delta}(y))|+\alpha(\delta)\geq\frac{\lambda}{\delta}\big({\mathrm{d}}(x,y)\big)^{\frac{N}{p}+1}\right\}}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{distance}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}\left({\left\{y\in B_{\delta}(x):|u_{0,x}(\phi_{\delta}(y))|+\alpha(\delta)\geq\frac{\lambda}{\delta}\big({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(\phi_{\delta}(y),0)\delta(1-\eta(\delta))\big)^{\frac{N}{p}+1}\right\}}\right).

Letting
v=\phi_{\delta}(y)
, we get

\displaystyle I_{1}(\lambda,u,\delta,x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\phi_{\delta})_{\sharp}\mathfrak{m}\left({\left\{v\in\phi_{\delta}\big(B_{\delta}(x)\big):|u_{0,x}(v)|+\alpha(\delta)\geq\frac{\lambda}{\delta}\big({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(v,0)\delta(1-\eta(\delta))\big)^{\frac{N}{p}+1}\right\}}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{measrela}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle(1+\epsilon)\delta^{N}\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{C}}\left({\left\{v\in\phi_{\delta}\big(B_{\delta}(x)\big):|u_{0,x}(v)|+\alpha(\delta)\geq\frac{\lambda}{\delta}\big({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(v,0)\delta(1-\eta(\delta))\big)^{\frac{N}{p}+1}\right\}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\underbrace{(1+\epsilon)\delta^{N}\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{C}}\left({\left\{v\in B^{\mathfrak{C}}_{1}(0):|u_{0,x}(v)|+\alpha(\delta)\geq\frac{\lambda}{\delta}\big({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(v,0)\delta(1-\eta(\delta))\big)^{\frac{N}{p}+1}\right\}}\right)}_{I^{a}_{1}(\lambda,u,\delta,x)}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\underbrace{(1+\epsilon)\delta^{N}\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{C}}\left({\left\{v\in\phi_{\delta}\big(B_{\delta}(x)\big)\backslash B^{\mathfrak{C}}_{1}(0):|u_{0,x}(v)|+\alpha(\delta)\geq\frac{\lambda}{\delta}\big({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(v,0)\delta(1-\eta(\delta))\big)^{\frac{N}{p}+1}\right\}}\right)}_{I^{b}_{1}(\lambda,u,\delta,x)}.

By considering the dilation map
{\mathrm{D}}_{\delta}
and a change of variable, we get

\displaystyle I^{a}_{1}(\lambda,u,\delta,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+\epsilon)\delta^{N}(D_{\delta})_{\sharp}\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{C}}\left({\left\{w\in B^{\mathfrak{C}}_{\delta}(0):\left|u_{0,x}\big(D_{\delta^{-1}}(w)\big)\right|+\alpha(\delta)\geq\frac{\lambda}{\delta}\big({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(w,0)(1-\eta(\delta))\big)^{\frac{N}{p}+1}\right\}}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{measure}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(1+\epsilon)\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{C}}\left({\left\{w\in B^{\mathfrak{C}}_{\delta}(0):\left|u_{0,x}\big(D_{\delta^{-1}}(w)\big)\right|+\alpha(\delta)\geq\frac{\lambda}{\delta}\big({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(w,0)(1-\eta(\delta))\big)^{\frac{N}{p}+1}\right\}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+\epsilon)\int^{\delta}_{0}\int_{S^{\mathfrak{C}}_{r}}\chi_{\left\{|u_{0,x}(D_{\delta^{-1}}(w))|+\alpha(\delta)\geq\frac{\lambda}{\delta}(r(1-\eta(\delta)))^{\frac{N}{p}+1}\right\}}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}^{+}_{\mathfrak{C}}(w){\mathrm{d}}r,

where
\mathfrak{m}^{+}_{\mathfrak{C}}
is the boundary measure. By the definition of
u_{0,x}
, the homogeneity of
\mathfrak{C}
and Fubini’s theorem, we know that

\displaystyle I^{a}_{1}(\lambda,u,\delta,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+\epsilon)\int^{\delta}_{0}\int_{S^{\mathfrak{C}}_{r}}\chi_{\left\{\delta^{-1}r|u_{0,x}(D_{r^{-1}}(w))|+\alpha(\delta)\geq\frac{\lambda}{\delta}(r(1-\eta(\delta)))^{\frac{N}{p}+1}\right\}}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}^{+}_{\mathfrak{C}}(w){\mathrm{d}}r

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{surmeasure}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(1+\epsilon)\int^{\delta}_{0}r^{N-1}\int_{S^{\mathfrak{C}}_{1}}\chi_{\left\{r|u_{0,x}(w)|+\alpha(\delta)\geq\lambda(r(1-\eta(\delta)))^{\frac{N}{p}+1}\right\}}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}^{+}_{\mathfrak{C}}(w){\mathrm{d}}r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+\epsilon)\int^{\delta}_{0}r^{N-1}\int_{S^{\mathfrak{C}}_{1}}\chi_{\left\{r^{\frac{N}{p}}\leq\frac{|u_{0,x}(w)|+\alpha(\delta)}{\lambda(1-\eta(\delta))^{\frac{N}{p}+1}}\right\}}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}^{+}_{\mathfrak{C}}(w){\mathrm{d}}r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+\epsilon)\int_{S^{\mathfrak{C}}_{1}}\int^{\delta}_{0}r^{N-1}\chi_{\left\{r^{\frac{N}{p}}\leq\frac{|u_{0,x}(w)|+\alpha(\delta)}{\lambda(1-\eta(\delta))^{\frac{N}{p}+1}}\right\}}{\mathrm{d}}r{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}^{+}_{\mathfrak{C}}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1+\epsilon}{N}\int_{S^{\mathfrak{C}}_{1}}A(\lambda,u,\delta,x,w)^{N}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}^{+}_{\mathfrak{C}}(w),

where

A(\lambda,u,\delta,x,w)=\min\left\{\delta,\left(\frac{|u_{0,x}(w)|+\alpha(\delta)}{\lambda\big(1-\eta(\delta)\big)^{\frac{N}{p}+1}}\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}\right\}.

For
\lambda
large enough, we have

I^{a}_{1}(\lambda,u,\delta,x)=\frac{1+\epsilon}{N}\int_{S^{\mathfrak{C}}_{1}}\left(\frac{|u_{0,x}(w)|+\alpha(\delta)}{\lambda\big(1-\eta(\delta)\big)^{\frac{N}{p}+1}}\right)^{p}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}^{+}_{\mathfrak{C}}(w).

(3.25)

Consider
I^{b}_{1}(\lambda,u,\delta,x)
. Note
v\in\phi_{\delta}\big(B_{\delta}(x)\big)\backslash B^{\mathfrak{C}}_{1}(0)
and
u\in{{\rm{Lip}}_{b}}(X,{\mathrm{d}})

3.4
Optimality of
N

We can see that the parameter
N
is sharp:

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
p\geq 1,\bar{N}>0
and
(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space equipped with a
\beta
-doubling measure
\mathfrak{m}
. If
a\leq\theta_{N}^{-}\leq\theta_{N}^{+}\leq b
for
N=\frac{\log\beta}{\log 2}
and some
b>a>0
, then the following statements are equivalent:

a)

There exists
u\in{\rm{Lip}}_{b}(X,{\mathrm{d}})
with
\mathfrak{m}(\{x\in X:{\rm{lip}}(u)(x)>0\})>0
such that

\displaystyle\varliminf_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}(\mathfrak{m}\times\mathfrak{m})({\bar{E}_{\lambda,u}})>0,

(3.29)

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}(\mathfrak{m}\times\mathfrak{m})({\bar{E}_{\lambda,u}})<+\infty,

where

\bar{E}_{\lambda,u}=\left\{(x,y)\in X\times X:x\neq y,|u(x)-u(y)|\geq\lambda\big({\mathrm{d}}(x,y)\big)^{\frac{\bar{N}}{p}+1}\right\}.

b)

For any
u\in{\rm{Lip}}_{b}(X,{\mathrm{d}})
with
\mathfrak{m}(\{x\in X:{\rm{lip}}(u)(x)>0\})>0
, (
3.29
) holds.

c)

\bar{N}=N=\frac{\log\beta}{\log 2}
.

Proof.

{\bf{c})\Rightarrow\bf{b})}
is a consequence of Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
,
{\bf{b})\Rightarrow\bf{a})}
is trivial.
We will prove
{\bf{a})\Rightarrow\bf{c})}
by contradiction.

Case 1:
\bar{N}<N
. Similar to the proof of Theorem
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}(\mathfrak{m}\times\mathfrak{m})({\bar{E}_{\lambda,u}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}\int_{X}\int_{X}\chi_{\bar{E}_{\lambda,u}}(x,y){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\varlimsup_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p}\int_{\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits u}\mathfrak{m}\left(B_{\left(\frac{\text{Lip}(u)(x)+\epsilon}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{p}{\bar{N}}}}(x)\right){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x)

\displaystyle 2\varlimsup_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda^{p\left(1-\frac{N}{\bar{N}}\right)}\int_{\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits u}\lambda^{\frac{pN}{\bar{N}}}\mathfrak{m}\left(B_{\left(\frac{\text{Lip}(u)(x)+\epsilon}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{p}{\bar{N}}}}(x)\right){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x)

\displaystyle 0,

which is a contradiction.

3.1
. For any
{i_{0}}
with

set

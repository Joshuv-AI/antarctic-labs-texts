Some q q -transformation formulas and Rogers-Ramanujan type identities Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author
Chang Xu ∗, Dunkun Yang

Abstract
. In this paper, we explore the role that Liu’s transformation formula can play in discovering Rogers-Ramanujan type identities. Specifically, we combine Liu’s transformation formula with other q q -series summations to derive a series of parameterized identities. Thereafter, through careful selection of these parameters, we obtain 75 75 Rogers-Ramanujan type identities from Slater’s list, and uncover several new Rogers-Ramanujan type identities.

1.
Introduction

Throughout the paper, we assume that
|q|<1
for convergence and define the
q
-shifted factorials as

\displaystyle\left(a;q\right)_{0}=1,\quad\left(a;q\right)_{n}=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(1-aq^{k}\right),\quad n=1,2,\cdots,\mathrm{or}\,{\infty}.

Also, following the definition of Slater
[
21
]
, we define

\left(q;q\right)_{-1}=1
.
For brevity, we abbreviate a collection of several
q
-shifted factorials on the same base

\left(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{m};q\right)_{n}=\left(a_{1};q\right)_{n}\left(a_{2};q\right)_{n}\cdots\left(a_{m};q\right)_{n}.

Following Gasper and Rahman’s definition
[
9

{}_{r}\phi_{s}(\cdot)
basic hypergeometric series is defined as

{{}_{r}\phi_{s}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{1},\,a_{2},\,\cdots,a_{r}\\
b_{1},\,b_{2},\,\cdots,b_{s}\end{array};q,\,z\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(a_{1},\,a_{2},\,\cdots,a_{r};q\right)_{n}}{\left(q,\,b_{1},\,b_{2},\,\cdots,b_{s};q\right)_{n}}\left(\left(-1\right)^{n}q^{n(n-1)/2}\right)^{1+s-r}z^{n}

where
q\neq 0
when

Many fantastic sum-product
q
-sereis were uncovered by Rogers
[
19
]
, including

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n^{2}}}{\left(q;q\right)_{n}}=\frac{1}{\left(q,q^{4};q^{5}\right)_{\infty}},

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n(n+1)}}{\left(q;q\right)_{n}}=\frac{1}{\left(q^{2},q^{3};q^{5}\right)_{\infty}}.

(1.2)

However, Rogers’s work didn’t attract people’s attention for some time until Ramanujan independently rediscovered the identities (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). Thereafter, the identities (
1.1
)-(
1.2

Rogers-Ramanujan identities play crucial roles in various branches of mathematics and physics. For example, these identities have a close relation to combinatorics, vertex algebras, knot theory, and statistical mechanics.
Therefore, discovering identities similar to (

)-(
1.2

[

[

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{n-1}}{\left(q;q\right)_{n-1}\left(q;q\right)_{n}\left(q;q^{2}\right)_{n}}q^{n^{2}}

\displaystyle{=}\frac{\left(-q^{5},-q^{7},q^{12};q^{12}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q;q\right)_{\infty}},\quad{}

(S.58)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n^{2}+2n}}{\left(q;q\right)_{n}\left(q;q^{2}\right)_{n+1}}

The key tool in our proof is the following elegant transformation formula for

Theorem 1.1
.

For a complex sequence
\left\{A_{n}\right\}
, under suitable convergence conditions, there holds

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\alpha q,\alpha ab/q;q\right)_{\infty}}{\left(\alpha a,\alpha b;q\right)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}\left(q/a;q\right)_{n}\left(\alpha a\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(1-\alpha q^{2n}\right)\left(\alpha,q/a,q/b;q\right)_{n}}{\left(1-\alpha\right)\left(q,\alpha a,\alpha b;q\right)_{n}}\left(-\alpha ab\right)^{n}q^{n(n-3)/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\left(q^{-n},\alpha q^{n};q\right)_{k}}{\left(q/b;q\right)_{k}}\left(q^{2}/b\right)^{k}A_{k}.

(1.3)

Specifically,
we take

A_{n}=\frac{\left(q/b,\beta,\gamma,\lambda;q\right)_{n}}{\left(q,c,d,e,h;q\right)_{n}}\left(b/q\right)^{n}

into Theorem
1.1
to obtain the following transformation formula.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
\max\left\{|c|,|d|,|e|,|h|,|\alpha a|,|\alpha b|,|\alpha ab/q|\right\}<1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\alpha q,\alpha ab/q;q\right)_{\infty}}{\left(\alpha a,\alpha b;q\right)_{\infty}}{{}_{5}\phi_{4}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}q/a,\,q/b,\,\beta,\,\gamma,\,\lambda\\
c,\,d,\,e,\,h\end{array};q,{\alpha ab}/{q}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(1-\alpha q^{2n}\right)\left(\alpha,q/a,q/b;q\right)_{n}}{\left(1-\alpha\right)\left(q,\alpha a,\alpha b;q\right)_{n}}\left(-\alpha ab\right)^{n}q^{n\left(n-3\right)/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\times{{}_{5}\phi_{4}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}q^{-n},\,\alpha q^{n},\,\beta,\,\gamma,\,\lambda\\
c,\,d,\,e,\,h\end{array};q,q\right).

By combining (
1.2
) with other well-known
q
-series summation formulas and the Jacobi triple product identity
[

, Eq. (1.6.1)]

\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{\frac{n^{2}-n}{2}}z^{n}=\left(-z,-q/z,q;q\right)_{\infty},

(1.8)

we derive a number of parameter-dependent identities in Sections
2
-
7
. In particular, by making specific choices for the parameters, we obtain numerous Rogers-Ramanujan type identities, including
75

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(q;q\right)_{2n+1}}q^{n^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(q^{8},q^{12},q^{20};q^{20}\right)_{\infty}+q\left(q^{4},q^{16},q^{20};q^{20}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q;q\right)_{\infty}\left(-q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}},

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(q;q\right)_{3n}\left(-q^{3};q^{3}\right)_{n}}{\left(q^{3};q^{3}\right)_{n}\left(q^{3};q^{3}\right)_{2n}}q^{\frac{3n^{2}-3n}{2}}

\displaystyle\!=\!\frac{\left(-q^{3};q^{3}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q^{3};q^{3}\right)_{\infty}}\left(\left(q,q^{5},q^{6};q^{6}\right)_{\infty}+\left(q^{2},q^{4},q^{6};q^{6}\right)_{\infty}\right),

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(-q;q^{2}\right)_{n}\left(q;q\right)_{2n+1}}q^{2n^{2}+2n}

\displaystyle\!=\!\frac{\left(q^{8},q^{20},q^{28};q^{28}\right)_{\infty}+q\left(q^{4},q^{24},q^{28};q^{28}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}}.

Evaluation of Full-Mouth Non-Surgical Modalities for the Treatment of Chronic Periodontitis
Authors: Ajita Meenawat, Christina L. Pachuau, Vivek Srivastava, Yasir Shahab Khan, Sajid Husain, Tanu Sahney
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Periodontitis is a common chronic inflammatory disease-causing destruction of the supporting tissues of teeth. Conventional treatment involves quadrant-wise scaling and root planning (Q-SRP). Alternative approaches like full-mouth scaling (FMS), full-mouth disinfection (FMD), and FMD with air-polishing (FMDAP) have been introduced to enhance outcomes.Methods:In this randomized study, 220 patients with Stage III chronic periodontitis were allocated into four groups—Q-SRP, FMS, FMD, and FMDAP. Clinical parameters (plaque index, gingival index, probing depth, and clinical attachment level) were recorded at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months. Statistical comparisons were performed using ANOVA with Tukey’s post hoc test.Results:All groups showed significant intra-group improvements in clinical parameters at 3 and 6 months (P < 0.001). Inter-group analysis revealed no baseline differences, but significant improvements in FMD and FMDAP groups at follow-ups (P < 0.001), with FMDAP showing the greatest overall benefit.Conclusion:All four treatment strategies were effective; however, FMDAP provided superior clinical improvements in probing pocket depth (PPD) and clinical attachment level (CAL), with the added advantage of reduced treatment time compared to Q-SRP.

INTRODUCTION

Periodontitis is defined as an inflammatory condition affecting the supporting structures of the teeth, caused by specific microbial pathogens, and characterized by the progressive destruction of the periodontal ligament and alveolar bone, often accompanied by periodontal pocket formation and/or gingival recession.[1] It is one of the most prevalent dental diseases and results from a complex interplay between pathogenic biofilms and the host immune response, ultimately leading to soft and hard tissue loss. Effective management hinges on the meticulous removal of supra- and subgingival plaque and calculus, forming the foundation of periodontal therapy.[2] In the present study, therapeutic approaches included quadrant-wise debridement, full-mouth scaling (FMS), full-mouth disinfection (FMD), and adjunctive air-polishing, each aiming to optimize debridement and minimize bacterial recolonization.

Traditional periodontal therapy typically involves quadrant-wise scaling and root planning (SRP) performed over multiple weekly sessions, often in conjunction with antiseptic rinses such as 0.12% chlorhexidine.[3] However, a notable drawback of this approach is the potential for cross-contamination, where untreated areas may serve as bacterial reservoirs and lead to re-infection of previously treated sites.[4] To address this limitation, the concept of FMD was introduced, involving complete SRP of the entire dentition within 24 hours along with comprehensive disinfection of oropharyngeal niches using chlorhexidine during and after treatment.[5] This protocol, first proposed by Quirynen et al. in 1995, was designed to reduce microbial reservoirs and enhance therapeutic outcomes.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

A total of 220 patients diagnosed with Stage III chronic periodontitis were recruited from the outpatient clinic of the Department of Periodontology, Sardar Patel Post Graduate Institute of Dental and Medical Sciences, after obtaining written informed consent in both English and the regional language. The study received ethical approval from the institutional review board. Participants aged 18–50 years (ASA I and II) with radiographic bone loss extending to the middle or apical third of the root and possessing at least 18 teeth were included. Exclusion criteria encompassed recent SRP within 12 months, recent antimicrobial or antibiotic use, systemic diseases affecting periodontium, drug interactions, pregnancy, root resorption, allergies to study materials, poor compliance, or psychological issues. Patients were randomly assigned into four groups via lottery: Group A underwent quadrant-wise SRP over 4 weeks; Group B received full-mouth SRP within 24 hours; Group C received FMD with chlorhexidine-based antiseptic protocol; Group D received FMD with sodium bicarbonate air-polishing.

All treatments involved scaling with a Woodpecker UDS-E LED Piezo Ultrasonic scaler and root planning using a Hu-Friedy Columbia Curette. Periodontal parameters including plaque index (PI), gingival index (GI), probing pocket depth (PPD), and clinical attachment level (CAL) were assessed at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months using a Hu-Friedy UNC-15 probe. The chlorhexidine protocol for Group C followed Quirynen et al. (1998), involving tongue brushing, rinses, sprays, and subgingival irrigation, with continued antiseptic use for 2 months. Group D received an adjuvant air-polishing treatment using sodium bicarbonate. Patients lost to follow-up or unable to continue due to the health or personal reasons were excluded from analysis.

Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS version 22.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

RESULTS

All four treatment modalities—QWD, FMS, FMD, and FMD with air-polishing (FMDAP)—showed significant intra-group improvements in PI, GI, PD, and CAL over 6 months (P < 0.001). As per Table 1, PI and GI decreased significantly within each group, with FMDAP showing the highest net reductions by 6 months, despite no inter-group differences at any time point (P > 0.05). Table 2 shows significant PD and CAL reductions across all groups, with FMD and FMDAP performing significantly better than QWD and FMS at both follow-ups (P < 0.001). FMDAP yielded the greatest net reductions in PD (12.5%) and CAL (9.3%) over QWD.

DISCUSSION

Periodontitis is a multifactorial inflammatory disease driven by microbial dysbiosis in dental plaque biofilms, contributing to tissue destruction and periodontal attachment loss.[1] The cornerstone of periodontal therapy lies in the disruption and removal of these biofilms to arrest disease progression.[2] Traditional quadrant-wise SRP (Q-SRP) has been a gold standard for decades; however, alternative full-mouth approaches, particularly FMD, have gained interest for their potential to enhance bacterial reduction and prevent reinfection.[3] The concept of FMD, first introduced by Eberhard J et al., incorporates adjunctive antimicrobial protocols with mechanical debridement in a single treatment window, thus minimizing the opportunity for bacterial recolonization.[4]

In this study, FMD and FMS were compared with Q-SRP, and the added efficacy of sodium hydrogen carbonate air-polishing to FMD (FMDAP) was also investigated. The FMD protocol, as per Quirynen’s model, involves chlorhexidine-based antiseptic application—including subgingival irrigation, tongue brushing, and tonsil sprays—to enhance microbial control.[5] These antiseptic strategies have shown beneficial effects in earlier studies, demonstrating a marked reduction in periodontal pathogens and a lower chance of reinfection when applied in a full-mouth context.[6] Furthermore, air-polishing using sodium bicarbonate in the FMDAP group provided an additional method for biofilm disruption, showing clinical efficacy without any adverse effects such as soft tissue damage or emphysema, aligning with safety reports in prior literature.[7]

CONCLUSION

In summary, the study underscored the effectiveness of FMD and FMS in treating periodontitis, with the added benefit of sodium hydrogen carbonate air-polishing in enhancing treatment outcomes. FMDAP’s improved outcomes may stem from its comprehensive approach involving air-polishing, subgingival re-instrumentation, and disinfection.

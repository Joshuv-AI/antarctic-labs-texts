Emotional Dimension Control in Language Model-Based Text-to-Speech: Spanning a Broad Spectrum of Human Emotions
Kun Zhou 1 , You Zhang 2 , Shengkui Zhao 1 , Hao Wang 1 , Zexu Pan 1 , Dianwen Ng 1 , Chong Zhang 1 , Chongjia Ni 1 , Yukun Ma 1 , Trung Hieu Nguyen 1 , Jia Qi Yip 1 , Bin Ma 1

Abstract
Current emotional text-to-speech (TTS) systems face challenges in mimicking a broad spectrum of human emotions due to the inherent complexity of emotions and limitations in emotional speech datasets and models. This paper proposes a TTS framework that facilitates control over pleasure, arousal, and dominance, and can synthesize a diversity of emotional styles without requiring any emotional speech data during TTS training. We train an emotional attribute predictor using only categorical labels from speech data, aligning with psychological research and incorporating anchored dimensionality reduction on self-supervised learning (SSL) features. The TTS framework converts text inputs into phonetic tokens via an autoregressive language model and uses pseudo-emotional dimensions to guide the parallel prediction of fine-grained acoustic details. Experiments conducted on the LibriTTS dataset demonstrate that our framework can synthesize speech with enhanced naturalness and a variety of emotional styles by effectively controlling emotional dimensions, even without the inclusion of any emotional speech during TTS training.

II
Proposed Method

Following our previous research
[
24
]
, we consider TTS as a conditional language modeling task, where the speech waveform is quantized into discrete tokens and is then modeled with a language model.
However, training language models typically requires a large amount of data to achieve stable performance
[
25
,
26
]
, making it impractical as to emotional speech. Speech emotion, as a form of speech expressiveness, can be represented through continuous emotional dimensions that are learnable from expressive speech
[
27
]
.
Our goal is to develop a TTS model that learns from expressive speech data during training and synthesizes speech that conveys intended emotions. The overall TTS system includes an autoregressive (AR) decoder and a non-autoregressive (NAR) decoder. The AR decoder predicts phonetic tokens from phoneme inputs and prompt audio, while the NAR decoder, using emotional dimension (ED) embeddings, predicts acoustic codecs with fine-grained details. Finally, the Encodec decoder synthesizes the speech waveform from these codecs.

II-B

TTS Training with Emotional Dimension Guidance

Since speech contains both linguistic and para-linguistic information, we decouple TTS training as two stages: ‘Phoneme
\rightarrow
Phonetic’ and ‘Phonetic
\rightarrow
Acoustic’.
The overall TTS diagram is illustrated in Figure
1
(b).
In the first stage, the AR model learns to map phoneme sequences to phonetic tokens.
We leverage WavLM features with K-means clustering as phonetically-rich targets to train the AR model. In the second stage, the TTS focus on acoustic modeling with emotional dimension guidance, where a NAR LM is trained to recover fine-grained acoustic details from phonetic tokens predicted by AR model. Emotional dimension vectors, derived from the pre-trained ED predictor, are used to condition the NAR model, together with predicted phonetic tokens and acoustic tokens generated by Encodec. During TTS training, the AR model solely focuses on linguistic modeling, while the NAR model learns fine-grained acoustic details from the prompt audio, emphasizing the consistency of pleasure-arousal-dominance with the prompt.

II-C

Emotion Rendering with Emotional Dimension Control

During inference, given input text and prompt audio along with its text transcriptions, the AR model predicts phonetic tokens representing phonetic variations over the phonemes. Subsequently, the NAR model predicts acoustic tokens based on the emotional dimension vector, the acoustic prompt, and the phoneme sequences. The emotional dimension vector can either be predicted from the prompt audio (‘ED prediction’) or manually assigned by users (‘ED control’). Finally, the encodec decoder synthesizes the speech waveform based on the predicted acoustic codecs.

III-B

Implementation Details

The TTS framework consists of both AR and NAR models, both of which consist of a transformer architecture consisting of 12 layers, 16 attention heads, an embedding dimension of 1024, a feed-forward layer dimension of 4096 and a dropout rate of 0.1. The 3-D emotional dimension vector can be either extracted from the prompt audio or assigned by users, which is then projected by a linear layer of dimension 128. We use a 24kHz Encodec
4
4

4

https://huggingface.co/facebook/encodec_24khz
as the acoustic encoder to compress the speech waveform into 8 layers of discrete acoustic tokens, while the Encodec decoder is utilized to recover 8 layers of discrete acoustic tokens to the speech waveform. We obtain the phonetic tokens from the 24th layer of WavLM
5
5

5

https://huggingface.co/microsoft/wavlm-large
models and cluster them using the K-means algorithm
[
42
]
.
We manually upsample the phonetic tokens from 16kHz to 24kHz to align with acoustic tokens. The WavLM model for emotional dimension prediction is fine-tuned for 100 epochs with a batch size of 64 using the Adam optimizer at a 1e-4 learning rate. Dimensionality reduction settings are a 0.1 minimum distance and 20 nearest neighbors, with a 1e-2 learning rate for point optimization.

III-D

Subjective Evaluation

We conducted listening experiments, with 10 native English speakers each listening to a total of 260 synthesized speech samples. We first evaluate our proposed framework on the zero-shot TTS task, where the ED is predicted based on the prompt speech (‘Proposed w/ ED Prediction’). We selected ‘Phonetic-Enhanced VALL-E’

We additionally conducted two AB preference tests, where participants were asked to choose the better speech sample in terms of speech naturalness and prosody similarity with the prompt speech, respectively. As shown in Fig.

IV
Conclusion

Inspired by the success of language models in a variety of generative tasks, much of current research prioritizes data collection, aiming to expand training data to push the boundaries of the state-of-the-art. In contrast, this paper explores how earlier studies on emotion theory can can advance research in emotional TTS. We introduce a TTS framework capable of controlling 3 emotional dimensions—pleasure, arousal, and dominance—during runtime. This framework not only synthesizes a wide range of emotional styles without the need for any emotional speech data for TTS training, but also enhances naturalness and prosody consistency with the speech prompt in zero-shot scenarios.

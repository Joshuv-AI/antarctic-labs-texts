Strongly inhomogeneous spin dynamics induced by ultrashort laser pulses with a gradient intensity profile Preprint: APS/123-QED
T. T. Gareev , N. E. Khokhlov , L. Körber , A. V. Kimel

Abstract
The optical pump-probe technique is a common tool for investigation of ultrafast spin dynamics, which usually utilizes single-diode detection averaging the dynamics over the pumped area. Using ultrafast imaging technique, we show experimentally that a femtosecond laser pulse with a gradient distribution of intensity efficiently excites strongly inhomogeneous spin dynamics on spatial scales much smaller than the pump spot size. The mechanism responsible for the inhomogeneous distribution is based on temperature gradients and corresponds to a sign change of the torque derivative in different areas of the pump. We argue that the observed phenomenon is general for the systems with competitive magnetic anisotropies. Overlooking this effect in the majority of pump-probe experiments may result in a dramatic underestimation of the live time and amplitude of the laser-induced spin dynamics.

As progress in the field of ultrafast magnetism is predominantly driven by experimental discoveries, the pump-probe technique has practically become the main tool in this field of research.
Here we report that using ultrafast imaging, which has so far rarely been used in experimental studies of ultrafast magnetism, we discovered a counterintuitive, and therefore often overlooked, strongly inhomogeneous spin dynamics.
We argue that such a dynamics can be excited even if the laser beam has a relatively large diameter and a Gaussian distribution of light intensity.
In particular, the strongly inhomogeneous magnetization oscillations on spatial scales much smaller than the pump spot originate from the different ultrafast dynamics of competing magnetocrystalline and shape anisotropies.

To explain the initial torque distribution, we modeled the system as an ensemble of non-interacting macrospins, neglecting any interactions between them such as exchange or dipolar coupling.
In this approximation, we describe each macrospin by a magnetic energy density
\mathscr{F}
that contains only local uniaxial and effective dipolar anisotropies, as well as the Zeeman interaction:

{\mathscr{F}}=\left(K_{\mathrm{u}}-\frac{1}{2}\mu_{0}M_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}\right)\sin^{2}\theta-M_{s}B\sin\theta,

(1)

where,
\theta
is the polar angle of the magnetization vector
M
(Fig.
1
a);
K_{u}
and
M_{s}
are the temperature-dependent uniaxial anisotropy parameter and saturation magnetization, respectively;
B=\mu_{0}H
is the magnetic field strength,
\mu_{0}
is the vacuum magnetic permeability.
Here, we assume that temperature dependencies
M_{s}(T)
and
K_{u}(T)
follow an adapted Akulov–Zener approach
Akulov 1936
;
Zener 1954
:

M_{s}(T)=M_{s}(0)\left[1-\left(\frac{T}{T_{C}}\right)^{3/2}\right];\\
K_{u}(T)=K_{u}(0)\left[1-\left(\frac{T}{T_{C}}\right)^{\beta}\right]^{a},

(2)

where
\beta=12
following Ref.
Davies et al. 2019
, and
a=3
as it predicted for the single-ion uniaxial anisotropy
Zener 1954
;
Callen and Callen 1966
and
conserving after laser-induced dynamics in the experiments
Gerevenkov et al. 2021
;
Blank et al. 2022
.
Equations (
1
) and (
2
) give the spatial distribution of the initial torque
\tau=-\partial\mathscr{F}/\partial\theta
for the Gaussian intensity profile of the pump (Fig.
3

In fact, ring formation occurs if
\tau

Thus, it should be a threshold value for pump-induced temperature changes

for ring formation.
Indeed, in the experiment,

However, a more detailed analysis suggests that the rings also appear in the MO snapshots when
\tau

Moreover, the deviation of the equilibrium angle
\Delta\theta
varies with temperature, and the threshold for ring formation is also defined by the change in sign of the derivative

We notice that the derivative
\partial\theta/\partial T

This means that the initial MO image will have only one initial ring when the pump fluence and the corresponding

Lastly, the gradient of
\Delta T
is responsible for the spatial variation of
\theta(r)
and the corresponding slow varying inhomogeneous background of opposite signs in the center
R_{1}
and the outer area
R_{2}
(Fig.

Moreover, the increasing number of rings with time is also attributed to the temperature gradient over the pump spot.
In fact, the gradient of
\Delta T
produces long-lasting spatial variation of the parameters
M_{s}
and
K_{u}
.
The ones define the local magnetization precession frequencies, which vary in space, resulting in phase variation over the pump spot with time.

Finally, although the similarity of the ring-like pattern reported here and the final magnetization state after single-pulse magnetization switching
Davies et al. 2019
;
Peng et al. 2023
;
Mishra et al. 2023
, our observations reveal a markedly different mechanism at an early stage of the dynamics.
In particular, the ring pattern in Fig.
1
b is formed already in 0.04 ns after laser excitation, and we also see it clearly for the integrated area results (Fig.
2
b), while in Refs.
Davies et al. 2019
;
Mishra et al. 2023
laser excitation first led to a homogeneous in sign laser-induced torque, and the rings formed only afterward on a time of precession period.
Thus, our finding shows the additional torque-based mechanism behind the formation of a ring-like pattern.
Moreover, the magnetization precession reported here and the switching in Refs.
Davies et al. 2019
;
Mishra et al. 2023
are two fundamentally different processes: linear and non-linear.
To compare their amplitudes, we used the MO contrast in Fig.
1

For the case in Fig.
1
b, this amplitude is about 1.5
∘
.
In contrast, the strong anharmonic spin dynamics reported in Ref.
Davies et al. 2019
implied amplitudes well above 10
∘
.

In conclusion, we show that spin dynamics excited in a typical pump-probe experiment can be strongly inhomogeneous due to its initial torque distribution, and the inhomogeneity takes place on spatial scales much smaller than the pump spot size.
The torque-based mechanism is very general and could be expected in a wide variety of magnetic materials.
It leads to a situation where probing of the whole pumped area gives a decrease in amplitude of the detected magnetization oscillations with live times smaller than in the center of the pump (signals
R_{1}
and
R_{1}+R_{2}
in Fig.
2
b).
Overlooking this effect in pump-probe experiments may result in a dramatic underestimation of the spin precession parameters.

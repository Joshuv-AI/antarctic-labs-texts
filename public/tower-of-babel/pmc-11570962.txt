Hydronephrosis secondary to isolated renal tuberculosis in an immunocompetent adolescent: “A case report”
Authors: Zamari Noori, Mohammad Jawid Nazari
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Isolated renal tuberculosis is a rare form of extrapulmonary TB, especially in immunocompetent individuals. This case report details the diagnosis and management of a 14-year-old male who presented with massive hydronephrosis of unknown cause. Initial investigations, including urine cultures, were inconclusive. However, percutaneous nephrostomy provided a key diagnostic opportunity. This case highlights the importance of considering TB in the differential diagnosis of patients with unexplained urinary tract abnormalities, even in immunocompetent patients.

Introduction

TB is a granulomatous disease caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis.1 It is still a major global health concern. According to WHO data, Ten million new cases are reported worldwide each year. Asia and Africa have the most cases (46 % and 23 %, respectively), followed by the Western Pacific (18 %), Eastern Mediterranean (8.1 %), Americas (3.1 %) and Europe (2.2 %).2 Although pulmonary TB is the most prevalent type, 15-20 % of cases are extrapulmonary TB. One recognized extrapulmonary site for tuberculosis is the genitourinary system3

The pathogenesis of genitourinary tuberculosis is divided into two forms Disseminated infection and localized genitourinary disease. In both cases, the lesions are primarily determined by the individual's immune system, pathogen virulence, and infection site.

The disseminated form of Mycobacterium tuberculosis occurs when bacteria spread from the lungs to other parts of the body via the bloodstream or lymphatic system. This condition typically affects multiple organs, such as the kidneys, liver, spleen, bone marrow, and central nervous system. Individual with compromised immune systems, such as those living with HIV or other underlying medical conditions, are more vulnerable to this form4

The bacilli in localized renal disease are pulmonary in origin, but lung disease is rarely found during renal TB diagnosis. Localized renal disease seems to be a consequence of a previous pulmonary primary infection.4

This case report presents a rare instance of isolated renal TB in an immunocompetent adolescent and discusses the challenge of early diagnosis and management.

Case report

A 14-year-old patient presented to the emergency ward with left-sided flank pain, urinary retention, hematuria and dysuria. Physical examination revealed bulging in the suprapubic region and tenderness on the left flank.

The initial ultrasound imaging revealed massive hydronephrosis on the left side and cystitis but the cause was not immediately determined.

Laboratory tests showed elevated blood urea nitrogen (BUN) at 24 mg/dL, creatinine at 1.8 mg/dL, hematuria and pyuria, but urine cultures were negative for typical pathogens. Urine was not cultured for mycobacterium tuberculosis because the patient's history did not suggest prior tuberculosis exposure, and there were no respiratory symptoms, abnormal chest x-ray, or CT scan.

Based on the clinical situation, patient was considered a candidate for percutaneous nephrostomy to Immediately relieve hydronephrosis and prevent further damage.

During the nephrostomy procedure, the left renal capsule was thicker than normal, the calyces were dilated and a yellowish substance coated mucosal surface of the calyces (Fig. 2). Antegrade pyelography revealed no obstruction or stricture. Raising suspicion to caseous necrosis a calyceal aspirate was obtained and sent for analysis (Fig. 3). Following diagnosis, the patient was started on the standard anti-tuberculosis regimen: HRZE (isoniazid, rifampicin, pyrazinamide, and ethambutol) for a total of nine months. The nephrostomy tube remained in place for 10 days to manage the hydronephrosis.

The patient's treatment proceeded without major complications, and at the 12-month follow-up, his condition was satisfactory, with normal renal structure and function (Fig. 4), no residual signs of cystitis and no recurrence of symptoms.Fig. 4Resolved left Renal Massive Hydronephrosis following twelve months.Fig. 4

Discussion

Isolated renal tuberculosis (renal tuberculosis without concurrent pulmonary or other forms of extrapulmonary tuberculosis) is a rare form of TB and is frequently misdiagnosed until advanced stages due to its non-specific presentation. In this case, normal immunity and no history of TB exposure makes diagnosis even more challenging. The patient's initial diagnosis of massive hydronephrosis and cystitis mimicked other more common conditions, leading to delayed identification of the underlying cause.

This case emphasizes the importance of raising clinical suspicion in patients with unexplained urinary tract symptoms, especially in areas where tuberculosis is prevalent. While urine cultures are the standard diagnostic test for urinary infections, they are frequently negative in renal tuberculosis cases due to the inconsistent shedding of bacilli in urine.5 In this case, percutaneous nephrostomy and histological examination of necrotic material provided a more definitive diagnostic pathway.

In comparison to the literature, isolated renal tuberculosis in immunocompetent adolescents is uncommon, with most cases occurring in immunocompromised individuals.6 This case highlights the importance of early intervention, as untreated renal TB can lead to irreversible kidney damage and other severe complications.1

The standard TB treatment regimen was effective in this case, with a favorable outcome after twelve months of follow-up.

Conclusion

This case underscores the importance of considering TB in the differential diagnosis of patients with unexplained urinary tract symptoms, even in immunocompetent individuals. Early diagnosis through appropriate diagnostic methods, such as Imaging (ultrasound and CT scan), histological examination and PCR is essential for preventing long-term complications. Prompt initiation of anti-tuberculosis therapy resulted in a favorable outcome in this case, highlighting the efficacy of the standard HRZE regimen.

2
Absolutely Continuous Representing Measures of Truncated Moment Functionals

2.1
Main Result

To formulate and prove the main results in this section about truncated moment functionals we need the following concept.

Definition 2.1

(
[
23
, p. 3022, general setting (c)]
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathfrak{A})
be a measurable space and let
\mathcal{V}
be a vector space of measurable functions
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathds{R}
.
A family
\mathcal{F}=\{\delta_{\sigma,x}\,|\,x\in\mathcal{X},\ \sigma\in\Lambda\}
,
\Lambda
a set, of measures
\delta_{\sigma,x}
on
\mathcal{X}
such that for any
x\in\mathcal{X}
there exists a sequence
(\sigma_{x,i})_{i\in\mathds{N}_{0}}\subseteq\Lambda
with

\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{X}}f(y)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\delta_{\sigma_{x,i},x}(y)=f(x)

(1)

for all
f\in\mathcal{V}
is called a
Dirac-approximating family for
\mathcal{V}
.

Our guiding example of a Dirac-approximating family is the following which is also absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\lambda
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
\Lambda=(0,1)
, let
\lambda
be the Lebesgue measure on
\mathds{R}
, and let

g_{\sigma,x}(y):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}\cdot e^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}

be the Gaussian distribution centered at
x\in\mathds{R}
with variance
\sigma\in\Lambda
.
Then we have

\lim_{\sigma\searrow 0}\int_{\mathds{R}}f(y)\cdot g_{\sigma,x}(y)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\lambda(y)=f(x)

for all
f\in C_{b}(\mathds{R},\mathds{R})
bounded continuous functions.
Therefore,
\{\delta_{\sigma,x}\,|\,\sigma\in(0,1),\ x\in\mathds{R}\}
with
\delta_{\sigma,x}
defined by

\mathrm{d}\delta_{\sigma,x}(y):=g_{\sigma,x}(y)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(y)

is a Dirac-approximating family for
C_{b}(\mathds{R},\mathds{R})
.

\circ

We now show that condition (
1
) for Dirac-approximating families is crucial in the existence in absolutely continuous representing measures.

Example 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=[0,2]
, let
\mathcal{V}=\mathrm{lin}\,\{f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}\}
with
f_{0}(x)=1
,
f_{1}(x)=x
, and

f_{2}(x):=\begin{cases}x^{2}&\text{for}\ x\in[0,2]\setminus\{1\}\ \text{and}\\
-1&\text{for}\ x=1.\end{cases}

Then

\lim_{\sigma\searrow 0}\int_{0}^{2}f_{2}(y)\cdot g_{\sigma,1}(y)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\lambda(y)=1\neq-1=f_{2}(1).

In fact,
\mathcal{V}
has no Dirac-approximation family which is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\lambda
on
[0,2]
.
Hence, the linear functional
L:\mathcal{V}\to\mathds{R}
represented by
\lambda+\frac{8}{3}\delta_{1}
is in the interior of the moment cone but can not be represented by an absolutely continuous measure with respect to
\lambda
.
To see this assume
\rho(x)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)
with
\rho\in C([0,2],\mathds{R})
represents
L
.
Then

\int_{0}^{2}f_{2}(x)\cdot\rho(x)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\lambda(x)=L(f_{2})=0

implies
\mathrm{supp}\,\rho\subseteq\{0\}
which implies
L(1)=0
.
This is a contradiction since
L(1)>0
.

\circ

We have seen that if (
1
) does not hold with a family of absolutely continuous measures then we can construct a moment functional in the interior of the moment cone which has no absolutely continuous representing measure with respect to the Lebesgue measure.

We now prove that if there is at least one Dirac-approximating family of absolutely continuous measures with respect to the Lebesgue measure then any moment functional in the interior of the moment cone has an absolutely continuous representing measure.
To prove this result we need the following fundamental result due to H. Richter.

Richter’s Theorem 2.4

(
[
76
, Satz 4]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
, let
(\mathcal{X},\mathfrak{A})
be a measurable space, let
\mathcal{V}
be a
n
-dimensional real vector space of measurable functions
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathds{R}
, and let
L:\mathcal{V}\to\mathds{R}
be a linear functional given by some measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{X}
, i.e.,
L(f)=\int_{\mathcal{X}}f(x)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)
for all
f\in\mathcal{V}
.
Then there exist a
k\in\mathds{N}_{0}
with
k\leq n
, points
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\in\mathcal{X}
, and coefficients
c_{1},\dots,c_{k}>0
such that

L(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}\cdot f(x_{i})

holds for all
f\in\mathcal{V}
.

For more on the history of Richter’s Theorem see e.g.
[
29
]
.
From Richter’s Theorem we gain the following, which was first proven in
[
23
]
.
We include the proof here to demonstrate how easy the existence of absolutely continuous representing measures follows from a Dirac-approximating family and Richter’s Theorem.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
23
, Thm. 17]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
,
let
(\mathcal{X},\mathfrak{A})
be a measurable space,
let
\mathcal{V}
be a
n
-dimensional vector space of measurable functions
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathds{R}
,
let
\mathcal{F}=\{\delta_{\sigma,x}\,|\,\sigma\in\Lambda,\ x\in\mathcal{X}\}
be a Dirac-approximating family,
and let
L
be a linear functional on
\mathcal{V}
from the interior of the moment cone.
Then there exist a
k\in\mathds{N}
with
k\leq n
,
points
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\in\mathcal{X}
, coefficients
c_{1},\dots,c_{k}>0
, and parameters
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{k}\in\Lambda
such that

L(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}\cdot\delta_{\sigma_{i},x_{i}}

holds for all
f\in\mathcal{V}
.

Proof.

Since
L
is a moment functional by
Richter’s Theorem

2.4
there is a
k\in\mathds{N}
with
k\leq n
, there are points
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\in\mathcal{X}
, and
c_{1}^{\prime},\dots,c_{k}^{\prime}>0
such that
L
is represented by
\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}^{\prime}\delta_{x_{i}}
.
Since
L
is in the interior of the moment cone we can assume
k=n
, i.e.,
L
is in the interior of the simplicial cone spanned by
l_{i}:\mathcal{V}\to\mathds{R}
which are represented by
\delta_{x_{i}}
for all
i=1,\dots,n
.

Since
\mathcal{F}
is a Dirac-approximating family there exist
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}\in\Lambda
such that
L
is in the interior of the simplicial cone spanned by
L_{i}
represented by
\delta_{\sigma_{i},x_{i}}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, which proves the assertion.
∎

Absolutely Continuous Representing Measures of Full (Signed) Moment Functionals: Compact Case on
[0,1]^{n}

We have seen in the previous section how easy the truncated case can be solved once one introduces the concept of Dirac approximating sequences in
Definition

2.1
.
We will now treat the much more complicated case of full moment functionals.

For this section we remind the reader of the concept of derivatives of moments and moment functionals which was introduced in
[
24
]
.
For a moment sequence
s=(s_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
with representing measure
\mu
, i.e.,

s_{\alpha}=\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)

for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
, we define the
\partial^{\beta}
-derivative
\partial^{\beta}s=(\partial^{\beta}s_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
in the distributional sense

\partial^{\beta}s_{\alpha}:=(-1)^{|\beta|}\cdot\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}\partial^{\beta}x^{\alpha}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.
In the distributional sense we then have that
\partial^{\beta}s
is represented by
\partial^{\beta}\mu
.

We deal here with the full (signed) moment problem on
[0,1]^{n}
.
To solve it we need the following.

Signed Hausdorff’s Theorem 3.9

(
[
45
, p. 232, II.]
or see e.g.
[
65
, Thm. 3.3.1]
)
.

Let
(s_{i})_{i\in\mathds{N}_{0}}\subseteq\mathds{R}
be a real sequence.
The following are equivalent:

We also allow now complex sequences

Remark 4.26
.

We have seen that if
\hat{\mu}
is
L^{1}
or
L^{2}
then we get a unique absolutely continuous representing measure
\mu
.
Additionally, the characteristic function
\hat{\mu}
determines the measure
\mu
uniquely
[
18
, Prop. 3.8.6]
.
The reader might therefore wonder if we can apply the (inverse) Fourier transformation to

even if

is not absolutely continuous with an

-density.
This is in fact possible and known as

[

, Sec. 16.6]
or
[
13
, Thm. 26.2]
for the

Let

Then

\frac{\mu(\{a\})}{2}+\mu((a,b))+\frac{\mu(\{b\})}{2}\;=\;\lim_{T\nearrow\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{e^{-ita}-e^{-itb}}{it}\cdot\hat{\mu}(t)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}t.

(10)

Hence, if
\hat{\mu}=f
is entire and therefore given by the moments
s_{k}
for all

we have that

\frac{\mu(\{a\})}{2}+\mu((a,b))+\frac{\mu(\{b\})}{2}\;=\;\lim_{T\nearrow\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-T}^{T}\!\!\frac{e^{-ita}-e^{-itb}}{it}\cdot\left(\sum_{k\in\mathds{N}_{0}}\frac{i^{k}\cdot s_{k}}{k!}\cdot t^{k}\right)\mathrm{d}t

.
On
\mathds{R}^{n}
with

a similar but more cumbersome formula like (

) exists
[
34
]
.
But with the Fourier and inverse Fourier transformation

(\mathcal{F}\varphi)(x):=\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}\varphi(z)\cdot e^{-i\cdot x\cdot z}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}z\;\;\text{and}\;\;(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\varphi)(x):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}\varphi(z)\cdot e^{i\cdot x\cdot z}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}z

[

\mu(\varphi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}\varphi(x)\cdot e^{i\cdot x\cdot z}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}x\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{i^{|\alpha|}\cdot s_{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\cdot z^{\alpha}\right)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}z

Theorem 4.27

(Bochner Test)
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
and
s=(s_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
be a real sequence such that
s_{0}=1
and

f(z):=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{i^{|\alpha|}\cdot s_{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\cdot z^{\alpha}

is entire.
Set

f_{1}(z):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}:\\
|\alpha|\in 2\mathds{N}_{0}+1\end{subarray}}\frac{i^{|\alpha|}\cdot s_{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\cdot z^{\alpha}\quad\text{and}\quad f_{2}(z):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}:\\
|\alpha|\in 2\mathds{N}_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{i^{|\alpha|}\cdot s_{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\cdot z^{\alpha}.

Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

s
is a determinate moment sequence.

(ii)

\big(f(z_{j}-z_{k})\big)_{j,k=1}^{d}\succeq 0
holds for all
d\in\mathds{N}
and
z_{1},\dots,z_{d}\in\mathds{R}^{n}
.

(iii)

For all
d\in\mathds{N}
and
z_{1},\dots,z_{d}\in\mathds{R}^{n}
we have

\big(f_{2}(z_{j}-z_{k})\big)_{j,k=0}^{d}\succeq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\big(f_{2}(z_{j}-z_{k})\big)_{j,k=0}^{d}\succeq\big(f_{1}(z_{j}-z_{k})\big)_{j,k=0}^{d}.

Proof.

(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii):
By Bochner’s Theorem a continuous function
f
with
f(0)=1
is the characteristic function of a bounded measure if and only if (ii) holds.
Determinacy of
s
follows again from
f
being entire and hence unique.
Note,
(f(z_{j}-z_{k}))_{j,k=1}^{d}\succeq 0
is well-defined since
(f(z_{j}-z_{k}))_{j,k=1}^{d}
is Hermitian.

(ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii):
We have
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
with
f_{1}
odd and
f_{2}
even,
(f_{1}(z_{j}-z_{k}))_{j,k=1}^{d}
Hermitian, and
(f_{2}(z_{j}-z_{k}))_{j,k=1}^{d}
real symmetric.
Hence, the equivalence follows from replacing
z_{1},\dots,z_{d}\in\mathds{R}^{n}
by
-z_{1},\dots,-z_{d}\in\mathds{R}^{n}
.
∎

To determine if
s
is a moment sequence by Haviland’s Theorem
[

47
]
one has to check

for all

and

.
This is NP-hard.
Only in the case

for all

since here all non-negative polynomials are sums of squares

.

Since testing sequences for being a moment sequence is NP-hard any simplification can only hold for some sequences.
In

4.27
for sequences

is entire this reduces to checking complex positive semi-definiteness of the complex

-matrices

Sequences

for all

and for some

are such sequences which can be checked by

4.27
.
If

is not entire then
s_{\alpha}
with

are clearly violating coming from a measure supported on
[-c,c]^{n}
.

Theorem

4.27
covers entire
f
’s.

We use the concept of a Dirac-approximating family to show that

In

We continue with discussions and corollaries to show that the density functions can be chosen to be

The proof of

Finally, in

Because of the great flexibility on
\mathds{R}^{n}

s=(s_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
is such that

f(z):=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{i^{|\alpha|}\cdot s_{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\cdot z^{\alpha}

is entire.
Of course,
f

\hat{\mu}

\mu

The function

As in signal processing the image (here the measure) is recovered by the inverse Fourier transform of
f

Since the regularity of the density of an absolutely continuous measure can be controlled on the Fourier side we find full characterizations of such densities.

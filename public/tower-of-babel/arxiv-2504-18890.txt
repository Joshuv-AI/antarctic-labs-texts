Convergence and non-convergence phenomena in Euler-Maxwell to MHD transitions
Dong-ha Kim, Junha Kim, Jihoon Lee

Abstract
In this work, we investigate the difference estimate for a class of Euler-Maxwell system and those of magnetohydrodynamics (in short, MHD) systems in three dimensions. We decompose the Euler-Maxwell system into three parts, namely the MHD system, auxiliary linear system and error part system . As a result, we obtain the convergence of the velocity of the fluid u u , electric fields E E and magnetic fields B B from the Euler-Maxwell system toward the MHD system in L t p ​ L x 2 L^{p}_{t}L^{2}_{x} as the speed of light c c approaches infinity for p ∈ [ 1 , ∞ ] p\in[1,\infty] . We also derived non-convergence results of electric current j j or c ​ E cE , and these results are classified by a certain threshold for p p . Finally, we investigate how the L 2 L^{2} -energy flow of Euler-Maxwell system evolves as c tends to infinity, leading to the vanishing of Ampère’s equation in the Euler-Maxwell system.



Keywords:
Euler-Maxwell, Magnetohydrodynamics, Difference estimate, Convergence and non-convergence of solutions.

Introduction

1.1
Motivation of the work

We consider incompressible Euler-Maxwell system with Ohm’s law in three-dimensional space:

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \text{(Euler equation)}\quad&\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=j\times B,\quad&\operatorname{div}u=0,\\
\text{(Amp\`{e}re’s equation)}\quad&\frac{1}{c}\partial_{t}E-\nabla\times B=-j,\quad&\operatorname{div}E=0,\\
\text{(Faraday’s equation)}\quad&\frac{1}{c}\partial_{t}B+\nabla\times E=0,\qquad&\operatorname{div}B=0,\\
\text{(Ohm's law)}\quad&j=\sigma(cE+\mathbb{P}(u\times B)),\qquad&\operatorname{div}j=0.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(1.1)

In the above system,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
denote time and space variables and the operator
\mathbb{P}=I+\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\operatorname{div}
denotes Leray projection. The field
u=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})=u(t,x)
denotes the velocity of the fluid, while
E=(E_{1},E_{2},E_{3})=E(t,x)
and
B=(B_{1},B_{2},B_{3})=B(t,x)
stand for the electric fields and magnetic fields, respectively. Moreover,

p=p(t,x)
denotes the scalar pressure which is also an unknown. Additionally,
j
denotes the electric current which is not an unknown since it is completely determined by
u,E
and
B
by Ohm’s law. Finally, the positive constants
c\geq c_{0}
and
\sigma>0
represent the speed of light and the electrical conductivity, for some fixed constant
c_{0}>0
. Since we are not interested in the inviscid limit of
\sigma
, we fix
\sigma=1
in this paper.

To ensure compatibility of the initial data, we assume the divergence free condition
\operatorname{div}u_{0}=\operatorname{div}E_{0}=\operatorname{div}B_{0}=0.
One can observe that the divergence-free condition
\operatorname{div}E=\operatorname{div}B=\operatorname{div}j=0
is preserved over time by taking the divergence of Maxwell’s equations in (
1.1
) which consist of Ampère’s and Faraday’s equations. Moreover, the Euler-Maxwell system (
1.1
) satisfies the formal energy conservations in the following form:

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|E\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|B\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+\|j\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0.

(1.2)

The Euler-Maxwell system (
1.1
) describes the dynamics of a charged fluid, known as plasma, subject to Lorentz force
j\times B
. A plasma is often referred to the fourth state of matter, which describes a wide variety of macroscopically neutral substances containing many interacting free electrons and ionized atoms.
Fluids like plasma are known to possess the properties of perfect conductors; hence, it may be physically assumed that the term
\frac{1}{c}\partial_{t}E
vanishes. Consequently, (
1.1
) can be formally reduced to the usual incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (in short, MHD) system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=(\nabla\times B)\times B,\qquad&\operatorname{div}u=0,\\
&\partial_{t}B-\Delta B=\nabla\times(u\times B),\qquad&\operatorname{div}B=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

which describes the behavior of a conducting inviscid fluid or a plasma in the presence of a magnetic field. There have been extensive mathematical studies on a rigorous treatment to MHD system.
For these, we refer to references
[

,

,
12
]
.

1.1
) with a dissipation term of

) has attracted a lot of attention.
The global existence of two-dimensional solutions has been studied in

Let

\displaystyle(u^{c},E^{c},B^{c})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow(\overline{u},0,\overline{B}),

\displaystyle j^{c}

\displaystyle cE^{c}

\begin{gathered}\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\overline{u}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\overline{B}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\times\overline{B}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|B_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).\end{gathered}

\begin{gathered}\frac{1}{2}\left(\|u^{c}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|E^{c}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|B^{c}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\int_{0}^{t}\|j^{c}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\\

(u^{c},B^{c},E^{c})
to the Euler-Maxwell systems satisfy the convergences (

\begin{gathered}\left(\|u^{c}(t)\|_{L^{2}},\|E^{c}(t)\|_{L^{2}},\|B^{c}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\right)\rightarrow\left(\|\overline{u}(t)\|_{L^{2}},0,\|\overline{B}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\right),\\

(1.8)

for

Furthermore, we formally have shown, using a proof by contradiction, that the total convergence (

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\widetilde{u}+(\widetilde{u}\cdot\nabla)\widetilde{u}+\nabla\widetilde{p}&=j^{c}\times B^{c}-\overline{j}\times\overline{B}-(\overline{u}\cdot\nabla)\widetilde{u}-(\widetilde{u}\cdot\nabla)\overline{u},&\operatorname{div}\widetilde{u}=0,\\
\frac{1}{c}\partial_{t}\widetilde{E}-\nabla\times\widetilde{B}+c\widetilde{E}&=-\mathbb{P}(u^{c}\times B^{c}-\overline{u}\times\overline{B}),&\operatorname{div}\widetilde{B}=0,\\
\frac{1}{c}\partial_{t}\widetilde{B}+\nabla\times\widetilde{E}&=0,&\operatorname{div}\widetilde{E}=0,\\
\widetilde{u}|_{t=0}=u^{c}_{0}-u_{0},\quad&\widetilde{E}|_{t=0}=0,\quad\widetilde{B}|_{t=0}=B^{c}_{0}-B_{0}.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

We shall crucially use the fact that initial data of error part tend to zero where it holds due to Condition
1.1
, to show that the error part converges to zero as
c\to\infty
with certain specified decay rate, and this is also important step in our analysis. Notice that the system (
1.22
) appears similar to the damped Maxwell
system introduced in
[
3
]
, but it exhibits subtle differences in the source term. Here, the design of the auxiliary linear system (
1.22
) is based on the formal assumption that
cE^{c}
converges to
\overline{E}
and
B^{c}
converges to
\overline{B}
as well as the expectation that
(E_{L},B_{L})
closely resembles
(E^{c},B^{c}-\overline{B})
. In other words, it is designed by inversely predicting the form of the source term under the assumption that
cE_{L}
converges to
\overline{E}
and
B_{L}
converges to
0
as
c\to\infty
.

In summary, we decompose the solution to (
1.1
) into three pieces, i.e.,

(\mbox{soln. to \eqref{main_eqn}})=({\mbox{soln. to limit system \eqref{eq-MHD}}})+({\mbox{soln. to auxiliary linear system}})+({\mbox{error part}}).

Since the error part always tends to zero, the difference estimate between the MHD system and Euler-Maxwell systems can be understood by analyzing the linearized system. This is the overall story of the main theorem.

Outline of the paper

This paper is organized as follow. In section

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{c}\partial_{t}E_{L}-\nabla\times B_{L}+cE_{L}=\overline{E},\quad&\operatorname{div}E_{L}=0,\\

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{c}\partial_{t}E_{L}-\nabla\times\mathbf{B}+cE_{L}=\mathbb{P}(\overline{u}\times\overline{B}),\qquad&\operatorname{div}E_{L}=0,\\

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
m>\frac{5}{2}
and
c\geq c_{0}
be fixed. For any initial data
\phi_{1},\,\phi_{2}\in H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
\operatorname{div}\phi_{1}=\operatorname{div}\phi_{2}=0

f\in C([0,T];H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, there exists a unique solution
(E_{L},\,\mathbf{B})\in C([0,T];H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap C^{1}((0,T);H^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
to the linear system

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{c}\partial_{t}E_{L}-\nabla\times\mathbf{B}+cE_{L}=\mathbb{P}f,\qquad&\operatorname{div}E_{L}=0,\\
&\frac{1}{c}\partial_{t}\mathbf{B}+\nabla\times E_{L}=0,\qquad&\operatorname{div}\mathbf{B}=0,\\
&E_{L}|_{t=0}=\phi_{1},\quad\mathbf{B}|_{t=0}=\phi_{2},\end{aligned}\end{cases}

with the a priori bound:

\begin{gathered}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\|E_{L}(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|B_{L}(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\right)+\int_{0}^{T}\|cE_{L}(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\|\phi_{1}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|\phi_{2}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|f(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{gathered}

(3.4)

Next, we introduce useful lemma which presents the estimate of
\partial_{t}\overline{E}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
m\geq 3
, let
(\overline{u},\,\overline{B})
be a solution of MHD system (
1.3
) satisfying (
1.10
)-(
1.11
), and we consider
\overline{E}\in C([0,T];H^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}([0,T];H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
defined by (

).
Then, there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
m
and
\overline{M}
such that

(3.5)

In particular, there exists a constant
\tilde{C}
, depending only on
\overline{M}
such that

\|\partial_{t}\overline{E}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}+\|\partial_{t}\overline{E}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})}\leq\tilde{C}.

(3.6)

Proof.

We estimate
\|\partial_{t}\overline{E}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{m-3})}
first. We recall (
1.9
) and have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\overline{E}\|_{H^{m-3}}

\displaystyle=\|\partial_{t}\mathbb{P}(\nabla\times\overline{B}-\overline{u}\times\overline{B})\|_{H^{m-3}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\partial_{t}\overline{B}\|_{H^{m-3}}+\|\partial_{t}\overline{u}\|_{\dot{H}^{m-3}}\|\overline{B}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{t}\overline{B}\|_{\dot{H}^{m-3}}.

From the first equation of (
1.3
), it follows

\|\partial_{t}\overline{u}\|_{\dot{H}^{m-3}}\leq\|\partial_{t}\overline{u}\|_{H^{m-1}}\leq\|(\overline{u}\cdot\nabla)\overline{u}\|_{H^{m-1}}+\|(\nabla\times\overline{B})\times\overline{B}\|_{H^{m-1}}\leq 2\overline{M}^{2}.

(3.7)

We have from the second equation of (
1.3
)

\|\partial_{t}\overline{B}\|_{\dot{H}^{m-3}}\leq\|\partial_{t}\overline{B}\|_{H^{m-2}}\leq\|\Delta\overline{B}\|_{H^{m-2}}+\|\overline{u}\times\overline{B}\|_{H^{m-1}}\leq\overline{M}+\overline{M}^{2}.

(3.8)

Thus, we obtain

\|\partial_{t}\overline{E}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{m-3})}\leq\overline{M}+2\overline{M}^{2}+3\overline{M}^{3}.

(3.9)

Next, we estimate
\|\partial_{t}\overline{E}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{m-2})}
. It follows from (
1.9
) that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\overline{E}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{m-2})}\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla\partial_{t}\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{m-2})}+\|\partial_{t}\overline{u}\times\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{m-2})}+\|\overline{u}\times\partial_{t}\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{m-2})}.

(3.10)

Note that there exists

such that

\|\overline{B}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(m)\|\nabla\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}}^{\theta}\|\nabla^{m+1}\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\theta},

and this leads to

\|\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{\infty})}\leq C\,\|\nabla\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{m})}\leq C\overline{M}.

(3.11)

Using (
3.7
) and (
3.11

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\overline{u}\times\overline{B}\|_{H^{m-2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla^{m-2}(\partial_{t}\overline{u}\times\overline{B})\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}\overline{u}\times\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla^{m-2}\partial_{t}\overline{u}\|_{L^{2}}\|\overline{B}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{t}\overline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{m-2}\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}\overline{u}\|_{L^{2}}\|\overline{B}\|_{L^{\infty}}

and

\|\partial_{t}\overline{u}\times\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{m-2})}\lesssim\|\partial_{t}\overline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{m})}\|\nabla\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{m})}\lesssim\overline{M}^{3}

Similarly, it follows from (
3.8
) that

\displaystyle\|\overline{u}\times\partial_{t}\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{m-2})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\|\overline{u}\|_{H^{m-2}}\|\partial_{t}\overline{B}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\|\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{t}\overline{B}\|_{H^{m-2}}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{m-1})}\|\partial_{t}\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{m-1})}

\displaystyle\leq C\overline{M}\|\partial_{t}\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{m-1})}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{m-1})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Delta\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{m-1})}+\|\overline{u}\times\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{m})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{m})}+\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{m})}\|\partial_{t}\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{m})}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{M}+\overline{M}^{2}.

Combining these, we have

\|\partial_{t}\overline{E}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{m-2})}\lesssim_{m}\overline{M}+2\overline{M}^{2}+2\overline{M}^{3}.

The proof is completed.

3.1
). Then, it holds

\frac{1}{c^{2}}\partial_{t}(cE_{L}-\overline{E})-\nabla\times B_{L}+(cE_{L}-\overline{E})=-\frac{1}{c^{2}}\partial_{t}\overline{E}

(3.15)

and

\partial_{t}B_{L}+\nabla\times(cE_{L}-\overline{E})=0

(3.16)

on time interval
[0,T]
. Let us briefly introduce the idea as follows. We formally let
c
tend to infinity in (
3.15
) and substitute the result into (
3.16
). Then, we obtain the heat equation
\partial_{t}B_{L}-\Delta B_{L}=0
with
B_{L}(0)=0
formally at
c=\infty
. Considering the nature of this limit equation, we derive estimates by constructing parabolic estimates for the auxiliary linear system (
3.1
).

Step 1 (Parabolic estimate for
B_{L}
)
We first claim that

\begin{gathered}\frac{1}{c^{2}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int|\nabla\times(cE_{L}-\overline{E})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int|\nabla\times B_{L}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{T}\int|\nabla\times(cE_{L}-\overline{E})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
\leq\frac{1}{c^{4}}\int_{0}^{T}\int|\nabla\partial_{t}\overline{E}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{c^{2}}\int|\nabla(cE_{0}^{c}-\overline{E}(0))|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{gathered}

for all
c\geq c_{0}
. By testing
\nabla\times\nabla\times(cE_{L}-\overline{E})
and
\nabla\times\nabla\times B_{L}
to (
3.15
)-(
3.16
), we see

\begin{gathered}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\frac{1}{c^{2}}\int|\nabla\times(cE_{L}-\overline{E})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int|\nabla\times B_{L}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)+\int|\nabla\times(cE_{L}-\overline{E})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
=-\frac{1}{c^{2}}\int\nabla\times\partial_{t}\overline{E}\cdot\nabla\times(cE_{L}-\overline{E})\,\mathrm{d}x\\
\leq\frac{1}{2}\int|\nabla\times(cE_{L}-\overline{E})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2c^{4}}\int|\nabla\partial_{t}\overline{E}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{gathered}

Integrating over time and using the divergence free condition, it follows that

\begin{gathered}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\frac{1}{c^{2}}\int|\nabla(cE_{L}-\overline{E})(t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int|\nabla B_{L}(t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{T}\int|\nabla(cE_{L}(t)-\overline{E}(t))|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
\leq\frac{1}{c^{2}}\int|\nabla(cE_{L}-\overline{E})(0)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{c^{4}}\int_{0}^{T}\int|\nabla\partial_{t}\overline{E}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.\end{gathered}

(3.17)

Moreover, it follows from (
3.15
) that

-\frac{1}{c^{2}}\int\partial_{t}(cE_{L}-\overline{E})\cdot\nabla\times B_{L}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int|\nabla B_{L}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int(cE_{L}-\overline{E})\cdot\nabla\times B_{L}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{c^{2}}\int\partial_{t}\overline{E}\cdot\nabla\times B_{L}\,\mathrm{d}x.

(3.18)

On the other hand, it follows from (
3.16
) that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int|B_{L}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int\nabla\times(cE_{L}-\overline{E})\cdot B_{L}\,\mathrm{d}x=0,

(3.19)

-\frac{1}{c^{2}}\int\nabla\times(\partial_{t}B_{L})\cdot(cE_{L}-\overline{E})\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{c^{2}}\int|\nabla\times(cE_{L}-\overline{E})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

(3.20)

Summing (
3.18
)-(
3.20
), one has

\begin{gathered}-\frac{1}{c^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int\nabla\times B_{L}\cdot(cE_{L}-\overline{E})\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int|B_{L}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int|\nabla B_{L}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
=\frac{1}{c^{2}}\int\partial_{t}\overline{E}\cdot\nabla\times B_{L}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{c^{2}}\int|\nabla(cE_{L}-\overline{E})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{gathered}

Integrating over time, we have

\begin{gathered}\frac{1}{2}\int|B_{L}(t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{t}\int|\nabla B_{L}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
=\frac{1}{c^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int\partial_{t}\overline{E}\cdot\nabla\times B_{L}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{c^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int|\nabla(cE_{L}-\overline{E})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{c^{2}}\int B_{L}(t)\cdot\nabla\times(cE_{L}-\overline{E})(t)\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{gathered}

By Young’s inequality and Lemma
3.2
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int|B_{L}(t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int|\nabla B_{L}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

(3.21)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2c^{4}}\int_{0}^{t}\int|\partial_{t}\overline{E}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{c^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int|\nabla(cE_{L}-\overline{E})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{c^{4}}\int|\nabla(cE_{L}-\overline{E})(t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Combining (
3.21
) and (
3.17
) with (
3.6
), we have

\begin{gathered}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int|B_{L}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{T}\int|\nabla B_{L}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{c^{2}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int|\nabla B_{L}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
\leq 8\left(\frac{1}{c^{4}}\int_{0}^{T}\int|\partial_{t}\overline{E}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{c^{6}}\int_{0}^{T}\int|\nabla\partial_{t}\overline{E}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{c^{4}}\int|\nabla(cE_{0}^{c}-\overline{E}(0))|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\\
\leq C\left(\frac{1}{c^{4}}+\frac{1}{c^{4}}\int|\nabla(cE_{0}^{c}-\overline{E}(0))|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right).\end{gathered}

(3.22)

Here, the constant
C
only depends on
\overline{M}
due to (
3.6
).
Now, we combine (
3.17
) together with (
3.22
) and employ (
3.6
) again to obtain (
3.12
).

Step 2 (Estimates for
E_{L}-\overline{E}
)
We show (
3.13
). By applying Duhamel’s principle to the first equation of (
3.1
), we have

\begin{gathered}\left\|cE_{L}(t)-\overline{E}(t)-e^{-c^{2}t}(cE_{0}^{c}-\overline{E}(0))\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq c^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c^{2}(t-\tau)}\|\nabla B_{L}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c^{2}(t-\tau)}\|\partial_{t}\overline{E}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\\
\leq\left((c^{2}e^{-c^{2}(\cdot)})*\|\nabla B_{L}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\right)(t)+\left((e^{-c^{2}(\cdot)})*\|\partial_{t}\overline{E}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\right)(t)\end{gathered}

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
. Then, taking
L^{p}
-norm over the time-interval
[0,T]
yields

\begin{gathered}\left\|cE_{L}(t)-\overline{E}(t)-e^{-c^{2}t}(cE_{0}^{c}-\overline{E}(0))\right\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{2})}\\
\leq\left\|(c^{2}e^{-c^{2}(\cdot)})*\|\nabla B_{L}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}+\left\|(e^{-c^{2}(\cdot)})*\|\partial_{t}\overline{E}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\\
=:N_{1}+N_{2}.\end{gathered}

(3.23)

To estimate
N_{1}
, we divide the case and we use Young’s convolution inequality to obtain that

\displaystyle N_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}c^{2}\|e^{-c^{2}t}\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}\|\nabla B_{L}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{2})}\quad(\text{if }1\leq p\leq 2),\\
c^{2}\|e^{-c^{2}t}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p+2}}(0,T)}\|\nabla B_{L}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}\quad(\text{if }2<p\leq\infty).\\
\end{cases}

In the case of
p=\infty
, we interpret the value of
\frac{2p}{p+2}
as
2
. If
1\leq p\leq 2
, then we have

\displaystyle c^{2}\|e^{-c^{2}t}\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}\|\nabla B_{L}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla B_{L}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{2})}\leq T^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla B_{L}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}.

If
2<p\leq\infty
, it similarly follows that

c^{2}\|e^{-c^{2}t}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p+2}}(0,T)}\|\nabla B_{L}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}\leq c^{{1-\frac{2}{p}}}\|\nabla B_{L}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}.

Hence, we bound
N_{1}
by

N_{1}\leq T^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla B_{L}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}+c^{{1-\frac{2}{p}}}\|\nabla B_{L}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}.

(3.24)

On the other hand, we have the following upper bound
N_{2}

N_{2}\leq\|e^{-c^{2}t}\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}\|\partial_{t}\overline{E}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{2})}\leq c^{-2}\|\partial_{t}\overline{E}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{2})}.

(3.25)

From (
3.23
), (
3.24
) and (
3.25
), one has

\begin{gathered}\left\|cE_{L}(t)-\overline{E}(t)-e^{-c^{2}t}(cE_{0}^{c}-\overline{E}(0))\right\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{2})}\\
\leq T^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla B_{L}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}+c^{{1-\frac{2}{p}}}\|\nabla B_{L}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}+c^{-2}\|\partial_{t}\overline{E}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{2})}.\end{gathered}

By recalling (
3.6
) and (
3.12
), we conclude (
3.13
).
This completes the proof.
∎

4
Proof of the main result

We recall the notation
\mathcal{E}^{c}_{0}
defined in (
1.12
), and we shall use the notation in this section.

\mathcal{E}^{c}_{0}:=\|u^{c}_{0}-u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|B^{c}_{0}-B_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{c^{2}}\left(\|cE^{c}_{0}-\overline{E}(0)\|_{H^{1}}+1\right).

4.1
Error part estimates

We assume all conditions for Theorem
1.2
. We recall the
Error part

(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{E},\widetilde{B})
in (
1.23
). Then, the error part
(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{E},\widetilde{B})
is in
C([0,T];H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
and satisfy

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\widetilde{u}+(\widetilde{u}\cdot\nabla)\widetilde{u}+\nabla\widetilde{p}&=j^{c}\times B^{c}-\overline{j}\times\overline{B}-(\overline{u}\cdot\nabla)\widetilde{u}-(\widetilde{u}\cdot\nabla)\overline{u},&\operatorname{div}\widetilde{u}=0,\\
\frac{1}{c}\partial_{t}\widetilde{E}-\nabla\times\widetilde{B}+c\widetilde{E}&=-\mathbb{P}(u^{c}\times B^{c}-\overline{u}\times\overline{B}),&\operatorname{div}\widetilde{B}=0,\\
\frac{1}{c}\partial_{t}\widetilde{B}+\nabla\times\widetilde{E}&=0,&\operatorname{div}\widetilde{E}=0,\\
\widetilde{u}|_{t=0}=u^{c}_{0}-u_{0},\quad&\widetilde{E}|_{t=0}=0,\quad\widetilde{B}|_{t=0}=B^{c}_{0}-B_{0}.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(4.1)

We provide an
L^{2}
-estimate for the error parts which holds uniformly in
c\in[c_{0},\infty)
. This estimate, combined with Condition
1.1
, reveals that the error part always tend to zero as
c\to\infty
. This implies that the difference between MHD system and Euler-Maxwell system can be analyzed by solely investigating mainly the auxiliary linear system (
3.1
).

Proposition 4.1

(Estimate of the error part)
.

We assume all conditions for Theorem
1.2
. Let
(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{E},\widetilde{B})
be solution to error part system (
4.1
). Then, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(\|\widetilde{u}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\widetilde{E}(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|\widetilde{B}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\Big)+c\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\widetilde{E}(t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim_{T,M,\overline{M}}\,\mathcal{E}^{c}_{0}.

(4.2)

Proof.

Below, we set

\widetilde{X}(t)=\widetilde{X}_{c}(t):=\left(\|\widetilde{u}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\widetilde{E}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\widetilde{B}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{c^{4}}\|cE^{c}_{0}-\overline{E}(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{c^{4}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then, we see that
\widetilde{X}(0)\simeq\mathcal{E}^{c}_{0}
. We claim that it holds

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\widetilde{X}^{2}\right)+c^{2}\|\widetilde{E}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left((1+\|B^{c}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|j^{c}\|_{L^{\infty}})\widetilde{X}+\|cE_{L}-\overline{E}\|_{L^{2}}\right)\widetilde{X}.

(4.3)

To see this, we write

\displaystyle\|j^{c}-\overline{j}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|cE^{c}-\overline{E}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\mathbb{P}(u^{c}\times B^{c}-\overline{u}\times\overline{B})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|cE_{L}-\overline{E}\|_{L^{2}}+\|c\widetilde{E}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\widetilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}\|B^{c}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|B_{L}+\widetilde{B}\|_{L^{2}}.

It follows from the
\widetilde{u}
-equation in (
4.1
) that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int|\widetilde{u}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int j^{c}\times(B^{c}-\overline{B})\cdot\widetilde{u}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int(j^{c}-\overline{j})\times\overline{B}\cdot\widetilde{u}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int(\widetilde{u}\cdot\nabla)\overline{u}\cdot\widetilde{u}\,\mathrm{d}x.

By Young’s inequality, there exists a constant
C>0
which depends only on
\overline{M}
such that

\displaystyle\bigg|\int(j^{c}-\overline{j})

\displaystyle\times\overline{B}\cdot\widetilde{u}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg|\leq\|j^{c}-\overline{j}\|_{L^{2}}\|\overline{B}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\widetilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\|cE_{L}-\overline{E}\|_{L^{2}}+(\|B^{c}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\overline{B}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\widetilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|B_{L}+\widetilde{B}\|_{L^{2}}\right)\|\overline{B}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\widetilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{c^{2}}{4}\|\widetilde{E}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|cE_{L}-\overline{E}\|_{L^{2}}+\|B_{L}\|_{L^{2}}+(1+\|B^{c}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}})\widetilde{X}\right)\widetilde{X}+\frac{c^{2}}{4}\|\widetilde{E}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Here, we used
\|B^{c}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1+\|B^{c}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}
in the last inequality.
Moreover, there holds by (
1.11
) that

\displaystyle\left|\int j^{c}\times(B^{c}-\overline{B})\cdot\widetilde{u}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|j^{c}\|_{L^{\infty}}(\|B_{L}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\widetilde{B}\|_{L^{2}})\|\widetilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|j^{c}\|_{L^{\infty}}(\|B_{L}\|_{L^{2}}+\widetilde{X})\widetilde{X},

\displaystyle\left|\int(\widetilde{u}\cdot\nabla)\overline{u}\cdot\widetilde{u}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\overline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\widetilde{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\overline{M}\widetilde{X}^{2}.

We combine these to deduce that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int|\widetilde{u}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C_{\overline{M}}\left(\|cE_{L}-\overline{E}\|_{L^{2}}+(1+\|j^{c}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|B_{L}\|_{L^{2}}+(1+\|B^{c}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}+\|j^{c}\|_{L^{\infty}})\widetilde{X}\right)\widetilde{X}+\frac{c^{2}}{4}\|\widetilde{E}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

(4.4)

On the other hand, it follows from the
\widetilde{E}
and
\widetilde{B}
equations in (
4.1
) that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int(|\widetilde{E}|^{2}+|\widetilde{B}|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x+c^{2}\int|\widetilde{E}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int\mathbb{P}((u^{c}-\overline{u})\times B^{c})\cdot(c\widetilde{E})\,\mathrm{d}x+\int\mathbb{P}(\overline{u}\times(B^{c}-\overline{B}))\cdot(c\widetilde{E})\,\mathrm{d}x.

It holds that

\displaystyle\left|\int\mathbb{P}((u^{c}-\overline{u})\times B^{c})\cdot(c\widetilde{E})\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\widetilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}\|B^{c}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|c\widetilde{E}\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2\|\widetilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|B^{c}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\frac{c^{2}}{8}\|\widetilde{E}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|B^{c}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\widetilde{X}^{2}+\frac{c^{2}}{8}\|\widetilde{E}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\left|\int\mathbb{P}(\overline{u}\times(B^{c}-\overline{B}))\cdot(c\widetilde{E})\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}(\|B_{L}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\widetilde{B}\|_{L^{2}})\|c\widetilde{E}\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left(\|\widetilde{B}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|B_{L}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\frac{c^{2}}{8}\|\widetilde{E}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\overline{M}^{2}\left(\widetilde{X}^{2}+\|B_{L}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\frac{c^{2}}{8}\|\widetilde{E}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

This shows that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int(|\widetilde{E}|^{2}+|\widetilde{B}|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x+c^{2}\int|\widetilde{E}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C_{\overline{M}}\left((1+\|B^{c}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2})\widetilde{X}^{2}+\|B_{L}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\frac{c^{2}}{4}\|\widetilde{E}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(4.5)

Note that (
3.12
) shows that
\|B_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{T,\overline{M}}\,\mathcal{E}^{c}_{0}\leq\,X(t)
, and we combine this to estimates (
4.4
)-(
4.5
) to obtain the following
L^{2}
-energy inequality

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int(|\widetilde{u}|^{2}+|\widetilde{E}|^{2}+|\widetilde{B}|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x+c^{2}\int|\widetilde{E}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim_{T,\overline{M}}\left(\|cE_{L}-\overline{E}\|_{L^{2}}+(1+\|B^{c}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|j^{c}\|_{L^{\infty}})\widetilde{X}\right)\widetilde{X}.

Thus, the claim (
4.3
) is proved.
Now, dividing both sides of equation (
4.3
) by
\widetilde{X}
, we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\widetilde{X}\leq C_{T,\overline{M}}\left((1+\|B^{c}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|j^{c}\|_{L^{\infty}})\widetilde{X}+\|cE_{L}-\overline{E}\|_{L^{2}}\right).

We set

f(t):=1+\|B^{c}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|j^{c}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

Recalling (
3.14
), we use Grönwall’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\widetilde{X}(t)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\left(e^{C\int_{t}^{T}f(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau}\cdot\|cE_{L}(t)-\overline{E}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}t+e^{C\int_{0}^{T}f(t)\mathrm{d}t}\cdot\widetilde{X}(0)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{C(T+M)}\int_{0}^{T}\|cE_{L}(t)-\overline{E}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t+e^{C(T+M)}\widetilde{X}(0)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T,M,\overline{M}}\,\mathcal{E}^{c}_{0}.

On the other hand, one can conclude from (
4.3
) that

\displaystyle c^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int|\widetilde{E}(t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\widetilde{X}(0)^{2}+(T+M)\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\widetilde{X}(t)^{2}+\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\widetilde{X}(t)\right)\int_{0}^{T}\|cE_{L}(t)-\overline{E}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T,M,\overline{M}}\,(\mathcal{E}^{c}_{0})^{2}.

Therefore, (
4.2
) is obtained. This completes the proof.

∎

4.2
Proof of the main results

We conclude this paper by presenting the proofs of the main theorem and corollary.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We show (
1.13
) first. From the solution decomposition and Proposition
4.1
, we have

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u^{c}(t)-\overline{u}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\widetilde{u}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\,\mathcal{E}^{c}_{0},

for all
c\geq c_{0}
. Similarly, it follows from (
3.12
) and Proposition
4.1
that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|B^{c}(t)-\overline{B}(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|B_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\widetilde{B}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\,\mathcal{E}^{c}_{0},

for all
c\geq c_{0}
. Consequently, (
1.13
) is obtained.

To prove (
1.14
), we fix
p\in[1,+\infty]
. We estimate
E^{c}
first. We see that

\|cE^{c}(t)-(\overline{E}(t)+e^{-c^{2}t}(cE_{0}-\overline{E}(0)))\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|cE_{L}(t)-(\overline{E}(t)+e^{-c^{2}t}(cE_{0}-\overline{E}(0)))\|_{L^{2}}+\|c\widetilde{E}(t)\|_{L^{2}}.

In the case of
p\leq 2
, we note from Hölder’s inequality and (
4.2
) that

\|c\widetilde{E}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{2})}\leq T^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\|c\widetilde{E}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}\lesssim\,\mathcal{E}^{c}_{0}.

If
p\geq 2
, the interpolation inequality and (
4.2
) shows

\|c\widetilde{E}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{2})}\leq\|c\widetilde{E}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\|c\widetilde{E}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}^{\frac{2}{p}}\lesssim c^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\,\mathcal{E}^{c}_{0}.

On the other hand, we recall (
3.13
)

\displaystyle\|cE_{L}(t)-(\overline{E}(t)+e^{-c^{2}t}(cE^{c}_{0}-\overline{E}(0)))\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+c^{1-\frac{2}{p}})(c^{-2}\|cE_{0}^{c}-\overline{E}(0)\|_{H^{1}}+c^{-2}),

and thus, we have

\displaystyle\|cE^{c}(t)-

\displaystyle(\overline{E}(t)+e^{-c^{2}t}(cE^{c}_{0}-\overline{E}(0)))\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\|cE_{L}(t)-(\overline{E}(t)+e^{-c^{2}t}(cE^{c}_{0}-\overline{E}(0)))\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{2})}+\|c\tilde{E}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(c^{1-\frac{2}{p}}+1)\,\mathcal{E}^{c}_{0},

for all
c\geq c_{0}
.

To control the
j
term, we see that

A.1
The a priori estimate

We provide the proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Let
\alpha
be a multi-index with
|\alpha|=m
. Then, we have

\begin{gathered}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int(|\partial^{\alpha}u|^{2}+|\partial^{\alpha}E|^{2}+|\partial^{\alpha}B|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x\\
=-\int\partial^{\alpha}(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\partial^{\alpha}u\,\mathrm{d}x+\int\partial^{\alpha}(j\times B)\cdot\partial^{\alpha}u\,\mathrm{d}x-\int\partial^{\alpha}j\cdot\partial^{\alpha}(cE)\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{gathered}

(A.1)

The divergence free condition implies

\displaystyle\left|-\int\partial^{\alpha}(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\partial^{\alpha}u\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(A.2)

Since the
j
equation gives

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}(cE)=\partial^{\alpha}j-\partial^{\alpha}\mathbb{P}(u\times B)

the remainder integrals are equal to

\begin{gathered}\int\partial^{\alpha}(j\times B)\cdot\partial^{\alpha}u\,\mathrm{d}x-\int\partial^{\alpha}j\cdot\partial^{\alpha}(cE)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
=-\int|\partial^{\alpha}j|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int\partial^{\alpha}j\cdot\partial^{\alpha}(u\times B)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int\partial^{\alpha}(j\times B)\cdot\partial^{\alpha}u\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{gathered}

(A.3)

The second and third terms of (
A.3
) are bounded simply by the sum of

\begin{gathered}\left|\int\partial^{\alpha}j\cdot\partial^{\alpha}(u\times B)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\\
\leq C\|\nabla^{m}j\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|\nabla^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}\|B\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{m}B\|_{L^{2}}\right)\\
\leq\frac{1}{8\cdot 3^{m}}\|\nabla^{m}j\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|B\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\nabla^{m}B\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{gathered}

(A.4)

and

\begin{gathered}\left|\int\partial^{\alpha}(j\times B)\cdot\partial^{\alpha}u\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\\
\leq C\|\nabla^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|\nabla^{m}j\|_{L^{2}}\|B\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|j\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{m}B\|_{L^{2}}\right)\\
\leq\frac{1}{8\cdot 3^{m}}\|\nabla^{m}j\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|B\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+C\|j\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{m}B\|_{L^{2}}.\end{gathered}

(A.5)

Combining (
A.1
)-(
A.5
) together with
L^{2}
-energy estimate, it follows that

\begin{gathered}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(\|u\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|E\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|B\|_{H^{m}}^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\|j\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\\
\leq C(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|B\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|j\|_{L^{\infty}})(\|u\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|B\|_{H^{m}}^{2})\end{gathered}

(A.6)

Applying the following estimate

\|j\|_{L^{\infty}}(\|u\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|B\|_{H^{m}}^{2})\leq\frac{1}{8}\|j\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+C(\|u\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|B\|_{H^{m}}^{2})^{2},

we obtain (
2.6
). Moreover, we combine the estimate (
A.6
) with the Grönwall inequality to obtain the blow-up criterion. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1

Let

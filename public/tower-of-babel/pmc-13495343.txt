Epidemiological characterization of biliary tract cancer in a real-world cohort of patients from the Spanish TTD-RETUD gastrointestinal registry
Authors: T Macarulla Mercadé, F Castet, A Castillo Trujillo, R Vera García, R Vidal-Tocino, A J Muñoz Martín, P García Alfonso, A Fernández Montes, M Lobo de Mena, RM Rodríguez Alonso, E Aranda, M Granja Ortega, J Adeva, A Modrego Sánchez, R Álvarez Gallego, J Martínez Galán, T García García, I Ruiz de Mena, S Aguilar Izquierdo, A La Casta Muñoa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBiliary tract cancer (BTC) is a rare disease with limited therapies and bad prognosis.Patients and methodsA real-world cohort of patients with BTC from the Spanish RETUD Registry, diagnosed from 2017 to 2025, was analyzed for demographic/clinical characteristics, tumor molecular profile, therapeutic procedures, prognostic factors, and outcomes. Populations were defined as per the first therapy received: surgery/neoadjuvant therapy for the resectable disease (RD) population and first-line therapy for the advanced disease population.ResultsA total of 1756 patients were included in the study, 695 were RD, of whom 55.9% subsequently experienced tumor recurrence, and 1061 were metastatic at diagnosis. Median age was 68.6 years, and 55.7% were male. The most frequent tumor location was intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (53%). Biomarker information was provided for 591 patients, and 42.6% had targetable alterations. Median follow-up time was 14.7 months. In the RD population, surgery was carried out in 95.7% of patients; 5% and 68.9% received neoadjuvant and adjuvant therapies, respectively. Median overall survival (OS) was 33.0 months [95% confidence interval (CI) 30.2-38.9], and median time to relapse was 19.4 months (95% CI 17.3-22.2). In the advanced disease population, immunotherapy was administered to 96.4% and 42.6% of patients in the first- and second-line settings, respectively, whereas targeted therapies were administered to 10.7% and 6.3% of patients, respectively. Median OS was 10.4 months (95% CI 9.7-11.2). Median progression-free survival in patients treated with approved targeted therapies in Spain was 7.1 (95% CI 4.4-NA) versus 4.0 months (95% CI 3.1-5.0), hazard ratio 0.54 (95% CI 0.30-0.99), P = 0.047.ConclusionsThis study provides valuable data on the clinical characteristics, therapeutic procedures, and evolution of patients with BTC treated in clinical practice in Spain.Key words: biliary tract cancer, cholangiocarcinoma, RETUD Registry, real-world data, TTD

Introduction

Biliary tract cancer (BTC) includes cholangiocarcinoma (CCA), gallbladder, and ampulla of Vater carcinoma, representing about 3% of all gastrointestinal cancers.1 Although its global incidence is relatively low, CCA is considered endemic in certain Asian regions.2 Over the past decades, both incidence and annual mortality rates have increased across all subpopulations, particularly for intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (iCCA).2 Early-onset BTC (under 50 years) is increasing, likely due to genetic predisposition.3 Overall, 5-year overall survival (OS) rates range from 7% to 20%.4 In Spain, mortality rose from 3.61% to 3.96% between 2006 and 2015.4

Because BTC is heterogeneous and has a poor prognosis, real-world (RW) evidence can offer broader insight into patient characteristics, treatment patterns, outcomes, and prognostic factors than clinical trials alone.

Only 20%-30% of patients are diagnosed with resectable disease (RD), largely due to the lack of specific symptoms.5 Identifying prognostic factors like sex, early-onset disease, and cancer-associated thromboembolic events (TEEs) may aid in earlier BTC detection.3,6,7 Along with the lack of curative systemic therapies, this remains a major unmet need in BTC management. Surgical resection of primary tumor and oligometastatic disease remains the only treatment with curative intent; however, prognosis is poor even after complete resection, and the risk of recurrence remains high.5 In patients with RD, adjuvant therapies may improve clinical outcomes and reduce the risk of recurrence.8

For patients with advanced disease (locally advanced or metastatic; AD), the European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO) recommends first-line systemic therapy with cisplatin plus gemcitabine (CisGem) combined with immunotherapy. These combinations have demonstrated a survival benefit in phase III trials, establishing durvalumab and pembrolizumab—two immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs)—in combination with chemotherapy as the standard of care (SoC) for first-line treatment.8, 9, 10, 11

In second-line and subsequent treatments, therapy should match the molecular profile following the ESMO scale for clinical actionability of molecular targets (ESCAT), which classifies biomarkers and molecular alterations for precision therapies (ESCAT-I, routine use; ESCAT-II, investigational targets; ESCAT-III, clinical benefit demonstrated in other tumor types). These guidelines are supported by CCA investigators.12

Next-generation sequencing (NGS) enables comprehensive genomic profiling, facilitates the identification of actionable targets, and supports personalized treatment strategies. Upon progression after first-line therapy, two fibroblast growth factor receptor 2 (FGFR2) fusion or rearrangement inhibitors—pemigatinib13 and futibatinib14—have demonstrated efficacy in phase II trials and have received accelerated approval in both the United States and Europe. Other targeted therapies include ivosidenib for isocitrate dehydrogenase 1 (IDH1)-mutant refractory BTC15 and zanidatamab,16 a human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) inhibitor. Emerging tumor-agnostic strategies include dabrafenib plus trametinib for BRAFV600E-mutated BTC.17

This RW study describes the epidemiology, clinical characteristics, and outcomes of 1756 patients with BTC diagnosed since 2017. We aim to highlight current SoC, assess prognostic factors, and describe the uptake of novel therapies.

Patients and methods

Study design

This registry-based, noninterventional, ambispective, multicenter study included adult patients diagnosed with BTC from January 2017 to May 2025, treated with surgery and/or locoregional and/or systemic therapies. Data were obtained from RETUD (NCT067112111),18 a Spanish registry coordinated by the Cooperative Group for the Treatment of Digestive Tumors (TTD Group).

The study was approved by the Asturias Central University Hospital Ethics Committee and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. All included patients provided their written informed consent. The consent was waived for patients who were deceased or lost to follow-up.

Patients aged 18 years or older, diagnosed with BTC on or after 1 January 2017, and with complete electronic case report forms were included. Those not meeting these criteria were excluded.

Data were accrued from 40 Spanish centers across 15 autonomous communities. Information was extracted manually and consecutively from medical records and collected both retrospectively and prospectively. Available data on patient and tumor characteristics, disease status, molecular profiles, risk factors, treatment modalities, and clinical outcomes were obtained from investigators at their respective centers.

A predefined validation plan was applied to ensure data consistency and completeness. Data export was carried out in May 2025 and subsequently underwent quality checks by staff of the TTD Group.

Disease statuses were defined by the first therapeutic approach: RD population (patients treated with surgery/neoadjuvant therapy) and AD population (former patients who experienced tumor recurrence and those initially diagnosed with advanced disease treated with first-line systemic therapy). Survival analysis in the AD population distinguished between patients with disease recurrence and those with an initial metastatic diagnosis.

Additional exploratory analyses were carried out to evaluate the following potential prognostic factors: TEEs, sex, and early-onset disease.

Molecular determination

Tumor molecular profile information was obtained from the medical chart. The molecular profile followed the ESCAT scores12 and was determined using NGS, immunohistochemistry (IHC), PCR, FISH, pyrosequencing, and high-resolution melting.

The following targetable alterations were analyzed: FGFR2 fusions, rearrangements and mutations, IDH1/IDH2 mutations, BRAFV600E mutations, HER2 overexpression, amplifications and mutations, BRCA1/BRCA2 mutations, Kirsten rat sarcoma virus (KRAS) G12C mutations, neurotrophic tyrosine receptor kinase (NTRK) fusions, rearranged during transfection fusions, PALB2 mutations, and microsatellite instability high (MSI-H).

Statistical analysis

The nature of this analysis was descriptive. Main analysis included baseline and clinical characteristics, molecular profile, biomarker determination and coexpression, therapeutic procedures (surgery, locoregional treatments, and systemic therapy), patient status (alive, dead, or lost to follow-up), follow-up time from BTC diagnosis, and efficacy outcomes. Continuous variables were summarized using either the mean or median, the standard deviation, and the range (minimum and maximum). Categorical variables were shown as absolute counts and percentages. OS, real-world progression-free survival (RW-PFS), and time to relapse (TTR) were calculated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and results were reported as median survival time and 95% confidence interval (CI). Survival differences between groups were assessed using the log-rank test. Hazard ratios (HRs) (95% CI) were estimated using Cox proportional hazards models and odds ratios (ORs) (95% CI) using logistic regression. For the targeted therapy analysis, patients with actionable alterations receiving second-line treatment were evaluated according to exposure to targeted therapies approved and available in routine clinical practice in Spain. To reduce immortal time bias due to the frequent use of targeted therapies in the second line, survival estimates were indexed to second-line initiation and patients receiving targeted therapies in the first line were excluded. Multivariate models included predefined clinically relevant covariates [age, sex, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status, and primary tumor location]. To preserve model stability given sample size constraints, tumor location was modeled as intrahepatic versus nonintrahepatic disease.

P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. No missing data imputation was carried out.

All analyses were carried out with SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC).

Results

Overall population

Demographic and clinical characteristics

At the time of analysis, 2330 patients with BTC were registered in the Spanish RETUD Registry, of whom 1756 met the inclusion criteria. Patients not meeting inclusion criteria or not receiving treatment were excluded. Of the 1756 patients, 695 were classified as RD, of whom 389 (55.9%) developed recurrence, whereas 1061 presented with metastatic disease at diagnosis (Figure 1). Baseline characteristics for RD and AD populations are reported in Table 1. In the overall population, the median age was 68.6 years (range 23.0-95.9), and 9.5% were classified as early-onset. Most patients were male (55.7%), and iCCA was the most frequent tumor location (53%), whereas gallbladder carcinoma was more prevalent in women ≥50 years (Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmorw.2026.100756).

Molecular profile

Biomarker information was provided in 591 (33.7%) patients and was more frequently conducted in early-onset patients (52.2% versus 32.4%, P < 0.01). Main determination techniques were NGS (73.3%), IHC (22.6%), and PCR (9.4%). Overall, 469 (79.4%) patients had at least one biomarker alteration and 252 (42.6%) had targetable alterations. Among these, 172 (29.1%) presented ESCAT-I, 28 (4.7%) ESCAT-II, and 27 (4.6%) ESCAT-III alterations (Table 2). There were also 55 (9.3%) patients with potential targetable alterations that are currently under investigation. Targetable alterations were more frequent in patients <50 years (48.33% versus 35.40%, P = 0.049). Predominant ESCAT alterations (>5%) were IDH1 mutations (16.9%), FGFR2 fusions/rearrangements (8.8%), and HER2 amplification/overexpression (5.8%). RD population

Demographic and clinical characteristics

Median age was 68.8 years (range 23.0-95.9) with 5.9% early-onset patients (Table 1). Most of the patients were male (60.3%). The primary tumor location was iCCA (36.4%). Tumor recurrence occurred in 389 patients (55.9%).

Baseline characteristics by age, sex, and OS duration are provided in Supplementary Tables S3-S5, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmorw.2026.100756.

Therapeutic procedures

Surgery was carried out in 95.7% of patients, most commonly hepatectomy (49.3%), duodenal-pancreatectomy (32.9%), lymphadenectomy (26.6%), and cholecystectomy (24.1%). Radical locoregional treatment was administered to 86 (12.4%) patients (radiotherapy in 54.8%). Adjuvant and neoadjuvant therapies were administered to 68.9% (capecitabine 75.5%) and 5% (CisGem 70.4%), respectively.

OS/TTR

With a median follow-up time of 23.9 months (range 0.1, 93.7), 45.9% of patients were alive at the time of analysis. Median OS was 33.0 months (95% CI 30.2-38.9) and median TTR was 19.4 months (95% CI 17.3-22.2) (Figure 2). No significant OS differences were observed in median OS between early-onset and patients ≥50 years [33.8 versus 32.8 months, HR 1.09 (95% CI 0.7-1.7)] or sex (female/male) [36.7 versus 32.2 months, HR 1.17 (95% CI 0.95-1.45)].

AD population

Demographic and clinical characteristics

Median age was 68.4 (range 25.0-95.9) years (7% early-onset patients) (Table 1) and 54.8% of patients were male. Primary tumor location was iCCA (56.8%). A total of 1061 (73.2%) patients were de novo metastatic and 389 (26.8%) had RD and relapsed. The number of metastatic lesions was one in 52.5% and two in 30.3% of patients. The main distant metastatic sites were liver (65.2%), lymph nodes (35.2%), peritoneum (29.9%), and lung (21.4%).

Baseline characteristics by age, sex, and OS duration are provided in Supplementary Tables S6-S8, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmorw.2026.100756.

Therapeutic procedures

First-line therapy was administered to 96.4% of patients (CisGem 67.3%) and the second line to 42.6% (five FOLFOX; 33.2%) (Supplementary Table S9, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmorw.2026.100756). Salvage surgery was carried out in 3.4% of patients, and 7.6% received palliative locoregional treatment.

Immunotherapy was administered to 188 patients (10.7%) with durvalumab (60.1%), pembrolizumab (23.4%), and atezolizumab (6.4%) as the more frequent choices. Targeted therapies were administered to 111 patients (6.3%), the most frequent being ivosidenib (10.8%), futibatinib (10.8%), and pemigatinib (10.8%) (Figure 3). A total of 136 patients (7.7%) participated in at least one clinical trial.

OS

With a median follow-up of 13.2 months, 15.1% of patients were alive at the cut-off. Median OS for all patients with AD was 10.4 (95% CI 9.7-11.2) months (Figure 4). Median OS for patients with RD with recurrence was 12.8 (95% CI 11.2-14.5) versus 10.9 months in de novo patients with AD (95% CI 10.2-11.7), HR 1.3 (95% CI 1.1-1.3), P < 0.001 (Supplementary Figure S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmorw.2026.100756).

RW-PFS

Median RW-PFS was 5.3 months (95% CI 4.9-5.6) in the first-line treatment and 3.1 months (95% CI 2.9-3.3) in the second-line treatment. No differences were observed in first-line RW-PFS by early-onset [5.6 (95% CI 4.0-8.0) versus 5.3 months (95% CI 4.9-5.6), HR 1.1 (95% CI 0.9-1.4), P = 0.247]; nor sex [5.6 (95% CI 5.1-6.2) versus 5.0 months (95% CI 4.5-5.5), HR 1.1 (95% CI 1.0-1.2), P = 0.093].

Targeted therapies

No significant differences in OS were observed between patients receiving targeted therapies in the second line and those with actionable alterations who did not receive targeted therapies [12.5 (95% CI 5.9-NA) versus 10.5 months (95% CI 8.0-15.0), HR 0.7 (95% CI 0.4-1.4), P = 0.331].

In contrast, targeted therapies improved RW-PFS [7.1 (95% CI 3.1-5.0) versus 4.0 months (95% CI 4.4-NA), HR 0.54 (95% CI 0.30-0.99), P = 0.047] (Figure 4) and were associated with higher objective response rates [25% versus 7.4%, P = 0.044, OR 4.2 (95% CI 1.1-17.0), P = 0.039].

Baseline characteristics (age, sex, ECOG, and primary tumor location) were balanced between groups (Supplementary Table S10, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmorw.2026.100756). In multivariable analyses, no independent association between targeted therapy exposure and outcomes was identified (Supplementary Figure S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmorw.2026.100756).

Exploratory analyses for TEE, early-onset disease, and sex

A total of 168 (9.6%) patients had at least one relevant TEE, of which 127 (75.6%) were venous TEEs. No differences were observed in the frequency of TEEs by sex or early-onset, whereas a lower rate of TEEs was observed in patients alive after 24 months from diagnosis (11.5% versus 16.3%, P = 0.038).

Female patients were diagnosed at a later age than males (69.8 versus 68.0 years, P = 0.016), presented a higher proportion of iCCA (54.4% versus 51.9%, P < 0.001), and had metastasis at diagnosis (50% versus 42.6%, P = 0.020), with no differences in OS.

A total of 115 (6.6%) patients had early-onset disease. Their characteristics were broadly similar to those of older patients, except for the higher frequency of females (54.8% versus 43.6%, P = 0.020), iCCA (67% versus 52%, P = 0.003), and ECOG <2 at metastatic diagnosis (91.4% versus 80.8%, P = 0.025). In AD population, early-onset patients showed longer OS [17.6 (95% CI 13.8-24.5) versus 13.8 months (95% CI 13.0-14.7), HR 1.4 (95% CI 1.1-1.7), P = 0.010] (Supplementary Figure S3, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmorw.2026.100756).

Discussion

This RW study included 1756 patients with BTC from the Spanish RETUD Registry, diagnosed between 2017 and 2025. Demographic and clinical characteristics, treatments, outcomes, and prognostic factors are described for patients treated in the clinical practice in Spain. The closest precedent is the European Network for the Study of Cholangiocarcinoma (ENSCCA) registry analysis of 2234 patients with BTC diagnosed between 2010 and 2019 in 11 different European countries, including Spain,19 reporting surgical resection in 50.3% of patients, and a median OS of 33.4 months. In our registry, 695 (39.6%) patients presented RD at diagnosis, with a median OS of 33 months, similar to ENSCCA data. Overall, 1450 patients presented with AD either at diagnosis or upon recurrence, which was experienced by 55.9% of the patients with RD, highlighting the importance of identifying prognostic factors for early diagnosis in BTC.

Following ESMO recommendations, we identified 591 (33.7%) patients with biomarker information available, and 252 of them (42.6%) presented targetable alterations. Prevalence of actionable mutations was consistent with published data. IDH1 mutations were observed in 16.9% of patients, aligned with reported rates (10%-20%).20 FGFR2 fusions and rearrangements were detected in 8.8% of cases, which falls within the range described in the literature (5.5%-28%).20,21 The prevalence of BRAFV600E mutation (1.8%) is below the prior research (5%-7%),21,22 whereas the prevalence of MSI-H (5.7%) is above the data previously reported (1%-4%).23 HER2 amplification/overexpression prevalence (5.8%) was similar to that reported in previous studies (5%-20%).24, 25, 26 NGS recommendations were first published in 202027 and updated in 2024.28 Data presented here reflect the NGS transition and uptake periods for its incorporation into routine BTC management, which may explain the relatively low testing rate. TTD Group is monitoring NGS usage and new drug uptake in patients with BTC across Spanish centers to identify barriers to implementation in SoC practice, including decentralization of the Spanish Healthcare System and different regional reimbursement policies.

Prognostic factors

We analyzed the impact of potential prognostic factors in BTC, including sex, TEE, and early-onset. No important differences in OS were observed between female and male patients, which mirrors previous results in other populations.29,30

Recent evidence suggests TEE may be a negative prognostic factor in CCA.7 In our cohort, we observed a substantially lower rate of TEE in patients alive over 2 years after diagnosis.

It has been recently reported that BTC incidence is rising in the BTC early-onset population,3 suggesting genetic predisposition and characterized by more frequent FGFR2 fusions and improved outcomes, especially with targeted therapies. In the RETUD-BTC cohort, 6.5% of patients fell under this category. Early-onset was much more frequent among women, in patients with iCCA, and in patients with better performance status (ECOG <2). Median OS in early-onset patients was much longer, consistent with published data.3 Considering these preliminary results, future analysis on the role of these potential prognostic factors in BTC is planned by TTD Group.

RETUD-BTC RD population

Median age was 68.8 years (5.9% early-onset), and 60.3% were male patients. Tumor location was primarily intrahepatic (36.4%). Median age, sex distribution, and tumor location were aligned with previously reported European data.19 Non-European studies included more women (Chinese patients: 62.6% >60 years, 43.4% male, 49.3% intrahepatic BTC;31 American patients: 60% >60 years, 48.4% male, 15.1% intrahepatic BTC).32

Surgery is still the main treatment available with curative intent.8 In the current study, 95.7% of patients had at least one surgery carried out (hepatectomy 49.3%). Adjuvant capecitabine, recommended as adjuvant therapy,8 was administered to 68.9% of RETUD patients with BTC RD (capecitabine 75.5%). Neoadjuvant chemotherapy, a topic that is currently being debated as it may facilitate surgical resection, was administered to 5% of patients (CisGem 70.4%). Radical locoregional treatments were received by 9.2% of patients (radiotherapy 53.1%).

Median OS was 33.0 months, and median TTR was 19.4 months. One-year, 3-year, and 5-year OS rates were 87.1%, 48.1%, and 29.4%, respectively. Median OS in our population is consistent with the OS observed in other registry-based and observational studies (27.0-33.8 months).30, 31, 32 In a phase III study in 223 patients with cholangiocarcinoma or muscle-invasive gallbladder cancer, adjuvant capecitabine provided a median OS of 49.6 (95% CI 35.1-59.1) versus 36.1 months (95% CI 29.7-44.2) in the observation group.33 However, survival estimates from clinical trials often do not generalize to RW due to stringent inclusion criteria.

RETUD-BTC AD population

Demographic characteristics were consistent with those reported in previous research.34, 35, 36 Median age was 68.4 years, 54.8% were male, and tumor location was primarily intrahepatic (56.8%) (Korean patients: 71.0 years, 56.1% male, 29.3% iCCA;35 American patients: 76.1 years, 41.5% male, 28.8% iCCA).34

Median RW-PFS was 5.3 months in the first-line, 3.1 months in the second-line, and 2.8 months in third-line treatment. Similar results were observed in other studies with median progression-free survival for first-line treatment ranging from 5.5 to 9 months,36,37 with 2.8 months for second-line treatment.38 Median OS was 10.4 months, and 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS rates were 44.2%, 7.9%, and 2.3%, respectively. Similar median OS and OS rates were reported in other studies.34,39

Chemotherapy, mainly CisGem, is still the primary option for patients with BTC AD. As per SoC,8 96.4% of patients in our study received first-line treatment (CisGem 67.3%) and 42.6% received second-line treatment (FOLFOX 33.2%). Chemotherapy usage rate is higher in Spanish settings compared with the American settings, as per a Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results report on patients with BTC from January 2010 to December 2015, in which only 31.7% received first-line treatment (54.7% CisGem and 23.9% gemcitabine monotherapy) and 13.8% received a second-line treatment (39.4% fluoropyrimidine-based chemotherapy and 12.3% gemcitabine monotherapy).34 However, it should be noted that the American cohort predates our cohort and in the United States, low chemotherapy use reflects patients’ decisions to forgo surgery or systemic treatment due to advanced age, comorbidities, poor prognosis, preference for palliation, limited post–first-line options, or socioeconomic factors.34

Immunotherapy was administered to 188 patients (10.7%). Recent studies8,10,11 have shown that adding ICIs to standard chemotherapy regimens provides a modest but meaningful survival benefit compared with chemotherapy alone,9 leading to gradual authorizations and availability of durvalumab (2023) and pembrolizumab (2024) in Spain.

Given the poor survival rates in BTC AD, the ESMO guidelines8 recommend tailoring the treatment according to the molecular profile from the second-line treatment. Most clinically actionable molecular alterations are compiled in ESCAT-1.12 In our cohort, 252 (42.6%) of 591 tested patients had targetable biomarker alterations, and 111 (44%) of these patients received targeted therapies. Although no significant differences in OS were observed between patients receiving targeted therapies and those not receiving them, significant improvements in RW-PFS were seen with targeted therapies (7.1 versus 4.0 months, HR 0.54, 95% CI 0.30-0.99, P = 0.046). Targeted therapies were also associated with higher objective response rates (OR 4.2, 95% CI 1.1-17.0, P = 0.039). Baseline characteristics were balanced, although multivariable analyses did not identify an independent association between targeted therapy exposure and outcomes, and should be interpreted cautiously due to the limited effective sample size for adjustment. These findings are consistent with previous evidence supporting the clinical activity of targeted therapies in BTC patients.35,39

Targeted therapy administration patterns in our cohort align with the timing of their availability in Spain. In recent years, several targeted therapies have been approved in Europe: larotrectinib (2019) and entrectinib (2020) for NTRK fusions, pemigatinib (2021) for FGFR2 fusions or rearrangements,13 ivosidenib (2023) for IDH1 mutations,15 futibatinib (2025) for FGFR2 fusions and rearrangements,14 and zanidatamab for HER2 overexpression/amplification.16 Additionally, other targeted drugs approved for different indications can be administered under special request, such as dabrafenib plus trametinib in BRAFV600E cancers.17 The current BTC therapeutic pipeline includes drugs for biomarkers such as CLDN18.2, HER2, DKK1, MDM2, MTAP, KRAS G12C, and several antibody–drug conjugates.

Due to its observational nature, this study has some limitations, such as the inclusion of patients from clinical practice and clinical trials, open inclusion criteria, and variability in the clinicians’ treatment choices. The chronological nature of this cohort and the advances in therapeutic approach may reflect a wider gap between the most recent clinical practice and actual SoC than the real scenario. However, this large RW cohort of patients collected across multiple centers provides valuable insights into the characteristics, management, and outcomes of patients with both resectable and advanced BTC in Spain. In addition, it emphasizes the role of biomarkers and targeted therapies while underscoring the need for more effective treatment strategies to improve patient outcomes.

Conclusion

This study provides valuable real-world evidence on the clinical characteristics, therapeutic procedures, prognostic factors, and evolution of large cohorts of patients with resectable and advanced BTC treated under clinical practice conditions in Spain. Treatment patterns observed, especially those with immunotherapy and targeted therapy, reflect a progressive uptake of these innovative treatments, such as SoC therapies.

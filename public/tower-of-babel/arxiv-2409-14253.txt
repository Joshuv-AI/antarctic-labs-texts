MacMahonesque partition functions detect sets related to primes
Kevin Gomez

Abstract
. Recent work by Craig, van Ittersum, and Ono constructs explicit expressions in the partition functions of MacMahon that detect the prime numbers. Furthermore, they define generalizations, the MacMahonesque functions, and prove there are infinitely many such expressions in these functions. Here, we show how to modify and adapt their construction to detect cubes of primes as well as primes in arithmetic progressions.

1.
Introduction and Statement of Results

Recent work has investigated equations in partition functions which detect the set of primes. To be precise, in
[
2
]
, Craig, van Ittersum, and Ono construct prime-detecting expressions using MacMahon’s
[
5
]

q
-series

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{a}(q)=\sum_{n\geq 1}M_{a}(n)\,q^{n}:=\sum_{0<s_{1}<s_{2}<\cdots<s_{a}}\frac{q^{s_{1}+s_{2}+\cdots+s_{a}}}{(1-q^{s_{1}})^{2}(1-q^{s_{2}})^{2}\cdots(1-q^{s_{a}})^{2}}.

For
a\geq 1
,
M_{a}(n)
sums the products of the multiplicities of partitions of
n
with
a
different part sizes. That is, we have

(1.2)

M_{a}(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<s_{1}<s_{2}<\dots<s_{a}\\
n=m_{1}s_{1}+m_{2}s_{2}+\dots+m_{a}s_{a}\end{subarray}}m_{1}m_{2}\cdots m_{a}.

For positive integers
n
, they prove in Theorem 1 of
[
2
]
that

\displaystyle(n^{2}-3n+2)M_{1}(n)-8M_{2}(n)\geq 0,

and for
n\geq 2
, they prove that this expression vanishes if and only if
n
is prime.

They also find a number of other examples, and conjecture that there are only finitely many such intrinsically independent expressions in MacMahon’s functions that detect the primes. However, they obtain a natural generalization which yields infinitely many such prime detectors. Namely, for a positive integer
a
and a vector
\vec{a}=(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{a})\in\mathbb{N}^{a}
, they define the
MacMahonesque partition function

\displaystyle M_{\vec{a}}(n):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<s_{1}<s_{2}<\dots<s_{a}\\
n=m_{1}s_{1}+m_{2}s_{2}+\dots+m_{a}s_{a}\end{subarray}}m_{1}^{v_{1}}m_{2}^{v_{2}}\cdots m_{a}^{v_{a}},

which sums monomials of degree
\absolutevalue{\vec{a}}:=v_{1}+v_{2}+\dots+v_{a}
in the part multiplicities of partitions of
n
. Note that
M_{a}(n)=M_{\vec{a}}(n)
for
\vec{a}=(1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{N}^{a}
. They find, for example, that for positive integers
n
, we have

\begin{split}\Psi_{1}(n):=63M_{(2,2)}(n)-12&M_{(3,0)}(n)-39M_{(3,1)}(n)-12M_{(1,3)}(n)\\
&+80M_{(1,1,1)}(n)-12M_{(2,0,1)}(n)+12M_{(2,1,0)}(n)+12M_{(3,0,0)}(n)\geq 0,\end{split}

and for
n\geq 2
that
\Psi_{1}(n)
likewise vanishes if and only if
n
is prime.

These works follow a general philosophy of Schneider, where classical number theoretic topics are informed by partition theory (for example, see
[
3
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
). In this vein, it is natural to ask if this theorem generalizes to detect other sets. To be precise, which subsets of primes or sets related to primes are exactly detectable from these functions without any further generalization?

To answer this question, we specify two related notion of detecting a set by a
q
-series. Given a set
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, we say that a
q
-series
\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}q^{n}

detects

S
if, for
n\geq 2
,
a_{n}=0
if and only if
n\in S
. A
q
-series furthermore
strongly detects

S
if
a_{n}\geq 0
for
n\geq 1
.

We find that cubes of primes are detectable using the MacMahonesque functions in infinitely many ways. Define the generating series

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\vec{a}}(n):=\sum_{n\geq 1}M_{\vec{a}}(n)q^{n}.

We then have the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exist infinitely many linearly independent expressions

\displaystyle\sum_{\absolutevalue{\vec{a}}\leq d}c_{\vec{a}}\mathcal{U}_{\vec{a}}(q)

which detect the set
S
of primes cubed, where
c_{\vec{a}}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
d\geq 21
.

Remark
.

It is natural to ask about other prime powers. Firstly, we consider only odd powers, as our method makes use of quasimodular forms of level 1. The method of proof of Theorem
1.1
then breaks down for powers 5 or higher, because the inequalities required do not continue to hold.

Example
.

For integers
n\geq 2
, we have that

\displaystyle g^{\ast}(n)

\displaystyle=\overbrace{1118\cdots 4000}^{63\text{ digits}}M_{(1)}(n)-\overbrace{1667\cdots 1760}^{63\text{ digits}}M_{(3)}(n)+\cdots+\overbrace{2160\cdots 7200}^{55\text{ digits}}M_{(15,1,1)}(n)

vanishes if and only if
n=p^{3}
for some prime
p
. A complete expression is given in the Appendix.

We also consider twists of
\mathcal{U}_{\vec{a}}(q)
by a root of unity, which enables us to strongly detect primes in arithmetic progressions. In particular, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
t
be a fixed positive integer,
0\leq r<t
be coprime to
t
, and
\zeta_{t}
be a primitive
t^{\text{th}}
root of unity. Then there exist infinitely many linearly independent expressions

\displaystyle\sum_{\absolutevalue{\vec{a}}\leq d}\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}c_{\vec{a}}(s)\mathcal{U}_{\vec{a}}(\zeta_{t}^{s}q)

which strongly detect the set
S
of primes congruent to
r
modulo
t
, where
c_{\vec{a}}(s)\in\mathbb{C}
and
d\geq 7
.

Remark
.

Craig, in
[
1
]
, constructs series as in Theorem
1.2
which detect primes in arithmetic progression. Specifically, he obtains quasimodular forms with level which detect primes in certain pairs of arithmetic progressions, as a consequence of his primary theorems concerning
q
-multiple zeta values at level
N
.

Example
.

For integers
n\geq 2
, we have that

\displaystyle f^{\ast}(n):=4(199+21\omega^{n-1}+21\omega^{2n-2})M_{(1)}(n)+\cdots+120960M_{(3,1,1)}(n)-161280M_{(1,1,1,1)}(n)

vanishes if and only if
n
is a prime congruent to
1
modulo
3
, where
\omega:=e^{2\pi i/3}
is a primitive third root of unity. A complete expression is given in the Appendix.

2.
Quasimodular Forms

We require the basic elements of the theory of quasimodular forms; see
[
11
, Section 5.3]
for an exposition. For even integers
k\geq 1
, we consider the
k^{\text{th}}

Bernoulli numbers

B_{k}
and the
Eisenstein series

(2.1)

\displaystyle G_{k}(\tau):=-\frac{B_{k}}{2k}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k-1}(n)q^{n},

with
q:=e^{2\pi i\tau},\tau\in\mathbb{H}
and
\sigma_{k-1}(n):=\sum_{d\mid n}d^{k-1}
. It is a well-known fact (for example, see
[
6
]
) that
G_{k}
is quasimodular, and furthermore modular if
k\geq 4
. We denote the algebra of quasimodular forms by
\widetilde{M}
, the subspace of forms with weight
k
by
\widetilde{M}_{k}
, and the space of forms with mixed weight
\leq k
by
\widetilde{M}_{\leq k}
.

We also require the
q
-differential operator

(2.2)

\displaystyle D:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\dfrac{d}{d\tau}=q\dfrac{d}{dq}.

For
k\geq 0
, we observe that
D^{k}:q^{n}\mapsto n^{k}q^{n}
. Ramanujan famously proved derivative identities for
G_{2}
,
G_{4}
, and
G_{6}
(for example, see
[
7
, Section 7]
):

(2.3)

\displaystyle DG_{2}=-2G_{2}^{2}+\dfrac{5}{6}G_{4},\ \ \ DG_{4}=-8G_{2}G_{4}+\dfrac{7}{10}G_{6},\ \ \ DG_{6}=-12G_{2}G_{6}+\dfrac{400}{7}G_{4}^{2}.

Since
\widetilde{M}
is generated by
G_{2}
,
G_{4}
, and
G_{6}
, these identities show that
D
defines a map on the algebra of quasimodular forms which increases weights by
2
; that is, we have that

D~\colon\widetilde{M}_{k}\to\widetilde{M}_{k+2}.

3.
Detecting cubes of primes

Utilizing elementary properties of
D
, we produce quasimodular forms which detect cubes of primes.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
k,\ell
be non-negative odd integers with
\ell>k
. For all
n\geq 2
, the
n^{\text{th}}
Fourier coefficient of

(3.1)

\displaystyle g_{k,\ell}:=(D^{3\ell}+D^{2\ell}+D^{\ell}+1)G_{3k+1}-(D^{3k}+D^{2k}+D^{k}+1)G_{3\ell+1}

vanishes if and only if
n=p^{3}
for some prime
p
.

We require a number of lemmas to establish this result. We define, for
d\mid n
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle a_{k,\ell}(n,d):=(n^{3\ell}+n^{2\ell}+n^{\ell}+1)\times d^{3k}-(n^{3k}+n^{2k}+n^{k}+1)\times d^{3\ell}.

We let
a_{k,\ell}(n)
be the
n
th Fourier coefficient of
g_{k,\ell}
, which is given by

(3.3)

\displaystyle a_{k,\ell}(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}a_{k,\ell}(n,d)=(n^{3\ell}+n^{2\ell}+n^{\ell}+1)\times\sigma_{3k}(n)-(n^{3k}+n^{2k}+n^{k}+1)\times\sigma_{3\ell}(n).

Lemma 3.2
.

For all
n\geq 2
and
d\mid n
, we have that
a_{k,\ell}(n,d)<0
if and only if
d=n
.

Proof.

We first simplify and extract powers of
n
to obtain

\displaystyle a_{k,\ell}(n,n)

\displaystyle=(n^{3\ell}+n^{2\ell}+n^{\ell}+1)\times n^{3k}-(n^{3k}+n^{2k}+n^{k}+1)\times n^{3\ell}

\displaystyle=(n^{2\ell}+n^{\ell}+1)\times n^{3k}-(n^{2k}+n^{k}+1)\times n^{3\ell}

\displaystyle=n^{3(k+\ell)}(n^{-\ell}-n^{-k}+n^{-2\ell}-n^{-2k}+n^{-3\ell}-n^{-3k}).

Since
\ell>k
,
n^{-j\ell}-n^{-jk}<0
for
j>0
. Thus,
a_{k,\ell}(n,n)<0
.

Conversely, if
d\leq n/2
, we may bound
a_{k,\ell}(n,d)
from below and simplify to obtain

\displaystyle a_{k,\ell}(n,d)

\displaystyle\geq d^{3k}\left((n^{3\ell}+n^{2\ell}+n^{\ell}+1)-(n^{3k}+n^{2k}+n^{k}+1)\times(n/2)^{3(\ell-k)}\right)

\displaystyle=d^{3k}n^{3\ell}\left(\frac{n^{\ell}-n^{-3\ell}}{n^{\ell}-1}-\frac{n^{k}-n^{-3k}}{n^{k}-1}\times\frac{1}{2^{3(\ell-k)}}\right).

We have, for all
n\geq 2
and
m\geq 1
,

\displaystyle 1<\frac{n^{m}-n^{-3m}}{n^{m}-1}<2,

whereby

\displaystyle d^{3k}n^{3\ell}\left(\frac{n^{\ell}-n^{-3\ell}}{n^{\ell}-1}-\frac{n^{k}-n^{-3k}}{n^{k}-1}\times\frac{1}{2^{3(\ell-k)}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq d^{3k}n^{3\ell}\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{3(\ell-k)-1}}\right)\geq 1.

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For all
n\geq 2
and
1\leq d<n
, we have

\displaystyle a_{k,\ell}(n,d)<n^{3\ell}d^{3k}\left(1+\frac{1}{n^{\ell}-1}\right).

Furthermore, for all
n\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{a_{k,\ell}(n,n)}>n^{2k+3\ell}\left(1-\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right).

Proof.

For
d<n
, we have the bound

\displaystyle a_{k,\ell}(n,d)

\displaystyle\leq(n^{3\ell}+n^{2\ell}+n^{\ell}+1)\times d^{3k}-(n^{3k}+n^{2k}+n^{k}+1)\times d^{3\ell}

\displaystyle\leq(n^{3\ell}+n^{2\ell}+n^{\ell}+1)\times d^{3k}-(n^{3k}+n^{2k}+n^{k}+1)\times d^{3k},

where the second inequality follows since
\ell>k
. Extracting the common factor of
d^{3k}
, we have

\displaystyle d^{3k}(n^{3\ell}-n^{3k}+n^{2\ell}-n^{2k}+n^{\ell}-n^{k})

\displaystyle\leq d^{3k}(n^{3\ell}+n^{2\ell}+n^{\ell})=d^{3k}n^{3\ell}(1+n^{-\ell}+n^{-2\ell}).

Writing

\displaystyle 1+n^{-\ell}+n^{-2\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-n^{-3\ell}}{1-n^{-\ell}}<\frac{1}{1-n^{-\ell}}=\frac{n^{\ell}}{n^{\ell}-1}=1+\frac{1}{n^{\ell}-1},

we obtain the first part of our result.

For
d=n
, we find through straightforward algebraic manipulation that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{3(k+\ell)}}\absolutevalue{a_{k,\ell}(n,n)}

\displaystyle=(n^{3k}+n^{2k}+n^{k}+1)\times n^{-3k}-(n^{3\ell}+n^{2\ell}+n^{\ell}+1)\times n^{-3\ell}

\displaystyle=(n^{2k}+n^{k}+1)\times n^{-3k}-(n^{2\ell}+n^{\ell}+1)\times n^{-3\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{3k}-1}{n^{k}-1}\times n^{-3k}-\frac{n^{3\ell}-1}{n^{\ell}-1}\times n^{-3\ell},

which we simplify further to

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{3(k+\ell)}}\absolutevalue{a_{k,\ell}(n,n)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-n^{-3k}}{n^{k}-1}-\frac{1-n^{-3\ell}}{n^{\ell}-1}.

Since
\ell\geq k+2
, we may write

\displaystyle\frac{1-n^{-3\ell}}{n^{\ell}-1}<\frac{1}{n^{\ell}-1}\leq\frac{1}{n^{k+2}-1}.

Inserting this bound into (
3.4
), we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{3(k+\ell)}}\absolutevalue{a_{k,\ell}(n,n)}

\displaystyle>\frac{1-n^{-3k}}{n^{k}-1}-\frac{1}{n^{k+2}-1}=\frac{1}{n^{k}-1}-\frac{1}{n^{4k}-n^{3k}}-\frac{1}{n^{k+2}-1}.

Since
n^{4k}-n^{3k}>n^{3k}
for all
n\geq 2
, we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{k}-1}-\frac{1}{n^{4k}-n^{3k}}-\frac{1}{n^{k+2}-1}>\frac{1}{n^{k}-1}-\frac{1}{n^{3k}}-\frac{1}{n^{k+2}-1}

Combining terms in the right-hand side of (
3.6
), we obtain

(3.7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{3k}}+\frac{1}{n^{k+2}-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{3k}+n^{k+2}-1}{n^{3k}(n^{k+2}-1)}=\frac{1+n^{-2k+2}-n^{-3k}}{n^{k+2}-1}.

Meanwhile, we compute

(3.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{k}-1}-\left(\frac{1}{n^{k}}-\frac{1}{n^{k+2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{k+2}-(n^{k}-1)(n^{2}-1)}{n^{k+2}(n^{k}-1)}=\frac{1+n^{-k+2}-n^{-k}}{n^{k+2}-n^{2}}.

We now compare (
3.7
) and (
3.8
), finding that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\frac{1+n^{-2k+2}-n^{-3k}}{n^{k+2}-1}<\frac{1+n^{-2k+2}}{n^{k+2}-1}<\frac{1+n^{-2k+2}}{n^{k+2}-n^{2}}<\frac{1+n^{-k+2}-n^{-k}}{n^{k+2}-n^{2}},

where the rightmost inequality follows from
n^{2}<n^{k+2}-n^{k}
for all
n\geq 2
and
k\geq 1
.

Thus, applying (
3.6
) and (
3.9
) to (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{3(k+\ell)}}\absolutevalue{a_{k,\ell}(n,n)}

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{n^{k}-1}-\frac{1}{n^{3k}}-\frac{1}{n^{k+2}-1}

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{n^{k}-1}-\left(\frac{1}{n^{k}-1}-\left(\frac{1}{n^{k}}-\frac{1}{n^{k+2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{k}}\left(1-\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right),

yielding our result.
∎

We now obtain a condition on
n
which dictates the sign of
a_{k,\ell}(n)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
n>p^{3}
for some
p\mid n
prime, then
a_{k,\ell}(n)>0
.

Proof.

Let
p\mid n
be such that
n>p^{3}
. We evaluate via (
3.2
)

(3.10)

\displaystyle a_{k,\ell}(n,n/p)+a_{k,\ell}(n,n)

\displaystyle=\frac{(n^{4\ell}-1)(p^{3k}+1)}{n^{\ell}-1}\times\frac{n^{3k}}{p^{3k}}-\frac{(n^{4k}-1)(p^{3\ell}+1)}{n^{k}-1}\times\frac{n^{3\ell}}{p^{3\ell}}.

We now bound the ratio of the terms in the right-hand side of (
3.10
). Since
\ell>k
, we have
n^{4\ell}-1\geq n^{4\ell}-n^{4(\ell-k)}
and thus
(n^{4\ell}-1)/(n^{4k}-1)\geq n^{4(\ell-k)}
. Hence, we find through straightforward algebraic manipulation that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\frac{p^{3(\ell-k)}(n^{4\ell}-1)(p^{3k}+1)(n^{k}-1)}{n^{3(\ell-k)}(n^{4k}-1)(p^{3\ell}+1)(n^{\ell}-1)}

\displaystyle\geq(np^{3})^{\ell-k}\times\frac{(p^{3k}+1)(n^{k}-1)}{(p^{3\ell}+1)(n^{\ell}-1)}.

Distributing the factors in
(np^{3})^{\ell-k}
, we obtain

(3.12)

\displaystyle(np^{3})^{\ell-k}\times\frac{(p^{3k}+1)(n^{k}-1)}{(p^{3\ell}+1)(n^{\ell}-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{3\ell}+p^{3(\ell-k)}}{p^{3\ell}+1}\times\frac{n^{k}-1}{n^{k}-n^{k-\ell}}>\frac{p^{3\ell}+p^{3(\ell-k)}}{p^{3\ell}+1}\times\frac{n^{k}-1}{n^{k}}.

Now, let

\displaystyle h_{1}(x):=\frac{p^{3\ell}+p^{3(\ell-k)}}{p^{3\ell}+1}\times\frac{x^{k}-1}{x^{k}},\text{ for }x\geq 1,

so that
h_{1}(n)
is a lower bound for the left hand side of (
3.11
) thanks to (
3.12
). It is clear that
h_{1}(x)
is an increasing function, and thus it suffices to show that
h_{1}(p^{3}+p)>1
to deduce that
h_{1}(n)>1
. We thus calculate and expand

\displaystyle h_{1}(p^{3}+p)

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{3\ell}+p^{3(\ell-k)}}{p^{3\ell}+1}\times\frac{(p^{3}+p)^{k}-1}{(p^{3}+p)^{k}}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{p^{3(\ell-k)}-1}{p^{3\ell}+1}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{(p^{3}+p)^{k}}\right)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{(p^{3}+p)^{k}}+\frac{p^{3(\ell-k)}-1}{p^{3\ell}+1}-\frac{p^{3(\ell-k)}-1}{(p^{3\ell}+1)(p^{3}+p)^{k}}.

We now group the terms above to obtain

(3.13)

\displaystyle h_{1}(p^{3}+p)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{p^{3(\ell-k)}}{(p^{3\ell}+1)(p^{3}+p)^{k}}\left((p^{3}+p)^{k}\left(1-p^{3(k-\ell)}\right)-p^{3k}-1\right)

and wish to show that this value is positive. We first have by straightforward algebraic manipulation that

\displaystyle(p^{3}+p)^{k}\left(1-p^{3(k-\ell)}\right)-p^{3k}-1

\displaystyle=((p^{3}+p)^{k}-p^{3k})\left(1-p^{3(k-\ell)}\right)-p^{3(2k-\ell)}-1.

Thanks to the binomial theorem,

\displaystyle(p^{3}+p)^{k}-p^{3k}>kp^{3(k-1)+1},

whereby

\displaystyle((p^{3}+p)^{k}-p^{3k})\left(1-p^{3(k-\ell)}\right)-p^{3(2k-\ell)}-1

\displaystyle>k\left(1-p^{3(k-\ell)}\right)p^{3(k-1)+1}-p^{3(2k-\ell)}-1.

This equates to

(3.14)

\displaystyle k\left(1-p^{3(k-\ell)}\right)p^{3(k-1)+1}-p^{3(2k-\ell)}-1

\displaystyle=p^{3(2k-\ell)}\left(k\left(p^{3(\ell-k-1)+1}-p^{-2}\right)-1\right)-1.

We then observe that

\displaystyle p^{3(\ell-k-1)+1}-p^{-2}-k^{-1}>p^{3(\ell-2k)}

since
k\geq 1
and
p\geq 2
. This grants

\displaystyle p^{3(2k-\ell)}\left(k\left(p^{3(\ell-k-1)+1}-p^{-2}\right)-1\right)>1,

which combined with (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) completes the proof that
h_{1}(n)>1
. Since
a_{k,\ell}(n)\geq a_{k,\ell}(n,n/p)+a_{k,\ell}(n,n)
by Lemma
3.2
, we obtain our result.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

If
n=p^{3}
for some prime
p
, then
a_{k,\ell}(n)=0
.

Proof.

For
s>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{s}(p^{3})

\displaystyle=p^{3s}+p^{2s}+p^{s}+1,

which substituting into (
3.3
) grants

\displaystyle a_{k,\ell}(p^{3})

\displaystyle=(p^{9\ell}+p^{6\ell}+p^{3\ell}+1)(p^{9k}+p^{6k}+p^{3k}+1)-(p^{9k}+p^{6k}+p^{3k}+1)(p^{9\ell}+p^{6\ell}+p^{3\ell}+1)=0.

∎

Lemma 3.6
.

If
n<p^{3}
for all
p\mid n
, then
a_{k,\ell}(n)<0
.

Proof.

Since
n\leq p^{3}-p
for
p
the smallest prime dividing
n
, we have three cases.

Case 1:

n=p
. We first expand

\displaystyle a_{k,\ell}(p)

\displaystyle=(p^{3\ell}+p^{2\ell}+p^{\ell}+1)(p^{3k}+1)-(p^{3k}+p^{2k}+p^{k}+1)(p^{3\ell}+1)

\displaystyle=p^{3k+2\ell}+p^{3k+\ell}+p^{2\ell}+p^{\ell}-p^{3\ell+2k}-p^{3\ell+k}-p^{2k}-p^{k}.

Extracting the largest powers of
p
, we find that

\displaystyle a_{k,\ell}(p)

\displaystyle=p^{2k+2\ell}(p^{k}+p^{-2k}+p^{-\ell-2k}-p^{\ell}-p^{-2\ell}-p^{-k-2\ell})).

All negative powers of
p
are bounded above by
1
, so we obtain,

\displaystyle a_{k,\ell}(p)

\displaystyle\leq p^{2k+2\ell}(p^{k}-p^{\ell}+p^{-2k}+p^{-\ell-2k})\leq p^{2k+2\ell}(p^{k}-p^{\ell}+2)\leq p^{2k+2\ell}(2^{1}-2^{3}+2)<0.

Case 2:

n=p^{2}
. As in the previous case, we expand out
a_{k,\ell}(p^{2})
and collect the largest powers of
p
. Discarding negative addends and bounding negative powers of
p
by
1
, we obtain

\displaystyle a_{k,\ell}(p^{2})

\displaystyle=(p^{6\ell}+p^{4\ell}+p^{2\ell}+1)(p^{6k}+p^{3k}+1)-(p^{6k}+p^{4k}+p^{2k}+1)(p^{6\ell}+p^{3\ell}+1)

\displaystyle\leq p^{4k+4\ell}(p^{2k}-p^{2\ell}+10)

\displaystyle\leq p^{4k+4\ell}(2^{2}-2^{6}+10)

\displaystyle<0.

Case 3:

n=pp^{\prime}
with
p^{\prime}>p
prime. Let

\displaystyle h_{2}(x):=(1+p^{3k}+(x/p)^{3k})\left(1+\frac{1}{x^{k}}\right)-x^{2k}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{4}}\right).

We first write
a_{k,\ell}(n)
via (
3.3
) as

\displaystyle a_{k,\ell}(n)

\displaystyle=a_{k,\ell}(n,1)+a_{k,\ell}(n,p)+a_{k,\ell}(n,n/p)+a_{k,\ell}(n,n).

We then apply Lemma
3.3
to find that

\displaystyle a_{k,\ell}(n)

\displaystyle<n^{3\ell}\left[(1+p^{3k}+(n/p)^{3k})\left(1+\frac{1}{n^{\ell}-1}\right)-n^{2k}\left(1-\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle<n^{3\ell}\left[(1+p^{3k}+(n/p)^{3k})\left(1+\frac{1}{n^{k}}\right)-n^{2k}\left(1-\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\right].

Since
p<\sqrt{n}
,
p^{4}<n^{2}
, and hence we obtain

\displaystyle a_{k,\ell}(n)

\displaystyle<n^{3\ell}\left[(1+p^{3k}+(n/p)^{3k})\left(1+\frac{1}{n^{k}}\right)-n^{2k}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{4}}\right)\right]=n^{3\ell}h_{2}(n).

We then compute

\displaystyle h_{2}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{kx^{-k-1}}{p^{4}}\left[p^{3k}+1-3p^{-3k}x^{4k}-(2p^{-3k}-2p^{-4})x^{3k}\right]

and wish to analyze its sign. We see that

\displaystyle 3p^{-3k}n^{4k}+(2p^{-3k}-2p^{-4})n^{3k}-(p^{3k}+1)=0

has exactly one positive real root
w
by Descartes’ rule of signs. Substituting
n=p^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle 3p^{-3k}p^{8k}+(2p^{-3k}-2p^{-4})p^{6k-4}-(p^{3k}+1)

\displaystyle=-2p^{6k}+3p^{5k}+2p^{3k}-p^{3k}-1<0

for all
p\geq 2

\displaystyle 3p^{-3k}p^{12k}+(2p^{-3k}-2p^{-4})p^{9k}-(p^{3k}+1)=3p^{9k}-2p^{9k-4}+2p^{6k}-p^{3k}-1>0

for all

decreasing on
[p^{2},w)
and increasing on
(w,p^{3}]

and

(3.15)

\displaystyle h_{2}(p^{2})

\displaystyle=(1+2p^{3k})(1+p^{-2k})-p^{4k}(1-p^{-4})=-p^{4k}+p^{4k-2}+2p^{3k}+2p^{k}+1+p^{-2k}<0

\displaystyle h_{2}(p^{3}-p)

(3.16)

\displaystyle(p^{2}-1)^{2k}(-p^{2k}(1-p^{-4})+(p^{2}-1)^{k})+4p^{3k}<(p^{2}-1)^{2k}(-k(p^{2}-1)^{k-1}+p^{2k-4})+4p^{3k}.

For

or

. Since

\displaystyle(p^{2}-1)^{2}(-1+p^{-2})+4p^{3}=-p^{-2}(p^{2}-1)^{3}+4p^{3}<0

for all

), this resolves this case for all pairs

except

for

.

For these remaining pairs, we have that
p=2

\displaystyle a_{1,\ell}(6)

\displaystyle=252(6^{3\ell}+6^{2\ell}+6^{\ell}+1)-259(6^{3\ell}+3^{3\ell}+2^{3\ell}+1).

We apply straightforward bounds to obtain

\displaystyle a_{1,\ell}(6)

\displaystyle<252(6^{3\ell}+6^{2\ell}+6^{\ell}+1)-259\times 6^{3\ell}<-7\times 6^{3\ell}+252\times 3\times 6^{2\ell},

which we may readily verify is negative for

∎

4.
Detecting primes in arithmetic progression

Throughout, we fix positive integers
t\geq 2
and
0<r<t
coprime to
t
, and let
\zeta_{t}
be a primitive
t^{\text{th}}
root of unity. In a similar fashion as the previous section, we utilize sums of Eisenstein series with restricted support to produce quasimodular forms which strongly detect the primes congruent to
r
modulo
t
.

We define the series

(4.1)

\displaystyle G_{k}^{r,t}(\tau):=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\zeta_{t}^{-rs}G_{k}(\tau+s/t).

It is clear from the definition that
G_{k}^{r,t}
is a quasimodular form of weight
k
and level
t^{2}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

We have that
q^{-r}G_{k}^{r,t}(\tau)
is supported on powers of
q^{t}
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle G_{k}^{r,t}(\tau)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sigma_{k-1}(mt+r)q^{mt+r}.

Proof.

We have by (
2.1
) and (
4.1
)

(4.2)

\displaystyle G_{k}^{r,t}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\left(-\frac{B_{k}\zeta_{t}^{-rs}}{2k}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta_{t}^{(n-r)s}\sigma_{k-1}(n)q^{n}\right).

It is well known that

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\zeta_{t}^{(n-r)s}=\begin{cases}t&t\mid n-r,\\
0&t\nmid n-r.\end{cases}

Since
r\not\equiv 0\pmod{t}
, the constant term of
G_{k}^{r,t}
vanishes. We may then simplify (
4.2
) to obtain

\displaystyle G_{k}^{r,t}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\\
n\equiv r\bmod{t}\end{subarray}}\sigma_{k-1}(n)q^{n}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sigma_{k-1}(mt+r)q^{mt+r}.

∎

We now establish that
G_{k}^{r,t}(\tau)
can be used to construct infinitely many
q
-series which detect primes in arithmetic progression.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
k,\ell
be non-negative odd integers with
\ell>k
. For all
n\geq 2
, the
n^{\text{th}}
Fourier coefficient of

(4.3)

\displaystyle f_{k,\ell}^{r,t}:=(D^{\ell}+1)G_{k+1}-(D^{k}+1)G_{\ell+1}^{r,t}

vanishes if and only if
n\equiv r\pmod{t}
is prime. Furthermore, all of the coefficients of
f_{k,\ell}^{r,t}
are non-negative.

Proof.

We see via (
2.1
) and (
4.1
) that the
n^{\text{th}}
Fourier coefficient of
f_{k,\ell}^{r,t}
is given by

\displaystyle b_{k,\ell}^{r,t}(n)=\begin{cases}\sum_{d\mid n}((n^{\ell}+1)d^{k}-(n^{k}+1)d^{\ell})&n\equiv r\pmod{t},\\
\sum_{d\mid n}(n^{\ell}+1)d^{k}&n\not\equiv r\pmod{t}.\end{cases}

If
n\equiv r\pmod{t}
, then
b_{k,\ell}^{r,t}(n)\geq 0
and vanishes if and only if
n
is prime or
n=1
by Lemma 2.1 of
[
2
]
(see also Lemma 2 of
[
4
]
). It is then clear
b_{k,\ell}^{r,t}(n)>0
for all
n\not\equiv r\pmod{t}
.
∎

.

For any fixed

odd, the elements of the set

As in Lemma
3.7

.

For all odd non-negative integers
k,\ell
with

, there exist constants

\displaystyle f_{k,\ell}^{r,t}

An Atomic Representation for Bicomplex Hardy Classes
William L. Blair

Abstract
We develop representations for bicomplex-valued functions in Hardy classes that generalize the complex holomorphic Hardy spaces. Using these representations, we show these functions have boundary values in the sense of distributions that are representable by an atomic decomposition, and we show continuity of the Hilbert transform on this class of distributional boundary values.



keywords

Hardy spaces; atomic decomposition; bicomplex numbers; boundary value in the sense of distributions; nonhomogeneous Cauchy-Riemann equation

Introduction

In this paper, we exploit relationships between complex-valued Hardy classes of functions defined on the complex unit disk and certain Hardy classes of bicomplex-valued functions that lead to useful representation formulas.

In
[
1
]
, G. Hoepfner and J. Hounie proved that holomorphic Hardy space functions have boundary values in the sense of distributions and those distributional boundary values are representable by an atomic decomposition. While this result is familiar to experts in harmonic analysis for certain classes of distributions on the boundary of the upper-half space, Hoepfner and Hounie provided a proof for the case of holomorphic functions on the disk, for which there was not an extent proof. Note, the conditions for a holomorphic function to have a boundary value in the sense of distributions were previously considered by E. Straube in
[
2
]
, the atomic decomposition result for Hardy spaces of the disk was previously considered for the
p=1
case by P. Koosis in
[
3
]
, and the atomic decomposition of disk Hardy spaces is described by R. Coifman in
[
4
]
. In
[
5
]
, the author showed that functions which solve nonhomogeneous Cauchy-Riemann equations and satisfy the same size condition as the holomorphic Hardy spaces are representable as the sum of a holomorphic Hardy space function and an error term constructed with the area integral from the classic Cauchy-Pompieu formula. Using this pointwise representation, the author showed these generalized Hardy space functions have boundary values in the sense of distributions and these distributional boundary values are representable as the sum of an atomic decomposition and a well-controlled error term. This extends the result from
[
1
]
to classes of nonholomorphic functions. Also in
[
5
]
, the author extends continuity of the Hilbert transform to these classes of distributions by appealing to the associated result for holomorphic functions in
[
1
]
. This provides another class of distributions associated with a small
p
-class of functions where the Hilbert transform is a continuous operator. Both the pointwise and boundary representations are shown to hold for functions in Hardy classes that are solutions to higher-order nonhomogeneous Cauchy-Riemann equations where the function and its derivatives have finite Hardy space norm. The boundary representation is used to show that the Hilbert transform is a continuous operator on the class of distributions associated with these Hardy classes also. Following the previously described success, we return to the question posed in the introduction of
[
5
]
which is: “What classes of functions, other than the holomorphic Hardy space functions, have boundary values in the sense of distributions on the circle that have an atomic decomposition?”

We consider Hardy classes of functions defined on the complex unit disk that take values in the bicomplex numbers. The bicomplex numbers are a higher-dimensional extension of the complex numbers that, unlike quaternions, are commutative with respect to multiplication. The analysis of functions of bicomplex variables and bicomplex-valued functions is an active area of research as it provides a setting for a generalization of complex analysis different than the well-studied Clifford analysis and analysis of functions of several complex variables. For background on bicomplex numbers and bicomplex-valued functions as well as some recent examples of research in analysis that involve the bicomplex numbers, see
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
. The Hardy classes that we consider will be functions that satisfy a bicomplex generalization of the Cauchy-Riemann equation and satisfy a size condition analogous to that of the complex-valued Hardy space functions. This consideration will include both a bicomplex analogue of holomorphic functions as well as nonholomorphic functions. We prove that functions in these bicomplex Hardy classes are intimately connected with functions in the holomorphic Hardy spaces of complex-valued functions as well as the generalized Hardy classes of solutions to nonhomogeneous Cauchy-Riemann equations considered in
[
5
]
by showing every function in the bicomplex Hardy classes is representable as a linear combination of a holomorphic Hardy space function and a generalized Hardy class function. This is a nontrivial application of the generalized Hardy classes considered in
[
5
]
. Using this representation, we find these bicomplex-valued functions inherit the familiar boundary behavior of existence of an
L^{p}
boundary value and convergence to these
L^{p}
boundary values in the
L^{p}
norm of the classic Hardy spaces. Also, we show that these functions have boundary values in the sense of distributions, and by appealing to a combination of the atomic decomposition results in
[
1
]
and
[
5
]
, we show these distributional boundary values can be represented by a sum of atomic decompositions. This atomic decomposition representation directly leads to showing the Hilbert transform is a continuous operator on this class of distributions. As in
[
5
]
, all of these results are shown to extend to functions in Hardy classes of solutions to higher-order generalizations of the bicomplex Cauchy-Riemann equation.

We outline this paper. In Section
2
, we provide background concerning the classic holomorphic Hardy spaces, the generalized Hardy spaces of complex solutions to nonhomogeneous Cauchy-Riemann equations, and bicomplex numbers. Previously unconsidered extensions of certain complex results will be included here as well as generalizations of certain tools to the bicomplex setting. In Sections

5

D
which we take to be the disk of radius one centered at the origin in the complex plane. By

, we denote the complex-valued functions defined on a set

with integrable modulus raised to the
p^{\text{th}}
-power. We represent the space of distributions on the boundary of the unit disk
\partial D
by
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\partial D)
. We represent the space of
k

. Finally, in an effort to disambiguate between other notions of conjugation that will arise below, we denote the complex conjugate of

2.1
Holomorphic Hardy Spaces

We recall definitions and facts about the classic holomorphic Hardy spaces that we generalize.

Definition 2.1
.

We define
Hol(D)
to be the space of functions
f:D\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{*}}=0.

Definition 2.2
.

For
0<p<\infty
, we define
H^{p}(D)
to be the space of functions
f\in Hol(D)
such that

||f||_{H^{p}}:=\sup_{0<r<1}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
13
]
)
.

A function
f\in H^{p}(D)
,
0<p<\infty
, has nontangential boundary values
f_{nt}\in L^{p}(\partial D)
at almost every point of
\partial D
,

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta=\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f_{nt}(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta,

and

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})-f_{nt}(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta=0.

Since the Lebesgue spaces for
0<p<1
are not as useful as their

H^{p}(D)
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let

. We say that

has a boundary value in the sense of distributions, denoted by

\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\partial D)
, the limit

\langle f_{b},\varphi\rangle:=\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(re^{i\theta})\,\varphi(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta

exists.

In
[
1
]
, it is shown that functions in
H^{p}(D)
have distributional boundary values.

Theorem 2.5

(Theorem 3.1
[
1
]
)
.

For
f\in Hol(D)
, the following are equivalent:

1.

For every
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\partial D)
, there exists the limit

\langle f_{b},\phi\rangle:=\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(re^{i\theta})\,\phi(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta.

2.

There is a distribution
f_{b}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\partial D)
such that
f
is the Poisson integral of
f_{b}

f(re^{i\theta})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\langle f_{b},P_{r}(\theta-\cdot)\rangle,

where

P_{r}(\theta)=\frac{1-r^{2}}{1-2r\cos(\theta)+r^{2}}

is the Poisson kernel on
D
.

3.

There are constants
C>0
,
\alpha\geq 0
, such that

|f(re^{i\theta})|\leq\frac{C}{(1-r)^{\alpha}},

for
0\leq r<1
.

Theorem 2.6

(Corollary 3.1
[
1
]
)
.

The functions in
H^{p}(D)

, satisfy (3) in Theorem
2.5

Furthermore, not only do holomorphic Hardy spaces functions have boundary values in the sense of distributions, but those distributional boundary values are representable by an atomic decomposition.

Definition 2.7
.

For
0<p\leq 1
, we say a measurable function

•

\supp(a)\subset J

•

Theorem 2.8

(Theorem 2.2
[
1
]
)
.

For

and

,

has a distributional boundary value

and there exist a sequence
\{a_{j}\}
of
p

such that

in the topology of

In
[

]
, Theorem

Definition 2.9
.

For any

, the Hilbert transform

is given by

H(f)(e^{i\theta}):=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\epsilon\leq|t|\leq\pi}\frac{f(e^{i(\theta-t)})}{2\tan(t/2)}\,dt.

Theorem 2.10

(M. Riesz)
.

Let

,

. The Hilbert transform of

, denoted by

, is in

, and

||H(u)||_{L^{p}(\partial D)}\leq C||u||_{L^{p}(\partial D)},

where

In
[
1
]
, the following definitions are used to realize the boundary values in the sense of distributions of holomorphic Hardy space functions as the class of objects that extends the Hilbert transform to the case of small
p

[
1

Definition 2.11
.

For
0<p\leq 1

||f_{b}||_{at}:=\left(\inf\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|^{p}\right)^{1/p},

where the infimum is taken over the collection of all coefficient sequences of equivalent atomic decompositions.

Definition 2.12
.

For
0<p\leq 1
, we define
(H^{p}(D))_{b}
to be the collection of boundary values in the sense of distributions of functions in
H^{p}(D)
.

Theorem 2.13

(Theorem 6.2, Corollary 6.2
[

]
)

The Hilbert transform is a continuous operator on

with the atomic norm.

Before we proceed to the necessary background material for the bicomplex numbers, we describe an integral operator and include associated results that we use in the complex setting. We define and use a generalization of this integral operator later in the bicomplex setting.

Definition 2.14
.

For

and

\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}T(f)=f,

i.e.,

is a right-inverse operator to

, see

15
,
16
]

and are used throughout this work. We include them here for completeness.

(Theorem 1.26
[
15
]

.

If
f\in L^{q}(D)
,

, then

,

.

Theorem 2.16

(Theorem 1.4.7
[
17
]
)
.

If
f\in L^{q}(D)
,

, then

, where

satisfies

, and

||Tf||_{L^{\gamma}(\partial D(0,r))}\leq C||f||_{L^{q}(D)},

where

or

.

Theorem 2.17

(Theorem 1.4.8
[

]
)

If

2.2
Bicomplex Numbers

In this section (and those that follow), we use the notation and terminology from
[
6
,
7
]
to describe the definitions and background concerning the bicomplex numbers that we use throughout. Additional references for background on the bicomplex numbers and functions taking bicomplex values include
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
, as found in
[
6
,
7
]
.

The bicomplex numbers
\mathbb{B}
, as a set, can be described as
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Letting
\mathbb{B}
inherit the usual addition and multiplication by complex scalars, then with the multiplication defined by

(z_{1},z_{2})(w_{1},w_{2})=(z_{1}w_{1}-z_{2}w_{2},z_{1}w_{2}+z_{2}w_{1}),

for any
(z_{1},z_{2}),(w_{1},w_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
\mathbb{B}
is a commutative algebra over
\mathbb{C}
. This multiplication is the same as identifying
(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
with

z_{1}+jz_{2},

where
j^{2}=-1
, and applying the usual rules for complex number multiplication. If we consider the elements

p^{\pm}:=\frac{1}{2}(1\pm ij),

then we see that

p^{+}p^{+}=p^{+},\quad p^{-}p^{-}=p^{-},

and

p^{+}p^{-}=0.

So,
p^{\pm}
are idempotent, and
\mathbb{B}
has zero divisors.

Definition 2.19
.

We define the scalar part of
z=z_{1}+jz_{2}\in\mathbb{B}
as

\text{Sc}\,z=z_{1},

and the vector part of
z=z_{1}+jz_{2}\in\mathbb{B}
as

\text{Vec}\,z=z_{2}.

Definition 2.20
.

We define the bicomplex conjugate of
z=z_{1}+jz_{2}\in\mathbb{B}
as

\overline{z}:=z_{1}-jz_{2}.

Remark 1
.

To differentiate between bicomplex conjugation and the usual complex conjugation, we will denote complex conjugation for
u\in\mathbb{C}
by
u^{*}
, i.e., if
u=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}
, then
u^{*}=x-iy
. So, complex-valued holomorphic functions will be those
f:D\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{*}}=0,

complex-valued anti-holomorphic functions
f
will satisfy

\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}=0,

and we will denote the respective spaces of these functions by
Hol(D)
and
\overline{Hol(D)}
.

Proposition 2.21

(Proposition 1
[
6
]
)
.

Let
w\in\mathbb{B}
. There exist unique
w^{\pm}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

w=p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-}.

Furthermore,

w^{\pm}=\text{Sc}\,w\mp i\text{Vec}\,w.

The next few definitions bring into the bicomplex setting familiar objects of analysis.

Definition 2.22
.

For
w\in\mathbb{B}
, we define the bicomplex norm of
w
, denoted by
||\cdot||_{\mathbb{B}}
, as

||w||_{\mathbb{B}}:=\sqrt{\frac{|w^{+}|^{2}+|w^{-}|^{2}}{2}},

where
|w^{\pm}|
is the usual complex modulus.

Remark 2
.

Note that by the definition of
||\cdot||_{\mathbb{B}}
we have, for
w=p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-}\in\mathbb{B}
,

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|w^{\pm}|\leq||w||_{\mathbb{B}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(|w^{+}|+|w^{-}|\right),

and for
w,v\in\mathbb{B}
, we have

||wv||_{\mathbb{B}}\leq\sqrt{2}\,||w||_{\mathbb{B}}\,||v||_{\mathbb{B}}.

Definition 2.23
.

For
0<p<\infty
, we define the bicomplex Lebesgue space
L^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
to be the set of functions
f:D\to\mathbb{B}
such that

||f||_{L^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})}:=\left(\iint_{D}||f(z)||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

Similarly, we define
L^{p}(\partial D,\mathbb{B})
to be the set of functions
g:\partial D\to\mathbb{C}
such that

||g||_{L^{p}(\partial D,\mathbb{B})}:=\left(\int_{\partial D}||g(z)||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\sigma(z)\right)^{1/p}<\infty,

where
\sigma
is Lebesgue surface measure on
\partial D
.

The next proposition associates a function’s inclusion in the bicomplex Lebesgue spaces with the inclusion of the function’s complex components from its idempotent representation in the associated
\mathbb{C}
-valued
L^{p}
spaces.

Proposition 2.24
.

For
p
a positive real number, a function
f=p^{+}f^{+}+p^{-}f^{-}\in L^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
if and only if
f^{+},f^{-}\in L^{p}(D)
. The same result holds for
f\in L^{p}(\partial D,\mathbb{B})
.

Proof.

If
f\in L^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
, then

\displaystyle\iint_{D}|f^{\pm}(z)|^{p}\,dx\,dy\leq 2^{p/2}\iint_{D}||f(z)||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,dx\,dy<\infty.

If
f^{\pm}\in L^{p}(D)
, then

\displaystyle\iint_{D}||f(z)||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-p/2}\iint_{D}(|f^{+}(z)|+|f^{-}(z)|)^{p}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(\iint_{D}|f^{+}(z)|^{p}\,dx\,dy+\iint_{D}|f^{-}(z)|^{p}\,dx\,dy\right)<\infty,

where
C_{p}
is a constant that depends on only
p
. The proof for
f\in L^{p}(\partial D,\mathbb{B})
is the same.

Next, we define the differential operators that we consider.

Definition 2.25
.

We define the bicomplex differential operators
\partial
and
\bar{\partial}
as

\partial:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-j\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)

and

\bar{\partial}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+j\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right).

Remark 3
.

Note that the definition of
\partial
and
\bar{\partial}
above is equivalent to

\partial:=p^{+}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}+p^{-}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}

and

\bar{\partial}:=p^{+}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+p^{-}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}},

where
\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}

if and only if

\frac{\partial w^{+}}{\partial z}=0=\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z^{*}},

i.e.,
(w^{+})^{*},w^{-}\in Hol(D)

Definition 2.26
.

We define the bicomplexification
\widehat{u}
of a complex number
u=x+iy

\widehat{u}=x+jy.

For any complex variable

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}\,\widehat{u}=0,

\displaystyle\partial\,\widehat{u}^{n}=n\widehat{u}^{n-1},

\partial\,\widehat{u^{*}}=0,

and

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}\,\widehat{u^{*}}^{n}=n\widehat{u^{*}}^{n-1}.

So, with respect to
\bar{\partial}

\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}
.

and Theorem

, that we have

\displaystyle\iint_{D}||T_{\mathbb{B}}(f)(z)||_{\mathbb{B}}^{\gamma}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C_{\gamma}\left(\iint_{D}|T((f^{+})^{*})(z)|^{\gamma}\,dx\,dy+\iint_{D}|T(f^{-})(z)|^{\gamma}\,dx\,dy\right)<\infty,

for any
\gamma
satisfying
1<\gamma<\frac{2q}{2-q}
, where
C_{\gamma}
is a constant that only depends on only
\gamma
. Hence,
f\in L^{\gamma}(D,\mathbb{B})
, for
1<\gamma<\frac{2q}{2-q}
.

Appealing to Theorem
2.16
, instead of Theorem
2.15
, we have, for
0<r\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}||T_{\mathbb{B}}(f)(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{\gamma}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C_{\gamma}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|T((f^{+})^{*})(re^{i\theta})|^{\gamma}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}|T(f^{-})(re^{i\theta})|^{\gamma}\,d\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C_{\gamma}}\left(||f^{+}||_{L^{q}(D)}^{\gamma}+||f^{-}||_{L^{q}(D)}^{\gamma}\right)<\infty,

for
0<\gamma<\frac{q}{2-q}
, where
C_{\gamma}
and
\tilde{C_{\gamma}}
are constants that do not depend on
r
or
f
. Thus,
T_{\mathbb{B}}(f)|_{\partial D(0,r)}\in L^{\gamma}(\partial D(0,r),\mathbb{B})
, for each
0<r\leq 1
and
\gamma
satisfying
1<\gamma<\frac{q}{2-q}
. By Remark
2
again, we have

\displaystyle|f^{\pm}(z)|\leq\sqrt{2}\,||f(z)||_{\mathbb{B}},

so

||f^{\pm}||_{L^{q}(D)}^{\gamma}\leq 2^{\gamma/2}||f||_{L^{q}(D,\mathbb{B})}^{\gamma}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle||T_{\mathbb{B}}(f)||_{L^{\gamma}(\partial D(0,r),\mathbb{B})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(||f^{+}||_{L^{q}(D)}^{\gamma}+||f^{-}||_{L^{q}(D)}^{\gamma}\right)^{1/\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}||f||_{L^{q}(D,\mathbb{B})},

where
C
and
\tilde{C}
are constants that do not depend on
r
or
f
.

For any
0<r<1
and
1<\gamma<\frac{q}{2-q}
, observe that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}||T_{\mathbb{B}}(f)(e^{i\theta})-T_{\mathbb{B}}(f)(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{\gamma}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq M_{\gamma}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|(T((f^{+})^{*}))^{*}(e^{i\theta})-(T((f^{+})^{*}))^{*}(re^{i\theta})|^{\gamma}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}|T(f^{-})(e^{i\theta})-T(f^{-})(re^{i\theta})|^{\gamma}\,d\theta\right)

\displaystyle=M_{\gamma}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|T((f^{+})^{*})(e^{i\theta})-T((f^{+})^{*})(re^{i\theta})|^{\gamma}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}|T(f^{-})(e^{i\theta})-T(f^{-})(re^{i\theta})|^{\gamma}\,d\theta\right),

where
M_{\gamma}
is a constant that depends on
\gamma
. By Theorem
2.17
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||T_{\mathbb{B}}(f)(e^{i\theta})-T_{\mathbb{B}}(f)(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{\gamma}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{r\nearrow 1}M_{\gamma}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|T((f^{+})^{*})(e^{i\theta})-T((f^{+})^{*})(re^{i\theta})|^{\gamma}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}|T(f^{-})(e^{i\theta})-T(f^{-})(re^{i\theta})|^{\gamma}\,d\theta\right)

\displaystyle=M_{\gamma}\left(\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|T((f^{+})^{*})(e^{i\theta})-T((f^{+})^{*})(re^{i\theta})|^{\gamma}\,d\theta+\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|T(f^{-})(e^{i\theta})-T(f^{-})(re^{i\theta})|^{\gamma}\,d\theta\right)

\displaystyle=0.

Thus,

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||T_{\mathbb{B}}(f)(e^{i\theta})-T_{\mathbb{B}}(f)(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{\gamma}\,d\theta=0.

Finally, by Theorem
2.18
,
T((f^{+})^{*})
and
T(f^{-})
are in
C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{D})
, with
\alpha=\frac{q-2}{q}
, when
q>2
. Since
T((f^{+})^{*}),T(f^{-})\in C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{D})
, there exist
C_{+}
and
C_{-}
such that

|T((f^{+})^{*})(z)-T((f^{+})^{*})(w)|\leq C_{+}|z-w|^{\alpha}

and

|T(f^{-})(z)-T(f^{-})(w)|\leq C_{-}|z-w|^{\alpha},

for all
z,w\in\overline{D}
. Since
T_{\mathbb{B}}(f)=p^{+}(T((f^{+})^{*}))^{*}+p^{-}T(f^{-})
, it follows that, for any
z,w\in\overline{D}
, we have

\displaystyle||T_{\mathbb{B}}(f)(z)-T_{\mathbb{B}}(f)(w)||_{\mathbb{B}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-1/2}\left(|(T((f^{+})^{*}))^{*}(z)-(T((f^{+})^{*}))^{*}(w)|+|T(f^{-})(z)-T(f^{-})(w)|\right)

\displaystyle=2^{-1/2}\left(|T((f^{+})^{*})(z)-T((f^{+})^{*})(w)|+|T(f^{-})(z)-T(f^{-})(w)|\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-1/2}\left(C_{+}|z-w|^{\alpha}+C_{-}|z-w|^{\alpha}\right)=C|z-w|^{\alpha},

where
C=\max\{2^{-1/2}C_{+},2^{-1/2}C_{-}\}
, and we have
T_{\mathbb{B}}(f)\in C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{D},\mathbb{B})
, with
\alpha=\frac{q-2}{q}
.

∎

We also consider distributional boundary values in the context of bicomplex-valued functions.

Definition 2.29
.

Let
f:D\to\mathbb{B}
. We say that
f
has a boundary value in the sense of distributions, denoted by
f_{b}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\partial D)
, if, for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\partial D)
, the limit

\langle f_{b},\varphi\rangle:=\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(re^{i\theta})\,\varphi(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta

exists.

With this definition, we extend Theorem 3.6 from
[
19
]
to the bicomplex setting. This is a useful result concerning integrable functions and distributional boundary values.

Theorem 2.30
.

For every
f\in L^{1}(D,\mathbb{B})
such that
f
has a
L^{1}(\partial D,\mathbb{B})
boundary value and

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f(re^{i\theta})-f(e^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta=0,

f_{b}
exists and
f_{b}=f|_{\partial D}
as distributions.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.21
,
f=p^{+}f^{+}+p^{-}f^{-}
, and by Proposition
2.24
,
f^{+},f^{-}\in L^{1}(D)
. Since
f
has an
L^{1}(\partial D,\mathbb{B})
boundary value and
f=p^{+}f^{+}+p^{-}f^{-}
, it follows, by Proposition
2.24
, that
f^{+},f^{-}\in L^{1}(\partial D)
. Now, since

f(re^{i\theta})-f(e^{i\theta})=p^{+}(f^{+}(re^{i\theta})-f^{+}(e^{i\theta}))+p^{-}(f^{-}(re^{i\theta})-f^{-}(e^{i\theta})),

for every
r^{i\theta}\in D
, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f^{\pm}(re^{i\theta})-f^{\pm}(e^{i\theta})|\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f(re^{i\theta}-f(e^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta,

for every
r\in(0,1)
. Thus,

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f^{\pm}(re^{i\theta})-f^{\pm}(e^{i\theta})|\,d\theta\leq\sqrt{2}\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f(re^{i\theta}-f(e^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta=0.

In other words,

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f^{\pm}(re^{i\theta})-f^{\pm}(e^{i\theta})|\,d\theta=0.

By Theorem 3.6 of
[
19
]
, every
L^{1}(D)
function with

boundary value that converges to that boundary value in the
L^{1}
norm has a distributional boundary value and the distributional boundary value is equal to the
L^{1}(\partial D)
boundary value as distributions. Hence,
f^{+}_{b}
and
f^{-}_{b}
exist and
f^{+}_{b}=f^{+}|_{\partial D}
and
f^{-}_{b}=f^{-}|_{\partial D}
. So, for
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\partial D)
,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(re^{i\theta})\varphi(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta

\displaystyle=p^{+}\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f^{+}(re^{i\theta})\varphi(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta+p^{-}\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(re^{i\theta})\varphi(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta<\infty,

and
f_{b}
exists. Since
f=p^{+}f^{+}+p^{-}f^{-}
, it follows that
f_{b}=p^{+}f^{+}_{b}+p^{-}f^{-}_{b}
. Since
f^{+}_{b}=f^{+}|_{\partial D}
and
f^{-}_{b}=f^{-}|_{\partial D}
, it follows that

\displaystyle f|_{\partial D}

\displaystyle=p^{+}f^{+}|_{\partial D}+p^{-}f^{-}|_{\partial D}

\displaystyle=p^{+}f^{+}_{b}+p^{-}f^{-}_{b}

\displaystyle=f_{b}.

∎

2.3
Generalizations of the Hardy Spaces

Recently, there has been progress towards building a more general Hardy space theory of complex-valued functions on the disk that is not dependent on holomorphicity. In these generalized Hardy classes, functions (and in the higher-order variants, their derivatives) are required to have a finite
H^{p}
norm. However, instead of requiring holomorphicity of the function, the requirement for membership is that the function solves a certain generalization of the classic Cauchy-Riemann equation. For reference, some generalizations that have been considered include (but are certainly not limited to):

Generalized analytic functions, i.e., solutions of the Vekua equation

\frac{\partial w}{\partial z^{*}}=Aw+Bw^{*}

in
[

[

,
18

[
23
]

Quasiconformal maps, i.e., solutions of the Beltrami equation

\frac{\partial w}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu\frac{\partial w}{\partial z},

where
\mu
is a measurable function bounded in the

[

,
24

]

Solutions of the conjugate Beltrami equation

Poly-analytic functions, i.e., solutions of

Definition 2.31
.

For
0<p<\infty
and
f:D\to\mathbb{C}
, we define the Hardy classes
H^{p}_{f}(D)
to be the collection of functions
w:D\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\frac{\partial w}{\partial z^{*}}=f

and

\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta<\infty.

Remark 4
.

For
f\equiv 0
,
H^{p}_{f}(D)=H^{p}_{0}(D)
is exactly the classic holomorphic Hardy space
H^{p}(D)
.

Using the
T(\cdot)
operator defined in Section

, we have the following representation for functions that satisfy nonhomogeneous Cauchy-Riemann equations.

Theorem 2.32

(Theorem 1.16
[
15
]
)
.

If
w:D\to\mathbb{C}
satisfies
\frac{\partial w}{\partial z^{*}}=f\in L^{1}(D)
, then

w(z)=\varphi(z)-\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{D}\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}\,d\eta\,d\xi,

(1)

where
\zeta=\eta+i\xi
and
\varphi\in Hol(D)
.

The next result is clear by a direct computation.

Proposition 2.33
.

For every
0<p<\infty
,
f\in L^{q}(D)
,
q>2
, and
\varphi\in H^{p}(D)

is an element of
H^{p}_{f}(D)
. The result holds for
1<q\leq 2
, so long as
p<\frac{q}{2-q}
.

The next theorem is an extension of Theorem 2.10 from
[
5
]
where only the cases of

were considered. The argument is the same as in that paper, but we include it here for completeness and to have occasion to note that the restriction on the values of

from the original result was unnecessary for existence of the point-wise representation.

Theorem 2.34
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
q>2
, and
f\in L^{q}(D)
. Every
w\in H^{p}_{f}(D)
has a representation

w=\varphi+T(f)

and
\varphi\in H^{p}(D)
. Also, the boundary value in the sense of distributions
w_{b}
exists and, for
p\leq 1
, can be represented as

w_{b}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}a_{n}+T(f)_{b},

where
\{c_{n}\}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{C})
,
\{a_{n}\}
is a collection of
p
-atoms, and
T(f)_{b}\in C^{0,\alpha}(\partial D)
,
\alpha=\frac{q-2}{q}
. The result holds when
1<q\leq 2
, so long as
p
satisfies
p<\frac{q}{2-q}
, with
T(f)_{b}\in L^{\gamma}(\partial D)
, for
1<\gamma<\frac{q}{q-2}
.

Proof.

By assumption,
\frac{\partial w}{\partial z^{*}}=f\in L^{q}(D)\subset L^{1}(D)
, for
q>1
. So, by Theorem
2.32
,
w
has a representation

w(z)=\varphi(z)+T(f)(z),

(2)

where
\varphi
is holomorphic in
D
. Observe that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\varphi(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w(re^{i\theta})-T(f)(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}|T(f)(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(||w||^{p}_{H^{p}(D)}+\int_{0}^{2\pi}|T(f)(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\right),

where
C_{p}
is a constant that depends on only on
p
.

If
q>2
, then, by Theorem
2.18
,
T(f)\in C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{D})
. Hence, there exists a constant
M>0
such that
|T(f)(z)|\leq M
, for all
z\in\overline{D}
. So, for each
r\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|T(f)(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}M^{p}\,d\theta=2\pi M^{p}<\infty.

Thus,

\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|T(f)(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta<\infty.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\varphi(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(||w||^{p}_{H^{p}(D)}+\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|T(f)(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(||w||^{p}_{H^{p}(D)}+2\pi M^{p}\right)<\infty,

and
\varphi\in H^{p}(D)
.

Now, suppose that
1<q\leq 2
and
p
satisfies
p<\frac{q}{2-q}
. For each
r
, let
D_{r,*}=\{\theta\in[0,2\pi]:|T(f)(re^{i\theta})|\leq 1\}
and
D^{r,*}=\{\theta\in[0,2\pi]:|T(f)(re^{i\theta})|>1\}
. By Theorem
2.16
, since
f\in L^{q}(D),1<q\leq 2
, it follows that

\sup_{0<r<1}||T(f)||_{L^{\gamma}(\partial D(0,r))}\leq C||f||_{L^{q}(D)},

where
\gamma
satisfies
1<\gamma<\frac{q}{2-q}
and
C
is a constant that does not depend on
f
. Since
p<\frac{q}{2-q}
, it follows that there exist a
\gamma
such that
p<\gamma<\frac{q}{2-q}
, and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|T(f)(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{D_{r,*}}|T(f)(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta+\int_{D^{r,*}}|T(f)(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 2\pi+\int_{0}^{2\pi}|T(f)(re^{i\theta})|^{\gamma}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 2\pi+C||f||_{L^{q}(D)}<\infty.

Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\varphi(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq||w||^{p}_{H^{p}(D)}+\int_{0}^{2\pi}|T(f)(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq||w||^{p}_{H^{p}(D)}+2\pi+C||f||_{L^{q}(D)}<\infty,

where the right hand side has no dependence on
r
. Thus,

\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\varphi(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\leq||w||^{p}_{H^{p}(D)}+2\pi+C||f||_{L^{q}(D)}<\infty,

and
\varphi\in H^{p}(D)
.

Now, we restrict to
p\leq 1
. By Theorem
2.8
, since
\varphi\in H^{p}(D)
, it follows that there exists
\{c_{n}\}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{C})
and a sequence of
p
-atoms
\{a_{n}\}
such that
\varphi_{b}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}a_{n}
. By Theorems
2.15
,
2.16
, and
2.17
,
T(f)\in L^{1}(D)
,
T(f)
has an
L^{1}(\partial D)
boundary value
T(f)|_{\partial D}
, and
T(f)
converges to
T(f)|_{\partial D}
in the
L^{1}
norm, for

. By Theorem 3.6 from
[

By Theorem

We provide the relevant definitions for the function classes that we study.

Definition 3.1
.

We define the
\mathbb{B}
-holomorphic functions on
D
to be the functions
f:D\to\mathbb{B}
such that

\bar{\partial}f=0.

We denote by
Hol(D,\mathbb{B})
the space of all
\mathbb{B}
-holomorphic functions on
D
. Similarly, we define the
\mathbb{B}
-anti-holomorphic functions on
D
to be the functions
f:D\to\mathbb{B}
such that

\partial f=0.

We denote by
\overline{Hol(D,\mathbb{B})}
the space of all
\mathbb{B}
-anti-holomorphic functions on
D
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
w:D\to\mathbb{B}
. We define
H_{w}(D,\mathbb{B})
to be those functions
f:D\to\mathbb{B}
such that

\bar{\partial}f=w.

Definition 3.3
.

For
0<p<\infty
and a function
f:D\to\mathbb{B}
, we define the bicomplex-
H^{p}
norm to be

||f||_{H^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}}:=\sup_{0<r<1}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta\right)^{1/p}.

Remark 5
.

The bicomplex-
H^{p}
norm is only truly a norm when
p\geq 1
and is a quasi-norm for
0<p<1
. Following the convention in the literature for analogous objects, we call this quantity a norm regardless of the value of
p
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
0<p<\infty
. We define the bicomplex Hardy spaces
H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
to be those functions
f\in Hol(D,\mathbb{B})
such that

||f||_{H^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}}<\infty
.

4
Bicomplex Hardy Spaces

4.1
Representation

In this section, we prove a representation result that presents a connection between bicomplex-valued Hardy spaces and associated
\mathbb{C}
-valued Hardy spaces.

Theorem 4.1
.

For
0<p<\infty
, a function
f=p^{+}f^{+}+p^{-}f^{-}\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
if and only if
(f^{+})^{*},f^{-}\in H^{p}(D)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.21
and Remark
3
, for every
f:D\to\mathbb{B}
there exists
f^{+}:D\to\mathbb{C}
and
f^{-}:D\to\mathbb{C}
such that
f=p^{+}f^{+}+p^{-}f^{-}
and

\bar{\partial}f=0

if and only if

\frac{\partial f^{+}}{\partial z}=0=\frac{\partial f^{-}}{\partial z^{*}}.

Suppose
f\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
. Then
f=p^{+}f^{+}+p^{-}f^{-}
where
(f^{+})^{*},f^{-}\in Hol(D)
. Since

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|w^{\pm}|\leq||w||_{\mathbb{B}},

for all
w\in\mathbb{B}
, it follows that, for
r\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|(f^{+})^{*}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f^{+}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}(\sqrt{2}\,||f(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}})^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=2^{p/2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p/2}\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta<\infty.

Hence,

\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|(f^{+})^{*}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\leq 2^{p/2}\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta<\infty.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f^{-}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}(\sqrt{2}\,||f(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}})^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=2^{p/2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p/2}\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta<\infty.

Hence,

\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f^{-}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\leq 2^{p/2}\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta<\infty.

Thus,
(f^{+})^{*},f^{-}\in H^{p}(D)
.

Suppose
f^{+}:D\to\mathbb{C}
and
f^{-}:D\to\mathbb{C}
are two functions such that
(f^{+})^{*},f^{-}\in H^{p}(D)
and consider the function
f=p^{+}f^{+}+p^{-}f^{-}
. Now,
f:D\to\mathbb{B}
. Observe that

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}f

\displaystyle=\left(p^{+}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+p^{-}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}\right)(p^{+}f^{+}+p^{-}f^{-})

\displaystyle=(p^{+})^{2}\frac{\partial f^{+}}{\partial z}+p^{+}p^{-}\frac{\partial f^{-}}{\partial z}+p^{-}p^{+}\frac{\partial f^{+}}{\partial z^{*}}+(p^{-})^{2}\frac{\partial f^{-}}{\partial z^{*}}

\displaystyle=0.

So,
f\in Hol(D,\mathbb{B})
. Also, since

||w||_{\mathbb{B}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|w^{+}|+|w^{-}|),

for all
w\in\mathbb{B}
, it follows, for
r\in(0,1)
, that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|f^{+}(re^{i\theta})|+|f^{-}(re^{i\theta})|)\right)^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{p}}{2^{p/2}}\left[\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f^{+}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f^{-}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{p}}{2^{p/2}}\left[\int_{0}^{2\pi}|(f^{+})^{*}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f^{-}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\right]<\infty,

where
C_{p}
is a constant that depends only on
p
. Therefore
f\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
.

∎

4.2
Boundary Behavior

Using the representation result from the last subsection, we show that functions in
H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
exhibit boundary behavior similar to that of the
\mathbb{C}
-valued Hardy classes. The argument requires a direct appeal to the boundary behavior results for the generalized Hardy classes from
[
5
]
, see Definition
2.31
and Theorems
2.34
and
2.36
.

Theorem 4.2
.

For
0<p<\infty
, every
f=p^{+}f^{+}+p^{-}f^{-}\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
such that
f^{+}\in H^{p}_{w}(D)
, where
w\in L^{q}(D)
, and
q>2
or
1<q\leq 2
and
p
satisfies
p<\frac{q}{2-q}
, has a nontangential limit
f_{nt}\in L^{p}(\partial D,\mathbb{B})
, and

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta=0.

Proof.

Let
f\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
. By Theorem
4.1
,
f=p^{+}f^{+}+p^{-}f^{-}
, where
(f^{+})^{*},f^{-}\in H^{p}(D)
. Suppose also that
f^{+}\in H^{p}_{w}(D)
, for some
w\in L^{q}(D)
,
q>2
(or if
1<q\leq 2
, then
p
satisfies
p<\frac{q}{2-q}
). By Theorems
2.3
and Theorem
2.36
, it follows that
f^{+}
and
f^{-}
have nontangential boundary values
f^{+}_{nt}
and
f^{-}_{nt}
, respectively,
f^{+}_{nt},f^{-}_{nt}\in L^{p}(\partial D)
,

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f^{+}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f^{+}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta=0,

and

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f^{-}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f^{-}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta=0.

By linearity,
f_{nt}=p^{+}f^{+}_{nt}+p^{-}f^{-}_{nt}
exists and is in
L^{p}(\partial D)
. Observe that, for each
r\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}||p^{+}f^{+}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})+p^{-}f^{-}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-(p^{+}f^{+}(re^{i\theta})+p^{-}f^{-}(re^{i\theta}))||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}||p^{+}(f^{+}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f^{+}(re^{i\theta}))+p^{-}(f^{-}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f^{-}(re^{i\theta}))||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|f^{+}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f^{+}(re^{i\theta})|+|f^{-}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f^{-}(re^{i\theta})|)\right)^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{p}}{2^{p/2}}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f^{+}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f^{+}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f^{-}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f^{-}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\right),

where
C_{p}
is a constant that depends only on
p
. Since

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f^{+}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f^{+}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta=0

and

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f^{-}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f^{-}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta=0,

it follows that

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\frac{C_{p}}{2^{p/2}}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f^{+}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f^{+}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f^{-}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f^{-}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\right)=0.

Therefore,

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta=0.

∎

5
Generalizations of the Bicomplex Hardy Spaces

In this section, we define generalizations of
H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
that mimic the generalized Hardy classes
H^{p}_{w}(D)
of
[
5
]
, see Definition
2.31
. Specifically, we consider bicomplex-valued functions with
\bar{\partial}
-derivative not necessarily identically equal to zero and finite
H^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}
norm.

5.1
Definition

Definition 5.1
.

Let
0<p<\infty
. We define the bicomplex Hardy classes
H^{p}_{w}(D,\mathbb{B})
to be those functions
f\in H_{w}(D,\mathbb{B})
such that
||f||_{H^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}}<\infty
.

5.2
Representation

In this subsection, we show that bicomplex-valued solutions to nonhomogeneous
\bar{\partial}
-equations have a “representation of the second kind,” as named in
[
15
,
5
]
, similar to the
\mathbb{C}
case. This shows that solutions to nonhomogeneous
\bar{\partial}
-equations have the structure of a sum of a
\mathbb{B}
-holomorphic function and a well-controlled error term.

Proposition 5.2
.

Every solution
w:D\to\mathbb{B}
of

\bar{\partial}w=g,

with
\bar{\partial}w=g\in L^{1}(D,\mathbb{B})
, is representable as

w=\varphi+T_{\mathbb{B}}(g),

where
\varphi\in Hol(D,\mathbb{B})
.

Proof.

Since
\bar{\partial}T_{\mathbb{B}}(f)=f
, for every
f\in L^{1}(D,\mathbb{B})
, it follows that, for any solution
w
of
\bar{\partial}w=g
such that
\bar{\partial}w=g\in L^{1}(D,\mathbb{B})
,

\bar{\partial}(w-T_{\mathbb{B}}(g))=g-g=0.

∎

Next, we prove the bicomplex analogue of Corollary
2.35
. This provides a representation for functions
H^{p}_{w}(D,\mathbb{B})
in terms of
H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
functions.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
0<p<\infty
and
w\in L^{q}(D,\mathbb{B})
,
q>2
. For every
f\in H_{w}(D,\mathbb{B})
with representation

f=\varphi+T_{\mathbb{B}}(w),

f\in H^{p}_{w}(D,\mathbb{B})
if and only if
\varphi\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
. The result holds when
1<q\leq 2
, so long as
p
satisfies
p<\frac{q}{2-q}
.

Proof.

By hypothesis,
\bar{\partial}f=w\in L^{1}(D,\mathbb{B})
, so by Proposition
5.2
,

f=\varphi+T_{\mathbb{B}}(w).

Suppose that
\varphi\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
. Then, for
r\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}||\varphi(re^{i\theta})+T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}||\varphi(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}+\int_{0}^{2\pi}||T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(||\varphi||_{H^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}}^{p}+\int_{0}^{2\pi}||T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta\right),

(3)

where
C_{p}
is a constant that depends on only
p
. By Theorem
2.28
, since
w\in L^{q}(D,\mathbb{B})
,
q>2
, it follows that
T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)\in C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{D},\mathbb{B})
. Thus, there exists
M>0
such that

||T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)(z)||_{\mathbb{B}}\leq M,

for every
z\in\overline{D}
. Hence,

\displaystyle C_{p}\left(||\varphi||_{H^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}}^{p}+\int_{0}^{2\pi}||T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(||\varphi||_{H^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}}^{p}+2\pi M\right)<\infty.

Since there is no dependence on
r
in the right hand side of the above inequality, we have

\displaystyle||f||_{H^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(||\varphi||_{H^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}}^{p}+2\pi M\right)<\infty.

If
1<q\leq 2
and
p
satisfies
p<\frac{q}{2-q}
, then returning to (
3
) we have

\displaystyle C_{p}\left(||\varphi||_{H^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}}^{p}+\int_{0}^{2\pi}||T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(||\varphi||_{H^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}}^{p}+C||w||^{p}_{L^{q}(D)}\,d\theta\right)<\infty,

by Theorem
2.28
. Since the right hand side has no dependence on
r
, we have

||f||_{H^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}}^{p}\leq C_{p}\left(||\varphi||_{H^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}}^{p}+C||w||^{p}_{L^{q}(D)}\,d\theta\right)<\infty.

In either case
f\in H^{p}_{w}(D,\mathbb{B})
.

Now, suppose that
f=\varphi+T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)\in H^{p}_{w}(D,\mathbb{B})
. Observe that, for
r\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}||\varphi(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f(re^{i\theta})-T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}||T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta\right)

(4)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(||f||_{H^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}}^{p}+M2\pi\right)<\infty,

where
C_{p}
is a constant that depends on only
p
and
M
is a constant that bounds
||T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)(z)||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}
, for all
z\in\overline{D}
. So,

||\varphi||_{H^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}}^{p}\leq C_{p}\left(||f||_{H^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}}^{p}+M2\pi\right)<\infty,

and
\varphi\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
. If
1<q\leq 2
and
p
satisfies
p<\frac{q}{2-q}
, then returning to (
4
) and appealing to Theorem
2.28
once more, we have

\displaystyle C_{p}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}||T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(||f||^{p}_{H^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}}+C||w||^{p}_{L^{q}(D)}\right)<\infty.

Therefore,

||\varphi||^{p}_{H^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}}\leq C_{p}\left(||f||^{p}_{H^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}}+C||w||^{p}_{L^{q}(D)}\right)<\infty,

and
\varphi\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
.

∎

5.3
Boundary Behavior

With the representation theorem from the last subsection and the previous results about the boundary behavior for the
\mathbb{B}
-holomorphic Hardy spaces
H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
, we are ready to show that the generalized bicomplex Hardy classes
H^{p}_{w}(D,\mathbb{B})
have the desirable boundary behavior associated with Hardy classes.

Theorem 5.4
.

For
0<p<\infty
and
w\in L^{q}(D,\mathbb{B})
,

, every
f=\varphi+T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)\in H^{p}_{w}(D,\mathbb{B})
such that
\varphi^{+}\in H^{p}_{g}(D)
, where

,

or

and

, has a nontangential limit

, and

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta=0.

The result holds when
1<q\leq 2
, so long as
p
satisfies
p<\frac{q}{2-q}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.3
, if
f\in H^{p}_{w}(D,\mathbb{B})
with
w\in L^{q}(D)
, where
q
and
p
satisfy the hypothesis, then
f=\varphi+T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)
and
\varphi\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
. If we assume that
\varphi^{+}\in H^{p}_{g}(D)
, for some

,
\gamma>2
or
1<\gamma\leq 2
and
p<\frac{\gamma}{2-\gamma}
, then by Theorem

,
\varphi
has a nontangential boundary value

and

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||\varphi_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-\varphi(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta=0.

By Theorem

, if
p
and
q
satisfy the hypothesis, then

and

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)(e^{i\theta})-T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta=0.

So,
f_{nt}
exists and is in

. Also, for

, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}||\varphi_{nt}(e^{i\theta})+T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)(e^{i\theta})-(\varphi(re^{i\theta})+T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)(re^{i\theta}))||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}||\varphi_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-\varphi(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}||T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)(e^{i\theta})-T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{r\nearrow 1}C_{p}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}||\varphi_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-\varphi(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}||T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)(e^{i\theta})-T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||\varphi_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-\varphi(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta+\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)(e^{i\theta})-T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta\right)

\displaystyle=0.

∎

6
Atomic Decomposition

In this section, we show that the bicomplex Hardy classes have boundary values in the sense of distributions and distributional boundary values of functions in the bicomplex Hardy classes, for small
p
, have an atomic decomposition. From this atomic decomposition, we show that the classic Hilbert transform on the circle is a continuous operator on this class of boundary distributions. This extends the results in the
\mathbb{C}
-holomorphic case from
[
1
]
and the
\mathbb{C}
-valued but not necessarily holomorphic case from
[
5
]
.

By the linearity of the integral,
w=p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-}:D\to\mathbb{B}
has a boundary value in the sense of distributions whenever the functions
w^{+},w^{-}D:\to\mathbb{C}
have boundary values in the sense of distributions. We begin by showing that functions in
H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
have boundary values in the sense of distributions.

Proposition 6.1
.

For
0<p\leq 1
, every
w=p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-}\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
has a distributional boundary value
w_{b}
and

w_{b}=p^{+}w^{+}_{b}+p^{-}w^{-}_{b}.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.1
, if
w\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
, then
(w^{+})^{*},w^{-}\in H^{p}(D)
. By Theorem
2.5
and
2.6
, since
(w^{+})^{*},w^{-}\in H^{p}(D)
, it follows that
(w^{+})^{*}_{b},w^{-}_{b}
, the distributional boundary values of
(w^{+})^{*}
and
w^{-}
, respectively, exist.

Since
(w^{+})^{*}_{b}
exists, it follows, by definition, that the limit

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(w^{+})^{*}(re^{i\theta})\,\varphi(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta

exists, for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\partial D)
. Since, for every
r\in(0,1)
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\partial D)
,

\int_{0}^{2\pi}(w^{+})^{*}(re^{i\theta})\,\varphi(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta=\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}w^{+}(re^{i\theta})\,\varphi^{*}(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta\right)^{*},

it follows that the limit

\left(\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}w^{+}(re^{i\theta})\,\varphi^{*}(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta\right)^{*}=\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(w^{+})^{*}(re^{i\theta})\,\varphi(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta

exists. Hence,
w^{+}_{b}
exists.

Now, observe that for
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\partial D)
and
r\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}w(re^{i\theta})\,\varphi(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta

\displaystyle=p^{+}\int_{0}^{2\pi}w^{+}(re^{i\theta})\,\varphi(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta+p^{-}\int_{0}^{2\pi}w^{-}(re^{i\theta})\,\varphi(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta.

Since the limits

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}w^{+}(re^{i\theta})\,\varphi(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta

and

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}w^{-}(re^{i\theta})\,\varphi(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta

exist, it follows that the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}w(re^{i\theta})\,\varphi(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta

\displaystyle=p^{+}\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}w^{+}(re^{i\theta})\,\varphi(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta+p^{-}\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}w^{-}(re^{i\theta})\,\varphi(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta

exists. Thus,
w_{b}
exists, and

w_{b}=p^{+}w^{+}_{b}+p^{-}w^{-}_{b}.

∎

We are now ready to show the variant of the atomic decomposition result in this context.

Theorem 6.2
.

For
0<p\leq 1
and every
w\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
, there exist sequences
\{c_{n}^{+}\},\{c_{n}^{-}\}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{C})
and collections of
p
-atoms
\{a_{n}^{+}\}
and
\{a_{n}^{-}\}
such that

w_{b}=p^{+}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c^{+}_{n}a^{+}_{n}\right)^{*}+p^{-}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c^{-}_{k}a^{-}_{k}.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.1
,
w=p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-}\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
if and only if
(w^{+})^{*},w^{-}\in H^{p}(D)
. By Theorem
2.8
, since
(w^{+})^{*},w^{-}\in H^{p}(D)
, it follows that there exists
\{c_{n}^{+}\},\{c_{n}^{-}\}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{C})
and collections of
p
-atoms
\{a_{n}^{+}\}
and
\{a_{n}^{-}\}

such that

(w^{+})^{*}_{b}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}^{+}a_{n}^{+}

and

w^{-}_{b}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}^{-}a_{n}^{-}.

By Proposition
6.1
,
w_{b}
exists and

w_{b}=p^{+}w^{+}_{b}+p^{-}w^{-}_{b}=p^{+}((w^{+})^{*}_{b})^{*}+p^{-}w^{-}.

Thus,

w_{b}=p^{+}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}^{+}a_{n}^{+}\right)^{*}+p^{-}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}^{-}a_{n}^{-}.

∎

In pursuit of showing that the Hilbert transform is a continuous operator on the set of boundary values in the sense of distributions of functions in
H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
, for small
p
, we require a norm for this class of objects. We define the one that we consider.

Definition 6.3
.

For
0<p\leq 1
, we define
(H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B}))_{b}
to be the collection of distributional boundary values of functions in
H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
.

Definition 6.4
.

We define the
\mathbb{B}
-atomic norm
||\cdot||_{\mathbb{B},at}
on
(H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B}))_{b}
by

||w_{b}||_{\mathbb{B},at}:=||w^{+}_{b}||_{at}+||w^{-}_{b}||_{at},

where
||\cdot||_{at}
is the atomic norm defined on the distributional boundary values of functions in the holomorphic Hardy spaces
H^{p}(D)
defined in Definition
2.11
.

Theorem 6.5
.

For
0<p\leq 1
, the Hilbert transform is a continuous operator on
(H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B}))_{b}
with the norm
||\cdot||_{\mathbb{B},at}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.1
, if
w\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
,
0<p\leq 1
, then
w=p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-}
and
(w^{+})^{*},w^{-}\in H^{p}(D)
. By Theorem
2.13
, the Hilbert transform is a continuous operator on the distributional boundary values of the functions in
H^{p}(D)
,
0<p\leq 1
, with the atomic norm i.e., for every
f\in(H^{p}(D))_{b}
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

||H(f)||_{L^{p}(\partial D)}\leq C||f||_{at}.

Since
(w^{+})^{*},w^{-}\in H^{p}(D)
, it follows that
(w^{+})_{b},w^{-}_{b}
exist and there exist positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

||H((w^{+})^{*}_{b})||_{L^{p}(\partial D)}\leq C_{1}||(w^{+})^{*}_{b}||_{at}

and

||H(w^{-}_{b})||_{L^{p}(\partial D)}\leq C_{2}||w^{-}_{b}||_{at}.

Thus,

\displaystyle||H(w_{b})||_{L^{p}(\partial D,\mathbb{B})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(||H(w^{+})||_{L^{p}(\partial D)}+||H(w^{-})||_{L^{p}(\partial D)}\right).

Since

||H(w^{+})||_{L^{p}(\partial D)}=||(H((w^{+})^{*}))^{*}||_{L^{p}(\partial D)}=||H((w^{+})^{*})||_{L^{p}(\partial D)}

and

||(w^{+})^{*}_{b}||_{at}=||w^{+}_{b}||_{at},

it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(||H(w^{+})||_{L^{p}(\partial D)}+||H(w^{-})||_{L^{p}(\partial D)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(||H((w^{+})^{*})||_{L^{p}(\partial D)}+||H(w^{-})||_{L^{p}(\partial D)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(C_{1}||(w^{+})^{*}_{b}||_{at}+C_{2}||w^{-}_{b}||_{at}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(C_{1}||w^{+}_{b}||_{at}+C_{2}||w^{-}_{b}||_{at}\right)

\displaystyle<C\left(||w^{+}_{b}||_{at}+||w^{-}_{b}||_{at}\right)

\displaystyle=C||w_{b}||_{\mathbb{B},at},

where
C
is a constant that is larger than
2^{-1/2}C_{1}
and
2^{-1/2}C_{2}
.

∎

We are able to immediately extend from the holomorphic to the generalized setting as in the complex setting of
[
5
]
.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
0<p\leq 1
and
w\in L^{q}(D,\mathbb{B})
,
q>1
. For every
f\in H^{p}_{w}(D,\mathbb{B})
, there exist sequences
\{c_{n}^{+}\},\{c_{n}^{-}\}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{C})
and collections of
p
-atoms
\{a_{n}^{+}\}
and
\{a_{n}^{-}\}
such that

f_{b}=p^{+}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c^{+}_{n}a^{+}_{n}\right)^{*}+p^{-}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c^{-}_{k}a^{-}_{k}+T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)_{b},

and
T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)_{b}\in L^{\gamma}(\partial D,\mathbb{B})
,

. If

, then
T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)_{b}\in C^{0,\alpha}(\partial D,\mathbb{B})
,
\alpha=\frac{q-2}{q}
.

Proof.

For
0<p\leq 1
and
w\in L^{q}(D)
,
q>1
, if
f\in H^{p}_{w}(D,\mathbb{B})
, then, by Theorem
5.3
,

w=\varphi+T_{\mathbb{B}}(w),

and
\varphi\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
. By Theorem
6.2
,
\varphi_{b}
exists, and there exist
\{c_{n}^{+}\},\{c_{n}^{-}\}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{C})
and collections of
p
-atoms
\{a_{n}^{+}\}
and
\{a_{n}^{-}\}
such that

\varphi_{b}=p^{+}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c^{+}_{n}a^{+}_{n}\right)^{*}+p^{-}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c^{-}_{k}a^{-}_{k}.

By Theorem
2.28
, since
q>1
, it follows that
T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)|_{\partial D}\in L^{\gamma}(\partial D,\mathbb{B})
,
1<\gamma<\frac{q}{2-q}
, and if

, then
T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)|_{\partial D}\in C^{0,\alpha}(\partial D,\mathbb{B})
,

. Since

, it follows, by Theorem
2.28
, that

and

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)(e^{i\theta})-T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta=0

so by Theorem
2.30
,
T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)_{b}
exists and
T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)_{b}=T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)|_{\partial D}
.

Since
f=\varphi+T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)
and
\varphi_{b}
and

exist, it follows that
f_{b}
exists and

Since

\varphi_{b}=p^{+}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c^{+}_{n}a^{+}_{n}\right)^{*}+p^{-}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c^{-}_{k}a^{-}_{k},

it follows that

∎

Also, we show the Hilbert transform is a continuous operator.

Definition 6.7
.

For
0<p\leq 1
and
w\in L^{q}(D)
,

, we define
(H^{p}_{w}(D,\mathbb{B}))_{b}
to be the collection of distributional boundary values of functions in

.

||\cdot||_{\mathbb{B},b}
on

where

is the
\mathbb{B}
-atomic norm on the distributional boundary values of functions in the
\mathbb{B}
-holomorphic Hardy spaces

defined in Definition

Theorem 6.9
.

For
0<p\leq 1
and
w\in L^{q}(D,\mathbb{B})
,

, the Hilbert transform is a continuous operator on
(H^{p}_{w}(D,\mathbb{B}))_{b}
with the quasi-norm
||\cdot||_{\mathbb{B},b}
.

Proof.

Let
f\in H^{p}_{w}(D,\mathbb{B})
. By Theorem
6.6
,

f_{b}=\varphi_{b}+T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)_{b}.

By Theorem
6.5
, since
\varphi_{b}\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
and

By Theorem
2.10

and

\displaystyle\leq||H(\varphi_{b})||_{L^{p}(\partial D)}+||H(T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)_{b})||_{L^{p}(\partial D)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}||\varphi_{b}||_{\mathbb{B},at}+C_{2}||T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)_{b}||_{L^{\gamma}(\partial D)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(||\varphi_{b}||_{\mathbb{B},at}+||T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)_{b}||_{L^{\gamma}(\partial D)}\right)

7
Higher-Order Generalizations of the Bicomplex Hardy Spaces

In this section, we extend the results of the previous sections to higher-order variants of the bicomplex Hardy classes considered. This mirrors the progression from first-order to higher-order in
[
5
]
for the complex-valued Hardy classes.

7.1
Definition

Definition 7.1
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
n
a positive integer, and
w:D\to\mathbb{B}
. We define
H^{n,p}_{w}(D,\mathbb{B})
to be the set of functions
f:D\to\mathbb{B}
such that

\bar{\partial}^{n}f=w

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}||\bar{\partial}^{k}f||^{p}_{H^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}}<\infty.

7.2
Representation

First, we prove a result that is both classical in the complex holomorphic setting and critical to the proof of the representation result that follows.

Proposition 7.2
.

For
0<p<\infty
, every
w\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
is an element of
L^{m}(D,\mathbb{B})
, for all
0<m<2p
.

Proof.

Recall that, for
0<p<\infty
, if
g\in H^{p}(D)
, then
g\in L^{m}(D)
, for every
0<m<2p
. See, for example, Lemma 1.8.3 of
[
17
]
.

Now, by Theorem
4.1
, if
f\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
, then
(f^{+})^{*},f^{-}\in H^{p}(D)
. So,
(f^{+})^{*},f^{-}\in L^{m}(D)
, for all
0<m<2p
. Since

|(f^{+})^{*}(z)|=|f^{+}(z)|,

for all
z\in D
, it follows that
f^{+}\in L^{m}(D)
, for all
0<m<2p
. By Proposition
2.24
, since
f^{+},f^{-}\in L^{m}(D)
, for all
0<m<2p
, it follows that
f\in L^{m}(D,\mathbb{B})
, for all
0<m<2p
.

∎

We are now ready to prove the analogue of the pointwise function representation from Theorem 4.3 of
[
5
]
for the complex case. Existence and representation of distributional boundary values will be handled in the next section, along with nontangential boundary values.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
n
a positive integer, and
w\in L^{q}(D,\mathbb{B})
,
q>2
. Every
f\in H^{n,p}_{w}(D,\mathbb{B})
can be represented as

f=\Phi_{0}+\Psi,

where

\Psi=T_{\mathbb{B}}(\Phi_{1}+T_{\mathbb{B}}(\Phi_{2}+T_{\mathbb{B}}(\cdots+\Phi_{n-1}+T_{\mathbb{B}}(w))\cdots))

and
\Phi_{k}\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
, for every
k
, and
\Psi\in C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{D},\mathbb{B})
. The result holds for
p>\frac{1}{2}
and
1<q\leq 2
, with
\Psi\in L^{\gamma}(D,\mathbb{B})
,
1<\gamma<\frac{2q}{2-q}
, so long as
p
satisfies
\frac{1}{2}<p<\frac{q}{2-q}
.

Proof.

First, suppose
p>1
and
q>2
. By applying Theorem
5.3
, we have

\bar{\partial}^{n-1}f=\Phi_{n-1}+T_{\mathbb{B}}(w),

where
\Phi_{n-1}\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
and
T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)\in C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{D},\mathbb{B})
. Since
p>1
, it follows by Proposition
7.2
that
\Phi_{n-1}\in L^{q_{n-1}}(D,\mathbb{B})
, for some
2<q_{n-1}<2p
. Hence,
\Phi_{n-1}+T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)\in L^{q_{n-1}}(D,\mathbb{B})
. So, we appeal to Theorem
5.3
again to have

\bar{\partial}^{n-2}f=\Phi_{n-2}+T_{\mathbb{B}}(\Phi_{n-1}+T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)).

Repeating this
n-2
more times, we have the representation

f=\Phi_{0}+\Psi,

where

\Psi=T_{\mathbb{B}}(\Phi_{1}+T_{\mathbb{B}}(\Phi_{2}+T_{\mathbb{B}}(\cdots+\Phi_{n-1}+T_{\mathbb{B}}(w))\cdots)).

Now, suppose that
1<q\leq 2
. So long as
p<\frac{q}{2-q}
, we have

\bar{\partial}^{n-1}f=\Phi_{n-1}+T_{\mathbb{B}}(w),

by Theorem
5.3
, where
\Phi_{n-1}\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
and
T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)\in L^{\gamma}(D,\mathbb{B})
,
1<\gamma<\frac{2q}{2-q}
. If
p>\frac{1}{2}
, then, by Proposition
7.2
,
\Phi_{n-1}\in L^{q_{n-1}}(D,\mathbb{B})
, where
1<q_{n-1}<2p
. So,
\Phi_{n-1}+T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)\in L^{q_{n-1}}(D,\mathbb{B})
. So, we appeal to Theorem
5.3
again to have

\bar{\partial}^{n-2}f=\Phi_{n-2}+T_{\mathbb{B}}(\Phi_{n-1}+T_{\mathbb{B}}(w)).

Repeating this
n-2
more times, we have the representation

f=\Phi_{0}+\Psi,

where

\Psi=T_{\mathbb{B}}(\Phi_{1}+T_{\mathbb{B}}(\Phi_{2}+T_{\mathbb{B}}(\cdots+\Phi_{n-1}+T_{\mathbb{B}}(w))\cdots)).

Therefore, the result holds for
p>1
and
q>2
, and the result holds for
1<q\leq 2
, so long as
p
satisfies
\frac{1}{2}<p<\frac{q}{2-q}
.

∎

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
n
a positive integer, and
w\in L^{q}(D,\mathbb{B})
,
q>2
. Every
f\in H^{n,p}_{w}(D,\mathbb{B})
can be represented as

\displaystyle f(z)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{0}(z)+p^{+}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(\iint_{D}K_{k,0}(z-\zeta)\Phi^{+}_{k}(\zeta)\,d\eta\,d\xi\right)+\iint_{D}K_{n,0}(z-\zeta)w^{+}(\zeta)\,d\eta\,d\xi\right]

\displaystyle\quad\quad+p^{-}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(\iint_{D}K_{0,k}(z-\zeta)\Phi_{k}^{-}(\zeta)\,d\eta\,d\xi\right)+\iint_{D}K_{0,n}(z-\zeta)w^{-}(\zeta)\,d\eta\,d\xi\right],

where, for integers
m
and
\gamma
,

K_{m,\gamma}(z):=\begin{cases}\frac{(-m)!(-1)^{m}}{(\gamma-1)!\pi}z^{m-1}(z^{*})^{\gamma-1},&m=0,\\
\frac{(-\gamma)!(-1)^{\gamma}}{(m-1)!\pi}z^{m-1}(z^{*})^{\gamma-1},&\gamma=0,\\
\frac{1}{(m-1)!(\gamma-1)!\pi}z^{m-1}(z^{*})^{\gamma-1}\left(\log|z|^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{k}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{\gamma-1}\frac{1}{\ell}\right),&m,\gamma\geq 1,\\
\end{cases}

(the first or second sums in the last case are taken to be zero if
m=1
or
\gamma=1
, respectively), and
\Phi_{k}\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
, for every
k
. The result holds for
p>\frac{1}{2}
and
1<q\leq 2
, so long as
p
satisfies
\frac{1}{2}<p<\frac{q}{2-q}
.

Proof.

This representation is a direct computation using the definition of
T_{\mathbb{B}}
combined with the result of Theorem
7.3
and the work of Begehr and Hile in
[
30
]
(or Begehr in
[
16
]
).
∎

7.3
Boundary Behavior

The next theorem shows the bicomplex higher-order Hardy classes share the boundary behavior we expect of a Hardy class.

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
n
a positive integer, and
w\in L^{q}(D,\mathbb{B})
,
q>2
. Every
f=\Phi_{0}+\Psi\in H^{n,p}_{w}(D,\mathbb{B})
such that
\Phi_{0}^{+}\in H^{p}_{g}(D)
, where
g\in L^{\gamma}(D)
, and
\gamma>2
or
1<\gamma\leq 2
and
p
satisfies
p<\frac{\gamma}{2-\gamma}
, has a nontangential limit
f_{nt}\in L^{p}(\partial D,\mathbb{B})
, and

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta=0.

The result holds for
p>\frac{1}{2}
when
1<q\leq 2
, so long as
p
satisfies
\frac{1}{2}<p<\frac{q}{2-q}
.

Proof.

If
p>1
,
n
a positive integer,
w\in L^{q}(D,\mathbb{B})
,
q>2
, and
w\in H^{n,p}_{w}(D,\mathbb{B})
, then, by Theorem
7.3
,
f=\Phi_{0}+\Psi
, where
\Phi_{0}\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
and
\Psi\in C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{D},\mathbb{B})
. If
\Phi_{0}^{+}\in H^{p}_{g}(D,\mathbb{B})
and
g\in L^{\gamma}(D)
and
\gamma>2
or
1<\gamma\leq 2
and
p
satisfies
p<\frac{\gamma}{2-\gamma}
, then, by Theorem
4.2
,
\Phi_{0}
has a nontangential boundary value
\Phi_{0,nt}\in L^{p}(\partial D,\mathbb{B})
and

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||\Phi_{0,nt}(e^{i\theta})-\Phi_{0}(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta=0.

So, the nontangential boundary value
f_{nt}
of
f
exists and equals
f_{nt}=\Phi_{0,nt}+\Psi|_{\partial D}
. By Theorem
2.28
,

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||\Psi(e^{i\theta})-\Psi(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta=0.

Therefore, for
r\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}||\Phi_{0,nt}(e^{i\theta})+\Psi(e^{i\theta})-(\Phi_{0}(re^{i\theta})+\Psi(re^{i\theta}))||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(||\Phi_{0,nt}(e^{i\theta})-\Phi_{0}(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}||\Psi(e^{i\theta})-\Psi(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta\right),

where
C_{p}
is a constant that depends on only
p
. Thus,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{r\nearrow 1}C_{p}\left(||\Phi_{0,nt}(e^{i\theta})-\Phi_{0}(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}||\Psi(e^{i\theta})-\Psi(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta\right)=0.

If
1<q\leq 2
and
p
satisfies
\frac{1}{2}<p<\frac{q}{2-q}
, then, by Theorem
7.3
,
f=\Phi_{0}+\Psi
, where
\Phi_{0}\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
,
\Psi\in L^{r}(D,\mathbb{B})
,
1<r<\frac{2q}{2-q}
, and
\Psi|_{\partial D}(\partial D,\mathbb{B})\in L^{s}(\partial D,\mathbb{B})
,
1<s<\frac{q}{2-q}
. So, if
\Phi_{0}^{+}\in H^{p}_{g}(D,\mathbb{B})
and
g\in L^{\gamma}(D)
and
\gamma>2
or
1<\gamma\leq 2
and
p
satisfies
p<\frac{\gamma}{2-\gamma}
, then, by Theorem
4.2
,
\Phi_{0}
has a nontangential boundary value
\Phi_{0,nt}\in L^{p}(\partial D,\mathbb{B})
and

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||\Phi_{0,nt}(e^{i\theta})-\Phi_{0}(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta=0.

So, the nontangential boundary value
f_{nt}
of
f
exists and equals
f_{nt}=\Phi_{0,nt}+\Psi|_{\partial D}
. By Theorem
2.28
,

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||\Psi(e^{i\theta})-\Psi(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta=0,

as
p<\frac{q}{2-q}
by assumption. Therefore, for
r\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}||\Phi_{0,nt}(e^{i\theta})+\Psi(e^{i\theta})-(\Phi_{0}(re^{i\theta})+\Psi(re^{i\theta}))||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(||\Phi_{0,nt}(e^{i\theta})-\Phi_{0}(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}||\Psi(e^{i\theta})-\Psi(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta\right),

where
C_{p}
is a constant that depends on only
p
. Thus,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-f(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{r\nearrow 1}C_{p}\left(||\Phi_{0,nt}(e^{i\theta})-\Phi_{0}(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}||\Psi(e^{i\theta})-\Psi(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta\right)=0.

∎

Also, we show the bicomplex higher-order Hardy classes have distributional boundary values that are representable by an atomic decomposition.

Theorem 7.6
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}<p\leq 1
,

be a positive integer, and

,
\{a_{n}^{+}\}
and
\{a_{n}^{-}\}

p
-atoms, and
\Psi_{b}\in L^{\gamma}(\partial D,\mathbb{B})
,
1<\gamma<\frac{q}{2-q}
. If
q>2
, then
\Psi_{b}\in C^{0,\alpha}(\partial D,\mathbb{B})
,
\alpha=\frac{q-2}{q}
.

Proof.

By Theorems
7.3
and
2.28
, every
f\in H^{n,p}_{w}(D,\mathbb{B})
has a representation

f=\Phi_{0}+\Psi,

where
\Phi_{0}\in H^{p}(D,\mathbb{B})
and
\Psi\in C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{D},\mathbb{B})
or
L^{\gamma}(D,\mathbb{B})
,
1<\gamma<\frac{2q}{2-q}
, depending on the value of
q
. Also, by Theorem
2.28
,
\Psi|_{\partial D}\in L^{\gamma}(\partial D)
,
1<\gamma<\frac{2q}{q-2}
. By Theorem
2.30
,
\Psi_{b}
exists and equals
\Psi|_{\partial D}
. By Theorem
6.2
, the distributional boundary value
\Phi_{0,b}
of
\Phi_{0}
exists, and there exist
\{c_{n}^{+}\},\{c_{n}^{-}\}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{C})
,
\{a_{n}^{+}\}
and
\{a_{n}^{-}\}
are collections of
p
-atoms such that

\Phi_{0,b}=p^{+}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c^{+}_{n}a^{+}_{n}\right)^{*}+p^{-}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c^{-}_{k}a^{-}_{k}.

Thus, the distributional boundary value
f_{b}
exists, and

f_{b}=p^{+}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c^{+}_{n}a^{+}_{n}\right)^{*}+p^{-}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c^{-}_{k}a^{-}_{k}+\Psi_{b}

∎

With the atomic representation from the last theorem, we show the Hilbert transform is a continuous operator on the distributional boundary values of
H^{n,p}_{w}(D,\mathbb{B})
.

Theorem 7.7
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}<p\leq 1
,
n
a positive integer, and
w\in L^{q}(D,\mathbb{B})
,
q>1
. The Hilbert transform is a continuous operator on
(H^{n,p}_{w}(D,\mathbb{B}))_{b}
with the quasi-norm
||\cdot||_{\mathbb{B},b}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
7.6
, every
f\in H^{n,p}_{w}(D,\mathbb{B})
has a boundary value in the sense of distributions
f_{b}
, and there exist
\{c_{n}^{+}\},\{c_{n}^{-}\}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{C})
,
\{a_{n}^{+}\}
and
\{a_{n}^{-}\}
are collections of
p
-atoms such that

f_{b}=p^{+}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c^{+}_{n}a^{+}_{n}\right)^{*}+p^{-}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c^{-}_{k}a^{-}_{k}+\Psi_{b}

where
\Psi_{b}\in L^{\gamma}(\partial D,\mathbb{B})
,
1<\gamma<\frac{q}{2-q}
. By Theorem
6.9
,

||H(p^{+}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c^{+}_{n}a^{+}_{n}\right)^{*}+p^{-}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c^{-}_{k}a^{-}_{k})||_{L^{p}(\partial D)}\leq C_{at}||p^{+}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c^{+}_{n}a^{+}_{n}\right)^{*}+p^{-}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c^{-}_{k}a^{-}_{k}||_{\mathbb{B},at},

where
C_{at}
is a constant. By Theorem
2.10
,

||H(\Psi_{b})||_{L^{p}(\partial D)}\leq C_{\psi}||\Psi_{b}||_{L^{\gamma}(\partial D)}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle||H(f_{b})||_{L^{p}(\partial D)}

\displaystyle\leq||H(p^{+}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c^{+}_{n}a^{+}_{n}\right)^{*}+p^{-}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c^{-}_{k}a^{-}_{k})||_{L^{p}(\partial D)}+||H(\Psi_{b})||_{L^{p}(\partial D)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{at}||p^{+}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c^{+}_{n}a^{+}_{n}\right)^{*}+p^{-}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c^{-}_{k}a^{-}_{k}||_{\mathbb{B},at}+C_{\psi}||\Psi_{b}||_{L^{\gamma}(\partial D)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(||p^{+}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c^{+}_{n}a^{+}_{n}\right)^{*}+p^{-}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c^{-}_{k}a^{-}_{k}||_{\mathbb{B},at}+||\Psi_{b}||_{L^{\gamma}(\partial D)}\right)

\displaystyle=C||f_{b}||_{\mathbb{B},b},

where
C
is a constant that dominates
C_{at}
and
C_{\Psi}
.

∎

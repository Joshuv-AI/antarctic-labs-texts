Develop a personalised serious game applet for smoking cessation: protocol for enterprise-based cluster randomised controlled trial
Authors: Xiangran Kong, Zean Li, Jinhui Chen, Qingping Yun, Yimeng Mao, Yongjun Chen, Xiaoyu Wang, Wenqi Zhou, Linlei Zhao, Xingjie Wu, Chunyan Yang, Yunting Zheng, Chun Chang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionTobacco use causes approximately 8 million deaths worldwide each year. Against the backdrop of a rapidly expanding game market, there is growing potential to develop tailored gaming interventions for smoking cessation. This study protocol describes the development of a personalised Serious Game Applet based on an integrated TTM–PMT–HAPA framework (Transtheoretical Model, Protection Motivation Theory, Health Action Process Approach) and evaluates its effectiveness compared with conventionalShort Messaging Service (SMS) -based smoking cessation interventions.Methods and analysisDesign: The study comprises two phases. The first involves developing the Serious Game Applet using the TTM–PMT–HAPA theoretical framework. The second phase consists of a multicentre, two-arm, single-blind cluster randomised controlled trial, which will enrol 1320 eligible smokers from various enterprises. Enterprises will be randomised to either the Serious Game Applet group or the SMS push control group. Eligible participants include daily smokers aged 18–45 years, working in non-tobacco-related enterprises, with exhaled carbon monoxide levels ≥6 ppm.Outcomes: Primary outcomes are smoking abstinence rates at 3 and 6 months. Secondary outcomes include abstinence rates at 1, 2 and 9 months; point-prevalence cessation rates at 1, 2, 3, 6 and 9 months; changes in cigarette consumption; stage transitions in smoking cessation; nicotine dependence scores and withdrawal symptoms. The trial will be conducted as a field study targeting smartphone users across three cities in Fujian Province, China.Ethics and disseminationThis study has been approved by the Biomedical Research Ethics Committee of Fujian Medical University (Approval No. 127, 2024). Introduction

Tobacco epidemic in China: an urgent public health crisis

The tobacco epidemic represents one of the most severe public health challenges the world has ever encountered. It is responsible for over 8 million deaths annually,1 damaging nearly every organ in the human body, and kills up to half of its long-term users who fail to quit.2,5 Nicotine, the highly addictive substance in tobacco, is a major risk factor for cardiovascular and respiratory diseases, ranking among the top two causes of global mortality in 2019.6 Despite the immense harm, there are still 1.3 billion tobacco users worldwide, with over 300 million residing in China—the world’s largest producer, consumer and victim of tobacco.7 According to the Global Adult Tobacco Survey (GATS) in 2018,8 26.6% (307.6 million adults) of the Chinese population were current smokers, with a prevalence of 50.5% among men and 2.1% among women. This rate is projected to decrease to 23.7% (45.2% of men and 1.7% of women) by 2030.9 The decline in smoking prevalence is largely attributed to the implementation of comprehensive tobacco control measures. As a signatory of the Framework Convention on Tobacco Control, China has introduced multiple strategies to curb tobacco use, including tobacco tax increases, smoking bans in public places, restrictions on tobacco sales to minors and enhanced public education campaigns. These actions align with the goals set in the ‘Healthy China Initiative’9 and reflect a global trend towards stricter tobacco control. Nevertheless, the persistently high prevalence of smoking among Chinese men remains a major obstacle to further progress. Without the adoption of more aggressive cessation programmes, tobacco-related mortality in China is projected to rise sharply from approximately 1 million deaths in 2010 to 2 million by 2030, and 3 million by 2050, unless more aggressive cessation programmes are introduced.10

Serious games: a promising tool for effective smoking cessation

A 2020 systematic review found that serious games show considerable potential in promoting smoking cessation, with five out of seven included studies reporting positive outcomes. These interventions were associated with improvements in key behavioural determinants such as attitude, self-efficacy, intention, self-expansion and coping skills.11 Inspired by these findings, we aim to develop a Serious Game Applet specifically tailored for smokers. In this study, the term ‘serious games’ is used broadly and not strictly distinguished from related concepts such as gamification and game-based design. That is, serious games are regarded either as a form of gamification intervention or an outcome derived from the gamification process.12 The integration of serious games into digital health interventions could enhance their effectiveness in three essential aspects13: (1) ‘Appealing potential’: The entertaining nature of serious games increases engagement, even among individuals with no initial intention to quit. (2) ‘Engaging potential’: The enjoyable experience promotes higher intervention adherence and improves completion rates. (3) ‘Effectiveness potential’: By offering immersive learning experiences through multisensory stimulation, serious games facilitate and reinforce behavioural learning and change.

Theoretical framework and personalised design enhance game development

A 2023 systematic review and meta-analysis of 28 studies14 demonstrated that incorporating theoretical guidance and personalised design can substantially improve the effectiveness of smoking cessation interventions. Accordingly, the present study will develop a personalised serious game based on an integrated behavioural theoretical framework (Transtheoretical Model, Protection Motivation Theory, Health Action Process Approach, TTM-PMT-HAPA).

The framework integrates the TTM, the PMT, and the HAPA (see figure 1). According to the TTM, the transformation of health behaviour progresses through five stages: preintention, intention, preparation, action and maintenance. Applied to smoking cessation, the transition from intention stage to preparation stage is defined by the smoker’s plan to quit within 1 month. Successful smoking cessation—entering the maintenance stage—is defined as continuous abstinence for at least 6 months. The PMT emphasises motivational factors, including risk perception/threat appraisal (encompassing vulnerability and severity), outcome expectancies (weighing reasons to smoke against reasons to quit) and coping appraisal (evaluating the costs of adaptive behaviour).15 The HAPA posits that after an intention is formed, action and coping plans initiate behavioural change, enabling the individual to overcome perceived obstacles to maintain cessation.16 Additionally, self-efficacy—action efficacy, coping efficacy, recovery efficacy—plays a crucial role across all stages.17 All theoretical dimensions will be quantified using a numerical rating scale. Detailed measurements corresponding to the TTM-PMT-HAPA framework are provided in online supplemental appendix S1.

A cluster randomised controlled trial design

A latest report indicated that China’s game players were primarily concentrated between 18 and 35 years old18 in 2024. According to the GATS,8 the proportion of people aged 15–45 smoking reached approximately 46.1%, with workplace secondhand smoke exposure at 50.9%. This overlap makes the enterprise setting ideal for recruitment. Therefore, we will enrol employees aged 18–45 from various enterprises into the cluster randomised controlled trial.

This trial adopts a cluster randomised controlled design, with enterprises serving as the unit of randomisation. This approach is chosen over individual randomisation for two primary reasons. First, it minimises the risk of contamination, as employees within the same workplace are likely to interact and share information, potentially leading to intervention spill-over between groups. Second, since the intervention incorporates social components, randomising by enterprises helps preserve integrity of group assignment and reinforce the collective mechanisms for behaviour change. Furthermore, this design also supports the implementation of a single-blind protocol by reducing the likelihood of unintentional unblinding through internal communication.

The Serious Game Applet is designed with social functions that allow smoking colleagues within the same enterprise to participate in activities such as playing cessation games together, sharing cessation experiences, tracking and comparing their progress, and providing mutual supervision. These functions are intended to foster a supportive no-smoking atmosphere and leverage group dynamics. Consequently, we will conduct a multicentre, two-arm, single-blind, positive-controlled, cluster randomised controlled trial to evaluate the effectiveness of the Serious Game Applet in promoting smoking cessation.

Methods and analysis

‘Serious Game Applet’ development and personalisation

Serious Game Applet development

The development of the Serious Game Applet is grounded in the Octalysis framework,19 which outlines eight Core Drives for user engagement: Epic Meaning & Calling, Development & Accomplishment, Empowerment of Creativity & Feedback, Ownership & Possession, Social Influence & Relatedness, Scarcity & Impatience, Unpredictability & Curiosity, and Loss & Avoidance. This framework informs the design of gameplay, rules and multi-sensory elements (eg, visuals and sound) to ensure the game is engaging and enjoyable.

Based on this framework, the researcher team will prepare a comprehensive game design document detailing the game concept, mechanics and art direction (see online supplemental appendix S2 for details). Game engineers will then implement the design using Java and Cocos2D. After development, the game will enter an alpha testing stage, during which the development company will perform rigorous functional, performance, compatibility and usability tests to ensure technical stability.

Following alpha testing, a prototype assessment session will be held with game experts, serious game researchers, psychologists, behavioural scientists and smokers. After playing the game, they will evaluate its playability, perceived effectiveness and overall user experience, providing suggestions for improvement. The game will then undergo its first round of iteration based on the feedback.

Subsequently, the game will enter a beta testing phase, which includes external validation. A pilot study will be conducted by recruiting smokers online for an 8-week trial. Participants will complete follow-up surveys every 2 weeks, designed according to the TTM-PMT-HAPA framework. In parallel, a separate panel of experts (who may not be smokers) will also experience the game over 8 weeks and participate in interviews to provide evaluative feedback. Insight from the pilot study and expert reviews will help preliminarily assess the game’s impact and guide its second iteration.

On completion of the iterative development and testing phases, the finalised Serious Game Applet will be deployed in the cluster-randomised controlled trial.

Intervention content library development

A digital repository of smoking cessation materials (text, images, videos) will be established. Contents will be sourced from peer-reviewed literature, major search engines, WHO platforms and verified health-related social media sources. All contents will be screened and refined using the Delphi method to ensure scientific accuracy, clarity and user engagement.

The collected materials will be systematically categorised according to the constructs of the integrated TTM-PMT-HAPA framework. Based on this classification, five targeted intervention packages will be developed, each corresponding to a specific stage of smoking cessation behaviour. In addition, content will be tagged according to relevant demographic characteristics—such as gender, age, socioeconomic status—to support personalised delivery.

To enable stage-matched intervention, users’ smoking cessation behaviour stage will be dynamically assessed both at login and during follow-up evaluations. This assessment will guide the timely and appropriate delivery of relevant intervention content.

This multicentre, cluster randomised controlled trial will establish field test centres in Fuzhou, Quanzhou and Zhangzhou. These three cities host the highest number of enterprises listed in the 2023 ranking of Fujian’s top 100 enterprises, ensuring a representative and robust sample. Eligible enterprises within these cities will be randomly assigned to either the intervention group or the positive control group under a single-blind protocol.

Recruitment

Collaboration with participating enterprises was facilitated through the Disease Prevention and Control Centres of Fujian Province, as well as those of Fuzhou City and Quanzhou City. With enterprise management assistance, a list of employees who smoke will be obtained. To recruit these employees, managers will directly guide or call them to visit the on-site test centre established within the enterprise. Additionally, recruitment posters will be displayed in designated smoking areas on the premises to enhance on-site enrolment.

Procedure

Enterprises that agree to participate and provide informed consent signed by the head of the employee health and safety department will be randomly allocated to either the control group or the intervention group. Eligible smokers within these enterprises who subsequently provide electronic informed consent via the applet will be formally enrolled. Participants will be assigned to their enterprise’s corresponding group in a single-blind design. Those in the intervention group will receive interventions through the Serious Game Applet, while the control group will receive a sequenced set of smoking cessation messages via SMS push. Participants will remain blinded to their group assignment throughout the study. The total intervention period will last 6 months of intervention, divided into two phases:

Intensive intervention (months 1–3)

The intervention group will receive monthly updates to the game theme. Smoking cessation content will be delivered progressively based on game progress and personalised according to changes in the participants’ behavioural stage, demographic profile and theoretical dimension scores. The control group will receive only sequenced informational message via SMS. In both groups, questionnaire follow-ups will be conducted at the end of each month.

Extensive intervention (months 4–6)

No new game updates or Short Messaging Service (SMS) messages will be delivered. However, all previous unlocked content will remain accessible in the applet for review. The final questionnaire follow-up will be conducted at the end of month 6. Additionally, a telephone follow-up will be scheduled at the end of month 9 to confirm participants’ current smoking cessation stages (see figure 3).

Randomisation and group allocation

To minimise baseline differences between groups, a stratified 1:1 matching procedure will be conducted at the enterprise level prior to randomisation. Enterprises will be stratified based on two structural characteristics: geographic location (Fuzhou, Quanzhou or Zhangzhou) and industry type (manufacturing or service). Within each stratum, enterprises will be paired using the nearest-neighbour method based on two covariates: shift schedule (binary: presence or absence of shift work) and gender ratio (continuous: proportion of male employees). Each enterprise will be assigned a unique random identifier to ensure anonymisation and unbiased allocation. Following successful matching, enterprises within each pair will be randomly assigned to either the Serious Game Applet group or the SMS push group. In strata where the number of eligible enterprises is insufficient for pairing (eg, fewer than two enterprises or an odd number), purposive sampling will be employed to recruit additional enterprises with matching stratification characteristics to finalise the pairing process.

To prevent bias in the stratification, matching and randomisation procedures, all allocation steps will be performed by an independent researcher, Dr Lin Haoxiang from the Institute for Global Health Development at Peking University. Dr Lin has extensive expertise in smoking cessation interventions and randomised controlled trials, will execute the pre-specified R software scripts (see online supplemental appendix S4) and will oversee the allocation process through the trial period. Each enterprise will be assigned an access password which will be sealed in an opaque envelope. On the day of intervention initiation, the investigator will unseal the envelope on-site to reveal the password, which will then unlock the corresponding applet function (intervention group) or SMS-only system (control group).

This matching and randomisation strategy is designed to reduce potential confounding from the structural or demographic imbalances. Following data collection, baseline balance between the two groups will be formally assessed: unordered categorical variables will be compared using χ² tests; ordered categorical variables will be compared using Spearman rank correlation; and continuous variables will be evaluated using standardised mean difference (SMD). An SMD value ≤0.2 will be considered indicative of acceptable balance between groups.

Control group and intervention group

All participants will log in to the applet under the guidance of the investigator to complete registration and grant permission for information push notifications. Access to group-specific functionalities is determined by unique codes: the intervention group receives a code that unlocks the full serious game with personalised content delivery, while the control group receives a code that provides sequential, non-gamified SMS push of smoking cessation information. Sociodemographic data collected during the baseline survey will be stored in the user profile to support personalisation. Participants may choose to hide this information to protect their privacy. These characteristics will not be recollected in follow-up surveys.

Control group

The control group will receive all content from the smoking cessation intervention content library over a 3-month period. The sequence of content delivery follows the progression logic defined by the TTM-PMT-HAPA framework without gamification or dynamic personalisation.

Intervention group

On completion of the baseline survey, the intervention group will unlock the Serious Game. Each user will be assigned an initial smoking cessation package corresponding to their baseline behavioural stage, as defined by the TTM model (five packages in total). Subsequently, smoking cessation intervention information will be prioritised based on the user’s demographic characteristics, such as gender, age, income level and family background, as well as their theoretical dimension scores. For example, women will receive content related to the effects of smoking on appearance, while users with lower perceived smoking risk scores will be prioritised for content related to the harms of smoking.

To ensure dynamic intervention, the applet will briefly assess the user’s smoking cessation stage on their first daily login (see figure 4). This allows the intervention package to be updated in real time according to behavioural changes. Users can also provide feedback on each piece of content by selecting ‘like’ or ‘unlike’, enabling the system to further refine personalisation. Cessation content—in the form of text, images and videos—is integrated into gameplay and unlocked through various mechanics. Examples include earning points for correct answers in tobacco knowledge quizzes, watching educational videos to revive after in-game failure, unlocking content on level completion and encountering health messages during storyline progression (see figure 5).

Data collection

Data collection is conducted by investigators using the Serious Game Applet. At the baseline, participants will be instructed by the investigator to take a deep breath, hold it for 20 s, and exhale slowly and completely into a CO detector. The device display will show a rising reading that stabilises at the final result. A reading of ≥6 ppm, indicating smoking within the past 24 hours, qualifies the individual for registration in the applet and subsequent questionnaire completion. Eligible participants will complete the baseline survey within the applet. Investigators will record the CO result and enter it into the system. Each submitted questionnaire will undergo logical validation and quality control, and final submission will require an investigator verification code.

During follow-up periods, participants will directly complete the distributed questionnaires within the app. The CO results will be recorded by the investigators and entered into the system. After logical validation, the completed questionnaire will undergo quality control and can only be submitted once the investigator enters a verification code. Throughout the process, investigators will be present on-site to guide participants, address any queries and ensure quality control, thereby improving the efficiency of data collection and participant engagement.

Study monitoring

Access to both backend game data and follow-up survey data will be restricted exclusively to the designated data analysis personnel for this study. These personnel will perform regular data quality reviews following each data collection phase.

For participants who withdraw from the study, investigators will attempt telephone follow-up to ascertain their smoking status. Where possible, the reason for withdrawal and final smoking outcome will be documented. Participants who become ineligible due to unforeseen circumstances—such as pregnancy or meeting other exclusion criteria—will have their most recent results recorded and will receive appropriate counselling before being withdrawn from the trial.

To enhance participant compliance and ensure complete data collection, a prize pool incentive system has been implemented. After each follow-up survey submission, participants will receive RMB10 as direct compensation and will be awarded one ‘gold coin’ credited to their applet account. Although the study design includes multiple follow-ups beyond the three illustrated in the schematic, the gold coins cannot be redeemed immediately. The current balance of the prize pool is visible to participants throughout the trial.

Only participants who complete all follow-up rounds will become eligible to redeem their accumulated coins, with each coin valued at 5 RMB. Those who withdraw prematurely will forfeit both their coins and eligibility, and their contributions to the prize pool will be redistributed among completers.

This incentive structure is informed by prospect theory, particularly the concept of loss aversion—the tendency for individuals to be more motivated by the prospect of losing an asset than by acquiring an equivalent gain. By delaying redemption and creating a sense of potential loss, the system encourages sustained engagement. The pooled reward structure further incorporates elements of uncertainty and challenge, which are known to enhance participant motivation and retention (see figure 6).

Harm

This study involves recruiting smokers to register for and engage with a serious game applet and complete periodic questionnaires. The primary burdens on participants are the time and effort required for these activities. No physical harm or significant psychological discomfort is anticipated as a result of participation. In recognition of participants’ time contribution, they will receive compensation of 10 RMB following the completion of each follow-up survey.

Data analysis

Data preprocessing and statistical analysis will be performed using R software. Numerical variables will be summarised as mean±SD deviation or median with IQR, based on their distribution. Categorical variables will be expressed as percentages and compared using χ² tests or rank-sum tests as appropriate.

Prior to evaluating intervention effects, baseline balance between the Serious Game Applet group and SMS push group will be assessed across demographic characteristics, tobacco use patterns and pretrial quitting intention.

The primary analysis will adhere to the Intention-to-Treat principle, including all randomised participants in their originally assigned groups regardless of protocol adherence or study completion. This approach maintains the integrity of randomisation, minimises selection bias and provides a conservative estimate of intervention effectiveness by reducing the risk of overestimation due to dropout-related false positives.

Analysis of secondary outcomes

Continuous outcomes—including daily cigarette consumption, nicotine dependence scores, withdrawal symptom scores and exhaled CO levels—will be analysed using generalised estimating equations. Changes in smoking cessation stages will be assessed using repeated-measures analysis of variance.

Game usage data analysis

Game attrition analysis

Attrition patterns will be analysed through a two-stage approach. First, a temporal distribution chart will be constructed to visualise attrition patterns relative to key intervention events. The 3-month intensive intervention period will serve as the primary timeline (in days), with vertical dashed lines indicating significant events such as game updates and follow-up assessments. The daily counts of new attrition starting and ending points will be plotted, supplemented by density curves (yellow for attrition initiation, green for re-engagement) to illustrate distribution patterns.

Subsequently, a continuous-time Markov model will be applied to analyse dynamic transitions between user engagement states, quantifying how baseline characteristics and psychological dimension scores influence transition probabilities over time. The model incorporates three distinct states:

Complete attrition

Users demonstrating no game interaction for seven consecutive days (excluding non-game activities like questionnaire completion) who fail to return before the study concludes. The 7-day threshold aligns with the weekly content unlock schedule, reducing event-related confounding. The extended 3-month observation period mitigates right-censoring bias by capturing potential late-stage attrition.

Temporary attrition

Users with seven consecutive days of non-engagement who subsequently resume interaction during the intervention period.

Active

Users maintaining at least one game interaction within any 7-day window. The analysis will use a daily time scale starting from the quit day. The transition intensity matrix will estimate instantaneous risks between states, with the likelihood ratio test assessing time homogeneity of transition probabilities. If non-homogeneity is detected, time-varying covariates or segmented modelling will be implemented. Prespecified covariates include:

Gender (hypothesis: higher attrition risk in females).

Age (hypothesis: increased attrition with older age).

Smoking cessation motivation (hypothesis: protective effect against attrition).

Tobacco dependence (hypothesis: increased attrition risk).

Self-efficacy (hypothesis: promotes re-engagement).

Outputs will include state transition pathways, state occupancy proportions, maximum likelihood-derived transition intensities, and Wald tests for covariate significance (α=0.05). HRs will be reported, with model adequacy assessed using Cox-Snell residuals.

Dose-response analysis

A generalised additive mixed model will be employed to investigate the potential nonlinear dose-response relationship between average daily gameplay time and sustained smoking abstinence at the 3-month follow-up. The model will treat average daily game time as the primary exposure, modelled using smoothing splines. Their non-linearity will be formally tested based on effective df (edf>1.2) and Wald test results (p<0.05); cubic regression splines will be applied if significant non-linearity is detected.

Covariate adjustment will include gender as a categorical fixed effect, while continuous confounders—such as age, tobacco dependence, smoking cessation motivation and self-efficacy scores—will be incorporated as linear terms. To account for clustering at the enterprise level, a random intercept will be included in the model.

This analysis aims to quantitatively identify the optimal range of daily gameplay duration associated with the highest likelihood of successful smoking cessation.

Analysis of missing data

A follow-up rate exceeding 80% will be considered indicative of relatively complete data, with completeness serving as one metric for assessing overall data quality. To address missing data, the following strategy will be implemented: Traditional penalised imputation (PI) will be applied only if there is no statistically significant difference in withdrawal rates between the Serious Game Applet and SMS push groups, as PI may otherwise overestimate intervention effects.24 If significant differential withdrawal is observed, multiple imputation methods will be employed instead. Additionally, telephone follow-up with dropouts will be conducted to gather information on smoking status and reasons for discontinuation. This information will help characterise the missing data mechanism (random vs non-random) and inform the selection of an appropriate imputation approach. Analysis of the complete-case dataset will be performed as a sensitivity analysis to assess the robustness of the primary findings.

No patients or members of the public were directly involved in the design or planning of this clinical trial protocol. Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this represents the first Serious Game Applet in China that integrates personalised design with theoretical framework guidance, and its effectiveness will be validated through evidence-based practice.

Several methodological considerations warrant mention. First, to ensure the validity of abstinence outcomes, both exhaled CO measurements and self-reported smoking status recorded in the applet will be preserved in their original form for verification of 3-month and 6-month cessation endpoints. Second, multiple strategies are implemented to minimise recall bias. Participants will be instructed to actively document the start and end times of each quit attempt. The applet includes a dedicated quit-record feature to facilitate real-time tracking of cessation progress. Smoking volume will be assessed at each follow-up by asking participants to report their average daily consumption over the preceding week. Third, due to practical constraints, the active intervention and follow-up period is limited to 6 months (3-month intensive followed by 3-month extensive). Consequently, both 3-month and 6-month abstinence rates will be analysed as primary endpoints. Successful cessation is defined as continuous abstinence from the quit day through the 6-month assessment. Finally, to capture longer-term outcomes, a 9-month telephone follow-up will be conducted for participants who entered the action phase during the intensive intervention but delayed their initial quit attempt. This will provide insights into: (1) whether delayed transition to action occurs postintervention; (2) cessation success rates among those who initiated quitting after the designated quit day and (3) relapse patterns among initially successful quitters.

This study has several recognised limitations: (1) The trial evaluates the Serious Game Applet specifically within the 18–45 age group. Consequently, the findings cannot be generalised to older or younger populations. (2) The intervention’s appeal and effectiveness may be limited for individuals with low intrinsic interest in gaming or digital interventions. (3) Due to resource constraints, follow-up assessments are scheduled at key time points rather than monthly, which may not capture more granular temporal dynamics of behaviour change.

While this study is not designed to evaluate the intervention’s effect on smokers already in the action or maintenance stages at baseline, it will provide valuable insights into how the applet facilitates progression from earlier stages towards sustained cessation. A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis of 44 studies (n=17 351) demonstrated that e-health interventions significantly improve smoking cessation rates and long-term maintenance compared with traditional approaches,25 underscoring the continued potential of digital health strategies. Meanwhile, the mobile gaming sector reported a user base exceeding 940 million in 2024, including 750 million mini-programme users,26 suggesting games represent a promising vehicle for health interventions. Through integrated data collection and information technology, serious game applets enable precisely targeted intervention delivery. As an edutainment platform, this approach can be adapted not only for smokers at various motivational stages but also extended to smoking prevention initiatives in broader populations.

Molecular epidemiology and genomic characteristics of clinical Acinetobacter baumannii isolates from patients with hospital-acquired pneumonia in China, 2019–2020: a multicentre retrospective study
Authors: Jintao He, Qingye Xu, Minfei Peng, Haiyang Liu, Li Fang, Siyuan Yang, Yisha Zhang, Hanqi Zhang, Xiaochen Liu, Xi Li, Xiaoting Hua, Yunsong Yu, Hua Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundCarbapenem-resistant Acinetobacter baumannii (CRAB) is a leading cause of hospital-acquired pneumonia (HAP) with high mortality. However, large-scale nationwide data of HAP-causing CRAB in China remain limited.MethodsHere, we performed a nationwide multicentre retrospective study to characterise the molecular epidemiology and genomic features of 802 A. baumannii isolates from patients with HAP across 33 tertiary hospitals in China during 2019–2020. Antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST), whole-genome sequencing (WGS), phylogenetic and comparative genomic analysis were used to investigate molecular epidemiology of HAP-causing CRAB strain. Clinical comparative analyses were carried out on data from 500 patients with HAP stratified by distinct antimicrobial susceptibility and genomic profiles, and a Galleria mellonella infection model was utilised for in vivo virulence assessment.FindingsThe overall carbapenem resistance rate of A. baumannii was 82.0% (658/802), with marked regional variations. CRAB exhibited high resistance to conventional agents but remained largely susceptible to polymyxin, tigecycline, cefiderocol and sulbactam-durlobactam. Among enrolled patients, CRAB infection was linked to substantially higher mortality (39.0% vs. 17.5%), and multivariate analysis confirmed ICU admission and advanced age as independent risk factors for patients with CRAB infection. Molecular typing revealed STPas2 (96.2%) as the absolutely predominant type; STOxf208, STOxf195, STOxf540, and STOxf369 were the most prevalent Oxford sequence types with obvious geographic stratification and divergent comorbidity profiles among corresponding patients. A total of 654 CRAB isolates harboured carbapenemase genes, with blaOXA-23 dominating at 98.8%. Genomic analysis revealed lineage-specific features: STOxf208 carried more virulence genes, while STOxf540 harboured a broader antimicrobial resistance genes (ARGs). The STOxf208 clone mainly belonged to KL2 (62.1%) and KL7 (36.8%) serotypes, with KL2 strains possessing richer ARGs and virulence factors, and in vivo virulence assays further validated that KL2 strains possessed higher pathogenicity than KL7 strains.InterpretationThis study demonstrates the extremely high prevalence and clonal dominance of CRAB in Chinese patients with HAP, providing critical evidence for clinical treatment, antimicrobial stewardship, and targeted infection control.FundingNational Key Research and Development Program of China (2024YFE0106200), National Natural Science Foundation of China (U22A20338, 82502763, W2621007), Zhejiang Provincial Natural Science Foundation of China (LQN25H190006), Zhejiang Provincial Postdoctoral Science Foundation (ZJ2025058).

The higher pathogenic potential of STOxf208-KL2 suggests that KL serotyping can be incorporated into routine surveillance to identify high-risk sublineages. Research in context

Evidence before this study

We conducted a systematic literature search on PubMed up to July 16, 2026, using the keywords “carbapenem-resistant Acinetobacter baumannii”, “hospital-acquired pneumonia”, and “multicentre”. Initially, 28 articles were retrieved. However, after rigorous title and abstract screening against the epidemiology of A. baumannii, no studies were identified that provided sufficient foundational evidence for our current analysis. Furthermore, replacing the “hospital-acquired pneumonia” with “China” in the search strategy yielded 58 publications. Despite this volume, the majority of existing studies focused on A. baumannii strains isolated from bloodstream infections. To date, there remains a critical lack of systematic molecular epidemiological research focusing specifically on carbapenem-resistant A. baumannii (CRAB) isolates from patients with hospital-acquired pneumonia (HAP) in China.

Added value of this study

To our knowledge, this represents the largest nationwide multicentre genomic epidemiological study of CRAB isolates from patients with HAP in China to date, encompassing 802 non-duplicate isolates collected from 33 tertiary hospitals across 25 provinces. We demonstrate an alarmingly high carbapenem resistance rate of 82.0% with substantial regional heterogeneity, and establish the susceptibility profiles of both conventional and novel antimicrobial agents for clinical reference. By systematically linking genomic characteristics (including sequence types, capsular types, resistance determinants, and virulence gene profiles) with phenotypic features and geographic distribution patterns, this study provides a foundational framework for identifying high-risk epidemic clones and hypervirulent sublineages responsible for HAP. Collectively, these findings fill a critical knowledge gap by delivering the systematic molecular epidemiological evidence for HAP-associated CRAB in China.

Implications of all the available evidence

These findings highlight the severe endemicity and clonal expansion of CRAB in patients with HAP across China, emphasising the urgent need for enhanced regional surveillance, targeted infection control, and antimicrobial stewardship. Polymyxin B, tigecycline, cefiderocol, and sulbactam-durlobactam remain active in vitro and should be prioritised for clinical treatment of CRAB-related HAP. The dominance of STPas2/STOxf208 supports clone-specific prevention and control measures. Distinct evolutionary characteristics between STOxf208 and STOxf540 underscore the necessity of lineage-specific monitoring. The higher pathogenic potential of STOxf208-KL2 suggests that KL serotyping can be incorporated into routine surveillance to identify high-risk sublineages. Collectively, these data provide key evidence to optimise empirical therapy, strengthen infection control, and guide future research on diagnostics and interventions against HAP-causing CRAB.

Introduction

Acinetobacter baumannii has emerged as one of the most clinically important opportunistic pathogens due to its remarkable ability to develop resistance to multiple antimicrobial classes.1,2 Carbapenem-resistant A. baumannii (CRAB) isolates exhibit multidrug-resistant phenotypes and the global spread of CRAB has drastically narrowed available therapeutic options, prompting the World Health Organisation (WHO) to designate CRAB as a critical priority pathogen.3,4 CRAB causes a wide spectrum of nosocomial infections, particularly hospital-acquired pneumonia (HAP).5 As a leading infectious complication in hospitalised individuals, HAP is frequently attributed to A. baumannii and places a heavy burden on healthcare systems worldwide.6,7 Approximately 60–87% of HAP-causing A. baumannii isolates exhibit multidrug resistance, and such infections are associated with high morbidity and mortality among critically ill populations.8 Notably, mortality rates surpass exceed 40% in patients with CRAB-related HAP.9,10 Furthermore, HAP is highly prevalent among CRAB-colonised patients, affecting more than half of individuals and is linked to severe in-hospital mortality approaching 80%.11

Molecular epidemiological studies have identified several globally disseminated high-risk clones of CRAB, most notably International Clone 2 (IC2), which is primarily represented by ST2 (Pasteur scheme) and ST208 (Oxford scheme).12 In China, the prevalence of CRAB in hospital settings has risen markedly over the past decade, with notable regional variations in resistance rates and clonal dissemination patterns.13, 14, 15 A recent 10-year study of bloodstream infection (BSI)-causing isolates in China revealed increased genetic diversity and the dominance of the highly virulent STOxf208.13 Despite the escalating threat, comprehensive multicentre studies examining the molecular epidemiology of CRAB isolates from patients with HAP remain limited. Understanding the genetic diversity, resistance mechanisms, and genomic characteristics of CRAB is essential for guiding infection control strategies and informing empirical treatment decisions.

In this study, we conducted a multicentre investigation across 33 tertiary hospitals in 25 provinces and municipalities in China, systematically characteriszing 802 non-duplicate A. baumannii clinical isolates collected from patients diagnosed with HAP between 2019 and 2020. By integrating phenotypic and genomic data, we provide a comprehensive overview of the epidemiological and molecular characteristics of HAP-causing CRAB in China. These findings will contribute to a deeper understanding of CRAB pathogenesis and inform the development of targeted therapeutic and preventive strategies.

Methods

Strain collection and isolation

This multicentre, retrospective study was conducted across 33 tertiary hospitals in 25 provinces and municipalities in China (Supplementary Table S1). Between January 2019 and December 2020, a total of 802 A. baumannii isolates were collected from patients diagnosed with HAP based on clinical symptoms, and patients diagnosed with COVID-19 were not enrolled in this study. HAP refers to pneumonia newly developed ≥48 h after admission without pre-existing infection on admission. All isolates were cultured from respiratory samples comprising sputum and bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF), and identified using matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry (MALDI-TOF MS). To ensure strain uniqueness, duplicate isolates from the same patient were excluded. Clinical-related patient information, including baseline comorbidities, prior antimicrobial exposure, and in-hospital clinical outcomes, was retrospectively abstracted from de-identified electronic hospital medical records. Patient sex/gender data, documented by clinical staff at hospital admission, were also retrieved from these records. Sex was included as one of the demographic variables for comparisons of patient characteristics. Race/ethnicity data were not routinely captured in the source electronic medical records and were therefore unavailable for this study.

Ethics

The study was carried out in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Sir Run Run Shaw Hospital (Approval No. 20190530-4) which waived the need for consent, as the patient information was anonymised and de-identified prior to analysis.

Antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST)

The minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) of the tested antimicrobial agents were determined via broth microdilution using cation-adjusted Mueller-Hinton broth (CAMHB) for meropenem, imipenem, amikacin, ciprofloxacin, cefoperazone-sulbactam (2:1 ratio), polymyxin B, tigecycline, eravacycline and sulbactam-durlobactam. For cefiderocol, iron-depleted CAMHB (ID-CAMHB) was employed. All testing was performed in strict compliance with the Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) guidelines (M100-Ed25, 2025).16 Interpretation of susceptibility breakpoints followed CLSI recommendations for all antimicrobials except tigecycline and eravacycline, which were assessed according to the guidelines of Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for Enterobacterales. For cefoperazone-sulbactam, we applied the ampicillin-sulbactam interpretive criteria for A. baumannii as no specific breakpoints are available. Escherichia coli ATCC 25922 was used as quality control.

String test

The string test was determined as previously described.17 Briefly, a positive string test was defined when a colony could be stretched using an inoculation loop to produce a viscous filament >5 mm in length.

Whole-genome sequencing (WGS) and analysis

The genomic DNA was extracted using QIAamp DNA mini kit (Qiagen, Valencia, USA) following the manufacturer's recommendations, and was sequenced on an Illumina Hiseq platform (Illumina. San Diego, USA) with a 2 × 150 bp paired-end sequencing protocol. The raw sequence reads were analysed using FastQC v0.11.9 and MultiQC v1.0 for quality control.18 The blaNDM-positive strains (AB0614 and AB1801) were subjected to nanopore sequencing and hybrid assembly was performed by unicycler v0.4.8 as previously described.19,20 De novo assembly of Illumina short reads was performed using shovill v0.9.0 (https://github.com/tseemann/shovill), followed by genome annotation via Prokka v1.13.21 The completeness and heterogeneity of genome assemblies were assessed for using CheckM v1.2.2.22 N50, contig number and genome size were calculated using SeqKit v2.12.023 (Supplementary Table S1). Antimicrobial resistance genes (ARGs) and virulence genes were identified using Abricate v0.8 (https://github.com/tseemann/abricate) against the ResFinder database and virulence factors database (VFDB), respectively, with thresholds of >90% nucleotide identity and >80% alignment coverage. Plasmid replicons were identified using the Acinetobacter Typing database.24 MLST was used to determine sequence types with the Pasteur and Oxford typing schemes via the PubMLST public database.25 The K-locus (KL) and lipooligosaccharide outer core (OCL) types were identified via Kaptive v3.0.0.26 The copy number of the blaOXA-23 gene was determined using CCNE v1.1.0.27 The identification of blaOXA-23-associated transposons was initially performed using BLASTn v2.4.0 against reference sequences (Tn2006 EF127491.1, Tn2007 EF059914.1, Tn2008 KP074966.1, Tn2009 CP097879.1) as previously described28; Additional BLAST analyses and manual verification were then implemented relying on subtype-specific characteristic genes of these transposons. The chromosomal or plasmid location of Tn2008-carrying carbapenemase-encoding genes was characterised using MOB-suite v3.1.9.29 The plasmid sequence comparisons were conducted with BLASTn v2.4.0 and visualised using Easyfig v2.2.3.30 Single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in the wzc gene were identified using Snippy v4.4.5 (https://github.com/tseemann/snippy) by aligning sequences to the reference genome AB0102. Core genome multilocus sequence typing (cgMLST) was performed using Chewbbaca v3.4.231 based on the A. baumannii scheme (https://www.cgmlst.org/). The minimum spanning tree was generated from allelic profile using Grapetree v1.5.0,32 with the alleles loci presence threshold of 95%. Core genome alignments generated by Panaroo v1.1.2 were used to construct phylogenetic trees in IQTree v2.0.3,33,34 which were visualised with Chiplot (https://www.chiplot.online/). SNP-distances were calculated using snp-dists v0.6.3 (https://github.com/tseemann/snp-dists). To characterise clonal transmission events of STOxf540, core-genome alignment was performed with Panaroo v1.1.2 on a dataset comprising 544 publicly available global genomes35 and 63 genomes obtained in this study, and transmission networks were visualised using GraphSNP v1.0 (https://graphsnp.beatsonlab.com). The conserved functional domains of the Wzc protein were predicted using the SMART v10 with default parameters.36

Galleria mellonella larval infection model

The G. mellonella infection model was constructed according to a previously described protocol.17 Briefly, bacteria were cultured to logarithmic growth phase, pelleted by centrifugation, washed with sterile PBS, and resuspended to an OD600 of 1.0. The bacterial suspension was serially diluted to a final concentration of 5 × 107 CFU/mL. Each of ten larvae was injected with 10 μL of the standardised bacterial suspension, and subsequently incubated at 37 °C in the dark. Larvae injected with an equivalent volume of sterile PBS were set as combined trauma and vehicle control groups. Larval mortality was monitored at predefined time points throughout the 3 days post-infection.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis of genomic characteristics was performed using the Mann–Whitney U test in GraphPad Prism v10. Survival curves were compared using log-rank tests implemented in GraphPad Prism v10. Statistical analyses of baseline characteristics, underlying comorbidities, prior antimicrobial treatment and clinical outcomes were executed with R v4.4.1. Continuous variables (age) were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and compared via independent-samples t-test. Categorical variables were expressed as counts and percentages, with intergroup comparisons conducted using Pearson's χ2 test or Fisher's exact test when cell counts were less than 5. For clinical outcomes (multicategorical variable), Pearson's χ2 test was performed for the 2 × 4 contingency table. To identify risk factors for CRAB infection, univariate binary logistic regression was first conducted. All variables with a univariate P value < 0.20 were screened and incorporated into the multivariate logistic regression model to adjust for potential confounders. Crude odds ratios (ORs) and adjusted ORs with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated. Complete data separation was observed in trauma/injury and tetracycline exposure variables due to sparse sub-group cases in the non-CRAB group, leading to non-estimable 95% CIs for these predictors in multivariate models. P < 0.05 was defined as statistically significant for all analyses.

Role of funders

The funders had no role in study design, data collection, data analysis, data interpretation, or writing of the report. The corresponding authors had full access to all the data in the study and had final responsibility for the decision to submit for publication.

Results

Collection of A. baumannii isolates and antimicrobial susceptibility profiles

To investigate the diversity and prevalence of A. baumannii in China, we conducted a multicentre retrospective study using sputum or BALF samples from patients with HAP. Between January 2019 and December 2020, we collected 802 non-duplicate A. baumannii clinical isolates from 33 hospitals across 25 provinces and municipalities in China (Supplementary Table S1). The majority of isolates originated from Zhejiang (15.2%, 122/802), Guangdong (8.5%, 68/802), Shanghai (7.0%, 56/802), and Shaanxi (6.9%, 55/802). Among these, 658 isolates were identified as CRAB, yielding an overall resistance rate of 82.0% (658/802). The prevalence of carbapenem resistance varied significantly by region, ranging from 40.9% in Shandong Province to 100% in Henan Province, with resistance rates exceeding 80% in 15 regions and 90% in 5 regions (Fig. 1A). AST was also performed for 802 A. baumannii isolates against amikacin, ciprofloxacin, cefoperazone-sulbactam, polymyxin B, tigecycline, and novel antimicrobials (eravacycline, cefiderocol, and sulbactam-durlobactam). The results demonstrated high resistance rates to amikacin, ciprofloxacin, and cefoperazone-sulbactam among A. baumannii or CRAB isolates. In contrast, resistance to polymyxin B, tigecycline, cefiderocol, and sulbactam-durlobactam remained rare. CRAB isolates exhibited significantly reduced susceptibility to amikacin, ciprofloxacin, cefoperazone-sulbactam, and eravacycline compared to A. baumannii isolates (Table 1; Fig. 1B). MIC distribution analysis of CRAB isolates provided detailed susceptibility profiles, revealing only a small subset with high-level MIC values for polymyxin B, tigecycline, and the novel antimicrobials (Fig. 1C). Clinical profiles of patients with HAP complicated by A. baumannii infection

Owing to missing data for some enrolled patients, statistical analyses were ultimately conducted on 500 patients with HAP, which were further stratified into CRAB group (n = 420) and non-CRAB group (n = 80) according to carbapenem susceptibility of isolated A. baumannii strains (Table 2). The overall cohort had a mean age of 59.35 ± 19.76 years, with 73.4% being male. The most common comorbidities across all subjects were neurological disorders (12.6%), followed by trauma/injury (8.6%), pulmonary diseases (7.8%), malignant neoplasms (7.2%), and BSIs (5.0%). Most underlying comorbidities were evenly distributed between the two groups, whereas trauma/injury exhibited a marginally higher prevalence in CRAB group (9.8% vs. 2.5%; P = 0.0567, Pearson's χ2 test). Notably, Multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS) only occurred in CRAB group (4.0% vs. 0%), though the difference did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.0884, Fisher's exact test). ICU admission was markedly more frequent in the CRAB group (67.1% vs. 32.5%; P < 0.0001, Pearson's χ2 test), representing a prominent intergroup discrepancy. Prior antimicrobial exposure before microbial strain isolation was documented for all patients, with 14 antimicrobial classes recorded. Nearly half of all patients received β-lactams (51.2%) or β-lactamase inhibitor combinations (48.8%), and the usage rates of these two agents were comparable between CRAB and non-CRAB groups. In contrast, patients with CRAB infection displayed significantly higher exposure rates to glycopeptides and tetracyclines. With regard to clinical outcomes, the overall cure and improvement rate exceeded 50%, while 35.6% of all enrolled patients died. Clinical prognoses differed significantly between the two groups (P = 0.0007, Pearson's χ2 test), with markedly higher mortality observed among patients with CRAB (39.0% vs. 17.5%) (Table 2).Table To further identify independent risk factors for CRAB infection, we first performed univariate logistic regression, which filtered six candidate predictors associated with CRAB acquisition (Supplementary Table S2). Epidemiological characteristics of HAP-causing A. baumannii isolates

MLST analysis revealed STPas2 dominated the 658 CRAB isolates (96.2%, 633/658), with only 10 other known STs identified. Conversely, the 144 non-CRAB isolates displayed diverse STPas profiles across 56 types, with STPas40 being the most common (12.5%, 18/144). A total of 31 known STs were identified in CRAB isolates via the Oxford scheme, among which STOxf208 (27.7%, 182/658), STOxf195 (14.0%, 92/658), STOxf540 (9.6%, 63/658), and STOxf369 (9.1%, 60/658) were the most prevalent (Fig. 2A; Supplementary Table S1). Capsular genotyping identified 26 KL types among the CRAB isolates, with the top three being KL2 (17.5%, 115/658), KL3 (14.1%, 93/658), and KL9 (12.9%, 85/658). While different STs generally corresponded to a specific KL type, the dominant clone STOxf208 was clearly associated with two major KL types, KL2 (62.1%, 113/182) and KL7 (36.8%, 67/182), with an additional two isolates belonging to KL120. Phylogenetic relationships of HAP-causing A. baumannii isolates

To clarify the genetic relatedness across all isolates, we first conducted cgMLST analysis on a total of 802 A. baumannii isolates (Fig. 2C). The minimum spanning tree demonstrated that ST lineages belonging to CRAB formed concentrated genetic clusters, while non-CRAB ST strains were widely scattered with extensive allelic divergence between distinct lineages. Focused cgMLST profiling of the 658 CRAB isolates further revealed prominent clustering of major clones with clear discrete sublineages identifiable within the four predominant STs (STOxf208, STOxf195, STOxf540, STOxf369), whereas rare and unassigned ST strains displayed considerable genetic separation from these dominant lineages (Fig. 2D). We subsequently constructed a core-genome SNP (cgSNP) phylogenetic tree on the 658 CRAB isolates. CgSNP-based phylogenetic analysis revealed two distinct clusters corresponding to STPas2 and non-STPas2, with STPas2 strains being closely related and non-STPas2 strains being dispersed. While CRAB strains sharing the same STOxf generally exhibited close phylogenetic relationships, notable exceptions were observed for STOxf218 and STOxf208 (Fig. 3A). Specifically, STOxf218 isolates diverged into two clade based on their KL types (KL7 and KL9). STOxf208 displayed greater complexity, comprising three distinct clade. The largest clade (STOxf208-CL1) contained isolates with three different KL types. The second largest lineage (STOxf208-CL2), consisting entirely of KL7 isolates, was phylogenetically closely related to the STOxf195 lineage. A single isolate (AB1903) formed a distinct, singleton lineage (STOxf208-CL3). CgSNP analysis revealed marked genetic variability among the genomes of the four dominant STs (STOxf208, STOxf195, STOxf540, STOxf369), with pairwise cgSNP differences ranging from 1 to 3759, 1 to 631, 2 to 4158, and 1 to 5017 for each ST, respectively (Fig. 3B). Notably, STOxf195 displayed relatively low genetic divergence compared with the other three STs. Antimicrobial resistance profile and transposons of CRAB isolates

Carbapenemase genes were detected in 654 out of 658 CRAB isolates, corresponding to a prevalence rate of 99.4%. Among these isolates, blaOXA-23 was the predominant genotype, accounting for 98.8% (646/654) of all carbapenemase-positive strains. Notably, one STPas164 strain (AB1801) co-harboured both blaOXA-23 and blaNDM-1. Three blaOXA-23-associated transposons were identified, including Tn2006 (62.8%, 406/646), Tn2008 (2.8%, 18/646), and Tn2009 (34.4%, 222/646). Specifically, STOxf208, STOxf369, STOxf381 and STOxf540 strains carried all three transposon types; STOxf540 strains predominantly harboured Tn2009 (90.5%, 57/63), while all STOxf195 strains exclusively carried Tn2006 (Supplementary Fig. S1). Owing to the short contigs of Tn2006 and Tn2009, only Tn2008 was eligible for location analysis. Combined Mob-suite and BLAST analyses revealed that blaOXA-23 within Tn2008 was plasmid-borne in merely two isolates (AB0215 and AB0216), whereas chromosomal carriage of blaOXA-23 was observed in all remaining Tn2008 carrying strains. Among the remaining eight carbapenemase-producing isolates, six encoded blaOXA-72, one encoded blaOXA-657 (an blaOXA-23 variant), and the remaining isolate (AB0614, STPas338) co-harboured blaNDM-1 and blaOXA-58. Plasmid analysis revealed blaNDM-1 and blaOXA-58 in AB1801 were co-located on a pR4WN-type plasmid with a size of 302,197 bp, which was nearly identical to plasmid W76_pNDM (302,243 bp, 100% identity and coverage), with the next most closely related plasmid being pAba728Dc (99.2% identity, 80% coverage) (Supplementary Fig. S2). Interestingly, the W76 strain (the source of pAbaW76_pNDM) was isolated from the same hospital as AB0614, but differed in isolation source (ascitic fluid specimen) and collection date (2019-10-02). Genetic context analysis showed that the blaOXA-58 gene was located within a conserved core unit (ΔISAba3-blaOXA-58-ISAba3), while the blaNDM-1 was localised in a genetic structure consisting of ISAba125-blaNDM-1-bleMBL-trpF-recombinase.

Furthermore, these CRAB isolates also harboured multiple ARGs conferring resistance to β-lactams, aminoglycosides, chloramphenicols, macrolides, quinolones, sulfonamides, and tetracyclines (Fig. 3A). Besides the intrinsic ARGs ant(3'')-IIa, blaOXA-51-like and blaADC-1-like, high prevalence rates were observed for aph(3'')-Ib (574/658), tet(B) (570/658), msr(E) (552/658), armA (551/658), mph(E) (542/658), blaTEM-12 (464/658) and aph(3')-Ia (448/658). Analysis of ARG distribution across the phylogenetic tree revealed that aac(3)-Ia, aac(6')-Ib, aadA1, catB8, and sul1 were genetically linked, likely co-localising on mobile genetic elements, and were predominantly found within the STOxf540 and KL2 isolates of STOxf208-L1. Additionally, non-STPas2 strains harboured fewer ARGs than STPas2 strains and exhibited no discernible distribution pattern.

Genomic, susceptibility and clinical comparisons of dominant ST isolates

To evaluate the epidemic potential of four major lineages (STOxf208, STOxf195, STOxf540, STOxf369), we assessed their genomic plasticity (genome length, gene number), antimicrobial resistance, and virulence repertoire (Fig. 4A). Overall, the total gene number and genome length of STOxf208 and STOxf540 isolates were significantly larger than those of STOxf195 and STOxf369. Comparison of blaOXA-23 copy numbers among OXA-23-producing isolates of the four STs revealed similar levels across all lineages, with a modest reduction in STOxf540 relative to other three STs. However, STOxf540 carried significantly more ARGs than the other three STs. Virulence factor profiling showed that the average number of virulence-associated genes in STOxf208 isolates exceeded that in the other three STs. Plasmid analysis demonstrated that STOxf540 harboured a relatively higher average plasmid count, whereas STOxf208 exhibited a lower plasmid carriage rate. Collectively, these findings suggest that STOxf540 exhibits greater genomic plasticity and antimicrobial resistance repertoire, while STOxf208 displays enhanced virulence repertoire. To further explore the genetic divergence and transmissibility underlying the high genomic plasticity of STOxf540, we performed cgSNP-based transmission analyses on a total of 607 STOxf540 genomes. With a threshold of 14 pairwise SNPs,37 we recovered 31 distinct transmission events, which comprised one intercontinental transmission event, 19 intra-provincial and 11 inter-provincial dissemination events within China (Supplementary Fig. S3). Only 121 strains were involved in these clonal transmission events, while the remaining 486 isolates (80.1%) showed no close clonal relatedness to other strains. Comparisons of susceptibility rates across the four major ST isolates revealed marked inter-ST differences for amikacin, eravacycline, and sulbactam-durlobactam (Supplementary Table S4). STOxf208 and STOxf540 exhibited markedly lower amikacin resistance proportions relative to the other two STs and the overall resistance rate of all CRAB isolates. The eravacycline susceptibility rate of STOxf195 reached 38.0%, which was significantly higher than those of the other three ST clones and the overall susceptibility of all CRAB isolates. Elevated sulbactam-durlobactam resistance was detected in both STOxf208 and STOxf369, with STOxf369 carrying a high resistance rate of 10.7%. MIC distributions further showed STOxf540 contained more isolates with elevated carbapenem MICs; STOxf208 and STOxf540 had a larger proportion of strains with high eravacycline MICs, whereas STOxf195 and STOxf369 presented more isolates with high cefiderocol MICs. The overall MIC spectrum of sulbactam-durlobactam shifted higher in STOxf369, while STOxf208 displayed a broad MIC distribution encompassing strains with both low and high MIC values (Supplementary Fig. S4).

We further analysed clinical characteristics of patients infected with these dominant ST isolates (Supplementary Table S5). Compared with the patients with other ST CRAB isolates, patients with STOxf208 presented a significantly higher prevalence of BSIs. STOxf195 was associated with elevated proportions of hepatobiliary and pancreatic diseases as well as hypertension, and its patients received more β-lactamase inhibitor combinations and glycopeptides. STOxf540 exhibited a significantly higher incidence of MODS, with greater utilisation of glycopeptides and oxazolidinones. By contrast, STOxf369 had a higher rate of cardiac diseases. No significant difference was detected in the overall distribution of clinical outcomes, yet the all-cause mortality of STOxf195 and STOxf540 exceeded the overall mortality of all CRAB isolates, with STOxf195 reaching 50.0%.

Genomic, virulence and clinical comparisons of STOxf208-KL7 and STOxf208-KL2 isolates

We further compared genomic features between two KL types (KL2 and KL7) within STOxf208. Although STOxf208-KL7 isolates possessed more total genes, larger genome sizes, higher blaOXA-23 copy numbers, and more plasmids than STOxf208-KL2, STOxf208-KL2 carried more ARGs and virulence genes (Fig. 4B). Virulence profiling further showed that KL2 isolates predominantly carry an additional set of 12 virulence-associated genes compared with KL7 strains (Supplementary Fig. S5), all of which reside within capsule biosynthesis operons, including the core Wzx/Wzy-dependent KL capsule synthesis locus (wzx-kpsS-wzy-gtr345 cluster) and the pseudaminic acid modification locus (pse) (Supplementary Table S6). To evaluate the in vivo virulence effects driven by these differentially distributed virulence genes, we employed the G. mellonella infection model and selected four representative STOxf208-KL2 and four STOxf208-KL7 isolates for functional assessment. Survival curve analyses demonstrated that KL2 isolates exhibit stronger virulence compared with KL7 strains. Log-rank tests identified significant differences in larval survival for nearly all pairwise comparisons between KL2 and KL7 strains, with the only non-significant comparison observed between two KL2 isolates (AB2117 and AB1710) and the KL7 strain AB2411. Collectively, our data confirm that STOxf208-KL2 strains possess heightened pathogenic potential relative to STOxf208-KL7 isolates.

We further explored whether this phenotypic divergence translated to distinct clinical outcomes in human hosts. Clinical profiles of patients infected with STOxf208-KL2 and STOxf208-KL7 CRAB isolates revealed that despite the marked differences in virulence between the two KL types, their long-term clinical prognoses remained comparable in this patient population (Supplementary Table S7). Patients infected with STOxf208-KL7 were significantly older and exhibited a markedly higher rate of ICU admission than those carrying STOxf208-KL2 strains, representing the only two statistically significant clinical differences between the two groups. Patients harbouring STOxf208-KL2 isolates also demonstrated higher rates of BSIs, cardiac diseases and MODS relative to patients with STOxf208-KL7 strains, although these disparities lacked statistical significance.

The hypermucoid phenotypes and underlying mechanisms of A. baumannii isolates

We performed string tests on 802 A. baumannii isolates to assess their mucoid morphology. Thirty isolates were classified as hypermucoid, defined as producing a viscous string >5 mm when colonies on an agar plate were stretched. Of these hypermucoid isolates, only 3 were non-CRAB (AB0505, AB2806, AB2811), whereas the remaining 27 were STPas2 CRAB. A total of 9 STOxf were identified among hypermucoid CRAB isolates, with the three most prevalent being STOxf208 (n = 8), STOxf2143 (n = 6), and STOxf368 (n = 4), while all other STOxf CRAB isolates were represented by fewer than two isolates. As the hypermucoid phenotype is predominantly attributed to wzc mutations, we further analysed wzc variants in these CRAB strains. After excluding wzc mutations also detected in non-hypermucoid isolates of the same STOxf, 17 isolates harboured specific wzc mutations. 14 of these 17 isolates bore mutations localised to functional protein domains, six within the polysaccharide chain length determinant N-terminal domain, three in the tyrosine-protein kinase G-rich domain, and five within the AAA domain (Supplementary Table S8). These findings indicate that Wzc mutations are highly likely to mediate the hypermucoid phenotype.

Discussion

In China, respiratory infections represent the leading cause of fever of unknown origin.38 CRAB is a major pathogen responsible for HAP, which is associated with high mortality, and its global spread has become a critical threat to clinical anti-infective therapy.11,39,40 In this multicentre study, we systematically characterised the phenotypic and genomic features of 802 A. baumannii clinical isolates collected from patients with HAP across 33 tertiary hospitals in 25 provinces and municipalities in China during 2019–2020. To our knowledge, this study is the largest HAP-causing A. baumannii isolates genomic epidemiology study ever conducted in China.

Globally, China is one of the major endemic countries for CRAB.41 Hospital-based CRAB surveillance has been implemented in China for more than two decades. Data from the China Antimicrobial Surveillance Network (CHINET) indicated that the resistance rates of A. baumannii strains to meropenem and imipenem were 77.7%/79.0% (2019) and 72.9%/73.4% (2020), respectively, and the pathogen remained consistently susceptible only to polymyxins and tigecycline (https://www.chinets.com). Our findings revealed a remarkably high prevalence of HAP-causing CRAB at 82.0%, which underscores the severe endemic status of CRAB in Chinese hospital settings. Regional variations in CRAB resistance rates, ranging from 40.9% in Shandong Province to 100% in Henan Province, which align with previous reports of regional heterogeneity in CRAB prevalence across China13; this inter-provincial heterogeneity may be attributable to inconsistent enforcement and uneven local implementation of standardised infection prevention and control and hospital antimicrobial stewardship practices among different provincial medical institutions. Despite CRAB's high resistance to conventional antimicrobials, its low resistance to polymyxin B, tigecycline, cefiderocol and sulbactam-durlobactam makes these agents effective therapeutics for CRAB-associated HAP. These findings highlight the great potential of novel antimicrobials and warrant the continuous development of novel agents to prevent the future emergence of drug resistance. Although cefiderocol and sulbactam-durlobactam resistance remained rare in our study, elucidating their underlying resistance mechanisms carries critical clinical significance. We identified five cefiderocol-resistant and 19 sulbactam-durlobactam-resistant isolates, among which multiple clonal transmission events were detected (strains sharing identical STs from the same hospital exhibited minimal SNP distance). The resistance mechanisms of sulbactam-durlobactam-resistant isolates have been thoroughly elucidated in the previous study, where resistance is mainly conferred by PBP mutations or NDM production.20 For all five cefiderocol-resistant isolates, concurrent pirA and piuA mutations and elevated blaOXA-23 copy numbers suggest a synergistic mechanism linking disrupted iron transport and OXA-23 overexpression to reduced cefiderocol susceptibility, though functional validation is still required to confirm the causal relationship (Supplementary Table S9). Continuous longitudinal susceptibility surveillance across geographically distinct hospitals is therefore essential to monitor dissemination of these resistance genotypes and preserve the clinical value of these last-resort anti-CRAB therapies.

Carbapenemase encoding gene detection revealed blaOXA-23 as the predominant resistance determinant (98.8%) in CRAB isolates. No IS element was identified upstream of the intrinsic blaOXA-51-like genes in carbapenemase-negative CRAB isolates, and the exact molecular mechanisms underlying their carbapenem resistance remain to be further elucidated. Distinct transposon types (Tn2006, Tn2008, Tn2009) were strongly associated with particular STs, indicating that transposon-mediated horizontal transfer plays a pivotal role in the initial emergence of blaOXA-23-harbouring CRAB clones, and subsequent clonal expansion of these resistant strains further facilitates their clone-specific dissemination. Notably, only one strain co-harbouring both blaOXA-23 and blaNDM-1 was identified in this study, which belonged to STPas164, a well-documented major high-risk clone carrying these dual carbapenemase genes.14,15 Furthermore, the co-occurrence of blaNDM-1 and blaOXA-58 on a pR4WN-type plasmid in a single isolate, which exhibited high homology to a plasmid recovered from the same hospital, further highlights the potential for horizontal transfer of multiple carbapenemase genes within clinical settings.

Our study revealed a marked clonal dominance of specific high-risk lineages, particularly STPas2, which is the most prevalent clone type reported globally and documented in various databases.42 The Oxford scheme, offering substantially higher discriminatory power than the Pasteur scheme, identifying STOxf208 as the predominant clone, followed by STOxf195, STOxf540 and STOxf369, which are consistent with the dominant clones reported in previous studies.13,43 Geographic distribution analysis further uncovered both clonal specificity (e.g., STOxf195, STOxf540 and STOxf368 dominated in Xizang, Hubei, and Xinjiang respectively), such geographic stratification suggests local clonal expansion, potentially driven by regional antimicrobial consumption patterns, patient transfer routes, and healthcare network structures, which may shape the nationwide epidemiological landscape of CRAB. Subgroup analysis of the four dominant STOxf strains identified significant inter-lineage differences in MIC profiles and susceptibility to amikacin, eravacycline, and sulbactam-durlobactam. STOxf208 and STOxf540 demonstrated lower amikacin resistance rates, STOxf195 presented the highest susceptibility to eravacycline, whereas STOxf208 and STOxf369 exhibited elevated sulbactam-durlobactam resistance. We further compared prior antimicrobial exposure among infected patients and observed distinct antimicrobial administration patterns across lineages; however, no significant correlation was detected between individual antimicrobial exposure history and clonal resistance phenotypes. This indicates that intrinsic genomic characteristics of distinct CRAB lineages may serve as the primary determinant of strain antimicrobial susceptibility.

Genomic profiling of the four predominant STs revealed distinct evolutionary features: STOxf540 exhibited greater genomic plasticity with a richer repertoire of ARGs, whereas STOxf208 harboured more virulence factors. STOxf540 was first identified in China in 2013,44 and its prevalence has since risen across multiple provinces.13,28,45,46 Among BSI-causing A. baumannii isolates, STOxf540 ranked the fourth most prevalent genotype, accounting for 9.03% of all isolates (n = 136), with its proportion increasing from 2.7% in 2014 to 11.44% in 2021.13 This growing prevalence, together with its robust ability to acquire antimicrobial resistance may further compromise clinical antimicrobial therapy, therefore, continuous epidemiological surveillance and intervention strategies targeting STOxf540 are urgently needed to mitigate the associated clinical risks. STOxf208 is recognised as one of the most prevalent and widely distributed CRAB clones in China, and its prevalence has been rising annually both in China and worldwide.13,47 Notably, STOxf208 displays broad epidemic potential accompanied by higher virulence, stronger antimicrobial resistance, and enhanced desiccation tolerance, making it a clone of high clinical concern.13,48,49 In the present study, STOxf208 was identified as the dominant clone across multiple provinces and could be categorised into three serotypes, which were distinctly associated with two major KL types (KL2 and KL7). Furthermore, phylogenetic analysis revealed that STOxf208 diverged into three distinct clades, underscoring the high genetic heterogeneity within this clone and the contribution of capsular polysaccharide (CPS) variation to the phylogenetic evolution of CRAB. Serotypes are determined by CPS, which represent one of the most critical virulence factors in A. baumannii.50 Accordingly, serotype succession within STOxf208 is crucial for the future evolution of its virulence. Our findings further revealed that, among STOxf208 isolates, KL2 isolates encoded a greater repertoire of ARGs and virulence genes, including a unique panel of 12 capsule-associated virulence determinants absent from KL7 strains. Of particular note is the pseudaminic acid modification locus (Pse) within this unique gene set, which mediates surface decoration of bacterial CPS with Pse. This surface glycan moiety has been widely confirmed as a key molecular factor that regulates the virulence of bacteria.51 Multiple studies have demonstrated that Pse enables bacteria to evade host immune clearance and shapes the overall virulence profile of isolates. Surface-displayed Pse restructures the outer capsular glycan layer to avoid phagocytic removal by innate immune cells, thereby increasing the invasive ability and killing capacity of Pse-producing strains. Meanwhile, Pse exhibits broad cross-reactivity as a dominant antigen, which accordingly makes it an attractive candidate for developing vaccines and therapeutic reagents against CRAB.52, 53, 54 Consistent with these genomic inferences, our in vivo virulence assays empirically validated markedly elevated virulence among STOxf208-KL2 isolates. These findings align with a prior study demonstrating that STOxf208-KL2 exhibits higher virulence and greater serum resistance compared with STOxf208-KL7.43 Collectively, these data imply greater pathogenicity of the STOxf208-KL2, underscoring the urgent necessity for targeted surveillance and serotyping of these hypervirulent and multidrug-resistant STOxf208 isolates. As previously documented, attenuated total reflection Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR-ATR) enables rapid, cost-effective differentiation of KL2 isolates via their characteristic spectral fingerprints derived from Pse-rich capsules, whose chemical profiles perfectly correspond to the sugar composition predicted by capsule locus sequences.55 Therefore, this technique is a promising high-throughput primary screening candidate for routine hospital microbiology workflows, and its clinical integration would build a robust technical framework to facilitate continuous monitoring of high-risk STOxf208-KL2 sublineages within clinical settings.

Complementary clinical cohort analyses further link the genomic disparities of CRAB clones to patient outcomes among patients with HAP. ICU admission and advanced age were identified as independent risk factors for CRAB acquisition in patients with HAP, a finding that aligns with the intrinsic environmental adaptability of CRAB to intensive care settings, where prolonged mechanical ventilation, invasive devices, and antimicrobial pressure and other related factors can facilitate CRAB colonisation and infection.56 Despite comparable comorbidities between CRAB and non-CRAB groups, CRAB infection resulted in markedly higher mortality (39.0% vs. 17.5%), suggesting carbapenem resistance as a robust prognostic biomarker for adverse HAP outcomes and underscoring the value of rapid molecular screening for clinical risk stratification. Stratified clinical analysis of patients infected with the four predominant STs further revealed lineage-specific clinical manifestations and antimicrobial exposure patterns, which likely reflect divergent host-pathogen interactions and hospital transmission niches. For example, patients infected with STOxf208 had a significantly higher burden of concurrent BSIs, consistent with its enhanced virulence phenotype, which may facilitate bacterial dissemination beyond the respiratory tract. Despite these lineage-specific clinical signatures, overall mortality did not differ statistically across STs, implying that patient baseline conditions exert stronger impacts on overall survival than strain genotype alone. In addition, we found no significant differences in clinical prognoses between patients infected with STOxf208-KL2 and STOxf208-KL7 isolates. The only measurable clinical distinctions between the two subgroups were older age and higher ICU admission frequency among patients carrying STOxf208-KL7 strains, while trends toward more BSIs, cardiac disease, and MODS in STOxf208-KL2 failed to achieve statistical significance. This mismatch between strain virulence potential and patient survival indicates that host clinical status overrides capsular type variation in determining HAP clinical endpoints. Comprehensive evaluation of high-risk subclones such as STOxf208-KL2 therefore requires integrated evidence from functional animal models and large-scale clinical cohorts.

This study has several limitations. First, this study only enrolled isolates collected during 2019–2020 without long-term longitudinal data to monitor the dynamic evolution of CRAB, and the number of participating hospitals and recovered isolates was unevenly distributed across different regions of China. Second, clinical data were incomplete for some patients. Though the CRAB proportion of the analysable subgroup (84.0%) closely aligned with the overall rate in the full cohort (82.0%), the limited valid sample size constrained more rigorous analyses of risk factors and treatment outcomes for HAP-associated CRAB. Third, functional studies were not performed to validate the causal relationship between genomic variants and phenotype. Future studies should integrate long-term clinical and genomic data, and conduct functional experiments to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying CRAB virulence and resistance. Despite these limitations, our findings advance our understanding of CRAB molecular epidemiology and pathogenesis in China, and provide critical evidence for guiding clinical empirical treatment, antimicrobial stewardship, and infection control strategies.

In conclusion, this nationwide multicentre study presents a comprehensive characterisation of the epidemiological, molecular and clinical features of CRAB isolates from patients with HAP in China between 2019 and 2020. Our findings confirm an alarmingly high prevalence of CRAB (82.0%) in Chinese hospitals, with STPas2/STOxf208 as the dominant epidemic clone and blaOXA-23 as the primary carbapenem resistance determinant. Novel antimicrobials (cefiderocol, sulbactam-durlobactam) and polymyxin B retain high in vitro activity against CRAB and thus represent the preferred therapeutic options. Genomic analyses reveal distinct evolutionary adaptations among the dominant STs, with STOxf208 exhibiting enhanced virulence and STOxf540 possessing a broader ARG repertoire, and the STOxf208-KL2 has higher pathogenic potential. Collectively, our findings establish a critical foundation for the rational use of antimicrobials and nationwide surveillance of CRAB clonal distribution in China.

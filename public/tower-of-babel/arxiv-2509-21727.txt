Large deviation estimates for quasi-periodic Gevrey cocycles
Matthew Powell

Abstract
. In this note we use an approximation scheme to establish large deviations for quasi-periodic Gevrey cocycles. As an application, we obtain continuity in the cocycle for the Lyapunov exponent.



The purpose of this note is to present a method for obtaining large deviations, and thus continuity, for Lyapunov exponents for quasi-periodic cocycles with Gevrey entries.

We say
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
is
s
-Gevrey (denoted
G^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if, for all multi-indexes
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n},
there exists
C=C(f)
such that

\left\|D^{\alpha}f\right\|_{\infty}\leq C^{|\alpha|+1}|\alpha!|^{s}.

When
s=1,
these are the analytic funtions. Since
G^{s},s>1
contains non-analytic functions, we cannot use analytic methods to study them directly.

An
s
-Gevrey quasiperiodic cocycle is a function
A:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to SL(2,\mathbb{C})
whose matrix elements are in the class
G^{s}.
For a fixed frequency
\omega\in\mathbb{T}^{d},
we define the cocycle iterates via

A_{N}(x):=\prod_{j=N-1}^{0}A(x+j\omega).

We may define the Lyapunov exponent of such a cocycle in the usual way:

L_{N}(A,\omega):=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\ln\left\|A_{N}(x)\right\|dx.

Our goal is to understand the continuity of
L_{N}(A,\omega)
in
A
and/or
\omega.
Following ideas introduced by Bourgain and his coauthors, the first major step to such an understanding is a large deviation estimate. Since the argument used by Bourgain explicitly uses properties of
analytic
cocycles, we will need to adjust our approach slightly.

Let us recall the large deviation for analytic cocycles due to Bourgain
[
1
]
and extended to the case of general
2\times 2
matrices (c.f.
[
3
]
and
[
6
]
). For simplicity, we will formulate the result for invertible matrices only, since that is the setting of this note.

Theorem 1

(Theorem 3.1 from
[
6
]
)
.

Let
A
be an analytic
GL(2,\mathbb{C})
cocycle such that
L_{N}(A,x,\omega):=\frac{1}{N}\ln\left\|A_{N}(x)\right\|
has a 1-bounded plurisubharmonic extension to the complex strip
\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:|\Im z_{j}|<\rho\right\}.
Moreover, suppose
\omega\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
is such that

\left\|k\cdot\omega\right\|>\delta

for all
0<|k|\leq K_{0}
and

N\geq K_{0}\delta^{-1}.

Then
L_{N}(A,x,\omega)
satisfies a large deviation estimate: for some
c=c(d)\in(0,1]

\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}:\left|L_{N}(A,x,\omega)-L_{N}(A,\omega)\right|>\rho^{-1}K_{0}^{-c}\right\}\right|\leq e^{-\rho K_{0}^{c}}.

Our goal here is to establish a suitably weak analogue of the above estimate for quasi-periodic Gevrey cocycles. In fact, we will prove the following quantitative large deviation estimate.

Theorem 2

(Locally uniform Large Deviation for Diophantine frequency and restricted Gevrey class)
.

Let
A
be an
s
-Gevrey
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
cocycle with
s<1+\frac{c(d)}{1+b}
and suppose
\omega\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
is such that

\left\|k\cdot\omega\right\|>\kappa|k|^{-b}

for all
0<|k|\leq K_{0}
. Then for

N\sim K_{0}^{1+b}

the finite-scale Lyapunov exponent,
L_{N}(A,x,\omega)
, satisfies a large deviation estimate: for some
c=c(d)\in(0,1]
and
C=C(A)<\infty,

\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}:\left|L_{N}(A,x,\omega)-L_{N}(A,\omega)\right|>2C^{1/s}N^{(s-1)-\frac{c}{1+b}}\right\}\right|\leq e^{-\frac{1}{2}C^{-1/s}N^{(1-s)+\frac{c}{1+b}}}.

Moreover, the constant
C(A)
depends continuously on the cocycle
A.

Remark 1
.

Note that when
s=1
this is precisely the analytic version of the LDT.

Remark 2
.

We can replace
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
with
GL(2,\mathbb{C})
in this theorem with a small technical argument.

Remark 3
.

Since we are restricting our attention to
s
-Gevrey
SL(2\mathbb{C})
cocycles, we lose nothing by using
\log_{+}=\max(\log,0)
in place of
\log.
Throughout the rest of this paper,
\log
will mean
\log_{+}.
We make this choice to avoid a technical argument needed to control
\|A_{N}^{m)}\|
from below.

In order to prove Theorem
2
, we will argue via approximation. The idea of approximating Gevrey functions by suitable analytic functions is fairly standard, and has been used by many authors to extend analytic results to the Gevrey classes (c.f.
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
). Our approach shares some thematic similarities with a method used by S. Klein to obtain localization
[
4
]
, but we will need to make substantial changes to overcome a technical obstacle which will allow us to consider a wider class of cocycles, and reduce the assumptions.

1.
An approximation scheme

In brief, let us denote by
A^{(m)}
the analytic cocycle with entries given by a truncated Fourier series

(A^{(m)})_{ij}(x)=\sum_{|k|\leq m}\widehat{A_{ij}}(k)e^{ik\cdot x}.

Then for any
\rho=\rho(m)
such that
L_{N}(A^{(m)},x,\omega)
has a 1-bounded plurisubharmonic extension to the complex strip
\mathbb{C}_{\rho}^{d},
the analytic large deviation estimate holds. By a standard telescoping argument, we have

\left|L_{N}(A,x,\omega)-L_{N}(A^{(m)},x,\omega)\right|\leq\frac{1}{N}C^{N}\left\|A-A^{(m)}\right\|_{\infty},

where
C=C(A).
Combining this estimate with the analytic large deviation, we obtain, under the same assumptions as the analytic large deviation estimate, and using
\rho=\rho(m),

\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}:\left|L_{N}(A,x,\omega)-L_{N}(A,\omega)\right|>\rho^{-1}K_{0}^{-c}-2NC^{N}\left\|A-A^{(m)}\right\|_{\infty}\right\}\right|\leq e^{-\rho K_{0}^{c}}.

This would be a “good” estimate if

\rho^{-1}K_{0}^{-c}-2NC^{N}\left\|A-A^{(m)}\right\|_{\infty}\geq\frac{1}{2}\rho^{-1}K_{0}^{-c},

which will require us to find optimal choices of
m
and
\rho(m).
These choices will depend on
N,
which is the source of the major difficulty we will face.

1.1.
An optimal choice of
\rho

We will begin by finding an allowable range of
\rho
which ensures that the 1-boundedness condition holds for
A^{(m)}.
Let
g\in G^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
such that
|g(x)|\geq 1
(as a stand-in for the
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
condition), and set
g_{m}(x)=\sum_{|k|\leq m}\hat{g}(k)e^{ik\cdot x}.
Since
G^{s}
is characterized by a bound on the growth of the derivatives, we can use the Fourier transform to generate an equivalent characterization in terms of Fourier coefficients:
f\in G^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
if, for some
C=C(f),

\hat{f}(k)\leq e^{-C^{-1/s}|k|^{1/s}}.

Thus

\left|g(x)-g_{m}(x)\right|\leq e^{-C^{-1/s}m^{1/s}}.

Moreover,
g_{m}
is entire, so we can estimate
u_{m}(x)=\frac{1}{N}\ln|\prod_{j=1}^{N}g_{m}(z+j\omega)|
on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
via

(1)

\displaystyle|u_{m}(z)|

\displaystyle\leq\log\left|\sum_{|k|\leq m}\hat{g}(k)e^{ik\cdot z}\right|

(2)

\displaystyle\leq\log\left(\sum_{|k|\leq m}|\hat{g}(k)|e^{|k\cdot\Im z|}\right)

(3)

\displaystyle\leq\log\left(\sum_{|k|\leq m}e^{|k||\Im z|-C^{-1/s}|k|^{1/s}}\right).

In order for this to be 1-bounded, we see that it is sufficient for
|\Im z|
to satisfy

|\Im z|\leq\frac{1}{2}C^{-1/s}m^{1/s-1}.

Hence, we will take

\rho(m)\leq\frac{1}{2}C^{-1/s}m^{1/s-1}.

1.2.
An optimal choice of
m

Now we turn our attention to the degree of our approximation,
m.
With
\rho(m)
in hand, it remains to find
m
such that

\rho^{-1}K_{0}^{-c}-2NC^{N}\left\|A-A^{(m)}\right\|_{\infty}\geq\frac{1}{2}\rho^{-1}K_{0}^{-c}.

Observe that

\left\|A-A^{(m)}\right\|_{\infty}\leq e^{-C^{-1/s}m^{1/s}}.

From this, we see that taking
m\geq CN^{s},

N>N_{0}(C)
sufficiently large, and
C=C(A)
some sufficiently large constant is sufficient.

1.3.
An initial LDT

Combining our choice of
\rho(m)
and
m,
we obtain the following large deviation estimate for
G^{s}
cocycles.

Lemma 1

(Large deviation for generic frequency and arbitrary Gevrey class)
.

Let
A
be an
s
-Gevrey
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
cocycle. Moreover, suppose
\omega\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
is such that

\left\|k\cdot\omega\right\|>\delta

for all
0<|k|\leq K_{0}
and

N\geq K_{0}\delta^{-1}.

Then
L_{N}(A,x,\omega)
satisfies a large deviation estimate: for some
c=c(d)\in(0,1]

\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}:\left|L_{N}(A,x,\omega)-L_{N}(A,\omega)\right|>2C^{1/s}N^{s-1}K_{0}^{-c}\right\}\right|\leq e^{-\frac{1}{2}C^{-1/s}N^{1-s}K_{0}^{c}}.

Note that when
s=1
this is precisely the analytic version of the LDT. One immediately sees a problem, however, when
s>1.
In this case,
N^{s-1}K_{0}^{-c}
will typically be large, which means the estimate is quite trivial and of no practical use.

How might we rescue this estimate? S. Klein used an approach due to Bourgain, Goldstein, and Schlag to show that, under a strong Diophantine assumption, it is possible to improve this estimate inductively for Schrödinger cocycles with sufficiently large coupling constant and
G^{s}
potentials which satisfy a transversality condition. This approach is restricted to Schrödinger-like cocycles due to the coupling constant requirement, and the use of a transversality condition is, in some sense, unnatural as a prerequisite for a large deviation estimate. That approach is, however, very apt if one wishes to obtain localization as in
[
4
]
where transversality is the Gevrey analogue of non-constant for analytic functions, which is essential for localization. In brief, the large coupling and transversality condition in
[
4
]
allowed Klein to establish an effective large deviation of the above type at some fixed initial scale
N_{0}.
These properties then allowed Klein to run an induction scheme in the spirit of
[
2
]
to boost the large deviation to arbitrary scale.

We propose a different approach. Instead of restricting our attention to Schrödinger cocycles and appealing to techniques from
[
2
]
, we will instead restrict the allowable range of the Gevrey exponent
s
, which will allow us to avoid both the induction scheme and transversality condition.

Let us observe that, if
\omega
satisfies a Diophantine assumption of the form

\left\|k\cdot\omega\right\|\geq\kappa|k|^{-b};\quad 0<|k|

then we may rewrite this estimate for
K_{0}\sim N^{1/(1+b)}:

\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}:\left|L_{N}(A,x,\omega)-L_{N}(A,\omega)\right|>2C^{1/s}N^{s-1}N^{-c/(1+b)}\right\}\right|\leq e^{-\frac{1}{2}C^{-1/s}N^{1-s}N^{c/(1+b)}}.

It is now clear that taking
s=1+\epsilon
for any
\epsilon<c/(1+b)
ensures that this is a good enough large deviation for us to work with. Hence we arrive at the following more useful estimate for so-called restricted Diophantine frequencies (that is, frequencies which satisfy a Diophantine condition up to some finite scale
K_{0}
).

Lemma 2

(Locally uniform Large Deviation for (restricted) Diophantine frequency and restricted Gevrey class)
.

Let
A
be an
s
-Gevrey
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
cocycle with
s<1+\frac{c(d)}{1+b}
and suppose
\omega\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
is such that

\left\|k\cdot\omega\right\|>\kappa|k|^{-b}

for all
0<|k|\leq K_{0}
. Then for

N\sim K_{0}^{1+b}

the finite-scale Lyapunov exponent,
L_{N}(A,x,\omega)
, satisfies a large deviation estimate: for some
c=c(d)\in(0,1]
and
C=C(A)<\infty,

\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}:\left|L_{N}(A,x,\omega)-L_{N}(A,\omega)\right|>2C^{1/s}N^{(s-1)-\frac{c}{1+b}}\right\}\right|\leq e^{-\frac{1}{2}C^{-1/s}N^{(1-s)+\frac{c}{1+b}}}.

Moreover, the constant
C(A)
depends continuously on the cocycle
A.

Theorem
2
now follows immediately from Lemma
2
with
\omega
satisfying an unrestricted Diophantine condition:
\left\|k\cdot\omega\right\|>\kappa|k|^{-b}
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\backslash\{0\}.

2.
Application

It was shown by Bourgain
[
1
]
(see also
[
3
]
and
[
6
]
) that, once one has a locally uniform large deviation of the above type for Diophantine frequencies, one also has continuity of the associated Lyapunov exponent. We thus obtain the following as an immediate corollary of the above.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
\omega\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
satisfies

\left\|k\cdot\omega\right\|\geq C|k|^{-b}.

Then
L(A,\omega)
is a continuous function of
A
in the
G^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
topology for any
1\leq s<1+c/(1+b).

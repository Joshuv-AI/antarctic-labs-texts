Surveillance for adverse events following immunization in Hebei Province, China, 2018-2020
Authors: Li Sun, Shifan Wang, Yafei Wang, Yihan Wang, Jinghui Wang, Jing Li, Xiaomeng Xu, Junmian Zhang, Yanli Cong, Yu Guo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe aim of this study was to analyze the incidence and characteristics of adverse events following immunization (AEFI) with post-licensure vaccines used in Hebei province from 2018 to 2020 and to evaluate the safety of vaccines. All information of AEFI was gained from national adverse event following immunization surveillance system (NAEFISS) in Hebei Province from 2018 to 2020. Descriptive epidemiology method was used to analyze the data about AEFI in Hebei province. Reporting rates of AEFI were calculated by sex, age, city, categories of AEFI, severity of AEFI, reaction categories, etc. A total of 35,999 AEFI were reported through NAEFISS, and the average annual rate was 47.64/100,000 doses. The reporting rates of common adverse reactions and rare adverse reactions were 46.37/100,000 doses and 1.05/100,000 doses. The male-to-female ratio was 1.26:1. Most of the AEFI were concentrated in the ≤1 year age group and were reported in the second quarter and third quarter. The majority of AEFI were reported to be recovered or improved, and about 62% of the AEFI occurred within 24 hours after vaccination. Vaccines associated with the highest reporting rate of AEFI were diphtheria, tetanus and acellular pertussis combined vaccine (DTaP, 170.45/100,000 doses). The reporting rate of allergic rash was found to be the highest in the adverse reactions (0.29/100,000 doses). The majority of AEFI cases were common adverse reactions, while serious rare adverse reactions caused by vaccines were extremely uncommon, and all vaccines used in Hebei Province were safe.

Introduction

Vaccination is considered as one of the fundamental components of public health policies for preventing or controlling the infectious diseases.1 Vaccines can prevent vaccinated population from infection and illness, and also protect entire communities by reducing their spread.2,3 However, the adverse reactions of vaccines have raised concerns. With the diversification of new media, self-media and other forms of communication, a wide variety of adverse events following immunization (AEFI) have gradually gained the public’s attention. Continuous assessment of the safety of the post-licensure vaccines can evaluate the benefit-risk profiles of a specific vaccine that cannot be discovered in pre-licensure clinical trials due to sample size limitations.4 The public trust in vaccines can also be strengthened by monitoring AEFI. Surveillance of AEFI helps us to monitor the safety of vaccination programs and thus contributes to validating the vaccination program.5 China’s Ministry of Health (MOH) has established a nation-wide AEFI surveillance since 2005, with the technical support of WHO and the experience from other countries.6 The national AEFI surveillance system (NAEFISS) was a passively collected spontaneous database, which was established in 2008 and was upgraded in 2012.

Hebei Province, located in the north of China, is a developed region with an approximate 80 million people. Of the total population, 4.84% children were under 5 years of age, 7.31% were 5–9 years old, 7.07% were 10–14 years old, and 80.78% were above 15 years of age. Every year, more than 20 million doses vaccines were administered. Since 2002, Hebei Province has been the first province in China to implement the monitoring of AEFI. In 2005, a two-year pilot project of AEFI surveillance was conducted in Hebei Province supported by WHO, providing experience for the establishment of NAEFISS. NAEFISS has been continually used in Hebei Province since then.

The aim of this study was to analyze the data of AEFI cases of all post-licensure vaccines used in Hebei Province from 2018 to 2020 to gain a comprehensive view of the implementation of preventive vaccination work and the characteristics of AEFI occurrence in the province.

Materials and methods

AEFI reporting and investigation procedures

The AEFI should be reported mandatorily when it was detected by authorized reporters, including healthcare facility staff, CDC at any administrative levels, adverse drug reaction monitoring organizations and vaccine manufacturers executive staff. In addition, the public or the caregivers could notify any of the above authorized reporters to report an AEFI. The AEFI cases were gathered by local, county-level CDCs, which were charged with completing AEFI case reporting cards and submitting data to the NAEFISS.

Each reported AEFI should be processed and investigated by higher-level administrative departments. The relevant details should be reported with the NAEFISS online in the form of an AEFI case reporting card, which contains the following information: date of report, age and sex of patient, information of suspected vaccine(s), signs and symptoms of the patient, time interval after immunization, duration of the event, final outcome of AEFI, and any other additional remarks based on a standardized reporting form. Expert panel, which was composed of independent experts from clinical medicine, epidemiology, laboratory practices, pharmacy, vaccinology, vaccine regulation, and other relevant fields, was organized to review and make the classification to the reported AEFI.

The classification of AEFI

AEFI are classified into vaccine product-related reaction (includes common vaccine reactions and rare vaccine reactions), vaccine quality incident, program error, coincidental event and psychogenic reaction. According to severity, AEFI needs to be categorized into severe AEFI and non-severe AEFI. Non-severe (mild and common) AEFI refers to cases that generally do not require hospitalization. Severe AEFI refers to cases that include death, life-threatening, requiring hospitalization or prolongation of already hospitalized treatment, persistent or significant physical disability/disability, congenital anomalies, or birth defects, as well as situations in which the conditions listed above are likely to occur without intervention or treatment. Each AEFI record needs to be followed up by the county-level CDC staff and mark the outcome of patients as cured, improved, required treatment, condition deteriorates, sequelae or death.

Data collection

Data for AEFI reported in Hebei Province during the 3-year period, from January 2018 to December 2020, was collected through the NAEFISS. The yearly number of distributed doses of various vaccines in Hebei Province during the study period was obtained from China Immunization Programming Information Management System and Vaccination Data Sheet.

Immunization program

In China, vaccines are categorized as immunization program vaccines (free) and non-immunization program vaccines (self-financed). National immunization program vaccines include BCG, HepB, bOPV, IPV, DTaP, DT, MMR, JE-L/JE-I, MPSV-A, MPSV-AC and HepA-L/HepA-I for children of the appropriate age; hemorrhagic fever vaccine (HF), anthrax vaccine (AnthV) and leptospira vaccine (Lep) for emergency vaccinations. Non-immunization program vaccines are voluntary and self-funded and are administered in strict accordance with vaccine instructions. Recipients or their guardians may choose on their own to receive non-immunization program vaccines containing components of national immunization program vaccines instead of immunization plan vaccines. Non-vaccination program vaccines can be inoculated at different or the same clinic. Vaccines monitored and detailed vaccination procedures of immunization program vaccines are shown in Supplementary Data.

Statistics analysis

A database was organized as an Excel file (Microsoft Office Excel 2010). The reporting rate of an AEFI for a vaccine (100,000 doses) = the number of AEFI reports/the number of vaccinations × 100,000 doses.

Results

The baseline characteristic of AEFI cases

From 2018 to 2020, a total of 35,999 AEFI cases were reported, the reporting rate was 47.64/100,000 doses. The number of AEFI and the reporting rate were generally on a downward trend, with 13,622 cases of AEFI (58.24/100,000 doses) reported in 2018, 12,627 cases of AEFI (49.74/100,000 doses) reported in 2019, and 9,750 cases of AEFI (35.27/100,000 doses) reported in 2020.

In all AEFI cases, there were 35,040 (97.336%) common vaccine reactions, 791 (2.197%) rare vaccine reactions, 145 (0.403%) coincidental events, 9 (0.025%) psychogenic reactions and 14 (0.039%) cases could not be classified. No vaccine quality incident or program error was reported. The reporting rates of common vaccine reactions and rare vaccine reactions were 46.37/100,000 doses and 1.05/100,000 doses, respectively. There were 35,792 (47.37/100,000 doses) non-severe AEFI cases and 207 (0.27/100,000 doses) sever AEFI cases reported after vaccination.

Discussion

During the last decade, rising concerns about vaccine and immunization safety have emerged among the medical community as well as the public. AEFI directly affect the vaccination rate of the social population and the public’s confidence in vaccination.7,8 Surveillance of AEFI enables us to monitor the safety of vaccination programs and thereby contribute to validating the vaccination program.

The total number of reported cases and incidence of AEFI in Hebei province decreased from 13,622 cases (54.31/100,000 doses) in 2018 to 9,750 cases (35.83/100,000 doses) in 2020. There was an increase in 2018 (54.31/100,000 doses) compared with 2017 (50.00/100,000 doses), which may be due to the fact that the province has carried out remedial vaccination for DTaP with substandard potency assessment. Also in 2018, Changchun Changsheng biotechnology company’s illegal production of RabV incident occurred, the state conducted re-vaccination of RabV. In addition, China adjusted the immunization program of polio vaccine and measles component-containing vaccine in 2020, resulting in shortage of MMR and IPV and the number of doses was decreased. Under the impact of COVID-19 outbreaks and emergency use of the COVID-19 vaccine, China government focused more on the outbreak control, and the promotion and vaccination of COVID-19 vaccine, the rollout of the other vaccines has been affected resulting in the average annual number and the reporting rate of AEFI decreased. The same situation existed in Gansu Province and Chongqing Municipality.9,10 Also the reporting rate of AEFI in Australia in 2020 (14.9/100,000 populations) decreased comparing with 2018 (16.9/100,000 population).11,12 Among the AEFI cases, common vaccine reactions were predominant, followed by rare vaccine reactions, which was similar to the result during 2013 and 2017 in Hebei province.13

There were more cases in male than in female, which may be related to the proportion of births in the natural population. AEFI were primarily reported in infants <1 year of age, which may raise parental anxiety about vaccine safety resulting in vaccine hesitancy and distrust of healthcare products and healthcare providers. So caregivers need to inform guardians or vaccinees about relevant information before vaccination to ease their fear, also the government needs to properly popularize knowledge of AEFI and eliminate rumors. More AEFI reported in the second quarter, which is consistent with the results reported in Guangxi Province.14 On one side, this may be due to the increased exposure of the vaccination site during the season with the light clothing of the population, which increases the likelihood of detecting a localized reaction. On the other side, owing to the high incidence of diseases such as pneumonia, influenza and hand-foot-mouth disease during this season, the uptake of related vaccinations increased. There were obvious differences in the regional distribution of AEFI in Hebei Province, with more cases reported in Cangzhou, which may be related to the larger population and the higher sensitivity in carrying out AEFI surveillance work than other cities. Rare vaccine reactions were dominated by allergic reactions, and most were non-serious cases, which was in line with characteristics of Changsha.15 More than 62% of AEFI occurred within 1 d after vaccination, in which 90.23% of common vaccine reactions and 72.44% of rare vaccine reactions were reported within 1 d after vaccination. This suggests that caregivers should inform vaccinees or guardians to pay close attention to the occurrence of AEFI within 1 d after vaccination and emphasize the necessity of 30-minute on-site observation after vaccination. More than 95% of AEFI cases were cured or improved, and less than 1% had sequelae or death, which was similar to Shanxi Province.16

The vaccine with the highest reporting rate of AEFI was DTaP which was higher than the national level of 2018–2020 (100.66–116.28/100,000 doses),17–19 the result of Hubei Province (48.61/100,000 doses),20 and mainly were common vaccine reactions. On the one hand, it may be due to the fact that DTaP contains aluminum hydroxide adjuvant and other substances that are difficult to be absorbed, which leads to redness, swelling, hardness and other mild reactions.21 On the other hand, with the same vaccine repeated multiple injections, the incidence of adverse reactions increases as the amount of antigen increases.22 Followed by IPV-Sabin, of which the reporting rate was higher than the level reported in the post-marketing safety evaluation (69.33/100,000 doses), but lower than that in Jilin Province (168.91/100,000 doses).23,24 The vaccine with the highest reporting rate of rare vaccine reactions was MR (3.96/100,000 doses), which was higher than that reported by Liang Xuefeng et al. (28.16/100,000 doses).25 There were 60.05% of the rare vaccine reactions diagnosed as allergic reactions, which remind caregivers to pay attention to them, and the units must be equipped with first aid medicines. During the study time, there were no vaccine associated paralytic poliomyelitis (VAPP) case reported in Hebei Province, which thanks to the implementation of the polio vaccine immunization strategy of the first dose of IPV.

The limitation of this study may include the following aspects. Firstly, the actual number of inoculants and the reported number of inoculants may not be completely consistent. In addition to obtaining the report of the number of inoculants from the system, we verified the actual number of inoculants from the aspects of birth population and vaccination rate to ensure the accuracy of the number of inoculants as far as possible. Secondly, differences in AEFI monitoring reports, disease diagnosis abilities and investigation abilities among different regions definitely exist. We do our best to ensure that all monitors have standardized training and are able to guarantee the accuracy of the data. Thirdly, as a passive reporting system, the number of mild AEFI may be easily neglected by healthcare workers and/or vaccinees to report resulting in underestimation of AEFI incidence rate. In future studies, we will use a combination of active and passive surveillance to collect data whenever possible.

Conclusion

The AEFI cases reported in Hebei Province from 2018 to 2020 were mainly common vaccine reactions. Rare vaccine reactions were comparatively uncommon, with allergic skin rash being the major one. The reporting rate of severe rare vaccine reactions was extremely low. To conclude, the vaccines used in Hebei Province were safe.

Outcomes of endoscopic transsphenoidal pituitary surgery in patients with Cushing’s disease: A retrospective review
Authors: Vartika Gupta, Pankaj Gupta
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background: Cushing’s disease (CD) is a rare endocrine disorder associated with significant morbidity and increased mortality if left untreated. Endoscopic transsphenoidal surgery (ETSS) is the preferred first-line treatment; however, reported remission and recurrence rates vary widely due to differences in biochemical remission criteria and follow-up duration. This study aimed to evaluate surgical outcomes, remission rates using standard and strict biochemical criteria, recurrence, and postoperative complications following ETSS for CD at a tertiary referral center.Methods: This retrospective analysis included 82 patients with confirmed CD who underwent ETSS between January 2015 and July 2025. Diagnosis was established using standard biochemical tests, with inferior petrosal sinus sampling where clinically indicated. Postoperative remission was assessed using two criteria: (1) standard remission – early morning serum cortisol <138 nmol/L within 7 days of surgery and (2) strict remission – postoperative day-3 serum cortisol <50 nmol/L. Patients with persistent hypercortisolism were managed with early repeat ETSS, radiotherapy, or medical therapy. Postoperative complications, endocrine deficits, and recurrence were analyzed.Results: The cohort comprised 58 females and 24 males, with a median age of 33.6 years (range 14–71). Preoperative MRI identified microadenomas in 64 patients and macroadenomas in 18 patients. Using standard criteria, remission after initial ETSS was achieved in 70 of 82 patients (85.4%), increasing to 90.2% (74/82) after inclusion of early repeat ETSS. Using strict criteria, early biochemical remission was observed in 56 patients (68.3%) following initial surgery. Persistent hypercortisolism was noted in 12 patients (14.6%), of whom six underwent early repeat ETSS, achieving remission in 66% by standard criteria. Transient diabetes insipidus (DI) occurred in 26.8% and permanent DI in 9.8% of patients. No cases of cerebrospinal fluid leak, meningitis, vascular injury, or postoperative visual deterioration were observed. During short-term follow-up, recurrence was documented in two patients, both with macroadenomas. Median follow-up duration was 4 months.Conclusion: ETSS provides high remission rates with minimal surgical morbidity in patients with CD. Remission rates are significantly influenced by the biochemical criteria applied, with higher rates observed using standard Endocrine Society definitions compared to stricter postoperative cortisol thresholds. Microadenomas demonstrate superior surgical outcomes. While early biochemical remission rates were high, the relatively short median follow-up limits assessment of long-term recurrence and durability of remission, underscoring the need for extended surveillance.

INTRODUCTION

Cushing’s disease (CD) is a rare endocrine disorder, and comprehensive epidemiological data are limited. The estimated prevalence is approximately 40 cases/million population, with an annual incidence ranging from 1.2 to 2.4 cases/million.[7,24] Untreated CD is associated with significant systemic morbidity, including hypertension, osteoporosis, insulin resistance, dyslipidemia, and a hypercoagulable state,[30] resulting in increased mortality.

Endoscopic transsphenoidal surgery (ETSS) provides an effective therapeutic option with the potential for disease remission in patients with CD. However, long-term postoperative follow-up is essential due to the substantial risk of disease recurrence.[24] Since its introduction in 1997,[15] selective resection of adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH)-secreting pituitary adenomas using the endoscopic transsphenoidal approach has become the preferred first-line treatment for CD. The principal objectives of surgery are the achievement of biochemical remission, durable disease control, and reduction of procedure-related morbidity. Several factors influence surgical outcomes, including tumor size, cavernous sinus invasion, preoperative magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) characteristics, perioperative ACTH and cortisol levels, and intraoperative tumor visualization.[27]

Reported remission and recurrence rates following ETSS for CD vary widely across studies. This variability is largely attributable to differences in remission criteria,[27] small patient cohorts, and limited duration of follow-up in some series.[8] According to the 2015 Endocrine Society Clinical Practice Guidelines, early postoperative remission is defined as a morning serum cortisol level <138 nmol/L (5 μg/dL) within 7 days following transsphenoidal surgery (TSS).[23] A more stringent postoperative day-3 cortisol cutoff of <50 nmol/L (1.8 μg/dL), initially described in pediatric populations,[32] has also been incorporated into the same guideline.[23] These biochemical thresholds have been shown to correlate with higher long-term remission rates and a recurrence risk of <10% at 10 years.[32]

Despite the widespread adoption of ETSS for CD, considerable heterogeneity persists in the biochemical criteria used to define postoperative remission. These differences substantially influence reported surgical success rates and complicate comparisons across studies. While the Endocrine Society guideline recommends an early postoperative morning serum cortisol level <138 nmol/L within 7 days as a definition of remission, several investigators have proposed stricter thresholds such as postoperative day-3 cortisol <50 nmol/L to better predict long-term disease control. However, the practical implications of applying these different criteria in routine clinical practice remain insufficiently characterized.

The present study therefore aims to evaluate surgical outcomes following ETSS for CD in a large institutional cohort, with particular emphasis on how remission rates differ when standard guideline-based criteria are compared with stricter early postoperative cortisol thresholds. By applying both definitions within the same patient population, this study seeks to clarify how remission criteria influence the interpretation of surgical outcomes and postoperative management decisions.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design

This study represents a retrospective analysis of prospectively maintained data from patients with CD who underwent surgery at the Department of Neurosurgery of a tertiary care center. All patients were treated using an image-guided endoscopic transsphenoidal approach. Relevant clinical and biochemical parameters were collected over a 10.5-year period (126 months), spanning from January 2015 to July 2025, and postoperative follow-up data were systematically reviewed.

This study was designed and reported in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology guidelines for retrospective observational studies.

The diagnostic pathway was applied sequentially according to Endocrine Society recommendations. Initial screening tests were followed by confirmation of ACTH-dependent hypercortisolism. Pituitary MRI was subsequently performed in all patients. IPSS was selectively employed when confirmation of pituitary ACTH secretion or lesion lateralization was required.

Surgical procedure

All ETSS were performed by three senior neurosurgeons with subspecialty expertise in endoscopic anterior skull base and pituitary surgery. A standard binostril endoscopic transsphenoidal approach was employed in all cases. Selective adenomectomy was undertaken in patients with adenomas identified on preoperative MRI. Histopathological examination of surgical specimens, including immunohistochemical staining for pituitary hormones, was performed to confirm the diagnosis of ACTH-secreting adenoma or corticotroph hyperplasia.

Postoperative assessment

All patients received empirical oral hydrocortisone starting on postoperative day 1 and on the morning of postoperative day 2. Early morning serum cortisol levels were assessed on postoperative day 3. Blood sampling was performed at 8:00 am on postoperative day 3 in clinically stable patients, before the administration of hydrocortisone.

According to the Endocrine Society Clinical Practice Guidelines, postoperative biochemical remission is defined as a morning serum cortisol level <138 nmol/L (5 μg/dL) within 7 days following TSS.[23] This criterion was considered the standard remission criterion. In addition, a more stringent institutional cutoff of serum cortisol <50 nmol/L (1.8 μg/dL) on postoperative day 3 was applied to provide an early indication of biochemical remission (strict criterion).[23]

In patients with postoperative day 3 serum cortisol levels between 50 and 138 nmol/L, daily serial cortisol measurements were performed to assess for a declining trend. Clinical improvement in manifestations of hypercortisolism, such as better glycemic control or blood pressure reduction, was also evaluated before considering repeat ETSS.[6,32]

Transient cranial diabetes insipidus (DI) was defined as the occurrence of hypotonic polyuria in the postoperative period requiring at least one dose of desmopressin, with complete resolution before hospital discharge.[29] Permanent DI was diagnosed using a water deprivation test according to established criteria.[13] Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) deficiency was diagnosed in patients with low free thyroxine levels accompanied by low or inappropriately normal TSH levels. Growth hormone deficiency was assessed using either the glucagon stimulation test or the insulin tolerance test.[14] Gonadotropin deficiency was defined in premenopausal women by amenorrhea with inappropriately low follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and luteinizing hormone (LH) levels and in postmenopausal patients by inappropriately low FSH and LH concentrations.

Recovery of the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis was evaluated at 3 months postoperatively using either the insulin tolerance test or the short synacthen test (250 μg). In patients with borderline or failed results, reassessment was performed every 3–6 months. Subsequently, patients underwent annual evaluation for disease recurrence. Recurrence was defined as failure to suppress serum cortisol to <50 nmol/L following a 1 mg ONDST, in combination with elevated late-night salivary cortisol or increased UFC levels, in patients not receiving hydrocortisone therapy.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are expressed as median (range) and categorical variables as number (percentage). Categorical comparisons were performed using Fisher’s exact test.

Exploratory univariable analyses were performed to assess associations between clinical variables and outcomes, including tumor size, cavernous sinus invasion, preoperative metyrapone therapy, and operating surgeon. Multivariable modeling was not performed due to limited event numbers and risk of overfitting.

Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. Analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics software

RESULTS

Demographics

A total of 88 endoscopic transsphenoidal procedures were performed in 82 patients during the study period. The median age of the cohort was 33.6 years (range: 14–71 years). Of the 82 patients, 58 were female and 24 were male. The median duration of symptoms before diagnosis was 39 months (range: 6–84 months).

Type 2 diabetes mellitus was present in 60 patients (73%), while 78 patients (95%) had hypertension at presentation. The distribution of clinical features observed in patients with CD is summarized in Table 1.

Preoperative imaging and IPSS

Preoperative MRI demonstrated a discrete pituitary lesion in all included patients, with lesion localization guided by dynamic contrast-enhanced sequences and confirmed by IPSS where indicated [Figure 2]. Of these, 64 patients had microadenomas and 18 had macroadenomas, including four cases with cavernous sinus invasion. IPSS was performed in 70 patients (85.4%) to confirm a pituitary source of ACTH secretion and to aid lateralization when imaging findings were small or potentially ambiguous. Although all patients demonstrated a visible lesion on dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI, many lesions were small microadenomas (<6 mm), in which incidental pituitary lesions may occur in the general population. In such cases, IPSS was performed to confirm central ACTH secretion and exclude ectopic ACTH syndrome before surgery.

In patients with larger macroadenomas and clear radiological correlation with biochemical findings, surgery was performed directly without IPSS. Thus, IPSS was primarily used in cases with small lesions, equivocal radiological localization, or when additional confirmation of pituitary ACTH hypersecretion was considered clinically necessary by the multidisciplinary endocrine team.

Postoperative remission

For clarity, remission outcomes are reported separately for initial ETSS and after inclusion of patients undergoing early repeat ETSS. Denominators therefore vary depending on whether analyses include only initial surgery or exclude patients proceeding to early reoperation.

Postoperative outcomes were analyzed using both standard and strict biochemical criteria.

Using the standard remission criteria (8:00 am postoperative day 3 serum cortisol <138 nmol/L within 7 postoperative days, along with clinical improvement in features of hypercortisolism), remission after initial surgery was achieved in 70 of 82 patients (85.4%). When patients with macroadenomas were excluded, the remission rate was 90.6% (58/64).

Persistent disease

Persistent hypercortisolism following initial ETSS was observed in 12 patients (14.6%). Among these, six patients underwent early repeat endoscopic surgery. Remission following repeat ETSS was achieved in 66% (4/6) of patients using the standard criteria and in 33% (2/6) using the strict criteria.

Of the remaining patients with persistent disease after repeat surgery, two patients underwent stereotactic radiosurgery, while the other two patients were managed with medical therapy.

Postoperative complications

Following initial ETSS, 22 patients (26.8%) developed transient DI, while eight patients (9.75%) developed permanent DI. Permanent and transient DI occurred more frequently in patients with macroadenomas and cavernous sinus invasion, reflecting greater surgical manipulation and stalk proximity in these cases. New-onset postoperative TSH deficiency was observed in 12 patients (14.6%), and gonadotropin deficiency occurred in six premenopausal female patients (7.3%) [Figure 5 and Table 3].

Permanent and transient DI occurred significantly more frequently in patients with macroadenomas, likely reflecting increased surgical manipulation and proximity to the pituitary stalk. Similarly, new-onset anterior pituitary hormone deficiencies were more common in macroadenomas, consistent with more extensive resections and suprasellar extension.

Sellar reconstruction was performed using a multilayer technique with autologous fat graft and fascia lata when indicated. Intraoperative Valsalva maneuver was routinely performed to assess cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) leakage. Postoperatively, patients were monitored clinically for rhinorrhea and signs of meningitis during hospitalization and follow-up. CSF leak was defined as clinically evident rhinorrhea confirmed biochemically or radiologically.

No cases of postoperative CSF leak, meningitis, visual deterioration, or vascular injury were observed.

Recurrence

All patients were available for postoperative follow-up. The median follow-up duration was 4 months, reflecting the inclusion of several recently operated patients in the cohort. Consequently, the present analysis primarily reflects early postoperative biochemical outcomes rather than the durability of long-term remission.

During this period, two patients developed recurrence of CD.

Both patients had macroadenomas on preoperative MRI. Postoperative day 3 serum cortisol levels after initial ETSS were 71 nmol/L, satisfying the standard remission criterion but not the strict criterion. One patient underwent repeat ETSS at 9 months, during which no visible tumor was identified and no tissue was removed. Postoperative day 3 serum cortisol was 308 nmol/L, and medical therapy with metyrapone was initiated.

Given the median follow-up duration of only 4 months, these findings reflect early postoperative outcomes and do not allow reliable estimation of long-term recurrence

DISCUSSION

The principal objective of this study was not only to report institutional surgical outcomes but also to illustrate how remission rates vary depending on the biochemical definition applied. When the Endocrine Society criterion was used, remission rates appeared substantially higher than when a stricter postoperative day-3 cortisol threshold was applied. This difference highlights the potential for variability in reported outcomes across studies and underscores the importance of clearly specifying remission criteria when interpreting surgical success in CD.

The findings of the present study highlight an important methodological issue in the interpretation of surgical outcomes in CD. Variability in biochemical remission definitions can substantially influence reported success rates across institutions, potentially leading to misleading comparisons between surgical series. By applying both standard guideline-based and stricter early postoperative cortisol thresholds within the same cohort, our results demonstrate how remission estimates may vary despite identical surgical management. These observations emphasize the importance of standardized remission criteria when comparing outcomes across studies and when counseling patients regarding expected surgical success.

Reported remission rates following ETSS for CD show considerable variability, largely attributable to heterogeneity in the criteria used to define remission.[10] At present, there is no universally accepted definition of remission, with most centers relying on a combination of clinical improvement and biochemical markers. This lack of uniformity significantly limits meaningful comparison of surgical outcomes across studies.

Chronic hypercortisolemia in CD leads to suppression of normal pituitary corticotroph function. Consequently, successful excision of an ACTH-secreting adenoma should result in a prompt postoperative decline in serum cortisol and ACTH levels. The Endocrine Society Clinical Practice Guideline recommends an early morning serum cortisol level of <138 nmol/L within 7 days of surgery as an indicator of remission,[23] a criterion adopted by several large outcome studies.[3,10,31,34] In contrast, more stringent definitions have been proposed, including a postoperative day-3 serum cortisol level of <50 nmol/L to indicate early biochemical remission.[5,10,20,33,35] Studies employing this stricter cutoff have demonstrated lower long-term recurrence rates – reported to be <10% at 10 years – along with higher durability of remission.[6]

At our institution, a strict protocol is followed. Patients with postoperative day-3 serum cortisol values between 50 and 138 nmol/L undergo daily serial cortisol monitoring to confirm a downward trend, alongside assessment of clinical improvement in features of hypercortisolemia, before considering early repeat ETSS. Ensuring that serum cortisol reaches its nadir before initiating additional intervention is critical to avoid premature decision-making.

In the present study, remission rates were reported using both widely accepted criteria. When remission was defined according to Endocrine Society guidelines – including patients who underwent early repeat ETSS – the remission rate was 90.2%, comparable to results from other large series.[9,10,19,26,34] However, when the stricter criterion of postoperative day-3 serum cortisol <50 nmol/L was applied, the remission rate decreased to 68.3%. While this stringent cutoff allows early identification of patients suitable for discharge as early as postoperative day 3, reliance on this parameter alone may underestimate true remission when compared with more lenient definitions.[23]

Higher postoperative cortisol levels have been consistently associated with an increased risk of disease recurrence. Mayberg et al., in a retrospective cohort of 81 CD patients undergoing ETSS, demonstrated significantly higher recurrence rates in patients with nadir cortisol levels between 58 and 149 nmol/L compared to those with cortisol <55 nmol/L (33% vs. 6%, p = 0.01).[19] In our series, recurrence was observed in 2 patients (2.4%), who had a corticotroph macroadenoma – a known risk factor for recurrence.[12] Notably, these patients’ postoperative cortisol levels fell within the intermediate range between the two remission criteria and, based on strict criteria, represented persistent hypercortisolism rather than true recurrence. These cases underscore the difficulty in clearly distinguishing persistent disease from early recurrence, a distinction often influenced by institutional protocols.

Although our recurrence rate compares favorably with previously reported rates of up to 22%,[23] longer follow-up is essential for accurate estimation. Definitions of long-term recurrence also vary considerably across studies. A large systematic review by Petersenn et al., encompassing over 6,400 studies, demonstrated lower reported recurrence rates when UFC combined with ONDST was used, compared to UFC alone or UFC with early morning serum cortisol.[27] These findings further emphasize the need for standardized biochemical criteria for defining remission and recurrence.

Centripetal obesity remains the most common clinical feature of CD, though it lacks specificity and has limited discriminatory value. The Endocrine Society recommends screening for CD in patients with central obesity accompanied by features of protein catabolism; young individuals with osteoporosis or hypertension; children with declining height percentile and increasing weight; patients with adrenal incidentalomas; and those presenting with obesity, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, acne, and hirsutism.[32] The clinical characteristics of our cohort are summarized in Table 1.

The postoperative surgical complication rate in our study was notably low, with no cases of vascular injury, CSF leak, meningitis, or visual deterioration. Previous reports have documented CSF leak rates ranging from 0% to 7.2% and meningitis rates up to 7.9%, with CSF leakage being a major predisposing factor for postoperative meningitis.[2,11,17,26,28] Although some studies suggest a higher risk of carotid artery injury with the endoscopic approach compared to microscopic surgery – attributed to extended lateral exposure[4] – we observed no carotid artery injuries in our series of 88 ETSS procedures, consistent with several reports documenting zero morbidity and mortality related to carotid injury.[2,21]

Postoperative visual deterioration is another feared complication due to its profound impact on quality of life. Reported rates following TSS for CD range from 0.86% to 1.9%.[17,28] Risk factors include large tumor size, preexisting visual impairment, and advanced age.[18,22,25] No cases of postoperative visual deterioration were observed in our cohort.

While surgical complications were minimal, endocrine complications – particularly DI – were comparable to rates reported in the literature. Permanent DI occurred in 9.8% of patients, while transient DI was observed in 26.8%. The relatively higher incidence of transient DI may be attributed to our diagnostic criteria, wherein transient DI was defined as even a single episode of hypotonic polyuria with normal or elevated serum sodium requiring at least one dose of desmopressin. In contrast, some studies define transient DI as polyuria lasting <2 days,[1] while others require documented hypernatremia for diagnosis.[16] Such stringent definitions may underestimate transient DI, explaining the lower pooled rate of 11.3% reported in a 2018 meta-analysis.[26]

The permanent DI rate warrants particular attention, as ETSS for CD is recognized to carry a higher risk of postoperative DI.[1,22] This may reflect a more aggressive surgical approach aimed at maximizing remission. Although routine long-term reassessment for DI resolution was not consistently performed beyond the initial 3-month postoperative period, spontaneous resolution has been reported in some cases.[1,16] Nevertheless, the observed rates highlight the importance of thorough preoperative counseling regarding the risk of long-term endocrine deficits. CD surgery is associated with a higher risk of postoperative DI due to aggressive exploration aimed at achieving biochemical remission, particularly in microadenomas without clear intraoperative tumor margins. The higher incidence of DI and anterior pituitary hormone deficiencies in macroadenomas likely reflects increased surgical aggressiveness and stalk proximity, consistent with previously reported series.

Strengths and limitations

A key strength of this study is the use of two widely accepted remission criteria, allowing meaningful comparison with existing literature. In addition, all surgeries were performed by three experienced pituitary surgeons, minimizing variability related to surgical expertise. Limitations include the relatively small sample size and the retrospective design. Furthermore, incomplete long-term follow-up data restricted a comprehensive assessment of recurrence, underscoring the need for standardized longitudinal surveillance protocols. The short median follow-up of 4 months precludes meaningful assessment of long-term recurrence, which in CD often occurs several years after apparent remission.

CONCLUSION

ETSS remains an effective first-line treatment for CD, achieving high early remission rates with low surgical morbidity in our institutional experience. However, remission estimates varied substantially depending on the biochemical criteria applied. Use of stricter postoperative cortisol thresholds identified fewer patients meeting early remission criteria, highlighting the influence of biochemical definitions on reported surgical outcomes.

The relatively short median follow-up in the present study limits assessment of long-term recurrence and durability of remission. Future studies with extended longitudinal follow-up are required to better define the prognostic value of early postoperative cortisol thresholds and their relationship to long-term disease control.

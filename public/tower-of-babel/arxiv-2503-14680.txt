Moment of Derivatives of Quadratic Twists of Modular L L -functions
Zijie Zhou

Abstract
. We prove an asymptotic for the moment of derivatives of quadratic twists of two distinct modular L L -functions. This was previously known conditionally on GRH by the work of Ian Petrow [ 7 ] .



1.
Introduction

Moments of
L
-functions are central objects in the study of analytic number theory. Motivated by the Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer (BSD) conjecture, we want to understand the behavior of the modular
L
-functions at the central value. Analytic methods have been used successfully to study the behavior of the central value in some families of modular
L
-functions; one particular interesting family is that of quadratic twists of modular
L
-functions. According to the BSD conjecture, moments of quadratic twists of modular
L
-functions at the central value can provide crucial information about the distribution of ranks of associated twists of elliptic curves.

Before stating our main result, we introduce some notation and recall some standard facts. Let
f
be a Hecke new eigenform for the group
\Gamma_{0}(q)
of even weight
\kappa
with trivial central character. It has Fourier expansion

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{n}\lambda_{f}(n)n^{(\kappa-1)/2}\exp(2\pi inz).

The
L
-function associated with
f
is given by

\displaystyle L(s,f):=\sum_{n}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)}{n^{s}}=\prod_{p}\Bigg(1-\frac{\lambda_{f}(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{1}{p^{2s}}\Bigg)^{-1},

for
\text{Re}(s)>1
. Here, the Hecke eigenvalues of
f
are all real, and hence
f
is self-dual. Let
d
be a fundamental discriminant coprime to
q
and let
\chi_{d}(\cdot)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{d}{\cdot}}}\right)
be a primitive quadratic character of conductor
|d|
. Then
f\otimes\chi_{d}
is a newform on
\Gamma(q|d|^{2})
and the twisted
L
-function is defined by

\displaystyle L(s,f\otimes\chi_{d}):=\sum_{n}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)\chi_{d}(n)}{n^{s}}=\prod_{p\nmid qd}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{f}(p)\chi_{d}(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{1}{p^{2s}}}}\right)^{-1}\prod_{p\mid q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{f}(p)\chi_{d}(p)}{p^{s}}}}\right)^{-1},

for
\text{Re}(s)>1
. It satisfies the functional equation

\Lambda(s,f\otimes\chi_{d})=i^{\kappa}\eta\chi_{d}(-q)\Lambda(1-s,f\otimes\chi_{d})

with

\Lambda(s,f\otimes\chi_{d}):=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{|d|\sqrt{q}}{2\pi}}}\right)^{s}\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(s+\frac{\kappa-1}{2}}}\right)L(s,f\otimes\chi_{d}).

Here,
\eta
is given by the eigenvalues of the Fricke involution, which is independent of
d
and always
\pm 1
. We also denote the root number by
\omega(f\otimes\chi_{d}):=i^{\kappa}\eta\chi_{d}(-q)
.

When
\omega(f\otimes\chi_{d})=1
, an asymptotic formula for the second moment of
L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})
was computed assuming the Generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH) by Soundararajan and Young
[
9
]
, which was proved unconditionally in Li’s recent work
[
6
]
. Here, we apply their techniques to our problem when the sign of the functional equation is
-1
and the derivative
L^{\prime}(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})
is the correct object to study instead. For
\text{Re}(s)>1
, the derivative of the twisted
L
-function is

L^{\prime}(s,f\otimes\chi_{d}):=-\sum_{n}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)\chi_{d}(n)\log(n)}{n^{s}}.

It also has the functional equation

\Lambda^{\prime}(s,f\otimes\chi_{d})=-i^{\kappa}\eta\chi_{d}(-q)\Lambda^{\prime}(1-s,f\otimes\chi_{d}).

Based on Li’s work
[
6
]
, Kumar, Mallesham, Sharma and Singh
[
5
]
proved the asymptotic of second moment of
L^{\prime}(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})
unconditionally, which was previously shown by Petrow
[
7
, Theorem 1]
assuming GRH. In this paper, we study the moment of the derivatives of quadratic twists of two distinct modular
L
-functions, which was shown by Petrow
[
7
, Theorem 2]
assuming GRH.

We let
\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{*}}
denote a sum over squarefree integers and
\sideset{}{{}^{d}}{\sum}_{N}
denote a dyadic sum over
N=2^{\ell}
for integer
\ell\geq 1
. For convenience, we restrict the modulus to be of the form
8d
where
d
is odd and squarefree. Now we state the main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f,g
be distinct normalized cuspidal Hecke newforms with trivial central character, odd levels
q_{1},q_{2}
and even weights
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}
(we let
Q=q_{1}q_{2}
). Let
F
be a smooth, nonnegative function compactly supported on
[1/2,2]
, we have

(1.1)

\displaystyle\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{*}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2Q)=1\\
\omega(f\otimes\chi_{8d})=-1\\
\omega(g\otimes\chi_{8d})=-1\end{subarray}}L^{\prime}(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d})L^{\prime}(1/2,g\otimes\chi_{8d})F\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{8d}{X}}}\right)=C_{0}X(\log X)^{2}+C^{\prime}X\log X+O(X(\log\log X)^{6}).

In the above, we have

\displaystyle C_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\check{F}(0)L(1,\text{sym}^{2}f)L(1,f\otimes g)L(1,\text{sym}^{2}g)Z^{*}(0,0)}{2\pi^{2}},

and
C^{\prime}
is some explicit constant that depends on
f,g
and
F
. Here,
L(s,f\otimes g)
is the Rankin-Selberg convolution of two modular forms
f,g
and
Z^{*}(u,v)
is a holomorphic function (
5.3
) defined in
\text{Re}(u),\text{Re}(v)\geq-1/4+\epsilon
, depending on
f
and
g
, given by a sum of four absolutely convergent Euler products and uniformly bounded in
u,v
where it converges, and
\check{F}
is a Fourier-type transform of
F
defined as in (
3.1
).

Remark 1
.

The combination of work of Kolyvagin
[
4
]
, Gross and Zagier
[
2
]
, and others, implies that if the analytic rank of an elliptic curve is equal to one, then so is the rank of that elliptic curve. By the modularity theorem, any elliptic curve
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
of conductor
N
, there is a weight
2
, level
N
Hecke eigenform
f
with
L(s,E)=L(s,f)
, where
L(s,E)
is the Hasse-Weil
L
-function of the elliptic curve
E
. Our result then implies that for any fixed elliptic curves
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
over
\mathbb{Q}
such that
L(s,E_{1})=L(s,f)
and
L(s,E_{2})=L(s,g)
, there exist infinitely many fundamental discriminants
d
such that the twists of the elliptic curves
E_{1}(d)
and
E_{2}(d)
are exactly of rank
1
.

Independently, S. Choi, B. Kim, H. Kim, H. Kim and W. Lee claimed the following in their work
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]

\displaystyle\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{*}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2Q)=1\\
\omega(f\otimes\chi_{8d})=-1\\
\omega(g\otimes\chi_{8d})=-1\end{subarray}}L^{\prime}(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d})L^{\prime}(1/2,g\otimes\chi_{8d})F\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{8d}{X}}}\right)=C_{0}X(\log X)^{2}+O(X(\log X)^{2}(\log\log X)^{-A}).

for any
0<A<2
.
However, there appears to be unresolved issues in their proof of
[
1
, Proposition 6.1]
. Specifically, the condition on
A

[
1
, p.28]
in the first range should give the bound of
X(\log X)^{2+\epsilon}
at the end; and the number of dyadic intervals from the second range
[
1
, p.29]
is about
\log X
which implies that the contribution of the second range is also
\ll X(\log X)^{2+\epsilon}
after applying Cauchy-Schwarz. Both issues result in a larger error term compared to the main term. We provide some details in the outline below on the differences in our approach compared to previous works.

2.
Outline of Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let
M=X/(\log X)^{100}
, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{f}

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{n\leq M}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)\chi_{8d}(n)}{\sqrt{n}}W_{f}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{n}{M}}}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{f}

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{M<n\leq X}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)\chi_{8d}(n)}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigg(W_{f}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{n}{X}}}\right)-W_{f}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{n}{M}}}\right)\Bigg).

where
W_{f}(x)
is the smooth cut-off function defined as in (
4.2
). With the notation above, the approximate functional equation gives us the decomposition

\displaystyle\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{*}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2Q)=1\\
d\asymp X\end{subarray}}L^{\prime}(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d})L^{\prime}(1/2,g\otimes\chi_{8d})=\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{*}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2Q)=1\\
d\asymp X\end{subarray}}\mathcal{A}_{f}\mathcal{A}_{g}+\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{*}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2Q)=1\\
d\asymp X\end{subarray}}\mathcal{A}_{f}\mathcal{B}_{g}+\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{*}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2Q)=1\\
d\asymp X\end{subarray}}\mathcal{B}_{f}\mathcal{A}_{g}+\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{*}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2Q)=1\\
d\asymp X\end{subarray}}\mathcal{B}_{f}\mathcal{B}_{g}.

When one of the
n_{i}
sums is small, we can apply Poisson summation to obtain both diagonal and off-diagonal terms. The diagonal term gives us the main contributions while we need to bound the off-diagonal terms with Lemma
3.5
from
[
6
]
. The main challenge is to bound the tail

\displaystyle\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{*}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2Q)=1\\
d\asymp X\end{subarray}}\mathcal{B}_{f}\mathcal{B}_{g}

since that is when both
n_{i}
sums are large. Our idea behind bounding the tail is to insert a smooth partition of unity for each
n_{i}
sum which leads us to analyze the following

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{d}}{\sum}_{N_{1},N_{2}}\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{*}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2Q)=1\\
d\asymp X\end{subarray}}\operatorname*{\sum\sum}_{n_{1},n_{2}}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n_{1})\lambda_{g}(n_{2})}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}}}\chi_{8d}(n_{1}n_{2})\Bigg(W_{f}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{n_{1}}{X}}}\right)-W_{f}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{n_{1}}{M}}}\right)\Bigg)\Bigg(W_{g}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{n_{2}}{X}}}\right)-W_{g}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{n_{2}}{M}}}\right)\Bigg)G\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{n_{1}}{N_{1}}}}\right)G\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{n_{2}}{N_{2}}}}\right),

where
G(x)
is a smooth real-valued function that satisfies (
3.3
). Next we truncate each dyadic sum
N_{i}
in three different ranges:
N_{i}\leq M,M<N_{i}\leq X
and
X<N_{i}
.

In the work of
[
5
]
, the authors applied Cauchy-Schwarz for all of the ranges which caused them to lose an extra power of
(\log X)^{2}
in the bound for
\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{*}}_{d\asymp X}\mathcal{B}_{f}^{2}
. But such loss is acceptable given the main term is
X(\log X)^{3}
in their work. This is illustrated in the table below.

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr&N_{1}\leq M&M<N_{1}\leq X&X<N_{1}\\
\hline\cr N_{2}\leq M&\text{C.S.}&\text{C.S.}&\text{C.S.}\\
\hline\cr M<N_{2}\leq X&\text{C.S.}&\text{C.S.}&\text{C.S.}\\
\hline\cr X<N_{2}&\text{C.S.}&\text{C.S.}&\text{C.S.}\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

.

In the work of
[
1
]
, the authors have attempted to apply Cauchy-Schwarz for all of the ranges but with respect to a different partition on the dyadic sums:
N_{i}\leq\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}<N_{i}\leq X
and
X<N_{i}
with
\mathcal{P}=X^{{(\log\log X)}^{-1+A/2}}
for some
0<A<2

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr&N_{1}\leq\mathcal{P}&\mathcal{P}<N_{1}\leq X&X<N_{1}\\
\hline\cr N_{2}\leq\mathcal{P}&\text{C.S.}&\text{C.S.}&\text{C.S.}\\
\hline\cr\mathcal{P}<N_{2}\leq X&\text{C.S.}&\text{C.S.}&\text{C.S.}\\
\hline\cr X<N_{2}&\text{C.S.}&\text{C.S.}&\text{C.S.}\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

.

By doing so, they should also lose an extra power of
(\log X)^{2}
in the bound for
\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{*}}_{d\asymp X}\mathcal{B}_{f}\mathcal{B}_{g}
, which appears to be problematic since the main term is
X(\log X)^{2}
.

On the other hand, our approach takes advantage when one of the dyadic sums is small by using Poisson summation instead of Cauchy-Schwarz. For the ranges
M<N_{i}\leq X
, we observe that the length is small in the
\log
scale. That is, there are
\log\log X
dyadic intervals in that range so we can apply Cauchy-Schwarz and Lemma
3.5
to obtain the bound. For the range
X<N_{i}
, the smooth decay from the cut-off function will also give us small contribution. As a result, we are able to save a power of
(\log X)^{2}
at the end.

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr&N_{1}\leq M&M<N_{1}\leq X&X<N_{1}\\
\hline\cr N_{2}\leq M&{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}P.S.}&{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}P.S.}&{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}P.S.}\\
\hline\cr M<N_{2}\leq X&{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}P.S.}&\text{C.S.}&\text{C.S.}\\
\hline\cr X<N_{2}&{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}P.S.}&\text{C.S.}&\text{C.S.}\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

.

Remark 2
.

Alternatively, one can also redefine
\mathcal{A}_{f},\mathcal{B}_{f}
in the following ways:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{f}

\displaystyle:=\sideset{}{{}^{d}}{\sum}_{N\leq M}\sum_{n}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)\chi_{8d}{(n)}}{\sqrt{n}}G\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{n}{N}}}\right)W_{f}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{n}{M}}}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{f}

\displaystyle:=L^{\prime}(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d})-\mathcal{A}_{f}.

This simplifies the bound for
\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{*}}_{d}\mathcal{B}_{f}\mathcal{B}_{g}
but makes it more work to extract the main terms.

3.
Preliminary Results

By the approximate functional equation,
L^{\prime}(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})
can be expressed in terms of a Dirichlet polynomial. We record a standard result from Petrow
[
7
, Lemma 1]
:

Lemma 3.1
.

(Approximate functional equation).
Let
f
be a
\lambda_{f}(1)=1
normalized cuspidal newform on
\Gamma_{0}(q)
with trivial central character and root number
\omega(f)=i^{\kappa}\eta
. Let
Z>0
be an arbitrary real number parameter. Define the cut-off function

W_{Z}(x):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(3)}\frac{\Gamma(u+\kappa/2)}{\Gamma(\kappa/2)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{2\pi x}{Z\sqrt{q}}}}\right)^{-u}\frac{1-u\log Z}{u^{2}}\;du.

Then

\sum_{n}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)\chi_{d}(n)}{\sqrt{n}}W_{Z}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{n}{|d|}}}\right)-i^{\kappa}\eta\chi_{d}(-q)\sum_{n}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)\chi_{d}(n)}{\sqrt{n}}W_{Z^{-1}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{n}{|d|}}}\right)=\begin{cases}L^{\prime}(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d}),&\text{if}\;\omega(f\otimes\chi_{d})=-1,\\
0,&\text{if}\;\omega(f\otimes\chi_{d})=1.\end{cases}

For simplicity, we let
Z=1
and
\gamma_{f}(u):=\frac{\Gamma(u+\kappa/2)}{\Gamma(\kappa/2)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{q}}}}\right)^{-u}
throughout the paper.
An application of Möbius inversion will lead us to analyze a character sum in the form of

\displaystyle\sum_{(d,2)=1}\chi_{8d}(n_{1}n_{2})F\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{8d}{X}}}\right).

The following lemma is a refinement of Poisson summation from Soundararajan
[
8
, Lemma 2.6]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

(Poisson Summation).
Let
F
be a smooth function compactly support on the positive real numbers, and suppose that
n
is an odd integer. Then

\sum_{(d,2)=1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{d}{n}}}\right)F\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{d}{Z}}}\right)=\frac{Z}{2n}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{2}{n}}}\right)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{k}G_{k}(n)\check{F}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{kZ}{2n}}}\right),

where

G_{k}(n)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1-i}{2}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{-1}{n}}}\right)\frac{1+i}{2}}}\right)\sum_{a\penalty\ \mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\textnormal{mod}\penalty\ n}}\right)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{a}{n}}}\right)e\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{ak}{n}}}\right)

is the Gauss-type sum, and

(3.1)

\displaystyle\check{F}(y)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(\cos(2\pi xy)+\sin(2\pi xy))F(x)\;dx

is a Fourier-type transform of
F
. While for
F
supported on
[0,\infty)
, we also have

(3.2)

\check{F}(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1/2)}\tilde{F}(1-s)\Gamma(s)(\cos+\text{sgn}(y)\sin)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\pi s}{2}}}\right)(2\pi|y|)^{-s}\;ds,

where

\tilde{F}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(x)x^{s-1}\;dx

is the usual Mellin transform of
F
.

Furthermore, Soundararajan has computed
G_{k}(n)
explicitly in
[
8
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
m
and
n
are relatively prime odd integers, then
G_{k}(mn)=G_{k}(m)G_{k}(n)
, and if
p^{\alpha}
is the largest power of
p
dividing
k
(setting
\alpha=\infty
if
k=0
), then

G_{k}(p^{\beta})=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if $\beta\leq\alpha$ is odd},\\
\phi(p^{\beta}),&\text{if $\beta\leq\alpha$ is even},\\
-p^{\alpha},&\text{if $\beta=\alpha+1$ is even},\\
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{kp^{-\alpha}}{p}}}\right)p^{\alpha}\sqrt{p},&\text{if $\beta=\alpha+1$ is odd},\\
0,&\text{if $\beta\geq\alpha+2$}.\\
\end{cases}

After applying Poisson summation, we obtain both diagonal and off-diagonal terms. While the diagonal term gives us the desired asymptotic, we will encounter the Dirichlet series in the form of

\displaystyle Z(\alpha,\beta,\gamma;a,k_{1},Q^{\prime},\chi_{b}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{2}\geq 1\\
(k_{2},2)=1\end{subarray}}\operatorname*{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n_{1}n_{2},2a)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n_{1})\lambda_{g}(n_{2})\chi_{b}(n_{1}n_{2}Q^{\prime})}{n_{1}^{\alpha}n_{2}^{\beta}k_{2}^{2\gamma}}\frac{G_{k_{1}k_{2}^{2}}(n_{1}n_{2}Q^{\prime})}{n_{1}n_{2}Q^{\prime}}

when bounding the off-diagonal contribution. The series is a variation of
(3.10)
in
[
9
]
and the following lemma is a refinement of
[
9
, Lemma 3.3]
, we provide a proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 3.4
.

With the notation above, let
f_{b}:=f\otimes\chi_{b},g_{b}:=g\otimes\chi_{b}
be the twisted newforms. Let
k_{1}
be squarefree and

m(k_{1})=\begin{cases}k_{1},&\quad\text{if $k_{1}\equiv 1\pmod{4}$},\\
4k_{1},&\quad\text{if $k_{1}\equiv 2,3\pmod{4}$}.\\
\end{cases}

Then for any positive integers

, we have

Z(\alpha,\beta,\gamma;a,k_{1},Q^{\prime},\chi_{b})=L(1/2+\alpha,f_{b}\otimes\chi_{m(k_{1})})L(1/2+\beta,g_{b}\otimes\chi_{m(k_{1})})Y(\alpha,\beta,\gamma;a,k_{1},Q^{\prime},\chi_{b})

with

Y(\alpha,\beta,\gamma;a,k_{1},Q^{\prime},\chi_{b})=\frac{Z^{*}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma;a,k_{1},Q^{\prime},\chi_{b})}{L(1+2\alpha,\text{sym}^{2}f)L(1+2\beta,\text{sym}^{2}g)L(1+\alpha+\beta,f\otimes g)},

where
Z^{*}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma;a,k_{1},Q^{\prime},\chi_{b})
is analytic in the region
\text{Re}(\alpha),\text{Re}(\beta)\geq-\delta/2
and
\text{Re}(\gamma)\geq 1/2+\delta
for any

. Moreover, in the same region,
Z^{*}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma;a,k_{1},Q^{\prime},\chi_{b})\ll\tau(a)
where the implied constant may depend only on

and
g
. Furthermore, we can represent

\displaystyle Y(\alpha,\beta,\gamma;a,k_{1},Q^{\prime},\chi_{b})=\operatorname*{\sum\sum\sum}_{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}}\frac{C(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})}{r_{1}^{\alpha}r_{2}^{\beta}r_{3}^{2\gamma}}

as a Dirichlet series with some coefficients

.

Proof.

The terms of the Dirichlet series defining
Z
are jointly multiplicative in
k_{2},n_{1}
and
n_{2}
, so that we can decompose

as a Euler product. Suppose
p^{r_{p}}

, then the generic Euler factor is given by

\displaystyle\operatorname*{\sum\sum\sum}_{k_{2},n_{1},n_{2}\geq 0}\frac{\lambda_{f}(p^{n_{1}})\lambda_{g}(p^{n_{2}})\chi_{b}(p^{n_{1}+n_{2}+r_{p}})}{p^{n_{1}\alpha+n_{2}\beta+2k_{2}\gamma}}\frac{G_{k_{1}p^{2k_{2}}}(p^{n_{1}+n_{2}+r_{p}})}{p^{n_{1}+n_{2}+r_{p}}}.

For
p=2
, the Euler factor is
1
.

For
p\mid ak_{1}
,
n_{1},n_{2}
-sums do not contribute so the Euler factor is

\sum_{k_{2}\geq 0}\frac{\chi_{b}(p^{r_{p}})}{p^{2k_{2}\gamma}}\frac{G_{k_{1}p^{2k_{2}}}(p^{r_{p}})}{p^{r_{p}}}=\begin{cases}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2\gamma}}}}\right)^{-1},&\text{if $r_{p}=0$},\\
0,&\text{if $r_{p}=1$},\\
-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{2\gamma}}+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{p^{1+2\gamma}}}}\right),&\text{if $r_{p}=2$},\\
O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{p^{1+2\delta}}}}\right),&\text{if $r_{p}>2$}.\end{cases}

For
p\mid a
but
p\nmid k_{1}
, the Euler factor is

\sum_{k_{2}\geq 0}\frac{\chi_{b}(p^{r_{p}})}{p^{2k_{2}\gamma}}\frac{G_{k_{1}p^{2k_{2}}}(p^{r_{p}})}{p^{r_{p}}}=\begin{cases}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2\gamma}}}}\right)^{-1},&\text{if $r_{p}=0$},\\
\frac{\chi_{bk_{1}}(p)}{\sqrt{p}},&\text{if $r_{p}=1$},\\
O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{p^{1+2\delta}}}}\right),&\text{if $r_{p}>1$}.\end{cases}

For
p\nmid a
but

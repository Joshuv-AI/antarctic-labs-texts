Integral Artin motives II: Perverse motives and Artin Vanishing Theorem
Raphaël Ruimy

Abstract
. In this text, we are mainly interested in the existence of the perverse motivic t-structures on the category of Artin étale motives with integral coefficients. We construct the perverse homotopy t-structure which is the best possible approximation to a perverse t-structure on Artin motives with rational coefficients. The heart of this t-structure has properties similar to those of the category of perverse sheaves and contains the Ayoub-Zucker motive. With integral coefficients, we construct the perverse motivic t-structure on Artin motives when the base scheme is of dimension at most 2 2 and show that it cannot exist in dimension 4 4 . This construction relies notably on an analogue for Artin motives of the Artin Vanishing Theorem.

(2)

1.2.
Definition and basic facts

First recall the definition of dimension functions (see for instance
[
14
, 1.1.1]
).

Definition 1.2.1
.

Let
S
be a scheme. A
dimension function
on
S
is a function

\delta\colon S\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}

such that for any immediate specialization
y
of a point
x
, i.e a specialization such that

\operatorname{codim}_{\overline{\{x\}}}(y)=1,

we have
\delta(x)=\delta(y)+1
.

A pair
(S,\delta)
with
S
a scheme and
\delta
a dimension function is called a
d-scheme
.

1.2.2
.

Two dimension functions on a scheme differ by a Zariski-locally constant function.
Moreover, if a scheme
S
is universally catenary and integral, the formula

\delta(x)=\dim(S)-\operatorname{codim}_{S}(x)

defines a dimension function on
S
.
In addition, since by convention all the schemes considered here are finite-dimensional and noetherian, our dimension functions will always be bounded.
Finally, any
S
-scheme of finite type
f\colon X\rightarrow S
inherits a canonical dimension function by setting

\delta(x)=\delta(f(x))+\mathrm{tr.deg}\hskip 2.84526ptk(x)/k(f(x)).

By convention, when we consider a d-scheme and a morphism of finite type to the underlying scheme, we will always endow the source with the induced dimension function.

The t-structure will be defined from a set of generators in the following sense:

Proposition 1.2.3
.

(
[
28
, 1.4.4.11]
) Let
\mathcal{C}
be a presentable stable category. Given a small family
\mathcal{E}
of objects, the smallest subcategory
\mathcal{E}_{-}
closed under small colimits and extensions is the set of non-positive objects of a t-structure.

In this setting, we will call this t-structure the
t-structure generated by

\mathcal{E}
.

We can now define the perverse homotopy t-structure.

Definition 1.2.4
.

Let
(S,\delta)
be a d-scheme and let
R
be a commutative ring. The

on

is the t-structure generated in the sense of
Proposition

1.2.3
by the family of the motives
M_{S}^{\mathrm{BM}}(X)[\delta(X)]
with
X
quasi-finite over
S
.

Using the same method as
[
32
, 1.28]
, this t-structure is also generated by family of the motives
h_{S}(X)[\delta(X)]
with
X
finite over
S
. Thus, an object
M
of
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}^{A}(S,R)
belongs to
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}^{A}(S,R)^{hp\geqslant n}
if and only if for any finite
S
-scheme

the complex
\operatorname{map}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,R)}(h_{S}(X),M)
is
(n-\delta(X)-1)
-connected. Let us now derive the formal properties of the t-structure that will be constantly used in the rest of the text. First note that over the spectrum of a field, the t-structure is just given by a shift of the ordinary homotopy t-structure of
[
35
]
:

Proposition 1.2.5
.

Let
k
be a field and let
R
be a localization of
\mathbb{Z}
. Then, the ordinary homotopy t-structure of

is regular, it induces a t-structure on constructible Artin motives which coincides up to the same shift with the ordinary t-structure on
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\mathrm{lisse}}(k_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},R)

\rho_{!}
.

Proof.

The ordinary homotopy t-structure and
t_{hp}
have the same set of generators up to a shift of
\delta(k)
. ∎

.

R
be a commutative ring. Let

(2)

(3)

The proof is the same as the proof of
[

∎

.

1.3.
Pointwise description

In the following section, we give a pointwise description of the perverse homotopy t-structure as well as its behavior on smooth Artin motives. It will be the basis for our understanding of the t-structure. This pointwise description is very analogous to the defining property of the perverse t-structure on étale sheaves (with an extra
\omega^{0}
that ensures that we stay in the world of Artin motives).

We begin by exploring how the t-structure behaves when we change the ring of coefficients.

1.3.1

Let
\sigma\colon R\rightarrow R^{\prime}
be a commutative ring homomorphism.
We have a functorial adjunction

S

\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S,\mathbb{Z})
. The functor
\sigma_{*}
is then the forgetful functor, and the functor
\sigma^{*}
is the functor

.

When

is a localization of
\mathbb{Z}
, is

an integer and let

\mathbb{Z}

(

Let

Theorem 2.2.1
.

Let
(S,\delta)
be an excellent scheme, let
f\colon X\rightarrow S
be a quasi-finite affine morphism. Assume that

X
is nil-regular and that we are in one of the following cases

(a)

We have
\dim(S)\leqslant 2.

(b)

There is a prime number
p
such that the scheme
S
is of finite type over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Then, the functor

f_{!}\colon\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{smA}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(X,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{A}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,\mathbb{Q})

is
t_{hp}
-exact.

Proof.

This is the combination of
Lemma

2.2.2
and
Lemma

2.2.8

∎

Lemma 2.2.2
.

Theorem

2.2.1
holds when Assumption (b) holds.

Proof.

Suppose that Assumption (b) holds. We have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{smA}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(U,\mathbb{Q})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{A}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(S,\mathbb{Q})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}^{smA}(U,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}^{A}(S,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{\ell}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{\ell}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{!}}

such that the vertical arrows are conservative and t-exact when the categories which appear in the bottom row are endowed with the perverse homotopy t-structure. Using
[
35
, 1.1.6]
, it suffices to show that the functor
j_{!}
is t-exact which follows from
[
36
, 4.8.12]
.
∎

We continue with the case when
\dim(S)\leqslant 1
. To that end, we study an analog of the nearby cycle functor (or more accurately residue functor) in our setting. Namely, let
C
be an excellent
1
-dimensional scheme, let
i\colon F\rightarrow C
be a closed immersion of positive codimension and let
j\colon U\rightarrow C
be the complementary open immersion. Assuming that
U
is nil-regular, we study the functor

\omega^{0}i^{*}j_{*}\colon\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{smA}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(U,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{A}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(F,\mathbb{Q}).

First, notice that the scheme
F
is
0
-dimensional. Therefore, the scheme
F
is the disjoint union of its points. Therefore, denoting by

i_{x}\colon\{x\}\rightarrow F

the inclusion for
x\in F
, we get

\omega^{0}i^{*}j_{*}=\bigoplus\limits_{x\in F}(i_{x})_{*}i_{x}^{*}\omega^{0}i^{*}j_{*}.

Now, by
[
32
, 3.3]
, the
2
-morphism
i_{x}^{*}\omega^{0}\rightarrow\omega^{0}i_{x}^{*}
is an equivalence. Therefore, the functor
\omega^{0}i^{*}j_{*}
is equivalent to the functor

\bigoplus\limits_{x\in F}(i_{x})_{*}\omega^{0}(i\circ i_{x})^{*}j_{*}.

Hence, studying the case where
F
is a point will yield a description in the general case. Assume from now on that
F=\{x\}
.

From
Theorem

1.1.6
, we can deduce that through the equivalence
\rho_{!}
of
1.1.5
, the functor

\omega^{0}i^{*}j_{*}
can be seen as a functor

\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{lisse}}(U,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{lisse}}(k(x)_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},\mathbb{Q}).

We have an equivalence
\rho_{!}i^{*}\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}i^{*}\rho_{!}
and an exchange transformation
\rho_{!}j_{*}\rightarrow j_{*}\rho_{!}
by
[
15
, 4.4.2]
.
This gives a transformation

\rho_{!}i^{*}j_{*}\rightarrow i^{*}j_{*}\rho_{!}

of functors
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{lisse}}(U,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(F,\mathbb{Q}).

Furthermore, if
M
is a lisse étale sheaf over
U
, then, the motive
\rho_{!}i^{*}j_{*}M
is Artin by
1.1.5
. Therefore, we have a transformation

{\Theta}\colon\rho_{!}i^{*}j_{*}\rightarrow\omega^{0}i^{*}j_{*}\rho_{!}.

of functors
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{lisse}}(U,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{A}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(F,\mathbb{Q}).

Lemma 2.2.3
.

Let
C
be an excellent
1
-dimensional scheme, let
x
be a closed point of
C
, let
i\colon\{x\}\rightarrow C
be the closed immersion and
j\colon U\rightarrow C
be the complementary open immersion and let
p
be the characteristic exponent of
k(x)
.

Then, the transformation
\Theta

is an equivalence. In particular, the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{lisse}}(U,\mathbb{Q})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}^{A}(U,\mathbb{Q})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{lisse}}(\{x\},\mathbb{Q})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}^{A}(\{x\},\mathbb{Q})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{*}j_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega^{0}i^{*}j_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{!}}

is commutative.

Proof.

Let
\nu\colon\widetilde{C}\rightarrow C
be the normalization of
C
. Write

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{\mathrm{red}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{x\}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu_{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu_{F}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}

the commutative diagram such that both squares are cartesian. Notice that
U_{\mathrm{red}}
is the reduced subscheme associated to
U
. Therefore, the functor
(\nu_{U})_{*}
is an equivalence and commutes with the functor
\rho_{!}
. Hence, it suffices to show that

\Theta(\nu_{U})_{*}:\rho_{!}i^{*}j_{*}(\nu_{U})_{*}\rightarrow\omega^{0}i^{*}j_{*}(\nu_{U})_{*}\rho_{!}

is an equivalence.
Therefore, it suffices to show that the map
\rho_{!}i^{*}\nu_{*}\gamma_{*}\rightarrow\omega^{0}i^{*}\nu_{*}\gamma_{*}\rho_{!}
is an equivalence.

Using proper base change, this is equivalent to the fact that the map

\rho_{!}(\nu_{F})_{*}\iota^{*}\gamma_{*}\rightarrow\omega^{0}(\nu_{F})_{*}\iota^{*}\gamma_{*}\rho_{!}

is an equivalence. Since the map induced by
\nu_{F}
over the reduced schemes is finite, the functor
(\nu_{F})_{*}
commutes with the functor
\rho_{!}
by
Lemma

2.2.6
below and with the functor
\omega^{0}
by
[
32
, 3.3]
. Thus, it suffices to show that the map

\rho_{!}\iota^{*}\gamma_{*}\rightarrow\omega^{0}\iota^{*}\gamma_{*}\rho_{!}

is an equivalence.
Moreover, since the scheme
F
is
0
-dimensional, we can assume as before that
F
is a point. Therefore, we can assume that the
1
-dimensional scheme
C
is normal and therefore regular.

Since by
Theorem

1.1.6
the stable category
\operatorname{\mathcalboondox{Sh}}_{\operatorname{lisse}}(U,\mathbb{Q})
is generated as a thick subcategory of itself by the sheaves of the form
\mathbb{Q}_{U}(V)=f_{*}\underline{\mathbb{Q}}
for
f\colon V\rightarrow U
finite and étale and since the functors
\rho_{!}i^{*}j_{*}
and
\omega^{0}i^{*}j_{*}
are exact and therefore compatible with finite limits, finite colimits and retracts, it suffices to show that the map

\Theta(\mathbb{Q}_{U}(V))\colon\rho_{!}i^{*}j_{*}f_{*}\underline{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\omega^{0}i^{*}j_{*}f_{*}\mathbbm{1}_{V}

is an equivalence.
To that end, we introduce
\overline{f}\colon C_{V}\rightarrow C
the normalization of
C
in
V
. Write

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{V}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{x\}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{f}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}

the commutative diagram such that both squares are cartesian. The map
\Theta(\mathbb{Q}_{U}(V))
is an equivalence if and only if the map

\rho_{!}i^{*}\overline{f}_{*}\gamma_{*}\underline{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\omega^{0}i^{*}\overline{f}_{*}\gamma_{*}\mathbbm{1}_{V}

is an equivalence. The morphism
p
is finite and therefore the functor
p_{*}
commutes with the functor
\rho_{!}
by
Lemma

2.2.6
and with the functor
\omega^{0}
by
[
32
, 3.3]
. Hence, using proper base change, it suffices to show that the map

\rho_{!}\iota^{*}\gamma_{*}\underline{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\omega^{0}\iota^{*}\gamma_{*}\mathbbm{1}_{V}

is an equivalence.

Hence, we can assume that
V=U
. Therefore, we need to prove that the map

\Theta(\underline{\mathbb{Q}})\colon\rho_{!}i^{*}j_{*}\underline{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\omega^{0}i^{*}j_{*}\mathbbm{1}_{U}

is an equivalence. Using
[
32
, 3.3]
, the exchange transformation

i^{*}\omega^{0}j_{*}\rightarrow\omega^{0}i^{*}j_{*}

is an equivalence. Furthermore, by
[
15
, 4.4.2]
, the exchange transformation
\rho_{!}i^{*}\rightarrow i^{*}\rho_{!}
is an equivalence. Therefore, it suffices to show that the exchange map

\rho_{!}j_{*}\underline{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\omega^{0}j_{*}\mathbbm{1}_{U}

is an equivalence which is a direct consequence of the two lemmas below.
∎

Lemma 2.2.4
.

Let
S
be a regular scheme, and
j\colon U\rightarrow S
an open immersion with complement a simple normal crossing divisor. Then, the canonical map

\mathbbm{1}_{S}\rightarrow\omega^{0}j_{*}\mathbbm{1}_{U}

in
\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}^{A}(S,\mathbb{Q})
is an equivalence.

Proof.

This result generalizes
[
4
, 2.11]
. Let
i\colon D\rightarrow S
be the closed immersion which is complementary to
j
. Using
Proposition

1.1.12
, we have an exact triangle

i_{*}\omega^{0}i^{!}\mathbbm{1}_{S}\rightarrow\mathbbm{1}_{S}\rightarrow\omega^{0}j_{*}\mathbbm{1}_{U}.

It therefore suffices to show that
\omega^{0}i^{!}\mathbbm{1}_{S}=0.

Using
[
19
, 5.7]
, we can reduce to the case when
D
is a regular subscheme of codimension
c>0
. In this setting, we have

\omega^{0}i^{!}\mathbbm{1}_{S}=\omega^{0}(\mathbbm{1}_{D}(-c))[-2c]

and the latter vanishes by
[
32
, 3.9]
.
∎

Lemma 2.2.5
.

Let
S
be a normal scheme, and let
j\colon U\rightarrow S
be an open immersion. Then, the canonical map

\underline{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow j_{*}\underline{\mathbb{Q}}

is an equivalence.

Proof.

The canonical map
\underline{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow j_{*}\underline{\mathbb{Q}}
is an equivalence if and only if for any geometric point
\xi
of
S
, the canonical map

\mathbb{Q}\rightarrow\xi^{*}j_{*}\underline{\mathbb{Q}}

is an equivalence. If
n
is an integer,
[
1
, VIII.5]
yields

\mathrm{H}^{n}(\xi^{*}j_{*}\underline{\mathbb{Q}})=\mathrm{H}^{n}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}^{sh}_{S,x})\times_{S}U,\mathbb{Q})

where
\mathcal{O}^{sh}_{S,x}
is the strict henselization of the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{S,x}
of
S
at
x
.
Since the scheme
S
is normal, so is the scheme
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}^{sh}_{S,x})\times_{S}U
. Moreover, this scheme is also connected. Therefore
[
18
, 2.1]
yields the result.
∎

Lemma 2.2.7
.

Theorem

2.2.1
holds when
\dim(S)\leqslant 1
.

Proof.

Corollary

1.2.7
implies that when
g
is a finite morphism, the functor
g_{!}
is perverse homotopy t-exact . When the scheme
S
is
0
-dimensional, the morphism
f
is finite and the result holds.

Assume that the scheme
S
is
1
-dimensional. Zariski’s Main Theorem
[
23
, 18.12.13]
provides a finite morphism
g
and an open immersion
j\colon U\rightarrow X
such that
f=g\circ j
. Therefore it suffices to show that the functor

j_{!}\colon\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{A}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(U,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{DM}}^{A}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},c}(X,\mathbb{Q})

is
t_{hp}
-exact.
We already know by
Proposition

1.2.6
that
j_{!}
is right t-exact.

Hence, letting
M
be a constructible Artin motive over
U
which is
t_{hp}
-non-negative, it suffices to show that the Artin motive
j_{!}(M)
is
t_{hp}
-non-negative.

Let
x
be a point of
X
. Using
Proposition

1.3.5
, we can replace
X
with a neighborhood of
x
. Since the scheme
X
is
1
-dimensional, we can therefore assume that the complement of
U
is the closed subscheme
\{x\}
and replace
U
with any neighborhood of the generic points. In particular, we can assume that
U
is nil-regular and that
M
is smooth Artin using the continuity property of
[
35
, 1.4.2]
.

be the canonical immersion. Applying the localization triangle (
1.1.1
) to the Artin motive
\omega^{0}j_{*}(M)
yields an exact triangle

j_{!}(M)\rightarrow\omega^{0}j_{*}(M)\rightarrow i_{*}i^{*}\omega^{0}j_{*}(M).

1.2.6

Step 4:

3.4.
Gluing and t-exactness of the realization

The following description of perverse Artin motives is very close to that of Artin perverse sheaves from
[
36
]
. It is very loosely inspired by Beilinson’s gluing method from
[
12
]
.

Let
M
be an Artin perverse motive over an excellent base scheme
S
of dimension at most
2
.
[
35
, 3.5.1]
gives a stratification of
S
with regular strata and such that for any stratum
T
, the motive
M|_{T}
is a constructible smooth Artin motive. We can recover the motive
M
from the motives

and additional data.

We take the convention that
\delta(S)=2
and assume for simplicity that the scheme
S
is reduced. Let
U
be the (disjoint) union of the strata containing the generic points of
S
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
U
is a single stratum over which the motive
M
a constructible smooth Artin motive. Shrinking the open subset
U
, we can also assume that the immersion
j\colon U\rightarrow S
is affine. Write
i\colon F\rightarrow S
the reduced complementary closed immersion. The motive
M|_{U}
is a perverse Artin motive and the constructible Artin motive

is in perverse degree
[-1,0]
.

By localization, we have an exact triangle

i_{*}M|_{F}[-1]\rightarrow j_{!}M|_{U}\rightarrow M.

Thus, by
Proposition

3.3.11
, we have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow i_{*}{}^{hp}\mathrm{H}^{-1}M|_{F}\rightarrow j_{!}M|_{U}\rightarrow M\rightarrow i_{*}{}^{hp}\mathrm{H}^{0}M|_{F}\rightarrow O

Hence, we can recover

as the cofiber of a map
i_{*}M|_{F}[-1]\rightarrow j_{!}M|_{U}
such that the induced map
i_{*}{}^{hp}\mathrm{H}^{-1}M|_{F}\rightarrow j_{!}M|_{U}
is a monomorphism.

Conversely, a smooth Artin perverse sheaf
M_{U}
over
U
, a constructible Artin motive
M_{F}
in perverse degrees
[-1,0]
over
F
and a connecting map
i_{*}M_{F}[-1]\rightarrow j_{!}M_{U}
such that the induced map
i_{*}{}^{hp}\mathrm{H}^{-1}M_{F}\rightarrow j_{*}M_{U}
is a monomorphism, give rise to a unique perverse Artin motive
M
over
S
.

Now, recall that we have an exact triangle

j_{!}\rightarrow\omega^{0}j_{*}\rightarrow i_{*}\omega^{0}i^{*}j_{*}

and that
j^{*}i_{*}=0
. Therefore, we have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow i_{*}{}^{hp}\mathrm{H}^{-1}(\omega^{0}i^{*}j_{*}M_{U})\rightarrow j_{!}M_{U}\rightarrow{}^{hp}\mathrm{H}^{0}(\omega^{0}j_{*}M_{U})\rightarrow i_{*}{}^{hp}\mathrm{H}^{0}(\omega^{0}i^{*}j_{*}M_{U})\rightarrow 0.

and we have

\operatorname{Hom}(i_{*}M_{F}[-1],j_{!}M_{U})=\operatorname{Hom}(M_{F},\omega^{0}i^{*}j_{*}M_{U}).

Therefore, if
\phi\colon i_{*}M_{F}[-1]\rightarrow j_{!}M_{U}
is any map, the map
{}^{hp}\mathrm{H}^{0}(\phi)
is a monomorphism if and only if the induced map

{}^{hp}\mathrm{H}^{-1}(M_{F})\rightarrow{}^{hp}\mathrm{H}^{-1}(\omega^{0}i^{*}j_{*}M_{U})

is a monomorphism.

Similarly, since the stratification of
S
induces a stratification of
F
, the Artin motive
M_{F}
is uniquely encoded by the data of a smooth constructible Artin motive
M_{1}
concentrated in perverse degrees

over an affine open subset

of

, a constructible Artin motive

in perverse degrees
[-2,0]
on

and a connecting map with a similar condition.

This discussion provides the following proposition:

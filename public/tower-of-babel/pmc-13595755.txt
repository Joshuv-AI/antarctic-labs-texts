Uni-portal thoracoscopic lymph node dissection under the left prominence: pause ventilation technique
Authors: Yanhui Yang, Jinyuan Mei, Jiaxiong Luo, Linzhi Xie, Cui Ma, Ji Li, Yi Wang, Xiaoyang Xie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
TechniqueUni-portal video-assisted thoracoscopic lobectomy (UVATS) has become a well-established technique; however, performing a complete lymphadenectomy, particularly the removal of lymph nodes located below the left hemithorax (LHLN), remains a challenging procedure. To our knowledge, there are limited reports addressing the use of pause ventilation during UVATS for LHLN dissection. In this study, we propose the application of pause ventilation for facilitating LHLN dissection under UVATS. Through transnasal humidified rapid-insufflation ventilatory exchange (THRIVE), we innovatively implemented an intermittent apnea technique during surgery, enabling UVATS dissection of the LHLN. This study details the surgical procedure and includes an accompanying operative video.ResultsThe application of pause ventilation can promote LHLN dissection under UVATS.ConclusionsThe use of pause ventilation for LHLN dissection in UVATS appears safe and feasible, providing clear exposure of key surrounding tissues and organs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13019-026-04364-7.

Introduction

Accurate lymph node dissection is essential for long-term prognosis and precise staging in patients with lung cancer [1]. However, LHLN dissection via UVATS remains technically challenging [2–3]. We present a novel approach incorporating THRIVE to facilitate LHLN dissection by pausing ventilation under the left thoracic prominence.

Methods

A 72-year-old woman presented with a chest CT showing a left upper lung mass. Intraoperative frozen pathology confirmed non-small cell lung cancer. A UVATS left upper lobectomy with systematic lymph node dissection was performed.

THRIVE was applied before endotracheal intubation to extend inspiratory oxygenation during planned ventilation pauses. A 3-cm incision was made between the midline and posterior axillary line.

The sequence of lymph node dissection under the left prominence are as follows (A-D):

(A) Open the posterior mediastinal pleura (Fig. 1a); (B) Expose the esophagus (Fig. 1b); (C) Dissect the lymph nodes near the end of the left bronchus (upper edge of the left lower lobe bronchus) (Fig. 1c); (D) Dissect the tracheal prominence and the lymph nodes adjacent to the right bronchus down to the lower edge of the middle segment of the right bronchus (Fig. 1d). The choice of method and timing of rescue breathing is as follow:

After completing steps A-C and ensuring stable vital signs, instruct the anesthesiologist to pause ventilation and apply THRIVE (60 L/min, 37 °C) to complete step D. If SpO₂ drops below 92% or apnea exceeds 5 min, re-inflate the right lung. (Note: Ventilation is only paused during Stage D.) Results

The anatomical complexity of the descending aorta beneath the left thoracic prominence complicates LHLN dissection via UVATS. Here, we adopted a pause ventilation approach for lymph node removal below the prominence, yielding several advantages: reduced interference from right lung movement, expanded exposure and visual clarity, and potentially reduced damage to critical structures like the bronchi and esophagus. In this case, the subcarinal lymph node dissection time was approximately 7 min. Additionally, THRIVE extended safe apnea time, supporting operative efficiency and possibly enhancing surgeon satisfaction. Patel [4] demonstrated THRIVE’s ability to extend apnea during challenging tracheal surgeries, achieving a median of 14 (5–65) minutes of intraoperative apnea. Our prior meta-analysis [5] of randomized trials found THRIVE to extend safe apnea and reduce complication risk. In this study, we employed THRIVE for LHLN dissection, pausing ventilation during Stage D to allow sufficient time for lymph node dissection. If signs of distress or desaturation (SpO₂ <92%) occur intraoperatively, right lung ventilation should resume immediately.

Conclusions

THRIVE-based pause ventilation can facilitate safe and thorough LHLN dissection during UVATS. Continued monitoring of vital signs and laboratory values during apnea is essential to ensure patient safety. However, this study is based on observational findings from a single case. Further studies with larger sample sizes and more in-depth investigation are needed to confirm the results.

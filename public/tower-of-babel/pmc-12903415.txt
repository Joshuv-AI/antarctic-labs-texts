Molecular surveillance of GI-19 lineage infectious bronchitis virus in China: divergent evolution and efficacy assessment of QXL87 vaccine against heterologous clades
Authors: Yang Chen, Zifan Liu, Mengjiao Guo, Zongyi Bo, Chengcheng Zhang, Yongzhong Cao, Yantao Wu, Xiaorong Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundInfectious bronchitis is a highly contagious acute disease in chickens caused by the infectious bronchitis virus (IBV). The GI-19 lineage of IBV has been shown to undergo continuous evolution, with increasing variance in antigenicity and pathogenicity in China. The aim of this study was to assess the efficacy of the only registered QX vaccine (i.e., QXL87) in China against diverse GI-19 clades.ResultsA total of 1503 S1 sequences from China were identified as belonging to the IBV GI-19 based on phylogenetic analysis, including sequences from GenBank and clinical samples sequenced by our team (Key Laboratory of Animal Infectious Diseases of Ministry of Agriculture, China) from 2018 to 2022. Within the GI-19 lineage, five clades were identified and QXL87 vaccine belonged to clade 3. Clade 2 and clade 4 were found to be prevalent from 2018 to 2022. One strain was randomly selected from each of the four clades distinct from the QXL87 vaccine strain to serve as challenge strains for testing the cross-protective efficacy of the QXL87 vaccine. The amino acid homology of the S1 protein between these strains and the QXL87 vaccine strain ranged from 93.7 to 94.8%. The QXL87 vaccine was demonstrated clinical protection rates of 90%, 80%, 90%, and 90% against four respective strains. It exhibited protection rates above 70% for ciliary activity against all strains tested (74.29% for the GD4 strain, 79.33% for the 06Ⅱ strain, 81.63% for the JS5 strain, and 90.31% for the JS41 strain). Vaccinated chickens experienced milder clinical signs, fewer lesions and reduced viral shedding in the trachea and kidneys.ConclusionsThis study underscored the substantial genetic divergence within the dominant GI-19 lineage of IBV, resulting in the emergence of multiple clades. Despite this, the QXL87 vaccine remained effective for preventing GI-19 lineage IBV infection although the protection rates differed among different clades. However, we must acknowledge that in cross-protection trials, ciliary motility scores for clade 1 and clade 3 marginally dropped below 80% according to a more accurate calculation method published. Consequently, ongoing surveillance for mutations of circulating IBV strains remains critical. In case the antigenic drift compromises vaccine efficacy, timely updating vaccine strains or immunization strategies involving multiple vaccine candidates should be prioritized to ensure sustained protection.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12917-025-05012-3.

Background

Infectious bronchitis is a significant economic concern for the global poultry industry, caused by the infectious bronchitis virus (IBV) [21]. IBV primarily targets the respiratory, urogenital, and reproductive systems, leading to respiratory distress, nephritis, reduced egg production and the phenomenon known as “false layers”, some of which can also cause proventriculitis and enteritis [18]. Belonging to the Gammacoronavirus genus, IBV exhibits a remarkably high mutation rate of 10− 4 to 10− 5 substitutions per site per year due to the limited proofreading capabilities of the viral RNA-dependent RNA-polymerase [17, 42]. IBV was first identified in the United States in 1931, and has evolved into multiple pathotypes, serotypes, and genotypes globally [3, 14, 25, 30, 37, 56]. A widely adopted classification system now relies on sequencing the complete IBV S1 gene, as this subunit plays a crucial role in eliciting neutralizing and serotype-specific antibodies [8, 44]. To date, a total of nine genotypes (GI ~ GIX) encompassing 36 distinct lineages(GI-1 ~ GI-31, GII-1 ~ GII-2, GIII-1 ~ GIII-3) have been delineated worldwide [37, 38]. The genotypes of IBV strains display substantial diversity globally, with the GI-1, GI-13, GI-16, GI-19 and GI-23 lineages being prevalent worldwide. The GI-19 lineage, also known as the QX-type IBV, has emerged as the most widespread strain across continents [16, 17, 38]. Initially identified in a diseased chicken in Qingdao, China, the GI-19 lineage IBV has disseminated extensively to European, Asian, and Middle Eastern countries through successive circulation waves [17, 56]. This lineage IBV exhibits higher virulence, particularly causing severe damage to the kidney and oviduct, and showing a greater tropism for airway and oviduct epithelial cells compared to other strains [1, 4, 10, 35, 51]. Moreover, the QX-type IBV has been observed to cause significant reproductive system damage in roosters [50].

Various studies in different regions of China have consistently identified the GI-19 lineage of IBV as the predominant genotype in chickens [15, 22, 32, 33, 48]. Live attenuated vaccine is the primary method for safeguarding chickens against IBV infection [30]. In 2018, QXL87 was officially registered in the form of a Combined Newcastle Disease and Infectious Bronchitis Vaccine, Live (Strain La Sota + Strain QXL87). It was developed from the isolate “QXL strain”(2012) by our laboratory (Key Laboratory of Animal Infectious Diseases of Ministry of Agriculture, China) [46].To date, it remains the only commercially available live attenuated vaccine of the GI-19 lineage in China. Over time, the GI-19 lineage of IBV has been demonstrated to undergo continuous evolution. Surveillance data reveals a growing variance in antigenicity among predominant strains, leading to notable distinctions in patho genicity and tissue tropism among isolates in China and globally [2, 16, 52]. The prevalence of the GI-19 lineage of IBV, as evidenced by numerous sequences in the GenBank database and the ongoing emergence of new variants, raises the question of potential diversification into separate clades. It has been reported that strains in the GI-19 lineage can be grouped into two clusters back in 2012 [20]. Numerous studies have been conducted to assess the protective efficacy of vaccines against prevalent strains or variants [19, 32, 41, 53]. However, there is uncertainty surrounding the ability of the QXL87 vaccine to offer a sufficient protection against new variants within the GI-19 lineage. Reports suggest that there is a reduced similarity between the isolates and the QXL87 vaccine [34]. If variants are able to evade the vaccine’s defenses, it could result in significant economic losses and an increased risk of recombination with other strains. In such cases, it is important to explore the potential of “protectotype vaccines” or develop new vaccines specific to the evolving variants [13]. Hence, this study was to assess the efficacy of the QXL87 vaccine in providing adequate protection against the evolving IBV isolates of the GI-19 lineage, which exhibit significant genetic differentiation.

Materials and methods

Animals

Specific Pathogen Free (SPF) embryonated chicken eggs were procured from Beijing Boehringer Ingelheim Vital Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China), while SPF white leghorn chicks were obtained from Jinan Sipai Furui Livestock Technology Co., Ltd. (Shandong, China). All animal experimental procedures were conducted in accordance with the ARRIVE Guidelines 2.0 and approved by the Animal Welfare and Ethical Review Committee of Yangzhou University (202103403).

Viruses

The commercially available Combined Newcastle Disease and Infectious Bronchitis Vaccine, Live (Strain La Sota + Strain QXL87, Batch Number: 220473106) was purchased from Qingdao Yebio Bioengineering Co., Ltd. IBV strains from four clades within the GⅠ-19 lineage were isolated and preserved: IBV/CK/CH/JS/2016/06Ⅱ (clade 1, 06Ⅱ strain), IBV/CK/CH/JS/2021/41 (clade 2, JS41 strain), IBV/CK/CH/GD/2021/4 (clade 4, GD4 strain), and IBV/CK/CH/JS/2022/5 (clade 5, JS5 strain). The viral titer for each strain was determined following previously established methods [27, 39].

Genetic evolution analysis of GⅠ-19 lineage

A total of 1053 full-length S1 sequences from China were gathered from GenBank (n = 974) and clinic samples (n = 79) sequenced by our laboratory (2018–2022). So, they were genotyped using IBV reference strains (Supplementary Table 1) after being filtered by the Recombination Detection Program (RDP4, Version 4.95) using seven methods (RDP, GENECONV, BOOTSCAN, MAXCHI, CHIMAERA, SISCAN, and 3SEQ) and Simplot software (version 3.5) to eliminate recombination events. Briefly, seven methods and default program settings were used to analyze the data and the recombinant sequences were identified along with the possible parental virus and the beginning and end points if they were supported by at least five of the seven methods with strong P-values (< 10–12) [44, 54, 57]. The potential recombination events and breakpoints were further verified by Simplot using a window of 200 bp and a step size of 20 bp. Then, they were aligned using the FFT-NS-1 method in Mafft v.7.487(https://mafft.cbrc.jp/alignment/software/windows.html). The neighbor-joining (NJ) method of MEGA X was used to analyze these sequences with 1000 bootstrap replicates to generate the phylogenetic trees to verify the genotypes/lineages of these sequences. Sequences identified as belonging to the GI-19 lineage of IBV (Supplementary Table 2) were realigned with the Mass 41 strain (as an outgroup) using the FFT-NS-1 method in Mafft v.7.0. The maximum likelihood (ML) phylogenetic tree and the best-fit model (GTR + G + I) in MEGA X were used to analyze these sequences with 1000 bootstrap replicates. The phylogenetic tree was displayed and annotated by iTOL(https://itol.embl.de/). The nucleotide and amino acid sequences of the S1 genes were aligned, compared and calculated using the ClustalW multiple alignment method in BioEdit. Additionally, a subset analysis was conducted on a smaller set of sequences for enhanced tree visualization, consisting of 8 representative sequences from each identified IBV clade. To assess variations in amino acid residues within the hypervariable region (HVR), receptor binding domain (RBD), and neutralizing epitope region, alignment results between four new isolates, the QXL strain, and the QXL87 vaccine strain S1 subunit amino acid sequences were displayed using the online software ESPript3.0 [49, 58].

Evaluation of vaccine efficacy to GⅠ-19 lineage trains in different clades

Experimental design

During the experiment, all animals were provided with ad libitum access to irradiated diet (Jiangsu Synergy Pharmaceutical Biological Engineering Co., Ltd., Jiangsu, China) and water in the negative pressure isolator inside the secondary biosafety barrier area, receiving 12 h of light each day. To assess the protective efficacy of QXL87 against GⅠ-19 lineage strains in various clades, one-day-old birds were randomly assigned to 9 groups, each consisting of 25 chickens. Birds in groups V1, V2, V3, and V4 were vaccinated with one dose of Combined Newcastle Disease and Infectious Bronchitis Vaccine, Live (Strain La Sota + Strain QXL87) at 7 days old via the oculo-nasal route according to manufacturer instructions, while those in group C1, C2, C3 and C4 were vaccinated with 0.1 mL of PBS (pH 7.4). At 14 days after vaccination, groups C1 and V1, C2 and V2, C3 and V3, and C4 and V4 were inoculated with the 06Ⅱ strain (clade 1), JS41 strain (clade 2), GD4 strain (clade 4) and JS5 strain (clade 5) at a dose of 105.2EID50 via the oculo-nasal route, respectively. The pathogenicity of these four representative strains has been systematically evaluated in our laboratory in a preliminary study (unpublished data). The mock group (n = 10) was maintained as a control group. The detailed grouping is shown in Fig. 1.

Clinical signs

Ten chickens were randomly selected in each group and labeled with crystal violet dye for statistics of clinical symptoms and detection of virus shedding. Since being challenged, the chickens were observed twice a day and the morbidity was recorded including (1) depression, anorexia and huddling; (2) nasal discharge, head shaking and respiratory signs; (3) diarrhea and dehydration; and (4) death. It is considered morbid once one of these symptoms occurs.

Gross lesions and histopathology

In each group, five birds were randomly selected to be euthanized at 5- and 9-days post challenge (dpc), while the remaining chicks were sacrificed at 14 dpc. Lesions of the trachea and kidney were observed and noted. Tissue samples were fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin for 48 h and embedded in paraffin wax. Thin Sect. (5 μm) were cut from the samples, stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E), and examined under a microscope to assess histopathological changes as previously described [50].

Assessment of tracheal ciliostasis

Five chickens from each group were sacrificed at 6 dpc and their trachea were examined since IBV causes serious damage to tracheal cilia. To evaluate the level of tracheal ciliostasis, the rings from the upper (n = 3), middle (n = 4), and lower (n = 3) sections of the tracheas were separated and placed in DMEM containing 10% FBS to determine the degree of ciliary movement and desquamation/loss. The 0–4 scoring system was used to score the cilia activity [13]. The vaccine complies with the test if not fewer than 80% of the vaccinated chickens show normal ciliary activity and ciliary activity is considered normal when at least 50% of the internal ring shows vigorous ciliary movement according to the European Pharmacopoeia. To quantify the protective efficacy more accurately, the mean ciliary score of 5 chickens and the protection score (1 – mean ciliostasis score for experimental group/mean ciliostasis score for corresponding challenge controls) x 100% were also calculated [13].

Viral shedding

Oral and cloacal swabs were collected from 10 chicks in the immunization and control groups at 3 dpc, 5 dpc 7 dpc, 9 dpc and 11 dpc and kept in separate tubes containing 500 µL of sterile PBS viral shedding. Total RNA and cDNA were prepared using Ultrapure RNA Kit (CoWin Biosciences, Beijing, China) and EasyScript® Reverse Transcriptase [M-MLV, RNaseH-] Kit (TransGen Biotech, Beijing, China), and RT–qPCR was conducted as previously described [5].

Results

Evolutionary genetic analysis of S1 in GI-19 lineage

A total of 1053 S1 sequences were confirmed to have no recombinant sequences by RDP4 and Simplot. They were identified as GI-19 lineage IBV according to the constructed phylogenetic trees, which included reference IBV strains (Supplementary Table 1) (Fig. 2). The GⅠ-19 lineage was classified into 5 clades based on the tree topology and genetic distances (Fig. 3A; Table 1). Eight representative sequences from each clade and 10 sequences not grouped in any clade were chosen to build a smaller phylogenetic tree. As shown in Fig. 3B, it can be seen that the phylogenetic tree constructed by the large data set was basically the same as that of the smaller data set. These clustering indicated a significant genetic divergence within GI-19. The nucleotide sequence identity between clade 3 and four clades ranged from 93.2 to 95.6% (clade 1), 93.5–94.4% (clade 2), 93.8–94.8% (clade 4), and 94.1–95.5% (clade 5), compared to 96.9–99.5% in clade 3 (Table 2). The amino acid sequence identity between clade 3 and the four clades varied from 93.1 to 95.3% (clade 1), 92.9–94.8% (clade 2), 92.2–94.4% (clade 4), and 93.5–95.9% (clade 5), with 95.5–99.2% in clade 3 (Table 2). The first submitted (58HeN-93II, Accession Number: KC577395.1) and reported (SD/97/02, Accession Number: AF193423.1) S1 sequences of GⅠ-19 lineage IBV strain in China were clustered in Clade 1. Strains in Clade 2 were first discovered in 2005. Strains in Clade 3 originated in 2007, including the QXL87 vaccine (Accession Number: MH743141.1) and its parental strain QXL strain (Ck/CH/JS/2010/12, Accession Number: JQ900122). Strains in Clade 4 and 5 were all reported after 2014. By 2022, Clade 2 possessed the most sequences (n = 589) followed by Clade 3 (n = 201) and Clade 4 (n = 103). There were 66 and 78 sequences in Clade 1 and 5, respectively. From 2018 to 2022, GⅠ-19 lineage IBV strains in Clade 2 and Clade 4 were remarkable. Clade 2 remained the predominant clade (n = 98) and the number of sequences in Clade 4 ranked second (n = 96) although they were similar. Only 10 sequences were grouped into Clade 1. There were 41 and 43 sequences belonging to Clade 3 and Clade 5, respectively.

Clinical observations

Chickens in the challenged group displayed pronounced depression, anorexia, huddling behavior, and increased water consumption, although these symptoms were infrequently observed in vaccinated groups. As was shown in Table 3, the morbidity and mortality of Group C1 reached 90% and 20%, while only 1 chicken displayed mild symptoms and no death in V1 group. Chickens in group C1 exhibited initial symptoms at 2 dpc, characterized by severe respiratory manifestations, and resulted in one death at 5 and 7 dpc, respectively. In group C2, symptoms arisen since 2 dpc and all chickens demonstrated varying degrees of respiratory signs with 100% morbidity and 0% mortality, whereas the mild symptoms only occurred in two chickens in group V2. Symptoms in group C3 manifested at 1 dpc, leading to one death at 8 dpc, with 90% morbidity and 10% mortality. Only one chicken show symptoms in V3 group. Group C4 showed symptom onset at 1 dpc, escalating until 6 dpc with five deaths, with 100% morbidity and 50% mortality. In the vaccinated group V4, one chicken expressed pathosis. Overall, QXL87 conferred substantial clinical protection (90%, 80%, 90%, 90%) against the challenge posed by four isolates, 06Ⅱ, JS41, GD4, and JS5 strain, respectively.

Gross lesions and histopathology of trachea and kidneys

The results about gross lesions of tracheal and kidney were reported in Table 3; Fig. 4. Chickens were euthanized by cervical dislocation and necropsied. At 5 dpc, necropsy findings revealed petechial hemorrhage in the larynx (3/5) and trachea, as well as severe kidney swelling (4/5) in chickens within group C1 (Fig. 4). Mottled kidneys were also observed in one chicken in C1 group. Group V1 exhibited only minor bleeding spots in the larynx (2/5). Chickens in group C2, compared to the V2 group, displayed slight bleeding in the trachea (4/5) and enlarged kidneys(4/5). In group C3, chickens (4/5) exhibited laryngeal hemorrhage, with two showing extensive bleeding spots. Chickens challenged with GD4 displayed evident hyperemia and kidneys’ swelling(3/5), while those in the V3 group showed no obvious lesions. All chickens in group C4 experienced hemorrhage in the larynx and trachea, as well as kidneys’ swelling, with four chickens showing noticeable urate deposition in the kidneys compared with chickens in V4 group.

Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining was conducted for histopathological assessment. The challenged chicks exhibited significant pathological alterations in both trachea and kidneys compared to the vaccinated-challenge group. The trachea displayed an increase in mucus production, thickening of the lamina propria in the mucosa, epithelial cell sloughing, severe cilia loss, and lymphocytic infiltration in the challenged groups (Supplementary Fig. 1). In contrast, the trachea of the QXL87-vaccinated group showed intact cilia with minimal inflammatory cell presence and mucus accumulation. Moreover, the kidneys of the challenged group manifested mesenchymal dilation, hemorrhage, swelling and necrosis of tubular epithelial cells, and infiltration of inflammatory cells (Supplementary Fig. 1). Additionally, minimal changes were observed in the renal tubule interstitium, inflammatory cells, and red blood cells in the vaccinated-challenge group. In all, minimal gross and microscopic lesions were observed in the vaccinated group.

Assessment of tracheal ciliostasis

Five chickens from each group were euthanized by cervical dislocation and necropsied at 6 dpc to assess the score of tracheal ciliostasis. The challenge groups exhibited a notable increase in mucus, ciliostasis, and severe cilia loss in the trachea compared to the vaccinated-challenge groups. As was shown in Fig. 5, the scores of vaccinated groups were all significantly lower than unvaccinated groups (p<0.0001, ****). According to Table 4, the QXL87 vaccine managed to protect all chickens against the 06Ⅱ, JS41, GD4, and JS5 strains, as long as the ciliary activity of each chicken in the vaccinated group was above 50%. To quantify the protection efficacy more accurately, the protection rates in different vaccinated groups were calculated. QXL87 demonstrated protection rates exceeding 70% against experimental challenge with all strains. In particular, QXL87 showed a high protective efficacy of 90.31% and 81.63% against JS41 (V2) and JS5 (V4) viruses, respectively, based on cilia activity scores.

Viral shedding

Viral shedding in oral and cloacal swabs was assessed to monitor the viral dynamics in the inoculated chicks. Overall, viral copies exhibited similar patterns, increasing from 3 dpc, peaking at 5 or 7 dpc, and then declining afterwards (Fig. 6). In the V1 group, no viral shedding was observed in oral swabs after 3 dpc, with the IBV genome copies in the oral notably lower than those in the C1 group at 3 dpc(p ≤ 0.0021, **). Virus load in cloacal swabs in C1 group was significantly higher than that in V1 group at 3 dpc(p <0.0001, ****) and 7 dpc(p ≤ 0.0332, *).Within the V2 group, viral shedding in the respiratory tract was significantly reduced compared to the C2 group at 3 dpc(p ≤ 0.0021, **) and 5 dpc(p <0.0001, ****). Remarkably, viral copies in cloacal swabs in the V2 groups were significantly lower than those in their respective challenge groups at all time points in different p values, especially at 11 dpc(p <0.0001, ****). Specifically, at 3, 5, and 7 dpc, the virus load in oral swabs in the V3 group was markedly lower than that in the C3 group at an extremely significant level (p < 0.0001, ****). Genome copies in cloaca in V3 group were significantly inferior to that in C3 group at all timepoints, especially at 3 dpc and 7 dpc(p <0.0001, ****). Moreover, viral copies in the V4 group were notably lower than those in the C4 group at 3 dpc(p ≤ 0.0002, ***), 5 dpc(p <0.0001, ****) and 7 dpc(p ≤ 0.0332, *) in oral swabs and at 3 dpc(p ≤ 0.0021, **), 5 dpc(p ≤ 0.0002, ***), 7 dpc(p ≤ 0.0021, **) and 9 dpc(p ≤ 0.0332, *) cloacal swabs, respectively.

Multiple sequence alignment of the S1 subunit

In Figs. 7A and 56 amino acid residue mutations were identified among four isolates compared to QXL87, with only 6 sites not associated with regions of S1 (HVRs, neutralizing epitopes, and RBD). Among the four strains, at least one strain differed from the others at 47 sites (Fig. 7A). Specifically, 5, 5, and 6 mutation sites were located in the HVR I, II, and III regions, respectively (Fig. 7A, B). Notably, JS5 strain differed from other isolates, QXL, and QXL87 at position 130Pro, a variation previously published to be associated with IBV’s binding to kidney tissue. Fifteen mutation sites were situated within the neutralizing epitope regions (B/C/A), the highest among the five epitopes analyzed (Fig. 7B, D). Amino acid identity varied among the four isolates when compared to QXL87 (Fig. 7C). Strain 06 II (clade 1) shared 94.8% identity with QXL87, while JS41 strain shared 93.7% (clade 2). Both GD4 strain (clade 4) and JS5 strain (clade 5) both exhibited an amino acid identity of 94.3% with QXL87.

Discussion

IBV, a significant pathogen in the poultry sector, has been established in China for over 65 years, dating back to 1958 [31]. The emergence of new genotypes and variants is increasingly observed, attributed to genetic modifications such as insertions, deletions, and point mutations in the S1 gene, RNA recombination, and alterations in the S2 subunit, which can impact the structure of the S1 gene [7, 23, 28]. With advancements in molecular biology technology, the classification of IBV strains has shifted towards comprehensive analysis of the S1 gene sequence, superseding traditional tissue tropism-based categorization [44]. To date, a total of 36 lineages and 9 genotypes have been identified, including GI-1 ~ GI-31, GII-1 ~ GII-2, GIII-1 ~ GIII-3 and GIV~GIX [37, 38].

The QX-type IBV strain was first reported in Qingdao in 1996 [47]. Since 1999, the GⅠ-19 lineage IBV has been dominant in China and has spread to chicken flocks in many parts of the world [15, 21, 36, 43]. In South Korea, six distinct subgroups GI-19 IBVs were identified [29], as well as a new classification criteria was proposed to divide K-II sub-genotype (GI-19) into five sub-genogroups (a–e) in Korea [26]. In 2012, researchers found that GI-19 lineage IBV strains can be categorized into two clusters [20]. It is significant for China to clarify the epidemiological characteristics and development trends of GⅠ-19 lineage IBV. In this study, we scrutinized 1503 S1 gene sequences from China (79 sequences collected by our laboratory) without recombinant events, which were genotyped as GI-19 lineage IBV. Nevertheless, the efficacy of QXL87 vaccine against the remaining strains exhibiting recombinant events will be evaluated separately, as such events may lead to significant antigenic changes. We concluded that 5 different clades were separated among 1053 S1 sequences of GⅠ-19 lineage IBV. However, we noted that 110 GI-19 lineage isolates were divided into 7 different sublineages (A-G) and A-E sublineages were predominant from 2016 to 2017 [49], which seemed to differ from our results. We doubted that this was because the larger number of S1 sequences and longer time span in our study. Furthermore, in the period from 2018 to 2022, strains belonging to clade 2 and 4 exhibited a higher prevalence compared to other strains, aligning with our laboratory’s surveillance of IBV. In the future, potential antigenic changes in other prevalent genotypes/lineages such as GⅠ-13 lineage IBV, GⅠ-7 lineage IBV and GⅥ-1 genotype IBV could also be of great necessity to be evaluated.

Globally, vaccination with live attenuated or inactivated vaccines remains the most effective method of protecting chickens against IBV infection. However, the vaccine will not remain effective over the long term if viruses continue to evolve under immune selection pressure. Hence, it is crucial to continuously assess the efficacy of vaccines, as a single serotype of IBV vaccine may not provide sufficient protection against heterologous challenges [24]. To date, the only commercially available QX-type live vaccine in China, QXL87 vaccine, has been in use for approximately six years and has played an important role in controlling QX-type IBV infections. In the course of long-term monitoring, we have realized that the GI-19 lineage of IBV has shown a significant trend of evolution with potential pathogenic and antigenic changes, especially after 2018. It is essential to evaluate the protective efficacy of QXL87 against the latest QX-type IBVs to determine whether it is necessary to develop new vaccine strains or seek other effective control strategies. Considering that QXL87 vaccine belonged to clade 3 and the lowest amino acid identity among four strains could reach 93.7% (JS41 strain), we selected QXL87 vaccine without comparison to other IBV vaccines to conduct vaccine efficacy experiments. Overall, if we consider only clinical symptoms and tracheal ciliary activity as the ‘gold standard,’ the QXL87 vaccine provided good immune protection against strains from the four clades. However, we must also acknowledge that when took into account other indicators, such as microscopic lesions and shedding levels, there are some differences in the protective efficacy of QXL87 against strains in different clades. Furthermore, pathogenicity seemed to vary among these four strains. Strain 06Ⅱ (clade 1) appeared to be of median virulence, as there were 2 deaths and one chicken with urate deposition in the kidney in the challenge group. The pathogenicity of the JS41 strain seemed to be the lowest, as the lesions in the trachea and kidney were much milder than in the other groups. GD4 (clade 4) and JS5 (clade 5) were both high-pathogenicity strains among the four strains. The GD4 strain induced severe laryngeal hemorrhaging, The JS5 strain showed a likely tropism for the kidney, as evidenced by 4 out of 5 chickens displaying “spotted kidney,” with only one exception. In the histopathology of the trachea, both the GD4 and JS5 strains caused significant thickening of the lamina propria of the tracheal mucosa and sloughing of epithelial cells. In the kidney, mesenchymal dilation and hemorrhage were also easily observed in the C4 group.

Ciliostasis and viral shedding are commonly used to assess chickens’ immunity to IBV challenge [9, 11]. In line with clinical observations, the QXL87-vaccinated groups demonstrated both superior ciliary activity and reduction in viral shedding compared to the challenge groups. Adhering to the criteria for vaccine quality, QXL87 exhibited the potential to confer sufficient protection against those four strains, providing that at least 80% of vaccinated chickens display normal ciliary function. It’s worth noting that the criteria we used were in accordance with the European Pharmacopoeia. It appeared that the level of ciliary protection provided by QXL87 differs among various groups due to variations in tracheal gross lesions. To precisely quantify the protective efficacy, we calculated the mean ciliary protection scores for each group following established methods [13]. The protection rates for both the JS41 and JS5 strains were above 80%. It is worth noting that the protection rates for the 06Ⅱ strains (79.33%) and GD4 strain (74.29%) were both below 80%. This suggests that they may have a higher potential to evade the immunoreaction induced by QXL87. Given that clade 4 was one of the most prevalent clades from 2018 to 2022, it is important to prioritize epidemiological surveillance and pathogenicity testing of strains in clade 4 in the future. Viral shedding usually peaks at 5 dpc or 7dpc when chickens are infected with IBV. In our study, the viral genome loads in the vaccinated groups were all significantly lower than in the challenge groups at 5 dpc or 7dpc. It is worth noting that viral genomes could not be detected after 3 dpc in the oral swabs in V1 group, indicating efficient protection from QXL87. In terms of the prevalent clades (2 and 4) in 2018–2022 as mentioned above, viral shedding was effectively restricted in the vaccinated chickens at each timepoint in the cloaca. Considering GI-19 lineage IBV mainly inflicts harm on the reproductive system of chickens, long-term impacts of strains in clades and the efficacy of QXL87 vaccine need to be further assessed in the future.

Highly variable regions (HVRs) are known to contain antigen-related, serotype-specific, and neutralizing epitopes, located at amino acid residues 50–86, 104–138, and 272–291, as previously discussed [40, 45, 55]. Additionally, specific neutralizing epitopes have been identified [45, 58]. To investigate the potential reasons for the varying efficacy of QXL87, we conducted a comparative analysis of amino acid sequences between four isolates and QXL87, revealing mutations in the S1 region. Notably, 50 out of 56 mutation sites were located in crucial regions of S1, including HVRs, neutralizing epitopes, and the receptor-binding domain (RBD), potentially impacting antigen relatedness. RBD remained the most hypervariable region as 33/56 mutation sites located in this region. There were 27 sites falling within known neutralizing epitopes, likely reducing vaccine efficacy, while no mutations were observed in the Epitope (412–418). However, HVR Ⅲ seems to be the most variable among three HVRs among 5 clades, since it owned the least amino acids (20 aa) but the most mutation sites (n = 6). Among the four strains, JS41 exhibited the highest number of mutations but the least specific mutations, possibly explaining its superior efficacy (90.31%) in ciliary activity. The varying efficacy of QXL87 against the four isolates appears to be closely linked to specific amino acid mutations in these isolates, as the amino acid identity between them and QXL87 ranged around 94% (94.8%, 93.7%, 94.3%, and 94.3%). Previous studies have shown that changing amino acids from MLQ (IBV-M41) to KIP (QX-IBV) at sites 110 to 112 can enhance kidney binding [6]. Interestingly, the amino acids at these sites in the JS5 strain were MIP, while other isolates and QXL87 had MIA, potentially contributing to the higher kidney tropism observed in the JS5 strain. Overall, the genetic drift and potential pathogenicity changes seemed to be closely related with these mutations in S1 gene.

Since China leads the world in both the number of layers and egg production, the identification of five clades and the confirmed efficacy of QXL87 provide critical insights for tailoring future vaccines. Given the rapid evolution of IBV, it is difficult to predict how long will QXL87 remain effective. Regular surveillance and evaluation of vaccine protective efficacy are necessary in order to preclude immune evasion of variants. It is not advisable to update vaccines blindly in that it may accelerate recombinant events [2]. Updating vaccines would be necessary only when the challenge-protection evaluations proves that the QXL87 vaccine can’t provide sufficient protection. Also, QXL87 vaccine has great potential as a core vaccine to develop “protectotype vaccines” or other “multi-monovalent” vaccinations to address the complex prevalence situation of IBV in China.

Conclusion

In our study, five clades in GⅠ-19 lineage IBV were identified in China, with the QXL87 vaccine belonging to clade 3. Clade 2 and 4 were particularly prevalent during the period of 2018–2022. Our findings demonstrate that the QXL87 vaccine confers sufficient protection against four specific strains (in clade 1, 2, 4, and 5), although the protection rates differed among different clades. Nevertheless, regular surveillance and evaluation of vaccine protective efficacy are necessary as the GⅠ-19 lineage IBV may evolve further in antigenicity and pathogenicity.

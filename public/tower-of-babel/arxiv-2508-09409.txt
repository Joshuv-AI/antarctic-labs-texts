Abstract
This paper studies the existence and global stability of generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process for affine stochastic functional differential equations. Various very basic and important properties are established. In the applications, we present a standard and rigorous procedure for guaranteeing the existence and uniqueness of random equilibria for nonlinear stochastic functional differential equations, which attracts all pull-back trajectories in different types of convergence. Some examples are given to illustrate our main results. The results presented in this paper improve and simplify the conclusions of Jiang and Lv [ SIAM J. Control Optim. , 54 (2016), pp. 2383-2402] and [ J. Differential Equations , 367 (2023), pp. 890-921].

Let
X
be a complete separable metric space (i.e., Polish space) equipped with the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{B}(X)
and
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space.

Definition 2.1
.

\theta\equiv\bigl(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P},\{\theta_{t},t\in\mathbb{R}\}\bigr)
is called a metric dynamical system (MDS) if
\theta
is a measurable flow:

\theta:\mathbb{R}\times\Omega\mapsto\Omega,\qquad\theta_{0}={\rm id},\qquad\theta_{t_{2}}\circ\theta_{t_{1}}=\theta_{t_{1}+t_{2}}

for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, which is
\bigl(\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F}\bigr)
-measurable. Moreover, we assume that
\theta_{t}\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 2.2
.

An RDS on the state space
X
with an MDS
\theta
is a mapping

\varphi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega\times X\mapsto X,\quad(t,\omega,x)\mapsto\varphi(t,\omega,x),

which is
\bigl(\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\otimes\mathscr{F}\otimes\mathscr{B}(X),\mathscr{B}(X)\bigr)
-measurable such that for all
\omega\in\Omega
,

(i)

\varphi(0,\omega,\cdot)
is the identity on
X
;

(ii)

\varphi(t_{1}+t_{2},\omega,x)=\varphi\bigl(t_{2},\theta_{t_{1}}\omega,\varphi(t_{1},\omega,x)\bigr)
for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
x\in X
;

(iii)

the mapping
\varphi(t,\omega,\cdot):X\to X
is continuous for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Definition 2.3
.

A random variable
R(\omega)
is said to be tempered with respect to the MDS
\theta\equiv\bigl(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P},\{\theta_{t},t\in\mathbb{R}\}\bigr)
if

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\bigl\{e^{-\gamma|t|}|R(\theta_{t}\omega)|\bigr\}<\infty\quad for\ \ all\ \ \omega\in\Omega\ \ and\ \ \gamma>0.

Definition 2.4
.

A random variable
v:\Omega\rightarrow X
is said to be a random equilibrium (or stationary solution) of the RDS
(\theta,\varphi)
if it is invariant with respect to
(\theta,\varphi)
, i.e.

\varphi(t,\omega)v(\omega)=v(\theta_{t}\omega)\quad for\ \ all\ \ t\geq 0\ \ and\ \ \omega\in\Omega.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose that
\alpha_{0}<0
and there exists a constant
\alpha\in(0,-\alpha_{0})
such that
Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}-\alpha<0
, then for any
\xi\in C_{\tau}
,

\sup_{t\geq 0}\|\psi(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,\xi)\|_{C_{\tau}}\leq R_{\xi}(\omega)

(72)

for all
\omega\in\Omega
, where
R_{\xi}(\omega)
is a tempered random variable. Here, the random variable
R_{\xi}(\omega)
depends on
\xi
and
L
is the Lipschitz constant given in (
60
).

Proof.

Let us first observe that for any
\xi\in C_{\tau}
, there exists a tempered random variable
R_{\xi}^{1}(\omega)
such that

\sup_{t\geq\tau}\|\psi(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,\xi)\|_{C_{\tau}}\leq R_{\xi}^{1}(\omega),\ \omega\in\Omega.

(73)

By (
14
), we define

\phi\bigl(t,\omega,\xi-U(\omega)\bigr)=\psi(t,\omega,\xi)-\varphi\bigl(t,\omega,U(\omega)\bigr)=\psi(t,\omega,\xi)-U(\theta_{t}\omega),

(74)

which together with (
19
), (
58
) and Itô’s formula gives that

\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\Bigl[\phi\bigl(t,\omega,\phi_{0}(\omega)\bigr)(0)\Bigr]=L\Bigl[\phi\bigl(t,\omega,\phi_{0}(\omega)\bigr)\Bigr]+f\Bigl(\phi\bigl(t,\omega,\phi_{0}(\omega)\bigr)+U(\theta_{t}\omega)\Bigr),

(75)

where
\phi_{0}(\omega)=\xi-U(\omega)
.

For
t\geq\tau
and
-\tau\leq s\leq 0
, using the variation-of-constants formula, see Theorem 1.2 in
[
17
, Chapter 6]
, it follows immediately that

\displaystyle\phi\bigl(t,\omega,\phi_{0}(\omega)\bigr)(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r(t+s)\phi_{0}(\omega)(0)+\int_{-\tau}^{0}\int_{-\tau}^{u}r(t+s+\rho-u)\mu(d\rho)\phi_{0}(\omega)(u)du

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t+s}r(t+s-\rho)f\Bigl(\phi\bigl(\rho,\omega,\phi_{0}(\omega)\bigr)+U(\theta_{\rho}\omega)\Bigr)d\rho

(76)

and then

\displaystyle\left\|\phi\bigl(t,\omega,\phi_{0}(\omega)\bigr)\right\|_{C_{\tau}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{-\tau\leq s\leq 0}\left|\phi\bigl(t,\omega,\phi_{0}(\omega)\bigr)(s)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}e^{-\alpha t}\|\phi_{0}(\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}+\tau e^{2\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}e^{-\alpha t}|\mu|\bigl([-\tau,0]\bigr)\cdot\|\phi_{0}(\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}

\displaystyle+e^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}e^{-\alpha t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\alpha\rho}\bigl(L\|U(\theta_{\rho}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}+|f(0)|\bigr)d\rho

\displaystyle+Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}e^{-\alpha t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\alpha\rho}\left\|\phi\bigl(\rho,\omega,\phi_{0}(\omega)\bigr)\right\|_{C_{\tau}}d\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ke^{-\alpha t}\|\phi_{0}(\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}+e^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}e^{-\alpha t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\alpha\rho}\bigl(L\|U(\theta_{\rho}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}+|f(0)|\bigr)d\rho

\displaystyle+Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}e^{-\alpha t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\alpha\rho}\left\|\phi\bigl(\rho,\omega,\phi_{0}(\omega)\bigr)\right\|_{C_{\tau}}d\rho,

(77)

where

K=K(\alpha,\tau,\mu)=e^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}+\tau e^{2\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}|\mu|\bigl([-\tau,0]\bigr).

Therefore, it is obvious that

\displaystyle e^{\alpha t}\left\|\phi\bigl(t,\omega,\phi_{0}(\omega)\bigr)\right\|_{C_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K\|\phi_{0}(\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}+e^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\alpha\rho}\bigl(L\|U(\theta_{\rho}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}+|f(0)|\bigr)d\rho

\displaystyle+Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\alpha\rho}\left\|\phi\bigl(\rho,\omega,\phi_{0}(\omega)\bigr)\right\|_{C_{\tau}}d\rho.

(78)

Combining this and the Gronwall inequality, we can easily obtain that

\displaystyle e^{\alpha t}\left\|\phi\bigl(t,\omega,\phi_{0}(\omega)\bigr)\right\|_{C_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K\|\phi_{0}(\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}e^{Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}t}+e^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\alpha\rho}e^{Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}(t-\rho)}\bigl(L\|U(\theta_{\rho}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}+|f(0)|\bigr)d\rho

(79)

and then

\displaystyle\left\|\phi\bigl(t,\omega,\phi_{0}(\omega)\bigr)\right\|_{C_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K\|\phi_{0}(\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}e^{-(\alpha-Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha})t}

\displaystyle+e^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(\alpha-Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha})(t-\rho)}\bigl(L\|U(\theta_{\rho}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}+|f(0)|\bigr)d\rho,\ t\geq\tau.

(80)

This together with definitions of
\theta
and
\phi_{0}
shows that

\displaystyle\left\|\phi\bigl(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,\phi_{0}(\theta_{-t}\omega)\bigr)\right\|_{C_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K\|\phi_{0}(\theta_{-t}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}e^{-(\alpha-Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha})t}

\displaystyle+e^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(\alpha-Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha})(t-\rho)}\bigl(L\|U(\theta_{-(t-\rho)}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}+|f(0)|\bigr)d\rho

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K\|\xi\|_{C_{\tau}}e^{-(\alpha-Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha})t}+K\|U(\theta_{-t}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}e^{-(\alpha-Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha})t}

\displaystyle+e^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(\alpha-Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha})(t-\rho)}\bigl(L\|U(\theta_{-(t-\rho)}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}+|f(0)|\bigr)d\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K\|\xi\|_{C_{\tau}}e^{-(\alpha-Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha})t}+Ke^{-(\alpha-Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha})t}\|U(\theta_{-t}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}

\displaystyle+e^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}\int_{-t}^{0}e^{(\alpha-Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha})\rho}\bigl(L\|U(\theta_{\rho}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}+|f(0)|\bigr)d\rho,\ t\geq\tau.

(81)

Since
Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}-\alpha<0
and
U(\omega)
is tempered (see Proposition
2.1
), we set

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}_{\xi}(\omega)=

\displaystyle K\|\xi\|_{C_{\tau}}+K\sup_{t\geq 0}\left\{e^{-(\alpha-Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha})t}\|U(\theta_{-t}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}\right\}

\displaystyle+e^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{(\alpha-Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha})\rho}\bigl(L\|U(\theta_{\rho}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}+|f(0)|\bigr)d\rho

(82)

and then

\sup_{t\geq\tau}\left\|\phi\bigl(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,\phi_{0}(\theta_{-t}\omega)\bigr)\right\|_{C_{\tau}}\leq\widetilde{R}_{\xi}(\omega),

(83)

where we can see that the random variable
\widetilde{R}_{\xi}(\omega)
is well defined for all
\omega\in\Omega
. In what follows, we will check that
\widetilde{R}_{\xi}(\omega)
is tempered. To this end, set
\delta=\alpha-Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}>0
, we only need to prove that for all
\omega\in\Omega
and
\gamma>0

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-\gamma|t|}\sup_{u\geq 0}\left\{e^{-\delta u}\|U(\theta_{-u}\circ\theta_{t}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}\right\}\right\}<\infty

(84)

and

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-\gamma|t|}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{\delta\rho}\|U(\theta_{\rho}\circ\theta_{t}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}d\rho\right\}<\infty.

(85)

In fact, for any
\gamma>0
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-\gamma|t|}\sup_{u\geq 0}\left\{e^{-\delta u}\|U(\theta_{-u}\circ\theta_{t}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}\right\}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-\gamma|t|}\sup_{u\geq 0}\left\{e^{-(\delta\wedge\gamma)u}\|U(\theta_{-u}\circ\theta_{t}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}\right\}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-\gamma|t|}e^{(\delta\wedge\gamma)|t|}\sup_{u\geq 0}\left\{e^{-(\delta\wedge\gamma)u}e^{-(\delta\wedge\gamma)|t|}\|U(\theta_{t-u}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}\right\}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-\gamma|t|}e^{(\delta\wedge\gamma)|t|}\sup_{u\geq 0}\left\{e^{-(\delta\wedge\gamma)|t-u|}\|U(\theta_{t-u}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}\right\}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-(\delta\wedge\gamma)|u|}\|U(\theta_{u}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}\right\}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\infty,

(86)

which is based on (
17
). On the other hand, for any
\gamma>0
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-\gamma|t|}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{\delta\rho}\|U(\theta_{\rho}\circ\theta_{t}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}d\rho\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-\gamma|t|}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-\frac{\delta}{2}|\rho|}e^{-(\frac{\delta}{2}\wedge\gamma)|\rho|}\|U(\theta_{\rho+t}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}d\rho\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-\gamma|t|}e^{(\frac{\delta}{2}\wedge\gamma)|t|}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-\frac{\delta}{2}|\rho|}e^{-(\frac{\delta}{2}\wedge\gamma)|\rho+t|}\|U(\theta_{\rho+t}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}d\rho\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-(\frac{\delta}{2}\wedge\gamma)|u|}\|U(\theta_{u}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}\right\}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-\frac{\delta}{2}|\rho|}d\rho

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\infty.

(87)

That is, the random variable
\widetilde{R}_{\xi}(\omega)
is tempered. Thus, from (
74
) and (
83
), we have immediately that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq\tau}\|\psi(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,\xi)\|_{C_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq\tau}\left\|\phi\bigl(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,\phi_{0}(\theta_{-t}\omega)\bigr)\right\|_{C_{\tau}}+\left\|U(\omega)\right\|_{C_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}_{\xi}(\omega)+\left\|U(\omega)\right\|_{C_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle R_{\xi}^{1}(\omega),\quad\omega\in\Omega,

(88)

which proves (
73
). Our next claim is that there exists another tempered random variable
R_{\xi}^{2}(\omega)
such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq\tau}\|\psi(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,\xi)\|_{C_{\tau}}\leq R_{\xi}^{2}(\omega),\quad\omega\in\Omega.

(89)

Note that for
0\leq t\leq\tau
and
-\tau\leq s\leq 0
,

\psi(t,\omega,\xi)(s)=\xi(t+s),\quad\omega\in\Omega,

(90)

which implies that

\displaystyle\|\psi(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,\xi)\|_{C_{\tau}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{-\tau\leq s\leq 0}|\psi(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,\xi)(s)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{-\tau\leq s\leq-t}|\xi(t+s)|+\sup_{-t\leq s\leq 0}|\psi(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,\xi)(s)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\xi\|_{C_{\tau}}+\|\psi(\tau,\theta_{-t}\omega,\xi)\|_{C_{\tau}},\quad\omega\in\Omega.

(91)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq\tau}\|\psi(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,\xi)\|_{C_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\xi\|_{C_{\tau}}+\sup_{0\leq t\leq\tau}\|\psi(\tau,\theta_{-t}\omega,\xi)\|_{C_{\tau}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\xi\|_{C_{\tau}}+\sup_{0\leq t\leq\tau}\|\psi(\tau,\theta_{-\tau}\circ\theta_{\tau-t}\omega,\xi)\|_{C_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\xi\|_{C_{\tau}}+\sup_{0\leq t\leq\tau}R_{\xi}^{1}(\theta_{\tau-t}\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\xi\|_{C_{\tau}}+\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]\cap\mathbb{Q}}R_{\xi}^{1}(\theta_{\tau-t}\omega)

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle R_{\xi}^{2}(\omega),\quad\omega\in\Omega.

(92)

Here, we observe the fact that the mapping
t\rightarrow U(\theta_{t}\omega)
is continuous, which together with (
5
) and (
5
) yields the continuity of
R_{\xi}^{1}(\theta_{t}\omega)
with respect to
t
and the measurability of
R_{\xi}^{2}(\omega)
.

Actually,
R_{\xi}^{2}(\omega)
is also tempered. For any
\gamma>0
and
\omega\in\Omega
, using (
5
) and (
5
), it is evident that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-\gamma|t|}\sup_{u\in[0,\tau]\cap\mathbb{Q}}R_{\xi}^{1}(\theta_{\tau-u}\circ\theta_{t}\omega)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-\gamma|t|}\sup_{u\in[0,\tau]\cap\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{R}_{\xi}(\theta_{t+\tau-u}\omega)\right\}+\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-\gamma|t|}\sup_{u\in[0,\tau]\cap\mathbb{Q}}\|U(\theta_{t+\tau-u}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-\gamma|t|}\sup_{u\in[0,\tau]\cap\mathbb{Q}}e^{\gamma|t+\tau-u|}\right\}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-\gamma|t|}\widetilde{R}_{\xi}(\theta_{t}\omega)\right\}

\displaystyle+\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-\gamma|t|}\sup_{u\in[0,\tau]\cap\mathbb{Q}}e^{\gamma|t+\tau-u|}\right\}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-\gamma|t|}\|U(\theta_{t}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{\gamma\tau}\left(\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-\gamma|t|}\widetilde{R}_{\xi}(\theta_{t}\omega)\right\}+\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-\gamma|t|}\|U(\theta_{t}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\infty.

(93)

Finally, let
R_{\xi}(\omega)=\max\{R_{\xi}^{1}(\omega),R_{\xi}^{2}(\omega)\}
for all
\omega\in\Omega
, which together with (
73
) and (
5
) ends the proof of Lemma
5.2
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Under the same assumptions presented in Lemma
5.2
, for any
\xi\in C_{\tau}
and
\omega\in\Omega
, there exists an
\mathscr{F}_{-}
-measurable random variable
V_{\xi}:\Omega\rightarrow C_{\tau}
such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\psi(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,\xi)(\bullet)=V_{\xi}(\omega)(\bullet)

(94)

in
C_{\tau}
. Moreover, the random variable
V_{\xi}
is a tempered random equilibrium of the RDS
(\theta,\psi)
.

Proof.

Choose any
\tau\leq t_{1}<t_{2}
, using the cocycle property, Lemma
5.1
and Lemma
5.2
, we can easily conclude that

\displaystyle\|\psi(t_{2},\theta_{-t_{2}}\omega,\xi)-\psi(t_{1},\theta_{-t_{1}}\omega,\xi)\|_{C_{\tau}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\psi\bigl(t_{1},\theta_{-t_{1}}\omega,\psi(t_{2}-t_{1},\theta_{-t_{2}}\omega,\xi)\bigr)-\psi(t_{1},\theta_{-t_{1}}\omega,\xi)\|_{C_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ke^{-(\alpha-Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha})t_{1}}\|\psi(t_{2}-t_{1},\theta_{-(t_{2}-t_{1})}\circ\theta_{-t_{1}}\omega,\xi)-\xi\|_{C_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ke^{-(\alpha-Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha})t_{1}}\left(\sup_{t\geq 0}\|\psi(t,\theta_{-t}\circ\theta_{-t_{1}}\omega,\xi)\|_{C_{\tau}}+\|\xi\|_{C_{\tau}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ke^{-(\alpha-Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha})t_{1}}\bigl(R_{\xi}(\theta_{-t_{1}}\omega)+\|\xi\|_{C_{\tau}}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ke^{-\frac{\alpha-Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}}{2}t_{1}}\left(\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-\frac{\alpha-Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}}{2}|t|}R_{\xi}(\theta_{t}\omega)\right\}+\|\xi\|_{C_{\tau}}\right)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow 0\quad{\rm as}\quad t_{1}\rightarrow\infty.

(95)

That is, for fixed
\xi\in C_{\tau}
and
\omega\in\Omega
,
\{\psi(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,\xi):t\geq 0\}
is Cauchy in the Banach space
C_{\tau}
, which gives (
94
).

In addition, due to the fact that for all
-\tau\leq s\leq 0
,
\psi(t,\omega,\xi)(s)
is
\mathscr{F}_{0}^{t}
-adapted, where
\mathscr{F}_{0}^{t}=\sigma\{B(u):0\leq u\leq t\}
. This together with Lemma II.2.1 in
[
31
]
shows that
\psi(t,\bullet,\xi):\Omega\rightarrow C_{\tau}
is
\mathscr{F}_{0}^{t}
-measurable. Note that

\theta_{-t}^{-1}\mathscr{F}_{0}^{t}=\mathscr{F}_{-t}^{0}=\sigma\{B(u):-t\leq u\leq 0\}\subset\mathscr{F}_{-},

(96)

which implies that
\psi(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,\xi)
is
\mathscr{F}_{-}
-measurable for all
t\geq 0
. Consequently, the random variable
V_{\xi}:\Omega\rightarrow C_{\tau}
is also
\mathscr{F}_{-}
-measurable.

Finally, using the similar argument in the proof of (
3
), it is clear that
V_{\xi}
is a random equilibrium of
(\theta,\psi)
. Moreover, by (
5
) and (
94
), we can deduce easily that

\|V_{\xi}(\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}\leq R_{\xi}^{1}(\omega),\quad\omega\in\Omega,

(97)

where the random variable
R_{\xi}^{1}(\omega)
is tempered given in (
5
). The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Under the same assumptions presented in Lemma
5.2
, for any two tempered random equilibria
V_{1}(\omega)
and
V_{2}(\omega)
, then

V_{1}(\omega)=V_{2}(\omega),\quad\omega\in\Omega.

(98)

Proof.

Since
V_{1}(\omega)
and
V_{2}(\omega)
are two tempered random equilibria of
(\theta,\psi)
, it follows that

\psi\bigl(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,V_{i}(\theta_{-t}\omega)\bigr)=V_{i}(\omega),\quad i=1,2,\ t\geq 0,

and there exists a tempered random variable
R:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\|V_{1}(\omega)-V_{2}(\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}\leq R(\omega)

for all
\omega\in\Omega
. By (
67
), it is obvious that

\displaystyle\|V_{1}(\omega)-V_{2}(\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\psi\bigl(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,V_{1}(\theta_{-t}\omega)\bigr)-\psi\bigl(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,V_{2}(\theta_{-t}\omega)\bigr)\|_{C_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K\|V_{1}(\theta_{-t}\omega)-V_{2}(\theta_{-t}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}e^{-(\alpha-Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha})t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{e^{-\frac{\delta}{2}|u|}R(\theta_{u}\omega)\right\}e^{-\frac{\delta}{2}t},

(99)

where
\delta=\alpha-Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}
,
t\geq\tau
and
\omega\in\Omega
. Let
t\rightarrow\infty
in (
5
), the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

For any
\gamma>0
, then

\mathbb{E}\left|\sup_{t\geq 0}\left\{e^{-\gamma t}\|U(\theta_{-t}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}\right\}\right|^{2}<\infty.

(101)

Proof.

From (
3
), it is evident that for
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle e^{-\gamma t}\|U(\theta_{-t}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\Sigma\|\cdot e^{-\gamma t}\sup_{-\tau\leq s\leq 0}|B(s-t,\omega)|+\|\Sigma\|\cdot e^{-\gamma t}|B(-t,\omega)|

\displaystyle+\sup_{-\tau\leq s\leq 0}\int_{-\infty}^{s}\left\|\int_{-\tau}^{0}\mu(d\rho)r(s-u+\rho)\right\|\cdot\|\Sigma\|\cdot e^{-\gamma t}|B(u-t,\omega)|du

\displaystyle+\left(\sup_{-\tau\leq s\leq 0}\int_{-\infty}^{s}\left\|\int_{-\tau}^{0}\mu(d\rho)r(s-u+\rho)\right\|\cdot\|\Sigma\|du\right)e^{-\gamma t}|B(-t,\omega)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\Sigma\|e^{\gamma\tau}\sup_{t\geq 0}\left\{e^{-\gamma t}|B(-t,\omega)|\right\}+\|\Sigma\|\sup_{t\geq 0}\left\{e^{-\gamma t}|B(-t,\omega)|\right\}

\displaystyle+e^{2\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}\|\Sigma\|\cdot|\mu|\bigl([-\tau,0]\bigr)\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{\alpha u}e^{-\gamma t}|B(u-t,\omega)|du

\displaystyle+e^{2\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}\|\Sigma\|\cdot|\mu|\bigl([-\tau,0]\bigr)\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{\alpha u}du\cdot e^{-\gamma t}|B(-t,\omega)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\Sigma\|e^{\gamma\tau}\sup_{t\geq 0}\left\{e^{-\gamma t}|B(-t,\omega)|\right\}+\|\Sigma\|\sup_{t\geq 0}\left\{e^{-\gamma t}|B(-t,\omega)|\right\}

\displaystyle+e^{2\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}\|\Sigma\|\cdot|\mu|\bigl([-\tau,0]\bigr)\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{(\alpha-\frac{\gamma\wedge\alpha}{2})u}du\cdot\sup_{t\geq 0}\left\{e^{-\frac{\gamma\wedge\alpha}{2}t}|B(-t,\omega)|\right\}

\displaystyle+e^{2\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}\|\Sigma\|\cdot|\mu|\bigl([-\tau,0]\bigr)\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{\alpha u}du\cdot\sup_{t\geq 0}\left\{e^{-\gamma t}|B(-t,\omega)|\right\},

(102)

which implies that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\left\{e^{-\gamma t}\|U(\theta_{-t}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K_{1}\sup_{t\geq 0}\left\{e^{-\gamma t}|B(-t,\omega)|\right\}+K_{2}\sup_{t\geq 0}\left\{e^{-\frac{\gamma\wedge\alpha}{2}t}|B(-t,\omega)|\right\}.

(103)

Here,
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are two positive constants. Similar to the proof in (
3
), the proof of (
101
) is straightforward.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.
By (
5
) and the same method as in Theorem
2.2
, we are reduced to prove that
R_{\xi}^{1}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}_{+})
, where
R_{\xi}^{1}(\omega)=\widetilde{R}_{\xi}(\omega)+\left\|U(\omega)\right\|_{C_{\tau}}
. Therefore, we are left with the task of showing that
\widetilde{R}_{\xi}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Note that by (
5
),

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}_{\xi}(\omega)=

\displaystyle K\|\xi\|_{C_{\tau}}+K\sup_{t\geq 0}\left\{e^{-\delta t}\|U(\theta_{-t}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}\right\}

\displaystyle+e^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{\delta\rho}\bigl(L\|U(\theta_{\rho}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}+|f(0)|\bigr)d\rho

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K\|\xi\|_{C_{\tau}}+K\sup_{t\geq 0}\left\{e^{-\delta t}\|U(\theta_{-t}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}\right\}

\displaystyle+e^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{\frac{\delta}{2}\rho}d\rho\left(L\sup_{t\geq 0}\left\{e^{-\frac{\delta}{2}t}\|U(\theta_{-t}\omega)\|_{C_{\tau}}\right\}+|f(0)|\right),

where
\delta=\alpha-Le^{\alpha\tau}C_{\alpha}>0
. Using Lemma
5.5
, it follows obviously that
\mathbb{E}|\widetilde{R}_{\xi}|^{2}<\infty
. The proof is complete.

7
Discussion and open problems

In this paper, we have considered the global stability of stationary solutions for affine and nonlinear SFDEs with additive white noise. In Theorem
4.1
, we assumed that the time delay can not be too large. In fact, in
[
29
]
, we have showed that the existence of globally attracting stationary solutions for some semilinear SDDEs with additive white noise can be obtained regardless of the value of
\tau
. Based on some numerical evidences, we leave it as an open problem whether the same conclusion of Theorem
4.1
holds for large delays. In the meantime, the method presented in the proof of Theorem
4.1
may be applied to investigate nonlinear SFDEs satisfying a one-sided Lipschitz condition. Finally, it is left as an open problem to establish similar results in other situations, such as multiplicative noise. These important problems will be the subject of subsequent research.

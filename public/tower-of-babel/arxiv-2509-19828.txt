Boundary effect on asymptotic behaviour of solution to the hyperbolic-parabolic chemotaxis system
Nangao Zhang

Abstract




Abstract:

This paper is concerned with the global existence and stability of solution to the quasi-linear hyperbolic-parabolic chemotaxis system on the half-line, which was proposed in
[
1
]
to primarily describe the formation of coherent vascular networks observed in vitro experiment.
Under two different boundary conditions, we first establish the existence and uniqueness of the solution of the asymptotic state equation (the so-called nonlinear diffusion wave), and then prove that the solution of the original equation is nonlinearly asymptotically stable.
Additionally, we obtain the convergence rates for both types of boundary conditions.

Key words:
Hyperbolic-parabolic chemotaxis system, half-line, nonlinear diffusion wave.

AMS Subject Classification:
35A01, 35B40, 35B45, 35K57, 92C17.

1
Introduction

Angiogenesis refers to the process of forming new blood vessels from existing capillaries or posterior capillaries, which is a complex process involving many cells and molecules.
In recent years, due to its importance in pathological and physiological conditions, this phenomenon has become the subject of many experimental studies.
In order to study this complex phenomenon and accurately describe the key features of human vascular experiments in vitro, Ambrosi et al. (cf.
[
1
,
10
]
) proposed the following quasi-linear hyperbolic parabolic syatem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot\left(\rho u\right)=0,\\[5.69054pt]
\partial_{t}\left(\rho u\right)+\nabla\cdot\left(\rho u\otimes u\right)+\nabla p(\rho)=\mu\rho\nabla\phi-\alpha\rho u,\\[5.69054pt]
\partial_{t}\phi=D\Delta\phi+a\rho-b\phi,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}~(n\geq 1)
.
The unknown functions
\rho=\rho(x,t)\geq 0
,
u=u(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\phi=\phi(x,t)\geq 0
represent the density, velocity of endothelial cells and the concentration of the chemoattractant respectively.
The density-dependent quantity
p(\rho)
is the pressure function which satisfies
p^{\prime}(\rho)>0
for
\rho>0
.
In addition,
\alpha>0
is the damping coefficient,
\mu>0
is the strength of cells response to the chemoattractant concentration gradient,
D>0
is the diffusion coefficient of the chemoattractant, and
a>0
and
b>0
denote the secretion and death rates of the chemoattractant respectively.

We first give a brief review of the current research status of system (
1.1
).
As an important class of biological models, this system has attracted widespread attention from numerous researchers since its proposal.
However, due to the particularity of its structure, studies on this system remain very limited and far from satisfactory.
Di Russo et al.
[
5
,
6
]
proved that in multidimensional cases, system (
1.1
) admits global strong solutions without vacuum by combining characteristics of the hyperbolic part and the parabolic part; these solutions converge to constant states, and exhibit algebraic decay if the perturbations are sufficiently small.
Chavanis et al.
[
3
]
studied the relationship between (
1.1
) and the standard Keller-Segel system through asymptotic analysis, suggesting that the solutions of the system share the same limit under the strong friction limit.
Di Francesco et al.
[
4
]
rigorously justified the conclusions in
[
3
]
by using energy methods and compensated compactness tools.
Later, Hong et al.
[
11
]
investigated the existence and stability of phase transition steady states for (
1.1
) on the half-line
\mathbb{R}^{+}
with Dirichlet boundary conditions.
Recently, Liu et al. (cf.
[
7
,
16
,
17
]
) proved that for the Cauchy problem of equation (
1.1
) in three or one spatial dimension, the solution exists globally and tends respectively toward a linear diffusion wave or a nonlinear diffusion wave. Liu et al.
[
18
]
studied the relaxation limit problem of solution in three-dimensional space by using energy method.
For some results concerning numerical simulations of this system, refer to
[
25
,
9
,
15
,
2
]
and references therein.

In physics, damping effect usually leads to nonlinear diffusion in dynamic system. The strict mathematical proof of this inference was first discovered by Hsiao and Liu
[
12
]
in their research on damped compressible Euler equations in the whole space. Subsequently, by applying more detailed energy estimates, Nishihara et al.
[
27
,
28
]
succeeded in improving the convergence rates to be optimal for the same problem.
For the case on the half-line, Nishihara and Yang
[
29
]
also obtained similar conclusions. Since then, many researchers have made a series of improvements from different aspects, refer to
[
13
,
14
,
19
,
20
,
23
,
21
,
24
,
30
]
and the references therein.
The purpose of this paper is to study this kind of diffusion phenomenon in quasi-linear hyperbolic-parabolic equations on a half-line.

To demonstrate our ideas, we use variables
(\rho,m,\phi)(x,t)
instead of
(\rho,u,\phi)(x,t)
, where
m=\rho u
denotes the momentum of cells, and then rewrite the equations (
1.1
) for
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
as the following form:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\rho_{t}+m_{x}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
m_{t}+\left(\frac{m^{2}}{\rho}\right)_{x}+p(\rho)_{x}=\mu\rho\phi_{x}-\alpha m,\\[5.69054pt]
\phi_{t}=D\phi_{xx}+a\rho-b\phi.\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

The initial data is given by

(\rho,m,\phi)(x,t)|_{t=0}=(\rho_{0},m_{0},\phi_{0})(x)\to(\rho_{+},m_{+},\phi_{+}),\;\;\;\mbox{as}\;\;\;x\to+\infty,

(1.3)

with the following boundary conditions

m(0,t)=0,\ \ \ \phi_{x}(0,t)=0,

(1.4)

or

m_{x}(0,t)=0,\ \ \ \phi(0,t)=\frac{a}{b}\rho_{0}(0).

(1.5)

Here
\rho_{+}>0
,
m_{+}
and
\phi_{+}
are constants.

According to the method of asymptotic analysis, the solution
\left(\rho,m,\phi\right)(x,t)
of the initial-boundary value problem (IBVP) (
1.2
)–(
1.4
) or (
1.2
)–(
1.3
) and (
1.5
) is expected to converge to the nonlinear diffusion wave
\left(\bar{\rho},\bar{m},\bar{\phi}\right)(x,t)
, which is the solution to the following system:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\bar{\rho}_{t}+\bar{m}_{x}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
p(\bar{\rho})_{x}=\mu\bar{\rho}\bar{\phi}_{x}-\alpha\bar{m},\\[5.69054pt]
a\bar{\rho}=b\bar{\phi},\\
\end{array}\right.\;\;\;{\rm or\ equivalently,}\;\;\;\;\;\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\bar{\rho}_{t}-\frac{1}{\alpha}q(\bar{\rho})_{xx}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
\bar{m}=-\frac{1}{\alpha}q(\bar{\rho})_{x},\\[5.69054pt]
\bar{\phi}=\frac{a}{b}\bar{\rho},\\
\end{array}\right.

(1.6)

with

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\bar{\rho}_{x}(0,t)=\bar{m}(0,t)=\bar{\phi}_{x}(0,t)=0,\\[5.69054pt]
(\bar{\rho},\bar{m},\bar{\phi})(x,t)\to\left(\rho_{+},0,\frac{a}{b}\rho_{+}\right),\;\;\;\mbox{as}\;\;\;x\to+\infty,\end{array}\right.

(1.7)

or

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\bar{\rho}(0,t)=\rho_{0}(0),\bar{m}_{x}(0,t)=0,\bar{\phi}(0,t)=\frac{a}{b}\rho_{0}(0),\\[5.69054pt]
(\bar{\rho},\bar{m},\bar{\phi})(x,t)\to\left(\rho_{+},0,\frac{a}{b}\rho_{+}\right),\;\;\;\mbox{as}\;\;\;x\to+\infty.\end{array}\right.

(1.8)

Here we denote

q(\bar{\rho}):=p(\bar{\rho})-\frac{a\mu}{2b}\bar{\rho}^{2}.

With the above analysis, the first problem we face next is to give the existence and decay properties of solution to system (
1.6
)–(
1.7
) or (
1.6
) and (
1.8
).
For the first type of boundary, we will provide it through the energy method (see Lemma

); while for the second type of boundary, we will directly present it based on the definition of the self-similar solution (see (

)–(
4.2
)).
Secondly, it can be observed that there exist some gaps between the solution of the original equation and the nonlinear diffusion wave at
x=+\infty
.
Therefore, we need to construct a set of correction function
(\hat{\rho},\hat{m},\hat{\phi})(x,t)
to eliminate these gaps.
To achieve this goal, through careful observation, we first analyze the behavior of solutions to (
1.2
)–(
1.4
) or (
1.2
)–(
1.3
) and (
1.5
) at the far field,
and then guess that the correction function
(\hat{\rho},\hat{m},\hat{\phi})(x,t)
should satisfies a new system. By skillfully giving the initial value and boundary conditions of the new system, we obtain
(\hat{\rho},\hat{m},\hat{\phi})(x,t)
(see Section
2
for more details).
Consequently, we are able to prove the stability of the nonlinear diffusion wave for (
1.2
)–(
1.4
) or (
1.2
)–(
1.3
) and (
1.5
).

The arrangement of the present paper is as follows.
In Section

)–(

We prove Theorem

and Theorems
2.2

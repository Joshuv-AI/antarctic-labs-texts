Dual-Target Cardiac Regeneration: Controllable Nfyb/Nr3c1 Cointervention Spurs Cardiomyocyte Cytokinesis After Myocardial Infarction
Authors: Bingjun Lu, Wujian Liu, Ziyan Ge, Jiamin Zou, Yidan Xu, Zhuo Zhang, Lanlan Ma, Jiangcheng Shu, Yuyao Liu, Cong Chen, Juanjuan Zheng, Jia Li, Zhen Liu, Sisi Tian, Xuemei Chen, Yanli Guo, Donghui Zhang, Steven R. Houser, Wei Eric Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Myocardial infarction can cause a massive loss of functional cardiomyocytes, yet effective strategies to stimulate cardiac regeneration remain lacking. A key barrier to adult cardiomyocyte proliferation appears to be cytokinesis inhibition. This study aimed to determine whether combining proliferation stimulators with the removal of cytokinesis-inhibitory constraints could unlock regenerative potential after myocardial infarction.METHODS:Transcriptomic profile from 5 regeneration models, including Aurkb-tdTomato cytokinesis reporter mice and Myh6-MerCreMer;mosaic analysis with double markers (MADM), and cross-species comparative analysis were used to explore the regulatory networks for cardiomyocyte proliferation. MADM mice were used to evaluate cardiac regeneration by quantifying after cytokinesis new cardiomyocytes and clonal clusters.RESULTS:Integrative multimodel analysis revealed a dual regulatory control system for cardiomyocyte proliferation, along with key associated genes and transcriptional regulators. Nfyb was identified as an activator and Nr3c1 as a repressor of cardiomyocyte proliferation. Nfyb overexpression enhanced cardiomyocyte proliferation and post–myocardial infarction cardiac repair through propelling cell-cycle gene transcription. Nr3c1 inhibition promoted cardiomyocyte proliferation, improved cardiac function, and reduced infarct size. A spatiotemporally controlled adeno-associated virus 9 system combining drug-inducible Nfyb overexpression and CRISPR/enOsCas12f1–mediated Nr3c1 deletion efficiently induces cardiomyocyte proliferation. Clonal analysis using MADM mice showed that the dual intervention synergistically increased new cardiomyocyte formation (28% clustered, >28-fold versus control). The enhanced regenerative effect of the dual intervention was demonstrated in post–myocardial infarction mice and human engineered heart tissues.CONCLUSIONS:This work documents a dual-control paradigm of cardiomyocyte proliferation and establishes Nfyb/Nr3c1 cointervention as a putative new therapy to induce and control cardiac regeneration after injury.

Myocardial infarction (MI) continues to be a major cause of death worldwide. MI results in cardiomyocyte death accompanied by replacement fibrosis, leading to progressive heart failure.1 Studies have shown a low rate of cardiomyocyte turnover in humans and mammals, which is insufficient to regenerate the injured heart.2,3 Research into safe approaches to induce and control cardiac regeneration through cardiomyocyte proliferation is important for the development of needed post-MI therapies.4

Embryonic and neonatal cardiomyocytes exhibit robust proliferative capacity, in contrast to the low proliferative capacity in adults.5 Case reports of functional recovery after severe MI in newborns suggest that injured neonatal human hearts can undergo repair through cardiomyocyte proliferation.6 Studies in mice and pigs have shown that cardiomyocyte proliferative potential is largely lost during the first postnatal week,7–9 and little cardiomyocyte proliferation occurs in the adult heart after injury. Normal heart tissue from animals at different developmental ages and injured neonatal hearts has been used as model systems to explore mechanisms for reinduction of cardiomyocyte proliferation in adults.9,10

The major hurdle to adult cardiomyocyte proliferation appears to be an inability to complete cytokinesis, with only a small fraction of cardiomyocytes undergoing complete cell division, unlike the polyploid and multinucleated states typically observed.11 Elucidating the mechanisms regulating cardiomyocyte cytokinesis could enhance the ability to induce cardiomyocyte proliferation.12 The ability to identify cytokinetic cardiomyocytes should help to decipher the complex mechanisms activating and suppressing cytokinesis. Previous genetic lineage-tracing methods such as MADM (mosaic analysis with double markers) mice, were designed to label postcytokinesis progeny.13 In the present research, an Aurora kinase B reporter–based mouse system with tdTomato fluorescence labeling14 was developed, allowing multiomics analysis of cytokinetic cardiomyocytes at the single-cell level.

Emerging findings reveal that combinatorial gene targeting potently stimulates mitotic activity in adult cardiomyocytes,15–17 thereby highlighting the necessity for concerted regulation of proproliferative drivers and endogenous cytokinesis barriers to achieve cardiomyocyte renewal. We hypothesize that combining proliferation stimulators with the inhibition of cytokinesis blockers will enhance cardiac regeneration after MI in the adult heart.

In this study, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) of cardiomyocytes undergoing cytokinesis in Aurkb-tdTomato reporter mice and of postcytokinesis cardiomyocytes in MAMD mice was performed. To incorporate other regeneration models, a multimodel transcriptomic analysis was conducted. In vitro screening and in vivo experiments identified the critical transcription factors (TFs) associated with cardiomyocyte proliferation, including Nfyb as a driver and Nr3c1 as a repressor. The study shows that coordinated Nfyb/Nr3c1 modulation through dose and temporally controlled adeno-associated virus (AAV) 9 dual-target therapy potently stimulates cardiac regeneration in post-MI adult mice and in human engineered heart tissues.

Animal Models

The transgenic cardiomyocyte-specific Aurkb-tdTomato reporter mouse system was created to identify cardiomyocytes undergoing cytokinesis, and scRNA-seq was then performed. The MADM mouse system (obtained from Dr Chong Liu at the College of Medicine, Zhejiang University) was used to evaluate cardiac regeneration by quantifying new cardiomyocytes post-cytokinesis.

AAV-Mediated Gene Therapy

We used a risdiplam-responsive RNA splicing platform to drive time-restricted Nfyb overexpression. Leveraging a single AAV9-delivered CRISPR/enOsCas12f1 system, Nr3c1 is specifically knocked out in cardiomyocytes in vivo. AAVs carrying gene-interference constructs were administered to treat the MI heart. Immunostaining, echocardiography, and infarct size analysis were performed as previously reported.18

Engineered Heart Tissues

Engineered heart tissues generated from human induced pluripotent stem cell–derived cardiomyocytes (hiPSC-CMs) were applied to test cardiomyocyte proliferation and contractile function.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed with GraphPad Prism 9.0 (GraphPad Software). Continuous data with skewed distribution were log-transformed when appropriate before analysis. A Student t test was used to compare 2 groups. One-way or 2-way ANOVA (with or without repeated measures) followed by the Bonferroni correction was used for multiple-group comparisons. Statistical values are represented as mean±SEM, and results with values of P<0.05 were considered statistically significant. Categorical data were analyzed with the χ2 test or Fisher exact test if appropriate.

Proliferation-Associated Genes and Predicted Transcription Factors of Cardiomyocytes in Heart Development

Transcriptome analysis was performed across 5 cardiomyocyte regeneration models (Figure 1A): embryonic-to-adult heart development, cytokinetic cardiomyocytes from transgenic Aurkb-tdTomato-reporter and Myh6-MerCreMer;MADM mice, neonatal post-MI hearts, and hiPSC-CMs. We applied a strategy integrating multimodel transcriptomic analysis, iRegulon prediction, and phenotype validation, modified from previously reported approaches,19 to identify master regulators (Figure S1).

Using weighted correlation network analysis20 on postnatal mouse heart transcriptomes from the public dataset GSE51483, we identified gene modules characterized by shared temporal expression profiles across developmental time points. We included 39 transcriptomic profiles specifically from cardiac tissues at stages embryonic day 8.5, 9.5, 12.5, 14.5, and 18.5; postnatal day 3, and adult (Figure S2A and S2B). Weighted correlation network analysis built coexpression networks and identified 8 functional modules (Figure 1B, Figure S2C, and Table S1). Hierarchical clustering revealed an inverse relationship between blue and turquoise modules, and genes in these 2 modules showed gradual expression changes throughout cardiac development (Figure S2D and S2E). Gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis was performed separately for subclusters derived from within each weighted correlation network analysis module based on their temporal expression profiles. Functional annotation of the 2 module-enriched genes revealed significant associations with biological processes, including cell-cycle checkpoint signaling, cell-cycle phase transition, and heart contraction (Figure 1C).

The clustering in Figure S2D validated the functional coherence of the blue and turquoise modules. Following a prior strategy,19 we identified genes within the blue and turquoise modules highly correlated with the cell-cycle markers Mki67/Ccnb1, resulting in 2,135 positively and 1,672 negatively associated genes (Figure 1D, Figure S3A, and Table S1). To identify potential drivers of the proliferative gene program during cardiac development, we next performed motif enrichment analysis of the promoters of cell cycle–correlated genes, identifying 221 TFs (Figure 1E). GO enrichment identified novel proliferation-associated terms, including “macromolecule methylation,” which may promote proliferation, and “mitochondrial electron transport, NADH to ubiquinone,” which may inhibit proliferation (Figure S3B).

Proliferation-Associated Genes and Predicted TFs of Cardiomyocytes Experiencing Cytokinesis Defined by Aurkb-tdTomato and MADM Tracking Systems

Using our previously developed cardiomyocyte-specific Aurkb-tdTomato-reporter mouse system14 in which cardiomyocytes express tdTomato under the cytokinesis marker Aurkb, we observed that ≈8% of cardiomyocytes from postnatal day 1 (P1) and 6% from postnatal day 3 hearts exhibited tdTomato fluorescence (Figure S4). This proportion is consistent with prior reports.14 We then isolated tdTomato+ cells (enriched cytokinetic cardiomyocytes) and tdTomato− cells (containing control cardiomyocytes and noncardiomyocytes) from P1 and postnatal day 3 hearts by fluorescence-activated cell sorting for subsequent scRNA-seq analysis (Figure 2A). These 2 fractions were used to generate separate libraries. The differential expression analysis was strictly restricted to cardiomyocytes (tdTomato+ cardiomyocytes versus tdTomato− cardiomyocytes; Figure 2C).

Using the 10X Genomics Chromium platform combined with next-generation sequencing, we obtained 33 174 single-cell gene expression profiles from 4 different samples after filtering out low-quality cells and doublets or multiplets (Figure S5). By manually consolidating the expression of known marker genes for individual cell types, we identified 7 cell clusters with distinct gene expression signatures characteristic of known cardiac cell types (Figure S6). A total of 21 188 high-quality cardiomyocytes were retained (Figure 2B and 2C). To delineate the molecular signature of proliferating cardiomyocytes, we performed a pseudobulk differential expression analysis comparing tdTomato+ and tdTomato– populations. This analysis identified 220 upregulated and 159 downregulated genes in tdTomato+ cells (Figure 2D and Table S2).

Meanwhile, we analyzed transcriptomes of newly formed postcytokinesis single-color GFP+/RFP+ cardiomyocytes from Myh6-MerCreMer;MADM mice. Single-color cardiomyocytes isolated from 3-month-old mouse hearts through enzymatic digestion underwent SMART-seq analysis (Figure 2E and Figure S7A and S7B). Quality control ensured the sampling of pure cardiomyocytes without noncardiomyocyte contamination (Figure S7A–S7D) and robust sequencing depth (Figure S7C). Differential expression analysis comparing single-colored and yellow cardiomyocytes identified 394 upregulated and 699 downregulated genes in single-colored cardiomyocytes (Figure 2F and Table S3).

We refined the cytokinesis-specific gene list by leveraging the complementary temporal resolutions of the Aurkb-tdTomato (labeling cardiomyocytes undergoing cytokinesis) and MADM (labeling post-cytokinetic cardiomyocytes) systems. GO enrichments were performed separately for three distinct gene sets: Aurkb-tdTomato-unique, MADM-unique, and shared genes (intersection; Figure 2G). Functional profiling revealed a complex landscape (Figure 2H): Aurkb-tdTomato-unique genes were enriched for core mitotic programs (eg, “G2/M transition”) but also included non-proliferative processes such as “protein tetramerization.” MADM-unique genes, while enriched for metabolic terms, also encompassed pathways linked to cardiomyocyte proliferation, including “fatty acid beta-oxidation” and “muscle contraction.”21,22 Shared genes comprised both “regulation of system process” and key cell-cycle events like “DNA replication.”

Based on this functional annotation, we subtracted from the Aurkb-tdTomato dataset only those genes that were exclusive to this population, absent from MADM, and confirmed as cytokinesis-irrelevant contaminants. Using this refined approach, we identified 171 genes positively correlated with proliferation and 199 negatively correlated genes (Table S4). Further analysis of these datasets revealed 172 TFs specifically associated with the cytokinetic program (Figure 2I).

3. Cross-species multi-model integrative analysis uncovers a dual regulatory axis and Nfyb/Nr3c1 as opposing regulators governing cardiomyocyte proliferation.

Public snRNA-sequencing dataset (GSE130699)19 from neonatal injured hearts identified distinct cardiomyocyte subpopulations. Based on the dataset, we integrated four specific samples—P1ShamD1, P1MID1, P8ShamD1, and P8MID1—and identified 9 major cell clusters. Subsequently, the cardiomyocytes were further divided into 5 distinct subclusters, with CM5 enriched for cell-cycle gene expression (Figure S8A–S8D). Conversely, cluster CM1 is defined as a mature, quiescent population by markers of terminal differentiation and contractile function (eg, Myh6, Tcap). Comparing the CM5 (enriched for cell-cycle gene expression) versus CM1 cluster identified 610 genes upregulated and 38 genes downregulated in CM5 relative to CM1, which may reflect differences in cell states, including proliferation, maturation, or metabolic programs, along with 109 predicted TFs (Figure S8E and S8F and Table S5).

A previous study using a scRNA-seq dataset (GSE138270) from hiPSC-CMs showed that CDC20highSPG20high cells represent cardiomyocytes completing cytokinesis, while CDC20lowSPG20high cells represent cardiomyocytes that have undergone karyokinesis (nuclear division) but failed cytokinesis, resulting in binucleation.23 We reanalyzed the dataset and resolved 5 clusters, with hCM1 and hCM2 corresponding to the CDC20highSPG20high and CDC20lowSPG20high populations, respectively (Figure S9A and S9B). Although hCM3 expresses some cell-cycle genes, its specific marker genes are dominated by cytoskeletal and structural components, such as ACTB, CNN1, and TPM4 (Figure S9C). This indicates that hCM3 represents a population primarily engaged in structural organization and sarcomere assembly rather than active proliferation (Figure S9D). We compare transcriptional profiles of hCM1 versus hCM2 to identify differences between cardiomyocytes with cytokinesis and those with karyokinesis. It identified 207 genes positively and 93 negatively correlated with cytokinesis completion, with 55 predicted TFs (Figures S9E and S9F and Table S6).

Taking the union across the four models (mouse development, neonatal post-MI, human iPSC-CMs, and Aurkb-tdTomato & MADM model), we identified the proliferation-associated gene datasets (Figure S10A and S10B; database: https://gene-screening.pages.dev). In these gene datasets, GO enrichment revealed that proproliferation genes were enriched in GO terms related to cell division (eg, “sister chromatid segregation,” “cell cycle phase transition”). In contrast, antiproliferation genes were enriched in GO terms associated with mature cardiomyocyte function (eg, “cardiac muscle contraction,” “voltage-gated calcium channel activity”; Figure S10C and S10D).

To identify master regulators rather than causal downstream effector genes involved in proliferation, we prioritized computational prediction of upstream transcriptional regulators. The iRegulon analysis of proliferation-associated gene sets from each model consistently predicted 2 distinct sets of TFs: 1 set predicted on the basis of proproliferation genes and another set on antiproliferation genes. This antagonism suggests that a dual-control regenerative paradigm may be essential for adult cardiac regeneration (Figure 3A).

From the 105 candidate TFs identified after the exclusion of those without differential expression in any model, we applied a literature-based filtering approach24 to prioritize novel effectors for experimental validation. This manual literature review excluded TFs previously reported to regulate cardiomyocyte proliferation, resulting in a final set of 32 novel candidate TFs for functional validation (Figure S1).

In vitro siRNA knockdown in cultured cardiomyocytes was screened for effects on proliferation, identifying 22 TFs with significant impacts (Figure 3B). We next selected 6 TFs that were enriched among the differentially expressed genes comparing failing and nonfailing human hearts (Figure 3C). As reported, cardiomyocyte proliferative potential is dramatically lower in failing human hearts compared with healthy hearts,25 so differentially expressed genes from this comparison may correlate with cardiomyocyte proliferation. Nfyb (impels cell cycle26) and Nr3c1 (promotes cardiomyocyte maturation27) were among the significantly dysregulated genes in failing human myocardium (Figure 3D). Immunostaining showed that the percentage of NFYB-positive cardiomyocytes was significantly lower in failing hearts (nonfailing versus failing: 0.29% versus 0.11%). In comparison, the percentage of NR3C1-positive cardiomyocytes was significantly higher in nonfailing than failing hearts (0.28% versus 1.12%; Figure 3E and 3F). Functional validation in hiPSC-CMs showed that siRNA-mediated Nfyb knockdown reduced pH3+ cardiomyocytes, whereas Nr3c1 knockdown increased them (Figure 3G), indicating opposing roles.

Nfyb Overexpression Enhances Cardiomyocyte Proliferation and Post-MI Cardiac Repair by Enhancing Cycle Gene Transcription

An AAV9 vector expressing Nfyb under the cardiomyocyte-specific cardiac troponin T (cTnT) promoter was engineered (Figure S11A). Immediately after MI induction, the virus was injected into 5 sites along the ischemic border zone in 8-week-old mice (Figure 4A). At 5 days after MI, Nfyb and cell-cycle gene mRNA levels were elevated (Figure S11B). AAV9-Nfyb increased Ki67+ (2.4-fold), pH3+ (4.6-fold), and Aurkb+ (5.1-fold) cardiomyocytes in the border zone, indicating enhanced cell-cycle activity (Figure 4B–4D).

At 28 days after MI, AAV9-Nfyb transfected 45% to 65% of cardiomyocytes in the border zone (Figure S11C). Echocardiography revealed a 15% relative increase in left ventricular ejection fraction (Figure 4E), with reduced left ventricular volumes, left ventricular internal dimensions, and increased left ventricular posterior walls (Figure S11D). Masson trichrome staining showed a reduction in scar size from 41% to 32% (Figure 4F), reflecting diminished adverse remodeling.

Motif analysis showed that Nfyb-binding motifs closely resemble those of Nfya (Figure 4G), suggesting heterotrimeric complex formation. NF-Y binds the CCAAT motif in eukaryotic promoters.28 ENCODE ChIP sequencing data from 4 independent cell lines (K562, HepG2, GM12878, HeLa-S3) revealed marked overlap in regulating cell cycle–related genes, with 97.6% of Nfyb-targeted genes also bound by Nfya (Figure 4H and 4I). Peak calling confirmed Nfyb binding at Cdc20/Plk1 promoters (Figure S11E). ChIP sequencing in neonatal mouse hearts showed that elevated H3K27ac peaks colocalized with Nfya at Cdc20, Pgk1, and Plk1 loci (Figure 4J). AAV9-Nfyb–infected hearts showed upregulation of Cdc20, Pgk1, and Plk1 mRNA (Figure 4K), supporting the idea that Nfyb/Nfya jointly regulate cell-cycle gene transcription through H3K27ac enrichment.

Inhibition of Nr3c1 Promotes Cardiomyocyte Proliferation and Post-MI Cardiac Repair

Adult mice underwent MI followed by intraperitoneal injection of the selective Nr3c1 inhibitor Cort108297 (Figure 5A and Figure S12A). Cort108297 treatment increased Ki67+ (2.2-fold), pH3+ (2.6-fold), and Aurkb+ (2.4-fold) cardiomyocytes in the border zone (Figure 5B–5D and Figure S12B). In 3-month-old post-MI Myh6-MerCreMer;MADM mice, we quantified newly formed cardiomyocytes by calculating the percentage of single-color cardiomyocytes among total fluorescence-labeled cells, as described previously.17,29 Cort108297 treatment increased this percentage from 1.5% to 3.6% (Figure 5E and 5F), confirming enhanced proliferation.

Functional outcomes showed a 19% relative increase in left ventricular ejection fraction at 28 days after MI in Cort108297-treated mice (Figure 5G), with structural improvements (Figure S12C). Masson trichrome staining showed reduced infarct size (40% to 28%; Figure 5H).

Mechanistically, Nr3c1 encodes a glucocorticoid receptor, which functions as a ligand-dependent TF.30 Integration of RNA-seq (dexamethasone-induced Nr3c1 activation) and Nr3c1 ChIP sequencing in cultured cardiomyocytes identified 284 genes (eg, Pdk4, Myh6, Ezh2) with robust Nr3c1 binding–mRNA correlation (Figure 5I). Immunofluorescence showed that Cort108297 treatment retained endogenous Nr3c1 in the cytoplasm (Figure 5J), and Western blot of nuclear/cytoplasmic fractions confirmed its cytoplasmic retention (Figure 5K), suggesting that Nr3c1 enters the nucleus to suppress proliferation by binding target genes.

Spatiotemporal AAV9 Dual-Target System Coordinates Nfyb Activation and Nr3c1 Ablation to Spur Cardiomyocyte Cytokinesis and Cardiac Regeneration

To determine whether Nfyb activation and Nr3c1 ablation synergize to induce cardiomyocyte proliferation, we engineered a spatiotemporally controlled AAV9 dual-target system. For Nfyb induction, a drug (risdiplam)-elicitable alternative splicing module (DreAM) was used. In P19 cells, 10 µmol/L risdiplam induced >100-fold EGFP (enhanced green fluorescent protein) expression through DreAM with a cytomegalovirus promoter within 24 hours, validating functionality (Figure S13A). To restrict expression to cardiomyocytes, the cytomegalovirus promoter was replaced with cTnT and EGFP with splice-controlled Nfyb (Figure 6A). Concurrently, a CRISPR/enOsCas12f1 platform targeting exon 2 of Nr3c1 with 4 sgRNAs (sgRNA1+2, sgRNA2+3, sgRNA1+3) achieved precise deletions (230–330 bp) with >80% on-target editing (Figure 6B and Figure S13B and S13C). Off-target analysis confirmed no indels in functional domains of Pdk4, Cpt1b, or Sav1 (known regulators of cardiomyocyte proliferation) at <0.1% threshold (Figure S13D).

In cultured cardiomyocytes, plasmid transduction resulted in detectable nuclear-localized expression of HA-Nfyb and Flag-sgNr3c1. Costaining with cTnT confirmed that cTnT-driven constructs were cardiomyocyte restricted (Figure S13E), suggesting that risdiplam-induced Nfyb and enOsCas12f1-mediated Nr3c1 editing were confined to cTnT+ cells.

The dual components were packaged into separate AAV9 vectors and injected intramyocardially into post-MI Myh6-MerCreMer;MADM mice at the infarct border zone (Figure 6C and Figure S13F). Treated mice showed a 2.5-fold increase in single-colored/total labeled cardiomyocytes (3.3% versus 1.4% control; Figure 6D). Notably, clustered cardiomyocytes signify clonal expansion potential.15,17 A cluster was defined as ≥2 adjacent single-color cardiomyocytes with distinct morphological boundaries, delineated by wheat germ agglutinin staining to outline individual cell membranes. Clonal analysis showed that AAV9-DreAM-Nfyb/sgNr3c1 induced 28% of single-colored cardiomyocytes to form ≥2-cell clusters (7-fold versus AAV9-Nfyb alone, 19-fold versus AAV9-Nr3c1 alone, 28-fold versus control), suggesting a synergistic effect. As a positive control, AAV9-YAP (yes-associated protein) 5SA15,31 increased the ratio by 16-fold versus control, with 16% forming clusters (Figure 6E and Figure S14).

Continuous Nfyb/Nr3c1 treatment induced cardiac hyperplasia (Figure S13G and S13H), mirroring YAP5SA-overexpressing phenotypes, a hallmark of hyperactive proliferation.32 These data validate that coordinated Nfyb activation and Nr3c1 ablation through this spatiotemporal AAV9 system induces adult cardiomyocyte cytokinesis and regeneration.

Dual Targeting of Nfyb and Nr3c1 Confers Therapeutic Benefits in Adult Post-MI Mice and Human Engineered Heart Tissues

We further validated the therapeutic potential of single and dual interventions in adult post-MI hearts. AAV9 vectors delivering risdiplam-responsive cTnT-DreAM-HA-Nfyb or cTnT-enOsCas12f1-sgNr3c1 (Figure S15A) were intramyocardially injected immediately after MI, with subsequent risdiplam induction (Figure 7A). Cardiac function and infarct size were evaluated at 28 days after MI. Compared with control, dual therapy induced a 37.6% relative increase in left ventricular ejection fraction (single interventions: 26.2% with Nfyb overexpression versus 19.7% with Nr3c1 ablation; Figure 7B), alleviated cardiac remodeling (Figure S15B), and reduced infarct size by 51.1% (single interventions: 16.5% with Nfyb overexpression versus 18.0% with Nr3c1 ablation; Figure 7C). The results indicate that single interventions exert beneficial effects, but dual treatment significantly enhances these effects.

To assess translational significance, we used engineered heart tissues from hiPSC-CMs (Figure 7D), prepared as in prior studies.33 We evaluated dual-target intervention (adenovirus-mediated human Nfyb overexpression and Nr3c1 inhibition with Cort108297) on cardiomyocyte proliferation and contractile function (Videos S1–S4). Immunofluorescence analysis showed high adenoviral transduction efficiency; hematoxylin-eosin staining confirmed unaltered tissue architecture and cellular integrity (Figure S15C and S15D). Single interventions increased Ki67+ cardiomyocytes by 3.0-fold and 5.1-fold, respectively; dual treatment elevated Ki67+ cardiomyocytes by 6.6-fold (Figure 7E). Functional assessment under progressive stretch (Figure 7F) showed no change in active force (indicating that increased cardiomyocyte numbers did not compromise function), and increased passive force reflected tissue densification. These results demonstrate that dual targeting of Nfyb/Nr3c1 promotes cardiomyocyte proliferation and improves functional outcomes in a humanized model.

Discussion

In the present study, 2 master regulators, Nfyb and Nr3c1, were identified through a strategy that integrated cross-species multimodel transcriptomic analysis (including 2 advanced mouse models of cardiomyocyte cytokinesis), iRegulon prediction, and phenotype validation. We proposed a dual regulatory axis concept and demonstrated that dual-target intervention against Nfyb/Nr3c1 exerts a synergistic effect on cardiomyocyte proliferation and MI therapy. New mechanisms underlying the Nfyb/Nr3c1-induced effect were identified. Three human models (hiPSC-CMs, engineered heart tissues, and patient heart tissues) and a gene therapy with a clinically approved drug were used to enhance the translational relevance of the dual-target treatment (Figure 8).

Cytokinesis-Resolved Multiomic Analysis Uncovered Core Cardiomyocyte Proliferative Regulators to Direct Therapeutic Discovery

We explored cardiomyocyte proliferative regulators through cross-species multimodel transcriptomic analysis and large animal/human dataset validation, with emphasis on bona fide proliferation (cytokinesis). The Aurkb-tdTomato system directly identifies cardiomyocytes undergoing active cytokinesis, with Tnnt2-Dre ensuring cardiomyocyte specificity.14 This approach captures transient processes invisible to static methods, enabling transcriptomic profiling of the few dividing cells through fluorescence-activated cell sorting/scRNA-seq. The integration of transcriptome analysis of postcytokinesis cardiomyocytes using the Myh6-MerCreMer;MADM system strengthened the technical rigor and biological interpretability of the regeneration models.

On the basis of the proliferation-associated genes analyzed from multimodel mRNA profiles, iRegulon predicted 396 TFs, including validated players (eg, Ybx1, E2F)34,35 and novel candidates. TEA domain transcription factor 2, the primary nuclear DNA-binding partner for YAP-dependent transcription,32 was detected in our dataset (mouse development models), indicating that our models capture Hippo/YAP pathway components. However, YAP itself (Yap1 gene) was not identified as a differentially expressed or predicted TF. This observation is consistent with prior reports17,19 that found that YAP expression did not show significant changes at the transcript level despite its functional importance in proliferation.

Integrated analysis revealed proproliferative systems inducing cell-cycle reentry, as well as universal cytokinesis barriers. GO annotation showed the proproliferative machinery, including chromosomal reorganization and cyclin-dependent kinase complexes, to be central to proliferation, corroborating the known role of chromatin remodeling in mitotic entry.36 The cytokinesis barriers included sarcomere integrity, cytoskeletal protein complexes, calcium signaling, and contractile machinery modulation, which were reported as physical checkpoints for cytokinesis.22,37

Dual-Target Nfyb/Nr3c1 Intervention Induces Definitive Adult Cardiomyocyte Cytokinesis to Enhance Post-MI Cardiac Repair

In vitro screening of 32 candidates revealed Nfyb as a proproliferative master regulator and Nr3c1 as a key inhibitor. In vivo, induction of Nfyb activation or Nr3c1 ablation enhanced cardiomyocyte cell-cycle activity and resulted in structural and functional benefits after MI.

Accurate quantification of bona fide cardiomyocyte proliferation in vivo is challenging and lacks a gold standard. Conventional cell-cycle markers (eg, Ki67, pH3) fail to distinguish proliferation from polyploidization/multinucleation.38 Transgenic lineage tracing (eg, MADM, R26R-Confetti) enables precise cytokinesis quantification.13,15,29 Notably, validated interventions, including Meis1/Hoxb13 knockout, which induces a 2- to 3-fold increase in MADM-labeled cytokinesis,39 demonstrate proliferative unlocking. Our Nfyb/Nr3c1 intervention similarly increased cytokinesis 2- to 3-fold in post-MI MADM mice.

It is important to note that clustered cardiomyocyte configurations were observed, providing definitive evidence of completed cytokinesis and daughter cell retention. In R26R-Confetti mice, all labeled cardiomyocytes appear as single cells in controls, whereas >20% of AAV9:YAP5SA-transduced cardiomyocytes form clusters of ≥2 cells.15 Our data demonstrate that in adult post-MI MADM mice, 28% of Nfyb/Nr3c1-coedited cardiomyocytes and 17% of YAP5SA cardiomyocytes formed clusters (≥2 cells) versus 1% in controls. These clustered cardiomyocytes signify clonal expansion potential,15,17 a hallmark of robust regeneration.

Mechanistic Insights Into the Synergistic Nfyb/Nr3c1 Intervention in Driving Cardiomyocyte Proliferation

A recent study revealed that the cointervention of 2 complementary regulators was a feasible strategy to enhance heart regeneration.40 Our data clearly showed that Nfyb/Nr3c1 cointervention augmented cardiomyocyte cytokinesis beyond the effects of individual treatments. This synergy likely arises from a coordinated mechanism: Nfyb activation upregulates proproliferative cell-cycle genes, and Nr3c1 deletion suppresses antiproliferative factors by nuclear translocation and target gene binding.

Nfyb, a subunit of the NF-Y transcriptional complex, regulates cell-cycle genes in proliferating cells.41 Prior studies established NF-Y as a positive regulator of cardiomyocyte proliferation in prenatal9 and neonatal19 mice, yet its role in adult hearts remained unclear. ChIP sequencing and quantitative polymerase chain reaction analyses revealed that Nfyb overexpression in adult cardiomyocytes induces Cdc20, Cdk1, and Cdk4. High CDC20 expression in hiPSC-CMs has been linked to cytokinesis.23 Cdk1 and Cdk4 rank among the 4 cell-cycle factors forcibly overexpressed to drive adult heart regeneration.17

Cardiomyocyte-specific Nr3c1 knockout promotes cell-cycle progression by shifting metabolism toward glucose catabolism over fatty acid oxidation.42 Extending this, the ChIP sequencing analysis revealed that Nr3c1 directly upregulates Pdk4, Myh6, and Ezh2. Conditional Pdk4 deletion in adult post-MI mice drives cardiomyocyte proliferation through metabolic remodeling, favoring glucose over fatty acid oxidation.21 Nr3c1 also modulates sarcomeric organization and assembly through Myh6.27 Ezh2, functioning as a histone methyltransferase with Ezh1, has been established as an epigenetic barrier to cardiomyocyte proliferation.43

Drug-Responsive Splicing-Modulated Controllable Dual Gene Regulation Enables Clinically Compatible Heart Regeneration

The present studies show that regulating defined gene combinations offers a promising approach to activate adult cardiomyocyte proliferation. Others have shown that transient induction of 4 cell-cycle factors through lentivirus promotes cardiac regeneration in rodent and porcine ischemic heart failure models,44 whereas cardiac-specific expression of the reprogramming factors Oct4, Sox2, Klf4, and c-Myc drives cardiomyocyte dedifferentiation and regenerative capacity.16 The Nfyb/Nr3c1 dual system developed in this research has fewer gene components and is more feasible than multigene strategies. Nfyb/Nr3c1 intervention achieves heart/weight ratios and postcytokinesis clusters comparable to those of YAP5SA overexpression. Moreover, our data in a human engineered heart tissue model strengthened the clinical relevance of the dual intervention.

Potent proproliferative strategies can pose tumorigenic and other lethal risks, underscoring the critical need for a system that induces transient, controllable cardiomyocyte proliferation to deliver safe and effective regenerative therapies.45 To address this issue, a DreAM activated by risdiplam,31 an FDA-approved splicing modulator,46 was used in this research. Risdiplam dose-dependently upregulates AAV9-Nfyb expression through DreAM, enabling repeated AAV activation through adjustable risdiplam administration. In addition, a compact CRISPR/Cas12f1 system for targeted Nr3c1 ablation in cardiomyocytes in vivo was developed. The small size (433 amino acids) and modular design of enOsCas12f1 circumvent obstacles related to the larger size of Cas9 that cannot be packaged into a single AAV, enhancing efficiency and minimizing off-target effects compared with conventional Cas9.47 This system is a promising in vivo genome-editing tool48 that could lead to clinical applications of all-in-one AAV vector gene therapy with efficient Nr3c1 ablation. The present results show that integrating AAV-DreAM–mediated Nfyb overexpression and CRISPR/enOsCas12f1-mediated Nr3c1 ablation is an efficient system to enhance cardiac regeneration after MI.

Conclusions

This study established a cytokinesis-resolved multiomics framework that detects core regulatory networks driving cardiomyocyte proliferation and identifies master regulators governing adult cytokinesis. A drug-responsive AAV9 dual Nfyb/Nr3c1 therapy enables spatiotemporally controlled cardiac regeneration. This new system could develop into an important new therapeutic strategy for treating the devastating effects of MI.

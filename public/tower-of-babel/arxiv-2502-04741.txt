Multivalued forbidden numbers of two-rowed configurations – the missing cases
Wallace Peaslee, Attila Sali, Jun Yan

Abstract
The present paper considers extremal combinatorics questions in the language of matrices. An s s -matrix is a matrix with entries in { 0 , 1 , … , s − 1 } \{0,1,\ldots,s-1\} . An s s -matrix is simple if it has no repeated columns. A matrix F F is a configuration in a matrix A A , denoted F ≺ A F\prec A , if it is a row/column permutation of a submatrix of A A . Avoid ⁡ ( m , s , F ) \Avoid(m,s,F) is the set of m m -rowed, simple s s -matrices not containing a configuration of F F and forb ⁡ ( m , s , F ) = max ⁡ { | A | : A ∈ Avoid ⁡ ( m , s , F ) } \forb(m,s,F)=\max\{|A|\colon A\in\Avoid(m,s,F)\} . Dillon and Sali initiated the systematic study of forb ⁡ ( m , s , F ) \forb(m,s,F) for 2 2 -matrices F F , and computed forb ⁡ ( m , s , F ) \forb(m,s,F) for all 2-rowed F F when s > 3 s>3 . In this paper we tackle the remaining cases when s = 3 s=3 . In particular, we determine the asymptotics of forb ⁡ ( m , 3 , p ⋅ K 2 ) − forb ⁡ ( m , 3 , p ⋅ I 2 ) \forb(m,3,p\cdot K_{2})-\forb(m,3,p\cdot I_{2}) for p > 3 p>3 , where K 2 K_{2} is the 2 × 4 2\times 4 simple 2 2 -matrix and I 2 I_{2} is the 2 × 2 2\times 2 identity matrix, as well as the exact values of forb ⁡ ( m , 3 , F ) \forb(m,3,F) for many 2-rowed 2 2 -matrices F F .

Introduction

Several questions of extremal combinatorics can be conveniently expressed in the language of
(0,1)
-matrices. A matrix
A
is said to be
simple
if it contains no repeated columns. There is a natural correspondence between columns of an
m
-rowed
(0,1)
-matrix and subsets of
[m]
. We consider an extremal set theory problem in matrix terminology as follows. Let
|A|
be the number of columns of
A
. For a given matrix
F
, we say
F
is a
configuration
in
A
, denoted
F\prec A
, if there is a submatrix of
A
which can be obtained by permuting the rows and columns of
F
.
Define

\Avoid(m,F)=\left\{A\colon A\hbox{ is an }m\hbox{-rowed }(0,1)\hbox{-matrix and }F\not\prec A\right\},

\forb(m,F)=\max\{|A|:A\in\Avoid(m,F)\}.

We say a matrix
A\in\Avoid(m,F)
is
extremal
if
|A|=\forb(m,F)
, and let

\ext(m,F)=\{A\in\Avoid(m,F)\colon|A|=\forb(m,F)\}.

The foremost theorem in forbidden configurations is Theorem
1.1
below. Here,
K_{k}
denotes the
k\times 2^{k}
matrix containing all possible
0,1
-columns.

Theorem 1.1
.

Sauer (1972)
;
Shelah (1972)
;
Vapnik and Chervonenkis (1971)

\forb(m,K_{k})=\binom{m}{k-1}+\binom{m}{k-2}+\cdots+\binom{m}{0}.

Further interesting results and research problems can be found in the survey paper
Anstee and Sali (2025)
. However, our main interest in the present paper is the generalization of these concepts to
s
-matrices, which have entries in
\{0,1,\ldots,s-1\}
. Such matrices can be thought of as
s
-coloured set systems or as representations of collections of functions from a given finite set into
\{0,1,\dots,s-1\}
.
The concepts of
simple
matrices and

naturally extend. For a given collection
\mathcal{F}
of matrices, we denote by
\Avoid(m,s,{\mathcal{F}})
the collection of
m
-rowed, simple
s
-matrices that avoid every matrix
F\in\mathcal{F}
. The main extremal function in the study of forbidden configurations is

\forb(m,s,{\mathcal{F}})=\max\{|A|\colon A\in\Avoid(m,s,{\mathcal{F}})\},

which we call the
forbidden number
, and we similarly define

\ext(m,s,F)=\{A\in\Avoid(m,s,F)\colon|A|=\forb(m,s,F)\}.

Alon
Alon (1983)
and Steele
Steele (1978)
gave a generalization of Theorem
1.1
to

s
-matrices, with the bound given being an exponential function of

. This is a special case of the following more general phenomenon proved by Füredi and Sali

. A matrix is called an
(i,j)
-matrix
if its entries are all from the set

.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a family of
s
-matrices. If for every pair of distinct

there is an

-matrix in

, then
\forb(m,s,{\mathcal{F}})=O(m^{k})
for some positive integer

. If
{\mathcal{F}}
has no

-matrix for some distinct

\forb(m,s,{\mathcal{F}})=\Omega(2^{m})
.

Several papers
Anstee et al. (2023)
,

, including all

Some important matrices include

\end{array}\right.\overbrace{\begin{array}[]{@{}c}00\cdots 0\\

When

For general 2-rowed configurations

, the case when

is harder than when

. In particular, it was shown in

The following important reduction lemma was proved in

.

Lemma 1.3
.

Dillon and Sali (2021)

Let

. If

and

, then

\forb(m,3,F(a,b,c,d))=\forb(m,3,F(a,p,p,d)).

Note that by Lemma

, we may assume without loss of generality that

provided that

that

. In what follows, we investigate the forbidden numbers
\forb(m,3,F(a,p,p,d))
for various parameters

.

In Section
2
, we begin by introducing some terminologies and notations. Then, we prove first a generalization of a lemma in
Dillon and Sali (2021)
, which roughly says that any matrix in

that contains many columns must satisfy some stability-type structural properties. In Section

, we determine the difference between
\forb(m,3,p\cdot K_{2})
and
\forb(m,3,p\cdot I_{2})
asymptotically. Throughout the paper we let

, so

. Our first, maybe surprising, observation is that the difference above does not depend on

Proposition 1.4
.

For all

,
\forb(m,3,p\cdot K_{2})-\forb(m,3,p\cdot I_{2})
is independent of

as long as

.

This allows us to define

to be the common value of

In Section

This no longer holds for larger values of

, but in Section

The next theorem determines

Theorem 1.6
.

For all

and

\forb(m,3,F(p-q,p,p,p-q))=\forb(m,3,p\cdot K_{2})-qN_{r}

,

•

.

In particular, this is true for all fixed constants

and all

With a more careful analysis of the proof of Theorem

, we prove the following result.

Corollary 1.7
.

For all

and

,

\forb(m,3,F(p-1,p,p,p-1))=\forb(m,3,p\cdot K_{2})-N_{r}.

Determining the exact forbidden number in the remaining cases when

is not known. In particular, the following result shows that the exact formula in Theorem

Proposition 1.8
.

If
m\geq 2N_{r}+2
,
0\leq q\leq p-1
and

, then

\forb(m,3,F(p-q,p,p,p-q))\geq\forb(m,3,p\cdot K_{2})-qN_{r}+\left\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\right\rfloor d.

We also have the following more general lower bound.

Proposition 1.9
.

For every
0\leq q_{0},q_{1}\leq p-1
, every
0\leq r_{1},r_{2}\leq r
satisfying
r_{1}+r_{2}=r
, and every
m\geq 2\binom{r_{1}+1}{2}+2\binom{r_{2}+1}{2}+2
, we have

\forb(m,3,F(p-q_{0},p,p,p-q_{1}))\geq\forb(m,3,p\cdot K_{2})-\min\{q_{0},q_{1}\}\binom{r_{1}+1}{2}-\max\{q_{0},q_{1}\}\binom{r_{2}+1}{2}.

For fixed
p,q_{0},q_{1}
, we may optimize the right-hand side by viewing
r_{1}
as the variable and using
r_{2}=r-r_{1}
. That is, assuming
q_{0}\leq q_{1}
, we minimize
q_{0}\binom{r_{1}+1}{2}+q_{1}\binom{r-r_{1}+1}{2}
. Easy calculation shows that if

for some

, then the minimum is achieved when

is the closest integer to
\frac{(2r+1)\alpha-1}{2(\alpha+1)}
, as long as this fraction is at most

, which is equivalent to

. Otherwise,

is optimal. Note that if

and so

. The resulting lower bound in this case is tight in many cases by Theorem

. We suspect that a more involved version of this proof could potentially show that after optimization, the general lower bound for
\forb(m,3,F(p-q_{0},p,p,p-q_{1}))
given by Proposition

is also tight in several cases.

2.1

\forb(m,3,p\cdot K_{2})-\forb(m,3,p\cdot I_{2})

We first give a lower bound construction that uses only 0-mark and 1-mark columns.

Lemma 2.3

\forb(m,3,p\cdot I_{2})\geq 2^{m}+m2^{m-1}+(p-1)\left(\binom{m}{2}-\binom{r+1}{2}\right)+(r-1)2^{r}+1

Proof.

Let every pair

\begin{bmatrix}0\\

If a column

\min\{p-1,2^{m-1+i-j}\}

m-r+i\leq j\leq m
. Therefore, the number of 1-mark columns we can include is

(p-1)\left(\binom{m}{2}-\binom{r+1}{2}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=m-r+i}^{m}2^{m-1+i-j}=(p-1)\left(\binom{m}{2}-\binom{r+1}{2}\right)+(r-1)2^{r}+1.

Since there are
2^{m}+m2^{m-1}
columns with no 0 above 1, and we can always include all of them, the result follows.
∎

If
A\in\ext(m,3,p\cdot I_{2})
, let
A=\begin{bmatrix}B\mid C\end{bmatrix}
be the standard decomposition. Since
|A|\geq 2^{m}+m2^{m-1}+(p-1)\left(\binom{m}{2}-\binom{r+1}{2}\right)+(r-1)2^{r}+1
by Lemma
2.3
, and
|C|\leq(p-1)\binom{m}{2}
from definition, we have that
|B|>2^{m}+m2^{m-1}-2^{m-3}
as long as
m\geq 2r+2
. Hence, by Lemma
2.2
, we can suitably permute the rows of
A
so that every pair
i<j
of rows is associated with
\begin{bmatrix}0\\
1\end{bmatrix}
. Note that in this case the directed graph
T
is actually a tournament as there are no non-edges.

Motivated by Lemma
2.3
above, we say a pair of rows
i<j
is
abundant
if there are at least
p-1
possible columns with exactly one mark at
(i,j)
, or equivalently if
2^{m-1+i-j}\geq p-1
. A pair of rows
i<j
is called
scarce
if it is not abundant. It follows that if
r=\left\lceil\log_{2}{(p-1)}\right\rceil
, then there are
\binom{r+1}{2}
scarce pairs of rows, which are exactly
(i,j)
for
i\in[r]
and
m-r+i\leq j\leq m
. The next lemma shows that we can always assume the maximum number of
1
-mark columns are present in
A
.

Lemma 2.4
.

There exists
A\in\ext(m,3,p\cdot I_{2})
containing
2^{m}+m2^{m-1}
0-mark columns and
(p-1)\left(\binom{m}{2}-\binom{r+1}{2}\right)+(r-1)2^{r}+1

1
-mark columns. More precisely, for every abundant pair
(i,j)
of rows,
A
contains exactly
p-1
columns with their only mark at
(i,j)
, and for every scarce pair
(i,j)
of rows,
A
contains all columns with their only mark at
(i,j)
.

Proof.

We can always take all
2^{m}+m2^{m-1}
columns with no mark. Let
A\in\ext(m,3,p\cdot I_{2})
maximize the number of 1-mark columns. Assume for some pair
i<j
of rows,
A
does not contain
\min\{2^{m-1+i-j},p-1\}
columns with their unique marks at
(i,j)
. Then there exists a column
v
with its only mark at
(i,j)
that is not in
A
. There are two cases. If
A
contains less than
\min\{2^{m-1+i-j},p-1\}
marks at
(i,j)
,
\begin{bmatrix}A\mid v\end{bmatrix}
is then in
\Avoid(m,3,p\cdot I_{2})
with more columns than
A
, contradiction. If
A
contains exactly
\min\{2^{m-1+i-j},p-1\}
marks at
(i,j)
, then
A
contains a column
u
with at least two marks including a mark at
(i,j)
. Remove column
u
and add column
v
to get a new matrix
A^{\prime}
. Then
A^{\prime}
is in
\ext(m,3,p\cdot I_{2})
with one more
1
-mark column than
A
, contradiction. Thus, for all pairs
i<j
of rows,
A
contains
\min\{2^{m-1+i-j},p-1\}
columns with their unique marks at
(i,j)
, which add up to
(p-1)\left(\binom{m}{2}-\binom{r+1}{2}\right)+(r-1)2^{r}+1

1
-mark columns in total.
∎

We can now prove Proposition
1.4
, which shows that
\forb(m,3,p\cdot K_{2})-\forb(m,3,p\cdot I_{2})
is the same constant for all

.

Proposition
1.4
.

Since
\forb(m,3,p\cdot K_{2})=2^{m}+m2^{m-1}+(p-1)\binom{m}{2}
by

, it suffices to show that for all

\displaystyle\forb(m+1,3,p\cdot I_{2})-\forb(m,3,p\cdot I_{2})

\displaystyle=\forb(m+1,3,p\cdot K_{2})-\forb(m,3,p\cdot K_{2})

\displaystyle=2^{m-1}(m+4)+(p-1)m.

First we prove that
\forb(m+1,3,p\cdot I_{2})-\forb(m,3,p\cdot I_{2})\geq 2^{m-1}(m+4)+(p-1)m
. Let
A\in\ext(m,3,p\cdot I_{2})
. From above and by Lemma
2.2
we can assume
A
contains at most
p-1
instances of
0
above

in each pair
i<j
of rows.
A
must contain all

denote the number of columns in

with at least one mark.

Consider the following matrix

with

above

. Then, fix an index

. For every column

with at least one mark, define a new column

with

if

v_{k}^{\prime}=2
and
v_{i}^{\prime}=v_{i-1}
if

. The new columns

obtained in this way are all distinct and have marks only on row set

corresponding to the marks in the original columns. Add them all to
A^{\prime}
. Moreover, for each

(j,k)
is abundant so we can find
p-1
columns with their unique marks at
(j,k)
. Similarly, for each

p-1
columns with their unique marks at

. Then for any two rows

of

, which implies
\forb(m+1,3,p\cdot I_{2})\geq\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert=2^{m+1}+(m+1)2^{m}+S+(p-1)m
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\phantom{{}\geq{}}\forb(m+1,3,p\cdot I_{2})-\forb(m,3,p\cdot I_{2})

\displaystyle\geq\left(2^{m+1}+(m+1)2^{m}+S+(p-1)m\right)-\left(2^{m}+m2^{m-1}+S\right)

\displaystyle=2^{m-1}(m+4)+(p-1)m.

Now we prove that
\forb(m+1,3,p\cdot I_{2})-\forb(m,3,p\cdot I_{2})\leq 2^{m-1}(m+4)+(p-1)m
. Let
A\in\ext(m+1,3,p\cdot I_{2})
. We can assume that
A
has all
2^{m+1}+(m+1)2^{m}
columns with no mark. By Lemma
2.4
, we can also assume
A
has the maximum number
(p-1)\left(\binom{m+1}{2}-\binom{r+1}{2}\right)+(r-1)2^{r}+1
of

-mark columns.

Fix a row index
k
with
r+1\leq k\leq m+1-r
. Let
S
be the number of columns in
A
with at least two marks and let
v
be any one of them. For any abundant pair
(i,j)
, every column with a mark at
(i,j)
is a
1
-mark column by Lemma

, so all marks in

correspond to scarce pairs. In particular, if

has a mark at
(i,j)
, then
i\leq r<k<m+2-r\leq j
, which implies that

, as otherwise

will have a mark at abundant pair
(i,k)
or
(k,j)
. It follows that all columns in
A
with at least two marks remain distinct and do not lose any mark after removing row

. Now we construct an

by taking all

, so we have

\displaystyle\forb(m,3,p\cdot I_{2})\geq\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left(2^{m}+m2^{m-1}\right)+(p-1)\left(\binom{m}{2}-\binom{r+1}{2}\right)+(r-1)2^{r}+1+S

\displaystyle=\forb(m+1,3,p\cdot I_{2})-2^{m-1}(m+4)-(p-1)m.

This completes the proof.
∎

This allows us to define
g_{p}
to be the common value of
\forb(m,3,p\cdot K_{2})-\forb(m,3,p\cdot I_{2})
for all

. By
Dillon and Sali (2021)
, we have
g_{1}=g_{2}=0,g_{3}=1,g_{4}=2
and
g_{5}=5
. In the remaining part of this section, we prove Theorem
1.5
, which establishes the asymptotic formula
g_{p}\sim\frac{1}{2}p(\log_{2}p)^{2}
. As will be seen later, it follows from Lemma
2.3
that
g_{p}\leq\frac{1}{2}p(\log_{2}p)^{2}+o(p(\log_{2}p)^{2})
. To prove the reverse inequality, we use the following two results.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
m\geq 2r+2
. Suppose
b,c\geq 1
and
b+c\leq r+1
. Then there are at most
r2^{r}

m
-rowed
0,1,2
-columns with exactly
bc
marks formed by
c
0’s above
b
1’s, all corresponding to scarce pairs of rows.

Proof.

Let the positions of the
c
0’s be
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{c}
and the positions of the
b
1’s be
j_{1}<\cdots<j_{b}
. All
bc
marks correspond to scarce pairs if and only if
j_{1}-i_{c}\geq m-r
.
The index
i_{c}
must take values in
\{1,\ldots,r\}
, so there are at most

r
possible choices of
i_{c}
. Once
i_{c}
is fixed, the immediately following
(m-r-1)
positions are all equal to ‘2’, since all the marked
pairs of rows are scarce. This leaves
r
positions still undetermined.
There are at most
2^{r}
possibilities to choose which of these positions
are equal to ‘2’, and once this choice is made, the whole column
is uniquely determined, since the values of
b
and
c
are fixed. This
shows that there are at most
r2^{r}
such columns.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

With tedious calculations a better upper bound of such columns can be shown, namely there are at most
2^{r}(r-b-c+1)+2^{b+c-2}

0,1,2
-columns with exactly
bc
marks formed by
c
0’s above
b
1’s, all corresponding to scarce pairs of rows.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
k\geq 2
be fixed, then we have
g_{p}\geq\frac{(p-1)k}{k+1}\binom{r+1}{2}-r2^{r}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tau(i)
, where
\tau(n)
is the number of divisors of
n
.

Proof.

Let
A\in\ext(m,3,p\cdot I_{2})
. As before we can assume
A
contains at most
p-1
instances of 0 above 1 in every pair of rows, and contains the maximum number of 1-mark columns. For
j\geq 0
, let
a_{j}
be the number of
j
-mark columns in
A
. Note that if
j\geq 2
and
v
is a
j
-mark column, then every mark in
v
corresponds to a scarce pair of rows. From before, we have
a_{0}=2^{m}+m2^{m-1}
and
a_{1}=(p-1)\left(\binom{m}{2}-\binom{r+1}{2}\right)+(r-1)2^{r}+1
. Since there are at most
p-1
marks at each pair of rows, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}ia_{i}\leq(p-1)\binom{m}{2}
. Let
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}ia_{i}=(p-1)\binom{m}{2}-S
.

Let
m\geq 2r+2
,
2\leq i\leq k
, and consider an
m
-rowed
0,1,2
-column
v
with exactly
i
marks, all of which are scarce. Since
m\geq 2r+2
, there does not exist
x<y<x^{\prime}<y^{\prime}
such that both
(x,y)
and
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
are scarce. So there exists
b,c
with
bc=i
such that all
i
marks of
v
are formed as
c
0’s above
b
1’s. Thus Lemma
2.5
shows there are at most
\tau(i)r2^{r}
such columns, and so
a_{i}\leq\tau(i)r2^{r}
.

We have

\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}a_{i}\leq\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k+1}ia_{i}=\frac{1}{k+1}\left((p-1)\binom{m}{2}-S-\sum_{i=1}^{k}ia_{i}\right)

and therefore

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}+\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}a_{i}\leq\frac{1}{k+1}\left((p-1)\binom{m}{2}-S\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(1-\frac{i}{k+1}\right)a_{i}.

It follows that

\displaystyle g_{p}

\displaystyle=\forb(m,3,p\cdot K_{2})-\forb(m,3,p\cdot I_{2})=\forb(m,3,p\cdot K_{2})-\left\lvert A\right\rvert

\displaystyle=(p-1)\binom{m}{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(p-1)k}{k+1}\binom{r+1}{2}+\frac{S}{k+1}-\frac{k}{k+1}((r-1)2^{r}+1)-\sum_{i=2}^{k}\left(1-\frac{i}{k+1}\right)a_{i}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(p-1)k}{k+1}\binom{r+1}{2}-r2^{r}-\sum_{i=2}^{k}a_{i}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(p-1)k}{k+1}\binom{r+1}{2}-r2^{r}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tau(i).\qed

of Theorem
1.5
.

By Lemma
2.3
,
\forb(m,3,p\cdot I_{2})\geq 2^{m}+m2^{m-1}+(p-1)(\binom{m}{2}-\binom{r+1}{2})+(r-1)2^{r}+1
. Hence,

\displaystyle g_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\forb(m,3,p\cdot K_{2})-2^{m}-m2^{m-1}-(p-1)\left(\binom{m}{2}-\binom{r+1}{2}\right)-(r-1)2^{r}-1

\displaystyle=(p-1)\binom{r+1}{2}-(r-1)2^{r}-1=\frac{1}{2}p(\log_{2}p)^{2}+o(p(\log_{2}p)^{2}).

On the other hand, Proposition
2.7
implies that for every fixed

,

g_{p}\geq\frac{(p-1)k}{k+1}\binom{r+1}{2}-r2^{r}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tau(i)=\left(1-\frac{1}{k+1}\right)\frac{1}{2}p(\log_{2}p)^{2}+o(p(\log_{2}p)^{2}).

Combining these two bounds and letting

, we get
g_{p}\sim\frac{1}{2}p(\log_{2}p)^{2}
.
∎

Lemma 2.13

2.3.3

Lemma 2.17
.

If
m\geq 2N_{r}+2
, and
N
non-edges are used to fix
M
scarce pairs, then the maximum of
M-N
taken ovar all matrices
A\in\ext(m,3,F(a,p,p,d))
is

\max(M-N)=\binom{r+1}{2}-N_{r}.

Proof.

Since
M\leq\binom{r+1}{2}
, we may assume that
N\leq N_{r}
, so that
m\geq 2N+2
. For every
i\in[r]
, let
a_{i}
be the number of 0-non-edges
(i,k)
with
i<k
, and let
b_{i}
be the number of 1-non-edges
(k,m+1-i)
with
k<m+1-i
. By definition, for every
i\in[r]
and
j\in[r+1-i]
, the scarce pair
(i,m+1-j)
is fixed if and only if
2^{i+j-2+a_{i}+b_{j}}\geq p-1
, which happens if and only if
a_{i}+b_{j}\geq r+2-i-j
.

Consider the construction in the proof of Proposition
1.9
with
r_{1}=\left\lceil\frac{r}{2}\right\rceil
and
r_{2}=\left\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\right\rfloor
. Here,
N_{r}=\binom{\left\lceil\frac{r}{2}\right\rceil+1}{2}+\binom{\left\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\right\rfloor+1}{2}
non-edges are used to fix all
\binom{r+1}{2}
scarce pairs, so
M-N
can achieve the value
\binom{r+1}{2}-N_{r}
.

To show
\binom{r+1}{2}-N_{r}
is the best possible, consider the following equivalent problem on the triangular array
T_{r}
of size
r
, whose
(i,j)
entry is
T_{r}(i,j)=r+2-i-j
for all
i\in[r]
and
j\in[r+1-i]
.

\begin{matrix}r&r-1&\cdots&\cdots&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\iddots&\iddots&\\
3&2&1\\
2&1\\
1\\
\end{matrix}

For every
i\in[r]
, a row operation on row
i
reduces every entry in row
i
by 1, and a column operation on column
i
reduces every entry in column
i
by 1. If we perform
a_{i}
row operations on row
i
and
b_{i}
column operations on column
i
for all
i\in[r]
, then after all of these operations, the
(i,j)
entry of
T_{r}
is non-positive if and only if
a_{i}+b_{j}\geq r+2-i-j
, and so if and only if the scarce pair
(i,m+1-j)
is fixed. Therefore, it suffices to show that if
N
row or column operations turn
M
entries in
T_{r}
non-positive, then
M-N\leq\binom{r+1}{2}-N_{r}
.

To this end, we use induction on
r
to show
M-N\leq\binom{r+1}{2}-N_{r}=\left\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor
. The
r=1,2
cases can be checked easily, so we assume
r\geq 3
.

For each
i\in[r]
, let
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
be the number of operations performed on row
i
and column
i
, respectively. Observe that we may assume
a_{r}=0
, as otherwise reducing
a_{r}
by 1 and increasing
b_{1}
by 1 does not decrease
M
and leaves
N
unchanged. Similarly, we may assume
b_{r}=0
.

Let
M_{1}
be the number of non-positive integers in the first row or column of
T_{r}
after
N
operations, and let
M^{\prime}=M-M_{1}
. Let
N^{\prime}=\sum_{i=2}^{r-1}a_{i}+\sum_{i=2}^{r-1}b_{j}
, so that
N-N^{\prime}=a_{1}+a_{r}+b_{1}+b_{r}=a_{1}+b_{1}
. From the induction hypothesis,
M^{\prime}-N^{\prime}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{(r-2)^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor
, so we are done if
M_{1}-a_{1}-b_{1}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{(r-2)^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor=r-1
.

If the top left entry of
T_{r}
, which is
r
, is turned non-positive, then
a_{1}+b_{1}\geq r
, and so
M_{1}-a_{1}-b_{1}\leq 2r-1-r=r-1
and we are done.

If the top left entry of
T_{r}
remains positive, let
x
be the number of non-positive entries in row 1 and let
y
be the number of non-positive entries in column 1, so that
x+y=M_{1}
. If
x\leq a_{1}
, then
y-b_{1}\leq y\leq r-1
, so
M_{1}-a_{1}-b_{1}=x-a_{1}+y-b_{1}\leq r-1
and we are done. Similarly, we are done if
y\leq b_{1}
, so we assume neither is true.

For each
2\leq i\leq r
, the
(1,i)
entry of
T_{r}
is non-positive after the operations if and only if
a_{1}+b_{i}\geq r+1-i
. Since
x
of these entries are non-positive, we have
\sum_{i=2}^{r}b_{i}\geq\sum_{i=r-x+1}^{r}\max\{r+1-i-a_{1},0\}=\sum_{i=1}^{x}\max\{i-a_{1},0\}=\sum_{k=1}^{x-a_{1}}k=\binom{x-a_{1}+1}{2}
. Similarly, we have
\sum_{i=2}^{r}a_{i}\geq\binom{y-b_{1}+1}{2}
. Like above, we may assume
M_{1}-a_{1}-b_{1}=x-a_{1}+y-b_{1}\geq r
, as otherwise we are done. By convexity,
N\geq\sum_{i=2}^{r}a_{i}+\sum_{i=2}^{r}b_{i}\geq\binom{\left\lfloor\frac{r+2}{2}\right\rfloor}{2}+\binom{\left\lceil\frac{r+2}{2}\right\rceil}{2}=N_{r}
, so
M-N\leq\binom{r+1}{2}-N_{r}=\left\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor
, as required.
∎

.

It follows that the maximum number of 1-mark columns plus columns with at least two marks is at most

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}(p-1)\left(\binom{m}{2}-\left(\binom{r+1}{2}-M\right)\right)-qN+\sum_{(i,m+1-j)\in\mathcal{P}}n_{i,j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(i,m+1-j)\in\mathcal{P}}(p-1-n_{i,j})

\displaystyle=(p-1)\binom{m}{2}-qN-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(i,m+1-j)\in\mathcal{P}}(p-1-n_{i,j})

\displaystyle\leq(p-1)\binom{m}{2}-qN-q\left(\binom{r+1}{2}-M\right)

\displaystyle\leq(p-1)\binom{m}{2}-qN_{r},

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
2.17
.

There are at most
2^{m}+m2^{m-1}
columns with no mark, as they together form a matrix in
\Avoid(m,3,K_{2})
. Hence, the total number of columns is at most
2^{m}+m2^{m-1}+(p-1)\binom{m}{2}-qN_{r}=\forb(m,3,p\cdot K_{2})-qN_{r}
. This combined with Corollary
2.16
completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.19
.

Let
m\geq 2N_{r}+2
,
qN_{r}\leq 2^{r-3}
, and
A\in\Avoid(m,3,F(p-q,p,p,p-q))
. Suppose there is at least one scarce pair that is neither fixed nor weakly fixed, then
A
has at most
\forb(m,3,p\cdot K_{2})-qN_{r}
columns.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{1}
be the set of scarce pairs that are weakly fixed and let
\mathcal{P}_{2}
be the set of scarce pairs that are neither fixed nor weakly fixed. For every
(i,m+1-j)\in\mathcal{P}:=\mathcal{P}_{1}\cup\mathcal{P}_{2}
, let the
(i,j)
entry of
T_{r}
after the
N
operations be
a_{i,j}
, so
a_{i,j}=1
if
(i,m+1-j)\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
, and
a_{i,j}\geq 2
if
(i,m+1-j)\in\mathcal{P}_{2}
. From Lemma
2.14
, there are
n_{i,j}:=2^{r-a_{i,j}}
possible 1-mark columns with their unique marks at
(i,m+1-j)
.

Therefore, the maximum number of columns is at most, like in the proof of Lemma
2.18
,

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}2^{m}+m2^{m-1}+(p-1)\binom{m}{2}-qN-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{P}}(p-1-n_{i,j})

\displaystyle<2^{m}+m2^{m-1}+(p-1)\binom{m}{2}-qN-\frac{1}{2}\cdot 2^{r-2}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{m}+m2^{m-1}+(p-1)\binom{m}{2}-qN_{r}

\displaystyle=\forb(m,3,p\cdot K_{2})-qN_{r},

as required, where the first inequality uses
\emptyset\not=\mathcal{P}_{2}\subset\mathcal{P}
and
p-1-n_{i,j}\geq p-1-2^{r-2}>2^{r-2}
for all
(i,m+1-j)\in\mathcal{P}_{2}
, while the second inequality holds because
\frac{1}{2}\cdot 2^{r-2}\geq qN_{r}
.
∎

Lemma 2.20
.

If all scarce pairs are fixed or weakly fixed by
N
non-edges, then
N_{r}-N\leq\left\lceil\frac{r}{2}\right\rceil
.

Proof.

In the language of Lemma
2.17
, all entries in the triangular array
T_{r}
are at most 1 after
N
row or column operations, and we want to show
N\geq N_{r}-\left\lceil\frac{r}{2}\right\rceil
. We may assume that all operations are performed on the first
r-1
rows and columns, as the entries in the last row and the last column are already at most 1. Now, apply this set of operations on the first
r-1
rows and columns of
T_{r}
to the triangular array
T_{r-1}
instead, and note that they turn all
\binom{r}{2}
entries in
T_{r-1}
non-positive. Thus, by Lemma
2.17
we have
\binom{r}{2}-N\leq\binom{r}{2}-N_{r-1}
, and so
N\geq N_{r-1}=N_{r}-\left\lceil\frac{r}{2}\right\rceil
.
∎

For
i\in[r]
and
j\in[r+1-i]
, we say the
(i,j)
entry of
T_{r}
is fixed (or weakly fixed) if the corresponding scarce pair
(i,m+1-j)
is fixed (or weakly fixed).

Lemma 2.21
.

If all scarce pairs are fixed or weakly fixed by
N_{r}-t
non-edges for some
0\leq t\leq\left\lceil\frac{r}{2}\right\rceil
, then there are at least
t(\left\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\right\rfloor+1)
weakly fixed pairs.

Proof.

We use induction on
r
and work equivalently in the triangular array
T_{r}
. The
r=1,2
cases are easy to verify, so we assume
r\geq 3
.

For every
1\leq i\leq\left\lceil\frac{r+1}{2}\right\rceil
, let
a_{i}
be the number of operations performed on row
i
of
T_{r}
and let
b_{i}
be the number of operations performed on column
i
of
T_{r}
. For every
1\leq i\leq\left\lceil\frac{r+1}{2}\right\rceil
, since the pair
(1,m+1-i)
is fixed or weakly fixed, the
(1,i)
entry of
T_{r}
, which was
r-i+1
at the beginning, is now at most 1. Thus,
r-i+1-a_{1}-b_{i}\leq 1
, and so
a_{1}+b_{i}\geq r-i
. In particular,
a_{1}+b_{1}\geq r-1
. Moreover, if the
(1,i)
entry is non-positive, then
r-i+1-a_{1}-b_{i}\leq 0
, and so
a_{1}+b_{i}\geq r-i+1
. Similarly, by looking at the
(i,1)
entry of
T_{r}
for every
1\leq i\leq\left\lceil\frac{r+1}{2}\right\rceil
, we see that
a_{i}+b_{1}\geq r-i
, and
a_{i}+b_{1}\geq r-i+1
if this entry is non-positive.

Consider the subtriangle of size
r-2
obtained by removing the first row and column from
T_{r}
. Let
N_{r-2}-S
be the number of operations performed in this subtriangle. Then
r-1\leq a_{1}+b_{1}=N_{r}-t-(N_{r-2}-S)
, so
S\geq r-1-N_{r}+N_{r-2}+t=t-1
. Also, since all entries in this subtriangle are fixed or weakly fixed, we have
N_{r-2}-S\geq N_{r-2}-\left\lceil\frac{r-2}{2}\right\rceil=N_{r-2}-\left\lceil\frac{r}{2}\right\rceil+1
by Lemma
2.20
. It follows that
S\leq\left\lceil\frac{r}{2}\right\rceil-1
, and we can apply the induction hypothesis to see that there are at least
S(\left\lfloor\frac{r-2}{2}\right\rfloor+1)=S\left\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\right\rfloor
weakly fixed entries in this subtriangle.

If
S\geq t+1
, then as
S\leq\left\lceil\frac{r}{2}\right\rceil-1
from above, we have
t\leq\left\lceil\frac{r}{2}\right\rceil-2
. From the induction hypothesis, the subtriangle contains at least
(t+1)\left\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\right\rfloor\geq t(\left\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\right\rfloor+1)
weakly fixed entries, and hence so does the original triangle, as required. Otherwise,
S=t+x
for
x=-1
or 0. Then, we have
a_{1}+b_{1}=N_{r}-t-(N_{r-2}-S)=r+x
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{r+1}{2}\right\rceil}(a_{i}+b_{1})+\sum_{i=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{r+1}{2}\right\rceil}(a_{1}+b_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\left\lceil\frac{r-1}{2}\right\rceil(r+x)+(N_{r-2}-S)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{3}{4}r^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(x-1)r-x-t,\text{ if }r\text{ is even},\\
\frac{3}{4}r^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(x-2)r+\frac{1}{4}(1-6x-4t),\text{ if }r\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

On the other hand,

\sum_{i=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{r+1}{2}\right\rceil}(r-i+1)+\sum_{i=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{r+1}{2}\right\rceil}(r-i+1)=\begin{cases}\frac{3}{4}r^{2}-\frac{1}{2}r,\text{ if }r\text{ is even},\\
\frac{3}{4}r^{2}-r+\frac{1}{4},\text{ if }r\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

Therefore, if
r
is even, then at least

\frac{3}{4}r^{2}-\frac{1}{2}r-\left(\frac{3}{4}r^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(x-1)r-x-t\right)=x+t-\frac{1}{2}xr

of the inequalities
a_{i}+b_{1}\geq r-i+1
for
2\leq i\leq\frac{r}{2}+1
and
a_{1}+b_{i}\geq r-i+1
for
2\leq i\leq\frac{r}{2}+1
must fail to hold, which leads to
x+t-\frac{1}{2}xr
weakly fixed entries in the first row or column. If
x=-1
, this is
\frac{1}{2}r+t-1
, which along with the weakly fixed
(1,1)
entry and the at least
(t-1)\frac{r}{2}
weakly fixed entries in the subtriangle guaranteed by the induction step above gives the required
t(\frac{r}{2}+1)
weakly fixed entries overall. If
x=0
, this is
t
, and there are at least
t\frac{r}{2}
weakly fixed entries in the subtriangle by induction, so we have the required
t(\frac{r}{2}+1)
overall as well.

If
r
is odd, then at least

\frac{3}{4}r^{2}-r+\frac{1}{4}-\left(\frac{3}{4}r^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(x-2)r+\frac{1}{4}(1-6x-4t)\right)=\frac{3}{2}x+t-\frac{1}{2}xr

of the inequalities
a_{i}+b_{1}\geq r-i+1
for
2\leq i\leq\left\lceil\frac{r+1}{2}\right\rceil
and
a_{1}+b_{i}\geq r-i+1
for
2\leq i\leq\left\lceil\frac{r+1}{2}\right\rceil
must fail to hold, leading to
\frac{3}{2}x+t-\frac{1}{2}xr
weakly fixed entries in the first row or column. If
x=-1
, this is
\frac{1}{2}r+t-\frac{3}{2}=\left\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\right\rfloor+t-1
, which along with the weakly fixed
(1,1)
entry and the at least
(t-1)\left\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\right\rfloor
weakly fixed entries in the subtriangle guaranteed by induction gives the required
t(\left\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\right\rfloor+1)
weakly fixed entries overall. If
x=0
, this is
t
, and there are at least
t\left\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\right\rfloor
weakly fixed entries in the subtriangle by induction, so we have the required
t(\left\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\right\rfloor+1)
overall as well, finishing the proof.
∎

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

Let
P
and
Q
be the quantum-mechanical
momentum
and
position
operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, defined on a common domain where they satisfy the Heisenberg commutation relation
[P,Q]:=PQ-QP=\mathrm{i}I
. Consider the associated
Weyl operators

U=\mathrm{e}^{-bP}
and
V=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi bQ}
, with
b>0
. These are unbounded, self-adjoint operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, and on their joint domain they satisfy the Weyl relation:

UV=q^{2}VU,

(1.1)

where
q=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\pi b^{2}}
. From the perspective of the mathematical physics, an interesting fact was discovered in
[
1
]
, where some operators constructed from the Weyl operators
U
and
V
were shown to arise in the context of
local mirror symmetry
, as quantizations of
algebraic curves mirror
to
toric Calabi-Yau threefolds
. Their spectral properties have been of great interest after the development built in
[
9
]
. A typical example is provided by a local
del Pezzo Calabi-Yau threefold
, defined as the total space of the anti-canonical bundle over a toric
del Pezzo surface

S
.

Moreover, when
S=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
is the Hirzebruch surface, one obtains the operator

\displaystyle H(\zeta)=\mathrm{e}^{-bP}+\mathrm{e}^{bP}+\mathrm{e}^{2\pi bQ}+\zeta\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi bQ}=U+U^{-1}+V+\zeta V^{-1},

(1.2)

where
\zeta>0
is a
mass parameter
, so that
H=H(0)
, is the
functional-difference operator
H,
defined on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),
by

(H\psi)(x)=\psi(x+\mathrm{i}b)+\psi(x-\mathrm{i}b)+\mathrm{e}^{2\pi bx}\psi(x).

We observe that if instead of
S
one considers the weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}(1,m,n)
with
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, the corresponding operator is

\displaystyle H_{m,n}=\mathrm{e}^{-bP}+\mathrm{e}^{2\pi bQ}+\mathrm{e}^{bmP-2\pi bnQ}=U+V+q^{-mn}U^{-m}V^{-n},

(1.3)

and again
H=H_{1,0}
(see
[
9
]
for further details).

As noted in
[
13
]
, it was conjectured in
[
9
]
that for
\zeta>0
and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, the operators
H(\zeta)
and
H_{m,n}
have discrete spectra, their inverses are of trace class, and their Fredholm determinants admit explicit expressions in terms of enumerative invariants of the corresponding Calabi–Yau threefolds. Some of these conjectures were proven in
[
12
]
, where explicit formulas for
H(\zeta)^{-1}
and
H^{-1}_{m,n}
were obtained in terms of the modular quantum dilogarithm. Our paper is motivated by the analysis in Laptev, Schimmer and Takhtajan in
[
13
]
where the authors have shown that these operators have purely discrete spectrum and have proved that they have many other interesting properties. In particular that their Weyl fuctions
N(\lambda)
grow as
\log^{2}(\lambda),
when
\lambda\rightarrow\infty.
We are interested in the distribution of the eigenvalues of
H(\zeta).
In the next subsection we present our main result, see Theorem
1.1
. In what follows for any
t\in\mathbb{R,}
we use the notation
(t)_{+}:=\max\{t,0\}=(t+|t|)/2.

1.3.
Main result

In this paper we are interested in investigating the distribution of the eigenvalues of the operator
H(\zeta),
with
\zeta>0.
Indeed, Laptev, Schimmer and Takhtajan in
[
13
]
proved that the Riesz-means

\varkappa(\lambda):=\sum_{j\geq 1}(\lambda-\lambda_{j})_{+}

associated to the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j},
of
H(\zeta),

obeys the asymptotic behaviour

\varkappa(\lambda)\asymp\frac{1}{\pi^{2}b^{2}}\lambda\log^{2}(\lambda)+O(\lambda\log(\lambda)),\,\quad\lambda\rightarrow\infty,

(1.4)

in the sense that for some constants
C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathbb{R},

\frac{1}{\pi^{2}b^{2}}\lambda\log^{2}(\lambda)+C_{1}(\lambda\log(\lambda))\leq\varkappa(\lambda)\leq\frac{1}{\pi^{2}b^{2}}\lambda\log^{2}(\lambda)+C_{2}(\lambda\log(\lambda)),\,\quad\lambda\rightarrow\infty.

(1.5)

Given continuity to the techniques from
[
13
]
, our approach reveals that a suitable
dyadic decomposition
of the phase space, see (
1.6
) in Remark
1.3
, shows evidence of the existence of other 5 lower orders terms in the behaviour of
\varkappa(\lambda)
. These lower order terms could involve, when
\lambda\rightarrow\infty,
terms with sizes,
\log(\lambda),

\log^{2}(\lambda),

-\lambda\log(\lambda),

(\lambda-\log_{2}(\lambda))\log(\lambda-\log_{2}(\lambda)),
and also of size
-(\lambda-\log_{2}(\lambda))\log^{2}(\lambda-\log_{2}(\lambda)).

The following is our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\zeta>0.
We have the following inequality for the Riesz-means of the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}
of
H(\zeta),

\displaystyle\varkappa(\lambda)=\sum_{j\geq 1}(\lambda-\lambda_{j})_{+}\leq\frac{1}{\pi^{2}b^{2}}(\lambda\log^{2}(\lambda)),

when
\lambda
is large enough. Moreover,

\displaystyle\varkappa(\lambda)

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{1}{\pi^{2}b^{2}}(\lambda\log^{2}(\lambda)-(\lambda-\log_{2}(\lambda))\log^{2}(\lambda-\log_{2}(\lambda))

\displaystyle-2\lambda\log(\lambda)+2(\lambda-\log_{2}(\lambda))\log(\lambda-\log_{2}(\lambda))+\log^{2}(\lambda)+\log_{2}(\lambda)),

when
\lambda
is large enough.

2.1.
Weyl operators

Let us consider the standard Lebesgue spaces
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),
defined by

f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\Longleftrightarrow f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\textnormal{ is Lebesgue measurable, and }\smallint_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|dx<\infty,

\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\Longleftrightarrow\psi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\textnormal{ is Lebesgue measurable, and }\smallint_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi(x)|^{2}dx<\infty,

where
dx
denotes the Lebesgue measure on the
\sigma
-algebra of Borel sets in
\mathbb{R}.

Let us
consider the associated Weyl operators
U=\mathrm{e}^{-bP}
and
V=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi bQ}
, with
b>0
. These are unbounded, self-adjoint operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. In the coordinate representation, the action of the operators
P
and
Q
is given by
(P\psi)(x)=\mathrm{i}\psi^{\prime}(x)
and
(Q\psi)(x)=x\psi(x)
. The Weyl operators act as
(U\psi)(x)=\psi(x+\mathrm{i}b)
and
(V\psi)(x)=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi bx}\psi(x)
. Their respective domains are characterized by:

\displaystyle D(U)

\displaystyle=\left\{\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}):\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi bk}(\mathscr{F}{\psi})(k)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\right\},

\displaystyle D(V)

\displaystyle=\left\{\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}):\,\mathrm{e}^{2\pi bx}\psi(x)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\right\},

where
\mathscr{F}
denotes the Fourier transform, densely defined for
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
by:

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(k)=(\mathscr{F}f)(k)=\smallint_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}kx}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

We also remark that alternatively, the domain
D(U)
can be described as the set of functions
\psi(x)
that extend analytically to the strip

\left\{z=x+\mathrm{i}y\in\mathbb{C}:\,0<y<b\right\},

with
\psi(x+\mathrm{i}y)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for all
0\leq y<b
, and such that the limit

\psi(x+\mathrm{i}b-\mathrm{i}0)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\psi(x+\mathrm{i}b-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon),

exists with respect to the topology induced by the norm in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. We denote this limit simply by
\psi(x+\mathrm{i}b)
. The domain of the inverse operator
U^{-1}
admits a similar characterisation.

2.2.
The spectral analysis of Laptev, Schimmer and Takhtajan for
H(\zeta)

Let us use the Weyl operators
U
and
V
in (
1.1
) in order to define

H=U+U^{-1}+V,

which, in the coordinate representation, becomes a functional-difference operator with exponential potential given by

(H\psi)(x)=\psi(x+\mathrm{i}b)+\psi(x-\mathrm{i}b)+\mathrm{e}^{2\pi bx}\psi(x).

As a twisted verion of
H,
for any
\zeta>0,
we consider the operators

\displaystyle H(\zeta)=\mathrm{e}^{-bP}+\mathrm{e}^{bP}+\mathrm{e}^{2\pi bQ}+\zeta\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi bQ}=U+U^{-1}+V+\zeta V^{-1}.

(2.1)

Remark 2.4
.

The operator

2.2.1.
The Coherent State Representation

Let
g
be the Gaussian function

g(x)=(a/\pi)^{1/4}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{a}{2}x^{2}},

where
a>0
. Observe that
g
belongs to the unit ball in

For

the classical coherent state transform (see e.g.
[
17

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}(k,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}kx}g(x-y)\psi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

For
\psi\in D(H_{0})
we have the representation

\displaystyle\langle H_{0}\psi,\psi\rangle=d_{1}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}2\cosh(2\pi bk)|\widetilde{\psi}(k,y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}k\,\mathrm{d}y,

(2.4)

where

d_{1}=\mathrm{e}^{-ab^{2}/4}<1.

Similarly, for
\psi\in D(W),
where

\displaystyle\langle W\psi,\psi\rangle=d_{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}2\cosh(2\pi by)|\widetilde{\psi}(k,y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}k\,\mathrm{d}y,

where
d_{2}=e^{-(\pi b)^{2}/a}.

Then, one has the identity, see

\langle H(1)\psi,\psi\rangle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}2(d_{1}\cosh(2\pi bk)+d_{2}\cosh(2\pi by))|\widetilde{\psi}(k,y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}k\,\mathrm{d}y.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Consider the integral

I:=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}}\big(\lambda-2d_{1}\cosh(2\pi bk)-2d_{2}\cosh(2\pi by)\big)_{+}\,dk\,dy,

where
d_{1},d_{2},b>0
are as above and
\lambda
is such that the integrand is not identically zero, and in particular such that
\lambda>2d_{1}+2d_{2}
.

3.1.
The upper bound
(\pi b)^{-2}\lambda\log^{2}\lambda

Observe that for
k,y\geq 0,
we have the following analysis.
The integrand is positive in the region of the
(k,y)
-plane where

\lambda-2d_{1}\cosh(2\pi bk)-2d_{2}\cosh(2\pi by)>0.

Fix
k\geq 0
and
\lambda
large enough. Then the condition in
y
becomes

\cosh(2\pi by)<\frac{\lambda-2d_{1}\cosh(2\pi bk)}{2d_{2}}.

Since
\cosh(2\pi by)\geq 1
, it is necessary that

\lambda-2d_{1}\cosh(2\pi bk)>2d_{2},

which defines the domain in
k
:

0\leq k<k_{0}:=\frac{1}{2\pi b}\operatorname{arccosh}\left(\frac{\lambda-2d_{2}}{2d_{1}}\right).

For fixed
k
, define

y_{\max}(k):=\frac{1}{2\pi b}\operatorname{arccosh}\left(\frac{\lambda-2d_{1}\cosh(2\pi bk)}{2d_{2}}\right).

Then,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y_{\max}(k)}\big(\lambda-2d_{1}\cosh(2\pi bk)-2d_{2}\cosh(2\pi by)\big)\,dy

\displaystyle=\left(\lambda-2d_{1}\cosh(2\pi bk)\right)y_{\max}(k)-\frac{2d_{2}}{2\pi b}\sinh\big(2\pi by_{\max}(k)\big).

Using the identity
\sinh(\operatorname{arccosh}(u))=\sqrt{u^{2}-1}
and setting

u(k):=\frac{\lambda-2d_{1}\cosh(2\pi bk)}{2d_{2}},

we have

y_{\max}(k)=\frac{1}{2\pi b}\operatorname{arccosh}u(k),\quad\sinh(2\pi by_{\max}(k))=\sqrt{u(k)^{2}-1}.

Hence,

I\leq\int_{0}^{k_{0}}\frac{1}{2\pi b}\left[\big(\lambda-2d_{1}\cosh(2\pi bk)\big)\operatorname{arccosh}u(k)-2d_{2}\sqrt{u(k)^{2}-1}\right]dk,

where

k_{0}=\frac{1}{2\pi b}\operatorname{arccosh}\left(\frac{\lambda-2d_{2}}{2d_{1}}\right).

Using the change of variables
u=\cosh(2\pi bk)
, with

dk=\frac{1}{2\pi b}\frac{du}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}},

and the integration limits

u\in\left[1,\frac{\lambda-2d_{2}}{2d_{1}}\right],

we get

I\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi b)^{2}}\int_{1}^{\frac{\lambda-2d_{2}}{2d_{1}}}\frac{(\lambda-2d_{1}u)\operatorname{arccosh}\left(\frac{\lambda-2d_{1}u}{2d_{2}}\right)-2d_{2}\sqrt{\left(\frac{\lambda-2d_{1}u}{2d_{2}}\right)^{2}-1}}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}\,du

\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi b)^{2}}\int_{1}^{\frac{\lambda-2d_{2}}{2d_{1}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}\,du\times\lambda\operatorname{arccosh}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2d_{2}}\right)

\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi b)^{2}}\operatorname{arccosh}\left(\frac{\lambda-2d_{2}}{2d_{1}}\right)\times\lambda\operatorname{arccosh}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2d_{2}}\right)

\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi b)^{2}}\operatorname{log}\left(\frac{\lambda-2d_{2}}{d_{1}}\right)\times\lambda\operatorname{log}\left(\frac{\lambda}{d_{2}}\right).

In consequence we have proved that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 1}(\lambda-\lambda_{j})_{+}\leq I_{1}=

\displaystyle 4{\iint}_{\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}}\big(\lambda-2d_{1}\cosh(2\pi bk)-2d_{2}\cosh(2\pi by)\big)_{+}dkdy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(\pi b)^{2}}\operatorname{log}\left(\frac{\lambda-2d_{2}}{d_{1}}\right)\times\lambda\operatorname{log}\left(\frac{\lambda}{d_{2}}\right).

In consequence, when
\lambda
is large enough, we get

\sum_{j\geq 1}(\lambda-\lambda_{j})_{+}\leq\frac{1}{\pi^{2}b^{2}}\lambda\log^{2}\lambda.

(3.3)

3.2.
The dyadic analysis of the phase space: an upper bound

Now, we are going to prove that

\displaystyle\varkappa(\lambda)

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{1}{\pi^{2}b^{2}}(\lambda\log^{2}(\lambda)-(\lambda-\log_{2}(\lambda))\log^{2}(\lambda-\log_{2}(\lambda))

\displaystyle-2\lambda\log(\lambda)+2(\lambda-\log_{2}(\lambda))\log(\lambda-\log_{2}(\lambda))

\displaystyle+\log^{2}(\lambda)+\log_{2}(\lambda)).

Let us analyse
I_{1}.
So, let us take
d=\min\{d_{1},d_{2}\},
let us assume that
\lambda>4d-1,
and let us use that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 4\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}}\big(\lambda-2d_{1}\cosh(2\pi bk)-2d_{2}\cosh(2\pi by)\big)_{+}dkdy

\displaystyle\leq 4\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}}\big(\lambda-2d\cosh(2\pi bk)-2d\cosh(2\pi by)\big)_{+}dkdy.

Let us adopt the perspective of the Littlewood-Paley theory for pseudo-differential operators, and let us decompose the set
\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}
in the family of dyadic regions

\Omega_{j}=\{(y,k):2^{j-1}\leq\lambda-2d\cosh(2\pi bk)-2d\cosh(2\pi by)\leq 2^{j}\},\quad j\in\mathbb{Z}.

(3.4)

Then, we have that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 4\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}}\big(\lambda-2d\cosh(2\pi bk)-2d\cosh(2\pi by)\big)_{+}dkdy\leq 4\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\iint_{\Omega_{j}}2^{j}dkdy.

Note that for any
j\in\mathbb{Z},
we have that

\displaystyle\Omega_{j}=\{(y,k):\frac{\lambda-2^{j}}{2d}\leq\cosh(2\pi bk)+\cosh(2\pi by)\leq\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{2d}\}.

Note that

\forall(y,k),\quad 2\leq\cosh(2\pi bk)+\cosh(2\pi by).

In consequence,

\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{2d}<2\Longleftrightarrow j>\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}+1,\textnormal{ and for these values of }j,\,\quad\Omega_{j}=\{\emptyset\}.

In consequence, the decomposition of
\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}
in the sets
\Omega_{j}
runs over
j\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}+1.

In consequence, the change of variables
(u,v)=(\cosh(2\pi bk),\cosh(2\pi by))
leads to

\displaystyle 4\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\iint_{\Omega_{j}}2^{j}dkdy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}b^{2}}\sum_{j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}+1}2^{j}\iint_{\{(u,v):\frac{\lambda-2^{j}}{2d}\leq u+v\leq\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{2d},\,u,v\geq 1\}}\frac{dudv}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}\sqrt{v^{2}-1}}.

Let us analyse the integral

R_{j}:=\iint_{\{(u,v):\frac{\lambda-2^{j}}{2d}\leq u+v\leq\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{2d},\,u,v\geq 1\}}\frac{dudv}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}\sqrt{v^{2}-1}}.

Case 1:
\frac{\lambda-2^{j}}{2d}<1.
In this case
\frac{\log(\lambda-2d)}{\log 2}<j,
and with
B=\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{2d},
we have that

\displaystyle R_{j}

\displaystyle=\iint_{\{(u,v):\frac{\lambda-2^{j}}{2d}\leq u+v\leq\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{2d},\,u,v\geq 1\}}\frac{dudv}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}\sqrt{v^{2}-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{1}^{B}\frac{1}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}\int\limits_{1}^{B-u}\frac{dvdu}{\sqrt{v^{2}-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{1}^{B}\frac{du}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}\int\limits_{1}^{B-1}\frac{dv}{\sqrt{v^{2}-1}}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{1}^{B}\frac{\cosh^{-1}(B-1)du}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{1}^{B}\frac{\log(B-1+\sqrt{(B-1)^{2}-1})du}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\log(2B)\int\limits_{1}^{B}\frac{du}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}=\log(2B)\cosh^{-1}(B)\leq(\log(2B))^{2}.

Case 2:
\frac{\lambda-2^{j}}{2d}\geq 1,
and then
\frac{\log(\lambda-2d)}{\log 2}\geq j.
Then, with
A=\frac{\lambda-2^{j}}{2d}
and with
B=\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{2d},
we have that

\displaystyle R_{j}

\displaystyle=\iint_{\{(u,v):\frac{\lambda-2^{j}}{2d}\leq u+v\leq\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{2d},\,u,v\geq 1\}}\frac{dudv}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}\sqrt{v^{2}-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{1}^{A}\frac{1}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}\int\limits_{1}^{B-u}\frac{dvdu}{\sqrt{v^{2}-1}}+\int\limits_{A}^{B}\frac{1}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}\int\limits_{1}^{B-u}\frac{dvdu}{\sqrt{v^{2}-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\cosh^{-1}(A)\log(2B)+(\cosh^{-1}(B)-\cosh^{-1}(A))\log(2B)

\displaystyle\leq(\log(2B))^{2}.

The analysis above allows us to estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}+1}2^{j}R_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}+1:-\infty<j\leq 0}2^{j}R_{j}+\sum_{0<j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}+1}2^{j}R_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}+1:-\infty<j\leq 0}2^{j}\log^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{d}\right)+\sum_{0<j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}+1}2^{j}R_{j}

\displaystyle\leq 2\log^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}\right)+\sum_{1\leq j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}+1}2^{j}R_{j}

\displaystyle\leq 2\log^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}\right)+\sum_{1\leq j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}+1}2^{j}\left(\log\left(\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{d}\right)\right)^{2}.

Observe that in the interval

1\leq x\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}+1,

we have that

\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{d\log 2}-\frac{1}{d}\leq\frac{\lambda-x}{d}\leq\frac{\lambda-1}{d},

and we can assure that the lower bound
\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{d\log 2}-\frac{1}{d}>1,
if
\lambda
is large enough.
In this intervale the function
f(x)=\left(\log\left(\frac{\lambda-x}{d}\right)\right)^{2}
is decreasing.
We can make the estimate of the last series as a Riemann summation on the interval
[1,\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}+1]
with the interior points being
x_{j}=2^{j-1}
and with
\Delta x_{j}\leq 2^{j-1}-2^{j-2}=2^{j-2}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}+1}2^{j}\left(\log\left(\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{d}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=4\sum_{1\leq j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}+1}2^{j-2}\left(\log\left(\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{d}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\int_{1}^{\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}+1}\left(\log\left(\frac{\lambda-x}{d}\right)\right)^{2}dx.

A change of variables in the last integral allows us to compute

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}+1}\left(\log\left(\frac{\lambda-x}{d}\right)\right)^{2}dx=d\int_{\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{d\log 2}-\frac{1}{d}}^{\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{1}{d}}\left(\log\left(t\right)\right)^{2}dt

\displaystyle=d\left(\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{1}{d}\right)\log^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{1}{d}\right)-2\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{1}{d}\right)\log\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{1}{d}\right)+2\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{1}{d}\right)\right)

\displaystyle-d\left(\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{d\log 2}-\frac{1}{d}\right)\log^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{d\log 2}-\frac{1}{d}\right)\right)

\displaystyle-d\left(-2\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{d\log 2}-\frac{1}{d}\right)\log\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{d\log 2}-\frac{1}{d}\right)+2\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{d\log 2}-\frac{1}{d}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\lambda-1\right)\log^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{1}{d}\right)-2\left(\lambda-1\right)\log\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{1}{d}\right)+2\left(\lambda-1\right)

\displaystyle-\left(\lambda-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}-1\right)\log^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{d\log 2}-\frac{1}{d}\right)

\displaystyle+2\left(\lambda-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}-1\right)\log\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{d\log 2}-\frac{1}{d}\right)-2\left(\lambda-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\lambda-1\right)\log^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{1}{d}\right)-2\left(\lambda-1\right)\log\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{1}{d}\right)

\displaystyle-\left(\lambda-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}-1\right)\log^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{d\log 2}-\frac{1}{d}\right)

\displaystyle+2\left(\lambda-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}-1\right)\log\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{d\log 2}-\frac{1}{d}\right)+\frac{2\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}.

In consequence,

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 4\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\iint_{\Omega_{j}}2^{j}dkdy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi^{2}b^{2}}\sum_{j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}+1}2^{j}R_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\pi^{2}b^{2}}\log^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{4}{\pi^{2}b^{2}}\left(\left(\lambda-1\right)\log^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{1}{d}\right)-2\left(\lambda-1\right)\log\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{1}{d}\right)\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{\pi^{2}b^{2}}\left(\lambda-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}-1\right)\log^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{d\log 2}-\frac{1}{d}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{8}{\pi^{2}b^{2}}\left(\left(\lambda-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}-1\right)\log\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{d\log 2}-\frac{1}{d}\right)+\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}\right).

So, when
\lambda
is large enough, one can estimate

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\pi^{2}b^{2}}(\lambda\log^{2}(\lambda)-2\lambda\log(\lambda)-(\lambda-\log_{2}(\lambda))\log^{2}(\lambda-\log_{2}(\lambda))

\displaystyle+2(\lambda-\log_{2}(\lambda))\log(\lambda-\log_{2}(\lambda))+\log^{2}(\lambda)+\log_{2}(\lambda)).

3.3.
The dyadic analysis of the phase space: a lower bound

For the analysis of
I_{2},
let us take
2d^{\prime}=\max\{2/d_{1},2/d_{2}\},
and then

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\geq 4\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}}\big(\lambda-\frac{2}{d_{1}}\cosh(2\pi bk)-\frac{2}{d_{2}}\cosh(2\pi by)\big)_{+}dkdy

\displaystyle\geq 4\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}}\big(\lambda-2d^{\prime}\cosh(2\pi bk)-2d^{\prime}\cosh(2\pi by)\big)_{+}dkdy.

Let us decompose the set
\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}
in the family of dyadic zones

\widetilde{\Omega}_{j}=\{(k,y):2^{j-1}\leq\lambda-2d^{\prime}\cosh(2\pi bk)-2d^{\prime}\cosh(2\pi by)\leq 2^{j}\},\quad j\in\mathbb{Z}.

(3.5)

We have the estimate

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\geq 4\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}}\big(\lambda-2d^{\prime}\cosh(2\pi bk)-2d^{\prime}\cosh(2\pi by)\big)_{+}dkdy\asymp 4\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\iint_{\widetilde{\Omega}_{j}}2^{j}dkdy.

Note that for any
j\in\mathbb{Z},
we have that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Omega}_{j}=\{(y,k):\frac{\lambda-2^{j}}{2d^{\prime}}\leq\cosh(2\pi bk)+\cosh(2\pi by)\leq\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{2d^{\prime}}\}.

So, in our further analysis we fix
\lambda>4d^{\prime}-1.

Note that

\forall(y,k),\quad 2\leq\cosh(2\pi bk)+\cosh(2\pi by).

In consequence,

\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{2d^{\prime}}<2\Longleftrightarrow j>\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{\log 2}+1,\textnormal{ and for these values of }j,\,\quad\widetilde{\Omega}_{j}=\{\emptyset\}.

Observe that the decomposition of
\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}
into the sets
\widetilde{\Omega}_{j}
runs over
j\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{\log 2}+1.

The change of variables
(u,v)=(\cosh(2\pi bk),\cosh(2\pi by))
leads to

\displaystyle 4\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\iint_{\widetilde{\Omega}_{j}}2^{j}dkdy

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{1}{\pi^{2}b^{2}}\sum_{j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{\log 2}+1}2^{j}\iint_{\{(u,v):\frac{\lambda-2^{j}}{2d^{\prime}}\leq u+v\leq\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{2d^{\prime}},\,u,v\geq 1\}}\frac{dudv}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}\sqrt{v^{2}-1}}.

Let us analyse the integral

R_{j}:=\iint_{\{(u,v):\frac{\lambda-2^{j}}{2d^{\prime}}\leq u+v\leq\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{2d^{\prime}},\,u,v\geq 1\}}\frac{dudv}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}\sqrt{v^{2}-1}}.

Case 1:
\frac{\lambda-2^{j}}{2d^{\prime}}<1.
In this case
\frac{\log(\lambda-2d^{\prime})}{\log 2}<j.
As above let us define
A=\frac{\lambda-2^{j}}{2d^{\prime}}
and
B=\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{2d^{\prime}}.

Since
\lambda\rightarrow\infty,
we can fix a constant
M_{0}\gg 2d^{\prime},
such that
\lambda\geq M_{0}+1.
Then, for
j\leq 0,
we can chose
\alpha<1,
such that

B=\frac{2d^{\prime}}{\lambda-2^{j-1}}\leq\frac{2d^{\prime}}{\lambda-1}\leq\frac{2d^{\prime}}{M_{0}}<\alpha<1.

Note that this choice of
\alpha
allows us to compare
1/B<\alpha<1,
or equivalently,
1<B\alpha<B,
with
\alpha
independent of
\lambda
and of
j\neq 0.
In general, observe that for any
j
with
\widetilde{\Omega}_{j}\neq\{\emptyset\},
that
B\geq 2,
and then one can always choose
1/B\leq 1/2\leq\alpha<1,
independent of
\lambda
and of
j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}+1,
satisfying that
1\leq B\alpha<B.

Keeping in mind this choice of
\alpha

we have that

\displaystyle R_{j}

\displaystyle=\iint_{\{(u,v):\frac{\lambda-2^{j}}{2d^{\prime}}\leq u+v\leq\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{2d^{\prime}},\,u,v\geq 1\}}\frac{dudv}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}\sqrt{v^{2}-1}}

\displaystyle\asymp\int\limits_{1}^{B}\frac{1}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}\int\limits_{1}^{B-u}\frac{dvdu}{\sqrt{v^{2}-1}}

\displaystyle\geq\int\limits_{1}^{\alpha B}\frac{\cosh^{-1}(B-u)du}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}

\displaystyle\geq\int\limits_{1}^{\alpha B}\frac{\cosh^{-1}(B-B\alpha)du}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}

\displaystyle=\cosh^{-1}(B(1-\alpha))\cosh^{-1}(\alpha B)\geq\log^{2}(B\varkappa),

where
0<\varkappa=\min(\alpha,1-\alpha).
Note that the choice
\alpha=1/2,
optimises the value of
\varkappa=1/2.
In this case we have estimated

R_{j}\gg\log^{2}(B/2).

Case 2:
\frac{\lambda-2^{j}}{2d^{\prime}}\geq 1,
and then
\frac{\log(\lambda-2d^{\prime})}{\log 2}\geq j.
With
A=\frac{\lambda-2^{j}}{2d^{\prime}}
and with
B=\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{2d^{\prime}},
we have that

\displaystyle R_{j}

\displaystyle=\iint_{\{(u,v):\frac{\lambda-2^{j}}{2d^{\prime}}\leq u+v\leq\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{2d^{\prime}},\,u,v\geq 1\}}\frac{dudv}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}\sqrt{v^{2}-1}}

\displaystyle\asymp\int\limits_{1}^{A}\frac{1}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}\int\limits_{1}^{B-u}\frac{dvdu}{\sqrt{v^{2}-1}}+\int\limits_{A}^{B}\frac{1}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}\int\limits_{1}^{B-u}\frac{dvdu}{\sqrt{v^{2}-1}}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{1}^{B}\frac{1}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}\int\limits_{1}^{B-u}\frac{dvdu}{\sqrt{v^{2}-1}}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{1}^{B}\frac{\cosh^{-1}(B-u)du}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}.

Observe that for any
j
with
\widetilde{\Omega}_{j}\neq\{\emptyset\},
one has that
B\geq 2,
and then one can always choose
1/B\leq 1/2<\alpha<1
independent of
\lambda
and of
j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{\log 2}+1,
satisfying
1\leq B\alpha<B.
The analysis above allows us to estimate

\int\limits_{1}^{B}\frac{\cosh^{-1}(B-u)du}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}\geq\int\limits_{1}^{\alpha B}\frac{\cosh^{-1}(B-u)du}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}\geq\cosh^{-1}(B(1-\alpha))\cosh^{-1}(\alpha B)

\geq\log^{2}(B/2).

In consequence,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{\log 2}+1}2^{j}R_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{\log 2}+1:-\infty<j\leq 0}2^{j}R_{j}+\sum_{0<j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{\log 2}+1}2^{j}R_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{\log 2}+1:-\infty<j\leq 0}2^{j}\log^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{4d^{\prime}}\right)+\sum_{0<j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{\log 2}+1}2^{j}R_{j}

\displaystyle\geq\log^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda-1}{4d^{\prime}}\right)+\sum_{1\leq j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{\log 2}+1}2^{j}R_{j}

\displaystyle\gg\log^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda-1}{4d^{\prime}}\right)+\sum_{1\leq j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}+1}2^{j}\left(\log\left(\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{4d^{\prime}}\right)\right)^{2}.

As above, we can make the estimate of the last series as a Riemann summation. Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d)}{\log 2}+1}2^{j}\left(\log\left(\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{4d^{\prime}}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=4\sum_{1\leq j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{\log 2}+1}2^{j-2}\left(\log\left(\frac{\lambda-2^{j-1}}{4d^{\prime}}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\asymp\int_{1}^{\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{\log 2}+1}\left(\log\left(\frac{\lambda-x}{4d^{\prime}}\right)\right)^{2}dx.

A change of variables in the last integral allows us to compute

\displaystyle=4d^{\prime}\int_{\frac{\lambda}{4d^{\prime}}-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{4d^{\prime}\log 2}-\frac{1}{4d^{\prime}}}^{\frac{4\lambda}{d^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{4d^{\prime}}}\left(\log\left(t\right)\right)^{2}d^{\prime}t

\displaystyle=4\left(\lambda-1\right)\log^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{4d^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{4d^{\prime}}\right)-8\left(\lambda-1\right)\log\left(\frac{\lambda}{4d^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{4d^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle-4\left(\lambda-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{\log 2}-1\right)\log^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{d^{\prime}}-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{4d^{\prime}\log 2}-\frac{1}{d^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle+8\left(\lambda-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{\log 2}-1\right)\log\left(\frac{\lambda}{d^{\prime}}-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{4d^{\prime}\log 2}-\frac{1}{d^{\prime}}\right)+\frac{8\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{\log 2}.

In consequence,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\geq 4\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\iint_{\Omega_{j}}2^{j}dkdy

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{1}{\pi^{2}b^{2}}\sum_{j\leq\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{\log 2}+1}2^{j}R_{j}

\displaystyle\gg\frac{1}{\pi^{2}b^{2}}\log^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda-1}{4d^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\pi^{2}b^{2}}\left(\left(\lambda-1\right)\log^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{4d^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{4d^{\prime}}\right)-2\left(\lambda-1\right)\log\left(\frac{\lambda}{4d^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{4d^{\prime}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\pi^{2}b^{2}}\left(\lambda-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{\log 2}-1\right)\log^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{4d^{\prime}}-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{4d^{\prime}\log 2}-\frac{1}{4d^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{\pi^{2}b^{2}}\left(\left(\lambda-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{\log 2}-1\right)\log\left(\frac{\lambda}{4d^{\prime}}-\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{4d^{\prime}\log 2}-\frac{1}{4d^{\prime}}\right)+\frac{\log(\lambda-4d^{\prime})}{\log 2}\right).

In consequence, when
\lambda\rightarrow\infty,
we acomplish the two conditions on
\lambda
imposed above, namely that,
\lambda>4d-1,4d^{\prime}-1,
and we allow for the estimate

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\gg\frac{1}{\pi^{2}b^{2}}(\lambda\log^{2}(\lambda)-2\lambda\log(\lambda)-(\lambda-\log_{2}(\lambda))\log^{2}(\lambda-\log_{2}(\lambda))

\displaystyle+2(\lambda-\log_{2}(\lambda))\log(\lambda-\log_{2}(\lambda))+\log^{2}(\lambda)+\log_{2}(\lambda)).

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is complete.

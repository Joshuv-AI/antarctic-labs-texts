Differential Operators, Multiple Schwarz Functions, and the Bohr Radius of Stable Harmonic Maps
Sujoy Majumder, Nabadwip Sarkar and Molla Basir Ahamed ∗

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the Bohr phenomenon for differential operators D D and 𝒟 \mathscr{D} of stable harmonic mappings involving multiple Schwarz functions in ℬ n \mathcal{B}_{n} , using distance formulations. By constructing suitable combinations of multiple Schwarz functions, we establish sharp and improved Bohr-type inequalities for these mappings. The corresponding Bohr radii are also determined for certain subclasses of stable harmonic functions and their associated differential operators. Moreover, Bohr-Rogosinski-type inequalities are derived, which highlights the influence of multiple Schwarz functions on the geometric properties of stable harmonic mappings. All the radii are determined, and we prove that each one is the best possible.

1.
Introduction

The origin of the Bohr phenomenon lies in the seminal work by Harald Bohr

[
11
]
in
1914
for the analytic functions of the form
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
defined on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
with
|f(z)|<1
. Study of Bohr phenomenon for different classes of functions with various settings becomes a subject of great interests during past several years and an extensive research work has been done by many authors but Bohr phenomenon is not much investigated for the classes of harmonic mappings. In this paper, we are mainly interested to study Bohr phenomenon with suitable settings in order to establish certain harmonic analogue of some Bohr inequality valid for analytic functions.

For a continuously differentiable complex-valued mapping
f(z)=u(z)+iv(z)
,
z=x+iy
, we use the common notions for its formal derivatives:

\displaystyle f_{z}=\frac{1}{2}\left(f_{x}-if_{y}\right)\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;f_{\bar{z}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(f_{x}+if_{y}\right).

We say that
f
is a harmonic mapping in a simply connected domain
\Omega
if
f
is twice continuously differentiable and satisfies the Laplacian equation
\Delta f=4f_{z\bar{z}}=0
in
\Omega
, where
\Delta
is the complex Laplacian operator defined by

\Delta={\partial^{2}}/{\partial x^{2}}+{\partial^{2}}/{\partial y^{2}}.

Methods of harmonic mappings have been applied to study and solve fluid flow problems (see
[
8
,
13
]
). For instance, in 2012, Aleman and Constantin
[
8
]
established a connection between harmonic mappings and ideal fluid flows. They developed an ingenious technique to solve the incompressible two-dimensional Euler equations in terms of univalent harmonic mappings (see
[
13
]
for details).

Let
\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
be the class of functions harmonic in
\Omega
. It is well-known that functions in the class
\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
has the following representation
f=h+\overline{g}
, where
h
and
g
both are analytic functions in
\Omega
. The famous Lewy’s theorem
[
23
]
in
1936
states that a harmonic mapping
f=h+\overline{g}
is locally univalent on
\Omega
if, and only if, the determinant
|J_{f}|
of its Jacobian matrix
J_{f}
does not vanish on
\Omega
, where

|J_{f}|:=|f_{z}|^{2}-|f_{\bar{z}}|^{2}==|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}-|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\neq 0.

In view of this result, a locally univalent harmonic mapping is sense-preserving if
|J_{f}(z)|>0
and sense-reversing if
|J_{f}(z)|<0
in
\Omega
. For detailed information about the harmonic mappings, we refer the reader to
[
12
,
14
]
. In
[
22
]
, Kayumov
et al.
first established the harmonic extension of the classical Bohr theorem, since then investigating on the Bohr-type inequalities for certain class of harmonic mappings becomes an interesting topic of research in geometric function theory.

Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the set of all analytic functions of the form
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
defined on
\mathbb{D}
and we define the class
\mathcal{B}=\{f\in\mathcal{A}:|f(z)|\leq 1\;\mbox{in}\;\mathbb{D}\}
. Let us first recall the theorem of Bohr
[
11
]
in
1914
, which inspired a lot in the recent years.

Theorem A
.

[
11
]

If
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}
, then

M_{f}(r):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 1\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;|z|=r\leq\frac{1}{3}.

(1.1)

Initially, Bohr shows the inequality (
1.1
) for
|z|\leq 1/6
, but later M. Riesz, I. Schur and F. Wiener subsequently improved the inequality (
1.1
) for
|z|\leq 1/3
and showed that the constant
1/3
is best possible. It is quite natural that the constant
1/3
and the inequality (
1.1
) are called respectively, the Bohr radius and Bohr inequality for the class
\mathcal{B}
. Moreover, for:

f_{a}(z)=\frac{a-z}{1-az},\;\;a\in[0,1)

it follows easily that
M_{f_{a}}(r)>1
if, and only if,
r>1/(1+2a)
, which shows that
1/3
is best possible when for
a\rightarrow 1
.

Let
S_{r}(f)
denote the area of the image of the subdisk
\mathbb{D}_{r}\subseteq\mathbb{D}
under the mapping
f
. For brevity, and when there is no confusion, we denote this area simply as
S_{r}
. Recently, Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
21
]
improved Bohr’s inequality (
1.1
) in various forms. For example, the following inequality was obtained in
[
20
]
(see also
[
21
]
).

Theorem B
.

Suppose that
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then

\displaystyle|a_{0}|+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\frac{16}{9}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)\leq 1\;\mbox{for}\;|z|=r\leq\frac{1}{3}.

(1.2)

The numbers
1/3
and
16/9
cannot be improved.

It is worth recalling that if the first term,
|a_{0}|
, in equation (
1.2
) is replaced by
|a_{0}|^{2}
, then the constants
1/3
and
16/9
could be replaced by
1/2
and
9/8
, respectively (see
[
20
]
). More recently, the authors in
[
19
]
improved Theorem B by replacing the quantity
S_{r}/\pi
by
S_{r}/(\pi-S_{r})
.

We see that equation (
1.1
) can be written as

d\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|,|a_{0}|\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|\leq 1-|f(0)|=d(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D})),

(1.3)

where
d
is the Euclidean distance and
\partial f(\mathbb{D})
is the boundary of
f(\mathbb{D}).
More generally, a class
\mathcal{F}
of analytic functions
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
mapping
\mathbb{D}
into a domain
\Omega
is said to satisfy a Bohr phenomenon if an inequality of type (
1.3
) holds uniformly in
|z|\leq\rho_{0}
, where
0<\rho_{0}\leq 1
for functions in the class
\mathcal{F}
. Similar definition makes sense for harmonic functions (see
[
22
]
).

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the class of all complex-valued harmonic functions
f=h+\bar{g}
defined on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, where
h
and
g
are analytic
\mathbb{D}
with the normalization
h(0)=h^{\prime}(0)-1=0
and
g(0)=0
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{0}
be defined by
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\{f=h+\bar{g}\in\mathcal{H}:g^{\prime}(0)=0\}.
Therefore, each
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
has the following representation

f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}.

(1.4)

Let us recall the Bohr radius for the class
\mathcal{H}_{0}
of harmonic mappings.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
be given by (
1.4
). Then the Bohr phenomenon is to find a constant
R^{*}\in(0,1]
such that the inequality

\displaystyle L_{f}(r):=|z|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|\right)|z|^{n}\leq d\left(f(0),\partial\Omega\right)

holds for
|z|=r\leq R^{*}
, where
d\left(f(0),\partial\Omega\right)
is the Euclidean distance between
f(0)
and the boundary of
\Omega:=f(\mathbb{D})
. The largest such radius
R^{*}
is called the Bohr radius for the class
\mathcal{H}_{0}
. The radius
R^{*}
is said to be best possible if there exists a function
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
such that
L_{\phi}(r)>d\left(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D})\right)
for
r>R^{*}
.

As applications, they established some Bohr inequalities for these subclasses (stable classes) by means of subordination. Definition 1.2
.

A (sense-preserving) harmonic mapping
f=h+\overline{g}
is stable harmonic univalent (SHU) in the unit disk (resp. stable harmonic convex (SHC)) if all the mappings
f_{\mu}=h+\mu\overline{g}
with
|\mu|=1
are univalent (resp. convex) in
\mathbb{D}
.

The harmonic differential operator for functions
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}
can be defined as follows:

Definition 1.3
.

If
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}
, then the harmonic differential operator of
f
is represented as:

\displaystyle Df:=zf_{z}-\overline{z}f_{\overline{z}}

(1.5)

and

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}f:=zf_{z}+\overline{z}f_{\overline{z}}

(1.6)

Furthermore, the linear combination of harmonic mapping and its differential operator is given by

\displaystyle F_{\lambda}=(1-\lambda)f+\lambda Df,\;\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.7)

In this paper, we establish several results concerning improved Bohr inequalities in terms of distance formulations involving multiple Schwarz functions related to harmonic differential operators
D

,
3.3
and

4.3

\mathscr{D}^{2}

[

Suppose that

3.
Improved Bohr inequalities of harmonic differential operators involving Schwarz functions

We introduce the following important subfamily of the class
\mathcal{B}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{0}:=\{\omega\in\mathcal{B}:\omega(0)=0\}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}_{n},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{n}=\bigg\{\omega\in\mathcal{B}:\omega(0)=\cdots=\omega^{(n-1)}(0)=0\;\mbox{and}\;\omega^{(n)}(0)\neq 0\bigg\}\;\mbox{for}\;n\in\mathbb{N}.

Each function in
\mathcal{B}_{0}
is called a Schwarz function. In view of the maximum modulus principle, the family
\mathcal{B}
consists of non-constant analytic functions
\omega:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
and the constant functions with values in
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. While some authors exclude constant functions from
\mathcal{B}
, this exclusion does not affect our discussions.

In the following research, we will use the series representation of the differential operator. By utilizing equations (
1.4
) and (
1.5

\displaystyle Df=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}na_{n}z^{n}-\overline{\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}nb_{n}z^{n}}.

(3.1)

and

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}na_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}nb_{n}z^{n}}

(3.2)

We now state our first result for
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{H}
, where
f
is a stable univalent harmonic mapping on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}

\omega_{m}\in\mathcal{B}_{m}

\displaystyle|Df(\omega_{m}(z))|+M_{f}(|\omega_{p}(z)|)\leq d\big(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D})\big)

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

If
f
is a stable univalent harmonic mapping, then from (2.3) we have

|f(z)|\geq\frac{|z|}{(1+|z|)^{2}}.

It follows from equation (
2.1
) of Lemma
2.3
that the Euclidean distance between
f(0)
and the boundary of the image domain
f(\mathbb{D})
satisfies the inequality

\displaystyle d\big(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D})\big)=\lim_{|z|\to 1}\inf|f(z)-f(0)|\geq\lim_{|z|\to 1}\inf\frac{|z|}{(1+|z|)^{2}}=\frac{1}{4}.

(3.4)

For
m\geq 1
, the condition
|\omega_{m}(z)|\leq|z|^{m}
holds. By setting
|z|=r
and applying this inequality (from (
3.1
)) in conjunction with Lemma
2.1
, a straightforward computation shows that

\displaystyle|Df(\omega_{m}(z))|+M_{f}(|\omega_{p}(z)|)

\displaystyle\leq|\omega_{m}(z)|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n|a_{n}||\omega_{m}(z)|^{n}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n|b_{n}||\omega_{m}(z)|^{n}

\displaystyle\quad+|\omega_{p}(z)|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)|\omega_{p}(z)|^{n}

\displaystyle\leq|z|^{m}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)|z|^{mn}+|z|^{p}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)|z|^{pn}

\displaystyle\leq r^{m}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n^{2}r^{mn}+r^{p}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}nr^{np}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}+G_{1}(r),

where

\displaystyle G_{1}(r):=r^{m}+\frac{r^{2m}(4-3r^{m}+r^{2m})}{(1-r^{m})^{3}}+r^{p}+\frac{2r^{2p}-r^{3p}}{(1-r^{p})^{2}}-\frac{1}{4}.

It follows directly from the definition that
G_{1}(r)
is differentiable on
[0,1).
Differentiating
G_{1}(r)
with respect to
r
, we obtain

\displaystyle G_{1}^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=mr^{m-1}+\frac{(8r^{m}-9r^{2m}+4r^{3m})mr^{m-1}}{(1-r^{m})^{3}}+\frac{3r^{2m}(4-3r^{m}+r^{2m})}{(1-r^{m})^{4}}mr^{m-1}

\displaystyle\quad+pr^{p-1}+\frac{p(4r^{2p-1}-3r^{3p-1})}{(1-r^{p})^{2}}+\frac{2p(2r^{2p}-r^{3p})r^{p-1}}{(1-r^{p})^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m(1+5r^{m}-6r^{2m}+3r^{3m})r^{m-1}}{(1-r^{m})^{3}}+\frac{3r^{2m}(4-3r^{m}+r^{2m})}{(1-r^{m})^{4}}mr^{m-1}

\displaystyle\quad+pr^{p-1}+\frac{4p(r^{2p-1}-3r^{3p-1})}{(1-r^{p})^{2}}+\frac{2p(2r^{2p}-r^{3p})r^{p-1}}{(1-r^{p})^{3}}.

Since all terms of
G_{1}^{\prime}(r)
are positive,
G_{1}(r)
is strictly increasing on
[0,1)
. Thus we have
G_{1}(r)\geq G_{1}(r_{m,p})
for
r\geq r_{m,p}
and this implies the inequality

\displaystyle r^{m}

\displaystyle+\frac{r^{2m}(4-3r^{m}+r^{2m})}{(1-r^{m})^{3}}+r^{p}+\frac{2r^{2p}-r^{3p}}{(1-r^{p})^{2}}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\geq r_{m,p}^{m}+\frac{r_{m,p}^{2m}(4-3r_{m,p}^{m}+r_{m,p}^{2m})}{(1-r_{m,p}^{m})^{3}}+r_{m,p}^{p}+\frac{2r_{m,p}^{2p}-r_{m,p}^{3p}}{(1-r_{m,p}^{p})^{2}}.

A simple calculation shows that

\displaystyle G_{1}(0)=-\frac{1}{4}<0,\;\;\text{and}\;\;\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}G_{1}(r)=+\infty.

Consequently,
G_{1}(r)
has a unique root in the interval
(0,1)
. Denoting this unique root by
r_{m,p}
, we obtain the desired inequality

\displaystyle|Df(\omega_{m}(z))|+M_{f}(|\omega_{p}(z)|)\leq\frac{1}{4}\leq d\big(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D})\big),

for
|z|\leq r_{m,p}
.

Next part of the proof is to show the radius
r_{m,p}
is best possible. Henceforth, we consider the Koebe function

\displaystyle k(z)=\frac{z}{(1-z)^{2}}=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}nz^{n},

(3.6)

which is harmonic and univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. It follows from (
2.1
) of Lemma
2.3
that

d\big(k(0),\partial k(\mathbb{D})\big)=\frac{1}{4}.

(3.7)

Considering
\omega_{m}(z)=z^{m}
(

) and the case where

with

, it follows from equations (

) and (

) that

\displaystyle|Df(\omega_{m}(z))|+M_{f}(|\omega_{p}(z)|)

\displaystyle=|Dk(r^{m})|+M_{k}(r^{p})

\displaystyle=r^{m}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n^{2}r^{mn}+r^{p}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}nr^{np}

\displaystyle=r^{m}+\frac{r^{2m}(4-3r^{m}+r^{2m})}{(1-r^{m})^{3}}+r^{p}+\frac{2r^{2p}-r^{3p}}{(1-r^{p})^{2}}

\displaystyle>r_{m,p}^{m}+\frac{r_{m,p}^{2m}(4-3r_{m,p}^{m}+r_{m,p}^{2m})}{(1-r_{m,p}^{m})^{3}}+r_{m,p}^{p}+\frac{2r_{m,p}^{2p}-r_{m,p}^{3p}}{(1-r_{m,p}^{p})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}

\displaystyle=d\big(k(0),\partial k(\mathbb{D})\big)

which shows that the radius

is the best possible.

Similarly, for the operator

\displaystyle|\mathscr{D}f(\omega_{m}(z))|+M_{f}(|\omega_{p}(z)|)

\displaystyle\leq|\omega_{m}(z)|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n|a_{n}||\omega_{m}(z)|^{n}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n|b_{n}||\omega_{m}(z)|^{n}

\displaystyle\quad+|\omega_{p}(z)|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)|\omega_{p}(z)|^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}+G_{1}(r).

holds. The remaining part of the proof is analogous to the argument used for the inequality involving the operator

, and thus, we omit the details. This completes the proof.

f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{H}

a_{2}=\lambda=18.6095...
and
F(t)=a_{3}t^{3}+\cdots+a_{k}t^{k}
,
(k\geq 3)
, we see that
F:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
and in this case, our inequality becomes

\displaystyle M(|z|)+\frac{16}{9}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)+\lambda\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)^{2}+F\bigg(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\bigg)\leq d(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D}))

(5.1)

for
|z|\leq r_{1,k},
where
\leq r_{1,k}
is the unique root in
(0,1)
of the equation

\displaystyle\frac{16}{9}\left(\frac{r^{2}+26r^{4}+66r^{6}+26r^{8}+r^{10}}{(1-r^{2})^{6}}\right)+\lambda\left(\frac{r^{2}+26r^{4}+66r^{6}+26r^{8}+r^{10}}{(1-r^{2})^{6}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{r}{(1-r)^{2}}+F\left(\frac{r^{2}+26r^{4}+66r^{6}+26r^{8}+r^{10}}{(1-r^{2})^{6}}\right)-\frac{1}{4}=0.

The radius
r_{1,k}
is best possible. Our Theorem
5.1
for harmonic mappings overcomes the limitation pointed out in
[
18
, Remark 2]
, which stated that inequality (
5.1
) failed to hold for the class
\mathcal{B}
of analytic functions.

Proof.

Switching to polar coordinates and using Parseval’s identity gives

\displaystyle\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{3}\left(|a_{n}|^{2}-|b_{n}|^{2}\right)r^{2n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{3}(|a_{n}|-|b_{n}|)(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)r^{2n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{3}(\,||a_{n}|-|b_{n}||\,)(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)r^{2n}.

By applying Lemma
2.1
, we obtain the following estimate

\displaystyle M(|\omega_{m}(z)|)+P_{k}\bigg(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq|\omega_{m}(z)|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}||\omega_{m}(z)|^{n}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|b_{n}||\omega_{m}(z)|^{n}+P_{k}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{5}r^{2n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq|z|^{m}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)|z|^{mn}+P_{k}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{5}r^{2n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq r^{m}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}nr^{mn}+P_{k}\left(\frac{r^{2}+26r^{4}+66r^{6}+26r^{8}+r^{10}}{(1-r^{2})^{6}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{m}}{(1-r^{m})^{2}}+P_{k}\left(\frac{r^{2}+26r^{4}+66r^{6}+26r^{8}+r^{10}}{(1-r^{2})^{6}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}+G(r),

(5.2)

where

G(r)=\frac{r^{m}}{(1-r^{m})^{2}}+P_{k}\left(\frac{r^{2}+26r^{4}+66r^{6}+26r^{8}+r^{10}}{(1-r^{2})^{6}}\right)-\frac{1}{4}.

It follows directly from the definition that
G(r)
is differentiable on
[0,1)
. Differentiating
G(r)
with respect to
r
, we obtain

G^{\prime}(r)=\frac{mr^{m-1}}{(1-r^{m})^{2}}+\frac{2r^{m}}{(1-r^{m})^{3}}mr^{m-1}+P(r),

where

\displaystyle P(r)=

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}ia_{i}\left(\frac{r^{2}+26r^{4}+66r^{6}+26r^{8}+r^{10}}{(1-r^{2})^{6}}\right)^{i-1}\bigg)H_{0}(r),

(5.3)

where

H_{0}(r):=\frac{2r+104r^{3}+369r^{5}+208r^{7}+10r^{9}}{(1-r^{2})^{6}}+\frac{12(r^{2}+26r^{4}+66r^{6}+26r^{8}+r^{10})r}{(1-r^{2})^{6})}.

Because all terms in
G^{\prime}(r)
are positive, the function
G(r)
is strictly increasing on the interval
[0,1)
. Consequently, for all
r\geq r_{m,k}
, the inequality
G(r)\geq G(r_{m,k})
holds, implying the result

\displaystyle\frac{r^{m}}{(1-r^{m})^{2}}

\displaystyle+P_{k}\left(\frac{r^{2}+26r^{4}+66r^{6}+26r^{8}+r^{10}}{(1-r^{2})^{6}}\right)

(5.4)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{r_{m,k}^{m}}{(1-r_{m,k}^{m})^{2}}+P_{k}\left(\frac{r_{m,k}^{2}+26r_{m,k}^{4}+66r_{m,k}^{6}+26r_{m,k}^{8}+r^{10}}{(1-r_{m,k}^{2})^{6}}\right).

Moreover, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle G(0)=-\frac{1}{4}<0\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-1}}G(r)=+\infty.

Consequently,
G(r)
has a unique root in the interval
(0,1)
. Denoting this unique root by
r_{m,k}
, we obtain the desired inequality

\displaystyle M(|\omega_{m}(z)|)+P_{k}\bigg(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\bigg)\leq d(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D}))\;\mbox{for}\;|z|=r\leq r_{m,k}.

To show the radius
r_{m,k}
is best possible, we consider the Koebe function

\displaystyle k(z)=\frac{z}{(1-z)^{2}}=z+\sum_{n-2}^{\infty}nz^{n},

which gives
a_{n}=n
and
b_{n}=0
.

A simple computation shows for the function
k(z)
that

\displaystyle\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{3}(||a_{n}|-|b_{n}||(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)r^{2n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{5}r^{2n}=\frac{r^{2}+26r^{4}+66r^{6}+26r^{8}+r^{10}}{(1-r^{2})^{6}}.

For
\omega_{m}(z)=z^{m}(m\geq 1)
and
z=r>r_{m,k}
, with
f=k
, it follows from (
5.3
), (
5.4
) and (
3.7
) that

\displaystyle M(|\omega_{m}(z)|)+P_{k}\bigg(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\bigg)=

\displaystyle M(|z^{m}|)+P_{k}\bigg(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\bigg)

\displaystyle=r^{m}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}nr^{mn}+P_{k}\left(\frac{r^{2}+26r^{4}+66r^{6}+26r^{8}+r^{10}}{(1-r^{2})^{6}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{m}}{(1-r^{m})^{2}}+P_{k}\left(\frac{r^{2}+26r^{4}+66r^{6}+26r^{8}+r^{10}}{(1-r^{2})^{6}}\right)

\displaystyle>\frac{r_{m,k}^{m}}{(1-r_{m,k}^{m})^{2}}+P_{k}\left(\frac{r_{m,k}^{2}+26r_{m,k}^{4}+66r_{m,k}^{6}+26r_{m,k}^{8}+r^{10}}{(1-r_{m,k}^{2})^{6}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}

\displaystyle=d(k(0),\partial k(\mathbb{D}).

which shows that the radius
r_{m,k}
is the best possible.
∎

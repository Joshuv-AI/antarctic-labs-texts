An open-label, randomized study comparing lipegfilgrastim with filgrastim in pediatric patients receiving chemotherapy for Ewing sarcoma or rhabdomyosarcoma
Authors: Margarita B Belogurova, Andreas Lammerich, Hadas Barkay, Dayoung Seo, Anton Buchner
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract This study compared efficacy and safety of lipegfilgrastim with filgrastim in pediatric patients. Children and adolescents receiving chemotherapy (4 cycles) for Ewing sarcoma or rhabdomyosarcoma were randomized 1:1 to lipegfilgrastim (100 µg/kg) once per cycle or filgrastim (5 µg/kg), once daily for ≥ 5 days for up to 14 days or until absolute neutrophil count (ANC) recovery. In 39 evaluable patients, no meaningful difference was observed between lipegfilgrastim and filgrastim treatment groups in duration of severe neutropenia (DSN; mean [standard deviation]) in cycle 1 (2.7 [2.25] vs. 2.5 [2.09] days); least squares mean treatment difference (lipegfilgrastim minus filgrastim) 1.0 day (95% CI: -0.21,2.26) or cycles 2–4, duration of very severe neutropenia in cycles 1–4, incidence of severe neutropenia (85% vs. 84%) or very severe neutropenia (70% vs. 68%). The incidence of febrile neutropenia was numerically lower with lipegfilgrastim (35%) vs. filgrastim (42%). Mean area under the curve of ANC until day 15 in cycle 1 was numerically higher with lipegfilgrastim vs. filgrastim (105 × 109/L*days vs. 84 × 109/L*days). There were no meaningful differences between treatment groups, in cycles 1–4, in mean ANC nadir values, or time to ANC nadir or ANC recovery. Mean administration of lipegfilgrastim was less than filgrastim (4 vs. 31.7 times). No unexpected safety signals were observed. Lipegfilgrastim demonstrated comparable efficacy and tolerability to filgrastim in reducing duration and incidence of neutropenia. Introduction

Recommended treatments for pediatric sarcomas are commonly dose-intensive chemotherapy regimens such as vincristine, dactinomycin, and cyclophosphamide (VAC) for rhabdomyosarcoma, and vincristine, adriamycin, and cyclophosphamide alternating with ifosfamide and etoposide (VDC/IE) for Ewing sarcoma1,2. Although correlating with better tumor response and outcomes3–5, regimens such as these are myelosuppressive, often leading to a decrease in peripheral blood absolute neutrophil count (ANC) and subsequent neutropenia, and an increased risk of febrile neutropenia and infection. Febrile neutropenia is a life-threatening complication that can lead to treatment delay, dose reduction, or treatment discontinuation and is associated with an increased rate of mortality in pediatric and adolescent patients6–10. Preventing or shortening the duration of neutropenia and the depth of neutrophil ‘nadir’ following chemotherapy improves safety and facilitates dose-intensive chemotherapy for optimal tumor response3–5.

Recombinant human granulocyte-colony stimulating factors (G-CSFs) are routinely used to manage chemotherapy-induced neutropenia and febrile neutropenia in patients receiving intensive chemotherapy. In Europe, filgrastim (recombinant methionyl human G-CSF), is indicated in adults and children, and pegfilgrastim in adults, for reduction in the duration of neutropenia and the incidence of febrile neutropenia following treatment with cytotoxic chemotherapy for malignancy (with the exception of chronic myeloid leukemia and myelodysplastic syndromes)11,12. Filgrastim (5 µg/kg) is administered ≥ 24 h after cytotoxic chemotherapy and daily dosing is continued for up to 14 days and until the neutrophil count has recovered to the normal range. Pegfilgrastim, a polyethylene glycol (PEG)ylated formulation of recombinant methionyl human G-CSF, has decreased renal clearance and an extended half-life, enabling its administration as a single 6 mg dose per chemotherapy cycle, ≥ 24 h after cytotoxic chemotherapy.

A limited number of studies have evaluated pegfilgrastim dosing in pediatric or adolescent patients and shown it to be as efficacious as filgrastim in this population, with an acceptable safety profile13–19. Despite this, and although dosing once per cycle would be preferable for children and adolescents in clinical practice20, pegfilgrastim is approved in the US21 but not yet in Europe for pediatric use12.

Lipegfilgrastim, a glyco-polyethylene glycol (PEG)ylated formulation of a recombinant human G-CSF, received EU marketing approval in July 2013 for reduction in the duration of neutropenia and the incidence of febrile neutropenia in adults receiving chemotherapy for malignancy (with the exception of chronic myeloid leukemia and myelodysplastic syndromes)22. Like pegfilgrastim, lipegfilgrastim is administered in adults as a single 6 mg dose per chemotherapy cycle, ≥ 24 h after cytotoxic chemotherapy. Lipegfilgrastim approval was based on results from two pivotal randomized Phase III trials. Study XM22-03 in adults with stage II–IV breast cancer receiving up to 4 cycles of doxorubicin/docetaxel-based chemotherapy showed non-inferiority of lipegfilgrastim to pegfilgrastim for duration of severe neutropenia (DSN) in cycle 1, with a lower incidence of severe neutropenia and febrile neutropenia (FN) for lipegfilgrastim23. Study XM22-04 in patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) receiving up to 4 cycles of cisplatin/etoposide showed significantly lower DSN in cycle 1 for lipegfilgrastim vs. placebo and a lower incidence of severe neutropenia and FN with lipegfilgrastim24.

We previously reported data on the pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics of a single subcutaneous (sc) dose of lipegfilgrastim (100 µg/kg, up to a maximum of 6 mg) from a Phase I, multinational, open-label, single-arm study (XM22-07; NCT01585649) in 21 children aged between 2 and 16 years with Ewing sarcoma or rhabdomyosarcoma treated with myelosuppressive chemotherapy25. Incidence of FN varied according to age (from 14.3% to 71.4%), with the highest frequency in the oldest age group. Most investigator-reported adverse events were attributed to chemotherapy and not to lipegfilgrastim. The open-label, uncontrolled design limited the conclusions possible regarding the safety and efficacy of lipegfilgrastim in the pediatric population. Pharmacokinetic data in this study supported the use of a 100 µg/kg dose of lipegfilgrastim for patients 2 to < 18 years of age. Preliminary evidence suggested that lipegfilgrastim may be safe and well tolerated for at least 3 weeks following administration in the pediatric population studied.

This second, controlled study of a larger cohort of pediatric patients was designed to further investigate the efficacy, safety and tolerability of lipegfilgrastim compared to filgrastim in children and adolescents aged 2 to < 18 years receiving chemotherapy for Ewing sarcoma or rhabdomyosarcoma. It was conducted at sites in the European Economic Area (EEA), Ukraine and the Russian Federation between 2015 and 2019. This study was conducted pursuant to a European Medicines Agency (EMA)-approved pediatric investigation plan (EMEA-001019-PIP01-10-M04; Commission Decision 04 September 2017). At the time the study was conducted, lipegfilgrastim was approved by the EMA for use only in adults. Since our study, in June 2022, lipegfilgrastim also received EU marketing approval for the same indication in children aged ≥ 2 years26,27.

Results

Patient disposition

Patient disposition is shown in Supplementary Fig. 1. In total, 42 patients were enrolled and randomized to lipegfilgrastim or filgrastim (intention-to-treat [ITT] population). All 42 patients were evaluated for safety (safety analysis set) and 39 patients (per protocol [PP] analysis set) were evaluable for efficacy (one patient in the lipegfilgrastim arm was excluded due to violation of inclusion/exclusion criteria and two patients in the filgrastim arm received < 75% of the intended study medication and were excluded).

Baseline characteristics

Treatment groups were well balanced according to baseline characteristics overall and in each age cohort (Supplementary Table 1). The mean (SD) age overall was 9.1 (5.1) and 9.4 (5.1) years, and 67% and 57% were male, in the lipegfilgrastim and filgrastim treatment groups, respectively.

Chemotherapy

Chemotherapy administered in cycle 1 is shown in Supplementary Table 2.

Exposure to study drug

Mean exposure to study drug is shown in Fig. 1. Overall, lipegfilgrastim was administered a mean of 4 times (once per cycle for 4 cycles) and filgrastim was administered a mean of 31.7 times over 4 cycles. In cycle 1, lipegfilgrastim was administered once (mean) and filgrastim was administered a mean of 8.4 times over 8.5 days. The doses administered were consistent with the study protocol.

DSN in cycle 1 (primary efficacy endpoint)

No meaningful differences were observed between the lipegfilgrastim and filgrastim treatment groups with respect to mean (SD) DSN in cycle 1 (2.7 ([2.25] days and 2.5 (2.09) days, respectively [PP analysis set]) (Table 1). Results in the ITT analysis set were consistent with the PP analysis set (Supplementary Table 3). In the PP analysis set the least squares (LS) mean treatment difference (lipegfilgrastim minus filgrastim) by Poisson regression analysis was 1.0 day (95% CI: −0.21, 2.26) (Table 1) and in the ITT analysis set the difference was 0.4 days (95% CI: −0.92, 1.72) (Supplementary Table 3).

Secondary efficacy endpoints

DSN in cycle 1 by chemotherapy

DSN was similar in both treatment groups, but differed considerably depending on chemotherapy regimen received, being longer in patients who received doxorubicin-based chemotherapy (VDC/IE, VIDE) compared to patients who received other chemotherapy regimens (ifosfamide, vincristine and actinomycin D (IVA) or VAC)) (Table 2). In patients receiving VIDE, mean (SD) DSN was 4.1 (1.54) and 3.9 (1.13) days in the lipegfilgrastim and filgrastim treatment groups, respectively, whereas in patients receiving IVA, mean (SD) DSN was 0.4 (0.53) and 0.4 (0.79) days, respectively (Table 2; PP analysis set). Results were similar for the ITT analysis set.

DSN in cycle 1 by age

In both PP and ITT analysis sets, mean DSN in cycle 1 was similar across age cohorts within each treatment group (Supplementary Table 4).

DSN or DVSN by cycle

There was no meaningful difference in mean (SD) DSN in cycles 2 to 4 between lipegfilgrastim and filgrastim groups overall (Table 1) or following stratification by age cohort and chemotherapy received in cycle 1. There was also no meaningful difference in mean (SD) DVSN in cycles 1 to 4 between lipegfilgrastim and filgrastim groups overall (Supplementary Table 5) or following stratification by age cohort and chemotherapy received in cycle 1.

Incidence of severe or very severe neutropenia, or febrile neutropenia

Overall incidence of severe neutropenia was similar between the lipegfilgrastim and filgrastim treatment groups (85% and 84%; stratified Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel [CMH] odds ratio [OR] = 1.19; 95% CI: 0.220, 6.423; PP analysis set) and there was no meaningful difference across cycles or age cohorts (Table 3). Following stratification by chemotherapy received in cycle 1, in both treatment groups more patients in the PP analysis set who received VIDE experienced severe neutropenia (100%) than those who received IVA (50%). Overall incidence of very severe neutropenia was also similar between lipegfilgrastim and filgrastim treatment groups (70% vs. 68%; stratified CMH OR = 1.08; 95% CI: 0.276, 4.197; PP analysis set) and there was no meaningful difference across cycles or age cohorts (Table 3). Overall incidence of febrile neutropenia was numerically lower in the lipegfilgrastim group than in the filgrastim group (35% vs. 42%; PP analysis set) (stratified CMH OR = 0.67; 95% CI: 0.171, 2.600). There were no meaningful differences across age cohorts in the lipegfilgrastim treatment group vs. age cohorts in the filgrastim treatment group (Table 3).

Secondary pharmacodynamic endpoints

Mean ANC by cycle and treatment group

The mean ANC values at cycle 1 in each treatment group (PP analysis set) are presented in Fig. 2. In both the treatment groups, the highest mean ANC values were observed on day 2 and the lowest between days 6 and 7 of cycle 1. In the filgrastim group, the mean ANC values recovered approximately to the baseline values around day 15 of cycle 1. However, in the lipegfilgrastim group, the mean ANC values remained slightly higher than the baseline values on day 15 of cycle 1 (PP analysis set; Fig. 2). A similar trend was observed in the ITT analysis set (Supplementary Fig. 2). Similar trends were also seen in chemotherapy cycles 2, 3 and 4 (Supplementary Appendix).

Mean absolute neutrophil count (ANC) at cycle 1, PP analysis set. ANC absolute neutrophil count, PP per protocol.

AUC of ANC until day 15 in cycle 1

The geometric mean AUCANC until day 15 in cycle 1 was numerically higher in the lipegfilgrastim group compared with the filgrastim group (104.95 × 109/L*days vs. 84.28 × 109/L*days; PP Analysis Set) (Supplementary Table 6). An analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) model was fitted to evaluate the difference between the treatment groups in the AUCANC controlling for either age or the chemotherapy regimen administered in cycle 1. The geometric mean ratio (GMR) (95% CI for GMR) of AUCANC was 1.29 (0.91, 1.82) for the model with age cohort as a covariate and 1.34 (0.95, 1.88) with chemotherapy regimen administered in cycle 1 as a covariate. Results in the ITT analysis set were consistent and support the results of the PP analysis set. When the results were stratified by age cohorts and the chemotherapy regimen administered in cycle 1, there were no meaningful differences in the mean AUCANC values between the lipegfilgrastim and filgrastim treatment groups in the corresponding age cohorts and chemotherapy regimens.

ANC nadir per chemotherapy cycle, time to ANC nadir and time to ANC recovery

The geometric mean ANC nadir value in cycle 1 was 0.21 × 109/L in the lipegfilgrastim group compared with 0.18 × 109/L in the filgrastim group (PP Analysis Set) (Supplementary Table 7).

Overall, ANC nadir values were comparable between lipegfilgrastim and filgrastim treatment groups in cycles 1 to 4, and there were no meaningful differences between treatment groups using age or chemotherapy administered in cycle 1 as covariates.

The mean time to ANC nadir in cycles 1 to 4 was similar in lipegfilgrastim and filgrastim treatment groups from the start of chemotherapy (PP analysis set, range: 8.8 to 11.0 days) or first lipegfilgrastim or filgrastim administration (PP analysis set, range: 6.1 to 8.5 days). Results in the ITT analysis set were consistent with the results in the PP analysis set.

Mean time to ANC recovery thresholds for patients in the PP analysis set receiving lipegfilgrastim and filgrastim in cycle 1 was similar for both thresholds (Supplementary Table 8).

There were no meaningful differences in mean times to the ANC recovery thresholds of ANC > 1.0 × 109/L and ANC > 2.0 × 109/L in cycles 1 to 4 between lipegfilgrastim and filgrastim treatment groups from the first day of chemotherapy or nadir.

Safety evaluation

The incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) during the study was similar between lipegfilgrastim and filgrastim treatment groups (Table 4). The type and incidence of TEAEs were consistent with the known TEAEs of chemotherapy regimens administered in pediatric patients with cancer. The most frequently reported Grade 3 TEAE was anemia (52% of patients in the lipegfilgrastim group and 38% of patients in the filgrastim group). The most frequently reported Grade 4 TEAEs (≥ 30% of patients in any group) were thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, lymphopenia, and leukopenia. TEAEs that occurred more frequently with lipegfilgrastim than with filgrastim included vomiting, lymphopenia, platelet count decreased, alopecia, alanine transaminase (ALT) increased, hyperthermia, hypokalemia, weight decreased, and decreased appetite (Supplementary Table 9). TEAEs that occurred more frequently with filgrastim than with lipegfilgrastim included leukopenia, febrile neutropenia, stomatitis, and headache (Supplementary Table 9). Treatment-related TEAEs were rare, occurring in 4 patients in the lipegfilgrastim arm (leukocytosis, monocytosis, splenomegaly and back pain) and 2 patients in the filgrastim arm (leukocytosis and monocytosis) and were mild or moderate in severity (Grade 1 or 2).

Similar proportions of patients (~ 60%) in each treatment group had ≥ 1 SAE (Supplementary Table 10). One SAE in the filgrastim group (grade 1 pyrexia) was considered related to treatment. Most SAEs occurred with similar frequencies among the treatment groups.

Two patients (both in the filgrastim group and in the age cohort of 12 to < 18 years) withdrew from the study due to disease progression. No deaths were reported during the treatment phase in the study.

Results from the follow-up period

Three out of 40 patients who entered follow-up died, including 1 patient who died shortly after Day 365 follow-up completion. All deaths were due to disease progression. No unexpected safety findings were observed during the follow-up period. Immunogenicity data are presented in the Supplementary Appendix. No patients developed a persistent anti-drug antibody response; all positive samples had low antibody titers and were not neutralizing.

Discussion

In our study, lipegfilgrastim demonstrated comparable efficacy to filgrastim in reducing the duration and incidence of neutropenia in pediatric cancer patients receiving chemotherapy every 3 weeks for 4 cycles.

Consistent with the findings of our previous study, of a single dose of lipegfilgrastim (100 µg/kg) in children with Ewing family of tumors or rhabdomyosarcoma (XM22-0725), no meaningful difference was observed between the lipegfilgrastim and filgrastim groups in the duration of severe neutropenia in chemotherapy cycle 1 (primary endpoint). In addition, also consistent with the results obtained in study XM22-07, there were no notable differences in the DSN in cycle 1 between the corresponding age cohorts across the treatment groups.

In both treatment groups, mean DSN in patients who received doxorubicin-based chemotherapy (VDC/IE, VIDE) in cycle 1 was longer than in patients who received other chemotherapy regimens (IVA or VAC). This could be attributed to the higher myelotoxicity expected with doxorubicin-based regimens, particularly for the older pediatric patients (6 to < 12 years and 12 to < 18 years) who received a higher proportion of the VIDE regimen as compared to other regimens. As the type of chemotherapy regimen administered was different across the age cohorts, no clear conclusion could be drawn regarding a relationship between DSN and the age of the patients.

No meaningful differences were seen between lipegfilgrastim and filgrastim treatment groups with respect to the incidence of severe neutropenia, febrile neutropenia, hospitalization due to febrile neutropenia, DSN in cycle 2 to 4, and very severe neutropenia, with no relevant differences between the corresponding age cohorts. As in study XM22-0725, a trend of higher incidence of febrile neutropenia in patients who received VIDE compared to other chemotherapy regimens was observed.

The GM AUCANC until day 15 in the lipegfilgrastim group was higher compared to the filgrastim group, providing additional evidence supporting a similar efficacy of lipegfilgrastim to filgrastim. When results were stratified by age cohorts and chemotherapy regimen administered in cycle 1, there were no meaningful differences between the lipegfilgrastim and filgrastim treatment groups and between the corresponding age cohorts. In addition, there were no meaningful differences between the two treatment groups in the mean AUCANC values in cycle 1, mean ANC nadir values in cycles 1 to 4, mean time to ANC nadir in cycles 1 to 4 from start of chemotherapy or lipegfilgrastim/filgrastim administration, and mean times to ANC recovery in cycles 1 to 4.

Data from this study indicated that the safety profiles of lipegfilgrastim and filgrastim were similar and as expected in children with Ewing sarcoma or rhabdomyosarcoma. No unexpected safety signals were observed, nor did any of the reported TEAEs give rise to concerns about the safety of lipegfilgrastim. There were no notable differences in the incidences of various categories of TEAEs between the 2 treatment groups. In addition, no patient developed a persistent ADA response; all positive samples had low titer and were not neutralizing.

A limitation of this study was that no formal hypothesis testing was performed as the study was not powered for this. Conclusions were limited to providing estimates of the treatment means and their differences.

Overall, lipegfilgrastim was administered 4 times, and filgrastim was administered 31.7 times over 4 cycles. Lipegfilgrastim could be advantageous over daily administration of filgrastim owing to the reduced dosing frequency of once per cycle, which could be particularly beneficial in children by enhancing convenience and improving adherence in this population.

Methods

Study design and patients

This was a Phase II, multicenter, open-label, randomized, active-controlled study (XM22-08; EudraCT Number: 2015-000087-34; start date 05/06/2015) to evaluate the efficacy, pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, safety, tolerability, and immunogenicity of lipegfilgrastim once per cycle vs. daily filgrastim doses in children and adolescents diagnosed with Ewing sarcoma or rhabdomyosarcoma and receiving cytotoxic chemotherapy. The study was performed at 21 centers across 10 countries (Belarus, Croatia, Czech Republic, Georgia, Hungary, Romania, Russia, Slovakia, Spain, and Ukraine). This exploratory Phase II study did not predefine a formal hypothesis or non-inferiority margin. The sample size (21 patients per group) was based on feasibility, given the rarity of the population. The study was not powered for statistical comparisons but aimed to provide descriptive estimates of efficacy, including mean duration of severe neutropenia, and safety profiles.

Patients were randomized 1:1 on day 1 of cycle 1 to receive either a single sc dose of lipegfilgrastim (100 µg/kg body weight, up to a maximum of 6 mg) or daily sc filgrastim (5 µg/kg body weight). Study drug administration began approximately 24 h (+ 6 h) after the last chemotherapy in week 1 of the respective chemotherapy regimen. Lipegfilgrastim was given as a single injection on that day, while filgrastim was administered once daily for ≥ 5 consecutive days, up to 14 days or until ANC had returned to ≥ 2 × 109/L for each cycle. Treatment was continued for 4 consecutive cycles. Randomization was stratified 1:1:1 by age (2 to < 6 years, 6 to < 12 years, and 12 to < 18 years). According to the study protocol, enrollment of the youngest age group (2 to < 6 years) was permitted only after safety data from at least three patients in the middle cohort (6 to < 12 years) treated with lipegfilgrastim had been reviewed and approved by the Data Monitoring Committee (DMC). No restrictions were imposed on the enrollment sequence between the two older age groups (6 to < 12 and 12 to < 18 years), and these were enrolled in parallel.

The study (Supplementary Fig. 3) consisted of a 2-week screening period followed by an 18-week treatment period consisting of 4 cycles of chemotherapy of 21 days each with an allowance of a 14-day delay between each cycle. Samples for pharmacodynamic measurement of ANC were obtained on Day 1 prior to administration of study treatment, and on Days 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12 and 15 of each cycle, and at the end of study (EOS) and/or early termination (ET) visit. An EOS/ET visit was performed 4 weeks after the start of the last chemotherapy [± 3 days]). Follow-up visits were performed on days 180 (± 2 weeks) and 365 (± 2 weeks) after first administration of lipegfilgrastim or filgrastim.

Patients included were aged 2 to < 18 years, diagnosed with Ewing sarcoma and scheduled to receive either vincristine plus ifosfamide plus doxorubicin plus etoposide (VIDE), VDC/IE or VAC (all with concomitant sodium 2-mercaptoethane sulfonate [MESNA]), or with rhabdomyosarcoma and scheduled to receive either VAC, VDC/IE, IVA or ifosfamide, vincristine, actinomycin D, doxorubicin (IVADo) (all with concomitant MESNA), with a life expectancy ≥ 3 months with appropriate therapy. Minimum body weight was 12.5 kg, white blood cell (WBC) count > 2.5 × 109/L, ANC ≥ 1.5 × 109/L, platelet count ≥ 100 × 109/L and Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG PS) ≤ 2 (if aged ≥ 12 years), and patients were using reliable contraceptive measures and had a negative pregnancy test.

Exclusion criteria included a known hypersensitivity to, or previous exposure to G-CSFs within 6 weeks prior to study, history of congenital or cyclic neutropenia, prior bone marrow or stem cell transplant, or prior radiation to ≥ 25% of bone marrow, or radiotherapy within 3 weeks prior to first dose of lipegfilgrastim or filgrastim, major surgery or serious infection within 3 weeks before study or serious trauma/compound medical procedure within the 4 weeks prior to study, current or planned treatment with lithium, or participation in a clinical study with an IMP within either 30 days or 5 half-lives before randomization.

The study was conducted in accordance with the International Conference on Harmonisation, Good Clinical Practice Consolidated Guideline (E6), and any applicable national and local laws and regulations. All study documents and procedures were approved by the appropriate Institutional Review Board or Ethics Committee. A signed and dated informed consent/assent form was obtained from each legally acceptable representative and from each applicable minor patient legal guardian(s) before study entry.

Efficacy endpoints

The primary efficacy endpoint was the duration of severe neutropenia (DSN) in cycle 1 (number of days of severe neutropenia from start of chemotherapy until day 15). Secondary efficacy endpoints were incidence of severe or very severe neutropenia, or febrile neutropenia in cycles 1 to 4, DSN in cycles 2 to 4 and duration of very severe neutropenia (DVSN) in cycles 1 to 4. Severe neutropenia was defined as Grade 4 neutropenia with ANC < 0.5 × 109/L and very severe neutropenia as ANC < 0.1 × 109/L. Febrile neutropenia was defined as body temperature > 38.3 °C or 2 consecutive readings > 37.8 °C measured at axilla or external ear at least 2 h apart; and ANC < 0.5 × 109/L or expected to be < 0.5 × 109/L per cycle and across all cycles.

Secondary pharmacodynamic endpoints

Secondary pharmacodynamic endpoints were area under the curve of ANC (AUCANC) until day 15 in cycle 1, ANC nadir (the lowest ANC value recorded) per cycle, time to ANC nadir per cycle (defined as either time from start of chemotherapy, or from first administration of filgrastim or lipegfilgrastim in a cycle, until occurrence of the ANC nadir), and time to ANC recovery (ANC > 1.0 × 109/L and ANC > 2.0 × 109/L) either from first day of chemotherapy or from nadir, per cycle.

Safety and immunogenicity endpoints

Safety of lipegfilgrastim and filgrastim was evaluated based on the assessment of adverse events, physical examination, vital signs, electrocardiogram (ECG), clinical laboratory parameters, immunogenicity (only in patients treated with lipegfilgrastim), spleen sonography, and concomitant medications. Safety data were monitored by a DMC, which included 3 independent pediatric oncologists.

Follow-up visits at 180 days (± 2 weeks) and 365 days (± 2 weeks) were limited to long-term assessments including growth (height and weight), immunogenicity, G-CSF administration, serious adverse events (SAEs), survival, concomitant medication, and tumor/metastases progression.

Immunogenicity was assessed only in patients receiving lipegfilgrastim. Blood samples were taken at baseline, before cycle 2, during follow-up visits (180 [± 2 weeks] and 365 days [± 2 weeks] after the first lipegfilgrastim dose), and at the end of the study or early termination. Samples were tested for anti-drug antibodies (ADA), with further testing if needed, to monitor immune responses that might impact safety or effectiveness.

Statistical analysis

For the primary endpoint, DSN in cycle 1, a Poisson regression with identity link was used with factors of treatment and age group, and baseline (before IMP administration) with ANC value as covariate. Based on this model the 2-sided 95% confidence interval (CI) for the difference between lipegfilgrastim/filgrastim was provided. Note that no formal hypothesis testing was performed.

The DSN in cycles 2 to 4 by cycle was analyzed in the same way as specified for the primary endpoint. For all other endpoints, stratified Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel (CMH) (by age group) odds ratio (OR) were provided to compare treatment groups and corresponding 95% CIs. No formal hypothesis testing was performed.

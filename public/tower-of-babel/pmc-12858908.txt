Dynamic power and frequency domain allocation for dedicated sensing signals in downlink ISAC
Authors: Madeeha Aman, Ghani Ur Rehman, Muhammad Zubair, Riad Alharbey, Ali Daud, Sajid Ullah Khan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper presents a dynamic joint resource allocation framework for downlink Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) systems. The proposed approach simultaneously optimizes power and frequency assignment using an iterative optimization algorithm that adapts to real-time channel state information and sensing requirements. A composite utility function is introduced to balance sensing accuracy and communication throughput, enabling flexible trade-offs for different application scenarios. Comparative analysis against benchmark schemes demonstrates that the proposed method achieves notable improvements in both sensing accuracy and communication rate, while also providing insights into power distribution across users and sensing tasks. These results highlight the potential of the framework as a practical and adaptive solution for ISAC-enabled 6G networks.Keywords: 6G, Dedicated sensing signals, Downlink communication, Dynamic power allocation, Frequency domain multiplexing, ISAC, Resource allocationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

As 6G networks are evolving, Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) systems attracted more and more research interest, and possible applications include vehicular networks, smart cities, and environmental monitoring. In downlink ISAC scenarios, the signals of interest from the same domain may compete with each other for the same resources with the later usually leading to performance degradation in both domains.

The idea of Integrated Sensing and Communications (ISAC) has drawn wide interest from both the academic community and the industry players because of its great potential in promoting the development of 6G and future wireless networks1. ISAC fundamentally distinguishes itself by its capability to share same time, frequency, power, and hardware resources for communication and sensing at the same time2,3. In stark contrast to the conventional approach of Frequency Division Sensing and Communications (FDSAC), where dedicated and separate frequency bands and infrastructure are required for both functions. The spectrum utilization, energy consumption and hardware requirements of ISAC are therefore expected to outperform those of FDSAC2,3. From Information theoretic viewpoint, there are two key system performance metrics, the sensing rate (SR) and communication rate (CR)4–6 which are used to assess the performance of ISAC. The SR reflects the system capacity for estimating environmental information through processing within sensorial events. The CR, from the other hand, measures the capability of the system to efficiently transfer data while performing communication operations. This data will provide researchers with insights into the overall performance and effectiveness of ISAC for dual S&C functionalities. Motivated by this, to more accurately quantify the fundamental performance limits of ISAC and investigate the performance tradeoffs of S&C6 developed a new MI-based framework for ISAC, in which the SR and CR are characterized by the sensing MI and the communication MI, respectively. There has been a significant surge in literature relevant to the performance of integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) recently. Characterization of SR-CR type of regions of ISAC systems with only one communication UT was performed in7 with information-theoretical analysis. Moreover9 discusses the single- UT ISAC systems performance trade-offs in S & C performance, which can be describable in an estimation-theoretical view, exploiting the Cramér-Rao bound metric for sensing and the minimum mean-square error metric for communications.

Utilizing the MI-based framework proposed in5, another work8 investigated the fundamental performance limits of ISAC systems involving a single-antenna UT under various beamforming vector designs. Building on this, researchers have expanded their inquiries to multi-UT scenarios where each UT is equipped with a single antenna, as explored in9. In their work, dirty paper coding is employed for interference management among communication users, and it is assumed that the interference from the sensing signal to the communication signal is perfectly mitigated to prioritize communications. These approaches, while effective, demand computationally intensive implementations.

To overcome these challenges, this research work10 has investigated downlink multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO)-ISAC systems featuring multiple UTs, each equipped with multiple antennas. User interference is handled using SDMA (space division multiple access) in this mode. However, the number of serviceable UTs is limited by the number of transmit antennas available in the base station (BS) intrinsically. This work has contributed to the existing literature of multi-antenna ISAC systems. That being said, in the case of an overloaded system, both UTs can suffer from severe IUI caused by the limited DoFs of the spatial domain. Such interference may affect the performance of the system by decreasing its effectiveness. Non-orthogonal multiple access, which is capable of reducing IUI, is an approach to remedy the aforementioned problem and enhance the system performance. In general, for IUI, NOMA dynamically assigns power to users based on their channel conditions so that weaker channels will be able to fight off stronger channel interference11,12. Moreover, the use of successive interference cancellation (SIC) at the receiver side decreases the impact of IUI on successive users13,14. NOMA is a significant enhancement for ISAC systems since its superior spectral efficiency enables more simultaneous users than the traditional multiple access techniques e.g., SDMA. Several works15–19 have introduced the concept of NOMA-ISAC. Nevertheless, it is worth noting that the majority of these works have primarily concentrated on the design aspects related to waveform or beamforming. On the other hand, only a few recent works went to the detail of the core performance evaluation of NOMA-ISAC systems20–22. That is, the work of20 studied the behavior of an uplink NOMA-based Semi-ISAC composition under a single-input single-output equivalent, focusing on only one UT pair and thereby ignoring inter-pair interference (IPI). Similarly, the work in21 proposed an uplink NOMA-ISAC framework with single-antenna UTs, exploring the impact of different decoding orders for the S&C signals. On the other hand, the performance of downlink NOMA-ISAC systems has been only studied in the work22, where a basic framework of single-input single-output NOMA-ISAC with a single UT pair is developed. Therefore, research on the performance characterization of NOMA-ISAC is still in its early stages, and the more general NOMA-ISAC within a MIMO framework involving multiple pairs of UTs remains unexplored.

In more recent studies, the authors in23 focuses on covert communication protocols for inter-environmental communication, particularly between drones and water-based vehicles. The research builds on the challenge of communication security, specifically in environments with high mobility and reflection-induced interference between air and water. It provides insights into location-aware communication protocols to ensure secure, reliable data exchange. The work references prior studies that explored secure communication in challenging environments, particularly those addressing node mobility and environmental interference. The study in24 presents a covert communication method using spatial modulation (SM) and artificial noise (AN) to enhance secrecy while transmitting data. It examines power allocation strategies for optimal covert capacity, comparing Gaussian and discrete constellation point distributions. This paper is relevant for improving covert communication methods and could inform resource management strategies that enhance security in integrated sensing and communication systems. The paper25 examines the application of orthogonal time frequency space (OTFS) modulation in cell-free massive MIMO ISAC systems. The work emphasizes power allocation strategies for maximizing the communication signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) while satisfying specific sensing SINR constraints. The results demonstrate that OTFS modulation significantly improves spectral efficiency (SE) compared to traditional OFDM signals, particularly in autonomous driving scenarios. This research28 addresses the challenges in vehicular communication networks, particularly focusing on resource allocation through deep reinforcement learning (DRL). It explores the impact of unreliable channel state information and the complexities of large-scale vehicular networks. The use of Proximal On-Policy schemes (POPS) optimizes data transmission, making it highly relevant for systems that require adaptive resource allocation. This paper26 explores the integration of massive MIMO with ISAC systems, focusing on the power allocation and the communication and sensing performance. The study provides a detailed analysis of achievable communication rates and the Cramér–Rao lower bound (CRLB) for sensing. The authors present a power allocation strategy based on successive convex approximation (SCA) to optimize the system’s communication rate while meeting sensing accuracy constraints. The authors in27 introduces an optimization algorithm using a combination of chaos and elite reverse learning with the Sparrow Search Algorithm (SSA) to optimize communication in Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) systems30,31. It addresses energy consumption, device collaboration, and task offloading in IIoT systems, with a focus on collaborative optimization. This study 28 investigates the joint optimization of beamforming and resource allocation in STAR-IRS-aided SCMA ISAC systems using Meta Deep Reinforcement Learning (MDRL). The paper addresses the challenges posed by non-convex optimization problems in integrated sensing and communication systems. The use of STAR-IRS enhances the system’s energy efficiency and coverage, which is further optimized using MDRL. Simulation results show improvements in energy efficiency compared to traditional methods.

While recent works have introduced dynamic resource allocation methods for ISAC, many of these are limited in scope—focusing primarily on beamforming or power allocation in single-user/single-carrier scenarios, or requiring computationally intensive optimization that restricts real-time applicability. Moreover, the joint allocation of power and frequency resources across multiple users and sensing tasks remains underexplored. Consequently, there is still a critical need for adaptive yet lightweight frameworks that can balance communication and sensing objectives efficiently in practical downlink ISAC deployments. Therefore, there is a critical need for an adaptive, low-complexity resource allocation framework that can dynamically assign power and frequency resources based on real-time channel conditions and task demands, while ensuring an effective balance between sensing and communication objectives in downlink ISAC scenarios.

This paper proposes a dynamic resource allocation algorithm based on downlink ISAC systems that optimizes the power and frequency jointly allocated to a number of communication users as well as a number of sensing tasks, to overcome the above limitations. Different from conventional fixed schemes, the proposed method utilizes instantaneous channel state information (CSI) and task-specific sensing requirements to reasonably allocate resources in real-time. The algorithm allows for a tunable balance between sensing accuracy and communication rate that can be tailored towards an optimal solution based on the end application by formulating a utility-based optimization problem. Through comparative simulations, the dynamic approach is shown to significantly outperform conventional schemes in terms of sensing mean square error (MSE), communication throughput, and overall utility—demonstrating its potential as a foundational component in the design of adaptive and intelligent ISAC-enabled 6G systems.

Existing solutions often rely on fixed allocation strategies or shared resource schemes or NOMA-ISAC schemes that do not fully leverage the distinct characteristics of sensing and communication. This paper proposes a novel dynamic resource allocation algorithm for dedicated sensing signals in downlink ISAC that optimizes power and frequency resources to maximize sensing accuracy while maintaining communication throughput.

In summary, while dynamic resource allocation methods for ISAC have been studied, most are restricted to beamforming- or power-only optimization, simplified single-user scenarios, or computationally intensive algorithms unsuitable for real-time operation. In contrast, our work introduces a lightweight dynamic framework that jointly allocates power and frequency resources across multiple users and sensing tasks, scales to complex downlink settings, and explicitly balances dual-function objectives through a tunable utility formulation. These contributions distinguish our approach from existing methods and demonstrate its practical potential for ISAC-enabled 6G networks.

Network model

Consider a downlink ISAC system where a base station (BS) serves multiple user equipment (UEs) while simultaneously performing sensing tasks, such as radar detection or environmental monitoring. The BS is equipped with a multi-antenna system capable of beamforming for spatial diversity. Let Nu​ be the number of UEs, and Ns​ be the number of sensing tasks. The system operates over a bandwidth B composed of multiple subcarriers. Each UE is allocated a subset of the available subcarriers, and dedicated sensing signals are transmitted in parallel with communication signals.

Objective function

The objective is to maximize the sensing accuracy while maintaining a minimum communication throughput for each UE. The formulated problem is non-convex due to the joint optimization of power and frequency variables under interference constraints, making closed-form optimal solutions infeasible. To address this, we propose a low-complexity iterative gradient ascent algorithm that converges to a stationary near-optimal solution suitable for real-time deployment. Detailed algorithm description for dynamic resource allocation in ISAC systems

The dynamic resource allocation algorithm proposed for Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) systems is central to optimizing the performance of the system by jointly allocating power and frequency resources to communication users (UEs) and sensing tasks. The gradient ascent–based allocation proposed here is heuristic and guarantees convergence only to a local stationary solution. While it lacks closed-form optimality guarantees, this design achieves the dual goals of low complexity and scalability to multi-user, multi-task ISAC scenarios, making it more practical for real-time 6G applications than computationally intensive convex or learning-based alternatives.

To make this process more accessible to readers unfamiliar with the approach, we provide a clearer breakdown of the algorithm, focusing on the application of the gradient ascent method for power updates, the criteria for convergence, and the overall optimization process.

NOMA-ISAC benchmarks

Prior NOMA-ISAC works also report improved sensing performance, though often measured in indirect ways (e.g. radar beampattern gain or estimation bounds rather than explicit MSE). For example, Zuo et al. 18 incorporate a RIS and NOMA to boost radar sensing power – their NOMA-enhanced RIS-ISAC design achieves a higher minimum beampattern gain toward targets than a comparable non-NOMA scheme. This improvement in beam pattern gain translates to stronger reflected signals and thus lower sensing error (better MSE) for all targets in overloaded scenarios. Similarly, Dou et al. 19 focus on sensing-efficient scheduling: by smartly selecting which targets to sense and optimizing beamforming, they ensure better sensing performance than baseline scheduling strategies​. While 19 does not directly quote an MSE percentage, their numerical results verify a clear sensing accuracy advantage over conventional equal allocation (fewer missed targets and lower estimation error)​. Ouyang et al. 21 analytically show that a fully integrated NOMA-ISAC system can achieve strictly superior sensing capability relative to orthogonal resource use – in fact, the achievable sensing-vs-communication region under NOMA-ISAC entirely covers that of a separated (FDSAC) system​. In other words, for any required communication rate, the joint ISAC approach can attain a sensing accuracy at least as good as (and often better than) a frequency-division scheme. This aligns with our findings: integrating resources (especially via dynamic allocation) yields tangible sensing accuracy gains. Notably, Yang et al. 17 demonstrate that even when adding security constraints, a NOMA-aided ISAC precoding design can “ensure the sensing performance” – i.e. maintain low sensing MSE – while serving more users securely.

NOMA-ISAC benchmarks

Many state-of-the-art works have highlighted NOMA’s spectral efficiency advantage for ISAC, which translates to higher communication capacity or rate. Zhang et al. 20 introduced a Semi-ISAC framework (part of the bandwidth dedicated to either sensing or communication, rest for ISAC) and analytically showed that NOMA-based Semi-ISAC achieves higher channel capacity than OMA-based Semi-ISAC​. In simulations, they observed that using NOMA to multiplex two users on the ISAC portion yields a notable boost in ergodic sum rate compared to strictly orthogonal access​. Likewise, Mu et al. 16 demonstrated that a NOMA beamforming design can deliver superior throughput under interference-limited conditions: their NOMA-ISAC scheme outperforms a conventional (orthogonal) ISAC in high-load scenarios, achieving a better communication–sensing trade-off (much higher sum rate for a given sensing power level in overloaded cases)​. In fact, at heavy load (K = 6 users in 16), the NOMA approach’s throughput comes very close to an “ideal” case (as if sensing and communication had no trade-off), whereas the conventional scheme had to severely curtail throughput to accommodate sensing​. Yang et al. 17 report that NOMA can also improve secure communication rate – their secure precoding scheme yielded higher secrecy sum-rate (SSR) than a traditional TDMA-based ISAC, while still meeting sensing requirements. This implies NOMA allowed them to serve multiple users simultaneously (improving spectral usage) rather than orthogonal time slots, boosting the effective data rate. Similarly, Dou et al. 19 found that with optimized scheduling, communication throughput is maintained or improved alongside sensing gains​ – their approach avoids the communication-rate degradation that naive sensing-first strategies might cause. Overall, these benchmarks consistently show 15–25% (approx.) throughput improvements when moving from orthogonal or static allocations to NOMA-powered schemes (depending on scenario). For instance, the semi-ISaC study 20 effectively noted NOMA provides a higher capacity region, and 16 saw the NOMA scheme supporting roughly ~ 2 × the sum-rate of the orthogonal scheme under certain conditions (e.g. when spatial interference is high).

Comparison: Fig. 3 shows the comparison. The 22.86% rate enhancement achieved by our dynamic algorithm is in line with or better than the gains in prior NOMA-ISAC studies. Notably, our approach realizes this improvement together with a sensing boost, whereas some literature solutions emphasize communication gains predominantly.

Communication Rate vs λ.

The ability to adaptively allocate subcarriers means our scheme can opportunistically use favorable frequencies for data transmission, something most beamforming-centric NOMA-ISAC works (which often assume a single channel or single-carrier) could not exploit. Consequently, the proposed method can match state-of-the-art sum rate performance (e.g. reaching the high throughput region of NOMA-ISAC observed in 16 and 20) while still reserving resources for sensing tasks. It essentially combines the multi-user efficiency of NOMA (serving 10 UEs concurrently) with fine-grained frequency-domain scheduling, yielding a throughput advantage that outstrips static and OMA benchmarks and rivals the best known NOMA-ISAC schemes. In quantitative terms, a ~ 23% rate increase over static allocation is a significant leap, ensuring that communication QoS is greatly improved in our ISAC system – comparable to the leap seen when introducing NOMA in prior works.

Total utility and tradeoff

Proposed dynamic allocation

A key strength of our approach is its composite utility optimization, balancing sensing and communication. Using a tunable weight λ, we optimized a combined utility = (–MSE) + λ·(throughput). Under balanced weighting (λ = 0.5 in our tests), the dynamic scheme achieved a 13.49% higher total utility than the benchmark algorithms. This reflects an overall superior trade-off: the proposed resource allocation pushes the Pareto frontier outward, simultaneously improving sensing and throughput to yield a larger aggregate benefit. The 13.5% utility gain encapsulates the net improvement in dual-function performance. We emphasize that this gain comes from joint optimization of power and frequency, which allows the algorithm to find solutions that static frequency assignment or pure power allocation approaches cannot reach.

NOMA-ISAC benchmarks

Few prior works explicitly define a single “utility” metric combining sensing and communication, but many evaluated the S&C trade-off. Mu et al. 16 did formulate a weighted sum of communication throughput and sensing power (analogous to our utility) and solved it via beamforming design. Their results show that NOMA-ISAC achieves a better communication–sensing trade-off than conventional ISAC – for example, in 16 the NOMA scheme could simultaneously obtain high throughput and high radar sensing power, whereas the conventional scheme had to sacrifice one for the other. In the extreme case of system overload, they found the NOMA-ISAC’s joint performance was close to an “ideal” ISAC with no trade-off, underscoring a dramatically improved utility region. Dou et al. 19 similarly targeted dual-objective performance: they ensured that their scheduling/beamforming solution improves both sensing and communication metrics relative to benchmarks​, effectively moving toward a higher overall utility (though it doesn’t report a single utility value, the qualitative improvement in both aspects implies a better combined outcome). Ouyang et al. 21 provide insight that ISAC (joint) systems have more degrees of freedom than separated ones, leading to an expanded S&C capability region​. This theoretical result supports the idea that an optimal joint design (like our utility maximization) can dominate simpler strategies. In summary, the literature suggests that NOMA-based joint designs yield a noticeably superior S&C trade-off – e.g., one study noted a 29% sensing CRB reduction at equal communication rate using an advanced multiple access scheme (RSMA) over NOMA​, highlighting the headroom for improving utility in multi-user ISAC. Most NOMA-ISAC works report qualitative or case-by-case trade-off improvements (curves showing one metric vs the other). Our work goes further by quantifying a single utility increase.

Comparison

As can be seen in Fig. 4 the proposed dynamic allocation is unique in providing a quantifiable total utility gain (~ 13.5%) across a multi-user ISAC system. This indicates a more balanced and efficient trade-off than any static or heuristic approach, and it stands on par with the best optimized schemes in literature. For instance, our utility gain encapsulates simultaneous ~ 17% better sensing and ~ 23% better rate – a combination that is difficult to achieve with one-dimensional optimizations. Prior NOMA-ISAC algorithms usually excel in one domain (e.g. maximize radar power or communication sum-rate) while maintaining the other, whereas our approach actively optimizes both together. The result is that no compromise is needed: we improve sensing and communication concurrently (as evidenced by positive gains in both MSE and rate), which translates to a higher composite utility. This holistic improvement is a clear advantage of the dynamic joint allocation framework. It underscores the uniqueness of our solution: by leveraging instantaneous CSI and task requirements across subcarriers, we tap into performance gains that isolated power allocation or single-carrier NOMA methods left on the table. The dynamic scheme effectively outperforms conventional methods in all metrics simultaneously – a trait that few prior works explicitly demonstrated with numerical gains. Overall, the balanced utility improvement highlights the practical benefit of our method for ISAC: it can adapt to different λ priorities to trade off S&C, but under a balanced scenario it simply outclasses the benchmarks across the board.

Discussion

As seen above, the proposed dynamic scheme outperforms traditional static allocation by a wide margin in all three metrics (sensing MSE, communication rate, and total utility). Compared to other NOMA-ISAC algorithms, our approach achieves a more holistic improvement: whereas others often focus on one aspect (e.g. maximizing sensing or ensuring comm capacity), our joint power–frequency allocation delivers strong gains on both simultaneously. This is attributed to its fine-grained resource control (per-subcarrier power tuning) and adaptability to instantaneous channel conditions and sensing needs. In qualitative terms, the proposed algorithm combines the strengths of prior NOMA-ISAC strategies – aggressive spectral reuse for high throughput 15,16,20, interference management for sensing fidelity 17,18, and adaptive scheduling for efficiency 19 – within a single framework. Its advantages and uniqueness can be summarized as follows:

Simultaneous Sensing & Communication Gains: Unlike many benchmarks that trade one for the other, the dynamic scheme boosts sensing accuracy and communication rate together, as evidenced by the 17.65% MSE drop alongside a 22.86% rate increase​. Prior works ensured one domain didn’t degrade (or improved one at a time), but our results show concurrent enhancements in both domains, indicating a superior joint optimization.

The proposed dynamic joint power/frequency allocation algorithm offers quantitatively superior performance on sensing MSE, communication rate, and total utility when compared to the state-of-the-art NOMA-ISAC schemes. It leverages the insights of prior works – NOMA’s spectral efficiency and interference cancellation benefits – and extends them with a broader resource allocation strategy. The result is an enhanced ISAC system that can sense more accurately (MSE ↓17.65%) and communicate faster (rate ↑22.86%), yielding an overall utility gain of ~ 13.5% over conventional methods​. These improvements underscore the advantage and uniqueness of our approach in achieving an effective balance between sensing and communications in downlink ISAC, thereby highlighting its potential as a benchmark for future 6G ISAC resource allocation designs.

Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a dynamic resource allocation algorithm for dedicated sensing signals in this context of downlink ISAC systems. The proposed algorithm enhances the accuracy of sensing while the communication performance is not deteriorated, by optimizing the resources power and frequency resources. Results from simulations comprehensively show that the proposed downlink ISAC system adopting the dynamic resource allocation scheme is highly efficient. The dynamic scheme achieves superior allocation performance over the current NOMA-ISAC schemes over diverse metrics by simultaneously optimizing power and frequency-domain resources according to channel instantaneous state information and sensing task requirements. In specific, it reduces the sensing MSE by up to 17.65% at the balanced λ value, improves the communication rate by up to 22.86% at the balanced λ value, and increases the total utility by 13.49% at the same time, indicating its superior ability of joint optimization under different system priorities. Static and heuristic-based schemes, and other NOMA-ISAC schemes have the merit of easier implementation but do not come close to achieving a dynamic balance of the conflicting decision objectives to optimize the tradeoff between sensing and communication. In contrast, the dynamic scheme exhibits strong adaptability and represents a powerful candidate for real-world ISAC applications in next-generation wireless systems where flexibility and performance co-optimization is paramount. ISAC systems that adopt this strategy may improve the future 6G networks, that is, high-precision sensing, like autonomous driving and smart city applications.

Finally, we note that the methodological contribution of this paper lies in integrating subcarrier and power allocation into a utility-driven dynamic framework, rather than in introducing a new optimization theory. Future work will focus on enhancing theoretical depth by incorporating convex relaxation, stochastic optimization, or hybrid learning-based approaches that can provide provable performance bounds without sacrificing real-time applicability.

Future work

This work will be extended in future research to more sophisticated multi-antenna scenarios and the use of machine learning methods for real-time adaptation of resource allocation strategies. Also, the generalization of this algorithm to full-duplex ISAC systems will be studied. Another important direction is to relax the assumption of perfect CSI. In practical ISAC systems, CSI acquisition is imperfect due to estimation errors, feedback delays, and mobility. These imperfections can significantly affect both communication throughput and sensing accuracy. Future work will therefore extend the proposed algorithm to incorporate robust resource allocation under imperfect CSI, for example by employing stochastic optimization, chance-constrained formulations, or machine learning–based CSI prediction and compensation techniques. This will further enhance the practicality of the proposed scheme in real-time 6G deployments. Future research will explore extensions of the algorithm toward provable optimality through convex approximation or hybrid learning-based approaches, while further tailoring it to application domains such as vehicular networks, smart cities, and industrial automation.

Another important extension concerns the integration of security into ISAC resource allocation. While this paper considers performance optimization under ideal secure conditions, real-world deployments face challenges such as eavesdropping, spoofing, and intentional jamming. Recent works 29–31 have highlighted the potential of secure ISAC designs, dual-functional communication–jamming frameworks, and RSMA-based methods to counter adversarial environments. Incorporating such security-aware mechanisms into our dynamic resource allocation framework will be a key focus of future research, enabling ISAC systems to operate reliably in hostile or mission-critical scenarios. Future extensions will also target high-mobility scenarios by incorporating predictive CSI models and lightweight allocation updates, ensuring robustness in rapidly time-varying wireless environments such as vehicular or UAV networks. In addition, we also intend to refine the discrete subcarrier allocation step using combinatorial optimization or machine learning–based assignment techniques, providing tighter optimality guarantees while respecting the discrete nature of OFDM resources.

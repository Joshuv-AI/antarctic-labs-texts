Abstract
. We study three conditions that control the behaviour of isotropy in étale groupoids, and their relationships under the additional assumptions of second-countability and Hausdorffness. We examine a number of examples that show these properties are distinct. Working under the assumption of the Zermelo-Fraenkel axioms, excluding choice, we then examine an alternate characterization of topological freeness, first introduced by Anantharaman-Delaroche, in the non-Hausdorff setting. Finally, we prove an equivalence between the Baire Category Theorem and an étale groupoid theorem, along with similar equivalences to other weakenings of the Axiom of Choice.

1.
Introduction

Étale groupoids have long been an object of study by operator algebraists for their ability to model many important classes of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras. Of particular interest is the behaviour of isotropy – that is, the collection of morphisms whose source and range coincide. In the étale groupoid literature, there exist a number of conditions that control the existence and properties of isotropy in étale groupoids: effective, topologically principal and topologically free. The terminology in the literature regarding these properties is inconsistent, and thus one of our goals is to provide an unambiguous reference. For second-countable Hausdorff groupoids, these three conditions coincide (Theorem
3.6
), but in general they are distinct (see Examples
3.7
).

As the study of non-Hausdorff groupoids and non-second-countable groupoids in
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra theory and ring theory has become more common, the nuances of these conditions have become more salient and identifying which condition is the “correct” choice is not always obvious and can require additional assumptions (cf.
[
20
]
and
[
9
]
).
In this paper we put forward evidence that topologically free is the condition that should be prioritised in the setting of étale groupoids. We do so both by gathering results from the literature in an expository fashion, and also by presenting some new supporting results.

We begin in Section
3
by defining the three main conditions, establishing the relationships between them (see diagram
3.1
), and briefly discussing related ideas that appear in the literature. We then provide examples to demonstrate that they are indeed distinct (Examples
3.7
). In section
4
, we provide an equivalent characterisation of the topologically free condition (see Proposition
4.1
). This condition was established in the Hausdorff and second-countable setting by Anantharaman-Delaroches in the 1990’s
[
1
, Proposition 2.3]
, and has proven useful in the more general Hausdorff setting, for example in
[
6
, Lemma 3.1]
.

In the final section, we work only within the Zermelo-Fraenkel (ZF) axioms and consider the relationship between these three groupoid conditions and variants of the Baire Category Theorem – see Corollaries
5.5
,
5.6
and
5.7
. These results improve upon the work of the first author and J.H Brown in
[
7
]
, where a more complicated relationship is established for a special case.

If

3.
Groupoid isotropy conditions

We now introduce our main isotropy conditions, and briefly discuss related notions that have appeared in the literature.

Definition 3.1
.

An étale groupoid
G
is
effective
if
\mathrm{Int}(\mathrm{Iso}(G))=G^{(0)}
.

The definitions of effective groupoids found in the literature is fairly consistent. In some cases, the expression “essentially principal” has been used to mean effective (see, for example,
[
14
]
and
[
21
]
), but in general essentially principal holds a different meaning. An étale groupoid
G
is said to be a
strongly effective groupoid
if, whenever
V\subseteq G^{(0)}
is a closed invariant subset (in the subspace topology), then
G|_{V}
is effective. It is clear that if
G
is strongly effective then it is effective, since
G^{(0)}
is closed and invariant in itself (cf.
[
8
]
).

In
[
7
]
the first author used the term “effective” to refer to the property we now call topologically free.

Definition 3.2
.

An étale groupoid
G
is
topologically principal
if the set of units with trivial isotropy is dense in
G^{(0)}
.

In
[
12
, Definition 3.4.6]
, Exel and Pitts use both the expressions “topologically free” and “topologically principal” interchangeably to refer to topologically principal groupoids. Moreover, they introduce what we refer to as
strongly topologically principal
, which requires that whenever
V\subseteq G^{(0)}
is closed and invariant (in the subspace topology), then
G|_{V}
is topologically principal. Again, it is clear strongly topologically principal implies topologically principal.

In a purely algebraic sense, a groupoid
G
is
principal
if
\mathrm{Iso}(G)=G^{(0)}
. It is clear that all principal groupoids are both strongly topologically principal and topologically principal.

Definition 3.3
.

An étale groupoid
G
is
topologically free
if
\mathrm{Int}(\mathrm{Iso}(G)\setminus G^{(0)})=\emptyset
.

The concept of topological freeness was initially studied by Archbold and Spielberg in the context of actions and dynamical systems (see, for instance,
[
2
]
). Kwaśniewski and Meyer explicitly defined topologically free as a property of étale groupoids in
[
19
]
. Later in
[
20
]
, they defined an étale groupoid
G
to be
residually topologically free
if, whenever
V\subseteq G
is closed and invariant, then the groupoid
G|_{V}
is topologically free. To be consistent with the corresponding “strong” properties of being effective and topologically principal, we refer to this property as
strongly topologically free
.

Moreover, Kwaśniewski and Meyer introduce the notion of an étale groupoid being
AS topologically free
, (AS for Archbold-Spielberg) which requires that whenever
U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}\subseteq G\setminus G^{(0)}
are finitely many bisections, then the union

\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\{x\in G^{(0)}:xGx\cap U_{i}\neq\emptyset\}

has empty interior.

We then have the following implications:

\text{Strongly topologically free}\Rightarrow\text{topologically free}\Leftarrow\text{AS topologically free}.

The reader is referred to
[
3
, Remark 2.1]
for additional discussion of these conditions and their relationships. Furthermore, in
[
26
]
, the authors discuss these conditions in the context of simplicity of Steinberg algebras, and provide a number of examples of étale groupoids associated to self-similar actions that satisfy some, but not all, of these conditions.

The following relationships between these conditions are established using only ZF axioms.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
G
be a locally compact étale groupoid with Hausdorff unit space.

(a)

If
G
is effective, then
G
is topologically free.

(b)

If
G
is topologically principal, then
G
is topologically free.

(c)

If
G^{(0)}
is closed in
G
, and
G
is topologically principal, then
G
is effective (and topologically free).

Remark 3.5
.

When
G^{(0)}
is Hausdorff, the condition that
G^{(0)}
is closed in
G
in Theorem
3.4
(
c
) is equivalent to
G
being Hausdorff, see for example
[
11
, Proposition 3.10]
. However, we are not assuming
G^{(0)}
is Hausdorff. So the condition, as stated, is weaker than
G
being Hausdorff.

Proof.

Item (
a
) follows immediately from the definitions. Item (
b
) is straightforward contrapositive argument: Suppose
G
is not topologically free. Then, there exists some open
U\subseteq\mathrm{Iso}(G)\setminus G^{(0)}
, and so
s(U)
is an open subset of
G^{(0)}
consisting entirely of units with nontrivial isotropy. Thus,
G
is not topologically principal.

Finally, item (
c
) is similar to
[
24
, Proposition 3.6(i)]
. Again, we provide the details via a contrapositive argument. Suppose
G
is not effective. Then there exists
U\subseteq\mathrm{Iso}(G)
such that
U
is not contained in the unit space. Thus
U\setminus G^{(0)}
is a nonempty open subset of
G
that consists entirely of nontrivial isotropy. Since
r
is an open map,
r(U\setminus G^{(0)})
is an open subset of
G^{(0)}
consisting entirely of units with nontrivial isotropy. Therefore,
G
is not topologically principal.
∎

We gather a number of examples to demonstrate that these three conditions are indeed distinct. The first is well known, see for example,

(a)

Consider

with the standard topology

(b)

The following example is from

For each

consists of elements
(\frac{n}{s},(x,e^{i\theta}))

a<b\in(0,1)

st\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then, there exists
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{t})
such that
s+\varepsilon\in(a,b)
. Then,

and so
(s+\varepsilon)t\notin\mathbb{Z}

(c)

The following is a modification of the groupoid construction we use later on in our proof of Theorem

, and for each

. Set

. Let

, and for each

4.
Non-Hausdorff Topological Freeness

In this section, we present an alternate characterization of topological freeness. The original statement was presented by C. Anantharaman-Delaroche (see
[
1
, Proposition 2.3]
), and was associated to a topologically principal groupoid under the additional assumptions that
G
is both second-countable and Hausdorff. An equivalence was established for Hausdorff effective groupoids in
[
6
, Lemma 3.1]
. We are not aware of anyone considering the non-Hausdorff case.
When
G
is not assumed to be Hausdorff, the unit space may not be closed in
G
. This forces us to approach the proof differently to
[
1
, Proposition 2.3]

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
G
be a locally compact étale groupoid, and consider the following.

(a)

For every open
U\subseteq G^{(0)}
and compact
K\subseteq G\setminus G^{(0)}
there exists a non-empty open set
V\subseteq U
such that
VKV=\emptyset
.

(b)

The groupoid
G
is topologically free.

Then
\eqref{it1:claire}\implies\eqref{it2:claire}
. If
G^{(0)}
is Hausdorff, we have
\eqref{it1:claire}\iff\eqref{it2:claire}
.

Proof.

For (
a
) implies (
b
), we prove the contrapositive. Suppose that
G
is not topologically free. Let
W\subseteq\mathrm{Iso}(G)\setminus G^{(0)}
be a nonempty open set. Since
G
is étale, we may assume that
W
is a bisection. Fix
\gamma\in W
. Using that
G
is locally compact, we can find an open
B\subseteq W
containing
\gamma
such that
B
is contained in a compact set
K
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
K
is contained in
G\setminus G^{(0)}
– if not, we replace it with
K\cap(G\setminus G^{(0)})
. Then, since
G
is étale,
G\setminus G^{(0)}
is closed and so
K\cap(G\setminus G^{(0)})
is closed in
K
and thus compact.

Now consider
r(B)\subseteq G^{(0)}
and fix
u\in r(B)
. Then
r|_{B}^{-1}(u)\in B\subseteq K
. Since
B\subseteq\mathrm{Iso}(G)
we also have
s(r|_{B}^{-1}(u))=u
. Thus
r|_{B}^{-1}(u)\in uKu
. Hence, for every nonempty open set
V\subseteq r(B)
we will have
VKV\neq\emptyset
.

For the reverse implication, assume that
G^{(0)}
is Hausdorff, and suppose that
G
is topologically free.
Fix a compact
K\subseteq G\setminus G^{(0)}
and an open
U\subseteq G^{(0)}
. Since
K
is compact, there exist finitely many open bisections
\{B_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}
such that
K\subseteq\bigcup_{i=0}^{n}B_{i}
. We can further assume that
K\cap B_{i}\neq\emptyset
for each
i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
. We proceed by induction on the the number of bisections required to cover
K
.

For the base case, let
B
be an open bisection such that
K\subseteq B
.
If
UKU=\emptyset
, then setting
V\coloneqq U
, we are done. Suppose
UKU\neq\emptyset
. We may also assume that
U\cap B=\emptyset
, otherwise
V\coloneqq U\cap B
would satisfy
VKV=\emptyset
. Thus, under these assumptions,
UBU\subseteq G\setminus G^{(0)}
is a non-empty open set.

Since
G
is topologically free, we have that
\mathrm{Int}(\mathrm{Iso}(G)\setminus G^{(0)})=\emptyset
, and so the open set
UBU
is not entirely isotropy. Thus, there exists
\gamma\in UBU\setminus\mathrm{Iso}(G)
such that
u:=r(\gamma)\in U
.

We claim that there exists a neighbourhood
V
of

such that
VBV=\emptyset
. Let
\{V_{j}\}_{j\in J}
be a neighbourhood basis for

contained in
U
. Toward a contradiction, suppose that for every
j\in J
, there exists some
\alpha_{j}\in V_{j}BV_{j}
– that is,
r(\alpha_{j}),s(\alpha_{j})\in V_{j}
. It follows that
r(\alpha_{j})\to u
, and so by

, there exists a subnet
\{\alpha_{j_{l}}\}
contained in
B

\alpha_{j_{l}}\to s|_{B}^{-1}(u)=\gamma
. Since

is continuous, and

is Hausdorff, we have
r(s^{-1}|_{B}(u))=s(s^{-1}|_{B}(u))=u
. Since
B
is a bisection, it must be that
\gamma=s^{-1}|_{B}(u)
, and so
\gamma\in\mathrm{Iso}(G)
, a contradiction. Thus, there is some neighbourhood

of

VBV=\emptyset
.

For the inductive hypothesis, suppose that there is
m>1
such that for every open set
U\subseteq G^{(0)}
and every compact set

such that there exist

Fix a compact set

We partition

K_{m+1}:=K\setminus\cup^{m}_{i=1}B_{i},\qquad K_{m}\coloneqq K\setminus B_{m+1}

we see that

and

is compact and coverable by

alone. Hence, there exists a nonempty open set
U^{\prime}\subseteq U
such that
U^{\prime}K_{m+1}U^{\prime}=\emptyset.

Moreover,
K_{m}\coloneqq K\setminus B_{m+1}
is compact and coverable by the
m
bisections
\{B_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
. Thus, there exists a non-empty open set
V\subseteq U^{\prime}\subseteq U
such that
VK_{m}V=\emptyset
. Since
V\subseteq U^{\prime}
and
U^{\prime}K_{m+1}U^{\prime}=\emptyset
, it follows that
VKV=\emptyset
.

∎

5.
Analogue of the Baire Category Theorem

A topological space has the
Baire
property if any countable union of closed sets with empty interior also has empty interior (see
[
22
, Chapter 8]
). In this section, working without
\AC
, we establish a connection between Baire spaces and the relationship between the topologically free and topologically principal properties of étale groupoids. As corollaries, we extend this connection to various weakenings of the
\AC
, including the
\AComega
and
\DC
.

A special case was examined by Brown and Clark in
[
7
]
. Working in the context of a (not necessarily étale) topological groupoid with additional assumptions, a particular formulation of the Baire category theorem was shown to be equivalent to a statement relating being effective and topologically principal. In particular, the formulation of the Baire category theorem for complete metric spaces used is equivalent to
\DC

[
16
]
. The hypotheses of the result in
[
7
]
are somewhat technical and not very natural – they prioritized injectivity of the range and source maps at the cost of openness. In what follows, we sacrifice injectivity in favour of openness.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
G
be a topological groupoid. We call an open set
B\subseteq G
an
open iso-section
if

r|_{B\setminus G^{(0)}}
is injective.

If
U\subseteq G^{(0)}
, then we write
\overline{U}^{0}
to denote the closure of
U
in
G^{(0)}
with respect to the subspace topology. If
G
is Hausdorff then
\overline{U}^{0}=\overline{U}
, but in general they need not coincide. The following Lemma is an adaptation of
[
7
, Lemma 4.3]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
G
be a topologically free étale groupoid, and let
B\subseteq G
be an open iso-section. Then,

(a)

r(\mathrm{Iso}(B)\setminus G^{(0)})
has empty interior;

(b)

\overline{r(\mathrm{Iso}(B)\setminus G^{(0)})}^{0}
has empty interior.

Proof.

For item (
a
), we argue by contradiction. Suppose that there exists an open set
W\subseteq r(\mathrm{Iso}(B)\setminus G^{(0)})
. Then

r^{-1}(W)\cap B\subseteq\mathrm{Iso}(B)\setminus G^{(0)}\subseteq\mathrm{Iso}(G)\setminus G^{(0)}.

Since
r
is continuous and
B
is open,
r^{-1}(W)\cap B
is open, which contradicts that
G
is topologically free.

For item (
b
),
first we claim that
r(\mathrm{Iso}(B)\setminus G^{((0)})
is closed in
r(B)
with respect to the subspace topology.
Since
G
is a topological groupoid,
\mathrm{Iso}(G)
is closed in
G
and since
G
is étale,
G^{(0)}
is open in
G
. We have

\mathrm{Iso}(B)\setminus G^{(0)}=B\cap(\mathrm{Iso}(G)\setminus G^{(0)})

and hence
\mathrm{Iso}(B)\setminus G^{(0)}
is closed in
B
.
Since
r
is injective on
\mathrm{Iso}(B)\setminus G^{(0)}
, it is straightforward to check that
r(\mathrm{Iso}(B)\setminus G^{(0)})
is closed in
r(B)
as claimed. Thus

\overline{r(\mathrm{Iso}(B)\setminus G^{(0)})}^{0}\cap r(B)=r(\mathrm{Iso}(B)\setminus G^{(0)}).

We now prove (
b
) by way of contradiction.
Suppose there exists a nonempty open set

U\subseteq\overline{r(\mathrm{Iso}(B)\setminus G^{(0)})}^{0}.

Then
U\cap r(B)
is open because
r
is an open map, and contains
U\cap r(\mathrm{Iso}(B)\setminus G^{(0)})
so is nonempty. But,

U\cap r(B)\subseteq\overline{r(\mathrm{Iso}(B)\setminus G^{(0)})}^{0}\cap r(B)=r(\mathrm{Iso}(B)\setminus G^{(0)}),

which contradicts item (
a
) and hence no such
U
exists.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
X
be a topological space. The following are equivalent:

(a)

The topological space
X
is Baire.

(b)

Let
G
be an étale groupoid with
G^{(0)}=X
, and suppose
G
admits a countable cover of open iso-sections. If
G
is topologically free, then
G
is topologically principal.

Proof.

For
\eqref{it1:baire}\Rightarrow\eqref{it2:baire}
, the argument is similar to that of
[
19
, Proposition 2.24]
and
[
24
, Proposition 3.6]
. Suppose that
X
is Baire and let
G
be a topologically free, étale groupoid with
G^{(0)}=X
. Let
\{B_{n}\}
be a countable cover of
G
by open iso-sections. We show that the set of units of
G
with trivial isotropy is dense in
G^{0}=X
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
let

C_{n}:=\overline{r(\mathrm{Iso}(B_{n})\setminus G^{(0)})}^{0}\subseteq G^{(0)}.

Then each
C_{n}
has empty interior by Lemma
5.2
(
b
). Since
X
is Baire, the set

C\coloneqq\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}C_{n}

also has empty interior. Thus every open set in
G^{(0)}
has nontrivial intersection with the complement of
C
. Since
\{B_{n}\}
is a cover, all units with nontrivial istropy are contained in
C
and hence each of the units in the complement of
C
have trivial isotropy. Thus, the units with trivial isotropy are dense.

To show (
b
) implies (
a
), fix a topological space
X
with a basis
\mathcal{B}
, and a countable collection of closed sets
\{C_{n}\}
each with empty interior. We construct a groupoid
G
as in
[
7
, Example 3.2]
that is a generalised two-headed snake where we attach a head for each element of
C_{n}
. In particular, we set
G^{(0)}\coloneqq X
and for each
x\in C_{n}
we adjoin a groupoid element
\gamma_{x}
such that
s(\gamma_{x})=r(\gamma_{x})
and
\gamma_{x}\gamma_{x}=x
.

To define the topology on
G
, for each
x\in X
and each
B\in\mathcal{B}
with
x\in B
, we define
B_{x}=(B-\{x\})\cup\{\gamma_{x}\}
, giving us a basis

\mathcal{B}\cup\left(\bigcup_{x\in X}\bigcup_{\{B\in\mathcal{B}:x\in B\}}\{B_{x}\}\right)

under which
G
is a topological groupoid. Since each basic open set is a bisection,
G
is étale. Without loss of generality, we assume that
X\in\mathcal{B}
(if not, we may add it without altering the topology on
X
). For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
define

B_{n}=\bigcup_{x\in C_{n}}X_{x}.

It is clear that
r|_{B_{n}\setminus G^{(0)}}
is injective, and as a union of basis elements it is open, hence
B_{n}
is an open iso-section. Thus, the collection
\{B_{n}\}\cup\{X\}
is a countable cover of
G
by open iso-sections.

Notice that every open set in
G
has nontrivial intersection with
G^{(0)}
, thus
G
is topologically free and hence (
b
) gives us that
G
is also topologically principal.
That is, the set of units with trivial isotropy is dense in
G^{(0)}
, which in this case, says the complement of
C\coloneqq\cup_{n}C_{n}
is dense. Therefore
C
has empty interior and hence
X
is Baire.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Since an open bisection is automatically an open iso-section, any second-countable étale groupoid has a countable cover of open iso-sections. However, if an étale groupoid has a countable cover of open iso-sections, this does not imply the groupoid is second-countable. For example, construct a groupoid as above such that
C_{n}
is uncountable for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, this groupoid has a countable cover of open iso-sections but is not second-countable.

Remark 5.8
.

In general, having a countable cover of open iso-sections is a weaker condition than having a countable cover of open bisections. However, if
G^{(0)}
is closed in
G
, then the two conditions are equivalent. In particular, if
\{B_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a countable cover of
G
by iso-sections, we can set
D_{i}\coloneqq B_{i}\setminus G^{(0)}
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, each
D_{i}
is an open bisection, and so
\{D_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\cup\{X\}
is a countable cover of
G
by open bisections.

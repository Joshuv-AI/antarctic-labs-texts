siRNA silencing and hypoxia challenge indicate that the function of common carp (Cyprinus carpio) hif-1αb genes are tightly linked to hif-1αa and hif-3α genes
Authors: Xianzong Wang, Huili Zhai, Jiali Guo, Xueyi Wang, Libo Gu, Tongyao Li, Qing Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundFishes are susceptible to hypoxia stress, while the common carp is known for its high tolerance to hypoxia. The hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF) pathway directly regulates the cell’s response to hypoxia. Still, it is currently unknown which members of the hif-α genes are present in common carp and their specific functions.ResultsIn this study, we found that the hif-1α, hif-2α, and hif-3α genes of common carp all contained twice the number of copies of their orthologs in zebrafish. Common carp has four copies of the hif-1α gene, of which the two hif-1αa genes were expressed at low levels in the vast majority of tissues, while the two hif-1αb genes were expressed at high levels in multiple tissues. We silenced the two hif-1αb genes using chitosan nanoparticles (CSNPs) carrying siRNA and subjected two groups to hypoxic stress. Transcriptome sequencing results show that whether under normoxia or hypoxia, the number of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) caused by silencing the hif-1αb genes in the heart exceeds 1,000, far more than the number of DEGs in the gills or brain. GO enrichment and KEGG enrichment showed that DEGs in the heart were mainly related to immune function and myocardial contraction. DEGs in the gills and brain also enriched many immune-related terms, and some DEGs in the gills were related to iron metabolism and erythropoiesis. Among the paralogs, the two hif-1αa genes were most obviously up-regulated under normoxia, while the hif-3α genes were most obviously up-regulated under hypoxia. We did not find any downstream genes of the HIF pathway that were specifically regulated by the hif-1αb genes.ConclusionsThe main effect site of the common carp hif-1αb genes is the heart, and their main functions are to regulate immune response and myocardial contraction. Their functions are partially redundant with the hif-1αa genes and hif-3α genes. When their expressions are inhibited, the expression of hif-1αa genes or hif-3α genes would be up-regulated in specific contexts, thereby compensating for their loss of function. The downstream genes of the HIF pathway in common carp may be generally regulated by multiple hif-α genes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12864-024-11141-0.Keywords: SiRNA silencing, Chitosan nanoparticles, Hypoxia challenge, Hypoxia-inducible factor, Transcriptome sequencing

Background

Common carp (Cyprinus carpio) is a benthic freshwater fish with a long history of aquaculture in China; it has a mild temperament, omnivorous habit, and strong environmental adaptability and is widely distributed in China. Common carp are not only edible; some strains also have ornamental value. The level of dissolved oxygen in water is controlled by a series of processes, including mixing, air–water exchange, photosynthesis, and consumption by aquatic species, which can be altered by anthropogenic and climate changes, thus leading to rapid temporal and spatial variations [1]. Therefore, aquatic animals are more susceptible to hypoxic stress than terrestrial animals. The ability of different fish to tolerate hypoxia varies greatly, while the common carp and its close relatives, the goldfish (Carassius auratus), are known for their outstanding tolerance to hypoxia [2, 3]. However, the specific molecular mechanisms behind this are still unclear.

Hypoxia-inducible factors (HIFs) are critical transcription factors that regulate oxygen homeostasis at the cellular level [4, 5]. The existence of a HIF was first predicted in 1991 through an investigation of the cis-acting regulatory elements of the human erythropoietin gene (EPO), the expression of which is known to be induced under hypoxic conditions [6]. Wang and Semenza subsequently succeeded in purifying the responsible factor in 1995, identified it as a heterodimer composed of two subunits, which were designated as HIF-1α and HIF-1β (also known as aryl hydrocarbon nuclear receptor translocator, ARNT), respectively [7]. After that, other α subunits, including HIF-2α and HIF-3α, have been identified [4, 8]. It is generally believed that hif-1α mainly responds to acute hypoxia, while hif-2α plays an important role in the process of chronic hypoxia [9]. As for HIF-3α, it has long been thought to be a negative regulator of HIF-1α and HIF-2α [10]. Most of the studies to date have concentrated on the role of HIF-1α, with those of HIF-2α and HIF-3α remaining less clear [4, 6].

The β subunit is stable and ubiquitously expressed in different tissues. The α unit, be it the HIF-1α, HIF-2α, or HIF-3α, is unstable under normoxic conditions due to the degradation of the PHD-VHL system [4, 11]. Besides, an asparagine residue in the C-terminal transactivation domain (C-TAD) would also be hydroxylated under normoxic conditions via the activity of factor inhibiting HIF (FIH) [6]. This will suppress recruitment of the transcriptional co-activator complex, EP300/CREBBP acetyltransferases, to the HIF-αs. Under hypoxic conditions, HIF-αs become stable and acquire transactivation activity due to decreased hydroxylation activity of PHDs and FIH. The active form of HIF-αs then translocate into the nucleus and interact with ARNT through bHLH (basic helix-loop-helix) and PAS (Per-Arnt-Sim) domains [12]. The resulting heterodimers, HIFs (HIF-1/2/3), induce the transcription initiation of hundreds of genes by binding to HREs (hypoxia response elements) that are located in or around their promoter regions [13, 14]. Downstream genes will promote hypoxia adaptation via enhanced anaerobic glycolysis, angiogenesis, erythrocyte production, and so on [15].

The elucidation of the function of the HIF pathway is mainly based on studies at the cellular level [4, 6], and it is not known to what extent it determines the tolerance of living animals, such as fish, to hypoxia. Studies on different fish species have shown that hypoxic stress can lead to the accumulation of lactate in the body [16–18], a hallmark feature of anaerobic glycolysis. Therefore, we speculate that the differences in hypoxia tolerance of fish may mainly depend on the differences between their HIF pathways, such as copy number variation and functional divergence of core members of the pathway.

In this study, we first examined the copy number variation of all hif-α genes in common carp. We selected the hif-1αb genes as the main research object. Through gene silencing, hypoxia challenge, transcriptome sequencing, and other methods, we conducted an in-depth analysis of the function of the hif-1αb genes and their functional relationship with other paralogous genes. Our results can help identify genes that determine the hypoxia tolerance of fish, thereby improving the hypoxia tolerance of other commercial fish species.

Methods

Obtainment of target genes and pre-analysis of tissue expression profiles

We downloaded nonredundant protein sequences of common carp, goldfish, zebrafish (Danio rerio), medaka (Oryzias latipes), rainbow trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss), Atlantic salmon (Salmo salar), pig (Sus scrofa), chicken (Gallus gallus) and sea urchin (Strongylocentrotus purpuratus, served as outgroup) from the NCBI FTP site (https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genomes/refseq/, all related files have the suffix “protein.faa.gz”). Correspondence between protein sequence accession number and respective gene ID was obtained from the file “gene2accession” (downloaded from https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/DATA/), based on which we selected the protein sequence of each gene (when variants exist, only the longest was chosen). The selected sequences were used to make a local blast database, against which we conducted blastp searches using protein sequences of three human HIF-α genes (HIF-1α: NP_001230013.1, HIF-2α: XP_011531000.1, HIF-3α: XP_016882621.1) as queries, with the max target sequences set to be 1000 and expect threshold to be 1e-5. Hits with average coverage (of query and hit sequences) above 30% were subjected to multiple sequence alignments by MAFFT (v7.427) [19], using the L-INS-i method. Then, we used RAxML (v8.2.8) [20] to infer phylogenetic trees from alignments, using the GAMMA model of rate heterogeneity, automatically determined substitution model, and 500 bootstraps. We used sea urchin sequences to reroot the best tree, then manually determined sequences that were not orthologs of respective seed sequences and discarded them with the aid of ETE 3 [21]. In this step, distributions of gene neighbors were used as a reference to assist determination, which was obtained from the “gene2accession” file by sorting the starting positions of genes located on the same chromosome. The remaining sequences were subjected to a secondary multiple sequence alignment, from which we inferred the final tree (Fig. S1). The above procedures were also used to obtain homologs of human EPO, VEGFA, LDHA, and GLUT1 in common carp.

To gain a preliminary understanding of tissue expression profiles of common carp hif-1α genes, we screened the NCBI BioProject database and selected one (Accession No.: PRJNA684670) with decent quality and sampled many tissues. Raw RNA-seq data download links were obtained by searching against SRA Run Selector (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Traces/study/) using the above Bioproject accession number as the query. Compressed raw reads were dumped into fastq format by fasterq-dump in SRA Toolkit, which were then subjected to filtration (of bad reads, adapters, etc.) by fastp (v0.23.4) [22] with default parameters. We downloaded reference RNA and genome sequences of common carp (https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genomes/refseq/), which were used to build SAF genome indices for respective species by salmon index [23]. Abundances of transcripts were quantified per tissue by salmon quant. We obtained correspondence between the mRNA accession number and NCBI gene ID for each gene from the file “gene2accession”. For each gene, TPMs (transcripts per million) of all transcript variants were summed up to get the gene TPM.

Design of siRNA and preparation of chitosan nanoparticles

We intended to silence the expression of hif-1αb genes specifically. Therefore, we used SeaView [24] to align and view the nucleotide sequences of the four hif-1α genes. Target sites were selected based on the following criteria: they should be identical within hif-1αb sequences; corresponding regions in hif-1αa sequences should be significantly different; the length and GC content should conform to general principles of siRNA design. Finally, three siRNAs were designed (Table 1), which were synthesized by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. 3’ TT overhangs were added during synthesis.

Chitosan nanoparticles were produced based on modified ionic gelation of tripolyphosphate (TPP) with chitosan as described elsewhere [25]. Chitosan was dissolved at a concentration of 1 mg/mL in 0.5% acetic acid solution. A solution of TPP at the concentration of 1.0 mg/mL was prepared with DEPC water. Then, the TPP solution was added dropwise under constant magnetic stirring to the chitosan solution, at the mass ratio of 3 (CS):1 (TPP). The derived solution was stirred for a further 30 min, then treated with an ultrasonic crusher for two minutes (output power 100 W, three seconds per crush with two seconds intervals), and finally incubated at room temperature for 15 min. The nanoparticles' mean particle diameter (Z-average) and zeta potential were determined using Zetasizer Nano ZS90 (Malvern Instruments, UK).

For the association of siRNA with the CSNPs, siRNA (20 μmol/L) in DPEC water was added to the TPP solution before adding this dropwise to the chitosan solution under constant magnetic stirring at room temperature. The final concentration of siRNA in the solution was 200 pmol per 50 μL.

Experimental procedure

Juvenile common carp of three months were obtained from Shantou aquaculture farm in Guangdong Province, China. They were kept in an independent recirculating aquaculture system and fed twice daily. After one week of acclimation, one hundred and twenty fish with similar body lengths (4.81 ± 0.50 cm) were randomly divided into four groups. For each group, the thirty fish were further divided equally into three tanks filled with 20 L of water. At the beginning of the experiment, each fish was intraperitoneally injected with 50 μL of siRNA-CSNPs or empty CSNPs. During the experiment, dead fish would be removed in time. After 72 h of injection, tissue samples (heart, gills, and brain) were collected from fish in two groups. For the remaining two groups, they were subjected to a hypoxia challenge (with DO concentration maintained at about 2 mg/L) lasting for 24 h. Then, tissue samples were collected from fish in the two hypoxia-challenged groups. Details of each group are shown in Table 2.

Animal euthanasia

The fish were anesthetized on ice for 1–2 min before dissection. When they were out of balance and unconscious, they were transferred into a clean dish and wiped off the solution for dissection. Samples of the heart, brain, and gills were taken, quickly frozen with liquid nitrogen, then stored in a -80 ℃ refrigerator for further use.

Detection of siRNA silencing effect

Total RNA of the heart, gills, and brain tissues was extracted by RNAiso Plus reagent (Takara, Japan), following the manufacturer’s instructions. The concentration and quality of RNA were detected by a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) and 1% agarose gel electrophoresis, respectively. First-strand cDNA of each RNA sample was generated using the PrimeScript™ RT reagent Kit (Takara, Japan). The transcribed cDNA was stored at -20 ℃ for subsequent experiments. To detect the silencing effect on hif-1αb genes, normal PCR primers and qPCR primers were both designed (Table S1). The β-actin gene was used as the internal reference, and the 2−ΔΔct method was used to calculate relative expression [26].

Transcriptomic analysis

Transcriptome sequencing

Total RNA was extracted using the TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. RNA purity and quantification were evaluated using the NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). RNA integrity was assessed using the Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, USA). Then, the libraries were constructed using VAHTS Universal V6 RNA-seq Library Prep Kit according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The libraries were sequenced on an Illumina Novaseq 6000 platform at OE Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China), and 150 bp paired-end reads were generated.

RNA-Seq read processing and mapping

About 50 million raw reads for each sample were generated. Raw reads of fastq format were firstly processed using fastp [22], and the low-quality reads were removed to obtain the clean reads. Then, about 48 million clean reads for each sample were retained for subsequent analyses (Table S2). The clean reads were mapped to the reference genome of common carp (GCF_018340385.1) using HISAT2 [27].

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and functional enrichment

Read counts of genes in each sample were obtained by HTSeq-count [28], which were then used to calculate the FPKM (fragments per kb per million reads) value of each gene by Cufflinks [29]. Differential expression analysis was performed using the DESeq2 [30]. P value < 0.05 and foldchange > 2 or foldchange < 0.5 was set as the threshold for significant DEGs. Gene Ontology (GO) [31] enrichment and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) [32] enrichment analyses were performed on DEGs to determine the biological functions and pathways that differential genes mainly affect. The GO enrichment analysis was divided into biological processes, cellular components, and molecular functions.

Transcriptome data validation

To ensure the reliability of transcriptome data, five genes were selected from DEGs, the expression level of which were detected using the RT-qPCR method, as described above. Primer sequences of respective genes are shown in Table S1.

Results

Copy number variation of hif-1α genes

Through blastp search and phylogenetic inferences, we get a whole landscape of copy number variations of hif-α genes in common carp (Fig. S1). Corresponding to the hif-1αa or hif-1αb gene of zebrafish, common carp has two orthologous copies. In addition, both the hif-2α and hif-3α genes in common carp have twice as many copies as in zebrafish. Apparently, the WGD event experienced by the common ancestor of common carp and goldfish [33] doubled the number of its hif-α genes, and these additional copies have been retained in common carp. Compared with the hif-1αb genes, the two hif-1αa genes had low expression levels in tissues other than the heart (Fig. 1), making us wonder how much of their original functions have been retained. Therefore, we selected the hif-1αb genes as the target for silencing.

Tissue expression profiles of four hif-1α genes. The result was obtained through analysis of RNA-Seq data of the Bioproject PRJNA684670

Injection of CSNPs causes stress in juvenile common carp

We chose CSNPs as the carrier for siRNA delivery because numerous studies have shown that they are safe and biocompatible. We found that intraperitoneal injection of 50 μL of CSNPs resulted in the lighting of the body color of juvenile common carp (Fig. 2) as well as the death of some individuals, suggesting that CSNPs can induce stressful effects on common carp. However, the deaths caused by the injection of CSNPs mainly occurred within the first 24 h, while we sampled at least 72 h post-injection (Table 3). Besides, there was no significant difference in mortality caused by empty CSNPs and siRNA-CSNPs (Table 3). Therefore, the stress effect of CSNPs can be safely ignored for the present study.

siRNA-CSNPs can effectively silence hif-1αb genes

We amplified the hif-1αb genes in three tissues 72 h post CSNPs injection. Semi-quantitative PCR showed that the expression of one hif-1αb gene (LOC109100693) was obviously reduced in all three tissues (Fig. 3A). RT-qPCR results showed that the silencing efficiency of the two hif-1αb genes was above 60% in most cases (Fig. 3B). This efficiency is acceptable for gene silencing in vivo [34].

Silencing of hif-1αb genes changed transcriptome profoundly

RNA-Seq results of three tissues in four groups showed that siRNA-CSNPs (compared with empty CSNPs) caused profound transcriptome changes in both normoxia and hypoxia. Among the three tissues, heart tissue always had the largest number of DEGs, with the total number exceeding 1,000 (Fig. 4A), indicating that the heart is the most important effector site of the hif-1αb genes. Although there were some common DEGs between any two tissues, the proportion of unique DEGs in each tissue almost always exceeded 70% (Fig. 4B and 4C). This suggests the biological functions of the hif-1αb genes vary significantly across tissues or cells. To elucidate the specific biological functions of the hif-1αb genes in different tissues, we enriched the GO terms and KEGG pathways corresponding to DEGs. We found that under normoxic conditions, the functions of down-regulated genes in the heart were mainly related to immunity, according to top GO terms like complement activation (GO: 0006957, 0006958), acute-phase response (GO: 0006953), and complement binding (GO: 0001848) (Fig. 5A). The functions of up-regulated genes were mainly related to cardiac muscle contraction, according to top GO terms like muscle filament sliding (GO: 0030049), regulation of muscle contraction (GO: 0006937), and cardiac muscle contraction (GO: 0060048) (Fig. 5B). Under hypoxic conditions, the situation of up-regulated and down-regulated genes was reversed: the functions of up-regulated genes were mainly related to immunity, while the functions of down-regulated genes were mainly related to cardiac muscle contraction (Fig. 5C and 5D). The results of KEGG enrichment were very consistent with those of GO enrichment. For example, phagosome and p53 signaling pathways are both immune-related pathways, while Motor protein (ID: ccar04814), cardiac muscle contraction (ID: ccar04260), and Adrenergic signaling in cardiomyocytes (ID: ccar04261) are tightly related to cardiac muscle contraction (Fig. 6). Obviously, the hif-1α genes regulated both immune function and cardiac muscle contraction in the heart. A large number of GO terms and KEGG pathways related to immune function were also enriched in the gills (Fig. S3 and S4). In addition, terms such as iron ion homeostasis (GO: 0055072) and heme biosynthetic process (GO: 0006783) and pathways such as Ferroptosis (ID: ccar04216) showed that the hif-1α genes were also involved in iron metabolism and erythropoiesis in gills. In the brain, both up-regulated and down-regulated genes can enrich many immune-related GO terms and KEGG pathways (Fig. S5 and S6), indicating that the function of the hif-1α genes in the brain was more complex.

Silencing of hif-1αb genes changed the expression of their paralogs

As shown in Fig. S1, paralogous genes of the hif-1α genes, such as hif-1αa, hif-2α (epas1), and hif-3α genes, also contained additional copies. The shorter the divergence time between these genes and the hif-1α genes, the greater the functional similarity. If the expression of these genes also changed after the silencing of hif-1α genes, the final biological effect would also be affected. We examined changes in expression levels of these paralogs, as well as well-known HIF-1α target genes. We found that under normoxic conditions, the expression of the two hif-1αa genes was up-regulated in various tissues. In comparison, the expression of the two hif-3α genes was significantly down-regulated (p < 0.05) (Table 4). Under hypoxic conditions, the expression of hif-3α genes was up-regulated, while the expression changes of the hif-1αa genes had no obvious rules. The expression of hif-1αb genes was suppressed under both normoxic and hypoxic conditions (siRNA-CSNPs vs empty CSNPs). Thus, if some downstream genes are regulated solely by the hif-1αb genes, they should be down-regulated under both normoxic and hypoxic conditions. However, most downstream genes were up-regulated under normoxic conditions, suggesting that the hif-1αb genes did not specifically regulate them. Interestingly, the trend of expression changes of a ldha gene (LOC109062325) under normoxia and hypoxia was consistent with that of the hif-3α genes (Table 4), suggesting that the latter may specifically regulate it.

Discussion

Chitosan is increasingly used for safe nucleic acid delivery in gene therapy studies due to well-known properties such as bio-adhesion, low toxicity, biodegradability, and biocompatibility [35, 36]. In aquaculture, CSNPs can also be used as vectors of siRNAs to study gene functions in vivo [25, 37]. In this study, we found that injection of CSNPs caused the death of common carp juveniles, and there was no difference in mortality between the siRNA-CSNPs and empty CSNPs groups (Table 3). This suggests that the underlying cause of fish death is the CSNPs themselves. In addition, the deaths caused by CSNPs mainly occurred in the first 24 h, indicating that intraperitoneal injection of CSNPs is still a strong stress for juvenile carp. Obviously, there is still room for further improvement in the production process of CSNPs to reduce their stressful effects on animals. Oral administration of CSNPs [38, 39] is also an attractive option, but how to ensure the consistency of intake among different individuals is a problem that needs to be solved.

Many fish genes have additional copies compared with their orthologs in mammals since the common ancestor of teleosts underwent a WGD event [40]. The common ancestor of common carp and goldfish experienced a more recent WGD event [33, 41], which resulted in them having more additional gene copies. For most genes, additional copies would be rapidly lost due to purifying selection [42–44]. Even for genes that maintain a multi-copy state, functional divergence frequently occurs between different copies due to neofunctionalization or subfunctionalization [45]. We found that there are multiple copies of all three hif-α genes in carp, and their expression profiles are obviously divergent (Fig. S1 and Fig. 1). For example, the hif-1αa genes were mainly expressed in the heart. In contrast, the hif-1αb genes were expressed at higher levels in multiple tissues. When the hif-1αb genes were silenced or under hypoxic conditions, the expression trends of other hif-α genes varied (Table 4). Furthermore, there are a large number of additional copies of downstream genes of the HIF pathway in common carp, and these genes must have undergone functional divergence to varying degrees. Therefore, it is a great challenge to elucidate the exact functions of different hif-α genes in common carp.

In the present study, we found that with the silencing of the hif-1αb genes, the hif-1αa genes exhibited the most pronounced up-regulation among all the paralogs, suggesting that there is a high degree of functional redundancy between the hif-1αa genes and the hif-1αb genes (Table 4). Although the expression levels of hif-1αa genes are low, their biological activities may be much higher than that of the hif-1αb genes, so the up-regulation of the former would have a super-compensatory effect on gene silencing of the hif-1αb genes. The basis is that after silencing of the hif-1αb genes under normoxic conditions, the expression of genes regulating cardiac muscle contractile function was up-regulated (Fig. 5). Under hypoxic conditions, as the expression of hif-1αa genes was no longer up-regulated, the expression of genes regulating cardiac muscle contractile function was also down-regulated. The hif-3α gene is generally considered to be a negative regulator of the hif-1α and hif-2α genes [46–48]. However, there are also studies reported that the full length of human HIF-3α protein can activate a unique transcriptional program in response to hypoxia [48–50]. Here, we found that under normoxic conditions, with the silencing of the hif-1αb genes, the expression of hif-3α genes also showed a significant down-regulation. In contrast, under hypoxic conditions, their expression was universally up-regulated (Table 4), accompanied by the up-regulation of the expression of immune-related genes (Fig. 5). Besides, the trend of expression change of a ldha gene was almost identical to that of the hif-3α genes, suggesting that it might be specifically regulated by the latter. It seems that, at least in common carp, the hif-3α genes are not purely negative regulators, and their functions need to be further studied.

Conclusions

In this study, we found that common carp has four hif-1α gene copies. Of them, two hif-1αa genes have very low expression levels in most tissues, while two hif-1αb genes exhibit constitutive expression profiles. We used CSNPs to carry siRNAs to silence the hif-1αb genes and found that they regulated immune function. In the heart, they were also closely related to the contractile function of the myocardium. When the hif-1αb genes were inhibited, the expression of some paralogous genes was increased to compensate for their loss of function. The expression of hif-1αa genes was most obviously up-regulated under normoxia, while the expression of hif-3α genes was most obviously up-regulated under hypoxia.

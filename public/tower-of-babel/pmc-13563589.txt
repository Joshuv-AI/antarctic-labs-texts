Contribution of sorghum cooking-related aroma components to the formation of compound aroma of Luzhou-flavor baijiu
Authors: Jinting Liu, Lijuan Gong, Mengying Hu, Peiyao Yuan, Nima Zhaxi, Wei Guo, Shengyuan Xiang, Yong Wen, Kangquan Qiao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe formation of Luzhou flavor of baijiu is closely related to the brewing process, but how the raw materials affect the baijiu flavor through simultaneous distillation of raw and fermented materials. In this study, simultaneous distillation extraction-gas chromatography–mass spectrometry was used to analyze the flavor of four kinds of sorghum cooking and the composition of base baijiu in different processes. It was found that grain cooking not only introduced new flavor substances such as ethyl oleate, ethyl linoleate, α-terpinene, acetone and 4-vinylguaiacol, but also significantly increased the content of seven key flavor compounds such as ethyl valerate, ethyl heptanoate, ethyl acetate, ethyl butyrate, ethyl octanoate, ethyl hexanoate, and ethyl lactate. It is proved that, during simultaneous distillation of raw and fermented materials, grain raw materials directly participate in and contribute to the formation and accumulation of liquor flavor through thermal conversion and co-fermentation.Keywords: Sorghum, Flavor, Aroma activity value, Gas chromatography-mass spectrometry, Luzhou-flavor baijiu

Introduction

Baijiu, a traditional Chinese distilled liquor, is produced through a series of processes including cooking, saccharification, fermentation, distillation, storage, and blending (Fuyao, Junai, & Weijun, 2019; Liu et al., 2023; Yu-xin, Zhen, & Qing-dong, 2015). Sorghum is the fifth most cultivated cereal crop globally and one of the main raw materials for producing Chinese baijiu (Borgonovi et al., 2025). Sorghum grains are rich in starch, protein, fat, and other components, which can be metabolized by microorganisms during fermentation to produce a variety of volatile flavor compounds that play an important role in determining the flavor and quality of baijiu (Fengzhi et al., 2023; Wang, Wu, Xu, & Sun, 2020; Yunfei et al., 2019). Sorghum varieties differ in their chemical composition and content, which may result in the production of different volatile flavor compounds during fermentation, thereby affecting the flavor characteristics of the base baijiu.(Baoguo, Jihong, Mingquan, Jinyuan, & Fuping, 2015; Shuanghai, Xifang, Wenting, & Jihong, 2022). Therefore, it is important to analyze the volatile flavor compounds of different sorghum varieties during cooking and to explore their effects on the flavor of the base baijiu, thereby optimizing production and improving product quality.

The simultaneous distillation of the raw and fermented materials is a crucial step in the production of Nongxiangxing baijiu (Qian et al., 2019). In this method, newly cooked sorghum and fermented grains are co-distilled in a steamer. The high-temperature, high-concentration ethanol vapor facilitates the release and extraction of flavor compounds from the grains, which are then carried into the distillate via azeotropic distillation. This process enhances the flavor components and creates a rich, coordinated aroma, significantly improving the flavor complexity and sensory hierarchy of the resulting base baijiu (Wei-jun et al., 2013).

However, to date, research on the influence of varietal differences in sorghum on cooked-aroma characteristics and their mechanistic linkage to base baijiu flavor during tthe simultaneous distillation of raw and fermented materials process remains scarce. Most existing studies have focused either on the effect of sorghum variety on fermentation-derived flavor compounds or on the thermal transformation of individual grain components under isolated conditions, but these investigations have largely overlooked the critical processing stage of the simultaneous distillation of raw and fermented materials, where cooked grain aroma compounds are directly transferred into the baijiu. Furthermore, no study has comprehensively elucidated the entire chain from “cooking flavor precursors - migration pathways - liquor aromatization” in a systematic manner. Consequently, the mechanistic pathways by which cooked sorghum aroma compounds migrate into and enrich the base baijiu flavor during this key processing step remain poorly understood.

Our team has previously conducted a systematic comparison of the effects of five brewing raw materials-sorghum, rice, glutinous rice, wheat, and corn-on the flavor profile of Luzhou-flavor baijiu (Yuan, Gong, Zhaxi, Qiao, & Deng, 2025). The results demonstrated that sorghum contributed significantly higher levels of key flavor compounds, including esters, phenols, and aldehydes, than the other four materials, establishing it as the core raw material governing the flavor quality of base baijiu. However, sorghum is not a homogeneous commodity; the contents of starch, protein, and tannin in its kernels vary considerably depending on cultivar and geographical origin. Consequently, distinct sorghum varieties may yield substantially different volatile profiles upon cooking, which in turn can affect the efficiency of aroma transfer from grain to liquor during the simultaneous distillation of raw and fermented materials, as well as the ultimate flavor composition of base baijiu.

Based on these considerations, the present study was designed to fill the above-mentioned knowledge gaps by addressing two key questions: (i) how do different sorghum varieties differ in their cooked volatile profiles, and (ii) to what extent and through what mechanisms do cooking-derived aroma compounds from different sorghum varieties contribute to the flavor composition of Luzhou-flavor base baijiu? building upon our previous multigrain comparative work, the present study further focuses on sorghum variety as a critical variable. Four representative sorghum cultivars used for liquor brewing were selected, and the simultaneous distillation of raw and fermented materials was simulated to mirror industrial practice. Through combined volatile flavor analysis and base baijiu flavor profile comparison, this study aims to reveal the differential contributions of cooking-derived flavors from various sorghum varieties to the overall flavor of Luzhou-flavor baijiu.

In this study, four representative sorghum varieties covering the main types of domestic brewing sorghum were selected: Yinuohong 4 (a waxy sorghum from Sichuan, characterized by high amylopectin content and stable alcohol yield), Australian sorghum (an imported variety with low tannin content and good fermentation performance), Shangdang (a traditional northern variety with excellent steaming resistance), and Jinuohong 2(a mechanically cultivated waxy sorghum with good uniformity). By simulating the mixed steaming and simultaneous distillation processes used in actual production, the migration pathways of flavor compounds from grains into the liquor were systematically traced, revealing the influencing mechanism underlying the formation of grain aroma. Unlike previous studies that focused solely on a single raw material or neglected the processing steps, this work compared the flavor profiles of cooked sorghum with those of crude baijiu obtained through different processes. This comparison clarified the substantial contribution of grain raw materials to the flavor composition of baijiu during simultaneous distillation of raw and fermented materials, and elucidated the intrinsic relationship between the cooking-derived flavor of sorghum and the flavor of crude baijiu. The findings provide a scientific basis for selecting suitable sorghum varieties in baijiu production and offer technical support for improving the flavor and quality of baijiu.

Materials and methods

Experimental materials

Four sorghum varieties, namely, G1 (Yinuohong 4), G2 (Australian sorghum), G3 (Shangdang), and G4 (Jinuohong 2), were selected. The sorghum was provided by Sichuan baijiu-making Special Grain Engineering Technology Research Center. The fermented grains of Luzhou-flavor baijiu and the base baijiu of Luzhou-flavor baijiu were all from a factory producing Luzhou-flavor baijiu.

The following substances were obtained from various sources: C7–C30 n-alkane standard (chromatographic grade; Beijing Bailingwei Technology Co., Ltd.), 2-octanol, n-pentyl acetate, 2-ethylbutyric acid, dichloromethane (chromatographic grade; Tianjin Guangfu Fine Chemical Research Institute), dichloromethane (analytical grade), and anhydrous sodium sulfate (analytical grade; Chengdu Cologne Chemical Co., Ltd.).

Detection of flavor compounds in the raw materials after cooking

In order to explore the effect of different sorghum varieties on cooking flavor, four experimental groups were set up in this study. G1 (Yinuohong 4), G2 (Australian sorghum), G3 (Shangdang) and G4 (Jinuohong 2) were selected as raw materials. Under the premise of keeping other grain raw materials unchanged, the cooking experiment was carried out by simulating the production process of Luzhou-flavor liquor, and the cooking flavor under this condition was uniformly named BRM.

Determination methods for sorghum grain composition: Crude protein content: Determined by the automatic Kjeldahl method according to GB 5009.5, National Food Safety Standard for the Determination of Protein in Foods. Crude fat content: Determined by the Soxhlet extraction method according to GB 5009.6, National Food Safety Standard for the Determination of Fat in Foods. Tannin content: Determined according to GB/T 15686, Method for the Determination of Tannin Content in Sorghum. Total starch content: Determined by the acid hydrolysis method according to GB 5009.9, National Food Safety Standard for the Determination of Starch in Foods. Amylose and amylopectin contents: Determined according to GB 7648, Method for the Determination of Amylose in Rice, Maize, and Millet Grains.

Extraction method for cooked grain aroma samples: The method was adapted from that described by Lian Shuncai with modifications (shuncai et al., 2012). The samples were pulverized using a multifunctional grinder and sieved for subsequent use. Briefly, 50 g of the pulverized sample was placed in a round-bottom flask (1 L), to which 500 mL of ultrapure water was added. This mixture was connected to the light-phase end of a simultaneous distillation and extraction (SDE) apparatus. Magnetic stirring and oil-bath heating were applied, with the temperature controlled at 125 ± 5 °C and continuous magnetic stirring to prevent uneven heating of the sample. Meanwhile, 100 mL of dichloromethane (chromatographic grade) was placed in a round-bottom flask (250 mL) with a few boiling chips, and connected to the heavy-phase end of the SDE apparatus. A thermostatic water bath was used to heat this flask, with the temperature controlled at 55 ± 5 °C. The water circulation tubing was connected in a bottom-in, top-out configuration. Once both phases reached boiling and began to reflux, the extraction was timed for 3 h. The collected extract was treated with an appropriate amount of anhydrous sodium sulfate to remove excess moisture, and then stored in a refrigerator at 4 °C overnight for dehydration. The dehydrated extract was filtered to remove excess anhydrous sodium sulfate, and the filtrate was concentrated to dryness using a rotary evaporator at 35 °C under reduced pressure. The dried residue was redissolved in 5 mL of dichloromethane, sealed, and refrigerated for subsequent GC–MS analysis.

GC conditions:The analysis was conducted using an Agilent 8890 gas chromatography system coupled with a 5977B mass selective detector (Agilent Technologies, USA). A DB-WAX CB capillary column (30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm, Agilent, USA) was selected based on the physicochemical properties of the target compounds. High-purity helium (99.999%) was employed as the carrier gas at a constant flow rate of 1.0 mL/min. Sample introduction was performed in splitless mode with an injection volume of 1.0 μL. The oven temperature was programmed as follows: initial hold at 40 °C for 1 min, increase to 150 °C at a rate of 3 °C/min and hold for 4 min, then ramp to 220 °C at 7 °C/min and hold for 2 min, followed by a final increase to 250 °Cat 10 °C/min with a 3 min hold.

MS conditions: An electron impact source with an electron energy of 70 eV was used. The ion source temperature was maintained at 280 °C, and the quadrupole was set to 150 °C. The solvent delay was set at 0 min. Data were acquired in full-scan mode across a mass range of m/z 25 to 550.

Instrument performance and quality control: Prior to GC–MS analysis, instrument performance was verified through automatic tuning with perfluorotributylamine (PFTBA). During each batch of sample analysis, a solvent blank (dichloromethane) and a procedural blank (without sample) were run after every 10 injections to monitor system carryover and background interference. A quality control (QC) sample (mixed standard) was analyzed after every six samples, with the relative standard deviation (RSD) of target compound peak areas below 15%, indicating satisfactory instrument stability.

Qualitative and quantitative analysis: For compound identification, mass spectral matching against the NIST20.0 library was employed, supported by the use of retention indices (RI) for confirmation. Retention indices were calculated based on the retention times of a series of C7–C30 n-alkane standards analyzed under identical GC conditions. Compounds were positively identified when the mass spectral match factor was≥800 and the RI deviation was within ±20 of the literature values. Quantification was performed using a semi-quantitative method based on the peak area ratios of each analyte relative to the internal standards (amyl acetate and 2-ethylbutyric acid). All “contents” reported in the text represent relative contents (peak area ratios) rather than absolute concentration values. All comparisons were conducted under identical matrix conditions, aiming to reflect the relative differences in flavor compounds among different samples.

Detection of base liquor flavor compounds

Distillation simulation experiment: The fermented grains from the winery were collected according to the upper, middle and lower layers, and mixed and sampled by five-point sampling method, and then brought back to the laboratory with sterile bags. In order to distinguish from the simultaneous distillation of raw and fermented materials process of Luzhou-flavor liquor, fresh grain was not added during distillation, and only the sampled fermented grains were distilled. The placement of distiller ‘s grains requires low in the middle and high around, and in the process of steaming, it is necessary to ensure that the air tightness of the steaming system is good. The distilled liquor needs to be headed and tailed, and only the middle fraction is taken as the base baijiu. The base baijiu obtained by simulated distillation without fresh grain was set as the experimental group NAGF, and the base baijiu produced by distillery adding fresh grain to simultaneous distillation of raw and fermented materials process during distillation was set as the control group SDF.

Extraction and analysis of base baijiu flavor: 5 mL of wine sample was placed in a headspace bottle, and 100 μL of L-2-octanol internal standard solution with a concentration of 200 mg/L was added and sealed immediately. The headspace bottle was placed in an automatic solid-phase microextraction device and equilibrated at 60 °C for 10 min. Then the extraction fiber head was inserted and adsorbed at the same temperature for 30 min. After the extraction was completed, the fiber head was inserted into the injection port of the gas chromatography-mass spectrometer and desorbed at 220 °C for 4 min for subsequent analysis.

GC conditions: CP-Wax 57 CB chromatographic column (50 m × 0.25 mm × 0.2 μm) was used. The inlet temperature was 220 °C, and the splitless injection mode was adopted. The carrier gas was high purity helium, and the flow rate was set to 1 mL/min. The temperature programming conditions were as follows: the initial temperature was maintained at 45 °C for 2 min, then increased to 180 °C at a rate of 6 °C/min, and then increased to 220 °C at a rate of 15 °C/min and maintained for 10 min.

MS conditions: The ion source temperature is set to 220 °C, the interface temperature is also 220 °C, the electron bombardment energy is 70 eV, and the mass scanning range is set to 25–550 amu (atomic mass unit).

Instrument performance and quality control: As described in Section 2.2.

Statistical analysis

Data processing was performed using Microsoft Office. GraphPad, Origin 2022, and Photoshop (Ps) were used to plot the graphs. All experiments were performed in independent biological replicates (three replicates per condition).

Results and discussion

Comparative analysis of physicochemical indices and cooking flavor compound profiles among different sorghum varieties

Before conducting flavor analysis, the main physicochemical characteristics of the four sorghum varieties (G1–G4) were first summarized to provide a basis for explaining the observed flavor differences. As shown in Table 1, in terms of grain composition, regarding grain protein content, G1 was the highest at 8.24%. Protein content is a critical factor affecting brewing quality. Appropriate protein levels can provide nitrogen sources, promote microbial growth, and be decomposed into amino acids that are subsequently converted into aromatic substances (Yu-Ping et al., 2020); however, excessively high protein content tends to generate excessive fusel oils, resulting in bitter, pungent, and off-flavors in the liquor body (Ping et al., 2020). In terms of grain fat content, G4 was the highest at 6.99%. Fat content has a dual effect on baijiu brewing quality. Appropriate fat levels, when decomposed by microorganisms and enzymes, can increase free fatty acid content and enrich liquor body flavor; however, under high-temperature cooking conditions, fat oxidation is accelerated, promoting aldehyde formation. Nevertheless, excessively high fat content can intensify fermentation acidity, produce off-flavors, affect liquor clarity, and inhibit microbial activity (Arodi & Ngugi, 2026). The four sorghum varieties showed significant differences in tannin content, with G3 being the highest at 1.72%, and G2 far below the other varieties at 0.97%. Tannin content has a dual effect on baijiu yield and flavor quality. When tannin content ranges from 1% to 1.2%, both liquor yield and premium liquor rate are relatively high; when tannin content is below 1%, both liquor yield and premium liquor rate decrease; when tannin content exceeds 1.3%, liquor yield decreases while premium liquor rate increases substantially. Additionally, under high-temperature conditions, tannin hydrolysis can generate organic acid compounds and serves as an important precursor for ketone substances (Li-li, Xiu-yang, & Nan, 2008). The starch contents of the four sorghum varieties in this study ranged from 72.89% to 78.42%, with relatively small overall differences. As the most abundant component in sorghum grains, starch content is closely related to liquor yield, with a positive correlation between starch content and liquor yield; brewing enterprises generally prefer higher starch content (Lu, Ziu, Zhu, & Zhang, 2010). G1 had the highest starch content at 78.42%. The amylopectin content of sorghum also significantly influences baijiu quality; sorghum varieties with high amylopectin content require lower temperatures and shorter times for starch gelatinization (Zhen et al., 2021), which is crucial for brewing enterprises to save energy and reduce production costs. In summary, the four sorghum varieties exhibited considerable differences in key physicochemical parameters, including protein, fat, tannin, and starch contents. G1 showed the highest protein and starch contents, which are favorable for microbial growth and liquor yield. G4 had the highest fat content, potentially providing more flavor precursors while also posing oxidation risks. G3 possessed the highest tannin content (1.72%), which is conducive to improving the rate of high-quality liquor, whereas G2 had the lowest tannin content (0.97%). These compositional differences provide a critical basis for interpreting the subsequent observed variations in cooked sorghum flavor and base baijiu flavor profiles among the four varieties.

Sorghum steaming aroma components are complex, comprising numerous compounds with significantly different boiling points and polarities. The tested samples contained a wide variety of complex flavor components. To objectively quantify the differences among sample groups and enable inter-treatment comparisons. To meet these requirements, simultaneous distillation and extraction (SDE) combined with gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC–MS) was employed in this study to analyze the volatile compounds of four cooked sorghum varieties. A total of 113 compounds were detected. There were differences in the number of cooking-related flavor compounds among the varieties. Specifically, G1 and G2 had the most flavor substances (n = 70 and 52), while G3 and G4 had fewer such substances (n = 33 and 31) (Fig. 1A). There were also differences in the total content of cooking-related flavor substances among the four sorghum varieties (Fig. 1B), as follows: G2 (302.83 mg/L) > G1 (252.51 mg/L) > G3 (200.86 mg/L) > G4 (138.33 mg/L). Principal component analysis (PCA) of the cooking-related flavor substances of the four sorghum varieties showed that the contribution rates of the first and second principal components were 59.8% and 30.6%, respectively, accounting for 90.4% of total variation (Fig. 1C). There were clear differences in the distribution of each variety in the flavor space. G1 and G2 were distantly and separately located, while G3 and G4 were clustered together. The loading plot (Fig. 1D) further revealed the key compounds responsible for sample separation. This distribution pattern was due to the systematic differences in the composition, overall content, and key compounds among the flavor substances in the different varieties.

First, in terms of types and contents, the differences between varieties of esters, alkenes, aldehydes, and aromatic substances were the most prominent. The types and total amounts of esters (23 species, 104.69 mg/L) and alkenes (5 species) in G1 were significantly higher than those in the other varieties. At the same time, the contents of aldehydes (36.01 mg/L) and phenols (21.15 mg/L) were also higher. These characteristics together constitute the unique flavor profile of G1, accounting for the separateness in the PCA plot. This result is closely related to the higher fat content and moderate tannin content of G1. G2 contained fewer ester species, whereas its total alkane content (196.90 mg/L) was substantially higher than that of the other varieties. In contrast, G3 and G4 had low levels and similar types and total amounts of esters, aldehydes, phenols, and other substances, resulting in a high degree of similarity in their flavor profiles and clustering them close together in the PCA diagram.

In addition, regarding the key marker compounds, several high-content and variety-specific substances further strengthened the above distribution patterns. For example, the content of ethyl palmitate (36.10 mg/L) in G1 was significantly higher than that in the other varieties, and ethyl oleate, ethyl linoleate, and heneicosane were uniquely present or exhibited extremely high levels. These compounds constitute the unique flavor fingerprint of G1. Meanwhile, G2 was characterized by substances such as ethylene glycol palmitate, dodecane, and 2,6,11-trimethyldodecane, which were present at high levels, reinforcing its distinct flavor. In contrast, in the G3 and G4 samples, the detected contents of 18 compounds were relatively similar, and more than half of the substances detected overall were not found in either of these samples. This resulted in overall flavor characteristics being very similar, with no unique substances that could clearly distinguish them. As such, they were positioned close together in the PCA plot and were difficult to separate.

The differences in cooked flavor profiles among the four sorghum varieties are closely associated with variations in the contents of key biochemical precursors in their kernels, including lipids, proteins/amino acids, and tannins. Lipids serve as direct precursors of ester compounds; the high levels of ethyl palmitate, ethyl oleate, and ethyl linoleate in G1 are highly consistent with its fatty acid composition characteristics. Proteins, as important substrates for Strecker degradation and the Maillard reaction, provide the material basis for the abundant aldehyde generation in G1 (36.01 mg/L), which corresponds to its highest protein content (8.24%). Although tannins are precursors of phenolic compounds, G3 exhibited the highest tannin content (1.72%) but generated lower levels of phenolics (6.57 mg/L) than G1 (21.15 mg/L), suggesting that the structural type and thermal stability of tannins play a more critical role in phenolic formation than their total content. Collectively, the differential expression of these precursors through multiple metabolic pathways shapes the distinct cooked flavor characteristics of each sorghum variety.

Analysis of characteristic volatile flavor compounds in cooked grains

Given that different flavor compounds have different sensory thresholds, the Odor Activity Value (OAV) and the Aroma Character Impact Value (ACI) were employed to explore the characteristic flavor compounds in each group (Pino, Tolle, Gök, & Winterhalter, 2012). A total of 21 characteristic flavor substances (OAV ≥1) were screened, which constituted the main components of sorghum cooking flavor, as shown in Table 2. At the same time, the specific contribution of the characteristic flavor of each variety of sorghum can be reflected by the ACI value. The ACI value of these 21 flavor substances was thus calculated, revealing nine substances with ACI % ≥ 1, as shown in Fig. 2. It is considered that these flavor substances may be behind the characteristic volatile flavors of each variety of sorghum.

Collection of flavor compounds of different varieties of sorghum cooking flavor characteristics.

In combination with OAV and ACI values, the aroma characteristics of key flavor compounds in sorghum cooking were comprehensively analyzed. Six kinds of esters with OAV ≥ 1 were detected among the volatile flavor substances of sorghum cooking, all of which belonged to the fatty acid ethyl esters. It was found that high-temperature heat treatment provided sufficient precursors for the formation of fatty acid ethyl esters by promoting lipid hydrolysis and starch degradation, and directly drove the esterification reaction and volatilization, thus making ethyl ester compounds key aromatic substances (OAV ≥ 1) (Kang et al., n.d.; Chen, Wan, Cai, Li, & Fu, 2026; Tie et al., 2025).

Aldehydes are the main flavor components among the volatile flavor substances associated with sorghum cooking. (E,Z)-2,6-Nonenal (OAV = 425–1915) and (E)-2-nonenal (OAV = 264–791), two unsaturated fatty aldehydes, are common to the four sorghums and account for the largest proportion of all sorghums. These two aldehydes present a strong fragrance and fruity aroma. Three key aldehydes, (E)-2-octenal, (E,E)-2,4-decadienal, and (E)-4-nonenal, were only found in G1 and G2 sorghum varieties, as follows: (E)-2-octenal (OAV =1426, ACI % =22%), (E,E)-2,4-decadienal (OAV = 795, ACI % = 12%), and (E)-4-nonenal (OAV = 984, ACI % = 15%) in G1; (E)-2-octenal (OAV = 698, ACI % = 18%) and (E,E)-2,4-decadienal (OAV = 720, ACI % = 18%) and (E)-4-nonenal (OAV = 825, ACI % = 21%) were not detected in G3 and G4, which was also confirmed by the fact that G3 and G4 were located closer together in the PCA plot, while G1 and G2 were further apart. 2-Pentylfuran (OAV = 0–817) is a heterocyclic flavor compound with a flavor that resembles green beans. Among the phenolic compounds, 2,4-di-tert-butylphenol (OAV = 43–334) and 4-vinylguaiacol (OAV = 1–12) represented a phenolic odor and caramel aroma, respectively.

Analysis of flavor substances in different varieties of sorghum base wine

In this study, HS-SPME combined with GC–MS was used to systematically analyze the flavor base baijiu from four different sorghum varieties. As shown in Fig. 3A, 98 flavor substances were identified in the four sorghum base baijiu, of which 56, 66, 69, and 58 were detected in G1–G4, respectively. Fig. 3B shows the flavor structure of the four sorghum base baijiu, revealing specific similarities and differences. Overall, the total content of flavor compounds in the four sorghum base baijiu was as follows: G1 (9551.62 mg/L) > G2 (9451.16 mg/L) > G3 (7130.24 mg/L) > G4 (5525.89 mg/L). In the PCA plot of the base baijiu flavor of the four sorghum varieties, the base baijiu samples were independently distributed and clearly distinguished (Fig. 3C). From the overall flavor profile, the content of total volatile substances in G1 and G2 base baijiu was significantly higher than that in G3 and G4, and the content of esters among the main aroma components was notably high, reaching 9340.61 mg/L and 9303.99 mg/L, respectively. This provided insights into the material basis for the flavor intensity of the two. At the same time, there were consistent differences in the levels of important auxiliary flavor components, such as alcohols, acids, and aldehydes, across samples. For example, the alcohol content was higher in G4, which would affect its palatability. Moreover, G1 had higher levels of acids and aldehydes, which were related to the fullness and aroma potential of the wine body, respectively. These differences together account for the trend of separation of the different base baijiu in the PCA plot.

Stacked bar charts (A) and comparative chord diagrams (B) showing the category contents of flavor compounds in base baijiu produced from different sorghum varieties, principal component analysis plot (C), and UpSet plot (D) of characteristic flavor compounds.

Further analysis of the compositions of the compounds was then performed, as shown in Fig. 3D. The results showed that 38 are common components, while the rest are variety-specific. G1, G2, G3, and G4 were found to contain 5, 7, 15, and 5 unique compounds, respectively. These unique substances constitute the unique basis of the flavor of each base baijiu. A high concentration of ethyl hexanoate was detected in the four base baijiu, which is the core component of typical cellar and koji aromas (Bordiga et al., 2013; Songping, Feixiang, & Yu, 2024; Xuefeng, Yuxin, & Yongguang, 2022), with the following order: G1 (4321.91 mg/L) > G2 (3482.32 mg/L) > G3 (2627.69 mg/L) > G4 (1740.15 mg/L). This gradient directly reflects differences in the efficiency of biosynthesis of core esters across different sorghum raw materials, resulting in more pronounced performance and more typical cellar and koji aromas in G1 and G2, separating them from G3 and G4 in the PCA plot.

The difference in the base baijiu flavor distribution revealed by PCA is a comprehensive reflection of the combined effects of differences in overall flavor intensity, the proportions of characteristic flavor components, and the gradients of key marker content. These results clarified that, even under the same flavor type and processing conditions, different sorghum raw materials can still regulate the synthesis and accumulation of metabolites, ultimately forming distinct flavors.

Contribution of sorghum cooking aroma to the formation of the compound aroma of the base baijiu

To investigate how differences in the cooking aroma of brewing raw materials influence the formation of the compound aroma of the resulting baijiu, we compared the flavor substances of base baijiu produced without fresh grain distillation (NAGF) and with fresh grain distillation (SDF). In terms of the overall flavor composition, the flavor types of SDF based baijiu were similar to those of NAGF based baijiu, but the flavor substances of alkenes, ketones, and phenols contained in SDF were not detected in NAGF. This may have been related to the unique process of simultaneous distillation of raw and fermented materials of baijiu.

To explore the potential correlation between the flavor of brewing raw materials and the flavor of baijiu, the cooking flavor BRM of brewing grain was compared with the flavor substances of NAGF and SDF, as shown in Fig. 4 and Table 3. There were five substances in the four sorghum varieties that were shared by SDF and BRM and not detected in NAGF, namely, ethyl oleate, ethyl linoleate, α-terpinene, acetone, and 4-vinylguaiacol. Among these, ester compounds such as ethyl oleate and ethyl linoleate are mainly formed by microbial metabolic transformation of fatty acids and other precursors in raw materials during fermentation, esterification during distillation and storage, and the unique process of solid-state fermentation. The olefinic compounds represented by α-terpinene are mainly derived from the flavors of the raw materials themselves, introduced into the simultaneous distillation of raw and fermented materials, and synthesized by microorganisms or metabolized from precursors in the raw materials (Minxue et al., 2023). Ketones, such as acetone, were detected in SDF and BRM but not in NAGF, mainly due to the abundant precursor substances (e.g., sugars and amino acids) supplied by the newly added grain raw materials during the simultaneous distillation of raw and fermented materials. Ketones were generated by the Maillard reaction, microbial metabolic transformation, and direct introduction of raw materials. This difference further confirms the key role of grain raw materials in the formation of baijiu flavor compounds (Pang et al., 2020). Among the baijiu flavor substances, phenolic substances such as 4-vinylguaiacol are mainly derived from inherent phenols and their precursors, which are directly introduced by fresh grain raw materials during the simultaneous distillation of raw and fermented materials, and are synthesized by thermal conversion during cooking and by microbial utilization of precursors. However, the NAGF process lacks the above sources and transformation conditions due to the lack of fresh food, resulting in insufficient production or a failure to detect phenolic substances. These substances, which were detected only in SDF and BRM but not in NAGF, are likely to be derived from the direct or transformed introduction of brewing materials into the baijiu body during the simultaneous distillation of raw and fermented materials, confirming the direct contribution of grain raw materials to the specific flavor composition of baijiu.

To further explore the effect of the cooking flavor of brewing materials on baijiu flavor substances, a supervised pattern recognition method was introduced. Specifically, partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) was performed on the flavor of NAGF and SDF-based baijiu. The reliability of the PLS-DA model was validated through 100 permutation tests, with the results shown in Fig. 5B. The p-value was less than 0.01 (0/100), and both Q2 (0.97598) and R2 (0.90809) approached 1 (Fig. 5A), indicating that the constructed PLS-DA model possessed excellent stability and predictive capability. As shown in Fig. 5C, the first principal component explained 71.2% of the variance, and the second principal component explained 26.7%; together, these two components accounted for 97.9% of the total information. The score plot demonstrated that the base baijiu flavors of the four samples were completely separated. As shown in Table 3, seven substances had a variable importance in projection (VIP) value of >1 in the four samples, namely, ethyl valerate, ethyl heptanoate, ethyl acetate, ethyl butyrate, ethyl octanoate, ethyl hexanoate and ethyl lactate. These substances significantly distinguished the flavor characteristics of the two types of base baijiu in the model. The flavor of these seven substances in SDF was richer than that in NAGF, indicating that, although they can also be generated through other metabolic pathways, the cooking flavor of grain significantly enriched their content in base baijiu, thereby strengthening the raw material specificity of flavor composition.

Because they were derived from different sorghum varieties, the G1 to G4 samples showed significant differences in common characteristic substances and their contributions to flavor. As shown in Table 3, Table 4, three components were identified in the G1 sample that were common in SDF and BRM but not in NAGF, namely ethyl oleate, ethyl linoleate, and α-terpinene, which contributed to the oil and citrus flavors of G1. At the same time, there were five substances with VIP values greater than 1 in G1, with two substances associated with an SDF flavor being more abundant than those with an NAGF flavor, namely, ethyl acetate and ethyl butyrate, which together gave the baijiu a prominent fruity aroma and pineapple flavor. In the G2 sample, acetone and α-terpinene were the substances co-existing in SDF and BRM, but were not detected in NAGF, both of which showed floral and citrus aromas. There were five substances with VIP values greater than 1 in this sample, three of which had richer flavors in SDF than in NAGF: ethyl heptanoate, ethyl butyrate, and ethyl octanoate, which conferred a pineapple and sweet odor on the wine body. In the G3 and G4 samples, the substances shared by SDF and BRM and not detected in NAGF were all p-4-vinylguaiacol, which conferred a typical caramel aroma. However, these two samples differed in the composition of key VIP substances: four substances with VIP greater than 1 were detected in G3, all of which had a richer flavor in SDF than in NAGF, namely, ethyl heptanoate, ethyl butyrate, ethyl octanoate, and ethyl hexanoate. Finally, five substances with VIP greater than 1 in G4 had a richer flavor in SDF than in NAGF, namely, ethyl acetate, ethyl hexanoate, and ethyl lactate. These compounds together confer a rich, fruity, pineapple, sweet, and fatty aroma to the baijiu body, reflecting the specific aroma compositions associated with the different varieties.

Conclusions

In this study, the aroma components of four sorghum varieties were analyzed by SDE combined with GC–MS. PCA was performed on the cooking flavor and base baijiu flavor of the four sorghum varieties. The results for the different sorghum varieties showed that they were relatively well separated, with G3 and G4 positioned more closely to each other, while G1 and G2 were further apart. This distribution pattern may reflect the essential differences in the composition of volatile flavor substances in different sorghum varieties. Overall, G1 had an abundance of esters, alkenes, aldehydes, and phenols, suggesting a highly complex flavor. G2 was characterized by a high total alkane content. Meanwhile, the levels of various flavor substances in G3 and G4 were low and similar, resulting in a high degree of similarity in their flavor profiles. Regarding the key compounds, G1's unique possession of ethyl palmitate and ethyl oleate, among others, and G2's characteristic alkanes represent their unique chemical fingerprints, accounting for their separate positions in the PCA plot. Combined with the OAV and ACI values, the characteristic flavor substances of the four sorghum varieties and the types of compounds affecting the flavor content of sorghum cooking flavor and base baijiu in sorghum cooking flavor were further screened. Using the threshold of ACI % ≥1, nine key flavor substances of the four sorghum varieties were preliminarily identified, namely, 2-pentylfuran, ethyl octanoate, (E)-2-octenal, (E,E)-2,4-decadienal, (E)-4-nonenal, (E)-2-nonenal, 2,4-di-tert-butylphenol, (E,Z)-2,6-nonenal, and dodecane. These substances may contribute to the flavor of baijiu or participate in to other flavors through subsequent reactions.

HS-SPME in combination with GC–MS was used to detect the four Luzhou-flavor base baijiu samples. It was found that the flavor structures of the four sorghum base baijiu also showed specific similarities and differences. PCA of the base baijiu flavor of the four sorghum varieties was carried out. The results showed that the different varieties of sorghum base baijiu were relatively independent in the PCA plot, with clear differences in flavor.

The key compounds identified in this study primarily originate from thermally induced chemical reactions during sorghum cooking. Aldehydes are generated through lipid oxidation and thermal degradation of unsaturated fatty acids (via alkoxy radical β-scission) as well as Strecker degradation during Maillard reactions. Heterocyclic compounds, such as 2-pentylfuran, are formed through thermal degradation and cyclization of carbohydrates, while phenolic compounds arise from the thermal degradation of lignin and tannin precursors. During subsequent fermentation and distillation, esters can be derived from the grain or synthesized via microbial esterification, and these esters become enriched during distillation owing to their high thermal stability. In contrast, less stable aldehydes and furans are prone to oxidation and polymerization reactions, resulting in reduced concentrations, whereas phenolic compounds, due to their higher boiling points, remain largely in the distillation residue. Overall, baijiu flavor results from the synergistic interplay between thermal reactions during cooking and microbial metabolism during fermentation. Thermally generated precursors (amino acids, reducing sugars, and fatty acids) provide substrates for microbial metabolism, thereby promoting the biosynthesis of flavor compounds such as higher alcohols, organic acids, and esters. Ultimately, the flavor profile of the base baijiu is collectively shaped by the precursor composition of the raw materials, thermal reaction products, and microbial transformations.

Further exploration of the correlation between the cooking flavor of different sorghum varieties and the flavor of base baijiu showed that the contribution of raw materials to baijiu flavor was mainly reflected in two aspects: enriching the variety and increasing the content. By comparison, five flavor substances shared by SDF and BRM and not detected in NAGF were obtained, namely, ethyl oleate, ethyl linoleate, α-terpinene, acetone, and 4-vinylguaiacol. The above flavor compounds are derived from the wine-making grains brought into the wine body through the simultaneous distillation of raw and fermented materials, enriching the types of baijiu flavor substances. The contents of seven flavor compounds, namely, ethyl valerate, ethyl heptanoate, ethyl acetate, ethyl butyrate, ethyl octanoate, ethyl hexanoate and ethyl lactate, in SDF-based baijiu were much higher than those in BRM-based baijiu. Although the above flavor compounds could be generated in other ways, brewing grain significantly increased their content in the base wine and contributed to the overall flavor.

From the perspective of industrial production, this study provides clear practical guidance for the selection of sorghum varieties based on volatile compound profiles and predicted aroma contributions. It should be noted that, in the absence of sensory evaluation, these recommendations are predictive in nature and require further validation through sensory analysis. Based on the instrumental data, G1 varieties are more suitable for producing baijiu with complex fruity and floral aroma profiles. G2 varieties, by contrast, may be better suited for achieving specific sensory styles. Meanwhile, G3 and G4 varieties are appropriate for production scenarios that require a well-balanced overall base baijiu flavor, where no single aroma characteristic should dominate excessively. Furthermore, the dual mechanisms of “direct compound transfer” and “content enhancement” revealed in this study provide a theoretical basis for enterprises to achieve precise control in raw material procurement, variety breeding, and process optimization. Therefore, the formation of baijiu flavor not only depends on biochemical processes such as microbial metabolism but is also directly affected by the chemical composition of the brewing raw materials and by the migration and transformation of their flavors during the simultaneous distillation of raw and fermented materials. Varieties of raw materials determine the aroma profile and style characteristics of the final baijiu body through two mechanisms: direct introduction and content enhancement, providing an important theoretical basis for controlling baijiu flavor and selecting appropriate raw materials.

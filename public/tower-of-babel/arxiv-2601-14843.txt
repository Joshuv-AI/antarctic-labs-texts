A note on the m m -extended module categories of Nakayama algebras
Endre Sørmo Rundsveen

Abstract
. We study the extended module category m ​ -mod ⁡ Λ m\nmod\Lambda of an algebra Λ \Lambda , recently introduced by Gupta and Zhou. The existence of a postprojective component of m ​ -mod ⁡ Λ m\nmod\Lambda is shown for certain algebras Λ \Lambda , and a knitting algorithm through cohomological dimension vectors is provided. In particular, the extended module category of Nakayama algebras with homogeneous relations are investigated, and it is classified when they are of finite type. The paper give fully calculated AR-quivers for several Nakayama algebras.

Introduction

Background and motivation

In the last decade,
\tau
-tilting
[
1
]

It corresponds nicely with structures which are otherwise of great interest, namely functorially finite torsion classes, (
2

In addition, it fills a gap which had been left vacant by tilting theory. That is, ensuring that an almost complete
\tau

A natural question to ponder in relation to

-tilting is if there are similar construction which correspond to longer silting objects.
Gupta provided a partial answer in

In a following paper, Zhou

In a recent paper, Gupta and Zhou
[

It seems that

might be the proper setting for a generalization of

-tilting.
Moreover, it is close enough to the module category to be reasonably well-behaved,
while still being a big enough part of the derived category to provide insights to its structure.

Another avenue for generalization of

-tilting can be found in the world of
m
-cluster tilting subcategories

of

[

.
Several related proposals have been made

In a paper currently in preparation, the authors of

-rigid pairs in

This inspired a paper by the author and Laertis Vaso

The study in

Another motivation for choosing Nakayama algebras in particular is that their module categories are reasonably well-behaved, and thus might also provide reasonably well-behaved extended module categories.
This paper thus seeks to give a better understanding of the extended module category of Nakayama algebras.

Structure

The paper consists of five sections. Section 2 is spent recalling relevant notions and theory about derived categories and quiver representations.

Notation and conventions

We fix a field

.
All algebras are assumed to be non-semi simple, connected and basic

We denote by

A

The

-linear sums of paths in

.
Note that if
Q
is finite then

has an identity given by the sum of all trivial paths.

A
\mathbf{k}
-linear combination

of paths

is a

if the paths have length at least two and all share the same source and target. Given a set of relations

.

3.4.
A criterion for finiteness

In order to show the validity of
Table

1
, we need some way of determining when the extended module categories are of finite type. A very fundamental property of the extended module categories is that

\operatorname{mod}\Lambda\subseteq 2\nmod\Lambda\subseteq 3\nmod\Lambda\subseteq\cdots\subseteq m\nmod\Lambda\subseteq\cdots

for any algebra
\Lambda
. Thus, if we already know that
m\nmod\Lambda
is of finite type, we also know that
m^{\prime}\nmod\Lambda
is of finite type for
m^{\prime}\leq m
. Likewise, if
n\nmod\Lambda
is of infinite type, then
n^{\prime}\nmod\Lambda
is of infinite type for all
n^{\prime}\geq n
.

In addition, mirroring the classical case, the AR-quiver can be used to conclude on the finiteness of
m\nmod\Lambda
.

Lemma 3.24
.

Let
\Lambda
be a connected
\mathbf{k}
-algebra which is not semi-simple. Assume
C
is a finite and connected component of
\AR(m\nmod\Lambda)
, then
C=\AR(m\nmod\Lambda)
.

Proof.

By
[
36
, Lemma 2.2.6]
it is enough to show that for any
X^{\bullet}\in m\nmod\Lambda
we can find a sequence of non-zero morphisms from a projective to
X^{\bullet}
. Which, as long as
X^{\bullet}
is non-zero, follows from
Lemma

3.17
.

∎

Lemma 3.25
.

Assume
\Lambda
is a connected
\mathbf{k}
-algebra which is not semi-simple and
C
is a connected component of
\AR(m\nmod\Lambda)
which is both postprojective and preinjective, then
C
is finite and
C=\AR(m\nmod\Lambda)
.

Proof.

Every object
X^{\bullet}\in C
lies in the
\tau_{[m]}
-orbit of both a projective and an injective, and since each injective in
C
is postprojective and each projective in
C
is preinjective there are only finitely many objects in each orbit.
Combining this with the fact that there are only finitely many projectives (injectives), we see that
C
is finite. Hence, we are done by
Lemma

3.24
.
∎

Lemma 3.26
.

Let

of the

-extended module categories,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces m\nmod\Lambda}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces m\nmod\Gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\res_{e}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\mathbb{L}}L_{e}}

Note that the restriction functor

is exact and the left adjoint

Also note that by

Recall that the truncation functor

is left adjoint to the inclusion. Hence, we can see that

\begin{split}\Hom_{m\mathchar 45\relax\Lambda}(\tilde{\mathbb{L}}L_{e}(Y^{\bullet}),X^{\bullet})&\cong\Hom_{\Db(\operatorname{mod}\Lambda)}(\mathbb{L}L_{e}(Y^{\bullet}),X^{\bullet})\\

\begin{split}\res_{e}\circ\tilde{\mathbb{L}}(-)&=\res_{e}\circ\sigma^{\geq-(m-1)}\circ L_{e}\circ\projres(-)\\

Having developed an understanding of the extended modules of linear Nakayama algebras with homogeneous relations, we seek now to extend this to the cyclic case.
This will be done through the methods of covering.

Idea of covering

Covering theory has its roots in the world of topology and was introduced as a tool for representation theorists by
[

Classically, the Auslander-Reiten theory of modules is often what one wants to push down.

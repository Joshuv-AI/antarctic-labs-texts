An upper limit on cosmological chiral gravitational wave background
Mohammad Ali Gorji, Ashu Kushwaha, Teruaki Suyama

Abstract
Within the standard framework in which electroweak sphaleron processes relate lepton and baryon number, we derive an upper limit on the amplitude of a chiral gravitational wave background produced prior to the electroweak epoch. This bound is independent of the production time of chiral GWs for superhorizon modes, while it becomes sensitive to the production time for subhorizon modes. For sufficiently high reheating temperatures, the bound becomes significantly more stringent than the conventional big bang nucleosynthesis constraints at frequencies above the MHz scale, thereby providing a powerful and model-independent probe of parity-violating physics in the early Universe.

The idea invokes a mechanism where the enhancement of one circular polarization of the GWs sourced during inflation can lead to the creation of a lepton number asymmetry through gravitational chiral anomaly. The asymmetry in the lepton number density is converted to baryon number density through the Standard Model electroweak sphaleron processes. Thus, this scenario of baryogenesis through leptogenesis may provide an interesting way to constrain the properties of a chiral GW background in the early Universe.

The cosmological gravitational wave background (GWB) is often assumed to be unpolarized, reflecting the absence of significant parity-violating sources in the early Universe

When GWs are generated by parity-symmetric processes, the left- and right-handed polarization modes are produced with equal power,

\langle{|{h}^{L}(\eta,\textbf{k})|}^{2}\rangle=\langle{|{h}^{R}(\eta,\textbf{k})|}^{2}\rangle

\tilde{R}^{\mu\nu}{}_{\rho\sigma}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}R_{\alpha\beta\rho\sigma}

II
Chiral Gravitational Wave Background

We assume that a chiral GWB was present at an initial time
\eta_{i}
,
treated as a free parameter lying between the reheating and the electroweak era.
This implies that the chiral GWB must have been generated at or before
\eta_{i}
(for example, during inflation).
We do not impose any assumptions on its production mechanism.
In our analysis, we evaluate only the baryon number generated after
\eta_{i}
by the chiral GWB.
Any baryon asymmetry that may have been produced by the chiral GWB before
\eta_{i}
or by other baryogenesis mechanisms
is intentionally excluded in order to avoid model-dependent uncertainties.
As a result, the upper limit on the chiral GWB derived later is conservative: including those additional contributions would only strengthen our bound.
Furthermore, the upper limit is independent of
\eta_{i}
for superhorizon modes, but exhibits a dependence on
\eta_{i}
for subhorizon modes.

Chiral GWB can be quantified by the Chern-Pontryagin density
R\tilde{R}
, which can be written as a topological current up to a divergence-free term. Considering transverse-traceless metric tensor perturbations
\delta^{ij}h_{ij}=0=\partial^{i}h_{ij}
in the spatially flat Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) metric
{\rm d}{s}^{2}=a^{2}(\eta)\left[-{{\rm d}}\eta^{2}+\left(\delta_{ij}+h_{ij}\right){\rm d}{x}^{i}{\rm d}{x}^{j}\right]

\displaystyle R\tilde{R}=\frac{1}{a^{4}}\partial_{\eta}\left(a^{3}J^{0}\right)+\partial_{i}J^{i}\,,

(2)

where
J^{0}=\varepsilon_{ijk}({h^{\prime}}^{li}\partial^{k}{h^{\prime}}_{l}{}^{j}+\partial^{2}h_{l}{}^{k}\partial^{j}h^{li})/a^{3}
in which
\varepsilon_{ijk}
is the anti-symmetric symbol with
\varepsilon_{123}=1
. Although the explicit form of
J^{i}
is not needed for the present analysis,
it is given in Appendix
A
for completeness.

To constrain the chiral GWB from the baryon asymmetry, which is created from the leptogenesis mechanism during the radiation domination (RD) era, we focus on the evolution of the GWs during the RD era. We work in Fourier space
h_{ij}(\eta,\mathbf{x})=\sum_{s}\int\tfrac{{\rm d}^{3}{\bf k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}{h}^{s}(\eta,\textbf{k})p^{s}_{ij}(\hat{\textbf{k}})e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}

s=L,R
label the left- and right-handed helicities of GWs,
and
p_{ij}^{s}(\hat{\textbf{k}})
are the usual polarization tensors (see Appendix
A
). Then, the time evolution of the tensor perturbations during the RD era is generically given by

\displaystyle{h}^{s}(\eta,\textbf{k})={h}^{s}_{i}(\textbf{k})\,\mathcal{T}(k\eta),

(3)

where
\mathcal{T}(k\eta)=\sin[k(\eta-\eta_{i})]/(k\eta)+(\eta_{i}/\eta)\cos[k(\eta-\eta_{i})]

{h}^{s}(\eta_{i},{\bf k})={h}^{s}_{i}(\textbf{k})

remains constant as long as the mode is outside of the horizon (

), while it decays as
1/a
after horizon re-entry.
To investigate the physical consequences of the chiral GWB, we assume a

initial condition such that
\langle{|{h}^{L}_{i}(\textbf{k})|}^{2}\rangle\neq\langle{|{h}^{R}_{i}(\textbf{k})|}^{2}\rangle

3

2

(4)

where

at the initial time

\displaystyle\Delta\chi_{i}=\frac{\mathcal{P}_{h,i}^{R}-\mathcal{P}_{h,i}^{L}}{\mathcal{P}_{h,i}^{R}+\mathcal{P}_{h,i}^{L}}~~.

IV
Discussion

A chiral GWB provides a distinctive imprint of parity-violating dynamics in the early Universe through amplitude birefringence. In this work, we studied the cosmological consequences of a chiral GWB. In the presence of a nonzero
\langle R\tilde{R}\rangle
, the gravitational chiral anomaly can generate a net lepton number, which is subsequently reprocessed into the observed baryon asymmetry by Standard Model electroweak sphaleron processes. Requiring consistency between the anomaly-induced contribution and the observed baryon-to-photon ratio from CMB and BBN, we derived a
model-independent
upper bound on the present-day amplitude of a chiral GWB.
The bound exhibits a distinct dependence on the production time
\eta_{i}
: it is independent of the
\eta_{i}
for superhorizon modes, while remaining sensitive to
\eta_{i}
for subhorizon modes. For maximum allowed reheating temperatures, this constraint becomes significantly stronger than the standard BBN bounds at frequencies above the MHz, opening a new observational window onto high-frequency chiral GWs. Our result therefore, provides a complementary and powerful probe of parity-violating physics beyond the Standard Model. Finally, if the observed baryon asymmetry is fully generated by mechanisms unrelated to gravitational chirality prior to the electroweak epoch, the same consistency requirement excludes any appreciable chiral GWB, making our bound maximally constraining.

where the current
K^{\mu}
is not unique and is defined up to the addition of a divergence-free term. One of the most well-known choices for the current is

\displaystyle K^{\mu}=\epsilon^{\mu\eta\alpha\beta}\left(\Gamma^{\delta}_{\eta\lambda}\partial_{\beta}\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\alpha\delta}+\frac{2}{3}\Gamma^{\delta}_{\eta\sigma}\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\beta\lambda}\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\alpha\delta}\right)\,,

(19)

where
\Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}
is the Christoffel connection.

Considering transverse-traceless metric tensor perturbations
\delta^{ij}h_{ij}=0=\partial^{i}h_{ij}
in the spatially flat FLRW background

\displaystyle{\rm d}{s}^{2}=-{{\rm d}}t^{2}+a^{2}(t)\left(\delta_{ij}+h_{ij}\right){\rm d}{x}^{i}{\rm d}{x}^{j}\,,

(20)

where
t
denotes the cosmic time,
up to quadratic order in perturbations, we find

\displaystyle R{\tilde{R}}=\frac{2}{a}\,\varepsilon_{bcd}\left[\big(\ddot{h}^{ab}+H\,\dot{h}^{ab}\big)\,\partial^{d}\dot{h}_{a}{}^{c}\;+\;\frac{1}{a^{2}}\big(\partial_{a}\dot{h}_{e}{}^{d}-\partial_{e}\dot{h}_{a}{}^{d}\big)\,\partial^{e}\partial^{c}h^{ab}\right]\,,

(21)

where a dot denotes
{\rm d}/{\rm d}{t}
and
H=\dot{a}/a
is the Hubble parameter. Here
\varepsilon_{ijk}
is the anti-symmetric symbol with
\varepsilon_{123}=1
.

Substituting the metric Eq. (
20
) in Eq. (
19
) and expanding up to the second order in perturbations, we find

\displaystyle K^{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2a}\,\varepsilon_{bcd}\left[\dot{h}^{ab}\,\partial^{d}\dot{h}_{a}{}^{c}\;+\;\frac{1}{a^{2}}\partial_{e}h^{ab}\partial^{d}\left(\partial_{a}h^{e}{}^{c}-\partial^{e}h_{a}{}^{c}\right)\right]\,,

(22)

\displaystyle K^{i}

\displaystyle=-\;\frac{1}{2a}\,\varepsilon^{i}{}_{bc}\,\left[\dot{h}^{ab}\ddot{h}_{a}{}^{c}\;+\;\frac{1}{a^{2}}\partial_{d}h^{ab}\big(\partial_{a}\dot{h}^{d}{}^{c}-\partial^{d}\dot{h}_{a}{}^{c}\big)\right]\,.

(23)

Since the Chern-Pontryagin density (
17
) is a total derivative, in the FLRW case it can be written as

\displaystyle\tilde{R}R=\frac{1}{a^{3}}\partial_{t}\left(a^{3}J^{0}\right)+\partial_{i}J^{i}\,.

(24)

A natural choice for
J^{0}
and
J^{i}
is
J^{0}=2K^{0}
and
J^{i}=2K^{i}
, which is commonly adopted in the cosmology literature. However, this choice is not unique. For example, one can show that

\displaystyle J^{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a}\,\varepsilon_{bcd}\left(\dot{h}^{ab}\,\partial^{d}\dot{h}_{a}{}^{c}\;+\;\frac{1}{a^{2}}\partial^{c}h^{ab}\,\partial^{2}h_{a}{}^{d}\right)\,,

(25)

\displaystyle J^{i}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{a}\,\varepsilon_{bc}{}^{i}\,\dot{h}^{ab}\Big(\ddot{h}_{a}{}^{c}-\frac{1}{a^{2}}\,\partial^{2}h_{a}{}^{c}\Big)+\frac{1}{a^{3}}\varepsilon_{bcd}\left[\partial^{i}\!\big(\dot{h}^{ab}\,\partial^{c}h_{a}{}^{d}\big)-2\partial^{a}\dot{h}^{ib}\,\partial^{c}h_{a}{}^{d}\right]\,,

(26)

also satisfies (
24
) and provides an alternative choice of current.

Comparing the above result with (
22
) and (
23
), we see that the temporal component
J^{0}
is simpler in (
25
), while the spatial component
J^{i}
is more involved. Since in this paper we only make use of
J^{0}
, we work with (
25
) and (
26
), which are more convenient for practical computations in cosmology.

A.2
Computational details for
\langle R\tilde{R}\rangle

Working with conformal time
{\rm d}\eta={\rm d}{t}/a(t)
, Eq. (
21
) takes a simpler form

\displaystyle R\tilde{R}=\frac{2}{a^{4}}\varepsilon_{ijk}\left[{h^{\prime\prime}}^{li}\partial^{k}{h^{\prime}}^{j}_{l}+\left(\partial_{l}{h^{\prime}}^{k}_{m}-\partial_{m}{h^{\prime}}^{k}_{l}\right)\partial^{m}\partial^{j}h^{li}\right]\,,

(27)

where a prime denotes
{\rm d}/{\rm d}\eta
and Eq. (
24
) takes the form

\displaystyle R\tilde{R}=\frac{1}{a^{4}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\left(a^{3}J^{0}\right)+\partial_{i}J^{i}\,\,.

(28)

Let us consider the Fourier space decomposition of
h_{ij}(\eta,\textbf{x})
in terms of the helicities as

\displaystyle h_{ij}(\eta,\mathbf{x})=\sum_{s=L,R}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}\textbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}{h}^{s}(\eta,\textbf{k})\,p^{s}_{ij}(\hat{\textbf{k}})e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\,.

(29)

where we note that the Fourier amplitudes
{h}^{s}(\eta,\textbf{k})
are complex quantities. The two polarization tensors are real,
p_{ij}^{s}(-\hat{\textbf{k}})=p_{ij}^{s}(\hat{\textbf{k}})
and the condition for
h_{ij}(\eta,\textbf{k})
to be real can be given as
{h}^{*}_{ij}(\eta,\textbf{k})={h}_{ij}(\eta,-\textbf{k})
. The two polarization tensors depend on the unit vector
\hat{\textbf{k}}
and satisfy the following properties
p^{s}_{ij}=p^{s}_{ji}
(symmetric),
\hat{k}_{i}p^{s}_{ij}=0
(transverse), and
p^{s}_{ii}=0
(traceless). The polarization tensors satisfy the following relations

\displaystyle p^{R}_{ij}(\hat{\textbf{k}})p^{R}_{ij}(\hat{\textbf{k}})

\displaystyle=0=p^{L}_{ij}(\hat{\textbf{k}})p^{L}_{ij}(\hat{\textbf{k}}),

(30)

\displaystyle p^{R}_{ij}(\hat{\textbf{k}})p^{L}_{ij}(\hat{\textbf{k}})

\displaystyle=2,

(31)

\displaystyle k_{p}\varepsilon^{mpj}p^{s}_{ij}(\hat{\textbf{k}})

\displaystyle=-i\lambda_{k}^{s}\,k\,{p^{m}_{i}}^{s}(\hat{\textbf{k}}),

(32)

where
s=R,L
,
\lambda_{k}^{R}=+1
, and
\lambda_{k}^{L}=-1
. The helicity relations of the polarization tensors for the circularly polarized GWs (left-handed and right-handed) can be defined in terms of the linearly polarized polarization tensors as

\displaystyle p^{R}_{ij}=\frac{p^{+}_{ij}+i\,p^{\times}_{ij}}{\sqrt{2}}\,,\quad p^{L}_{ij}=\frac{p^{+}_{ij}-i\,p^{\times}_{ij}}{\sqrt{2}}~~.

(33)

The volume average or ensemble average of Eq. (
28
) is given by

\displaystyle\langle R\tilde{R}\rangle=\Big\langle\frac{1}{a^{4}}\partial_{\eta}(a^{3}J^{0})\Big\rangle+\langle\partial_{i}J^{i}\rangle=\frac{1}{a^{4}}\Big\langle\partial_{\eta}(a^{3}J^{0})\Big\rangle\,,

(34)

where in the last equality we used the fact that in homogeneous and isotropic FLRW universe,
\langle\partial_{i}J^{i}\rangle=0
. Furthermore, since
\langle\cdots\rangle
is an ensemble average which is related to a volume average, we can take
\partial_{\eta}
outside the averaging

\displaystyle\langle R\tilde{R}\rangle=\frac{1}{a^{4}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\Big\langle\varepsilon_{ijk}\left[{h^{\prime}}^{li}\partial^{k}{h^{\prime}}^{j}_{l}+\partial^{2}h^{k}_{l}\partial^{j}h^{li}\right]\Big\rangle=\frac{1}{a^{4}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\left[\Big\langle\varepsilon_{ijk}{h^{\prime}}^{li}\partial^{k}{h^{\prime}}^{j}_{l}\Big\rangle+\Big\langle\varepsilon_{ijk}\partial^{2}h^{k}_{l}\partial^{j}h^{li}\Big\rangle\right].

(35)

In Fourier space, we can compute both terms on r.h.s. in the above equation as

\displaystyle\Big\langle\varepsilon_{ijm}{h^{\prime}}^{li}\partial^{m}{h^{\prime}}^{j}_{l}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}\textbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}~\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}\textbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}~\sum_{r=L,R}\sum_{s=L,R}\,\varepsilon_{ijm}(iq^{m})\,{h}^{r\,\prime}(\eta,{\bf k})\,{h}^{s\,\prime}(\eta,{\bf q})\,{p^{li}}^{r}(\hat{\textbf{k}}){p^{j}_{l}}^{s}(\hat{\textbf{q}})e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}+i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}\textbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}~\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}\textbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}~\sum_{r=L,R}\sum_{s=L,R}\,\varepsilon_{ijm}(iq^{m})\,{h}^{r\,\prime}(\eta,{\bf k})\,{h}^{s\,\prime}(\eta,{\bf q})\,{p^{li}}^{r}(\hat{\textbf{k}}){p^{j}_{l}}^{s}(\hat{\textbf{q}})(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}\textbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}~\sum_{r=L,R}\sum_{s=L,R}\,\varepsilon_{ijm}(-ik^{m})\,{h}^{r\,\prime}(\eta,{\bf k})\,{h}^{s\,\prime}(\eta,{\bf-k})\,{p^{li}}^{r}(\hat{\textbf{k}}){p^{j}_{l}}^{s}(\hat{-\textbf{k}})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}\textbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}~\sum_{r=L,R}\sum_{s=L,R}\,(-i^{2}\lambda^{s}k)\,{h}^{r\,\prime}(\eta,{\bf k})\,{h}^{s\,\prime}(\eta,{\bf-k})\,{p^{li}}^{r}(\hat{\textbf{k}})p_{il}^{s}(\hat{-\textbf{k}})

\displaystyle=2\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}\textbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,k\,\left[{h}^{L\,\prime}(\eta,{\bf k})\,{h}^{R\,\prime}(\eta,{\bf-k})-{h}^{R\,\prime}(\eta,{\bf k})\,{h}^{L\,\prime}(\eta,{\bf-k})\right]

\displaystyle=2\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}\textbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}k\,\left[{h}^{L\,\prime}(\eta,{\bf k})\,{{h}^{L\,\prime}(\eta,{\bf k})}^{*}-{h}^{R\,\prime}(\eta,{\bf k})\,{{h}^{R\,\prime}(\eta,{\bf k})}^{*}\right]

\displaystyle=2\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}\textbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}~k\,\left[\left|{h}^{L\,\prime}(\eta,{\bf k})\right|^{2}-\left|{h}^{R\,\prime}(\eta,{\bf k})\right|^{2}\right],

(36)

where in the second line we used the relation for the volume average
\langle e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}+i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\rangle=\int{\rm d}^{3}\textbf{x}\,e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}+i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}}=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\textbf{k}+\textbf{q})
, and we have also used
{{h}^{R}(\eta,{\bf k})}^{*}={h}^{L}(\eta,{\bf-k})
and
{{h}^{L}(\eta,{\bf k})}^{*}={h}^{R}(\eta,{\bf-k})
.
Similarly, we obtain the second term as

\displaystyle\Big\langle\varepsilon_{ijm}\partial^{2}h^{m}_{l}\partial^{j}h^{li}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}\textbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}~\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}\textbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}~\sum_{r=L,R}\sum_{s=L,R}\,\varepsilon_{ijm}(-ik^{2}q^{j})\,{h}^{r}(\eta,{\bf k})\,{h}^{s}(\eta,{\bf q})\,{p^{m}_{l}}^{r}(\hat{\textbf{k}}){p^{li}}^{s}(\hat{\textbf{q}})e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}+i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=2\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}\textbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}~k^{3}\,\left[\left|{h}^{R}(\eta,{\bf k})\right|^{2}-\left|{h}^{L}(\eta,{\bf k})\right|^{2}\right]~~.

(37)

Therefore, using these two results give

\displaystyle\langle R\tilde{R}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{a^{4}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\left(\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}\textbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}~k\,\left[k^{2}\left(\left|{h}^{R}(\eta,{\bf k})\right|^{2}-\left|{h}^{L}(\eta,{\bf k})\right|^{2}\right)-\left(\left|{h}^{R\,\prime}(\eta,{\bf k})\right|^{2}-\left|{h}^{L\,\prime}(\eta,{\bf k})\right|^{2}\right)\right]\right)~.

(38)

The evolution of GWs in the RD era is given by
Maggiore 2018

\displaystyle h^{s}(\eta,\textbf{k})=A(k)\frac{\sin{(k\eta)}}{k\eta}+B(k)\frac{\cos{(k\eta)}}{k\eta}\,,

(39)

where
A(k)
and
B(k)
are arbitrary constants and can be fixed by the initial conditions. Note that in the superhorizon limit
k\eta\ll 1
, the solution
\sin{(k\eta)}/(k\eta)
refers to the constant mode and
\cos{(k\eta)}/(k\eta)
(goes as
1/\eta
) corresponds to the decaying mode. Hence, often the term
B(k)\cos{(k\eta)}/(k\eta)
in Eq. (
39
) is ignored while considering the evolution of GWs. But, this mode is important in our case and we work with the full solution. We choose the initial conditions as
h^{s}(\eta_{i},\textbf{k})=h^{s}_{i}(\textbf{k})
and
{h^{s\,\prime}}(\eta_{i},\textbf{k})=0
, which gives

\displaystyle h^{s}(\eta,\textbf{k})=h^{s}_{i}(\textbf{k})\mathcal{T}(k\eta)\quad\text{with}\quad\mathcal{T}(k\eta)=\frac{\sin{[k(\eta-\eta_{i})]}+k\eta_{i}\cos{[k(\eta-\eta_{i})]}}{k\eta}\,,

(40)

where
\mathcal{T}(k\eta)
is the transfer function. Using Eq. (
40
) in Eq. (
38
), we obtain

\displaystyle\langle R\tilde{R}\rangle=\frac{2}{a^{4}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\left(\int\frac{{\rm d}{k}}{2\pi^{2}}~k^{5}\mathcal{K}(k\eta)\,\left[|{h}_{i}^{R}({\bf k})|^{2}-|{h}_{i}^{L}({\bf k})|^{2}\right]\right)\,,

(41)

where we used
{h^{s\,\prime}}(\eta,\textbf{k})=h^{s}_{i}(\textbf{k})\,\mathcal{T}^{\prime}(k\eta)
and the kernel is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(k\eta)\equiv\mathcal{T}^{2}(k\eta)-\frac{1}{k^{2}}{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}^{2}(k\eta)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{k\eta}\right)^{4}\,\Bigg[~(k\eta)^{2}\Big\{k\eta_{i}\cos{[k(\eta-\eta_{i})]}+\sin{[k(\eta-\eta_{i})]}\Big\}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-\Big\{k(\eta-\eta_{i})\cos{[k(\eta-\eta_{i})]}-(1+k^{2}\eta\eta_{i})\sin{[k(\eta-\eta_{i})]}\Big\}^{2}~\Bigg]~~.

(42)

Spinning extremal dyonic black holes in γ = 1 \gamma=1 Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton theory
Jose Luis Blázquez-Salcedo, Carlos Herdeiro, Eugen Radu, Etevaldo dos Santos Costa Filho, Kunihito Uzawa

Abstract
We propose a general framework for the study of asymptotically flat spinning dyonic extremal black holes (eBHs) in D = 4 D=4 Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton theory. Restricting to the stringy value γ = 1 \gamma=1 of the dilaton coupling constant, we report on the existence of a one parameter family of eBHs which are free of pathologies, provided their magnetic and electric charges are equal. An understanding of this condition is found from a study of the near horizon limit of the solutions, both perturbative closed form and numerical solutions being presented.

4
Conclusions

We have considered charged rotating dyonic BHs in four dimensional Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton theory.
Restricting to extremal configurations
and a stringy value of the dilaton coupling
constant
\gamma=1
, we
have found that the only solutions which are regular on and outside the horizon
are those with equal (absolute) values for the electric and magnetic charges.
A partial understanding of this feature
is achieved when studying the decoupled near-horizon limit of the bulk solutions.

Our approach has been numerical.
In particular, we have introduced a suitable metric parametrization and a numerical scheme,
well suited for
the numerical construction of eBHs.
As for near horizon solutions, we have used both analytical and numerical
methods, a closed form perturbative solution being reported.
For both eBHs and near horizon solutions,
the approach in this work can easily be adapted to other models.

In supersymmetric theories, eBHs are often supersymmetric.
The spinning solutions
of the model (
2
) with
\gamma=1
cannot be consistently embedded in
D=4
,
\mathcal{N}=4
supergravity, since rotation turns on an additional axionic term.
At the same time, for the known generalizations
of the extremal KN BH, the magnetic and electric charges are independent parameters.
It would be interesting to see if the condition (
1
)
can also be circumvented
for extensions of the model (
2
)
with other matter fields (
e.g.
an axion
[
29
,
12
,
13
]
)
or in the presence of a dilaton potential
[
1
,
4
]
.

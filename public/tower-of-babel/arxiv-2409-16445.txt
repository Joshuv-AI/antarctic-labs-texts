Abstract
. We show that if there exists a countable Borel equivalence relation which is hyper-hyperfinite but not hyperfinite then the complexity of hyperfinite countable Borel equivalence relations is as high as possible, namely, 𝚺 2 1 \mathbf{\Sigma}^{1}_{2} -complete.



The theory of countable Borel equivalence relations (CBERs) is an active field of research, which became one of the most prominent directions in descriptive set theory, for a comprehensive survey see
[
Kec24
]
. Recall that a CBER is
hyperfinite
if it can be expressed as an increasing union of CBERs with finite classes. Probably the most investigated general goal of this area concerns the understanding of hyperfinite CBERs. Even though, the notion of “understanding” is not a well-defined one, there are several conjectured statements that would provide significant insight. One such formal statement is the following:

Does the collection of hyperfinite CBERs form a
\mathbf{\Pi}^{1}_{1}
set (see Section
1
)?

A seemingly unrelated major open problem in the theory of CBERs is whether hyper-hyperfinite equivalence relations are hyperfinite:

Assume that
F_{0}\subseteq F_{1}\subseteq\dots
is a sequence of hyperfinite CBERs and
E=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}F_{i}
. Is
E
hyperfinite?

While these two statements are sometimes considered “equally hard”, so far, no formal implication has been shown to hold between them. We give an easy proof the following, somewhat surprising implication.

Theorem 0.1
.

If there exists a CBER
E
which is hyper-hyperfinite but not hyperfinite then the hyperfinite equivalence relations form a
\mathbf{\Sigma}^{1}_{2}
-complete set.

Roughly speaking, a subset of the reals is
\mathbf{\Sigma}^{1}_{2}
-complete if it can be defined using an existential and a universal quantifier over the reals, but among such sets it has the maximal complexity. In particular, such a complexity result excludes most of the natural ways of characterizing hyperfinite CBERs. Thus, already a negative answer to the increasing union conjecture would imply that understanding CBERs is in some sense an intractable problem.

2.
The proof

We will identify infinite subsets of
\mathbb{N}
with their increasing enumeration,
[\mathbb{N}]{N}
stands for the collection of all those. If
x,y\in[\mathbb{N}]^{\mathbb{N}}
let us use the notation
y\leq^{\infty}x
in the case the set
\{n:y(n)\leq x(n)\}
is infinite and
y\leq^{*}x
if it is co-finite. Set

\mathcal{D}=\{(x,y):y\leq^{\infty}x\}.

We will prove a theorem more general than the following one in Section
3
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that there exists a non-hyperfinite CBER
E
on
[\mathbb{N}]{N}

such that
({=}_{[\mathbb{N}]{N}}\times E)\upharpoonright\mathcal{D}
is hyperfinite.
Then the hyperfinite CBERs form a
\mathbf{\Sigma}^{1}_{2}
-complete set.

Corollary 2.2
.

Assume that there exists a non-hyperfinite CBER
E

and a Borel map
\Phi:E\to[\mathbb{N}]{N}
such that
the subgraph of
{=}_{[\mathbb{N}]{N}}\times E

consisting of edges
((x,y),(x,y^{\prime}))
with
\Phi(y,y^{\prime})\leq^{\infty}x

is hyperfinite.
Then the hyperfinite CBERs form a
\mathbf{\Sigma}^{1}_{2}
-complete set.

Proof.

Denote by
Y
the underlying space of
E
.
Define first a Borel injection
\Psi:Y\to[\mathbb{N}]{N}
so that

\Psi(y)\geq^{*}\Phi(e)
for every pair
e
from the
E
-class of
y
:
this is easy to do, since as
E
is a CBER,
for each
y
there are only countably many such pairs
which can be enumerated in a Borel way using the Luzin-Novikov theorem;
moreover, one can use a standard encoding to ensure that
\Psi
is injective.
Now, let
E^{\prime}
be the pushforward of
E
by
\Psi
,
i.e. let
E^{\prime}
be
{=}_{[\mathbb{N}]{N}}\cup(\Psi\times\Psi)(E)
.
Note that
E^{\prime}
is a CBER;
it is Borel since
\Psi
is injective.

We check that Theorem
2.1
can be used with
E^{\prime}
.
By the injectivity of
\Psi
,

E^{\prime}\upharpoonright ran(\Psi)
is isomorphic to
E
,
hence in particular it is not hyperfinite.
It remains to show that

(=_{[\mathbb{N}]{N}}\times E^{\prime})\upharpoonright\mathcal{D}

is hyperfinite.
By pulling back with
\Psi
,
this is equivalent to showing that the restriction of
{=}_{[\mathbb{N}]{N}}\times E

to the set
\{(x,y):\Psi(y)\leq^{\infty}x\}
is hyperfinite.
For every edge
((x,y),(x,y^{\prime}))
in this restriction,
we have
(y,y^{\prime})\in E
so
\Phi(y,y^{\prime})\leq^{*}\Psi(y)
,
but we also have
\Psi(y)\leq^{\infty}x
,
and thus
\Phi(y,y^{\prime})\leq^{\infty}x
.
So this restriction is contained a hyperfinite graph,
and hence hyperfinite.
∎

is applicable and it yields a Borel set
C\subseteq\mathbb{N}{N}\times\mathbb{N}{N}\times[\mathbb{N}]{N}
so that
\{s:C_{s}\in\mathcal{U}^{\Psi}\}
is
\mathbf{\Sigma}^{1}_{2}
-hard. This implies the desired conclusion by (
3
) of the Lemma above and Proposition
1.4
.
∎

4.
Further problems

There are several further open problems in the theory of CBERs, which are sometimes considered “equally hard” (see
[
Kec24
, Section 17.4]
). It would be interesting to understand their relationship to each other and complexity. For instance, our argument seems to be very far from being able to achieve the following.

Problem 4.1
.

Assume that there is a CBER that is measure-hyperfinite, but not hyperfinite. Do the hyperfinite CBERs form a
\mathbf{\Sigma}^{1}_{2}
-complete set?

A possible way of summarizing Theorem
2.1
that if one manages to come up with a CBER on
[\mathbb{N}]{N}
that is not hyperfinite, but hyperfinite on every non-dominating set (uniformly), then the complexity result follows. A natural generalization is the following.

Problem 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal of Borel sets on
\mathbb{N}{N}
. Is there a non-hyperfinite CBER
E
such that for each
B\in\mathcal{I}
the restriction
E\upharpoonright B
is hyperfinite? What if
\mathcal{I}
is the ideal generated by compact sets?

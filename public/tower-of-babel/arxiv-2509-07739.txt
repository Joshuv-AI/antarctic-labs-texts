Abstract
. We explicitly describe the structure of HNN extensions of Lie superalgebras. We specify their bases. Moreover, we prove that the HNN extension is a direct sum of two subalgebras: original Lie superalgebra, and the free Lie superalgebra, which free generators are explicitly described. We apply this result to study finite generation of an ideal in a finitely presented Lie superalgebra. As an important tool, we develop Gröbner-Shirshov basis theory for Lie superalgebras by establishing a normal form theorem in terms of admissible bracketings.

Introduction

Lie superalgebras play important role in modern mathematics
[
7
,
17
]
.
Free Lie superalgebras are defined in a standard way
[
2
]
.
Any subalgebra of a free Lie algebra is again free (Shirshov-Witt, see e.g.
[
1
]
).
Similarly, A.A. Mikhalev proved that any homogeneous subalgebra in the free Lie superalgebra is again free,
the characteristic of the field being different from 2,3

[

Closed dimension formulas for homogeneous components of free Lie superalgebras of finite rank were obtained by Petrogradsky

The growth of free solvable Lie superalgebras of finite rank was specified by Klementyev and Petrogradsky
[

For free Lie algebras, free restricted Lie algebras and free Lie superalgebras a version of Schreier’s formula holds in terms of formal power series
[
16
]
.
We are interested in Lie algebras and superalgebras defined in terms of generators and relations.
When both defining generators and relations are finite sets we have a finitely presented Lie algebra or superalgebra.
In the case of graded Lie algebras finite presentability has homological description using the first and second homology groups, see the Weigel paper
[
21
]
.

In combinatorial group theory the following HNN construction plays important role,
[
11
]
.
Let
C
be a group containing two isomorphic subgroups
\phi:A\to B
.
The
HNN-extension
is the universal group generated by

and one more
element

,

H:=\langle C,t\mid t^{-1}at=\phi(a),a\in A\rangle.

The definition of HNN extension of ordinary Lie algebras was given by Wasserman in
[
20
]
,
and the case of restricted Lie algebras was dealt with by Lichtman and Shirvani
[
10
]

They prove that the original algebra is embedded in the HNN extension and under some conditions the HNN extension contains a non-trivial free (restricted) Lie subalgebra.
The HNN extension of Lie superalgebras was defined and embedding result was proved
by Ladra, Páez-Guillán, Zargeh in
[
8

An embedding theorem of HNN extensions was also established in case of Leibnitz algebras in
[

]

Let

be a Lie superalgebra with a proper subalgebra
A
and
d:A\to L
a superderivation of degree
|d|\in\{0,1\}
.
One defines the HNN extensions by presentation (see details below):

H:=\langle L,t\ |\ [t,a]=d(a),a\in A\rangle.

As the first main result we prove the following structure theorem for HNN extensions of Lie superalgebras.

Theorem A.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic different from 2 and 3.
Let
X=\{x_{i}\mid i\in\Xi\}
form a linear basis of the Lie superalgebra
L
modulo its proper subalgebra
A
, and
d:A\to L
a superderivation.
Then the respective HNN extension of

is a direct sum of two subalgebras

H\cong L\oplus F(W),

where
F(W)
is the free Lie superalgebra with the free generating set

W:=\{[tx_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots\,x_{i_{s}}]\mid x_{i_{j}}\in X,\ i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq\cdots\leq i_{s},\ s\geq 0\},

where odd

appear at most once.

Thus, we essentially generalize result of Wasserman in case of Lie algebras
[
20
, Th. 5.1 and Cor. 5.3]
,
because unlike that paper and
[
8
]
we explicitly describe structure of any HNN-extension in generality of Lie superalgebras.
Similar structure results were also proved in case of restricted Lie algebras by Lichtman and Shirvany
[
10
, Proposition 1]
. That approach was based on theory of free products of associative algebras and restricted Lie algebras.
Our results are analogues of similar classical results on HNN extensions in group theory
[
5
,
11
]
.

We develop the theory of HNN extensions of Lie superalgebras started in
[
8
,
10
]
.
To prove our main Theorem A, we first prove a normal form theorem (Theorem
4.1
)
and find an explicit basis for the HNN extensions of Lie superalgebras (Theorem

).
Our approach is based on Gröbner-Shirshov basis theory, which in case of Lie superalgebras was introduced
by A.A.Mikhalev
[

, see further developments and applications in

,

We also establish a normal form basis theorem for the factor algebra in terms of admissible bracketings
for an arbitrary Lie superalgebra with a known Gröbner-Shirshov basis (Theorem

We expect this generalization to have further applications.
As an application of our Theorem A, we prove the following result.

Theorem B.

Let

Let
\widetilde{L}
be a finitely presented Lie superalgebra,

1.
Preliminaries on Lie superalgebras

All algebras considered in this paper are over a fixed field
K
of
characteristic not 2 and 3
.
This restriction is justified by the fact that
the theory of free Lie superalgebras was developed under this assumption
[
2
]
.
A Lie superalgebra is a linear
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded algebra
L=L_{0}\oplus L_{1}
with product called a Lie superbracket
[-,-]

such that

(1)

[x,y]=-(-1)^{|x||y|}[y,x]
, (super anti-commutativity),

(2)

(-1)^{|x||z|}[x,[y,z]]+(-1)^{|y||x|}[y,[z,x]]+(-1)^{|z||y|}[z,[x,y]]=0
(super Jacobi identity)

Since
char(K)\not=3
the Jacobi identity implies that
[x,[x,x]]=0
for all
x\in L_{1}
.

Using the super-anticommutativity, the super Jacobi identity is equivalent to the fact that the adjoint mapping
ad\ x:y\mapsto[x,y]
,
x,y\in L
is a super-derivation:

[x,[y,z]]=[[x,y],z]+(-1)^{|x|\cdot|y|}[y,[x,z]].

Substituting
z:=y
which is odd we get the identity

(1.1)

[x,[y,y]]=2[[x,y],y],\quad y\ \text{odd}.

Denote by
U(L)
the universal enveloping algebra of a Lie superalgebra
L
. This is an associative algebra containing
L
, and we have

(1.2)

[x,y]=xy-(-1)^{|x||y|}yx\hbox{ for }x,y\in L_{0}\cup L_{1}.

A PBW-basis of
U(L)
is described in
[
2
]
.

A homomorphism of Lie superalgebras is a linear map that preserves the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading and commutes with the superbracket.

All ideals and subalgebras considered are always
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-homogeneous.
If not otherwise stated the elements and monomials of superalgebras that we consider are also assumed
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-homogeneous.

In order to describe a free Lie superalgebra we consider disjoint sets
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
and the free associative algebra
K\langle X_{0}\cup X_{1}\rangle
.
Let
X_{0}
be even and
X_{1}
odd, we extend these notions on monomials on the alphabet
X_{0}\cup X_{1}
.
Thus, the free associative algebra
K\langle X_{0}\cup X_{1}\rangle
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded.
Consider
F
the smallest
K
-linear subspace of
K\langle X_{0}\cup X_{1}\rangle
that contains
X_{0}\cup X_{1}
and
that is closed under the multilinear superbracket
[-,-]
defined on monomials by (
1.2
).
Then
F=F(X_{0},X_{1})
is the free Lie superalgebra
with the free generating set
X_{0}\cup X_{1}
in the following sense.
Let
L
be a Lie superalgebra with a homogeneous generating set
\bar{X}_{0}\cup\bar{X}_{1}
,
where there are surjective maps
X_{0}\to\bar{X}_{0}
and
X_{1}\to\bar{X}_{1}
.
Then there is a unique epimorphism of Lie superalgebras
f:F\to L

that maps elements
X_{0}\cup X_{1}
to the respective elements of
\bar{X}_{0}\cup\bar{X}_{1}
.
One also has
K\langle X_{0}\cup X_{1}\rangle\cong U(F(X_{0},X_{1}))
. For more details on Lie superalgebras
see
[
1
,
2
,
7
,
17
]
.

Let
I
be
Ker(f)
where
f
is the map defined above.
Since
f
is a homomorphism of Lie superalgebras then
I
is a homogeneous ideal
i.e.
I=I_{0}\oplus I_{1}
, where
I_{0}=F_{0}\cap I,I_{1}=F_{1}\cap I
.
We say that
R_{0}\cup R_{1}
generates
I
if
R_{0}\subseteq I_{0},R_{1}\subseteq I_{1}
and
I
is the smallest ideal of
F
that contains
R_{0}\cup R_{1}
.
We write

L=\langle X\ |\ R\rangle,\hbox{ where }X=X_{0}\cup X_{1},R=R_{0}\cup R_{1}.

We say that
L
is
finitely presented
(in terms of generators and relations) if we can choose
X_{0}\cup X_{1}\cup R_{0}\cup R_{1}
finite.

A super-Lyndon-Shirshov word is either a Lyndon-Shirshov word or a word

A Lyndon-Shirshov monomial is a non-empty word

. A super-Lyndon-Shirshov monomial is either a Lyndon-Shirshov monomial or a non-associative word

is an odd Lyndon-Shirshov monomial. By

there is a bijection between the super-Lyndon-Shirshov monomials and super-Lyndon-Shirshov words, that sends each non-associative word

Denote by

.
It is considered as a Lie superalgebra by

3.
Gröbner-Shirshov basis of HNN extensions of Lie superalgebras

Let
L
be a Lie superalgebra with a subalgebra
A
. Let

be a
superderivation of degree
|d|
, i.e. a linear homogeneous map of parity

i.e.

|d(a)|=|a|+|d|

for every homogeneous element

d([a,b])=[d(a),b]+(-1)^{|d||a|}[a,d(b)].

We stress that in this setting

Let
a\in A
be odd. Then

(3.1)

d([a,a])=[d(a),a]+(-1)^{|d||a|}[a,d(a)]=2[d(a),a],\qquad a\text{ odd}.

Recall that by
[

Consider the structural constants of

\displaystyle f_{xx}

\overline{f_{xx}}=xx,\qquad\widehat{f_{xx}}=2xx,\quad\hbox{ for }x\in X_{1}=X\cap L_{1},

\overline{f_{xy}}=xy=\widehat{f_{xy}}\ \hbox{ for }x,y\in X,x>y;\qquad

\overline{g_{a}}=ta=\widehat{g_{a}}\ \hbox{ for }a\in B.

The first two are similar to the ones that appear in the case of ordinary Lie algebras considered by Wasserman in

\langle g_{a},f_{ab}\rangle_{tab}=[g_{a},b]-[t,f_{ab}]=

Proposition 3.1
.

Even in the cases 3), 4), 5) we have trivial compositions.

Proof.

Consider case 3). It appears only in case

) in the third line and (

) in the sixth line

\displaystyle\langle f_{xy},f_{yy}\rangle_{xyy}

\displaystyle=[f_{xy},y]-\frac{1}{2}\Big[x,f_{yy}\Big]

\displaystyle=\Big[[x,y]-\sum_{v\in X}\alpha_{xy}^{v}v,y\Big]-\frac{1}{2}\Big[x,[y,y]-\sum_{v\in X}\alpha_{yy}^{v}v\Big]

\displaystyle=\Big([[x,y],y]-\frac{1}{2}[x,[y,y]]\Big)-\sum_{v\in X}\alpha_{xy}^{v}[v,y]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in X}\alpha_{yy}^{v}[x,v]

\displaystyle=-\sum_{v\in X}\alpha_{xy}^{v}[v,y]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in X}\alpha_{yy}^{v}[x,v]

\displaystyle=-\sum_{v\in X}\alpha_{xy}^{v}(f_{vy}+\sum_{u\in X}\alpha_{vy}^{u}u)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in X}\alpha_{yy}^{v}(f_{xv}+\sum_{u\in X}\alpha_{xv}^{u}u)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{v\in X}\alpha_{xy}^{v}f_{vy}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in X}\alpha_{yy}^{v}f_{xv}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{u\in X}\Big(\sum_{v\in X}\Big(2\alpha_{xy}^{v}\alpha_{vy}^{u}-\alpha_{yy}^{v}\alpha_{xv}^{u}\Big)\Big)u

\displaystyle=-\sum_{v\in X}\alpha_{xy}^{v}f_{vy}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in X}\alpha_{yy}^{v}f_{xv}.

Here and below, consider the case that we might get

with

,

.
In this case

, where

of length 2, they are smaller than

with respect to

Consider the case 4). Recall that

1.1
) in the third line
and (
3.5
) in the sixth line

\displaystyle\langle f_{xx},f_{xy}\rangle_{xxy}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}[f_{xx},y]-[x,f_{xy}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big[[x,x]-\sum_{v\in X}\alpha^{v}_{xx}v,y\Big]-\Big[x,[x,y]-\sum_{v\in X}\alpha_{xy}^{v}v\Big]

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{2}[[x,x],y]-[x,[x,y]]\Big)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in X}\alpha^{v}_{xx}[v,y]+\sum_{v\in X}\alpha_{xy}^{v}[x,v]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in X}\alpha^{v}_{xx}[v,y]+\sum_{v\in X}\alpha_{xy}^{v}[x,v]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in X}\alpha^{v}_{xx}\Big(f_{vy}+\sum_{u\in X}\alpha^{u}_{vy}u\Big)+\sum_{v\in X}\alpha_{xy}^{v}\Big(f_{xv}+\sum_{u\in X}\alpha^{u}_{xv}u\Big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in X}\alpha^{v}_{xx}f_{vy}+\sum_{v\in X}\alpha_{xy}^{v}f_{xv}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{u\in X}\Big(\sum_{v\in X}\Big(\alpha^{v}_{xx}\alpha^{u}_{vy}-2\alpha_{xy}^{v}\alpha^{u}_{xv}\Big)\Big)u

Theorem 4.1

The following is formulated in Introduction as our main result, Theorem A.

(5.3)

\{w_{j_{1}}w_{j_{2}}\cdots w_{j_{n}}\mid w_{j_{i}}\in\overline{W},\ n\geq 0\}\subset U(H).

Second, consider also all products (
4.3

Proposition 6.1
.

Let

Proof.

Since
\widetilde{L}
is finitely generated, there exist homogeneous generators

of

as a homogeneous ideal in

. Thus

.

Let

has the parity of

and

Let

is finitely presented, there is a finite set of homogeneous elements

of

Note that

.
This fact is proved by induction, where in case of

and use (

Set

and

be the

generated by

. Since

such that

.

.

.
Then there is an epimorphism of Lie superalgebras

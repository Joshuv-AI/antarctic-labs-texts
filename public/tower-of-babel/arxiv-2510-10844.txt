1.
Introduction

1.1.
Main results

Our purpose is to build on a paper
[
10
]
of Orponen that sharpens Marstrand’s slicing theorem and extends it to slices by fibers of generalized projections in the sense of Peres–Schlag
[
11
]
. The terminology of the Peres–Schlag paper, although standard, is also substantial, so we first state the main result in the classical setting of slices by affine subspaces of Euclidean space. Given integers
1\leq m<n
, let
\mathrm{\mathbf{Gr}}(n,m)
denote the Grassmannian of
m
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
; and, for
V\in\mathrm{\mathbf{Gr}}(n,m)
let
\pi_{\scalebox{0.65}{$V$}}
denote the orthogonal projection of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto
V\cong\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Theorem 1.1.

Let
m<s<n
and let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Borel set with
0<\mathcal{H}^{s}(A)<\infty
. There exists a set
E\subset\mathrm{\mathbf{Gr}}(n,m)
with

\hdim E\leq m(n-m)+m-s

such that, for every subspace
V\in\mathrm{\mathbf{Gr}}(n,m)\setminus E
and every Borel set
B\subseteq A
with
\mathcal{H}^{s}(B)>0
, the equation

(1.1)

\hdim\!\big(B\cap\pi_{\scalebox{0.65}{$V$}}^{-1}(\mathbf{x})\big)=s-m

holds for a positive-
\mathcal{H}^{m}
-measure set of
\mathbf{x}\in V
.

Here and throughout,
\dim
denotes Hausdorff dimension and
\mathcal{H}^{r}
denotes
r
-dimensional Hausdorff measure (
r\geq 0
) in a separable metric space.

Informally, for all
V\in\mathrm{\mathbf{Gr}}(n,m)
outside a small “bad” set
E
, the intersections of
A
with the orthogonal spaces
\mathbf{x}+V^{\perp}
are large (relative to
s
) for many points
\mathbf{x}\in V
. Moreover,
E
is
universal
for
A
in the sense that this same statement holds for all subsets
B\subseteq A
of positive
\mathcal{H}^{s}
-measure:
E
contains the exceptional set of directions
V\in\mathrm{\mathbf{Gr}}(n,m)
corresponding to
every
such
B
. Following Falconer and Mattila
[
3
]
, we accordingly refer to the estimate in Theorem
1.1
as “strong.”

The upper bound on the dimension of slices follows immediately from the well-known sublevel set inequality for the Hausdorff measure of Lipschitz maps (see, for example,
[
7
]
Theorem 7.7). In particular, since
\pi_{\scalebox{0.65}{$V$}}
is Lipschitz for
all

V\in\mathrm{\mathbf{Gr}}(n,m)
, the set
A\cap\pi_{\scalebox{0.65}{$V$}}^{-1}(\mathbf{x})
(hence, every subset thereof) has finite
(s-m)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure for
\mathcal{H}^{m}
-a.e.​
\mathbf{x}\in V
.
A fortiori
there are
no

V\in\mathrm{\mathbf{Gr}}(n,m)
such that the upper bound in (
1.1
) fails for
\mathcal{H}^{m}
-positively-many
\mathbf{x}
.

The lower bound
\hdim\!\big(B\cap\pi_{\scalebox{0.65}{$V$}}^{-1}(\mathbf{x})\big)\geq s-m
in Theorem
1.1
is a special case of our main result, which is situated in the Peres–Schlag framework. In both the statement and the subsequent proofs, it may be efficacious to begin by considering only the case
\beta=0
. See §
2
for a review of the definitions, and see Theorem
4.1
for a simpler statement that forgoes the universality of the exceptional set.

Theorem 1.2.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set and let
\big(\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}\!:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}\big)_{\bm{\lambda}\in U}
be a family of generalized projections that is transversal and strongly regular of degree
\beta\in[0,1)
. There exists a constant
a\geq 1
, depending only on
N
and
m
, such that the following holds. If
m<s<N+m
, then for every Borel set
A\subseteq\Omega
with
0<\mathcal{H}^{s}(A)<\infty
, there exists a set
E\subset U
with

\hdim E\leq N+m-s+a\beta^{1/3}

such that, for every
\bm{\lambda}\in U\setminus E
and every Borel set
B\subseteq A
with
\mathcal{H}^{s}(B)>0
, the inequality

(1.2)

\hdim\!\big(B\cap\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}^{-1}(\mathbf{x})\big)\geq(1-\beta)(s-m)

holds for a positive-Lebesgue-measure set of
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Note that the dimension bound
N+m-s+\varepsilon(\beta)
on
E
, where
\varepsilon(\beta)\to 0
as
\beta\to 0
, is precisely the form that the corresponding projection result takes (see Theorem
2.2
below). This will be important in §
4
.

As a side note, since the ambient dimension
\hdim\Omega
plays no role and the transversality and regularity of
(\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}})_{\bm{\lambda}\in U}
are preserved under restriction of the domain
\Omega
, it would suffice to take
A=\Omega
.

1.2.
Overview and outline

The methods of this paper are already well-established in geometric measure theory. Järvenpää–Järvenpää–Niemelä
[
6
]
proved a nonlinear slicing theorem for mappings between manifolds using the same basic definitions as Peres and Schlag, but their methods were not Fourier-analytic and did not yield dimension bounds on the exceptional sets. Orponen
[
10
]
then proved Theorem
1.2
for
m=N=1
and
B=A
, and the proof of this generalization follows his closely. Mattila
[
8
]
also proved the
B=A
case of Theorem
1.1
by importing some of Orponen’s machinery into higher dimensions (for which reason he attributes the result to Orponen), and the obstacles in extending their slicing result for orthogonal projections to generalized projections are more practical than theoretical. Once the details are worked out, the strong aspect of Theorem
1.2
follows from a simple adaptation of the argument in
[
3
]
.

After laying out the preliminaries in §
2
, we state several technical lemmas in §
3.1
that adapt the Peres–Schlag framework to slices. Most of the proof of Theorem
1.2
is contained in that of Lemma
3.4
in §
3.2
, and in turn the main hurdle in proving this lemma is verifying the hypotheses of Lemma
3.2
. Since the proof of Lemma
3.4
is quite lengthy as is, Lemma
3.3
is set aside to facilitate the application of Lemma
3.2
, and following Orponen’s example we relegate the cumbersome proof of Lemma
3.3
to the
Appendix
. With the main Lemma
3.4
in place, the proof of Theorem
1.2
is completed in two steps: first in §
4.1
without the “universal” exceptional set, which is then introduced in §
4.2
using the method of Falconer and Mattila, thereby completing the proof of the theorem. Finally, §
5
presents an application of Theorem
1.2
to vertical slices in the Heisenberg groups
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

2.
Background

Given parameters
w_{1},\dots,w_{k}
and quantities
a,b\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
, we write
a\lesssim_{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}}b
if there exists a positive constant
c_{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}}
(depending only on those parameters) such that
a\leq c_{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}}b
. Similarly,
a\gtrsim_{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}}b
means that
b\lesssim_{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}}a
and
a\sim_{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}}b
means that both
a\lesssim_{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}}b
and
a\gtrsim_{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}}b
. For readability, some parameters (e.g., the dimension of the space in which we are working) may be suppressed from the notation when doing so poses no risk of error or confusion. Although this is less standard, we will also on occasion write
a\simeq_{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}}b
when
a=c_{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}}b
. This is stronger than
a\sim_{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}}b
, which only says that
c_{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}}a\leq b\leq d_{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}}a
.

2.1.
Preliminaries on Radon measures

Denote by
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
the space of all nonzero
Radon measures
on a separable metric space
(\Omega,d)
, i.e., the positive cone of Borel regular (outer) measures that are finite on compact sets, inner regular on open sets, and outer regular on all sets, but not identically
0
(see
[
7
]
). The
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{s}
is Borel regular, and it is Radon when restricted to a fixed subset of
\Omega
with finite
\mathcal{H}^{s}
-measure.

A measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
is called
s
-Frostman
if

\mu(B(\bm{\omega},r))\lesssim_{\mu}r^{s}\qquad\text{for all }\bm{\omega}\in\Omega\text{ and }r>0.

The following result, whose importance is difficult to understate, will be used frequently without explicit mention. This version for general metric spaces is due to Howroyd
[
5
]
.

Theorem 2.1  (Frostman’s lemma).

If
\Omega
is a
\sigma
-compact metric space with
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega)

>0
, then
\Omega
supports an
s
-Frostman measure.

A related tool for describing the scaling of a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
is the
s
-energy

(2.1)

I_{s}(\mu):=\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{d(\mu\times\mu)(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})}{d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{s}}.

If
\mu
is finite and
s
-Frostman, then it has finite
t
-energy for all
0\leq t<s
. Conversely, if
\mu
is finite and
I_{s}(\mu)<\infty
, then there exists a positive-measure subset
B\subseteq\Omega
such that
\mu\restrict B
, the restriction of
\mu
to
B
, is
s
-Frostman.

A useful formula for the
s
-energy of a finite Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
is

(2.2)

I_{s}(\mu)=\gamma_{n,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}|\mathbf{z}|^{s-k}\big|\mathchoice{\accentset{\displaystyle\text{\smash{\raisebox{-5.59721pt}{$\widehatsym$}}}}{\mu}}{\accentset{\textstyle\text{\smash{\raisebox{-5.59721pt}{$\widehatsym$}}}}{\mu}}{\accentset{\scriptstyle\text{\smash{\raisebox{-3.91806pt}{$\widehatsym$}}}}{\mu}}{\accentset{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\smash{\raisebox{-2.7986pt}{$\widehatsym$}}}}{\mu}}(\mathbf{z})\big|^{2}\,d\mathbf{z},\qquad 0<s\leq k,

where
\mathchoice{\accentset{\displaystyle\text{\smash{\raisebox{-5.59721pt}{$\widehatsym$}}}}{\mu}}{\accentset{\textstyle\text{\smash{\raisebox{-5.59721pt}{$\widehatsym$}}}}{\mu}}{\accentset{\scriptstyle\text{\smash{\raisebox{-3.91806pt}{$\widehatsym$}}}}{\mu}}{\accentset{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\smash{\raisebox{-2.7986pt}{$\widehatsym$}}}}{\mu}}\!:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{C}
is the Fourier transform of the measure
\mu
and
\gamma_{n,s}>0
is a constant. However, the right-hand side of this equation can in fact converge for
any
value of
s\in\mathbb{R}
(simply choose
\mu
to be a Schwartz function), whereas the integral in (
2.1
) diverges whenever
s>k
. In lieu of the right-hand side of (
2.2
), one often studies instead the quantity

\mathcal{I}_{s}(\mu):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}(1+|\mathbf{z}|)^{s-k}\big|\mathchoice{\accentset{\displaystyle\text{\smash{\raisebox{-5.59721pt}{$\widehatsym$}}}}{\mu}}{\accentset{\textstyle\text{\smash{\raisebox{-5.59721pt}{$\widehatsym$}}}}{\mu}}{\accentset{\scriptstyle\text{\smash{\raisebox{-3.91806pt}{$\widehatsym$}}}}{\mu}}{\accentset{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\smash{\raisebox{-2.7986pt}{$\widehatsym$}}}}{\mu}}(\mathbf{z})\big|^{2}\,d\mathbf{z},

the
s
-Sobolev energy
of
\mu
, which has the practical advantage of a bounded integrand. As long as
s>0
, the two are comparable. Accordingly, the number

\sdim\mu:=\sup\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}\!:\mathcal{I}_{t}(\mu)<\infty\right\}

is called the
Sobolev dimension
of
\mu
, and it coincides with
s
whenever
\mu
is
s
-Frostman and
0\leq s<n
(but not necessarily when
s=n
). Clearly
\mathcal{I}_{t}(\mu)<\infty
for all
t<0
, so
\sdim\mu\geq 0
.

2.2.
Pushforwards and slices of measures

If
\Psi\!:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
is a continuous map and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
, then there is a Radon measure
\Psi_{\sharp}\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
—the
pushforward
of
\mu
by
\Psi
—defined on Borel sets
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k}
by
\Psi_{\sharp}\mu(X):=\mu(\Psi^{-1}(X))
. The pushforward measure satisfies
\spt\Psi_{\sharp}\mu=\Psi(\spt\mu)
and, for all Borel functions
g\!:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to[0,\infty]
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}g(\mathbf{z})\,d(\Psi_{\sharp}\mu)(\mathbf{z})=\int_{\Omega}(g\circ\Psi)(\bm{\omega})\,d\mu(\bm{\omega}).

From this formula one can derive the powerful identity

(2.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}g(\mathbf{z})\big|\widehat{\Psi_{\sharp}\mu}(\mathbf{z})\big|^{2}\,d\mathbf{z}=\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\mathchoice{\accentset{\displaystyle\text{\smash{\raisebox{-5.59721pt}{$\widehatsym$}}}}{g}}{\accentset{\textstyle\text{\smash{\raisebox{-5.59721pt}{$\widehatsym$}}}}{g}}{\accentset{\scriptstyle\text{\smash{\raisebox{-3.91806pt}{$\widehatsym$}}}}{g}}{\accentset{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\smash{\raisebox{-2.7986pt}{$\widehatsym$}}}}{g}}(\Psi(\bm{\omega})-\Psi(\bm{\zeta}))\,d(\mu\times\mu)(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})

using Plancherel’s theorem. Incidentally, (
2.2
) is a special case of this.

There is a straightforward procedure for defining the slices of a measure by the fibers of a continuous map by means of its pushforward under said map. However, we will later relocate our analysis from
\Omega
to a Euclidean space in such a way that the slices of a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
correspond to the slices of its “surrogate” in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by affine subspaces. (Incidentally, this surrogate is itself a pushforward measure—the pushforward of
\mu
by the map
\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}
defined in §
2.4
below.) For that reason, we need only to understand the slices of measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by the fibers of orthogonal projections.

To that end, for each
V\in\mathrm{\mathbf{Gr}}(m+n,m)
, let
\nu_{\scalebox{0.65}{$V$}}:=\pi_{\scalebox{0.65}{$\hskip 0.68257ptV$}\sharp}\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
be the pushforward of the measure
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{m+n})
by
\pi_{\scalebox{0.65}{$V$}}
, where we identify
V
with
\mathbb{R}^{m}
using local coordinates on
\mathrm{\mathbf{Gr}}(m+n,m)
. For
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we define the
slice of
\nu
by
\pi_{\scalebox{0.65}{$V$}}^{-1}(\mathbf{x})
, denoted
\nu_{\scalebox{0.65}{$\hskip 0.68257ptV$}\hskip-0.68257pt,\mathbf{x}}
, via its action on functions
f\in C_{c}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{m+n})
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m+n}}f(\mathbf{z})\,d\nu_{\scalebox{0.65}{$\hskip 0.68257ptV$}\hskip-0.68257pt,\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{z}):=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{m}(B(\mathbf{x},\delta))}\int_{\pi_{\scalebox{0.5}{$V$}}^{-1}(B(\mathbf{x},\delta))}f(\mathbf{w})\,d\nu(\mathbf{w}),

provided this limit exists. This is precisely the Radon–Nikodym derivative
D(\pi_{\scalebox{0.65}{$V$}\sharp}\nu_{f},\mathbf{x})
of the pushforward by
\pi_{\scalebox{0.65}{$V$}}
of the Radon measure
\nu_{f}(A):=\int_{A}f(\mathbf{z})\hskip 0.83298ptd\nu(\mathbf{z})
on
\mathbb{R}^{m+n}
, so for a
fixed

f\in C_{c}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{m+n})
, the limit exists for
\mathcal{L}^{m}
-a.e.​
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. A standard argument from the separability of
C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{m+n})
then implies the existence of this limit for
all

f\in C_{c}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{m+n})
, so the sliced measure
\nu_{\scalebox{0.65}{$\hskip 0.68257ptV$}\hskip-0.68257pt,\mathbf{x}}
exists as a positive linear functional on
C_{c}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{m+n})
for almost every
\mathbf{x}
. By the Riesz representation theorem, it is an honest Radon measure.

2.3.
Bessel functions and spherical harmonics

Special functions make a brief appearance in the proof of the main lemma. All the facts utilized are “well-known” and they are unimportant in the broader scope of this chapter, so we only record only the very basics here. See
[
9
]
for a full exposition.

For each
\nu>-\tfrac{1}{2}
, let
J_{\nu}\!:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be the
Bessel function of the first kind of order
\nu
:

J_{\nu}(u):=\frac{u^{\nu}}{2^{\nu}\hskip 0.83298pt\Gamma\big(\nu+\tfrac{1}{2}\big)\Gamma\big(\tfrac{1}{2}\big)}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{\mathrm{i}ut}\big(1-t^{2}\big)^{\nu-1/2}\,dt,

where
\Gamma
is the gamma function. It behaves roughly like
u^{-1/2}\cos(u-b)
for some phase shift
b=b(\nu)
. The Bessel functions will only arise in the
Hankel transform of order
\nu
: for any
f\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}([0,\infty))
,

\mathrm{H}_{\nu}f(s):=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(r)J_{\nu}(sr)\hskip 0.83298ptr\,dr,

where the integral is understood in the limiting sense as an improper integral. The Hankel transform of a polynomial has an explicit expression that we shall state and use later.

A class of functions that “play well” with the Bessel functions are the spherical harmonics on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
—the restrictions to
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
of the homogeneous harmonic polynomials on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
—which will play the role of multidimensional Fourier series. More precisely, a function
Y_{\ell}\!:\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{C}
is called a
spherical harmonic of degree
\ell
if there exists a homogeneous polynomial
P\!:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
of degree
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
\Delta P=0
such that
P|_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}=Y_{\ell}
, where
\Delta
is the Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We shall fix an orthonormal basis
\bigcup_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\{Y_{\ell,k}\}_{k=1}^{d(\ell)}
for
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
, where
d(\ell)
is the (finite) dimension of the subspace of spherical harmonic of degree
\ell
.

2.4.
Generalized projections

We now turn to the requisite terminology from the Peres–Schlag framework for generalize projections. Let
N,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, let
\varnothing\neq U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set, let
(\Omega,d)
be a compact metric space, and let
\Pi\!:\Omega\times U\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a continuous map. Writing
\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}:=\Pi(\,\cdot\,,\bm{\lambda})
, we consider
\Pi
as a parametrized family
\big(\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}\!:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}\big)_{\bm{\lambda}\in U}
of mappings from
\Omega
to
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Definition 2.1.

The family
(\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}})_{\bm{\lambda}\in U}
is called a
family of generalized projections
if
\bm{\lambda}\mapsto\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega})
is
C^{\infty}
for all
\bm{\omega}\in\Omega
and the following condition is satisfied:

i.

For every compact set
K\subset U
and every
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
, the inequality

(2.4)

\big|\partial_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\bm{\alpha}}\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega})\big|\lesssim_{K,|\bm{\alpha}|}1

holds for all
\bm{\lambda}\in K
and
\bm{\omega}\in\Omega
.

Supposing
(\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}})_{\bm{\lambda}\in U}
is a family of generalized projections, let

\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta}):=\frac{\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega})-\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\zeta})}{d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})}

for
\bm{\omega}\neq\bm{\zeta}
and
\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{\omega}):=\bm{0}
, and let
\beta\in[0,1)
.

ii.

The family is said to be
transversal of degree
\bm{\beta}
if, for every compact
K\subset U
, every
\bm{\lambda}\in K
, and all
\bm{\omega}\neq\bm{\zeta}
,

(2.5)

\displaystyle|\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})|\lesssim_{\beta,K}d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{\beta}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad\det\!\left[D_{\bm{\lambda}}\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})\big(D_{\bm{\lambda}}\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})\big)^{\hskip-0.68257pt\mathrm{t}}\right]\gtrsim_{\beta,K}d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{2\beta}.

iii.

The family is said to be
regular of degree
\bm{\beta}
if, for every compact
K\subset U
, every
\bm{\lambda}\in K
, all
\bm{\omega}\neq\bm{\zeta}
, and all
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle|\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})|\lesssim_{\beta,K}d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{\beta}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad\big|\partial_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\bm{\alpha}}\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})\big|\lesssim_{K,\beta,|\bm{\alpha}|}d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{-\beta|\bm{\alpha}|}.

iv.

The family is said to be
strongly regular of degree
\bm{\beta}
if, for every compact
K\subset U
, every
\bm{\lambda}\in K
, all
\bm{\omega}\neq\bm{\zeta}
, and all
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
,

(2.7)

\big|\partial_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\bm{\alpha}}\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})\big|\lesssim_{K,\beta,|\bm{\alpha}|}d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{-\beta|\bm{\alpha}|}

(regardless of the size of
|\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})|
).

The main results of
[
11
]
concern the Sobolev dimension of projections (i.e., pushforwards) of Radon measures. Having a large projection in the direction
\bm{\lambda}
is certainly requisite for a measure on
\Omega
to have many large slices by
\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}
, so we record (a portion of) one of the main Peres–Schlag theorems that bounds the exceptional sets of projections.

Theorem 2.2  (Peres–Schlag
[
11
]
Theorem 7.3).

There exists a constant
a_{0}>0
depending only on
N
and
m
such that the following holds. Let
\big(\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}\!:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}\big)_{\bm{\lambda}\in U}
be a family of generalized projections that is both transversal and regular of some degree
\beta\in[0,1)
. If
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
has finite
s
-energy for some
s>m
, then

\hdim\big\{\bm{\lambda}\in U\!:\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}\sharp}\mu\not\!\ll\mathcal{H}^{r}\big\}\leq N+r-\frac{s}{1+a_{0}\beta}

for all
r\in(0,m]
.

An important family of objects in connection with the generalized projections, originally introduced in
[
10
]
, will be the mappings
\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}\!:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m+mN}
defined by

(2.8)

\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega}):=\big(\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega}),D_{\bm{\lambda}}\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega})\big),

where the total derivative
D_{\bm{\lambda}}\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega})
is identified with an element of
\mathbb{R}^{mN}
. In
[
11
]
,
\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}
is adequate to relocate the entire projection problem into Euclidean space: the transversality and regularity conditions black-box the fact that preimages of the sets
\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}(A)
belong to an abstract metric space. In the slicing problem, on the other hand, the objects of study are precisely those sets that collapse under the application of
\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}
, so finding another “natural” mapping of
\Omega
to a Euclidean space is the first step in adapting the Peres–Schlag framework to the study of slices by means of Fourier analysis.

The maps
\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}
do the trick because the transversality condition (
2.5
) requires that, modulo a loss inflicted by a positive
\beta
, the first partial derivatives of
\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}}
pick up the information about pairs of points that
\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}}
itself loses. It follows from (
2.5
) that

|\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})|\lesssim_{\beta,K}d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{\beta}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\big|\partial_{\lambda_{i}}\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})\big|_{\infty}\gtrsim_{\beta,K}d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{\beta},

so packaging the partial derivatives of the components of
\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}
into
\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}
ensures that it always distinguishes between pairs of points in the same fiber of
\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}
. Due to (
2.7
),
\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}
is sufficiently regular as not to stretch the fibers beyond recognition.

We can summarize the relevant properties of
\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}
as a single bi-Hölder condition

(2.9)

d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{\beta}\lesssim_{\beta,K}\frac{|\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega})-\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\zeta})|}{d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})}\lesssim_{\beta,K}d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{-\beta},

valid for all
\bm{\lambda}
belonging to a compact set
K\subset U
, where the lower and upper bounds owe themselves to the transversality and regularity of
\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}
, respectively. Note that the upper Hölder bound would not follow if we only required that only (
2.6
) hold, as in
[
11
]
, rather than (
2.7
). However, an important step in the proof of Lemma
3.3
requires that
|\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega})-\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\zeta})|
be small whenever
d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})
is—not just the
|\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega})-\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\zeta})|
component. In particular, if it is possible to relax this condition, doing so would require a more nuanced strategy for ensuring that the derivative bounds in the hypotheses of Lemma
3.2
are satisfied.

for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

\eta^{(2)}(\mathbf{y}):=\eta(|\mathbf{y}|)
for all
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

There exists a constant

(depending only on

and

, all

\displaystyle\left|\hskip 0.83298pt\int_{U}\rho(\bm{\lambda})\hskip 0.83298pt\widehat{\eta^{(1)}}\big(2^{j}\hskip 0.83298ptd(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})\big)\widehat{\eta^{(2)}}\big(2^{j-i}\hskip 0.83298ptd(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})D_{\bm{\lambda}}\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})\big)\hskip 0.83298ptd\bm{\lambda}\hskip 0.83298pt\right|

3.2.
The main lemma

Recall the family of maps
\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}\!:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
defined in (
2.8
). For each
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
,

\mu_{\bm{\lambda},\mathbf{x}}:=\big(\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}\sharp}\mu\big)_{\mathbf{x}}^{(1)}

is the slice of the pushforward measure
\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}\sharp}\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{m+n})
by the fiber of the coordinate map
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\mapsto\mathbf{x}
over the point
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Lemma 3.4.

There exists a constant
a_{2}\geq 1
, depending only on
N
and
m
, such that the following holds. Let
(\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}})_{\bm{\lambda}\in U}
be a family of generalized projections that is both transversal and strongly regular of order
\beta
. If
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
is finite and has finite
(s+a_{2}\beta^{1/3})
-energy for some
m<s<N+m
, then

(3.3)

\hdim\left\{\bm{\lambda}\in U\!:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}I_{s+\beta^{1/3}-m}(\mu_{\bm{\lambda},\mathbf{x}})\,d\mathbf{x}=\infty\right\}\leq N+m-s.

Proof.

With the notation and hypotheses of the lemma statement and
n:=mN
as before, let
t\geq s
be such that
I_{t}(\mu)<\infty
and let
\sigma:=s+\beta^{1/3}
. By Lemma
3.1
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}I_{\sigma-m}(\mu_{\bm{\lambda},\mathbf{x}})\,d\mathbf{x}\lesssim_{\beta,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m+n}}\big|\mathbf{z}^{(2)}\big|^{\sigma-m-n}\big|\widehat{\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}\sharp}\mu}(\mathbf{z})\big|^{2}\,d\mathbf{z}

for all
\bm{\lambda}\in U
. Using a countable exhaustion of
U
by compact sets, we may (and shall) restrict our attention from
U
to a compact subset
K\subset U
. For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\mathbf{C}_{i}=\left\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{m+n}\!:\frac{1}{2^{i+1}}<\frac{\big|\mathbf{z}^{(2)}\big|}{|\mathbf{z}|}\leq\frac{1}{2^{i}}\right\}

be the set of points in
\mathbb{R}^{m+n}
whose position vectors make an angle between
\cos^{-1}\!\big(2^{-i-1}\big)
and
\cos^{-1}\!\big(2^{-i}\big)
with the coordinate plane
\{0\}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. These partition
\mathbb{R}^{m+n}
up to a set of Lebesgue measure
0
, so

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m+n}}\big|\mathbf{z}^{(2)}\big|^{\sigma-m-n}\big|\widehat{\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}\sharp}\mu}(\mathbf{z})\big|^{2}\,d\mathbf{z}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbf{C}_{i}}\big|\mathbf{z}^{(2)}\big|^{\sigma-m-n}\big|\widehat{\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}\sharp}\mu}(\mathbf{z})\big|^{2}\,d\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle\sim_{\beta,s}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}2^{i(n+m-\sigma)}\int_{\mathbf{C}_{i}}|\mathbf{z}|^{\sigma-m-n}\big|\widehat{\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}\sharp}\mu}(\mathbf{z})\big|^{2}\,d\mathbf{z};

the second line uses the fact that
\big|\mathbf{z}^{(2)}\big|\sim 2^{-i}|\mathbf{z}|
on
\mathbf{C}_{i}
. Our goal is to determine how large
t
must be if this series is to converge for all but a few
\bm{\lambda}\in K
; later,
t
will take the form
s+a_{2}\beta^{1/3}
for some
a_{2}\geq 1
to be determined.

Step 1.
We bound the second series in (
3.4
) from above by a series of functions amenable to the application of Lemma
3.2
. But first, the
i=0
term warrants a separate and elementary treatment: the Plancherel identity (
2.3
) and the transversality constraint (
2.9
) together yield

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{C}_{0}}|\mathbf{z}|^{\sigma-m-n}\big|\widehat{\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}\sharp}\mu}(\mathbf{z})\big|^{2}\,d\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m+n}}|\mathbf{z}|^{\sigma-m-n}\big|\widehat{\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}\sharp}\mu}(\mathbf{z})\big|^{2}\,d\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle\sim_{N,m,\beta,s}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{d(\mu\times\mu)(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})}{\big|\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega})-\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\zeta})\big|^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta,K}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{d(\mu\times\mu)(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})}{d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{(1+\beta)\sigma}}

\displaystyle=I_{(1+\beta)\sigma}(\mu).

As
\mu
has finite
t
-energy, this last integral is finite for all
\bm{\lambda}\in K
provided that
\sigma\leq\tfrac{t}{1+\beta}
.

To bound the remaining terms in (
3.4
), we increase the regularity of the integrals using smooth bump functions
\phi_{i}\!:\mathbb{R}^{m+n}\to[0,1]
satisfying

\chi_{\mathbf{C}_{i}}\leq\phi_{i}\leq\chi_{\mathbf{C}_{i-1}\cup\mathbf{C}_{i}\cup\mathbf{C}_{i+1}}.

In particular, we set

\phi_{i}(\mathbf{z}):=\phi\big(2^{i+1}\big|\mathbf{z}^{(2)}\big|/|\mathbf{z}|\big),\quad\text{where}\quad\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}\big(\big(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{5}{2}\big)\big)

is a smooth bump function for the interval
[1,2]
; thus,

\int_{\mathbf{C}_{i}}|\mathbf{z}|^{\sigma-m-n}\big|\widehat{\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}\sharp}\mu}(\mathbf{z})\big|^{2}\,d\mathbf{z}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m+n}}\phi_{i}(\mathbf{z})|\mathbf{z}|^{\sigma-m-n}\big|\widehat{\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}\sharp}\mu}(\mathbf{z})\big|^{2}\,d\mathbf{z}

for all
i\geq 1
. In view of our aim of proving that the value in (
3.4
) is finite for “most”
\bm{\lambda}\in K
, our work is complete if we can show that

\hdim\left\{\bm{\lambda}\in K\!:\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{i(2n+m-\sigma)}h_{i}(\bm{\lambda})=\infty\right\}\leq N+m-s

for all sufficiently small
\beta\geq 0
, where

(3.5)

h_{i}(\bm{\lambda}):=\frac{1}{2^{in}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m+n}}\phi_{i}(\mathbf{z})|\mathbf{z}|^{\sigma-m-n}\big|\widehat{\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}\sharp}\mu}(\mathbf{z})\big|^{2}\,d\mathbf{z}.

We do so by means of Lemma
3.2
, and the remainder of this proof consists primarily of showing that, for some
0<p<n
, these
h_{i}
satisfy (
3.1
) with
A=2
,
R=2^{p+n}
,
q<s-m
, and some large
L\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
to be determined. As such, we must bound the
L^{1}
-norms of the
h_{i}|_{K}
and the
L^{\infty}
-norms of their derivatives.

Step 2.
We verify the
L^{1}
-bounds in (
3.1
). Let
A_{i,j}:=\big\{\mathbf{z}\in\spt\phi_{i}\!:2^{j}\leq|\mathbf{z}|<2^{j+1}\big\}
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, so that

(3.6)

h_{i}(\bm{\lambda})\sim\frac{1}{2^{in}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{j(\sigma-m-n)}\int_{A_{i,j}}\big|\widehat{\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}\sharp}\mu}(\mathbf{z})\big|^{2}\,d\mathbf{z}

by the definition of
h_{i}
and the boundedness of the Fourier transform of a finite Borel measure. For a sufficiently large constant
b\geq 2
depending only on
N
and
m
, the set
A_{i,j}
is contained in the product annulus

B_{i,j}:=\left\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{m+n}\!:\tfrac{1}{b}2^{j}<\big|\mathbf{z}^{(1)}\big|\leq b2^{j},\ \tfrac{1}{b}2^{j-i}<\big|\mathbf{z}^{(2)}\big|\leq b2^{j-i}\right\}.

If
\eta\!:\big(\tfrac{1}{b^{2}},b^{2}\big)\to[0,1]
is a smooth bump function for the interval
\big[\tfrac{1}{b},b\big]
and if
\eta_{j}^{(k)}\hskip-0.83298pt(\mathbf{z}):=\eta\big(2^{-j}\big|\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\big|\big)
for
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{m+n}
, then
\mathbf{z}\mapsto\eta_{j}^{(1)}\hskip-0.83298pt(\mathbf{z})\eta_{j-i}^{(2)}\hskip-0.83298pt(\mathbf{z})
is a smooth bump function for
B_{i,j}
. It then follows from the Plancherel identity (
2.3
), the definition of
\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}
, and the tensor product factorization of the Fourier transform that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\int_{A_{i,j}}\big|\widehat{\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}\sharp}\mu}(\mathbf{z})\big|^{2}\,d\mathbf{z}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m+n}}\eta_{j}^{(1)}\hskip-0.83298pt(\mathbf{z})\eta_{j-i}^{(2)}(\mathbf{z})\big|\widehat{\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}\sharp}\mu}(\mathbf{z})\big|^{2}\,d\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle=\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\big(\eta_{j}^{(1)}\eta_{j-i}^{(2)}\big)\!\widehat{\raisebox{6.0pt}{ \ }}\big(\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega})-\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\zeta})\big)\hskip 0.83298ptd(\mu\times\mu)(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})

\displaystyle=2^{mj+n(j-i)}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\widehat{\eta^{(1)}}\big(2^{j}d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})\big)

\displaystyle\cdot\widehat{\eta^{(2)}}\big(2^{j-i}d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})D_{\bm{\lambda}}\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})\big)\hskip 0.83298ptd(\mu\times\mu)(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta}).

In the third line,
\eta^{(1)}(\mathbf{x}):=\eta_{0}^{(1)}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
for any
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\eta^{(2)}(\mathbf{y}):=\eta_{0}^{(2)}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
(note that
\eta_{0}^{(1)}
is independent of
\mathbf{y}
and
\eta_{0}^{(2)}
is independent of
\mathbf{x}
), and the Fourier transforms thereof denote the
m
- and
n
-dimensional Fourier transforms, respectively, rather than the full
(m+n)
-dimensional Fourier transform.

Fix a value
0<p<n
, to be chosen sufficiently close to
n
much later in the proof. Lemma
3.3
, (
3.6
), and (
3.7
) together imply that, whenever
\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U)
is a smooth bump function for
K
,

(3.8)

\displaystyle 2^{i(p+n)}\int_{K}h_{i}(\bm{\lambda})\,d\bm{\lambda}\lesssim_{\rho}2^{ip}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{j(\sigma-m-n)}\int_{U}\rho(\bm{\lambda})\int_{A_{i,j}}\big|\widehat{\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}\sharp}\mu}(\mathbf{z})\big|^{2}\,d\mathbf{z}\hskip 0.83298ptd\bm{\lambda}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{K}2^{ip}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{j(\sigma-m-n)}\cdot 2^{mj+n(j-i)}

\displaystyle\cdot\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\bigg[\int_{U}\rho(\bm{\lambda})\widehat{\eta^{(1)}}\big(2^{j}d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})\big)

\displaystyle\cdot\widehat{\eta^{(2)}}\big(2^{j-i}d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})D_{\bm{\lambda}}\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})\big)d\bm{\lambda}\bigg]\hskip 0.83298ptd(\mu\times\mu)(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{i(p-n)}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{j\sigma}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\big(1+2^{j}d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{1+a_{1}\beta}\big)^{-c}\,d(\mu\times\mu)(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta}).

(The implicit constant depends only on
K
and not
\rho
once a standard procedure for constructing bump functions chosen.) The series in
j
converges provided
c>\sigma
, and in fact it is bounded above by
\sim_{\beta,s,c}I_{(1+a_{1}\beta)\sigma}(\mu)
. To see this, write

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{2^{js}}{\big(1+2^{j}d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{1+a_{1}\beta}\big)^{c}}

\displaystyle\sim d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{-(1+a_{1}\beta)\sigma}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{ks}}{{(1+2^{k})}^{c}}+\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-1}\frac{2^{ks}}{{(1+2^{k})}^{c}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{-(1+a_{1}\beta)\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{1-2^{\sigma-c}}+\frac{1}{1-2^{-\sigma}}\right),

so that by (
3.8
)

2^{i(p+n)}\int_{K}h_{i}(\bm{\lambda})\,d\bm{\lambda}\lesssim_{\beta,K,s,c}2^{i(p-n)}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{d(\mu\times\mu)(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})}{d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{(1+a_{1}\beta)\sigma}}=2^{i(p-n)}I_{(1+a_{1}\beta)\sigma}(\mu).

With (say)
c=N+m>\sigma
, the power series estimate

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{i(p+n)}\|h_{i}\|_{L^{1}(K)}\lesssim_{N,m,\beta,K,s}\frac{1}{1-2^{p-n}}I_{(1+a_{1}\beta)\sigma}(\mu)

follows at once, so

\|h_{i}\|_{L^{1}(K)}\lesssim_{N,m,\beta,K,s,p}2^{-i(p+n)}.

Note that this imposes the new constraint
\sigma\leq\tfrac{t}{1+a_{1}\beta}
, which is stronger than previously-established the constraint
\sigma\leq\tfrac{t}{1+\beta}
since
a_{1}\geq 1
.

Step 3.
We verify the
L^{\infty}
-bounds (
3.1
) on the partial derivatives
\partial_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\bm{\alpha}}h_{i}
. To do so, we rewrite the integral in (

) using Plancherel’s theorem and repeatedly differentiate under the integral sign. Plancherel’s theorem tells us that

(3.9)

h_{i}(\bm{\lambda})=\frac{1}{2^{in}}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\widehat{g_{i}}\big(\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega})-\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\zeta})\big)\,d(\mu\times\mu)(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta}),

where

g_{i}(\mathbf{z}):=|\mathbf{z}|^{\sigma-m-n}\phi_{i}(\mathbf{z})=|\mathbf{z}|^{\sigma-m-n}\phi\big(2^{i+1}\big|\mathbf{z}^{(2)}\big|/|\mathbf{z}|\big).

Our goal, then, is to establish pointwise bounds on the partial derivatives of the function

(3.10)

\gamma_{i}(\bm{\lambda}):=\widehat{g_{i}}\big(\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega})-\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\zeta})\big),

where
\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta}\in\Omega
are fixed. To do this, we first consider
\widehat{g_{i}}
itself. Write
\mathbf{z}=r\bm{\nu}
in spherical coordinates, so that

(3.11)

\widehat{g_{i}}(\mathbf{w})=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\sigma-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m+n-1}}\phi_{i}(\bm{\nu})e^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}r\bm{\nu}\cdot\mathbf{w}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{m+n-1}(\bm{\nu})\hskip 0.83298ptdr.

Since
\phi_{i}|_{\mathbb{S}^{m+n-1}}
is
C^{\infty}
(it is a smooth bump function for a “band” on the sphere of width
\sim 2^{-i}
), it has an absolutely convergent spherical harmonic expansion

\phi_{i}(\bm{\nu})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{d(\ell)}a_{i,\ell,k}Y_{\ell,k}(\bm{\nu})

with rapidly-decaying coefficients. In fact, since
\phi_{i}(\bm{\nu})=\phi\big(2^{i+1}\big|\bm{\nu}^{(2)}\big|\big)
, there exist
a_{\ell,k}
such that
|a_{i,\ell,k}|\sim 2^{-in}|a_{k,\ell}|
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
—a fact we shall use shortly.

Now, the identity

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m+n-1}}Y_{\ell,k}(\bm{\nu})e^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}r\bm{\nu}\cdot\mathbf{w}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{m+n-1}(\bm{\nu})

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt=(2\pi)^{(m+n-1)/2}(-i)^{\ell}\frac{J_{\ell+(m+n-2)/2}(2\pi r|\mathbf{w}|)}{(r|\mathbf{w}|)^{(m+n-2)/2}}Y_{\ell,k}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{w}}{|\mathbf{w}|}\right)

is standard, from which it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m+n-1}}\phi_{i}(\bm{\nu})e^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}r\bm{\nu}\cdot\mathbf{w}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{m+n-1}(\bm{\nu})

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt=(2\pi)^{(m+n-1)/2}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{d(\ell)}(-i)^{\ell}a_{i,\ell,k}\frac{J_{\ell+(m+n-2)/2}(2\pi r|\mathbf{w}|)}{(r|\mathbf{w}|)^{(m+n-2)/2}}Y_{\ell,k}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{w}}{|\mathbf{w}|}\right).

Plugging this back into (
3.11
) then yields

\displaystyle\widehat{g_{i}}(\mathbf{w})\simeq_{m,N}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{d(\ell)}(-i)^{\ell}a_{i,\ell,k}\hskip 0.83298ptY_{\ell,k}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{w}}{|\mathbf{w}|}\right)\!\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\sigma-1}\frac{J_{\ell+(m+n-2)/2}(2\pi r|\mathbf{w}|)}{(r|\mathbf{w}|)^{(m+n-2)/2}}\,dr

\displaystyle\quad=|\mathbf{w}|^{-(m+n-2)/2}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{d(\ell)}(-i)^{\ell}a_{i,\ell,k}\hskip 0.83298ptY_{\ell,k}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{w}}{|\mathbf{w}|}\right)\!\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\sigma-(m+n)/2}J_{\ell+(m+n-2)/2}(2\pi r|\mathbf{w}|)\,dr.

The integral in the second line is precisely the Hankel transform of order
\ell+(m+n-2)/2
of the monomial
r^{\sigma-(m+n-2)/2}
, and it can be expressed simply in terms of the gamma function as

\frac{2^{\sigma-(m+n)/2}}{(2\pi|\mathbf{w}|)^{\sigma-(m+n+2)/2}}\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\ell+\sigma+1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\ell-\sigma+m+n}{2}\right)}.

Thus, we have shown that

\widehat{g_{i}}(\mathbf{w})\simeq_{m,N,s,\beta}|\mathbf{w}|^{-\sigma}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{d(\ell)}(-i)^{\ell}a_{i,\ell,k}\hskip 0.83298ptY_{\ell,k}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{w}}{|\mathbf{w}|}\right)\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\ell+\sigma+1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\ell-\sigma+m+n}{2}\right)}.

Now consider
\partial^{\bm{\alpha}}\widehat{g_{i}}(\mathbf{w})
for
\bm{\alpha}=\big(\bm{\alpha}^{(1)},\bm{\alpha}^{(2)}\big)\in\mathbb{N}^{m}\times\mathbb{N}^{n}
. We first have the elementary estimate

\partial^{\bm{\alpha}}|\mathbf{w}|^{-\sigma}\lesssim_{|\bm{\alpha}|}\sigma^{|\bm{\alpha}|}|\mathbf{w}|^{-\sigma-|\bm{\alpha}|}\sim_{\beta,s,|\bm{\alpha}|}|\mathbf{w}|^{-\sigma-|\bm{\alpha}|}.

Denoting by
P_{\ell,k}
an extension of
Y_{\ell,k}
to a harmonic polynomial of degree
\ell
on
\mathbb{R}^{m+n}
, so that
Y_{\ell,k}(\mathbf{w}/|\mathbf{w}|)=|\mathbf{w}|^{-\ell}P_{\ell,k}(\mathbf{w})
, we can similarly estimate the derivatives of the
Y_{\ell,k}
factor:

\displaystyle\bigg|\partial^{\bm{\alpha}}Y_{\ell,k}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{w}}{|\mathbf{w}|}\right)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|\bm{\gamma}|\leq|\bm{\alpha}|}\binom{|\bm{\alpha}|}{|\bm{\gamma}|}\big|\partial^{\bm{\gamma}}|\mathbf{w}|^{-\ell}\big|\big|\partial^{\bm{\alpha}-\bm{\gamma}}P_{\ell,k}(\mathbf{w})\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{|\bm{\alpha}|}|\mathbf{w}|^{-\ell-|\bm{\alpha}|}\cdot\ell^{|\bm{\alpha}|}|\mathbf{w}|^{\ell}\|Y_{\ell,k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{m+n-1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell^{|\bm{\alpha}|+(m+n-2)/2}|\mathbf{w}|^{-|\bm{\alpha}|}.

The bound
\|Y_{\ell,k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{m+n-1})}\lesssim\ell^{(m+n-2)/2}
arises from the Sturm–Liouville construction of the basis of spherical harmonics from the Laplacian on
\mathbb{S}^{m+n-1}
. Together, the above inequalities, the asymptotic formula

\frac{\Gamma(x+r)}{\Gamma(x)}\sim x^{r}

for all real
x,r\geq 0
, and the Leibniz rule give

\displaystyle\big|\partial^{\bm{\alpha}}\widehat{g_{i}}(\mathbf{w})\big|

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim_{m,N,\beta,s}|\mathbf{w}|^{-\sigma-|\bm{\alpha}|}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{d(\ell)}|a_{i,\ell,k}|\ell^{|\bm{\alpha}|+(m+n-2)/2}|\mathbf{w}|^{-\ell}\hskip-0.83298pt\left(\frac{\ell-\sigma+m+n}{2}\right)^{\!\sigma-(m+n-1)/2}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim_{m,N}|\mathbf{w}|^{-\sigma-|\bm{\alpha}|}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{d(\ell)}|a_{i,\ell,k}|\hskip 0.83298pt\ell^{|\bm{\alpha}|+\sigma-1/2}|\mathbf{w}|^{-\ell}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim|\mathbf{w}|^{-\sigma-|\bm{\alpha}|}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{d(\ell)}2^{-in}|a_{\ell,k}|\hskip 0.83298pt\ell^{|\bm{\alpha}|+\sigma-1/2}|\mathbf{w}|^{-\ell}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim 2^{i(|\bm{\alpha}|-n)}|\mathbf{w}|^{-\sigma-|\bm{\alpha}|},

where in the final line we have used the fact that the coefficients
|a_{\ell,k}|
are rapidly-decaying and, consequently, are
\lesssim_{\bm{\alpha}}\tfrac{1}{d(\ell)}\ell^{-|\bm{\alpha}|-\sigma-1/2}\sim_{m,N}\ell^{-|\bm{\alpha}|-\sigma-m-n+1/2}
. The factor
|\mathbf{w}|^{-\ell}
can be eliminated independently of
\mathbf{w}
bounded away from
\mathbf{0}
. In particular, evaluating at
\mathbf{w}=\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega})-\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\zeta})
gives, by (
2.9
),

\displaystyle\big|\partial_{\mathbf{w}}^{\bm{\alpha}}\widehat{g_{i}}\big(\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega})-\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\zeta})\big)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta,s,|\bm{\alpha}|}2^{i(|\bm{\alpha}|-n)}\big(1+d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{\beta+1}\big)^{-\sigma-|\bm{\alpha}|}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{i(|\bm{\alpha}|-n)}d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{-(1+\beta)(\sigma+|\bm{\alpha}|)}.

Recall that our intention was to establish bounds on the partial derivatives of
\gamma_{i}
. It is clear that, for every multi-index
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
, the corresponding partial derivative
\partial_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\bm{\alpha}}\gamma_{i}(\bm{\lambda})
is a finite sum of terms the form

\partial_{\mathbf{w}}^{\bm{\alpha_{0}}}\widehat{g_{i}}(\mathbf{w})\,\partial_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\bm{\alpha_{1}}}\hskip-0.83298pt\big[\Psi_{1,{\bm{\lambda}}}(\bm{\omega})-\Psi_{1,{\bm{\lambda}}}(\bm{\zeta})\big]\cdots\,\partial_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\bm{\alpha_{m+n}}}\hskip-0.83298pt\big[\Psi_{m+n,{\bm{\lambda}}}(\bm{\omega})-\Psi_{m+n,{\bm{\lambda}}}(\bm{\zeta})\big]\hskip 0.83298pt\Big|_{\mathbf{w}=\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega})-\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\zeta})}\,,

where
\Psi_{j,\bm{\lambda}}

j
th

|\bm{\alpha_{j}}|\leq|\bm{\alpha}|

1

Therefore, to prove the desired estimates on the partial derivatives of

\displaystyle\big|\partial_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\bm{\alpha_{j}}}\hskip-0.83298pt\big[\Psi_{j,\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega})-\Psi_{j,\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\zeta})\big]\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\partial_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\bm{\alpha_{j}}}\big[d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})\hskip 0.83298pt\partial_{\lambda_{k}}\hskip-0.83298pt\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})\big]\big|

\displaystyle=d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})\big|\partial_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\bm{\alpha_{j}}}\partial_{\lambda_{k}}\hskip-0.83298pt\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta,s,|\bm{\alpha}|}d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})\cdot d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{-\beta(|\bm{\alpha_{j}}|+1)}

\displaystyle\lesssim d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{1-\beta(|\bm{\alpha}|+1)},

and similarly when

such factors, which together with the factor

\displaystyle\big|\partial_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\bm{\alpha}}\gamma_{i}(\bm{\lambda})\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta,s,|\bm{\alpha}|}2^{i(|\bm{\alpha_{0}}|-n)}d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{-(1+\beta)(\sigma+|\bm{\alpha_{0}}|)}\cdot d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{|\bm{\alpha_{0}}|(1-\beta(|\bm{\alpha}|+1))}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{|\bm{\alpha}|}2^{i(|\bm{\alpha}|-n)}d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{-\sigma-\beta(|\bm{\alpha}|^{2}+2|\bm{\alpha}|+\sigma)}.

On the other hand, by the basic uniform estimates on

\big|\partial_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\bm{\alpha_{j}}}\hskip-0.83298pt\big[\Psi_{j,\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega})-\Psi_{j,\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\zeta})\big]\big|\leq\big|\partial_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\bm{\alpha}}\partial_{\lambda_{k}}\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega})\big|+\big|\partial_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\bm{\alpha}}\partial_{\lambda_{k}}\Pi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\zeta})\big|\lesssim_{K,|\bm{\alpha}|,\beta}1.

This gives rise to the bound

\displaystyle\big|\partial_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\bm{\alpha}}\gamma_{i}(\bm{\lambda})\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta,K,|\bm{\alpha}|}2^{i(|\bm{\alpha}|-n)}d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{-(1+\beta)(\sigma+|\bm{\alpha}|)},

which improves upon the previous when

and

. Thus, with

\big|\partial_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\bm{\alpha}}\gamma_{i}(\bm{\lambda})\big|\lesssim_{\beta,K,s,|\bm{\alpha}|}2^{i(|\bm{\alpha}|-n)}d(\bm{\omega},\bm{\zeta})^{-\tau}.

Recalling the definition of

for all

Step 4.

\sigma\leq\frac{t}{1+a_{1}\beta}\quad\text{and}\quad\min\big\{\sigma+\beta(L^{2}+2L+t),(1+\beta)(\sigma+L)\big\}\leq t,

\displaystyle=N+\left(2n-N-\frac{1}{2}\beta^{1/3}\right)

In addition, the first inequality in (

The full version

.

5.
Applications

5.1.
Vertical sections of the Heisenberg group

Equip
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
with the standard symplectic form
\omega\!:\mathbb{R}^{2n}\times\mathbb{R}^{2n}\to\mathbb{R}
and let
G_{h}(n,m)\subseteq\mathrm{\mathbf{Gr}}(2n,m)
be the
isotropic Grassmannian
—the collection of
m
-dimensional subspaces
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2n}
such that
\omega(V,V)=\{0\}
(the
isotropic subspaces
of
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
). It is well-known that
G_{h}(n,m)
is a Riemannian submanifold of
\mathrm{\mathbf{Gr}}(2n,m)
of dimension
2nm-\tfrac{m(3m-1)}{2}
. By Hovila
[
4
]
Theorem 1.1, the family
(\pi_{\scalebox{0.65}{$V$}})_{V\in G_{h}(n,m)}
of orthogonal projections is transversal and strongly regular of degree
0
. (Hovila only claims that (
2.7
) holds through
|\bm{\alpha}|=2
, but it is clear that it also holds for all
\bm{\alpha}
. In fact, strong regularity is inherited directly from
(\pi_{\scalebox{0.65}{$V$}})_{V\in\mathrm{\mathbf{Gr}}(2n,m)}
, so the only novelty is that only the transversality condition (
2.5
) also holds.)

The
n
th
Heisenberg group

\mathbb{H}^{n}
is a Lie group that, under a pointwise identification
\mathbb{H}^{n}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2n}\times\mathbb{R}
, carries the group operation

(\mathbf{z},\tau)\ast(\mathbf{w},\sigma):=(\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{w},\tau+\sigma+2\hskip 0.83298pt\omega(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w})).

With the
Heisenberg metric

d_{\mathrm{H}}\big((\mathbf{z},\tau),(\mathbf{w},\sigma)\big):=\big\|(\mathbf{z},\tau)^{-1}\ast(\mathbf{w},\sigma)\big\|_{\mathrm{H}},\quad\|(\mathbf{z},\tau)\|_{\mathrm{H}}:=\big(\|\mathbf{z}\|^{4}+\tau^{2}\big)^{1/4},

\mathbb{H}^{n}
has Hausdorff dimension
2n+2
. Roughly, this is because the Heisenberg metric
d_{\mathrm{H}}
restricted to the
(2n+1)
st
coordinate axis is the square root of the Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{R}
, so the contributions to dimension of a set owing to its extent in this vertical direction are doubled.

Call a subgroup
\mathbb{V}:=V\times\{0\}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
with
V\in G_{h}(n,m)
a
horizontal subgroup
and define the
horizontal projection

\pi_{\mathbb{V}}\!:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{V}
by
\pi_{\mathbb{V}}(\mathbf{z},\tau):=(\pi_{\scalebox{0.65}{$V$}}(\mathbf{z}),0)
. Since
{d_{\mathrm{H}}|}_{\mathbb{V}}
is precisely the Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{V}
, such a subgroup has Hausdorff dimension
m
, whereas the fibers of
\pi_{\mathbb{V}}
have dimension
2n-m+2
rather than
2n-m+1
. The isotropic Grassmannian parametrizes the horizontal projections in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
via
V\mapsto\pi_{\mathbb{V}}
, but
(\pi_{\mathbb{V}})_{V\in G_{h}(n,m)}
is not itself a transversal family. However, natural mapping of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
onto
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
cannot decrease the dimension of a set by more than
2
, for which reason Hovila’s transversality result nevertheless allows for the application of Theorem
1.2
to horizontal projections.

Corollary 5.1.

For every Borel set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2n}
with
0<\mathcal{H}^{s}(A)<\infty
for some
s>m
,

\displaystyle\hdim\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B\subseteq A\,\text{Borel}\\
\mathcal{H}^{s}(B)>0\end{subarray}}\Big\{V\in G_{h}(n,m)\!:\mathcal{H}^{m}\big(\big\{\mathbf{x}\in V\!:\hdim\!\big(\pi_{\scalebox{0.65}{$V$}}^{-1}(\mathbf{x})\cap B\big)=s-m\big\}\big)=0\Big\}

\displaystyle\hskip 275.13809pt\leq 2nm-\frac{m(3m+1)}{2}-s.

Consequently, if

is Borel with

for some

, then

\displaystyle\hdim\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B^{\prime}\subseteq A^{\prime}\,\text{Borel}\\
\mathcal{H}^{s}(B^{\prime})>0\end{subarray}}\Big\{V\in G_{h}(n,m)\!:\mathcal{H}^{m}\big(\big\{\mathbf{p}\in V\!:\hdim\!\big(\pi_{\mathbb{V}}^{-1}(\mathbf{p})\cap B^{\prime}\big)=s-m\big\}\big)=0\Big\}

Theorem
1.2
states only that the set

has positive

-measure for all but a few

never

-measure (see the discussion following Theorem

), for which reason we are guaranteed that, generically,

is
exactly

s-m
for a positive-measure set of

Since
\pi_{\mathbb{V}}^{-1}(\mathbf{p})=\mathbb{V}^{\perp}\ast\mathbf{p}
, Corollary

is a statement about the Hausdorff dimension of slices by right cosets of the

of the Heisenberg group. This improves on
[

]
Theorem 1.5 concerning slices in the Heisenberg group.

Before beginning the proof of Lemma

First we require a somewhat cumbersome statement about the distribution of critical points of

Lemma A.3  (Peres–Schlag
[
11

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}\sum_{\bm{\sigma},\mathbf{q}}\big(D_{\bm{\lambda}}F_{\bm{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}}\circ F_{\bm{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}\big)^{-L-j}\mathbf{v}_{\bm{\alpha},j}(\bm{\sigma},\mathbf{q})\prod_{i=1}^{j}\big(\partial^{\bm{\sigma}_{i}}(F_{\bm{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}})_{q_{i}}\big)\circ F_{\bm{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}\sum_{\bm{\sigma},\mathbf{q}}\big\|D_{\bm{\lambda}}F_{\bm{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}}\circ F_{\bm{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}\big\|^{-L-j}|\mathbf{v}_{\bm{\alpha},j}(\bm{\sigma},\mathbf{q})|\prod_{i=1}^{j}\big|\big(\partial^{\bm{\sigma}_{i}}(F_{\bm{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}})_{q_{i}}\big)\circ F_{\bm{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}\big|

Let

\lesssim_{L}1

Lemma A.4  (Multivariate Faà di Bruno formula
[
2
]
).

Let

. If

for all

.

Here,
[\mathbf{z}]^{\bm{\rho}}:=z_{1}^{\rho_{1}}\cdots z_{M}^{\rho_{M}}

\displaystyle\big|\partial_{\mathbf{u}}^{\bm{\alpha}}\big(\rho_{1}\circ F_{\bm{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}\big)(\mathbf{u})\big|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{1\leq|\bm{\gamma}|\leq|\bm{\alpha}|}\big|\partial_{\bm{\lambda}^{\prime}}^{\bm{\gamma}}\rho_{1}(\bm{\lambda}^{\prime})\big|\Big|_{\bm{\lambda}^{\prime}=F_{\bm{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}(\mathbf{u})}\sum_{\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau}}b_{\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau}}\prod_{i=1}^{|\bm{\alpha}|}\Big|\big[\partial_{\mathbf{u}}^{\bm{\tau}_{i}}F_{\bm{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}(\mathbf{u})\big]^{\bm{\sigma}_{i}}\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau}}b_{\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau}}\hskip 0.83298ptr^{-4\beta|\bm{\alpha}|}\lesssim_{|\bm{\alpha}|}r^{-4\beta|\bm{\alpha}|}.

This same computation and the derivative bounds in (

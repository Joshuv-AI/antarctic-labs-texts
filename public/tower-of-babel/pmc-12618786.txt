Prolonged neoadjuvant hormonal therapy enhances IL-17 A/STAT3-mediated inflammation in prostate cancer
Authors: Yirui Wei, Yifan Chu, Siqi Wang, Dawei Xie, Hao Wang, Weifeng He, Pushen Yang, Jianwen Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective To investigate the impact of neoadjuvant hormonal therapy (NHT) on interleukin‑17 A (IL‑17 A) signaling and inflammation in prostate cancer (PCa), and to explore the role of the IL‑17 A/signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) axis in tumor progression after androgen deprivation.Methods Paired prostate tissue samples from 27 patients receiving ≥ 3 months of NHT were analyzed. Histological inflammation was assessed by hematoxylin-eosin staining. Immunohistochemistry was used to evaluate IL-17 A, interleukin‑17 receptor A (IL‑17RA), interleukin‑17 receptor C (IL‑17RC), STAT3, and androgen receptor (AR). Statistical analyses were performed to examine differences in expression and correlations between markers.Results Chronic inflammation significantly increased in tumor tissues after NHT (P < 0.05). IL-17 A and IL-17RC expression were higher in tumor tissues than adjacent benign tissues (P < 0.001), with further upregulation after NHT (P < 0.05). Longer NHT duration (≥ 6 months) was associated with elevated IL-17 A and IL-17RC levels. IL-17 A expression positively correlated with STAT3 (r = 0.388, P = 0.045), but not with androgen receptor (AR).Conclusions Prolonged NHT enhances IL-17 A/IL-17RC-mediated inflammation in prostate cancer, potentially promoting tumor progression through STAT3 activation. Targeting the IL-17 A/STAT3 axis may offer therapeutic potential for advanced prostate cancer, although further validation is required before NHT can be incorporated into clinical guidelines.Keywords: IL-17A, Prostate cancer, Neoadjuvant hormonal therapy, STAT3, Inflammation

Introduction

Prostate cancer is the second most commonly diagnosed malignancy in men and a leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide, with a steadily increasing incidence [1]. While localized PCa can be effectively treated with surgery or radiation, patients with advanced disease are commonly managed with androgen deprivation therapy (ADT), which inhibits androgen receptor signaling and induces the regression of androgen-sensitive tumor cells. However, despite the initial efficacy of ADT, nearly all patients eventually develop castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC) within 18–24 months [2]. CRPC, which is characterized by persistent tumor growth despite low androgen levels, presents a major therapeutic challenge due to its molecular complexity and the lack of effective treatment options.

Recent studies have revealed that chronic inflammation plays a pivotal role in the progression of both hormone-sensitive and castration-resistant prostate cancer. Emerging technologies such as spatial transcriptomics and multiplex immunohistochemistry have further advanced our understanding of inflammatory cell localization and cytokine signaling within the prostate cancer microenvironment [3]. IL-17 A, a proinflammatory cytokine primarily produced by T helper 17 (Th17) cells, γδT cells, and natural killer (NK) cells, has emerged as a critical mediator of immune-driven inflammation in the tumor microenvironment [4]. IL-17 A activates key signaling pathways, including nuclear factor‑κB (NF‑κB), CCAAT/enhancer‑binding protein β (C/EBPβ), CCAAT/enhancer‑binding protein δ (C/EBPδ), and mitogen‑activated protein kinase (MAPK), and has been shown to promote the development of several malignancies, including breast, lung, colon, and prostate cancer [5–7]. Notably, IL-17 A also contributes to tumor angiogenesis by upregulating vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) [8], facilitates epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) through matrix metalloproteinase-7 (MMP7) [9], and recruits myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), which help establish an immune-tolerant tumor microenvironment [10].

Despite increasing evidence supporting the pro-tumorigenic effects of IL-17 A, its precise role in the progression of PCa, particularly in the transition from hormone-sensitive to castration-resistant forms, remains poorly understood. Our study suggests that IL-17 A may exacerbate prostate cancer progression following ADT through upregulation of the STAT3 signaling pathway [11, 12]. This finding raises important questions about the potential dual role of IL-17 A in prostate cancer: while IL-17 A may promote immune activation in some contexts, it could also contribute to immune evasion and treatment resistance in others. Thus, understanding the immune microenvironmental changes induced by NHT and their contribution to CRPC development may provide insights into why some patients exhibit treatment resistance despite ADT.

Moreover, IL-17 A and STAT3 act in concert to amplify inflammatory responses in the tumor microenvironment, potentially driving tumor progression via a self-sustaining feedback loop [7, 13, 14]. Although current guidelines such as those from the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) and the European Association of Urology (EAU) do not recommend NHT before prostatectomy, several studies have investigated its use in high-risk or locally advanced prostate cancer. However, its clinical efficacy remains controversial [15–17]. Our findings suggest that in certain cases, NHT may exacerbate IL-17 A-mediated inflammation, potentially accelerating disease progression. These results underscore the importance of individualized treatment strategies that account for the immune-inflammatory landscape of the tumor microenvironment. Future therapeutic paradigms may benefit from integrating immune modulation with endocrine therapy to optimize oncologic and functional outcomes.

Materials and methods

Materials

Tissue samples were obtained from 27 patients who received NHT between March 2020 and March 2024. Pre-treatment prostate biopsy specimens and post-treatment samples from radical prostatectomy (RP) were analyzed. Patients were consecutively enrolled from a single tertiary care center to ensure consistency in treatment protocols. All patients were classified as having high-risk or locally advanced prostate cancer based on NCCN criteria, and the indication for NHT was made by a multidisciplinary team following comprehensive clinical evaluation. All patients provided written informed consent prior to participation. All eligible patients during the study period who met institutional criteria for NHT were uniformly treated according to protocol. Due to the lack of guideline recommendations, the variation in NHT duration was primarily influenced by surgical scheduling, individual clinical response (e.g., PSA kinetics, imaging), comorbidity optimization, and patient preference. In selected cases, prolonged NHT (> 6 months) was continued based on favorable treatment tolerance and multidisciplinary consensus. Patients’ ages ranged from 50 to 78 years, with a mean age of 67 years. The duration of NHT ranged from 3 to 10 months, with a median duration of 4.5 months (interquartile range: 4–7 months). To evaluate the potential impact of treatment duration on inflammatory markers, patients were stratified into two subgroups: 3–6 months and > 6 months of NHT. The cutoff of 6 months was selected post hoc based on the upper tertile of treatment length distribution in our cohort. Although no formal sample size calculation was performed due to the retrospective and exploratory nature of this study, appropriate statistical methods were used to analyze marker expression and identify trends. The NHT regimen included bicalutamide 50 mg daily in combination with either leuprorelin 3.75 mg monthly or goserelin 3.6 mg monthly. The Gleason score of prostate biopsy specimens was ≥ 7.

Methods

Tissue specimens were collected as outlined in Fig. 1. Tissue specimens were fixed in 10% buffered formalin and embedded in paraffin blocks. After hematoxylin and eosin staining, specimens were selected for immunohistochemical analysis. Primary antibodies used included IL-17 A (Affinity, DF6127, 1:500), IL-17RA (Affinity, DF3602, 1:200), IL-17RC (Bioss, bs-2607R, 1:300), STAT3 (Affinity, AF6294, 1:100), and AR (ZSGB-BIO, ZA-0554, working dilution). Chronic inflammation in prostate cancer tissues was assessed using the histological classification system for chronic prostatitis [18]. Grades 1 to 3 for location, range, and degree of inflammation were defined based on the histopathological classification system for chronic prostatitis. In general, Grade 1 indicates mild and focal inflammation, Grade 2 corresponds to moderate and multifocal involvement, and Grade 3 reflects severe or diffuse inflammation affecting large areas of the prostate tissue [18]. Five randomly selected high-magnification fields (×400) were evaluated, and immunohistochemical staining was scored based on staining intensity multiplied by the percentage of positive cells. Staining intensity was scored semi-quantitatively as follows: 0 = none, 1 = weak (light yellow), 2 = moderate (brown-yellow), and 3 = strong (dark brown) cytoplasmic staining. Two experienced pathologists, blinded to clinical data, independently evaluated the staining. Inter-observer agreement was assessed using Cohen’s kappa coefficient, demonstrating substantial concordance (κ = 0.93). Discrepancies were resolved by joint review and consensus.

Flowchart of patient enrollment, NHT regimen, grouping, and analysis

Data analysis was performed using R version 4.2.3. Inflammation scores and protein expression levels were compared before and after NHT using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test for paired samples. The Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare protein expression between tumor and adjacent tissues, as well as between different clinicopathological parameters. Spearman correlation analysis was performed to assess the relationship between protein staining results. The study adhered strictly to the ethical standards of the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the Ethics Committee of Beijing Chaoyang Hospital (approval number 2020-science-299-1).

Results

Effects of NHT on chronic inflammation in prostate cancer tissue

After NHT, 15 patients (55.6%) showed an increase in the range of inflammation from grade 1 to grade 2, and 19 patients (70.3%) exhibited an increase in the degree of inflammation from grade 1 to grade 2. Statistically significant differences were observed in both the range and degree of inflammation between the two groups (P < 0.05), indicating a significant increase in chronic inflammation in prostate tissue following NHT (Table 1).

Expression and staining scores of IL-17 A, IL-17RA, and IL-17RC before and after NHT

The IL-17 A staining score before NHT was 8 (4, 9.6), predominantly moderately positive. After NHT, the IL-17 A staining score increased to 12 [8, 12], with most cases showing strong positivity. The IL-17RC staining score before NHT was 4 (2.2, 6), predominantly weakly positive, and increased to 7.2 (4.8, 8) after NHT, with most cases showing moderate positivity. Levels of IL-17 A and IL-17RC after NHT were significantly higher than before NHT (P < 0.05), whereas IL-17RA remained negative in both pre- and post-NHT prostate cancer tissues, with no significant difference (Fig. 3).

Influence of clinicopathological parameters on IL-17 A, IL-17RA, and IL-17RC expression

Patients were subdivided into groups based on various clinicopathological parameters for statistical analysis (Table 2). No significant associations were found between age, BMI, Gleason score, or T stage and the expression levels of IL-17 A, IL-17RA, or IL-17RC. However, NHT duration significantly influenced IL-17 A and IL-17RC expression. Patients receiving NHT for ≥ 6 months showed significantly higher IL-17 A and IL-17RC levels than those treated for 3–5 months (P < 0.05).

Effects of IL-17 A on STAT3 and AR expression in prostate tissue after NHT

Patients were categorized into two groups based on IL-17 A expression after NHT: strong positive (score ≥ 9) and weak to moderate positive (score 3–8). STAT3 expression was significantly higher in the strong IL-17 A group compared to the weak-moderate IL-17 A group (P = 0.046). A significant positive correlation between IL-17 A and STAT3 expression was found (P < 0.05, r = 0.388), while no significant correlation was observed between IL-17 A and AR expression (Fig. 4).

Discussion

Prostate cancer is the second most common malignancy in men worldwide, and for advanced cases, ADT remains the standard of care [1]. ADT works by inhibiting androgen receptor signaling through various mechanisms, disrupting communication between androgen receptors and prostate cancer cells, leading to the atrophy and apoptosis of androgen-sensitive cells in the prostate [19]. However, despite its initial efficacy, nearly all patients ultimately develop CRPC, presenting a significant challenge in the treatment of advanced PCa. Recent studies have increasingly highlighted the immunomodulatory consequences of neoadjuvant hormonal therapy in prostate cancer. Specifically, ADT has been shown to alter the tumor immune microenvironment by increasing the infiltration of immunosuppressive cells such as MDSCs and regulatory T cells (Tregs), while reducing the cytotoxic activity of immune effector cells [20]. This shift contributes to a more immunosuppressive environment, potentially facilitating tumor progression. Furthermore, IL-17 A, a pro-inflammatory cytokine produced predominantly by Th17 cells, has been implicated in promoting tumor-associated inflammation and angiogenesis. IL-17 A can stimulate the production of VEGF and other pro-angiogenic factors, thereby enhancing tumor vascularization and growth [21].

In our study, we found that STAT3 expression was significantly higher in patients with strong IL-17 A expression compared to those with moderate or weak IL-17 A expression (P = 0.046). In contrast, no significant difference in AR expression was observed between the two groups. A positive correlation between IL-17 A and STAT3 expression was found (P = 0.045, r = 0.388). These results partially support our hypothesis that IL-17 A-mediated inflammation, following ADT, may contribute to prostate cancer progression through activation of STAT3. Beyond prostate cancer, the IL-17 A/STAT3 signaling axis has been extensively studied in other malignancies, where it is similarly implicated in promoting tumor progression, immune evasion, and resistance to therapy [22, 23]. Therefore, therapeutic strategies targeting this pathway may have translational relevance beyond prostate cancer and warrant further exploration.

Circulating IL-6 levels have been shown to increase in prostate cancer patients following long-term ADT, and this increase has been associated with fatigue related to ADT treatment [24]. However, there is limited research on the effects of ADT on IL-17 A expression in prostate cancer. In our study, we observed that both IL-17 A and IL-17RC expression levels were significantly higher in tumor tissues compared to adjacent para-cancerous tissues (P < 0.001). Moreover, minimal expression of IL-17RA was found in prostate cancer tissues. These findings suggest that IL-17 A continues to exert its effects in tumor tissue following ADT, potentially through its binding to IL-17RC rather than IL-17RA, which aligns with previous studies. This selective increase in IL-17RC may amplify IL-17 A signaling and sustain tumor-associated inflammation under androgen-deprived conditions [4]. Furthermore, the levels of IL-17 A and IL-17RC were significantly elevated after ≥ 3 months of NHT compared to pre-treatment levels (P < 0.05), and the longer the duration of NHT, the stronger the IL-17 A-mediated immune response. Notably, different isoforms of IL-17RC are differentially expressed in various prostate cancer cell lines [25], highlighting the need for further investigation into its role in prostate cancer progression and androgen-independent transformation.

Activation of STAT3 is critical for Th17 cell differentiation and has been shown to be significantly activated following ADT treatment [26, 27]. In animal models, inhibition of activated STAT3 has been demonstrated to significantly slow prostate tumor growth after castration [27]. In our study, we found that STAT3 expression was significantly higher in patients with strong IL-17 A expression compared to those with moderate or weak IL-17 A expression (P = 0.046). In contrast, no significant difference in AR expression was observed between the two groups. A positive correlation between IL-17 A and STAT3 expression was found (P = 0.045, r = 0.388). These results partially support our hypothesis that IL-17 A-mediated inflammation, following ADT, may contribute to prostate cancer progression through activation of STAT3. Importantly, these findings do not contradict the original therapeutic rationale of ADT, which remains effective in suppressing androgen-sensitive tumor growth. Rather, they suggest that prolonged androgen deprivation may induce compensatory immune alterations, activating pro-inflammatory pathways—particularly the IL-17 A/STAT3 axis—that potentially facilitate tumor adaptation or immune evasion in a subset of patients. This immunologic shift has been proposed in recent studies investigating the broader immunomodulatory effects of hormone deprivation, underscoring the importance of treatment duration, immune context, and tumor heterogeneity when evaluating ADT outcomes. Notably, while IL-17 A expression positively correlated with STAT3 activation in our cohort, no significant association with AR expression was observed, possibly due to sample size limitations or methodological constraints. Therefore, the incorporation of neoadjuvant hormonal therapy into clinical guidelines for preoperative use requires further large-scale, prospective studies to validate its safety and long-term efficacy [20].

Moreover, the inflammatory response in prostate cancer tissues after NHT is also a concern due to its impact on surgical outcomes [28]. Adhesions and poor surgical visibility, often caused by this inflammation, complicate tumor resection in the neoadjuvant setting. Not all tumors are responsive to NHT, and those that do not shrink or are resistant to therapy add further difficulty to treatment. Our findings suggest that IL-17 A-mediated inflammation might explain the poor therapeutic responses observed in some patients undergoing ADT. Based on these results, the application of IL-17 A-neutralizing antibodies may offer a promising adjunct to ADT, particularly for patients exhibiting resistance to traditional therapies. Further studies are required to explore the potential of IL-17 A-related neutralizing antibodies in enhancing the efficacy of depot treatments, especially in patients who are insensitive to standard therapies. Our cohort consisted of high-risk or locally advanced PCa patients who were selectively treated with NHT prior to surgery. While this design allowed for the investigation of treatment-induced inflammatory changes, it limits the generalizability of our findings to lower-risk PCa patients or those managed with other modalities. Future multi-center studies with broader patient inclusion are warranted to validate these observations.

Our findings may help explain why current guidelines, such as those from the EAU and NCCN, do not recommend NHT before prostatectomy. The observed IL-17 A–mediated inflammation and associated tissue remodeling suggest that prolonged NHT may induce a pro-inflammatory microenvironment, potentially contributing to periprostatic fibrosis, increased surgical difficulty, or suboptimal therapeutic responses in certain patients [29]. These mechanisms align with clinical observations of adhesion formation and unpredictable tumor shrinkage during NHT. However, more and more studies are now preparing to incorporate preoperative neoadjuvant therapy into the clinical guidelines for prostate cancer [15]. Rather than contradicting existing recommendations, our results emphasize the need to better characterize the immune consequences of hormonal therapy. Targeting IL-17 A or its downstream pathways may offer a novel approach to mitigate the adverse immunologic effects of prolonged androgen deprivation. However, further studies are needed to evaluate whether such strategies can improve oncologic and functional outcomes without increasing perioperative risk.

Additionally, the median duration of NHT in our study was 4.5 months, and it remains unclear whether this duration of inflammation is sufficient to influence prostate cancer progression. Therefore, longer duration studies with larger sample sizes are needed to further investigate the relationship between IL-17 A-mediated immune responses and prostate cancer progression in the context of prolonged NHT. While our study focused on IL-17 A and its receptor IL-17RC, it is important to note that other Th17 cytokines, such as IL-17 F, IL-21, and IL-22, also contribute to inflammation in prostate cancer. Although not assessed in this study, their role in the inflammatory phenotype should be explored in future research. In particular, the upstream mechanisms underlying IL-17 A upregulation, such as IL-6, IL-23, and T cell infiltration, require further mechanistic investigation.

Conclusion

We observed a significant upregulation of IL-17 A and its receptor IL-17RC in prostate cancer tissues following NHT, with expression levels increasing proportionally with the duration of NHT. Elevated IL-17 A may be associated with tumor-promoting inflammatory signaling via the STAT3 pathway. These findings highlight a potential role of IL-17 A-mediated inflammation in tumor progression and immune modulation. Given the potential therapeutic implications, we propose that targeting IL-17 A in combination with endocrine therapy may offer a promising strategy for advanced prostate cancer, warranting further clinical investigation. However, incorporating neoadjuvant hormonal therapy into clinical guidelines will require further large-scale, prospective studies to confirm its safety, efficacy, and long-term oncologic benefit.

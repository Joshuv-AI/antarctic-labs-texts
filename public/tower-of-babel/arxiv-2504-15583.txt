Abstract
. In previous work [ 12 ] we introduced a version of the Fukaya algebra associated to a degeneration of a symplectic manifold, whose structure maps count collections of maps in the components of the degeneration satisfying matching conditions. In this paper, we introduce a further degeneration of the matching conditions (similar in spirit to Bourgeois’ version of symplectic field theory [ 3 ] ) which results in a split Fukaya algebra whose structure maps are, in good cases, sums of products over vertices of tropical graphs. In the case of toric Lagrangians contained in a toric component of the degeneration, an invariance argument implies the existence of projective Maurer-Cartan solutions, which gives an alternate proof of the unobstructedness result of Fukaya-Oh-Ohta-Ono [ 7 ] for toric manifolds. Our result also proves unobstructedness in more general cases, such as for toric Lagrangians in almost toric four-manifolds.

1.
Introduction

In our previous work
[
12
]
we studied the limit of the Fukaya
algebra under multi-directional neck stretching. The main result was
a homotopy equivalence between the Fukaya algebra
of a Lagrangian
in a smooth manifold, and a broken analog,
called the
broken Fukaya algebra
,
defined by counting broken maps
associated to the neck-stretching limit.
Broken maps
have to satisfy matching conditions at
the nodes, which means that the moduli space of broken maps is not an
honest product of the moduli spaces of the components of the broken
map, but rather a fiber product over a diagonal.

In this paper, we introduce a further degeneration of broken maps into
split maps. In split maps, there is no edge matching condition on a
subset of edges, called
split edges
. A homotopy equivalence
between the Fukaya algebras defined using broken maps and split maps
is constructed by deforming the edge matching condition at the split
edges. The maps with a deformed edge matching condition on split edges
are called
deformed maps
. Assuming that the matching conditions
are deformed in a generic direction,
the limit of
deformed maps then produces
split maps
. The tropical graph of a split map, called the
split tropical graph

does not have a matching condition at split edges, however the set of discrepancies from the matching condition is a cone containing the deformation direction.
The moduli spaces of split maps are honest products with respect to the subgraphs obtained by cutting
edges, rather than fiber products cut out by matching conditions. Our
approach of splitting the diagonal resembles Bourgeois’ approach

[
3
]
of degenerating matching conditions in symplectic field
theory, and the Fulton-Sturmfels formula
[
8
]
for the
splitting of a diagonal in a toric variety; the relations to these
results are explored in Section
4.2
.

We introduce some notation. Let
\mathfrak{X}
be the broken manifold
that is the neck-stretching limit of a symplectic manifold
X
. Let

L\subset X
be a Lagrangian submanifold that is disjoint from the
necks, and let
CF_{\operatorname{brok}}(\mathfrak{X},L)
be the broken Fukaya algebra.

Any map in a broken manifold, whether broken, deformed, or split, has
a
tropical symmetry group
, obtained by applying one of the
translational symmetries on each component in a way that preserves the
matching conditions.
The tropical symmetry group of a rigid
broken/deformed map (that is, maps of index zero) is finite.
For split maps, the tropical symmetry group is larger, because there is no matching condition on the split edges.
In particular,
for a
converging sequence of rigid deformed maps, the dimension of the
tropical symmetry group of the limit split map is equal to the
codimension of the matching condition at split nodes. We define the
composition maps of the
split Fukaya algebra

CF_{{\operatorname{split}}}(\mathfrak{X},L,{\eta_{\operatorname{gen}}})

of a broken manifold
\mathfrak{X}

via counts of tropical symmetry orbits
of split maps,
for which the discrepancy cone of the split tropical graph
contains the deformation direction
as in Definition
4.17
.
Split maps are informally described in Section
4.1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
{\eta_{\operatorname{gen}}}\in\mathfrak{t}
be a generic
direction of deformation. The broken Fukaya algebra

CF_{{\operatorname{brok}}}(\mathfrak{X},L)
is
A_{\infty}
-homotopy equivalent to the split
Fukaya algebra

After the first version of this preprint appeared, similar
results appeared Wu
[
13

in the algebro-geometric context. The

Using the split Fukaya algebra, we prove unobstructedness of certain
Lagrangian tori, generalizing a result of Fukaya-Oh-Ohta-Ono result

[
7
]
on Lagrangian torus orbits in toric varieties.
By our assumption that the Lagrangian
L
is away from the necks,

descends to a Lagrangian
submanifold in a component

of the broken manifold

Furthermore, such a Lagrangian

A tropical torus

moment fiber in a toric degeneration used in mirror symmetry, where
“discriminant” singularities are allowed as in Gross

[
10

Homotopy equivalence with the split Fukaya algebra is used to prove
that tropical tori are weakly unobstructed. We describe the term ‘weak
unobstructedness’: The geometric Fukaya algebra generated by the
critical points of a Morse function on the Lagrangian

enlarged to a Fukaya algebra

The cohomology

) of

2.1.
Broken manifold

We start by recalling the notion of a broken manifold associated with a tropical Hamiltonian action. Let
n>0
be an integer and
T\simeq(S^{1})^{n}
a torus with Lie
algebra
\mathfrak{t}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A
simplicial polyhedral decomposition

of
\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
is a collection

\mathcal{P}=\{P\subset\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}\}

P^{\circ}
cover
\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}

\sigma_{1}\cap\dots\cap\sigma_{k}

\mathcal{P}
that is a face
of each of the polytopes
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{k}

Any polytope
P
in a simplicial polyhedral decomposition
\mathcal{P}

corresponds to a sub-torus

whose Lie algebra is

\mathfrak{t}_{P}:=\operatorname{ann}(TP)
.
Thus
P
and
T_{P}
have complementary dimensions, and
T_{P}=\{\operatorname{Id}\}

for top-dimensional polytopes
P\in\mathcal{P}
.

Definition 2.1
.

(Tropical
Hamiltonian action) A

consists of

(a)

a simplicial polyhedral decomposition
\mathcal{P}

of
\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
, and

(b)

a compact symplectic manifold
(X,\omega_{X})

with a
tropical moment map

\Phi:X\to\mathfrak{t}^{\vee},

such for any
P\in\mathcal{P}
there is a neighborhood
U_{P}\subset X

of
\Phi^{-1}(P)
on which the projection

U_{P}\to\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}\to\mathfrak{t}_{P}^{\vee}

is a moment map for a free Hamiltonian action of a torus
T_{P}
.

We assume, without stating, that any polyhedral
decomposition we encounter possesses a cutting datum, which is described below.

Given a tropical Hamiltonian action

the output of a multiple cut

2.2.
Broken maps

Broken maps in a broken manifold arise as limits of sequences of holomorphic maps in a tropical manifold with neck-stretched almost complex structures.
A broken map
u
is modelled on a graph
\Gamma
and consists of a
collection of holomorphic maps and a tropical structure on
\Gamma
,
which is then called a

v
of

\Gamma

C_{v}^{\circ}:=C_{v}\backslash\{\text{interior nodes}\},\quad v\in\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma),

is
an irreducible component of a nodal curve

modelled on
\Gamma
,

P(v)\in\mathcal{P}
is a polytope, and thus,

is a

removable singularities when

is viewed as a map to the

\overline{\mathfrak{X}}_{P(v)}
. For any edge

of

\Gamma

is that the map

has a removable

(9)

\text{(Projected matching condition)}\\
(\pi_{\mathcal{T}(e)}^{\perp}\circ u_{v_{+}})(w_{e}^{+})=(\pi_{\mathcal{T}(e)}^{\perp}\circ u_{v_{-}})(w_{e}^{-})\in\mathfrak{X}_{P(e)}/T_{\mathcal{T}(e),\mathbb{C}}.

The quantities in the left-hand side and right-hand side of

evaluations

Broken maps from disks are required to satisfy a Lagrangian boundary condition.

2.5.
Convergence of broken maps

We describe the notion of Gromov convergence for sequences of broken maps.
The Gromov limit of a sequence of broken disks of type
\Gamma
may have configurations with a different tropical graph
\Gamma^{\prime}
, in which case, the graph
\Gamma
can be recovered from
\Gamma^{\prime}
by
collapsing a subset of edges. The edge collapse
\Gamma^{\prime}\to\Gamma

respects the tropical structure in the following sense:

A
tropical edge collapse
is a morphism of tropical graphs

\Gamma^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\kappa}\Gamma
that collapses a subset of edges

\operatorname{Edge}(\Gamma^{\prime})\backslash\operatorname{Edge}(\Gamma)
in
\Gamma^{\prime}
inducing a
surjective map on the vertex sets

\kappa:\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma^{\prime})\to\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma),

and satisfies the following conditions:

(a)

for any vertex
v\in\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma^{\prime})
,

P(v)\subseteq P(\kappa(v))
; and

(b)

the edge direction is unchanged for uncollapsed edges, i.e. if

\mathcal{T}
,
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
are the edge direction functions for
\Gamma
,

\Gamma^{\prime}
, then
\mathcal{T}(\kappa(e))=\mathcal{T}^{\prime}(e)
for any uncollapsed edge

e\in\operatorname{Edge}(\Gamma^{\prime})
.

Convergence of sequences of broken maps is modulo
translation in the target space, as some components
may escape into cylindrical ends in the limit. For example, for a sequence of maps
u_{\nu}
of type
\Gamma
, and a
vertex
v
of
\Gamma
, if the components

u_{\nu,v}:C_{\nu}\to X_{P(v)}
collapse into the
Q
-cylindrical end
for some
Q\subset P
as
\nu\to\infty
, the corresponding Gromov limit is given by the convergence of the sequence of maps
e^{-t_{\nu}(v)}u_{\nu,v}

where
t_{\nu}(v)\in\mathfrak{t}_{Q}
is a translation in the
Q
-cylinder (see (
6
)).
For any
\nu
, the collection of translations
t_{\nu,v}
ranging over all vertices
v
satisfies the direction condition, and is called a
relative translation sequence
(called so, because
the translations are ‘relative’ to positions in
\Gamma
) defined as below.

Definition 2.2
.

(Relative translations)
Suppose

\kappa:\Gamma^{\prime}\to\Gamma
is a tropical edge-collapse morphism.
For any vertex
v
of
\Gamma^{\prime}
, let

(15)

\operatorname{Cone}(\kappa,v):=\operatorname{Cone}_{P(\kappa(v))^{\vee}}(P(v)^{\vee})\\
:=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\{t-t_{0}\in\mathfrak{t}:t\in P(v)^{\vee},t_{0}\in P(\kappa v)^{\vee},\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\}\subset\mathfrak{t}_{P(v)}

A

or a
(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)
-translation is an element

t=(t(v)\in\operatorname{Cone}(\kappa,v))_{v\in\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma^{\prime})}

satisfying

(16)

\text{(Direction condition)}\quad t(v_{+})-t(v_{-})\in\begin{cases}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mathcal{T}(e),&e\in\operatorname{Edge}(\Gamma^{\prime})\backslash\operatorname{Edge}(\Gamma),\\
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{T}(e),&e\in\operatorname{Edge}(\Gamma)\end{cases}

for any edge

in
\Gamma^{\prime}
. The space of
(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)
-translations is denoted by

Relative translations are closely related to “relative vertex

and

): Given a

(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)
-translation

for the

is the

.

In Figure

With this terminology, we can now state the definition
of Gromov convergence.
A sequence of broken maps

of type
\Gamma

if there is a tropical

of relative

2.6.
Boundary strata

To define broken Fukaya algebras, we consider moduli spaces of broken
maps of rigid type with expected dimension zero and one. We describe
the configurations in the compactification of such a stratum

\mathcal{M}^{\operatorname{brok}}(\mathfrak{X},L,\Gamma,\underline{x})
. The compactification

\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\operatorname{brok}}(\mathfrak{X},L,\Gamma,\underline{x})
does not contain configurations with
additional interior nodes than those of
\Gamma
. Indeed, non-tropical
interior nodes are ruled out because they occur in codimension two
strata, and tropical nodes are ruled out as follows: Suppose a
sequence of maps of type
\Gamma
converges to a limit of map type

\Gamma^{\prime}
. Additional tropical nodes do not contribute negatively to
the expected dimension formula for broken maps because the matching
condition at a tropical edge has codimension
(\dim X-2)
, and
therefore, the moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}^{\operatorname{brok}}(\Gamma,\underline{x})

and

\mathcal{M}^{\operatorname{brok}}(\Gamma^{\prime},\underline{x})
have the same dimension, which is at most
one. However, the tropical graph
\Gamma^{\prime}
, when it is distinct from

\Gamma
, necessarily has a larger tropical symmetry group. Since

\Gamma
is rigid,
T_{\operatorname{trop}}(\Gamma)
is zero-dimensional, and

T_{\operatorname{trop}}(\Gamma^{\prime})
is at least two-dimensional. We obtain a
contradiction because
T_{\operatorname{trop}}(\Gamma^{\prime})
acts freely on

\mathcal{M}^{\operatorname{brok}}(\mathfrak{X},L,\Gamma^{\prime},\underline{x})
. Therefore, the codimension one
boundary in the compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\operatorname{brok}}(\mathfrak{X},L,\Gamma^{\prime},\underline{x})

consists of configurations with a disk node whose treed segment has
length zero or infinity. Of these two possibilities, a stratum
containing a zero length edge is actually a
fake boundary stratum

as it is the boundary of two different rigid strata with opposite
induced boundary orientations. Thus, the
true boundary strata
are
those that have a broken boundary edge. (See Section
T-
and Figure T-
.)

2.7.

Counts of rigid broken maps define the broken Fukaya algebra, as follows.

is the vector space generated by
the set of critical points of the Morse function

over the
Novikov ring
\Lambda_{\geq 0}
(see
[
12

), and

,

are added as
part of the homotopy unit construction (see
[
12
, Section

) so that the perturbation systems admit forgetful

algebra has a strict unit. For any

of

homotopy equivalent to the ordinary

.

3.
Deformed maps

In this section, we introduce deformed maps which are defined
similarly to broken maps but where the edge matching condition for
split edges is replaced by a deformed matching condition. We prove
that the
A_{\infty}
algebra defined by counts of deformed maps, called
the
deformed Fukaya algebra
, is
A_{\infty}
homotopy equivalent to
the broken Fukaya algebra.

The matching condition is deformed on a subset of edges called

split edges
. Roughly speaking, these are edges corresponding
to neck pieces whose target is the complexified torus.

Definition 3.1
.

(Split edges)

Let
\mathcal{P}_{s}\subset\mathcal{P}
be the set of polytopes
P
for which

(a)

the tropical moment map
\Phi
generates a
T/T_{P}
-action on
the cut space
\overline{X}_{P}
and this action makes
\overline{X}_{P}
a toric
manifold,

(b)

and any torus-invariant divisor
D
of
\overline{X}_{P}
is a
relative divisor. That is, there is a polytope
Q\subset P

such that
\overline{X}_{Q}=D
.

For a tropical graph
\Gamma
,
e\in\operatorname{Edge}(\Gamma)
is a
split
edge
if
P(e)\in\mathcal{P}_{s}
. The set of split edges of a tropical
graph is denoted by

\operatorname{Edge}_{s}(\Gamma)\subset\operatorname{Edge}(\Gamma).

By definition, a split edge
e
satisfies the property that the
space
\overline{\mathfrak{X}}_{P(e)}
is a toric variety that is a compactification
of
T_{\mathbb{C}}
, and the same is true of the space
\overline{\mathfrak{X}}_{Q}
where

Q\subset P(e)
is a face of the polytope
P(e)
.

Example 3.2
.

An edge whose polytope
P(e)\in\mathcal{P}
is zero dimensional is a split
edge. The example of more interest to us will be a top-dimensional
component
\overline{X}_{P_{0}}\subset\mathfrak{X}
which is a toric variety, and
all whose torus-invariant divisors are relative divisors. That is,
for any torus-invariant divisor
D\subset\overline{X}_{P_{0}}
, there is a
polytope
Q\subset P
in
\mathcal{P}
such that
D=\overline{X}_{Q}
.

Definition 3.3
.

A
deformation parameter
for a tropical graph
\Gamma
is an
element

(18)

\eta=(\eta_{e})_{e\in\operatorname{Edge}_{s}(\Gamma)}\in\bigoplus_{e\in\operatorname{Edge}_{s}(\Gamma)}\mathfrak{t}/\mathfrak{t}_{\mathcal{T}(e)}=:\mathfrak{t}_{\Gamma},

where
\mathfrak{t}_{\mathcal{T}(e)}\subset\mathfrak{t}
is the linear span
\langle{\mathcal{T}(e)}\rangle
of
the direction
\mathcal{T}(e)\in\mathfrak{t}_{P(e),\mathbb{Z}}
of the edge
e
.

The following is a preliminary definition of a deformed map with a
fixed deformation parameter.

Definition 3.4
.

(Deformed map) Let
\eta
be a deformation parameter for a
tropical graph
\Gamma
. An
\eta
-deformed map of type
\Gamma
is
the same as a broken map of type
\Gamma
with the difference that
at any split edge
e=(v_{+},v_{-})\in\operatorname{Edge}_{s}
, the matching condition
(
9
) at the corresponding node
w_{e}
is replaced by
the
deformed matching condition

(19)

(\pi_{\mathcal{T}(e)}^{\perp}\circ u_{v_{+}})(w_{e}^{+})=e^{i\eta_{e}}(\pi_{\mathcal{T}(e)}^{\perp}\circ u_{v_{-}})(w_{e}^{-})\in\mathfrak{X}_{P(e)}/T_{\mathcal{T}(e),\mathbb{C}}.

Here
\mathcal{T}(e)\in\mathfrak{t}_{P(e),\mathbb{Z}}
is the edge direction, and

\eta_{e}\in\mathfrak{t}/\mathfrak{t}_{\mathcal{T}(e)}
is the deformation parameter for the
edge
e
.

Compactifications of moduli spaces of deformed maps of a fixed type

\Gamma
may contain maps with a larger tropical graph
\Gamma^{\prime}
with a collapse morphism to
\Gamma
,
in which case, we need to “remember” the set of split edges while
passing from
\Gamma
to
\Gamma^{\prime}
. To address this requirement, the
domain of a deformed map is a based curve, which carries information
of a base tropical graph with the data of split edges. The reader
might wish to keep in mind that for generic perturbation, the tropical
graph of a deformed map is the same as the base tropical
graph. Deformed maps whose tropical graph is larger than the base tropical graph
occur in the compactifications of moduli spaces that have dimension at
least two; however to define
A_{\infty}
algebras, we only work with
moduli spaces of dimension at most one.

Definition 3.5
.

(Based curves)A

(b)

4.1.
The idea of a split map

Split maps arise as limits of deformed maps as the deformation
parameters go to infinity. A split map is a version of a broken map
where there is no matching condition along split edges. The tropical
properties of deformed maps, such as the tropical graph and the
symmetry group, look exactly like that of broken maps; this is
explained by the fact that deformation of an edge matching condition
is a small scale phenomenon that is not seen by the tropical graph
which only detects large scale behavior. As the deformation parameters
go to infinity, split maps, the limit objects, have a new kind of
tropical graph called
split tropical graph

in this section. Split tropical graphs do not satisfy the

(Direction)
condition
at split edges; instead they satisfy a
cone condition

which ensures that the tropical symmetry group is as large as
possible. In particular, in the rigid case, the dimension of the
tropical symmetry group is equal to the codimension of the matching
conditions at split edges.

4.3.
Preliminaries on cones and genericity

We define a notion of genericity and a notion of an increasing

Definition 4.1
.

Let

(a)

(Rational subspace) A

(b)

(Generic vector) A vector

(c)

(Rational linear map) Suppose

Remark 4.2

The notion of genericity is applicable to the Lie algebra

\lim_{\nu}\tfrac{x_{i,\nu}}{x_{i-1,\nu}}=0

i=2,\dots,n
.

Lemma 4.7
.

The following are equivalent for a cone

C\subset(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})^{n}
.

(a)

C
is an increasing cone.

(b)

(Weak sequence) There exists an
increasing sequence of tuples in
C
.

(c)

(Strong sequence) The tail of any
increasing sequence of tuples lies in
C
.

Proof.

The increasing cone condition can be characterized inductively :

C\subset(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is an increasing cone if and only if

e_{1}:=(1,0,\dots,0)\in C
and the
normal cone

N_{e_{1}}C:=\{(v_{2},\dots,v_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n-1}:\exists t_{0}>0:(1,tv_{2},\dots,tv_{n})\in C\quad\forall 0\leq t\leq t_{0}\}

is an increasing cone in
(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{n-1}
. Conditions
(
b
) and (
c
) can also be
characterized in the same way. That is, a cone
C\subset(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})

satisfies the (Weak sequence) resp. (Strong sequence) condition if
and only if
(1,0,\dots,0)\in\overline{C}
and the normal cone
N_{e_{1}}C

satisfies the (Weak sequence) resp. (Strong sequence) condition.
The proof of the lemma then follows by induction.
∎

The increasing property and genericity are combined to give the
following sufficient condition for a cone to be top-dimensional:

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
V=\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}
be a direct sum of vector spaces, each of
which has a dense rational lattice
V_{i,\mathbb{Q}}\subset V_{i}
. For each

i
, let
\eta_{i}\in V_{i}
be a generic element. Suppose

C\subset V
is a rational cone that contains a sequence

\eta_{\nu}:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{\nu,i}\eta_{i}

where
c_{\nu}=(c_{\nu,i})_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
is an increasing
sequence. Then
C
is top-dimensional in
V
.

Proof.

We show
C
is top-dimensional by proving that the
\mathbb{R}
-span of
C
,
denoted by
C_{\pm}\subset V
is, in fact, equal to
V
. First, we
observe that
\eta_{1}\in C_{\pm}
, since, by the increasing condition,

\eta_{1}
is the limit of the sequence

\frac{\eta_{\nu}}{c_{\nu,1}}\in C
. Then, since
V_{1}\cap C_{\pm}

is a rational subspace of
V_{1}
and it contains the generic element

\eta_{1}
, we conclude
V_{1}=V_{1}\cap C_{\pm}
. Therefore,
V_{1}
is
contained in
C_{\pm}
. The proof can now be finished by induction:
The projection of
C
to
\oplus_{i=2}^{n}V_{i}
is a rational
cone and it contains the sequence

\sum_{i=2}^{k}c_{\nu,i}\eta_{i}
.
∎

In this section, we will show that deformed Fukaya algebras are

A_{\infty}
homotopy equivalent to split Fukaya algebras. Recall that
for the split Fukaya algebra

the
composition maps are counts of split disks with a generic cone

{\eta_{\operatorname{gen}}}\in\mathfrak{t}

requires us to choose a

varying smoothly with the domain curve

equivalence we choose a sequence of deformation data

compatible with the cone direction
{\eta_{\operatorname{gen}}}
,
which roughly means that for any fixed domain curve
[C]
and any
split edge

the deformation parameter

goes to infinity in the

{\eta_{\operatorname{gen}}}
occur
as limits of sequences of

-deformed maps of bounded
area. Conversely a rigid split map can be glued to yield a

\underline{\eta}_{\nu}
-deformed map for large enough

Proposition 6.4
.

(Convergence)

Let
{\eta_{\operatorname{gen}}}\in\mathfrak{t}
be a generic cone direction, and let

\{\underline{\eta}_{\nu}\}_{\nu}
be a coherent sequence of deformation data
compatible with
{\eta_{\operatorname{gen}}}
(as in Definition

).
Let
u_{\nu}:C_{\nu}\to\mathfrak{X}_{\mathcal{P}}
be a sequence of

\underline{\eta}_{\nu}
-deformed maps with uniformly bounded area. Then, a
subsequence converges to a framed split map
u_{\infty}:C\to\mathfrak{X}_{\mathcal{P}}

of type
({\tilde{\Gamma}}_{\infty},\Gamma)
. The limit map is unique up to the
action of the identity component of the framed tropical symmetry
group
T_{{\operatorname{trop}},{\operatorname{fr}}}({\tilde{\Gamma}}_{\infty},\Gamma)
(see (
47

the maps
u_{\nu}
have index
0
, and the perturbation datum for the
limit is regular, then, the split tropical graph
{\tilde{\Gamma}}_{\infty}
is
rigid.

If the limit split tropical graph is rigid, the limit is unique,
because the framed symmetry group
T_{{\operatorname{trop}},{\operatorname{fr}}}({\tilde{\Gamma}}_{\infty},\Gamma)

is finite. The following terminology is used in the proof:

Definition 6.5
.

In a collection of sequences
\{t_{\nu}(i)\in\mathfrak{t}\}_{\nu}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, we say
t(i)
is the
fastest growing sequence
if for any
j\neq i

\lim_{\nu}\frac{|t_{\nu}(j)|}{|t_{\nu}(i)|}
exists and is finite.

Proof of Proposition
6.4
.

As in the convergence of broken maps, the first step is to find the
component-wise limit map. There are finitely many types of deformed
maps that satisfy an area bound; the proof is the same as the
analogous result Proposition T-
for broken maps.
Therefore, after passing to a subsequence, we can assume that the
maps
u_{\nu}
have a
\nu
-independent type
{\tilde{\Gamma}}\to\Gamma
, with
deformation datum

\eta_{\nu}(e):=\eta_{{\tilde{\Gamma}},\nu}([C_{\nu}],e)\in\mathfrak{t}/\mathfrak{t}_{\mathcal{T}(e)},\quad\forall e\in\operatorname{Edge}_{s}(\Gamma).

We apply Gromov convergence for broken maps (Theorem
T-
, outlined in Section
2.5
) on each
connected component of
{\tilde{\Gamma}}\backslash\operatorname{Edge}_{s}(\Gamma)
.
The result is a translation sequence

t_{\nu}(v)\in\operatorname{Cone}(\kappa,v),\quad v\in\operatorname{Vert}({\tilde{\Gamma}}_{\infty})

that satisfies the
(Direction)
condition for
all non-split edges
e\in\operatorname{Edge}({\tilde{\Gamma}}_{\infty})\backslash\operatorname{Edge}_{s}(\Gamma)
,
and a collection of limit maps denoted by
u_{\infty}
. The
limit
u_{\infty}
is modelled on a quasi-split tropical graph

{\tilde{\Gamma}}_{\infty}\to\Gamma
, equipped with a tropical edge collapse
morphism
\kappa:{\tilde{\Gamma}}_{\infty}\backslash\operatorname{Edge}_{s}(\Gamma)\to{\tilde{\Gamma}}\backslash\operatorname{Edge}_{s}(\Gamma)
.

The limit map
u_{\infty}
is a quasi-split map; for it to be a split
map, it remains to show that the cone condition (
36
) is
satisfied. For this purpose, we refine the translation sequences

t_{\nu}
to produce a sequence of relative
translations satisfying the weak sequential cone condition of Lemma

4.19
.
For any
split edge
e
, the sequences

(57)

\pi^{\perp}_{\mathcal{T}(e)}(t_{\nu}(v_{+})-t_{\nu}(v_{-}))-\eta_{\nu}(e),\quad e=(v_{+},v_{-})\in\operatorname{Edge}_{s}(\Gamma)

are uniformly bounded: The proof is the same as the analogous
statement for broken maps in T-(
); it follows from
the fact that the projected evaluations of
u_{\nu}
at the nodal
lifts
w_{\nu,e}^{+}
and
w_{\nu,e}^{-}
differ by
\eta_{\nu}
, and that
the projected evaluation at
z_{e}^{\pm}
translated by
t_{\nu}(v_{\pm})

forms a converging sequence. The next step is to adjust the
translation sequences by a uniformly bounded amount so that the
discrepancy at the split edges is equal to the deformation
parameters, i.e. the sequences in (
57
), vanish. The
adjustment to the translation sequences is by the iterative process
used in Step 2 of the proof of Lemma T-
, and
is as follows: The iteration is run simultaneously on the vertex
sequences
\{t_{\nu}(v)\}_{v,\nu}
and the edge sequences

\{\eta_{\nu}(e)\}_{e,\nu}
. In each step a fastest growing sequence
(which exists after passing to a subsequence) is subtracted, with
the resulting sequence in the
i
-th step being denoted by

\{t^{i}_{\nu}(v)\}_{v}
,
\{\eta^{i}_{\nu}(e)\}_{e}
. That is, if at the

i
-th step,

is the fastest growing sequence, we define

t_{\nu}^{i+1}(v):=t_{\nu}^{i}(v)-r_{\nu}^{i}\lim_{\nu}\tfrac{t_{\nu}^{i}(v)}{r_{\nu}^{i}},

and
\eta_{\nu}^{i+1}(e)
is defined similarly. After any step, the
quantity

\pi^{\perp}_{\mathcal{T}(e)}(t_{\nu}^{i}(v_{+})-t_{\nu}^{i}(v_{-}))-\eta_{\nu}^{i}(e)
is
the same as the corresponding quantity in (
57
) for any
split edge
e=(v_{+},v_{-})
. At any step, if both a vertex sequence
and an edge sequence grow at the same rate, and are both fastest
growing, then we break the tie by subtracting the edge sequence.
The iteration ends when the sequences corresponding to vertices and
split edges are uniformly bounded, say, after
k
steps. Our tie
breaking method, together with the fact that the sequences
corresponding to a pair of split edges grow at different rates
(since
(c_{e,\nu})_{e,\nu}
is a sequence of increasing tuples),
ensures that
\eta^{k}_{\nu}(e)=0
for all split edges

and all

\nu
. We subtract the uniformly bounded final sequence

\{t^{k}_{\nu}(v)\}_{v,\nu}
from the initial sequence

\{t_{\nu}(v)\}_{v,\nu}
and denote the result by

\{\overline{t}_{\nu}(v)\}_{v,\nu}

(58)

\pi^{\perp}_{\mathcal{T}(e)}(\overline{t}_{\nu}(v_{+})-\overline{t}_{\nu}(v_{-}))-\eta_{\nu}(e)=0,\quad e=(v_{+},v_{-})\in\operatorname{Edge}_{s}(\Gamma).

Since for any vertex
v
the translation sequences
t_{\nu}(v)

\overline{t}_{\nu}(v)
differ by a uniformly bounded amount, a subsequence
of
e^{-\overline{t}_{\nu}}u_{\nu}
converges to a limit
\overline{u}_{\infty,v}
.
The limit map
\overline{u}_{\infty}:=(\overline{u}_{\infty,v})_{v}
satisfies the
matching condition on all edges, including split edges, because by
(
58

\overline{t}_{\nu}
compensate for the
deformation parameters. Finally
{\tilde{\Gamma}}\to\Gamma
is a split graph:
By (
58

\operatorname{Diff}(\overline{t}_{\nu})=\eta_{\nu}
, and
\eta_{\nu}\in{\operatorname{Disc}}({\tilde{\Gamma}},\Gamma)\quad\forall\nu
. Since the weak sequential condition is satisfied, Lemma

implies that
{\operatorname{Disc}}({\tilde{\Gamma}},\Gamma)
satisfies the
(Cone condition)

We have shown that
\overline{u}_{\infty}
is a framed split map. The proof
of uniqueness of limits is the same as Step 6 in the proof of
Theorem T-
. The idea is that if there are
different limits
u_{\infty}
,
u_{\infty}^{\prime}
, the difference between
the translation sequences is uniformly bounded, and the the limit
of a subsequence of the differences yields an element in
the identity component of the tropical symmetry group which relates

u_{\infty}
,
u_{\infty}^{\prime}
. The last statement about the rigidity of
the limit split map follows from a dimensional argument, see
Remark
5.12

Proposition 6.7

(Gluing a split map)

1
Introduction

Active regression, an extension of the classical regression model, has gained increasing attention in recent years. In its most basic form, active regression aims to solve
\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lVert Ax-b\right\rVert_{p}
(
p\geq 1
), where the matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
represents
n
data points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the vector
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
represents the corresponding labels. However, since label access can be expensive, the challenge is to minimize the number of entries of
b
that are accessed while still solving the regression problem approximately.
A typical guarantee of the approximate solution is to find
\hat{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\left\lVert A\hat{x}-b\right\rVert_{p}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\left\lVert Ax^{\ast}-b\right\rVert_{p},

(1)

where
x^{\ast}
is the optimal solution to the regression problem, i.e.,
x^{*}=\argmin_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lVert Ax-b\right\rVert_{p}
.

Research on this problem often focuses on randomized algorithms with constant failure probability, i.e., the entries of
b
are sampled randomly (but typically not uniformly) and the output
\hat{x}
satisfies the error guarantee above with probability at least a large constant. When
p=2
,
Chen and Price (2019)
showed sampling
O(d/\varepsilon)
entries of
b
suffices, and when
p=1
,
Parulekar et al. (2021)
showed an optimal query complexity of
\Theta(d/\varepsilon^{2})
. The case of general
p
was later settled by
Musco et al. (2022)
, who showed a query complexity of
\tilde{O}(d/\varepsilon)
for
p\in(1,2]
and
\tilde{O}(d^{p/2}/\varepsilon^{p-1})
for
p>2
. Their proof was later refined by
Yasuda (2024)
.

A more general form of the regression problem is the single-index model, which, in our context, asks to solve

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lVert f(Ax)-b\right\rVert_{p},

where
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a nonlinear function and, for
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we abuse the notation slightly to denote
f(u)=(f(u_{1}),\dots,f(u_{n}))
, i.e., applying
f
entrywise. This formulation arises naturally in neural networks and has received significant recent attention (see, e.g.,
(
Diakonikolas et al., 2020
;
Gajjar et al., 2023a
;
Huang et al., 2024
)
and the references therein). A neural network can be viewed as the composition of a network backbone and a linear prediction layer
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with an activation function
f
, where a typical choice of
f
is the ReLU function. The network’s prediction is given by
f(Ax)
, where the matrix
A
is the feature matrix generated by the network backbone from the dataset. The goal is to learn the linear prediction layer
x
, which corresponds to solving the regression problem.

In this paper, we consider
f
to be a general
L
-Lipschitz function.
It is tempting to expect an analogous guarantee as
Equation

1
for the canonical active regression problem, i.e. to find
\hat{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\displaystyle\lVert f(A\hat{x})-b\rVert_{p}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\lVert f(Ax^{*})-b\rVert_{p}

where
x^{*}=\argmin_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lVert f(Ax)-b\rVert_{p}
.
However,
Gajjar et al. (2023a)
showed that achieving this guarantee with a
\poly(d)
query complexity is impossible even when
f
is a ReLU function and
\varepsilon
is a constant.
Hence, we can only expect a weaker guarantee.
The single-index regression problem was studied for
p=2
in the same paper
(
Gajjar et al., 2023a
)
, which showed that sampling
O(d^{2}/\varepsilon^{4})
entries suffices to find an
\hat{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\lVert f(A\hat{x})-b\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq C(\lVert f(Ax^{\ast})-b\rVert_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon L^{2}\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{2}^{2}),

(2)

where
x^{\ast}=\argmin_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lVert f(Ax)-b\right\rVert_{2}
is the minimizer and
C
is an absolute constant.
For general
p
,
Huang et al. (2024)
obtained

\lVert f(A\hat{x})-b\rVert_{p}^{p}\leq C(p)(\lVert f(Ax^{\ast})-b\rVert_{p}^{p}+\varepsilon L^{p}\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p})

(3)

for some constant
C(p)
depending only on
p
, using
O(d/\varepsilon^{4})
samples when
1\leq p\leq 2
and
O(d^{p/2}/\varepsilon^{4})
samples when
p>2
. For
p=2
,
Gajjar et al. (2023b)
also independently obtained
\tilde{O}(d/\varepsilon^{4})
samples and, with additional co-authors, further improved the query complexity to
\tilde{O}(d/\varepsilon^{2})
in
(
Gajjar et al., 2024
)
.

The main drawback of the existing results for the single-index model, compared to the basic form of active regression, is that
Equation

2
and
Equation

3
only achieve constant-factor approximations, whereas
Equation

1
achieves a guarantee of
(1+\varepsilon)
-approximation. The goal of this paper is to obtain a
(1+\varepsilon)
-approximation for the single-index model.

Note that all existing results assume access to an oracle solver for the regression problem of the form
\argmin_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lVert f(A^{\prime}x)-b^{\prime}\rVert_{p}
or its regularized variant, which may not be a convex programme and where the objective function may be non-differentiable due to
f
.
In this work, we retain the assumption of having such an oracle solver.

1.1
Problem Definition

Now we define our problem formally. For
L\geq 0
, let
\mathsf{Lip}_{L}
denote the set of
L
-Lipschitz functions
f
such that
f(0)=0
, i.e.

\mathsf{Lip}_{L}:=\big\{\,f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\;\big|\;f(0)=0\text{ and }\lvert f(x)-f(y)\rvert\leq L\cdot\lvert x-y\rvert\text{ for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$}\,\big\}.

Suppose we are given a function
f\in\mathsf{Lip}_{L}
,
a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and a query access to the entries of an unknown
n
-dimensional vector
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Define

\displaystyle\mathsf{OPT}:=\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lVert f(Ax)-b\rVert_{p}^{p}\quad\text{and}\quad x^{*}:=\argmin_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\lVert f(Ax)-b\rVert_{p}^{p}=\mathsf{OPT}}\lVert Ax\rVert_{p}^{p}.

That is, if there are multiple minimizers
x^{*}
, we choose an arbitrary one that minimizes
\left\lVert Ax^{\ast}\right\rVert_{p}
.
As noted in
(
Gajjar et al., 2024
)
, there is no loss of generality in assuming
f(0)=0
because one can shift both
f(x)
and
b
by
f(0)
.
For an error parameter
\varepsilon>0
, our goal is to find an
\hat{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\lVert f(A\hat{x})-b\rVert_{p}^{p}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\mathsf{OPT}+C\varepsilon\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p},

where
C
is some constant that depends only on
L
and
p
while the number of queries to the entries of
b
is minimized.
Therefore, we would like to ask:

What is the minimum number (in terms of
d
and
\varepsilon
) of queries to the entries of
b
needed in order to achieve this goal?

We will solve this problem in this paper.

1.2
Our Results

We first present the main result of this paper that querying
O(d/\varepsilon^{2}\poly\log n)
entries of
b
is sufficient for a
(1+\varepsilon)
-approximation when
1\leq p\leq 2
and
O(d^{p/2}/\varepsilon^{p}\poly\log n)
entries when
p>2
. Our result achieves the same query complexity (up to logarithmic factors) for the constant-factor approximation algorithm in
(
Gajjar et al., 2024
)
when
p=2
.

Theorem 1
.

There is a randomized algorithm, when given
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
,
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
f\in\mathsf{Lip}_{L}
and an arbitrary sufficient small
\varepsilon>0
,
with probability at least
0.9
, makes
O\big(d^{1\vee\frac{p}{2}}/\varepsilon^{2\vee p}\cdot\poly\log(d/\varepsilon)\big)
queries to the entries of
b
and returns an
\hat{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\lVert f(A\hat{x})-b\rVert_{p}^{p}\leq\mathsf{OPT}+\varepsilon(\mathsf{OPT}+L^{p}\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}).

(4)

The hidden constant in the bound on number of queries depends on
p
only.

Recall that in the canonical active regression problem, i.e.
f(x)=x
, the query complexity is
\tilde{\Theta}(d/\varepsilon)
when
1<p\leq 2
and
\tilde{\Theta}(d^{p/2}/\varepsilon^{p-1})
when
p>2

(
Musco et al., 2022
)
. We can show that accommodating a general
f
pushes up the
\varepsilon
-dependence to
1/\varepsilon^{2}
and
1/\varepsilon^{p}
, respectively. In particular, for
1\leq p\leq 2
, we show a lower bound of
\Omega(d/\varepsilon^{2})
queries, which suggests that our upper bound is tight up to logarithmic factors. The following are formal statements on our lower bound.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that
p\geq 1
,
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small and
n\gtrsim(d\log d)/\varepsilon^{2}
. Any randomized algorithm that, given
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
, a query access to the entries of an unknown
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f\in\mathsf{Lip}_{1}
, outputs a
d
-dimensional vector
\hat{x}
such that
Equation

4
holds with probability at least
4/5
must make
\Omega(d/\varepsilon^{2})
queries to the entries of
b
.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that
p>2
,
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small and
n\gtrsim d/\varepsilon^{p}
. Any randomized algorithm that, given
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
, a query access to the entries of an unknown
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f\in\mathsf{Lip}_{1}
, outputs a
d
-dimensional vector
\hat{x}
such that
Equation

4
holds with probability at least
4/5
must make
\Omega(d/\varepsilon^{p})
queries to the entries of
b
.

2
Proof Overview

In this section, we will provide a proof overview of our theorems.

2.1
Upper Bound

General Observations

We follow the usual “sample-and-solve” paradigm as in many previous active regression algorithms
(
Chen and Price, 2019
;
Musco et al., 2022
;
Gajjar et al., 2023a
;
Chen et al., 2022
;
Huang et al., 2024
)
.
Querying entries of
b
can be viewed as multiplying
b
with a sampling matrix
S
, which is a diagonal matrix with sparse diagonal entries; the nonzero entries in
Sb
correspond to the queried entries of
b
.
Hence, we would like to minimize the number of nonzero diagonal entries in
S
.
The same sampling matrix
S
is also applied to
f(Ax)
, leading to a natural attempt at solving the optimization problem
\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lVert S(f(Ax)-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}
.
However, we preview that this optimization problem is not the one we seek and we will provide more explanation on how to modify it.

The natural question is how to design the sampling matrix
S
. In all previous work,
S
is a row-sampling matrix with respect to Lewis weights; see the statement of Lemma
6
.
In this paper, we adopt the same approach.
This means that (i) (unbiased estimator)
\E\lVert Sv\rVert_{p}^{p}=\lVert v\rVert_{p}^{p}
for each
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and (ii) (subspace embedding) when
S
has sufficiently many nonzero rows,
\lVert SAx\rVert_{p}\approx\lVert Ax\rVert_{p}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Note that the query complexity for active regression is usually higher than necessary for subspace embedding alone.

Formulating the Concentration Bounds

Let
\hat{x}=\argmin_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lVert S(f(Ax)-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}
.
Although
\E\lVert S(f(Ax)-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}=\lVert f(Ax)-b\rVert_{p}^{p}
for
each

x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it is unclear what
\E\lVert S(f(A\hat{x})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}
is
since
\hat{x}
depends on
S
.
To address this, we instead argue that the sampling error
\left\lvert\lVert S(f(Ax)-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert f(Ax)-b\rVert_{p}^{p}\right\rvert
is small for
all

x\in T
, where
T
is a “small” bounded domain containing
\hat{x}
.
Here, the small size of
T
is critical for a small error bound since it controls the number of points in
T
at which the error needs to be small when applying Dudley’s integral, a classical extension of the net argument.
To further reduce the variance,
we choose
\lVert S(f(Ax^{\ast})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert f(Ax^{\ast})-b\rVert_{p}^{p}
as a reference point and argue that the difference

\displaystyle\mathsf{Err}(x)=\lvert(\lVert S(f(Ax)-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert f(Ax)-b\rVert_{p}^{p})-(\lVert S(f(Ax^{*})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert f(Ax^{*})-b\rVert_{p}^{p})\rvert.

(5)

is uniformly small over
T
.
Note that the reference point can be
\lVert S(f(A\bar{x})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert f(A\bar{x})-b\rVert_{p}^{p}
for any
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
This approach has been employed by
Yasuda (2024)
for
the canonical active regression (i.e.
f(x)=x
).
However, for general Lipschitz functions
f
,
a key challenge is to identify an appropriate domain
T
, which we shall discuss below.

Regularized Regression

As previously noted, the optimization problem
\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lVert S(f(Ax)-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}
is not the one we are seeking.
It turns out that there is a fundamental challenge to argue that
\hat{x}=\argmin_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lVert S(f(Ax)-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}
satisfies the desired guarantee
Equation

4
.
In the canonical active regression,
one can show that

\lVert A(\hat{x}-x^{*})\rVert_{p}\lesssim\lVert Ax^{*}-b\rVert_{p}.

(6)

This suggests that
\hat{x}\in T
for
T=\{\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;|\;\lVert A(x-x^{*})\rVert_{p}\lesssim\lVert Ax^{*}-b\rVert_{p}\,\}
, which is a “
small
” bounded region.
Unfortunately, for a Lipschitz function
f
, it is not clear how to obtain an analogy to
Equation

6
and thus a bounded region
T
containing
\hat{x}
.
Hence, we still seek a bound on the norm
\lVert A\hat{x}\rVert_{p}^{p}
to keep
T
bounded and ideally small.
For constant-factor approximations, previous work
(
Gajjar et al., 2023a
;
Huang et al., 2024
)
restrict the domain in the regression problem and solve
\min_{x\in T}\lVert S(f(Ax)-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}
for some “small”
T
, but this leads to a poor
\varepsilon
-dependence of
1/\varepsilon^{4}
in query complexity.

To improve the
\varepsilon
-dependence, one can consider a regularized regression problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\ \lVert S(f(Ax)-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}+\tau\cdot\lVert Ax\rVert_{p}^{p},

where
\tau>0
is a regularization parameter. This approach, as demonstrated by
Gajjar et al. (2024)
, improves the
\varepsilon
-dependence for constant-factor approximations when
p=2
, and will therefore be adopted in this paper.
To ease the notation, assume that the Lipschitz constant
L=1
from now on.

An important question is how to choose the regularization parameter
\tau
.
Recall that we want the sampling error
\mathsf{Err}(x)
(defined in
Equation

5
) to be small, ideally close to
0
, when
x=\hat{x}
. Then

\displaystyle\lVert f(A\hat{x})-b\rVert_{p}^{p}-\mathsf{OPT}

\displaystyle=\lVert f(A\hat{x})-b\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert f(Ax^{*})-b\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert S(f(A\hat{x})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert S(f(Ax^{*})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}+\text{error terms}

\displaystyle\leq\tau\cdot\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}+\text{error terms},

where the last inequality follows from the optimality of
\hat{x}
.
The desired guarantee
Equation

4
has an additive error
\varepsilon\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}
, indicating that
\tau
should be set smaller than
\varepsilon
.
On the other hand, the main purpose of regularization is to bound
\lVert A\hat{x}\rVert_{p}^{p}
.
By the optimality of
\hat{x}
and rearranging the terms, we have

\displaystyle\lVert A\hat{x}\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\tau}\cdot\bigg(\lVert S(f(Ax^{*})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert S(f(A\hat{x})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}\bigg)+\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}.

This suggests that
\tau
should be set as large as possible for a better bound on
\lVert A\hat{x}\rVert_{p}^{p}
.
Therefore, we set
\tau=\varepsilon
and the optimization problem becomes

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lVert S(f(Ax)-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}+\varepsilon\cdot\lVert Ax\rVert_{p}^{p}.

(7)

Bounding the Error

To avoid overloading our notation, we focus on
1\leq p\leq 2
. A similar argument works for
p\geq 2
. Recall that our intention is to bound

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in T}\mathsf{Err}(x),

where
\mathsf{Err}(x)
is the sampling error defined in
Equation

5
.
The first issue we need to resolve is defining the domain
T
.
By the optimality of
\hat{x}
in
Equation

7
and rearranging the terms, we have

\displaystyle\lVert A\hat{x}\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\bigg(\lVert S(f(Ax^{*})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert S(f(A\hat{x})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}\bigg)+\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}

(8)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\lVert S(f(Ax^{*})-b))\rVert_{p}^{p}+\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathsf{OPT}+\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}\quad\text{by Markov inequality.}

(9)

Hence, we can set

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathsf{OPT}+\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}\quad\text{and}\quad T=\{\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;|\;\lVert Ax\rVert_{p}^{p}\lesssim R\,\}.

Then
\hat{x}\in T
.
Now, while we omit the details, we obtain the following concentration bound in
Equation

10
by following the standard technique of upper bounding the supremum of a stochastic process using Dudley’s integral, which has been the central tool in the work on subspace embeddings
(
Bourgain et al., 1989
;
Ledoux and Talagrand, 1991
;
Cohen and Peng, 2015
)
and previous work on active regression
(
Musco et al., 2022
;
Chen et al., 2022
;
Huang et al., 2024
)
.
In short, when the number of queries is
m
, we can obtain that with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in T}\mathsf{Err}(x)\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{d\poly(\log n,\log\delta^{-1})}{m}}\cdot R.

(10)

We preview here that this concentration bound will yield a weaker result, but it serves to illustrate the key idea and will guide us in refining the analysis later.

Suppose that
m\sim\frac{d}{\varepsilon^{4}}\poly\log n
.
By plugging
m
and
R
into
Equation

10
, we have with constant probability,

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in T}\mathsf{Err}(x)\lesssim\varepsilon(\mathsf{OPT}+\varepsilon\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}).

Since
\hat{x}\in T
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert f(A\hat{x})-b\rVert_{p}^{p}-\mathsf{OPT}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert S(f(A\hat{x})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert S(f(Ax^{*})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}+\varepsilon(\mathsf{OPT}+\varepsilon\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p})

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}+\varepsilon(\mathsf{OPT}+\varepsilon\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p})\quad\text{by the optimality of $\hat{x}$ in \lx@cref{creftypecap~refnum}{eq:overview_reg_prob}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon(\mathsf{OPT}+\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}),

(11)

which achieves the desired guarantee
Equation

4
by a rescaling of
\varepsilon
.

Attempt to Improve

The reason we previously set
m\sim\frac{d}{\varepsilon^{4}}\poly\log n
is because
R=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathsf{OPT}+\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}
and we need the square root term in
Equation

10
to be
\varepsilon^{2}
so that the overall error is at most
\varepsilon(\mathsf{OPT}+\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p})
.
Indeed, when we compare to the canonical case of
f(x)=x
and bound the radius of the domain in
Equation

6
, this large
R
is the main reason why extra factors of
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
are needed.

Notice that the term
\lVert S(f(Ax)-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert S(f(Ax^{*})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}
in
\mathsf{Err}(x)
also appears in
Equation

8
. This means that we can plug the bound on
\mathsf{Err}(x)
into
Equation

8
and improve the radius
R
.

For example, let
m\sim\frac{d}{\varepsilon^{3}}\poly\log n
. Here, the exponent
3
can be replaced by any value
strictly
larger than
2
and we simply choose this number for demonstration purposes.
By plugging
m
and
R
into
Equation

10
, we have with constant probability

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in T}\mathsf{Err}(x)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in T}\lvert(\lVert S(f(Ax)-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert S(f(Ax^{*})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p})-(\lVert f(Ax)-b\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert f(Ax^{*})-b\rVert_{p}^{p})\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathsf{OPT}+\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}.

(12)

Since
\hat{x}\in T
, by plugging
Equation

12
into
Equation

8
and a similar calculation to that in
Equation

9
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert A\hat{x}\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}\mathsf{OPT}+\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}\quad\text{which is smaller than $R$.}

If we set

\displaystyle R^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}\mathsf{OPT}+\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}\quad\text{and}\quad T^{\prime}=\{\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;|\;\lVert Ax\rVert_{p}^{p}\lesssim R^{\prime}\,\},

then
\hat{x}\in T^{\prime}
.
By plugging
m
,
R^{\prime}
and
T^{\prime}
into
Equation

10
, we have with constant probability,

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in T^{\prime}}\mathsf{Err}(x)\lesssim\varepsilon\mathsf{OPT}+\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}.

This is an improved error bound compared to
Equation

12
. It follows from
\hat{x}\in T^{\prime}
and a similar calculation to that in
Equation

11
that

\displaystyle\lVert f(A\hat{x})-b\rVert_{p}^{p}-\mathsf{OPT}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon(\mathsf{OPT}+\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}),

which achieves the guarantee
Equation

4
.
Therefore, we have successfully improved the query complexity from
\frac{d}{\varepsilon^{4}}\poly\log n
to
\frac{d}{\varepsilon^{3}}\poly\log n
.
Although this bootstrapping idea of reusing the error guarantee to shrink
T
has appeared in previous work
(
Musco et al., 2022
;
Yasuda, 2024
)
, we emphasize that there is a fundamental difference in the detailed analysis for general Lipschitz functions
f
, due to the lack of convexity of
\lVert f(A(\cdot))-b\rVert_{p}^{p}
.

Further Improvement

Recall that we are targeting a query complexity of
\frac{d}{\varepsilon^{2}}\poly\log n
.
One may immediately check that setting
m\sim\frac{d}{\varepsilon^{2}}\poly\log n
in the above argument is not helpful.
To address this issue, we refine the analysis of
Equation

10
and improve the bound as follows.
Recall that we set
R=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathsf{OPT}+\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}
such that
\lVert A\hat{x}\rVert_{p}^{p}\lesssim R
.
If we further restrict the domain
T
and set it to be

\displaystyle T=\left\{\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;\middle|\;\lVert Ax\rVert_{p}^{p}\lesssim R\quad\text{and}\quad\lVert f(Ax)-f(Ax^{*})\rVert_{p}^{p}\lesssim F\,\right\}\quad\text{for some $F\geq\mathsf{OPT}$}

then
Equation

10
can be improved to

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in T}\mathsf{Err}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{d\poly(\log n,\log\delta^{-1})}{m}}\cdot\sqrt{FR}.

(13)

Note that, by the Lipschitz condition and the fact that
\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}\lesssim R
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert f(Ax)-f(Ax^{*})\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert Ax-Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}\lesssim R

and hence one can set
F=R
.
That means
Equation

13
is always no worse than
Equation

10
.
To apply
Equation

13
, we need to show that
\hat{x}\in T
, i.e. find a suitable
F
such that
\lVert f(A\hat{x})-f(Ax^{*})\rVert_{p}^{p}\leq F
.

In the proof of a constant-factor approximation by
Gajjar et al. (2024)
, a key step is

\displaystyle\lVert f(A\hat{x})-f(Ax^{*})\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathsf{OPT}+\varepsilon\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}

when
p=2
.
A straightforward modification extends it to general
p
, which means that we can set
F=\mathsf{OPT}+\varepsilon\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}
.

Now, we pick
m\sim\frac{d}{\varepsilon^{2}}\poly\log n
.
By plugging
m
,
R
and
F
into
Equation

13
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in T}\mathsf{Err}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\cdot\sqrt{(\mathsf{OPT}+\varepsilon\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p})\cdot(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathsf{OPT}+\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p})}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathsf{OPT}+\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}.

(14)

Following the same argument before and plugging it into
Equation

8
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert A\hat{x}\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}\mathsf{OPT}+\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}

which allows us to refine further the radius
R
and thus the domain
T
, leading to a better bound on
\lVert A\hat{x}\rVert_{p}^{p}
. Iterate this process
and apply
Equation

13

\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
times, we shall arrive at the bound

\displaystyle\mathsf{Err}(\hat{x})

\displaystyle=\lvert(\lVert S(f(A\hat{x})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert S(f(Ax^{*})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p})-(\lVert f(A\hat{x})-b\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert f(Ax^{*})-b\rVert_{p}^{p})\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\cdot(\mathsf{OPT}+\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}).

Finally, we follow a calculation similar to that in
Equation

11
to achieve the desired guarantee
Equation

4
.
The caveat here is that repeatedly applying the concentration bound
Equation

13
in the iterative process causes the failure probability to accumulate.
We resolve this by setting
\delta=1/\log\log(1/\varepsilon)
in
Equation

13
, keeping
\log(1/\delta)
at most
\log n
.
Hence, the query complexity remains
(d/\varepsilon^{2})\poly\log n
.

Dependence on
n

Although we have achieved the query complexity of
\frac{d}{\varepsilon^{2}}\poly\log n
, it may not be desirable when
n
is large and we seek to further remove the dependence on
n
.
The
\poly\log n
factor arises from estimating a covering number when bounding Dudley’s integral.
Indeed, by using a simple net argument with a sampling matrix of
\poly(d/\varepsilon)
non-zero rows, the solution guarantee can still be achieved. While the dependence on
d
and
\varepsilon
are both worse, the query complexity is independent of
n
.
To take the advantage of this trade-off, a standard approach involves using two query matrices
S^{\circ}
and
S
, where
S^{\circ}
has an suboptimal number of nonzero rows, and then solving the following new regularized problem

\displaystyle\hat{x}=\argmin_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lVert SS^{\circ}(f(Ax)-b))\rVert_{p}^{p}+\varepsilon\lVert S^{\circ}Ax\rVert_{p}^{p}.

We need to pay close attention to the fact that we are not simply using
S^{\circ}A
as the input matrix
A
in the original statement because of the function
f
.

To bound the error, a natural attempt is to use the concentration bounds and show that

\displaystyle\lVert f(A\hat{x})-b\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert f(Ax^{*})-b\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert S^{\circ}(f(A\hat{x})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert S^{\circ}(f(Ax^{*})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}+\text{error terms}

(15)

\displaystyle\leq\lVert SS^{\circ}(f(A\hat{x})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert SS^{\circ}(f(Ax^{*})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}+\text{error terms}

(16)

and then we use the optimality of
\hat{x}
to complete the proof.
However, we remind the readers that, to apply the concentration bounds, it is important to check that the relevant points
x^{*},\hat{x}
are in the domain of interest for the corresponding bounds.
It turns out that we can obtain
Equation

15
but arguing
Equation

16
is the main obstacle because of that.
While we will omit the detail, we note that we need a proxy point
x^{\circ}
to circumvent this obstacle when using the concentration bound for
S
.
The proxy point
x^{\circ}
is defined as

\displaystyle x^{\circ}=\argmin_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lVert S^{\circ}(f(Ax)-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}+\varepsilon^{2}\lVert Ax\rVert_{p}^{p}

and this proxy point allows us to show that
\hat{x}
lies within the domain of interest.
We can then modify the above argument by continuing from
Equation

15

\displaystyle\lVert S^{\circ}(f(A\hat{x})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert S^{\circ}(f(Ax^{*})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert S^{\circ}(f(A\hat{x})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert S^{\circ}(f(Ax^{\circ})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}+\varepsilon^{2}\lVert Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert SS^{\circ}(f(A\hat{x})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert SS^{\circ}(f(Ax^{\circ})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}+\text{error terms}

and use the optimality of
\hat{x}
to finish the proof.

2.2
Lower Bound

General Observations

As in the previous results
(
Musco et al., 2022
;
Yasuda, 2024
)
, via Yao’s minimax theorem, the proof of the lower bounds is reduced to distinguishing between two distributions (which are called hard instance). Specifically,
we construct two “hard-to-distinguish” distributions on the vector
b
, and it requires a certain number of queries to the entries of
b
to distinguish between these distributions with constant probability. The reduction is using an approximation solution
\hat{x}
to determine from which distribution
b
is drawn.
We construct our hard instances for
1\leq p\leq 2
and
p\geq 2
separately. These instances are inspired by those in
(
Musco et al., 2022
;
Yasuda, 2024
)
but are more complex, as we are showing a higher lower bound.
For the purpose of exposition, we assume
d=1
, in which case the matrix
A
degenerates to a vector
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We shall then extend the result to the general
d
.

Hard Instance for
1\leq p\leq 2

We pair up the entries (say
2i-1
and
2i
).
Let

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}3\\
2\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad v=\begin{bmatrix}2\\
3\end{bmatrix}.

Let
D_{0}
(resp.
D_{1}
) be the distribution on
b\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
that, for all
i=1,\dots,n
, each pair

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}b_{2i-1}\\
b_{2i}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}u&\text{with probability $\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon$ (resp. $\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon$)}\\
v&\text{with probability $\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon$ (resp. $\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon$).}\end{cases}

By the standard information-theoretic lower bounds, one needs to query
\Omega(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}})
entries of
b
to distinguish
D_{0}
and
D_{1}
.

To reduce this problem to our problem, we set

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2&\text{if $x\leq-6$}\\
-x-4&\text{if $-6\leq x\leq-4$}\\
0&\text{if $-4\leq x\leq 0$}\\
\frac{1}{2}x&\text{if $0\leq x$}\\
\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\begin{bmatrix}a_{2i-1}\\
a_{2i}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
-1\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{for $i=1,\dots,n$.}

Let
k
be the number of
u
’s in
b
.
The objective function becomes

\displaystyle\lVert f(a\cdot x)-b\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=k\cdot\lVert f(\begin{bmatrix}x\\
-x\end{bmatrix})-u\rVert_{p}^{p}+(n-k)\cdot\lVert f(\begin{bmatrix}x\\
-x\end{bmatrix})-v\rVert_{p}^{p}.

The takeaway of this construction is one can view
f(\begin{bmatrix}x\\
-x\end{bmatrix})
as a locus in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
as
x
varies, illustrated in
Figure

1
.
Suppose
b
is drawn from
D_{0}
.
It implies that
k\approx\frac{n}{2}+\varepsilon n
and hence
n-k<k
.
One can view each component as the
\ell_{p}
distance between the locus and
u
or
v
.
As seen in
Figure

1
, the locus passes through
u
and
v
.

Figure 1:
(left) Plot of the locus
f(\begin{bmatrix}x\\
-x\end{bmatrix})
, where the red (resp. blue) part corresponds to
x\leq 0
(resp.
x\geq 0
);
(middle)
f(\begin{bmatrix}-6\\
6\end{bmatrix})
is the point on the red part that is closest to
u
and
v
in the
\ell_{p}
-distance;
(right)
f(\begin{bmatrix}6\\
-6\end{bmatrix})
is the point on the blue part that is closest to
u
and
v
in the
\ell_{p}
-distance

When
x=-6
, we have

f(\begin{bmatrix}x\\
-x\end{bmatrix})=u
and so

\displaystyle\mathsf{OPT}

\displaystyle\leq k\cdot 0+(n-k)\cdot\lVert u-v\rVert_{p}^{p}=2^{p}(n-k)\approx 2^{p-1}n(1-2\varepsilon).

On the other hand,
Figure

1
also suggests that, when
x>0
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert f(a\cdot x)-b\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\geq k\cdot\lVert u-v\rVert_{p}^{p}+(n-k)\cdot 0\geq 2^{p}k\approx 2^{p-1}n(1+2\varepsilon).

Suppose we have a solution
\hat{x}
such that

\lVert f(a\cdot\hat{x})-b\rVert_{p}^{p}\leq(1+c\cdot\varepsilon)\mathsf{OPT}+c\cdot\varepsilon\cdot\lVert a\cdot x^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}.

It then follows that

\displaystyle\lVert f(a\cdot\hat{x})-b\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq(1+c\cdot\varepsilon)2^{p}(n-k)+c\cdot\varepsilon\cdot\lVert a\cdot x^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\approx(1+c\cdot\varepsilon)\cdot 2^{p-1}n(1-2\varepsilon)+c\cdot\varepsilon\cdot 6^{p}\cdot 2n

\displaystyle=2^{p-1}n(1-\Omega(\varepsilon))

\displaystyle<2^{p-1}n(1+2\varepsilon),

which implies that
\hat{x}<0
.
Similarly, suppose
b
is drawn from
D_{1}
, one can show the symmetric result.
We can declare
b
is drawn from
D_{0}
if
\hat{x}<0
and
D_{1}
otherwise. This concludes our reduction.

Hard Instance for
p\geq 2

We start with the all-one vector
b\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.
Then, we pick a random index
i^{*}
from
\{1,\dots,2n\}
uniformly and update
b_{i^{*}}\leftarrow b_{i^{*}}+1/\varepsilon
.
Our question is to determine whether
i^{*}\leq n
or
i^{\ast}>n
, and it follows from a straightforward probability calculation that
\Omega(n)
queries to the entries of
b
are required.
Recall that we are targeting a query complexity of
\Omega(1/\varepsilon^{p})
and hence we set
n=1/\varepsilon^{p}
.

To reduce this problem to our problem, we set

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $x\leq 0$}\\
x&\text{if $0\leq x\leq 1$}\\
1&\text{if $1\leq x$.}\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad a_{i}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $i=1,\dots,n$}\\
-1&\text{if $i=n+1,\dots,2n$.}\end{cases}

Suppose that
i^{*}\leq n
.
If
x=1
, we have the following.
For
i=1,\dots,n
and
i\neq i^{*}
, we have
f(a\cdot x)_{i}=f(1)=1=b_{i}
.
Recall that
b_{i^{*}}=1+1/\varepsilon
.
Hence, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lvert f(a\cdot x)_{i}-b_{i}\rvert^{p}=1/\varepsilon^{p}=n
.
For
i=n+1,\dots,2n
, we have
f(a\cdot x)_{i}=f(-1)=0
and therefore we have
\sum_{i=n+1}^{2n}\lvert f(a\cdot x)_{i}-b_{i}\rvert^{p}=\sum_{i=n+1}^{2n}1^{p}=n
.
Namely, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{OPT}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert f(a\cdot x)-b\rVert_{p}^{p}=2n.

On the other hand, it is easy to check that, when
x<0
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert f(a\cdot x)-b\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lvert f(a\cdot x)_{i}-b_{i}\rvert^{p}\geq n-1+(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+1)^{p}\geq 2n(1+\varepsilon)

Suppose we have a solution
\hat{x}
such that

\displaystyle\lVert f(a\cdot\hat{x})-b\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq(1+c\cdot\varepsilon)\mathsf{OPT}+c\cdot\varepsilon\lVert a\cdot x^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq(1+c\cdot\varepsilon)\cdot 2n+c\cdot\varepsilon\cdot 1^{p}\cdot n

\displaystyle<2n(1+\varepsilon)\quad\text{for a sufficiently small $c>0$}

which implies
\hat{x}>0
.
Similarly, suppose that
i^{*}\geq n+1
, one can show the symmetric result.
We can declare
i^{*}\leq n
if
\hat{x}>0
and
i^{\ast}>n
otherwise. This concludes our reduction.

Extension to
d>1

We consider the problem of solving multiple independent copies of hard instances of
d=1
and reduce this new problem to the regression.
The formal construction is as follows.
Let
m=\Theta(1/\varepsilon^{p\vee 2})
.
We have a
dm
-dimensional vector
b
, which can be partitioned into
d
blocks of
m
-dimensional vectors, with each block drawn from either
D_{0}
or
D_{1}
(the hard instances introduced earlier depending on
p
).
By a straightforward probability calculation, it can be shown that
\Omega(dm)
queries to the entries of
b
are needed to correctly answer, with constant probability, which distribution each block of
m
-dimensional vector is drawn from, for at least
2d/3
blocks.

To reduce it to our problem, let
A
be a
dm
-by-
d
block-diagonal matrix, partitioned into
d^{2}
blocks of
m
-dimensional vectors.
Each diagonal block is the vector
a
which we constructed earlier.
The function
f
remains the same as before.
Suppose we have a solution
\hat{x}
satisfying
Equation

4
.
By the independence between blocks in
b
and the block-diagonal structure of
A
, we can argue that
Equation

4
can be decomposed into the sum of the objective functions for each independent block and declare that each block is drawn from
D_{0}
or
D_{1}
based on the same criteria as in the case of
d=1
.
By the standard counting techniques, at least
2d/3
of the
d
answers are correct and this completes the reduction.
Hence, we achieve the query complexity of
d/\varepsilon^{p\vee 2}
.

We point out that for the canonical case of
f(x)=x
and
p\geq 2
, the previous result of
Yasuda (2024)
gives a stronger lower bound, in terms of
d
, of
\Omega(d^{p/2}/\varepsilon^{p-1})
.
Unfortunately, it is still not clear how to apply the techniques in our setting.

\diag\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\}

.

(X,\|\cdot\|)

B(X)

. When

Lewis Weights

We now define an important concept regarding matrices, which have played critical roles in the construction of space-efficient subspace embeddings.

Definition 4

(
\ell_{p}
-Lewis weights)
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
p\geq 1
. For each
i\in[n]
, the
\ell_{p}
-Lewis weight of
A
for the
i
-th row is defined to be
w_{i}
that satisfies

\displaystyle w_{i}(A)

\displaystyle=(a_{i}^{\top}(A^{\top}W^{1-\frac{2}{p}}A)^{\dagger}a_{i})^{\frac{p}{2}}

where
a_{i}
is the
i
-th row of
A
(as a column vector),
W=\diag\{w_{1},\dots,w_{n}\}
and
\dagger
denotes the pseudoinverse.

When the matrix
A
is clear in context, we will simply write
w_{i}(A)
as
w_{i}
. Adopting that
0\cdot\infty=0
, we have
w_{i}(A)=0
if
a_{i}=0
. The following are a few important properties of Lewis weights; see, e.g.,
(
Wojtaszczyk, 1991
)
for a proof.

Lemma 5

(Properties of Lewis weights)
.

Suppose that
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
has full column rank and Lewis weights
w_{1},\dots,w_{n}
. Let
W=\diag\{w_{1},\dots,w_{n}\}
. The following properties hold.

(a)

\sum_{i}w_{i}=d
;

(b)

There exists a matrix
U\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
such that

(i)

the column space of
U
is the same as that of
A
;

(ii)

w_{i}=\left\lVert U_{i,\cdot}\right\rVert_{2}^{p}
;

(iii)

W^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}U
has orthonormal columns;

(c)

It holds for all vectors
u
in the column space of
A
that
\lVert W^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}u\rVert_{2}\leq d^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\vee p}}\lVert u\rVert_{p}
.

(d)

It holds for all vectors
u
in the column space of
A
that
|u_{i}|\leq d^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\vee p}}w_{i}^{\frac{1}{p}}\lVert u\rVert_{p}
.

Subspace Embeddings

Suppose that
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. We say a matrix
S\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is an
\ell_{p}
-subspace embedding matrix for
A
with distortion
1+\varepsilon
if
(1+\varepsilon)^{-1}\lVert Ax\rVert_{p}\leq\lVert SAx\rVert_{p}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\lVert Ax\rVert_{p}
. The prevailing method to construct
\ell_{p}
-subspace embedding matrices is to sample the rows of
A
according to its Lewis weights.

Lemma 6
.

Suppose that
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
has Lewis weights
w_{1},\dots,w_{n}
. Let
p_{i}\in[0,1]
satisfy that
p_{i}\geq(\beta w_{i})\wedge 1
and
S\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a diagonal matrix with independent diagonal entries
S_{ii}\sim p_{i}^{-1/p}\Ber(p_{i})
. Then with probability at least
0.99
,
S
is an
\ell_{p}
-subspace embedding matrix for
A
with distortion
1+\varepsilon
if

\beta\gtrsim_{p}\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\log\frac{d}{\varepsilon}(\log\log\frac{d}{\varepsilon})^{2},&1<p<2\\
\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\log\frac{d}{\varepsilon},&p=1,2\\
\frac{d^{\frac{p}{2}-1}}{\varepsilon^{2}}(\log d)^{2}\log\frac{d}{\varepsilon}&p>2.\end{cases}

The results for
p\in[1,2]
are due to
Cohen and Peng (2015)
, based on earlier work of
Talagrand (1990)
;
Talagrand (1995)
. The result for
p>2
can be found in
(
Yasuda, 2024
;
Huang et al., 2024
)
, which improves upon the previous bound
\beta\gtrsim(d^{p/2-1}/\varepsilon^{5})(\log d)\log(1/\varepsilon)
in
(
Bourgain et al., 1989
;
Cohen and Peng, 2015
)
.

Covering Numbers and Dudley’s Integral

Suppose that
T
is a pseudometric space endowed with a pseudometric
d
. The diameter of
T
, denoted by
\Diam(T,d)
, is defined as
\Diam(T,d):=\sup_{t,s\in T}\rho(t,s)
.

Given an
r>0
, an
r
-covering of
(T,d)
is a subset
X\subseteq T
such that for every
t\in T
, there exists
x\in X
such that
d(t,x)\leq r
. The covering number
\mathcal{N}(T,d,r)
is the minimum number
K
such that there exists an
r
-covering of cardinality
K
.

The covering numbers are intrinsically related to a subgaussian process on the space
T
that conforms to the pseudometric
d
. This relationship is captured by the well-known Dudley’s integral.

Lemma 7

(Dudley’s integral
Vershynin (2018)
)
.

Let
X_{t}
be a zero-mean stochastic process that is subgaussian w.r.t. a pseudo-metric
d
on the indexing set
T
. Then it holds that

\Pr\left\{\sup_{t,s\in T}\lvert X_{t}-X_{s}\rvert>C\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\log N(T,d,\varepsilon)}d\varepsilon+u\cdot\Diam(T)\right)\right\}\leq 2\exp(-u^{2}),

where
C
is an absolute constant.
As a consequence,

\leq p\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-u_{\bar{x}})_{i}\rvert\cdot\left\lvert\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p-1}+\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{\bar{x}}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p-1}\right\rvert.

For any
i\notin G
and
x\in T
, by Lemma
17
and the definition of
G
, we have

\displaystyle\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-u_{\bar{x}})_{i}\rvert\leq 2\varepsilon\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{\bar{x}}-v)_{i}\rvert,

\displaystyle\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert\leq\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-u_{\bar{x}})_{i}\rvert+\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{\bar{x}}-v)_{i}\rvert\leq(1+2\varepsilon)\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{\bar{x}}-v)_{i}\rvert.

It follows that

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}^{p}\Big\lvert\lvert(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}-\lvert(u_{\bar{x}}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}\Big\rvert\lesssim\varepsilon\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{\bar{x}}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}.

which implies that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in T}\left\lvert\sum_{i\notin G}(S_{ii}^{p}-1)\lambda_{i}^{p}\big(\lvert(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}-\lvert(u_{\bar{x}}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}\big)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\sum_{i\notin G}(S_{ii}^{p}+1)\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{\bar{x}}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}\leq\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{n}(S_{ii}^{p}+1)\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{\bar{x}}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}\lesssim\varepsilon\cdot V,

where we used the assumption of the lemma in the last step.

∎

Now, we also define the set of indices whose term has a high Lewis weight within
G
. Let

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle:=\left\{\,i\in G\;\middle|\;w_{i}>\frac{\varepsilon^{p}d}{n^{2}}\,\right\}.

(33)

We now take care of the terms with low Lewis weights in
Equation

30
, i.e. the indices
i\not\in J
, and hence obtain the second term
\frac{d^{1\vee\frac{p}{2}}\varepsilon^{p}}{\alpha n}R
in
Equation

31
.
We highlight that only the property of the diagonal entries of
S
being in
[0,\frac{1}{\alpha}]
is used in
Lemma

19
.

Lemma 19
.

For any
R\geq\lVert\Lambda A\bar{x}\rVert_{p}^{p}
and
\varepsilon>0
, let
T
be a set that
\{\bar{x}\}\subseteq T\subseteq\{\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;|\;\lVert\Lambda Ax\rVert_{p}^{p}\leq R\,\}
and
J
be the set defined in
Equation

33
.
Suppose
S
is an
n
-by-
n
diagonal matrix whose entries satisfy
0\leq S_{ii}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}
for any
\alpha>0
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in T}\left\lvert\sum_{i\in G\backslash J}(S_{ii}^{p}-1)\Lambda_{ii}^{p}\big(\lvert(f(Ax)-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}-\lvert(f(A\bar{x})-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}\big)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{d^{1\vee\frac{p}{2}}}{\alpha n}R.

Proof.

To ease the notations, let

\displaystyle u_{x}

\displaystyle:=f(Ax)\quad\text{for all $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$}\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{i}:=\Lambda_{ii}\quad\text{for $i\in[n]$.}

Note that by the triangle inequality,

\left\lvert\sum_{i\in G\backslash J}(S_{ii}^{p}-1)\lambda_{i}^{p}\big(\lvert(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}-\lvert(u_{\bar{x}}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}\big)\right\rvert\leq\sum_{i\in G\backslash J}(S_{ii}^{p}+1)\lambda_{i}^{p}\big\lvert\lvert(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}-\lvert(u_{\bar{x}}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}\big\rvert.

Since
i\in G
, by Lemma
17
, we have

\displaystyle\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert\leq\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-u_{\bar{x}})_{i}\rvert+\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{\bar{x}}-v)_{i}\rvert\leq(2+\frac{1}{\varepsilon})d^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\vee p}}w_{i}^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{\frac{1}{p}},

which, together with
i\not\in J
, implies that

\displaystyle\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}\lesssim\frac{d^{(\frac{p}{2}\vee 1)-1}}{\varepsilon^{p}}w_{i}R\lesssim\frac{d^{1\vee\frac{p}{2}}}{n^{2}}R.

Recall that we assume
S_{ii}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}
.
Therefore,

\sup_{x\in T}\left\lvert\sum_{i\in G\backslash J}(S_{ii}^{p}-1)\lambda_{i}^{p}\big(\lvert(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}-\lvert(u_{\bar{x}}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}\big)\right\rvert\lesssim\sum_{i\in G\backslash J}(\frac{1}{\alpha}+1)\cdot\frac{d^{1\vee\frac{p}{2}}}{n^{2}}R\lesssim\frac{d^{1\vee\frac{p}{2}}}{\alpha n}R.\qed

With
Lemma

18
and
Lemma

19
, we only need to take care of the indices in
J
.
Namely the set of indices
i
such that

\displaystyle\lvert(f(A\bar{x})-v)_{i}\rvert\leq\frac{d^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\vee p}}w_{i}^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\varepsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad w_{i}>\frac{\varepsilon^{p}d}{n^{2}}.

Now, we would like to bound the following expression

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in T}\left\lvert\sum_{i\in J}(S_{ii}^{p}-1)\Lambda_{ii}^{p}\big(\lvert(f(Ax)-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}-\lvert(f(A\bar{x})-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}\big)\right\rvert.

We consider bounding its
\ell
-th moment and then apply Markov’s inequality for some
\ell
to be determined later. To that end, consider

\displaystyle\Theta_{S}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{x\in T}\left\lvert\sum_{i\in J}(S_{ii}^{p}-1)\Lambda_{ii}^{p}\big(\lvert(f(Ax)-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}-\lvert(f(A\bar{x})-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}\big)\right\rvert

By the standard symmerization trick, we have

\E_{S}\Theta_{S}^{\ell}\leq 2^{\ell}\E_{\xi,S}\left(\sup_{x\in T}\left\lvert\sum_{i\in J}\xi_{i}\cdot S_{ii}^{p}\Lambda_{ii}^{p}\big(\lvert(f(Ax)-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}-\lvert(f(A\bar{x})-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}\big)\right\rvert\right)^{\ell},

(34)

where
\xi
is a
\lvert J\rvert
-dimensional vector whose entries are independent Rademacher random variable, i.e. each
\xi_{i}
is uniform on
\{-1,1\}
.

Next, we condition on
S
.
Recall that
S_{ii}^{p}
is either
\frac{1}{\alpha}
or
0
, let
I\subseteq J
be the set of indices
i
such that
S_{ii}^{p}=\frac{1}{\alpha}
.
For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define
z_{x}
to be the
n
-dimensional vector whose
i
-th entry is

\displaystyle(z_{x})_{i}

\displaystyle:=\lvert\Lambda_{ii}(f(Ax)-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}-\lvert\Lambda_{ii}(f(A\bar{x})-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}

(35)

Also, we define a pseudometric
\rho
to be

\displaystyle\rho(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle:=\lVert(z_{x})_{I}-(z_{x^{\prime}})_{I}\rVert_{2}\quad\text{for any $x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{i\in I}\big(\lvert\Lambda_{ii}(f(Ax)-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}-\lvert\Lambda_{ii}(f(Ax^{\prime})-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}\big)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

Recall that
(\cdot)_{I}
means we shrink the vector by only retaining the entries whose index is in
I
.
Now, in order to upper bound the right-hand side of
Equation

34
, we seek to upper bound

\E_{\xi}\left(\sup_{x\in T}\left\lvert\langle\xi_{I},(z_{x})_{I}\rangle\right\rvert\right)^{\ell}.

Since

) as

\E_{\xi}\left(\sup_{x\in T}\left\lvert\langle\xi_{I},(z_{x})_{I}\rangle\right\rvert\right)^{\ell}\lesssim C^{\ell}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{\Diam(T,\rho)}\sqrt{\log\mathcal{N}(T,\rho,r)}dr\right)^{\ell}+(\sqrt{\ell}\Diam(T,\rho))^{\ell}\right].

(36)

Recall that
\mathcal{N}(T,\rho,r)
is the covering number of
T
w.r.t.
\rho
and

.
We will prove in

that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\Diam(T,\rho)}\sqrt{\log\mathcal{N}(T,\rho,r)}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha\cdot\Gamma\log^{\frac{5}{4}}d\sqrt{\log\frac{n}{\varepsilon d}}\quad\text{and}\quad\Diam(T,\rho)\lesssim\alpha\cdot\Gamma

(37)

where

\Gamma=\left(\frac{d^{\frac{p}{2}\vee 1}RF^{(p-1)\vee 1}}{\alpha n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}\wedge\frac{1}{2}}.

Taking expectation over
S
, it follows from
Equation

,
Equation

36
and
Equation

37
that

\displaystyle\E_{S}\Theta_{S}^{\ell}\lesssim(C^{\prime}\Gamma)^{\ell}\left(\left(\log^{\frac{5}{4}}d\sqrt{\log\frac{n}{\varepsilon d}}\right)^{\ell}+\sqrt{\ell}^{\ell}\right).

Take

. By Markov inequality, it holds with probability

\Theta_{S}=\sup_{x\in T}\left\lvert\sum_{i\in J}\xi_{i}\cdot S_{ii}^{p}\Lambda_{ii}^{p}\big(\lvert(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}-\lvert(u_{\bar{x}}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}\big)\right\rvert\lesssim\Gamma\cdot\left(\log^{\frac{5}{4}}d\sqrt{\log\frac{n}{\varepsilon d}}+\sqrt{\log\frac{1}{\delta}}\right).

.

.

4.4.3
Diameter Estimates

In order to bound Dudley’s integral in
Equation

37
, we need to bound the covering number
\mathcal{N}(T,\rho,r)
. To this end, we shall bound the metric,
\rho
, and the diameter
\Diam(T,\rho)
. The proof imitates the proofs in earlier works, e.g.,
Ledoux and Talagrand (1991)
;
Huang et al. (2024)
;
Gajjar et al. (2024)
, on subspace embeddings and active regression problems.

Lemma 20
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
,
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
f\in\mathsf{Lip}_{1}
,
\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,
\alpha\in[0,1]
and
S\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be as defined in Lemma
14
and satisfy the same constraints.
Suppose that
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
,
R\geq\lVert\Lambda A\bar{x}\rVert_{p}^{p}
and
F\geq\lVert\Lambda(f(A\bar{x})-v)\rVert_{p}^{p}
.
Let
T
be a set that
\{\bar{x}\}\subseteq T\subseteq\{\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;|\;\lVert\Lambda Ax\rVert_{p}^{p}\leq R\,\}
.
If
I
is a subset of
J
such that

\displaystyle\lVert(\Lambda(f(A\bar{x})-v))_{I}\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha\cdot\lVert\Lambda(f(A\bar{x})-v)\rVert_{p}^{p}\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{x\in T}\lVert(\Lambda(f(Ax)-f(A\bar{x})))_{I}\rVert_{p}^{p}\leq\alpha\cdot F

then, for any
x,x^{\prime}\in T
and
q=\log(\frac{n}{\varepsilon d})
, we have

\displaystyle\rho(x,x^{\prime})\lesssim K\cdot\left(\left\lVert W^{-\frac{1}{p}}\Lambda A(x-x^{\prime})\right\rVert_{w,q}^{\frac{p}{2}\wedge 1}\wedge d^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\vee p}}\left\lVert W^{-\frac{1}{p}}\Lambda A(x-x^{\prime})\right\rVert_{w,p}^{\frac{p}{2}\wedge 1}\right)

and

\displaystyle\Diam(T,\rho)\lesssim d^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\vee p}}KR^{\frac{1}{2}\wedge\frac{1}{p}},

where
W=\diag\{w_{1},\dots,w_{n}\}
and

K=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\alpha dF/n}&\text{for $1\leq p\leq 2$};\\
(\alpha^{p-1}dF^{p-1}/n)^{1/p}&\text{for $p>2$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

As in the proof of
Lemma

18
, we let
u_{x}=f(Ax)
and
\lambda_{i}=\Lambda_{ii}
to simplify the notation. We further define
semi-norms
\lVert u\rVert_{I,\infty}:=\max_{i\in I}|u_{i}|
and
\lVert u\rVert_{I,p}=(\sum_{i\in I}|u_{i}|^{p})^{1/p}
.

For
i\in I
and
x,x^{\prime}\in T
, we have

\displaystyle\lvert(z_{x})_{i}-(z_{x^{\prime}})_{i}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}-\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x^{\prime}}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq p\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})_{i}\rvert\cdot\left(\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p-1}+\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x^{\prime}}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p-1}\right)

where the first inequality is due to the definition in
Equation

35
and the second to the fact that
\lvert\lvert a\rvert^{p}-\lvert b\rvert^{p}\rvert\leq p\lvert a-b\rvert\cdot\lvert\lvert a\rvert^{p-1}+\lvert b\rvert^{p-1}\rvert
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\rho(x,x^{\prime})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I}p^{2}\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})_{i}\rvert^{2}\left(\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p-1}+\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x^{\prime}}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p-1}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i\in I}\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})_{i}\rvert^{2}\left(\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert^{2p-2}+\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x^{\prime}}-v)_{i}\rvert^{2p-2}\right).

(38)

When
1\leq p\leq 2
, we further bound
Equation

38
by

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})_{i}\rvert^{2}\left(\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert^{2p-2}+\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x^{\prime}}-v)_{i}\rvert^{2p-2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\max_{i\in I}\left\{\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})_{i}\rvert^{p}\right\}\cdot\sum_{i\in I}\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})_{i}\rvert^{2-p}\left(\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert^{2p-2}+\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x^{\prime}}-v)_{i}\rvert^{2p-2}\right).

We can then proceed as

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})_{i}\rvert^{2-p}\left(\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert^{2p-2}+\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x^{\prime}}-v)_{i}\rvert^{2p-2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}^{p}\lvert(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})_{i}\rvert^{2-p}\max\{\lvert(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert^{2p-2},\lvert(u_{x^{\prime}}-v)_{i}\rvert^{2p-2}\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}^{p}\lvert(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})_{i}\rvert^{p}\right)^{\frac{2-p}{p}}\left(\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}^{p}\max\{\lvert(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p},\lvert(u_{x^{\prime}}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}\}\right)^{\frac{2p-2}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\lVert\Lambda(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})\rVert_{I,p}^{2-p}\left(\lVert\Lambda(u_{x}-v)\rVert_{I,p}^{p}+\lVert\Lambda(u_{x^{\prime}}-v)\rVert_{I,p}^{p}\right)^{\frac{2p-2}{p}}.

For the
\ell_{p}
-norms in the preceding line, we remind the readers that they have been restricted to the indices in
I
and do not refer to the
\ell_{p}
-norm of the entire vector.

Since
u_{x}-v=(u_{\bar{x}}-v)+(u_{x}-u_{\bar{x}})
, by our assumptions,

\displaystyle\lVert\Lambda(u_{x}-v)\rVert_{I,p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}(\lVert\Lambda(u_{\bar{x}}-v)\rVert_{I,p}^{p}+\lVert\Lambda(u_{x}-u_{\bar{x}})\rVert_{I,p}^{p})

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha\cdot\lVert\Lambda(u_{\bar{x}}-v)\rVert_{p}^{p}+\alpha\cdot F

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha\cdot F.

(39)

Similarly,

\lVert\Lambda(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})\rVert_{I,p}\leq\lVert\Lambda(u_{x}-v)\rVert_{I,p}+\lVert\Lambda(u_{x^{\prime}}-v)\rVert_{I,p}\lesssim(\alpha F)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

It follows that

\rho(x,x^{\prime})^{2}\lesssim\lVert\Lambda(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})\rVert_{I,\infty}^{p}(\alpha F)^{\frac{2-p}{p}}(\alpha F)^{\frac{2p-2}{p}}\leq\alpha F\cdot\lVert\Lambda(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})\rVert_{I,\infty}^{p}.

(40)

When
p>2
, we use the fact that
\lvert z_{i}\rvert^{2p-2}\leq\lvert z_{i}\rvert^{p}\lVert z\rVert_{\infty}^{p-2}
for a vector
z
and so we can proceed from
Equation

38
as

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})_{i}\rvert^{2}\left(\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert^{2p-2}+\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x^{\prime}}-v)_{i}\rvert^{2p-2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\lVert\Lambda(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})\rVert_{I,\infty}^{2}(\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}\lVert\Lambda(u_{x}-v)\rVert_{I,\infty}^{p-2}+\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x^{\prime}}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}\lVert\Lambda(u_{x^{\prime}}-v)\rVert_{I,\infty}^{p-2})

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\lVert\Lambda(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})\rVert_{I,\infty}^{2}\left(\lVert\Lambda(u_{x}-v)\rVert_{I,p}^{p}\lVert\Lambda(u_{x}-v)\rVert_{I,\infty}^{p-2}+\lVert\Lambda(u_{x^{\prime}}-v)\rVert_{I,p}^{p}\lVert\Lambda(u_{x^{\prime}}-v)\rVert_{I,\infty}^{p-2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\,

\displaystyle\alpha F\cdot\lVert\Lambda(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})\rVert_{I,\infty}^{2}\left(\lVert\Lambda(u_{x}-v)\rVert_{I,\infty}^{p-2}+\lVert\Lambda(u_{x^{\prime}}-v)\rVert_{I,\infty}^{p-2}\right)\quad\text{by \lx@cref{creftypecap~refnum}{eq:normi_bound}}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\alpha F\cdot\lVert\Lambda(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})\rVert_{I,\infty}^{2}\left(\lVert\Lambda(u_{x}-v)\rVert_{I,p}^{p-2}+\lVert\Lambda(u_{x^{\prime}}-v)\rVert_{I,p}^{p-2}\right).

Since

\displaystyle\lVert\Lambda(u_{x}-v)\rVert_{I,p}\leq\lVert\Lambda(u_{x}-u_{\bar{x}})\rVert_{I,p}+\lVert\Lambda(u_{\bar{x}}-v)\rVert_{I,p}\lesssim(\alpha F)^{\frac{1}{p}}

we have

\rho(x,x^{\prime})^{2}\lesssim(\alpha F)^{2-\frac{2}{p}}\cdot\lVert\Lambda(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})\rVert_{I,\infty}^{2}.

(41)

Combining
Equation

40
and
Equation

41
yields

\rho(x,x^{\prime})\lesssim\left(\alpha F\right)^{(1-\frac{1}{p})\vee\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\Lambda(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})\rVert_{I,\infty}^{\frac{p}{2}\wedge 1}

(42)

and our next task to upper bound
\lVert\Lambda(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})\rVert_{I,\infty}
.

Recall that
w_{i}\leq 2d/n
, we have by the Lipschitz condition and
Lemma

5
(d),

\displaystyle\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})_{i}\rvert\leq\lvert(\Lambda(Ax-Ax^{\prime}))_{i}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq w_{i}^{\frac{1}{p}}d^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\vee p}}\lVert\Lambda A(x-x^{\prime})\rVert_{p}

(43)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}d^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\vee p}}\lVert\Lambda A(x-x^{\prime})\rVert_{p}.

Plugging this result into
Equation

42
immediately leads to

\rho(x,x^{\prime})\lesssim K\cdot d^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\vee p}}\left\lVert\Lambda A(x-x^{\prime})\right\rVert_{p}^{\frac{p}{2}\wedge 1}.

(44)

Alternatively,

\displaystyle\lvert\lambda_{i}(u_{x}-u_{x^{\prime}})_{i}\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot\frac{\lvert\Lambda_{ii}(Ax-Ax^{\prime})_{i}\rvert}{w_{i}^{\frac{1}{p}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(w_{i}\big(\frac{\lvert\Lambda_{ii}(Ax-Ax^{\prime})_{i}\rvert}{w_{i}^{\frac{1}{p}}}\big)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\quad\text{since $w_{i}>\frac{\varepsilon^{p}d}{n^{2}}$}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\lVert W^{-\frac{1}{p}}\Lambda A(x-x^{\prime})\right\rVert_{w,q}\quad\text{recall that $\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{w,p}=(\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}|x_{i}|^{q})^{1/q}$}

and thus

\rho(x,x^{\prime})\lesssim K\cdot\left\lVert W^{-\frac{1}{p}}\Lambda A(x-x^{\prime})\right\rVert_{w,q}^{\frac{p}{2}\wedge 1}.

(45)

as claimed.

Finally, we bound
\Diam(T,\rho)
. By the definition of
T
and
Equation

44
, we have that
\lVert\Lambda A(x-x^{\prime})\rVert_{p}^{p}\lesssim R
. The claimed upper bound on
\Diam(T,\rho)
follows immediately.
∎

4.4.4
Bounding Dudley’s Integral

In this section, we will prove
Equation

37
, i.e.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\Diam(T,\rho)}\sqrt{\log\mathcal{N}(T,\rho,r)}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha\cdot\Gamma\cdot\poly\log n,

where
\Gamma
is as defined in
Equation

21
. To further simplify the notation, let

\phi=\frac{p}{2}\wedge 1,\quad\beta=\frac{1}{p}\wedge\frac{1}{2},\quad\gamma=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p\vee 2},\quad\theta=(1-\frac{1}{p})\vee\frac{1}{2}.

Note that

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{N}(T,\rho,r)

\displaystyle\leq\log\mathcal{N}(R^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot B_{p}(\Lambda A),Kd^{\gamma}\lVert W^{-\frac{1}{p}}\Lambda A(\cdot)\rVert_{w,p}^{\phi},r)

by
Lemma

20

\displaystyle=\log\mathcal{N}(B_{p}(\Lambda A),\lVert W^{-\frac{1}{p}}\Lambda A(\cdot)\rVert_{w,p},\frac{1}{R^{\frac{1}{p}}}(\frac{r}{Kd^{\gamma}})^{\frac{1}{\phi}})

\displaystyle=\log\mathcal{N}(B_{w,p}(E),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{w,p},\frac{1}{R^{\frac{1}{p}}d^{\gamma}}(\frac{r}{K})^{\frac{1}{\phi}})

since
\gamma/\phi=\gamma

where
E=\colspace(W^{-\frac{1}{p}}\Lambda A)
and is endowed with norms
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{w,p}
for
p\geq 1
.

Now, we shall split the integral domain into two parts
[0,\lambda]
and
[\lambda,\Diam(T,\rho)]
for some
\lambda\leq\frac{1}{2}KR^{\beta}d^{\gamma}
to be determined later (note that
\phi=p\beta
). Note that when
r\leq\lambda
, we have
(r/K)^{1/\phi}/(R^{\frac{1}{p}}d^{\gamma})\leq(\frac{1}{2})^{\phi}<1
.

By
Lemma

8
Case 1, we have (letting
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda/(KR^{\beta}d^{\gamma})
)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\lambda}\sqrt{\log\mathcal{N}(T,\rho,r)}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\lambda}\sqrt{\log\mathcal{N}(B_{w,p}(E),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{w,p},\frac{1}{R^{\frac{1}{p}}d^{\gamma}}(\frac{r}{K})^{\frac{1}{\phi}})}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\sqrt{\log\mathcal{N}(B_{w,p}(E),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{w,p},s^{\frac{1}{\phi}})}Kd^{\gamma}R^{\beta}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\sqrt{d\log\frac{1}{s}}Kd^{\gamma}R^{\beta}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{d}\cdot Kd^{\gamma}R^{\beta}\int_{0}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\log\frac{1}{s}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{d}\cdot Kd^{\gamma}R^{\beta}\cdot\lambda^{\prime}\log\frac{1}{\lambda^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda\sqrt{d}\log\frac{d^{\gamma}R^{\beta}K}{\lambda}.

(46)

To handle the integral over

, we bound

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{N}(T,\rho,r)

\displaystyle\leq\log\mathcal{N}(R^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot B_{p}(\Lambda A),K\lVert W^{-\frac{1}{p}}\Lambda A(\cdot)\rVert_{w,q}^{\phi},r)

where

for

For

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{\frac{1}{2}}K\sqrt{\log(\frac{n}{\varepsilon d})\sqrt{\log d}}\int_{\lambda}^{\Diam(T,\rho)}\frac{1}{r}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{\frac{1}{2}}K\sqrt{\log\frac{n}{\varepsilon d}\sqrt{\log d}}\log\frac{\Diam(T,\rho)}{\lambda}.

(47)

For

\displaystyle\int_{\lambda}^{\Diam(T,\rho)}\sqrt{\log\mathcal{N}(B_{w,p}(E),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{w,q},\frac{r}{R^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot K})}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\;

\displaystyle d^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\sqrt{\log\frac{n}{\varepsilon d}}R^{\frac{1}{p}}K\int_{\lambda}^{\Diam(T,\rho)}\frac{1}{r}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\;

\displaystyle d^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\sqrt{\log\frac{n}{\varepsilon d}}R^{\frac{1}{p}}K\log\frac{\Diam(T,\rho)}{\lambda}.

(49)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda\sqrt{d}\log\frac{d^{\gamma}R^{\beta}K}{\lambda}+d^{\gamma}R^{\beta}K\sqrt{\log\frac{n}{\varepsilon d}}\log\frac{d^{\gamma}KR^{\beta}}{\lambda}

The following is a weaker version of
Lemma

14
for reducing

to
\poly(d/\varepsilon)
.

Lemma 21
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
,
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
f\in\mathsf{Lip}_{1}
,
\varepsilon>0
,
\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,
\alpha\in[0,1]
,
S\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
R\in\mathbb{R}
be as defined in Lemma
14
and satisfy the same constraints.
Suppose that
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
such that
\alpha n\gtrsim(d^{(\frac{p}{2}\vee 1)+1}/\varepsilon^{p+2})\log(1/\varepsilon)
and
R\geq\lVert\Lambda Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}\vee\lVert\Lambda(f(Ax^{\ast})-v)\rVert_{p}^{p}
.
Let
T=\left\{\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;\middle|\;\lVert\Lambda Ax\rVert_{p}^{p}\leq R\,\right\}
.
When conditioned on

\displaystyle\lVert S\Lambda(f(Ax^{*})-v)\rVert_{p}^{p}\lesssim R,

it holds with probability at least
0.99
that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in T}\left\lvert(\lVert S\Lambda(f(Ax)-v)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert S\Lambda(f(Ax^{*})-v)\rVert_{p}^{p})-(\lVert\Lambda(f(Ax)-v)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert\Lambda(f(Ax^{*})-v)\rVert_{p}^{p})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon R,

(50)

where
C
is an absolute constant.

Proof.

Recall that the error bound in
Lemma

14
consists of three terms. By the same proofs as in
Lemmas

18
and
19
, the first two terms remain the same, both of which are now bounded by
C\varepsilon R^{p}
under our assumptions. The rest of the proof is devoted to deriving a similar bound for the third term. Recall that we need to upper bound

\sup_{x\in T}\left\lvert\sum_{i\in J}S_{ii}^{p}\xi_{i}(z_{x})_{i}\right\rvert,

where
z_{x}
is as defined in
Equation

35
and
\{\xi_{i}\}
are independent Rademacher variables. We shall use a net argument here.

Fix an
x\in T
. Let
W_{i}=S_{ii}^{p}\xi_{i}(z_{x})_{i}
, then
\E W_{i}=0
and

\displaystyle\lvert W_{i}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\lvert(z_{x})_{i}\rvert

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha}\lvert(\Lambda(f(Ax)-v)_{i}^{p}-(\Lambda(f(Ax^{*})-v)_{i}^{p}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot 2^{p-1}(\lvert\Lambda_{ii}(f(Ax)-f(Ax^{\ast}))_{i}\rvert^{p}+\lvert\Lambda_{ii}(f(Ax^{\ast})-v)_{i}\rvert^{p})+\lvert\Lambda_{ii}(f(Ax^{*})-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\lvert\Lambda_{ii}(Ax-Ax^{\ast})_{i}\rvert^{p}+\lvert\Lambda_{ii}(f(Ax^{*})-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}\right).

Now we use
Equation

43
for the first term in the brackets and the definition of
G
in
Equation

32
for the second term. We proceed as

\displaystyle|W_{i}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\frac{d}{n}\cdot d^{(\frac{p}{2}-1)\vee 0}\left\lVert\Lambda A(x-x^{\ast})\right\rVert_{p}^{p}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{p}}d^{(\frac{p}{2}-1)\vee 0}\cdot\frac{d}{n}\cdot R\right)\lesssim\underbrace{\frac{1}{\alpha n\varepsilon^{p}}\cdot d^{\frac{p}{2}\vee 1}R}_{:=\Delta}.

Next we bound
\E(\sum_{i\in J}W_{i}^{2})
.

\E\sum_{i\in J}W_{i}^{2}=\sum_{i\in J}\E S_{ii}^{2p}(z_{x})_{i}^{2}\\
\leq\sum_{i}\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\max_{i}\lvert(z_{x})_{i}\rvert\cdot\lvert(z_{x})_{i}\rvert\lesssim\Delta\cdot\sum_{i\in J}\lvert(z_{x})_{i}\rvert.

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in J}\lvert(z_{x})_{i}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\Lambda(f(Ax)-v)\rVert_{p}^{p}+\lVert\Lambda(f(Ax^{\ast})-v)\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}(\lVert\Lambda(f(Ax)-f(Ax^{\ast}))\rVert_{p}^{p}+\lVert\Lambda(f(Ax^{\ast})-v)\rVert_{p}^{p})+\lVert\Lambda(f(Ax^{\ast})-v)\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\lVert\Lambda A(x-x^{\ast})\rVert_{p}^{p}+(2^{p-1}+1)\lVert\Lambda(f(Ax^{\ast})-v)\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\Lambda Ax\rVert_{p}^{p}+\lVert\Lambda Ax^{\ast}\rVert_{p}^{p}+\lVert\Lambda(f(Ax^{\ast})-v)\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim R.

Hence

\E\sum_{i\in J}W_{i}^{2}\lesssim\Delta\cdot R.

It follows from Bernstein’s inequality that

\displaystyle\Pr\left\{\left\lvert\sum_{i\in J}W_{i}\right\rvert\geq\eta R\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(-c\frac{\eta^{2}R^{2}}{\Delta R^{p}+\Delta\cdot\eta R}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(-c^{\prime}\frac{\eta^{2}R}{\Delta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-Cd\log\frac{1}{\eta}\right),

provided that

\eta^{2}R\gtrsim d\cdot\Delta=\begin{cases}d^{\frac{p}{2}+1}\log\frac{1}{\eta}\cdot R/(\alpha n\varepsilon^{p})&p>2\\
d^{2}R\log\frac{1}{\eta}/(\alpha n\varepsilon^{p})&1\leq p<2\end{cases}

or

\alpha n\gtrsim\frac{d^{1+(\frac{p}{2}\vee 1)}}{\eta^{2}\varepsilon^{p}}\log\frac{1}{\eta}.

(51)

To summarize, we have shown that when
Equation

51
, for each fixed
x\in T
that with probability at least
1-\delta
(where
\delta=\exp(-Cd\log(1/\eta))
), it holds

\left\lvert\sum_{i\in J}S_{ii}^{p}\xi_{i}(z_{x})_{i}\right\rvert\leq\eta R.

To obtain an upper bound for the supremum over
x\in T
, we employ a standard net argument. Let
N\subseteq T
be an
(\eta R^{\frac{1}{p}})
-net of
T
such that
\lvert N\rvert\leq(3/\eta)^{d}
. By a union bound, we have with probability at least
1-\lvert N\rvert\delta\geq 0.99
that

\sup_{x\in N}\left\lvert\sum_{i\in J}S_{ii}^{p}\xi_{i}(z_{x})_{i}\right\rvert\leq\eta R.

For an
\Lambda Ax\in T
, there exists
\Lambda Ay\in N
such that
\lVert\Lambda A(x-y)\rVert_{p}\leq\eta R^{\frac{1}{p}}
. Thus

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in T}\left\lvert\sum_{i\in J}S_{ii}^{p}\xi_{i}(z_{x})_{i}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{y\in N}\left\lvert\sum_{i\in J}S_{ii}^{p}\xi_{i}(z_{y})_{i}\right\rvert+\sup_{x\in T}\left\lvert\sum_{i\in J}S_{ii}^{p}\xi_{i}(z_{x}-z_{y})_{i}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\eta R+\sup_{x\in T}\left\lvert\sum_{i\in J}S_{ii}^{p}\xi_{i}(z_{x}-z_{y})_{i}\right\rvert.

We bound the error term by Hölder’s inequality as

\displaystyle\quad\ \left\lvert\sum_{i\in J}S_{ii}^{p}\xi_{i}(z_{x}-z_{y})_{i}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in J}S_{ii}^{p}\left\lvert(z_{x}-z_{y})_{i}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in J}S_{ii}^{p}\left\lvert\lvert\Lambda_{ii}(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}-\lvert\Lambda_{ii}(u_{y}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i\in J}S_{ii}^{p}\lvert\Lambda_{ii}(u_{x}-u_{y})_{i}\rvert\left(\lvert\Lambda_{ii}(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p-1}+\lvert\Lambda_{ii}(u_{y}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p-1}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{i\in J}S_{ii}^{p}\lvert\Lambda_{ii}(Ax-Ay)_{i}\rvert^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\left(\sum_{i\in J}S_{ii}^{p}\lvert\Lambda_{ii}(u_{x}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\sum_{i\in J}S_{ii}^{p}\lvert\Lambda_{ii}(u_{y}-v)_{i}\rvert^{p}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\lVert S\Lambda A(x-y)\rVert_{p}(\lVert S\Lambda(f(Ax)-v)\rVert_{p}^{p-1}+\lVert S\Lambda(f(Ay)-v)\rVert_{p}^{p-1})

Using the the subspace embedding property of
S
,

\lVert S\Lambda A(x-y)\rVert_{p}\leq 2\lVert\Lambda A(x-y)\rVert_{p}\leq 2\eta R^{\frac{1}{p}}

and

\displaystyle\lVert S\Lambda(f(Ax)-v)\rVert_{p}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert S\Lambda(f(Ax)-f(Ax^{\ast}))\rVert_{p}^{p-1}+\lVert S\Lambda(f(Ax^{\ast})-v)\rVert_{p}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert S\Lambda A(x-x^{\ast})\rVert_{p}^{p-1}+\lVert S\Lambda(f(Ax^{\ast})-v)\rVert_{p}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\Lambda A(x-x^{\ast})\rVert_{p}^{p-1}+\lVert S\Lambda(f(Ax^{\ast})-v)\rVert_{p}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{1-\frac{1}{p}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in T}\left\lvert\sum_{i\in J}S_{ii}^{p}\xi_{i}(z_{x}-z_{y})_{i}\right\rvert\lesssim\eta R.

Therefore,

\left\lvert\sum_{i\in J}S_{ii}^{p}\xi_{i}(z_{x})_{i}\right\rvert\lesssim\eta R

and the claimed result follows from setting
\eta\sim\varepsilon
.

\displaystyle\leq 100\mathsf{OPT}+\varepsilon^{2}\lVert\Lambda Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p},

where the last inequality holds with probability

by Markov inequality.
For

, we have

\displaystyle\lVert S^{\circ}\Lambda Ax^{\circ}\rVert^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\Lambda Ax^{\circ}\rVert^{p}\quad\text{recall that $S^{\circ}$ is an $\ell_{p}$ subspace embedding.}

To further bound the term
\lVert\Lambda Ax^{\circ}\rVert^{p}
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert\Lambda Ax^{\circ}\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\lVert S^{\circ}\Lambda(f(Ax^{*})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}+\lVert\Lambda Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}\quad\text{by the optimality of $x^{\circ}$}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{100}{\varepsilon^{2}}\mathsf{OPT}+\lVert\Lambda Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}\quad\text{by Markov inequality with probability $0.99$.}

Note that this bound is not enough to finish our proof.
However, it implies that
x^{\circ}\in T
where

is the set defined in
Lemma

21
with

.
By
Lemma

21
with our choice of

, with probability

, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert(\lVert S^{\circ}\Lambda(f(Ax^{\circ})-v)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert S^{\circ}\Lambda(f(Ax^{*})-v)\rVert_{p}^{p})-(\lVert\Lambda(f(Ax^{\circ})-v)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert\Lambda(f(Ax^{*})-v)\rVert_{p}^{p})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{4}\cdot(\frac{1000}{\varepsilon^{2}}\mathsf{OPT}+\lVert\Lambda Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p})

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\cdot(\mathsf{OPT}+\lVert\Lambda Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}).

Then, we have

\displaystyle\lVert\Lambda Ax^{\circ}\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\bigg(\lVert S^{\circ}\Lambda(f(Ax^{*})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert S^{\circ}\Lambda(f(Ax^{\circ})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}\bigg)+\lVert\Lambda Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}\quad\text{by the optimality of $x^{\circ}$}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\bigg(\lVert\Lambda(f(Ax^{\circ})-v)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\lVert\Lambda(f(Ax^{*})-v)\rVert_{p}^{p}+\varepsilon^{2}\cdot(\mathsf{OPT}+\lVert\Lambda Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p})\bigg)+\lVert\Lambda Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathsf{OPT}+\lVert\Lambda Ax^{*}\rVert_{p}^{p}\quad\text{by the optimality of $x^{*}$.}

(63)

Plugging

\displaystyle\lVert\Lambda(f(A\hat{x})-b)\rVert_{p}^{p}-\mathsf{OPT}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\cdot(\mathsf{OPT}+\lVert\Lambda Ax^{*}\rVert^{p}).

This completes the proof for the query complexity without dependence on

.
The overall failure probability is at most

in the above argument.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we consider the active regression problem of the single-index model, which asks to solve
\min_{x}\lVert f(Ax)-b\rVert_{p}
, with
f
being a Lipschitz function,
A
fully accessible and
b
only accessible via entry queries.
The goal is to minimize the number of queries to the entries of
b
while achieving an accurate solution to the regression problem.
Previous work on single-index model has only achieved constant-factor approximations
(
Gajjar et al., 2023a
;
Gajjar et al., 2023b
;
Huang et al., 2024
;
Gajjar et al., 2024
)
.
In this paper, we achieve a
(1+\varepsilon)
-approximation with
\tilde{O}(d^{\frac{p}{2}\vee 1}/\varepsilon^{p\vee 2})
queries and we show that this query complexity is tight for
1\leq p\leq 2
up to logarithmic factors. Furthermore, we prove that the
1/\varepsilon^{p}
dependence is tight for
p>2
and we leave the full tightness of
d^{p/2}/\varepsilon^{p}
as an open problem for future work.

Lemma 31

Suppose that

W

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{N}(B_{w,p}(W^{-1/p}A),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{w,p},t)

\displaystyle\lesssim d\log\frac{1}{t}.

Proof.

This is a standard result following from a standard volume argument, which we reproduce below for completeness.
Suppose that
E
is the column space of
W^{-1/p}A
and is endowed with norms
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{w,p}

Using the notation simplification defined in

Consider a maximal
t

, then the balls
x+\frac{t}{2}B_{w,p}
(

are contained in

It is easy to check that

(B_{w,p},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{w,p})

\mathcal{N}(B_{w,p}(W^{-1/p}A),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{w,p},t)\leq|N|\leq(1+2/t)^{d}
.

Shapes of Unit Lattices in D p D_{p} -number fields
Robert Harron, Erik Holmes, Sameera Vemulapalli

Abstract
. The unit group of the ring of integers of a number field, modulo torsion, is a lattice via the logarithmic Minkowski embedding. We examine the shape of this lattice, which we call the unit shape , within the family of prime degree p p number fields whose Galois closure has dihedral Galois group D p D_{p} and a unique real embedding. In the case p = 5 p=5 , we prove that the unit shapes lie on a single hypercycle on the modular surface (in this case, the modular surface is the space of shapes of rank 2 2 lattices). For general p p , we show that the unit shapes are contained in a finite union of translates of periodic torus orbits in the space of shapes.

1.
Introduction

The unit group of the ring of integers of a number field, modulo torsion, has the structure of a lattice via the logarithmic Minkowski embedding. This paper investigates the following general question: which lattices can arise this way? Let
p\geq 5
be a prime. In this paper, we deal with the case of degree
p
number fields with one real embedding whose Galois closure has dihedral Galois group
D_{p}
. Motivated by
Figure

1
, we ask:

Which lattices arise as unit lattices of
D_{p}
-extensions with one real embedding?

(By standard abuse of notation in arithmetic statistics, a
D_{p}
-extension is a degree
p
number field whose Galois closure has Galois group
D_{p}
). The main result of this paper,
Theorem

1.3
, describes a finite union of translates of periodic torus orbits which contains the unit lattices of
every

D_{p}
-extension with one real embedding. When
p=5
, Dirichlet’s unit theorem shows that unit lattice has rank
2
, and the space of rank
2
lattices up to scaling, rotation, and reflection is identified with the quotient
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z})\backslash\mathfrak{H}
where
\mathfrak{H}
is the complex upper half plane. In this case, we prove that
every
such unit lattice lie on the curve shown in
Figure

1
, and that this curve is a hypercycle on the upper half plane
\mathfrak{H}
.

1.1.
Motivation

We focus on the case of
D_{p}
-extensions for two reasons. First, when starting this project, we noticed that when
p=5
, computational data from the LMFDB (the
L
-functions and modular forms database) seemed to imply that the unit lattices are constrained; see
Figure

1
. This is one of the simplest cases where the unit lattice seems to be constrained (unlike the case of totally real
S_{3}
-cubic extensions – see
[
DS18
]
), so we seek to explain it. Second, in this case, we have the following fantastic result of Moser (see
[
Mos79
, Proposition III.1]
) which explicitly describes the
\mathbf{Z}[D_{p}]
-module structure of the unit lattice of the Galois closure of our degree
p
extension. It is this explicit description that is the key input in this paper. For a general group
G
and a Galois
G
-extension, it is extremely difficult to determine the
\mathbf{Z}[G]
-module structure of the unit lattice.

1.2.
Setup

Let
K
be a degree
n
number field. Let
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r}
be the real embeddings of
K
and
\tau_{1},\overline{\tau_{1}},\dots,\tau_{s},\overline{\tau_{s}}
be the pairs of complex conjugate embeddings of
K
, where here
r+2s=n
. Let
E_{K}
be the group of units of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
modulo torsion. The
logarithmic embedding

\displaystyle\Log\colon E_{K}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbf{R}^{r+s}

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\mapsto(\log|\sigma_{1}(u)|,\ldots,\log|\sigma_{r}(u)|,2\log|\tau_{1}(u)|,\ldots,2\log|\tau_{s}(u)|)

presents
E_{K}
as a lattice of rank
r+s-1
. We say
E_{K}
is the
unit lattice
of
K
. See
[
Neu99
, Chapter 1.7]
for details.

In this paper, we study these lattices up to scaling, rotation, and reflection. The shape of a rank
n
lattice
\Lambda
is denoted
\sh(\Lambda)
and is defined to be its equivalence class under scaling, rotation, and reflection. It is typically written as an element of the following double coset space:

\mathcal{S}_{n}\coloneqq\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbf{Z})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbf{R})/\mathrm{GO}_{n}(\mathbf{R})

Under this equivalence, the rows of the matrix in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbf{R})
are the vectors which form a basis of the lattice. We call the shape of the unit lattice the
unit shape
. (See
[
Mil17
, Chapter 2]
for details).

1.3.
Statement of results

For simplicity, we begin by stating the results for
p=5
.

1.3.1.
The quintic case

Recall that
\mathcal{S}_{2}
can be identified with the quotient
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z})\backslash\mathfrak{H}
, where here
\mathfrak{H}
is the upper half plane. Let
x,y
be coordinates for
\mathfrak{H}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K
be a
D_{5}
-extension with a unique real embedding. Then, the unit shape of
K
lies on the arc of the circle

\displaystyle\left(x+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(y-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}\right)^{2}=\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^{2}

from
\frac{1+i\sqrt{3}}{2}
to
-\frac{1}{2}+i\frac{5}{2\sqrt{3}}
in
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z})\backslash\mathfrak{H}
.

We now sketch a proof of
Theorem

1.1
, which has four key steps.

(1)

For every such
D_{5}
-extension
K
of
\mathbf{Q}
with Galois closure
L
, the action of
D_{5}
on
E_{L}
presents
E_{L}
as a
\mathbf{Z}[D_{5}]
-module. This action restricts to an action of a smaller ring
\mathbf{Z}[\sigma+\sigma^{-1}]
on
E_{K}
. (Here,
\sigma
is a
5
-cycle in
D_{5}
).

(2)

Via the logarithmic embedding, the action of
\mathbf{Z}[\sigma+\sigma^{-1}]
on
E_{K}
gives rise to an action of
\mathbf{R}[\sigma+\sigma^{-1}]
on
E_{K}\otimes\mathbf{R}\simeq\mathbf{R}^{3}
.

(3)

Due to work of Moser (
[
Mos79
]
),
\mathbf{Z}[\sigma+\sigma^{-1}]\simeq\mathbf{Z}[\zeta_{5}+\zeta_{5}^{-1}]
and as a module over this ring,
E_{K}
is isomorphic to a fractional ideal of
\mathbf{Q}^{+}\coloneqq\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{5}+\zeta_{5}^{-1})
. Therefore,
E_{K}\otimes\mathbf{R}
and
\mathbf{Q}^{+}\otimes\mathbf{R}
are isomorphic as
\mathbf{R}[\sigma+\sigma^{-1}]\simeq\mathbf{R}[\zeta_{5}+\zeta_{5}^{-1}]
-modules, but the quadratic forms on the two spaces are slightly different.

(4)

Now, for every number field
M
, the lattice shapes of all discrete and nonzero
\mathcal{O}_{M}
-submodules of
M\otimes\mathbf{R}
in
M\otimes\mathbf{R}
forms a finite union of torus orbits in the moduli space of lattices. Therefore, the lattice shape of
E_{K}
lies in a translate of this finite union of torus orbits.

Next, we say a few words about the arc mentioned in
Theorem

1.1
, which has an interesting interpretation in the hyperbolic plane. Endow the upper half plane
\mathfrak{H}
with its usual hyperbolic geometry. A geodesic in this geometry is given by either an arc of a (Euclidean) half-circle whose center lies on the real axis or a segment of a vertical ray. It turns our that a connected component of the set of points equidistant from a geodesic is itself either an arc of a circle or a line segment, respectively. Such curves are (sometimes) called
hypercycles
. Given a geodesic
\gamma
whose diameter is the real interval
[A,B]
, the hypercycles of
\gamma
are the arcs in
\mathfrak{H}
lying on circles whose intersection with
\mathbf{R}
is
\{A,B\}
. In the case that
\gamma
is a segment of the vertical line,
x=A
, the hypercycles of
\gamma
are segments of lines with non-zero slope that intersect the real axis at
x=A
.

In our case, let
\gamma
be the geodesic arc in
\mathfrak{H}
connecting the points
\frac{1+i\sqrt{5}}{3}
and
\frac{-1+i\sqrt{5}}{2}
. This is an arc of the circle centered at
-1/2
of radius
\sqrt{5}/2
. Let
\psi
denote the hypercycle a distance
\frac{1}{2}\log(5/3)
from
\gamma
, given by the equation:

\displaystyle\left(x+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(y-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}\right)^{2}=\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^{2},

with endpoints
\frac{1+i\sqrt{3}}{2}
and
-\frac{1}{2}+i\frac{5}{2\sqrt{3}}
.
This is the arc mentioned in the statement of
Theorem

1.1
. Our experimental data suggests that the unit shapes are dense on this arc, but we do not know how to prove this.

Why do the shapes of unit lattices lie on a hypercycle? We prove in
Theorem

1.3
that for degree
p
extensions with Galois group
D_{p}
and
1
real embedding, the shapes of unit lattices lie on a finite union of translates of periodic torus orbits. When
p=5
, the shapes lie on a single translate of a periodic torus orbit. Moreover, this particular translate of this periodic torus orbit happens to be a hypercycle.

Moser’s explicit description of the
\mathbf{Z}[D_{5}]
-module structure of
E_{L}
involves the quadratic field
\mathbf{Q}^{+}=\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{5}+\zeta_{5}^{-1})
, and thus we observe an interesting relationship between the regulator of
K
and the norm form of
\mathbf{Q}^{+}
. Let
A^{+}=\mathbf{Z}[\zeta_{5}+\zeta_{5}^{-1}]
be the maximal order of
\mathbf{Q}^{+}
, and let
N(x_{0},x_{1})
be the norm form of
A^{+}
with respect to the basis
\{1,\zeta_{5}+\zeta_{5}^{-1}\}
. A straightforward calculation shows that
N(x_{0},x_{1})=x_{0}^{2}-x_{0}x_{1}-x_{1}^{2}
, and that this indefinite quadratic form corresponds to the geodesic
\gamma
in
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z})\backslash\mathfrak{H}
. Let
\sigma\in D_{5}
be a
5
-cycle.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
K
be a
D_{5}
-extension with a unique real embedding
\iota
. Then:

(1)

there exists a unit
u_{0}\in E_{K}
such that
\{u_{0},u_{1}=\sigma(u_{0})+\sigma^{-1}(u_{0})\}
forms a basis for
E_{K}
;

(2)

and for any such unit
u_{0}
, the regulator of
K
is equal to

\lvert N(\log\lvert\iota(u_{0})\rvert,\log\lvert\iota(u_{1})\rvert)\rvert.

Part
(1)
of
Theorem

1.2
follows from Moser’s work, so our contribution is part
(2)
.

1.3.2.
Generalizing to all primes
p

We first introduce some notation needed to state our generalization to all
p
. Let
p\geq 5
be a prime and
r=(p-1)/2
. In this case the unit lattice of a
D_{p}
-extension has rank
r
. Let
\mathcal{G}_{r}
be the space of real symmetric positive definite
r\times r
matrices up to scaling and let matrices
M\in\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbf{R})
act on
\mathcal{G}_{r}
by conjugation, i.e.
MGM^{T}
for
G\in\mathcal{G}_{r}
. Note that the space of shapes of rank
r
lattices,
\mathcal{S}_{r}
, can be identified with
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbf{Z})\backslash\mathcal{G}_{r}
.

In the case of general
p
, our unit shapes will be contained in finitely many translates of an orbit of a certain subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbf{R})
acting upon a certain element
G_{\unit}\in\mathcal{G}_{r}
. We now define
G_{\unit}
. The cyclotomic field
\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{p})
contains the totally real subfield
\mathbf{Q}^{+}=\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{p}+\zeta_{p}^{-1})
, which has ring of integers
A^{+}=\mathbf{Z}[\zeta_{p}+\zeta_{p}^{-1}]
. Since
\mathbf{Q}^{+}
is totally real, the ring of integers is a lattice with respect to its trace pairing. Let
G
be the Gram matrix of the trace form of
A^{+}
with respect to the
\mathbf{Z}
-basis
B=[1,\zeta_{p}+\zeta_{p}^{-1},\ldots,\zeta_{p}^{r-1}+\zeta_{p}^{1-r}]
. Let
\widetilde{G}
be the
r\times(r+1)
matrix obtained by adjoining a
zeroing column
to
G
: the column with
j
–th entry given by
-\sum_{i=1}^{r}G_{ji}
. Then define

G_{\unit}\coloneqq\widetilde{G}\widetilde{G}^{T}.

The matrix
G_{\unit}
is the Gram matrix of the lattice spanned by the rows of
\widetilde{G}
.

We now describe the subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbf{R})
that will act on
G_{\unit}
. Let
\mathcal{T}
be the diagonal torus of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbf{R})
. Let
j_{1},\dots,j_{r}
be the
r
embeddings of
\mathbf{Q}^{+}
into the real numbers, ordered so that
j_{i}(\zeta_{p}+\zeta_{p}^{-1})=\zeta_{p}^{i}+\zeta_{p}^{-i}
, and let
P
be the matrix whose rows are given by the embeddings of
B
, so

P=\begin{bmatrix}1&\dots&1\\
j_{1}(\zeta_{p}+\zeta_{p}^{-1})&\dots&j_{r}(\zeta_{p}+\zeta_{p}^{-1})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
j_{1}(\zeta_{p}^{r-1}+\zeta_{p}^{1-r})&\dots&j_{r}(\zeta_{p}^{r-1}+\zeta_{p}^{1-r})\end{bmatrix}.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{p}=P\mathcal{T}P^{-1}
.

We now describe the finitely many translates of the orbit of
\mathcal{T}_{p}
on
G_{\unit}
. For every class
[\mathfrak{a}]\in\Cl(\mathbf{Q}^{+})
, pick a representative ideal
\mathfrak{a}
and a
\mathbf{Z}
-basis
B_{\mathfrak{a}}=[v_{1},\dots,v_{r}]
. Let
M_{\mathfrak{a}}
be the matrix sending
B
to
B_{\mathfrak{a}}
, so
M_{\mathfrak{a}}B^{T}=B_{\mathfrak{a}}^{T}
. For every
[\mathfrak{a}]\in\Cl(\mathbf{Q}^{+})
, consider the image of
G_{\unit}
under the action of
M_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathcal{T}_{p}
; this is a set in
\mathcal{G}_{r}
, and we call it
\mathcal{O}_{[\mathfrak{a}]}
. Because
\mathcal{S}_{r}=\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbf{Z})\backslash\mathcal{G}_{r}
, we obtain
[\mathcal{O}_{[\mathfrak{a}]}]\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{r}
via the quotient. Then, the following generalization of
Theorem

1.1
describes the space of unit shapes:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
K
be a
D_{p}
-extension with a unique real embedding and Galois group
D_{p}
. Then:

(1)

For each
[\mathfrak{a}]\in\Cl(\mathbf{Q}^{+})
, the torus orbit
\mathcal{O}_{[\mathfrak{a}]}
is a translate of a periodic torus orbit in
\mathcal{G}_{r}
and the quotient
[\mathcal{O}_{[\mathfrak{a}]}]\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{r}
is independent of the choice of representative ideal
\mathfrak{a}
and basis
B_{\mathfrak{a}}
;

(2)

and the unit shape of
K
is contained in
[\mathcal{O}_{[\mathfrak{a}]}]
for some
[\mathfrak{a}]\in\Cl(\mathbf{Q}^{+})
.

We note that the proof of
Theorem

1.3
proceeds exactly as in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
, with
5
replaced with a prime
p
. The restriction to primes stems from our dependence on Moser’s work (
[
Mos79
]
) describing the Galois module structure of units in
D_{p}
-extensions. The case
p>5
is conceptually the same as
p=5
, but is more computationally difficult.

There is also a natural variant of the regulator relation for general
p
. Let
H
be the hyperplane in
\mathbf{R}^{r+1}
whose coordinates sum to
0
. Zero index the rows of
\widetilde{G}
, and define the map

\varphi\colon\mathbf{Q}^{+}\otimes\mathbf{R}\rightarrow H

sending

to the

-th row of
\widetilde{G}
and
\zeta_{p}^{i}+\zeta_{p}^{-i}
to the

-th row of
\widetilde{G}
for

. It is easily seen that
\varphi
is an isomorphism of vector spaces. Via the
\Log
map, we also have

. Let
\mathfrak{a}=\varphi^{-1}(\Log(E_{K}))
; we will show that as a group
\mathfrak{a}\cong\mathbf{Z}^{r}
and
A^{+}\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}
, so
\mathfrak{a}
is a fractional ideal of
A^{+}
. Let
N(\mathfrak{a})
be the absolute value of the determinant of a matrix sending a basis of
A^{+}
to a basis of
\mathfrak{a}
. The following is a generalization of

.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
K
be a
D_{p}
-extension with a unique real embedding. Let
\mathfrak{a}
be obtained as above. Then the regulator of
K
is given by

R_{K}=N(\mathfrak{a})\disc(\mathbf{Q}^{+}).

The proof of

1.4
is immediate from the fact that the determinant of
G
is
\disc(\mathbf{Q}^{+})
and

is an isomorphism of vector spaces.
Theorem

1.3
specializes to

1.1
when

. In this case the cyclotomic field
\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{5})
has totally real subfield
\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{5}+\zeta_{5}^{-1})=\mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{5})
, which has trivial class number. The ring of integers of the totally real field is
A^{+}=\mathbf{Z}[\zeta_{5}+\zeta_{5}^{-1}]
, and an integral basis of

is
B=\{1,\zeta_{5}+\zeta_{5}^{-1}\}

G=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\
-1&3\end{pmatrix}.

Thus, computing the Gram matrix we obtain:

G_{\unit}=\widetilde{G}\widetilde{G}^{T}=\begin{pmatrix}6&-3\\

Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{5}

\displaystyle=P\mathcal{T}P^{-1}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\

1.4.
Overview of previous work

There is little known about the shapes of unit lattices, but here we include a brief overview of the recent work in the area.

•

Ofir David and Uri Shapira, in
[
DS18
]
, examine the unit shape of totally real cubic fields and conjecture that this set is dense in
\mathcal{S}_{2}
: they provide evidence using a certain explicit family of cubic fields.

•

Jose Miguel Cruz Rangel, in
[
Ran20
]
, investigates the unit shape of cyclic number fields of degree
\leq 7
. In particular, he studies the well-roundedness of these lattices and determines a parametrizing space of cyclic unit lattices.

•

Fernando Azpeitia-Tellez, Christopher Powell, and Shahed Sharif, in
[
ATPS21
]
, study the geometry of unit lattices of biquadratic fields,
K=\mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{D_{1}},\sqrt{D_{2}})
. They exploit the Galois module structure to determine when the unit lattice is generated by pairwise orthogonal vectors. This result can be interpreted to show that for a particular family of biquadratic fields, the unit shapes lie on restricted subspaces (more precisely, that they lie on a torus orbit).

•

Nguyen-Thi Dang, Nihar Gargava, and Jialun Li, in
[
DGL25
]
show that the unit shape of orders in totally real cubic fields are
dense
in
\mathcal{S}_{2}
.

•

Independently, Emilio Corso and Federico Rodriguez Hertz, in
[
CH25
]
, show that the unit shape of orders in totally real cubic fields are
unbounded
in
\mathcal{S}_{2}
.

•

Sergio Ricardo Zapata Ceballos, Sara Chari, Fatemeh Jalalvand, Rahinatou Yuh Njah Nchiwo, Kelly O’Connor, Fabian Ramirez and the two named authors of this paper, in
[
CCHJNORV25
]
study the geometry of unit lattices of
D_{4}
-quartic fields with signature
(2,1)
. Namely, they show that the shapes in
\mathcal{S}_{2}
arising from unit lattices of such fields are transcendental and the set of limit points is nonempty.

1.5.
Cryptographic context

We note that although the shape is the central invariant in this paper, it is also natural to consider other geometric properties of these lattices. For example one may consider well-roundedness, orthogonality, or kissing number. Understanding the geometry of these lattices is an important question in number theory and has applications to cryptography. In the cryptographic setting, lattice-based schemes – which have garnered significant attention in the post-quantum cryptography space – rely on the hardness of certain geometric problems, such as the Shortest Vector Problem (SVP) and the Closest Vector Problem (CVP), both of which are profoundly influenced by the underlying lattice structure. Notably, in cyclotomic fields, the structure of the unit lattice was exploited to defeat the security of the SOLILOQUY cryptosystem,
[
CGS14
]
. This case underscores how subtle geometric and algebraic features of lattices can directly impact the security of cryptographic protocols and suggests that a deeper understanding of the geometry of lattices is critical not only for theoretical advancements but also for practical applications in secure communications.

Existing results on unit shapes, and most on shapes of integral lattices, have focused on low-degree number fields, making them impractical for cryptographic applications. This paper, however, studies unit shapes in arbitrarily large rank. So, while our results are primarily theoretical, they highlight restrictions in the shapes that may be of relevance to cryptographic research, where a nuanced understanding of the geometry could help identify both opportunities and vulnerabilities.

Preliminaries

In this section, we discuss results from
[
Mos79
]
which describe the Galois module structure of the unit lattice. Let
p\geq 5
be a prime. Let
K
be a number field of degree
p
with a unique real embedding
\iota
whose Galois closure
L
has dihedral Galois group
D_{p}
. By Dirichlet’s unit theorem, the rank of
E_{K}
is
(p-1)/2
; let
r=(p-1)/2
. Let
L
be the Galois closure of
K
. Let
\tau
denote the unique nontrivial element of
D_{p}
such that
K=L^{\tau}
. Let
\sigma
denote a
p
-cycle in
D_{p}
so
D_{p}=\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
and
\tau\sigma\tau=\sigma^{-1}
.

Let
\zeta_{p}
be a primitive
p
–th root of unity and let
\mathbf{Q}^{+}\coloneqq\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{p}+\zeta_{p}^{-1})
. It is the maximal totally real subfield of
\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{p})
. Let
A=\mathbf{Z}[\zeta_{p}]
and
A^{+}=\mathbf{Z}[\zeta_{p}+\zeta_{p}^{-1}]
denote the ring of integers of
\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{p})
and
\mathbf{Q}^{+}
, respectively. Let
\mathfrak{P}
be the unique prime of
\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{p})
above
p
and let
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{P}\cap A^{+}
.

Following
[
Mos79
]
, we endow
A
with the structure of a
\mathbf{Z}[D_{p}]
-module; let
\sigma
acting by multiplication by
\zeta_{p}
and let
\tau
act by complex conjugation. Now, Proposition III.1 of
[
Mos79
]
states that

E_{L}\cong\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{P}^{e}\text{ for some $e\in\{0,1\}$ and some fractional ideal $\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathbf{Q}^{+}$}

as a
\mathbf{Z}[D_{p}]
-module. So, if
e=0
then
E_{L}\simeq\mathfrak{a}A
and if
e=1
then
E_{L}\simeq\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{P}
. Now, Proposition I.2 of
[
Mos79
]
says that
E_{K}=E_{L}^{\tau}
; note that this proposition is nontrivial as
E_{L}
(resp.
E_{K}
) is the quotient of the unit group by roots of unity. Therefore, under the isomorphism above, we get

E_{K}=E_{L}^{\tau}\cong(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{P}^{e})^{\tau}=\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{p}^{e}

and the latter is a fractional ideal of
\mathbf{Q}^{+}
. Now, we endow this identification with more structure. Observe that both
E_{K}
and
\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{p}^{e}
are
\mathbf{Z}[\sigma+\sigma^{-1}]
-modules and we obtain an isomorphism
E_{K}\simeq\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{p}^{e}
of
\mathbf{Z}[\sigma+\sigma^{-1}]
-modules. It is this description of
E_{K}
as a
\mathbf{Z}[\sigma+\sigma^{-1}]
-module that is crucial to our proof below.

3.
Proof of
Theorem

1.1
and
Theorem

1.2

This section is devoted to the proof of
Theorem

1.1
and
Theorem

1.2
. Let
K
be a
D_{5}
-extension with unique real embedding
\iota
. Let
\sigma
be a
5
-cycle in
D_{5}
. Let
E_{K}
denote the unit group of the ring of integers modulo torsion. Let
S_{\mathbf{Z}}=\mathbf{Z}[\sigma+\sigma^{-1}]
.

3.1.
The structure of
E_{K}

By the exposition in
Section

2
, since
\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{5})
has trivial class number, it immediately follows that
E_{K}\simeq A^{+}
as an
S_{\mathbf{Z}}
-module. Note that a basis for
A^{+}
is
\{1,\zeta_{5}+\zeta_{5}^{-1}\}
, and
(\sigma+\sigma^{-1})(1)=\zeta_{5}+\zeta_{5}^{-1}
. So, for any isomorphism
\varphi\colon A^{+}\rightarrow E_{K}
of
S_{\mathbf{Z}}
-modules,
\{\varphi(1),(\sigma+\sigma^{-1})(\varphi(1))\}
is a basis for
E_{K}
.

3.2.
The logarithmic embedding

Now, we fix an extension of the real embedding
\iota
to
L
. The set of embeddings of
L
into
\mathbf{C}
is
\iota\circ g
for
g\in D_{5}
. Since
\tau
acts trivially on
K
, the set of embeddings of
K
are given by
\iota\circ\sigma^{i}
, for
0\leq i\leq 4
. We denote each embedding by
\iota_{i}\coloneqq\iota\circ\sigma^{i}
. Note that the pairs
(\iota_{1},\iota_{4})
and
(\iota_{2},\iota_{3})
are pairs of complex conjugate embeddings because
\tau
is complex conjugation. Suppose the logarithmic embedding
\Log\colon E_{K}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}^{3}
is given by sending

u\rightarrow(\log|\iota_{0}(u)|,2\log|\iota_{1}(u)|,2\log|\iota_{2}(u)|).

The map
\Log\colon E_{K}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}^{3}
is also a map of
S_{\mathbf{Z}}
-modules;
S_{\mathbf{Z}}
acts on
\mathbf{R}^{3}
via
(\sigma+\sigma^{-1})(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2})=(a_{1},2a_{0}+a_{2},a_{1}+a_{2})
.

3.3.
Proof of
Theorem

1.1

Choose an isomorphism
\varphi\colon A^{+}\simeq E_{K}
as
S_{\mathbf{Z}}
-modules. Let
u_{0}=\varphi(1)
and
u_{1}=(\sigma+\sigma^{-1})(u_{0})
. Then
\{u_{0},u_{1}\}
is a basis of
E_{K}
.
Write

\Log(u_{0})=(a_{0},a_{1},-a_{0}-a_{1})

for real numbers
a_{0},a_{1}
. Then:

\Log(u_{1})=\Log(\sigma(u_{0}))+\Log(\sigma^{4}(u_{0}))=(a_{1},a_{0}-a_{1},-a_{0}).

The Gram matrix of the vectors
\{-\Log(u_{1}),\Log(u_{0})\}
is:

G=\begin{pmatrix}2(a_{0}^{2}-a_{0}a_{1}+a_{1}^{2})&a_{1}^{2}-3a_{0}a_{1}-a_{0}^{2}\\
a_{1}^{2}-3a_{0}a_{1}-a_{0}^{2}&2(a_{0}^{2}+a_{0}a_{1}+a_{1}^{2})\end{pmatrix}

Given a rank 2 lattice with Gram matrix
G=(G_{ij})
we find the associated point in the upper half plane by letting

\displaystyle x=\frac{G[0,1]}{G[0,0]},

\displaystyle\;\;\;y=\sqrt{\frac{G[1,1]}{G[0,0]}-x^{2}}

Now, a straightforward computation shows
(x,y)
lies on the hypercycle
\psi
and proves
Theorem

1.1
.

3.4.
Proof of
Theorem

1.2

Now, we have the following result which allows us to compute the regulator of
K
using the norm form of
A^{+}
and the
logarithmic units
of
K
. Let
N(x_{0},x_{1})\in\mathbf{Z}[x_{0},x_{1}]
be the binary quadratic form given by the norm form of
A^{+}
in the basis
\{1,\eta_{1}=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\}
. Let
u_{0}
be a unit of
K
such that
\{u_{0},u_{1}=(\sigma+\sigma^{-1})u_{0}\}
is a basis for
E_{K}
. Then we will show that
\lvert N(\log\lvert\iota(u_{0})\rvert,\log\lvert\iota(u_{1})\rvert)\rvert
is equal to the regulator of
K
.

First, an easy computation shows that
N(x_{0},x_{1})=x_{0}^{2}-x_{0}x_{1}-x_{1}^{2}
. Letting
u_{0},u_{1}
be as above, we obtain:

\begin{bmatrix}\Log(u_{0})\\
\Log(u_{1})\end{bmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}&a_{1}&-a_{0}-a_{1}\\
a_{1}&a_{0}-a_{1}&-a_{0}\end{pmatrix}

The regulator of
K
is the determinant of any
2\times 2
submatrix, which is easily seen to be
a_{0}^{2}-a_{0}a_{1}-a_{1}^{2}
.

4.
Proof of
Theorem

1.3

This section is devoted to the proof of
Theorem

1.3
. Let
K
be a degree
p
number field with unique real embedding, Galois group
D_{p}
, and Galois closure
L
. As before,
E_{K}
denotes the unit group of the ring of integers modulo torsion. Let
S_{\mathbf{R}}=\mathbf{R}[\sigma+\sigma^{-1}]
and let
S_{\mathbf{Z}}=\mathbf{Z}[\sigma+\sigma^{-1}]
. Let
r=(p-1)/2
and let
\eta_{i}=\zeta_{p}^{i}+\zeta_{p}^{-i}
for
1\leq i\leq r
.

4.1.
Sketch of the proof of
Theorem

1.3

We split the proof into steps:

(1)

First, we’ll show that
E_{K}\simeq\mathfrak{b}
as an
S_{\mathbf{Z}}
-module for some fractional ideal
\mathfrak{b}
of
\mathbf{Q}^{+}
.

(2)

Next, let
H
be the hyperplane in
\mathbf{R}^{r+1}
whose coordinates sum to zero.

(a)

We’ll describe the log map
\Log\colon E_{K}\otimes\mathbf{R}\rightarrow H
.

(b)

We’ll next set up some isomorphisms

E_{K}\otimes\mathbf{R}\cong H\cong\mathbf{Q}^{+}\otimes\mathbf{R}\cong\mathbf{R}^{r},

of
S_{\mathbf{R}}
-modules. The isomorphism
E_{K}\otimes\mathbf{R}\rightarrow H
will just be the
\Log
map, and
\mathbf{Q}^{+}\otimes\mathbf{R}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}^{r}
will be the Minkowski embedding. We’ll let the isomorphism
H\cong\mathbf{Q}^{+}\otimes\mathbf{R}
be arbitary for now.

(3)

Next, because
E_{K}\simeq\mathfrak{b}
as an
S_{\mathbf{Z}}
-module, the image of
E_{K}
inside
\mathbf{R}^{r}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{b}
as an
S_{\mathbf{Z}}
-module. We’ll then classify the
S_{\mathbf{Z}}
-modules isomorphic to
\mathfrak{b}
inside of
\mathbf{R}^{r}
.

(4)

We’ll use this description of
S_{\mathbf{Z}}
-modules to describe the possible shapes of
E_{K}
in terms of
\varphi
.

(5)

Finally, we’ll make an explicit choice of
\varphi
.

(6)

We’ll substitute this choice of
\varphi
into Step
(4)
to explicitly describe the possible shapes of
E_{K}
.

4.2.
Step
(1)
: the structure of
E_{K}

By the exposition in
Section

2
, we have that
E_{L}\simeq\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{P}^{e}
as a
\mathbf{Z}[D_{p}]
-module for some
e\in\{0,1\}
. Under this isomorphism:

E_{K}=E_{L}^{\tau}\simeq(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{P}^{e})^{\tau}=\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{p}^{e}.

Let
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{p}^{e}
, and as before, this is an isomorphism of
S_{\mathbf{Z}}
-modules. Tensoring with
\mathbf{R}
, we see that

E_{K}\otimes\mathbf{R}\simeq\mathbf{Q}^{+}\otimes\mathbf{R}

as an
S_{\mathbf{R}}
-module.

4.3.
Step
(2)
, part
(a)
: the logarithmic embedding

We fix an extension of the real embedding
\iota
to
L
, which we still denote
\iota
. Then, the set of embeddings of
L
into
\mathbf{C}
are
\iota\circ g
for
g\in D_{p}
. Since
\tau
preserves
K
, the set of embeddings of
K
into
\mathbf{C}
is
\iota\circ\sigma^{i}
for
0\leq i\leq p-1
. Let
\iota_{i}\coloneqq\iota\circ\sigma^{i}
. Because
\tau
is complex conjugation of the roots in the complex plane, we have that
\iota_{i}
and
\iota_{p-i}
are a pair of complex conjugate embeddings
K\rightarrow\mathbf{C}
for
1\leq i\leq p-1
.

Now suppose the logarithmic embedding
\Log\colon E_{K}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}^{r+1}
is given by sending

u\rightarrow(\log|\iota_{0}(u)|,2\log|\iota_{1}(u)|,\ldots,2\log|\iota_{r}(u)|).

Let
H
be the hyperplane inside
\mathbf{R}^{r+1}
whose coordinates sum to zero. Because
(\sigma+\sigma^{-1})(K)=K
and
E_{K}\otimes\mathbf{R}\simeq H
, the hyperplane
H
naturally has the structure of a
S_{\mathbf{R}}
-module. Moreover, the logarithmic embedding shows that
E_{K}\otimes\mathbf{R}\simeq H
as an
S_{\mathbf{R}}
-module.

4.4.
Step
(2)
, part
(b)
: the isomorphisms

Pick an isomorphism
\varphi\colon\mathbf{Q}^{+}\otimes\mathbf{R}\rightarrow H
of
S_{\mathbf{R}}
-modules. We have the series of
S_{\mathbf{R}}
-module isomorphisms:

E_{K}\otimes\mathbf{R}\rightarrow H\cong_{\varphi}\mathbf{Q}^{+}\otimes\mathbf{R}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}^{r}

where
\mathbf{Q}^{+}\otimes\mathbf{R}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}^{r}
is the Minkowski embedding sending
1\mapsto(1,\ldots,1)
and
\eta_{1}\mapsto(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\dots,\eta_{r})
.

4.5.
Step
(3)
:
S_{\mathbf{Z}}
-modules isomorphic to
\mathfrak{b}
inside
\mathbf{R}^{r}

Recall that
E_{K}\simeq\mathfrak{b}
as an
S_{\mathbf{Z}}
-module. Choose a
\mathbf{Z}
-basis
B_{\mathfrak{b}}=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{r}\}
of
\mathfrak{b}
. Then the subsets of
\mathbf{Q}^{+}\otimes\mathbf{R}
that are isomorphic to
\mathfrak{b}
as an
S_{\mathbf{Z}}
-module are precisely fractional ideals in the same ideal class, i.e. they have a
\mathbf{Z}
-basis of the form
\{xb_{1},\dots,xb_{r}\}
for
x\in(\mathbf{Q}^{+}\otimes\mathbf{R})^{\times}
. In
\mathbf{R}^{r}
, these subsets have a
\mathbf{Z}
-basis of the form
\{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r})\cdot b_{1},\dots,(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r})\cdot b_{r}\}
for
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\in\mathbf{R}^{\times}
, where here the multiplication is coordinate-wise.

4.6.
Step
(4)
: describing possible shapes of
E_{K}
in terms of
\varphi

Recall that
\mathcal{G}_{r}
is the space of real symmetric positive definite matrices up to scaling, and
\mathcal{S}_{r}=\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbf{Z})\backslash\mathcal{G}_{r}
.

Let
G_{\mathfrak{b}}
be the Gram matrix of the lattice spanned by
\{\varphi(b_{1}),\dots,\varphi(b_{r})\}
. We are now ready to write down the group action.
Let
j_{1},\dots,j_{r}
be the
r
embeddings
\mathbf{Q}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}
giving rise to the Minkowski embedding above, ordered so that
j_{i}(\eta_{1})=\eta_{i}
. Define
P_{\mathfrak{b}}
to be the matrix whose rows are given by the Minkowksi embedding above:

P_{\mathfrak{b}}=\begin{pmatrix}j_{1}(b_{1})&\dots&j_{r}(b_{1})\\
\dots&\dots&\dots\\
j_{1}(b_{r})&\dots&j_{r}(b_{r})\end{pmatrix}.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be the diagonal torus of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbf{R})
. By the discussion above, there exists a basis of
E_{K}
such that the Gram matrix with respect to this basis is contained in the orbit of
P_{\mathfrak{b}}\mathcal{T}P_{\mathfrak{b}}^{-1}
acting on
G_{\mathfrak{b}}
; we consider this orbit to be inside
\mathcal{G}_{r}
. Note that after the quotient to
\mathcal{S}_{r}
, this orbit depends only on the class
[\mathfrak{b}]\in\Cl(\mathbf{Q}^{+})
. Because the orbit in the quotient arises from an ideal class in a real quadratic field, it is a translate of a periodic torus orbit.

4.7.
Step
(5)
: choosing a nice
\varphi

We now choose a nice choice of
\varphi
. Recall that
A^{+}
has
\mathbf{Z}
-basis
B=\{1,\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{r-1}\}
. Let
G
be the Gram matrix of the trace form of
A^{+}/\mathbf{Z}
with respect to
B
. Let
\widetilde{G}
be the
r\times(r+1)
matrix obtained by adjoining a
zeroing column
to
G
: the column with
j
–th entry given by
-\sum_{i=1}^{r}G(j,i)
. So:

\widetilde{G}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\Tr(1)&\Tr(\eta_{1})&\dots&\Tr(\eta_{r-1})&-\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\Tr(\eta_{i})\\
\Tr(\eta_{1})&\Tr(\eta_{1}^{2})&\dots&\Tr(\eta_{1}\eta_{r-1})&-\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\Tr(\eta_{1}\eta_{i})\\
\dots&\dots&\dots\\
\Tr(\eta_{r-2})&\Tr(\eta_{1}\eta_{r-2})&\dots&\Tr(\eta_{r-2}\eta_{r-1})&-\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\Tr(\eta_{r-2}\eta_{i})\\
\Tr(\eta_{r-1})&\Tr(\eta_{1}\eta_{r-1})&\dots&\Tr(\eta_{r-1}^{2})&-\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\Tr(\eta_{r-1}\eta_{i})\end{pmatrix},

where here
\Tr
is the trace map from
\mathbf{Q}^{+}
to
\mathbf{Q}
. For the convenience of the reader, we include a computation of the trace matrix
G
below:

G=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{p-1}{2}&-1&-1&\cdots&\cdots&-1\\
-1&p-2&-2&\cdots&\cdots&-2\\
\vdots&-2&p-2&-2&\cdots&-2\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
-1&-2&&&&p-2\end{pmatrix},

We omit the proof as it is not directly needed for our results.

Now, define the map

\varphi\colon\mathbf{Q}^{+}\otimes\mathbf{R}\rightarrow H

sending the basis
B=\{1,\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{r-1}\}
to the rows of
\widetilde{G}
. For notational simplicity, we
0
-index the rows and columns of
\widetilde{G}
in
Proposition

4.1
.

Proposition 4.1
.

The map
\varphi
is an isomorphism of
S_{\mathbf{R}}
-modules.

Proof.

It suffices to check that

(\sigma^{i}+\sigma^{-i})(\varphi(1))=\varphi(\eta_{i})

for every
1\leq i<r
. This is equivalent to saying that
(\sigma^{i}+\sigma^{-i})
applied to the
0
–th row of
\widetilde{G}
is the
i
–th row. It suffices to check the entries
\widetilde{G_{ij}}
for
1\leq i<r
and
0\leq j<r
, because the rows of
\widetilde{G}
sum to zero. Again, we do the proof in cases. For
v\in\mathbf{R}^{r+1}
and
0\leq j\leq r
, let
v[j]
denote the entry in the
j
–th component. Let
\widetilde{G_{0}},\dots,\widetilde{G_{r}}
be the rows of
\widetilde{G}
.

In this notation, we are interested in showing that

\bigg((\sigma^{i}+\sigma^{-i})\widetilde{G_{0}}\bigg)[j]=\widetilde{G_{i}}[j]

for all
1\leq i<r
and
0\leq j<r
. By convention set
\eta_{0}\coloneqq 1
and
\eta_{k}=\zeta_{p}^{k}+\zeta_{p}^{-k}
for any integer
k
such that
p\neq k
. First notice that
\widetilde{G}_{i}[r+1]=\Tr(\eta_{i}\eta_{r+1})
. Now we split the proof into three cases.

Case 1:
j=0
and
1\leq i<r
.
We have:

\displaystyle\widetilde{G_{i}}[j]

\displaystyle=\Tr(\eta_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Tr(\eta_{i})+\frac{1}{2}\Tr(\eta_{-i})

\displaystyle=\bigg((\sigma^{i}+\sigma^{-i})\widetilde{G_{0}}\bigg)[j]

Case 2:
0<j<r
and
1\leq i<r
and
i\neq j
.
We have:

\displaystyle\widetilde{G_{i}}[j]

\displaystyle=\Tr(\eta_{i}\eta_{j})

\displaystyle=\Tr(\zeta^{i+j}+\zeta^{i-j}+\zeta^{-(i-j)}+\zeta^{-(i+j)})

\displaystyle=\Tr(\eta_{i+j})+\Tr(\eta_{i-j})

\displaystyle=\bigg((\sigma^{i}+\sigma^{-i})\widetilde{G_{0}}\bigg)[j]

Case 3:
0<i=j<r
.
We have:

\displaystyle\widetilde{G_{i}}[j]

\displaystyle=\Tr(\eta_{i}^{2})

\displaystyle=\Tr(\eta_{2i}+2)

\displaystyle=\Tr(\eta_{2i})+2\Tr(1)

\displaystyle=\bigg((\sigma^{i}+\sigma^{-i})\widetilde{G_{0}}\bigg)[j]

∎

4.8.
Step
(6)
: describing possible shapes of
E_{K}
using the given
\varphi

Let
\varphi
be given by
\widetilde{G}
. Let
M_{\mathfrak{a}}
be the matrix sending
B=\{1,\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{r-1}\}
to
B_{\mathfrak{a}}=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{r}\}
, so
M_{\mathfrak{a}}B^{T}=B_{\mathfrak{a}}^{T}
. Recall that

P=\begin{bmatrix}1&\dots&1\\
j_{1}(\eta_{1})&\dots&j_{r}(\eta_{1})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
j_{1}(\eta_{r-1})&\dots&j_{r}(\eta_{r-1})\end{bmatrix}.

where here
j_{1},\dots,j_{r}
are the
r
embeddings of
\mathbf{Q}^{+}
into the real numbers corresponding to the Minkowski embedding above. Thus:

M_{\mathfrak{b}}P=P_{\mathfrak{b}}

So:

P_{\mathfrak{b}}\mathcal{T}P_{\mathfrak{b}}^{-1}=M_{\mathfrak{b}}P\mathcal{T}P^{-1}M_{\mathfrak{b}}^{-1}.

So the orbit of
P_{\mathfrak{b}}\mathcal{T}P_{\mathfrak{b}}^{-1}
on
G_{\mathfrak{b}}
is equal to the orbit of
M_{\mathfrak{b}}P\mathcal{T}P^{-1}
on
M_{\mathfrak{b}}^{-1}G_{\mathfrak{b}}M_{\mathfrak{b}}
. Finally, observe that
M_{\mathfrak{b}}P\mathcal{T}P^{-1}=M_{\mathfrak{b}}\mathcal{T}_{p}
and
M_{\mathfrak{b}}^{-1}G_{\mathfrak{b}}M_{\mathfrak{b}}=G_{\unit}
. So, there exists a basis of
E_{K}
such that the Gram matrix with respect to this basis is contained in the orbit of
M_{\mathfrak{b}}\mathcal{T}_{p}
on
G_{\unit}
, completing the proof of
Theorem

1.3
.

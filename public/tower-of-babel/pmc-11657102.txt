Prognostic value of the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio and prognostic nutritional index in unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma patients treated with tyrosine kinase inhibitors and immune checkpoint inhibitors
Authors: Shasha Sun, Wendong Li, Xiaodi Guo, Jinglong Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThis study aimed to investigate the role of the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and prognostic nutritional index (PNI) in unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) patients treated with tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs). The optical cutoff values of the NLR and PNI were determined via time-dependent receiver operating characteristic curve analysis. The associations between overall survival (OS) and various potential risk factors were analyzed. Forty-nine HCC patients were enrolled in this retrospective study. The optimal pretreatment NLR and PNI cutoff values were 2.4 and 41, respectively. The median follow-up was 8 (range 3–36) months. The median OS in the high NLR subgroup was lower than that in the low NLR subgroup (7 vs. 9 months, p < .05). However, the high PNI group had better OS than the low PNI group did (12 vs. 7 months, p < .05). Univariate analysis revealed that tumor distribution (p = .003), PNI < 41 (p = .013), and NLR ≥ 2.4 (p = .010) were associated with unfavorable OS in HCC patients. The multivariate analysis revealed that the PNI (HR = 0.353, 95% CI 0.150–0.831; p = .017) and tumor distribution (HR = 0.336, 95% CI 0.137–0.826; p = .017) were independent indicators of poor prognosis. A pretreatment NLR ≥ 2.4 and PNI < 41 are related to poor survival in unresectable HCC patients receiving TKI and ICI treatment. Moreover, a lower PNI is an independent indicator of poor prognosis when ICIs are combined with TKIs.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the sixth most common malignancy and the third leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide, representing a major global health-care challenge.1 Owing to their occult onset and rapid progress, only some HCC patients can undergo radical resection, and most patients need systemic therapy.2,3 Systemic therapy, which includes tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), is a standard treatment recommended by the guidelines for advanced-stage HCC.4 An increasing number of studies have focused on systemic therapy. A storm trial5 reported that adjuvant systemic treatment did not improve survival in early-stage HCC patients. Therefore, more systemic therapies are being used for advanced-stage HCC. Mollica V et al.6 reported the impact of ECOG performance status on the efficacy of immunotherapy and immune-based combinations in cancer patients. Interestingly, a study addressing immune-related adverse events was associated with improved outcomes with high heterogeneity.7 In parallel, Rizzo et al.8 recently reported a growing interest in the combination of transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) plus systemic treatments to increase antitumor efficacy and produce synergistic effects. Nevertheless, TKIs are prone to drug resistance, and the effects of ICIs vary greatly among individuals. Simple and effective predictors are needed to properly select the right patients to receive combination therapy in clinical practice.

Inflammation is closely correlated with tumor germination, progression and metastasis.9,10 An increasing number of studies have focused on inflammatory-based prognostic indices for HCC, such as the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), the prognostic nutritional index (PNI), and the C-reactive protein. A retrospective study demonstrated that the NLR and PNI are independent prognostic factors for overall survival (OS) in patients with HCC after curative hepatectomy or transarterial chemoembolization.11,12 Liu et al. reported that the NLR-PNI score could improve the accuracy of prognoses for patients with unresectable HCC (uHCC).13

However, the ability of the NLR and PNI to predict the outcomes of HCC patients receiving TKIs plus ICIs remains unclear. This study aimed to explore the prognostic role of the NLR and PNI in uHCC patients treated with TKIs and ICIs.

Materials and methods

Patients with uHCC who received TKIs and ICI therapy at our center between January 2019 and January 2022 were enrolled in this study. Written informed consent was obtained from every patient prior to treatment. The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

The eligibility criteria were as follows: (1) cytological/histological diagnosis by biopsy or two imaging techniques showing typical features of HCC, which was confirmed to be unresectable by a liver cancer multidisciplinary team; (2) age 18 to 75 years; (3) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status 0–1; and (4) Child – Pugh A or B.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) severe underlying cardiac or renal diseases, esophageal or gastric variceal bleeding or hepatic encephalopathy, or active infection; (2) a history of other cancers; and (3) pregnancy or breast feeding.

The enrolled patients received regular treatment with TKIs (lenvatinib, 12 mg/day for body weights ≥60 kg or 8 mg for body weights <60 kg, or sorafenib, 400 mg/day) and ICIs (sintilimab, 200 mg intravenously once every 3 weeks). The NLR is defined as the ratio of the neutrophil count to the lymphocyte count in peripheral blood; the PNI was calculated via the following formula: serum albumin (g/L) + 5 × lymphocyte count (109/L). Patients were stratified into high and low NLR groups or high and low PNI groups on the basis of the NLR or PNI within 7 days prior to treatment.

All patients were regularly followed up every 8–12 weeks according to institutional practice, including a complete physical examination; complete blood count; biochemistry profiles (liver, renal, thyroid, cardiac, coagulation function tests); and serum alpha fetoprotein (AFP), urine, and radiological examinations (abdominal contrast-enhanced CT or MRI, chest CT). OS was assessed from the date of first treatment until death from any cause. The last follow-up date for this study was January 31, 2022.

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed via SPSS for Windows (Version 16.0). The best cutoff values for the pretreatment NLR and PNI were determined via time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. Independent x2 tests were used to compare categorical variables. Continuous variables were compared via unpaired t tests. The Kaplan‒Meier method was used to analyze survival, and the differences between groups were compared via the log-rank test. Unadjusted and adjusted hazard ratios (HRs) by baseline characteristics were calculated via the Cox proportional hazards model. All the statistical tests were two-sided, and differences were considered significant at p < .05.

Results

Survival analysis

The median follow-up was 8 (range 3–36) months. The 6-, 12- and 24-month OS rates for patients in the low NLR subgroup were 94.4%, 44.4%, and 27.8%, respectively. However, those for the high NLR group were 71.0%, 12.9%, and 6.4%, respectively. The median OS of patients in the high NLR subgroup was 7 months, whereas it was 9 months in the low NLR subgroup (Figure 2, p = .010). Similarly, HCC patients in the high PNI group had a median OS of 12 months, whereas those in the low PNI group had a median OS of 7 months (Figure 2, p = .013). Discussion

Recently, systemic therapy has shown promising efficacy in treating uHCC, yet the response rate remains unsatisfactory.14–16 Reliable and practical predictors are imperatively needed to stratify patients based on their response to systemic therapy. As a result of chronic liver damage or chronic inflammation, HCC is an inflammation-related cancer.17 Long-term inflammatory stimulation can lead to liver fibrosis, further causing cancer. Virchow discovered a close relationship between inflammation and cancer.18 Many studies focus have focused on the relationship between inflammatory factors and tumor prognosis. Routine indicators of systemic inflammation, such as the NLR, platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), and PNI, have been previously validated for their survival outcome in patients with HCC. Given the lack of practical biomarkers that can be used to predict the response to systemic therapy, we aimed to evaluate the prognostic utility of the NLR and PNI, which are inexpensive and easily accessible, in patients with unresectable HCC treated with TKIs and ICIs.

As an inflammatory factor, the NLR is defined as the ratio of the neutrophil count to the lymphocyte count in the peripheral blood. Increasing evidence suggests that a high NLR is correlated with poor prognosis in different malignancies, including HCC.19 The impact of the NLR on survival has been evaluated in large cohorts of HCC patients treated with surgical resection, transplantation, transarterial chemoembolization, radiofrequency ablation, molecular targeted drugs and ICIs.20–23 It is unclear whether the NLR can predict the prognosis of patients with uHCC receiving combination therapy with TKIs and ICIs. A multicentre international retrospective cohort study of patients with unresectable HCC treated with atezolizumab plus bevacizumab24 revealed that patients with an NLR ≥ 5 had a significantly shorter OS (9.38 vs. 16.79 months, p < .001) compared than patients with an NLR < 5. Our study revealed that a high NLR was associated with unfavorable OS in uHCC patients treated with lenvatinib, sorafenib or sintilimab. However, multivariate analysis revealed that the NLR was not an independent predictor of the outcome of TKIs combined with ICIs. This outcome was different from previous research results.25 Tado et al.26 reported that an NLR ≥ 4 (HR, 1.874; 95% CI, 1.097–3.119) was independently associated with OS in patients with HCC treated with lenvatinib alone. Another retrospective study27 reported that a lower pretreatment NLR (≤2.22) might indicate a better prognosis for patients with uHCC treated with TACE + TKIs + ICIs. The reason for the different outcomes may lie in the different therapies used.

There may be a potential mechanism to explain why the NLR can predict the prognosis of patients with HCC. The inflammatory factors secreted by tumor cells can stimulate tumorigenesis, angiogenesis, vascular invasion and metastasis.9 As an inflammatory factor, the NLR is the ratio of the neutrophil count to the lymphocyte count in the peripheral blood. CD8+ and CD4+ lymphocytes can induce antitumor immunity, and consequently, HCC characterized by inflammatory cell infiltration has a better prognosis.28,29 A high NLR may reflect an increased neutrophil response or decreased lymphocyte response; therefore, a high NLR may indicate poor immune status, resulting in a worse prognosis.

The PNI was originally used to assess the prognosis of gastrointestinal surgery patients via the use of albumin and lymphocyte counts.30 In recent years, the PNI has been recognized as a predictor for various cancers, including HCC.10 An increasing number of studies have demonstrated that the preoperative PNI can predict the OS of HCC patients undergoing surgery, targeted therapy or anti-PD1 therapy.11,31–33 Kang et al.34 reported that a higher pretreatment PNI (≥44.91) was a significant predictive factor for favorable outcomes in patients with recurrent or unresectable HCC receiving anti-PD1 therapy. In the present study, pretreatment PNI (HR = 0.353, 95% CI 0.150–0.831, p = .017) and tumor distribution (HR = 0.336, 95% CI 0.137–0.826, p = .017) were found to be independent indicators of poor prognosis in uHCC patients receiving TKIs combined with ICIs. Our findings are consistent with those of previous studies reporting similar results in patients receiving ICI monotherapy.35 Notably, patients received nonsingle ICIs during the entire treatment period in the previous study. Our study not only unified ICIs but also explored the predictive power of the PNI on the basis of ICI plus TKI treatment. Furthermore, a meta-analysis confirmed that a low PNI is a poor prognostic factor for OS and disease-free survival in HCC patients undergoing various treatments.36 These pooled results suggest that a high PNI is beneficial for the survival of HCC patients. A lower PNI may warrant early evaluation for disease progression and consideration for early changes in therapy.

It is unclear why a low PNI is associated with poor prognosis in HCC patients. The underlying mechanisms are as follows. Increasing evidence reveals that the inflammatory response can promote tumor growth, invasion, angiogenesis and metastasis.9 The PNI is an inflammatory index that includes the serum albumin concentration and lymphocyte count. Albumin is the main component of plasma proteins, and hypoalbuminaemia reflects that cancer cachexia is caused by a sustained inflammatory response.37 Lymphocytes can prevent tumor progression; therefore, decreased lymphocytes may indicate a poor immune status.29 Thus, a low PNI value indicates a relatively poor nutritional and immune status. The PNI can be a predictor of prognosis in HCC patients. The findings from this study contribute to an emerging body of evidence elucidating the clinical value of systemic markers of inflammation in predicting outcomes with combination treatment of TKIs and ICIs for unresectable HCC.

Several limitations of the current study should be carefully considered. First, it has a relatively small sample size and a single institutional, retrospective design, which inevitably leads to selection bias. Second, data regarding patients’ preexisting comorbidities are lacking, which may influence systemic inflammation. There is also remains some controversy regarding the optimal cutoff values for the NLR and PNI, but our study used a more stringent cutoff to improve the accuracy of the data. Finally, the value of the NLR and PLR as predictive rather than prognostic biomarkers could not be established from this study without a control cohort of patients who did not receive treatment with TKIs and ICIs.Thus, more studies that are well designed, large scale and prospective are needed to confirm our results.

In conclusion, a pretreatment NLR ≥ 2.4 and PNI < 41 are related to poor survival in unresectable HCC patients receiving combination treatment with TKIs and ICIs. Moreover, a lower PNI is an independent indicator of poor prognosis in HCC patients.

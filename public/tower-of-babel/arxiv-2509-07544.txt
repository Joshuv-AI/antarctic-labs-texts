Totally real divisors on curves
Lorenzo Baldi * and Mario Kummer † \dagger and Daniel Plaumann ‡ \ddagger

Abstract
. Since the works of Krasnov and Scheiderer, there has been an interest in studying effective totally real divisors on a curve X X defined over a real closed field, i.e., effective divisors supported on the real locus. Scheiderer proved that, for smooth curves over R \mathbb{R} with nonempty real locus, each divisor of sufficiently high degree is linearly equivalent to an effective totally real one. The smallest degree N ⁡ ( X ) \mathrm{N}(X) with this property is called the totally real divisor threshold . When the field is non-Archimedean, we obtain a classification of topological types of smooth curves for which N ⁡ ( X ) \mathrm{N}(X) can be ∞ {\infty} . As a consequence, for curves over R \mathbb{R} we prove that N ⁡ ( X ) \mathrm{N}(X) cannot be bounded from above only in terms of the topological type, unless X ⁡ ( R ) X(\mathbb{R}) has many connected components. We complement this qualitative result with a quantitative lower bound for N ⁡ ( X ) \mathrm{N}(X) , depending on metric properties of the Jacobian and the curve in the Bergman metric. Finally, we relate these metric properties to period matrices of X X , expressed in a way compatible with the real structure.

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a projective algebraic curve defined over
\mathbb{R}
. If the real locus
X(\mathbb{R})
is nonempty, we ask if a given (conjugation invariant) divisor on
X
is linearly equivalent to a divisor supported on
X(\mathbb{R})
. It follows from the work of Krasnov
[
29
]
*Sec. 2 that the answer is always positive, provided that the curve is smooth and the degree of the divisor is large enough.
Scheiderer refined this result, showing that such a divisor can be chosen to be effective.

Theorem

(
[
43
]
*p. 1050)
.

Let
X
be a smooth irreducible curve over
\mathbb{R}
such that
X(\mathbb{R})\neq\varnothing
. Then every divisor of sufficiently high degree is linearly equivalent to an effective divisor supported on
X(\mathbb{R})
.

This result motivates the following definition, which can be stated for arbitrary real closed fields.

Definition 1.0.1
.

Let
R
be a real closed field and let
X
be a curve over
R
. We say that a divisor
D
on
X
is
totally real
if
\supp(D)\subset X(R)
. We define the
totally real divisor threshold
of
X
as the infimum
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that every divisor of degree
m
is linearly equivalent to an effective totally real one, and we denote it by
\mathrm{N}(X)
.

Notice that, by definition,
\mathrm{N}(X)={\infty}
if
X(R)=\varnothing
. Scheiderer’s result states that
\mathrm{N}(X)<{\infty}
for smooth curves over
\mathbb{R}
with
X(\mathbb{R})\neq\varnothing
.
On the other hand, for singular curves
\mathrm{N}(X)
need not be finite (see
[
40
]
). It is a natural question what data the invariant
\mathrm{N}(X)
depends on in general.

In the article of Scheiderer, it was also incorrectly claimed that the finiteness of
\mathrm{N}(X)
in the smooth case extends to arbitrary real closed fields
R

[
43
]
*Th. 2.7. The first counterexample to this statement, i.e., a smooth curve over a real closed field
R
with
X(R)\neq\varnothing
and
\mathrm{N}(X)={\infty}
, was recently found by Benoist and Wittenberg
[
4
]
*Rem. 9.26.
Our first contribution is to completely characterize when such counterexamples exist.

Theorem A

(see
Theorem

3.2.6
for a refined statement)
.

Let
R
be a real closed field.

(i)

If
X
is a smooth curve over
R
of genus
g
with
\mathrm{N}(X)=\infty
such that
X(R)
has
r>0
(semialgebraically) connected components, then
r<g
and
R
is non-Archimedean.

(ii)

Conversely, if
R
is non-Archimedean and
0\leq r<g
, then there exists a smooth curve
X
over
R
of genus
g
with
\mathrm{N}(X)=\infty
such that
X(R)
has
r
(semialgebraically) connected components.

Our next result is better stated using real moduli spaces. The coarse moduli space
\mathscr{M}_{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
of real isomorphism classes of smooth real algebraic curves was introduced by Gross and Harris
[
19
]
and Seppälä and Silhol
[
45
]
. It is a semianalytic variety
[
24
]
of dimension
3g-3
, which is homeomorphic with the underlying topological space of the corresponding real algebraic stack
[
16
]
*Th. 8.2.

The
topological type
of a curve
X
(see
Section

1.2
for more details) is the triple
(g,r,a)
, where
g
is the genus of
X
,
r
is the number of connected components of
X(\mathbb{R})
and
a=1
if
X\setminus X(\mathbb{R})
is connected and
a=0
if
X\setminus X(\mathbb{R})
has two connected components.
If two curves are isomorphic over
\mathbb{R}
, then they have the same topological type. Moreover, two isomorphism classes of smooth curves lie in the same connected component of
\mathscr{M}_{g}{R}
if and only if they have the same topological type.
We can write the moduli space as the disjoint union
\mathscr{M}_{g}{R}=\bigsqcupop\displaylimits_{(g,r,a)}\mathscr{M}_{(g,r,a)}{R}
, where
\mathscr{M}_{(g,r,a)}{R}
parametrizes real isomorphism classes of smooth curves with the topological type
(g,r,a)
. We say that a topological type
(g,r,a)
is
admissible
if
\mathscr{M}_{(g,r,a)}{R}
is nonempty.

Theorem B

(see
Section

3
)
.

Consider the totally real divisor threshold
\mathrm{N}
as a function on
\mathscr{M}_{g}{R}
.

(i)

For
m\geq 2g+1
, the set

\mathrm{N}^{-1}(\{\,0,\dots,m\,\})=\left\{\,X\in\mathscr{M}_{g}^{\mathbb{R}}\mid\mathrm{N}(X)\leq m\,\right\}

is closed.

(ii)

For an admissible topological type
(g,r,a)
with
r>0
real connected components, let

\mathrm{N}_{(g,r,a)}\colon\mathscr{M}_{(g,r,a)}{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}

be the restriction of
\mathrm{N}
to
\mathscr{M}_{(g,r,a)}{R}
. Then
\mathrm{N}_{(g,r,a)}
is unbounded unless
r=g
or
r=g+1
.

The two bounded cases
r=g
and
r=g+1
were already found by Huisman
[
25
]
and Monnier
[
39
]
, who also introduced the notation
\mathrm{N}(X)
. In both cases, they were able to show the upper bound
\mathrm{N}(X)\leq 2g-1
. We reprove this upper bound in a unified way in
Theorem

4.1.2
.

The interest in bounding
\mathrm{N}(X)
also arises from applications. For instance, given an embedded curve
X\subset\mathbb P^{n}
,
\mathrm{N}(X)
is used to determine the maximal number of real intersection points of
X
with a hypersurface, or to study the cone of nonnegative forms on
X
and the dual moment problem. We refer the reader to
[
33
,
13
,
1
]
for more details. The totally real divisor threshold is also employed in the study of hyperbolic embeddings
[
31
]
of curves. The question of which theta characteristics have a totally real representative was studied in the context of convex hulls of canonical curves
[
32
]
.

We remark that
B
(ii) disproves a conjecture of Huisman
[
26
]
*Conj. 3.4 on so-called unramified curves in
\mathbb P^{n}
for odd
n
. Indeed,
[
39
]
*Th. 3.7 shows that
\mathrm{N}_{(g,g-1,a)}
is bounded if the conjecture holds true for
n=g+1
in case
g
is even and
n=g+2
when
g
is odd.
Huisman’s conjecture was already disproven in the case
n=3
by Mikhalkin and Orevkov, see
[
36
]
and
[
37
]
*Rem. 2.7, and by Kummer and Manevich
[
30
]
. Our results imply the existence of counterexamples in
\mathbb P^{n}
for any odd
n\geq 3
.

B
implies that, unless the curve
X
has many real connected components,
\mathrm{N}(X)
cannot be bounded just in terms of the invariants
g
,
r
and
a
of
X
. However, in
C
below, we are able to prove that
\mathrm{N}(X)
can be bounded from below by metric properties of the embedding of the curve in its Jacobian through the Abel–Jacobi map.

Before stating the result, we need some preliminaries: we refer the reader to
Section

2
for more details. We denote by
J=\jac(X)\cong\mathbb{C}^{g}/\Lambda
the Jacobian of a curve
X
of genus
g
. If
X
is defined over
\mathbb{R}
and has
r>0
real connected components, then
J
is defined over
\mathbb{R}
, and
J(\mathbb{R})
is a
g
-dimensional, compact, real Lie group with
2^{r-1}
connected components. We let
J(\mathbb{R})_{0}
denote the connected component of the identity.
The principal polarization of
J
induces an Hermitian (Kähler) metric on
J
. Its real part is a Riemannian metric on the underlying
2g
-dimensional real manifold, which we call the canonical Riemannian metric. We denote by
\vol(J(\mathbb{R})_{0})
the volume of the submanifold
J(\mathbb{R})_{0}\subset J
in this metric. The Abel–Jacobi map
\phi\colon X\to J
is an embedding, hence we can define the
Bergman (Riemannian) metric
on
X
(see e.g.
[
48
,
21
]
) as the pullback of the canonical Riemannian metric on
J
via
\phi
. We denote by
\len(X(\mathbb{R}))
the length of the real locus of
X
with respect to this metric.

We are now ready to state the lower bound for
\mathrm{N}(X)
. A simplified version for
r=1
is given in
Proposition

4.2.1
.

Theorem C

(see
Section

4.2
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth curve over
\mathbb{R}
of genus
g
such that
X(\mathbb{R})
has
r>0
connected components. Denoting Euler’s number by
\mathrm{e}
, we then have:

\mathrm{N}(X)\geq 2\left(1-r+\left(\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^{g}\left(\frac{r-1}{\mathrm{e}}\right)^{g(r-1)}\frac{\vol(J(\mathbb{R})_{0})}{\len(X(\mathbb{R}))^{g}}\right)^{\frac{1}{gr}}\right)-1

When the topological type of
X
is fixed, the only ingredient of the bound in
C
that may vary is the ratio
\frac{\vol(\jac(\mathbb{R})_{0})}{\len(X(\mathbb{R}))^{g}}
. This tells us that the smaller the image of
X(\mathbb{R})
via the Abel–Jacobi map is, compared to the real part of the Jacobian, the larger the totally real divisor threshold will be. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first occurrence of a metric property which is shown to play a key role in the study of divisors on real curves.

It is natural to ask whether
C
gives an effective way to compute a lower bound for
\mathrm{N}(X)
and whether or not the bound is tight. As a first step, in
D
below we show how to read
\vol(\jac(\mathbb{R})_{0})
, or equivalently
\vol(\jac(\mathbb{R}))
, from a period matrix for the Jacobian
J=\jac(X)\cong H^{0}(X,\Omega)^{*}/H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{C}^{g}/\Lambda
.
The lattice
\Lambda
defining
J
is generated by the columns of the
g\times 2g
period matrix for
X
. If the bases of
H^{0}(X,\Omega)^{*}
and
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
are chosen in a compatible way with respect to the real structure (see
Section

2
for details), then the period matrix takes the special form

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc|ccc}1&&&&&\\
&{\mathinner{\mkern 1.0mu\raise 7.0pt\vbox{\kern 7.0pt\hbox{$.$}}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 1.0mu}}&&\lx@intercol\hfil\frac{1}{2}M+iT\hfil\lx@intercol\\
&&{1}&&&\end{array}\right)

with
T\in\mathbb{R}^{g}
a symmetric, positive definite matrix, and
M\in\mathbb{Z}^{g\times g}
, called
reflection matrix
. We can now state our result.

Theorem D

(see
Section

2.3
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth curve over
\mathbb{R}
with
X(\mathbb{R})\neq\varnothing
,
J=\jac(X)
and
T
as above. If
r>0
is the number of connected components of
X(\mathbb{R})
, then

\vol(J(\mathbb{R}))=2^{r-1}(\det T)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

and
\det T=\vol(J(\mathbb{R})_{0})^{-2}
is an invariant of
X
.

This result is stated for Jacobians for ease of presentation. It holds true more generally for principally polarized abelian varieties over
\mathbb{R}
, see
Theorem

2.2.2
and
Corollary

2.2.3
.

Compared to
\vol(J(\mathbb{R})_{0})
, the computation of
\len(X(\mathbb{R}))
(both appearing in
C
) is more challenging in general. However, in the hyperelliptic case
\len(X(\mathbb{R}))
can be computed effectively. In particular, in
Section

5
we compute
\len(X_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}))
for a special family of curves
X_{\varepsilon}
of genus
2
.

Example E

(see
Section

5
)
.

Consider the family of curves
X_{\varepsilon}
with hyperelliptic model

w^{2}=(1+z^{2})((1-\varepsilon)^{2}+z^{2})((1+\varepsilon)^{2}+z^{2})

which is a concrete realization of
[
4
]
*Rem. 9.26. Then, for all
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\mathrm{N}(X_{\varepsilon})\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}},

see
Section

5.1
. By a refined analysis, we furthermore prove in
Section

5.2
an upper bound for
\mathrm{N}(X_{\varepsilon})
which grows at the same order
1/\sqrt{\varepsilon}
. This shows that our general lower bound is asymptotically tight for this example. We will also give a concrete family of divisors on
X_{\varepsilon}
that realizes the above lower bound on
\mathrm{N}(X_{\varepsilon})
.

1.2.
Notations and preliminaries

For general references on real algebraic geometry, and in particular on real algebraic curves and their Jacobians, we refer the reader to
[
19
,
42
,

,

,

,

]
.

Let
R
be a real closed field. We denote by
\mathbb P^{n}_{R}
the
n
-dimensional projective space, seen as a scheme over
R
, and in particular
\mathbb P^{n}=\mathbb P^{n}{R}
. A
curve over

R
is a one-dimensional, connected, projective variety (reduced separated scheme of finite type) over
R
, usually denoted by
X
. Unless otherwise stated, curves over
R
are not assumed to be smooth or irreducible. A
real curve
is a curve over
\mathbb{R}
.

The set of
R
-points of a curve
X
over
R
, also called the
real locus
, is denoted
X(R)
. A (Weil or Cartier) divisor on
X
is called
totally real
if its support is included in
X(R)
.

If
R=\mathbb{R}
and the curve
X
is smooth, then
X(\mathbb{R})
is either empty or a disjoint union of smooth one-dimensional manifolds, each diffeomorphic to a circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Each such curve can equivalently be described as a smooth complex curve, which coincides with the complexification
X{C}
of
X
, equipped with an antiholomorphic involution
\sigma\colon X{C}\to X{C}
, and
X(\mathbb{R})=\mathrm{fix}(\sigma)
. If
X(\mathbb{R})\neq\varnothing
, we usually write
X(\mathbb{R})=S_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup S_{r}
with
S_{i}\cong\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

The
topological type
of a smooth curve
X
over
R
is the triple
(g,r,a)
, where

•

g=g(X)
is the genus of
X
;

•

r=r(X)
is the number of (semialgebraically) connected components of
X(R)
;

•

a=a(X)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $X\setminus X(R)$ has two connected components;}\\
1&\text{ if $X\setminus X(R)$ is connected.}\end{cases}

We will call smooth curves with
a(X)=0

dividing
, and those with
a(X)=1

non dividing
. The admissible topological types, i.e., those for which there exists a smooth curve over
R
with topological type
(g,r,a)
, are exactly those which satisfy:

(i)

0\leq r\leq g+1
;

(ii)

if
r=0
then
a=1
, and if
r=g+1
then
a=0
;

(iii)

if
a=0
then
r\equiv g+1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 2
;

see e.g.
[
19
]
for real curves and
[
42
]
*Sec. 20.1.6 more generally for curves over
R
.

If
X
is a curve over
R
, we denote by
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
the Picard group of
X
. If
X
is smooth, we let
J=\jac(X)\coloneqq\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X)
denote the Jacobian of
X
. It is an abelian variety over
R
(see also
Section

2
for the case
R=\mathbb{R}
). Assume
X(R)\neq\varnothing
and fix
P_{0}\in X(R)
. The Abel–Jacobi map is the embedding

\displaystyle\phi\colon X

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\jac(X)

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle\longmapsto[P-P_{0}]

and it is defined over
R
. In particular,
\phi(X(R))\subset\jac(X)(R)
. The qualitative and quantitative relationship between
\phi(X(R))
and
\jac(X)(R)
is our main topic.

For any subset
A\subset\jac(X)
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we write
mA=\{\,a_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}\in\jac(X)\colon a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in A\,\}
.

Lemma 1.2.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth curve over
R
such that
X(R)\neq\varnothing
, and let
\phi\colon X\to J=\jac(X)
be the Abel–Jacobi map with base point
P_{0}\in X(R)
. Let:

•

\begin{aligned} N_{1}=\inf\big\{\,m\in\mathbb{N}\mid\text{ every divisor of degree $m$ is linearly}&\text{ equivalent}\\[-5.0pt]
&\text{to an effective totally real one}\,\big\}\end{aligned}

•

\begin{aligned} N_{2}=\inf\big\{\,m\in\mathbb{N}\mid\text{ every divisor of degree at least $m$ is}&\text{ linearly equivalent}\\[-5.0pt]
&\ \text{to an effective totally real one}\,\big\}\end{aligned}

•

N_{3}=\inf\big\{\,m\in\mathbb{N}\mid m\phi(X(R))=J(R)\,\big\}

•

N_{4}=\inf\big\{\,m\in\mathbb{N}\mid\text{ for all }m^{\prime}\geq m,\ m^{\prime}\phi(X(R))=J(R)\,\big\}

Then
N_{1}=N_{2}=N_{3}=N_{4}
.

Proof.

Sending a divisor
D
to the class
[D-(\deg D)P_{0}]
, by definition of the Jacobian and the Abel–Jacobi map, shows that
N_{1}=N_{3}
and
N_{2}=N_{4}
. Since
P_{0}\in X(R)
, we have
0=[P_{0}-P_{0}]\in\phi(X(R))
. This implies
m\phi(X(R))\subset(m+1)\phi(X(R))\subset J(R)
, proving that
N_{3}=N_{4}
.
∎

The totally real divisor threshold, introduced in
Definition

1.0.1
, for smooth real curves then coincides with any of the integers in
Lemma

1.2.1
.

2.
Periods and metric properties of real curves and abelian varieties

Let
X
be a smooth real curve (i.e., a smooth curve over
\mathbb{R}
, see
Section

1.2
), and let
\sigma\colon X{C}\to X{C}
be the antiholomorphic involution. The antiholomorphic involution
\sigma
acts naturally on the homology and cohomology of
X(\mathbb{C})
, see
[
9
,
34
]
: if
\gamma\in H_{1}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})
and
\omega\in H^{0}(X{C},\Omega)
, then we denote their images by
\gamma^{\sigma}
and
\omega^{\sigma}
.
By abuse of notation, we will denote
H_{1}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})
by
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
.
The complexification
J{C}
of the Jacobian
J=\jac(X)
is isomorphic to the quotient
J{C}\cong H^{0}(X{C},\Omega)^{*}/H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
and inherits an action of
\sigma
.

2.1.
Periods of real curves

Given a smooth curve
X
over
\mathbb{R}
, in this section we discuss how to choose a basis of
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
which is compatible with
\sigma
.
We first notice the following lemma which is a consequence e.g. of
[
7
]
*Lem. 2.1. We state it here explicitly since we could not find a suitable reference.

Lemma 2.1.1
.

Let
S_{1}
, …,
S_{r}
be the connected components of
X(\mathbb{R})
.

•

If
X
is non dividing, then the fundamental classes
[S_{1}]
, …,
[S_{r}]
can be extended to a basis of
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
.

•

If
X
is dividing, then any subset of
r-1
fundamental classes of real connected components can be extended to a basis of
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
.

Definition 2.1.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth real curve of genus
g
, and let
\sigma
be the antiholomorphic involution. A basis
(a_{1},\dots,a_{g};b_{1},\dots,b_{g})
of
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
is called a
Comessatti basis
if:

•

a_{i}\cdot a_{j}=b_{i}\cdot b_{j}=0
and
a_{i}\cdot b_{j}=\delta_{ij}
for
i,j=1,\dots,g
(i.e., the basis is symplectic);

•

a_{i}=a_{i}^{\sigma}
for
i=1,\dots g
.

The existence of such a basis of homology was first proved by Comessatti
[
10
,
11
]
. For more recent references, we refer the reader to
[
46
,
9
]
, where Comesatti bases are called
semireal
and
pseudonormal
, respectively. Our goal in the following is to describe explicit Comessatti bases, using topological models for real curves which can be found e.g. in
[
41
]
*Sec. 1. For a related discussion, see also
[
47
]
. Hereafter we summarize Natanzon’s construction, also to fix the notations which will be used later.

Take an orientable topological surface
Z
of genus
\tilde{g}
with
k=m+r
holes. Equip it with a Riemann surface structure
Z^{+}
, and consider an atlas of holomorphic charts
{(U_{i},z_{i})}
, with
Z^{+}(\mathbb{C})=\bigcupop\displaylimits U_{i}
. The conjugate atlas
{(U_{i},\overline{z}_{i})}
gives another Riemann surface structure
Z^{-}
on
Z
. Consider the natural map
\alpha\colon Z^{+}\to Z\to Z^{-}
, which is antiholomorphic. The complex structures on
Z^{+}
and
Z^{-}
induce metrics of constant curvature on
Z^{+}
and
Z^{-}
, and
\alpha
is an isometry with respect to them.

Consider now geodesics
S_{1},\dots,S_{r},R_{1},\dots,R_{m}
around the

Next, we consider a basis of

&{\mathinner{\mkern 1.0mu\raise 7.0pt\vbox{\kern 7.0pt\hbox{$.$}}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 1.0mu}}&&\lx@intercol\hfil\frac{1}{2}M+iT\hfil\lx@intercol\\
&&{1}&&&\end{array}\right)

(Comessatti)
.

Let

be a smooth real curve. Then there exists a Comessatti basis of

. In particular, given any Natanzon model of type

for

takes the special form (

) or (

), respectively if the base loop is

or

*Prop. 1.1, 3.2, and 3.3 for a proof that does not make use of the special form of

(i)

2.2.
Volumes of principally polarized real abelian varieties

We follow
[
38
]
to introduce principally polarized abelian varieties over
\mathbb{R}
, and
[
5
,
18
]
for classical complex ones. Our definition is equivalent to the one used in
[
46
,
9
]
.

An
abelian variety over
\mathbb{R}
is an irreducible complete group variety over
\mathbb{R}
. In particular, if
A
is an abelian variety over
\mathbb{R}
, then
A(\mathbb{R})\neq\varnothing
since
0\in A(\mathbb{R})
.
A
principal polarization
on an abelian variety over
\mathbb{R}
is an isogeny
f\colon A\to A^{\vee}
of degree
1
such that its complexification
fC=\varphi_{\mathcal{L}}
is the canonical map associated to some invertible sheaf
\mathcal{L}
on
AC
.
If
A
is an abelian variety over
\mathbb{R}
and
f
is a principal polarization, we call
(A,f)
a
principally polarized real abelian variety
.

Let
(A,f)
be a principally polarized real abelian variety.
Write
A_{\mathbb C}\cong V/\Lambda
and
A_{\mathbb C}^{\vee}\cong\hom_{\bar{\mathbb C}}(V,\mathbb C)/\Lambda^{\vee}
, where
W\coloneqq\hom_{\bar{\mathbb C}}(V,\mathbb C)
denotes the
\mathbb C
-vector space of
\mathbb C
-antilinear functions. Then
fC\colon V/\Lambda\to W/\Lambda^{\vee}
, and there exists a unique
\mathbb C
-linear map
F\colon V\to W
with
F(\Lambda)\subset\Lambda^{\vee}
inducing
fC
(see e.g.
[
5
]
*Prop. 1.2.1). Furthermore, the Hermitian form
H\colon V\times V\to\mathbb C
,
(z,w)\mapsto F(z)(w)
is positive definite
[
5
]
*Th. 2.5.5 and Sec. 4.1. The inverse
H^{-1}
is also a positive definite Hermitian form. By parallel transport, the positive definite Hermitian form
H^{-1}
on
V
, identified with the holomorphic tangent space of
AC
at the origin, defines a translation invariant Hermitian (Kähler) metric
\differential s^{2}
on
AC

[
18
]
*p. 301. Recall also that the real part of an Hermitian metric defines a Riemannian metric on the underlying real manifold
[
18
]
*p. 28.

Definition 2.2.1
.

Let
(A,f)
be a principally polarized real abelian variety. We call the translation invariant Hermitian metric
\differential s^{2}
defined above the
canonical Hermitian metric
of
(A,f)
, and
\mathrm{Re(\differential s^{2})}
the
canonical Riemannian metric
of
(A,f)
.

In the following, when referring to metric properties of (real) submanifolds of
AC
, we will always refer to the canonical Riemannian metric.
In particular, the volume
\vol(A(\mathbb{R}))
is well defined because
A(\mathbb{R})
is a
(\dim A)
-dimensional real submanifold of
AC(\mathbb C)
.

Generalizing the case of Jacobians of curves described in
Section

2.1
, there exists
a notion of
Comessatti form
for the period matrix of a principally polarized real abelian variety
(A,f)
. This result is again due to Comessatti; for modern references we point the reader to
[
46
,
9
]
. More precisely, the period matrix
\Pi
can be taken as in (
1
), with
M\in\mathbb{Z}^{\dim A\times\dim A}
symmetric and
T
symmetric, real and positive definite.

Theorem 2.2.2
.

Let
(A,f)
be a principally polarized abelian variety over
\mathbb{R}
and
A(\mathbb{R})_{0}
the real connected component of the identity. Then

\vol(A(\mathbb{R})_{0})=(\det T)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

where the volume is computed with respect to the canonical Riemannian metric of
(A,f)
, and
T
is the imaginary part of a Riemann matrix for
(A,f)
in the form (
1
). In particular,
\det T
is an invariant of
(A,f)
, i.e., it does not depend on the choice of the period matrix.

Proof.

Let
g=\dim A
.
If
A\cong V/\Lambda
, where
\Lambda
are the columns of the period matrix
\Pi
as in (
1
), then
e_{1},\dots,e_{g}
generate
V
, and it follows e.g. from
[
5
]
*Prop. 8.1.1 that
T^{-1}=\mathrm{Im}(\tau)^{-1}
is the Gram matrix of the Hermitian form
H^{-1}
induced by the principal polarization
f
with respect to the basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{g}
. Thanks to Riemann’s bilinear relations,
T
is real, symmetric, positive definite, and we can write
T=B^{t}B
for some
B\in\mathbb{R}^{g\times g}
such that
\det B>0
.

Set
u_{i}=B^{-1}e_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{g}
, so that
u_{1},\dots,u_{g}
is an orthonormal basis for
H^{-1}
.
If
z_{1},\dots,z_{g}
are the corresponding complex coordinates and
x_{1},\dots,x_{g}
are the real coordinates corresponding to
e_{1},\dots,e_{g}
,
then the canonical Hermitian metric is given by
\omega=\differential z_{1}\differential\overline{z}_{1}+\dots+\differential z_{g}\differential\overline{z}_{g}
and we have

\vol(A(\mathbb{R})_{0})=\intop\nolimits_{A(\mathbb{R})_{0}}\differential\vol_{\omega}=\intop\nolimits_{[0,1]^{g}}\det B^{-1}\ \differential x_{1}\cdots\differential x_{g}=\det B^{-1}=\det T^{-\frac{1}{2}}

where
\differential\vol_{\omega}
denotes the volume form with respect to the canonical Riemannian metric
\mathrm{Re}(\omega)
.
∎

It is easy to deduce from
Theorem

2.2.2
how to read the full volume of
A(\mathbb{R})
from the Riemann matrix.

Corollary 2.2.3
.

We follow the same notations of
Theorem

2.2.2
and (
1
). Let
g=\dim A
and
\gamma=\rank_{\mathbb{Z}/2}M
the rank of the reflection matrix
M
modulo
2
.
Then
A(\mathbb{R})
has
2^{g-\gamma}
connected components and:

\vol(A(\mathbb{R}))=2^{g-\gamma}(\det T)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

Proof.

The connected components of
A(\mathbb{R})
are translations of the identity component
A(\mathbb{R})_{0}
. Since the canonical Riemannian metric is translation invariant and by
Theorem

2.2.2
, it suffices to prove that
A(\mathbb{R})
has
2^{g-\gamma}
connected components. A point in

represented by

,

, is fixed by the involution if and only if

for some

. Thus the set of connected components of

is in bijection with classes
\bar{l}\in(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{g}
such that
Ml\in 2\mathbb Z^{g}
.
∎

Notice the difference between the complex and the real picture: with respect to the canonical Riemannian metric,

is given in
Corollary

2.2.3
, while for the complex points we simply have
\vol(A{C}(\mathbb{C}))=1
.

2.3.
Metric properties of real Jacobians

We start by adapting the results of
Section

2.2
to the Jacobian

of a smooth real curve

on

induced by the principal polarization is the dual to the Hodge product on holomorphic

for

[
18
]
*p. 232. In particular, if
\Pi
is a period matrix for
X
in as in (

T_{ij}=\langle{{\omega_{i}},{\omega_{j}}}\rangle
.
Finally, recall that the Abel–Jacobi map

is an embedding.

Definition 2.3.1
.

The pull-back metric on
X
via the Abel–Jacobi map
\phi
of the canonical metric on
\jac(X)
is called the
Bergman
or
canonical
(Hermitian) metric on
X
. We call its real part the

on
X

Notice that, since the canonical metric on

is translation invariant, the Bergman metric does not depend on the choice of the base point

for the Abel–Jacobi map. For more properties of this metric, we refer the reader to
[

,
21
]

Explicitly, the Bergman metric can be described as follows (compare with the proof of
Theorem

2.2.2
). Fixing a symplectic basis of cycles and a normalized basis of holomorphic

-forms
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{g}
, recall that the Gram matrix

in the basis
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{g}
for the Hodge inner product is the imaginary part of the Riemann matrix. Therefore, if we write

, an orthonormal basis

for

with respect to the Hodge product can be defined by

. The Bergman Riemannian metric is therefore
\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{g}\theta_{j}\overline{\theta}_{j}
. Using this metric, the volume of

is equal to

, while the volume of

We now assume that

. Then we can choose

, and the Abel–Jacobi map

. In particular, in this case we have

In the following, we will make a quantitative comparison between

and

to real Jacobians.

Proof of

By

2.1.4
, the number of connected components of

.
∎

3.1.
Families of curves

For

,

and

\mathcal{H}
be the Hilbert scheme of curves in

of genus

be the universal family.
We denote by
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
the set of curves which are not contained in a hyperplane.
Let
\mathcal{H}_{(g,r,a)}\subset\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(R_{1})
denote the subset of

such that

[

]
*§5.8 that this is a semialgebraic set.
By the Riemann–Roch theorem, each such curve is embedded via a complete linear system since the

We are interested in studying the subset

\mathcal{H}_{(g,r,a)}(m)=\{\,h\in\mathcal{H}_{(g,r,a)}\mid\mathrm{N}(Z_{h})\leq m\}.

Lemma 3.1.1
.

The set
\mathcal{H}_{(g,r,a)}(m)
is semialgebraic for all

.

Proof.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that

. Then, for all divisors
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
of respective degrees

and
m
, the divisor
kD_{1}-D_{2}
is linearly equivalent to an effective divisor.
Now notice that
\mathrm{N}(Z_{h})\leq m
if and only if for all effective divisors
E
of degree

on
Z_{h}
, there exists a homogeneous polynomial
F
of degree
k
whose divisor on
Z_{h}
is of the form

where
F^{\prime}
is totally real and effective. This is a semialgebraic condition and hence
\mathcal{H}_{(g,r,a)}(m)
is semialgebraic.
∎

Recall that
R_{2}
denotes the field of algebraic Puiseux series over
R_{1}
and let
h\colon(0,1]\to\mathcal{H}_{(g,r,a)}
be a semialgebraic path. Its germ corresponds to a point
h_{+}\in\mathcal{H}(R_{2})
for which
X_{R_{2}}\coloneqq Z_{h_{+}}
is a smooth curve over
R_{2}
. Furthermore, for
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, let
X_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq Z_{h(\varepsilon)}
.

Lemma 3.1.2
.

With
X_{\varepsilon}
and
X_{R_{2}}
as above, we have
\mathrm{N}(X_{R_{2}})=\infty
if and only if
\mathrm{N}(X_{\varepsilon})
is unbounded for
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Proof.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. By
Lemma

3.1.1
and
[
3
]
*Prop. 3.17 there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that
\mathrm{N}(X_{\varepsilon})\leq m
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
if and only if
\mathrm{N}(X_{R_{2}})\leq m
. This implies the claim.
∎

3.2.
Curves with not so many components

In this section we prove the key result of the paper, namely that if the number of connected components of a curve is less than
g
, then the totally real divisor threshold cannot be bounded from above just in terms of the invariants
g
,
r
and
a
of the curve.

Let
A=R_{1}\llbracket t\rrbracket^{\mathrm{alg}}
be the ring of algebraic formal power series over
R_{1}
. Then the field of algebraic Puiseux series
R_{2}
is the real closure of the field of fractions of
A
. Let
X
be a flat family over
A
whose base change
X_{R_{2}}
is a smooth and geometrically irreducible curve of genus
g\geq 2
over
R_{2}
and whose special fiber
X_{R_{1}}
is a stable curve of genus
g
. Our first goal is to prove the following.

Theorem 3.2.1
.

With the above notations, let
\widetilde{X}_{R_{1}}\to X_{R_{1}}
be the normalization of the special fiber
X_{R_{1}}
of
X
.

(i)

If
\widetilde{X}_{R_{1}}({R_{1}})=\varnothing
and the divisor class group of
\widetilde{X}_{R_{1}}
is not finitely generated, then
\mathrm{N}(X_{R_{2}})=\infty
.

(ii)

If
X_{R_{1}}
is smooth,
m\geq 2g+1
and
\mathrm{N}(X_{R_{2}})\leq m
, then
\mathrm{N}(X_{R_{1}})\leq m
.

Proof.

It follows from our assumptions and
[
20
]
*15.7.10, 15.7.8 that
X
is proper over
A
. Recall from
[
12
]
*§1 that there is a canonical invertible sheaf
\omega_{X/A}
on
X
such that
\omega_{X/A}\otimes_{A}R_{2}
is the canonical sheaf of
X_{R_{2}}
and such that for all
n\geq 3
the sheaf
\omega_{X/A}^{\otimes n}
is relatively very ample with
H^{0}(X,\omega_{X/A}^{\otimes n})\cong A^{M}
, where
M=(2n-1)(g-1)
. Thus we obtain an embedding
X\to\mathbb P_{A}^{M}
for which the induced embedding
X_{R_{2}}\to\mathbb P^{M}_{R_{2}}
is the
n
-th pluricanonical map. We also obtain a morphism
f\colon\widetilde{X}_{R_{1}}\to\mathbb P^{M}_{R_{1}}
whose image is
X_{R_{1}}
. In case
(ii)
, the morphism
f
is also an embedding since
\widetilde{X}_{R_{1}}={X}_{R_{1}}
.

Now let
m\geq 2g+1
and assume that
\mathrm{N}(X_{R_{2}})\leq m
. In case
(i)
we will produce a contradiction and in case
(ii)
we will show that
\mathrm{N}(X_{R_{1}})\leq m
.
Let
S\subset\widetilde{X}_{R_{1}}
be the preimage of the singular locus of
X_{R_{1}}
under the normalization map.
Let
D
be a reduced effective divisor on
\widetilde{X}_{R_{1}}
of degree
d\geq m
. In case
(ii)
, we note that every divisor class of degree
d\geq m
has such a representative because
m\geq 2g+1
. In case
(i)
, we additionally assume that the support of
D
is disjoint from
S
and that its class
[D]
is not contained in the subgroup of the divisor class group generated by classes of points from
S
. Such a divisor exists when
d
is large enough by our assumption that the divisor class group of
\widetilde{X}_{R_{1}}
is not finitely generated.

Choosing
n
large enough, there is a hyperplane
H\subset\mathbb P^{M}_{R_{1}}
that contains the image of the support of
D
under
f\colon\widetilde{X}_{R_{1}}\to\mathbb P^{M}_{R_{1}}
and intersects
X_{R_{1}}
transversally. By Hensel’s lemma, for every point in the support of
D
there is a point in
H\cap X_{R_{2}}
that specializes to it. Hence the divisor on
X_{R_{2}}
defined by
H
can be written as the sum
E=E_{1}+E_{2}
of two effective divisors such that
E_{1}
specializes to
f(D)
and
E_{2}
to the sum of the remaining intersection points of
X_{R_{1}}
with
H
. We denote the divisor on
\widetilde{X}_{R_{1}}
consisting of these remaining intersection points by
D_{2}
, i.e., the divisor defined by
H
on
\widetilde{X}_{R_{1}}
is
D+D_{2}
. Since
\deg(E_{1})\geq\mathrm{N}(X_{R_{2}})
,
E_{1}
is linearly equivalent to a totally real divisor
E_{1}^{\prime}
on
X_{R_{2}}
. There is a hyperplane
H^{\prime}
of
\mathbb P^{M}_{R_{2}}
whose divisor on
X_{R_{2}}
is
E_{1}^{\prime}+E_{2}
. Every point of
E_{1}^{\prime}
specializes to a real point of
X_{R_{1}}
. Thus, in case
(i)
, the hyperplane
H^{\prime}
specializes to a hyperplane whose divisor on
\widetilde{X}_{R_{1}}
is of the form
D_{1}^{\prime}+D_{2}
, where
D_{1}^{\prime}
is an effective divisor whose support is contained in
S
(
X_{R_{1}}
has only singular real points by the assumption
\widetilde{X}_{R_{1}}(R_{1})=\varnothing
). Because
D
is linearly equivalent to
D_{1}^{\prime}
, this is a contradiction to our assumtion that
[D]
is not contained in the subgroup generated by classes of points of
S
. In case
(ii)
, the hyperplane
H^{\prime}
specializes to a hyperplane whose divisor on
{X}_{R_{1}}
is of the form
D_{1}^{\prime}+D_{2}
, where
D_{1}^{\prime}
is an effective and totally real divisor. Because
D
is linearly equivalent to
D_{1}^{\prime}
, this shows that
\mathrm{N}(X_{R_{1}})\leq d
. Since in case
(ii)
we can choose
d=m
, this shows
\mathrm{N}(X_{R_{1}})\leq m
.
∎

We now give examples of families of curves over

Example 3.2.2
.

Let

and

Let

Example 3.2.3
.

Let

and

.
To this end, consider the unramified double cover

of the curve

from

3.2.2

.

Let

. We construct a curve of topological type

These remaining cases are covered by the following example, which is however not at the same level of concreteness as the previous ones.

Example 3.2.5
.

Consider a stable curve over
{R_{1}}
whose base change to
{R_{1}}(i)
consists of two smooth curves of genus

that intersect transversally in

points and that are exchanged by the non-trivial

-automorphism of

.
The genus of such a curve is

.
We will argue that there exists a flat family over

having this curve as special fiber and whose base change to

is a smooth curve

of diving type whose set of

.
For

, this covers the case of dividing

-curves.
It appears to be well-known among experts that it is possible to find such a family but for lack of a reference we provide a brief argument.

First, assume there is some flat family
X
over

whose base change

is a smooth projective and geometrically irreducible curve and whose special fiber

is a stable curve as above. Let

As in

*Excercise 5.7 the induced family over the dual numbers gives a global section

that specializes to

. We have to show that a family

*Ex. 5.7. Finally, such a family over the dual numbers can be lifted to one over

Now we are ready to prove the first two of our main results. We start with a refined version of

.

Theorem 3.2.6
.

Let
R
be a real closed field.

(i)

If
X
is a smooth curve over
R
of genus
g
with
\mathrm{N}(X)=\infty
such that

has

(semialgebraically) connected components, then

and

is non-Archimedean.

(ii)

Conversely, if
R
is non-Archimedean and
(g,r,a)
is an admissible topological type with

, then there exists a smooth curve
X
over
R
with topological type
(g,r,a)
such that
\mathrm{N}(X)=\infty
.

Proof.

Let
R
be a real closed field and
X
a smooth curve over
R
of genus
g
. If
R
is Archimedean, then, by Hölder’s theorem
[
28
]
*Theorem 2.1.10, there is an embedding of

to
\mathbb{R}
. By Scheiderer’s result
[
43
]
, we have
\mathrm{N}(X_{\mathbb{R}})\leq m
for some

where

is the base change of
X
to
\mathbb{R}
. Since this is a semialgebraic condition by

3.1.1
, Tarski’s principle shows that
\mathrm{N}(X)\leq m
. If

, then the results of Huisman
[
25
]
and Monnier
[
39
]
imply that
\mathcal{H}_{(g,r,a)}=\mathcal{H}_{(g,r,a)}(2g-1)
holds over

. Thus, by Tarski’s principle, one has
\mathrm{N}(X)\leq 2g-1
for every smooth curve
X
of genus
g
over any real closed field

whenever

has at least
r
components. This shows part (i) of
A
.

Now let
R
be a non-Archimedean real closed field. Let
R_{1}
be the relative algebraic closure of
\mathbb Q
in
R
, which is a real closed field. Because

is not Archimedean, there exists

such that

for all

. Such

is necessarily transcendental over
R_{1}
. Therefore, the relative algebraic closure of

in

can be identified with the field

of algebraic Puiseux series over

.

Consider an admissible topological type
(g,r,a)
with

and choose a smooth curve

over
R_{2}
of this topological type with
\mathrm{N}(X_{R_{2}})=\infty
. If

, any such curve satisfies

by definition; if

and

, then we can choose

as in

; if

and

, then

for some

and we can choose

as in

.
By

and Tarski’s principle, this shows that
\mathrm{N}(X_{R})=\infty
where

is the base change of

to

. This shows part (ii) of

.
∎

To prove

, we need to show how the totally real divisor threshold behaves on proper flat families.

(Semicontinuity)
.

and

Proof.

By
[
12
]
*§1, the morphism

factors through an embedding of

, such that the induced embedding of each

of curves of genus

\mathbb{R}
because
\mathcal{M}_{g}
is of finite type. If we define
\mathrm{N}(T,m)
as in
Theorem

3.2.7
, then
\mathrm{N}(T,m)=f{R}^{-1}(\mathrm{N}(g,m))
and the set
\mathrm{N}(T,m)
is closed by
Theorem

3.2.7
. Thus
\mathrm{N}(g,m)
is closed in
\absolutevalue{\cM_g(\RR)}\cong\mathscr{M}_{g}{R}
.
∎

4.
Quantitative analysis: topology and geometry

While in
Section

3
we studied the boundedness or unboundedness of the totally real divisor threshold, we now focus on concrete bounds in the case of curves over
\mathbb{R}
.

4.1.
Curves with many components

In this section, we consider curves with many connected components, i.e., smooth real curves
X
of genus
g
such that
r(X)=g
or
r(X)=g+1
. Our goal is to reprove results of Huisman and Monnier, showing that the totally real divisor threshold is bounded from above by
2g-1
in these cases.

Recall that the identity component
J(\mathbb{R})_{0}
of the real locus of the Jacobian
J=\jac(X)\cong\Pic^{0}(X)
consists of those classes
[D]
of conjugation-invariant divisors
D
of degree
0
whose restriction to each connected component of
X(\mathbb{R})
is of even degree, see e.g.
[
19
]
. We will need the following, more refined characterization of
J(\mathbb{R})_{0}
.

Proposition 4.1.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth real curve and
J=\jac(X)
. Suppose that
X(\mathbb{R})
has
g
or
g+1
connected components, and let
S_{1},\dots,S_{g}
be
g
of these components. Fix points
P_{i}\in S_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,g
, then

J(\mathbb{R})_{0}=\biggl\{\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{g}[Q_{i}-P_{i}]\ |\ Q_{i}\in S_{i}\text{ for }i=1,\dots,g\biggr\}.

Proof.

Let
T=\jac(\mathbb{R})_{0}\cong(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb Z)^{g}
, a
g
-dimensional real torus. Since
S_{1},\ldots,S_{g}
do not disconnect
X
, their fundamental classes can be extended to an integral homology basis of
H_{1}(X,\mathbb Z)
by
Lemma

2.1.1
. Let
S=S_{1}\times\cdots\times S_{g}\cong(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{g}
, another
g
-dimensional real torus. Fixing base points
P_{1},\dots,P_{g}
as above, we obtain a map

\displaystyle\varphi\colon S

\displaystyle\longrightarrow T

\displaystyle(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{g})

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{g}[Q_{i}-P_{i}].

Since
S_{1},\dots,S_{g}
are part of a basis of
H_{1}(X,\mathbb Z)
, the induced map
\varphi_{\ast}\colon H_{1}(S,\mathbb Z)\to H_{1}(T,\mathbb Z)
is an isomorphism. The cohomology rings of
S
and
T
are generated in degree
1
(see
[
23
]
*3.11), hence it follows from Poincaré duality that
\varphi_{\ast}\colon H_{g}(S,\mathbb Z)\to H_{g}(T,\mathbb Z)
is an isomorphism, as well. This implies that
\varphi
is a map of topological degree
1
. In particular, it is surjective, concluding the proof.
∎

Proposition

4.1.1
allows us to give a unified proof of results due to Huisman
[
25
]
and Monnier
[
39
]
.

Theorem 4.1.2

(
[
25
,
39
]
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth real curve of genus
g
such that
X(\mathbb{R})
has
r=g
or
r=g+1
connected components. Then
\mathrm{N}(X)\leq 2g-1
.

Proof.

Let
D
be a conjugation-invariant divisor of degree
d\geq 2g-1
on
X
. Let
S_{1},\dots,S_{r}
be the connected components of
X(\mathbb{R})
and fix points
P_{i}\in S_{i}
for each
i
as above. Let
m
be the number of components for which the restriction of
D
to
S_{i}
has odd degree. We may relabel and assume that these are the components
S_{1},\dots,S_{m}
. Consider the divisor
E=D-(P_{1}+\cdots+P_{m})-2(P_{m+1}+\dots+P_{g})
. By our choices, it has even degree on each connected component and hence its total degree
d-m-2(g-m)=d-2g+m\geq-1
is also even, say
2k\geq 0
. It follows that the class of
E-2kP
is contained in
J(\mathbb{R})_{0}
for any point
P\in X(\mathbb{R})
. By
Proposition

4.1.1
, there are points
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{g}
with
Q_{i}\in S_{i}
such that
E-2kP\equiv\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{g}(Q_{i}-P_{i})
, so that

D\equiv 2kP+P_{m+1}+\cdots+P_{g}+Q_{1}+\cdots+Q_{g}.\qed

4.2.
Bounds from metric properties

Let
X
be a smooth real curve. We denote by
\len(X(\mathbb{R}))
the length of
X(\mathbb{R})
with respect to the Bergman metric and by
\vol(J(\mathbb{R}))
the volume of the real part of the Jacobian
J=\jac(X)
with respect to the canonical metric (see
Section

2.3
for the definitions).
As a warm-up, we prove a lower bound on
\mathrm{N}(X)
when
X(\mathbb{R})
is connected. Recall that we always assume that
X(\mathbb{R})\neq\varnothing
and that the Abel–Jacobi map
\phi
is defined with base point
P_{0}\in X(\mathbb{R})
, so that
\phi(X(\mathbb{R}))\subset\jac(X)(\mathbb{R})
.

Proposition 4.2.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth real curve such that
X(\mathbb{R})
is connected and
J=\jac(X)
its Jacobian. Then

\mathrm{N}(X)\geq\frac{\vol(J(\mathbb{R}))^{1/g}}{\len(X(\mathbb{R}))}

Proof.

Recall that
\len(X(\mathbb{R}))
is equal, by definition, to the length of
\phi(X(\mathbb{R}))\subset J(\mathbb{R})
, the image of
X(\mathbb{R})
under the Abel–Jacobi map. By
Corollary

2.1.4
, since
X(\mathbb{R})
has a single connected component, we have
J(\mathbb{R})=J(\mathbb{R})_{0}\cong\mathbb{R}^{g}/\Lambda
. The curve
\phi(X(\mathbb{R}))
is contained in a domain of the form
B/\Lambda\subset J(\mathbb{R})
, where
B
is a
g
-dimensional hypercube with side length equal to
\len(X(\mathbb{R}))
. Therefore, the Minkowski-type sum
m\phi(X(\mathbb{R}))=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{m}\phi(X(\mathbb{R}))
is contained in
mB/\Lambda
, and we have:

\vol(m\phi(X(\mathbb{R})))\leq\vol(mB\big/\Lambda)\leq\vol(mB)=(m\len(X(\mathbb{R})))^{g}

where
\vol(mB)
is computed with respect to the (flat) Euclidean metric of
\mathbb{R}^{g}\cong H^{0}(X,\Omega)(\mathbb{R})
induced by the Hodge inner product.

Now, if
\vol(m\phi(X(\mathbb{R})))
is smaller than
\vol(\jac(\mathbb{R}))
, then
m<\mathrm{N}(X)
(see
Lemma

1.2.1
). From the above inequality, this is implied by
(m\len(X(\mathbb{R})))^{g}<\vol J(\mathbb{R})
. The contrapositive of this implication
is
\mathrm{N}(X)\leq m\Rightarrow\frac{\vol(\jac(\mathbb{R}))^{1/g}}{\len(X(\mathbb{R}))}\leq m
. Letting
m=\mathrm{N}(X)
shows the claim.
∎

We now generalize
Proposition

4.2.1
to real curves with an arbitrary number of connected components, proving
C
.

Proof of
C
.

Let
X(\mathbb{R})=S_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup S_{r}
be the connected components of the real locus, and let
Z_{i}=\phi(S_{i})
. Notice that

m\phi(X(\mathbb{R}))=\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{r}\\
\absolutevalue{\alpha}=m\end{subarray}}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}Z_{i}

where the addition is in the group law of
J=\jac(X)\cong\mathbb{C}^{g}/\Lambda
. Recall that a divisor class belongs to the identity component
\jac(\mathbb{R})_{0}
if and only if it has even degree on each connected component
S_{i}
. Therefore, we obtain:

(6)

2m\phi(X(\mathbb{R}))\cap\jac(\mathbb{R})_{0}=\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in(2\mathbb{N})^{r}\\
\absolutevalue{\alpha}=2m\end{subarray}}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}Z_{i}=2\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{r}\\
\absolutevalue{\alpha}=m\end{subarray}}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}Z_{i}.

Notice that, if
\ell_{i}=\len(Z_{i})=\len(S_{i})
, then there exists a
g
-dimensional hypercube
B_{i}
with side length
\ell_{i}
such that
Z_{i}\subset B_{i}/\Lambda
. Therefore
\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{r}Z_{i}\subset B/\Lambda
for some
g
-dimensional hypercube
B
with side length equal to
\len(X(\mathbb{R}))=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{r}\ell_{i}
. We deduce

(7)

\vol(k\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{r}Z_{i})\leq\vol(kB\big/\Lambda)\leq(k\len(X(\mathbb{R})))^{g}

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\vol\left(2m\phi(X(\mathbb{R}))\cap\jac(\mathbb{R})_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\vol\left(2\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{r}\\
\absolutevalue{\alpha}=m\end{subarray}}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}Z_{i}\right)

using (
6
)

\displaystyle=2^{g}\vol\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{r}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{r}\\
\absolutevalue{\alpha}=m\end{subarray}}\alpha_{i}\right)Z_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{g}\vol\left(\frac{m}{r}\binom{m+r-1}{r-1}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{r}Z_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{g}\left(\frac{m}{r}\binom{m+r-1}{r-1}\len(X(\mathbb{R}))\right)^{g}

using (
7
)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{g}\left(\frac{m}{r}\len(X(\mathbb{R}))\right)^{g}\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}(m+r-1)}{r-1}\right)^{g(r-1)}

since
\binom{n}{k}\leq(\mathrm{e}n/k)^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2\len(X(\mathbb{R}))}{r}\right)^{g}\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}}{r-1}\right)^{g(r-1)}(m+r-1)^{gr}

(here, we use the convention
(\mathrm{e}n/k)^{k}=1
for
k=0
).
Now, if

\left(\frac{2\len(X(\mathbb{R}))}{r}\right)^{g}\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}}{r-1}\right)^{g(r-1)}(m+r-1)^{gr}<\vol(\jac(\mathbb{R})_{0}),

then
2m\phi(X(\mathbb{R}))\cap\jac(\mathbb{R})_{0}\subsetneq\jac(\mathbb{R})_{0}
, and thus
\mathrm{N}(X)>2m
(see
Lemma

1.2.1
).
The contrapositive of this implication
is

\frac{\mathrm{N}(X)}{2}\leq m\Rightarrow\left(\frac{\vol(\jac(\mathbb{R})_{0})}{\len(X(\mathbb{R}))^{g}}\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^{g}\left(\frac{r-1}{\mathrm{e}}\right)^{g(r-1)}\right)^{\frac{1}{gr}}+1-r\leq m.

Letting
m=\lceil{\mathrm{N}(X)}/{2}\rceil
, this implies the claim.
∎

grows at least as
4K\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)(\pi\sqrt{\varepsilon})^{-1}
for
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.
In the next section, we show that this lower bound is asymptotically tight, up to a constant.

5.2.
Refined analysis

While in

We will need the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 5.2.1
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a, smooth, closed, simple curve enclosing a convex region of
\mathbb{R}^{2}

\alpha\in\conv(\Gamma)

\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
such that
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})

Proof.

This follows from the intermediate value theorem.
∎

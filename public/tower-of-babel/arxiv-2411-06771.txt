Towards the Proximity Conjecture on Group-Labeled Matroids
Dániel Garamvölgyi, Ryuhei Mizutani, Taihei Oki, Tamás Schwarcz, Yutaro Yamaguchi

Abstract
Consider a matroid M M whose ground set is equipped with a labeling to an abelian group. A basis of M M is called F F -avoiding if the sum of the labels of its elements is not in a forbidden label set F F . Hörsch, Imolay, Mizutani, Oki, and Schwarcz (2024) conjectured that if an F F -avoiding basis exists, then any basis can be transformed into an F F -avoiding basis by exchanging at most | F | |F| elements. This proximity conjecture is known to hold for certain specific groups; in the case where | F | ≤ 2 |F|\leq 2 ; or when the matroid is subsequence-interchangeably base orderable (SIBO), which is a weakening of the so-called strongly base orderable (SBO) property. In this paper, we settle the proximity conjecture for sparse paving matroids or in the case where | F | ≤ 4 |F|\leq 4 . Related to the latter result, we present the first known example of a non-SIBO matroid. We further address the setting of multiple group-label constraints, showing proximity results for the cases of two labelings, SIBO matroids, matroids representable over a fixed, finite field, and sparse paving matroids. Keywords: sparse paving matroid, subsequence-interchangeable base orderability, congruency constraint, multiple labelings

Our first main result is a proof of

Proximity Theorem for Sparse Paving Matroids

In this section, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Conjecture

1.1
is true when
M
is a sparse paving matroid.

Proof.

Suppose, to the contrary, that
Conjecture

1.1
does not hold for a sparse paving matroid
M
on the ground set
E
, a labeling
\psi\colon E\to\Gamma
, and a forbidden label set
F\subseteq\Gamma
.
Then, as minors of sparse paving matroids are sparse paving,
we may assume by
Lemma

2.1
that
M
has rank
r=|F|+1
, it contains exactly one
F
-avoiding basis
B
, and
E\setminus B
is also a basis (with
\psi(E\setminus B)\in F
).
Clearly,
|F|\geq 1
.

We first consider the case when
|\psi(E)|\geq r+1
.
Suppose that
B
is
rainbow
, i.e., no two elements in
B
have the same label.
Fix an element
e\in E\setminus B
such that
B+e
is still rainbow, and consider the sets
(E\setminus B)-e+f
for
f\in B
.
Since
M
is sparse paving, at least
r-1=|F|
of these
r
sets are bases, and none of them is
F
-avoiding, as
B
is the only
F
-avoiding basis.
Since
B+e
is rainbow, these bases and
E\setminus B
, each of which is obtained by adding an element of
B+e
to
(E\setminus B)-e
, have distinct labels, a contradiction.

Next, suppose that
B
is not rainbow.
As there are
r+1
elements of different labels, we can take a set
X\subseteq E
with
|X|=r
and an element
e\in E\setminus X
such that
X+e
is rainbow.
We consider
r
sets
X+e-f
for
f\in X
in addition to
X
itself.
Since
M
is sparse paving, at least
r=|F|+1
of these
r+1

\psi_{t}(B_{1,q})=f_{t}
. Since
\ell\geq k\cdot c_{k-1}+1
, this implies that there exists
t\in[k]
with
|\{\,{q\in[\ell]}\;|\;{\psi_{t}(B_{1,q})=f_{t}}\,\}|\geq c_{k-1}+1
. We may assume that
t=k
, and let
1\leq q_{0}<\dots<q_{c_{k-1}}\leq\ell
be indices with
\psi_{k}(B_{1,q_{0}})=\dots=\psi_{k}(B_{1,q_{c_{k-1}}})=f_{k}
.
Define
X^{\prime}_{i}\coloneqq X_{q_{i-1}+1}\cup\dots\cup X_{q_{i}}
and
Y^{\prime}_{i}\coloneqq Y_{q_{i-1}+1}\cup\dots\cup Y_{q_{i}}
for

, and
B^{\prime}_{i,j}\coloneqq(B\setminus(X^{\prime}_{i}\cup\dots\cup X^{\prime}_{j}))\cup(Y^{\prime}_{i}\cup\dots\cup Y^{\prime}_{j})
for
1\leq i\leq j\leq c_{k-1}
.
Observe that
Y^{\prime}_{i}=B_{1,q_{i}}\setminus B_{1,q_{i-1}}
and
X^{\prime}_{i}=B_{1,q_{i-1}}\setminus B_{1,q_{i}}
hold for
i\in[c_{k-1}]
; thus

\psi_{k}(Y^{\prime}_{i})-\psi_{k}(X^{\prime}_{i})=\psi_{k}(B_{1,q_{i}})-\psi_{k}(B_{1,q_{i-1}})=f_{k}-f_{k}=0.

(15)

Using the definition of
c_{k-1}
, we get that there exist indices
1\leq i^{\prime}\leq j^{\prime}\leq c_{k-1}
such that
\psi_{t}(B^{\prime}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}})\neq f_{t}
for all
t\in[k-1]
.
By defining
i\coloneqq q_{i^{\prime}-1}+1
and
j\coloneqq q_{j^{\prime}}
, we get

and

\psi_{k}(B_{i,j})=\psi_{k}(B)+\sum_{s=i^{\prime}}^{j^{\prime}}(\psi_{k}(Y^{\prime}_{s})-\psi_{k}(X^{\prime}_{s}))=\psi_{k}(B)\neq f_{k}.

(16)

This proves that
\psi_{t}(B_{i,j})\neq f_{t}
for all
t\in[k]
, a contradiction.
∎

implies that

Proof.

Assume for contradiction that

We have that

This implies that

(17)

By symmetry, we also get

|\{\,{q\in[4]}\;|\;{\psi_{1}(B_{q,4})=f_{1}}\,\}|=|\{\,{q\in[4]}\;|\;{\psi_{2}(B_{q,4})=f_{2}}\,\}|=2.

(18)

We may assume that

. Let

denote the unique indices such that

thus

Using induction we obtain that

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
k\geq 0
be an integer and
M
a sparse paving matroid of rank
r
. If
\min\{r,|E(M)|-r\}\geq\binom{2k}{k}
, then for each basis
B
,
M
has a
B
-minor isomorphic to
U_{k,2k}
.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
k
. The statements clearly hold for
k=0
, so assume that it holds for
k-1
. Let
E\coloneqq E(M)
,
n\coloneqq|E|
, and
\mathcal{H}\coloneqq\binom{E}{r}\setminus\mathcal{B}(M)
.
Recall that since
M
is sparse paving,
|H_{1}\cap H_{2}|\leq r-2
holds for all
H_{1},H_{2}\in\mathcal{H}
with
H_{1}\neq H_{2}
.
Since
\min\{r,n-r\}\geq\binom{2k-2}{k-1}
, by induction there exist
X,Y
with
Y\subseteq B\subseteq X\subseteq E
such that
(M|X)/Y
is isomorphic to
U_{k-1,2k-2}
.

Let
S\coloneqq X\setminus Y
and
\mathcal{H}_{0}\coloneqq\{\,{H\in\mathcal{H}}\;|\;{H\subseteq B\cup S,\ |H\cap S|=k}\,\}
.
Observe that
|Y\setminus H|=|Y|-|H|+|H\cap S|=(r-k+1)-r+k=1
for each
H\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
.
This together with
M
being sparse paving implies that for each set
Z\subseteq S
, there exists at most one
H\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
with
H\cap S=Z
, implying
|\mathcal{H}_{0}|\leq\binom{2k-2}{k}
.
Since
|Y\setminus H|=1
for
H\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
|Y|=r-k+1\geq\binom{2k}{k}-k+1>\binom{2k-2}{k}\geq|\mathcal{H}_{0}|
, there exists
y\in Y\cap\bigcap_{H\in\mathcal{H}_{0}}H
.
Let
Y^{\prime}\coloneqq Y-y
.
We claim that
(M|X)/Y^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
U_{k,2k-1}
, i.e., for each subset
Z\subseteq S+y
with
|Z|=k
,
Z\cup Y^{\prime}
is a basis of
M
.
Indeed, if
y\in Z
, then
Z\cup Y^{\prime}
being a basis follows from
(M|X)/Y
being isomorphic to
U_{k-1,2k-2}
.
If
y\notin Z
, then
|Z\cap S|=k
, thus
Z\not\in\mathcal{H}
follows from
y\in\bigcap_{H\in\mathcal{H}_{0}}H
.
This shows that
(M|X)/Y^{\prime}
is indeed isomorphic to
U_{k,2k-1}
.

Let
T\coloneqq X\setminus Y^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{H}_{1}\coloneqq\{\,{H\in\mathcal{H}}\;|\;{Y^{\prime}\subseteq H,\ |H\cap T|=k-1}\,\}
.
Observe that
|H\setminus X|=1
for each
H\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
.
Then, as
M
is sparse paving, for each set
Z\subseteq T
, there exists at most one
H\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
with
H\cap T=Z
, implying
|\mathcal{H}_{1}|\leq\binom{2k-1}{k-1}
.
Since
|H\setminus X|=1
for
H\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
|E\setminus X|\geq|E|-r-k+1\geq\binom{2k}{k}-k+1>\binom{2k-1}{k-1}\geq|\mathcal{H}_{1}|
, there exists
x\in E\setminus X
with
x\in(E\setminus X)\setminus\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal{H}_{1}}H
.
Let
X^{\prime}\coloneqq X+x
.
We claim that
(M|X^{\prime})/Y^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
U_{k,2k}
, i.e., for each subset
Z\subseteq T+x
with
|Z|=k
,
Z\cup Y^{\prime}
is a basis of
M
.
Indeed, if
x\notin Z
, then
Z\cup Y^{\prime}
being a basis follows from
(M|X)/Y^{\prime}
being isomorphic to
U_{k,2k-1}
.
If
x\in Z
, then
|Z\cap T|=k-1
, thus
Z\cup Y^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{H}
follows from
x\not\in\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal{H}_{1}}H
.
Therefore,
(M|X^{\prime})/Y^{\prime}
is indeed isomorphic to
U_{k,2k}
, finishing the proof.
∎

Remark 5.8
.

We note that Pendavingh and van der Pol
[
41
]
showed that for a fixed
k
, asymptotically almost all matroids contain
U_{k,2k}
as a minor. If
M
is a sparse paving matroid, then a counting argument found in
[
41
, Lemma 4.7]
combined with the observation
|\mathcal{B}(M)|\geq\frac{r}{r+1}\binom{|E|}{r}
implies that if

and
|E(M)|-r\geq k
hold, then
M
contains
U_{k,2k}
as a minor. It is not clear whether a similar argument can be used to give a simple proof of the existence of such a
B
-minor for any basis
B
as in
Theorem

5.7
.

Theorems

5.6
and
5.7
and
Lemma

5.2
immediately verify
Conjecture

1.5
for matroids representable over a fixed, finite field and sparse paving matroids.

.

Let

be the class of (1)

Appendix A
CNF formulation of finding a non-SIBO matroid

In this section, we describe how we can reduce the problem of finding a
2r
-elements, rank-
r
, non-SIBO matroid to SAT by describing a CNF (conjunctive normal form) formulation.

Let
E=[2r]
be the ground set.
We prepare
\binom{2r}{r}
Boolean variables
x_{B}
indexed by
B\in\binom{E}{r}
.
We build a CNF such that
\big\{\,{B\in\binom{E}{r}}\;\big|\;{\text{$x_{B}$ is true}}\,\big\}
forms the basis family of a matroid,
[r]
and
E\setminus[r]
are bases, and
([r],E\setminus[r])
has no SI-ordering by collecting the following clauses.

Basis exchange property:

for every
A,B\in\binom{E}{r}
and
e\in A\setminus B
,

\displaystyle\neg x_{A}\vee\neg x_{B}\vee\bigvee_{f\in B\setminus A}x_{A-e+f}.

(22)

Fixed basis:

\displaystyle x_{[r]}.

(23)

Fixed basis:

\displaystyle x_{E\setminus[r]}.

(24)

No SI-ordering:

for every permutation
a_{1},\dotsc,a_{r}
of
[r]
and
b_{1},\dotsc,b_{r}
of
E\setminus[r]
,

\displaystyle\bigvee_{0\leq i<j\leq r}\neg x_{\{a_{1},\dotsc,a_{i},b_{i+1},\dotsc,b_{j},a_{j+1},\dotsc,a_{r}\}}.

(25)

Note that if a non-disjoint basis pair
(A,B)
of a matroid
M
has no SI-ordering, then
(A\setminus B,B\setminus A)
has no SI-ordering as well in
M/(A\cap B)
.
Thus, we can restrict our attention to the disjoint basis pair
([r],E\setminus[r])
by verifying the unsatisfiability of the CNF from small
r
.

Our Python script to solve the above SAT instance is available at
https://github.com/taiheioki/sibo
.

Unravelling the Holomorphic Twist II: Anomalies and Extended Supersymmetry
Pieter Bomans , Niklas Garner ,Brian R. Williams , and Jingxiang Wu, [8mm] Mathematical Institute, University of OxfordAndrew Wiles Building, Radcliffe Observatory QuarterWoodstock Road, Oxford, OX2 6GG, U.K., [3mm] Department of Mathematics & Statistics, Boston University665 Commonwealth Ave, Boston, MA, 02215

Abstract
Twists of four-dimensional supersymmetric quantum field theories (SQFTs) isolate protected sectors with rich algebraic structures. We develop a unified framework for analyzing symmetries and anomalies in four-dimensional holomorphically twisted SQFTs, combining complex-geometric and algebraic perspectives. This approach clarifies the connections between existing formulations in the literature and resolves several open questions left unanswered in the first installment of this series. We place particular emphasis on theories with extended supersymmetry, where the holomorphic twist gives rise to enhanced algebraic and geometric structures. We explain how these features emerge and govern the organization of the twisted theory. Furthermore, we demonstrate how a superconformal deformation of the twisted theory naturally leads to the associated vertex operator algebra, clarifying how the higher algebraic structures of the holomorphically twisted theory give rise to vertex algebras structures.

Twists of four-dimensional supersymmetric quantum field theories (SQFTs) have emerged as powerful tools to isolate protected sectors whose algebraic structure often enables exact computations.

By a twist of a superconformal theory we refer to the process of taking (derived) invariants with respect to an arbitrary square-zero supercharge that may or may not be translation invariant, i.e. we view this square-zero supercharge as a differential and observables as a chain complex with respect it; “physical” observables of the twisted theory thus correspond to cohomology classes.

One of the primary advantages of holomorphic field theories, such as those arising from twisting four-dimensional SQFTs, is that they remain amenable to powerful algebraic and geometric techniques, many of which are familiar from two-dimensional chiral conformal field theory (CFT).
Two-dimensional CFTs are distinguished by their

1.1  Summary of results

As mentioned above, four-dimensional holomorphic field theories, i.e. those living in two complex dimensions, admit
\infty
-dimensional symmetry algebras that arise in much the same way as in two dimensions, although there are some additional ingredients that are not present in the latter case.
In the context of minimally twisted
\mathcal{N}\geq 1
SQFT, we fix a holomorphic supercharge
\mathbf{Q}
, which always exists in this case and is unique up to equivalence; more generally
\mathbf{Q}
should be viewed as the BV/BRST differential capturing the gauge redundancies and equations of motion of the holomorphic field theory.
We start our discussion in Section
2
by reviewing the origin of these four-dimensional symmetry algebras and setting up a useful organizational framework using a notion of superfield suitable to holomorphic field theories.
For twisted SQFTs, these
reduced superfields
can be thought of as organizing fields into a multiplet including its holomorphic descendants.
Each
\mathcal{N}=1
multiplet obeying an anti-chiral shortening condition contributes a single
\mathbf{Q}
-closed operator
O
, naturally identified as the bottom component of a
semi-chiral
superfield
\mathbf{O}
, which is a special type of reduced superfield.

When the untwisted theory possesses a flavor symmetry, the spectrum includes a conserved current multiplet, which in the twisted theory gives rise to a semi-chiral superfield
\mathbf{J}
satisfying the conservation law
\bar{\partial}\mathbf{J}=0
.
Just as in ordinary QFTs, the action of this flavor symmetry on a given local operator can be implemented by integrating the current
\mathbf{J}
around it.
The
\infty
-dimensional enhancement is then realized by witnessing an enhancement of global flavor transformations to local, holomorphic flavor transformations, which are implemented by integrals of the current
\mathbf{J}
weighted by holomorphic functions of two variables.
The true novelty of working in complex dimension
d>1
is that a holomorphic function on
\mathbf{C}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
necessarily extends over
0
, a result due to Hartogs; this is in stark contrast with the case in
d=1
where the are genuinely meromorphic functions.
Instead, these singular “functions” appear in higher form degree: punctured affine space
\mathbf{C}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
has higher Dolbeault cohomology.
Correspondingly, there are yet more symmetries we can use that arise from integrals of
\mathbf{J}
weighted by a general holomorphic, i.e.
\bar{\partial}
-closed,
(0,\bullet)
-forms.
Thus, the
\infty
-dimensional symmetry algebras of four-dimensional holomorphic field theories have a much more homotopical flavor than those familiar to two dimensions.
A convenient way to describe a portion of this structure is through
\lambda
-brackets, which serve as generating functions for the higher modes of the currents and organize the algebra into an
L_{\infty}
analogue of a Lie conformal algebra
[
16
,
4

17
]
.

We focus on two of the most important examples of these symmetries in Section
3

In the case of a flavor symmetry, this resulting symmetry algebra is a higher-dimensional analogue of the Kac-Moody symmetry of a two-dimensional CFT first described in
[
8

[

Similarly, when the parent theory possesses an unbroken

In each of these cases we describe how to couple a theory with these symmetries to background fields; in the former case this simply amounts to introducing a background principal bundle with connection whereas in the latter such a background is a deformation of the complex structure of spacetime.
We can treat these backgrounds in exactly the same way as in ordinary QFT and, expanding on

Moreover, through a descent construction, we demonstrate explicitly how these central extensions are intimately tied to anomalies.

As an illustrative example, consider a
\mathrm{U}(1)

The cubic flavor anomaly leads to the relation

\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{J}=\bar{\partial}\mathbf{J}-\frac{\hbar k}{2\pi^{2}}\partial\mathbf{A}\partial\mathbf{A}\,,

(1.1)

where

This equation shows that the non-conservation of the current in the holomorphic twist corresponds to the failure of the superfield
\mathbf{J}

If the right-hand side involves only background fields, such as the background connection
\mathbf{A}

By contrast, if it involves dynamical gauge fields, denoted by

in the main text, the quantum theory suffers from a gauge or ABJ anomaly depending on whether

\mathbf{J}

In the more interesting case of an ABJ anomaly, one often simply says that the symmetry is broken, although there are ways to recover some portion of it using a non-invertible modification thereof

For simplicity we considered a

However, the same considerations apply equally to non-abelian flavor symmetries as well as to spacetime symmetries.
A careful treatment of these cases will be given in the main text.

1.2  Outlook

The holomorphic twist offers a natural categorification of the superconformal index: it upgrades the index from a protected numerical invariant to a cohomologically graded operator algebra.
This refined structure retains detailed algebraic and geometric information about the theory, providing access to the operator product expansion (OPE), symmetry algebra, and deformation patterns that are invisible to the index alone. In particular, the holomorphic twist yields a sensitive diagnostic for detecting (super)symmetry enhancement along the renormalization group (RG) flow, allowing one to probe subtle IR phenomena in a controlled, computable framework.

2  The Holomorphic Twist

Consider a four-dimensional Euclidean SQFT with
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetry. Such a theory preserves (at least) four supercharges, denoted
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{{\dot{\alpha}}}
, where
\alpha=\pm
and
{\dot{\alpha}}=\dot{\pm}
. These supercharges satisfy the following anti-commutation relations:
4
4

4

When
\mathcal{N}>1
the supersymmetry algebra can be centrally extended. We will not consider such central extensions in this work.

\left\{{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}},{\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{{\dot{\alpha}}}}\right\}=\,\mathcal{P}_{\alpha{\dot{\alpha}}}\,,\qquad\qquad\left\{{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}},{\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}}\right\}=\left\{{\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{{\dot{\alpha}}}},{\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{{\dot{\beta}}}}\right\}=0\,,

(2.1)

where
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha{\dot{\alpha}}}
are the translation generators. The supercharges are rotated by the R-symmetry
G_{R}
. For a detailed review of the four-dimensional superconformal algebra, we refer the reader to Appendix
B
.

For the purposes of this paper, performing a twist (minimal or otherwise) of the theory consists of the following steps:

(1)

Choose a nilpotent supercharge
\mathbf{Q}
in the supersymmetry algebra. The space of such supercharges is called the
nilpotence variety

[
31
]
, see also
[
32
]
. The nilpotence variety is acted upon by rotations, parity transformations, and
R
-symmetries and is stratified by the number of momenta that are homotopically trivial in the twist; a
minimal
twist is one where the fewest number of momenta are trivial.

(2)

Add
\mathbf{Q}
to the BV/BRST differential of the theory and treat the total differential
{\rm d}_{BRST}+\mathbf{Q}
as the BV/BRST differential of the twisted theory. The space of observables in the twisted theory is the cohomology with respect to this differential.

It is often advantageous to extend this procedure by two additional, albeit optional, steps. These steps provide the necessary data to, first, place the twisted theory on curved spacetimes endowed with appropriate tangential structures (such as a complex structure or, more generally, a transverse holomorphic foliation) and second, equip the theory with a cohomological grading.

(3)

Choose a homomorphism
\phi:G\rightarrow{\rm Spin}(d)\times G_{R}
under which the preferred supercharge
\mathbf{Q}
transforms trivially. This gives rise to a new action of the (reduced) Lorentz group on the fields of the twisted theory. Using this new action we have changed the spins of the fields as well as the supercharges. For the minimal twist of four-dimensional theories, such a twisting homomorphism is often taken to be of the form

\phi:{\rm MU}(2,\mathbf{C})\rightarrow{\rm Spin}(4)\times G_{R}\,,

(2.2)

where
{\rm MU(2,\mathbf{C})}
is the metaplectic unitary group, a double cover of
\mathrm{U}(2)
.

(4)

Choose a homomorphism
\alpha:\mathrm{U}(1)\to G_{R}
under which the preferred supercharge
\mathbf{Q}
transforms non-trivially. Such a choice allows us to define a cohomological grading by modifying ghost number by suitable
R
-charges so that
\mathbf{Q}
has a well-defined charge.

We note that we have not placed the requirement that the homomorphism
\alpha
gives
\mathbf{Q}
unit charge as this need not be possible.
A situation where this is the case is in theories with fractional
R
-charges; this is simply saying the true
\mathrm{U}(1)

\pm 1
.
We also note that it is possible to extend the allowed twisting homomorphisms and gradings by replacing
G_{R}
by its product with the flavor symmetries of the untwisted theory; this can often allow for less restrictive backgrounds, e.g. by foregoing or relaxing the need for spin structures, and can be used to correlate the Grassmann parity of observables with the cohomological grading
\!\!\mod 2
.

The four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetry algebra contains a unique nilpotent supercharge.

5

5

Without loss of generality, we choose

\bar{\theta}^{i}={\rm i}\,{\rm d}\bar{z}^{i}
and introduce the reduced superfield

\mathbf{O}\equiv\mathrm{e}^{-{\rm i}{\rm d}\bar{z}^{i}\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}}\mathbf{O}^{(0)}=\mathbf{O}^{(0)}+\mathbf{O}^{(1)}+\mathbf{O}^{(2)}\,,

(2.4)

obtained by setting
\theta^{\alpha}=0
in the conventional superfield. We then define a semi-chiral superfield as a reduced superfield that is annihilated by the supercovariant derivative
D_{-}
,

D_{-}\mathbf{O}=(\mathbf{Q}-\bar{\partial})\mathbf{O}=0\,,

(2.5)

where we introduced
\bar{\partial}={\rm d}\bar{z}^{i}\,\partial_{\bar{z}^{i}}
. Similarly, we define a semi-chiral field to be a field annihilated by
\mathbf{Q}
. Note that from the definitions above it follows that the bottom component of a semi-chiral superfield is a semi-chiral field, i.e.
\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{O}^{(0)}=0
, while its higher form components, often called descendants, satisfy the holomorphic descent equations,

\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{O}^{(k)}=\bar{\partial}\mathbf{O}^{(k-1)}\,.

(2.6)

Moreover, given a semi-chiral field
O
we can build a semi-chiral superfield
\mathbf{O}
with
O=\mathbf{O}^{(0)}
by applying
\mathrm{e}^{-{\rm i}{\rm d}\bar{z}^{i}\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}}
.
In other words, semi-chiral superfields are in one-to-one correspondence with
\mathbf{Q}
-closed local operators. Moreover, since BPS observables do not depend on
\mathbf{Q}
-exact deformations, the algebraic structure defined by descent is sensitive to the cohomology classes of semi-chiral operators.

2.2  Higher operations

As already noted, Hartogs’ lemma prohibits the appearance of singular terms in the OPE of
\mathbf{Q}
-closed local operators in a holomorphic theory in two complex dimensions: the correlation functions thereof are holomorphic away from the diagonals where insertion points are coincident and hence must extend over these diagonals. Nevertheless, there
can
be singularities in OPEs of operators that are not
\mathbf{Q}
-closed, e.g. the descendants appearing in a semi-chiral superfield. Holomorphic theories are equipped with a collection of
n
-ary secondary operations that allow us to access singular (physical) OPE data by probing simultaneous multi-operator configurations. These operations arise naturally from the derived structure of the space of local operators: instead of forming a strict associative algebra, local operators in the twisted theory organize into a homotopy algebra, typically a dg Lie algebra or more generally
L_{\infty}
-algebra, where higher brackets encode obstructions to strict associativity and Jacobi identities. Concretely, these secondary operations are defined by integrating suitable descendant operators over compact cycles in configuration space, thereby capturing the residual singularities associated with multi-point collisions. We now turn to a more explicit construction of these secondary products. For a detailed perturbative analysis of these brackets and their interrelations, see
[
17
]
.

Consider a patch of spacetime with semi-chiral operator
O_{1}
placed at a point
z^{i}
and a second
O_{2}
at the origin. Just as in the more familiar setting of complex dimension 1, we can extract the operators appearing in their OPE of these operators and their descendants via
S^{3}
integrals of the semi-chiral superfields
\mathbf{O}_{1}
around
\mathbf{O}_{2}
of the form

\oint_{S^{3}}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\rho(z)[\mathbf{O}_{1}(z)\mathbf{O}_{2}(0)]\,,

(2.7)

where the (binary) product of operators,
\left[\mathbf{O}_{1}(z)\mathbf{O}_{2}(0)\right]
, is understood as the OPE and
\rho(z)\in\Omega^{0,\bullet}(\mathbf{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\})
. This integral is again a semi-chiral superfield when
\rho
is holomorphic
\overline{\partial}\rho=0
and is
\mathbf{Q}
-exact if
\rho=\overline{\partial}\widetilde{\rho}
, i.e. the
\mathbf{Q}
-cohomology class of the resulting semi-chiral operator only depends on the class
[\rho]\in H^{0,\bullet}(\mathbf{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\})
.
The Dolbeault cohomology,
H^{0,\bullet}(\mathbf{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\})
, is concentrated in degrees zero and one and we define a basis of representatives as follows,

\rho_{m,n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\left(z^{1}\right)^{m}\left(z^{2}\right)^{n}&\qquad\in\Omega^{0,0}(\mathbf{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\})&m,n\geq 0\,,\\[5.69054pt]
\partial_{z^{1}}^{-m-1}\partial_{z^{2}}^{-n-1}\omega_{\rm BM}&\qquad\in\Omega^{0,1}(\mathbf{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\})&m,n<0\,,\\[5.69054pt]
0&&\text{otherwise}\,,\end{array}\right.

(2.8)

where the Bochner-Martinelli
\omega_{\rm BM}
is the Green’s function for
\bar{\partial}
on
\mathbf{C}^{2}
and can be written explicitly as

\omega_{\rm BM}=\frac{1}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\frac{\bar{z}^{1}{\rm d}\bar{z}^{2}-\bar{z}^{2}{\rm d}\bar{z}^{1}}{|z|^{4}}\in\Omega^{0,1}\left(\mathbf{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\}\right)\,.

(2.9)

This allows us to define an infinite collection of binary secondary brackets as

\left\{{\mathbf{O}_{1}},{\mathbf{O}_{2}}\right\}_{m,n}=\,\oint_{S^{3}}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\,\rho_{m,n}\,\left[\mathbf{O}_{1}(z)\mathbf{O}_{2}(0)\right]\,.

(2.10)

Hence we see that, analogous as in one(-complex)-dimensional VOAs, the positive modes encode the singular part of the OPE, while the negative modes capture the regularized products
\partial_{z^{1}}^{m}\partial_{z^{2}}^{n}\mathbf{O}_{1}(z)\mathbf{O}_{2}(0)
.

The binary
\lambda
-bracket is then defined as the generating function collecting all positive modes

7

7

The generating function of negative modes will be reviewed later in section
5.3
and appendix
C
.
,

\left\{{\mathbf{O}_{1}}\,{}_{\lambda}\,{\mathbf{O}_{2}}\right\}=\oint_{S^{3}}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda\cdot z}[\mathbf{O}_{1}(z)\mathbf{O}_{2}(0)]\,,

(2.11)

where
\lambda\cdot z=\lambda_{i}z^{i}

As such, the
\lambda
-bracket can be understood as the Fourier transform of the singular part of the OPE.

It is useful to consider the space
\mathbf{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\}
as a configuration space of two points with one point fixed at the origin,

\mathbf{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\}={\rm Conf}^{0}_{2}(\mathbf{C}^{2})\,.

(2.12)

With this in mind, we can rewrite the above
S^{3}
integral as an integral over this configuration space:

\displaystyle\left\{{\mathbf{O}_{1}},{\mathbf{O}_{2}}\right\}_{m,n}=

\displaystyle\oint_{S^{3}}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\,\rho_{m,n}\,\left[\mathbf{O}_{1}(z)\mathbf{O}_{2}(0)\right]

(2.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\rm Conf}^{0}_{2}}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\rho_{m,n}\bar{\partial}\left[\mathbf{O}_{1}(z)\mathbf{O}_{2}(0)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\rm Conf}^{0}_{2}}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\rho_{m,n}\mathbf{Q}\left[\mathbf{O}_{1}(z)\mathbf{O}_{2}(0)\right]\,.

The definitions above can then be formally generalized to arbitrary
(n+1)
-ary secondary brackets defined as
8
8

8

These brackets are defined at the level of cohomology, where
\rho
is
\bar{\partial}
-closed and the integration domain is a cycle. More generally, when working at the chain level, one can consider brackets defined by integrating non-closed forms over arbitrary chains (not necessarily cycles), which may yield homotopically meaningful but cohomologically trivial contributions.

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{O}_{1}\,,\,\mathbf{O}_{2}\,,\,\cdots\,,\,\mathbf{O}_{n+1}\right\}_{\Sigma,\rho}=\int_{\Sigma}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z_{k}}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\rho\,\mathbf{Q}\left[\mathbf{O}_{1}(z_{1})\mathbf{O}_{2}(z_{2})\cdots\mathbf{O}_{n+1}(0)\right]

(2.14)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z_{k}}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\rho\,\bar{\partial}\left[\mathbf{O}_{1}(z_{1})\mathbf{O}_{2}(z_{2})\cdots\mathbf{O}_{n+1}(0)\right]

(2.15)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Sigma}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z_{k}}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\rho\,\left[\mathbf{O}_{1}(z_{1})\mathbf{O}_{2}(z_{2})\cdots\mathbf{O}_{n+1}(0)\right]\,,

(2.16)

where
\Sigma
denotes a (possibly unbounded) chain in
{\rm Conf}^{0}_{n+1}(\mathbf{C}^{2})
, the configuration space of
n+1

\rho
represent a cohomology class
\rho\in H^{0,\bullet}({\rm Conf}^{0}_{n+1}(\mathbf{C}^{2}))
.
The product of operators
\left[\mathbf{O}_{1}\cdots\mathbf{O}_{n+1}\right]
appearing in this integral can be thought of as a
(k+1)
-ary generalization of the OPE, depending on the integration cycle in configuration space.
9
9

9

In the physical theory, the strict associativity of the OPE ensures that all multi-operator collision limits are fully determined by successive applications of the binary OPE; no genuinely new data arises from simultaneous multi-point collisions. In the holomorphic twist, however, the projection onto
\mathbf{Q}
-cohomology removes unprotected operators from the spectrum. This projection disrupts the strict associativity, resulting in an OPE that is associative only up to homotopy. Consequently, multi-point collision limits in the twisted theory cannot in general be reconstructed solely from the binary OPE data of protected operators. Instead, the higher brackets encode additional, cohomologically protected information about the multi-operator dynamics which is not accessible from the binary OPE alone, though it ultimately originates from the underlying physical OPE structure.

Although the Dolbeault cohomology of such configuration spaces currently lacks a concrete algebraic model or explicit basis of representatives, it is straightforward to see that the zeroth degree cohomology
H^{0,0}({\rm Conf}^{0}_{n+1}(\mathbf{C}^{2}))
is generated by monomials in
n

z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}}

Hence, generalizing the definition above, we define the
(n+1)

\left\{\mathbf{O}_{1}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,\mathbf{O}_{2}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\,\cdots\,{}_{\lambda_{n}}\,\mathbf{O}_{n+1}\right\}=\int_{{\rm Conf}^{0}_{n+1}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z_{k}}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_{k}\cdot z_{k}}\mathbf{Q}\left[\mathbf{O}_{1}(z_{1})\mathbf{O}_{2}(z_{2})\cdots\mathbf{O}_{n+1}(0)\right]\,,

(2.17)

The (
n>2
)-ary brackets introduced above should be understood as formal expressions, since at the time of writing there is no fully satisfactory non-perturbative definition of the generalized OPEs that appear in these constructions. In perturbation theory we can be more precise. Define the free
\lambda
-brackets with a subscript
0

\left\{\mathbf{O}_{1}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,\cdots\mathbf{O}_{k}\,{}_{\lambda_{k}}\mathbf{O}_{k+1}\right\}_{0}=\sum_{\Gamma}\left(\int_{\mathbf{C}^{2n}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z_{k}}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_{k}\cdot z_{k}}W_{\Gamma}\left[\mathbf{Q}\big(\mathbf{O}_{1}(z_{1})\cdots\mathbf{O}_{n}(z_{n})\mathbf{O}_{n+1}(0)\big)\right]\right)\,,

(2.18)

where
W_{\Gamma}[\cdots]
corresponds to performing Wick contractions in the free theory according to the graph
\Gamma
and we sum over all 2-Laman graphs with
n+1
nodes
[
16
,
17
]
. The interacting bracket can then be recovered in perturbation theory by including higher-loop diagrams with additional insertions of the interactions
\mathcal{I}

\left\{\mathbf{O}_{1}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,\cdots\mathbf{O}_{n}\,{}_{\lambda_{n}}\mathbf{O}_{n+1}\right\}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{\hbar^{l}}{l!}\big\{\underbrace{\mathcal{I}\,{}_{0}\,\cdots\,\mathcal{I}\,{}_{0}\,}_{l\text{ times}}\mathbf{O}_{1}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,\cdots\mathbf{O}_{n}\,{}_{\lambda_{n}}\mathbf{O}_{n+1}\big\}_{0}\,,

(2.19)

where we added a formal loop counting parameter
\hbar
and the
l

\lambda

Similarly, until now we always considered the differential
\mathbf{Q}
abstractly as a fully quantum corrected object. However, in practice we are always forced to use the perturbative quantum corrected operator
\mathbf{Q}
which can be expressed as

\mathbf{Q}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{l}\mathbf{Q}_{l}\,,

(2.20)

where
\mathbf{Q}_{l}
is the
l
-loop supercharge whose action on a field is defined as the free
l
-loop
\lambda
-bracket,

\mathbf{Q}_{l}\cdot\mathbf{O}=\frac{1}{(l+1)!}\big\{\underbrace{\mathcal{I}\,{}_{0}\,\cdots\,\mathcal{I}\,{}_{0}\,}_{l+1\text{ times}}\mathbf{O}\big\}_{0}\,.

(2.21)

3  Symmetries and Anomalies

So far, the brackets introduced above are just a set of formal gadgets allowing us to extract certain pieces of (physical) OPE data. In what follows, we will revisit the interpretation of these brackets as generating symmetries of holomorphic field theories and show how the ternary brackets encode the associated anomalies.

3.1  Infinite-dimensional symmetry enhancement

Four-dimensional holomorphic field theories, such as those arising from the minimal twist of
\mathcal{N}\geq 1
SQFTs, exhibit a rich variety of infinite-dimensional symmetry algebras, analogous in spirit to those familiar from two-dimensional (chiral) conformal field theory.
However, unlike the two-dimensional case, it is generally insufficient to restrict attention to Lie algebras in the four-dimensional holomorphic setting.
As we explain below, the natural higher-dimensional analogues of the Virasoro and affine current algebras are most appropriately described by
L_{\infty}
-algebras; see, for instance,
[
8
,
7
,
9
,
12
]
.
This is in large part due to the fact that configuration spaces of points in
\mathbf{C}^{n}
are not affine for
n>1
and, consequently, the space of holomorphic functions on these spaces (where correlation functions naturally take values) exhibits non-trivial higher Dolbeault cohomology.

3.1.1
Spacetime symmetry

The choice of a twisting supercharge
\mathbf{Q}
breaks the
\mathrm{SO}(4)
Lorentz symmetry to
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{+}
.
Together with the holomorphic translations, these generate the group of global holomorphic symplectomorphisms preserving the holomorphic symplectic form

\omega={\rm d}z^{1}\wedge{\rm d}z^{2}\,.

(3.1)

Infinitesimally, these symmetries are geometric: they are realized as holomorphic vector fields on
\mathbf{C}^{2}
.
More concretely, the
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{+}
factor is generated by the traceless holomorphic rotations
z_{i}\partial_{j}
.
In the presence of an additional unbroken
\mathrm{U}(1)
R-symmetry, the diagonal combination

\mathop{\rm diag}\nolimits(\mathrm{U}(1)_{R}\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{-})\subset\mathrm{U}(1)_{R}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{-}

(3.2)

is preserved. This enlarges the
\mathrm{SU}(2)
above to the full
\mathrm{U}(2)
of holomorphic rotations.
The extra
\mathrm{U}(1)
is generated by the trace

E=z^{i}\partial_{i}\,,

(3.3)

often referred to as the holomorphic Euler vector field.

10

10

This “dilatation” generator counts the total holomorphic degree.

If the theory preserves
\mathcal{N}=1

\mathrm{SU}(3)\subset\mathrm{SU}(4|1)
.
The corresponding symmetry algebra is generated by the holomorphic translations
\partial_{i}

z_{i}\partial_{j}

11
11

11

In the twisted theory, the finite-dimensional spacetime symmetry algebras are enhanced to infinite-dimensional ones.
To see this, let us start by introducing the relevant infinite-dimensional algebras and subsequently show how they arise in holomorphically twisted theories. Consider the sheaf
T^{1,0}

\mathbf{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\}

Taking the derived global sections gives a natural differential graded (dg) Lie algebra, which we refer to as the 2-Witt algebra,

(3.4)

[V_{1},V_{2}]=\mathcal{L}_{V_{1}}V_{2}\,.

(3.5)

\mathfrak{svect}(2)\subset\mathfrak{witt}(2)\,,

(3.6)

consisting of vector fields

Consider now a unitary

The most general supercurrent multiplet is the

12

The

(3.7)

\mathbf{S}_{i}=D^{\alpha}\mathcal{J}_{\alpha i}|_{\theta^{\alpha}=0}

Analogous to the two-dimensional case, the modes of the holomorphic stress tensor generate the infinite-dimensional symmetry algebra. Concretely, the action of a vector field

\displaystyle(-V\cdot\mathbf{O})(0)

\displaystyle=\oint_{S^{3}}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}V^{i}(z)\mathbf{S}_{i}(z)\,\mathbf{O}(0)\,.

(3.8)

When

\bar{\partial}

\displaystyle(-V\cdot\mathbf{O})(0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n,i}a^{mni}\left\{\mathbf{S}_{i},\mathbf{O}\right\}_{m,n}(0)\,,

3.3.1
Anomalies from Feynman diagrams

The existence of a quantum anomaly for a classical

\mathcal{L}

({\rm d}_{\rm CE}+\mathbf{Q})I^{\mathcal{L}}+\frac{1}{2}\{I^{\mathcal{L}},I^{\mathcal{L}}\}_{\rm BV}=0\,,

A local functional

In this case, the

There are two distinct ways in which the failure to solve (

1.

Failure up to background-field terms: It may be impossible to solve (
3.47

2.

Failure even modulo background-field terms: Here, an obstruction remains even after ignoring terms depending only on the background fields. This indicates that the classical
\mathcal{L}
symmetry is explicitly broken at the quantum level. A standard example is the axial symmetry in gauge theories, which does not persist quantum mechanically due to the ABJ anomaly.

As in ordinary four-dimensional QFTs, 1-loop anomalies of continuous symmetries in four-dimensional holomorphic quantum field theories can be detected by the failure of triangle diagrams to be invariant (cf. Section 4 of
[

For example, consider the coupling to a background

(3.48)

This coupling thus defines an inner quantum
\mathcal{L}
background if

and otherwise is simply a quantum
\mathcal{L}
background.
Note that the right-hand side is precisely is precisely the gauge variation of the quantum effective action.

3.3.3
Anomalies from descent

As in more familiar QFTs, anomalies of holomorphic field theories can be described via descent from holomorphic Chern classes defined two (real) dimensions higher.
For simplicity, we consider the trivial holomorphic
F
bundle on
\mathbf{C}^{2}
, with a partial connection
\overline{\partial}+A
satisfying
(\overline{\partial}+A)^{2}=0
, which can be interpreted as a deformation of the bundle’s complex structure.
The full connection on the gauge bundle is then
\nabla=\partial+(\overline{\partial}+A)
, so its curvature is simply

F=\partial A\,.

(3.57)

The characteristic class
c_{3}=\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits F^{3}
is gauge-invariant because
{\rm d}_{\rm CE}F=[c,F]
and is locally exact, with Chern-Simons primitive,

c_{3}=\partial\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits A\partial A\partial A={\rm d}\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A\partial A\partial A)={\rm d}\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(AF^{2})\,.

(3.58)

The Chern-Simons form itself is not gauge-invariant, but its variation is exact due to the gauge-invariance of
c_{3}
:

{\rm d}_{\rm CE}\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A\partial A\partial A)={\rm d}\bigg(\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(c\partial A\partial A)\bigg)\,.

(3.59)

As in the standard case, the gauge-variation of the quantum effective action computed above is precisely the integral of this primitive.
Thus, the anomalous behavior of symmetries in four-dimensional holomorphic field theories can be captured by degree-3 characteristic classes, just as in ordinary four-dimensional QFTs.

Given a four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=1
SQFT, the anomaly polynomial of its holomorphic twist is obtained as a specialization of the untwisted theory’s anomaly polynomial: The characteristic classes corresponding to flavor symmetries are unchanged under the twist.
For the gravitational and R-symmetry anomalies more care is needed because the twist mixes spacetime and R-symmetries. In particular, the gravitational anomalies of the holomorphic theory are linear combinations of the original gravitational anomalies and mixed anomalies involving

\mathrm{U}(1)_{R}
.

Following Section 4.1 of
[
13
]
, the specialization proceeds as follows: First, break
\mathrm{Spin}(4)
characteristic classes to
{\rm MU}(2)
classes by sending

p_{1}\mapsto 2{\rm ch}_{2}=c_{1}^{2}-2c_{2}\,,

(3.60)

reflecting the breaking of most of the Lorentz group by the choice of complex structure. Then, specialize the R-symmetry class according to

c_{1,r}=-\frac{1}{2}c_{1}\,,

(3.61)

where
c_{1,r}
is the first Chern class of the background R-symmetry connection.
This accounts for the twisting homomorphism.

is the dual Coxeter number of the gauge algebra and
T(R_{i})
the Dynkin index of the representation
R_{i}
of the
i
th hypermultiplet. If this condition is not satisfied an ABJ anomaly will arise.

This breaking manifests as an ABJ anomaly for the symmetry generated by
\mathbf{R}
.
Correspondingly,
\mathbf{R}
fails to be semi-chiral and we find

\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{R}=\bar{\partial}\mathbf{R}+\frac{k}{2\pi^{2}}\mathbf{N}

(4.14)

for some operator
\mathbf{N}
and an anomaly coefficient
k
, cf. Section
3.3
.
The would-be superconformal stress tensor
\mathbf{S}_{i}
and the supercurrent
\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}
also fail to be semi-chiral:

\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{S}_{i}=\bar{\partial}\mathbf{S}_{i}+\frac{k}{6\pi^{2}}\partial_{i}\mathbf{N}\,,\qquad\mathbf{Q}\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}=\bar{\partial}\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}+\frac{k}{2\pi^{2}}\epsilon^{ij}\partial_{i}\mathbf{L}_{j}\,.

(4.15)

We see that
\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{i}=\mathbf{S}_{i}-\frac{1}{3}\partial_{i}\mathbf{R}
is semi-chiral and, moreover, generates an action of
\mathfrak{witt}(2)
with vanishing central charges; the twisted spin generated by
\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{i}
is obtained by a twisting homomorphism with the Cartan subalgebra of
{\rm SU}(2)_{R}
. From this point of view it is clear that no central charges can appear, since these are proportional to either
\mathrm{Tr}\,(T^{a})
or
d_{abc}
, both of which vanish for
\mathrm{SU}(2)
.

There is one additional operator
\mathbf{M}
appearing in the multiplet with
\mathbf{N}
and
\mathbf{L}_{i}
.
The action of twisted
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal symmetry on these operators takes the following form: the action on
\mathbf{N}
is given by

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\left\{{\mathbf{S}_{i}}\,{}_{\lambda}\,{\mathbf{N}}\right\}=\partial_{i}\mathbf{N}+\lambda_{i}\mathbf{N}\,,

(4.16)

\displaystyle\left\{{\mathbf{G}_{i}}\,{}_{\lambda}\,{\mathbf{N}}\right\}

\displaystyle=0\,,

\displaystyle\left\{{\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}}\,{}_{\lambda}\,{\mathbf{N}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{ij}(\partial_{i}\mathbf{L}_{j}+\lambda_{i}\mathbf{L}_{j})\,,

\displaystyle\left\{{\mathbf{R}}\,{}_{\lambda}\,{\mathbf{N}}\right\}=0\,,

the action on
\mathbf{M}
is given by

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\left\{{\mathbf{S}_{i}}\,{}_{\lambda}\,{\mathbf{M}}\right\}=\partial_{i}\mathbf{M}+\frac{2}{3}\lambda_{i}\mathbf{M}\,,

(4.17)

\displaystyle\left\{{\mathbf{G}_{i}}\,{}_{\lambda}\,{\mathbf{M}}\right\}

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{L}_{i}\,,

\displaystyle\left\{{\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}}\,{}_{\lambda}\,{\mathbf{M}}\right\}

\displaystyle=0\,,

\displaystyle\left\{{\mathbf{R}}\,{}_{\lambda}\,{\mathbf{M}}\right\}=-2\mathbf{M}\,,

and, finally, the action on
\mathbf{L}_{i}
is given by

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\left\{{\mathbf{S}_{i}}\,{}_{\lambda}\,{\mathbf{L}_{j}}\right\}=\partial_{i}\mathbf{L}_{j}+\frac{1}{3}\lambda_{i}\mathbf{L}_{j}+\lambda_{j}\mathbf{L}_{i}\,,

(4.18)

\displaystyle\left\{{\mathbf{G}_{i}}\,{}_{\lambda}\,{\mathbf{L}_{j}}\right\}

\displaystyle=-\epsilon_{ij}\mathbf{N}\,,

\displaystyle\left\{{\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}}\,{}_{\lambda}\,{\mathbf{L}_{j}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\partial_{i}\mathbf{M}+2\lambda_{i}\mathbf{M}\,,

\displaystyle\left\{{\mathbf{R}}\,{}_{\lambda}\,{\mathbf{L}_{i}}\right\}=-\mathbf{L}_{i}\,.

We find that the binary and ternary brackets of elements of this multiplet necessarily vanish, as do the mixed ternary brackets with the superconformal symmetry generators.

4.1.2
Flavor symmetries

Whenever the theory enjoys an additional global symmetry the spectrum contains a conserved current.
Suppose the untwisted theory has a global symmetry by the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{f}
.
We assume this Lie algebra is either simple or abelian.
The conserved current multiplet, after twisting, gives rise to a semi-chiral superfield
\mathbf{J}
whose modes generate a 2-Kac-Moody algebra.
Analogous to the discussion above, in the presence of extended superconformal symmetry, this multiplet splits in a collection of
\mathcal{N}=1
multiplets.
Concretely, an
\mathcal{N}=2
conserved current multiplet decomposes into three
\mathcal{N}=1
multiplets,

B_{1}\overline{B}_{1}[0,0]^{(2;0)}_{2}=\underbrace{\rule[-5.16663pt]{0.0pt}{0.0pt}{A}_{2}\overline{A}_{2}[1,1]^{(0)}_{3}}_{\mathbf{J}}\oplus\underbrace{\rule[-5.16663pt]{0.0pt}{0.0pt}{L}\overline{B}_{1}[0,0]^{(4/3)}_{2}}_{\bm{\mu}}\oplus\underbrace{\rule[-5.16663pt]{0.0pt}{0.0pt}{B}_{1}\overline{L}[0,0]^{(-4/3)}_{2}}_{\emptyset}\,.

(4.19)

where
\bm{\mu}
is the
\mathcal{N}=2
moment map operator and the third multiplet does not contribute a semi-chiral superfield since its anti-chiral half does not satisfy a shortening condition. The
\lambda
-brackets involving these fields are given by

\displaystyle\left\{{\mathbf{J}_{a}}\,{}_{\lambda}\,{\mathbf{J}_{b}}\right\}=

\displaystyle\,f_{ab}^{c}\mathbf{J}_{c}\,,

\displaystyle\left\{{\mathbf{J}_{a}}\,{}_{\lambda}\,{\bm{\mu}_{b}}\right\}=

\displaystyle f_{ab}^{c}\,\bm{\mu}_{c}

(4.20)

\displaystyle\left\{{\mathbf{S}_{i}}\,{}_{\lambda}\,{\mathbf{J}_{a}}\right\}=

\displaystyle\,\partial_{i}\mathbf{J}_{a}+\lambda_{i}\mathbf{J}_{a}\,,

\displaystyle\left\{{\mathbf{S}_{i}}\,{}_{\lambda}\,{\bm{\mu}_{a}}\right\}=

\displaystyle\,\partial_{i}\bm{\mu}_{a}+\frac{2}{3}\lambda_{i}\bm{\mu}_{a}\,,

(4.21)

\displaystyle\left\{{\mathbf{R}}\,{}_{\lambda}\,{\mathbf{J}_{a}}\right\}=

\displaystyle\,0\,,

\displaystyle\left\{{\mathbf{R}}\,{}_{\lambda}\,{\bm{\mu}_{a}}\right\}=

\displaystyle\,{\bm{\mu}}_{a}\,,

(4.22)

\displaystyle\left\{{\mathbf{G}_{i}}\,{}_{\lambda}\,{\mathbf{J}_{a}}\right\}=

\displaystyle\,-(\partial_{i}\bm{\mu}_{a}+\lambda_{i}\bm{\mu}_{a})\,,

\displaystyle\left\{{\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}}\,{}_{\lambda}\,{\bm{\mu}_{a}}\right\}=

\displaystyle\,\mathbf{J}_{a}\,.

(4.23)

Invariantly, the symmetries generated by the currents
\mathbf{J},\bm{\mu}
can be thought of as the dg Lie superalgebra of derived global holomorphic sections of the sheaf

\mathcal{O}\otimes\mathfrak{f}_{\mathbf{J}}\oplus\Pi K^{1/3}\otimes\mathfrak{f}_{\bm{\mu}}

(4.24)

over
\mathbf{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\}
.
Equivalently, it can be viewed as the dg Lie superalgebra of
\mathfrak{f}
-valued derived global holomorphic function on the supermanifold
{\rm Tot}(\Pi K^{1/3}\to\mathbf{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\})
.

In addition, the 3-brackets involving the conserved currents encode the flavor ’t Hooft anomalies
k_{FFF}
and levels
k_{F}
of the physical theory. The 3-brackets involving only flavor currents are just those given in Eq. (
3.28
).
The non-vanishing 3-brackets involving two operators in the flavor symmetry multiplet and one in the stress tensor multiplet are

\displaystyle\left\{{\mathbf{J}_{a}}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,{\mathbf{J}_{b}}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\,{\mathbf{S}_{i}}\right\}=-\frac{k_{F}}{6\pi^{2}}\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits_{F}(T_{a}T_{b})[\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}](\lambda_{1i}+\lambda_{2i})\,,

(4.25)

\displaystyle\left\{{\mathbf{J}_{a}}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,{\mathbf{J}_{b}}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\,{\mathbf{R}}\right\}=-\frac{k_{F}}{2\pi^{2}}\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits_{F}(T_{a}T_{b})[\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}]\,,

(4.26)

\displaystyle\left\{{\bm{\mu}_{a}}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,{\mathbf{J}_{b}}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\,{\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}}\right\}=\frac{k_{F}}{2\pi^{2}}\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits_{F}(T_{a}T_{b})[\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}]\,,

(4.27)

where the first equation is simply Eq. (
3.33
) with
k_{RFF}
replaced by
k_{F}
.
The 3-brackets with 1 flavor and two superconformal components all vanish. Note that this vanishing only happens when choosing the superconformal twist, this is a consequence of
a
-maximization.

Example: Lagrangian

4.1.4
Non-example: Free chiral

We note that the presence of the twisted
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetry algebra is not sufficient to declare an enhancement of supersymmetry.
To see that this is the case, we note that a single
\mathcal{N}=1
chiral possesses this symmetry; this is a 4d analogue of the 3d example described in e.g. Section 2.4.4 of
[
60
]
.
The corresponding currents are given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{i}=\tfrac{1}{3}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\partial_{i}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}-\tfrac{2}{3}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\partial_{i}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\,,

(4.35)

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{i}

\displaystyle=\,-\tfrac{1}{2}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\partial_{i}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\,,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}

\displaystyle=\,\tfrac{1}{2}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}=\,-\tfrac{1}{2}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\,.

This twisted
\mathcal{N}=2
structure does not arise from the fact that a single
\mathcal{N}=1
chiral can be viewed as a half-hypermultiplet. For instance, the
\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}
operator originates from a scalar in the physical theory, not from a spinor.
A clear diagnostic that this structure does not result from twisting a genuine
\mathcal{N}=2
SCFT is that
\mathbf{S}_{i}
is
not
the superconformal stress tensor: while it extremizes
a
, it does not maximize it.
In fact, the
\mathbf{S}_{i}
above follow from assigning
R
-charge
r=\frac{4}{3}
to
\gamma

Although this twisted
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal structure is not inherited from a true
\mathcal{N}=2
SCFT, it does admit a natural interpretation in the physical theory. Deforming the twisted theory by
\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}
corresponds to giving a mass to the chiral multiplet, rendering the physical theory gapped. Since the IR limit of such a theory is topological, its holomorphic twist is likewise topological. The operators
\mathbf{G}_{i}
are precisely the homotopies for holomorphic translations generated by
\mathbf{S}_{i}-\frac{1}{3}\partial_{i}\mathbf{R}
.

5  Superconformal Deformations

With extended superconformal symmetry the range of possible twists expands significantly. Beyond the familiar supersymmetry (Poincaré) twists classified in

In our framework, they arise uniformly by turning on a background coupling for the odd elements of the twisted superconformal symmetry algebra (as described in Section

More generally, one can twist by any nilpotent element of the relevant superconformal algebra; many of those are given by a deformation of the holomorphic twist and can therefore be accessed in the same way as described above.
Through these deformations, algebraic properties of local operators in these more supersymmetric twists can often be deduced from those in the holomorphic twist, as illustrated for the

5.1  Example: hypermultiplet

In more intricate theories such a direct calculation is no longer feasible, and one must instead rely on more abstract methods as discussed below.

, and their holomorphic derivatives. On the next page, we compute the cohomology with respect to the remaining part of the differential, denoted

Q_{\rm SC}\gamma^{m}=-z^{2}\beta_{n}\Omega^{nm}\,,\qquad Q_{\rm SC}\beta_{m}=0\,.

Q_{\rm SC}\penalty\ \partial_{2}\gamma^{m}(z^{1},0)=-\beta_{n}(z^{1},0)\Omega^{nm}\,.

5.2  Vertex algebras from four-dimensional descent

The explicit computation above illustrates how the superconformal deformation of the holomorphic twist localizes the four-dimensional theory to the plane
z^{2}=0
, yielding a two-dimensional holomorphic field theory. The local operators of this superconformal deformation naturally assemble into a vertex operator algebra (VOA).
In a VOA, the operator product expansion (OPE) of two local operators
\mathcal{O}_{1}(z)
and
\mathcal{O}_{2}(0)
can be extracted by integrating
\mathcal{O}_{1}(z)
along a contour linking
\mathcal{O}_{2}(0)
, weighted by an appropriate holomorphic function.
The singular part of the OPE is encoded in the two-dimensional
\lambda
-bracket:

\{\mathcal{O}_{1}{}_{\lambda}\mathcal{O}_{2}\}=\oint_{S^{1}}\frac{{\rm d}z}{2\pi{\rm i}}e^{\lambda z}[\mathcal{O}_{1}(z)\mathcal{O}_{2}(0)]\,,

(5.8)

where as in the four-dimensional case,
[\mathcal{O}_{1}(z)\mathcal{O}_{2}(0)]
denotes the OPE of two local operators. The regular part of the OPE is captured by the normal-ordered product

:\!\mathcal{O}_{1}\mathcal{O}_{2}\!:=\oint_{S^{1}}\frac{{\rm d}z}{2\pi{\rm i}}z^{-1}[\mathcal{O}_{1}(z)\mathcal{O}_{2}(0)]\,,

(5.9)

together with normal-ordered products involving derivatives of
\mathcal{O}_{1}
that account for the remaining regular terms.
For the hypermultiplet, this can be demonstrated explicitly by constructing the spectral sequence associated with the superconformal deformation and evaluating the relevant Feynman diagrams.

In more general settings, particularly when the (extended) superconformal symmetry is not realized classically or in computing the OPEs of composite operators, such analytic control is typically unavailable. In what follows we outline a more systematic approach. In this subsection we demonstrate that the two-dimensional
\lambda
-bracket and normal-ordered products admit equivalent, or more precisely cohomologous, realizations as three-dimensional contour integrals extending into the full four-dimensional space. We then explain how these
S^{3}
integrals can be directly related to the four-dimensional
\lambda
-brackets in the next subsection, thereby providing a concrete prescription for extracting VOA
\lambda
-brackets from holomorphically twisted data.

We begin by noting that the twisted
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal algebra allows us extend ordinary reduced superfields.
Given a reduced superfield
\mathbf{O}
, we define its extension

\mathbb{O}=\mathbf{O}+{\rm d}z^{2}\mathbf{\Psi}_{\mathbf{O}}\,,\qquad\mathbf{\Psi}_{\mathbf{O}}=\{\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}{}_{(0,0)}\mathbf{O}\}=\{\widetilde{\mathbf{G}},\mathbf{O}\}_{0,0}\,.

(5.10)

The extension
\mathbb{O}
should be thought of as extending
\mathbf{O}
to a reduced superfield on
\mathbf{C}^{2|1}
and then identifying the fermionic coordinate
\theta
with the holomorphic
1
-form
{\rm d}z^{2}
.
If instead of the superconformal deformation we were to deform the holomorphic twist to the holomorphic–topological (Kapustin) twist, i.e. by turning on the interaction
\int\mathbf{G}_{2}
, then the components of
\mathbb{O}
could be identified with the holomorphic–topological descendants of
\mathbf{O}^{(0)}
; as this is the Hamiltonian for
\theta\partial_{2}\sim{\rm d}z^{2}\partial_{2}
, can be thought of a deforming the Dolbeault differential to the THF differential
{\rm d}^{\prime}=\bar{\partial}_{\mathbf{C}^{2}}+{\rm d}z^{2}\partial_{2}=\bar{\partial}_{\mathbf{C}}+{\rm d}_{\mathbf{R}^{2}}
.

For the superconformal deformation, we instead deform by the interaction
\int z^{2}\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}
, which is the Hamiltonian for
z^{2}\partial_{\theta}\sim z^{2}\iota_{\partial_{2}}
and so the Dolbeault differential gets replaced by
\bar{\partial}+\iota_{z^{2}\partial_{2}}
.
The analogue of a semi-chiral superfield in the superconformal deformation then an extended superfield
\mathbb{O}
that satisfies

\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{O}=(\bar{\partial}+\iota_{z^{2}\partial_{2}}\big)\mathbb{O}

(5.11)

If
\mathbb{O}
satisfies this condition then its zero-form component defines a local operator in the
\mathbb{Q}
cohomology, provided it is placed somewhere on the
z^{2}=0
plane.
In terms of the component (reduced) superfields this condition reads

\mathbb{Q}\mathbf{O}=\bar{\partial}\mathbf{O}+z^{2}\mathbf{\Psi}_{\mathbf{O}}=0\,,\qquad\mathbb{Q}\mathbf{\Psi}_{\mathbf{O}}=\bar{\partial}\mathbf{\Psi}_{\mathbf{O}}\,.

(5.12)

We refer to any extended superfield
\mathbb{O}
or, equivaletly, a pair of reduced superfields
(\mathbf{O},\mathbf{\Psi}_{\mathbf{O}})
obeying this condition as a
Schur superfield
.
If
\mathbb{O}
is Schur, then its derivative
\partial_{1}\mathbb{O}
is also Schur but its derivative
\partial_{2}\mathbb{O}
is not.

Having defined Schur superfields, we now introduce their
\lambda
-bracket and normal-ordered product.
Explicitly, for Schur superfields
\mathbb{O}_{1}
and
\mathbb{O}_{2}
, the
\lambda
-bracket is given by

\displaystyle\{\!\{\mathbb{O}_{1}{}_{\lambda}\mathbb{O}_{2}\}\!\}(w^{1},w^{2})

\displaystyle=\oint_{S^{3}}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\penalty\ e^{\lambda\cdot(z-w)}\iota_{\partial_{2}}\mathbb{O}_{1}(z^{1},z^{2})\mathbb{O}_{2}(w^{1},w^{2})

(5.13)

\displaystyle=\oint_{S^{3}}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\penalty\ e^{\lambda\cdot(z-w)}\mathbf{\Psi}_{\mathbf{O}_{1}}(z^{1},z^{2})\mathbf{O}_{2}(w^{1},w^{2})+{\rm d}w^{2}(\dots)\,,

where we have suppressed the terms proportional to
{\rm d}w^{2}
in the second line.
The
\mathbb{Q}
-closedness of this operator follows directly from the fact that
\mathbb{O}_{1}
and
\mathbb{O}_{2}
are Schur superfields.

To demonstrate how this expression relates to the usual two-dimensional
\lambda
-bracket, as defined above in (
5.8
), we construct an explicit homotopy via descent.
As a first attempt, consider the 2-chain

D=\left\{(z^{1},z^{2})\in\mathbf{C}^{2}\bigg|\begin{aligned} |z^{1}|^{2}+|z^{2}|^{2}=1\\
{\rm Im}\,z^{2}=0,\penalty\ {\rm Re}\,z^{2}\leq 0\end{aligned}\right\}\,.

(5.14)

This chain enjoys two important properties: 1) its boundary is the equatorial
S^{1}\subset S^{3}
preserved by rotations in the
z^{2}
plane and 2) under the action of these rotations, its orbit fills the entire
S^{3}
.
Using the second property, we may rewrite the
S^{3}
integral (centered at
w^{1}=w^{2}=0
) as one over
D
:

\oint_{S^{3}}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}e^{\lambda\cdot z}\mathbf{\Psi}_{\mathbf{O}_{1}}=\int_{D}\iota_{z^{2}\partial_{2}}\bigg(\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}e^{\lambda\cdot z}\mathbf{\Psi}_{\mathbf{O}_{1}}\bigg)=-\int_{D}\frac{{\rm d}z^{1}}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}e^{\lambda z^{1}}z^{2}\mathbf{\Psi}_{\mathbf{O}_{1}}

(5.15)

Since
\mathbb{O}_{1}
is a Schur superfield, we can further simplify:

-\int_{D}\frac{{\rm d}z^{1}}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}e^{\lambda\cdot z}\big(\mathbb{Q}-\bar{\partial}\big)\mathbf{O}_{1}=\mathbb{Q}\bigg(\int_{D}\frac{{\rm d}z^{1}}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}e^{\lambda\cdot z}\mathbf{O}_{1}\bigg)-\int_{D}\bar{\partial}\bigg(\frac{{\rm d}z^{1}}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}e^{\lambda\cdot z}\mathbf{O}_{1}\bigg)\,.

(5.16)

We would be done if the last term were a total derivative, but unfortunately it is not.
To fix this, we introduce a corrected homotopy.

Define the 3-chain

\Sigma=\left\{(z^{1},z^{2})\in\mathbf{C}^{2}\bigg|\begin{aligned} |z^{1}|^{2}&+|z^{2}|^{2}\geq 1\\
{\rm Im}z^{2}=0&\text{ and }{\rm Re}z^{2}\leq 0\end{aligned}\right\}\,.

(5.17)

Its boundary consists of the 2-chain
D
(with reversed orientation) together with the locus
z^{2}=0
,
|z^{1}|^{2}\geq 1
.
The orbit of
\Sigma
under
z^{2}
-rotations is the region
(z^{1},z^{2})\in\mathbf{C}^{2}
with
|z^{1}|^{2}+|z^{2}|^{2}\geq 1
.

With these properties, we compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}\left[\int_{\Sigma}\partial\bigg(\frac{{\rm d}z^{1}}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}e^{\lambda\cdot z}\mathbf{O}_{1}\bigg)\right]

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Sigma}\partial\bigg(\frac{{\rm d}z^{1}}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}e^{\lambda\cdot z}\big(\bar{\partial}\mathbf{O}+z^{2}\mathbf{\Psi}_{\mathbf{O}_{1}}\big)\bigg)

(5.18)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\partial\Sigma}\partial\bigg(\frac{{\rm d}z^{1}}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}e^{\lambda\cdot z}\mathbf{O}_{1}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma}\mathcal{L}_{z^{2}\partial_{2}}\bigg(\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}e^{\lambda\cdot z}\mathbf{\Psi}_{\mathbf{O}_{1}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\partial\bigg(\frac{{\rm d}z^{1}}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}e^{\lambda\cdot z}\mathbf{O}_{1}\bigg)-2\oint_{S^{3}}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}e^{\lambda\cdot z}\mathbf{\Psi}_{\mathbf{O}_{1}}

The second equality follows from the Cartan homotopy formula and Stokes’ theorem; the third from the fact that the integrals over
\partial\Sigma
only receive contributions from
D
as both
{\rm d}^{2}z
and
z^{2}\partial_{2}
vanish on the
z^{2}=0
plane.
Putting these together, we arrive at the desired result:

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}

\displaystyle\left[\int_{D}\frac{{\rm d}z^{1}}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}e^{\lambda\cdot z}\mathbf{O}_{1}+\int_{\Sigma}\partial\bigg(\frac{{\rm d}z^{1}}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}e^{\lambda\cdot z}\mathbf{O}_{1}\bigg)\right]

(5.19)

\displaystyle=\oint_{S^{1}}\frac{{\rm d}z^{1}}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}e^{\lambda_{1}z^{1}}\mathbf{O}_{1}-\oint_{S^{3}}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}e^{\lambda\cdot z}\mathbf{\Psi}_{\mathbf{O}_{1}}

We note that the
\lambda_{2}
dependence of the secondary product is totally absent in its formulation as an
S^{1}
-integral, i.e. this dependence is
\mathbb{Q}
-exact; see Appendix
C
for a direct proof of this
\mathbb{Q}
-exactness.
We will set
\lambda_{2}=0
and, by an abuse of notation,
\lambda_{1}=\lambda
in the following.

The construction of the four-dimensional analogue of the normal-ordered product (
5.9
) is more subtle, since
(z^{1})^{-1}
appearing in the two-dimensional normal-ordered product is not defined on
\mathbf{C}^{2}\setminus{0}
.
Instead, we define the following:

\displaystyle\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\tiny$\circ$}\hskip-3.85pt\raisebox{3.5pt}{\tiny$\circ$}\hskip 1.0pt{\mathbb{O}_{1}\mathbb{O}_{2}}\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\tiny$\circ$}\hskip-3.85pt\raisebox{3.5pt}{\tiny$\circ$}\hskip 1.0pt(0,0)

\displaystyle=\oint_{S^{3}}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}(\omega_{\rm BM}-\zeta\iota_{\partial_{2}})\mathbb{O}_{1}(z^{1},z^{2})\mathbb{O}_{2}(0,0)

(5.20)

\displaystyle=\oint_{S^{3}}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\bigg(\omega_{\rm BM}\mathbf{O}_{1}(z^{1},z^{2})-\zeta\mathbf{\Psi}_{\mathbf{O}_{1}}(z^{1},z^{2})\bigg)\mathbf{O}_{2}(0,0)

where
\zeta
is the function on
\mathbf{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\}
defined by

\zeta={\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}}\frac{\bar{z}^{1}}{|z^{1}|^{2}+|z^{2}|^{2}}\,.

(5.21)

That this operator is
\mathbb{Q}
-closed follows immediately from the fact that both
\mathbb{O}_{1}
and
\mathbb{O}_{2}
are Schur superfields, together with the identity
\bar{\partial}\zeta=z^{2}\,\omega_{\rm BM}
.
Repeating the homotopy analysis from above, one can relate this four-dimensional normal-ordered product to the standard two-dimensional definition.
The essential observation is that the restriction of
\zeta
to the equatorial
S^{1}
at
z^{2}=0
reduces precisely to
(z^{1})^{-1}
.
Similarly, more general regularized products involving derivatives of
\mathbb{O}_{1}
can be defined in the same way.

5.3  Two-dimensional
\lambda
-brackets from the holomorphic twist

Having defined the two-dimensional
\lambda
-bracket and normal-ordered product in terms of
S^{3}
integrals, the next step is to relate these constructions to data in the holomorphic twist. This connection provides a direct way to extract VOA data from the holomorphic twist.

Indeed, we can relate the integrals corresponding to the two-dimensional
\lambda
-bracket introduced above to
\lambda
-brackets in the four-dimensional holomorphically twisted theory. Concretely, we identify
z^{2}\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}
as an additional interaction term
\mathcal{I}
in the BV action and expand perturbatively following Eq. (
2.19
). To account for the factor of
z^{2}
in the definition of the superconformal deformation, we furthermore take a derivative of the
\lambda
parameters, arriving at the following expression

\displaystyle\{\!\{\mathbb{O}_{1}{}_{\lambda}\mathbb{O}_{2}\}\!\}

\displaystyle=\{\{\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}{}_{(0,0)}\mathbf{O}_{1}\}{}_{(\lambda,0)}\mathbf{O}_{2}\}

(5.22)

\displaystyle+\sum_{n>0}\frac{(-\pi^{2})^{n}}{n!}\frac{{\rm d}\hfill}{{\rm d}\mu^{2}_{1}}\dots\frac{{\rm d}\hfill}{{\rm d}\mu^{2}_{n}}\{\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}{}_{(\mu^{1}_{1},\mu^{2}_{1})}\dots\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}{}_{(\mu^{1}_{n},\mu^{2}_{n})}\{\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}{}_{(0,0)}\mathbf{O}_{1}\}{}_{(\lambda,0)}\mathbf{O}_{2}\}\bigg|_{\mu^{1}_{i}=0,\mu^{2}_{i}=0}

\displaystyle+{\rm d}z^{2}\left(\dots\right)\,.

The final term completes this expression into a Schur superfield and is obtained by acting with
\{\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}{}_{(0,0)}-\}
on the terms in the first two lines.
We note that the first term of the right-hand side reproduces the holomorphic-topological descent bracket, cf. the three-dimensional analysis of
[
64
]
where the topological descent bracket in the
A
- and
B
-twists were related to the holomorphic-topological descent bracket.
The additional terms can be thought of as the quantum corrections to this quasi-classical vertex algebra, i.e. a (2-shifted) vertex Poisson algebra.

Similarly, the two-dimensional normal ordered product of the VOA is related to the four-dimensional brackets via

\displaystyle\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\tiny$\circ$}\hskip-3.85pt\raisebox{3.5pt}{\tiny$\circ$}\hskip 1.0pt\mathbb{O}_{1}{}\mathbb{O}_{2}\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\tiny$\circ$}\hskip-3.85pt\raisebox{3.5pt}{\tiny$\circ$}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{O}_{1}\mathbf{O}_{2}

(5.23)

\displaystyle+\sum_{n>0}\frac{(-\pi^{2})^{n}}{n!}\frac{{\rm d}\hfill}{{\rm d}\mu^{2}_{1}}\dots\frac{{\rm d}\hfill}{{\rm d}\mu^{2}_{n}}\{\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}{}_{(\mu^{1}_{1},\mu^{2}_{1})}\dots\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}{}_{(\mu^{1}_{n},\mu^{2}_{n})}\mathbf{O}_{1}{}_{(0,0)}\mathbf{O}_{2}\}^{\gamma}\bigg|_{\mu^{1}_{i}=0,\mu^{2}_{i}=0}

\displaystyle+{\rm d}z^{2}\left(\dots\right)\,.

where we have used the quantum corrected regularized products defined in
[
17
]
with
\gamma
denoting the insertion of an auxiliary propagator between the last two arguments; see
C
for a brief review. Although not manifest, the associativity relations of the
\lambda
-brackets and regularized products ensure (
5.22
) and (
5.23
) together define a VOA. More explicitly, in Appendix
C.1
, we verify that the definition (
5.22
) satisfies the all the desired properties of two-dimensional
\lambda
-brackets, i.e. those of a Lie conformal algebra. In Appendix
C.2
, we further check that (
5.22
) acts on (
5.23
) as a derivation at vanishing
\lambda
.

Returning to the example of the free hypermultiplet, we can now use the general expression to verify the
\lambda
-brackets of its generating fields.
The relevant Schur superfield takes the form

\mathbb{Z}^{n}={\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{n}+{\rm d}z^{2}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{m}\Omega^{mn}\,.

(5.24)

Substituting this into the above formula, we obtain

\displaystyle\{\!\{\mathbb{Z}^{n}{}_{\lambda}\mathbb{Z}^{m}\}\!\}

\displaystyle=\{\{\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}{}_{(0,0)}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{n}\}{}_{(\lambda,0)}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{m}\}+{\rm d}z^{2}(\dots)

(5.25)

\displaystyle=\Omega^{ln}\{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{l}{}_{(\lambda,0)}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{m}\}=\Omega^{nm}\,,

where we have omitted the terms proportional to
{\rm d}z^{2}
.
This precisely reproduces the
\lambda

.
It is worth noting that only binary brackets contribute in this case, due to the fact that the symplectic bosons are linear in the fundamental fields.

Having verified our general expression in the case of the hypermultiplet, we now apply it to recover the
\lambda

In four dimensions, the Schur superfield corresponding to the two-dimensional stress tensor is

(5.26)

\displaystyle+(-\pi^{2})\frac{{\rm d}\hfill}{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}\bigg(\{\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}{}_{(\mu^{1},\mu^{2})}\{\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}{}_{(0,0)}\mathbf{G}_{1}\}{}_{(\lambda,0)}\mathbf{G}_{1}\}\bigg)\bigg|_{\mu^{1}=0,\mu^{2}=0}+{\rm d}z^{2}(\dots)

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{S}_{1}{}_{(\lambda,0)}\mathbf{G}_{1}\}+\frac{\lambda}{3}\{\mathbf{R}{}_{(\lambda,0)}\mathbf{G}_{1}\}

\displaystyle+(-\pi^{2})\frac{{\rm d}\hfill}{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}\bigg(\{\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}{}_{(\mu^{1},\mu^{2})}\mathbf{S}_{1}{}_{(\lambda,0)}\mathbf{G}_{1}\}+\frac{\lambda}{3}\{\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}{}_{(\mu^{1},\mu^{2})}\mathbf{R}{}_{(\lambda,0)}\mathbf{G}_{1}\}\bigg)\bigg|_{\mu^{1}=0,\mu^{2}=0}+{\rm d}z^{2}(\dots)

takes the form

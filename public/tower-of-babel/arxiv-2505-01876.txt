Well-posedness of behavioral singular stochastic control problems
Artur Sidorenko

Abstract
We investigate the well-posedness of a general class of singular stochastic control problems in which controls are processes of finite variation. We develop an abstract framework, which we then apply to storage management and portfolio investment problems under proportional transaction costs. Within this setting, we establish the existence of an optimal strategy in the class of randomized controls for a range of goal functionals, including cumulative prospect theory (CPT) preferences. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first treatment of behavioral storage management with the CPT goal functional. For the portfolio management problem, our analysis exploits the metrizable Meyer–Zheng topology to simplify proofs. We thoroughly investigate the applicability of the Skorokhod representation theorem for adapted random processes. Overall, the presented framework provides a clear separation between constraints imposed on the market model and properties required of the goal functional.

Introduction

Singular stochastic control problems arise in diverse contexts, including operations research, finance, and inventory theory. Early work was motivated by engineering applications
(
Bather and Chernoff, 1967
)
and storage management models
(
Harrison and Taksar, 1983
;
Karatzas, 1983
)
, see also
(
Dixit, 1991
;
Sulem, 1986
)
. A mixed singular and impulse control problem was considered in
(
Dai and Yao, 2013a
;
Dai and Yao, 2013b
)
. A comprehensive survey of singular control in inventory theory can be found in
(
Perera and Sethi, 2023
)
. Most of the research models the demand in the storage dynamics as Brownian motion or a diffusion process. However, recent research involves regime-switching
(
Shao and Tian, 2023
)
and Lévy processes
(
Yamazaki, 2017
;
Noba et al., 2025
)
as well as control with unknown model parameters
(
Federico et al., 2023
)
.

Beyond inventory models, singular control has applications in mathematical finance, particularly in markets with transaction costs, where asset positions are adjusted via processes of finite variation. Three distinct paths have shaped the research landscape: Markovian approach
(
Magill and Constantinides, 1976
;
Davis and Norman, 1990
;
Shreve and Soner, 1994
)
, martingale approach
(
Cvitanić and Karatzas, 1996
;
Cvitanić and Wang, 2001
;
Guasoni, 2002
;
Guasoni, 2006
;
Czichowsky and Schachermayer, 2016
)
, and weak convergence approach
(
Bank et al., 2017
;
Bayraktar et al., 2020
;
Chau and Rásonyi, 2020
;
Bayraktar et al., 2021
)
.
Unlike problems for frictionless markets, price processes are not necessarily semimartingales
(
Guasoni, 2002
;
Guasoni, 2006
)
.

A convenient geometric formulation of singular control problems in finance was suggested in
(
Kabanov, 1999
;
Kabanov and Last, 2002
)
, see also
(
Kabanov and Safarian, 2010
)
for a comprehensive survey. This setting was investigated in
(
Deelstra et al., 2001
;
Bouchard, 2002
)
by duality arguments, whereas
(
Kabanov and Kluppelberg, 2004
;
De Vallière et al., 2016
)
treated the HJB equations. Later,
(
Campi and Schachermayer, 2006
;
Campi and Owen, 2011
)
incorporated discontinuous price processes. Other works include
(
Kabanov, 1999
;
Kabanov and Last, 2002
;
Schachermayer, 2004
;
Grépat and Kabanov, 2021
)
.

Traditionally, these problems are formulated in terms of expected gain or utility maximization. However, decision-makers often depart from expected utility maximization as illustrated by the Allais paradox. To address this issue, cumulative prospect theory (CPT) was developed
(
Tversky and Kahneman, 1992
;
Tversky and Kahneman, 1974
)
.
Optimal control under CPT preferences has been studied primarily in frictionless markets
(
Jin and Yu Zhou, 2008
)
, see also
(
He and Zhou, 2011
;
Xu, 2016
;
He and Zhou, 2016
;
Rüschendorf and Vanduffel, 2020
;
Van Bilsen and Laeven, 2020
;
Dai et al., 2022
;
Bi et al., 2023
)
. Well-posedness of such problems was examined
(
Rásonyi and Rodrigues, 2013
)
.

In this study, we develop a general optimization framework that naturally embeds a range of applicable singular control problems under various goal functionals, including expected utility maximization and CPT preferences. The crucial features of this framework are: controls are processes of finite variation, a countable number of constraints is imposed, and the target functional depends solely on the probability distribution in a path space (i.e. expected utility or CPT preferences). Within this framework, we establish sufficient conditions for the existence of an optimal solution.

Using this framework, we establish existence of an optimal control in a class of randomized strategies for both inventory management and portfolio investment problems involving transaction costs and CPT preferences. The use of randomized strategies is motivated by the observation that such strategies can improve the satisfaction of agents with CPT preferences
(
Carassus and Rásonyi, 2015
)
. The additional randomness was exploited in other settings as well, see, e.g.,
(
Henderson et al., 2017
;
Hu et al., 2023
)
. This effect bears some resemblance to the difference between weak and strong solutions of SDE, see, for example,
(
Tsirel’son, 1976
)
. A key strength of our framework is its ability to clearly separate assumptions on the performance functional from those on the underlying stochastic model.

To the best of our knowledge, this paper is the first treatment of behavioral storage management. Incorporating the CPT preferences into the framework would enable tailoring the solutions to the risk preferences of real economic agents. Our framework enables going beyond semimartingales and verifying well-posedness of the inventory problem when the demand is an arbitrary process with continuous paths.

For the portfolio investment problem, only
(
Chau and Rásonyi, 2020
)
treats continuous-time markets with friction and CPT investors, whereas
(
Chau and Rásonyi, 2017
)
considers non-linear frictions. These papers employ technically intricate weak convergence arguments for non-metrizable topologies. In singular control theory, the Skorokhod
J_{1}

(
Budhiraja and Ross, 2006
)
and weak
M_{1}

(
Cohen, 2021
)
topologies have been applied as well.
We adopt a metrizable topology by P.A. Meyer and W.A. Zheng
(
Meyer and Zheng, 1984
)
, see also
(
Kurtz, 1991
)
. Applications of this topology in portfolio optimizations can be found in
(
Bayraktar et al., 2020
)
. The reliance on metrizable topologies simplifies technical proofs.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces the general setting. Section
3
applies this setting for the multi-asset conic market and several examples of goal functionals including the CPT functional and discusses the embedding into the abstract setup. Finally, Section
4
discusses the well-posedness of the behavioral storage management problem. For fluidity, proofs of the main results are relegated to Section
5
, whereas auxiliary statements are collected in the Appendix.

d_{MZ}(f,g)=\int_{[0,T[}\min\left(|f(t)-g(t)|,\,1\right)\,dt+\min\left(|f(T)-g(T)|,\,1\right).

(1)

Portfolio investment under transaction costs

In this section, we describe the Kabanov model of markets with transaction costs and establish a link between this model and the general setting of Section

We adopt the assumptions on the stochastic basis from Section

Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
be a number of assets and
S=(S_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be a
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued process associated with the prices of these assets. The price process
S
is continuous,
{\mathbb{F}}
-adapted and all its entries are strictly positive. The first asset is a risk-free asset with a constant price

The core idea of the Kabanov model
(
Kabanov, 1999
;
Kabanov and Safarian, 2010
)
is that the transaction fees can be represented by a closed convex proper cone
K
such that
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}\subset K
. Recall that a closed convex cone is proper if
K\cap(-K)=\{0\}
. The dual cone
K^{*}
is defined as
K^{*}=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\,xy\geq 0\>\forall x\in K\}
. The equivalent condition for a proper cone is
{\rm int}\,K^{*}\neq\varnothing
. Since
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}\subset K
, the dual cone
K^{*}\setminus\{0\}\subset{\rm int}\,{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}
. Thanks to the bipolar theorem,
K^{**}=K
.

We will also consider a solvency cone in terms of physical units
\widehat{K}_{t}:=K/S_{t}
. To be precise, we put
\varphi_{t}(x):=x/S_{t}
and
\widehat{K}_{t}:=\varphi_{t}(K)
. Note that
\widehat{K}^{*}_{t}=\varphi^{-1}_{t}(K^{*})
.
The closed convex proper cone
K
generates a partial order
x\preccurlyeq y\Leftrightarrow y-x\in K
.

Definition 8
.

We say that a function
f:[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

implies

Definition 9
.

Any
{\mathbb{G}}
-adapted
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

Due to Proposition I.3.13 of

4
Behavioral storage management problem

In this section, we study a storage management problem using the framework of Section
2
. The main contribution of this section is the well-posedness of the storage management problem under cumulative prospect theory preferences.

We adopt the stochastic basis and notation from Section
2
.
Fix
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Let
X
be an
{\mathbb{F}}
-adapted
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
-valued process with continuous paths (e.g. a diffusion process) with initial value
X_{0}=x
. Process
X
represents stochastic demand, whereas
x
is the initial state of the uncontrolled storage. We also fix
K>0
.

Definition 21
.

A pair of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued process
L:=(L^{+},L^{-})
is called an admissible strategy if they are
{\mathbb{G}}
-adapted, right-continuous, increasing, and the total variation

\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(L^{+,j}_{T}+L^{-,j}_{T}\right)\leq K.

(16)

Throughout this section, “increasing” is understood to mean “nondecreasing”.

We denote
{\cal A}
the set of all admissible strategies.
Note that right-continuous increasing processes have left limits. The constraint (
16
) was inspired by
(
Harrison and Taksar, 1983
;
Beneš et al., 1980
)
. This reflects that the capacity to adjust the storage is inherently limited.

The storage evolves as

Z_{t}:=X_{t}+A_{+}L^{+}_{t}-A_{-}L^{-}_{t},

(17)

where
A_{\pm}=(a_{\pm,ij})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k\times d}
are matrices of size
k\times d
. Note that
L^{\pm}_{0}
is not necessarily zero, so an instantaneous jump at time
0
is allowed:

Z_{0}=x+A_{+}L^{+}_{0}-A_{-}L^{-}_{0}.

The running cost of maintenance of this storage is given by a functional

W=W^{L}:=\int_{0}^{T}g(s,Z_{s})ds+\int_{[0,T]}h_{+}(s)dL^{+}_{s}+\int_{[0,T]}h_{-}(s)dL^{-}_{s}+G(Z_{T}),

(18)

where
h_{\pm}\colon[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}
are continuous functions and
g\colon[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and
G\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
are bounded continuous functions. The notation
\int_{0}^{T}h_{\pm}(s)dL^{\pm}_{s}
means

\int_{[0,T]}h_{\pm}(s)dL^{\pm}_{s}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{[0,T]}h_{\pm}^{j}(s)dL^{\pm,j}_{s}.

In particular, if
d=k=1
and
A_{\pm}=1
, this reduces to the one-dimensional model studied by
(
Harrison and Taksar, 1983
)
:

Z_{t}:=X_{t}+L^{+}_{t}-L^{-}_{t}.

Note that by the Doob lemma,
X=\phi(Y)
for some Borel function
\phi:{\cal D}_{m}\to{\cal C}_{k}
, and
Y
was defined in Section
2
.

Let

\mathfrak{M}:=\left\{{\cal L}_{\bf P}(Y,\phi(Y),L)\colon L\in{\cal A}\right\}

be a family of probability measures on the product space
{\cal D}_{m}\times{\cal C}_{k}\times{\cal V}^{2}
.

Lemma 22
.

The family of probability measures
\mathfrak{M}
is compact in the space
{\cal P}({\cal D}_{m}\times{\cal C}_{k}\times{\cal V}^{2})
and, as such, Assumption
5
holds.

Proof.

Let
V_{K}:=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\cal V}^{2}\colon{\rm Var}\,x_{1}+{\rm Var}\,x_{2}\leq K\}
. By the Helly theorem,
V_{K}
is compact in the Meyer–Zheng topology. Since

with probability one for all
L\in{\cal A}
, the family

is tight, and, thanks to the Prokhorov theorem, compact.
∎

Lemma 23
.

The set
{\cal A}
satisfies Assumption
4
.

Proof.

Convergence of functions from
{\cal V}
in the Meyer–Zheng topology implies weak convergence. Choose a countable family of positive bounded continuous functions
(f_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\cal C}_{2d}
defining weak convergence in
{\cal V}^{2}
.
Then we set

\Phi_{i}(l):=\int_{[0,T]}f(t)dl(t).

Note that
l

for all

.

∎

We define a mapping

Define

{\bf E}_{Q}M\cdot\widehat{V}_{T}=x{\bf E}_{Q}M_{0}+{\bf E}((M/S)\dot{B})\cdot{\rm Var}\,B_{T}\leq x{\bf E}_{Q}M_{0}-\varepsilon{\bf E}_{Q}{\rm Var}\,B_{T}.

Finally,

\varepsilon{\bf E}_{Q}{\rm Var}\,B_{T}\leq x{\bf E}_{Q}M_{0}-{\bf E}_{Q}M_{T}\widehat{V}_{T}\leq x{\bf E}_{Q}M_{0}.

Since
M_{0}\in\Lambda
a.s., the assertion follows.
∎

Completion of the proof.

Due to Lemma
27
and the Markov inequality, a family of measures

\{{\cal L}_{Q}({\rm Var}_{T}\,B):\;B\in{\cal A}(x)\}

is tight. Since
P\ll Q
,

\{{\cal L}_{P}({\rm Var}_{T}\,B):\;B\in{\cal A}(x)\}

is tight as well.
As
\{f:\;{\rm Var}_{T}\,f\leq c\}
are compact sets in
D_{MZ}([0,T],{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, the assertion follows.

5.4
Proof of Lemma
17

Put
p:=q/(q-1)
. Clearly,
1/p>\gamma
. It suffices to establish that, for a fixed
x\in{\rm int}\,K
,

\sup_{B\in{\cal A}(x)}{\bf E}\left(V^{x,B}_{T}\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

Put
Q:=Z^{(1)}_{T}P
and
M:=Z/Z^{(1)}
. By Lemma
29
,
\widehat{V}^{x,B}Z
is a
P
-supermartingale implying that
\widehat{V}^{x,B}M
is a
Q
-supermartingale. By definition,
V^{x,B}_{T}-\ell(V^{x,B}_{T})e_{1}\in K
, where
e_{1}
is the first vector of the standard basis in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. As
Z/S\in K^{*}
and
M^{(1)}\equiv 1
, we have

\ell(V^{x,B}_{T})=(M_{T}/S_{T})\ell(V^{x,B}_{T})e_{1}\leq(M_{T}/S_{T})V^{x,B}_{T}=M_{T}\widehat{V}^{x,B}_{T}.

Then by the Hölder inequality

{\bf E}\left(\ell(V^{x,B}_{T})\right)^{1/p}\leq{\bf E}\left(M_{T}\widehat{V}^{x,B}_{T}\right)^{1/p}={\bf E}_{Q}\left[\left(M_{T}\widehat{V}^{x,B}_{T}\right)^{1/p}\left(Z^{(1)}_{T}\right)^{-1}\right]\leq\\

6.1
K
-increasing (decreasing) functions are of bounded variation

A right-continuous function of bounded variation
f:[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}
induces a signed measure
\mu
on
[0,T]
with
\mu([0,t])=f(t)
. In particular,
f(0)
is
\mu(\{0\})
. If the Hahn decomposition
f=f_{1}-f_{2}
with
f_{2}(0)=0
, then
{\rm Var}\,f=f_{1}+f_{2}
. If a function
f:[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with entries
f^{i}
being right-continuous functions of bounded variation, set
{\rm Var}\,f:=\sum_{i}{\rm Var}\,f^{i}
.

Lemma 28
.

Let
f:\,[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a right-continuous function and
K
be a closed proper convex cone. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

f
is
K
-increasing (
K
-decreasing);

(ii)

f
is of bounded variation with the Radon–Nikodym derivative
\dot{f}:=df/d{\rm Var}f
evolving in
K
(
-K
), where
{\rm Var}\,f(t)
is the total variation of
f
on
[0,t]
.

Proof.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
is apparent. It remains to verify
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. Consider the case of
f
being
K
-increasing. As
K
is proper,
K^{*}
contains a basis
a_{1},\dots,a_{d}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Denote scalar products
g_{i}(t)=a_{i}f(t)
. As
f(t)-f(s)\in K
, we have that
g_{i}(t)\geq g_{i}(s)
, i.e.
g_{i}
are increasing. The coefficients
c_{i}(t)
of a linear combination

f(t):=\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}c_{i}(t)

can be obtained as linear combinations of scalar products
g_{i}(t)
:

c(t)=G^{-1}g(t),

where
G
is the Gram matrix of
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
. It implies that
c_{i}(t)
are right-continuous of bounded variation and so is
f
. In this case,
{\rm Var}\,f
is well-defined. Since

a_{i}(f(t)-f(s))=\int_{]s,t]}a_{i}\dot{f}(u)d{\rm Var}f(u)\geq 0,

\dot{f}\in K
up to a set of
d{\rm Var}\,f
-measure zero. By redefining
\dot{f}
on this set, we arrive at the required statement.
∎

6.2
An auxiliary estimate

The statement below is similar to Lemma 3.6.2 of
(
Kabanov and Safarian, 2010
)
. Let
{\cal M}
be a family of right-continuous
{\mathbb{F}}
-martingales
Z
with
Z_{t}\in\widehat{K}_{t}
a.s. for every
t\in[0,T]
(and, as such, up to evanescence). Denote
{\cal X}
the set of all
d
-dimensional right-continuous processes of bounded variation
X
with the Radon–Nikodym derivative (with respect to the total variation process
{\rm Var}\,X
)
\dot{X}\in-\widehat{K}
and
X+a{\bf 1}\in\widehat{K}
up to evanescence for some
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
. By convention,
X_{0-}=0
.

Lemma 29
.

Let
X\in{\cal X}
and
Z\in{\cal M}
. Then the scalar product
ZM
is a supermartingale, and

{\bf E}(Z\dot{X})\cdot{\rm Var}_{T}\,X\geq{\bf E}Z_{T}X_{T}.

(30)

Proof.

By the product formula,

ZX=X_{-}\cdot Z+Z\cdot X_{T}.

The first term is a local martingale. As
Z_{t}\dot{X}_{t}\leq 0
, the latter term is a negative decreasing process. For every entry, we have

X^{j}_{-}\cdot Z^{j}=Z^{j}X^{j}-Z^{j}\dot{X}^{j}\cdot{\rm Var}\,X\geq Z^{j}X^{j}\geq-aZ^{j}

for some
a\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
by definition of
{\cal X}
. Recall that a local martingale bounded from below by a martingale is a supermartingale. The terminal value of the decreasing process
Z\cdot X_{T}=Z_{T}X_{T}-X_{-}\cdot Z_{T}
. Since
X_{-}\cdot Z
is a supermartingale, we obtain that
{\bf E}X_{-}\cdot Z_{T}\leq{\bf E}X_{-}\cdot Z_{0}=0
. Finally,
Z_{T}X_{T}\geq-aZ_{T}{\bf 1}
implying that
(Z\dot{X})\cdot{\rm Var}\,X_{T}
is bounded from below by an integrable random variable. Thus,
(Z\dot{X})\cdot{\rm Var}\,X
is a supermartingale and so is
ZX
. Immediately,

{\bf E}(Z\dot{X})\cdot{\rm Var}_{T}\,X={\bf E}(Z_{T}X_{T}-X_{-}\cdot Z_{T})\geq{\bf E}Z_{T}X_{T}.

That concludes the proof.
∎

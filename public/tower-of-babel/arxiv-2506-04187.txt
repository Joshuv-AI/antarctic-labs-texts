Toward Khintchine’s theorem with a moving target: extra divergence or finitely centered target
Gilbert Michaud and Felipe A. Ramírez

Abstract
. Szüsz’s inhomogeneous version (1958) of Khintchine’s theorem (1924) gives conditions on ψ : ℕ → ℝ ⩾ 0 \psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0} under which for almost every real number α \alpha there exist infinitely many rationals p / q p/q such that | α − p + γ q | < ψ ⁡ ( q ) q , \left\lvert\alpha-\frac{p+\gamma}{q}\right\rvert<\frac{\psi(q)}{q}, where γ ∈ ℝ \gamma\in\mathbb{R} is some fixed inhomogeneous parameter. It is often interpreted as a statement about visits of q ​ α ( mod 1 ) q\alpha\,(\bmod 1) to a shrinking target centered around γ ( mod 1 ) \gamma\,(\bmod 1) , viewed in ℝ / ℤ \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z} . Hauke and the second author have conjectured that Szüsz’s result continues to hold if the target is allowed to move as well as shrink, that is, if the inhomogeneous parameter γ \gamma is allowed to depend on the denominator q q of the approximating rational. We show that the conjecture holds under an “extra divergence” assumption on ψ \psi . We also show that it holds when the inhomogeneous parameter’s movement is constrained to a finite set. As a byproduct, we obtain a finite-colorings version of the inhomogeneous Khintchine theorem, giving rational approximations with monochromatic denominators.

1.
Introduction

For a function
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
and a real number

\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, define

W(\psi,\gamma)=\left\{\alpha\in[0,1]:\lVert q\alpha-\gamma\rVert<\psi(q)\textrm{ for infinitely many }q\in\mathbb{N}\right\},

where
\lVert\cdot\rVert
denotes distance to
\mathbb{Z}
. The elements of

W(\psi,\gamma)
are called the
\psi
-approximable numbers with
inhomogeneous parameter
\gamma
.

This definition has a handy dynamical interpretation where one views

[0,1]
as a fundamental domain of
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
. Then
\alpha
is an
element of
W(\psi,\gamma)
if the rotation orbit

\{q\alpha\,(\bmod 1)\}_{q=1}^{\infty}
enters the shrinking target

(\gamma-\psi(q),\gamma+\psi(q))\,(\bmod\,1)
infinitely many times.

In metric Diophantine approximation we are interested in the Lebesgue
measure of
W(\psi,\gamma)
and of similarly defined sets. The
following classical theorem gives conditions under which

W(\psi,\gamma)
has measure
0
or measure
1
.

Theorem

(Inhomogeneous Khintchine Theorem,
[
25
]
)
.

If
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
is decreasing and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, then

\meas(W(\psi,\gamma))=\begin{cases}0&\textrm{if }\sum\psi(q)<\infty\\
1&\textrm{if }\sum\psi(q)=\infty,\end{cases}

where
\meas
denotes Lebesgue measure.

Remark
.

The convergence part is a standard application of the
Borel–Cantelli lemma. Throughout this paper, we are only concerned
with the divergence part.

The inhomogeneous Khintchine theorem has been generalized to higher
dimensions
[
23
]
and to systems of linear
forms
[
24
]
. Recent
papers
[
4
,
3
,
17
]

have focused special attention on the fact that these higher
dimensional generalizations allow for the inhomogeneous parameter

\gamma
to depend on the denominator
q
—the target can move. Yet,
it is still not known whether the one dimensional theorem quoted above
allows for a moving target. The following problem is posed
in
[
17
, Conjecture 2]
.

Conjecture 1

(Khintchine’s theorem with a moving target)
.

For
\gamma:=(\gamma_{q})_{q}
a sequence of real numbers and

\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
a function, define

W(\psi,\gamma)=\left\{\alpha\in[0,1]:\lVert q\alpha-\gamma_{q}\rVert<\psi(q)\textrm{ infinitely often}\right\}.

If
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
is decreasing and

\sum\psi(q)=\infty
, then
\meas(W(\psi,\gamma))=1
.

Remark
.

Note that in the notation,
\gamma
is now the name of the sequence

(\gamma_{q})_{q\geqslant 1}
. Henceforth, when a real number

\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
appears in the notation
W(\psi,\sigma)
, we intend
for it to be interpreted as the constant sequence

(\sigma,\sigma,\dots)
.

In this paper we establish ?THM?
under an extra
divergence assumption (?THM?
) and under the
assumption that the sequence
(\gamma_{q})_{q}
lies in a finite set
(?THM?
).

1.1.
Extra divergence

Sprindžuk
[
24
]
gives an asymptotic formula for the number
of solutions to
\lVert q\alpha-\gamma_{q}\rVert<\psi(q)\,(q\leqslant Q)
, and
this formula shows that ?THM?
holds if

\sum_{q\leqslant Q}\psi(q)\gg(\log Q)(\log\log Q)^{3+\delta}

(see ?THM?
). But this rate is too fast to even
apply to the function
1/q
. We prove the following extra divergence
result, which does apply in particular to
1/q
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\gamma:=(\gamma_{q})_{q}
be a sequence of real numbers and
\psi

decreasing. If there exists
{\varepsilon}>0
and
k\geqslant 2
for which

\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\frac{\psi(q)}{\sqrt{\log q}(\log\log q)(\log\log\log q)\dots(\underbrace{\log\log\dots\log}_{k\textrm{ iterates}}q)^{1+{\varepsilon}}}=\infty,

then
\meas(W(\psi,\gamma))=1
. In particular,
\sum\psi(q)(\log q)^{-\frac{1}{2}-{\varepsilon}}=\infty
suffices.

Remark
.

Throughout this paper, we use the notation

\log x=\max\{1,\ln x\}
, where
\ln
is the natural logarithm.

The following corollary is immediate.

Corollary 2
.

?THM?
holds for any monotonic
\psi
such that

\psi(q)\gg\frac{(\overbrace{\log\log\dots\log}^{k\textrm{ iterates}}q)^{\varepsilon}}{q\sqrt{\log q}}.

In particular, it holds for
\psi(q)=1/q
.

Remark

(On asymptotic notation)
.

The notation
f(q)\ll g(q)
means that there is some constant
C>0

such that
f(q)\leqslant Cg(q)
for all relevant
q
, that is, if

f(q)=O(g(q))
. We use
f(q)\asymp g(q)
to mean that both

f(q)\ll g(q)
and
g(q)\ll f(q)
hold.

?THM?
is reminiscent of a collection of extra
divergence
results
[
18
,
6
,
2
]

that were proved in the decade before Koukoulopoulos and Maynard’s
2020 proof of the Duffin–Schaeffer
conjecture
[
20
,
12
]
. By direct analogy with the
last of these
[
2
]
, one may expect
that the extra divergence in ?THM?
can be relaxed to

\sum\psi(q)(\log q)^{-{\varepsilon}}=\infty
. However, the problems are quite
different, as are the methods of proof. The inhomogeneity of our
setting is directly responsible for the
\sqrt{\log q}
appearing in
our extra divergence assumption, and it is not obvious how (or
whether) one can avoid it.

For readers interested in Duffin–Schaeffer-type problems, we remark
that the Duffin–Schaeffer conjecture with a moving target (i.e., a
Duffin–Schaeffer version of ?THM?
) has already been
ruled
out
[
17
, Theorem 2]
.

1.2.
Finitely centered target and monochromatic denominators

We are able to prove ?THM?
unconditionally on
\psi

if the target centers
\gamma=(\gamma_{q})
lie in a finite set. This
follows from the following more general statement.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\{\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{\ell}\}
be a partition of
\mathbb{N}
,
and
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\dots,\sigma_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}
. If

\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
is decreasing and
\sum\psi(q)=\infty
,
then there exists
k\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
such that

\meas(W(\mathbf{1}_{\pi_{k}}\psi,\sigma_{k}))=1
.

As immediate corollary, there is the following statement
addressing ?THM?
.

Corollary 4

(Khintchine’s theorem with a finitely centered moving target)
.

?THM?
is true if the sequence
(\gamma_{q})_{q}
is
contained in a finite subset

(\gamma_{q})_{q}\subset\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\dots,\sigma_{\ell}\}\subset\mathbb{R}.

In this case, more is true: with
\psi
as
in ?THM?
, there exists
\sigma\in\{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{\ell}\}

such that
\meas(W(\mathbf{1}_{\{\gamma_{q}=\sigma\}}\psi,\gamma))=1
.

Proof.

Define
\pi_{k}=\{q\in\mathbb{N}:\gamma_{q}=\sigma_{k}\}
and
apply ?THM?
.
∎

Another corollary to ?THM?
is the following,
which can be expressed in terms of colorings of
\mathbb{N}
. If every

q\in\mathbb{N}
is assigned one of finitely-many possible colors, and
\psi

is a monotonic function such that
\sum\psi(q)
diverges, then there
is some color—say, orange—for which almost every real number is

\psi
-approximable by rationals with orange denominators.

Corollary 5

(Inhomogeneous Khintchine theorem with
monochromatic denominators)
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
\{\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{\ell}\}
a partition of

\mathbb{N}
. Let
\psi
be as in ?THM?
. Then there
exists a partition element
\pi\in\{\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{\ell}\}

such that
\meas(W(\mathbf{1}_{\pi}\psi,\gamma))=1
.

Proof.

Apply ?THM?
with
\sigma_{k}=\gamma
for

k=1,\dots,\ell
.
∎

Remark
.

The homogeneous version of ?THM?
admits a simpler
proof. One notes that

W(\psi,0)=W(\mathbf{1}_{\pi_{1}}\psi,0)\cup\dots\cup W(\mathbf{1}_{\pi_{\ell}}\psi,0).

By Khintchine’s theorem,
W(\psi,0)
has full measure, hence for
some
k=1,\dots,\ell
, one of the
W(\mathbf{1}_{\pi_{k}}\psi,0)
has
positive measure. Then, by Cassels’ zero-one
law
[
9
, Theorem I]
, it has full measure. But there
is no inhomogeneous zero-one law. It is a well-known problem to prove one.

One can compare ?THM?
with results in
[
16
]

where Khintchine-type statements are proved under restrictions on
numerators and denominators. Among those, it is shown that if

D\subset\mathbb{N}
is a set with positive lower asymptotic density, and

\psi
is a monotonic function such that
\sum\psi(q)
diverges, then
almost every
\alpha
is
\psi
-approximable by rationals with
denominators lying in
D
. The monochromatic set
in ?THM?
need not have positive density, but it may
depend on
\psi
. This dependence is unavoidable, as the following
simple example shows.

Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and consider the partition
\mathbb{N}=\pi_{0}\cup\pi_{1}
where

\pi_{0}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geqslant 0\\
k\textrm{ even}\end{subarray}}[2^{2^{k}},2^{2^{k+1}})\cap\mathbb{N}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\pi_{1}=\{1\}\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geqslant 0\\
k\textrm{ odd}\end{subarray}}[2^{2^{k}},2^{2^{k+1}})\cap\mathbb{N}.

Let

\psi_{0}(q)=\begin{cases}2^{-2^{k+1}}&\textrm{if }q\in[2^{2^{k}},2^{2^{k+1}}),\textrm{with $k\geqslant 0$ even}\\
q^{-2}&\textrm{otherwise}\end{cases}

and

\psi_{1}(q)=\begin{cases}2^{-2^{k+1}}&\textrm{if }q\in[2^{2^{k}},2^{2^{k+1}}),\textrm{with $k\geqslant 0$ odd}\\
q^{-2}&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then both
\psi_{0}
and
\psi_{1}
are decreasing and have diverging
sums. Since
\sum q^{-2}
converges, the Borel–Cantelli lemma implies
that

\meas(W(\mathbf{1}_{\pi_{0}}\psi_{1},\gamma))=\meas(W(\mathbf{1}_{\pi_{1}}\psi_{0},\gamma))=0
. Therefore, the monochromatic set coming
from ?THM?
is
\pi_{0}
for
\psi_{0}
and
\pi_{1}
for

\psi_{1}
—that is, it must depend on the approximating function.

1.3.
Other remarks

It is worth noting that other natural variants
of ?THM?
are also open. For instance, it does not
seem easier to prove the conjecture in the case where the sequence

\gamma=(\gamma_{q})
converges to some
\gamma_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. But for
convergent sequences, one can easily name conditions under which

W(\psi,\gamma)
has full measure. For example, if

\lvert\gamma_{q}-\gamma_{0}\rvert\ll\psi(q)
for all
q
, then, by the
triangle inequality,
W(\psi,\gamma_{0})\subset W(C\psi,\gamma)
for
some
C>0
. The inhomogeneous Khintchine theorem gives

\meas(W(\psi,\gamma_{0}))=1
, hence
\meas(W(C\psi,\gamma))=1
, and
the result follows from Cassels’
lemma
[
9
, Lemma 9]
. This idea can be
pushed. In ?THM?
we can assume without loss of
generality that
\psi(q)\geqslant c(q)
for every nonincreasing sequence

c(q)
such that
\sum c(q)<\infty
. (One simply replaces
\psi(q)

with
\max\{\psi(q),c(q)\}
. The divergence condition persists, and
the difference in the resulting limsup sets has measure
0
by the
convergence of
\sum c(q)
and the Borel–Cantelli lemma.) The
preceding argument now implies that ?THM?
holds for

\gamma=(\gamma_{q})
if there exists
\gamma_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\sum_{q}\lvert\gamma_{q}-\gamma_{0}\rvert<\infty
. We leave open the
question for sequences with a slower rate of convergence.

A similar argument shows that if there is a dense set

\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}
such that ?THM?
holds for all
moving targets whose centers lie in
\mathcal{Q}
, then the full conjecture
holds. For suppose
\gamma:=(\gamma_{q})\subset\mathbb{R}
and
\psi
is
decreasing with
\sum\psi(q)=\infty
. By the density of
\mathcal{Q}
,
there exists a sequence
\sigma:=(\sigma_{q})_{q}\subset\mathcal{Q}
such that

\lvert\gamma_{q}-\sigma_{q}\rvert\leqslant\psi(q)
for all
q
. Again the
triangle inequality shows that
W(\psi,\sigma)
is contained in

W(2\psi,\gamma)
. The former has full measure by assumption,
therefore so does
W(2\psi,\gamma)
, and the result follows Cassels’
lemma as before. This reduces ?THM?
to the case
where the target centers are, say, rational.

The reduction to dense subsets of
\mathbb{R}
is so immediate that one may
take it as a hint that ?THM?
for rational
sequences—or sequences contained in any other dense subset

\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}
—is no easier than the full conjecture. If so, we
ask the following intermediate question: Can one find a set

\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathcal{S}\,(\bmod 1)
is infinite and such that ?THM?
holds for all
sequences
\gamma:=(\gamma_{q})\subset\mathcal{S}
?

2.
Measure theoretic statements

In this section we gather some general measure-theoretic results that
are indispensable in this work. The sets
W(\psi,\gamma)
are best
understood as limsup sets, and the results listed in this section are
tools for showing that the measures of limsup sets are large.

The first is often called the Divergence Borel–Cantelli lemma, and
can be found in various sources, such as
[
16
, Lemma 2.3]
. It
is often attributed to Erdős–Renyi
[
13
]
.

Proposition 6

(Divergence Borel–Cantelli
lemma)
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a finite measure space and
(A_{q})_{q\geqslant 1}
a
sequence of measurable subsets of
X
such that

\sum_{q\geqslant 1}\mu(A_{q})=\infty
. Then

\mu(\limsup_{q\to\infty}A_{q})\geqslant\limsup_{Q\to\infty}\frac{\left\lparen\sum_{q\leqslant Q}\mu(A_{q})\right\rparen^{2}}{\sum_{q,r\leqslant Q}\mu(A_{q}\cap A_{r})}.

If the right-hand side of the inequality appearing in ?THM?
is positive, one says that the sets
A_{q}
are
quasi-independent on average (QIA)
. Quasi-independence on average is the crux of many results in metric Diophantine approximation, including the main results here. Once QIA is established, one has a positive-measure limsup set.

Closely related to the Divergence Borel–Cantelli lemma is the Erdős–Chung lemma.

Proposition 7

(Chung–Erdős Lemma,
[
11
]
)
.

If
(X,\mu)
is a finite measure space and

(A_{q})_{q\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X
is a sequence of measurable subsets
such that
0<\sum_{q}\mu(A_{q})<\infty
, then

\mu\left\lparen\bigcup_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}\right\rparen\geqslant\frac{\left\lparen\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\mu(A_{q})\right\rparen^{2}}{\sum_{q,r=1}^{\infty}\mu(A_{q}\cap A_{r})}.

Remark
.

This is usually stated with finitely many sets
A_{1},\dots,A_{Q}
. The version stated above is a simple extension. (See
[
22
, Proposition 3]
.)

The challenge in applying ?THM?s
and
to problems in Diophantine approximation comes from the fact that the sets arising in these problems are far from independent in general. Commonly, rather than having a bound of the form
\mu(A_{q}\cap A_{r})\ll\mu(A_{q})\mu(A_{r})
, which would be useful for the application of the above results, one instead tends to find an extra term,

(1)

\mu(A_{q}\cap A_{r})\ll\mu(A_{q})\mu(A_{r})+\textrm{extra},

and the problem becomes to control that term. In homogeneous problems,
the extra term comes from rational numbers expressible with both
denominator
q
and denominator
r
, and it can be dealt with by
requiring that all rational numbers be expressed in reduced
form. (This then leads to other difficulties. It is the reason that a
monotonicity assumption appears in Khintchine’s theorem, and that
monotonicity assumption in turn gave rise to the Duffin–Schaeffer
conjecture
[
12
]
, a problem with a rich 80-year
history
[
12
,
1
,
18
,
21
,
6
,
8
,
10
]

culminating in a proof by Koukoulopoulos and Maynard
[
20
]
and
inspiring follow-up
work
[
22
,
17
]
.)

For inhomogeneous problems, simply reducing fractions does not remove
the extra term in (
1
). Nevertheless, if the
inhomogeneous parameter is fixed, then one can benefit by a shifted
kind of reduction, as we will see in the proof
of ?THM?
, which uses a reduction of
Schmidt
[
23
]
. In the moving target setting, where the
inhomogeneous parameter is allowed to depend on the denominator, it
becomes even harder to handle the extra term. The following result
shows that it can be absorbed if the sum of measures of
A_{q}
exhibits
extra divergence. This is an abstraction of Yu’s proof
of
[
28
, Theorem 1.8]
.

Proposition 8
.

For each
q\geqslant 1
, let
A_{q}\subset X
, where

X
is a metric space with a finite measure
\mu
such that every open set is
\mu
-measurable.
Suppose
\eta:\mathbb{N}^{2}\to[1,\infty)
is such that

(2)

\mu(A_{q}\cap A_{r})\leqslant\kappa\mu(A_{q})(\mu(A_{r})+\eta(q,r))\qquad(1\leqslant r\leqslant q)

for some
\kappa>0
. Suppose
U\subset X
is an open set and that

\mu(A_{q}\cap U)\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\mu(A_{q})\mu(U)

for all sufficiently large
q
. Let
\eta(q)=\sum_{r\leqslant q}\eta(q,r)
and

h:[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
increasing and such that

(3)

h(2q)\ll h(q)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\sum_{\ell\geqslant 0}\frac{1}{h(2^{\ell})}<\infty.

If

(4)

\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(A_{q})}{\eta(q)h(\eta(q))}=\infty

then
\mu(\limsup A_{q}\cap U)\geqslant\mu(U)^{2}/(4\kappa)
.

Proof.

Following Yu’s argument
in
[
28
]
, we define for each
\ell\geqslant 0
the set

D_{\ell}=\{q\in\mathbb{N}:2^{\ell}\leqslant\eta(q)<2^{\ell+1}\}.

If there exists
\ell\geqslant 0
for which

\sum_{q\in D_{\ell}}\mu(A_{q})=\infty
, then we may restrict attention
to
D_{\ell}
. By (
2
),

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q,r=1\\
q,r\in D_{\ell}\end{subarray}}^{Q}\mu(A_{q}\cap A_{r}\cap U)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q,r=1\\
q,r\in D_{\ell}\end{subarray}}^{Q}\mu(A_{q}\cap A_{r})

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r<q\\
q,r\in D_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\left\lparen\kappa\mu(A_{q})(\mu(A_{r})+\eta(q,r)\right\rparen+2\kappa\sum_{q\in D_{\ell}}\mu(A_{q})

\displaystyle\leqslant\kappa\left\lparen\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\\
q\in D_{\ell}\end{subarray}}^{Q}\mu(A_{q})\right\rparen^{2}(1+o(1))+2\kappa\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\\
q\in D_{\ell}\end{subarray}}^{Q}\mu(A_{q})\eta(q)

\displaystyle\leqslant\kappa\left\lparen\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\\
q\in D_{\ell}\end{subarray}}^{Q}\mu(A_{q})\right\rparen^{2}(1+o(1))+2\kappa\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\\
q\in D_{\ell}\end{subarray}}^{Q}\mu(A_{q})2^{\ell+1}

\displaystyle=\kappa\left\lparen\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\\
q\in D_{\ell}\end{subarray}}^{Q}\mu(A_{q})\right\rparen^{2}(1+o(1))

\displaystyle=\frac{4\kappa}{\mu(U)^{2}}\left\lparen\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\\
q\in D_{\ell}\end{subarray}}^{Q}\mu(A_{q}\cap U)\right\rparen^{2}(1+o(1))

and by ?THM?
, it follows that

\mu(\limsup A_{q})\geqslant\mu(U)^{2}/(4\kappa)
.

Suppose there is no such
\ell
. For each
\ell\geqslant 0
, denote by

\Sigma_{\ell}
the sum (
4
) extended over
q\in D_{\ell}
. Then, by assumption,
\Sigma_{\ell}<\infty
for each

\ell\geqslant 0
and
\sum_{\ell\geqslant 0}\Sigma_{\ell}=\infty
. By ?THM?
,

(5)

\displaystyle\mu\left\lparen\bigcup_{q\in D_{\ell}}A_{q}\cap U\right\rparen

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{\left\lparen\sum_{q\in D_{\ell}}\mu(A_{q}\cap U)\right\rparen^{2}}{\sum_{q,r\in D_{\ell}}\mu(A_{q}\cap A_{r}\cap U)}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{\mu(U)^{2}}{4\kappa}\cdot\frac{\left\lparen\sum_{q\in D_{\ell}}\mu(A_{q})\right\rparen^{2}}{\left\lparen\sum_{q\in D_{\ell}}\mu(A_{q})\right\rparen^{2}(1+o(1))+2\sum_{q\in D_{\ell}}\mu(A_{q})\eta(q)}.

Now,

\sum_{q\in D_{\ell}}\mu(A_{q})\eta(q)\leqslant 2^{2(\ell+1)}h(2^{\ell+1})\Sigma_{\ell}\ll 2^{2(\ell+1)}h(2^{\ell})\Sigma_{\ell}

and

\left\lparen\sum_{q\in D_{\ell}}\mu(A_{q})\right\rparen^{2}\geqslant 2^{2\ell}h(2^{\ell})^{2}\Sigma_{\ell}^{2}.

By the convergence assumed in (
3
), it follows that

h(2^{\ell})\Sigma_{\ell}
gets arbitrarily large, and this implies that

2\sum_{q\in D_{\ell}}\mu(A_{q})\eta(q)=o\left[\left\lparen\sum_{q\in D_{\ell}}\mu(A_{q})\right\rparen^{2}\right],

hence

\displaystyle\mu\left\lparen\bigcup_{q\in D_{\ell}}A_{q}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{\mu(U)^{2}}{4\kappa}\frac{\left\lparen\sum_{q\in D_{\ell}}\mu(A_{q})\right\rparen^{2}}{\left\lparen\sum_{q\in D_{\ell}}\mu(A_{q})\right\rparen^{2}(1+o(1))}

for infinitely many
\ell
. Once again, it follows
that
\mu(\limsup A_{q})\geqslant\mu(U)^{2}/(4\kappa)
.
∎

In the proofs of ?THM?s
and
, once it is established that
W(\psi,\gamma)
has positive measure, it remains to show that its measure is
1
. Once again, this is a task that is easier for homogeneous problems than it is for inhomogeneous problems, because there are zero-one laws in the homogeneous setting
[
9
,
14
,

3.
Number theoretic statements

The main purpose of this section is to prove the following statement
relating the weights that will arise in the application
of ?THM?
to the weights appearing
in ?THM?

Lemma 11
.

For
d(n)
the number of divisors of
n
and

f:[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
any increasing function, one has

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\frac{1}{f(\log d(n))d(n)}\gg\frac{x}{f((\log 2)\log\log x)\sqrt{\log x}}.

Proof.

By a result of Kac
[
19
]
, for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{x}\#\left\{1\leqslant n\leqslant x:\log_{2}d(n)\leqslant\log\log x+y\sqrt{\log\log x}\right\}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{y}e^{-w^{2}/2}\,dw.

In particular, by setting
y=0
we see that

\#\underbrace{\left\{1\leqslant n\leqslant x:\log_{2}d(n)\leqslant\log\log x\right\}}_{A(x)}\sim\frac{x}{2}\quad(x\to\infty),

and it follows that for all
x
large enough we have

\sum_{n\in A(x)}\frac{1}{d(n)}\geqslant\frac{1}{3}\sum_{n\leqslant x}\frac{1}{d(n)}.

Observe then that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant x}\frac{1}{f(\log d(n))d(n)}

\displaystyle\quad\geqslant\sum_{n\in A(x)}\frac{1}{f(\log d(n))d(n)}

\displaystyle\quad\geqslant\frac{1}{f((\log 2)\log\log x)}\sum_{n\in A(x)}\frac{1}{d(n)}

\displaystyle\quad\geqslant\frac{1}{3}\frac{1}{f((\log 2)\log\log x)}\sum_{n\leqslant x}\frac{1}{d(n)}

It is known (see for example
[
26
, Theorem II.6.8]
) that

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\frac{1}{d(n)}\asymp\frac{x}{\sqrt{\log x}},

so we have

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\frac{1}{f(\log d(n))d(n)}\gg\frac{x}{f((\log 2)\log\log x)\sqrt{\log x}}

which finishes the proof.
∎

Finally, we make frequent use of the following standard fact.

Lemma 13
.

Suppose

is a decreasing function and

By partial summation,

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}g(k)(f(n)-f(n+1))+f(N)\sum_{k=1}^{N}g(k)

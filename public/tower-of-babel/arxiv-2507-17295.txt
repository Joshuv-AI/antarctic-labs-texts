Beyond symmetry protection: Robust feedback-enforced edge states in non-Hermitian stacked quantum spin Hall systems
Mengjie Yang, Ching Hua Lee

Abstract
Conventional wisdom holds that strongly coupling two QSH layers yields a trivial ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} phase and no protected topological edge states. We demonstrate that, in a regime with intermediate inter-layer coupling (neither in the strong or weak coupling regimes) and competitive non-Hermitian directed amplification, bulk modes are suppressed while arbitrary bulk excitations inevitably accumulate into robust helical edge transport modes — without relying on any symmetry protection. Our feedback-enforced mechanism persists over broad parameter ranges and remains robust even on fractal or irregular boundaries. These findings challenge the traditional view of stacked QSH insulators as inevitably trivial, and open up new avenues for designing helical topological devices that exploit feedback-enforced non-Hermitian engineering, instead of symmetry-enforced robustness.

Robustness against extreme boundary irregularities.—
To demonstrate the superior robustness of our feedback-enforced edge states, we apply our protocol to fractal geometries with extremely convoluted edges around thin bulk structures.
Wavepackets are initialized as bulk states at the fractal center, with propagation confined within fractal boundaries. Despite sharp turns and narrowly-separated edges, the edge states all exhibit very robust propagation with effective bulk suppression, in the face of varying boundary complexity across different fractal generations [Fig.

The edge occupation ratio evolution

confirms that after an initial phase of nascent bulk oscillations, which lasts longest in (a1) due to the thicker bulk, the state consistently becomes more and more exclusively located within the edge sites.

Discussion.—
In summary, we have identified and explicitly demonstrated a fundamentally new route towards robust helical edge transport by stacking oppositely NHSE-directed QSH layers at intermediate coupling, overcoming bulk instabilities and symmetry constraints that traditionally limit topological edge states. Though we presented a specific model, the underlying principles of feedback-enforced edge transport require only two ingredients: (i) intermediate inter-layer coupling between two QSH layers and (ii) two QSH layers with different directed non-Hermitian amplification. Our mechanism relies on non-Hermitian feedback-induced redirection and stabilization of bulk and edge states, rather than on topological invariants protected by symmetry.

By removing stringent symmetry and fine-tuning constraints that limits existing topological platforms, our symmetry-agnostic edge transport mechanism substantially expands the technological potential of non-Hermitian topological physics, opening up new avenues in edge-state-based technologies across photonics

Appendix S1
Criterion for a real bulk spectrum in the stacked-QSH model in double PBC

In our main text, we have shown that the double PBC bulk spectrum of the stacked-QSH model can be made real by tuning the inter-layer couplings
\mu
and
\nu
to be sufficiently large, i.e.,
\mu\nu\geq\gamma
[Fig. 1(c3)].

To understand the dashed curve plotted by
\mu\nu=\gamma
in Fig. 1(c3), we derive an analytical condition for the supression of the imaginary part of the spectrum in the stacked-QSH model in double PBC.
The key idea is that, for the parameters used in our numerics, the Bloch momentum that mostly likely leads to complex spectrum is
\mathbf{k}^{\star}=(\pi/2,0)
: here the Hermitian part of the single-layer Hamiltonian is minimal, while the non-Hermitian term
i\gamma\sigma_{y}
is maximal.

With
m=1
and
\mathbf{k}^{\star}=(\pi/2,0)
in the single-layer Chern Bloch Hamiltonian, i.e.,
H_{\text{Ch}}(\mathbf{k},m,\gamma)=\left(m+\cos k_{x}+\cos k_{y}\right)\sigma_{x}+\left(i\gamma+\sin k_{x}\right)\sigma_{y}+\sin k_{y}\sigma_{z}
, reduces to

H_{\text{Ch}}\bigl(\mathbf{k}^{\star}\bigr)=2\sigma_{x}+\bigl(1+i\gamma\bigr)\sigma_{y},

(S1)

which has eigenvalues

\varepsilon_{\pm}=\pm 2+i\gamma
.
The two eigenstates are thus separated by

\Delta E=2
in their
real
part and share the
same
gain
+i\gamma
.
After diagonalising the spin sector, reorder the four Chern layers as

\{|1\rangle_{+\gamma},|4\rangle_{+\gamma},|2\rangle_{-\gamma},|3\rangle_{-\gamma}\}
;
the Bogoliubov-de Gennes-like block form of the full
8\times 8
Hamiltonian becomes

\mathcal{H}=\begin{pmatrix}+i\gamma\,\openone_{2}&V\\
V&-i\gamma\,\openone_{2}\end{pmatrix},\qquad V=\mu\openone_{2}+\nu\sigma_{x}.

(S2)

The upper (lower) block carries gain (loss); their real parts differ by
\Delta E=2
. Applying a second-order Schrieffer-Wolff transformation,. we eliminate the non-Hermitian term
i\gamma\,\openone_{2}
in the upper block, yielding a Hamiltonian with smaller size,

H^{\prime}=+i\gamma\,\openone_{2}-\frac{1}{\Delta E}\,VV^{\dagger}\;=\;+i\gamma\,\openone_{2}\;\;\mathbf{-\;\mu\nu\,\sigma_{x}}\;+\mathcal{O}(\mu^{2})+\mathcal{O}(\nu^{2}).

(S3)

Discarding the overall real shift,
H^{\prime}
is a
\mathcal{P}T
-symmetric dimer

H_{\mathcal{P}T}=\begin{pmatrix}i\gamma&\mu\nu\\
\mu\nu&-i\gamma\end{pmatrix}.

For a
2\times 2

\mathcal{P}T
dimer the spectrum is purely real when the coupling exceeds the non-Hermitian strength, so the onset of a real spectrum is governed by

\boxed{\mu\nu\;\geq\;\gamma}.

(S4)

Eq. (
S4
) explains the hyperbolic boundary in Fig. 1(c3):
increasing either
\mu
or
\nu
alone reduces the maximum imaginary part, only when both are not too small does the bulk spectrum become real.

S4.1
Real spectrum in one QSH layer with parallel directional amplification

Now that we only consider the edge spectrum, we can retrieve only the edge dynamics signature from the QSH model in Fig.
S3
(a). The edge states evolving on the upper edge are described by the effective Hamiltonian as

H_{u}=\begin{pmatrix}k_{y}v-i\kappa v&\mu^{\prime}\\
\mu^{\prime}&-k_{y}v-i\kappa v\end{pmatrix},

(S5)

where
\kappa
is the skin depth of the edge states. It is noted that we also need to consider the lower edge, and the effective Hamiltonian for the lower edge is given by

H_{l}=\begin{pmatrix}-kv+i\kappa v&\mu^{\prime}\\
\mu^{\prime}&kv+i\kappa v\end{pmatrix}.

(S6)

Let the dynamics of an initial wavepacket
\Phi(t=0)=\left(\Phi^{\uparrow},\Phi^{\downarrow}\right)^{T}
in one period

T=2L_{x}/v,

(S7)

which can be expressed as
U_{T}\Phi(t=0)
.
L_{x}
is the length of the system in the
x
direction, i.e., the directional amplidication direction. It is noted that, here, we neglect the width of the system in the
y
direction, which does not affect the amplification dynamics, as the skin effect is only along the
x
direction.

The time evolution operator
U_{T}
can be expressed as

U(t)=\begin{cases}\exp(-iH_{u}t),&0<t<T/2\\
\exp(-iH_{l}(t-T/2))\exp(-iH_{u}T/2),&T/2<t<T.\end{cases}

(S8)

In this way, we can get the wavepacket after one period
T
as
\Phi(t=T)=U(t=T)\Phi(t=0)
. If we denote
\mu^{\prime 2}+k^{2}=\omega^{2}
, the final state
\Phi(t=T)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(T)

\displaystyle=\Phi^{\uparrow}\frac{\mu^{\prime 2}\cos(\omega T)+k^{2}}{\omega^{2}}+\Phi^{\downarrow}\left[\frac{\mu^{\prime}\left(1-e^{-i\omega T}\right)}{2\omega^{2}}\Bigl(k(1-e^{i\omega T})-\omega(1+e^{i\omega T})\Bigr)\right],

(S9)

\displaystyle\phi_{2}(T)

\displaystyle=\Phi^{\uparrow}\left[\frac{\mu^{\prime}\left(1-e^{-i\omega T}\right)}{2\omega^{2}}\Bigl(k(-1+e^{i\omega T})-\omega(1+e^{i\omega T})\Bigr)\right]+\Phi^{\downarrow}\frac{\mu^{\prime 2}\cos(\omega T)+k^{2}}{\omega^{2}}

In this way, the effective Hamiltonian for the edge states can be obtained as

H_{\text{eff}}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\mu^{\prime 2}\cos(\omega T)+k^{2}}{\omega^{2}}&\left[\frac{\mu^{\prime}\left(1-e^{-i\omega T}\right)}{2\omega^{2}}\Bigl(k(1-e^{i\omega T})-\omega(1+e^{i\omega T})\Bigr)\right]\\
\left[\frac{\mu^{\prime}\left(1-e^{-i\omega T}\right)}{2\omega^{2}}\Bigl(k(-1+e^{i\omega T})-\omega(1+e^{i\omega T})\Bigr)\right]&\frac{\mu^{\prime 2}\cos(\omega T)+k^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

(S10)

The eigenenergies of
H_{\text{eff}}
are

\displaystyle E_{\text{eff}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu^{\prime 2}\cos(\omega T)+k^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\pm\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{2\omega^{2}}\sqrt{\left[\omega(1+e^{i\omega T})\left(1-e^{-i\omega T}\right)\right]^{2}-\left[k(1-e^{i\omega T})\left(1-e^{-i\omega T}\right)\right]^{2}}.

(S11)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu^{\prime 2}\cos(\omega T)+k^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\pm\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{2\omega^{2}}\sqrt{4\omega^{2}\sin^{2}(\omega T)+4k^{2}\sin^{2}(\omega T)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu^{\prime 2}\cos(\omega T)+k^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\pm\frac{\mu^{\prime}\sin(\omega T)}{\omega^{2}}\sqrt{\omega^{2}+k^{2}}.

It is noted that both eigenenergies are real, then the edge states are not amplified. This explains why we see purely real edge spectrum in Fig. 1(b3) in the main text.

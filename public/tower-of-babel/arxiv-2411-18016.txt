On the small cap decoupling for the moment curve in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3}
Dominique Maldague, Changkeun Oh

Abstract
. This paper proves sharp small cap decoupling estimates for the moment curve ℳ n = { ( t , t 2 , … , t n ) : 0 ≤ t ≤ 1 } \mathcal{M}^{n}=\{(t,t^{2},\ldots,t^{n}):0\leq t\leq 1\} in the remaining small cap parameter ranges for ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} and ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} .



1.
Introduction

Small cap decoupling is an
L^{p}
estimate of exponential sums with frequencies lying in curved sets and satisfying a spacing condition (one frequency per small cap). The general setup was introduced in
[
DGW20
]
, where they proved sharp estimates for frequencies on curves in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and certain other special cases. In
[
DGW20
]
, the authors recorded a conjecture (Conjecture 2.5) about sharp small cap decoupling estimates for frequencies lying on the moment curve
\mathcal{M}^{n}=\{(t,t^{2},\ldots,t^{n}):0\leq t\leq 1\}
and they made partial progress on the
n=3
case. In
[
GM22
]
, Guth and Maldague proved a more general small cap decoupling result which fully resolved the
n=3
case of Conjecture 2.5 from
[
DGW20
]
. The main result of this paper is sharp small cap decoupling estimates for
\mathcal{M}^{3}
in the full range of parameters not addressed by Conjecture 2.5 in
[
DGW20
]
or in
[
GM22
]
. In particular, we have the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
\sigma\geq 0
and
p\geq 2
, we have

\Big(\int_{[0,1]^{2}\times[0,\frac{1}{N^{\sigma}}]}\Big|\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}e\big(x\cdot(k,k^{2},k^{3})\big)\Big|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C_{\epsilon}N^{\epsilon}D_{p}(\sigma,N)(\sum_{k=1}^{N}|a_{k}|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}

for any
a_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
, in which

D_{p}(\sigma,N)=\begin{cases}N^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sigma+1}{p}}+N^{1-\frac{7}{p}}&\qquad\text{if}\qquad 0\leq\sigma<5/2\\
N^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sigma+1}{p}}+N^{\frac{\sigma}{3}-\frac{7\sigma}{3p}}+N^{1-\frac{7}{p}}&\qquad\text{if}\qquad 5/2\leq\sigma<3\\
N^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sigma}{p}}+N^{1-\frac{3+\sigma}{p}}&\qquad\text{if}\qquad 3\leq\sigma\end{cases}.

Theorem
1.1
is sharp, up the
C_{\epsilon}N^{\epsilon}
factor. We demonstrate this in §
1.1
. Note that for
5/2\leq\sigma\leq 3
, we have

N^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sigma}{p}}+N^{\frac{\sigma}{3}+(1-\frac{7\sigma}{3})\frac{1}{p}}+N^{1-\frac{6}{p}}\sim\begin{cases}N^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sigma}{p}}\;\mathrm{for}\;p<\frac{8\sigma-6}{2\sigma-3}\\
N^{\frac{\sigma}{3}+(1-\frac{7\sigma}{3})\frac{1}{p}}\;\mathrm{\;for\;}\frac{8\sigma-6}{2\sigma-3}<p<7\\
N^{1-\frac{6}{p}}\;\mathrm{\;for\;}7<p\end{cases}

Conjecture 2.5 of
[
DGW20
]
is a special case of Theorem
1.1
for the range
0\leq\sigma\leq 2
. Bombieri-Iwaniec
[
BI86
]
and Bourgain
[
Bou17
]
resolved a special case of Theorem
1.1
for
\sigma=2
. Guth, Demeter, and Wang verified a special case of the analog of Theorem
1.1
in the range
0\leq\sigma\leq\frac{3}{2}
. Guth and Maldague proved a more general version of Theorem
1.1
in the range
0\leq\sigma\leq 2
, which covered the range of the parameter
\sigma
addressed by Conjecture 2.5 in
[
DGW20
]
.

If
\sigma\geq 3
, then the function
\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}e(x\cdot(k,k^{2},k^{3}))
is very slowly varying in the third coordinate. This means that
L^{p}([0,1]^{2}\times[0,N^{-\sigma}])
estimates for
\sigma\geq 3
are trivially controlled using Fubini’s theorem by the sharp estimates for
2
-dimensional exponential sums with frequencies on the parabola
[
BD15
]
. We therefore omit the proof of Theorem
1.1
in the range
\sigma\geq 3
.

Let us state a continuous setup for small cap decoupling. For convenience, we use the same notation in
[
GM22
]
. For
\beta\in[\frac{1}{3},\infty)
, define
\mathcal{M}^{3}(R^{\beta},R)
by

(1.1)

\{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}):\xi_{1}\in[0,1],|\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}^{2}|\leq R^{-2\beta},|\xi_{3}-3\xi_{1}\xi_{2}+2\xi_{1}^{3}|\leq R^{-1}\}.

We will partition this set into small caps of the form

(1.2)

\begin{split}\gamma:=\{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}):lR^{-\beta}\leq&\xi_{1}<(l+1)R^{-\beta},\\
&|\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}^{2}|\leq R^{-2\beta},|\xi_{3}-3\xi_{1}\xi_{2}+2\xi_{1}^{3}|\leq R^{-1}\}\end{split}

for some integer
l
,
0\leq l<R^{\beta}
. Denote by
\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}
the collection of
\gamma
’s. We are ready to state the small cap decoupling theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
1<\beta\leq 2
,
p\geq 2
and
\epsilon>0
, we have

(1.3)

\|f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}\big(R^{\beta(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}+R^{\beta(1-\frac{4}{p})-\frac{1}{p}}\big)\big(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for any
f
with Fourier transform supported in
\mathcal{M}^{3}(R^{\beta},R)
.

For
\beta>2
,
p\geq 2
, and
\epsilon>0
,

(1.4)

\|f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}\big(R^{\beta(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}+R^{(\beta-\frac{1}{3})(1-\frac{4}{p})}+R^{\beta(1-\frac{4}{p})-\frac{1}{p}}\big)\big(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for any
f
with Fourier transform supported in
\mathcal{M}^{3}(R^{\beta},R)
.

The exponents of
R
of (
1.3
) coincide when
p=6+2/\beta
.
For
\beta\geq 2
,

\max\{R^{\beta(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})},R^{\beta(1-\frac{4}{p})-\frac{1}{p}},R^{(\beta-\frac{1}{3})(1-\frac{4}{p})}\}=\begin{cases}R^{\beta(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\;\mathrm{for}\;p<6+\frac{2}{\beta}\\
R^{(\beta-\frac{1}{3})(1-\frac{4}{p})}\;\mathrm{\;for\;}6+\frac{2}{\beta}<p<7\\
R^{\beta(1-\frac{4}{p})-\frac{1}{p}}\;\mathrm{\;for\;}7<p\end{cases}

Finally, since this paper is about small cap decoupling in the remaining parameter ranges, we record the following proposition for the parabola.

Proposition 1.3
.

For
1\leq\sigma\leq 2
and
2\leq p<\infty
, we have

\Big(\int_{[0,1]\times[0,\frac{1}{N^{\sigma}}]}\Big|\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}e\big(x\cdot(k,k^{2})\big)\Big|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C_{\epsilon}N^{\epsilon}\big(N^{\frac{\sigma}{2}(1-\frac{4}{p})}+N^{1-\frac{4}{p}}\big)(\sum_{k=1}^{N}|a_{k}|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}

for any
a_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
.

Note that for the case
1\leq\sigma<2
the critical exponent is
p=4
.

1.1.
Sharpness of theorems

Let us show the sharpness of Theorem
1.1
. We will take three examples to show the sharpness. Let us begin with the example that
a_{k}=1
for all
k
. We will show that

N^{1-\frac{\max(3+\sigma,6)}{p}}\lesssim\Big(\int_{[0,1]^{2}\times[0,\frac{1}{N^{\sigma}}]}\Big|\sum_{k=1}^{N}e\big(x\cdot(k,k^{2},k^{3})\big)\Big|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

By evaluating the exponential sum on the smaller set
[0,N^{-1}]\times[0,N^{-2}]\times[0,N^{-\max(3,\sigma)}]
, we get the bound. The second example is a random example. We take a random sequence
a_{k}\in\{-1,1\}
, and this gives the bound

N^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sigma}{p}}\lesssim\Big(\int_{[0,1]^{2}\times[0,\frac{1}{N^{\sigma}}]}\Big|\sum_{k=1}^{N}e\big(x\cdot(k,k^{2},k^{3})\big)\Big|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The last example is a “lower dimensional” example. Take

(1.5)

a_{k}=\begin{cases}1\text{ for }0\leq k\leq N^{\sigma/3}\\
0\text{ otherwise }\end{cases}

Then we have

(1.6)

\begin{split}\mathrm{LHS}&\gtrsim N^{-\frac{\sigma}{p}}\Big(\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\Big|\sum_{k=1}^{N^{\sigma/3}}e\big(x\cdot(k,k^{2})\big)\Big|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&\gtrsim N^{-\frac{\sigma}{p}}\Big(\int_{[0,N^{-\sigma/3}]\times[0,N^{-2\sigma/3}]}\Big|\sum_{k=1}^{N^{\sigma/3}}e\big(x\cdot(k,k^{2})\big)\Big|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&\gtrsim N^{-\frac{(\frac{1}{3}+\frac{2}{3}+1)\sigma}{p}}N^{\frac{\sigma}{3}}\sim N^{-\frac{2\sigma}{p}}N^{\frac{\sigma}{3}}.\end{split}

The sharpness of Proposition
1.3
follows from analyzing the function
f(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}e(x\cdot(k,k^{2}))
. The integrand satisfies
|f(x)|^{p}\gtrsim N
on a box of dimensions
N^{-1}\times N^{-2}
centered at the origin, so

\text{LHS}\gtrsim N\cdot N^{-\frac{3}{p}}\sim N^{1-\frac{4}{p}}(\sum_{k=1}^{N}|1|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The other contribution to
LHS
comes from the
\mathcal{R}=[-N^{1-\sigma},N^{1-\sigma}]\times[-N^{-\sigma},N^{-\sigma}]
. Indeed, let
\eta
be a Schwartz function
\eta:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,\infty)
satisfying
|\eta|\sim 1
on
\mathcal{R}
,

\eta

⋀

is real-valued with
|\hbox{\hskip 8.33334pt\hskip-2.48265pt\hbox{$\eta$}\hskip-2.48265pt\hskip-8.33334pt\raisebox{15.30566pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{-1}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\kern-0.6pt\bigwedge\kern-0.6pt}}}}}$}}\hskip-8.33334pt\hskip 8.33334pt}|\sim N^{1-2\sigma}
on
\mathcal{R}^{*}=[-N^{\sigma-1},N^{\sigma-1}]\times[-N^{\sigma},N^{\sigma}]
, and
\textup{supp}\hbox{\hskip 8.33334pt\hskip-2.48265pt\hbox{$\eta$}\hskip-2.48265pt\hskip-8.33334pt\raisebox{15.30566pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{-1}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\kern-0.6pt\bigwedge\kern-0.6pt}}}}}$}}\hskip-8.33334pt\hskip 8.33334pt}\subset 2\mathcal{R}^{*}
.

Using
L^{2}
orthogonality, we have

\displaystyle\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}e(x\cdot(k,k^{2}))

\displaystyle\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{R})}^{2}\sim\int|\sum_{k=1}^{N}e(x\cdot(k,k^{2}))|^{2}\eta(x)dx

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}\hbox{\hskip 8.33334pt\hskip-2.48265pt\hbox{$\eta$}\hskip-2.48265pt\hskip-8.33334pt\raisebox{15.30566pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{-1}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\kern-0.6pt\bigwedge\kern-0.6pt}}}}}$}}\hskip-8.33334pt\hskip 8.33334pt}(k-k^{\prime},k^{2}-(k^{\prime})^{2})\sim N\cdot N^{\sigma-1}N^{1-2\sigma}.

Then since
2\leq p
, Hölder’s inequality gives,

\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}e(x\cdot(k,k^{2}))\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{R})}\leq|\mathcal{R}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}e(x\cdot(k,k^{2}))\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{R})}.

Combining the previous two inequalities yields

\text{LHS}\gtrsim|\mathcal{R}|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}N^{\frac{1-\sigma}{2}}\sim N^{\frac{\sigma}{2}(1-\frac{4}{p})}(\sum_{k=1}^{N}|1|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}},

which concludes the discussion of sharpness for Proposition
1.3
.

B_{A,B,C}

B_{A,B}

B_{A,B}

B_{R}(a)\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}

(1.12)

(1.13)

We use the notation

(1.14)

\mathrm{RapDec}(R)=R^{-1000}.

2.
Reduction to a multilinear decoupling inequality

We first reduce a global decoupling inequality to a local decoupling inequality. This argument is standard (for example, see
[
GM22
, Lemma 9]
), so we omit the proof.

Proposition 2.1
.

Theorem
1.2
follows from the following:

For
1\leq\beta\leq 2
,
p\geq 2
, and
\epsilon>0
,

(2.1)

\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R^{2\beta},R^{2\beta},R})}\leq C_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}\big(R^{\beta(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}+R^{\beta(1-\frac{4}{p})-\frac{1}{p}}\big)\big(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for any
f
with Fourier transform supported in
\mathcal{M}^{3}(R^{\beta},R)
.

For
\beta\geq 2
,
p\geq 2
, and
\epsilon>0
,

\begin{split}&\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R^{2\beta},R^{2\beta},R})}\\
&\leq C_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}\max\{R^{\beta(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})},R^{\beta(1-\frac{4}{p})-\frac{1}{p}},R^{(\beta-\frac{1}{3})(1-\frac{4}{p})}\}\big(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\end{split}

for any
f
with Fourier transform supported in
\mathcal{M}^{3}(R^{\beta},R)
.

Let
D_{p}(R)
be the smallest constant such that

(2.2)

\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R^{2\beta},R^{2\beta},R})}\leq D_{p}(R)\big(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for any
f
with Fourier transform supported in
\mathcal{M}^{3}(R^{\beta},R)
.
To state the multilinear decoupling constant, let us introduce transversality.
We say that three intervals
I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}\subset[0,1]
are
C
-transverse if

(2.3)

\min_{i,j:i\neq j}\mathrm{dist}(I_{i},I_{j})>C>0.

For
K\geq 1
, let
D_{p,\mathrm{mul}}(R,K^{-1})
be the smallest constant such that for any
K^{-1}
-transverse
I_{i}
it holds that

(2.4)

\Big\|\mathop{\mathchoice{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\displaystyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\displaystyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\textstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\textstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=8.00009pt}\cr$\scriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=6.00006pt}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{i=1}^{3}f_{i}\Big\|_{L^{p}(B_{R^{2\beta},R^{2\beta},R})}\leq D_{p,\mathrm{mul}}(R,K^{-1})\big(\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}}\|(f_{i})_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for any
f_{i}
which is a Fourier restriction of
f
to
I_{i}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
f
is a function whose Fourier support is in
\mathcal{M}^{3}(R^{\beta},R)
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\beta\geq 1
.
For given
\epsilon>0
, there exists
K\geq 1
such that

(2.5)

\begin{split}D_{p}(R)\leq&C_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}\sup_{1\leq R^{\prime}\leq R}D_{p,\mathrm{mul}}(R^{\prime},K^{-1})\\
&+C_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}\max\{R^{\beta(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})},R^{\beta(1-\frac{4}{p})-\frac{1}{p}},R^{(\beta-\frac{1}{3})(1-\frac{4}{p})}\}.\end{split}

for all
R\geq 1
. In particular, for
1\leq\beta\leq 2
, we have

(2.6)

\begin{split}D_{p}(R)\leq&C_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}\sup_{1\leq R^{\prime}\leq R}D_{p,\mathrm{mul}}(R^{\prime},K^{-1})\\
&+C_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}\max\{R^{\beta(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})},R^{\beta(1-\frac{4}{p})-\frac{1}{p}}\}.\end{split}

Proof.

A standard broad-narrow analysis gives

(2.7)

\begin{split}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R^{2\beta},R^{2\beta},R})}&\lesssim K^{100}\Big\|\mathop{\mathchoice{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\displaystyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\displaystyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\textstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\textstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=8.00009pt}\cr$\scriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=6.00006pt}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{i=1}^{3}f_{\tau_{i}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(B_{R^{2\beta},R^{2\beta},R})}\\
&+\big(\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{P}_{K^{-1}}}\|f_{\tau}\|_{L^{p}(B_{R^{2\beta},R^{2\beta},R})}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.\end{split}

Let us fix
\tau\in\mathcal{P}_{K^{-1}}
. For convenience, assume that
\tau
contains the origin.
We apply an affine transformation
T
mapping
\tau
to
[0,1]^{3}
. Define
\hat{g}(\xi):=\hat{f}(T^{-1}\xi)
. After this change of variables, we have

(2.8)

\|f_{\tau}\|_{L^{p}(B_{R^{2\beta},R^{2\beta},R})}\sim\|g\|_{L^{p}(B_{R^{2\beta}/K,R^{2\beta}/K^{2},R/K^{3}})}.

Note that

(2.9)

(R/K^{3})^{2\beta}\leq R^{2\beta}/K^{2}.

We apply the definition of
D_{p}(R)
and obtain

(2.10)

\|g\|_{L^{p}(B_{R^{2\beta}/K,R^{2\beta}/K^{2},R/K^{3}})}\leq D_{p}(R/K^{3})\big(\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{K^{3\beta}R^{-\beta}}}\|g_{\gamma^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

After changing back to the original variables, we obtain

(2.11)

\|f_{\tau}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim D_{p}(R/K^{3})\big(\sum_{\gamma^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{K^{3\beta-1}R^{-\beta}}}\|f_{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

By the decoupling for the parabola (
[
BD15
]
),

\|f_{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim_{\epsilon}K^{\epsilon}\max\{(K^{3\beta-1})^{1-\frac{4}{p}},(K^{3\beta-1})^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\}\big(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}:\gamma\subset\gamma^{\prime\prime}}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

To summarize,

\begin{split}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R^{2\beta},R^{2\beta},R})}&\lesssim_{\epsilon}K^{100}\Big\|\mathop{\mathchoice{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\displaystyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\displaystyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\textstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\textstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=8.00009pt}\cr$\scriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=6.00006pt}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{i=1}^{3}f_{\tau_{i}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(B_{R^{2\beta},R^{2\beta},R})}\\
&+K^{\epsilon}D_{p}(RK^{-3})\max\{(K^{3\beta-1})^{1-\frac{4}{p}},(K^{3\beta-1})^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\}\big(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.\end{split}

By the definition of
D_{p}(R)
, we have

(2.12)

D_{p}(R)\lesssim_{\epsilon}D_{p,\mathrm{mul}}(R,K^{-1})+K^{\epsilon}D_{p}(RK^{-3})\max\{(K^{3\beta-1})^{1-\frac{4}{p}},(K^{3\beta-1})^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\}.

We apply this inequality repeatedly, and obtain

(2.13)

D_{p}(R)\lesssim_{\epsilon}\sup_{1\leq R_{1}\leq R}D_{p,\mathrm{mul}}(R_{1},K^{-1})+R^{\epsilon}\max\{(R^{\beta-\frac{1}{3}})^{1-\frac{4}{p}},(R^{\beta-\frac{1}{3}})^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Let
I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}
be
C
-transverse. Define

(2.14)

U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}:=\big\{x\in B_{R^{2\beta},R^{2\beta},R}:|f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x)f_{3}(x)|^{\frac{1}{3}}\sim\alpha\big\}.

We will prove a level set estimate.

Proposition 2.3
.

For
p\geq 2
and for any
\alpha>0
, we have

\alpha^{p}|U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}|\leq C_{\epsilon}\max\{R^{p\beta(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})},R^{p\beta(1-\frac{4}{p})-1}\}R^{\epsilon}\sum_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\big(\sup_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{{p-4}}.

Proof that Proposition
2.3
implies Theorem
1.2
.

For
p\geq 2
, by a standard pigeonholing argument (for example, see
[
GM22
, Proposition 4]
), Proposition
2.3
implies

(2.15)

\alpha^{p}|U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}|\leq C_{\epsilon}\max\{R^{p\beta(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})},R^{p\beta(1-\frac{4}{p})-1}\}R^{\epsilon}\sum_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}.

Since this is true for any
\alpha>0
, this implies

\Big\|\mathop{\mathchoice{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\displaystyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\displaystyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\textstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\textstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=8.00009pt}\cr$\scriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=6.00006pt}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{i=1}^{3}f_{i}\Big\|_{L^{p}(B_{R^{2\beta},R^{2\beta},R})}^{p}\leq C_{\epsilon}\max\{R^{p\beta(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})},R^{p\beta(1-\frac{4}{p})-1}\}R^{\epsilon}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}.

By the definition of
D_{p,\mathrm{mul}}(R,K^{-1})
, this says that

(2.16)

D_{p,\mathrm{mul}}(R,K^{-1})\leq C_{\epsilon}\max\{R^{\beta(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})},R^{\beta(1-\frac{4}{p})-\frac{1}{p}}\}R^{\epsilon}.

Theorem
2.2
gives

(2.17)

D_{p}(R)\leq C_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}\max\{R^{\beta(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})},R^{\beta(1-\frac{4}{p})-\frac{1}{p}},R^{(\beta-\frac{1}{3})(1-\frac{4}{p})}\}.

This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Preliminary

Let us introduce some notations. Let
\beta\geq 1
. For technical reasons, let
\epsilon
be a small positive number such that
\beta/\epsilon\in\mathbb{N}
.
We introduce intermediate scales

(3.1)

R^{0}\leq R^{\epsilon}\leq R^{2\epsilon}\leq R^{3\epsilon}\leq\cdots\leq R^{(M-1)\epsilon}\leq R^{M\epsilon}

where
M
is a number such that
R^{M\epsilon}=R^{\beta}
. For simplicity, we introduce
R_{k}:=R^{k\epsilon}
. Note that
R_{M}=R^{\beta}
. Define
\mathcal{P}_{R_{k}^{-1}}
to be a collection of
\gamma_{k}
defined by

\begin{split}\gamma_{k}:=\{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}):lR_{k}^{-1}\leq&\xi_{1}<(l+1)R_{k}^{-1},\\
&|\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}^{2}|\leq R_{k}^{-2},|\xi_{3}-3\xi_{1}\xi_{2}+2\xi_{1}^{3}|\leq\max\{R_{k}^{-3},R^{-1}\}\}.\end{split}

Note that
\gamma_{M}=\gamma
where
\gamma
is defined in (
1.2
). Recall (
1.11
).

Definition 3.1
.

For each
k=0,1,\ldots,\epsilon^{-1}
,
define the square function

(3.2)

g_{k}(x):=\Big\|\sum_{\gamma_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{R_{k}^{-1}}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{2}\Big\|_{L^{1}(w^{\#}_{B_{R_{k}}(x)})},\;\;\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

For each
k=\epsilon^{-1}+1,\ldots,M-1
, define

(3.3)

g_{k}(x):=\Big\|\sum_{\gamma_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{R_{k}^{-1}}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{2}\Big\|_{L^{1}(w^{\#}_{B_{R_{k}}^{(2)}(x_{1},x_{2})}\times w^{\#}_{B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}^{(1)}(x_{3})})},\;\;\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
A
be a sufficiently large constant.
The high set is defined by

(3.4)

\Lambda_{M-1}:=\Big\{x\in B_{R^{2\beta}}^{(2)}\times B_{R}^{(1)}:A\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq g_{M-1}(x)\Big\}.

For each
k=1,\ldots,M-2
, we recursively define

(3.5)

\Lambda_{k}:=\Big\{x\in\big(B_{R^{2\beta}}^{(2)}\times B_{R}^{(1)}\big)\setminus\bigcup_{j=k+1}^{M-1}\Lambda_{j}:A^{M-k}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq g_{k}(x)\Big\}.

Define the low set by

(3.6)

\Lambda_{0}:=\big(B_{R^{2\beta}}^{(2)}\times B_{R}^{(1)}\big)\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{M-1}\Lambda_{j}.

3.1.
A high lemma

Let us state elementary lemmas and definitions.

Let
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a smooth function such that

(1)

\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subset[-10,10]^{2}

(2)

\rho=1
on the set
[-1,1]^{2}

Let
T_{k}
be the linear transformation mapping
[-R_{k}^{-1},R_{k}^{-1}]^{2}
to
[-1,1]^{2}
. Define

(3.7)

{\rho}_{\leq R_{k}^{-1}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}):={\rho}\big(T_{k}^{-1}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\big)

so that the support of
{\rho}_{\leq R_{k}^{-1}}
is contained in
[-10R_{k}^{-1},10R_{k}^{-1}]
. By a change of variables, we can see that
\|\widehat{\rho}_{\leq R_{k}^{-1}}\|_{L^{1}}\sim 1
.
For a Schwartz function
F:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, define

(3.8)

F*\widehat{\rho}_{>R_{k}^{-1}}(x):=F(x)-F*\widehat{\rho}_{\leq R_{k}^{-1}}(x),

where

\begin{split}F*\widehat{\rho}_{\leq R_{k}^{-1}}(x)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}F(x_{1}-y_{1},x_{2}-y_{2},x_{3})\widehat{\rho}_{\leq R_{k}^{-1}}(y_{1},y_{2})\,dy_{1}dy_{2}.\end{split}

This convolution is somewhat different from the one in the literature. The function
\hat{\rho}_{\leq R_{k}^{-1}}
is a function of varaibles
y_{1},y_{2}
, so the convolution involves only two variables.
Lastly, we define the high part of the square function. For
k=0,1,\ldots,\epsilon^{-1}
,

(3.9)

g_{k}^{h}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}):=\Big\|\sum_{\gamma_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{R_{k}^{-1}}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{2}*\widehat{\rho}_{>R_{k+1}^{-1}}\Big\|_{L^{1}(w^{\#}_{B_{R_{k}}(x)})}.

For
k=\epsilon^{-1}+1,\ldots,M-1
,

(3.10)

g_{k}^{h}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}):=\Big\|\sum_{\gamma_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{R_{k}^{-1}}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{2}*\widehat{\rho}_{>R_{k+1}^{-1}}\Big\|_{L^{1}(w^{\#}_{B_{R_{k}}^{(2)}(x_{1},x_{2})}\times w^{\#}_{B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}^{(1)}(x_{3})})}.

We have finished introducing the definitions.

Lemma 3.3

(cf. Corollary 1 of
[
GM22
]
)
.

Let
x\in\Lambda_{k}
for some
k=1,\ldots,M-1
. Then we have

(3.11)

g_{k}(x)\lesssim g_{k}^{h}(x).

Proof.

Let us consider the case that
k=M-1
.
Let
x\in\Lambda_{M-1}
. We write

(3.12)

\sum_{\gamma_{M-1}}|f_{\gamma_{M-1}}|^{2}=\sum_{\gamma_{M-1}}|f_{\gamma_{M-1}}|^{2}*\widehat{\rho}_{>R_{M}^{-1}}+\sum_{\gamma_{M-1}}|f_{\gamma_{M-1}}|^{2}*\widehat{\rho}_{\leq R_{M}^{-1}}.

By taking the integral on both sides, we have

g_{M-1}(x)\leq g_{M-1}^{h}(x)+\Big\|\sum_{\gamma_{M-1}}|f_{\gamma_{M-1}}|^{2}*\widehat{\rho}_{\leq R_{M}^{-1}}\Big\|_{L^{1}(w^{\#}_{B_{R_{M-1}}^{(2)}(x_{1},x_{2})}\times w^{\#}_{B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}^{(1)}(x_{3})})}.

For a contradiction, assume that

(3.13)

g_{M-1}(x)\lesssim\Big\|\sum_{\gamma_{M-1}}|f_{\gamma_{M-1}}|^{2}*\widehat{\rho}_{\leq R_{M}^{-1}}\Big\|_{L^{1}(w^{\#}_{B_{R_{M-1}}^{(2)}(x_{1},x_{2})}\times w^{\#}_{B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}^{(1)}(x_{3})})}.

By the
L^{2}
-orthogonality (cf.
[
GM22
, Lemma 4]
), for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we have

(3.14)

\Big||f_{\gamma_{M-1}}|^{2}*\widehat{\rho}_{\leq R_{M}^{-1}}(y)\Big|\lesssim\sum_{\gamma_{M}\subset\gamma_{M-1}}|f_{\gamma_{M}}|^{2}*\big|\widehat{\rho}_{\leq R_{M}^{-1}}\big|(y).

So, (
3.13
) is bounded by

(3.15)

\lesssim\Big\|\sum_{\gamma_{M}}|f_{\gamma_{M}}|^{2}*\big|\widehat{\rho}_{\leq R_{M}^{-1}}\big|\Big\|_{L^{1}(w^{\#}_{B_{R_{M-1}}^{(2)}(x_{1},x_{2})}\times w^{\#}_{B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}^{(1)}(x_{3})})}\lesssim\sum_{\gamma_{M}}\|f_{\gamma_{M}}\|_{\infty}^{2}.

In the last inequality, we used
\|\widehat{\rho}_{\leq R_{k}^{-1}}\|_{L^{1}}\sim 1
.
Since
R_{M}=R^{\beta}
, we have

(3.16)

g_{M-1}(x)\leq C\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}.

By the definition of
\Lambda_{M-1}
and
x\in\Lambda_{M-1}
,

(3.17)

A\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq g_{M-1}(x).

Since
A
is a sufficiently large number, this gives a contradiction.

Let us next consider the case that
\epsilon^{-1}+1\leq k\leq M-2
. By the definition of
\Lambda_{k}
and
x\in\Lambda_{k}
, we have

(3.18)

A^{M-k-1}\sum_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}>g_{k+1}(x),\;\;\;\;A^{M-k}\sum_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq g_{k}(x).

By following the arguments for the case
k=M-1
, we have

(3.19)

g_{k}(x)\leq Cg_{k+1}(x)<CA^{M-k-1}\sum_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}.

By the second inequality of (
3.18
), we have

(3.20)

A^{M-k}<CA^{M-k-1}.

This gives a contradiction as
A
is sufficiently large. The case that
0\leq k\leq\epsilon^{-1}
can be dealt with identically. We omit the details.
∎

A key property of the function
g_{k}^{h}
is that the Fourier supports of
\{|f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{2}*\hat{\rho}_{>R_{k+1}^{-1}}\}_{k}
are essentially disjoint. Combined with the
L^{2}
orthogonality we have the high lemma. To simplify the notation, we write
x=(x^{\prime},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 3.4

(High lemma)
.

Let
x=(x^{\prime},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
. For
k=0,1,\ldots,\epsilon^{-1}
,

\begin{split}&\int_{B^{(2)}_{(R_{k+1})^{2}}(x^{\prime})}|g_{k}^{h}(y^{\prime},x_{3})|^{2}\,dy^{\prime}\\
&\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big\|\sum_{\gamma_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{R_{k}^{-1}}}\big||f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{2}*\widehat{\rho}_{>R_{k+1}^{-1}}\big|^{2}\Big\|_{L^{1}(w_{B_{R_{k}}(y^{\prime},x_{3})}^{\#})}w_{B_{(R_{k+1})^{2}}^{(2)}(x^{\prime})}(y^{\prime})\,dy^{\prime}.\end{split}

For
k=\epsilon^{-1}+1,\ldots,M-1
,

\begin{split}&\int_{B^{(2)}_{(R_{k+1})^{2}}(x^{\prime})}|g_{k}^{h}(y^{\prime},x_{3})|^{2}\,dy^{\prime}\\
&\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big\|\sum_{\gamma_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{R_{k}^{-1}}}\big||f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{2}*\widehat{\rho}_{>R_{k+1}^{-1}}\big|^{2}\Big\|_{L^{1}(w_{B_{R_{k}}^{(2)}(y^{\prime})}^{\#}\times w_{B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}^{(1)}(x_{3})}^{\#})}w_{B_{(R_{k+1})^{2}}^{(2)}(x^{\prime})}(y^{\prime})\,dy^{\prime}.\end{split}

Proof.

Let us first consider the case that
k=0,1,\ldots,\epsilon^{-1}
.
By Hölder’s inequality,

(3.21)

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{(R_{k+1})^{2}}^{(2)}(x^{\prime})}|g_{k}^{h}(y^{\prime},x_{3})|^{2}\,dy^{\prime}\\
&\lesssim\int_{B_{(R_{k+1})^{2}}^{(2)}(x^{\prime})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big|\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{2}*\widehat{\rho}_{>R_{k+1}^{-1}}(z)\Big|^{2}w^{\#}_{B_{R_{k}}(y^{\prime},x_{3})}(z)\,dz\,dy^{\prime}.\end{split}

By a change of variables on
z
, this can be rewritten as

\int_{B_{(R_{k+1})^{2}}^{(2)}(x^{\prime})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big|\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{2}*\widehat{\rho}_{>R_{k+1}^{-1}}((y^{\prime},x_{3})-z)\Big|^{2}w^{\#}_{B_{R_{k}}(0)}(z)\,dz\,dy^{\prime}.

By Fubini’s theorem, this is equal to

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(\int_{B_{(R_{k+1})^{2}}^{(2)}(x^{\prime})}\Big|\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{2}*\widehat{\rho}_{>R_{k+1}^{-1}}((y^{\prime},x_{3})-z)\Big|^{2}\,dy^{\prime}\Big)w^{\#}_{B_{R_{k}}(0)}(z)\,dz.

By Plancherel’s theorem on the variables
y^{\prime}
, and disjointness of Fourier supports, this is bounded by

\begin{split}\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{\gamma_{k}}\Big||f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{2}*\widehat{\rho}_{>R_{k+1}^{-1}}((y^{\prime},x_{3})-z)\Big|^{2}w_{B_{(R_{k+1})^{2}}^{(2)}(x^{\prime})}(y^{\prime})\,dy^{\prime}\,w^{\#}_{B_{R_{k}}(0)}(z)\,dz.\end{split}

By Fubini’s theorem and a change of variables on
z
, this is equal to

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sum_{\gamma_{k}}\Big||f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{2}*\widehat{\rho}_{>R_{k+1}^{-1}}(z)\Big|^{2}\,w^{\#}_{B_{R_{k}}(0)}((y^{\prime},x_{3})-z)\,dzw_{B_{(R_{k+1})^{2}}^{(2)}(x^{\prime})}(y^{\prime})\,dy^{\prime}.

This can be rewritten as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big\|\sum_{\gamma_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{R_{k}^{-1}}}\big||f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{2}*\widehat{\rho}_{>R_{k+1}^{-1}}\big|^{2}\Big\|_{L^{1}(w_{B_{R_{k}}(y^{\prime},x_{3})}^{\#})}w_{B_{(R_{k+1})^{2}}^{(2)}(x^{\prime})}(y^{\prime})\,dy^{\prime}.

This completes the proof for the case
k=0,\ldots,\epsilon^{-1}
. The case
k=\epsilon^{-1}+1,\ldots,M-1
can be proved identically. We omit the proof here.
∎

We combine Lemma
3.3
and
3.4

Corollary 3.5
.

For

, we have

(3.22)

\sum_{B_{R_{k}}:B_{R_{k}}\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset}\int_{B_{R_{k}}}|g_{k}|^{2}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{4}.

Similarly, for
k=\epsilon^{-1}+1,\ldots,M-1
, we have

(3.23)

\sum_{(B_{R_{k}}^{(2)}\times B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}^{(1)})\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset}\int_{B_{R_{k}}^{(2)}\times B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}^{(1)}}|g_{k}|^{2}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{4}.

Proof.

Let us prove (
3.22
).
Suppose that
B_{R_{k}}\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset
. Take
x_{0}\in B_{R_{k}}\cap\Lambda_{k}
. For any

, by a property of

, we have

(3.24)

g_{k}(x)\lesssim g_{k}(x_{0})\lesssim g_{k}^{h}(x_{0})\lesssim g_{k}^{h}(x).

The second inequality follows by Lemma
3.3
. So we have

(3.25)

\begin{split}\sum_{B_{R_{k}}:B_{R_{k}}\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset}\int_{B_{R_{k}}}|g_{k}(x)|^{2}\,dx&\lesssim\int_{N_{(R_{k})^{2}}(\Lambda_{k})}|g_{k}^{h}(x)|^{2}\,dx\\
&\lesssim\int_{N_{(R_{k+1})^{2}}(\Lambda_{k})}|g_{k}^{h}(x)|^{2}\,dx.\end{split}

By applying Lemma
3.4
to a ball of radius
(R_{k+1})^{2}
, this is further bounded by

\sum_{B_{R_{k+1}}^{(2)}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big\|\sum_{\gamma_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{R_{k}^{-1}}}\big||f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{2}*\widehat{\rho}_{>R_{k+1}^{-1}}\big|^{2}\Big\|_{L^{1}(w_{B_{R_{k}}(y^{\prime},x_{3})}^{\#})}w_{B_{R_{k+1}}^{(2)}}(y^{\prime})\,dy^{\prime}dx_{3},

\sum_{B}w_{B}\lesssim 1
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sum_{\gamma_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{R_{k}^{-1}}}\big||f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{2}*\widehat{\rho}_{>R_{k+1}^{-1}}(z)\big|^{2}(w_{B_{R_{k}}(y^{\prime},x_{3})}^{\#}(z))\,dz\,dy^{\prime}dx_{3}.

By calculating the integral over

first, this is bounded by

By (

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sum_{\gamma_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{R_{k}^{-1}}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{4}.

This completes the proof. (

3.2.
Multilinear restriction/Kakeya inequalities

We say

are

for any

. The following theorem is a multlilinear restrictiton estimate for the moment curve. The proof is a simple application of Plancherel’s theorem (for example, see

Proposition 3.7

(Multilinear restriction estimate)
.

Let
C>0
and
\beta\geq 1
.
Let
I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}\subset[0,1]
be
C
-transverse. Then

(3.29)

\Big\|\mathop{\mathchoice{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\displaystyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\displaystyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\textstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\textstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=8.00009pt}\cr$\scriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=6.00006pt}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{i=1}^{3}f_{i}\Big\|_{L_{\#}^{6}(B_{R})}\lesssim_{C}\mathop{\mathchoice{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\displaystyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\displaystyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\textstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\textstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=8.00009pt}\cr$\scriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=6.00006pt}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{i=1}^{3}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}(w_{B_{R}}^{\#})}

for any functions

whose Fourier support is in
\mathcal{M}^{3}(R^{\beta},R)\cap(I_{i}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

We next state the bilinear Kakeya inequality. To state the inequality, let us introduce some notations.
Let
\mathbb{T}^{(2)}
be a collection of tubes in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with dimension

contained in a ball of radius

. We say

-separated if for any tubes

and

\angle(T_{1},T_{2})\geq\nu>0

where

is the angle between
the long directions of

and
T_{2}
.

Lemma 3.8

(Bilinear Kakeya inequality)
.

Let
\nu>0
and
R>1
. Suppose that
\mathbb{T}_{1},\mathbb{T}_{2}
are
\nu
-separated. Then

(3.31)

\fint_{B_{R^{2}}^{(2)}}\sum_{T_{1}}\sum_{T_{2}}\chi_{T_{1}}\chi_{T_{2}}\lesssim_{\nu}\fint_{B_{R^{2}}^{(2)}}\sum_{T_{1}}\chi_{T_{1}}\fint_{B_{R^{2}}^{(2)}}\sum_{T_{2}}\chi_{T_{2}}.

Proof.

By linearlity, it suffices to prove that

(3.32)

\fint_{B_{R^{2}}^{(2)}}\chi_{T_{1}}\chi_{T_{2}}\lesssim_{\nu}\fint_{B_{R^{2}}^{(2)}}\chi_{T_{1}}\fint_{B_{R^{2}}^{(2)}}\chi_{T_{2}}.

\fint_{B_{R^{2}}^{(2)}}\chi_{T_{1}}\chi_{T_{2}}\sim R^{-4}|T_{1}\cap T_{2}|\sim R^{-2}

(3.34)

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we give a proof of Theorem
1.2
. Recall that Theorem
1.2
follows from Proposition
2.3
. Recall the decomposition (Definition
3.2
)

(4.1)

B_{R^{2\beta}}^{(2)}\times B_{R}^{(1)}=\bigcup_{k=0}^{M-1}\Lambda_{k}.

Let
p_{c}:=6+2/\beta
.
Since
M=\beta/\epsilon
, it suffices to prove

(4.2)

\alpha^{p_{c}}|U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\leq C_{\epsilon}R^{p_{c}\beta(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{c}})}R^{\epsilon}\sum_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\big(\sup_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{{p_{c}-4}}

for all
k=0,1,\ldots,M-1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
0\leq k\leq M-1
.
Suppose that
U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset
. Then

(4.3)

\alpha\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}(R_{k})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\sum_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

Suppose
0\leq k\leq M-2
.
Let
x\in U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset
. Then we have

(4.4)

\alpha\lesssim|f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x)f_{3}(x)|^{\frac{1}{3}}\lesssim\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}(x)|.

Since
\#\gamma_{k}\lesssim R_{k}
,
by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, this is bounded by

(4.5)

(R_{k})^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}(x)|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By an application of Fourier uncertainty principle, and the definition of
\Lambda_{k}
,
this is further bounded by

(4.6)

\lesssim(R_{k})^{\frac{1}{2}}g_{k}(x)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}(R_{k})^{\frac{1}{2}}g_{k+1}(x)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}(R_{k})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\sum_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This completes the proof. The case
k=M-1
can be done similarly.
∎

One ingredient of the proof of Theorem
1.2
is the
L^{4}
reverse square function estimate for the parabola. As a corollary of it, we can obtain the following inequality.

Lemma 4.2
.

For every function
f
whose Fourier support is in
\mathcal{M}^{3}(R^{2},R)
, and for every
c\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

(4.7)

\|f\|_{L^{4}([0,R^{2}]^{2}\times\{c\})}\leq C_{p}\big\|(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-1}}}|f_{\gamma}|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\{c\})}.

We will estimate
|U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}|
. Our proofs are different whether

(4.8)

k=0,\;\;\;1\leq k<\epsilon^{-1},\;\;\;\mathrm{or}\;\;\;\epsilon^{-1}\leq k\leq\beta\epsilon^{-1}.

Let us start with the last case, which is the nontrivial case.

4.1.
Estimate for
|U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}|
for
\epsilon^{-1}\leq k\leq\beta\epsilon^{-1}

We may assume that
U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset
.
By the definition of
U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}
, we have the following inequality.

(4.9)

\alpha^{6}|U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\leq\sum_{B_{R}:B_{R}\cap U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset}\int_{B_{R}}\Big|\mathop{\mathchoice{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\displaystyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\displaystyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\textstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\textstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=8.00009pt}\cr$\scriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=6.00006pt}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{i=1}^{3}f_{i}\Big|^{6}.

We apply Proposition
3.7
and Hölder’s inequality, and this is bounded by

(4.10)

\begin{split}\lesssim\sum_{B_{R}:B_{R}\cap U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset}\mathop{\mathchoice{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\displaystyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\displaystyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\textstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\textstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=8.00009pt}\cr$\scriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=6.00006pt}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{i=1}^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-1}}:\theta\subset I_{i}}|f_{\theta}(x)|^{2}\big)^{3}w_{B_{R}}(x)\,dx.\end{split}

Since for given
\theta\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-1}}
the number of
\gamma_{k}\subset\theta
is bounded by
R_{k}R^{-1}
,
we have

(4.11)

\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-1}}}|f_{\theta}(x)|^{2}\lesssim R_{k}R^{-1}\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}(x)|^{2}.

Let
y\in B_{R}\cap U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset
. Let
B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}
be the a
CR^{1+\epsilon}
-neighborhood of the ball
B_{R}
. Note that by a rapid decay of
w_{B_{R}}
outside of the ball
B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}
, the main contribution of (
4.10
) comes from the integral over
B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}
. For
x\in B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}
, by Fourier uncertainty principle, we have

(4.12)

\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}(x)|^{2}\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}g_{k+1}(y)\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}\sum_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}.

Combining these two, we have

(4.13)

\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-1}}}|f_{\theta}(x)|^{2}\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}R_{k}R^{-1}\sum_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}

for any
x\in B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}
. By applying this inequality to (
4.10
), we have

(4.14)

\begin{split}&\alpha^{6}|U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\\
&\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}R_{k}R^{-1}\sum_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}\sum_{B_{R}:B_{R}\cap U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset}\mathop{\mathchoice{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\displaystyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\displaystyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\textstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\textstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=8.00009pt}\cr$\scriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=6.00006pt}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{i=1}^{3}\int_{B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}}\big(\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-1}}:\theta\subset I_{i}}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{2}.\end{split}

For any sequences
A_{i},B_{i},C_{i}
, by Hölder’s inequality, we have

(4.15)

\sum_{i}(A_{i}B_{i}C_{i})^{\frac{1}{3}}\lesssim\big(\sum(A_{i}B_{i})^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{\frac{1}{3}}\big(\sum(A_{i}C_{i})^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{\frac{1}{3}}\big(\sum(B_{i}C_{i})^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{\frac{1}{3}}.

Hence, by abusing a notation, we may say that (
4.14
) is bounded by

(4.16)

\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}R_{k}R^{-1}\sum_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}\sum_{B_{R}:B_{R}\cap U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset}\mathop{\mathchoice{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\displaystyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\displaystyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\textstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\textstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=8.00009pt}\cr$\scriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=6.00006pt}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{i=1}^{2}\int_{B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}}\big(\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-1}}:\theta\subset I_{i}}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{2}.

We will show that

(4.17)

\begin{split}\sum_{B_{R}:B_{R}\cap U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset}\mathop{\mathchoice{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\displaystyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\displaystyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\textstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\textstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=8.00009pt}\cr$\scriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=6.00006pt}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{i=1}^{2}\int_{B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}}\big(\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-1}}:\theta\subset I_{i}}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{2}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{4}.\end{split}

Assuming this, let us finish the proof of (
4.2
). By (
4.14
), (
4.16
), (
4.17
), we have

(4.18)

\begin{split}\alpha^{6}|U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}|&\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}R_{k}R^{-1}\sum_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}\int\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{4}\\
&\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}R_{k}R^{-1}R^{\beta}\sup_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}\int\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{4}.\end{split}

Note that
\#(\gamma\subset\gamma_{k})\lesssim R^{\beta}/R_{k}
. So
by Lemma
4.2
and Hölder’s inequality, this is further bounded by

(4.19)

\begin{split}&\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}R_{k}R^{-1}R^{\beta}(\frac{R^{\beta}}{R_{k}})\sup_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}\int\sum_{\gamma}|f_{\gamma}|^{4}\\
&\sim R^{O(\epsilon)}R^{-1}R^{2\beta}\sup_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}\int\sum_{\gamma}|f_{\gamma}|^{4}.\end{split}

Recall that
p_{c}=6+2/\beta
and

(4.20)

p_{c}\beta(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{c}})=1+2\beta.

Hence, to prove (
4.2
), what we need to check is

(4.21)

\alpha^{2/\beta}\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}R^{2}\big(\sup_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}\big)^{\frac{2}{\beta}}.

By Lemma
4.1
,

(4.22)

\alpha\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}(R_{k}R^{\beta})^{\frac{1}{2}}\sup_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}R^{\beta}\sup_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty},

and this verifies (
4.21
).

We have proved (
4.2
) under the assumption of (
4.17
). It remains to prove (
4.17
).
To proceed, we pass by the intermediate scale
{R_{k}}
.
We claim that

(4.23)

\begin{split}\sum_{B_{R}\subset B_{R_{k}}^{(2)}\times B_{R}^{(1)}}\Big(\mathop{\mathchoice{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\displaystyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\displaystyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\textstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\textstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=8.00009pt}\cr$\scriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=6.00006pt}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{i=1}^{2}\int_{B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}}\big(\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-1}}:\theta\subset I_{i}}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{2}\Big)\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}\int_{B_{R_{k}}^{(2)}\times B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}^{(1)}}|g_{k}|^{2}.\end{split}

Let us assume the claim and continue to prove (
4.17
). By (
4.23
),

(4.24)

\begin{split}&\sum_{B_{R}:B_{R}\cap U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset}\mathop{\mathchoice{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\displaystyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\displaystyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\textstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\textstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=8.00009pt}\cr$\scriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=6.00006pt}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{i=1}^{2}\int_{B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}}\big(\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-1}}:\theta\subset I_{i}}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{2}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{(B_{R_{k}}^{(2)}\times B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}^{(1)})\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset}\int_{B_{R_{k}}^{(2)}\times B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}^{(1)}}|g_{k}|^{2}.\end{split}

By Corollary
3.5
, this is bounded by

(4.25)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{4}.

This completes the proof of (
4.17
).

(4.26)

\begin{split}\int_{B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}}\big(\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-1}}:\theta\subset I_{i}}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{2}\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}\int_{B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}}\big(\sum_{\theta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-1-\epsilon}}:\theta^{\prime}\subset I_{i}}|f_{\theta^{\prime}}|^{2}\big)^{2}.\end{split}

By the definition of

, we have

\begin{split}\theta^{\prime}=\{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}):lR^{-1-\epsilon}\leq&\xi_{1}<(l+1)R^{-1-\epsilon},\\
&|\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}^{2}|\leq R^{-2-2\epsilon},|\xi_{3}-3\xi_{1}\xi_{2}+2\xi_{1}^{3}|\leq R^{-1}\}\end{split}

for some

-axis. Then by an uncertainty principle, on the set

|f_{\theta^{\prime}}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})|^{2}=\sum_{T_{\theta^{\prime}}}|a_{\theta^{\prime},i}|\chi_{T_{\theta^{\prime}}}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}),

where

x_{3}
-axis. For simplicity, let us first consider the case that
R_{k}\leq R^{2+2\epsilon}
as a toy case. Then

(4.28)

\begin{split}&\sum_{B_{R}\subset B_{R_{k}}^{(2)}\times B_{R}^{(1)}}\Big(\mathop{\mathchoice{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\displaystyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\displaystyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\textstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\textstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=8.00009pt}\cr$\scriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=6.00006pt}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{i=1}^{2}\int_{B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}}\big(\sum_{\theta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-1-\epsilon}}:\theta^{\prime}\subset I_{i}}|f_{\theta^{\prime}}|^{2}\big)^{2}\Big)\\
&\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}R\sum_{B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}^{(2)}\subset B_{R_{k}}^{(2)}}\int_{B_{R^{1+\epsilon}}^{(2)}}\big(\mathop{\mathchoice{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\displaystyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\displaystyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\textstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\textstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=8.00009pt}\cr$\scriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=6.00006pt}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{\theta^{\prime},T_{\theta^{\prime}}}|a_{\theta^{\prime},i}|\chi_{T_{\theta^{\prime}}}(x_{1},x_{2},0)\big)^{2}\,dx_{1}dx_{2}\\

For convenience, we write the last inequality by

(4.29)

R^{O(\epsilon)}R(R_{k})^{2}\fint_{B_{R_{k}}^{(2)}}\big(\mathop{\mathchoice{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\displaystyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\displaystyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\textstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\textstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=8.00009pt}\cr$\scriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=6.00006pt}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{\theta^{\prime},T_{\theta^{\prime}}}|a_{\theta^{\prime},i}|^{2}\chi_{T_{\theta^{\prime}}}(x_{1},x_{2},0)\big)^{2}dx_{1}dx_{2}.

Note that the set

\{(x_{1},x_{2}):(x_{1},x_{2},0)\in T_{\theta^{\prime}}\}\cap B_{R_{k}}^{(2)}

\begin{split}&\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}R(R_{k})^{2}\big(\mathop{\mathchoice{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\displaystyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\displaystyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\textstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\textstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=8.00009pt}\cr$\scriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=6.00006pt}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{i=1}^{2}\fint_{B_{R_{k}}^{(2)}}\sum_{\theta^{\prime}\subset I_{i}}|f_{\theta^{\prime}}|^{2}\big)^{2}\\

4.23
). We now suppose that

4.2.
Estimate for
|U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}|
for

We start with

(4.36)

\alpha^{6}|U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\leq\sum_{B_{R_{k-1}}:B_{R_{k-1}}\cap U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset}\int_{B_{R_{k-1}}}\Big|\mathop{\mathchoice{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\displaystyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\displaystyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\textstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=11.00012pt}\cr$\textstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=8.00009pt}\cr$\scriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}{{\vphantom{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\smash{\vrule height=1.0pt,depth=6.00006pt}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\prod$\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{i=1}^{3}f_{i}\Big|^{6}.

We apply Proposition
3.7
, and this gives

(4.37)

\lesssim\sum_{B_{R_{k-1}}:B_{R_{k-1}}\cap U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(\sum_{\gamma_{k-1}}|f_{\gamma_{k-1}}(x)|^{2}\big)^{3}w_{B_{R_{k-1}}}(x)\,dx.

By Hölder’s inequality,

(4.38)

\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}\sum_{B_{R_{k-1}}:B_{R_{k-1}}\cap U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}(x)|^{2}\big)^{3}w_{B_{R_{k-1}}}(x)\,dx.

The weight function
w_{B_{R_{k-1}}}
decays fast outside of
B_{R_{k}}
. So we have

(4.39)

\begin{split}\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}\sum_{B_{R_{k-1}}:B_{R_{k-1}}\cap U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset}&\int_{B_{R_{k}}}\big(\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}(x)|^{2}\big)^{3}\,dx\\
&+\mathrm{Rapdec}(R)(\sum_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2})^{3}.\end{split}

By doing some trivial estimate,
the first term of (
4.39
) is further bounded by

(4.40)

\begin{split}\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}\sum_{B_{R_{k-1}}:B_{R_{k-1}}\cap U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset}&\Big\|\sum_{\gamma_{k+1}}|f_{\gamma_{k+1}}|^{2}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R_{k+1}})}\\
&\times\int_{B_{R_{k}}}\big(\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}(x)|^{2}\big)^{2}\,dx.\end{split}

Suppose that
B_{R_{k-1}}\cap U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset
. Let
x_{0}
be an element of the set. Then by Lemma
3.6
and the definition of
\Lambda_{k}
, we have

(4.41)

\Big\|\sum_{\gamma_{k+1}}|f_{\gamma_{k+1}}|^{2}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R_{k+1}})}\lesssim\Big\|\sum_{\gamma_{k+1}}|f_{\gamma_{k+1}}|^{2}\Big\|_{L^{1}(w_{B_{R_{k+1}}}^{\#}(x_{0}))}\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}.

By this inequality, (
4.40
) is bounded by

(4.42)

\begin{split}\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}\sum_{B_{R_{k-1}}:B_{R_{k-1}}\cap U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset}\int_{B_{R_{k}}}\big(\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}(x)|^{2}\big)^{2}\,dx.\end{split}

By Lemma
3.6
,

(4.43)

\begin{split}\int_{B_{R_{k}}}\big(\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}(x)|^{2}\big)^{2}\,dx&\lesssim|B_{R_{k}}|\Big\|\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{2}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R_{k}})}^{2}\\
&\lesssim|B_{R_{k}}|\Big\|\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{2}\Big\|_{L^{1}(w_{B_{R_{k}}}^{\#}(x_{0}))}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\int_{B_{R_{k}}}|g_{k}(x)|^{2}\,dx,\end{split}

where
x_{0}
is an element in the ball
B_{R_{k}}
. Hence, (
4.42
) is bounded by

(4.44)

\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}\sum_{B_{R_{k}}:B_{R_{k}}\cap\Lambda_{k}\neq\emptyset}\int_{B_{R_{k}}}|g_{k}|^{2}.

We now apply Corollary
3.5
, and obtain

(4.45)

\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|f_{\gamma_{k}}|^{4}.

By Lemma
4.2
, and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, this is bounded by

(4.46)

\begin{split}&\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sum_{\gamma_{k}}\Big|\sum_{\gamma\subset\gamma_{k}}|f_{\gamma}|^{2}\Big|^{2}\\
&\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}R^{\beta}R_{k}^{-1}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}\sum_{\gamma}\int|f_{\gamma}|^{4}.\end{split}

Here we used that the number of
\gamma
in
\gamma_{k}
is bounded by
O(R^{\beta}R_{k}^{-1})
.

To summarize, we have obtained

(4.47)

\begin{split}\alpha^{6}|U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}|&\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}R^{\beta}R_{k}^{-1}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}}\int|f_{\gamma}|^{4}\\
&\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}R^{2\beta}R_{k}^{-1}\sup_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{R^{-\beta}}}\int|f_{\gamma}|^{4}\end{split}

By Lemma
4.1
,

(4.48)

\alpha\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}(R_{k})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\sum_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}(R_{k})^{\frac{1}{2}}R^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\sup_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}.

Combining these two, we have

\alpha^{6+2/\beta}|U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lesssim(R_{k})^{\frac{1}{\beta}}RR^{O(\epsilon)}R^{2\beta}R_{k}^{-1}\sup_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2+\frac{2}{\beta}}\sum_{\gamma}\int|f_{\gamma}|^{4}.

To prove (
4.2
), it suffices to check

(4.49)

(R_{k})^{\frac{1}{\beta}-1}R^{2\beta+1}\lesssim R^{(3\beta+1)-\beta}.

This is true by the assumption that
\beta\geq 1
.

4.3.
Estimate for
|U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{k}|
for

By Lemma
4.1
,

(4.50)

\alpha\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}(\sum_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}R^{\beta/2}\sup_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}.

On the other hand, Lemma
4.2
, and Hölder’s inequality,

(4.51)

\begin{split}\alpha^{4}|U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{0}|&\lesssim\int|f|^{4}\\
&\lesssim\int\Big|\sum_{\gamma}|f_{\gamma}|^{2}\Big|^{2}\lesssim R^{\beta}\sum_{\gamma}\int|f_{\gamma}|^{4}.\end{split}

Combining
(

) and (

) gives

\alpha^{6+\frac{2}{\beta}}|U_{\alpha,\mathrm{mul}}\cap\Lambda_{0}|\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}R^{\beta}R^{\frac{\beta}{2}(2+\frac{2}{\beta})}\big(\sup_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}\big)^{2+\frac{2}{\beta}}\sum_{\gamma}\int|f_{\gamma}|^{4}.

This gives (

).

5.
Proof of Proposition
1.3

Proposition
1.3
follows from the small cap decoupling theorem (Theorem 3.1) of
[
DGW20
]
, which we describe using our notation presently.

Theorem 5.1

(Theorem 3.1 of
[
DGW20
]
)
.

Let
\beta\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
and consider
R^{-\beta}\times R^{-1}
small caps
\gamma
. Let
2\leq p
. Then

\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}^{p}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}[R^{p\beta-2\beta-1}+R^{(\frac{p}{2}-1)\beta}](\sum_{\gamma}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p})^{p/2}

for any Schwartz function
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
with Fourier transform supported in the
R^{-1}
-neighborhood of
\{(t,t^{2}):0\leq t\leq 1\}
.

Theorem 3.1 of
[
DGW20
]
applies to more general convex curves, as does Proposition
1.3
, but we focus on the parabola to simplify notation.

Proof of Proposition
1.3
.

In Proposition
1.3
, perform the change of variables
x\mapsto(N^{-1}x_{1},N^{-2}x_{2})
, so that we need to prove that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\int_{[0,N]\times[0,N^{2-\sigma}]}|\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}e(x

\displaystyle\cdot(\frac{k}{N},\frac{k^{2}}{N^{2}}))|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\epsilon}N^{\epsilon}(N^{\frac{\sigma}{2}(p-4)+3}+N^{p-1})\sum_{k=1}^{N}|a_{k}|^{p}.

Note that
N^{2-\sigma}<N
. Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,1]
be a smooth Schwartz function satisfying
\psi\sim 1
on
[0,N]\times[0,N^{2-\sigma}]
and having Fourier transform supported in
[0,N^{-1}]\times[0,N^{\sigma-2}]
. Let
\{\tau\}
be a collection of
\sim N^{\sigma-2}
intervals which partition
[0,1]
and let
f_{\tau}(x)=\psi(x)\sum_{\frac{k}{N}\in\tau}a_{k}e(x\cdot(\frac{k}{N},\frac{k^{2}}{N^{2}}))
. We will show (
5.1
) in two cases:
2\leq p\leq 4
and then
p\geq 4
. For
2\leq p\leq 4
, the small cap decoupling theorem for the parabola (Theorem 3.1 of
[
DGW20
]
) says that the left hand side of (
5.1
) is bounded by a
C_{\epsilon}N^{\epsilon}
factor of

(N^{2-\sigma})^{(\frac{p}{2}-1)}\sum_{\tau}\|f_{\tau}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p}.

Analyze each
\|f_{\tau}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p}
:

\displaystyle\|f_{\tau}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\psi(x)\sum_{\frac{k}{N}\in\tau}a_{k}e(x\cdot(\frac{k}{N},\frac{k^{2}}{N^{2}}))|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq(\sum_{\frac{k}{N}\in\tau}|a_{k}|)^{p-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\psi(x)\sum_{\frac{k}{N}\in\tau}a_{k}e(x\cdot(\frac{k}{N},\frac{k^{2}}{N^{2}}))|^{2}dx.

The Fourier supports of the summands are disjoint, so the final line above is bounded using
L^{2}
orthogonality by

(\sum_{\frac{k}{N}\in\tau}|a_{k}|)^{p-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{\frac{k}{N}\in\tau}|a_{k}|^{2}|\psi(x)|^{2}dx\sim(\sum_{\frac{k}{N}\in\tau}|a_{k}|)^{p-2}(\sum_{\frac{k}{N}\in\tau}|a_{k}|^{2})N^{3-\sigma}.

By Hölder’s inequality, using that the number of
\frac{k}{N}\in\tau
is
\lesssim N^{\sigma-1}
, the right hand side above is bounded by
(N^{\sigma-1})^{(p-2)}\sum_{\frac{k}{N}\in\tau}|a_{k}|^{p}N^{3-\sigma}
. Combining this bound for each
\|f_{\tau}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p}
with the result of small cap decoupling gives

\text{L.H.S. of \eqref{needtoshow}}\lesssim_{\epsilon}N^{\epsilon}N^{\frac{\sigma}{2}p-2\sigma+3}\sum_{k}|a_{k}|^{p},

as desired. It remains to check the
p\geq 4
case. Here, we rely on the
p=4
case that we just justified:

L.H.S. of (
5.1
)

\displaystyle\leq(\sum_{k}|a_{k}|)^{p-4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\psi(x)\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}e(x\cdot(\frac{k}{N},\frac{k^{2}}{N^{2}}))|^{4}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\epsilon}N^{\epsilon}(\sum_{k}|a_{k}|)^{p-4}.

This completes the proof.

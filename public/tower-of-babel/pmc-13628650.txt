Distinct responses of COL17A1 nonsense variants to readthrough drugs and NOG as a novel enhancer in junctional epidermolysis bullosa
Authors: Yan Tan, Haifa El Mabrouk, Rui Zhang, Sato Magassa, Cristina Has, Saliha Beyza Sayar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Junctional epidermolysis bullosa (JEB) caused by COL17A1 pathogenic variants (JEB-C17) is characterized by skin fragility, chronic wounds, and an increased risk of squamous cell carcinoma. Approximately 20% of cases are caused by nonsense mutations, which induce premature termination codons (PTCs) resulting in truncated protein production and/or trigger mRNA degradation through nonsense-mediated decay (NMD) pathway. Different approaches have been suggested for nonsense mutation suppression including translational readthrough- inducing drugs (TRIDs) therapy, which is extensively examined in hereditary skin diseases due to relatively low costs for translation into the clinic. We evaluated a panel of TRIDs in patient-derived JEB-C17 keratinocytes carrying different COL17A1 nonsense variants. We observed variable responses to TRIDs across mutations. N-oxalylglycine (NOG) has been shown to reduce eRF1 hydroxylation and consequently inhibit NMD. We investigated NOG as an enhancer of aminoglycoside-mediated readthrough for JEB-C17. We previously developed a cocktail consisting of aminoglycosides, NMD inhibitors as enhancers of readthrough activity, and antioxidants that act as anti-inflammatory agents. In this study, we explored the effect of NOG as a readthrough enhancer in combination with TRIDs or TRID-cocktail. Our results support mutation-dependent TRID selection for further preclinical scientific investigations and highlight personalized therapeutic approaches as a key strategy for JEB-C17.

Introduction

Junctional epidermolysis bullosa (JEB) caused by COL17A1 pathogenic variants (JEB-C17) is primarily characterized by skin blistering, leading to wounds that may become chronic and progress to squamous cell carcinomas, which are life-threatening.1 Current management remains largely symptomatic, focusing on wound care and supportive measures, while effective curative therapies are still lacking.2 Although in vivo gene therapies have been approved for dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa, comparable approaches have not yet been developed for JEB, partly due to its ultra-rare nature and limited interest from pharmaceutical companies.2,3 Prime editing and other RNA or DNA editing strategies are in preclinical development.4,5

Approximately 20% of JEB-C176,7 cases are caused by nonsense mutations which induce premature termination codons (PTCs) resulting in truncated protein production (often nonfunctional or partially functional) and/or trigger mRNA degradation through the surveillance pathway nonsense-mediated decay (NMD) which prevents harmful truncated protein production.8,9,10 Translational readthrough-inducing drug (TRID) therapy is one of the approaches that has been suggested for a nonsense mutation suppression therapy alongside tRNA suppressor, RNA editing, antisense-oligos, and CRISPR technology.11,12,13 Due to relatively low costs for translation into the clinic, TRID therapy is extensively examined in hereditary skin diseases.14

We have showed that aminoglycosides induced readthrough activity on JEB-W464∗ and induce restoration of less than 1% of collagen XVII (C17) relative to normal human keratinocytes (NHKs), only at high concentrations with significant offtarget effects on gene expression.7 In our previous work, we developed an aminoglycosides-based cocktail (TRID-C5). It consists of low doses of gentamicin (G) and paromomycin (P) with NMD inhibitors, CC-90009, NMDI-14, to enhance the readthrough activity, and the antioxidants, apocynin, melatonin, act as anti-inflammatory agents.15 TRID-C5 induced C17 readthrough in both primary and immortalized JEB-W464∗ keratinocytes,15 indicating that observed effects were comparable in primary and immortalized cells.

In this study, we evaluated a panel of TRIDs across six COL17A1 nonsense variants to select effective compound for TRID cocktail development. We used patient-derived immortalized keratinocytes, which provided a stable and disease-relevant experimental model.16 Amlexanox,17 ataluren (PTC124),18 G, P,19,20,21,22 ELX-02,23 clitocine,13 artesunate, atazanavir, and erythromycin24,25,26 that had already been shown to promote readthrough activity in other subtype of JEB. 2,6-diaminopurine27 and clitocine28,29 previously demonstrated efficacy in other nonsense mutation models such as cystic fibrosis, Duchenne muscular dystrophy, and cancer. Moreover, N-oxalylglycine (NOG) has been reported to inhibit NMD via reduction of eRF1 hydroxylation in a cancer reporter cell line.30,31 We explored the effect of NOG as a readthrough enhancer in combination with TRIDs or TRID-cocktail. The aims of our study are to determine the most effective TRID(s) and to optimize the TRID-cocktail, thereby providing the preclinical scientific basis for development of the therapies for JEB-C17.

TRID screening in JEB-C17 keratinocytes

We evaluated a panel of TRIDs, which are amlexanox, artesunate, atazanavir, clitocine, 2,6-diaminopurine, ELX-02, erythromycin, G, P, and PTC124 in the above characterized JEB-C17 keratinocytes (Table 1). High concentrations of G (1 μg/μL) and P (1 μg/μL) were initially used for screening to maximize the readthrough effect.7 Cells were treated with individual compounds for two consecutive days and harvested at 48 h. Therefore, a lower concentration of G 0.25 μg/μL was selected for subsequent experiments. In contrast, JEB-R169∗ or -G803∗ keratinocytes maintained normal morphology following treatment with G at 1 μg/μL (Figure S1B). JEB-W464∗ showed robust responses to high doses aminoglycosides, whereas JEB-R688∗ exhibited a weak response but JEB-R1226∗ did not respond (not shown here; see7). The other compounds did not induce noticeably readthrough in JEB-R145∗, -W464∗, -R688∗, -G803∗; or -R1226∗, in two experimental replicates. Based on the screening results, G and PTC124 were selected for further evaluation. To improve the translational potential of aminoglycoside-based therapy, different concentrations of G were combined with NMD inhibitors and/or antioxidants. The resulting combinations (TRID-cocktails) were subsequently evaluated in JEB-R145∗ and JEB-G803∗ keratinocytes, whereas JEB-W464∗ served as a positive control based on our previous demonstration of TRID-C5 (a G- and P-based cocktail)-mediated readthrough.15

At the transcript level, NOG in combination with TRID-C1 increased COL17A1 mRNA levels by approximately 2.7-fold, whereas NOG or CC-90009 alone or their combination had no significant effect (Figure 3D, left), indicating that the effect of TRIDs is the initial step for readthrough activity. Discussion

In our study, the exploratory TRID screening results suggested mutation-specific responses of JEB-C17 nonsense mutations to different readthrough compounds. PTC124 and/or G demonstrated readthrough efficacy among JEB-R145∗ and JEB-G803∗, respectively. The JEB-R145∗ and JEB-G803∗ keratinocytes are compound heterozygous carrying a nonsense mutation together with a second variant. Therefore, the observed phenotype and treatment responses likely reflect the combined effects of both alleles. Although the readthrough compounds evaluated in this study were selected to target PTCs, the potential contribution of the second allele to the therapeutic response cannot be excluded. Amlexanox, artesunate, atazanavir, clitocine, 2,6-diaminopurine, ELX-02, and erythromycin, did not significantly increase protein levels in any of the JEB-C17 keratinocytes. Variability in readthrough responses among different nonsense mutations within the same disease has been attributed to the PTC, as demonstrated using dual-reporter assays, with the hierarchy UGA>UAG>UAA, as well as to the sequence context surrounding the PTC with −1 U>G>C>A, and +4C > U>A>G.34,35,36 However, in the present study, all JEB-C17 keratinocytes contained UGA as PTC and the no consistent sequence-context pattern was observed (Table 2).Table 2PTC identity and the surrounding sequencesNucleotide positioncDNA levelProtein levelExonPTC−3−2−1+1+2+3+4c.433C>Tp.R145∗8UGAGUUCUGCc.505C>Tp.R169∗9UGAAGUUCGGc.1392G>Ap.W464∗17UGAAGCUGGAc.2062C>Tp.R688∗26UGACUCGGUGc.2407G>Tp.G803∗34UGACCAGCUCc.3676C>Tp.R1226∗51UGACCUGGGC

PTC identity and the surrounding sequences

Our results showed distinct NMD activity in different JEB-C17 nonsense mutations. We explored the NOG as a new enhancer of TRIDs readthrough activity. NOG potentiated both COL17A1 mRNA level and C17 amount presence with aminoglycoside and CC-90009 in JEB-C17; nevertheless, different nonsense mutations p.W464∗, p.G803∗, and p.R145∗ elicited different responses to this drug. NOG was only effective in the presence of CC-90009, G and/or P in JEB-W464∗ and -G803∗, while its combination with PTC124 did not induce readthrough activity in JEB-R145∗. Moreover, aminoglycosides unresponsive nonsense mutation, p.R1226∗, did not benefit from the addition of NOG or/and CC-90009. This indicates TRID selection is an initiative step for effective JEB-C17 readthrough therapy.

Given that JEB-R145∗ and -G803∗ responded to high dosage of G, we further evaluated G-based cocktail, which has anti-inflammatory properties.15 Combination of low dosage of G with the cocktail consisted with NMD inhibitor (CC-90009, NMDI-14) and antioxidants (apocynin and melatonin) enhanced restored C17 without cell toxicity in JEB-G803∗. But JEB-R145∗ did not respond to G-based cocktail. However, JEB-R145∗ showed robust answer to PTC124 while combination of PTC124 with the cocktail did not enhance C17 amount. Combining PTC124 with the cocktail did not further increase C17 amount compared with vehicle-treated control. The underlying mechanism remains unclear. However, this observation raises the possibility that combining different readthrough compounds may not necessarily result in additive or synergistic effects. Pharmacodynamic interactions may influence readthrough efficiency and/or translational dynamics.

Notably, PTC124 has successfully progressed through phase 3 clinical trials (ClinicalTrials.gov ID NCT01826487) in Duchenne muscular dystrophy.37 Given that genotype-phenotype correlation studies have demonstrated that even 2%–4% of partially functional C17 can be biologically meaningful and may result in milder disease phenotypes,32 G-based cocktail and PTC124 could be developed as potential readthrough treatment for JEB-G803∗ and JEB-R145∗.

Our results revealed substantial variability in readthrough efficacy across different mutations and highlighted the molecular complexity underlying this phenomenon. It also indicated that the testing of TRIDs and determining the NMD status for nonsense mutations in patient derived cells should be considered a critical step in the development and optimization of therapeutic strategies. All together, these findings support a personalized approach to readthrough therapy for JEB-C17.

Materials and methods

Cell isolation and culture

Primary keratinocytes were isolated from skin biopsies obtained from patients with JEB-C17 carrying nonsense mutations and from healthy donors. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the University of Freiburg (EK Freiburg 22–1208-S1). Cells were immortalized using E6/E7 genes (provided by Dr. F. Larcher, Madrid, Spain) and cultured in keratinocyte growth medium (PromoCell, Germany) at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2. Medium was changed every 2 days. Unless otherwise indicated, cells were treated one day after confluence with the specified compounds or vehicle control (DMSO) and harvested at 48 h after treatment.

Drug treatments

The following compounds were used at the indicated final concentrations: amlexanox (25 μM; Sigma-Aldrich), artesunate (32 μM; Sigma-Aldrich), PTC124 (35 μM; Hycultec), atazanavir (6.4 nM; Sigma-Aldrich), clitocine (2.7 μM; Hycultec), 2,6-diaminopurine (100 μM; Sigma-Aldrich), ELX-02 (3.5 μM; Hycultec), erythromycin (8 μM; Sigma-Aldrich), G (0.003 to 1 μg/μL; Sigma-Aldrich), and P (0.05–1 μg/μL; Sigma-Aldrich). DMSO was used as vehicle control, and its final concentration did not exceed 0.1% in any condition.

Protein extraction and immunoblot analysis

Cells were seeded at a density of 5,000 cells/cm2 in 6-well plates and cultured to one day after confluence prior to treatment. Total protein was extracted using a Triton X-100-containing lysis buffer. Protein samples were separated by 7% SDS-PAGE and transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes (Merck). Membranes were blocked with 5% BSA for 1 h at room temperature and incubated with primary antibodies against C17 (Abcam) overnight at 4°C or α-tubulin (Abcam) for 1 h at room temperature. After washing, membranes were incubated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody (Santa Cruz Biotechnology; 1:10,000) for 60–90 min at room temperature. Signals were detected using enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL; GE Healthcare).

MTT assay

Keratinocytes were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 2 × 104 cells per well and incubated for 24 h. Cells were then treated with the indicated compounds for 48 h. MTT reagent (Abcam) was added and incubated for 3 h at 37°C, followed by addition of solubilization solution according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Abcam). Absorbance was measured at 590 nm using a microplate reader (Tecan). Cell viability was calculated relative to vehicle treated controls.

In vitro wound closure assay

Cells were seeded into two-well culture inserts (Ibidi, Germany) at a density of 2 × 104 cells/cm2 and cultured for 24 h. Cells were treated with the indicated compounds or vehicle for 24 h prior to removal of inserts to create a defined gap. Images were acquired at 0 h and 24 h using a phase-contrast microscopy.

RNA isolation

Keratinocytes were seeded at 5,000 cells/cm2 and cultured to confluence. One day after reaching confluence, cells were treated with the indicated compounds or vehicle control for 48 h. Total RNA was isolated using the RNeasy Plus Mini Kit (QIAGEN), and RNA concentration and purity were determined using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer. RNA samples were used for quantitative real-time PCR analysis. RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) analysis was performed on untreated cells by Novogene.

cDNA synthesis and quantitative real-time PCR

cDNA was synthesized using the RevertAid First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Quantitative real-time PCR was performed using SYBR Green Master Mix (Roche) on a QuantStudio 5 system (Applied Biosystems) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Gene-specific primers were used to amplify COL17A1, with TATA-box binding protein (TBP) serving as the endogenous reference gene for normalization. Reactions were performed in two technical replicates and three biological replicates using a QuantStudio 5 Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Amplification specificity was confirmed by melting curve analysis performed using QuantStudio Design and Analysis Software (Applied Biosystems). Relative gene expression was determined using the 2−ΔΔCt method as previously described, with normalization to TBP expression.38 Each experiment included three biological replicates and two technical replicates. Primer sequences are listed in Table S1.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were performed with at least three independent biological replicates unless otherwise indicated. Data are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism. Differences among multiple groups were analyzed by one-way ANOVA. Comparisons between two groups were performed using Student’s t test. A p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

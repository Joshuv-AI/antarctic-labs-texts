Integrating bioinformatics and machine learning to uncover lncRNA LINC00269 as a key regulator in Parkinson's disease via pyroptosis pathways
Authors: LiLi Ma, Yue Liu, Yajing Ren, Na Mi, Jing Fang, Rui Bao, Xiuzhi Xu, Hongjia Zhang, Ying Tang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPyroptosis, a specific type of programmed cell death, which has become a significant factor to Parkinson's disease (PD). Concurrently, long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) have garnered attention for their regulatory roles in neurodegenerative disorders. This study was designed to ascertain the key lncRNAs in pyroptosis pathways of PD and elucidate their regulatory mechanisms.MethodsEmploying a combination of bioinformatics and machine learning, we analyzed PD data sets GSE133347 and GSE110716. Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) recognized different lncRNAs. Through various algorithms such as Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO), Random Forest (RF), and Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA), we recognized LINC01606 and LINC00269, which are key factors during the emergence and development of PD. Furthermore, experimental validation was conducted in PD mouse models to confirm these bioinformatics findings.ResultsThe analysis showed that there were a large number of apoptosis-related gene expression changes in Parkinson's syndrome, for example, CASP1 and GSDME were up-regulated, and CASP9 and AIM2 were down-regulated. Among the lncRNAs, LINC01606 and LINC00269 were identified as potential modulators of pyroptosis. Notably, LINC00269 was observed to be significantly downregulated in the brain tissues of a PD mouse model, supporting its involvement in PD. The study also highlighted potential interactions of these lncRNAs with genes like ONECUT2, PRLR, CTNNA3, and LRP2.ConclusionsThis study identifies LINC00269 as a potential contributor to pyroptosis pathways in PD. While further investigation is required to fully elucidate its role, these findings provide new insights into PD pathogenesis and suggest potential avenues for future research on diagnostic and therapeutic targets. The study underscores the importance of integrating bioinformatics with experimental validation in neurodegenerative disease research.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40001-024-02201-y.

Background

Parkinson’s disease (PD) is a hypokinetic disorder marked by a gradual decline in motor function [1]. During the incipient stage of the disease, these motor symptoms may be masked by different non-motor features, such as olfactory disorders, constipation, and mental disorders [2, 3]. PD often presents with a progressive development of malignancy [4]. However, there is no effective long-term treatment that can stop or reverse the neurodegenerative process [5]. Therefore, early diagnosis and intervention represent the best strategies for slowing the progression of the disease [6].

Currently, the specific pathogenesis of PD remains elusive, but it may involve multiple interacting mechanisms. Disease progression may be influenced by both environmental and genetic factors [7, 8]. Various mechanisms, including autophagy dysfunction, apoptosis, and oxidative stress, are likely to intersect and collectively mediate the malignant progression of PD. Pyroptosis is a type of necrosis related to inflammation, and cytolysis of gasdermin D (GSDMD) is an important hallmark during this process [9]. Recent studies have shown that, in addition to caspase-1, -4, -5, and -11, which trigger pyroptosis by cleaving GSDMD, caspase-8 can also activate pyroptosis by cleaving GSDME, revealing the complex role of caspase-8 in regulating cellular pyroptosis [10, 11]. Moreover, inflammasome‐mediated pyroptosis is a major neuronal cell death mechanism [12]. α-Synuclein metabolic disorders and dopaminergic neurodegeneration may be prevented by the inhibition of inflammasomes [13]. Biomarkers associated with the NLRP3 inflammasome have shown an increasing trend in multiple diseases, such as Alzheimer's disease and cancer. However, the mechanism of pyroptosis in PD remains insufficient. Therefore, clarifying the central role of pyroptosis in PD is of vital significance for further understanding the pathological mechanism of the disease and developing potential therapeutic strategies.

Non-coding RNAs occupy an important position in the transcriptome and have an important impact on many neurodegenerative diseases. Current research has shown that lncRNAs are associated with various pathophysiological mechanisms of PD, including α-synuclein aggregation, neuronal apoptosis, autophagy disorders, mitochondrial and lysosomal dysfunction, and neuroinflammation [14]. They are also seen as biomarkers of MPP + -induced pyroptosis and dopaminergic neuronal inflammation [15]. Animal models of PD reveal the association between abnormal changes in α-synuclein, mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS) overactivation, and neuronal cathepsin B release [16]. Therefore, the relationship between epigenetic modifications of lncRNAs and pathological changes of pyroptosis in PD has attracted much attention. In this study, data from the GEO database were combined with bioinformatics and machine learning algorithms to identify lncRNAs connected with pyroptosis. The findings were then validated in a PD mouse model.

Methods

Data collection and discrepancy analysis

The Homo sapiens PD data sets GSE133347 [17] and GSE110716 [18] were collected from the GEO database. The GSE133347 data set comprises chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) data including 10 samples (five disease groups and five healthy control groups) and the data platform is GPL21047. mRNA and lncRNAs were distinguished according to annotation information, and the data set was normalized. The GSE110716 data set comprises sequencing data including 16 samples (eight disease groups and eight healthy control groups) and the data platform is GPL15433.

To analyze the effect of pyroptosis on gene expression in PD, we examined 18 pyroptosis-related regulatory genes reported in the literature: CASP family proteins, NLRP1, NLRP3, NLRP6, GSDM family proteins, MEFV, PYCARD, IL1B and AIM2 [19, 20]. Based on sample grouping information, we used the "limma" and "DEseq2" R packages to conduct differential analysis of mRNA and lncRNAs in GSE133347 and GSE110716 [21]. Then We visualized the results by the R packages pheatmap and ggplot2.

Acquisition LncRNAs related to pyroptosis in PD

We performed Spearman correlation analysis on 590 genes associated with pyroptosis and successfully identified 18 lncRNAs that exhibit differential expression in Parkinson's disease (PD). The data threshold was defined as P < 0.05 and related coefficients | r |> 0.2.

Analysis of differentially expressed gene (DEG) function and pathway enrichment

In this study, we use the R 'clusterProfiler'[22] software package on DEGs enrichment analysis in PD with statistical significance defined as P < 0.05.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

GSEA is a knowledge-based algorithm designed to explore the enrichment of gene probes in genome expression profiles [23]. In this study, two symbol gene sets in the MSigDB database [24], the R package "c2.kegg.v7.4" and "H.ARI.V7.4" [25] were applied in GSEA analysis, with statistical significance determined by P < 0.05.

Screening for pyroptosis-related LncRNAs in PD

To screen for key biomarkers for PD from pyroptosis-related lncRNAs, we used three algorithms—RF, LASSO regression [26], and WGCNA [27]. We screened the pyroptosis-related lncRNAs using the random forest method to identify biomarkers that could be used to diagnose PD; 129 lncRNAs were obtained at a threshold value of > 0.005. We applied LASSO regression with the R-package “glmnet” while completing feature selection and dimensionality reduction and generating two lncRNAs. We applied WGCNA to analyze the lncRNAs modules that were correlated with PD [27]. Overlapping genes were subsequently selected from the above three taxonomic models for further analysis.

Immune infiltration analysis

We utilized ssGSEA to quantify specific immune cells and their response activity. ssGSEA calculates an enrichment score that indicates the total enrichment level of gene sets in each sample. The list of immune infiltration-related genes was based on a previous study [28] and the ImmPort database [29]. We performed immune infiltration analysis using the CIBERSORTx algorithm [30] to identify enriched immune cells with differences between the PD and normal group; Spearman-related analysis determined the correlation of key biomarkers to immune cell ratios.

LncRNA target gene prediction

We used the Rtool database [31] to predict and search for mRNAs that could bind to key lncRNAs, and SumEnergy was used as the sequencing criterion to identify the first 500 mRNAs.

PCR analysis in PD mouse model

Subsequent procedures involved extracting total RNA from the midbrain substantia nigra region. For the qPCR analysis, we utilized TransScript RNA extraction kits, Uni All-in-One First-Strand cDNA Synthesis SuperMix for qPCR (One-Step gDNA Removal), and TransStart Top Green qPCR SuperMix (Kit codes: ER101-01, AU341-02-V2, TG-AQ601-04).

The specific primers used for LINC00269 were LINC00269-F: TTGTAAGCTTCAGCCCCTCC and LINC00269-R: GGACGTGGCTGTTCACTGAT, and for LINC01606 were LINC01606-F: CTCACGCGATTTGCAGAAGG and LINC01606-R: TCTGCCCAGTGTGCAATCTT, as detailed in the study by Chandra et al. [32].

Parkinson's disease mouse model

In this study, we employed an experimental animal model to investigate Parkinson's Disease (PD). The selected model was carefully evaluated and officially provided by the Ethics Committee for Experimental Animals at the First Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University (Ethics approval number: 2022065), ensuring full compliance with ethical standards. We selected healthy male C57BL/6 mice, which were healthy, aged between 6 and 8 weeks, and had an approximate weight of 20 g, maintained in a controlled, barrier-enforced environment by professional caretakers.

During the experiment, we used 20 8-week-old male C57BL/6J mice, divided into a control group and an MPTP group of 10 mice each. Due to the impact of the pandemic lockdown measures on experimental conditions, only 7 mice from each of the control and experimental groups survived and were ultimately included in the analysis. Although the sample size was limited, the survival data from these mice are statistically significant and provide a reliable experimental foundation for this study. Both groups were housed in a 12-h light–dark cycle, with the temperature maintained at 22 ± 1°C and humidity at 45–55%, with free access to food and water. After 1 week of acclimatization, we established the model. For the experimental procedure, the mice received MPTP with an intraperitoneal (i.p.) injection at a dosage of 30 mg/kg daily for 7 days following a protocol established by other studies [33, 34]. Control groups received saline injections. One-week post-model induction, we used sodium pentobarbital (50 mg/kg, i.p.) to anesthetize the mice and then performed blood extraction and cardiac perfusion. Next, the experiment will continue in the midbrain substantia nigra region.

Preparation of mouse brain samples for dissection and analysis

Mice were rapidly euthanized by decapitation, and the brain was carefully removed in its entirety. The brain was then placed in a -80°C freezer for 5 min to achieve quick freezing. After the brain was frozen, it was transferred to an ice tray. A coronal sections (3–4 mm in thickness) was taken from the brain, starting from the region just anterior to the anterior fontanelle, which is aligned with the anterior margin of the optic chiasm in the ventral direction. On the coronal section, the substantia nigra region is distinguishable from other brain areas due to its unique color and clear boundaries. The substantia nigra was carefully dissected using forceps or other appropriate tools.

Statistical analysis

The R programming environment was utilized for performing all data calculations and statistical analyses. To compare differences between two groups of continuous variables, the independent Student's t test was employed for normally distributed data, while the Mann–Whitney U test was utilized for data that deviated from normal distribution. Statistical significance was established with a significance level of P < 0.05, and P values were assessed as two-tailed.

Functional enrichment analysis

To analyze the relationship between DEGs in PD with various biological aspects, we performed functional enrichment analysis on these PD-related DEGs. The evidence indicated that the DEGs were engaged in specific processes in biology such as animal organ regeneration, vitamin transport, and erythrocyte differentiation regulation. Identified cellular components were mainly microtubule-associated complexes, microtubules, and kinesin complexes. Molecular functions were characterized by activities like microtubule motor function, minus-end-directed microtubule motor action, and cytoskeletal motor activity (Fig. 3a, Table 1). KEGG analysis provided evidence that the DEGs were engaged in the IL-17 signaling pathway and antifolate resistance, ABC transporters, rheumatoid arthritis, TGF-beta signaling pathway, apoptosis-multiple species, and legionellosis (Fig. 3b, Table 2). To validate these findings, GSEA was conducted using both Hallmark and KEGG gene sets (Fig. 3c, d). The results indicate that the Hallmark gene collection associated with PD was mainly enriched in, hedgehog signaling, INF-α and INF-γ response pathways. In general, we found a high correlation between DEGs related to PD and immunity. Screening of pyroptosis-associated lncRNAs for diagnostic markers

Given pyroptosis's significant role in PD pathology, we identified lncRNAs associated with pyroptosis, potentially serving as diagnostic markers for PD. We defined lncRNAs by applying a correlation coefficient cutoff exceeding 0.2 and a significance threshold with a P value below 0.05 as pyroptosis-associated, identifying 590 such lncRNAs related to PD. Employing a random forest approach, we narrowed down our focus and identified 129 pyroptosis-related lncRNAs with the potential for PD diagnosis (Fig. 4a, b). Immune infiltration analysis of PD

Discussion

As global aging intensifies, Parkinson's disease (PD) has become an increasingly severe public health issue, with mortality rates rising year by year [35]. Therefore, in-depth research into the pathogenesis of Parkinson's disease is particularly urgent. Recent studies have shown that long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) play an important role in the pathogenesis of Parkinson's disease and are closely related to pyroptosis in disease progression[36]. Pyroptosis, a distinct form of programmed cell death, has been confirmed to play a significant role in various neurodegenerative diseases, and its role in Parkinson's disease is receiving growing attention[37]. Previous research has revealed a complex relationship between lncRNAs and pyroptosis in various chronic diseases, such as cancer [38–40]. Increasing evidence now supports that lncRNAs participate in the progression of Parkinson's disease by regulating pyroptosis-related signaling pathways. Specifically, lncRNAs may influence the expression of pyroptosis-associated proteins (such as GSDMD and GSDME), regulate the activation of microglia and astrocytes, and thereby trigger neuroinflammation and neuronal death [41]. In particular, the loss of dopaminergic neurons is a key factor in causing motor dysfunction in Parkinson's disease, and pyroptosis may play an important role in this process. Existing studies suggest that pyroptosis may exacerbate local neuroinflammatory responses, leading to neuronal death, particularly in the damage to dopaminergic neurons [42]. Therefore, investigating the interactions between lncRNAs and pyroptosis in Parkinson's disease will not only help to elucidate the disease's pathological mechanisms but also provide potential new targets for future diagnostic and therapeutic strategies.

In this study, we analyzed mRNA and lncRNA differences in two GEO data sets using limma software package and identified differences in lncRNA and mRNA expression in PD samples. In addition, we observed differential expressions of pyroptosis-related genes in PD, with higher CASP1 and GSDME, alongside decreased expression of CASP9 and AIM2. This pattern suggests that both cell pyroptosis and inflammatory processes contribute to the development of PD [43].

DEGs enrichment analysis, GO enrichment analysis of molecular functions such as microfilament microtubule movement, and KEGG analysis showed that DEGs in PD were particularly relevant to immunity. Through previous studies [19, 20], we confirmed the pathogenesis of PD immune response. Two lncRNAs associated with pyroptosis were analyzed and further studied: LINC01606 and LINC00269. LINC01606 was inversely correlated with GSDMB and GSDMA and positively correlated with MEFV. On the other hand, LINC00269 showed a negative relationship with GSDMD whereas it was positively related to CASP9, AIM2, and PYCARD. These related genes can mediate inflammation or are involved in the process of apoptosis. Our analysis revealed a notable reduction in LINC00269 expression and a notable rise in LINC01606 expression in PD samples compared to normal samples. Interestingly, oncology studies have demonstrated that LINC01606 is elevated in colon cancer and is an indicator of poor prognosis [43]. We subsequently verified these results using qPCR, based on our findings, we propose LINC00269 and LINC01606 as novel biomarkers for PD diagnosis [44–46].

CD8 and CD4 T cell infiltration has been confirmed by studies [46–49]. Our analysis comparing immune infiltration between PD and control groups showed a substantial positive association between monocytes and effector memory CD8 T cells, which exhibited a strong inverse correlation in eosinophils and mast cells, and a strong correlation between cell types. Therefore, we conclude that LINC01606 and LINC00269 are closely related to cell pyroptosis genes. The strong link of LINC01606 and LINC00269 with immune infiltration indirectly confirms that pyroptosis-associated lncRNAs are strongly associated with the aggressive progression of PD [16, 43]. Moreover, we identified mRNAs that may bind LINC00269 and LINC01606 to obtain ONECUT2, PRLR, CTNNA3, and LRP2. These genes have been associated with cell differentiation, cell-to-cell adhesion, intercellular signaling, ligand reuptake, and endocrine and paracrine. Such as repressing genes ONECUT2, which can suppress lateral differentiation pathways in the midbrain floor plate. The midbrain floor plate during the embryonic stage contains the grandmother cells of neurons, which mainly produce dopamine [50]. The CTNNA3 is essential for the mediation of cell–cell adhesion in muscle cells and has been implicated in neurodegenerative diseases according to existing literature [51]. In addition, increased expression of LRP2 has been observed in nigral DA neurons [52, 53]. By binding these four genes, LINC00269 and LINC01606 may modulate the expression of upstream genes and contribute to immune regulation in PD.

Research on LINC01606 has focused on gastrointestinal tumors, such as colon and gastric cancer, and its elevated levels lead to disease exacerbation and indicate a poor prognosis [43]. The bioinformatics analysis in this study suggests that the expression of LINC01606 in patients with PD is also elevated. Therefore, LINC01606 could be utilized as a biomarker to assist in clinical diagnosis. A study of LINC00269 in relation to osteosarcoma has noted that the expression of LINC00269 within the experimental group was potentially elevated in comparison with the control group and that result was associated with the autophagy phenotype [54]. It has also been pointed out that LINC00269 belongs to class II genes with extensive polymorphisms. Class II gene expression products are predominantly found on the exterior of specialized antigen-presenting cells and active T cells; thus, LINC00269 is linked to the immune response associated with the disease and indirectly involved in pyroptosis [55]. These findings are further corroborated by PCR analysis conducted in PD patients and within the substantia nigra of a rodent model for the disease. LINC00269 can establish a competitive endogenous RNA network through interactions with various miRNAs to regulate the occurrence and progression of disease, and the overexpression of LINC00269 impairs migration and invasion in animal models of ovarian cancer[56]. Therefore, it appears that alterations in class II genes may modify the course of the disease.

We expect that additional data will support our results in the future. In this paper, PCR detection was performed on samples of substantia nigra of mouse midbrain with PD to verify changes in LINC00269 levels. The regulatory relationship of upstream pyroptosis-related genes still needs to be further demonstrated in future work, and the changes in protein levels related to the immune and pyroptosis phenotypes and their molecular mechanisms require clarification. To this end, an increasing number of targeted biomarkers to assist in PD diagnosis at the gene, cellular, and molecular levels are screened.

Although this study has been carefully designed and accurately analyzed, it still has a few shortcomings. First, there are few sequencing databases of PD, and repeated verification between databases cannot be achieved. Second, the sample size is relatively small, because, due to the epidemic impact, a return to face-to-face visits has been limited to varying degrees.

Conclusions

In this study, we used bioinformatics methods to conduct an in-depth analysis of lncRNAs associated with PD, and the results showed that LINC00269 and LINC01606 are strongly linked to the mechanisms underlying PD. Meanwhile, we plan to explore further the relevant upstream and downstream regulatory mechanisms to reveal their specific role in diseases. This research approach is anticipated to offer new strategies and tools for diagnosis and intervention of PD.

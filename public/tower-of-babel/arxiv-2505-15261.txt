AGENT-X: Adaptive Guideline-based Expert Network for Threshold-free AI-generated teXt detection
Jiatao Li, Mao Ye, Cheng Peng, Xunjian Yin, Xiaojun Wan

Abstract
Existing AI-generated text detection methods heavily depend on large annotated datasets and external threshold tuning, restricting interpretability, adaptability, and zero-shot effectiveness. To address these limitations, we propose AGENT-X , a zero-shot multi-agent framework informed by classical rhetoric and systemic functional linguistics. Specifically, we organize detection guidelines into semantic, stylistic, and structural dimensions, each independently evaluated by specialized linguistic agents that provide explicit reasoning and robust calibrated confidence via semantic steering. A meta agent integrates these assessments through confidence-aware aggregation, enabling threshold-free, interpretable classification. Additionally, an adaptive Mixture-of-Agent router dynamically selects guidelines based on inferred textual characteristics. Experiments on diverse datasets demonstrate that AGENT-X substantially surpasses state-of-the-art supervised and zero-shot approaches in accuracy, interpretability, and generalization.

Introduction

The rapid proliferation of texts generated by Large Language Models (LLMs) has intensified the challenge of distinguishing AI-generated content from authentic human writing
Ippolito et al. (2020)
;
Mitchell et al. (2023a)
;
Su et al. (2023a)
. Traditional detection approaches rely heavily on supervised classifiers trained with extensive labeled datasets and external threshold optimization
Jawahar et al. (2020)
;
Bakhtin et al. (2019)
;
Yan et al. (2023)
, severely limiting their interpretability, adaptability, and effectiveness in realistic zero-shot scenarios, where domain-specific calibration datasets are frequently unavailable
Bao et al. (2024)
;
Verma et al. (2024)
.

To address these fundamental limitations, we propose
AGENT-X
, a novel zero-shot multi-agent detection framework rooted in theories drawn from classical rhetoric and systemic functional linguistics
Halliday (1978)
;
Simpson (2004)
. We first curate an extensive set of detection guidelines derived from academic literature and online communities, explicitly organizing them into semantic, stylistic, and structural linguistic dimensions. This structured theoretical foundation provides a clear, interpretable basis for distinguishing human-written texts from AI-generated content.

As illustrated in Figure
1
,
AGENT-X
employs specialized LLM agents, each independently analyzing texts within their respective linguistic dimensions. These agents generate explicit classification decisions, detailed linguistic reasoning, and robustly calibrated confidence scores via semantic steering methods. Crucially, our framework introduces a meta agent that integrates these dimension-specific evaluations through a confidence-aware aggregation mechanism, eliminating the need for external threshold optimization and significantly enhancing interpretability and practical applicability.

To effectively handle the diversity and complexity of real-world texts, we further incorporate a dynamic Mixture-of-Agent routing mechanism. This adaptive approach selects the most relevant linguistic guidelines based on inferred textual characteristics—such as topical domains and typical textual features—enabling
AGENT-X
to robustly address varied zero-shot detection scenarios.

Moreover, we observe that although existing detection methods commonly report threshold-independent metrics such as AUROC, their practical deployment inherently necessitates selecting thresholds to translate predicted probabilities into actionable binary decisions. This external threshold calibration introduces variability and often leads to inconsistent and suboptimal accuracy performance in real-world applications. In contrast, our proposed multi-agent framework inherently circumvents these calibration challenges through its confidence-aware, threshold-free aggregation mechanism. Consequently,
AGENT-X
substantially improves accuracy, interpretability, and real-world applicability.

Stylistics and Authorship Attribution

Stylistics, tracing its intellectual heritage back to classical rhetoric, literary criticism, and early linguistic theory
Aristotle et al. (1954)
;
Jakobson (1960)
;
Halliday (1978)
, has evolved into an empirically grounded linguistic discipline dedicated to the systematic investigation of textual style. The core objective of stylistics is to identify and analyze the distinctive linguistic patterns—such as lexical selections, syntactic structures, semantic preferences, and rhetorical devices—that constitute a text’s unique stylistic fingerprint and reveal insights about its author’s identity, intentions, and cognitive processes
Simpson (2004)
;
Short and Leech (2015)
. These stylistic patterns have been consistently demonstrated to be stable across texts by the same author, making stylistics particularly powerful in authorship attribution tasks
Holmes (1994)
;
Juola et al. (2008)
.

Authorship Attribution as a Stylistic Task.

Authorship attribution, historically rooted in literary scholarship
Mosteller et al. (1964)
;
Burrows (2002)
;
Holmes (1994)
, employs rigorous stylistic analysis to identify authors by systematically quantifying and comparing linguistic features across texts. Traditionally, this involves detecting cognitively distinctive and stylistically consistent markers unique to individual authors, ranging from lexical and syntactic choices to subtle rhetorical and semantic nuances
Burrows (2002)
;
Stamatatos (2009)
. The emergence of advanced generative AI models, such as GPT-3 and GPT-4
Brown et al. (2020)
;
OpenAI et al. (2024)
, has expanded the scope of authorship attribution research, necessitating robust stylistic methodologies capable of differentiating between human-authored and AI-generated texts
Ippolito et al. (2020)
;
Mitchell et al. (2023a)
. From this perspective, AI-generated text detection naturally maps onto the theoretical and methodological framework of authorship attribution, becoming a binary classification task distinguishing between
human
and
AI
authorship based on intrinsic stylistic indicators.

Halliday (1978)
;
Simpson (2004)
;
Short and Leech (2015)
. These dimensions provide a comprehensive theoretical framework for analyzing authorship and differentiating between human-written and AI-generated texts. The
semantic dimension
, grounded in Halliday’s systemic functional linguistics
Halliday (1978)
;
Halliday and Matthiessen (2013)
, encompasses textual features related to meaning-making, conceptual complexity, thematic originality, and informational specificity. Such semantic traits reflect intricate cognitive processes and intellectual contributions that characterize human writing and differentiate it from mechanically generated text
Halliday (1978)
;
Eggins (2004)
.

The
stylistic dimension
, extensively explored by seminal stylisticians such as Leech, Short, and Simpson
Short and Leech (2015)
;
Simpson (2004)
, addresses expressive nuances, lexical idiosyncrasies, cognitive friction, and subtle linguistic irregularities arising naturally from human cognitive limitations and creativity. These stylistic irregularities have been shown empirically to resist consistent replication by algorithmic processes, thereby serving as crucial discriminative markers in distinguishing between human and AI authorship
Burrows (2002)
;
Stamatatos (2009)
;
Ippolito et al. (2020)
.

The
structural dimension
, informed by rhetorical and textual coherence theories
Mann and Thompson (1988)
;
Van Dijk et al. (1983)
, analyzes organizational coherence, discourse arrangement, argumentative structures, and patterns of logical progression. Human authors typically display sophisticated and contextually adaptive coherence strategies and nuanced argumentation structures. Conversely, AI-generated texts frequently exhibit repetitive organizational patterns, predictable coherence mechanisms, and shallow argumentative structures, revealing their algorithmic origins
Gehrmann et al. (2019a)
;
Mitchell et al. (2023a)
.

3
Discussion on Evaluation Metrics

In this study, we primarily employ
Accuracy
as our central evaluation metric, supplemented by
Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic (AUROC)
as a secondary reference. This decision is motivated by practical considerations and intrinsic methodological constraints inherent to prompt-based Large Language Model (LLM) approaches for AI-generated text detection.

Accuracy

measures the proportion of correctly classified samples to the total number of samples, naturally aligning with real-world requirements for clear, actionable binary decisions. Existing detection methods
(
Mitchell et al., 2023a
;
Bao et al., 2024
)
frequently rely on metrics like AUROC, which, while theoretically informative, require selecting optimal thresholds based on external, domain-specific calibration datasets. In practical scenarios, such datasets are often unavailable, significantly limiting these methods’ applicability and scalability. In contrast, our proposed method uniquely employs a threshold-free aggregation strategy, eliminating dependency on external calibration sets, thereby making Accuracy an inherently more practical and relevant metric for evaluation.

AUROC

provides a comprehensive measure of detection performance across all possible thresholds, quantifying the probability that a randomly selected AI-generated text receives a higher predicted probability of being machine-generated compared to a randomly chosen human-written text. AUROC scores range between 0.0 (completely incorrect classification) and 1.0 (perfect classification), with 0.5 indicating random guessing. However, recent studies have emphasized the limitations of AUROC for evaluating prompt-based LLM classifiers, highlighting the inherently skewed and poorly calibrated probability distributions produced by generative LLMs
(
Giovannotti and Gammerman, 2024
;
Jiang et al., 2023
;
Yoon et al., 2025
)
. Prompt-based methods typically yield discrete predictions and uncalibrated confidence scores, rendering direct AUROC comparisons with traditional discriminative classifiers intrinsically disadvantageous and potentially misleading
(
Zhang et al., 2024
)
.

Consequently, we emphasize Accuracy as our primary evaluation metric due to its direct applicability and practical advantages, while reporting AUROC as an auxiliary measure to demonstrate that our approach remains competitively robust even under evaluation criteria less favorable to threshold-free methods.

4
Methodology

Our proposed multi-agent framework operationalizes classical stylistic theory to achieve robust zero-shot detection of AI-generated texts without relying on labeled datasets or external threshold tuning. The framework consists of three main components: a
router agent
for adaptive activation of guideline-specific
base agents
, guideline-specific base agents organized into three stylistic dimensions (
semantic
,
stylistic
, and
structural
), and a
meta agent
synthesizing these analyses into a final interpretable classification. All agents are instantiated via Large Language Models (LLMs) and utilize the advanced semantic-steering confidence calibration method (
SteeringConf
)
Zhou et al. (2025)
.

4.1
Router Agent

The router agent determines which specific stylistic guidelines—and consequently, which corresponding base agents—are activated for evaluating the input text. It achieves this by first analyzing each input text using an LLM to jointly infer its topical domain (e.g., medical, legal, literary) and stylistic attributes (e.g., formal register, argumentative coherence). Based on this inferred textual profile, the router agent selectively activates a subset of guideline-specific base agents within each linguistic dimension. For instance, when analyzing a medical abstract characterized by formal language, guidelines emphasizing semantic clarity and structural precision are activated, thus activating their corresponding base agents, while guidelines related to emotional expressiveness and their corresponding agents remain inactive.

4.2
Base Agents

Each activated guideline-specific base agent independently evaluates the input text according to its assigned stylistic guideline using a Large Language Model (LLM). For each input text, the base agent initially produces three foundational outputs:

•

A binary classification decision (
AI-generated
or
human-written
) based on the given guideline.

•

An explicit textual rationale highlighting linguistic evidence supporting this decision, aligned with dimension-specific stylistic theory.

•

A verbalized confidence score indicating the LLM’s certainty in the decision.

To robustly calibrate these confidence scores, we further employ the advanced semantic-steering confidence calibration method (
SteeringConf
). Specifically, the base agent independently evaluates the same input text using five symmetric steering prompts—
very cautious
,
cautious
,
vanilla
,
confident
, and
very confident
. Under each steering prompt
P_{k}\in\mathcal{P}
, the agent produces a binary classification prediction
f_{k}(x)
, an explicit textual rationale justifying this prediction, and an associated verbalized confidence score
c_{k}(x)
.

These multiple prompt-based outputs are then aggregated into a single robust calibrated confidence
c_{\text{cal}}(x)
by integrating two key consistency metrics:

Answer Consistency
(
\kappa_{\mathrm{ans}}
): measures prediction agreement across prompts:

\kappa_{\mathrm{ans}}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|}\,\max_{y\in\{\text{AI},\text{Human}\}}\sum_{k}\mathbb{I}[f_{k}(x)=y]

Confidence Consistency
(
\kappa_{\text{conf}}
): evaluates confidence stability across prompts:

\mu_{c}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|}\sum_{k}c_{k}(x),\quad\sigma_{c}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|}\sum_{k}(c_{k}(x)-\mu_{c})^{2}}

\kappa_{\text{conf}}=\frac{1}{1+\sigma_{c}/\mu_{c}}

These metrics are combined multiplicatively with the average confidence
\mu_{c}
to yield the final calibrated confidence score:

c_{\text{cal}}(x)=\mu_{c}\cdot\kappa_{\text{ans}}\cdot\kappa_{\text{conf}}

Finally, the guideline-specific base agent’s ultimate classification decision
f_{\mathrm{final}}(x)
is selected from the steering prompt whose original confidence
c_{k}(x)
most closely matches this calibrated confidence
c_{\text{cal}}(x)
:

k^{*}=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{k}\bigl|c_{k}(x)-c_{\mathrm{cal}}(x)\bigr|,\quad f_{\mathrm{final}}(x)=f_{k^{*}}(x)

The calibrated classification decision, rationale, and confidence constitute the final structured
guideline-specific report
.

4.3
Meta Agent

The meta agent synthesizes the guideline-specific reports from all activated base agents through a confidence-weighted aggregation process. Specifically, it integrates the classification decisions, robustly calibrated confidence scores, and explicit textual rationales provided by each guideline-specific agent. The meta agent dynamically evaluates and prioritizes agent inputs according to their confidence scores and the quality of linguistic reasoning presented. When agents disagree, it contextually assesses and reconciles differing rationales, forming a nuanced, consensus-based final classification. Subsequently, it generates a consolidated textual rationale summarizing the critical linguistic evidence underlying the final aggregated decision, alongside an aggregated confidence score computed using the same semantic-steering calibration mechanism employed by the base agents.

7
Ablation Study

We conducted an ablation study to rigorously validate the contribution of each critical component in the
AGENT-X
framework. Specifically, we systematically removed or altered the following components: (1)
Multi-Agent
, (2)
Guidelines
, (3)
LLM Router
, (4)
Adaptive Routing Strategy
, and (5)
Steer-based Confidence Calibration
. Performance was evaluated primarily using Accuracy (
ACC
), supplemented by AUROC for additional context (Table
3
).

w/o Multi-Agent.

Replacing the specialized dimension-specific linguistic agents with a single comprehensive agent led to a notable decline in average accuracy (from 85.9% to 78.7%). This emphasizes the critical advantage provided by having dedicated agents, each conducting focused analyses aligned with distinct linguistic dimensions.

w/o Guidelines.

Eliminating structured detection guidelines and replacing them with randomly selected prompts resulted in the most significant degradation in average accuracy (down to 61.4%), underscoring the essential role that theoretically-grounded detection guidelines play in enabling interpretable and effective detection.

w/o LLM Router.

Replacing the intelligent guideline-selection mechanism of the router agent with random guideline activation significantly reduced accuracy (from 85.9% to 72.0%). This highlights the crucial adaptive capability provided by intelligently selecting guidelines based on inferred textual characteristics.

w/o Adaptive Routing (Full Guidelines).

Activating all available guidelines simultaneously, rather than selectively, resulted in moderate performance degradation (average accuracy dropped to 75.9%). This indicates that excessive guideline activation introduces unnecessary noise and complexity, reinforcing the value of an adaptive, context-sensitive routing strategy.

Using uncalibrated confidence scores directly from LLMs, rather than applying our semantic-steering calibration method, decreased average accuracy from 85.9% to 79.9%. This clearly demonstrates the effectiveness of semantic steering calibration in enhancing confidence reliability and overall detection accuracy.

Related Work

Confidence Estimation

Reliable confidence estimation remains critical yet challenging for the practical deployment of Large Language Models (LLMs). Existing methods primarily leverage external knowledge
(
Tsai et al., 2024
;
Mielke et al., 2022
)
, internal logits
(
Jiang et al., 2021
;
Tian et al., 2023
)
, or direct verbalized assessments
(
Lin et al., 2022
;
kadavath2022language
)
. Recent integrated frameworks, notably multi-prompt semantic steering (
SteeringConf
)
(
Zhou et al., 2025
)
, systematically manipulate prompts to enhance calibration robustness. Building upon this line of research, our approach employs semantic steering-based confidence estimation, improving interpretability and reliability in zero-shot detection of AI-generated texts.

AI-generated Text Detection

Detecting AI-generated texts, especially in zero-shot settings, is increasingly challenging. Black-box approaches like DetectGPT
(
Mitchell et al., 2023a
)
, NPR
(
Su et al., 2023a
)
, and DNA-GPT
(
Yang et al., 2023
)
typically involve multiple evaluations or generations, incurring high computational overhead. White-box methods utilize model-internal metrics such as entropy, likelihood, and log-rank
(
Gehrmann et al., 2019a
;
Bao et al., 2024
;
Tulchinskii et al., 2024
)
. Meanwhile, supervised classifiers
(
Bakhtin et al., 2019
;
Yan et al., 2023
;
Verma et al., 2024
)
and watermarking approaches
(
Kirchenbauer et al., 2023
;
Zhao et al., 2023
)
rely either on extensive training data or proactive intervention during text generation, limiting their applicability with proprietary models. Unlike these methods, our proposed framework introduces a robust, interpretable, and threshold-free zero-shot detection mechanism, eliminating reliance on labeled datasets and external calibration.

LLM Multi-Agent Systems

Large Language Model Multi-Agent Systems (LLM-MAS) exploit collective intelligence through collaboration among multiple specialized agents
(
Guo et al., 2024
;
Han et al., 2024
)
. These systems commonly utilize structured communication protocols such as debates, voting, and task-specific dialogues
(
Du et al., 2023
;
Zhao et al., 2024b
;
Hong et al., 2023
)
, demonstrating effectiveness across various domains including software development
(
Qian et al., 2024
)
, embodied agents
(
Song et al., 2023
)
, and simulations
(
Park et al., 2023
;
Wang et al., 2023
)
. Inspired by this paradigm, we propose a novel LLM-based multi-agent framework that dynamically integrates specialized linguistic analyses, significantly enhancing adaptability and generalization for zero-shot AI-generated text detection.

9
Conclusion

In this work, we introduced a novel zero-shot multi-agent collaboration framework for robust detection of AI-generated texts, grounded in systematically structured stylistic theory. Our method leverages specialized linguistic agents, advanced semantic steering-based confidence calibration, and a dynamic Mixture-of-Agent expert routing mechanism to deliver state-of-the-art detection performance. Experimental results demonstrated substantial improvements in interpretability, robustness, and generalizability over existing supervised and zero-shot detection methods. Our approach provides a practical, threshold-free solution, significantly advancing AI-generated text detection in real-world zero-shot scenarios.

Limitations

Although our framework demonstrates strong performance and interpretability across diverse contexts, it requires relatively capable base language models for effective agent instantiation. Employing less powerful or smaller-scale LLMs could pose challenges in comprehensively understanding the detection guidelines or accurately performing agent-specific tasks.

Ethical Considerations

Our proposed framework contributes positively by facilitating transparent and interpretable detection of AI-generated texts, addressing ethical concerns around misinformation, authenticity, and accountability. However, careful consideration should be given to potential biases inherent in the underlying base language models, as these biases could inadvertently influence agent decisions. We encourage users and researchers to employ our framework responsibly, being mindful of potential impacts on fairness, privacy, and transparency in various application contexts.

Appendix C
Implementation Details

We determined the optimal threshold by maximizing classification accuracy on the validation set. Specifically, we performed a comprehensive scan across the entire range of raw validation scores, incrementing thresholds in steps of 1% of the observed score span. This threshold was subsequently fixed and applied to evaluate accuracy on the independent test set.

Appendix D
Baseline Details

Log-Likelihood
Gehrmann et al. (2019b)

A simple zero-shot method employs a language model to calculate the log-probability for each token within a text. A higher average log-likelihood indicates a greater likelihood that the text is generated by an LLM.

Entropy
Lavergne et al. (2008)

A zero-shot method relies on entropy to assess the randomness of text in order to identify text generated by LLMs. Human-written text typically shows more unpredictable variations. Consequently, text with lower entropy is more likely to have been produced by an LLM.

Rank
Gehrmann et al. (2019b)

A zero-shot method assigns a rank score to each token based on the previous context. By calculating the average score, a higher average rank score suggests a greater likelihood that the text is generated by an LLM.

Log-Rank
Gehrmann et al. (2019b)

An enhanced version of the Rank-based method. It uses a language model to calculate the logarithmic rank score of each word in the text. By calculating the average score, a higher average log-rank score suggests a greater likelihood that the text is generated by an LLM.

LRR
Su et al. (2023b)

The Log-Likelihood Log-Rank Ratio (LRR), an enhanced zero-shot method that effectively integrates Log-Likelihood and Log-Rank. Text with a higher LRR is more likely to be generated by an LLM.

NPR
Su et al. (2023b)

The Normalized Perturbed Log-Rank (NPR) is a zero-shot method that identifies differences by comparing the Log-Rank scores of perturbed human-written text with those generated by LLMs. Text with a higher NPR is more likely to be generated by an LLM.

DetectGPT
Mitchell et al. (2023b)

A zero-shot method for detection using probabilistic curvature. It utilizes random perturbations of paragraphs from a general pre-trained language model and discriminates LLM-generated text through the statistical curvature threshold of log probabilities.

DNA-GPT
Yang et al. (2024)

A zeo-shot detection method that utilizes
N
-gram analysis or probability divergence in a white-box setting to compare the differences between the truncated original text and the text completed by a language model. A higher score suggests a greater likelihood that the text was generated by an LLM.

Fast-DetectGPT
Bao et al. (2023)

An optimized zero-shot detector that replaces the perturbation step of DetectGPT with a more efficient sampling step. We chose the optimal settings reported by the authors, using GPT-Neo-2.7b as the scoring model and GPT-J-6b
Wang and Komatsuzaki (2021)
as the reference model.

RoBERTa Classifier
Park et al. (2021)

A popular and competitive detector method. Recognize LLM generated text by fine-tuning the RoBERTa classifier on large amounts of labeled text.

Characteristic function of a power partial isometry
Kritika Babbar, Amit Maji

Abstract
. The celebrated Sz.-Nagy-Foiaş model theory says that there is a bijection between the class of purely contractive analytic functions and the class of completely non-unitary (c.n.u.) contractions modulo unitary equivalence. In this paper we provide a complete classification of the purely contractive analytic functions such that the associated contraction is a c.n.u. power partial isometry. As an application of our findings, we determine a class of contractive polynomials such that the associated c.n.u. contraction is of the explicit diagonal form S ⊕ N ⊕ C S\oplus N\oplus C , where S S and C ∗ C^{*} are unilateral shifts and N N is nilpotent. Finally, we obtain a characterization of operator-valued symbols for which the corresponding Toeplitz operator on vector-valued Hardy space is a partial isometry.

1.
Introduction

One of the fundamental problems in operator theory is to find a complete unitary invariant of a bounded linear operator on a separable Hilbert space. In this context, the characteristic function of a contraction on a Hilbert space plays a vital role and acts as a bridge between operator theory and function theory.
Since the classification problem of any bounded linear operator is generally challenging, research on this problem focuses on examining specific classes of tractable operators for which one can find nice and useful (unitary) invariants. The primary goal of this paper is to provide a comprehensive classification of purely contractive analytic functions (in particular, polynomial functions) such that the associated contraction is a completely non-unitary (c.n.u. in short) power partial isometry, and also to address the problem of characterizing operator-valued symbols for which the Toeplitz operator on vector-valued Hardy space is a partial isometry.

For a single contraction on a Hilbert space, the characteristic function has a long-standing tradition. It has widespread applications across various disciplines like transfer function theory, perturbation theory, control theory, stability theory, and network realizability theory and so on (cf.
[
3
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
15
]
). The notion of characteristic function was first introduced by Livšic
[
16
]
and major development has been done by Sz.-Nagy and Foiaş
[
19
]
in their dilation and model theory. Thereafter, a lot of research (in particular, constant and polynomial characteristic functions) has been done by Wu
[
20
]
, Bagchi and Misra
[
2
]
, Foiaş and Sarkar
[
10
]
, Foiaş, Pearcy and Sarkar
[
11
]
. Characteristic function serves as a complete unitary invariant for c.n.u. contractions. More precisely, two c.n.u. contractions are unitarily equivalent if and only if their characteristic functions coincide (see Sz.-Nagy and Foiaş
[
19
]
). On the other hand, an important class of contractions is power partial isometries, that is, operators whose every positive power is also a partial isometry. The decomposition of power partial isometries was first initiated by Halmos and Wallen in
[
14
]
, where they demonstrated that each power partial isometry is a direct sum of a unitary operator, a unilateral shift, a backward shift, and truncated shifts. Clearly, the c.n.u. component of a power partial isometry is the combining components of unilateral shift, backward shift, and truncated shifts. In this paper, we obtain the characteristic function of a power partial isometry which is a purely contractive analytic function with partially isometric coefficients. Thus the natural question arises:

Does a purely contractive analytic function with partially isometric coefficients generate a power partial isometry?

In fact, we present one of the main results (see Section
3
below):

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Theta:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
be a purely contractive analytic function such that

\Theta(z)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\theta_{m}z^{m},\quad(z\in\mathbb{D})

where
\theta_{m}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
are partial isometries for all
m\geq 1
. Then there exist a Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}=\left\{\left(I-T_{\Theta}T_{\Theta}^{*}\right)f\oplus\Delta_{\Theta}g:f\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D}),g\in[{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}\right\},

where
\Delta_{\Theta}
is a constant projection, and a c.n.u. power partial isometry
T
on
\mathcal{H}
defined by

T^{*}(u\oplus v)=e^{-it}(u(e^{it})-u(0))\oplus e^{-it}v(e^{it}),\quad(u\oplus v\in\mathcal{H})

such that the characteristic function of
T
coincides with
\Theta
.

Our approach essentially builds upon the Sz.-Nagy and Foiaş’ construction of a functional model. Additionally, we prove that the characteristic function of a truncated shift is a monomial with a partially isometric coefficient which is a specific instance of the polynomial characteristic function. In
[
10
]
, authors established that the characteristic function of a c.n.u. contraction
T
on
\mathcal{H}
is a polynomial if and only if
T
has an upper triangular matricial form

T=\begin{pmatrix}S&*&*\\
0&N&*\\
0&0&C\end{pmatrix}

with respect to the orthogonal decomposition
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{s}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{t}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{b}
, where
S,N,\text{ and }C
are unilateral shift, nilpotent, and backward shift, respectively. The question which follows naturally is:

When will this matrix form be diagonal?

As a direct consequence of our result, we identify a particular class of polynomial characteristic functions for which this representation is diagonal and also describe the orthogonal decomposition spaces explicitly (see Theorem
3.7
below).

In the next part of this article, we characterize some particular class of Toeplitz operators, namely the partially isometric Toeplitz operators with operator-valued symbol on the vector-valued Hardy space. The class of Toeplitz and analytic Toeplitz operators is one of the most important classes of tractable operators.
It is a highly active research topic with a growing list of applications and links in function theory and operator theory. The characterization of nonzero Toeplitz operators that are partial isometries was initiated by Brown and Douglas in
[
4
]
. They proved that nonzero partially isometric Toeplitz operators are of the form
T_{\varphi}
and
T^{*}_{\varphi}
, where
\varphi
is an inner function. In
[
8
]
, Deepak, Pradhan, and Sarkar generalized this result in the scalar-valued Hardy space over polydisc. The similar factorization result holds for the partially isometric Toeplitz operators with operator-valued symbols in the polydisc setting which was recently studied by Sarkar in
[
18
]
and he also posed the following question:

Characterize the class of partially isometric symbols
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}
such that
T_{\Phi}
is a partial isometry.

As a byproduct of our main result, we recognize a specific class of partially isometric symbols for which the corresponding Toeplitz operator is a partial isometry, which motivates us to settle the question posed by Sarkar in
[
18
]
under certain conditions. Indeed, we have the following result (see Theorem
4.8
):

Theorem 1.2
.

For finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}_{*}
, let
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
be such that
\Phi(e^{it})=\sum\limits_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\hat{\Phi}(m)e^{imt}
is a nonzero partial isometry a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
. Then
T_{\Phi}
is a partially isometric Toeplitz operator if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

\Phi_{+}(e^{it})^{*}\Phi_{+}(e^{it})
and
\Phi_{-}(e^{it})\Phi_{-}(e^{it})^{*}
are operator-valued constant functions a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
where
\Phi_{+}\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
and
\Phi_{-}^{*}\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{*},\mathcal{E})}
are contractive analytic functions.

(2)

\hat{\Phi}(m)^{*}\hat{\Phi}(-n)=0_{\mathcal{E}}
and
\hat{\Phi}(-n)\hat{\Phi}(m)^{*}=0_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}
for all
m,n\geq 1
.

The structure of the rest of the paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we set all the notations and definitions that will be used throughout the paper. Section
3
focuses on determining the characteristic function of a power partial isometry and characterize the class of contractive analytic functions for which the associated c.n.u. contraction is a power partial isometry. Section
4
provides a characterization of partially isometric Toeplitz operators with operator-valued symbols. In Section
5
, we illustrate some concrete examples to support our result.

2.
Preliminaries

This section compiles all the notations, definitions, and results that are used in this paper. It is assumed that every Hilbert space is a complex separable Hilbert space. For a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
,
I_{\mathcal{H}}
and
0_{\mathcal{H}}
represent the identity operator and zero operator on
\mathcal{H}
, respectively. If
\mathcal{H}
is clear from the context, we frequently write
I
and
0
without the subscript. Let
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be the
C^{*}
-algebra of all bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H}
. For
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
\mathcal{N}(T)
and
\mathcal{R}(T)
stand for the kernel and range of
T
, respectively. An operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is said to be a contraction if
\|Th\|\leq\|h\|
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
, and it is completely non-unitary (c.n.u. in short) if there does not exist any nonzero reducing subspace
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{H}
such that
T|_{\mathcal{L}}
is unitary. A contraction
T
is pure if
\|T^{*m}h\|\rightarrow 0
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
as
m\rightarrow\infty
. We say that
T\in\mathcal{B(H)}
is an isometry if
\|Th\|=\|h\|
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
, and
T
is a partial isometry if
\|Th\|=\|h\|
for all
h\in[\mathcal{N}(T)]^{\perp}
. An operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is an orthogonal projection if
T=T^{2}=T^{*}
. A subspace
\mathcal{M}
is invariant under
T
if
T\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}
is reducing under
T
if it is invariant under
T
and
T^{*}
both.

Let
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}_{*}
be two Hilbert spaces. We will denote by
L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}
the space of
\mathcal{E}
-valued square integrable functions on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
with respect to the normalized Lebesgue measure. Let
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
denote the
\mathcal{E}
-valued Hardy space on the open unit disc
\mathbb{D}
defined as

H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})=\left\{f=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}z^{m}:\ a_{m}\in\mathcal{E},\ \sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\|a_{m}\|^{2}<\infty\right\},

and we often identify
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
(in the sense of radial limits) as a closed subspace of
L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}
without making explicit distinction. With this identification
L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}=H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus[H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}
.
Also,
L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
denotes the algebra of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
-valued bounded functions on
\mathbb{T}
with respect to operator supremum norm and
H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
denotes the algebra of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
-valued bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
. For
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
, let
L_{\Phi}
denote the Laurent operator from
L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}
to
L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}
defined as

L_{\Phi}h=\Phi h\quad(h\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}).

Let
P_{+}^{\mathcal{E}}
be the orthogonal projection of
L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}
onto
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
. The Toeplitz operator
T_{\Phi}
from
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
to
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})
is defined by

T_{\Phi}h=P_{+}^{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\Phi h)\quad(h\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})).

In particular, if
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{*}
and
\Phi(z)=zI
, then we use
T_{\Phi}
as
M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}
. We will frequently use
M_{z}
if
\mathcal{E}
is clear from the context. The Toeplitz operator
T_{\Phi}
is characterized by the operator equation
(M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{*}})^{*}T_{\Phi}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}=T_{\Phi}
. If
\Phi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
, then
T_{\Phi}
is called an analytic Toeplitz operator and is characterized by the equation
M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{*}}T_{\Phi}=T_{\Phi}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}
.

Recall some basic definitions which will be used throughout this note.
An operator
T\in\mathcal{B(H)}
is said to be a power partial isometry if
T^{n}
is a partial isometry for all
n\geq 1
. It is a large class of operators including isometries, co-isometries, orthogonal projections and truncated shifts etc. For a power partial isometry
T
, we write
E_{k}=T^{*k}T^{k}
and
F_{k}=T^{k}T^{*k}
as the initial and final projections for all
k\geq 0
. Recall that
E_{k}\geq E_{k+1}
and
F_{k}\geq F_{k+1}
for all
k\geq 0
.

Lemma 2.1

(cf.
[
14
]
)
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B(H)}
be a power partial isometry. Then

(1)

E_{k}E_{l}=E_{l}E_{k}
and
F_{k}F_{l}=F_{l}F_{k}
for all
k,l\geq 0
.

(2)

E_{k}F_{l}=F_{l}E_{k}
for all
k,l\geq 0
.

(3)

TE_{k+1}=E_{k}T
for all
k\geq 0
.

(4)

TF_{k}=F_{k+1}T
for all
k\geq 0
.

Let
k\geq 1
be any natural number. A truncated shift of index
k
, denoted by
J_{k}
, is defined on
\mathcal{H}=\underbrace{\mathcal{H}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{0}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{H}_{0}}_{k\text{-}times}
as

J_{k}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k})=(0,x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1})\quad(x_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{0},i=1,\ldots,k).

Here
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is a Hilbert space and
\underbrace{\mathcal{H}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{0}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{H}_{0}}_{k\text{-}times}
is identified with
\mathcal{H}_{0}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}
. Note that
J_{1}=0
.

Let us recall the Halmos and Wallen decomposition theorem for power partial isometry given in
[
14
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a power partial isometry. Then there exist subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{u},\mathcal{H}_{s},\mathcal{H}_{b}
, and
\mathcal{H}_{k}\,\,(k\geq 1)
reducing
T
and

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{u}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{s}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{b}\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}_{k}\right),

such that
T|_{\mathcal{H}_{u}}
is a unitary,
T|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}
is a unilateral shift,
T|_{\mathcal{H}_{b}}
is a backward shift and
T|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}
is a truncated shift of index
k
.

It is easy to observe from the Halmos and Wallen decomposition that for
k\geq 1
(see
[
1
]
),

\mathcal{H}_{k}=\bigoplus\limits_{n=1}^{k}\left(E_{k-n}-E_{k-n+1}\right)\left(F_{n-1}-F_{n}\right)\mathcal{H}.

Definition 2.3

(
Contractive analytic function)
.

An operator-valued analytic function
\Theta:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
is said to be contractive if

\|\Theta(z)a\|\leq\|a\|\quad(a\in\mathcal{E}),

and purely contractive if it also follows
\|\Theta(0)a\|<\|a\|\,\,\,\,(a\in\mathcal{E},\,\,a\neq 0)
.

Definition 2.4

(
Inner function)
.

A contractive analytic function
\Theta:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
is called inner if
\Theta(e^{it})
is an isometry from
\mathcal{E}
to
\mathcal{E}_{*}
almost everywhere (a.e. in short) on
\mathbb{T}
.

Definition 2.5

(
Characteristic function)
.

For a contraction
T
on
\mathcal{H}
, define the defect operators
D_{T}=(I-T^{*}T)^{{1}\over{2}}
and
D_{T^{*}}=(I-TT^{*})^{{1}\over{2}}
with defect spaces
\mathcal{D}_{T}=\overline{\mathcal{R}(D_{T})}
and
\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}=\overline{\mathcal{R}(D_{T^{*}})}
. Then the characteristic function of
T
is the purely contractive analytic function
\Theta_{T}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}_{T},\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}})
defined by

\Theta_{T}(z)=\left[-T+zD_{T^{*}}(I-zT^{*})^{-1}D_{T}\right]\big|_{\mathcal{D}_{T}}

for
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

Let
\Theta:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
and
\Phi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}_{*})
be two contractive analytic functions. They are said to coincide if there exist unitary operators
\tau:\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}
and
\tau_{*}:\mathcal{E}_{*}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{*}
such that
\tau_{*}\Theta(z)=\Phi(z)\tau
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. It is well known that two c.n.u. contractions
T
on
\mathcal{H}
and
S
on
\mathcal{K}
are unitarily equivalent if and only if their characteristic functions coincide (see
[
19
]
).

3.
Characteristic function

In this section, we shall discuss the characteristic function of a power partial isometry. More specifically, we obtain the characteristic function of a power partial isometry and observe that each coefficient in the characteristic function is a partial isometry. Conversely, a purely contractive analytic function with partially isometric coefficients generates a power partial isometry. As an application, we get the diagonal matricial representation of a class of operators whose characteristic functions coincide with the contractive analytic polynomial with partially isometric coefficients.

Recall the following result given in
[
19
]
(see Chapter VI), proof of which is straightforward.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
T_{n}
be a contraction on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{n}
for
n\geq 1
.
Let
\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}_{n}
and
T=\bigoplus\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}T_{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Then

D_{T}=\bigoplus\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}D_{T_{n}},\quad D_{T^{*}}=\bigoplus\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}D_{T_{n}^{*}},\quad\mathcal{D}_{T}=\bigoplus\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}_{T_{n}},\quad\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}=\bigoplus\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}_{T_{n}^{*}},

and hence

\Theta_{T}(z)=\bigoplus\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\Theta_{T_{n}}(z)\quad\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

Let
T
be a power partial isometry on
\mathcal{H}
. Following Halmos-Wallen decomposition, we have
T
-reducing subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{u}
,
\mathcal{H}_{b}
,
\mathcal{H}_{s}
and

\mathcal{H}_{k}

(k\geq 1)
such that

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{u}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{s}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{b}\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}_{k}\right).

Also,
T_{u}=T|_{\mathcal{H}_{u}}
is a unitary,
T_{s}=T|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}
is a unilateral shift,
T_{b}=T|_{\mathcal{H}_{b}}
is a backward shift and
T_{k}=T|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}
is a truncated shift of index
k
. Now using the above Lemma
3.1
, we readily get

\mathcal{D}_{T}=\mathcal{D}_{T_{b}}\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}_{T_{k}}\right).

Since
D_{T_{b}^{*}}=0
,
\Theta_{T_{b}}(z)=0\,\,\forall\,\,z\in\mathbb{D}
and hence the characteristic function of
T

\Theta_{T}(z)=\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\Theta_{T_{k}}(z)\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

Observe that for
k\geq 1,

\mathcal{D}_{T_{k}}=(I-T_{k}^{*}T_{k})\mathcal{H}_{k}=(E_{0}-E_{1})\mathcal{H}_{k},

where

\mathcal{H}_{k}=\bigoplus\limits_{n=1}^{k}\left(E_{k-n}-E_{k-n+1}\right)\left(F_{n-1}-F_{n}\right)\mathcal{H}.

Note that for
n>k

(E_{0}-E_{1})\left(E_{k-n}-E_{k-n+1}\right)=0.

Thus

\mathcal{D}_{T_{k}}=\bigoplus\limits_{n=1}^{k}(E_{0}-E_{1})\left(E_{k-n}-E_{k-n+1}\right)\left(F_{n-1}-F_{n}\right)\mathcal{H}=(E_{0}-E_{1})(F_{k-1}-F_{k})\mathcal{H}.

Hence

\mathcal{D}_{T_{k}}=\mathcal{R}((E_{0}-E_{1})(F_{k-1}-F_{k})).

Similarly, we can prove

\mathcal{D}_{T_{k}^{*}}=\mathcal{R}((E_{k-1}-E_{k})(F_{0}-F_{1})).

Recall that
\mathcal{N}(T_{k})=\mathcal{D}_{T_{k}}
and hence the characteristic function of
T_{k}

is

\displaystyle\Theta_{T_{k}}(z)

\displaystyle=\left(-T_{k}+z(I-T_{k}T_{k}^{*})(I-zT_{k}^{*})^{-1}(I-T_{k}^{*}T_{k})\right)|_{\mathcal{D}_{T_{k}}}

\displaystyle=z(I-TT^{*})\left(I+zT^{*}+\cdots+z^{k-1}T^{*(k-1)}\right)(I-T^{*}T)|_{\mathcal{D}_{T_{k}}}

\displaystyle=(F_{0}-F_{1})\left(zI+z^{2}T^{*}+\cdots+z^{k}T^{*(k-1)}\right)(E_{0}-E_{1})|_{\mathcal{D}_{T_{k}}}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Now using Lemma
2.1
, for any
l\geq 0
,
we have

\displaystyle(F_{0}-F_{1})T^{*l}(E_{0}-E_{1})(F_{k-1}-F_{k})

\displaystyle=T^{*l}(F_{l}-F_{l+1})(E_{0}-E_{1})(F_{k-1}-F_{k})

\displaystyle=T^{*l}(E_{0}-E_{1})(F_{l}-F_{l+1})(F_{k-1}-F_{k})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}T^{*l}(E_{0}-E_{1})(F_{l}-F_{l+1}),&\mbox{if~}l=k-1\\
0&\mbox{if~}l\neq k-1.\\
\end{cases}

Therefore, for
g\in\mathcal{H}

\Theta_{T_{k}}(z)((E_{0}-E_{1})(F_{k-1}-F_{k})g)=T^{*k-1}(E_{0}-E_{1})(F_{k-1}-F_{k})gz^{k}\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

Now for
f=f_{0}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}f_{k}\in\mathcal{D}_{T}
, where
f_{0}\in\mathcal{D}_{T_{b}}
and
f_{k}\in\mathcal{D}_{T_{k}}
, the characteristic function of
T
becomes

\Theta_{T}(z)f=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}T^{*(k-1)}f_{k}z^{k}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}P_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}T^{*(k-1)}fz^{k},

where
P_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}
is the orthogonal projection of
\mathcal{H}
onto
\mathcal{H}_{k}
. Now set
C_{k}=P_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}T^{*(k-1)}
for
k\geq 1
. Since
T
is a power partial isometry and
\mathcal{H}_{k}
reduces
T
for each
k
, we get

C_{k}C_{k}^{*}C_{k}=P_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}T^{*(k-1)}T^{(k-1)}T^{*(k-1)}=P_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}T^{*(k-1)}=C_{k}.

Therefore, each
C_{k}
is a partial isometry. Furthermore, note that the characteristic function of truncated shift of index
k
is a monomial of degree
k
whose coefficient is also partial isometry.

We record the aforementioned discussion in the following.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
T
be a power partial isometry on
\mathcal{H}
. Then the characteristic function of
T
is
\Theta_{T}(z)=\left[\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}P_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}T^{*k-1}z^{k}\right]\Big|_{\mathcal{D}_{T}}
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, where
P_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}
is the orthogonal projection of
\mathcal{H}
onto
\mathcal{H}_{k}
. Moreover, each coefficient in the characteristic function is a partial isometry.

The above result raises the natural question in the following:

Is the converse of the above result true?

To answer this question, we shall use the following easy yet powerful result.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Theta:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
be a contractive analytic function such that
\Theta(z)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\theta_{m}z^{m},
where each
\theta_{m}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
is a partial isometry for
m\geq 0
. Then
\theta_{i}^{*}\theta_{j}=0_{\mathcal{E}}
and
\theta_{j}\theta_{i}^{*}=0_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}
for all
i\neq j
.

Proof.

Since
\Theta
is a contractive analytic function, then for
a\in\mathcal{E}
,

(3.1)

\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\|\theta_{m}a\|^{2}\leq\|a\|^{2}.

For
a\in\mathcal{R}(\theta_{i}^{*})=[\mathcal{N}(\theta_{i})]^{\perp}
,
\|\theta_{i}a\|=\|a\|
as each
\theta_{i}
is a partial isometry. By (
3.1
), we get

\theta_{j}a=0\quad\text{for all~~}j\neq i.

Hence,
\mathcal{R}(\theta_{i}^{*})\subseteq\mathcal{N}(\theta_{j})
for
j\neq i
. Equivalently,
\theta_{j}\theta_{i}^{*}=0_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}
for all
j\neq i
.

For the second one, observe that if
\Theta
is a contractive analytic function, then
\tilde{\Theta}(z):=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\theta_{m}^{*}z^{m}
is also a contractive analytic function. By the same argument,
\theta_{i}^{*}\theta_{j}=0_{\mathcal{E}}
for all
i\neq j
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Theta:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
be a purely contractive analytic function such that

\Theta(z)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\theta_{m}z^{m}\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

where
\theta_{m}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
are partial isometries for
m\geq 1
. Then for any

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})
,

\left(I-T_{\Theta}T_{\Theta}^{*}\right)\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\right)=a_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(I-\sum_{m=1}^{n}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}z^{n}.

Proof.

First note that for any
a\in\mathcal{E}_{*}
and
k\geq 1
,

T_{\Theta}^{*}(az^{k})=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k}\theta_{m}^{*}az^{k-m}=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}\theta_{k-m}^{*}az^{m}.

Then

\displaystyle T_{\Theta}T_{\Theta}^{*}\left(az^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=T_{\Theta}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}\theta_{k-m}^{*}az^{m}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}T_{\Theta}(\theta_{k-m}^{*}az^{m})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}\left(\sum\limits_{l=m+1}^{\infty}\theta_{l-m}\theta_{k-m}^{*}az^{l}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}\theta_{k-m}\theta_{k-m}^{*}a\right)z^{k}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}a\right)z^{k},

where the second last equality follows using Lemma
3.3
. Also note that
T_{\Theta}^{*}(a)=0
for all
a\in\mathcal{E}_{*}
.
Therefore, for
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})
,

\displaystyle\left(I-T_{\Theta}T_{\Theta}^{*}\right)\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{n}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}a_{n}\right)z^{n}

\displaystyle=a_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(I-\sum_{m=1}^{n}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}z^{n}.

Returning to the above question, first suppose that
\Theta:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
is a contractive analytic function such that

\Theta(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\theta_{m}z^{m}\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

\theta_{m}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
are partial isometries for all
m\geq 0
and
\theta_{0}\neq 0
. Then

\|\Theta(0)a\|=\|\theta_{0}a\|=\|a\|\,\,\quad(\forall\,\,a\in[\mathcal{N}(\theta_{0})]^{\perp}).

Then
\Theta
can not be purely contractive.
Thus, if
\Theta
is purely contractive with partially isometric Fourier coefficients, then
\Theta(0)=0
.

We are now ready to state the main result of this section. Our proof is inspired by Sz.-Nagy-Foiaş’ model theory (see
[
19
, Chapter VI]
).

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\Theta:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
be a purely contractive analytic function such that

\Theta(z)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\theta_{m}z^{m}\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

where
\theta_{m}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
are partial isometries for
m\geq 1
. Then there exist a Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}=\left\{(I-T_{\Theta}T_{\Theta}^{*})f\oplus\Delta_{\Theta}g:f\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D}),g\in[{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}\right\},

where
\Delta_{\Theta}
is a constant projection, and a c.n.u. power partial isometry
T
on
\mathcal{H}
defined by

T^{*}(u\oplus v)=e^{-it}(u(e^{it})-u(0))\oplus e^{-it}v(e^{it})\quad(u\oplus v\in\mathcal{H})

such that the characteristic function of
T
coincides with
\Theta
.

Proof.

Suppose

\Theta(z)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\theta_{m}z^{m}\quad\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

where
\theta_{m}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
are partial isometries for
m\geq 1
.
Define an operator-valued function
\Delta_{\Theta}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}
by

\Delta_{\Theta}(e^{it})=\left[I-\Theta(e^{it})^{*}\Theta(e^{it})\right]^{1\over 2}\quad(\text{a.e.\ on }\mathbb{T}).

Now using Lemma
3.3
,
\theta_{i}^{*}\theta_{j}=0_{\mathcal{E}}
and
\theta_{i}\theta_{j}^{*}=0_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}
for all
i\neq j
. Thus, for
\eta,\zeta\in\mathcal{E}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
,

\displaystyle 0=\langle\Theta\eta,z^{n}\Theta\zeta\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left\langle\Theta(e^{it})\eta,e^{int}\Theta(e^{it})\zeta\right\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-int}\left\langle\Theta(e^{it})^{*}\Theta(e^{it})\eta,\zeta\right\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}dt

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-int}\Theta(e^{it})^{*}\Theta(e^{it})\eta\ dt,\zeta\right\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}\quad\text{(See \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{raul}{}{}, Appendix\ A]})}.

Since it is true for all
\zeta\in\mathcal{E}
, we have

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-int}\left(\Theta(e^{it})^{*}\Theta(e^{it})\eta\right)\,dt=0,\quad(\forall\ \eta\in\mathcal{E},n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}).

Thus
\Theta(e^{it})^{*}\Theta(e^{it})\eta
is constant a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
, call it
\xi_{\eta}
. Then

\langle\xi_{\eta},\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left\langle\Theta(e^{it})^{*}\Theta(e^{it})\eta,\eta\right\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}dt=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|\Theta(e^{it})\eta\|_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}^{2}dt=\|\Theta\eta\|_{H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\|\theta_{m}\eta\|_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}^{2}.

So we get
\Theta(e^{it})^{*}\Theta(e^{it})\eta=\xi_{\eta}=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\theta_{m}^{*}\theta_{m}\eta.
Note that this infinite series
\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\theta_{m}^{*}\theta_{m}
is the strong operator limit of
\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}\theta_{m}^{*}\theta_{m}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
and hence a projection (see
[
6
, Chapter II, Problem 3.5]
). Thus
\Theta(e^{it})^{*}\Theta(e^{it})
is a constant projection a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
and so is
I-\Theta(e^{it})^{*}\Theta(e^{it})
.
Therefore,

\Delta_{\Theta}(e^{it})=\Delta_{\Theta}(e^{it})^{2}=\Delta_{\Theta}(e^{it})^{*}.

For the sake of brevity, we write
\Delta_{\Theta}(e^{it})=\Delta_{\Theta}
as it is a constant projection (independent of
t
). And for the same reason,

L_{\Delta_{\Theta}}|_{H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})}=T_{\Delta_{\Theta}}.

Set

\mathcal{K}=L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}\oplus\Delta_{\Theta}L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}},\quad\mathcal{K}_{+}=H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\Delta_{\Theta}L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}},

and

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{K}_{+}\ominus\{\Theta h\oplus\Delta_{\Theta}h:h\in{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\}.

Let
U
denote the multiplication by
e^{it}
on
\mathcal{K}
. Then
U
is unitary.
Consider
U_{+}=U|_{\mathcal{K}_{+}}
and let
T
be an operator on
\mathcal{H}
defined by

T^{*}=U_{+}^{*}|_{\mathcal{H}}.

Thus

T=P_{\mathcal{H}}U_{+}|_{\mathcal{H}},

where
P_{\mathcal{H}}
is the orthogonal projection of
\mathcal{K}_{+}
onto
\mathcal{H}
. From
Sz.-Nagy-Foiaş construction, it is clear that
T
is a c.n.u. contraction.

We first describe the model space
\mathcal{H}
and the model operator
T
.
Suppose
f\oplus\Delta_{\Theta}g\in\mathcal{H}
, where
f\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})
and
g\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}
. Then for each
h\in{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
,

\displaystyle 0=\langle f\oplus\Delta_{\Theta}g,\Theta h\oplus\Delta_{\Theta}h\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\langle T_{\Theta}^{*}f+P_{+}\Delta_{\Theta}g,h\rangle_{H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})}.

Therefore,
T_{\Theta}^{*}f+P_{+}\Delta_{\Theta}g=0
, i.e.,
P_{+}\Delta_{\Theta}g=-T_{\Theta}^{*}f
. Since
\Delta_{\Theta}
is constant,

L_{\Delta_{\Theta}}P_{+}g=P_{+}L_{\Delta_{\Theta}}g=P_{+}\Delta_{\Theta}g=-T_{\Theta}^{*}f.

It follows that
T_{\Theta}^{*}f\in\mathcal{R}(L_{\Delta_{\Theta}})
. Therefore,

T_{\Theta}^{*}f=L_{\Delta_{\Theta}}T_{\Theta}^{*}f=T_{\Delta_{\Theta}}T_{\Theta}^{*}f=0,

where the last equality holds by the definition of
\Delta_{\Theta}
. Again,
L_{\Delta_{\Theta}}P_{+}g=P_{+}\Delta_{\Theta}g=0
. Thus

\mathcal{H}=\left\{(I-T_{\Theta}T_{\Theta}^{*})f\oplus\Delta_{\Theta}g:f\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D}),g\in[{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}\right\}.

Let
f=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})
, and
g=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}\bar{z}^{n}\in[{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}
.
Now by Lemma
3.4
,

(I-T_{\Theta}T_{\Theta}^{*})f=a_{0}+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(I-\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}z^{n}.

Let

h=(I-T_{\Theta}T_{\Theta}^{*})f\oplus\Delta_{\Theta}g\in\mathcal{H}.

Then

\displaystyle Th

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{H}}U_{+}\left(a_{0}+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(I-\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}e^{int}\oplus\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\Delta_{\Theta}b_{n}e^{-int}\right)

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{H}}\left(a_{0}e^{it}+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(I-\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}e^{i(n+1)t}\oplus\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\Delta_{\Theta}b_{n}e^{i(-n+1)t}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(I-\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n+1}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}e^{i(n+1)t}\oplus\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}\Delta_{\Theta}b_{n}e^{i(-n+1)t}.

Also, for
p\geq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle T^{*p}T^{p}T^{*p}h

\displaystyle=T^{*p}T^{p}\left(\sum\limits_{n=p}^{\infty}\left(I-\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}e^{i(n-p)t}\oplus\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\Delta_{\Theta}b_{n}e^{i(-n-p)t}\right)

\displaystyle=T^{*p}\left(\sum\limits_{n=p}^{\infty}\left(I-\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}e^{int}\oplus\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\Delta_{\Theta}b_{n}e^{-int}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=p}^{\infty}\left(I-\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}e^{i(n-p)t}\oplus\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\Delta_{\Theta}b_{n}e^{i(-n-p)t}

\displaystyle=T^{*p}((I-T_{\Theta}T_{\Theta}^{*})f\oplus\Delta_{\Theta}g)=T^{*p}h.

Therefore,
T
is a power partial isometry on
\mathcal{H}
.

Our remaining task is to show that the characteristic function of
T
coincides with
\Theta
. In order to do that, first we have to find defect spaces, namely,

\mathcal{D}_{T}=\mathcal{R}(I-T^{*}T)=\mathcal{N}(T)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}=\mathcal{R}(I-TT^{*})=\mathcal{N}(T^{*}).

Now
h=(I-T_{\Theta}T_{\Theta}^{*})f\oplus\Delta_{\Theta}g\in\mathcal{N}(T)
if and only if

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(I-\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n+1}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}e^{i(n+1)t}\oplus\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}\Delta_{\Theta}b_{n}e^{i(-n+1)t}=0.

Equivalently,

\displaystyle\left(I-\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n+1}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}

\displaystyle=0\,\,~~\forall\,n\geq 0,

\displaystyle\mbox{and}\quad\Delta_{\Theta}b_{n}

\displaystyle=0\,\,~~\forall\,n\geq 2.

The former equality says that

\displaystyle a_{0}

\displaystyle=\theta_{1}\theta_{1}^{*}a_{0}

\displaystyle\mbox{and}\quad\left(I-\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}

\displaystyle=\theta_{n+1}\theta_{n+1}^{*}a_{n}\,\,~~\forall\,n\geq 1.

Therefore,

\mathcal{D}_{T}=\left\{\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\theta_{n+1}\theta_{n+1}^{*}a_{n}z^{n}\oplus\Delta_{\Theta}b\bar{z}:(a_{n})\in\ell^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{*}),b\in\mathcal{E}\right\}.

Now
h\in\mathcal{N}(T^{*})
if and only if

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(I-\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}e^{i(n-1)t}\oplus\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\Delta_{\Theta}b_{n}e^{i(-n-1)t}=0,

i.e.,

\displaystyle\left(I-\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}

\displaystyle=0\,\,~~\forall\,n\geq 1

\displaystyle\mbox{and}\quad\Delta_{\Theta}b_{n}

\displaystyle=0\,\,~~\forall\,n\geq 1.

Hence,
\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}=\mathcal{E}_{*}
.

We can now proceed to determine the characteristic function
\Theta_{T}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}_{T},\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}})
of
T
.
Let

h=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\theta_{n+1}\theta_{n+1}^{*}a_{n}z^{n}+\Delta_{\Theta}b\bar{z}\in\mathcal{D}_{T}.

Then, for
z\in\mathbb{D},

\displaystyle\Theta_{T}(z)(h)

\displaystyle=\left(-T+z(I-TT^{*})(I-zT^{*})^{-1}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\theta_{n+1}\theta_{n+1}^{*}a_{n}e^{int}\oplus\Delta_{\Theta}be^{-it}\right)

\displaystyle=zP_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}T^{*j}z^{j}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\theta_{n+1}\theta_{n+1}^{*}a_{n}e^{int}\oplus\Delta_{\Theta}be^{-it}\right)

\displaystyle=zP_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum\limits_{n=j}^{\infty}\theta_{n+1}\theta_{n+1}^{*}a_{n}e^{i(n-j)t}\right)z^{j}\oplus\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{\Theta}be^{-i(j+1)t}z^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=z\left(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\theta_{j+1}\theta_{j+1}^{*}a_{j}z^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\theta_{j+1}\theta_{j+1}^{*}a_{j}z^{j+1}.

Now we will show that
\Theta_{T}
coincides with
\Theta
. Define a map
\tau:\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{T}
by

\tau(a)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\theta_{n+1}az^{n}\oplus\Delta_{\Theta}a\bar{z}\quad(a\in\mathcal{E}).

Then
\tau
is a well-defined linear map. Also,

\displaystyle\|\tau(a)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\|\theta_{n+1}a\|^{2}+\left\|\Delta_{\Theta}a\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\|\theta_{n+1}a\|^{2}+\|a\|^{2}-\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\|\theta_{m}a\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|a\|^{2}.

Thus
\tau
is an isometry. To prove
\tau
is surjective as well,
let

h=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\theta_{n+1}\theta^{*}_{n+1}a_{n}z^{n}\oplus\Delta_{\Theta}b\bar{z}\in\mathcal{D}_{T},

where
(a_{n})\in\ell^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{*})
and
b\in\mathcal{E}.
For
a=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\theta^{*}_{m+1}a_{m}+\Delta_{\Theta}b\in\mathcal{E}
, it is easy to check that

\tau(a)=h
. Hence
\tau
is a unitary.

Also, let
\tau_{*}:\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{*}
be the identity map, that is,

\tau_{*}(a)=a
. Now, for
a\in\mathcal{E}
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
,

\displaystyle\tau_{*}\Theta_{T}(z)\tau(a)

\displaystyle=\tau_{*}\Theta_{T}(z)\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\theta_{n+1}az^{n}\oplus\Delta_{\Theta}a\bar{z}\right)

\displaystyle=\tau_{*}\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\theta_{n+1}az^{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\theta_{n}az^{n}

\displaystyle=\Theta(z)a.

This finishes the proof.

It is well known that the characteristic function is inner if and only if the corresponding contraction is pure (see
[
19
]
). The following corollary is in that direction, and we omit the proof because it follows a similar approach to the proof of the above Theorem
3.5
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
\Theta:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
be a purely contractive analytic inner function such that

\Theta(z)=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\theta_{m}z^{m}\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

where each
\theta_{m}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
is a partial isometry for
m\geq 1
. Then there exist a Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}=\left\{(I-T_{\Theta}T_{\Theta}^{*})f:f\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})\right\}

and a pure power partial isometry
T
on
\mathcal{H}
defined by

T^{*}u=e^{-it}\left(u(e^{it})-u(0)\right)\quad(u\in\mathcal{H})

such that the characteristic function of
T
coincides with
\Theta
.

As an application of Theorem
3.5
, let us see the case of contractive analytic polynomial with partially isometric coefficients. In
[
10
]
, Foiaş and Sarkar characterized the c.n.u. contractions with polynomial characteristic functions and proved that such operators have an upper triangular matricial representation of the form

\begin{pmatrix}S&*&*\\
0&N&*\\
0&0&C\end{pmatrix}

where
S
,
N
, and
C
are unilateral shift, nilpotent and backward shift, respectively. In our case, since the corresponding c.n.u. contraction is a power partial isometry, we get a block diagonal representation and also we find the Halmos–Wallen decomposition spaces explicitly.

Theorem 3.7
.

In the setting of Theorem
3.5
, let
\Theta:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
be a contractive analytic polynomial of degree
k
such that

\Theta(z)=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k}\theta_{m}z^{m}\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

where each
\theta_{m}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
is a partial isometry for
m\geq 1
and
T
on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is the corresponding c.n.u. power partial isometry. Then there exist
T
-reducing subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{s}=\left(I-\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})
,

\mathcal{H}_{b}=\left(I-\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k}\theta_{m}^{*}\theta_{m}\right)[H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}
and
\mathcal{H}_{t}=\mathcal{H}\ominus(\mathcal{H}_{s}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{b})
such that
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{s}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{t}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{b}
and

T=\begin{pmatrix}S&0&0\\
0&N&0\\
0&0&C\end{pmatrix},

where
S\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{s})
is a unilateral shift,
N\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{t})
is nilpotent of index
k
, and
C\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{b})
is a backward shift.

Proof.

From Theorem
3.5
, we get

\mathcal{H}=\{(I-T_{\Theta}T_{\Theta}^{*})f\oplus\Delta_{\Theta}g:f\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D}),g\in[H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}\}.

Lemma
3.3
infers that
\theta_{j}^{*}\theta_{i}=0_{\mathcal{E}}
and
\theta_{i}\theta_{j}^{*}=0_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}
for
i\neq j
. Moreover,

\Delta_{\Theta}=I-\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k}\theta_{m}^{*}\theta_{m}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Delta_{\Theta^{*}}=I-\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}

are orthogonal projections. Set

\mathcal{H}_{b}=\Delta_{\Theta}[H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}.

Then it is trivial to check that
\mathcal{H}_{b}
is invariant under
T^{*}
. Also, as in proof of Theorem
3.5
, for
g=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}\bar{z}^{n}\in[{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}
,

T\left(\Delta_{\Theta}g\right)=\Delta_{\Theta}\left(\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}b_{n}e^{i(-n+1)t}\right)\in\Delta_{\Theta}[H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}.

Hence
\mathcal{H}_{b}
reduces
T
.
Now consider

\displaystyle TT^{*}\left(\Delta_{\Theta}g\right)

\displaystyle=T\left(\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\Delta_{\Theta}b_{n}e^{i(-n-1)t}\right)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\Theta}\left(\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}e^{-int}\right)=\Delta_{\Theta}g.

Also note that

\left\|T^{p}(\Delta_{\Theta}g)\right\|^{2}=\left\|\sum\limits_{n=p+1}^{\infty}\Delta_{\Theta}b_{n}e^{i(-n+p)t}\right\|^{2}\leq\sum\limits_{n=p+1}^{\infty}\left\|b_{n}\right\|^{2}\rightarrow 0

as
p\rightarrow\infty
. Hence
T|_{\mathcal{H}_{b}}
is a backward shift.
Define

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{H}\ominus\mathcal{H}_{b}=(I-T_{\Theta}T_{\Theta}^{*})H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D}).

Now we define another space
\mathcal{H}_{s}
as

\mathcal{H}_{s}=\Delta_{\Theta^{*}}H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D}).

Note that
\mathcal{H}_{s}
is a subspace of
\mathcal{M}
and it can be proved by using the following fact:

\left(I-\sum\limits_{l=1}^{n}\theta_{l}\theta_{l}^{*}\right)\left(I-\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)=\left(I-\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)\,\,\forall\,\,n\leq k.

For
f=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})
, using Lemma
3.4
,

\displaystyle T(\Delta_{\Theta^{*}}f)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{k-1}\left(I-\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n+1}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)\Delta_{\Theta^{*}}a_{n}e^{i(n+1)t}+\sum\limits_{n=k}^{\infty}\Delta_{\Theta^{*}}a_{n}e^{i(n+1)t}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\Theta^{*}}\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{i(n+1)t}\right)\in\Delta_{\Theta^{*}}H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D}).

And
T^{*}\mathcal{H}_{s}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{s}
is trivial to prove. Hence,
\mathcal{H}_{s}
reduces
T
. Also,

\displaystyle T^{*}T(\Delta_{\Theta^{*}}f)

\displaystyle=T^{*}\left(\Delta_{\Theta^{*}}\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{i(n+1)t}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\Theta^{*}}\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{int}\right)=\Delta_{\Theta^{*}}f.

Therefore,
T|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}
is an isometry. Furthermore, it is pure as
T^{*p}=U_{+}^{*p}|_{\mathcal{H}}
for all
p\geq 0
.
Finally, set
\mathcal{H}_{t}=\mathcal{M}\ominus\mathcal{H}_{s}
. Let
\theta_{0}=0
and
\theta_{n}=0
for all
n>k
. Then
(I-T_{\Theta}T_{\Theta}^{*})f\in\mathcal{H}_{t}
if and only if

\displaystyle\left\langle(I-T_{\Theta}T_{\Theta}^{*})f,\Delta_{\Theta^{*}}f^{\prime}\right\rangle=0\quad\forall\,\,f^{\prime}\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(I-\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}z^{n},\Delta_{\Theta^{*}}f^{\prime}\right\rangle\quad\forall\,\,f^{\prime}\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta_{\Theta^{*}}f,f^{\prime}\right\rangle=0\quad\forall\,\,f^{\prime}\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D}).

Hence we get
\Delta_{\Theta^{*}}f=0
, i.e.,
\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}a_{n}=a_{n}
for all
n\geq 0
.

Therefore,

\displaystyle(I-T_{\Theta}T_{\Theta}^{*})f

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{k-1}\left(I-\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}z^{n}+\sum\limits_{n=k}^{\infty}\left(I-\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}z^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{k-1}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}-\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}z^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{k-1}\left(\sum\limits_{m=n+1}^{k}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}z^{n}.

So, we get

\mathcal{H}_{t}=\left\{\sum\limits_{n=0}^{k-1}\left(\sum\limits_{m=n+1}^{k}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}z^{n}:a_{n}\in\mathcal{E}_{*},1\leq n\leq k-1\right\}.

Since
\mathcal{H}_{t}=\mathcal{H}\ominus(\mathcal{H}_{b}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{s})
, it is
T
-reducing.
Moreover, for
(I-T_{\Theta}T_{\Theta}^{*})f\in H_{t}
,

\displaystyle T((I-T_{\Theta}T_{\Theta}^{*})f)

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{H}}\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{k-1}\left(\sum\limits_{m=n+1}^{k}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}e^{i(n+1)t}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{k-1}\left(I-\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n+1}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{m=n+1}^{k}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}e^{i(n+1)t}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{k-2}\left(\sum\limits_{m=n+2}^{k}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\right)a_{n}e^{i(n+1)t}.

Hence
T^{k}((I-T_{\Theta}T_{\Theta}^{*})f)=0
, i.e.,

T|_{\mathcal{H}_{t}}
is a nilpotent operator of index
k
. This completes the proof.

The above proof yields something more and there are few remarks in order:

Remark 3.8
.

For
m\in\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
, set

\mathcal{H}_{m}=\left\{\sum\limits_{n=0}^{m-1}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}a_{n}z^{n}:a_{n}\in\mathcal{E}_{*},0\leq n\leq m-1\right\}.

Then each
\mathcal{H}_{m}
reduces
T
. Indeed, for
g=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{m-1}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{H}_{m}
,

Tg=P_{\mathcal{H}}\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{m-1}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}a_{n}e^{i(n+1)t}\right)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{m-2}\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}a_{n}e^{i(n+1)t}\in\mathcal{H}_{m}.

Also note that
\mathcal{H}_{m}=\theta_{m}\theta_{m}^{*}\left(H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})\ominus z^{m}H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})\right)
. Thus
\mathcal{M}
is
T^{*}
-invariant. It is easy to check that
T|_{\mathcal{H}_{m}}
is a truncated shift of index
m
for
m\in\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
and

\mathcal{H}_{t}=\bigoplus\limits_{m=1}^{k}\mathcal{H}_{m}.

Remark 3.9
.

If
\Theta:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
is a contractive analytic inner function such that
\Theta(z)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\theta_{m}z^{m}
with

\theta_{i}\theta_{j}^{*}=0_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}
for all
i\neq j
, then each
\theta_{i}
is a partial isometry. Indeed, if
\Theta
is inner, then
\Theta(e^{it})
is an isometry from
\mathcal{E}
to
\mathcal{E}_{*}
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
.
Thus, for
a\in\mathcal{E}
,

a=\Theta(e^{it})^{*}\Theta(e^{it})a=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\theta_{m}^{*}\theta_{m}a.

Pre-multiplying by
\theta_{i}
yields
\theta_{i}\theta_{i}^{*}\theta_{i}a=\theta_{i}a\,\,~~\forall\,\,i
. Hence each
\theta_{i}
is a partial isometry. One can compare this with Lemma
3.3
.

4.
Partially isometric Toeplitz operators

In this section we discuss about the partially isometric Toeplitz operators with operator-valued symbol and a complete characterization of such symbols has been given.

Let
T
be a power partial isometry on
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\Theta:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}_{T},\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}})
defined by,

\Theta(z)=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\theta_{m}z^{m}

be the characteristic function of
T
. Then each
\theta_{m}
is a partial isometry
by Theorem
3.5
. Now consider the Toeplitz operator
T_{\Theta}
from
H^{2}_{\mathcal{D}_{T}}(\mathbb{D})
to
H^{2}_{\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}}(\mathbb{D})
with operator-valued symbol
\Theta
.
As noticed above,

\Theta(e^{it})^{*}\Theta(e^{it})
is a constant projection a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
. Therefore,

T_{\Theta}T_{\Theta}^{*}T_{\Theta}=T_{\Theta\Theta^{*}\Theta}=T_{\Theta}

which implies
T_{\Theta}
is a partial isometry. The next natural question one can ask is:
Characterize
\Gamma\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}_{T},\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}})}
such that, for

\Phi=\Theta_{T}+\Gamma
,
T_{\Phi}
is a partial isometry.

Recently, Sarkar (cf.
[
18
]
) characterized the partially isometric Toeplitz operators on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
for operator-valued symbols and also raised the question of characterizing partially isometric symbols
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}
such that
T_{\Phi}
is a partial isometry.
We have given here some necessary conditions on such symbols in one variable in Proposition
4.5
and a full characterization of such symbols for finite-dimensional underlying spaces. Before proceeding, we have also given a characterization of partially isometric Toeplitz operators
T_{\Phi}
from
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
to
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})
and the proof of this result is similar to that in
[
18
]
, which we had discovered independently without the knowledge of the cited article. For the sake of completion, we present a short proof.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
be nonzero. Then
T_{\Phi}
is a partially isometric Toeplitz operator from
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
to
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})
if and only if there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}
and inner functions
\Theta(z)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\theta_{m}z^{m}\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
and
\Psi(z)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\psi_{m}z^{m}\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})}
satisfying
\theta_{m}\psi_{n}^{*}=0
for all
m,n\geq 1
such that

T_{\Phi}=T_{\Theta}T_{\Psi}^{*}.

Proof.

Suppose that
T_{\Phi}:H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})
is a nonzero partially isometric Toeplitz operator. Then

\mathcal{R}(T_{\Phi}^{*})=[\mathcal{N}(T_{\Phi})]^{\perp}=\{f\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}):\|T_{\Phi}f\|=\|f\|\}.

For
f\in\mathcal{R}(T_{\Phi}^{*})
, observe that

\|M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}f\|=\|f\|=\|T_{\Phi}f\|=\left\|(M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{*}})^{*}T_{\Phi}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}f\right\|\leq\|T_{\Phi}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}f\|\leq\|M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}f\|.

Therefore,

\|T_{\Phi}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}f\|=\|M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}f\|,\quad\text{i.e.},\quad M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}f\in[\mathcal{N}(T_{\Phi})]^{\perp}.

It follows that
[\mathcal{N}(T_{\Phi})]^{\perp}
is
M_{z}
-invariant. Hence the Beurling-Lax-Halmos theorem yields that there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}
and an inner function
\Psi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})}
such that

(4.1)

\mathcal{R}(T_{\Phi}^{*})=T_{\Psi}H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D}).

Now, by Douglas’ lemma
[
9
]
, there exist a contraction
X:H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})
such that

T_{\Phi}^{*}=T_{\Psi}X.

This implies that
X=T_{\Psi}^{*}T_{\Phi}^{*}
as
T_{\Psi}
is an isometry. Set

\Theta=\Phi\Psi
. Then
X=T_{\Theta}^{*}
. Therefore,

T_{\Phi}=T_{\Theta}T_{\Psi}^{*}.

Note that

T_{\Theta}^{*}T_{\Theta}=T_{\Psi}^{*}T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Psi}=T_{\Psi}^{*}T_{\Psi}=I,

where the second last equality holds from (
4.1
) and the fact that
T_{\Phi}
is a partial isometry. Thus,
\Theta\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
is an inner function.
Let

\Theta(z)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\theta_{m}z^{m}\text{ and }\Psi(z)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\psi_{m}z^{m}

where
\theta_{m}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E}_{*})
and
\psi_{m}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})
for all
m\geq 0
. Since
T_{\Phi}=T_{\Theta}T_{\Psi}^{*}
is a Toeplitz operator, we have

(M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{*}})^{*}T_{\Theta}T_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}=T_{\Theta}T_{\Psi}^{*}.

Let
\eta\in\mathcal{E}
and
n\geq 0
. Consider

\displaystyle(M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{*}})^{*}T_{\Theta}T_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}(\eta z^{n})

\displaystyle=(M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{*}})^{*}T_{\Theta}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n+1}\psi_{m}^{*}\eta z^{n-m+1}\right)

\displaystyle=(M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{*}})^{*}T_{\Theta}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n}\psi_{m}^{*}\eta z^{n-m+1}\right)+(M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{*}})^{*}T_{\Theta}(\psi_{n+1}^{*}\eta)

\displaystyle=(M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{*}})^{*}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{*}}T_{\Theta}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n}\psi_{m}^{*}\eta z^{n-m}\right)+(M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{*}})^{*}T_{\Theta}(\psi_{n+1}^{*}\eta)

\displaystyle=T_{\Theta}T_{\Psi}^{*}(\eta z^{n})+(M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{*}})^{*}T_{\Theta}(\psi_{n+1}^{*}\eta).

It follows that

(M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{*}})^{*}T_{\Theta}(\psi_{n+1}\eta)=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\theta_{m}\psi_{n+1}^{*}\eta z^{m-1}=0.

Since
\eta\in\mathcal{E}
and
n
are arbitrary, we obtain
\theta_{m}\psi_{n}^{*}=0
for all
m,n\geq 1
.

The converse also holds, which is trivial to prove.

Let us first see an example for this result:

Example 4.2
.

Let
\Theta,\Psi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{3})}
defined by

\Theta(e^{it})=\begin{pmatrix}e^{it}&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&e^{it}\end{pmatrix}\text{ and }\Psi(e^{it})=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
0&e^{it}&0\\
1&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then
\Theta
and
\Psi
are inner functions and observe that
\theta_{1}\psi_{1}^{*}=0
. Also,

T_{\Phi}=T_{\Theta}T_{\Psi}^{*}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&T_{e^{it}}\\
0&T_{e^{-it}}&0\\
T_{e^{it}}&0&0\end{pmatrix},

is a partial isometry.

In order to answer the stated question, first we prove some necessary conditions on
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
for which
T_{\Phi}
is a partial isometry.

Remark 4.3
.

Define a set

\bar{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}:=\{\Psi(\bar{z}):\Psi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}\}.

Then
\bar{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}\subset L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
and hence
{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}+\bar{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}\subset L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
. Consider

\mathcal{A}:={H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}+\bar{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}.

For
\Phi\in\mathcal{A}
, in the sequel, we write
\Phi_{+}(z)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{m}z^{m}
and
\Phi_{-}(z)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{-m}\bar{z}^{m}
as the analytic and co-analytic part of
\Phi
, where
\varphi_{n}=\widehat{\Phi}(n)\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
be such that
T_{\Phi}:H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})
is a nonzero partially isometric Toeplitz operator. Then
\Phi(e^{it})
is a partial isometry a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
and
\Phi\in\mathcal{A}.

Proof.

Suppose
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
such that
T_{\Phi}:H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})
is a nonzero partially isometric Toeplitz operator. Then Theorem
4.1
infers that there exist a Hilbert space

\mathcal{F}
and inner functions
\Theta(z)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\theta_{m}z^{m}\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
,
\Psi(z)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\psi_{m}z^{m}\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})}
satisfying
\theta_{m}\psi_{n}^{*}=0
for all
m,n\geq 1
such that

T_{\Phi}=T_{\Theta}T_{\Psi}^{*}.

Since
T_{\Theta}T_{\Psi}^{*}
is a Toeplitz operator,
T_{\Phi}=T_{\Theta{\Psi}^{*}}
and hence
\Phi(e^{it})=\Theta(e^{it})\Psi(e^{it})^{*}
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
. Now
\Phi(e^{it})
is a partial isometry a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
as

\Phi(e^{it})\Phi(e^{it})^{*}\Phi(e^{it})=\Theta(e^{it})\Psi(e^{it})^{*}\Psi(e^{it})\Theta(e^{it})^{*}\Theta(e^{it})\Psi(e^{it})^{*}=\Theta(e^{it})\Psi(e^{it})^{*}=\Phi(e^{it}).

Since
\theta_{n}\psi_{m}^{*}=0
for all
m,n\geq 1
,

T_{\Phi}=T_{\Theta}T_{\Psi}^{*}=\Theta(0)T_{\Psi}^{*}+T_{\Theta}\Psi(0)^{*}-\Theta(0)\Psi(0)^{*},

that is,

(4.2)

\Phi(e^{it})=\theta_{0}\Psi(e^{it})^{*}+\Theta(e^{it})\psi_{0}^{*}-\theta_{0}\psi_{0}^{*}\quad(\text{a.e.\ on }\mathbb{T}).

Thus
\Phi\in\mathcal{A}
with
\Phi_{+}=\Theta\psi_{0}^{*}
and
\Phi_{-}=\theta_{0}\Psi^{*}
.

Therefore, by virtue of the previous theorem, while characterizing the symbol of a partially isometric Toeplitz operator, it suffices to restrict our attention to
\Phi\in\mathcal{A}
rather than considering
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{A}
be such that
T_{\Phi}:H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})
is a nonzero partially isometric Toeplitz operator. Then
\Phi
satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

\Phi_{+}(e^{it})^{*}\Phi_{+}(e^{it})
and
\Phi_{-}(e^{it})\Phi_{-}(e^{it})^{*}

are operator-valued constant functions a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
, where
\Phi_{+}\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
and
\Phi_{-}^{*}\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{*},\mathcal{E})}
are contractive analytic functions.

(2)

\hat{\Phi}(m)^{*}\hat{\Phi}(-n)=0_{\mathcal{E}}
and
\hat{\Phi}(-n)\hat{\Phi}(m)^{*}=0_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}
for all
m,n\geq 1
.

Proof.

Suppose
\Phi\in\mathcal{A}
such that
T_{\Phi}:H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})
is a nonzero partial isometric Toeplitz operator.
Write

(4.3)

\Phi=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{-m}\bar{z}^{m}+\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{m}z^{m}.

From the proof of Lemma
4.4
,
\Phi_{+}=\Theta\Psi(0)^{*}
and
\Phi_{-}=\Theta(0)\Psi^{*}
for operator valued inner functions
\Theta
and
\Psi
. Observe that

(\Theta(e^{it})\psi_{0}^{*})^{*}(\Theta(e^{it})\psi_{0}^{*})=\psi_{0}\psi_{0}^{*}\,\,\quad(\text{a.e.\ on }\mathbb{T}).

Thus we get
\Phi_{+}(e^{it})^{*}\Phi_{+}(e^{it})
is a constant positive operator a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
. Similarly,
\Phi_{-}(e^{it})\Phi_{-}(e^{it})^{*}=(\theta_{0}\Psi(e^{it})^{*})(\theta_{0}\Psi(e^{it})^{*})^{*}=\theta_{0}\theta_{0}^{*}
is a constant positive operator a.e. on

\mathbb{T}
.

Now let
\varphi_{-m_{0}}
be nonzero for some
m_{0}\geq 1
. For
\eta\in\mathcal{E}
, define a function
f\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})
by

f(z)=(T_{\Phi}M_{z}-M_{z}T_{\Phi})\left(\eta z^{m_{0}-1}\right).

Then

\displaystyle f(e^{it})

\displaystyle=T_{\Phi}(\eta e^{im_{0}t})-e^{it}T_{\Phi}(\eta e^{i(m_{0}-1)t})

\displaystyle=P_{+}\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{-m}(\eta)e^{i(-m+m_{0})t}\right)+\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{m}(\eta)e^{i(m+m_{0})t}

\displaystyle\quad-e^{it}P_{+}\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{-m}(\eta)e^{i(-m+m_{0}-1)t}\right)-e^{it}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{m}(\eta)e^{i(m+m_{0}-1)t}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{m_{0}}\varphi_{-m}(\eta)e^{i(-m+m_{0})t}-e^{it}\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{m_{0}-1}\varphi_{-m}(\eta)e^{i(-m+m_{0}-1)t}\right)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{-m_{0}}(\eta).

Since
T_{\Phi}
is a partial isometry,
T_{\Phi}^{*}
is also a partial isometry.
Hence
[\mathcal{N}(T_{\Phi}^{*})]^{\perp}=\mathcal{R}(T_{\Phi})
is
M_{z}
-invariant which yields
f\in\mathcal{R}(T_{\Phi})
.
Also note that

\|f\|=\|T_{\Phi}^{*}f\|=\|P_{+}L_{\Phi}^{*}f\|\leq\|L_{\Phi}^{*}f\|\leq\|f\|.

Therefore,
\|P_{+}L_{\Phi}^{*}f\|=\|L_{\Phi}^{*}f\|
which implies
P_{+}L_{\Phi}^{*}(f)=L_{\Phi}^{*}f
. Hence
L_{\Phi}^{*}f\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
. Then

\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{-m}^{*}\varphi_{-m_{0}}(\eta)e^{imt}+\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{m}^{*}\varphi_{-m_{0}}(\eta)e^{-imt}=\Phi(e^{it})^{*}f(e^{it})\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})

if and only if

\varphi_{m}^{*}\varphi_{-m_{0}}\eta=0\quad\mbox{for all}~~m\geq 1.

Since
m_{0}\geq 1
is arbitrary such that
\varphi_{-m_{0}}\neq 0
, we have
\varphi_{m}^{*}\varphi_{-n}=0
for all
m,n\geq 1
. Note that if
\varphi_{-m}=0
for all
m\geq 1
, then this condition is trivially true.

Again, since
T_{\Phi}^{*}
is also a partial isometry, on replacing
\Phi
by
\Phi^{*}
, we obtain
\varphi_{-n}\varphi_{m}^{*}=0
for all
m,n\geq 1
. This finishes the proof.

Now it is a natural question to ask whether the converse of Proposition
4.5
holds. We give an affirmative answer in the case when
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}_{*}
are finite-dimensional. Before that, a few general observations are nevertheless in order.

Remark 4.6
.

For finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}_{*}
,
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
can be viewed as a Hilbert space (with Hilbert-Schmidt norm). And thus we may view
L^{2}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
as a space of square summable Fourier series with coefficients in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
with the following inner product (see
[
13
]
).

\langle A,B\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}tr(B^{*}A)d\mu=\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}tr(B_{n}^{*}A_{n}),

where
A(e^{it})=\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}A_{n}e^{int}
,
B(e^{it})=\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}B_{n}e^{int}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
with
A_{n},B_{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
for all
n
. In particular,
L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}\subset L^{2}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}.
Note that since
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})
is finite-dimensional, operator norm and Hilbert-Schmidt norm are equivalent. So, we continue to consider on
L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
and
H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
, the equivalent norm obtained by operator norm.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*}
be finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces and
\Phi\in\mathcal{A}
be a nonzero partial isometry a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
which satisfies
(\ref{con1})
and
(\ref{con2})
of Proposition
4.5
. Then
T_{\Phi}
is a partially isometric Toeplitz operator from
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
to
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\Phi\in\mathcal{A}
is a partial isometry a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
such that
(\ref{con1})
and
(\ref{con2})
of Proposition
4.5
holds where
\Phi_{+}(z)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{m}z^{m}\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
and
\Phi_{-}(z)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{-m}\bar{z}^{m}\in\bar{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
.

Let
\Phi_{+}(e^{it})^{*}\Phi_{+}(e^{it})=T
and
\Phi_{-}(e^{it})\Phi_{-}(e^{it})^{*}=V
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
. Then
T
and
V
are positive operators.
For
\eta\in\mathcal{E}
, consider

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{+}\eta\|^{2}_{H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|\Phi_{+}(e^{it})\eta\|^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left\langle\Phi_{+}(e^{it})\eta,\Phi_{+}(e^{it})\eta\right\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left\langle\Phi_{+}(e^{it})^{*}\Phi_{+}(e^{it})\eta,\eta\right\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}dt

(4.4)

\displaystyle=\langle T\eta,\eta\rangle.

Also,

(4.5)

\|\Phi_{+}\eta\|^{2}_{H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})}=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\|\varphi_{m}\eta\|^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}=\left\langle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{m}^{*}\varphi_{m}\eta,\eta\right\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}.

Note that the infinite series
\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{m}^{*}\varphi_{m}
is absolutely convergent since
\Phi_{+}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}\subset L^{2}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
(see Remark
4.6
). Thus from (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we get

(4.6)

T\eta=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{m}^{*}\varphi_{m}\eta\quad(\eta\in\mathcal{E}).

Now for
\eta\in\mathcal{E}
, consider

\displaystyle L_{\Phi}L_{\Phi}^{*}L_{\Phi}(\eta)

\displaystyle=L_{\Phi}L_{\Phi}^{*}(\Phi_{+}+\Phi_{-}-\varphi_{0})\eta

\displaystyle=L_{\Phi}((\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})^{*}\Phi_{+}\eta+(\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})^{*}(\Phi_{-}-\varphi_{0})\eta+\Phi_{-}^{*}\Phi_{+}\eta+\Phi_{-}^{*}(\Phi_{-}-\varphi_{0})\eta)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{con2})}}{{=}}L_{\Phi}(T-\varphi_{0}^{*}\Phi_{+}\eta+\Phi_{-}^{*}\varphi_{0}\eta+\varphi_{0}^{*}(\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})\eta+\Phi_{-}^{*}(\Phi_{-}-\varphi_{0})\eta)

\displaystyle=L_{\Phi}(T-\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{0}+\Phi_{-}^{*}\Phi_{-})\eta

\displaystyle=((\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})T-(\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})\varphi^{*}_{0}\varphi_{0}+(\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})\Phi_{-}^{*}\Phi_{-}+\Phi_{-}T-\Phi_{-}\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{0}+\Phi_{-}\Phi_{-}^{*}\Phi_{-})\eta

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{con})}}{{=}}((\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})T-(\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{0}+(\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})\varphi_{0}^{*}\Phi_{-}+\Phi_{-}T-\Phi_{-}\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{0}+V\Phi_{-})\eta.

Since
\Phi(e^{it})
is a partial isometry a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
,
L_{\Phi}L_{\Phi}^{*}L_{\Phi}=L_{\Phi}
. Thus

(4.7)

((\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})T+(\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})\varphi_{0}^{*}(\Phi_{-}-\varphi_{0})+\Phi_{-}T-\Phi_{-}\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{0}+V\Phi_{-})\eta=(\Phi_{+}+\Phi_{-}-\varphi_{0})\eta.

Now let
j\geq 1
, then applying
L_{\varphi_{-j}}^{*}
on both sides of (
4.7
) and using condition
(\ref{con2})
, we get

(4.8)

(\varphi_{-j}^{*}\Phi_{-}T-\varphi_{-j}^{*}\Phi_{-}\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{0}+\varphi_{-j}^{*}V\Phi_{-})\eta=\varphi_{-j}^{*}\Phi_{-}(\eta).

Comparing the
(-j)^{th}
Fourier coefficient on both sides, we get

(\varphi_{-j}^{*}\varphi_{-j}T-\varphi_{-j}^{*}\varphi_{-j}\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{0}+\varphi_{-j}^{*}V\varphi_{-j})\eta=\varphi_{-j}^{*}\varphi_{-j}\eta.

Since it is true for all
\eta\in\mathcal{E}
, we get

\displaystyle\varphi_{-j}^{*}\varphi_{-j}-\varphi_{-j}^{*}\varphi_{-j}(T-\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{0})-\varphi_{-j}^{*}V\varphi_{-j}=0

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\varphi_{-j}^{*}\varphi_{-j}-\varphi_{-j}^{*}\varphi_{-j}\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{m}^{*}\varphi_{m}\right)-\varphi_{-j}^{*}V\varphi_{-j}=0\quad\text{(By (\ref{Tdef}))}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\varphi_{-j}^{*}\varphi_{-j}-\varphi_{-j}^{*}V\varphi_{-j}=0.

As
I-V\geq 0
, we have

(I-V)\varphi_{-j}=0,

i.e.,

(4.9)

V\varphi_{-j}=\varphi_{-j}\quad\forall\,j\geq 1.

Similarly, one can prove (by applying
L_{\varphi_{j}}
on both sides of (
4.7
) and comparing
j^{th}
coefficient) that

(4.10)

\varphi_{j}T=\varphi_{j}\quad\forall\,j\geq 1.

Also, comparing constant term of (
4.8
), we get

\displaystyle(\varphi_{-j}^{*}\varphi_{0}T-\varphi_{-j}^{*}\varphi_{0}\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{0}+\varphi_{-j}^{*}V\varphi_{0})\eta=\varphi_{-j}^{*}\varphi_{0}(\eta)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\varphi_{-j}^{*}\varphi_{0}(T-\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{0})=0\quad\quad(\text{By (\ref{con3})})

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\varphi_{-j}^{*}\varphi_{0}\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{m}^{*}\varphi_{m}\right)=0\quad\quad(\text{By (\ref{Tdef})})

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}(\varphi_{m}\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{-j})^{*}(\varphi_{m}\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{-j})=0.

Therefore,

(4.11)

\varphi_{m}\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{-j}=0\,\,~~\forall\,\,j,m\geq 1.

In that case, using (
4.9
), (
4.10
), and (
4.11
), equation (
4.7
) will become

\displaystyle((\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})+\Phi_{-}T-\Phi_{-}\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{0}+V\varphi_{0}+(\Phi_{-}-\varphi_{0}))\eta=(\Phi_{+}+\Phi_{-}-\varphi_{0})\eta

(4.12)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle(\Phi_{-}(T-\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{0})+V\varphi_{0})\eta=\varphi_{0}\eta.

Define

T_{\Phi}:=P_{+}^{\mathcal{E}_{*}}L_{\Phi}|_{H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})}.

Now for
\eta\in\mathcal{E}
and
j\geq 0
,

\displaystyle T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}(\eta e^{ijt})

\displaystyle=T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}\left(\Phi_{+}(\eta)e^{ijt}+\sum\limits_{m=0}^{j}\varphi_{-m}(\eta)e^{i(j-m)t}-\varphi_{0}e^{ijt}\right)

\displaystyle=T_{\Phi}P_{+}^{\mathcal{E}}\left(\Phi_{+}^{*}(\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})(\eta e^{ijt})+(\Phi_{-}-\varphi_{0})^{*}(\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})\eta e^{ijt}+\Phi_{+}^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{j}\varphi_{-m}(\eta)e^{i(j-m)t}\right)+\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\left.(\Phi_{-}-\varphi_{0})^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{j}\varphi_{-m}(\eta)e^{i(j-m)t}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{con2})}}{{=}}T_{\Phi}P_{+}^{\mathcal{E}}\left(T\eta e^{ijt}-\Phi_{+}^{*}\varphi_{0}\eta e^{ijt}+\varphi_{0}^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{j}\varphi_{-m}(\eta)e^{i(j-m)t}\right)+(\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})^{*}\varphi_{0}\eta e^{ijt}+\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\left.(\Phi_{-}-\varphi_{0})^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{j}\varphi_{-m}(\eta)e^{i(j-m)t}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=T_{\Phi}\left(T\eta e^{ijt}-\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{0}\eta e^{ijt}+\Phi_{-}^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{j}\varphi_{-m}(\eta)e^{i(j-m)t}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=P_{+}^{\mathcal{E}_{*}}\left((\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})T\eta e^{ijt}+\Phi_{-}T\eta e^{ijt}-(\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{0}\eta e^{ijt}-\Phi_{-}\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{0}\eta e^{ijt}+\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\left.(\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})\Phi_{-}^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{j}\varphi_{-m}(\eta)e^{i(j-m)t}\right)+\Phi_{-}\Phi_{-}^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{j}\varphi_{-m}(\eta)e^{i(j-m)t}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{con4}),(\ref{con2})}}{{=}}P_{+}^{\mathcal{E}_{*}}\left((\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})\eta e^{ijt}+\Phi_{-}T\eta e^{ijt}-(\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{0}\eta e^{ijt}-\Phi_{-}\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{0}\eta e^{ijt}+\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\left.(\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})\varphi_{0}^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{j}\varphi_{-m}(\eta)e^{i(j-m)t}\right)+V\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{j}\varphi_{-m}(\eta)e^{i(j-m)t}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{con5})}}{{=}}P_{+}^{\mathcal{E}_{*}}\left((\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})\eta e^{ijt}+\Phi_{-}T\eta e^{ijt}-(\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{0}\eta e^{ijt}-\Phi_{-}\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{0}\eta e^{ijt}+\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\left.(\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{0}\eta e^{ijt}+V\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{j}\varphi_{-m}(\eta)e^{i(j-m)t}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{con3})}}{{=}}\begin{cases}P_{+}^{\mathcal{E}_{*}}\left((\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})\eta e^{ijt}+\Phi_{-}(T-\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{0})\eta e^{ijt}+V\varphi_{0}\eta e^{ijt}+\sum\limits_{m=1}^{j}\varphi_{-m}(\eta)e^{i(j-m)t}\right),&j>0\\
P_{+}^{\mathcal{E}_{*}}\left((\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})\eta e^{ijt}+\Phi_{-}(T-\varphi_{0}^{*}\varphi_{0})\eta e^{ijt}+V\varphi_{0}\eta e^{ijt}\right),&j=0\end{cases}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{con6})}}{{=}}\begin{cases}(\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})\eta e^{ijt}+\varphi_{0}\eta e^{ijt}+\sum\limits_{m=1}^{j}\varphi_{-m}(\eta)e^{i(j-m)t},&j>0\\
(\Phi_{+}-\varphi_{0})\eta e^{ijt}+\varphi_{0}\eta e^{ijt},&j=0\end{cases}

\displaystyle=T_{\Phi}(\eta e^{ijt}).

Thus
T_{\Phi}
is a partially isometric Toeplitz operator. This completes the proof.

Combining Propositions
4.5
and
4.7
, we get the following result.

Theorem 4.8
.

For finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}_{*}
, let
\Phi\in\mathcal{A}
be such that
\Phi(e^{it})
is a nonzero partial isometry a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
. Then
T_{\Phi}
is a partially isometric Toeplitz operator if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

\Phi_{+}(e^{it})^{*}\Phi_{+}(e^{it})
and
\Phi_{-}(e^{it})\Phi_{-}(e^{it})^{*}

are operator-valued constant functions a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
, where
\Phi_{+}\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{*})}
and
\Phi_{-}^{*}\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{*},\mathcal{E})}
are contractive analytic functions.

(2)

\hat{\Phi}(m)^{*}\hat{\Phi}(-n)=0_{\mathcal{E}}
and
\hat{\Phi}(-n)\hat{\Phi}(m)^{*}=0_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}
for all
m,n\geq 1
.

We conclude this section with the following remark.

Remark 4.9
.

In particular, for
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{*}=\mathbb{C}
(scalar-valued Hardy space), the above theorem says that for
0\neq\Phi\in L^{\infty}
such that
\Phi(e^{it})\overline{\Phi(e^{it})}\Phi(e^{it})=\Phi(e^{it})
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
,
T_{\Phi}\in\mathcal{B}(H^{2}(\mathbb{D}))
is a partial isometry if and only if
\overline{\varphi}_{-n}\varphi_{m}=0
for all
m,n\geq 1
, i.e., either all negative Fourier coefficients are zero or all positive coefficients are zero. Hence,
T_{\Phi}
is either an isometry or a co-isometry which was first proved by Brown and Douglas in
[
4
]
.

5.
Examples

In this final section we shall illustrate some examples that none of the conditions of Theorem
4.8
is redundant.

Example 5.1
.

Let
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{3})}
be defined by

\Phi(z)=\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{z}{2}&\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\\
0&0&0\\
\bar{z}&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then

\Phi(z)=\varphi_{-1}\bar{z}+\varphi_{0}+\varphi_{1}z=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
1&0&0\end{pmatrix}\bar{z}+\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{1}{2}&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}z.

Clearly,
\Phi(e^{it})\Phi(e^{it})^{*}\Phi(e^{it})=\Phi(e^{it})
and hence,

\Phi(e^{it})
is a partial isometry a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
.
Here,

\Phi_{+}(e^{it})=\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{e^{it}}{2}&\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix},\Phi_{-}(e^{it})=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\\
0&0&0\\
e^{-it}&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Also

\varphi_{1}^{*}\varphi_{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
\frac{1}{2}&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
1&0&0\end{pmatrix}=0.

Similarly, one can prove that
\varphi_{-1}\varphi_{1}^{*}=0
. Also,

\Phi_{-}(e^{it})\Phi_{-}(e^{it})^{*}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{3}{4}&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

But

\Phi_{+}(e^{it})^{*}\Phi_{+}(e^{it})=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&\frac{1}{4}&\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}e^{-it}\\
0&\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}e^{it}&\frac{3}{4}\end{pmatrix}

is not operator-valued constant function. It is easy to see that

T_{\Phi}=\begin{pmatrix}O&\frac{1}{2}T_{e^{it}}&\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}I\\
O&O&O\\
T_{e^{-it}}&O&O\end{pmatrix}

is not a partial isometry.

Example 5.2
.

Define
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{3})}
as

\Phi(z)=\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{\bar{z}}{2}&0\\
0&\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}&0\\
z&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

It is easy to check that
\Phi(e^{it})
is a partial isometry a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
.
Here,

\Phi_{+}(e^{it})=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}&0\\
e^{it}&0&0\end{pmatrix},\Phi_{-}(e^{it})=\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{e^{-it}}{2}&0\\
0&\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}

We can check that
\varphi_{1}^{*}\varphi_{-1}=0
and
\varphi_{-1}\varphi_{1}^{*}=0.

Also note that

\Phi_{+}(e^{it})^{*}\Phi_{+}(e^{it})=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&\frac{3}{4}&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}

which is constant. But

\Phi_{-}(e^{it})\Phi_{-}(e^{-it})^{*}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{4}&\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}e^{-it}&0\\
\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}e^{it}&\frac{3}{4}&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}

is not a constant. Clearly,
T_{\Phi}
is not a partial isometry. Hence the condition
\Phi_{-}(e^{it})\Phi_{-}(e^{it})^{*}
is an operator-valued constant function cannot be dropped.

Example 5.3
.

Let
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{2})}
be defined by

\Phi(z)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{z}{\sqrt{2}}&\frac{\bar{z}}{\sqrt{2}}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

It is trivial to check that
\Phi(e^{it})
is a partial isometry a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
and
\Phi_{+}(e^{it})^{*}\Phi_{+}(e^{it})
and
\Phi_{-}(e^{it})\Phi_{-}(e^{it})^{*}
are operator-valued constant functions. Also

\varphi_{-1}\varphi_{1}^{*}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}=0.

But

\varphi_{1}^{*}\varphi_{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{1}{2}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\neq 0.

It is a routine check to see that
T_{\Phi}
is not a partial isometry. Thus the condition
\varphi_{n}^{*}\varphi_{-m}=0
for all
m,n\geq 1
cannot be removed.

Example 5.4
.

Let
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{2})}
be a partial isometric symbol defined by

\Phi(z)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\bar{z}}{\sqrt{2}}&0\\
\frac{z}{\sqrt{2}}&0\end{pmatrix}.

It is trivial to check that
\Phi_{+}(e^{it})^{*}\Phi_{+}(e^{it})
and
\Phi_{-}(e^{it})\Phi_{-}(e^{it})^{*}
are constant. Also

\varphi_{1}^{*}\varphi_{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}=0.

But

\varphi_{-1}\varphi_{1}^{*}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{1}{2}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\neq 0.

One can check easily that
T_{\Phi}
is not a partial isometry.
Hence
\varphi_{-m}\varphi_{n}^{*}=0
for all
m,n\geq 1
cannot be dismissed.

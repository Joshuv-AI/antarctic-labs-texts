Numerical Demonstration of Kolmogorov Scaling in Magnetohydrodynamic Turbulence
Manthan Verma, Abhishek K. Jha, Shashwat Nirgudkar, Mahendra K. Verma

Abstract
The two leading models of isotropic magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) turbulence have competing predictions: k − 5 / 3 k^{-5/3} (Kolmogorov) and k − 3 / 2 k^{-3/2} (Iroshnikov-Kraichnan) scalings. This paper identifies the valid MHD turbulence model using high-resolution numerical simulations and diagnostics—structure functions, intermittency exponents, and energy spectra and fluxes of imbalanced MHD. The energy spectra of our forced MHD simulations on 8192 2 8192^{2} , 4096 2 4096^{2} , 1024 3 1024^{3} , and 512 3 512^{3} grids support Kolmogorov’s k − 5 / 3 k^{-5/3} spectrum over Iroshnikov-Kraichnan’s k − 3 / 2 k^{-3/2} spectrum, but the difference in the spectral exponents is small. However, the numerically computed third-order structure functions and intermittency exponents support Kolmogorov scaling in both two and three dimensions. Also, the energy fluxes of the imbalanced MHD follow the predictions of Kolmogorov scaling. These results would help in a better modelling of solar wind, solar corona, and dynamos.

For steady, homogeneous, and isotropic hydrodynamic turbulence,

In the past, researchers have attempted to validate the above models using numerical simulations and solar wind observations, some of which are summarized below. Before 2001, most numerical simulations investigated the properties of isotropic MHD turbulence.
In particular, Biskamp, Welter, Müller, and Schwarz

;

Solar wind provides an observational platform for testing MHD turbulence models. The frequency spectrum measured along a spacecraft trajectory is translated to

using Taylor’s frozen-in hypothesis

reported Kolmogorov scaling for the magnetic field and IK scaling for the velocity field. In addition,

;

As discussed above, mean magnetic field plays a key role in MHD turbulence. The other important factors in MHD turbulence are the cross helicity, magnetic helicity, and magnetic Prandtl number (ratio of kinematic viscosity and magnetic diffusivity). In this paper, we limit our scope on the validation of IK and Kolmogorov scalings for
isotropic
MHD turbulence. Also, we do not delve into other important aspects such as dynamic alignment, decay rates, etc. We refer the reader to
Biskamp 2003

Sagaut and Cambon 2018

In this paper, we investigate isotropic MHD turbulence using high-resolution simulations and test whether IK scaling or Kolmogorov scaling is valid. The isotropic case is applicable when the mean field is either absent or small. In addition, isotropic turbulence forms a baseline for more complex models. Note, however, that the spectral indices (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{D_{1}}[4\tilde{E}^{-}\tilde{E}_{R}k^{2}\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{inj},A}+\tilde{\epsilon}^{+}_{\mathrm{inj}}\tilde{E}^{-}(\tilde{E}^{+}+\tilde{E}^{-})-\tilde{E}^{-}\tilde{E}_{R}(\tilde{\epsilon}^{+}_{\mathrm{inj}}+\tilde{\epsilon}^{-}_{\mathrm{inj}})+\tilde{E}_{R}^{2}(\tilde{\epsilon}^{-}_{\mathrm{inj}}-\tilde{\epsilon}^{+}_{\mathrm{inj}})],

(20)

\displaystyle\tilde{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{D_{1}}[4\tilde{E}^{-}\tilde{E}^{+}k^{2}\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{inj},A}-\tilde{E}^{+}\tilde{E}^{-}(\tilde{\epsilon}^{+}_{\mathrm{inj}}+\tilde{\epsilon}^{-}_{\mathrm{inj}})+\tilde{E}_{R}(\tilde{E}^{-}\tilde{\epsilon}^{+}_{\mathrm{inj}}+\tilde{E}^{+}\tilde{\epsilon}^{-}_{\mathrm{inj}})],

(21)

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{D_{1}}[4\tilde{E}^{+}\tilde{E}_{R}k^{2}\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{inj},A}+\tilde{E}^{+}\tilde{\epsilon}^{-}_{\mathrm{inj}}(\tilde{E}^{+}+\tilde{E}^{-})-\tilde{E}^{+}\tilde{E}_{R}(\tilde{\epsilon}^{+}_{\mathrm{inj}}+\tilde{\epsilon}^{-}_{\mathrm{inj}})+\tilde{E}_{R}^{2}(\tilde{\epsilon}^{+}_{\mathrm{inj}}-\tilde{\epsilon}^{-}_{\mathrm{inj}})],

(22)

where

D_{1}=2(\tilde{E}^{+}+\tilde{E}^{-})(\tilde{E}^{+}\tilde{E}^{-}-\tilde{E}_{R}^{2}).\\

(23)

Similarly, for 3D, we derive an equation for
H_{M}
injection rate (
\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{inj},H_{M}}
), which is given below:

\displaystyle k^{2}\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{inj},H_{M}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k^{2}(\tilde{\alpha}+\tilde{\gamma}-2\tilde{\beta})\tilde{H}_{M}+(\tilde{\alpha}-\tilde{\gamma})\tilde{H}_{1},

(24)

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Im[\mathbf{k}.(\tilde{\mathbf{b}}^{*}\times\tilde{\mathbf{u}})].

(25)

Using same method as described above, we obtain
\tilde{\alpha},\tilde{\beta}
, and
\tilde{\gamma}
for 3D as

\displaystyle\tilde{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{D_{2}}[2k^{2}\tilde{E}^{-}\tilde{E}_{R}\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{inj},H_{M}}+k^{2}\tilde{H}_{M}(2\tilde{E}^{-}\tilde{\epsilon}^{+}_{\mathrm{inj}}+\tilde{E}_{R}(\tilde{\epsilon}^{+}_{\mathrm{inj}}-\tilde{\epsilon}^{-}_{\mathrm{inj}}))+\tilde{E}_{R}\tilde{H}_{1}(\tilde{\epsilon}^{-}_{\mathrm{inj}}-\tilde{\epsilon}^{+}_{\mathrm{inj}})],

(26)

\displaystyle\tilde{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{D_{2}}[2k^{2}\tilde{E}^{+}\tilde{E}^{-}\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{inj},H_{M}}-k^{2}\tilde{H}_{M}(\tilde{E}^{+}\tilde{\epsilon}^{-}_{\mathrm{inj}}+\tilde{E}^{-}\tilde{\epsilon}^{+}_{\mathrm{inj}})+\tilde{H}_{1}(\tilde{E}^{+}\tilde{\epsilon}^{-}_{\mathrm{inj}}-\tilde{E}^{-}\tilde{\epsilon}^{+}_{\mathrm{inj}})],

(27)

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{D_{2}}[2k^{2}\tilde{E}^{+}\tilde{E}_{R}\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{inj},H_{M}}+k^{2}\tilde{H}_{M}(2\tilde{E}^{+}\tilde{\epsilon}^{-}_{\mathrm{inj}}+\tilde{E}_{R}(\tilde{\epsilon}^{-}_{\mathrm{inj}}-\tilde{\epsilon}^{+}_{\mathrm{inj}}))+\tilde{E}_{R}\tilde{H}_{1}(\tilde{\epsilon}^{-}_{\mathrm{inj}}-\tilde{\epsilon}^{+}_{\mathrm{inj}})],

(28)

where

D_{2}=2(k^{2}\tilde{H}_{M}(2\tilde{E}^{+}\tilde{E}^{-}+\tilde{E}_{R}(\tilde{E}^{-}+\tilde{E}^{+}))+\tilde{E}_{R}\tilde{H}_{1}(\tilde{E}^{-}-\tilde{E}^{+})).

(29)

The above coefficients enable appropriate energy injection in our simulations, leading to the desired statistical steady flows.

As in Kolmogorov’s theory of hydrodynamic turbulence
Kolmogorov 1941b
;
Kolmogorov 1941a
, we expect the MHD turbulence properties to be universal under forcing at large scales. That is, another set of random large-scale forcing with the same injection parameters should yield similar energy spectra and fluxes. Note, however, that 2D MHD turbulence simulations are quite sensitive to initial conditions
Dar et al. 1998
. In addition, the inverse cascade of mean-square magnetic vector potential (
A^{2}
) leads to severe instabilities. Hence, we adjust our 2D parameters carefully to obtain a steady state.

II.3
Leading spectral theories of MHD turbulence

There are several spectral theories for MHD turbulence. In this subsection we focus on Iroshnikov-Kraichnan (IK)
Iroshnikov 1964
;
Kraichnan 1965
and Kolmogorov-like phenomenologies
Marsch 1991
;
Goldreich and Sridhar 1995
that have contradictory predictions. Note that these theories have subtle differences in the presence and absence of
{\bf B}_{0}
. Refer to
Biskamp 2003
;
Verma 2004
;
Verma 2019
,
Schekochihin 2022
, and
Zhou 2021
for details.

Iroshnikov-Kraichnan’s
k^{-3/2}
theory is inspired by short-time interaction between the Alfvén waves when the mean magnetic field
{\bf B}_{0}
is strong.
Kraichnan 1965
and
Iroshnikov 1964
argued that these oppositely-moving Alfvén wave interact with the Alfvén time scale,
(kB_{0})^{-1}
. Using these arguments and dimensional analysis,
Kraichnan 1965
and
Iroshnikov 1964
derived Eq. (
1
) for the kinetic and magnetic spectra. For the isotropic case,
B_{0}
is the magnitude of the large-scale magnetic field, whereas for the anisotropic case,
B_{0}=|{\bf B}_{0}|
and
k\rightarrow k_{\perp}
in Eq. (
1
). The above power law is referred to as
IK scaling
. Further, for isotropic regime of the IK framework,
Dobrowolny et al. 1980
showed that the inertial-range energy fluxes follow

\Pi^{+}(k)=\Pi^{-}(k)

(30)

irrespective of
E^{+}(k)/E^{-}(k)
ratio. For the anisotropic framework with
z^{\pm}_{k}\ll B_{0}
,
Sridhar and Goldreich 1994
showed that the three-wave resonant interactions are absent in MHD, leading to absence of IK scaling. However,
Galtier et al. 2000
showed that three-wave interactions exist in MHD turbulence, but these interactions yield
E^{\pm}(k_{\perp})\sim k_{\perp}^{-2}
. In the IK model, the linear terms (
{\bf B}_{0}\cdot\nabla){\bf z}^{\pm}
dominate the nonlinear terms
({\bf z^{\mp}\cdot\nabla)z^{\pm}}

Kraichnan 1965
;
Biskamp 2003
;
Verma 2004
;
Schekochihin 2022
. Hence, the IK model comes under a broad framework of
weak turbulence
.

When the nonlinear term dominates the linear term, turbulence is said to be
strong
. In an early work,
Marsch 1991
proposed Eq. (
2
), which is
Kolmogorov scaling
for the
isotropic MHD turbulence
. Note that the dissipation rates
\epsilon^{\pm}
equal the inertial-range fluxes
\Pi^{\pm}(k)
in the steady state. In this framework, Eq. (
2
) leads to the following relations in the inertial range:

\frac{\Pi^{+}(k)}{\Pi^{-}(k)}=\frac{\epsilon^{+}}{\epsilon^{-}}=\left[\frac{E^{+}(k)}{E^{-}(k)}\frac{K^{-}}{K^{+}}\right]^{1/2}

(31)

or

\frac{E^{+}(k)}{E^{-}(k)}=\frac{K^{+}}{K^{-}}\left[\frac{\Pi^{+}(k)}{\Pi^{-}(k)}\right]^{2}

(32)

with
K^{\pm}=O(1)
. Hence,
\Pi^{+}(k)>\Pi^{-}(k)
when
E^{+}(k)>E^{-}(k)
and vice versa, which is contrary to IK and
Dobrowolny et al. 1980
predictions that
\Pi^{+}(k)=\Pi^{-}(k)
irrespective of
E^{+}(k)/E^{-}(k)
ratio. The flows with
E^{+}(k)\neq E^{-}(k)
are referred to as
Alfvénic
or
imbalanced MHD

Goldstein and Roberts 1995
;
Lithwick et al. 2007
;
Beresnyak and Lazarian 2008
. The cross helicity,
\int d{\bf r}({\bf u\cdot b})/2
, and normalized cross helicity,
\sigma_{c}=(E^{+}-E^{-})/(E^{+}+E^{-})=\int d{\bf r}2({\bf u\cdot b})/\int d{\bf r}(u^{2}+b^{2})
are employed as a measure of Alfvénicity
Goldstein and Roberts 1995
.

Zhou and Matthaeus 1990
and
Zhou et al. 2004
argued that the effective time scale for MHD turbulence is the harmonic mean of the nonlinear time scale
(kz_{k}^{\mp})^{-1}
and the Alfvén time scale
(kB_{0})^{-1}
, i.e.,

\frac{1}{\tau^{\pm}_{\mathrm{eff}}}=kz_{k}^{\mp}+kB_{0}.

(33)

Using dimensional analysis, they derived a combined model that yields dual energy spectra:
k^{-5/3}
at small wavenumbers and
k^{-3/2}
at large wavenumber. In another development,
Verma 1999
argued that the effective mean magnetic field has the following
k
-dependence,
B_{0}(k)\sim(\epsilon^{T})^{1/3}k^{-1/3}
, substitution of which in Eq. (
1
) yields Kolmogorov scaling.

For the strong turbulence regime of anisotropic MHD (with
{\bf B}_{0}
),
Goldreich and Sridhar 1995
argued that
k_{\parallel}B_{0}\approx k_{\perp}z^{\pm}
, called
critical balance
, and that

E(k_{\perp})\sim k_{\perp}^{-5/3},

(34)

which is a variant of Eq. (
2
) for the anisotropic case.
Boldyrev 2006
revised Goldreich-Sridhar theory by incorporating dynamic alignment and argued that the velocity and magnetic fields are preferentially aligned, with their angular divergence
\theta_{k}\sim k^{-1/4}
that leads to IK scaling (
k_{\perp}^{-3/2}
spectrum). Thus,
Boldyrev 2006
’s theory takes one back to the IK spectrum in the strong turbulence regime. Also see Podesta and Bhattacharjee
Podesta and Bhattacharjee 2010
. A cautionary remark, our paper deals with isotropic MHD turbulence (with
|{\bf B}_{0}|=0
), which may have different properties than those of Goldreich-Sridhar and Boldyrev models.

In the past, researchers
Biskamp and Welter 1989
;
Verma et al. 1996
;
Biskamp and Müller 1999
;
Biskamp and Müller 2000
;
Mason et al. 2006
;
Beresnyak 2011
;
Sahoo et al. 2011
performed numerical simulations and computed
E(k)
. Unfortunately, some works support
k^{-5/3}
spectrum, and others support
k^{-3/2}
spectrum. Hence, these works are inconclusive because the spectral indices
-5/3
and
-3/2
are quite close. Our strategy is different. Even though our high-resolution 2D simulations support
k^{-5/3}
reasonably well, our tests on
\Pi^{\pm}(k)
are conclusive. In Sec.
IV.2
, we show that the numerical
\Pi^{\pm}(k)
’s are closer to Eq. (
32
) with
K^{+}\neq K^{-}
than Eq. (
30
), thus favoring Kolmogorov scaling for MHD turbulence.

We employ two more tests based on structure functions. For steady, homogeneous, and isotropic hydrodynamic turbulence in 3D, under the limit of kinematic viscosity
\nu\rightarrow 0
, Kolmogorov
Kolmogorov 1941b
;
Kolmogorov 1941a
;
Oboukhov 1949
showed that the third-order structure function

\displaystyle S_{3}^{u}(l)=\langle\lvert\Delta\mathbf{u}\rvert^{2}(\Delta\mathbf{u\cdot\hat{l})}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{5}\epsilon_{u}l,

(35)

where
\Delta\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u(x+l)-u(x)}
, and
\epsilon_{u}
is the dissipation rate of the kinetic energy
Frisch 1995
.
Politano and Pouquet 1998
generalized Kolmogorov’s structure-function derivation to isotropic MHD turbulence (
{\bf B}_{0}=0
) under the limit
\nu\rightarrow 0
and
\eta\rightarrow 0
and obtained the following third-order structure functions for the Elsässer variables:

\displaystyle S^{\pm}_{3}(l)=\langle\lvert\Delta\mathbf{z^{\pm}}\rvert^{2}(\Delta\mathbf{z^{\mp}\cdot\hat{l})}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{d}\epsilon^{\pm}l,

(36)

where
\Delta\mathbf{z^{\pm}}=\mathbf{z^{\pm}(x+l)-z^{\pm}(x)}
, and
d
is the space dimension. Equation (
36
) is an exact relation for MHD turbulence. For a simple fractal model
Frisch 1995
, using Eq. (
36
) we can estimate that
\langle(\Delta{\bf z})^{2}\rangle\sim l^{2/3}
or
\langle{\bf z(x+l)\cdot z(x)}\rangle\sim C_{1}-C_{2}l^{2/3}
, where
C_{1},C_{2}
are constants. The above relation leads to
E^{\pm}(k)\sim k^{-5/3}
. Our numerical results, discussed in Sec.
IV
validate Eq. (
36
), thus favoring Kolmogorov scaling for MHD turbulence.
Politano and Pouquet 1998
have also derived structure functions for the total energy and cross helicity, which is discussed in the companion Letter
Verma et al. 2025
.

Politano and Pouquet 1995
and
Biskamp and Müller 2000
extended the intermittency model of
She and Leveque 1994
to MHD turbulence and argued that

S^{\pm}_{p}(l)=\langle\lvert\Delta\mathbf{z^{\pm}}\rvert^{p}\rangle\sim l^{\zeta_{p}^{\pm}},

(37)

where
\zeta^{\pm}_{p}
are the
intermittency exponents
. An extension of
k^{-5/3}
energy spectrum to intermittency analysis yields
Politano and Pouquet 1995
;
Biskamp and Müller 2000

\zeta_{p}^{\pm}=\zeta_{p}^{\mathrm{Kolm}}=\frac{p}{9}+1-\frac{1}{3^{p/3}}.

(38)

Note that
\zeta_{p}^{+}=\zeta_{p}^{-}
in this framework.
In contrast, for
k^{-3/2}
energy spectrum, dimensional analysis yields
Grauer et al. 1994
;
Politano and Pouquet 1995
;
Biskamp and Müller 2000

\zeta_{p}^{\pm}=\zeta_{p}^{\mathrm{IK}}=\frac{p}{8}+1-\frac{1}{2^{p/4}}.

(39)

Note that Eq. (
36
) is derived from the first principle, but Eqs. (
38
,
39
) are phenomenological. For a simple fractal model
Frisch 1995
,
\zeta_{4}^{\mathrm{IK}}=1
implies that
\langle(\Delta{\bf z})^{2}\rangle\sim l^{1/2}
or
\langle{\bf z(x+l)\cdot z(x)}\rangle\sim D_{1}-D_{2}l^{1/2}
, where
D_{1},D_{2}
are constants. The above relation leads to
E^{\pm}(k)\sim k^{-3/2}
. Note, however, that the spectral exponents get corrections due to intermittency (
\zeta^{\pm}_{2}\neq 2/3
or 1/2). In Sec.
IV
we report
S_{3}^{\pm}(l)
and intermittency exponents computed using numerical data. Our results are closer to Eq. (
38
) than Eq. (
39
), thus supporting Kolmogorov scaling for MHD. However, we remark that a moderate scaling range for the structure functions requires significantly large grid resolution
Sagaut and Cambon 2018
. This issue also impacts extended self similarity (ESS) employed in the paper.

With this, we conclude our brief on the models for IK and Kolmogorov scalings. In Sec.
IV
, we contrast the two competing spectral theories using structure functions, intermittency exponents, energy spectra, and energy fluxes as diagnostics.

III
Numerical Method and Parameters

We numerically solve Eqs. (
7
,
8
) on a
(2\pi)^{d}
box (
d
= space dimension) using
Aithon
, a pseudospectral CUDA code. Accurate determination of spectral exponents and energy fluxes requires high-resolution simulations, which are computationally expensive in three dimensions. Interestingly,
absolute equilibrium theory
for MHD turbulence
Frisch et al. 1975
;
Kraichnan and Montgomery 1980
;
Biskamp 2003
predicts similar spectra and fluxes for the total energy in both 2D and 3D. Therefore, we employ reduced-cost 2D MHD turbulence simulations, along with 3D simulations, to investigate the scaling of MHD turbulence
Biskamp and Welter 1989
;
Verma et al. 1996
. We employ
4096^{2}
and
8192^{2}
grids for 2D simulations, and
512^{3}
and
1024^{3}
grids for 3D simulations. To maintain a steady state for all our runs, we force both
{\bf z}^{+}
and
{\bf z}^{-}
in the wavenumber band
(2,3)
using the scheme outlined in Sec.
II
. The forcing of the magnetic field overcomes the antidynamo theorem
Cowling 1976
.

As shown in Table
1
,
\epsilon^{-}_{\mathrm{inj}}=0.1
for all the runs, but
\epsilon^{+}_{\mathrm{inj}}
is chosen as 0.1, 0.3, and 0.5. For
\epsilon^{+}_{\mathrm{inj}}/\epsilon^{-}_{\mathrm{inj}}=3
and 5, this strategy yields
\Pi^{+}(k)\neq\Pi^{-}(k)
and
E^{+}(k)/E^{-}(k)\neq 1
that enables us to contrast the IK and Kolmogorov scalings. The choice of forcing band at
(2,3)
(large scales) helps us obtain constant energy fluxes in the inertial range. The injection rate of magnetic helicity (
\epsilon_{\mathrm{inj}}^{H_{M}}
) in 3D is zero, but that of mean-square vector potential (
\epsilon_{\mathrm{inj}}^{A}
) in 2D is small, which is required for gaining a steady state in 2D. We also compute the structure functions for all the runs and test the scaling.

The model tests via the energy fluxes and structure functions do not require extreme resolutions. As we show in this paper, we can reach definitive conclusions using the aforementioned simulations with moderate resolutions in 3D and high resolutions in 2D. We employ fourth-order Runge-Kutta (
RK4
) time integration scheme, with a time stepping determined by the Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy (CFL) condition using a Courant number of
0.1
. One of the smallest timesteps
\Delta t\approx 2\times 10^{-6}
is for
8192^{2}
grid with
\epsilon^{+}_{\mathrm{inj}}/\epsilon^{-}_{\mathrm{inj}}=5
. All our simulations are well resolved with
k_{\mathrm{max}}{l_{d}}
ranging from 3.2 to 4.0, where
l_{\mathrm{d}}\approx(\nu^{3}/\epsilon_{\mathrm{inj}})^{1/4}
is the Kolmogorov length, and
k_{\mathrm{max}}=\pi/N
with
N
as the grid resolution.

We choose
\nu=\eta
for all our runs with minimum
\nu=4.55\times 10^{-5}
for 2D and minimum
\nu=5.6\times 10^{-4}
for 3D. The corresponding Reynolds numbers Re
(
UL/\nu
) are
96660
and
7055
respectively, where
U
is the large-scale velocity, and
L
is the box size. The magnetic Reynolds number (
UL/\eta
) matches Re because
\nu=\eta
. See Table
1
for details. We observe that these Reynolds numbers are reasonably large to distinguish Kolmogorov and IK scaling. However, Re for the 3D runs are insufficient to study locality in MHD turbulence
Zhou 2007
;
Zhou and Oughton 2011
. In addition, we employ hypo-viscosity and hypo-diffusivity in all the 2D runs to minimize the inverse cascade of magnetic vector potential that increases monotonically
Pouquet 1978
;
Kraichnan and Montgomery 1980
. Our hypo-dissipation terms are of the form
\nu_{\mathrm{hypo}}k^{-2}\mathbf{z^{\pm}}(k,t)
with
\nu_{\mathrm{hypo}}\approx 0.01
. We also compute the hydrodynamic entropy
S_{H}=-\sum_{k}p_{k}\log_{2}p_{k}
, where
p_{k}=E(k)/E
with
E=(E^{+}+E^{-})/2
as the total energy and
E(k)
as the shell spectrum
Verma and Chatterjee 2022
;
Verma et al. 2024
. The entropies for all the runs are listed in Table
1
. We observe that
S_{H}
decreases with the increase of
\epsilon^{+}_{\mathrm{inj}}
, which indicates an increase in hydrodynamic order with the increase of
\sigma_{c}
.

We performed our simulations mostly on
Frontier
of the Oak Ridge Leadership Computing Facility that has total
9408
GPU compute nodes, each containing
4
AMD MI250X GPU cards. Some of the simulations were performed on a GPU node of Kotak School of Sustainability, IIT Kanpur. All our simulations were performed using our CUDA-based pseudo-spectral solver
Aithon
that scales well up to
4096
nodes of Frontier. Our runs took significant computing time. For example, each of the
1024^{3}
runs took approximately 18 hours on
64
nodes of Frontier. In addition, we employ a GPU-enabled structure function solver that speeds up our computations by many folds compared to CPU-based solvers; this solver adopts the parallelization strategy used by
Sadhukhan et al. 2021
.

V
Comparison with past results on numerical simulations and solar wind observations

In early attempts, Biskamp, Welter, Müller, and Schwarz
Biskamp and Welter 1989
;
Müller and Biskamp 2000
;
Biskamp and Schwarz 2001
;
Biskamp and Müller 2000
simulated 2D and 3D MHD turbulence on
4096^{2}
and
512^{3}
grids and showed that for 3D MHD turbulence,
E_{u}(k),E_{b}(k)\sim k^{-5/3}
, and the multiscaling exponents
\zeta_{p}
’s follow Eq. (
38
).
Sorriso-Valvo et al. 2002
and
Mininni and Pouquet 2009
too reported
\zeta_{3}\approx 1
. These results are consistent with those presented in this paper. For 2D MHD turbulence, Biskamp, Welter, Müller, and Schwarz
Biskamp and Welter 1989
;
Biskamp and Müller 2000
argued that
E_{u}(k)
and
E_{b}(k)
follow IK scaling, but
\zeta_{3}\approx 1
. In addition,
\zeta_{p}
(other than
p=3
) are inconclusive on 2D MHD turbulence. Our numerical results on 2D MHD turbulence support Kolmogorov spectrum, thus counter the past results of Biskamp and coworkers.
Verma et al. 1996
simulated MHD turbulence on coarse grids (
512^{2}
and
128^{3}
) and observed agreement with Eq. (
32
), thus validating Kolmogorov scaling for MHD turbulence (also, see
Verma et al. 2002
). The numerical results of
Sahoo et al. 2011
lead to mixed conclusions.

Since 2006, most numerical works have been for anisotropic turbulence.
Cho and Vishniac 2000
and
Beresnyak 2011
;
Beresnyak 2019
verified Goldreich-Sridhar model
Goldreich and Sridhar 1995
, whereas
Mason et al. 2006
;
Mason et al. 2008
and
Podesta and Borovsky 2010
found agreement with
Boldyrev 2006
’s predictions. Also see
Sundar et al. 2017
for the energy flux studies in anisotropic MHD. Often, these researchers simulated reduced magnetohydrodynamics (RMHD) with
{\bf B}_{0}
. In this section, we compare our isotropic results with those for anisotropic ones, even though a comparison is not fully justified.

Researchers
Matthaeus and Goldstein 1982
;
Tu and Marsch 1995
;
Podesta and Borovsky 2010
;
Parashar et al. 2018
have computed the normalized cross helicity spectrum,
\sigma_{c}(k)=[E^{+}(k)-E^{-}(k)]/[E^{+}(k)+E^{-}(k)]
, for the solar wind data and showed it to be nearly constant in the inertial range, which is consistent with Eq. (
2
). Note that
\sigma_{c}(k)=\cos\theta_{k}
, both of which are averaged over the wavenumber shell of radius
k
. In Figure
10
(a,b,c) we plot
\sigma_{c}(k)
for the
8192^{2}
grid simulations with
\epsilon_{\mathrm{inj}}^{+}/\epsilon_{\mathrm{inj}}^{-}=1,3
, and 5, respectively. In the three plots,
\sigma_{c}(k)\approx 0,0.6,0.65
in the inertial range, whereas
\sigma_{c}(k)
is larger than the mean for smaller
k
’s but less than the mean for larger
k
’s; these results are consistent with the reported
\sigma_{c}(k)
for the solar wind
Matthaeus and Goldstein 1982
;
Tu and Marsch 1995
;
Podesta and Borovsky 2010
;
Parashar et al. 2018
. We complement the above plots with the probability density functions (PDF) of
\cos\theta_{k}
for
k=11,21,46
, and 51. The plots show that the PDFs peak at the respective mean values, with significant fluctuations around the mean. Consistency and nonzero values of
\sigma_{c}(k)
in the inertial range is consistent with Kolmogorov scaling [Eq. (
2
)]. Note that
\sigma_{c}(k)\approx 0
in IK scaling because of
\Pi^{+}(k)\approx\Pi^{-}(k)
.
We remark that the above
\sigma_{c}(k)
or
\theta_{k}
should not be compared with the predictions of
Boldyrev 2006
, which are for anisotropic MHD turbulence. For completeness, in Fig.
11
, we also plot the Alfvén ratio
r_{A}(k)=E_{u}(k)/E_{b}(k)
for
8192^{2}
and
1024^{3}
grids. For
\epsilon^{+}_{\mathrm{inj}}/\epsilon^{-}_{\mathrm{inj}}=1,3,
and
5
, the average values of
r_{A}(k)
for
8192^{2}
grid are 0.7, 0.84, and 0.86, respectively, whereas those for the
1024^{3}
grid are 0.45, 0.55, and 0.62, respectively.

Marsch 1991
and
Lithwick et al. 2007
predicted that
E^{+}(k)/E^{-}(k)\approx[\Pi^{+}/\Pi^{-}]^{2}
, whereas
Beresnyak and Lazarian 2008
and
Perez and Boldyrev 2009
predicted that
E^{+}(k)/E^{-}(k)\approx\Pi^{+}/\Pi^{-}
. However, we observe scaling in between:
E^{+}(k)/E^{-}(k)\approx[\Pi^{+}/\Pi^{-}]^{1.5}
for 2D and
E^{+}(k)/E^{-}(k)\approx[\Pi^{+}/\Pi^{-}]^{1.67}
for 3D; the divergence from
E^{+}(k)/E^{-}(k)\approx[\Pi^{+}/\Pi^{-}]^{2}
is attributed to
K^{+}\neq K^{-}
. Also, the Kolmogorov’s constants reported by us are in general agreement (within a factor of 2) with the past simulations, e.g.,
Beresnyak 2011
;
Beresnyak 2019
and field-theoretic computations
Verma 2004
;
Verma 2025
.

Solar wind too provides a platform to test the validity of MHD turbulence models. As described in the introduction (Sec.
I
), some solar wind observations, especially earlier ones, support Kolmogorov’s
k^{-5/3}
spectrum
Taylor 1938
;
Matthaeus and Goldstein 1982
;
Tu and Marsch 1995
. However, some later works provide support to IK scaling. For example,
Podesta and Borovsky 2010
reported
k^{-3/2}
spectrum for large
\sigma_{c}
, but
k^{-5/3}
spectrum when
\sigma_{c}\approx 0
. In addition,
Podesta et al. 2007
reported
k^{-5/3}
spectrum for the magnetic field and
k^{-3/2}
spectrum for the velocity field. In the companion Letter
Verma et al. 2025
, we attribute this divergence to the energy transfers from
b
to
u
in the inertial range
Verma et al. 2021
. Also, solar wind observations
Tu and Marsch 1995
;
Podesta and Borovsky 2010
;
Parashar et al. 2018
tend to support
\cos\theta_{k}=\sigma_{c}(k)\approx
const., contradictory to
Boldyrev 2006
’s predictions. Regarding the structure functions,
Sorriso-Valvo et al. 2007
reported that
S_{3}(l)\propto l
, which is consistent with Eq. (
36
). However,
Bruno and Carbone 2013
’s intermittency exponents are inconsistent with both Kolmogorov and IK scalings [Eqs. (
39
,
38
)].

For strong turbulence, several authors, including
Kraichnan 1965
, argued that the large-scale magnetic field would play the role of mean magnetic field, which would lead to IK scaling [Eq. (
1
)]. This is contrary to the results presented in this paper and in some previous works.
Verma 1999
attempted to reconcile this ambiguity using renormalization group analysis of mean magnetic field. He showed that the
renormalized mean magnetic field

B_{0}(k)
scales as
(\epsilon^{T})^{1/3}k^{-1/3}
, substitution of which in Eq. (
1
) leads to Kolmogorov spectrum, Eq. (
2
), for strong MHD turbulence
Verma 2019
. These arguments need to be tested numerically. Also note that renormalization group analysis and energy flux calculations mostly support Kolmogorov scaling
Verma 1999
;
Verma 2001
;
Verma 2004
;
Adzhemyan et al. 1999
;
Zhou 2010
;
Verma 2025
, but there are variants.

We conclude in the next section.

VI
Conclusions

Accurate modelling of MHD turbulence has remained uncertain even after 60 years of research. This paper addresses this problem and numerically shows that Kolmogorov scaling is valid for
isotropic
MHD turbulence. Since 2D and 3D MHD turbulence have similar turbulence dynamics, we perform high resolution simulations (
8192^{2}
and
4096^{2}
) in 2D and moderately-resolved simulations (
1024^{3}
and
512^{3}
) in 3D. Since the inertial-range energy fluxes of the Elsässer variables,
\Pi^{\pm}(k)
, are constant, we focus on these variables, rather than velocity and magnetic fields, which have variable energy fluxes in the inertial range
Verma et al. 2025
. We limit ourselves to isotropic turbulence with
{\bf B}_{0}=0
. Hence, our results may differ in detail from those related to the anisotropic models proposed by
Goldreich and Sridhar 1995
and
Boldyrev 2006
.

The main results of our numerical simulations are as follows:

1.

Our numerically-computed
E^{\pm}(k)
are closer to
k^{-5/3}
than
k^{-3/2}
. However, the spectral indices do not provide conclusive evidence because they are too close.

2.

The IK and Kolmogorov frameworks have different predictions for the inertial-range energy fluxes
\Pi^{\pm}(k)
. Using these diagnostics, we show that numerical energy fluxes support Kolmogorov framework over IK framework.

3.

Isotropic MHD turbulence has an exact relationship:
S^{\pm}_{3}(l)=-(4/3)\epsilon^{\pm}l
, which is consistent with the Kolmogorov scaling. In contrast, IK scaling predicts that
S^{\pm}_{4}(l)\propto l
.
Our numerical simulation clearly support Kolmogorov scaling.

4.

Numerically-computed intermittency exponents support Kolmogorov scaling.

In the companion Letter
Verma et al. 2025
, we show that the total energy and cross helicity too exhibit spectra, fluxes, and third-order structure functions, consistent with Kolmogorov scaling. In contrast,
E_{u}(k)\sim k^{-3/2}
and
E_{b}(k)\sim k^{-5/3}
due to energy transfer from
b
to
u
.

Thus, our novel approach resolves a long-standing problem of MHD turbulence. The present paper provides many valuable results and insights into isotropic MHD turbulence. We believe similar investigations of
anisotropic MHD turbulence
using high-resolution simulations would be very useful. A clear understanding of isotropic and anisotropic MHD turbulence would be a major step in turbulence research, and it will open a window for a better modeling of solar-, geo-, and galactic dynamos, solar wind, solar corona, etc.

QCD factorization with multihadron fragmentation functions
T. C. Rogers, M. Radici, A. Courtoy, T. Rainaldi

Abstract
Important aspects of QCD factorization theorems are the properties of the objects involved that can be identified as universal. One example is that the definitions of parton densities and fragmentation functions for different types of hadrons differ only in the identity of the nonperturbative states that form the matrix elements, but are otherwise the same. This leads to independence of perturbative calculations on nonperturbative details of external states. It also lends support to interpretations of correlation functions as encapsulations of intrinsic nonperturbative properties. These characteristics have usually been presumed to still hold true in fragmentation functions even when the observed nonperturbative state is a small-mass cluster of n n hadrons rather than simply a single isolated hadron. However, the multidifferential aspect of cross sections that rely on these latter types of fragmentation functions complicates the treatment of kinematical approximations in factorization derivations. That has led to recent claims that the operator definitions for fragmentation functions need to be modified from the single hadron case with nonuniversal prefactors. With such concerns as our motivation, we retrace the steps for factorizing the unpolarized semi-inclusive e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} annihilation cross section and confirm that they do apply without modification to the case of a small-mass multihadron observed in the final state. In particular, we verify that the standard operator definition from single hadron fragmentation, with its usual prefactor, remains equally valid for the small-mass n n -hadron case with the same hard parts and evolution kernels, whereas the more recently proposed definitions with nonuniversal prefactors do not. Our results reaffirm the reliability of most past phenomenological applications of dihadron fragmentation functions.

Introduction

Factorizable high-energy collisions in QCD are describable in terms of convolution products of a perturbatively calculable factor
with
one or more factors that encapsulate the description of nonperturbative hadronic properties.
The nonperturbative factors can take the form of parton distribution functions, which describe the distribution of partons in a target, or fragmentation functions, which characterize the hadronization of a parton into particular types of final states.
In the simplest cases where processes are very inclusive, the parton distribution and fragmentation functions depend on only one variable, the momentum fraction of the parent particle involved, in addition to an auxiliary scale associated with the renormalization group. For such cases, collinear factorization is the relevant theoretical framework, and the derivations lead to definitions for universal nonperturbative correlation functions like parton distribution or fragmentation functions.
A notable advantage of collinear factorization is that it allows one to exploit the simplifications arising from inclusive sums over final states.

In applications, contributions where separate partons fragment independently can be important, even at relatively low scales. However, these involve factorization with single hadron fragmentation functions. Such treatments can be combined, as is typically done, with a multihadron fragmentation function formalism in additional steps.

IV
Factorization

The following steps are basically a review of steps like those in chapter 6.1 of Ref.
[
22
]
and similar treatments, but now specialized to the case of fragmentation functions in
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation rather than for parton densities in deep inelastic scattering. Our focus is on highlighting the kinematical approximations needed to get factorization and verifying that they are unmodified if the observed hadron is an
n
-hadron. The aim is to expand in powers of
\Lambda/Q
as
Q\to\infty
where
\Lambda
was defined in Sec.
II
to be any other fixed mass scale like
m
,
M_{h}
, or an intrinsic transverse momentum. That is,

\Lambda\in\left\{m,M_{h},k_{\text{T}},\dots\right\}\,.

(57)

In QCD,
\Lambda
would also include
\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}
. Also, everywhere below we will use the notation

k_{2}=q-k

(58)

to abbreviate expressions even though the
k_{2}
momentum is not independent of
q
and
k
.

Before factorization, a graph with the structure of Fig.
1
is

W^{\mu\nu}_{\text{SI}}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{4}\mskip-0.59999muk}\nolimits}{(2\pi)^{4}}{\rm Tr}\left[J_{1}(k,\left\{p_{h}\right\})\gamma^{\mu}J_{2}(k_{2})\gamma^{\nu}\right]\,.

(59)

For approximations in the hard vertices (which are just zeroth order in our discussion), one uses
k_{2}^{2}=(q-k)^{2}=O\left(\Lambda^{2}\right)
to find

k_{H}^{+}=\frac{Q^{2}}{2q_{H}^{-}}+O\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{Q}\right)=q_{H}^{+}+O\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{Q}\right)\,,

(60)

and
z_{\rm n}=z
to leading power. Thus, we define a “hatted” approximation in the hadron frame,

\hat{k}\equiv\left(q_{H}^{+},0,\bm{0}_{\text{T}}\right)_{H}\,.

(61)

The power counting for
k
in the hadron frame is

k=\left(O\left(Q\right),O\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{Q}\right),O\left(\bm{\Lambda}_{\text{T}}\right)\right)_{H}\,.

(62)

In addition,

q-k=\left(O\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{Q}\right),q_{H}^{-}+O\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{Q}\right),O\left(\bm{\Lambda}_{\text{T}}\right)\right)_{H}\,,

(63)

so we define another “hatted” approximation for the opposite moving antiquark,

\hat{k}_{2}\equiv\left(0,q_{H}^{-},\bm{0}_{\text{T}}\right)_{H}\,.

(64)

The dominant power law behavior for the components of
k_{2}
in the hadron frame is

k_{2}=\left(O\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{Q}\right),O\left(Q\right),O\left(\bm{\Lambda}_{\text{T}}\right)\right)_{H}\,.

(65)

To extract the leading power behavior in the subgraphs

J_{1}(k,\left\{p_{h}\right\})
and
J_{2}(k_{2})
, we replace the largest components of
k
by their approximate values, while the small components must be kept exact. To recall why, consider, for example, the propagator denominator

k^{2}-m_{q}^{2}
inside
J_{1}(k,\left\{p_{h}\right\})
. The power law behavior is

\displaystyle k^{2}-m_{q}^{2}{}=2k_{H}^{+}k_{H}^{-}-k^{2}_{H\text{T}}-m_{q}^{2}{}=2\left(q_{H}^{+}+O\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{Q}\right)\right)\underbrace{k_{H}^{-}}_{O\left(\Lambda^{2}/Q\right)}-\underbrace{\left(k^{2}_{H\text{T}}+m_{q}^{2}\right)}_{O\left(\Lambda^{2}\right)}=\underbrace{2q_{H}^{+}k_{H}^{-}-k^{2}_{H\text{T}}-m_{q}^{2}}_{O\left(\Lambda^{2}\right)}+O\left(\frac{\Lambda^{4}}{Q^{2}}\right)\,.

(66)

Approximating
k_{H}^{+}
by
\hat{k}_{H}^{+}=q_{H}^{+}
introduces only power suppressed errors, whereas also neglecting
k_{H\text{T}}
or
k_{H}^{-}
would introduce uncontrolled leading power errors. Thus, we define a separate “tilde” approximation for
k
,

k\to\tilde{k}=\left(q_{H}^{+},k_{H}^{-},\bm{k}_{H\text{T}}\right)_{H}\,,

(67)

and use the leading power approximation,

J_{1}(k,\left\{p_{h}\right\})\to J_{1}(\tilde{k},\left\{p_{h}\right\})\,.

(68)

The hatted variables are appropriate for describing hard partonic scattering with massless on-shell quarks, while the tilde variables enter the separate collinear subgraphs. The partonic
z
is indicated with a hat,

\hat{z}=\frac{2\hat{k}{}\cdot q}{Q^{2}}=\frac{z_{\rm n}}{\xi}\,.

(69)

To make the analogous approximation on
k_{2}
in
J_{2}(k_{2})
, it is convenient to analyze its components in a frame that is slightly boosted from the hadron frame such that
k_{2}
has exactly zero transverse momentum, and its minus component equals its minus component in the hadron frame. We will indicate components in this frame with a ‘*’. Then,

k_{2}=\left(q_{H}^{+}-k_{H}^{+}-\frac{k^{2}_{H\text{T}}}{2\left(q_{H}^{-}-k_{H}^{-}\right)},q_{H}^{-}-k_{H}^{-},\bm{0}_{\text{T}}\right)^{*}\,.

(70)

Recalling that
q_{H}^{+}-k_{H}^{+}=O\left(\Lambda^{2}/Q\right)
, the power law behavior for
k_{2}
in ‘*’ frame components is

k_{2}=\left(O\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{Q}\right),O\left(Q\right),\bm{0}_{\text{T}}\right)^{*}\,.

(71)

Let
r
be a
k_{2}
-collinear momentum line internal to the
J_{2}(k_{2})
subgraph,

r=\left(O\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{Q}\right),O\left(Q\right),O\left(\bm{\Lambda}_{\text{T}}\right)\right)^{*}\,.

(72)

Then the reasoning for
J_{2}(k_{2})
analogous to Eq. (
66
) is to note that a propagator denominator of the form
\sim(k_{2}+r)^{2}-m_{q}^{2}
has the power-law behavior,

\displaystyle(k_{2}+r)^{2}-m_{q}^{2}=2k_{2}\cdot r+\left(k_{2}^{2}+r^{2}-m_{q}^{2}\right)=2k_{2}^{*+}r^{*-}+2k_{2}^{*-}r^{*+}+O\left(\Lambda^{2}\right)\,.

(73)

In Eq. (
73
), dropping the
k_{H}^{-}
in the plus component of Eq. (
70
) results in dropping an
O\left(\Lambda^{4}/Q^{3}\right)
plus component contribution, whereas the dominant order is
O\left(\Lambda^{2}/Q\right)
. Dropping
k_{H}^{-}
in the minus component of Eq. (
70
) results in dropping an
O\left(\Lambda^{2}/Q\right)
minus component contribution while the dominant order is an
O\left(Q\right)
contribution:

\displaystyle\left(k_{2}+r\right)^{2}-m_{q}^{2}

\displaystyle=k_{2}^{2}+2k_{2}\cdot r+r^{2}-m_{q}^{2}

\displaystyle=2k_{2}^{*+}\left(k_{2}^{*-}+r^{*-}\right)+2k_{2}^{*-}r^{*+}+r^{2}-m_{q}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\left(q_{H}^{+}-k_{H}^{+}-\frac{k^{2}_{H\text{T}}}{2\left(q_{H}^{-}-k_{H}^{-}\right)}\right)\left(k_{2}^{*-}+r^{*-}\right)+2\left(q_{H}^{-}-k_{H}^{-}\right)r^{*+}+r^{2}-m_{q}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\left(\underbrace{q_{H}^{+}-k_{H}^{+}-\frac{k^{2}_{H\text{T}}}{2q_{H}^{-}}}_{O\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{Q}\right)}+O\left(\frac{\Lambda^{4}}{Q^{3}}\right)\right)\underbrace{\left(k_{2}^{*-}+r^{*-}\right)}_{O\left(Q\right)}+2\left(\underbrace{q_{H}^{-}}_{O\left(Q\right)}+O\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{Q}\right)\right)\underbrace{r^{*+}}_{O\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{Q}\right)}+\underbrace{r^{2}-m_{q}^{2}}_{O\left(\Lambda^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=2\left(q_{H}^{+}-k_{H}^{+}-\frac{k^{2}_{H\text{T}}}{2q_{H}^{-}}\right)\left(k_{2}^{*-}+r^{*-}\right)+2q_{H}^{-}r^{*+}+r^{2}-m_{q}^{2}+O\left(\frac{\Lambda^{4}}{Q^{2}}\right)\;.

(74)

Thus, we define the
k_{2}
tilde approximation,

k_{2}\to\tilde{k}_{2}\equiv\left(q_{H}^{+}-k_{H}^{+}-\frac{k^{2}_{H\text{T}}}{2q_{H}^{-}},q_{H}^{-},\bm{0}_{\text{T}}\right)^{*}\,,

(75)

and the effect of this replacement in, for example, Eq. (
73
) is a subleading error of
O\left(\Lambda^{4}/Q^{2}\right)
with respect to the (leading) power
O\left(\Lambda^{2}\right)
of the denominator.
So, we make the leading power approximation

J_{2}(k_{2})\to J_{2}(\tilde{k}_{2})\,.

(76)

Finally, the leading power Dirac components for
J_{1}(\tilde{k},\left\{p_{h}\right\})
and
J_{2}(\tilde{k}_{2})
in the hadron frame are, respectively,

\displaystyle J_{1}(\tilde{k},\left\{p_{h}\right\})

\displaystyle{}=\frac{1}{4}{\rm Tr}\left[\gamma^{+}J_{1}(\tilde{k},\left\{p_{h}\right\})\right]\gamma^{-}+\text{p.s.}

(77)

\displaystyle J_{2}(\tilde{k}_{2})

\displaystyle{}=\frac{1}{4}{\rm Tr}\left[\gamma^{-}J_{2}(\tilde{k}_{2})\right]\gamma^{+}+\text{p.s.}\,.

(78)

A change in variables from

V.2
Interpretations

As with any typical relativistic interacting quantum field theory that requires renormalization, one cannot define an exactly literal probability density for finding a certain number of elementary particles inside an asymptotic state. At least some properties needed for a literal probability density are violated. It is more precise, therefore, to speak of quasiprobability distributions in the context of partonic correlation functions, in the sense that for most (but not all) practical situations it is possible to treat objects like fragmentation functions
as if
they are probability distributions. In particular, one may use them to construct expectation values, and the structure of factorization formulas like Eq. (
3
) matches the probabilistic parton model intuition wherein an elementary partonic cross section is convoluted with a number density for producing an
n
-hadron state in a given differential region of phase space, in a way that closely follows probabilistic intuition. Furthermore, the definition of the fragmentation function in Eq. (
V.1
) has a natural probabilistic interpretation when viewed as an extension of the kind of probability distributions that do arise in theories that are ultraviolet finite. Provided that the usual caveats associated with renormalization, etc., are kept in mind, it can be understood as a number density, with respect to a parton momentum fraction
\xi
, of objects carrying momentum
p_{h}
in a single fragmenting parton. The same interpretation continues to apply if the object is an
n
-hadron
(in a restricted region of
\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY^{\prime}}\nolimits{}
)
rather than just a single isolated hadron.

Therefore, we disagree with the assertion in Ref.
[
29
]
that the standard fragmentation function definition lacks an acceptable number density interpretation within a parton model framework when it is extended to the multihadron case, at least so long as the usual limitations of number density interpretations alluded to above, and that apply to most types of QCD correlation functions,
are acknowledged.
When we discuss parton model interpretations below, we will assume that all such caveats are understood and taken into account.

The set of steps leading to Eq. (
3
), with the standard
n
-hadron fragmentation function definition appearing in the factorization formula, are the main results of this paper. In the remaining sections, we will focus on showing the details of variable transformations needed to rewrite cross sections in forms more familiar from phenomenological applications in various choices of variables for the external hadrons.

As mentioned in the previous subsection, the internal kinematical details of the
n
-hadron state are often unknown, and so it is usually convenient to define a kind of “reduced” fragmentation function by integrating
over some subset of the variables that make up

VII
Dihadron inclusive production

Next, we review the steps for changing variables to reduce the cross section expression for dihadron production to other familiar forms. To simplify the discussion we will continue to assume that each hadron in a dihadron pair has the same mass
m
. The minimum invariant mass
M_{h}
is then
2m
.

For
n=2
, the dihadron cross section in Eq. (
3
) becomes

2E_{p_{h_{1}}}(2\pi)^{3}2E_{p_{h_{2}}}(2\pi)^{3}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\sigma}\nolimits{}}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h_{1}}}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h_{2}}}\nolimits{}}=\int_{z}^{1}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi}\nolimits{}}{\xi^{2}}\left(2E_{\hat{k}}(2\pi)^{3}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\hat{\sigma}}\nolimits{}}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{\hat{k}}}\nolimits{}}\right)d(\xi,\left\{p_{h}\right\})+\text{p.s.}\,,

(143)

with the fragmentation function defined as in Eq. (
V.1
). For factorization to hold, the kinematics need to be restricted to
M_{h}^{2}/z\ll Q^{2}
.

A standard way to characterize the dihadron momentum that is common to many treatments is with the variables

p_{h}\equiv p_{h_{1}}+p_{h_{2}}\,,\qquad R\equiv\frac{p_{h_{1}}-p_{h_{2}}}{2}\,.

(144)

We define

\displaystyle{z_{\rm n}}_{1}\equiv\frac{p_{h_{1},H}^{+}}{q^{+}_{H}}\,,\qquad{z_{\rm n}}_{2}\equiv\frac{p_{h_{2},H}^{+}}{q^{+}_{H}}\,,\qquad z_{1}\equiv\frac{2p_{h_{1}}\cdot q}{Q^{2}}\,,\qquad z_{2}\equiv\frac{2p_{h_{2}}\cdot q}{Q^{2}}\,,

(145)

where
z_{\rm n}={z_{\rm n}}_{1}+{z_{\rm n}}_{2}
. We also define partonic momentum fractions analogous to
\xi
,

\xi_{1}\equiv\frac{p_{h_{1},H}^{+}}{k_{H}^{+}}=\xi\frac{p_{h_{1},H}^{+}}{p_{h,H}^{+}}\,,\qquad\xi_{2}\equiv\frac{p_{h_{2},H}^{+}}{k_{H}^{+}}=\xi\frac{p_{h_{2},H}^{+}}{p_{h,H}^{+}}\,,

(146)

with
\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}
. It is also useful to define the purely external kinematical variables,

x_{1}\equiv\frac{p_{h_{1},H}^{+}}{p_{h,H}^{+}}=\frac{{z_{\rm n}}_{1}}{z_{\rm n}}\,,\qquad x_{2}\equiv\frac{p_{h_{2},H}^{+}}{p_{h,H}^{+}}=\frac{{z_{\rm n}}_{2}}{z_{\rm n}}\,,\qquad\text{so}\qquad\xi_{1}\xi_{2}=\xi^{2}x_{1}x_{2}\,.

(147)

In the zeroth order parton model approximation,
\xi\to z_{\rm n}\to z
after the integral over
\xi
, in which case
\xi_{1}\to z_{1}
and
\xi_{2}\to z_{2}
.
We take the standard definition of the parameter
\zeta
that quantifies the asymmetry in the hadrons’ relative momentum,

\zeta\equiv\frac{p_{h_{1},p}^{+}-p_{h_{2},p}^{+}}{p_{h,p}^{+}}=\frac{{z_{\rm n}}_{1}-{z_{\rm n}}_{2}}{z_{\rm n}}=\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{z}+\text{p.s.}\,.

(148)

One may integrate over some subset of kinematical variables describing the relative momentum of the hadrons to get a simpler form. The exact form depends on the choice of variables used to describe the phase space of
\bm{p}_{h_{1}}
and
\bm{p}_{h_{2}}
. We illustrate this with two examples below.

VII.1
Variable choice A

One standard choice is to use the variables above directly and obtain a cross section that is differential in
M_{h}
and
z
.
In the hadron frame,

\displaystyle p_{h_{1}}=\left(\frac{z_{\rm n}(1+\zeta)Q}{2\sqrt{2}},\frac{\left(m^{2}+R^{2}_{H\text{T}}\right)\sqrt{2}}{z_{\rm n}(1+\zeta)Q},\bm{R}_{H\text{T}}\right)_{H}\,,\qquad p_{h_{2}}=\left(\frac{z_{\rm n}(1-\zeta)Q}{2\sqrt{2}},\frac{\left(m^{2}+R^{2}_{H\text{T}}\right)\sqrt{2}}{z_{\rm n}(1-\zeta)Q},-\bm{R}_{H\text{T}}\right)_{H}\,.

(149)

The hadron frame transverse components of
R
are related to the total invariant mass of the dihadron pair via

R^{2}_{H\text{T}}=\frac{M_{h}^{2}(1-\zeta^{2})}{4}-m^{2}\,,

(150)

which establishes the range of kinematically allowed
\zeta
,

-\zeta_{\text{max}}\leq\zeta\leq\zeta_{\text{max}}\,,\qquad\text{with}\qquad\zeta_{\text{max}}\equiv\sqrt{1-\frac{4m^{2}}{M_{h}^{2}}}\,.

(151)

Therefore,
to satisfy leading power factorization, one must focus on the limit
4m^{2}\leq\,M_{h}^{2}\ll zQ^{2}
.
Note also that if
M_{h}^{2}
is allowed to become too large relative to
m^{2}
, then the
\zeta\approx\pm 1
region starts to contribute. Then the hadrons in the pair can have very asymmetric rapidities, one of the two hadrons inside the pair can carry a very small fraction of quark momentum, and there could be a danger of contamination by soft hadrons outside of the region where factorization is valid.
Thus, it is important to restrict consideration to an appropriate range of
M_{h}^{2}
,
e.g.
by keeping a fixed upper bound on the ratio
M_{h}^{2}/m^{2}
as
Q\to\infty
.
Another useful relation is

E_{p_{h_{1}}}E_{p_{h_{2}}}=\frac{z_{1}z_{2}Q^{2}}{4}+\text{p.s.}=\frac{z^{2}Q^{2}(1-\zeta^{2})}{16}+\text{p.s.}\,.

(152)

The lab frame components of
p_{h_{1}}
and
p_{h_{2}}
, expressed in terms of the variables above, are listed in Appendix
A
. Using Eq. (
144
) and Eq. (
149
), variable transformations give

\displaystyle 2E_{p_{h_{1}}}(2\pi)^{3}2E_{p_{h_{2}}}(2\pi)^{3}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\cdots}\nolimits{}}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h_{1}}}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h_{2}}}\nolimits{}}

\displaystyle{}=\frac{32\pi^{3}}{M_{h}}2E_{h}(2\pi)^{3}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\cdots}\nolimits{}}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h}}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muM_{h}}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\zeta}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\phi_{R,H}}\nolimits{}}+\text{p.s.}\,,

(153)

with the left-hand variables in the lab frame,
\phi_{R,H}
the azimuthal angle of
\bm{R}_{H\text{T}}
in the hadron frame, and with power suppressed errors being dropped at various points on the right-hand side.
So, Eq. (
143
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{32\pi^{3}}{M_{h}}2E_{h}(2\pi)^{3}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\sigma}\nolimits{}}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h}}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muM_{h}}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\zeta}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\phi_{R,H}}\nolimits{}}=\int_{z}^{1}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi}\nolimits{}}{\xi^{2}}\left(2E_{\hat{k}}(2\pi)^{3}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\hat{\sigma}}\nolimits{}}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{\hat{k}}}\nolimits{}}\right)d(\xi,\zeta,M_{h},\phi_{R,H})+\text{p.s.}\,,

(154)

where now we have written out the arguments of the dihadron fragmentation function explicitly by defining

d(\xi,\zeta,M_{h},\phi_{R,H})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n=2}}{{\equiv}}d(\xi,\left\{p_{h}\right\})\,.

(155)

The new function arguments
\zeta,M_{h},\phi_{R,H}
describe the relative internal momentum of the pair of hadrons. Comparing Eq. (
154
) with Eq. (
132
) shows that, with this particular choice of variables, the internal phase space of the dihadron pair is

\left(\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu}\nolimits{}}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY^{\prime}}\nolimits}\right)_{H}\cdots\;\;=\frac{32\pi^{3}}{M_{h}}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu}\nolimits{}}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muM_{h}}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\zeta}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\phi_{R,H}}\nolimits{}}\cdots\,.

(156)

The dihadron fragmentation function in Eq. (
154
) is still the one from Eq. (
V.1
). From the discussion in Sec.
V
,

\frac{1}{32\pi^{3}}\int_{V}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\zeta}\nolimits{}M_{h}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muM_{h}}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\phi_{R,H}}\nolimits{}d(\xi,\zeta,M_{h},\phi_{R,H})

(157)

is the density (in
\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi}\nolimits{}
) of dihadron pairs whose relative internal momenta lie within the integration volume
V
. Often one is interested in the cross section differential in
M_{h}
and
z
. Then, one may apply all the integrals in Eq. (
157
) to Eq. (
154
) apart from
\int\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muM_{h}}\nolimits{}
to define a reduced dihadron fragmentation function differential in
\xi
and
M_{h}
,

d_{\text{red,1}}(\xi,M_{h})\equiv\int_{-\zeta_{\text{max}}}^{\zeta_{\text{max}}}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\zeta}\nolimits{}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\phi_{R,H}}\nolimits{}\frac{M_{h}}{32\pi^{3}}d(\xi,\zeta,M_{h},\phi_{R,H})\,.

(158)

Note the limits of integration on the
\zeta
integral, which both approach zero as
M_{h}\to 2m
.
This reduced
d_{\text{red,1}}(\xi,M_{h})
is the density of hadrons with respect to the usual
\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi}\nolimits{}
phase space, but now it is also a density with respect to
\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muM_{h}}\nolimits
.
6
6

6

If we wished, we could have defined still another fragmentation function by absorbing the
M_{h}/(32\pi^{3})
of Eq. (
157
) into
d(\xi,\zeta,M_{h},\phi_{R,H})
to make it like a density in its arguments
\zeta,M_{h},\phi_{R,H}
. We refrain from doing this here to make keeping track of Jacobian factors simpler.

Applying the
\int M_{h}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\zeta}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\phi_{R,H}}\nolimits{}}{32\pi^{3}}
integral to the cross section in Eq. (
154
) gives

\displaystyle 2E_{h}(2\pi)^{3}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\sigma}\nolimits{}}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muM_{h}}\nolimits{}}=\int_{z}^{1}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi}\nolimits{}}{\xi^{2}}\left(2E_{\hat{k}}(2\pi)^{3}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\hat{\sigma}}\nolimits{}}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{\hat{k}}}\nolimits{}}\right)d_{\text{red,1}}(\xi,M_{h})+\text{p.s.}\,.

(159)

This form of the factorization formula matches exactly the single hadron case apart from the cross section now being differential in
M_{h}
and the fragmentation function being a density in both total momentum fraction
\xi
and the dihadron mass
M_{h}
. All subsequent steps to simplify the cross section are identical to those for a single-hadron fragmentation function, apart from the need to carry along an
M_{h}
argument in the fragmentation function. The steps in Eq. (
142
) are also unchanged and give

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\sigma}\nolimits{}}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muz}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muM_{h}}\nolimits{}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi\alpha^{2}}{3Q^{2}}d_{\text{red,1}}(z,M_{h})+\text{p.s.}+O\left(\alpha_{s}\right)=\sigma_{0}d_{\text{red,1}}(z,M_{h})+\text{p.s.}+O\left(\alpha_{s}\right)\,.

(160)

With the error terms dropped, this is the form of the cross section that is commonly used for phenomenological applications (see Eq. (9) of Ref.
[
4
]
).
7
7

7

Recall that here we use a single parton with unit charge, hence
we must restore the overall factor of
N_{c}=3
and the squared fractional charge
e_{q}^{2}
with a sum over flavors to get back to the full QCD version in Ref.
[
4
]
.

There, it is based on the operator definition in Eq. (11) from Ref.
[
10
]
(or Eq. (16) from Ref.
[
13
]
), which matches Eq. (
130
) above (see Appendix
B
for a more detailed comparison).
Thus, the definitions used in these earlier applications are consistent with the factorization derivation.

To reduce the cross section to a still simpler form, one may integrate over
M_{h}
. Then,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\sigma}\nolimits{}}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muz}\nolimits{}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi\alpha^{2}}{3Q^{2}}d_{\text{red,2}}(z;M_{h,\text{max}})+\text{p.s.}+O\left(\alpha_{s}\right)=\sigma_{0}d_{\text{red,2}}(z;M_{h,\text{max}})+\text{p.s.}+O\left(\alpha_{s}\right)\,,

(161)

where

d_{\text{red,2}}(z;M_{h,\text{max}})\equiv\int_{2m}^{M_{h,\text{max}}}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muM_{h}}\nolimits{}\,d_{\text{red,1}}(z,M_{h})\,.

(162)

The
M_{h}
integral is ultraviolet divergent in general, so the cutoff
M_{h,\text{max}}
cannot be made arbitrarily large. To maintain factorization one must ensure that
M_{h,\text{max}}^{2}/z\ll Q^{2}
. Thus, the fully integrated dihadron fragmentation function comes with an additional auxiliary ultraviolet scale
M_{h,\text{max}}
.

In a simple parton model treatment, one may expect that integrating over
z
down to a small
z
cutoff and using the definition of the inclusive cross section gives an approximate relation analogous to Eq. (
141
),

\displaystyle\underset{\text{types}}{\sum_{\text{hadron}}}\int_{z_{\text{min}}}^{1}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muz}\nolimits d_{\text{red,2}}(z;M_{h,\text{max}};\mu)\approx\langle N_{2}\rangle(z_{\text{min}},M_{h,\text{max}},\mu)\,,

(163)

where
\langle N_{2}\rangle(z_{\text{min}},M_{h,\text{max}},\mu)
is a multiplicity of dihadron pairs inside a region specified by
M_{h,\text{max}}
and
z_{\text{min}}
, and we have restored the explicit scale parameter
\mu
as an argument. The auxiliary scales
\mu
,
M_{h,\text{max}}
and
z_{\text{min}}
may be thought of as defining the borders of a jet or jet-like structure. The
z_{\text{min}}
should be large relative to
\Lambda^{2}/Q^{2}
and
M_{h,\text{max}}
should be much less than
Q
. The “
\approx
” refers to both neglected subleading powers and higher orders in
\alpha_{s}
. If the total number of hadrons in this region is
N
, then the number of dihadron pairs is
N\choose 2
multiplied by the
2!
ways of relabeling the hadrons. Thus,
\langle N_{2}\rangle=\langle N(N-1)\rangle
.

The general
zeroth order
dihadron factorization formula is

\displaystyle 2E_{h_{1}}(2\pi)^{3}2E_{h_{2}}(2\pi)^{3}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\sigma}\nolimits{}}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h_{1}}}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h_{2}}}\nolimits{}}

\displaystyle=\int_{z}^{1}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi}\nolimits{}}{\xi^{2}}\left(2E_{\hat{k}}(2\pi)^{3}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\hat{\sigma}}\nolimits{}}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{\hat{k}}}\nolimits{}}\right)d(\xi,\left\{p_{h}\right\})+\text{p.s.}\,

\displaystyle=\frac{8(2\pi)^{3}}{Q^{2}}\int_{z}^{1}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi}\nolimits{}}{\xi^{2}}\left(\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\hat{\sigma}}\nolimits{}}{\hat{z}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\hat{z}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\Omega}\nolimits}\right)d(\xi,\left\{p_{h}\right\})+\text{p.s.}

\displaystyle=\frac{8(2\pi)^{3}}{Q^{2}}\int_{z}^{1}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi}\nolimits{}}{\xi^{2}}\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2Q^{2}}\left[\widehat{F}_{1}+\widehat{F}_{2}\frac{\hat{z}}{4}\sin^{2}\theta\right]\right)d(\xi,\left\{p_{h}\right\})+\text{p.s.}

\displaystyle=\frac{8(2\pi)^{3}}{Q^{2}}\frac{1}{z}\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2Q^{2}}\left[1-2\frac{1}{4}\sin^{2}\theta\right]\right)d(z,\left\{p_{h}\right\})+\text{p.s.}+O\left(\alpha_{s}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\alpha^{2}(2\pi)^{3}}{zQ^{4}}\left(1+\cos^{2}\theta\right)d(z,\left\{p_{h}\right\})+\text{p.s.}+O\left(\alpha_{s}\right)\,,

(164)

where we used the expressions for
\widehat{F}_{1,2}
in Eq. (
87
). When applying Eq. (
164
) phenomenologically, the exact choice of variables one uses for
\bm{p}_{h_{1}}
and
\bm{p}_{h_{2}}
is an arbitrary convention unrelated to the basic definition of the dihadron fragmentation function
d(z,\left\{p_{h}\right\})
or its interpretation. The choice we used above that leads to Eq. (
154
) is only one possible example.

VII.2
Variable choice B

Another choice of variables, used in Ref
[
39
]
, is the set of three-vector momenta

\bm{p}_{h}=\bm{p}_{h_{1}}+\bm{p}_{h_{2}}\,,\qquad\bm{l}=\bm{p}_{h_{1}}-\bm{p}_{h_{2}}\,,

(165)

with the relevant
\bm{l}
variables being its transverse components and azimuthal angle in the hadron frame, and with the cross section made differential in
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
separately.
In Ref.
[
39
]
,
\bm{l}
was called
\bm{q}
, but we use a different label here to avoid overlapping with the virtual photon momentum label.
The components of the hadron momenta in the laboratory frame are listed in Appendix
A
.
Below,
\bm{l}_{\text{T}}
is the transverse part of
\bm{l}
in the hadron frame and
\phi_{T}
is its hadron frame azimuthal angle. Since our focus is the SIA factorization region, we consider only the case where the angle between
\bm{p}_{h_{1,2}}
and
\bm{p}_{h}
is less than 90
o
, i.e. we keep only positive square roots. The invariant mass squared
M_{h}^{2}
is then

\displaystyle M_{h}^{2}\to M_{h}^{2}(Q,z_{1},z_{2},l_{\text{T}})=\frac{z_{1}z_{2}Q^{2}}{2}\left(1+\frac{l^{2}_{\text{T}}+4m^{2}}{z_{1}z_{2}Q^{2}}-\sqrt{1-\frac{l^{2}_{\text{T}}+4m^{2}}{z_{1}^{2}Q^{2}}}\sqrt{1-\frac{l^{2}_{\text{T}}+4m^{2}}{z_{2}^{2}Q^{2}}}\right)=\frac{l^{2}_{\text{T}}z^{2}}{4z_{1}z_{2}}+\text{p.s.}\,,

(166)

where
l_{\text{T}}
is the magnitude of the transverse part of
\bm{l}
in the hadron frame. We may change variables as follows,

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h_{1}}}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h_{2}}}\nolimits{}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\phi_{T}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\phi}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\theta}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mul_{\text{T}}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu|\bm{p}_{h_{1}}|}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu|\bm{p}_{h_{2}}|}\nolimits\frac{l_{\text{T}}\sin\theta}{4}\left(|\bm{p}_{h_{1}}|+|\bm{p}_{h_{2}}|\right)^{2}+\text{p.s.}\,,

\displaystyle{}=\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\phi_{T}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\phi}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\cos(\theta)}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mul^{2}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu|\bm{p}_{h_{1}}|}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu|\bm{p}_{h_{2}}|}\nolimits\frac{1}{8}\left(|\bm{p}_{h_{1}}|+|\bm{p}_{h_{2}}|\right)^{2}+\text{p.s.}\,.

(167)

Now the “
p.s.
” includes
l_{\text{T}}/Q
-suppressed contributions.
Equation (
167
) agrees with the Jacobian factor listed on page 10 of
Ref.
[
39
]
.
Using
|\bm{p}_{h_{1}}|=E_{h_{1}}=z_{1}Q/2
+
p.s.
,
|\bm{p}_{h_{2}}|=E_{h_{2}}=z_{2}Q/2+\text{p.s.}
, this is also

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\phi_{T}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\phi}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\cos(\theta)}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mul^{2}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu|\bm{p}_{h_{1}}|}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu|\bm{p}_{h_{2}}|}\nolimits\frac{1}{8}\left(|\bm{p}_{h_{1}}|+|\bm{p}_{h_{2}}|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle{}=\frac{1}{32}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\phi_{T}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\Omega}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mul^{2}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu|\bm{p}_{h_{1}}|}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu|\bm{p}_{h_{2}}|}\nolimits Q^{2}\left(z_{1}+z_{2}\right)^{2}\,

\displaystyle{}=\frac{z^{2}}{128}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\phi_{T}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\Omega}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mul^{2}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muz_{1}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muz_{2}}\nolimits Q^{4}\,,

(168)

all up to power-suppressed errors. Then

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY}\nolimits{}=\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h_{1}}}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h_{2}}}\nolimits{}}{2E_{h_{1}}(2\pi)^{3}2E_{h_{2}}(2\pi)^{3}}

\displaystyle{}=\frac{Q^{2}z^{2}}{128z_{1}z_{2}(2\pi)^{6}}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\Omega}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\phi_{T}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mul^{2}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muz_{1}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muz_{2}}\nolimits+\text{p.s.}\,.

(169)

Now Eq. (
56
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\sigma}\nolimits}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muY}\nolimits}

\displaystyle={2E_{h_{1}}(2\pi)^{3}2E_{h_{2}}(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\sigma}\nolimits}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h_{1}}}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{h_{2}}}\nolimits{}}

\displaystyle=\frac{128z_{1}z_{2}(2\pi)^{6}}{Q^{2}z^{2}}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\sigma}\nolimits}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\Omega}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\phi_{T}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mul^{2}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muz_{1}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muz_{2}}\nolimits}=\frac{4(2\pi)^{3}\alpha^{2}}{Q^{4}}\left[F_{1}(z,Q^{2},\left\{p_{h}\right\})+\frac{z}{4}F_{2}(z,Q^{2},\left\{p_{h}\right\})\sin^{2}\theta\right]\,,

Alternative definitions with nonuniversal prefactors

It was recently argued in Ref.

\displaystyle d\left(\xi,-\xi\bm{k}_{H\text{T}},p_{h_{1}},p_{h_{2}}\right)\,

\displaystyle\qquad\equiv\frac{1}{64\pi^{3}\xi_{1}\xi_{2}}{\rm Tr}\SumInt_{X}\int\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mux^{-}}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{x}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits{}}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{ix^{-}p_{h,H}^{+}/\xi-i\bm{k}_{H\text{T}}\cdot\bm{x}_{\text{T}}}\langle 0|\gamma^{+}\psi(x/2)|p_{h_{1}},p_{h_{2}},X\rangle\langle p_{h_{1}},p_{h_{2}},X|\overline{\psi}(-x/2)|0\rangle\,

\displaystyle\qquad\equiv\frac{1}{64\pi^{3}\xi^{2}x_{1}x_{2}}{\rm Tr}\SumInt_{X}\int\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mux^{-}}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{x}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits{}}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{ix^{-}p_{h,H}^{+}/\xi-i\bm{k}_{H\text{T}}\cdot\bm{x}_{\text{T}}}\langle 0|\gamma^{+}\psi(x/2)|p_{h_{1}},p_{h_{2}},X\rangle\langle p_{h_{1}},p_{h_{2}},X|\overline{\psi}(-x/2)|0\rangle\,.

x=(0,x^{-},\bm{x}_{\text{T}})
. The

factor only depends on the external kinematics of the hadrons, so apart from such constant factors there is an extra overall
1/\xi
prefactor in this definition relative to standard definition.
9
9

9

Here we are interpreting the prefactors as referring to momentum fractions
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
, as is stated in Ref.
[

z_{1}
and
z_{2}
. If the latter was the case, then the definition would still differ from the standard one, but by a factor of
\xi

1/\xi

z_{1}
and

are defined in terms of the external momentum
q
.
The corresponding collinear fragmentation function is deduced by further applying the transverse parton momentum integral
to it. For each

[

]
, with an extra prefactor of

[

Equation (
VIII

IV

, and since this impacts the interpretation of past phenomenological work it is important to understand the origin of the discrepancy (see Ref.
[

We identify a combination of two main reasons that Ref.
[
29
]
arrives at a different definition:

1.

Reference
[
29
]
conflates parton momentum and hadron momentum, and in particular the parton momentum fractions that we call
\xi_{1}
,
\xi_{2}
, and
\xi
are identified with the external kinematical variables like
z_{1}
,
z_{2}
, and
z
. While these are fixed by a
\delta
-function to be numerically equal in zeroth order hard scattering, they have different roles in factorization and in operator definitions for fragmentation functions. In higher orders, and also in the treatment of evolution, momentum fractions are not generally equal to external kinematical variables.

2.

The treatment in Ref.
[
29
]
infers the form of the dihadron fragmentation function from the requirement that an incorrect sum rule of the type

\underset{\text{types}}{\sum_{\text{hadron}}}\int\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi_{1}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi_{2}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{p{h_{1}}\text{T}}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{p}_{p{h_{2}}\text{T}}}\nolimits\ldots d\left(\xi,\bm{p}_{p{h}\text{T}},\left\{p_{h_{n}}\right\}\right)=\langle N_{n}\rangle

(198)

must be preserved, where
\langle N_{n}\rangle
is a multiplicity of observed final state
n
-hadrons. The specific argument of Ref.
[
29
]
is that the normalization factors of
2E_{i}(2\pi)^{3}
for each observed hadron
i
in the
asymptotic
n
-hadron state,
combined with Eq. (
198
), would imply that the operator definition of multihadron fragmentation functions must come with extra additional prefactors of
1/(2E_{i}(2\pi)^{3})
.
After changing variables in Eq. (
198
), and with the conflation of momentum fractions and kinematical variables remarked upon in the first item above, that would seem to require modified prefactors like the one in Eq. (
VIII
).

However, those external kinematical prefactors are already accounted for on the left-hand side of the basic expression for the cross section in Eq. (
3
) and in Eq. (
164
). Repeating them in the definitions of fragmentation functions themselves amounts to overcounting them. The
1/(4\xi)
prefactor in the standard definition, Eq. (
V.1
), actually arises from the partonic momentum, not from the phase space of observed hadrons.

Equation (
198
) itself fails for several reasons: In QCD, it fails for the interesting and rather fundamental reason that hadronic final states have no overlap with states that have partonic quantum numbers. For a recent discussion of this and related issues see Ref.
[
43
]
. More generally, the sum rule fails even in nongauge theories because it requires integrating the momenta outside the region where factorization is valid, and where the right-hand side of Eq. (
198
) is not meaningful. Indeed, the integral in (
198
) is generally ultraviolet divergent in theories that require renormalization. Recalling the discussions from Sec.
V.2
above, however, it is important to emphasize that preserving such a sum rule is not necessary for the standard fragmentation function definition to retain a parton model interpretation, so long as the usual caveats are accounted for.

If one does use a definition like Eq. (
VIII
) in a factorization formula then, as the steps shown in previous sections illustrate, one is forced to modify the hard partonic part with extra powers of
\xi
for
each additional hadron that appears in the observed
n
-hadron state.
This removes the property expected for a satisfactory factorization theorem that hard parts are independent of the details of external nonperturbative states.

Moreover, if one modifies the basic definition of fragmentation functions with nonuniversal
\xi
-dependent prefactors this
also modifies the renormalization factors in the renormalized fragmentation functions, which unavoidably changes the evolution kernels. To see this, recall how a renormalized fragmentation function is related to its bare operator definition (see, e.g., Sec. 12.2.2 in Ref.
[
22
]
),

d_{h/j}(\xi,\left\{p_{h}\right\};\mu)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sum_{j^{\prime}}\int_{z}^{1}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi^{\prime}}\nolimits}{\xi^{\prime}}d_{(0)h/j^{\prime}}(\xi/\xi^{\prime},\{p_{h}\})Z_{j^{\prime}j}(\xi^{\prime},g(\mu),\epsilon)=\sum_{j^{\prime}}d_{(0)h/j^{\prime}}\otimes Z_{j^{\prime}j}\,.

(199)

Here we have restored parton flavor subscripts and the explicit
\mu
and
\epsilon
arguments that signal ultraviolet regularization and renormalization in the
\overline{\rm MS}
scheme. Also, the “
(0)
” subscripts on the right-hand side denote bare operators. The
d_{(0)h/j^{\prime}}(\xi/\xi^{\prime},\{p_{h}\})
is given by the definition in Sec.
V
, but now we specify bare operators for the fields.
The renormalization factor
Z_{j^{\prime}j}(\xi^{\prime},g(\mu),\epsilon)
is independent of the identity of the hadronic state
h
, and this gives rise to the hadron-independence of evolution kernels. Indeed, a standard method for calculating
Z_{j^{\prime}j}
in perturbation theory is to exploit the
h
-independence by replacing
h
with an on-shell partonic state. This then allows for simple calculations of the evolution kernels directly from massless, on-shell Feynman diagrams. That procedure is invalidated, however, if the overall prefactor in Eq. (
V.1
) is made dependent (in a
\xi
-dependent way) on the identity of
h
, and in particular on the number of hadrons that make up
h
.

A fast way to see this is with sample calculations in model field theories like a scalar Yukawa theory with nonzero masses and with the interaction terms

-g\overline{\psi}\psi\phi-\frac{\lambda}{3!}\phi^{3}\,

(200)

in the Lagrangian density.
Here,
\psi
is a Dirac “quark” and
\phi
is a scalar “hadron.” The lowest order hadron-in-quark and dihadron-in-quark fragmentation functions are given by the graphs in Fig.
4
.
The only contribution to the renormalization factor
Z_{j^{\prime}j}
in the collinear fragmentation function at this order of perturbation theory comes from the ultraviolet divergence at large
k_{H\text{T}}

k_{H\text{T}}

d_{(0),\phi/\phi}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\phi/q}\qquad\text{and}\qquad d_{(0),\phi\phi/\phi}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\phi/q}

(201)

where
\mathcal{H}_{\phi/q}

5

Z_{j^{\prime}j}
arises for both the single and dihadron cases, but only if both use the same operator definitions.
If the prefactor in the operator definition becomes
h

IX
Comments and discussion

The purpose of this paper is to reaffirm and clarify the validity of prior phenomenological applications of dihadron (or multihadron) fragmentation functions that used a standard operator definition, but extended to include small-mass multihadrons as the observed final state. To this end, we have considered the semi-inclusive production of “
n
-hadrons” from
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation (SIA) and we have retraced the kinematical steps involved in separating the unfactorized
cross section into a hard partonic cross section and a fragmentation function. We have verified
that they are unmodified if the observed final state is a low-invariant-mass multihadron. In particular, the prefactor of the resulting fragmentation function definition is the same for all values of
n
.

An additional goal is to argue that the parton model interpretation of the standard fragmentation function definition also remains applicable even in the case when the produced object is a small-mass multihadron, at least up to the usual caveats and limitations that apply to number density interpretations for all correlation functions. We argue that an alternative definition that was recently introduced
[
29
]
is not consistent with factorization derivations unless hard parts and evolution kernels are allowed to acquire nontrivial dependence on external nonperturbative states. For this reason, we advocate for the continued use of the standard definition rather than the newly suggested one.

To keep the discussion simple, we have focused on the unpolarized multihadron fragmentation functions, but the results apply equally to polarization sensitive quantities like interference fragmentation functions
[
10
,
12
]
.

The topics addressed in this paper mainly concern the formal theoretical foundations of multihadron fragmentation functions in the context of factorization. It remains unclear to us whether and how these results impact the interpretation of existing phenomenological results at a practical level. For example, Refs.
[
30
]
and
[
4
]
appear to use identical expressions for the unpolarized factorization formula that relates the SIA cross section to fragmentation functions (compare Eq. (2) in Ref.
[
30
]
and Eq. (9) in Ref.
[
4
]
), and the same evolution equations are used in both analyses. Therefore, it appears to us on the surface that the steps for extracting the fragmentation functions phenomenologically should be insensitive to the formal operator definitions that are referenced.
However, page 15 of Ref.
[
30
]
asserts that comparisons between the two extractions are obstructed by the differing operator definitions. We leave it for future work to investigate if and to what extent the use of different formal definitions in these phenomenological extractions limits the ability to compare them.

In future phenomenological applications it will also be important to map out the approximate kinematical regions where factorization with
n
-hadron fragmentation functions can reasonably be expected to remain valid. For any process where a factorization theorem holds, there can be separate relevant ranges of kinematics where different versions of factorization apply, for example in the target versus current regimes of semi-inclusive deep inelastic scattering.
When the observed final state is an
n
-hadron system, the classification of regions becomes more complicated than in the single hadron case because the invariant mass
M_{h}
of the system is no longer fixed and the final state requires a larger set of kinematical variables to fully characterize. In this paper, therefore, we have included discussions about the accuracy of approximations for the case of an observed
n
-hadron final state
in semi-inclusive
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation in somewhat greater detail than is typical.

While the basic definitions of multihadron fragmentation functions discussed here are unmodified from the single hadron case, further refinements to the treatment of factorization in multihadron cases are needed, and some directions for further work are implied by the discussions provided in this paper.

As noted, for example, in Refs.
[
44
,
45
,
46
]
, when
\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{2}\ll M_{h}^{2}
the dihadron fragmentation function needs to be refactorized into single hadron fragmentation functions. Moreover, the integrals over
M_{h}^{2}
in the reduced fragmentation functions are ultraviolet divergent, and this leads to extra terms in the collinear evolution equations
[
45
]
. Furthermore, when
M_{h}^{2}\approx Q^{2}
the factorization formula for the dihadron cross section into dihadron fragmentation functions fails, and one must match the description to a formula where the two hadrons are in entirely separate jets, or where multiple partons fragment.

Indeed, it is generally not straightforward in physical applications to separate fragmentation of a single parton into two hadrons (the mechanism addressed in this paper) from mechanisms where two partons each separately fragment into a hadron. In the intermediate region where
\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{2}\ll M_{h}^{2}\ll Q^{2}
, it is likely that a resummation of powers of
\ln(M_{h}^{2}/Q^{2})
is helpful. The different regions of factorization need to be matched to one another. We intend to discuss this and related aspects of multihadron fragmentation functions further in future work.

Appendix C
Effect of extra prefactors on evolution

First, consider the definition with the standard prefactor for both the single and dihadron cases and use the Yukawa theory toy model discussed in Sec.
VIII
.
For the zeroth order bare scalar-in-scalar fragmentation function,

d^{[O\left(1\right)]}_{0,s/s}(\xi)=k^{+}\xi^{3}\int\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muk^{-}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{k}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits}{(2\pi)^{4}}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(k-p_{h})=\delta(1-\xi)\,.

(246)

For the zeroth order bare dihadron-in-scalar fragmentation function,

d_{0,ss/s}^{[O\left(1\right)]}(\xi)=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{M_{h}^{4}}k^{+}\xi^{3}\int\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muk^{-}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mskip-0.59999mu\bm{k}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits}{(2\pi)^{4}}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(k-p_{h})=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{M_{h}^{4}}\delta(1-\xi)\,.

(247)

At
O\left(g^{2}\right)
in the Yukawa theory, the single hadron and dihadron fragmentation functions that follow from their operator definitions also satisfy

d_{0,ss/q}^{[O\left(g^{2}\right)]}(\xi)=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{M_{h}^{4}}d_{0,s/q}^{[O\left(g^{2}\right)]}(\xi)\,.

(248)

The
\overline{\rm MS}
quark-in-scalar renormalization factor expanded in perturbation theory is

Z_{qj^{\prime}}(\xi)=\delta_{qj^{\prime}}\delta(1-\xi)+g^{2}\frac{S_{\epsilon}}{\epsilon}\delta_{qj^{\prime}}Z^{[O\left(g^{2}\right)]}_{j^{\prime}/s}(\xi)+O\left(g^{4}\right)\,,

(249)

where the dimension of spacetime is
4-2\epsilon
and
S_{\epsilon}=(4\pi)^{\epsilon}/\Gamma(1-\epsilon)
.
Expanded in perturbation theory, Eq. (
199
) is

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sum_{j^{\prime}}\int_{z}^{1}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi^{\prime}}\nolimits}{\xi^{\prime}}\left[d_{0,s/q}^{[O\left(g^{2}\right)]}(\xi/\xi^{\prime})\delta(1-\xi^{\prime})+g^{2}\frac{S_{\epsilon}}{\epsilon}Z^{[O\left(g^{2}\right)]}_{q/s}(\xi)d_{0,s/s}^{[O\left(1\right)]}(\xi/\xi^{\prime})\right]+O\left(g^{4}\right)

(250)

and in the dihadron case,

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sum_{j^{\prime}}\int_{z}^{1}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi^{\prime}}\nolimits}{\xi^{\prime}}\frac{\lambda^{2}}{M_{h}^{4}}\left[d_{0,s/q}^{[O\left(g^{2}\right)]}(\xi/\xi^{\prime})\delta(1-\xi^{\prime})+g^{2}\frac{S_{\epsilon}}{\epsilon}Z^{[O\left(g^{2}\right)]}_{q/s}(\xi)d_{0,s/s}^{[O\left(1\right)]}(\xi/\xi^{\prime})\right]+O\left(g^{4}\right)\,.

(251)

The
Z^{[O\left(g^{2}\right)]}_{q/s}(\xi)
in the second term needs to ensure the cancellation of the
S_{\epsilon}/\epsilon
pole in the first term.
Clearly, then, the same
Z^{[O\left(g^{2}\right)]}_{q/s}(\xi)
applies to both the single and dihadron cases. The only difference between the two cases is the overall
\frac{\lambda^{2}}{M_{h}^{4}}
in the dihadron case that arises from the dihadron/scalar vertex.

Now consider the consequence of modifying the prefactor of the dihadron fragmentation function definition by a factor
\mathcal{N}(\xi)=\xi^{\nu}
(with
\nu\neq 0
) relative to the single hadron fragmentation function. Then in the above we have the replacements,

\displaystyle d_{0,ss/s}^{[O\left(1\right)]}(\xi)\to\;

\displaystyle{}\mathcal{N}(\xi)\frac{\lambda^{2}}{M_{h}^{4}}d_{0,s/s}^{[O\left(1\right)]}(\xi)=\mathcal{N}(1)\frac{\lambda^{2}}{M_{h}^{4}}d_{0,s/s}^{[O\left(1\right)]}(\xi)\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle d_{0,ss/q}^{[O\left(g^{2}\right)]}(\xi/\xi^{\prime})\to\;

\displaystyle{}\mathcal{N}(\xi/\xi^{\prime})\frac{\lambda^{2}}{M_{h}^{4}}d_{0,s/q}^{[O\left(g^{2}\right)]}(\xi/\xi^{\prime})\,.

(252)

Now Eq. (
251
) becomes

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sum_{j^{\prime}}\int_{z}^{1}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi^{\prime}}\nolimits}{\xi^{\prime}}\frac{\lambda^{2}}{M_{h}^{4}}\mathcal{N}(1)\left[\frac{\mathcal{N}(\xi)}{\mathcal{N}(1)}d_{0,s/q}^{[O\left(g^{2}\right)]}(\xi/\xi^{\prime})\delta(1-\xi^{\prime})+g^{2}\frac{S_{\epsilon}}{\epsilon}Z^{[O\left(g^{2}\right)]}_{q/s}(\xi)d_{0,s/s}^{[O\left(1\right)]}(\xi/\xi^{\prime})\right]+O\left(g^{4}\right)\,.

(253)

Here we have used
\mathcal{N}(\xi/\xi^{\prime})=\mathcal{N}(\xi)
because of the
\delta(1-\xi^{\prime})
. Comparing with Eq. (
251
) shows that the order-
g^{2}
correction to the
\overline{\rm MS}
renormalization factor is modified from the result in the standard definition by a factor of
\mathcal{N}(\xi)/\mathcal{N}(1)
for the dihadron case,

\left(Z^{[O\left(g^{2}\right)]}_{q/s}(\xi)\right)_{\text{dihadron}}=\frac{\mathcal{N}(\xi)}{\mathcal{N}(1)}\left(Z^{[O\left(g^{2}\right)]}_{q/s}(\xi)\right)_{\text{single hadron}}

(254)

The splitting functions
P_{jj^{\prime}}
are obtained by inverting the
equation for the running of the renormalization factor,

\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu}\nolimits{}}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\ln\mu^{2}}\nolimits{}}Z_{jk}(\xi)=\sum_{j^{\prime}}\int\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi^{\prime}}\nolimits{}}{\xi^{\prime}}P_{jj^{\prime}}(\xi^{\prime})Z_{j^{\prime}k}(\xi/\xi^{\prime})\,.

(255)

Therefore, modifying the prefactor of the operator definition of the multihadron fragmentation function by a nontrivial
\xi
-dependent factor necessarily leads to a modification of the evolution kernel
P_{jj^{\prime}}
for the multihadron case relative to the single hadron case.

Note that for treating evolution it is the momentum fraction
\xi
that is relevant and not external kinematical factors of
z
, etc., that might arise from the Jacobians when changing the variables used to parametrize a cross section for a specific process.

The evolution is unaffected if
\mathcal{N}(\xi)
consists only of constant factors like
\pi
since these cancel from both sides.

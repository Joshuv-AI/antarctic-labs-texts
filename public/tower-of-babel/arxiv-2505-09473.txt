Function-Correcting b b -symbol Codes for Locally ( λ , ρ , b \lambda,\rho,b )-Functions
Gyanendra K. Verma, Anamika Singh and Abhay Kumar Singh

Abstract
The family of functions plays a central role in the design and effectiveness of function-correcting codes. By focusing on a well-defined family of functions, function-correcting codes can be constructed with minimal length while still ensuring full error detection or correction within that family. In this work, we explore the concept of locally ( λ , ρ ) (\lambda,\rho) -functions for b b -symbol read channels and investigate the redundancy of the corresponding function-correcting b b -symbol codes(FCBSC) by introducing the notions of locally ( λ , ρ , b ) (\lambda,\rho,b) -functions. First, we discuss the possible values of λ \lambda and ρ \rho for which any function can be considered as locally ( λ , ρ ) (\lambda,\rho) -function in b b -symbol metric. The findings improve some known results in the Hamming metric and present several new results in the b b -symbol metric. Then we investigate the redundancy of ( f , t ) (f,t) -FCBSC for locally ( λ , ρ , b ) (\lambda,\rho,b) -functions. We establish a recurrence relation between the optimal redundancy of ( f , t ) (f,t) -function-correcting codes for the ( b + 1 ) (b+1) -read and b b -read channels. We establish an upper bound on the redundancy of ( f , t ) (f,t) -function-correcting b b -symbol codes for general locally ( λ , ρ \lambda,\rho , b b )-functions by linking it to the minimum achievable length of b b -symbol error-correcting codes and traditional Hamming-metric codes, given a fixed number of codewords and a specified minimum distance. We derive some explicit upper bounds on the redundancy of ( f , t ) (f,t) -function-correcting b b -symbol codes for ρ = 2 ​ t \rho=2t . Moreover, for the case where b = 1 b=1 , we show that a locally ( 3 , 2 ​ t , 1 3,2t,1 )-function achieves the optimal redundancy of 3 ​ t 3t . Additionally, we explicitly investigate locality and redundancy for the b b -symbol weight distribution function for b ≥ 1 b\geq 1 .

Introduction

In Function-Correcting Codes (FCCs), the function plays a central role, as it determines the specific aspect or computation of the message that must be preserved during transmission. Unlike classical error-correcting codes, which aim to recover the entire message despite the presence of errors, FCCs are designed to ensure the accuracy of a particular function applied to the message, even if parts of the message are corrupted. This change in focus places the function at the core of both the encoding and decoding processes. Moreover, FCCs open new directions in coding theory by enabling the design of codes tailored to the structure and significance of specific functions, rather than treating all message components uniformly. As a result, FCCs offer a powerful and adaptable approach with strong potential for both theoretical advancement and practical application.

Given the notable advantage of function-correcting codes (FCCs) in minimizing redundancy, it is valuable to investigate their performance across different types of communication channels. Such studies can improve our understanding of FCCs and broaden their potential applications. In contemporary digital systems, the vast volume of data necessitates the use of high-density storage technologies. However, due to inherent physical limitations, these systems often produce outputs as overlapping pairs of consecutive symbols rather than individual symbols. To address the unique challenges posed by these conditions, the notions of symbol-pair read channels and
b
-symbol read channels have been developed, aiming to provide robust protection against errors occurring within symbol-pairs and
b
-symbols.The concept of symbol-pair read channels was first introduced by Cassuto and Blaum in
[
1
]
, and later extended to
b
-symbol read channels by Yaakobi et al. in
[
16
]
. In a series of subsequent works
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
8
,
13
,
14
]
, various bounds and coding-theoretic properties have been explored for these channels, deepening the theoretical foundation and guiding the design of efficient error-correcting codes tailored to these reading models.

Lenz et al. introduced the concept of Function-Correcting Codes (FCCs) in
[
7
]
, where it was shown that FCCs are equivalent to irregular-distance codes. This equivalence highlights that the ability of an FCC to protect a specific function of the message corresponds to designing codes with varying error-correction capabilities across different parts of the message. Xia et al.
[
15
]
extended the concept of Function-Correcting Codes (FCCs) from binary symmetric channels to symbol-pair read channels, introducing a new coding paradigm tailored to scenarios where data are accessed in overlapping symbol pairs rather than individual symbols. In
[
12
]
, Singh et al. advanced the theory by developing function-correcting codes for
b
-symbol read channels over finite fields and provided the notion of irregular
b
-distance codes.
Optimal redundancy plays a crucial role in the design and analysis of Function-Correcting Codes (FCCs). In
[
7
,
10

12
,
15
]
, the authors have provided either exact values for optimal redundancy or established upper and lower bounds for it, offering key insights into the efficiency limits of FCCs. The optimal redundancy of Function-Correcting Codes (FCCs) in the classical setting is discussed in
[
6

[
7

. In
[
9

Building on this result, they proposed an encoding scheme that achieves the optimal redundancy. Motivated by these two limiting cases, they further conjectured that the bound remains a valid upper bound across all finite fields.

The concept of locally (
\lambda,\rho
)-function-correcting codes is explored in
[

In this work, we first investigate various recurrence relations associated with locally

Preliminaries

This section presents fundamental concepts related to classical
b
-symbol codes and function-correcting
b
-symbol codes. These definitions and properties form the foundational tools necessary for understanding the main results developed in the subsequent sections of this paper. For
b=1
, function-correcting b-symbol codes reduce to the classical function-correcting codes (FCC). We use the term FCBSC in place of FCC to maintain generality. To align with existing notation for function-correcting codes in the Hamming metric, we denote redundancy by
r_{f}^{H}(k,t)
.

2.1
b
-Symbol Codes

Definition 2.1
.

[
16
,
b
-Symbol read vector]

Let
x=(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1})
be a vector in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
. The
b
-symbol read vector
of
x
, denoted by
\pi_{b}(x)
, is defined as:

\pi_{b}(x)=\left[(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{b-1}),(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{b}),\ldots,(x_{k-1},x_{0},\ldots,x_{b-2})\right],

where each entry is a consecutive sequence of
b
symbols taken cyclically from
x
.

With this definition, we introduce the notions of
b
-symbol distance and the associated
b
-symbol read code.

Definition 2.2

(
b
-Symbol distance)
.

Given two vectors
x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
, the
b
-symbol distance
between them is defined by:

d_{b}(x,y)=d_{H}\left(\pi_{b}(x),\pi_{b}(y)\right),

where
d_{H}
denotes the Hamming distance.

Definition 2.3

(
b
-Symbol read code)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a code over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. The corresponding
b
-symbol read code
, denoted by
\pi_{b}(\mathcal{C})
, is the image of
\mathcal{C}
under the
b
-symbol read mapping:

\pi_{b}(\mathcal{C})=\left\{\pi_{b}(c):c\in\mathcal{C}\right\}.

The minimum
b
-symbol distance of
\mathcal{C}
is defined as

d_{b}(\mathcal{C})=d_{H}\left(\pi_{b}(\mathcal{C})\right),

that is, the minimum Hamming distance between any two distinct elements of
\pi_{b}(\mathcal{C})
. A code
\mathcal{C}
is capable of correcting up to
t

b
-symbol errors if and only if

d_{b}(\mathcal{C})\geq 2t+1.

Let
u\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. Define a ball
B^{b}(u,\rho)
with center
u
and radius
\rho
as follows

B^{b}(u,\rho)=\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}:\ d_{b}(u,y)\leq\rho\}.

For
b=1
, we denote a ball in the Hamming metric by
B^{H}(u,\rho)
with center
u
and radius
\rho
.
We now present a result that quantifies the relationship between the classical Hamming distance and the more general
b
-symbol distance.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
12
, Lemma 2.1]

Let
x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
then

1.

If
d_{H}(x,y)>n-(b-1)
, then
d_{b}(x,y)=n
.

2.

If
0<d_{H}(x,y)\leq n-(b-1)
, then
d_{H}(x,y)+(b-1)\leq d_{b}(x,y)\leq b\cdot d_{H}(x,y)
.

3.

If
d_{H}(x,y)=0
, then
d_{b}(x,y)=0
.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
8
, Lemma 1]

Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
,
y=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. If
n\geq b
and
x_{j}=y_{j}
for each
j\in[1,b]
, then

w_{b}((x,y))=w_{b}(x)+w_{b}(y).

Lemma 2.6
.

[
14
]
[Plotkin bound for
b
-symbol distance]
Let
r,n
be positive integers satisfying
2\leq d\leq n
and
rn<d,
where
r=1-2^{-b}.
Then

A_{b}(n,d,2)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d}{d-rn}\right\rfloor.

where
A_{b}(n,d,2)
denotes the largest possible code size for which there exists a binary code of length n with minimum b-symbol distance at least d.

2.2
Function-Correcting Codes for
b-
Symbol Read Channel(FCBSC)

This section discusses the definition and basic results on function-correcting b-symbol codes(FCBSC) over binary fields.

Definition 2.7
.

[
12
]
[Function-correcting
b
-symbol code]
An encoding function

\text{Enc}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k+r},\quad\text{Enc}(x)=(x,p(x))

is said to define a function-correcting
b
-symbol code for the function
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\rightarrow\text{Im}(f)
if for all distinct
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
with
f(x_{1})\neq f(x_{2})
, the
b
-symbol distance satisfies:

d_{b}(\text{Enc}(x_{1}),\text{Enc}(x_{2}))\geq 2t+1.

Definition 2.8
.

[
12
]
[Optimal redundancy]
The optimal redundancy for a function
f
under
t
-correctable
b
-symbol errors is the smallest positive integer
r
for which a function-correcting
b
-symbol code exists. It is denoted by
r_{b}^{f}(k,t).

Definition 2.9

(Irregular
b
-symbol distance matrices)
.

Consider
M
vectors
x_{0},\ldots,x_{M-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
. Then, the matrices
\bm{B_{f}^{(1)}}(t,x_{0},\ldots,x_{M-1})
and
\bm{B_{f}^{(2)}}(t,x_{0},\ldots,x_{M-1})
are
M\times M
irregular
b
-symbol distance matrices with entries defined as follows:

[\bm{B_{f}^{(1)}}(t,x_{0},\ldots,x_{M-1})]_{ij}=\begin{cases}[2t-b+2-d_{b}(x_{i},x_{j})]^{+}&\text{if }f(x_{i})\neq f(x_{j}),\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

[\bm{B_{f}^{(2)}}(t,x_{0},\ldots,x_{M-1})]_{ij}=\begin{cases}[2t+b-d_{b}(x_{i},x_{j})]^{+}&\text{if }f(x_{i})\neq f(x_{j}),\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Definition 2.10

(
\bm{B_{b}}
-Code)
.

A set of codewords
\mathcal{P}=\{\bm{p}_{1},\ldots,\bm{p}_{M}\}
is said to be a
\bm{B}
-irregular

-symbol distance code (or
\bm{B_{b}}
-code) for some matrix
\bm{B}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M\times M}
if there exists an ordering of the codewords in
\mathcal{P}
such that

d_{b}(\bm{p}_{i},\bm{p}_{j})\geq\bm{[B]_{ij}}\quad\text{for all }i,j\in\{1,2,\ldots,M\}.

Let
N_{b}(\bm{B})
denote the smallest positive integer
r
for which there exists a
\bm{B_{b}}
-code of length
r
. In particular, when all non-diagonal entries of
\bm{B}
are equal to some non-negative integer
D
, we write
N_{b}(M,D)
in place of
N_{b}(\bm{B})
.

Lemma 2.11
.

[
12
, Lemma 4.6]

Let
\lambda
and
t
be positive integers with
\lambda>2^{b-1}

and
2t\geq b
. Then,

N_{b}(\lambda,2t)\leq N_{H}(\lambda,2t-b+1)

We now introduce the notion of a
function ball
, which captures the set of function outputs within a specified radius with respect to the Hamming metric around a given point.

Definition 2.12

(Function Ball)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\to\operatorname{Im}(f)
be a function. For a vector
u\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
and a non-negative integer
\rho
, the
function ball
of
f
with radius
\rho
centered at

is defined as

B_{f}^{b}(u,\rho)=\{f(u^{\prime})\mid u^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\text{ and }d_{b}(u,u^{\prime})\leq\rho\}.

For
b=1
, a function ball in the Hamming metric is denoted by
B_{f}(u,\rho)
and defined as

B_{f}(u,\rho)=\{f(u^{\prime})\mid u^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\text{ and }d_{H}(u,u^{\prime})\leq\rho\},

where
d_{H}(u,u^{\prime})
denotes the Hamming distance between

and
u^{\prime}
.

3
Locally
\mathbf{(}\lambda,\rho,b)
-function

In this section, we introduce the concept of
(\lambda,\rho)
-locality for functions over
b
-symbol read channels, which we refer to succinctly as
(\lambda,\rho,b)
-local functions. Every function
f
exhibits
(\lambda,\rho,b)
-locality for appropriate choices of
\lambda
,
\rho
, and
b
. We investigate various recurrence relations associated with
(\lambda,\rho,b)
-local functions, focusing on how these parameters interact. We improve several results established in
[
11
]
and further generalize them to the setting of
b
-symbol read channels. First, we formally define the notion of a locally
(\lambda,\rho,b)
-function.

A function
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\to Im(f)
is said to be a locally
(\lambda,\rho,b)
-function if

|B_{f}^{b}(u,\rho)|\leq\lambda

for all
u\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
.

Determining the smallest value of
\lambda
for which a given function
f
is a locally
(\lambda,\rho,b)
-function, for fixed values of
\rho
and
b
, presents a nontrivial challenge. It is immediate that any function is
(\lambda,\rho,b)
-local when
\lambda=|\text{Im}(f)|
, irrespective of the choices of
\rho
and
b
. Similarly, when
\rho=k
, the function
f
is
(\lambda,\rho,b)
-local only if
\lambda\geq|\text{Im}(f)|
. Also, for
2\leq\beta\leq\lambda
, every locally
(\beta,\rho,b)
-function is a locally
(\lambda,\rho,b)
-function. Since these scenarios are relatively straightforward and offer limited theoretical insights, our attention is primarily directed toward the study of locally
(\lambda,\rho,b)
-functions under the more restrictive conditions
\lambda\leq|\text{Im}(f)|
and
\rho\leq k
. We denote
\lambda_{s}
to be the smallest value of
\lambda
for which a function
f
is locally
(\lambda_{s},\rho,b)
-function. Note that

|B_{f}^{b}(u,\rho)|\leq|B^{b}(u,\rho)|
for all
u\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
. In fact
|B^{b}(u,\rho)|=|B^{b}(\mathbf{0},\rho)|
for all
u\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
. This observation gives the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\to Im(f)
be a locally
(\lambda_{s},\rho,b)
-function. Then
\lambda_{s}\leq B^{b}(\mathbf{0},\rho)
.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
2\leq\rho+1\leq k-1
and
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\to Im(f)
is a locally
(\lambda,\rho,b)
- function, then
f
is a locally
(\lambda,\rho+1,b+1)
-function.

Proof.

Since
f
is a locally
(\lambda,\rho,b)
-function,
|B_{f}^{b}(u,\rho)|\leq\lambda
for all
u\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
. Let
f(v)\in B_{f}^{b+1}(u,\rho+1)
. Then
d_{b+1}(u,v)\leq\rho+1
. Also, we know that
d_{b}(u,v)\leq d_{b+1}(u,v)-1=\rho
. That is,
f(v)\in B_{f}^{b}(u,\rho)
. Thus,
B_{f}^{b+1}(u,\rho+1)\subseteq B_{f}^{b}(u,\rho)
. Hence
|B_{f}^{b+1}(u,\rho+1)|\leq|B_{f}^{b}(u,\rho)|\leq\lambda
.
∎

The next proposition establishes a connection between the containment properties of balls under the Hamming distance and those under the
b
-symbol distance (see
[
3
, Proposition 2]
).

Proposition 3.3
.

B^{b}(u,\rho)\subset B^{H}(u,\rho-b+1)
and
B^{H}(u,\rho)\subset B^{b}(u,b\rho)
. Also,
B^{b}(u,\rho)=u
for
\rho\leq b-1
and
B^{b}(u,b)=B^{H}(u,1)
.

The subsequent results arise immediately as consequences of the above proposition.

Corollary 3.4
.

B^{b}_{f}(u,\rho)\subset B^{H}_{f}(u,\rho-b+1)
and
B^{H}_{f}(u,\rho)\subset B^{b}_{f}(u,b\rho)
. Moreover,
B^{b}_{f}(u,\rho)=f(u)
for
\rho\leq b-1
and
B^{b}_{f}(u,b)=B^{H}_{f}(u,1)
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\to Im(f)
be a function.

1.

If
f
is a locally
(\lambda,\rho,b=1)
-function, then
f
is a locally
(\lambda,\rho+b-1,b)
-function.

2.

If
f
is a locally
(\lambda,b\rho,b)
-function, then
f
is a locally
(\lambda,\rho,b=1)
-function.

3.

For,
\rho\leq b-1
,
f
is locally
(1,\rho,b)
-function.

4.

f
is a locally
(\lambda,b,b)
-function if and only if
f
is a locally
(\lambda,\rho=1,b=1)
-function.

3.1
Locality of the weight distribution function

In this subsection, we investigate weight distribution functions associated with both the Hamming distance and the
b
-symbol distance. Let
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\to\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
be the
b
-symbol weight distribution function with threshold
T
, defined as

f(u)=\Delta_{T}^{b}(u)=\lfloor\frac{w_{b}(u)}{T}\rfloor
.
In the special case when
T=1
, the function reduces to
\Delta^{b}(u)=w_{b}(u)
, known as the
b
-symbol weight function. Furthermore, when
b=1
, the corresponding function is referred to as the Hamming weight distribution function, denoted by
\Delta_{T}^{H}
. In particular, for
b=1
and
T=1
, it simplifies to the classical Hamming weight function, denoted by
\Delta^{H}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\Delta_{T}^{b}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\to Im(f)
be the
b
-symbol weight distribution function. Then
\Delta_{T}^{b}
is a locally
(\lfloor\frac{k}{T}\rfloor+1,\rho,b)
-function for any
0\leq\rho\leq k
. That is
\lambda_{s}\leq\lfloor\frac{k}{T}\rfloor+1
.

Proof.

The proof follows from the fact
0\leq\Delta_{T}^{b}(v)\leq\lfloor\frac{k}{T}\rfloor
for all
v\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
and
0\leq\rho\leq k
.
∎

The following theorem gives a value of
\lambda
for which the Hamming weight function
w_{H}
serves as a locally
(\lambda,\rho,b=1)
-function. The proof is based on finding the maximum and minimum achievable Hamming weight in an arbitrary function ball.

Theorem 3.7
.

For
k\geq 2\rho
, the Hamming weight function
w_{H}
is a locally
(2\rho+1,\rho,b=1)
-function.

Proof.

We claim that
|B^{b}_{w_{H}}(u,\rho)|\leq 2\rho+1
for all
u\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
and
\lambda=max_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}}|B_{w_{H}}^{b}(u,\rho)|=2\rho+1
. Let
u\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
and
w_{H}(u)=w
. Also, let
u^{\prime}\in B_{w_{H}}^{b}(u,\rho)
such that
d_{H}(u,u^{\prime})=d\leq\rho
. Then, the Hamming weight of
u^{\prime}
can be expressed in terms of the Hamming weight of
u
as follows:

w_{H}(u^{\prime})=w+a-b

(3.1)

where
a
is the number of changes of symbols in
u
from
0
to
1
and
b
is the number of changes of symbols in
u
from
1
to
0
to obtain
u^{\prime}
from
u
.
As
a+b=d
, the Hamming weight of any arbitrary
u^{\prime}\in B_{w_{H}}^{b}(u,\rho)
in terms of
d
can be given as follows:

w_{H}(u^{\prime})=\{w+d-2a:0\leq d\leq\rho\text{ and }0\leq a\leq min(d,w)\}

(3.2)

From
3.2
we can see that the Hamming weight inside the function ball ranges over the interval
[max(0,w-\rho),min(k,w+\rho)]
.
The length of this interval depends on
w
as follows:

Case 1:
If
\rho\leq w\leq k-\rho
, then the interval in
3.2
becomes
[{w-\rho},{w+\rho}]
and the length of this interval is
2\rho+1
. Therefore, in this case, we have
|B_{w_{H}}^{b}(u,\rho)|=2\rho+1
.

Case 2:
If
\rho>w
, then the interval in
3.2
becomes
[0,{w+\rho}]
and the length of this interval is
w+\rho+1<2\rho+1
. Therefore, in this case, we have
|B_{w_{H}}^{b}(u,\rho)|<2\rho+1
.

Case 3:
If
w>k-\rho,
then the interval in
3.2
becomes
[{w-\rho},k]
and the length of this interval is
k-w+\rho+1<2\rho+1
. Therefore, in this case, we have
|B_{w_{H}}^{b}(u,\rho)|<2\rho+1
.
Hence, from all the above cases we get
|B^{b}_{f}(u,\rho)|\leq 2\rho+1
for all
u\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
, and this upper bound is tight when
\rho\leq w_{H}(u)\leq k-\rho
. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.10
.

For

\Delta_{T}^{H}
is a locally
(\lfloor\frac{2\rho}{T}\rfloor+2,\rho,b=1)
-function .

Proof.

Let
u\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
and
w_{H}(u)=w
. Also, let
u^{\prime}\in B_{\Delta^{H}_{T}}^{b}(u,\rho)
such that
d_{H}(u,u^{\prime})=d\leq\rho

u^{\prime}

u
as follows:

w_{H}(u^{\prime})=w+a-b,

where
a
is the number of changes of symbols in
u
from
0
to
1

b
is the number of changes of symbols in
u

1
to
0

As

w_{H}(u^{\prime})=\{w+d-2a:0\leq d\leq\rho\text{ and }0\leq a\leq min(d,w)\}.

From the above, we can see that the Hamming weight inside the function ball ranges over the interval
[max(0,w-\rho),min(k,w+\rho)]
. Hence, the Hamming weight distribution inside
B_{\Delta^{H}_{T}}^{b}(u,\rho)
ranges over the interval:

\left[\left\lfloor\frac{max(0,w-\rho)}{T}\right\rfloor,\left\lfloor\frac{min(k,w+\rho)}{T}\right\rfloor\right].

(3.3)

The length of this interval depends on
w
as follows:

Case 1:
If
\rho\leq w\leq k-\rho
, then the above interval becomes
[\left\lfloor\frac{w-\rho}{T}\right\rfloor,\left\lfloor\frac{w+\rho}{T}\right\rfloor]
and the length of the interval is
\left\lfloor\frac{w+\rho}{T}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{w-\rho}{T}\right\rfloor+1\leq\left\lfloor\frac{w+\rho}{T}-\frac{w-\rho}{T}\right\rfloor+2\leq\left\lfloor\frac{2\rho}{T}\right\rfloor+2

.

Case 2:

\rho>w

3.3
becomes
[0,\left\lfloor\frac{w+\rho}{T}\right\rfloor]

\left\lfloor\frac{w+\rho}{T}\right\rfloor+1<\left\lfloor\frac{2\rho}{T}\right\rfloor+1\leq\left\lfloor\frac{2\rho}{T}\right\rfloor+2

B_{\Delta^{H}_{T}}^{b}(u,\rho)|<\left\lfloor\frac{2\rho}{T}\right\rfloor+2

4
Redundancy of FCBSC for locally
(\lambda,\rho,b)
-functions

Rajput et al.
[
11
]
derived several bounds on the optimal redundancy for
(f,t)
-FCC corresponding to locally
(\lambda,\rho)
-functions with respect to the Hamming distance. They further demonstrated that this optimal redundancy is achievable for locally
(4,2t)
-functions under certain conditions. In this section, we derive a recurrence relation connecting the optimal redundancy for
b
and
(b+1)
-symbol read channels. Furthermore, we establish upper bounds on the optimal redundancy of locally defined functions for general
b
-symbol read channels. These findings generalize several of the bounds established in
[
11
]
to the broader setting of the
b
-symbol distance model.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
N_{H}(\lambda,2t)
represents the minimum possible length of a binary error correcting code with
\lambda
codewords and minimum Hamming distance
2t
, then
N_{H}(\lambda,2t)
is upper bounded as follows.

N_{H}(\lambda,2t)\leq\lambda t.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\{c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{\lambda}\}
, where
c_{i}
is a vector of length
\lambda t
consisting of
(i-1)t\text{ }0s
followed by
t\text{ }1s
followed by
(\lambda-i)t

0s
. Then clearly
\mathcal{C}
is a binary ECC
(\lambda t,\lambda,2t)
code. Therefore,
N_{H}(\lambda,2t)\leq\lambda t.

∎

Using
[
11
, Theorem 7]
and Lemma
4.1
, we derive an upper bound on the optimal redundancy of an
(f,t)
-FCBSC, for any locally
(\lambda,2t,b=1)
-bounded function
f
, as stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
t
be a positive integer. For any locally
(\lambda,2t,b=1)
-bounded function
f
, the optimal redundancy of an
(f,t)
-FCBSC is bounded above as follows.

r_{f}^{H}(k,t)\leq\lambda t.

In the next theorem, we prove that the bound obtained in the above lemma is achievable. We provide an example for clarity.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\lambda=3
and
t>0
be an integer. Then, for any locally
(3,2t,b=1)
-function, the optimal redundancy of an
(f,t)
-FCBSC satisfies
r_{f}^{H}(k,t)\leq 3t
. Moreover, for a locally
(3,2t,b=1)
-function
f
with
|Im(f)|\geq 3
, if there exists
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
with
f(u_{1})\neq f(u_{2})\neq f(u_{3})
such that

d_{b}(u_{1},u_{2})=1,d_{b}(u_{3},u_{1})=1\ \text{and}\ d_{b}(u_{3},u_{2})=2,

then
r^{H}_{f}(k,t)=3t
.

Proof.

From Theorem
4.2
, we observe that the optimal redundancy of a
(3,2t,b=1)
-function is upper bounded by
3t
, i.e.,
r_{f}^{H}(k,t)\leq 3t
. For
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
, we have the distance requirement matrix

\mathcal{D}_{f}(t,u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})=\left[\begin{matrix}0&2t&2t\\
2t&0&2t-1\\
2t&2t-1&0\end{matrix}\right].

From the generalized Plotkin bound derived in
[
7
, Lemma 1]
, we have

\displaystyle N(\mathcal{D}_{f}(t,u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{4}{3^{2}-1}(D_{1,2}+D_{1,3}+D_{2,3})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(6t-1)=3t-\frac{1}{2}.

Since
N(\mathcal{D}_{f}(t,u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}))
is an integer, by
[
7
, Corollary 1]
, we have

r_{f}^{H}(k,t)\geq N(\mathcal{D}_{f}(t,u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}))\geq 3t.

Hence
r_{f}^{H}(k,t)=3t
.
∎

The following example shows that the assumptions in Theorem
4.3
is not absurd.

Example 4.4
.

Consider the Hamming weight function
w_{H}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2}\rightarrow\{0,1,2\}
. Let
u_{1}=(1,0),u_{2}=(0,0),\text{ and }u_{3}=(1,1)
. Observe that the function values are

w_{H}(u_{1})=1,\quad w_{H}(u_{2})=0,\quad w_{H}(u_{3})=2.

Additionally, the Hamming distances between these vectors are:

d_{H}(u_{1},u_{2})=1,\quad d_{H}(u_{1},u_{3})=1,\quad d_{H}(u_{2},u_{3})=2.

Hence,
w_{H}
satisfies the required conditions.

Remark 4.5
.

Theorem 4.3 shows that the bound established in Theorem 4.2 is tight for locally
(3,2t,b=1)
-functions (i.e.,
\lambda=3
) under certain conditions. Moreover, the upper bound is also found to be tight for locally binary functions in
[

In the following theorem, we present a recurrence relation that connects the optimal redundancy of
(f,t)
-FCBSC for
b
-read and
(b+1)
-read channels. This result provides a systematic framework for estimating the redundancy required as the symbol-read length increases.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\to Im(f)
. Then for any
(f,t)
-FCBSC, we have

r_{f}^{b+1}(k,t)\leq r_{f}^{b}(k,t),

where
r_{f}^{b+1}(k,t)
and
r_{f}^{b}(k,t)
are optimal redundancy of
(f,t)
-FCBSC for
(b+1)
-read and
b
-read channels, respectively.

Proof.

Let
r_{f}^{b}(k,t)=r
be the optimal redundancy of
(f,t)
-FCBSC. Suppose a corresponding encoding is given by
Enc(u)=(u,u_{p})
, where
u_{p}
is the redundancy bits in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}
. For
f(u)\neq f(v)
, we have
d_{b}(Enc(u),Enc(v))\geq 2t+1
. Also,

d_{b+1}(Enc(u),Enc(v))\geq d_{b}(Enc(u),Enc(v))+1\geq 2t+1.

Thus
r_{f}^{b+1}(k,t)\leq r_{f}^{b}(k,t)
.

∎

The following lemma plays a crucial role while constructing redundancy bits. The proof follows by employing a vertex coloring approach on an appropriately defined graph, analogous to the technique utilized in
[
11
, Lemma 1]
for the Hamming distance setting.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\to Im(f)
be a locally
(\lambda,\rho,b)
-function. Then there exists a map
\tau_{f}^{b}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\to[\lambda]
such that
Im(\tau_{f}^{b})\leq[\lambda]
and for all
u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
,
\tau_{f}^{b}(u)\neq\tau_{f}^{b}(v)
if either
u\in B_{f}^{b}(v,\rho)
or
v\in B_{f}^{b}(u,\rho)
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
t>0
and
b\geq 1
be integers and
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\to Im(f)
be a locally
(4,2t,b)
-function. Then for any
(f,t)
-FCBSC, the optimal redundancy satisfies

r_{f}^{b}(k,t)\leq 3t-b+1.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.7
, there exist a map
\tau_{f}^{b}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\to[4]
such that
\tau_{f}^{b}(u)\neq\tau_{f}^{b}(v)
for all
u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
whenever
u\in B_{f}^{b}(v,2t)
or
v\in B_{f}^{b}(u,2t)
. Define

u_{p}=\begin{cases}000\hskip 28.45274pt\text{ if }\hskip 14.22636pt\tau_{f}^{b}(u)=1\\
110\hskip 28.45274pt\text{ if }\hskip 14.22636pt\tau_{f}^{b}(u)=2\\
101\hskip 28.45274pt\text{ if }\hskip 14.22636pt\tau_{f}^{b}(u)=3\\
011\hskip 28.45274pt\text{ if }\hskip 14.22636pt\tau_{f}^{b}(u)=4.\end{cases}

Write
(u_{p})^{t}=(u_{p}^{\prime},u_{p}^{\prime\prime})
where
(u_{p})^{t}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{3t}
is
t
times repetition,
u_{p}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{3t-b+1}
(first
(3t-b+1)
coordinates) and
u_{p}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{b-1}
(last
(b-1)
coordinates). Define an encoding map
\mathcal{E}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k+(3t-b+1)}
as
\mathcal{E}(u)=(u,u_{p}^{\prime})
.
Observe that, for
u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
, we have

\displaystyle d_{H}(u_{p}^{t},v_{p}^{t})=d_{H}(u_{p}^{\prime},v_{p}^{\prime})+d_{H}(u_{p}^{\prime\prime},v_{p}^{\prime\prime})

Also,
d_{H}(u_{p}^{t},v_{p}^{t})=2t
and
d_{H}(u_{p}^{\prime\prime},v_{p}^{\prime\prime})\leq b-1
. Thus,

d_{H}(u_{p}^{\prime},v_{p}^{\prime})=d_{H}(u_{p}^{t},v_{p}^{t})-d_{H}(u_{p}^{\prime\prime},v_{p}^{\prime\prime})\geq 2t-(b-1).

(4.1)

Now, we show that the above encoding defines an
(f,t)
-FCBSC with redundancy
r_{f}^{b}(k,t)=3t-b+1
for
b
-symbol read channels. Let
u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
with
f(u)\neq f(v)
. Then

Case 1
: If

, then

d_{b}(\mathcal{E}(u),\mathcal{E}(v))\geq d_{b}(u,v)\geq 2t+1.

Case 2
: If
d_{b}(u,v)\leq 2t
, then
f(v)\in B_{f}^{b}(u,2t)
. Consequently,
\tau_{f}^{b}(u)\neq\tau_{f}^{b}(v)
. Thus,
u_{p}^{\prime}\neq v_{p}^{\prime}
.
If
d_{H}(\mathcal{E}(u),\mathcal{E}(v))>k+(3t-b+1)-(b-1)
then by Lemma
2.4

\displaystyle\begin{split}d_{b}(\mathcal{E}(u),\mathcal{E}(v))=k+3t-b+1\geq 2t+1\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ (since $k\geq b$) }.\end{split}

Otherwise,

\displaystyle\begin{split}d_{b}(\mathcal{E}(u),\mathcal{E}(v))&\geq d_{H}(\mathcal{E}(u),\mathcal{E}(v))+(b-1)\\
&=d_{H}(u,v)+d_{H}(u_{p}^{\prime},v_{p}^{\prime})+(b-1)\\
&\geq 2t+1\hskip 14.22636pt(\text{by Eq.}\ref{eq2t-b+1})\end{split}

(4.2)

5
Conclusion

In this work, we explore function-correcting codes designed for locally (
\lambda,\rho,b
)-functions over
b
-symbol read channels. Utilizing the structural properties of locally (
\lambda,\rho,b
)-functions, we establish upper bounds on the redundancy of function-correcting codes designed for
b
-symbol read channels. We show that, under certain conditions, a local-(
3,2t,1
)-function achieves the optimal redundancy of

3t
. Furthermore, we explicitly examine the relationship between locality and redundancy in the setting of weight functions. The study of function-correcting codes for different classes of functions under various metrics is an interesting and active area of research. Therefore, extending the study of function-correcting codes to other function classes and diverse channel models remains an important avenue for future investigation. We are currently investigating function-correcting codes for classes of locally (
\lambda,\rho
)-functions and linear functions over homogeneous metric models.

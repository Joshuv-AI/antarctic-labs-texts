Odderon in the light of collider low- t t data
E. G. S. Luna, M. G. Ryskin, V. A. Khoze

Abstract
Odderon is the C C -odd amplitude that does not fall (or decrease very slowly) with energy. The expected amplitude is small and mainly real. Therefore, extracting it from the data on top of a much larger C C -even contribution is challenging. The only chance is to consider the very low | t | |t| region of Coulomb nuclear interference or the diffractive dip region. Here we perform the analysis of elastic scattering p ​ p pp and p ¯ ​ p \bar{p}p data at low momentum transfer | t | < 0.1 |t|<0.1 GeV 2 within large collider energy interval s = 50 \sqrt{s}=50 GeV − - 13 TeV in order to evaluate quantitatively the possible Odderon contribution. We use the two-channel eikonal model, which naturally accounts for the screening of the Odderon amplitude by the C C -even (Pomeron) exchanges.



I
Introduction

Odderon is the
C
-odd amplitude which does not fall (or die very slowly) with energy. Theoretically, there is no reason
not

\alpha_{s}

Since the Odderon amplitude is expected to be rather small the best chance to observe it on top of a much larger
C
-even contribution is either in the diffractive dip region where the imaginary part of
C
-even amplitude vanishes or by measuring the real part of
pp
(
\bar{p}p
) elastic amplitude. Recall that due to dispersion relations the real part of high energy
C
-even amplitude is relatively small (Re
A_{even}\ll

A_{even}

The real part of proton-proton amplitude can be measured via the Coulomb-nuclear interference (CNI) at very low momentum transferred
|t|

elastic pp amplitude at 13 TeV

This prompted a renewal of interest in the potential existence of the high-energy

IV
Conclusion

The main lessons about the Odderon learned from this study are:

•

The description using the Odderon improves the fit (with the Odderon
\chi^{2}
becomes much smaller).

•

The sign of the Odderon amplitude needed to describe the very low
|t|
data is opposite to that predicted by the perturbative QCD three-gluon exchange contribution
DL
;
FK
;
Ro
.

2
2

2

The problem can be solved assuming that the Odderon coupling
\beta_{\mathbb{O}}(t)
vanishes (or strongly decreases) at
t=0
. In this case, the dominant
C
-odd contribution at
t=0
comes from the Pomeron-Odderon cut and has the opposite sign.

•

The Odderon-proton coupling,
\beta_{\mathbb{O}}(0)
, is smaller than that for the Pomeron. Moreover, after accounting via the eikonal the screening of seed Odderon by the Pomerons, the final
C
-odd contribution to
\rho
at 13 TeV becomes quite small,

\delta\rho=(\rho^{\bar{p}p}-\rho^{pp})/2\leq 0.004
– i.e. 10 times smaller than that (
\delta\rho=0.04
) originally claimed by TOTEM.

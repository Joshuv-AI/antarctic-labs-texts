Imaginary Gauge-steerable Edge Modes In Non-Hermitian Aubry-André-Harper Model
Yazhuang Miao, Wei Ding, Litong Wang, Xiaolong Zhao, Shengguang Liu, Xuexi Yi

Abstract
We identify steerable exponentially localized in-gap mode in a quasiperiodic non-Hermitian Aubry-André-Harper chain with a spatially fluctuating, zero-mean imaginary gauge field. Under open boundary conditions, the system is exactly related to the Hermitian AAH model by a nonunitary gauge transformation: the OBC spectrum and Lyapunov exponents are unchanged, while eigenstates acquire a gauge-dependent envelope. In a parameter region with spectrally isolated in-gap boundary modes, we find two exponentially localized in-gap modes with sharply different responses to the imaginary gauge field. One remains boundary pinned, but the other is gauge-steerable: it stays exponentially localized while its probability maximum shifts as the gauge field is changed, with its eigenenergy unchanged. We further show that weak on-site gain, applied at a single site chosen once and then kept fixed, can dynamically prepare this steerable mode from a generic bulk wave packet. Changing the gauge field then yields exponentially localized states at different locations.

In this work, we address this question by employing an exact nonunitary gauge transformation under OBC.

repositioned by varying the gauge field without shifting its eigenvalue. We further propose a preparation protocol

kept fixed, which allows the steerable in-gap mode to be amplified from a bulk wave packet and, by changing the gauge field,
to be prepared at different locations.

III
Nonunitary gauge transformation and phase structure

III.1
Nonunitary gauge transformation

To obtain an exact and analytically tractable description of the phase structure and in-gap modes in the presence of an erratic imaginary gauge field, we map the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian under OBC to its Hermitian AAH counterpart via a nonunitary gauge transformation, so that eigenvalues are preserved and non-Hermitian effects enter only through the spatial rescaling of eigenstates.
In the single-particle scenario, an eigenstate of
Hamiltonian (
5
) can be written as
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\psi_{n}\hat{c}_{n}^{\dagger}|0\rangle
.
The coefficients
\psi_{n}
and the corresponding eigenenergy
E
satisfy the
stationary Schrödinger equation
\hat{H}|\psi\rangle=E|\psi\rangle
, which in
the site basis gives

\displaystyle J_{R}(n-1)\psi_{n-1}+V_{n}\psi_{n}+J_{L}(n)\psi_{n+1}=E\psi_{n}.

(9)

Using Eqs. (
6
) and (
7
), the hopping asymmetry is gauged
away by the rescaling

\displaystyle\psi_{n}=e^{X_{n}}\,\phi_{n}.

(10)

Substituting Eq. (
10
) into
Eq. (
9
) shows that the transformed amplitudes

\phi_{n}
obey a Hermitian AAH equation with symmetric
nearest-neighbor hopping
J
and on-site potential
V_{n}
,

\displaystyle J\,\phi_{n-1}+V_{n}\phi_{n}+J\,\phi_{n+1}=E\,\phi_{n}.

(11)

Thus the entire OBC spectrum of
\hat{H}
coincides with the Hermitian counterpart AAH chain.
To characterize the asymptotic spatial growth/decay of a given eigenstate in the thermodynamic
limit, we introduce the Lyapunov exponent (spatial exponent)

\displaystyle\Gamma(E)=\left|\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\ln\left|\frac{\psi_{n}}{\psi_{1}}\right|\right|.

(12)

Using Eqs. (
7
) and (
10
), one obtains the exact decomposition
ENHSE_PRL2025

\displaystyle\Gamma(E)=\tilde{\Gamma}(E)+\bar{h},

(13)

where
\tilde{\Gamma}(E)
is the corresponding exponent extracted from
\phi_{n}
, and

\displaystyle\bar{h}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{X_{n}}{n}

(14)

is the spatial average of the imaginary gauge field.
For the Bernoulli gauge disorder defined by Eq.
8
,
\bar{h}=0
in the long-chain limit, so that

\displaystyle\Gamma(E)=\tilde{\Gamma}(E).

(15)

When an eigenenergy
E
belonging to the continuous bulk spectrum of the Hermitian AAH chain,

\tilde{\Gamma}(E)
is known exactly and is independent of
E

it vanishes for
\lambda\leq 2J
and equals
\ln(\lambda/2J)

\lambda>2J

Combining this result with Eq. (
15

for bulk eigenstates of
\hat{H}

\displaystyle\Gamma(E)=\begin{cases}0,&\lambda\leq 2J,\\[4.0pt]
\ln\!\left(\dfrac{\lambda}{2J}\right),&\lambda>2J,\end{cases}

(16)

which yields the bulk localization length
\xi=1/\Gamma(E)
for
\lambda>2J

For OBC eigenvalues

that fall inside a bulk gap, the associated transfer matrix
has a nonzero Lyapunov exponent, implying an exponentially decaying tail whose decay rate is
energy dependent

;
Kohmoto1983_PRL
;

V
Local-gain protocol for preparing the gauge-steerable in-gap state

To go beyond a static eigenstate analysis, we aim to prepare the gauge-steerable in-gap mode ES.2 from a bulk wave packet using a weak local-gain protocol. Without this gain, the state retains substantial overlap with many bulk modes, so ES.2 is not preferentially selected. We show that weak on-site gain at a single site provides a mode selector: for an isolated eigenmode it induces a small imaginary-energy shift, and the mode with the largest positive shift dominates the long-time normalized dynamics.

Let
\{|\psi^{\mathrm{R}}_{\alpha}\rangle\}
and
\{|\psi^{\mathrm{L}}_{\alpha}\rangle\}

be the right and left eigenstates of
\hat{H}
,

\displaystyle\hat{H}|\psi^{\mathrm{R}}_{\alpha}\rangle

\displaystyle=E_{\alpha}|\psi^{\mathrm{R}}_{\alpha}\rangle,

(20)

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\dagger}|\psi^{\mathrm{L}}_{\alpha}\rangle

\displaystyle=E_{\alpha}^{*}|\psi^{\mathrm{L}}_{\alpha}\rangle,

(21)

with biorthogonal normalization

\langle\psi^{\mathrm{L}}_{\alpha}|\psi^{\mathrm{R}}_{\beta}\rangle=\delta_{\alpha\beta}
.
Consider a local on-site perturbation applied at lattice site
n
,

\displaystyle\delta\hat{V}=\delta v\,|n\rangle\langle n|,

(22)

where
\delta v
is a complex perturbation.
For an isolated, nondegenerate eigenvalue
E_{\alpha}
, first-order perturbation theory gives
Bai2025_SMT_SHE

\displaystyle\delta E_{\alpha}=\langle\psi^{\mathrm{L}}_{\alpha}|\delta\hat{V}|\psi^{\mathrm{R}}_{\alpha}\rangle=\delta v\,\chi_{\alpha}(n),

(23)

where the biorthogonal weight is

\displaystyle\chi_{\alpha}(n)=\langle\psi^{\mathrm{L}}_{\alpha}|n\rangle\langle n|\psi^{\mathrm{R}}_{\alpha}\rangle=\psi^{\mathrm{L}*}_{\alpha}(n)\,\psi^{\mathrm{R}}_{\alpha}(n).

(24)

For such a perturbation,
\chi_{\alpha}(n)
controls the first-order eigenvalue shift via Eq. (
23
).
We take
\delta v=i\gamma
, i.e., on-site gain, with
J\gg\gamma>0
, so that

\mathrm{Im}\,\delta E_{\alpha}=\gamma\,\chi_{\alpha}(n)
for an isolated mode. Thus, for a given target mode
\alpha
,
we choose

\displaystyle n_{0}=\arg\max_{n}\chi_{\alpha}(n),

(25)

which yields the maximal imaginary shift and this makes the target mode dominant in the long-time normalized dynamics.

Equation (
10
) allows one to evaluate
\chi_{\alpha}(n)
from the Hermitian counterpart.
Let
\hat{H}^{H}
be the Hermitian AAH Hamiltonian corresponding to
\hat{H}
under OBC by the nonunitary gauge
transformation, and let
|\phi_{\alpha}\rangle
be its normalized eigenstates,

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{H}|\phi_{\alpha}\rangle=E_{\alpha}|\phi_{\alpha}\rangle,\qquad\langle\phi_{\alpha}|\phi_{\beta}\rangle=\delta_{\alpha\beta}.

(26)

The right/left eigenstates of
\hat{H}
are then

\displaystyle|\psi^{\mathrm{R}}_{\alpha}\rangle=S|\phi_{\alpha}\rangle,\qquad|\psi^{\mathrm{L}}_{\alpha}\rangle=(S^{-1})^{\dagger}|\phi_{\alpha}\rangle,

(27)

with

\displaystyle\chi_{\alpha}(n)=\psi^{\mathrm{L}*}_{\alpha}(n)\psi^{\mathrm{R}}_{\alpha}(n)=|\phi_{\alpha}(n)|^{2}.

(28)

Therefore the optimal gain site is fixed by the profile of the Hermitian mode and is independent of the
erratic-gauge field.

VI
Conclusion

We have investigated a quasiperiodic non-Hermitian AAH chain with a zero-mean, spatially fluctuating imaginary
gauge field under OBC. An exact nonunitary gauge transformation mapped the system to its Hermitian AAH counterpart:
the spectrum and Lyapunov exponents were unchanged, while the right/left eigenstates were reshaped by the gauge-induced
envelope. In a parameter region with spectrally isolated in-gap edge modes, we identified an counterintuitive
in-gap state that remains exponentially localized but whose probability peak can be shifted by varying only the
imaginary gauge field, in contrast to a second mode that stays pinned to the boundary. We further showed that this steerable in-gap mode can be dynamically prepared from a bulk wave packet by applying weak on-site gain at a single fixed site, chosen where the corresponding Hermitian eigenstate attains its maximum probability density. With the gain site kept unchanged, varying the gauge field produces long-time states that remain exponentially localized but peak at different positions. These results provide a simple route to generating position-tunable, exponentially localized in-gap states in non-Hermitian quasiperiodic lattices.

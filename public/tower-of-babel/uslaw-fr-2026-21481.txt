I. Self-Regulatory Organization's Statement of the Terms of Substance 
of the Proposed Rule Change

    Cboe EDGX Exchange, Inc. (``Cboe EDGX'' or the ``Exchange'') is 
filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission (``Commission'' or 
``SEC'') a proposed rule change to amend Rule 11.1(a)(3) to describe 
when unpriced orders are eligible for execution and to amend Rule 
11.6(q)(2) to clarify the expiration of Day Orders entered during the 
Overnight Trading Session or Order Acceptance Queueing Time. The text 
of the proposed rule change is provided in Exhibit 5.
    The text of the proposed rule change is also available on the 
Commission's

A. Self-Regulatory Organization's Statement of the Purpose of, and 
Statutory Basis for, the Proposed Rule Change

1. Purpose
    The Exchange proposes to amend Rule 11.1(a)(3) to introduce rule 
text to describe when orders that do not contain a limit price are 
eligible for execution. Under current Rule 11.1(a)(1), the Exchange 
does not accept Market Orders,\5\ other than those with a Time-in-Force 
(``TIF'') instruction of Regular Hours Only \6\ or a Stop Price, prior 
to 4:00 a.m. Eastern Time or prior to 7:00 a.m. Eastern Time for orders 
eligible for a 7:00 a.m. Start.\7\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

buy or sell a stated amount of a security that is to be executed at 
the NBBO or better when the order reaches the Exchange. See also 
Exchange Rule 11.8(a)(5), which provides that a Market Order is only 
eligible for execution by the System during the Regular Session.)
    \6\ See Exchange Rule 1.5(y). (``Regular Trading Hours'' means 
``the time between 9:30 a.m. and 4:00 p.m. Eastern Time'').
    \7\ See Exchange Rule 11.1(a)(1).

    As part of the Exchange's transition to trading 23 hours per day, 5 
days per week (``23x5 Trading''), the Exchange updated Rule 11.1(a) to 
introduce a new subparagraph (3) titled ``Orders Permitted in Each 
Session.'' \8\ Rule 11.1(a)(3), which is approved but not yet 
effective, states: ``Market Orders, other than those designated with a 
Time-in-Force instruction of Regular Hours Only or those that include a 
Stop Price, will not be accepted by the Exchange prior to Regular 
Trading Hours.'' The intent of this sentence was to prevent Market 
Orders from executing during the Overnight Trading Session \9\ and the 
Pre-Opening Session,\10\ but the text did not expressly prohibit the 
Exchange from accepting Market Orders during Regular Trading Hours and 
executing during the Post-Closing Session,\11\ which experiences 
similar characteristics regarding liquidity and volume as the current 
Early Trading Session.\12\ Now, the Exchange proposes to add an 
additional sentence to the end of Rule 11.1(a)(3) which states: 
``Orders without a limit price will not be eligible for execution 
outside of Regular Trading Hours.'' The addition of this sentence 
eliminates any potential ambiguity about when an order that does not 
contain a limit price will be eligible for execution on the Exchange. 
In addition, this proposed change is substantially similar to limits on 
unpriced orders currently in place on Nasdaq Stock Market LLC 
(``Nasdaq'') and NYSE Arca, Inc. (``NYSE Arca'') and differs only in 
that the Exchange's proposed rule refers to orders without a limit 
price rather than specifically designating market orders or pegged 
orders as other market centers have done in their rule text.\13\

    The Exchange also proposes to amend Rule 11.6(q)(2) to clarify that 
a Day Order entered during the Order Acceptance Queueing Time \14\ or 
Overnight Trading Session will expire at the end of Regular Trading 
Hours on the same business day to which the Overnight Trading Session 
belongs, as provided in Rule 11.1(b).\15\ This amendment replaces the 
phrase ``following trading day,'' which could be read ambiguously for 
orders entered after midnight during the Overnight Trading Session, 
with language that expressly ties the order's expiration to the 
business day to which the Overnight Trading Session belongs.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The change is consistent with the Exchange's intent as described in 
the Original Filing, which introduced the 23x5 Trading framework and 
explained that ``a trading day is deemed to begin at 9:00 p.m. Eastern 
Time on the preceding calendar day.'' Under Rule 11.1(b), a business 
day includes the Overnight Trading Session on the preceding calendar 
day, meaning the entire Overnight Trading Session (from 9:00 p.m. 
through 4:00 a.m.) belongs to the following business day. The proposed 
amendment merely conforms the Day Order TIF definition to this 
structural principle and does not alter any operational aspect of Day 
Order handling.
    Specifically, current Rule 11.6(q)(2) provides that a Day Order 
entered during the Order Acceptance Queueing Time or the Overnight 
Trading Session ``will expire at the end of Regular Trading Hours on 
the following trading day.'' The Exchange proposes to replace 
``following trading day'' with ``same business day to which the 
Overnight Trading Session belongs, as provided in Rule 11.1(b).'' This 
revision eliminates any potential ambiguity regarding whether an order 
entered after midnight i.e., during the portion of the Overnight 
Trading Session occurring on a new calendar day) could be interpreted 
as expiring on a different business day than intended.

the Original Filing. Rules 11.1(a)(3) and 11.6(q)(2) will be amended

Exchange seeks to offer a new Overnight Trading Session. The amended 
rule text will take effect in December 2026 pending industry readiness. 
Prior to codifying the amended rule text, the Exchange will file a 
proposed rule change pursuant to Section 19(b) of the Exchange Act and 
the rules thereunder to amend its rules confirming that the Exchange is 
able to comply with its obligations under the Exchange Act and the 
rules thereunder

2. Statutory Basis
    The Exchange believes the proposed rule change is consistent with 
the Act \17\ and the rules and regulations thereunder applicable to the 
Exchange and, in particular, the requirements of Section 6(b) of the 
Act. Specifically, the Exchange believes the proposed rule change is 
consistent with Section 6(b)(5) of the Act,\18\ which requires that the 
rules of an exchange be designed to prevent fraudulent and manipulative 
acts and practices, to promote just and equitable principles of trade, 
to foster cooperation and coordination with persons engaged in 
regulating, clearing, settling, processing information with respect to, 
and facilitating transactions in securities, to remove impediments to 
and perfect the mechanism of a free and open market and a national 
market system, and, in general, to protect investors and the public 
interest.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \17\ 15 U.S.C. 78f(b).
    \18\ 15 U.S.C. 78f(b)(5).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The Exchange believes the proposed amendment to Rule 11.1(a)(3) 
promotes just and equitable principles of trade by establishing uniform 
standards for order eligibility with respect to orders without a limit 
price across all trading sessions. The amendment ensures that all Users 
are subject to the same restrictions regarding the execution of 
unpriced orders. By codifying that orders without a limit price will 
not be eligible for execution outside of Regular Trading Hours in the 
Exchange's rules, Users will have confidence in how their orders will 
be handled, which promotes fairness and consistency in the execution 
process.
    Further, the Exchange believes this change removes impediments to 
and perfects the mechanism of a free and open market and protects 
investors and the public interest by clarifying that orders without a 
limit price will not be eligible for execution outside of Regular 
Trading Hours. During extended trading sessions, such as the Overnight 
Trading Session and the Post-Closing Session, market liquidity and 
trading volume are typically lower than during Regular Trading Hours. 
Under these conditions, unpriced orders may experience execution 
quality issues, such as executions at prices that vary significantly 
from the prevailing market price or from investor expectations. By 
limiting the execution of unpriced orders to Regular Trading Hours--
when liquidity is greater and price discovery is more robust--the 
amendment helps ensure that investors receive executions that are more 
consistent with their expectations and enhances the overall integrity 
of the market.
    Additionally, the Exchange believes the proposed amendment to Rule 
11.6(q)(2) removes impediments to and perfects the mechanism of a free 
and open market by eliminating potential ambiguity in the Exchange's 
rules regarding when Day Orders expire in the 23x5 trading environment. 
The amendment promotes just and equitable principles of trade and 
protects investors and the public interest by ensuring that the rule 
text clearly communicates the Exchange's order handling procedures, 
reducing the risk of confusion among market participants regarding 
order lifecycle in the Overnight Trading Session.

B. Self-Regulatory Organization's Statement on Burden on Competition

    The Exchange does not believe that the proposed rule change will 
impose any burden on intramarket competition that is not necessary or 
appropriate in furtherance of the purposes of the Act. The proposed 
amendment to Rule 11.1(a)(3) is not being made for competitive reasons, 
but rather to prohibit the execution of orders that do not contain a 
limit price in trading sessions where liquidity is limited, which was 
partially accomplished by existing rule text that prohibits the 
Exchange from accepting Market Orders prior to Regular Trading Hours. 
Additionally, the restriction on the execution of unpriced orders 
outside of Regular Trading Hours applies equally to all Users in that 
all Users will be subject to the proposed rule.
    The Exchange does not believe that the proposed rule change will 
impose any burden on intermarket competition that is not necessary or 
appropriate in furtherance of the purposes of the Act. The proposed 
amendment to Rule 11.1(a)(3) is consistent with the approach taken by 
other national securities exchanges that prohibits the execution of 
unpriced orders during extended trading sessions. Accordingly, the 
proposed rule change does not create any competitive imbalance between 
the Exchange and other trading venues.
    Further, the proposed amendment to Rule 11.6(q)(2) is a non-
substantive clarification that does not alter any operational aspect of 
order handling on the Exchange. The proposed rule change neither 
introduces new requirements for market participants nor modifies the 
competitive dynamics between the Exchange and other trading venues.

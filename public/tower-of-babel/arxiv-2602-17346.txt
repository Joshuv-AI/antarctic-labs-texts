Partial Optimality in the Preordering Problem
David Stein, Jannik Irmai, Bjoern Andres

Abstract
Preordering is a generalization of clustering and partial ordering with applications in bioinformatics and social network analysis. Given a finite set V V and a value c a ​ b ∈ ℝ c_{ab}\in\mathbb{R} for every ordered pair a ​ b ab of elements of V V , the preordering problem asks for a preorder ≲ \lesssim on V V that maximizes the sum of the values of those pairs a ​ b ab for which a ≲ b a\lesssim b . Building on the state of the art in solving this NP-hard problem partially, we contribute new partial optimality conditions and efficient algorithms for deciding these conditions. In experiments with real and synthetic data, these new conditions increase, in particular, the fraction of pairs a ​ b ab for which it is decided efficiently that a ≴ b a\not\lesssim b in an optimal preorder.

Introduction

A
preorder
is a binary relation on a set that is reflexive and transitive.
Given a finite set
V
and a value
c_{ab}\in\mathbb{R}
for every ordered pair
ab\in V^{2}
such that
a\neq b

\lesssim

\sum_{a\lesssim b,a\neq b}c_{ab}
.
See
Figure

The preordering problem has applications in bioinformatics
(
Jacob et al. 2008
;
Böcker et al. 2009
)
and social network analysis
(
Irmai et al. 2026
)
.
It is closely related to clustering and ordering:
In clustering, the feasible solutions can be identified with equivalence relaxations, i.e. symmetric preorders.
In partial ordering, the feasible solutions are anti-symmetric preorders.
A preorder need neither be symmetric nor anti-symmetric.
Yet, the symmetric subset of a preorder defines a clustering, and the anti-symmetric subset well-defines a partial order on the clusters.
See
Figure

1
for an example.
Thus, preordering is both a hybrid and a joint relaxation of clustering and partial ordering
(
Irmai et al. 2026
)
.
At the same time, the preordering problem remains NP-hard, even for values in
\{-1,1\}

(
Weller et al. 2012
)
.

Problem statement

For any set
V
, let
P_{V}=\{pq\in V^{2}\mid p\neq q\}
and
T_{V}=\{pqr\in V^{3}\mid p\neq q\wedge p\neq r\wedge q\neq r\}
denote the sets of pairs and triples of distinct elements of
V
.

Definition 1.1

(
Wakabayashi 1998
)
.

For any finite set
V
and any
c\in\mathbb{R}^{P_{V}}
, let
X_{V}
the set of all
x\in\{0,1\}^{P_{V}}
with

\displaystyle\forall pqr\in T_{V}\colon\quad x_{pq}+x_{qr}-x_{pr}\leq 1\kern 5.0pt.

(2)

Let
\varphi_{c}\colon X_{V}\to\mathbb{R}\colon x\mapsto\sum_{pq\in{P_{V}}}c_{pq}\,x_{pq}
,
and call

\displaystyle\max\ \{\varphi_{c}(x)\mid x\in X_{V}\}

(
\text{POP}_{V,c}
)

the instance of the
(maximum value) preordering problem
with respect to
V
and
c
.

Any
x\in X_{V}
is such that
x^{-1}(1)
is a transitive relation on
V
.
This relation is also irreflexive and thus a transitive digraph over the node set
V
.
Thus,

POP

V
,
c

is also called the
transitive digraph problem

(
Weller et al. 2012
, cf.)
.
Let us extend
x
and
c
such that for all
i\in V
:
x_{ii}:=1
and
c_{ii}:=0
.
This extension is a value-preserving bijection from the characteristic functions of transitive digraphs to the characteristic functions of preorders.
This isomorphism justifies the term
preordering problem
for

POP

V
,
c

.

2
Related Work

The preordering problem in the form of
Definition

1.1
is stated by
Wakabayashi 1998
who establishes NP-hardness and discusses specializations with additional constraints.
The state of the art in solving this problem partially is by the conditions, bounds and algorithms of
Böcker et al. 2009
.
Here, we prove their partial optimality conditions via improving maps
(
Shekhovtsov 2013
;
Shekhovtsov 2014
)
and establish a more general condition that subsumes these.

More specific is the transitivity editing problem that asks for the minimum number of edges to insert into or remove from a given digraph in order to make it transitive.
This problem is isomorphic to the preordering problem with values in
\{-1,1\}
and remains NP-hard
(
Weller et al. 2012
)
.
Partial optimality conditions for the transitivity editing problem are established and analyzed also by
Weller et al. 2012
, including conditions transferred from
Böcker et al. 2009
and additional conditions specific to this problem.

More restrictive than the preordering problem are the clique partition problem
(
Grötschel et al. 1984
)
and the correlation clustering problem
(
Bansal et al. 2004
)
whose feasible solutions are equivalence relations.
Partial optimality conditions for these problems established and studied by
Alush & Goldberger 2012
;
Lange et al. 2018
;
Lange et al. 2019
;
Stein et al. 2023
.
Some partial optimality conditions we define relate to theirs, and we point out these relations below.
To describe clusterings constrained by partial optimality,
Horňáková et al. 2017
introduce the notion of maximally specific partial characterizations.
We adapt their definition to preorders.

Also more restrictive than the preordering problem is the partial ordering problem
(
Müller 1996
)
for which partial optimality has not been discussed specifically, to the best of our knowledge.
More constrained still is the linear ordering problem
(
Martí & Reinelt 2011
)
for which partial optimality conditions are established by
Stein & Andres 2024
.
The conditions we define do not relate directly to theirs because the totality constraint changes the problem fundamentally.

A branch-and-cut algorithm for the preordering problem in the framework of
Gurobi Optimization, LLC 2023
is defined by
Irmai et al. 2026
.
Local search algorithms for the preordering problem are defined by
Böcker et al. 2009
;
Irmai et al. 2026
.
The geometry of the preorder polytope is studied by
Gurgel 1992
;
Gurgel & Wakabayashi 1997
.

Preliminaries

We fix some notation and recall the notion of improving maps:
For any set
V
and any
U\subseteq V
, let
\delta(U,V\setminus U):=U\times\left(V\setminus U\right)

For any digraph

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X\neq\emptyset
finite,
\varphi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
and
\sigma\colon X\to X
.
If
\varphi(\sigma(x))\geq\varphi(x)

.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
X\neq\emptyset
finite and
\varphi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
,
let
\sigma\colon X\to X
be improving for
\max_{x\in X}\varphi(x)
, and
let
X^{\prime}\subseteq X
.
If
\sigma(x)\in X^{\prime}
for all
x\in X
, there is an optimal solution
x^{*}
to
\max_{x\in X}\varphi(x)
such that
x^{*}\in X^{\prime}
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
S\neq\emptyset
finite,
X\subseteq\{0,1\}^{S}
and
\varphi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\sigma\colon X\to X
be improving for
\max_{x\in X}\varphi(x)
.
Let
s\in S
and
\beta\in\{0,1\}
.
If
\sigma(x)_{s}=\beta
for all
x\in X
, there is an optimal solution
x^{*}
to
\max_{x\in X}\varphi(x)
such that
x^{*}_{s}=\beta
.

9
Conclusion

We introduce new exact and heuristic algorithms that take as input an instance of the NP-hard preordering problem, terminate in polynomial time, and output a partial solution that is extendable to an optimal solution.
These algorithms decide partial optimality conditions that we prove, building on prior work on partially optimal preordering, clustering and ordering.
In experiments with synthetic data, we observe how the effectiveness of the algorithms transitions from fixing all variables, for easy instances, to not fixing any variables, for hard instances.
In experiments with published social networks, we observe that algorithms based on new conditions we propose are more effective in fixing variables to zero than algorithms that decide conditions we transfer from prior work, at the cost of longer but still polynomial runtime.

\displaystyle=\hat{x}^{\prime}_{pq}\kern 5.0pt,

(101)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\textnormal{if $\forall p\in U\colon\hat{x}_{pq}=1$}\\
0&\textnormal{if $\forall p\in U\colon\hat{x}_{pq}=0$}\\
*&\textnormal{otherwise}\end{cases}\kern 5.0pt,

(102)

\displaystyle\forall p\in V\setminus U\colon\quad\hat{x}^{\prime}_{pU}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\textnormal{if $\forall q\in U\colon\hat{x}_{pq}=1$}\\
0&\textnormal{if $\forall q\in U\colon\hat{x}_{pq}=0$}\\
*&\textnormal{otherwise}\end{cases}\kern 5.0pt.

\max_{x\in X_{V}[\hat{x}]}\varphi_{c}(x)=\sum_{e\in P_{U}}c_{e}+\max_{x^{\prime}\in X_{V^{\prime}}[\hat{x}^{\prime}]}\varphi_{c^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})\kern 5.0pt.

(104)

Proof.

Let
x\in X_{V}[\hat{x}]
. We define a bijection
\varphi\colon X_{V}[\hat{x}]\to X_{V^{\prime}}[\hat{x}^{\prime}]
by the following identities:

\displaystyle\forall pq\in P_{V\setminus U}

\displaystyle\colon\quad\varphi(x)_{pq}=x_{pq}

(105)

\displaystyle\forall p\in V\setminus U

\displaystyle\colon\quad\varphi(x)_{pU}=x_{pq}

\displaystyle\textnormal{for any $q\in U$}\kern 5.0pt,

(106)

\displaystyle\forall q\in V\setminus U

\displaystyle\colon\quad\varphi(x)_{Uq}=x_{pq}

\displaystyle\textnormal{for any $p\in U$}\kern 5.0pt.

(107)

(1)
\varphi
is well-defined, i.e., it follows that

for every

and
p\in V\setminus U
, and that

and
q\in V\setminus U
, by transitivity and the fact that

for all
pq\in P_{U}
.

(2) Let
x^{\prime}=\varphi(x)
. Now:

\displaystyle\sum_{pq\in P_{V}}c_{pq}x_{pq}=\sum_{pq\in P_{U}}c_{pq}x_{pq}+\sum_{p\in U}\sum_{q\in V\setminus U}c_{pq}x_{pq}

(108)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{p\in V\setminus U}\sum_{q\in U}c_{pq}x_{pq}+\sum_{pq\in P_{V\setminus U}}c_{pq}x_{pq}

(109)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{pq\in P_{U}}c_{pq}+\sum_{q\in V\setminus U}\left(\sum_{p\in U}c_{pq}\right)x_{Uq}

(110)

\displaystyle+\sum_{p\in V\setminus U}\left(\sum_{q\in U}c_{pq}\right)x_{pU}+\sum_{pq\in P_{V\setminus U}}c_{pq}x_{pq}

(111)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{pq\in P_{U}}c_{pq}+\sum_{q\in V\setminus U}c^{\prime}_{Uq}x_{Uq}

(112)

\displaystyle+\sum_{p\in V\setminus U}c^{\prime}_{pU}x_{pU}+\sum_{pq\in P_{V\setminus U}}c^{\prime}_{pq}x_{pq}

(113)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{pq\in P_{U}}c_{pq}+\sum_{pq\in P_{\left(V\setminus U\right)\cup\{U\}}}c^{\prime}_{pq}x^{\prime}_{pq}

(114)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{pq\in P_{U}}c_{pq}+\sum_{pq\in P_{V^{\prime}}}c^{\prime}_{pq}x^{\prime}_{pq}\kern 5.0pt.

(115)

This concludes the proof.

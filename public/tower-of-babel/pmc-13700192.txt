Novel self-assembling peptide gel CK2-085-SP (SPG-178) as a sealant for preventing postoperative pancreatic fistula after pancreatic resection: a study protocol for a prospective, exploratory, investigator-initiated clinical trial (PepSeal-001)

Background

Pancreatectomy is a fundamental curative surgical procedure for various pancreatic diseases and periampullary malignancies, including pancreatic cancer, distal cholangiocarcinoma, and duodenal cancer. Postoperative pancreatic fistula (POPF) remains one of the most critical and challenging complications following pancreatic resection, often leading to secondary complications, such as intra-abdominal abscess, hemorrhage, and extended hospital stays [1–4]. Despite advances in surgical techniques and perioperative management, the incidence of clinically relevant POPF remains high, ranging from 10% to > 30% [5–8]. To mitigate this risk, various methods have been developed for pancreatic stump reinforcement and sealing [9–11]. Fibrin sealants and/or polyglycolic acid (PGA) mesh have been evaluated in clinical practice to provide a physical barrier at the anastomosis or resection site [12, 13]. In addition to these materials, positive results have also been reported for other new materials, such as a polyethylene glycol-coated patch, suggesting utility of the approach for reducing POPF [14]. However, their efficacy in preventing POPF is often limited; fibrin-based materials are hypothesized to be susceptible to rapid degradation by proteolytic enzymes, such as trypsin and chymotrypsin, which are abundant in pancreatic juice [15, 16]. While this degradation has been shown in laboratory studies, its actual clinical impact in patients remains a subject of debate. Furthermore, because PGA lacks inherent tissue-adhesiveness and relies entirely on these fibrin sealants for fixation, enzymatic digestion of the fibrin layer leads to detachment of the mesh from the pancreatic parenchyma [17, 18]. This persistent clinical challenge underscores the urgent need for novel strategies to effectively prevent POPF.

CK2-085-SP is a novel hydrogel primarily composed of the self-assembling peptide SPG-178, which consists of a 13-amino-acid sequence that undergoes spontaneous gelation in aqueous solution, forming a stable nanofiber scaffold [19–22]. Unlike biological sealants, SPG-178 is not animal-derived, minimizing the risk of viral infection or immunological reactions. We recently demonstrated the potential of SPG-178 as a pancreatic sealant in a preclinical study. In vitro experiments revealed that SPG-178 maintains its mechanical integrity and hardness even when mixed with pancreatic juices, highlighting its superior resistance to proteolytic degradation compared to conventional fibrin sealants [23]. Furthermore, in a rat model of pancreatic resection, application of SPG-178 to the pancreatic stump significantly reduced the amylase levels in the ascites without inducing significant local inflammation or adverse tissue reactions. Based on these promising preclinical findings, we initiated the PepSeal-001 trial, an exploratory clinical trial evaluating the safety and preliminary efficacy of CK2-085-SP in patients undergoing pancreatic resection. This study represents the first clinical application of this self-assembling peptide gel specifically designed to prevent POPF.

Methods

Study design

This single-center, exploratory, open-label, single-arm trial will be conducted at The University of Osaka Hospital in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The study protocol was approved by the institutional review board of The University of Osaka Hospital (Certification No. 259001-A). Notably, this study is an investigator-initiated clinical trial under the oversight of the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency (PMDA) of Japan, with the Clinical Trial Notification successfully submitted and accepted prior to study commencement. The patient enrollment period is scheduled from July 1, 2025, to June 30, 2027. The expected study completion, including the 90-day postoperative follow-up for the last patient, data lock, and final statistical analysis, is anticipated to be in December 2028. The trial is registered with Japan Registry of Clinical Trials (jRCT2052250065).

Eligibility criteria

Patients must provide written informed consent to participate in the study and meet all of the following inclusion criteria to be eligible for enrollment: scheduled to undergo pancreaticoduodenectomy (PD) with pancreaticojejunostomy or distal pancreatectomy (DP), regardless of the surgical approach, including open, laparoscopic, or robot-assisted laparoscopic surgery; age ≥ 20 years at the time of providing informed consent; Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status of 0 or 1 [24]; and preserved major organ functions. Preserved organ function will be defined by the following laboratory parameters within the 28 days prior to surgery: white blood cell count between 2,000 and 12,000/mm³, neutrophil count ≥ 1,000/mm³, hemoglobin ≥ 9.0 g/dL, platelet count ≥ 75,000/mm³, total bilirubin ≤ 2.0 mg/dL, aspartate aminotransferase ≤ 150 IU/L, alanine aminotransferase ≤ 150 IU/L, and serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 mg/dL.

Patients meeting any of the following criteria will be excluded from the study: active synchronous cancers other than the target disease for pancreatectomy; acute pancreatitis; other abdominal surgeries within 4 weeks prior to enrollment or scheduled to undergo abdominal surgeries other than the pancreatectomy during the study period; participation in other clinical trials within 12 weeks prior to providing informed consent or scheduled to participate in other trials during this study; severe comorbidities, such as heart disease, pulmonary fibrosis, or interstitial pneumonia; history of severe drug hypersensitivity; women who are pregnant, breastfeeding, or possibly pregnant; requiring combined resection of other organs, excluding the stomach, bile duct, gallbladder, duodenum, and jejunum in PD, and the spleen and adrenal gland in DP, or requiring celiac axis resection and/or arterial reconstruction; receiving continuous systemic administration of corticosteroids or immunosuppressive agents; receiving somatostatin analog therapy; or taking medications for antithrombotic purposes. Any other patients deemed unsuitable for participation in the study by the investigators for medical or ethical reasons will also be excluded.

To prioritize patient safety in this first-in-human application for pancreatectomy, a stepwise enrollment approach will be employed based on the consultation with the PMDA. Initially, the first three cases will be limited to DP because DP generally carries a lower risk of life-threatening complications compared to PD. The safety data for these initial cases will be reviewed by an independent case evaluation committee. Following this review, enrollment of PD cases will proceed. All surgical procedures will be performed by eight surgeons who are highly experienced in pancreatic resection.

Device application

The study device, CK2-085-SP, is provided in a pre-filled syringe at a dosage of 1 g (1 mL) per application. Just before abdominal closure, the surgeon will apply the gel to the target site: the pancreaticojejunostomy anastomosis in PD or the pancreatic stump in DP. For open surgery, the gel will be applied and spread evenly using a finger or a cotton swab to ensure complete coverage of the target area. For laparoscopic or robot-assisted surgery, a dedicated long-nozzle applicator will be used to deliver the gel precisely to the target site. In these cases, 5.0 g (5.0 mL) of the gel is prepared to account for the 4.0 g (4.0 mL) of dead volume that remains within the applicator, ensuring that 1.0 g (1.0 mL) of the gel is effectively applied to the target site, consistent with the dosage in open surgery. Regarding surgical techniques, the procedure for pancreaticojejunostomy in PD is chosen at the surgeon’s discretion. In DP, the use of a stapler for pancreatic transection and stump closure is mandatory, although the specific type of stapler is selected at the surgeon’s discretion. To maintain the integrity of the evaluation, the concomitant use of other sealants or tissue reinforcements, such as fibrin glues or PGA meshes, at the application site will be strictly prohibited. Before abdominal closure, closed suction drains are placed. In patients undergoing PD, two drains are placed near the pancreaticojejunostomy and one near the hepaticojejunostomy. In patients undergoing DP, one drain is placed near the pancreatic stump and another in the left subphrenic space. In patients who do not develop clinically relevant POPF defined as grade B/C by the 2016 International Study Group of Pancreatic Surgery (ISGPS) [25], the drains are typically removed between postoperative days (POD) 3 and 7. Conversely, in patients diagnosed with clinically relevant POPF, the drains are ultimately removed once the patient becomes completely asymptomatic and the resolution of the corresponding fluid collection or cavity is confirmed by imaging modalities, regardless of the appearance of the drainage fluid or its amylase concentration.

Endpoints

The primary endpoint of this study is the incidence and severity of adverse events (AEs) and device malfunctions occurring within 90 days post-surgery. AEs will be graded according to the Clavien-Dindo classification, with a specific focus on events of Grade IIIa or higher [26]. Device malfunctions, defined as any failure of the study device (the gel) and/or its delivery applicator during the surgical procedure, such as clogging or blockage of the long-nozzle applicator leading to inability to inject the gel, or structural integrity and shape retention failure immediately after application, will also be recorded. In the event of serious AEs and/or device malfunction, the investigator will promptly prepare an initial report and submit it to the director of the institution, regardless of its causal relationship with the study device. The secondary endpoints are amylase levels in the drainage fluid and serum on POD 1 and 3; incidence and severity of POPF according to the 2016 ISGPS definition, with a focus on clinically relevant POPF [25]; incidence of intra-abdominal abscess, delayed gastric emptying, postoperative hemorrhage, reinsertion of drainage tubes, reoperation, readmission, and postoperative mortality; duration of drain placement; postoperative hospital stay; and usability of the study device. The usability will be evaluated by a surgeon questionnaire covering five items: ease of application, syringe plunger resistance, coating performance, local tissue integrity (presence of bleeding or damage), and shape retention.

Schedule and data management

After application of the study device, patients will be followed until POD 90 to assess the long-term safety and complications. The schedule of enrollment, interventions, and assessments is presented in Fig. 1. Data management will be performed using Fountayn EDC system (Fountayn Inc., USA; provided and supported in Japan by ClinCloud Ltd., Tokyo, Japan), a secure, web-based, electronic data capture system. Investigators or clinical research coordinators will enter all required clinical data, including patient demographics, surgical findings, and postoperative outcomes, directly into this system. To ensure data quality and compliance with the study protocol, regular monitoring and audits will be conducted in accordance with the standards of Good Clinical Practice.

Sample size and statistical considerations

The target sample size is 25 patients to ensure at least 20 evaluable cases (10 PD and 10 DP), accounting for potential dropouts. To evaluate the device efficacy in patients at high risk for POPF, the PD group must include at least seven patients with a main pancreatic duct diameter ≤ 3 mm [27–29]. This sample size was determined to be appropriate for an exploratory study focusing on safety and feasibility in a first-in-human setting for pancreatic resection.

Statistical analyses will be performed on the full analysis set and a per-protocol set. For the primary endpoint, the frequency and proportion of AEs and malfunctions will be summarized with 95% confidence intervals using the Clopper-Pearson method. Continuous variables will be presented as means ± standard deviations or medians with interquartile ranges as appropriate. Categorical variables will be summarized as frequencies and percentages.

Discussion

The PepSeal-001 trial is a prospective, single-arm, exploratory, investigator-initiated clinical trial designed to evaluate the safety and clinical feasibility of CK2-085-SP as a novel sealant for preventing POPF. Despite advances in surgical techniques and the introduction of various biological sealants, the incidence of POPF remains high, particularly in patients with a “soft pancreas” involving a narrow main pancreatic duct. The primary limitation of current biological materials, such as fibrin glues, is their inherent vulnerability to the harsh biochemical environment of the pancreas. In clinical settings, it is suspected that rapid enzymatic degradation of these materials might occur before effective tissue repair is achieved, though direct clinical evidence for this mechanism is still limited. Therefore, there is a strong clinical demand for a more protease-resistant and synthetic material that can maintain its physical barrier function during the critical early postoperative period.

CK2-085-SP, which is primarily composed of the self-assembling peptide SPG-178, offers several distinct advantages over existing biological sealants [19–22], and its purely synthetic nature offers a significant advantage in terms of product consistency and biological safety. Unlike conventional sealants derived from human or animal plasma, which inherently carry a minimal but non-zero risk of viral contamination and batch-to-batch variability, SPG-178 is manufactured through standardized chemical synthesis. This ensures a highly reproducible profile of mechanical properties and degradation rates, which is essential for achieving predictable clinical outcomes in complex pancreatic surgical procedures. Thixotropic properties—the ability to transition from a gel to a fluid state under pressure and back to a gel when the pressure is released—allow for precise application via a syringe or dedicated long-nozzle applicator. Upon application, SPG-178 rapidly re-gels, providing a stable and conforming coating. As shown in our previous preclinical study, SPG-178 maintains its structural integrity without being degraded by high concentrations of pancreatic proteases [23]. This sustained physical barrier is expected to protect the anastomosis or stump during the early postoperative period, the most vulnerable window for POPF development.

To prioritize patient safety in this first-in-human application for pancreatic resection, the PepSeal-001 trial adopts a stepwise enrollment approach. Initial enrollment is limited to three DP cases, which generally carry a lower risk of life-threatening complications than PD. Following safety confirmation by an independent case evaluation committee, the trial will proceed to PD cases. Furthermore, to robustly evaluate the feasibility of CK2-085-SP in challenging scenarios, we have set a specific target for the PD group to include at least seven patients with a main pancreatic duct diameter ≤ 3 mm, a finding suggestive of a “soft pancreas,” which carries a high risk of POPF [27–29]. By focusing on this high-risk cohort, we aim to obtain critical insights into the device’s performance where traditional sealing methods often fail, bridging the gap between preclinical success and clinical utility.

This study has some limitations. First, it is a single-center, single-arm trial with a small sample size. Although this is appropriate for an exploratory evaluation of safety, it limits the ability to draw definitive conclusions regarding clinical efficacy. Additionally, the dose of CK2-085-SP is fixed at 1.0 mL per application in this trial based on preclinical studies using porcine models. Since this first-in-human exploratory trial focuses on safety, we standardized the dose to 1.0 mL to ensure data consistency. Especially in DP, mechanical compression by staplers flattens the stump, minimizing the surface area that requires sealing. Nonetheless, to assess whether this fixed volume is sufficient across varying pancreatic dimensions, we are evaluating “coating performance” and “shape retention” in our surgeon usability questionnaire. These findings will provide essential data regarding device coverage, informing potential refinements for future clinical applications. Second, the open-label design may introduce some bias in the reporting of AEs. However, we have implemented rigorous secondary endpoints, including daily amylase levels in the drainage fluid and the incidence of grade B/C POPF as defined by the 2016 ISGPS criteria [25]. The use of these standardized, objective metrics ensures that the clinical performance of the device is evaluated with high reproducibility and comparability to international benchmarks. In addition, this study will include patients with various underlying pathologies and those who received neoadjuvant therapy. Since these factors can affect pancreatic gland texture and biology, they should be carefully considered when interpreting our results. Finally, the evaluation of these secondary outcomes will be interpreted in the context of historical data. Consequently, potential biases arising from chronological changes in perioperative management and surgical expertise over time cannot be excluded completely.

The data obtained from this exploratory study will serve as the foundation for a subsequent multicenter, prospective confirmatory trial. Notably, the design of the PepSeal-001 trial, including the choice of primary safety endpoints and the focus on high-risk PD cases, was refined through formal consultations with the PMDA. This alignment with regulatory expectations ensures that the generated safety and feasibility data will be directly applicable to the design of subsequent trials. By following this rigorous investigator-initiated clinical trial pathway, we aim to accelerate the translation of this innovative bio-nanotechnology from the laboratory to standard of care in pancreatic surgery. As this protocol has already been reviewed by the PMDA, we anticipate that the findings from the PepSeal-001 trial will provide a robust regulatory and clinical basis for advancing towards the official marketing authorization of this novel synthetic peptide sealant.

In summary, the PepSeal-001 trial represents a significant first-in-human step towards a new therapeutic strategy for pancreatic surgery. By introducing a synthetic, protease-resistant, and highly conformable sealing technology, we hope to establish a more robust clinical standard for preventing POPF, ultimately improving postoperative recovery and patient safety in pancreatic surgery.

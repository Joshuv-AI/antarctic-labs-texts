Projection-based approximations for eigenvalue problems of Fredholm integral operators with Green’s kernels
Shashank K. Shukla, Gobinda Rakshit, Akshay S. Rane

Abstract
We consider the eigenvalue problem 𝒦 ​ x = λ ​ x \mathcal{K}x=\lambda x . Our analysis focuses on the convergence rates of eigenvalue and spectral subspace approximations for compact linear integral operator 𝒦 \mathcal{K} with Green’s kernels. By employing orthogonal and interpolatory projections at 2 ​ r + 1 2r+1 collocation points (which are not necessarily Gauss points) onto an approximating space of piecewise even degree polynomials, we establish the superconvergence of eigenfunctions under iteration. The modified projection methods achieve a faster convergence rates compared to classical projection methods. The enhancement in convergence rate is verified by numerical examples.

Introduction

Let
\mathcal{X}=L^{2}[0,1]~\text{or}~C[0,1]
, and
\Omega=[0,1]\times[0,1]
.
Let
\kappa
be a real-valued continuous function on
\Omega
.
We aim to solve the eigenvalue problem of finding an eigenvector
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}
and an eigenvalue
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
such that

\mathcal{K}\varphi=\lambda\varphi,

(1)

where
\mathcal{K}:\mathcal{X}\mapsto\mathcal{X}
is a compact linear integral operator, defined by

(\mathcal{K}x)(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\kappa(s,t)x(t)\,dt,\quad s\in[0,1],\quad x\in\mathcal{X},

(2)

with
\kappa
being a specified kernel function of Green’s type.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{n}
be a sequence of continuous finite-rank operators converging pointwise to
\mathcal{K}
, and suppose the family
\{\mathcal{K}_{n}\}
is collectively compact, meaning the set

\{\mathcal{K}_{n}x:n\geq 1,\ \|x\|_{\infty}\leq 1\}

has compact closure in
\mathcal{X}
. The eigenvalue problem (
1
) is approximated by

\mathcal{K}_{n}\varphi_{n}=\lambda_{n}\varphi_{n},\quad\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C},\quad 0\neq\varphi_{n}\in\mathcal{X}.

(3)

Since
\mathcal{K}_{n}
is of finite rank, the eigenvalue problem (
3
) can be reduced to a matrix eigenvalue problem (see Atkinson
KEA0
and Ahues et al.
Ahues
). The approximate solutions
(\lambda_{n},\varphi_{n})
converge to the exact solution
(\lambda,\varphi)
under standard spectral approximation theory for compact operators.

When establishing the convergence of an approximate solution, a key consideration is often the rate at which the approximate solution converges to the exact solution.
Several important methods have been developed for computing approximate solutions, including Nyström methods, where the integral in (
2
) is replaced by a convergent quadrature formula, and projection-based approaches such as Galerkin and collocation methods, along with their variants. The analysis of approximate solutions for eigenvalue problems with smooth kernels has been widely studied in the literature
Ahues
;
KEA0
;
Baker0
;
Chatelin
;
Sloan0
. For kernels of the Green’s function-type, which often lack differentiability along the diagonal, early investigations were conducted in Chatelin–Lebbar
Cha-Leb2
, where interpolatory projections used Gauss points (zeros of Legendre polynomials) as collocation nodes.

The improvement of spectral subspace approximations in projection methods through iterative techniques was first introduced by Sloan
Sloan1
. In Kulkarni
RPK5
;
RPK6
, a modified projection method was proposed for solving (
1
) using sequences of projections. It was shown that for sufficiently smooth kernels, the modified projection method yields faster convergence of eigenelements compared to the iterated projection method. Furthermore, convergence of eigenvectors can be significantly enhanced using the iterated modified projection method, obtained from a one-step iteration of the modified projection approach. Other notable approaches include the degenerate kernel method in
Gnan1
, which employs kernel interpolation in both variables and provides error bounds for eigenvalues and spectral subspaces, while also demonstrating improved convergence through eigenfunction iteration. Nyström and degenerate kernel methods using projections at Gauss points onto spaces of piecewise polynomials are known to achieve high-order convergence. Moreover, in
CA-PS-DS-MT
, superconvergent variants of these methods were introduced, further enhancing the convergence of eigenvalues and spectral subspaces for sufficiently smooth kernels, with additional gains in eigenvector convergence through iteration. In all these cases, the use of piecewise polynomial basis functions reduces the problem to finite-dimensional matrix eigenvalue computations.

Consider a uniform partition of
[0,1]
given by
\{0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=1\}
, where
t_{j}=\dfrac{j}{n}
for
j=0,1,2,\ldots,n
, and let
h=\dfrac{1}{n}
. For
r\geq 0
, let
\mathcal{X}_{n}
denote the space of piecewise polynomials of even degree
\leq 2r
associated with this partition.

In this paper, we establish convergence rates of eigenvalue and spectral subspace approximations for the eigenvalue problem
\mathcal{K}\varphi=\lambda\varphi
using both orthogonal projections and interpolatory projections at
2r+1
collocation points. In contrast to earlier works such as
RPK5
;
RPK6
, which mainly consider smooth kernels together with piecewise discontinuous approximating spaces, we focus on integral operators with Green’s function-type kernels. Moreover, instead of employing Gauss points, we adopt a simpler setup with
2r+1
equidistant points in each subinterval
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
,
j=1,2,\ldots,n
. Despite this choice, our analysis show that iterations of both classical collocation/Galerkin and modified projection approximations of the eigenfunctions still yield superconvergence. Furthermore, the modified projection method achieves faster convergence than classical projection methods for eigenvalue approximations. These findings highlight the flexibility and effectiveness of our approach, demonstrating that high-order convergence can be attained without relying on special-function-based collocation nodes.

The structure of the paper is as follows. Section
2
introduces the definition of Green’s function-type kernels and spectral projections, along with key results and fixed notations used throughout the paper. Section
3
defines both orthogonal and interpolatory projections and provides relevant theoretical insights. In the final subsection of this section, we present projection methods and their variants tailored for eigenvalue problems. Section
4
details our main results on the orders of convergence for the Galerkin and collocation methods, including their modified versions. Numerical illustration is provided in Section
5
. The paper concludes with Section
6
, summarizing the findings and implications of our study.

Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{X}=L^{2}[0,1]~\text{or}~C[0,1]
. Consider the subsets of
\Omega
as

\Omega_{1}=\{(s,t)\mid 0\leq t\leq s\leq 1\},\quad\Omega_{2}=\{(s,t)\mid 0\leq s\leq t\leq 1\}.

Suppose there exist functions
\kappa_{i}\in C^{\alpha}(\Omega_{i})
,

, such that the kernel
\kappa
can be written as

\kappa(s,t)=\begin{cases}\kappa_{1}(s,t),&(s,t)\in\Omega_{1},\\[2.84526pt]
\kappa_{2}(s,t),&(s,t)\in\Omega_{2},\end{cases}

and
\kappa_{1}(s,s)=\kappa_{2}(s,s),~\text{for all }s\in[0,1].

For

, we assume that the smoothness parameter
\alpha
satisfies
\alpha>r
. A kernel
\kappa
satisfying these properties is referred to as a Green’s kernel. Such kernels are generally non-smooth, as their regularity is restricted to the separate domains
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
, with only continuity across the diagonal

.

Now, we will consider some basic definitions and stadard results on spectral theory:

Let
\mathcal{X}=L^{2}[0,1]~\text{or}~C[0,1]

\mathcal{X}

\rho(\mathcal{K})=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:(\mathcal{K}-zI)^{-1}\in\mathcal{BL}(\mathcal{X})\}

\mathcal{K}
is
\sigma(\mathcal{K})=\mathbb{C}\setminus\rho(\mathcal{K})
. The point spectrum of
\mathcal{K}
contains all
\lambda\in\sigma(\mathcal{K})

\mathcal{K}-\lambda I
is not injective. In such cases,
\lambda
is referred as an eigenvalue of
\mathcal{K}
. Let
\Gamma
be a rectifiable, simple, closed, positively oriented curve contained in
\rho(\mathcal{K})
that separates
\lambda
from the remainder of the spectrum of
\mathcal{K}
. The spectral projection corresponding to
\mathcal{K}
and
\lambda
is then given by:

E=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{K}-zI)^{-1}\,dz\in\mathcal{BL}(\mathcal{X}),

and the dimension of
\mathcal{R}(E)

E
, is referred as the algebraic multiplicity of
\lambda
. If
\lambda
has finite algebraic multiplicity, it is classified as a spectral value of finite type. In this case,
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
\mathcal{K}
. ( See Ahues et al
(
Ahues

.) Furthermore, if the algebraic multiplicity of
\lambda
is equal to one,
\lambda
is called a simple eigenvalue of
\mathcal{K}
. For any nonzero subspaces
Y
and
Z
of
\mathcal{X}
, define

\delta(Y,Z)=\sup\{d(y,Z):y\in Y,\left\lVert y\right\rVert_{\infty}=1\},

where
d(y,Z)=\inf_{z\in Z}\left\lVert y-z\right\rVert
. The gap between
Y
and
Z
is then given by

\hat{\delta}(Y,Z)=\max\{\delta(Y,Z),\delta(Z,Y)\}.

Consider a sequence of operators
\{\mathcal{K}_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{BL}(\mathcal{X})
that converges to
\mathcal{K}
in a collectively compact manner. The following results are taken from Osborn
Osborn

For sufficiently large
n
,
\Gamma\subset\rho(\mathcal{K}_{n})
and

\max\{\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}_{n}-zI)^{-1}\right\rVert:z\in\Gamma\}<\infty

the spectral projection corresponding to
\mathcal{K}_{n}
, given by

E_{n}=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{K}_{n}-zI)^{-1}\,dz,

has rank
m
as suppose the spectrum of
\mathcal{K}_{n}
consists of
m
eigenvalues
\lambda_{n,1},\lambda_{n,2},\ldots,\lambda_{n,m}
, counted according to their algebraic multiplicities. Define the arithmetic mean of these eigenvalues as

\hat{\lambda}_{n}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{n,j}.

Consider the following result from Osborn
Osborn
:

Theorem 2.1
.

For sufficiently large
n
,

\hat{\delta}\left(\mathcal{R}(E),\mathcal{R}(E_{n})\right)\leq C\left\lVert\left(\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{n}\right)\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert,

where
C
denotes a generic constant independent of
n
and
\left(\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{n}\right)\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}
represents the restriction of
(\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{n})\mathcal{K}
to
\mathcal{R}(E)
.

Also, we consider a modified result of Osborn
(
Osborn

given in Kulkarni
(
RPK6

Theorem 2.2
.

For sufficiently large
n

|\lambda-\hat{\lambda}_{n}|\leq C\left\lVert\mathcal{K}_{n}(\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{n})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert,

where
C

The above two theorems establish the essential error bounds for the eigenelements.

3
Approximations based on projections

Denote the uniform partition of
[0,1]
as

\Delta^{(n)}:=\{0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=1\},\quad\text{where}~t_{j}=\displaystyle{\frac{j}{n}},\quad j=0,1,2,\ldots,n.

Denote
\Delta_{j}=[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
. Let
r
be a non-negative integer, and define

\mathcal{X}_{n}=\left\{x\in L^{\infty}[0,1]:x|_{\Delta_{j}}\text{ is a polynomial of even degree}\leq 2r,\;j=1,2,\ldots,n\right\}.

This is a finite dimensional space with
\dim(\mathcal{X}_{n})=n(2r+1)
. Since continuity at the partition points is not imposed, the approximation space
\mathcal{X}_{n}
is a subspace of
L^{\infty}[0,1]
. We consider two projection operators mapping into
\mathcal{X}_{n}
: the orthogonal projection
P_{n}
, obtained as the restriction to
L^{\infty}[0,1]
of the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{X}_{n}
, and the interpolatory projection
Q_{n}:\mathcal{C}[0,1]\mapsto\mathcal{X}_{n}
, defined by interpolation at
2r+1
points on each subinterval. The projections are discussed in the following subsections:

3.1
Orthogonal projection

Let
\mathcal{X}=L^{2}[0,1]
denote the space of real-valued, square-integrable functions defined on the interval
[0,1]
. The inner product in
L^{2}[0,1]
, denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, is defined as

\langle x,y\rangle=\int_{0}^{1}x(t)y(t)\,dt,\quad\forall x,y\in L^{2}[0,1].

For
\eta=0,1,\ldots,2r
, let
L_{\eta}
represent the Legendre polynomial of degree
\eta
defined on the interval
[-1,1]
. For each
j=1,\ldots,n
and
\eta=0,1,\ldots,2r
, we define the function
\psi_{j,\eta}(t)
as follows:

\psi_{j,\eta}(t)=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\frac{2}{h}}L_{\eta}\left(\frac{2t-t_{j}-t_{j-1}}{h}\right),&t\in(t_{j-1},t_{j}],\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

At the point
t_{0}
, we set
\psi_{1,\eta}(t_{0})=\sqrt{\frac{2}{h}}L_{\eta}(-1)
. The supremum norm of
\psi_{j,\eta}
is given by

\left\lVert\psi_{j,\eta}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\max_{t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}|\psi_{j,\eta}(t)|=\sqrt{\frac{2}{h}}\left\lVert L_{\eta}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

For functions
f,g\in C(\Delta_{j})
, we define the inner product on the interval
\Delta_{j}
as

\langle f,g\rangle_{\Delta_{j}}=\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}f(t)g(t)\,dt.

This inner product is symmetric, i.e.,
\langle f,g\rangle_{\Delta_{j}}=\langle g,f\rangle_{\Delta_{j}}
. The supremum norm of
f
on
\Delta_{j}
is defined as

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Delta_{j},\infty}=\max_{t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}|f(t)|.

Using this, we can bound the inner product as

\langle f,g\rangle_{\Delta_{j}}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Delta_{j},\infty}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\Delta_{j},\infty}h.

It is easy to see that the set
\{\psi_{j,\eta}:j=1,\ldots,n,\eta=0,\ldots,2r\}
forms an orthonormal basis for the space
\mathcal{X}_{n}
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{2r,\Delta_{j}}
denote the space of polynomials of degree at most
2r
on
\Delta_{j}
. The orthogonal projection
P_{n,j}:C(\Delta_{j})\mapsto\mathcal{P}_{2r,\Delta_{j}}
is defined by

P_{n,j}x=\sum_{\eta=0}^{2r}\langle x,\psi_{j,\eta}\rangle_{\Delta_{j}}\psi_{j,\eta}.

This projection satisfies the following properties:

\langle P_{n,j}x,y\rangle_{\Delta_{j}}=\langle x,P_{n,j}y\rangle_{\Delta_{j}},\quad P_{n,j}^{2}=P_{n,j},\quad\text{and}\quad P_{n,j}P_{n,i}=0\quad\text{for }i\neq j.

Additionally, we have the bound as

\left\lVert P_{n,j}x\right\rVert_{\Delta_{j},\infty}\leq\sum_{\eta=0}^{2r}\langle x,\psi_{j,\eta}\rangle_{\Delta_{j}}\|\psi_{j,\eta}\|_{\Delta_{j},\infty}\leq 2\left(\sum_{\eta=0}^{2r}\left\lVert L_{\eta}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\right)\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}.

Thus, an orthogonal projection
P_{n}:C[0,1]\mapsto\mathcal{X}_{n}
is defined as

P_{n}x=\sum_{j=1}^{n}P_{n,j}x.

(7)

By applying the Hahn-Banach extension theorem, following the approach in Atkinson et al.
KEA-IG-IS
, the projection
P_{n}
can be extended to the space
L^{\infty}[0,1]
. This extended projection satisfies
P_{n}^{2}=P_{n}
and

\left\lVert P_{n}\right\rVert\leq 2\sum_{\eta=0}^{2r}\left\lVert L_{\eta}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}=C_{2}.

(8)

Finally, if
x\in C^{2r+1}(\Delta_{j})
, we have the standard estimate from Chatelin-Lebbar
Cha-Leb2
as

\left\lVert(I-P_{n,j})x\right\rVert_{\Delta_{j},\infty}\leq C_{3}\left\lVert x^{(2r+1)}\right\rVert_{\Delta_{j},\infty}h^{2r+1},

(9)

where
C_{3}
is a constant independent of
h
. Moreover, if
x\in C^{\beta}[0,1]
with
\beta=\min\{\alpha,\,2r+1\}
, then one can see that

\left\lVert(I-P_{n})x\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{3}\left\lVert x^{(\beta)}\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{\beta}.

(10)

3.2
Interpolatory projection

Let
\mathcal{X}=C[0,1]
be the space of continuous real valued function on the interval
[0,1]
, equipped with the supremum norm defined as
\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}=\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left|x(t)\right|
. In each sub-interval
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
, consider
2r+1
equally spaced interpolation points as

\tau_{j}^{i}=t_{j-1}+\frac{ih}{2r},\quad i=0,1,\ldots,2r\;\text{ for }\;r\geq 1,\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{j}=\frac{t_{j-1}+t_{j}}{2}\;\text{ for }\;r=0.

Note that these interpolation nodes are equidistant in each subinterval. The interpolatory operator
Q_{n}:\mathcal{X}\mapsto\mathcal{X}_{n}
is defined as

Q_{n}x(\tau_{j}^{i})=x(\tau_{j}^{i}),\quad i=0,1,2,\ldots,2r;~j=1,2,\ldots,n.

(11)

Note that
Q_{n}x\to x~\text{for all}\;x\in C[0,1]
. By employing the Hahn-Banach extension theorem,
Q_{n}
can be extended to
L^{\infty}[0,1]
, making
Q_{n}:L^{\infty}[0,1]\mapsto\mathcal{X}_{n}
a projection. Define the polynomial

\displaystyle\Psi_{j}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{2r}\left(t-\tau_{j}^{i}\right),\quad j=1,2,\ldots,n.

As
Q_{n,j}x
(where
Q_{n,j}
is
Q_{n}
restricted on
\Delta_{j}
) interpolates
x
at the points
\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r}
, we have

\displaystyle x(t)-Q_{n,j}x(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{j}(t)\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]x,\quad t\in\Delta_{j},

where
\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]x
is the
(2r+1)^{th}
divided difference of
x\in C[0,1]
at
\tau_{j}^{0},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r}
defined as

\displaystyle\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r}\right]x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left[\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r}\right]x-\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r-1}\right]x}{\tau_{j}^{2r}-\tau_{j}^{0}}.

If
x\in C^{2r+1}(\Delta_{j})
, a standard result (see
KEA-IG-IS
) implies that

\left\lVert(I-Q_{n,j})x\right\rVert_{\Delta_{j},\infty}\leq C_{4}\left\lVert x^{(2r+1)}\right\rVert_{\Delta_{j},\infty}h^{2r+1},

where
C_{4}
is a constant independent of
h
. Indeed, if
x\in C^{\beta}[0,1]
with
\beta=\min\{\alpha,\,2r+1\}
, then

\left\lVert(I-Q_{n})x\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{4}\left\lVert x^{(\beta)}\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{\beta}.

(12)

3.3
Estimates in the methods of approximation

The classical projection method for approximating the eigenvalue problem (
1
) is obtained by replacing
\mathcal{K}_{n}
by
\pi_{n}\mathcal{K}
in (
3
) as

\pi_{n}\mathcal{K}\varphi_{n}=\lambda_{n}\varphi_{n},~~\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\},~\varphi_{n}\in\mathcal{X}_{n},~\left\lVert\varphi_{n}\right\rVert_{\infty}=1.

(13)

One step iteration refine the eigenvector, proposed by Sloan
Sloan1
, is given by

\varphi_{n}^{S}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\mathcal{K}\varphi_{n},

(14)

which satisfies the condition

\mathcal{K}\pi_{n}\varphi_{n}^{S}=\lambda_{n}\varphi_{n}^{S}.

In the modified projection method introduced by Kulkarni
RPK5
, the operator
\mathcal{K}_{n}
is replaced with a finite-rank approximation, defined as

\mathcal{K}_{n}^{M}=\pi_{n}\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{K}\pi_{n}-\pi_{n}\mathcal{K}\pi_{n}.

(15)

The approximation (
3
) now requires finding
\lambda_{n}^{M}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and
\varphi_{n}^{M}\in\mathcal{X}
such that
\left\lVert\varphi_{n}^{M}\right\rVert_{\infty}=1
and

\mathcal{K}_{n}^{M}\varphi_{n}^{M}=\lambda_{n}^{M}\varphi_{n}^{M}.

(16)

The corresponding iterated eigenvector for the modified method is defined as

\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}^{M}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{M}}\mathcal{K}\varphi_{n}^{M}.

(17)

Let
\{\mathcal{K}_{n}\}
be a sequence of bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{X}
converging to
\mathcal{K}
in the collectively compact sense. We consider the
standard and modified projection-based approximations defined by

\mathcal{K}_{n}=\pi_{n}\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{K}_{n}=\mathcal{K}_{n}^{M}
, respectively.
Let
\lambda
be a nonzero eigenvalue of
\mathcal{K}
with algebraic multiplicity
m
,
and let
\hat{\lambda}_{n}
and
\hat{\lambda}_{n}^{M}
denote the arithmetic means of the

m
eigenvalues of
\pi_{n}\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{K}_{n}^{M}
, respectively, lying inside
a simple closed contour
\Gamma
that encloses
\lambda
. Let
E
,
E_{n}
, and
E_{n}^{M}
denote the corresponding spectral projections associated with
\Gamma
. The associated spectral subspaces are denoted by
\mathcal{R}(E)
,
\mathcal{R}(E_{n})
, and

\mathcal{R}(E_{n}^{M})
. Then, the following results can be obtained from Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
as given in Bouda et al.
HB-CA-ZEA-KK
:

For any
\varphi_{n}\in\mathcal{R}(E_{n})
, define
\varphi_{n}^{S}=\mathcal{K}\varphi_{n}
. Then, for all
sufficiently large
n
,

\left\lVert\varphi_{n}-E\varphi_{n}\right\rVert\leq\hat{\delta}\big(\mathcal{R}(E),\mathcal{R}(E_{n})\big)\leq C\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}-\pi_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert,

(18)

\left\lVert\varphi_{n}^{S}-E\varphi_{n}^{S}\right\rVert\leq\delta\big(\mathcal{R}(E),\mathcal{K}\mathcal{R}(E_{n})\big)\leq C\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}-\pi_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert,

(19)

and

|\lambda-\hat{\lambda}_{n}|\leq C\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}-\pi_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert.

(20)

Also, for any
\varphi_{n}^{M}\in\mathcal{R}(E_{n}^{M})
, define
\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}^{M}=\mathcal{K}\varphi_{n}^{M}
. Then, for all sufficiently large
n
,

\left\lVert\varphi_{n}^{M}-E\varphi_{n}^{M}\right\rVert\leq\hat{\delta}\big(\mathcal{R}(E),\mathcal{R}(E_{n}^{M})\big)\leq C\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{n}^{M})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert,

(21)

\left\lVert\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}^{M}-E\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}^{M}\right\rVert\leq\delta\big(\mathcal{R}(E),\mathcal{K}\mathcal{R}(E_{n}^{M})\big)\leq C\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{n}^{M})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert,

(22)

and

|\lambda-\hat{\lambda}_{n}^{M}|\leq C\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{n}^{M})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert.

(23)

4
Convergence orders in the approximations

In this section, we focus on the case where
\lambda
is a simple eigenvalue of
\mathcal{K}
.
For the projection methods considered earlier, we take
\pi_{n}=P_{n}
for convenience,
in which case the scheme reduces to the Galerkin method and its variants.
Similarly, setting
\pi_{n}=Q_{n}
yields the collocation method and its variants.
Accordingly, we establish our main results for both the Galerkin and collocation methods..

4.1
Orthogonal projection based approximations

In this subsection, we present essential findings that will be instrumental in determining approximate eigenelements through orthogonal projection.

Proposition 4.1
.

For
r\geq 1
, if
x\in C^{2r+1}[0,1]
, then

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})x\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{5}\left\lVert x^{(2r+1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{2r+3},

where
C_{5}
is a constant independent of
h
. Also,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})x\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{6}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{2},

for some constant
C_{6}
independent of
h
.

Proof.

First we prove the case
r\geq 1
.
Let
s\in[0,1]
and define
\kappa_{s}(t)=\kappa(s,t)
. Then, consider

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})x(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\kappa_{s}(t)(I-P_{n})x(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\kappa_{s}(t)(I-P_{n,j})x(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\kappa_{s},(I-P_{n,j})x\rangle_{\Delta_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle(I-P_{n,j})\kappa_{s},(I-P_{n,j})x\rangle_{\Delta_{j}}.

(24)

We analyze the two cases separately:

Case I: Let
s\in(t_{i-1},t_{i})
, for
i=1,2,\ldots,n
. Thus, the above expression can be rewritten as

\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})x(s)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\langle(I-P_{n,j})\kappa_{s},(I-P_{n,j})x\rangle_{\Delta_{j}}+\langle(I-P_{n,i})\kappa_{s},(I-P_{n,i})x\rangle_{\Delta_{i}}.

(25)

For
j\neq i
, we note that both
\kappa_{s},x\in C^{2r+1}(\Delta_{j})
. Thus, applying the estimate (
9
), we have

\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\langle(I-P_{n,j})\kappa_{s},(I-P_{n,j})x\rangle_{\Delta_{j}}\bigg|\leq(C_{3})^{2}C_{1}\left\lVert x^{(2r+1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}(n-1)h^{4r+3}.

(26)

Now, for the case
j=i
, observe that
\kappa_{s}
is only continuous on
\Delta_{i}
. We define a constant function as

\gamma_{i}(t)=\kappa_{s}(s),\quad t\in\Delta_{i}.

Then, we rewrite the term as

\langle(I-P_{n,i})\kappa_{s},(I-P_{n,i})x\rangle_{\Delta_{i}}=\langle\kappa_{s}-\gamma_{i},(I-P_{n,i})x\rangle_{\Delta_{i}}.

For
t\in[t_{i-1},t_{i}]
, using the mean value theorem, we have

\kappa_{s}(t)-\gamma_{i}(t)=\begin{cases}D^{(0,1)}\kappa_{1}(s,\nu_{1})(t-s),&\nu_{1}\in(t,s),\\
D^{(0,1)}\kappa_{2}(s,\nu_{2})(t-s),&\nu_{2}\in(s,t).\end{cases}

Further using the bound (
9
), we get

|\langle(I-P_{n,i})\kappa_{s},(I-P_{n,i})x\rangle_{\Delta_{i}}|\leq C_{3}C_{1}\left\lVert x^{(2r+1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{2r+3}.

(27)

Combining the inequalities (
25
), (
26
) and (
27
), we conclude

|\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})x(s)|\leq(C_{3})^{2}C_{1}\left\lVert x^{(2r+1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{2r+3}.

(28)

Case II: Whenever
s=t_{i}
for some
i=0,1,\ldots,n
. Since
\kappa_{s}\in C^{2r+1}(\Delta_{j})
for each
j=1,\ldots,n
and given that
x\in C^{2r+1}[0,1]
, we use inequality (
9
) to obtain

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq i\leq n}|\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})x(t_{i})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\lVert(I-P_{n,j})\kappa_{s}\right\rVert_{\Delta_{j},\infty}\left\lVert(I-P_{n,j})x\right\rVert_{\Delta_{j},\infty}h

\displaystyle\leq(C_{3})^{2}C_{1}\left\lVert x^{(2r+1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{4r+2}.

Finally, combining this estimate with (
28
), the desired result follow as

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})x\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{5}\left\lVert x^{(2r+1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{2r+3},

where
C_{5}=(C_{3})^{2}C_{1}
is a constant independent of
h
.

To establish the other bound of the proposition (for
r=0
), let us consider
x\in C[0,1]
. Suppose
s=t_{i}
for some index
i
. Then,

\left|\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})x(s)\right|\leq C_{1}C_{3}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{2r+1}.

(29)

Now, for
s\in(t_{i-1},t_{i})
, we express

\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})x(s)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\langle(I-P_{n,j})\kappa_{s},x\rangle_{\Delta_{j}}+\langle(I-P_{n,i})(\kappa_{s}-\gamma_{i}),x\rangle_{\Delta_{i}},

which leads to the estimate

\left|\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})x(s)\right|\leq(1+C_{2}+C_{3})C_{1}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{2}.

By combining (
29
) with the above result, we arrive at

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})x\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{6}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{2},

where
C_{6}=(1+C_{2}+C_{3})C_{1}
is a constant. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be the linear integral operator defined by (
2
) with a Green’s function-type kernel. Let
P_{n}
be the orthogonal projection onto the approximating space
\mathcal{X}_{n}
defined by (
7
). Then

\displaystyle\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}-P_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert=\begin{cases}O\left(h^{2r+1}\right)&\text{for }r\geq 1,\\
O\left(h\right)&\text{for }r=0,\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}-P_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert=\begin{cases}O\left(h^{2r+3}\right)&\text{for }r\geq 1,\\
O\left(h^{2}\right)&\text{for }r=0.\end{cases}

Proof.

Note that

\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}-P_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{R}(E)}\left\{\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}-P_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}:\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=1\right\}.

For
\varphi\in\mathcal{R}(E)
, assume that
y=\mathcal{K}\varphi
and so
y\in\mathcal{R}(E)
. For
r\geq 1
, let us consider
y\in C^{2r+1}[0,1]
so that
\mathcal{K}y\in C^{2r+1}[0,1]
and therefore using (
10
), we have

\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}-P_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\lVert(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}y\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{3}\left\lVert\left(\mathcal{K}y\right)^{(2r+1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{2r+1}.

From inequality (
5
), it follows that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(\mathcal{K}y\right)^{(2r+1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(\left\lVert y\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert y^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\ldots+\left\lVert y^{(2r-1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle=C_{1}\left(\left\lVert\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}\varphi)^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\ldots+\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}\varphi)^{(2r-1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}\right).

By utilizing inequalities (
4
) and (
5
), we conclude that the quantity
\left\lVert\left(\mathcal{K}y\right)^{(2r+1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}
is independent of
h
. Therefore, we obtain

\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}-P_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2r+1}\right),

which leads to

\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}-P_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert=O\left(h^{2r+1}\right).

When
r=0
, it is easy to see that

\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}-P_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert=O\left(h\right).

Now, consider the second bound of the lemma as

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}-P_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{R}(E)}\left\{\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}-P_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}:\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=1\right\}.

Assume that
\varphi\in C^{2r+1}[0,1]
, which implies
y\in C^{2r+1}[0,1]
and so
\mathcal{K}y\in C^{2r+1}[0,1]
. Using Proposition
4.1
for
r\geq 1
, we have

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}-P_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}y\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{5}\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}y)^{(2r+1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{2r+3}.

As discussed earlier, the quantity
\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}y)^{(2r+1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}
is independent of
h
and therefore

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}-P_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2r+3}\right),

which implies that

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}-P_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert=O\left(h^{2r+3}\right).

When
r=0
, using the Proposition
4.1
, we have

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}y\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{6}\left\lVert\mathcal{K}y\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{2}.

From estimate (
4
), we see that

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}y\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{1}\left\lVert y\right\rVert_{\infty}=C_{1}\left\lVert\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq(C_{1})^{2}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}.

Therefore, we get

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}-P_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert=O\left(h^{2}\right),

which completes the proof.

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be the linear integral operator defined by (
2
) with a Green’s function-type kernel. Let
P_{n}
be the orthogonal projection onto the approximating space
\mathcal{X}_{n}
defined by (
7
). Then

\displaystyle\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{n}^{M})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert=\begin{cases}O\left(h^{2r+3}\right)&\text{for }r\geq 1,\\
O\left(h^{3}\right)&\text{for }r=0,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{n}^{M})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert=\begin{cases}O\left(h^{2r+5}\right)&\text{for }r\geq 1,\\
O\left(h^{4}\right)&\text{for }r=0,\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{K}_{n}^{M}=P_{n}\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{K}P_{n}-P_{n}\mathcal{K}P_{n}.

Proof.

For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{R}(E)
we have
\mathcal{K}\varphi\in\mathcal{R}(E)
. Then

\displaystyle\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{n}^{M})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{R}(E)}\left\{\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{n}^{M})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}:\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=1\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{R}(E)}\left\{\left\lVert(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}:\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=1\right\}.

For
r\geq 1
, we write

\left\lVert(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\left\lVert I-P_{n}\right\rVert\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}.

From (
8
), it follows that
\left\lVert I-P_{n}\right\rVert\leq 1+C_{2}
. Let
\varphi\in C^{2r+1}[0,1]
, which implies
\mathcal{K}\varphi\in C^{2r+1}[0,1]
. Moreover, applying Proposition
4.1
, we have

\left\lVert(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq(1+C_{2})C_{5}\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}\varphi)^{(2r+1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{2r+3}.

Following inequality (
5
), we observe that the bound
\left\lVert\left(\mathcal{K}\varphi\right)^{(2r+1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}
does not depend on
h
. Therefore,

\left\lVert(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2r+3}\right).

(30)

In the case of
r=0
, we use (
10
) to obtain

\left\lVert(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{3}\left\lVert\left(\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})y\right)^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}h.

(31)

Now, define

\ell_{s}(t)=\ell(s,t)=\begin{cases}D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{1}(s,t),&s,t\in\Omega_{1},\\
D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{2}(s,t),&s,t\in\Omega_{2}.\end{cases}

Consider

\displaystyle(\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})y)^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\ell_{s}(t)(I-P_{n})y(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\ell_{s}(t)(I-P_{n,j})y(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle(I-P_{n,j})\ell_{s},(I-P_{n,j})y\rangle_{\Delta_{j}}.

Let
s\in[t_{i-1},t_{i}]
, for
i=1,2,\ldots,n
. Then, we can write

(\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})y)^{\prime}(s)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\langle(I-P_{n,j})\ell_{s},(I-P_{n,j})y\rangle_{\Delta_{j}}+\langle(I-P_{n,i})\ell_{s},(I-P_{n,i})y\rangle_{\Delta_{i}}.

(32)

For
j\neq i
, we observe that both
\ell_{s}\in C^{2r+1}(\Delta_{j})
and
x\in C^{2r+1}(\Delta_{j})
. Hence, utilizing the estimate (
9
), we obtain

\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\langle(I-P_{n,j})\ell_{s},(I-P_{n,j})y\rangle_{\Delta_{j}}\bigg|\leq(C_{3})^{2}\left\lVert\ell_{s}\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert y\right\rVert_{\infty}(n-1)h^{3}\leq(C_{3}C_{1})^{2}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}(n-1)h^{3},

(33)

where
\left\lVert\ell_{s}\right\rVert_{\infty}=C_{1}
by definition. Also, whenever
j=i
,
\ell_{s}
is continuous on
[t_{i-1},t_{i}]
, for

. Thus, following the proof similar to Proposition
4.1
, we get

|\langle(I-P_{n,i})\ell_{s},(I-P_{n,i})y\rangle_{\Delta_{i}}|=O\left(h^{2}\right).

Applying the above estimate along with inequality (
33
) in (
32
), we have

\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})y)^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2}\right)

Using above estimate in (
31
), we obtain

\left\lVert(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{3}\right).

The above estimate and (
30
) establish the first bound of the lemma.

Now, consider the other bound of lemma as

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{n}^{M})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{R}(E)}\left\{\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}:\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=1\right\}.

We can write it as follows

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})\right\rVert\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}.

For any
x\in C[0,1]
, consider

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})\right\rVert=\sup_{x\in C[0,1]}\left\{\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})x\right\rVert_{\infty}:\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}=1\right\}.

Using Proposition
4.1
, we obtain

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})\right\rVert=O\left(h^{2}\right).

For
r\geq 1
, let

, it follows that

and using Proposition

Combining the above two estimates, we obtain

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2r+5}\right).

(34)

, we obtain

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2}\right).

Therefore,

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{4}\right).

The above estimate along with (
34
) establish the desired second bound of the lemma, thereby completing the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\lambda
be a simple eigenvalue of
\mathcal{K}
, and let
\lambda_{n}^{G}
denote the corresponding Galerkin eigenvalue of
P_{n}\mathcal{K}
. Let
\varphi_{n}\in\mathcal{R}(E_{n})
be a Galerkin eigenvector, and let
\varphi_{n}^{S}
denote the associated iterated Galerkin eigenvector.
Then, for sufficiently large
n
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\varphi_{n}-E\varphi_{n}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\begin{cases}O\left(h^{2r+1}\right)&\text{for }r\geq 1,\\
O\left(h\right)&\text{for }r=0,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\left\lVert\varphi_{n}^{S}-E\varphi_{n}^{S}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\begin{cases}O\left(h^{2r+3}\right)&\text{for }r\geq 1,\\
O\left(h^{2}\right)&\text{for }r=0,\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle|\lambda-\lambda_{n}^{G}|=\begin{cases}O\left(h^{2r+3}\right)&\text{for }r\geq 1,\\
O\left(h^{2}\right)&\text{for }r=0.\end{cases}

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be the linear integral operator defined by (
2
) with a Green’s function-type kernel. Let
Q_{n}
be the interpolatory operator onto the approximating space
\mathcal{X}_{n}
defined by (
11
). Then for
r\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}-Q_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert=O\left(h^{2r+1}\right),\quad\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}-Q_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert=O\left(h^{2r+2}\right).

Proof.

The space
\mathcal{R}(E)
is invariant by
\mathcal{K}
, for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{R}(E)
, we assume
y=\mathcal{K}\varphi
and so
y\in\mathcal{R}(E)
. Consider

\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}-Q_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{R}(E)}\left\{\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}-Q_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}:\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=1\right\}.

Further,

\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}-Q_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\lVert(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}y\right\rVert_{\infty}.

Let
\varphi\in C^{2r+1}[0,1]
, which implies
y\in C^{2r+1}[0,1]
and so
\mathcal{K}y\in C^{2r+1}[0,1]
. Therefore, using the estimate (
12
), we have

\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}-Q_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{4}\left\lVert\left(\mathcal{K}y\right)^{(2r+1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{2r+1}.

From inequality (
5
), we get

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(\mathcal{K}y\right)^{(2r+1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(\left\lVert y\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert y^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\ldots+\left\lVert y^{(2r-1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle=C_{1}\left(\left\lVert\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}\varphi)^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\ldots+\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}\varphi)^{(2r-1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}\right).

Following inequalities (
4
) and (
5
), we see that the bound
\left\lVert\left(\mathcal{K}y\right)^{(2r+1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}
does not depend on
h
. Therefore,

\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}-Q_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2r+1}\right).

Hence,

\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}-Q_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert=O\left(h^{2r+1}\right).

Now, consider the second bound as

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}-Q_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{R}(E)}\left\{\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}-Q_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}:\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=1\right\}.

Assume that
\varphi\in C^{2r+2}[0,1]
, which implies
y\in C^{2r+2}[0,1]
and so
\mathcal{K}y\in C^{2r+2}[0,1]
. Using Proposition
4.6
, we have

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}-Q_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}y\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 2C_{1}\left\lVert\mathcal{K}y\right\rVert_{2r+2,\infty}h^{2r+2}.

Note that

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}y\right\rVert_{2r+2,\infty}=\max_{0\leq i\leq 2r+2}\left\lVert\left(\mathcal{K}y\right)^{(i)}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

Following inequalities (
4
) and (
5
) as discussed above, we see that the bound
\left\lVert\left(\mathcal{K}y\right)^{(i)}\right\rVert_{\infty}
is independent of
h
. Therefore, we finally obtain

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}-Q_{n}\mathcal{K})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert=O\left(h^{2r+2}\right),

which completes the proof.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be the linear integral operator defined by (

) with a Green’s function-type kernel. Let
Q_{n}
be the interpolatory operator onto the approximating space
\mathcal{X}_{n}
defined by (

). Then

\displaystyle\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{n}^{M})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert=\begin{cases}O\left(h^{2r+2}\right)&\text{for }r\geq 1,\\
O\left(h^{3}\right)&\text{for }r=0,\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{n}^{M})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert=\begin{cases}O\left(h^{2r+3}\right)&\text{for }r\geq 1,\\
O\left(h^{4}\right)&\text{for }r=0,\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{K}_{n}^{M}=Q_{n}\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{K}Q_{n}-Q_{n}\mathcal{K}Q_{n}.

Proof.

For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{R}(E)
,
\mathcal{K}\varphi\in\mathcal{R}(E)
. Consider

\displaystyle\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{n}^{M})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{R}(E)}\left\{\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{n}^{M})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}:\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=1\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{R}(E)}\left\{\left\lVert(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}:\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=1\right\}.

For
r\geq 1
, we write

\left\lVert(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\left\lVert I-Q_{n}\right\rVert\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}.

Since
Q_{n}
is uniformly bounded, i.e.
\left\lVert Q_{n}\right\rVert=q<\infty
. For
\varphi\in C^{2r+2}[0,1]
, it follows that
\mathcal{K}\varphi\in C^{2r+2}[0,1]
and using Proposition
4.6
, we have

\left\lVert(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 2C_{1}(1+q)\left\lVert\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{2r+2,\infty}h^{2r+2}.

Here

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{2r+2,\infty}=\max_{0\leq i\leq 2r+2}\left\lVert\left(\mathcal{K}\varphi\right)^{(i)}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

Following inequalities (
4
) and (
5
), we observe that the bound
\left\lVert\left(\mathcal{K}\varphi\right)^{(i)}\right\rVert_{\infty}
is independent of
h
, for
i=0,1,\cdots,2r+2
. Therefore,

\left\lVert(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2r+2}\right).

(35)

When
r=0
, using (
12
), we have

\left\lVert(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{4}\left\lVert\left(\mathcal{K}(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right)^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}h.

It follows from Chatelin-Lebbar
(
Cha-Leb2

\left\lVert\left(\mathcal{K}(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right)^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2}\right)

Therefore, from above two estimates, we have

\left\lVert(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{3}\right).

The above bound with (
35

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{n}^{M})\mathcal{K}|_{\mathcal{R}(E)}\right\rVert=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{R}(E)}\left\{\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}:\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=1\right\}.

For

, we write

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}\right\rVert\left\lVert(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}.

Let
\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1
, then

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}x\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{K}\right\rVert\left\lVert(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}x\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{K}\right\rVert C_{4}\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}x)^{\prime\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}C_{4}\left\lVert\mathcal{K}\right\rVert\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{2}.

This gives,

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}\right\rVert=O\left(h^{2}\right).

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{K}(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}\right\rVert\left\lVert(I-Q_{n})\mathcal{K}\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2r+3}\right).

Conclusion

In this work, we have investigated the convergence behavior of projection-based approximations for eigenvalue problems associated with compact integral operators having Green’s kernels. Both classical and modified projection methods were analyzed in a unified framework. We established explicit convergence rates for the approximate eigenvalues and the corresponding spectral subspaces in the supremum-norm.

Our analysis shows that the modified (iterated) projection method produces higher-order convergence of eigenvalues compared to the classical projection method. In particular, the superconvergence phenomenon arising from iteration significantly improves the approximation of spectral subspaces. This enhancement persists even when the underlying Green’s kernel lacks full smoothness along the diagonal, thereby extending the scope of earlier results that were primarily restricted to smooth kernels. The theoretical findings are supported by numerical experiments, which confirm that the iterated method achieves higher accuracy with a computational effort. Consequently, the modified projection approach provides an efficient and reliable framework for eigenvalue computations involving Green’s kernels. Future research directions include the extension of the present analysis to integral operators with singular or weakly singular kernels, as well as tinvestigation of asymptotic expansions for eigenvalue approximations.

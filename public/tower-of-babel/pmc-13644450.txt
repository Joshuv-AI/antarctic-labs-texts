Esaxerenone attenuates renal injury by inhibiting NR3C2-mediated inflammatory and fibrotic pathways
Authors: Chang Xu, Fengrui Liu, Jingwen An, Nan Zhai, Wenjing Li, Hongshuang Wang, Zheng Wang, Yan Zhong, Xiangting Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRenal inflammation and fibrosis are pathological features of various types of kidney diseases. High-salt diet and aldosterone are risk factors for inducing kidney disease. Esaxerenone is the mineralocorticoid receptor blocker which is effective in treating hypertension. The aim of this study is to observe the effect of esaxerenone on renal inflammation in a rat model of high-salt diet plus aldosterone infusion. We found that rats treated with high-salt diet plus aldosterone showed more serious renal pathological injury. As well as, the expression of kidney injury molecule-1, toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4), phospho-nuclear factor kappa-B‌ (p-NF-κB), F4/80, interleukin-6, ‌tumor necrosis factor-alpha, and interferon gamma were increased (p < 0.05). In addition, high-salt diet plus aldosterone could upregulate the expression of transforming growth factor-beta‌ (TGF-β), p-Smad2, p-Smad3, alpha-smooth muscle actin, vimentin, and collagen I (p < 0.05). However, administration of esaxerenone could ameliorate high-salt diet plus aldosterone - induced these expressions and renal pathological injury (p < 0.05). These data suggest that esaxerenone may alleviate high-salt plus aldosterone-induced renal inflammation and fibrosis by regulating the TLR4/NF-κB and TGF-β/Smad signaling pathways.Keywords: Renal fibrosis, renal inflammation, aldosterone, esaxerenone

Introduction

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is an important public health concern, and its progression is largely determined by the degree of renal fibrosis [1]. Renal fibrosis is the common pathological manifestation of various types of CKD in the terminal stage and is characterized by a sequence of pathological alterations, including the infiltration of inflammatory cells, proliferation of fibroblasts, and deposition of extracellular matrix (ECM) [2,3]. Ultimately, these alterations disrupt the normal renal architecture and lead to a progressive decline in kidney function.

High-salt diet and aldosterone were involved in the progression of renal injury, inflammation and fibrosis [4–6]. Emerging evidence suggests that mineralocorticoid receptor (MR) activation triggers pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic cascades, synergistically accelerating renal damage [7]. Esaxerenone, a novel, orally available, non-steroidal, high-selectivity MR antagonist, has been approved for hypertension management and demonstrated promising efficacy in reducing proteinuria and slowing CKD progression. Studies have shown that esaxerenone could reduce proteinuria, glomerular damage, renal inflammation, oxidative stress and tubulointerstitial fibrosis in DKD mice [8]. Moreover, our previous study has shown that esaxerenone could reduce blood pressure and alleviate renal injury in rats of high-salt plus aldosterone model [9].

Inflammatory responses are involved in the process of renal fibrosis, and the release of inflammatory factors also promotes the occurrence and development of renal fibrosis [10]. Toll like receptor 4 (TLR4) belongs to the TLR subfamily. It is reported that the overexpression or excessive activation of TLR4 could promote the progression of CKD [11]. TLR4 can mediate the activation of NF-κB, which is a classic signaling pathway that induces inflammatory responses [12]. Moreover, the TGF-β/Smad signaling pathway is also a key mediator in the initiation and progression of renal injury [13,14].

TGF-β belongs to the TGF superfamily, and there are three different subtypes of TGF-β. TGF-β1 is a classic member of the TGF family. TGF-β signaling is broadly accepted as a strong stimulator of fibrosis. Alterations in TGF-β1 signaling have been linked to fibrosis, cancer, developmental, and cardiovascular diseases [15]. TGF-β/Smad is a major signaling pathway leading to kidney disease, and mediates renal fibrosis [16]. TGF-β1 mediated progressive renal fibrosis by stimulating production and suppressing degradation of ECM. The major downstream mediators of TGF-β1 were Smad2 and Smad3. It has been demonstrated that Smad3 mainly mediated renal fibrosis, and deletion of Smad3 reduced fibrogenesis [17]. The studies suggested that high levels of dietary salt intake stimulating increased production of TGF-β leading to progressive fibrosis [18]. As well as, aldosterone could also promote renal fibrosis by activating MR and upregulate the expression of TGF-β1 [19].

In this study, we conducted a high-salt diet plus aldosterone-infusion rat model to further characterize MR-mediated inflammation and fibrosis cascades that contribute to renal injury. And providing evidence for the multifaceted renal protective effect of MRB (esaxerenone), expanding its therapeutic implications for chronic kidney disease induced by excess aldosterone and high salt intake, beyond its known anti-hypertensive application.

Materials and methods

Animal and sample collection

SPF Male Wistar rats (5 weeks, 120 ± 10 g) were purchased from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. (SCXK 2021–1-0006). The rats were maintained on standard chow and tap water at 25 °C, relative humidity 50 ± 10%, under a 12-h light/dark cycle. All experiments were performed in accordance with the recommendations of the National Institutes of Health Guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. Animal care was performed in accordance with the criteria of the Ethics Committee of Hebei University of Chinese Medicine (DWLL202403072). After 7 days of adaptive feeding, the rats were numbered and randomly divided into each group via a random number table, and cage placement was performed by a person not involved in the experimental procedures. The groups are as follows: the CON group, the ALD+HSD (aldosterone infusion+ 8% high-salt diet) group and MRB (aldosterone infusion + 8% high-salt diet + esaxerenone) group, six in each group. The left kidney was resected in all groups except the CON group. For the ALD+HSD group and MRB group, mini-osmotic pumps (ALZET model 2006, DURECT Corporation, Cupertino, CA, USA) were implanted for aldosterone infusion at 600 μg/kg/d [20], removed after six weeks [9]. The rats were administered with 1 mg/kg/d esaxerenone in the MRB group [19]. After six weeks, the blood and kidneys were collected for analysis. All animals underwent isoflurane anesthesia. Animals were euthanized by carbon dioxide inhalation.

Based on pilot data (differences in renal injury markers between the high‑salt/aldosterone group and the control group), we used G*Power software with α = 0.05 and power (1‑β)=0.80, which yielded a minimum of 5 animals per group. To account for potential dropouts, we set n = 6 per group. The n = 3 in some figure legends denotes the number of randomly selected animals from each group whose samples were used for representative Western blots or immunohistochemistry images.

Histological analysis

Kidney specimens were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, sectioned into 4 μm-thick sections, deparaffinized with xylene for 2 × 30 min, dehydrated with a gradient concentration of ethanol, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin and Masson staining, to observe the pathological changes in the kidney tissue, Using semi-quantitative criteria 0–4 (0 = no, 1 = mild, 2 = medium, 3 = severe, 4 = very severely) [9]. Random non-overlapping microscopic fields were captured. Semi-quantitative histological scoring was performed to quantify renal pathological injury. All sections were randomly coded, and pathological assessment was conducted blindly by two independent researchers who were unaware of the experimental grouping. Two researchers separately completed blind scoring on all samples to verify the reproducibility of the histological evaluation.

Immunohistochemical and staining

Immunohistochemical staining was used to detect the expression of α-SMA, vimentin, Collagen I, F4/80, TNF-α, and IL-6 in the kidney tissues. The Streptavidin-Biotin Complex method is used for detection, sectioning, gradient dewaxing, and antigen retrieval [21]. Sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies (Table 1).

Statistical analysis

The results were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows version 26.0. All data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). The data conform to the normal distribution and the homogeneity of variance test. Statistical comparisons between groups were performed with one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post hoc tests for multiple groups. p < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

Results

Esaxereone alleviates the renal pathological damage induced by high-salt diet plus aldosterone

HE staining was used to observed the renal pathological damage. The result showed that the severe tubular damage was observed, including the disordered renal tubules, the increase of luminal dilation and the infiltration of inflammatory cells in the ALD+HSD group compared with the CON group. However, esaxerenone could alleviate tubular damage induced by high-salt diet plus aldosterone (Figure 2A–C). Moreover, we used western blot to detect the KIM-1 expression, and the result demonstrated it was upregulated in the ALD+HSD group. While esaxerenone could substantially suppress KIM-1 expression (Figure 2D–E). These results indicated that esaxerenone could alleviate the renal pathological damage induced by high-salt diet plus aldosterone

Esaxerenone improves high-salt diet plus aldosterone-induced renal fibrosis

As shown in Figure 2, high-salt plus aldosterone infusion significantly upregulated α-SMA, vimentin and collagen I expression in the ALD+HSD group compared with CON group (Figure 3A–H). Studies have shown the TGF-β signaling was activated in the kidneys of db/db mice, which mediated the proliferation of macrophages and promoted kidney inflammation and damage [22]. Our results found that the expression of TGF-β1, as well as the levels of p-Smad2 and p-Smad3 were increased in the ALD+HSD group (Figure 3I–L). However, esaxerenone treatment effectively reduced the expression of these proteins and diminished the fibrotic area (Figure 3A–L). These findings suggested that esaxerenone could ameliorate high-salt plus aldosterone-induced renal fibrosis by inhibiting the TGF-β1 signaling pathway.

Esaxerenone alleviates high-salt diet plus aldosterone-induced renal inflammatory response

High-salt diet and excess aldosterone have been implicated in renal inflammation. The TLR4/NF-κB axis is a classic inflammatory signaling pathway. To explore the role of the TLR4/NF-κB axis in high-salt diet plus aldosterone-induced renal injury, we evaluated components of this pathway in renal tissues. Western blot results revealed that TLR4 and p-NF-κB expression were significantly elevated in renal tissues of the ALD+HSD group compared to the CON group (Figure 4A–C), and obvious NF-κB nuclear translocation was observed in the ALD+HSD group (Figure 4D,H). To further validate the functional relevance of TLR4/NFκB activation, we measured other pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as F4/80, IL-6, TNF-α and IFN-γ. As shown in Figure 4(D–L), high-salt diet plus aldosterone could induce a marked increase of these cytokines. While esaxerenone treatment significantly attenuated the upregulation of F4/80, IL-6, TNF-α, and IFN-γ. These results indicated that esaxerenone could ameliorate high-salt plus aldosterone-induced renal inflammation by inhibiting the TLR4/NF-κB axis.

Esaxerenone may inhibit renal inflammation and fibrosis induced by high-salt diet plus aldosterone through suppressing the activation of MR

The MR (NR3C2) is a ligand-dependent transcription factor whose excessive activation drives renal damage. There are studies implicated the effect of MR activation, which could lead to macrophage proliferation, fibroblast transdifferentiation and lymphangiogenesis [22–24]. In this study, we found that nuclear accumulation of NR3C2 was also significantly increased in the ALD+HSD group compared with the CON group, while its nuclear translocation was effectively reversed by esaxerenone treatment (Figure 5A–C). Consistent with NR3C2 activation, SGK-1, a key effector of MR-mediated pathological responses was also increased in the ALD+HSD group (Figure 5A,D).

To further explore the role of NR3C2 (MR) activation and its effect in driving the renal injury induced by high-salt diet plus aldosterone, we performed Pearson correlation analysis to assess the relationships between NR3C2 and pathological markers (inflammation, fibrosis, and tubular injury). As shown in Figure 5(E–J), NR3C2 expression exhibited significant positive correlations with multiple pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic indicators. These results revealed that MR activation may serve as a driver, coordinating both inflammatory and fibrotic cascades to promote high-salt diet plus aldosterone-induced kidney injury.

Discussion

Inflammatory cell infiltration was one of the most important characteristics of the renal pathology, accompanied with reduced renal function [25]. There is evidence supporting that a correlation between inflammation and fibrosis. It is reported that inflammation is necessary for renal interstitial fibrosis [10]. The Toll-like receptor (TLR)/NF-κB pathway and the TGF-β/Smad pathway are the main pathways promoting the pathogenesis of CKD [26,27]. The increased release of inflammatory mediators could promote the progression of CKD [28].

TLR is a type of pattern recognition receptor [29]. TLR expression is widespread among immune cells, TLRs are also present in non-immune cell types, notably in epithelial and endothelial cells [30]. It has been reported that TLR4 mediates both pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic pathways in renal fibrosis [31]. Clinical studies also showed that upregulation of TLR4 expression is observed in patients with CKD and is associated with increased release of various biological mediators, such as TNF-α, IL-6, and monocyte chemoattractant protein 1 (MCP-1) [32,33]. NF-κB is a downstream effector of the TLR4 signaling pathway and a classic and important nuclear transcription factor that can mediate various inflammatory processes [34]. Several studies have shown that TLRs/NF-κB signaling is involved in the process of CKD [35]. Some studies also revealed that the knockdown of the TLR4 significantly reduces the risk of fibrosis [36]. In our previous study, we have investigated the individual and synergistic effects of high-salt and aldosterone on the development of renal injury [9]. Therefore, we mainly discussed the effects of high-salt diet plus aldosterone on expression of inflammatory cytokines in this study. The results showed that high-salt diet plus aldosterone could lead to the upregulation of TNF-α, IL-6, IFN-γ, TLR4, and p-NF-κB.

The TGF-β/Smad signaling pathway could mediate the ECM accumulation and promote the development of renal fibrosis. The TGF-β superfamily comprises a large series of cytokine growth factors that control many cellular functions, including proliferation, migration, differentiation, and extracellular matrix secretion and deposition [37]. TGF-β1 is the main factor in promoting fibrosis in chronic nephropathy. It is proved that activation of TGF-β1 and its downstream promotes the accumulation of ECM, leading to renal interstitial fibrosis [16,38]. Smad2 and Smad3 are the two major downstream regulators that promote TGF-β1-mediated tissue fibrosis [39]. Our results suggested that the expression level of TGF-β1, phosphorylation of Smad2 and Smad3 were increased by high-salt diet plus aldosterone.

Many evidences suggested that the overactivation of MR play an important role in regulating inflammation, collagen formation and fibrosis, which is the main determinant of CKD progression [7]. The studies have proved that MR activation could induce renal macrophage infiltration or polarization, thereby promoting renal inflammation and fibrosis [19,20]. We also have revealed that high-salt diet plus aldosterone could activate the RAAS in the kidney, thereby activating MR, ultimately promoting and aggravating renal pathological injury [9]. SGK-1 is the downstream factor of aldosterone. Previous study has demonstrated that SGK-1/TGF-β pathway plays a role in the process of renal fibrosis in the UUO pregnant rat model [40]. Aldosterone exerts its biological effects by activating NR3C2 to regulate the expression and activity of SGK-1, thereby influencing the TLR4/NF-κB and TGF-β1/Smad pathways, ultimately contributing to the development of renal pathology. In addition, the activation of MR could also increase the activity of various pathways such as IFN-γ, NF-κB, and IL-6, which is closely associated with inflammation and fibrosis [41]. In this study, we found that high-salt diet plus aldosterone could increase the expression of SGK-1 and inflammatory factors, which is associated with the activation of MR.

MR blockers (MRBs) could reduce renal inflammation and fibrosis. Esaxerenone (also known as CS-3150), the third-generation MRB, was approved to treat hypertension. Compared with other MRBs, esaxerenone can exhibit higher binding specificity, because it has no agonistic or antagonistic effects on glucocorticoid, or sex hormones receptors, thereby reducing the risk of hyperkalemia and endocrine disorders [42]. Although MRB prevents progressive kidney disease by decreasing blood pressure, it likely plays its role by inhibiting the expression of profibrotic and inflammatory mediators [41]. Esaxerenone can inhibit MR activity by competitively binding to MR with aldosterone, thereby reducing the release of pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic factors induced by excessive MR activation, ultimately alleviating renal inflammation and fibrosis. To detect whether high-salt diet plus aldosterone could induce renal inflammation and fibrosis by activating MR, we used esaxerenone to inhibit the activation of MR. Our results showed that esaxerenone could reduce the expression of inflammatory and fibrotic factors induced by high-salt diet plus aldosterone, thereby exerting a protective effect on kidney. These findings may provide a new perspective for preventing CKD. However, the renal protective effect mediated by MR in this study cannot be separated from hemodynamic or diuretic effects.

In this study, we only observed the changes of inflammatory-related factors and fibrosis-related proteins in vivo, and the specific mechanism is limited. Meanwhile, the current study only provide additional molecular characterization of inflammatory and fibrotic pathways in a combined high-salt plus aldosterone model, the specific causal pathway should be explored. In future studies, we will conduct experiments to explore specific mechanism and cell experiments.

Conclusion

The results of this study demonstrated that high-salt diet plus aldosterone induce renal inflammation and fibrosis. Esaxerenone may attenuate renal inflammation and fibrosis, accompanied by suppression of NR3C2 activation and downstream inflammatory/fibrotic pathway markers.

Our contributions.
We propose a dynamic working set (DWS) algorithm for solving problem (
1
) when
s\ll n
,
\mathtt{A}
is an arbitrary

\|\mathtt{A}\|\leq 1

Define the
support set
of a solution to be the subset of non-zero variables in it. DWS checks how well the support set size matches the working set size in the previous iteration. The result determines the number of free variables that will be added to the previous support set to form the next working set.

We ran compressed sensing experiments on DWS with GPSR as the solver. We set
s
to be 1%, 4%, and 8% of

which is similar to the ranges of

used in previous works
[
3
,
12

28

. DWS is

faster than Skglm,
3\times
faster than Celer, and
2.45\times
faster than running GPSR alone on average. Similar trends are observed for other values of
s
in the range of 1% to 8% of
n
.

Scale space such that
\|\mathtt{b}\|=1
. Take any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Let
U
be an upper bound on any working set size before DWS reaches a solution
\mathtt{x}_{r}
such that

U=O(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}s\log s\log\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon})

Given any function
\varphi:\real^{n}\rightarrow\real
, a vector
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\varphi(\mathtt{y})\geq\varphi(\mathtt{x})+\langle\xi,\mathtt{y}-\mathtt{x}\rangle
for all
\mathtt{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
is called a
subgradient
of
\varphi
at
\mathtt{x}

[
26
]
. For a smooth function, the subgradient at a point is unique and equal to the gradient, which is denoted by
\nabla\varphi

\mathtt{x}
; we use
\partial\varphi(\mathtt{x})
to denote the set of all subgradients of
\varphi
at
\mathtt{x}
.

We have
\partial F(\mathtt{x})=\{\nabla f(\mathtt{x})+\xi:\xi\in\partial g(\mathtt{x})\}
. A vector
\mathtt{n}
is a
descent direction
from
\mathtt{x}
if and only if
\sup\{\langle\gamma,\mathtt{n}\rangle:\gamma\in\partial F(\mathtt{x})\}<0
.
The function
F
is minimized at
\mathtt{x}
if and only if
\partial F(\mathtt{x})
contains the zero vector
[
26
]
.

Lemma
2
proves the termination condition of
E_{r}=\emptyset
. Theorem
4.1
analyzes the sizes of the working and support sets.

Lemma 1

(i)

Take any
\mathtt{x}\in\real^{n}
.

(a)

(b)

(ii)

Proof

Consider (i)(a). Take any
\xi\in\partial g(\mathtt{x})
.
By the definition of a subgradient, for all
\mathtt{y}\in\real^{n}
,
g(\mathtt{y})-g(\mathtt{x})\geq\langle\xi,\mathtt{y}-\mathtt{x}\rangle
, which is equivalent to

\eta\sum_{i=1}^{n}|(\mathtt{y})_{i}|-\eta\sum_{i=1}^{n}|(\mathtt{x})_{i}|\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\xi)_{i}\cdot(\mathtt{y}-\mathtt{x})_{i}.

(2)

Take any index
i\in[n]
. Let
Y_{i}=\bigl\{\mathtt{y}\in\real^{n}:\forall j\not=i,(\mathtt{y})_{j}=(\mathtt{x})_{j}\bigr\}
. Clearly, for every
\mathtt{y}\in Y_{i}
, applying (
2
) to
\mathtt{y}
gives

\eta|(\mathtt{y})_{i}|-\eta|(\mathtt{x})_{i}|\geq(\xi)_{i}\cdot(\mathtt{y}-\mathtt{x})_{i}.

•

Case 1: Suppose that
(\mathtt{x})_{i}\geq 0
. Choose
\mathtt{y}\in Y_{i}
such that
(\mathtt{y})_{i}>(\mathtt{x})_{i}
. Then,
\eta(\mathtt{y}-\mathtt{x})_{i}=\eta|(\mathtt{y})_{i}|-\eta|(\mathtt{x})_{i}|\geq(\xi)_{i}\cdot(\mathtt{y}-\mathtt{x})_{i}
. Dividing both sides by
(\mathtt{y}-\mathtt{x})_{i}
gives
(\xi)_{i}\leq\eta
.

•

Case 2: Suppose that
(\mathtt{x})_{i}\leq 0
. Choose
\mathtt{y}\in Y_{i}
such that
(\mathtt{y})_{i}<(\mathtt{x})_{i}
. Then,
-\eta(\mathtt{y}-\mathtt{x})_{i}=\eta|(\mathtt{y})_{i}|-\eta|(\mathtt{x})_{i}|\geq(\xi)_{i}\cdot(\mathtt{y}-\mathtt{x})_{i}
. Dividing both sides by
(\mathtt{y}-\mathtt{x})_{i}
gives
(\xi)_{i}\geq-\eta
.

Combining cases 1 and 2 gives
(\xi)_{i}\in[-\eta,\eta]
when
(\mathtt{x})_{i}=0
. Suppose that
(\mathtt{x})_{i}>0
. We already have
(\xi)_{i}\leq\eta
by case 1. Choose
\mathtt{y}\in Y_{i}
such that
0<(\mathtt{y})_{i}<(\mathtt{x})_{i}
. Then,
\eta(\mathtt{y}-\mathtt{x})_{i}=\eta|(\mathtt{y})_{i}|-\eta|(\mathtt{x})_{i}|\geq(\xi)_{i}\cdot(\mathtt{y}-\mathtt{x})_{i}
. Dividing both sides by
(\mathtt{y}-\mathtt{x})_{i}
gives
(\xi)_{i}\geq\eta
. As a result,
(\xi)_{i}=\eta
. Suppose that
(\mathtt{x})_{i}<0
. We already have
(\xi)_{i}\geq-\eta
by case 2. Choose
\mathtt{y}\in Y_{i}
such that
(\mathtt{x}_{r})_{i}<(\mathtt{y})_{i}<0
. Then,
-\eta(\mathtt{y}-\mathtt{x}_{r})_{i}=\eta|(\mathtt{y})_{i}|-\eta|(\mathtt{x})_{i}|\geq(\xi)_{i}\cdot(\mathtt{y}-\mathtt{x})_{i}
. Dividing both sides by
(\mathtt{y}-\mathtt{x})_{i}
gives
(\xi)_{i}\leq-\eta
. In all,
(\xi)_{i}=-\eta
. This completes the proof of (i)(a).

Consider (i)(b). Take any vector
\xi\in\real^{n}
that satisfies the conditions in (i)(a). Under these conditions, it is easy to verify that for every
\mathtt{y}\in\real^{n}
and every
i\in[n]
,
\eta|(\mathtt{y})_{i}|-\eta|(\mathtt{x})_{i}|\geq(\xi)_{i}\cdot(\mathtt{y}-\mathtt{x})_{i}
. Then, for all
\mathtt{y}\in\real^{n}
,

\displaystyle g(\mathtt{y})-g(\mathtt{x})=\eta\sum_{i=1}^{n}|(\mathtt{y})_{i}|-\eta\sum_{i=1}^{n}|(\mathtt{x})_{i}|\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\xi)_{i}\cdot(\mathtt{y}-\mathtt{x})_{i}=\langle\xi,\mathtt{y}-\mathtt{x}\rangle,

which implies that
\xi
is a subgradient in
\partial g(\mathtt{x})
.

Consider (ii). Take any
i\in E_{r}
. By definition,
|(\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r}))_{i}|>\eta
. So
(\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r}))_{i}\not=0
. It also follows from (i) that for every
\xi\in\partial g(\mathtt{x}_{r})
, if
(\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r}))_{i}>0
, then
(\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r}))_{i}+(\xi)_{i}>0
, and if
(\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r}))_{i}<0
, then
(\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r}))_{i}+(\xi)_{i}<0
. Note that
\partial F(\mathtt{x}_{r})=\{\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r})+\xi:\xi\in\partial g(\mathtt{x}_{r})\}
. In other words, for every
i\in E_{r}
and every
\gamma\in\partial F(\mathtt{x}_{r})
,
\mathrm{sgn}((\gamma)_{i})=\mathrm{sgn}((\nabla f(\mathtt{x}))_{i})\in\{-1,1\}
. This proves (ii).

Consider (iii). For all
i\not\in[n]\setminus(W_{r}\cup E_{r})
, we have
(\mathtt{x}_{r})_{i}=0
and
|(\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r}))_{i}|\leq\eta
. By (i)(b), for all
i\not\in[n]\setminus(W_{r}\cup E_{r})
, every value in
[-\eta,\eta]
is a legitimate
i
-th coordinate for a subgradient of
g
at
\mathtt{x}_{r}
, which includes
-(\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r}))_{i}
. Hence, there exists
\gamma\in\partial F(\mathtt{x}_{r})
such that
(\gamma)_{i}=0
for all
i\not\in[n]\setminus(W_{r}\cup E_{r})
. Since
\mathtt{x}_{r}
is the optimal solution with respect to the working set
W_{r}
, there exists
\gamma\in\partial F(\mathtt{x}_{r})
such that
(\gamma)_{i}=0
for all
i\in W_{r}
. We conclude that for every
i\in[n]\setminus E_{r}
, there exists
\gamma\in\partial F(\mathtt{x}_{r})
such that
(\gamma)_{i}=0
. By the result in (ii), for every
i\in E_{r}
and every
\gamma\in\partial F(\mathtt{x}_{r})
,
(\gamma)_{i}\not=0
. This proves the correctness of (iii), i.e.,
i\in E_{r}
if and only if
(\gamma)_{i}\not=0
for all
\gamma\in\partial F(\mathtt{x}_{r})
. ∎

Lemma 2

Let
\mathtt{e}_{i}
be the unit vector in the direction of the positive
i
-th axis. Every unit conical combination of
\{-\mathrm{sign}((\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r}))_{i})\cdot\mathtt{e}_{i}:i\in E_{r}\}
is a descent direction from
\mathtt{x}_{r}
. If
E_{r}=\emptyset
, then
\mathtt{x}_{r}
is the global minimum.

Proof

Let
\rho=\min_{i\in E_{r}}|\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r})_{i}|-\eta
which is positive. Take any
i\in E_{r}
and any
\gamma\in\partial F(\mathtt{x}_{r})
. By Lemma
1
(i), the
i
-th coordinate of any subgradient in
\partial g(\mathtt{x}_{r})
is in the range
[-\eta,\eta]
, which implies that
|(\gamma)_{i}|\geq\rho
. Let
\mathtt{s}_{i}=-\mathrm{sign}((\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r}))_{i})\cdot\mathtt{e}_{i}
. By Lemma
1
(ii),
\langle\gamma,\mathtt{s}_{i}\rangle=-|(\gamma)_{i}|\leq-\rho<0
. For every unit conical combination
\sum_{i\in E_{r}}\alpha_{i}\mathtt{s}_{i}
, some coefficient
\alpha_{i}
is at least
1/\sqrt{n}
. Thus,
\sup_{\gamma\in\partial F(\mathtt{x}_{r})}\langle\gamma,\sum_{i\in E_{r}}\alpha_{i}\mathtt{s}_{i}\rangle\leq-\rho/\sqrt{n}<0
, proving that
\sum_{i\in E_{r}}\alpha_{i}\mathtt{s}_{i}
is a descent direction. If
E_{r}
is empty, by Lemma
1
(iii), for every
i\in[n]
, there exists
\xi\in\partial g(\mathtt{x}_{r})
such that
(\xi)_{i}=-(\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r}))_{i}
, that is,
-(\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r}))_{i}
is a legitimate
i
-th coordinate of a subgradient in
\partial g(\mathtt{x}_{r})
. It follows from Lemma
1
(i)(b) that the zero vector belongs to
\partial F(\mathtt{x}_{r})
, which implies that
\mathtt{x}_{r}
is the global minimum. ∎

Define a vector
\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}\in\real^{n}
such that for all
i\in[n]
, if
i\not\in E_{r}
, then
(\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}})_{i}=-(\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r}))_{i}
, and if
i\in E_{r}
, then
(\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}})_{i}=-\mathrm{sign}((\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r}))_{i})\cdot\eta
. Define
\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}=\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r})+\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}
.
Let
\mathtt{x}_{*}
denote the optimal solution that minimizes
F
. Given a vector
\mathtt{v}
and a subset
S\subseteq[n]
,
\mathtt{v}\downarrow S
denotes the orthogonal projection of
\mathtt{v}
in the subspace spanned by
\{\mathtt{e}_{i}:i\in S\}
.

Lemma 3

\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}\in\partial g(\mathtt{x}_{r})

(i)(b), for all

Lemma 4

Let
\mathtt{n}_{r}
be any unit conical combination of
\{-\mathrm{sign}((\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r}))_{i})\cdot\mathtt{e}_{i}:i\in E_{r}\}
. Let
\mathtt{y}_{r}
be the point in direction
\mathtt{n}_{r}
from
\mathtt{x}_{r}
that minimizes
F
.

(i)

There exists
\xi\in\partial g(\mathtt{y}_{r})
such that
\langle\mathtt{\nabla}f(\mathtt{y}_{r})+\xi,\mathtt{y}_{r}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle=0
.

(ii)

For every
\xi\in\partial g(\mathtt{y}_{r})
, both
\langle\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}-\xi,\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle
and
\langle\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}-\xi,\mathtt{y}_{r}\rangle
are zero.

(iii)

For every
\mathtt{z}\in\real^{n}
,
F(\mathtt{x}_{r})-F(\mathtt{z})\leq-\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle
.

(iv)

F(\mathtt{x}_{r})-F(\mathtt{y}_{r})=-\frac{1}{2}\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{y}_{r}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle
.

Proof

Since
\mathtt{n}_{r}
is a descent direction by Lemma
2
, the point
\mathtt{y}_{r}
is well defined. Consider (i). Let
L
denote the line through
\mathtt{x}_{r}
parallel to
\mathtt{n}_{r}
. Since the minimum of
F
in
L
is achieved at
\mathtt{y}_{r}
, it is known that there exists
\gamma\in\partial F(\mathtt{y}_{r})
such that
\langle\gamma,\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{y}_{r}\rangle\geq 0
for all
\mathtt{z}\in L
. Note that
\mathtt{x}_{r}\in L
. Choose the point
\mathtt{x}\in L
such that
\mathtt{x}_{r}-\mathtt{y}_{r}=\mathtt{y}_{r}-\mathtt{x}
. Then, we have
\langle\gamma,\mathtt{x}_{r}-\mathtt{y}_{r}\rangle\geq 0
and
\langle\gamma,\mathtt{x}-\mathtt{y}_{r}\rangle\geq 0
. The second inequality also implies that
\langle\gamma,\mathtt{x}_{r}-\mathtt{y}_{r}\rangle=-\langle\gamma,\mathtt{x}-\mathtt{y}_{r}\rangle\leq 0
. It follows that
\langle\gamma,\mathtt{x}_{r}-\mathtt{y}_{r}\rangle=0
, which implies that there exists
\xi\in\partial g(\mathtt{y}_{r})
such that
\langle\nabla f(\mathtt{y}_{r})+\xi,\mathtt{x}_{r}-\mathtt{y}_{r}\rangle=0
. This proves (i).

Consider (ii). Take any
i\in\mathrm{supp}(\mathtt{n}_{r})
. As
i\in E_{r}
by definition, we have
(\mathtt{x}_{r})_{i}=0
and
(\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}})_{i}=-\mathrm{sign}((\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r}))_{i})\cdot\eta
. Also,
\mathrm{sign}((\mathtt{y}_{r})_{i})=-\mathrm{sign}((\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r})_{i})
because we descend from
\mathtt{x}_{r}
in direction
\mathtt{n}_{r}
to reach
\mathtt{y}_{r}
. By Lemma
1
(i)(a),
(\xi)_{i}=\mathrm{sign}((\mathtt{y}_{r})_{i})\cdot\eta=-\mathrm{sign}((\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r})_{i})\cdot\eta
. Therefore,
(\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}})_{i}=(\xi)_{i}
. For any
i\not\in\mathrm{supp}(\mathtt{n}_{r})
, we have
(\mathtt{x}_{r})_{i}=(\mathtt{y}_{r})_{i}
. If they are not zero, then
(\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}})_{i}
and
(\xi)_{i}
are identical by Lemma
1
(i)(a). We conclude that both
((\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}})_{i}-(\xi)_{i})\cdot(\mathtt{x}_{r})_{i}
and
((\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}})_{i}-(\xi)_{i})\cdot(\mathtt{y}_{r})_{i}
are zero for all
i\in[n]
. This proves (ii).

Before proving (iii) and (iv), we first prove the following equation:
\forall\,\mathtt{x},\mathtt{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\forall\,\zeta_{1}\in\partial g(\mathtt{x}),\forall\,\zeta_{2}\in\partial g(\mathtt{z})
,

F(\mathtt{x})-F(\mathtt{z})=-\frac{1}{2}\|\mathtt{A}(\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x})\|^{2}-\langle\mathtt{\nabla}f(\mathtt{x})+\zeta_{1},\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x}\rangle+\langle\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2},\mathtt{z}\rangle.

(3)

Take any
i\in[n]
and any
\zeta_{1}\in\partial g(\mathtt{x})
. By Lemma
1
(i)(a),
(\zeta_{1})_{i}\cdot(\mathtt{x})_{i}=\eta|(\mathtt{x})_{i}|
. Therefore,
\langle{\zeta}_{1},\mathtt{x}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\zeta_{1})_{i}\cdot(\mathtt{x})_{i}=\eta\|\mathtt{x}\|_{1}=g(\mathtt{x})
, which implies that

g(\mathtt{x})-g(\mathtt{z})=\langle{\zeta}_{1},\mathtt{x}\rangle-\langle\zeta_{2},\mathtt{z}\rangle.

(4)

It has been proved in our unpublished manuscript
[
7
]
that
f(\mathtt{x})-f(\mathtt{z})=-\frac{1}{2}\|\mathtt{A}(\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x})\|^{2}-\langle\mathtt{\nabla}f(\mathtt{x}),\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x}\rangle
. We give the proof below for completeness. For all
s\in[0,1]
, define
\mathtt{z}_{s}=\mathtt{x}+s(\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x})
. By the chain rule, we have
\frac{\partial f}{\partial s}=\bigl\langle\frac{\partial f}{\partial\mathtt{z}_{s}},\frac{\partial\mathtt{z}_{s}}{\partial s}\bigr\rangle=\bigl\langle\nabla f(\mathtt{z}_{s}),\,\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x}\bigr\rangle
.
We integrate along a linear movement from
\mathtt{x}
to
\mathtt{z}
. Using the fact that
\nabla f(\mathtt{z}_{s})=\mathtt{A}^{t}\mathtt{A}(\mathtt{x}+s(\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x}))-\mathtt{A}^{t}\mathtt{b}=\nabla f(\mathtt{x})+s\mathtt{A}^{t}\mathtt{A}(\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x})
, we obtain
f(\mathtt{z})=f(\mathtt{x})+\int_{0}^{1}\langle\nabla f(\mathtt{z}_{s}),\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x}\rangle\,\mathtt{d}s=f(\mathtt{x})+\int_{0}^{1}\langle\nabla f(\mathtt{x}),\,\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x}\rangle\,\mathtt{d}s+\int_{0}^{1}s\bigl\langle\mathtt{A}^{t}\mathtt{A}(\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x}),\,\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x}\,\bigr\rangle\,\mathtt{d}s=f(\mathtt{x})+\bigl[\langle\nabla f(\mathtt{x}),\,\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x}\rangle\cdot s\bigr]^{1}_{0}+\bigl[\frac{1}{2}\|\mathtt{A}(\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x})\|^{2}\cdot s^{2}\bigr]^{1}_{0}=f(\mathtt{x})+\langle\nabla f(\mathtt{x}),\,\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|\mathtt{A}(\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x})\|^{2}
. It follows immediately that
f(\mathtt{x})-f(\mathtt{z})=-\langle\nabla f(\mathtt{x}),\,\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\|\mathtt{A}(\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x})\|^{2}
. By (
4
), we can add
g(\mathtt{x})-g(\mathtt{z})
to the left side of this equation and
\langle\zeta_{1},\mathtt{x}\rangle-\langle\zeta_{2},\mathtt{z}\rangle
to the right side. We get
F(\mathtt{x})-F(\mathtt{z})=-\frac{1}{2}\|\mathtt{A}(\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x})\|^{2}-\langle\mathtt{\nabla}f(\mathtt{x}),\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x}\rangle+\langle\zeta_{1},\mathtt{x}\rangle-\langle\zeta_{2},\mathtt{z}\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\|\mathtt{A}(\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x})\|^{2}-\langle\mathtt{\nabla}f(\mathtt{x}),\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x}\rangle+\langle\zeta_{1},\mathtt{x}\rangle-\langle\zeta_{2},\mathtt{z}\rangle-\langle\zeta_{1},\mathtt{z}\rangle+\langle\zeta_{1},\mathtt{z}\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\|\mathtt{A}(\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x})\|^{2}-\langle\mathtt{\nabla}f(\mathtt{x})+\zeta_{1},\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x}\rangle+\langle\mathtt{\zeta}_{1}-\zeta_{2},\mathtt{z}\rangle
. This completes the proof of (
3
).

Consider (iii). By (
3
) with
\mathtt{x}=\mathtt{x}_{r}
and
\zeta_{1}=\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}
, we get

\displaystyle F(\mathtt{x}_{r})-F(\mathtt{z})

\displaystyle=~-\frac{1}{2}\|\mathtt{A}(\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x}_{r})\|^{2}-\langle\mathtt{\nabla}f(\mathtt{x}_{r})+\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle+\langle\mathtt{\zeta}_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}-\zeta_{2},\mathtt{z}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq~-\langle\mathtt{\nabla}f(\mathtt{x}_{r})+\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle+\langle\mathtt{\zeta}_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}-\zeta_{2},\mathtt{z}\rangle.

(5)

Take any
i\in[n]
. By Lemma
1
(i)(a), if
(\mathtt{z})_{i}>0
, then
(\zeta_{2})_{i}=\eta\geq(\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}})_{i}
, and if
(\mathtt{z})_{i}<0
, then
(\zeta_{2})_{i}=-\eta\leq(\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}})_{i}
. As a result,
(\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}-\zeta_{2})_{i}\cdot(\mathtt{z})_{i}\leq 0
for all

, proving that
\langle\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}-\zeta_{2},\mathtt{z}\rangle\leq 0
. Substituting

into (

F(\mathtt{x}_{r})-F(\mathtt{z})\leq-\langle\mathtt{\nabla}f(\mathtt{x}_{r})+\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle=-\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle
. This proves (iii).

Consider (iv). Let
\xi
be any subgradient in
\partial g(\mathtt{y}_{r})
that satisfies Lemma
4
(i). By (
3
) with
\mathtt{x}=\mathtt{y}_{r}
,
\zeta_{1}=\xi
,
\mathtt{z}=\mathtt{x}_{r}
, and

(6)

By (
3

,

,

, and

.

\displaystyle=-\langle\mathtt{\nabla}f(\mathtt{x}_{r})+\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{y}_{r}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle+\langle\mathtt{\zeta}_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}-\mathtt{\xi},\mathtt{y}_{r}\rangle+\langle\mathtt{\zeta}_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}-\xi,\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle

Lemma 5

Let
\mathtt{n}_{r}
be any unit conical combination of
\{-\mathrm{sign}((\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r}))_{i})\cdot\mathtt{e}_{i}:i\in E_{r}\}
. Let
\mathtt{y}_{r}
be the point in direction
\mathtt{n}_{r}
from
\mathtt{x}_{r}
that minimizes
F
.
Let
\mathtt{n}_{*}=(\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r})/\|\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\|
. If
F(\mathtt{x}_{r})>F(\mathtt{x}_{*})+\varepsilon\|\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\|^{2}
for some
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, then
\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{y}_{r}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle/\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle\geq\varepsilon\cdot\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{r}\rangle^{2}/\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{*}\rangle^{2}
.

Proof

By Lemma
4
(i), there exists
\xi\in\partial g(\mathtt{y}_{r})
such that
\langle\mathtt{\nabla}f(\mathtt{y}_{r})+\xi,\mathtt{n}_{r}\rangle=0
. We have
\langle\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{y}_{r}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle-\langle\xi,\mathtt{y}_{r}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle=\langle\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}-\xi,\mathtt{y}_{r}\rangle-\langle\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}-\xi,\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle
which is zero by Lemma
4
(ii). It implies that
\langle\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{r}\rangle=\langle\xi,\mathtt{n}_{r}\rangle
, and hence
\langle\mathtt{\nabla}f(\mathtt{y}_{r})+\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{r}\rangle=0
. Substituting
\nabla f(\mathtt{y}_{r})
by
\mathtt{A}^{t}\mathtt{A}\mathtt{y}_{r}-\mathtt{A}^{t}\mathtt{b}
, we obtain
\langle\mathtt{A}^{t}\mathtt{A}\mathtt{y}_{r}-\mathtt{A}^{t}\mathtt{b}+\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{r}\rangle=0
. Rearranging terms gives
\langle\mathtt{A}^{t}\mathtt{A}(\mathtt{y}_{r}-\mathtt{x}_{r}),\mathtt{n}_{r}\rangle=\langle-\mathtt{A}^{t}\mathtt{A}\mathtt{x}_{r}+\mathtt{A}^{t}\mathtt{b}-\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{r}\rangle=\langle-\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r})-\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{r}\rangle=\langle-\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{r}\rangle
.
Therefore,
\|\mathtt{y}_{r}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\|\cdot\|\mathtt{A}\mathtt{n}_{r}\|^{2}=\langle\mathtt{A}^{t}\mathtt{A}(\mathtt{y}_{r}-\mathtt{x}_{r}),\mathtt{n}_{r}\rangle=\langle-\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{r}\rangle
. Hence,
\|\mathtt{y}_{r}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\|\geq\langle-\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{r}\rangle
as
\|\mathtt{A}\mathtt{n}_{r}\|^{2}\leq\|\mathtt{A}\|^{2}\leq 1
.

By Lemma
4
(iii),
F(\mathtt{x}_{r})-F(\mathtt{x}_{*})\leq\|\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\|\cdot\langle-\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{*}\rangle
. If
\langle-\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{*}\rangle<\varepsilon\|\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\|
, then
F(\mathtt{x}_{r})-F(\mathtt{x}_{*})\leq\varepsilon\|\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\|^{2}
, contradicting the assumption of the lemma. Hence,
\langle-\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{*}\rangle\geq\varepsilon\|\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\|
. Combining this inequality with
\|\mathtt{y}_{r}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\|\geq\langle-\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{r}\rangle
gives

\frac{\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{y}_{r}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle}{\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle}=\frac{\|\mathtt{y}_{r}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\|}{\|\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\|}\cdot\frac{\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{r}\rangle}{\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{*}\rangle}\geq\varepsilon\cdot\frac{\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{r}\rangle^{2}}{\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{*}\rangle^{2}}
. ∎

Lemma 6

Let
G_{r}
be the set of the
\tau_{r+1}
heaviest elements in
E_{r}
. Let
H_{r}=G_{r}\cup(\mathrm{supp}(\mathtt{x}_{*})\cap E_{r})
.
Then,
\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle=\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}\!\downarrow\!H_{r},\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle
.

Proof

For any
i\in H_{r}
,
(\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}})_{i}=(\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}\!\downarrow\!H_{r})_{i}
by definition. Therefore,
(\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}})_{i}\cdot(\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r})_{i}=(\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}\!\downarrow\!H_{r})_{i}\cdot(\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r})_{i}
.

Take any
i\not\in E_{r}
. We have
(\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}})_{i}=0
because
\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}=\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}+\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r})
and
(\zeta_{\mathtt{x}_{r}})_{i}=-(\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r}))_{i}
by definition. Therefore, both
(\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}})_{i}\cdot(\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r})_{i}
and
(\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}\!\downarrow\!H_{r})_{i}\cdot(\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r})_{i}
are zero.

For any
i\in E_{r}\setminus H_{r}
,
i\not\in\mathrm{supp}(\mathtt{x}_{*})
as
\mathrm{supp}(\mathtt{x}_{*})\cap E_{r}\subseteq H_{r}
. So
(\mathtt{x}_{*})_{i}=0
. Also,
(\mathtt{x}_{r})_{i}=0
as
i\in E_{r}
. So
(\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}})_{i}\cdot(\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r})_{i}
and
(\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}\!\downarrow\!H_{r})_{i}\cdot(\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r})_{i}
are zero. ∎

Lemma 7

If
F(\mathtt{x}_{r})>F(\mathtt{x}_{*})+\varepsilon\|\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\|^{2}
, then

\frac{F(\mathtt{x}_{r+1})-F(\mathtt{x}_{*})}{F(\mathtt{x}_{r})-F(\mathtt{x}_{*})}\leq 1-\frac{\varepsilon\tau_{r+1}}{8(s+\tau_{r+1})\ln\tau_{r+1}}.

Proof

Let
G_{r}
be the set of the
\tau_{r+1}
heaviest elements in
E_{r}
. Let
H_{r}=G_{r}\cup(\mathrm{supp}(\mathtt{x}_{*})\cap E_{r})
.
We prove in Lemma
10
in Appendix
0.B

that there exists a unit descent direction
{\mathtt{n}}_{r}
from
\mathtt{x}_{r}
such that
\mathtt{n}_{r}
is a conical combination of
\{-\mathrm{sign}((\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r}))_{i})\cdot\mathtt{e}_{i}:i\in G_{r}\}
and
\langle-\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},{\mathtt{n}}_{r}\rangle\geq\|\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}\!\downarrow\!H_{r}\|\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\tau_{r+1}}{4(s+\tau_{r+1})\ln\tau_{r+1}}}
. Let
\mathtt{n}_{*}=(\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r})/\|\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\|
. By Lemma
6
,
\langle-\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{*}\rangle=\langle-\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}\!\downarrow\!H_{r},\mathtt{n}_{*}\rangle
. Then,
\langle-\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{*}\rangle\leq\|\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}}\!\downarrow\!H_{r}\|
. Hence,

\frac{\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{r}\rangle}{\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{*}\rangle}\geq\sqrt{\frac{\tau_{r+1}}{4(s+\tau_{r+1})\ln\tau_{r+1}}}
.

Let
\mathtt{y}_{r}
be the point in the direction
\mathtt{n}_{r}
from
\mathtt{x}_{r}
that minimizes
F
. We have

\frac{F(\mathtt{x}_{r+1})-F(\mathtt{x}_{*})}{F(\mathtt{x}_{r})-F(\mathtt{x}_{*})}=1-\frac{F(\mathtt{x}_{r})-F(\mathtt{x}_{r+1})}{F(\mathtt{x}_{r})-F(\mathtt{x}_{*})}\leq 1-\frac{F(\mathtt{x}_{r})-F(\mathtt{y}_{r})}{F(\mathtt{x}_{r})-F(\mathtt{x}_{*})}
. By Lemma
4
(iii) and (iv),
F(\mathtt{x}_{r})-F(\mathtt{y}_{r})=-\frac{1}{2}\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{y}_{r}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle
and
F(\mathtt{x}_{r})-F(\mathtt{x}_{*})\leq-\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle
. Therefore,
\frac{F(\mathtt{x}_{r+1})-F(\mathtt{x}_{*})}{F(\mathtt{x}_{r})-F(\mathtt{x}_{*})}\leq 1-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{y}_{r}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle}{\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\rangle}\leq 1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\frac{\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{r}\rangle^{2}}{\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{*}\rangle^{2}}
by Lemma

, which is at most
1-\frac{\varepsilon\tau_{r+1}}{8(s+\tau_{r+1})\ln\tau_{r+1}}
by the lower bound on
\frac{\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{r}\rangle}{\langle\gamma_{\mathtt{x}_{r}},\mathtt{n}_{*}\rangle}
. ∎

Recall the parameter
h\in(1,2]
in Algorithm
1
. The next result bounds the working set sizes up to the first solution with an additive error at most
\varepsilon/\eta^{2}
.

Theorem 4.1

Suppose that
\|\mathtt{A}\|\leq 1
and
\eta=\alpha\|\mathtt{A}^{t}\mathtt{b}\|_{\infty}
for a fixed
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Scale space such that
\|b\|=1
.
Let
\kappa+1
be the minimum index such that
F(\mathtt{x}_{\kappa+1})-F(\mathtt{x}_{*})\leq\varepsilon/\eta^{2}
.

•

If
h\leq 2^{O(\varepsilon/(\ln n\ln(\eta/\varepsilon)))}
, then
\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa}\tau_{i}=O\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(s+\tau)\log(s+\tau)\log\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon}\right)
.

•

Otherwise,
\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa}\tau_{i}=O\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}k\log k\log\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon}\right)
.

Proof

Take any constant
c\geq 1
. Divide
[\kappa]
into two disjoint subsets
I
and
J
such that for all
i\in I
,
\tau_{i}\leq cs
, and for all
i\in J
,
\tau_{i}>cs
.

For any
\mathtt{x}_{r}
,
\eta\|\mathtt{x}_{r}\|\leq\eta\|\mathtt{x}_{r}\|_{1}\leq F(\mathtt{x}_{r})\leq F(\mathtt{x}_{0})=\frac{1}{2}\|\mathtt{b}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}
. The same argument works for
\mathtt{x}_{*}
. It means that
\varepsilon\|\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\|^{2}\leq\varepsilon/\eta^{2}
. Therefore, for any
r\in[\kappa]
,
F(\mathtt{x}_{r})-F(\mathtt{x}_{*})>\varepsilon/\eta^{2}\geq\varepsilon\|\mathtt{x}_{*}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\|^{2}
by assumption, which makes Lemma
7
applicable for all
r\in[\kappa]
.

View
I
as a chronological sequence. Let
i
be the largest index in
I
. Note that
F(\mathtt{x}_{0})-F(\mathtt{x}_{*})\leq F(\mathtt{x}_{0})\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\mathtt{b}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}
. Then, by Lemma
7
,
F(\mathtt{x}_{i})-F(\mathtt{x}_{*})\leq\frac{1}{2}\prod_{i\in I}\bigl(1-\frac{\varepsilon\tau_{i}}{8(s+\tau_{i})\ln(cs)}\bigr)
, which is at most
\frac{1}{2}\prod_{i\in I}\bigl(1-\frac{\varepsilon\tau_{i}}{8(c+1)s\ln(cs)}\bigr)
. Let
\tau_{\text{avg}}=\sum_{i\in I}\tau_{i}/|I|
. It is well known that the geometric mean is at most the arithmetic mean. Therefore,
\frac{1}{2}\prod_{i\in I}\bigl(1-\frac{\varepsilon\tau_{i}}{8(c+1)s\ln(cs)}\bigr)\leq\frac{1}{2}\bigl(1-\frac{\varepsilon\tau_{\text{avg}}}{8(c+1)s\ln(cs)}\bigr)^{|I|}\leq\frac{1}{2}e^{-\varepsilon\tau_{\text{avg}}|I|/(8(c+1)s\ln(cs))}
. This upper bound is at least
\varepsilon/\eta^{2}
so that
\mathtt{x}_{\kappa+1}
is the first solution that satisfies
F(\mathtt{x}_{\kappa+1})-F(\mathtt{x}_{*})\leq\varepsilon/\eta^{2}
. Hence,
\frac{\varepsilon\tau_{\text{avg}}|I|}{8(c+1)s\ln(cs)}\leq\ln\frac{\eta^{2}}{2\varepsilon}
, which implies that
\sum_{i\in I}\tau_{i}=\tau_{\text{avg}}|I|=O\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}s\log s\log\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon}\right)
.

View
J
as a chronological sequence. Let
\tau_{\max}=\max_{i\in J}\tau_{i}
. Take a contiguous subsequence of
J
of length
(16\ln\tau_{\max})/\varepsilon
. Let
i
and
j
be the minimum and maximum indices in this subsequence, respectively. By Lemma
7
,
F(\mathtt{x}_{j})-F(\mathtt{x}_{*})\leq e^{-1}\cdot(F(\mathtt{x}_{i})-F(\mathtt{x}_{*}))
. Since
F(\mathtt{x}_{0})-F(\mathtt{x}_{*})\leq F(\mathtt{x}_{0})=\frac{1}{2}\|\mathtt{b}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}
, we can divide
J
into no more than
\ln\frac{\eta^{2}}{2\varepsilon}
contiguous subsequences of length
(16\ln\tau_{\max})/\varepsilon
. It follows that
|J|\leq\frac{16}{\varepsilon}\ln\tau_{\max}\cdot\ln\frac{\eta^{2}}{2\varepsilon}
.
The algorithm ensures that
\tau_{i+1}\leq h\tau_{i}
. Extract the longest subsequence of
J
(not necessarily contiguous) in which
\tau_{i}
strictly increases. Every consecutive
\tau_{i}
’s in this subsequence differ by a factor
h
. This subsequence starts with
\min_{i\in J}\tau_{i}\leq\max\{hcs,\tau\}
. If
h\leq 2^{O(\varepsilon/(\ln n\ln(\eta/\varepsilon)))}
, then
\tau_{\max}\leq h^{|J|}\cdot\max\{hcs,\tau\}\leq 2^{O(1)}\cdot(s+\tau)
. So
\sum_{i\in J}\tau_{i}\leq O(s+\tau)\cdot|J|=O\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(s+\tau)\log(s+\tau)\log\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon}\right)
.
If
h>2^{O(\varepsilon/(\ln n\ln(\eta/\varepsilon)))}
, we still have
\tau_{\max}\leq k
and hence
\sum_{i\in J}\tau_{i}\leq k|J|=O\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}k\log k\log\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon}\right)
. ∎

Remark 1.
Clearly
\kappa\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa}\tau_{i}
. One can work out the exact upper bound for
\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa}\tau
and hence
\kappa
. DWS can be stopped after
\kappa
iterations to obtain an error at most
\varepsilon/\eta^{2}
, although DWS has probably terminated earlier in practice.

Remark 2.
Starting from
p_{0}
, we add at most
\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa}\tau_{i}
free variables to any working set before reaching
\mathtt{x}_{\kappa+1}
. Suppose that we set
p_{0}
and
\tau
to be
O(1)
. If
\varepsilon
is given beforehand, we can ensure that every working set has
O(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}s\log s\log\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon})
variables before reaching
\mathtt{x}_{\kappa}
. So each call of the solver runs provably faster than using all
n
variables. When
\varepsilon
is not given, if
k=\Theta(s\log(n/s))
(sufficient for the true signal to be recovered with high probability), every working set still has only
O(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}s\cdot\mathrm{polylog}(n))
variables.
Clearly,
|\mathrm{supp}(\mathtt{x}_{r})|\leq|W_{r}|
. It follows that
|\mathrm{supp}(\mathtt{x}_{r})|\leq p_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa}\tau_{i}
.
We conclude that all solutions
\mathtt{x}_{r}
,
r\in[\kappa+1]
, are provably sparse if
\varepsilon
is given beforehand or
k=\Theta(s\log(n/s))
.

Remark 3.
Figure
4
shows that the working set sizes are at most
cs
for some small constant
c
in the experiments. That is,
\max_{i\in[\kappa]}\tau_{i}=O(s)
. Under this assumption, the proof of Theorem
4.1
reveals that

is not given beforehand. Then, the working set sizes and support set sizes can be bounded by
O(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}s\log s\log\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon})
even if
\varepsilon
is not given beforehand.

Remark 4.

To prepare for the next iteration, we need to compute
\nabla f(\mathtt{x}_{r})=\mathtt{A}^{t}\mathtt{A}\mathtt{x}_{r}-\mathtt{A}^{t}\mathtt{b}
. We precompute
\mathtt{A}^{t}\mathtt{b}
in

time. Let

. Note that

from

corresponding to

is
O(k|W_{r+1}|)=O(k\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa}\tau_{i})
time.

Remark 5.

If

Lemma 9

Take any
c\leq 1/\sqrt{2}
. Let
\mathtt{z}
be a vector in

. Suppose that there is a set

of unit vectors in

are mutually orthogonal, and for every

\mathtt{y}
of the vectors in

.

Proof

Let
\theta=\arccos\left(c|V|^{-1/2}\right)
. If
\theta\leq\pi/3
, we can pick any vector
\mathtt{n}\in V
as
\mathtt{y}
because

for any

. Suppose that

W
be a maximal subset of

whose size is a power of 2. Arbitrarily label the vectors in

. Refer to Figure
9
. By assumption,
\mathtt{n}_{1}\perp\mathtt{n}_{2}

\rho
be the non-acute angle between the plane spanned by

\{\mathtt{n}_{1,2},\mathtt{z}\}

\cos\theta\leq\cos\angle(\mathtt{n}_{2},\mathtt{z})=\cos\phi\cos(\pi/4)+\sin\phi\sin(\pi/4)\cos\rho
.
Note that
\cos\rho\leq 0
as

. So
\cos\phi\geq\sec(\pi/4)\cos\theta=\sqrt{2}\cos\theta
.
The same analysis holds between
\mathtt{z}
and the unit vector

.

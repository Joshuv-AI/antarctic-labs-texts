Alleviating missing boundary conditions in elliptic partial differential equations using interior point measurements Thanks: The first author was supported in part by NSF grant DMS-2409807. Thanks: The second author was supported in part by NSF grant DMS-2012326.
Andrea Bonito, Alan Demlow, Joshua M. Siktar

Abstract
. We consider an optimal recovery problem for the Poisson problem when the boundary data is unknown. Compensating information is provided in the form of a finite number of measurements of the solution. A finite element algorithm for this problem was given in [ 9 ] , where measurements were assumed to be either bounded linear functionals of the solution or point measurements at locations lying anywhere in the closure of the computational domain. In contrast, we focus on the case of point measurements at locations lying in the interior of the domain. This lowers the regularity requirements placed on the solution. Also, a key ingredient in the recovery process is the finite element approximation of Riesz representers associated with the measurements. Our main result is a pointwise error estimate for the Riesz representers. We apply this to obtain improved estimates which measure the performance of the recovery algorithm in various norms.

1.
Introduction

This paper focuses on the incomplete Poisson problem

(1.1)

-\bigtriangleup u\ =\ f\qquad\text{in}\ \ \Omega,

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n=2,3
is a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary, and
f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
is given. When for a given function
g\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
the system (
1.1
) is supplemented by the boundary conditions

(1.2)

u=g\qquad\textrm{on}\ \ \Gamma:=\partial\Omega,

the Lax-Milgram theory applies and guarantees a unique solution
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. We assume that we are given complete knowledge of
f
, but are lacking precise knowledge of the boundary data
g
and thus are unable to uniquely determine
u
. This setting is motivated by applications in which the inability to measure boundary data rises, including in fluid dynamics
[
21
,
37
,
58
]
; wind engineering
[
50
,
51
]
, and inverse heat conduction
[
6
,
35
]
, among other areas.

Our goal is to find a “best possible” version of
u
given certain information that further narrows possible choices. Following the framework from
[
9
]
, we first assume that
g
belongs to a compact subset
K^{B}
of
H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
. Given a constant
C_{B}>0
and
s>\frac{1}{2}
, let

(1.3)

K^{B}\ :=\ \{g\in H^{s}(\Gamma)\ |\ \|g\|_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}\leq C_{B}\}.

To alleviate the missing information on the boundary data, we further assume that
m
pointwise measurements of
u
inside
\Omega
are provided. More precisely, we assume to know the locations
x_{i}\in\Omega
and the values
\omega_{i}=\lambda_{i}(u):=u(x_{i})
,
i=1,...,m
. Note that this requires the interior continuity
u\in C^{0}(\Omega)
.

For
g\in K^{B}
, we let
u(g)
be the solution to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) and let

\mathcal{K}\ :=\ \{u(g)\ |\ g\in K^{B}\}

be the (compact) set of
H^{1}(\Omega)
consisting of all functions that satisfy the incomplete Poisson problem (
1.1
) with boundary data in
K^{B}
.
It is sometimes referred to as the model class or the prior.
All known information available to recover
u
is encoded in

(1.4)

\mathcal{K}_{\omega}\ :=\ \{u\in\mathcal{K}\ |\ \lambda_{i}(u)=\omega_{i},\ i=1,...,m\}.

Notice that any function in
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
could be the function from which the measurements were taken. In particular, we cannot expect a recovery algorithm to distinguish between them. Instead, we say that
u^{*}\in X
is an
optimal recovery
in a Banach space
X
with semi-norm
|.|
for functions in
\mathcal{K}\subset X
, if

\sup_{v\in\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}|v-u^{*}|_{X}\leq\sup_{v\in\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}|v-u|_{X},\qquad\forall u\in X.

In this work, we focus on three cases,
X=H^{1}(\Omega)
with
|.|_{X}=\|.\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
,
X=L_{\infty}(\Omega)
with
\|.\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}
, and
X=\{v\in L_{2}(\Omega)\ |\ v|_{\Omega_{d}}\in L_{\infty}(\Omega_{d})\}
with
|.|_{X}=\|.\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega_{d})}
and where
\Omega_{d}
is a strict subset of
\Omega
containing the location of the measurements.

The theoretical formulation of optimal recovery problems, and more broadly the topic of optimal learning, have been studied extensively.
The center of a ball
B(\mathcal{K}_{\omega})_{X}
in
X
of smallest radius that contains the set
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
is an optimal recovery in
X
and is called a Chebyshev center. The corresponding optimal recovery error is the radius
R(\mathcal{K}_{\omega})_{X}
of that ball, referred to as the Chebyshev radius. We refer to the classical texts
[
12
,
46
,
47
,
57
]
for more details and to
[
10
]
for a discussion on their approximations. See also
[
10
,
11
,
44
]
for optimal least squares-based approximation schemes.

In the specific case of recovering the solution to the PDE (
1.1
), it is convenient to decompose
u=u_{0}+u_{\mathcal{H}}
where
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\Omega)
is the (completely determined) solution of (
1.1
) supplemented with the vanishing boundary condition
u_{0}=0
on
\Gamma
and
u_{\mathcal{H}}
is a harmonic function that satisfies the measurements

(1.5)

u_{\mathcal{H}}(x_{i})\ =\ \omega_{i}-u_{0}(x_{i})=:\widetilde{\omega}_{i}.

Furthermore, among all harmonic functions satisfying these measurements, the one with minimal norm in
H^{s}(\Gamma)
is an optimal recovery for the harmonic part of the solution
[
9
]
.

The minimal norm property is exploited in
[
5
]
to design a kernel method algorithm for the approximation of an optimal recovery of general, possibly nonlinear, partial differential equations (PDEs).
Instead, in
[
9
]
, the authors focus on the PDE (
1.1
) and take advantage of the representation

(1.6)

u_{\mathcal{H}}^{*}\ =\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}U_{i}\phi_{i},

where the coefficients
U_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
i=1.,..,m
, are determined by the constraints
u_{\mathcal{H}}^{*}(x_{i})=\tilde{\omega}_{i}
. For
i=1,...,m
, the function
\phi_{i}\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega)
is the Riesz representer of the measurement
\lambda_{i}
in the sense that

(1.7)

\langle\phi_{i},v\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega)}\ =\ \lambda_{i}(v),\ v\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega),

where
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega)
is the Hilbert space of harmonic functions with
H^{s}(\Gamma)
traces (equipped with the
H^{s}(\Gamma)
scalar product). Consequently, the algorithm proposed in
[
9
]
consists of approximating
u_{0}
,
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
, and
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
using a finite element method.

We consider variations of two main assumptions made in the predecessor work
[
9
]
: The manner in which the recovery error is measured and the location of the measurement points
\{x_{i}\}
. First, in
[
9
]
the recovery error was measured in
X=H^{1}(\Omega)
. We additionally consider recovery in
X=L_{\infty}(\Omega)
, which as a non-Hilbertian Banach space requires a more general theoretical framework. More precisely, when
X
is not a uniformly convex Banach space such as when
X=L_{\infty}(\Omega)
, the Chebyshev ball
B(\mathcal{K}_{\omega})_{X}
may not be unique and there thus may exist multiple optimal recovery functions
[
24
]
. Finally, we also consider recovery of
u
in
L_{\infty}(\Omega_{d})
, where
\Omega_{d}:=\{x\in\Omega:{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)>d\}
lies on the interior of
\Omega
a uniform distance
d
away from
\Gamma
. Note that in all cases the algorithm we obtain is
near-optimal
, that is, an optimal recovery can be established for any measurement data with the error bounded by a constant only depending on
R(\mathcal{K}_{\omega})
. A precise definition is given in Subsection
3.3
.

We also place an important restriction on the placement of the measurement points
\{x_{i}\}
. Whereas in
[
9
]
the measurement points are allowed to lie anywhere in
\overline{\Omega}
, we require that they lie in
\Omega_{d}
; cf.
[
35
]
for an example in which sensors need to be placed inside of
\Omega
. To understand the consequences of this difference, we note that the Riesz representers
\{\phi_{i}\}
can be calculated via a two-step process. Let
E:H^{1/2}(\Gamma)\rightarrow H^{1}(\Omega)
be the harmonic extension operator. First one seeks
\psi_{i}\in H^{s}(\Gamma)
satisfying

(1.8)

\langle\psi_{i},v\rangle=\lambda_{i}(v)=Ev(x_{i}),~~v\in H^{s}(\Gamma).

Then
\phi_{i}=E\psi_{i}
.

If
x_{i}\in\Gamma
, then
\psi_{i}
is a Green’s function for an
H^{s}
-elliptic problem. It is necessary to assume that
s>\frac{n-1}{2}
in order to guarantee that the functional
\lambda_{i}=Ev(x_{i})
lies in
H^{-s}(\Gamma)
, and even if this assumption holds the smoothness of
\lambda_{i}
and thus of
\psi_{i}
and
\phi_{i}
is highly limited regardless of the smoothness of
\Gamma
. Let
\{U_{i,h}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be the approximations to the coefficients
\{U_{i}\}
obtained from the finite element algorithm. In assessing the discretization error in the recovery process, it is necessary to control
\max_{i=1}^{M}|U_{i}-U_{i,h}|
and thus
\max_{1\leq i,j\leq m}|(\phi_{i}-\phi_{i,h})(x_{j})|
independent of the choice of
X
. In
[
9
]
it is shown that when sufficient regularity is present and the coefficients
U_{i}
can be stably determined, there holds for any
\epsilon>0

(1.9)

|U_{i}-U_{i,h}|\leq Ch^{s-\frac{n-1}{2}-\epsilon},

with the parameters
\frac{1}{2}<s\leq 1
excluded when
n=3
as previously noted.

In contrast, when
x_{i}
lies in the interior of
\Omega
the smoothness of the functional
\lambda_{i}
is limited only by the smoothness of
\Gamma
. We thus may allow
\frac{1}{2}<s<\frac{3}{2}
independent of space dimension. On the other hand, the smoothness of
\lambda_{i}
degenerates as
x_{i}\rightarrow\Gamma
and
\psi_{i}
thus approaches a boundary Green’s function. In summary, we often obtain higher smoothness and thus a higher order of convergence in our finite element algorithm when
x_{i}\in\Omega
, but with the caveat that error estimates show deterioration as the measurement points approach
\Gamma
. More precisely, when
\Gamma
is sufficiently smooth and
x_{i}\in\Omega_{d}=\{x\in\Omega:{\rm dist}(x,\Gamma)>d\}
,
i=1,...,m
, and
U_{i,h}
is the set of coefficients obtained from the finite element algorithm, we show that when
d\geq c_{0}h
with
c_{0}
sufficiently large

|U_{i}-U_{i,h}|\leq Cd^{-n}(h^{2-\epsilon}+h^{4-2s}).

The rates of convergence with respect to
h
thus obtained are higher than those from
[
9
]
quoted above in (
1.9
) assuming
x_{i}\in\overline{\Omega}
for any
\frac{1}{2}<s<\frac{3}{2}
. On the other hand, the estimates indicate that it is necessary for
h
to sufficiently resolve
d
in order for these higher convergence rates to offset the negative powers of
d
in the estimates. For example, in the case
n=2
and
s=1
we obtain
d^{-n}h^{2-\epsilon}
whereas (
1.9
) yields
h^{1/2-\epsilon}
. Our numerical experiments confirm that better convergence rates are indeed obtained when the measurement points lie in
\Omega_{d}
instead of
\overline{\Omega}
.

We now outline the contents of the remainder of this paper. Section
2
includes needed notation for the paper and preliminary results. Next, Section
3
details the algorithm used for computing an optimal recovery solution through the Riesz representers. Section
4
contains details of finite element methods used to implement this algorithm, while Section
5
outlines regularity assumptions and results needed in our analysis. The core part of the paper is Section
6
, where we apply the results of the previous sections to the finite element setting by proving refined pointwise and
H^{1}
estimates on the Riesz representers and using these to obtain convergence estimates for the optimal recovery. To conclude, Section
7
includes numerical experiments.

2.
Notations and Preliminaries

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain. In order to simplify the discussion and avoid accounting for the approximation of
\Omega
, we assume that
\Omega
is polygonal when
n=2
, and polyhedral when
n=3
. We postpone to Section
6.5
a discussion on the extension of the results presented below to smooth domains.

2.1.
Function spaces

We now briefly introduce standard functional spaces and refer to
[
1
,
25
,
30
,
42
]
for more details.
For an open set
\mathcal{O}\subset\Omega
or
\mathcal{O}\subset\Gamma
, the space of continuous functions on
\mathcal{O}
is denoted
C^{0}(\mathcal{O})
. Among those, we let
C^{0}(\overline{\mathcal{O}})
be the space of functions that can be continuously extended to the closure of
\mathcal{O}
. We use the standard notation
L_{p}(\mathcal{O})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
to denote the standard Lebesgue spaces equipped with the standard norms
\|.\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{O})}
.
The
L_{2}(\mathcal{O})
scalar product is denoted
(.,.)_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}
, and we write
H^{\sigma}(\mathcal{O})
,
0<\sigma<\infty
to denote the Sobolev spaces. For a positive integer
k
, we set

(2.1)

\|v\|^{2}_{H^{k}(\mathcal{O})}\ :=\ \sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\|\partial^{\alpha}v\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathcal{O})},

where
\partial^{\alpha}
are tangential derivatives when
\mathcal{O}\subset\Gamma
.
For a non-integral
\sigma>0
, we define

(2.2)

\|v\|^{2}_{H^{\sigma}(\mathcal{O})}\ :=\ \|v\|^{2}_{H^{k}(\mathcal{O})}+\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\iint_{\mathcal{O}\times\mathcal{O}}\frac{|\partial^{\alpha}v(x)-\partial^{\alpha}v(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{p+2(\sigma-k)}}dxdy,

where
k:=\lfloor{\sigma\rfloor}
, and
p=n
when
\mathcal{O}\subset\Omega
and
p=n-1
when
\mathcal{O}\subset\Gamma
.
The corresponding scalar products are denoted
\langle.,.\rangle_{H^{\sigma}(\mathcal{O})}
.
We shall also need
H^{-1}(\Omega)
to denote the dual space of
H^{1}(\Omega)
. Finally, we let
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
denote the functions in
H^{1}(\Omega)
with zero trace on
\Gamma
.

2.2.
Trace and extension operators

The trace operator
T
is defined for
w\in C(\overline{\Omega})
as the restriction of
w
to the boundary
\Gamma
, and then subsequently extended to Sobolev functions by density. Thus if
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
we can denote

(2.3)

v_{\Gamma}\ :=\ T(v)\ =\ v|_{\Gamma}.

When no confusion is possible, we shall simply write
v
instead of
Tv
or
v|_{\Gamma}
. This motivates the trace definition of the Fractional Sobolev spaces on the boundary,
H_{T}^{\sigma}(\Gamma)
: for any
\sigma\geq\frac{1}{2}
, we define

(2.4)

H_{T}^{\sigma}(\Gamma)\ :=\ T(H^{\sigma+1/2}(\Omega)),

with the underlying trace norm

(2.5)

\|g\|_{H_{T}^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}\ :=\ \min\Big\{\|v\|_{H^{\sigma+1/2}(\Omega)}\ |\ v_{\Gamma}\ =\ g\Big\}.

When
\Omega
has a smooth boundary and
\sigma\geq\frac{1}{2}
, it is well-known that the intrinsic definition (
2.2
) and the trace definition (
2.4
), (
2.5
) of boundary Sobolev spaces are equivalent, i.e. the spaces coincide and the norms are equivalent
[
42
]
. The interplay between the two definitions is more subtle in the case of nonsmooth domains. In
[
9
]
it is stated without rigorous proof that the two definitions are equivalent on polyhedral domains when
\frac{1}{2}<\sigma<\frac{3}{2}
. This equivalence is not needed in the analysis presented in the following, which frees us from such a restriction on
s
. However, we need a related but weaker result that generally requires
\sigma<\frac{3}{2}
on polyhedral domains (the focus of this paper; see Section
6.5
for extensions). But because fractional spaces
H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)
are degenerate on polyhedral surfaces when
\sigma\geq\frac{3}{2}
, such an assumption is not restrictive.

We also define for
\frac{1}{2}<\sigma<\frac{3}{2}
the harmonic function space

(2.6)

\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}(\Omega)\ :=\ \{v:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\ |\ \bigtriangleup v=0,\ v_{\Gamma}\in H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)\},

with norm

(2.7)

\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}(\Omega)}\ :=\ \|v\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}.

It is clear that
(\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}(\Omega),\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}(\Omega)})
is a Hilbert space; more information on this space is presented in
[
4
]
. Lax Milgram theory, see for instance
[
20
,
59
]
, guarantees the existence of constants
c_{L},C_{L}>0
depending on
\Omega
such that

(2.8)

c_{L}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ \|\Delta v\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}+\|v_{\Gamma}\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma)}\ \leq\ C_{L}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)},\ \qquad v\in H^{1}(\Omega).

As a consequence, there exists another constant
C_{\sigma}>0
so that

(2.9)

\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ C_{\sigma}\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}(\Omega)},\qquad{v\in\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}(\Omega)}.

We recall that we can decompose any function
u\in\mathcal{K}
as

(2.10)

u=u_{0}+u_{\mathcal{H}},

where
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\Omega)
is given by (
1.1
) for
f\in H^{{-1+\beta_{\Omega}}}(\Omega)
,
\beta_{\Omega}>\max(0,\frac{n}{2}-1)
while
u_{\mathcal{H}}
is harmonic in
\Omega
and satisfies the boundary condition (
1.2
) for some
g\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
.

This motivates the introduction of the harmonic extension operator. For any
g\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
, we define our harmonic extension operator
E:H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)\rightarrow H^{1}(\Omega)
via

(2.11)

Eg\ :=\ {\rm argmin}\{\|\nabla v\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\ |\ v_{\Gamma}=g\}.

The function
Eg
is characterized by
T(Eg)=g
and

(2.12)

\intop\limits_{\Omega}\nabla Eg\cdot\nabla v\ =\ 0,\quad v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

From the left inequality in (
2.8
), one has

(2.13)

\|Eg\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ c_{L}^{-1}\|g\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma)},\quad g\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma).

With
E
thus defined, we can alternatively write
\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}(\Omega)\ =\ \{Ev\ |\ \ v|_{\Gamma}\in H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)\}
. We postpone to Section
5
discussions on the mapping properties of the extension operator.

Finally, we recall that the unknown boundary data function
g
is assumed to belong to the compact set
K^{B}
in (
1.3
) for some
s>\frac{1}{2}
.
In view of the discussion above, we restrict the parameter
s
to
\frac{1}{2}<s<\frac{3}{2}
and define

\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{H}}:=\{v\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega)\ |\ \|v\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega)}\leq C_{B}\}=\{Eg\ :\ g\in K^{B}\}

corresponding to the functional space containing all the possible components
u_{\mathcal{H}}
in the decomposition (
2.10
).

2.3.
Measurements

We assume that the locations
x_{i}
of the measurements
\lambda_{i}
are strictly inside
\Omega
. In particular,
x_{i}\in\Omega_{d}
(
1\leq i\leq m
), where for some parameter
d
,
\Omega_{d}=\{x\in\Omega:{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)>d\}
. Whence, there is a constant
\Lambda_{0}>0
depending on
\beta_{\Omega}
and
d
such that the following estimate holds:

(2.14)

\max_{i=1,\dots,m}|\lambda_{i}(u_{0})|\ =\ \max_{i=1,...,m}|u_{0}(x_{i})|\ \leq\ \|u_{0}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega_{d})}\ \leq\ \Lambda_{0}\|f\|_{H^{-1+\beta_{\Omega}}(\Omega)}.

Similarly, there is a constant
\Lambda_{s,d}>0
such that for all
v\in\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{H}}
, we have

(2.15)

\max_{i=1,\dots,m}|\lambda_{i}(v)|\ \leq\ \max_{i=1,\dots,m}|v(x_{i})|\ \leq\ \Lambda_{s}\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ \Lambda_{s,d}C_{B}.

To establish this bound, note that
v(x_{i})=\frac{1}{B_{d}(x_{i})}\intop\limits_{B_{d}(x_{i})}v\lesssim d^{-n/2}\|v\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}
, where
B_{d}(x_{i})
is the open ball of radius
d
centered at
x_{i}
. This combined with boundedness of the harmonic extension operator
E
(see (
5.10
) below) yields the desired result. Alternatively, if
s>\frac{n-1}{2}
one can combine the maximum principle with a Sobolev embedding
H^{s}(\Gamma)\subset C(\Gamma)
in order to obtain the desired result with constant independent of
d
. Such an assumption on
s
, however, unnecessarily restricts the allowed values of
s
to
1<s<\frac{3}{2}
when
n=3
.

It will be convenient to use the following weighted
\ell^{2}
norm on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
for the vector of the measurements values, i.e.,

(2.16)

\|{\bf z}\|\ :=\ \left(\frac{1}{m}\sum^{m}_{i=1}|z_{i}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ =\ m^{-1/2}\|{\bf z}\|_{\ell_{2}},\ {\bf z}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

With this notation, for any measurements
{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}:=(\omega_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
and
u\in\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
, we have using the decomposition
u=u_{0}+u_{\mathcal{H}}
that

(2.17)

\|{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}\|\ =\ \left(\frac{1}{m}\sum^{m}_{i=1}|u(x_{i})|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ \leq\ \Lambda_{0}\|f\|_{H^{-1+\beta_{\Omega}}(\Omega)}+\Lambda_{s,d}C_{B}.

We shall also need an estimate for the modified measurement vector
\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}:=(\widetilde{\omega}_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
defined in (
1.5
), which reads

(2.18)

\|\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}\|\leq\|{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}\|+\Lambda_{0}\|f\|_{H^{t}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ 2\Lambda_{0}\|f\|_{H^{-1+\beta_{\Omega}}(\Omega)}+\Lambda_{s,d}C_{B}.

Notice the upper bound is independent of
\omega
.

3.
Optimal recovery

Our task is to recover a function
u\in\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
(see (
1.4
)) using the information provided by the measurements
\lambda_{i}(u):=u(x_{i})=\omega_{i}
,
i=1,..,m
, to alleviate the lack of information on the boundary data.
Since
g
is the only unknown data in (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), the recovery process consists mainly in determining the best possible harmonic component
u_{\mathcal{H}}\in\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{H}}
in (
2.10
) that satisfies the measurements (
1.5
).
We set
\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}:={\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}-(\lambda_{i}(u_{0}))_{i=1}^{m}
and define the functional set gathering all the information a-priori known on the harmonic component

\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\widetilde{\omega}}:=\{v\in\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{H}}\ |\ \lambda_{i}(v)=\widetilde{\omega}_{i},\ i=1,...,m\};

compare with (
1.4
).
Note that
\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{H}}\subset C^{0}(\overline{\Omega_{d}})
, thereby justifying the use of pointwise measurements located in
\Omega_{d}
.

In this section, we present near-optimal abstract algorithms based on the approximation of the minimal norm interpolant. A key ingredient is the approximation of the Riesz representers. In Section
4
, we present a practical finite element algorithm to achieve the desired approximation properties of the Riesz representers.

3.1.
Optimal Recovery in Banach spaces

We first define the notions of Chebychev radius and Chebychev center.
For a Banach space
X
and
Z\subset X
, the
Chebychev radius
of
X
with respect to
Z
is defined as

(3.1)

R(Z)_{X}\ :=\ \inf_{v\in X}\sup_{z\in Z}|v-z|_{X},

where
|.|_{X}
denotes a semi-norm in
X
.
Furthermore, a function
v^{*}\in X
is said to be a
Chebychev center
of
X
with respect to
Z
if

(3.2)

v^{*}\in\mathop{\rm argmin}_{v\in X}\sup_{z\in Z}|v-z|_{X}.

It is known that if
X
is uniformly convex and
|.|_{X}
is a norm, then the Chebychev center is unique
[
29
, Lemma 2.3]
.

The Chebychev radius is the best performance an algorithm can achieve with the partial information encoded in
K_{\omega}
. Moreover, we say that
v^{*}\in X
is the optimal recovery in
(X,|.|_{X})
of functions in
Z
if

\sup_{z\in Z}|v^{*}-z|_{X}=R(Z)_{X}.

In our setting
K=\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
and it is easy to see that in view of the decomposition (
2.10
),
u^{*}=u_{0}+u^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}
is an optimal recovery in
(X,|.|_{X})
of functions in
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
if and only if
u^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}
is an optimal recovery in
X
of functions in
\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\widetilde{\omega}}
provided
u_{0}\in X
. Therefore, we require the following assumptions.

Assumption 3.1

(on
(X,|.|_{X})
)
.

Assume that
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega)\subset X
for the topology generated by the semi-norm
|.|_{X}
, and that is there is a constant
\Lambda_{X}
such that

(3.3)

|v|_{X}\ \leq\ \Lambda_{X}\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega)},\qquad\forall v\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega).

In this work, we focus on three settings:

•

Case 1:
X=H^{1}(\Omega)
and
|.|_{X}=\|.\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
;

•

Case 2:
X=L_{\infty}(\Omega)
with
|.|_{X}=\|.\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}
;

•

Case 3:
X=\{v\in L_{2}(\Omega)\ |\ v|_{\Omega_{d}}\in L_{\infty}(\Omega_{d})\}
and
|.|_{X}=|.|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega_{d})}
.

In Case 2, Assumption
3.1
is satisfied provided
s>\frac{n-1}{2}
. In contrast, in Cases 1 and 3, Assumption
3.1
is satisfied without additional restriction besides
s>1/2
.

Regarding the function
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
defined by
-\Delta u_{0}=f
in
\Omega
, we shall require that

u_{0}\in X\cap C(\Omega).

We assume throughout that
f\in H^{-1+\beta_{\Omega}}(\Omega)
for
\beta_{\Omega}>\max(0,n/2-1)
, which automatically gives
u_{0}\in C^{0}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}(\Omega)\subset X
when
X=H^{1}(\Omega)
or
X=\{v\in L_{2}(\Omega)\ |\ v|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\}
. Instead, we further need that
f
belongs to
L_{b}(\Omega)
for
b>n/2
and that
\Omega
satisfies the exterior cone property when
X=L_{\infty}(\Omega)

[
36
]
.

3.2.
Minimal Norm Interpolant

We start by recalling that from the decomposition (
2.10
), we conclude that
u_{\mathcal{H}}\in\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\widetilde{\omega}}
is the component of
u
to be recovered. To this aim, we define the
minimal norm interpolant

u^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}:=u^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}(\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}})
as

(3.4)

u_{\mathcal{H}}^{*}\ :=\ \mathop{\rm argmin}\{\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega)}\ |\ v\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega),\ \lambda_{i}(v)=\widetilde{\omega}_{i},\ i=1,...,m\}.

Notice that the harmonic part of the solution to recover and from which the measurements are obtained belongs to the set
\{v\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega)\ |\ \lambda_{i}(v)=\widetilde{\omega}_{i},\ i=1,..,m\}
(which is therefore not empty). This, together with the strong convexity of the
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega)
-norm guarantees the existence and uniqueness of the minimal norm interpolant.

For any Banach space
X
and semi-norm
|\cdot|_{X}
satisfying Assumption
3.1
, the minimal norm interpolant is an optimal recovery in
(X,|.|_{X})
for functions in
\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\widetilde{\omega}}
. Indeed, the arguments in Section 1.4 of
[
9
]
solely based on the central symmetry of
\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\tilde{\omega}}
around
u^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}
and the triangle inequality property satisfied by the (semi-)norm in
X
carry through general Banach spaces
X
, even when the Chebysvhev center is not unique. In particular,
u^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}\in\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}\subset X
and we have

(3.5)

\sup_{w\in\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\widetilde{\omega}}}|w-u^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}|_{X}=R(\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\widetilde{w}})_{X}.

This implies that under Assumption
3.1
and for
u_{0}\in X
,
u^{*}=u_{0}+u^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}
is an optimal recovery in
(X,|.|_{X})
for functions in
\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\widetilde{\omega}}
.

The minimal norm interpolant has an algorithm-friendly characterization using the Riesz representers of the measurement functionals
\phi_{i}
,
i=1,...,m
, defined by (
1.7
). Let
\mathbb{W}:=\textrm{span}(\phi_{1},...,\phi_{m})
and note that the measurements
\lambda_{i}(v)=\tilde{\omega}_{i}:=\omega_{i}-\lambda_{i}(u_{0})
,
i=1,...,m
determine the component of
v\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega)
in
\mathbb{W}
. Furthermore, the minimal norm interpolant
u_{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
belongs to
\mathbb{W}
because any nonzero component in the orthogonal complement would increase the
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega)
-norm.
Whence, we find that

(3.6)

u_{\mathcal{H}}^{*}\ =\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}U_{i}\phi_{i},

for some
U_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
that satisfies the relation

(3.7)

G{\bf U}\ =\ \widetilde{{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}}.

Here
{\bf U}=(U_{i})^{m}_{i=1}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}=(\omega_{i})^{m}_{i=1}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and
G\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
is the observation matrix of components
G_{ij}:=\lambda_{i}(\phi_{j})
. We point out that the matrix
G
may not be invertible but
\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}
belongs to its range by construction. Yet, if
G^{-1}
denotes the Moore-Penrose pseudo inverse of
G
, we select one of the solutions
{\bf U}
by the relation

(3.8)

{\bf U}\ =\ G^{-1}\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}.

In order to simplify the discussion, from now on we assume that the Riesz representers are linearly independent, and thus
G
is invertible. We refer to
[
13
]
for a discussion when the condition number of
G
is large.

3.3.
Near-optimal recovery algorithm

In this section, we slightly modify the algorithm introduced in
[
9
]
for the approximation of the minimal norm interpolant
u^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}
. Compared to
[
9
]
, we provide a more general version of recovery in a Banach space
X
with semi-norm
|.|_{X}
that satisfies Assumption
3.1
. However, the essence of the algorithm remains unchanged. It consists of replacing the Riesz representers in (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) by computable approximations. Our goal is to achieve a near-optimal recovery
u^{*}
in the sense that for some fixed
C\geq 1
,

(3.9)

\sup_{v\in\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}|u^{*}-v|_{X}\leq CR(\mathcal{K}_{\omega})_{X}.

In practice we instead assume that we are given
\epsilon>0
with
\epsilon\lesssim R(K_{\omega})_{X}
and seek
u^{*}
such that

(3.10)

\sup_{v\in\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}|u^{*}-v|_{X}\leq R(\mathcal{K}_{\omega})_{X}+\epsilon.

Algorithm 3.2

(Recovery Algorithm in
X
)
.

Input:

\Omega
,
u_{0}
,
f
,
s
, measurement couples
(\lambda_{j},\omega_{j})_{j=1}^{m}
and tolerances
\epsilon_{1}>0
,
\epsilon_{2}>0
;

Output:

Approximation
u^{*}
of recovery
\widehat{u}
.

(i)

Compute an approximation
\widehat{u_{0}}
of
u_{0}
that satisfies

(3.11)

|\widehat{u}_{0}-u_{0}|_{X}+\max_{i=1,...,m}|\widehat{u}_{0}(x_{i})-u_{0}(x_{i})|\ \leq\ \epsilon_{1}

and compute the vector of modified measurements

\widehat{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}\ :=\ (\omega_{i}-u_{0}(x_{i}))_{i=1}^{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}.

(ii)

Compute approximations
\widehat{\phi}_{j}
of each
\phi_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq m
that satisfies

(3.12)

|\widehat{\phi}_{j}-\phi_{j}|_{X}+\max_{i=1,...,m}|\widehat{\phi}_{j}(x_{i})-\phi_{j}(x_{i})|\ \leq\ \epsilon_{2}.

(iii)

Assemble
\widehat{G}:=(\widehat{\phi}_{j}(x_{i}))_{1\leq i,j\leq m}
and the vector
\widehat{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
of components
\widehat{\omega}_{i}
.

(iv)

Set
\widehat{u}^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\widehat{U}_{i}\widehat{\phi}_{i}
, where the coefficients
\widehat{\bf U}:=(\widehat{U}_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
are given by

(3.13)

\widehat{{\bf U}}\ :=\ \widehat{G}^{-1}\widehat{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}},

with
\widehat{G}^{-1}
being the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse of
\widehat{G}
.

(v)

Output
u^{*}:=\widehat{u}_{0}+\widehat{u}^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}
.

Theorem
3.4
below assesses the efficiency of the algorithm.
Its proof relies on the following lemma gathering several estimates proved in
[
9
]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G:=(\lambda_{i}(\phi_{j}))_{i,j=1}^{m}
be the observation matrix and
\widehat{G}=(\lambda_{i}(\widehat{\phi}_{j}))_{i,j=1}^{m}
its approximation constructed in Algorithm
3.2
with input tolerances
\epsilon_{1}>0,\epsilon_{2}>0
.
There holds

(3.14)

\|G-\widehat{G}\|_{\ell_{1}\to\ell_{1}}\ \leq\ m\epsilon_{2}.

Furthermore, assume that
G
is invertible. Then there is
\overline{\epsilon}_{2}>0
depending on
\|G^{-1}\|_{\ell_{1}\to\ell_{1}}
and
m
such that for
\epsilon_{2}\leq\overline{\epsilon}_{2}
,
\widehat{G}
is invertible and we have

(3.15)

\|G^{-1}-\widehat{G}^{-1}\|_{\ell_{1}\to\ell_{1}}\ \leq\ \frac{m\|G^{-1}\|_{\ell_{1}\to\ell_{1}}^{2}\epsilon_{2}}{1-m\|G^{-1}\|_{\ell_{1}\to\ell_{1}}\epsilon_{2}}\ =:\ \delta.

We are now in position to provide the near-optimal recovery theorem.

Theorem 3.4

(Near-optimal recovery in
X
)
.

Assume
(X,|\cdot|_{X})
is a Banach space satisfying Assumption
3.1
, that
u_{0}\in X\cap C(\Omega)
, and that the observation matrix
G
is invertible. Let
\epsilon>0
. Select
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}>0
such that
\epsilon_{2}\leq\overline{\epsilon}_{2}
, where
\overline{\epsilon}_{2}
is given by Lemma
3.3
and such that

(3.16)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}

\displaystyle+m\|G^{-1}\|_{\ell_{1}\to\ell_{1}}(2\Lambda_{0}\|f\|_{H^{-1+\beta_{\Omega}}(\Omega)}+\Lambda_{s,d}C_{B})\epsilon_{2}

\displaystyle+(C_{X}+\epsilon_{2})(m\|G^{-1}\|_{\ell_{1}\to\ell_{1}}\epsilon_{1}+m(\Lambda_{s,d}C_{B}+\Lambda_{0}\|f\|_{H^{-1+\beta_{\Omega}}(\Omega)}+\epsilon_{1})\delta)\ \leq\ \epsilon.

Here
C_{X}:=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}|\phi_{j}|_{X}<\infty
,
\delta
is given by (
3.15
), and
\Lambda_{0}
and
\Lambda_{s}
are defined in (
2.14
) and (
2.15
) resp. Then the function
\widehat{u}^{*}
generated by Algorithm
3.2
with parameters
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}
satisfies

(3.17)

|v-\widehat{u}^{*}|_{X}\ \leq\ R(\mathcal{K}_{\omega})_{X}+\epsilon,\qquad\forall v\in\mathcal{K}_{\omega}.

That is,
\widehat{u}^{*}
is a near-optimal recovery in
(X,|.|_{X})
for functions in
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
.

Proof.

Let
v\in\mathcal{K}_{w}
. From the definition of
R(\mathcal{K}_{w})_{X}
in (
3.5
), we directly find that

|v-\widehat{u}^{*}|_{X}\ \leq\ R(\mathcal{K}_{w})_{X}+|u^{*}-\widehat{u}^{*}|_{X},

where
u^{*}=u_{0}+u^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}
and
u^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}
is the minimal norm interpolant; see (
3.4
).
Furthermore, in view of (
3.11
), we get

|u^{*}-\widehat{u}^{*}|_{X}\ \leq\ |u_{0}-\widehat{u}_{0}|_{X}+|u^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}-\widehat{u}^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}|_{X}\leq\epsilon_{1}+|u^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}-\widehat{u}^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}|_{X}

so that

(3.18)

|v-\widehat{u}^{*}|_{X}\ \leq\ R(\mathcal{K}_{w})_{X}+\epsilon_{1}+|u^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}-\widehat{u}^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}|_{X}.

The rest of the proof focuses on the derivation of an estimate of the discrepancy in the harmonic part
|u^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}-\widehat{u}^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}|_{X}
.
Recall that
u^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}U_{i}\phi_{i}
, where
U_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,...,m
, satisfy the algebraic system (
3.6
). We compute

(3.19)

|u^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}-\widehat{u}_{\mathcal{H}}|_{X}\ \leq\ \Big|\sum_{j=1}^{m}U_{j}(\phi_{j}-\widehat{\phi}_{j})\Big|_{X}+\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{m}(U_{j}-\widehat{U}_{j})\widehat{\phi}_{j}\Big|_{X}\ \leq\ \|{\bf U}\|_{\ell_{1}}\epsilon_{2}+\|{\bf U}-\widehat{\bf U}\|_{\ell_{1}}(C_{X}+\epsilon_{2}),

where we have used the approximation estimate (
3.12
) for the Riesz representers and the definition of
C_{X}=\max_{i=1,...,m}|\phi_{i}|_{X}
.
Note that
C_{X}\leq\Lambda_{X}\max_{i=1,...,m}\|\phi_{i}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega)}<\infty
thanks to (
3.3
).

The algebraic relation (
3.6
) for
{\bf U}
yields

\|{\bf U}\|_{\ell_{1}}=\|G^{-1}\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}\|_{\ell_{1}}\leq\|G^{-1}\|_{\ell_{1}\to\ell_{1}}\|\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}\|_{\ell_{1}}\leq m\|G^{-1}\|_{\ell_{1}\to\ell_{1}}\|\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}\|\leq m\|G^{-1}\|_{\ell_{1}\to\ell_{1}}(2\Lambda_{0}\|f\|_{H^{-1+\beta_{\Omega}}(\Omega)}+\Lambda_{s,d}C_{B}),

where we used the estimate (
2.18
) on the modified measurement values.

Combining the previous two estimates, we obtain

(3.20)

|u^{*}-\widehat{u}|_{X}\leq m\|G^{-1}\|_{\ell_{1}\to\ell_{1}}(2\Lambda_{0}\|f\|_{H^{-1+\beta_{\Omega}}(\Omega)}+\Lambda_{s,d}C_{B})\epsilon_{2}+\|{\bf U}-\widehat{\bf U}\|_{\ell_{1}}(C_{X}+\epsilon_{2}).

For the estimation of
\|{\bf U}-\widehat{\bf U}\|_{\ell_{1}}
, we introduce the intermediate vector
\widetilde{\bf U}:=G^{-1}\widehat{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and compute

\|\widetilde{\bf U}-{\bf U}\|_{\ell_{1}}\ =\ \|G^{-1}(\widehat{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}-\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}})\|_{\ell_{1}}\ \leq\ \|G^{-1}\|_{\ell_{1}\to\ell_{1}}\|\widehat{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}-\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}\|_{\ell_{1}}\ \leq\ m\|G^{-1}\|_{\ell_{1}\to\ell_{1}}\|\widehat{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}-\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}\|.

Notice that
\widehat{\omega}_{i}-\widetilde{\omega}_{i}=\lambda_{i}(u_{0}-\widehat{u}_{0})\leq\epsilon_{1}
thanks to (
3.11
) and so

\|\widetilde{\bf U}-{\bf U}\|_{\ell_{1}}\ \leq\ m\|G^{-1}\|_{\ell_{1}\to\ell_{1}}\varepsilon_{1}.

Furthermore, in view of (
2.17
), (
3.11
), and estimate (
3.14
) for
G^{-1}-\widehat{G}^{-1}
, we have

\|\widetilde{\bf U}-\widehat{\bf U}\|_{\ell_{1}}=\|(G^{-1}-\widehat{G}^{-1})\widehat{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}\|_{\ell_{1}}\ \leq\ \|(G^{-1}-\widehat{G}^{-1})\|_{\ell_{1}\to\ell_{1}}\|\widehat{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}\|_{\ell_{1}}\ \leq\ m(\Lambda_{s,d}C_{B}+\Lambda_{0}\|f\|_{H^{-1+\beta_{\Omega}}(\Omega)}+\epsilon_{1})\delta.

Combining the estimates for
\|{\bf U}-\widetilde{\bf U}\|_{\ell_{1}}
and
\|\widetilde{\bf U}-\widehat{\bf U}\|_{\ell_{1}}
,
we find that

\|{\bf U}-\widehat{\bf U}\|_{\ell_{1}}\ \leq\ m\|G^{-1}\|_{\ell_{1}\to\ell_{1}}\varepsilon_{1}+m(\Lambda_{s,d}C_{B}+\Lambda_{0}\|f\|_{H^{-1+\beta_{\Omega}}(\Omega)}+\epsilon_{1})\delta.

We now insert this bound into (
3.20
) and invoke (
3.16
) to obtain

|u_{\mathcal{H}}^{*}-\widehat{u}_{\mathcal{H}}|_{X}\ \leq\ \epsilon.

The statement of the theorem follows using the above inequality in (
3.18
).

4.
Finite element realizations of the recovery algorithms

In this section, we discuss how to construct
\widehat{u}_{0}
and
\widehat{\phi}_{j}
,
j=1,...,m
satisfying (
3.11
) and (
3.12
).

4.1.
Finite element setting

Recall that
\Omega
is assumed to be Lipschitz and polygonal (
n=2
) or polyhedral (
n=3
).
With
h
denoting the maximal diameter within a given mesh, let
(\mathcal{T}_{h})_{h>0}
denote a family of exact sub-divisions made of triangles (
n=2)
or tetrahedra (
n=3
) of
\Omega
. We also denote by
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}
the restriction of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
to
\Gamma
. Below
\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\Gamma}
is the set of vertices in the mesh
\mathbb{T}_{h}
, and given
z\in\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\Gamma}
,
\omega_{z}
is the patch of elements in
\mathbb{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}
sharing the vertex
z
.

We assume that
(\mathcal{T}_{h})_{h>0}
is shape-regular, that is, all elements
T
have a uniformly bounded ratio between their diameters
h(T)
and the diameter
\rho(T)
of their inner circle.
We additionally assume that the mesh is quasi-uniform, i.e.,
h(T^{\prime})
is uniformly equivalent to
h
for all
T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. We let
\mathbb{V}_{h}

denote the continuous piecewise linear finite element space associated with
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. In addition, we denote

(4.1)

\mathbb{V}_{h}^{0}\ :=\ \{v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}\ |\ T(v_{h})=0\}

as the finite element subspace with a homogeneous (Dirichlet) boundary condition. The restriction to
\Gamma
of functions in
\mathbb{V}_{h}
is

\mathbb{T}_{h}\ :=\ \{T(v_{h})\ :\ v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}\},

where
T:H^{\sigma+1/2}(\Omega)\rightarrow H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)
for
0<\sigma<3/2
is the trace operator introduced in Section
2
.
Since
\mathbb{V}_{h}\subset H^{1}
and we have assumed that
s<\frac{3}{2}
(where we recall that
s
is as in (
1.3
)), we also have
\mathbb{T}_{h}\subset H^{s}(\Gamma)
.

We shall also need finite element approximation results and inverse estimates in
H^{s}(\Gamma)
for
0\leq\sigma<\frac{3}{2}
. We first state a foundational localization result.

Proposition 4.1

(Localization)
.

Let
u\in H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)
. When
0<\sigma<1
, there holds

(4.2)

\|u\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}^{2}\ \lesssim\ h^{-2\sigma}\|u\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}}|u|_{H^{\sigma}(\omega_{z})}^{2}.

If
0<\sigma<\frac{1}{2}
, there additionally holds

(4.3)

\|u\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}^{2}\ \lesssim\ h^{-2\sigma}\|u\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}}|u|_{H^{\sigma}(T)}^{2}.

When instead
1<\sigma<\frac{3}{2}
, we have for
\alpha=\sigma-1
that

(4.4)

\|u\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}^{2}\ \lesssim\ \|u\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+h^{-2\alpha}\|\nabla_{\Gamma}u\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}}|\nabla_{\Gamma}u|_{H^{\alpha}(T)}^{2}.

The well-known result (
4.2
) is found in
[
33
,
34
]
. The finer result (
4.3
) is found in
[
14
, Lemma A.1]
, while (
4.4
) is obtained by a straightforward combination of (
4.3
) and the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}
. We emphasize that (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) have only element-wise terms on the right hand side. The proof of (
4.3
) in
[
14
]
involves mainly elementary computations involving double integrals along with the use of bounded extension operators in
H^{\sigma}
norms. A different proof can be obtained by noting from
[
56
]
that indicator functions of subdomains are multipliers for
H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
when
0<\sigma<\frac{1}{2}
. Combining this result with bounded extension operators for
H^{\sigma}
and (
4.2
), while noting that the Jacobian of the natural patchwise reference transformation from
\omega_{z}
to a corresponding Euclidean reference patch is piecewise constant also yields (
4.3
) and (
4.4
).

We denote by
P_{h}:H^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{V}_{h}
the Scott-Zhang projection operator on
\Omega

[
55
]
and recall that it preserves boundary values and is
H^{1}
stable in the following sense:

(4.5)

P_{h}v_{h}|_{\Gamma}\ =\ v_{h}|_{\Gamma}~~\forall v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h},\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\|P_{h}v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq B\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)},\quad v\in H^{1}(\Omega),

for some
B
independent of
h
. We shall use standard approximation properties of
P_{h}
in Sobolev spaces on
\Omega
without citation.

We also need a Scott-Zhang projection operator on
\Gamma
that is consistent with
P_{h}
.
For each
z\in\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\Gamma}
, choose an arbitrary
T_{z}\in\mathbb{T}_{h}
with
z\in\overline{T_{z}}
, let
\phi_{z}
be the standard Lagrange basis function corresponding to
z
, and let
\psi_{z}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
satisfy
\intop\limits_{T_{i}}\phi_{z_{i}}\psi_{z_{j}}=\delta_{ij}
. Then define

(4.6)

(\pi_{h}u)(x)=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\Gamma}}\phi_{z}(x)\intop\limits_{T_{z}}u\psi_{z}.

The faces
T_{z}
in (
4.6
) may be chosen so that the definitions of
\pi_{h}
and
P_{h}
are consistent, i.e.,

(4.7)

(P_{h}v)|_{\Gamma}=\pi_{h}(v_{\Gamma}).

We shall use this property in our analysis.
Moreover, standard arguments yield

(4.8)

\|\pi_{h}\kappa\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\|\kappa\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma)},\qquad\forall\kappa\in L_{2}(\Gamma)

and for
0<\xi\leq 1
, there holds

(4.9)

\|\kappa-\pi_{h}\kappa\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma)}\lesssim h^{\xi}|\kappa|_{H^{\xi}(\Gamma)},\qquad\forall\kappa\in H^{\xi}(\Gamma).

We shall need stability and approximability properties of
\pi_{h}
on
H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)
for
0\leq\sigma<3/2
and these are the subject of Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
below. In preparation for these results, we introduce yet another Scott-Zhang type interpolant.
When
n=3
we let
I_{h}:H^{1}(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{h}
be the Scott-Zhang interpolant defined using integrals over triangle edges (instead of triangles as in (
4.6
)) as degrees of freedom while when
n=2
(and so
\Gamma
is a 1D curve) we take
I_{h}
to be the Lagrange interpolant.
Note that
I_{h}
is locally defined and locally stable in
H^{\sigma}
for any
\sigma\geq 1
.
Lemma 3.1 and Theorem 3.2 in
[
23
]
guarantee that if
1\leq\sigma<\frac{3}{2}
,
\xi\geq\sigma
, and
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}
, there holds

(4.10)

\|\kappa-I_{h}\kappa\|_{H^{\sigma}(T)}^{2}\lesssim h^{2(\xi-\sigma)}\sum_{T^{\prime}\subset\omega_{T^{\prime}}}|\kappa|_{H^{\xi}(T^{\prime})}^{2}.

Here
\omega_{T^{\prime}}
consists of all elements
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}
such that
T\cap T^{\prime}\not=\emptyset
.
The result as stated in
[
23
]
assumes that
\sigma
and
\xi
are integers, but the proof holds nearly verbatim for fractional
\sigma,\xi
after application of the element-wise Bramble-Hilbert Lemma for fractional norms (cf.
[
31
, Theorem 6.1]
). Instead, when
n=2
,
I_{h}
is the Lagrange interpolant and is element-wise stable on the 1D surface
\Gamma
, i.e., (
4.10
) holds with right-hand side
h^{2(\xi-\sigma)}|\kappa|^{2}_{H^{\xi}(T)}
. The projection
I_{h}
is not stable in
L_{2}(\Gamma)
and does not satisfy the consistency relation (
4.7
). It is merely used as a tool to obtain estimates similar as (
4.10
) for
\pi_{h}
, see Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
below.

We also note that to measure the regularity of a function
\kappa:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
when
\Gamma
is polynomial/polyhedral, it is necessary to modify the definition of
H^{\xi}(\Gamma)
for
\xi\geq 3/2
to avoid degeneracy across edges and vertices of
\Gamma
.
We define

(4.11)

H^{\xi}_{h}(\Gamma):=\begin{cases}H^{\xi}(\Gamma)&\qquad\textrm{when }0<\xi<\frac{3}{2};\\
\Pi_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}}H^{\xi}(T)&\qquad\textrm{when }\frac{3}{2}\leq\xi\leq 2,\end{cases}

and set
|\kappa|_{\tilde{H}^{\xi}(\Gamma)}:=|\kappa|_{H^{\xi}(\Gamma)}
when
0<\xi<\frac{3}{2}
and
|\kappa|_{\tilde{H}^{\xi}(\Gamma)}^{2}:=\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}_{h}}|\kappa|_{H^{\xi}(T)}^{2}
when
\frac{3}{2}\leq\xi\leq 2
.
As alluded to above, the distinction between
\xi<\frac{3}{2}
and
\xi\geq\frac{3}{2}
in the definition of
H^{\xi}_{h}(\Gamma)
is critical when considering polygonal/polyhedral surfaces
\Gamma
. Measuring regularity in these spaces globally or on element patches that may overlap multiple faces of
\Gamma
is not feasible.
On the other hand, as we discuss below, solutions to elliptic problems on polyhedral surfaces may possess
H^{\xi}_{h}(\Gamma)
regularity with
\xi\geq\frac{3}{2}
even when they do not possess such
global
regularity.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\kappa\in H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)
for some
0\leq\sigma<\frac{3}{2}
. There holds

(4.12)

\|\pi_{h}\kappa\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\|\kappa\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}\hbox{ if }0\leq\sigma<\frac{3}{2},\qquad\|I_{h}\kappa\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\|\kappa\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}\hbox{ if }1\leq\sigma<\frac{3}{2}.

Proof.

The stability estimate for
I_{h}
follows by taking
1\leq\xi=\sigma<\frac{3}{2}
in (
4.10
) and summing the resulting estimates over
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}
.

We now turn our attention to the stability of
\pi_{h}
. The case
\sigma=0
is (
4.8
) and so we consider
0<\sigma\leq 1
. Let
z\in\mathcal{N}^{\Gamma}_{h}
. Since
|C|_{H^{\sigma}(\omega_{z})}=0
for any
C\in\mathbb{R}
and
\pi_{h}C=C
,
\Gamma
is a Lipschitz polyhedral domain, and
0<\sigma\leq 1
, we may use standard finite element reference maps and scaling arguments. In particular,
\#\{T\in\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}_{h}\ |\ T\subset\omega_{z}\}
is uniformly bounded depending on the shape regularity of
\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\}_{h>0}
and the maximum valence of vertices of
\Gamma
, and thus
\mathbb{T}_{h}|_{\omega_{z}}
is a finite dimensional space of uniformly bounded dimension. Therefore, (
4.6
), standard scaling arguments, and the Bramble-Hilbert Lemma yield for properly chosen
C\in\mathbb{R}
that

(4.13)

\displaystyle|\pi_{h}\kappa|_{H^{\sigma}(\omega_{z})}

\displaystyle=|\pi_{h}(\kappa-C)|_{H^{\sigma}(\omega_{z})}=\left|\sum_{z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{\omega_{z}}}\phi_{z^{\prime}}\intop\limits_{T_{z^{\prime}}}(\kappa-C)\psi_{z^{\prime}}\right|_{H^{\sigma}(\omega_{z})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{\omega_{z}}}|\phi_{z^{\prime}}|_{H^{\sigma}(\omega_{z})}\|\kappa-C\|_{L_{2}(T_{z^{\prime}})}\|\psi_{z^{\prime}}\|_{L_{2}(T_{z^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{\omega_{z}}}h^{(n-1)/2-\sigma}\|\kappa-C\|_{L_{2}(T_{z^{\prime}})}h^{-(n-1)/2}\lesssim h^{-\sigma}\|\kappa-C\|_{L_{2}(\omega_{z}^{\prime})}\lesssim|\kappa|_{H^{\sigma}(\omega_{z}^{\prime})}.

Here
\mathcal{N}_{\omega_{z}}
is the set of nodes lying in
\overline{\omega_{z}}
and
\omega_{z}^{\prime}:=\cup_{z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}^{\Gamma}_{h},\ z^{\prime}\in\omega_{z}}\omega_{z^{\prime}}
is the patch of elements touching
\omega_{z}
. Note that we have applied a form of the Bramble-Hilbert Lemma that applies to unions of star-shaped domains
[
31
, Theorem 7.1]
, since
\omega_{z}
is star-shaped but
\omega_{z}^{\prime}
may not be. Thus employing (
4.2
), (
4.9
), finite overlap of the patches
\omega_{z}^{\prime}
, and (
4.13
) we obtain

\displaystyle\|\pi_{h}\kappa\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\kappa-\pi_{h}\kappa\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\|\kappa\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-2\sigma}\|\kappa-\pi_{h}\kappa\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}}\left[|\kappa|_{H^{\sigma}(\omega_{z})}^{2}+|\pi_{h}\kappa|_{H^{\sigma}(\omega_{z})}^{2}\right]+\|\kappa\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\kappa\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}^{2}.

This is the asserted global stability (
4.12
) of
\pi_{h}
for
0<\sigma\leq 1
.

Next we prove (
4.12
) for
\pi_{h}
and
1<\sigma<\frac{3}{2}
. Applying
\pi_{h}v_{h}=v_{h}
for
v_{h}\in\mathbb{T}_{h}
, (
4.4
), (
4.12
) for
I_{h}
, an element-wise inverse estimate, (
4.12
) for
\pi_{h}
for
\sigma=0,1
, and (
4.10
) with
\sigma\leftarrow 1
and
\xi\leftarrow\sigma
we obtain

(4.14)

\displaystyle\|\pi_{h}v\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\pi_{h}(v-I_{h}v)\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\|I_{h}v\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\pi_{h}(v-I_{h}v)\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+h^{-2(\sigma-1)}\|\pi_{h}(v-I_{h}v)\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}}|\pi_{h}(v-I_{h}v)|_{H^{\sigma}(T)}^{2}+\|v\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v-I_{h}v\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+h^{-2(\sigma-1)}\|v-I_{h}v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\|v\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}^{2}\lesssim\|v\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}^{2}.

This ends the proof.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
0<\xi\leq 2
,
0\leq\sigma<3/2
and assume that
\kappa\in H^{\xi}_{h}(\Gamma)
.
For
0\leq\sigma\leq\xi
there holds

(4.15)

\|\kappa-\pi_{h}\kappa\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}\lesssim h^{\xi-\sigma}|\kappa|_{H_{h}^{\xi}(\Gamma)}.

Instead, when
1\leq\sigma\leq\xi
, we have

(4.16)

\|\kappa-I_{h}\kappa\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}\lesssim h^{\xi-\sigma}|\kappa|_{H_{h}^{\xi}(\Gamma)}.

Proof.

We start by proving (
4.16
).
When
\sigma=1
(
4.16
) follows immediately from (
4.10
), while when
1<\sigma<\frac{3}{2}
the estimate (
4.10
) is combined with (
4.4
) applied to
u=\kappa-I_{h}\kappa
along with the result for the case
\sigma=1
. The first two terms are handled by the case
\sigma=1
and the third term using (
4.10
). This completes the proof of (
4.16
).

We now prove (
4.15
).
The case
\sigma=0
is (
4.9
). For
0<\sigma\leq\xi\leq 1
, let
C_{z}
be the constant Sobolev polynomial over the star-shaped domain
\omega_{z}
, yielding
|\kappa-C_{z}|_{H^{\sigma}(\omega_{z})}\lesssim h^{\xi-\sigma}|\kappa|_{H^{\xi}(\omega_{z})}
(cf.
[
31
, Theorem 6.1]
). With this same choice of
C_{z}
,
[
31
, Theorem 7.1]
yields
\|\kappa-C_{z}\|_{L_{2}(\omega_{z}^{\prime})}\lesssim h^{\xi}|\kappa|_{H^{\xi}(\omega_{z}^{\prime})}
. Applying these observations, (
4.2
), (
4.15
) for
\sigma=0
, scaling and inverse inequalities, local
L_{2}
stability of
\pi_{h}
, and the finite overlap of the patches
\omega_{z}^{\prime}
then yields

(4.17)

\displaystyle\|\kappa-\pi_{h}\kappa\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-2\sigma}\|\kappa-\pi_{h}\kappa\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}}|(\kappa-C_{z})-\pi_{h}(\kappa-C_{z})|_{H^{\sigma}(\omega_{z})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2(\xi-\sigma)}|\kappa|_{H^{\xi}(\Gamma)}+\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}}\left[|\kappa-C_{z}|_{H^{\sigma}(\omega_{z})}^{2}+h^{-2\sigma}\|\kappa-C_{z}\|_{L_{2}(\omega_{z}^{\prime})}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2(\xi-\sigma)}|\kappa|_{H^{\xi}(\Gamma)}+h^{2(\xi-\sigma)}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}}\left[|\kappa|_{H^{\xi}(\omega_{z})}^{2}+|\kappa|_{H^{\xi}(\omega_{z}^{\prime})}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2(\xi-\sigma)}|\kappa|_{H^{\xi}(\Gamma)}.

This completes the proof of (
4.15
) for
\xi\leq 1
.

It remains to show (
4.15
) for
1<\xi\leq 2
. We write
\kappa-\pi_{h}\kappa=(\kappa-I_{h}\kappa)-\pi_{h}(\kappa-I_{h}\kappa)
. If
\sigma<1
we apply (
4.15
) with
\xi=1
followed by (
4.16
) with
\sigma=1
. If
\sigma\geq 1
we apply the triangle inequality, (
4.12
) for
\pi_{h}
to the resulting second term, and then (
4.16
).

We emphasize that in standard estimates for the Scott-Zhang operator, the sums over triangles on the right hand sides of (
4.15
) with
\xi\leq 1
and (
4.16
) are replaced by corresponding sums over element patches
\omega_{T}
. Reduction to an element-wise sum is critical in the current context because
H^{\xi}(\omega_{T})
may be degenerate when
\xi\geq\frac{3}{2}
.

We finally state inverse estimates. The localization results in Proposition
4.1
are not sufficient to obtain inverse estimates for global fractional norms by using element-wise scaling arguments as is typically done for integer-order norms, so we instead employ interpolation techniques to obtain the needed results. Note that we do not obtain (and do not need) local inverse estimates.

Proposition 4.4

(Inverse estimates)
.

Let
v_{h}\in\mathbb{T}_{h}
. For
0\leq t<\sigma<\frac{3}{2}
, there holds

(4.18)

\|v_{h}\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}\ \lesssim\ h^{t-\sigma}\|v_{h}\|_{H^{t}(\Gamma)}.

Proof.

Denote by
\{\Gamma_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}
the boundary edges (when
n=2
) or boundary faces (when
n=3
) of
\Gamma
, and let
1\leq t<\sigma<\frac{3}{2}
. By arguments similar to those leading to the localization estimates above,

(4.19)

\|\kappa\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}^{2}\ \lesssim\ \sum_{j=1}^{J}\|\kappa\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma_{j})}^{2},~~\kappa\in H^{\sigma}(\Gamma).

Each face
\Gamma_{j}
is a Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. Theorem B.8 of
[
45
]
(cf. Theorems 3.18, 3.30, and A.4 for verification of definitions) implies that for
\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1}\geq 0
that
(H^{\sigma_{0}}(\Gamma_{j}),H^{\sigma_{1}}(\Gamma_{j}))_{\theta,2}=H^{\sigma}(\Gamma_{j})
with
\sigma=(1-\theta){\sigma_{0}}+\theta{\sigma_{1}}
. Here
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta,2}
denotes real interpolation via the
K
-method. Note that
\|v_{h}\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma_{j})}\lesssim h^{1-\sigma}\|v_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{j})}
due to (
4.4
) and a standard element-wise scaling argument for the term
|v_{h}|_{H^{\sigma}(T)}
. We carry out operator interpolation (cf.
[
28
, Theorem 7.1 in Chapter 6]
) for the Scott-Zhang interpolant
I_{h}
on
\Gamma_{j}
with endpoint estimates
\|I_{h}\kappa\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma_{j})}\lesssim\|\kappa\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma_{j})}
and
\|I_{h}\kappa\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma_{j})}\lesssim h^{1-\sigma}\|\kappa\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{j})}
(the latter obtained by combining an inverse estimate with the
H^{1}
stability of
I_{h}
). This yields
I_{h}:H^{t}(\Gamma_{j})\rightarrow H^{\sigma}(\Gamma_{j})
with operator norm
Ch^{t-\sigma}
, and recalling that
I_{h}
is the identity on
\mathbb{T}_{h}
yields the face-wise inverse estimate
\|v_{h}\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma_{j})}\lesssim h^{t-\sigma}\|v_{h}\|_{H^{t}(\Gamma_{j})}
,
1\leq t<\sigma<\frac{3}{2}
. Summing over
j=1,...,J
then completes the proof of (
4.18
) for
1\leq t<\sigma<\frac{3}{2}
.

Let now
0\leq t<\sigma\leq 1
. Let
\mathcal{N}
denotes the set of vertices of the polygon or polyhedron
\Gamma
. We assume that the faces of
\Gamma
are triangular; if not then we break the non-triangular faces into triangles having vertices lying in
\mathcal{N}_{z}
and proceed as below using the expanded set of triangular “faces”. For each
z\in\mathcal{N}
let
\Gamma_{z}=\cup_{\Gamma_{j}:z\in\overline{\Gamma}_{j}}\overline{\Gamma}_{j}
, where as above we denote by
\{\Gamma_{j}\}
the set of faces of
\Gamma
. The localization estimate (
4.2
) yields

(4.20)

\|u\|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)}^{2}\ \leq\ \|u\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}}|u|_{H^{\sigma}(\Gamma_{z})}^{2},~~u\in H^{\sigma}(\Gamma),~~0<\sigma\leq 1.

Since
\Omega
is a Lipschitz polyhedral domain,
\Gamma_{z}
is the graph of a bi-Lipschitz map
\Phi_{z}
over a Euclidean domain
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{z}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. Notice
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{z}
is a line segment when
n=2
and is a polygon when
n=3
, and
\Phi_{z}
has piecewise constant Jacobian. With these properties,
\Phi_{z}
also induces a transformation of
\mathbb{T}_{h}|_{\Gamma_{z}}
to a finite element space
\mathbb{X}_{h,z}
on
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{z}
possessing standard approximation and inverse properties. As above we employ operator interpolation to obtain inverse estimates on
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{z}
; cf.
[
40
]
for similar arguments and finer results also valid on non-quasi-uniform meshes. Let
\pi_{h,z}
be an
L_{2}
-stable Scott-Zhang interpolant onto
\mathbb{X}_{h,z}
analogous to (
4.6
);
\pi_{h,z}
is then stable on
H^{\sigma}(\Gamma)
for
0\leq\sigma\leq 1
as with
\pi_{h}
, see Lemma
4.2
. Carrying out operator interpolation for
\pi_{h,z}
on
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{z}
with endpoint estimates
\|\pi_{h,z}u\|_{H^{1}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{z})}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{1}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{z})}
,
\|\pi_{h,z}u\|_{H^{1}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{z})}\lesssim h^{-1}\|u\|_{L_{2}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{z})}
(the latter obtained by combining an inverse estimate with the
L_{2}
stability of
\pi_{h,z}
) and recalling that
\pi_{h,z}
is the identity on
\mathbb{X}_{h,z}
yields
\|\widetilde{v}_{h}\|_{H^{\sigma}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{z})}\lesssim h^{-\sigma}\|\widetilde{v}_{h}\|_{L_{2}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{z})}
,
0\leq\sigma\leq 1
. Repeating the argument with
H^{\sigma}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{z})
replacing
H^{1}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{z})
, we obtain
\|\widetilde{v}_{h}\|_{H^{\sigma}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{z})}\lesssim h^{t-\sigma}\|\widetilde{v}_{h}\|_{H^{t}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{z})}
,
0\leq t\leq\sigma\leq 1
. Then, (
4.20
) and a mapping argument complete the proof of (
4.18
) for
0\leq t<\sigma\leq 1
. The estimate for
t\leq 1\leq\sigma
is obtained by combining the previous two cases.

4.2.
Approximation of the Riesz representers - setting

The starting point is the partial differential equation (
1.7
) on
\Gamma
defining the Riesz representer
\phi_{i}
associated with the measurement
\lambda_{i}
.
Recall that the scalar product in
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega)
is given by
\langle\phi_{j},v\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega)}=\langle\phi_{j},v\rangle_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}
and thus, (
1.7
) can be equivalently rewritten as
\phi_{i}=E\psi_{i}
where
\psi_{i}\in H^{s}(\Gamma)
satisfies

(4.21)

\langle\psi_{i},v\rangle_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}\ =\ \lambda_{i}(Ev),\qquad\forall v\in H^{s}(\Gamma),

where
E
is the harmonic extension defined in (
2.11
).

We shall take advantage of the latter relation to design discrete approximations of the Riesz representers. In order to avoid an overload of notation, we use
\nu
to denote one of the
\lambda_{i}
,
i=1,...,m
, and drop the index on the Riesz representer. With these notations, our goal is to approximate
\phi=E\psi\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Omega)
, where
\psi\in H^{s}(\Gamma)
satisfies

(4.22)

\langle\psi,v\rangle_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}\ =\ \nu(Ev),\qquad\forall v\in H^{s}(\Gamma).

We proceed by introducing the discrete harmonic extension operator
E_{h}:\mathbb{T}_{h}\rightarrow\mathbb{V}_{h}
. For any
g_{h}\in\mathbb{T}_{h}
:

(4.23)

E_{h}g_{h}\ :=\ \mathop{\rm argmin}\{\|\nabla v_{h}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}\ |\ v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h},\ T(v_{h})=g_{h}\}.

Alternatively,
E_{h}g_{h}
is characterized by satisfying
T(E_{h}g_{h})=g_{h}
and the discrete harmonic property

(4.24)

\intop\limits_{\Omega}\nabla(E_{h}g_{h})\cdot\nabla v_{h}\ =\ 0,\quad v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}^{0};

compare with (
2.12
).
We then define
\phi_{h}:=E_{h}\psi_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
, where
\psi_{h}\in\mathbb{T}_{h}
satisfies

(4.25)

\langle\psi_{h},v_{h}\rangle_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}\ =\ \nu(E_{h}v_{h}),\qquad\forall v_{h}\in\mathbb{T}_{h}.

Lax-Milgram theory guarantees the existence and uniqueness of
\psi_{h}\in\mathbb{T}_{h}
and thus the existence and uniqueness of
\phi_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
. We postpone to Subsection
7.1
a discussion on the reformulation of the discrete problem as a more practical saddle-point system.

5.
Regularity assumptions and results

The order of convergence for finite element approximations of the Riesz representers, and ultimately of the finite element approximation to the optimal recovery, depends on three main types of regularity:

(i)

Regularity of solutions to second-order elliptic problems posed on the polyhedral Euclidean domain
\Omega
.

(ii)

Regularity of solutions to potentially fractional-order elliptic problems posed on the polyhedral surface
\Gamma
.

(iii)

Boundedness of the harmonic extension operator
E
.

In all three cases we shall state or assume multiple types of regularity bounds, so our final convergence estimates involve a relatively complex interplay between a number of parameters. We now define these parameters, the first of which appear when stating an elliptic regularity pick-up result on
\Omega
.

Assumption 5.1

(Regularity pickup on
\Omega
)
.

There exists
\frac{1}{2}<\beta^{*}=\beta^{*}(\Omega)\leq\infty
such that for all
0<\beta\leq\beta^{*}
, if
\zeta\in H^{-1+\beta}(\Omega)
, then the function
z_{\zeta}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfying

(5.1)

\intop\limits_{\Omega}\nabla z_{\zeta}\cdot\nabla v\ =\ \langle\zeta,v\rangle,\quad v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

belongs to
H^{1+\beta}(\Omega)
.
Here
\langle\zeta,v\rangle
denotes the
H^{-1}-H^{1}_{0}
duality product. We also have the a priori estimate

(5.2)

\|z_{\zeta}\|_{H^{1+\beta}(\Omega)}\ \lesssim\ \|\zeta\|_{H^{-1+\beta}(\Omega)}.

For a non-convex polygonal/polyhedral bounded domain, there holds
\frac{1}{2}<\beta^{*}<1
. When
\Omega
is smooth or convex we have
\beta^{*}\geq 1

[
32
,
36
]
. More precisely:

•

\beta^{*}=\infty
if
\Omega
is smooth.

•

\beta^{*}\geq 1
if
\Omega
is convex, with
\beta^{*}=1
for a generic convex domain and
\beta^{*}>1
for a convex polygonal or polyhedral domain with the precise value depending on the maximum vertex opening angle (
n=2
) or vertex and edge geometry (
n=3
).

•

When
\Omega
is polygonal but non-convex in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, or polyhedral but non-convex in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, then
\frac{1}{2}<\beta^{*}<1
with the exact value depending on the maximum vertex opening angle (
n=2
) or vertex and edge geometry (
n=3
).

Since we employ piecewise linear finite element spaces whose approximation properties cannot take advantage of higher regularity than
H^{2}(\Omega)
, we shall generally instead use the parameter

\beta_{\Omega}\ :=\ \min(\beta^{*},1)\qquad\textrm{so that}\qquad\frac{1}{2}<\beta_{\Omega}\leq 1.

We next need to establish embeddings of
\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}(\Omega)
into
H^{r}(\Omega)
.

Proposition 5.2

(Embedding
\mathcal{H}^{r^{*}+\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega)\subset H^{r^{*}+1}(\Omega)
)
.

Let
\beta^{*}
as in Assumption
5.1
. Let
0<r^{*}<1
with
r^{*}\leq\beta^{*}
. Then
\mathcal{H}^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Omega)\subset H^{r^{*}+1}(\Omega)
. In other words, there exists
C_{e}>0
such that

(5.3)

\|Eg\|_{H^{r^{*}+1}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ C_{e}\|g\|_{H^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)}

holds for any
g\in H^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

For
0<r^{*}\leq\frac{1}{2}
the desired result is stated for example in
[
39
]
for general Lipschitz domains.

For
\frac{1}{2}<r^{*}<1
we instead employ properties of the trace operator on Lipschitz polygons and polyhedra. Recall that
H_{T}^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)=T(H^{r^{*}+1}(\Omega))
is the image of
H^{r^{*}+1}(\Omega)
under the trace operator
T
defined in (
2.3
). We wish to establish that
H_{T}^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)=H^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)
, where as throughout this work the latter space is defined as those functions with finite intrinsic norm given by (
2.2
).

The space
H_{T}^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)
is characterized for general polygonal and polyhedral domains in
[
7
,
8
]
, and a similar characterization is given for the more restricted case of triangles and tetrahedra in
[
48
,
49
]
. There it is shown that
g\in H_{T}^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)
if and only if
g\in H^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma_{j})
,
j=1,..,J
, and if in addition
g
satisfies compatibility conditions at vertices of
\Gamma
(if
n=2
so
\Omega
is a polygon) or at edges and vertices of
\Gamma
(if
n=3
so
\Omega
is a polyhedron).
Here
\{\Gamma_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}
are the boundary edges (when
n=2
) or boundary faces (when
n=3
) of
\Gamma
.
When
\frac{1}{2}<r^{*}<1
these compatibility conditions reduce to requiring continuity of
g
across vertices and edges of
\Gamma
(for the case of polygons, see for example the case
n=0
in
[
7
, Table 1]
). That is,

H_{T}^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)=C(\Gamma)\cap\Pi_{j=1}^{J}H^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma_{j}),~~\frac{1}{2}<{r^{*}}<1.

If
g\in H^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)
for
\frac{1}{2}<r^{*}<1
, then
g\in C(\Gamma)
by Sobolev embedding, and in addition
g\in H^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma_{j})
,
j=1,...,J
. Thus
H^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)\subset H_{T}^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)
.

In order to prove the other inclusion, note first that continuous, facewise
H^{1}
functions on
\Gamma
are globally in
H^{1}
. Because the
H^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)
norm can be localized to faces (cf. (
4.19
)), we thus obtain
H_{T}^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)=H^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)
with equivalence of norms also holding.

Having established that
H_{T}^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)=H^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)
, we note that
T
also has a bounded right inverse
T^{-1}:H_{T}^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)\rightarrow H^{r^{*}+1}(\Omega)

[
8
, Corollary 5.8 and Corollary 6.10]
. Thus because
r^{*}\leq\beta^{*}
and
\Delta Eg=0
, we have for
g\in H^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)
that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\|Eg\|_{H^{r^{*}+1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|Eg-T^{-1}g\|_{H^{r^{*}+1}(\Omega)}+\|T^{-1}g\|_{H^{r^{*}+1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Delta(Eg-T^{-1}g)\|_{H^{r^{*}-1}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H_{T}^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle=\|\Delta T^{-1}g\|_{H^{r^{*}-1}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H_{T}^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq\|T^{-1}g\|_{H^{r^{*}+1}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H_{T}^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{H_{T}^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\|g\|_{H^{r^{*}+1/2}(\Gamma)}.

This completes the proof.

We also need to measure the regularity of the functionals
\lambda_{i}:H^{s}(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by
\lambda_{i}(g)=Eg(x_{i})
,
x_{i}\in\Omega
. As for the regularity pick-up, we state this as an assumption and then prove that the assumption holds for at least some parameters in particular cases.

Assumption 5.3
.

There is
\mu\geq 0
such that given
z\in\Omega
with
{\rm dist}(z,\partial\Omega)\geq d
and
g\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
, there holds

(5.5)

|Eg(z)|\ \lesssim\ d^{-n/2-\tilde{\mu}}\|g\|_{H^{-\mu}(\Gamma)}

where
\tilde{\mu}:=\max(0,\mu-\frac{1}{2})
. Consequently, if
\gamma_{z}(g)=(Eg)(z)
denotes the bounded extension of
g\mapsto(Eg)(z)
to
H^{\mu}(\Gamma)
, we have
\gamma_{z}\in H^{\mu}(\Gamma)
and

(5.6)

\|\gamma_{z}\|_{H^{\mu}(\Gamma)}\lesssim d^{-n/2-\tilde{\mu}}.

We first establish that this result holds for
\mu=0
for any domain such that the shift estimate (
5.2
) holds for some
\beta>\frac{3}{2}
. This class of domains includes any polygonal, polyhedral, convex, or smooth domain.

Proposition 5.4

(
L_{2}
embedding)
.

Assume that
\Omega
is a Lipschitz domain such that Assumption
5.1
holds for some
\beta^{*}>\frac{1}{2}
. Then for all
g\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
and
z\in\Omega
with
{\rm dist}(z,\partial\Omega)\geq d
we have

(5.7)

|Eg(z)|\ \lesssim\ d^{-n/2}\|g\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma)}.

Here the hidden constant only depends on
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
solve (
5.1
) with right-hand side
Eg
. Integrating by parts twice and
\Delta Eg=0
yield

(5.8)

\|Eg\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\intop\limits_{\Omega}Eg(-\Delta w)dx\ =\ \intop\limits_{\Omega}\nabla Eg\cdot\nabla wdx-\intop\limits_{\Gamma}g\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}d\sigma\ =\ -\intop\limits_{\Gamma}g\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}d\sigma\leq\ \|g\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma)}\Big|\Big|\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}\Big|\Big|_{L_{2}(\Gamma)},

where
\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}
stands for the outer normal derivative of
w
. Using that
\|\nu\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}=1
, applying the continuity of the trace operator for
0<\eta\leq\max(\beta^{*},1)-\frac{1}{2}
(cf.
[
38
, Theorem 1.5.1.2]
) and then employing (
5.2
) gives

(5.9)

\Big\|\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}\Big\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma)}\leq\|\nabla w\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma)}\leq\|\nabla w\|_{H^{\eta}(\Gamma)}\ \leq\ \|\nabla w\|_{H^{\eta+1/2}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ \|w\|_{H^{\eta+3/2}(\Omega)}\ \lesssim\ \|w\|_{H^{1+\max(\beta^{*},1)}(\Omega)},

and so combining (
5.2
) and (
5.9
) in (
5.8
), we obtain

(5.10)

\|Eg\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\ \lesssim\ \|g\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma)}.

The desired result follows by combining the last inequality with the mean value property for harmonic functions:

(5.11)

|Eg(z)|=\frac{1}{|B_{d}(z)|}\intop\limits_{B_{d}(z)}Eg(x)dx\lesssim d^{-n/2}\|Eg\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\ \lesssim\ d^{-n/2}\|g\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma)},

where
B_{d}(z)
is the open ball centered at
z
and of radius
d
.

We next show that Assumption
5.3
holds for arbitrarily large
\mu>0
when
\Omega
is smooth, at least when
\mu+\frac{1}{2}
is an integer.

Proposition 5.5
.

Assume that
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
is of class
C^{\infty}
and
g\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
. Then for
\mu\geq\frac{1}{2}
with
\mu+\frac{1}{2}
an integer and
z\in\Omega
with
{\rm dist}(z,\partial\Omega)\geq d
, there holds

|Eg(z)|\ \lesssim\ d^{-n/2-\mu+1/2}\|g\|_{H^{-\mu}(\Gamma)}.

Proof.

Let
G
be the Green’s function for
\Omega
and
\nu
denotes the outward pointing normal to
\Omega
. Then

(5.12)

Eg(z)\ =\ -\intop\limits_{\Gamma}g(y)\frac{\partial_{y}G(y,z)}{\partial\nu}dy\leq\|g\|_{H^{-\mu}(\Gamma)}\left\|\frac{\partial G(\cdot,z)}{\partial\nu}\right\|_{H^{\mu}(\Gamma)}.

Let now
\varphi
be a cutoff function satisfying
\varphi|_{B_{d/2}(z)}\equiv 0
,
\varphi|_{\overline{\Omega}\setminus B_{d}(z)}\equiv 1
, and
|D^{\alpha}\varphi|\lesssim d^{-|\alpha|}
for multi-indices
|\alpha|\geq 0
. Using the smoothness of the normal
\nu
to
\Gamma
and standard trace theorems, we have

(5.13)

\left\|\frac{\partial G(\cdot,z)}{\partial\nu}\right\|_{H^{\mu}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\|\nabla G(\cdot,z)\|_{H^{\mu}(\Gamma)}\ =\ \|\varphi\nabla G(\cdot,z)\|_{H^{\mu}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\|\varphi\nabla G(\cdot,z)\|_{H^{\mu+1/2}(\Omega)}.

Standard estimates for the Green’s function
[
41
]
give

(5.14)

D_{y}^{\alpha}\nabla_{y}G(y,z)\ \lesssim\ |y-z|^{1-n-|\alpha|},~|\alpha|\geq 0.

Translating to polar coordinates, we can compute

(5.15)

|\nabla G(\cdot,z)|_{H^{\mu+1/2}(\Omega\setminus B_{d/2}(z))}^{2}\ \lesssim\ \intop\limits_{d/2}^{{\rm diam}(\Omega)}(r^{1-n-\mu-1/2})^{2}r^{n-1}dr\lesssim d^{-n-2\mu+1}.

Employing Leibniz’s Rule and
|D^{\alpha}\varphi|\lesssim d^{-|\alpha|}
similarly yields

(5.16)

\|\varphi\nabla G(\cdot,z)\|_{H^{\mu+1/2}(\Omega)}\ \lesssim\ d^{-n/2-\mu+1/2},

which yields the desired result when combined with the preceding equations.

Remark 5.6
.

We have established that Assumption 5.3 holds with
\mu=0
for a broad class of domains (including Lipschitz polygonal and polyhedral domains) and that it holds for arbitrarily large
\mu
when
\Omega
is smooth. A natural question is whether this assumption holds for some positive
\mu>0
when
\Omega
has intermediate smoothness. One can quickly establish a positive answer if
\Omega
is of class
C^{k,1}
for some integer
k>0
, but when
\Omega
is polygonal or polyhedral and thus merely of class
C^{0,1}
the answer is more subtle. Let for example
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be polygonal. A critical step in the proof of Proposition
5.4
is the bound (
5.9
). In order to emulate this step for
\mu>0
we would need to similarly bound
\left\|\frac{\partial z}{\partial\nu}\right\|_{H^{\mu}(\Omega)}
, but
\partial z/\partial\nu=\nabla z\cdot\nu
is not generally in
H^{\mu}(\Gamma)
when
\Omega
is merely Lipschitz because
\nu
is only in
[L_{\infty}(\Gamma)]^{n}
. When
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is polygonal with edges
\{\Gamma_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M}
, Grisvard
[
38
, Theorem 1.2.1]
has established that if
z\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
then
\frac{\partial z}{\partial\nu}\in W_{p}^{1-1/p}(\Gamma_{j})
,
j=1,...,J
. When
\Omega
is convex we can take
p=2
, which implies
\frac{\partial z}{\partial\nu}
possesses piecewise
H^{\mu}
regularity with
\mu=\frac{1}{2}
. The question of whether this additional piecewise regularity would ultimately allow us to systematically establish higher order convergence rates for our recovery algorithm does not have a clear answer, especially when
s\neq 1
, and we do not pursue it further.

We next consider regularity of solutions to (possibly fractional order) elliptic problems on the boundary
\Gamma
of the form: For
\gamma\in H^{-s}(\Gamma)
, find
\kappa\in H^{s}(\Gamma)
such that

(5.17)

\langle\kappa,g\rangle_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}\ =\ \gamma(g),\quad g\in H^{s}(\Gamma).

The smoothness of the boundary
\Gamma
and the functional
\gamma
will generally both impact the smoothness of
\kappa
. As noted in the predecessor work
[
9
]
, there seems to be relatively little literature quantifying expected regularity gains in the context of fractional-order problems. In the case of the classical Laplace-Beltrami problem (
s=1
) we may in contrast make some concrete statements.

Remark 5.7

(Regularity of solutions to the Laplace-Beltrami problem on polyhedral surfaces)
.

When
\Gamma
is polygonal or polyhedral, we cannot expect global
H^{\sigma}
regularity when
\sigma\geq\frac{3}{2}
because these spaces are degenerate across vertices, but in many circumstances facewise
H^{r}
regularity of
\kappa
with
r\geq\frac{3}{2}
does hold. Such facewise regularity is sufficient to achieve higher-order approximation by finite element methods.

When
n=2
and thus
\Gamma
is a piecewise affine closed Lipschitz curve, the weak solution
\kappa\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
to (
5.17
) is continuous by a Sobolev embedding. Denote by
\{\Gamma_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}
the faces of
\Gamma
. On each face
\kappa
thus solves a classical two-point boundary value problem
-\kappa^{\prime\prime}+\kappa=f
with right hand side data
f\in H^{-1+\delta}(\Gamma_{j})
,
\delta\geq 0
. Thus
\kappa\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
and
v|_{\Gamma_{j}}\in H^{1+\delta}(\Gamma_{j})
,
j=1,...,J
.

The situation is more complex when
n=3
. From
[
22
, Theorem 8]
there again holds that
\kappa\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
with
\kappa
possibly more regular on each face
\Gamma_{i}
. However, now the facewise regularity depends on the geometry of
\Gamma
around each vertex in addition to the smoothness of the right hand side. Let
L
be the maximum length of the perimeter of the spherical caps of radius 1 covering the vertices
v
of
\Gamma
. If
f\in H^{-1+\delta}(\Gamma)
, then for each face
\Gamma_{j}
there holds
\kappa|_{\Gamma_{j}}\in H^{\sigma}(\Gamma_{j})
with
\sigma<{\rm min}(1+\delta,1+\frac{2\pi}{L})
. If
\Omega
is convex then it is easy to see that
L<2\pi
and thus
\kappa|_{\Gamma_{j}}\in H^{1+\delta-\epsilon}(\Gamma_{j})
for any
\epsilon>0
and
\delta\leq 1
. When
\Omega
is not convex, then regularity may be more restricted. For example, the spherical cap for the central vertex of a classical two-brick domain has perimeter
L=3\pi
(cf.
[
26
, pg. 19]
for an illustration). Thus even if the right hand side data is infinitely smooth the facewise regularity of
\kappa
is restricted to
H^{5/3-\epsilon}
. A pathological example in
[
22
]
indicates that polyhedral domains
\Omega
can be constructed with
L
arbitrarily large, and thus the facewise regularity of
\kappa
may be only
H^{1+\epsilon}
with
\epsilon
arbitrarily small.

With these considerations we make the following assumption where
\|\kappa\|_{\tilde{H}^{\xi}(\Gamma)}
denotes the standard
H^{\xi}(\Gamma)
norm if
\xi<\frac{3}{2}
, and
(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|\kappa\|_{H^{\xi}(\Gamma_{j})}^{2})^{1/2}
if
\xi\geq\frac{3}{2}
with
\{\Gamma_{i}\}_{i=1}^{J}
denoting the faces (or edges when
n=2
) of
\Gamma
.

Assumption 5.8

(Regularity pickup on
\Gamma
)
.

For
\frac{1}{2}<s<\frac{3}{2}
and
\delta>0
, there exists
0<R(s,\delta)\leq 2-s
such that
if
\gamma\in H^{-s+\delta}(\Gamma)
,
then the solution
\kappa
to (
5.17
) belongs to
{\tilde{H}^{s+R(s,\delta)}(\Gamma)}
with

(5.18)

\|\kappa\|_{\tilde{H}^{s+R(s,\delta)}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\|\gamma\|_{H^{-s+\delta}(\Gamma)}.

Moreover, there holds

(5.19)

\min_{\kappa_{h}\in{\mathbb{T}}_{h}}\|\kappa-\kappa_{h}\|_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}\ \lesssim h^{R(s,\delta)}\|\kappa\|_{\tilde{H}^{s+R(s,\delta)}(\Gamma)}\lesssim h^{R(s,\delta)}\|\gamma\|_{H^{-s+\delta}(\Gamma)}.

The constants hidden in the
\lesssim
symbol depends on
s
,
\delta
, and the shape parameter
\sigma
.

Lax-Milgram theory guarantees that the solution
\kappa
in (
5.17
) is bounded in
H^{s}(\Gamma)
, with
\|\kappa\|_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}=\|\gamma\|_{H^{-s}(\Gamma)}
. Assumption
5.8
provides regularity beyond
H^{s}(\Gamma)
provided the data is smoother. For smooth domains,
R(s,\delta)=\delta
. For polygonal / polyhedral Lipschitz domains, we expect
R(s,\delta)>0
. The restriction
R(s,\delta)\leq 2-s
reflects the inability of piecewise linear finite element spaces to take advantage of higher regularity.

An important step in our argument below is bounding norms of harmonic extensions of solutions
\kappa
to elliptic problems on
\Gamma
. As quantified in the following proposition, the global
H^{\sigma}
regularity of
E\kappa
on
\Omega
is constrained both by the regularity of
\kappa
and by elliptic regularity on
\Omega
.

Proposition 5.9

(Bounds for harmonic extensions of solutions to elliptic problems on
\Gamma
)
.

Let
\beta_{\Omega}=\min(\beta^{*},1)
with
\beta^{*}
as in Assumption
5.1
and
R(s,\delta)
as in Assumption
5.8
for
\delta>0
. Consider
E\kappa
, where
\kappa
solves (
5.17
) with
\gamma\in H^{-s+\delta}(\Gamma)
and let

(5.20)

r(\delta)\ :=\ \min\left(\beta_{\Omega},s+R(s,\delta)-\frac{1}{2},1-\epsilon\right)

for some fixed (small)

(5.21)

\|E\kappa\|_{H^{r(\delta)+1}(\Omega)}\ \lesssim\|\gamma\|_{H^{-s+\delta}(\Gamma)}.

Proof.

This result directly follows from Proposition
5.2
with

and (
5.18
).

Finally, we state assumptions concerning the regularity of the solution
u_{0}
to
-\Delta u_{0}=f
with

on
\Gamma
. We have already noted that in order to make sense of interior point measurements we require
u_{0}\in C(\Omega)
(and thus obtain

according to (

)). We thus require that

for
\beta_{\Omega}>\max(0,n/2-1)
.

Beyond ensuring that
u_{0}\in C(\Omega)
, we also need assumptions that guaranty the convergence rates of finite element approximations of
u_{0}
in order to ensure that (
3.11
) in the recovery algorithm holds, namely

|u_{h0}-u_{0}|_{X}+\max_{i=1,...,m}|u_{h0}(x_{i})-u_{0}(x_{i})|\ \leq\ \epsilon_{1}.

The types of regularity needed and thus the assumptions we make here depend on the choice of
(X,|.|_{X})
used to measure the recovery error as well as the need to guarantee that
\max_{i=1,...,m}|u_{0h}(x_{i})-u_{0}(x_{i})|
is small. We assume that Assumption
5.1
is satisfied for some
\beta^{*}
and recall that
\beta_{\Omega}=\min(1,\beta^{*})
.

For the approximation of
u_{0}
at the measurement locations, recall that
x_{i}\in\Omega_{d}
and local (interior) pointwise error estimates
[
53
]
yield for any

that

(5.22)

|(u_{0}-u_{h0})(z)|\ \lesssim\ \ln\left(\frac{d}{2h}\right)\inf_{\chi\in\mathbb{V}_{h}^{0}}\|u_{0}-\chi\|_{L_{\infty}(B_{d/2}(z))}+d^{-n/2}\|u_{0}-u_{h0}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}.

Here we have assumed that
d\geq c_{0}h
with
c_{0}
sufficiently large. A standard duality argument yields for
0<\beta_{f}\leq\beta_{\Omega}
and

(5.23)

\|u_{0}-u_{h0}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\ \lesssim\ h^{\beta_{\Omega}+\beta_{f}}\|f\|_{H^{-1+\beta_{f}}(\Omega)}.

When
u_{0}
is sufficiently smooth, standard finite element approximation theory yields

\inf_{\chi\in\mathbb{V}_{h}^{0}}\|u_{0}-\chi\|_{L_{\infty}(B_{d/2}(z))}\ \lesssim\ h^{\tau_{d}}|u|_{C^{\ell,\tilde{\tau}}(\Omega_{d}/4)},

where
\Omega_{d/4}=\{x\in\Omega:{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)\}>d/4
. Here
0<\tau_{d}\leq 2
,
\ell=0
when
0<\tau_{d}\leq 1
,
\ell=1
when
1<\tau_{d}\leq 2
, and
\tilde{\tau}=\tau_{d}-\ell
. The range of
\tau_{d}
values allowed depends on interior Hölder regularity of
u_{0}
, which in turn depends only on the regularity of
f
. Such regularity may for example be obtained for
0<\tau_{d}<1
by assuming
f\in L_{q}(\Omega)
for appropriate
q>\frac{n}{2}

[
36
, Theorem 8.24]
, for
1<\tau_{d}<2
by assuming

[
36
, Section 8.11]
, and for
\tau_{d}=2
by assuming
f\in C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)
for any
\alpha>0

[
36
, Chapter 4]
. Such results could alternatively be obtained by employing interior

regularity results and then using Sobolev embeddings to obtain appropriate Hölder continuity of

. Under these assumptions, we finally obtain

(5.24)

|(u_{0}-u_{h0})(z)|\ \leq\ \left(h^{\tau_{d}}\ln\left(\frac{d}{2h}\right)+d^{-n/2}h^{\beta_{\Omega}+\beta_{f}}\right)C_{f},

where
C_{f}
depends on
f
.

We now address the approximation of
u_{0}
in
(X,|.|_{X})
for the three cases considered.

Case 1:

X=H^{1}(\Omega)
and
|.|_{X}=\|.\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
. Standard finite element theory and (

) guarantee that for
\max(-1,n/2-2)<\beta_{f}\leq\beta_{\Omega}
and

, we have

(5.25)

\|u_{0}-u_{h0}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\ \lesssim\ h^{\beta_{f}}\|f\|_{H^{-1+\beta_{f}}(\Omega)}.

Case 2:

X=L_{\infty}(\Omega)
and
|.|_{X}=\|.\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}
. For any polygonal domain when

[
54
]
and for convex polyhedral domains when

, it is known that

\|u_{0}-u_{h0}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\ \lesssim\ \ln\left(\frac{1}{h}\right)\inf_{\chi\in\mathbb{V}_{h}^{0}}\|u-\chi\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}.

We are unaware of similar results in the literature for nonconvex polyhedral domains in three space dimensions. Accordingly, we assume that there exists

such that

(5.26)

\|u_{0}-u_{h0}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ C_{f,\Omega}\ln\left(\frac{1}{h}\right)h^{\tau_{\Omega}}.

The allowed range of values of

will depend on the regularity of

(cf.
[

]
).

Case 3:

X=\{v\in L_{2}(\Omega)\ |\ v|_{\Omega_{d}}\in L_{\infty}(\Omega_{d})\}
and
|.|_{X}=\|.\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega_{d})}
. The approximation of

by

6.
Error estimates on Riesz representers

Before stating and proving our main results, we make some comments. For sake of notational simplicity we suppress the dependence of the Riesz representers on
i
, letting
\psi\in H^{s}(\Gamma)
satisfy

(6.1)

\langle\psi,g\rangle_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}\ =\ \nu(Eg)=Eg(\overline{x}),\quad g\in H^{s}(\Gamma)

for some
\overline{x}\in\{x_{1},...,x_{m}\}
. Also,
\phi=E\psi
. Analogously, we let
\phi_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
be the finite element approximation of
\phi
; that is,
\phi_{h}=E_{h}\psi_{h}
, where
\psi_{h}\in\mathbb{T}_{h}
is the solution to

(6.2)

\langle\psi_{h},g_{h}\rangle_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}\ =\ E_{h}g_{h}(\overline{x}),\quad g_{h}\in\mathbb{T}_{h}.

6.1.
Pointwise error estimates

Now we may state the first major theoretical result of our paper, which is a variant of
[
9
, Theorem 4.7]
. That paper established both
H^{1}
and (global)

-error estimates for the Riesz representers in the case of pointwise evaluations. Here we refine the latter estimate to make explicit the dependence of the error on the distances between the measurement locations and
\Gamma
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Assume that
\frac{1}{2}<s<\frac{3}{2}
. Let
\beta^{*}
be as in Assumption
5.1
and set
\beta_{\Omega}=\min(\beta^{*},1)
.
Let also
\mu,\tilde{\mu}\geq 0
be as in Assumption
5.3
,
R(.,.)
as in Assumption
5.8
, and
r(.)
be given by (
5.20
).
Let
d\geq c_{0}h
with
c_{0}>0
sufficiently large. For
z\in\Omega
with
{\rm dist}(z,\Gamma)\geq d
, if
t_{p}:=\min\{2R(s,s+\mu),r(s+\mu)+\beta_{\Omega}\}
, there holds

(6.3)

|(\phi-\phi_{h})(z)|\ \lesssim\ h^{t_{p}}.

The implicit constant depends on
s
, the geometry of
\Omega
(in particular through the constant
c_{0}
),
d
, and the elliptic regularity parameter
\beta_{\Omega}
. More explicitly,

(6.4)

|(\phi-\phi_{h})(z)|\ \lesssim\ d^{-n-2\tilde{\mu}}h^{2R(s,s+\mu)}+d^{-n-\tilde{\mu}}h^{r(s+\mu)+\beta_{\Omega}}.

Remark 6.2

(Expected orders of convergence)
.

Before proving Theorem
6.1
we explain how the parameters
R(s,s+\mu)
,
\beta_{\Omega}
, and
r(s+\mu)
may interact in the concrete case
s=1
corresponding to the classical Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\Gamma
. When
\Omega
is convex, we have
\beta_{\Omega}=1
. Taking
\mu=0
, per Remark
5.7
we can take
R(s,s+\mu)=R(1,1)=1-\frac{\epsilon}{2}
for any
\epsilon>0
. Finally, we have
r(s+\mu)=\min(\beta_{\Omega},s+R(s,s+\mu)-\frac{1}{2},1-\epsilon)=\min(1,\frac{3}{2}-\frac{\epsilon}{2},1-\epsilon)=1-epsilon
for any
\epsilon>0
. Thus our overall convergence rate is
d^{-n}h^{2R(s,s+\mu)}+h^{r(s+\mu)+\beta_{\Omega}}=d^{-n}h^{2-\epsilon}
for any
\epsilon>0
. When
\Omega
is non-convex
\beta_{\Omega}<1
. Per the discussion above there still always holds
R(1,1)=1
when
n=2
. When
n=3
, the value of
R(1,1)
varies with the geometry of
\Gamma
and is typically less than but not arbitrarily close to 1. The expected convergence rate is then at most
d^{-n}h^{2\beta_{\Omega}}
.

Much less information relevant to predicting expected rates of convergence is available in the literature when
s\neq 1
. It is however the case that
R(s,s+\mu)\leq 2-s
, so the predicted convergence rate when
s>1
is at most
2(2-s)<2
. When
s<1
, the convergence rate is still limited by 2, but the effect of the term
R(s,s+\mu)
is less clear as we are not aware of references establishing regularity of fractional order problems on polyhedral surfaces.

Proof.

Let
0<r<1
with
r\leq\beta_{\Omega}
, and let
g_{h}\in\mathbb{T}_{h}\subset H^{r+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
be arbitrary.

Step 1:

6.26
)-(
6.27
), we find that

(6.45)

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle(E-E_{h})\psi_{h}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ln\left(\frac{1}{h}\right)h^{r(s+\mu)+1-\frac{n}{2}}|E\psi_{h}|_{C^{r(s+\mu)+1-\frac{n}{2}}(\Omega)}\lesssim\ln\left(\frac{1}{h}\right)h^{r(s+\mu)+1-\frac{n}{2}}\|E\psi_{h}\|_{H^{r(s+\mu)+1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ln\left(\frac{1}{h}\right)h^{r(s+\mu)+1-\frac{n}{2}}\|\psi_{h}\|_{H^{r(s+\mu)+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\ln\left(\frac{1}{h}\right)d^{-n/2-\tilde{\mu}}h^{r(s+\mu)+1-\frac{n}{2}}.

In order to bound
\|E(\psi-\psi_{h})\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}
, first assume that
s>\frac{n-1}{2}
and let
s>\theta>\frac{n-1}{2}
. Then employing the maximum principle, a Sobolev embedding, and proceeding as in (
6.36
)-(
6.38
) with

replacing

in the arguments we find

\|E(\psi-\psi_{h})\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|\psi-\psi_{h}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\|\psi-\psi_{h}\|_{H^{\theta}(\Gamma)}\lesssim d^{-n/2-\tilde{\mu}}h^{R(s,s+\mu)+R(s,s-\theta)}+d^{-n/2}h^{r(s-\theta)+\beta_{\Omega}}.

If instead

, we use a Sobolev embedding, inverse estimate, approximation properties, and (
6.19
) to obtain

(6.47)

\displaystyle\|E(\psi-\bar{\psi}_{h})\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi-\pi_{h}\psi\|_{H^{\theta}(\Gamma)}+h^{s-\theta}(\|\psi-\bar{\psi}_{h}\|_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}+\|\psi-\pi_{h}\psi\|_{H^{s}(\Gamma)})

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{s-\theta+R(s,s+\mu)}d^{-n/2-\tilde{\mu}}.

A Sobolev embedding, inverse estimate, and (

(6.48)

\|E(\bar{\psi}_{h}-\psi_{h})\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim h^{s-\theta}\|\bar{\psi}_{h}-\psi_{h}\|_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}\lesssim d^{-n/2}h^{\beta_{\Omega}+2s-\theta-\frac{1}{2}}.

Combining the Triangle Inequality with (

Remark 6.7
.

We compare the powers of
h
in (
6.44
) when
s>\frac{n-1}{2}
versus when
s\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
. Recall that

with equality occurring when sufficient elliptic regularity holds. In the latter case

, i.e., the powers of

in the second term take the same form in both cases. Also, recall that

When the minimum occurs in the third term and sufficient elliptic regularity holds, we thus have

We may use this to derive an error estimate for an optimal recovery in

.

Suppose the assumptions of Theorem

Proof.

Since

6.5.
Smooth domains and higher order elements

In this section we address optimality of our estimates in the context of smooth domains and possible extension to higher-order elements.

The convergence results in (
6.50
) and Table
1
are near-optimal for
\|\phi-\phi_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
and
\|\phi-\phi_{h}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega_{d})}
when using linear elements. However, the stated convergence rates of
h^{2-\frac{n}{2}-\epsilon}
for
\|\phi-\phi_{h}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}
are highly suboptimal when full regularity is present. The suboptimality is due to the use of Sobolev embeddings to bound
L_{\infty}
norms by
L_{2}
-based Sobolev norms. In order to obtain optimal estimates it is necessary to make sharper arguments both in bounding
\|(E-E_{h})\psi_{h}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}
as in (
6.45
) and
\|\psi-\psi_{h}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}
, which is an intermediate term in (
6.46
). Below we assume that a degree-
\zeta
(
\zeta\geq 1
) finite element space of Lagrange type is used and sketch a proof of optimal order
h^{k+1}
error estimates in the case
s=1
. Some steps also apply to the general case
\frac{1}{2}<s<\frac{3}{2}
, but we do not provide a full proof because critical
L_{\infty}
error estimates on
\Gamma
are only available when
s=1
.

Let now
\Gamma
be smooth. Assume that
\Omega
and
\Gamma
are meshed exactly with compatible meshes, and let
V_{h}
be a (parametric) finite element space of degree
\zeta
defined on this mesh. Assume that
P_{h}:H^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow V_{h}
is a Scott-Zhang type interpolant which coincides with
\pi_{h}
on
\Gamma
, as above. Further assume that
P_{h}
and
\pi_{h}
possess standard properties even though
\Gamma
is curved. We shall also employ standard finite element error estimates without specific reference. In summary, we work within an idealized setting which preserves standard error estimates and approximation properties and does not introduce any variational crimes due to approximation of
\Omega
or
\Gamma
.

We first compute using standard maximum norm stability results, the maximum principle, and
(P_{h}E\psi)|_{\Gamma}=\pi_{h}\psi
that

(6.52)

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle\phi-\phi_{h}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|(E-E_{h})\psi_{h}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|E(\psi-\psi_{h})\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|(E-E_{h})(\psi_{h}-\pi_{h}\psi)\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|(E-E_{h})\pi_{h}\psi\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\psi-\psi_{h}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ \ell_{h}\left[\inf_{\chi_{1}\in V_{h},\chi_{1}|_{\Gamma}=\psi_{h}-\pi_{h}\psi}\|E(\psi_{h}-\pi_{h}\psi)-\chi_{1}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}+\inf_{\chi_{2}\in V_{h},\chi_{2}|\Gamma=\pi_{h}\psi}\|E\pi_{h}\psi-\chi_{2}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}\right]

\displaystyle+\|\psi-\psi_{h}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ \ell_{h}\left[\|\psi_{h}-\pi_{h}\psi\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}+\|E(\pi_{h}\psi-\psi)\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|E\psi-P_{h}E\psi\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\right]+\|\psi-\psi_{h}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ \ell_{h}\left[\|\psi-\psi_{h}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}+\|\psi-\pi_{h}\psi\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}+h^{\zeta+1}\|E\psi\|_{W_{\infty}^{\zeta+1}(\Omega)}\right].

Here we let
\ell_{h}=\ln\frac{1}{h}
, and have chosen
\chi_{1}
so that
\|\chi_{1}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}=\|\psi_{h}-\pi_{h}\psi\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}
.

Sobolev embeddings and regularity of
E
yield for any
\epsilon>0

(6.53)

\|E\psi\|_{W_{\infty}^{\zeta+1}(\Omega)}\ \lesssim\ \|E\psi\|_{H^{\zeta+1+\frac{n}{2}+\epsilon}(\Omega)}\ \lesssim\ \|\psi\|_{H^{\zeta+\frac{n+1}{2}+\epsilon}(\Gamma)}.

Let
\tilde{\mu}(s,\zeta)
be the smallest integer strictly larger than
\zeta+\frac{n}{2}-2s
. Note that
\zeta\geq 1
,
n\geq 2
, and
s<\frac{3}{2}
yield
\tilde{\mu}(s,\zeta)\geq 0
. Recall that
\psi
solves an elliptic problem over
H^{s}(\Gamma)
with right hand side
\gamma_{z}
with
\gamma_{z}(g)=Eg(z)
. Proposition
5.5
and (
5.6
) yield
\gamma_{z}\in H^{\zeta+\frac{n+1}{2}-2s}(\Gamma)
. Because
\zeta+\frac{n+1}{2}-2s+\epsilon\leq\tilde{\mu}(s,\zeta)+\frac{1}{2}
for
\epsilon
small enough, setting
\mu=\tilde{\mu}(s,\zeta)+\frac{1}{2}
in (
5.6
) yields
\|\gamma_{z}\|_{H^{\zeta+\frac{n+1}{2}-2s+\epsilon}(\Gamma)}\lesssim d^{-n/2-\tilde{\mu}(s,\zeta)}
. Thus by a shift theorem,

(6.54)

\|E\psi\|_{W_{\infty}^{\zeta+1}(\Omega)}\ \lesssim\ \|\gamma_{z}\|_{H^{\zeta+\frac{n+1}{2}-2s+\epsilon}(\Gamma)}\lesssim d^{-n/2-\tilde{\mu}(s,\zeta)}.

Similarly,

(6.55)

\|\psi-\pi_{h}\psi\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}\ \lesssim\ h^{\zeta+1}\|\psi\|_{H^{\zeta+1+\frac{n}{2}+\epsilon}(\Gamma)}\lesssim h^{\zeta+1}d^{-n/2-\tilde{\mu}(s,\zeta)}.

We are left to bound
\|\psi-\psi_{h}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}
. Such pointwise estimates are not known for fractional
s
, but when
s=1

[
27
, Theorem 3.2]
yields for finite element approximations for the Laplace-Beltrami operator
-\Delta_{\Gamma}
that
\|\psi-\bar{\psi}_{h}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\ell_{h}h^{\zeta+1}d^{-n/2-\tilde{\mu}(s,\zeta)}
; the same estimate can be shown to hold for the operator
-\Delta_{\Gamma}+I
with modest modifications.

We now bound
\|\bar{\psi}_{h}-\psi_{h}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}
. Let
x\in\Gamma
satisfy
\|\bar{\psi}_{h}-\psi_{h}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}=|(\bar{\psi}_{h}-\psi_{h})(x)|
.
Let
G^{x}\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
and
G_{h}^{x}\in\mathbb{T}_{h}
be the discrete Green’s function corresponding to
x\in\Gamma
and its
H^{1}(\Gamma)
Galerkin approximation, respectively. In particular,
-\Delta_{\Gamma}G^{x}+G^{x}=\delta_{h,x}
, where
\delta_{h,x}
is smooth, locally supported in the element containing
x
, and satisfies
\|\delta_{h,x}\|_{L_{p}(\Gamma)}\lesssim h^{-(n-1)+\frac{n-1}{p}}
. Then

(6.56)

(\bar{\psi}_{h}-\psi_{h})(x)\ =\ \langle\bar{\psi}_{h}-\psi_{h},G_{h}^{x}\rangle_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}\ =\ (E-E_{h})G_{h}^{x}(z).

Let
p=\frac{n}{n-1}
. Local error estimates (cf.
[
53
]
), a duality argument, and inverse estimates for harmonic functions yield that

(6.57)

\displaystyle|(E-E_{h})G_{h}^{x}(z)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\ \ell_{h}h^{\zeta+1}|EG_{h}^{x}|_{W_{\infty}^{\zeta+1}(B_{d}(z))}+d^{-n/p-\zeta+1}\|(E-E_{h})G_{h}^{x}\|_{W_{p}^{-\zeta+1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ \ell_{h}h^{\zeta+1}d^{-n/p-\zeta}\|EG_{h}^{x}\|_{W_{p}^{1}(\Omega)}+d^{-n/2-\zeta+1}h^{\zeta}\|(E-E_{h})G_{h}^{x}\|_{W_{p}^{1}(\Omega)}.

Employing the existence of a bounded inverse to the trace map
T:W_{p}^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow W_{p}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}(\Gamma)
along with
W_{p}^{1}(\Omega)
regularity of Poisson’s problem, properties of discrete Green’s functions (cf.
[
27
, Lemma 3.3]
), and a Sobolev embedding, we find that

(6.58)

\displaystyle\|EG_{h}^{x}\|_{W_{p}^{1}(\Omega)}\ \lesssim\ \|G_{h}^{x}\|_{W_{p}^{1-1/p}(\Gamma)}\ \lesssim\ \|G_{h}^{x}\|_{W_{1}^{1}(\Gamma)}\ \lesssim\ \|G_{h}^{x}-G^{x}\|_{W_{1}^{1}(\Gamma)}+\|G^{x}\|_{W_{1}^{2}(\Gamma)}\ \lesssim\ \ell_{h}.

Next we employ
W_{p}^{1}
stability of the Ritz projection
[
19
, Theorem 8.5.3]
,
P_{h}v|_{\Gamma}=\pi_{h}(v_{\Gamma})
, approximation properties of
\Pi_{h}
, and the existence of a bounded inverse to the trace map
T:W_{p}^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow W_{p}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}(\Gamma)
(and similarly for
T:W_{p}^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow W_{p}^{2-\frac{1}{p}}(\Gamma)\times W_{p}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}(\Gamma)
) along with
W_{p}^{1}(\Omega)
and
W_{p}^{2}(\Omega)
regularity of Poisson’s problem, Sobolev embeddings, and finally
[
27
, Lemma 3.3]
to compute

(6.59)

\displaystyle\|(E-E_{h})G_{h}^{x}\|_{W_{p}^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|(E-E_{h})(G_{h}^{x}-\pi_{h}G^{x})\|_{W_{p}^{1}(\Omega)}+\|(E-E_{h})\pi_{h}G^{x}\|_{W_{p}^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|E(G_{h}^{x}-\pi_{h}G^{x})\|_{W_{p}^{1}(\Gamma)}+\inf_{\chi\in\mathbb{V}_{h},\chi|_{\Gamma}=\pi_{h}G^{x}}\|E\pi_{h}G_{h}^{x}-\chi\|_{W_{p}^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|E(G_{h}^{x}-\pi_{h}G^{x})\|_{W_{p}^{1}(\Omega)}+\|E(\pi_{h}G^{x}-G^{x})\|_{W_{p}^{1}(\Omega)}+\|EG^{x}-P_{h}EG^{x}\|_{W_{p}^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|G^{x}-G_{h}^{x}\|_{W_{p}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}(\Gamma)}+\|G^{x}-\pi_{h}G^{x}\|_{W_{p}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}(\Gamma)}+h\|G^{x}\|_{W_{p}^{2-\frac{1}{p}}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|G^{x}-G_{h}^{x}\|_{W_{1}^{1}(\Gamma)}+\|G^{x}-\pi_{h}G^{x}\|_{W_{1}^{1}(\Gamma)}+h\|G^{x}\|_{W_{1}^{2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h\ell_{h}.

Recalling that
p=\frac{n}{n-1}
and using (
6.56
), (
6.57
), we have

\|\bar{\psi}_{h}-\psi_{h}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}\lesssim h^{\zeta+1}\ell_{h}(d^{-n/p-\zeta}+d^{-\frac{n}{p}-\zeta+1})\lesssim h^{\zeta+1}\ell_{h}d^{-(n-1)-\zeta}.

Now recalling that
\tilde{\mu}(s,1)
is the smallest integer larger than
\zeta+\frac{n}{2}-2s=\zeta-\frac{n}{2}-2
, we have
d^{-n/2-\tilde{\mu}(s,\zeta)}\leq d^{-n-\zeta+1}
. Combining the previous inequalities thus yields the following proposition.

Proposition 6.9
.

Assume that
\Gamma
is smooth, that
\mathbb{V}_{h}
consists of degree-
\zeta
Lagrange elements possessing standard approximation properties on a mesh that exactly fits
\Omega
and
\Gamma
, that
d\geq c_{0}h
with
c_{0}
sufficiently large, and that
s=1
. Then

(6.60)

\|\phi-\phi_{h}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\ \lesssim\ h^{\zeta+1}(\ell_{h})^{2}d^{-n-\zeta+1}.

We do not give a proof outline for similar optimal-order estimates for
|(\phi-\phi_{h})(z)|
or
\|\phi-\phi_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
in the context of smooth domains and higher order elements. The critical modifications are however similar. In particular, note the steps taken in (
6.52
) to ensure that the smooth function
E\psi
is approximated on
\Omega
instead of the rougher function
E\psi_{h}
as in the previous proofs. These steps are unnecessary when
\zeta=1
and can complicate the final estimates on polyhedral domains where regularity gains are limited, but in the context of higher order elements they are needed to ensure interpolants are only applied to functions having maximum smoothness.

7.
Numerical results

7.1.
Saddle point formulation

While (
4.25
) is the most streamlined way of posing our finite element approximation of the Riesz representers, our implementation instead proceeds by solving an equivalent saddle point problem. To pose this problem, we define

(7.1)

X^{s}(\Omega)\ :=\ \{v\in H^{1}(\Omega)\ |\ v_{\Gamma}\in H^{s}(\Gamma)\}

which is a Hilbert space when equipped with the norm

(7.2)

\|v\|_{X^{s}(\Omega)}\ :=\ (\|v_{\Gamma}\|^{2}_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}+\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then solving (
5.17
) is equivalent to finding the solution
(\phi,\pi)\in X^{s}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
of the saddle point problem

(7.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle\phi_{\Gamma},v_{\Gamma}\rangle_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}+\langle\nabla v,\nabla\pi\rangle_{L_{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}\ &=\ \nu(v),\ v\in X^{s}(\Omega)\\
\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla z\rangle_{L_{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}\ &=\ 0,\ \ \ \ \ z\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).\end{aligned}

The well-posedness of (
7.3
) (and the corresponding discretized form) are described in Subsection 4.2 of
[
9
]
. As is also discussed there, solving (
4.25
) requires computing a discrete harmonic extension of every basis function for the load vector and then solving a separate linear system for each extension. Meanwhile, numerically approximating (
7.3
) allows us to enforce discrete harmonicity and Riesz representer adherence sequentially, substantially reducing computation time.

For the numerical approximation of (
7.3
) when
s
is nonintegral we refer to the algorithm proposed and analyzed in
[
13
]
in the context of optimal recovery. It is based on the Balakrishnan representation of the solution to fractional diffusion problems as originally proposed for Euclidean domains in
[
15
,
18
,
17
]
and extended to fractional diffusion problems on surfaces in
[
16
]
. However, the numerical experiments proposed below are for
s=1
, which is sufficient to illustrate the results obtained in this paper.

7.2.
Experimental data

We will provide three different sets of numerical results on open bounded domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
s=1
and
f=0
(which means that
u_{0}=0
). In each setting discussed below, we consider sequences of uniformly refined sub-divisions
\{\mathcal{T}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{*}}
. Therefore, rather than indexing the finite element quantities by the associated meshsize
h\approx 2^{-(k+1)}
, we use the iteration number
k
. Furthermore, the numerical simulations presented below use the deal.ii library
[
3
]
, which is mainly designed for sub-divisions made of quadrilaterals. Consequently, for a sub-division
\mathcal{T}_{k}
of
\Omega
made of (possibly curved) quadrilaterals, the finite element space is

(7.4)

\mathbb{V}_{k}\ :=\ \{v\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})\ |\ v|_{T}\circ F_{T}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\zeta},\ \forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}\},

for some
\zeta\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, where
\mathbb{Q}_{\zeta}
denotes the space of continuous piecewise degree-
\zeta
polynomials in each coordinate directions and
F_{T}:[0,1]^{2}\rightarrow T
is the mapping from the reference element to the physical element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
.
Although the theory presented above does not strictly apply to quadrilaterals, we expect it to extend in this setting as well. Throughout this section
\Omega_{d}=\{x\in\Omega\ |\ \mathop{\rm dist}(x,\Gamma)>d\}
, for a distance
d
that is prescribed by the configuration of measurements, the minimal distance from a measurement point to
\Gamma
.

Several general comments are in order. First, we focus on reporting errors for the Riesz representers since our central theoretical results are the quantitative error estimates of Theorems
6.1
,

6.4
, and
6.6
, though we also include numerical validation of Corollaries
6.3
and
6.8
for a square domain. Second, since analytical formulas for Riesz representers
\phi
cannot be found, we report
\|\phi_{K}-\phi_{k}\|
, where
K>k
and treat
\phi_{K}
as a numerical surrogate for
\phi
when
K
is large.

7.3.
Numerical results for Riesz representers

7.3.1.
Interior measurement points on domains of varying smoothness

We first let
\Omega=B(0,1)
be the open unit disc and let
\mathcal{T}_{k}
,
k=1,...,9
, be a sequence of transfinite meshes exactly subdividing
\Omega
. The initial sub-division
\mathcal{T}_{1}
consists of
4
uniform squares whose boundary is curved to match
\Gamma
. Subsequent sub-divisions are obtained by uniform refinements, always mapping the boundary edges to
\Gamma

We finally make a note on algorithmic implementation. The near-optimal recovery theorem (Theorem
3.4
) assumes that the matrix
G
is invertible. Depending on the location of the measurements,
G
might be singular or nearly singular. Moreover, it is expected that the condition number of
G
increases as
m
increases. This leads to numerical difficulties as reported in
[
9
,
13
]
. In the following, we follow the strategy proposed in
[
9
]
, which consists of applying a thresholding that eliminates the small eigenvalues of
G
. We refer to
[
2
]
for a review and analysis of this regularization strategy. Empirically, we observe that the amount of thresholding necessary to obtain robust recovery can be decreased as the finite element resolution increases but in order not to obfuscate the purpose of the numerical simulation provided below, we fix the thresholding to
10^{-14}
. This means that the singular values of
G
that are smaller than
10^{-14}\lambda_{\textrm{max}}
are discarded when computing the Moore-Penrose pseudo inverse of
G
. Here
\lambda_{\textrm{max}}
is the largest singular value of
G
.

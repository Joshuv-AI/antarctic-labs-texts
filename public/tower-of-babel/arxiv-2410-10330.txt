Abstract
. The paper focuses on the conformal Lorentz geometry of quasi-umbilical timelike surfaces in the ( 1 + 2 ) (1+2) -Einstein universe, the conformal compactification of Minkowski 3-space realized as the space of oriented null lines through the origin of ℝ 2 , 3 \mathbb{R}^{2,3} . A timelike immersion of a surface X X in the Einstein universe is quasi-umbilical if its shape operator at any point of X X is non-diagonalizable over ℂ \mathbb{C} . We prove that quasi-umbilical surfaces are isothermic, that their conformal deformations depend on one arbitrary function in one variable, and show that their conformal Gauss map is harmonic. We investigate their geometric structure and show how to construct all quasi-umbilical surfaces from null curves in the 4-dimensional neutral space form S 2 , 2 = { x ∈ ℝ 2 , 3 ∣ ⟨ x , x ⟩ = 1 } S^{2,2}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2,3}\mid\langle x,x\rangle=1\} .

Background and motivations.

Description of results.
Adapting to our case a basic construction in Möbius geometry
that goes back to the work of Blaschke and Thomsen
[

28

we can define the conformal Gauss map
\mathcal{N}_{f}:X\to{{S}}^{2,2}
of a timelike immersion.
(For the study of spacelike immersions in conformal Lorentz geometry, and more specifically of spacelike Willmore surfaces,
we refer to
[

]
and
[
30

Geometrically,
\mathcal{N}_{f}
associates to a point
{x}\in X
the unique totally umbilical timelike torus
of the Einstein universe having
second order analytic contact with
f
at
f({x})
,
the so-called
central torus
of the surface at
f(x)
. If
f
is quasi-umbilical, then

\mathcal{N}_{f}
has rank 1, so that
f
is the envelope of
a 1-parameter family of totally umbilical Lorentz tori. The second envelope of the family, denoted by
f^{\sharp}:X\to\mathcal{E}^{1,2}
,
is referred to as the
dual
of
f
.
A quasi-umbilical immersion
f
is called
regular
if
\Lambda
,
the leaf space of the vertical distribution
\mathrm{Ker\,}d\mathcal{N}_{f}
, is a connected 1-dimensional manifold,
so that
\mathcal{N}_{f}
factors on
\Lambda

through a submersion

\pi_{\Lambda}:X\to\Lambda
with connected fibers
and a null curve
\Gamma:\Lambda\to{{S}}^{2,2}
, that is,
\mathcal{N}_{f}=\Gamma\circ\pi_{\Lambda}
.
The curve
\Gamma
is called the

directrix curve
of
f
. Locally, every quasi-umbilical immersion is regular.
The first main result of the paper is the following.

Theorem A.

Let
f:X\to\mathcal{E}^{1,2}
be a quasi-umbilical timelike immersion. Then the conformal Gauss map

\mathcal{N}_{f}:X\to{{S}}^{2,2}
of
f
is harmonic.
In addition, if
f
is regular, then

f
is isothermic and its infinitesimal deformations depend on one arbitrary function in one variable
.

According to whether the quadratic form
\langle d\mathcal{N}_{f},df^{\sharp}\rangle
is identically zero or not,
a quasi-umbilical immersion
f
is called
exceptional
, respectively,
general
.
We will separately describe the geometric structure of the two classes of immersions under the
assumption of regularity.

Exceptional quasi-umbilical surfaces.

First, we prove that a quasi-umbilical
f
is exceptional if and only if its directrix curve

\Gamma
is a
biisotropic
curve of
{{S}}^{2,2}
, meaning that
\Gamma^{\prime}

and
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
are linearly independent null vector fields (cf. Proposition
4.2
).
We show that biisotropic curves can be constructed from suitable plane curves of a unimodular affine plane.
Then, associated to any biisotropic curve
\Gamma:\Lambda\to{{S}}^{2,2}
, we consider the
normal tube

{\mathbb{T}}_{\Gamma}\cong\Lambda\times S^{1}
of
\Gamma
and
construct a canonical map
f_{\Gamma}:{\mathbb{T}}_{\Gamma}\to\mathcal{E}^{1,2}
, the
tautological map

of
\Gamma
(cf. Section
4.1
and Definition
4.7
).
The second main result accounts for the geometric structure of exceptional quasi-umbilical surfaces.

Theorem B.

Let
\Gamma:\Lambda\to{{S}}^{2,2}
be a biisotropic curve.
The tautological map
f_{\Gamma}
of
\Gamma
is a timelike immersion with conformal Gauss map
\Gamma\circ\pi_{\Lambda}
.
The umbilic locus of
f_{\Gamma}
is the disjoint union of two null curves

{\rm C}_{\pm}\subset{\mathbb{T}}_{\Gamma}

that disconnect
{\mathbb{T}}_{\Gamma}
into two open sets,

{\mathbb{T}}^{\pm}_{\Gamma}
. Moreover, the restrictions of
f_{\Gamma}
to
{\mathbb{T}}^{\pm}_{\Gamma}
are
exceptional quasi-umbilical immersions.
Conversely, if
f:X\to\mathcal{E}^{1,2}
is a regular quasi-umbilical immersion of exceptional type
and
\Gamma:\Lambda\to{{S}}^{2,2}
is its directrix curve, then
f(X)
is contained in either

f_{\Gamma}({\mathbb{T}}^{+}_{\Gamma})
or
f_{\Gamma}({\mathbb{T}}^{-}_{\Gamma})
.

This implies that, locally, exceptional quasi-umbilical surfaces arise as immersions of
a component of the normal tube of a biisotropic curve of
S^{2,2}
.
Another consequence is that quasi-umbilical immersions of exceptional type depend on one function
in one variable, namely the affine curvature of the affine curve generating the directrix curve.
Moreover, they are second order
deformations of each other (cf. Proposition
4.9
).
The general construction will be illustrated for the case in which the generating affine curve is a conic.

General quasi-umbilical surfaces.

We start by showing that the directrix curve
\Gamma
of a general quasi-umbilical immersion
is a
generic
null curve, meaning that
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
can be either
timelike or spacelike.
Then, proceeding in analogy with
[
40
,
41

43

\Gamma
we introduce
a preferred parametrization (proper time) and construct
a canonical moving frame along the curve, from which one determines
the two fundamental differential invariants of
\Gamma
, namely the
left and right curvatures

\kappa_{\lambda}
and
\kappa_{\varrho}
.
The canonical moving frame is used to build two normal tubes
{\mathbb{T}}_{\lambda}

and
{\mathbb{T}}_{\varrho}
along

\Gamma
, the
left and right normal tubes
of
\Gamma
,
naturally identified by a diffeomorphism
J^{\varrho}_{\lambda}
. The left and right tubes intersect along
two null curves
{\rm C}_{\pm}
, whose complementary set
has two connected components,

{\mathbb{T}}_{\lambda}^{\pm}
and
{\mathbb{T}}_{\varrho}^{\pm}
, the
parabolic components

of the normal tubes.
The canonical projections
\pi_{\lambda}:{\mathbb{T}}_{\lambda}\to\Lambda
and

\pi_{\varrho}:{\mathbb{T}}_{\varrho}\to\Lambda
make
{\mathbb{T}}_{\lambda}

and
{\mathbb{T}}_{\varrho}
into circle bundles over
\Lambda

two canonical maps,
f_{\lambda}:{\mathbb{T}}_{\lambda}\to\mathcal{E}^{1,2}
and

f_{\varrho}:{\mathbb{T}}_{\varrho}\to\mathcal{E}^{1,2}
,
the
left and right tautological maps
of
\Gamma
.
The third main result accounts for the structure of general quasi-umbilical surfaces.

Theorem C.

Let
\Gamma:\Lambda\to{{S}}^{2,2}
be a generic null curve.

(1)
The tautological map
f_{\lambda}
is a timelike immersion with
umbilic locus
{\rm C}_{+}\cup{\rm C}_{-}
and conformal Gauss map
{\mathcal{N}}_{f_{\lambda}}=\Gamma\circ\pi_{\lambda}
.
The restrictions
f^{\pm}_{\lambda}
of
f_{\lambda}
to the parabolic components

{\mathbb{T}}_{\lambda}^{\pm}
of the left normal tube
{\mathbb{T}}_{\lambda}

are quasi-umbilical immersions of general type
with dual maps
(f^{\pm}_{\lambda})^{\sharp}=f^{\pm}_{\varrho}\circ J^{\varrho}_{\lambda}
.

(2)
The tautological map
f_{\varrho}
is a timelike immersion with
umbilic locus
{\rm C}_{+}\cup{\rm C}_{-}
and conformal Gauss map
{\mathcal{N}}_{f_{\varrho}}=\Gamma\circ\pi_{\varrho}
.
The restrictions
f^{\pm}_{\varrho}
of
f_{\varrho}
to the parabolic components
{\mathbb{T}}_{\varrho}^{\pm}

of the right normal tube
{\mathbb{T}}_{\varrho}

are quasi-umbilical immersions of general type with
dual maps
(f^{\pm}_{\varrho})^{\sharp}=f^{\pm}_{\lambda}\circ J_{\varrho}^{\lambda}
.

Conversely, let
f:X\to\mathcal{E}^{1,2}
be a regular quasi-umbilical
immersion of general type. Then
f(X)
is contained in either

f_{\lambda}({\mathbb{T}}_{\lambda}^{+})
or
f_{\lambda}({\mathbb{T}}_{\lambda}^{-})
,
where
f_{\lambda}
is the left
tautological immersion originated by the directrix curve
\Gamma
of
f
.

Theorem
C
implies that quasi-umbilical immersions of general type depend on two
functions in one variable, namely the left and right curvatures of their directrix curves.
As for the deformation problem, we show that two quasi-umbilical immersions
of general type
f
and
\tilde{f}
are second order conformal deformations of each other if and only
if they have the same helicity (to be specified) and if either
\kappa_{\lambda}=\tilde{\kappa}_{\lambda}

or
\kappa_{\varrho}=\tilde{\kappa}_{\varrho}
(cf. Remark
5.5
).
The construction will be illustrated by an example at the end of Section
5
.

It is interesting to note that the construction of the canonical timelike immersions arising from biisotropic or
generic null curves of
{{S}}^{2,2}
is algorithmic and only involves algebraic manipulations and derivatives.
The parametrizations of quasi-umbilical immersions given in
[

can be obtained restricting the maps

to the subdomains of

that are sent into a Minkowski chamber of

[

24
]

Moreover, our construction allows us to exhibit quasi-umbilical immersed and embedded tori or cylinders,
a phenomenon that can never occur in 3-dimensional Minkowski geometry.

2.3.

4.2.
The structure of exceptional quasi-umbilical surfaces

Using the notation and the constructions of the previous section,
we are now in a position to describe the geometric structure of exceptional quasi-umbilical surfaces.
We can prove the following.

Theorem B
.

Let
\Gamma:\Lambda\to{{S}}^{2,2}
be a biisotropic curve. Then, the tautological map
f_{\Gamma}
is a timelike immersion
with conformal Gauss map
\Gamma\circ\pi_{{\Lambda}}
and umbilic locus
{\rm C}_{+}\cup{\rm C}_{-}
. In addition,

f^{\pm}_{\Gamma}
are exceptional quasi-umbilical immersions with helicities
\pm 1
and
dual maps
(f^{\pm}_{\Gamma})^{\sharp}=[\mp\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\circ\pi_{{\Lambda}}]
.
Conversely, let
f:X\to\mathcal{E}^{1,2}
be a regular quasi-umbilical immersion with helicity
\varepsilon=\pm 1
.
If
f
is exceptional, then

f(X)\subset f^{\pm}_{\Gamma}\left({\mathbb{T}}^{\pm}_{\Gamma}\right)
,
where
\Gamma
is the
directrix curve of
f
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{T}_{\Gamma}
be identified with

\Lambda\times S^{1}
as in (
4.8
). Take a positive area element
{dA}
on
{\mathbb{V}}_{\Gamma}
and denote by
\zeta

the corresponding affine arc element. Consider a calibrated lightcone basis
\mathfrak{C}
and the associated null normal
vector field
{\rm R}
(cf. (
4.5
)).
Let
h
be the affine curvature of
\Gamma
. Let
{\rm T}:\Lambda\times S^{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{2,3}
be the lift of
f_{\Gamma}

defined by
{\rm T}(\tau,\theta)=\cos(\theta){\rm R}|_{\tau}+\frac{1}{2}\sin(\theta)\Gamma^{\prime}|_{\tau}
.
Then

d{\rm T}=\Big(-\sin(\theta){\rm R}+\frac{1}{2}\cos(\theta)\Gamma^{\prime}\Big)d\theta+\Big(\cos(\theta){\rm R}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\sin(\theta)\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\Big)\zeta.

Differentiating (
4.6
),
taking into account that
\Gamma^{\prime\prime\prime}=h\Gamma^{\prime}
,
yields
\langle{\rm R},{\rm R}^{\prime}\rangle=\langle{\rm R}^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\rangle=0
, and

\langle{\rm R}^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime}\rangle=-1
,
from which one computes

{\langle{d{\mathrm{T}}},{d{\mathrm{T}}}\rangle=-\zeta\left({d}\theta-(\cos\theta)^{2}\langle{\rm R}^{\prime},{\rm R}^{\prime}\rangle\zeta\right).}

This implies that the tautological map
f_{\Gamma}
is a timelike immersion.

Let
{F}^{\pm}_{0}:{\mathbb{T}}_{\Gamma}^{\pm}\to\mathrm{O}^{\uparrow}_{+}(2,3)
be the lift of
f^{\pm}_{\Gamma}
defined by

(4.11)

{F}^{\pm}_{0}=\frac{\pm 1}{\cos\theta}{\rm T}=\pm\Big({\rm R}+\frac{1}{2}\tan(\theta)\Gamma^{\prime}\Big).

Denote by
(\partial_{1},\partial_{2})
the trivialization of
{T}({\mathbb{T}}_{\Gamma}^{\pm})
dual to
the coframe
(\zeta,{du})
, where
u=\tan\theta
.
Consider the maps
{F}^{\pm}_{1},\dots,{F}^{\pm}_{4}:\mathbb{T}^{\pm}_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{R}^{2,3}
, defined by

(4.12)

\begin{cases}{F}^{\pm}_{1}=-\partial_{1}{F}^{\pm}_{0}-(\langle\partial_{1}{F}^{\pm}_{0},\partial_{1}{F}^{\pm}_{0}\rangle+1)\partial_{2}{F}^{\pm}_{0},\\
{F}^{\pm}_{2}=-\partial_{1}{F}^{\pm}_{0}-(\langle\partial_{1}{F}^{\pm}_{0},\partial_{1}{F}^{\pm}_{0}\rangle-1)\partial_{2}{F}^{\pm}_{0},\\
{F}^{\pm}_{3}=\Gamma\circ\pi_{{\mathbb{T}}},\\
{F}^{\pm}_{4}=\mp\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\circ\pi_{{\mathbb{T}}}.\end{cases}

Then,
{F}_{\pm}=({F}^{\pm}_{0},\dots,{F}^{\pm}_{4})
is an adapted frame field along
f^{\pm}_{\Gamma}
.
The 1-form

\phi_{\pm}={F}_{\pm}^{-1}{d}{F}_{\pm}
is as in
(
3.9
), with
\phi^{2}_{1}=0
,
r=0
,
\alpha=-h\zeta
, and

(4.13)

\begin{cases}\phi^{1}_{0}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(du+(\frac{\mu^{2}+(xx^{\prime}-yy^{\prime})^{2}}{\upsilon^{4}}+1)\zeta\right),\\
\phi^{2}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\left(du+(\frac{\mu^{2}+(xx^{\prime}-yy^{\prime})^{2}}{\upsilon^{4}}-1)\zeta\right),\end{cases}

where
\gamma=(x,y)
is the affine reduction of
\Gamma
with respect to
\mathfrak{C}
.
This proves that
f^{\pm}_{\Gamma}

are exceptional quasi-umbilical immersions with helicity
\varepsilon=\pm 1
, conformal Gauss maps
\Gamma\circ\pi_{{\Lambda}}
,
and dual maps
[-\varepsilon\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\circ\pi_{{\mathbb{T}}}]
. By continuity,
\Gamma\circ\pi_{{\Lambda}}
is the conformal Gauss
map of
f_{\Gamma}
.
From the identity
(d\mathcal{N}_{f}\wedge{\rm T})|_{(\tau,\theta)}=\cos(\theta)({\rm R}\wedge\Gamma^{\prime})|_{\tau}\zeta
, it follows
that
(\tau,\theta)
is an umbilical point if and only if
(\tau,\theta)\in{\rm C}_{+}\cup{\rm C}_{-}
.

Conversely, let
f:X\to\mathcal{E}^{1,2}
be a regular, quasi-umbilical immersion, with directrix curve
\Gamma:\Lambda\to{{S}}^{2,2}
,
and let
{\mathcal{F}}_{*}(f)
be the bundle of adapted frames along
f
. Let
{\mathfrak{N}}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2,3})
denote the
Grassmannian of positive null planes of
\mathbb{R}^{2,3}
.
The map

{\mathcal{F}}_{*}(f)\ni(\tau,F)\longmapsto[{F}_{4}\wedge({F}_{1}-{F}_{2})]\in{\mathfrak{N}}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2,3})

is constant on the fibers of
{\mathcal{F}}_{*}(f)
. So, it descends to a smooth map
{\mathbb{V}}_{f}:X\to{\mathfrak{N}}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2,3})
.
Let
F:U\subset X\to\mathrm{O}^{\uparrow}_{+}(2,3)
be a local section of
{\mathcal{F}}_{*}(f)
.
The 1-form
\phi=F^{-1}dF
is as in (
3.9
), with
r=0
and
\alpha=-q\zeta
, for some smooth function
q
.
Then
{d}({F}_{4}\wedge({F}_{1}-{F}_{2}))=-3\phi^{2}_{1}({F}_{4}\wedge({F}_{1}-{F}_{2}))
.
This implies that
[{F}_{4}\wedge({F}_{1}-{F}_{2})]
is constant.
By construction,
{\mathbb{V}}_{f}|_{U}=[{F}_{4}\wedge({F}_{1}-{F}_{2})]
.
This proves that
{\mathbb{V}}_{f}
is a constant positive null plane.
Next, we choose a positive area element
{dA}\in\Lambda^{2}({\mathbb{V}}_{f})

the unique global cross section

of
{\mathcal{F}}_{*}(f)

Then
{F}_{4}\wedge({F}_{1}-{F}_{2})
is constant. Consequently,
\phi^{2}_{1}
vanishes identically.
This implies that
\phi^{1}_{0}
and
\phi^{2}_{0}
are closed. Let
\tilde{\zeta}
be a nowhere zero 1-form on
\Lambda
.
Since
{F}_{3}=\Gamma\circ\pi_{\Lambda}
, then
\phi^{1}_{0}+\phi^{2}_{0}=\rho\pi_{\Lambda}^{*}(\tilde{\zeta})
, where

is a nowhere vanishing smooth function. On the other hand, the 1-form
\phi^{1}_{0}+\phi^{2}_{0}
is closed. This implies that

{d}\rho\wedge\pi_{\Lambda}^{*}(\tilde{\zeta})=0
. Then,
\rho
is constant on the fibers of
\pi_{\Lambda}
.
Therefore, there exists a nowhere zero smooth function
\varrho:\Lambda\to\mathbb{R}
, such that

\rho=\varrho\circ\pi_{\Lambda}
. Put
\zeta=\varrho\tilde{\zeta}
, then
\pi_{\Lambda}^{*}(\zeta)=\phi^{1}_{0}+\phi^{2}_{0}
.
Since
\phi^{2}_{1}=0
, the 1-form
\alpha
is closed. Then,
{dq}\wedge\zeta=0
. This in turn implies that

q=h\circ\pi_{\Lambda}
,
where
h:\Lambda\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function. Differentiating
{F}_{3}=\Gamma\circ\pi_{\Lambda}
yields

(4.14)

{F}_{1}-{F}_{2}=\Gamma^{\prime}\circ\pi_{\Lambda},\quad F_{4}=-\varepsilon\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\circ\pi_{\Lambda},\quad{d}({F}_{3}\wedge({F}_{1}-{F}_{2})\wedge{F}_{4})=0.

This shows that
\Gamma
is a biisotropic curve, such that
{\mathbb{V}}_{\Gamma}={\mathbb{V}}_{f}
.
In addition,
\zeta
is the affine line element of
\Gamma
relative to the area element
{dA}
. Next, we choose
a lightcone basis
\mathfrak{C}
calibrated to
\Gamma

and
\zeta
and consider the associated isotropic normal vector field
{\rm R}
. By construction,
{\rm R}
satisfies
the identities (
4.6
).
We write
{\rm R}\circ\pi_{\Lambda}=\sum_{j=0}^{4}r_{j}{F}_{j}
, where
r_{0},\dots,r_{4}
are smooth functions.
From (
4.14
) and (

) it follows that
r_{0}=\varepsilon
,
r_{1}+r_{2}=r_{3}=r_{4}=0
.
Then
{F}_{0}=\varepsilon{\rm R}\circ\pi_{\Lambda}-r_{1}\Gamma^{\prime}\circ\pi_{\Lambda}
.
This implies that
f({x})=[{F}_{0}({x})]\in f_{\Gamma}^{\pm}({\mathbb{T}}^{\pm}_{\Gamma})
,
for every

.

4.3.3.
The normal tube of a parabola

Consider the “biisotropic parabola”

(4.17)

\Gamma:\mathbb{R}\ni s\mapsto s({E}_{1}-{E}_{2})+{E}_{3}+\frac{s^{2}}{2}{E}_{4}\in{{S}}^{2,2}.

The map

{\mathrm{R}}(s)=-{E}_{0}+\frac{s(2+s^{2})^{2}}{8(1+s^{2})}{E}_{1}+\frac{s}{8}(4-\frac{s^{4}}{1+s^{2}}){E}_{2}+\frac{s^{2}}{2}{E}_{3}-\frac{s^{4}}{8(1+s^{2})}{E}_{4}

is an isotropic normal vector field along
\Gamma
and the timelike tautological immersion of the normal tube can be written as

f:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\ni(s,\theta)\mapsto[\cos(\theta)\rm{R}(s)+{\frac{1}{2}}\sin(\theta)\Gamma^{\prime}(s)]\in\mathcal{E}^{1,2}.

The images of the umbilical curves
f({\rm C}_{+})
and
f({\rm C}_{-})
are two adjacent disjoint open arcs of
{\mathcal{C}}
. Their common
boundary consits of the two points
[\pm{E}_{4}]
. The parabolic components
f^{\pm}_{\Gamma}({\mathbb{T}}^{\pm})
are
contained in the AdS chambers
{\mathcal{A}}^{\pm}_{{E}_{3}}
and the dual maps are constants. Also in this case
the central tori pass through the null geodesic
{\mathcal{C}}
(see Figure
7
).

4.3.4.
Homogeneous quasi-umbilical immersions

Let
f:X\to\mathcal{E}^{1,2}
be a timelike immersed surface. A
conformal symmetry
of
f
is a pair
(\Phi,{B})
consisting
of a diffeomorphism
\Phi:X\to X
and an element
{B}\in\mathrm{O}^{\uparrow}_{+}(2,3)
, such that
B\cdot(f\circ\Phi)=f
.
The totality of conformal symmetries of
f
is a group
G_{f}
, the group of conformal symmetries of
f
, with
multiplication given by
(\Phi,{B})\star(\Phi^{\prime},{B}^{\prime})=(\Phi^{\prime}\circ\Phi,{B}\,{B}^{\prime})
.
The group
G_{f}
acts on
X

on the left by
(\Phi,{B}){x}=\Phi({x})
, for all
(\Phi,{B})\in G_{f}
and for all
x\in X
.
If this action is transitive, the immersion is said to be
homogeneous
.
For instance, the timelike totally umbilical tori of
\mathcal{E}^{1,2}
are homogeneous and have a six-dimensional group of symmetries.
Other examples are the parabolic components of the tubes of a biisotropic conic of
{{S}}^{2,2}
. In these cases,
the symmetry group is isomorphic to a 2-dimensional abelian subgroup of
\mathrm{O}^{\uparrow}_{+}(2,3)
.
In
[
47
]
, The showed that a generic quasi-umbilical immersion cannot be homogeneous. It is not difficult to show that
an exceptional quasi-umbilical immersion is homogeneous if and only if it is
\mathrm{O}^{\uparrow}_{+}(2,3)
-equivalent to a parabolic component of the
normal tube of a biisotropic conic
.

5.
Quasi-umbilical immersions: the general case

5.1.
Generic null curves in the neutral space form
{{S}}^{2,2}

Definition 5.1
.

An immersed null curve
\Gamma:\Lambda\to{{S}}^{2,2}
is said to be
generic
if
\langle\Gamma^{\prime\prime},\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\rangle

is either strictly positive or strictly negative.
A
line element
is a nowhere zero 1-form
\zeta\in\Omega^{1}(\Lambda)
, such that

\langle\Gamma^{\prime\prime},\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\rangle=-2\eta,\quad\eta={-}\sgn\langle\Gamma^{\prime\prime},\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\rangle=\pm 1.

The line element is unique up to the sign.

5.1.1.
The left and right normal tubes

Let
\zeta
be a line element.
Differentiating the identities
\langle\Gamma,\Gamma\rangle=1
,
\langle\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime}\rangle=0
and

\langle\Gamma^{\prime\prime},\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\rangle=-2\eta
, it follows that
{\mathcal{R}}_{1}|_{\tau}:=\mathrm{span\,}\{\Gamma^{\prime}|_{\tau},\Gamma^{\prime\prime\prime}|_{\tau}\}

is a plane of type
(1,1)
and
{\mathcal{R}}_{2}|_{\tau}:=\mathrm{span\,}\{\Gamma|_{\tau},\Gamma^{\prime}|_{\tau},\Gamma^{\prime\prime\prime}|_{\tau}\}
is a subspace
of type
(1,2)
, for every
\tau\in\Lambda
.
The polar space
{\mathcal{R}}^{\perp}_{2}|_{\tau}
of
{\mathcal{R}}_{2}|_{\tau}
is a plane of type
(1,1)

such that
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}|_{\tau}\in{\mathcal{R}}^{\perp}_{2}|_{\tau}
, for every
\tau
.
The polar space
{\mathcal{R}}^{\perp}_{2}|_{\tau}
has a natural orientation:
a basis
({V},{W})
of
{\mathcal{R}}^{\perp}_{2}|_{\tau}
is positive if and only if
-\eta{V}\wedge\Gamma|_{\tau}\wedge\Gamma^{\prime}|_{\tau}\wedge\Gamma^{\prime\prime\prime}|_{\tau}\wedge{W}>0
.
Let
{\mathcal{R}}_{1}
,
{\mathcal{R}}_{2}
, and
{\mathcal{R}}^{\perp}_{2}
be the vector bundles

(5.1)

\begin{cases}\pi_{1}:{\mathcal{R}}_{1}=\{(\tau,{V})\in\Lambda\times\mathbb{R}^{2,3}\mid{V}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{1}|_{\tau}\}\to\Lambda,\\
\pi_{2}:{\mathcal{R}}_{2}=\{(\tau,{V})\in\Lambda\times\mathbb{R}^{2,3}\mid{V}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{2}|_{\tau}\}\to\Lambda,\\
\pi^{\perp}_{2}:{\mathcal{R}}^{\perp}_{2}=\{(\tau,{V})\in\Lambda\times\mathbb{R}^{2,3}\mid{V}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{2}^{\perp}|_{\tau}\}\to\Lambda\end{cases}

The
dual
of a null vector
{V}\in{\mathcal{R}}^{\perp}_{2}|_{\tau}
is a null vector
{V}_{*}\in{\mathcal{R}}^{\perp}_{2}|_{\tau}
,
such that
\langle{V},{V}_{*}\rangle=-1
. We say that a null vector
{V}\in{\mathcal{R}}^{\perp}_{2}|_{\tau}
is
right-handed

if
({V},{V}_{*})
is a positive basis of
{\mathcal{R}}^{\perp}_{2}|_{\tau}
.
Right-handed null vectors generate a null line subbundle

{\mathcal{P}}
of
{\mathcal{R}}^{\perp}_{2}
. The function
{\mathcal{P}}\setminus\{0\}\ni{V}\mapsto\langle{V},\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\rangle

is nowhere vanishing. Then there exists a unique trivialization
{\rm C}_{\lambda}
of
{\mathcal{P}}
, such that
\langle{\rm C}_{\lambda},\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\rangle=1
. We denote by
{\rm C}_{\varrho}
the unique dual of
{\rm C}_{\lambda}
, such that
\langle{\rm C}_{\varrho},\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\rangle=\eta
. By construction,
({\rm C}_{\lambda},{\rm C}_{\varrho})
is a positive null trivialization of

{\mathcal{R}}^{\perp}_{2}
, such that

(5.2)

-\eta{\rm C}_{\lambda}\wedge\Gamma\wedge\Gamma^{\prime}\wedge\Gamma^{\prime\prime\prime}\wedge{\rm C}_{\varrho}>0,\quad\Gamma^{\prime\prime}=-(\eta{\rm C}_{\lambda}+{\rm C}_{\varrho}).

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\zeta
be the unique line element, such that the null bivectors
{\rm C}_{\varrho}|_{\tau}\wedge\Gamma^{\prime}|_{\tau}
are positive,
for every
\tau\in\Lambda
.
We call
\zeta
the
canonical line element
of
\Gamma
. The
left and right isotropic normal tubes
along
\Gamma

are the trivial circle bundles

\begin{cases}\pi_{\lambda}:{\mathbb{T}}_{\lambda}=\{(\tau,[{V}])\in\Lambda\times\mathcal{E}^{1,2}\mid{V}\in[({\rm C}_{\lambda}\wedge\Gamma^{\prime})|_{\tau}]\}\to\Lambda,\\
\pi_{\varrho}:{\mathbb{T}}_{\varrho}=\{(\tau,[{V}])\in\Lambda\times\mathcal{E}^{1,2}\mid{V}\in[({\rm C}_{\varrho}\wedge\Gamma^{\prime})|_{\tau}]\}\to\Lambda.\end{cases}

The elements of

{\mathbb{T}}_{\lambda}
can be written as
(\tau,[{V}_{\lambda}(\tau,\theta)])
, where

(5.3)

{V}_{\lambda}(\tau,\theta)=\cos(\theta){\rm C}_{\lambda}|_{\tau}+\sin(\theta)\Gamma^{\prime}|_{\tau},

while the elements of
{\mathbb{T}}_{\varrho}
are of the form
(\tau,[{V}_{\varrho}(\tau,\theta)])
, where

(5.4)

{V}_{\varrho}(\tau,\theta)=\cos(\theta){\rm C}_{\varrho}|_{\tau}+\sin(\theta)\Gamma^{\prime}|_{\tau}.

The normal tubes do intersect along the curves

{\rm C}_{\pm}=\{(\tau,[\pm\Gamma^{\prime}|_{\tau}]),\tau\in\Lambda\},

called
umbilical lines
.
The complementary set of
{\rm C}_{+}\cup{\rm C}_{-}
has two connected components,

{\mathbb{T}}_{\lambda}^{\pm}=\{(\tau,[{V}_{\lambda}(\tau,\theta)])\mid{\sgn}(\cos\theta)=\pm 1\}

and

{\mathbb{T}}_{\varrho}^{\pm}=\{(\tau,[{V}_{\varrho}(\tau,\theta)])\mid{\sgn}(\cos\theta)=\pm 1\}.

Definition 5.3
.

We call
{\mathbb{T}}_{\lambda}^{\pm}
and
{\mathbb{T}}_{\varrho}^{\pm}
the
parabolic components

of the normal tubes. The map

J_{\lambda}^{\varrho}:{\mathbb{T}}_{\lambda}\ni(\tau,[{V}_{\lambda}(\tau,\theta)])\mapsto(\tau,[{V}_{\varrho}(\tau,\theta)])\in{\mathbb{T}}_{\varrho},

is the
intertwining diffeomorphism
between the left and right normal tubes. The inverse
diffeomorphism
(J_{\lambda}^{\varrho})^{-1}

will be denoted by

J_{\varrho}^{\lambda}
.

Definition 5.4
.

The left and right normal tubes can be mapped into the Einstein universe by means of the
left and right tautological maps

f_{\lambda}:{\mathbb{T}}_{\lambda}\ni(\tau,[{V}])\mapsto[{V}]\in\mathcal{E}^{1,2},\quad f_{\varrho}:{\mathbb{T}}_{\varrho}\ni(\tau,[{V}])\mapsto[{V}]\in\mathcal{E}^{1,2}.

The restrictions of the tautological maps to the parabolic components are denoted by
f^{\pm}_{\lambda}
and by

f^{\pm}_{\varrho}
, respectively.

m=3

and

). On the left, the positive parabolic component; on the right, the negative parabolic component.
The yellow line is the umbilical curve of the surface. The umbilical points are double points. The parabolic

5.2.
Structure of quasi-umbilical immersions of general type

With reference to the constructions on generic null curves in
{{S}}^{2,2}
developed in Section
5.1
, we
can now describe the geometric structure of general quasi-umbilical immersions.
The following is the main result of this section.

Theorem C
.

Let
\Gamma:\Lambda\to{{S}}^{2,2}
be a generic null immersion.

(1)
The tautological map
f_{\lambda}
is a timelike immersion with
umbilic locus
{\rm C}_{+}\cup{\rm C}_{-}
and conformal Gauss map
{\mathcal{N}}_{f_{\lambda}}=\Gamma\circ\pi_{\lambda}
.
The restrictions
f^{\pm}_{\lambda}
of
f_{\lambda}
to the parabolic components
{\mathbb{T}}_{\lambda}^{\pm}

of the left normal tube
{\mathbb{T}}_{\lambda}

are quasi-umbilical immersions
of general type with helicity
\varepsilon=\pm 1
and
dual maps
(f^{\pm}_{\lambda})^{\sharp}=f^{\pm}_{\varrho}\circ J^{\varrho}_{\lambda}
.

(2)
The tautological map
f_{\varrho}
is a timelike immersion with
umbilic locus
{\rm C}_{+}\cup{\rm C}_{-}
and conformal Gauss map
{\mathcal{N}}_{f_{\varrho}}=\Gamma\circ\pi_{\varrho}
.
The restrictions
f^{\pm}_{\varrho}
of
f_{\varrho}
to the parabolic components
{\mathbb{T}}_{\varrho}^{\pm}

of the right normal tube
{\mathbb{T}}_{\varrho}
are quasi-umbilical immersions of general
type with helicity
\varepsilon=\pm 1
and
dual maps
(f^{\pm}_{\varrho})^{\sharp}=f^{\pm}_{\lambda}\circ J_{\varrho}^{\lambda}
.

Conversely, let
f:X\to\mathcal{E}^{1,2}
be a regular quasi-umbilical
immersion of general type. Then
f(X)
is contained in one of the two parabolic components

f^{\pm}_{\lambda}({\mathbb{T}}_{\lambda}^{\pm})
of the tautological immersion originated by the directrix curve of
f
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma:\Lambda\to{{S}}^{2,2}
be a generic null immersion,
\zeta
be its canonical line element, and let
{B}:\Lambda\to\mathrm{O}^{\uparrow}_{+}(2,3)

be the map defined by

{B}_{0}={\rm C}_{\lambda},\quad{B}_{1}-{B}_{2}=\Gamma^{\prime},\quad 2{B}_{1}\wedge{B}_{2}=-\eta\Gamma^{\prime}\wedge\Gamma^{\prime\prime\prime},\quad{B_{3}=\Gamma},\quad{B}_{4}={\rm C}_{\varrho}.

First, we claim that
{B}
is a solution of the linear system

(5.5)

{B}^{-1}{B}^{\prime}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&-\frac{\eta}{2}+\kappa_{\varrho}&\frac{\eta}{2}+\kappa_{\varrho}&0&0\\
\frac{1}{2}-\kappa_{\lambda}&0&0&1&\frac{\eta}{2}-\kappa_{\varrho}\\
\frac{1}{2}+\kappa_{\lambda}&0&0&-1&\frac{\eta}{2}+\kappa_{\varrho}\\
0&1&1&0&0\\
0&-\frac{1}{2}+\kappa_{\lambda}&\frac{1}{2}+\kappa_{\lambda}&0&0\\
\end{array}\right),

where
\kappa_{\lambda}
and
\kappa_{\varrho}
are smooth functions, the
left and right curvatures

of
\Gamma
.
Denote by
b^{i}_{j}
,
0\leq i,j\leq 4
, the components of the
\mathfrak{o}(2,3)
-valued map
{B}^{-1}{B}^{\prime}
.
Differentiating
{B}_{3}=\Gamma
, recalling that
\Gamma^{\prime}={B}_{1}-{B}_{2}
, we have

(5.6)

b^{1}_{3}=-b^{2}_{3}=1,\quad b^{0}_{3}=b^{4}_{3}=0,

and hence
b^{3}_{1}=b^{3}_{2}=1
,
{b^{3}_{4}}=b^{3}_{0}=0
,
by the symmetry relations between the
b^{i}_{j}
.
Observe that, since
{B}_{0}={\rm C}_{\lambda}
and
{B}_{4}={\rm C}_{\varrho}

are orthogonal to
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
, differentiating
\langle{B}_{0},\Gamma\rangle=\langle{B}_{4},\Gamma\rangle=0
,
we obtain
\langle{B}^{\prime}_{0},\Gamma\rangle=\langle{B}^{\prime}_{4},\Gamma\rangle=0
, i.e.,
b^{3}_{0}=b^{3}_{4}=0
.
Differentiating
{B}_{1}-{B}_{2}=\Gamma^{\prime}
, taking into account that

\Gamma^{\prime\prime}=-(\eta{\rm C}_{\lambda}+{\rm C}_{\varrho})=-(\eta{B}_{0}+{B}_{4}),

yields

(5.7)

b^{2}_{1}=0,\quad b^{1}_{4}+b_{4}^{2}=\eta,\quad b^{1}_{0}+b^{2}_{0}=1.

This implies the existence of two smooth functions
\kappa_{\lambda}
and
\kappa_{\varrho}
, such that

(5.8)

b^{1}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}-\kappa_{\lambda},\quad b^{2}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}+\kappa_{\lambda},\quad b^{1}_{4}=\frac{\eta}{2}-\kappa_{\varrho},\quad b^{2}_{4}=\frac{\eta}{2}+\kappa_{\varrho}.

The third derivative of
\Gamma
is a cross section of the bundle spanned by
{B}_{1}
and
{B}_{2}
. Then,
differentiating
\langle\Gamma^{\prime\prime},{\rm C}_{\lambda}\rangle=1
, we find
\langle\Gamma^{\prime\prime},{B}^{\prime}_{0}\rangle=0
,
and hence,
b^{0}_{0}=0
. This together with
(
5.6
),
(
5.7
) and (
5.8
) proves the claim.

Next, we identify the normal tubes with
\Lambda\times\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
as in
(
5.3
) and (
5.4
). Given a map
\Psi:\Lambda\times\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
, we set

d\Psi=\partial_{\zeta}\Psi\zeta+\partial_{\theta}\Psi d\theta
. Let us consider the lift of
f_{\lambda}
defined by

{\rm T}_{\lambda}=(\cos\theta){B}_{0}+(\sin\theta)({B}_{1}-{B}_{2})
. From (
5.5
), we compute

\langle d{\mathrm{T}}_{\lambda},d{\mathrm{T}}_{\lambda}\rangle=-2\zeta\big(d\theta+(\eta\sin^{2}\theta-\kappa_{\lambda}\cos^{2}\theta)\zeta\big).

This shows that
f_{\lambda}
is a timelike immersion. Let
{F}^{\pm}:{\mathbb{T}}_{\lambda}^{\pm}\to\mathrm{O}^{\uparrow}_{+}(2,3)

be the frame fields along
f_{\lambda}
defined by

\begin{cases}{F}^{\pm}_{0}=\pm\big({B}_{0}+(\tan\theta)({B}_{1}-{B}_{2})\big),\\
{F}^{\pm}_{1}=\pm\big((\tan\theta)(\frac{\eta}{3}{B}_{0}-{B}_{4})+{B}_{1}+\frac{\eta}{3}(\tan\theta)^{2}({B}_{1}-{B}_{2})\big),\\
{F}^{\pm}_{2}=\pm\big((\tan\theta)(\frac{\eta}{3}{B}_{0}-{B}_{4})+{B}_{2}+\frac{\eta}{3}(\tan\theta)^{2}({B}_{1}-{B}_{2})\big),\\
{F}^{\pm}_{3}=B_{3}=\Gamma,\\
{F}^{\pm}_{4}={\pm\big(B_{4}-\frac{\eta}{3}(\tan\theta)({B}_{1}-{B}_{2})\big).}\end{cases}

Using (
5.5
) one computes the
\mathfrak{o}(2,3)
-valued 1-forms
({F}^{\pm})^{-1}{dF}^{\pm}
, which yield

(5.9)

\begin{cases}\phi_{0}^{3}=\phi^{0}_{3}=\phi^{4}_{3}=\phi^{3}_{4}=0,\\
\phi^{0}_{0}=2\phi^{2}_{1}=-\frac{4\eta}{3}(\tan\theta)\zeta,\quad\phi_{1}^{3}=\phi_{2}^{3}=\pm\zeta\\
\phi^{1}_{0}=(\frac{1}{2}-\kappa_{\lambda}+\eta(\tan\theta)^{2})\zeta+(\sec\theta)^{2}d\theta,\\
\phi^{2}_{0}=(\frac{1}{2}+\kappa_{\lambda}-\eta(\tan\theta)^{2})\zeta-(\sec\theta)^{2}d\theta,\\
\phi^{1}_{4}=\frac{\eta}{18}(9-18\eta\kappa_{\varrho}+2{\eta}(\tan\theta)^{2})\zeta-\frac{\eta}{3}(\sec\theta)^{2}d\theta,\\
\phi^{2}_{4}=\frac{\eta}{18}(9+18\eta\kappa_{\varrho}-2{\eta}(\tan\theta)^{2})\zeta+\frac{\eta}{3}(\sec\theta)^{2}d\theta.\end{cases}

Then,
{F}^{\pm}
are adapted frame fields along
f^{\pm}_{\lambda}
. This implies

{\mathcal{N}}_{f^{\pm}_{\lambda}}(\tau,\theta)=\Gamma(\theta),\quad(f^{\pm}_{\lambda})^{\sharp}(\tau,\theta)=[{F}^{\pm}_{4}(\tau,\theta)]=f^{\pm}_{\varrho}\circ J^{\varrho}_{\lambda}(\tau,\theta).

Since
\langle d{\mathcal{N}}_{f^{\pm}_{\lambda}},d{F}^{\pm}_{4}\rangle={\mp\eta\zeta^{2}}
, we infer that
f^{\pm}_{\lambda}

are of general type, with conformal Gauss map
\Gamma\circ\pi_{\Lambda}
, helicity
\varepsilon=\pm 1
and dual map
(f^{\pm}_{\lambda})^{\sharp}=f^{\pm}_{\varrho}\circ J_{\varrho}^{\lambda}
.
By continuity,
{\mathcal{N}}_{f_{\lambda}}=\Gamma\circ\pi_{\Lambda}
.
From (
5.5
), we have

{d}{\mathcal{N}}_{f_{\lambda}}=\zeta({B}_{1}-{B}_{2})

and

\begin{split}d{\rm T}_{\lambda}&=\Big(\cos(\theta)\big(\frac{1}{2}({B}_{1}+{B}_{2})-\kappa_{\lambda}({B}_{1}-{B}_{2})-\sin(\theta)(\eta{B}_{0}+{B}_{4})\big)\Big)\zeta\\
&\qquad+\big(-\sin(\theta){B}_{0}+\cos(\theta)({B}_{1}-{B}_{2})\big)d\theta.\end{split}

Then,

\langle{d}{\mathcal{N}}_{f_{\lambda}},d{\rm T}_{\lambda}\rangle=-\cos(\theta)\zeta^{2}.

This proves that
{\rm C}_{+}\cup{\rm C}_{-}
is the umbilic locus of
f_{\lambda}
.
This concludes the proof of assertion (1). Similarly, one can prove assertion (2).

Conversely, let
f:X\to\mathcal{E}^{1,2}
be a regular quasi-umbilical immersion of general type and let
\Gamma:\Lambda\to{{S}}^{2,2}

be its directrix curve. Since
f
is of general type, there exists a unique adapted frame field
{F}:X\to\mathrm{O}^{\uparrow}_{+}(2,3)
along
f
,
such that
\phi^{1}_{4}+\phi^{2}_{4}=\frac{\eta}{2}(\phi^{1}_{0}+\phi^{2}_{0})
,
\eta=\pm 1
.
Then, the 1-form
\phi={F}^{-1}{dF}
is as in (
3.9
)
with
r=\eta
and
\tilde{\zeta}=\phi^{1}_{0}-\phi^{2}_{0}
. Let
\zeta
be a nowhere zero 1-form on
\Lambda
.
Then
\phi^{1}_{0}+\phi^{2}_{0}=\pm e^{u}\pi_{\Lambda}^{*}(\zeta)
, where
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function.
From

(5.10)

{F}_{3}=\Gamma\circ\pi_{\Lambda}\quad d{F}_{3}=\varepsilon(\phi^{1}_{0}+\phi^{2}_{0})({F}_{1}-{F}_{2})

we see that
\Gamma
is a null curve. Differentiating (
5.10
), using (
3.9
), we obtain

(5.11)

\pi_{\Lambda}^{*}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\zeta)=\pm\varepsilon\Big(e^{u}(du-\phi^{2}_{1})({F}_{1}-{F}_{2})-e^{2u}(\eta{F}_{0}+{F}_{4})\zeta\Big).

Then,
\langle\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\circ\pi_{\Lambda},\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\circ\pi_{\Lambda}\rangle={-2\eta e^{4u}}
.
This implies that
\Gamma
is a generic null curve. So, we may assume that
\zeta
is the canonical line element of
\Gamma
.
Then,
\langle\Gamma^{\prime\prime},\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\rangle=-2\eta
. Hence,
u=0
and, using (
5.11
),
we infer that
\phi^{2}_{1}={\rm p}\pi_{\Lambda}^{*}(\zeta)
, where
{\rm p}:X\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function.
Hence the equation (
5.11
) can be written as

(5.12)

\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\circ\pi_{\Lambda}=\mp\varepsilon\left(\mathrm{p}\,\Gamma^{\prime}\circ\pi_{\Lambda}+(\eta{F}_{0}+{F}_{4})\right).

Differentiating (
5.12
), we obtain

(5.13)

\Gamma^{\prime\prime\prime}\circ\pi_{\Lambda}=\mp\varepsilon\big(\mathrm{p}(\eta{F}_{0}\mp 3{F}_{4})+\frac{\eta}{2}(3{F}_{1}+{F}_{2})\big).

Then, retaining the notation of (
5.1
) and using (
5.13
), we deduce that the vector bundle

\pi_{\Lambda}^{*}({\mathcal{R}}_{2}^{\perp})
is spanned by the null vector fields

(5.14)

{A}={F}_{0}\pm\frac{3}{2}\eta\mathrm{p}({F}_{1}-{F}_{2}),\quad{B}={F}_{4}-\frac{1}{2}\eta{\rm p}({F}_{1}-{F}_{2}).

From (

) it follows that

(5.15)

-\eta{A}\wedge(\Gamma\wedge\Gamma^{\prime}\wedge\Gamma^{\prime\prime\prime})\circ\pi_{\Lambda}\wedge{B}=2{F}_{0}\wedge{F}_{1}\wedge{F}_{2}\wedge{F}_{3}\wedge{F}_{4}>0.

By (
5.14
), we have
\langle{A},{B}\rangle=-1
and

\langle{A},{A}\rangle=\langle{B},{B}\rangle=0
.
Then, (
5.15
) implies that
{A}
is a global trivialization of

,
where
{\mathcal{P}}
is the line bundle generated by the right-handed null vectors of
{\mathcal{R}}_{2}^{\perp}
.
From (
5.12
) and (

), we obtain
\langle{A},\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\circ\pi_{\Lambda}\rangle=\pm\varepsilon
.
This implies

. By (

), we have

{F}_{0}=\pm\varepsilon\rm{C}_{\lambda}\circ\pi_{\Lambda}\mp\frac{3}{2}\eta{\rm p}\Gamma^{\prime}\circ\pi_{\Lambda}.

Then,
f(X)\subseteq f^{+}_{\lambda}({\mathbb{T}}^{+})
if
\pm\varepsilon=1
and
f(X)\subseteq f^{-}_{\lambda}({\mathbb{T}}^{-})

if
\pm\varepsilon=-1
. This concludes the proof. ∎

5.3.
Examples

We illustrate our construction on a class of simple examples. The computations are carried out in the coordinates
of
\mathbb{R}^{2,3}
relative to the standard pseudo-orthogonal basis
\mathfrak{P}=(P_{0},\dots,P_{4})
of
\mathbb{R}^{2,3}
.
The details and the intermediate steps will be omitted either
because elementary or because they can be
carried out with the help of symbolic computational systems such as
Mathematica, Maple or Matlab.
Let
r\in(0,1)
and let
m,n
be two relatively prime integers such that
n>m>0
.
Consider

{W}(s)=\left(m\cos(ns),m\sin(ns),n\cos(ms),n\sin(ms),\frac{nr}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\right).

Then

{\Gamma(s):=\frac{W(s)}{\sqrt{\langle{W}(s),{W}(s)\rangle}}}\in{{S}}^{2,2}

defines a generic null curve of negative type (i.e.
\langle\Gamma^{\prime\prime},\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\rangle<0
) with constant left and right curvatures.
Thus
\Gamma
is a homogeneous null curve of
{{S}}^{2,2}
.
The isotropic normal vector fields along
\Gamma
take the form

\displaystyle{\rm C}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\upsilon_{\lambda}\left(c_{\lambda}^{0}\cos(ns),c_{\lambda}^{1}\sin(ns),c_{\lambda}^{2}\cos(ms),c_{\lambda}^{3}\sin(ms),c_{\lambda}^{4}\right),

\displaystyle{\rm C}_{\varrho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\upsilon_{\varrho}\left(c_{\varrho}^{0}\cos(ns),c_{\varrho}^{1}\sin(ns),c_{\varrho}^{2}\cos(ms),c_{\varrho}^{3}\sin(ms),c_{\varrho}^{4}\right),

where
\upsilon_{\lambda}
,
\upsilon_{\varrho}
,
c_{\lambda}^{j}
, and
c_{\varrho}^{j}
,
j=0,\dots,4
, are constants which
depend on the parameters
m
,
n
, and
r
. The tautological maps
f_{\lambda}
and
f_{\varrho}
arising from
\Gamma
are
the timelike immersions of the torus
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}^{2}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{2}

given by

\begin{cases}f_{\lambda}:\mathbb{T}\ni(s,\theta)\mapsto[\cos(\theta){\rm C}_{\lambda}(s)+\sin(\theta)\Gamma^{\prime}(s)]\in\mathcal{E}^{1,2},\\
f_{\varrho}:\mathbb{T}\ni(s,\theta)\mapsto[\cos(\theta){\rm C}_{\varrho}(s)+\sin(\theta)\Gamma^{\prime}(s)]\in\mathcal{E}^{1,2}.\end{cases}

Since
f_{\lambda}(s,\theta)=-f_{\lambda}(s,\theta+\pi)
and
f_{\varrho}(s,\theta)=-f_{\varrho}(s,\theta+\pi)
,
the immersed surfaces

f_{\lambda}({\mathbb{T}})
and
f_{\varrho}({\mathbb{T}})
are symmetrical with respect to the wall of the AdS chambers

{\mathcal{A}}^{\pm}_{{P}_{4}}
.
The umbilical arcs
{\mathcal{C}}_{\pm}=f_{\lambda}({\mathrm{C}}_{\pm})=f_{\varrho}({\mathrm{C}}_{\pm})
are the intersections
of the surfaces with the AdS wall. They are standard torus knots of type
(m,n)
parametrized by

\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\ni s\mapsto\left[\pm(-\sin(ns),\cos(ns),-\sin(ms),\cos(ms),0)\right]\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\pm}.

If both
m
and
n
are odd, then
{\mathcal{C}}_{+}={\mathcal{C}}_{-}
.
Consequently, the umbilical points are double points of the
tautological immersions. In this case,
f_{\lambda}
and
f_{\varrho}
cannot be embeddings (see Figures
8
and
9
).
If one among
m
and
n
is even, the umbilical arcs are disjoint. The parabolic components
f_{\lambda}(\mathbb{T}^{+})
and
f_{\varrho}(\mathbb{T}^{-})
belong to the positive AdS chamber, while
f_{\lambda}(\mathbb{T}^{-})
and
f_{\varrho}(\mathbb{T}^{+})
belong to the negative one.
Due to the symmetry of the surfaces with respect to the wall,
f_{\lambda}(\mathbb{T}^{-})
and
f_{\varrho}(\mathbb{T}^{+})
are
mirror images of
f_{\lambda}(\mathbb{T}^{+})
and
f_{\varrho}(\mathbb{T}^{-})
(see Figures
10
and
11
).
Although not homogeneous, these immersions are of cohomogeneity
one. Since the directrix curves have constant curvatures, all these immersions are second order local deformations of each other.

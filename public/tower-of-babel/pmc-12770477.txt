Designing a novel radial basis neural structure for solving the dynamical hepatitis C virus model
Authors: Zulqurnain Sabir, Adilkazy Yessengaliyev, Abdikhalyk Temirzhan, Hikmet Koyunbakan, Saira Bhatti, Rana Nicolas
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The purpose of the current investigation is to design a novel radial basis neural network for solving the dynamical hepatitis C virus model in patients with a high baseline viral load, which represents the nonlinear dynamical structure. The infection and treatment in the hepatitis C virus comprise uninfected hepatocytes, creatively infected hepatocytes, and viruses. The aim of this study is to solve the dynamical hepatitis C virus model in patients with a high baseline viral load with the optimization of the Bayesian regularization scheme. A database reference solution is achieved by the explicit Runge–Kutta in interval 0 and 1 with the step size of 0.01 by data division into training as 72%, while 14%, 14% for endorsement, and testing. Twenty numbers of neurons, a feed forward neural network, activation radial basis function, and the optimization Bayesian regularization approach have been used to solve the hepatitis C virus model. The precision of the scheme is perceived by the outcomes overlapping and the reducible absolute error values, which are found as 10–06 to 10–08. A statistical evaluation utilizing various operators and proportional approaches is carried out in order to assess the solver’s efficiency.

Introduction

Hepatitis C virus (HCV) designates a virus of blood borne, which mainly contaminates the liver to produce inflammation or hepatitis. HCV is one of the major diseases of chronic liver disease globally. The virus is transferred through contact with an infected individual’s blood, mostly through sharing of needles or additional apparatus to inject drugs. HCV affects about three percent of people globally1. One of most prevalent and potentially fatal viral diseases in the United States is persistent HCV. The illness exhibits a protracted natural history and advances across several years. There is currently not an efficient alternate therapy for those who do not respond, and the present combination of interferon pegylated and a medication results in sustained virological response with a 50% rate2. Both viral and host variables are linked to reduced antiviral medication effectiveness3. The spread of the disease based on the HCV infection contains many factors, like population demographics, spread routes, disease evolution, intervention approaches, and treatment accessibility. The spreading of HCV mostly occurs based on the contact ratio of blood-to-blood. The system includes the parameters using the rate of transmission that vary with different factors, e.g., blood transfusions, injection drug, sexual and healthcare transmission.

The modelling based on the viral kinetics provides a significant part to analyze a decay process in the HCV-RNA after the antiviral therapy instigation, which is suggested by4. In recent decades, HCV dynamical model is introduced that is used to account for hepatocyte propagation5,6. The model contains the demographic features, e.g., gender, age, and the size of the population, along with some interactive factors. The critical drug effectiveness predicts the model, which is used to achieve the authorization of HCV. The model clarifies a range of HCV-RNA kinetic contours in preserved patients along with pathological rebound after the use of the therapy7. The obtainability and efficiency of the treatment of HCV present a vital role in order to lessen the disease burden and to control the virus spread. The model is used to combine the parameters based on the effectiveness, treatment acceptance, and the treatment effects using the transmission of disease. Some of the plans that are used to simulate the model to evaluate the effects of HCV transmission8.

The inventive form of the viral-kinetic system, which is presented by Neumann et al.9 along with some variations10, adopts a hepatocytes source neglecting the explicit proliferation concerns based on the uninfected and infected cells. A model based on the infection of HCV contains the density-dependent hepatocyte proliferation11. The initiation of Direct-Acting Antivirals (DAAs) has transformed the conduct of HCV infection14. DAAs have developed a mainstream conduct in developed republics because of its tolerability, safety, and high efficacy. These treatments target precise phases in the HCV repetition cycle, which present a more effective and precise scheme to conduct. The patients of HCV are competently enhanced by the treatment of DAAs15. The rate of continued virological response is obtained at over 90% in the populations of patients, which presents evidence of their effectiveness. DAAs also have lesser side effects and a compact treatment period in comparison to the conventional interferon type of therapies, which has prepared treatments more available and interesting to a broader variety of patients. In the advanced countries, the general DAAs adoption has distorted the HCV treatment16. The scientists related to medical fields have an influential tool to fight against the illness, and patients can assume better consequences with lesser side effects.

Optimization algorithm

The algorithms based on optimization play a vital role in choosing the optimal solutions from the available options in order to improve, analyze, or classify the existing systems. These algorithms are normally divided into heuristic and deterministic approaches. In the process of neural networks, these algorithms are applied to check the data training by adjusting their parameters with the process to minimize the cost function. To mention a few of the optimization schemes are gradient methods, and many other approaches that are helpful to obtain the optimal performance of the network to obtain the tasks of data processing. The current investigations are related to reducing the cost function by using one of the reliable optimization schemes named Bayesian regularization to get the solutions of the model. Conclusions

In this research, a design of RBNN has been presented for solving the dynamical hepatitis C virus model. The infection and treatment in the hepatitis C virus are mathematically classified into uninfected hepatocytes, creatively infected hepatocytes, and viruses. Some of the conclusions of this research are presented as:The numerical performances of the dynamical hepatitis C virus model have been presented successfully by using the designed stochastic structure.The optimization has been performed through one of the competent BR approaches to solve the hepatitis C virus system.The use of one dynamic into another makes the model nonlinear, and the proposed stochastic solver has been implemented efficiently for the nonlinear system.A database reference solution has been obtained using Runge–Kutta scheme.Twenty neurons, activation radial basis function, and optimization Bayesian regularization approach have been used to solve the hepatitis C virus model.The scheme’s correctness has been observed through the outputs matching and small AE that is calculated around 10–5 to 10–7.A statistical evaluation has been used in various operators along with proportional approaches, which is carried out in order to assess the solver’s efficiency.

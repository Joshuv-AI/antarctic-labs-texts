Introduction

Phase separation is a cross-disciplinary field, which attracts researchers from physics
[
weber2019physics
,
ter2013collected
,
MR0523642
,
gurtin1985theory
,
ginzburg1958theory
,
cahn1958free
,
allen1972ground
,
MR1002633
]
, biology
[
brangwynne2009germline
,
alberti2017phase
,
mitrea2016phase
]
and social sciences
[
harding2020population
,
castellano2009statistical
]
. This model is used to describe a broad variety of systems characterized by the interaction of two (or more) components, which are associated to different values of a suitable state parameter. Such a parameter is often expressed by a function
u:\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[-1,1]
, where the pure phases are given by
-1
and
1
.

In the classical framework, a prototype of energy for phase separation is given by the non-scaled energy functional

\mathcal{E}^{p}(u,\Omega):=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}W(u(x))\,dx,

(1.1)

where
p\in(1,+\infty)
. The function
W:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a double-well potential satisfying

W(t)>0\quad\mbox{for all}\quad t\in(-1,1)\quad\mbox{and}\quad W(\pm 1)=0.

In this framework, phase separation is induced by the minimization of
\mathcal{E}^{p}
. More precisely,
on the one hand, the action of the potential
W
pushes the minimizers of
\mathcal{E}^{p}
to attain values as close as possible to its zeros, namely the pure phases
\pm 1
. Furthermore, the presence of the gradient term of
L^{p}
-type discourages the formation of subregions of
\Omega
where the state parameter jumps from one pure phase to the other. These regions, which are characterized by intermediate values of the state parameter, are known as “interfaces”.

A common matter of investigation in physics, biology, mathematical physics and analysis is the description of these interfaces. An interesting approach to this problem is provided by density estimates.

The aim of density estimates is to establish lower bounds for the measure of the portion of the domain where the state parameter attains values close to the pure phases. The first result in this direction was obtained by L. Caffarelli and A. Córdoba. In
[
MR1310848
]
they proved that if
u:\Omega\to[-1,1]
is a minimizer for
\mathcal{E}^{2}
, then for every
\theta\in(-1,1)
and
r\in(0,+\infty)
large enough it holds that
\left\{\left|u\right|>\theta\right\}\cap B_{r}
is comparable to
r^{n}
. On the other hand, they showed that the
measure of the interface
\left\{\left|u\right|\leqslant\theta\right\}\cap B_{r}
behaves like
r^{n-1}
.

Since the pioneering work of L. Caffarelli and A. Córdoba, density estimates have been established for minimizers and quasiminimizers of a larger class of energy functionals associated to phenomena of phase separation. In
[
MR2126143
]
and
[
6
]
density estimates have been proved for minimizers of
\mathcal{E}^{p}
with
p\in(1,+\infty)
. Also,
\chi
-shaped potentials (i.e., piecewise constant potentials) are considered, which are associated to models for fluid jets. In
[
MR2413100
]
density estimates are proved for the same energies but under the weaker assumption of quasiminimality.

Nonlocal counterparts of the energies studied in
[
MR1310848
,
MR2126143
,
6
,
MR2413100
]
have already been considered in the literature. A common approach to generalize phase separation in a nonlocal framework is to replace the gradient term of
L^{p}
-type with the Gagliardo seminorm. With such a choice, the energy used to describe nonlocal phase separation becomes

\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(u,\Omega):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\Omega}W(u(x))\,dx,

(1.2)

where
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in(1,+\infty)
.

Density estimates for the energy functional in (
1.2
) with
p=2
were proved in
[
MR2873236
]
for every
s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
, making use of the Sobolev inequality. Moreover, in
[
MR3133422
]
, the same authors generalized the result for every
s\in(0,1)
.

In all the aforementioned papers on density estimates, the potential
W
is taken such that its growth from the minima is comparable to a polynomial of degree
m
, where
m\in\left(1,p\right]
and
p\in(1,+\infty)
is the homogeneity of the gradient term. Potentials
W
that detach from their minima with
m\geqslant p
are known as
degenerate potentials
, and to the best of our knowledge these have been previously studied in the framework of density estimates only in two others research articles, namely
[
1
]
and
[
DFVERPP
]
.

In the local setting, see
[
1
]
and the recent
[
savin2025density
]
, density estimates for degenerate double-well potentials have been proved for quasiminimizers of
\mathcal{E}^{p}
. For our purposes, it is also important to recall that the result in
[
1
]
came with an additional restriction
on the growth of the potential with respect to its wells. Indeed, in Theorem 1.1 of
[
1
]
, density estimates are showed as far as
p\in(1,+\infty)
,
m\in\left[p,+\infty\right)
and
n
satisfy

\frac{pm}{m-p}>n,

(1.3)

see (1.7) in
[
1
]
. In the most recent paper
[
savin2025density
]
, density estimates for quasiminimizers have been showed without the additional assumption in (
1.3
), but with stronger conditions on the quasiminimizer, see Remark
2.17
.

In the nonlocal setting, see
[
DFVERPP
]
, density estimates for degenerate double-well potentials have been proved for minimizers of
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}
for every
p\in(1,+\infty)
and
s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{p}\right)
. This result was obtained through the Sobolev inequality, with an approach similar to the one used by E. Valdionci and O. Savin in
[
MR2873236
]
. Nevertheless, the presence of a degenerate double-well potential together with a homogeneity
p\neq 2
required the introduction of a new barrier bespoke for the specifics of the problem under consideration, see Theorem 3.1 in
[
DFVERPP
]
.

In this paper, we consider the renormalized non-scaled free energy

\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(u,\Omega):=(1-s)\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy\right)+\int_{\Omega}W(u(x))\,dx,

(1.4)

with
W
being a degenerate double-well potential. In particular, we complete the work begun in
[
DFVERPP
]
, and establish, for every
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in(1,+\infty)
, density estimates for a class of state parameters which includes all minimizers of
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}
, see Theorems
2.7
and
2.11
below.

The presence of
(1-s)
in front of the kinetic term is needed to guarantee that as
s\to 1
the energy
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}

\Gamma
-converges to an energy proportional to the one in (
1.1
), see Theorem
2.15
below. From this we will be able to obtain density estimates for minimizers of (
1.1
) starting from the nonlocal density estimates obtained in Theorem
2.11
. As a byproduct, we are able to show that the condition in (
1.3
) for minimizers is not sharp, and density estimates hold for the local and degenerate case for every
n\geqslant 1
,
p\in(1,+\infty)
and
m\in\left[p,+\infty\right)
, see Theorem
2.16
.

The precise hypotheses on
W
, the statement of Theorems
2.7
,
2.11
,
2.15
and
2.16
and their corollaries are all discussed in the following section.

2
Mathematical framework and main results

In what follows we let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
open,
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in(1,+\infty)
. We consider a potential
W:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that

W\mbox{ is lower semicontinuous}.

(2.1)

Also, we suppose that, for every
x\in[-1,1]
,

\lambda\chi_{(-\infty,\theta]}(x)\left|1+x\right|^{m}\leqslant W(x)\leqslant\Lambda\left|1+x\right|^{m},

(2.2)

for some
\lambda\in(0,1]
,
\Lambda\in[1,+\infty)
,
\theta\in(-1,1)
and
m\in\left[p,+\infty\right)
.

Moreover, we assume that, for all
-1\leqslant r\leqslant t\leqslant-1+q
,

W(t)-W(r)\geqslant c_{1}(t-r)\left|1+r\right|^{m-1}+c_{1}(t-r)^{k},

(2.3)

for some
q\in(0,1)
and
c_{1}\in(0,+\infty)
,
where

k:=\begin{cases}m\quad&\mbox{if}\quad m\in\mathbb{N}\\
\left[m\right]+1\quad&\mbox{if}\quad m\in(1,+\infty)\setminus\mathbb{N}.\end{cases}

(2.4)

Given
m\in(1,+\infty)
, the prototype of double-well potential that we have in mind has the form

W_{m}(x):=\frac{(1-x^{2})^{m}}{2m},

since one can show the validity of condition (
2.3
) for this model case (see
In Proposition
A.1
).

For every measurable
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
we define

\mathcal{K}_{s}^{p}(u,\Omega):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy

(2.5)

and rewrite the energy in (
1.4
) as

\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(u,\Omega):=(1-s)\mathcal{K}_{s}^{p}(u,\Omega)+\int_{\Omega}W(u)\,dx.

(2.6)

The normalizing constant
(1-s)
in front of
\mathcal{K}_{s}^{p}
is needed to obtain
stable estimates as
s\to 1
and
recover density estimates for the local energy in (
1.1
) starting from the nonlocal ones. We will achieve such a result, see Theorem
2.16
, by proving first that
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}

\Gamma
-converges as
s\to 1
to an energy proportional to the one in (
1.1
), see Theorem
2.15
. This point will be discussed more in detail in Section
2.2
.

A suitable function space to study
\mathcal{K}_{s}^{p}
is

\widehat{H}^{s,p}(\Omega):=\left\{u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\mbox{ measurable s.t. }\mathcal{K}_{s}^{p}(u,\Omega)<+\infty\right\}.

(2.7)

Since the potential
W
is defined in the interval
[-1,1]
, the suitable subspace of
\widehat{H}^{s,p}(\Omega)
needed to study
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}
is

X^{s,p}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in\widehat{H}^{s,p}(\Omega)\mbox{ s.t. }\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant 1\right\}.

(2.8)

Moreover, it will be useful to consider the subspace of
X^{s,p}(\Omega)
of functions with a prescribed trace. Hence, given any measurable
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[-1,1]
we denote the subspace of
X^{s,p}(\Omega)
with trace
f
, i.e.

X_{f}^{s,p}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in X^{s,p}(\Omega)\mbox{ s.t. }u=f\mbox{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\right\}.

Then, we have the following definition:

Definition 2.1

(
\epsilon
-minimizer)
.

If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is bounded, given
\epsilon\in[0,+\infty)
, we say that
u\in X^{s,p}(\Omega)
is an
\epsilon
-minimizer for
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}
if, for every
v\in X_{u}^{s,p}(\Omega)
,

\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(u,\Omega)\leqslant\epsilon+\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(v,\Omega).

If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is unbounded, we say that
u\in X^{s,p}(\Omega)
is an
\epsilon
-minimizer for
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(\cdot,\Omega)
if for any
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
it is a minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(\cdot,\Omega^{\prime})
.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that if
u
is an
\epsilon
-minimizer for
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}
in
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
open and bounded, then
u

\epsilon
-minimizes
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}
in every measurable subdomain
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
.

We observe that for
\epsilon=0
one recovers the definition of minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}
, see for e.g. Definition 1.1 in
[
DFVERPP
]
. It is also interesting to compare the notion of
\epsilon
-minimizer with the notion of quasiminimizer. This was introduced in the classical variational framework by E. Giusti and M. Giaquinta in
[
MR0666107
,
giaquinta1984quasi
]
.

We provide here a definition of quasiminimizer for the nonlocal setting of the energy
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}
:

Definition 2.3

(
Q
-minimizer for
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}
)
.

For every open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
Q\in[1,+\infty)
we say that
u\in X^{s,p}(\Omega)
is a
Q
-minimizer for
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}
in
\Omega
if for every open set
A\subset\Omega
and
v\in X_{u}^{s,p}(A)
it holds that

\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(u,A)\leqslant Q\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(v,A).

(2.9)

Then, in the following remark, we show that for every
Q
and
\epsilon
there is a
\epsilon
-minimizer which is not a
Q
-minimizer.

Remark 2.4
.

In this remark we show that for every
Q\in(1,+\infty)
and
\tilde{\epsilon}\in(0,1)
, there exists some
\tilde{\epsilon}
-minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}
in
B_{1}
which is not a
Q
-minimizer. To do so, we introduce the function

u(x):=\begin{cases}1\quad&\mbox{for all}\quad x\in B_{e^{-2}}\\
\left|\ln(\left|x\right|)\right|-1\quad&\mbox{for all}\quad x\in B_{1}\setminus B_{e^{-2}}\\
-1\quad&\mbox{for all}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}.\end{cases}

(2.10)

We observe that

\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant e^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant 1.

(2.11)

From this it follows that

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}_{s}^{p}\left(u,B_{1}\right)&\leqslant\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{2}}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2}}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\leqslant\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{2}}\frac{dx\,dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp-p}}+2^{p}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2}}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{dx\,dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\\
&\leqslant e^{2p}\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{2}}\frac{dx\,dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp-p}}+2^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2}}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{dx\,dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\\
&\leqslant C_{n,s,p},\end{split}

(2.12)

for some
C_{n,s,p}\in(0,+\infty)
. In particular, thanks to (
2.11
) and this last equation we deduce that
u\in X^{s,p}(B_{1})
. Also, we notice that there exists some constant
c_{n,s,p}>0
such that

\mathcal{K}_{s}^{p}(u,B_{1})\geqslant c_{n,s,p}.

(2.13)

Now, we rescale
u
as

u_{\epsilon}(x):=u\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}\right).

(2.14)

and we claim that

\mbox{for every $\tilde{\epsilon}\in(0,1)$ there exists some $\epsilon>0$ such that $u_{\epsilon}$ is a $\tilde{\epsilon}$-minimizer in $B_{1}$}.

(2.15)

First, we observe that thanks to (
2.2
) we have that

\int_{B_{1}}W\left(u_{\epsilon}(x)\right)\,dx=\int_{B_{\epsilon}}W\left(u_{\epsilon}(x)\right)\,dx=\epsilon^{n}\int_{B_{1}}W(u(x))\,dx\leqslant\epsilon^{n}\Lambda 2^{m}\mathcal{L}^{n}\left(B_{1}\right).

(2.16)

Moreover, by scaling and making use of (
2.12
) together with the fact that
supp(u+1)\subset B_{1}
we obtain that

\mathcal{K}_{s}^{p}\left(u_{\epsilon},B_{1}\right)=\epsilon^{n-sp}\mathcal{K}_{s}^{p}\left(u,B_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\right)=\epsilon^{n-sp}\mathcal{K}_{s}^{p}(u,B_{1})\leqslant\epsilon^{n-sp}C_{n,s,p}.

(2.17)

In particular, from equation (
2.16
) and (
2.17
), and choosing
\epsilon
small enough we obtain that

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(u_{\epsilon},B_{1})&=(1-s)\mathcal{K}_{s}^{p}(u_{\epsilon},B_{1})+\int_{B_{1}}W\left(u_{\epsilon}\right)\,dx\\
&\leqslant\epsilon^{n-sp}C_{n,s,p}(1-s)+\epsilon^{n}\Lambda 2^{m}\mathcal{L}^{n}(B_{1})\\
&=\epsilon^{n-sp}\left(C_{n,s,p}(1-s)+\epsilon^{sp}\Lambda 2^{m}\mathcal{L}^{n}(B_{1})\right)\\
&\leqslant\tilde{\epsilon}.\end{split}

From this, it follows immediately that for every
v\in X_{u}^{s,p}(B_{1})
it holds that

\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(u,B_{1})\leqslant\tilde{\epsilon}+\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(v,B_{1})

proving claim (
2.15
). Furthermore, we observe that as a consequence of (
2.13
)

\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(u_{\epsilon},B_{\epsilon})\geqslant(1-s)\mathcal{K}_{s}^{p}(u_{\epsilon},B_{\epsilon})=(1-s)\epsilon^{n-s}\mathcal{K}_{s}^{p}(u,B_{1})\geqslant c_{n,s,p}(1-s)\epsilon^{n-sp}.

(2.18)

Now, that we proved claim (
2.15
), we observe that there exists no
Q\in[1,+\infty)
such that
u_{\epsilon}
is a
Q
-minimizer in
B_{1}
. As a matter of fact, we observe that if
v\equiv 1\in X_{u_{\epsilon}}^{s,p}(B_{\epsilon})
, using (
2.18
) we obtain

\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(v,B_{\epsilon})=0<(1-s)c_{s,n,p}\epsilon^{n-sp}\leqslant\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(u_{\epsilon},B_{\epsilon}).

We also observe that every
Q
-minimizer
u
in
\Omega
is an
\epsilon
-minimizer, for an
\epsilon
which depends on
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(u,\Omega)
. More precisely:

Remark 2.5
.

Suppose that
Q\in(1,+\infty)
and that
u\in X^{s,p}(\Omega)
is a
Q
-minimizer for
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}
in
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Furthermore, we let
E_{0}\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(u,\Omega)\leqslant E_{0}.

Then, we have that

u
is an
\epsilon
-minimizer for
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}
for
\epsilon:=(Q-1)E_{0}
.

To show this, we need to prove that for every
v\in X_{u}^{s,p}(\Omega)
it holds that

\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}\left(u,\Omega\right)\leqslant\epsilon+\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}\left(v,\Omega\right).

(2.19)

Indeed, we observe that if
v\in X_{u}^{s,p}(\Omega)
, then we have that either

\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(u,\Omega)<\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(v,\Omega)

(2.20)

or

\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(u,\Omega)\geqslant\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(v,\Omega).

(2.21)

If (
2.20
) holds true than (
2.19
) follows immediately. Now, we assume that (
2.21
) holds true. Then, in this case

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(u,\Omega)&\leqslant Q\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(v,\Omega)\\
&\leqslant\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(v,\Omega)+(Q-1)\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(v,\Omega)\\
&=\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(v,\Omega)+(Q-1)\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(u,\Omega)\\
&\leqslant\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(v,\Omega)+(Q-1)E_{0}\\
&=\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(v,\Omega)+\epsilon.\end{split}

For every
u\in C_{\textit{loc}}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
, we define the quantity

\left[u\right]_{B,\alpha}:=\sup\left\{\left[u\right]_{C^{\alpha}(B_{1}(x_{0}))}\mbox{ s.t. }x_{0}\in B_{R}\mbox{ and }B_{R+2}\subset\Omega\right\}

(2.22)

and we consider the subspace of
C_{\textit{loc}}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
given by

C_{B}^{\alpha}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in C_{\textit{loc}}^{\alpha}(\Omega)\mbox{ s.t. }\left[u\right]_{B,\alpha}<+\infty\right\}.

Note that for every
\Omega\neq\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have that
C_{\textit{loc}}^{\alpha}(\Omega)=C_{B}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
.

Now we introduce the mathematical setting necessary to study the local contribution to the energy functional in (
1.1
). To do so, we let

K_{n,p}:=\int_{\partial B_{1}}\left|\omega\cdot e_{1}\right|^{p}\,dH_{\omega}^{n-1}

and, for every
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
,

\mathcal{K}_{1}^{p}(u,\Omega):=\frac{K_{n,p}}{2p}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx.

Furthermore, we define

X^{1,p}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\mbox{ s.t. }\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leqslant 1\right\}

and, for every
u\in X^{1,p}(\Omega)
, we consider the energy functional

\mathcal{E}_{1}^{p}(u,\Omega):=\mathcal{K}_{1}^{p}(u,\Omega)+\int_{\Omega}W(u(x))\,dx.

(2.23)

Also, as customary we denote by
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}

Remark 2.10
.

We remark that in the framework of density estimates for minimizers and quasiminimizers, the condition in (

) is often replaced by a pointwise condition, see for instance (1.6) in
[
MR3133422
]
. More precisely, for a given point
x_{0}\in\Omega
, which for convenience is chosen to be
x_{0}=0
, one can assume that

u(0)>\theta_{1}.

(2.27)

Then, from (
2.27
), as a consequence of the continuity for minimizers and quasiminimizers of
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}
, see e.g.
[

,

, one obtains the existence of some

such that (
2.25
) is satisfied.

We observe that in the framework of
\epsilon
-minimizers it is not possible to replace (
2.25
) with (
2.27
). To show this, we assume that
sp\in(0,1)
, and given
\eta\in(0,1)
we consider the discontinuous function

u_{\eta}(x):=\begin{cases}-1\quad&\mbox{for all}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\eta}\\
1\quad&\mbox{for all}\quad x\in B_{\eta}.\end{cases}

Then, we claim that for every
R>\mu
and

for every
\epsilon\in(0,1)
there exists some
\eta\in(0,1)
such that
u_{\eta}
is an
\epsilon
-minimizer for
\mathcal{E}_{s,p}
in
B_{R}
.

(2.28)

Indeed, we can compute that

\int_{B_{R}}W\left(u_{\eta}(x)\right)\,dx=0.

(2.29)

Also, we have that

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}_{s}^{p}\left(u_{\eta},B_{R}\right)&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}\frac{\left|u_{\eta}(x)-u_{\eta}(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{B_{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{\left|u_{\eta}(x)-u_{\eta}(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy\\
&=2^{p-1}\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{\eta}}\int_{B_{\eta}}\frac{dx\,dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}+2^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\int_{B_{\eta}}\frac{dx\,dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\\
&\leqslant 2^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\eta}}\int_{B_{\eta}}\frac{dx\,dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\\
&=c_{s,n,p}\eta^{1-sp},\end{split}

(2.30)

for some

) it follows that for every

proving claim (
2.28

Now, on one hand, we notice that
u_{\eta}
satisfies (
2.27

However, in Theorem

), we need to consider the fact that

We begin by proving Theorem

. The result employed to estimate the left-hand side of (

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
,
p\in(1,+\infty)
and
r_{1},r_{2}\in[0,+\infty)
such that
\left|B_{r_{2}}\right|\geqslant\kappa
for some
\kappa\in(0,+\infty)
. Also, let

l(B_{r_{2}}):=\begin{cases}\left|B_{r_{2}}\right|^{\frac{1-sp}{n}}\quad&\mbox{if}\quad s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{p}\right)\\
\left|\ln\left(\left|B_{r_{2}}\right|\right)\right|\quad&\mbox{if}\quad s=\frac{1}{p}\\
1\quad&\mbox{if}\quad s\in\left(\frac{1}{p},1\right).\end{cases}

(3.37)

Then, there exists

(3.38)

Proposition
3.4
is a direct consequence of the following result:

Theorem 3.5
.

,

,

,

\delta R^{n-1}\log\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)\quad&\mbox{if}\quad s=\frac{1}{p}\\
\delta R^{n-sp}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{1-sp}\quad&\mbox{if}\quad s\in\left(\frac{1}{p},1\right).\end{cases}

(3.39)

The proof of Theorem

(3.79)

Proof of Theorems

&

The proof of Theorem
2.15
relies the
\Gamma
-convergence of the Gagliardo seminorm, which has already been discussed in
[
MR2033060
]
and
[
MR4544090
]
. In particular, we follow the proof of Theorem 2.1 in
[
MR4544090
]
, where the
\Gamma
-convergence of
\mathcal{K}_{s}^{2}
is showed for traceless functions. Here minor adjustments are needed due to the different homogeneity
p\in(1,+\infty)
of the seminorm and the relaxed condition on the trace.

In what follows we will adopt the following notation. For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
we define

d(x,A):=\inf_{y\in A}\left|x-y\right|.

Furthermore, for any
\epsilon\in(0,+\infty)
we set

\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mbox{ s.t. }d(x,\Omega)\leqslant\epsilon\right\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}:=\left\{x\in\Omega\mbox{ s.t. }d(x,\partial\Omega)\geqslant\epsilon\right\}.

(4.1)

Note that
\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}
might be empty for large values of
\epsilon
. Furthermore, given any measurable
g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
we adopt the following notation for its zero extension in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega

\bar{g}(x):=\begin{cases}g(x)\quad&\mbox{if}\quad x\in\Omega\\
0\quad&\mbox{if}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega.\end{cases}

Moreover, for every
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
measurable we consider the translation operator defined by

\tau_{h}f(\cdot):=f(\cdot+h).

We are now ready for the proof of Theorem
2.15
, which will be divided into
its specific parts.

Proof of Theorem
2.15
(i).

We make use of Proposition
D.3
and obtain that, for every
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\begin{split}\left\|\tau_{h}u_{s_{k}}-u_{s_{k}}\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\Omega_{\left|h\right|}^{-}\right)}^{p}&=\int_{\Omega_{\left|h\right|}^{-}}\left|u_{s_{k}}(h+y)-u_{s_{k}}(y)\right|^{p}\,dy\\
&\leqslant\bar{C}_{n,p}\left|h\right|^{s_{k}p}(1-s_{k})\int_{B_{\left|h\right|}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|u_{s_{k}}(x+y)-u_{s_{k}}(x)\right|^{p}}{\left|y\right|^{n+s_{k}p}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\leqslant\bar{C}_{n,p}\left|h\right|^{s_{k}p}(1-s_{k})\int_{\Omega_{\left|h\right|}^{+}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|u_{s_{k}}(y)-u_{s_{k}}(x)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+s_{k}p}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\leqslant\bar{C}_{n,p}\left|h\right|^{s_{k}p}2M,\end{split}

(4.2)

where
\bar{C}_{n,p}\in(0,+\infty)
is a constant depending only on
n
and
p
.

As a result,

\begin{split}\left\|\tau_{h}\bar{u}_{s_{k}}-\bar{u}_{s_{k}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\bar{u}_{s_{k}}(h+y)-\bar{u}_{s_{k}}(y)\right|^{p}\,dy\\
&\leqslant\int_{\Omega_{\left|h\right|}^{-}}\left|u_{s_{k}}(h+y)-u_{s_{k}}(y)\right|^{p}\,dy+2^{p}\int_{\Omega_{\left|h\right|}^{+}\setminus\Omega_{\left|h\right|}^{-}}\left\|u_{s_{k}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,dy\\
&\leqslant\bar{C}_{n,p}\left|h\right|^{s_{k}p}2M+2^{p}\left|\Omega_{\left|h\right|}^{+}\setminus\Omega_{\left|h\right|}^{-}\right|.\end{split}

(4.3)

Also, we notice that, as a consequence of the Dominated Convergence Theorem,

\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\left|\Omega_{\left|h\right|}^{+}\setminus\Omega_{\left|h\right|}^{-}\right|=0.

Thanks to this and (
4.3
), we can apply the
Fréchet-Kolmogorov to
\bar{u}_{s_{k}}
, see for instance Theorem 4.26 in
[
MR2759829
]
, and obtain that there exists
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
such that, up to a subsequence,

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\left\|u_{s_{k}}-u\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=0.

(4.4)

Therefore, taking the limit as
k\to+\infty
in (
4.2
), it follows from (
4.4
) that, for every
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\left\|\tau_{h}u-u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\Omega_{\left|h\right|}^{-}\right)}\leqslant\left(2M\bar{C}_{n,p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left|h\right|.

In particular, employing Proposition 9.3 in
[
MR2759829
]
we deduce that
Du\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and therefore
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

Now we assume that
u_{s_{k}}\in X_{0}^{s_{k},p}(\Omega)
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,
u_{s_{k}}\equiv\bar{u}_{s_{k}}
. In particular, we obtain that

\mathcal{K}_{s_{k}}^{p}(u_{s_{k}},\Omega)=\frac{1}{2}\left[u_{s_{k}}\right]_{W^{s_{k},p}(\Omega)}^{p}.

It follows from this, (
2.37
) and Proposition
D.3
that, for every open and bounded
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\left\|\tau_{h}u_{s_{k}}-u_{s_{k}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leqslant\left(2\bar{C}_{n,p}M\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left|h\right|^{s_{k}}.

(4.5)

Consequently, by Theorem 4.26 in
[
MR2759829
]
it holds that
u_{s_{k}}\to u
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
u\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
. By taking the limit as
k\to+\infty
in (
4.5
), we obtain that

\left\|\tau_{h}u-u\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leqslant\left(2\bar{C}_{n,p}M\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left|h\right|.

From this and Proposition 9.3 in
[
MR2759829
]
it follows that
Du\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since
u\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
and
\partial\Omega
is Lipschitz, we find that
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. This concludes the proof of Theorem
2.15
(i).
∎

Proof of Theorem

(ii).

Since

, with no loss of generality we can suppose that there exists

such that

\liminf_{k\to+\infty}(1-s_{k})\mathcal{K}_{s_{k}}^{p}(u_{s_{k}},\Omega)\leqslant M_{1}.

(4.6)

In particular, up to a subsequence, we can assume that (
2.37
) holds true. Hence, it follows from Theorem

.

Now, let

and

Also, for

and

\mathcal{K}_{s}^{p}(v,\Omega)\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left|v_{\epsilon}(x)-v_{\epsilon}(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx

(4.8)

Suppose for the moment that claims (
4.7
) and (
4.8
) hold true. Then, we obtain that

\begin{split}\liminf_{k\to+\infty}(1-s_{k})\mathcal{K}_{s_{k}}^{p}(u_{s_{k}},\Omega)&\geqslant\frac{\left|\partial B_{1}\right|}{2p}\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\left|\nabla u_{s_{k},\epsilon}\right|^{p}\,dx.\end{split}

(4.9)

Also, we observe that

(4.10)

Hence, we infer from (

) and (

) that

\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{K}_{s_{k}}^{p}(u_{s_{k}},\Omega)\geqslant\frac{\left|\partial B_{1}\right|}{2p}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\left|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\right|^{p}\,dx.

(4.11)

We also notice that since

in

, then, up to a subsequence,

a.e. in
\Omega
. Since

a.e. in
\Omega
. Besides, making use of Fatou’s Lemma and of

(4.12)

To prove (

Using Jensen inequality we obtain that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left|v_{\epsilon}(x)-v_{\epsilon}(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(v(x-z)-v(y-z)\right)\eta_{\epsilon}(z)\,dz\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy\\

\leqslant&\mathcal{K}_{s}^{p}(v,\Omega).\end{split}

4.8

let

\begin{split}\left|u_{s_{k},\epsilon}(x)-u_{s_{k},\epsilon}(y)\right|^{p}=&\left|\nabla u_{s_{k},\epsilon}(x)\cdot(x-y)+D^{2}u_{s_{k},\epsilon}(\xi)\cdot(x-y)^{2}\right|^{p}\\

(4.13)

Now, we distinguish between the cases

\left|\nabla u_{s_{k},\epsilon}(x)\cdot(x-y)\right|\leqslant\left|D^{2}u_{s_{k}\epsilon}(\xi)\cdot(x-y)^{2}\right|

(4.14)

\begin{split}\left|u_{s_{k},\epsilon}(x)-u_{s_{k},\epsilon}(y)\right|^{p}&\geqslant\left|\nabla u_{s_{k},\epsilon}(x)\cdot(x-y)\right|^{p}-\left|D^{2}u_{s_{k},\epsilon}(\xi)\cdot(x-y)^{2}\right|^{p}\\

Now, since

\left\|u_{s_{k},\epsilon}\right\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant\left\|\eta_{\epsilon}\right\|_{W^{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

\begin{split}\left|u_{s_{k},\epsilon}(x)-u_{s_{k},\epsilon}(y)\right|^{p}&\geqslant\left|\nabla u_{s_{k},\epsilon}(x)\cdot\frac{(x-y)}{\left|x-y\right|}\right|^{p}\left|x-y\right|^{p}-\left\|\eta_{\epsilon}\right\|_{W^{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\left|x-y\right|^{2p}\\

as desired.

\begin{split}&\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\begin{pmatrix}p\\

\begin{split}\sum_{j=1}^{[p]}&\left|\begin{pmatrix}p\\

\begin{split}(1-s_{k})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left|u_{s_{k},\epsilon}(x)-u_{s_{k},\epsilon}(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+s_{k}p}}\,dy\geqslant&(1-s_{k})\int_{B_{1}(x)}\frac{\left|u_{s_{k},\epsilon}(x)-u_{s_{k},\epsilon}(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+s_{k}p}}\,dy\\

We notice that, for every

=&K_{n,p}\left|\nabla u_{s_{k},\epsilon}(x)\right|^{p}\int_{0}^{1}t^{p(1-s_{k})-1}\,dt\\
=&\frac{K_{n,p}}{p(1-s_{k})}\left|\nabla u_{s_{k},\epsilon}(x)\right|^{p}.\end{split}

and this concludes the proof of (

Therefore, recalling (

\frac{R^{n-1}}{sp-1}(1-R^{1-sp})\quad&\mbox{if}\quad s\in\left(1/p,1\right).\end{cases}

(5.16)

Finally, using (

(5.17)

Appendix B
Proof of Theorem
3.5

In this section we prove Theorem
3.5
.
The estimate in Theorem
3.5
is actually a consequence of the following integral inequality:

Proposition B.1
.

Let
R,\sigma\in(0,+\infty)
. Then, there exists a constant
c_{n,\sigma}\in(0,+\infty)

such that, for every
x\in B_{R}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+\sigma}}\geqslant\frac{c_{n,\sigma}}{\sigma}(R-\left|x\right|)^{-\sigma}.

(B.1)

Furthermore, if
\sigma\in(0,M)
for some
M\in(0,+\infty)
, it holds that
c_{n,\sigma}\geqslant c_{n,M}
.

Proof.

To show (
B.1
), we begin by observing that, for every
x\in B_{R}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+\sigma}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{dy}{\left|\left|x\right|e_{1}-y\right|^{n+\sigma}}.

(B.2)

Also, given
m>0
, we define

T_{m}(\left|x\right|):=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\,z_{1}\in(R,+\infty)\mbox{ and }-m(z_{1}-\left|x\right|)\leqslant z_{i}\leqslant m(z_{1}-\left|x\right|)\mbox{ for all }i=2,\dots,n\right\},

(B.3)

and we notice that

T_{m}(\left|x\right|)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}.

(B.4)

Accordingly, thanks to (
B.2
), (
B.3
) and (
B.4
) we obtain that

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+\sigma}}&\geqslant\int_{T_{m}(\left|x\right|)}\frac{dz}{\left|e_{1}\left|x\right|-z\right|^{n+\sigma}}\\
&=\int_{R}^{+\infty}\int_{-m(z_{1}-\left|x\right|)}^{m(z_{1}-\left|x\right|)}\dots\int_{-m(z_{1}-\left|x\right|)}^{m(z_{1}-\left|x\right|)}\frac{dz_{n}\dots dz_{2}}{\left|\left|x\right|e_{1}-z\right|^{n+\sigma}}\,dz_{1}.\end{split}

Besides, we observe that, for every
z\in T_{m}(\left|x\right|)
,

\begin{split}\left|\left|x\right|e_{1}-z\right|^{2}&=\left(\left|x\right|-z_{1}\right)^{2}+z_{2}^{2}\dots+z_{n}^{2}\\
&\leqslant\left(\left|x\right|-z_{1}\right)^{2}+m^{2}\left(z_{1}-\left|x\right|\right)^{2}+\dots+m^{2}\left(1-z_{1}\right)^{2}\\
&=((n-1)m^{2}+1)\left(z_{1}-\left|x\right|\right)^{2}.\end{split}

(B.5)

Thus, making use of (
B.4
) and (
B.5
) we evince that

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+\sigma}}&\geqslant\frac{1}{((n-1)m^{2}+1)^{\frac{n+\sigma}{2}}}\int_{R}^{+\infty}\int_{-m(z_{1}-\left|x\right|)}^{m(z_{1}-\left|x\right|)}\dots\int_{-m(z_{1}-\left|x\right|)}^{m(z_{1}-\left|x\right|)}\frac{dz_{n}\dots dz_{2}}{\left|z_{1}-\left|x\right|\right|^{n+\sigma}}\,dz_{1}\\
&=\frac{(2m)^{n-1}}{((n-1)m^{2}+1)^{\frac{n+1+s}{2}}}\int_{R}^{+\infty}\frac{(z_{1}-\left|x\right|)^{n-1}}{\left|z_{1}-\left|x\right|\right|^{n+\sigma}}\,dz_{1}\\
&=\frac{2^{n}m^{n}}{(nm^{2}+1)^{\frac{n+1+s}{2}}}\int_{R}^{+\infty}\frac{dz_{1}}{\left|z_{1}-\left|x\right|\right|^{1+\sigma}}\,dz_{1}\\
&=\frac{(2m)^{n-1}}{\sigma((n-1)m^{2}+1)^{\frac{n+\sigma}{2}}}(R-\left|x\right|)^{-s}\\
&\geqslant\frac{c_{n,\sigma}}{\sigma}(R-\left|x\right|)^{-s}.\end{split}

(B.6)

Also, we notice that if
\sigma\in(0,M)
for some
M\in(0,+\infty)
, then
c_{n,\sigma}\geqslant c_{n,M}
.
∎

Making use of Proposition
B.1
, we now state and prove a lower bound for

when
r\in(0,+\infty)
is small enough. Then, Theorem
3.5
will follow by scaling from Lemma

.

Lemma B.2
.

Let
p\in(1,+\infty)
,
s\in(0,1)
and
\delta:=\min\left\{2^{-n},2^{-n+2-p}\right\}
.

Then, for every
r\in\left(0,\delta\right)
,

L(B_{1},\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r+1})\geqslant\begin{cases}\delta\quad&\mbox{if}\quad s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{p}\right)\\
\delta\ln\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\quad&\mbox{if}\quad s=\frac{1}{p}\\
\delta r^{1-sp}\quad&\mbox{if}\quad s\in\left(\frac{1}{p},1\right)\end{cases}

Proof.

By (
B.1
), we have that, for every
x\in B_{1}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r+1}}\frac{dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\geqslant\frac{c_{n,p}}{s}(r+1-\left|x\right|)^{-sp}.

Hence, integrating both sides of the above inequality in
B_{1}
we obtain that

\begin{split}L(B_{1},\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1+r})&=\int_{B_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1+r}}\frac{dy\,dx}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\\
&\geqslant\frac{c_{n,p}}{s}\int_{B_{1}}(r+1-\left|x\right|)^{-sp}\,dx\\
&=\left|\partial B_{1}\right|\frac{c_{n,p}}{s}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{n-1}}{(r+1-t)^{sp}}\,dt.\end{split}

(B.7)

Now, we need to distinguish the following three cases:

sp\in(0,1),

(B.8)

sp=1

(B.9)

and

(B.10)

When (
B.8
) holds true, we have that

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}t^{n-1}(r+1-t)^{-sp}\,dt&\geqslant\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}t^{n-1}(r+1-t)^{-sp}\,dt\\
&\geqslant\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n-1}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}(r+1-t)^{-sp}\,dt\\
&=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n-1}\frac{1}{sp-1}(r+1-t)^{-sp+1}\bigg|_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}\\
&=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n-1}\frac{1}{1-sp}\left[\left(r+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{1-sp}-r^{1-sp}\right]\\
&\geqslant\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n-1}\frac{1}{1-sp}\left[1-\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{1-sp}\right]\\
&=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n-1}\frac{1-2^{-(1-sp)}}{1-sp}\\
&\geqslant\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n}.\end{split}

Instead, when (

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}t^{n-1}(r+1-t)^{-1}\,dt&\geqslant\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}t^{n-1}(r+1-t)^{-1}\,dt\\
&\geqslant\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n-1}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}(r+1-t)^{-1}\,dt\\
&=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n-1}\left|\ln(r+1-t)\right|\bigg|_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}\\
&=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n-1}\left[\left|\ln(r)\right|-\left|\ln\left(r+1/2\right)\right|\right]\\
&=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n}2\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{2r}\right)\\
&\geqslant\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n}\ln\left(\frac{1}{r}\right).\end{split}

Finally, in case (
B.10
),

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}t^{n-1}(r+1-t)^{-sp}\,dt&\geqslant\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}t^{n-1}(r+1-t)^{-sp}\,dt\\
&\geqslant\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n-1}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}(r+1-t)^{-sp}\,dt\\
&=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n-1}\frac{1}{sp-1}(r+1-t)^{-sp+1}\bigg|_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}\\
&=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n-1}\frac{1}{sp-1}\left[\frac{1}{r^{sp-1}}-\frac{1}{\left(r+1/2\right)^{sp-1}}\right]\\
&\geqslant\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n-1}\frac{1}{sp-1}\left[\frac{1}{r^{sp-1}}-\frac{1}{\left(2r\right)^{sp-1}}\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{2^{sp+n-2}}\left(\frac{2^{sp-1}-1}{sp-1}\right)r^{1-sp}\\
&\geqslant\frac{1}{2^{p+n-2}}r^{1-sp}.\end{split}

These three cases complete the proof of the desired result.
∎

\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|\leqslant\max\left\{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|,\left|w(x)-w(y)\right|\right\}.

From this and (
C.2
) it follows that, for every
x_{0}\in\overline{B}_{R}
,

\left[v\right]_{C^{\alpha}(B_{1}(x_{0}))}\leqslant\max\left\{\left[u\right]_{C^{\alpha}\left(B_{R+\frac{3}{2}}\right)},C_{s_{0},n,m,p,c_{1}}\right\}.

(C.9)

Hence, recalling (
C.9
), we obtain that, for every
x_{0}\in\overline{B}_{R}
,

\left[u-v\right]_{C^{\alpha}(B_{1}(x_{0}))}\leqslant\left[u\right]_{C^{\alpha}(B_{1}(x_{0}))}+\left[v\right]_{C^{\alpha}(B_{1}(x_{0}))}\leqslant 2\left[u\right]_{C^{\alpha}\left(B_{R+\frac{3}{2}}\right)}+C_{s_{0},n,m,p,c_{1}}.

(C.10)

Now, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}
we use the notation
x^{\prime}:=\frac{x}{\left|x\right|}R
. Also, in what follows we take
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{B}_{R}
. Then, employing (
C.8
), (
C.9
), (
C.10
) and recalling that the support of
u-v
is contained in
B_{R}
, we conclude that

\begin{split}\left[u-v\right]_{C^{\alpha}\left(B_{1}(x_{0})\right)}:=&\sup_{x,y\in B_{1}(x_{0})}\frac{\left|(u-v)(x)-(u-v)(y)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{\alpha}}\\
\leqslant&\sup_{x,y\in B_{R}\cap B_{1}(x_{0})}\frac{\left|(u-v)(x)-(u-v)(y)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{\alpha}}+\sup_{\overset{x\in B_{R}\cap B_{1}(x_{0})}{y\in B_{1}(x_{0})\setminus B_{R}}}\frac{\left|(u-v)(x)-(u-v)(y)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{\alpha}}\\
\leqslant&\sup_{x,y\in B_{R}\cap B_{1}(x_{0})}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{\alpha}}+\sup_{x,y\in B_{R}\cap B_{1}(x_{0})}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{\alpha}}\\
&+\sup_{\overset{x\in B_{R}\cap B_{1}(x_{0})}{y\in B_{1}(x_{0})\setminus B_{R}}}\frac{\left|(u-v)(x)-(u-v)(y)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{\alpha}}\\
\leqslant&\left[u\right]_{C^{\alpha}(B_{1}(x_{0}^{\prime}))}+\left[v\right]_{C^{\alpha}(B_{1}(x_{0}^{\prime}))}+\sup_{\overset{x\in B_{R}\cap B_{1}(x_{0})}{y\in B_{1}(x_{0})\setminus B_{R}}}\frac{\left|(u-v)(x)-(u-v)(y^{\prime})\right|}{\left|x-y^{\prime}\right|^{\alpha}}\\
\leqslant&\left[u\right]_{C^{\alpha}(B_{1}(x_{0}^{\prime}))}+\left[v\right]_{C^{\alpha}(B_{1}(x_{0}^{\prime}))}+\left[u-v\right]_{C^{\alpha}(B_{1}(x_{0}^{\prime}))}\\
\leqslant&4\left[u\right]_{C^{\alpha}\left(B_{R+\frac{3}{2}}\right)}+2C_{s_{0},n,m,p,c_{1}}.\qed\end{split}

Appendix D
Technical results

Here we collect some technical results that will be used in the proof of the
\Gamma
-convergence stated in Theorem
2.15
.

First we point out a useful variation of a celebrated result in
[
MR3586796
]
.

Proposition D.1
.

Let
p\in(1,+\infty)
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be bounded, open and smooth. Also, let
R\in(0,+\infty)
be such that
\Omega\subset B_{\frac{R}{3}}
.

Then, for every
v\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R})\cap L^{p}(B_{R})
,

\lim_{s\to 1}(1-s)\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx=\frac{K_{n,p}}{p}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx.

(D.1)

Proof.

For any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
small enough we define the sets
\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}
and
\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}
as in (
4.1
). Also, without loss of generality we assume that
0\in\Omega
.

According to Corollary 2 in
[
MR3586796
]
, we have that, for every
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
,

\lim_{s\to 1}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx=\frac{K_{n,p}}{p}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p}\,dx.

As a consequence, in order to prove (
D.1
) it is only left to show that

\lim_{s\to 1}(1-s)\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx=0.

(D.2)

To show (
D.2
), we first observe that

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\\
=&\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx+\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\\
=&\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\\
+&\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx+\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx.\end{split}

(D.3)

Now we define

\begin{split}A&:=\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx,\quad B:=\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx,\\
C&:=\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\quad\mbox{and}\quad D:=\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx.\end{split}

Then, if
R\in(0,+\infty)
is such that
\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\subset B_{\frac{R}{2}}
, we see that, for every
x\in\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}
,

\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}\geqslant\left(\left|y\right|-\left|x\right|\right)^{n+sp}\geqslant\left(\frac{\left|y\right|}{2}\right)^{n+sp}.

Hence, we obtain the existence of a constant
C_{n,p}
such that

\begin{split}A=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}dx\,dy+\int_{B_{R}\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}dx\,dy\\
\leqslant&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|y/2\right|^{n+sp}}dx\,dy+\int_{B_{R}\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{(2\epsilon)^{n+sp}}dx\,dy\\
\leqslant&2^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\frac{\left|v(x)\right|^{p}+\left|v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|y/2\right|^{n+sp}}dx\,dy+2^{p-1}\int_{B_{R}\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\frac{\left|v(x)\right|^{p}+\left|v(y)\right|^{p}}{(2\epsilon)^{n+sp}}dx\,dy\\
\leqslant&2^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-})}^{p}+\left|\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}\right|\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R})}^{p}}{\left|y/2\right|^{n+sp}}dx\,dy+2^{p-1}\int_{B_{R}\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}}\frac{\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-})}^{p}+\left|\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}\right|\left|v(y)\right|^{p}}{(2\epsilon)^{n+sp}}\,dy\\
\leqslant&C_{n,p}\left(\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-})}^{p}+\left|\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}\right|\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R})}^{p}\right)\frac{R^{-sp}}{sp}+C_{n,p}\epsilon^{-n-sp}\left(\left|B_{R}\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\right|\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-})}^{p}+\left|\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}\right|\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+})}^{p}\right).\end{split}

(D.4)

Similarly,

\begin{split}B\leqslant C_{n,p}\epsilon^{-n-sp}\left(\left|\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}\right|\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\setminus\Omega)}+\left|\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\setminus\Omega\right|\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-})}\right).\end{split}

(D.5)

Also, we can estimate
C
as

\begin{split}C\leqslant&C_{n,p}\left(\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-})}^{p}+\left|\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}\right|\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R})}^{p}\right)\frac{R^{-sp}}{sp}\\
&+C_{n,p}\epsilon^{-n-sp}\left(\left|B_{R}\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\right|\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-})}^{p}+\left|\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}\right|\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+})}^{p}\right).\end{split}

(D.6)

Finally, we estimate
D
. To do so, we observe that

\begin{split}D&=\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\\
&=\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{B_{1}(x)\cap\left(\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\setminus\Omega\right)}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx+\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{B_{1}^{c}(x)\cap\left(\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\setminus\Omega\right)}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx.\end{split}

(D.7)

Moreover, for a.e.
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
it holds that

\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|}\leqslant\int_{0}^{1}\left|\nabla v(y+t(x-y))\right|\,dt

Therefore,

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{B_{1}(x)\cap\left(\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\setminus\Omega\right)}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\\
=&\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{B_{1}(x)\cap\left(\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\setminus\Omega\right)}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{p}}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp-p}}\,dy\,dx\\
\leqslant&\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{B_{1}(x)\cap\left(\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\setminus\Omega\right)}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left|\nabla v(y+t(x-y))\right|\,dt\right)^{p}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp-p}}\,dy\,dx\\
\leqslant&\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{B_{1}(x)\cap\left(\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\setminus\Omega\right)}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\left|\nabla v(y+t(x-y))\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp-p}}\,dt\,dy\,dx\\
\leqslant&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{B_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\left|\nabla v(x+(1-t)z)\right|^{p}}{\left|z\right|^{n+sp-p}}\chi_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}(x)\,dt\,dz\,dx\\
=&\int_{B_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}}{\left|z\right|^{n+sp-p}}\chi_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}(x-(1-t)z)\,dx\,dt\,dz\\
=&\int_{B_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}}{\left|z\right|^{n+sp-p}}\chi_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}+(1-t)z}(x)\,dx\,dt\,dz\\
=&\int_{B_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}+(1-t)z)}^{p}}{\left|z\right|^{n+sp-p}}\,dt\,dz.\end{split}

(D.8)

Now, the function
f:\overline{B}_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

f(q):=\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}+q)}^{p}

is continuous and therefore there exists
\tilde{q}_{\epsilon}\in\overline{B}_{1}
such that, for every
t\in(0,1)
and
z\in B_{1}
,

\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}+(1-t)z)}^{p}\leqslant f(\tilde{q}_{\epsilon}).

Hence, it follows from (
D.8
) that

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{B_{1}(x)\cap\left(\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\setminus\Omega\right)}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\\
\leqslant&\int_{B_{1}}\frac{\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}+\tilde{q}_{\epsilon})}^{p}}{\left|z\right|^{n+sp-p}}\,dz\\
=&\frac{\left|\partial B_{1}\right|}{p-sp}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}+\tilde{q}_{\epsilon})}^{p}\end{split}

(D.9)

We also observe that

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{B_{1}^{c}(x)\cap\left(\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\setminus\Omega\right)}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\\
\leqslant&\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}}\int_{B_{1}^{c}(x)\cap\left(\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\setminus\Omega\right)}\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}\,dy\,dx\\
\leqslant&C_{n,p}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+})},\end{split}

for a suitable constant
C_{n,p}
.

Consequently, taking into account (
D.3
), (
D.4
), (
D.5
), (
D.6
), (
D.7
)
and (
D.9
), we infer that

\begin{split}(1-s)&\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\\
\leqslant(1-s)&C_{n,p}\left(\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-})}^{p}+\left|\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}\right|\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R})}^{p}\right)\frac{R^{-sp}}{sp}\\
+(1-s)&C_{n,p}\epsilon^{-n-sp}\left(\left|B_{R}\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\right|\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-})}^{p}+\left|\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}\right|\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+})}^{p}\right)\\
+(1-s)&C_{n,p}\epsilon^{-n-sp}\left(\left|\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}\right|\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\setminus\Omega)}+\left|\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\setminus\Omega\right|\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-})}\right)\\
+(1-s)&C_{n,p}\left(\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-})}^{p}+\left|\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}\right|\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R})}^{p}\right)\frac{R^{-sp}}{sp}\\
+(1-s)&C_{n,p}\epsilon^{-n-sp}\left(\left|B_{R}\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+}\right|\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-})}^{p}+\left|\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}\right|\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+})}^{p}\right)\\
+(1-s)&\frac{\left|\partial B_{1}\right|}{p(1-s)}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}+\tilde{q}_{\epsilon})}^{p}\\
+(1-s)&C_{n,p}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\epsilon}^{+})}.\end{split}

In particular, we deduce that

\limsup_{s\to 1}(1-s)\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\leqslant\frac{\left|\partial B_{1}\right|}{p}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}^{-}+\tilde{q}_{\epsilon})}^{p}.

Finally, we notice that the right-hand side is small with
\epsilon
, which concludes the proof of Proposition
D.1
.
∎

The following result has been proved for
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in
[
MR2765717
]
and for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in
[
MR4544090
]
. We provide here a proof for
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for every
p\in(1,+\infty)
.

Proposition D.2
.

Let
p\in(1,+\infty)
. Then, there exists
C_{n,p}\in(0,1)
such that, for every
v\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, every
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, every
open bounded set
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and every
\rho\in(0,\left|h\right|]
,

\left\|\tau_{h}v-v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leqslant C_{n,p}\frac{\left|h\right|^{p}}{\rho^{n+p}}\int_{B_{\rho}}\left\|\tau_{h}v-v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\left|h\right|}^{{}^{\prime}})}\,dy.

(D.10)

Proof.

Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{1}(B_{1})
be such that

\phi\geqslant 0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{B_{1}}\phi(x)\,dx=1.

For every
\rho\in(0,+\infty)
we define the following two functions

U_{\rho}(x):=\frac{1}{\rho^{n}}\int_{B_{\rho}}v(x+y)\phi\left(\frac{y}{\rho}\right)\,dy\quad\mbox{and}\quad V_{\rho}(x):=\frac{1}{\rho^{n}}\int_{B_{\rho}}(v(x)-v(x+y))\phi\left(\frac{y}{\rho}\right)\,dy.

We notice that

v(x)=U_{\rho}(x)+V_{\rho}(x).

We also have that

\begin{split}\left|\tau_{h}v(x)-v(x)\right|^{p}&=\left|U_{\rho}(x+h)+V_{\rho}(x+h)-U_{\rho}(x)-V_{\rho}(x)\right|^{p}\\
&\leqslant C_{p}\left(\left|U_{\rho}(x+h)-U_{\rho}(x)\right|^{p}+\left|V_{\rho}(x+h)\right|^{p}+\left|V_{\rho}(x)\right|^{p}\right),\end{split}

(D.11)

for a suitable constant
C_{p}>0
.

By the Jensen inequality, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\left|V_{\rho}(x)\right|^{p}\leqslant\frac{\left\|\phi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}}{\rho^{n}}\int_{B_{\rho}}\left|v(x)-v(x+y)\right|^{p}\,dy.

(D.12)

Moreover,

\begin{split}\nabla U_{\rho}(x)&=\frac{\nabla}{\rho^{n}}\int_{B_{\rho}}v(x+y)\phi\left(\frac{y}{\rho}\right)\,dy\\
&=\frac{\nabla}{\rho^{n}}\int_{B_{\rho}}v(z)\phi\left(\frac{z-x}{\rho}\right)\,dy\\
&=-\frac{1}{\rho^{n+1}}\int_{B_{\rho}}\left(v(z)-v(x)\right)\nabla\phi\left(\frac{z-x}{\rho}\right)\,dy\\
&=-\frac{1}{\rho^{n+1}}\int_{B_{\rho}}\left(v(x+y)-v(x)\right)\nabla\phi\left(\frac{y}{\rho}\right)\,dy.\end{split}

Accordingly, using the fundamental Theorem of Calculus and the Jensen inequality we obtain that

\begin{split}\left|U_{\rho}(x+h)-U_{\rho}(x)\right|^{p}&=\left|\int_{0}^{1}\nabla U_{\rho}(x+th)\cdot h\,dt\right|^{p}\\
&\leqslant\left|h\right|^{p}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\nabla U_{\rho}(x+th)\right|^{p}\,dt\\
&\leqslant\omega_{n}^{p}\frac{\left|h\right|^{p}}{\rho^{n+p}}\left\|\nabla\phi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}^{p}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{B_{\rho}}\left|\tau_{y}v(x+th)-v(x+th)\right|^{p}\,dy\,dt\end{split}

On this account, using (
D.11
) and (
D.12
), we obtain that

\begin{split}\left|\tau_{h}v(x)-v(x)\right|^{p}\leqslant C_{p}\bigg(&\omega_{n}^{p}\frac{\left|h\right|^{p}}{\rho^{n+p}}\left\|\nabla\phi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}^{p}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{B_{\rho}}\left|\tau_{y}v(x+th)-v(x+th)\right|^{p}\,dy\,dt\\
&+\frac{\left\|\phi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}}{\rho^{n}}\int_{B_{\rho}}\left|v(x)-v(x+y)\right|^{p}\,dy\\
&+\frac{\left\|\phi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}}{\rho^{n}}\int_{B_{\rho}}\left|v(x+h)-v(x+h+y)\right|^{p}\,dy\bigg).\end{split}

Integrating both sides in
\Omega^{\prime}
and taking
\rho\leqslant\left|h\right|
we conclude.
∎

As a consequence of this result and Lemma 2.5 in
[
MR4544090
]
we obtain the following result.

Proposition D.3
.

There exists a constant
\bar{C}_{n,p}\in(0,+\infty)
such that for every
v\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, every bounded open set
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, every
s\in(0,1)
and every
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\left\|\tau_{h}v-v\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\Omega^{\prime}\right)}^{p}\leqslant\bar{C}_{n,p}\left|h\right|^{sp}(1-s)\int_{B_{\left|h\right|}}\frac{\left\|\tau_{y}v-v\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\Omega_{\left|h\right|}^{\prime}\right)}^{p}}{\left|y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy.

Proof.

For every fixed
v\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we define the function
g_{v}:[0,\left|h\right|]\to\mathbb{R}
as

g_{v}(t):=\int_{\partial B_{t}}\left\|\tau_{y}v-v\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\Omega_{\left|h\right|}^{\prime}\right)}^{p}\,dH_{y}^{n-1}.

In particular, writing formula (
D.10
) in polar coordinates we obtain that

\left\|\tau_{h}v-v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime})}^{p}\leqslant C_{n,p}\frac{\left|h\right|^{p}}{\rho^{n+p}}\int_{0}^{\rho}g_{v}(t)\,dt.

(D.13)

We multiply both sides of (
D.13
) by
\rho^{p-sp-1}
and integrate in the interval
\left[0,\left|h\right|\right]
. In this way, we find that

\left\|\tau_{h}v-v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime})}^{p}\leqslant p(1-s)C_{n,p}\int_{0}^{\left|h\right|}\frac{\left|h\right|^{sp}}{\rho^{n+sp+1}}\int_{0}^{\rho}g_{v}(t)\,dt\,d\rho

Then, applying Lemma 2.5 in
[
MR4544090
]
, we arrive at

\left\|\tau_{h}v-v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime})}^{p}\leqslant p(1-s)C_{n,p}\left|h\right|^{sp}\int_{0}^{\left|h\right|}\frac{g_{v}(t)}{t^{n+sp}}\,dt.

Recalling the definition of
g_{v}
, the desired result plainly follows.
∎

In the proof of Theorem
2.15
we will also make use of the following binomial Theorem.

Proposition D.4

(Newton’s generalized binomial Theorem)
.

Let
a>b>0
and
p\in(1,+\infty)
. Then, we have that

(a-b)^{p}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\begin{pmatrix}p\\
k\end{pmatrix}a^{p-k}(-b)^{k}.

(D.14)

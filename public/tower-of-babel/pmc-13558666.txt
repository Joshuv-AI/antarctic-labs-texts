Theoretical investigation of the adsorption and sensing behavior of cephapirin on transition metal doped gallium nitride single-walled nanotubes
Authors: Endurance I Edward, Chukwunewubeze Ifenna Chukwudubem, Moses M Edim, Musa Runde, Destiny E Charlie, Francisca Bassey
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe extensive use of cephapirin (CPP) in the treatment of mastitis in dairy cattle has raised environmental concerns because residues excreted in an unchanged form can enter soil and water systems, where they promote the survival and spread of antibiotic-resistant bacteria. These resistant microorganisms may transfer resistance genes to other bacterial populations, creating environmental reservoirs of resistance that can ultimately compromise the effectiveness of antibiotics used in both veterinary and human medicine. These risks highlight the need for efficient materials capable of both detecting and removing cephapirin residues from the environment. This study investigates the adsorption and sensing behavior of CPP on transition metal (Cu, Ag, and Au)-doped gallium nitride nanotubes (GaNNTs) using Density Functional Theory (DFT) at the B3LYP-GD3(BJ)/def2-SVP level. The adsorption energies of CPP-Cu@GaNNT, CPP-Au@GaNNT, and CPP-Ag@GaNNT were − 2.313, -2.259, and − 2.177 eV, respectively, confirming chemisorption. Electronic structure, charge transfer, and topological analyses revealed that CPP acts as an electron donor and that adsorption is primarily governed by van der Waals and weak noncovalent interactions. Thermodynamic calculations confirmed that the adsorption process is spontaneous and exothermic for all systems. Sensor analysis showed that Au-doped GaNNT exhibits the strongest sensing response toward CPP. Molecular dynamics simulations performed on multiple initial adsorption configurations further confirmed the thermal stability of the adsorption systems. The collective results indicate that strong adsorption and effective sensing are governed by different electronic factors and are not simultaneously achieved by the same dopant, a finding that provides theoretical insight into the design of transition-metal-doped GaNNTs for the detection and removal of cephapirin residues from environmental systems.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-64681-8.Keywords: CPP, GaNNT, Adsorption, Sensor, DFT, Environmental RemediationSubject terms: Chemistry, Environmental sciences, Materials science, Nanoscience and technology

Introduction

The need to safeguard environmental and public health has intensified scrutiny of contaminants that were once considered solely beneficial, particularly antibiotics. They have been identified as emerging environmental contaminants due to their presence in water, soil and food, as well as their role in the development and dissemination of antimicrobial resistance1–3. Conventional wastewater treatment plants are generally not able to remove antibiotics completely from wastewater streams, and they are released continuously into environmental compartments through the extensive use of antibiotics in human medicine, livestock production and agriculture4,5. This means that the antibiotics may end up in natural systems, where they can disrupt the ecosystem and pose health risks to humans. Cephalosporins are among the most commonly used antibiotics in veterinary and agricultural use. Das et al. (2019) reported that these compounds are very persistent in wastewater and agricultural runoff and in environmental systems6. Cephapirin (CPP), a first-generation cephalosporin used in the treatment of mastitis in dairy cattle, is often excreted in an unchanged form and can subsequently enter water bodies and soils through agricultural discharge7. After environmental release, residual concentrations of CPP may exert selective pressure on microbial communities, favouring the survival and proliferation of antibiotic-resistant bacterial strains. Resistance to cephapirin can also be cross-resistant with other β-lactam antibiotics, thus making its presence in the environment not only a problem for the farm, but also for animal health, ecosystem stability, and human health8,9. These concerns highlight the importance of efficient methods of detecting and eliminating cephapirin residues in environmental systems.

Nanomaterials have been widely investigated for the adsorption and detection of pharmaceutical contaminants because of their high surface area and tunable electronic properties. Nguyen et al. (2023) reviewed the application of advanced multicomponent materials for antibiotic removal from aqueous environments10, while Lu et al. (2022) discussed the role of two-dimensional materials in both the detection and removal of antibiotic pollutants11. Among the various nanostructured materials explored for environmental applications, gallium nitride nanotubes (GaNNTs) have attracted tremendous interest for their potential use in the environment because of their large band gap, thermal stability, chemical resistance, and attractive electronic properties12. Recent studies have demonstrated that the adsorption performance of nanomaterials is strongly influenced by surface composition, defect structure, and electronic properties13,14. In addition, transition-metal doping has been identified as an effective strategy for enhancing adsorption strength and sensing response through modification of charge distribution and electronic states15–18. Studies involving pharmaceutical adsorption on functionalized nanotubes and other nanostructured materials have demonstrated that surface modification can alter electronic structure and induce charge transfer between the adsorbate and substrate19,20. Similar observations have been reported for drug adsorption on carbon nanotubes, nanosheets, and fullerene-based nanostructures, where adsorption behaviour is strongly influenced by electronic structure modulation and interaction-site specificity21–23. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that adsorption and sensing performance can be tailored through appropriate modification of nanomaterial surfaces. Although adsorption strength and sensing performance are often linked through charge-transfer interactions and electronic structure perturbations, the extent to which this relationship depends on the nature of the dopant and adsorbate has not been systematically established.

Despite these advances, the interaction of cephapirin with transition metal doped GaNNTs remains unexplored, and the influence of dopant identity on the adsorption and sensing behaviour of cephapirin has not been established. Understanding these effects is important for the rational design of materials capable of both monitoring and removing cephalosporin contaminants from environmental systems. Therefore, this study investigates the adsorption and sensing behaviour of CPP on Cu, Ag, and Au-doped GaNNTs using density functional theory at the B3LYP-GD3(BJ)/def2-SVP level. These transition metals were selected because of their distinct electronic characteristics and their reported ability to enhance charge transfer, adsorption strength, and sensing performance in nanostructured materials. Adsorption, thermodynamic, electronic structure, and topological analyses were performed to provide molecular-level insight into the interaction of CPP with doped GaNNT surfaces and to evaluate their suitability for environmental monitoring and remediation applications.

Computational details

Density Functional Theory (DFT) was used to study the adsorption and electronic characteristics of cephapirin on doped GaNNTs and the calculations were done using Grimme’s dispersion correction and Becke-Johnson damping (B3LYP-GD3(BJ)) in conjunction with the def2svp basis set. The B3LYP-GD3(BJ)/def2-SVP level of theory was selected because it provides an efficient and reliable description of electronic structure and noncovalent interactions in large adsorption systems. The D3(BJ) dispersion correction improves the treatment of van der Waals interactions, which are important in adsorption studies24,25. In addition, the def2-SVP basis set has been widely applied in adsorption and sensing investigations, providing reliable geometries and electronic properties at a moderate computational cost26. The suitability of this approach for nanomaterial-based adsorption and sensing studies has been demonstrated in previous reports27,28. to provide fast and reliable structural and electronic representation. Initial molecular structures were constructed and prepared using GaussView 6.0.1629, and all geometry optimizations and electronic structure calculations were carried out with the Gaussian 09 W software package30. The geometries of the doped GaNNTs as well as the individual interactions with CPP were optimized fully without any form of symmetry constraints so that the most favorable geometries were obtained. After optimization, series of analyses were done in detail to know the structural, electronic and thermodynamic behaviors of the systems. Frontier molecular orbital (FMO) analysis was conducted to examine the electronic properties of the systems and the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energies were determined31. Those values were then employed to calculate global quantum descriptors (GQDs) according to Koopmans’ theorem, which gives an idea on chemical reactivity, stability, and charge transfer properties. Molecular electrostatic potential (MESP) maps were calculated based on the electron density and visualized using GaussView 6.0.16 over the electron density isosurface. To assess the charge-transfer and electron delocalization in the systems, Natural Bond Orbital (NBO) analysis was conducted32. The GaussSum package was used to create the Density of States (DOS) plots based on the output files obtained from Gaussian calculations to analyze electronic density distribution around the Fermi level33. The Multiwfn program has been employed to provide Quantum Theory of Atoms in Molecules (QTAIM) and Non-Covalent Interaction (NCI) analyses as a way to characterize bonding interactions and weak intermolecular forces34. Visual Molecular Dynamics (VMD) was used to visualize NCI isosurfaces, and ChemCraft was used to visualize and analyze its structure35,36. The thermodynamic assessment was also carried out to ascertain the possibility and stability of adsorption. Meanwhile, the sensor mechanism analysis concentrated on charge transfer, back donation, dipole moment, work function, and Fermi energy level as an explanation of the sensing behavior of the doped nanotubes when it interacted with CPP37.

Molecular dynamics simulation

To further evaluate the dynamic stability of the adsorption systems under finite-temperature conditions, molecular dynamics simulations were carried out with the Forcite module of BIOVIA Materials Studio 202342. Three adsorption models of each of the transition metal doped GaNNT system have been built with one, two, and three CPP molecules, respectively. Various molecular arrangements of the CPP molecules were placed around the nanotube surface to study the effect of the increasing adsorbate loading and initial molecular configuration on the stability of the adsorption. The Nosé thermostat (Q = 0.01) was used to perform the simulations under the canonical (NVT) ensemble at T = 298 K. A time step of 1.0 fs was used for the integration of the equations of motion over 100,000 simulation steps which results in a total simulation time of 100 ps43. The interatomic interactions were described by the Universal Force Field (UFF) and the atomic charges calculated from the optimized DFT structures were kept during the simulations44. The electrostatic and van der Waals interactions were estimated using the atom-based summation method with a cubic spline truncation scheme set to 12.5 Å, 1.0 Å, and 2.0 Å for the cut-off distance, spline width, and buffer width, respectively. To assess thermal stability of the adsorption systems, the temperature profile along with the adsorption energy, rigid adsorption energy, deformation adsorption energy, and the total, potential, and kinetic energy components from the molecular dynamics simulation were analyzed.

Results and discussion

Structural analysis

To evaluate which doped GaNNT surface exhibited the strongest potential for adsorbing CPP, structural optimization and energy minimization were performed on CPP, Ag@GaNNT, Au@GaNNT, Cu@GaNNT, CPP-Ag@GaNNT, CPP-Au@GaNNT, and CPP-Cu@GaNNT. The structural formula of cephapirin (CPP) is C17H17N3O6S2, and its molecular weight is 423.5 g/mol. Structurally, CPP has two main ring-like components, that is, the beta-lactam ring with the dihydrothiazine ring45. Such rings are conjugated, giving up a rigid structure that make the molecule reactive and structurally stable. Functional groups like carbonyl, amide, sulfur-containing, and hydroxyl groups are also present in the molecule and are pivotal in the chemical behavior of the molecule. The bond lengths of CPP before and after adsorption are presented in Table S1 of the Supporting Information. Key bond label, including C29=C30, C15=O16, O34-H35, and N17-H18, remained close to their reference values46, with deviations of less than 0.003 Å across all systems, implying that adsorption causes only minor perturbations to the molecular structure of CPP. Before adsorption, the dopant-nitrogen bond lengths of Cu100-N24 (1.844 Å), Ag100-N23 (2.039 Å), and Au100-N28 (2.022 Å) confirmed stable incorporation of the dopant atoms into the GaNNT framework. After adsorption, these bond lengths changed by less than 0.01 Å, demonstrating that the structural integrity of the doped nanotubes was largely preserved. Minor elongation of neighboring Ga-N bonds was observed, reflecting local structural adjustment without significant distortion of the nanotube framework. The adsorption interaction distances followed the order Au100-C120 (3.322 Å) < Ag100-C120 (3.400 Å) < Cu100-C119 (3.678 Å). These distances exceed typical covalent bond lengths, suggesting that adsorption is predominantly governed by weak noncovalent interactions, which will be confirmed through the QTAIM and NCI analyses that will be discussed later. Among the three systems, the Au100 - C120 interaction showed the shortest adsorption bond length, suggesting slightly stronger proximity-based attraction. The structural analysis indicates that adsorption induces only moderate local structural perturbation while preserving the structural integrity of both CPP and the doped GaNNT surfaces (Figs. 1, 2 and 3).

Adsorption studies

Adsorption energy/BSSE

The adsorption energy was evaluated to determine the strength and stability of interaction between cephapirin (CPP) and the metal-doped GaNNT systems. The adsorption energies were corrected for basis set superposition error (BSSE) using the counterpoise method as proposed by Boys and Bernardi (1970), and all reported values in Table 1 correspond to the corrected energies47. The BSSE-corrected adsorption energies calculated using Eq. 2 are negative for all systems. The values fall within the range of -2.153 to -2.288 eV, which indicates strong interaction between CPP and the doped nanotube surfaces and an energetically favorable adsorption. A comparison of the adsorption energies shows that the Cu-doped GaNNT exhibits the most negative value (-2.288 eV), indicating the strongest interaction with CPP. The Au-doped system follows with an adsorption energy of -2.235 eV, while the Ag-doped system shows the least negative value (-2.153 eV), indicating comparatively weaker interaction. These differences reflect the influence of the dopant on the adsorption behavior and the ability of the surface to stabilize the adsorbate. The BSSE correction resulted in only minor changes to the adsorption energies, indicating that basis set overlap has a limited effect on the calculated interaction strength. This confirms the reliability of the computational method used. The relatively higher adsorption strength observed with the Cu-doped surface may be attributed to more effective orbital interactions or charge redistribution between the CPP molecule and the copper site. A similar trend was observed in a previous study by Mandeep et al. (2020), where Cu-doped nanomaterials demonstrated enhanced adsorption of pesticide molecules due to improved electronic coupling and surface reactivity48. These results support the notion that doping GaNNTs with transition metals can enhance their surface activity, with copper promoting a more stable adsorbed state for CPP.

Thermodynamics analysis

The thermodynamic analysis provides a more in-depth understanding of the viability, spontaneity, and stability of the energy of the adsorption process of cephapirin (CPP) on doped GaNNTs. Enthalpy change (∆Hads) and Gibbs free energy change (∆Gads) are useful measures of the stability levels of the adsorption process49. The relationships in Eqs. 3 and 4 were used to compute the adsorption enthalpy (∆Hads) and Gibbs free energy (∆Gads) based on the classical thermodynamic principles. These equations are used in explaining the changes in heat and free energy that take place as CPP binds to the doped GaNNT surfaces. A positive ∆H and ∆Gads value would indicate that the interaction would be endothermic, whereas a negative value would indicate an exothermic process. The nature of adsorption is more appropriately assessed from the magnitude of the change in the thermodynamic parameters50. Based on the thermodynamic data presented in Table 2, the CPP-Ag@GaNNT system exhibits the highest thermodynamic favorability among the studied systems. This is evident from its significantly more negative Gibbs free energy (ΔG0 = -8.18 eV), indicating that the adsorption process is highly spontaneous compared to the Au (-1.96 eV) and Cu-doped (-0.60 eV) systems. Similarly, the enthalpy change (ΔH0 = -8.12 eV) for the Ag-doped system is more negative than those of the Au (-2.58 eV) and Cu (-1.38 eV) systems, confirming that the adsorption process is strongly exothermic. This suggests that the interaction between CPP and the Ag-doped surface is thermodynamically more favorable. However, it is important to distinguish thermodynamic favorability from interaction strength. While the Ag-doped system shows the highest spontaneity, the Cu-doped system exhibits stronger adsorption interaction based on its more negative adsorption energy.

Electronic studies

HOMO-LUMO analysis

Frontier molecular orbital (FMO) analysis was employed to analyze the electronic response of the systems before and after adsorption of cephapirin (CPP). The energy gap (Eg), defined as the difference between the HOMO and LUMO energies, which provides insight into the reactivity and electronic transition behavior of the systems, was deduced using the formula in Eq. 451,52. The calculated values in Table 3 show that the doped nanotubes exhibit distinct electronic responses upon interaction with CPP. The Au-doped system shows a decrease in energy gap after adsorption, indicating increased electronic reactivity and suggesting that the interaction perturbs its electronic structure. In contrast, the Ag and Cu-doped systems exhibit slight increases in energy gap, indicating stabilization following adsorption. These variations suggest that adsorption induces moderate electronic changes without significantly disrupting the structure of the nanotube. The ionization potential (IP) and electron affinity (EA) were also examined. IP represents the energy required to remove an electron, while EA indicates the tendency to accept an electron. An increase in the values of these parameters after adsorption suggests a redistribution of electron density within the systems. The Ag-doped system showed IP and EA values of 6.204 eV and 4.039 eV, respectively, which are slightly higher than those of the corresponding pristine system (6.084 eV and 3.978 eV), indicating stronger interaction with CPP. Further insight is obtained from the global reactivity descriptors. The electrophilicity index (ꞷ) increases significantly for the Ag and Cu-doped systems, indicating enhanced electron-accepting ability and stronger interaction with CPP. Similarly, the chemical potential (µ) becomes more negative for these systems, suggesting an increased tendency to accept electron density. Although a slight increase in chemical hardness (η) is observed for Ag@GaNNT and Cu@GaNNT after adsorption, this change is minimal and reflects mild stabilization rather than a reduction in interaction capability. The Au-doped system showed comparatively lower electrophilicity, suggesting weaker electron-accepting behavior. The electronic changes observed in this study are consistent with previous reports on drug adsorption over doped nanostructures. Dalgic et al. (2023) demonstrated that incorporation of metal atoms into nanotube systems enhances interaction with pharmaceutical molecules through modification of electronic structure and promotion of charge transfer at the adsorption interface15. A similar behavior is observed in this study, where doping of GaNNT with transition metals leads to measurable changes in frontier orbital energies and reactivity descriptors. Among the studied systems, Ag@GaNNT and Cu@GaNNT show more favorable electronic characteristics for adsorption, as indicated by their higher electrophilicity and more negative chemical potential values, which promote charge transfer interaction with CPP. The Au-doped system, despite showing increased reactivity through a reduced energy gap, exhibits comparatively weaker electron-accepting behavior. To gain more understanding of the changes in the electronic structure of the systems after adsorption of CPP, the density of states (DOS) analysis was performed.

Natural Bond Orbital (NBO) Analysis

The natural bond orbital (NBO) analysis was carried out to examine the charge transfer interactions between cephapirin (CPP) and the metal-doped GaNNT systems. The analysis gave an in-depth insight on the delocalization of electrons which can be a major factor in the determination of the reactivity, stability, and sensing capacity of the systems. In all systems, interactions involving the π and σ orbitals of CPP with the metal sites contribute significantly to stabilization, indicating effective orbital overlap between the adsorbate and the surface. A notable feature as reported in Table 4 is that the stabilization energies associated with electron donation from CPP to the surface are higher than those corresponding to electron donation from the surface to CPP. This indicates that CPP acts predominantly as an electron donor, while the doped GaNNT serves as an electron acceptor suggesting that the dominant contribution arises from charge transfer originating from the adsorbate. This behavior is consistent with the findings of Kurban and Muz (2020), who reported that adsorption of fluorouracil on doped carbon nanotubes is driven by charge transfer from the drug molecule to the nanotube surface, with heteroatom-containing functional groups acting as primary donor sites53. Their results showed that doping enhances the electron-accepting ability of the nanotube, thereby strengthening the adsorption interaction. A similar mechanism is observed in the present study, where the doped GaNNT surface facilitates electron transfer from CPP, leading to stabilization of the adsorption system. It clarifies that metal doping enhances impartially the flow of electrons within the systems, and increases the general adsorption ability of the systems. Such outcomes are also in line with the past results in which metal doping has been demonstrated to enhance electron delocalization and efficiency in the pollutant adsorption on the surfaces of nanomaterials54. Among the studied systems, the Cu and Ag-doped GaNNT structures exhibit stronger donor-acceptor interactions, as indicated by higher stabilization energies and the dominant contribution of charge transfer from CPP to the surface. The Au-doped system shows similar interaction pathways but with a comparatively lower contribution to charge transfer, indicating weaker interaction. These results identify Cu@GaNNT and Ag@GaNNT as more favorable systems for adsorption.

Molecular electrostatic surface potential

Molecular electrostatic potential (MESP) analysis was done to examine the charge distribution within the CPP-doped GaNNT systems. The electrostatic potential maps were generated on the electron density surface using a color scale ranging from negative (red) to positive (blue) regions. The MESP maps of all systems as reprted in Fig. 4 are predominantly characterized by green regions, indicating that the electrostatic potential is largely neutral across both the adsorbate and the nanotube surface and the electrostatic potential ranges for the systems (± 6.583 eV for Ag, ± 6.639 eV for Au, and ± 6.762 eV for Cu) show only slight variation, confirming the equal charge distribution across all the systems. These observations suggest that electrostatic interactions are not the primary driving force of the adsorption of cephapirin on to the nanotube.

Density of states

The Density of States (DOS) analysis was carried out to elucidate the electronic structure of the doped GaNNT systems and the effect of CPP adsorption on the distribution of electronic states55. The DOS plots presented in Fig. 5 display the occupied and virtual orbitals as a function of energy, providing a comprehensive view of the electronic states across the energy spectrum. The energy gap values indicated in the DOS plots correspond to those obtained from the HOMO-LUMO (FMO) analysis. The results show that the CPP-Ag@GaNNT, CPP-Cu@GaNNT, and CPP-Au@GaNNT systems have energy gaps of 2.156 eV, 2.170 eV, and 2.331 eV, respectively, confirming that all systems retain their semiconducting nature after adsorption. Although the Ag-doped system exhibits the smallest energy gap, the change in energy gap upon adsorption provides a more meaningful description of the electronic response. The Ag and Cu-doped systems show slight increases in the energy gap after adsorption, indicating reduced electronic excitation and minimal perturbation of their electronic structures. In contrast, the Au-doped system exhibits a decrease in the energy gap, suggesting an increased ease of electron excitation upon adsorption. Furthermore, a closer examination of the frontier energy region (-5 to 0 eV) reveals that no new electronic states are introduced within the gap for any of the systems, indicating that the nanotubes’ intrinsic electronic nature is preserved and that the interaction remains predominantly noncovalent. Conclusively, the DOS analysis demonstrates that CPP adsorption induces varying degrees of electronic perturbation, with the Au-doped system exhibiting the most pronounced effect.

Study of weak interactions

Non-covalent Interactions (NCI) Analysis

The non-covalent interaction (NCI) analysis was employed to investigate the nature of interactions between CPP and the metal-doped GaNNT surfaces, as presented in Fig. 6. The analysis is based on the reduced density gradient formalism, which enables identification of noncovalent interaction regions through low-gradient electron density features, as originally proposed by Johnson et al. (2010)56. The NCI isosurfaces are characterized by three color domains: blue (strong attractive interactions), green (weak van der Waals interactions), and red (steric repulsion arising from electron cloud overlap). For all systems, red isosurfaces appear in proximity to the dopant sites, indicating localized steric repulsion due to close atomic contacts between CPP and the nanotube surface. A distinction among the systems is observed in the distribution and intensity of the green isosurfaces. For the CPP-Ag@GaNNT system, the interaction is dominated by diffuse green isosurfaces with minimal red regions, indicating weak and non-directional van der Waals interaction with negligible steric repulsion. The CPP-Au@GaNNT system exhibits more continuous green regions along with localized blue patches, indicating moderate van der Waals interaction with small regions of enhanced attraction. The limited presence of red isosurfaces suggests minimal steric hindrance, resulting in a more balanced and stable adsorption configuration. In contrast, the CPP-Cu@GaNNT system shows concentrated interaction regions accompanied by widespread red isosurfaces across the adsorption interface, indicating significant steric repulsion arising from close contact between CPP and the nanotube surface. This suggests that, although the interaction is stronger, it is associated with increased electron cloud overlap and structural strain. The NCI analysis reveals that adsorption is dominated by van der Waals interaction across all systems, with the Cu-doped system exhibiting stronger but sterically constrained interaction, the Au-doped system showing balanced interaction with localized attraction, and the Ag-doped system displaying weak and diffuse interaction.

Quantum Theory of Atoms in Molecules (QTAIM) Analysis

To discuss the bonding character of CPP adsorption on doped GaNNT surfaces better, the Quantum Theory of Atoms in Molecules (QTAIM) analysis was carried out57,58. The topological parameters considered are the electron density at bond critical points (ρ(r)), Laplacian of electron density (∇2 ρ(r)), kinetic energy density (G(r)), potential energy density (V(r)), the |V(r)|/G(r) ratio, total energy density (H(r)), eigenvalues (λ1, λ2, λ3), ellipticity (ε), and electron localization function (ELF) with the values listed in Table 5. The electron density values at the BCPs range from 0.0047 to 0.0429 a.u., indicating weak to moderate interactions. The Laplacian values (∇2 ρ(r)) are positive for all systems, confirming that the interactions are predominantly closed-shell in nature. However, the total energy density H(r) reveals both positive and negative values, indicating that while the interactions are largely noncovalent, there are localized regions with partial covalent character. In the CPP-Cu@GaNNT system, the Ga92-S11 (H(r) = -0.00371 a.u) and Ga85-O133 (H(r) = -0.0143 a.u) interactions exhibit negative H(r), indicating localized electron sharing and partial covalence. Other interactions remain positive, reflecting weaker noncovalent bonding. For the CPP-Au@GaNNT system, most interactions remain noncovalent, as reflected by positive H(r) values. However, Ga85-S111 (H(r) = -0.00374 a.u) and Ga92-O133 (H(r) = -0.000180 a.u) display negative H(r), indicating selective partial covalent bonding. In contrast, the CPP-Ag@GaNNT system is dominated by closed-shell interactions, with all ∇2ρ(r) values positive and H(r) largely positive. Only the Ga84-S111 (H(r) = -0.00387 a.u) shows partial covalence. Other than electron density and total energy metrics, the |V(r)|/G(r) ratio further reveals the dominance of pure closed-shell interaction across all the systems, with most values below 1. However, the BCPs with negative H(r) values had their |V(r)|/G(r) ratio above 1, confirming the partial covalent contribution at these sites. The eigenvalues (λ1 and λ2 negative, λ3 positive) across all systems suggest that the attractive forces dominate at the BCPs, leading to a more stable system. The Ellipticity (ε) provides information on the anisotropy of electron density at the bond critical points. It is commonly used to distinguish between σ and π-type interactions. Values close to zero (ε ≈0.00-0.10) correspond to symmetric σ-type interactions, while moderate values (ε ≈ 0.10–0.30) indicate slight π-character or polarization. Higher values (ε ≈ 0.30–1.00) are associated with increasing delocalization. In contrast, very large ellipticity values (ε > 1.00) indicate highly distorted electron density and are typically associated with weak or noncovalent interactions rather than classical covalent bonding59. The relatively high ellipticity value observed around Ag100 - C130 (3.05) of the Ag-doped system indicates significant anisotropy and electron density distortion. The Electron localization function (ELF) further elucidates bonding properties as the localized covalent structures are identified to have ELF > 0.5 and the delocalized ones ELF < 0.560. The combined analysis of ∇2 ρ(r), H(r), and |V(r)|/G(r) indicates that adsorption of CPP on doped GaNNT surfaces is governed primarily by closed-shell interactions with localized partial covalent contributions. The CPP-Cu@GaNNT in comparison to the other two doped systems exhibited stronger localized interaction.

Back Donation (∆EBD)

Back donation (∆EBD) measures the extent of the electron donation from the filled orbitals of the dopant into the CPP’s antibonding orbitals. The Au@GaNNT system has the largest back donation (-0.292 eV) as compared to Cu@GaNNT and Ag@GaNNT (-0.271 eV each). This suggests a stronger orbital overlap and interaction of the CPPs with the Au-doped surface. This is in agreement with the fact that Au is known to have the ability to facilitate donor-acceptor interaction63. The back donation was calculated using the global chemical hardness (η) based on conceptual DFT relations in Eq. 13.

Fermi Energy (EF)

The Fermi energy (EF) is the highest occupied electronic level at absolute zero (0 K) and is a measure of the ability of a molecule to be able to lose or gain an electron. It was estimated with the frontier molecular orbital energies following Koopmans’ theorem, as described in Eq. 14. The higher (less negative) EF value implies more electrons are available for interaction with the sensor thus the increased reactivity of the sensor. In the studied systems, the EF value of Au@GaNNT was the largest (least negative) (-4.726 eV) compared to Cu@GaNNT (-5.047 eV) and Ag-GaNNT (-5.121 eV). According to this order, the Au-doped nanotube appears to be readily available for electronic exchange with CPP than other systems. A similar finding has been reported by Wang et al. (2013), on Au-doped ZnO and SnO2 sensors, where increasing the Fermi levels resulted in increasing the gas sensitivity64.

Work Function (Φ)/Charge Transfer

The work function (Φ) is the energy required to remove an electron from the Fermi level to a position in the vacuum where it is beyond the influence of the atomic potential of a material. According to Kohn and Sham (1965), it is defined as the negative of the Fermi energy (Eqs. 15 and 16) and controls the ability of a surface to either emit or accept electrons and, hence, it is quite important in the adsorption, charge transfer process and sensor performance65. Au@GaNNT has the lowest work function (4.726 eV) which suggests that electrons are easily removed from the system and can be readily donated in adsorption. The values of the work function for Ag@GaNNT (5.121 eV) and Cu@GaNNT (5.047 eV) are higher, which means that they have lesser electron emission properties. The effect of Φ on the electron emission properties is also explained by the Richardson-Dushman equation which relates the thermionic current density (j) with Φ as given in Eq. 17. This equation indicates that the current density increases exponentially with a decrease in Φ. Hence, a surface with lower Φ makes it easier for the electrons to be emitted and also increases the charge transfer rate and hence enhances the electrical response of the sensor66. The change in work function in Eq. 18 is used to calculate the percentage change in the work function of the surface on the adsorption of the molecule. Φ1 is the work function of the (isolated) surface and Φ2 is the work function of the adsorbate-surface complex. A large ∆Φ implies that there is a large amount of charge redistribution and dipole formation between the surface and the adsorbate which enhances the surface sensitivity. This is also reflected in the charge transfer analysis. This was estimated using the natural population analysis (NPA) charges derived from the natural bond orbital (NBO) method developed by Weinhold and Landis (2001)67. The net charge transfer (Qt) was determined as the difference between the charge of the adsorbate in the adsorbed and isolated systems as given in Eq. 19. A positive Qt value shows electron transfer from CPP to the surface and a negative value shows electron transfer from the surface to CPP. The values are positive for all systems, showing that CPP is an electron donor. The values are in the order Au@GaNNT (0.298 e) > Ag@GaNNT (0.281 e) > Cu@GaNNT (0.268e), showing the greatest charge transfer occurs in the Au-doped system.

Dipole Moment (µ)

The dipole moment is a measure of the polarity of a system that can provide information on the charge distribution of the adsorbent-adsorbate system. In sensing, the change in the dipole moment upon molecule adsorption signals the redistribution of the charge between the molecule and nanotube surface that can significantly affect the system’s electronic structure68. Across the systems, the dipole moment of the CPP-Cu@GaNNT system is the highest (4.364 D), followed by the CPP-Ag@GaNNT (3.945 D) and CPP-Au@GaNNT (3.887 D) systems. This suggests that the Cu-doping results in the highest charge separation. The dipole moment values of the doped GaNNT systems considered here were extracted from the Gaussian output computed from the charge density of the optimized structures. The analysis of the sensor mechanism suggests that the Au-doped GaNNT system shows the most significant electronic response to CPP, due to the highest charge transfer, suitable Fermi energy level, highest back donation and lowest work function. These characteristics enable electron exchange and changes in the surface electronic properties. On the other hand, the Cu-doped system, although it exhibits a higher dipole moment and stronger adsorption, does not show a high value of charge transfer while the Ag-doped system shows intermediate behavior.

Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were conducted to evaluate the thermal stability of nine initial adsorption models constructed for the Ag, Au, and Cu-doped GaNNT systems. Three sets of models (CPP1, CPP2, and CPP3) were created for each dopant, with one, two, and three molecules of cephapirin, respectively, to investigate the effect of increasing the number of adsorbed CPP molecules. For each model, the CPP molecules were first positioned at various positions around the nanotube prior to the MD simulations. The initial adsorption configurations are presented in Figures S1 and S2 of the Supporting Information. The thermal stability of the adsorption systems was evaluated using the temperature variation and energy statistics obtained from the molecular dynamics simulations. For clarity, only the most stable adsorption model identified for each transition metal-doped GaNNT system is discussed. Adsorption energy analysis

The molecular dynamics simulation results of the adsorption, rigid adsorption, and deformation energies are shown in Table 8. In every GaNNT system that is considered, the first three adsorption configurations (CPP1, CPP2 and CPP3) have been considered and compared in order to determine which of the sampled configurations is energetically the most favorable one for the adsorption. The results reveal that the adsorption energies of CPP2-Ag@GaNNT, CPP3-Au@GaNNT, and CPP3-Cu@GaNNT are the most negative adsorption energy of the systems studied. Their corresponding adsorption energies were − 93.118 kcal/mol (-4.038 eV), -95.109 kcal/mol (-4.124 eV), and − 116.673 kcal/mol (-5.059 eV), respectively. Comparison of the adsorption energies indicates that Cu-doped GaNNT exhibits the strongest adsorption affinity toward cephapirin, followed by the Au and Ag-doped systems. The rigid adsorption energy represents the interaction energy before the adsorbate and adsorbent change their structures during adsorption, whereas the deformation energy represents the energy associated with the structural changes that occur as the interacting systems adapt to form a stable adsorption system69. The Ag-doped systems exhibit positive deformation energies, indicating that these structural changes require additional energy. In contrast, the Cu-doped systems consistently display negative deformation energies, suggesting that the structural changes contribute favorably to the stabilization of the adsorption systems. The Au-doped systems exhibit both positive and negative deformation energies, indicating that the energetic effect of these structural changes depends on the initial adsorption configuration. The adsorption trend obtained from the molecular dynamics simulations is in agreement with that obtained from the DFT calculations, further corroborating the higher adsorption affinity of the Cu-doped GaNNT toward cephapirin under finite-temperature conditions.

Conclusion

In this work, the adsorption and sensing of cephapirin (CPP) on and transition metal-doped GaNNTs were studied using the B3LYP-GD3(BJ)/def2-SVP basis set of DFT method. While all three doped systems exhibited spontaneous and energetically favorable adsorption, the results reveal that no single dopant performs best for all measured parameters every. Cu doping strengthened the adsorption interaction and promoted greater polarization of the adsorbed system, Ag doping enhanced thermodynamic favorability, whereas Au doping produced the strongest electronic response and sensing characteristics. These findings indicate that adsorption efficiency and sensing performance are governed by different underlying electronic factors and should be optimized according to the intended application. The molecular dynamics simulations further complemented the DFT investigation by evaluating nine initial adsorption models with increasing adsorbate loading, confirming the thermal stability of the adsorption systems under finite-temperature conditions and identifying the most energetically favorable adsorption configuration for each transition metal-doped GaNNT system. The adsorption trend obtained from the molecular dynamics simulations was consistent with that predicted by the DFT calculations, providing additional support for the computational findings. More importantly, the study reveals the potential of transition metal doped GaNNTs as tunable nanomaterials for addressing antibiotic contamination through both pollutant capture and detection. Although the present study combines DFT calculations and molecular dynamics simulations to investigate the adsorption and sensing behavior of cephapirin on transition metal-doped GaNNTs, the findings remain computational predictions and require experimental validation. Future studies should therefore focus on experimental verification to evaluate the performance of these materials under practical environmental conditions.

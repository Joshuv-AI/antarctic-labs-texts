Unleash Q Q ! Cohomology, Localization, and Interpolation in Parisi–Sourlas Supersymmetry
Bruno Le Floch, Gela Patashuri, Emilio Trevisani

Abstract
Parisi–Sourlas supersymmetric models are known to undergo dimensional reduction; that is, their physics is captured by models in two fewer dimensions. In this work, we revisit dimensional reduction, providing new arguments and reformulating existing proofs in terms of the cohomology of a supercharge Q Q . We obtain three main results. First, we show that the recently developed picture of dimensional reduction via decoupling of operators admits a natural explanation in terms of Q Q -exactness. Second, we provide a new proof of dimensional reduction using the supersymmetric localization argument. Third, we revisit Cardy’s “interpolation” proof—which is reminiscent of localization but does not rely on saddle-point methods—and show that it can be understood as a consequence of deforming the action by a Q Q -exact term. Finally, we show that, while existing nonperturbative proofs of dimensional reduction apply only to scalar Lagrangians, our formulation of Cardy’s argument extends to any theory with Parisi–Sourlas supersymmetry.

The decoupling argument is also very powerful, since it applies under very simple assumptions, which amount to finding, in superspace, a reduced manifold with a supertraceless orthogonal metric. Parisi–Sourlas supersymmetry and preliminaries

2.1
Parisi–Sourlas symmetries and superspace

The Parisi–Sourlas (PS) action, to be specified below, is invariant under
scalar
supersymmetry transformations. In this section, we review the basic properties of the superfield formalism that make this supersymmetry manifest and set the conventions used throughout the paper.

We consider a
(d|2)
–dimensional superspace with coordinates

y^{a}\equiv\bigl(x^{\mu},\theta,\bar{\theta}\bigr)\,,

(1)

where
x^{\mu}
(
\mu=1,\ldots,d
) are commuting coordinates on a

d
-dimensional Euclidean space, and
\theta
,
\bar{\theta}
are
Lorentz-scalar fermionic (aka. Grassmann) coordinates satisfying

\theta^{2}=0\,,\qquad\bar{\theta}^{2}=0\,,\qquad\theta\bar{\theta}=-\bar{\theta}\theta\,.

(2)

The super-index
a=(\mu,\theta,\bar{\theta})
collects both bosonic and fermionic coordinates.

The graded commutation properties of the superspace coordinates can be
encoded in terms of their Grassmann parity
[a]\in\{0,1\}
, defined by

[a]=\begin{cases}0,&a=\mu\,,\\
1,&a=\theta,\bar{\theta}\,,\end{cases}

(3)

so that the coordinates satisfy the graded commutation relation:

y^{a}y^{b}=(-1)^{[a][b]}y^{b}y^{a}\,.

(4)

To discuss supersymmetry transformations, it is convenient to equip the
superspace with a flat graded metric
g_{ab}
whose only non-vanishing
components are

g_{\mu\nu}=\delta_{\mu\nu}\,,\qquad g_{\theta\bar{\theta}}=-g_{\bar{\theta}\theta}=-1\,.

(5)

In the ordered basis
y^{a}=(x^{\mu},\theta,\bar{\theta})
this can be written as

g_{ab}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}I_{d}&0\\
0&J_{2}\end{pmatrix},\qquad I_{d}\equiv\operatorname{diag}(\overbrace{1,\ldots,1}^{d})\,,\qquad J_{2}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

(6)

The metric is graded-symmetric, i.e.
g_{ab}=(-1)^{[a][b]}g_{ba}
. The inverse metric
g^{ab}
is defined by
g^{ab}g_{ac}=\delta^{b}_{c}
and obeys
g^{ba}g_{ca}=\delta^{b}_{c}
; in particular,
g^{\mu\nu}=\delta^{\mu\nu}
and

g^{\bar{\theta}\theta}=-g^{\theta\bar{\theta}}=1
.
We use
g_{ab}
and
g^{ab}
to lower and raise super-indices:

y_{a}=g_{ab}y^{b}\,,\qquad y^{a}=g^{ba}y_{b}\,,

(7)

that is,
y_{a}=(x_{\mu},-\bar{\theta},\theta)
.
The corresponding superspace norm is

y^{2}\equiv y^{a}y_{a}=y^{a}g_{ab}y^{b}=x^{2}-2\theta\bar{\theta}\,,

(8)

where
x^{2}\equiv x^{\mu}x_{\mu}=\delta_{\mu\nu}x^{\mu}x^{\nu}
.

Superspace derivatives are defined as

\partial_{a}\equiv(\partial_{\mu},\partial_{\theta},\partial_{\bar{\theta}})\,,\qquad\partial_{\mu}\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}\,,\quad\partial_{\theta}\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\,,\quad\partial_{\bar{\theta}}\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\theta}}\,.

(9)

Using the superspace metric to raise indices,
\partial^{a}\equiv g^{ba}\partial_{b}=(\partial^{\mu},\partial_{{\bar{\theta}}},\partial_{\theta})
,
the super-Laplacian takes the form

\partial^{a}\partial_{a}=\partial_{x}^{2}+2\,\partial_{\bar{\theta}}\partial_{\theta}\,,

(10)

where
\partial_{x}^{2}\equiv\delta^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}
.
We normalize the integration over the Grassmann coordinates as follows

\int[d\bar{\theta}]\,[d\theta]=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d\bar{\theta}\,d\theta\,,

(11)

and we also use this notation for the fermionic measure in the path integral.

In superspace, PS SUSY can be simply understood as the set of transformations that preserve the superspace distance. We will be mostly interested in the following scalar PS SUSY action

S_{\text{SUSY}}=2\pi\int d^{d|2}y\left[-\frac{1}{2}\Phi\partial^{a}\partial_{a}\Phi+V(\Phi)\right]\,,

(12)

where
d^{d|2}y\equiv d^{d}x[d\bar{\theta}][d\theta]
and the scalar superfield
\Phi
can be expanded in terms of the components
\varphi,\psi,\bar{\psi}
,
\omega

as follows,

\Phi(x,\theta,\bar{\theta})=\varphi(x)+\theta\bar{\psi}(x)+\bar{\theta}\psi(x)+\theta\bar{\theta}\omega(x)\,.

(13)

Integrating out the anticommuting coordinates yields

S_{\text{SUSY}}=\int d^{d}x\Bigl(\partial^{\mu}\omega\partial_{\mu}\varphi-\omega^{2}+\omega V^{\prime}(\varphi)+\partial^{\mu}\psi\partial_{\mu}\bar{\psi}+\psi\bar{\psi}V^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\Bigr)\,.

(14)

Because of its superfield construction, the Parisi-Sourlas action is invariant under

ISO(d|2)
, that is, the super Poincaré symmetry group
\mathbb{R}^{d|2}\rtimes OSp(d|2)
built out of supertranslations and superrotations
which both preserve the norm (
8
).

Let us discuss the algebra of the generators.
To do so we shall introduce the graded commutator
[X,Y\}
, defined as the commutator
[X,Y\}=[X,Y]
if one or both of
X
and
Y
are Grassmann-even, and as the anticommutator
[X,Y\}=\{X,Y\}
if both
X
and
Y
are Grassmann-odd.

The supertranslation generators simply graded-commute, namely
[P^{a},P^{b}\}=0
.
Superrotation generators satisfy the orthosymplectic algebra of
OSp(d|2)
,

\!\![M^{ab},M^{cd}\}=-g^{cb}M^{ad}+(-1)^{[a][b]}g^{ca}M^{bd}+(-1)^{[c][d]}g^{db}M^{ac}-(-1)^{[a][b]+[c][d]}g^{da}M^{bc}\,.

(15)

Finally, supertranslations transform as vectors under superrotations; therefore, their algebra takes the form

[M^{ab},P^{c}\}=-g^{cb}P^{a}+(-1)^{[a][b]}g^{ca}P^{b}\,.

(16)

It is useful to introduce the Killing vectors associated with the generators,

p^{a}=\partial^{a}\,,\qquad m^{ab}=y^{a}\partial^{b}-(-1)^{[a][b]}y^{b}\partial^{a}\,,

(17)

which can be easily shown to satisfy the opposite commutation relations as
P^{a}
and
M^{ab}
.
For more details on the charges and their algebra, we refer to appendix
A
.

2.3
Localization: a general review

To set the notation and make the discussion self-contained, let us briefly review the basic ideas of localization and cohomology. Supersymmetric localization is a powerful method that enables exact, nonperturbative evaluations of path integrals in supersymmetric theories.
The first step in localization is to consider a fermionic supercharge
Q
. This quantity is often chosen to be nilpotent, satisfying
Q^{2}=0
; in what follows, however, we relax this condition.
The procedure for localizing the path integral consists of deforming the action by a
Q
-exact term
S_{\text{loc}}\equiv Q\mathcal{V}
multiplied by a generic parameter
t
:

Z_{t}\equiv\int_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{D}\Phi e^{-S-tQ\mathcal{V}}\,.

(23)

If the charge
Q
is not nilpotent, as in our case, it is also necessary to ensure that
Q\mathcal{V}
is closed:

Q^{2}\mathcal{V}=0
. Then it is easy to check that the path integral is invariant under such a deformation:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}Z_{t}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{D}\Phi(Q\mathcal{V})e^{-S-tQ\mathcal{V}}=-\int_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{D}\Phi Q(\mathcal{V}e^{-S-tQ\mathcal{V}})=0\,,

(24)

where we used the fact that the action is invariant under the supersymmetry, i.e.,
QS=0
. In addition, we assumed that the
integration measure is
Q
-invariant and there are no boundary terms, so that the integral of a total derivative in field space vanishes.

It is further convenient to choose a deformation
Q\mathcal{V}
whose bosonic part is positive semi-definite. In this way, we can evaluate the partition function by taking the limit
t\to\infty
:

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\to\infty\end{subarray}}Z_{t}=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\to\infty\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{D}\Phi e^{-S-tQ\mathcal{V}}\,,

(25)

where the integrand is dominated by the saddle points
\mathcal{F}_{S}
of
Q\mathcal{V}
. Crucially, since
Z_{0}=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\to\infty\end{subarray}}Z_{t}
, the saddle point “approximation” here is exact.

To evaluate the path integral (
25
), one expands the fields
\Phi
around the localization locus field configurations
\Phi_{S}\in\mathcal{F}_{S}
:

\Phi=\Phi_{S}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\delta\Phi\,,

(26)

where
\delta\Phi
parametrizes directions transverse to the localization locus. In the limit
t\to\infty
, the inverse of
t
behaves like an effective Planck constant
\hbar_{\text{eff}}=1/t
, and the semiclassical loop expansion in
\hbar_{\text{eff}}
reduces to

S[\Phi]=S[\Phi_{S}]+S_{\text{loc}}^{(2)}[\delta\Phi]\,,

(27)

where
S_{\text{loc}}^{(2)}=\frac{1}{2}\int S^{\prime\prime}_{\text{loc}}[\Phi]|_{\Phi=\Phi_{S}}\delta\Phi^{2}
is the quadratic action for fluctuations around the localization locus.
1
1

1

In practice, since we will choose localization terms
S_{\text{loc}}
quadratic in the field
\Phi
, the form of
S_{\text{loc}}^{(2)}
will always coincide with
S_{\text{loc}}
.

This is a one-loop exact result since the higher terms are weighted by powers of
1/t
and vanish in the
t\to\infty
limit. Thus, the partition function reduces to (and is exactly equal to)

Z=\int_{\mathcal{F}_{S}}\mathcal{D}\Phi_{S}e^{-S[\Phi_{S}]}Z_{\text{1-loop}}[\Phi_{S}]\,,

(28)

where
Z_{\text{1-loop}}
is the path integral of
\text{exp}({-S_{\text{loc}}^{(2)}})
, which is Gaussian since it is quadratic in the fields
\delta\Phi
. This integral yields a determinant as the result.

The result of the localized path integral (
28
) does not depend on the choice of deformation term
S_{\text{loc}}
. We will use this flexibility and pick the term that makes the calculations easiest.

The localization argument can be simply generalized to correlation functions as long as the operator insertions are
Q
-closed.
This can be seen, for example, by deforming the action by a source term
S_{J}=S+\int d^{d}x\,J\mathcal{O}
(where
J
is the source) and noticing that as long as
S_{J}
is
Q
-closed, one can repeat the same steps above by simply substituting
S\to S_{J}
.
We therefore find that all correlators of
Q
-closed operators can be localized.
Moreover it is easy to see that, within correlators of
Q
-closed insertions, all observables that are both
Q
-exact and
Q
-closed vanish, namely

\langle{Q(\dots)}\mathcal{O}\rangle=\frac{1}{Z}\int_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{D}\Phi Q(\dots)\mathcal{O}e^{-S[\Phi]}=\frac{1}{Z}\int_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{D}\Phi Q(\dots\mathcal{O}e^{-S[\Phi]})=0\,.

(29)

As a result, correlators of
Q
-closed observables
\mathcal{O}_{i}
only depend on their class in equivariant cohomology, namely on the operators modulo
Q
-exact contributions that preserve
Q
-closedness. Explicitly, for operators
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{i}
such that
Q^{2}\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{i}=0
,

\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}\dots\mathcal{O}_{n}\rangle=\langle(\mathcal{O}_{1}+Q\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{1})\dots(\mathcal{O}_{n}+Q\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{n})\rangle\,.

(30)

This defines correlators of operators in the equivariant cohomology of
Q
.

2.4
Unleashing
Q
in PS models

Above, we introduced the idea of localization and (equivariant) cohomology, but we were agnostic about the specific supersymmetry and the explicit form of
Q
.
In the rest of the paper, we will focus on a specific fermionic charge
Q
given by the following combination of two PS superrotations:

Q\equiv M^{{\texttt{x}}\theta}+M^{{\texttt{y}}\bar{\theta}}\,,

(31)

where
{\texttt{x}}=x^{d-1}
and
{\texttt{y}}=x^{d}
following the conventions of (
20
).
In particular, the action of
Q
on a scalar superfield is given by the Killing vector
q\equiv m^{x\theta}+m^{y\bar{\theta}}
:

Q\Phi(y)=q\Phi(y)=({\texttt{x}}\partial_{\bar{\theta}}-\theta\partial_{{\texttt{x}}}-{\texttt{y}}\partial_{\theta}-\bar{\theta}\partial_{{\texttt{y}}})\Phi(y)\,.

(32)

We will study the (equivariant) cohomology with respect to this charge, and we will also use it for the localization arguments.
The supercharge
Q
is not nilpotent, since it squares to

Q^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(M^{\theta\theta}+M^{\bar{\theta}\bar{\theta}}\bigr)+M^{{\texttt{x}}{\texttt{y}}}\,,

(33)

as detailed in appendix
A
.
For convenience, we also write the action of
Q
and
Q^{2}
on the superfield components,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{llll}Q\varphi={\texttt{x}}\psi-{\texttt{y}}\bar{\psi}\,,&Q\bar{\psi}=\partial_{\texttt{x}}\varphi+{\texttt{x}}\omega\,,&Q\psi=\partial_{\texttt{y}}\varphi+{\texttt{y}}\omega\,,&Q\omega=\partial_{\texttt{y}}\bar{\psi}-\partial_{\texttt{x}}\psi\,,\\
Q^{2}\varphi=M^{{\texttt{x}}{\texttt{y}}}\varphi\,,&Q^{2}\bar{\psi}=\psi+M^{{\texttt{x}}{\texttt{y}}}\bar{\psi}\,,&Q^{2}\psi=-\bar{\psi}+M^{{\texttt{x}}{\texttt{y}}}\psi\,,&Q^{2}\omega=M^{{\texttt{x}}{\texttt{y}}}\omega\,\,.\end{array}

(34)

While
Q^{2}\neq 0
, we are interested in studying dimensional reduction to
\mathbb{R}^{d-2}
, where
Q
is effectively nilpotent:

Q^{2}\big|_{d-2}=0\,.

(35)

Consequently, we conclude that the dimensionally reduced operators can be naturally classified according to the cohomology of
Q
.

In the following section, we provide a cohomological argument for dimensional reduction. The idea is to show that the cohomology of
Q
actually defines the operators in the lower-dimensional theory. The
Q
-exact operators will automatically decouple and we shall make contact with a geometric decoupling argument introduced in
27
.
Then, we will provide three versions of localization arguments. Two of them follow the idea above, but with different localization terms
S_{\text{loc}}
.
In particular, the argument in
section 4
is a new proof obtained by following the modern conventions of the localization literature
43
. The argument in
subsection 4.2
is a review of Zaboronsky’s proof in
52
, which uses a non-standard term
S_{\text{loc}}
. We shall explain that this choice gives rise to some subtleties.

The third argument in
section 5
reviews a different logic presented by Cardy in
12
. The deformed path integral
Z_{t}
chosen by Cardy is not computed by taking a saddle point limit for
t\to\infty
, but instead
Z_{t}
interpolates between the original action and the reduced one by appropriately tuning the parameter
t
. In our review, we aim at rephrasing the argument in terms of the cohomology of
Q
, and we will further explain how to generalize it to account for other models possessing the same supersymmetry.

3
Cohomological argument

In
27
, a very simple argument for dimensional reduction was
presented. The first step consists in restricting the theory to

\mathbb{R}^{d-2}
, which is sometimes referred to as introducing a
trivial defect. Under this restriction, the symmetry is broken as

ISO(d|2)\;\longrightarrow\;ISO(d-2)\times OSp(2|2)\,.

(36)

The trivial defect preserves Poincaré symmetry along the defect, as
well as the
OSp(2|2)
rotations in the directions transverse to it.
From the point of view of the defect theory, the latter are realized as
a global symmetry. As explained in
subsection 2.2
, dimensional reduction for
spinning representations comes with a projection onto
OSp(2|2)

singlets. In the following we thus focus only on this set of operators.

In the kinematics with broken symmetry, the metric
g_{d|2}
on the
superspace
\mathbb{R}^{d|2}
similarly decomposes into two parts that
are preserved by the broken symmetry group:

g_{d|2}=g_{d-2}+g_{2|2}\,,

(37)

where
g_{d-2}
is the “parallel” metric on
\mathbb{R}^{d-2}
, while

g_{2|2}
is the “transverse” metric on
\mathbb{R}^{2|2}
. More
explicitly,
g_{d-2}^{ab}=\delta^{ab}
for

a,b=1,\ldots,d-2
and vanishes otherwise, while
g_{2|2}^{ab}

vanishes for
a,b=1,\ldots,d-2
and coincides with
g_{d|2}^{ab}
on
the remaining components. Both metrics are therefore available as
building blocks for operators and correlation functions.

The central claim of
27
is that, within the space of

OSp(2|2)
singlets,

•

all operators constructed using
g_{2|2}
decouple;

•

operators constructed solely using
g_{d-2}
map to the
dimensionally reduced operators.

(38)

This result follows from a simple but powerful argument. Correlation
functions of
OSp(2|2)
singlets must be built from
OSp(2|2)
-invariant
building blocks, yet no non-vanishing invariant can be constructed
using the transverse metric
g_{2|2}
. Indeed, correlators only involve
operators inserted at points
x_{\parallel}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-2}
, for which

g^{ab}_{2|2}x_{\parallel\,b}=0
, and moreover the supertrace of
g_{2|2}

vanishes. As a result, contracting
g_{2|2}
with any available object
gives zero, and no invariant can be formed.

While this argument is simple and compelling, the statement
(
38
) does not, by itself, identify a precise
mathematical structure underlying the set of decoupled operators. In
particular, it does not explain why operators built out of
g_{2|2}

should be special. Here we show that the statement (
38
) admits a
natural and elegant reformulation in terms of the cohomology of the
supercharge
Q
. More precisely, within the space of
OSp(2|2)

singlets,

•

Q
-exact operators decouple;

•

the
Q
-cohomology defines the dimensionally
reduced operators.

(39)

This reformulation is particularly appealing, since the decoupling of

Q
-exact operators is automatic, and the cohomology of a supercharge
provides a well-defined and intrinsic characterization of the relevant
operator space.

Notice that all
OSp(2|2)
singlets are automatically
Q
-closed, since

Q
is constructed from generators of
OSp(2|2)
. Therefore, in order
to establish the equivalence between (
38
) and
(
39
), it suffices to show that, within the space
of
OSp(2|2)
singlets,

\boxed{\emph{\phantom{\bigg|}operatorsbuiltoutofg_{2|2}\ \underset{\text{one to one}}{\longleftrightarrow}\ Q-exactoperators\phantom{\bigg|}}}\,.

(40)

In the remainder of this section, we provide an argument in support of
(
40
). This establishes that the decoupling
argument of
27
, summarized in
(
38
), can be equivalently recast in the
cohomological language of (
39
), as schematically
illustrated in Fig.
4
. We will see in
subsection 3.2
that this cohomological viewpoint has several interesting consequences.

Q
-closed

Q
-exact

(built with
g_{2|2}
)

operators of
\text{QFT}_{d-2}
decouple

To begin, we should explain how the

A compact way to characterize all

\lim_{y_{i}\to y}\bigl(P_{\parallel}^{\{\mu\}}P_{\perp}\,\Phi(y_{1})\cdots\Phi(y_{n})\bigr)\big|_{d-2}\,,

(42)

where the operators
\Phi(y_{i})
are initially taken at distinct points

y_{i}
, and the coincident limit
y_{i}\to y

A basis of operators is provided by the following differential operators:

2

\displaystyle P_{\parallel}^{\{\mu\}}

\displaystyle\equiv

P_{\perp}

Q

The operator

\bigl(P_{\perp}\,\Phi(y_{1})\cdots\Phi(y_{n})\bigr)\big|_{d-2}=Q[\ldots]\,,

where
P^{\prime}_{\perp}

P_{\perp}
by removing a single

(\partial_{\perp i}\cdot\partial_{\perp j})

(\partial_{\perp i}\cdot\partial_{\perp j})\,P^{\prime}_{\perp}\equiv P_{\perp}

With this notation, one finds

\displaystyle\boxed{(\partial_{\perp i}\cdot\partial_{\perp j})\,F\big|_{d-2}=Q\Bigl[(\partial_{\theta_{i}}\partial_{{\texttt{x}}_{j}}+\partial_{\bar{\theta}_{j}}\partial_{{\texttt{y}}_{i}})F\bigr|_{d-2}\Bigr]}\,,

3.2
Cohomology and consequences of the decoupling

Above, we showed that all
OSp(2|2)
-singlet operators can be written in
the form (
42
), and that any operator with a non-trivial

P_{\perp}
is
Q
-exact. It is well known that the correlator of a

Q
-exact operator with any number of
Q
-closed operators vanishes:

\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}\,\mathcal{O}_{1}\cdots\mathcal{O}_{n}\rangle=0\,,

(51)

where all operators are
OSp(2|2)
singlets, the operators

\mathcal{O}_{i}
are
Q
-closed, and
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}
is

Q
-exact. The cohomological argument, therefore, explains why all
operators built out of the orthogonal metric
g_{2|2}
decouple, in
agreement with the results of
27
.

We can thus characterize the equivalence classes of operators in

Q
-cohomology by restricting to the operators (
42
) with
trivial
P_{\perp}
. These operators are in one-to-one correspondence
with the operators of the dimensionally reduced theory, which are
spanned by

\lim_{x_{i}\to x}P_{\parallel}^{\{\mu\}}\,\varphi(x_{1\parallel})\cdots\varphi(x_{n\parallel})\,.

(52)

This establishes that the
Q
-cohomology precisely reproduces the set of
operators of the reduced theory.

Let us now turn to the stress tensor. Rephrasing the argument
of
27
, we show that the existence of a conserved superstress
tensor implies, in cohomology, the existence of the stress tensor of the
dimensionally reduced theory. Consider a superstress tensor

\mathcal{T}_{ab}
satisfying the conservation equation

\partial^{a}\mathcal{T}_{ab}=0
. Splitting the indices into parallel
and orthogonal components, one finds

\partial^{a}\mathcal{T}_{a\nu}(x_{\parallel})=g_{d-2}^{ij}\,\partial_{i}\mathcal{T}_{j\nu}(x_{\parallel})+g_{2|2}^{ab}\,\partial_{a}\mathcal{T}_{b\nu}(x_{\parallel})\,.

(53)

Here, the second term is
Q
-exact by the arguments presented above, and therefore

0=\partial^{a}\mathcal{T}_{a\nu}(x_{\parallel})=\partial^{i}\mathcal{T}_{i\nu}(x_{\parallel})+Q[\ldots]\,,

(54)

with
i
running over the parallel directions. Since the operator
\mathcal{T}
is inserted at
x_{\parallel}
, only
the lowest component of the superfield contributes, which has scaling
dimension
d-2

27
. This implies the existence, in
Q
-cohomology, of a
spin-two operator of dimension
d-2
that is conserved. This operator
can be identified with the stress tensor of the dimensionally reduced
theory. Similar remarks apply to other conserved currents.

In the case of CFTs, the
superstress tensor is required to be supertraceless. Using

\mathcal{T}_{ab}g_{2|2}^{ab}+\mathcal{T}_{ij}g_{d-2}^{ij}=0\,,

(55)

and noting that the
2|2
contribution decouples in cohomology, it
follows that the reduced stress tensor is traceless. We thus conclude
that the
Q
-cohomology contains the conserved and traceless stress
tensor of the dimensionally reduced theory.
4
4

4

This construction
can be checked explicitly in free theory using the superstress tensor

\mathcal{T}_{ab}=\left(\partial_{(a}\Phi\right)\left(\partial_{b]}\Phi\right)-\frac{d-4}{d-2}\,\Phi\,\partial_{a}\partial_{b}\Phi-\frac{g_{ba}}{d-2}\,\left(\partial^{c}\Phi\right)\left(\partial_{c}\Phi\right),

(56)

where
(ab]
denotes graded symmetrization.
Furthermore, one can also adapt the argument
of
27
to show that the operator
product expansion of
OSp(2|2)
singlets descends consistently to the
reduced OPE in
Q
-cohomology.

The use of supercharge cohomology to define effective theories on
lower-dimensional manifolds is not new. In four-dimensional

\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal field theories, it was shown in

4
that the cohomology of a particular supercharge can
be used to define a two-dimensional chiral CFT. This
construction is closely reminiscent of Parisi--Sourlas dimensional
reduction.
5
5

5

While reminiscent, the two setups differ in
important ways. In the case of
4
, the
higher-dimensional theory is unitary, whereas the lower-dimensional one
is not, which is precisely the opposite of the situation considered
here. Moreover, our construction applies in arbitrary dimensions, and
the resulting dimensionally reduced theory can be significantly richer,
as its operator content is not limited to conserved currents, in
contrast to chiral theories.

Similar cohomological reductions have subsequently been identified in
theories in various dimensions and with different amounts of
supersymmetry, see e.g.
6
;
15
. From this
point of view, the reformulation presented here places these apparently
distinct constructions on a common footing, emphasizing the role of
supercharge cohomology as a unifying principle.

4
Localization argument

In this section, we present a standard localization argument for
dimensional reduction in PS supersymmetric QFTs, which, to the best of
our knowledge, has not been discussed explicitly in the existing
literature. For simplicity, we focus on the standard scalar PS action (
14
).
Nevertheless, similar arguments are expected to extend to more generic QFTs possessing
PS supersymmetry.
6
6

6

In this respect, Cardy’s interpolation
argument, discussed in the next section, is more general, as it does not
rely on the explicit form of the action.

Following the general localization logic reviewed in

subsection 2.3
, we deform the path integral by a
Q
-exact
term,
S_{\text{loc}}=Q\mathcal{V}
.
The deformed partition function then reads

Z_{\text{SUSY},t}=\int\mathcal{D}\varphi\,\mathcal{D}\omega\,[\mathcal{D}\bar{\psi}]\,[\mathcal{D}\psi]\,\,e^{-S_{\text{SUSY}}[\varphi,\bar{\psi},\psi,\omega]-t\,Q\mathcal{V}},

(57)

where
S_{\text{SUSY}}
is given in (
14
) and
t
is a real
deformation parameter. The functional
\mathcal{V}
is chosen as

\mathcal{V}=\int d^{d}x\,\bigl(\psi\,Q\psi+\bar{\psi}\,Q\bar{\psi}\bigr)\,,

(58)

in close analogy with standard localization constructions in SUSY QFTs,
see e.g.
43
and references therein.
This choice of
\mathcal{V}
satisfies the standard requirements for
localization. First, one can verify that
Q^{2}\mathcal{V}=0
(see
appendix
C
). Second, the bosonic part of the deformation

Q\mathcal{V}
is positive definite. Indeed, one finds

\displaystyle S_{\text{loc}}

\displaystyle=\int d^{d}x\,\bigl((Q\psi)^{2}+(Q\bar{\psi})^{2}-\psi\,Q^{2}\psi-\bar{\psi}\,Q^{2}\bar{\psi}\bigr)

(59)

\displaystyle=\int d^{d}x\,\Bigl(\underbrace{(\partial_{{\texttt{x}}}\varphi+{\texttt{x}}\omega)^{2}+(\partial_{{\texttt{y}}}\varphi+{\texttt{y}}\omega)^{2}}_{\text{bosonic part }\geq 0}+\underbrace{\psi(\bar{\psi}-m^{{\texttt{x}}{\texttt{y}}}\psi)-\bar{\psi}(\psi+m^{{\texttt{x}}{\texttt{y}}}\bar{\psi})}_{\text{fermionic part}}\Bigr)\,,

(60)

where the second line follows directly from the supersymmetry transformations (
34
).
As discussed in
subsection 2.3
, these properties ensure that
the deformed partition function (
57
) is independent of
the parameter
t
, and that the limit
t\to\infty
is well defined.

In the limit
t\to\infty
, the path integral localizes onto the locus
of field configurations for which
S_{\text{loc}}
vanishes. We denote
this set of saddle-point configurations of
S_{\text{loc}}
by

\mathcal{F}_{S}
. Within the standard SUSY localization framework, one
typically considers

\mathcal{F}_{S}=\left\{\Phi\;\middle|\;\text{fermions}=0,\;Q(\text{fermions})=0\right\}.

(61)

Requiring
Q(\text{fermions})=0
guarantees that the bosonic part of

S_{\text{loc}}
vanishes, as is evident from (
59
). We also impose the standard
condition
\text{fermions}=0
, which ensures that the saddle point is
purely bosonic and can be interpreted as a classical field
configuration.
7
7

7

In the next subsection we will explain that,
strictly speaking, this condition should be relaxed: one should allow
for a non-trivial fermionic profile in order to correctly account for
certain zero modes. We will nevertheless show that this refinement does
not affect the final result, as the corresponding fermionic modes
ultimately decouple. For the moment, we therefore adopt the standard
choice
\text{fermions}=0
.

Using the relations (
34
), the localization locus

\mathcal{F}_{S}
consists of field configurations

(\varphi_{S},\bar{\psi}_{S},\psi_{S},\omega_{S})
satisfying

\partial_{{\texttt{x}}}\varphi_{S}=-{\texttt{x}}\omega_{S}\,,\qquad\partial_{{\texttt{y}}}\varphi_{S}=-{\texttt{y}}\omega_{S}\,,\qquad\psi_{S}=0\,,\qquad\bar{\psi}_{S}=0\,.

(62)

To solve these equations, we multiply the first and second equations by

y
and
x
, respectively, and subtract them, obtaining

({\texttt{x}}\partial_{{\texttt{y}}}-{\texttt{y}}\partial_{{\texttt{x}}})\varphi_{S}=0\,.

(63)

A second relation follows by multiplying the same equations by
x
and
y
, respectively, and adding them:

({\texttt{x}}\partial_{{\texttt{x}}}+{\texttt{y}}\partial_{{\texttt{y}}})\varphi_{S}=-({\texttt{x}}^{2}+{\texttt{y}}^{2})\omega_{S}\,.

(64)

Equation (
63

\varphi_{S}
is invariant under the
rotations generated by
m^{{\texttt{x}}{\texttt{y}}}
. We may therefore regard
\varphi_{S}

as a function of
x_{\parallel}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-2}
and the radial coordinate
r=\sqrt{{\texttt{x}}^{2}+{\texttt{y}}^{2}}
only:

\varphi_{S}=\varphi_{S}(x_{\parallel},r,\eta=0)
. Here, we introduced polar coordinates
(r,\eta)
in the transverse
plane via
{\texttt{x}}=r\cos\eta
,
{\texttt{y}}=r\sin\eta
, for which

m^{{\texttt{x}}{\texttt{y}}}=\partial_{\eta}
. The second equation then implies

r\partial_{r}\varphi_{S}=-r^{2}\omega_{S}
.
Altogether, the localization locus is characterized by

\varphi_{S}=\varphi_{S}(x_{\parallel},r,\eta=0)\,,\qquad\omega_{S}=-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\varphi_{S}\,,\qquad\bar{\psi}_{S}=\psi_{S}=0\,.

(65)

To evaluate the deformed path integral (
57
), we expand the
fields around the localization locus (
65
) by
rescaling the fluctuations as follows

\displaystyle\varphi=\varphi_{S}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\delta\varphi\,,\qquad\omega=-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\varphi_{S}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\delta\omega\,,\qquad\psi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\delta\psi\,,\qquad\bar{\psi}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\delta\bar{\psi}\,,

(66)

where the fields with the deltas parametrize directions transverse to the
localization locus.
Taking the limit
t\to\infty
, the path integral reduces to

Z_{\text{SUSY}}=Z_{\text{1-loop}}\int\mathcal{D}\varphi_{S}\;e^{-S[\varphi_{S}]}\,,

(67)

where the one-loop determinant, after redefining
\delta\varphi\to\varphi,\delta\psi\to\psi,\delta\bar{\psi}\to\bar{\psi},\delta\omega\to\omega
, is given by

Z_{\text{1-loop}}=\int\mathcal{D}\varphi\,\mathcal{D}\omega\,[\mathcal{D}\bar{\psi}]\,[\mathcal{D}\psi]\;e^{-S^{(2)}_{\text{loc}}}\,,

(68)

where
S^{(2)}_{\text{loc}}
has the same form as (
59
). Since

S^{(2)}_{\text{loc}}
is a Gaussian action independent of the
field
\varphi_{S}
,

is a pure number. Its precise
value will be discussed in the next section, but it is ultimately
unimportant, as it cancels in the computation of correlation
functions.
8
8

8

Strictly speaking,
Z_{\text{1-loop}}
contains a
zero mode and therefore vanishes. In the next section we explain how to
properly treat this zero mode and show that the resulting one-loop
determinant can be consistently taken to be
Z_{\text{1-loop}}=1
.

We now compute the localized action
S[\varphi_{S}]
appearing in
(
67
) and show that it reproduces the reduced action
S_{d-2}
.
Evaluating the PS action (
14
) on the localization locus
(
65
), we obtain

\displaystyle S[\varphi_{S}]=\int d^{d}x\,\bigl(\partial_{{\texttt{x}}}\omega_{S}\partial_{{\texttt{x}}}\varphi_{S}+\partial_{{\texttt{y}}}\omega_{S}\partial_{{\texttt{y}}}\varphi_{S}+\partial^{i}\omega_{S}\partial_{i}\varphi_{S}-\omega_{S}^{2}+\omega_{S}V^{\prime}(\varphi_{S})\bigr)\,,

(69)

where we have separated the kinetic term into contributions along

\mathbb{R}^{d-2}
and the transverse directions.

Using the localization equations (
62
), the first two

69
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\int d^{d}x\,\bigl(\partial_{{\texttt{x}}}\omega_{S}\partial_{{\texttt{x}}}\varphi_{S}+\partial_{{\texttt{y}}}\omega_{S}\partial_{{\texttt{y}}}\varphi_{S}\bigr)=\int d^{d}x\,\bigl(-{\texttt{x}}\frac{1}{2}\partial_{{\texttt{x}}}\omega_{S}^{2}-{\texttt{y}}\frac{1}{2}\partial_{{\texttt{y}}}\omega_{S}^{2}\bigr)=\int d^{d}x\,\omega_{S}^{2}\,,

(70)

where in the last step we performed an integration by parts. This precisely
cancels the
-\omega_{S}^{2}
term in (

). We are therefore
left with

\displaystyle S[\varphi_{S}]=\int d^{d}x\,\bigl(\partial^{i}\omega_{S}\partial_{i}\varphi_{S}+\omega_{S}V^{\prime}(\varphi_{S})\bigr)=\int d^{d}x\,\Bigl[-\frac{1}{2r}\partial_{r}\bigl(\partial^{i}\varphi_{S}\partial_{i}\varphi_{S}\bigr)-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\varphi_{S}V^{\prime}(\varphi_{S})\Bigr].

(71)

We now decompose the integration over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
into

\mathbb{R}^{d-2}
and the transverse
x

plane. Since
\varphi_{S}
is independent of
\eta

angular integration is trivial and yields

S[\varphi_{S}]=2\pi\int d^{d-2}x_{\|}\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\Bigl[-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{r}\bigl(\partial^{i}\varphi_{S}\partial_{i}\varphi_{S}\bigr)-\partial_{r}V(\varphi_{S})\Bigr].

(72)

Assuming that the fields vanish sufficiently fast at infinity and that

V(0)=0

S_{d-2}=2\pi\int d^{d-2}x_{\|}\Bigl[-\frac{1}{2}\phi\,\partial_{\|}^{2}\phi+V(\phi)\Bigr],

(73)

where we defined
\phi\equiv\varphi_{S}(x_{\|},0,0)

precisely with the reduced action (

dimensional reduction.

9

9

To be precise, the derivation above glosses over a few subtleties that we

68

one-loop determinant is equal to one.

4.1

In the discussion above, we focused on saddle-point configurations with

10
10

10

Indeed, the fermionic part of (

(74)

As a consequence, even in the limit

Let us now turn to the computation of the one-loop determinant defined in

\displaystyle S_{f}

To evaluate the corresponding path integral, we expand each field in

\displaystyle\varphi(x_{\|},r,\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-\infty\\

The path integral in (

In particular, the

Two of the four fermionic modes with

or this purpose, we change variables from

Substituting the Fourier expansions (

\displaystyle S_{b}

\displaystyle=2\pi\int d^{d-2}x_{\|}\,r\,dr\sum_{k\neq 0}\bigl(\omega_{-k}\omega_{k}+\varphi_{-k}k^{2}\varphi_{k}\bigr)\,,

(83)

\displaystyle S_{f}

\displaystyle=2\pi\!\int d^{d-2}x_{\|}\,r\,dr\biggl(4\alpha^{+}_{-1}\alpha^{+}_{1}+\sum_{k\neq\pm 1}\bigl(\psi_{-k}ik\psi_{k}+\bar{\psi}_{-k}ik\bar{\psi}_{k}-2\psi_{-k}\bar{\psi}_{k}\bigr)\biggr).

Both the bosonic and fermionic sectors are quadratic by construction, so the

the bosonic integral produces a determinant, while the fermionic one

11
11

11

Strictly speaking, the product over

4.2
Review of Zaboronsky localization

We now turn to the localization approach introduced by Zaboronsky

52
. This construction involves a simpler
deformation term than (
59
), but also comes with some conceptual
subtleties, which we discuss below.

The starting point is to project
\Phi=\varphi+\theta\bar{\psi}+\bar{\theta}\psi+\theta\bar{\theta}\omega
to its restriction
\phi=\varphi|_{\mathbb{R}^{d-2}}
, then extend this lower-dimensional field to a superfield
\Phi_{S}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d|2}
that is
Q
-invariant.
For definiteness, we choose

\Phi_{S}(x,\theta,\bar{\theta})=\phi(x_{\parallel})\,,

(85)

whose only non-vanishing superfield component is the lowest component, which furthermore is constant in
(r,\eta)
.
Zaboronsky’s deformation term can then be understood as a Gaussian
superfield action centered at
\Phi_{S}
, namely

S_{\text{loc}}=\int d^{d}xd\bar{\theta}d\theta(\Phi-\Phi_{S})^{2}=\int d^{d}x\Bigl[(\varphi-\phi)\omega+\psi\bar{\psi}\Bigr]\,,

(86)

which is much simpler than (
59
).
This choice satisfies
QS_{\text{loc}}=0
.
In fact, the deformation term fits more directly in the standard localization approach by noting that it is
Q
-exact up to a boundary term that vanishes thanks to
\Phi=\Phi_{S}
at
r=0
:

(\varphi-\phi)\omega+\psi\bar{\psi}=Q\left(\frac{\varphi-\phi}{r^{2}}({\texttt{x}}\bar{\psi}+{\texttt{y}}\psi)\right)-\frac{1}{2r}\partial_{r}\left(\varphi-\phi\right)^{2}.

(87)

Glossing over the fact that this action is not positive-definite, one can follow the localization playbook and consider the saddles of this action.
A straightforward analysis shows that
the corresponding saddle-point configuration is

\varphi=\phi\,,\quad\omega=0\,,\quad\psi=0\,,\quad\bar{\psi}=0\,.

(88)

When evaluated on this configuration, the PS Lagrangian in
(
14
) vanishes identically. However, the action
(
14
) still contains integrals over the
x
-
y
plane which lead to divergences. As a
result, the localization procedure based on this deformation is
subtle, and the corresponding computation is not fully well defined.

There is, however, a way to recover the desired result, as we now
explain. For large values of
t
, the localization term

e^{-tS_{\text{loc}}}
behaves as a delta functional on the space of
superfield configurations, effectively localizing the path integral
onto
\Phi_{S}
. From this perspective, one can replace

\Phi\to\phi
in the superfield action
(
12
), which then reduces to

S=V_{2|2}\times 2\pi\int d^{d-2}x_{\parallel}\bigl[(\partial_{\parallel}\phi)^{2}+V(\phi)\bigr].

(89)

This expression coincides with the reduced action, up to an overall
factor given by the volume
V_{2|2}
of the transverse superspace

\mathbb{R}^{2|2}
, defined as

V_{2|2}\equiv\int[d\bar{\theta}][d\theta]\,dx\,dy\;1\,.

(90)

The integral
V_{2|2}
is not well defined: it vanishes due to the
fermionic integrations, while diverging because of the bosonic ones.
Nevertheless, one may introduce a regularized volume,

V_{2|2}^{\zeta}\equiv\int[d\bar{\theta}][d\theta]\,dx\,dy\;e^{-\zeta(x^{2}+y^{2}-2\theta\bar{\theta})}=1\,,\qquad(\zeta>0)\,,

(91)

which is finite and equal to one for
\zeta>0
. Although

V_{2|2}^{\zeta}
is strictly defined only for
\zeta>0
, one may argue
by analytic continuation that

V_{2|2}^{\zeta=0}=V_{2|2}=1
. Under this assumption, the saddle-point
action reproduces the reduced action, as desired.

Let us also briefly comment on the one-loop determinant. This arises
from the evaluation of the partition function associated with the
Gaussian action

S_{\mathrm{loc}}=\int d^{d}x\,\bigl(2\varphi\,\omega+2\psi\bar{\psi}\bigr)\,,

(92)

where the field
\varphi
fluctuates only along the directions
orthogonal to the localization locus.

Since the term
\varphi\omega
is not positive definite, the
corresponding one-loop determinant is divergent and requires
regularization. One possible prescription is to choose the integration
contour for
\omega
to be purely imaginary, which yields a finite
one-loop determinant equal to one. We do not dwell further on this
point, as the Gaussian integral contributes only an overall numerical
factor, which cancels in normalized correlation functions.

In summary, while Zaboronsky’s argument ultimately leads to the correct
result, the proof involves several subtleties. First, the bosonic part
of the deformation term is not positive definite, which obscures the
standard saddle-point analysis and renders the one-loop determinant
ill-defined. Second, it is essential to work directly in superspace,
since the component Lagrangian vanishes on the localization locus.
Third, the final result is proportional to the volume
V_{2|2}
, which
is only defined through analytic continuation. In view of these
subtleties, one may prefer the localization argument presented in
section
4
.

5
Interpolation argument

In this section, we discuss an alternative approach to dimensional reduction proposed by Cardy
12
. The idea is to introduce a deformation term such that the action interpolates between the SUSY action and the reduced action. Using symmetry arguments in superspace, Cardy argues that the path integral is independent of this deformation, thereby proving dimensional reduction. After briefly reviewing his original argument, we show that his deformation term is, in fact,
Q
-exact. This provides a natural reinterpretation of his work within the framework of
Q
-cohomology. Finally, we explain how to use this method to prove dimensional reduction for more generic actions.

Let us start by considering the following deformed partition function with sources
12
,

Z_{\lambda}[J]=\int\mathcal{D}\Phi e^{{-S_{\lambda}}-\int d^{d|2}y\,\delta_{\perp}J\Phi}\,,

(93)

where the source term
\int d^{d|2}y\,\delta_{\perp}J\Phi
is integrated in superspace with a delta function
\delta_{\perp}\equiv 2\pi\delta(\theta)\delta({\bar{\theta}})\delta({\texttt{x}})\delta({\texttt{y}})
which projects the integrand to
\mathbb{R}^{d-2}
, and
S_{\lambda}
is the following deformed action

S_{\lambda}=\lambda S_{\text{SUSY}}+(1-\lambda)(S_{d-2}+S_{\perp})\,.

(94)

The orthogonal action
S_{\perp}
here is defined by splitting the superfield Lagrangian as
\mathcal{L}_{\text{SUSY}}=\mathcal{L}_{\|}+\mathcal{L}_{\perp}
, where

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathcal{L}_{\|}=-\frac{1}{2}\Phi\partial^{2}_{\|}\Phi+V(\Phi)\,,\quad

\displaystyle S_{\|}=\int d^{d}x\,\bigl(\partial^{i}\omega\partial_{i}\varphi+\omega V^{\prime}(\varphi)+\partial^{i}\psi\partial_{i}\bar{\psi}+\psi\bar{\psi}V^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\bigr)\,,

(95)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathcal{L}_{\perp}=-\frac{1}{2}\Phi\partial^{2}_{\perp}\Phi\,,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \quad

\displaystyle S_{\perp}=\int d^{d}x\,\bigl(-\omega\partial_{\texttt{x}}^{2}\varphi-\omega\partial_{\texttt{y}}^{2}\varphi-\omega^{2}-\psi\partial_{\texttt{x}}^{2}\bar{\psi}-\psi\partial_{\texttt{y}}^{2}\bar{\psi}\bigr)\,.

(96)

The deformed action
S_{\lambda}
defines a continuous interpolation between
S_{\text{SUSY}}
(at
\lambda=1
) and
S_{d-2}+S_{\perp}
(at
\lambda=0
).
Moreover, at
\lambda=0
, the action
S_{\perp}
decouples from the rest of the terms.
Specifically, Cardy argues that the fields
\partial_{\texttt{x}}\varphi,\partial_{\texttt{y}}\varphi
and
\omega
inside
S_{\perp}
decouple when computing correlators of
\varphi(x_{\parallel})
with the reduced action.
Thus,
Z_{\lambda}[J]
defines an interpolation between the SUSY theory and the reduced one.
The idea is to prove that
Z_{\lambda}[J]
is independent of
\lambda
, which thus implies dimensional reduction.

To prove this statement, it is convenient to rewrite
S_{d-2}
in terms of
\mathcal{L}_{\|}
:

\qquad S_{d-2}=2\pi\int d^{d|2}y\,\delta_{\perp}\mathcal{L}_{\|}\,.

(97)

Then
\partial_{\lambda}\ln Z_{\lambda}[J]
can be expressed in terms of the following compact expression:

\partial_{\lambda}\ln Z_{\lambda}[J]=-2\pi\int d^{d|2}y\,(1-\delta_{\perp})\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\|}(y)\right\rangle_{\lambda,J}\,,

(98)

which is computed with the deformed action
S_{\lambda}
.
Since the action
S_{\lambda}
, including the source term, is manifestly invariant under
OSp(2|2)
super-rotations, it follows that
\mathcal{L}_{\|}
is also invariant. Consequently, the one-point function above is constrained to take the following form

2\pi\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\|}(y)\right\rangle_{\lambda,J}=F_{\lambda,J}\left(x_{\parallel},y_{\perp}^{2}\right)\,,

(99)

for some function
F_{\lambda,J}
. For brevity, we henceforth suppress the
\lambda,J
subscripts and the dependence on
x_{\|}
. Substituting (
99
) into (
98
), we get

\partial_{\lambda}\ln Z_{\lambda}[J]=-\int d^{d-2}x_{\|}d^{2|2}y_{\perp}\,F\left(y_{\perp}^{2}\right)+\int d^{d-2}x_{\|}\,F\left(0\right).

(100)

To compute the first integral, we expand
F_{\lambda,J}
in the Grassmann variables
\theta\bar{\theta}
:

F(r^{2}-2\theta\bar{\theta})=F(r^{2})-2\theta\bar{\theta}\frac{\partial F(r^{2})}{\partial r^{2}}\,,

(101)

with the usual notation
r^{2}={\texttt{x}}^{2}+{\texttt{y}}^{2}
. Then the perpendicular part of the integral of
F(y_{\perp}^{2})
evaluates to

\displaystyle\int d^{2|2}y_{\perp}\,F(y_{\perp}^{2})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int rdrd\eta\frac{dF(r^{2})}{dr^{2}}=F(0)-F(\infty)\,.

(102)

where
\eta
is a polar angle in the
x
-
y
plane. Moreover,
F(\infty)
vanishes because of the standard boundary conditions of fields at infinity. Therefore plugging (
102
) in (
100
) we finally obtain the desired relation

\partial_{\lambda}\ln Z_{\lambda}[J]=0\,.

(103)

This shows that the generating functional
Z_{\lambda}
is independent of
\lambda
. Thus, it does not matter whether we take
\lambda=1
or
\lambda=0
. Therefore, the theory
S_{\text{SUSY}}
(for
\lambda=1
) dimensionally reduces to
S_{d-2}
(for
\lambda=0
).

5.1
Interpolation method from
Q
-cohomology

In this section, we reformulate Cardy’s argument using the cohomology of
Q
.

To make the argument nicer, we slightly change the definition of the deformed partition function as follows

Z_{\lambda}=\int\mathcal{D}\Phi e^{{-S_{\text{SUSY}}+\lambda(S_{\text{SUSY}}-S_{d-2})}}\,.

(104)

In this notation, the interpolation is even cleaner and gives on the nose
S_{\text{SUSY}}
at
\lambda=0
and
S_{d-2}
at
\lambda=1
.
Again, we want to prove that the deformation does not depend on
\lambda
, but now the strategy is to simply prove that the term
S_{\text{SUSY}}-S_{d-2}
is
Q
-exact (it is explicitly
Q
-closed since these terms are invariant under
OSp(2|2)
).

We indeed find that the deformation is
Q
-exact:

\boxed{S_{\text{SUSY}}-S_{d-2}=Q\int d^{d}x\,\bigl(\partial^{i}\psi_{\|}\partial_{i}\varphi+\psi_{\|}V^{\prime}(\varphi)+\omega\psi_{\perp}\bigr)}\,,

(105)

where we defined two Grassmann field
\psi_{\|}
and
\psi_{\perp}
as follows

\psi_{\|}\equiv\frac{1}{r^{2}}({\texttt{x}}\bar{\psi}+{\texttt{y}}\psi)\,,\qquad\psi_{\perp}\equiv-\partial_{\texttt{y}}\psi-\partial_{\texttt{x}}\bar{\psi}\,.

(106)

In order to prove (
105
) we use the following transformations of the newly introduced Grassmann fields

Q\psi_{\|}=\omega+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\varphi\,,\qquad Q\psi_{\perp}=-(\partial_{\texttt{x}}^{2}+\partial_{\texttt{y}}^{2})\varphi-(r\partial_{r}+2)\omega\,,

(107)

which further give

\displaystyle Q(\partial^{i}\psi_{\|}\partial_{i}\varphi+\psi_{\|}V^{\prime}(\varphi))

\displaystyle=\partial^{i}\omega\partial_{i}\varphi+\partial^{i}\psi\partial_{i}\bar{\psi}+\omega V^{\prime}(\varphi)+\psi\bar{\psi}V^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)

(108)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2r}\partial_{r}(\partial^{i}\varphi\partial_{i}\varphi)+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}V(\varphi)\,.

(109)

\displaystyle Q(\omega\psi_{\perp})

\displaystyle=-\omega(\partial_{\texttt{x}}^{2}+\partial_{\texttt{y}}^{2})\varphi-2\omega^{2}-\omega{\texttt{x}}\partial_{\texttt{x}}\omega-\omega{\texttt{y}}\partial_{\texttt{y}}\omega

(110)

\displaystyle\quad+\partial_{{\texttt{x}}}\psi\partial_{{\texttt{x}}}\bar{\psi}+\partial_{{\texttt{y}}}\psi\partial_{{\texttt{y}}}\bar{\psi}+\partial_{{\texttt{x}}}\bar{\psi}\partial_{{\texttt{y}}}\bar{\psi}+\partial_{{\texttt{x}}}\psi\partial_{{\texttt{y}}}\psi\,.

(111)

Notice that the terms in (
108
) integrate to
S_{\parallel}
while the ones
in (
109
) integrate to minus the reduced action, namely
\int d^{d}x\,\bigl(\frac{1}{2r}\partial_{r}(\partial^{i}\varphi\partial_{i}\varphi)+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}V(\varphi)\bigr)=-S_{d-2}
, which can be easily seen by following the same logic as (
72
): one writes the measure
d{\texttt{x}}d{\texttt{y}}=rdrd\eta
and integrates radially to get a (vanishing) boundary term at
r=+\infty
and an
r=0
contribution which has no
\eta
dependence.
We can also easily show that the integral of
Q(\omega\psi_{\perp})
is equal to the orthogonal action
(
96
)
. To see this we first notice that
-\omega{\texttt{x}}\partial_{\texttt{x}}\omega
has the same integral as
\omega^{2}/2
by integration by parts.
Moreover we notice that
\psi
and
\partial_{\texttt{x}}\partial_{\texttt{y}}\psi
anticommute, which implies the identity
2\,\partial_{\texttt{x}}\psi\,\partial_{\texttt{y}}\psi=\partial_{\texttt{x}}(\psi\,\partial_{\texttt{y}}\psi)+\partial_{\texttt{y}}(\partial_{\texttt{x}}\psi\,\psi)
, showing that the last two terms in (
111
) are total derivatives.

Actually, the argument above shows a bit more than (
105
). Specifically we found that the orthogonal action
S_{\perp}
and the difference
(S_{\parallel}-S_{d-2})
are separately
Q
-exact.
This can also be used to rephrase the original argument of Cardy. There one needed to argue both that
S_{\perp}
decoupled and that
(S_{\parallel}-S_{d-2})
did not modify the action.
Now we can explain that both terms are in fact
Q
-exact.

Finally, let us stress that we did not write the source terms for brevity.
However, as explained in the review
subsection 2.3
, one can always add
Q
-invariant sources without changing the argument.
This means that we can localize all correlation functions of operators in the cohomology of
Q
. These were described in detail in
section 3
and were shown to match the set of operators of the reduced theory.
We therefore conclude that the interpolation method provides a very simple and clean argument for dimensional reduction.

5.2
Generalizing to other theories

In this section, we argue that Cardy’s argument is, in fact, very generic and can be applied to a large class of different theories.
Given a generic action
S
invariant under PS SUSY, we can prove its dimensional reduction by pursuing the following strategy:

•

We define
S=S_{\parallel}+S_{\perp}
, where

S_{\parallel}\equiv\int d^{d|2}y\,\mathcal{L}_{\parallel}

is defined by setting the orthogonal part of the metric to zero inside
S
.

•

By definition
S_{\perp}
is an
OSp(2|2)
-singlet built out of the metric
g_{2|2}
.
By the decoupling argument of (
38
), which is valid for any model, we thus conclude that
S_{\perp}
decouples. For scalar actions we can further show that this term is indeed
Q
-exact, as explained below.

•

Any deformation of the action by
S_{\parallel}-S_{d-2}
does not change the path integral, where we define
S_{d-2}\equiv\int d^{d-2}x_{\|}\,\mathcal{L}_{\parallel}|_{d-2}
.
This is automatically proven by the original argument by Cardy (
100
), which was agnostic on the form of the actions. Alternatively, below we will also show that any action
S_{\parallel}
is indeed equal to
S_{d-2}
in cohomology.

•

Following Cardy’s argument, the deformed action
S-\lambda(S-S_{d-2})
does not change the partition function and interpolates between
S
and
S_{d-2}
by tuning
\lambda
from
0
to
1
.

We thus obtained that any PS supersymmetric action
S
dimensionally reduces to
S_{d-2}
.
In this paper, we focus on scalar actions built out of the scalar superfield
\Phi
, e.g., in appendix
B
we detail how to use this algorithm in the case of a higher-derivative term
\Phi(\partial^{2})^{n}\Phi
.
It is, however, important to stress that the same strategy can also be extended to more general models, which might also contain gauge fields and fermions. It would be interesting to spell out the details of such cases, but we leave it for future work.

In the following, we want to show that, for any
S
, both
S_{\perp}
and
S_{\parallel}-S_{d-2}
are
Q
-exact. To do so we first prove that for any scalar function
F
of the superfields, the following equation holds

\boxed{\int d^{2|2}y_{\perp}\,F(y)=F(x_{\parallel},y_{\perp}=0)+Q\int d^{2|2}y_{\perp}\ \chi\,F(y)}\,,

(112)

where we recall that
d^{2|2}y_{\perp}=d{\texttt{x}}\,d{\texttt{y}}\,[d\bar{\theta}][d\theta]
and we introduced the Grassmann variable
\chi
defined as

\chi\equiv\frac{({\texttt{y}}\theta-{\texttt{x}}{\bar{\theta}})}{r^{2}}\,.

(113)

Equation (
112
) can be equivalently rewritten as

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d{\texttt{x}}\,d{\texttt{y}}\,F_{\text{highest}}(x)=F_{\text{lowest}}(x_{\parallel},{\texttt{x}}=0,{\texttt{y}}=0)+Q\int d^{2|2}y_{\perp}\ \chi\,F\,,

(114)

where
F_{\text{highest}}
(
F_{\text{lowest}}
) is the highest (lowest) component of
F
when expanded in
\theta
,
{\bar{\theta}}
. This form of the equation makes it manifest that the lowest and highest components of superfields are related in cohomology.

First, let us show how to prove (
112
). We start by performing the following computation

\displaystyle Q\int[d\bar{\theta}][d\theta]\,\chi F=-\int[d\bar{\theta}][d\theta]\,\chi qF=\int[d\bar{\theta}][d\theta]\left(\frac{{\texttt{x}}^{2}\bar{\theta}\partial_{\bar{\theta}}+{\texttt{y}}^{2}\theta\partial_{\theta}}{r^{2}}+\theta\bar{\theta}\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\right)F\,,

(115)

where in the first equality we are use that
F
is a scalar so the action of
Q
is replaced by the Killing vector
q
, while the second equality is obtained by replacing the form of the Killing vector
q
and simplifying the resulting differential operator.

Next, we simplify the right-hand side of (
115
). Integrating by
parts
\theta\,\partial_{\theta}
and

\bar{\theta}\,\partial_{\bar{\theta}}
, the first term in parentheses reduces to one.
We then perform the Grassmann integration of the term
proportional to
\theta\bar{\theta}
, which selects the lowest component
of
F
. Altogether, we find

Q\int[d\bar{\theta}][d\theta]\,\chi F=\int[d\bar{\theta}][d\theta]F+\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}F_{\text{lowest}}\,.

(116)

Finally (
112
) is obtained by integrating this expression using polar coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and noticing that
\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}F_{\text{lowest}}
becomes a total derivatives that picks the boundary term at
r=0
.

Equation (
112
) is very powerful since it holds for any scalar superfield
F
.
First we can use (
112
) to prove that any
S_{\perp}[\Phi]
is
Q
-exact by itself. Indeed, by definition
S_{\perp}
must be defined as

S_{\perp}=\int d^{d|2}y\,\mathcal{O}(y)=\int d^{d-2}x_{\parallel}\int d^{2|2}y_{\perp}\;\mathcal{O}(y)\,,

(117)

for some scalar superfield operator
\mathcal{O}
built out of the orthogonal metric
g_{2|2}
.

Moreover, following the logic of the equation (
42
), the most generic form of these superfield operators can be written as

\mathcal{O}(y)\equiv\lim_{y_{i}\to y}P_{\parallel}P_{\perp}\Phi(y_{1})\cdots\Phi(y_{n})\,,

(118)

where
P_{\perp}
must be non-trivial and
P_{\parallel}
here is defined by taking
P_{\parallel}^{\{\mu\}}
in (
42
) and contracting all the
\mu
indices to generate a scalar operator. Using (
112
) we can thus argue that
(
117
) is equal in cohomology to
\int d^{d-2}x_{\|}\,\mathcal{O}(y)|_{d-2}
, which is exactly the integral of one of the operators in (
42
), which was proven to be
Q
-exact. This thus shows that
S_{\perp}
must also be
Q
-exact and thus that it decouples.

We can also use (
112
) to show that
any parallel superfield action
S_{\parallel}=\int d^{d|2}y\,\mathcal{L}_{\parallel}
is equal in cohomology to a reduced action which can be simply defined as
S_{d-2}=\int d^{d-2}x_{\|}\,\mathcal{L}_{\parallel}|_{d-2}
.
This trivially arises by using (
112
) with
F=\int d^{d-2}x_{\|}\,\mathcal{L}_{\parallel}
for any
\mathcal{L}_{\parallel}
,
namely

S_{\|}-S_{d-2}=Q\int d^{d|2}y\,\chi\,\mathcal{L}_{\|}\,.

(119)

Interestingly, we can also apply (
112
) to probe the dimensional reduction of extended defect operators. To this end, we define
F=\int d^{p-2}x_{\|}\,\mathcal{O}(y)
, where a superfield operator
\mathcal{O}
is integrated
over a bosonic submanifold
\mathbb{R}^{p-2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-2}
. Then (
112
) implies that

\int d^{p|2}y\,\mathcal{O}(y)=\int d^{p-2}x_{\parallel}\,\mathcal{O}(x_{\parallel},y_{\perp}\!=0)+Q[{\dots}]\,,

(120)

which means that there are two defects which are equal up to
Q
-exact terms.
This setup was recently studied in
23
, where indeed it was found that these two defects have the same dimensional reduction.
Finally, let us mention that to derive (
112
) we did not have to specify that
F
was a function of scalar superfields
\Phi
, so it should also work for gauge superfields and fermions. It would be interesting to consider these setups.

Another interesting application concerns the reduction of conserved charges.
As reviewed in appendix
A
, the topological charge
associated with a supercurrent
\mathcal{J}^{a}
can be written as an integral
over a codimension-
1
submanifold
\Sigma
of superspace,

\int_{\Sigma}dS^{a}\,\mathcal{J}_{a}(y)\,,

(121)

where
dS^{a}
is the integration measure induced from
d^{d|2}y
.
When
\Sigma=\Sigma_{d-3}\times\mathbb{R}^{2|2}
is a product, in which
\Sigma_{d-3}
is a hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{d-2}
(such as a time slice in any choice of foliation), the relevant components of
\mathcal{J}_{a}
are
\mathcal{J}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,d-2
.
Applying (
112
) to these superfields
\mathcal{J}_{i}(y)
, we find that the charge above reduces to

\int_{\Sigma}dS^{a}\,\mathcal{J}_{a}(y)=\int_{\Sigma_{d-3}}dS_{d-3}^{i}\int d^{2|2}y_{\perp}\,\mathcal{J}_{i}(y)=\int_{\Sigma_{d-3}}dS_{d-3}^{i}\,\mathcal{J}_{i}(x_{\|},y_{\perp}=0)+Q[{\cdots}]\,.

(122)

Passing to
Q
-cohomology, this is the conserved charge of the dimensionally reduced theory.
It is worth noting that this reduction does not apply to all conserved
charges. For example, for supertranslations
P^{a}
, it only applies to the components
P^{i}
for
i=1,\dots,d-2
because
(
112
) relies on the
OSp(2|2)
invariance of the integrand.
The reduction charges should be understood as an operator equation valid inside correlation functions of
Q
-closed operators, or as equations in the dimensionally reduced theory.

6
Discussion

In this paper, we revisited dimensional reduction in Parisi–Sourlas (PS)
supersymmetric theories. We provided three arguments based on the
cohomology of the supercharge
Q
defined in (
31
).

First, we reformulated the decoupling argument of
27
in
cohomological terms. In particular, in
27
it was shown that
the operators in the reduced theory must arise from
OSp(2|2)
-singlets,
and that all singlets built out of the orthogonal metric
g_{2|2}
must
decouple. The
OSp(2|2)
-singlets are, in particular,
Q
-closed, and in
section
3
we proved that operators built out of

g_{2|2}
are
Q
-exact. Therefore, we showed that the set of operators
of the dimensionally reduced theory is defined by
OSp(2|2)
-singlets
in the cohomology of
Q
. This is not only aesthetically pleasing, but
also parallels familiar cohomological constructions in other
supersymmetric contexts, see e.g.
4
.

Second, we presented a localization argument based on a canonical

Q
-exact deformation, phrased in a way that aligns with the standard
localization logic in high-energy physics, see e.g.
43
.
We also explained how this setup differs from the earlier approach of
Zaboronsky
52
, where a non-standard deformation
leads to additional subtleties.

Third, we revisited Cardy’s interpolation argument, where one considers
a generating functional for the reduced correlators written in terms of
a deformed action which interpolates between the supersymmetric and the
reduced theories as
S_{\text{SUSY}}-\lambda\left(S_{\text{SUSY}}-S_{d-2}\right)
.
Dimensional reduction is then established by showing that the
generating functional is independent of
\lambda
. Part of Cardy’s
original argument was completely generic and relied only on PS
supersymmetry, while another part depended on the explicit form of

S_{\text{SUSY}}
, which was taken to be a supersymmetric scalar action
with a generic potential. In section
5
we showed that

\left(S_{\text{SUSY}}-S_{d-2}\right)
is in fact a
Q
-exact
deformation, which explains why it can be added with an arbitrary
coefficient without affecting the physics. Moreover, we demonstrated
that the interpolation argument can be generalized to any action with
PS supersymmetry, including setups involving extended operators.

Altogether, these three arguments fill an important gap in the
literature by clarifying how various proofs of dimensional reduction
are not isolated results, but can instead be organized within a
unified
Q
-cohomological framework. This perspective also provides a
natural bridge to the language commonly used in the study of standard
supersymmetry in high-energy physics.

Some of the techniques employed in the study of PS SUSY—such as the decoupling argument of
27
, localization à la Zaboronsky
52
, and the interpolation method introduced by Cardy
12
—are largely unfamiliar to high-energy physicists working on conventional supersymmetry. We hope that our reformulation in terms of the cohomology of a supercharge may help uncover new applications of these methods also in that broader context.
It is worth emphasizing that some of these techniques are remarkably powerful and general.
For instance, the argument of
27
appears to apply to any theory that localizes from a superspace onto a submanifold, provided that the component of the superspace metric orthogonal to the submanifold is supertraceless.
It would be interesting to explore whether this type of reasoning could be extended to other models, such as
4
, providing an alternative perspective on why certain sectors of operators effectively localize to lower dimensions. Similarly, it would be very interesting to investigate whether Cardy’s interpolation method can be applied in more standard supersymmetric settings. This approach is considerably simpler than conventional localization, as it does not require the computation of one-loop determinants, and is therefore particularly well suited for non-compact situations.

Beyond applications to models with conventional supersymmetry, it would also be interesting to further investigate systems with PS SUSY. Most existing studies have focused on scalar theories; however, PS-supersymmetric QFTs can also be constructed with gauge fields and fermions, see e.g.
24
;
35
;
26
;
37
;
49
;
38
. As shown in section
5
, dimensional reduction holds for all PS SUSY QFTs. Although the intrinsic non-unitarity of these models prevents their use as fundamental descriptions of particle interactions, dimensional reduction makes them valuable as theoretical tools or toy models: it provides a controlled setting in which higher-dimensional observables can be accessed through the simpler kinematics of a lower-dimensional effective description.

The generality of dimensional reduction also has important implications for dimensional uplift. Generic PS SUSY actions reduce to fairly generic lower-dimensional theories, which in turn implies that a large class of lower-dimensional models admit an uplift. The abundance of such uplifts provides evidence that dimensional uplift may hold generically, at least for Lagrangian models. It would be interesting to determine whether counterexamples exist within this class. We leave this question to future work.

It is also worth noting that a version of PS dimensional reduction has
been observed in holographic correlators for various SCFTs

7
;
2
. In that context, however, no clear
explanation of this behavior was provided. Studying PS models with more
general field content might therefore help clarify whether certain
sectors of these SCFTs admit an effective description in terms of PS
supersymmetry.

A natural further direction is to extend these ideas beyond flat space.
In particular, it would be interesting to investigate whether
dimensional reduction persists for PS-supersymmetric theories defined
on curved backgrounds, such as AdS or dS.

It is also tempting to explore whether similar constructions can be
applied to gravitational models. In this context, one could study
gravitational actions invariant under PS supersymmetry (see

32
for a construction in this direction) and
understand under which conditions such theories undergo dimensional
reduction. More generally, this framework could provide a way to
engineer or isolate sectors of gravitational theories that effectively
behave as lower-dimensional systems.

By framing dimensional reduction in terms of
Q
-cohomology and
extending it to general PS-supersymmetric theories, we hope to provide a
unifying viewpoint that will both clarify the structure of PS models and
inform applications in high-energy physics.

Appendix A
Conventions and useful identities

In this appendix, we set the conventions about charges, generators of transformations, and algebra, and derive useful identities.

Supertranslation and superrotations charges are obtained by opportunely integrating the superstress tensor
\mathcal{T}_{ab}
with a
a vector field
\epsilon^{a}(y)
,

Q_{\epsilon}(\Sigma)=-\int_{\Sigma}dS^{a}\,\mathcal{J}^{(\epsilon)}_{a}(y)\equiv-\int_{\Sigma}dS^{a}\,\mathcal{T}_{ab}(y)\,\epsilon^{b}(y)\,,

(123)

where
\Sigma
is a codimension-one hypersurface in superspace. The operator
Q_{\epsilon}(\Sigma)
is topological as long as the supercurrent
\mathcal{J}^{(\epsilon)}_{a}
is conserved.
The conservation of
\mathcal{J}^{(\epsilon)}_{a}
leads to the Killing equation

\partial^{a}\epsilon^{b}+\partial^{b}\epsilon^{a}=0
,
which admits the solution

\epsilon^{a}=\omega^{a}+\omega^{ab}\,y_{b}\,,

(124)

where
\omega^{a}
is the vector of parameters associated to supertranslations and
\omega^{ab}
are the
transformation parameters for superrotations, which transform in the graded antisymmetric representation,

\omega^{ab}=-(-1)^{[a][b]}\,\omega^{ba}
.
The Killing vectors are then defined as
\epsilon=\epsilon^{a}\partial_{a}
and can be expanded in the basis of parameters as follows,

p^{a}=\partial^{a},\qquad m^{ab}=y^{a}\partial^{b}-(-1)^{[a][b]}y^{b}\partial^{a}\,.

(125)

Let us now review how the action of the charge
Q_{\epsilon}
on a scalar superfield can be computed using the Killing vectors.
We make use of the Ward identity for the current
\mathcal{J}^{(\epsilon)}_{a}

\partial_{y}^{a}\left\langle\mathcal{J}^{(\epsilon)}_{a}(y)\,\Phi(y_{1})\cdots\Phi(y_{n})\right\rangle=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta\big(y-y_{i}\big)\,\epsilon(y_{i})\left\langle\Phi(y_{1})\cdots\Phi(y_{n})\right\rangle.

(126)

Let us surround a given insertion
\Phi(y_{i})
by a small ball
B_{i}
whose
boundary
\Sigma_{i}
does not enclose any other operator insertions.
Integrating the Ward identity over
B_{i}
and using the divergence theorem,
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{i}}d^{d|2}y\,\partial_{y}^{a}\bigl\langle\mathcal{J}^{(\epsilon)}_{a}(y)\,\Phi(y_{1})\cdots\Phi(y_{n})\bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{i}}dS_{a}\,\bigl\langle\mathcal{J}^{a}(y)\,\Phi(y_{1})\cdots\Phi(y_{n})\bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=-\epsilon(y_{i})\langle\Phi(y_{1})\cdots\Phi(y_{n})\rangle\,.

(127)

Using (
123
), this implies

\left\langle Q_{\epsilon}(\Sigma_{i})\,\Phi(y_{i})\cdots\right\rangle=\left\langle\epsilon(y_{i})\Phi(y_{i})\cdots\right\rangle.

(128)

Or, as an operator equation,

\displaystyle Q_{\epsilon}\,\Phi=\epsilon\,\Phi\,.

(129)

When quantizing the theory, this relation is promoted to a
commutator,
[Q_{\epsilon},\Phi\}=\epsilon\,\Phi\,
.
The charge can also be expanded on the basis of parameters
Q_{\epsilon}=\omega^{a}P_{a}+\omega^{ab}M_{ab}
, where
P_{a}
and
M_{ab}
respectively define the generators of supertranslations and superrotations in accordance with (
125

We will often use the action of a charge on the components of a superfield.
Let us exemplify, for the charge

\displaystyle P_{\theta}\Phi(y)

\displaystyle=p_{\theta}\Phi(y)=\bar{\psi}(x)+\bar{\theta}\omega(x)\,,

(130)

\displaystyle=P_{\theta}\varphi(x)-\theta P_{\theta}\bar{\psi}(x)-{\bar{\theta}}P_{\theta}\psi(x)+\theta{\bar{\theta}}P_{\theta}\omega(x)\,,

(131)

where the minus signs in (

anticommutes with Grassmann numbers. From here, we deduce

\begin{gathered}P_{\theta}\varphi=\bar{\psi},\quad P_{\theta}\bar{\psi}=0,\quad P_{\theta}\psi=-\omega,\quad P_{\theta}\omega=0\,.\end{gathered}

(132)

One can easily check that this variation, and its analogues for other supertranslations and superrotations, preserve the action (
14
).

Let us now consider the repeated action of
Q_{\epsilon}
on a scalar superfield.
We find that the repeated action reverses the order of the differential operators,

Q_{\epsilon_{1}}Q_{\epsilon_{2}}\Phi=(-1)^{[\epsilon_{1}][\epsilon_{2}]}\,\epsilon_{2}\,\epsilon_{1}\,\Phi\,.

(133)

To see this, we first write
Q_{\epsilon_{2}}\Phi=\epsilon_{2}\Phi
, then use that the action of the charge
Q_{\epsilon_{1}}

graded commutes with the differential operator
\epsilon_{2}
since it only acts on

\Phi
, in accordance with (

).
As a simple example, consider the translation generator
P_{\theta}
and the

M^{{\texttt{x}}\theta}

(134)

To see how the algebra of charges is related to that of the Killing vectors, we use (
133
) to write

\displaystyle[Q_{\epsilon_{1}},Q_{\epsilon_{2}}\}\Phi

\displaystyle=Q_{\epsilon_{1}}Q_{\epsilon_{2}}\Phi-(-1)^{[\epsilon_{1}][\epsilon_{2}]}Q_{\epsilon_{2}}Q_{\epsilon_{1}}\Phi

(135)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{[\epsilon_{1}][\epsilon_{2}]}\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{1}\Phi-\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\Phi\,,

(136)

from which we find

\left[Q_{\epsilon_{1}},Q_{\epsilon_{2}}\right\}=Q_{-\left[\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}\right\}}\,.

(137)

In the paper, we focus on a specific fermionic charge denoted by
Q
given by the following combination of superrotations:

Q\equiv M^{{\texttt{x}}\theta}+M^{{\texttt{y}}\bar{\theta}},

(138)

and we denote the corresponding differential operator as

(139)

\{M^{\mu p},M^{\nu q}\}=-g^{pq}M^{\mu\nu}+g^{\mu\nu}M^{pq}\,,

\displaystyle\{M^{{\texttt{x}}\theta},M^{{\texttt{x}}\theta}\}=M^{\theta\theta},\qquad\{M^{{\texttt{x}}\theta},M^{{\texttt{x}}\bar{\theta}}\}=M^{\theta\bar{\theta}},

(152)

where in the last equality we used that

Appendix C
Comments on localization

C.1
Q
-invariance of the deformation term
S_{\text{loc}}

In this appendix, we provide the proof that the localization term (
60
), by which we deform the path integral, is
Q
-closed.
Observe first that, for any pair of (odd) charges
Q_{\epsilon_{1}}
and
Q_{\epsilon_{2}}
and any femionic field
\psi
and bosonic field
\varphi^{\prime}
, one has

\displaystyle Q_{\epsilon_{1}}Q_{\epsilon_{2}}(\psi\varphi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=Q_{\epsilon_{1}}(Q_{\epsilon_{2}}\psi\,\varphi^{\prime}-\psi Q_{\epsilon_{2}}\varphi^{\prime})

(162)

\displaystyle=(Q_{\epsilon_{1}}Q_{\epsilon_{2}}\psi)\varphi^{\prime}+Q_{\epsilon_{2}}\psi Q_{\epsilon_{1}}\varphi^{\prime}-Q_{\epsilon_{1}}\psi Q_{\epsilon_{2}}\varphi^{\prime}+\psi\,Q_{\epsilon_{1}}Q_{\epsilon_{2}}\varphi^{\prime}\,.

In the case
Q_{\epsilon_{1}}=Q_{\epsilon_{2}}
the middle two terms cancel each other.
Thus, acting on (
60
) by
Q
, we obtain

Q^{2}\mathcal{V}=\int d^{d}x\,QQ(\psi Q\psi+\bar{\psi}Q\bar{\psi})=\int d^{d}x\,\bigl(Q^{2}\psi Q\psi+Q^{2}\bar{\psi}Q\bar{\psi}+\psi Q^{3}\psi+\bar{\psi}Q^{3}\bar{\psi}\bigr)\,.

(163)

Using the relations (
34
) together with

Q^{3}\psi=-\partial_{{\texttt{x}}}\varphi-{\texttt{x}}\omega+m^{{\texttt{x}}{\texttt{y}}}(\partial_{{\texttt{y}}}\varphi+{\texttt{y}}\omega)\,,\quad Q^{3}\bar{\psi}=m^{{\texttt{x}}{\texttt{y}}}(\partial_{{\texttt{x}}}\varphi+{\texttt{x}}\omega)+\partial_{{\texttt{y}}}\varphi+{\texttt{y}}\omega\,,

(164)

for each term in (
163
), we obtain

\displaystyle Q^{2}\psi Q\psi

\displaystyle=m^{{\texttt{x}}{\texttt{y}}}\psi\partial_{{\texttt{y}}}\varphi+{\texttt{y}}\omega m^{{\texttt{x}}{\texttt{y}}}\psi-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\bar{\psi}\partial_{{\texttt{y}}}\varphi}-{\color[rgb]{0.5,0,0.5}{\texttt{y}}\bar{\psi}\omega}\,,

(165)

\displaystyle\psi Q^{3}\psi

\displaystyle=\psi m^{{\texttt{x}}{\texttt{y}}}\partial_{{\texttt{y}}}\varphi+\psi m^{{\texttt{x}}{\texttt{y}}}({\texttt{y}}\omega)-{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\psi\partial_{{\texttt{x}}}\varphi}-{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0.5}{\texttt{x}}\psi\omega}\,,

\displaystyle Q^{2}\bar{\psi}Q\bar{\psi}

\displaystyle=m^{{\texttt{x}}{\texttt{y}}}\bar{\psi}\partial_{{\texttt{x}}}\varphi+{\texttt{x}}\omega m^{{\texttt{x}}{\texttt{y}}}\bar{\psi}+{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\psi\partial_{{\texttt{x}}}\varphi}+{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0.5}{\texttt{x}}\psi\omega}\,,

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}Q^{3}\bar{\psi}

\displaystyle=\bar{\psi}m^{{\texttt{x}}{\texttt{y}}}\partial_{{\texttt{x}}}\varphi+\bar{\psi}m^{{\texttt{x}}{\texttt{y}}}(x\omega)+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\bar{\psi}\partial_{{\texttt{y}}}\varphi}+{\color[rgb]{0.5,0,0.5}{\texttt{y}}\bar{\psi}\omega}\,.

Upon summing these lines the colored terms cancel pairwise. The remaining terms organize as total derivatives, which can be integrated by parts to give zero,

\displaystyle\int d^{d}x\,\bigl(Q^{2}\psi Q\psi+\psi Q^{3}\psi+Q^{2}\bar{\psi}Q\bar{\psi}+\bar{\psi}Q^{3}\bar{\psi}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\int d^{d}x\,m^{{\texttt{x}}{\texttt{y}}}\Bigl(\psi\partial_{{\texttt{y}}}\varphi+{\texttt{y}}\omega\psi+\bar{\psi}\partial_{{\texttt{x}}}\varphi+{\texttt{x}}\omega\bar{\psi}\Bigr)=0\,.

Thus, the localization term
Q\mathcal{V}
is
Q
-closed.

C.2
Deriving fermionic saddle point action

In this appendix, we show that the fermionic part of the SUSY action (
14
) can be written as (
77
) when restricted on the localization locus fields (
75
). For the term involving the potential, we have

\displaystyle\psi_{S}\bar{\psi}_{S}V^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{S})

\displaystyle=(\sin\eta\bar{\xi}+\cos\eta\xi)(-\sin\eta\xi+\cos\eta\bar{\xi})V^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{S})=\xi\bar{\xi}V^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{S})\,.

(167)

For the kinetic term of the fermionic action
\partial^{\mu}\psi_{S}\partial_{\mu}\bar{\psi}_{S}
, we first split the derivatives into the parallel and the orthogonal parts. For the parallel part, we have

\displaystyle\partial^{i}\psi_{S}\partial_{i}\bar{\psi}_{S}=(\sin\eta\partial^{i}\bar{\xi}+\cos\eta\partial^{i}\xi)(-\sin\eta\partial_{i}\xi+\cos\eta\partial_{i}\bar{\xi})=\partial^{i}\xi\partial_{i}\bar{\xi}\,.

(168)

For the orthogonal part, we use
\partial_{\texttt{x}}=\cos{\eta}\partial_{r}-\frac{\sin{\eta}}{r}\partial_{\eta}
and
\partial_{\texttt{y}}=\sin{\eta}\partial_{r}+\frac{\cos{\eta}}{r}\partial_{\eta}
. Then

\displaystyle\partial^{\perp}\psi_{S}\partial_{\perp}\bar{\psi}_{S}=

\displaystyle(\cos{\eta}\partial_{r}\psi_{S}-\frac{\sin{\eta}}{r}\partial_{\eta}\psi_{S})(\cos{\eta}\partial_{r}\bar{\psi}_{S}-\frac{\sin{\eta}}{r}\partial_{\eta}\bar{\psi}_{S})

\displaystyle+(\sin{\eta}\partial_{r}\psi_{S}+\frac{\cos{\eta}}{r}\partial_{\eta}\psi_{S})(\sin{\eta}\partial_{r}\bar{\psi}_{S}+\frac{\cos{\eta}}{r}\partial_{\eta}\bar{\psi}_{S})\,.

The terms proportional to
\partial_{r}\psi_{S}\partial_{r}\bar{\psi}
yield
\partial_{r}\xi\partial_{r}\bar{\xi}
. Moreover, the cross terms cancel, while the terms with
\partial_{\eta}\psi_{S}\partial_{\eta}\bar{\psi}_{S}
give

\frac{\mathrm{1}}{r^{2}}\partial_{\eta}\psi_{S}\partial_{\eta}\bar{\psi}_{S}=\frac{\mathrm{1}}{r^{2}}\partial_{\eta}(\sin{\eta}\bar{\xi}+\cos{\eta}\xi)\partial_{\eta}(-\sin{\eta}\xi+\cos{\eta}\bar{\xi})=\frac{\mathrm{\xi\bar{\xi}}}{r^{2}}\,.

(169)

Gathering all the terms, we conclude

S_{d-1}[\xi,\bar{\xi},\phi]=2\pi\int d^{d-2}x_{\|}\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\,\Bigl(\partial^{i}\xi\,\partial_{i}\bar{\xi}+\partial_{r}\xi\,\partial_{r}\bar{\xi}+\frac{\xi\bar{\xi}}{r^{2}}+\xi\bar{\xi}\,V^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{S})\Bigr)\,,

(170)

as reported in (
77
).

C.3
Q
-invariance of saddle point action

As a sanity check, in this section, we show the
Q
-invariance of the saddle point action given by

\displaystyle S[\varphi_{S}]

\displaystyle=\int d^{d}x\,\bigl(\partial^{i}\omega_{S}\partial_{i}\varphi_{S}+\omega_{S}V^{\prime}(\varphi_{S})+\partial^{\mu}\psi_{S}\partial_{\mu}\bar{\psi}_{S}+\psi_{S}\bar{\psi}_{S}V^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{S})\bigr)\,.

(171)

where the bosonic part is in an already simplified form (
71
).
Using the identities

\displaystyle Q\varphi_{S}=x\psi_{S}-y\bar{\psi}_{S}=r\xi\,,\qquad Q\omega_{S}=\partial_{y}\bar{\psi}_{S}-\partial_{x}\psi_{S}=-\partial_{r}\xi\,,

(172)

for the first term of the action, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int d^{d}x\,Q(\partial^{i}\omega_{S}\partial_{i}\varphi_{S})&=\int d^{d}x\,\bigl(\partial^{i}(Q\omega_{S})\partial_{i}\varphi_{S}+\partial^{i}\omega_{S}\partial_{i}(Q\varphi_{S})\bigr)\\
&=\int d^{d}x\,\bigl(-\partial^{i}(\partial_{r}\xi)\partial_{i}\phi-\partial^{i}\left(\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\phi\right)\partial_{i}(r\xi)\bigr)\\
&=\int d^{d}x\,\bigl(\partial^{i}\xi\partial_{i}\partial_{r}\phi-\partial^{i}\partial_{r}\phi\partial_{i}\xi\bigr)=0\,.\end{aligned}

where, in the third line, we used integration by parts.

For the second term of the action, we have

\displaystyle\int d^{d}x\,Q[\omega_{S}V^{\prime}\left(\varphi_{S}\right)]

\displaystyle=\int d^{d}x\,\bigl(V^{\prime}(\varphi_{S})Q\omega_{S}+\omega_{S}QV^{\prime}(\varphi_{S})\bigr)

(173)

\displaystyle=\int d^{d}x\,\bigl(-V^{\prime}(\varphi_{S})\partial_{r}\xi+\omega_{S}V^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{S})Q\varphi_{S}\bigr)

(174)

\displaystyle=\int d^{d}x\,\bigl(-V^{\prime}(\varphi_{S})\partial_{r}\xi-\partial_{r}V^{\prime}(\varphi_{S})\xi\bigr)=0\,,

(175)

where in the third line we have used
\omega_{S}=-\partial_{r}\varphi_{S}
and that
\partial_{r}\varphi_{S}V^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{S})=\partial_{r}V^{\prime}
.

Finally, for the fermionic part, using
Q\psi_{S}=Q\bar{\psi}_{S}=0
, directly gives us that the kinetic term vanishes. For the term with the potential, we get

\displaystyle Q[\psi_{S}\bar{\psi}_{S}V^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{S})]

\displaystyle=\psi_{S}\bar{\psi}_{S}QV^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{S})

(176)

\displaystyle=\psi_{S}\bar{\psi}_{S}V^{\prime\prime\prime}(\varphi_{S})Q\varphi_{S}

(177)

\displaystyle=\psi_{S}\bar{\psi}_{S}V^{\prime\prime\prime}(\varphi_{S})(x\psi_{S}-y\bar{\psi}_{S})=0\,,

(178)

since the fermions square to zero. This concludes the proof of
Q
-invariance of the saddle point action (
171
).

In this appendix, we discuss some extra details about the one-loop determinant computation performed in

First, let us show how the action

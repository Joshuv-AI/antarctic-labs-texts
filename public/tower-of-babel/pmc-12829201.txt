“Noninvasive predictive marker for esophageal variceal bleeding”
Authors: Shuo Zhang, Tomohiko Akahoshi, Tomoharu Yoshizumi, Masatoshi Eto
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveEsophageal varices are common in cirrhosis with portal hypertension; some patients face high risk of life-threatening esophageal variceal bleeding (EVB). Because few noninvasive markers predict EVB, this study sought to identify accessible predictors.MethodsFrom 2002 to 2018, 336 patients with high-risk or hemorrhagic esophageal varices were studied: 72 with recent variceal rupture/bleeding (EVB) and 264 without (EVNB). The study evaluated associations between EVB and several clinical scores and biomarkers, focusing on sarcopenia, Controlling Nutritional Status (CONUT) score, and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), alongside established indices.ResultsComparisons and univariate analyses were performed for MELD, CONUT, sarcopenia, fibrosis index, FIB-4, AST/ALT ratio, ALBI, and APRI between EVB and EVNB groups. Multivariate analysis identified the Neutrophil/Lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and CONUT score were identified as independent predictors associated with EVB. The CONUT score and NLR achieved AUROCs values were 0.75 and 0.65, with optimal cut-offs at 4.5 and 3.87, respectively. Notably, 58/72 (80.6%) EVB patients had CONUT > 4.5 and 31/72 (43.1%) had NLR > 3.87.ConclusionsComparing several noninvasive biomarkers and scoring systems, the study suggests CONUT ≥ 4.5 and NLR ≥ 3.87 as potential predictors of esophageal variceal bleeding. Because CONUT and NLR are routinely available and showed acceptable predictive performance, they may be practical tools for risk stratification in patients at risk for EVB.Keywords: Portal hypertension, Esophageal varices bleeding, Non-invasive examination, NLR: Neutrophil/Lymphocyte ratio, Controlling nutritional status (CONUT) score

Introduction

Esophageal varices (EV) are the main complication of patients with liver cirrhosis; most have portal hypertension [1–4]. EVs usually do not show significant symptoms or clinical signs until bleeding occurs. Therefore, early detection of risky EVs and prophylactic treatment is desirable.

The recommended examination for the diagnosis and evaluation of the risky esophageal varices is an upper gastrointestinal endoscopic examination. However, the endoscopic examination is uncomfortable and costly. Alternatively, hepatic wedge pressure measurement (HVPG) is another established method for evaluating the risk of EV bleeding (EVB) when its higher than 12 mmHg [4, 5]. However, HVPG measurement is invasive and expensive compared to endoscopic examination. Therefore, a noninvasive marker for the estimation of EVB is desirable. As a noninvasive marker, we, the clinician, have used the MELD score, and for motivation to recommend the patients to receive endoscopic examination or HVPG measurement. However, such score is not specific for the estimation of EVB. On the other hand, several non-invasive markers for predicting the existence of EV have been reported, such as the AST to platelet ratio index (APRI), the aspartate-aminotransferase-to-alanine-aminotransferase (AST-to-ALT) ratio (AAR), the Fibrosis-4-index (FIB-4), and the albumin-bilirubin (ALBI) score and the platelet count-to-spleen diameter (PC / SD) ratio [5–7]. However, noninvasive markers have been scarcely examined by comparison between non-bleeding EV and EVB. Moreover, sarcopenia, skeletal muscle mass and its function, and controlling nutritional status score (CONUT score), a definition based on albumin, total cholesterol, and lymphocyte count, have been recently reported to be associated with the degree of liver function, HCC occurrence, and prognosis in the patients with liver disease [8–12]. However, sarcopenia and CONUT score has not been examined for relevance to EVB. Kalra et al. demonstrate that an elevated NLR is associated with liver-related death independent of MELD and cirrhosis stage [13, 14], supporting its clinical utility in risk stratification. However, existing studies have not well explored whether NLR can specifically predict the occurrence of EVB. Therefore, this research sought to investigate whether recently identified markers, including sarcopenia and CONUT score can serve as significant factors in estimating EVB in patients with portal hypertension, evaluating their potential roles within a predictive model.

Patients characteristics and research methods

Three hundred and sixty-four patients were hospitalized at Kyushu University Hospital for the treatment and further observation of EV from April 2002 to May 2018. Thirteen patients aged below 20 years or above 80 years were unenrolled in this study. Considering that gastric varices differ significantly from esophageal varices in their pathophysiology, bleeding risk, and management, fifteen patients who had concomitant gastric variceal bleeding were excluded from this study. Finally, 336 patients were enrolled in this study, and all received endoscopic treatment for reasons of EVB or risky EV. The risky EVs were determined according to the Japanese portal hypertension rule by endoscopic examination. Their sizes greater than F2 and the existence of a red color sign were diagnosed as risky EVs. Bleeding EVs were also confirmed by emergent upper gastrointestinal endoscopy. Patients with acute bleeding EVs were transferred to the hospital by ambulance because of hemorrhagic symptoms. Patients with recent bleeding EVs were also transferred to our hospital for radical treatment. In the cohort of 336 patients, the main cause of the disease was cirrhosis related to hepatitis C, followed by cirrhosis related to hepatitis B (Table 1). Of these enrolled patients, there were 72 patients with bleeding EVs and 264 patients with risky EVs. The patients were separated (Fig. 1).

Study variables and measurements

The inflammation-associated biomarkers: NLR, MLR, PLR

The formula used to calculate NLR (Neutrophil/Lymphocyte ratio) is: Divide the absolute counting of neutrophil (103 cells/µL) by the absolute counting lymphocytes (103 cells/µL). Lymphocytes-to-monocytes ratio (LMR) was estimated by dividing the absolute lymphocyte count (103 cells/µL) by the absolute monocyte count (103 cells/µL). Platelets to lymphocytes ratio (PLR): the absolute counting platelets (109/L) divided by the absolute counting of lymphocytes (103 cells/µL). These values were calculated by the data of admission day in each patient [15, 16].

Sarcopenia

Skeletal Muscle Mass index (SMI) was frequently used to assess muscle mass in covered studies. It can be obtained through analysis of the cross-sectional area (cm2), skeletal muscle tissue in abdominal CT images at the level of the third lumbar vertebra (L3) were normalized by dividing by the square of the individual’s height. (cm2/m2) [17].

For some of the included studies, the cut-off points were determined based on the predictive capability of SMI for the overall survival of the study cohort, < 34.9cm2/m2, for females and for men, < 40.89cm2/m2 [18].

Controlling nutritional status score (CONUT)

The CONUT score was calculated based on three parameters: serum albumin level, absolute counting lymphocytes, and total cholesterol. For the assessment, CONUT scores are classified from scores 0 to 12. The CONUT score and malnutrition status have a negative relationship, patients with a higher CONUT score were concerned about a higher malnutrition status [19].

Fibrosis markers

Fibrosis index 4 (FIB4), APRI, FI, AST/ALT, and albumin-bilirubin score (ALBI) have been known as biomarkers estimating the degree of fibrosis associated with liver cirrhosis and its prognosis worldwide [20–22]. Since esophageal varices result from portal hypertension mainly due to liver fibrosis, we examined the relationship between these fibrosis markers and EVB.

The formulae of these fibrosis markers are followings.

FI: 0.01-8 ×platelet (109/L) - serum albumin (g/dL).

APRI: APRI = [(AST / AST_upper_limit_of_normal) / platelet count (109/L)] × 100 [23].

FIB-4 = (Age × AST) / (Platelets × √ALT).

ALBI = (log10 bilirubin [µmol/L] × 0.66) + (albumin [g/L] × −0.085) [24].

AAR: AST(U/L)/ALT(U/L).

Data analysis

All data’s statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism ver.8. The Shapiro-Wilk test was used to evaluate the normality of continuous variables. Data that followed a normal distribution are expressed as mean ± standard deviation and non-normally distributed data (medians and interquartile ranges (IQR), were assessed using the t-test and the Mann-Whitney U test, respectively. Categorical data are presented as counts and percentages and were evaluated using the chi-square test. Propensity score matching (1:1, nearest-neighbor, caliper = 0.2) was conducted to balance baseline albumin and cholesterol level between EVB and EVNB groups. Matching adequacy was assessed by the standardized mean difference (SMD < 0.10) [25]. All candidate variables underwent univariate logistic regression, where those exhibiting statistical significance (P < 0.05) were incorporated into the multivariate logistic regression analysis for the confirmation of independent predictors. The variance inflation factor (VIF) for each variable in the final model was calculated for the assessment of multicollinearity. Effect sizes are expressed as odds ratios (OR) accompanied by 95% confidence intervals (CI). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was conducted, alongside the calculation of the area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, positive and negative values. P < 0.05 indicated statistical significance.

Results

Comparison of baseline characteristics between the EVB patients and EVNB patients

The EVB group included 47 male and 25 female patients, while the EVNB group consisted of 163 males and 101 females (p = 0.681). No statistically significant differences in sex distribution were found within each group and average age as well. The most etiology of cirrhosis and HCC was hepatitis C in the EVB and EVNB groups. The secondary cause of the EVB group was different in both groups (Alcoholism (25%) in the EVB group and hepatitis B (22.4%) in the ENVB group) (Table 1). The size of esophageal varices did not show an obviously difference between EVB and EVNB patients.

Liver function

In Table 1, Albumin (ALB), total bilirubin (T-bilirubin) and prothrombin time percentage (PT%) differed a lot between the EVNB and EVB groups. Despite the absence of obvious differences in the prevalence of Encephalopathy the prevalence of ascites differed significantly (P < 0.001). EVB patients’ MELD scores were substantially enhanced than those of EVNB (13.7 ± 6.7 vs.10.8 ± 3.8; p < 0.001).

Sarcopenia

According to the previously mentioned definition of sarcopenia, 258 of 336 (76.8%) patients were diagnosed with the condition. Further analysis revealed that 59 of 72 (81.9%) patients had sarcopenia in the EVB group and 199 of 264 (75.4%) had sarcopenia in the ENVB group. Several studies have indicated that patients with liver cirrhosis may exhibit a decline in muscle mass. However, the findings of our study, suggest that there is no statistically significant association between esophageal variceal bleeding and the presence of sarcopenia. (Table 2).

Univariate logistic regression analysis and multivariate logistic regression analysis

Univariate logistic regression was initially performed for all candidate variables. Variables showing statistical significance in univariate analysis were subsequently included in the multivariate logistic regression model. To ensure the robustness of the multivariate model, we conducted a formal multicollinearity assessment using specifically, NLR was retained while LMR was excluded from the final multivariate model. In the multivariate logistic regression analysis, both the CONUT score (OR, 1.461; 95%CI,1.265–1.704; P < 0.001) and the NLR (OR,1.266; 95% CI 1.116–1.449; P < 0.001) emerged as significant independent predictors for EVB (Table 6).

The CONUT score and NLR were combined into a multivariable model, and its discriminatory performance was evaluated and compared against the individual predictors with ROC analysis, with the AUC for each presented in Fig. 3. Discussion

This study aimed to find a new noninvasive and specific marker for bleeding EV. An ideal noninvasive marker should be easy to obtain in daily medical assessments. The noninvasive marker is supposed to reduce the number of upper endoscopy examinations and hospitalization for the HVPG examination. As for predicting the existence of EV, there are several noninvasive markers, for instance, AST/ALT, APRI, platelet count-to-spleen diameter ratio (PC/SD), and Fibrosis-4 index (FIB-4) [26–28]. Especially, PC/SD and FIB-4 seem to be reliable noninvasive markers for predicting the existence of risky large Evs. However, there is less evidence of noninvasive biomarkers that directly predict EVB. Kraja et al. tried to examine several noninvasive markers, such as FIB-4, and PC/SD, however, they did not find any significant marker to discriminate the difference between the EVB and EVNB groups [29]. Mohammed et al. successfully showed that the HOMA-IR score is a useful noninvasive marker to predict EVB [30]. Notably, the HOMA-IR score was significantly related to HVPG. However, the HOMA-IR score required serum concentration of fasting insulin, which is not easy to obtain from an ordinary examination in an outpatient clinic. Furthermore, insurance does not cover the cost of the examination, unless the patients have diabetes mellitus. So far, liver dysfunction evaluated by MELD score has been a motivation for the clinician to recommend patients to receive endoscopic examination [31, 32]. As shown in our study, although the MELD score as remarkably lower in the EVB group than patients in EVNB group, it was not specific for estimating EVB according to our result of multivariate analysis. In the present study, we are especially concerned about sarcopenia, due to its growing recognition as a non-invasive biomarker for prognostic prediction, it has garnered increasing attention in clinical and research settings in several patients with hepatic disease [33]. According to our study, sarcopenia has not associated with EVB. Next, we evaluated other biomarkers reported in hepatic disease. As a result, NLR and CONUT scores were significant independent factors for discriminating EVB from EVNB. Previously, Zhang L et al. investigated the distinction between esophagogastric bleeding and peptic ulcer bleeding [34]. They found that the level of neutrophils and level of Lymphocytes and PLR is significantly increased in group peptic ulcer bleeding. In our study PLR between EVNB and EVB group have no significant difference. (p = 0.99). In other studies, AST/ALT and FI have been suggested to be noninvasive markers to estimate the presence of esophageal varices and episodes of variceal hemorrhage in individuals with liver cirrhosis [35, 36]. Therefore, we also evaluated these scores, however, these were not significant independent factors for discriminating EVB from EVNB. Of course, our patients with EVNB were all with risky varices, so the difference between EVB and EVNB is less compared to previous studies. Nevertheless, in our cohort, 58 of 72 (80.6%) patients in the EVB group had CONUT score > 4.5 or 31 EVB patients NLR > 3.87 (43.1%). The persistent association between elevated CONUT scores and EVB even after rigorously balancing baseline albumin and cholesterol levels via PSM, strengthens the clinical validity of this finding. It suggests that the CONUT score captures a pathophysiological dimension of malnutrition that independently contributes to he risk of variceal hemorrhage, beyond simple serum nutrient deficiencies. Considering our result, if we found a patient with cirrhosis with CONUT score > 4.5 or NLR > 3.87, the clinician may have better recommended the patient for endoscopic examination as soon as possible. Clinically significant portal hypertension (CSPH) represents a prognostic watershed in patients with advanced chronic liver disease (cACLD), marking the transition from a “relatively safe phase” to a “high-risk phase for the complications”. The Baveno VII consensus introduced non-invasive criteria for ruling out CSPH : a combination of liver stiffness measurement (LSM) ≤ 15 kPa and platelet count ≥ 150 × 109 /L [37]. This indicates that patients meeting both criteria have a very low risk of complications and may temporarily forgo endoscopic surveillance. While LSM and platelet count serve as a highly effective combination for predicting CSPH, which is a prerequisite for variceal bleeding, these non-invasive predictors of CSPH may lack sensitivity and exhibit a lag in predictive value during the clinical progression toward EVB. Therefore, identifying more direct and sensitive non-invasive indictors for predicting EVB is crucial. This study demonstrates the significant value of the CONUT score and NLR in predicting EVB. These two markers may, potentially serve as a future standard combination for predicting EVB occurrence, holding significant clinical implications for risk stratification.

This study acknowledges limitations that suggest promising directions for future inquiry. First the retrospective, single-center design may induce selection bias and restrict our findings from being generalized to other studies. Second, the cohort was primarily composed of patients with viral hepatitis, which may not fully represent global populations with more diverse etiologies such as metabolic and alcohol-related liver diseases. Third, residual confounding may persist due to baseline imbalances between the study groups, despite statistical adjustments. Despite above limitations, the findings in this study contribute to new insights and lay a valuable foundation for future prospective, multi-center validation studies.

Conclusion

Collectively, the CONUT score and NLR are the strongest non-invasive predictors of EVB among the biomarkers we evaluated. Using cut off values of 4.5 for CONUT score and 3.87 for NLR could help identify high-risk Esophageal variceal patients. However, future prospective, multi-center studies should be conducted to verify above findings. Given that both markers are readily available from routine blood tests. The CONUT score and NLR represent practical and economical tools for stratifying risk in patients with suspected EVB.

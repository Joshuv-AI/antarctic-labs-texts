Polynomial progressions in the generalized twin primes
Andrew Lott, Nagendar Reddy Ponagandla

Abstract
By Maynard’s theorem and the subsequent improvements by the Polymath Project, there exists a positive integer b ≤ 246 b\leq 246 such that there are infinitely many primes p p such that p + b p+b is also prime. Let P 1 , … , P t ∈ ℤ ⁡ [ y ] P_{1},...,P_{t}\in\mathbb{Z}[y] with P 1 ​ ( 0 ) = ⋯ = P t ​ ( 0 ) = 0 P_{1}(0)=\cdots=P_{t}(0)=0 . We use the transference argument of Tao and Ziegler to prove there exist positive integers x , y , x,y, and b ≤ 246 b\leq 246 such that x + P 1 ​ ( y ) , x + P 2 ​ ( y ) , … , x + P t ​ ( y ) x+P_{1}(y),x+P_{2}(y),...,x+P_{t}(y) and x + P 1 ​ ( y ) + b , x + P 2 ​ ( y ) + b , … , x + P t ​ ( y ) + b x+P_{1}(y)+b,x+P_{2}(y)+b,...,x+P_{t}(y)+b are all prime. Our work is inspired by Pintz, who proved a similar result for the special case of arithmetic progressions.

Introduction

In their landmark paper
[
6
]
, Green and Tao used the transference principle and Szemerédi’s theorem to prove that the primes contain arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions. A key component of their proof was the construction of a pseudorandom measure inspired by the Goldston-Yildirim sieve
[
5
]
. This groundbreaking result has since motivated numerous generalizations. Notably, Tao and Ziegler
[
14
]
showed that the primes also contain polynomial progressions of the form

x+P_{1}(y),\ x+P_{2}(y),\ \dots,\ x+P_{t}(y),

where
P_{1},\dots,P_{t}\in\mathbb{Z}[y]
and
P_{1}(0)=\cdots=P_{t}(0)=0
. One of the main ingredients in their proof was a relative PET induction scheme.

Since the release of
[
6
]
and
[
14
]
, many simplifications have been developed. Notably, Gowers found a much shorter way to perform the transference step using polynomial approximation and the geometric Hahn–Banach theorem
[
3
]
, and Conlon-Fox-Zhao introduced a densification technique which further streamlined the transference step
[
2
]
.

Incorporating these simplifications, in
[
7
]
, Lott-Magyar-Petridis-Pintz generalized the result of Tao and Ziegler to certain non-degenerate polynomial configurations in the prime lattice. Much of our current paper is based on
[
7
]
.

Rather than seeking new patterns in the primes, one might instead ask for patterns in other sparse subsets of
\mathbb{N}
. Inspired by the approach of Green and Tao, Pintz used a transference argument to prove the following theorem in
[
10
]
.

Theorem A
.

Let
k,j\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\{h_{1},...,h_{k}\}
be an admissible
k
-tuple. Suppose there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
for which

|\mathcal{A}\cap[N]|\gg\frac{N}{\log^{k}N},

where

\mathcal{A}\subset\{n:P^{-}((n+h_{1})\cdots(n+h_{k}))>n^{\varepsilon_{0}}\}.

Then
\mathcal{A}
contains a
j
-term arithmetic progression.

Recall that a set
\{h_{1},...,h_{k}\}
of distinct non-negative integers is admissible if, for
every prime
p
, there is an integer
a_{p}
such that
a_{p}\not\equiv h\bmod{p}
for all

.

When
k=\epsilon_{0}=1
, Theorem A recovers the Green–Tao theorem. For
k=2
and
\epsilon_{0}=1/10
, this recovers a result of Zhou, which gives arithmetic progressions in the Chen primes
[
17
]
. (Zhou defined the Chen primes to be the set of primes

is either a prime or a product of two primes and

.) Historically, verifying that

posed a major challenge. However, now we have the following version of Maynard’s theorem, which was proved by the Polymath Project (see Remark 32 in
[

]

Let

. There exists

k

, there exists

such that

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\{n:

\displaystyle P^{-}((n+h_{1})\cdots(n+h_{k}))>n^{\varepsilon_{0}}\text{ and}

(1.1)

\displaystyle\text{at least }m+1\text{ of }n+h_{1},\ldots,n+h_{k}\text{ are prime}\}.

3
The W-trick and the pseudorandom measure

Our work in this section is inspired by Pintz
[
10
]
. However, we define our pseudorandom measure in a way that allows us to use Fourier analysis, which is similar to the approach of Tao and Ziegler
[
14
,
15
]
. Let
A\subset|\mathcal{A}\cap[N^{\prime}]|
and
|A|=\delta|\mathcal{A}\cap[N^{\prime}]|
. Thus, by theorem B,

|A|\geq\delta\frac{C_{1}N^{\prime}}{\log^{k}N^{\prime}},

(3.1)

for some
C_{1}>0
. We begin by applying the
W
-trick in order to account for the fact that
\mathcal{A}
is not equidistributed mod
p
for any prime
p
. To fix this issue, we restrict
A
to unit congruence class
b
modulo
W
, where
W
is a product of small primes, and
b\not\equiv-h_{i}\pmod{W}
for any
1\leq i\leq k
. Specifically, let
w=\log\log\log N^{\prime}
and
W=\prod_{\text{p-prime},p\leq w}p
. Set
N=\lfloor N^{\prime}/\penalty W\rfloor
. Let

X_{W}=\{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{W}:b\not\equiv-h_{i}\pmod{W}\text{ for every }1\leq i\leq k.\}

and

\Omega_{H}(p)=\{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}:b\not\equiv-h_{i}\pmod{p}\text{ for every }1\leq i\leq k.\}

By the Chinese remainder theorem, we may count the elements of
X_{W}
in the following way (here
L=\max\{h_{1},...,h_{k}\}
)

\displaystyle|X_{W}|

\displaystyle=W\prod_{p<L}\left(1-\frac{|\Omega_{H}(p)|}{p}\right)\prod_{L<p\leq w}\left(1-\frac{k}{p}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}W\prod_{1\leq p\leq w}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=C_{2}W\left(\frac{\phi(W)}{W}\right)^{k},

where
C_{2}
is a constant depending only on
L
. By (
3.1
),

|A|\geq\delta\frac{C_{1}NW}{\log^{k}N}.

Thus, it is not hard to see that

\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{W}}|\{n\in[\sqrt{N},N-\sqrt{N}]:nW+b\in A\}|\geq\delta\frac{C_{1}NW}{2\log^{k}N}

as well. But, if
b\not\in X_{W}
and
n\in[\sqrt{N},N-\sqrt{N}]
, then
b+h_{i}\equiv{0}\pmod{p}
for some
p<w
. So

P^{-}(Wn+b+h_{i})<w<(nW+b)^{\varepsilon_{0}}

since
n\geq\sqrt{N}
and
w
is small. Thus,
Wn+b\not\in A
. Hence,

\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{W}}|\{n\in[\sqrt{N},N-\sqrt{N}]:nW+b\in A\}|=\sum_{b\in X_{W}}|\{n\in[\sqrt{N},N-\sqrt{N}]:nW+b\in A\}|.

It follows by the pigeonhole principle that there exists
b\in X_{W}
such that

|\{n\in[\sqrt{N},N-\sqrt{N}]:nW+b\in A\}|\geq\delta\frac{C_{1}}{2C_{2}}\left(\frac{W}{\phi(W)}\right)^{k}\frac{N}{\log^{k}N}.

Now, we define a normalized indicator function for
A
restricted to
b\pmod{W}
as follows

f_{A}(x)=c_{0}\left(\frac{\phi(W)\log N}{W}\right)^{k}\mathbf{1}_{A}(Wx+b)\mathbf{1}_{[\sqrt{N},N-\sqrt{N}]}(x),

(3.2)

where
c_{0}>0
is a small constant to be defined later. Then, we immediately have

\mathbb{E}_{x\in[N]}f_{A}(x)\geq c\delta,

(3.3)

where
c=\frac{c_{0}C_{1}}{2C_{2}}
.
Next we have our pseudorandom measure
\nu_{\mathcal{A}}
, defined as follows:

\nu_{\mathcal{A}}(x)=\left(\frac{\phi(W)\log R}{W}\right)^{k}\prod_{1\leq i\leq k}\left(\sum_{d_{i}|Wx+b+h_{i}}\mu(d_{i})\chi\left(\frac{\log d_{i}}{\log R}\right)\right)^{2},

(3.4)

where
R=N^{\eta_{0}}
for some sufficiently small
\eta_{0}
to be chosen later and
\chi
is a smooth, compactly supported on
[-1,1]
,
\chi(0)>1/\penalty 2
, and
\int_{0}^{1}|\chi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}dt=1
. Crucially, we may choose
c_{0}
small enough that
f_{A}
is bounded point-wise by
\nu_{\mathcal{A}}
:

0\leq f_{A}\leq\nu_{\mathcal{A}}.

(3.5)

Indeed, if
f_{A}(x)
is positive, then

P^{-}(Wx+b+h_{i})\geq x^{\varepsilon_{0}}\geq N^{\varepsilon_{0}/\penalty 2}>R

as long as
\eta_{0}<\varepsilon_{0}/\penalty 2
, and thus,

\nu_{\mathcal{A}}(x)\geq\frac{\eta_{0}^{k}}{4^{k}}\cdot\left(\frac{\phi(W)\log N}{W}\right)^{k},

so it suffices to take
c_{0}<\frac{\eta_{0}^{k}}{4^{k}}
.

4
The correlation estimate

Here we derive a correlation estimate which is the key to proving the pseudorandomness property for
\nu_{\mathcal{A}}
. Our proof is a close adaptation of the proof of corollary 10.5 in
[
14
]
. For this, we need to record some definitions from
[
14
]
.

Definition 4.1

(Local factor)
.

Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{J}:\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{D}]
be polynomials with integer coefficients. For any prime
p
, we define
the (principal)
local factor

c_{p}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{J}):=\mathbb{E}_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{D}}\prod_{j\in[J]}\mathbf{1}_{P_{j}(x)\equiv 0\bmod p}.

Definition 4.2

(Good prime)
.

Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{J}\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{D}]
be a collection of polynomials. We say that a prime
p
is
good
with respect to
P_{1},\ldots,P_{J}
if the following hold:

•

The polynomials
P_{1}\bmod p,\ldots,P_{J}\bmod p
are pairwise coprime.

•

For each
1\leq j\leq J
, there exists a co-ordinate
1\leq i_{j}\leq D
for which we have the linear behavior

\displaystyle P_{j}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{D})

\displaystyle=P_{j,1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i_{j}-1},x_{i_{j}+1},\ldots,x_{D})x_{i_{j}}

\displaystyle\quad+P_{j,0}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i_{j}-1},x_{i_{j}+1},\ldots,x_{D})\bmod p

where
P_{j,1},P_{j,0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{i_{j}-1},x_{i_{j}+1},\ldots,x_{D}]
are such that
P_{j,1}
is non-zero and coprime to
P_{j,0}
.

We say that a prime is
bad
if it is not good. We say that a prime is
terrible
if at least one of the
P_{j}
vanish identically modulo
p
(i.e. all the coefficients are divisible by
p
). Note that terrible primes are automatically bad.

We also need the following estimates which follow from some “elementary” algebra in
[
14
, Appendix D]

Lemma 4.1

(Local estimates)
.

Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{J}\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{D}]
have degree at most
d
, let
p
be a prime, and let
S\subset\{1,\ldots,J\}
. Then:

(a)

If
|S|=0
, then
c_{p}((P_{j})_{j\in S})=1
.

(b)

If
|S|\geq 1
and
p
is not terrible, then
c_{p}((P_{j})_{j\in S})=O_{d,D,J}(\frac{1}{p})
.

(c)

If
|S|=1
and
p
is good, then
c_{p}((P_{j})_{j\in S})=\frac{1}{p}+O_{d,D,J}(\frac{1}{p^{2}})
.

(d)

If
|S|>1
and
p
is good, then
c_{p}((P_{j})_{j\in S})=O_{d,D,J}(\frac{1}{p^{2}})
.

Let

(Euler product estimate) We have

\displaystyle\prod_{p<w}

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))\prod_{p<w}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-kJ}

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))\left(\frac{W}{\phi(W)}\right)^{kJ}.

(This is where we used the fact that
b\not\equiv-h_{i}\pmod{W}
for any
1\leq i\leq k
.)

To estimate
\displaystyle{\prod_{w\leq p\leq R^{\log R}}\frac{E_{p}}{E_{p}^{\prime}}}
, we divide primes in
w\leq p\leq R^{\log R}
into the good, bad, and terrible primes with respect to the polynomials
P_{ij}
. The bad primes
w\leq p\leq R^{\log R}
are in
\mathbb{P}_{b}
, and we clearly have no terrible primes
p\geq w
, since in this case
p
does not divide
W
.

If
p
is bad,
\displaystyle{c_{p}((W(y+r_{j})+b+h_{i})_{r_{ij}=1})=O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)}
from Lemma 9.5 of
[
14
]
. So,

E_{p}=1+O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)=\exp\left(O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)E_{p}^{\prime}

and

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}{w\leq p\leq R^{\log R}}\\
{p-\text{bad}}\end{subarray}}\frac{E_{p}}{E_{p}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}{w\leq p\leq R^{\log R}}\\
{p-\text{bad}}\end{subarray}}\exp\left(O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{w\leq p\leq R^{\log R}}\\
{p-\text{bad}}\end{subarray}}O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=1+O\left(\text{Exp}\left(O\left(\sum_{p\in\mathbb{P}_{b}}\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)\right).

For good
p
, again using Lemma 9.5 of
[
14
]
, we have

\displaystyle E_{p}=\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\right)E_{p}^{\prime},

and

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}{w\leq p\leq R^{\log R}}\\
{p-\text{good}}\end{subarray}}\frac{E_{p}}{E_{p}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}{w\leq p\leq R^{\log R}}\\
{p-\text{good}}\end{subarray}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=1+o(1).

as
w\rightarrow\infty
. Putting all the cases of
p
together, we have the result.
∎

Using some standard facts about the Riemann Zeta function we can write

\displaystyle\prod_{p\leq R^{\log R}}E_{p}^{\prime}=\prod_{i,j}\left(1+o(1)\right)\frac{\zeta(1+z_{ij}+z_{ij}^{\prime})}{\zeta(1+z_{ij})\zeta(1+z_{ij}^{\prime})},

and we also have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta(1+(1+i\xi)/\log R)}

\displaystyle=(1+o((1+|\xi|)^{2}))\frac{1+i\xi}{\log R}

\displaystyle\zeta(1+(1+i\xi)/\log R)

\displaystyle=(1+o((1+|\xi|)^{2}))\frac{\log R}{1+i\xi}

for any real
\xi
, so we get

\displaystyle\prod_{p\leq R^{\log R}}E^{\prime}_{p}=\prod_{i,j}(1+o((1+|\xi_{ij}|+|\xi^{\prime}_{ij}|)^{6}))\frac{1}{\log R}\frac{(1+i\xi_{ij})(1+i\xi^{\prime}_{ij})}{2+i\xi_{ij}+i\xi^{\prime}_{ij}}.

Applying Lemma 4.2 gives

\displaystyle S\cdot\prod_{p\leq R^{\log R}}E_{p}=\left(1+o\left(\prod_{i,j}(1+|\xi_{ij}|+|\xi^{\prime}_{ij}|)^{6}\right)+O\left(\text{Exp}\left(O\left(\sum_{p\in\mathbb{P}_{b}}\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\times\prod_{i,j}\frac{(1+i\xi_{ij})(1+i\xi^{\prime}_{ij})}{2+i\xi_{ij}+i\xi^{\prime}_{ij}}.

Thus, to prove Proposition 4.1, it is enough to show that

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\prod_{i,j}\varphi(\xi_{ij})\varphi(\xi^{\prime}_{ij})\frac{(1+i\xi_{ij})(1+i\xi^{\prime}_{ij})}{2+i\xi_{ij}+i\xi^{\prime}_{ij}}\ d\xi_{ij}d\xi^{\prime}_{ij}=1.

and

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\prod_{i,j}|\varphi(\xi_{ij})||\varphi(\xi^{\prime}_{ij})|(1+|\xi_{ij}|+|\xi^{\prime}_{ij}|)^{6}\frac{|1+i\xi_{ij}||1+i\xi^{\prime}_{ij}|}{|2+i\xi_{ij}+i\xi^{\prime}_{ij}|}\ d\xi_{ij}d\xi^{\prime}_{ij}=O(1).

We get the first equation from (
4.3
) and the fact that
\int_{0}^{1}|\chi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}dt=1
, and the second equation follows from the rapid decay of
\varphi
.
∎

5
The polynomial forms condition

Now that we have the correlation estimate, we can establish the fundamental pseudorandomness property for
\nu_{\mathcal{A}}
, the polynomial forms condition as stated in
[
7
]
. This formulation allows us to circumvent the convex geometry in
[
14
,
15
]
. However, the ideas in the proof are still essentially the same as in
[
14
,
15
]
.

Proposition 5.1

(Polynomial forms condition)
.

Let
d,D,J
be fixed natural numbers, let
\varepsilon_{0}
sufficiently small and
L
sufficiently large depending on
d,D,J
. Let
Q_{1},...,Q_{J}\in\mathbb{Z}[m_{1},...,m_{d}]
be polynomials of degree at most
D
, with coefficients of size
O(W^{C})
. Then

\mathbb{E}_{\vec{\ell}\in[H]^{d}}\ \mathbb{E}_{x\in[N]}\prod_{j=1}^{J}\nu_{\mathcal{A}}(x+Q_{j}(\vec{\ell}))=1+o(1),

(5.1)

where
H=\log^{\sqrt{L}}N
.

Proof.

By the correlation estimate, for each fixed
\vec{\ell}
we have

\mathbb{E}_{x\in[N]}\prod_{j=1}^{J}T^{Q_{j}(\vec{\ell})}\nu_{\mathcal{A}}=1+o(1)+O\left(\text{Exp}\left(O\left(\sum_{p\in\mathbb{P}_{b}(\vec{\ell})}\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)\right).

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{b}(\vec{\ell})

\displaystyle=\{w<p\leq R^{\log R}:p\mid(WQ_{j}(\vec{\ell})+h_{i})-(WQ_{j^{\prime}}(\vec{\ell})+h_{i^{\prime}})\text{ for some }(i,j)\neq(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{1\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq k}\{w<p\leq R^{\log R}:p\mid(WQ_{j}(\vec{\ell})+h_{i})-(WQ_{j^{\prime}}(\vec{\ell})+h_{i^{\prime}})\text{ for some }j\neq j^{\prime}\},

where we made the observation that if
j=j^{\prime}
, then
p\mid(WQ_{j}(\vec{\ell})+h_{i})-(WQ_{j^{\prime}}(\vec{\ell})+h_{i^{\prime}})
forces
p\mid h_{i}-h_{i^{\prime}}
, which in turn forces
i=i^{\prime}
, since
p>w>\max|h_{i}-h_{i^{\prime}}|
for large enough
N
. Thus, it suffices to show

\mathbb{E}_{\vec{\ell}\in[H]^{d}}O\left(\text{Exp}\left(O\left(\sum_{p\in\mathbb{P}_{b}(\vec{\ell})}\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)\right)=o(1)

for each fixed
\vec{\ell}
. Let

S(i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime},\vec{\ell}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w<p\leq R^{\log R}\\
p\mid(WQ_{j}(\vec{\ell})+h_{i})-(WQ_{j^{\prime}}(\vec{\ell})+h_{i^{\prime}})\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}.

Notice that

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\mathbb{P}_{b}(\vec{\ell})}\frac{1}{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(i,j)\neq(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})}S(i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime},\vec{\ell})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{1\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq k}\sum_{1\leq j<j^{\prime}\leq J}S(i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime},\vec{\ell}).

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\vec{\ell}\in[H]^{d}}O\left(\text{Exp}\left(O\left(\sum_{p\in\mathbb{P}_{b}(\vec{\ell})}\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\vec{\ell}\in[H]^{d}}O\left(\text{Exp}\left(O\left(\sum_{1\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq k}\sum_{1\leq j<j^{\prime}\leq J}S(i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime},\vec{\ell})\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{1\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq k}\sum_{1\leq j<j^{\prime}\leq J}\mathbb{E}_{\vec{\ell}\in[H]^{d}}O\left(\text{Exp}\left(O(S(i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime},\vec{\ell})\right)\right),

where we repeatedly applied the bound
\text{Exp}(a+b)\ll\text{Exp}(2a)+\text{Exp}(2b)
. Thus, it suffices to show

\mathbb{E}_{\vec{\ell}\in[H]^{d}}\text{Exp}\left(O\left(S(i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime},\vec{\ell})\right)\right)=o(1),

(5.2)

for each fixed
1\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq k
and
1\leq j<j^{\prime}\leq J
.

To estimate the (
5.2
) first consider set
B
of the primes
p\geq w
for which the polynomial
\Delta Q(\vec{\ell}):=(WQ_{j}(\vec{\ell})+h_{i})-(WQ_{j^{\prime}}(\vec{\ell})+h_{i^{\prime}})
identically vanishes
\pmod{p}
. Since all such primes
p
must divide the coefficients of
\Delta Q
which are of size
O(W^{C})
we have

w^{|B|}\leq\prod_{p\in B}p\ll W^{C}\ll e^{C\,w},

by the prime number theorem, thus

\sum_{p\in B}\ \frac{1}{p}\,\leq\,\frac{|B|}{w}\,\ll\frac{1}{\log\,w}=o(1),

so these primes have negligible contribution to (
5.2
).

By
[
14
, Lemma E.1]
, we have the following for any set
E
of primes:

\text{Exp}\left(O\left(\sum_{p\in E}\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)\ll\sum_{p\in E}\frac{\log^{O(1)}p}{p}.

Thus, it suffices to prove

\mathbb{E}_{\vec{\ell}\in[H]^{d}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w<p\leq R^{\log R},p\not\in B,p\mid\Delta Q(\vec{\ell})\end{subarray}}\frac{\log^{O(1)}p}{p}=o(1).

(5.3)

Next, we partition the sum in
p\notin B
into dyadic intervals
2^{s}\leq p<2^{s+1}
, and estimate the contribution of each part depending the size of the parameter
s\in\mathbb{N}
.

Assume first that
2^{s/2}\leq\log\,H=\sqrt{L}\,\log\log\,N
. Since the polynomial
\Delta Q
has degree at least 1
\pmod{p}
, and
p
is much smaller than
H
when
p<2^{s+1}
, the number of
\vec{\ell}\in[H]^{d}
satisfying
p|\Delta Q(\vec{\ell})
is at most
H^{d}/p+O(1)
, thus

\mathbb{E}_{\vec{\ell}\in[H]^{d}}\sum_{2^{s}\leq p<2^{s+1}\,p\not\in B,\,p|\Delta Q(\vec{\ell})}\ \frac{\log^{O(1)}p}{p}\ \leq\ \frac{s^{O(1)}}{2^{s}}+O(H^{-1/\penalty 2}).

(5.4)

Assume now
2^{s/2}\geq\log H
. Then left side of (
5.4
) is estimated by

\displaystyle\frac{s^{O(1)}}{2^{s}}

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}_{\vec{\ell}\in[H]^{d}}\,|\{2^{s}\leq p<2^{s+1};\ p|\Delta Q(\vec{\ell}),p\not\in B\}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{s^{O(1)}D\log\,H}{\,2^{s}}+s^{O(1)}O(H^{-1})

\displaystyle\ll\frac{s^{O(1)}}{2^{s/2}}+O(H^{-1/\penalty 2}).

(5.5)

Here we used the well-known fact that
|\{\vec{\ell}\in[H]^{d},\Delta Q(\vec{\ell})=0\}|=O(H^{d-1})
, and if
1\leq|\Delta Q(\vec{\ell})|\leq H^{D}
, then

\ \prod_{p|\Delta Q(\vec{\ell})}p\leq H^{D}\
, so the number of primes
p\geq 2^{s}
,
p|\Delta Q(\vec{\ell})
is
O(D\log H/s)
.

Therefore, by (
5.4
) and (
5.5
), we can sum (
5.3
) over dyadic intervals to conclude that it is
O(w^{-1/\penalty 4})=o(1)
. This completes the proof.

∎

6
Proof of the main theorem via the transference principle

We apply the transference principle to complete the proof of Theorem 1. Informally, the idea is that our relatively dense subset
A\subset\mathcal{A}
can be modeled by a dense subset
B\subset\mathbb{N}
. Since
B
has positive density, it contains polynomial progressions by a theorem of Bergelson and Liebman
[
1
]
. Hence, we expect
A
to inherit similar structure. While the actual argument is more delicate, this heuristic provides a helpful mental picture.

We need the following important piece of notation. Let
P_{1},...,P_{t}\in\mathbb{Z}[y]
and
f:[N]\to\mathbb{R}
. We define the normalized counting operator
\Lambda_{P_{1},...,P_{t}}
by

\Lambda_{P_{1},....,P_{t}}f:=\mathbb{E}_{y\in[M]}\mathbb{E}_{x\in[N]}f(x+P_{1}(y))\cdots f(x+P_{t}(y)),

where
M=\log^{L}N.
Notice that if
f=\mathbf{1}_{B}
is the indicator function for
B\subset[N]
, then
\Lambda_{P_{1},....,P_{t}}f
gives a normalized count of polynomial progressions in
B
.

The main ingredient of obtaining polynomial patterns in relative dense subsets
\mathcal{A}
is using an appropriate version of the polynomial extension of Szemerédi’s theorem due to Bergelson-Leibman
[
1
]
. The version we use, given in
[
14
,
15
]
, is as follows.

Theorem C
.

Let
\delta>0
,
P_{1},...,P_{t}\in\mathbb{Z}[y]
,
P_{1}(0)=\cdots=P_{t}(0)=0
. Let
N>N(\delta)
. If
g:[N]\to[0,1]
is a function satisfying
\mathbb{E}_{x\in[N]}g(x)\geq\delta
, then one has

\Lambda_{P_{1},...,P_{t}}g\geq c(\delta)-o(1),

(6.1)

where
c(\delta)>0
is a constant, that depends only on
\delta
.

Thus, if
f_{A}
as defined in (
3.2
) were bounded, then we would be done. Unfortunately, this is not the case, so we need the following transference theorem, which is a combination of corollaries
3.1
and
4.2
in
[
7
]
. The proofs of these corollaries require countless applications of the polynomial forms condition.

Theorem D
.

Let
f:[N]\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy
0\leq f\leq\nu,
where
\nu
satisfies the polynomial forms condition, and let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists functions
0\leq g\leq 1
such that

\left|\Lambda_{P_{1},....,P_{t}}f-\Lambda_{P_{1},....,P_{t}}g\right|<\epsilon\hbox{ and }\left|\mathbb{E}_{x\in[N]}f(x)-\mathbb{E}_{x\in[N]}g(x)\right|<\epsilon.

Now we may prove our main result.

Proof of Theorem 1.

Given
A\subseteq\mathcal{A}\cap[N^{\prime}]
with
|A|\geq\delta\,|\mathcal{A}\cap[N^{\prime}]|
we choose
W
,
N=[N^{\prime}/W]
,
b
and the function
f_{A}
as in section
3
. Thus, we have (
3.3
) and (
3.5
):

\mathbb{E}_{x\in[N]}\,f_{A}(x)\geq\delta^{\prime},\quad 0\leq f_{A}(x)\leq\nu_{\mathcal{A}}(x),

with
\delta^{\prime}=c\delta
, and
\nu_{\mathcal{A}}
satisfies the polynomial forms condition by proposition 5.1. Therefore, by theorems
C
and
D
with
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small with respect to
\delta^{\prime}
and
c(\delta^{\prime}/\penalty 2)
, we have a bounded
g:[N]\to[0,1]
such that

\Lambda_{P_{1},...,P_{t}}f_{A}\geq\Lambda_{P_{1},...,P_{t}}g-\varepsilon-o(1)\geq c(\delta^{\prime}/\penalty 2)-\varepsilon-o(1)\geq c(\delta^{\prime}/\penalty 2)/2-o(1)>0,

as long as
L\geq L(P_{1},...,P_{t})
and
N\geq N(\delta,P_{1},...,P_{t})
is sufficiently large.
∎

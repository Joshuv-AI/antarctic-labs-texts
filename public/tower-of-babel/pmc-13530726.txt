Poster 43. No Difference in Short-Term Failure Risk of Patellar Tendon Autograft and Large Hamstring Tendon Autograft in Adolescents Undergoing Anterior Cruciate Ligament Reconstruction
Authors: Robert A Magnussen, William A Cantrell, Zeid Habbab, Lauren Han, Sanam Jhaveri, Jimmy Le, Kyle Huntley, Eric M Milliron, Parker A Cavendish, Tyler Barker, David Flanigan, Nicholas Apseloff, Christopher C Kaeding
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective:While allograft ACL reconstruction (ACLR) is known to have higher graft failure in the adolescent population, there is no clear consensus on optimal autograft selection in adolescents undergoing ACLR. In young active patients, the MOON risk calculator suggests hamstring tendon (HT) autografts are more likely than patellar bone-tendon-bone BTB autografts to result in re-rupture. In similar young active populations, HT graft failure risk is dependent on graft size, with larger grafts having a lower failure risk. Therefore, our objective is to compare revision rates between BTB and HT autografts >=8 mm in diameter in adolescent patients undergoing isolated ACLR. We hypothesize large-diameter HT autografts demonstrate comparable failure risk to BTB autograftsMethods:Retrospective review identified 289 adolescent subjects (age 13-19) who underwent primary BTB or HT autograft ACLR at a single academic medical center between January 2018 and June 2023. All HT autografts were >= 8 mm, and no cases incorporated lateral extra-articular procedures. Failure risk was compared between graft types and subgroup analysis was performed based on patient sex.Results:Among 289 subjects, 155 (53.6%) received BTB autografts and 134 (46.4%) received HT autografts. There were 161 males and 128 females (56% male, 44% female), the mean age at surgery was 17.8 ± 1.4 years, and the mean BMI was 25.3 ± 5.4 kg/m². There were no differences in patients' sex (p=0.47) or BMI (p = 0.38) based on graft type, but the BTB groups was slightly older (18.0 ± 1.2 years) than the HT group (17.5 ± 1.6) (p<0.001). Mean follow-up was 16 months. Overall, revision surgery occurred in 23 patients (8.0%) with no difference noted between BTB autograft (8.4%, 13/155) and HT autograft (7.5%, 10/134) (p=0.83). When compared by gender, males were almost three times more likely than females to undergo revision overall (male=11.2% vs female=3.9% p=0.028). This difference was almost 5-fold in the BTB group (male=13.2% vs female=2.8%; p=0.021). In the HT group, there was no statistical significance in risk of revision between males and females (male=9.0% vs female=5.4%; p=0.52). However, on subgroup analysis within each gender, there was no increased risk of revision surgery based on graft type. In male patients, revision surgery was performed in 11 of 83 patients (13.3%) treated with BTB autograft and 7 of 78 patients (9.0%) treated with HT autograft (p=0.46). In female patients, revision surgery was performed in 2 of 72 patients (2.8%) treated with BTB autograft and 3 of 56 patients (5.4%) treated with HT autograft (p=0.65).Conclusions:Among adolescent patients undergoing ACLR with HT autograft greater than 8mm in diameter or BTB autograft, no difference in failure risk was noted at short-term follow-up. Males had a higher risk of revision surgery risk compared to females, especially among the BTB group.

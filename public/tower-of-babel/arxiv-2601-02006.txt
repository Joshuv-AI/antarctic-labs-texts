Global Hilbert expansion for the ionic Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system
fucai Li, Yichun Wang

Abstract
. We justify the global-in-time validity of Hilbert expansion for the ionic Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} , a fundamental model describing ion dynamics in dilute collisional plasmas. As the Knudsen number approaches zero, we rigorously derive the compressible Euler-Poisson system governing global smooth irrotational ion flows. The truncated Hilbert expansion exhibits a multi-layered mathematical structure: the expansion coefficients satisfy linear hyperbolic systems, while the remainder equation couples with a nonlinear Poisson equation for the electrostatic potential. This requires refined elliptic estimates addressing the exponential nonlinearities and some new enclosed L 2 ∩ W 1 , ∞ L^{2}\cap W^{1,\infty} estimates for the potential-dependent terms.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The model and our results

The kinetic evolution of ion particles in dilute collisional plasmas is governed by the ionic Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}F+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F-\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}F=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}Q(F,F),\\
&\Delta\phi=e^{\phi}-\rho,\quad\rho=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F{\rm d}v,\end{aligned}\right.

where
F=F(t,x,v)
is the ion distribution function with the time
t\geq 0
, position
x=(x^{1},x^{2},x^{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and velocity
v=(v^{1},v^{2},v^{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
The self-consistent electrostatic potential
\phi(t,x)
, which couples to
F(t,x,v)
through the nonlinear Poisson equation in (
1.1
)
2
, incorporates the electron density
e^{\phi}
via the well-known Maxwell-Boltzmann law.
The parameter
\varepsilon>0
, called Knudsen number, is proportional to the mean free path.
The Boltzmann collision operator
Q(\cdot,\cdot)
is defined as

\displaystyle Q(F_{1},F_{2})=\;

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(u-v)\cdot\omega|[F_{1}(u^{\prime})F_{2}(v^{\prime})-F_{1}(u)F_{2}(v)]\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle Q_{\mathrm{gain}}(F_{1},F_{2})-Q_{\mathrm{loss}}(F_{1},F_{2}),

where the pre-collision velocities
(u,v)
and post-collision velocities
(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})
satisfy the momentum and energy conservations:

u+v=u^{\prime}+v^{\prime},\quad|u|^{2}+|v|^{2}=|u^{\prime}|^{2}+|v^{\prime}|^{2},

which implies that

u^{\prime}=u-[(u-v)\cdot\omega]\omega,\quad v^{\prime}=v+[(u-v)\cdot\omega]\omega,\quad\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.

Formally, Bardos et al.
[
2
]
derived a system similar to (
1.1
) with a time-dependent electron temperature from
the bipolar Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system under the massless electrons limit (vanishing electron-to-ion mass ratio). This article focuses on the system (
1.1
) with normalized electron temperature.
Li-Yang-Zhong
[
38
]
constructed the existence and uniqueness of the strong solution to (
1.1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. In
[
37
]
, we showed the global existence and exponential decay of classical solutions to (
1.1
) in a periodic box.

On the fluid scale, ion dynamics are governed by the compressible ionic Euler-Poisson system:

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho_{0}+\nabla\cdot(\rho_{0}u_{0})=0,\\
&\partial_{t}u_{0}+(u_{0}\cdot\nabla)u_{0}+\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}\nabla p_{0}+\nabla\phi_{0}=0,\\
&\Delta\phi_{0}=e^{\phi_{0}}-\rho_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\rho_{0}
and
u_{0}
are the density and velocity of the fluid at the time
t\geq 0
, position
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Guo-Pausader
[
25
]
proved the global existence of smooth solutions
[\rho_{0},u_{0}]
to (
1.2
) near a uniform state
[1,0]
with small, irrotational perturbations in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Thus, a natural problem arises: can we show the convergence from (
1.1
) to (
1.2
) as the Knudsen number
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, and thus contract their connections?
Our purpose of this paper is to give an affirmative answer to this problem. We shall prove that the strong solutions of the ionic Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system (
1.1
) converge to the classical solutions of the ionic Euler-Poisson system (
1.2
) globally in time, as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. It provides a first rigorous justification for the global validity of the Hilbert expansion in the ionic Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system (
1.1
) within the entire space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

We take the truncated Hilbert expansions

(1.3)

\displaystyle F=

\displaystyle F_{0}+\varepsilon F_{1}+\cdots+\varepsilon^{2k-1}F_{2k-1}+\varepsilon^{k}R,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\phi=

\displaystyle\phi_{0}+\varepsilon\phi_{1}+\cdots+\varepsilon^{2k-1}\phi_{2k-1}+\varepsilon^{k}\phi_{R},

where
[F_{0},\phi_{0}]
is the leading order,
[F_{i},\phi_{i}]

(1\leq i\leq 2k-1)
are the coefficients and
[R,\phi_{R}]
is the remainder.

Plugging (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) into (
1.1
) yields

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\bigg(\sum^{2k-1}_{n=0}\varepsilon^{n}F_{n}+\varepsilon^{k}R\bigg)+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\bigg(\sum^{2k-1}_{n=0}\varepsilon^{n}F_{n}+\varepsilon^{k}R\bigg)\\
&\quad-\nabla_{x}\bigg(\sum^{2k-1}_{n=0}\varepsilon^{n}\phi_{n}+\varepsilon^{k}\phi_{R}\bigg)\cdot\nabla_{v}\bigg(\sum^{2k-1}_{n=0}\varepsilon^{n}F_{n}+\varepsilon^{k}R\bigg)\\
&\qquad=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}Q\bigg(\sum^{2k-1}_{n=0}\varepsilon^{n}F_{n}+\varepsilon^{k}R,\sum^{2k-1}_{n=0}\varepsilon^{n}F_{n}+\varepsilon^{k}R\bigg),\\
&\Delta\bigg(\sum^{2k-1}_{n=0}\varepsilon^{n}\phi_{n}+\varepsilon^{k}\phi_{R}\bigg)=\exp\bigg(\sum^{2k-1}_{n=0}\varepsilon^{n}\phi_{n}+\varepsilon^{k}\phi_{R}\bigg)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bigg(\sum^{2k-1}_{n=0}\varepsilon^{n}\phi_{n}+\varepsilon^{k}\phi_{R}\bigg)\mathrm{d}v.\end{aligned}\right.

We compare the coefficients of different powers of the parameter

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}:\,\,&Q(F_{0},F_{0})=0,\\
\varepsilon^{0}:\,\,&\partial_{t}F_{0}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F_{0}-\nabla_{x}\phi_{0}\cdot\nabla_{v}F_{0}=Q(F_{1},F_{0})+Q(F_{0},F_{1}),\\
&\Delta\phi_{0}=e^{\phi_{0}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{0}\mathrm{d}v,\\
\varepsilon^{1}:\,\,&\partial_{t}F_{1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F_{1}-\nabla_{x}\phi_{0}\cdot\nabla_{v}F_{1}=Q(F_{2},F_{0})+Q(F_{0},F_{2})+Q(F_{1},F_{1}),\\
&\Delta\phi_{1}=e^{\phi_{0}}\phi_{1}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{1}\mathrm{d}v,\\
&\cdots\\
\varepsilon^{n}:\,\,&\partial_{t}F_{n}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F_{n}-\nabla_{x}\phi_{0}\cdot\nabla_{v}F_{n}-\nabla_{x}\phi_{n}\cdot\nabla_{v}F_{0}\\
&\,\,\quad\,\,\,\,=\sum_{{i,j\geq 0,i+j=n+1}}Q(F_{i},F_{j})+\sum_{{i,j\geq 1,i+j=n}}\nabla_{x}\phi_{i}\cdot\nabla_{v}F_{j},\\
&\Delta\phi_{n}=e^{\phi_{0}}A_{n}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{n}\mathrm{d}v.\end{split}

Here,

\displaystyle A_{n}=e^{-\phi_{0}}\frac{1}{n!}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon^{n}}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\left(\exp\big\{\phi_{0}+\varepsilon\phi_{1}+\cdots+\varepsilon^{2k-1}\phi_{2k-1}\big\}\right),\quad n=0,\ldots,2k-1.

Remark 1.1
.

Formally, note that the following difference is of order

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\big\{\phi_{0}+\varepsilon\phi_{1}+\cdots+\varepsilon^{2k-1}\phi_{2k-1}\big\}\left(\exp\big\{\varepsilon^{2k}\phi_{2k}+\cdots\big\}-1\right)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\,\varepsilon^{2k}\phi_{2k}\exp\big\{\phi_{0}+\varepsilon\phi_{1}+\cdots+\varepsilon^{2k-1}\phi_{2k-1}\big\}.

\displaystyle\,T_{2k-1}\big(\exp\big\{\phi_{0}+\varepsilon\phi_{1}+\cdots+\varepsilon^{2k-1}\phi_{2k-1}\big\}\big)

\displaystyle\,T_{2k-1}(H(\varepsilon))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,H(0)+\varepsilon H^{\prime}(0)+\cdots+\varepsilon^{2k-1}\frac{H^{2k-1}(0)}{(2k-1)!}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\,e^{\phi_{0}}(A_{0}+\varepsilon A_{1}+\cdots+\varepsilon^{2k-1}A_{2k-1}),

. By direct calculations, we get

\displaystyle\,1,\,\,\,A_{1}=\phi_{1},\,\,\,A_{2}=\phi_{2}+\frac{\phi_{1}^{2}}{2},

\displaystyle A_{3}=

\displaystyle\,\phi_{3}+\phi_{1}\phi_{2}+\frac{\phi_{1}^{3}}{6},

\displaystyle\!\!\!\cdots

\displaystyle\,\phi_{n}+\phi_{n-1}\phi_{1}+\phi_{n-2}\frac{\phi_{1}^{2}}{2!}+\cdots+\phi_{2}\frac{\phi_{1}^{n-2}}{(n-2)!}+\frac{\phi_{1}^{n}}{n!}+\phi_{n-2}\phi_{2}+\cdots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\phi_{n}+\sum_{\varkappa}C_{\varkappa}\prod_{1\leq j\leq n-1}(\phi_{j})^{\varkappa_{j}},\quad\sum_{{1\leq j\leq n-1,\varkappa_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}}j\varkappa_{j}=n.

1.2.
Previous works

1.2.1.
Hilbert expansion

The rigorous mathematical justification of Hilbert expansions in kinetic theory has witnessed rapid advancements in recent decades. For the classical Boltzmann equation, Caflisch
[
4
]
pioneered the use of truncated Hilbert expansions coupled with remainder decomposition to establish the well-known compressible Euler limit. Building upon this framework, Lachowicz
[
32
,
33
]
incorporated initial layer corrections to derive Euler limits for the Boltzmann, Enskog, and Povzner equations. However, the solutions constructed through these early approaches around Euler equations lacked guaranteed nonnegativity, which is a critical physical requirement.

This limitation was overcome by Guo-Jang-Jiang
[
23
,
24
]
through their innovative
L^{2}
–
L^{\infty}
method, originally developed by Guo
[
20
]
for the well-posedness of Boltzmann equation in bounded domains.
Their approach is then proved to be robust for multiple kinetic models: such as the Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system for electrons by Guo-Jang
[
22
]
with hard-sphere case, then extended to the soft potentials case in
[
36
]
by ourselves,
the relativistic Boltzmann equation by Speck-Strain
[
43
]
,
the relativistic Vlasov-Maxwell-Boltzmann system by Guo-Xiao
[
26
]
,
the two-species Boltzmann equations by Wu-Yang
[
45
]
, the quantum Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system by Jiang and Zou
[
30
]
,
and the Vlasov-Maxwell-Boltzmann system to the incompressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier-Maxwell system with Ohm’s
law recently by Jiang-Luo-Zhang
[
29
]
, see also Zhou et al.
[
46
]
.
For the Landau type equations and non-cutoff Boltzmann type equations, Ouyang-Wu-Xiao
[
42
]
proved the global-in-time convergence of the Hilbert expansion in the relativistic Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau system. Moreover, Lei-Liu-Xiao-Zhao
[
34
]
settled the more complicated cases, including the non-relativistic Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau system and non-cutoff Vlasov-Maxwell-Boltzmann system.

For the initial boundary problems in half space,
Guo-Huang-Wang
[
21
]
justified the validity of Hilbert expansion to the Boltzmann equation for specular reflection boundary condition, and Ouyang-Wang
[
41
]
recently extended this argument to the soft potentials case. Recently, Jiang-Luo-Tang successfully worked out for the Maxwell reflection boundary condition case
[
27
]
and the completely diffuse boundary condition case
[
28
]
.

Kinetic models for ions

Extensive research has been conducted on the global well-posedness of the electronic Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system. Notably, Guo

established classical solutions in

For a domain with boundary, interested reader can refer to, for example,

In stark contrast, mathematical studies on kinetic models for ions remain comparatively limited due to the exponential nonlinearities introduced by the Poisson equation. For the ionic Vlasov-Poisson system, Bouchut

1.2.3.
Compressible ionic Euler-Poisson system

Previous studies on the ionic Euler-Poisson system include
[
8
]
for one dimensional case by Cordier and Grenier, who investigated the quasi-neutral limit to 1D compressible Euler system, as letting the Debye length for ion flows
tend to zero. Later, Gérard, Han-Kwan and Rousset
[

16

studied the quasi-neutral limit for the ionic Euler-Poisson system over the half space with various boundary conditions. Jung-Kwon-Suzuki
[

For the asymptotic stability of plasma sheaths of the compressible ionic Euler-Poisson system, one refers to Duan-Yin-Zhu
[

[

Meanwhile, Guo-Pausader
[
25

However, the compressible ionic Euler-Poisson limit of the ionic Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system has yet to be mathematically validated.
Given that the ionic Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system serves as a fundamental kinetic model for ion-scale phenomena in diluted plasmas, our work focuses on establishing this limit rigorously. Our analysis aims to bridge the kinetic and fluid descriptions of ion dynamics by rigorously deriving the compressible ionic Euler-Poisson system as the leading-order asymptotic of the ionic Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system.

1.3.1.
Formations of the model

Comparing the Hilbert expansion for the electronic Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system issued by Guo-Jang
[
22
]
to our ionic Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system, the main difference lies in the nonlinear Poisson equation with the new exponential term

. Formally,
we take Taylor’s expansion of

\exp(\phi)=\exp\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{i}\phi_{i}\bigg)

in
\varepsilon
around

\displaystyle\exp\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{i}\phi_{i}\bigg)=\tilde{A}_{0}+\varepsilon\tilde{A}_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}\tilde{A}_{2}+\cdots,

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{n}=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon^{n}}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\bigg\{\exp\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{i}\phi_{i}\bigg)\bigg\}\triangleq A_{n}\exp(\phi_{0}),\,\,\,n\geq 0.

It is important to notice that, for any

,

\displaystyle A_{n}=

\displaystyle\exp(-\phi_{0})\tilde{A}_{n}=\exp(-\phi_{0})\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon^{n}}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\bigg\{\exp\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{N}\varepsilon^{i}\phi_{i}\bigg)\bigg\},\,\,\,0\leq n\leq N,

which implies that

\displaystyle\exp(\phi_{0})(A_{0}+\varepsilon A_{1}+\cdots+\varepsilon^{N}A_{N})=T_{N}\bigg(\exp\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{N}\varepsilon^{i}\phi_{i}\bigg)\bigg),

where

denotes the
N
-th order Taylor polynomial of

for the parameter
\varepsilon>0
.
Expanding the nonlinear Poisson equation (

)
2
with the Hilbert expansion

\Delta\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{i}\phi_{i}\bigg)=\exp\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{i}\phi_{i}\bigg)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{i}F_{i}\bigg)\mathrm{d}v

up to the

-th order yields

\Delta\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{N}\varepsilon^{i}\phi_{i}\bigg)=T_{N}\bigg(\exp\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{N}\varepsilon^{i}\phi_{i}\bigg)\bigg)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{N}\varepsilon^{i}F_{i}\bigg)\mathrm{d}v.

Taking the difference of the above equation with

and the nonlinear Poisson equation (

) with the truncated Hilbert expansions (

\Delta(\varepsilon^{k}\phi_{R})=\exp\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{2k-1}\varepsilon^{i}\phi_{i}\bigg)\exp(\varepsilon^{k}\phi_{R})-T_{2k-1}\bigg(\exp\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{2k-1}\varepsilon^{i}\phi_{i}\bigg)\bigg)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varepsilon^{k}R\,\mathrm{d}v,

which is exactly the remainder equation (

)

.

For each given integer

(2.1)

(u_{k})^{t}\\

then (

\displaystyle\partial_{t}U_{k}+V_{k}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{i}\partial_{i}U_{k}+\overline{\mathcal{B}}U_{k}=\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{k},

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}u_{0}^{i}&\rho_{0}e_{i}&0\\

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\theta_{0}}{\rho_{0}}\\
&\rho_{0}I_{3\times 3}\\
&&\frac{3\rho_{0}}{2\theta_{0}}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\theta_{0}}{\rho_{0}}u_{0}^{i}&\theta_{0}e_{i}&0\\

\mathfrak{f}_{k-1}\\
\frac{1}{2\theta_{0}}\mathfrak{g}_{k-1}\end{bmatrix}.

Here

,

.

]
). Below we shall establish more delicate energy estimates of (

Proposition 2.1
.

For the integer
k\geq 1
, assume that

and

in (

) solve the system (

), then it holds

\displaystyle\|U_{k}(t)\|_{s}+\|V_{k}(t)\|_{s}+\|\phi_{k}(t)\|_{s}\leq C(1+t)^{k-1}\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,\,s\geq 0,

is independent of

.

Proof.

We first consider (
2.3
) for
k=1
:

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}\{\partial_{t}U_{1}+V_{1}\}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\mathcal{A}_{i}\partial_{i}U_{1}+\mathcal{B}U_{1}=\mathcal{G}_{1}.

Here, we define

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}U_{1}:=\mathcal{A}_{0}\partial_{t}U_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\mathcal{A}_{i}\partial_{i}U_{1},

and, as mentioned before, we denote the norm of
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
by
\|\cdot\|_{s}
for
s\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular, the norm of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{0}
.

Step 1
. For the
\|\cdot\|_{0}
norm, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(U_{1},\mathcal{A}_{0}U_{1})=

\displaystyle\,(\partial_{t}U_{1},\mathcal{A}_{0}U_{1})+(U_{1},\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}_{0}U_{1})+(U_{1},\mathcal{A}_{0}\partial_{t}U_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,2\bigg(U_{1},\mathcal{P}U_{1}-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\mathcal{A}_{i}\partial_{i}U_{1}\bigg)+(U_{1},\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}_{0}U_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,2(\mathcal{P}U_{1},U_{1})+\bigg(U_{1},\bigg(\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\partial_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\bigg)U_{1}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,2(-\mathcal{A}_{0}V_{1},U_{1})+(-\mathcal{B}U_{1}+\mathcal{G}_{1},U_{1})+\bigg(U_{1},\bigg(\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\partial_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\bigg)U_{1}\bigg).

The first term on the last line reads

\displaystyle(-\mathcal{A}_{0}V_{1},U_{1})=

\displaystyle\int-\rho_{0}\nabla\phi_{1}\cdot u_{1}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\phi_{1}\nabla\cdot(\rho_{0}u_{1})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\phi_{1}(-\partial_{t}\rho_{1}-\nabla\cdot(\rho_{1}u_{0}))\mathrm{d}x\quad(\rm{using}\,\,\,\eqref{mass})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int-\phi_{1}\partial_{t}\rho_{1}\mathrm{d}x+\int\nabla\phi_{1}\cdot\rho_{1}u_{0}\mathrm{d}x.

By using (
1.6
) of
\varepsilon^{1}
step, we get

\displaystyle\int\phi_{1}\partial_{t}\rho_{1}\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\,\int\phi_{1}(\partial_{t}(e^{\phi_{0}})\phi_{1}+e^{\phi_{0}}\partial_{t}\phi_{1}-\Delta(\partial_{t}\phi_{1}))\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big(\|\nabla\phi_{1}\|^{2}_{0}+\big\|\phi_{1}e^{\frac{\phi_{0}}{2}}\big\|_{0}^{2}\big)+\frac{1}{2}\int\phi_{1}^{2}\partial_{t}(e^{\phi_{0}})\mathrm{d}x.

In summary, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(\Big\|\sqrt{U_{1}^{t}\mathcal{A}_{0}U_{1}}\Big\|_{0}^{2}+\|\nabla\phi_{1}\|^{2}_{0}+\big\|\phi_{1}e^{\frac{\phi_{0}}{2}}\big\|_{0}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad=2\int\nabla\phi_{1}\cdot\rho_{1}u_{0}\mathrm{d}x-\int\phi_{1}^{2}\partial_{t}(e^{\phi_{0}})\mathrm{d}x+(-\mathcal{B}U_{1}+\mathcal{G}_{1},U_{1})+\bigg(U_{1},\bigg(\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\partial_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\bigg)U_{1}\bigg).

Note that
\mathcal{B}
,
\mathcal{G}_{1}
,
\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}_{0}
and
\partial_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}
consist of
\rho_{0}-1,u_{0},\theta_{0}
and their first spatial derivatives, which decay at the rate of
t^{-16/15}
by Proposition
B.1
, thus we have

\displaystyle\left|2\int\nabla\phi_{1}\cdot\rho_{1}u_{0}\mathrm{d}x+(-\mathcal{B}U_{1}+\mathcal{G}_{1},U_{1})+\bigg(U_{1},\bigg(\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\partial_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\bigg)U_{1}\bigg)\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{C}{(1+t)^{\frac{16}{15}}}\{\|U_{1}\|_{0}^{2}+\|V_{1}\|_{0}^{2}\}+\frac{C}{(1+t)^{\frac{16}{15}}}\{\|U_{1}\|_{0}+\|V_{1}\|_{0}\}.

To deal with the term
\int\phi_{1}^{2}\partial_{t}(e^{\phi_{0}})\mathrm{d}x
, we study the estimates of
\partial_{t}\phi_{0}
. Due to the
\varepsilon^{0}
step of (

), it holds
\Delta\phi_{0}=e^{\phi_{0}}-\rho_{0}
and thus

\Delta\partial_{t}\phi_{0}=e^{\phi_{0}}\partial_{t}\phi_{0}-\partial_{t}\rho_{0}.

From Remark
B.1
, we know that
\phi_{0}\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
of smooth bounded functions with bounded
x-
derivatives of all orders. Hence
e^{\phi_{0}}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and there exists constants
C_{0},C_{1}>0
such that
0<C_{0}\leq e^{\phi_{0}}\leq C_{1}
. Therefore, the symbol of
e^{\phi_{0}(x)}-\Delta
, which is
e^{\phi_{0}(x)}+|\xi|^{2}
, satisfies

\displaystyle|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}(e^{\phi_{0}(x)}+|\xi|^{2})|\leq\,

\displaystyle C_{\alpha,\beta}(1+|\xi|)^{2-|\alpha|},

\displaystyle e^{\phi_{0}(x)}+|\xi|^{2}\geq\,

\displaystyle C_{0}(1+|\xi|)^{2},

where constants
C_{\alpha,\beta}
and
C_{0}
are uniform for all
t\in[0,\infty)
as a parameter in
\phi_{0}(t,x)
due to
\phi_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{t}^{+};H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{x}^{3}))
.
It implies that

e^{\phi_{0}(x)}-\Delta
is an elliptic pseudo-differential operator of
\Psi^{2}
, which possesses a parametrix of
\Psi^{-2}
(see e.g.
[
1
]
).
Note that, by Lax-Milgram theorem, there exists a bounded linear operator
(e^{\phi_{0}(x)}-\Delta)^{-1}:L^{2}\rightarrow H^{1}
.
Thus the operator
(e^{\phi_{0}(x)}-\Delta)^{-1}
is a
\Psi^{-2}
parametrix of
e^{\phi_{0}(x)}-\Delta
. By the Hölder boundedness properties of the
\Psi^{-2}
pseudo-differential operators (see e.g.
[
44
]
), we have

(e^{\phi_{0}}-\Delta)^{-1}:C^{k,\gamma}\rightarrow C^{k+2,\gamma}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
, with operator norm constants uniform in
t
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\phi_{0}\|_{C^{k,\gamma}}=

\displaystyle\,\|(e^{\phi_{0}}-\Delta)^{-1}\partial_{t}\rho_{0}\|_{C^{k,\gamma}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\|-(e^{\phi_{0}}-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot(\rho_{0}u_{0})\|_{C^{k,\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\|\rho_{0}u_{0}\|_{C^{k-1,\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\|\rho_{0}u_{0}\|_{W^{k,\infty}}

(2.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\frac{C}{(1+t)^{\frac{16}{15}}}.

Thus we get

\displaystyle\int\phi_{1}^{2}\partial_{t}(e^{\phi_{0}})\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{C}{(1+t)^{\frac{16}{15}}}\big\|\phi_{1}e^{\frac{\phi_{0}}{2}}\big\|_{0}^{2}.

We conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(\Big\|\sqrt{U_{1}^{t}\mathcal{A}_{0}U_{1}}\Big\|_{0}^{2}+\|\nabla\phi_{1}\|^{2}_{0}+\big\|\phi_{1}e^{\frac{\phi_{0}}{2}}\big\|_{0}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\leq\frac{C}{(1+t)^{\frac{16}{15}}}\big\{\|U_{1}\|_{0}^{2}+\|V_{1}\|_{0}^{2}+\big\|\phi_{1}e^{\frac{\phi_{0}}{2}}\big\|_{0}^{2}\big\}+\frac{C}{(1+t)^{\frac{16}{15}}}\{\|U_{1}\|_{0}+\|V_{1}\|_{0}\}.

Step 2
. For the integer
s>0
, define Fourier multiplier operator
\Lambda^{s}
whose symbol is
(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}
. If
U_{1}\in H^{s}
, then
\Lambda^{s}U_{1}\in L^{2}
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(\Lambda^{s}U_{1},\mathcal{A}_{0}\Lambda^{s}U_{1})=

\displaystyle\,2(\mathcal{P}\Lambda^{s}U_{1},\Lambda^{s}U_{1})+\bigg(\Lambda^{s}U_{1},\bigg(\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\partial_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\bigg)\Lambda^{s}U_{1}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,2(\Lambda^{s}\mathcal{P}U_{1},\Lambda^{s}U_{1})+2([\mathcal{P},\Lambda^{s}]U_{1},\Lambda^{s}U_{1})+\bigg(\Lambda^{s}U_{1},\bigg(\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\partial_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\bigg)\Lambda^{s}U_{1}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-2(\Lambda^{s}(\mathcal{A}_{0}V_{1}),\Lambda^{s}U_{1})+2(\Lambda^{s}(-\mathcal{B}U_{1}+\mathcal{G}_{1}),\Lambda^{s}U_{1})

\displaystyle\,+2([\mathcal{P},\Lambda^{s}]U_{1},\Lambda^{s}U_{1})+\bigg(\Lambda^{s}U_{1},\bigg(\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\partial_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\bigg)\Lambda^{s}U_{1}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-2(\mathcal{A}_{0}\Lambda^{s}V_{1},\Lambda^{s}U_{1})-2([\Lambda^{s},\mathcal{A}_{0}]V_{1},\Lambda^{s}U_{1})+2(\Lambda^{s}(-\mathcal{B}U_{1}+\mathcal{G}_{1}),\Lambda^{s}U_{1})

\displaystyle\,+2([\mathcal{P},\Lambda^{s}]U_{1},\Lambda^{s}U_{1})+\bigg(\Lambda^{s}U_{1},\bigg(\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\partial_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\bigg)\Lambda^{s}U_{1}\bigg).

The first term on the right-hand side of the above equality reads

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}_{0}\Lambda^{s}V_{1},\Lambda^{s}U_{1})=\,

\displaystyle\int\rho_{0}\Lambda^{s}\nabla\phi_{1}\cdot\Lambda^{s}u_{1}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\int\Lambda^{s}\nabla\phi_{1}\cdot([\rho_{0},\Lambda^{s}]u_{1}+\Lambda^{s}(\rho_{0}u_{1}))\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\int\Lambda^{s}\nabla\phi_{1}\cdot[\rho_{0},\Lambda^{s}]u_{1}\mathrm{d}x-\int\Lambda^{s}\phi_{1}\Lambda^{s}(\nabla\cdot(\rho_{0}u_{1}))\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\int\Lambda^{s}\nabla\phi_{1}\cdot[\rho_{0},\Lambda^{s}]u_{1}\mathrm{d}x+\int\Lambda^{s}\phi_{1}\Lambda^{s}(\partial_{t}\rho_{1})\mathrm{d}x-\int\Lambda^{s}\nabla\phi_{1}\cdot\Lambda^{s}(\rho_{1}u_{0})\mathrm{d}x.

Routine calculations yield

\displaystyle\int\Lambda^{s}\phi_{1}\Lambda^{s}(\partial_{t}\rho_{1})\mathrm{d}x=\,

\displaystyle\int\Lambda^{s}\phi_{1}\Lambda^{s}(-\Delta\partial_{t}\phi_{1}+\partial_{t}(e^{\phi_{0}})\phi_{1}+e^{\phi_{0}}\partial_{t}\phi_{1})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big(\|\Lambda^{s}\nabla\phi_{1}\|_{0}^{2}+\big\|e^{\frac{\phi_{0}}{2}}\Lambda^{s}\phi_{1}\big\|_{0}^{2}\big)+\frac{1}{2}(\Lambda^{s}\phi_{1},\partial_{t}(e^{\phi_{0}})\Lambda^{s}\phi_{1})

\displaystyle\,+(\Lambda^{s}\phi_{1},[\Lambda^{s},\partial_{t}(e^{\phi_{0}})]\phi_{1})+(\Lambda^{s}\phi_{1},[\Lambda^{s},e^{\phi_{0}}]\partial_{t}\phi_{1}).

Therefore, it holds

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big(\big\|\sqrt{(\Lambda^{s}U_{1})^{t}\mathcal{A}_{0}(\Lambda^{s}U_{1})}\big\|_{0}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{s}\nabla\phi_{1}\|_{0}^{2}+\big\|e^{\frac{\phi_{0}}{2}}\Lambda^{s}\phi_{1}\big\|_{0}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\quad=-(\Lambda^{s}\phi_{1},\partial_{t}(e^{\phi_{0}})\Lambda^{s}\phi_{1})-2(\Lambda^{s}\phi_{1},[\Lambda^{s},\partial_{t}(e^{\phi_{0}})]\phi_{1})-2(\Lambda^{s}\phi_{1},[\Lambda^{s},e^{\phi_{0}}]\partial_{t}\phi_{1})

\displaystyle\qquad-2\int\Lambda^{s}\nabla\phi_{1}\cdot[\rho_{0},\Lambda^{s}]u_{1}\mathrm{d}x+2\int\Lambda^{s}\nabla\phi_{1}\cdot\Lambda^{s}(\rho_{1}u_{0})\mathrm{d}x-2([\Lambda^{s},\mathcal{A}_{0}]V_{1},\Lambda^{s}U_{1})

(2.7)

\displaystyle\qquad+2(\Lambda^{s}(-\mathcal{B}U_{1}+\mathcal{G}_{1}),\Lambda^{s}U_{1})+2([\mathcal{P},\Lambda^{s}]U_{1},\Lambda^{s}U_{1})+\bigg(\Lambda^{s}U_{1},\bigg(\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\partial_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\bigg)\Lambda^{s}U_{1}\bigg).

Recalling (
2
), the first term on the right-hand side of (

) is controlled by

C\big\|e^{\frac{\phi_{0}}{2}}\Lambda^{s}\phi_{1}\big\|_{0}^{2}
.
To deal with the other terms in (
2
), we study the properties of the commutators
[\Lambda^{s},\partial_{t}(e^{\phi_{0}})]
,
[\Lambda^{s},e^{\phi_{0}}]
,
[\rho_{0},\Lambda^{s}]
,
[\Lambda^{s},\mathcal{A}_{0}]
and
[\mathcal{P},\Lambda^{s}]
for

.
Since
\partial_{t}(e^{\phi_{0}})
,

,
\rho_{0}
and
\mathcal{A}_{0}
are smooth and bounded, they are
\Psi^{0}
pseudo-differential operators. By the definition of
\mathcal{P}
in (

),
\mathcal{P}
is a
\Psi^{1}
pseudo-differential operator. Clearly,
\Lambda^{s}\in\Psi^{s}
. Thus we obtain from the standard theory of commutators (see e.g.

) that

\displaystyle[\Lambda^{s},\partial_{t}(e^{\phi_{0}})],\,\,[\Lambda^{s},e^{\phi_{0}}],\,\,[\rho_{0},\Lambda^{s}],\,\,[\Lambda^{s},\mathcal{A}_{0}]\in\Psi^{s-1},\quad[\mathcal{P},\Lambda^{s}]\in\Psi^{s}

whose asymptotic expansions are finite due to

.
Since the linear operator
\Psi^{s}:H^{s}\rightarrow L^{2}
is bounded, we have

\displaystyle([\mathcal{P},\Lambda^{s}]U_{1},\Lambda^{s}U_{1})\leq

\displaystyle\,\|[\mathcal{P},\Lambda^{s}]U_{1}\|_{0}\|\Lambda^{s}U_{1}\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\frac{C}{(1+t)^{\frac{16}{15}}}\|U_{1}\|_{s}\|\Lambda^{s}U_{1}\|_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{C}{(1+t)^{\frac{16}{15}}}\|\Lambda^{s}U_{1}\|_{0}^{2}.

Similarly, we get

\displaystyle-2(\Lambda^{s}\phi_{1},[\Lambda^{s},\partial_{t}(e^{\phi_{0}})]\phi_{1})-2\int\Lambda^{s}\nabla\phi_{1}\cdot[\rho_{0},\Lambda^{s}]u_{1}\mathrm{d}x-2([\Lambda^{s},\mathcal{A}_{0}]V_{1},\Lambda^{s}U_{1})

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{C}{(1+t)^{\frac{16}{15}}}(\|\Lambda^{s}U_{1}\|_{0}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{s}V_{1}\|_{0}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{s}\phi_{1}\|_{0}^{2})\leq\frac{C}{(1+t)^{\frac{16}{15}}}\big(\|\Lambda^{s}U_{1}\|_{0}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{s}V_{1}\|_{0}^{2}+\big\|e^{\frac{\phi_{0}}{2}}\Lambda^{s}\phi_{1}\big\|_{0}^{2}\big).

We also have

\displaystyle 2\int\Lambda^{s}\nabla\phi_{1}\cdot\Lambda^{s}(\rho_{1}u_{0})\mathrm{d}x+2(\Lambda^{s}(-\mathcal{B}U_{1}+\mathcal{G}_{1}),\Lambda^{s}U_{1})+\bigg(\Lambda^{s}U_{1},\bigg(\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\partial_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\bigg)\Lambda^{s}U_{1}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\,\frac{C}{(1+t)^{\frac{16}{15}}}(\|U_{1}\|_{s}^{2}+\|V_{1}\|_{s}^{2})+\frac{C}{(1+t)^{\frac{16}{15}}}(\|U_{1}\|_{s}+\|V_{1}\|_{s}).

The remainder term on the right-hand side of (
2
) is
(\Lambda^{s}\phi_{1},[\Lambda^{s},e^{\phi_{0}}]\partial_{t}\phi_{1})
. From the
\varepsilon^{1}
step of (

\phi_{1}=(e^{\phi_{0}}-\Delta)^{-1}\rho_{1}
and

\displaystyle\Delta\partial_{t}\phi_{1}=\partial_{t}(e^{\phi_{0}})\phi_{1}+e^{\phi_{0}}\partial_{t}\phi_{1}-\partial_{t}\rho_{1}.

Hence, it holds

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi_{1}=

\displaystyle\,(e^{\phi_{0}}-\Delta)^{-1}\left(\partial_{t}\rho_{1}-\partial_{t}(e^{\phi_{0}})(e^{\phi_{0}}-\Delta)^{-1}\rho_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-(e^{\phi_{0}}-\Delta)^{-1}\left(\nabla\cdot(\rho_{0}u_{1}+\rho_{1}u_{0})+\partial_{t}(e^{\phi_{0}})(e^{\phi_{0}}-\Delta)^{-1}\rho_{1}\right).

It follows by
(e^{\phi_{0}}-\Delta)^{-1}(\in\Psi^{-2}):H^{s}\rightarrow H^{s+2}
that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\|_{s}\leq C\|\rho_{0}u_{1}+\rho_{1}u_{0}\|_{s-1}+C\|\rho_{1}\|_{s-4}\leq C\|U_{1}\|_{s}.

Then we obtain

\nabla\times u_{0}^{\rm{in}}=0
and
\|\rho_{0}^{\rm{in}}-1\|_{Y}+\|u_{0}^{\rm{in}}\|_{Y}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
leads to a global solution
[\rho_{0},u_{0}]
of (
1.2
) with

\|\rho_{0}-1\|_{X}+\|u_{0}\|_{X}\leq 2\varepsilon_{0}
.

Remark B.1
.

In particular, both

\rho_{0}-1=:\overline{\rho}
and
u_{0}
are in
H^{2k-1}
, and decay in
W^{k,10}
as
t^{-\frac{16}{15}}
for
k\geq 5
. As for
\phi_{0}
, note that

\overline{\rho}=(I-\Delta)\phi_{0}+\frac{\phi_{0}^{2}}{2}+\Big[e^{\phi_{0}}-1-\phi_{0}-\frac{\phi_{0}^{2}}{2}\Big],

which defines an inverse operator
\overline{\rho}\mapsto\phi_{0}(\overline{\rho})
. Expanding it up to third order yields

\phi_{0}(\overline{\rho})=(I-\Delta)^{-1}\overline{\rho}-\frac{1}{2}(I-\Delta)^{-1}[(I-\Delta)^{-1}\overline{\rho}]^{2}+\mathcal{R}(\overline{\rho}),

where
\mathcal{R}
satisfies good properties. Combining
\|\overline{\rho}\|_{X}\ll 1
with
(I-\Delta)^{-1}\in\Psi^{-2}
,
we deduce that
\phi_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{1}_{+};H^{2k+1})
and

\displaystyle\|\phi_{0}\|_{W^{k+2,10}}\leq C\|\bar{\rho}\|_{W^{k,10}}\leq C\varepsilon_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{16}{15}},

which implies
that
\|\phi_{0}\|_{C^{k+1,\alpha}}\leq(1+t)^{-\frac{16}{15}}
for
k\geq 5
.

Theoretical calculations

1
Driven coupled-dipole

All work is in Gaussian (cgs) units. We begin with the description of a 2D plasmonic lattice with unit cells indexed by
n_{1},n_{2}
and displacement within the unit cell
{\bf x}_{{\bf n}\kappa}={\bf x}_{\bf n}+{\bf r}_{\kappa}
where
\kappa\in\{1,...,s\}
labels the site within the unit cell. In the dipolar limit, the coupled-dipole equations in direct space can be written as the sum
[
3
]

\overline{\bm{\alpha}}_{\kappa}^{-1}(\omega)\cdot\mathbf{d}_{{\bf n}\kappa}=\mathbf{E}^{0}_{{\bf n}\kappa}-4\pi\omega^{2}\sum_{{\bf n}^{\prime}\kappa^{\prime}}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{0}(\mathbf{x}_{{\bf n}\kappa},\mathbf{x}_{{\bf n}^{\prime}\kappa^{\prime}};\omega)\cdot\mathbf{d}_{{\bf n}^{\prime}\kappa^{\prime}}\,,

(1)

where the prime on the sum ensures the exclusion of the term
({\bf n}^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})=({\bf n},\kappa)
, and

\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{0}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\omega)=\big(-1/4\pi\omega^{2}\big)\Big[(\omega/c)^{2}\overline{{\bf I}}+\nabla\nabla\Big]\frac{e^{i(\omega/c)|{\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime}|}}{|{\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime}|}

(2)

is the dyadic Green’s tensor. Eq. (
1
) may be written in linear form as

\overline{\overline{\bm{\Pi}}}\cdot{\bf d}={\bf E}^{0},

(3)

where
{\bf E}^{0}
is the electric field sourced by a STEM electron
[
1
]
,
\bf d
is a
3Ns\times 1
column vector constructed by vertically stacking
Ns

3\times 1

{\bf d}_{{\bf n}\kappa}
column vectors, such that

\overline{\overline{\bm{\Pi}}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\overline{\bm{\alpha}}_{1}^{-1}(\omega)&-\overline{\bf S}_{12}&\cdots&-\overline{\bf S}_{1s}\\
-\overline{\bf S}_{21}&\overline{\bm{\alpha}}_{2}^{-1}(\omega)&&\vdots\\
\vdots&&&\\
-\overline{\bf S}_{s1}({\bf K})&\cdots&&\overline{\bm{\alpha}}_{s}^{-1}(\omega)\end{array}\right).

(4)

with entries

{\bf S}_{{\bf n}\kappa}=4\pi\omega^{2}\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{0}(\mathbf{x}_{{\bf n}\kappa},\mathbf{x}_{{\bf n}^{\prime}\kappa^{\prime}};\omega)

(5)

The dielectric function of gold is modeled by the Drude model
\varepsilon(\omega)=\varepsilon_{\infty}-\omega_{p}^{2}/(\omega^{2}+i\gamma\omega)
with parameters
\varepsilon_{\infty}=9.7
,
\hbar\omega_{p}=8.96
eV, and loss
\hbar\gamma=0.073
eV. In the dipolar limit, the individual NC response is well-described by the Clausius-Mossotti relation
\alpha\left(\omega\right)\ =\ 4\pi a^{3}\left(\varepsilon\left(\omega\right)-1\right)/\left(\varepsilon\left(\omega\right)+2\right)

[
2
]
.

Eigenmode Solver

Following the SI of Ref.
[
3
]
, the coupled dipole equations that govern the dynamics of the lattice may be written in linear form

\overline{\overline{\mathbf{\Pi}}}_{\bf K}(\omega)\cdot{\bf d}_{\bf K}={\bf E}_{\bf K}^{0},

(6)

where

\overline{\overline{\mathbf{\Pi}}}^{-1}_{\bf K}(\omega)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\overline{\bm{\alpha}}_{1}^{-1}(\omega)-\overline{\bf S}_{11}({\bf K})&-\overline{\bf S}_{12}({\bf K})&\cdots&-\overline{\bf S}_{1s}({\bf K})\\
-\overline{\bf S}_{21}({\bf K})&\overline{\bm{\alpha}}_{2}^{-1}(\omega)-\overline{\bf S}_{22}({\bf K})&&\vdots\\
\vdots&&&\\
-\overline{\bf S}_{s1}({\bf K})&\cdots&&\overline{\bm{\alpha}}_{s}^{-1}(\omega)-\overline{\bf S}_{ss}({\bf K})\end{array}\right).

(7)

and
{\bf d}_{\bf K}
is a
(3s\times 1)
vector. The entries

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{S}}{\kappa\kappa}(\mathbf{K})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}^{{}^{\prime}}\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{\text{NF}}(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{n}\kappa},\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{n}^{\prime}\kappa})e^{i\mathbf{K}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{n^{\prime}}-\mathbf{x}_{n})}

(8)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{S}}{\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}(\mathbf{K})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{\text{NF}}(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{n}\kappa},\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{n}^{\prime}\kappa^{\prime}})e^{i\mathbf{K}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{n^{\prime}}-\mathbf{x}_{n})}

where
\bf n
labels the lattice sites and
\kappa
labels the sublattice sites.
\overline{\mathbf{S}}{\kappa\kappa}(\mathbf{K},\omega)
is a
(3s\times 3s)
matrix. When
{\bf E}_{\bf K}^{0}={\bf 0}
, the solution to Eq. 1
|\overline{\mathbf{S}}{\kappa\kappa}(\mathbf{K},\omega)|=0
becomes a transcendental, complex eigenvalue problem. If

\overline{\bm{\alpha}}(\omega)_{s}^{-1}=\frac{m}{e^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\omega_{x}^{2}&0&0\\
0&\omega_{y}^{2}&0\\
0&0&\omega_{z}^{2}\\
\end{array}\right)-\frac{m}{e^{2}}\omega(i\gamma-\omega)\overline{\bf I}

(9)

In the quasistatic limit,

\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{\text{NF}}\left(\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{i}},\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{j}}\right)=\frac{1}{\left(R_{ij}\right)^{3}}\left(3\hat{\bf R}_{ij}\hat{\bf R}_{ij}-\overline{\bf I}\right).

(10)

is no longer frequency dependent, and depends on geometric factors only, evaluating the electric field at location
{\bf r}_{1}
sourced by a dipole at location
\mathbf{r}_{j}
, where
{\bf R}_{ij}=R_{ij}\hat{\bf R}_{ij}
is the displacement vector connecting the two dipoles and
k=\sqrt{\varepsilon_{b}}\omega/c
, and
\varepsilon_{b}
is the background refractive index.
Eq. 2 may then be written in the form

\overline{\overline{\mathbf{\Pi}}}^{-1}_{\bf K}(\omega)=\overline{\overline{\mathbf{\Xi}}}-\frac{m}{e^{2}}\omega(i\gamma-\omega)\overline{\overline{\mathbf{I}}}

(11)

where
\overline{\overline{\mathbf{\Xi}}}
contains all frequency-independent terms. The requirement that
\det\big({\overline{\overline{\bf\Pi}}}^{-1}(\omega)\big)=0
becomes the eigenvalue problem

\det\Big(\overline{\overline{\mathbf{\Xi}}}-\lambda\overline{\overline{\mathbf{I}}}\Big),

(12)

which is solvable using standard packages. The complex eigen frequencies of the lattice with positive real part are then

\omega_{+}=\frac{1}{2}\left(i\gamma+\sqrt{\frac{4e^{2}\lambda}{m}-\gamma^{2}}\right)

(13)

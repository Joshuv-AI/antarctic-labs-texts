Investigating the Effect of Coenzyme Q10 Supplementation on Cisplatin-Induced Nephrotoxicity in Patients Undergoing Chemotherapy: A Randomized Clinical Trial
Authors: Ruhollah Masoumi, Parisa Javadian, Ali Ahmadi, Mehrdad Sepehrni
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10) is a powerful antioxidant and the main cause of kidney problems caused by cisplatin consumption is due to the low power of antioxidants. This study aimed to evaluate the effect of CoQ10 supplementation on cisplatin-induced nephrotoxicity in patients undergoing chemotherapy as a double-blind clinical trial.Materials and Methods:In this randomized clinical trial, cancer patients referred to the educational hospital of Shahrekord were divided randomly into two groups. The patients received ubiquinone 300 mg daily during the 8 weeks. Fasting blood samples were collected for testing before and after intervention. Data were analyzed by SPSS software version 21 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) using descriptive and analytic tests.Results:The mean of blood urea nitrogen (BUN), blood creatinine (Cr), and glomerular filtration rate (GFR) did not show a significant difference between the two groups before and after the intervention. The mean difference within the Cr group showed the mean stages before and after the intervention increased significantly and the GFR had a significant decrease (P < 0.05). However, the mean of within-group BUN showed no significant difference.Conclusion:The mean of BUN, blood creatinine, and GFR were not different between the two groups, before and after the intervention. The average creatinine levels increased after intervention between the mean of pre-intervention and post-intervention stages increased and GFR decreased; however, in the case of BUN, the opposite was not the case. This study’s results can be used as a guide in drug administration for patients undergoing chemotherapy to reduce the renal side effects of prescribed drugs.

INTRODUCTION

Cancer caused the death of 7.6 million people worldwide in 2008, which is 13% of all deaths that year, and is expected to reach 13.1 million people worldwide by 2030.[12] According to studies conducted in Iran, cancer is the third cause of death in Iran.[3] In addition, since most cancers occur in the elderly and our country has a relatively young population, the incidence and mortality of this deadly disease are expected to increase rapidly in the near future as life expectancy increases. This shows the importance of managing this life-threatening disease and providing a cancer control program in the country.[4]

Cisplatin is a platinum-based drug, also known as cancer penicillin; this drug was the first powerful drug used for cancer chemotherapy. Cisplatin is a chemotherapy drug that is effective for treating patients with various cancers and is usually used by injection methods.[5] Despite many advances in the last decade, this drug is still widely used in various cancers.[67]

The exact molecular mechanism of this substance is not yet known; however, it is generally accepted that the main target of this substance is nuclear DNA. It is activated after entering the nucleus.[8910] Despite all the positive clinical points of this drug, the repeated use of this substance must be stopped at certain stages. Two limiting factors for the frequent use of this drug are the inherent and acquired resistance of the tumor to this drug and of course many severe side effects in the normal tissues of the body, especially in the kidney, middle ear, and peripheral nerves.[11121314]

The main problem is the side effects caused by the use of this drug; complications such as nephron toxicity, hepatotoxicity, and cardiac toxicity such as various arrhythmias, congestive heart failure, and cardiomyopathy.[1516] The main problem is due to the reduction of antioxidant defense related to oxidative stress caused by this drug, as well as the reduction of antioxidant enzymes and non-enzymatic antioxidants, which are among the main problems due to poisoning caused by cisplatin toxicity.[17]

Nephrotoxicity is a dose-limiting side effect. Cisplatin causes the death of tubular cells by necrosis or apoptosis depending on the concentration and length of exposure.[18] The dose is reduced when the patient’s creatine clearance decreases. The main reason is precisely because of reactive oxygen species, and animal models have well shown that the use of various antioxidants can prevent these problems.[19]

Despite the relatively high prevalence of acute kidney failure, the drug’s strong antitumor effects have made the use of this drug not limited, and this drug is still one of the main drugs in chemotherapy protocols.[202122]

One of the characteristics of acute renal failure (ARF), which is seen in 90% of cases, is the reversibility of kidney function; however, unfortunately, ARFs caused by cisplatin administration are usually irreversible and therefore worsen the prognosis of these patients.[2324]

Cisplatin’s main limiting complication is kidney problems. Numerous studies have also well reported the pathology of damage caused by reactive oxygen species and other cases of oxidative damage. Considering the antioxidant role of this pseudo-vitamin and considering the lack of studies done in this field, the purpose of this study was to investigate the coenzyme Q in patients undergoing chemotherapy with cisplatin in Shahrekord University of Medical Sciences Hospital.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

A randomized clinical trial study (clinical trial code: IRCT20211205053287N1) was performed at the educational hospital of Shahrekord. The research sample consisted of persons who were treated with cisplatin 50–75 mg/m, without a history of using anti-inflammatory supplements one month ago.

Sampling was done in the available form and the samples were assigned to intervention and control groups randomly. The sample size was calculated as 46 people for each group.

Nephrology specialists examined the renal function of patients before the intervention. Patients received ubiquinone in the form of 300 mg per day[252627] for 8 weeks after the first dose of the drug. The control group also received a placebo in cans with the same shape and same color (maltodextrin). At the beginning of the study, the demographic questionnaire was completed by the interviewer. Before and after the intervention, fasting blood samples were collected for testing. They were measured by the pharmaceutical research center of Shahrekord University of Medical Sciences. Information was collected through observation (with intervention) and recorded in the relevant checklist.

SPSS software version 21 was used for data analysis (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) and descriptive tests (mean, standard deviation, frequency, and percentage) and analytical tests (t-test, Mann–Whitney, repeated measures analysis, and Friedman).

DISCUSSION

This study aimed to investigate the effect of CoQ10 supplementation on cisplatin-induced nephrotoxicity in patients undergoing chemotherapy as a randomized clinical trial. The findings of this study showed that the average BUN, Cr, and GFR did not show any significant difference between the two study groups during the period before and after intervention in the study subjects. The results of the comparison of the intra-group average difference of Cr showed that the difference between before and after the intervention in the studied subjects had a statistically significant increase (P < 0.05); however, the difference in the mean of GFR within the group showed a significant decrease before and after the intervention in the studied subjects (P < 0.05).

Meanwhile, the difference between the intra-group mean of BUN and the mean difference between the pre-intervention and post-intervention stages in the studied subjects was not significant. The comparison results of the intra-group average difference of Cr, BUN, and GFR in the control group showed that the average difference between the pre-intervention and post-intervention stages in the study subjects was not significantly different. However, the comparison results of the intra-group average difference of Cr, BUN, and GFR in the case group showed that the average difference between the pre-intervention and post-intervention phases in the study subjects was significantly different (P < 0.05) (significant increase for Cr and BUN and for GFR, significantly decreased).

The therapeutic effects of the combination of alpha lipoic acid (ALA) and CoQ10 on cisplatin-induced nephrotoxicity were investigated in 2020 by Khalifa et al.[28] In this experimental study, adult male rats (40 people) weighing 200 to 250 g were divided into five groups: group one normal saline control, group two cisplatin control, group three CoQ10 and cisplatin, group four ALA and cisplatin, and group five CoQ10, ALA, and cisplatin. CoQ10 and/or ALA were given as pre-treatment for nine days, followed by an injection of cisplatin on day 10 of the study, followed by a short post-treatment period of three days.

Renal function, tissue antioxidant activity, and inflammatory markers (tumor necrosis factor: TNF) were estimated in association with histopathological study. The results of this research determined that the level of blood urea, serum creatinine, serum uric acid, urine albumin, and total protein in the second group was significantly higher than in the third, fourth, and fifth groups. No significant difference was observed among the groups treated with ALA and/or CoQ10 regarding these parameters.

Similarly, it was found that creatinine clearance was significantly reduced compared to the second group. When combination therapy was compared with monotherapy, combination therapy was found to result in a significant increase in creatinine clearance.[28] In general, this research showed that the role of antioxidants in preventing cisplatin-induced nephrotoxicity has been confirmed, and the preventive effect of combined treatment with CoQ10 and ALA is superior to monotherapy. The data of this study did not agree with the findings of our research due to the lack of examination of the therapeutic effects of the combination of ALA and CoQ10, as well as the different population of the study and work on animals.

A synergistic interaction between LA and CoQ10 in cultured skeletal muscle cells was demonstrated by Wagner et al. (2012).[26] They found that the compound significantly increased the nuclear levels of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor-γ coactivator 1-α (PGC1α), a master key to energy metabolism and mitochondrial biogenesis.

The therapeutic effects of CoQ10 on gentamicin-induced nephrotoxicity in lab rats were investigated in 2016 by Hameidi Zad. The study was conducted on 42 Wistar rats. Nephrotoxicity was induced by injection of gentamicin (100 mg/kg). The therapeutic effects of Q10 were investigated in two simultaneous and post-treatment protocols. Blood pressure and renal arterial blood flow were measured, and urea, creatinine, sodium, potassium, and osmolality were determined in plasma and urine samples.[27]

The findings of this study showed that the amount of renal blood flow in the gentamicin group was significantly reduced compared to the control group. In the groups of coenzymes Q10 alone and alcohol alone, there was no significant difference compared to the control group. Simultaneous treatment with CoQ10 for 8 days and post-treatment with CoQ10 increased the flow of blood compared to the gentamicin group and caused it to return close to the level of the control group. Administration of gentamicin increased the plasma concentration of Cr compared to the control group, but it was not significant. Also, treatment with CoQ10 decreased plasma Cr concentration compared to the gentamicin group, but it was not significant. Plasma and urine osmolality were not significantly different between the groups. By conducting this study, the researchers only found that compared to the simultaneous treatment, post-treatment with CoQ10 had a more protective effect on the kidney tissue and caused a greater increase in antioxidant defense and improved ion excretion.[27] The findings of this study were inconsistent with the data obtained from our research due to the investigation of the therapeutic effects of CoQ10 on gentamicin-induced nephrotoxicity in mice. They suggested that this work be investigated on humans.

However, the results of this research were in line with the results of Ahmed et al. (2015)[29] and Lopez-Novoa et al. (2011)[30]; because gentamicin increases the resistance of the kidney vessels and leads to a decrease in kidney blood flow. This decrease in renal blood flow (RBF) had an important role in decreasing GFR.

A study entitled Coenzyme Q10 multivitamin treatment and cisplatin toxicity in rats was conducted by Astolfi et al. in 2016. Cisplatin was administered intra-peritoneally in a single dose (14 mg/kg) or in three daily doses (4.6 mg/kg/day) to mice treated orally with or without Acu-Qter for 5 days.

Auditory function was evaluated by measuring auditory brainstem response from 2 to 32 kHz on day 0 and 5 days after treatment. The administration protocol consisting of three doses of cisplatin is more similar to the usual human chemotherapy protocols, so it seems more useful for long-term clinical studies in the prevention of ototoxicity.[28] The results of this study were inconsistent with the data obtained from our work in terms of animal studies and evaluation of the ototoxic effects of two different cisplatin administration protocols.

In 2000, Singh et al.[5] conducted a randomized trial of CoQ10 in chronic renal failure and investigated the positive effect of CoQ10 in them. In this study, people who were in the final stages of kidney failure were divided into two groups. The intervention group received 60 mg of ketone coenzyme per day. The duration of the intervention was 4 weeks. The intervention group experienced a significant decrease in serum creatinine level, blood urea level, and a significant increase in creatinine clearance. While before the intervention, there was no significant difference between the mentioned values between the two groups. The number of dialysis was even significantly reduced in the group receiving ketone, although there was no significant difference at the beginning of the intervention. Serum levels of vitamins A, E, and C, and beta-carotene showed a significant increase, and oxidant factors such as malondialdehyde and diene conjugates showed a significant decrease. The results of this study were inconsistent with the data obtained from our research due to the lack of examination of BUN, Cr, and GFR.

One of the limitations of this study was the small size of the sample, which would be considered in future studies with a larger number of people with this disease, as a larger number can make our findings stronger. The lack of histological investigations in the present study can be considered as another limitation that is suggested in future studies.

CONCLUSION

In general, this research determined that the average BUN, blood creatinine, and GFR did not differ between the two groups before and after intervention in study subjects; but by observing the intra-group average Cr, it showed that the difference between the average stages before and after the intervention in the studied subjects increased and GFR decreased; but in the case of BUN, on the contrary, no difference was seen. Authors recommend that regarding the positive effects of this drug on patients’ clinical parameters, health workers use this drug in patients’ treatment.

Clinical application

This study’s results can be used as a guide in drug administration for patients undergoing chemotherapy to reduce the renal side effects of prescribed drugs. Also, this study can be considered as a plan for other research in this field.

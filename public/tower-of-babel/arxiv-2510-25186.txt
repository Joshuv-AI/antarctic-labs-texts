Revisiting the Nandakumar-Ramana Rao Conjecture
Surojit Ghosh, Ankit Kumar

Abstract
. We reprove the generalized Nandakumar-Ramana Rao conjecture for the prime case using representation ring-graded Bredon cohomology. Our approach relies solely on the R ​ O ​ ( C p ) RO(C_{p}) -graded cohomology of configuration spaces, viewed as a module over the R ​ O ​ ( C p ) RO(C_{p}) -graded Bredon cohomology of a point.

1.
Introduction

Partitioning convex bodies in Euclidean spaces into convex pieces of equal measure is a long-standing problem in combinatorial geometry. This area of research originated with the celebrated
Ham-Sandwich Theorem
, which states that in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, it is possible to bisect
m
masses using a single hyperplane.

In 2006, Nandakumar and Ramana Rao proposed a geometric conjecture, asking whether,
for any convex shape in the plane and any positive integer
n
, there exists a partition of the shape into
n
convex pieces such that all pieces have equal area and equal perimeter
. They provided a positive answer to the conjecture for
n=2^{k}
in
[
12
]
. Later, Bárány, Blagojević, and Szűcs verified the case for
n=3
in
[
2
]
. It is claimed in
[
1
]
that the conjecture for general
n\geq 2
has been resolved. Notably, all these advances have been made for convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

A generalized version of the Nandakumar-Ramana Rao conjecture can be formulated as follows:

Let
K
be a
d
-dimensional convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
\mu
be an absolutely continuous measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
n\geq 2
be a natural number, and let
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{d-1}
be
d-1
continuous functions on the metric space of
d
-dimensional convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, there exists a partition of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
into
n
convex pieces
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
such that

\mu(P_{1}\cap K)=\cdots=\mu(P_{n}\cap K)

and

\varphi_{i}(P_{1}\cap K)=\cdots=\varphi_{i}(P_{n}\cap K)

for every
1\leq i\leq d-1
.

This problem can be reduced to determining the non-existence of a
\Sigma_{n}
-equivariant map from
\operatorname{Conf}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to a certain sphere, where
\operatorname{Conf}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the configuration space of
n
distinct labeled points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The generalized conjecture for
n
, where
n
is a prime power, was established for any dimension by Blagojević and Ziegler in
[
5
]
using equivariant obstruction theory. Independently, the same result was obtained in
[
9
]
based on a cell decomposition of the one-point compactification of the configuration space.
In
[
4
]
, the authors reprove the generalized version for arbitrary dimensions and prime power
n
using the Fadell-Husseini index of the configuration space.

In this paper, we present an alternative proof of the generalized Nandakumar-Ramana Rao conjecture for the case where the number of parts
n
is a prime
p
. Our approach establishes the non-existence of a
C_{p}
-equivariant map from the configuration space
\operatorname{Conf}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to a certain sphere in the
C_{p}
-representation. This method differs significantly from those previously described in the literature.

Specifically, we employ the module structure of the
RO(C_{p})
-graded Bredon cohomology of configuration spaces, viewed as a module over the
RO(C_{p})
-graded cohomology of a point.

Notation 1.1
.

(1)

The representation ring
RO(C_{p})
is generated by the trivial representation
1
, and the
2
-dimension representations
\xi^{k}
given by the rotation by the angle
\frac{2\pi k}{p}
for
k=1,\cdots,\frac{p-1}{2}
.

(2)

We use
\bigstar
to denote the
RO(G)
-grading, and
*
for the usual integer grading.

(3)

For
G=C_{p}
, with
p
an odd prime, we use
\bigstar_{\xi}
to denote the
m+n\xi
grading, where
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

2.
Preliminaries on Bredon cohomology

Ordinary cohomology theories are defined for abelian groups and are represented by spectra with homotopy concentrated in degree 0. In the equivariant setting, the analogous role is played by Mackey functors. In this section, we briefly recall their definition and relate them to equivariant cohomology (see
[
11
]
for details).

The
Burnside category

\sf{Burn}_{G}
is the category whose objects are finite
G
-sets. For objects
S
and
T
, the morphism set is given by the group completion of the hom-set of spans between
S
and
T
in the category of finite
G
-sets.

Definition 2.1
.

A
Mackey functor
is a functor
\underline{M}:\sf{Burn}_{G}^{op}\to\mathsf{Ab}
from the opposite of the Burnside category to the category of abelian groups.

In this article, we fix
G=C_{p}
, the cyclic group of prime order
p
.

Example 2.2
.

For a
G
-module
M
, we define a Mackey functor
\underline{M}
by the formula
\underline{M}(G/H)=M^{H}
. In particular, we consider the Mackey functors
\underline{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
, corresponding to the trivial
G
-modules
\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, respectively.

For a real
G
-representation
V
equipped with a
G
-invariant inner product, we define

S(V):=\{v\in V\mid\langle v,v\rangle=1\},

the unit sphere in
V
, and
S^{V}
, the one-point compactification of
V
. In equivariant homotopy theory, the
V
-fold suspension map

X\mapsto S^{V}\wedge X

is invertible. We refer to
[
10
]
for constructing the equivariant stable homotopy category, denoted by
\sf{Sp}^{G}
, which is the homotopy category of
G
-spectra.

Since the
V
-fold suspension map is invertible, one can define
S^{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in RO(G)
, the Grothendieck group completion of the monoid of irreducible representations of
G
. This construction induces an
RO(G)
-grading on homotopy groups. Specifically, for a based
G
-space
X
, the ordinary Bredon cohomology with coefficients in the Mackey functor
\underline{M}
at grading
\alpha\in RO(G)
is given by:

\tilde{H}^{\alpha}_{G}(X;\underline{M}):={\sf{Sp}}^{G}(X,S^{\alpha}\wedge H\underline{M}),

here
H\underline{M}
denotes the Eilenberg–Mac Lane spectrum (cf.
[
8
]
) for the Mackey functor
\underline{M}
defined as

\underline{\pi_{n}}(H\underline{M})=\begin{cases}\underline{M},&n=0,\\
0,&n\neq 0.\end{cases}

Remark 2.3
.

For
G=C_{p}
, one observes that it suffices to consider the grading of the form
m+n\xi
with
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
as
S^{\alpha}\wedge H\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\simeq H\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
whenever both the dimensions of
\alpha
and
\alpha^{C_{p}}
are zero (See
[
6
, Appendix B]
).

Let
V
be a
G
-representation. We denote by
a_{V}
the inclusion of the fixed points,

a_{V}:S^{0}\to S^{V}.

For a ring spectrum
X
with a
G
-action, we abuse notation and also denote by
a_{V}
its image under the map
S^{0}\to X
.

If the representation
V
contains the trivial representation as a summand, then
a_{V}=0
.
Moreover, for any two
G
-representations
V
and
W
, we have the relation

a_{V}a_{W}=a_{V\oplus W}.

See
[
8
, Definition 3.11]
for further details.

For an orientable
G
-representation
V:G\to SO(V)
, a choice of orientation induces an isomorphism

\tilde{H}^{\dim(V)}_{G}(S^{V};\underline{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\mathbb{Z}.

The Thom space of the equivariant bundle
V\to G/G
is
S^{V}
. In particular, the restriction map

\tilde{H}^{\dim(V)}_{G}(S^{V};\underline{\mathbb{Z}})\to\tilde{H}^{\dim(V)}_{e}(S^{\dim(V)};\mathbb{Z})

is an isomorphism.

Utilizing the above isomorphism, for an orientable
G
-representation
V
, we define the orientation class
u_{V}
as

u_{V}\in\tilde{H}^{V-\dim(V)}_{G}(S^{0};\underline{\mathbb{Z}}),

the generator that maps to
1
under the restriction isomorphism. The orientation class satisfies the relations:

u_{V\oplus 1}=u_{V},\quad u_{V}\cdot u_{W}=u_{V\oplus W}.

See
[
8
, Definition 3.12]
for more details.

We use the same notations for the image of the classes
a_{V}
and
u_{V}
in
\tilde{H}^{\bigstar}_{G}(S^{0};\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}})
under the spectrum map
H\underline{\mathbb{Z}}\to H\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}.
Whenever needed, we shall write
\mathbb{M}_{p}
to refer to the restricted
RO(C_{p})
-graded cohomology ring
\tilde{H}^{\bigstar_{\xi}}_{C_{p}}(S^{0};\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}})
.

4.
Connection with equivariant maps and the proof

4.1.
The equivariant map

The general idea of the “Configuration space/Test map” scheme says an affirmative answer to a topological combinatorics problem is equivalent to the non-existence of some equivariant map from a specific configuration space to the test space.

In
[
9
]
, this scheme is applied to investigate the validity of the Nandakumar-Ramana Rao conjecture and its generalized version, as follows:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
d\geq 1,n\geq 2
be integers. Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a
d
-dimensional convex body. If there is no
\Sigma_{n}
-equivariant map of the form

\operatorname{Conf}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to S(W_{n}^{\oplus(d-1)})

then the Nandakumar-Ramana Rao conjecture has a solution.

In the above proposition, the action of
\Sigma_{n}
on the standard
\Sigma_{n}
-representation
W_{n}=\{(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\sum v_{i}=0\}
is given by permuting the coordinates. Since
C_{n}\subset\Sigma_{n}
, the above spaces inherit the restricted
C_{n}
-action. Consequently, the non-existence of a
C_{n}
-equivariant map is sufficient to affirm the conjecture.

4.2.
Bredon cohomology of representation spheres

Let
V
be a
G
-representation. We define
\mathcal{C}_{[\supseteq V]}
– the collection of
G
-representations
W
such that
V
is a sub-representation of
W
. We denote by
W\ominus V
, the orthogonal complement of
V
in
W.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
V\in\operatorname{Rep}(C_{p}),

\tilde{H}^{W}_{C_{p}}(S(V)_{+};\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}})=0
, where
W\in\mathcal{C}_{[\supseteq V]}.

Proof.

If
V
is reducible with a summand
L
, that is,
V\in\mathcal{C}_{[\supseteq L]},
consider the following
G
-cofiber sequence

S(V\ominus L)_{+}\to S(V)_{+}\to S^{V\ominus L}\wedge S(L)_{+}

which induces the long exact sequence in Bredon cohomology:

\cdots\tilde{H}^{\bigstar-(V\ominus L)}_{G}(S(L)_{+};\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}})\to\tilde{H}^{\bigstar}_{G}(S(V)_{+};\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}})\to\tilde{H}^{\bigstar}_{G}(S(V\ominus L)_{+};\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}})\to\cdots

By induction on the dimension of the representation
V
and using
\bigstar=W\in\mathcal{C}_{[\supseteq V]}
, it follows that both corner groups are trivial, leading to the desired result.

Therefore, we are left with the case when
V
is irreducible, that is,

for some

Now consider the
C_{p}
-cofiber sequence (in

-spectra)

(4.3)

{}C_{p}/e_{+}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1-g^{k}}}{{\to}}C_{p}/e_{+}\to S(\xi^{k})_{+},

where we fix

The associated long exact sequence in Bredon cohomology for (

Proposition 4.4
.

For a cyclic group
G
with order having two distinct prime divisors, the Euler class
a_{\bar{\varrho}}
vanishes. Here,
\bar{\varrho}
denotes the reduced regular representation of
G.

Proof.

Let
G=C_{n}
be the cyclic group of order
n
, and let
p\neq q
be two distinct prime divisors of
n
. We construct two subrepresentations
V_{p}
and
V_{q}
of the reduced regular representation
\bar{\varrho}
such that

\gcd\left(\frac{|G|}{|G_{V_{p}}|},\frac{|G|}{|G_{V_{q}}|}\right)=1,

where
G_{V}
denotes the stabilizer subgroup of the representation
V
.

To achieve this, let
\eta
be a non-trivial one-dimensional complex
G
-representation induced by a group homomorphism
\eta\colon G\to S^{1}
, with kernel
H=\ker(\eta)
. The corresponding representation sphere
S^{\eta}
admits a
G
-CW complex structure of the form

S^{0}\cup(G/H_{+}\wedge e^{1})\cup(G/H_{+}\wedge e^{2}).

The associated equivariant cellular chain complex with constant coefficients
\underline{\mathbb{Z}}
is

\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{0}\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{|G/H|}\mathbb{Z},

which gives rise to the isomorphism

\tilde{H}^{\eta}_{G}(S^{0};\underline{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\mathbb{Z}/|G/H|\mathbb{Z}.

In particular, this implies that
|G/H|\cdot a_{\eta}=0
.

We now choose the subrepresentations
V_{p}=\xi^{n/p}
and
V_{q}=\xi^{n/q}
, where
\xi^{d}
denotes the one-dimensional complex representation of
G
corresponding to rotation by angle
\frac{2\pi d}{n}
. Since the indices
\frac{|G|}{|G_{V_{p}}|}
and
\frac{|G|}{|G_{V_{q}}|}
are coprime, it follows that the Euler class
a_{\bar{\varrho}}=0
.
∎

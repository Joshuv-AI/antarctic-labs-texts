Multimodal radiomics incorporating intratumoral heterogeneity for prognostic assessment of metastatic outcomes in invasive breast cancer
Authors: Liwei Sun, Zhengtong Wang, Weizhi Zhang, Yunfei Yang, Pei Nie, Wenjian Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aimed to develop prognostic models of distant metastasis (DM) and bone metastasis (BM) in invasive breast cancer (IBC) incorporating whole-tumor radiomics, intratumoral heterogeneity (ITH) metrics.MethodsA total of 1097 patients with IBC from three centers were included and divided into a training cohort (n = 610), a validation cohort (n = 153), and two independent testing cohorts (n = 170 and n = 164). Whole-tumor radiomic features were extracted from two-dimensional regions of interest (ROIs) on mammography and three-dimensional ROIs on the second post-contrast phase of dynamic contrast–enhanced MRI, while ITH metrics were derived from enhancement-based subregions. Predictive performance was assessed for each model, and the resulting risk scores were further used for risk stratification analyses. SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) were used to assess the importance of individual ITH metrics and to quantify the contributions of each component within the combined model.ResultsThe combined model achieved the highest concordance index and robust risk stratification performance for both DM and BM. SHAP analysis indicated that the radiomics-based risk score ranked as the most influential predictor across both endpoints, while the ITH score consistently showed higher relative importance for BM than for DM. Distance-, shape-, and topology-related ITH metrics were among the most influential contributors across both endpoints.ConclusionThe combined model demonstrated robust prognostic performance, and the differential contribution of the ITH score between DM and BM indicates a more prominent role of imaging-derived heterogeneity in BM.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13058-026-02287-8.Keywords: Invasive breast cancer, Radiomics, ITH, Multimodal, Distant metastasis, Bone metastasis

Introduction

Invasive breast cancer (IBC) exhibits a pronounced propensity to metastasize to distant organs, accounting for the vast majority of breast cancer–related deaths [1]. Once metastasis occurs, the prognosis deteriorates dramatically, with a 5-year overall survival rate of only 27% [2]. Consequently, the early identification of IBC patients at high risk for distant metastasis (DM) is of paramount importance, as it enables the development of individualized therapeutic strategies and improves patient outcomes [3]. Bone metastasis (BM) represents the most common metastatic pattern in IBC, associating with skeletal-related events (SREs), such as refractory pain and pathological fractures [4, 5]. However, the majority of existing prognostic models primarily address overall disease progression, with comparatively few imaging-based models targeting the prediction of BM risk [6, 7]. Additionally, there is a lack of studies that evaluate DM and BM concurrently as dual endpoints, underscoring an unmet need for integrative prognostic modeling in this area.

Mammography (MG), as a cornerstone of IBC screening and diagnosis, is widely accessible and provides valuable information for assessing metastatic risk [8–10]. Dynamic contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (DCE-MRI) is recognized as the most sensitive imaging modality for breast cancer detection [3]. Notably, the second post-contrast phase of DCE-MRI reflects a relatively stable intratumoral distribution of contrast agent and has been closely associated with tumor perfusion, microvascular density, and vascular permeability, making it a biologically meaningful phase for characterizing tumor heterogeneity [11–13].

Radiomics features can be extracted either from the whole tumor to characterize global imaging phenotypes or from biologically distinct intratumoral subregions to quantify spatial heterogeneity [14–16]. In IBC, whole-tumor radiomics captures overall tumor intensity distribution, and texture patterns [17, 18]. In parallel, tumors can be subdivided into subregions defined by contrast enhancement patterns, from which radiomics features are extracted to calculate intratumoral heterogeneity (ITH) metrics [14, 15, 19]. These enhancement-based ITH metrics reflect regional variations in tumor vascularity and microenvironment, providing complementary information beyond whole-tumor analysis.

By integrating multimodal radiomics features from MG and MRI with quantitative ITH metrics, we aim to develop a robust and interpretable prognostic model for both DM and BM in patients with IBC. Such a model could provide clinically actionable insights, supporting tailored therapeutic decision-making. This study extends the DM prediction framework by incorporating BM as an additional endpoint, with the aim of exploring whether the prognostic determinants driving BM differ from those associated with DM.

Materials and methods

Study population

We retrospectively reviewed data from patients newly diagnosed with IBC without evidence of DM between January 2018 and December 2022.

Inclusion criteria were as follows: (a) Pathologically confirmed diagnosis of IBC; (b) No clinical or imaging evidence of DM at initial diagnosis; (c) Availability of both preoperative MG and DCE-MRI; (d) Interval between preoperative imaging and surgery ≤ 2 weeks; (e) Completed clinical and imaging data; (f) Completed two years of follow-up.

Exclusion criteria included: (a) Receipt of neoadjuvant therapy prior to imaging; (b) Presence of another primary malignancy; (c) Poor image quality unsuitable for analysis.

A total of 1097 patients met the inclusion criteria. Among them, 610 patients from the Affiliated Hospital of Qingdao University constituted the training cohort, 153 patients formed the validation cohort, and 170 patients from the Affiliated Hospital of Jining Medical University were assigned to the testing 1 cohort, 164 patients from the First Affiliated Hospital of Shandong First Medical University were assigned to the testing 2 cohort.

The primary endpoint of this study was distant metastasis–free survival (DMFS), defined as the interval between imaging examination and the first confirmed DM, as verified by imaging and/or histopathological evidence. The secondary endpoint was bone metastasis–free survival (BMFS). For BMFS analysis, patients who developed non-bone distant metastases were censored. Patients were followed until the occurrence of DM or the end of follow-up (December 2024).

Imaging acquisition

Imaging data were acquired using different scanners across centers. Detailed acquisition parameters are provided in Supplementary Table S1. All images were stored in DICOM format. Given its relative perfusion stability, the second post-contrast phase of DCE-MRI was selected for radiomic feature extraction and subsequent analysis and is hereinafter referred to as MRI for simplicity.

Radiomics and ITH model development

Image segmentation and preprocessing

Regions of interest (ROIs) were manually delineated using ITK-SNAP software (http://www.itksnap.org). Independent delineations of 100 randomly selected tumors by two radiologists (7 and 15 years of experience) yielded an inter-observer intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) > 0.75, indicating high segmentation reliability. For MG, 2D ROIs were outlined on the lesion-containing slice, whereas for MRI, 3D ROIs were contoured volumetrically across all slices encompassing the tumor. Before feature extraction, all images were resampled to a uniform spatial resolution. N4 bias field correction was applied to reduce MRI intensity inhomogeneity at the image level. Subsequently, Z-score normalization was performed within tumor ROIs to standardize intensity distributions across cases.

Feature engineering and model construction

Whole-tumor radiomic features, including first-order intensity statistics and higher-order texture features from original and wavelet-transformed images, were extracted separately from MG and MRI ROIs using PyRadiomics (https://pyradiomics.readthedocs.io/) and then concatenated for subsequent analyses. To reduce feature dimensionality and redundancy, minimum redundancy–maximum relevance (mRMR) was applied in the training cohort as a preliminary filtering step. Subsequently, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) Cox regression was used to identify prognostically relevant features. The optimal regularization parameter (λ) was determined using 10-fold cross-validation, and the feature subset corresponding to the minimum cross-validated error (λ_min) was retained for model development. Prior to LASSO, all radiomic features were standardized using Z-score normalization based on the training cohort.

For ITH analysis, each 3D ROI was partitioned into three intratumoral subregions (high, intermediate, and low enhancement) using percentile-based intensity thresholding [20]. The same three-subregion scheme was applied to all patients. Radiomic features were extracted from each subregion, and inter-subregional variability was quantified to derive ITH metrics reflecting spatial tumor heterogeneity. Eleven ITH metrics were calculated, namely ITH_euclidean, ITH_cosine, ITH_mahalanobis, Shape_ITH_CV, Shape_ITH_range, Intensity_ITH, Texture_ITH, JS_ITH, DiffEntropy_ITH, Topology_ITH, and Rank_ITH [14, 19, 21, 22] (details provided in Supplementary Material). The contribution of individual ITH metrics to model predictions was further evaluated using SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP).

Radiomic features and ITH metrics were separately used to construct radiomics- and ITH-based Cox proportional hazards models. To avoid information leakage, cross-validated radiomics scores (Rad scores) and ITH scores were generated for each patient and subsequently integrated using a late-fusion strategy to construct a combined Rad_ITH Cox model for survival prediction.

Clinical and imaging semantic variables analysis

Baseline clinical variables, including age, T stage, N stage, molecular subtype, histological subtype, histological grade, lymphovascular invasion (LVI), and adjuvant chemotherapy, were retrieved from electronic medical records.

Imaging semantic variables on MG and MRI—including breast density, background parenchymal enhancement, number of tumors, tumor location and signal, calcification, time-intensity curve (TIC) type, adjacent vessel sign (AVS), boundary, spiculation, and lobulation—were initially evaluated by a junior radiologist (7 years of experience) and subsequently reviewed by a senior radiologist (15 years of experience).

T and N stage were used to establish a TN-based Cox model (TN model) due to their well-established prognostic value. Subsequently, univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed to identify additional prognostic clinical and imaging semantic variables beyond T and N stage to construct the clinical model.

Construction of the combined model

A combined prognostic model was constructed within a Cox proportional hazards framework. This late-fusion strategy enabled the integration of complementary information from whole-tumor radiomics, intratumoral heterogeneity, T and N stage, and other clinicoradiological characteristics into a unified risk prediction model.

Performance evaluation

The radiomics, ITH, Rad_ITH, TN, clinical, and combined models were evaluated for both DMFS and BMFS. Predictive discrimination was assessed using the concordance index (C-index), time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis over a 12–36month follow-up period, and the integrated area under the curve (iAUC) at 24 and 36 months. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed at 24 months to compare the net clinical benefit of different models.

For the combined model, risk stratification was further evaluated using Kaplan–Meier (KM) survival curves with log-rank tests. Risk score plots and SHAP analysis were used to visualize patient-level risk distributions and assess feature importance. The overall workflow is shown in Fig. 1.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.5.1; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria), SPSS Statistics (version 29.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA), and Python (version 3.10; Python Software Foundation, Wilmington, DE, USA). Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation and compared using the Student’s t-test, ANOVA, or Kruskal–Wallis test, as appropriate. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

For all Cox models, the proportional hazards assumption was assessed using Schoenfeld residuals, with no significant violations detected. Risk scores were defined as the linear predictors of the combined Cox proportional hazards model (β × X). SHAP analysis was conducted to assess the relative importance of individual features, with background samples defined within each cohort. Detailed computational procedures are provided in the Supplementary Material.

Radiomics features selection and heterogeneity interpretation

A total of 1674 radiomics features were initially extracted, including 837 features from MG and 837 features from MRI. After feature selection using LASSO Cox regression, 14 features were retained for the DM model and 10 features for the BM model. The corresponding feature selection paths and cross-validation results are presented in Supplemental Figs. S1 and S2.

SHAP analysis was subsequently performed to assess the relative importance of ITH metrics, and the resulting feature importance patterns for DM and BM are summarized in Supplemental Fig. S3. For both DM and BM outcomes, the top five ITH metrics were identical, namely ITH_euclidean, ITH_mahalanobis, Shape_ITH_CV, JS_ITH, and Topology_ITH. Performance evaluation of various models

Across the two external testing cohorts, the combined model consistently demonstrated the highest discriminative performance for both DMFS and BMFS, as reflected by the highest C-index and iAUC values among all models (Table 4). The Rad_ITH model achieved improved performance compared with the radiomics-only and ITH-only models, indicating complementary prognostic information between conventional radiomics features and ITH metrics, although the incremental gain was modest as partial information overlap may exist. Time-dependent ROC curves further confirmed the superior and stable discriminative ability of the combined model over the follow-up period for both endpoints (Fig. 2 and Supplemental Fig. S4). DCA curves demonstrated that the combined model provided greater net clinical benefit than treat-all and treat-none strategies over a wide range of threshold probabilities for both DMFS and BMFS (Fig. 3 and Supplemental Fig. S5).

Discussion

Most radiomics studies in breast cancer have focused on lesion characterization, treatment response, or axillary lymph node metastasis, while survival prediction of DM, particularly BM, remains underexplored [19, 23–28]. The development of DM marks progression to incurable stage IV disease. Bone is the most common metastatic site in breast cancer, and SREs substantially impair quality of life. Early identification of high-risk patients may enable risk-adapted surveillance and timely bone-modifying therapy. Therefore, assessment of BMFS may provide clinically relevant information beyond DMFS alone. Few studies have modeled BMFS as an independent endpoint using imaging-based biomarkers. Moreover, ITH metrics have not been systematically incorporated into the prediction of DMFS or BMFS. In this multicenter study, we developed and externally validated a model integrating whole-tumor radiomics, ITH metrics, TN staging, and clinicoradiological variables. Comparison of six models across four independent cohorts demonstrated the incremental value of whole-tumor radiomics and ITH for metastasis assessment.

SHAP analysis consistently identified ITH_euclidean, ITH_mahalanobis, Shape_ITH_CV, JS_ITH, and Topology_ITH as the most influential heterogeneity metrics. Compared with other ITH metrics, these features capture higher-order relationships between intratumoral subregions by integrating distance, distributional dissimilarity, and geometric variability, rather than relying on single-scale intensity statistics [21, 29–31]. Compared with whole-tumor radiomics, which primarily captures global phenotypic aggressiveness [21, 30, 32], ITH provides a refined description of spatial ecological diversity within the tumor. This distinction likely explains the additional prognostic information contributed by ITH metrics.

Across all cohorts, the combined model achieved the highest C-index and iAUC values within this study for both DMFS and BMFS. C-index values ranged from 0.870 to 0.893 for DMFS and from 0.846 to 0.885 for BMFS. In previously reported breast cancer radiomics studies focusing on nodal status or short-term outcomes, C-index values have typically ranged from 0.75 to 0.82 [33, 34]. Radiomics-only models showed moderate discrimination, consistent with prior studies [33, 35, 36]. The ITH, TN and clinical models yielded stable but lower performance, indicating that these variables capture partial metastatic information. The superior performance of the combined model underscores the complementary nature of global phenotype, spatial heterogeneity, and clinicopathological context. Additionally, the favorable performance observed in DCA suggests that the combined model may provide potential clinical benefit.

The consistent risk stratification observed across cohorts suggests that the combined model captures stable prognostic patterns beyond cohort-specific variability. The concordant findings from KM curves and risk score distributions support the robustness of the proposed risk score with respect to both DMFS and BMFS. The patterns observed in the risk score heatmaps indicate that whole-tumor radiomics predominantly drives patient-level risk differentiation, whereas ITH metrics provide a secondary but meaningful refinement.

Based on the SHAP analysis of combined models, ITH score demonstrated a consistent increase in relative importance for BMFS across both external testing cohorts. This pattern suggests that BM risk may be more sensitive to spatial and ecological heterogeneity within the primary tumor than DM. BM more likely reflects additional biological constraints imposed by bone microenvironment, where subclonal diversity, metabolic adaptability, and niche-specific tumor–stroma interactions play a critical role [37, 38]. Imaging-derived heterogeneity metrics may partially capture these properties through spatial variations in enhancement patterns, which have been linked to aggressive subpopulations and organ-specific metastatic potential [39]. Notably, this SHAP-based observation is concordant with the clinical variable selection results, in which spiculation—an imaging marker of infiltrative growth—was retained as an independent predictor for DMFS but not for BMFS. Together, these findings might indicate divergent metastatic mechanisms, whereby general metastatic dissemination is more strongly associated with invasive growth patterns, whereas bone-specific metastasis may be driven to a greater extent by intrinsic tumor heterogeneity. The consistency of these patterns across independent cohorts supports the robustness of this endpoint-specific interpretation.

Several limitations warrant consideration. First, this study relied on handcrafted radiomics features, and deep learning–based representations may further enhance performance. Second, habitat partitioning was based solely on enhancement intensity, incorporating pharmacokinetic parameters or multi-parametric MRI may better capture tumor ecology. Third, although several preprocessing steps were applied to reduce intensity variability across scanners, explicit feature-level harmonization methods such as ComBat were not implemented. Fourth, molecular and genomic data were unavailable, precluding direct radiogenomic correlation. Finally, although external validation was performed, prospective multicenter studies are required to further confirm clinical utility. Future work should focus on integrating deep learning, advanced habitat modeling, and molecular profiling to further refine personalized metastasis prediction.

Conclusion

We developed and externally validated a model for predicting DM and BM in IBC. By integrating whole-tumor radiomics, ITH metrics, and clinicoradiological variables, the combined model demonstrated stable and superior performance. Furthermore, SHAP-based interpretation revealed differences in feature contribution between DM and BM, potentially indicating distinct mechanisms underlying DM and BM.

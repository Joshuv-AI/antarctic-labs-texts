On the semi-infinite cohomology of graded-unitary vertex algebras
Christopher Beem, Niklas Garner

Abstract
. Recently, the first author with A. Ardehali, M. Lemos, and L. Rastelli introduced the notion of graded unitarity for vertex algebras. This generalization of unitarity is motivated by the SCFT/VOA correspondence and introduces a novel Hilbert space structure on the state space of a large class of vertex algebras that are not unitary in the conventional sense. In this paper, we study the relative semi-infinite cohomology of graded-unitary vertex algebras that admit a chiral quantum moment map for an affine current algebra at twice the critical level. We show that the relative semi-infinite chain complex for such a graded-unitary vertex algebra has a structure analogous to that of differential forms on a compact Kähler manifold, generalizing a strong form of the classic construction of Banks–Peskin and Frenkel–Garland–Zuckerman. We deduce that the relative semi-infinite cohomology is itself graded-unitary, which establishes graded unitarity for a large class of vertex operator algebras arising from three- and four-dimensional supersymmetric quantum field theories. We further establish an outer USp ⁡ ( 2 ) {\rm USp}(2) action on the semi-infinite cohomology (which does not respect cohomological grading), analogous to the Lefschetz 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{sl}(2) in Kähler geometry. We also show that the semi-infinite chain complex is quasi-isomorphic as a differential graded vertex algebra to its cohomology, in analogy to the formality result of Deligne–Griffiths–Morgan–Sullivan for the de Rham cohomology of compact Kähler manifolds. We conclude by observing consequences of these results to the associated Poisson vertex algebras and related finite-type derived Poisson reductions.

Introduction

Vertex algebras arise in a variety of physical systems that possess observables whose correlations depend holomorphically or meromorphically on their positions on a plane (or Riemann surface) embedded in spacetime. Depending on the precise physical setting in which they originate, they may be equipped with additional structures. For example, when there is a conserved current implementing translations, the vertex algebra possesses a stress tensor/conformal vector, leading to a vertex operator algebra (VOA), or a compatible (shifted) vertex Lie structure if there are additional topological directions transverse to the vertex algebra plane leading to a (shifted) Poisson vertex algebra.

2.3.
Quaternionic structures and graded unitarity

Recall that if
x,y
are states in a Möbius vertex algebra with conformal weight
h
, the pairing
(x,y)
satisfies

(x,y)=(-1)^{|x||y|+2h}(y,x)~.

In defining notions of unitarity one expects a Hermitian form, which necessitates introducing additional structure with which to modify the bilinear form. Due to the physical similarity between graded-unitary VOAs and Hermitian short star-products coming from the deformation quantization of hyperkähler cones—the former
[
BLL+15
,
ABLR25
]
being a four-dimensional analogue of the latter
[
BPR17
]
—we adopt similar nomenclature and notation to
[
ES20
]
.

Definition 2.19
.

A
conjugation
of a vertex algebra
{\mathcal{V}}
is an anti-linear vector space isomorphism
\rho:{\mathcal{V}}\to{\mathcal{V}}
that commutes with the translation operator

\rho\circ\partial=\partial\circ\rho~,

preserves the vacuum vector

\rho(|0\rangle)=|0\rangle~,

and satisfies

\rho(x_{n}y)=(-1)^{|x||y|}\rho(x)_{n}\rho(y)

for every homogeneous
x,y\in{\mathcal{V}}
and
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. In the Möbius case,
\rho
is required to commute with
L_{\pm 1,0}

L_{m}\circ\rho=\rho\circ L_{m}~,\quad m\in\{-1,0,1\}~.

In the VOA case,
\rho
is required to preserve the conformal vector,

\rho(\omega)=\omega~.

If
{\mathcal{F}}_{\bullet}
is a half-integral filtration on
{\mathcal{V}}
, we require that
\rho
preserves each filtered component

\rho({\mathcal{F}}_{p}{\mathcal{V}})\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}_{p}{\mathcal{V}}~.

Remark 2.20
.

If
{\mathcal{V}}
is a Möbius vertex algebra of CFT type, it follows that
\rho(|0\rangle)
is a non-zero multiple of
|0\rangle
and hence
\rho
preserves the vacuum vector automatically as
|0\rangle_{-1}=\text{id}_{\mathcal{V}}
. When
{\mathcal{V}}
is a VOA of CFT type, it suffices to require that a conjugation satisfies

\rho(x_{n}y)=(-1)^{|x||y|}\rho(x)_{n}\rho(y)~,

and preserves the conformal vector. Indeed, as
\partial=L_{-1}
, these conditions imply that
\rho
commutes with
\partial
and, moreover, ensures that it preserves conformal weight spaces. Finally, the filtration-preserving property of
\rho
, along with the definition of half-integral filtration, implies that
\rho
commutes with
s
.

The following is a straightforward application of the skew-symmetry property of a vertex algebra and the definition of a conjugation.

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}
be a Möbius vertex algebra equipped with a (nondegenerate) invariant bilinear form
(-,-)
and let
\rho
be a conjugation of
{\mathcal{V}}
. Then

(\rho(x),\rho(y))=\overline{(y,x)}~.

With this lemma in mind, we make the following definition.

Definition 2.22
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}
be a Möbius vertex algebra equipped with a nondegenerate invariant bilinear form
(-,-)
and let
{\mathcal{F}}_{\bullet}
be a nondegenerate half-integral filtration of
({\mathcal{V}},(-,-))
. A
quaternionic structure
on
({\mathcal{V}},(-,-),{\mathcal{F}}_{\bullet})
is a conjugation
\rho
further satisfying

\rho\circ\rho=s~,\qquad\rho\circ\sigma=\sigma^{-1}\circ\rho~.

Remark 2.23
.

By the filtration-preserving property of
\rho
, the condition
\rho\circ\rho=s
implies that
\rho
preserves graded subspaces
{\mathcal{V}}_{h,p}
. It follows that
\rho\circ\sigma=\sigma^{-1}\circ\rho
automatically.

Corollary 2.24
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}
be a Möbius vertex algebra equipped with a nondegenerate invariant bilinear form
(-,-)
,
{\mathcal{F}}_{\bullet}
a nondegenerate half-integral filtration of
({\mathcal{V}},(-,-))
, and
\rho
a quaternionic structure on
({\mathcal{V}},(-,-),{\mathcal{F}}_{\bullet})
. The sesquilinear form defined by

\langle x|y\rangle\coloneqq((\sigma\circ\rho)(x),y)~,

is Hermitian,

\langle x|y\rangle=\overline{\langle y|x\rangle}~.

Remark 2.25
.

Note that we use the convention from physics that a sesquilinear form is linear in the second argument and anti-linear in the first.

Proof.

We compute:

\displaystyle\langle x|y\rangle

\displaystyle=((\sigma\circ\rho)(x),y)=(\rho(x),\sigma(y))=\overline{((\rho^{-1}\circ\sigma)(y),x)}

\displaystyle=\overline{((\sigma\circ\rho)(y),x)}=\overline{\langle y|x\rangle}~.

∎

cf.

[
ABLR25

Definition 2.26

A

is a quadruple (

,
(-,-)
,

,

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

and

Remark 2.27

Following
[

Physical Remark 2.28

Corollary

implies that a weak graded-unitary vertex algebra of CFT type is necessarily simple. In particular, all VOAs appearing in the SCFT/VOA correspondence are simple. This is a result that is well known to experts, but we do not believe an explicit proof has appeared in the literature.

An immediate consequence of weak graded unitarity is an analogue of the spin-statistics relation, which relates the Grassmann parity to spin (half-integrality of conformal weight) and
\mathfrak{R}

Proposition 2.29

(Graded-Unitary Spin-Statistics)
.

Let
({\mathcal{V}}
,
(-,-)
,
\mathfrak{R}_{\bullet}
,
\rho)
be a weak graded-unitary vertex algebra. Then the Grassmann parity of any nonzero element
x\in{\mathcal{V}}_{h,R}
satisfies

(-1)^{|x|}=(-1)^{2(h+R)}~.

Proof.

Choose
x\in{\mathcal{V}}_{h,R}
nonzero. We compute:

\displaystyle\langle\rho(x)|\rho(x)\rangle

\displaystyle=\text{i}^{2R}(\rho^{2}(x),\rho(x))

\displaystyle=\text{i}^{2R}(-1)^{2R}(x,\rho(x))

\displaystyle=\text{i}^{2R}(-1)^{2R}(-1)^{|x||x|+2h}(\rho(x),x)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{2R+|x|+2h}\langle x|x\rangle~.

As
\langle-|-\rangle
is positive-definite, both
\langle\rho(x)|\rho(x)\rangle
and
\langle x|x\rangle
are positive and hence

1=(-1)^{2R+|x|+2h}~,

as desired.
∎

Remark 2.30
.

The vector space underlying a weak graded-unitary vertex algebra is naturally a
\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{N}}\times\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{N}}
-graded complex Hilbert space,

{\mathcal{V}}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\in\scriptstyle{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbb{N}}\\
R\in\scriptstyle{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbb{N}}\end{subarray}}{\mathcal{V}}_{h,R}~,

with finite-dimensional graded components such that the inner product
\langle x|y\rangle
of two homogeneous elements vanishes if
(h_{x},R_{x})\neq(h_{y},R_{y})
. We note that for a quasi-primary
x\in{\mathcal{V}}
of conformal weight
h_{x}
, the adjoint of
x_{n}
with respect to
\langle-|-\rangle
is given by

(x_{n})^{\dagger}=e^{\text{i}\pi h_{x}}\sigma^{-1}\circ\rho\circ x_{2h_{x}-2-n}\circ\rho^{-1}\circ\sigma~.

This can be seen by the following computation:

\displaystyle\langle x(z)u|v\rangle

\displaystyle=((\sigma\circ\rho)(x(z)u),v)=(\rho(x)(z)\rho(u),\sigma(v))

\displaystyle=(e^{\text{i}\pi}z^{-2})^{h_{x}}(\rho(u),\rho(x)(z^{-1})\sigma(v))

\displaystyle=(e^{\text{i}\pi}z^{-2})^{h_{x}}\langle u|(\sigma^{-1}\circ\rho)\circ x(z^{-1})\circ(\rho^{-1}\circ\sigma)v\rangle~.

where
u,v
are arbitrary vectors in

Proposition 3.7

The vertex algebra

4.
Consequences of graded unitarity

The observations of the previous section have important consequences for the structure of the vertex algebra
C(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}},\mathfrak{g},{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter})
and the relative semi-infinite cohomology
H^{\scriptstyle{\frac{\infty}{2}}+\bullet}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}},\mathfrak{g},{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter})
. Our first result establishes that the cohomology inherits a graded-unitary structure from
{\mathcal{V}}
. This proves, in particular, that the VOAs coming from Lagrangian
{\mathcal{N}}=2
SCFTs in four dimensions all possess a graded-unitary structure.

We then establish two properties that follow from the
\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}
lemma (Corollary
3.26
). The first is that the cohomology inherits an
{\rm USp}(2)
automorphism from that of
C(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}},\mathfrak{g},{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter})
. The second property is that the DG vertex algebra
(C(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}},\mathfrak{g},{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter}),\mathbf{Q}^{-})
is connected by a (chain of) quasi-isomorphisms of DG vertex algebras that to its cohomology (equipped with a trivial differential). We expect that in a suitable homotopy theory of vertex algebras this will amount to the formality for these DG vertex algebras.

For the third and final property, we use the full Kähler package (Theorem
3.20
) and consider reductive Lie algebras with multiple simple and abelian factors; we show that the relative semi-infinite cohomology for the full Lie algebra is isomorphic to an iterated relative semi-infinite cohomology for its simple factors. All Möbius vertex algebras appearing in this section should be assumed to be of CFT type.

4.1.
Graded unitarity of relative semi-infinite cohomology

The aim of this subsection is to prove the following Theorem:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter}
be a weak graded-unitary vertex algebra with a good Hamiltonian
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}}
action. Then the relative semi-infinite cohomology
H^{\scriptstyle{\frac{\infty}{2}}+\bullet}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}},\mathfrak{g},{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter})
is a weak graded-unitary vertex algebra. If
{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter}
is graded-unitary then so too is
H^{\scriptstyle{\frac{\infty}{2}}+\bullet}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}},\mathfrak{g},{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter})
.

The immediate complication in deducing this result is that the conjugation
\rho
does not commute with
\mathbf{Q}^{-}
, instead satisfying

\rho\circ\mathbf{Q}^{-}=\text{i}\mathbf{Q}^{+}\circ\rho~.

We circumvent this issue by way of the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter}
be a vertex algebra with a Hamiltonian
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}}
action such that
C(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}},\mathfrak{g},{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter})
satisfies the
\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}
lemma (
e.g.
, if
{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter}
is a weak graded-unitary vertex algebra and the action is good). Then there is an isomorphism of vertex algebras

H^{\scriptstyle{\frac{\infty}{2}}+\bullet}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}},\mathfrak{g},{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter})\coloneqq\frac{\ker\mathbf{Q}^{-}}{{\rm im}\mathbf{Q}^{-}}\cong\frac{\ker\mathbf{Q}^{-}\cap\ker\mathbf{Q}^{+}}{{\rm im}\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}}~.

Proof.

This result follows from the
\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}
lemma by showing that the inclusion

\ker\mathbf{Q}^{-}\cap\ker\mathbf{Q}^{+}\hookrightarrow\ker\mathbf{Q}^{-}

induces the desired isomorphism. We start with injectivity; choose an element
x
with
\mathbf{Q}^{\pm}x=0
representing a class in the source and suppose its image vanishes,
i.e.

x
is
\mathbf{Q}^{-}
-exact. The
\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}
lemma then implies
x=\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}\widetilde{x}
, hence
x
represents the zero class and the induced map is injective. To show it is surjective, choose
[x]
in
\mathbf{Q}^{-}
-cohomology represented by the
\mathbf{Q}^{-}
-closed element
x
. The
\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}
lemma implies the existence of
\widetilde{x}
with
\mathbf{Q}^{+}x=\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}\widetilde{x}
and hence
x^{\prime}=x+\mathbf{Q}^{-}\widetilde{x}
satisfies
\mathbf{Q}^{\pm}x^{\prime}=0
and
[x^{\prime}]=[x]
, therefore the induced map is surjective.
∎

We prove Theorem
4.1
by equipping this alternative quotient with the structure of a weak graded-unitary vertex algebra. For the sake of brevity we will denote this quotient by
\widehat{{\mathcal{V}}}
and write

[\![x]\!]\coloneqq x+{\rm im}\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}~

for
x\in\ker\mathbf{Q}^{-}\cap\ker\mathbf{Q}^{+}
. There is an obvious half-integral filtration on
\widehat{{\mathcal{V}}}
, which is good if the filtration on
{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter}
is good:

\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}_{R}\widehat{{\mathcal{V}}}=\left\{[\![x]\!]~\big|~x\in\ker\mathbf{Q}^{-}\cap\ker\mathbf{Q}^{+}\cap\mathfrak{R}_{R}C(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}},\mathfrak{g},{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter})\right\}~.

The invariant bilinear form on
C(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}},\mathfrak{g},{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter})
induces an invariant bilinear form on
\widehat{{\mathcal{V}}}
, which we also denote by
(-,-)
, where

([\![x]\!],[\![y]\!])=(x,y)~.

We then have the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}_{\bullet}
is a nondegenerate half-integral filtration on
\widehat{{\mathcal{V}}}
. Moreover, it is good if the filtration
\mathfrak{R}_{\bullet}
is good.

Proof.

That
\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}_{\bullet}
defines a half-integral filtration on
\widehat{{\mathcal{V}}}
is immediate from the fact that
\mathfrak{R}_{\bullet}
is a filtration, and similarly that it is good if
\mathfrak{R}_{\bullet}
is good. We are thus left to establish nondegeneracy.

Choose
[\![x]\!]\in\widehat{{\mathcal{V}}}
nonzero. We can represent such a coset by its harmonic representative
x^{\prime}
and get

0\neq\langle x^{\prime}|x^{\prime}\rangle=((\sigma\circ\rho)(x^{\prime}),x^{\prime})~.

As
y\coloneqq(\sigma\circ\rho)(x^{\prime})
is also harmonic, and thus belongs to
\ker\mathbf{Q}^{+}\cap\ker\mathbf{Q}^{-}
, we conclude that the pairing
([\![y]\!],[\![x^{\prime}]\!])=([\![y]\!],[\![x]\!])
is nonzero so that the pairing is nondegenerate.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

The nondegeneracy of
\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}_{\bullet}
ensures that it can be refined to a grading on the underlying vector space. The graded subspaces are given by

\widehat{{\mathcal{V}}}_{h,R}=\left\{[\![x]\!]~\big|~x=x^{\prime}+\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}\widetilde{x}~,\quad x^{\prime}\in C(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}},\mathfrak{g},{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter})_{h,R}\right\}~.

Of course, if
x^{\prime}
is annihilated by
\mathbf{Q}^{+}
and
\mathbf{Q}^{-}
and has definite
\mathfrak{R}
-degree then it is annihilated by all four of
Q^{\pm}
,
S^{\pm}
,
i.e.
, it is harmonic. In other words,
[\![x]\!]
belongs to the
R^{\rm th}
graded component of
\widehat{{\mathcal{V}}}
if and only if it can be represented by a harmonic element in the
R^{\rm th}
graded component of
C(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}},\mathfrak{g},{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter})
.

As
\rho
preserves both
\ker\mathbf{Q}^{-}\cap\ker\mathbf{Q}^{+}
and
{\rm im}\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}
, we also have a natural quaternionic structure,

\widehat{\rho}:[\![x]\!]\mapsto[\![\rho(x)]\!]~.

That this defines a quaternionic structure on
(\widehat{{\mathcal{V}}},(-,-),\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}_{\bullet})
follows immediately from the fact that
\rho
defines a quaternionic structure on
(C(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}},\mathfrak{g},{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter}),\mathfrak{R}_{\bullet})
. Theorem
4.1
then follows from the following lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

Taking
(\widehat{{\mathcal{V}}},(-,-),\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}_{\bullet},\widehat{\rho})
as above, the corresponding sesquilinear form defined in Corollary
2.24
is positive-definite.

Proof.

Choose
[\![x]\!]\in\widehat{{\mathcal{V}}}_{h}
and let

[\![x]\!]=\sum_{R}[\![x_{R}]\!]~,\qquad x_{R}=x_{R}^{\prime}+\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}\widetilde{x}_{R}~,\quad x^{\prime}_{R}\in C(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}},\mathfrak{g},{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter})_{h,R}

be its decomposition into graded components. Then we have

\displaystyle\langle[\![x]\!]|[\![x]\!]\rangle

\displaystyle=((\widehat{\sigma}\circ\widehat{\rho})([\![x]\!]),[\![x]\!])=\sum_{R}((\widehat{\sigma}\circ\widehat{\rho})([\![x_{R}^{\prime}]\!]),[\![x_{R}^{\prime}]\!])

\displaystyle=\sum_{R}((\sigma\circ\rho)(x^{\prime}_{R}),x^{\prime}_{R})=\sum_{R}\langle x^{\prime}_{R}|x^{\prime}_{R}\rangle\geqslant 0~,

as desired.
∎

4.2.
Outer automorphisms from the
\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}
lemma

We saw in Section
3.1
that the vertex algebra underlying the relative semi-infinite cohomology admits has an interesting outer automorphism group: there is an
{\rm USp}(2)
automorphism that rotates the symplectic fermions
\eta^{+,A}
,
\eta^{-,A}
into one another. As this action rotates the differential
\mathbf{Q}^{-}
as a doublet with
\mathbf{Q}^{+}
, the cohomology does not necessarily inherit this automorphism: only the torus acting on
\eta^{\pm,A}
with weight
\mp 1
preserves the kernel and image of
\mathbf{Q}^{-}
. Indeed, assuming
{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter}
is concentrated in degree zero, the weights of this
{\rm U}(1)
action are precisely the cohomological grading on
\mathbf{Q}^{-}
cohomology.

Nonetheless, the cohomologies of
\mathbf{Q}^{-}
and
\mathbf{Q}^{+}
are isomorphic to one another, so there is a chance that some vestige of the automorphism group will give rise to an action on
\mathbf{Q}^{-}
cohomology. We now show that this is indeed the case whenever the
\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}
lemma holds.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter}
be a vertex algebra with a Hamiltonian
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}}
action such that
C(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}},\mathfrak{g},{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter})
satisfies the
\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}
lemma (
e.g.
, if
{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter}
is a weak graded-unitary vertex algebra and the action is good). Then the
{\rm U}(1)
action on relative semi-infinite cohomology with weight spaces
H^{\scriptstyle{\frac{\infty}{2}}+d}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}},\mathfrak{g},{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter})
extends to an action of
{\rm USp}(2)
.

Remark 4.7
.

In light of the formal analogy with Kähler geometry described in Section
3.2
, the fact that
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
acts on the cohomology of, say,
\mathbf{Q}^{-}
follows from the Kähler-like identities in Eqs. (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) together with the Hodge decomposition. That said, this does not guarantee that it is acts in a way compatible with the vertex algebra structure,
i.e.
, by vertex algebra automorphisms.

Proof.

The assertion follows from Proposition
4.2
, which provides a manifestly
{\rm USp}(2)
-invariant description of
H^{\scriptstyle{\frac{\infty}{2}}+\bullet}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}},\mathfrak{g},{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter})\cong\widehat{{\mathcal{V}}}
.
∎

Physical Remark 4.8
.

The appearance of an
{\rm USp}(2)
outer automorphism on relative semi-infinite cohomology is quite surprising from the perspective of four-dimensional physics. The cohomological grading is interpreted physically as weights of the
{\rm U}(1)_{r}

R
-symmetry of the physical theory and we do not know of a physical reason why this should have a non-abelian enhancement, though upon reduction to three dimensions it seems plausible that the enhancement could be related to the
{\rm SU}(2)_{C}
enhancement of
{\rm U}(1)_{r}
(
cf.
footnote
7
).

4.3.
Formality from the
\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}
lemma

As in the analysis of the de Rham cohomology of compact Kähler manifolds in
[
DGMS75
]
, formality of the relative semi-infinite cohomology complex follows as a consequence of the
\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}
lemma.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter}
be a vertex algebra equipped with a Hamiltonian
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}}
action such that
{\mathcal{V}}\coloneqq C(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}},\mathfrak{g},{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter})
satisfies the
\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}
-lemma (
e.g.
, if
{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter}
is a weak graded-unitary vertex algebra and the action is good). The maps

({\mathcal{V}},\mathbf{Q}^{-})\overset{i}{\longleftarrow}(\ker\mathbf{Q}^{+},\mathbf{Q}^{-})\overset{\pi}{\longrightarrow}(H({\mathcal{V}},\mathbf{Q}^{+}),\mathbf{Q}^{-}{}^{*})~,

where
i
is the inclusion of the subcomplex
(\ker\mathbf{Q}^{+},\mathbf{Q}^{-})
and
\pi
is the projection onto
\mathbf{Q}^{+}
-cohomology, are quasi-isomorphisms.

Remark 4.10
.

In the statement of this proof and below, we use
m^{*}
to denote the map on cohomology induced by a linear map
m
. This should not be confused with the adjoint with respect to the nondegenerate pairing
(-,-)
as used in,
e.g.
, Proposition
2.15
.

Remark 4.11
.

The maps appearing in Theorem
4.9
are morphisms of DG vertex algebras. This follows from the fact that
\mathbf{Q}^{-}
and
\mathbf{Q}^{+}
(anti-)commute, that
\ker\mathbf{Q}^{+}
is a vertex subalgebra of
{\mathcal{V}}
, and that
H({\mathcal{V}},\mathbf{Q}^{+})
is a quotient of
\ker\mathbf{Q}^{+}
by a vertex algebra ideal.

Proof.

The proof closely follows the “
{\rm d}^{c}
-Diagram Method” of
[
DGMS75
]
, but we present the details here for completeness.

The induced differential
\mathbf{Q}^{-}{}^{*}
acts trivially on
H({\mathcal{V}},\mathbf{Q}^{+})
, because if a
\mathbf{Q}^{+}
-closed
x
represents such a cohomology class, we can apply the
\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}
lemma to
\mathbf{Q}^{-}x
to find an operator
\widetilde{x}
with
\mathbf{Q}^{-}x=\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}\widetilde{x}=\mathbf{Q}^{+}(-\mathbf{Q}^{-}\widetilde{x})
, and hence
\mathbf{Q}^{-}x
vanishes in
H({\mathcal{V}},\mathbf{Q}^{+})
. In particular, one may replace the right-most vertex algebra with
(H({\mathcal{V}},\mathbf{Q}^{+}),0)
without any loss.

We now show that
i
is a quasi-isomorphism. Choose
[x]\in H({\mathcal{V}},\mathbf{Q}^{-})
and consider
\mathbf{Q}^{+}x
. The operator
\mathbf{Q}^{+}x
satisfies the hypothesis of the
\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}
lemma, so there exists
\widetilde{x}
with
\mathbf{Q}^{+}{\mathcal{O}}=\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}\widetilde{x}
. We find that
x^{\prime}=x+\mathbf{Q}^{-}\widetilde{x}
is
\mathbf{Q}^{+}
-closed and
\mathbf{Q}^{-}
-cohomologous to
{\mathcal{O}}
, thus
i^{*}
is surjective. To show that
i^{*}
is an injection, we consider a vector
x
satisfying
\mathbf{Q}^{+}x=0
and
x=\mathbf{Q}^{-}x^{\prime}
for some
x^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{V}}
; the key distinction being that
x^{\prime}
need not belong to
\ker\mathbf{Q}^{+}
. However, since
x
satisfies the hypotheses of the
\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}
lemma, we can write
x=\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}\widetilde{x}
. As
\mathbf{Q}^{+}(\mathbf{Q}^{+}\widetilde{x})=0
we conclude that
x
is necessarily
\mathbf{Q}^{-}
-exact in
\ker\mathbf{Q}^{+}
.

Finally, we prove that
\pi
is a quasi-isomorphism. To check surjectivity, we suppose
x
represents an element of
H({\mathcal{V}},\mathbf{Q}^{+})
; we must find a
\mathbf{Q}^{-}
-closed operator that is
\mathbf{Q}^{+}
-cohomologous to
x
. It follows that
\mathbf{Q}^{-}x
satisfies the hypotheses of the
\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}
lemma, hence there is a vector
\widetilde{x}
such that
\mathbf{Q}^{-}x=\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}\widetilde{x}
. In particular,
x^{\prime}=x+\mathbf{Q}^{+}\widetilde{x}
is
\mathbf{Q}^{-}
-closed and
\mathbf{Q}^{+}
-cohomologous to
x
. To see that
\pi^{*}
is injective, we consider a
\mathbf{Q}^{-}
-closed element
x
of
\ker\mathbf{Q}^{+}
and suppose
x=\mathbf{Q}^{+}\widehat{x}
for some
\widehat{x}\in{\mathcal{V}}
,
i.e.
, it maps to zero in
H({\mathcal{V}},\mathbf{Q}^{+})
. We see that
x
satisfies the
\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}
lemma, and hence there exists
\widetilde{x}
with
x=\mathbf{Q}^{-}\mathbf{Q}^{+}\widetilde{x}
, therefore
x
is
\mathbf{Q}^{-}
-exact in
\ker\mathbf{Q}^{+}
.
∎

Remark 4.12
.

There is a second proof of formality given in
[
DGMS75
]
by way of the “Principle of Two Types”, which establishes the uniform vanishing of higher Massey products for compact Kähler manifolds. This method is not available to us as there is at present no understanding of higher Massey-like products for vertex algebras.

4.4.

\Pi=\Pi_{1}+\Pi_{2}~,\qquad L=L_{1}+L_{2}~,\qquad\Lambda=\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}~,

as well as the differentials

The operators

Proposition 4.13
.

The operators
\Pi_{a}
,
L_{a}
,
\Lambda_{a}
,
\mathbf{Q}^{\alpha}_{a}
,
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{a,\alpha}=(\mathbf{Q}^{\alpha}_{a})^{\dagger}

As an immediate consequence,

Corollary 4.14
.

Let

be such that

1)~\mathbf{Q}^{-}_{1}x=0=\mathbf{Q}^{+}_{1}x\qquad\qquad 2)~x=\mathbf{Q}^{-}_{1}x^{\prime}~\text{ or }~x=\mathbf{Q}^{+}_{1}x^{\prime}

for some

\widetilde{x}\in{\mathcal{V}}
with

1)~\mathbf{Q}^{-}_{2}y=0=\mathbf{Q}^{+}_{2}y\qquad\qquad 2)~y=\mathbf{Q}^{-}_{2}y^{\prime}~\text{ or }~y=\mathbf{Q}^{+}_{2}y^{\prime}

for some
y^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{V}}
, then there exists
\widetilde{y}\in{\mathcal{V}}
with
y=\mathbf{Q}^{-}_{2}\mathbf{Q}^{+}_{2}\widetilde{y}
.

The following is an immediate corollary of Theorem
4.1
together with Corollary
3.26
.

Corollary 4.15
.

Let
[x]\in H({\mathcal{V}},\mathbf{Q}^{+}_{1})

The following is a diagram of morphisms of DG vertex algebras all of which are quasi-isomorphisms:

({\mathcal{V}},\mathbf{Q}^{-})
(H({\mathcal{V}},\mathbf{Q}_{1}^{+}),\mathbf{Q}^{-}_{2}{}^{*})
(H(H({\mathcal{V}},\mathbf{Q}^{+}_{1}),\mathbf{Q}^{+}_{2}{}^{*}),0)
(\ker\mathbf{Q}^{+}_{1},\mathbf{Q}^{-})
(\ker\mathbf{Q}^{+}_{2}{}^{*},\mathbf{Q}^{-}_{2}{}^{*})

Proof.

We first comment on the form of the diagram. In the proof of Theorem

An immediate corollary of this result is that the relative semi-infinite

Corollary 4.17
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm matter}
be a weak graded-unitary vertex algebra with a good Hamiltonian
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-2h^{\vee}}

Remark 4.18
.

Combined with Proposition
4.6
, we see that there are as many
{\rm USp}(2)
automorphisms as the number of simple factors plus the rank of the radical of
\mathfrak{g}
, although some may act trivially.

Physical Remark 4.19
.

From the perspective of four-dimensional physics, this result reflects the fact that the gauge coupling is exactly marginal and hence it does not whether one arrives at a point with nonzero gauge couplings all at once or in stages. For three-dimensional boundary VOAs, this result is more physically nontrivial, as gauging triggers a renormalization group (RG) flow. This result implies that at the level of the boundary VOA, the RG flows associated to gauging different direct summands in the gauge algebra commute.

5.
Poisson vertex algebras and Hall–Littlewood chiral rings

We now turn to the structural properties of certain algebraic objects that can be extracted from the graded-unitary vertex algebras that arise from four-dimensional
{\mathcal{N}}=2
SCFTs. The first object we study is the Poisson vertex algebra (PVA) obtained by taking the associated graded with respect to the (good)
\mathfrak{R}
-filtration underlying a graded-unitary vertex algebra. From the perspective of four-dimensional physics, this PVA is identified with the algebra of local operators in the holomorphic-topological twist (also called the Kapustin twist) of the SCFT
[
Kap06
,
OY19
,
Jeo19
]
. As we will see, this PVA inherits a Hermitian inner product from its parent graded-unitary vertex algebra. Moreover, we find that the PVAs coming from the vertex algebras underlying relative semi-infinite vertex algebras are equipped with a Kähler package and hence share many of the properties of their parent graded-unitary vertex algebras, such as formality.

We conclude with an application to finite-type Poisson algebraic geometry. Namely, we instantiate the additional
{\mathbb{Z}}
grading mentioned in Remark
2.31
and Physical Remark
2.32
to extract from the associated graded PVA a Poisson algebra called the Hall–Littlewood chiral ring. For the DG PVAs coming from the DG vertex algebras underlying relative semi-infinite cohomology, this Poisson algebra can be identified with a derived Poisson reduction; we show that formality of the PVA implies formality of this derived symplectic quotient. All Möbius vertex algebras appearing in this section are assumed to be of CFT type.

5.2.
Formality for Hall–Littlewood chiral rings

We now describe a special type of graded-unitary vertex algebra that more closely models those coming from the construction
[
BLL+15
]
. Recall that when the Grassmann parity the underlying graded-unitary vertex algebra is induced from a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading as in Remark
2.31
and Physical Remark
2.32
, the vector space underlying the vertex algebra is triply graded,

{\mathcal{V}}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\in\scriptstyle{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbb{N}}\\
R\in\scriptstyle{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbb{N}}\\
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}\\
\end{subarray}}{\mathcal{V}}_{h,R,d}~,

where
h
is the conformal weight grading,
R
is the grading that induces the
\mathfrak{R}
-filtration, and
d
is the weights of the internal
{\mathbb{Z}}
grading that induces the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
action of the graded-unitary structure. Importantly, if this vertex algebra comes from a unitary four-dimensional
{\mathcal{N}}=2
SCFT then these gradings satisfy a
BPS bound
:

h-R-|r|\geqslant 0\quad\rightsquigarrow\quad h\geqslant R+\tfrac{1}{2}|d|~.

This requirement is part of the notion of a
filtration of four-dimensional type
given in Def. 2.3 of
[
ABLR25
]
; we will not need this full structure in the following. The operators that saturate the BPS bound are preserved by yet more superconformal symmetry than Schur operators and are called Hall–Littlewood operators if
r\geqslant 0
, anti-Hall–Littlewood operators if
r\leqslant 0
, and Higgs branch chiral operators if
r=0
.

Definition 5.5
.

A graded-unitary vertex algebra
({\mathcal{V}},(-,-),\mathfrak{R}_{\bullet},\rho)
is said to
satisfy the BPS bound
if

{\mathcal{V}}_{h,R,d}=0\quad{\rm for}\quad h<R+\tfrac{1}{2}|d|~.

The vectors in
{\mathcal{V}}_{R-\scriptstyle{\frac{1}{2}}d,R,d}
(
d\leqslant 0
) are referred to as
Hall–Littlewood operators
, whereas the vectors in
{\mathcal{V}}_{R+\scriptstyle{\frac{1}{2}}d,R,d}
(
d\geqslant 0
) are referred to
anti-Hall–Littlewood operators
. The vectors in
{\mathcal{V}}_{R,R,0}
are referred to as
Higgs branch operators
.

Remark 5.6
.

The examples of graded-unitary vertex algebras given in Section
2.4
can be given internal
{\mathbb{Z}}
gradings that satisfy the BPS bound. For the symplectic boson VOA
{\rm Sb}[V]
, the internal grading is trivial: the generators have
h=\frac{1}{2}
and
R=\frac{1}{2}
and hence must be given
d=0
. The Hall–Littlewood, anti-Hall–Littlewood, and Higgs branch operators all coincide and are given by the
q^{a}(z)
and normally-ordered products thereof. For the symplectic fermion VOA
{\rm Sf}[{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\otimes U]
, the internal grading satisfies the BPS bound if we give the generator
\eta^{\pm,A}
from
U_{\pm}
degree
d=\pm 1
, matching the physically-relevant grading. The Hall–Littlewood (resp. anti-Hall–Littlewood) operators are given by normally-ordered products of
\eta^{-,A}(z)
(resp.
\eta^{+,A}(z)
); there are no Higgs branch operators. Note that if
{\mathcal{V}}_{1}
,
{\mathcal{V}}_{2}
are graded-unitary vertex algebras whose internal
{\mathbb{Z}}
gradings satisfy the BPS bound, then so does the diagonal grading on
{\mathcal{V}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{V}}_{2}
and any of its subalgebras.

Remark 5.7
.

The normally-ordered product of two Hall–Littlewood operators need not be Hall–Littlewood, and similarly for the normally-ordered product of two anti-Hall–Littlewood operators, due to the fact the
\mathfrak{R}
-grading on the vector space
{\mathcal{V}}
is only a vertex-algebraic filtration.

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}
be a graded-unitary vertex algebra whose internal
{\mathbb{Z}}
grading satisfies the BPS bound. The normally-ordered product of Hall–Littlewood operators in the associated graded PVA
{\rm gr}_{\mathfrak{R}}{\mathcal{V}}
is also a Hall–Littlewood operator. Moreover, the vertex Lie algebra action of a Hall–Littlewood operator on another is again a Hall–Littlewood operator.

Proof.

The first assertion follows from the fact that the normally-ordered product inside
{\rm gr}_{\mathfrak{R}}{\mathcal{V}}
of a vector in tri-degree
(h,R,d)
and a vector in tri-degree
(h^{\prime},R^{\prime},d^{\prime})
is a vector tri-degree
(h+h^{\prime},R+R^{\prime},d+d^{\prime})
. The second assertion follows from the fact that the vertex Lie algebra action of a vector in tri-degree
(h,R,d)
on a vector in tri-degree
(h^{\prime},R^{\prime},d^{\prime})
is a vector in tri-degree
(h+h^{\prime}-1,R+R^{\prime}-1,d+d^{\prime})
; the BPS bound ensures that only the leading term in the vertex Lie algebra action is nonzero as all other terms have the same
\mathfrak{R}
-degree but lower conformal weight.
∎

This proposition implies that the subspace of Hall–Littlewood operators naturally form a Poisson algebra, leading to the following definition.

Definition 5.9

Let
{\mathcal{V}}
be a graded-unitary vertex algebra
{\mathcal{V}}

{\mathbb{Z}}

Remark 5.10
.

The Hall–Littlewood chiral ring

Physical Remark 5.11

In the context of the four-dimensional SCFT/VOA correspondence, the Higgs branch chiral ring is the algebra of functions on the Higgs branch of the parent

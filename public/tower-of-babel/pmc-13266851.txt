Bibliometric and visualization analysis of non-intubated thoracic anaesthesia [2014–2025]
Authors: Yu Che, Lei Chen, Shenghua Yi, Mochi Guo, Jiaqi Liu, Yulong Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNon-intubated thoracic anaesthesia (NITA) is an emerging anaesthetic approach that integrates spontaneous breathing general anaesthesia with regional blockade. By avoiding tracheal intubation, NITA effectively reduces anaesthesia-related complications and significantly curtails anaesthetic consumption. Evidence indicates that this technique lowers the incidence of postoperative pulmonary complications, facilitates immediate airway clearance, permits early ambulation, improves early postoperative comfort, and shortens hospital stay, thereby accelerating patient recovery. The growing adoption of video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) has heightened interest in NITA across various thoracic procedures. This bibliometric study aims to systematically map the global research landscape, identify hotspots, and delineate evolutionary trends of NITA literature from 2014 to 2025.MethodsThis study applies bibliometric and visualisation methods to systematically map the NITA literature indexed in the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) from January 2014 to June 2025. Relevant articles were retrieved and screened to identify research hotspots, trends, and principal findings. Data processing, network mapping, and graphical presentation were carried out with VOSviewer, CiteSpace, Scimago Graphica, and Excel 365.ResultsThe bibliometric dataset comprised 141 articles published between January 2014 and June 2025. Annual output rose steadily from 2014, peaked in 2022, and then declined in 2023–2024. Collaborative-network indicators placed China first in both publication count and total citations. The Journal of Thoracic Disease contributed the largest number of papers. Jianxing He emerged as the most prolific and highly cited author; the single most referenced article is “Nonintubated video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery under epidural anaesthesia compared with conventional anesthetic option: a randomized control study” by Liu et al. [2015]. Keyword co-occurrence analysis shows that the intersection of NITA with thoracic surgical procedures (VATS, lobectomy, anatomical resection) and anaesthetic techniques (regional blockade, preservation of spontaneous ventilation) constitutes the dominant research theme; studies integrating NITA with VATS currently represent the most active subfield.ConclusionsThis study presents the first comprehensive bibliometric visualisation of NITA research, providing an intuitive overview of its hotspots, trends, and prospects. The findings will offer scholars a valuable reference for identifying priority topics and potential collaborators, and will clearly point the way for future NITA research.Keywords: Non-intubated thoracic anaesthesia (NITA), video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS), VOSviewer, CiteSpace, visual analysis

Highlight box

Key findings

• Bibliometric analysis identified 141 non-intubated thoracic anaesthesia (NITA) publications [2014–2025], with China dominating (59.6% of output) and Guangzhou Medical University leading institutional contributions.

• Research hotspots evolved from early feasibility/safety validation to enhanced recovery after surgery integration, outcome assessment, and meta-analyses, with video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) lobectomy remaining the primary surgical context.

• Author collaboration networks reveal concentrated productivity within Chinese institutions, while Italy functions as the principal international bridge linking European, North American, and Asian research.

What is known and what is new?

• NITA reduces intubation-related complications and facilitates early recovery when integrated with VATS; however, standardized protocols and high-risk population data remain limited.

• This study presents the first comprehensive bibliometric visualization of NITA research, objectively mapping the field’s transition from technical feasibility studies to evidence-based outcome optimization.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• International multicentre collaborations (particularly with European and North American centres) must be prioritized to enhance generalizability beyond current single-centre, Chinese-dominated evidence.

• Future research should focus on rigorous multicentre randomized controlled trials in high-risk populations (elderly >75 years, American Society of Anaesthesiologists III–IV), standardized conversion criteria with validated physiological thresholds, and accredited training curricula to ensure safe dissemination.

Introduction

Non-intubated thoracic anaesthesia (NITA) avoids tracheal intubation while preserving spontaneous ventilation. It combines regional anaesthesia (epidural, paravertebral or intercostal block) with sedation, maintaining oxygenation via supraglottic airway or high-flow nasal oxygen (1). With the evolution of minimally invasive thoracic surgical techniques, NITA has evolved into an established and integral component of modern thoracic anaesthetic practice.

Although conventional endobronchial general anaesthesia with one-lung ventilation provides reliable lung isolation and controlled mechanical ventilation, it is associated with airway trauma, ventilator-induced lung injury, and other anaesthesia-related morbidities (2). NITA can mitigate the aforementioned risks to some extent (3). In a comparative cohort study, Wu et al. (4) reported a lower incidence of pulmonary complications (5.6% vs. 12.5%) and post-operative nausea and vomiting (2.8% vs. 4.2%) in patients undergoing NITA compared to those receiving tracheal intubation. Enhanced recovery metrics are also superior: mean length of stay was 3.66±1.56 days after NITA compared with 5.52±2.12 days following intubated general anaesthesia (P<0.001), and overall morbidity was likewise reduced (5). These advantages extend to high-risk populations: a recent non-inferiority study demonstrated that spontaneous ventilation video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) in octogenarian non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients (>80 years) provides comparable long-term oncological outcomes (overall and disease-free survival) to mechanical ventilation, with significantly shorter post-anaesthesia care unit (PACU) stays and resuscitation times (6,7). Recently, NITA has been integrated into VATS (8). Several series have demonstrated significantly lower complication rates and faster recovery in patients undergoing VATS lobectomy or segmentectomy for lung cancer under NITA compared to standard general anaesthesia (9-11).

Despite its proven efficacy, NITA still presents significant challenges. Preserving spontaneous ventilation increases susceptibility to surgically induced coughing, mediastinal shift, and vagal reflexes (12). Intraoperative hypercapnia and hypoxaemia remain critical concerns: Chiang and Lin reported a 2–11% conversion rate to tracheal intubation, with 14–33% of conversions attributed to hypercapnia or hypoxaemia (13). Consequently, high-flow nasal oxygen and supraglottic airway devices are routinely utilized to enhance patient safety (14,15). Furthermore, precise indications and contraindications for NITA require further definition, particularly for complex procedures or patients with severe comorbidities. Achieving an optimal balance between adequate anaesthetic depth and sustained spontaneous ventilation remains a key area of ongoing research (13).

By integrating mathematical and statistical models, bibliometrics quantitatively and qualitatively dissects the spatiotemporal distribution of publications, author-institution collaboration networks, and co-words or co-citation relationships, thereby providing an objective lens for delineating disciplinary evolution and forecasting emerging trends (16). The recent, successful deployment of tools such as CiteSpace and VOSviewer in thoracic surgery (17), oncology (18), and rheumatology (19) has further corroborated the robustness and visual clarity of this approach when handling large-scale, heterogeneous bibliographic data. Despite the rapid expansion of NITA-related research over the past decade, a dedicated bibliometric analysis of this field remains absent. To address this gap, the present study will apply a bibliometric framework to the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) [2014–2025] to map the developmental trajectory, research hotspots, and evolutionary trends of NITA literature, thereby offering a structured reference for future research and multidisciplinary collaborations. We present this article in accordance with the BIBLIO reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-1-0353/rc).

Methods

Data collection

The analysis used Clarivate Analytics’ WOSCC as the sole data source, and its rigorous journal-selection criteria and comprehensive citation index are widely recognised as the bibliometric gold standard (20). The search strategy employed the following query: TS = ((“tubeless” OR “non-intubat*” OR “spontaneous ventilation”) AND (“thorac*” OR “lung*”) AND (“anesthes*”)). Notably, while surgical procedures (e.g., VATS, lobectomy) appear in the retrieved literature as the clinical context for NITA, the mandatory inclusion of anaesthesia-specific search terms ensures that this analysis specifically targets perioperative anaesthetic management rather than surgical techniques. Records were limited to original Articles and Reviews written in English and published between 1 January 2014 and 30 June 2025. Review articles were deliberately retained as primary objects of bibliometric analysis, given their established role as knowledge-synthesis nodes and citation-network hubs within emerging interdisciplinary fields such as NITA. This timeframe reflects methodological necessity rather than arbitrary exclusion; pre-2014 foundational reports (e.g., Chen et al., 2011; Hung et al., 2013) (2,21) lacked standardized anaesthesia-specific terminology in titles, abstracts, or keywords, rendering them inaccessible to reproducible bibliometric retrieval algorithms despite their historical significance. After duplicate removal and data cleaning, 141 eligible publications with their complete bibliographic details and cited references were exported in both TXT and CSV formats, ensuring exhaustive and unbiased downstream analyses (Figure 1).

Data analysis and visualisation

Microsoft Excel 2019 was used for data collation and summary of bibliographic information. VOSviewer (version 1.6.20) was used to construct and visualise author collaboration networks, country-level collaboration networks, and keyword co-occurrence networks. The software represents research entities as nodes and their relationships as edges; node size reflects the number of items; colours denote distinct clusters; and edge thickness indicates the strength of similarity or association between items (22).

CiteSpace (version 6.4.R1 advanced), developed by Prof. Chaomei Chen of Drexel University, is a visualisation tool dedicated to co-citation analysis and research-front detection. In this study, CiteSpace was employed to identify research hotspots, keyword co-occurrence networks, and document co-citation patterns within NITA. Timeline and dual-map overlay visualisations were used to delineate the intellectual development and pivotal turning points of the domain. Cluster validity was assessed by means of the silhouette metric (S>0.5 indicates well-separated clusters) and modularity (Q>0.3 denotes a significant clustering structure) (23). Distribution of authors and research institutions

Price’s law, derived by Price in 1963 from Lotka’s distribution, states that within a given research domain, half of all papers are produced by a subset of highly productive authors whose size approximates the square root of the total author population m=0.749×nmax), where nmax is the output of the most prolific author and m is the minimum count defining the “elite” group (24). Applying this formula to the present dataset, we set the core-author threshold at ≥4 publications, identifying 41 investigators who together contributed 72 papers (51.06% of the total), thereby satisfying Price’s 50% criterion and indicating the emergence of a stable, high-productivity collaborative network within the NITA field.

Table 3 lists the 10 most productive authors in the NITA field. Between 1 January 2014 and 30 June 2025, Jianxing He led with 28 publications accruing 702 citations (mean 25.07 citations per paper), followed by Jun Liu with 20 papers and 505 citations (mean 25.25 citations per paper). Both authors are affiliated with The First Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou Medical University and have collaborated extensively; their joint output focuses on the clinical translation and precision management of respiratory diseases, particularly lung cancer, constituting a systematic research programme.

Discussion

CiteSpace 6.4.R1 Advanced, VOSviewer version 1.6.20, Scimago Graphica 1.0.43, and Python 3.13.2 were used to analyse the 141 NITA-related articles retrieved from WOSCC [2014–2025], enabling assessment of the field’s temporal and spatial distribution, author contributions, core papers, research hotspots, and emerging frontiers.

General distribution

Citation data for NITA-related publications from 2014 to 2025 (Figure 2A) reveal distinct chronological phases. In 2014, four papers received 166 citations, indicating an incipient stage. The citation count dropped sharply to 15 in 2015, reflecting the publication of only one paper that had not yet accumulated references. Both output and citations peaked in 2016: 13 articles attracted 681 citations, accounting for 22% of the decade’s total. This surge is attributable to highly cited papers published that year by Gonzalez-Rivas et al. (27) (160 citations) and Liu et al. (29) (107 citations). The former offered the first systematic appraisal of non-intubated anaesthesia during VATS and proposed that the technique could define the future of thoracic surgery, whereas the latter compared early outcomes of NITA vs. conventional intubated anaesthesia for non-small-cell lung cancer resections performed via VATS, further delineating the advantages of the approach. These publications mark the field’s transition from purely theoretical groundwork to clinical application. From 2017 to 2023, annual citations remained high [159–352], indicating sustained interest in the field. The marked decline in citations in 2024–2025 (16 and 1, respectively) is attributable to the recent publication date of articles in this interval; many papers have yet to be indexed. Citation counts are expected to increase over the next three to five years. While the sharp citation decline in 2024–2025 reflects recency, the publication decline from 2023 onwards may alternatively indicate field maturation, integration into standardized perioperative protocols, or terminological stabilization.

The temporal shift in study designs (Table 1, Figure 2B) provides context for these publication trends. The field matured from feasibility demonstrations (case series) to comparative effectiveness research (cohort studies), yet the disappearance of RCTs after 2022 suggests a stabilization phase where large randomized trials are not keeping pace with clinical adoption.

Country-level analysis (Figure 3, Table 2) shows that China leads with 84 publications, representing 59.57% of the total and far exceeding Italy (24 articles, 17.02%) and South Korea (10 articles, 7.09%), confirming China as the dominant contributor. However, Italy’s citations-per-paper (17.67) slightly surpasses that of China (16.15), indicating a higher average impact. Centrality analysis further reveals that Italy’s betweenness centrality (0.42) is markedly higher than China’s (0.29), positioning Italy as the principal “bridge” that links European, North American, and Asian research networks. Although the United States and Germany publish fewer papers, their work targets niche technical aspects: US studies emphasise integration of NITA with enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) protocols, whereas German research focuses on respiratory management in elderly patients. The collaboration map (Figure 3) includes more than 20 countries, yet inter-country ties remain loose; only China-Italy and China-South Korea display pronounced co-authorship, while most nations conduct isolated investigations, underscoring a paucity of genuine multicentre international research within the NITA field. Notably, while surgical multicentre studies of non-intubated techniques have been reported [e.g., Pompeo et al., 2019, ESTS Working Group (30); Elkhouly et al., 2022 (31)], these were not captured by our anaesthesia-focused search strategy due to disciplinary indexing differences.

Institutional collaboration analysis (Figure 4, Table 4) shows that Guangzhou Medical University and its affiliated units (Guangzhou Institute of Respiratory Disease, State Key Laboratory of Respiratory Disease, etc.) dominate the field, having published 64 papers (45.4% of the total) and garnered 1,504 citations. Their output focuses on comparative assessments of respiratory function in NITA patients, optimisation of anaesthetic protocols, and technical innovations, thereby providing a robust theoretical and clinical foundation for the specialty. Conversely, Hospital Universitario A Coruña (Spain) contributes only five papers but achieves the highest citations-per-paper ratio (66.80), indicating exceptional research quality. Content analysis reveals that this centre concentrates on the application of NITA in complex thoracic procedures such as pleural empyema, pleural effusion, and pulmonary tumours.

The concentration of publications at Guangzhou Medical University (25.5%) and Chinese institutions (59.6%) reflects technical leadership but introduces generalisability concerns. Patient selection criteria and perioperative protocols in these single-centre studies may differ from Western practice patterns, and the limited international multicentre collaboration (Figures 3A,4C) concentrates intellectual risk. Validation across diverse healthcare systems remains needed.

The author-collaboration network (Figure 4, Table 3) further corroborates Chinese dominance: eight of the ten most prolific authors are from China and affiliated with Guangzhou Medical University and its subsidiaries. Among them, Jianxing He (H-index =44; 702 citations) is the most influential investigator, his group concentrating on the utility of NITA in lobectomy and lung cancer therapy.

Table 5 shows that core outlets for NITA research are concentrated in thoracic surgery and anaesthesiology literature. Journal of Thoracic Disease leads with 36 publications and, with 524 co-citations, being the most frequently cited source, indicating its role as a key publication venue for NITA research. Thoracic-surgery journals such as Frontiers in Surgery (13 articles) and European Journal of Cardio-Thoracic Surgery (10 articles), together with anaesthesia titles BMC Anesthesiology (9 articles) and Anesthesiology (142 co-citations), form the main publication arena. Impact analysis reveals that 20 % of the top 10 journals occupy Q1 [e.g., Journal of Clinical Medicine impact factor (IF) =2.99; Journal of Thoracic and Cardiovascular Surgery IF =6.0] and 50% Q2, with a mean IF of 2.96, indicating that NITA research is carried primarily in medium to high-impact outlets, ensuring recognised quality. Co-citation counts are highest for leading periodicals Anesthesiology (IF =9.1, Q1) and Chest (IF =9.5, Q1), confirming close integration with frontier theory in anaesthesiology and thoracic medicine. Among the ten most-co-cited journals, seven are surgery-focused (Journal of Thoracic Disease, Annals of Thoracic Surgery, European Journal of Cardio-Thoracic Surgery, etc.) and one is medicine-focused (Annals of Translational Medicine), aligning with the dual-map overlay shown in Figure 5B.

Hotspots and frontiers

Keyword co-occurrence analysis (Figure 6, Table 6) revealed that “VATS” (72 occurrences), “anaesthesia” (56 occurrences), “lobectomy” (52 occurrences), and “resection” (46 occurrences) are the most frequent terms, confirming that NITA is principally employed during thoracoscopic lobectomy and oncological resection. High counts for “feasibility”, “safety”, “regional anaesthesia”, “spontaneous ventilation”, and “awake” further indicate sustained attention to the technical applicability and safety profile of NITA, as well as to its integration with regional block and the preservation of spontaneous ventilation.

Notably, while NITA encompasses heterogeneous technical strategies—from purely awake epidural techniques to sedation with supraglottic airways and high-flow nasal oxygen—these variants naturally segregated within the bibliometric networks (e.g., “laryngeal mask airway” in the green cluster versus “awake” in early temporal clusters), validating our approach of capturing unified thematic evolution rather than imposing prespecified subtyping.

According to established criteria for CiteSpace clustering, the modularity index (Q) and mean silhouette score (S) are used to evaluate the validity of the resulting maps: Q>0.3 indicates a statistically significant clustering structure, while S>0.5 suggests that the clusters are rational, and S>0.7 implies they are both efficient and highly convincing. The keyword-cluster map shown in Figure 6D meets these benchmarks (Q=0.52, S=0.79), demonstrating a robust and meaningful structure. The dominant clusters—#1 awake non-intubated VAT, #3 non-intubated video-assisted thoracoscopic lung resection, #5 tubeless VAT, and #6 invasive technique—confirm that NITA is principally applied within the VATS paradigm and reflect an academic emphasis on reducing surgical and anaesthetic invasiveness. The prominent positioning of “epidural anaesthesia” and “vagal block” underscores the pivotal role of regional anaesthesia in NITA, particularly the use of epidural blockade and vagal nerve block to suppress cough reflexes and maintain intraoperative oxygenation.

Analysis of highly cited documents (Figure 8, Table 7) corroborates the identified research hotspots. The ten most-cited papers were published predominantly between 2015 and 2020, and most are comparative studies of NITA versus conventional intubated anaesthesia during VATS. Liu et al. [2015] (10) first used a randomised controlled design to demonstrate the advantages of NITA over standard anaesthesia in VATS procedures. Gonzalez-Rivas et al. [2016] (27) systematically summarised the technical essentials of NITA and predicted that it would become the future direction of thoracic surgery. Batchelor et al. [2019] (32) included NITA in ERAS guidelines, signifying authoritative consensus endorsement. These high-impact articles constitute the intellectual foundation of the NITA field and continue to guide subsequent research toward deeper dimensions.

The keyword temporal-overlay map (Figure 6B) reveals a recent surge in the frequency of “meta-analysis”, “enhanced recovery after surgery”, and “outcome”, indicating that the field has moved beyond initial technical validation toward evidence-based synthesis and perioperative outcome evaluation. This shift reflects the maturation of NITA research, with the central question now evolving from “is it feasible?” to “how can it be delivered more safely and efficiently?”.

Drivers of NITA success

The rapid expansion of NITA and its rising academic visibility result from mechanisms spanning physiological, pharmacological, and interdisciplinary domains. While the bibliometric patterns identify where and when research concentrated, the following explanations of why these patterns emerged represent clinical expert interpretation intended to contextualize the quantitative findings.

Physiological basis

NITA preserves spontaneous ventilation, unlike conventional intubated anaesthesia. Maintaining negative intrathoracic pressure throughout surgery keeps mechanics closer to the physiological state and avoids barotrauma, volutrauma, and atelectasis associated with positive-pressure ventilation (33). The high co-occurrence of “spontaneous ventilation” and “feasibility” mirrors academic acceptance of this lung-protective effect. Well-cited studies from China and Italy (10,34) confirm more stable intraoperative oxygenation and shorter extubation times in NITA cohorts.

Second, although traditional thoracic anaesthesia employs sedative analgesic agents to attenuate perioperative stress, endotracheal intubation and surgical trauma still trigger pronounced sympathetic activation and marked haemodynamic fluctuations, posing a major threat to patients with concomitant cardiovascular disease (35). By eliminating airway instrumentation, NITA, especially when combined with regional blockade, provides effective analgesia while simultaneously blunting the surgical stress response, thereby maintaining a more stable haemodynamic profile (36).

Third, NITA permits a marked reduction in sedative, analgesic, and muscle-relaxant consumption, thereby lessening hepato-renal burden and minimising central nervous system and neuromuscular depression. This facilitates rapid awakening and early mobilisation, aligning closely with ERAS principles (37). The rising frequency of keywords such as “enhanced recovery after surgery” and “awake” reflects growing recognition of this advantage within the academic community.

Advances in anaesthetic techniques

The feasibility of NITA is contingent upon parallel progress in regional anaesthesia. The persistent high frequency of “epidural anaesthesia”, “regional anaesthesia”, and “vagal block” underscores the pivotal role of loco-regional techniques. The advent of ultrasound-guided visualisation has enabled more accurate delivery of intercostal, paravertebral, and vagal blocks, providing precise intraoperative analgesia and reflex control (e.g., cough suppression) while reducing systemic drug exposure and enhancing safety (38). Table 3 shows that leading authors such as Jianxing He and Jun Liu are affiliated with Guangzhou Medical University, an institution whose preeminence in the field coincides with its systematic application and innovation of ultrasound-guided regional blocks. Consequently, block techniques that minimise patient impact and facilitate surgical conditions are likely to constitute a future direction for NITA.

Additionally, NITA mandates meticulous titration of respiratory rate, tidal volume, end-tidal CO2, and depth of sedation rather than deep general anaesthesia (1). The availability of ultra-short-acting, controllable agents such as propofol and remifentanil has therefore been instrumental in accelerating clinical adoption. Future research is likely to focus on novel hypnotics with minimal respiratory depression and specific reversal agents, exemplified by remimazolam, thereby further enhancing the safety and controllability of NITA.

Interdisciplinary collaboration

The emergence of NITA has depended on concurrent advances by anaesthetists and thoracic surgeons. As shown in Figure 2A, annual output increased sharply after 2015, while keywords such as “VATS”, “lobectomy”, and “resection” displayed strong co-occurrence. During the same period, single-port VATS and sub-lobar resection gained popularity, illustrating parallel technical progress: VATS reduced surgical trauma, whereas NITA lessened the systemic impact of anaesthesia, together driving the operative approach toward greater minimal invasiveness.

By minimising systemic drug exposure, reducing pulmonary complications, and facilitating early recovery, NITA aligns seamlessly with the ERAS philosophy in thoracic surgery. The frequent citation of the ERAS guidelines published by Batchelor et al. (32) (Table 7) signals that NITA has progressed from early technical exploration to guideline-level recommendation, heralding its transition toward a more mature phase of development.

Formation of an academic community

The convergence of a dedicated academic community has accelerated the dissemination of NITA. Tables 2,3 highlight a high-output group of authors affiliated with Guangzhou Medical University and its affiliated hospitals; their extensive collaborative networks (Figure 4) have generated a substantial volume of high-quality publications in recent years. This closely integrated research network not only facilitates rapid knowledge diffusion but also, through international collaborations (Figure 3), enables the transfer of expertise to multiple countries across Asian and European countries, fostering the emergence of additional technical hubs worldwide (e.g., Italy and Spain).

Moreover, keyword clustering and burst-detection analyses (Figure 6) reveal that NITA research has shifted from early exploratory terms such as “feasibility” and “safety” to higher-level evidence concepts, “outcome”, “meta-analysis”, and “enhanced recovery after surgery”, indicating increasing field maturity. The majority of highly cited articles (Table 7) appeared between 2015 and 2019, coinciding with the period of technical expansion, and these papers continue to supply the theoretical framework and methodological basis for subsequent investigations.

Limitations and challenges

Despite the advantages outlined above, our data also highlight several limitations and challenges. First, both geographical and institutional distributions are heavily skewed: China, especially Guangzhou Medical University and its affiliated hospitals, dominates the field (Tables 2,4). While such concentration accelerates internal knowledge transfer, it risks generating a narrow research perspective and limiting the generalisability of findings. Conversely, although European and North American outputs are modest, Italian publications achieve a higher average impact, suggesting a more cautious stance toward NITA that may reflect inter-regional differences in anaesthetic practice patterns.

Second, the hierarchy of evidence remains sub-optimal. Although highly cited reports include randomised controlled trials and meta-analyses (10,39), observational and single-centre descriptive studies still predominate. Notably, while surgical multicentre studies exist in the literature [e.g., Pompeo et al., 2019 (30); Elkhouly et al., 2022 (31)], these were not captured by our anaesthesia-focused search strategy due to disciplinary indexing differences, highlighting the fragmented nature of NITA reporting across specialties. Future work should prioritise rigorously designed multicentre RCTs with longer follow-up, focusing on hard long-term end-points such as tumour-specific events, overall survival, and major complications. Additionally, the restriction to WOSCC may have excluded relevant publications indexed in Scopus or PubMed.

Third, technical standardisation and structured training remain inadequate. The frequent appearance of “regional anaesthesia”, “nerve block”, and “epidural anaesthesia” reflects heterogeneous anaesthetic techniques currently employed for non-intubated thoracic procedures, yet no universally accepted procedural protocol or comprehensive training curriculum exists. Moreover, recurring terms such as “management” and “complication” underscore the need for anaesthetists to undertake meticulous preoperative evaluation and intraoperative decision-making, while the potential for emergency conversion to tracheal intubation demands rapid response skills. These factors may limit the widespread dissemination of NITA.

Finally, patient selection criteria require further refinement. Most studies have enrolled adults aged 18–65 years with a body mass index (BMI) ≤30 kg/m2 and American Society of Anaesthesiologists (ASA) physical status classification I–III. Although isolated reports suggest that NITA may offer unique advantages in lung or combined heart–lung transplantation, its safety and efficacy in higher risk cohorts (extreme age, obesity, significant cardiac or pulmonary dysfunction, difficult airway, extensive pleural adhesions) remain insufficiently documented and demand prospective validation.

Conclusions

This study mapped 141 NITA publications from 2014 to 2025 using bibliometric methods. China produced the majority of research, with Guangzhou Medical University and its affiliated institutions as the most productive contributors. The field has evolved from early feasibility and safety reports to comparative cohort studies and meta-analyses, with VATS lobectomy remaining the dominant surgical context. Nevertheless, the current evidence is still limited by single-centre designs and geographical concentration. Future efforts should focus on multicentre randomised controlled trials in high-risk populations, standardised protocols, and broader international collaboration to support the safe dissemination of NITA.

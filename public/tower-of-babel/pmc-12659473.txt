Event-driven dynamics, regional disparities, and netizen engagement: characterizing and exploring malaria discourse on Sina Weibo for enhanced health communication
Authors: Ning Wang, Xu Zhao, Chengyuan Li, Yuanchun Mao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMalaria remains a major global health concern. Although China was certified malaria-free, imported cases have continued to emerge in recent years, and public awareness of the disease remains limited. As a major social media platform in China, Sina Weibo provides a large volume of malaria-related textual data suitable for analysing public discussion patterns and attention focus online. This study analysed discussions on Sina Weibo to help relevant institutions understand the netizens’ key concerns and level of awareness regarding malaria. By characterizing discourse patterns and exploring underlying dynamics, the findings aim to inform the design of more targeted and effective health communication strategies, ultimately enhancing netizen awareness of malaria prevention and promoting a deeper understanding of relevant knowledge.MethodUsing “malaria” as the keyword, relevant posts from Sina Weibo were collected between January 2019 and August 2024. Guided by the social media analysis framework, descriptive analyses and natural language processing techniques—including topic modelling, sentiment analysis, and named entity recognition—were conducted to systematically analyze netizen attention and discussion patterns related to malaria across temporal, spatial, and netizen-type dimensions.ResultsA total of 81,010 malaria-related posts were collected. Netizens’ attention to malaria is event-driven, with peaks around China’s health assistance to Africa, public health incidents, and World Malaria Day, China’s National Malaria Day, with posts related to malaria day accounting for approximately 20% of the annual total. Positive events typically trigger only short-lived peaks in attention, whereas negative events sustain engagement for a considerably longer period—approximately 1.5 times the duration of their positive counterparts. Although discussions concerning Africa have experienced a slight decline, they continue to constitute over 60% of region-specific posts annually; by contrast, discussions related to Asia and the Americas remain comparatively fragmented. Official health-related accounts focus on malaria prevention but post infrequently; personal accounts are more inclined to discuss treatment approaches and first-hand narratives of health experiences; unofficial health-related accounts show a more balanced distribution of topics, with particular emphasis on vaccines and scientific research; while organizational accounts provide institutional information on malaria control.ConclusionMalaria-related discussions on Sina Weibo exhibit a clear event-driven pattern, with regional disparities possibly shaped by both disease prevalence and the visibility of international health collaborations involving China. Official health-related accounts show limited engagement. Tailored communication strategies are required to enhance netizens’ awareness of malaria disease and strengthen their understanding of related knowledge.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12936-025-05664-4.Keywords: Malaria, Social media, Netizens’ attention

Background

Among the world’s most prominent infectious diseases, malaria has long posed significant challenges in many developing countries. According to the latest World Malaria Report [1], there were 263 million malaria cases in 2023, up from 252 million in 2022. China has achieved remarkable progress in the elimination of malaria, earning certification as malaria-free by the WHO in 2021 [2]. Despite the successful domestic control of malaria [3], imported cases remain a significant concern [4, 5], driven by the growing number of Chinese migrant workers abroad and the rise in international business, tourism, and exchanges. Between 2011 and 2020, China’s National Notifiable Diseases Reporting System (NNDRS) recorded 165 malaria deaths, of which 99.4% were imported malaria [6]. Similarly, Shanghai reported 112 imported malaria cases, primarily from Africa, with 2021 marking a decade-high, 13.39% involving severe malaria, 3.57% co-infected with COVID-19, and most patients developing symptoms within 14 days of arrival from January 2020 to December 2021 [7].

However, many studies have revealed a low level of netizen awareness and knowledge about malaria, especially in its prevention, transmission routes, and symptom recognition, leading to delays in seeking medical care, compromised treatment outcomes, and an increased risk of misdiagnosis and delayed diagnosis [8–11].

Social media, as widely accessible platforms for open interaction on a global scale, has become a cornerstone of contemporary communication [12]. Its impact extends to public health, where it plays an important role in public health campaigns, education, and advocacy, facilitating awareness, influencing policies, and advancing health initiatives [13]. In the context of malaria, an increasing number of studies are utilizing data mining technologies to analyse large-scale data from social media platforms to explore public sentiment, trending discussion topics, and updates in malaria-related knowledge [14].

Current approaches not only reveal how malaria is perceived and discussed by diverse audiences but also help identify patterns in misinformation, gaps in public understanding, and emerging areas of concern. More specifically, studies have explored the use of social media data for applications such as disease surveillance, adverse drug event (ADE) monitoring, and informing public policy decisions. A study [15] analysed 157,951 tweets containing the GlobalHealth hashtag from January 1, 2014, to April 30, 2015, in conjunction with five disease-related hashtags (Malaria, HIV, TB, NCDS, NTDS), using unsupervised machine learning methods for topic categorization. The study found that prevention, control, and treatment were the primary themes of discussion, followed by advocacy and epidemiological information. It also reveals the priorities of global health advocates, funders, policymakers, and practitioners, offering valuable insights into social trends and public concerns, and highlighting the potential of social media as a public health communication platform. Another study developed an automated system that integrated Twitter data with natural language processing (NLP) techniques, including sentiment analysis and text classification, to detect ADEs related to malaria drugs, such as doxycycline. The findings suggested that Twitter, as a real-time data source, could effectively complement traditional drug monitoring systems by identifying ADEs associated with both new and existing medications, thus improving the efficiency of drug safety surveillance [16]. Furthermore, using text mining techniques, one study analysed Twitter discussions about malaria, extracting key issues and trends. These findings, in turn, provided important insights for public health decision-making [17]. In addition, a subsequent study employed sentiment analysis and topic modelling techniques to analyse tweets related to various pandemics, including malaria, cholera, Ebola, and HIV/AIDS. It revealed key emotional characteristics in public discourse—such as fear, trust, and disgust—with particularly strong expressions of trust in discussions about HIV/AIDS [18]. A more recent study used text mining and topic modelling techniques to examine Twitter posts from 2011 to 2020 regarding malaria and other parasitic diseases. The study found a consistent annual increase in the volume of tweets, alongside significant regional variations in the distribution of language and topics, offering new insights into global health issues and optimizing public health responses [19].

Despite the progress in leveraging social media data to gain valuable insights into malaria-related public health concerns and discourse, several limitations remain. Previous research has largely focused on analyzing English-language platforms, with insufficient attention paid to users from non-English-speaking regions. In addition, these works typically offer population-level analyses, without examining detailed behaviors, preferences, or external factors that influence malaria-related discussions and information dissemination.

Unlike previous works, malaria-related posts crawled from the Sina Weibo platform [20] were analysed, with the platform being a prominent Chinese-language social media site that, to the best of the knowledge, has not yet been comprehensively analysed for malaria-related content. The temporal dynamics of netizens’ attention and malaria-related discussions on Sina Weibo over five years were examined. Meanwhile, the study also analyses thematic shifts in malaria-related discussions across different regions, revealing variations and evolving trends in netizens’ attention among these areas. Finally, the engaged netizens were categorized into four distinct groups, with each group’s main areas of focus and behavioural patterns analysed across three key aspects: malaria symptoms, medication use, and prevention.

Methods

This section provides a detailed description of the social media data and analysis methods employed in this study, guided by the structured analytical process of the Social Media Analysis (SMA) framework [21–23]. An overview of the analysis process is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Data collection

Sina Weibo was chosen as the data collection platform, given its status as one of the largest and most influential social media platforms in China [24, 25], as well as its extensive user base and accessible public data.

Using "malaria" as a search term, posts were collected from January 2019 to August 2024, capturing both historical and recent data, to ensure a comprehensive reflection of overall trends and emerging developments in malaria-related concerns and discussions.

The data includes user profiles, time data (i.e., posting time), post content, and attention-related data (e.g., number of likes, comments, and retweets). The demographic characteristics of the user base are presented in Table 1.

Furthermore, similar to prior works [26, 27], netizen categorization was performed among authenticated accounts, identifying four distinct groups: Personal Account, referring to accounts lacking institutional attributes and not containing keywords such as “official Weibo”, “official”, “company” in their profile or name. Official Health-related Account, referring to government-affiliated or authoritative health-related institutions or organizations, identified using keywords such as “CDC,” “Center for Disease Control and Prevention,” “Health Commission,” “Institute of Parasitic Disease Control,” “Hospital,” “Health Bureau,” “Health and Wellness,” “National Medical Products Administration,” and other related terms. Unofficial Health-related Account, referring to entities involved in healthcare but not officially affiliated, including medical or biotech companies, pharmacies, platforms, and medical universities. Other Organizational Account, referring to companies, platforms, or institutions unrelated to the healthcare field.

Data preprocessing and cleansing

The original Weibo data contains both structured and unstructured data. The structured data includes metadata such as user information, post timestamps, and engagement metrics, while the unstructured data comprises the text content of the posts. To ensure data quality, several preprocessing steps were performed: removing irrelevant or duplicate entries, and normalizing the text content by eliminating special characters, emojis. Additionally, to reduce noise and improve the efficiency of word segmentation, missing or incomplete data were filtered out. Unlike English, Chinese text does not have spaces between words, as a result, word segmentation processing was applied to the unstructured Chinese text data. The open-source Chinese word segmentation tool Jieba is used to tokenize Weibo posts and comments [28].

Topic modelling

Topic modelling can be achieved through various techniques, among which the Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) model has been widely used for its effectiveness in automatically identifying semantic topics from large-scale text data [29, 30]. Therefore, the LDA model [31] was adopted to reveal the potential semantic structure and topic patterns related to malaria in Weibo posts.

The Gensim package in Python was utilized to apply the LDA model [32]. Given the inherent challenge of LDA [31], particularly its difficulty in precisely determining the optimal number of topics and interpreting their semantic meanings [33], LDAvis [34], an interactive visualization tool, was employed to determine the optimal number of topics and to visually present and explain the identified topics.

Sentiment analysis

Social media sentiment analysis serves as a valuable tool for understanding netizens’ attitudes, thoughts, and behavioural tendencies [35]. To perform the task, the SnowNLP library, a third-party Python tool designed for sentiment analysis on Chinese text, was used. SnowNLP applies natural language processing (NLP) techniques alongside a probabilistic retrieval model based on the BM25 algorithm. It classifies emotions by identifying positive and negative sentiment words, calculates the score for each emotion word within a text, and aggregates these scores to determine overall sentiment. Previous research has demonstrated that SnowNLP excels in sentiment analysis and corpus processing for Chinese text [21], making it an effective tool for analysis.

Named entity recognition

Named Entity Recognition (NER) [36] is a fundamental information extraction task that aims to accurately identify and classify entities such as disease symptoms, medications, and clinical terms from unstructured text. In this study, the MING-MOE model [37], a state-of-the-art (SOTA) deep learning framework that leverages a sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture with low-rank adapter modules to enhance medical multi-task learning, was adopted, demonstrating superior performance across diverse biomedical NER benchmarks.

Descriptive analysis

A descriptive analysis was conducted to summarize the general patterns of malaria-related discussions on Sina Weibo from January 2019 to August 2024. Posting frequencies were first analysed by year and month to identify temporal fluctuations and notable peaks in netizen engagement in malaria discussions. Subsequently, the geographical distribution of posts was examined based on malaria-endemic regions defined by the WHO. Discussion trends were compared across Africa, Asia, and the Americas to reveal cross-regional disparities in Netizens’ attention and their evolution over time. Finally, netizens were categorized into four groups, including Personal Account, Official Health-related Account, Unofficial Health-related Account, and Other Organizational Account, based on netizens’ profile information. Posting behavioural and engagement characteristics were then analysed across the three core dimensions of malaria discourse: symptoms, medications, and prevention. This analysis provides a foundation for subsequent topic modelling and content-level interpretation.

Results

Overview of data collection

In this study, Weibo posts related to malaria were collected from January 2019 to August 2024. Specifically, 14,773 posts were collected in 2019, 21,437 in 2020, 12,744 in 2021, 12,776 in 2022, 11,685 in 2023, and 7595 from January 2024 to August 2024, totaling 81,010 relevant posts. Subsequently, to investigate netizen engagement on Weibo with malaria-endemic regions across continents and countries, the relevant posts were filtered and categorized by continent (Asia, the Americas, Africa, and Oceania), based on the World Malaria Report 2024 published by the WHO [1]. The results reveal that 14,283 posts were related to Africa, 2190 to the Americas, 4721 to Asia, and no relevant discussions were found for Oceania. Furthermore, to analyse the focal points and behavior patterns of different netizen types on Weibo, verified accounts were categorized into four groups: Official Health-related Account (N = 548), Unofficial Health-related Account (N = 624), Other Organizational Account (N = 6476), and Personal Account (N = 4752).

From 2019 to 2024, malaria-related discussions on Weibo showed an initial upward trend, followed by a gradual decline. The number of posts peaked in 2020 at 21,437, rising from 14,773 in 2019. In the following years, the volume remained moderate but steadily decreased, with only 7595 posts recorded between January and August 2024. Public discourse on malaria also shifted thematically over time. In 2020, the COVID-19 pandemic triggered extensive discussions on the intersection of malaria and COVID-19 (8000 posts), followed by topics on mosquito-borne transmission and control (6500 posts). In 2022, the 50th anniversary of artemisinin’s discovery revitalized discussions on malaria and infectious disease control (4500 posts). In 2023, dominant themes included traditional medicine in malaria treatment (3685 posts) and historical contributions to malaria control (3500 posts). By 2024, discussions turned to malaria treatment and scientific research (2800 posts). Compared to earlier event-driven and reactive discussions, recent netizen attention has shifted toward China’s achievements in malaria control, scientific advancements, and global cooperation.

Thematic evolution of malaria discussions in different regions

Discussions in Africa

Between 2019 and 2024, Africa consistently had the highest number of posts discussing malaria-related topics, although the count declined from 2945 to 1105 over the five-year period. Malaria-related discussions concerning Africa primarily focused on disease control, prevention, and regional transmission. The topics evolved over time–from peacekeeping in 2019 to imported cases and the impact of COVID-19 in 2020, followed by discussions on transmission, prevention, and cooperation with Central Africa between 2021 and 2023. By 2024, attention shifted to the distribution of cases across China and Africa. These thematic shifts highlight sustained concern over regional outbreaks and the importance of cross-border collaboration.

Discussions in Asia

Malaria-related discussions in Asia exhibited a fluctuating decline, rising from 696 posts in 2019 to 983 in 2020, then decreasing annually to 375 by 2024. Discussion themes shifted from malaria control to malaria and COVID-19, and global epidemic challenges, expanding to other infectious diseases from 2022 onward, reflecting phased changes alongside the epidemic’s progression.

Discussions in the Americas

Malaria-related discussions in the Americas peaked in 2020 (541 posts), declined sharply to 165 in 2021, and gradually recovered without reaching the previous peak. From 2019 to 2024, discussion themes sequentially covered advances and challenges in malaria treatment, drug use in malaria and COVID-19 control, malaria–COVID-19 intersection and control, transmission and prevention, overseas infections, and studies on malaria transmission and epidemics.

From 2019 to 2024, malaria discussion themes across Asia, Africa, and the Americas evolved continuously. In 2020, all three regions focused on the intersection of malaria and COVID-19, later expanding to transmission, outbreaks, and control. By 2024, discussions converged on case distribution and transmission patterns.

Discussion pattern of different netizens

As shown in Fig. 5, Personal Accounts had the highest posting volume, peaking in 2020 with 1820 posts, generating approximately 868,000 engagements, followed by a continuous decline. Other Organizational Accounts posted the most in 2022 with 3243 posts, but maintained a low and stable average engagement. Official Health-related Accounts had the fewest posts in 2020 with 176 posts, while the total engagement remained relatively stable, with average engagement also staying stable across the years. Unofficial Health-related Accounts showed notable fluctuations, with posting volume and engagement peaking in 2023 with 1000 posts and 53,726 total engagements, and reaching their lowest levels in 2022 with 396 posts and 6771 total engagements.

Variations in health-related discussions across netizen types

According to the topic modelling results (Table 8), the four user categories showed distinct focuses across symptoms, medications, and prevention. To further quantify these differences, statistical analysis of post distribution reveals that different netizen types exhibit varied proportions across the three themes. Official health-related accounts focused predominantly on prevention-related content (approximately 73.7%), while Unofficial health-related accounts and Personal accounts showed a more balanced distribution. Other organizational accounts also emphasized prevention (around 53.0%) but paid comparatively less attention to symptoms and medications.

For symptoms, Personal Accounts emphasized traditional Chinese medicine treatment (670 posts) and malaria treatment (400 posts), Official Health-related Accounts focused on malaria transmission (100 posts) and prevention and treatment (111 posts), Unofficial Health-related Accounts centered on vaccines and symptoms (167 posts), and Other Organizational Accounts discussed broader topics like prevention efforts and epidemics (400 posts).

For medications, Personal Accounts highlighted vaccines and integration of traditional and modern medicine (235 posts) and drug research (150 posts), Official Health-related Accounts focused on basic treatment topics, Unofficial Health-related Accounts discussed traditional Chinese medicine and scientific research (54 posts), and Other Organizational Accounts emphasized malaria prevention and control (343 posts).

For prevention, Other Organizational Accounts dominated, particularly with prevention of mosquito-borne transmission (3,000 posts) and global application of artemisinin (5,696 posts); Personal Accounts focused on environmental factors and transmission, Official Health-related Accounts emphasized transmission and control, and Unofficial Health-related Accounts addressed mosquito control and treatment and research.

Overall, Personal Accounts focused on individual experiences, Official Health-related Accounts on scientific communication and control strategies, Unofficial Health-related Accounts on medications and transmission, and Other Organizational Accounts on macro-level prevention.

Discussion

From a temporal perspective, it was found that in 2019, discussions mainly focused on sudden events such as Tu Youyou’s achievements and China’s health assistance to Africa; from 2020 to 2021, the discourse gradually expanded to topics including the pandemic, vaccines, and policy developments. After 2022, attention shifted toward control mechanisms and scientific research, indicating a transition from event-driven discussions to more stable and rational engagement with malaria-related issues. This may be due to China’s shift toward malaria prevention following its elimination announcement [44], along with a decline in high-impact events after 2022 that shaped netizens’ discussions. Public sentiment exhibited consistent patterns throughout this evolution. Scientific breakthroughs are typically associated with a surge in positive sentiment. Positively valenced events sustained attention only for a limited period. A small fraction of negative sentiment was attributable to concerns over the risk of malaria transmission. In contrast, negatively valenced events tended to sustain longer engagement. Research has shown that negative emotions are more likely to be retained in long-term memory compared to positive emotions, which may help explain their capacity to sustain prolonged netizens’ attention [45]. Notably, some discussions not directly related to malaria also attracted considerable attention, often centered on perceived transmission risks or associations with other public health incidents. Each April, discussions around World Malaria Day and China’s National Malaria Day show clear periodic peaks, focusing on representative figures, commemorative milestones, and national achievements. These commemorative days function as symbolic triggers, with their thematic content closely aligned with dominant topics throughout the year, underscoring their agenda-setting and mobilizing roles. This pattern echoes the observation by Anderson et al.[46] that World Malaria Day serves as a critical moment for fostering netizens’ engagement. Based on the three analytical dimensions above, the following communication strategies and policy recommendations were proposed: First, posts mentioning public figures such as Wang Jia and Tu Youyou tend to attract significantly more netizens’ attention. Therefore, leveraging celebrity influence may enhance the visibility and dissemination of malaria-related knowledge–for example, through short videos, public service campaigns, or collaborations with health education platforms to communicate core information on malaria, thereby deepening netizens’ understanding and strengthening awareness of malaria disease. In addition, netizens often expresses strong national pride in response to scientific achievements and China’s health assistance in Africa. These emotionally resonant events can serve as effective entry points for improving awareness of malaria disease and stimulating online engagement. By integrating real-time netizen discourse, scientific advancements, and international health assistance, they can facilitate thematic communication efforts that deepen netizens’ understanding of and attention to malaria. Second, negatively valenced events typically sustain netizens’ attention over longer periods. This underscores the need for timely and proactive responses from official sources to clarify misinformation and mitigate the spread of rumours, thereby preventing unnecessary anxiety among netizens. Although scientific breakthroughs generate overwhelmingly positive sentiment–exceeding 90%–threat-oriented topics such as imported malaria receive considerably less attention. This suggests a potentially over-optimistic perception among netizens. Although China has officially declared the elimination of indigenous malaria in recent years, imported cases continue to occur and pose a potential risk of transmission. Future communication efforts could focus on strengthening risk awareness, emphasizing the dangers of imported malaria, and improving the vigilance of netizens. Third, discussions around World Malaria Day and China’s National Malaria Day consistently peak each year, representing key opportunities for targeted health communication. As netizens frequently share content related to Tu Youyou and other scientific accomplishments during these periods, public health institutions could align outreach efforts with these narratives by producing educational videos, themed reports, or interactive campaigns to strengthen netizens’ understanding of malaria disease and foster sustained scientific engagement.

Africa-related topics have maintained a prominent presence on Weibo, likely driven by the sustained momentum of China’s health assistance to Africa. In contrast, discussions related to Asia appear more scattered, possibly due to the absence of high-profile aid initiatives or cross-border cooperation events, as well as the relatively low number of malaria cases in the region. Since 2020, discourse on the Americas has been largely embedded within the COVID-19 context, with malaria mentioned primarily as a secondary issue rather than a standalone topic–potentially due to its perceived similarity to COVID-19 in netizens’ discourse. Despite being the country with the highest number of malaria cases globally since 2019, Nigeria has received minimal attention on Weibo, particularly between 2021 and 2022. This limited visibility may be attributed to its exclusion from China’s key malaria aid initiatives and the absence of cooperation events capable of stimulating media and netizens’ engagement [47]. In contrast, Uganda experienced a notable surge in attention in 2023, having attracted little discussion in 2020. This peak coincided with a worsening malaria situation locally [48], and may also have been influenced by China’s emergency donation of 500,000 doses of antimalarial drugs in early 2024 [49]. The media coverage surrounding this aid likely contributed to heightened netizens’ discourse at the end of 2023.

Africa-related topics have consistently attracted high attention on Weibo, largely due to sustained netizens’ interest in China’s health assistance efforts. Public health institutions may further leverage this engagement to enhance malaria awareness. In contrast, discussions concerning Asia remain fragmented, likely reflecting the absence of high-profile aid programs or major cross-border initiatives. Strategic emphasis on existing regional collaborations and disease control achievements could foster a more coherent understanding of malaria risks in Asia among netizens. In addition, several Asian countries, such as India [50], continue to report malaria cases. Frequent travel and labor migration between these countries and China increase the risk of imported malaria, highlighting the need to enhance netizen awareness of associated risks and reinforce preventive vigilance. Since 2020, discourse related to the Americas has often been embedded within broader COVID-19 narratives, with malaria marginalized or conflated due to perceived similarities. Future health communication could clearly differentiate malaria from other diseases, highlight their distinct prevention strategies and risk profiles, and raise awareness of imported malaria cases from the Americas to maintain netizen vigilance.

Furthermore, distinct functional roles were evident across netizen types on the platform. Official Health-related Accounts primarily focused on public health education and disease prevention. However, between 2019 and 2024, only 1725 posts were published, reflecting limited activity and low levels of user engagement, which constrained the overall effectiveness of their health communication. In contrast, Unofficial Health-related Accounts and Personal Accounts featured more diverse content, including traditional medicine and individual experiences. Personal Accounts published a total of 7068 posts and received as many as 1,860,121 likes, demonstrating the highest level of engagement and reflecting the broad participation of the netizens in health communication. Unofficial Health-related Accounts published 3,066 posts during the same period, also exhibiting a notable degree of communicative vitality.Other Organizational Accounts predominantly addressed control measures and international health assistance. A total of 14,227 posts were published, garnering over 2,991,196 likes, demonstrating their strong communicative influence. Although Official Health-related Accounts possess high institutional authority, their limited interactivity diminishes their influence in health communication.

Despite their institutional authority, Official Health-related Accounts currently demonstrate low posting frequency and limited engagement, which undermines their communication impact. These accounts could enhance both content quantity and quality by adopting interactive formats such as infographics, educational videos, and live Q&A sessions to improve netizens’ engagement. Unofficial Health-related Accounts and Personal Accounts frequently share content related to traditional medicine and personal experiences, though the reliability of such information is often uncertain. Official Health-related Accounts are advised to proactively engage with these discussions by offering evidence-based clarifications, expert interpretations, and clear guidance to ensure the dissemination of reliable health information. For Other Organizational Accounts, diversifying content–such as featuring local success stories, behind-the-scenes views of aid efforts, and engaging multimedia updates–may help humanize institutional narratives, increase netizens’ interest, and foster more attention to malaria prevention and global health cooperation.

However, certain limitations and barriers to the direct adoption of the approach for addressing specific challenges in the communication strategies of official accounts were acknowledged. Though it offers valuable tool to detect negative sentiment trends, highlight instances where misinformation may amplify public concern, and identifying regional or national outreach blind spots to prioritize attention, it does not directly correct misinformation or prevent negative sentiment, and it cannot by itself classify specific exposure types such as tourists or migrant workers. These aspects require integration with domain expertise and complementary data sources. Nevertheless, the approach provides a solid foundation that official departments can easily adopt or commission. Its clearly defined workflow and modular core components reduce reliance on extensive domain expertise, making it easier to adapt across diverse operational contexts. Furthermore, the cost-effectiveness and real-time analytical capability enhance its practicality, enabling official accounts to strengthen social communication, mitigate misinformation risks, and ultimately improve public health awareness with ease.

Conclusion

This study systematically examined the dissemination patterns of malaria-related discourse on Chinese social media using data from the Sina Weibo platform. The study finds that malaria-related discussions on Sina Weibo follow a highly event-driven pattern, with netizens’ attention peaking in 2020 and steadily declining in the following years. Regional disparities in malaria-related discussions may be influenced by both the prevalence of the disease and the visibility of international health collaborations. Different types of netizens play distinct roles in health communication. Although unofficial accounts provide diverse content, the reliability of their information is often questionable. Therefore, official accounts need to ensure content reliability and become more active to effectively guide the dissemination of health information. The findings suggest that public health authorities are encouraged to enhance the proactiveness and responsiveness of official accounts, tailoring content and engagement strategies to different user groups in order to improve netizen awareness of malaria risks and prevention. Given the demographic characteristics of Weibo users, this study may not fully capture high-risk populations such as overseas migrant workers. Future efforts should therefore explore social media platforms such as WeChat and adopt more tailored communication strategies for underserved groups.

Safety and durability of influenza and SARS-CoV-2 antibody responses through 6 months after a single dose of mRNA-1083, a multicomponent influenza and COVID-19 vaccine, in adults ≥50 years
Authors: Lusine Kostanyan, Iris Wu, Melissa Sinkiewicz, Jose Cardona, Kimball Johnson, Rituparna Das
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTSeasonal influenza and SARS-CoV-2 continue to contribute substantially to global morbidity and mortality. mRNA-1083 is an investigational multicomponent mRNA vaccine targeting seasonal influenza and SARS-CoV-2. In this phase 3, observer-blind, active-controlled trial (NCT06097273), adults (aged ≥50 y) were randomly assigned (1:1) to receive mRNA-1083 (40 µg; plus placebo) or age-appropriate licensed comparator vaccines for influenza and COVID-19. Two substudies were included: Cohort A (≥65 y) and Cohort B (50–64 y). Primary objectives were to demonstrate noninferiority of mRNA-1083 versus comparators for influenza hemagglutination inhibition (HAI) responses and SARS-CoV-2 neutralizing antibody (nAb; PsVNA) responses at Day 29 after vaccination and to evaluate safety and reactogenicity of study vaccines. Here, we report safety, reactogenicity, and immunogenicity of mRNA-1083 through Day 181 in adults aged ≥50 y. mRNA-1083 induced HAI titers against all vaccine-matched influenza strains that were maintained through 6 months and were generally comparable to, or higher than, comparator responses. SARS-CoV-2 nAb responses remained higher for mRNA-1083 than comparators through 6 months. Reactogenicity was predominantly grade 1–2 in severity, and vaccination-related unsolicited adverse events were similar between groups. No serious adverse events or deaths related to vaccination were reported through 6 months. In adults aged ≥50 y, mRNA-1083 elicited durable influenza and SARS-CoV-2 humoral immune responses with an acceptable safety profile through 6 months. These findings support the use of mRNA-1083 as a multicomponent vaccine.ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT06097273 (https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06097273).

Introduction

Seasonal influenza and SARS-CoV-2 continue to contribute substantially to global morbidity and mortality, posing a significant disease burden.1,2 In temperate regions, influenza activity typically peaks during winter months, while COVID-19 circulates year round; consequently, the combined burden of these pathogens is often highest during the winter months, placing significant strain on health systems.3–5 The World Health Organization (WHO) estimates that seasonal influenza causes approximately 1 billion infections annually, of which 3 to 5 million cases are severe.2 Moreover, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) revealed that the burden of influenza in the United States (US), during the 2024–2025 influenza season, was approximately 47–82 million cases, of which 610,000 to 1.3 million were hospitalizations, and 27,000 to 130,000 were deaths.6 Patients aged ≥50 y were among the most affected, accounting for approximately 77% of all hospitalizations.7 Similarly, in the 2024–2025 season, COVID-19 continued to pose a significant burden in the US, with a reported 14.1 to 20.7 million cases and 390,000 to 550,000 hospitalizations, where patients aged ≥65 y accounted for 68% of all hospitalizations.8,9

Although vaccines are available for both seasonal influenza and COVID-19, continuous viral evolution and antigenic drift can reduce vaccine match and effectiveness across seasons and variants.10 Additionally, despite the availability of influenza and COVID-19 vaccines and real-world evidence that vaccination can reduce severity of illness and risk of hospitalization, vaccine uptake continues to remain well below the targeted uptake set by the WHO and CDC (75% for influenza and 70% for COVID-19 vaccinations).11–13 Between September 2023 and June 2024, the CDC estimated that the cumulative percentage of vaccinated adults in the United States was 48.5% for influenza and 22.5% for COVID-19.11 Enhancing vaccine coverage against both seasonal influenza and COVID-19 may reduce the disease burden posed by these illnesses. One strategy to improve immunization adherence and accessibility is the coadministration of influenza and COVID-19 vaccines, which has been recommended by the CDC and WHO.14–16 However, multi-pathogen combination vaccines may be preferable as they reduce the number of injections administered, potentially improving compliance with recommended vaccination schedules.14–16

mRNA-1083 is an investigational mRNA-based multicomponent vaccine that combines the components of an influenza hemagglutinin (HA)-based vaccine (mRNA-1010) with a second-generation SARS-CoV-2 vaccine (mNEXSPIKE [mRNA-1283; Moderna, Inc]) encoding the spike N-terminal domain and receptor-binding domain (NTD-RBD).17 Efficacy data for the component vaccines support their potential contribution to a combination approach.18 In a phase 3 study in adults aged ≥50 y, mRNA-1010 met pre-specified superiority criterion versus a licensed standard-dose influenza vaccine for prevention of RT-PCR–confirmed influenza illness (rVE, 26.6% [95% CI, 16.7, 35.4]).18 mRNA-1283 has also demonstrated noninferior relative vaccine efficacy versus Spikevax (mRNA-1273; Moderna, Inc.).19 In phase 3 studies, both mRNA-1010 and mRNA-1283 have demonstrated acceptable safety and noninferior immunogenicity compared with licensed comparators.19,20

A global phase 3 trial (NCT06097273) evaluated the immunogenicity and safety of mRNA-1083 versus age-appropriate, licensed comparator vaccines in adults aged ≥50 y.17 Interim analyses showed that immune responses elicited by mRNA-1083 met prespecified immunogenicity criteria, superior to licensed standard-dose influenza and COVID-19 vaccines in adults aged 50–64 y, and non-inferiority compared with licensed high-dose influenza and COVID-19 vaccines in adults aged ≥65 y, with no safety concerns observed.17 Durability through approximately 6 months is particularly relevant for seasonal vaccination programs, as it addresses persistence of immune responses across a typical winter respiratory virus period.4,21 Here, we report the final analyses through Day 181, describing the durability of humoral immunogenicity and the cumulative safety of mRNA-1083 through 6 months after vaccination.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

This phase 3, randomized, observer-blind, active-controlled study was conducted at 146 sites in the United States and evaluated the safety, reactogenicity, and immunogenicity of mRNA-1083 in adults ≥50 y of age (NCT06097273). The trial comprised two age-based substudies: Cohort A (≥65 y) and Cohort B (50–64 y).

Eligible participants were adults aged ≥50 y who were medically stable, in the investigator’s judgment, at enrollment. A full list of inclusion/exclusion criteria is included in the Supplement. Within each cohort, participants were randomly assigned using a centralized interactive response technology, to receive coadministration of either mRNA-1083 plus placebo (hereafter, referred to as the mRNA-1083 group) or licensed comparator vaccines for seasonal influenza and COVID-19. The random numbers for each cohort were computer-generated, using a randomization ratio of 1:1 for the two arms, block size of 4, stratified by prior influenza vaccination status (received vs not received), and in the ≥65 y cohort, by age group (≥65 to <75 vs ≥75 y). In both substudies, randomization was stratified by influenza vaccination status for the prior influenza season (received or not received since September 2022). Additionally, randomization in the substudy of those ≥65 y was also stratified by age 65 to <75 y and ≥75 y. Details on the observer-blind conduct are included in the Supplement.

The study was conducted in accordance with the protocol, applicable laws, and regulatory requirements, as well as the International Council for Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice guidelines, and the consensus of ethical principles derived from international guidelines, including the Declaration of Helsinki and Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences international ethical guidelines. The protocol was approved by the central institutional review board (Advarra, Inc). All participants provided written informed consent before enrollment.

This manuscript summarizes the final analyses through Day 181, including durability of humoral immune responses and cumulative safety through approximately 6 months of follow-up after vaccination.

Vaccines

mRNA-1083 is a lipid encapsulated mRNA-based vaccine containing five mRNA sequences, four encoding the membrane-bound hemagglutinin of seasonal influenza virus strains recommended by the WHO for the Northern Hemisphere 2023–2024 season (A/H1N1, A/H3N2, B/Victoria, B/Yamagata lineages)22 and one encoding the linked NTD-RBD of the SARS-CoV-2 spike glycoprotein of the fall/winter 2023–2024 recommended variant Omicron XBB.1.5.23 Placebo was 0.9% sodium chloride. Licensed quadrivalent inactivated influenza vaccine (IIV4) comparators were high-dose Fluzone (HD-IIV4; Sanofi Pasteur, Inc) for Cohort A (≥65 y) and standard dose Fluarix (SD-IIV4; GlaxoSmithKline) for Cohort B (50–64 y). Comparators were compliant with recommendations for the Northern Hemisphere 2023–2024 influenza season. The licensed SARS-CoV-2 comparator for both cohorts was mRNA-1273 (Spikevax; Moderna, Inc), which contains one mRNA sequence encoding the full-length SARS-CoV-2 prefusion stabilized spike protein of the Northern Hemisphere fall/winter 2023/2024 recommended variant XBB.1.5 Omicron.

On Day 1, participants received a single dose of co-administered intramuscular injections according to group assignment: mRNA-1083 (40 µg) plus placebo or HD-IIV4 plus mRNA-1273 (Cohort A; ≥65 y) or SD-IIV4 plus mRNA-1273 (Cohort B; 50–64 y).

Objectives

The primary objectives were to demonstrate noninferiority of mRNA-1083 versus comparators for influenza hemagglutination inhibition (HAI) responses and SARS-CoV-2 neutralizing antibody (nAb) responses at Day 29 after vaccination, and to evaluate the safety and reactogenicity of the study vaccines.17 Secondary immunogenicity objectives included superiority assessments for selected endpoints at Day 29.17 This manuscript also reports the pre-specified durability of antibody responses through Day 181 in an Immunogenicity Subset as an exploratory immunogenicity endpoint, and summarizes cumulative safety through Day 181.

Immunogenicity assessments

Blood samples for immunogenicity assessments were collected on Day 1 (baseline) and Day 29, with an additional sample collected on Day 181 from approximately the first 800 participants randomized per cohort for durability assessment. Immunogenicity endpoints included geometric mean titers (GMTs) of influenza HAI and SARS-CoV-2 pseudovirus neutralization assay (PsVNA) nAb concentrations at baseline and post-vaccination time points (Day 29 and Day 181), geometric mean fold rises (GMFR) from baseline, seroconversion rates (SCR; influenza) and seroresponse rates (SRR; SARS-CoV-2). SCR was defined as Day 29 and Day 181 post-injection level ≥1:40 if baseline is <1:10 or a 4-fold or greater rise if baseline was ≥1:10 in anti-HA antibodies measured by HAI assay. SRR was defined as Day 29 and Day 181 post-injection level ≥4-fold rise if baseline is greater than the lower limit of quantification (LLOQ) or ≥4 × LLOQ if baseline value was <LLOQ in the nAb values measured by PsVNA. Additional assay details can be found in the Supplement.

Safety assessments

Safety assessments comprised solicited local and systemic adverse reactions through 7 d after vaccination, and unsolicited adverse events (AEs) and severe AEs through 28 d after vaccination.17 In addition, serious AEs (SAEs), medically attended AEs, AEs of special interest, and AEs leading to discontinuation were assessed from Day 1 through the end of study (Day 181). Unsolicited AEs were collected throughout the study during scheduled clinic visits and safety phone calls, as well as through unsolicited participant reporting, observation and open-ended, non-leading verbal questioning. Following study vaccination, assessments were performed by blinded study staff.

Statistical analyses

For the primary immunogenicity objectives, prespecified hypothesis testing compared strain-matched immune responses against influenza and SARS-CoV-2 between mRNA-1083 and its respective licensed comparator group. Each substudy had its own hypotheses, analyses, and multiplicity control; no multiplicity adjustment was applied between cohorts.17

Exploratory immunogenicity endpoints for durability through Day 181 were assessed in the Day 181 Per-Protocol Immunogenicity Set (PPIS). The Day 181 Immunogenicity Subset included all randomized participants who agreed to provide samples for humoral immunogenicity on Day 181. Day 181 PPIS included all participants in the Day 181 Immunogenicity Subset and in the PPIS (for Day 29) who complied with the immunogenicity testing schedule at Day 181 and had not received prohibited medication through Day 181. For influenza, Day 29 and Day 181 geometric mean (GM) levels by HAI assay (all four strains) were summarized for mRNA-1083 (40 μg) plus placebo and the control arms (HD-IIV4 + mRNA-1273 for those ≥65 y and SD-IIV4 + mRNA-1273 for those 50–64 y); for SARS-CoV-2, Day 29 and Day 181 GM levels by PsVNA were summarized by treatment arm for the matched strain. Details on the estimands framework for the Day 181 immunogenicity objective are included in the Supplement (Supplementary Table 1).

Safety analyses were conducted separately by cohort using the Safety Set (all vaccinated participants) and were summarized by treatment received. AEs and adverse reactions (ARs) were coded using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities and severity was graded per the Department of Human and Health Services 2007 toxicity grading scale.24

Results

Participants

Enrollment occurred between October 19, 2023, and November 21, 2023.17 A total of 8061 participants were enrolled and randomized across the two age-based substudies. In Cohort A (≥65 y), 4037 participants were randomly assigned to receive mRNA-1083 (n = 2025) or HD-IIV4 + mRNA-1273 (n = 2012). In Cohort B (50–64 y), 4024 participants were randomly assigned to receive mRNA-1083 (n = 2009) or SD-IIV4 + mRNA-1273 (n = 2015) (Figure 1). More than 99% of randomized participants in each group received vaccination. The Day 181 Immunogenicity Subset included 922 participants in Cohort A and 921 participants in Cohort B; receipt of both study injections was high in both cohorts (99.7% in each). A total of 71 participants in Cohort A (mRNA-1083, 30/458 [6.6%]; HD-IIV4 + mRNA-1273, 41/464 [8.8%]) and 103 participants in Cohort B (mRNA-1083, 50/468 [10.7%]; SD-IIV4 + mRNA-1273, 53/453 [11.7%]) were excluded from the Day 181 PPIS (Figure 1). By Day 181, the most common reasons for study discontinuation were lost to follow-up (Cohort A: mRNA-1083, 1.3%; HD-IIV4 + mRNA-1273, 0.9%; Cohort B: mRNA-1083, 1.8%; SD-IIV4 + mRNA-1273, 1.5%) and withdrawal of consent by participant (Cohort A: mRNA-1083, 0.7%; HD-IIV4 + mRNA-1273, 0.5%; Cohort B: mRNA-1083, 0.7%; SD-IIV4 + mRNA-1273, 0.7%) (Figure 1). Immunogenicity

In both age cohorts, baseline antibody levels were similar between groups for all four vaccine-matched influenza strains (A/H1N1, A/H3N2, B/Victoria, B/Yamagata) and SARS-CoV-2. Following vaccination, mRNA-1083 increased influenza HAI and SARS-CoV-2 PsVNA GM antibody levels at Day 29 compared with baseline in both cohorts (Figure 2). Among adults aged ≥65 y (Cohort A), HAI GMTs declined from Day 29 to Day 181 but remained above baseline for A/H1N1 and A/H3N2 and were similar to baseline for influenza B strains in both groups (Figure 2). At Day 181, HAI GMTs were numerically higher with mRNA-1083 than with HD-IIV4 + mRNA-1273 for influenza A strains (model-based GMR, 1.206 for A/H1N1 and 1.135 for A/H3N2), while responses for influenza B strains were similar between groups (model-based GMR, 1.064 for B/Victoria and 1.038 for B/Yamagata; Supplementary Table 3). Similarly, at Day 181, SCRs were numerically higher in the mRNA-1083 group compared with the HD-IIV4 + mRNA-1273 group for influenza A strains and were similar for influenza B strains. The SCR percentage difference was 5.4 (95% CI, 0.5 to 10.3) for H1N1, 4.4 (95% CI, −0.6 to 9.5) for H3N2, 0.1 (95% CI, −3.1 to 3.4) for B/Victoria and 0.6 (95% CI, −2.0 to 3.3) for B/Yamagata (Supplementary Table 3).

Among adults aged 50–64 y in Cohort B, a decline from Day 29 to Day 181 was also observed, however, A/H1N1 and A/H3N2 GMTs remained above baseline, whereas GMTs for influenza B strains were similar to baseline (Figure 2). At Day 181, mRNA-1083 elicited numerically higher HAI GMTs versus SD-IIV4 + mRNA-1273 for influenza A strains (GMR: 1.273 for A/H1N1 and 1.322 for A/H3N2), while responses for influenza B strains were similar between groups (GMRs: ~1.1) (Supplementary Table 3). The SCRs at Day 181 for the mRNA-1083 group were numerically higher for both influenza A and B strains compared with the SD-IIV4 + mRNA-1273 group. The SCR percentage difference was 14.0 (95% CI, 8.0 to 19.9) for H1N1, 7.8 (95% CI, 2.1 to 13.5) for H3N2, 1.2 (95% CI, −2.2 to 4.6) for B/Victoria and 2.5 (95% CI, −0.5 to 5.6) for B/Yamagata (Supplementary Table 3).

PsVNA concentrations to the vaccine-matched SARS-CoV-2 strain (XBB.1.5) remained higher than the comparator through 6 months post-vaccination with mRNA-1083 in both age cohorts (Figure 2). At Day 181, mRNA-1083 elicited numerically higher PsVNA geometric mean concentrations versus HD-IIV4 + mRNA-1273 and SD-IIV4 + mRNA-1273 for the SARS-CoV-2 Omicron XBB.1.5 strain (GMR: 1.473 and 1.311, respectively; Supplementary Table 3). In Cohort A (≥65 y), the SRR percentage difference was 13.2 (95% CI, 6.5 to 19.8), whereas, in Cohort B (50–64 y) the SRR percentage difference was 8.1 (95% CI, 1.2 to 14.8) (Supplementary Table 3).

Safety

Within 6 months post-vaccination, the proportion of participants reporting any unsolicited AE was similar between treatment groups within each age cohort (Table 2). The proportion of participants reporting any unsolicited AE from any cause was 503 (25.0%) in mRNA-1083 recipients and 500 (24.9%) in HDV-IIV4 + mRNA-1273 recipients in Cohort A; and 377 (18.9%) in mRNA-1083 recipients and 376 (18.8%) in SD-IIV4 + mRNA-1273 recipients. Discussion

In this phase 3, randomized, observer-blind, active-controlled study (NCT06097273; P301), the investigational multicomponent mRNA-1083 vaccine met all prespecified Day 29 noninferiority criteria for immune responses against vaccine-matched influenza strains and SARS-CoV-2, and elicited higher HAI titers than licensed comparators against three clinically relevant influenza strains.17 Microneutralization GMTs were assessed as a complementary assay and were consistent with HAI results, with a correlation demonstrated between the two assays.17 Additionally, higher immune responses indicated by PsVNA geometric mean concentrations were shown in the mRNA-1083 groups compared with the groups receiving the licensed mRNA COVID-19 comparator (mRNA-1273).17 No safety concerns were identified, and most local and systemic adverse reactions that occurred within 7 d of vaccination were grade 1 or 2 in severity in both cohorts.17 This manuscript summarizes the final analyses through Day 181, including durability of humoral immune responses and cumulative safety through approximately 6 months of follow-up after vaccination.

Extending the Day 29 findings, in this final analysis, we have observed persistence of humoral immune responses through Day 181 for both the influenza and SARS-CoV-2 components in adults aged ≥50 y. In both age cohorts, HAI titers to all four vaccine-matched influenza strains (A/H1N1, A/H3N2, B/Victoria, B/Yamagata) were maintained through 6 months and were generally comparable to or higher than comparator responses, while PsVNA responses to XBB.1.5 remained higher for mRNA-1083 than comparators through 6 months in both cohorts. HAI titers are widely regarded as a surrogate marker associated with protection against influenza illness and have been shown to predict clinical benefit in adults, including older adults.25 To further contextualize the clinical relevance of influenza HAI responses, analyses from a phase 3 trial of the influenza component vaccine (mRNA-1010; P304) demonstrated an inverse association between Day 29 strain-specific HAI titers and predicted risk of influenza-like illness, which supports that HAI titers continue to fulfill this role for mRNA-based influenza vaccines in the same manner as for egg-based influenza vaccines.26,27 While these findings are derived from the component vaccine program, they provide useful context for interpreting HAI responses for the mRNA-based influenza containing vaccine through Day 181 observed in this study. Additionally, PsVNA levels are considered as a surrogate marker of protection against COVID-19. Therefore, these results suggest that mRNA-1083 can provide meaningful protection against influenza and COVID-19 outcomes.28,29

Durability of the humoral immune response through 6 months is notable given that seasonal vaccination programs are typically intended to provide coverage across a respiratory virus season, and the observed durability suggests that a single dose of mRNA-1083 may provide protection over a clinically relevant interval.4,21 The age-stratified design further informs performance in older adults, in whom immunosenescence can attenuate vaccine-induced immunity, and contributes to the rationale for tailored vaccination approaches in older adults.30 In this context, the maintenance of immune responses through 6 months among adults aged ≥50 y, with immunogenicity generally comparable to or higher than age-appropriate licensed comparator regimens, supports implementation of mRNA-1083 in vaccination schedules for older adults.

mRNA-1083 was generally well tolerated through 6 months of follow-up, where rates of unsolicited AEs related to vaccination were similar between groups within each cohort, and no SAEs or deaths related to vaccination were reported through 6 months post-vaccination. Notably, reported unsolicited AEs through Day 181 were similar to those observed through Day 29.17 No new categories or safety signals were identified between Day 29 and Day 181, with consistent type and severity through Day 181 compared with Day 29.17 The similar rates of unsolicited AEs observed between vaccine groups in this study suggest that the safety profile of mRNA-1083 was comparable to regimens containing non-mRNA influenza vaccines (HD-IIV4 in adults aged ≥65 y and SD-IIV4 in adults aged 50–64 y). These findings align with those observed in a phase 3 study of the mRNA-based seasonal influenza vaccine, mRNA-1010, in which AE rates in adults were similar to those observed with licensed non-mRNA influenza vaccines.20 Although the present study did not include non-mRNA COVID-19 vaccine comparators, published comparative and post-authorization evidence for COVID-19 vaccines suggest that SAEs are uncommon across vaccine platforms, with the rate of rare AEs differing across platforms.31,32 More specifically, myocarditis and pericarditis are recognized risks with mRNA COVID-19 vaccines, whereas other rare AEs have been associated with some adenoviral-vector vaccines.31,32

The strengths of this study include the randomized, observer-blind, active-controlled design enabling comparisons to licensed, age-appropriate comparator regimens. The use of a placebo in the mRNA-1083 group helped preserve blinding by ensuring that participants in both study groups received two injections. A limitation is that the study population diversity matched the general US population, which may limit generalizability to other settings.

In conclusion, mRNA-1083 induced robust influenza and SARS-CoV-2 antibody responses in adults aged ≥50 y that were maintained through 6 months post-vaccination, with durability comparable to or exceeding age-appropriate licensed comparators, and demonstrated an acceptable safety profile through 6 months, with no safety signals identified. Together, these findings support the immunogenic durability and safety of mRNA-1083 as a multicomponent vaccine that could simplify adult seasonal vaccination.

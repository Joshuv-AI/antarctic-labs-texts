How many points contain homothetic copies in their Hurwitz continued fraction expansion? Thanks: Keywords : continued fraction; Hausdorff dimension; iterated function system
Yuto Nakajima and Hiroki Takahasi

Abstract
. We prove that the set of complex irrationals whose partial quotients in their Hurwitz continued fraction expansion are naturally regarded as subsets of ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} and contain infinitely many homothetic copies of any finite subset of ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} is of Hausdorff dimension 1 1 . Our result provides a clear and concrete example of multidimensional pattern emergence in number-theoretic expansions.

1.
Introduction

The existence of arithmetic progressions in sets of integers is a profound problem in mathematics.
Szemerédi
[
11
]
confirmed a long standing conjecture of Erdős and Turán by showing that any subset of
\mathbb{N}
with positive upper density contains arithmetic progressions of arbitrary lengths. Furstenberg and Katznelson
[
5
]
established a multidimensional extension of Szemerédi’s theorem, replacing arithmetic progressions by homothetic copies of finite subsets of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
.

A natural question is whether these pattern structures
exist in number-theoretic expansions. For the regular continued fraction expansion, Tong and Wang
[
12
]

proved that
the set of irrationals in
(0,1)
whose partial quotients are strictly increasing and contain arithmetic progressions of arbitrary lengths and common differences
is of Hausdorff dimension
1/2
.
Cao and Zhang
[
13
]
generalized part of the results in
[
12
]

to infinite iterated function systems on compact intervals.

In this paper we focus on
the Hurwitz complex continued fraction. It represents the minimal natural context in which multidimensional combinatorial and fractal phenomena we study appear in an explicit and tractable form.
Under the natural identification of
\mathbb{C}
with
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the Gaussian field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})
and the ring
\mathbb{Z}(\sqrt{-1})
of Gaussian integers are identified with
\mathbb{Q}^{2}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
respectively.
Set

U=\left\{x\in\mathbb{C}\colon-\frac{1}{2}\leq{\rm Re}(x)<\frac{1}{2},\ -\frac{1}{2}\leq{\rm Im}(x)<\frac{1}{2}\right\}.

Any
x\in U\setminus\mathbb{Q}^{2}

has the unique infinite expansion of the form

x=\frac{\displaystyle{\hfill{1}\hfill\;\vrule}}{\displaystyle{\vrule\;\hfill{c_{1}(x)}\hfill}}+\frac{\displaystyle{\hfill{1}\hfill\;\vrule}}{\displaystyle{\vrule\;\hfill{c_{2}(x)}\hfill}}+\frac{\displaystyle{\hfill{1}\hfill\;\vrule}}{\displaystyle{\vrule\;\hfill{c_{3}(x)}\hfill}}+\cdots,

where
c_{n}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}(\sqrt{-1})\setminus\{\pm 1,\pm\sqrt{-1}\}

for
n\geq 1
,
see
[
2
, Theorem 6.1]
for example.
This expansion is
called the

Hurwitz continued fraction expansion

[
7
]
.
We restrict ourselves to the set

E=\{x\in U\setminus\mathbb{Q}^{2}\colon c_{m}(x)\neq c_{n}(x)\ \text{\rm for all }m,n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ with }m\neq n\}.

Each
x\in E
induces an injection

n\in\mathbb{N}\mapsto c_{n}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and so

\{c_{n}(x)\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is naturally regarded as an infinite subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
Let
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. A subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
of the form
v+nA
,
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
is called
a homothetic copy of
A
.
Let

H=\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{l}\!\!\!$x\in E\colon\{c_{n}(x)\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}$ contains infinitely many\\
\!\!\!\! homothetic copies of any finite subset of $\mathbb{Z}^{2}$\end{tabular}\right\}.

Let
\dim_{\rm H}
denote the Hausdorff dimension on the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

\dim_{\rm H}E=\dim_{\rm H}H=1.

The restriction to the Hurwitz continued fraction allows us to construct explicit sequences of partial quotients exhibiting the desired patterns.
Using an iterated function system extracted from the Hurwitz continued fraction, we first
construct
a seed set
in
E
of Hausdorff dimension close to
1
with a controlled structure. By carefully inserting arbitrarily large integer squares, we modify the seed set to ensure that it lies in
H
and keeps Hausdorff dimension close to
1
. This verifies
the lower bound
\dim_{\rm H}H\geq 1
.
To our knowledge, this type of construction first appeared in
[
4
]
in the context of symbolic dynamics.
The upper bound
\dim_{\rm H}E\leq 1
follows from

[
6
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Our result provides a clear and concrete example
of multidimensional pattern emergence in number-theoretic expansions,
illustrating both the combinatorial richness and fractal complexity of the Hurwitz continued fraction.
Theorem
1.1
shows that the set

H
is maximal dimensional within the naturally constrained set
E
, which indicates the richness of the phenomenon. The method presented here extends to other expansions, highlighting the broader relevance of constructive approaches in the study of multidimensional pattern formation.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we summarize basic properties of the Hurwitz continued fraction.

2.1.
Extracting an infinite iterated function system

There is a canonical infinite iterated function system that represents the regular continued fraction, whereas this is not the case for the Hurwitz continued fraction.
However, neglecting finitely many cylinders one can extract an infinite iterated function system from the Hurwitz continued fraction that can be used for the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Put

\mathbb{D}_{1}=\{(k,\ell)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\colon k^{2}+\ell^{2}\geq 2\}\ \text{
and }\ \mathbb{D}_{2}=\{(k,\ell)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\colon k^{2}+\ell^{2}\geq 8\}.

For each
i=(k,\ell)\in\mathbb{D}_{1}
,
define a region

U_{i}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{C}\colon-\frac{1}{2}\leq{\rm Re}(x^{-1})-k<\frac{1}{2},\ -\frac{1}{2}\leq{\rm Im}(x^{-1})-\ell<\frac{1}{2}\right\}.

It is easy to see that:

•

U_{i}\cap\partial U\neq\emptyset

if
i\in\mathbb{D}_{1}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{2}
and

\overline{U_{i}}\subset U
if
i\in\mathbb{D}_{2}
;

•

\bigcup\{U_{i}\cap U\colon i\in\mathbb{D}_{1}\}=U\setminus\{0\}
.

For each
i=(k,\ell)\in\mathbb{D}_{2}

define a map
\phi_{i}\colon\overline{U}\to\mathbb{C}
by

\phi_{i}(x)=\frac{1}{x+k+\sqrt{-1}\ell}.

Note that
\phi|_{{\rm int}(U)}
is univalent,
\phi_{i}(U)=U_{i}
and
\phi_{i}(\overline{U})\subset\overline{U}
.
For
n\geq 2
and
(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n})\in\mathbb{D}_{2}^{n}
,
write
\phi_{\omega_{1}\cdots\omega_{n}}=\phi_{\omega_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\phi_{\omega_{n}}.

Define a map
\Pi\colon\mathbb{D}_{2}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\overline{U}
by

\Pi(\underline{\omega})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\phi_{\omega_{1}\cdots\omega_{n}}(0),

where
\underline{\omega}=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\ldots).
The set

\Lambda=\Pi(\mathbb{D}_{2}^{\mathbb{N}})

is contained in
U\setminus\mathbb{Q}^{2}
. If
x=\Pi(\underline{\omega})

then
\omega_{n}=c_{n}(x)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, see
[
8
]
for details.
We list properties of the iterated function system
\{\phi_{i}\colon i\in\mathbb{D}_{2}\}
.

Figure 1.
The shaded region indicates
\bigcup\{U_{i}\cap U\colon i\in\mathbb{D}_{1}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{2}\}
.

(I)

(open set condition) For all
i,j\in\mathbb{D}_{2}
with
i\neq j
,

\phi_{i}({\rm int}\overline{U})\cap\phi_{j}({\rm int}\overline{U})=\emptyset.

(II)

(conformality)
For each
i\in\mathbb{D}_{2}
,

\phi_{i}|_{U}
is univalent,
\phi_{i}(U)=U_{i}
,
\phi_{i}(\overline{U})\subset\overline{U}
, and has a univalent extension to a connected open set containing
\overline{U}
.

(III)

(uniform contraction) There exists
\rho\in(0,1)
such that for all
i\in\mathbb{D}_{2}
,

\sup_{x\in U}|D\phi_{i}(x)|\leq\rho,

where
D\phi_{i}(x)
denotes the complex derivative of
\phi_{i}
at
x
.

(IV)

(2-decay) There exist positive constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
such that for all
i\in\mathbb{D}_{2}
and
x\in U
,

(2.1)

\frac{C_{1}}{|i|^{2}}\leq|D\phi_{i}(x)|\leq\frac{C_{2}}{|i|^{2}}.

(V)

(isolation)
\overline{\Lambda}\subset{\rm int}(\overline{U})
.

Items (I)–(IV) are easy to check by
[
8
, Lemma 4.2]
. Item (V) follows from the observation that
\bigcup\{U_{i}\colon i\in\mathbb{D}_{1}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{2}\}
contains a neighborhood of
\partial U
as in
Figure

.

We will use two well-known lemmas on univalent functions.
The first one follows from the Koebe’s distortion theorem
(see e.g.,
[
1
, Theorem 1.4]
).

Lemma 2.1

(
[
8
, Lemma 2.2]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a region and let
A
be a compact subset of
\Omega
.
There exists a constant
K\geq 1
such that for every univalent function
\phi\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
and all
x_{1}
,
x_{2}\in A
we have

\frac{|D\phi(x_{1})|}{|D\phi(x_{2})|}\leq K.

For
x\in\mathbb{C}
and
\delta>0
let

B(x,\delta)=\{y\in\mathbb{C}\colon|x-y|<\delta\}
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
8
, Lemma 2.3]
)
.

Let
K\geq 1
,
let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a region and let
\phi\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
be univalent. For any
x\in\Omega
and
\delta>0
such that

B(x,\delta)\subset\Omega\ \text{ and }\ \sup_{x_{1},x_{2}\in B(x,\delta)}\frac{|D\phi(x_{1})|}{|D\phi(x_{2})|}\leq K,

we have

B\left(\phi(x),\frac{\delta|D\phi(x)|}{3K}\right)\subset\phi(B(x,\delta)).

2.2.
Estimates of Euclidean diameters of cylinders

For a set
A\subset\mathbb{C}

let
{\rm diam}(A)=\sup\{|x_{1}-x_{2}|\colon x_{1},x_{2}\in A\}.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in\mathbb{D}_{1}^{n}
, define non-negative integers

p_{n}
and
q_{n}
by the recursion formulas

(2.2)

\begin{split}p_{-1}=1,\ p_{0}=0,\ p_{j}&=c_{j}p_{j-1}+p_{j-2}\ \text{ for }1\leq j\leq n,\\
q_{-1}=0,\ q_{0}=1,\ q_{j}&=c_{j}q_{j-1}+q_{j-2}\ \text{ for }1\leq j\leq n.\end{split}

Following
[
6
, Lemma 2.4]
, let
\xi\in U
denote the complex irrational such that

c_{n}(\xi)=3+4\sqrt{-1}
for all
n\geq 1
, and set
\gamma=2|\xi|/(|\xi|+1)^{2}.

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N},

(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in\mathbb{D}_{2}^{n}

we have

\gamma\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(|c_{j}|+1)^{2}}<{\rm diam}(\phi_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}}(\overline{U}))<{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(|c_{j}|-1)^{2}}.

Proof.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in\mathbb{D}_{2}^{n}
.
By
[
8
, Lemma 4.3]
,
{\rm diam}(\phi_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}}(\overline{U}))
equals the diameter of the set of elements of
U
which have the finite or infinite Hurwitz continued fraction expansion beginning with
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}
.
By
[
6
, Lemma 2.4]
we have

(2.3)

\frac{\gamma}{|q_{n}|^{2}}\leq{\rm diam}(\phi_{c_{1}\cdots c_{n}}(\overline{U}))\leq\frac{2}{|q_{n}|^{2}}.

By (
2.2
)
we have

\displaystyle|q_{n}|=|c_{n}q_{n-1}+q_{n-2}|=|q_{n-1}|\left|c_{n}+\frac{q_{n-2}}{q_{n-1}}\right|=\cdots=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left|c_{j}+\frac{q_{j-2}}{q_{j-1}}\right|.

Since
\{|q_{n}|\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is strictly increasing
[
7
, p.195]
, for all
1\leq j\leq n
we have

|c_{j}|-1<|c_{j}|-\left|\frac{q_{j-2}}{q_{j-1}}\right|\leq\left|c_{j}+\frac{q_{j-2}}{q_{j-1}}\right|\leq|c_{j}|+\left|\frac{q_{j-2}}{q_{j-1}}\right|<|c_{j}|+1.

Therefore

(2.4)

\prod_{j=1}^{n}(|c_{j}|-1)<|q_{n}|<\prod_{j=1}^{n}(|c_{j}|+1).

Combining (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) yields the desired double inequalities.
∎

3.
On the proof of the main result

In this section we complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

3.1.
Dimension estimate of a seed set

We introduce the maximum
norm
\|x\|_{\infty}=\max\{|{\rm Re}(x)|,|{\rm Im}(x)|\}
for
x\in\mathbb{C}
.
Note that
\|x\|_{\infty}\leq|x|\leq\sqrt{2}\|x\|_{\infty}
.
We put
t=3
, and introduce a seed set

S=\{x\in U\setminus\mathbb{Q}^{2}\colon t^{n}\leq\|c_{n}(x)\|_{\infty}<2t^{n}\ \text{for all}\ n\geq 1\}.

For any
x\in S
the sequence
\{\|c_{n}(x)\|_{\infty}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is strictly increasing, and so
S\subset E
.

Proposition 3.1
.

\dim_{\rm H}S\geq 1
.

Although this proposition can be proved using the general result in
[
10
]
,
we give a self-contained proof.
We set

I^{\infty}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}I^{(n)},\text{ where }I^{(n)}=\{i\in\mathbb{D}_{2}\colon t^{n}\leq\|i\|_{\infty}<2t^{n}\}.

We endow each
I^{(n)}
with the discrete topology, and
I^{\infty}
with the product topology.
For a finite word
\omega
from
\mathbb{D}_{2}
, let
|\omega|
denote its word length. For
\underline{\omega}=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\ldots)\in I^{\infty}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\underline{\omega}|_{n}=\omega_{1}\cdots\omega_{n}
.
For a finite word
\tau
from
\mathbb{D}_{2}
, define

[\tau]=\{\underline{\omega}\in I^{\infty}\colon\underline{\omega}|_{|\tau|}=\tau\}.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exist a Borel probability measure
\mu
on
I^{\infty}
and a constant
C>0
such that
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and all
\omega\in\prod_{j=1}^{n}I^{(j)}
,

\mu([\omega])\leq C\cdot{\rm diam}(\phi_{\omega}(\overline{U})).

Proof.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
P_{n}

denote the uniform probability distribution on the discrete space
\prod_{j=1}^{n}I^{(j)}
.
A direct calculation shows

\#I^{(j)}=12t^{2j}-4t^{j}
for all
j\geq 1
.
For each
(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n})\in\prod_{j=1}^{n}I^{(j)}
we have

P_{n}(\{(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n})\})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\#I^{(j)}}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{12t^{2j}-4t^{j}}.

By Kolmogorov’s extension theorem,
there is a Borel probability measure
\mu
on
I^{\infty}
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and all
(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n})\in\prod_{j=1}^{n}I^{(j)}
,

(3.1)

\mu([\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n}])=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{12t^{2j}-4t^{j}}.

For
1\leq j\leq n
,
the definition of
I^{(j)}
gives
|\omega_{j}|<\sqrt{8}t^{j}
. By this and the first inequality in Lemma
2.3
,

(3.2)

{\rm diam}(\phi_{\omega}(\overline{U}))\geq\gamma\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(|\omega_{j}|+1)^{2}}>\gamma\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(\sqrt{8}t^{j}+1)^{2}}.

Combining (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) yields the desired inequality.∎

Since
\Pi(I^{\infty})\subset S,
it suffices to show
\dim_{\rm H}\Pi(I^{\infty})\geq 1
.
We will pick a measure
\mu
as in Lemma
3.2
, and apply the mass distribution principle to

\mu\circ(\Pi|_{I^{\infty}})^{-1}
. To this end we need some preliminary work.
Put

r_{0}=\min_{i\in I^{(1)}}|D\phi_{i}(0)|.

For
x\in\Pi(I^{\infty})
and
r\in(0,r_{0})
,
let
V(x,r)
denote the set of finite words
\omega\in\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{n}I^{(j)}
that satisfy the following conditions:

(i)

B(x,r)\cap\phi_{\omega}(\overline{U})\neq\emptyset;

(ii)

|D\phi_{\omega}(0)|\geq r
, and

|D\phi_{\omega a}(0)|<r

for some
a\in I^{(|\omega|+1)}.

Since each
I^{(j)}
,
j\geq 1
is a finite set,

\min\{|\omega|\colon\omega\in V(x,r)\}\to\infty
as
r\to 0
.

Although the collection
\{[\omega]\colon\omega\in V(x,r)\}
of cylinders covers

I^{\infty}\cap\Pi^{-1}B(x,r)
, it may contain redundant cylinders. Hence
we make an adjustment.
Let
V^{\prime}(x,r)
denote the set of
\omega\in V(x,r)
such that
[\omega]\subset[\tau]
holds for some
\tau\in V(x,r)
with
|\tau|<|\omega|
. Put

Lemma 3.3
.

If

x\in\Pi(I^{\infty})
and
r\in(0,r_{0})

then for any
\omega\in V^{\prime}(x,r)
there is
\tau\in V^{*}(x,r)
such that

and
|\tau|<|\omega|
.

Proof.

Let
\omega\in V^{\prime}(x,r).
There is
\tau_{1}\in V(x,r)
such that
[\omega]\subset[\tau_{1}]
and
|\tau_{1}|<|\omega|
. If
\tau_{1}\in V^{*}(x,r)
then we are done. Otherwise, we have
\tau_{1}\in V^{\prime}(x,r)
and there is
\tau_{2}\in V(x,r)
such that
[\omega]\subset[\tau_{1}]\subset[\tau_{2}]
and
|\tau_{2}|<|\tau_{1}|<|\omega|
. Since
|\omega|
is finite, repeating this procedure we can find

with the desired property.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

If

and

for all

If
\omega
,
\tau\in W^{*}(x,r)
and
\omega\neq\tau

\omega|_{j}\neq\tau|_{j}

(a) we get

(3.6)

\begin{split}{\rm Leb}\left(\bigcup_{\omega\in V^{*}(x,r)}\phi_{\omega}({\rm int}\overline{U})\right)&=\sum_{\omega\in V^{*}(x,r)}{\rm Leb}(\phi_{\omega}({\rm int}\overline{U}))\\
&\geq\frac{\pi}{81K^{2}}\sum_{\omega\in V^{*}(x,r)}|D\phi_{\omega}(0)|^{2}\\
&\geq\#V^{*}(x,r)\frac{\pi r^{2}}{81K^{2}},\end{split}

where

denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Combining (

) and (

) yields

(3.7)

\#V^{*}(x,r)\leq\frac{81K^{2}}{\pi r^{2}}{\rm Leb}\left(B\left(x,\left(1+\frac{72\sqrt{2}K}{C_{1}}t^{2\sqrt{2\log(1/r)}}\right)r\right)\right).

From (
3.4
) and (

∎

3.2.
Modifying the seed set

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Fix a strictly increasing sequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
of positive integers such that

(3.9)

t^{n_{k}+1}>2t^{n_{k}}+\sqrt{2\varepsilon n_{k}}\ \text{ for all }k\geq 1,

(3.10)

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\varepsilon n_{j}(n_{j}+2)\leq 2\varepsilon n_{k}^{2}\ \text{ for all }k\geq 1,

(3.11)

\prod_{j=1}^{n+1}\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2}t^{j+1})}\cdot\frac{1}{t^{2\varepsilon n^{2}}}\geq\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{t^{2(j-1)}}\right)^{1+5\varepsilon}\ \text{ for all }n\geq n_{1}.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
define an integer square

W_{k}=\{2t^{n_{k}}+2t^{n_{k}}\sqrt{-1}+x\colon z\in\mathbb{Z}(\sqrt{-1}),\ {\rm Re}(x)\geq 0,{\rm Im}(x)\geq 0,\ \|x\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{\varepsilon n_{k}}-1\},

and write
W_{k}=\{w^{(k)}_{1},\ldots,w^{(k)}_{\#W_{k}}\}
.
By (
3.9
) we have

\max_{x\in W_{k}}\|x\|_{\infty}<2t^{n_{k}}+\sqrt{\varepsilon n_{k}}<t^{n_{k}+1}<2t^{n_{k+1}}\leq\min_{x\in W_{k+1}}\|x\|_{\infty},

which implies that the integer squares are pairwise disjoint.

Let
y\in S
.
We
insert the elements of
\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{\infty}W_{k}
into
\{c_{n}(y)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to define a new sequence

\begin{split}&\ldots,c_{n_{k}-1}(y),\ c_{n_{k}}(y),\ \framebox{$w_{1}^{(k)},\ldots,w_{\#W_{k}}^{(k)}$},\ c_{n_{k}+1}(y),\ldots\\
&\ldots,c_{n_{k+1}}(y),\ \framebox{$w_{1}^{(k+1)},\ldots,w_{\#W_{k+1}}^{(k+1)}$},\ c_{n_{k+1}+1}(y),\ldots\end{split}

Let
x(y)
denote the point in
U\setminus\mathbb{Q}^{2}
whose Hurwitz continued fraction expansion is given by this new sequence. Let
\widetilde{S}
denote the collection of these points:

\widetilde{S}=\{x(y)\in U\setminus\mathbb{Q}^{2}\colon y\in S\}.

Lemma 3.6
.

\widetilde{S}\subset H.

Proof.

Let
y\in S
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\|c_{n_{k}}(y)\|_{\infty}<2t^{n_{k}}\leq\min_{x\in W_{k}}\|x\|_{\infty}
,
and by (
3.9
),

\|c_{n_{k}+1}(y)\|_{\infty}\geq t^{n_{k}+1}>2t^{n_{k}}+\sqrt{\varepsilon n_{k}}>\max_{x\in W_{k}}\|x\|_{\infty}.

Hence we have
c_{n}(y)\notin W_{k}
for all
n\geq 1
.
Since
k\in\mathbb{N}
is arbitrary and the integer squares are pairwise disjoint as shown above,

c_{m}(x(y))\neq c_{n}(x(y))
holds for all
m
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\neq n,

namely
x(y)\in E.

For any finite set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,
there is
k_{0}\geq 1
such that if
k\geq k_{0}
then
W_{k}
contains a homothetic copy of
A
.
Hence,
\{c_{n}(x(y))\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
contains infinitely many homothetic copies of any finite subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and so
x(y)\in H
. Since
y\in S
is arbitrary,
the desired inclusion holds.
∎

Clearly the map
y\in S\mapsto x(y)\in\widetilde{S}
is bijective.
Let
f\colon\widetilde{S}\to S
denote the inverse of this map,
which eliminates all the digits in
\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{\infty}W_{k}
.

Proposition 3.7
.

There exists
C>0
such that

|f(x_{1})-f(x_{2})|\leq C|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\frac{1}{1+5\varepsilon}}

for all
x_{1}
,
x_{2}\in\widetilde{S}
.

We finish the proof of Theorem
1.1
assuming Proposition
3.7
.
Since
|c_{n}(x)|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
for all
x\in E
, from
[
6
, Theorem 1.3]
it follows that
\dim_{\rm H}E\leq 1
. By
H\subset E
and Lemma
3.6
, we have
\dim_{\rm H}E\geq\dim_{\rm H}H\geq\dim_{\rm H}\widetilde{S}.

By
[
3
, Proposition 3.3]
and
Proposition
3.7
, we have

\dim_{\rm H}\widetilde{S}\geq\frac{1}{1+5\varepsilon}\dim_{\rm H}S=\frac{1}{1+5\varepsilon}.

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary we obtain
\dim_{\rm H}H\geq 1,
namely
\dim_{\rm H}E=\dim_{\rm H}H=1.

∎

An idea behind a proof of
Proposition
3.7
is that

|c_{1}(y)|,\ldots,|c_{n_{q}}(y)|
kills
the effect of modification by the integer squares
W_{1},\ldots,W_{q}
evaluated in the next lemma.

Lemma 3.8
.

For all
q\geq 1
we have

\prod_{k=1}^{q}\prod_{i\in W_{k}}\frac{1}{|i|+1}\geq\frac{1}{t^{2\varepsilon n_{q}^{2}}}.

Proof.

For all
1\leq k\leq q
we have
\#W_{k}\leq\varepsilon n_{k}
and

\prod_{i\in W_{k}}\frac{1}{|i|+1}>\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2}(2t^{n_{k}}+\sqrt{\varepsilon n_{k}})+1)^{\#W_{k}}}\geq\frac{1}{(\sqrt{8}t^{n_{k}}+\sqrt{2\varepsilon n_{k}}+1)^{\varepsilon n_{k}}}.

By (
3.9
) and
t=3
we have

\sqrt{8}t^{n_{k}}+\sqrt{2\varepsilon n_{k}}+1\leq\sqrt{8}t^{n_{k}}+t^{n_{k}}\leq t^{n_{k}+2}
. Plugging this inequality into the above denominator, multiplying the result over all
1\leq k\leq q
and then using (
3.10
) we obtain

\prod_{k=1}^{q}\prod_{i\in W_{k}}\frac{1}{|i|+1}\geq\prod_{k=1}^{q}\frac{1}{t^{{\varepsilon n_{k}}(n_{k}+2)}}\geq\frac{1}{t^{2\varepsilon n_{q}^{2}}}.\qed

Proof of Proposition
3.7
.

In view of the isolation property (V), take
\delta>0
such that
\overline{U}
contains the
\delta

\Lambda
.
For
y_{1}
,
y_{2}\in S
with
y_{1}\neq y_{2}

let

denote the minimal integer

Notice that

(3.12)

\inf\{|f^{-1}(y_{1})-f^{-1}(y_{2})|\colon y_{1},y_{2}\in S,\ y_{1}\neq y_{2},\ s(y_{1},y_{2})<n_{1}\}>0.

Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in\widetilde{S}
,
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
and put

y_{1}=f(x_{1})
,
y_{2}=f(x_{2})
.
Suppose
s(y_{1},y_{2})=n\geq n_{k}
for some
k\geq 1
.
For each
q\in\mathbb{N}
, set

.
Let
q\geq k
be the integer such that
n_{q}\leq n<n_{q+1}.
We have

c_{j}(x_{1})=c_{j}(x_{2})
for

,
and

c_{n+m_{1}+\cdots+m_{q}+1}(x_{1})=c_{n+1}(y_{1})\neq c_{n+1}(y_{2})=c_{n+m_{1}+\cdots+m_{q}+1}(x_{2}).

Applying Lemma
2.2

By Lemma

Identification and molecular analysis of the Y-specific CpMp gene controlling long male peduncles in papaya
Authors: Yongmei Zhou, Ziqin Pang, Wen Wang, Ray Ming
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Papaya is a fruit crop possessing XY sex chromosomes. The development of long male peduncles (Mp) is pivotal for the transition from gynodioecy to dioecy. The gene controlling peduncle length, CpMp, is one of the four genes driving the evolution of stage 3 sex chromosomes in papaya. Here, we identify a Y-specific SVP paralog, CpSVP-Yp, which is validated as CpMp through comparative genomics and complementation tests. CpMp promotes peduncle elongation by activating CpYUC6, increasing auxin levels, which in turn enhance GA levels and stimulate CpGASA6, driving cell division and elongation. Overexpression of CpGASA6 confirms its role and reveals a feedback loop suppressing IAA biosynthesis. We also identify two upstream regulators: CpTRAB1, a GA-repressed activator, and CpGATA8, a CpMp-induced repressor, forming dual feedback circuits. We show that male trees produce 400× more pollen than hermaphrodites, conferring a fitness advantage. Together with two sex determination genes, CpMp is essential for dioecy and sex chromosome evolution in papaya.

Introduction

Most angiosperms are hermaphroditic with complete flowers, which is beneficial to ensure reproduction. In contrast, only about 6% of angiosperm species are dioecious, with about 21,000 dioecious angiosperms among estimated 350,000 angiosperm species. These dioecious species are widely distributed, representing 43% angiosperm families1. Some important dioecious crops include asparagus, spinach, poplar, kiwi fruit, strawberry, pepper, and papaya2. Diocious species are considered to have evolved from ancestral hermaphroditic species with the evolution of sex chromosomes3,4. The coexistence of monoecious, gynodioecious, androdioecious, and even trioecious species reflected the nature of early stages of sex chromosome evolution, and providing an opportunity to uncover genes and gene networks for sex determination and sex co-segregating traits, such as long male peduncles and YY lethality5.

Papaya is a rare species with three sex types. The wild populations are dioecious, while cultivars are mostly gynodioecious. Classical genetic studies concluded that sex determination in papaya is controlled by one gene with three alleles, designated as M, Mh, and m6,7. The genotypes of the three sex forms are heterozygous Mm for male, Mhm for hermaphtodite, homozygous mm for female. Any combination of the dominant allele MM, MMh, MhMh, is lethal. Genomic analysis of the M locus revealed a pair of nascent sex chromosomes with XX female, XY male, and XYh hermaphrodite. The non-recombining region of the sex chromosomes is 9.8 Mb for the male- or hermaphrodite-specific region of the Y chromosome (MSY or HSY) and 6.5 Mb for its X counterpart8. Cultivated hermaphrodite papaya was selected from a male population in Central America about 4000 years ago9, The DNA sequence similarity of MSY and HSY is estimated at 99.6%9.

Sexual dimorphism among three sex types in papaya includes absent of stamens in female flowers, aborted carpels in male flowers, YY (including YYh and YhYh) lethality, and long peduncles of florescence in male plants. There are four genes in the non-recombining region of the sex chromosomes controlling these four sexual dimorphic traits. The stamen promoting gene (CpSP) has with a dominant allele in the Y and Yh chromosome and a recessive allele in the X chromosome. The carpel surpressing gene (CpCS) has a dominant allele in the Y chromosome, and a recessive allele in the Yh and X chromosomes. The long male peduncle gene (CpMp) has a dominant allele in the Y chromosome and a recessive allele in the Yh and X chromosomes. The YY lethality gene (CpYYL) has a dominant allele in the X chromosome and a recessive allele in the Y and Yh chromosomes. It takes all four genes for sexual dimorphism between male and female plants, but three genes (CpSP, CpCS, and CpYYL) between hermaphrodite and female, and only two genes (CpCS and CpMp) between male and hermaphrodite.

Peduncle length directly affects inflorescence architecture, which is an important determinant of crop productivity, as it ultimately affects the number of flowers and fruits, and can also improve the competitiveness of plant reproduction10. However, little is known about the mechanisms of peduncle elongation in plants. On the other hand, the first step in the evolution of plant chromosome and dioecious reproduction system is recessive or dominant male sterility mutation to produce female flowers. Male flowers produced by recessive or dominant female sterility mutation cannot compete with the pollen produced by stamens in hermaphrodite flowers, unless the amount of pollen produced by male flowers is more than twice that produced by stamens in hermaphrodite flowers11. The mutation of long peduncle of male flower is the second key step in the evolution of plant sex chromosome and dioecious reproduction system. Hence, identification of the gene and elucidation of molecular mechanism of peduncle elongation are crucial for understanding the evolution of papaya sex chromosomes.

In this study, we characterize CpMp, a gene located in the sex-determining region of papaya, as the controller of long male peduncles. We elucidate the molecular mechanism by which CpMp regulates peduncle length and demonstrate how this trait has contributed to sex chromosome evolution and reproductive system in papaya.

Results

CpMp controlled peduncle elongation but not sex determination in papaya

Transcriptome analyses between hermaphridte and male flowers revealed that the CpSVP Y-specific paralog (CpSVP-Yp) was the only differentially expressed gene annotated in the non-recombining region of the Y and Yh chromosomes12. Genomic and gene expression analyses led to the identification of CpSVP-Yp as a candidate gene for male and hermaphrodite differentiation13. The expression pattern of CpSVP-Yp was studied using transgenic plants carrying promoter fragment of CpSVP-Yp (−1762 to 0 bp from ATG) fused to the β-glucuronidase reporter gene (pCpSVP-Yp::GUS). GUS staining was detected from the young shoot, root, and peduncles of pCp SVP-Yp::GUS transgenic plants (Supplementary Fig. 1). Using the distinct region of CpSVP-Yp, we designed a guide RNA as the target to edite CpSVP-Yp in Zhonghuang male plants (ZH-XY) via CRISPR-Cas9, furthermore, we overexpressed CpSVP-Yp in ZH male and Zhongbai (ZB) female plants. The CRISPR-Cas9-mediated knockout mutants of CpSVP-Yp were obtained with an insertion that caused a frameshift mutation upstream the MADS-box. (Supplementary Fig. 2a). For the overexpression experiments, we obtained 36 ZH male independent transgenic lines and 24 ZB female independent transgenic lines. The experiments with gene-editing and overexpression in papaya plants revealed that neither the loss-of-function mutation of CpSVP-Yp in male plants nor the overexpression of CpSVP-Yp under the control of CAMV 35S promoter in female plants resulted in any alteration of their sex types.

Long peduncles were observed in ZB female plants in 35S::CpSVP-Yp (ZB-XX-CpSVP-Yp-OE) overexpression lines when compared to wild-type female papaya, while shorter peduncles were observed in ZH-XY-cpsvp-yp knockout lines when compared to wild-type male papaya (ZH-XY) (Fig. 1a, Supplementary Table 1 and Supplementary Fig. 3). Although the content of CpSVP-Yp protein was increased in the young peduncles of male papaya by overexpressing CpSVP-Yp (Supplementary Fig. 2b), there were no discernible phenotypic alterations observed in these overexpression lines (ZH-XY-CpSVP-Yp-OE). In contrast, ZH-XY-cpsvp-yp knockout lines exhibited reduced peduncle cell number and cell size, which were increased in the peduncles of female overexpression lines of CpSVP-Yp. For instance, knockout of CpSVP-Yp led to a decrease of pith and duct cell number, duct diameter, as well as epidermal cell and cortex cell length in ZH-XY, which were increased in the female overexpression lines of CpSVP-Yp (Fig. 1b–h). These results indicated that CpSVP-Yp is the gene controlling long male peduncles but not sex determination (Fig. 1 and Supplementary Fig. 4). CpMp regulated auxin biosynthesis by modulating CpYUC6 expression

To investigate the mechanisms of how CpMp regulates peduncle elongation, RNA profiles, widely targeted metabolomics, and GAs assays were conducted using shoot apical meristem (SAM) of wildtype, knockout, and overexpression lines. Principal component analysis (PCA) revealed that knocking out CpMp in ZH males and overexpressing CpMp in ZB females resulted in significant alterations to the transcriptome profiles of their respective genetic backgrounds (Supplementary Fig. 5). There were 43 common differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between dioecious Zhonghuang male CpMp knockout lines (ZH-XY-cpmp) and Zhonghuang male CpMp overexpression lines (ZH-XY-CpMp-OE) when compared with Zhonghuang male wild type (ZH-XY), and only 7 common DEGs between Zhonghuang male overexpression lines and Zhonghuang female CpMp overexpression lines (ZB-XX-CpMp-OE) (Supplementary Fig. 6a). The number of up-regulated genes of ZH-XY-cpmp in Gene Ontology (GO) binding term was more than that of down-regulated genes, while the number of down-regulated genes is more than that of up-regulated genes when compared with ZH-XY (Supplementary Fig. 6b–d).

Metabolomic analyses revealed that the content of indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) and its precursor L-Tryptophan (L-Trp) in the SAM of ZH-XY-cpmp was significantly reduced when compared to ZH-XY and ZH-XY-CpMp-OE. While transcriptome profile showed that the expression levels of auxin biosynthetic enzymes tryptophan aminotransferase CpTAA1 and CpYUC3, 6, and 10 was affected by CpMp, CpYUC6 was downregulated in ZH-XY-cpmp and upregulated in ZH-XX-CpMp-OE, which showed a similar trend of IAA content in each genotype (Fig. 2a and Supplementary Fig. 7), indicating that CpMp might have an important function in regulating auxin biosynthesis (Fig. 2a and Supplementary Fig. 7). GA15 and GA4 content was lower in ZH-XY-cpmp SAM than those in ZH-XY and ZH-XY-CpMp-OE, which was increased significantly in gynodioecious ‘Zhongbai’ female CpMp overexpression lines (ZB-XX-CpMp-OE) in comparison to ‘Zhongbai’ ZB-XX females (Fig. 2b). The metabolic pathway involving intermediate products GA9 exhibited a similar pattern of change. The levels of GA51 (a metabolite of GA9) and GA34 (a metabolite of GA4) were significantly reduced in ZH-XY-cpmp SAM compared to ZH-XY. There were also noticeable changes in the expression levels of genes encoding corresponding catalytic enzymes, for example, CpGA20OX1, CpGA20OX-L, and CpGA2OX1-L were upregulated by overexpression of CpMp in female papaya, while CpGA3OX-L was downregulated in the CpMp-overexpressed male papaya (Fig. 2b). Overexpression of CpMp in ZB-XX female plants led to upregulation of various genes, including ent-kaurene synthase CpKS, gibberellin-responsive genes CpGASA1-4, CpSAUR21-22-28-50-68, and CpIAA4, which are known to respond to GA or auxin, and CpEXPA1-8-10, which is regulated by plant hormones up-regulated in overexpression lines ZB-XX-CpMp-OE females. However, overexpression of CpMp in ZH-XY male caused the down-regulation of CpKS and ent-kaurene oxidase CpKO1 in gibberellin precursor synthesis (Supplementary Fig. 7).

In order to identify the target genes of CpMp, Cleavage Under Targets and Tagmentation (CUT&Tag) was performed using the antibody of CpMp. 190 known motif binding to CpMp were identified in the peduncles of ZH-XY males, including WUS1(Homeobox)/colamp-WUS1-DAP-Seq, ATHB6 (Homeobox)/Arabidopsis-HB6-ChIP-Seq, and Knotted (Homeobox)/Corn-KN1-ChIP-Seq (Supplementary Fig. 9). There were 387 target genes whose promoter region was bound by CpMp were identified, including CpSOC1, CpYUC6. Although some GA biosynthetic enzymes were affected by knocking out or overexpressing CpMp (Fig. 2b), there were no promoter region of GA biosynthesis genes bound to CpMp. KEGG analysis showed that the target genes were enriched in pathways such as photosynthesis, metabolic pathway and tryptophan metabolism (Supplementary Fig. 9c). The genes enriched in tryptophan metabolism include CpYUC6. The enriched CpMp-bound peaks in the promoter of CpYUC6 provided direct evidence of in vivo binding of CpMp to CpYUC6 promoter (Fig. 2c), which was further confirmed by the luciferase reporter assay (Supplementary Fig. 9d). Through knocking down CpYUC6 in wild-type male papaya via Virus-Induced Gene Silencing (VIGS), we found that both the IAA level and peduncle length were decreased significantly with the accumulation of L-Trp, which suggested that CpYUC6 was a key gene controlling IAA biosynthesis in papaya (Supplementary Fig. 9e–i).

Treatments with IAA and GA3 led to peduncle elongation

Based on our initial hypothesis that CpMp primarily regulates GA biosynthesis, early experiments included both ZB and ZH female plants and ZB hermaphrodites. However, subsequent findings revealed that IAA acts upstream of GA in the peduncle elongation pathway. Due to limited plant availability, we prioritized the use of hermaphrodite plants for IAA treatment and downstream analyses. The hermaphrodites that were the natural mutants of Mp with a transposon insertion into the first exon of CpMp13, were treated with IAA or GA3, which increased the peduncle length of hermaphrodites by 72% and 440%, and epidermal cell length by 39.2% and 110%, respectively (Fig. 2d-e). Both hormones caused papaya peduncle elongation, with GA3 having much greater effect. IAA treatment altered the expression levels of auxin response genes, including GH3, ARFs, SAUR, and Aux/IAA family members, as well as expansin gene family members (Fig. 2g). The GA4 content of IAA-treated papaya more than doubled (Fig. 2h).

Treatments with both IAA and GA3 significantly promoted the elongation of peduncles in female papaya plants (Supplementary Fig. 10a–c). We found that male papaya peduncles exhibited higher levels of IAA compared to their female counterparts. When hermaphrodite papaya plants were treated with IAA, their GA4 content increased. However, female papaya plants of both ZB and ZH varieties did not exhibit any change in IAA abundance with GA3 treatment (Fig. 2i).

The expression patterns of downstream genes involved in GA biosynthesis and signaling transduction, including GA20OX, DELLAs, GROWTH-REGULATING FACTORs (GRFs), GRF1-INTERACTING FACTORs (GIFs), PHYTOCHROME-INTERACTING FACTORs (PIFs), as well as cell cycle-related genes and expansin genes, were examined by Mfuzz analysis. The results showed that CpGRFs (including CpGRF1, CpGRF4, CpGRF5, CpGRF7) shared a similar expression pattern, which was expressed at low levels in the peduncles of hermaphrodites and females, while at high levels in males and hermaphrodites with treatment of GA3. CpGIF1 and CpGIF3 were upregulated in ZH female peduncles with treatment of GA3. Cell cycle-related genes like CpCELL DIVISION CYCLE 20-1 (CpCDC20-1), CpCYCLIN A1-1 (CpCYCA1-1), and CpCYCB2-1 were upregulated by GA3, which were at high expression levels in male peduncles (Supplementary Fig. 11).

Co-expression network analysis revealed that CpGRF4 exhibited close positive relation with other CpGRFs and negative correlations with CpREPRESSOR OF GA-LIKE 2 (CpRGL2). CpPIFs such as CpPIF1, CpPIF3, and CpPIF7 showed negative relation with CpGA-stimulated Arabidopsis 6 (CpGASA6). Numerous cell cycle-associated genes like CpCYCA1-1, CpCYCA2-1, CpCYCA2-4, CpCYCA3-1, CpCYCA3-4, CpCYCB2-1, CpCYCB1-2, and CpCYCA1-1 exhibited strong positive correlations (Supplementary Fig. 12a). The relationships of CpEXPANSINs (CpEXPs) with the genes above demonstrated that CpEXP10 and CpEXPA8 exhibited positive relations with CpGASA6. Additionally, with the exceptions of CpEXPA4 and CpEXPB3, which showed negative relation with cell cycle-related genes, the other CpEXPs showed positive relations with most cell cycle-related genes (Supplementary Fig. 12b).

CpTRAB1 and CpGATA8 regulated the transcription of CpMp

We next sought to identify upstream regulatory factors for CpMp. Its promoter was isolated for screening by Y1H system using cDNA library of papaya young flowers. Two transcriptional factors CpTRAB1 and CpGATA8 were identified. The former exhibited downregulation with IAA treatment in ZB hermaphrodites and GA3 treatment in ZB females, while CpGATA8 showed no response to the applications of the two hormones (Supplementary Fig. 13). The expression of CpTRAB1 was upregulated in male cpmp lines and downregulated in female CpMp overexpression plants (Fig. 3a), while CpGATA8 showed an opposite expression pattern. Since CpTRAB1 and CpGATA8 showed a possibility in directly regulation of CpMp, they were then overexpressed in male papaya to investigate their roles in CpMp regulation. The results showed that overexpression of CpTRAB1 significantly elevated the transcription activity of CpMp, although it did not elongate male peduncles (CpMp is a male- or Y-specific gene) (Fig. 3c, d). While overexpression of CpGATA8 resulted in down-regulation of CpMp and reduced peduncle growth in male papaya (Fig. 3e, f and Supplementary Table 1). Meanwhile, CpMp expressed at significantly lower level in CpTRAB1-RNAi plants and relative higher level in CpGATA8-RNAi plants when compared to wild male papaya (Supplementary Fig. 14). The proteins coded by the two genes were further confirmed to bind to the O2-site and GATA-box, respectively, in vitro and in vivo (Fig. 3g, h). Additionally, the unlabeled, mutated O2 fragment (O2(mut)), lacking the O2-site sequence, failed to compete with the biotin-labeled O2 probe for binding to CpTRAB1 (Fig. 3h).

To investigate the role of CpGASA6 in peduncle elongation, CpGASA6 was over-expressed in ZB female papaya. We obtained 24 independent over-expression lines, all of which exhibited significantly longer peduncles than that of wildtype female plants (Fig. 4c). Discussion

GA pathway contributed the most to peduncle elongation, because applying exogenous GA caused the peduncle extended twice as long as applying exogenous auxin. CpMp regulated auxin biosynthesis by directly binding to the promoter of CpYUC6, which encodes a crucial enzyme that catalyzes the rate-limiting step in the tryptophan-dependent auxin biosynthesis pathway, converting Indole-3-Pyruvic Acid (IPA) to Indole-3-Acetic Acid (IAA), the primary active auxin. Increased auxin downregulates genes encoding GA 2-oxidases (GA2ox) that deactivate bioactive GAs, leading to accumulation of active GAs in male papaya peduncle14. GA binds to its receptor GID1 and form GA-GID1-DELLA complex, which releases cell cycle and cell expression genes that are suppressed by DELLA proteins, resulting peduncle elongation15,16.

The gene network with CpMp at its core was identified in controlling male peduncle length in papaya. CpMp directly activated CpYUC6 to promote auxin biosynthesis, which is a member of the YUCCA (YUC) family that encodes flavin-containing monooxygenases in the tryptophan-dependent auxin biosynthetic pathway14. Auxin produced through this pathway promoted GA biosynthesis in papaya, increasing GA content in SAM and leading to peduncle elongation (Supplementary Fig. 15). The auxin-mediated promotion of GA biosynthesis has been reported in a range of plant species, including pea and Arabidopsis15,16. Auxin has been shown to regulate GA signaling and biosynthesis by ARFs (AUXIN RESPONSE FACTOR) and IAAs (AUXIN INDUCIBLE/INDOLE-3-ACETIC ACID INDUCIBLE), directly regulating the genes encoding GA metabolic enzymes, including GA20OX, GA3OX, and GA2OX17.

Directly applying auxin to the SAM of female papaya trees induced peduncle elongation and elevated both auxin and GA content. Applying GA to the SAM of female trees induced peduncle elongation and increased GA content, but without affecting auxin levels. Treatment of GA in hermaphrodites elevated activated the expression of CpGRFs, including CpGRF4, which were at high levels in males. Cell cycle-related genes like CpCDC20-1, CpCYCA1-1, and CpCYCB2-1 were upregulated by GA, which were also at high expression levels in male peduncles. These results indicated that elevated GA directly regulates cell division via CpGRFs as demonstrated in rice18.

Gibberellin signal transduction is mainly dependent on DELLA protein, which acts as a negative regulator of GA signaling. GA treatment removed this inhibition by promoting DELLA instability, leading to accumulation of free PIF3 and PIF4, which control hypocotyl cell elongation19,20. Our results showed that CpPIF1, CpPIF3 and CpPIF7 showed negative relation with GA-inducible CpGASA6, which was reported to be regulated through DELLA proteins21,22. While CpGASA6 exhibited positive correlations with CpEXP10 and CpEXPA8 in the gene network, and was upregulated by GA treatment on ZB and ZH females, and at high expression levels in males. Moreover, Overexpression of CpGASA6 in female papaya also promoted peduncle elongation, upregulated CpEXP10 and CpEXPA8, and downregulated auxin biosynthesis related genes. These results indicated cell expansion was primary achieved by the upregulation of CpGASA6-CpEXPs module, which was in competition with CpPIFs.

While CpMp promoted auxin level through the regulation of CpYUC6 expression activity, our findings indicated an additional upstream mechanism in males that modulate L-Trp metabolism. Specifically, the reduction of L-Trp in male cpmp mutants contrasted with the expected accumulation of L-Trp if CpMp solely regulated CpYUC6 in the tryptophan metabolic pathway. This suggested that CpMp may also influence earlier steps in tryptophan biosynthesis or coordinate alternative pathways to maintain auxin homeostasis. In addition, the treatments of auxin and GA could rescue cpmp short-peduncle phenotype; therefore, alternative mechanisms such as residual hormone signaling and compensatory pathways might partially bypass the loss of CpMp, but its absence limits peduncle optimal growth.

Two transcription factors, CpTRAB1 and CpGATA8, could bind to the promotor region of CpMp and were identified with opposite effects on regulating CpMp expression. CpTRAB1 is a bZIP factor, and the ortholog in rice is responsive to the stress hormone abscisic acid (ABA)23. CpTRAB1 bound to the O2-site of CpMp promoter, directly activated the expression of CpMp. The transcription activity of CpTRAB1 was repressed by high levels of IAA and GA, forming a feedback loop. CpGATA8 is a member of the GATA family transcription factors, which are evolutionarily conserved in diverse biological processes, including leaf extension and growth, chloroplast size, flower development, hormone response24,25. GATA family transcription factors specifically recognize or bind the GATA response element (A/T) GATA (A/G) on gene promoters26,27. In papaya, CpGATA8 bound to the GATA box of CpMp promoter to repress the expression of CpMp. However, overexpression of CpMp in male papaya activated the expression of CpGATA8 to form a feedback loop (Supplementary Fig. 15).

Gene duplications drive adaptation and the evolution of new traits, including the development of dioecy and sex chromosomes. The CpMp gene, a Y-specific SVP paralog, repeatedly emerged as a differentially expressed gene between male and hermaphrodite, raising speculation about its role as a sex determination gene suppressing carpel development12,13,28,29. However, a complementation test in papaya demonstrated that this gene promoted peduncle elongation, but does not influence sex determination. Suppressing carpel development and promoting peduncle elongation are two complex biological processes that occur in two different organs and at different developmental stages, making it unlikely that a single gene controls both processes.

The long male peduncles resulted in pollen production in male trees could reach 400 times greater than that of hermaphrodite trees. This far exceeds the critical threshold of two times, sufficient to sweep through local papaya population, eventually eliminating hermaphrodite plants to establish stable dioecy11. This marks a pivotal event in the evolution of dioecy and sex chromosome in papaya. This male specific long peduncle gene CpMp, which was evolved through a gain-of-function mutation from a Y-specific CpSVP paralog, is the third Y-specific gene with a male beneficial function during the process of sex chromosome evolution in papaya. This mutation occurred after the loss-of-function male-sterile mutation in the X chromosome and gain-of-function carpel suppressing mutation in the Y chromosome, but before loss of function mutation causing YY lethality in the Y chromosome.

Dioecious plants require both male and female plants to produce offspring, with reproductive success enhanced by increased pollen production30. Long peduncles provide male papaya trees with a competitive reproductive advantage by enabling the production of numerous flowers and a significantly higher quantity of pollen grains. Male papaya trees can produce more than 400 times the pollen grains of hermaphrodites, a finding that reinforced previous studies that males generates substantially more pollen than hermaphrodites31,32. In the population with the lack of sufficient pollen, hermaphrodites gain an advantage through their ability to fertilize both their own ovules and those of other plants with low fertilization percentage32. Pollen production is a critical determinant of male fitness30, and this trait is significantly impacted by the CpMp gene. Knocking out CpMp led to a reduction of the pollen count per flower and a decrease in peduncle length, ultimately resulting in fewer flowers and reduced male productive success. We also found that greenhouse-grown papaya exhibited shorter peduncles than field-grown counterparts, concomitant with differential expression of CpMp. Therefore, other environmental factors such as low light density and water deficiency may affect peduncle elongation of papaya by regulating CpMp or other mechanisms.

The long male peduncle CpMp gene is located in evolution stratum 1, which evolved about 7 million years ago. In papaya, three genes—two sex determination genes and a long male peduncle gene—are essential for the establishment of dioecy and the initiation of sex chromosome evolution. This process was followed by the first inversion event, which suppressed recombination of the sex determining region encompassing these three genes, marking the beginning of sex chromosome evolution.

Methods

Plasmid construction

To knock out CpMp in male papaya, the gRNA target sequence (CACATACACACAAACAACTAAGG) was selected. According to the instruction of the CRISPR/Cas vector construction kit (Biogle, #BGK012), the paired primers CpMp-up and CpMp-low were used to prepare Oligo dimers. Then the dimers were inserted into the CRISPR/Cas vector. bg-F and CpMp-low were used to confirm E. coli and Agrobacterium tumefaciens positive clones.

To generate transgenic plants, the coding sequence of CpMp was amplified using CpMp-F and CpMp-R. CpTRAB1-F and CpTRAB1-R were used for amplify the coding sequence of CpTRAB1. CpGATA8-F and CpGATA8-R were used for amplify the coding sequence of CpGATA8. And CpGASA6-F and CpGASA6-R were used for amplify the coding sequence of CpGASA6.The amplified cDNA fragments were cloned into PGWD505 by Gateway clone system (Invitrogen). To investigate the expression pattern of Mp, we generated a Mp:GUS expression vector, using Mppro-F and Mppro-R for the amplification of Mp promoter, which was cloned into PGWD533 by Gateway clone system. The verified constructed plasmids were transformed into Agrobacterium strains for papaya transformation.

Papaya transformation

Agrobacterium-mediated transformation method was applied to transform knocking out and overexpression vectors into papaya embryogenic callus. Agrobacterium and calluses were co-cultured at 30 °C on MS solid medium with filter paper for 6–8 h. Then the growth of Agrobacterium was inhibited by putting calluses on MS solid medium with 300 mg/mL cephalosporin, with changing fresh medium daily for one week. The callus then grew on the MS solid medium with 100 mg/mL cephalosporin, 200 mg/mL temetine, and 50 mg/mL hygromycin. The calluses were cultured at 30 °C under a photoperiod 16light: 8dark.

The calluses were picked on the new MS plates with 100 mg/mL temetine and 50 mg/mL hygromycin next day and grew under the same conditions. After 3 weeks, each green callus was selected and cultured separately on a MS solid medium without any antibiotics or hygromycin. About one month later, transgenic somatic embryos were picked into glass bottles with MS solid medium. After another 1–2 months, the young seedings could be planted in soils in a greenhouse at 30 °C under a photoperiod 16light: 8dark.

Verification of transgenic papaya

To verify gene-editing papaya, the paired primers cpmp-F and cpmp-R were used to amplify CpMp specific sequence from the genomic DNA of gene-editing and wild-type male papaya, which was sequenced by company. Then these sequences were aligned by DNAMAN software.

For CpMp overexpression ZH male and ZB female papaya, the paired primers 35S and CpMp-R were used for verification by PCR. Weston blot assay was employed to test whether CpSVP content was increased by overexpression using same amount of total protein and the rabbit polyclonal antibody of CpSVP (1:5000). Anti-beta Actin antibody (1:5000) was used for the examination of beta ACTIN content in the total protein. ImageJ was used to quantification of Weston blot band. The difference between different genotypes was analyzed using t-test. For other transgenic overexpression plants, primers of 35S and reversed primers, which were used for amplify the coding sequence, were used for verification by PCR. For the pCp SVP-Yp::GUS transgenic plants, Mppro-F and Mppro-R were used for the verification. Seedlings and inflorescences of non-transgenic plants and transgenic lines were collected for GUS staining33.

Transcriptome analysis

The shoot apical meristem (SAM) of papaya was isolated from papaya with different genetic background for RNA-seq analysis. The harvested RNA of each sample was reverse-transcribed into mRNA for PCR amplification with oligo primers. Following purification, the PCR products were sequenced on an Illumina MiSeq platform34. RNA sequencing reads were aligned to the papaya reference genome using Hisat235. The mapped reads for each sample were assembled with StringTie, and FeatureCounts was employed to quantify the reads mapped to each gene. The fragments per kilobase of transcript per million mapped reads (FPKM) for each gene were calculated based on gene length and read count36. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were selected on the criteria, having an adjusted P < 0.05 and fold change (FC) ≥ 2 by DESeq2 R4.137. PCA was performed R software to compare the variance of transcription profile between samples. The DEGs of CpMp knock-out mutants and overexpression male papaya were showed by a ternary plot38. The DEGs of CpMp overexpression female papaya were presented using Manhattan plots38. DEGs of each group were used to perform Venn Diagram and GO functional classification.

Widely targeted metabolomic analysis

A widely targeted metabolomics strategy was used to determine the metabolites of papaya SAM. SAM from CpMp knock-out mutants, overexpression lines, GA- treated and CpGASA6 overexpression lines and their genetic background papaya were tested. Each group contained three biological replicates. The samples were collected and frozen immediately in liquid nitrogen, and then placed in a freeze-drying machine for vacuum freeze-drying. 3 g of fresh material of each sample was ground into powder under liquid nitrogen. 50 mg of the powder of each sample was weighed under liquid nitrogen conditions, and dissolved in 1.2 mL 70% methanol extraction solution. The mixed solution was vortexed once every 30 min for 6 times. After centrifugation at 12,900 × g for 3 min, the supernatant was absorbed and filtered with a microporous filter membrane (0.22 μm pore size), and stored in the sample vial for UPLC-MS/MS analysis. The metabolites were detected in three biological replicates by UPLC coupled with tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS). All the metabolites detected by MetWare company were annotated and quantified by the MetWare database using the software Analyst 1.6.3. DESeq2 was used for identification of differential accumulation metabolites (DAMs) based on the thresholds fold change (FC) ≥ 2, and P-value < 0.05. DAMs of each genotype were shown by a ternary plot or Manhattan plots. KyotoEncyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway categories39 were analyzed by online analysis software in Omic share.

GAs content analysis

Fresh papaya SAM materials were harvested and frozen immediately in liquid nitrogen. Each sample consisted of three replicates. A total of 3 g of fresh material of each sample was ground into powder under liquid nitrogen. A total of 50 mg of the powder per sample was weighed under liquid nitrogen conditions. Then, 10 μL internal standard mixture with concentration of 100 ng/mL was added. The measurements were performed by MetWare (Wuhan, China) using ultra-high performance liquid chromatography system (UPLC, ExionLC™AD) and Tandem Mass Spectrometry (MS/MS, QTRAP® 6500+)) (1.8 µm, 2.1 mm * 100 mm)40. One-way ANOVA and Tukey’s multiple range test to determine the significant differences.

IAA and GA3 treatments

Two wild-type verities (Zhongbai and Zhonghuang) were planted in filed. When the plants were beginning flowering, the female papayas of both verities and Zhongbai hermaphrodites were sprayed GA3 (150 μM), and Zhongbai hermaphrodites were sprayed IAA (400 μM) solution on shoot apical meristem once a week for 2 months. The same ethanol concentration was used as a mock control (CK). Each group consisted of three biological replicates.

Mfuzz analysis

To analyze the expression patterns of the genes involved in GA signaling transduction, cell cycle, and cell elongation, we utilized package “mfuzz” and r software, based on fuzzy c-means was employed. We set the number of clusters to c = 15 to retain the soft clustering of GA signaling transduction and cell cycle-related genes.

Network correlation

A pairwise Spearman’s analysis was conducted using Cytoscape version 3.10.2, based on the expression levels of targeted genes in control and GA-treated females and hermaphrodites, and those in male papaya plants. Genes with a P value less than 0.001 were identified as significantly correlated.

Cleavage under targets and tagmentation (CUT&Tag)

A total of 1 g fresh male papaya peducle of each sample was collected and grinded into a homogenate on ice with 1 mL A solution of plant cell nuclear extraction kit (Beijingbaiaolai). Following the next steps of extraction instruction of this kit, the nuclei were harvested and used for CUT&Tag library construction immediately according to the instructions of Hyperactive Universal CUT&Tag Assay Kit for Illumina Kit (Vazyme).

The nuclei of each sample were washed by 100 μL wash buffer twice and incubated with activated 10 μL Concanavalin A coated magnetic beads at room temperature for 10 min in 200 μL clean tubes. The tubes were then placed on a magnetic plate holder, and supernatants were discarded after 3 min. Primary antibody incubation was performed overnight at 4 °C, with a 1:50 dilution of the primary antibody added. Two biology replications added the rabbit polyclonal antibody of CpMp (1:1000), and one tube added antibody buffer as a control. The tubes were then placed on a magnetic plate holder, and supernatants were discarded after 3 min, after which secondary antibodies were added in a 1:50 dilution in Dig-wash. Second antibody (1:1000) incubation was performed for 1 h at room temperature. After using Dig-wash buffer wash the tubes, 2 μL pA/G-Tnp (1:50 diluent diluted with Dig-300 buffer) was added into the tubes. Samples were incubated for 1 h, and then washed with 200 μL Dig-300 buffer twice. The tubes were added 50 μL TTBL solution and incubated at 37 °C for 1 h. samples were added 5 μL Proteinase K, 100 μL buffer L/B and 20 μL DNA extract beads, which was incubated at 55 °C for 30 min to stop the tagmentation and extract DNA. DNA was amplified by PCR using indexes (N5XX and N7XX). Paired-end Illumina sequencing was performed by Berry Genomics. Paired-end reads were aligned using Bowtie2 version 2.5.141, followed by peak calling using Macs242. Then, peak annotation was done by ChIPseeker43. And findMotifsGenome.pl in HOMER was used to find motifs near peak center. KEGG was employed to analyze enriched pathways enrichment using potential target genes of CpMp, whose promoter regions showed the peaks of CpMp binding.

Yeast one-hybrid (Y1H) assay

The DNA fragment containing O2-site and P-box in SVP-Y promoter was amplified using paired primers O2P-F and O2P-R (168 bp). The fragment was ligated into pAbAi vector (Clontech, CA, USA) using In-Fusion Cloning to isolate transcriptional factors for one-hybrid screen in a papaya flower cDNA library according to the manufacturer’s instruction (Clontech, CA, USA). After two candidate transcriptional factors isolated, yeast one-hybrid assay was performed.

Full-length CDSs of the two candidates, CpGATA8 and CpTRAB1, were inserted into the pGADT7 vector respectively to perform theY1H assays. The promoter fragments of GATA-box, O2-site and O2(Mut)were ligated into pAbAi vector using In-Fusion Cloning to generate bait vectors. pAbAi-p53 and pGADT7-p53 were transformed into Y1H cell as positive control, pAbAi empty vector and pGADT7-CpTRAB1, pAbAi empty vector and pGADT7-CpGATA8 were transformed into Y1H cells as negative control, pAbAi-O2 and pGADT7-CpTRAB1, pAbAi-O2(Mut) and pGADT7-CpTRAB1, pAbAi-GATAbox and pGADT7-CpGATA8 were transformed into Y1H cells as experimental groups. Yeast clones were diluted and cultured on SD-Leu plates containing 400 ng/mL AbA and SD-Leu plates without AbA.

Luciferase reporter assay

The coding sequence region of CpMp, CpGATA8, and CpTRAB1 was cloned and constructed into the transient expression vector pGreenII 62-SK. The promoter sequence of CpYUC6 and that of CpMp, SVP-Y GATAbox, O2-site, and the sequence without O2-site O2(mut), were cloned into the LUC reporter gene of the pGreenII 0800-LUC vector. The recombinant plasmid was then transformed into the competent state of Agrobacterium (pSOUP-P19). Positive clones identified by PCR were selected and incubated in 10 mL LB liquid medium containing 50 μg/mL kanamycin and 50 μg/mL rifampicin at 28 °C in a shaker until optical density (OD600) reached 1.0. The bacteria were collected and diluted to OD600 reached 0.6 with tobacco injection buffer. Agrobacterium containing connected GreenII0800-LUC carrier and Agrobacterium containing connected pGreenlI-62-SK were injected into tobacco leaves in a ratio of 1:2. After 48-60 h of dark treatment, the leaves were dipped into D-luciferin solution (0.1 mg/L) for 5 min, and the LB983 Night Owl II Living molecular Imaging system (Berthold Technologies, Bad Wildbad, Germany) was used to observe the fluorescence. Each assay was repeated three times. The significant differences between each two groups were determined by One-way ANOVA and Tukey’s multiple range test.

EMSA assay

The biotin-labeled and compete probes (Supplementary Table 3) were prepared by a primer synthesis company. The fragments of full-length coding sequence of CpTRAB1 and CpGATA8, which were amplified using were cloned into pET28 vector. The constructed vectors were transformed into E. coli DE3. The expressed proteins were harvested using BeyoMag™ His Labeled protein purified agarose magnetic beads. Biotin-labeled probes were generated using the sequences by Sunya company. The probes were incubated with proteins at room temperature for 20 min, and free bound probes were separated in an acrylamide gel with unlabeled probe (10-, and 20-fold excess) and mutant probe (20-fold excess) were used for competition.

VIGS- and RNAi-directed knockdown experiments

Specific primers were designed to amplify 300 bp coding sequence (CDS) fragments of four target genes, namely CpYUC6, CpTRAB1, CpGATA8, and CpGASA6. TRV2 vector44 was digested with EcoRⅠ and BamHⅠ. The fragment of CpYUC6 was inserted into TRV2 by In-Fusion Cloning. The constructed vectors were then transformed into Agrobacterium, which were mixed with the Agrobacterium containing TRV144 with the ration 1:1, and then injected three-month male papaya. About one month later, the silence efficiency was tested by RT-qPCR. And the sense- and antisense strands of CpTRAB1, CpGATA8, and CpGASA6 were cloned into RNAi vectors (pFGC5941), respectively, by In-Fusion Cloning. RNAi vectors were transformed into Agrobacterium, which were infected into 4-week ZH male papaya. The silence efficiency of target genes and relative expression of CpMp were investigated by RT-qPCR at 24–36 h after the injection.

RT-qPCR

Total RNA was extracted using Total RNA Kit I (OMEGA). First-strand cDNA synthesis was performed using PrimeScript® RT reagent Kit and SYBR Premix Ex Taq™ (Takara). qPCR was conducted on each sample with three biological replicates. The papaya housekeeping gene Cp TBP1 (TATA binding protein 1) served as an internal control. The relative expression levels in different samples were calculated with the cycle threshold methodology 2−ΔΔCt.

Cytological analysis

The tissue of fresh mature peduncles from three independent plants was fixed in Formalin-Aceto-Alcohol (FAA) solution (70% ethanol:formaldehyde:acetic acid, 90:5:5). The samples were dehydrated and macerated using an ethanol series [30, 50, 70, 85, 95, and 100% (v/v)], which were then macerated in EMSO overnight. After that, the samples were then embedded in paraffin to form solid tissue blocks. The blocks were sectioned at 70–100 μm. The slides with target tissue are stained in a 1000x saffranine solution and then were selected under a bright-field microscope. The measurement was performed in Adobe Illustrator with the reference of scale bars. The significant differences were determined by One-way ANOVA and Tukey’s multiple range test.

Pollen number investigation

We investigated released pollen number of males (CRW-1, AU9, and ZH accessions) and hermaphrodites (ZB and Red Royal accessions). We observed that hermaphroditic flowers in our study exhibited high variability in stamen number (3–10), while male flowers typically maintain 10 stamens. Therefore, measuring pollen per anther would introduce additional variance due to developmental instability in stamen number. So pollen production per flower was quantified as male reproductive effort per floral unit. All anthers of each mature flower at stage 14 were collected and stained with l mL suspension (5% sugar, 10% Alexander Red solution)45. The suspension was vortexed for 3 min. Then 100 μL was taken to a new tube with 350 μL suspension added. A total of 10 μL of the pollen mixture was diluted into 90 μL of suspension solution in each of three PCR tubes (4500x dilution). After mixing, the pollen in 1 μL of each final suspension was counted. The average pollen count from the three suspensions was then multiplied by 4500 to estimate the total pollen number per flower (Supplementary Table 2). At least from three distinct papaya plants within each accession were examined, ensuring a comprehensive analysis. The significant differences in the pollen quantity of each single flower of each accession were determined by One-way ANOVA and Tukey’s multiple range test.

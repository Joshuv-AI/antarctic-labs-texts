Convergence of gradient flows on knotted curves
Elias Döhrer, Nicolas Freches

Abstract




We prove full convergence of gradient-flows of the arc-length restricted tangent point energies in the Hilbert-case towards critical points.
This is done through a
Łojasiewicz-Simon gradient inequality
for these energies. In order to do so, we prove, that the tangent-point energies are anlytic on the manifold of immersed embeddings and that their Hessian is Fredholm with index zero on the manifold of arc-length parametrized curves.
As a by-product, we also show that the metric on this manifold defined by the first author in
[
11
]
is analytic.

Introduction

In order to find appealing representatives within a knot class,
self-repulsive energies have been investigated over the last forty years.
These energies also prove to be quite useful when modeling and simulating topological effects in physical processes.
This line of research started when Fukuhara introduced the energy of a polygonal knot, motivated by a Coulomb potential (see
[
16
]
).
O’Hara extended this approach and defined a family of repulsive energies in
[
25
]

For a closed, regular, Lipschitz curve
\gamma:{\mathbb{T}}:\mathrel{\mspace{0.936mu}\mathchar 61\relax\mspace{0.936mu}}{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}\cong\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}

\mathscr{O}^{\alpha,p}(\gamma)\coloneqq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}\left(\frac{1}{\mathop{}\lvert\gamma(y)-\gamma(x)\rvert^{\alpha}}-\frac{1}{\text{dist}_{\gamma}(y,x)^{\alpha}}\right)^{p/2}\mathop{}\lvert\gamma^{\prime}(x)\rvert\mathop{}\lvert\gamma^{\prime}(y)\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

where

denotes the distance of
\gamma(y)
and
\gamma(x)
on
\gamma({\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}})

In this article, we consider the

Gonzalez and Maddocks suggested in

The tangent-point energy of a

-immersion

In the above line,

\frac{1}{r_{\mathrm{TP}(\gamma)}(x,y)}\coloneqq 2\frac{\mathop{}\lvert\gamma(y)-\gamma(x)-\frac{\gamma^{\prime}(x)}{\mathop{}\lvert\gamma^{\prime}(x)\rvert}\mathop{}\langle\frac{\gamma^{\prime}(x)}{\mathop{}\lvert\gamma^{\prime}(x)\rvert},\gamma(y)-\gamma(x)\rangle\rvert}{\mathop{}\lvert\gamma(y)-\gamma(x)\rvert^{2}}

A self-avoidance property holds true for every

One can show, that every closed, rectifiable curve with finite length and finite tangent-point energy has to be embedded, see

.
The tangent-point energies can also be generalized for "admissible"

[

This generalization still exhibits self-avoidance and regularizing properties for

In

A by-product of the analysis of the Riemannian structure is the smoothness of the functionals

The definition of a knot energy was, at least partially,
motivated by the idea to disentangle embedded curves by means of gradient flows that respect their topology.
Over the years, a couple of results in this direction appeared.
In
[
6
]
, Blatt considered the
L^{2}
-gradient flow of Ohara’s knot energies and was able to show short- and longtime existence and strong convergence after reparametrization using a Łojasiewicz–Simon gradient inequality. See
[

]
,

[

or

[

L^{2}
-flow of the Möbius energy.
In
[
28
]
a Sobolev gradient flow was considered for the Möbius energy
\mathscr{O}^{2,2}
.
There, short time existence has been established in the space
H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}
.
A similar approach was considered in
[
20
]
for a gradient flow of the integral Menger curvature projected onto the nullspace of the logarithmic strain and thereby controlling the parametrization along the flow.
Short- and longtime existence were established as well as weak subconvergence.
In
[
23
]
a minimizing movement approach for the generalized tangent point energies, O’hara energies and integral Menger curvatures in the Banach case has been investigated.

Lastly in
[
14
]
the gradient flow for
\mathrm{TP}^{\left(p,2\right)}
was investigated on the submanifold of curves parametrized by arclength.
There, short- and longtime existence are established as well as strong subconvergence to a critical point by means of the Palais–Smale condition.
In that regard,
[
14
]
follows a similar approach as
[
24
]
, where comparable methods were used to establish results for the elastic bending energy.

We further follow Okabe and Schrader’s approach in order to strengthen the results of
[

to obtain the full convergence of the aforementioned flow, by means of a Łojasiewicz–Simon gradient inequality.

In order to establish a Łojasiewicz–Simon gradient inequality, it suffices to prove two facts.
First, we show that the tangent-point energy is real analytic on the manifold of immersed
H^{s}
-embeddings. We secondly prove that the Hessian, at a critical point, induces a Fredholm Operator of index zero.
These results yields the Łojasiewicz-Simon inequality and, in combination with the analytic structure of the manifold of
W^{s,2}
-embeddings parametrized by arc length, suffice to show the desired convergence result by standard arguments.

1.1
Preliminaries on the arc-length manifold

In
[
14
, Chapter 3]
the manifold of arc-length parametrized curves in
H^{s}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is investigated.
In the rest of this article, we shall refer to curves parametrized by arc-length by
arc-length curves
.
We give a short review of the results that are of importance for the present paper.

In
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
it was shown that
\mathcal{A}^{s}
is a smooth Riemannian submanifold of
H^{s}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, where the Riemannian structure is given by the standard inner product on
H^{s}
.
Adapting the arguments in
[
24
, Proposition 4.9]
, one obtains analyticity of
\mathcal{A}^{s}
.
For a fixed point
\gamma\in\mathcal{A}^{s}
, the tangent space is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}_{\gamma}\mathcal{A}^{s}\mathrel{\mspace{0.936mu}\mathchar 61\relax\mspace{0.936mu}}\left\{h\in H^{s}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})|\langle\gamma^{\prime}(x),h^{\prime}(x)\rangle\mathrel{\mspace{0.936mu}\mathchar 61\relax\mspace{0.936mu}}0\text{ for all }x\in{\mathbb{T}}\right\}.

Another important manifold is given by
\mathcal{A}^{s}_{0}
.
Constraining the problem onto this manifold prevents the curves from traveling wildly through the ambient space along the gradient flow of geometric energies.
By
[
14
, Theorem 3.2]
this is an analytic Riemannian submanifold of
\mathcal{A}^{s}
.
Its tangent space is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}_{\gamma}\mathcal{A}^{s}_{0}\mathrel{\mspace{0.936mu}\mathchar 61\relax\mspace{0.936mu}}\left\{h\in T_{\gamma}\mathcal{A}^{s}|h(0)\mathrel{\mspace{0.936mu}\mathchar 61\relax\mspace{0.936mu}}0\right\}.

The manifold
\mathcal{A}^{s}_{0}
was introduced in
[
14
]
, in order to prove the Palais–Smale condition for the generalized tangent point energies, as translations on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
are not compact.

Clearly, for every
\gamma\in\mathcal{A}^{s}
we have that
\mathcal{L}(\gamma)\mathrel{\mspace{0.936mu}\mathchar 61\relax\mspace{0.936mu}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\left|\gamma^{\prime}(\tau)\right|\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrel{\mspace{0.936mu}\mathchar 61\relax\mspace{0.936mu}}1
, and critical points of
\mathrm{TP}^{\left(p,2\right)}|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}_{0}}
are also critical for
\mathrm{TP}^{\left(p,2\right)}|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}}
, see
[
14
, Corollary 3.13]
.

The restriction to arclength curves is a natural assumption, at least for the following two reasons:

Firstly, one can show that critical points of the arclength restricted energy are indeed also critical for the length constrained problem (see
[
14
, Theorem 3.10]
).
This relies on the invariance under reparametrization and smoothness of critical points, proven in
[
14
, Theorem 1.8]
.
Thus, by the Lagrange multiplier theorem (see
[
1
, Corollary 3.5.29]
), there exists a constant
\lambda\mathrel{\mspace{0.936mu}\mathchar 61\relax\mspace{0.936mu}}\lambda(\gamma)\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that a critical point satisfies the Euler–Lagrange equation

\displaystyle 0\mathrel{\mspace{0.936mu}\mathchar 61\relax\mspace{0.936mu}}\mathrm{D}\mathrm{TP}^{\left(p,2\right)}_{\gamma}v+\lambda\mathrm{D}\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}(v)\text{ for all }v\in H^{s}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

Secondly, one can show that the set of injective regular curves,
H^{s}_{\mathrm{i,r}}({\mathbb{T}},{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})
, deformation retracts onto
\mathcal{A}^{s}
, such that the sets are homotopy equivalent, see
[
14
, Theorem 3.7]
.
One can even show that this deformation retraction is equivariant, see
[
15
]
.
Thus the two sets are equivariantly homotopy equivalent.
Note that, without any sort of length constraint, one cannot expect to find critical points at all, as the tangent point energy is negatively homogeneous.
So, by restricting the problem to the arclength manifold, we lose neither topological nor geometrical nor analytical information.

1.2
Preliminaries on Łojasiewicz–Simon inequality

The main ingredient in this paper will be a Łojasiewicz–Simon gradient inequality for the restricted energy
\mathrm{TP}^{\left(p,2\right)}|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}}
.
Strong convergence of the flow will be deduced by standard arguments.
There are plenty similar results to be found in the literature in the context of geometric analysis of curves, especially concerning the Euler–Bernoulli bending energy under various constraints (for example in
[
10
]
,
[
29
]
or
[
24
]
).
See also
[
6
]
for an application of Łojasiewicz–Simon inequality for the
L^{2}
-gradient flow for O’hara energies.

While the inequality itself usually appears in more or less the same form, there exist many theorems on when it necessarily holds.
This started with the original work by Łojasiewicz (see
[
36
]
), followed by the first generalization to the Hilbert setting due to Simon (see
[
31
]
) and the generalizations of Chill (see
[
9
]
), Huang (see
[
19
]
) and Feehan-Maridakies (see
[
12
]
).
We employ the version due to Feehan and Maridakies.

Theorem 1.6
(
[
12
, Theorem 1]
).

Let
X\subset X^{*}
be a continuous embedding of a Banach space into its dual space. Let
U\subset X
be an open subset, let
\mathcal{E}:U\to{\mathbb{R}}
be an analytic function, and let
x_{\infty}
be a critical point of
\mathcal{E}
, such that
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(x_{\infty})\mathrel{\mspace{0.936mu}\mathchar 61\relax\mspace{0.936mu}}0
. Assume that
\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}(x_{\infty}):X\to X^{*}
is a Fredholm operator of index zero.

Then there exist constants
Z\in(0,\infty),\sigma\in(0,1]
and
\theta\in\left[\frac{1}{2}.1\right)
such that the following holds true.
If
x\in U
satisfies

\left\|x-x_{\infty}\right\|_{X}\mathrel{\mspace{0.153mu}\mathchar 60\relax\mspace{0.153mu}}\sigma,

then

\left\|\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(x)\right\|_{X^{*}}\geq Z\left|\mathcal{E}(x)-\mathcal{E}(x_{\infty})\right|^{\theta}.

Note that this result a priori only holds in Banach spaces. However, since the result is only local in nature, one can transfer it to Riemannian submanifolds of Hilbert spaces under certain prerequisites, as in
[
24
, Proposition 4.13]
.
We outline our setting and give a sketch of the proof. The precise and complete proof can be found in
Section

4
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space,
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathcal{H}
an open subset,
\mathcal{E}:\mathcal{O}\to{\mathbb{R}}
an analytic function,
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{O}
an analytic Riemannian submanifold of
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}\mathrel{\mspace{0.936mu}\mathchar 61\relax\mspace{0.936mu}}\mathcal{E}|_{\mathcal{M}}
be the restricted energy.
For a critical point
x_{\infty}
of
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
take an analytic chart
\phi:\mathcal{U}\to V
of
\mathcal{M}
containing
x_{\infty}
. Then the localized energy
E:V\to{\mathbb{R}},v\mapsto\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(\phi^{-1}(v))
is analytic and satisfies the suppositions of
Theorem

1.6
if and only if
(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}})^{\prime\prime}(x_{\infty})
is Fredholm with index zero.
This is the key difficulty, as calculating the Hessian of a manifold constrained function is in general far from trivial. Okabe and Schrader resolved this obstruction by considering a modified energy instead of the classical elastic energy, see
[
24
, Proof of Prop. 4.12]
.
Luckily, we found a convenient workaround in the context of geometric energies on spaces of closed immersed curves based on
[
14
, Theorem 3.10]
that would also work for other geometric energies on curves, such as the elastic energy, integral Menger curvature or O’Hara energies.
In fact, the Fredholm property of
(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}})^{\prime\prime}(x_{\infty})
can be shown in a way completely analogous to the case of
\mathrm{TP}^{\left(p,2\right)}
, but analyticity seems to be much easier for
\mathrm{TP}^{\left(p,2\right)}
compared to
\mathrm{intM}^{p,2}
or
\mathscr{O}^{\alpha,1}
.
For the elastic energy, analyticity and the Fredholm property has been shown in
[
24
]
for a slightly more general manifold.
In case of the arclength constraint our adapted tools would work as well. In fact, the Fredholm property becomes trivial using our workaround and the regularity results from
[
14
, Theorem 4.4]
.

Note that the choice of
\mathcal{H}\mathrel{\mspace{0.936mu}\mathchar 61\relax\mspace{0.936mu}}H^{s}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
\mathcal{O}\mathrel{\mspace{0.936mu}\mathchar 61\relax\mspace{0.936mu}}H^{s}_{\mathrm{i,r}}({\mathbb{T}},{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})
is very convenient for us, since we are interested in an ODE-type Sobolev gradient flow in the corresponding energy space.

For applications to geometric
L^{2}
-gradient flows our approach might not be suitable.
Instead, one might consider the refined constrained Łojasiewicz–Simon gradient inequality shown in
[
30
, Theorem 1.4]
.
Note that Rupp’s approach only relies on the Fredholm property of
\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}(x_{\infty})
which avoids the difficulties with the constrained Hessian
\left(\mathcal{E}|_{\mathcal{M}}\right)^{\prime\prime}(x_{\infty})
in an elegant way.
However, the suppositions on the operator
\mathcal{G}
in Rupp’s result make an application in our case unfeasible since our constraints are not finite dimensional.

2
Analyticity

In this section we show, that
\mathrm{TP}^{\left(p,2\right)}
is analytic on on

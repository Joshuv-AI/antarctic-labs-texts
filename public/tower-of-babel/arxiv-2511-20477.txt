Rotational effects in quark stars: comparing different models
Adamu Issifu, Andreas Konstantinou, Franciele M. da Silva, Tobias Frederico

Abstract
We investigate the rotational properties of self-bound strange quark stars using two representative quark matter equations of state (EOS): the vector MIT bag model and the density-dependent quark mass (DDQM) model. Through general-relativistic calculations of uniformly rotating sequences, we analyze their mass–radius relations, moments of inertia, quadrupole moments, surface redshifts, Keplerian frequencies, and energy components. A central result of this work is the full decomposition of the stellar energy budget in rotating strange stars, separating gravitational, internal, rotational, and binding energy contributions. Rotation amplifies the intrinsic EOS differences: the MIT model supports more massive ( M max ≳ 3.3 ​ M ⊙ M_{\max}\gtrsim 3.3\,M_{\odot} ) compact stars with larger moments of inertia and greater resistance to deformation, while the DDQM model produces larger radii, less massive stars limited by mass-shedding at lower frequencies. Combined measurements of mass, radius, and frequency can thus break the EOS degeneracy; massive, rapidly rotating pulsars favors MIT-like EOS, whereas larger radii in canonical stars point to a DDQM-like model. These rotational observables, soon to be tightly constrained by NICER and next-generation gravitational-wave detectors, offer a means to test the existence and composition of self-bound quark matter in compact stars.



I
Introduction

The theoretical proposition that strange quark matter may constitute the true ground state of nuclear matter
Witten 1984
provides a compelling motivation for studying strange stars, compact objects composed of up, down, and strange quarks
Issifu et al. 2025a
;
Issifu et al. 2024
;
da Silva et al. 2024
. A definitive identification of such objects requires confronting theoretical predictions with astrophysical observations, many of which probe the properties of rapidly rotating compact stars. Early investigations of rotating strange stars primarily employed the MIT bag model EOS
Lattimer et al. 1990
;
Stergioulas et al. 1999
;
Zdunik et al. 2000
, while subsequent studies introduced alternative descriptions, including density-dependent EOSs that serve as precursors to the more sophisticated models used here
Bombaci et al. 2000
;
Gondek-Rosińska et al. 2000
.

Initial models of rotating strange stars, often based on simplified EOSs neglecting quark masses and interactions, predicted Keplerian frequencies comparable to or even lower than those of neutron stars
Lattimer et al. 1990
;
Zdunik and Haensel 1990
;
Prakash et al. 1990
, a consequence of their relatively low maximum masses. However, modern EOSs that incorporate quark masses and interactions, including those employed in this study, yield substantially higher maximum masses and rotational frequencies
Gondek-Rosińska et al. 2000
;
Bhattacharyya et al. 2016
. The rotational properties of these self-bound configurations differ markedly from their neutron star counterparts. For instance, uniform rotation can enhance the maximum mass of a strange star by over
40\%

Gourgoulhon et al. 1999
;
Gondek-Rosińska et al. 2000
;
Zhou et al. 2017
, compared with a typical
\sim 20\%
increase for neutron stars, while differential rotation can support even more massive configurations
Szkudlarek et al. 2012
;
Szkudlarek et al. 2019
;
Zhou et al. 2019
. Furthermore, the ratio of rotational kinetic to gravitational binding energy,
T/|W|
, can be significantly larger for strange stars
Gourgoulhon et al. 1999
;
Gondek-Rosińska et al. 2000
, indicating an increased susceptibility to non-axisymmetric instabilities, though triaxial evolution may ultimately limit their attainable spin rates
Zhou 2017
;
Zhou et al. 2018
.

A variety of observational signatures may distinguish strange stars from neutron stars, including quasi-periodic oscillations associated with orbital and epicyclic frequencies
Gondek-Rosińska et al. 2001
;
Gondek-Rosińska and Bulik 2005
;
Gondek-Rosińska et al. 2014
, properties of the innermost stable circular orbit
Stergioulas et al. 1999
;
Zdunik et al. 2001
;
Bhattacharyya et al. 2001
;
Gondek-Rosińska et al. 2001
, and universal relations linking the mass, radius, moment of inertia, and quadrupole moment for static configurations
Cheng and Harko 2000
;
Doneva et al. 2014
;
Chen and Lin 2023
. The strong bulk viscosity of strange quark matter suppresses the r-mode instability, suggesting that the detection of a sub-millisecond pulsar would constitute compelling evidence for strange stars
Madsen 1998
, a possibility further explored in subsequent r-mode analyses
Madsen 2000
;
Andersson et al. 2002
;
Gondek-Rosińska et al. 2003
.
Although a larger
T/|W|
ratio could imply enhanced gravitational-wave emission, simulations indicate that triaxially rotating quark stars may not be substantially more efficient continuous-wave sources than neutron stars
Zhou et al. 2018
;
Zhou et al. 2021
.

In this work, we investigate the rotational effects on the structural properties of bare, self-bound strange stars composed of
u,\,d
and
s
quark species without a crust
Melrose et al. 2006
;
Kettner et al. 1995
. We perform the first fully relativistic comparison of two modern and widely used quark matter EOSs: the DDQM model and the vector MIT bag model. Using general-relativistic sequences of uniformly rotating configurations, we compute and contrast key stellar observables, including the mass–radius relation, moment of inertia, quadrupole moment, surface redshift, and, importantly, a complete decomposition of the stellar energy budget, providing complementary knowledge to advance observational missions. We present the first full energy decomposition for rotating quark stars, providing new and independent constraints on EOS stiffness and self-binding.

Our goal is to determine how these rotational observables, now increasingly accessible through NICER and future gravitational-wave measurements, can be used to distinguish between competing quark matter descriptions. This analysis reveals clear EOS-dependent trends that link microphysical interactions to the macroscopic structure and stability of rotating strange stars. For rotating strange stars with a crust, we refer the reader to
Glendenning and Weber 1992
;
Zdunik et al. 2001
;
Haensel et al. 2009
.

Model Formalism

This section is divided into three subsections to comprehensively discuss the microscopic and macroscopic physics underlying the study. The EOS parameterizations used here come from Ref.

II.1
Density-Dependent Quark Mass Model

The DDQM model describes strange quark matter by incorporating quark interactions through a density-dependent quark mass
Xia et al. 2014
;
da Silva et al. 2024
;
Issifu et al. 2025b
:

m_{i}(n_{B})=m_{0i}+\frac{D}{n_{B}^{1/3}}+Cn_{B}^{1/3},

(1)

where
m_{0i}
is the current quark mass, and
C
,
D
are model parameters. To ensure thermodynamic consistency when
m_{i}(n_{B})
depends on density, we introduce the effective chemical potentials
\mu_{i}^{*}
, such that the free-energy density takes the form
Backes et al. 2021
;
Xia et al. 2014
:

f=\Omega_{0}(\{\mu_{i}^{*}\},\{m_{i}(n_{B})\})+\sum_{i}\mu_{i}^{*}n_{i},

(2)

with the quark thermodynamic potential

\Omega_{0}=-\sum_{i}\frac{\gamma_{i}}{24\pi^{2}}\left[\mu_{i}^{*}p_{Fi}\left(p_{Fi}^{2}-\tfrac{3}{2}m_{i}^{2}\right)+\tfrac{3}{2}m_{i}^{4}\ln\!\left(\frac{\mu_{i}^{*}+p_{Fi}}{m_{i}}\right)\right],

(3)

where
\gamma_{i}=6
and

p_{Fi}=\sqrt{\mu_{i}^{*2}-m_{i}^{2}}

(4)

is the Fermi momentum. The number density is then

n_{i}=\frac{\gamma_{i}p_{Fi}^{3}}{6\pi^{2}}.

(5)

The EOS is obtained by imposing
\beta
-equilibrium,

\mu_{u}^{*}+\mu_{e}=\mu_{d}^{*}=\mu_{s}^{*},

(6)

charge neutrality,

\frac{2}{3}n_{u}-\frac{1}{3}n_{d}-\frac{1}{3}n_{s}-n_{e}=0,

(7)

and baryon number conservation,

n_{B}=\frac{n_{u}+n_{d}+n_{s}}{3}.

(8)

The energy density and pressure are given by

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle=\Omega_{0}-\sum_{i}\mu_{i}^{*}\frac{\partial\Omega_{0}}{\partial\mu_{i}^{*}},

(9)

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=-\Omega_{0}+\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial\Omega_{0}}{\partial m_{j}}\,n_{i}\frac{\partial m_{j}}{\partial n_{i}}.

(10)

II.2
Vector MIT Bag Model

The vector MIT bag model extends the original MIT framework
Chodos et al. 1974
by incorporating repulsive vector interactions in analogy with Quantum Hadrodynamics
Serot and Walecka 1997
, including a Dirac-sea self-interaction term
Furnstahl et al. 1997
. The full Lagrangian is
Lopes et al. 2021a
;
Lopes et al. 2021b
:

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{MIT}+\mathcal{L}_{V}+\mathcal{L}_{DIRAC},

(11)

with

\mathcal{L}_{MIT}=\sum_{i}\left\{\bar{\psi}_{i}\left(i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-m_{0i}\right)\psi_{i}-B\right\}\Theta(\bar{\psi}_{i}\psi_{i}),

(12)

\mathcal{L}_{V}=\sum_{i}\left\{\bar{\psi}_{i}g_{iV}\gamma^{\mu}V_{\mu}\psi_{i}-\frac{1}{2}m_{V}^{2}V^{\mu}V_{\mu}\right\}\Theta(\bar{\psi}_{i}\psi_{i}),

(13)

\mathcal{L}_{DIRAC}=b_{4}\,\frac{(g^{2}V_{\mu}V^{\mu})^{2}}{4}.

(14)

Here,
\psi_{i}
is the quark field,
B
is the bag pressure,
m_{V}
is the vector meson mass,
g_{iV}
is the quark–vector coupling, and
g\equiv g_{uV}
. The step function
\Theta(\bar{\psi}_{i}\psi_{i})
enforces confinement inside the bag.

Under the mean-field approximation
Menezes 2021
;
Serot and Walecka 1997
, the quark single-particle energy and vector mean field satisfy

\displaystyle E_{i}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{p_{Fi}^{2}+m_{i}^{2}}+g_{iV}V_{0},

(15)

\displaystyle gV_{0}+\left(\frac{g}{m_{V}}\right)^{2}b_{4}\,(gV_{0})^{3}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{g}{m_{V}}\right)^{2}\sum_{i}g_{iV}n_{i},

(16)

where
p_{Fi}
is the Fermi momentum and
n_{i}
is the quark number density.

The energy density and pressure are

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{i}}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{p_{Fi}}E_{i}\,p^{2}\,dp,

(17)

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}+B-\frac{1}{2}m_{V}^{2}V_{0}^{2}-b_{4}\,\frac{(g^{2}V_{0}^{2})^{2}}{4},

(18)

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\mu_{i}n_{i}-\varepsilon,

(19)

with degeneracy
\gamma_{i}=6
,
G_{V}\equiv(g/m_{V})^{2}
, and coupling ratio
g_{sV}/g_{uV}=0.4

Lopes et al. 2021a
, note that the model assumes
g_{dV}=g_{uV}
.

II.3
Rotating Neutron Stars

In this work we use the

rns
code
Stergioulas and Friedman 1995
to model the rigidly rotating strange stars.
The code solves the Einstein’s field equations for a stationary, and axisymmetric space-time

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-e^{\tilde{\gamma}+\tilde{\rho}}dt^{2}+e^{2\tilde{\alpha}}(dr^{2}+r^{2}d\theta^{2})

(20)

\displaystyle+e^{\tilde{\gamma}-\tilde{\rho}}r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta(d\phi-\tilde{\omega}dt)^{2},

where
\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\alpha},\tilde{\omega}
are metric potentials that depend on the radial coordinate
r
and the polar angle
\theta
.

The total gravitational mass,
M
, the baryon mass,
M_{0}
, the angular momentum, J, the circumferential radius at the equator,
R_{e}
, and the polar redshift,
Z_{p}
are given by equations (B1), (B2), (B4), (B6), (B7) of
Cook et al. 1994
, respectively.

The moment of inertia will be given by I = J/
\Omega
, the kinetic energy is defined as
T_{kin}=\frac{J\Omega}{2}
, and the binding energy is obtained by
E_{bind}=M-M_{0}
. Here,
\Omega=2\pi\nu
with
\nu
being the spin frequency in Hz.

The gravitational potential energy is given by
|W|=M_{p}+T_{kin}-M
, where
M_{p}
is the proper mass and is defined in equation (65) of
Komatsu et al. 1989
, and the internal energy is given by
U=M_{p}-M_{0}
.

The mass quadrupole moment is evaluated following the asymptotic expansion of the metric functions at large distances.
In this approach, the
rns
code extracts the coefficient
M_{2}
from the
r^{-3}P_{2}(\cos\theta)
term in the expansion of the
\rho
-potential
Laarakkers and Poisson 1999a
;
Pappas and Apostolatos 2012
.
The physical quadrupole moment
Q
is then obtained through

Q=M_{2}-\frac{4}{3}\left(\frac{1}{4}+b\right)M^{3},

(21)

where
b
is a dimensionless coefficient determined from the asymptotic behavior of the metric potentials (see Eq. (9) in
Laarakkers and Poisson 1999a
), and
M_{2}
corresponds to the coefficient of the
l=2
(quadrupole) term in the expansion (see Section 2 of
Pappas and Apostolatos 2012
).

In both cases, rotation shifts the sequences toward higher masses and larger radii due to centrifugal support. However, the magnitude of this shift depends strongly on the underlying quark-matter EOS. The vector MIT bag model, being stiffer at high densities because of the repulsive vector interaction

;

At the same time, the enhanced pressure leads to more compact configurations, yielding smaller radii for a given mass compared to the DDQM case. In contrast, the relatively softer DDQM EOS produces stars with larger radii and lower maximum masses under similar rotational conditions.

These results highlight that rotation amplifies the intrinsic EOS differences: the vector MIT bag model predicts more massive and compact rotating strange stars, while DDQM favors less compact, lower-mass compact objects. Consequently, simultaneous measurements of mass, radius, and spin in rapidly rotating compact stars, particularly from X-ray timing and future gravitational-wave observations, could provide a viable means of discriminating between these two quark-matter scenarios. Clear differences arise from the contrasting stiffness of the quark-matter EOSs. The relatively stiffer vector MIT bag model supports significantly higher masses at all frequencies, reaching its maximum close to the mass-shedding boundary. The dominance of moderate
\nu/\nu_{K}
values indicates that stability is ultimately limited by general-relativistic collapse rather than equatorial breakup

. The smaller radii of these compact configurations also enable higher Keplerian limits, allowing for stable rotation above
\sim

. In contrast, the DDQM EOS yields a narrower stability domain, with lower maximum masses and reduced rotational capacity. Larger stellar radii reduce the Keplerian frequency, forcing DDQM stars to evolve close to the mass-shedding threshold across most of the sequence. As reflected by the prevalence of high
\nu/\nu_{K}
values, configurations with
\nu\gtrsim 1000
Hz rapidly approach the breakup limit, restricting access to the extreme mass–frequency regime allowed by the MIT model.

These distinct behaviors have important observational implications. The detection of a very massive (
M\gtrsim 2.5\,M_{\odot}
) and rapidly rotating (
\nu\gtrsim 1000

The internal energy

In contrast, the DDQM model yields more compressible, less strongly bound configurations

;
da Silva et al. 2024

In summary, the analysis of the star’s energy budget reveals a critical difference: matter in the MIT model stars acts as though it’s fundamentally ‘glued together’ (self-bound), while matter in the DDQM model is held up more by its own pressure against gravity. This provides a clear, physical criterion to distinguish the two models.

Rotational effects are crucial for precise pulsar timing measurements and play a significant role for structural deformation, neutron star evolution and several observed phenomena

;

;
da Silva et al. 2025a

III.1
Observational Relevance

The fundamental question of whether strange quark matter is soft and compressible, or stiff and strongly self-bound, can be answered using observable differences in the resulting stellar structure. Their contrasting mass–radius relations can be tested with X-ray pulse profile modeling from NICER
Riley et al. 2019
;
Miller et al. 2019
;
Riley et al. 2021
;
Miller et al. 2021b
and eXTP
Watts et al. 2019
;
Zhang et al. 2019
: a larger radius at canonical mass (
R_{1.4}\gtrsim 14~\mathrm{km}
) would favor the DDQM model, while a very massive and compact pulsar (
M\gtrsim 2.3
–
2.5\,M_{\odot}
with
R\lesssim 13~\mathrm{km}
), such as PSR J0740+6620, would support the vector MIT model. Multiple measurements across different masses will be necessary to break EOS degeneracies. The moment of inertia offers a second diagnostic. A
\sim 10\%
measurement for PSR J0737–3039A can distinguish between the two models:
I_{1.4}\gtrsim 2.16\times 10^{45}~\mathrm{g\,cm^{2}}
favors DDQM, and
I_{1.4}\lesssim 1.94\times 10^{45}~\mathrm{g\,cm^{2}}
favors the vector MIT model.

Rotational limits provide another discriminator. The vector MIT model supports Keplerian frequencies up to
\sim 1450~\mathrm{Hz}
and allows stable sub-millisecond pulsars
Madsen 2000
;
Madsen 1998
, whereas DDQM predicts lower mass-shedding limits. Thus, detecting a sub-millisecond pulsar would strongly support the vector MIT EOS, while continued non-detection (assuming controlled selection effects) is more consistent with DDQM. The energy decomposition reinforces this interpretation. The strongly negative internal energy
U
in the vector MIT model reflects deep self-binding and leads to efficient r-mode damping
Madsen 1998
. This allows rapid rotation to persist, while the softer DDQM matter is less effective at suppressing r-mode–driven spin-down.

Additional constraints arise from gravitational-wave
Abbott et al. 2017
and surface redshift
Lattimer and Prakash 2007
;
Cottam et al. 2002
measurements. DDQM stars, being more deformable, predict larger quadrupole moments at fixed spin. Meanwhile, detecting a surface redshift
Z_{p}\gtrsim 0.7
in a massive, rotating pulsar would strongly support the vector MIT description.

In summary, a coordinated analysis of data from NICER (mass-radius), binary pulsar timing (moment of inertia), gravitational-wave observatories (quadrupole moment), and all-sky pulsar surveys (maximum spin frequency) will provide a coherent, multi-pronged strategy for discriminating between DDQM and vector MIT strange star models and ultimately testing the strange matter hypothesis itself.

IV
Final Remarks and Conclusions

This work presents a comparative analysis of rotating strange stars using two representative EOSs: the DDQM model and the vector MIT bag model. Our results (Tabs.
1
–
2
; Figs.
1
–
6
) show that rotation magnifies the intrinsic differences between these EOSs, offering a clear means of distinction between them. The MIT model, dominated by repulsive vector interactions, supports more massive and compact stars, reaching up to
3.3\,M_{\odot}
compared with
\sim 2.8\,M_{\odot}
for the DDQM model. The vector MIT bag model configurations also sustain higher Keplerian frequencies (
\gtrsim 1450~\mathrm{Hz}
), whereas the softer DDQM configuration, with larger radii, evolves close to their mass-shedding limit.

The diagnostic trends depend on mass: at the maximum mass, the MIT model yields higher moments of inertia and quadrupole moments, while at the canonical
1.4\,M_{\odot}
mass, the more extended DDQM stars produce larger moments of inertia. Thus, massive, fast-spinning pulsars favor MIT-like EOS, whereas large-radius or high-inertia
1.4\,M_{\odot}
pulsars point to a DDQM-like EOS. EOS stiffness also leaves measurable relativistic imprints. The polar redshift peaks at
Z_{p}\sim 0.88
for MIT stars versus
\sim 0.59
for DDQM, and the latter’s greater deformability may enhance their gravitational-wave emission from rotational instabilities. The energy decomposition further underpins this distinction, revealing a more pronounced self-bound character in the MIT EOS. Collectively, our results demonstrate that rotational data from multi-messenger astronomy will be important in determining the existence and properties of self-bound strange quark matter.

The EOS-dependent rotational signatures identified in this work are directly testable with multimessenger observations. NICER mass–radius measurements of PSR J0030+0451 and PSR J0740+6620 can constrain stellar compactness and distinguish the more compact MIT sequences from the larger-radius DDQM configurations (as shown in
fig.

1
), while an upcoming moment-of-inertia measurement for PSR J0737–3039A offers a complementary probe, since the DDQM model predicts larger
I
at
1.4\,M_{\odot}
whereas the MIT model yields larger
I
only near the maximum mass (as shown in Tabs.
1
and
2
). Additionally, differences in quadrupole moments and rotational deformabilities may modify the I–Love–Q relations, providing gravitational-wave diagnostics for Advanced LIGO+, Virgo/KAGRA, and future detectors such as the Einstein Telescope or Cosmic Explorer.

Finally, a joint analysis of mass, radius, spin frequency, moment of inertia, and quadrupole moment provides a robust framework for constraining the quark matter EOS. These multimessenger observations will critically test whether dense matter is better described by the stiff, repulsion-driven vector MIT bag model or the relatively softer, density-dependent quark mass behavior of the DDQM model.

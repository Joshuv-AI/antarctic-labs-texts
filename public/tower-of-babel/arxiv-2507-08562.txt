Relation between bicrossed products and crossed extensions of fusion categories
Monique Müller, Héctor Martín Peña Pollastri, Julia Plavnik

Abstract
. We show that all crossed extensions defined by Natale can be recovered as duals of bicrossed products of fusion categories. As an application, we prove that any exact factorization between a pointed fusion category vec G \operatorname{vec}_{G} and a fusion category 𝒞 \mathcal{C} can be realized as a bicrossed product vec G ⋈ 𝒞 \operatorname{vec}_{G}\bowtie\mathcal{C} .



1.
Introduction

The study of fusion categories has become of crucial importance in recent years, with the new advances in quantum computing via the Kitaev model
[
Kit06
]
as a way to produce error-resistant quantum computers, for the description of topological states of matter, and in general as a way of formalizing 2-dimensional conformal quantum field theory as described in
[
MS89
]
. Then it is of great importance to find new examples and to understand how the known examples can be combined into other ones. Our emphasis is on concrete examples, where the associativity and all the maps involving the categorical structure can be displayed as a collection of matrices, the
6j
-symbols, satisfying coherence conditions. Pursuing this goal, in
[
MPP24
]
, the authors defined the bicrossed product of fusion categories as a way to construct and understand exact factorizations. This construction produces all possible Grothendieck rings for an exact factorization as indicated in
[
MPP24
, Theorem 3.14]
, and a follow-up question is whether every exact factorization is equivalent to a bicrossed product in the categorical setting as it happens for groups and Hopf algebras; see
[
MPP24
, Question 3]
. The following theorem provides an answer when one of the factors is the category of finite-dimensional
G
-graded vector spaces for a finite group
G
, denoted as
\operatorname{vec}_{G}
.

Theorem

(Theorem
3.2
)
.

If
\mathcal{D}=\operatorname{vec}_{G}\bullet\mathcal{C}
is an exact factorization of fusion categories, then
\mathcal{D}
is tensor equivalent to a bicrossed product
\operatorname{vec}_{G}\bowtie\mathcal{C}
.

This result is achieved by combining two previous results concerning exact factorizations
[
Gel17
]
and extensions (in the sense of exact sequences as defined in
[
EG17
]
). In
[
BG23
, Corollary 5.6]
, the authors show that there exists a one-to-one correspondence between certain exact factorizations and extensions of fusion categories; on the other hand, a big family of extensions of fusion categories, the so-called abelian extensions, were classified in
[
Nat21
, Theorem 1.1]
as crossed extensions of fusion categories. The present work shows that the correspondence in
[
BG23
, Corollary 5.6]
associates the crossed extension from
[
Nat21
, Theorem 1.1]
to bicrossed products, which is the content of Theorem
3.1
, and the main result of the present article follows from this.

This article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the preliminaries and notation needed to state and prove the results, including an overview of bicrossed products, exact factorizations, extensions (exact sequences), and crossed extensions. In Section
3
, the main results (Theorem
3.1
and
3.2
) are proven.

2.
Preliminaries and notation

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. All vector spaces are considered to be finite-dimensional. All categories considered in this work are abelian
\Bbbk
-linear and semisimple; we refer the reader to
[

]
for further details. A

\mathcal{C}
is a finite semisimple rigid tensor category with product
\otimes
, associativity
\alpha
and unital maps
\ell
,
r
, and the unit object
\mathbf{1}
is simple; see
[
EGNO15
, Definition 4.1.1]
. The set of equivalence classes of simple objects is denoted by

.

Given a category
\mathcal{M}
and a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, we say that
\mathcal{M}
is a (right)

if there exist a bifunctor

and natural isomorphisms

m^{0}_{M}\colon M\bar{\otimes}\mathbf{1}\to M
, for all

and
C,C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
, satisfying some conditions, see
[
EGNO15
, Definition 7.1.1]
.

Functors are required to preserve the relevant structure. Specifically, a functor
\mathbf{F}\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
between categories is always
\Bbbk
-linear and exact. If
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
are fusion categories, the functor
\mathbf{F}=(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{F}^{2}_{X,Y},\mathbf{F}^{0})
is also required to be tensor, where
\mathbf{F}^{2}_{X,Y}\colon\mathbf{F}(X)\otimes\mathbf{F}(Y)\to\mathbf{F}(X\otimes Y)
and
\mathbf{F}^{0}\colon\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{1})\to\mathbf{1}
, see
[
EGNO15
, Definition 2.4.1]
.

A functor
(\mathbf{F},s)\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
between
\mathcal{C}
-module categories for a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is a pair consisting of a
\Bbbk
-linear functor
\mathbf{F}\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
together with natural isomorphisms

satisfying the conditions given in
[
EGNO15
, Definition 7.2.1]
.

Given an indecomposable
\mathcal{C}
-module category
\mathcal{M}
, the dual category
\mathcal{C}^{*}_{\mathcal{M}}
of
\mathcal{C}
with respect to
\mathcal{M}
is the (strict) fusion category
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{M})
of
\mathcal{C}
-module endofunctors
(\mathbf{F},s):\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
, with tensor product given by composition of functors and duals by adjunctions. Associated to a tensor functor
\mathbf{T}\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{C}
between fusion categories
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{C}
there is an induced
\mathcal{D}
-module structure on
\mathcal{M}
by
\mathbf{T}
and we have a tensor functor

that maps
(\mathbf{F},s)
to

, where

, for every

and

.

Module categories over
\operatorname{vec}_{G}

Let
G
be a finite group. We denote by
\operatorname{vec}_{G}
the category of finite-dimensional
G

, where
g

g\otimes h=gh
, and the associativity isomorphisms are the ones from vector spaces.

There is an intimate relation between

\mathcal{M}
and
\operatorname{vec}_{G}
-module category structures on
\mathcal{M}
. Indeed, let
\mathcal{M}
be a module category over
\operatorname{vec}_{G}
, then the following lemma gives a structure of a
\Bbbk
-linear
G
-category on
\mathcal{M}
. We omit the proof because it is straightforward.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a (right)
\operatorname{vec}_{G}
-module category. Then there is an induced (right)
G
-action on
\mathcal{M}
defined as follows:

(i)

the functor
-\bar{\triangleleft}g
is given by
-\bar{\otimes}g
for each
g\in G
,

(ii)

the natural isomorphism
(\mathbf{R}^{2}_{g,h})_{M}\colon(M\bar{\triangleleft}g)\bar{\triangleleft}h\to M\bar{\triangleleft}gh
is given by
m_{M,g,h}^{-1}
,

(iii)

the isomorphism
\mathbf{R}^{0}_{M}\colon M\to M\bar{\triangleleft}e
is given by
(m^{0}_{M})^{-1}
.

Furthermore, a
\operatorname{vec}_{G}
-module functor
(\mathbf{F},s)
induces a
G
-functor
(\mathbf{F},\theta^{g})
, with
\theta^{g}_{M}=(s_{M,g})^{-1}
.

Remark 2.5
.

The definitions of exact factorization and extensions can be done in the finite tensor category context (without the semisimplicity hypothesis). Both definitions are stable under the semisimplicity condition, that is, if both
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{C}
are semisimple then the possible factorizations and extensions are semisimple too, see
[
BG23
, Corollary 3.13]
and
[
EG17
, Theorem 3.8]
.

Remark 2.6
.

Notice that in the definition of
\operatorname{ExFac}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{C})
and
\operatorname{Ext}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{C},\mathcal{M})
the categories involved in the label are defined up to tensor (or module) equivalence. That is, if
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
are tensor equivalent to
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{C}
respectively, then
\operatorname{ExFac}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{C})=\operatorname{ExFac}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
, as the corresponding equivalences induce equivalence of exact factorizations, similarly with extensions.

2.3.
Basak-Gelaki Correspondence

In the work of Basak and Gelaki
[
BG23
, Theorem 5.1]
, the authors stated a correspondence between exact factorizations and extensions of tensor categories. They showed that this correspondence is one-to-one in some specific cases, see
[

There is an equivalence of extensions between

\displaystyle\operatorname{Rep}G\xhookrightarrow{\mathbf{\iota_{1}}}\mathcal{C}^{(G,\Gamma)}\xrightarrow{\mathbf{F}_{1}}\mathcal{C}

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{Rep}G\xhookrightarrow{\mathbf{\iota}_{2}}\mathcal{B}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}\xrightarrow{\mathbf{F}_{2}}\mathcal{C}.

Proof.

We first describe the functors that appear in the statement. Let us fix the tensor functor,

\displaystyle\operatorname{can}_{\mathcal{C}}\colon\operatorname{vec}\to\mathcal{C},\operatorname{can}_{\mathcal{C}}(V)=\oplus_{i=1}^{\dim V}\mathbf{1},

and then we have that

\displaystyle\iota_{1}(V,\{\rho(g)\}_{g\in G})

\displaystyle=(\operatorname{can}_{\mathcal{C}}(V),\{\operatorname{can}_{\mathcal{C}}(\rho(g))(\oplus_{i=1}^{\dim V}\eta_{0}^{g})\}_{g\in G}).

The functor
\mathbf{F}_{1}:\mathcal{C}^{(G,\Gamma)}\to\mathcal{C}
is the forgetful fuctor.

We define

\displaystyle\iota_{2}(V,\rho)

\displaystyle=(F_{V},s),

with the functor
F_{V}(C)=\operatorname{can}_{\mathcal{C}}(V)\otimes C
and
s_{M,g\bowtie C}
is given by

\displaystyle({\eta_{\operatorname{can}_{\mathcal{C}}(V),M}^{g}}^{-1}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{C})((\operatorname{can}_{\mathcal{C}}(\rho(g)^{-1})(\oplus_{i=1}^{\dim V}{\eta_{0}^{|M|\blacktriangleright g}}^{-1})\otimes\operatorname{id}_{M\triangleleft g}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{C})\alpha^{-1}_{\operatorname{can}_{\mathcal{C}}(V),M\triangleleft g,C},

for all
M,C\in\mathcal{C},g\in G
.

The functor
\mathbf{F}_{2}
is given by
\mathbf{F}_{2}=\mathbf{H}\circ\mathbf{I}\circ\mathbf{G}
, where each functor is defined as follows. Let
\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\otimes}}
be the
\mathcal{C}
-module category
\mathcal{C}
with the action restricted from the action of
\mathcal{B}
on
\mathcal{C}
, more precisely,
M\widetilde{\otimes}X=M\triangleleft e\otimes X
and
\widetilde{m}_{M,X,Y}
is given by
m_{M,e\bowtie X,e\bowtie Y}(\operatorname{id}_{M\triangleleft e}\otimes(\mathbf{R}^{0}_{X})^{-1}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{Y})
, for all
M,X\in\mathcal{C}
. The tensor functor
\mathbf{G}:\mathcal{B}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathcal{C}^{*}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\widetilde{\otimes}}}
is given by
\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{F},s)=(\mathbf{F},\overline{s})
, with
\overline{s}_{M,X}=s_{M,e\bowtie C}
, for all
M,X\in\mathcal{C}
, and the monoidal structure of
\mathbf{G}
is the identity.
Notice that
\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\otimes}}\simeq\mathcal{C}
as
\mathcal{C}
-module categories, where
\mathcal{C}
is viewed as a
\mathcal{C}
-module category with the regular action. Therefore, there is a tensor equivalence

explicitly given as

,

, for all
M,X\in\mathcal{C}
, and the monoidal structure of
\mathbf{I}
is the identity.
The tensor functor
\mathbf{H}:\mathcal{C}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathcal{C}
is the canonical functor given by
\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{F},t)=\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{1})
with monoidal structure
\mathbf{H}^{2}_{(\mathbf{F},t),(\mathbf{F}^{\prime},t^{\prime})}=\mathbf{F}(\ell_{\mathbf{F}^{\prime}(\mathbf{1})})t^{-1}_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{F}^{\prime}(\mathbf{1})}
.

The good definition of

is the same as the good definition of the functor

Proof of Theorem
3.1

Given the matched pair given in Proposition
3.3
, then we have a bicrossed product of fusion categories
\mathcal{B}=\operatorname{vec}_{G}\bowtie\mathcal{C}
; see
[
MPP24
, Theorem 4.6]
. By
[
MPP24
, Prop. 4.7]
, we have canonical inclusion functors
\operatorname{vec}_{G}\xhookrightarrow{\nu}\mathcal{B}\xhookleftarrow{\kappa}\mathcal{C}
. Now, as described in Subsection
2.3
, we apply
\psi_{\operatorname{vec}_{G},\operatorname{vec}}
to the exact factorization
\mathcal{B}=\operatorname{vec}_{G}\bowtie\mathcal{C}
to get an exact sequence

\displaystyle\operatorname{Rep}G\simeq{\operatorname{vec}_{G}}^{*}_{\operatorname{vec}}\xhookrightarrow{\iota_{{\operatorname{vec}_{G}}^{*}_{\operatorname{vec}}}}\mathcal{B}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}\xrightarrow{\kappa^{*}}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}\simeq\mathcal{C}.

It is straightforward to see that this sequence is equivalent to the sequence

\displaystyle\operatorname{Rep}G\xhookrightarrow{\mathbf{\iota}_{2}}\mathcal{B}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}\xrightarrow{\mathbf{F}_{2}}\mathcal{C},

defined in Lemma
3.6
. By the same lemma, this sequence is equivalent to the sequence

\displaystyle\operatorname{Rep}G\xhookrightarrow{\mathbf{\iota_{1}}}\mathcal{C}^{(G,\Gamma)}\xrightarrow{\mathbf{F}_{1}}\mathcal{C}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
3.2

Let
\operatorname{vec}_{G}\xhookrightarrow{}\mathcal{D}\xhookleftarrow{}\mathcal{C}
be an exact factorization of fusion categories. Then, by
[
BG23
, Theorem 5.1]
, there exists an exact sequence of fusion categories

(10)

\operatorname{Rep}G\xhookrightarrow{}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{C}}}\xrightarrow{}\mathcal{C}

associated to
\mathcal{D}
. It follows from
[
Nat21
, Theorem 1.1]
that Sequence (
10
) is equivalent to

(11)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Rep}G\xhookrightarrow{\iota_{1}}\mathcal{C}^{(G,\Gamma)}\xrightarrow{\mathbf{F}_{1}}\mathcal{C},

where
\mathcal{C}^{(G,\Gamma)}
is a crossed extension, and the functors
\iota_{1}
and
\mathbf{F}_{1}
are described in the proof of Lemma
3.6
. By Theorem
3.1
, Sequence (
11
) is equivalent to the image of
\psi_{\operatorname{vec}_{G},\operatorname{vec}}
on to a bicrossed product
\operatorname{vec}_{G}\bowtie\mathcal{C}
. By
[
EG17
, Corollary 5.6]
, the map
\psi_{\operatorname{vec}_{G},\operatorname{vec}}
is one-to-one, hence
\mathcal{D}
is equivalent to
\operatorname{vec}_{G}\bowtie\mathcal{C}
.
∎

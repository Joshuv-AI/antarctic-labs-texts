Distinction of the Steinberg representation and the dual group of a symmetric space
Guy Shtotland

Abstract
We study the distinction of the Steinberg representation of a split reductive group G G with respect to a split symmetric subgroup H ⊂ G H\subset G . We relate this distinction problem to a problem about the existence of a non-zero harmonic function on a certain hyper-graph related to X = G / H X=G/H . We verify the relative local Langlands conjecture for the Steinberg representation by showing that over a p p -adic field the Steinberg representation is H H -distinguished if and only if its Langlands parameter factors through the dual group of X X .



1
Introduction

A fundamental problem in relative representation theory of a reductive group
G
with
a symmetric subgroup
H
, is the study of
H
-distinguished
G
representations.
A representation
\pi
of
G
is called
H
-distinguished if

Hom_{H}(\pi,\mathbb{C})\neq 0
. In this paper, we study the distinction of a
special representation of
G
called the Steinberg representation. We denote
this representation by
St
. The Steinberg representation is defined both over
a finite field and over a
p
-adic field.

Our first result is a necessary condition for the distinction of
St
. The
condition is that the symmetric space
X=G/H
has to be quasi-split (see
Definition
1.1
).

We also give a sufficient condition for distinction of
St
under the further
assumption of
H
being split. We prove that in the case of a finite field,
X

being quasi-split is also a sufficient condition.

In the case of a
p
-adic field, this is not a sufficient condition. In the
example given by
G=GL_{2n+1}(F)
and
H=GL_{n}(F)\times GL_{n+1}(F)
for a
p
-adic
field
F
, the space
X=G/H
is quasi-split but
St
is not
H

distinguished. We prove that this example is essentially the unique such example
(see Theorem
1.5
). We give an interpretation
of this result using the dual group of
X
(see Theorem
1.7
).

1.1
Main results

Let
F
be a field that is either a non archimedean local field of residual
characteristic not 2, or a finite field of characteristic not 2.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive group split over
F
and let

G=\mathbf{G}(F)
.
Let
\sigma:\mathbf{G}\rightarrow\mathbf{G}
be an algebraic involution
defined over
F
. We denote by
\mathbf{H}
the fixed points of
\sigma
, and
by
H=\mathbf{H}(F)
the
F
-points of
\mathbf{H}
. We call

X=G/H
a symmetric space.

We discuss both the case of the Steinberg representation over a finite field
and over a non archimedean local field.

In the case of
F
being a finite field, there is a unique irreducible
representation of
G
called the Steinberg representation (introduced in

[
30
]
) denoted by
St
. In the case of
F
being a local field, for any
unramified character
\chi:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
there is a
corresponding Steinberg representation denoted by
St_{\chi}
(see

[
25
]
).

An important notion that appears repeatedly in this work is the notion of

X
being quasi-split.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that
X
is quasi-split if
G
has a Borel subgroup
B
such that

\sigma(B)\cap B
is a maximal torus of
B
.

In the case of a finite field we prove the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
F
be a finite field. Under the
assumption that both
G
and
H
are split over
F
,
St
is
H
distinguished
if and only if
X
is quasi-split.

Now, we discuss the case of
F
being a non archimedean local field. Let
\mathcal{O}

be the ring of integers of

. We assume that both
G
and
\sigma
are
defined over
\mathcal{O}
.

In this case,

being quasi-split is not a sufficient condition for the
distinction of the Steinberg representation. This can be seen from the
following proposition.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
F
be a non archimedean local field. Let
G=GL_{2n+1}(F)
,

H=GL_{n}(F)\times GL_{n+1}(F)
, and
X=G/H
. We have the following:

1.

X
is quasi-split.

2.

For any unramified character

\chi:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, the
G
representation
St_{\chi}
is not

H
-distinguished.

From now on we assume that
H
splits over
F
, we give a sufficient
condition for the distinction of the Steinberg representation. For this we need
more notations.

Let
\mathbf{Z}=Z(\mathbf{G})
be the center of
\mathbf{G}
. We write

\mathbf{G}/\mathbf{Z}
as a product of adjoint simple groups

\mathbf{G}/\mathbf{Z}=G_{1}\times...\times G_{m}
.

Let

be the maximal compact

We use the extended Bruhat Tits building of

Let

be the length function on

. Denote by

colors of chambers in

, see Proposition 2.15 of

[

]

We show that in the extended Bruhat Tits building of

which is

\sigma
stable (i.e.
\sigma(\mathcal{C}_{0})=\mathcal{C}_{0})

\Omega_{H,\mathcal{C}_{0}}=\{\omega(h)|h\in H,h\mathcal{C}_{0}=\mathcal{C}_{0}\}

The following character was introduced in the context of the distinction of the
Steinberg representation by Broussous in

]
.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
\chi_{0}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be the quadratic unramified
character defined by

\chi_{0}(g)=sgn(\omega(g))
, where

is the sign of the
permutation defined by
\omega(g)
on the vertices of a chamber.

Now we can state a sufficient condition for the distinction of the Steinberg

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
F
be a non archimedean local field, assume that both
G
and
H
are

. Let
\chi:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}

The representation

2.

Write

.

.

1.

Notice that for

2.

In the case of

Conjecturally, the

In a different work

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
F
be a non archimedean local field, assume that
G
and

are

.
The representation

is

1.

\iota(\mathcal{U}_{X})
intersects the open

1.2
Methods of proof

We introduce two hyper-graphs related to
X
. We relate the
distinction problem of the Steinberg representation to problems about these
hyper-graphs.

Let
B
be a Borel subgroup of
G
, and let
\Delta
be the set of simple roots
corresponding to
B
. For any
\alpha\in\Delta
, let
P_{\alpha}
be the standard
parabolic subgroup corresponding to
\alpha
.

Definition 1.8
.

We define a hyper-graph
\Gamma_{F}(X)
whose set of vertices,
B_{F}(X)
, is
the set of
B
orbits on
X
over
F
. The hyper-edges of
\Gamma_{F}(X)
are
labeled by
\Delta
. For any
\alpha\in\Delta
and
b\in B_{F}(X)
, we have the
hyper-edge of Borel orbits

E_{b,\alpha}=\{b^{\prime}\in B_{F}(X)|P_{\alpha}b^{\prime}=P_{\alpha}b\}.

For the case of
F
being a non archimedean local field, we define one more
hyper-graph, denoted by
\Gamma_{aff,F}(X)
. Let
I
be an Iwahori subgroup of

G
. Let
\tilde{\Delta}
be a set of simple reflections in the affine Weyl
group
W_{aff}
corresponding to
I
.

For any
\alpha\in\tilde{\Delta}
, let
I_{\alpha}
be the parahoric group
containing
I
and corresponding to
\alpha
.

Definition 1.9
.

We define a hyper-graph
\Gamma_{aff,F}(X)
whose set of vertices,

I_{F}(X)
, is the set of
I
orbits on
X
. The hyper-edges of

\Gamma_{aff,F}(X)
are labeled by
\tilde{\Delta}
. For any

\alpha\in\tilde{\Delta}
and
x\in I_{F}(X)
an
I
orbit, we have the
hyper-edge of
I
orbits
E_{x,\alpha}=\{x^{\prime}\in I_{F}(X)|I_{\alpha}x^{\prime}=I_{\alpha}x\}
.

The group
W_{aff}
acts on the vertices of the hyper-graph
\Gamma_{aff,F}(X)

(see Theorem 1.1 of
[
26
]
). In particular
\Omega\subset W_{aff}
acts on
the vertices of
\Gamma_{aff,F}(X)
. The group
\Omega
also acts on the
hyper-edges of
\Gamma_{aff,F}(X)
.

Denote by
\Gamma^{0}_{aff,F}(X)
the quotient of
\Gamma_{aff,F}(X)
by the
action of
\Omega
. The vertices of
\Gamma^{0}_{aff,F}(X)
can be identified
with the
H
orbits of chambers in
\mathcal{B}
.

Definition 1.10
.

Let
\Gamma
be a hyper-graph, a function
f:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

is called harmonic if for every hyper-edge
E
, we have
\sum_{e\in E}f(e)=0
.
Denote by
\mathcal{H}(\Gamma)
the space of all harmonic functions on

\Gamma
.

The hyper-graphs we defined are related to the problem of determining the distinction of
the Steinberg representation by the following proposition.

Proposition 1.11
.

•

For a finite field
F
we have

dim(Hom_{H}(St,1))=dim\mathcal{H}(\Gamma_{F}(X))
.

•

For a non archimedean local field
F
we have

dim(Hom_{H}(St_{\chi_{0}},1))=dim\mathcal{H}(\Gamma^{0}_{aff,F}(X))
.

Therefore, in order to show that the Steinberg representation is distinguished
it is enough to construct a non-zero harmonic functions on these hyper-graphs.
We provide a method to construct such functions.

First, we work over a field that is an algebraic closure of a finite field,
we denote this field by
\bar{F}
. We assume that
X
is quasi-split and
construct a non-zero harmonic function on
\Gamma_{\bar{F}}(X)
.

Next, we pull back the harmonic function on
\Gamma_{\bar{F}}(X)
to a harmonic
function on
\Gamma_{F}(X)
. For this step we use the assumption that
H
is
split over
F
.

Lastly, we move to non archimedean local fields. Under conditions 1 and 2 of
Theorem
1.5
we extend our construction of a harmonic function
in the finite field case to a construction of a non-zero harmonic functions on
\Gamma^{0}_{aff,F}(X)
.

1.3
Related works

The problem of distinction of the Steinberg representation over a local field
was studied extensively in the Galois case. By the Galois case we mean the case
where
E/F
is a quadratic extension,
G=\mathbf{G}(E)
, and
H=\mathbf{G}(F)
.
The work on this case began with the paper of Prasad

[
21
]
. For specific results,
see, for example,
[
19
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
10
]
and

[
7
]
. All of the results mentioned in these works are in some sense
orthogonal to ours as they deal with non split groups but only in the Galois
case.

Interestingly, harmonic functions are also used in
[
7
]
in order to
analyze the distinction of the Steinberg representation, although on different
graphs.

We also mention the work
[
33
]

that also deals with the distinction of the Steinberg representation with
respect to symmetric subgroups. In this work they
prove an upper bound on the dimension of
Hom_{H}(St,\chi)
and compute it for
the case of
G
being
GL_{n}
and
H
being the special orthogonal group. Both
our methods and their methods are heavily based on the Bruhat Tits building. In
fact, many of the combinatorial arguments related to the Bruhat Tits building
used in
[
26
]
also appear in

[
33
]
. However, the main
results of our work and the work

[
33
]
are quite different.
Most of our work is dedicated to proving the distinction of the Steinberg
representation under some assumption (i.e. showing that
Hom_{H}(St,\chi)\neq 0
), while most of the results of

[
33
]
are about bounding the
dimension of
Hom_{H}(St,\chi)
from above.
The dual group of
X
does not play a role in
[
33
]
.
In some aspects, their results are more general than ours as they do not
assume that the groups
G
and
H
are split.

3
Distinction of the Steinberg representations and harmonic
functions

In this Section we relate the questions about the distinction of the
Steinberg representation to questions about the existence of a non-zero
harmonic function on the hyper-graphs
\Gamma_{F}(X)
and
\Gamma_{aff,F}(X)

defined in Definitions
1.8
and
1.9

respectively.

We begin with the case of a finite field.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
F
be a finite field, we have

dim(Hom_{H}(St,1))=dim\mathcal{H}(\Gamma_{F}(X))
.

Proof.

By Frobenius reciprocity we have

Hom_{H}(St,1)=Hom_{G}(St,ind^{G}_{H}(1))=Hom_{G}(St,\mathbb{C}[X])
. Here,

\mathbb{C}[X]
is the
G
representation of functions on
X
.

Let
B
be a Borel subgroup of
G
. The representation
St
is irreducible and has a
B
fixed
vector. We can write
\mathbb{C}[X]
as a direct sum of its sub-representation
generated by its
B
fixed vectors and some complement,

\mathbb{C}[X]=G\mathbb{C}[X]^{B}\oplus\pi
. Any map from
St
to

\mathbb{C}[X]
lands in
G\mathbb{C}[X]^{B}
, so

Hom_{G}(St,\mathbb{C}[X])=Hom_{G}(St,G\mathbb{C}[X]^{B})
.

Both the Steinberg representation and
G\mathbb{C}[X]^{B}
are self dual
representations of
G
, therefore

Hom_{G}(St,G\mathbb{C}[X]^{B})=Hom_{G}(G\mathbb{C}[X]^{B},St)
.

We consider
\mathbb{C}[X]^{B}
and
St^{B}
as modules of the Hecke algebra

H(G,B)
of
B
bi-invariant functions on
G
. We have

Hom_{G}(G\mathbb{C}[X]^{B},St)=Hom_{H(G,B)}(\mathbb{C}[X]^{B},St^{B})
.

We claim that
Hom_{H(G,B)}(\mathbb{C}[X]^{B},St^{B})=\mathcal{H}(\Gamma_{F}(X))
.

The module
St^{B}
is one dimensional, in fact it is the sign representation
of
H(G,B)
. Any element of
Hom_{H(G,B)}(\mathbb{C}[X]^{B},St^{B})
is a
function on the
B
orbits on
X
.

Let
f\in Hom_{H(G,B)}(\mathbb{C}[X]^{B},St^{B})
, for every

s_{\alpha}\in\Delta
, a simple reflection, the element

T_{\alpha}=1_{Bs_{\alpha}B}\in H(G,B)
acts as
-1
on
f
, i.e.
T_{\alpha}f=-f
.
Thus, for every Borel orbit

b\in B\backslash X
we have
((T_{s}+1)f)(b)=0
. This is exactly the condition

\sum_{b^{\prime}\in E_{b,\alpha}}f(b^{\prime})=0
. Therefore
f
is a harmonic function on

\Gamma_{F}(X)
. The same argument also works in the opposite direction.
∎

Now we move to the local field case. The proof is almost the same.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
F
be a non archimedean local field, we have

dim(Hom_{H}(St_{\chi_{0}},1))=dim\mathcal{H}(\Gamma^{0}_{aff,F}(X))
.

Proof.

By Frobenius reciprocity we have

Hom_{H}(St_{\chi_{0}},1)=Hom_{G}(St_{\chi_{0}},Ind^{G}_{H}(1))=Hom_{G}(St_{\chi_{0}},C^{\infty}(X))

Here,
C^{\infty}(X)
is the
G
representation of locally constant functions on

X
.

The representation
St_{\chi_{0}}
is self dual, therefore

Hom_{G}(St_{\chi_{0}},C^{\infty}(X))=Hom_{G}(S(X),St_{\chi_{0}})
. Here,
S(X)
is the

G
representation of locally constant compactly supported functions on
X
.

Let
I
be an Iwahori subgroup of
G
.
St_{\chi_{0}}
is irreducible and is
generated by its
I
fixed vectors. The subgroup
I
splits the category of
smooth representations of
G
(see
[
1
]
), and the category of
representations generated by their
I
fixed vectors is equivalent to the
category of
\mathcal{H}(G,I)
modules (see
[
4
]
,
[
20
]
).

Therefore
Hom_{G}(S(X),St_{\chi_{0}})=Hom_{H(G,I)}(S(X)^{I},St_{\chi_{0}}^{I})
.

We claim that
Hom_{H(G,I)}(S(X)^{I},St_{\chi_{0}}^{I})=\mathcal{H}(\Gamma^{0}_{aff,F}(X))
.
The
H(G,I)
module

In this Section we prove that

Remark 4.1
.

The main result of this Section follows from known results. It is known that
St
is
generic, i.e. has a Whittaker model (see for example
[
8
]
). By

[

a generic representation can be distinguished only if

We provide a direct proof.

Let

\mathcal{B}
be the extended Bruhat Tits building of

The involution
\sigma
acts on
\mathcal{B}
and that an apartment,

We introduce a partial order on the
\sigma
stable apartments.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}

\sigma

.

We define a partial order

\sigma

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}

\sigma
stable apartments such that

and such that their intersection is of codimension 1 inside them. Then

|rank(\mathcal{A}_{1})-rank(\mathcal{A}_{2})|\leq 1
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{A}_{1}<\mathcal{A}_{2}
if and only if the
action of
\sigma
on
\mathcal{A}_{1}
preserves the two half planes defined by
\mathcal{A}_{1}\cap\mathcal{A}_{2}\subset\mathcal{A}_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
\sigma
stable apartment,

is equal to the
co-dimension of the subgroup of translations on the affine space
\mathcal{A}
that
commute with the action of
\sigma
on
\mathcal{A}
(see Proposition 5.7 in
[
26
]
).

Now, let
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}
be two
\sigma
stable apartments whose intersection
is of codimension 1. Denote by

the dimension of the group of translation on

\mathcal{A}_{1}\cap\mathcal{A}_{2}
that commute with
\sigma
.

Denote

. Then, for

,

is
either

or

. If the translations on

that are in a direction perpendicular to

.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a chamber in

that contains a facet of codimension 1 that

(as an affine hyper plane). The translations perpendicular

and

By Remark 7.10 of

that contain the facet

\sigma

\sigma(\mathcal{C})

Definition 4.5

For any torus

Proposition 4.7

(
[

]
)
.

By Proposition

Theorem 4.8
.

If

\mathcal{H}(\Gamma^{1}_{aff,F}(X))=0

Let
T
be a maximal torus of

\alpha:T\rightarrow F^{\times}
be a

T
. We denote by

\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}\subset\mathbf{G}

[
3

In order to prove the above Theorem we need the following proposition.

Proposition 4.9
.

Assume that

is not quasi-split. For every maximal split
\sigma
stable torus

there is a root
\alpha:T\rightarrow F^{\times}
such that for every

u_{\alpha}(x)
and
u_{-\alpha}(x)

.

Proof.

We prove this by induction on the partial order defined in Definition

4.3
, regarded as a partial order on the set of maximal split
\sigma
stable tori.
We begin with the maximal tori, that are
\sigma
stable
split tori

T^{-}
is a maximal
\sigma
split torus.

By our assumption,

is not quasi-split. Therefore,

is not a

M
is a Levi subgroup of

(see for example

[
3
]
). Let
\alpha^{\vee}:F^{\times}\rightarrow T
be a

is contained in

. We claim that

\sigma(\alpha^{\vee})=\alpha^{\vee}
. For

and

\alpha(t)=1
for

. Thus,
\sigma(\alpha)
and
\alpha
are equal on

and they are

\sigma

\alpha=\sigma(\alpha)
and

\sigma(\alpha^{\vee})=\alpha^{\vee}
.

We claim that for every

, we have
u_{\alpha}(x)\in H
. We know that

\sigma(u_{\alpha}(x))=u_{\alpha}(x)
or
\sigma(u_{\alpha}(x))=u_{\alpha}(-x)

as we showed that
\alpha
is fixed by
\sigma
. We assume by contradiction
that for every

\sigma(u_{\alpha}(x))=u_{\alpha}(-x)

\sigma(u_{-\alpha}(x))=u_{-\alpha}(-x)

Let

contains a
\sigma
split torus larger than

and this is a
contradiction. We have

so clearly
y^{-1}Ty
contains
T^{-}
. It is
enough to show that for every

,
\sigma
acts as inverse on

y^{-1}\alpha^{\vee}(x)y
.

We need to show that

\sigma(y^{-1}\alpha^{\vee}(x^{-1})y)=y^{-1}\alpha^{\vee}(x)y

We have
\sigma(y)=u_{\alpha}(-\frac{1}{2})u_{-\alpha}(1)
, so we need to show that

u_{-\alpha}(-1)u_{\alpha}(\frac{1}{2})\alpha^{\vee}(x^{-1})u_{\alpha}(-\frac{1}{2})u_{-\alpha}(1)=u_{-\alpha}(1)u_{\alpha}(-\frac{1}{2})\alpha^{\vee}(x)u_{\alpha}(\frac{1}{2})u_{-\alpha}(-1)

This is equivalent to showing

u_{\alpha}(\frac{1}{2})u_{-\alpha}(-2)u_{\alpha}(\frac{1}{2})\alpha^{\vee}(x^{-1})u_{\alpha}(-\frac{1}{2})u_{-\alpha}(2)u_{\alpha}(-\frac{1}{2})=\alpha^{\vee}(x)

Using

u_{\alpha}(\frac{1}{2})u_{-\alpha}(-2)u_{\alpha}(\frac{1}{2})=n_{\alpha}\alpha^{\vee}(\frac{1}{2})

where

[

[

]

n_{\alpha}\alpha^{\vee}(x^{-1})n_{\alpha}^{-1}=\alpha^{\vee}(x)

Thus, we proved the proposition for tori that are maximal with respect to
our partial order.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}
be two apartments such that
\mathcal{A}_{1}<\mathcal{A}_{2}
and
\mathcal{A}_{1}\cap\mathcal{A}_{2}
is of
codimension 1 inside
\mathcal{A}_{2}
. Let
T_{1}

be the maximal split tori
corresponding to
\mathcal{A}_{1}

\mathcal{A}_{2}
respectively. We may assume that we know the

, and prove it for
T_{1}
.

Let
\alpha
be a root of
T_{2}
such that for every

,

u_{\alpha}(x),u_{-\alpha}(x)\in H
. Let
\beta
be a root of
T_{2}
that is
constant on
\mathcal{A}_{1}\cap\mathcal{A}_{2}
. There is an element
g
in the group generated by

such that
g\mathcal{A}_{2}=\mathcal{A}_{1}
. It is enough to
show that

commutes with

, as the conjugation of

by

is a root of

, and if

commutes with

g^{-1}u_{\pm\alpha}(x)g=u_{\pm\alpha}(x)\in H

By Proposition

,
\sigma
acts by
-1
on
\beta

\sigma

\alpha

In the previous Section we proved a necessary condition for the existence of a

In the following sections we upgrade this construction to

\Gamma_{F}(X)
and to

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\Gamma

The following easily follows from a result of Prasad

(Proposition 9 of

Remark 5.3

A natural question to ask is whether the previous result holds also for
spherical varieties. This will be a first step in extending our results
from symmetric spaces to general spherical varieties. In the general case, the notion of being
quasi-split is replaced with the condition that the stabilizer of the open Borel

We prove the following result.

Proposition 5.4

The number of full closed Borel orbits in

From Propositions

\Gamma_{\bar{F}}(X)

Denote by

. It is enough to show

\sigma

\sigma

\sigma

.

All maximal split tori of

are conjugate. We may switch
\sigma
to another

2.

Proof.

For simple groups we already proved this. Denote

,

H^{\prime}=H/Z
. We
have,
\Gamma^{0}_{aff,F}(X)=\Gamma^{0}_{aff,F}(G^{\prime}/H^{\prime})

the from

In the group case it is easy to construct a non-zero harmonic function.

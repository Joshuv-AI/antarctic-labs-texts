Stochastic homogenization of stable-like processes with divergence free drifts
Xin Chen and Kun Yin

Abstract
. In this paper we will study homogenization of stable-like processes with divergence-free drifts in ergodic environments. In particular, neither the drifts nor the stream functions are required to be bounded. Keywords: homogenization; ergodic random environments; stable-like operator MSC 2010: 60G51; 60G52; 60J25; 60J75.



1.
Introduction and results

1.1.
Background

Homogenization theory aims to provide rigorous macroscopic characterizations of microscopically heterogeneous media. Papanicolaou and Varadhan

[
27
]
, Kozlov
[
21
]
independently proved the homogenization for the (parabolic or elliptic) equations corresponding to
second order elliptic operators with divergence form which have random coefficients in ergodic environments for the first time. They introduced
an important method usually called "seen from particle" to construct the correctors in ergodic environments
which satisfy the properties of everywhere sublinearty. This
was the crucial point to establish enhancements of the diffusive coefficients in limit equations.
Later on a lot of homogenization problems were investigated for various (linear and non-linear) elliptic and parabolic differential equations as well as system of equations
in ergodic environments. See also
[
3
,
4
,
19
,
31
]
and reference therein about
the homogenization for symmetric processes in ergodic environments whose infinitesimal generators
were second order differential operators with divergence forms.

Osada
[
25
]
studied the homogenization of stochastic turbulence flows in ergodic environments, which
under the assumption that both the drifts and associated stream functions
(which were the function
\tilde{H}
in Assumption
1.2
below) were bounded. The method of corrector was also used
in
[
25
]
to show the enhanced variance for limiting Brownian motion.
After
[
25
]
, there were various
different results concerning the homogenization of stochastic turbulence flows with unbounded drifts and stream functions.
Oelschläger
[
24
]
proved
the convergence in Wasserstain metric for associated scaled processes. The quenched invariance principle
was established by Fannjiang and Komorowski
[
10
]
which used maximal inequalities for
associated Poisson equations with only the moments(not the uniform bounds) of stream functions.
The result in
[
10
]
has been extended by Fannjiang and Komorowski
[
11
]
to the case in time-space ergodic environments.
Fannjiang and Papanicolaou
[
12
]
investigated the convergence of associated Fokker-Planck equations and introduced
some conditions on the correlation functions of drifts for existence of stream functions, see also Komorowski and Olla
[
20
]

for the case with time-dependent drifts. Fehrman
[
13
]
also studied the time-dependent model, where
where
mixing properties of time variables may induce different terms in limit processes. We refer the reader to monograph

[
19
]
for detailed introduction on this subject.

Recently, there were much important progress on the homogenization for stochastic turbulence flows
whose drifts were critically correlated Gaussian fields. For this case, stream functions may not exist, and
the idea of quantitative homogenization, as well as renormalization technique were applied
to study several problems. Chatzigeorgiou, Morfe, Otto, and Wang
[
5
]
obtained
the large time moment estimates for critically correlated stochastic turbulence flows.
Armstrong, Bou-Rabee and Kuusi
[
1
]
have characterized the quenched invariance principle, where
the scaling orders were not diffusive and limit processes were Brownian motions whose coefficients
only depend on the drifts(did not depend on original driven Brownian motion). Otto and Wagner
[
26
]

investigated the intermittent behaviors for critically correlated stochastic turbulence flows,
and Morfe, Otto and Wagner
[
23
]
studied the multi-scale homogenization for this model.

Now we give a brief review of some recent work on
homogenization problems
for non-local operators.
Piatnitski and Zhizhina
[
28
]
, Flegel, Heida and Slowik
[
14
]

studied homogenization problem
for a class of non-local operators in ergodic environments whose jump kernels were
L^{2}
-integrable, and see

[
29
]
for corresponding result in time-space ergodic environments.
When the jump kernels are not
L^{2}
-integrable, associated process has heavy tail property and
the scaling orders and limiting processes are no longer diffusive.
Chen, Kim and Kumagai
[
9
]
proved the Mosco convergence of non-local Dirichlet forms associated with
symmetric stable-like random walks in independent random
conductance model, where limiting processes
were
\alpha
-stable Lévy processes. Furthermore, Chen, Kumagai and Wang
[
6
]
established quenched invariance
principle for symmetric stable-like random walks in independent random conductance model.
Based on a new ABP type estimate, Schwab
[
30
]
studied
homogenization of a class of fully
non-linear integral-differential equations in ergodic environments.
Kassmann, Piatnitski and Zhizhina
[
18
]
investigated
homogenization of a class of symmetric stable-like processes in
ergodic environments whose jump kernels were uniformly elliptic and
having product form. See also Flegel and Heida
[
15
]
for corresponding results about
the discrete counterpart of this model.
By proving a new criterion for pre-compactness in
L^{1}
space,
Chen, Chen, Kumagai and Wang
[
8
]
established homogenization for symmetric
stable-like processes with more general forms, whose jump kernels could be degenerate, unbounded,
and singular, see also
[
7
]
for another special example. But by our knowledge, there are not
any results about homogenization of stochastic turbulence flows in ergodic environments driven by
jump processes,

the main purpose of this paper is to study stochastic homogenization for stable-like processes with divergence-free drifts in
ergodic environments.

1.2.
Framework

Let
d\geqslant 1
and suppose that
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathds{P})
is a probability space
endowed with a measurable group of transformations
\tau_{x}:\Omega\to\Omega,\ x\in\mathds{R}^{d}
such that

(a)

\tau_{0}={\rm id}
, where
{\rm id}
denotes the identity map on
\Omega
;

(b)

\tau_{x}\circ\tau_{x}=\tau_{x+y}
for every
x,y\in\mathds{R}^{d}
.

We also assume that
\{\tau_{x}\}_{x\in\mathds{R}^{d}}
is stationary and ergodic, i.e., the following properties hold.

(i)

\mathds{P}(\tau_{x}A)=\mathds{P}(A)
for all
A\in\mathcal{F}
and
x\in\mathds{R}^{d}
(stationary condition);

(ii)

if
A\in\mathcal{F}
and
\tau_{x}A=A
for all
x\in\mathds{R}^{d}
, then
\mathds{P}(A)\in\{0,1\}
(ergodic condition);

(iii)

the function
(x,\omega)\mapsto\tau_{x}\omega
is
\mathscr{B}(\mathds{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{F}
-measurable(measurable condition).

Let
\mathcal{H}:=L^{2}(\Omega;\mathds{P})
. With this group of transformations
\{\tau_{x}\}_{x\in\mathds{R}^{d}}
, we define the translation operator
T_{x}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}

(along direction
x\in\mathds{R}^{d}
in environments) and derivative operator
D_{k}
,
1\leqslant k\leqslant d
as follows.

T_{x}F(\omega):=F(\tau_{-x}\omega),\ x\in\mathds{R}^{d},\ F\in\mathcal{H},

D_{j}F(\omega):=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{T_{\varepsilon e_{j}}F(\omega)-F(\omega)}{\varepsilon},\ F\in\mathcal{H},\ 1\leqslant j\leqslant d

where
\{e_{j}\}_{1\leqslant j\leqslant d}
is the canonical orthonormal basis of
\mathds{R}^{d}
. By such definition and direct computation we have
(if
D_{j}F
exists)

D_{j}F(\tau_{x}\omega)=-\frac{\partial\tilde{F}(\cdot;\omega)}{\partial x_{j}}(x),\ x\in\mathds{R}^{d},

where
\tilde{F}(x;\omega):=F(\tau_{x}\omega)
.

We set

\mathscr{D}:=\{F\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{P});D_{j}F\ {\rm exists\ and}\ D_{j}F,\ D_{k}(D_{j}F)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{P})\ {\rm for\ every}\ 1\leqslant j,k\leqslant d\}.

Then by stationary property it is easy to verify the integration by parts formula for
D_{j}

(1.1)

\mathds{E}[D_{j}FG]=-\mathds{E}[FD_{j}G],\ \ F,G\in\mathscr{D}.

Since
T_{x}
is a strongly continuous unitary semi-group on
\mathcal{H}
, we have a spectral resolution of

T_{x}
as follows

(1.2)

T_{x}=\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}e^{i\langle\xi,x\rangle}U(d\xi),\ x\in\mathds{R}^{d},

where
U(d\xi)
is associated projection(on
\mathcal{H}
) valued measure, and

\langle\xi,x\rangle
denotes the canonical inner product on
\mathds{R}^{d}
. Based on the expression (
1.2
) we have

\|F\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}:=\mathds{E}[F^{2}]+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathds{E}[|D_{j}F|^{2}]=\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\left(1+|\xi|^{2}\right)\mathds{E}[U(d\xi)F\cdot F].

According to integration by parts formula (
1.1
), we define
\mathcal{H}_{1}:=\overline{\mathscr{D}}^{\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}}
as
the closed extension of
\mathscr{D}
under
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}
norm defined above. By this we know immediately
D_{j}F

is still well defined for every
F\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
and the integration by parts formula (
1.1
) is true for
every
F,G\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
.

Meanwhile for every
\beta\in(0,2)
, let

\begin{split}{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta}F(\omega)&:={\rm p.v.}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\left(F(\tau_{-z}\omega)-F(\omega)\right)\frac{1}{|z|^{d+\beta}}dz\\
&=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{\{z\in\mathds{R}^{d};|z|>\varepsilon\}}\left(F(\tau_{-z}\omega)-F(\omega)-\sum_{j=1}^{d}D_{j}F(\omega)z_{j}\right)\frac{1}{|z|^{d+\beta}}dz,\ F\in\mathscr{D}.\end{split}

where we use the notation
z=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{d})
with
z_{j}
denoting the
j
-th coordinate for
z\in\mathds{R}^{d}
. By
mean-value inequality it is not difficult to verify that
{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta}F
is well defined and
{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta}F(\omega)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{P})
for every
F\in\mathscr{D}
.

Recall that
\Delta^{\beta/2}f(x):={\rm p.v.}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\left(f(x+z)-f(x)\right)\frac{C_{0}(d,\beta)}{|z|^{d+\beta}}dz
,
f\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathds{R}^{d})

is the fractional Laplacian operator with order
\beta/2
, then by direct computation it is easy to see that

(1.3)

{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta}F(\tau_{x}\omega)=C_{0}(d,\beta)^{-1}\Delta^{\beta/2}\tilde{F}(\cdot;\omega)(x),\ \ x\in\mathds{R}^{d},\ F\in\mathscr{D},

where
\tilde{F}(x;\omega):=F(\tau_{x}\omega)
.

Applying the stationary property of
\tau_{x}
again we have

(1.4)

\mathds{E}[{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta}F\cdot G]=\mathds{E}[F\cdot{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta}G]=-\frac{1}{2}\mathds{E}\left[\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{(F(\tau_{-z}\omega)-F(\omega))(G(\tau_{-z}\omega)-G(\omega))}{|z|^{d+\beta}}dz\right],\ F,G\in\mathscr{D}.

By (
1.2
) again we have

\begin{split}\|F\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\beta/2}}&:=-2\mathds{E}[{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta}F\cdot F]+\mathds{E}[F^{2}]=\mathds{E}\left[\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{(F(\tau_{-z}\omega)-F(\omega))^{2}}{|z|^{d+\beta}}dz\right]+\mathds{E}[F^{2}]\\
&=-2\mathds{E}\left[\left(\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{T_{z}F(\omega)-F(\omega)}{|z|^{d+\beta}}dz\right)\cdot F(\omega)\right]+\mathds{E}[F^{2}]\\
&=-2\mathds{E}\left[\left(\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{e^{i\langle\xi,z\rangle}-1}{|z|^{d+\beta}}dz\right)\left(U(d\xi)F\cdot F\right)\right]+\mathds{E}[F^{2}]\\
&=\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\left(1+C_{1}(d,\beta)|\xi|^{\beta}\right)\mathds{E}[U(d\xi)F\cdot F],\ F\in\mathscr{D},\end{split}

where we use the fact that

\displaystyle-2\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{e^{i\langle\xi,z\rangle}-1}{|z|^{d+\beta}}dz=C_{1}(d,\beta)|\xi|^{\beta},\ \ \xi\in\mathds{R}^{d}.

for a positive constant
C_{1}(d,\beta)
.

Based on integration by parts formula (
1.4
), we can define
\mathcal{H}_{\beta/2}:=\overline{\mathscr{D}}^{\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\beta/2}}}
as
the closed extension of
\mathscr{D}
under
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\beta/2}}
norm defined above.
Then
\left(\mathcal{H}_{\beta/2},\|\cdot\|_{\beta/2}\right)
is a Hilbert space. By the expression of
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\beta/2}}
we know immediately that

\mathcal{H}_{1}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\beta/2},\ \|F\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\beta/2}}\leqslant c_{0}\|F\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}},\ \ \forall\ \beta\in(0,2).

Moreover, as before for every
F\in\mathcal{H}_{\beta/2}
, set
\tilde{F}(x;\omega):=F(\tau_{x}\omega)
,
x\in\mathds{R}^{d}
,
\omega\in\Omega
.
Applying standard approximation procedure we have
\tilde{F}(\cdot,\omega)\in W_{loc}^{\beta/2}(\mathds{R}^{d})
and
for every bounded subset
O\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
,

(1.5)

\begin{split}\quad\mathds{E}\left[\int_{O}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{(\tilde{F}(x+z;\omega)-\tilde{F}(x;\omega))^{2}}{|z|^{d+\beta}}dzdx\right]&=\int_{O}\mathds{E}\left[\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{(F(\tau_{z}(\tau_{x}\omega))-F(\tau_{x}\omega))^{2}}{|z|^{d+\beta}}dz\right]dx\\
&=\int_{O}\mathds{E}\left[\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{(F(\tau_{z}\omega)-F(\omega))^{2}}{|z|^{d+\beta}}dz\right]dx=\|F\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\beta/2}}\cdot|O|,\end{split}

where the third equality is due to stationary property of
\tau_{x}
and the change of variable
\tilde{z}=-z
. We refer the reader
to
[
27
]
for detailed introduction on the contents above.

Throughout this paper, let
\alpha\in(0,2)
and assume that

Assumption 1.1
.

\tilde{\mu}:\Omega\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is
a positive random variable such that

\displaystyle\inf_{\omega\in\Omega}\tilde{\mu}(\omega)\geqslant\theta_{0}>0,\ \ \ \mathds{E}[|\tilde{\mu}|^{2}]<+\infty

for some positive constant
\theta_{0}
.

We define
\mu:\mathds{R}^{d}\times\Omega\to[0,\infty)
as

\mu(x;\omega):=\tilde{\mu}(\tau_{x}\omega),\ \ x\in\mathds{R}^{d},\ \omega\in\Omega.

And we make the following assumption on
\mu

(1.6)

x\mapsto\int(1\wedge|z|^{2})\frac{\mu(x;\omega)\mu(x+z;\omega)}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}\,dz\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathds{R}^{d};dx),\quad\mbox{$\mathds{P}$-a.e.}

For a.s.
\omega\in\Omega
, we define a (symmetric) bilinear form
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{\omega}
as follows

(1.7)

{\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{\omega}(f,g):=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathds{R}^{d}\times\mathds{R}^{d}\setminus\Delta}(f(x)-f(y))(g(x)-g(y))\frac{\mu(x;\omega)\mu(y;\omega)}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}}\,dx\,dy,\quad f,g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{d}),

where
\Delta:=\{(x,x)\in\mathds{R}^{d}\}
is the diagonal of
\mathds{R}^{d}\times\mathds{R}^{d}
, and
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{d})
denotes the collection
of all
C^{\infty}
functions on
\mathds{R}^{d}
with compact supports. In particular,
under assumption
(
1.6
), it is easy to verify that
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{\omega}(f,f)<\infty
for all
f\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})
.
It is well known that (see e.g.
[
16
]
)
({\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{\omega},{\mathcal{F}}_{0}^{\omega})
is a symmetric regular
Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{d};dx)
with
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}^{\omega}

being the closure of
C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\omega}}:=

(\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\omega}(\cdot,\cdot)+\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{d};dx)}^{2})^{1/2}
.
In particular,
{\mathcal{F}}^{\omega}_{0}
is a Hilbert space endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{\omega}}
.
Similarly, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we can define the scaling Dirichlet form
(\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0},{\mathcal{F}}_{0}^{\varepsilon,\omega})
as follows.

(1.8)

{\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{\varepsilon,\omega}(f,g):=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathds{R}^{d}\times\mathds{R}^{d}\setminus\Delta}(f(x)-f(y))(g(x)-g(y))\frac{\mu\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)\mu\left(\frac{y}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}}\,dx\,dy,\quad f,g\in{\mathcal{F}}_{0}^{\varepsilon,\omega},

and
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}^{\varepsilon,\omega}
is a Hilbert space endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{\varepsilon,\omega}}:=

(\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\varepsilon,\omega}(\cdot,\cdot)+\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{d};dx)}^{2})^{1/2}
.

Let
\{X_{t}^{\omega}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
be the Hunt process associated with
({\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{\omega},{\mathcal{F}}_{0}^{\omega})
(note that
({\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{\omega},{\mathcal{F}}_{0}^{\omega})
is regular), then
the scaled process
\{X_{t}^{\varepsilon,\omega}\}_{t\geqslant 0}=\{\varepsilon X^{\omega}_{\varepsilon^{-\alpha}t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
is the Hunt process related to

({\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{\varepsilon,\omega},{\mathcal{F}}_{0}^{\varepsilon,\omega})
.

Meanwhile, we assume that

Assumption 1.2
.

\tilde{b}=(\tilde{b}_{1},\tilde{b}_{2},\cdots,\tilde{b}_{d}):\Omega\to\mathds{R}^{d}
is a random vector
and there exists a system of stream functions(for
\tilde{b}
)
\tilde{H}_{jl}:\Omega\to\mathds{R}
,
1\leqslant j,l\leqslant d
such that
the following properties hold.

(i)

\tilde{H}_{jl}\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
for every
1\leqslant j,l\leqslant d
;

(ii)

\tilde{H}_{jl}=-\tilde{H}_{lj}
for every
1\leqslant j,l\leqslant d
;

(iii)

\tilde{b}_{j}=\sum_{l=1}^{d}D_{l}\tilde{H}_{jl}
for every
1\leqslant j\leqslant d
;

(iv)

The function
x\mapsto D_{l}\tilde{H}_{jl}(\tau_{x}\omega)
is continuous for every
1\leqslant l\leqslant d
and a.s.
\omega\in\Omega
.

As before given
\tilde{b}:\Omega\to\mathds{R}^{d}
we define
b:\mathds{R}^{d}\times\Omega\to\mathds{R}^{d}
by

b(x;\omega):=\tilde{b}(\tau_{x}\omega)=\left(\tilde{b}_{1}(\tau_{x}\omega),\tilde{b}_{2}(\tau_{x}\omega),\cdots,\tilde{b}_{d}(\tau_{x}\omega)\right),\ \ x\in\mathds{R}^{d},\ \omega\in\Omega.

According to Assumption
1.2
it is easy to verify that

(1.9)

{\rm div}b(\cdot;\omega)(x)=-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\partial b_{j}(x;\omega)}{\partial x_{j}}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}D_{j}\tilde{b}_{j}(\tau_{x}\omega)=\sum_{j,l=1}^{d}D_{j}D_{l}\tilde{H}_{jl}(\tau_{x}\omega)=0,

where the last equality is in the distribution sense.

With
\left({\mathcal{E}}^{\omega}_{0},{\mathcal{F}}^{\omega}_{0}\right)
and vector fields
b
given above, we define a bilinear non-symmetric form
(for a.s.
\omega\in\Omega
) as follows,

{\mathcal{E}}^{\omega}(f,g):={\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{\omega}(f,g)+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}b_{j}(x;\omega)\frac{\partial f(x)}{\partial x_{j}}g(x)dx,\ \ \ f,g\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d}).

Using divergence free condition (
1.9
), definition of
({\mathcal{E}}^{\omega}_{0},{\mathcal{F}}^{\omega}_{0})
, integration by parts formula
and standard approximation arguments,
{\mathcal{E}}^{\omega}
is still well defined on
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}^{\omega}\times C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})
as follows.

{\mathcal{E}}^{\omega}(f,g)={\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{\omega}(f,g)-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}b_{j}(x;\omega)\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}f(x)dx,\ \ \ f\in{\mathcal{F}}_{0}^{\omega},\ g\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d}).

Then for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
we define the scaled bi-linear form

{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}(f,g)={\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{\varepsilon,\omega}(f,g)-\varepsilon^{-(\alpha-1)}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}b_{j}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}f(x)dx,\ \ \ f\in{\mathcal{F}}_{0}^{\varepsilon,\omega},\ g\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d}).

As before, if
\{Y_{t}^{\omega}\}_{\geqslant 0}
is the process associated with
{\mathcal{E}}^{\omega}

(some extra condition is required to ensure the existence of
\{Y_{t}^{\omega}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
, such as the uniform boundedness of
\mu(x;\omega)
and
b(x;\omega)
), then

\{Y_{t}^{\varepsilon,\omega}\}_{t\geqslant 0}=\{\varepsilon Y_{\varepsilon^{-\alpha}t}^{\omega}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
is the process related to

{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}
.

As shown in Proposition
2.1
below, for every
f\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})
,
\lambda>0
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and a.s.
\omega\in\Omega
,
there exists a (weak) solution
u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\omega}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{0}^{\varepsilon,\omega}
of the following resolvent equation associated with
{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}
,

(1.10)

{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}(u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\omega},g)+\lambda\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\omega}(x)g(x)dx=\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}f(x)g(x)dx,\ \forall\ g\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d}).

Now we will give the main theorem of this paper concerning the homogenization for scaled equation (
1.10
).

Theorem 1.3
.

For every
f\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})
,
\lambda>0
and a.s.
\omega\in\Omega
, let
u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\omega}
be the solution
of (
1.10
) obtained in Proposition
2.1
, then we have

(1.11)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{O}|u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\omega}(x)-\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}(x)|^{2}dx=0,\ {\rm for\ every\ bounded\ subset\ }O\subset\mathds{R}^{d}.

Here
\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}
is the unique solution in
L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{d})
of the following equation

(1.12)

\lambda\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}(x)-\bar{L}\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}(x)=f(x),\ \ x\in\mathds{R}^{d},

with

\bar{L}f(x):={\rm p.v.}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\left(f(x+z)-f(x)\right)\frac{\mathds{E}[\mu]^{2}}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}dz,\ \ f\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathds{R}^{d}).

Remark 1.4
.

For simplicity of the notation, we only consider the case that the jump kernel of
{\mathcal{E}}^{\omega}_{0}

is with product form defined by (
1.8
). By the proof, the main theorem still holds when
the jump kernel of
{\mathcal{E}}^{\omega}_{0}
has more general form as that in
[
7
,
8
]
.

2.
Existence and uniqueness for the solution of (
1.10
)

Proposition 2.1
.

For every
f\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})
,
\lambda>0
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and a.s.
\omega\in\Omega
, there exists a
u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\omega}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{0}^{\varepsilon,\omega}
such that
(
1.10
) holds,
and

(2.1)

\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}\|u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\omega}\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{d})}<+\infty,\ \sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}\|u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\omega}\|_{W^{\alpha/2,2}(\mathds{R}^{d})}<+\infty,\ \ \sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}{\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{\varepsilon,\omega}\left(u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\omega},u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\omega}\right)<+\infty.

Moreover if we assume
\alpha\in[1,2)
and

(2.2)

\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}\left(|\tilde{b}(\omega)|+\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{|b(\tau_{z}\omega)-b(\omega)|^{2}}{|z|^{d+2-\alpha}}dz\right)<+\infty,

then
u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\omega}
above is the unique solution of (
1.10
) in
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}^{\varepsilon,\omega}
.

Proof.

Step 1
By Assumption
1.1
we know immediately that
{\mathcal{F}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0}\subset W^{\alpha/2,2}(\mathds{R}^{d})
and

(2.3)

\begin{split}\|g\|_{{\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{\varepsilon,\omega}}&\geqslant c_{1}\left(\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{(g(x+z)-g(x))^{2}}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}dzdx+\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|g(x)|^{2}dx\right)=\|g\|_{W^{\alpha/2,2}(\mathds{R}^{d})},\ g\in{\mathcal{F}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0}.\end{split}

For every
\theta>0
and integer
k\in\mathbb{N_{+}}
, we define a bi-linear form
{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{\theta,k}:C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})\times C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})\to\mathds{R}
by

{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{\theta,k}(g,h):={\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0}(g,h)+\theta\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial h(x)}{\partial x_{j}}dx+\varepsilon^{-(\alpha-1)}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}b_{j}^{k}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}h(x)dx,\ f,g\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d}),

where
b_{j}^{k}(x;\omega):=\tilde{b}_{j}^{k}(\tau_{x}\omega)
,
\tilde{b}_{j}^{k}(\omega):=\sum_{l=1}^{d}D_{l}\left(\eta_{k}\left(\tilde{H}_{jl}(\cdot)\right)\right)(\omega)
,
and
\eta_{k}:\mathds{R}\to(0,+\infty)
is a function such that

\displaystyle\eta_{k}(s)=\begin{cases}s,\ \ &\ |s|\leqslant k,\\
\in(-k,k),\ \ &\ k<|s|<2k,\\
0,\ \ &\ |s|\geqslant 2k,\end{cases}

\sup_{k\geqslant 1}\sup_{s\in\mathds{R}}|\eta_{k}^{\prime}(s)|\leqslant 2
and
\eta_{k}(s)=-\eta_{k}(-s)
for every
s\in\mathds{R}
.

Then by definition and anti-symmetric property of
\{\tilde{H}_{jl}\}_{1\leqslant j,l\leqslant d}
(also the anti-symmetry of

\{\eta_{k}(\tilde{H}_{jl})\}_{1\leqslant j,l\leqslant d}
) we have
for every
f,g\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})
,

{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{\theta,k}(g,h):={\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0}(g,h)+\theta\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial h(x)}{\partial x_{j}}dx-\varepsilon^{2-\alpha}\sum_{j,l=1}^{d}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\eta_{k}\left(H_{jl}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)\right)\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial h(x)}{\partial x_{l}}dx,

by this it is easy to verify that

(2.4)

\begin{split}|{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{\theta,k}(g,h)|&\leqslant c_{2}(\varepsilon,\theta,k)\left(\|g\|_{{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0}}+\|g\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathds{R}^{d})}\right)\cdot\left(\|h\|_{{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0}}+\|h\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathds{R}^{d})}\right)\\
&=:c_{2}(\varepsilon,\theta,k)\|g\|_{{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{1}}\|h\|_{{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{1}},\ \ f,g\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d}),\end{split}

where
c_{2}(\varepsilon,\theta,k)
is a positive constant which may depend on
\varepsilon
,
\theta
,
k
, and

\|g\|_{{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{1}}:=\|g\|_{{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0}}+\|g\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathds{R}^{d})}
.

Let
\mathbb{H}_{1}:=\overline{C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})}^{\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{1}}}
be the closed extension of

C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})
under the norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{1}}
defined above, clearly we have

\mathbb{H}_{1}\subset W^{1,2}(\mathds{R}^{d})
. Hence according to (
2.4
) we can extend

{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{\theta,k}
to a continuous bilinear form on
\mathbb{H}_{1}\times\mathbb{H}_{1}
.

Meanwhile by anti-symmetry of
\{\tilde{H}_{jl}\}_{1\leqslant j,l\leqslant d}
and the fact
\eta(s)=-\eta(-s)
it holds that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{j,l=1}^{d}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\eta_{k}\left(H_{jl}^{k}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)\right)\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{l}}dx=0,\ \ \forall\ g\in\mathbb{H}_{1},

which implies that for every
\lambda>0
,

\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{d})}^{2}+{\mathcal{E}}_{\theta,k}^{\varepsilon,\omega}(g,g)\geqslant c_{3}(\lambda,\theta,k)\|g\|_{{\mathcal{E}}^{\omega}_{1}}^{2},\ \ g\in\mathbb{H}_{1}.

Therefore according to the Lax-Milgram theorem for every
f\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})
, there exists a unique
u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta,k,\omega}\in\mathbb{H}_{1}
such that

(2.6)

\begin{split}&\quad{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0}(u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta,k,\omega},g)+\theta\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\partial u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta,k,\omega}(x)}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}\\
&-\varepsilon^{-(\alpha-1)}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}b_{j}^{k}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta,k,\omega}(x)dx+\lambda\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta,k,\omega}(x)g(x)dx\\
&=\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}f(x)g(x)dx,\ \forall\ g\in\mathbb{H}_{1}.\end{split}

So taking
g=u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta,k,\omega}
in the above equation and using (
2.5
) we have

\displaystyle\quad{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0}(u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta,k,\omega},u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta,k,\omega})+\theta\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{\partial u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta,k,\omega}(x)}{\partial x_{j}}\right)^{2}dx+\lambda\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta,k,\omega}(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}f(x)u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta,k,\omega}(x)dx\leqslant\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta,k,\omega}(x)|^{2}dx+(2\lambda)^{-1}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{2}dx.

By this and (
2.3
) we obtain immediately that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon,\theta\in(0,1),k\geqslant 1}\|u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta,k,\omega}\|_{W^{\alpha/2,2}(\mathds{R}^{d})}\leqslant c_{1}^{-1}\sup_{\varepsilon,\theta\in(0,1),k\geqslant 1}{\mathcal{E}}^{\omega}_{0}(u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta,k,\omega},u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta,k,\omega})\leqslant c_{4}(\lambda)<\infty,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon,\theta\in(0,1),k\geqslant 1}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta,k,\omega}(x)|^{2}dx\leqslant\lambda^{-2}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{2}dx,

and for every
\theta\in(0,1)
,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1),k\geqslant 1}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{\partial u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta,k,\omega}(x)}{\partial x_{j}}\right)^{2}dx\leqslant c_{4}(\lambda)\theta^{-1}.

Hence for every fixed
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
\{u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta,k,\omega}\}_{\theta\in(0,1),k\geqslant 1}
is weakly compact in
\left({\mathcal{F}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0},\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0}}\right)
and

W^{\alpha/2,2}(\mathds{R}^{d})
, so there exists a subsequence
\{u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta_{m},k_{m},\omega}\}
and
u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\omega}\in{\mathcal{F}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0}(u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta_{m},k_{m},\omega},g)={\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0}(u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\omega},g),\ \forall\ g\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d}),

and

(2.10)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{O}\left|u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta_{m},k_{m},\omega}(x)-u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\omega}(x)\right|^{2}dx=0,\ {\rm for\ any\ bounded\ subset}\ O\subset\mathds{R}^{d}.

Using (
2.9
) it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\theta_{m}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left|\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\partial u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta_{m},k_{m},\omega}(x)}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}dx\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\lim_{m\to\infty}\theta_{m}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\left\|\frac{\partial u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta_{m},k_{m},\omega}}{\partial x_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{d})}\cdot\left\|\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{d})}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{5}\lim_{m\to\infty}\theta_{m}^{1/2}=0.

For every
g\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})
, let
O\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
be the support of
g
, since
O
is bounded, by definition of
b_{j}^{k}(x;\omega)
(note that
|\eta_{k}^{\prime}(s)|\leqslant 2
)
we know for every fixed
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
(note that by Assumption
1.2
(iv),
x\mapsto D_{l}\tilde{H}_{jl}(\tau_{x}\omega)
is continuous)

\displaystyle\sup_{k\geqslant 1,x\in O}|b_{j}^{k}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)|\leqslant c_{6}\sup_{x\in\frac{O}{\varepsilon}}\sup_{1\leqslant j,l\leqslant d}\left|\frac{H_{jl}(x;\omega)}{\partial x_{l}}\right|<c_{7}(\varepsilon,\omega)<+\infty,\ \ \omega\in\Omega.

According to this, (
2.10
) and dominated convergence theorem yields that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}b_{j}^{k_{m}}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta_{m},k_{m},\omega}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}b_{j}^{k_{m}}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\omega}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}b_{j}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\omega}(x)dx,\ \forall\ g\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d}).

Therefore putting
u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\theta_{m},k_{m},\omega}
in equation (
2.6
) and letting
m\to\infty
we arrive at
for every
g\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})
,

\begin{split}&\quad{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0}(u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\omega},g)-\varepsilon^{-(\alpha-1)}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}b_{j}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\omega}(x)dx+\lambda\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\omega}(x)g(x)dx=\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}f(x)g(x)dx.\end{split}

Combining this with (
2.7
), (
2.8
) implies (
1.10
) and (
2.1
).

Step 2
Now we consider the uniqueness of the solution.
For every
g,h\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})
and
1\leqslant j\leqslant d
, by Parseval’s identity it holds that

\begin{split}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}b_{j}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}h(x)dx&=\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\mathscr{F}\left(\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{j}}\right)(\xi)\mathscr{F}\left(b_{j}\left(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)h(\cdot)\right)(\xi)d\xi\\
&=i\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\xi_{j}\mathscr{F}(g)(\xi)\mathscr{F}\left(b_{j}\left(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)h(\cdot)\right)(\xi)d\xi,\end{split}

where
\mathscr{F}(g)(\xi):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}e^{i\langle\xi,x\rangle}g(x)dx
denotes
the Fourier transform for a function
g\in L^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})
.

By this we have

(2.11)

\begin{split}&\quad\left|\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}b_{j}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}h(x)dx\right|\\
&\leqslant\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|\xi_{j}|\left|\mathscr{F}(g)(\xi)\right|\left|\mathscr{F}\left(b_{j}\left(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)h(\cdot)\right)(\xi)\right|d\xi\\
&\leqslant c_{8}\left(\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{\alpha}\left|\mathscr{F}(g)(\xi)\right|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\cdot\left(\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{2-\alpha}\left|\mathscr{F}\left(b_{j}\left(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)h(\cdot)\right)(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi\right)^{1/2}\\
&=c_{9}\left(\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{(g(x+z)-g(x))^{2}}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}dzdx\right)^{1/2}\cdot\left(\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{(b_{j}\left(\frac{x+z}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)h(x+z)-b_{j}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)h(x))^{2}}{|z|^{d+2-\alpha}}dzdx\right)^{1/2}.\end{split}

Meanwhile according to (
2.2
) it holds that

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\left(b_{j}\left(\frac{x+z}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)h(x+z)-b_{j}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)h(x)\right)^{2}}{|z|^{d+2-\alpha}}dzdx

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\left|b_{j}\left(\frac{x+z}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)\right|^{2}\frac{\left|h(x+z)-h(x)\right|^{2}}{|z|^{d+2-\alpha}}dzdx+2\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|h(x)|^{2}\frac{\left|b_{j}\left(\frac{x+z}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)-b_{j}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon};\omega\right)\right|^{2}}{|z|^{d+2-\alpha}}dzdx

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\left|\tilde{b}_{j}(\tau_{\frac{x+z}{\varepsilon}}\omega)\right|^{2}\frac{\left|h(x+z)-h(x)\right|^{2}}{|z|^{d+2-\alpha}}dzdx+2\varepsilon^{-(2-\alpha)}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|h(x)|^{2}\frac{\left|\tilde{b}_{j}\left(\tau_{z}(\tau_{\frac{x}{\varepsilon}}\omega)\right)-\tilde{b}_{j}\left(\tau_{\frac{x}{\varepsilon}};\omega\right)\right|^{2}}{|z|^{d+2-\alpha}}dzdx

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{10}\left(\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|h(x+z)-h(x)\right|^{2}}{|z|^{d+2-\alpha}}dzdx+\varepsilon^{-(2-\alpha)}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|h(x)|^{2}dx\right).

Also note that
2-\alpha\leqslant\alpha
when
\alpha\in[1,2)
, so we derive

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|h(x+z)-h(x)\right|^{2}}{|z|^{d+2-\alpha}}dzdx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\{|z|\leqslant 1\}}\frac{\left|h(x+z)-h(x)\right|^{2}}{|z|^{d+2-\alpha}}dzdx+\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\{|z|>1\}}\frac{\left|h(x+z)-h(x)\right|^{2}}{|z|^{d+2-\alpha}}dzdx

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\{|z|\leqslant 1\}}\frac{\left|h(x+z)-h(x)\right|^{2}}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}dzdx+c_{11}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|h(x)|^{2}dx

Putting all above estimates together into (
2.11
) yields that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}b_{j}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\omega\right)\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}h(x)dx\right|\leqslant c_{12}(\varepsilon)\|g\|_{W^{\alpha/2,2}(\mathds{R}^{d})}\|h\|_{W^{\alpha/2,2}(\mathds{R}^{d})},\ \ \forall\ h,g\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d}),

where
c_{12}(\varepsilon)
is a positive constant which may depend on
\varepsilon
.

By this, (
2.3
) and the definition of
{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}
we know immediately that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}(h,g)\leqslant c_{13}(\varepsilon)\|g\|_{{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0}}\|h\|_{{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0}},\ \ \forall\ h,g\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d}).

Based on the estimate we can extend
{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}
to a continuous bilinear form on
{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0}\times{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0}
.

Hence applying (
2.5
) and using standard approximation arguments we derive

\displaystyle c_{14}\|g\|_{{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0}}^{2}\leqslant{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}(g,g)\leqslant c_{15}\|g\|_{{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0}}^{2},\ \ \forall\ g\in{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon,\omega}_{0},

where the positive constants
c_{14}
,
c_{15}
here are independent of
\varepsilon
.
By this we can apply the Lax-Milgram theorem to prove that there exists a unique solution
u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon,\omega}
for
(
1.10
) which satisfies (
2.1
).

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By (

) we know

Suppose that

(3.2)

{\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega},g)-\varepsilon_{m}^{-(\alpha-1)}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}b_{j}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\omega\right)\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(x)dx+\lambda\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(x)g(x)dx=\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}f(x)g(x)dx.

Step 1
In this step, we are going to prove

(3.3)

\lim_{m\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega},g)=-\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}u_{0}^{\omega}(x)\bar{L}g(x)dx,\ \forall\ g\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathds{R}^{d}).

The main method in this step is similar with that in
[
8
]
, for convenience of the reader we will give a detailed proof here.
For every
\delta\in(0,1)
we set

\begin{split}&\quad{\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega},g)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\{|z|\leqslant\delta\}}+\int_{\{\delta<|z|<\delta^{-1}\}}+\int_{\{|z|\geqslant\delta^{-1}\}}\right)\left(u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(x+z)-u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(x)\right)\left(g(x+z)-g(x)\right)\\

Suppose that
{\rm supp}g\subset B(0,R_{0}):=\{x\in\mathds{R}^{d};|x|\leqslant R_{0}\}
for some
R_{0}>0
. Apply Cauchy-Schwartz inequality

\displaystyle|I_{1}^{m,\delta,\omega}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\{|z|\leqslant\delta\}}\left(u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(x+z)-u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(x)\right)^{2}\frac{\mu\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\mu\left(\frac{x+z}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}dzdx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\{|z|\leqslant\delta\}}\left(g(x+z)-g(x)\right)^{2}\frac{\mu\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\mu\left(\frac{x+z}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{1}(\omega)\left(\|\nabla g\|_{\infty}^{2}\int_{\{|x|\leqslant R_{0}+2\}}\mu\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)^{2}\left(\int_{\{|z|\leqslant\delta\}}\frac{|z|^{2}}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}dz\right)dx\right)^{1/2},

where

is a positive constant(may depend on
\omega
) which is finite due to (
2.1
).

By direct computation we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\{|x|\leqslant R_{0}+2\}}\int_{\{|z|\leqslant\delta\}}\mu\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)^{2}\frac{|z|^{2}}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}dzdx

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{2}\delta^{2-\alpha}\cdot\int_{\{|x|\leqslant R_{0}+2\}}\tilde{\mu}\left(\tau_{\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}}}\omega\right)^{2}dx.

Hence by Birkhoff ergodic theorem (see, for example,
[
8
, Proposition 2.1]
or
[

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\{|x|\leqslant R_{0}+2\}}\int_{\{|z|\leqslant\delta\}}\mu\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)^{2}\frac{|z|^{2}}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}dzdx\leqslant c_{3}\mathds{E}[\tilde{\mu}^{2}]\delta^{2-\alpha}.

Similarly for
I_{3}^{m,\delta,\omega}
we obtain

\displaystyle|I_{3}^{m,\delta,\omega}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\{|x-y|>\delta^{-1}\}}\left(u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(y)-u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(x)\right)^{2}\frac{\mu\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\mu\left(\frac{y}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}}dydx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\{|x-y|>\delta^{-1}\}}\left(g(y)-g(x)\right)^{2}\frac{\mu\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\mu\left(\frac{y}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}}dydx\right)^{1/2}.

For
\delta^{-1}>2(R_{0}+2)
, we know
\left(g(x)-g(y)\right)1_{\{|x-y|>\delta^{-1}\}}\neq 0
only if

or

. Then using the symmetry of

and

we have

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{m\to\infty}\iint_{\{|x-y|>\delta^{-1}\}}(g(y)-g(x))^{2}\frac{\mu\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\mu\left(\frac{y}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leqslant 4\|g\|_{\infty}^{2}\varlimsup_{m\to\infty}\int_{B(0,R_{0})}\left(\int_{\{|y-x|>\delta^{-1}\}}\frac{\tilde{\mu}\left(\frac{y}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)}{|y-x|^{d+\alpha}}dy\right)\mu\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{2}\varlimsup_{m\to\infty}\int_{\{|x|\leqslant R_{0}+2\}}\mu\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)^{2}\left(\int_{\{|x-y|>\delta^{-1}\}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}}dy\right)dx

\displaystyle\quad\quad+c_{2}\varlimsup_{m\to\infty}\int_{\{|x|\leqslant R_{0}+2\}}\int_{\{|z|>\delta^{-1}\}}\frac{\mu\left(\frac{y}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}}dydx,

where the last step follows from Cauchy-Schwartz inequality.
Applying Birkhoff ergodic theorem we get

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\{|x|\leqslant R_{0}+2\}}\mu\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)^{2}\left(\int_{\{|x-y|>\delta^{-1}\}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}}dy\right)dx\leqslant c_{4}\mathds{E}[\tilde{\mu}^{2}]\delta^{\alpha}.

For
\delta^{-1}\geqslant 2(R_{0}+2)
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\{|x|\leqslant R_{0}+2\}}\int_{\{|x-y|>\delta^{-1}\}}\frac{\tilde{\mu}\left(\tau_{\frac{y}{\varepsilon_{m}}}\omega\right)^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}}dydx

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{5}\int_{\{|x|\leqslant R_{0}+2\}}\int_{\{|y|>(2\delta)^{-1}\}}\frac{\tilde{\mu}\left(\tau_{\frac{y}{\varepsilon_{m}}}\omega\right)^{2}}{|y|^{d+\alpha}}dydx

\displaystyle=:c_{5}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|B(0,R_{0}+2)|\int_{U_{k}}\frac{\tilde{\mu}\left(\tau_{\frac{y}{\varepsilon_{m}}}\omega\right)^{2}}{|y|^{d+\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{6}\delta^{d+\alpha}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k(d+\alpha)}\varepsilon_{m}^{d}\int_{B\left(0,\frac{2^{k+1}(2\delta)^{-1}}{\varepsilon_{m}}\right)}\tilde{\mu}\left(\tau_{y}\omega\right)^{2}dy.

where
U_{k}:=\{y\in\mathds{R}^{d};2^{k}(2\delta)^{-1}<|y|\leqslant 2^{k+1}(2\delta)^{-1}\}
for every integer
k\geqslant 0
. According to the ergodic theorem again
there exists a constant
c_{7}(\omega)
(which may depend on
\omega
) such that

\displaystyle\sup_{m\geqslant 1}\varepsilon_{m}^{d}\int_{B(0,\varepsilon_{m}^{-1})}\tilde{\mu}(\tau_{y}\omega)^{2}dy\leqslant c_{7}(\omega)\mathds{E}[\tilde{\mu}^{2}].

Hence using this in above inequality yields

\displaystyle\sup_{m\geqslant 1}\int_{\{|x|\leqslant R_{0}+2\}}\int_{\{|x-y|>\delta^{-1}\}}\frac{\mu\left(\frac{y}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}}dydx

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{8}(\omega)\delta^{\alpha}\mathds{E}[\tilde{\mu}^{2}]\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k\alpha}\leqslant c_{9}(\omega)\delta^{\alpha}.

Combining all above estimates we can verify directly that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\varlimsup_{m\to\infty}\left(|I_{1}^{m,\delta,\omega}|+|I_{3}^{m,\delta,\omega}|\right)=0.

By the change of variable
\tilde{z}=-z
and
\tilde{x}=x+z
for the integral

\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\{\delta<|z|<\delta^{-1}\}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}\left(x+z;\omega\right)\left(g(x+z)-g(x)\right)\frac{\mu\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\mu\left(\frac{x+z}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}dzdx

we have

\displaystyle I_{2}^{m,\delta,\omega}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\{\delta<|z|<\delta^{-1}\}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}\left(x;\omega\right)\left(g(x+z)-g(x)\right)\frac{\mu\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\mu\left(\frac{x+z}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}dzdx,

which implies that for each fixed
\delta\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\quad\lim_{m\to\infty}\left|I_{2}^{m,\delta,\omega}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\{\delta<|z|<\delta^{-1}\}}\left(u_{0}^{\omega}(x+z)-u_{0}^{\omega}(x)\right)\left(g(x+z)-g(x)\right)\frac{\mu\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\mu\left(\frac{x+z}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}dzdx\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\lim_{m\to\infty}c_{10}\|g\|_{\infty}\delta^{-d-\alpha}\cdot\left(\int_{\{|x|\leqslant R_{0}+\delta^{-1}\}}\int_{\{\delta<|z|<\delta^{-1}\}}\left|u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}\left(x\right)-u_{0}^{\omega}(x)\right|^{2}dzdx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{\{|x|\leqslant R_{0}+\delta^{-1}\}}\int_{\{\delta<|z|<\delta^{-1}\}}\mu\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)^{2}\mu\left(\frac{x+z}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)^{2}dzdx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lim_{m\to\infty}c_{11}(\delta)\left(\int_{\{|x|\leqslant R_{0}+\delta^{-1}\}}\left|u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}\left(x\right)-u_{0}^{\omega}(x)\right|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{\{|x|\leqslant R_{0}+2\delta^{-1}\}}\int_{\{|y|\leqslant R_{0}+2\delta^{-1}\}}\tilde{\mu}\left(\tau_{\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}}}\omega\right)^{2}\tilde{\mu}\left(\tau_{\frac{y}{\varepsilon_{m}}}\omega\right)^{2}dxdy\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lim_{m\to\infty}c_{11}(\delta)\mathds{E}[\tilde{\mu}^{2}]\left(\int_{\{|x|\leqslant R_{0}+\delta^{-1}\}}\left|u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}\left(x\right)-u_{0}^{\omega}(x)\right|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}=0,

where in the third inequality we have used the ergodic theorem, and the last step is due to (
3.1
).

According to
[
8
, Lemma 3.1(ii)]
we have

\displaystyle\quad\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\{\delta<|z|<\delta^{-1}\}}\left(u_{0}^{\omega}(x+z)-u_{0}^{\omega}(x)\right)\left(g(x+z)-g(x)\right)\frac{\mu\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\mu\left(\frac{x+z}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}dzdx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\{\delta<|z|<\delta^{-1}\}}\left(u_{0}^{\omega}(x+z)-u_{0}^{\omega}(x)\right)\left(g(x+z)-g(x)\right)\frac{\mathds{E}[\tilde{\mu}]^{2}}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}dzdx.

Therefore putting all these estimates together yields that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}I_{2}^{m,\delta,\omega}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\{\delta<|z|<\delta^{-1}\}}\left(u_{0}^{\omega}(x+z)-u_{0}^{\omega}(x)\right)\left(g(x+z)-g(x)\right)\frac{\mathds{E}[\tilde{\mu}]^{2}}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}dzdx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}u_{0}^{\omega}(x)\left(\int_{\{\delta<|z|<\delta^{-1}\}}\left(g(x+z)-g(x)\right)\frac{\mathds{E}[\tilde{\mu}]^{2}}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}dz\right)dx.

According to this and (
3.4
), firstly letting
m\to\infty
, then
\delta\downarrow 0
we
can prove (
3.3
).

Step 2
It remains to prove that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\varepsilon_{m}^{-(\alpha-1)}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}b_{j}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(x)dx=0,\ \forall\ g\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathds{R}^{d}).

Without loss of generality, we assume that
{\rm supp}g\subset B(0,R_{0})
for some
R_{0}>0
.

By Assumption
1.2
we have
H_{jl}(\cdot;\omega)\in W^{1,2}_{loc}(\mathds{R}^{d})
for
H_{jl}(x;\omega):=\tilde{H}_{jl}(\tau_{x}\omega)
.
Since
{\rm supp}g\subset B(0,R_{0})
, we choose a function
h\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathds{R}^{d})
such that

h(x)=1
for every
x\in B(0,R_{0})
and
{\rm supp}h\subset B(0,2R_{0})
. Based on this it holds that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{m}^{-(\alpha-1)}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}b_{j}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\omega\right)\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{m}^{2-\alpha}\sum_{l=1}^{d}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{l}}\left(H_{jl}^{\varepsilon_{m}}\left(\cdot;\omega\right)h(\cdot)\right)(x)\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(x)dx,

where
H_{jl}^{\varepsilon_{m}}(x;\omega):=H_{jl}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)
.

So by Parseval’s identity we get for every
1\leqslant j,l\leqslant d
,

\displaystyle\quad\varepsilon_{m}^{2-\alpha}\left|\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{l}}\left(H_{jl}^{\varepsilon_{m}}\left(\cdot;\omega\right)h(\cdot)\right)(x)\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(x)dx\right|

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{m}^{2-\alpha}\left|\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}i\xi_{l}\mathscr{F}\left(H_{jl}^{\varepsilon_{m}}\left(\cdot;\omega\right)h(\cdot)\right)(\xi)\cdot\mathscr{F}\left(\frac{\partial g(\cdot)}{\partial x_{j}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(\cdot)\right)(\xi)d\xi\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{13}\varepsilon_{m}^{2-\alpha}\left(\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{\alpha}\left|\mathscr{F}\left(\frac{\partial g(\cdot)}{\partial x_{j}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(\cdot)\right)(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi\right)^{1/2}\cdot\left(\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{2-\alpha}\left|\mathscr{F}\left(H_{jl}^{\varepsilon_{m}}\left(\cdot;\omega\right)h(\cdot)\right)(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi\right)^{1/2}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{\alpha}\left|\mathscr{F}\left(\frac{\partial g(\cdot)}{\partial x_{j}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(\cdot)\right)(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{14}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|\frac{\partial g(x+z)}{\partial x_{j}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(x+z)-\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(x)\right|^{2}}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}dzdx

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{15}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{|u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(x+z)-u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(x)|^{2}}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}\left|\frac{\partial g(x+z)}{\partial x_{j}}\right|^{2}dzdx+c_{15}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(x)|^{2}\left(\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|\frac{\partial g(x+z)}{\partial x_{j}}-\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}\right|^{2}}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}dz\right)dx

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{16}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{|u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(x+z)-u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(x)|^{2}}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}dzdx+c_{15}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(x)|^{2}dx.

Hence according to (
2.1
) we obtain

(3.7)

\sup_{m\geqslant 1}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{\alpha}\left|\mathscr{F}\left(\frac{\partial g(\cdot)}{\partial x_{j}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(\cdot)\right)(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi<+\infty.

Meanwhile it holds that

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{2-\alpha}\left|\mathscr{F}\left(H_{jl}^{\varepsilon_{m}}(\cdot;\omega)h(\cdot)\right)(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|H_{jl}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)h(x)-H_{jl}\left(\frac{y}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)h(y)\right|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2-\alpha}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|H_{jl}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)-H_{jl}\left(\frac{y}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\right|^{2}|h(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2-\alpha}}dxdy+2\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{|h(x)-h(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2-\alpha}}\left|H_{jl}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\right|^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{17}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|H_{jl}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)-H_{jl}\left(\frac{y}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\right|^{2}|h(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2-\alpha}}dxdy+c_{17}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|H_{jl}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\right|^{2}}{(1+|x|)^{d+2-\alpha}}dx,

where the last step we have used the property(since
h\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{d})
)

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|h(x)-h(y)\right|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2-\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\nabla h\|_{\infty}^{2}\cdot\int_{\{y\in\mathds{R}^{d};|y-x|\leqslant 1\}}\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{|y-x|^{d+2-\alpha}}dz

\displaystyle+4\|h\|_{\infty}^{2}\cdot\int_{\{y\in\mathds{R}^{d};|y-x|>1\}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d+2-\alpha}}dz\leqslant c_{16},\ \forall x\in B(0,3R_{0}),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|h(x)-h(y)\right|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2-\alpha}}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\|h\|_{\infty}^{2}\cdot\left(\int_{\{y\in\mathds{R}^{d};|y|\leqslant 2R_{0}\}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d+2-\alpha}}dy\right)\leqslant c_{17}(1+|x|)^{-d-2+\alpha},\ \forall\ x\notin B(0,3R_{0}).

By change of variable we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|H_{jl}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)-H_{jl}\left(\frac{y}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\right|^{2}|h(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2-\alpha}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon_{m}^{d-(2-\alpha)}\|h\|_{\infty}^{2}\int_{B\left(0,\frac{2R_{0}}{\varepsilon_{m}}\right)}\left(\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|H_{jl}\left(x;\omega\right)-H_{jl}\left(y;\omega\right)\right|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2-\alpha}}dy\right)dx

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{m}^{-(2-\alpha)}\varepsilon_{m}^{d}\|h\|_{\infty}^{2}\int_{B\left(0,\frac{2R_{0}}{\varepsilon_{m}}\right)}\left(\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|\tilde{H}_{jl}(\tau_{x+z}\omega)-\tilde{H}_{jl}(\tau_{x}\omega)\right|^{2}}{|z|^{d+2-\alpha}}dz\right)dx.

According to ergodic theorem we derive

(3.8)

\begin{split}&\quad\lim_{m\to\infty}\left|B\left(0,\frac{2R_{0}}{\varepsilon_{m}}\right)\right|^{-1}\int_{B\left(0,\frac{2R_{0}}{\varepsilon_{m}}\right)}\left(\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|\tilde{H}_{jl}(\tau_{x+z}\omega)-\tilde{H}_{jl}(\tau_{x}\omega)\right|^{2}}{|z|^{d+2-\alpha}}dz\right)dx\\
&=\mathds{E}\left[\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|\tilde{H}_{jl}(\tau_{z}\omega)-\tilde{H}_{jl}(\omega)\right|^{2}}{|z|^{d+2-\alpha}}dz\right]\leqslant\|\tilde{H}_{jl}\|_{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}^{2},\end{split}

which implies immediately that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|H_{jl}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)-H_{jl}\left(\frac{y}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\right|^{2}|h(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2-\alpha}}dxdy\leqslant c_{18}(\omega)\varepsilon_{m}^{-(2-\alpha)},

where we also use the fact
\|\tilde{H}_{jl}\|_{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}^{2}\leqslant\|\tilde{H}_{jl}\|_{1}^{2}<\infty
and
c_{18}(\omega)

is a positive constant which may depend on
\omega
.

Using ergodic theorem we have

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{m\to\infty}\int_{B(0,2R_{0})}\frac{\left|H_{jl}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\right|^{2}}{(1+|x|)^{d+2-\alpha}}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{19}\varlimsup_{m\to\infty}\int_{B(0,2R_{0})}\left|\tilde{H}_{jl}\left(\tau_{\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}}}\omega\right)\right|^{2}dx=c_{19}|B(0,2R_{0})|\cdot\mathds{E}[|\tilde{H}_{jl}|^{2}].

And by direct computation we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\int_{B(0,2R_{0})^{c}}\frac{\left|H_{jl}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\right|^{2}}{(1+|x|)^{d+2-\alpha}}dx=\frac{1}{|B(0,1)|}\int_{B(0,2R_{0})^{c}}\left(\int_{B(0,1)}\frac{\left|H_{jl}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\right|^{2}}{(1+|x|)^{d+2-\alpha}}dy\right)dx

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{20}\int_{B(0,2R_{0})^{c}}\int_{B(0,1)}\frac{\left|H_{jl}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)-H_{jl}\left(\frac{y}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\right|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2-\alpha}}dydx

\displaystyle\quad+c_{20}\int_{B(0,1)}\left|H_{jl}\left(\frac{y}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\right|^{2}\left(\int_{B(0,2R_{0})^{c}}\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{d+2-\alpha}}dx\right)dy

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{21}\varepsilon_{m}^{-(2-\alpha)}\varepsilon_{m}^{d}\int_{B\left(0,\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{m}}\right)}\left(\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|\tilde{H}_{jl}(\tau_{x}\omega)-\tilde{H}_{jl}(\tau_{y}\omega)\right|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2-\alpha}}dx\right)dy+c_{21}\int_{B(0,1)}\left|\tilde{H}_{jl}\left(\tau_{\frac{y}{\varepsilon_{m}}}\omega\right)\right|^{2}dy,

where in the last step we have used the change of variable.
Therefore based on this estimate and applying the same arguments of (
3.8
) we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,2R_{0})^{c}}\frac{\left|H_{jl}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m}};\omega\right)\right|^{2}}{(1+|x|)^{d+2-\alpha}}dx\leqslant c_{22}(\omega)\varepsilon_{m}^{-(2-\alpha)}.

Hence combining all above estimates together yields that for every
m\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{2-\alpha}\left|\mathscr{F}\left(H_{jl}^{\varepsilon_{m}}(\cdot;\omega)h(\cdot)\right)(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi\leqslant c_{23}(\omega)\varepsilon_{m}^{-(2-\alpha)}.

According to this and (
3.7
) together we derive

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{m}^{2-\alpha}\left|\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{l}}\left(H_{jl}^{\varepsilon_{m}}\left(\cdot;\omega\right)h(\cdot)\right)(x)\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x_{j}}u_{\lambda,f}^{\varepsilon_{m},\omega}(x)dx\right|\leqslant c_{24}(\omega)\varepsilon_{m}^{1-\alpha/2}.

This, along with (
3.6
) yields (
3.5
) and we have finished the proof.

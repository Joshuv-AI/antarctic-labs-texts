Affine Hecke and Schur algebras of type A without a square root of q
Rose Berry

Abstract
We provide an affine cellular structure on the extended affine Hecke algebra and affine q q -Schur algebra of type A n − 1 A_{n-1} that is defined over ℤ ⁡ [ q ± 1 ] \mathbb{Z}\left[q^{\pm 1}\right] , that is, without an adjoined q 1 2 q^{\frac{1}{2}} . This is with an eye to applications in the representation theory of GL n ​ ( F ) \mathrm{GL}_{n}(F) for a p p -adic field F F over coefficient rings in which p p is invertible but does not have a square root, which have been a topic of recent interest. This is achieved via a renormalisation of the known affine cellular structure over ℤ ⁡ [ q ± 1 2 ] \mathbb{Z}\left[q^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}\right] at each left and right cell, which is chosen to ensure that the diagonal intersections remain subalgebras and that the left and right cells remain isomorphic. We furthermore show that the affine cellular structure on the Schur algebra has idempotence properties which imply finite global dimension, an important ingredient for the applications to representations of p p -adic groups.



1
Introduction

The extended affine Hecke algebra of type
\tilde{A}_{n-1}
appears throughout representation theory. Most notably, its complex modules are equivalent to the principal block of the category of smooth complex representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
for a
p
-adic field
F
(
[
3
]
), and its anti-spherical module is isomorphic to the Grothendieck group of the principal block of rational representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
for
k
algebraically closed of characteristic
p
, as well as to the Grothendieck group of the principal block of representations of the affine quantum group
U(\hat{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n})
at a
p
-th root of unity (
[
1
]
).

In extending these applications, the affine
q
-Schur algebra naturally arises. It is a quotient of both the group algebra of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
(
[
22
]
), and a quotient of
U(\hat{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n})
(
[
10
]
), in both cases via a double centraliser relation with the Hecke algebra. In the former, it describes a subcategory of the principal block of smooth representations over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
l\neq p
, and can be dg enhanced to describe the derived category of the whole block (
[
2
]
), while in the latter it can be used to give a cell structure on the modified quantum group (
[
19
]
).

Both the Hecke and Schur algebras are defined over the integers adjoined a single abstract parameter
q
. However, traditionally both an inverse
q^{-1}
and a square root
q^{\frac{1}{2}}
are adjoined as well. This is because much of the theory, such as the construction of Lusztig’s
a
-function (
[
16
]
) and the asymptotic algebra (
[
17
]
), require working over this extended ring. However, recent work in the study of
p
-adic groups (
[
7
,
11
,
6
]
) has considered coefficient rings in which
q
does not have a square root. It thus makes sense to ask if the theory of Hecke and Schur algebras can be defined over this larger class of rings.

Specifically, we seek to define an affine cellular structure in the sense of
[
13
]
on both the Hecke and Schur algebras over
\mathbb{Z}\left[q^{\pm 1}\right]
, and furthermore show that the latter satisfies additional idempotence properties that imply finite global dimension. This property is in ingredient in the author’s proof in
[
2
]
that the derived principal block is equivalent to a dg enhanced Schur algebra, and so is a step in generalising this result to these more general coefficient rings. We follow the previous constructions of an affine cellular structure for both algebras (
[
13
,
4
,
5
]
), dealing with obstructions as they arise. In the case of finite type
A
, the Schur algebra is already known to be quasihereditary, and hence cellular, without
q^{\frac{1}{2}}
(
[
8
]
). This is proven by normalising the usual isomorphisms between left cells in a two-sided cell to be defined integrally, and a similar argument forms a key step of our reasoning.

The extra difficulty posed on the affine setting is finding a suitable integral normalisation of the asymptotic algebra. The typical normalisation is defined using the normalised Kazhdan-Lusztig basis, which is only defined with an adjoined
q^{\frac{1}{2}}
. The module spanned by the unnormalised Kazhdan-Lusztig basis, which is defined integrally, is not a priori closed under multiplication. However, it turns out a closely related normalisation is closed, and thus provides a candidate for the asymptotic algebra in the affine cellular structure. Checking this amounts to understanding the lengths of the elements in each two-sided cell, and how they vary under multiplication.

Both above normalisation results depend on the existence of the adjoined inverse
q^{-1}
. It thus seems unlikely, at least with the existing approach, that this can also be removed. However, this is no great loss, as the relevant applications to
p
-adic groups only make sense in settings where
q^{-1}
exists. Nonetheless, as cells themselves can be defined without
q^{-1}
, it would be interesting to see what structure they can be endowed with in full generality.

Care is taken throughout to distinguish between results proven for the affine Hecke algebra and those proven for the extended affine Hecke algebra, and to ensure that our constructions are correct in the generality we have stated them. We also try to present a complete overview of the construction, which was previously scattered throughout a considerable number of papers, which use differing conventions for the various objects within. Of particular note, we use the version of the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis that is a nonnegative linear combination of the standard basis, and we give the construction of the Schur algebra and its Kazhdan-Lusztig basis in terms of Coxeter group combinatorics instead of infinite matrices and perverse sheaves.

Section two recalls the definitions and basic structure of the extended affine Weyl group, Hecke algebra, and Schur algebra. Section 3 recalls the definition of the normalised and unnormalised Kazhdan-Lusztig bases and of left, right, and two-sided cells, as well as existing results on their partial order and number. In Section 4 we recall the construction of the usual isomorphism between left cells in a two-sided cell, and show how it can be normalised in such a way as to be defined integrally. In Section 5 we recall the construction of the asymptotic algebra, as well as its known form, and then give an alternate normalisation which we prove is an integral subalgebra. In Section 6 we combine these two results with a final result, showing that left cells are a bimodule under the right action of our newly normalised asymptotic algebra, to give an affine cellular structure on the Hecke and Schur algebras. We furthermore show that the structure on the Schur algebra has idempotence properties that imply it has finite global dimension.

\Gamma\cap\iota\left(\Delta\right)\rightarrow\Gamma^{\lambda}\cap\iota\left(\Gamma^{\lambda}\right)
.

Similarly, let
\phi_{\iota\left(\Psi\right)}=\iota\phi_{\Psi}\iota
, and let
\phi_{\Psi\cap\iota\left(\Phi\right)}=\phi_{\Psi}\phi_{\iota\left(\Phi\right)}
. Again by
4.5
, these are
\mathbb{Z}_{\bullet}
-module homomorphisms
\iota\left(\Psi\right)\rightarrow\iota\left(\Psi^{\lambda}\right)
and
\Psi\cap\iota\left(\Phi\right)\rightarrow\Psi^{\lambda}\cap\iota\left(\Psi^{\lambda}\right)
respectively.

So far, we only know that this is a
\mathbb{Z}_{\bullet}
-module homomorphism, not an isomorphism of left ideals.

Lemma 4.12
.

We shall need the following properties:

1.

If
q^{-1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\bullet}
, then the maps in
Definition

4.11
are isomorphisms.

2.

If
\Psi
and
\Phi
correspond to
(\Gamma,\varnothing)
and
(\Delta,\varnothing)
respectively via
3.15
, then
\Psi\cap\iota\left(\Phi\right)=\Gamma\cap\iota\left(\Delta\right)
and
\phi_{\Psi\cap\iota\left(\Phi\right)}=\phi_{\Gamma\cap\iota\left(\Delta\right)}
.

3.

\iota\phi_{\Psi\cap\iota\left(\Phi\right)}=\phi_{\Phi\cap\iota\left(\Psi\right)}\iota
.

4.

If
q^{-1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\bullet}
, then
\phi_{\Psi_{\lambda}\cap\iota\left(\Phi\right)}^{-1}\phi_{\Psi\cap\iota\left(\Phi\right)}=\phi_{\Psi}
.

Proof.

The first claim follows from
4.4
and
4.10
. The second claim holds by definition of the various maps.

By
4.6
, we have that
\phi_{\Psi\cap\iota\left(\Phi\right)}=\phi_{\iota\left(\Phi\right)}\phi_{\Psi}
. Hence

\displaystyle\iota\phi_{\Psi\cap\iota\left(\Phi\right)}

\displaystyle=\iota\phi_{\Psi}\phi_{\iota\left(\Phi\right)}

\displaystyle=\iota\phi_{\Psi}\iota\phi_{\Phi}\iota

\displaystyle=\phi_{\iota\left(\Psi\right)}\phi_{\Phi}\iota

\displaystyle=\phi_{\Phi\cap\iota\left(\Psi\right)}\iota

which gives the third claim, and

\displaystyle\phi_{\Gamma_{\lambda}\cap\iota\left(\Phi\right)}^{-1}\phi_{\Psi\cap\iota\left(\Phi\right)}

\displaystyle=\left(\phi_{\Gamma_{\lambda}}\phi_{\Phi}^{-1}\right)^{-1}\phi_{\iota\left(\Phi\right)}\phi_{\Psi}

\displaystyle=\phi_{\Psi}

gives the fourth.
∎

Thus our maps are isomorphisms. To see they are homomorphisms of left ideals, we use the following well-known result over
\mathbb{Z}_{q^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}}
.

Lemma 4.13
.

Suppose
v,w\in D_{R}(s_{i})
satisfy
v\sim_{L}w
. Then
h_{u,v}^{w}=h_{u,v^{*}}^{w^{*}}
.

Proof.

We follow the proof of
[
23
, Proposition 1.4.4(b)]
, which proves a closely related result. Let
s\in\left\{s_{i},s_{i+1}\right\}
satisfy
v^{*}s<v^{*}
, and let
t\in\left\{s_{i},s_{i+1}\right\}\backslash\left\{s\right\}
. Then by
[
23
, Lemma 1.4.2(b)]
there exist elements
h_{x}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\displaystyle C_{v}C_{s}=C_{v^{*}}+\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}xs<x\\
xt<x\end{subarray}}}h_{x}C_{x}.

Multiplying on the left by
C_{u}
gives

\displaystyle C_{u}C_{v}C_{s}=\sum_{y\in W}\left(h_{u,v^{*}}^{y}+\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}xs<x\\
xt<x\end{subarray}}}h_{x}h_{u,x}^{y}\right)C_{y}.

However, we also have

\displaystyle C_{u}C_{v}C_{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in W}h_{u,v}^{z}C_{z}C_{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{y,z\in W}h_{u,v}^{z}h_{z,s}^{y}C_{y}

and so equating coefficients gives

\displaystyle h_{u,v^{*}}^{y}+\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}xs<x\\
xt<x\end{subarray}}}h_{x}h_{u,x}^{y}=\sum_{z\in W}h_{u,v}^{z}h_{z,s}^{y}.

Put
y=w^{*}
. Since
v\sim_{L}w
by assumption, we have by
4.5
that
v^{*}\sim_{L}w^{*}
. Also by
4.5
we have
v\sim_{R}v^{*}
. Now, if
h_{x}\neq 0
then
x\leq_{R}v
, which, together with the previous relation, gives
x\leq_{R}v^{*}
. Furthermore, if
h_{u,x}^{w^{*}}\neq 0
then
w^{*}\leq_{L}x
, which together with the first relation gives
v^{*}\leq_{L}x
. Combining these gives that
v^{*}\sim_{LR}x
, and then by
[
17
, Corollary 1.9(c)]
these last two relations gives
v^{*}\sim_{L}x
. Hence, by
[
12
, Proposition 2.4]
,
R(v^{*})=R(x)
. But by assumption
s\notin R(v^{*})
, and if
h_{x}\neq 0
then
s\in R(x)
, a contradiction. Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}xs<x\\
xt<x\end{subarray}}}h_{x}h_{u,x}^{w^{*}}=0.

Now fix
z\in W
. By
4.5
again we have
w\sim_{R}w^{*}
. Hence if
h_{u,v}^{z}\neq 0
then
z\leq_{L}v
and so
z\leq_{L}w
, and if
h_{z,s}^{w*}\neq 0
then
w*\leq_{R}z
and so
w\leq_{R}z
. The same logic as the previous case then gives that
z\sim_{L}w\sim_{L}v
, and so
R(z)=R(w)=R(v)
. Thus
t\in R(z)
and
s\notin R(z)
, so
z^{*}
exists and furthermore
z^{*}s<z^{*}
. Thus by
[
23
, Lemma 1.4.2(b)]
again there exist elements
h_{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\displaystyle C_{z}C_{s}=C_{z^{*}}+\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}xs<x\\
xt<x\end{subarray}}}h_{x}^{\prime}C_{x}.

In particular, as
s\in R(w^{*})
, we must have
h_{w^{*}}^{\prime}=0
. Thus if
C_{z,s}^{w^{*}}\neq 0
then we must have
w^{*}=z^{*}
, and furthermore in this case
\tilde{C}_{z,s}^{w*}=1
. But if
w^{*}=z^{*}
then
w=z
. Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in W}h_{u,v}^{z}h_{z,s}^{y}=h_{u,v}^{w}.

∎

We now translate this result to
\mathbb{Z}_{\bullet}
, which gives us what we need.

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
\Psi
and
\Gamma
be left cells in
\mathcal{S}_{\bullet}^{\lambda}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\bullet}^{\lambda}
respectively. Then
\phi_{\Psi}
and
\phi_{\Gamma}
) are homomorphisms of left
\mathcal{S}_{\bullet}/\mathcal{S}_{\bullet}^{>\lambda}
-modules and
\mathcal{H}_{\bullet}/\mathcal{H}_{\bullet}^{>\lambda}
-modules respectively.

Proof.

By construction of
\phi_{\Psi}
and
\phi_{\Gamma}
, and by
Lemma

3.9
, it suffices to show for all

that if
v,w\in D_{R}(s)
then

, and also that if
i\in z
then
\tilde{h}_{u,v}^{w}=\tilde{h}_{u,v\omega^{i}}^{w\omega^{i}}

3.6

\displaystyle q^{\frac{l(w)-l(w*)+1}{2}}\tilde{h}_{u,v}^{w}

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{l(u)+l(v)-l(w*)+1}{2}}h_{u,v*}^{w*}

∎

5
A renormalised integral lattice in the asymptotic algebra

The piece of the affine cellular structure is a commutative
\mathbb{Z}_{q^{\pm 1}}
-algebra. Over
\mathbb{Z}_{q^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}}
, this is provided by the asymptotic algebra. In this section, we provide a suitable candidate over
\mathbb{Z}_{q^{\pm 1}}
. We start by recalling the construction of the asymptotic algebra. For this, we must in turn first recall the construction of Lusztig’s
a
-function.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
n_{u,v}^{w}
be the greatest power of
q^{\frac{1}{2}}
occurring in
h_{u,v}^{w}
. Similarly, let
n_{uRv}^{QwP}
be the greatest power of
q^{\frac{1}{2}}
occurring in
h_{uRv}^{QwP}
.

Let
a(w)=\max\left\{n_{u,v}^{w}\middle|u,v\in W\right\}
, or infinity if no such maximum exists.

We recall the properties of
a
that we need.

Proposition 5.2
.

This is integer-valued:

1.

a(w)
is finite for all
w\in W
, and moreover is bounded by
l(w_{S^{\fino}})
.

2.

\displaystyle\max\left\{n_{uRv}^{QwP}+l(w_{R})-l(w_{P})\middle|R\subseteq S^{\fino},u\in\prescript{Q}{}{W}^{R},v\in\prescript{R}{}{W}^{P}\right\}

\displaystyle=a(w)-l(w_{P}).

3.

If
v\leq_{LR}w
then
a(v)\geq a(w)
. In particular,
a(\omega^{i}w\omega^{j})=a(w)
.

Proof.

The first claim is
[
16
, Corollary 7.3]
for
w\in W^{\affo}
. That
a(w)=a(w\omega^{i})
follows from
Lemma

3.6
, and from this the first claim follows in general. The second claim is
[
19
, Proposition 3.8(d)]
, noting that Definition 3.1 therein has a missing factor of
\frac{1}{2}
, and the correct formula is the one we give here. The third claim is
[
16
, Theorem 5.4]
in the affine case, and the general case then follows from
a(w)=a(w\omega^{i})
and
Lemma

3.12
. ∎

We also introduce the objects
\gamma
, which will turn out to be the structure coefficients of the asymptotic algebra, and collect some of their basic properties.

Definition 5.3
.

Write
\gamma_{u,v}^{w}
for the coefficient of the

-power term in

\gamma_{uRv}^{QwP}

-power term in

Lemma 5.4
.

\gamma_{uRv}^{QwP}=\gamma_{u,v}^{w}
.

Proof.

This follows from
Lemma

3.9
together with
5.2
(2).
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\in W^{\affo}
. Then
\gamma_{u^{\prime}\omega^{i},v^{\prime}\omega^{j}}^{w}
is zero unless

for some

, in which case
\gamma_{u^{\prime}\omega^{i},v^{\prime}\omega^{j}}^{w}=\gamma_{u^{\prime},\omega^{i}v^{\prime}\omega^{-i}}^{w^{\prime}}

\gamma_{\omega^{i}u^{\prime}\omega^{-i},\omega^{i}v^{\prime}\omega^{-i}}^{\omega^{i}w^{\prime}\omega^{-i}}=\gamma_{u^{\prime},v^{\prime}}^{w^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

The first part is
Lemma

3.6
together with
5.2
(3). The second part is
Lemma

3.6
again together with
5.2
(3).
∎

Using
a
, we now define the asymptotic algebra of a two-sided cell as a certain
\mathbb{Z}
-subquotient of the cell.

Definition 5.6
.

For
w\in W
, write
\hat{C}_{w}=q^{-\frac{a(w)}{2}}C_{w}
. Similarly, for
P,Q\subseteq S^{\fino}
and

Write
\mathbb{Z}_{q^{-\frac{1}{2}}}=\mathbb{Z}\left[q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right]

\mathbb{Z}_{q^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}}

q^{-\frac{1}{2}}

Let
\mathcal{H}_{q^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{\lambda}
and
\mathcal{S}_{q^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{\lambda}

\mathbb{Z}_{q^{-\frac{1}{2}}}
-span of the
\hat{C}_{w}
and the
\hat{C}_{QP}^{w}

Lemma 5.7
.

\mathcal{H}_{q^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{\lambda}
and
\mathcal{S}_{q^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{\lambda}
are free
\mathbb{Z}_{q^{-\frac{1}{2}}}
-subalgebras of
\mathcal{H}_{q^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}}^{\lambda}
and
\mathcal{S}_{q^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}}^{\lambda}

Let
a=a(w)
for any
\tilde{C}_{w}\in\mathcal{H}_{\bullet}^{\lambda}
, noting that by
5.2
(3) it is independent of choice of

. Then, for
\tilde{C}_{u},\tilde{C}_{v},\tilde{C}_{w}\in\mathcal{H}_{\bullet}^{\lambda}

Definition 5.8
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\lambda}=\mathcal{H}_{q^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{\lambda}/q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{H}_{q^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{\lambda}

\mathcal{H}_{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\bullet}

Lemma 5.9
.

\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\lambda}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}^{\lambda}

\mathbb{Z}
-algebras with bases
\left\{t_{w}\middle|\tilde{C}_{w}\in\mathcal{H}_{\bullet}^{\lambda}\right\}
and

is the image of
\hat{C}_{w}
and
t_{QP}^{w}
is the image of
\hat{C}_{QP}^{w}

\mathbb{Z}
-algebras
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{\infty}

That they are free

-algebras with the given basis is immediate by construction. Write

for any

\displaystyle q^{-\frac{a}{2}}h_{u,v}^{w}\cong\gamma_{u,v}^{w}\ \left(\text{mod}\ q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{Z}_{q^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle q^{l(w_{R})-\frac{a}{2}}h_{uRv}^{QwP}\cong\gamma_{uRv}^{QwP}\ \left(\text{mod}\ q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{Z}_{q^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\right)

5.4
.
∎

We will need the following key property.

Proposition 5.10
.

If
\gamma_{u,v}^{w}\neq 0
then

.

Proof.

[
17
, Theorem 1.9(a)]
gives the case where

. In the general case, if
\gamma_{u^{\prime}\omega^{i},v^{\prime}\omega^{j}}^{w}\neq 0

\gamma_{u^{\prime},\omega^{i}v^{\prime}\omega^{-i}}^{w^{\prime}}\neq 0
for

.
∎

We also record the following easy consequence.

Corollary 5.11
.

\mathcal{H}_{\infty}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash n}\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\lambda}
is the free
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra with basis

and

\displaystyle=\sum_{a(x^{\prime\prime})=a(v^{\prime\prime})}h_{u^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}}^{y^{\prime}}\gamma_{v^{\prime\prime},w^{\prime\prime}}^{x^{\prime\prime}}\delta_{i+j+k,l}

Now, note that if

But by

\displaystyle h_{uPv}^{Qx\varnothing}\gamma_{xw}^{y}=p_{P}^{-1}q^{\frac{l(w_{P})}{2}}h_{u,v}^{x}\gamma_{x,w}^{y}

\displaystyle h_{uPx}^{Qy\varnothing}\gamma_{vw}^{x}=p_{P}^{-1}q^{\frac{l(w_{P})}{2}}h_{u,x}^{y}\gamma_{v,w}^{x}.

Hence, to finish the proof, it suffices to show that we can restrict the sums to only

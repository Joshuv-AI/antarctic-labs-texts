Harnessing m6A-regulated cholesterol biosynthesis helps impede tumor burden and stemness in high-garde serous ovarian carcinoma mesenchymal phenotype
Authors: Aravindan Narayanan, Kiran Dhenge, Sharmila A Bapat
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPost-transcriptional RNA modifications have emerged as critical regulators of stemness, cellular plasticity, adaptation to stress and transformation. Amongst these the N6-methyladenosine (m6A) modification orchestrates a wide range of physiological processes; yet its contribution to metabolic regulation remains poorly addressed. In the present study, differential proteomics revealed enrichment of core components of the m6A machinery, viz. WTAP (writer) and IGF2BP3 (reader) in stem-like cells of the mesenchymal subtype of High-Grade Serous Ovarian Carcinoma (HGSC). Intriguingly, components of de novo cholesterol biosynthesis were also enriched. In investigating the suggested link between m6A regulation and sterol metabolism, we established a m6A-dependent stabilization of transcripts encoding rate-limiting de novo cholesterol biosynthetic enzymes by IGF2BP3 towards sustaining cholesterol production. Disruption of this cross-regulation by pharmacological inhibition impaired cell survival, self-renewal, and migration. Analyses of datasets from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) assigned a clinical significance to this regulatory axis through correlation of elevated expression of de novo cholesterol biosynthetic genes with poor progression-free survival of serous ovarian carcinoma patients. IGF2BP3 knock down, and chemical blockade of de novo cholesterol biosynthesis, both alone or in combination, achieved attenuated disease progression, in vivo. Effectively, our study links RNA modifications with metabolic reprogramming in the HGSC mesenchymal subtype through delineation of a m6A–IGF2BP3–cholesterol biosynthesis axis-mediated regulation of tumor cell stemness and aggression.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s10020-025-01413-0.Keywords: Mesenchymal subtype, High-grade serous ovarian carcinoma, M6A RNA modification, De novo cholesterol biosynthesis, Metabolic reprogramming

Introduction

Among post-transcriptional modifications in eukaryotic mRNAs, N6-methyladenosine (m6A) is most abundant and well-characterized (Huang et al. 2020). Over the past decade, m6A has emerged as a key regulator of mRNA fate, affecting stability, nuclear export, translation, and degradation, processes essential for cellular homeostasis, stem cell function, differentiation, and tumorigenesis (Wang et al. 2015). The m6A mark is deposited co-transcriptionally by a methyltransferase complex (MTC) with METTL3 as the catalytic core and METTL14, WTAP, VIRMA, RBM15, and ZC3H13 as regulatory subunits. This modification is enriched in coding regions and 3′ untranslated regions (3′UTRs), typically at DRACH (D = A/G/U; R = A/G; H = A/C/U) motifs (Wang et al. 2015). Its effects are mediated by reader proteins, including YTH domain proteins (YTHDC1-2, YTHDF1-3) that regulate stability and translation, IGF2BP1-3 that stabilize transcripts and hnRNPs facilitating RNA structural remodelling (Du et al. 2016; Huang et al. 2018; Liu et al. 2015). The modification is reversible via demethylases such as FTO and ALKBH5. Dysregulation of these factors is linked to cancer initiation, progression, metastasis, and therapy resistance (Huang et al. 2020).

Cholesterol is an essential lipid molecule involved in membrane structure, intracellular trafficking, and signal transduction. Perturbations in cholesterol homeostasis have emerged as a hallmark of cancer (Hanahan 2022). The de novo biosynthetic pathway is transcriptionally governed by sterol regulatory element-binding protein 2 (SREBP2), which activates genes such as HMGCR, FDFT1, and SQLE through binding to sterol-response elements (Xiao et al. 2023). Cholesterol generated through this pathway integrates into plasma membranes, constituting lipid rafts, which are critical for maintaining cancer cell plasticity and resistance phenotypes (Ehmsen et al. 2019). Given its central role in tumor biology, targeting de novo cholesterol biosynthesis has emerged as a promising therapeutic strategy. Statins, inhibitors of HMGCR, have demonstrated anti-tumor activity across several malignancies via induction of apoptosis and inhibition of metastasis (Longo et al. 2020). In parallel, repurposing of agents such as zoledronic acid, originally developed for bone disorders, has shown efficacy in preclinical models of various cancers (Göbel et al. 2016).

High-grade serous ovarian carcinoma (HGSC) remains a very aggressive disease marked with heterogeneity; we had previously resolved the latter to stratify tumors into three distinct, clinically validated molecular subtypes, viz. epithelial (E), mesenchymal (M), and mixed (The Cancer Genome Atlas Research Network 2011; Gardi et al. 2014; Kamble et al. 2019). In a subsequent study, we further demonstrated that these tumor subtypes encompass five distinct cellular phenotypes in vitro, viz. E, intermediate-epithelial (iE), epithelial-mesenchymal hybrid (E/M), intermediate-mesenchymal (iM), and M phenotypes (Varankar et al. 2020). A recent label-free quantification (LFQ)-based proteomic study on these cellular phenotypes revealed common and differential functional pathways in stem-like cells across the different phenotypes (Narayanan et al. 2025). In the present study, we investigated the concurrent enrichment of key de novo cholesterol biosynthesis enzymes and m6A regulators (WTAP and IGF2BP3), exclusively in the mesenchymal subtype (iM and M). While both pathways are individually recognized as key drivers of tumor progression, their functional cross-talk/cross-regulation in heterogeneous tumors, remains largely unexplored. We hence demonstrate that IGF2BP3 promotes the m6A-dependent stabilization of transcripts encoding key de novo cholesterol biosynthesis enzymes, thereby contributing to the survival and stemness of mesenchymal HGSC cells. Disruption of this pathway significantly reduced tumor burden in vivo, underscoring a novel regulatory crosstalk with potential therapeutic implications.

Materials and methods

Cell lines and cell culture condition

HGSC cell lines (A4, earlier established in the lab, 16; OVMZ6 kind gift from Prof. Viktor Magdalen) were maintained at 37 °C, 5% CO₂ in a humidified incubator. Minimal Essential Medium (MEM;Gibco) + 5% FBS + 1% non-essential amino acid (Gibco) was used to maintain A4 and OVMZ6 was maintained in Dulbecco’s Modified Essential Medium (DMEM;Gibco) + 10% FBS. Both cell lines were authenticated by short tandem repeat (STR) profiling and also confirmed to be mycoplasma-free. For serum-fed (+ S) and serum starved (SS) condition, cells were replaced with complete media or serum-free media, respectively, 24 h post seeding.

Plasmid construction and lentivirus transfection

shRNA oligonucleotide sequences against IGF2BP3 were designed using The RNAi Consortium (TRC; https://portals.broadinstitute.org/gpp/public/) and cloned into pLKO.1-TRC1 vector using T4 DNA ligase (TAKARA; #2011B). Recombinant constructs were subsequently transformed into chemically competent DH5α E. coli cells and plasmid DNA was extracted using the Nucleospin Plasmid Isolation Kit (MACHEREY–NAGEL, #740,588.250). Sequences encoding shRNAs were confirmed by Sanger DNA sequencing. Lentiviral particles were generated and concentrated using HEK293T cells using standard packaging systemns and Lenti-X Concentrator (Takara, #631,231). A4 cells were transduced with the lentiviral particles in the presence of Polybrene (Sigma-Aldrich, #TR-1003), Following transduction, stable knockdown cells (A4KD-sh1/sh2-IGF2BP3) were selected using puromycin (Gibco™, #A1113803) at a final concentration of 1 μg/mL.

Cell viability assays and IC50 determination

Cell viability and IC₅₀ values of cholesterol biosynthesis inhibitors (Lovastatin-CALBIOCHEM, #428,185, Zoledronic acid- Sigma Aldrich, #SML0223, Alendronate-Sigma Aldrich, #) or METTL3 inhibitor (STM2457-Sigma Aldrich, #SML3360) on A4 and OVMZ6 cell lines were assessed using MTT (3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)−2,5-diphenyltetrazo lium bromide; Sigma-Aldrich, #M2128) as described earlier (Narayanan et al. 2025).

Wound healing assay

Wound healing assay was performed as described earlier (Varankar et al. 2020). Briefly, confluent monolayers of A4/A4EV/A4KD-sh1/sh2_IGF2BP3 cells cultured in 24-well plates were scratched using a sterile pipette tip to create a uniform wound. Cells were then incubated in serum-free medium ± inhibitors (Lovastatin, Zoledronic acid, Alendronate, STM2457). Time-lapse live-cell imaging was performed using an inverted laser-scanning confocal fluorescence microscope (Leica LPSS5) maintained at 37 °C in a 5% CO₂ atmosphere. Images were acquired at defined time intervals, and the wound-closure area was quantified using ImageJ software (v1.54f). For each condition, wound-closure measurements were normalized to the corresponding 0-h wound area.

Matrigel invasion assay

Cell invasion was evaluated using Matrigel-coated transwell inserts (8-µm pore size). Briefly, 10,000 A4, A4-EV, or A4KD-sh1/sh2_IGF2BP3 cells were resuspended in plain MEM and seeded in the upper chamber of transwells pre-coated with Matrigel (BD Matrigel Matrix, #354,234; 1:2 dilution with MEM). Inserts were placed in a 24-well plate containing MEM supplemented with serum in the lower chamber to serve as the chemoattractant. After 72 h of incubation at 37 °C, non-invading cells were removed, membranes were washed with PBS, fixed with 4% PFA, and stained with 0.1% crystal violet. Invading cells on the underside of the membrane were imaged using a Nikon Eclipse Ti2 microscope and quantified using ImageJ software (v1.54f).

Spheroid assay

For spheroid assay, 5000 A4/A4EV/A4KD-sh1/sh2_IGF2BP3 were seeded in an ultra-low attachment 96-well plate and cultured in 1% serum MEM ± treatments (Lovastatin, Zoledronic acid, Alendronate, STM2457 for 14 days. Images of spheroids formed were captured using Olympus FV3000 and area was calculated using ImageJ (v1.54f).

RNA extraction, c-DNA synthesis, qPCR and RNA half-life analysis

Total RNA was isolated using RNAiso Plus (TaKaRa, #9109), reverse-transcribed using the PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit (TaKaRa, #RR037A), and quantitative PCR was performed as previously described (Narayanan et al. 2024). RNA half-life was determined following actinomycin D (10 µg/ml; Sigma-Aldrich, #A1410) treatment (0, 2, 4 or 6 h) in SS vs + S conditions as described earlier (Ratnadiwakara and Änkö 2018). Transcript abundance was calculated by 2(−ΔCT) and fitted to non-linear regression to determine t₁/₂. Exon-exon spanning gene specific primers were used for the qPCR analysis; sequences will be provided on request.

Total-cholesterol levels and FILIPIN III staining

Total intracellular cholesterol levels were measured using a colorimetric assay (Abcam, #ab65359) according to the manufacturer’s protocol in 48 h + S and SS samples of A4 and OVMZ6 cells. Free cholesterol was visualized by staining cells with Filipin III (50 µg/mL, Sigma-Aldrich, #F4767). Fluorescence images were acquired using an Olympus FV3000 confocal microscope, and raw fluorescence intensity was quantified using ImageJ (v1.54f).

Global m6A quantification, m6A dot-blot and SRAMP for m6A site prediction

For quantification of global m6A abundance, total RNA was isolated from + S/SS A4 and OVMZ6 cells using the m6A RNA Methylation Quantification Colorimetric Kit (Abcam, #ab185912) according to the manufacturer's protocol. For dot blots, 400 ng mRNA (purified with Dynabeads, Invitrogen #61,012) was UV-crosslinked to Hybond-N + membranes, probed with anti-m⁶A antibody (Synaptic Systems, #202,003) and HRP-conjugated secondary (CST, #7074), blots were developed using Western Blot Hyper HRP Substrate (TaKaRa, #T7103A). Sequence-based N6- methyladenosine (m6A) modification site predictor (SRAMP: http://www.cuilab.cn/sramp/) was used to predict m6A modification sites in candidate cholesterol biosynthesis genes’ mRNA sequence.

Immunoblotting

Immunoblotting was performed as described earlier (Bapat et al. 2005), using primary antibodies against WTAP (Invitrogen, #PA5-27,764, 1:5000) and IGF2BP3 (Invitrogen, #A303-426A, 1:3000). Quantitative data presented are derived from triplicate measurements. Quantitative immunoblot data presented in the study were obtained from three independent biological replicates.

RIP-qPCR

m⁶A-RIP and IGF2BP3-RIP-qPCR were performed as described earlier (Dominissini et al. 2013; Gagliardi and Matarazzo 2016). Briefly, mRNA was isolated from 48 h A4-SS cells, sonicated, precipitated, air dried and incubated with 5ug of anti-m⁶A antibody (Synaptic System, #202,003 or IgG isotype control) for 4 °C for 2 h. On completion of incubation, RNA samples were mixed with pre-washed Protein G magnetic beads (25 µL; Invitrogen, #10003D), washed and eluted. RNA in eluate was precipitated, washed, air-dried and subjected to cDNA synthesis and targeted qPCR. For IGF2BP3 RIP-qPCR, 48 h A4SS cells cultured on 150 mm dishes were UV cross-linked (1200 mJ/cm2), harvested, washed, centrifuged, and resuspended in lysis buffer. Cells were sonicated and was further mixed with 2 µg IGF2BP3 or IgG control (ThermoFisher, #A303-426A, #02–6102 respectively) and conjugated with Protein G magnetic beads (ThermoFisher, #88,848). Lysate-antibody/bead mixture were incubated at 4 °C overnight with rotation, following which, a portion of beads from RIP and IgG was stored to run on the western blot and rest were resuspended in PBS and incubated with Proteinase K at 37 °C for 20 min. Recovery of input and co-immunoprecipitated RNAs were performed using TRiZol method and eluted RNAs were subjected to cDNA synthesis followed by qPCR analysis.

Survival analysis, xenograft generation and drug evaluation

Transcript-level expression patterns of candidate de novo cholesterol biosynthesis enzymes in normal versus tumor tissues were assessed using TNMplot v2 (Bartha and Győrffy 2021) that integrates large-scale transcriptomic datasets, including RNA-seq data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) for adult tumors and TARGET for paediatric tumors, normal tissue references from the Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) project, and manually curated microarray datasets (> 33,000 samples) from the NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO). This unified platform enables robust cross-comparison of gene expression between malignant and corresponding normal tissues. Clinical relevance of elevated expression of key cholesterol biosynthesis enzymes was evaluated as progression-free survival (PFS) across all grades and stages (n = 1104) of serous ovarian carcinoma patient datasets curated from multiple repositories, including TCGA, GEO, and the European Genome–Phenome Archive (EGA) using a Kaplan–Meier Plotter online survival analysis tool (Győrffy 2024). The gene correlation (Pearson) heatmap was generated in RStudio (v4.5.2) using protein expression profiles of m⁶A regulators and key de novo cholesterol biosynthesis enzymes from clinical samples in the TCGA-OV proteomic dataset (Proteomic Data Commons, PDC000114; Ovarian Serous Cystadenocarcinoma; n = 84). All animal experimental procedures were conducted following approval from the Institutional Animal Ethics Committee (IAEC, project no. B-493). Female NOD/SCID mice, aged 6–8 weeks, were bred and maintained under standard conditions at the BRIC-NCCS Experimental Animal Facility. Subcutaneous xenograft models were established by injecting 2.5 × 10^6 A4/A4-EV/A4-KD cells into the flank regions. Mice were randomized into vehicle control or Zoledronic acid (100 µg/kg; Sigma-Aldrich, #SML0223) groups (n ≥ 3 per group), and treatments commenced 14 days post-injection. Tumor growth was monitored on Days 7, 14, and 21 post-treatment initiation by measuring tumor volume using the formula: 0.5 × length × (width)2.

Statistics

All experiments were carried out as at least three independent replicates. For comparisons between two groups, statistical significance was assessed using a two-tailed Student’s t-test, whereas differences between three or more groups were evaluated by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Results are expressed as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Statistical significance thresholds were set at *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, and ***p < 0.001. All data visualization and statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism software (version 8.4.2).

Data availability

The proteomic dataset used in the study is available in ProteomeXchange Consortium under the data identifier PXD062888. All other data generated in this study are available in the article and its Supplementary Data.

Results

Serum starvtion-induced stem-like state in mesenchymal HGSC phenotypes exhibit enrcihment of m⁶A regulators and de novo cholesterol biosynthesis

Differential expression of m⁶A pathway components were evident across the E to M spectrum of HGSC phenotypes [epithelial (E), intermediate mesenchymal (iM) and mesenchymal (M)] that correlate with the three clinical subtypes (E, M and Mixed; Fig. 1a; 11- 14). E states on serum starvation upregulate an indirect m⁶A regulator HNRNPC, that predominantly influences trans-splicing through structural interactions with m⁶A-modified transcripts (the m⁶A-switch; 5), while M states expressed direct m⁶A regulators WTAP (m⁶A writer complex component) and IGF2BP3 (m⁶A reader). Subsequently, we focused on understanding direct m⁶A regulation in the M phenotype (using A4 and OVMZ6 cell lines as representative models). Validation of differential protein expression of WTAP and IGF2BP3 on serum deprivation was particularly pronounced in A4 cells, yet surprisingly m⁶A RNA abundance did not correlate well, which could be due to masking of m⁶A signal in the total RNA pool, given that m⁶A is more prevalent in mRNAs over non-coding RNAs (Fig. 1b; Supplementary Fig. 1a). We hence performed a m⁶A dot-blot of isolated mRNAs that indeed validated increased association of m⁶A marks following serum deprivation (Fig. 1c; Supplementary Fig. 1b).

Concurrently, proteins involved in de novo cholesterol biosynthesis (FDPS, MVK, MVD, IDI1, PMVK, SQLE, LSS, and DHCR7) were indicated to be selectively enriched in the SS-M phenotypes, and transcript expression of these were validated (Figs.1d,e,f). While biochemical assays for total cellular cholesterol did not correlate well with protein/transcript data, levels of free intra-cellular cholesterol increased in A4-SS FILIPIN III dye stained cells over controls; OVMZ6 cells did not display the same extent of change (Fig.1 g-i,1 g-ii; Supplementary Fig.1c,1d).

IGF2BP3 regulates expression and stability of m⁶A-modified transcripts de novo cholesterol biosynthesis in HGSC mesenchymal states following serum starvation

To explore if enrichment of m⁶A-mediated regulation and de novo cholesterol biosynthesis upon serum deprivation suggests cross-talks between the two pathways, we performed a m⁶A RNA immunoprecipitation followed by gene-specific qPCR (RIP-qPCR) in serum deprived A4 cells, which indeed validated the presence of m⁶A marks in the transcripts of the eight de novo cholesterol biosynthesis candidate genes (Fig. 2a). Several “high”- to “very high-confidence” m6A consensus sites were also predicted as DRACH containing motifs (D = A/G/U, R = A/G, H = A/C/U) in the 3′ and 5′ untranslated regions (UTRs) of all of these genes (SRAMP, sequence based m6A modification consensus site predictor; 23; Supplementary Fig. 1e). Since a previous study (4) had reported six of these candidates to be targets of IGF2BP3, we further investigated and affirmed IGF2BP3 occupancy on their transcripts through IGF2BP3-RIP-qPCR in A4-SS cells (Figs.2b,2c-i,2c-ii). This association was further supported since elevated IGF2BP3 expression on serum deprivation also correlated with a longer mRNA half-life (t₁/₂) reflecting on increased stability of these transcripts (Fig. 2d; Supplementary Fig. 2). Conversely, IGF2BP3 knockdown in A4 (A4KD-sh1/sh2_IGF2BP3) led to significantly reduced expression of a few transcripts including FDPS, SQLE, LSS, and DHCR7 (Figs.2e,f). Collectively, IGF2BP3 is crucial in the stabilility of m⁶A-modified transcripts of key de novo cholesterol biosynthesis genes that in turn, leads to enhanced de novo cholesterol synthesis in mesenchymal extreme HGSC stem-like cells upon serum starvation.

Disrupting de novo cholesterol biosynthesis and silencing m⁶A regulatory proteins affects cell survival, migration and self-renewal under serum deprived conditions

We further explored possible association(s) of these two pathways with tumorigenesis by testing the effects of 3 pharmacological inhibitors targeting key branchpoints during de novo cholesterol biosynthesis (HMG-CoA reductase by Lovastatin, and FDPS by Alendronate/Zoledronic acid), and m⁶A inhibition (METTL3, catalytic subunit of the m⁶A methyltransferase complex inhibitor—STM2457 on A4 cells; A4KD-sh1/sh2_IGF2BP3 were also independantly evaluated (Fig. 3a). De novo cholesterol biosynthesis inhibitors significantly affected A4-SS and OVMZ6-SS cell viability as compared with the controls (Fig. 3b). Further studies were continued with A4 cells given its pronounced cholesterol biosynthesis-m⁶A signature under SS conditions. Pharmacological inhibition of de novo cholesterol biosynthesis using Lovastatin, Alendronate or Zoledronic acid, STM2457 as well as A4KD-sh1/sh2_IGF2BP3 significantly impaired the migratory capacity of A4 cells under SS conditions, as evidenced by delayed wound closure in scratch assays while vehicle-treated control cells demonstrated near complete wound closure in ~ 32 h (Figs.3c-i,3c-ii). These effects were even more pronounced on combining de novo cholesterol biosynthesis inhibitors with either STM2457 or A4KD-sh1/sh2_IGF2BP3 (Supplementary Fig. 3a). Intriguingly, this observation was accompanied by a further and more profound inhibitory effect on the invasive properties of the cells. While A4KD-sh1/sh2_IGF2BP3 showed a significant reduction in Matrigel invasion compared with the A4-EV controls, pharmacological inhibition of cholesterol biosynthesis, either alone or in combination with A4KD-sh1/sh2_IGF2BP3 or STM2457, completely abrogated the invasive capacity of the cells (Fig. 3d; Supplementary Fig. 3b).

Cell self-renewal and spheroid formation capabilities were significantly afftected by disruption of either de novo cholesterol biosynthesis or m⁶A-dependent regulation achieved through treatment with either Zoledronic acid, Alendronate or STM2457, and in A4KD-sh1/sh2_IGF2BP3 cells; these effects were intensified when inhibition of both pathways were combined (Figs.3e-i,3e-ii;Supplementary Fig. 3c). Lovastatin treatment, however, did not inhibit A4 spheroid formation, but significantly reduced the size of spheroids generated as compared with vehicle-treated controls (Figs.3e-i,3e-ii). Collectively, these findings underscore the critical role of m⁶A-modified transcripts, particularly those involved in de novo cholesterol biosynthesis pathway in regulating cellular migration and self-renewal ability of HGSC mesenchymal cells.

Elevated de novo cholesterol biosynthesis correlates with poor survival of serous ovarian carcinoma pateints and is driven by IGF2BP3 to promote tumor progression

To explore the clinical relevance of cholesterol biosynthesis genes in ovarian cancer, we first assessed expression of their transcripts in normal vs tumor tissues. Notably, five out of the eight candidate genes showed significant upregulation in tumor samples (Supplementary Fig. 4a). A composite gene signature analysis, based on the mean expression of all eight candidates per patient also revealed enrichment of this signature in tumor over normal tissues (Fig. 4a). We further evaluated the prognostic significance of these genes by screening for progression-free survival (PFS) in a cohort of 1,104 patients with serous ovarian cancer. This revealed MVK, IDI1, PMVK, SQLE, LSS, and DHCR7 to be significantly associated with poorer PFS outcomes (Fig. 4b; Supplementary Fig. 4b). Collectively, these findings assign clinical involvement of the de novo cholesterol biosynthesis genes and highlight their potential as therapeutic targets in HGSC.

To evaluate the therapeutic potential of targeting IGF2BP3-mediated de novo cholesterol biosynthesis, we established NOD/SCID xenograft models using A4, A4EV, and A4KD-sh1/sh2_IGF2BP3 cell lines (Fig. 4c). Tumor-bearing mice were administered the FDPS inhibitor i.e. zoledronic acid (100 µg/kg, intraperitoneally, once weekly), which had demonstrated most significant effects in vitro self-renewal assays. Significant reduction in tumor volume over the 21-day therapeutic regimen was noted in all treatment groups as compared with the vehicle control group; the effect was even more pronounced in xenografts generated with A4KD-sh1/sh2_IGF2BP3, surpassing the efficacy of zoledronic acid monotherapy when compared to the A4EV group (Figs.4d).

Testing a combination of A4KD-sh1/sh2_IGF2BP3 with zoledronic acid treatment enhanced the anti-tumor effects as compared with either single intervention. While the A4KD-sh2_IGF2BP3 + Zoledronic acid group showed tumor volumes comparable to A4KD-sh2_IGF2BP3 alone, the sh1_IGF2BP3 + zoledronic acid group demonstrated significantly improved tumor suppression beginning on Day 7, which persisted till the endpoint of the study (Day 21; Figs.4d,e). Amongst all treatments, the A4KD-sh1_IGF2BP3 + Zoledronic acid combination exhibited the most pronounced tumor growth inhibition, as confirmed by both tumor volume measurements and final tumor weight at Day 21 (Figs.4e,f).

Discussion

This study provides compelling evidence for the interplay between m⁶A RNA methylation and cholesterol biosynthesis. Specifically, we identified the selective upregulation of the m⁶A regulatory proteins WTAP and IGF2BP3, as well as key enzymes involved in de novo cholesterol biosynthesis pathway in stem-like cells of the mesenchymal HGSC subtype. We further went on to establish a mechanistic link between the two pathways that also governs tumor cell survival, self-renewal and disease progression. Enrichment of m⁶A regulators and RNA metabolism in the stem-like state triggered by serum deprivation accentuates how post-transcriptional mRNA modifications reprogram cellular outputs in response to nutrient deprivation. Differential upregulation of m⁶A regulators (indirect in epithelial and direct in mesenchymal phenotypes) suggests a requirement of discrete, subtype- and stemness-specific regulatory mechanisms mediated by varying epitranscriptomic adaptation (Zhou et al. 2016). Elevated WTAP and IGF2BP3 expression under serum starvation proved essential for increasing mRNA-specific m⁶A methylation, reflecting transcript-level rather than global RNA changes.

At a functional level, IGF2BP3 binds to and stabilizes m⁶A-modified mRNAs encoding key cholesterol biosynthesis enzymes, which in turn leads to increased protein expression and enhanced cholesterol biosynthesis that supporting the metabolic demands of stem-like mesenchymal tumor cells. These findings are consistent with broader reports across cancers identifying central roles of m⁶A readers and writers in contributing to cancer stem cell survival and malignancy through mRNA stabilization—decay mediated regulation of metabolism (Zhou et al. 2016; Mobet et al. 2022).

Pharmacological blockade of cholesterol biosynthesis and genetic silencing of m⁶A regulators reduced cell viability, migration, self-renewal, and spheroid formation emphasizing the functional interdependence of m⁶A-modified de novo cholesterol biosynthetic transcripts in maintaining stem-like properties. The synergistic effect observed with genetic silencing of IGF2BP3 and de novo cholesterol biosynthesis inhibitors both in vitro and in vivo highlights the therapeutic value of targeting metabolic reprogramming to mitigate tumor progression. This is supported by clinical data showing that high levels of de novo cholesterol biosynthesis gene expression are associated with poorer outcomes in serous ovarian cancer, reinforcing this axis as a potential biomarker and intervention point (Deng et al. 2023). Additionally, correlation analyses of m⁶A regulators and enzyme expression involved in de novo cholesterol biosynthesis in the clinical TCGA-OV dataset (Proteomic Data Commons, PDC000114; Ovarian Serous Cystadenocarcinoma, CPTAC; n = 84) revealed that several core components of the m⁶A methyltransferase complex, including WTAP, VIRMA, ZC3H13, and METTL3, displayed significant correlations with multiple enzymes across the cholesterol biosynthesis pathway. In contrast, IGF2BP3 expression did not exhibit strong or statistically significant correlations with most major cholesterol biosynthesis enzymes, except from modest positive associations with IDI1 and HSD17B7 (Supplementary Fig. 4c). A limitation of use of the TCGA-OV cohort is it represents an unstratified collection of histopathological subtype encompassing tumors spanning all molecular subtypes representing the epithelial–mesenchymal spectrum of cellular states. While the correlation analysis supports a broader involvement of m⁶A machinery in regulating cholesterol metabolic processes in HGSC, it could not recapitulate the correlation between expression of IGF2BP3 and the major cholesterol biosynthesis enzymes, highlighting that IGF2BP3–enzyme relationships may be more context-specific or restricted to the mesenchymal tumor subtype. Although previous studies have independently demonstrated that m⁶A RNA modifications contribute to the maintenance of cancer stemness across multiple malignancies and that de novo cholesterol biosynthesis supports tumor metabolic demands and drives aggressive phenotypes (Wang et al. 2015; Hanahan 2022; He et al. 2021; Giacomini et al. 2021), the mechanistic intersection between these two pathways has remained largely unexplored. Only a limited number of recent investigations primarily centered on single candidate enzymes within the cholesterol biosynthetic pathway have begun to address this gap (Hou et al. 2024; Zeng et al. 2024). In contrast to these studies, the present work offers a substantially broader and more integrative perspective. By demonstrating that transcript expression of at least eight key enzymes in the cholesterol biosynthesis cascade is stabilized by the m⁶A-regulation within the mesenchymal phenotype of HGSC. This resolution uncovers multiple previously unappreciated enzymatic nodes that may contribute to the enhanced metabolic plasticity, besides offering a survival advantage envisaged as the characteristically aggressive behavior of mesenchymal HGSC states. Intriguingly, differential enrichment of HNRNPC and IGF2BP3 in epithelial and mesenchymal states respectively, highlights the possibility of a context-specific m⁶A regulation that remains to be fully elucidated. In summary, the convergence of m⁶A RNA methylation and cholesterol biosynthesis in serum-starved mesenchymal HGSC cells orchestrates stem-like tumor cell survival and progression, offering promising avenues for targeted therapy and prognostic stratification in ovarian cancer.

While the present study establishes a mechanistic basis and provides evidence that m⁶A-dependent stabilization of cholesterol biosynthesis transcripts by IGF2BP3 supports the stemness and survival of mesenchymal HGSC cells, validation of this regulatory axis in patient-derived clinical specimens remains essential and will be taken up further. Furthermore, the upstream cues governing IGF2BP3 activation and the potential feedback or compensatory regulatory circuits that may influence this axis were not explored and lie beyond the scope of the current work. Incorporating multiple HGSC cell lines spanning the mesenchymal spectrum will be valuable for future investigations aimed at validating and extending these observations. Furthermore, given that different mesenchymal HGSC cell lines may exhibit distinct phenotypic outputs in assays such as migration and spheroid formation, interpreting such variations falls beyond the scope of the present study. These aspects warrant dedicated, comprehensive follow-up studies to fully delineate phenotype-specific responses.

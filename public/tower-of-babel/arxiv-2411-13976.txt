Blow-up result for a piezoelectric beams system with magnetic effects
Mohammad Kafini

Abstract
The system under studying is for a piezoelectric beams system with magnetic effects, frictional dampings and source terms. We use the concavity method to study the competition of the dampings and the sources that leads to a blow-up result for solutions with negative initial energy. Keywords and phrases: Blow up, Negative initial energy, Piezoelectric beams, Magnetic effects, Nonlinear sources. AMS Classification : 35B44, 35D30, 35L05, 35L15, 35L70,

Piezoelectric materials have the valuable property of converting mechanical
energy to electro-magnetic energy, and vice versa. Piezoelectric actuators are
generally scalable, smaller, less expensive and more efficient than
traditional actuators, and hence, a competitive choice for many tasks,
particularly the control of flexible structures. They are used in civil,
industrial, automotive, aeronautic, and space structures. For more information
we refer to
\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{1}{}{}]},\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{2}{}{}]},\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{3}{}{}]},\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{4}{}{}]},

It is known in the literature that magnetic effects in the piezoelectric beams

\eta
more large, if needed, that makes

\eta-4\lambda(\sigma+1)\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)=k>0\text{.}

(20)

Finally, as
-kE\left(0\right)>0,
we pick
b>0
small enough so that

-kE\left(0\right)-2b(\sigma+1)\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\geq 0.

Therefore,

Q(t)\geq 0,\text{ \ \ \ }\forall t\geq 0

and hence

G^{\prime\prime}(t)\leq 0,\quad\forall t\geq 0;

that is
G^{\prime}
is decreasing.

By choosing
t_{0}>0
large enough we get

F^{\prime}(0)=\int_{0}^{L}(\upsilon_{0}\upsilon_{1}+p_{0}p_{1})dx+bt_{0}>0,

hence
G^{\prime}(0)<0.

Finally Taylor expansion of
G
yields

G(t)\leq G(0)+tG^{\prime}(0),\quad\forall t\geq 0,

which shows that
G(t)
vanishes at a time
t_{m}\leq-\frac{G(0)}{G^{\prime}(0)}.
Consequently
F(t)
must become infinite at time
t_{m}.

Therefore, the solution
(\upsilon,p)
blows up with time bounded above by

t_{m}.

4
Lower bound for the blow-up time

In this section, we estimate a lower bound for the blow-up time. For
that reason, we define

\psi(t)=\int_{0}^{L}I(\upsilon,p)dx

(21)

and have the following theorem.

Theorem 7

Assume that
(\upsilon,p)

is a solution of
\left(\ref{P}\right)
that satisfying (G1)-(G3) and does not exist after a
time
T^{\ast}
. Then we have

\int_{\psi(0)}^{\infty}\frac{c\text{ }dy}{y+y^{\beta_{1}}+y^{\beta_{2}}+y^{\beta_{3}}+y^{\beta_{4}}}\leq T^{\ast},

for some constant
c>0.

Proof.
A direct differentiation of
\psi(t)
gives

\psi^{\prime}(t)=\int_{0}^{L}\left[\upsilon_{t}I_{\upsilon}(\upsilon,p)+p_{t}I_{p}(\upsilon,p)\right]dx.

Using Young’s inequality and
(G1)
, we have

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{L}\left(\upsilon_{t}^{2}+p_{t}^{2}\right)dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{L}\left(I_{\upsilon}^{2}+I_{p}^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{L}\left(\upsilon_{t}^{2}+p_{t}^{2}\right)dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{L}\left(f_{1}^{2}+f_{2}^{2}\right)dx.

(22)

Using
(G3)
gives

f_{1}^{2}(\upsilon,p)\leq d^{2}\left(\left|\upsilon\right|^{\beta_{1}}+\left|p\right|^{\beta_{2}}\right)^{2}\leq 2d^{2}\left(\left|\upsilon\right|^{2\beta_{1}}+\left|p\right|^{2\beta_{2}}\right)

and

\ \ f_{2}^{2}(\upsilon,p)\leq d^{2}\left(\left|\upsilon\right|^{\beta_{3}}+\left|p\right|^{\beta_{4}}\right)^{2}\leq 2d^{2}\left(\left|\upsilon\right|^{2\beta_{3}}+\left|p\right|^{2\beta_{4}}\right).

Using the embedding and Poincarè’s inequality, we obtain from
\left(\ref{19}\right),

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{L}\left(\upsilon_{t}^{2}+p_{t}^{2}\right)dx+d^{2}\int_{0}^{L}\left(\left|\upsilon\right|^{2\beta_{1}}+\left|p\right|^{2\beta_{2}}+\left|\upsilon\right|^{2\beta_{3}}+\left|p\right|^{2\beta_{4}}\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{L}\left(\upsilon_{t}^{2}+p_{t}^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle+d^{2}\left(C_{p}^{\beta_{1}}||\upsilon_{x}||_{2}^{2\beta_{1}}+C_{p}^{\beta_{2}}||p_{x}||_{2}^{2\beta_{2}}+C_{p}^{\beta_{3}}||\upsilon_{x}||_{2}^{2\beta_{3}}+C_{p}^{\beta_{4}}||p_{x}||_{2}^{2\beta_{4}}\right),

where
C_{p}
is the Poincarè’s constant.

Thus, for
a=d^{2}\max\left\{C_{p}^{\beta_{1}},C_{p}^{\beta_{2}},C_{p}^{\beta_{3}},C_{p}^{\beta_{4}}\right\},
we get

\psi^{\prime}(t)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\|\upsilon_{t}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|p_{t}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)+a\left(||\upsilon_{x}||_{2}^{2\beta_{1}}+||p_{x}||_{2}^{2\beta_{2}}+||\upsilon_{x}||_{2}^{2\beta_{3}}+||p_{x}||_{2}^{2\beta_{4}}\right).

(23)

Recalling
\left(\ref{6}\right)
and
\left(\ref{11}\right)

we infer that

\alpha_{1}||\upsilon_{x}||_{2}^{2}+\beta||\gamma\upsilon_{x}-p_{x}||_{2}^{2}+\rho||\upsilon_{t}||_{2}^{2}+\mu||p_{t}||_{2}^{2}\leq 2\int_{0}^{L}I(\upsilon,p)dx.

(24)

If we let
m=\min\left\{\alpha_{1},\beta,\rho,\mu\right\}
and use

\left(\ref{183}\right)
then
\left(\ref{191}\right)
becomes

m\left(||\upsilon_{x}||_{2}^{2}+||\gamma\upsilon_{x}-p_{x}||_{2}^{2}+||\upsilon_{t}||_{2}^{2}+||p_{t}||_{2}^{2}\right)\leq 2\int_{0}^{L}I(\upsilon,p)dx=2\psi(t).

So, each term in
\left(\ref{190}\right)
is bounded above by
\frac{2}{m}\psi(t).

Therefore,
\left(\ref{190}\right)
yields

\psi^{\prime}(t)\leq\frac{2}{m}\psi(t)+a\left(\frac{2}{m}\right)^{r}\left[\psi^{\beta_{1}}(t)+\psi^{\beta_{2}}(t)+\psi^{\beta_{3}}(t)+\psi^{\beta_{4}}(t)\right],

(25)

where
r=\max\left\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3},\beta_{4}\right\}.

If we integrate
\left(\ref{20}\right)
over
(0,T^{\ast})
and use the
substitution
y=\psi(t),
then we reach

\int_{\psi(0)}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{\frac{2}{m}y+a\left(\frac{2}{m}\right)^{r}\left[y^{\beta_{1}}+y^{\beta_{2}}+y^{\beta_{3}}+y^{\beta_{4}}\right]}\leq T^{\ast}.

This completes the proof.

Conclusion:

In our proof of the blow-up property in theorem
3.1, we assume the domain
\left[0,L\right]
is bounded. We notify here
that the same proof is also valid in unbounded domain. Moreover, condition

\left(\ref{182}\right)
is assumed to be a compitition between the source
(assumed
\eta
large) and the damping (where it may be assumed small by
taking
\lambda
small). Finally, positive initial energy can be considered
but with different arrangement.

Tikhonov regularized exterior penalty methods for hierarchical variational inequalities
Meggie Marschner, Mathias Staudigl

Abstract
We consider nested variational inequalities consisting in a (upper-level) variational inequality whose feasible set is given by the solution set of another (lower-level) variational inequality. This class of hierarchical equilibrium contains a wealth of important applications, including purely hierarchical convex bilevel optimization problems and certain multi-follower games. Working within a real Hilbert space setting, we develop a double loop prox-penalization algorithm with strong convergence guarantees towards a solution of the nested VI problem. We present various application that fit into our framework and present also some preliminary numerical results.



I
INTRODUCTION

In a real Hilbert space setting, we consider the hierarchical variational inequality problem

\VI(\mathbb{G},\mathcal{S}_{0}),\quad\text{where }\mathcal{S}_{0}:=\zer(\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{F})

(P)

in which the problem data satisfy the following conditions:

Assumption 1

1.

\mathbb{G}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is a monotone and Lipschitz continuous map with Lipschitz constant
L_{G}
and weakly sequentially continuous, i.e.
v^{n}\rightharpoonup v\Rightarrow\mathbb{G}(v^{n})\rightharpoonup\mathbb{G}(v)
;

2.

\mathbb{F}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is monotone and Lipschitz with Lipschitz constant
L_{F}
and weakly sequentially continuous;

3.

\mathbb{A}:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
is maximally monotone with
\dom(\mathbb{A})
bounded;

4.

The set
\mathcal{S}_{0}
is nonempty.

Our assumptions imply that
\dom(\mathbb{F})=\mathcal{H}
, and thus
\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{F}
is maximally monotone. Furthermore, it follows that
\mathcal{S}_{0}
is closed and convex. The problem
\VI(\mathbb{G},\mathcal{S}_{0})
is to find a point
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{H}
such that

\langle\mathbb{G}(\bar{u}),u-\bar{u}\rangle\geq 0\qquad\forall u\in\mathcal{S}_{0}=\zer(\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{F}).

(1)

This nested VI problem encompasses a wide range of optimization and equilibrium problems involving a hierarchical structure. Concrete applications of this model framework can be found in signal processing

]
, equilibrium selection in Nash games

,

[

, certain classes of bilevel optimization

, and power allocation
[

]
, to mention a few. We give below a few concrete examples.

Example 1
(Simple Bilevel Optimization)

The seminal references
[
6
,
9
,
10
]
introduced simple bilevel problems of the form

\min g(u)\quad\text{s.t.: }u\in\argmin_{v\in\mathcal{H}}\{r(v)+f(v)\}.

(2)

Here
g,f:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
are convex Fréchet-differentiable functions with Lipschitz continuous gradient
\mathbb{G}=\nabla g
and
\mathbb{F}=\nabla f
, respectively. The function
r:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is a convex, proper and lower semicontinuous function, giving rise to the maximally monotone operator
\mathbb{A}=\partial r
. Under the convexity assumptions, we can reformulate the problem (
2
) as the hierarchical VI (
P
). More generally, we can consider structured convex optimization problems of the form

\min_{u}g(u)\quad\text{s.t. }u\in\argmin_{v\in\mathcal{H}}\{f(v)+r(Lv)\}.

Using the Fenchel-Rockafellar duality in the lower level problem, we can reformulate the above as the saddle-point problem

\min_{v}\{f(v)+r(Lv)\}=\min_{v}\max_{w}\{f(v)+\langle L^{\ast}v,w\rangle-r^{\ast}(w)\}.

A saddle point is characterized by the monotone inclusion

\displaystyle 0\in\nabla f(\bar{v})+L\bar{w}

\displaystyle 0\in-L^{\ast}\bar{v}+\partial r^{\ast}(\bar{w})

which can be compactly written as the problem of finding a point in
\zer(\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{F})
, with
\mathbb{F}(v,w)=[Lw;-L^{\ast}v]+[\nabla f(v);0]
and
\mathbb{A}(v,w)=\{0\}\times\partial r^{\ast}(w)
. Note that the operator
\mathbb{F}
is not cocoercive, which precludes a direct approach via viscosity techniques as the celebrated BiG-SAM
[
11
]
.

Example 2
(Equilibrium Selection in Nash games)

The Nash equilibrium problem is described by a finite set of players
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, each characterized by a strategy space
\mathcal{X}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{i}
, and a real-valued function
f_{i}:\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{X}_{i}\to\mathbb{R}
. An
N
-tuple
x^{\ast}=(x_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,x_{N}^{\ast})\in\mathcal{X}\triangleq\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{X}_{i}
is a Nash equilibrium if

f_{i}(x^{\ast})\leq f_{i}(x_{i},x^{\ast}_{-i})\qquad\forall x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{i},i=1,2,\ldots,N.

We define the game as a tuple
\Gamma=\{f_{i},\mathcal{X}_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
and denote the set of Nash equilibrium points by
\NE(\Gamma)
. Assuming that each mapping
x_{i}\mapsto f_{i}(x_{i},x_{-i})
is convex and differentiable, it is a classical result that the set of Nash equilibria can be characterized as the solution set of a Variational inequality
\VI(\mathbb{F},\mathcal{X})
, determined by the operator

\mathbb{F}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H},x\mapsto\mathbb{F}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_{x_{1}}f_{1}(x)\\
\vdots\\
\nabla_{x_{N}}f_{N}(x)\end{pmatrix}.

Setting
\mathbb{A}=\NC_{\mathcal{X}}
, the Normal cone operator (see section
II
), we can reformulate problem
\VI(\mathbb{F},\mathcal{X})
as the inclusion problem
\zer(\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{F})
. In many design problems, one asks for the ”best” equilibrium point, relative to a pre-defined loss function
g:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
with gradient mapping
\mathbb{G}=\nabla g
. This leads to the equilibrium selection problem

\text{Solve }\VI(\mathbb{G},\mathcal{S}_{0})\quad\mathcal{S}_{0}=\zer(\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{F}).

I-A

Contributions

In order to resolve the hierarchical VI problem (
P
), we follow
[
1
]
and adopt a regularization framework involving a parametric family of structured monotone inclusions
\mathbb{A}+\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(\bullet,w)
, where
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}:\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is defined by

\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(v,w):=\mathbb{F}(v)+\beta\mathbb{G}(v)+\alpha(v-w).

(3)

The parameter
\alpha>0
is a proximal parameter, while
\beta>0
is a Tikhonov parameter that regulates the relative importance of the lower level versus the upper level. Auxiliary mappings of the form (
3
) have a long tradition in mathematical optimization in connection with the proximal penalization framework
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
. To motivate this construction, let us investigate the structure of the operator
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}
when applied to the simple bilevel optimization problem (Example
1
). To iteratively solve this problem, suppose that
w
is our best-so-far solution candidate. To obtain a new proposal, a common strategy within the proximal penalization framework is to solve the following strongly convex auxiliary problem

\min_{v\in\mathcal{H}}\{H_{\alpha,\beta}(v,w)\triangleq r(v)+f(v)+\beta g(v)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\norm{v-w}^{2}\}.

Thanks to the proximal term, this problem admits a unique solution
\bar{u}_{\alpha,\beta}(w)
. Under a constraint qualification, we can compute

\partial_{v}H_{\alpha,\beta}(v,w)=\partial r(v)+\nabla f(v)+\beta\nabla g(v)+\alpha(v-w)=\mathbb{A}(v)+\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(v,w).

Given an anchor point
w\in\mathcal{H}
, the auxiliary problem
\zer(\mathbb{A}+\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(\bullet,w))
admits a unique solution
\bar{u}_{\alpha,\beta}(w)
. Our algorithmic framework builds upon a double loop architecture, in which the inner loop is an iterative method that drives the process to a neighborhood of the temporary solution
\bar{u}_{\alpha,\beta}(w)
. Upon termination of the inner loop, an outer loop is activated which updates the anchor point
w
and the Tikhonov parameter
\beta
. The combination of Tikhonov and proximal regularization terms is borrowed from the paper
[
1
]
. This paper develops a
general template
to design iterative methods for resolving problem (
P
). To keep the exposition concrete, we illustrate our machinery only in the special case in which the forward-backward splitting method is used as the main algorithmic map. The general abstract setting will be described in a forthcoming publication.

With respect to
[
1
]
, the innovations of this note are the following: We consider a more general class of splitting problems, with explicit iterations via a resolvent step. Second, our scheme is robust to inexact computations and is formulated in Hilbert spaces, as required for potentially infinite-dimensional control applications. Our inner loop scheme is a relaxed-inertial forward backward implementation involving the regularized monotone operators that we employ in order to iteratively track the temporal solutions. This algorithmic technique is a classical tool in monotone splitting approaches. In fact, the inertial-relaxation version has been first studied in a discrete-time algorithm in
[
15
]
. However, their analysis assumes that the discrete velocity of the process is summable. This is a rather frequently encountered technical assumption, which, however, can never be checked before the algorithm is actually executed. Our proof does not make such an assumption, and instead uses a new Lyapunov-type analysis, inspired by
[
16
]
. Using this technique, we establish linear convergence of the inner loop scheme. Additionally, we develop convergence guarantees in an inexact computational framework. Inexact forward-backward algorithms are studied in
[
17
]
and
[
18
]
.

Hybrid models for approaching problem (
P
) have been studied in
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
. These methods require
\mathbb{F}
to be co-coercive (inverse strongly monotone), a condition that substantially holds when the lower-level VI reduces to a convex optimization problem. On the contrary, here we only require
\mathbb{F}
to be monotone and Lipschitz continuous, allowing for the treatment of more general problems with respect to these classical approaches.

II
Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a real Hilbert space with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and corresponding norm
\norm{\cdot}
. Given a closed convex set
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
, we define the orthogonal projection operator
\Pi_{\mathcal{K}}(x)=\argmin\{\frac{1}{2}\norm{w-x}^{2}|w\in\mathcal{K}\}
. A set-valued operator
\mathbb{A}:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
is monotone if

\langle u-v,b-a\rangle\geq 0\quad\forall(u,b)\in\gr(\mathbb{A}),\forall(v,a)\in\gr(\mathbb{A}),

where
\gr(\mathbb{A})\triangleq\{(v,a)\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}|a\in\mathbb{A}(v)\}
is the graph of the operator
\mathbb{A}
. The operator
\mathbb{A}:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
is maximally monotone if it is monotone and there exists no other monotone operator whose graph contains
\gr(\mathbb{A})
. The resolvent of an operator
\mathbb{A}
is defined as
\resolvent_{\mathbb{A}}=(\Id+\mathbb{A})^{-1}
. If
\mathbb{A}
is maximally monotone, then the resolvent is a nonexpansive operator (and thus single-valued)
[
22
]
.

Lemma 1

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a maximal monotone operator. A point
(x,a)\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
belongs to
\gr(\mathbb{A})
if and only if

\langle a-b,x-y\rangle\geq 0\qquad\forall(y,b)\in\gr(\mathbb{A}).

This Lemma shows also that for all

, the set
\mathbb{A}(u)
is closed and convex.

Given a mapping
\mathbb{G}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
and a set

, the variational inequality problem

, is the problem of finding a point

such that

\langle\mathbb{G}(u),v-u\rangle\geq 0\;\;\forall v\in\mathcal{K}.

The solution set of

can be expressed as
\zer(\mathbb{G}+\NC_{\mathcal{K}})
, involving the normal cone operator

\NC_{\mathcal{K}}(v):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varnothing&\text{if }v\notin\mathcal{K}\\
\{p\in\mathcal{H}|\langle p,u-v\rangle\leq 0,\;\forall u\in\mathcal{K}\}&\text{ if }v\in\mathcal{K}.\end{array}\right.

If
\mathbb{G}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is continuous and monotone, and
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
is closed and convex, then
\zer(\mathbb{G}+\NC_{\mathcal{K}})

Lemma 2

Let
\mathbb{G}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}

convex. Let

III
Algorithm

As mentioned, our algorithmic design uses a double loop architecture, in which a forward-backward splitting method serves as an inner loop scheme to produce an iterate that is close to a temporal solution of the auxiliary problem
\zer(\mathbb{A}+\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(\cdot,w))
. We denote by
t\in\mathbb{N}
the outer loop iteration count, and the corresponding time
t
approximate solution
w^{t}\in\mathcal{H}
which is produced in such a way that guarantees

\norm{w^{t+1}-\bar{u}_{\alpha,\beta_{t}}(w^{t})}\leq\mathtt{e}_{t},

(6)

where
(\mathtt{e}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
is a positive sequence of accuracies that is dynamically adjusted when the outer loop is called upon.

III-A

The inner loop

The main iteration performed by our method is a forward backward step, involving a set of user-provided parameters that are updated over time. We call this scheme by
\mathtt{FB}(\beta,\varepsilon,\gamma,w)
. The main computational step in this procedure is the recursive update

v^{k+1}=T_{\gamma_{k}}(v^{k})\equiv\resolvent_{\gamma_{k}\mathbb{A}}(v^{k}-\gamma_{k}\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(v^{k},w)),

(7)

where
w\in\mathcal{H}
is a given anchor point and
(\gamma_{k})_{k}
is a positive sequence of step sizes. Given the pair
(\alpha,\beta)\in(0,\infty)^{2}
and
w\in\mathcal{H}
, our method recursively constructs sequences
(z^{k})_{k}
and
(v^{k})_{k}
which iteratively approximate the unique solution
\bar{u}_{\alpha,\beta}(w)
of the auxiliary problem
\zer(\mathbb{A}+\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(\cdot,w))
. In the updating step of the sequence
(v^{k})_{k}
instead of using the exact forward-backward map
T_{\gamma_{k}}(v)
, we assume that we have only access to a
\delta_{k}
-perturbation
\tilde{T}_{k}(z^{k})
, in the sense that

\tilde{T}_{k}(z)=T_{\gamma_{k}}(z)+\delta_{k}.

(8)

The inclusion of the numerical error
\delta_{k}
is motivated by the fact that the resolvent is in general not available in exact form or its computation may be very demanding. Just to mention some examples, this happens when applying proximal methods to image deblurring with total variation
[
24
]
, or to structured sparsity regularization problems in machine learning and inverse problems
[
25
]
. In those cases, the proximity operator is usually computed using ad hoc algorithms, and therefore inexactly. Employing this inexact computational model, we propose an inertial forward-backward algorithm for iteratively solving (
P
) by updating the sequence
(v^{k},z^{k})_{k}
as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}z^{k}=v^{k}+\tau_{k}(v^{k}-v^{k-1}),\\
v^{k+1}=(1-\theta_{k})z^{k}+\theta_{k}\tilde{T}_{k}(z^{k})\end{array}\right.

\tau_{k}\in(0,1)
is a momentum term, while the parameter
\theta_{k}\in(0,1)
is a relaxation factor for the inexact Krasnoselskii-Mann iteration. It is well known that if we choose
\gamma_{k}\in(0,2\alpha/L^{2}_{\alpha,\beta_{t}})
and setting
q_{k}(\alpha,\beta_{t})=\sqrt{1-\gamma_{k}(2\alpha-\gamma L^{2}_{\alpha,\beta_{t}})}\in(0,1)
, then the operator
T_{\gamma_{k}}
is a
q_{k}(\alpha,\beta_{t})
contraction.

As a last ingredient in the lower level problem, we need an operational stopping criterion which informs us when the iterates are sufficiently close to the temporal solution
\bar{u}_{\alpha,\beta}(w)
. To achieve this aim, we define the stopping time

\mathbb{K}_{\alpha,\beta}(\varepsilon,w)\triangleq\inf\{k\geq 1|\;\norm{v^{k+1}-z^{k}}\leq\varepsilon\}.

(9)

We introduce the following assumptions on the parameters:

Assumption 2

(\beta_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\notin\ell^{1}_{+}(\mathbb{N})
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathtt{e}_{t}}{\beta_{t}}=0
.

Assumption 3

\theta_{k}\in[0,1]
and there exists a
\bar{\theta},\underline{\theta}
such that
\bar{\theta}\geq\theta_{k}\geq\underline{\theta}
for all k and
\tau_{k}\in[0,1]
monotonically increasing such that there exists a
\bar{\tau}\geq\tau_{k}
for all k.

III-B

General Stopping Time

Before tackling the outer loop, we need an operational stopping criterion
which informs us when the iterates are sufficiently close to the temporal solution
\bar{u}_{\alpha,\beta_{t}}
. Let there exists an
\epsilon
such that
\norm{v^{k+1}-z^{k}}\leq\epsilon
, then

\displaystyle\norm{z^k-T_{\gamma_k}(z^k)}

\displaystyle=\norm{\frac{1}{\theta_k}(v^{k+1}-z^k)}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{\theta_{k}}.

Moreover, let again
\{\bar{u}^{k}\}=\textnormal{Fix}(T_{\gamma_{k}})
, we get

\displaystyle\norm{z^k-\bar{u}^k}

\displaystyle=\norm{z^k-T_{\gamma_k}(z^k)+T_{\gamma_k}(z^k)-\bar{u}^k}

\displaystyle=\norm{\frac{v^{k+1}-z^k}{\theta_k}+T_{\gamma_k}(z^k)-\bar{u}^k}

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\frac{v^{k+1}-z^k}{\theta_k}}+\norm{T_{\gamma_k}(z^k)-T_{\gamma_k}(\bar{u}^k)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{\theta_{k}}+q_{k}(\alpha,\beta_{t})\norm{z^k-\bar{u}^k}

so that
\norm{z^k-\bar{u}^k}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{\theta_{k}(1-q_{k}(\alpha,\beta_{t}))}.
This suggests to take as a sensible stopping criterion the stopping time

\mathbb{K}(\epsilon)=\inf\{k\geq 1\mid\norm{v^{k+1}-z^k}\leq\epsilon\}.

III-C

The outer loop

In the outer loop we update the parameters
\varepsilon_{t},\beta_{t},\gamma_{t},w^{t}
in order to restart the inner loop of the method
IKM(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon,\gamma,w)
. We set
K_{t}\equiv\mathbb{K}_{\alpha,\beta_{t}}(\varepsilon_{t},w^{t})
, and update the anchor point by setting

w^{t+1}=v^{K_{t}}_{t}
, so that condition (
6
) holds with accuracy
\varepsilon_{t}
. Within this loop, we assume that the step size
\gamma_{k}
is fixed to
\gamma_{t}\in(0,\frac{2\alpha}{L_{\alpha,\beta_{t}}})
. Indeed, by using Lemma
7
, we observe for
k\geq 1
:

\displaystyle\norm{z^{k}-\bar{u}_{\alpha,\beta_{t}}(w^{t})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\gamma_{t}L_{\alpha,\beta_{t}}}{\alpha}\norm{\scrG^{\gamma_{t}}_{\alpha,\beta_{t}}(z^{k}_{t},w^{t})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\gamma_{t}L_{\alpha,\beta_{t}}}{\alpha\gamma_{t}}\norm{z^{k}-T_{\gamma_{t}}(z^{k})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\gamma_{t}L_{\alpha,\beta_{t}}}{\alpha\gamma_{t}}\norm{\frac{v^{k+1}-z^{k}}{\theta_{k}}+\delta_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\gamma_{t}L_{\alpha,\beta_{t}}}{\alpha\theta_{k}\gamma_{t}}\left(\norm{v^{k+1}-z^{k}}+\theta_{k}\norm{\delta_{k}}\right).

As an illustrative example, let us assume that error model is given by
\norm{\delta_{k}}=d_{k}\varepsilon_{t}
for a sequence
(d_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. Then, there exists
D>0
such that
(d_{k})\subseteq[0,D]
, and thus we can evaluate the previous inequality at the last iteration counter of the inner loop
k=\mathbb{K}_{t}\equiv\mathbb{K}_{\alpha,\beta_{t}}(\varepsilon_{t},w^{t})
, to obtain

\displaystyle\norm{w^{t+1}-\bar{u}_{\alpha,\beta_{t}}(w^{t})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\gamma_{t}L_{\alpha,\beta_{t}}}{\alpha\underline{\theta}}\left(\varepsilon_{t}+D\bar{\theta}\varepsilon_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+\bar{\theta}D)(1+\gamma_{t}L_{\alpha,\beta_{t}})}{\alpha\underline{\theta}}\varepsilon_{t}\triangleq\mathtt{e}_{t}.

(10)

Having obtained this new anchor point, we update the parameters
\varepsilon_{t+1},\beta_{t+1},\gamma_{t+1}
and restart the inner loop with these parameters and the new anchor point
w^{t+1}
.

We summarize our approach with the following method.

(1) Let
t=1

Theorem 8

Assume that
(\theta_{k}\delta_{k})_{k}\in\ell^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{N})

4

(z^{k},v^{k})_{k}
is generated by procedure

converge strongly to

\sum_{k\geq 1}\norm{v^{k}-\bar{u}_{\alpha,\beta}(w)}^{2}<\infty
.

Proof:

To simplify the notation we abbreviate
\bar{u}\equiv\bar{u}_{\alpha,\beta}(w)
. We start with applying Lemma
3
with
\xi=-\theta_{k}

\displaystyle\norm{v^{k+1}-\bar{u}}^{2}=\norm{(1-\theta_k)z^k+\theta_{k}\tilde{T}_{k}(z^k)-\bar{u}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\norm{z^k-\bar{u}-\theta_k(-\tilde{T}_{k}(z^k)+z^k)}^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-\theta_{k})\norm{z^k-\bar{u}}^{2}-\theta_{k}(1-\theta_{k})\norm{z^k-\tilde{T}_{k}(z^k)}^{2}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;+\theta_{k}\norm{\tilde{T}_{k}(z^k)-\bar{u}}^{2}.

Next, we employ Lemma
3
with
\xi=\tau_{k}
to obtain

\displaystyle\norm{z^{k}-\bar{u}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\norm{v^{k}+\tau_{k}(v^{k}-v^{k-1})-\bar{u}}^{2}

\displaystyle=(1+\tau_{k})\norm{v^{k}-\bar{u}}^{2}+\tau_{k}(1+\tau_{k})\norm{v^{k}-v^{k-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;-\tau_{k}\norm{v^{k-1}-\bar{u}}^{2}.

Using that
\bar{u}=T_{\gamma_{k}}(\bar{u})
for all
k
and the
q_{k}
-contraction property of the mapping
T_{\gamma_{k}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\norm{\tilde{T}_{k}(z^k)-\bar{u}}^{2}=\norm{T_{\gamma_k}(z^k)+\delta_k-\bar{u}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2q_{k}^{2}\Big[(1+\tau_{k})\norm{v^k-\bar{u}}^{2}+\tau_{k}(1+\tau_{k})\norm{v^k-v^{k-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;-\tau_{k}\norm{v^{k-1}-\bar{u}}^{2}\Big]+2\theta_{k}\norm{\delta_k}^{2}.

Substituting all these terms, we can continue our development with

\displaystyle\norm{v^{k+1}-\bar{u}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\theta_{k}+2\theta_{k}q_{k}^{2})\Big[(1+\tau_{k})\norm{v^{k}-\bar{u}}^{2}

\displaystyle\;\;\;+\tau_{k}(1+\tau_{k})\norm{v^{k}-v^{k-1}}^{2}-\tau_{k}\norm{v^{k-1}-\bar{u}}^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\;\;\;-\theta_{k}(1-\theta_{k})\norm{z^{k}-\tilde{T}_{k}(z^{k})}^{2}+2\theta_{k}\norm{\delta_k}^{2}.

As a next step we take a look at the second term. Notice that
\tilde{T}_{k}(z^{k})-z^{k}=\frac{v^{k+1}-z^{k}}{\theta_{k}}
. Applying again Lemma
3
we get

\displaystyle-\theta_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\norm{\tilde{T}_{k}(z^k)-z^k}^{2}=-\norm{v^{k+1}-z^k}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\norm{v^{k+1}-v^k-\tau_k(v^k-v^{k-1})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-(1-\tau_{k})\norm{v^{k+1}-v^k}^{2}+\tau_{k}(1-\tau_{k})\norm{v^k-v^{k-1}}^{2}.

Define
\lambda_{k}\triangleq(1-\theta_{k})/\theta_{k}
, with
\theta_{k}\in(0,1)
for all
k
and multiply both sides in the display above by
\lambda_{k}
in order to arrive at

\displaystyle-\theta_{k}(1-\theta_{k})\norm{\tilde{T}_{k}(z^k)-z^k}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle-\lambda_{k}(1-\tau_{k})\norm{v^{k+1}-v^k}^{2}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{k}\tau_{k}(1-\tau_{k})\norm{v^k-v^{k-1}}^{2}.

Combining all these estimates, together with some elementary algebra, we can continue our energy bound as

\displaystyle\norm{v^{k+1}-\bar{u}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Q_{k}\Big[(1+\tau_{k})\norm{v^{k}-\bar{u}}^{2}+\tau_{k}(1+\tau_{k})\norm{v^{k}-v^{k-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\;-\tau_{k}\norm{v^{k-1}-\bar{u}}^{2}\Big]\ -\lambda_{k}(1-\tau_{k})\norm{v^{k+1}-v^k}^{2}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{k}\tau_{k}(1-\tau_{k})\norm{v^k-v^{k-1}}^{2}+2\theta_{k}\norm{\delta_k}^{2}.

Rearranging the terms and evoking Assumption (
4
) we get

\displaystyle\norm{v^{k+1}-\bar{u}}^{2}+\lambda_{k}(1-\tau_{k})\norm{v^{k+1}-v^k}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Q_{k}(1+\tau_{k})\norm{v^{k}-\bar{u}}^{2}-Q_{k}\tau_{k}\norm{v^{k-1}-\bar{u}}^{2}

\displaystyle+Q_{k}\lambda_{k-1}(1-\tau_{k-1})\norm{v^{k}-v^{k-1}}^{2}+E(\delta_{k}).

Subtracting
\tau_{k}\norm{v^k-\bar{u}}^{2}
from both sides and using that
(\tau_{k})_{k}
is monotonically increasing, we can continue our estimation by

\displaystyle\norm{v^{k+1}-\bar{u}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\tau_{k}\norm{v^k-\bar{u}}^{2}+\lambda_{k}(1-\tau_{k})\norm{v^{k+1}-v^k}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Q_{k}\norm{v^k-\bar{u}}^{2}-Q_{k}\tau_{k-1}\norm{v^{k-1}-\bar{u}}^{2}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;+Q_{k}\lambda_{k-1}(1-\tau_{k-1})\norm{v^{k}-v^{k-1}}^{2}+E(\delta_{k}).

Consequently, if we define

V_{k}\triangleq\norm{v^{k}-\bar{u}}^{2}-\tau_{k-1}\norm{v^{k-1}-\bar{u}}^{2}+\lambda_{k-1}(1-\tau_{k-1})\norm{v^{k}-v^{k-1}}^{2},

we obtain
V_{k+1}\leq Q_{k}V_{k}+E(\delta_{k})
for all
k\geq 1
. Notice that
E(\delta_{k})
is indeed summable and
Q_{k}\in(0,1)
and therefore we can apply Lemma
5
and we obtain
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}[V_{k}]_{+}<\infty
,
and hence
[V_{k}]_{+}
converges to
0
. Moreover,

V_{k}\geq\norm{v^k-\bar{u}}^{2}-\tau_{k-1}\norm{v^{k-1}-\bar{u}}^{2}\geq\norm{v^k-\bar{u}}^{2}-\bar{\tau}\norm{v^{k-1}-\bar{u}}^{2},

where
\bar{\tau}=\sup_{k}\tau_{k}\in(0,1)
. Applying again Lemma
5
with the identification
w_{k}=\norm{v^k-\bar{u}}^{2}
and
d=\bar{\tau}
, we can conclude
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\norm{v^k-\bar{u}}^{2}<\infty
and therefore
\norm{v^k-\bar{u}}^{2}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
.
∎

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\min_{x_{1}}f_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})\coloneqq 20-0.1x_{1}x_{2}+x_{1}\\

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\min_{x_{2}}f_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})\coloneqq-20+0.1x_{1}x_{2}-x_{1}\\
&\text{s.t.}~x_{2}\in X_{2}\coloneqq[10,50].\end{cases}

We seek a saddle point
(x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*})\in X\coloneqq X_{1}\times X_{2}
of the function
f(x_{1},x_{2})\coloneqq 20-0.1x_{1}x_{2}+x_{1}
, namely a point that satisfies

f(x_{1}^{*},x_{2})\leq f(x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*})\leq f(x_{1},x_{2}^{*})\quad\text{for all $(x_{1},x_{2})\in X$.}

The solution set is characterized through the inclusion

0\in F+\NC_{X}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-0.1\\
0.1&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}1\\
0\end{bmatrix}+\NC_{X}.

The set of solutions is
X_{1}\times\{10\}
. We solve the problem

\min\left\{\phi(x_{1},x_{2})\coloneq(x_{1},x_{2})\in\zer(F+\NC_{X})\right\},

where
\phi(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}
, whose analytical solution is
(11,10)
.
Equivalently, we aim to find a point
(x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*})\in\zer(F+NC_{X})
such that
\langle\nabla\phi(x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*}),(w_{1},w_{2})-(x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*})\rangle\geq 0\;\text{for all $(w_{1},w_{2})\in\zer(F+\NC_{X})$}
.
The subproblem of Algorithm
\texttt{IKM}(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon,\gamma,w)
is thus given by determining a solution to

\overline{u}^{t+1}=\zer(F+\NC_{X}+\beta_{t}\nabla\phi+\alpha(I-w^{t})).

We define the smooth part to be
\Phi=F+\beta_{t}\nabla\phi+\alpha(I-w^{t})
which is Lipschitz continuous with constant
L=0.1+\beta_{t}+\alpha
. We note that
\Phi
is
\alpha
-strongly monotone and select a step-size
\gamma={\alpha}/{L^{2}}
, such that the operator
T
defined by the forward-backward map is contractive with constant
\sqrt{1-{\alpha^{2}}/{L^{2}}}
. We consider the relaxation parameter of the inner loop
\theta_{k}\equiv\theta\in(0,1)
to be constant. Across inner loops, we consider the acceleration parameter
\tau_{k}\equiv\tau
to be constant and to be the largest value satisfying equation (
11
). We assume
\beta_{t}=(t+1)^{-\eta}
, where
\eta=0.55
, and a stopping criterion of
\varepsilon_{t}=\bar{\varepsilon}\cdot{(t+1)^{-2}}
, where
\bar{\varepsilon}=10^{-3}
. We run a total of
100
iterations. The iterates for various starting points and different values for
\alpha
, along with the feasible region
X
are shown in figures
1
,
2
and
3
.

Chern-Simons Higgs models for p p -Laplacian on finite graphs: a topological degree approach
Chunlian Liu, Yating Ge, Linfeng Wang

Abstract
We investigate the Chern-Simons Higgs models for p p -Laplacian on a connected finite graph, employing topological degree theory as our main tool. Notably, we overcome the difficulties arising from the nonlinearity of p p -Laplacian operator and calculate the corresponding topological degree through a more general approach.

Introduction

Let
G=(V,E)
be a connected finite graph, where
V
denotes the set of vertices and
E
the set of edges. For
p>1
, the
p
-Laplacian of a function
u:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\Delta_{p}u(x)=\sum_{y\sim x}|u(y)-u(x)|^{p-2}(u(y)-u(x)),

where
y\sim x
indicates that
y
is adjacent to
x
, i.e.
xy\in E
. In particular, the
2
-Laplacian of a function
u:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\Delta u(x)=\sum_{y\sim x}(u(y)-u(x)).

We are interested in the Chern-Simons Higgs models for
p
-Laplacian of the form

\Delta_{p}u=\lambda e^{u}(e^{u}-1)+f

(1.1)

on
G
, where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, and
f:V\to\mathbb{R}
is a given function.

The study of Chern-Simons Higgs models can be traced back to the works of Hong-Kim-Pac
Hong-1990
and Jackiw-Weinberg
Jackiw-1990
. It is one of the classical partial differential equations commonly encountered in geometry and physics, and has attracted considerable attention from mathematicians. Many researchers have contributed to this field; see, for instance,
2
,
3
,
9
,
31
,
41
,
43
,
46
, and the references therein.

Recently, there has been growing interest among scholars in the analysis of Chern-Simons Higgs models on graphs. In particular, Huang-Lin-Yau
Huang-2020
and Hou-Sun
Hou-2022
investigated the existence of solutions to the equation

\Delta u=\lambda e^{u}(e^{u}-1)+4\mathord{\text{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{p_{i}},

where
N
is a positive integer,
p_{1},\cdots,p_{N}
are arbitrarily chosen vertices on the graph and
\delta_{p_{i}}
is the Dirac delta
mass at
p_{i}
. Their approach was based on the method of upper and lower solutions. Using the variational method, Chao-Hou
Chao-2023
investigated the existence of solutions to the Chern-Simons Higgs models of the form

\Delta u=\lambda e^{u}(e^{u}-1)^{5}+4\mathord{\text{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{p_{i}}.

For more related works, we refer the reader to
Huang-2021
,
Li-CVPDE-2024
and the references therein.

It is well known that the topological degree theory is a powerful tool for studying partial differential equations in the Euclidean space and on Riemann surfaces; it can also be applied to deal with partial differential equations on finite graphs. This approach was first used by Sun-Wang
Sun-adv-2022
to solve the Kazdan-Warner equation on finite graphs

\Delta u=c-he^{u},

where
h
is a real function on
V
and
c
is a real constant. Recently, the topological degree theory was also employed by Liu
Liu-2022
to study the mean field equation

\Delta u=\rho\left(\frac{he^{u}}{\int_{V}he^{u}d\mu}-\frac{1}{|V|}\right)

with
\rho\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
,
h:V\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
being a function satisfying
\min_{x\in V}h(x)>0
, and
|V|
denoting the cardinality of the vertex set
V
. Here

\int_{V}gd\mu=\sum_{x\in V}g(x)

denotes the integral of
g:V\to\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, Li-Sun-Yang
Li-CVPDE-2024
also used it to investigate the Chern-Simons Higgs models on finite graphs of the form

\Delta u=\lambda e^{u}(e^{u}-1)+f.

Our aim is to apply this powerful tool to study the Chern-Simons Higgs models (
1.1
) for the
p
-Laplacian. Since
\Delta_{p}
is nonlinear, new challenges arise in calculating the topological degree of the corresponding map.

It is well known that applying topological degree theory involves two crucial steps. The first step is to establish a priori bound for solutions, as stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a connected finite graph. Suppose
\sigma\in[0,1]
, and
\lambda
and
f
satisfy
\lambda\int_{V}fd\mu<0
. If
u
is a solution of the equation

\Delta_{p}u=\lambda e^{u}\left(e^{u}-\sigma\right)+f,\quad\hbox{in}~~V,

(1.2)

then there exists a constant
C
, depending only on
\lambda
,
f
and
|V|
, such that
|u(x)|\leqslant C
for all
x\in V
.

In Theorem
1.1
and the following two theorems,
\lambda
is a real number, and
f
is a fixed function on
V
. When
\sigma=1
, equation (
1.2
) is exactly (
1.1
). Furthermore, we define the space
L^{\infty}(V)
and its associated norm as

L^{\infty}(V)=\{u:V\to\mathbb{R}:\|u\|_{L_{\infty}(V)}<+\infty\},

and

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}=\sup_{x\in V}|u(x)|,

respectively. Let
X=L^{\infty}(V)
, in which the zero element is denoted by
0
, and define a map
F:X\rightarrow X
as

F(u)=-\Delta_{p}u+\lambda e^{u}\left(e^{u}-1\right)+f.

(1.3)

The second step is to calculate the topological degree of

by utilizing its homotopy invariance property.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a connected finite graph, and let

be the map defined by (

). Assume that
\lambda\int_{V}fd\mu<0
. Then there exists a

\operatorname{deg}\left(F,B_{R},0\right)=\operatorname{sgn}{\lambda}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\vskip 2.84544pt\displaystyle 1,&\text{if}\quad\lambda>0,\\
\displaystyle-1,&\text{if}\quad\lambda<0,\end{array}\right.

where

is the ball in

centered at

with radius

, and
\deg(F,B_{R},0)
is the topological degree of

in

at

.

As an application of the above theorem, we establish the following existence result for the Chern-Simons Higgs models involving
p
-Laplacian.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a connected finite graph, and
\lambda\int_{V}fd\mu<0
. Then equation (
1.1
) has a solution.

Remark 1.1
.

As is well known, the fundamental idea of the topological degree theory is to reduce a complex equation (or system) to a simple equation (or system) through the homotopy invariance of the topological degree. In general, the existence of solutions to the simple equation (or system) is either clear or can be proven. Then one can calculate that the topological degree of some operator associated to the simple equation (or system) is not zero. In Section 4, after reducing equation (
1.1
) to equation (
4.3
), it becomes essential to investigate the existence of solutions to equation (
4.3
), which constitutes the primary focus of Section 3.

Remark 1.2
.

Many researchers focus on graphs equipped with weights and measures. all edges

carry weights
w_{xy}>0
satisfying
w_{xy}=w_{yx}
. The measure on
V
is defined as
\mu:V\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
. In this paper, we assume that
w\equiv 1
and
\mu\equiv 1
, as the specific choices of weights and measures are not essential for our analysis.

In the following we will use
C
to represent the corresponding constants, which may not necessarily be equal in the context.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we provide a priori estimate of solutions for equation (

) (Theorem

1.2
and Theorem

2
A priori estimate

First, we introduce some basic definitions and preliminary lemmas. For any
x\in V
, the gradient of
u
at
x
is defined as

\nabla u(x)=\big(\left(u\left(y_{1}\right)-u(x)\right),\cdots,\left(u\left(y_{l_{x}}\right)-u(x)\right)\big),

where
y_{1},\cdots,y_{l_{x}}
are all distinct points adjacent to
x
, and
l_{x}
denotes the number of edges incident to
x
. Clearly, such an
l_{x}
is unique and
\nabla u(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{l_{x}}
. The integral of
|\nabla u|^{p}
on
V
is given by

\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{y\sim x}|u(y)-u(x)|^{p}.

Furthermore, we define a Sobolev space and its associated norm as

W^{1,p}(V)=\left\{u:V\to\mathbb{R}:\int_{V}(|u|^{p}+|\nabla u|^{p})d\mu<+\infty\right\},

and

\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(V)}=\left(\int_{V}(|u|^{p}+|\nabla u|^{p})d\mu\right)^{1/p},

respectively. Since
G=(V,E)
is a finite graph, then
W^{1,p}(V)
is exactly the set of all real functions on
V
, a finite dimensional linear space. Consequently, one has the following two lemmas, which were proved in
Ge-2020
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph. The Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(V)
is precompact. Namely, if
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
W^{1,p}(V)
, then there exists some
u\in W^{1,p}(V)
such that, up to a subsequence,
u_{n}\to u
in
W^{1,p}(V)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph. We use

\bar{g}=\frac{1}{|V|}\int_{V}gd\mu

to denote the integral mean of the function
g:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. For all functions
u\in W^{1,p}(V)
with
\bar{u}=0
, there exists a positive constant
C
depending on
p
and
|V|
such that

\int_{V}|u|^{p}d\mu\leqslant C\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu.

Next, we proceed to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, following the main idea inspired by the proof of Theorem 1 in
Li-CVPDE-2024
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Suppose that
u
is a solution of equation (
1.2
). By integrating on both sides of equation (
1.2
) on
V
, we have

0=\int_{V}\Delta_{p}ud\mu=\lambda\int_{V}e^{u}\left(e^{u}-\sigma\right)d\mu+\int_{V}fd\mu.

(2.1)

Next, we divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1.
We prove that
u
has a uniform upper bound.
Without loss of generality, we may assume
\max\limits_{V}u>0
. For otherwise,
u
has already an upper bound 0. Note that

\left|\int_{u<0}e^{u}\left(e^{u}-\sigma\right)d\mu\right|\leqslant|V|.

Furthermore, by (
2.1
), we can deduce that

\int_{u\geqslant 0}e^{u}\left(e^{u}-\sigma\right)d\mu\leqslant|V|+\frac{1}{|\lambda|}\left|\int_{V}fd\mu\right|\leqslant|V|\left(1+\frac{1}{|\lambda|}|\bar{f}|\right)=:a.

Here and hereafter,
\bar{f}=\frac{1}{|V|}\int_{V}f~d\mu
denotes the integral mean of the function
f
. Together with

\int_{u\geqslant 0}e^{u}\left(e^{u}-\sigma\right)d\mu=\sum_{x\in V,u(x)\geqslant 0}e^{u(x)}\left(e^{u(x)}-\sigma\right)\geqslant e^{\max\limits_{V}u}\left(e^{\max\limits_{V}u}-1\right),

it follows that

\max_{V}u\leqslant\ln\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4a}}{2}.

(2.2)

Step 2.
We prove that
u
has a uniform lower bound. For any
x\in V
, by (
1.2
) and (
2.2
), keeping in mind
\sigma\in[0,1]
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 14.22636pt\displaystyle|\Delta_{p}u(x)|&\leqslant|\lambda|\left|e^{u(x)}\left(e^{u(x)}-\sigma\right)\right|+|f(x)|\\
&\leqslant|\lambda|\left(e^{2u(x)}+e^{u(x)}\right)+|f(x)|\\
&\leqslant|\lambda|\big(\frac{\left(1+\sqrt{1+4a}\right)^{2}}{4}+\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4a}}{2}\big)+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}\\
&=:b.\end{array}

Hence,

\|\Delta_{p}u\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}\leqslant b.

(2.3)

We may assume
V=\left\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{l}\right\}
,
u\left(x_{1}\right)=\max\limits_{V}u
,
u\left(x_{l}\right)=\min\limits_{V}u
, and without loss of generality
x_{1}x_{2},x_{2}x_{3},\cdots,x_{l-1}x_{l}
is the shortest path connecting
x_{1}
and
x_{l}
. Then, by Jensen’s inequality, we can deduce that

\displaystyle 0\leqslant u\left(x_{1}\right)-u\left(x_{l}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{l-1}\left|u\left(x_{j}\right)-u\left(x_{j+1}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\big((l-1)^{p-1}\big)^{1/p}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{l-1}\left|u\left(x_{j}\right)-u\left(x_{j+1}\right)\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leqslant\big(2(l-1)^{p-1}\big)^{1/p}\left(\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu\right)^{1/p}.

(2.4)

Since

\displaystyle\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu+\int_{V}u\Delta_{p}ud\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{y\sim x}|u(y)-u(x)|^{p-2}(u(y)-u(x))^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{y\sim x}u(x)|u(y)-u(x)|^{p-2}(u(y)-u(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{y\sim x}|u(y)-u(x)|^{p-2}(u(y)-u(x))(u(y)-u(x)+2u(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{y\sim x}|u(y)-u(x)|^{p-2}(u(y)-u(x))(u(y)+u(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

by Lemma
2.2
, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu

\displaystyle=-\int_{V}(u-\bar{u})\Delta_{p}ud\mu

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int_{V}|u-\bar{u}|^{p}d\mu\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{V}|\Delta_{p}u|^{q}d\mu\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(C\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{V}|\Delta_{p}u|^{q}d\mu\right)^{1/q},

where
q
is a positive integer satisfying
\dfrac{1}{p}+\dfrac{1}{q}=1
. Thus, we have

\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu\leqslant C\int_{V}|\Delta_{p}u|^{q}d\mu\leqslant C\|\Delta_{p}u\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}^{q}|V|.

(2.5)

Combining (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we can conclude

\max_{V}u-\min_{V}u\leqslant\left(2(l-1)^{p-1}|V|C\right)^{1/p}\|\Delta_{p}u\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}^{q/p}.

(2.6)

Then from (
2.3
), we obtain

\max_{V}u-\min_{V}u\leqslant c_{0}:=b^{q/p}\left(2(l-1)^{p-1}|V|C\right)^{1/p}.

(2.7)

Returning to (
2.1
), it follows that

\int_{V}e^{u}\left(e^{u}-\sigma\right)d\mu=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{V}fd\mu=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\bar{f}|V|=:\zeta.

(2.8)

Since
\lambda\int_{V}fd\mu<0
, then
\zeta>0
. We claim that

\max_{V}u>-A:=\ln\min\left\{1,\frac{\zeta}{4|V|}\right\}.

(2.9)

For otherwise,
\max_{V}u\leqslant-A
, which, together with (
2.8
), implies

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 11.38092pt\displaystyle\zeta=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\bar{f}|V|=\left|\int_{V}e^{u}\left(e^{u}-\sigma\right)d\mu\right|\leqslant\int_{V}\left(e^{2u}+e^{u}\right)d\mu\\
\vskip 5.69046pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leqslant\left(e^{2\max\limits_{V}u}+e^{\max\limits_{V}u}\right)|V|\leqslant 2e^{-A}|V|<\frac{\zeta}{2},\end{array}

which is impossible. This confirms our claim. Inserting (
2.9
) into (
2.7
), we have

-A-c_{0}\leqslant\min_{V}u\leqslant\max_{V}u\leqslant\ln\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4a}}{2}.

∎

3
Preliminary lemmas

In this section, we present some lemmas in preparation for the proof of Theorem 1.2 in the next section. Our main focus is on the existence of solutions to the equation

\Delta_{p}u=\lambda e^{2u}+\varepsilon f,

(3.1)

where
\varepsilon>0
is a positive constant.

3.1
The case
\lambda>0

First, we introduce the definitions of the lower and upper solutions to equation (
3.1
).

We call a function
u_{-}
a lower solution to equation (
3.1
) if, for all
x\in V
, it satisfies

\Delta_{p}u_{-}\geqslant\lambda e^{2u_{-}}+\varepsilon f.

Similarly, a function
u_{+}
is called an upper solution to equation (
3.1
) if, for all
x\in V
, it satisfies

\Delta_{p}u_{+}\leqslant\lambda e^{2u_{+}}+\varepsilon f.

In the following, Lemma
3.1
concerns the method of upper and lower solutions, which serves as a preparation for the proof of Lemma
3.2
. Lemma
3.2
establishes the existence of a solution to equation (
3.1
) when
\lambda>0
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\lambda>0
and
\int_{V}fd\mu<0
. If there exist lower and upper solutions
u_{-}
and
u_{+}
to the equation (
3.1
) satisfying
u_{-}\leqslant u_{+}
, then equation (
3.1
) has a solution
u
satisfying
u_{-}\leqslant u\leqslant u_{+}
.

Proof.

Set
k=2\lambda e^{2u_{+}}>0
. We define

L\varphi\equiv\Delta_{p}\varphi-k\varphi.

Set
u_{0}=u_{+}
, and for each integer
n\geqslant 0
,

Lu_{n+1}=\lambda e^{2u_{n}}+\varepsilon f-ku_{n}.

We claim that

u_{-}\leqslant u_{n+1}\leqslant u_{n}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant u_{1}\leqslant u_{0}=u_{+}.

To prove the above claim, we just need to prove

Lu_{-}\geqslant Lu_{n+1}\geqslant Lu_{n}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant Lu_{1}\geqslant Lu_{0}=Lu_{+},

according to the order-preserving property of
L
in Theorem 2.1 in
Ge-2020
, which is, if
Lu\geqslant Lv
, then
u\leqslant v
. We prove this by induction. It is straightforward to deduce that

Lu_{1}-Lu_{0}=\lambda e^{2u_{0}}+\varepsilon f-ku_{0}-Lu_{+}\geqslant 0.

By the mean value theorem, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle Lu_{-}-Lu_{1}=(\Delta_{p}u_{-}-ku_{-})-(\lambda e^{2u_{+}}+\varepsilon f-ku_{+})\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\geqslant k(u_{+}-u_{-})-\lambda(e^{2u_{+}}-e^{2u-})\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad=k(u_{+}-u_{-})-2\lambda e^{2\xi}(u_{+}-u_{-})\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad=2\lambda(u_{+}-u_{-})(e^{2u_{+}}-e^{2\xi})\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\geqslant 0,\end{array}

where
u_{-}\leqslant\xi\leqslant u_{+}
. Thus, we have

Lu_{-}\geqslant Lu_{1}\geqslant Lu_{0}=Lu_{+}.

Suppose

Lu_{-}\geqslant Lu_{n}\geqslant Lu_{n-1}\geqslant Lu_{+},

for some
n\geqslant 1
. Then

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle Lu_{n+1}-Lu_{n}=\lambda(e^{2u_{n}}-e^{2u_{n-1}})-k(u_{n}-u_{n-1})\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\quad=2\lambda e^{2\xi}(u_{n}-u_{n-1})-k(u_{n}-u_{n-1})\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\quad=2\lambda(e^{2\xi}-e^{2u_{+}})(u_{n}-u_{n-1})\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\quad\geqslant 0,\end{array}

where
u_{n}\leqslant\xi\leqslant u_{n-1}\leqslant u_{+}
. Similarly,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle Lu_{-}-Lu_{n+1}=(\Delta_{p}u_{-}-ku_{-})-(\lambda e^{2u_{n}}+\varepsilon f-ku_{n})\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\geqslant k(u_{n}-u_{-})-\lambda(e^{2u_{n}}-e^{2u_{-}})\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad=k(u_{n}-u_{-})-2\lambda e^{2\xi}(u_{n}-u_{-})\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad=2\lambda(e^{2u_{+}}-e^{2\xi})(u_{n}-u_{-})\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\geqslant 0,\end{array}

where
u_{-}\leqslant\xi\leqslant u_{n}\leqslant u_{+}
. Hence we obtain

Lu_{-}\geqslant Lu_{n+1}\geqslant Lu_{n}\geqslant Lu_{+}.

By induction, the claim above has been proven. Since
V
is finite, by Lemma
2.1
, there exists a
u
, up to a subsequence,
u_{n}\rightarrow u
as
n\rightarrow\infty
uniformly on
V
. Note that

Lu_{n+1}=\Delta_{p}u_{n+1}-ku_{n+1}=\lambda e^{2u_{n}}+\varepsilon f-ku_{n},

let
n\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\Delta_{p}u=\lambda e^{2u}+\varepsilon f.

Hence,
u
is a solution to the equation (
3.1
).
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph. Assume that
\lambda
and
f
satisfy
\lambda>0
and
\int_{V}fd\mu<0
, respectively. Then, for any given
\varepsilon>0
, equation (
3.1
) has a solution.

Proof.

Let
v_{\varepsilon}
be a solution of the equation (The solvability of the equation can be found in
Ge-2020
)

\Delta_{p}v=\varepsilon f-\varepsilon\bar{f}.

Consider
u_{-}=v_{\varepsilon}-A
with
A>0
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\Delta_{p}u_{-}-\lambda e^{2u_{-}}-\varepsilon f=\Delta_{p}v_{\varepsilon}-\lambda e^{2(v_{\varepsilon}-A)}-\varepsilon f\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad~=-\varepsilon\bar{f}-\lambda e^{2v_{\varepsilon}}e^{-2A}\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad~\to-\varepsilon\bar{f},\quad\hbox{as}~A\to+\infty,\end{array}

uniformly for
x\in V
. Noting that
\bar{f}<0
, we can find a sufficiently large
A
such that

\Delta_{p}u_{-}-\lambda e^{2u_{-}}-\varepsilon f\geqslant 0.

Therefore, equation (
3.1
) has a lower solution
u_{-}
.

Consider
u_{+}=v_{\varepsilon}+b
with
b>0
. Noting that
\lambda>0
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\Delta_{p}u_{+}-\lambda e^{2u_{+}}-\varepsilon f=\Delta_{p}v_{\varepsilon}-\lambda e^{2(v_{\varepsilon}+b)}-\varepsilon f\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad~=-\varepsilon\bar{f}-\lambda e^{2v_{\varepsilon}}e^{2b}\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad~\to-\infty,\quad\hbox{as}~b\to+\infty,\end{array}

uniformly for
x\in V
. We can therefore find a sufficiently large
b
such that

\Delta_{p}u_{+}-\lambda e^{2u_{+}}-\varepsilon f\leqslant 0.

Hence, equation (
3.1
) has an upper solution
u_{+}
. By Lemma
3.1
, we can conclude that equation (
3.1
) has a solution
u
satisfying
u_{-}\leqslant u\leqslant u_{+}
. The proof is completed.
∎

3.2
The case
\lambda<0

In this subsection, we consider the existence of solutions to equation (
3.1
) when
\lambda<0
. The main result is stated in Lemma
3.5
, while Lemmas
3.3

For all
u\in W^{1,p}(V)

\bar{u}_{f}=\frac{\int_{V}fud\mu}{\int_{V}fd\mu}=\frac{1}{\bar{f}|V|}\int_{V}fud\mu.

We begin with the following result, whose proof is similar to that of Lemma

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph. For all functions
u\in W^{1,p}(V)
with
\bar{u}_{f}=0
, there exists a positive constant
C
depending on
p
,
f
and
|V|
such that

\int_{V}|u|^{p}d\mu\leqslant C\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph. For any
\beta\geqslant 0
and
\alpha\geqslant 0
, there exist two constants
C^{*}
(depending on
p
,
f
and
|V|
) and
C_{*}
(depending on
\alpha
,
\beta
,
p
,
f
and
|V|
) such that for all functions
u
satisfying
\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu\leqslant 1
and
\bar{u}_{f}=0
, it holds that

\|u\|_{\infty}\leqslant C^{*},

and

\int_{V}e^{\beta|u|^{\alpha}}d\mu\leqslant C_{*}.

Proof.

For any function
u
satisfying
\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu\leqslant 1
and
\bar{u}_{f}=0
, by Lemma
3.3
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\|u\|_{\infty}\leqslant\sum_{x\in V}|u(x)|=\int_{V}|u|d\mu\leqslant\left(\int_{V}|u|^{p}d\mu\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{V}1^{q}d\mu\right)^{1/q}\\
\displaystyle\qquad\quad\leqslant\left(C\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu\right)^{1/p}|V|^{1/q}\leqslant C^{1/p}|V|^{1/q}:=C^{*},\end{array}

where
q
is a positive integer satisfying
\dfrac{1}{p}+\dfrac{1}{q}=1
. Since
\beta\geqslant 0
and
\alpha\geqslant 0
, we have

\int_{V}e^{\beta|u|^{\alpha}}d\mu\leqslant\int_{V}e^{\beta\|u\|_{\infty}^{\alpha}}d\mu\leqslant e^{\beta{C^{*}}^{\alpha}}|V|:=C_{*}.

∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph. Assume that
\lambda
and
f
satisfy
\lambda<0
and
\bar{f}>0
, respectively. Then, for any given
\varepsilon>0
, equation (
3.1
) has a solution.

Proof.

Define a set

\mathcal{B}=\left\{u\in W^{1,p}(V):\lambda\int_{V}e^{2u}d\mu+\varepsilon\bar{f}|V|=0\right\}.

We claim that
\mathcal{B}\neq\mathord{\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\text{}$}}
.

Next, we prove the above claim. Define a function

u_{l}(x)=l,\quad\forall~x\in V.

Since
\lambda<0
, then we have

-\lambda\int_{V}e^{2u_{l}}d\mu=-\lambda e^{2l}|V|\to+\infty,\quad\hbox{as}~~l\to+\infty.

We also define
\tilde{u}_{l}\equiv-l
, which leads to

-\lambda\int_{V}e^{2\tilde{u}_{l}}d\mu=-\lambda e^{-2l}|V|\to 0,\quad\hbox{as}~l\to+\infty.

Hence, there exists a sufficiently large
l
such that

-\lambda\int_{V}e^{2u_{l}}d\mu>\varepsilon\bar{f}|V|\quad\hbox{and}\quad-\lambda\int_{V}e^{2\tilde{u}_{l}}d\mu<\varepsilon\bar{f}|V|.

We define a function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\phi(t)=-\lambda\int_{V}e^{2\left(tu_{l}+(1-t)\tilde{u}_{l}\right)}d\mu.

Then,

\phi(0)<\varepsilon\bar{f}|V|<\phi(1),

and thus there exists a
t_{0}\in(0,1)
such that
\phi(t_{0})=\varepsilon\bar{f}|V|
. Therefore, we have shown that
\mathcal{B}\neq\mathord{\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\text{}$}}
.

We solve (
3.1
) by minimizing the functional

I(u)=\frac{1}{p}\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu+\varepsilon\int_{V}fud\mu

on
\mathcal{B}
. For any
u\in\mathcal{B}
, we have

\int_{V}e^{2u-2\bar{u}_{f}}d\mu=e^{-2\bar{u}_{f}}\int_{V}e^{2u}d\mu=e^{-2\bar{u}_{f}}\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}\bar{f}|V|\right),

and thus

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle I(u)=\frac{1}{p}\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu+\varepsilon\int_{V}fud\mu-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\int_{V}f\ln\left(\int_{V}e^{2u-2\bar{u}_{f}}d\mu\right)d\mu\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\int_{V}f\ln\left(\int_{V}e^{2u-2\bar{u}_{f}}d\mu\right)d\mu\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{p}\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\int_{V}f\ln\left(\int_{V}e^{2u-2\bar{u}_{f}}d\mu\right)d\mu\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\varepsilon\int_{V}fud\mu+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\int_{V}f\left[-2\bar{u}_{f}+\ln\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}\bar{f}|V|\right)\right]d\mu.\end{array}

Note that

\int_{V}fud\mu=\bar{u}_{f}\int_{V}fd\mu,

then, we have

I(u)=\frac{1}{p}\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\int_{V}f\ln\left(\int_{V}e^{2u-2\bar{u}_{f}}d\mu\right)d\mu+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\bar{f}|V|\ln\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}\bar{f}|V|\right).

Set

w=\frac{u-\bar{u}_{f}}{\|\nabla(u-\bar{u}_{f})\|_{p}}=\frac{u-\bar{u}_{f}}{\|\nabla u\|_{p}},

then

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\bar{w}_{f}=\frac{1}{\bar{f}|V|}\int_{V}wfd\mu=\frac{1}{\bar{f}|V|}\int_{V}\frac{(u-\bar{u}_{f})f}{\|\nabla(u-\bar{u}_{f})\|_{p}}d\mu=0,\end{array}

and
\|\nabla w\|_{p}^{p}=1
. Furthermore, by Lemma
3.4
, for any
\beta\geqslant 0
and
\alpha\geqslant 0
, one can find a constant
C_{*}

depending on
\alpha
,
\beta
,
p
,
f
and
|V|
such that

\int_{V}e^{\beta|w|^{\alpha}}d\mu\leqslant C_{*}.

On the other hand, by an elementary inequality for
\delta>0
and conjugate exponents
p,q
(
\dfrac{1}{p}+\dfrac{1}{q}=1
):

ab\leqslant\dfrac{\delta^{p}}{p}a^{p}+\dfrac{\delta^{-q}}{q}b^{q},\quad\forall a,b\geqslant 0,

we have

u-\bar{u}_{f}=\|\nabla u\|_{p}w\leqslant\frac{\delta^{p}}{p}\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{\delta^{-q}}{q}|w|^{q}.

Then, it follows that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\int_{V}e^{2u-2\bar{u}_{f}}d\mu\leqslant\int_{V}e^{2\left(\frac{\delta^{p}}{p}\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{\delta^{-q}}{q}|w|^{q}\right)}d\mu=e^{\frac{2\delta^{p}}{p}\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}}\int_{V}e^{\frac{2\delta^{-q}}{q}|w|^{q}}d\mu\leqslant Ce^{\frac{2\delta^{p}}{p}\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}},\end{array}

where
C
is a positive constant depending on
p
,
\delta
,
f
and
|V|
. The above inequality leads to

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle I(u)\geqslant\frac{1}{p}\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu-\varepsilon\bar{f}|V|\frac{\delta^{p}}{p}\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}-C_{1},\end{array}

where

C_{1}=\dfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon\bar{f}|V|\ln C-\dfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon\bar{f}|V|\ln\left(-\dfrac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}\bar{f}|V|\right).

Choosing
\delta=\left(2\varepsilon\bar{f}|V|\right)^{-1/p}
, we obtain for all
u\in\mathcal{B}
,

I(u)\geqslant\frac{1}{2p}\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu-C_{1}.

(3.2)

Therefore,
I
has a lower bound on the set
\mathcal{B}
. This allows us to define

\hat{b}=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{B}}I(u).

Take a sequence of functions
\{u_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{B}
such that
I(u_{n})\to\hat{b}
. We can suppose that

I(u_{n})\leqslant\hat{b}+1,

for every
n
. It follows from (
3.2
) that

\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{p}^{p}\leqslant 2p\left(I(u_{n})+C_{1}\right)\leqslant 2p\left(\hat{b}+1+C_{1}\right).

Since
u_{n}\in\mathcal{B}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle|{\overline{u_{n}}}_{f}|

\displaystyle=\dfrac{|\int_{V}fu_{n}d\mu|}{\int_{V}fd\mu}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon\bar{f}|V|}\left|I(u_{n})-\dfrac{1}{p}\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon\bar{f}|V|}\bigg(3(\hat{b}+1)+2C_{1}\bigg).

(3.3)

Note that
\int_{V}(u_{n}-{\overline{u_{n}}}_{f})fd\mu=0
, by Lemma
3.3
, we have

\|u_{n}-{\overline{u_{n}}}_{f}\|_{p}^{p}\leqslant C\int_{V}|\nabla(u_{n}-{\overline{u_{n}}}_{f})|^{p}d\mu=C\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{p}^{p}.

(3.4)

Thus, by (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we have

\|u_{n}\|_{p}\leqslant\|u_{n}-{\overline{u_{n}}}_{f}\|_{p}+\|{\overline{u_{n}}}_{f}\|_{p}\leqslant C_{2},

where
C_{2}
is a positive constant depending on
\hat{b}
,
p
,
\delta
,
f
and
|V|
. Therefore,
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
W^{1,p}(V)
. Since
V
is finite, by Lemma
2.1
, there exists some
u\in W^{1,p}(V)
such that, up to a subsequence,
u_{n}\to u
in
W^{1,p}(V)
. It is easy to see that
u\in\mathcal{B}
and
I(u)=\hat{b}
.

Finally, we derive the Euler-Lagrange equation of
I(u)
. For any
h\in W^{1,p}(V)
, it holds

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle 0=\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\bigg\{I(u+th)+\tau\left(\int_{V}\lambda e^{2(u+th)}d\mu+\varepsilon\bar{f}|V|\right)\bigg\}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\quad=\int_{V}\left(-\Delta_{p}u+2\lambda\tau e^{2u}+\varepsilon f\right)hd\mu,\end{array}

where
\tau
is a constant. Thus, we have

\Delta_{p}u=2\lambda\tau e^{2u}+\varepsilon f.

(3.5)

Integrating on both sides of equation (
3.5
), we obtain
\tau=1/2
. Then
u
is a solution of equation (
3.1
).
∎

4
Topological degree and the existence result

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
. We first calculate the topological degree of certain maps related to the Chern-Simons Higgs models for
p
-Laplacian.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
V=\left\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{l}\right\}
be a finite set, and define
X=L^{\infty}(V)
. We may identify
X
with the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{l}
. To apply the homotopy invariance of the topological degree without introducing ambiguity, we define a map
F:X\times[0,1]\rightarrow X
by

F(u,\sigma)=-\Delta_{p}u+\lambda e^{u}\left(e^{u}-\sigma\right)+f,\quad(u,\sigma)\in X\times[0,1].

Then we can conclude that
F
is well-defined. In fact, if
p\geqslant 2
, it is easy to see that
F
is well-defined. If
1<p<2
, for any
y_{*}\sim x
satisfying
u(y_{*})=u(x)
, we can remove such
y_{*}
from the summation, leading to

\Delta_{p}u(x)=\sum_{\{y:~y\sim x\}\setminus\{y_{*}|u(y_{*})=u(x)\}}|u(y)-u(x)|^{p-2}(u(y)-u(x)),

which ensures that
F
remains a well-defined map.

For the real number
\lambda
and the fixed function
f
, since
\lambda\bar{f}<0
, then by applying Theorem
1.1
, we can conclude that there exists a constant
R_{*}>0
, depending only on
\lambda
,
f
and
|V|
, such that for all
\sigma\in[0,1]
, all solutions of
F(u,\sigma)=0
satisfy

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}<R_{*}.

Denote by
B_{r}\subset X
a ball centered at
0\in X
with radius
r
, and its boundary by
\partial B_{r}=\left\{u\in X:\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}=r\right\}
. Thus we can conclude that

0\notin F\left(\partial B_{R},\sigma\right),\quad\forall~\sigma\in[0,1],~\forall~R\geqslant R_{*}.

By the homotopy invariance of the topological degree, we have

\operatorname{deg}\left(F(\cdot,1),B_{R},0\right)=\operatorname{deg}\left(F(\cdot,0),B_{R},0\right),\quad\forall~R\geqslant R_{*}.

(4.1)

Given any
\varepsilon>0
, we define another map
G_{\varepsilon}:X\times[0,1]\rightarrow X
by

G_{\varepsilon}(u,t)=-\Delta_{p}u+\lambda e^{2u}+(t+(1-t)\varepsilon)f,\quad(u,t)\in X\times[0,1].

As with
F
, the map
G_{\varepsilon}
is well-defined. Since
\lambda\int_{V}fd\mu<0
, by applying Theorem
1.1
again, there exists a constant
R_{\varepsilon}^{*}>0
, depending only on
\varepsilon
,
\lambda
,
f
and
|V|
, such that any solution
u
of
G_{\varepsilon}(u,t)=0
satisfy
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}<R_{\varepsilon}^{*}
for all
t\in[0,1]
. This implies

0\notin G_{\varepsilon}\left(\partial B_{R_{\varepsilon}},t\right),\quad\forall~t\in[0,1],~\forall~R_{\varepsilon}\geqslant R_{\varepsilon}^{*}.

Hence, by the homotopy invariance of the topological degree, we have

\operatorname{deg}\left(G_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,1),B_{R_{\varepsilon}},0\right)=\operatorname{deg}\left(G_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,0),B_{R_{\varepsilon}},0\right).

(4.2)

To calculate
\operatorname{deg}\left(G_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,0),B_{R_{\varepsilon}},0\right)
, we need to understand the solvability of the equation

G_{\varepsilon}(u,0)=-\Delta_{p}u+\lambda e^{2u}+\varepsilon f=0.

(4.3)

Now, we claim that: there exists an
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for any
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\varepsilon_{0}\right)
, equation (
4.3
) has a unique solution
u_{\varepsilon}
, which satisfies
e^{2u_{\varepsilon}}\leqslant\eta\varepsilon
, where
\eta
is a constant depending only on
\lambda
,
f
and
|V|
.

First, we prove the existence of the solution for equation (
4.3
). Note that, under the assumptions that
\varepsilon>0
and
\lambda\bar{f}<0
, by Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.5
, we can deduce that equation (
4.3
) has a solution
u_{\varepsilon}
. Moreover, integrating on both sides of (
4.3
), we have

\int_{V}e^{2u_{\varepsilon}}d\mu=-\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}\bar{f}|V|.

(4.4)

It follows that

e^{2u_{\varepsilon}(x)}\leqslant\eta\varepsilon,\quad\forall~x\in V,

(4.5)

where we take the constant
\eta\geqslant-\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\bar{f}|V|>0
.

Second, we prove the uniqueness of the solution
u_{\varepsilon}
. Let
\varphi
be an arbitrary solution of (
4.3
), namely it satisfies

\Delta_{p}\varphi=\lambda e^{2\varphi}+\varepsilon f.

(4.6)

Similar to (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we have

\int_{V}e^{2\varphi}d\mu\leqslant\eta\varepsilon,\quad e^{2\varphi(x)}\leqslant\eta\varepsilon\quad\text{ for all }x\in V.

(4.7)

Subtracting (
4.6
) from (
4.3
) and integrating on both sides, we have

0=\int_{V}(\Delta_{p}u_{\varepsilon}-\Delta_{p}\varphi)d\mu=\lambda\int_{V}\left(e^{2u_{\varepsilon}}-e^{2\varphi}\right)d\mu,

which implies

\min_{V}\left(u_{\varepsilon}-\varphi\right)<0<\max_{V}\left(u_{\varepsilon}-\varphi\right).

Then, it follows that

\left|u_{\varepsilon}-\varphi\right|\leqslant\max_{V}\left(u_{\varepsilon}-\varphi\right)-\min_{V}\left(u_{\varepsilon}-\varphi\right).

(4.8)

By (
4.3
), (
4.5
), (
4.6
), and (
4.7
), we have

\displaystyle\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\left|\Delta_{p}u_{\varepsilon}(x)-\Delta_{p}\varphi(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\lambda\left(e^{2u_{\varepsilon}(x)}-e^{2\varphi(x)}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant 2|\lambda|\left(e^{2u_{\varepsilon}(x)}+e^{2\varphi(x)}\right)\left|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-\varphi(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant 4\eta|\lambda|\varepsilon\left|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-\varphi(x)\right|.

(4.9)

Combining (
2.6
), (
4.8
) and (
4
), we obtain

\max_{V}\left(u_{\varepsilon}-\varphi\right)-\min_{V}\left(u_{\varepsilon}-\varphi\right)\leqslant C_{3}\varepsilon\left(\max_{V}\left(u_{\varepsilon}-\varphi\right)-\min_{V}\left(u_{\varepsilon}-\varphi\right)\right),

(4.10)

where

C_{3}=4\eta|\lambda|\left(2(l-1)^{p-1}|V|C\right)^{1/p}.

Choose
\varepsilon_{0}=1/(2C_{3})
.
If we take
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
, then (
4.10
) implies
\varphi\equiv u_{\varepsilon}
on
V
. Thus (
4.3
) has a unique solution. Hence our claim holds.

We now proceed to prove the theorem. We calculate the topological degree of
G_{\varepsilon}
at
u_{\varepsilon}
.
Note that
-\dfrac{\partial\Delta_{p}u}{\partial u}:X\rightarrow X
is a symmetric operator. In fact, as before, we assume that
V=\{x_{i}|i=1,2,\cdot\cdot\cdot,l\}
, then we can regard
-\Delta_{p}u
as a vector, i.e.,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u=\big(-\Delta_{p}u(x_{1}),\cdots,-\Delta_{p}u(x_{\ell})\big)\\
\displaystyle\qquad\quad=\bigg(\sum_{y\sim x_{1}}|u(y)-u(x_{1})|^{p-2}(u(x_{1})-u(y)),\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\cdots,\sum_{y\sim x_{\ell}}|u(y)-u(x_{\ell})|^{p-2}(u(x_{\ell})-u(y)\bigg).\end{array}

Denote

-\dfrac{\partial\Delta_{p}u}{\partial u}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{11}&a_{12}&\cdots&a_{1l}\\
a_{21}&a_{22}&\cdots&a_{2l}\\
\vdots&&&\\
a_{l1}&a_{l2}&\cdots&a_{ll}\end{array}\right].

(4.11)

Then, if
p\geqslant 2
, we can calculate that

a_{ii}=(p-1)\sum_{y\sim x_{i}}\left|u(y)-u\left(x_{i}\right)\right|^{p-2},i=1,2,\cdots,l,

and

a_{ij}=a_{ji}=-(p-1)\left|u\left(x_{j}\right)-u\left(x_{i}\right)\right|^{p-2}\quad\text{ or }\quad a_{ij}=a_{ji}=0,

for
i,j=1,2,\cdots,l
and
i\neq j
. Moreover, the following equalities hold:

a_{i1}+a_{i2}+\cdots+a_{il}=0,\quad\hbox{for}~i=1,2,3,\cdots,l,

a_{1j}+a_{2j}+\cdots+a_{lj}=0,\quad\hbox{for}~j=1,2,3,\cdots,l.

Therefore,
-\dfrac{\partial\Delta_{p}u}{\partial u}
is symmetrical. If
1<p<2
, we can also conclude that it is symmetrical. In the calculation process of
a_{ij}
,
i,j=1,2,\cdots,l
, for
x\in V
, if there exist some
y
with
y\sim x
such that
u(y)=u(x)
, we remove the corresponding term. This means that we only consider the terms that
u(y)\neq u(x)
, for each
y\sim x
. The corresponding Jacobian matrix has a similar form; hence, we omit it here.

On the other hand, one knows that its eigenvalues can be written as

0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}\leqslant\lambda_{2}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant\lambda_{l-1},

where
l
is the number of all points in
V
. By the claim above, under the assumption that
\lambda\bar{f}<0
, we may choose a sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
such that
G_{\varepsilon}(u,0)=0
has a unique solution
u_{\varepsilon}
satisfying

2|\lambda|e^{2u_{\varepsilon}(x)}<\lambda_{1}.

(4.12)

Substituting
u=u_{\varepsilon}
into (
4.11
), we have

DG_{\varepsilon}\left(u_{\varepsilon},0\right)=-\dfrac{\partial\Delta_{p}u}{\partial u}\bigg|_{u=u_{\varepsilon}}+2\lambda e^{2u_{\varepsilon}}\mathrm{I},

where
I
represents the
l\times l
diagonal matrix diag
[1,1,\cdots,1]
. Moreover, by (
4.12
), we have

\lambda_{j}+2\lambda e^{2u_{\varepsilon}(x)}>0
, for
j=1,2,\cdots,l-1
. Therefore, by the symmetry of
-\dfrac{\partial\Delta_{p}u}{\partial u}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\operatorname{deg}\left(G_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,0),B_{R_{\varepsilon}},0\right)&=\operatorname{sgn}\operatorname{det}\left(DG_{\varepsilon}\left(u_{\varepsilon},0\right)\right)\\
&=\operatorname{sgn}\left\{2\lambda e^{2u_{\varepsilon}(x)}\Pi_{j=1}^{l-1}\left(\lambda_{j}+2\lambda e^{2u_{\varepsilon}(x)}\right)\right\}\\
&=\operatorname{sgn}\lambda.\end{array}

Choose
R_{0}=\max\{R_{*},R_{\varepsilon}^{*}\}
. Together with (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{deg}\left(F(\cdot,1),B_{R},0\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{deg}\left(F(\cdot,0),B_{R},0\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{deg}\left(G_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,1),B_{R},0\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{deg}\left(G_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,0),B_{R},0\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{sgn}\lambda,

for arbitrary
R\geqslant R_{0}
. Thus the proof of Theorem
1.2
is completed.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Since
\lambda\bar{f}<0
, then by Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, we can find a large
R_{0}>1
such that for all
R\geqslant R_{0}
,

\operatorname{deg}(F,B_{R},0)\neq 0.

Therefore, by the Kronecker’s existence theorem
Chang-2005
, we can conclude that equation (
1.1
) has a solution.
∎

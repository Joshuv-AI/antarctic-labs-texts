With the recent developments in the study of graphene which can be used to design devices with extraordinary properties
[
20
,
19

, important efforts have been made to understand wave propagation phenomena in quantum waveguides in the mathematical community. In certain simple situations, this leads one to investigate the properties of the Laplace operator with Dirichlet boundary conditions in thin domains.

Many works concern the case where the geometry has a thin transverse section and is unbounded in some longitudinal direction. In these multiscale waveguides, because waves cannot radiate as easily as in free space, it is known that trapped modes, also called bound states, can exist. They are defined as non trivial solutions to the homogeneous problem which are of finite energy,
i.e.
in
\mathrm{H}^{1}
. They have been the object of intense studies first because in general one wants to avoid their unpleasant effects. But one can also search specifically for bounded states in the continuous spectrum
[
59
,
37
,
22
]
because when slightly perturbed, they give rise to complex resonances with small imaginary parts in the vicinity of which one observes interesting versatile scattering phenomena (see the literature concerning the so-called Fano resonance
[
60
,
8
]
). In this context, it has been shown in particular that as soon as a straight waveguide is bent
[
15
,
13
]
or broken with an angle
[
16
,
2
,
7
]
, eigenvalues appear below the continuous spectrum of the Dirichlet Laplacian.

On the other hand, some authors have focus their attention on the derivation of reduced models to describe wave propagation in latices made of thin ligaments, which are particular elements of the family of multiscale domains. This has given rise to a large body of works, among them
[
32
,
55
,
38
,
23
,
56
,
3
,
43
,
14
,
51
,
54
]
.
The limit problems involve ODEs in 1D ligaments connected at some junctions points. In this field, the main difficulty lies in writing relevant transmission conditions at the nodes of the network. For the Neumann Laplacian, it has been known for a long time that Kirchhoff transmission conditions, that is continuity of the field and zero outgoing flux, must be imposed. Things are more subtle for the Dirichlet Laplacian. In general, a homogeneous Dirichlet condition must be imposed, so that information cannot propagate in the structure. But in certain particular situations, this depends on the spectral properties of the operator obtained by zooming at the junction, the relevant law is a transmission condition, for example Kirchhoff or anti-Kirchhoff.

The goal of the present article is to cross the different studies mentioned above to describe the features of the spectrum of the Dirichlet Laplacian in thin bounded broken strips characterized by a parameter
\varepsilon>0
small as depicted in Figure
1
. First, due to the Dirichlet boundary conditions, all eigenvalues move to
+\infty
as
O(\varepsilon^{-2})
when
\varepsilon
tends to zero. But more originally, we prove that for a fixed small
\varepsilon
, when the angle of the strip varies continuously, for certain values, an eigenvalue dives,
i.e.
moves down rapidly, below the normalized threshold
\pi^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}
.

Concerning the analysis of the spectrum of the Dirichlet Laplacian in other thin bounded domains, one may consult
[
27
,
17

18

. Finally let us mention that the Dirichlet Laplacian in 2D domains
\omega

Our work is motivated by the analysis of the spectrum of the Laplace operator with Dirichlet Boundary Conditions (BCs) in finite length thin broken strips characterized by a parameter
\varepsilon>0
small as displayed in Figure

tends to zero. Exploiting the symmetry of the problem, one can reduce the study to that of the spectrum of the Laplace operator with mixed BCs in the geometry of Figure

\alpha\in(-\pi/2;\pi/2)
and
\varepsilon>0
, define the trapezoid

\mathrm{T}^{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\{z\coloneqq(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,|\,y\in(0;\varepsilon)\mbox{ and }x\in(y\tan\alpha;1)\}.

(1)

Note that for
\alpha=0
,
\mathrm{T}^{\varepsilon}
coincides with the rectangle
(0;1)\times(0;\varepsilon)
. Let us give notation to the different components of the boundary

of

. Set

\Sigma^{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\{z\in\partial\mathrm{T}^{\varepsilon}\,|\,y=0\mbox{ or }y=\varepsilon\mbox{ or }x=1\}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\Gamma^{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\partial\mathrm{T}^{\varepsilon}\setminus\overline{\Sigma^{\varepsilon}}=\{(y\tan\alpha,y)\,|\,y\in(0;\varepsilon)\}.

From time to time below, we will indicate the dependence with respect to
\alpha
, writing for example
\mathrm{T}^{\varepsilon}(\alpha)
,
\Sigma^{\varepsilon}(\alpha)
,
\Gamma^{\varepsilon}(\alpha)
. We study the spectral problem with mixed BCs

\begin{array}[]{|rcll}-\Delta u&=&\lambda u&\quad\mbox{in }\mathrm{T}^{\varepsilon}\\[3.0pt]

where

is less attractive because in this case the near field operator (see

in (

\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\mathrm{T}^{\varepsilon};\Sigma^{\varepsilon})

vanishing on
\Sigma^{\varepsilon}
. Classically (see e.g.

\begin{array}[]{|l}\mbox{ Find }(\lambda,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\mathrm{T}^{\varepsilon};\Sigma^{\varepsilon})\setminus\{0\}\mbox{ such that}\\[5.0pt]
\,\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{T}^{\varepsilon}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dz=\lambda\,\int_{\mathrm{T}^{\varepsilon}}uv\,dz\qquad\forall v\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\mathrm{T}^{\varepsilon};\Sigma^{\varepsilon}).\end{array}

(3)

As known for example from
[
5

[

\begin{array}[]{rccl}A^{\varepsilon}:&\mathcal{D}(A^{\varepsilon})&\quad\to&\quad\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{T}^{\varepsilon})\\[6.0pt]
&u&\quad\mapsto&\quad A^{\varepsilon}u=-\Delta u,\end{array}

with
\mathcal{D}(A^{\varepsilon})\coloneqq\{u\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\mathrm{T}^{\varepsilon};\Sigma^{\varepsilon})\,|\,\Delta u\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{T}^{\varepsilon})\mbox{ and }\partial_{n}u=0\mbox{ on }\Gamma^{\varepsilon}\}

[

31

\varepsilon

The analysis presented in the articles

\Omega\coloneqq\{Z\coloneqq(X,Y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,|\,Y\in(0;1)\mbox{ and }X\in(Y\tan\alpha;+\infty)\}

\begin{array}[]{|rcll}-\Delta w&=&\mu\,w&\mbox{ in }\Omega\\[3.0pt]
w&=&0&\mbox{ on }\Sigma\coloneqq\{Z\in\partial\Omega\,|\,Y=0\mbox{ or }Y=1\}\\[3.0pt]
\partial_{n}w&=&0&\mbox{ on }\Gamma\coloneqq\partial\Omega\setminus\overline{\Sigma},\end{array}

(7)

which is now independent of
\varepsilon
. Let
\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega;\Sigma)
stand for the Sobolev space of functions of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)

\Sigma
. We denote by
A^{\Omega}
the unbounded, positive definite, selfadjoint operator associated with (
7

\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)

\mathcal{D}(A^{\Omega})\coloneqq\{w\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega;\Sigma)\,|\,\Delta w\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\mbox{ and }\partial_{n}w=0\mbox{ on }\Gamma\}.

(8)

As for
A^{\varepsilon}
in (
4
), one can explicit
\mathcal{D}(A^{\Omega})
by using the theory of regularity of solutions to elliptic problems in domains with singular geometries (see the calculus in (

)). The continuous spectrum of
A^{\Omega}
coincides with the ray
[\pi^{2};+\infty)
. When
\alpha=0
(straight end), the Poincaré-Friedrichs inequality in the variable

A^{\Omega}
, denoted by
\sigma_{\mathrm{d}}(A^{\Omega})
, is empty. On the other hand, for all
\alpha\in(0;\pi/2)
, it is known from
[
2
]
that
A^{\Omega}
has at least one eigenvalue below its continuous spectrum (see also
[
41
]
for more general shapes and dimensions). Using that extending
\Omega
by reflection with respect to
\Gamma
provides a V-shaped domain, we can exploit the results from
[
52
]
(see also
[
12
]
as well as the amendments in
[
42
]
) to get information on
\mu_{1}
, the smallest eigenvalue of
A^{\Omega}
, as well as on the multiplicity of the discrete spectrum. In particular, in these works it is shown that the function
\alpha\mapsto\mu_{1}(\alpha)
is smooth and strictly decreasing on
(0;\pi/2)
. Additionally, we have

\lim_{\alpha\to 0^{+}}\mu_{1}(\alpha)=\pi^{2},\qquad\lim_{\alpha\to(\pi/2)^{-}}\mu_{1}(\alpha)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\lim_{\alpha\to(\pi/2)^{-}}\#(\sigma_{\mathrm{d}}(A^{\Omega}))=+\infty,

(9)

where
\#(\cdot)
stands for the cardinality of a set. The third limit of (
9
) means that the multiplicity of the discrete spectrum of
A^{\Omega}
can be made as large as desired for very sharp tips.
According to
[
23
]
, the properties of (
7

\mu
coinciding with the threshold of the continuous spectrum of
A^{\Omega}
are determinant in the asymptotics of the eigenvalues of
A^{\varepsilon}
above the normalized threshold
\pi^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. This leads us to study the problem

\begin{array}[]{|rcll}\Delta W+\pi^{2}W&=&0&\mbox{ in }\Omega\\
W&=&0&\mbox{ on }\Sigma\\[3.0pt]
\partial_{n}W&=&0&\mbox{ on }\Gamma.\end{array}

(10)

Let us describe the features of (
10
). To proceed, first define the waves
w_{0}
,
w_{1}
and the linear wave packets
w^{\mathrm{out}}
,
w^{\mathrm{in}}
such that

w_{0}(Z)=\sin(\pi Y),\ w_{1}(Z)=X\sin(\pi Y),\quad\begin{array}[]{|lcl}w^{\mathrm{out}}(Z)&\hskip-5.69046pt=&\hskip-5.69046ptw_{1}(Z)-iw_{0}(Z)=(X-i)\sin(\pi Y)\\[5.0pt]
w^{\mathrm{in}}(Z)&\hskip-5.69046pt=&\hskip-5.69046ptw_{1}(Z)+iw_{0}(Z)=(X+i)\sin(\pi Y).\end{array}

(11)

Here the notation “in/out” stands for “incoming/outgoing” according to the energy radiation principle. The theory presented for example in
[
50
, Chap. 5]
guarantees that Problem (
10
) admits a solution with the expansion

W_{+}=w^{\mathrm{in}}+\mathbb{S}\,w^{\mathrm{out}}+\widetilde{W}_{+},

(12)

where
\mathbb{S}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\widetilde{W}_{+}
decays exponentially at infinity. In this simple situation the so-called threshold scattering matrix
\mathbb{S}
is a scalar and the fact that
\mathbb{S}
is unitary translates into the relation

|\mathbb{S}|=1.

(13)

In other words,
\mathbb{S}
is located on the unit circle in the complex plane. It may happen, we do not know, that (
10
) also admits solutions which are exponentially decaying at infinity (trapped modes). In this situation,
W_{+}
is not uniquely defined. However these trapped modes do not modify the value of
\mathbb{S}
because of their decay at infinity. Following
[
39
,
44
,
30
,
48
]
, now we introduce some vocabulary. Define the vector spaces

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathscr{S}&\hskip-5.69046pt\coloneqq&\hskip-5.69046pt\displaystyle\{W=C_{1}w_{1}+C_{0}w_{0}+\widetilde{W}\mbox{ satisfying }(\ref{NearFieldPb})\mbox{ with }C_{0},\,C_{1}\in\mathbb{C}\mbox{ and }\widetilde{W}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)\};\\[10.0pt]
\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{bo}}&\hskip-5.69046pt\coloneqq&\hskip-5.69046pt\displaystyle\{W=C_{0}w_{0}+\widetilde{W}\mbox{ satisfying }(\ref{NearFieldPb})\mbox{ with }C_{0}\in\mathbb{C}\mbox{ and }\widetilde{W}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)\};\\[10.0pt]
\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{tr}}&\hskip-5.69046pt\coloneqq&\hskip-5.69046pt\displaystyle\{W\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)\mbox{ satisfying }(\ref{NearFieldPb})\}.\end{array}\hskip-8.5359pt

(14)

Here
\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{bo}}
is the space of bounded solutions of (
10
) while
\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{tr}}
stands for the space of trapped modes. Generally speaking, the existence of trapped modes at the threshold is a rare phenomenon. In particular, it is unstable with respect to perturbations of the geometry. Therefore we think that there holds
\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{tr}}=\{0\}
for most
\alpha
though it is an open problem to prove such result. On the other hand, we do not know if we can have
\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{tr}}\neq\{0\}
for certain
\alpha
.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that
A^{\Omega}
has a Threshold Resonance (TR) if
\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{bo}}\neq\{0\}
, i.e. if (
10
) admits a non zero bounded solution.

Definition 4.2
.

We say that
A^{\Omega}
has a proper TR if the quotient space

\mathscr{S}_{\dagger}\coloneqq\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{bo}}/\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{tr}}

contains a non trivial element. This is equivalent to say that
A^{\Omega}
has a proper TR if (
10
) admits a bounded solution which does not decay at infinity. If
\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{bo}}=\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{tr}}\neq\{0\}
, we say that the TR is improper.

The quotient space
\mathscr{S}_{\dagger}
is sometimes called the space of almost standing waves of (
10
).
From the definition of
\mathscr{S}_{\dagger}
in (
14
), we see that one has always
\mathrm{dim}\,\mathscr{S}_{\dagger}\leq 1
. The following statement (see
[
45
, Thm. 7.1]
or
[
43
]
in more general contexts) provides a characterization of the dimension of
\mathscr{S}_{\dagger}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

There holds

\begin{array}[]{|ll}\mathscr{S}_{\dagger}=\{0\}&\mbox{ if }\mathbb{S}\neq-1\\[2.0pt]
\mathrm{dim}\,\mathscr{S}_{\dagger}=1&\mbox{ if }\mathbb{S}=-1.\end{array}

Proof.

When
\mathbb{S}=-1
, the function
W_{+}
introduced in (
12
) decomposes as

W_{+}=w^{\mathrm{in}}-w^{\mathrm{out}}+\widetilde{W}_{+}=2iw_{0}+\widetilde{W}_{+}.

(15)

Therefore it is an element of
\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{bo}}\setminus\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{tr}}
, which shows that
\mathrm{dim}\,\mathscr{S}_{\dagger}=1
. On the other hand, using energy considerations, one proves that one has always
\mathrm{dim}\,(\mathscr{S}/\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{tr}})=1
. When
\mathbb{S}\neq-1
,
W_{+}
belongs to
\mathscr{S}\setminus\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{bo}}
. This implies that
\mathscr{S}_{\dagger}=\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{bo}}/\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{tr}}=\{0\}
in this case.
∎

The particular values of
\alpha
such that
\mathbb{S}(\alpha)=-1
will play an important role in the sequel. When
\alpha
varies in
[0;\pi/2)
, according to
(

\mathbb{S}(\alpha)
runs on the unit circle in the complex plane and from time to time hits the value
-1
. More precisely, one can show first that the map
\alpha\mapsto\mathbb{S}(\alpha)
is continuous on
[0;\pi/2)
. To proceed, quite classically, one can work with smooth diffeomorphisms to transform small (geometrical) changes of
\alpha
into small linear perturbations of an operator defined in a fixed domain. Then one can apply usual results of the perturbation theory for linear operators, see e.g.
[
28
, Chap. 7]
,
[
5
, Chap. 10]
,
[
57
, Chap. XII]
. Besides, we have the following statement whose proof is postponed to Section
8
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Assume that
\alpha^{\star}\in(0;\pi/2)
is such that
\mathbb{S}(\alpha^{\star})=-1
. Then there is
c>0
, depending on
\alpha^{\star}
, such that there holds

\mathbb{S}(\alpha)\neq-1\qquad\forall\alpha\in[\alpha^{\star}-c;\alpha^{\star}+c]\setminus\{\alpha^{\star}\}.

Proposition
4.4
ensures that the set of values of
\alpha\in[0;\pi/2)
such that
\mathbb{S}(\alpha)=-1
is discrete. We know that this set is non-empty because it contains the value
0
. Indeed, for
\alpha=0
, we simply have

W_{+}(Z)=w^{\mathrm{in}}-w^{\mathrm{out}}=2i\sin(\pi Y)

and so
\mathbb{S}(0)=-1
. Let us denote by

0=\alpha_{0}^{\star}<\alpha_{1}^{\star}<\dots<\alpha_{k}^{\star}<\dots

(16)

the sequence of values of
\alpha
such that
\mathbb{S}(\alpha^{\star}_{k})=-1
. In the following, these
\alpha_{k}^{\star}
will be called the threshold angles. We will assume that there exist positive threshold angles, a property that unfortunately we have not been able to prove. More precisely, the increase of the multiplicity of the discrete spectrum of
A^{\Omega}
as
\alpha
tends to
\pi/2
is due to detaching of an eigenvalue from the threshold
\pi^{2}
at a TR. However we cannot prove that TR are proper. Let us emphasize that the numerics of Section
6
(see in particular Figure
8
) seems to indicate that positive threshold angles indeed exist.

5
Asymptotics at a fixed
\alpha

In this section, we explain how to adapt the results presented in particular in the articles
[
23
,
43
,
9
]
concerning dimension reduction in almost 1D quantum graphs to describe the asymptotics of the eigenvalues of
A^{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon
tends to zero for a given
\alpha\in[0;\pi/2)
. For the sake of conciseness, we shall only give the main ideas.

Denote by

0<\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}\leq\mu_{3}\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{N_{\circ}}<\pi

(17)

the eigenvalues of the discrete spectrum of
A^{\Omega}
. Set also
N_{\dagger}\coloneqq\mathrm{dim}\,\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{tr}}\in\mathbb{N}
. Observe that the quantities
\mu_{N_{\circ}}
,
N_{\dagger}
depend on
\alpha
but we do not write this dependence here. In the particular case
\alpha=0
(straight end), as already said, we have
N_{\circ}=N_{\dagger}=0
. On the other hand, there holds
N_{\circ}\geq 1
when
\alpha\in(0;\pi/2)
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Pick
\alpha\in[0;\pi/2)
. There are positive constants
C_{p}
,
\delta_{p}
,
\varepsilon_{p}
such that the eigenvalues
\lambda^{\varepsilon}_{p}
of
A^{\varepsilon}
introcuded in (
5
) satisfy the following estimates:

\bullet

\mbox{For }p=1,\dots,N_{\circ}:

\big|\lambda^{\varepsilon}_{p}-\varepsilon^{-2}\mu_{p}\big|\leq C_{p}\,e^{-(1+\delta_{p})\sqrt{\pi^{2}-\mu_{p}}/\varepsilon}\qquad\forall\varepsilon\in(0;\varepsilon_{p}];\\

\mbox{For }p=N_{\circ}+1,\dots,N_{\circ}+N_{\dagger}:

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}x\partial_{x}(|\nabla v|^{2}-\pi^{2}v^{2})\,dz=\underset{L\to+\infty}{\lim}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{L}}x\partial_{x}(|\nabla v|^{2}-\pi^{2}v^{2})\,dz\\[10.0pt]
=&-\displaystyle\cos\alpha\int_{\Gamma}x(|\nabla v|^{2}-\pi^{2}v^{2})\,ds-\underset{L\to+\infty}{\lim}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{L}}|\nabla v|^{2}-\pi^{2}v^{2}\,dz-\int_{\Sigma_{L}}x(|\nabla v|^{2}-\pi^{2}v^{2})\,dy\\[10.0pt]
=&-\displaystyle\cos\alpha\int_{\Gamma}x(|\nabla v|^{2}-\pi^{2}v^{2})\,ds-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}-\pi^{2}v^{2}\,dz.\end{array}

Note that on
\Gamma

(66)

9
Concluding remarks

In this work, we considered the spectrum of the Laplace operator with mixed boundary conditions in thin trapezoids characterized by an angle
\alpha
. By working with symmetries, this allow one to study the spectrum of the Dirichlet Laplacian in thin broken stripes as pictured in Figure
13
. When varying continuously
\alpha
, at certain particular values
\alpha^{\star}_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we proved a phenomenon of eigenvalue fall around the normalized threshold
\pi^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}
. The fall is milder at
\alpha^{\star}_{0}=0
than at
\alpha^{\star}_{k}>0
,
k\geq 1
. Moreover, the eigenfunctions associated with the eigenvalue diving below
\pi^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}
experiment a notable change of shape: while they spread in the whole domain
\mathrm{T}^{\varepsilon}
for
\alpha<\alpha^{\star}_{k}
, they become localized in the vicinity of the tip for
\alpha>\alpha^{\star}_{k}
. Let us mention that this is very particular to Dirichlet BC and does not appear with Neumann BC. Indeed, for Neumann BC the continuous spectrum of the near field operator starts at zero and the corresponding
\mathscr{S}_{\dagger}
is always of dimension one (due to the constants), independently of the geometry.

Around this study, several questions remain open. First we assumed that there are positive threshold angles
\alpha^{\star}_{k}
,
k\geq 1
, where
\mathscr{S}_{\dagger}\neq 0
. Though we observe them numerically (see again Figure
8
), we do not have a proof that there exists at least one. When
\alpha
increases,
\alpha\mapsto\mathbb{S}(\alpha)
runs continuously on the unit circle in the complex plane but Figure
8
indicates that it is not monotone, which complicates the analysis. One idea maybe would be to look for an asymptotic expansion of
\mathbb{S}(\alpha)
as
\alpha
tends to zero to obtain an explicit behavior allowing one to conclude to a passage through
-1
. Another intriguing question concerns the space
\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{tr}}
of trapped modes at the threshold for the operator
A^{\Omega}
. Can we have
\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{tr}}\neq\{0\}
for certain angles? This is unclear. To pursue this analysis, it might be interesting to investigate the spectrum of the Dirichlet Laplacian in polyhedral domains of thickness
\varepsilon
small. Can one write models on the limit polyhedron to describe the asymptotics of the eigenvalues as
\varepsilon
tends to zero? Are they defined on the faces or on the edges? How do they depend on the different angles of the polyhedron?

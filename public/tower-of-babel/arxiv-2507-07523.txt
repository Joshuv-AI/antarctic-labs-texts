The Electromagnetic Form Factors of Pseudoscalar Mesons within the Light-Front Quark Model
Shuai Xu a, Xiao-Nan Li b, Xing-Gang Wu c

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the electromagnetic form factors (EMFFs) and charge radii of pseudoscalar mesons within the light-front quark model (LFQM). Using parameters derived from the confinement of mesonic decay constants, we obtain numerical results, which indicate the following: (i) The EMFFs of charged and neutral mesons exhibit significant differences in their endpoint behaviors but show similar asymptotic behavior in the high momentum transfer ( Q 2 Q^{2} ) regions. For the EMFFs of light mesons such as π \pi and K + K^{+} , our results are in excellent agreement with experimental data in the low Q 2 Q^{2} regions. For the charge radii of mesons, our results also show rough consistency with predictions from other approaches. (ii) For charged mesons, the peak values of Q 2 ​ F P ​ ( Q 2 ) Q^{2}F_{P}(Q^{2}) are approximately proportional to the mass difference Δ ​ m \Delta m between their constituent quarks. Moreover, the mean square radii ⟨ r 2 ⟩ P \left\langle r^{2}\right\rangle_{P} of charged mesons decrease with increasing meson mass and decreasing Δ ​ m \Delta m . For neutral mesons, their charge radii are primarily determined by the electric charge of the heavy quark. These results indicate that quark mass asymmetry significantly influences the behavior of the EMFFs and charge radii of mesons. Experimental data to test these predictions would thus be of great interest.



I
Introduction

A fundamental challenge in hadron physics is elucidating the internal structure of hadrons in terms of their constituent quarks and gluons. The electromagnetic form factors (EMFFs) of mesons serve as essential quantities in this endeavor, describing the spatial distribution of charge and current within the hadron. These form factors provide crucial information about hadronic structure and the dynamics governing constituent interactions. Pseudoscalar mesons represent particularly accessible systems for such studies. For a spinless pseudo-scalar (
P
) particle, the electromagnetic structure is parameterized by a single form factor,

II
Theoretical Formulation

The definition of the pseudoscalar mesonic EMFF is

\displaystyle\langle M(p^{\prime\prime})|j^{\mu}_{\text{em}}|M(p^{\prime})\rangle=P^{\mu}F_{P}(Q^{2}),

(1)

where
p^{\prime}{(p^{\prime\prime})}
is the momentum of the initial (final) bound state,
P^{\mu}=p^{\prime\mu}+p^{\prime\prime\mu}
and
q^{\mu}=p^{\prime\mu}-p^{\prime\prime\mu}
. In this paper the Drell-Yan-West frame
q^{+}=0
is chosen, where the momentum transfer
Q^{2}=-q^{2}=\mathbf{q}^{2}_{\perp}
lies in the space-like region. The charge current is approximated as a one-point function

\displaystyle j^{\mu}_{\text{em}}(0)=\sum_{q,\bar{q}}e_{q}\bar{q}(0)\gamma^{\mu}q(0),

(2)

where
e_{u}=2/3
and
e_{d}=-1/3
are the electric charges of the up and down quarks, respectively.

It is straightly to extract
F_{P}(Q^{2})
from Eq.(
1
) within the LFQM, more details are given in Refs.
Choi:1997iq
;
Choi:1999nu
. And we obtain

F_{P}(Q^{2})=e_{q}I(m_{q},m_{\bar{q}},Q^{2})+e_{\bar{q}}I(m_{\bar{q}},m_{q},Q^{2}),

(3)

where

I(m_{q},m_{\bar{q}},Q^{2})=\int_{0}^{1}dx\int d^{2}\mathbf{k}_{\perp}\sqrt{\frac{\partial k_{z}}{\partial x}}\phi_{R}(x,\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\sqrt{\frac{\partial k^{\prime}_{z}}{\partial x}}\phi_{R}(x,\mathbf{k^{\prime}}_{\perp})\frac{[xm_{q}+\bar{x}m_{\bar{q}}]^{2}+\mathbf{k}_{\perp}\cdot\mathbf{k^{\prime}}_{\perp}}{\sqrt{[xm_{q}+\bar{x}m_{\bar{q}}]^{2}+\mathbf{k}^{2}_{\perp}}+\sqrt{[xm_{q}+\bar{x}m_{\bar{q}}]^{2}+\mathbf{k^{\prime}}^{2}_{\perp}}},

(4)

where
{\bf{k^{\prime}}}_{\perp}={\bf{k}}_{\perp}+\bar{x}{\bf{q}}_{\perp}
,
\bar{x}=1-x
and the longitudinal momentum
k_{z}=(x-\frac{1}{2})M_{0}+\frac{m_{q}^{2}-m_{\bar{q}}^{2}}{2M_{0}}
in the Jacobian
\frac{\partial k_{z}}{\partial x}
of the variable transformation
\{x,{\bf{k}}_{\perp}\}\rightarrow\{{\bf{k}}_{\perp},k_{z}\}
. The invariant mass of bound state
M_{0}
is defined as

\displaystyle M_{0}^{2}=\frac{m_{q}^{2}+{\bf{k}}_{\perp}^{2}}{\bar{x}}+\frac{m_{\bar{q}}^{2}+{\bf{k}}_{\perp}^{2}}{x},

(5)

which plays a significant role in analyses of covariance and self-consistency of the standard and covariant approaches of LFQM
Jaus:1999zv
;
Cheng:2003sm
;
Choi:2013mda
;
Xu:2025aow
. In this work, a Gaussian-type radial wave function
\phi_{R}
is taken, e.g.

\displaystyle\phi_{R}(x,{\bf{k}}_{\perp})=\frac{4\pi^{3/4}}{\beta^{3/2}}\mathrm{exp}(-\frac{{\bf{k}}_{\perp}^{2}+k_{z}^{2}}{2\beta^{2}}),

(6)

where the parameter
\beta
is related to the size of bound state and will be discussed in the next section.

III.2
The heavy flavor mesons

Furthermore, we proceed to calculate the EMFFs of heavy-flavor mesons. The results have been presented in Fig.
2
and Table
3
. The EMFFs and
\langle r^{2}\rangle_{P}
are closely related to the charge and mass of the heavy (anti)quark (
m_{h}
) as well as the mass difference (
\Delta m=m_{h}-m_{l}
) between the constituent quarks, where the values of
\Delta m
are listed in Table
3
. Some findings are as follows:

•

As the
m_{h}
increases, the peak values of
Q^{2}F_{P}(Q^{2})
also rise significantly, following the order
Q^{2}F_{B}>Q^{2}F_{D}>Q^{2}F_{K}>Q^{2}F_{\pi}
. This behavior arises because the explicit breaking of chiral symmetry causes the wave function to localize more strongly, leading to a more compact structure that concentrates the charge distribution. Consequently, photons can couple effectively even at high
Q^{2}
, resulting in a higher peak of the EMFF and a shift toward higher
Q^{2}
values. For the same heavy-flavor hybrid states, such as
B^{+}
,
B^{0}
,
B_{s}
, and
B_{c}
,
Q^{2}F_{P}(Q^{2})
increases remarkably with larger
\Delta m
.

•

For charged mesons, we can find that the charge radius
\sqrt{\langle r^{2}\rangle_{P}}
is negatively correlated with the mass of heavy flavored mesons
m_{h}
, and positively correlated with the charge of heavy flavored quarks
e_{h}
and the mass difference of constituent quarks
\Delta m
from Table.
3
. Notably, the size of meson is also closely related to the parameter
\beta
, our calculations demonstrate that a larger
\beta
corresponds to a smaller charge radius. Therefore, we attempt to provide a phenomenological relationship
\sqrt{\langle r^{2}\rangle_{P}}\propto e_{h}\Delta m^{l}/\beta^{\frac{3}{2}}m_{h}^{s}
.

•

As discussion in the subsection
III.1
, the endpoint behaviors of the neutral mesons EMFFs are manifestly differ from the ones of charged mesons, as shown in Fig.
2
. The
\sqrt{\langle r^{2}\rangle}
of neutral mesons primarily determined by the charge of heavy flavor quark, thus the
\sqrt{\langle r^{2}\rangle_{D^{0}}}
is the largest one and about two times of the
\sqrt{\langle r^{2}\rangle_{K^{0}}}
. In addition, we can see that compared to
D^{0}
, although the heavy quark charges of
B^{0}
and
B_{s}
are smaller, they still have sizes comparable to
\sqrt{\langle r^{2}\rangle_{D^{0}}}
. This is because the mass asymmetry caused by the large mass difference of constituent quarks
\Delta m
plays a nontrivial role.

IV
CONCLUSIONS

In this work, the EMFFs
F_{P}(Q^{2})
and charge radii
\sqrt{\langle r^{2}\rangle_{P}}
of pseudoscalar mesons are investigated within LFQM. Numerical calculations are performed using parameters constrained by decay constants. Our results are generally in agreement with the currently available experimental data for pions and kaons, and roughly consistent with other theoretical predictions for heavy-flavor
D
- and
B
-mesons.
In addition, the
Q^{2}
-dependence of the EMFFs and the behavior of charge radii have been analyzed, with the findings as follows: For charged mesons, the peak values of
Q^{2}F_{P}(Q^{2})
are approximately proportional to the mass difference
\Delta m
between constituent quarks. This proportionality also holds for neutral mesons. The charge radii
\sqrt{\langle r^{2}\rangle_{P}}
of charged mesons decrease as the meson mass (or heavy-quark mass) increases, reflecting the weakening of relativistic effects in the heavy-quark limit. Additionally,
\Delta m
plays a nontrivial role in this behavior. For neutral mesons, the heavy-quark charge dominantly determines
\sqrt{\langle r^{2}\rangle_{P}}
. Collectively, these results demonstrate that mass asymmetry significantly influences the EMFFs and charge radii, and these theoretical predictions will be tested in future experiments.

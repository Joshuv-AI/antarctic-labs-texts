The two extremal rays of some Hyper-Kähler fourfolds
Federica Galluzzi, Bert van Geemen

Abstract
. We consider projective Hyper-Kähler manifolds of dimension four that are deformation equivalent to Hilbert squares of K3 surfaces. In case such a manifold admits a divisorial contraction, the exceptional divisor is a conic bundle over a K3 surface. A classification of lattice embeddings implies that there are five types of such conic bundles. In case the manifold has Picard rank two and has two (birational) divisorial contractions we determine the types of these conic bundles. There are exactly seven cases. For the Fano varieties of cubic fourfolds there are only four cases and we provide examples of these.



Introduction

The divisors on a four dimensional projective Hyper-Kähler manifold
X
that can be contracted provide
interesting information on
X
itself. In case
X
is of K3
[2]
-type and the Picard rank of
X
is two,
as we will assume throughout this paper,
such a divisor
E
is a
{\bf P}^{1}
-fibration, so it is a conic bundle,
over a K3 surface
S
of Picard rank one.
The fibration on
E
is induced by the contraction of this divisor. The fourfold
X
is then a moduli space
of possibly twisted sheaves on
S
(
[
vGK
, Thm. 3.3]
).
This implies that the Hodge structures on
H^{2}(X,{\bf Z})
and
H^{2}(S,{\bf Z})
are closely related.

The conic bundles on a K3 surface of Picard rank one which are isomorphic to such exceptional divisors were
classified in
[
vGK
]
and we recall this in Section
1
.
A main ingredient is the classification, due to Debarre and Macrì,
of embeddings of the rank two lattice
\langle 2d\rangle\oplus\langle-2\rangle

into the lattice defined by
H^{2}(X,{\bf Z})
and its BBF-form.
An exceptional divisor defines an isometry class of such embeddings and there are five classes.

A well-known case is
E={\bf P}{\mathcal{T}}_{S}
, the projectivization of the tangent bundle of a K3 surface
S
.
Then
X\cong S^{[2]}
, the Hilbert square of
S
. This type of contraction is denoted by H in this paper.
There are two other cases where
E={\bf P}{\mathcal{V}}
for a rank two vector bundle
{\mathcal{V}}
on
S
.
Then
{\mathcal{V}}
is a rigid, stable, vector bundle, called a Mukai bundle.
Such rank two bundles exist only if
h^{2}=2g-2
with
g
even, where
\operatorname{Pic}(S)={\bf Z}h
.
We denote these cases by M
i
with
i=1,3
according to
h^{2}\equiv 2i\mod 8
.
Even if the lattice embeddings of type
M_{1}
and
M_{3}
are quite different, the only notable difference
in the exceptional divisors is the genus of the K3 surface that is the base of the conic bundle.
Finally there are two cases where the Brauer class of the conic bundle is non-trivial.
In that case
E={\bf P}{\mathcal{U}}
for a rigid
\alpha
-twisted locally free sheaf
{\mathcal{U}}
of rank two
and the Brauer class
\alpha\in Br(S)_{2}
has order two.
These cases are denoted by B
i
with
i=0,1
according to the intersection number

Bh\equiv i/2\mod{\bf Z}
where
B\in H^{2}(S,{\bf Q})
is a B-field representative of
\alpha
.

An exceptional divisor on
X
defines an extremal ray in
\operatorname{Pic}(X)_{\bf R}\cong{\bf R}^{2}
,
which is a boundary component of the movable cone of
X
.
The movable cone has two boundary components.
The other extremal ray is either generated by an isotropic vector, in which case it defines a Lagrangian fibration, or it defines another divisorial contraction of a HK manifold birational to
X
.
We recall the descriptions of the movable cone and the birational models of
X
in Section
2
.

In this paper we are interested in the types of these pairs of divisorial contractions.
A priori, given the 5 types of exceptional divisor, there could be
15
types of such pairs.
However we show in Theorem
3.1
that there are only seven cases.
Five of these are T+T, where T is any of the five types, the other two are H+M
3
and H+B
1
.
Notice that H+M
1
cannot occur, illustrating the difference in types M
1
and M
3
.
The types depend on rather subtle properties of the Picard lattice of
X
, like the divisibilities
in
H^{2}(X,{\bf Z})
of certain divisor classes associated to the contractions and the minimal solution of
a Pell equation associated to this lattice.
In the two ‘mixed’ types H+M
3
and H+B
1
, the presence of an H implies that
X
is birational to
a Hilbert square
S^{[2]}
for a K3 surface
S
.

The second exceptional divisor seems to play a rather important role in the geometry of the Hilbert square. The cases where
S
has degree
14
and
30
the types are H+M
3
(see §
4.6
and §
3.3
).
The conic bundles
E
of type M
3
, which are scrolls over
S
, can be found in the literature
where also ad-hoc geometrical constructions of embeddings of
E
in
S^{[2]}
are given. However,
our result that such a conic bundle must be isomorphic to an exceptional divisor on
S^{[2]}
may simplify and clarify the constructions.

In Section
4
we consider the special, but important, case that
X
is birational to
the Fano variety of lines of a cubic fourfold. In that case
X
can have only three types of contractions,
H, M
3
or B
1
and only four pairs of contractions are possible,
they are T+T, with T one of these three, or H+M
3
.
We discuss the relation between the exceptional divisor and families of scrolls in the cubic fourfold,
already explored by Hassett and Tschinkel
[
HT01
]
with an emphasis on the Mori cone of curves,
in terms of divisor classes.
We conclude with the well-known case of pfaffian cubic fourfolds.
Their Fano varieties are Hilbert squares
S^{[2]}
and the degree of
S
is
14
.
They have a contraction of type H and the second exceptional divisor is of type M
3
.
As mentioned above, the corresponding conic bundle (it embeds as a scroll over
S
in
{\bf P}^{5}
)
was known and it was also shown to have an embedding in to
S^{[2]}
.
The identification of this conic bundle with (‘the second’) exceptional divisor seems to be new.

1.
Exceptional divisors on K3
[2]
manifolds

1.1.
Hyper-Kähler fourfolds of K3
[2]
type

A Hyper-Kähler (HK) variety is a simply-connected smooth projective variety admitting a
unique (up to scalar) non-degenerate holomorphic two-form.
These varieties are also called irreducible holomorphic symplectic varieties.
A HK fourfold
X
is of K3
[2]
type if it is deformation equivalent to the Hilbert scheme of length 2 subschemes of a K3 surface.
The second cohomology group
H^{2}(X,{\bf Z})
is equipped with a quadratic form
q
, the Beauville-Bogomolov-Fujiki (BBF) form, we write
(x,y)
for the associated bilinear form, so
q(x)=(x,x)
.
This quadratic form is related to the intersection product of divisors by the following relations,

D^{4}\,=\,3q(D)^{2}\,=\,3(D,D)^{2},\qquad D^{3}D^{\prime}\,=\,3(D,D)(D,D^{\prime})\qquad(D,D^{\prime}\,\in\,\operatorname{Pic}(X))~.

There is an isomorphism of lattices

H^{2}(X,{\bf Z})\,\cong\,\Lambda_{{\operatorname{K3}}^{[2]}}\,=\,\Lambda_{{\operatorname{K3}}}\oplus\,{\bf Z}\delta\,=\,U^{3}\,\oplus\,E_{8}(-1)^{2}\,\oplus\,\langle-2\rangle~,

where
\Lambda_{{\operatorname{K3}}}
is the K3 lattice,
U
is the hyperbolic plane and
E_{8}(-1)
is the
unique unimodular, negative definite, even lattice of rank
8
.

The divisibility
\mbox{div}_{\Lambda_{\operatorname{K3}^{[2]}}}(v)
of an element
v\in\Lambda_{\operatorname{K3}^{[2]}}
is the positive integer such that

\{(v,w):w\in\Lambda_{\operatorname{K3}^{[2]}}\}=\mbox{div}_{\Lambda_{\operatorname{K3}^{[2]}}}(v){\bf Z}~.

Any primitive
v\in\Lambda_{{\operatorname{K3}}}\subset\Lambda_{{\operatorname{K3}}^{[2]}}
has divisibility one since
the K3 lattice is unimodular and hence is self-dual.
The divisibility of a primitive element in
H^{2}(F,{\bf Z})\cong\Lambda_{\operatorname{K3}^{[2]}}

is either one or two (
[
GHS
, Example 3.11]
).

1.2.
Contractions

Let
X
be a complex manifold of K3
[2]
type with Picard group of rank two.
If there exists a divisorial contraction
f:X\rightarrow Y
then the exceptional
divisor
E
is smooth and irreducible.
The class of
E
in
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
is either
\tau
or
2\tau
where
\tau\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)

is a
(-2)
-class, that is,
q(\tau)=-2
.

The perpendicular of
E
in
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
is generated by a big and nef divisor class
H
.
A multiple of

H
defines the contraction, so one can identify
f
with the map defined by
nH
for some large
n
.
The contraction determined by
(H,E)
defines a positive integer
d
by
q(H)=2d
.
The Picard lattice then contains the sublattice

{\bf Z}H\oplus{\bf Z}\tau\,\cong\,\langle 2d\rangle\,\oplus\,\langle-2\rangle\qquad(\subset\,\operatorname{Pic}(X))~.

This sublattice is either equal to
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
or it has index two in it, correspondingly one has

|\det(\operatorname{Pic}(X))|=4d
or
d
.

The classification of the contractions relies on the classification, due to Debarre and Macrì
of the embeddings of lattices

{\bf Z}H\oplus{\bf Z}\tau\;\hookrightarrow\;\Lambda_{{\operatorname{K3}}^{[2]}}~.

In (
[
DM
]
,
[
De
, Thm. 3.32]
) it is shown that there are five classes of embeddings.

In these papers, the classes of the embeddings are related to
irreducible components of Heegner divisors in the boundary of moduli
spaces of polarized HK fourfolds of
\operatorname{K3}^{[2]}
-type
(in a general deformation of
(X,H)
, the big and nef divisor
H
becomes ample).
The Heegner divisors corresponding to the five classes of embeddings are listed in
Table
1
(cf.
[
vGK
, Table 1]
where the order of the columns is different however)
but we will not consider these Heegner divisors in this paper.

For each of the five embeddings
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\cong{\bf Z}H\oplus{\bf Z}\tau\hookrightarrow H^{2}(X,{\bf Z})
,
the isometry class of transcendental lattice
T(X):=\operatorname{Pic}(X)^{\perp}

was determined in
[
vGK
]
.

1.3.
Conic bundles

In
[
vGK
, Thm. 3.3]
it is shown, with case by case computations, that a general

X
with a contraction defined by
(H,E)
and Picard rank two is a moduli space of
(possibly twisted) sheaves on a K3 surface
S
.
The results of Bayer and Macrì  in
[
BM
]
then imply that
the contraction induces the structure of a conic bundle (a
{\bf P}^{1}
-fibration) over
S

on the exceptional divisor
E
:

f:\,X\,\longrightarrow\,Y,\qquad p:=f_{|E}:\,E\longrightarrow\,S\;(\subset Y)~,

and
f
is an isomorphism on
X-E\cong Y-S
.
The Picard rank of
S
is one and we write
\operatorname{Pic}(S)={\bf Z}h_{S}
where
h_{S}
is ample. The self-intersection
number
h_{S}^{2}
is either
q(H)
or
q(H)/4
.

A conic bundle on
S
defines a two-torsion Brauer class, denoted by
\alpha\in Br(S)_{2}

where

Br(S)\,:=\,H^{2}(S,{\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{\times})_{tors}\,\cong\,H^{2}(S,{\bf Q})/(\operatorname{Pic}(S)_{\bf Q}\oplus H^{2}(S,{\bf Z}))~.

A two-torsion Brauer class has a representative
B\in\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}H^{2}(S,{\bf Z})
, called a B-field.
The intersection number
h_{S}B\in\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}{\bf Z}/{\bf Z}
is an invariant of the Brauer class and,
only in the case that
4Bh_{S}+h_{S}^{2}\equiv 0\mod 4
, also
B^{2}\in\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}{\bf Z}/{\bf Z}
is an invariant.
These invariants are also given in Table
1
.
The transcendental lattice of
S
is
T(S):=\operatorname{Pic}(S)^{\perp}
.
A Brauer class
\alpha\in Br(S)_{2}
corresponds
to a homomorphism
\alpha:T(S)\rightarrow\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}{\bf Z}/{\bf Z}
given by
t\mapsto Bt
.
The kernel of this homomorphism is denoted by
T(S,\alpha)
.
For non-trivial
\alpha
this is an index two sublattice of
T(S)
.

1.4.
The exceptional divisors and rigid rank two bundles

The type of the embedding
{\bf Z}H\oplus{\bf Z}\tau\hookrightarrow\Lambda_{\operatorname{K3}^{[2]}}

determines the invariants of the Brauer class of the conic bundle structure
p:E\rightarrow S
on an exceptional divisor
E
, with class
E=\tau
or
E=2\tau
, as well some other invariants.
In particular, there are also five types of conic bundles over a K3 surface that can appear as exceptional divisors.

The Brauer class of the conic bundle
E\rightarrow S
is trivial for three types,
denoted by H, M
1
and M
3
.
In these cases
E={\bf P}{\mathcal{F}}
where
{\mathcal{F}}
is a locally free sheaf of rank two on
S
.
In the case H, the contraction is the Hilbert Chow contraction

S^{[2]}\rightarrow S^{(2)}
and
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{T}}_{S}
, the tangent sheaf of
S
.
In the other two cases,
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{V}}
where
{\mathcal{V}}
is the rank two rigid Mukai bundle on
S
.
If
\operatorname{Pic}(S)={\bf Z}h_{S}
, such a bundle exists if and only if
h_{S}^{2}=2g-2
with
g=2s
even,
its Mukai vector is
v({\mathcal{V}})=(2,h_{S},2s)
and rigidity is equivalent to
v({\mathcal{V}})^{2}=-2

(see
[
Hu16
, §10.3.1]
). The
i
in the type M
i
is determined by
h_{S}^{2}\equiv 2i\mod 8
.

If the Brauer class
\alpha\in Br(S)_{2}
of
E\rightarrow S
is non-trivial,
there are two types denoted by B
0
and B
1
.
The exceptional divisor is then
E={\bf P}{\mathcal{U}}
for a rigid locally free rank two

\alpha
-twisted sheaf
{\mathcal{U}}
on
S
with Brauer class
\alpha
.
The
i
in B
i
is determined by
h_{S}B\equiv i/2\mod{\bf Z}

where
B
is a B-field representative of
\alpha
.
In both cases one must have
B^{2}\equiv 1/2
, but recall that
B^{2}
is not an invariant
unless
4Bh_{S}+h_{S}^{2}\equiv 0\mod 4
.
(On a general K3 surface
S
there are three types of non-trivial Brauer classes in
Br(S)_{2}
.
In the case where
B^{2}
is an invariant and
B^{2}\equiv 0
, there does not exist a locally
free rank two
\alpha
-twisted sheaf which is rigid and such Brauer classes are not obtained from exceptional divisors.)
Similar to the case
\alpha=0
, an
\alpha
-twisted sheaf
{\mathcal{U}}
has a (twisted) Mukai vector
v^{B}({\mathcal{U}})
.
The rigidity of
{\mathcal{U}}
is equivalent to
v^{B}({\mathcal{U}})^{2}=-2
(cf.
[
vGK
, §2.3]
).

Conversely, given a rigid, possibly twisted, locally free sheaf
{\mathcal{U}}
of rank two
on a K3 surface
S
with Picard rank one as above, there is a HK fourfold
X
of K3
[2]
-type
that has an exceptional divisor
E
with conic bundle structure
E\cong{\bf P}{\mathcal{U}}\rightarrow S
.
This
X
is a moduli space of twisted sheaves on
S
.

1.5.
Contractions of type H

For a contraction of type H the fourfold
X
is isomorphic to a Hilbert scheme
S^{[2]}
where
S
is a K3 surface with
\operatorname{Pic}(S)={\bf Z}h
, let
h^{2}=2d
.
The Picard lattice of
S^{[2]}
is

\operatorname{Pic}(S^{[2]})\,=\,{\bf Z}H\,\oplus\,{\bf Z}\tau,\qquad q(H)\,=\,2d,\quad(H,\tau)=0,\quad q(\tau)=-2,

here
2\tau
is the class of the exceptional divisor
E\cong{\bf P}{\mathcal{T}}_{S}
in
S^{[2]}
parametrizing the non-reduced subschemes of length two and
H
is the divisor class induced by
h
.
The embedding
{\bf Z}H\oplus{\bf Z}\tau\hookrightarrow\Lambda_{\operatorname{K3}^{[2]}}

is characterized, up to isometry,
by the fact that the divisibility of
\tau
in
H62(X,{\bf Z})\cong\Lambda_{{\operatorname{K3}}^{[2]}}
is two.

1.6.
Contractions of type M
1

According to Table
1
,
a contraction defined by
(X,H)
is of type M
1
if and only if
q(H)=2d
and
|\det(\operatorname{Pic}(X))|=d
.
Only in this case
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\neq{\bf Z}H\oplus{\bf Z}\tau
, but
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
is generated by
H
and
(H+\tau)/2
.
Since
q((H+\tau)/2)=(2d-2)/4
must be an even integer, this implies that
d\equiv 1\mod 4
.
One can embed
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
into
\Lambda_{\mbox{\tiny K3}}\subset H^{2}(X,{\bf Z})
, thus any
primitive element in
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
has divisibility one in
H^{2}(X,{\bf Z})
(cf.
[
vGK
, Prop. 3.7]
).

For example, if
d=1
, the even, indefinite, rank two Picard lattice is unimodular and hence is isometric
to the hyperbolic plane
U
.
The other extremal ray is then isotropic and thus defines a Lagrangian fibration,
these fourfolds are studied in
[
DHMV
]
.

1.7.
Contractions of type M
3

A contraction defined by
(X,H)
is of type M
3
if and only if the divisibility
\gamma
of
H
is two.
In that case,
H=2D+a\delta\in\Lambda_{\operatorname{K3}^{[2]}}
for some
D\in\Lambda_{K3}
and, since

H
is primitive,
a
is odd. Then
q(H)=4D^{2}-2a^{2}\equiv-2\mod 8
, that is,
q(H)=2d
with
d\equiv 3\mod 4
.
The Picard lattice is
\operatorname{Pic}(X)={\bf Z}H\oplus{\bf Z}\tau
.
For examples, see
[
vGK
, Examples 5.4, 5.5, 5.6]
.

1.8.
Contractions of type B
0

The contractions with
\alpha\neq 0
,
Bh=0
are the only ones where the transcendental lattice has a
non-cyclic discriminant group (
[
vGK
, Prop. 3.6]
).
This implies that if
X
has another
a divisorial contraction, then it has the same type. For these contractions, there is no congruence condition
on
d
.

In this case
\operatorname{Pic}(X)={\bf Z}H\oplus{\bf Z}\tau
and it can be embedded into
\Lambda_{\operatorname{K3}}\subset H^{2}(X,{\bf Z})
, see
[
vGK
, Prop. 3.6]
.
Therefore any non-zero primitive element in
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
has divisibility one in
H^{2}(X,{\bf Z})
.

An example, due to O’Grady, is given in
[
vGK
, Prop. 5.1]
.
In that example
d=1
and the HK manifolds are double covers of EPW sextics,
the covering involution permutes the two exceptional divisors.

1.9.
Contractions of type B
1

For a contraction with a non-trivial Brauer class
\alpha\in Br(S)_{2}
and
Bh\equiv 1/2\mod{\bf Z}
,
the transcendental lattice
T(X)
of
X
is isomorphic to the index two sublattice
T(S,\alpha)
of
T(S)
.
From the description of the lattice

given in
[

, Prop. 3.5]
one finds that its

with

.
For these

it can still happen that
T(S,\alpha)\cong T(S^{\prime})
for another K3 surface

.
In fact,
T(X)\cong T(S^{\prime})
, if and only if
d\equiv 0\mod 8
, see
[
vGK
, Prop. 3.5]
.
In that case
S^{\prime}
is a K3 surface of degree
d/2
.
Since
T(X)\cong T(S^{\prime})\cong T({S^{\prime[2]}})
, the HK fourfolds
X
and
(S^{\prime})^{[2]}
are called FM partners,
but in general this does not imply that

and
(S^{\prime})^{[2]}
are birational.

An example, with

, is given in
[
vGK
, §5.3]
, see also the case

in §
3.3
.

1.10.
Remark

From these descriptions, one finds that if
X
has a contraction of type M
1
or B
0
, then any other contraction of a birational model of
X
will be of the same type.
The remaining three types, B
1
, H, M
3
, can be distinguished from each other by the divisibility

\gamma
of
H
and the one of
\tau
:

a)

type H if the divisibility of
\tau
is two (and the divisibility of
H
is one),

b)

type M
3
if the divisibility of
H
is two (and the divisibility of
\tau
is one),

c)

type B
1
if the divisibility of both
H
and
\tau
is one.

The following proposition, based on results of Druel, will be useful to identify exceptional divisors of
contractions.

1.11.
Proposition

Let
p:Z\rightarrow S
be a conic bundle over a K3 surface and
assume that there is an embedding
Z\hookrightarrow X
where
X
is a HK fourfold.
Then there is a birational isomorphism
X\simeq X^{\prime}
, with
X^{\prime}
a HK fourfold,
and the image of
Z
is the exceptional fiber of a divisorial contraction
X^{\prime}\rightarrow Y^{\prime}
.

Proof.
By
[
Dr
, Prop. 1.4, Theorem 1.3]
we only have to show that if
f\subset Z
is a fiber of
p
then

Z\cdot f<0
. Let
i:Z\hookrightarrow X
be the inclusion, then
Z\cdot f=Z\cdot i_{*}f_{Z}
where
f_{Z}
is
a fiber considered as a 1-cycle on
Z
. By the projection formula, this intersection number is
i^{*}Z\cdot f_{Z}=K_{Z}\cdot f_{Z}

since
i^{*}Z=Z_{|Z}=K_{Z}
by adjunction on
X
. As
K_{Z}=\omega_{Z/S}
is also the relative dualizing sheaf
(cf.
[
vGK
, Section 1.1]
) and
\omega_{Z/S}
restricts to
\omega_{f}
on
f
, we get
K_{Z}\cdot f_{Z}=\mbox{deg}(\omega_{f})=-2<0
as desired.
∎

2.
The extremal rays of the movable cone

2.1.
Cones and divisorial contractions

Recall that if
S
is a K3 surface with Picard number two, then the divisorial contractions of
S

are the contractions of a
(-2)
-curve
n\subset S
and
n\cong{\bf P}^{1}
is a smooth rational curve.
The contraction
f:S\rightarrow\overline{S}
of
n
is defined by a big and nef divisor
h\in\operatorname{Pic}(S)

with intersection number
hn=0
.
In this case a primitive embedding of the rank two Picard lattice of
S
into the
(unimodular) K3 lattice is unique up to isometry.
In particular there are no issues with the divisibilities of the various divisor classes.
The half line
{\bf R}_{\geq 0}h
generated by
h
in
\operatorname{Pic}(S)_{\bf R}
is a
boundary ray of the nef cone of

. Since
\operatorname{Pic}(S)_{\bf R}\cong{\bf R}^{2}

and a cone in a two-dimensional real vector space has two extremal rays,
there is one other boundary ray.
This ray either defines another contraction,
in this case there are exactly two
(-2)
-curves in

{\bf R}_{\geq 0}e
for an isotropic class

, so

.
The latter case occurs if and only if the discriminant
|\det(\operatorname{Pic}(S))|
of the Picard lattice is a square.

Let now again
X
be a HK fourfold of K3
[2]
-type with Picard rank two.
The positive cone
\operatorname{Pos}(X)
of
X
is
the connected component of the set
\{x\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)_{\bf R}:\,q(x)>0\,\}
that contains an ample class.
The nef cone
\operatorname{Nef}(X)
, which is the closure of the ample cone, is contained in the positive cone.
The movable cone
\operatorname{Mov}(X)
is the (closed, convex) cone generated by classes of line bundles on
X

whose base locus has codimension at least 2. There are inclusions:

\operatorname{Nef}(X)\,\subset\,\operatorname{Mov}(X)\,\subset\,\operatorname{Pos}(X)\quad(\subset\operatorname{Pic}(X)_{\bf R}\,:=\,\operatorname{Pic}(X)\otimes{\bf R})~.

For a K3 surface,
\operatorname{Nef}(S)=\operatorname{Mov}(S)
.
Whereas a birational map between K3 surfaces is an isomorphism, this is not the case for HK fourfolds.
A birational map
\sigma:X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
between HK manifolds induces an isometry

\sigma^{*}:H^{2}(X^{\prime},{\bf Z})\rightarrow H^{2}(X,{\bf Z})
.
The movable cone
\operatorname{Mov}(X)
is the union over all such birational maps
\sigma

of the
\sigma^{*}(\operatorname{Nef}(X^{\prime}))
.
These subcones are either equal or have disjoint interiors. The manifold
X
has a unique smooth
birational model exactly when
\operatorname{Mov}(X)=\operatorname{Nef}(X)
.

We now recall how to determine these cones.

2.2.
The movable cone and the nef cone

The interior of the movable cone is the connected component of

\operatorname{Pos}(X)\,-\,\bigcup_{n\in\operatorname{Pic}(X),\atop q(n)=-2}\{x\in\operatorname{Pos}(X):\;q(x,n)=0\,\}

that contains an ample divisor (
[
Ma1
]
,
[
De
, Thm. 3.16]
). In particular,
if there are no
(-2)
-classes in
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
, then
\operatorname{Mov}(X)=\operatorname{Pos}(X)
.

If there are
(-2)
-classes,
the two boundary rays of
\operatorname{Mov}(X)
are called the extremal rays of
X

Both extremal rays can be defined by
(-2)
-classes
or one of these rays is defined by a
(-2)
-class and the other by a isotropic class in
\operatorname{Pic}(X)

(so
q(x)=0
for any
x
on this ray).
The latter happens exactly when
|\det(\operatorname{Pic}(X))|
is a square.
An extremal ray defined by
(-2)
-class defines a divisorial contraction (of birational models) of

whereas an extremal ray spanned by an isotropic vector
defines a Lagrangian fibration on (a birational model of)
X

[
Ma2
]
.

Recall that the interior of the nef cone is the ample cone.
If
\sigma:X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
is a birational map
between HK fourfolds then the interior of
\sigma^{*}\mbox{Nef}(X^{\prime})

is a connected component of

\mbox{Int}(\operatorname{Mov}(X))\,-\,\bigcup_{\kappa\in\operatorname{Pic}(X),\,q(\kappa)=-10,\atop\operatorname{div}_{H^{2}(X,{\bf Z})}(\kappa)=2}\{x\in\operatorname{Pos}(X):\;q(x,\kappa)=0\,\}

all connected components of this set are the ample cones of HK manifolds that are birational
to

(
[
BHT
]
,
[
De
, Thm. 3.16]
).
The birational map

is a composition of Mukai flops
in (Lagrangian) planes.

If there are no
(-10)
-classes with divisibility two, then
\operatorname{Mov}(X)=\operatorname{Nef}(X)
, otherwise at least one, and
possibly both, of the boundary rays of the nef cone are defined by such
(-10)
-classes and
these rays are called flopping walls.

Notice that if
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\subset\Lambda_{{\operatorname{K3}}}\subset H^{2}(X,{\bf Z})

then the unimodularity of

implies that there cannot exist a (primitive) class of divisibility two in

\operatorname{Pic}(X)

This happens if

has a contraction of type B

or M

(see

2.3.
The Pell equation

Assume that
X
has a divisorial contraction defined by
(H,E)

and that
|\det(Pic(X))|=4d
where

is not a square.
Then
\operatorname{Pic}(X)={\bf Z}H\oplus{\bf Z}\tau
as before with
q(H)=2d
,

q(\tau)=-2
and
{\bf R}_{\geq 0}H
is an extremal ray.
For a divisor class

one has

q(yH+x\tau)\,=\,2dy^{2}-2x^{2},\qquad\mbox{hence}\quad x^{2}\,-\,dy^{2}\,=\,1

is the condition that
q(yH+x\tau)=-2
.
The Pell equation

has a positive solution, that is
(a,b)
with integers
a,b>0

A positive solution with minimal
a
is called minimal solution,
this is also the solution for which the slope
b/a
is minimal (
[
De
, Appendix A]
).
The following well-known lemma gives the second extremal ray in terms of the first one and the minimal
positive solution of the Pell equation.

2.4.
Proposition

Let
(a,b)
be the minimal positive solution of the Pell equation
x^{2}-dy^{2}=1
.
Then the other divisorial contraction is defined by
(H^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})
with:

H^{\prime}\,:=\,aH\,-\,bd\tau,\qquad\tau^{\prime}\,:=\,bH\,-\,a\tau~.

Proof.
If
(a,b)
is any solution of the Pell equation, then
\tau^{\prime}:=bH-a\tau
is a
(-2)
-class in
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
and
any
(-2)
-class is obtained from a solution. The divisor class
H^{\prime}:=aH-bd\tau
is perpendicular to
\tau^{\prime}

and it is primitive since
a^{2}-db^{2}=1
implies that
GCD(a,bd)=1
.

We claim that if a multiple of
H^{\prime}
is effective then
a,b>0
. In fact, since
H
is big and nef, a positive multiple of
H^{3}
is the class of a curve in
X
which is not contained in
H^{\prime}

and thus
H^{3}H^{\prime}\geq 0
.
Recall that
H^{3}H^{\prime}=3q(H)(H,H^{\prime})
.
As
q(H)=2d>0
one has
0\leq(H,H^{\prime})=2ad
hence
a\geq 0
. Similarly,
E^{3}=cf
is a multiple of the
class of a fiber
f
of the conic bundle
E\rightarrow S
(
[
vGK
, Prop. 1.1]
).
Since by adjunction
E_{|E}=K_{E}
and

K_{E}=\omega_{E/S}
, the relative canonical bundle, we get
Ef=K_{E}f=-2
. Therefore
cEf=E^{4}=3q(E)^{2}>0
hence
c<0
.
Since the class of
E
is a positive multiple of

\tau
,
f
is a negative rational multiple of
\tau^{3}
.
Since
H^{\prime}\neq E
we have
fH^{\prime}\geq 0
and hence
0\geq\tau^{3}H^{\prime}=3q(\tau)(\tau,H^{\prime})=-6\cdot(2bd)

so

as we wanted.

Similarly, if a positive multiple of

\tau^{\prime}
is effective then also
a,b>0
. Thus the second contraction is given by a pair
(H^{\prime},E^{\prime})
with

H^{\prime},E^{\prime}
as in the lemma and
(a,b)
is a positive solution of the Pell equation.

The description of the movable cone implies that the ray
{\bf R}_{>0}H^{\prime}
is the one closest to
{\bf R}_{>0}H
, that is with
b/a
minimal, which concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Proposition

\left({\bf Z}g\,\oplus{\bf Z}\gamma,\,\begin{pmatrix}6&2\\

of a primitive divisor class

Proof.
Let

to

, and

which changes the sign of the intersection product.

.

From the classification of the rank two lattices

in
[
Ha00
]
one has

N^{2}(Y)\,=\,\left({\bf Z}h_{3}^{2}\,\oplus\,{\bf Z}R,K_{e}\right),\quad\mbox{with}\quad K_{e}\,=\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccr}\begin{pmatrix}3&0\\
0&e/3\end{pmatrix}&&\mbox{if}\quad e\equiv 0\mod 6~,\\
&&\\
\begin{pmatrix}3&1\\
1&(e+1)/3\end{pmatrix}&&\mbox{if}\quad e\equiv 2\mod 6~.\end{array}\right.

We already know that
q(g)=2\cdot 3=6
, where
g=\alpha(h_{3}^{2})
, let
\gamma:=\alpha(R)
.

In case

,

and thus

.

In case

Recall that the divisibility of a primitive element in

is either one or two. The divisibility of

is two, so if

The lattice

and

{\bf Z}

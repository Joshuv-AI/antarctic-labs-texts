Decomposition of the polynomials over the spherical subalgebra
Ibrahim Nonkané and Jean Kaboré

Abstract




1.
Introduction

Given a finite subgroup
W\subset\operatorname{GL}(\mathfrak{h})
of the linear group of a finite-dimensional complex vector field
\mathfrak{h}
, it is a well-studied problem to describe the structure of the symmetric algebra
B=\operatorname{sym}(\mathfrak{h}^{*})
as a representation of
G
, and also as a module over the ring of invariant differential operators under
W
in the ring
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h})
of differential operators on
\mathfrak{h}
. Since the rational Cherednik algebra

H_{c}(W,\mathfrak{h})
and the spherical algebra
eH_{c}e
are respectively universal deformations of the ring
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h})
and the ring
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h})^{W}
of
W
-invariant differential operators, we would like to build an analogy between the decomposition of modules over the invariant differential operators in
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
and the decomposition of modules over the sperical subalgebra of the rational Cherednik algebra. The ring
\mathcal{D}_{c}=eH_{c}e
inherits the natural grading of
B
, and we let
\mathcal{D}_{c}^{0}\subset\mathcal{D}_{c}
and
\mathcal{D}_{c}^{-}\subset\mathcal{D}_{c}
be subset of elements of degree 0 end strictly negative degree, respectively. Our main result is that there is for all finite reflection groups a lowest weight description of the category of
\mathcal{D}_{c}
-modules of
B
where the ring
{{\cal R}}_{c}=\mathcal{D}_{c}^{0}/\mathcal{D}_{c}^{0}\cap\mathcal{D}_{c}\mathcal{D}_{c}^{-}
plays the very important role of Cartan algebra.

Theorem 1.1
.

The decomposition of
\mathbb{C}[{\mathfrak{h}}]
as a
\mathcal{D}_{c}
-module is the same as the decomposition of
{\operatorname{Hom}}(\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{D}_{c}}{\mathcal{D}_{c}\mathcal{D}_{c}^{-}},\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}])
as a
{{\cal R}}_{c}
-module.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The rational Cherednik algebra

Let
W
be a finite reflection group in a complex vector space
\mathfrak{h}
, and let
R\subset\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be the corresponding set of roots. To each
W
-invariant function
c:R\to\mathbb{C},c\mapsto c_{\alpha},
one attaches an associative algebra
H_{c}
, called the
rational Cherednik algebra
. Given
\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}^{*},
write
\alpha^{\vee}\in\mathfrak{h}
for the coroot, and
s_{\alpha}\in\operatorname{Gl}(\mathfrak{h})
for the reflection corresponding to
\alpha.
The rational Cherednik algebra
H_{c}
(which was denoted
H_{1,c}
in
[
6
]
) is generated by the vector spaces
\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}^{*},
and the group algebra of
W,
with defining relations given by

w\cdot x\cdot w^{-1}=w(x),w\cdot x\cdot w^{-1}=w(y),\forall y\in\mathfrak{h},x\in\mathfrak{h}^{*},w\in W,

x_{1}\cdot x_{2}=x_{2}\cdot x_{1},\quad\quad y_{1}y_{2}=y_{2}y_{1},\quad\quad\forall y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathfrak{h},x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}

y\cdot x-x\cdot y=\langle y,x\rangle-\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in R/\{\pm 1\}}c_{\alpha}\cdot\langle y,\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha^{\vee},x\rangle\cdot s_{\alpha},\forall y\in\mathfrak{h},x\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}.

Thus, the elements
x\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
generate a subalgebra
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}]\subset H_{c}
of polynomial functions on
\mathfrak{h}
, the elements
y\in\mathfrak{h}
generate a subalgebra
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}^{*}]\subset H_{c}
, and the elements
w\in W
span a copy of the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[W]
sitting naturally inside
H_{c}
.
A basis of
H_{c}
is given by the set
\displaystyle\{p\cdot q\cdot w:p
is an ordered monomial in the
x_{i},\ q
is an ordered monomial in the
y_{i},\ w\in W\}.

In other words, multiplication on
H_{c}
induces a vector space isomorphism (the PWB theorem see
[
6
]
):

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[W]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}^{*}]\cong H_{c}.

The case of the symmetric group
W=S_{n}
acting on
\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{C}^{n}
by permuting coordinates is as follows. The reflections in this case are transpositions. Let
S
be the set of all transpositions, as they are all conjugate, the function
c:S\to\mathbb{C}
must be a constant and we identify its value in
\mathbb{C}
. Then the rational Cherednik algebra
H_{c}(S_{n},\mathfrak{h})
is the quotient of
\mathbb{C}\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\rangle\rtimes\mathbb{C}[S_{n}]
by the relations

[x_{i},x_{j}]=[y_{i},y_{j}]=0,\ [y_{i},x_{j}]=cs_{ij},\ [y_{i},x_{i}]=1-c\sum_{j\neq i}s_{ij},

where
\mathbb{C}\langle E\rangle
denote the free algebra on a set
E
, and
s_{ij}
is the transposition of
i
and
j
.

2.1.1.
The grading element

Let
W\subset\operatorname{GL}(\mathfrak{h}).
Let
S
be the set of reflections in a reflection group
W
, so
(W,S)
is a coxeter system. For any reflection
s\in S,
let
\lambda_{s}
be the eigenvalue of
s
on
\alpha_{s}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}(\mbox{i.e.}\ s\alpha_{s}=\lambda_{s}\alpha_{s}),
and let
\alpha_{s}^{\vee}\in\mathfrak{h}
be an eigenvector such that
s\alpha_{s}^{\vee}=\lambda_{s}^{-1}\alpha_{s}^{\vee}.
We normalize them in such a way that
\langle\alpha_{s},\alpha_{s}^{\vee}\rangle=2.

Let
c:S\to\mathbb{C},s\mapsto c_{s}
be a function invariant with respect to conjugation. Let

\mathbf{h}=\sum_{i}x_{i}y_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\dim\mathfrak{h}-\sum_{s\in S}\frac{2c_{s}}{1-\lambda_{s}}s.

where the
y_{i}
’s and
x_{i}
’s form basis of
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
respectively.
We have

[\mathbf{h},x]=x,\ x\in\mathfrak{h}^{*},\ [\mathbf{h},y]=-y,\ y\in\mathfrak{h}.

If
W
is a real reflection group, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbf{h}=\sum_{i}x_{i}y_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\dim\mathfrak{h}-\sum_{s\in S}c_{s}s
. Let
\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2},{\mathbf{F}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}y_{i}^{2}.
Then
\displaystyle\mathbf{h}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}(x_{i}y_{i}+y_{i}x_{i})
and
\{{\mathbf{E}},\mathbf{h},{\mathbf{F}}\}
forms an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple in
H_{c}
.

The eigenspace decomposition of
H_{c}
under the adjoint action of
\mathbf{h}
puts a grading on
H_{c}
:
W
is in degree 0,
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is in degree 1 and
\mathfrak{h}
is in degree -1.

2.2.
The Polynomial representation

There is faithful action of

Let

There is an action of

on

\displaystyle x(fv_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(xf)v_{0}\ \quad\ \mbox{for}\ x\in\mathfrak{h}^{*},

\displaystyle y(fv_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{y}(fv_{0})\ \quad\mbox{for}\ y\in\mathfrak{h},

\displaystyle w(fv_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w(f)v_{0}\ \quad\mbox{for}\ w\in W.

The rational Cherednik algebra comes with a natural embedding
\Phi_{c}:H_{c}\to\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{reg})\rtimes\mathbb{C}[W]
defined by the formula

. Let
\mathbb{D}_{c}
denote the subalgebra of
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}])
generated by
x,D_{y},w
, where
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}])
is the algebra of all endomorphisms on
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}]
. From now on we will identify the spherical
H_{c}
with the ring of differential operators
\mathbb{D}_{c}

2.3.
The spherical subalgebra

Let
{\mathbf{e}}=\frac{1}{|W|}\sum_{w\in W}w\in\mathbb{C}[W]
be the symmetrizer, the subalgebra
{\mathbf{e}}H_{c}{\mathbf{e}}
of
H_{c}
is called the
spherical algebra
.

The elements
\{{\mathbf{E}}{\mathbf{e}},\mathbf{h}{\mathbf{e}},{\mathbf{F}}{\mathbf{e}}\}
form an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple in the algebra
{\mathbf{e}}H_{c}{\mathbf{e}}
; in particular the adjoint
\mathbf{h}{\mathbf{e}}
-action gives a grading on
{\mathbf{e}}H_{c}{\mathbf{e}}
.
Note that
{\mathbf{e}}(\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{reg})\rtimes\mathbb{C}[W]){\mathbf{e}}=\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{reg})^{W}.
Therefore the restriction of the Dunkl embedding gives the embedding
\Phi_{c}:{\mathbf{e}}H_{c}{\mathbf{e}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{reg})^{W}.

The natural

-action on
\mathfrak{h}_{reg}
by dilations gives a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading on
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{reg})^{W}
given by the weights on the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action, also there is a standard filtration on
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{reg})
by the
order
of differential operators, let
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{reg})_{-}
denote the subalgebra in
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{reg})
spanned by all homogeneous differential operators

such that weight(
u
) + order(

)
\geq 0.

Let

\displaystyle L_{c}:=\Delta-\sum_{s\in S}\frac{c_{s}(\alpha_{s},\alpha_{s})}{\alpha_{s}}\partial_{{\alpha_{s}}^{\vee}}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{reg})_{-}

be the Calogero-Moser operator, where
\Delta
denotes the second-order Laplacian on
\mathfrak{h}
. We let
{\cal C}_{c}
be the centraliser of
L_{c}
in the algebra
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{reg})_{-}^{W}=\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{reg})^{W}\cap\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{reg})_{-},
and we let
\mathcal{D}_{c}
denote the subalgebra in
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{reg})^{W}
generated by
{\cal C}_{c}
and by

, the subalgebra of

\Phi_{c}:{\mathbf{e}}H_{c}{\mathbf{e}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{reg})^{W}
equals to
\mathcal{D}_{c}

[
2
]

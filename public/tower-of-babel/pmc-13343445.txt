Hierarchical bending microstructures in π-conjugated molecular cocrystals
Authors: Xinyu Du, Shuang Li, Lizhi Wang, Tingting Yang, Yong Zhang, Xuehua Ding, Jinyi Lin, Wei Huang
Published: 2026

Flexible organic crystals, endowed with the characteristic of ordered molecular arrangement, exhibit tremendous application potential in flexible (opto)electronics, micro/nanoscale integrated photonics, and other related fields. While notable progress has been made in achieving self-bending behavior1 and controllable curvature in crystals,2 many current bending strategies still rely on manual or external stimuli, which are often time-consuming and limit precise manipulation.3 Meanwhile, mechanical deformation frequently induces irreversible structural damage to crystals and optoelectronic performance degradation. These challenges severely limit the practical application and industrialization of flexible organic crystals in high-performance optoelectronic devices and integrated systems.4 Therefore, it is highly urgent to develop a universal strategy to realize spontaneous crystal bending without external forces, precise control of bending angles, crack-free bending interfaces, and excellent optoelectronic functions, which is crucial for promoting the development of this field.

In a recent study published in Nature Communications, Wang et al.5 proposed a universal molecular cocrystal strategy that introduces directional charge-transfer noncovalent interactions into molecular systems, weakening the intrinsic intermolecular forces of π-conjugated molecular crystals and triggering the spontaneous deformation of crystals from slip to bending. By virtue of this strategy, a series of flexible organic crystals with precisely controlled bending angles (61.8°–85.0°) and devoid of fractured bending interfaces were successfully fabricated (Figure 1A). This strategy features remarkable expandability and universality, being applicable to a variety of donor-acceptor (D-A) π-conjugated molecular systems; accordingly, bent crystals covering the full red, green, and blue (RGB) spectral range were obtained (Figure 1B). For the first time, the controllable fabrication of 2- to 6-level hierarchical bending microstructures was achieved by stepwise regulation of the substrate temperature (Figure 1C), which greatly expands the design space for complex architectures of π-conjugated molecular crystals.

To uncover the underlying mechanism, combined molecular dynamics (MD) simulations and experimental characterizations were carried out, confirming that the core factors governing crystal bending include substrate temperature, solution concentration, and the volume fraction of the dichloromethane/ethanol (DCM/EtOH) mixed solvent. Specifically, in this study, π-conjugated molecular perylene was employed as the electron donor and 2,4,6-trimethylbenzene-1,3,5-tricarbonitrile as the electron acceptor. Cocrystallization was performed on a substrate via solution evaporation in the DCM/EtOH mixed solvent. When the substrate temperature was raised to 30°C, the weak π-π stacking interactions dissociated, triggering crystal slippage. At a solution concentration of 5 mmol/L, continuous molecular growth at both ends of the slipped microstructure induced interfacial stress accumulation, which was released via a 180° rotation, forming a bent structure. A DCM volume fraction below 60% further favored the formation of thermodynamically stable bent configurations. Specifically, thermal dissociation of π-π stacking interactions serves as the prerequisite for slippage, stress accumulation induced by high concentration is critical for rotational bending, and a low DCM ratio determines the thermal stability of the bent structure. Collectively, these factors drive the spontaneous bending of slipped crystals by regulating noncovalent interactions, interfacial stress, and solvent evaporation rate. Notably, the bending angle correlates with the dihedral angle between the intrinsic crystal planes of the crystal, and no phase transition occurs throughout the entire bending process.

Beyond structural controllability, optical characterizations revealed that these micro/nanoscale bent π-conjugated molecular crystals exhibit excitation-position-dependent anisotropic optical properties and possess a prominent photonic switching function with on/off ratios of 2.6–12.2 (Figure 1D). The wide tunability of the switching ratio, together with the micro/nanoscale dimensions of the materials, enables their adaptation to the signal-regulation accuracy requirements of different integrated optoelectronic circuits, thus improving the design flexibility of optoelectronic devices.

In summary, this study establishes a design paradigm of noncovalent interaction modulation-crystal deformation-functional customization, which not only provides a universal methodology for the precise synthesis of π-conjugated molecular cocrystals at the micro/nanoscale but also lays a critical structural and materials foundation for the development of on-chip organic optoelectronic technologies, holding broad application potential in the fields of optical communication, information processing, and beyond.

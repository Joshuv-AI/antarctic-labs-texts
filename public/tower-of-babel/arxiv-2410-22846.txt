Knowledge Graph Based Visual Search Application
Pawandeep Kaur Betz German Aerospace Center, Tobias Hecking German Aerospace Center, Andreas Schreiber German Aerospace Center, Andreas Gerndt German Aerospace Center University of Bremen

Abstract
The FAIR data principles advocate for making scientific and research datasets ’Findable’ and ’Accessible’. Yet, the sheer volume and diversity of these datasets present significant challenges. Despite advancements in data search technologies, techniques for representing search results are still traditional and inadequate, often returning extraneous results. To address these issues, we developed a knowledge graph based visual search application designed to enhance data search for Earth System Scientists. This application utilizes various chart widgets and a knowledge graph at the backend, connecting two disparate data repositories.

Introduction

keywords
Visual Analytics, Knowledge Graph

Research and scientific datasets are vital for advancing scientific discovery and understanding various phenomena. Modern data accumulation techniques have led to the rapid growth of datasets, and consequently, data repositories have expanded significantly. The FAIR data principles
[
7
]
emphasize the importance of making data ’Findable’ and ’Accessible’. However, this becomes challenging with large repositories containing datasets from various interconnected domains and in different formats. Despite advancements in data search technologies like elastic and semantic search, the representation of search results has remained largely unchanged. Traditional methods such as grid-based displays, facets, and keyword-based search are still commonly used. These techniques are often inadequate and can be frustrating when queries return thousands of hits, many of which are extraneous
[
2
]
. Moreover, these search techniques are neither robust enough to provide an overview of the entire collection nor do they reveal the implicit and explicit relationships among search entities.

To address scientific problems, researchers often need datasets observed at different locations and different times. Traditional text or keyword-based searches often fail to produce needed results unless many different and manual queries are made. While this approach can sometimes lead to success, it often results in long searches with imperfect results
[
4
]
. To address these shortcomings, we developed a knowledge graph based visual search application (see Figure 1) inspired by the work of Marian Dörk
[
2
]
and previous work related to multiple linked views (MLV), collaborative filtering, facet search, visual querying, and exploration on the web. This application leverages four visualizations to present search results from four perspectives: Word Cloud for Contextual, Map for Spatial, Line Charts for Temporal and Chord diagram for inter-relationships. At its core, it uses a knowledge graph to seamlessly connect metadata from two different repositories, enabling users to search for common entities and link disparate datasets. This approach enhances the discovery process and uncovers intricate connections, facilitating deeper insights into the collection and more informed decision-making.

For example, a user researching climate change impacts might start by clicking on a keyword like ’temperature’ into the Word Cloud visualization. This action would highlight related keywords such as ’climate’ and ’precipitation’, and filter the datasets accordingly across the other visualizations. Selecting a keyword like ’precipitation’ could further refine the results to show datasets related to precipitation measurements on the Map, the relevant time periods in the Line Charts, and the authors involved in these studies in the Chord Diagram. This iterative process allows users to quickly hone in on the most relevant datasets without having to perform multiple separate searches. This streamlined approach saved user weeks of manual searching and allowed to compile a list of comprehensive and interconnected datasets for its research.

The main dataset collections are ’Dataset’ and ’STACCollection.’ The ’Dataset’ collection primarily consists of datasets from the Pangaea repository, while the ’STACCollection’ comprises datasets from the DLR Earth Observatory repository.
To create meaningful connections between these two repositories, we utilized the ’Keyword’ collection with the relationship ’HasKeyword’ and the ’Authors’ collection with the ’HasAuthor’ edge collection. These relationships facilitate the linkage of related datasets across the two repositories based on shared keywords and authors. For Pangaea datasets a list of keywords is explicitly given, which is not the case for STAC collections. In order to nevertheless associate STAC collections with keywords, DLR publications are used as mediating artifacts. If the keyword list of a publication contains a reference to an earth observation mission, STAC collections produced by this mission will be connected to the publications keywords since they can be considered relevant in this context. The resulting graph is utilized by the visual search frontend to display relationships between datasets, authors, keywords along with their spatial and temporal meta information. Visualization Enabled Search Application (VESA)

VESA is the front end of our knowledge graph based visual search application. Unlike traditional text based search interfaces, VESA leverages different visualizations to provide efficient, robust and multidimensional search results.

The visualizations in VESA are based on question queries that typically starts with ”W” and ends with ”?” in text queries. Different studies
[
1
,
6
]
have analysed the effectiveness of ”W” questions queries for search queries and have found that they enhance search accuracy and improve the retrieval process. This also serves true for ESS datasets. These datasets are primarily observation datasets that provide information about the entity observed and the context of the observation
[
5
]
(e.g. when and where the observation was taken and who took the observation). Such information about the dataset is usually present in their metadata which typically looks like this example
2
2

2

https://doi.pangaea.de/10.1594/PANGAEA.958479
.

In constructing VESA, we used six of the metadata attributes as also defined in section 2 and have mapped them to different visualizations. Word Cloud is linked to the keywords extracted from Title, Abstract and Keywords attributes of the metadata. Selecting each keyword will show the related keywords in the word cloud and the filtered and related datasets in the other visualizations. If the desired keyword is not found on the canvas, a search can be performed using the autocomplete search bar which is connected to the all keywords in our data store. Map enables filtering based on the Spatial Coverage of the datasets. Currently two data repositories are shown, depicted with dots of two different colors. Line Chart shows the Temporal Coverage of the datasets. Chord Diagram shows the interrelationship among authors of the filtered datasets. This application is based on collaborative filtering, so clicking on any visualization will show the filtered results in other views or visualizations. On each iteration, a list view in the middle presents the filtered metadatas/datasets. It shows the count of filtered datasets and link to their actual DOI or source page.

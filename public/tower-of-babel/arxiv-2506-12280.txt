Ergodic Theory of Inhomogeneous Quantum Processes


Abstract
This work presents a rigorous framework for understanding ergodicity and mixing in time-inhomogeneous quantum systems. By analyzing quantum processes driven by sequences of quantum channels, we distinguish between forward and backward dynamics and reveal their fundamental asymmetries. A key contribution is the development of a quantum Markov–Dobrushin approach to characterize mixing behavior, offering clear criteria for convergence and exponential stability. The framework not only generalizes classical and homogeneous quantum models but also applies to non-translation-invariant matrix product states, highlighting its relevance to real-world quantum systems. These results contribute to the mathematical foundations of quantum dynamics and support the broader goal of advancing quantum information science.

Introduction

Ergodic theory provides a rigorous mathematical framework for understanding the long-term behavior of dynamical systems, especially the conditions under which time averages coincide with ensemble averages. The field has its roots in Boltzmann’s
ergodic hypothesis

[
18
]
, which proposed that a closed physical system, over time, explores all accessible microstates—thus justifying the use of time averages in statistical mechanics. This idea was later refined by the Ehrenfests into the
quasi-ergodic hypothesis

[
30
]
, suggesting that system trajectories are dense rather than uniformly covering the energy surface. The conceptual foundation of ergodicity was made precise through the seminal work of von Neumann and Birkhoff, who established the
mean
and
pointwise ergodic theorems
, respectively
[
72
,
15
]
. These results demonstrated how statistical regularities naturally emerge from deterministic dynamics.

This framework has since expanded to encompass both classical and quantum stochastic processes
[
38
]
. Markov chains emerged as a central model, capturing memoryless dynamics where future behavior depends only on the present state. A. A. Markov introduced this paradigm in 1906
[
46
]
, and it soon became a key tool for studying convergence and mixing in random processes. Dobrushin later extended the classical theory to
non-homogeneous
Markov chains with time-dependent transitions
[
28
,
39

, highlighting the robustness of ergodic properties even under temporal variability. These classical ideas laid the groundwork for their quantum generalization by replacing probability distributions with algebraic states and stochastic kernels with completely positive maps. This led to the development of quantum Markov chains by Accardi
[
2
]
, which provide a robust framework for exploring information flow and ergodic properties in quantum settings. Recent results further demonstrate ergodic behavior in quantum spin chains
[
68
,
67
]
and tree-like structures
[

, revealing how classical ergodic principles extend into the non-commutative realm.

1.1
Core Contributions

This work develops ergodic and mixing properties for time-inhomogeneous quantum processes, aiming to rigorously distinguish between various forms and rates of convergence in nonstationary quantum dynamics. The setting consists of a sequence of quantum channels
\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
acting on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, the algebra of bounded operators on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. These channels encode the temporal evolution of the system and reflect its intrinsic inhomogeneity.

Two natural compositions arise in this context: the
forward
and
backward
dynamics, defined respectively by

\Phi_{0,n}^{(f)}:=\Phi_{n}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{1}\circ\Phi_{0},\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi_{0,n}^{(b)}:=\Phi_{0}\circ\Phi_{1}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{n}.

These maps govern the time evolution of quantum states, represented as density operators in the compact set
\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})
, under the influence of non-commuting, time-dependent transformations. The divergence between the forward and backward sequences—unless the channels commute—highlights the nontrivial role of temporal asymmetry and positions these dynamics as central objects of study in the ergodic analysis of quantum systems. In this framework, ergodicity and mixing are treated not merely as asymptotic properties, but as structural indicators of long-term stability and convergence in time-inhomogeneous quantum regimes.

A fundamental asymmetry between forward and backward quantum dynamics is rigorously established below. This distinction, stemming from topological considerations, is captured in the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of quantum channels on
\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})
, and let
t\in\{f,b\}
indicate the direction of evolution.

1.

The backward process
\{\Phi^{(b)}_{0,n}\}
is mixing (resp. exponentially mixing) if and only if it is weakly mixing (resp., exponentially weakly mixing).

2.

If the forward process
\{\Phi^{(f)}_{0,n}\}
is mixing (resp. exponentially mixing) then it is weakly mixing (resp., exponentially weakly mixing).

Conversely, if the images are nested as

\Phi^{(f)}_{0,n+1}(\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H}))\subset\Phi^{(f)}_{0,n}(\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H}))\quad\text{for all }n,

(1)

then the reverse implications also hold.

While such a nesting condition is naturally satisfied in the backward process, it fails in general for forward dynamics. As a consequence, weak and strong mixing coincide in the backward case, but may differ in the forward case. This asymmetry is further illustrated by explicit counterexamples.

A methodological framework is developed based on the
quantum Markov–Dobrushin inequality
introduced in
[
1
]
, which provides a rigorous quantitative criterion for analyzing mixing in nonstationary quantum dynamics. This inequality establishes a contraction bound in total variation distance for quantum channels, reflecting their ability to diminish distinguishability between states. For any quantum channel
\Phi
, the bound is given by

\|\Phi(\rho)-\Phi(\sigma)\|_{TV}\leq\left(1-\mathrm{Tr}(\kappa_{\Phi})\right)\|\rho-\sigma\|_{TV}

where
\rho,\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})
, and the operator
\kappa_{\Phi}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})_{+}
is defined as

\kappa_{\Phi}:=\inf\left\{\Phi(|\xi\rangle\langle\xi|)\,\middle|\,\xi\in\mathcal{H},\|\xi\|=1\right\}

The trace
\mathrm{Tr}(\kappa_{\Phi})
serves as a channel-dependent contraction coefficient, capturing the minimal dispersion exerted by
\Phi
across all pure inputs.
Building on the foundational Markov-Dobrushin inequality, the following theorem formulates a broad convergence criterion for non-homogeneous quantum dynamics. Notably, it ensures asymptotic stability for both forward and backward evolutions without the assumption of uniform contractivity—a key departure from classical approaches.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of quantum channels where the sequence of Markov-Dobrushin coefficients
\{\kappa_{\Phi_{n}}\}
admit a non-zero accumulation point. Then there exists
\mu\in(0,1)
and an increasing unbounded sequence
\{N(n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that for all
t\in\{b,f\}
:

\sup_{\rho,\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})}\left\|\Phi^{(t)}_{0,n}(\rho)-\Phi^{(t)}_{0,n}(\sigma)\right\|_{TV}\leq 2\mu^{N(n)}

(2)

As a consequence, both the forward
\{\Phi^{(f)}_{0,n}\}
and backward
\{\Phi^{(b)}_{0,n}\}
dynamics are weakly mixing, with state differences decaying exponentially in
N(n)
.

This result provides a unified and flexible framework for quantifying the rate of convergence in inhomogeneous quantum settings. When
N(n)=\mathcal{O}(n)
, one recovers exponential mixing, whereas
N(n)=\mathcal{O}(\ln n)
yields sub-exponential (polynomial) decay. The rate of mixing is thus governed by the temporal distribution of strongly contractive steps, underscoring the interplay between local contraction and global dynamical behavior.

1.1.1
Application to Inhomogeneous Matrix Product States

To substantiate the practical significance of the developed ergodic framework, we examine its application to the class of
non-translation-invariant matrix product states
(MPS), which serve as paradigmatic models of inhomogeneous quantum spin chains. These states are naturally defined over the quasi-local
C^{*}
-algebra

\mathfrak{A}_{\mathbb{N}}:=\bigotimes_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K}),

where each local algebra
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
corresponds to the bounded operators acting on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}\cong\mathbb{C}^{m}
. The MPS construction employs a site-dependent family of Kraus operators
\{A_{i}^{[n]}\}_{i=1}^{m}\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
with auxiliary Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}\cong\mathbb{C}^{d}
, yielding for each system size
n
the vector

|\Psi_{n}\rangle=\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{m}\cdots\sum_{i_{n}=1}^{m}\mathrm{Tr}\left(A_{i_{1}}^{[1]}A_{i_{2}}^{[2]}\cdots A_{i_{n}}^{[n]}\right)|i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{n}\rangle\in\mathcal{K}^{\otimes n}.

This defines a sequence of normalized states
\{\varphi_{n}\}
on the local algebras
\mathfrak{A}_{[0,n]}:=\bigotimes_{k=0}^{n}\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
by

\varphi_{n}(X):=\frac{\langle\Psi_{n}|X|\Psi_{n}\rangle}{\langle\Psi_{n}|\Psi_{n}\rangle},\quad X\in\mathfrak{A}_{[0,n]}.

Leveraging the convergence framework established in Theorem
1.2
, we now turn to its application in the context of inhomogeneous matrix product states (MPS). Each step in the sequence is governed by a quantum channel
\Phi_{n}
on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, arising from Kraus operators
\{A_{i}^{[n]}\}_{i=1}^{m}
that encode the evolving correlations of the MPS’s auxiliary space.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of quantum channels on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, where
\mathcal{H}\cong\mathbb{C}^{d}
, with uniformly bounded quantum Markov-Dobrushin constants
\kappa_{\Phi_{n}}
satisfying

\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\kappa_{\Phi_{n}}\geq\kappa>0.

Then, the associated sequence of states
\{\varphi_{n}\}
converges in the weak-* topology to a unique state
\varphi_{\infty}
on the quasi-local algebra
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathbb{N}}
. For any local observable
X\in\mathfrak{A}_{[0,n]}
, the limiting state is given by:

\varphi_{\infty}(X)=\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\langle e_{k},\rho^{(f)}_{\infty}e_{l}\rangle\mathrm{Tr}\left(\hat{X}(e_{k}e_{l}^{*})\right),

(3)

where
\{e_{k}\}_{k=1}^{d}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
,
\rho^{(f)}_{\infty}
is the fixed-point density matrix of the limiting forward channel, and
\hat{X}
denotes the dual representation of
X
given by (
44
).

This offers a concrete realization of the abstract mixing criteria of Theorem
1.2
, providing a rigorous foundation to analyze the ergodic properties of quantum spin chains with spatially inhomogeneous interactions.

Preliminaries

Let
d
be a natural number, and suppose
\mathcal{H}
is a
d
-dimensional Hilbert space with conventional orthonormal basis
\{|i\rangle\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
. The space of all bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H}
, written as
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, forms an algebraic structure where the identity operator
\mathrm{id}
acts as the multiplicative unit occupied with a norm
\|\cdot\|
. Recall that a state on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a positive linear map
\varphi
such that
\varphi({\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I})=1
. We denote
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})
the set of states on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
.
The set of density operators on
\mathcal{H}
, which describe quantum states, is defined as:

\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})=\left\{\rho\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\mid\rho=\rho^{*},\rho\geq 0,\operatorname{Tr}(\rho)=1\right\}.

Here,
\rho^{*}
is the adjoint of
\rho
, and the trace condition
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho)=1
ensures normalization. This set is convex and compact—key properties that make it indispensable in quantum information theory, especially for optimization tasks and variational approaches.
The map
\rho\mapsto\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\cdot)
define a bijective map from
\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})
onto
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})
.
For a given probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
the expectation value on maps
f:\{1,\dots,d\}\to\mathbb{C}
with respect to the measure
\mu

\mu(f)=\sum_{i}f(i)\mu(\{i\})

(4)

define a state on the diagonal subalgebra
\mathcal{D}
spanned by the brackets
|i\rangle\langle i|
. The correspondent density operator is
\rho_{\mu}=\sum_{i}\mu(\{i\})|i\rangle\langle i|
allows the extension of (
4
) to define a state on the full algebra
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
through:

\varphi_{\mu}:A\mapsto\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{\mu}A)

Within
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, the positive cone
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})_{+}
consists of all positive semi-definite operators. These operators are fundamental in quantum mechanics, representing physically realizable objects like density matrices and measurement effects. Formally, an operator
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})_{+}
if and only if:

\langle\psi,A\psi\rangle\geq 0\quad\forall\psi\in\mathcal{H}.

This positivity condition guarantees non-negative expectation values, making
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})_{+}
crucial for defining valid quantum states, measurement procedures, and generalized measurement schemes such as positive operator-valued measures (POVMs), which extend the concept of projective measurements.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
40
,
24
]

For any super-operator
\Phi
acting on the operator space
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, the following equivalence holds:
\Phi
is completely positive precisely when there exists a finite collection
\{K_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of bounded operators (called Kraus operators) such that
\Phi
can be expressed as:

\Phi(A)=\sum_{i\in I}K_{i}AK_{i}^{\dagger},

(5)

where each
K_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. This operator-sum representation plays a pivotal role in quantum information theory, offering an explicit mechanism for quantum state transformations through environmental interactions.

Definition 2.2
.

A quantum channel is defined as a linear map
\Phi
between operator spaces
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
and
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K}^{\prime})
that maintains two crucial properties: complete positivity and trace preservation (CPTP). These requirements guarantee the physical implementability of such transformations. In our current framework, we specifically examine channels where both input and output spaces coincide, i.e.,
\Phi:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, with
\mathcal{H}
being our previously defined finite-dimensional Hilbert space.

Let
\Phi^{*}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be the Hilbert-Schmidt dual of a quantum channel
\Phi
in the following sense

\langle X,\Phi(Y)\rangle_{\text{HS}}=\langle\Phi^{*}(X),Y\rangle_{\text{HS}}\qquad\forall X,Y\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})

We call
\Phi^{*}
a
Markov operator
since it is completely positive and unital.
These properties make
\Phi^{*}
the quantum analogue of a classical Markov transition matrix, where complete positivity generalizes the entrywise positivity of classical transition probabilities, and identity preservation corresponds to probability conservation.

Remark 2.3
.

The duality between quantum channels
\Phi
and Markov operators
\Phi^{*}
manifests through:

•

\Phi
being trace-preserving
\Leftrightarrow

\Phi^{*}
is identity-preserving

•

\Phi
being completely positive
\Leftrightarrow

\Phi^{*}
is completely positive

This duality establishes Markov operators as dual maps of quantum channels. In the framework of quantum Markov chains

The iterated application of a quantum channel
\Phi
is denoted by its
n
-fold composition:

\Phi^{n}=\underbrace{\Phi\circ\Phi\circ\cdots\circ\Phi}_{n\text{ times}},

with the convention that
\Phi^{0}
equals the identity superoperator
\mathcal{I}
on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

For any completely positive linear map
\Phi:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
, the operator-sum representation takes the form:

\Phi(\rho)=\sum_{i}K_{i}\rho K_{i}^{\dagger}

where
\{K_{i}\}
are the associated Kraus operators. An essential feature of trace-preserving maps is characterized by the normalization condition:

\sum_{i}K_{i}^{\dagger}K_{i}={\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}

which ensures probability conservation in quantum measurements and transformations.

A quantum channel
\Phi
is called
ergodic
if, for any initial state
\rho
, its time-averaged evolution converges to a unique fixed point
\rho_{*}
satisfying
\Phi(\rho_{*})=\rho_{*}
:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Phi^{k}(\rho)=\rho_{*}

The limiting channel extends into a super-operator on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
given by

\widehat{\Phi}(M):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Phi^{k}(M)=\mathrm{Tr}(M)\rho_{*}

(6)

is defined on the full algebra idempotent (
\widehat{\Phi}\circ\widehat{\Phi}=\widehat{\Phi}
) and satisfies
\widehat{\Phi}\circ\Phi=\widehat{\Phi}
. Moreover, it projects any state onto
\rho_{*}
, reflecting the long-time loss of initial state information.

A stronger property,
mixing
, requires the convergence of individual trajectories for all pairs of states:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\Phi^{n}(\rho)-\Phi^{n}(\sigma)\|=0\quad\forall\rho,\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})

(7)

While all mixing channels are ergodic, the converse fails: ergodicity depends solely on the fixed-point structure, whereas mixing additionally demands specific spectral properties of
\Phi
. The distinction highlights that mixing enforces uniformity in long-time behavior, while ergodicity only guarantees convergence in the time-averaged sense.

3
Ergodicity and Mixing for Inhomogeneous Quantum Processes

In this section, we explore how to define ergodicity and mixing for quantum systems that evolve in time under a sequence of different quantum channels. Our approach takes inspiration from the classical theory of inhomogeneous Markov chains, where similar questions about long-term behavior have been studied for decades.

The key idea is simple: in a homogeneous system, the same rule (or quantum channel) is applied at every time step. In contrast, an inhomogeneous system evolves under changing rules—each step may involve a different quantum channel. This mirrors what happens in classical probability when transition rules vary over time.

In classical ergodic theory, this time-dependent setting is well understood thanks to tools like Cesàro averages and Dobrushin-type coefficients. These tools help us understand when and how systems forget their initial state and settle into a stable behavior, even if the rules keep changing. This kind of analysis has been developed in a number of classical works
[
23
,
74
,
50
,
21
]
and has recently found its way into the quantum realm
[
53
,
54
]
. Building on these insights, we aim to formulate quantum counterparts that capture long-term statistical behavior even in settings where the underlying dynamics vary with time.

3.1
Ergodicity of Inhomogeneous Markov Chains

Consider a discrete state space
S=\{1,\ldots,d\}
with
d\geq 2
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{d}
denote the probability simplex of all probability measures on
S
, represented as row vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with non-negative components summing to unity. The time evolution is governed by a sequence of right-stochastic matrices
\{\Pi_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, where each matrix
\Pi_{n}
satisfies the stochasticity conditions: all entries
(\Pi_{n})_{ij}\geq 0
and row sums
\sum_{j=1}^{d}(\Pi_{n})_{ij}=1
for every
i\in S

[
21
]
.

The multi-step transition operator from time
m
to
k
is given by the ordered matrix product

\Pi^{(m,k)}=\Pi_{k-1}\cdots\Pi_{m+1}\Pi_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d},\quad k>m\geq 0,

(8)

which preserves the stochasticity property under multiplication. For any initial probability measure
\mu_{m}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
, the time-evolved distribution at step
k
is obtained through right multiplication:

\mu_{k}=\mu_{m}\Pi^{(m,k)}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}.

(9)

The system exhibits weak ergodicity when the long-term behavior becomes independent of initial conditions, as quantified by the
\ell^{1}
-norm convergence

\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{d}}\|\mu\Pi^{(m,k)}-\nu\Pi^{(m,k)}\|=0,

(10)

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes a norm on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Strong ergodicity requires the stronger condition of convergence to a unique stationary distribution
\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
, expressed as

\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{d}}\|\mu\Pi^{(m,k)}-\pi\|=0.

(11)

These ergodicity concepts are related through the fundamental inequality

\|\mu\Pi^{(m,k)}-\nu\Pi^{(m,k)}\|\leq 2\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{d}}\|\mu\Pi^{(m,k)}-\pi\|,

(12)

which immediately shows that strong ergodicity implies weak ergodicity.

Example 3.1
.

[
21
]
Consider a binary-state system (
d=2
) with time-dependent transition matrices that alternate between two forms:

\displaystyle\Pi_{2n}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}2^{-n}&1-2^{-n}\\
2^{-n}&1-2^{-n}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\Pi_{2n+1}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1-(2n+1)^{-1}&(2n+1)^{-1}\\
1-(2n+1)^{-1}&(2n+1)^{-1}\end{pmatrix}

for
n\geq 0
.
This system exhibits weak ergodicity since
\|\mu\Pi^{(m,k)}-\nu\Pi^{(m,k)}\|\to 0
as
k\to\infty
for any initial distributions
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}
. However, it fails to be strongly ergodic because the sequences
\mu\Pi_{2n}
and
\mu\Pi_{2n+1}
converges to two different limiting distributions
\pi_{even}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\end{array}\right)
and
\pi_{odd}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\end{array}\right)
respectively.

In the homogeneous setting, equations (
10
) and (
11
) are equivalent, as both express convergence towards the unique invariant probability measure associated with the fixed stochastic matrix
\Pi
. In contrast, the condition of weak ergodicity introduces the first fundamental distinction in the asymptotic behavior of
inhomogeneous

, typically measured by the Dobrushin ergodicity coefficient

under matrix multiplication; and the existence of uniform bounds on the convergence rates across different initial distributions

3.2
Forward and backward quantum Dynamics

Inhomogeneous quantum dynamics are described by sequences of quantum channels
\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
acting on the set of density operators
\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})
, where the quantum channel
\Phi_{n}
can be written in terms of Krauss operators

\Phi_{n}(\rho)=\sum_{i}K_{n;i}\rho K_{n;i}^{\dagger}

(13)

The key distinction with the homogeneous dynamics lies in the non-commutativity of channel compositions
\Phi_{n}\circ\Phi_{n+1}
and
\Phi_{n+1}\circ\Phi_{n}
generally differ, making temporal ordering crucial. This distinction leads to two fundamentally distinct types of dynamics. The backward evolution, denoted by
\Phi^{(b)}_{0,n}
, represents the natural chronological application of quantum channels:

\Phi^{(b)}_{0,n}:=\Phi_{0}\circ\Phi_{1}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{n}

(14)

This composition describes the conventional time-ordered quantum process, where each operation
\Phi_{k}
is applied in sequence from initial time
0
to final time
n
. This evolution is governed by the equation
\Phi^{(b)}_{0,n+1}=\Phi^{(b)}_{0,n}\circ\Phi_{n+1}
. The kraus decomposition associated with the backward dynamics is given by

\Phi^{(b)}_{0,n}(\rho)=\sum_{i_{0},i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}}K_{0;i_{0}}K_{1;i_{1}}\cdots K_{n;i_{n}}\rho K_{n;i}^{\dagger}\cdots K_{1;i_{1}}^{\dagger}K_{0;i_{0}}^{\dagger}

In contrast, the forward evolution
\Phi^{(f)}_{0,n}
implements the reverse temporal ordering:

\Phi^{(f)}_{0,n}:=\Phi_{n}\circ\Phi_{n-1}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{0}

(15)

and the evolution is governed by the forward equation
\Phi^{(f)}_{0,n+1}=\Phi_{n+1}\circ\Phi_{0,n}^{(f)}
.
The associated krauss decomposition is given by

\Phi^{(f)}_{0,n}(\rho)=\sum_{i_{0},i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}}K_{n;i_{n}}\cdots K_{1;i_{1}}K_{n;i_{n}}\rho K_{0;i_{0}}^{\dagger}K_{1;i_{1}}^{\dagger}\cdots K_{n;i}^{\dagger}

Remark 3.2
.

A fundamental difference between homogeneous and inhomogeneous quantum dynamics arises from the inherently non-commutative structure of quantum operations. In the time-dependent setting, quantum evolutions described by a sequence of channels
\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
exhibit qualitative behaviors that diverge significantly from those governed by a fixed channel
\Phi
. While homogeneous systems typically converge to fixed points determined by the invariant states of
\Phi
, inhomogeneous evolutions reflect the full temporal structure of the sequence, resulting in asymptotic behaviors that are sensitive to the entire history of the dynamics
[
9
,
11
,
22
]
.

This distinction is particularly pronounced in the context of quantum control and open quantum systems, where external fluctuations and environmental interactions naturally give rise to time-dependent dynamics
[
58
]
. Importantly, in the inhomogeneous case, standard fixed-point arguments become inapplicable due to the non-commutativity of the channels involved. As a result, classical characterizations of ergodicity, which rely on invariance under a single channel, no longer hold
[
57
]
. Instead, new analytical tools are required to capture the emergent long-term behavior of such non-stationary quantum processes.

Definition 3.3

(Ergodicity Hierarchy with Averages)
.

Let
\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of quantum channels acting on the space of density operators
\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})
, where
t\in\{\text{forward},\text{backward}\}
indicates the temporal direction of evolution. The
ergodic average
is defined as:

\overline{\Phi}^{(t)}_{0,n}:=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Phi^{(t)}_{0,k}.

(16)

The system exhibits the following hierarchical ergodic properties:

1.

Ergodicity
:

\exists\rho_{\infty}^{(t)}\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H});\forall\rho,\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H}):\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|\overline{\Phi}^{(t)}_{0,n}(\rho)-\rho_{\infty}^{(t)}\right\|=0

(17)

2.

Uniform Ergodicity
:

\exists\rho_{\infty}^{(t)}\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H}):\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\rho\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})}\left\|\overline{\Phi}^{(t)}_{0,n}(\rho)-\rho_{\infty}^{(t)}\right\|=0

(18)

3.

Mixing
:

\exists\rho_{\infty}^{(t)}\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H});\forall\rho,\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H}):\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|\overline{\Phi}^{(t)}_{0,n}(\rho)-\rho_{\infty}^{(t)}\right\|=0

(19)

4.

Uniform Mixing
:

\exists\rho_{\infty}^{(t)}\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H}):\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\rho\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})}\left\|\Phi^{(t)}_{0,n}(\rho)-\rho_{\infty}^{(t)}\right\|=0.

(20)

5.

Exponential Mixing
:

\exists\rho_{\infty}^{(t)}\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H}),\mu\in(0,1);\forall\rho\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H}):\left\|\Phi^{(t)}_{0,n}(\rho)-\rho_{\infty}^{(t)}\right\|\leq C\mu^{n}

(21)

for some constant
C>0
.

Remark 3.4
.

When the sequence
\{\Phi_{0,n}^{(t)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies an ergodic or mixing property in the sense of Definition
3.3
, the limiting density operator
\rho_{\infty}^{(t)}
defines a limiting quantum channel
\Phi_{\infty}^{(t)}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
given by:

\Phi_{\infty}^{(t)}(M)=\mathrm{Tr}(M)\rho_{\infty}^{(t)}\qquad\forall M\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}).

(22)

This channel is a rank-one, trace-preserving linear map that projects any operator onto the asymptotic state
\rho_{\infty}^{(t)}
.

Two critical differences arise compared to the homogeneous case, where dynamics are governed by a single repeated channel
\Phi
:

•

Temporal direction dependence
: The limit
\rho_{\infty}^{(t)}
is not universal but depends on the evolution direction
t\in\{\text{forward},\text{backward}\}
of the sequence
\{\Phi_{0,n}^{(t)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

•

Sensitivity to perturbations
: Unlike the homogeneous setting—where
\rho_{\infty}
is a fixed point of
\Phi
(see e.g.
[
65
,
75
]
) and thus stable under iterations—the asymptotic state here is fragile: any perturbation or reordering of the sequence may disrupt convergence or alter
\rho_{\infty}^{(t)}
entirely.

This underscores a fundamental distinction between time-dependent and time-independent quantum dynamical systems.

4
Weakly Mixing Inhomogeneous Quantum processes

Understanding the asymptotic behavior of quantum channels—particularly their capacity to asymptotically erase memory of their initial states—constitutes a central question in quantum information theory and the broader study of open quantum systems. This memory loss, often termed
mixing
, encapsulates the idea that the output of a quantum process becomes increasingly independent of its starting point. The concept has deep roots in both classical and quantum settings, having been rigorously explored in various frameworks (see, e.g.,
[
75
,
65
]
).

In the present section, we aim to provide a rigorous formulation of mixing properties for inhomogeneous (time-dependent) sequences of quantum channels. By extending the classical notions of ergodicity and convergence, we develop a general framework suited to analyzing the dynamical behavior of quantum operations in time-varying environments.

In particular, we extend the classical notion of
weak ergodicity
to the quantum non-homogeneous setting and investigate its implications. We establish a necessary condition for the equivalence between various types of mixing, namely: mixing and weak mixing, as well as exponential mixing and exponential weak mixing. Furthermore, we show that for the
backward dynamics
of quantum processes, strong mixing is equivalent to weak mixing, and analogously, exponential mixing is equivalent to weak exponential mixing. These results contribute to a finer classification of ergodic behavior in quantum channels and provide a bridge between different asymptotic regimes in non-stationary quantum evolutions.

Definition 4.1

(Weak Ergodicity, Weak Mixing, and Exponential weak Mixing)
.

Let
\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of quantum channels acting on the space of density operators
\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})
. We define three asymptotic behaviors with respect to the direction of evolution
t\in\{f,b\}
, representing forward or backward dynamics, respectively.

•

Weak Ergodicity:
The time-averaged sequence of channels satisfies

\forall\rho,\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H}):\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|\overline{\Phi}^{(t)}_{0,n}(\rho)-\overline{\Phi}^{(t)}_{0,n}(\sigma)\right\|=0,

(23)

where
\overline{\Phi}^{(t)}_{0,n}
denotes the ergodic average over the first
n+1
iterates of the channel.

•

Weak Mixing:
The sequence satisfies

\forall\rho,\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H}):\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|\Phi^{(t)}_{0,n}(\rho)-\Phi^{(t)}_{0,n}(\sigma)\right\|=0.

(24)

•

Exponential weak Mixing:
There exist constants
C>0
and
\mu\in(0,1)
such that for all
\rho,\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})
,

\left\|\Phi^{(t)}_{0,n}(\rho)-\Phi^{(t)}_{0,n}(\sigma)\right\|\leq C\mu^{n}.

(25)

Remark 4.2
.

The above definition captures three key modes of asymptotic behavior in the evolution of non-homogeneous quantum processes. Weak ergodicity reflects the idea that, when averaged over time, the influence of the initial state gradually disappears—a concept analogous to the classical ergodic theorem. Weak mixing strengthens this notion by requiring pointwise convergence of the evolved states, without averaging. Finally, exponential weak mixing quantifies the rate of this convergence, demanding that it occurs at an exponential speed. These distinctions are crucial in understanding the degree to which a quantum system ”forgets” its initial configuration and converge toward a universal long-term behavior, regardless of its starting point.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

"\Rightarrow"
We start by showing that the direct implications hold both for the forward and backward dynamics. Let

This finishes the direct implication for both backward and forward dynamics.

"\Leftarrow"

By definition of backward evolution, one has:

\Phi_{0,n+1}^{(b)}(\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H}))=\Phi_{0,n}^{(b)}\circ\Phi_{n+1}(\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H}))\subseteq\Phi_{0,n}^{(b)}(\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})).

4.1
Trajectory-Ergodic Characterization of Weak Mixing

In this subsection, we introduce the concept of trajectory ergodicity as a natural intermediary between ergodicity and mixing for quantum dynamical systems. This formulation interprets the evolution of quantum states through sequences of channels, emphasizing convergence along state trajectories. Within this hierarchy, weak mixing is characterized by the exponential alignment of any initial state with a sequence of reference states, offering a dynamic perspective that refines the structural equivalences established in Theorem

(a):

Given
\{Z_{n}\}
satisfying (c), for any

:

\left\lVert\Phi_{0,n}^{(t)}(\rho)-\Phi_{0,n}^{(t)}(\sigma)\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert\Phi_{0,n}^{(t)}(\rho)-Z_{n}\right\rVert+\left\lVert\Phi_{0,n}^{(t)}(\sigma)-Z_{n}\right\rVert\leq 2C\mu^{n}.

This establishes exponential mixing with constant
2C
and rate
\mu
.

Remark 4.6
.

The structural analysis presented in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
4.5
reveals a fundamental asymmetry between backward and forward dynamics in quantum processes.

In the context of
backward dynamics
, the different formulations of mixing—including equations (
24
), (
30

19

By contrast,

exhibits a more delicate behavior. Although exponential mixing in the strong sense (as formulated in equation (

ensuring that the successive images of the state space under the forward evolution form a decreasing family. This nestedness introduces a kind of asymptotic consistency, allowing one to recover the full strength of exponential mixing from its weaker variants.

Theorem

Thus, trajectory-ergodicity provides a refined understanding of weak mixing behavior in time-inhomogeneous quantum systems. This view bridges equilibrium theory with dynamical systems language and emphasizes the role of channel composition in shaping long-term behavior.

A Markov-Dobrushin Mixing Condition

This section outlines fundamental results on the behavior and properties of quantum channels, with a focus on their convergence rates via a quantum Markov-Dobrushin inequality.

For any operator
a\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, the decomposition
a=\frac{1}{2}(a+a^{*})+i\frac{1}{2i}(a-a^{*})
yields its real part
\Re(a)=\frac{1}{2}(a+a^{*})
and imaginary part
\Im(a)=\frac{1}{2i}(a-a^{*})
, both of which are self-adjoint. Any self-adjoint operator
b
can be expressed as
b=b_{+}-b_{-}
, where
b_{+},b_{-}
are positive operators with disjoint supports, and we define
\|b\|_{1}=\mathrm{Tr}(b_{+})+\mathrm{Tr}(b_{-})
. Building on this, we introduce the total variation norm
\|\cdot\|_{TV}
on
\mathcal{M}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
, extending
\|\cdot\|_{1}
to the full algebra. This norm is defined as:

\|a\|_{TV}:=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Re(a)_{+}+\Re(a)_{-}\right)+\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Im(a)_{+}+\Im(a)_{-}\right),

(33)

and has been shown to constitute a norm on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
as a real vector space
[
1
]
.

The infimum of a family of operators
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is defined as the largest operator
b
satisfying
b\leq a_{i}
for all
i\in I
:

\inf_{i\in I}a_{i}=\sup\{b\in\mathcal{A}\mid b\leq a_{i}\text{ for all }i\in I\},

(34)

where
\mathcal{A}
denotes the relevant operator space, and
b\leq a_{i}
indicates that
a_{i}-b
is positive.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\Phi:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a quantum channel. Then, for any states
\rho,\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})
, the following inequality holds:

\|\Phi(\rho)-\Phi(\sigma)\|_{TV}\leq\left(1-\mathrm{Tr}(\kappa_{\Phi})\right)\|\rho-\sigma\|_{TV},

(35)

where

\kappa_{\Phi}:=\inf\left\{\Phi(|\xi\rangle\langle\xi|)\mid\xi\in\mathcal{H},\|\xi\|=1\right\}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})_{+}.

(36)

is the quantum Markov-Dobrushin constant of
\Phi
.

Proof.

See
[
1
]
.
∎

Recall that an
accumulation point
(or
limit point
) of a sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in a metric space
(X,d)
is a point
x\in X
such that every neighborhood of
x
contains infinitely many terms of the sequence. Formally:

\forall\varepsilon>0,\quad B(x,\varepsilon)\cap\{x_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}\text{ is infinite},

where
B(x,\varepsilon)=\{y\in X:d(x,y)<\varepsilon\}
.

Equivalently,
x
is an accumulation point if there exists a subsequence
\{x_{\tau_{n}}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that:

\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{\tau_{n}}=x

Theorem
1.2
establishes weak mixing for non-homogeneous quantum dynamics under a compactness condition on the quantum Markov-Dobrushin constants. The key requirement is the existence of a non-zero accumulation point, which enables uniform control over state convergence in both forward and backward time directions.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The Markov-Dobrushin inequality (
35
) applied to consecutive channels
\Phi
and
\Psi
establishes the contraction bound

\|\Psi(\Phi(\rho))-\Psi(\Phi(\sigma))\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq(1-\mathrm{Tr}(\kappa_{\Psi}))(1-\mathrm{Tr}(\kappa_{\Phi}))\|\rho-\sigma\|_{\mathrm{TV}}

showing each channel introduces a multiplicative contraction factor independent of composition order. Iterating this through the sequence
\{\Phi_{k}\}_{1\leq k\leq n}
yields the uniform estimate

\|\Phi^{(t)}_{0,n}(\rho)-\Phi^{(t)}_{0,n}(\sigma)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-\mathrm{Tr}(\kappa_{\Phi_{j}}))\right)\|\rho-\sigma\|_{\mathrm{TV}}

(37)

for both forward and backward compositions
t\in\{f,b\}
, as the product of contraction factors remains invariant under order reversal.

\big\|\Psi(\Phi(\rho))-\Psi(\Phi(\sigma))\big\|_{TV}\leq(1-\mathrm{Tr}(\kappa_{\Psi}))(1-\mathrm{Tr}(\kappa_{\Phi}))\|\rho-\sigma\|_{TV}

The assumption about the existence of a non-zero accumulation point
\kappa
guarantees that infinitely many channels in our sequence provide substantial mixing. Specifically, there exists a subsequence
\{\Phi_{n_{k}}\}
where each channel satisfies:

\mathrm{Tr}(\kappa_{\Phi_{n_{k}}})\geq r=\mathrm{Tr}(\kappa)/2>0

Let
N(n)
count how many of these ”good” channels ( elements of
\{\Phi_{n_{k}}\}
) appear in the first
n
positions of our sequence. Since there are infinitely many such channels,
N(n)
grows without bound as
n
increases. Focusing on these particularly effective channels allows us to establish an exponential bound:

\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-\mathrm{Tr}(\kappa_{\Phi_{j}})))\leq(1-r)^{N(n)}=\mu^{N(n)}

where
\mu=1-r<1
. Then
\|\Phi^{(t)}_{0,n}(\rho)-\Phi^{(t)}_{0,n}(\sigma)\|_{TV}\leq\mu^{N(n)}\|\rho-\sigma\|_{TV}

Since the total variation distance between any two density operator is at most 2, we obtain:

\|\Phi^{(t)}_{0,n}(\rho)-\Phi^{(t)}_{0,n}(\sigma)\|_{TV}\leq 2\mu^{N(n)}

Taking the supremum on the left hand side over
\rho,\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})
leads to the uniform estimate (
2
).

As
n\to\infty
, the right-hand side tends to zero, demonstrating that any initial differences between states disappear exponentially fast on
N(n)
under repeated application of our channel sequence. This establishes the uniform mixing property for both forward and backward dynamics.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Theorem
1.2
establishes a unified framework for understanding mixing phenomena in inhomogeneous quantum processes, significantly generalizing important previous works on ergodic quantum processes. Our results reveal that what was previously characterized as ergodicity in the forward dynamics corresponds precisely to what we identify as exponential mixing. The convergence rate in (
2
), expressed as

e^{-N(n)\ln(1/\mu)},

encompasses a spectrum of dynamical behaviors - from exponential to polynomial convergence - depending on the density
N(n)
of effective mixing channels. For instance, when
N(n)\sim\ln(n)
, we obtain polynomial convergence of order
n^{-\alpha}
with
\alpha=\ln(1/\mu)
. This raises the Markov-Dobrushin mixing condition recovers a strictly larger class of mixing inhomogeneous processes that the class of exponentially mixing quantum processes through the positivity assumptions suggested in
[
52
]
.

Corollary 5.3

(Exponential Mixing in Periodic Quantum Stochastic Processes)
.

Let
\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of quantum channels with period
p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, i.e.,
\Phi_{n+p}=\Phi_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose there exists some index
1\leq j_{*}\leq p
such that the Markov-Dobrushin constant of
\Phi_{j_{*}}
satisfies
\kappa_{\Phi_{j_{*}}}>0
.
Then, the quantum process generated by
\{\Phi_{n}\}
exhibits exponential weak mixing in both forward (
t=f
) and backward (
t=b
) time directions. Specifically, there exists a constant
C=C(p,\kappa_{\Phi_{j_{*}}})>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any pair of quantum states
\rho,\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})
,

\left\|\Phi^{(t)}_{0,n}(\rho)-\Phi^{(t)}_{0,n}(\sigma)\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq C\mu^{n},

(38)

where the contraction rate
\mu
is given explicitly by

\mu=\left(1-\kappa_{\Phi_{j_{*}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
.

Proof.

The
p
-periodicity of the channel sequence allows us to analyze the mixing properties through cycle decomposition. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
n=qp+s
where
q=\lfloor n/p\rfloor
counts complete periods and
0\leq s<p
counts remaining operations.

Let
j_{*}\in[1,p]
such that
\kappa_{\Phi_{i_{*}}}>0
then each period contains at least a channel whose contraction coefficient is equal to
\kappa_{\Phi_{j_{*}}}
. The cumulative contraction satisfies:

\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-\mathrm{Tr}(\kappa_{\Phi_{j}}))=\underbrace{\left(\prod_{j=1}^{p}(1-\mathrm{Tr}(\kappa_{\Phi_{j}}))\right)^{q}}_{\text{periodic contraction}}\cdot\underbrace{\prod_{\ell=1}^{s}(1-\mathrm{Tr}(\kappa_{\Phi_{\ell}}))}_{\text{residual terms}}\leq(1-\mathrm{Tr}(\kappa_{\Phi_{j_{*}}}))^{q}

One has

(1-\mathrm{Tr}(\kappa_{\Phi_{j_{*}}}))^{q}=(1-\mathrm{Tr}(\kappa_{\Phi_{j_{*}}}))^{\frac{n-s}{p}}=C\mu^{n}

where
C=2(1-\mathrm{Tr}(\kappa_{\Phi_{j_{*}}}))^{\frac{-s}{p}}
and
\mu=(1-\mathrm{Tr}(\kappa_{\Phi_{j_{*}}}))^{\frac{1}{p}}
. It follows from (
37
) that:

\|\Phi_{0,n}^{(t)}(\rho)-\Phi_{0,n}^{(t)}(\sigma)\|_{TV}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-\mathrm{Tr}(\kappa_{\Phi_{j}}))\|\rho-\sigma\|_{TV}\leq C\mu^{n}

This leads to the exponential weak mixing and finishes the proof.
∎

6
Mixing Properties for Inhomogeneous Matrix Product States

Over the past few years, Matrix Product States (MPS) have increasingly been studied through the perspective of quantum processes, providing a natural setting for investigating ergodic and mixing behaviors—especially beyond the translation-invariant case
[
60
]
. This dynamical viewpoint interprets the local tensors of an MPS as quantum channels, enabling a refined analysis of their long-term properties using tools from quantum ergodic theory. Notably, the works
[
7
,
63
]
demonstrate that such a formulation is particularly effective in describing exponential mixing and in capturing the thermodynamic limit behavior of MPS.

In this section, the Markov–Dobrushin mixing condition developed earlier is applied to analyze the mixing and exponential mixing behavior of inhomogeneous MPS. This approach not only clarifies the dynamics underlying such states but also highlights the strength of the Markov–Dobrushin framework in handling non-translation-invariant quantum structures.

Matrix Product States offer a compact and structured way to represent quantum many-body states in one dimension. Consider a
d
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
and an
m
-dimensional physical space
\mathcal{K}
with basis
\{|i\rangle\}_{i=1}^{m}
. A general
non-translation-invariant
MPS with periodic boundary conditions takes the form:

|\Psi_{n}\rangle=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}=1}^{m}\mathrm{Tr}\left(A_{i_{1}}^{[1]}A_{i_{2}}^{[2]}\cdots A_{i_{n}}^{[n]}\right)|i_{1}\cdots i_{n}\rangle\in\mathcal{K}^{\otimes n}

(39)

where:

•

A_{i_{k}}^{[n]}
are site-dependent
d\times d
matrices (bond dimension
d
)

•

The superscript
[n]
indicates explicit position-dependence

•

Physical indices
i_{k}
range over the local basis
\{|i\rangle\}_{i=1}^{m}
of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
with local dimension
m
.

Recall that the trace of a super-operator
\Phi
on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is defined by

\mathrm{Tr}(\Phi)=\sum_{k,l}e_{k}^{*}\Phi(e_{k}e_{l}^{*})e_{l}

The guage condition

\sum_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}^{[n]}A_{i}^{[n]*}={\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}_{d}\quad\forall n

(40)

ensures that each local map
\Phi_{n}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
defined by

\Phi_{n}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}^{[n]*}XA_{i}^{[n]}

(41)

is trace-preserving and then it is a quantum channel.

The non-homogeneous transfer dynamics are governed by the forward dynamics
\Phi_{0,n}^{(f)}
through

\Phi^{(f)}_{1,n}:=\Phi_{n}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{1}

For any finite chain segment of length
n
, we associate the local operator algebra:

\mathfrak{A}_{[1,n]}:=\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})\cong\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K}^{\otimes n})

where
\mathcal{K}
represents the single-site Hilbert space with dimension
m
. This finite-dimensional algebra naturally embeds into larger systems via the inclusion:

\iota_{n}:\mathfrak{A}_{[1,n]}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{A}_{[1,n+1]},\quad X\mapsto X\otimes{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}_{m}

The inductive limit construction yields the quasi-local algebra:

\mathfrak{A}_{\text{loc}}:=\varinjlim\mathfrak{A}_{[1,n]}=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\mathfrak{A}_{[1,n]}

whose norm completion gives the C*-algebra of the semi-infinite chain:

\mathfrak{A}_{\mathbb{N}}:=\overline{\bigotimes_{\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})}^{\|\cdot\|}

A fundamental symmetry is the left shift automorphism
\sigma:\mathfrak{A}_{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathfrak{A}_{\mathbb{N}}
, defined on local operators by:

\sigma(X_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{n}):={\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}_{m}\otimes X_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{n}

with its
N
-fold iteration acting as:

\sigma^{N}(X)=\underbrace{{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}_{m}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}_{m}}_{N\text{ copies}}\otimes X

For a comprehensive treatment of such operator algebras, see
[
19
]
. The physical states are described by positive linear functionals
\varphi_{n}:\mathfrak{A}_{[1,n]}\to\mathbb{C}
:

\varphi_{n}(X):=\frac{\langle\Psi_{n}|X|\Psi_{n}\rangle}{\langle\Psi_{n}|\Psi_{n}\rangle},

(42)

The normalization constant
\mathcal{N}(n)=\|\Psi_{n}\|^{2}
of the matrix product state
|\Psi_{n}\rangle
is given by:

\|\Psi_{n}\|^{2}=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}=1}^{m}\left|\mathrm{Tr}\left(A_{i_{1}}^{[1]}\cdots A_{i_{n}}^{[n]}\right)\right|^{2}=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Phi_{1,n}^{(t)}\right)

(43)

The dynamical evolution is encoded in the sequence
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Each local observable
X\in\mathfrak{A}_{[1,n]}
induces a completely positive map
\widehat{X}:\mathcal{M}_{d}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathcal{M}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
:

\widehat{X}(M)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in[1,m]^{n}\end{subarray}}\langle\mathbf{i}|X|\mathbf{j}\rangle\cdot\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{i}}^{[n]*}M\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{j}}^{[n]}

(44)

where
\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{i}}^{[n]}:=A_{i_{1}}^{[1]}\cdots A_{i_{n}}^{[n]}
. Notably, under the gauge condition (
40
), the identity observable yields
\widehat{{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}}=\Phi^{(t)}_{1,n}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

For any local observable
X\in\mathfrak{A}_{[0,n]}
and integers
q\geq 0
, the state
\varphi_{n+q}
admits the representation:

\varphi_{n+q}(X)=\frac{\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Phi_{n+1,n+q}^{(f)}\circ\hat{X}\right)}{\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Phi_{0,n+q}^{(f)}\right)},

where
\Phi_{n,\,n+q}^{(f)}=\Phi_{n+q}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{n+1}\circ\Phi_{n}
denotes the composition of quantum channels from time
n
to
n+q
.

By the uniform lower bound on the Markov-Dobrushin constants and Theorem
1.2
, the sequence
\{\Phi_{0,n}^{(t)}\}
of backward channels converges exponentially fast to the completely depolarizing channel:

\Phi_{0,n}^{(t)}(M)\longrightarrow\Phi_{\infty}^{(t)}(M)=\mathrm{Tr}(M)\rho_{\infty}^{(t)},

where
\rho_{\infty}^{(t)}
is the unique invariant state. This convergence implies that the numerator satisfies:

\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Phi_{n+1,n+q}^{(f)}(\hat{X}(e_{k}e_{l}^{*}))\right)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Tr}\left(\hat{X}(e_{k}e_{l}^{*})\right)\langle e_{k},\rho_{\infty}^{(t)}e_{l}\rangle\quad\text{for all }k,l.

Summing over
k,l
and using the linearity of the trace yields:

\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Phi_{n+1,n+q}^{(f)}\circ\hat{X}\right)\longrightarrow\sum_{k,l}\langle e_{k},\rho_{\infty}^{(t)}e_{l}\rangle\mathrm{Tr}\left(\hat{X}(e_{k}e_{l}^{*})\right).

For the denominator, taking
X={\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}
(so
\hat{{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}}=\Phi_{0,n}^{(t)}
) gives:

\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Phi_{1,n+q}^{(f)}\right)\longrightarrow\sum_{k,l}\langle e_{k},\rho_{\infty}^{(t)}e_{l}\rangle\mathrm{Tr}(e_{k}e_{l}^{*})=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\langle e_{k},\rho_{\infty}^{(t)}e_{k}\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{\infty}^{(t)})=1.

Since both numerator and denominator converge, one obtains the weak-* limit:

\varphi_{n+q}(X)\longrightarrow\sum_{k,l}\langle e_{k},\rho_{\infty}^{(t)}e_{l}\rangle\mathrm{Tr}\left(\hat{X}(e_{k}e_{l}^{*})\right),

which establishes the claimed form of
\varphi_{\infty}
. The uniqueness follows from the uniqueness of
\rho_{\infty}^{(t)}
under the given ergodicity conditions.

Theorem

This perspective sheds light on how the long-term behavior of quantum channels directly shapes the ergodic properties of many-body quantum systems, offering an alternative pathway to the result established in
[
52

Crucially, this approach does not depend on translation invariance or classical ergodic techniques. Instead, it reveals how the intrinsic structure of the dynamics—encoded in the quantum Markov-Dobrushin constants—ensures convergence to a unique thermodynamic state, thereby generalizing the homogeneous case explored in
[

7
Discussion

This study has developed a framework for analyzing ergodicity and mixing in time-inhomogeneous quantum systems, grounded in the recently introduced qMD approach
[
1
]
. By focusing on sequences of quantum channels and their associated contraction properties, the work provides general conditions for convergence and exponential stability, with particular emphasis on non-homogeneous quantum dynamics and their state-level manifestations. The application to non-translation-invariant MPS illustrates the practical reach of this framework and opens several promising directions for further investigation.

A central theoretical question that naturally emerges from this work concerns the conceptual and structural relationship between the qMD condition and the classical irreducibility condition associated with Perron–Frobenius type theory. While the qMD approach provides an effective mixing criterion, it remains to be understood whether it is fundamentally equivalent—or perhaps only complementary—to the irreducibility-based framework used in
[
51
]
. Clarifying this relationship would not only unify distinct perspectives on quantum mixing but may also shed new light on the design of mixing criteria suitable for broader classes of quantum systems.

Another avenue for future research lies in the extension of the qMD mixing framework to multidimensional and networked settings, such as quantum processes evolving on graphs, trees, or higher-dimensional lattices. In such settings, the notion of spatial inhomogeneity becomes even more pronounced, and tools from quantum Markov fields and operator algebras may be required to capture the subtleties of local versus global mixing behavior. The development of a coherent ergodic theory for quantum processes on infinite-dimensional von Neumann algebras—extending the current finite-dimensional setting—would also be a substantial contribution to the mathematical foundations of quantum dynamics.

Furthermore, there is growing interest in incorporating randomness into quantum channel dynamics. A random quantum channel formulation of the qMD condition could offer new probabilistic tools to study average-case and almost-sure ergodic properties in fluctuating environments. Such a perspective aligns naturally with recent advances in the study of random matrix product states
[
25
]
. These connections underscore the practical importance of developing robust criteria for mixing and stability that remain valid under realistic conditions involving noise, randomness, and partial controllability.

In summary, this work contributes to the understanding of mixing in time-inhomogeneous quantum systems and offers a versatile approach that invites both theoretical development and applied exploration in quantum information science.

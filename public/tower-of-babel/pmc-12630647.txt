Polygenic risk score and cluster-based analysis suggests links between type 2 diabetes and vascular dementia in the KARE study
Authors: Wanqing Dong, Zijian Tian, Jie Bai, Benrui Wu, Mengjie Zhao, Yingyu Zhang, Jian Shao, Tian Xie, Qian Li, Peng Wu, Jingnan Xue, Ying Chen, Xiaozhou Zhou, Yuxuan Du, Shiteng Gao, Haonan Pan, Anjie Peng, Ying Pan, Kaixin Zhou, Hongwei Jiang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractType 2 diabetes is an established risk factor for dementia. However, how its genetic heterogeneity affects different dementia subtypes remains unclear. In this study, we investigate the associations between genetic risk of type 2 diabetes and dementia subtypes among 33,136 older Chinese adults from the KARE cohort. We find that a higher overall polygenic risk score for type 2 diabetes is significantly associated with an increased risk of vascular dementia, but not Alzheimer’s disease. Further analyses using cluster-specific partitioned polygenic score show that elevated genetic risk specific to the hyperinsulinemia pathway is strongly associated with increased incidence of vascular dementia. These findings highlight the potential role of insulin-related metabolic abnormalities in the pathogenesis of vascular dementia and provide genetic evidence to support the use of the hyperinsulinaemia pathway as a clinically relevant marker for early risk stratification and precision prevention strategies.Subject terms: Disease genetics, Population genetics, Genome informatics

Introduction

Dementia affecting over 50 million individuals globally, with nearly 10 million new cases reported annually1,2. This devastating condition, primarily encompassing Alzheimer’s disease (AD), vascular dementia (VaD) and other/unspecified dementia, is influenced by a complex interplay of genetic and environmental factors3. The immense burden of dementia is placing significant economic strain on families and societies1,2. Understanding the causes and mechanisms of dementia is therefore crucial for developing effective strategies for prevention and treatment2,3.

Type 2 diabetes (T2D) has been established as a major risk factor for dementia, including AD and VaD3. A meta-analysis of 144 studies has shown that individuals with T2D face 1.25–1.91 times the risk of developing dementia compared to those without diabetes4. While previous research has largely focused on phenotypic indicators of T2D (e.g. blood glucose, glycosylated haemoglobin) in relation to dementia5, investigations into the impact of T2D genetic susceptibility on dementia have been sparse6.

The polygenic risk score (PRS) for T2D has been examined as a predictor of dementia risk, yielding inconsistent results across studies6–15. While some studies suggested higher T2D PRS correlated with an increased dementia risk6,8, others failed to replicate the association9,11,15. When it came to subtype-specific impact on different forms of dementia, the picture gets more elusive. A few studies reported that genetic predisposition primarily elevates the risk of VaD, rather than AD6,8. However, other investigations showed that T2D PRS was associated not only with VaD but also with AD7,14. These discrepancies likely stem from not only the heterogeneity in dementia pathophysiology, but also variations in study design, analytical methods, study populations and the method to construct the PRS.

The above-mentioned genetic studies all treated T2D as a homogeneous entity, assessing genetic risk based on all T2D variants to build an overall PRS. In reality, T2D is a heterogeneous condition encompassing multiple pathogenic pathways. Recently a method has been proposed to construct cluster-specific partitioned polygenic score (pPS) by grouping single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) according to their associations with specific metabolic traits16. This method16 utilised data from 37 GWAS involving over 1.4 million multi-ancestry individuals. Twelve biologically interpretable pathways, including insulin deficiency, insulin resistance and abnormalities in lipid metabolism were identified and have been validated in East Asian populations17. These pPS have shown significant associations with clinical features and complications of diabetes among Chinese populations, providing insights into the biological mechanisms underlying T2D heterogeneity. However, whether T2D pPS are associated with dementia risk remains unknown.

In this work, we show that this study leverages a large prospective cohort of older Chinese adults to comprehensively assess the associations between T2D genetic susceptibility and dementia. Genetic investigations are conducted at multiple levels of the overall PRS, specific pPS and individual variants. Dementia is categorised into its major subtypes (AD and VaD) to accommodate its clinical heterogeneity. Our endeavours aim to provide further insights into the underlying biological mechanisms of dementia and contribute to the formulation of targeted prevention and intervention strategies within the realm of precision medicine.

Results

Population characteristics

A total of 33,136 individuals who passed the quality control procedures were included in this study, comprising 14,968 men and 18,168 women. After stratification according to tertiles of the T2D PRS, the baseline characteristics were summarised in Table 1. Participants in the high PRS tertile presented distinct metabolic profiles, characterised by a lower mean age (−0.54 years), a higher proportion of female participants (+1.92%), elevated fasting glucose (+0.60 mmol/L), as well as increased prevalence of coronary heart disease (+1.58%) and stroke (+2.04%) (Table 1). In contrast, sociodemographic factors such as education level, marital status and place of residence, as well as lifestyle behaviours like smoking and alcohol consumption, showed minimal variation among different PRS groups.

Association between partitioned T2D PRS and risk of VaD

Of the 12 T2D pathway-specific pPSs proposed by Smith et al. 16, only the Hyper Insulin pPS was significantly associated with risk of VaD after Bonferroni correction (HR = 1.220, 95% CI: 1.099–1.354, Table 3). To further explore the association between the hyperinsulin pPS and risk of VaD, we stratified participants into tertiles based on the pPS. Using the lowest tertile as the reference group, individuals in the medium tertile had a significantly higher risk of VaD (HR = 1.366, 95% CI: 1.042–1.792), and those in the high tertile had an even higher risk (HR = 1.553, 95% CI: 1.193–2.020) (Supplementary Data 1). All pPSs were associated with an increased risk of T2D, although Bilirubin, SHBG-LpA and cholesterol did not reach Bonferroni-adjusted statistical significance, potentially due to the limited number of variants comprising these pPS (2, 3 and 4 variants, respectively, the fewest among all 12 pPSs in Supplementary Table 2).

Association between T2D-related variants and risk of VaD

To further investigate the variants driving the association between T2D pPS and risk of VaD, we tested each variant composing the one pPS significantly associated with risk of VaD. Among the 39 variants in the Hyper Insulin cluster, rs11129735 (TRANK1) and rs329118 (JADE2) showed nominal significance (Supplementary Table 3). After adjusting for all covariates, individuals carrying the AA genotype at rs11129735 (TRANK1) exhibited a significantly increased risk of VaD compared to those with the GG genotype (HR = 1.447, 95% CI: 1.070–1.957). Similarly, carriers of the TT genotype at rs329118 (JADE2) showed a significantly higher risk compared to individuals with the CC genotype (HR = 1.406, 95% CI: 1.050–1.883) (Supplementary Data 2).

Sensitivity analysis

After excluding APOE variant (rs429358), the pattern of associations between T2D PRS and risk of dementia and its subtypes remained unchanged (Supplementary Data 3). In sex-stratified analyses, the association trends remained consistent (Supplementary Table 4). No significant sex-specific effects were observed. Our primary results remained unchanged after adjusting for potential confounders (Tables 2, 3, Supplementary Table 4 and Supplementary Data 1–3).

Discussion

This study, utilising the prospective KARE cohort in China (n = 33,136), found a positive association between the overall T2D PRS and VaD risk. No significant associations were observed with AD or other/unspecified dementia, suggesting that the genetic burden of T2D has subtype-specific effects on VaD risk. Further analysis employing pPS revealed that, among the 12 T2D genetic clusters, only the hyperinsulinemia-specific pPS was associated with VaD risk. This association suggests that hyperinsulinemia may represent a biologically plausible pathway linking T2D genetic heterogeneity to VaD, with potential clinical implications for early identification of high-risk individuals.

Previous studies investigating the association between T2D PRS and dementia-subtype risk have yielded inconsistent results, partly attributable to the use of broad or composite outcomes that did not clearly distinguish between AD and VaD10,11. Jeong et al.10, for example, adopted a composite category termed Alzheimer’s disease and related dementia and did not report separate analyses for VaD. Similarly, a UK birth cohort study also utilised broad cognitive outcomes without specifically distinguishing between AD and VaD15. In contrast, we analysed VaD and AD separately, thereby avoiding signal dilution and appropriately adjusting for primary dementia risk factors.

Additionally, the limited cohort size and short follow-up may partly explain previous inconsistent findings. Chung et al.9 performed a cross-sectional analysis of 32 T2D GWAS loci among 400 AD cases, reporting no convincing associations. Similarly, James et al.15 followed 1780 participants longitudinally and observed no significant association between T2D genetic risk and cognitive outcomes, likely due to insufficient statistical power. To address these limitations, we leveraged a large prospective cohort of 33,136 older adults from southern China (KARE cohort), providing more than 6 years of follow-up.

Furthermore, previous research has predominantly involved European populations, with very scarce data from East Asians who possess distinct genetic and environmental backgrounds. Genetic heterogeneity across populations, illustrated by the MVP study6, which found significant associations between T2D genetic risk and VaD in Europeans but not in Africans or Hispanics, highlights the need for validation across diverse ethnicities. To address this gap, we constructed our PRS using the largest multi-ancestry T2D GWAS meta-analysis to date (over 2.5 million individuals, approximately 20% East Asian ancestry)18, comprehensively capturing genetic variations relevant to T2D across diverse populations, including 1289 variants available in our cohort. Our findings revealed a significant positive association between T2D PRS and VaD risk, but not with AD. Consistent with previous European studies (e.g. Swedish8 and MVP cohorts6), our findings offer preliminary evidence supporting this genetic association in East Asians, and may help guide future ancestry-informed studies.

T2D is a highly heterogeneous metabolic disorder, and individuals with different underlying genetic mechanisms may have distinct risks of developing VaD. Clarifying which specific mechanisms play a dominant role is essential and could also help explain inconsistent findings in previous studies. To better identify the genetic contributions of these distinct mechanisms, we applied pPS derived from validated T2D genetic clusters16,17. Our findings demonstrated that genetic risk for VaD predominantly aligns with the hyperinsulinemia-specific pPS, suggesting added mechanistic resolution beyond overall PRS approaches. Specifically, individuals with a higher hyperinsulinemia genetic risk exhibited an elevated risk of VaD.

The association between hyperinsulinemia and VaD risk has been insufficiently studied. Previously a small cross-sectional study19 reported higher insulin levels among VaD patients. However, a single insulin measurement is prone to transient influences (e.g. diet, stress, medication)19–24, limiting mechanistic insights and reproducibility25. In contrast, the genetic approach adopted in this study provides a measure of lifelong stable exposure in a large cohort26. Our findings demonstrate a significant positive relationship between hyperinsulinemia-specific pPS and VaD risk, even after comprehensive adjustment for multiple confounders, confirming the robust association between hyperinsulinemia and VaD risk.

Our genetic findings are further supported by complementary experimental evidence. Animal studies indicate that chronic hyperinsulinemia induces cerebral microvascular abnormalities27, endothelial dysfunction and blood–brain barrier impairment, all characteristic of small-vessel disease in VaD28,29. Mechanistically, excess insulin suppresses endothelial nitric oxide synthesis via the PI3K–Akt–eNOS pathway30,31, thereby impairing cerebrovascular dilation and protective vascular functions. Elevated insulin also provokes systemic and cerebral inflammation32,33, activates the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS)34, increases microvascular load and promotes thrombosis through MAPK-mediated upregulation of plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1)35. Collectively, these lines of evidence reinforce hyperinsulinemia as a biologically plausible contributor to VaD pathogenesis and lend support to our pPS-based genetic association.

Given the current lack of available treatments for VaD, our genetic findings may have clinical relevance, particularly by enabling early identification of individuals at risk through genetically predicted hyperinsulinemia, which may serve as a marker for future VaD risk and support timely preventive strategies. While the hyperinsulinemia-specific pPS comprises 39 variants, individual variant-level association analyses revealed only nominal associations for two SNPs (TRANK1 rs11129735 and JADE2 rs329118), neither of which has a clearly established neurocognitive role in VaD. Variants within or near GLP1R and PDE3A are also part of this hyperinsulinemia genetic cluster. The variant rs10305420 is a missense variant of GLP1R (Pro7Leu) located in the signal peptide region, which is essential for receptor synthesis and trafficking, potentially impacting receptor function36. Evidence from the REWIND trial37, a meta-analysis of 26 randomised controlled trials38 and animal studies39 suggests that glucose-lowering GLP-1 receptor agonists could reduce cognitive impairment. Additionally, rs7134150 is an intronic eQTL variant of PDE3A, significantly affecting the expression of PDE3A in adipose tissue40. PDE3A encodes phosphodiesterase-3A, involved in the cAMP signaling pathway. PDE3 inhibitors, such as cilostazol, are widely used for secondary stroke prevention41 and may protect cerebral white matter and improve cognitive function, as indicated by animal studies42. Therefore, our genetic findings provide a rationale to explore whether these agents effectively reduce VaD risk in individuals genetically predisposed to hyperinsulinemia. However, due to limited medication usage and VaD events in our cohort, we could not directly assess their real-world efficacy. Future large-scale clinical trials with genetic risk stratification will be important to validate these findings.

This study also has several limitations. First, despite careful verification of dementia diagnoses, clinical overlap and potential misdiagnoses between VaD and AD could still influence the results. Future studies incorporating imaging assessments and biomarkers are needed for further validation. Second, the construction of pPS relies on current understanding of T2D genetic mechanisms, and as emerging genes and pathways are identified, the stability of these scores may change. While the PRS used in this study was derived from a multi-ancestry GWAS, our findings provide supportive evidence for this genetic association in East Asians. Further validation in larger and more diverse East Asian populations will be valuable in future research. Third, we assumed independence among different genetic pathways and did not account for possible biological interactions, which could be explored and validated through functional experiments in future research. Fourth, the study participants were exclusively of East Asian ancestry, which limits the generalisability of these findings, and further validation in other populations is necessary. Moreover, as the KARE cohort recruited participants through annual health examinations, selection bias favouring healthier or more health-conscious individuals may be present. This pattern is commonly observed in volunteer-based cohorts. However, such bias is unlikely to substantially affect the relative association estimates in this mechanism-oriented analysis. Finally, this study was based on observational associations, which preclude determination of causal directions, and future research employing extended follow-up and methods such as Mendelian randomisation will be valuable to further explore these relationships.

In summary, this prospective study, based on 33,136 older Chinese adults, identified a significant positive association between the overall genetic risk of T2D and VaD. Moreover, our analysis employing pPS demonstrated that genetic risk specifically linked to the hyperinsulinemia pathway significantly increases VaD risk. These results highlight the role of T2D genetic heterogeneity in influencing dementia subtypes through distinct pathogenic mechanisms, and suggest that the hyperinsulinemia pathway may represent a potential metabolic contributor to VaD pathogenesis, while also serving as a clinically relevant marker for early identification of individuals at elevated risk, thereby supporting timely and targeted prevention.

Methods

Study population

This study was conducted in the Kunshan Aging Research with E-health (KARE) Cohort, a population-based, bidirectional study established in Kunshan, China43. Participants were enroled through annual health examinations for older adults, and comprehensive data were collected through the local public health system, including detailed health records and medical histories (Supplementary Note 1). Residual blood samples from annual health examinations were gathered for genotyping with written informed consent. Given that dementia mainly affects older adults44, this study included individuals aged 50 or above as of January 1, 2018. Follow-up endpoints were defined as the first dementia diagnosis, death, or the date of the last available record prior to the study’s conclusion on August 24, 2024.

Definition of dementia

Dementia cases were identified by integrating multiple electronic medical record (EMR) sources, including inpatient and outpatient records, chronic disease follow-up data, prescription information and death registrations. Dementia was classified according to ICD-10 codes for Alzheimer’s disease (AD: F00, G30), vascular dementia (VaD: F01) and other/unspecified types of dementia (F02, F03), with consideration given to the use of dementia-specific medications (e.g. cholinesterase inhibitors or memantine)45,46. The date of the first dementia-related record was defined as the date of onset. To improve diagnostic accuracy, medical history, physical examination findings and imaging reports were reviewed, and diagnoses were confirmed by a neurologist45,46. The primary outcome was all-cause dementia and its subtypes, including AD and VaD (Supplementary Table 5). The inclusion and exclusion criteria are detailed in Supplementary Fig. 1.

Genotype data processing

Genotyping, imputation and quality control were performed as follows. Participants from the KARE cohort were genotyped using the CAS Array, designed for large-scale biobanking in the Chinese population47. Variants were excluded if they exhibited a call rate below 0.98, a minor allele frequency (MAF) below 0.05, or a Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (HWE) p value below 1 × 10⁻⁶. Samples with a call rate <0.9 or sex mismatches were also removed. Samples that were probably contaminated (identified by excess heterozygosity) or duplicated (PIE-HAT > 0.9 in identical-by-descent analysis) were also removed. We also performed principal component analysis (PCA) using a pruned set of independent variants to assess the genetic ancestry. No significant population outliers were detected according to the plot of top principal components. To control any residual population stratification, the first five principal components (PC1–PC5) were retained for adjustment in subsequent analyses. After these quality control procedures, genotypes were phased using SHAPEIT 4.2.248 and imputed with Beagle 5.449, using the high-coverage reference panel from the 1000 Genomes Project (1KGP)50. Variants with an INFO score <0.3 were considered low quality and excluded.

Polygenic risk score calculation

The polygenic risk score (PRS) for T2D was calculated using the R package bigsnpr51 following the classical clumping and thresholding (C + T) method52 based on a large-scale meta-analysis of T2D GWAS involving 2,535,601 individuals18. Overlapping variants between the GWAS summary statistics and the imputed genotype data were retained. Using the genotype data from the KARE cohort, clumping was performed using an r2 threshold of 0.2 and a 500 kb window. Variants with a p value < 5 × 10⁻8 were then selected. Using the corresponding beta coefficients from the GWAS summary statistics, the PRS for each individual was calculated as the weighted sum of the allelic dosages of the selected variants. The PRS was standardised by subtracting its mean and dividing by its standard deviation to ensure comparability. In total, 1289 variants were retained for the T2D PRS calculation. The variants and their weights are summarised in Supplementary Data 4.

Twelve pPS were also calculated using the same package, based on the weights reported by Smith et al.16. In their study, T2D-associated GWAS signals were clustered into 12 trait-specific components, i.e. Beta Cell 1, Beta Cell 2, Proinsulin, ALP Negative, Obesity, Hyper Insulin, Cholesterol, Lipodystrophy 1, Lipodystrophy 2, Liver-Lipid, Bilirubin, SHBG-LpA. The clustering threshold was set at 0.7802 for optimal signal-to-noise ratio, as originally proposed by Kim et al.53. The final variant sets and their weights used for pPS calculation are summarised in Supplementary Data 5, and detailed descriptions of each genetic cluster, including the number of SNPs used in our study compared to the original report, are provided in Supplementary Data 6.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation. Categorical variables were expressed as counts (percentages). To enhance interpretability, all T2D PRSs were re-scaled using z-score standardisation. Logistic regression models were employed to examine the association between T2D PRS and T2D status, with PRS analysed both continuously and in tertiles. Additionally, associations between the 12 pPS and T2D were evaluated using logistic regression. Cox proportional hazards models were utilised to examine associations of the T2D PRS and pPS with risk of dementia and its subtypes. For the Hyper Insulin pPS, initially identified as significantly associated with VaD, further tertile-stratified Cox analyses were conducted to confirm robustness. All logistic and Cox models were adjusted for age, sex and the first five genetic principal components to account for population stratification. Proportional hazards assumptions for Cox models were assessed using Grambsch and Therneau’s test based on scaled Schoenfeld residuals, and no violations were detected. Bonferroni correction was applied to analyses involving the 12 pPS, setting the significance threshold at p < 0.004 (0.05/12).

We further explored individual variants within the pPS to identify potential drivers of the observed associations with VaD using generalised linear mixed models implemented in GCTA (v1.94.1)54, adjusted for age, sex and the first five genetic principal components. Of the 41 loci originally reported by Smith et al.16, two were excluded due to low minor allele frequencies in the Chinese population, leaving 39 variants for subsequent analyses. Multiple testing correction was applied, establishing a significance threshold of p < 0.00128 (0.05/39) and nominal significance at p < 0.05. Subsequently, Cox proportional hazards models using a genotypic (codominant) model were applied to further assess the associations of variants showing nominal significance (raw P < 0.05) with risk of VaD, adjusting for the covariates described previously. All statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.4.2. Two-sided tests were used throughout, with statistical significance defined as p < 0.05 unless otherwise specified.

Sensitivity analysis

Several sensitivity analyses were conducted to evaluate the robustness of the observed associations. First, the T2D PRS was recalculated after excluding the APOE variant (rs429358) to determine if observed associations were primarily driven by the established APOE-dementia relationship6. Second, analyses were stratified by sex, which was determined from electronic medical records, to identify potential sex-specific effects. Lastly, we employed three progressively adjusted models: Model 1 was adjusted for age, sex, and the first five genetic principal components. Model 2 included all covariates from Model 1 and further adjusted for T2D status. Model 3 further comprehensively adjusted for potential confounders3, including education level, marital status, residential location, behavioral factors (smoking and alcohol consumption), risk biomarkers (LDL-C, HDL-C, total cholesterol, triglycerides, blood pressure, BMI, fasting blood glucose), and comorbidities (coronary artery disease, cerebrovascular disease, sleep disorders, depression). Definitions of demographic characteristics, behavioral factors, risk biomarkers, and comorbidities are detailed in Supplementary Table 6.

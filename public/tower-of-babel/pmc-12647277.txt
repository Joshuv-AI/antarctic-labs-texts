Transperitoneal versus retroperitoneal laparoscopic nephroureterectomy: a meta-analysis of technical approaches for upper tract urothelial carcinoma
Authors: Dan Li, Zhuowei Ruan, Feng Lin, Gang Wang, Jiadong Xu, Tianbo Luo, Tianqi Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveIt was to compare perioperative outcomes between transperitoneal (TLNU) and retroperitoneal (RLNU) approaches for laparoscopic nephroureterectomy in UTUC.MethodsLiterature pertaining to “TLNU”, “RLNU”, and “upper-tract urothelial carcinoma (UTUC)” was systematically retrieved from databases, covering the period from January 2000 to July 2024. Quality and risk of bias were assessed utilizing Cochrane Handbook, and extracted basic information and outcome data. Meta-analysis (MA) was conducted utilizing Review Manager 5.3.ResultsThe study included a total of 11 articles. The analysis revealed no significant differences between the RLNU and TLNU groups in terms of surgical duration (mean difference (MD) = 2.83, 95% confidence interval (CI): -29.40 to 35.07, Z = 0.17, P = 0.86 > 0.05), time to bowel function recovery (MD = -0.28, 95% CI: -0.81 to 0.25, Z = 1.03, P = 0.30 > 0.05), length of hospital stay (MD = 0.70, 95% CI: -0.51 to 1.91, Z = 1.14, P = 0.26 > 0.05), incidence of complications (MD = 1.02, 95% CI: 0.43 to 2.41, Z = 0.05, P = 0.96 > 0.05), recurrence rate (MD = 1.25, 95% CI: 0.94 to 1.66, Z = 1.55, P = 0.12 > 0.05), and overall survival (OS) rate (MD = 1.07, 95% CI: 0.98 to 1.18, Z = 1.49, P = 0.14 > 0.05). However, a significant difference was observed in intraoperative blood loss (BL) between the RLNU and TLNU groups (MD = 6.78, 95% CI: 2.44 to 11.13, Z = 3.06, P = 0.002 < 0.05).ConclusionPerioperative outcomes between RLNU and TLNU show significant differences in operative time, bowel recovery, hospital stay, complications, recurrence, and OS. However, RLNU had significantly higher intraoperative blood loss (P < 0.05), though its clinical relevance remains uncertain. This analysis focused on perioperative metrics; long-term oncologic efficacy requires further investigation.

Background

Upper urinary tract urothelial carcinoma (UTUC), although relatively rare compared to bladder cancer, poses a significant threat to patients’ quality of life and life expectancy due to its high degree of insidiousness and rapid progression [1]. With the rapid advancement of surgical techniques, laparoscopic surgery has emerged as a pivotal milestone in the treatment of UTUC, offering patients a less invasive and quicker recovery surgical option [2]. Within the scope of laparoscopic surgery, transperitoneal laparoscopic nephroureterectomy (TLNU) and retroperitoneal LNU (RLNU) have become the two mainstream procedures widely applied in clinical practice.

TLNU, which involves entry through the peritoneum, facilitates the management of complex intra-abdominal structures, particularly in cases involving abdominal tumors or requiring lymph node dissection, providing a more direct approach [3]. However, this approach may increase surgical trauma and blood loss (BL). In contrast, RLNU accesses via the retroperitoneal space, theoretically avoiding direct contact with the intestines, reducing intraoperative intestinal handling and resulting in less BL. Yet, its limited operative space may increase surgical difficulty and prolong operation time (OT). Despite theoretical advantages and disadvantages of TLNU and RLNU, there is no unified conclusion regarding their comparative safety, efficacy, and patient prognosis in clinical practice. Previous studies have largely focused on single indicators (such as surgical duration or complications), lacking a comprehensive quantitative integration of perioperative outcomes [4]. Furthermore, most studies are single-center, small-sample research, with some including cases from early-stage, less mature technologies, which may confound the interpretation of results and limit the generalizability of conclusions [5]. To address these gaps, the present study made an innovative contribution by integrating six major indicators for the first time: surgical efficiency (OT), intraoperative safety (BL), postoperative recovery (bowel function, length of hospital stay), complications, and oncological prognosis (recurrence rate, overall survival (OS)). This comprehensive approach enables a precise analysis of key contentious issues, particularly the verification of the incremental bleeding risk associated with RLNU and its clinical significance based on pooled data. The study also strictly controlled for the inclusion of literature based on publication year and quality (Cochrane bias assessment), ensuring that the conclusions reflect the true capabilities of modern laparoscopic techniques.

Therefore, this work employed meta-analysis (MA) to systematically analyze the efficacy of TLNU and RLNU in the treatment of upper UTUC, aiming to provide reference data for clinical treatment of UTUC.

Methods

Search strategy

A computerized search of relevant literature from January 2000 to July 2024 in PubMed, Medline, Embase, and Web of Science was conducted. A combination of free-text and controlled vocabulary (MeSH terms) was adopted. Search terms included “UTUC,” “upper urinary tract bladder-like cancer,” “renal pelvis and ureter urothelial carcinoma,” “upper urinary tract urothelial tumor,” “upper UTUC,” along with “transperitoneal laparoscopic nephroureterectomy,” “retroperitoneal laparoscopic nephroureterectomy,” “open laparoscopic nephroureterectomy,” “post-peritoneal cavity laparoscopic nephroureterectomy,” “TLNU,” “RLNU,” and “laparoscopic nephroureterectomy”. No language restrictions were applied.

Criteria

Inclusion criteria: studies eligible for inclusion were randomized controlled trials involving patients with upper UTUC. The intervention of interest was TLNU, with RLNU used as a control. Studies included in the analysis must report baseline patient characteristics, including clinical tumor stage (cTNM), lymph node metastasis status, degree of hydronephrosis (mild/moderate/severe), BMI, and whether cases with distant metastasis were excluded. The study must also clearly define the eligibility criteria for the two surgical approaches (e.g., tumor size, location) and the indications for conversion to open surgery (e.g., severe adhesions, intraoperative complications).

Exclusion criteria: case reports, reviews, treatment experience reports, conference abstracts, in vitro or basic research studies, studies with incomplete clinical data, unclear outcome measures, studies lacking specific treatment protocols, and duplicate publications.

Screening and data extraction

Independently, 2 researchers conducted literature search, screening, and full-text assessment regarding PRISMA guidelines. From eligible studies, they independently extracted key data into a predefined Excel spreadsheet to ensure systematic and consistent data management. The extracted data encompassed several critical elements, including publication year, first author of the paper, study design type, description of study participants and inclusion criteria, details of interventions and implementation periods, baseline characteristics of study participants, and a series of outcome measures. The outcome measures included surgical duration, intraoperative BL, time to bowel function recovery, length of hospital stay, incidence of complications, recurrence rate, and OS. Surgical duration was defined as the time from incision to the completion of suturing, excluding anesthesia and positioning time. Bowel recovery time was based on the time to first flatus or bowel movement. A BL difference greater than 200 mL was considered clinically significant.

In data processing, continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation to reflect both central tendency and variability of the data. Categorical variables were reported as frequencies to visually display the occurrence rates of events. In cases where discrepancies arose between the two researchers during data extraction or literature assessment, consensus was reached through discussion. A third independent researcher was consulted to arbitrate if must, ensuring the accuracy and fairness of results.

Quality assessment

Study quality and risk of bias were assessed via Cochrane Risk of Bias assessment tool [6]. Assessment criteria included random sequence generation (selection bias), allocation concealment (selection bias), participants and personnel blinding (performance bias), outcome assessment blinding (detection bias), incomplete outcome data (attrition bias), selective reporting (reporting bias), and other bias. Each bias domain was graded as “Low risk,” “Unclear risk,” or “High risk” based on predefined criteria.

Statistical analysis

All outcome measures were analyzed independently. Review Manager 5.3 implemented MA. Heterogeneity among included papers was initially assessed using the I2 statistic. When I2 was less than 50%, implying low heterogeneity, fixed-effect model (FEM) was employed. When I2 was 50% or higher, implying substantial heterogeneity, a random-effects model (REM) was chosen for pooled analysis. Potential sources of heterogeneity were explored, and subgroup analysis or sensitivity analysis was conducted for correction. Results of the MA were presented using mean difference (MD), standardized MD (SMD), or weighted MD (WMD) with 95%CI for continuous variables, and odds ratio (OR), risk ratio (RR), or risk difference (RD) with 95%CI for categorical variables. α = 0.05 was used as significance level, where P < 0.05 indicated statistical significance of differences. Risk of bias across studies was assessed using funnel plots.

Certainty of evidence assessment

The certainty of evidence for each outcome was assessed using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE) framework. Evidence from randomized trials started as high quality but was downgraded based on risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias. Results were categorized into four levels: high, moderate, low, or very low certainty.

A total of 436 articles were retrieved. After a preliminary screening of titles and abstracts, 414 articles were excluded due to being case reports, reviews, duplicates, irrelevant studies, or failing to meet other inclusion criteria. Twenty-two articles proceeded to the next stage of full-text screening. After full-text evaluation, 11 articles were excluded due to being basic research, conference abstracts, unavailable full texts, unclear outcome measures, undefined treatment protocols, single-arm studies, or studies involving combination therapies. Discussion

This study performed a MA to compare the perioperative outcomes of RLNU versus TLNU in UTUC patients, including a total of 11 studies. The results showed no statistically significant differences between RLNU and TLNU in six key indicators: surgical duration, time to recovery of bowel function, length of hospital stay, complication incidence, recurrence rate, and OS rate (all P >0.05). Both approaches were comparable in terms of surgical efficiency (time), postoperative recovery speed (bowel function, length of hospital stay), and short-term safety (complications). Neither surgical approach demonstrated a superior oncological prognosis: the confidence intervals for recurrence risk (RR = 1.25, 95% CI: 0.94–1.66) and OS rate (HR = 1.07, 95% CI: 0.98–1.18) crossed the null value (1.0), indicating that both techniques had similar tumor control outcomes, consistent with the findings of Liu et al. (2017) [7]. However, the RLNU group exhibited significantly higher intraoperative BL compared to the TLNU group (MD = 6.78 mL, 95% CI: 2.44–11.13, P = 0.002). Potential reasons for this include the limited working space in the retroperitoneum, restricted visualization of major vessels (e.g., renal veins, gonadal vessels), and increased risk of inadvertent injury. RLNU requires the establishment of an operative access in non-natural spaces, which demands greater surgical experience and a learning curve that may affect hemostasis. Although the difference was statistically significant, the clinical relevance of the mean difference in BL should be interpreted with caution. This increase in BL did not translate into a higher transfusion rate (no difference in complications), suggesting that the practical impact may be limited.

The GRADE assessment indicated low-to-moderate certainty for most outcomes, primarily due to high heterogeneity (I2 > 75% for operative time, blood loss, and bowel recovery) and the retrospective design of all included studies. This suggests that non-significant differences (e.g., operative time, hospital stay) should be interpreted with caution, while the significant finding of higher blood loss in RLNU (low certainty) warrants further validation in prospective studies.

The study by Aron et al. (2023) [8] confirmed that TLNU is associated with simplified surgery and shorter operative times for the treatment of UTUC, which is consistent with the findings of this work. Fatima et al. (2023) [9] demonstrated that RLNU minimizes bowel trauma in UTUC treatment and promotes faster postoperative recovery, a viewpoint that slightly contrasts with the current study. This discrepancy may be attributed to differences in study design, sample size, and patient characteristics. The differences between TLNU and RLNU in surgical duration, intraoperative BL, and bowel recovery time highlight their varying advantages in terms of surgical simplicity, BL control, and patient recovery. TLNU, through its direct transperitoneal approach, shows faster OT, allowing surgeons to effectively identify and manage pathological tissue in the more spacious abdominal cavity. TLNU represents a simpler surgical option that requires surgeons to possess exceptional skills and extensive experience to ensure surgical speed and precision, while minimizing unnecessary tissue damage. RLNU, entering via the retroperitoneal route, benefits from a more centralized and controllable vascular distribution in this area. This facilitates surgeons in precise hemostasis during manipulation of the renal arteries and ureters, significantly reducing intraoperative BL [10]. Particularly in managing complex vascular structures, RLNU demonstrates pronounced advantages by providing a gentler and more controlled surgical environment, thereby reducing the occurrence of intraoperative complications [11]. RLNU, through the retroperitoneal approach, avoids direct contact with abdominal organs, theoretically reducing the risk of bowel injury. In contrast, TLNU requires entry into the abdominal cavity, which may increase the risk of bowel manipulation. However, this study found no significant difference in bowel recovery time between the two groups, which may be attributed to the more limited operating space in RLNU and the higher tension required during the procedure. This allows for earlier resumption of diet and daily activities, shortening hospital stays, thereby reducing economic burdens and improving quality of life.

In this study, no significant differences were observed in surgical duration, time to recovery of bowel function, or length of hospital stay between the two surgical approaches, suggesting that both approaches are comparable in terms of surgical time, postoperative bowel function recovery, and hospital stay management. This can be attributed to the limited interference of the surgical approach on the core procedural steps, such as nephrectomy and ureterectomy: TLNU utilizes the peritoneal cavity space, while RLNU leverages the retroperitoneal space. Despite differences in the anatomical pathways, both approaches allow for the completion of standardized surgical steps, which explains the absence of significant differences in time and recovery. There were also no statistically significant differences in complication incidence, recurrence rate, or OS rate, reflecting similar effects of the two approaches on short-term safety (complications) and long-term oncological outcomes (recurrence, survival) in UTUC patients. With the maturation of laparoscopic techniques, both peritoneal and retroperitoneal approaches now enable precise operation and effective clearance, reducing the risk of local injury and tumor residue [12]. The recurrence and survival of UTUC are more closely related to biological characteristics such as tumor stage and grade, with the surgical approach having a limited impact on the natural course of the tumor. Regarding complication incidence and long-term outcomes, our findings slightly differ from those reported by Hashimoto et al. (2023) [13], who suggested that TLNU may be more advantageous in reducing complications. This discrepancy could be attributed to differences in surgeon experience, technical proficiency, and patient characteristics. Intraoperative BL was significantly higher in the RLNU group compared to the TLNU group, which is directly related to the anatomical characteristics of the retroperitoneal space: the retroperitoneal space is relatively narrow, with abundant loose connective tissue, and there is considerable anatomical variation in vascular branching. During dissection, small blood vessels are more easily damaged, and achieving hemostasis is more challenging due to limited visibility compared to the peritoneal cavity. In contrast, TLNU benefits from the natural separation provided by abdominal organs, offering a more spacious operative field where bleeding is easier to identify and manage [14]. In clinical practice, RLNU requires greater familiarity with anatomy and more refined surgical techniques. If not handled properly, the risk of bleeding increases. On the other hand, the abdominal cavity in TLNU provides surgeons with a more intuitive operative interface, facilitating timely hemostasis and thus reducing BL. Current evidence supports the “equivalence” of both approaches in most perioperative outcomes; however, the difference in intraoperative BL suggests that for UTUC patients at high risk of bleeding, TLNU may be more advantageous. If the patient has abdominal adhesions, RLNU may serve as an alternative, demonstrating the clinical value of a “personalized approach” [15].

This study focused on the comparison of traditional laparoscopic surgical approaches; however, recent rapid advancements in robotic-assisted laparoscopic technology have provided new options for UTUC surgery. Robotic systems, with their three-dimensional high-definition magnified views (10–15 times) and 540° free-rotation biomimetic robotic arms [16], have significantly improved surgical precision in confined anatomical spaces. The robotic platform has also facilitated innovations in retroperitoneal approach positioning. The fully retroperitoneal approach, which eliminates the need for intraoperative repositioning, has been applied to nephroureterectomy. In this approach, the patient is placed in a 90° lateral position and the surgical table is tilted by 20°, allowing the robotic arms to complete the full-length dissection of the ureter in the non-natural spaces. Compared to the cumbersome process of changing positions during traditional RLNU, this technique streamlines the steps and may further reduce operative time. The successful implementation of the world’s first remote robotic heart surgery in 2025 (conducted over 286 km with only a 40-millisecond delay) has confirmed the feasibility of remote, precise operations supported by 5G technology. If this technology is applied to UTUC surgeries, it could overcome geographical limitations, allowing patients in resource-poor areas to receive expert-level RLNU or TLNU procedures. This would be particularly beneficial for promoting techniques like the retroperitoneal approach, which rely heavily on the surgeon’s experience.

A limitation of this study is the failure to analyze the pooled effect sizes for OSor recurrence-free survival (RFS) (e.g., HR/RR), nor were we able to aggregate and assess the positive margin rate, a critical prognostic indicator. This is due to the insufficient reporting of survival data in the included studies, which represents a significant methodological limitation of the current work. Additionally, there is technical heterogeneity between the original studies (e.g., methods of handling the ureteral stump), which may impact the generalizability of outcomes such as BL. All included studies were retrospective in design, which carries the risk of unmeasured confounding bias. Moreover, subgroup data for special populations (e.g., patients with cT3-4 stage or renal failure) were lacking, which limits the precision of personalized treatment decisions. Future prospective studies should standardize the reporting of oncological endpoints to address these gaps. Subsequent research efforts should focus on enhancing our understanding of patient-specific factors and surgical nuances to achieve more accurate, personalized treatments. Heterogeneity in outcome measurements (e.g., inconsistent criteria for bowel recovery time, variable follow-up durations for recurrence) may affect comparability. Future studies should standardize these definitions.

Conclusion

This study demonstrated that there are no statistically significant differences between RLNU and TLNU in terms of surgical duration, postoperative bowel function recovery speed, length of hospital stay, complication rates, tumor recurrence rates, and OS. This meta-analysis primarily compared perioperative outcomes, while long-term oncological efficacy (e.g., metastasis rate, cancer-specific survival) was not evaluated due to insufficient data in included studies. However, intraoperative BL in the RLNU group was significantly higher than in the TLNU group, although this difference did not translate into increased transfusion requirements or complications. The perioperative safety and oncological outcomes of RLNU and TLNU were generally comparable. Surgeon experience and individual patient factors (e.g., history of abdominal surgery, risk of adhesions) should be the primary considerations when choosing the surgical approach. Limitations of this study include variability in the quality of the included literature, heterogeneity in study design, and differences in baseline patient characteristics. Future research should focus on expanding sample sizes, evaluating long-term efficacy, performing stratified analyses of patients, and exploring the application of new technologies to deepen our understanding of UTUC treatment and facilitate more precise, personalized medical decision-making.

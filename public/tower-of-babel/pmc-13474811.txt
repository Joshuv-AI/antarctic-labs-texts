Worldwide research trends on left ventricular assist devices for heart failure: a bibliometric analysis
Authors: Guangcheng Sun, Fangfang Xuan, Shaoyi Tao, Jinpeng Xu, Liang Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundWith advancements in medicine, left ventricular assist devices (LVADs) have emerged as a viable treatment option for heart failure (HF). Numerous experts have examined the primary challenges and societal implications associated with the use of LVADs in managing heart failure. This study seeks to analyze the global literature on LVADs for the treatment of heart failure published between 2004 and 2024, aiming to identify key research hotspots and emerging trends within this field.MethodsThis study conducted a bibliometric review of literature on LVADs for the treatment of heart failure from 2004 to 2024. Visual analysis was conducted on a total of 871 publications using CiteSpace (version 5.8) and VOSviewer (version 1.6.15.0).ResultsOver the past two decades, research on LVADs has developed continuously, accompanied by a stable annual growth in publications. The United States contributed the largest number of publications (187 articles), followed by other highly active countries and research networks. Columbia University was the most productive institution, while Rogers Joseph G and Farra David J were the most prolific authors. Keyword analysis indicated that “left ventricular assist device” was the core terminology, reflecting the central focus of global research. Further thematic analysis revealed principal research themes including device optimization, clinical outcomes and complication management, patient selection and prognostic evaluation, and long-term quality of life in supported patients.ConclusionsThe field of left ventricular adjuvant therapy for heart failure has undergone steady development for 20 years and has now begun to show results. The results of this bibliometric analysis provide ideas and references for future researchers to further promote the development of this field.

Introduction

Heart failure is a major cause of illness and death around the globe, and its prevalence is increasing due to aging populations and better survival rates from cardiovascular diseases [1]. Clinicians treating these patients encounter significant challenges, as individuals with heart failure often suffer from severe symptoms, experience a markedly diminished quality of life, and face a heightened risk of short-term hospitalization and mortality [2]. Despite notable progress in both medication and device-based treatments, many patients continue to advance to severe heart failure, where standard therapies frequently fall short in alleviating symptoms or enhancing survival rates. For eligible patients with end-stage heart failure, heart transplantation remains the gold standard treatment. In this scenario, left ventricular assist devices (LVADs) have become an essential treatment option, providing mechanical support for patients with end-stage heart failure. LVAD therapy is primarily utilized in four key clinical scenarios: bridge to transplantation, bridge to recovery, bridge to decision, and destination therapy for patients who are not candidates for heart transplantation. A randomized clinical trial [3] has shown that LVADs outperform drug therapy in managing patients with end-stage heart failure.

LVADs, or left ventricular assist devices, are mechanical pumps that help the left ventricle maintain adequate blood flow, which can relieve symptoms and enhance survival rates for patients with heart issues. Originally designed as a temporary solution to support patients awaiting heart transplants, the technology behind LVADs has advanced significantly over the years. Newer models are more durable, have lower complication rates, and lead to better outcomes for patients [4]. Consequently, LVADs are now frequently used not only as a bridge to transplantation but also as a long-term treatment option for those who suffer from end-stage heart failure, as well as in certain cases where recovery is possible [5]. The introduction of LVADs has had a major positive impact on the lives of patients suffering from severe heart failure, improving both their survival rates and overall quality of life. Nonetheless, there are still challenges to address, including complications related to the devices, such as pump thrombosis, strokes, infections, and right heart failure [6]. Furthermore, the long-term use of LVADs brings psychological and social challenges that require a comprehensive, multidisciplinary approach to patient care [7].

This study aims to provide the latest overview of LVADs treatment for heart failure, with a focus on its evolution, clinical applications, and outcomes. We will also discuss current challenges and future directions, including advances in device technology, patient selection, and post implantation management. By integrating the latest evidence and clinical insights, we contribute to the continuous optimization of LVADs treatment for patients with advanced heart failure.

Method

This investigation aims to gather and analyze studies that provide insights into the efficacy, safety, and outcomes associated with left ventricular assist device (LVAD) therapy.

Study identification and PRISMA flow diagram

Fig. 1Study Flow Chart. TS indicates topic search; and WoSCC indicates Web of Science Core Collection

Study Flow Chart. TS indicates topic search; and WoSCC indicates Web of Science Core Collection

A systematic literature search was performed in the Web of Science Core Collection database. The search timeframe was restricted to the past two decades. Database fields searched included topic, title, abstract, author keywords, and keywords plus. The search strings were constructed using Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) and entry terms related to left ventricular assist devices and heart failure: (“left ventricular assist device” OR “LVAD” OR “mechanical circulatory support”) AND (“heart failure” OR “end-stage heart failure” OR “cardiac failure”).

Inclusion criteria were: (1) studies focused on LVADs for heart failure; (2) original research articles and review articles; (3) publications in English; (4) articles published within the defined 20-year period.

Exclusion criteria were: (1) non-original studies (editorials, letters, comments, case reports, conference abstracts, protocols); (2) studies not related to LVADs or heart failure; (3) non-English publications; (4) duplicate records.

The PRISMA-style flow diagram in Fig. 1 details the study selection process: a total of 871 articles were initially retrieved. After screening by title, abstract, and full text, and applying inclusion and exclusion criteria, 344 articles (original research articles and review articles) were ultimately included for bibliometric analysis.

Extraction and analysis of bibliometric data

Bibliometric data were extracted from each included article, including journal name, journal impact factor, publication year, country, affiliation, author keywords, and cited references.

Data analysis

CiteSpace software, VOSviewer software, and Excel were used to conduct comprehensive analyses of publication trends, country/author/institution contributions, and research hotspots in the field.

Discussion

General information

A bibliometric analysis was conducted on the annual publication volume and citation impact to quantitatively assess the global progression of research on left ventricular assist devices (LVADs) for heart failure from 2004 to 2024. Time-series analysis indicated a fluctuating yet generally upward trajectory, with notable peaks in 2017, 2020, and 2023, coinciding with the publication of landmark clinical trials and the expansion of clinical indications. The annual number of publications remained relatively low between 2004 and 2010, reflecting the nascent stage of continuous-flow device technology development. Since 2011, coinciding with the widespread approval of second-generation devices, research output has steadily increased, indicating heightened global academic and clinical interest.

Bibliometric network analysis confirmed that the United States led in total publications, followed by Germany, Japan, and the United Kingdom. Centrality metrics further illustrated that the United States had the most robust international collaborative network. Developing countries demonstrated significantly lower publication volumes and weaker cross-national collaborations, underscoring global research disparities. The top five most productive institutions—Columbia University, Duke University, Texas Heart Institute, University of Pennsylvania, and Harvard University—were all based in the United States, quantitatively affirming U.S. leadership in device innovation, clinical trials, and long-term outcome research. Institutional collaboration networks were highly concentrated within U.S. academic medical centers, while cross-border partnerships outside North America remained limited. These findings highlight the urgent need to strengthen international collaboration, particularly involving low- and middle-income countries with restricted access to LVADs and limited research capacity.

Among the most prolific authors, Joseph G. Rogers, David J. Farrar, Andrew J. Boyle, Ranjit John, and Emma J. Birks were predominantly affiliated with U.S. institutions, further confirming American dominance in this field. Journal distribution analysis revealed that high-impact cardiology and mechanical circulatory support journals published the majority of studies, reflecting the clinical maturity of LVAD research. Although the United States performs the majority of global LVAD implantations, bibliometric data revealed a significant research gap between developed and developing regions. Promoting multinational data sharing, collaborative clinical research, and technical training is crucial to enhancing global equity in advanced heart failure care.

Hotspots and frontiers

Based on keyword co-occurrence cluster analysis and citation burst detection, which are core bibliometric methods, we have identified the following evidence-based hotspots and frontiers directly derived from bibliometric data. Left ventricular assist devices (LVADs) represent a promising and cutting-edge therapeutic approach in the field of heart failure management. Serving as a bridge to transplantation, LVADs constitute a critical treatment modality for patients awaiting transplantation [10, 11]. The landmark 2001 REMATCH Trial marked the first pivotal randomized controlled trial to establish the clinical efficacy of LVAD therapy, evaluating the pulsatile HeartMate XVE in patients with advanced heart failure. This trial demonstrated a 48% reduction in all-cause mortality at one year in patients treated with LVADs compared to those receiving optimal medical therapy alone, thereby establishing the foundational survival benefit of mechanical circulatory support [3]. The evolution of LVAD technology is intricately linked to bibliometric publication trends. First-generation implantable LVADs, characterized by pulsatile flow, were utilized as a bridge to transplantation and contributed to improved survival rates and enhanced daily living activities [12]. The transition from first-generation (pulsatile) to second-generation (continuous flow) devices has yielded numerous clinical advantages, including reduced mechanical failures and minimized device size [13]. The advent of third-generation devices, featuring mobile impellers suspended by magnetic and/or hydrodynamic forces, has further enhanced the overall reliability and durability of these systems [14]. Contemporary real-world data have revised earlier statistics: global annual LVAD implantations now exceed 4,000 cases, with 1-year and 2-year survival rates of 85% and 75%, respectively, significantly surpassing previous figures [15]. In addition to making substantial contributions to the care of patients with end-stage heart failure, clinical and basic research on patients with LVADs and their tissues has provided a wealth of insights into the biology of heart failure and the potential for myocardial reverse remodeling [16], remission [17], and recovery [18]. These studies primarily focus on the direct myocardial effects associated with LVADs, including mechanical and metabolic ventricular unloading. They also explore the secondary beneficial myocardial effects linked to LVADs, such as supporting and improving end-organ perfusion, as well as inhibiting systemic inflammation and neurohormonal system activation. With the widespread adoption of LVADs, postoperative complications have emerged as a central focus of bibliometric research. Right ventricular failure represents a significant concern following LVAD implantation and is associated with a 22%–25% reduction in perioperative survival rates, revised from the previous estimate of 20% [19]. A prospective randomized trial confirmed that a dedicated right ventricular protection strategy can substantially reduce the incidence of post-LVAD right ventricular dysfunction and enhance early survival [20]. The gastrointestinal tract is the most common site of bleeding in LVAD patients, partly due to systemic anticoagulant therapy with warfarin. Updated clinical data indicate that the incidence of gastrointestinal bleeding ranges from 21% to 27%, offering a more precise estimate than the previously reported 23% [21]. Additional related complications include pump thrombosis [22], infection [23], and ventricular arrhythmia [24]. To address these complications, the MOMENTUM 3 trial investigated 294 patients randomized to receive either the HeartMate 3, a fully magnetically suspended centrifugal continuous-flow (CF) LVAD, or the axial-flow HeartMate II [15]. The 5-year final results of the MOMENTUM 3 trial confirmed that the HeartMate 3 group exhibited significantly lower rates of pump thrombosis, disabling stroke, reoperation, and major bleeding compared to the HeartMate II group, with 5-year overall survival reaching 58.4% [25]. Of these patients, 152 received the HeartMate 3, while 142 received the HeartMate II. Furthermore, postoperative management of LVADs is of paramount importance. Limited patient self-management capabilities and a lack of professional care can lead to various complications with long-term device use. Therefore, preoperative training for patients and their families is particularly valuable [26].

Bibliometric analysis identifies two emerging research frontiers characterized by rapid citation bursts: AI-driven risk prediction and novel bioengineered left ventricular assist devices (LVADs). Machine learning models utilizing acoustic harmonic power demonstrate high accuracy in predicting LVAD thrombosis, offering a non-invasive early warning system [27]. Furthermore, artificial intelligence algorithms effectively detect prognostically significant left ventricular dysfunction from echocardiographic data, providing robust support for preoperative risk stratification [28]. In the realm of bioengineering, fully magnetically levitated LVADs, exemplified by the CH-VAD, exhibit exceptional hemocompatibility, achieving a 91.6% one-year survival rate and an extremely low driveline infection rate, thereby presenting a novel therapeutic option for global LVAD therapy [29, 30]. A newly developed weaning protocol for assessing myocardial recovery in HeartMate 3 patients has achieved a high successful explantation rate, marking a new research direction in LVAD studies [31]. Minimally invasive microaxial LVADs, such as the Impella 5.5, demonstrate favorable safety profiles in providing prolonged circulatory support for cardiogenic shock, thereby expanding the scope of mechanical circulatory support applications [32]. SGLT2 inhibitors have demonstrated significant cardiac benefits in LVAD patients, offering a new strategy for optimizing perioperative medical management [33]. Surgical correction of de novo aortic insufficiency following LVAD implantation effectively improves long-term hemodynamic status and clinical outcomes [34]. The temporal distribution of keywords reveals a distinct evolution in research focus: In the earlier period (2004–2010), research was predominantly centered on indications, foundational technologies, and surgical strategies, including heart failure, mechanical circulatory support, transplantation, bridge-to-transplant, destination therapy, and continuous flow. In the subsequent years (2012–2017), the focus shifted towards clinical outcomes, complications, and management, encompassing right heart failure, management strategies, and patient outcomes. In recent years (2018–2024), the emphasis has been on AI-based prediction, bioengineered devices, myocardial recovery, and global real-world evidence. This trajectory underscores the maturation of the LVAD field, transitioning from establishing indications and mechanical support principles to addressing complications, long-term outcomes, perioperative management, and innovative technological advancements, which aligns perfectly with the findings of bibliometric cluster and burst analyses.

Limitation

This study is the first to conduct a bibliometric analysis on the treatment of heart failure with left ventricular assist devices (LVADs) and has several limitations. First, database-specific bias exists because only the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) was searched, which may have excluded relevant literature indexed in other databases. Second, only English-language publications were included, potentially leading to omissions of non-English studies. Third, citation lag bias was present, as recently published articles had insufficient time to be widely cited, which may have weakened the detection of emerging research fronts. Fourth, author name disambiguation issues could not be completely avoided during bibliometric processing, which may have slightly influenced the accuracy of author contribution and collaboration analyses. Fifth, the included articles varied in methodological quality and scientific rigor, which may have affected the reliability of the synthesized findings.

Conclusion

This study collected 871 papers published from 2004 to 2024 to illustrate the research progress, hotspots, and trends of LVADs in treating heart failure. In the past 20 years, this function has undergone significant development, providing a comprehensive understanding of the development of LVADs.

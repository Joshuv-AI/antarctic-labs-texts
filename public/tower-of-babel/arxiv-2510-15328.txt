Flat pseudo-Euclidean Leibniz superalgebras Thanks: S.B. was supported by the grant NYUAD-065
Saïd Benayadi, Sofiane Bouarroudj, Hamza El Ouali

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce pre-Lie and pre-Leibniz superalgebras, which generalize pre-Lie and pre-Leibniz algebras to the super setting. Additionally, we define a Levi-Civita product associated with a symmetric non-degenerate bilinear form on a non-associative superalgebra. This leads to the definition of flat pseudo-Euclidean left Leibniz superalgebras as those whose Levi-Civita product induces a pre-Leibniz structure. We study the structure of flat pseudo-Euclidean left Leibniz superalgebras and provide a characterization theorem. In the second part, we focus on quadratic Leibniz superalgebras and show that such a superalgebra is flat if and only if it is symmetric Leibniz and 2-step nilpotent. We further study the structure of quadratic 2-step nilpotent symmetric Leibniz superalgebras. Finally, we introduce the notion of double extension for flat pseudo-Euclidean (resp. Lie) left Leibniz superalgebras and prove that any flat pseudo-Euclidean non-Lie left Leibniz superalgebra can be obtained by a sequence of double extensions starting from a flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie superalgebra.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
On a Milnor’s problem

A pseudo-Riemannian Lie group is a Lie group
G
equipped with a left-invariant pseudo-Riemannian metric
\mu
. The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=T_{e}G
, endowed with the scalar product
\langle\;,\;\rangle=\mu_{e}
, is called a pseudo-Riemannian Lie algebra. The Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
associated with the pseudo-Riemannian metric
\mu
defines a bilinear product
(u,v)\mapsto u\star v
on
\mathfrak{g}
, called the Levi-Civita product, given by the Koszul’s formula:

2\langle u\star v,w\rangle=\langle[u,v],w\rangle+\langle[w,u],v\rangle+\langle[w,v],u\rangle\quad\text{for all }u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

This product is uniquely determined by the metric and satisfies

u\star v-v\star u=[u,v],\quad\mbox{and}\quad\langle u\star v,w\rangle=-\langle v,u\star w\rangle,\;\text{for all }u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

The curvature of
\mathfrak{g}
at the identity element
e
is given by (where
v\mapsto{\mathrm{L}}_{u}(v):=u\star v
is the left multiplication):

K(u,v):={\mathrm{L}}_{[u,v]}-[{\mathrm{L}}_{u},{\mathrm{L}}_{v}],

for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}
. If the curvature
K
vanishes, then
(G,\mu)
is called a flat pseudo-Riemannian Lie group, and its Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is called a flat pseudo-Riemannian Lie algebra.
The vanishing of the curvature is equivalent to the fact that
\mathfrak{g}
, endowed with the Levi-Civita product, is a left-symmetric algebra, see Definition
2.2.5.  Definition
. Thus, a flat pseudo-Riemannian Lie algebra can be viewed as a left-symmetric algebra equipped with a symmetric non-degenerate bilinear form such that the left multiplications are skew-symmetric with respect to this bilinear form.

In 1976, J. Milnor
[
39
]
characterized flat Euclidean Lie algebras by proving that a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a flat Euclidean Lie algebra if and only if it splits orthogonally as
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{b}\oplus\mathfrak{u}
, where
\mathfrak{u}
is an abelian ideal,
\mathfrak{b}
is an abelian subalgebra, and
\operatorname{ad}_{b}
is skew-symmetric with respect to the inner product for every
b\in\mathfrak{b}
, where
\operatorname{ad}
is the adjoint operator on
\mathfrak{g}
. A. Aubert and A. Medina initiated the study of flat
Lorentzian
nilpotent Lie algebras in
[
5
]
. Later, they introduced a construction method known as the double extension of flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebras, which is an analogue of the quadratic double extension method developed earlier by A. Medina and P. Revoy
[
6
]
. They showed that flat Lorentzian nilpotent Lie algebras can be obtained by this method, and that they are at most 3-step nilpotent. Furthermore, M. Guediri
[
27
]
classified 2-step nilpotent flat Lorentzian Lie groups and proved that their Lie algebras are a trivial extension of the 3-dimensional Heisenberg algebra
H_{3}
, and that the restriction of the metric to
H_{3}
is Lorentzian with a degenerate center. More recently, M. Ait Ben Haddou, M. Boucetta and H. Lebzioui
[
2
]
studied flat Lorentzian Lie algebras. They showed that flat Lorentzian nilpotent Lie algebras with degenerate center are obtained by the double extension method. Additionally, they proved in
[
19
]
that non-unimodular flat Lorentzian nilpotent Lie algebras can be constructed using this method. Later, M. Boucetta and H. Lebzioui
[
20
]
studied flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebras of signature
(2,n-2)
. They showed that every nilpotent flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebra with such a signature can be obtained as a double extension of a nilpotent flat Lorentzian Lie algebra.

1.2.
Leibniz (super)algebras

Leibniz algebras were originally introduced by A. Bloh under the name of “D-algebras” in the mid-1960s (see
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
). In the early 1990s, these algebras were rediscovered and developed by Jean-Louis Loday while studying cohomology groups of Lie algebras (see
[
33
,
34
]
). Leibniz algebras can be viewed as analogues of Lie algebras without the anticommutativity condition. This gives rise to two types of Leibniz algebras: left Leibniz algebras and right Leibniz algebras. The name “left” (resp. “right”) Leibniz algebra comes from the fact that the left (resp. right) multiplication is a derivation. It is worth noting that by adding the anticommutativity condition to a (left or right) Leibniz algebra, a Leibniz algebra turns into a Lie algebra. Therefore, the class of Leibniz algebras is a generalization of the class of Lie algebras. A non-associative algebra is called a symmetric Leibniz algebra if it is both a left and a right Leibniz algebra
[
36
]
. On the other hand, in
[
42
]
, the authors introduced the notion of a pre-Leibniz algebra, originally referred to as a Leibniz-dendriform algebra. A pre-Leibniz algebra naturally induces a Leibniz algebra structure on its underlying vector space.

Leibniz algebras appear nowadays in various fields such as differential geometry, homological algebra, K-theory, classical algebraic topology, noncommutative geometry, and physics. In particular, real left Leibniz algebras are the infinitesimal versions of Lie racks. In 2004, Kinyon
[
29
]
proved that if
(X,e)
is a pointed Lie rack, then
T_{e}X
carries a structure of a left Leibniz algebra.

A. S. Dzhumadil’daev introduced the notion of Leibniz superalgebra in
[
26
]
, which is both a non-anti-supercommutative analogue of Lie superalgebras and an extension of Leibniz superalgebras. Recently, left and right Leibniz superalgebras have been extensively studied (see, for example,
[
3
,
7
,
16
,
24
,
25
,
28
,
31
,
32
,
41
]
).

There is also the notion of Zinbiel superalgebras; see, for instance,
[
18
]
and references therein, but their study is outside the scope of this paper.

1.3.
Double and
T^{*}
-extensions

Quadratic non-associative algebras are non-associative algebras equipped with a pseudo-Euclidean bilinear form
\langle\;,\;\rangle
that is invariant (also called associative). It is well known that the existence of quadratic non-associative (super)algebras is a powerful tool for studying their structure (for examples the Killing form on semisimple Lie algebras, the super-Killing form on certain classes of simple Lie superalgebras, and the Albert form on semisimple Jordan algebras). Quadratic Lie (super)algebras have been studied in
[
4
,
8
,
11
]
and references therin. In
[
12
,
13
]
, the authors studied even quadratic Leibniz superalgebras, and in
[
14
]
, they considered the odd quadratic case. On the other hand, in
[
6
]
, it was shown that a quadratic Lie algebra is flat if and only if it is 2-step nilpotent.
In this paper, we extend this result by proving that a quadratic left Leibniz superalgebra is flat if and only if it is a 2-step nilpotent symmetric Leibniz superalgebra.

In
[
15
]
, M. Bordemann introduced the notion of
T^{*}
-extension for quadratic non-associative algebras. He proved that a non-associative algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
, endowed with a quadratic structure, is either a
T^{*}
-extension (or a non-degenerate ideal of codimension one in a
T^{*}
-extension) of another non-associative algebra
\mathfrak{h}
if and only if
{\mathcal{A}}
contains a totally isotropic ideal of dimension
\left\lfloor\frac{\dim{\mathcal{A}}}{2}\right\rfloor
, where
\left\lfloor\cdot\right\rfloor
denotes the integer part. In the context of even quadratic Leibniz superalgebras, this notion was introduced in
[
12
]
. In that paper, the authors provided a complete description of such quadratic solvable Leibniz superalgebras via the
T^{*}
-extension construction.

1.4.
On the results

In this paper, we introduce the concept of pre-left (resp. pre-right) Leibniz superalgebras
({\mathcal{A}},\star,\circ)
, which generalizes the concept of pre-Leibniz algebras (see Definition
3.1.1.  Definition
). We show that a pre-left (resp. pre-right) Leibniz superalgebra
(\mathcal{A},\star,\circ)
naturally induces a left (resp. right) Leibniz superalgebra structure on the underlying
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector superspace. In particular, if
\star=-\circ
, then the pre-Leibniz superalgebra reduces to a left-symmetric superalgebra, showing that the class of left-symmetric superalgebras is a subclass of pre-Leibniz superalgebras.

We introduce an analogue of the Levi-Civita product in the context of pseudo-Euclidean non-associative superalgebras (see Proposition
3.2.1.  Proposition
). Recall that a superalgebra
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
is called pseudo-Euclidean if it is equipped with a symmetric non-degenerate bilinear form
\langle\;,\;\rangle
. Since left Leibniz superalgebras generalize both Lie superalgebras and Lie algebras, we introduce a notion of curvature that extends the classical curvature concept defined in the Lie algebra setting (see Definition
3.2.2.  Definition
).
If the curvature of a pseudo-Euclidean left Leibniz superalgebra
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
vanishes, then
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is called a flat pseudo-Euclidean left Leibniz superalgebra. This vanishing is equivalent to the fact that
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
, endowed with the Levi-Civita products, is a pre-left Leibniz superalgebra. Moreover, we study flat pseudo-Euclidean left Leibniz superalgebra and we show that every flat pseudo-Euclidean left Leibniz superalgebra is a symmetric Leibniz superalgebra. We provide a characterization of flat pseudo-Euclidean left Leibniz superalgebra (see Theorem
3.2.12.  Theorem
).

Next, in this paper, we study flat even (resp. odd) quadratic Leibniz superalgebras. We show that an even (resp. odd) quadratic Leibniz superalgebra is flat if and only if it is a 2-step nilpotent symmetric Leibniz superalgebra. We also provide a characterization of flat even (resp. odd) quadratic Leibniz superalgebras (see Theorem
4.1.5.  Theorem
). Furthermore, the notion of
T^{*}
-extension of even quadratic Leibniz superalgebras, introduced by S. Benayadi and M. Fahmi in
[
12
]
, is extended by introducing the concept of odd quadratic Leibniz superalgebras. We prove that every even (resp. odd) reduced 2-step nilpotent symmetric quadratic Leibniz superalgebra can be obtained as a
T^{*}
-extension (resp.
\Pi(T^{*})
-extension) of a trivial superalgebra by means a non-degenerate bilinear form. We also provide an explicit description of quadratic 2-step nilpotent symmetric Leibniz superalgebras.

Finally, we introduce the notion of double extension of flat even (resp. odd) pseudo-Euclidean left Leibniz superalgebras, and we describe the particular case of Lie superalgebras within this framework. We prove that every flat even (resp. odd) pseudo-Euclidean non-Lie left Leibniz superalgebra can be obtained by a sequence of double extensions starting from a flat even (resp. odd) pseudo-Euclidean Lie superalgebra.

Backgrounds

Let
\mathbb{K}
be an arbitrary field of characteristic zero. The group of integers modulo
2
is denoted by
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

Let
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded space defined over an arbitray field
{\mathbb{K}}
. The degree of a homogeneous element
v\in V_{\bar{i}}
is denoted by
|v|:=\bar{i}
. The element
v
is called
even
if
v\in V_{\bar{0}}
and
odd
if
v\in V_{\bar{1}}
. Throughout the text, all elements are supposed to be homogeneous unless otherwise stated. A linear map
\varphi:V\rightarrow W
between
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded space is called
even
if
\varphi(V_{\bar{i}})\subset W_{\bar{i}}
and
odd
if
\varphi(V_{\bar{i}})\subset W_{\overline{i}+\overline{1}}
.

Let
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space. We denote by
\Pi
the
change of parity functor

\Pi:V\mapsto\Pi(V)
, where
\Pi(V)
is another copy of
V
such that
\Pi(V)_{\bar{0}}:=V_{\bar{1}};~~\Pi(V)_{\bar{1}}:=V_{\bar{0}}
. Elements of
\Pi(V)
shall be denoted by
\Pi(v),~\forall v\in V
. In fact,
|\Pi(v)|=|v|+{\bar{1}}
for every homogeneous
v\in V
.

Let
U
and
V
be two
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector spaces. Given a morphism
G:U\to V
, we define the morphism

G^{\Pi}:\Pi(U)\to\Pi(V)

defined by
G^{\Pi}(\Pi(u))=\Pi(G(u))
. It is straightforward to verify that this construction makes the assignment

U\mapsto\Pi(U)
into a functor satisfying
\Pi^{2}=\mathrm{id}
.

Moreover, given a morphism
G:U\to V
, one can construct morphisms
\Pi(G):U\to\Pi(V),
defined by
\Pi(G)(u)=\Pi(G(u)),
and

G\Pi:\Pi(U)\to V,
defined by
(G\Pi)(\Pi(u))=G(u).

Clearly, we have the factorization
G^{\Pi}=\Pi(G\Pi)
, see

[
35
]
.

2.1.
Bilinear forms on a superspace

Let
V
and
W
be two superspaces. Let
{\mathcal{B}}\in\text{Bil}(V,W)
be a homogeneous bilinear form. Recall that the Gram matrix
B=(B_{ij})
associated to
\mathcal{B}
is given by the following formula:

B_{ij}=(-1)^{p({\mathcal{B}})p(v_{i})}{\mathcal{B}}(v_{i},w_{j})\text{~~for the basis vectors $v_{i}\in V$ and $w_{j}\in W$.}

This definition allows us to identify a bilinear form
B(V,W)
with an element of
\mathrm{Hom}(V,W^{*})
.
Consider the
upsetting
of bilinear forms

u:\text{Bil}(V,W)\rightarrow\text{Bil}(W,V)
given by the formula

u({\mathcal{B}})(w,v)=(-1)^{p(v)p(w)}{\mathcal{B}}(v,w)\text{~~for any $v\in V$ and $w\in W$.}

In terms of the Gram matrix
B
of
{\mathcal{B}}
, we have

u(B)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}R^{t}&(-1)^{p(B)}T^{t}\\
(-1)^{p(B)}S^{t}&-U^{t}\end{array}\right),\text{ for $B=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}R&S\\
T&U\end{array}\right)$}.

Suppose now that
V=W
. Following
[
30
]
, we say that:

(i)

the form

\mathcal{B}
is
symmetric
if and only if
u(B)=B
;

(ii)

the form
\mathcal{B}
is
anti-symmetric
if and only if
u(B)=-B
.

(iii)

the form
\mathcal{B}
is non-degenerate if
u\in V
satisfies
\mathcal{B}(v,u)=0,
for any
v\in V
, then
u=0
,

(iv)

the form
\mathcal{B}
is even if
\mathcal{B}(V_{\bar{0}},V_{\bar{1}})=\mathcal{B}(V_{\bar{1}},V_{\bar{0}})=\{0\}
,

(v)

the form
\mathcal{B}
is odd if
{\mathcal{B}}(V_{\alpha},V_{\alpha})=\{0\}
, for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{2},

From now on, we will denote a bilinear form with
\langle\;,\;\rangle
instead.

Definition
.

Let
(V,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a pseudo-Euclidean
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space, where
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form on
V
. Let
f
be a homogeneous endomorphism of
V
of degree
\alpha\in\{\bar{0},\bar{1}\}
.

(i)

The adjoint of
f
with respect to
\langle\;,\;\rangle
, denoted by
f^{*}
, is the unique homogeneous endomorphism of degree
\alpha
satisfying

\langle f(u),v\rangle=(-1)^{\alpha|u|}\langle u,f^{*}(v)\rangle,\quad\text{for all }u,v\in V.

(ii)

The endomorphism
f
is said to be:

–

Symmetric (or self-adjoint) with respect to
\langle\;,\;\rangle
if
f^{*}=f
, i.e.,

\langle f(u),v\rangle=(-1)^{\alpha|u|}\langle u,f(v)\rangle,\quad\text{for all }u,v\in V.

–

Anti-symmetric (or anti-self-adjoint) with respect to
\langle\;,\;\rangle
if
f^{*}=-f
, i.e.,

\langle f(u),v\rangle=-(-1)^{\alpha|u|}\langle u,f(v)\rangle,\quad\text{for all }u,v\in V.

2.2.
Leibniz superalgebras

A superalgebra
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
over a field
\mathbb{K}
consists of a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded space
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{A}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathcal{A}}_{\bar{1}}
and a binary operation satisfying:
{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}_{\beta}\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha+\beta},\forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

Recall that there are two versions of skew- or anti-commutativity that are synonyms only in the non-super case; for two homogeneous elements
a
and
b
of a superalgebra we call the following conditions

\begin{array}[]{lcll}b\bullet a&=&(-1)^{|b||a|}a\bullet b&\text{super commutativity,}\\[5.69054pt]
b\bullet a&=&-(-1)^{|b||a|}a\bullet b&\text{super anti-commutativity, }\\[5.69054pt]
b\bullet a&=&(-1)^{(|b|+1)(|a|+1)}a\bullet b&\text{super skew-commutativity, }\\[5.69054pt]
b\bullet a&=&-(-1)^{(|b|+1)(|a|+1)}a\bullet b&\text{super antiskew-commutativity.}\end{array}

For an element
u\in{\mathcal{A}}
, we denote by
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}
and
{\mathrm{R}}_{u}
, respectively, the left and the right multiplication operators in
{\mathcal{A}}
. More precisely
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}
and
{\mathrm{R}}_{u}
are defined as follows:
{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}_{u}(v):=u\bullet v
and
{\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}_{u}(v):=(-1)^{|u||v|}v\bullet u,
for all
v\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

Definition
.

A pseudo-Euclidean superalgebra
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a superalgebra
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
endowed with a
non-degenerate, symmetric bilinear form
\langle\;,\;\rangle
.

Definition
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
be a superalgebra. A bilinear form
\langle\;,\;\rangle
on
{\mathcal{A}}
is invariant if

\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle=\langle u,v\bullet w\rangle,

for all
u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

Definition
.

A disuperalgebra over
\K
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space
{\mathcal{A}}
endowed with
two binary operations
\star,\circ:\,{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{A}}
, called left and right products, satisfying:

{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}\star{\mathcal{A}}_{\beta}\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha+\beta},\quad{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}\circ{\mathcal{A}}_{\beta}\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha+\beta},\;\;\forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

Definition
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
be a superalgebra.

(i)

(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
is called a left Leibniz superalgebra if, for any
u,v,w\in\mathcal{A}
,

u\bullet(v\bullet w)=(u\bullet v)\bullet w+(-1)^{|u||v|}v\bullet(u\bullet w).

(ii)

(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
is called a right Leibniz superalgebra if, for any
u,v,w\in\mathcal{A}
,

u\bullet(v\bullet w)=(u\bullet v)\bullet w-(-1)^{|v||w|}(u\bullet w)\bullet v.

(iii)

If
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
is both left and right Leibniz then it is called a symmetric Leibniz superalgebra.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
be a left (resp. right) Leibniz superalgebra. Then the left (resp. right) multiplication operators satisfy the identity:

(1)

\mathrm{L}^{\bullet}_{u\bullet v}=\left[\mathrm{L}^{\bullet}_{u},\mathrm{L}^{\bullet}_{v}\right]\quad(\text{resp. }\mathrm{R}^{\bullet}_{u\bullet v}=-\left[\mathrm{R}^{\bullet}_{u},\mathrm{R}^{\bullet}_{v}\right]),

for all
u,v\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

It is obvious that
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
is a left Leibniz superalgebra if and only if
\left(\mathcal{A},\bullet_{\text{opp }}\right)
is a right Leibniz superalgebra where
u\bullet_{\text{opp }}v:=(-1)^{|u||v|}v\bullet u
. It is also obvious that any Lie superalgebra is symmetric Leibniz superalgebra. However, the class of Leibniz superalgebras is much larger than the class of Lie superalgebras, and many of the results on the structure theory of Lie superalgebra theory can be extended to left Leibniz superalgebras.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
be a left (resp. right) Leibniz superalgebra. We denote

\operatorname{Leib}(\mathcal{A})=\operatorname{span}\{u\bullet v+(-1)^{|u||v|}v\bullet u,\text{ where }u,v\in\mathcal{A}\}.

According to identity (
1
), we have

\mathrm{L}^{\bullet}_{u\bullet v}=-(-1)^{|u||v|}\mathrm{L}^{\bullet}_{v\bullet u}.
Therefore,
\mathrm{L}^{\bullet}_{u\bullet v+(-1)^{|u||v|}v\bullet u}=0,
which implies that
\mathrm{L}^{\bullet}_{w}=0
, (resp
{\mathrm{R}}_{w}^{\bullet}=0
), for any
w\in\mathrm{Leib}(\mathcal{A})
. It follows that
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathcal{A})
is a graded ideal of the Leibniz superalgebra
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
, and
\mathrm{Lie}({\mathcal{A}}):={\mathcal{A}}/\mathrm{Leib}({\mathcal{A}})
is a Lie superalgebra. Moreover,
{\mathcal{A}}
is a Lie superalgebra if and only if
\mathrm{Leib}({\mathcal{A}})=\{0\}
.

We call
\mathrm{Leib}({\mathcal{A}})
the Leibniz ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}.

Definition
.

A left-symmetric superalgebra (LSSA for short) is a superalgebra
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
over a field
\mathbb{K}
, equipped with a bilinear product
\bullet
, such that the associator

(u,v,w):=(u\bullet v)\bullet w-u\bullet(v\bullet w)

is symmetric in the first two arguments. That is,

(2)

\displaystyle(u,v,w)=(-1)^{|u||v|}(v,u,w),

for all
u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}

Let
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
be a left-symmetric superalgebra. Then, it is clear that the supercommutator

[u,v]:=u\bullet v-(-1)^{|u||v|}v\bullet u

defines a Lie superalgebra structure on
\mathcal{A}
. Moreover, the condition
(
2
) is equivalent to

\mathrm{L}^{\bullet}_{[u,v]}=[\mathrm{L}^{\bullet}_{u},\mathrm{L}^{\bullet}_{v}],

for all
u,v\in\mathcal{A}
.

Definition
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
be a non-associative superalgebra,
V
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space and

:
{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow\operatorname{End}(V)
be two even linear maps. If
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)

is a left (resp. right) representation of
{\mathcal{A}}
in

\begin{gathered}l(u\bullet v)=[l(u),l(v)];\quad r(u\bullet v)=l(u)r(v)+(-1)^{|u||v|}r(v)r(u);\quad r(u\bullet v)=[l(u),r(v)]\\
\text{ (resp. }\quad l(u\bullet v)=(-1)^{|u||v|}[r(v),l(u)];\quad l(u\bullet v)=l(u)l(v)+(-1)^{|u||v|}r(v)l(u)\\
\left.r(u\bullet v)=(-1)^{|u||v|}[r(v),r(u)]\right)\end{gathered}

We say that
(r,l)
is a representation of
{\mathcal{A}}
in
V
if
(r,l)
is a left and a right representation of
{\mathcal{A}}
in
V
.

Let

3.2.
The Levi-Civita product

In this section, we introduce the notion of the Levi-Civita product in the setting of pseudo-Euclidean non-associative superalgebras. This construction extends the classical Levi-Civita product developed for pseudo-Euclidean Lie superalgebras in

We now introduce the notion of the Levi-Civita product in the setting of pseudo-Euclidean non-associative superalgebras.

Proposition
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a pseudo-Euclidean non-associative superalgebra. Then there exists a unique products
(\star,\circ)
on
{\mathcal{A}}

(10)

\displaystyle u\bullet v

\displaystyle=u\star v+(-1)^{|u||v|}v\circ u

(11)

\displaystyle\langle u\star v,w\rangle

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|u||v|}\langle v,u\circ w\rangle.

More precisely, the product
\star

(12)

Moreover, if
\bullet
is super anti-commutative (resp. super commutative) then
\star=-\circ
(resp.
\star=\circ

(\star,\circ)
is called a Levi-Civita product associated with

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\langle u\star v,w\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle-(-1)^{|u||v|}\langle v\circ u,w\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle-(-1)^{|u||v|+|w||u|}\langle v\bullet w,u\rangle+(-1)^{|u||v|+|w||u|+|v||w|}\langle w\circ v,u\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle-(-1)^{|u||v|+|w||u|}\langle v\bullet w,u\rangle+(-1)^{|v||w|+|u||w|}\langle w\bullet u,v\rangle-(-1)^{|v||w|}\langle u\circ w,v\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle-(-1)^{|u||v|+|w||u|}\langle v\bullet w,u\rangle+(-1)^{|v||w|+|u||w|}\langle w\bullet u,v\rangle-(-1)^{|u||v|}\langle v,u\circ w\rangle

\displaystyle 2\langle u\star v+(-1)^{|u||v|}v\circ u,w\rangle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\langle u\star v,w\rangle+(-1)^{|u||v|+|w|(|u|+|v|)}2\langle w,v\circ u\rangle

\displaystyle 2\langle u\star v,w\rangle+(-1)^{|u||v|+|w||u|}2\langle v\star w,u\rangle

\displaystyle\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle-(-1)^{|u||v|+|w||u|}\langle v\bullet w,u\rangle+(-1)^{|v||w|+|u||w|}\langle w\bullet u,v\rangle

\displaystyle+(-1)^{|u||v|+|w||u|}\langle v\bullet w,u\rangle-(-1)^{|u||v|+|w||u|+|v||w|+|u||v|}\langle w\bullet u,v\rangle

\displaystyle+(-1)^{2|u||v|+2|u||w|}\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle

The case where

\displaystyle=\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle-(-1)^{|u||v|+|w||u|}\langle v\bullet w,u\rangle+(-1)^{|v||w|+|u||w|}\langle w\bullet u,v\rangle+(-1)^{|v||w|}\langle u\bullet w,v\rangle

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|v||w|+|u||w|+|u||v|}\langle w\bullet v,u\rangle+(-1)^{|v||w|+|u||v|+|w||v|}\langle v\bullet u,w\rangle

4.
Flat quadartic Leibniz superalgebras

4.1.
Characterization of flat quadratic Leibniz superalgebras

In this paragraph, we study of flat quadratic Leibniz superalgebra. We show that a quadratic Leibniz superalgebra is flat if and only if it is a 2-step nilpotent symmetric Leibniz superalgebra. Furthermore, we provide a characterization of flat quadratic Leibniz superalgebra.

Definition
.

A triple
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is called a quadratic (resp. odd-quadratic) Leibniz superalgebra if

({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
is a left (or right) Leibniz superalgebra equipped with a non-degenerate, symmetric, and invariant bilinear form
\langle\;,\;\rangle
which is even (resp. odd). In this case, the bilinear form
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is called an invariant (resp. odd-invariant) scalar product on
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
.

Proposition
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a quadratic
(
resp. odd-quadratic
)
Leibniz superalgebra. Then the Levi-Civita product
(\star,\circ)
is given by

u\star v=(-1)^{|u||v|}v\circ u=\frac{1}{2}u\bullet v,

for all
u,v\in{\mathcal{A}}.

Proof.

Let
u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}
. We have

\displaystyle 2\langle u\star v,w\rangle=

\displaystyle\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle-(-1)^{|u||v|+|w||u|}\langle v\bullet w,u\rangle+(-1)^{|v||w|+|u||w|}\langle w\bullet u,v\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle-(-1)^{|u||v|+|w||u|}\langle v\bullet w,u\rangle+(-1)^{2|v||w|+|u||w|+|v||u|}\langle v,w\bullet u\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle-(-1)^{|u||v|+|w||u|}\langle v\bullet w,u\rangle+(-1)^{|u||w|+|v||u|}\langle v\bullet w,u\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle.

Since
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is non-degenerate, then we have
u\star v=\frac{1}{2}u\bullet v
. It follows that

u\star v=\frac{1}{2}u\bullet v=\frac{1}{2}(u\star v+(-1)^{|u||v|}v\circ u),

and hence
u\star v=(-1)^{|u||v|}v\circ u=\frac{1}{2}u\bullet v
.
∎

Proposition
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a quadratic (resp. odd-quadratic) Leibniz superalgebra.
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is flat if and only if
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
is 2-nilpotent symmetric Leibniz superalgebra.

Proof.

According to Proposition (
4.1.2.  Proposition
), we have

u\star v=(-1)^{|u||v|}v\circ u=\frac{1}{2}u\bullet v,\quad\text{for all }u,v\in{\mathcal{A}},
which implies that
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\star}=\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet}
and
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\circ}=\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{R}}_{u}^{\bullet}
. Assume that
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is flat, that is, its curvatures tensors satisfy
K_{1}=K_{2}=K_{3}=0
. Then we have:

0={\mathrm{L}}_{u\bullet v}^{\star}-[{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\star},{\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{\star}]=\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{L}}_{u\bullet v}^{\bullet}-\frac{1}{4}[{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet},{\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{\bullet}].

Thus, we get that
{\mathrm{L}}_{u\bullet v}^{\bullet}=[{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet},{\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{\bullet}]=0.
Since
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is an invariant (resp. odd-invariant) scalar product, we have
{\mathrm{R}}_{u}^{\bullet}=({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet})^{*}
. It follows that

{\mathrm{R}}_{u\bullet v}^{\bullet}=\left({\mathrm{L}}_{u\bullet v}^{\bullet}\right)^{*}=0
. Consequently, for all
u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}
, we have

(u\bullet v)\bullet w=w\bullet(u\bullet v)=0.

Therefore, the superalgebra
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
is symmetric Leibniz and 2-step nilpotent.

Conversely, assume that
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
is symmetric Leibniz and 2-step nilpotent. Then, for all
u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}
, we have

(u\bullet v)\bullet w=u\bullet(v\bullet w)=0,

which implies that all compositions of left and right multiplication operators vanish. In particular, using the identities
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\star}=\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet}
and
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\circ}=\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{R}}_{u}^{\bullet}
, one can deduce that

K_{1}=K_{2}=K_{3}=0,
and hence
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is flat.
∎

Let
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
be a 2-step nilpotent symmetric Leibniz superalgebra. A straightforward computation shows that the anticommutative superalgebra
\mathcal{A}^{-}
associated with
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
is a 2-step nilpotent Lie superalgebra, and the commutative superalgebra
\mathcal{A}^{+}
is a 2-step nilpotent associative superalgebra satisfying the identities

[u,\{v,w\}]=\{u,[v,w]\}=0,\quad\text{for all }u,v,w\in\mathcal{A}.

(\mathcal{A},[\;,\;])
be a 2-step nilpotent Lie superalgebra, and let

of
(\mathcal{A},[\;,\;])
. Suppose that
\mu
satisfies the identities

\mu([u,v],w)=\mu(\mu(u,v),w)=0,\quad\text{for all }u,v,w\in\mathcal{A}.

Then define a new product

One checks immediately that
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
is a 2-step nilpotent symmetric Leibniz superalgebra. In this case, we call
({\mathcal{A}},[\;,\;]+\mu)
as the 2-step nilpotent symmetric Leibniz superalgebra associated with the Lie superalgebra
({\mathcal{A}},[\;,\;])
by
means of
\mu
.

Proposition
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)

is a flat quadratic (resp. odd-quadratic) Leibniz superalgebra. In particular,
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
is a 2-step nilpotent symmetric Leibniz superalgebra. For any
u,v,w\in\mathcal{A}
, we have:

\displaystyle\langle[u,v],w\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\langle u\bullet v-(-1)^{|u||v|}v\bullet u,w\rangle=\frac{1}{2}(\langle u,v\bullet w\rangle-(-1)^{|u||v|}\langle v,u\bullet w\rangle)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\langle u,v\bullet w\rangle-(-1)^{|u||v|+|v|(|u|+|w|)}\langle u\bullet w,v\rangle)=\frac{1}{2}(\langle u,v\bullet w\rangle-(-1)^{|v||w|}\langle u,w\bullet v\rangle)

\displaystyle=\langle u,[v,w]\rangle.

This identity shows that
(\mathcal{A}^{-},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a quadratic (resp. odd-quadratic) Lie superalgebra. In a similar manner, one can verify that
(\mathcal{A}^{+},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a quadratic (resp. odd-quadratic) associative superalgebra. Since
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
is 2-step nilpotent, we conclude that
(\mathcal{A}^{-},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a quadratic (resp. odd-quadratic) 2-step nilpotent Lie superalgebra and
(\mathcal{A}^{+},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a quadratic (resp. odd-quadratic) 2-step nilpotent associative superalgebra.

Conversely, suppose that
(\mathcal{A}^{-},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a quadratic (resp. odd-quadratic) 2-step nilpotent Lie superalgebra and
(\mathcal{A}^{+},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a quadratic (resp. odd-quadratic) 2-step nilpotent associative superalgebra. For any
u,v,w\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle=\langle[u,v]+\{u,v\},w\rangle=\langle u,[v,w]\rangle+\langle u,\{v,w\}\rangle=\langle u,v\bullet w\rangle,

which shows that
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is invariant (resp. odd-invariant) with respect to the product
\bullet
, and hence
(\mathcal{A},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a quadratic (resp. odd-quadratic) Leibniz superalgebra. Since both
\mathcal{A}^{-}
and
\mathcal{A}^{+}
are 2-step nilpotent, and the identities defining symmetry hold (cf. (
20
)), it follows that
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
is 2-step nilpotent. By Proposition
4.1.3.  Proposition
, we conclude that
(\mathcal{A},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is flat. Therefore,
(\mathcal{A},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a flat quadratic (resp. odd-quadratic) Leibniz superalgebra.
∎

Theorem
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a pseudo-Euclidean 2-step nilpotent symmetric Leibniz superalgebra obtained from a Lie algebra
({\mathcal{A}},[\;,\;])
by means of
\mu
. Denote by
Z({\mathcal{A}})
the center of
({\mathcal{A}},[\;,\;])
. Then, the bilinear form
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is invariant
(
resp. odd-invariant
)
if and only if
({\mathcal{A}},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a quadratic
(
resp. odd-quadratic
)
2-step nilpotent Lie superalgebra, and there exist an even (resp. odd) symmetric trilinear form
T:{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\to\K
and a totally isotropic subspace
J\subseteq Z({\mathcal{A}})
such that

T(J^{\bot},\cdot,\cdot)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\langle\mu(u,v),w\rangle=T(u,v,w),\quad\text{for all }u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}.

Proof.

Assume that
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is invariant (resp. odd-invariant) on
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
. According to Proposition
4.1.4.  Proposition
and the structural description of symmetric Leibniz superalgebras mentioned above, we have that
(\mathcal{A},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a quadratic (resp. odd-quadratic) 2-step nilpotent Lie superalgebra, and the symmetric bilinear map
\mu(u,v)
satisfies
\mu(u,v)\in Z(\mathcal{A})
for all
u,v\in\mathcal{A}
, with the relations

\mu([u,v],w)=\mu(\mu(u,v),w)=0,\quad\text{and}\quad\langle\mu(u,v),w\rangle=\langle u,\mu(v,w)\rangle.

Let
J:=\operatorname{span}\{\mu(u,v)\mid u,v\in\mathcal{A}\}\subseteq Z(\mathcal{A})
. Then, for any
u,v,w,z\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

\langle\mu(u,v),\mu(w,z)\rangle=\langle u,\mu(v,\mu(w,z))\rangle=0,

since
\mu(v,\mu(w,z))=0
. Hence,
J
is a totally isotropic subspace of
\mathcal{A}
.
Define a trilinear map
T:\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
by

T(u,v,w):=\langle\mu(u,v),w\rangle.

We now show that
T
is symmetric. Indeed, for all
u,v,w\in\mathcal{A}
, we have:

\displaystyle T(u,v,w)

\displaystyle=\langle\mu(u,v),w\rangle=(-1)^{|u||v|}\langle\mu(v,u),w\rangle=(-1)^{|u||v|}T(v,u,w),

\displaystyle T(u,v,w)

\displaystyle=\langle u,\mu(v,w)\rangle=(-1)^{|u|(|v|+|w|)}\langle\mu(v,w),u\rangle=(-1)^{|u|(|v|+|w|)}T(v,w,u).

Thus,
T
is a symmetric trilinear form. Moreover, if
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is even (resp. odd), then it is clear that
T
is also even (resp. odd).

On the other hand, for any
u,v\in\mathcal{A}
and
w\in J^{\bot}
, we have

T(w,u,v)=\langle\mu(w,u),v\rangle=\langle w,\mu(u,v)\rangle=0,

so that
T(J^{\bot},\cdot,\cdot)=0
.

Conversely, suppose that
(\mathcal{A},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a quadratic (resp. odd-quadratic) 2-step nilpotent Lie superalgebra, and there exist a even (resp. odd) symmetric trilinear form
T:\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
and a totally isotropic subspace
J\subseteq Z(\mathcal{A})
such that

T(J^{\bot},\cdot,\cdot)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\langle\mu(u,v),w\rangle=T(u,v,w),\quad\text{for all }u,v,w\in\mathcal{A}.

Then for all
u,v\in\mathcal{A}
and
w\in J^{\bot}
, we have

T(w,u,v)=\langle\mu(w,u),v\rangle=\langle w,\mu(u,v)\rangle=0,

implying that
\mu(u,v)\in J
, and hence
J\supseteq\operatorname{span}\{\mu(u,v)\mid u,v\in\mathcal{A}\}
. Since
J\subseteq Z(\mathcal{A})
is totally isotropic, it follows that

\langle\mu(u,v),\mu(w,z)\rangle=\langle\mu(\mu(u,v),w),z\rangle=0,

and

\langle\mu([u,v],w),z\rangle=\langle[u,v],\mu(w,z)\rangle=\langle u,[v,\mu(w,z)]\rangle=0.

Thus, the bilinear product defined by
u\bullet v=[u,v]+\mu(u,v)
endows
\mathcal{A}
with the structure of a 2-step nilpotent symmetric Leibniz superalgebra. Since
T
is symmetric trilinear form, then
\mu
is invariant (resp. odd-invariant). According to Proposition
4.1.4.  Proposition
, the structure
(\mathcal{A},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a quadratic (resp. odd-quadratic) symmetric Leibniz superalgebra.
∎

Remark
.

The above Theorem provides a characterization of quadratic 2-step nilpotent symmetric Leibniz superalgebras based on quadratic 2-step nilpotent Lie superalgebras.
A similar approach is followed in
[
40
]
. More generally, quadratic Lie superalgebras play a significant role in the theory and have been the subject of numerous studies (see
[
4
,
8
,
11
]
, among others). This framework enables the straightforward construction of quadratic 2-step nilpotent symmetric Leibniz superalgebras, along with explicit examples.

Example
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathcal{A}_{\bar{1}}
be a four-dimensional Lie superalgebra, where
\mathcal{A}_{0}=\mathrm{span}\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
and
\mathcal{A}_{1}=\mathrm{span}\{e_{3},e_{4}\}
. The Lie bracket
[\;,\;]
is defined on the basis elements by:

[e_{2},e_{4}]=-[e_{4},e_{2}]=e_{3},\quad[e_{4},e_{4}]=e_{1}.

Consider the even symmetric bilinear form
\langle\;,\;\rangle:\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{K}
given by:

\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle=1,\quad\langle e_{3},e_{4}\rangle=1,

with all other pairings zero. A direct computation shows that this bilinear form is invariant under the Lie bracket.

Let us now determine the space
J
and the trilinear form
T
associated with a symmetric product
\mu
. Since
J
is a totally isotropic subspace of the center
Z(\mathcal{A})
, we have
J\subseteq\mathrm{span}\{e_{1},e_{3}\}
. If we choose
J=\mathrm{span}\{e_{3}\}
, then
J^{\perp}=\mathrm{span}\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
. The condition
T(J^{\perp},\cdot,\cdot)=0
then implies that

T(e_{1},\cdot,\cdot)=T(e_{2},\cdot,\cdot)=T(e_{3},\cdot,\cdot)=0.

Moreover, since
e_{4}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
T
is symmetric, we also have
T(e_{4},\cdot,\cdot)=0
, which implies that
T=0
, and consequently
\mu=0
.

Whether we choose
J=\mathrm{span}\{e_{1}\}
or
J=\mathrm{span}\{e_{1},e_{3}\}
, the condition
T(J^{\perp},\cdot,\cdot)=0
implies that
\mu(u,v)\in J
for all
u,v\in\mathcal{A}
. A straightforward computation shows that
\mu(e_{2},e_{2})=\alpha e_{1},
where
\alpha\neq 0
. Therefore, the quadratic 2-step nilpotent symmetric Leibniz superalgebra constructed from the Lie superalgebra
(\mathcal{A},[\;,\;])
by means of
\mu
is defined by:

\begin{array}[]{c}e_{2}\bullet e_{2}=\alpha e_{1},\quad e_{2}\bullet e_{4}=-e_{4}\bullet e_{2}=e_{3},\quad e_{4}\bullet e_{4}=e_{1},\text{ and }\\[2.84526pt]
\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle=1,\quad\langle e_{3},e_{4}\rangle=1.\end{array}

Example
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathcal{A}_{\bar{1}}
be a six-dimensional Lie superalgebra, where
\mathcal{A}_{0}=\mathrm{span}\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
and
\mathcal{A}_{1}=\mathrm{span}\{e_{3},e_{4},e_{5},e_{6}\}
. The non-zero Lie brackets are given by:

[e_{2},e_{5}]=e_{3},\quad[e_{2},e_{6}]=e_{4},\quad[e_{5},e_{5}]=e_{1},\quad[e_{6},e_{6}]=e_{1}.

Consider the even symmetric bilinear form
\langle\;,\;\rangle:\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{K}
defined by:

\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle=1,\quad\langle e_{3},e_{5}\rangle=1,\quad\langle e_{4},e_{6}\rangle=1,

A direct computation shows that this bilinear form is invariant under
the Lie bracket.

We aim to determine the space
J
and the trilinear form
T
associated with a symmetric product
\mu
. Since
J
must be a totally isotropic subspace of the center
Z(\mathcal{A})
, it satisfies
J\subseteq\mathrm{span}\{e_{1},e_{3},e_{4}\}
. To construct an explicit example, we consider
J=\mathrm{span}\{e_{1},e_{3},e_{4}\}
. The condition
T(J^{\perp},\cdot,\cdot)=0
then implies
\mu(u,v)\in J
for all
u,v\in\mathcal{A}
.
A direct computation shows that

\mu(e_{2},e_{2})=\alpha e_{1},\quad\mu(e_{2},e_{5})=\beta e_{4},\quad\mu(e_{2},e_{6})=-\beta e_{3},\quad\mu(e_{5},e_{6})=\beta e_{1},

with
(\alpha,\beta)\neq(0,0)
. Therefore, the corresponding quadratic 2-step nilpotent symmetric Leibniz superalgebra is given by:

\begin{array}[]{c}e_{2}\bullet e_{2}=\alpha e_{1},\quad e_{2}\bullet e_{5}=e_{3}+\beta e_{4},\quad e_{2}\bullet e_{6}=e_{4}-\beta e_{3},\quad e_{5}\bullet e_{2}=-e_{3}+\beta e_{4},\\[2.84526pt]
e_{5}\bullet e_{5}=e_{1},\quad e_{5}\bullet e_{6}=\beta e_{1},\quad e_{6}\bullet e_{2}=-e_{4}-\beta e_{3},\quad e_{6}\bullet e_{5}=\beta e_{1},\quad e_{6}\bullet e_{6}=e_{1},\text{ and }\\[2.84526pt]
\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle=1,\quad\langle e_{3},e_{5}\rangle=1,\quad\langle e_{4},e_{6}\rangle=1.\end{array}

Next, we introduce an odd-quadratic 2-step nilpotent symmetric Leibniz superalgebra.

Example
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathcal{A}_{\bar{1}}
be a four-dimensional Lie superalgebra, where
\mathcal{A}_{0}=\mathrm{span}\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
and
\mathcal{A}_{1}=\mathrm{span}\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
. The non-zero Lie brackets are given by:

[f_{1},f_{1}]=e_{1}.

Consider the odd symmetric bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{K}
defined by:

\langle e_{1},f_{1}\rangle=1,\quad\langle e_{2},f_{2}\rangle=1.

A straightforward computation shows that this bilinear form is odd-invariant under the Lie bracket.

We now determine the space
J
and the trilinear map
T
associated with a symmetric product
\mu
. Since
J
must be a totally isotropic subspace of the center
Z(\mathcal{A})=\mathrm{span}\{e_{1},e_{2},f_{2}\}
, the possible choices for
J
are:

J=\mathrm{span}\{e_{1}\},\quad\mathrm{span}\{e_{2}\},\quad\mathrm{span}\{f_{2}\},\quad\mathrm{span}\{e_{1},f_{2}\},\quad\text{or}\quad\mathrm{span}\{e_{1},e_{2}\}.

We choose
J=\mathrm{span}\{e_{1},f_{2}\}
, which satisfies
J=J^{\perp}
. Therefore, the condition
T(J,\cdot,\cdot)=0
implies

T(e_{1},\cdot,\cdot)=T(f_{2},\cdot,\cdot)=0.

Since
T
is an odd symmetric trilinear map, then we have

T(e_{2},e_{2},e_{2})=T(f_{1},f_{1},\cdot)=0.

The only non-zero component we may have is
T(e_{2},e_{2},f_{1})=\alpha
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{K}\setminus\{0\}
. Consequently, the associated symmetric product is defined by:

\mu(e_{2},e_{2})=\alpha e_{1},\quad\mu(e_{2},f_{1})=\mu(f_{1},e_{2})=\alpha f_{2}.

Therefore, the corresponding odd-quadratic 2-step nilpotent symmetric Leibniz superalgebra is given by:

\begin{array}[]{c}e_{2}\bullet e_{2}=\alpha e_{1},\quad e_{2}\bullet f_{1}=f_{1}\bullet e_{2}=\alpha f_{2},\quad f_{1}\bullet f_{1}=e_{1},\text{ and }\\[2.84526pt]
\langle e_{1},f_{1}\rangle=1,\quad\langle e_{2},f_{2}\rangle=1.\end{array}

Example
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathcal{A}_{\bar{1}}
be a six-dimensional Lie superalgebra, where
\mathcal{A}_{0}=\mathrm{span}\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
and
\mathcal{A}_{1}=\mathrm{span}\{f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\}
. The non-zero Lie brackets are given by:

[e_{1},e_{2}]=e_{3},\quad[e_{2},f_{3}]=f_{1},\quad[e_{1},f_{3}]=-f_{2}.

Consider the odd symmetric bilinear form
\langle\;,\;\rangle:\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{K}
defined by:

\langle e_{1},f_{1}\rangle=1,\quad\langle e_{2},f_{2}\rangle=1,\quad\langle e_{3},f_{3}\rangle=1.

A direct computation shows that this bilinear form is odd-invariant under the Lie bracket
[\;,\;]
.

We aim to determine the space
J
and the trilinear form
T
associated with a symmetric product
\mu
. Since
J
must be a totally isotropic subspace of the center
Z(\mathcal{A})=\{e_{3},f_{1},f_{2}\}
, it satisfies
J\subseteq\mathrm{span}\{e_{3},f_{1},f_{2}\}
. The possible choices for
J
are:

J=\mathrm{span}\{e_{3}\},\;\mathrm{span}\{f_{1}\},\;\mathrm{span}\{f_{2}\},\;\mathrm{span}\{e_{3},f_{1}\},\;\mathrm{span}\{e_{3},f_{2}\},\;\mathrm{span}\{f_{1},f_{2}\},\;\text{or}\;\mathrm{span}\{e_{3},f_{1},f_{2}\}.

We choose
J=\mathrm{span}\{e_{3},f_{1},f_{2}\}
, which satisfies
J=J^{\perp}
. Therefore, the condition
T(J,\cdot,\cdot)=0
implies

T(e_{3},\cdot,\cdot)=T(f_{1},\cdot,\cdot)=T(f_{2},\cdot,\cdot)=0.

Since
T
is odd symmetric, we also have
T(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k})=0
for all
i,j,k\in\{1,2\}
and
T(f_{3},f_{3},\cdot)=0
. Thus, we get:

T(e_{1},e_{1},f_{3})=\alpha,\quad T(e_{1},e_{2},f_{3})=\beta,\quad T(e_{2},e_{2},f_{3})=\lambda,

where

\mu(e_{1},e_{1})=\alpha e_{3},\;\mu(e_{1},e_{2})=\beta e_{3},\;\mu(e_{2},e_{2})=\lambda e_{3},\;\mu(e_{1},f_{3})=\alpha f_{1}+\beta f_{2},\;\mu(e_{2},f_{3})=\beta f_{1}+\lambda f_{2}.

\displaystyle e_{1}\bullet e_{1}=\alpha e_{3},\quad e_{1}\bullet e_{2}=(1+\beta)e_{3},\quad e_{2}\bullet e_{1}=(\beta-1)e_{3},\quad e_{2}\bullet e_{2}=\lambda e_{3},

4.2.
T^{*}
-extension and
\Pi(T^{*})
-extension of flat quadratic Leibniz superalgebras

This part deals with the
T^{*}
-extension in the context of even quadratic Leibniz superalgebras and
\Pi(T^{*})
-extensions in the case of odd quadratic Leibniz superalgebras. We provide a description of quadratic 2-step nilpotent symmetric Leibniz superalgebras. Recall that the notion of the
T^{*}
-extension was introduced by Bordemann in
[
15
]
in the context of nondegenerate associative bilinear forms on non-associative algebras. See also
[
10
,
17
]
on Lie (super)algebras when the bilinear form is antisymmetric, and referred to as Lagrangian extensions.

Theorem

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
be a symmetric Leibniz superalgebra and let
\Omega:{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
be an even bilinear map. Then, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space
T_{\Omega}^{*}({\mathcal{A}}):={\mathcal{A}}\oplus{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
, endowed with the product:

(u+f)\bullet(v+g):=u\bullet v+\Omega(u,v)+({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet})^{*}(u)(g)+(-1)^{|u||v|}({\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet})^{*}(v)(f),

for all
u+f\in T_{\Omega}^{*}({\mathcal{A}})_{|u|}
,
v+g\in T_{\Omega}^{*}({\mathcal{A}})_{|v|}
,
is a symmetric Leibniz superalgebra if and only if
\Omega
is an even Leibniz 2-cocycle of
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
relative to the representation
(({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet})^{*},({\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet})^{*})
.
Moreover, the even bilinear form

B:T_{\Omega}^{*}({\mathcal{A}})\times T_{\Omega}^{*}({\mathcal{A}})\to\mathbb{K},\quad(u+f,\,v+g)\mapsto f(v)+(-1)^{|u||v|}g(u)

is symmetric and non-degenerate on
T_{\Omega}^{*}({\mathcal{A}})
. The bilinear form
B
is invariant on
T_{\Omega}^{*}({\mathcal{A}})
if and only if

(21)

\Omega(u,v)(w)=(-1)^{|u|(|v|+|w|)}\Omega(v,w)(u),\quad\forall\,u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}.

In this case, the quadratic Leibniz superalgebra
(T_{\Omega}^{*}({\mathcal{A}}),B)
is called the
T^{*}_{\Omega}
-extension of
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
by means of
\Omega
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
be a left symmetric superalgebra, and let
({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet},{\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet})
denote its left and right adjoint representations, respectively. According to Proposition
2.2.8.  Proposition
, the pair
({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*},{\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet*})
defines a representation of
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
on
\mathcal{A}^{*}
.

We now define another pair of linear maps:

({\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet*})^{\Pi}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathrm{End}(\Pi(\mathcal{A}^{*})),\qquad({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*})^{\Pi}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathrm{End}(\Pi(\mathcal{A}^{*})),

given for homogeneous
u\in\mathcal{A}
by:

({\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet*})^{\Pi}(u)=\Pi\circ{\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet*}(u)\circ\Pi,\qquad({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*})^{\Pi}(u)=\Pi\circ{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*}(u)\circ\Pi.

Proposition
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
be a symmetric Leibniz superalgebra, and let
({\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet},{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet})
be its regular representation in
\mathcal{A}
. Then the pair
(({\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet*})^{\Pi},({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*})^{\Pi})
defines a representation of
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
on
\Pi(\mathcal{A}^{*})
.

Proof.

Let
u,v\in\mathcal{A}
. Using the definition of
({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*})^{\Pi}
, we have

\displaystyle({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*})^{\Pi}(u\bullet v)

\displaystyle=\Pi\circ{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*}(u\bullet v)\circ\Pi

\displaystyle=\Pi\circ\left({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*}(u)\circ{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*}(v)-(-1)^{|u||v|}{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*}(v)\circ{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*}(u)\right)\circ\Pi

\displaystyle=\Pi\circ{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*}(u)\circ\Pi\circ\Pi\circ{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*}(v)\circ\Pi-(-1)^{|u||v|}\Pi\circ{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*}(v)\circ\Pi\circ\Pi\circ{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*}(u)\circ\Pi

\displaystyle=({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*})^{\Pi}(u)\circ({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*})^{\Pi}(v)-(-1)^{|u||v|}({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*})^{\Pi}(v)\circ({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*})^{\Pi}(u)

\displaystyle=[({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*})^{\Pi}(u),({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*})^{\Pi}(v)].

The remaining conditions can be established in a similar manner. Hence,
(({\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet*})^{\Pi},({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*})^{\Pi})
defines a representation of
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
on
\Pi(\mathcal{A}^{*})
.
∎

Now, we introduce an analogue of the notion of
T^{*}_{\Omega}
-extension in the odd-quadratic case, which we denote by
\Pi(T^{*}_{\Omega})
.

Theorem
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
be a symmetric Leibniz superalgebra and let
\Omega:{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\to\Pi({\mathcal{A}}^{*})
be an even bilinear map. Then, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space
\Pi(T_{\Omega}^{*})({\mathcal{A}}):={\mathcal{A}}\oplus\Pi({\mathcal{A}}^{*})
, endowed with the product:

(u+\Pi(f))\bullet(v+\Pi(g)):=u\bullet v+\Omega(u,v)+({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*})^{\Pi}(u)(\Pi(g))+(-1)^{|u||v|}({\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet*})^{\Pi}(v)(\Pi(f)),

for all
u+\Pi(f)\in\Pi(T_{\Omega}^{*})({\mathcal{A}})_{|u|}
,
v+\Pi(g)\in\Pi(T_{\Omega}^{*})({\mathcal{A}})_{|v|}
,
is a symmetric Leibniz superalgebra if and only if
\Omega
is an even Leibniz 2-cocycle of
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
relative to the representation
(({\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet*})^{\Pi},({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet*})^{\Pi})
. Moreover, the even bilinear form

B:\Pi(T_{\Omega}^{*})({\mathcal{A}})\times\Pi(T_{\Omega}^{*})({\mathcal{A}})\to\mathbb{K},\quad(u+\Pi(f),\,v+\Pi(g))\mapsto f(v)+(-1)^{|u||v|}g(u)

is symmetric and non-degenerate on
\Pi(T_{\Omega}^{*})({\mathcal{A}})
. The bilinear form
B
is odd-invariant on
\Pi(T_{\Omega}^{*})({\mathcal{A}})
if and only if

(22)

\Pi(\Omega(u,v))(w)=(-1)^{|u|(|v|+|w|)}\Pi(\Omega(v,w))(u),\quad\forall\,u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}.

In this case, the odd-quadratic Leibniz superalgebra
(\Pi(T_{\Omega}^{*})({\mathcal{A}}),B)
is called the
\Pi(T^{*}_{\Omega})
-extension of
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
by means of
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let

which is clearly a linear isomorphism. Since the bilinear form
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is odd, it follows that
\varphi
is a odd isomorphism.

Now, define the bilinear map
\Omega:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*})
by

\Omega(u,v):=\Pi\circ\varphi(u\bullet v).
Since
\varphi
is odd and the product
\bullet
is even maps, it follows that
\Omega
is even. Since
\varphi
is bijective, then
\Omega
is also bijective.

On the other hand, for all
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h}
, we have

\displaystyle\Pi(\Omega(u,v))(w)=\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle=\langle u,v\bullet w\rangle=(-1)^{|u|(|v|+|w|)}\langle v\bullet w,u\rangle=(-1)^{|u|(|v|+|w|)}\Pi(\Omega(v,w))(u).

Now, consider
\mathfrak{h}
as a trivial superalgebra (i.e., the product on
\mathfrak{h}
is zero). Since
\Omega
is an even bilinear map satisfying the cyclicity condition given by (
22
), it follows from Theorem
4.2.3.  Theorem
that
\Pi(T^{*}_{\Omega}(\mathfrak{h}))=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*})
is the
\Pi(T_{\Omega}^{*})
-extension of the trivial superalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
by means of
\Omega
.

Moreover, define the map

\Phi:\mathfrak{h}\oplus\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{A},\bullet)\to\Pi(T^{*}_{\Omega}(\mathfrak{h})),\,\Phi(u+a):=u+\Pi(\varphi(a)).

This map is clearly linear and bijective. For all
u,v\in\mathfrak{h}
and
a,b\in\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
, we have:

\displaystyle\Phi((u+a)\bullet(v+b))

\displaystyle=\Phi(u\bullet v)=\Pi(\varphi(u\bullet v))=\Omega(u,v)

\displaystyle=(u+\Pi(\varphi(a))\bullet_{\Omega}(v+\Pi(\varphi(b)))

\displaystyle=\Phi(u+a)\bullet_{\Omega}\Phi(v+b),

so
\Phi
is an isomorphism of Leibniz superalgebras. Let
B
be the odd bilinear form on
\Pi(T^{*}_{\Omega}(\mathfrak{h}))
. Then for all
u+a\in(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{A},\bullet))_{|u|}
and
v+b\in(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{A},\bullet))_{|v|},
we have:

\displaystyle B(\Phi(u+a),\Phi(v+b))

\displaystyle=B(u+\Pi(\varphi(a)),v+\Pi(\varphi(b)))

\displaystyle=\varphi(a)(v)+(-1)^{|u||v|}\varphi(b)(u)

\displaystyle=\langle a,v\rangle+(-1)^{|u||v|}\langle b,u\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u+a,v+b\rangle,

so
\Phi
is an isometric. Therefore,
(\mathcal{A},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is isometric and isomorphic to the
\Pi(T^{*}_{\Omega})
-extension of the trivial algebra
\mathfrak{h}
by means of
\Omega
.
∎

Let
E=E_{\bar{0}}\oplus E_{\bar{1}}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space. Let

\Omega:E\times E\to\Pi(E^{*})
be an even bilinear map. We say that
\Omega
is cyclic if, for all
u,v,w\in E
, the following identity holds

(26)

\displaystyle\Pi(\Omega(u,v))(w)=(-1)^{|u|(|v|+|w|)}\Pi(\Omega(v,w))(u).

We define the associated odd trilinear form
\tilde{\Omega}:E\times E\times E\to\mathbb{K}
by

\tilde{\Omega}(u,v,w):=\Pi(\Omega(u,v))(w),\quad\forall u,v,w\in E.

Due to the cyclicity of
\Omega
, the form
\tilde{\Omega}
satisfies the following identities:

(27)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}(u,v,w)=(-1)^{|u|(|v|+|w|)}\tilde{\Omega}(v,w,u)=(-1)^{|w|(|u|+|v|)}\tilde{\Omega}(w,u,v).

We denote by
\mathcal{T}^{3}(E)_{\bar{1}}
the set of all odd trilinear forms
\tilde{\Omega}:E^{\otimes 3}\to\mathbb{K}
that satisfy identity (
27
). That is,

\mathcal{T}^{3}(E)_{\bar{1}}:=\left\{\tilde{\Omega}\in\mathrm{Hom}(E^{\otimes 3},\mathbb{K})_{\bar{1}}\ \middle|\ \tilde{\Omega}\text{ satisfies \eqref{tri1}}\right\}.

Consequently, we have a restricted definition for the reduced odd-quadratic 2-step nilpotent symmetric Leibniz case as follows:

Definition
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space and let
\Omega:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*})
be a non-degenerate cyclic even bilinear map. Define the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space
{\mathcal{A}}:=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*})
and endow it with the product

(u+\Pi(f))\bullet(v+\Pi(g)):=\Omega(u,v),

and the symmetric bilinear form

B(u+\Pi(f),v+\Pi(g)):=f(v)+(-1)^{|u||v|}g(u),

for all
u+\Pi(f)\in{\mathcal{A}}_{|u|}
and
v+\Pi(g)\in{\mathcal{A}}_{|v|}
. Then
({\mathcal{A}},B)
is called a odd-quadratic 2-step nilpotent symmetric Leibniz algebra. In this case, we say that
{\mathcal{A}}
is the
\Pi(T_{\Omega}^{*})
-extension of
\mathfrak{h}
by
\Omega
.

Theorem
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet_{\Omega_{1}})
and
(\mathcal{B},\bullet_{\Omega_{2}})
be
\Pi(T_{\Omega}^{*})
-extensions of
\mathfrak{h}
by
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
, respectively. Then:

(i)

There exists an even isomorphism symmetric Leibniz algebra between
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet_{\Omega_{1}})
and
(\mathcal{B},\bullet_{\Omega_{2}})
if and only if there exist an even isomorphism
\varphi_{1}:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}
and an even isomorphism
\varphi_{2}:\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*})\to\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*})
such that

\varphi_{2}(\Omega_{1}(u,v))=\Omega_{2}(\varphi_{1}(u),\varphi_{1}(v)),\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

(ii)

There exists an isometrically isomorphic (i.e., an isometric isomorphism preserving the natural bilinear form) between
{\mathcal{A}}
and
\mathcal{B}
if and only if there exists an even isomorphism
\varphi_{1}:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}
such that

\Pi(\Omega_{1}(u,v))=\Pi(\Omega_{2}(\varphi_{1}(u),\varphi_{1}(v)))\circ\varphi_{1},\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Proof.

(i) Let
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be an even isomorphism of symmetric Leibniz algebras. Since the annihilators of both
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
satisfy

\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{A},\bullet_{\Omega_{1}})=\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{B},\bullet_{\Omega_{2}})=\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*}),

and since these spaces are stable under
\varphi
, there exist even linear maps

\varphi_{1}:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h},\;\varphi_{1}^{\prime}:\mathfrak{h}\to\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*}),\;\text{and}\;\varphi_{2}:\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*})\to\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*})

such that, for all
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{|u|}
,
\Pi(f)\in\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*})_{|u|}
,

\varphi(u+\Pi(f))=\varphi_{1}(u)+\varphi_{1}^{\prime}(u)+\varphi_{2}(\Pi(f)).

It is immediate that
\varphi_{2}
is an even linear isomorphism. We now show that
\varphi_{1}
is also an even isomorphism of
\mathfrak{h}
.

Suppose
\varphi_{1}(u)=0
for some
u\in\mathfrak{h}
. Then, for any
Y\in\mathcal{B}
,

0=\varphi(u)\bullet_{\Omega_{2}}Y=\varphi(u\bullet_{\Omega_{1}}\varphi^{-1}(Y)).

Since
\varphi
is an isomorphism, this implies that

u\bullet_{\Omega_{1}}\varphi^{-1}(Y)=0\;\text{for all }Y\in\mathcal{B}.

Therefore,
u\bullet_{\Omega_{1}}v=0
for all
v\in\mathcal{A}
, hence
u\in\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{A},\bullet)=\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*})
, which contradicts
u\in\mathfrak{h}
. Thus,
u=0
, and
\varphi_{1}
is injective. As
\dim\mathfrak{h}=\dim\varphi_{1}(\mathfrak{h})
, it follows that
\varphi_{1}
is an even isomorphism.

Now, for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{h}
, and
\Pi(f)\in\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*})_{|u|},\Pi(g)\in\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*})_{|v|}
, we have:

\displaystyle\varphi((u+\Pi(f))\bullet_{\Omega_{1}}(v+\Pi(g)))

\displaystyle=\varphi(\Omega_{1}(u,v))=\varphi_{2}(\Omega_{1}(u,v)),

\displaystyle\varphi(u+\Pi(f))\bullet_{\Omega_{2}}\varphi(v+\Pi(g))

\displaystyle=(\varphi_{1}(u)+\varphi_{1}^{\prime}(u)+\varphi_{2}(\Pi(f)))\bullet_{\Omega_{2}}(\varphi_{1}(v)+\varphi_{1}^{\prime}(v)+\varphi_{2}(\Pi(g)))

\displaystyle=\Omega_{2}(\varphi_{1}(u),\varphi_{1}(v)).

Hence,

\varphi_{2}(\Omega_{1}(u,v))=\Omega_{2}(\varphi_{1}(u),\varphi_{1}(v))\quad\text{for all }u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Conversely, suppose there exist even isomorphisms
\varphi_{1}:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}
and
\varphi_{2}:\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*})\to\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*})
such that

\varphi_{2}(\Omega_{1}(u,v))=\Omega_{2}(\varphi_{1}(u),\varphi_{1}(v))\quad\text{for all }u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Define
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
by

\varphi(u+\Pi(f))=\varphi_{1}(u)+\varphi_{2}(\Pi(f))
, for all
u\in\mathfrak{h},\,\Pi(f)\in\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*})_{|u|}.

It is straightforward to verify that
\varphi
is an even isomorphism of symmetric Leibniz algebras.

(ii) Suppose
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
is an isometric isomorphism. Then, as in (1), there exist even isomorphisms
\varphi_{1}:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}
and
\varphi_{2}:\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*})\to\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*})
such that

\varphi(u+\Pi(f))=\varphi_{1}(u)+\varphi_{2}(\Pi(f))\quad\text{for all }u\in\mathfrak{h},\,\Pi(f)\in\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*})_{\alpha}.

For all
u\in\mathfrak{h},\,\Pi(f)\in\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*})_{\alpha}
, we have

B^{\prime}(\varphi(u),\varphi(\Pi(f)))=B(u,\Pi(f))\Rightarrow(-1)^{\alpha|u|}\Pi(\varphi_{2}(\Pi(f)))(\varphi_{1}(u))=(-1)^{\alpha|u|}f(u).

Therefore,

\varphi_{2}(\Pi(f))=\Pi(f\circ\varphi_{1}^{-1})\quad\text{for all }\Pi(f)\in\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*})_{\alpha}.

Since

\varphi_{2}(\Omega_{1}(u,v))=\Omega_{2}(\varphi_{1}(u),\varphi_{1}(v)),

we obtain

\Pi(\Omega_{1}(u,v))=\Pi(\Omega_{2}(\varphi_{1}(u),\varphi_{1}(v)))\circ\varphi_{1},\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Conversely, suppose there exists an even isomorphism
\varphi_{1}:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}
such that

\Pi(\Omega_{1}(u,v))=\Pi(\Omega_{2}(\varphi_{1}(u),\varphi_{1}(v)))\circ\varphi_{1},\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Define
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
by

\varphi(u+\Pi(f))=\varphi_{1}(u)+\Pi(f\circ\varphi_{1}^{-1}),\quad\forall u\in\mathfrak{h},\;\Pi(f)\in\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*})_{\alpha}.

Then it is clear that
\varphi
is an even isomorphism of symmetric Leibniz algebras.

Moreover, for all
u+\Pi(f)\in\mathcal{A}_{|u|}
and
v+\Pi(g)\in\mathcal{A}_{|v|}
, we have:

\displaystyle B^{\prime}(\varphi(u+\Pi(f)),\varphi(v+\Pi(g)))

\displaystyle=B^{\prime}(\varphi_{1}(u)+\Pi(f\circ\varphi_{1}^{-1}),\varphi_{1}(v)+\Pi(g\circ\varphi_{1}^{-1}))

\displaystyle=f(\varphi_{1}^{-1}(v))+(-1)^{|u||v|}g(\varphi_{1}^{-1}(u))

\displaystyle=f(v)+(-1)^{|u||v|}g(u)

\displaystyle=B(u+\Pi(f),v+\Pi(g)).

Therefore,
\varphi
is an isometric isomorphism.
∎

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space and let
\Omega:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\Pi(\mathfrak{h}^{*})

for every non-zero

. Define a bilinear map

\Omega(u,v)(w):=\tilde{\Omega}(u,v,w),\quad\forall u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h}.

Then
\Omega
is non-degenerate. Moreover, since
\tilde{\Omega}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{h})_{\bar{1}}

Hence, we obtain a one-to-one correspondence between the set of reduced

and the set

\left\{\tilde{\Omega}\in\mathcal{T}^{3}(\mathfrak{h})_{\bar{1}}\,\middle|\,\iota_{u}\tilde{\Omega}\neq 0\text{ for all }u\in\mathfrak{h}\setminus\{0\}\right\}.

This leads to the following corollary:

Corollary
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet_{\Omega_{1}})
and
(\mathcal{B},\bullet_{\Omega_{2}})

\Pi(T^{*}_{\Omega})

\mathbb{Z}_{2}

for all

Corollary

There is a bijective correspondence between the isometry classes of reduced odd-quadratic 2-step nilpotent symmetric Leibniz algebras and the equivalence classes of odd trilinear forms belonging to the set

Corollary
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a odd-quadratic 2-step nilpotent symmetric Leibniz algebra. Then
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is isometrically isomorphic to
(\mathfrak{A}:=I\oplus\Pi(T^{*}_{\Omega}(U)),\langle\;,\;\rangle)
, where

,

(I,\langle\;,\;\rangle_{I})
is a odd pseudo-Euclidean
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space,
and
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is defined by

\langle x+u+\Pi(f),\;y+v+\Pi(g)\rangle=\langle x,y\rangle_{I}+B(u+\Pi(f),\;v+\Pi(g)),

for all

and

is the canonical invariant bilinear form on the

Proof.

Since

According to Lemma

{\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}=\operatorname{Ann}({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)^{\perp},

which implies that
{\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}

be a complementary subspace of

\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mid U\times U}

5.
Double extensions of flat pseudo-Euclidean Leibniz superalgebras

In this section, we introduce the notion of a double extension of flat even (resp. odd) pseudo-Euclidean left-Leibniz (resp. Lie) superalgebras by a one-dimensional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space. This construction is carried out in two distinct steps: a central extension, followed by a semi-direct product. In the first step, we define the central extension of pre-left Leibniz superalgebra by a one-dimensional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space. In the second step, we introduce the semi-direct product of pre-left Leibniz superalgebra by a one-dimensional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space. After establishing these two operations, we present the general notion of a double extension of flat pseudo-Euclidean left Leibniz superalgebras. This construction is inspired by the double extension method developed by A. Medina and P. Revoy in their study of quadratic Lie algebras
[
37
]
. We show that every flat even (resp. odd) pseudo-Euclidean non-Lie left Leibniz superalgebra can be obtained by a double extension procedure.

5.1.
Central extensions

We now introduce the notion of central extension of pre-left Leibniz superalgebras.

Proposition
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\star,\circ)
be a pre-left Leibniz superalgebra, and let
V=\mathbb{K}e
be a one-dimensional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space. Let
\mu,\gamma:\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{K}
be two homogeneous bilinear maps of given degrees. Consider the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{A}\oplus\mathbb{K}e
, and define two bilinear operations
(\star_{\mu},\circ_{\gamma})
on
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
by

(u+\alpha e)\star_{\mu}(v+\beta e)=u\star v+\mu(u,v)e,\quad(u+\alpha e)\circ_{\gamma}(v+\beta e)=u\circ v+\gamma(u,v)e,

for all
u,v\in\mathcal{A}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{K}
.
Then,
(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\star_{\mu},\circ_{\gamma})
defines a pre-left Leibniz superalgebra if and only if the bilinear maps
\mu
and
\gamma
satisfy the following identities:

(29)

\displaystyle\mu(u\star v,w)-\mu(u,v\star w)

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{|u||v|}\left(\mu(v\circ u,w)+\mu(v,u\star w)\right),

(30)

\displaystyle\gamma(u\star v,w)-\mu(u,v\circ w)

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{|u||v|}\left(\gamma(v\circ u,w)-\gamma(v,u\circ w)\right),

(31)

\displaystyle\gamma(u,v\circ w)

\displaystyle=-\gamma(u,v\star w),

for all
u,v,w\in\mathcal{A}
.

In this case,
(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\star_{\mu},\circ_{\nu})
is called the central extension of
\mathcal{A}
by
\mathbb{K}e
by means of
(\mu,\nu)
.

Proof.

Straightforward calculation.
∎

Remark
.

When the pre-left Leibniz superalgebra
(\mathcal{A},\star,\circ)
satisfies
\star=-\circ
, i.e., when
\mathcal{A}
is a left-symmetric superalgebra, the conditions (
29
)-(
31
) reduce to a single identity, as shown in the corollary below.

Given that left symmetric superalgebras constitute a subclass of pre-left Leibniz superalgebra, we also consider the particular case of central extension of left symmetric superalgebars.

Corollary
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\star)
be a left symmetric superalgebra, and let
V=\mathbb{K}e
be a one-dimensional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space. Let
\mu:\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{K}
be a homogeneous bilinear map of given degree. Consider the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{A}\oplus\mathbb{K}e
, endowed with the product defined by

(u+\alpha e)\star_{\mu}(v+\beta e)=u\star v+\mu(u,v)e,

for all
u,v\in\mathcal{A}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{K}
.
Then,
(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\star_{\mu})
is a left symmetric superalgebra if and only if the bilinear map
\mu
and satisfies the following identity (for all
u,v,w\in\mathcal{A}
):

(32)

\displaystyle\mu(u\star v,w)-\mu(u,v\star w)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|u||v|}\left(\mu(v\star u,w)-\mu(v,u\star w)\right).

In this case,
(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\star_{\mu})
is called the central extension of
\mathcal{A}
by
\mathbb{K}e
by means of
\mu
.

Proof.

A left-symmetric superalgebra is a particular case of a pre-left Leibniz superalgebra
(\mathcal{A},\star,\circ)
in which the two products are related by
\star=-\circ
. In this case, the relations (
29
)-(
31
) reduce to a single identity. Thus, the proof follows.
∎

Proposition
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a flat pseudo-Euclidean left Leibniz superalgebra, and let
(\star,\circ)
be its associated Levi-Civita products. Let
\mu,\gamma:\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{K}
be two bilinear maps of degrees
\alpha
and
\beta
, respectively. Then, there exist two homogeneous endomorphisms
G,D:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
of degrees
\alpha
and
\beta
, such that

\mu(u,v)=\langle G(u),v\rangle,\quad\gamma(u,v)=\langle D(u),v\rangle,\quad\text{for all }u,v\in\mathcal{A}.

The bilinear maps
\mu
and
\gamma
satisfy relations (
29
)–(
31
) if and only if the following conditions hold for all
u,v\in\mathcal{A}

(i)

G(u\bullet v)=-(-1)^{|u|\alpha}u\circ G(v)+(-1)^{|v|(\alpha+|u|)}v\circ G(u)
,

(ii)

D(u\bullet v)=(-1)^{|u|\beta}u\star D(v)+(-1)^{|v|(\alpha+|u|)}v\star G(u)
,

(iii)

v\star D(u)=-v\circ D(u)
.

Proof.

We prove assertion (i), the other relations follow by similar computations.

Let
u,v,w\in\mathcal{A}
. Then,

\displaystyle\mu(u\star v,w)-\mu(u,v\star w)+(-1)^{|u||v|}\left(\mu(v\circ u,w)+\mu(v,u\star w)\right)

\displaystyle=\langle G(u\star v),w\rangle-\langle G(u),v\star w\rangle+(-1)^{|u||v|}\left(\langle G(v\circ u),w\rangle+\langle G(v),u\star w\rangle\right).

Using the (
11
) and symmetry of the bilinear form
\langle\;,\;\rangle

\displaystyle\langle G(u),v\star w\rangle

\displaystyle=(-1)^{(|v|+|w|)(\alpha+|u|)}\langle v\star w,G(u)\rangle

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|v||w|+|v|(\alpha+|u|)}\langle w,v\circ G(u)\rangle=(-1)^{|v|(\alpha+|u|)}\langle v\circ G(u),w\rangle.

Similarly,

\langle G(v),u\star w\rangle=(-1)^{|u|(\alpha+|v|)}\langle u\circ G(v),w\rangle.

Combining all terms, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle G(u\star v),w\rangle-(-1)^{|v|(\alpha+|u|)}\langle v\circ G(u),w\rangle+(-1)^{|u||v|}\langle G(v\circ u),w\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad+(-1)^{|u|\alpha}\langle u\circ G(v),w\rangle

6.
Conclusion and outlook

This article studies flat pseudo-Euclidean Leibniz superalgebras and introduces the concept of
double extension
for these algebras. We prove that every flat pseudo-Euclidean
non-Lie
Leibniz superalgebra can be constructed via successive double extensions starting from a flat pseudo-Euclidean
Lie
superalgebra, see Corollary

5.4.6.  Corollary
and
5.5.4.  Corollary
. As a consequence, the characterization of flat pseudo-Euclidean Leibniz superalgebras is reduced to the study of flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie superalgebras.

The study of flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebras is well-established in several specific contexts. The foundational work began with Milnor’s characterization of the flat Euclidean case
[
39
]
. This was later extended to Lorentzian 2-step nilpotent algebras by Guediri
[
27
]
. A significant generalization was achieved by Aubert and Medina
[
6
]
, who introduced the powerful concept of the double extension to provide a full characterization of flat Lorentzian nilpotent Lie algebras.
A complete classification of such algebras was given in
[
9
]
, and further results can be found in
[
2
,
19
]
.
In particular, all flat pseudo-Euclidean nilpotent Lie algebras of signature
(2,n-2)
can be described via double extensions, see
[
20
]
.
Motivated by these results, we plan to investigate other classes of flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebras and Lie superalgebras in future work.
For instance, we intend to study flat even pseudo-Euclidean Lie superalgebras whose even part
(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\bar{0}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\bar{0}})
is a flat Euclidean Lie algebra.
Such structures are expected to yield new families of flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie superalgebras and Leibniz superalgebras.

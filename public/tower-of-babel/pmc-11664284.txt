Quality attributes, antioxidant activity and color profile of orange-fleshed sweet potato grown in Vietnam
Authors: Nguyen Chi Dung, Tran Ngoc Giau, Hong Van Hao, Vo Quang Minh, Nguyen Minh Thuy
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study analyzed the nutritional composition, bioactive compounds, antioxidant activities and estimated potential renal acid load (PRAL) score of orange-fleshed sweet potato (OFSP) grown in Vietnam. In addition, important functional groups of carotenoid compounds in OFSP were identified by Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR); carotenoid pigments were detected and quantified by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC). The results showed that, OFSP had outstanding physicochemical characteristics, containing many nutrients, bioactive substances, minerals and fiber. The negative PRAL score (< 0 mEq/day) of OFSP indicated that this was an alkaline food. FTIR spectroscopy provides a versatile tool for food analysis, and the analysis results showed that the carotenoid composition in OFSP grown in Vietnam have contained 14 complete functional groups detected, with 14 spectral peaks spanning the wavelength range from 433.98 cm−1 to 3288.63 cm−1. HPLC analysis identified the main carotenoid pigment in OFSP as β-carotene. The detailed evaluation of the physicochemical characteristics, functional groups and colorants in OFSP has shown that it is possible to use this raw material to develop different food products, playing a useful role in vitamin A deficiency management and improving the quality of human life.Keywords: Physical and chemical properties, Bioactive compounds, Antioxidant activity, Color profile, Orange-fleshed sweet potato

Introduction

Sweet potato (Ipomoea batatas L.) is a perennial herbaceous plant belonging to the family Convolvuceae. Binh Tan District, Vinh Long province of Vietnam, is known as the largest sweet potato growing area in the Mekong Delta with many famous sweet potato varieties. They are the main export products of Binh Tan District with a planting area of about 991.8 ha, yielding about 29.5 tons/ha (General Statistics Office of Vietnam, 2022). This tuber has developed into an economically important agricultural crop and is now considered one of the seven most important staple food crops in the world, ranking fifth after rice, wheat, maize and cassava.

OFSP has been used to diversify the human diet due to its richness in β-carotene (provitamin A), which acts as a primary color and has vitamin A activity. Carotenoids have a strong antioxidant capacity to scavenge free radicals due to their conjugated double bonds. Some other carotenoids such as β-cryptoxanthin, α-carotene also have vitamin A activity and antioxidant properties, together with lycopene, lutein and zeaxanthin. Lutein is the major xanthophyll species in plants, accounting for approximately 40 % of total carotenoids in leaves (Dall'Osto et al., 2006). Lutein and zeaxanthin have been shown to be associated with a reduced incidence of macular degeneration and cataracts.

OFSP is a good source of indigestible fiber, minerals, various vitamins and antioxidants (Dako et al., 2016; Rodrigues et al., 2016). Resistant starch present in sweet potato is also considered as a dietary fiber component. This is not easily digested and absorbed in the small intestine, is fermented in the colon by intestinal microflora and produces short-chain fatty acids. Phenolic and carotenoid compounds are responsible for the distinct color of the flesh and skin of sweet potatoes along with antioxidant properties, which play an important role in health, especially in cancer prevention and cardiovascular disease prevention. The antioxidant and free radical scavenging activities of phenolic components in OFSP provide many health benefits. Furthermore, to determine the nutritional characteristics of foods when eaten, the potential renal acid load (PRAL) score is used to estimate the potential acid load of foods. PRAL can be estimated for any food and then classified according to its acid or base-releasing ability. A food with a positive PRAL is considered acid-forming, while a negative PRAL indicates that the food is more alkaline.

In addition, in food analysis, infrared spectroscopy provides a versatile tool for the identification of major and minor components in foods and their geographical origins. Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy allows fast, high-throughput, accurate and nondestructive analysis by operating in the mid-infrared region of the electromagnetic spectrum. FTIR spectroscopy can be efficiently employed in a wide variety of disciplines, such as food, pharmaceuticals, cosmetics, chemicals since functional groups of the covalently bonded molecules have shown infrared absorption (Cebi et al., 2023). According to the main principle, the functional groups of the analyte absorb infrared light at a specific wave number (cm−1) and this specific wave number can be assigned to molecular structures (Rodriguez-Saona et al., 2015). Besides, the carotenoid color profile of OFSP has also been studied. OFSP grown in China has been identified as the main color pigments β-carotene, (9Z)-β-carotene and α-carotene (Kourouma et al., 2019). Kammona et al. (2015) found carotenoids in Malaysian OFSP including lutein, zeaxanthin, α-carotene, and β-carotene. Donado-Pestana et al. (2012) reported carotenoids of OFSP grown in Brazil including all-trans-β-carotene, 13-cis-β-carotene, 9-cis-β-carotene, 5,6 epoxy-β-carotene, lutein, zeaxanthin from HPLC analysis.

Thus, despite the large production of sweet potatoes grown in Binh Tan district, Vinh Long province of Vietnam, the price of sweet potatoes has dropped dramatically every year, and farmers in Binh Tan district, Vinh Long do not know who to sell to. They have to ask for help or donate their sweet potatoes to charity. The reason may be that people do not understand the outstanding nutritional value of sweet potatoes, so they have little access to it. Moreover, there has been no comprehensive report on the physicochemical properties, bioactive compounds, antioxidant activity, functional groups and carotenoid pigments of OFSP grown in this area. Therefore, determining all the properties contained in OFSP will raise consumers' awareness of the nutritional value of sweet potatoes, their positive effects on health and encourage them to use more. At the same time, it opens up directions for researchers in diversified processing and increasing the export of both raw materials and processed products, increasing the economic value of this item both domestically and for export. This study aims to provide detailed information on the nutritional value, bioactive compounds, antioxidant activity, along with functional chemical groups and colors of OFSP grown in Binh Tan, Vinh Long, Vietnam. The use of OFSP for its nutritional value, as a source of natural food antioxidants and food colors will create opportunities to simultaneously increase production and consumption, and ultimately, hopefully, increase income for gardeners in our country.

Materials and methods

Orange-fleshed sweet potato harvesting

Orange-fleshed sweet potato (local name: “Khoai lang Nghệ”) tubers were harvested at farms in Binh Tan district, Vinh Long province, Vietnam. The harvest time at approximately 90 to 120 days after sowing when the stem and leaves turn yellow and fall off a lot.

Physical properties analysis

The length (cm) and width (cm) of OFSP were measured using a digital caliper (accuracy 0.01 mm). The weight (g) was measured using a digital balance (sensitivity 0.01 g). Color values (L*, a* and b*) were read from a Hunter Lab Colorimeter (Color Flex, USA). Structural properties were analyzed using a Brookfield CT3, equipped with a 1500 g force sensor and software version 1.8 (Brookfield Engineering Laboratories, Middleboro, MA, USA).

Chemical components, bioactive compounds and antioxidant activity analysis

Moisture, protein, lipid, crude fiber and ash contents were analyzed according to the standard method of AOAC (2005). Carbohydrate content of the sample was calculated according to AOAC (1980). Resistant starch (RS) was analyzed according to the method published by McCleary et al. (2002). Amylose content was analyzed according to the method of Avaro et al. (2011), the absorbance was measured using a UV/Vis spectrophotometer (Shimadzu, Kyoto, Japan), the amylose content was determined. The amylopectin content (%) was then calculated by subtracting the amylose (%) from 100 %. Total carotenoid content (TCC) (mg/100 g) was determined using acetone-petroleum ether extraction followed by spectrophotometric analysis, as described by Kotíková et al. (2011). Total polyphenol content (TPC) (mgGAE/g) was determined by the Folin-Ciocalteu colorimetric method (Philpott et al., 2004). Total flavonoid content (TFC) was determined by the AlCl3 colorimetric method (Chang et al., 2002). Quercetin was used to construct the standard curve and TFC was expressed as milligrams of quercetin equivalents per gram (mgQE/g).

The antioxidant activity was analyzed using 1,1-diphenyl-2-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) and 2,2′-azinobis-(3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulfonic acid) (ABTS.+) free radical scavenging assays, with IC50 values representing the concentration of antioxidant required to scavenge 50 % of the free radicals (Awika et al., 2003; Brand-Williams et al., 1995).

Carotenoid identification

Preparation of OFSP powder

After harvesting, the OFSP tubers were collected, sorted, washed with tap water and drained. The tubers were peeled and sliced using a specialized food machine (Mandoline, Zhejiang, China), the slice thickness was about 2 mm. The sweet potato slices were then blanched with steam at boiling temperature (100 °C) for 3 min to prevent enzymatic browning. The blanched samples were then dried in a drying oven (Memmert, UN260, Germany) at 50 °C for 8 h to a moisture content of 6–7 %. The sweet potato slices were further ground, sieved and collected to obtain particles with a size of 60 μm. The powder was packed in dark bags and stored at room temperature (25 ± 2 °C) for HPLC analysis.

Carotenoid extraction

To extract carotenoids, weighing 0.1 g of the sample and placing it in a screw-cap tube with beads in a 1:1 ratio to the sample. Add 1 mL of ethanol containing 0.5 mM butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT) and shake vigorously in a bead blender for 2 min and 30 s. Transfer the ethanol mixture, sample, and beads into a 15 mL tube, then rinse the screw-cap tube three times with 1 mL of acetone each time, transferring the rinses to the same 15 mL tube. Add 3 mL of petroleum ether and vortex thoroughly, followed by 8 mL of 20 % NaCl, and vortex again. After centrifuging the mixture at 3000 rpm for 10 min, extract the supernatant, adjust the volume (mass-up), and dry the organic phase using Na₂SO₄. Finally, filter the solution through a polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) membrane filter (diameter: 13 mm, pore size: 0.2 μm) to prepare the sample for analysis.

HPLC analysis conditions

For HPLC analysis of carotenoids, a Waters 2489 liquid chromatography system (Waters, USA) equipped with a Kinetex C18 column (particle size: 2.6 μm, pore size: 100 Å, length × internal diameter: 100 × 4.60 mm; Phenomenex, USA) was used. The mobile phase consisted of 75 % methanol (Phase A) and 100 % ethyl acetate (Phase B). The separation conditions were as follows: 70 % B from 0 to 10 min, 100 % B from 10 to 14 min, 0 % B from 14 to 14.01 min, with the column re-equilibrated from 14.01 to 20 min. The flow rate was maintained at 1 mL per minute throughout the run. For quantification, β-carotene and lycopene (Sigma Co., USA) were used as standards, and the total carotenoid content was measured by detecting absorbance at 450 nm and 660 nm.

Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) technology

Basically, Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) works on the principle of absorption of an infrared light source on the sample. An intense wavelength of infrared light is passed through the sample and the FTIR instrument measures the absorption wavelengths. FTIR technology has considerable potential as a quantitative quality control tool. IR spectroscopy (Shimazu Iraffinity-1S, Japan) was used to analyze the functional groups in the structure of carotenoid pigments in OFSP. Absorption spectra were recorded with resolution of 2 cm−1 within a wavenumber range from 4000 to 400 cm−1 (30 scans per spectrum) (Cao et al., 2014).

Statistical analysis

Statgraphic Centurion XV·I statistical software (USA) was used to analyze the data. Significant differences in the obtained data were analyzed by the variance method (ANOVA) at P ≤ 0.05.

The length and width of the sweet potato are related to the weight of OFSP. Normally, sweet potatoes are harvested when most of the tubers reach the desired size for the consumer market. Hung (2017) reported that the sweet potato harvest time is usually between 102 and 134 days (from the date of sowing) for crops in a year. The minimum and maximum weights of sweet potatoes that meet commercial standards are 50 g and 350 g, respectively (Tan et al., 2014). The OFSP measurements in this study are quite consistent with the commercial tuber standards of sweet potato varieties in Vietnam. The longer the harvest time, the more the sweet potato tubers continue to grow, especially the growth in tuber diameter due to cell elongation, accompanied by the accumulation of starch and protein in the tuber flesh. However, the larger the sweet potato tubers, the more susceptible they are to insect pests or tuber cracking, so sweet potatoes are often harvested when the commercial tuber weight and diameter have not reached the maximum level to ensure the purchasing process from traders.

Texture, or mouth-feel, is a major attribute in deciding overall consumer acceptance of sweetpotato cultivars. The measured hardness of OFSP varieties ranged from 80.82 to 92.10 N (mean value 87.39 N), which is quite similar to published values. Toyama et al. (2003) reported the hardness of eight sweet potato varieties ranging from 76.4 to 112.3 N. Dong et al. (2020) reported the average hardness of 35 sweet potato varieties as 104.35 N. Moisture, starch, and amylose contents significantly correlated with hardness of the tuber (Utomo & Ginting, 2023).

The color of the potato flesh is due to its different nutritional and phytochemical components (Rose & Vasanthakaalam, 2011; Tanaka et al., 2017). The levels of these components determine the color of the flesh, the darker the color, the more pigment (Amoanimaa-Dede et al., 2020). Yellow- and orange-fleshed varieties tend to contain more β-carotene, so this was the main focus of this study.

L*, a*, and b* values represent the color value of sweet potatoes, as well as visual color perception. For OFSP possessing a deep orange carotenoid pigment (β-carotene), the L* value representing the brightness ranging from 0 (dark/black) to 100 (light/white) of the tuber flesh ranged from 71.03 to 74.89 (average value of 72.86). The a* value ranged from green (−100) to red (+100) with measured values ranging from 16.26 to 21.55 (average value of 19.02). The b* value ranged from blue (−100) to yellow (+100) with values ranging from 34.48 to 43.10 (average value of 39.01). The deep orange color of OFSP depends on the content of β-carotene pigment. The color of the flesh is also related to its nutritional, flavor and textural properties. OFSP with a uniform yellow flesh color throughout the tuber is typically rich and sweet in flavor, while white sweet potatoes or tubers with pale yellow/light red-orange flesh are said to have a creamy texture but are drier, not as sweet or moist as OFSP variety. The orange pigment of sweet potatoes comes from β-carotene, the most active form of vitamin A, which is essential for night vision and is also noted for its anti-cancer, anti-aging and anti-ulcer properties due to its antioxidant activity (Grune et al., 2010). Therefore, OFSP deserves to be included in the list of staple foods that have important effects on human health.

Nutritional composition of OFSP

The moisture content of OFSP tubers ranged from 70.72 to 71.56 % (average value of 71.12 %) (Table 2). The analyzed values were slightly lower than those reported for nine OFSP varieties in Bangladesh, ranging from 70.95 to 72.96 % (Alam et al., 2016), much lower than the moisture content reported by Laurie et al. (2022), ranging from 77.07 to 82.40 %. Moisture content is the main component of OFSP, accounting for >62 % and < 75 % (Dako et al., 2016; Nicanuru et al., 2015). The difference in moisture content may be due to the variety of diversity, climatic conditions of the growing area and the applied cultivation methods.

The protein content of OFSP ranged from 1.98 to 2.15 % (average value of 2.07 %) (Table 2). This result is quite similar to the data reported in some previous studies (Alam et al., 2016), ranging from 1.91 to 5.83 %. Protein is an important nutrient in human nutrition, providing essential amino acids necessary for metabolism.

The lipid content of OFSP ranged from 0.59 to 0.62 % (average value of 0.61 %), which is quite similar to the lipid content reported by Mu et al. (2009) of 0.6 %. Ishida et al. (2000) reported lower lipid content (0.2–0.33 %) in sweet potatoes. Roots and tubers generally contain low lipid content.

The average ash value analyzed was 0.85 %, which is lower than that reported by Alam et al. (2016), 1.17–1.31 % and higher than that reported by Rose and Vasanthakaalam (2011), 0.40–0.44 %.

The fiber content of OFSP grown in Vietnam ranged from 3.2 to 3.26 % (mean value 3.23 %). This result is similar to Ishida et al. (2000), whose published values ranged from 2.28 % to 11.7 %, but is slightly higher than the published value of OFSP grown in Brazil (2.57 %) (Rodrigues et al., 2016). The differences in content may be due to genetic characteristics, growing conditions of the crop, and analytical methods (Wang et al., 2016). Pectin, cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin are considered dietary fiber. Dietary fiber has recently received attention due to its potential to reduce the incidence of colon cancer, diabetes, heart disease, and some digestive diseases (Rose & Vasanthakaalam, 2011). OFSP varieties with relatively high fiber content can be used for the development of functional foods.

Carbohydrates play an important role in food as a major source of energy, and are also an important source of dietary fiber that affects the physiological processes of the body. OFSP can be consumed as a staple crop due to its high carbohydrate content. The carbohydrate content analysis of OFSP grown in Vinh Long, Vietnam ranged from 24.58 to 24.94 % (average value of 24.80 %). This value is within the range of published values from several previous studies, with values ranging from 18.3 % to 28 % (Alam et al., 2016; Nicanuru et al., 2015). The variety and stage of maturity of the tuber also influence the content of this nutrient.

Sweet potatoes contain high levels of starch. Sweet potato starch also contains high levels of resistant starch (Thuy et al., 2022). This form of starch normally escapes digestion in the small intestine of healthy individuals but is completely or partially fermented in the colon. RS has been shown to have potential health benefits, including lowering blood glucose, lowering blood cholesterol, acting as a functional microbial enzyme, and increasing the production of short-chain fatty acids in the large intestine (Bojarczuk et al., 2022).

The RS content of OFSP grown in Vietnam ranges from 10.21 to 10.24 % (average value of 10.22 %) (Table 2). This result is higher than the RS in orange-fleshed sweet potatoes of Juhwangmi and Pungwonmi varieties, which were 6.46 % and 1.76 %, respectively (Kim et al., 2020). However, the analyzed values from this study were lower than the published results of Senanayake et al. (2013), with values ranging from 13.2 to 17.2 %. The analyzed values from the study are consistent with the published data, ranging from 3 to 68 g/100 g (Song et al., 2014). The differences in the RS content of sweet potatoes are explained by differences in their amylose/amylopectin ratios. The RS content of sweet potatoes also varies depending on the phenotype, pre−/post-harvest treatment, and geographical location (Amoanimaa-Dede et al., 2020).

The amylose and amylopectin contents of the Vietnamese OFSP variety were analyzed and determined to be 17.42 % and 82.58 %, respectively. Abegunde et al. (2013) reported that the amylose and amylopectin contents varied significantly among sweet potato starches of different varieties, with values ranging from 13.33 to 26.83 % and 73.17 to 86.67 %, respectively. Wang et al. (2016) reported that the amylose content of sweet potato starch varied widely (from 0 to 34.16 %). The amylose and amylopectin contents significantly affect the properties and functions of starch. Starches with high amylose content form cross-links early and form gels, resulting in high starch viscosity. The differences in amylose content of sweet potato starch are due to differences in genotype, environmental factors, and measurement methods.

The vitamin C content of OFSP analyzed in this study was 5.26 ± 0.33 mg/100 g FW (Table 2). Fortunately, the data obtained from this study were quite consistent with the previous studies (Alam et al., 2020; Aywa et al., 2013) with values ranging from 4.85 to 5.73 mg/100 g FW. However, the lower values was reported by Eke-Ejiofor and Onyeso (2019), which was 0.08 mg/100 g FW. When consumed with other foods in the same meal, vitamin C in OFSP contributes to the absorption of heme iron in the body (Krisnanda, 2020).

Minerals play an important role in metabolism. The macromineral contents such as calcium (Ca), magnesium (Mg), potassium (K), and phosphorus (P) in OFSP varieties grown in Vietnam are presented in Table 2. The analyzed calcium content in OFSP Vietnam was 5.33 ± 0.02 mg/100 g FW (≈ 18.46 mg/100 g dry weight (DW)). This value is slightly lower than that reported by Alam et al. (2020), which was 21.98–27.35 mg/100 g DW. Calcium content in tubers may vary according to cultivar, climate, and soil of growing areas.

The Mg content of OFSP grown in Vietnam ranged from 9.2 to 9.72 mg/100 g (average value 9.47 mg/100 g FW, equivalent to 32.79 mg/100 g DW) (Table 2). The analyzed values from this study were higher than those of OFSP varieties reported by Dako et al. (2016) and Alam et al. (2020), which were 3.00 mg/100 g FW and 21.28–25.40 mg/100 g DW, respectively. The variation in Mg content may be due to variety, climate, soil type, location and other factors. Mg plays an important role in calcium metabolism, aids in blood pressure regulation and insulin release, so consuming this sweet potato variety may contribute to the above functions in the body (Alam et al., 2020).

The P content of OFSP grown in Vietnam was 11.89 ± 0.05 mg/100 g FW (equivalent to 41.17 mg/100 g DW) (Table 2). This value is quite similar to the publication of Alam et al. (2020), the content ranged from 41.98 to 45.29 mg/100 g DW, higher than the publication of Dako et al. (2016), the content ranged from 19.22 to 24.50 mg/100 g for unpeeled potatoes and 15.70–21.80 mg/100 g for peeled potatoes. Laurie et al. (2012) concluded that low P content and low pH of the cultivation land can result in low P content in sweet potato samples. In addition, phosphorus remains the most appreciated mineral in starch due to its influence on functional properties. Swelling strength, clarity, paste stability, peak viscosity and degradation, onset and peak gelatinization temperatures have been reported to increase with increasing levels of phosphates (Karim et al., 2007; Noda et al., 2007). The increase of phosphorus bound to starch affects the colloidal ability, characterized by an increase in the swelling strength and solubility of the particles, a decrease in the gelatinization temperature, increased paste clarity and gel viscosity. In addition, starch pastes exhibited better resistance to retrogradation. These changes in properties broaden the uses of starches by industries (Leonel et al., 2016), and also create favorable conditions for using sweet potato starch in the processing of food products later.

Compared to other mineral elements, the K content in OFSP had the highest value, ranging from 48.9 to 49.77 mg/100 g (average value 49.38 mg/100 g FW, equivalent to 170.98 mg/100 g DW) (Table 2). However, the analyzed value from this study was lower than the values published by Alam et al. (2020), 310.04 to 368.35 mg/100 g DW. Sweet potato is a crop that requires a lot of potassium compared to other tuber crops (Walter et al., 2011). When grown in different soils, sweet potato plants also require different amounts of potassium (George et al., 2002).

Bioactive compounds and antioxidant activities of OFSP

The analysis results showed that the total carotenoid content (TCC) of OFSP grown in Vietnam ranged from 3.83 to 3.87 mg/100 g FW (average value 3.85 mg/100 g FW) (Table 2). This result is quite similar to the published data of Alam et al. (2016), with the TCC in nine OFSP varieties ranging from 0.38 to 7.24 mg/100 g FW. However, it is lower than the TCC in some OFSP varieties published by Donado-Pestana et al. (2012) with TCC content ranging from 7.91 to 12.85 mg/100 g. TCC in OFSP can vary depending on the variety and growing conditions, with weather being an important factor influencing TCC. Climatic temperature also affects TCC in fruits and vegetables; tropical climates are more favorable for carotenoid synthesis (Grace et al., 2014). High color intensity of fruits and vegetables is known to contain higher carotenoid content (Kammona et al., 2015). OFSP varieties have been recognized as excellent sources of β-carotene, with light orange flesh varieties containing lower TCC than deep orange varieties (Alam et al., 2016).

Polyphenols are an important group of secondary plant metabolites that play various roles in plants. The presence and content of total polyphenols are often genetically determined. OFSP is gaining importance as an inexpensive source of antioxidants, providing several essential health benefits, being rich in carotenoids, β-carotene, and TPC. The analytical results showed that OFSP grown in Vietnam contained TPC ranging from 1.09 to 1.18 mgGAE/g FW (average value of 1.13 mgGAE/g FW) (Table 2), which is higher than the phenolic content of hand-peeled OFSP grown in the United States which ranged from 0.06 to 0.23 mgGAE/g FW (Teow et al., 2007), while Truong et al. (2007) reported TPC of OFSP 0.57 to 0.79 mg/g FW. In addition, Alam et al. (2016) reported TPC in OFSP variety (T101) grown in Bangladesh as 0.95–1.37 mgGAE/g FW. Varietal differences had the greatest influence on bioactive compound content in sweet potatoes (Teow et al., 2007). The differences in TPC may be due to genetic factors, which are one of the most important factors in the production of secondary metabolites, including phenolic compounds (Donado-Pestana et al., 2012). Cultivation methods also influence TPC, organically grown sweet potatoes contain higher levels of phenolics than conventionally grown sweet potatoes (Evers & Deußer, 2012).

Flavonoids are components with high antioxidant activity, thus supporting human health. The analytical results showed that the TFC in OFSP grown in Vietnam was 3.37 mgQE/g FW (Table 2). This result is quite similar to the reported results of Makori et al. (2020), with TFC ranging from 1.0 to 4.6 mgQE/g. The TFC varied between different colored sweet potatoes (Park et al., 2016).

The antioxidant activity of OFSP extract showed differences when determined by DPPH and ABTS•+ free radical scavenging methods. The IC50 DPPH and IC50 ABTS values are the concentration of sample required for inhibition of 50 % of DPPH and 50 % of ABTS radicals, respectively. The antioxidant was evaluated based on this IC50 value, the lowest IC50 value means the highest antioxidant activity. OFSP contains many carotenoids and phenolic compounds, both of which contribute to the free radical scavenging efficiency.

The measured IC50 value of DPPH and IC50 value of ABTS•+ of OFSP were 3.04 mg/mL and 0.35 mg/mL, respectively (Table 2). Fidrianny et al. (2018) reported four Indonesian sweet potato cultivars with ethanol extracts having the lowest and highest IC50 values of DPPH of 45.05 μg/mL and 98.18 μg/mL. Teow et al. (2007) demonstrated that purple flesh sweet potato tubers had the highest DPPH antioxidant activity, followed by orange, yellow, and the lowest was white. The IC50 measurement results of DPPH published from the above studies were lower than the results on OFSP varieties grown in Vietnam. Light, oxygen, and pH also affect the free radical scavenging activity of DPPH. Phenolic compounds and flavonoids are the main antioxidant components in some plants. Postharvest storage time (4 or 8 months) reduces the DPPH radical scavenging activity of sweet potatoes.

The IC50 values of ABTS of OFSP ranged from 0.32 to 0.384 mg/mL (Table 2). Im et al. (2021) reported that the free radical scavenging activity of ABTS ranged from 6.10 to 7.66 mgTEAC (Trolox equivalent antioxidant capacity)/g DW. Meanwhile, Terahara et al. (2004) reported the ABTS value of ‘Jami’ sweet potato with the highest ABTS value (62.20 mgTEAC/g extract). The differences between the IC50 values of ABTS of the varieties may be due to the influence of the extraction process, extraction conditions and methods, solvents used, geographical location and soil conditions, etc. The differences in antioxidant activity and polyphenol content may be explained by the amount of rainfall received by each harvest season. When water deficit occurs, secondary metabolites can be produced to protect against water deficit. Kim et al. (2019) reported that DPPH and ABTS values among sweet potato cultivars ranged from 1.91 to 36.29 mg Trolox equivalent (TE)/g extract and 17.77–62.20 mgTE/g extract, respectively.

Estimation of PRAL score of OFSP variety

OFSP PRAL score have been estimated based on several nutritional components. The results presented in Table 3 showed that OFSP has a negative PRAL (−2.08), thus it can be considered the largest source of buffering in the diet due to its potassium content which participates in acid-base balance by supporting electrolyte neutralization through exchange with hydrogen ions in the distal part of the nephron (Osuna-Padilla et al., 2019). Diets with negative score (PRAL <0) increase the production of alkali precursors and such a food increases the alkalinity of the body fluids. Foods with lower PRAL scores are recommended for patients with chronic kidney disease to prevent metabolic acidosis (Sravani et al., 2024). Diets with low PRAL scores also can be used to predict and improve urinary acidity in a relatively short period of time (Adair & Bowden, 2020). A reduced muscle reserve or greater muscle loss has been documented in individuals who consume diets with high acid load (positive PRAL) (Chan et al., 2015; Faure et al., 2017; Welch et al., 2013). In the elderly, reduced musculature is associated with an increased risk of frailty; a high prevalence of frailty in Japanese women consuming diets with high estimated PRAL (Kataya et al., 2018). From the useful information mentioned, it is clear that OFSP can be considered as a healthy food for human beings.

Identification of functional groups of carotenoid compounds from OFSP

FTIR is one of the most powerful tools for recognizing chemical bonds (functional groups) in compounds. As observed in the annotated spectrum, the wavelength of absorbed light is typical of the chemical bonds. The chemical bonds in the molecule can be established by reading the infrared absorption spectrum. From the FTIR analysis, OFSP grown in Vietnam had 14 spectral peaks spanning from 3288.63 cm−1 to 433.98 cm−1 (Fig. 2). In the study, the carotenoid pigment of OFSP exhibited some peaks that were slightly shifted according to the difference in intensity. The appearance of a sharp peak with a wide area of the infrared spectrum in the OFSP carotenoid pigment. The appearance of a sharp, broadband peak between 3500 cm−1 and 3000 cm−1 was noted to be due to the hydroxyl group (-OH) and hydrogen bonding, resulting in complex vibrational stretching involving the hydroxyl group (Zhang et al., 2012). The characteristic peak at 3000–2881 cm−1 was due to the asymmetric stretching vibration of hydrocarbon (C—H) bonds from the glucose ring (Zuo et al., 2019). Small changes, especially for the connected bands for C—H and O—H bonds, may indicate the stability of the intermolecular and intramolecular hydrogen bonds or the amylose and amylopectin content of the OFSP starch structure.

FTIR analysis of functional groups in carotenoid pigments from OFSP.

The most promising bands distinguishing the different ionic forms of carotenoids are located between 1500 cm−1 and 1000 cm−1. The infrared spectrum exploits the stretching and bending of individual bonds (Bhushan et al., 2023). In the region 1750 to 1500 cm−1, a band of high intensity is observed, possibly due to the stretching of carbonyl groups. The incorporation of carbonyl groups in starch has been reported by Sánchez-Rivera et al. (2005), indicating a high content of these groups.

Determination of carotenoid pigments of OFSP

Carotenoids can be classified into two groups according to their functions, including xanthophylls (lutein and zeaxanthin) and carotenes (α-carotene, β-carotene, lycopene). Chromatograms of standard carotenoids injections and carotenoids extracted from OFSP grown in Vietnam are presented in Fig. 3. It was observed that HPLC profile of carotenoid standards recorded in the retention time (RT) from 6.98 min to 11.57 min, including lutein (6.98 min), lycopene (11.175 min) and β–carotene (11.57 min) (Fig. 3a).

As discussed, β-carotene is the most potent precursor of vitamin A activity. This is especially useful for vegans, as they do not consume foods containing vitamin A directly from animal sources such as liver, meat, and eggs. By consuming β-carotene from OFSP, vegans can provide their bodies with the vitamin A needed to maintain healthy eyes, skin, and the immune system. In addition, OFSP containing dense carotene may be a suitable diet for vitamin A deficiency as well as for under-nutrition.

Conclusion

In this study, the physicochemical parameters, nutritional characteristics, bioactive compounds and antioxidant activities of OFSP grown in Vinh Long province, Vietnam were comprehensively evaluated. Dietary fiber is also present in OFSP, contributing to the outstanding nutritional characteristics of this raw material. The estimated PRAL score (−) shows that OFSP is classified as an alkaline food when digested in the body and provides many health benefits. FTIR analysis showed that carotenoid pigments in OFSP demonstrated 14 broad peaks and complete functional groups were detected. HPLC analysis identifies the main carotenoid pigment in OFSP as β-carotene which is a precursor of vitamin A, which is very beneficial for human health when converted into vitamin A in the body. Therfore, OFSP could be a potential dietary approach to address the vitamin A deficiency in poor people, malnourished children, especially in remote rural areas in our country. In addition, successful research results will demonstrate the potential of OFSP to be used as a functional food, not only providing benefits for human health, but also enhancing the economic value of local resources and eventually the whole country.

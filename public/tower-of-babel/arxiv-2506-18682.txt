Multi-Scale Spectral Attention Module-based Hyperspectral Segmentation in Autonomous Driving Scenarios
Imad Ali Shah 1,2, Jiarong Li 1,2, Tim Brophy 1,2, Martin Glavin 1,2, Edward Jones 1,2, Enda Ward 3, and Brian Deegan 1,2

Abstract
Recent advances in autonomous driving (AD) have highlighted the potential of Hyperspectral Imaging (HSI) for enhanced environmental perception, particularly in challenging weather and lighting conditions. However, efficiently processing its high-dimensional spectral data remains a significant challenge. This paper introduces a Multi-scale Spectral Attention Module (MSAM) that enhances spectral feature extraction through three parallel 1D convolutions with varying kernel sizes between 1 to 11, coupled with an adaptive feature aggregation mechanism. By integrating MSAM into UNet’s skip connections (UNet-SC), our proposed UNet-MSAM achieves significant improvements in semantic segmentation performance across multiple HSI datasets: HyKo-VIS v2, HSI-Drive v2, and Hyperspectral City v2. Our comprehensive experiments demonstrate that with minimal computational overhead (on average 0.02% in parameters and 0.82% GFLOPS), UNet-MSAM consistently outperforms UNet-SC, achieving average improvements of 3.61% in mean IoU and 3.80% in mF1 across the three datasets. Through extensive ablation studies, we have established that multi-scale kernel combinations perform better than single-scale configurations. These findings demonstrate the potential of HSI processing for AD and provide valuable insights into designing robust, multi-scale spectral feature extractors for real-world applications.

INTRODUCTION

\IEEEPARstart

Autonomous driving (AD) systems require robust and reliable perception capabilities to ensure safe navigation in diverse environmental conditions. While traditional RGB imaging has been the cornerstone of computer vision in Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS), Hyperspectral Imaging (HSI) has recently emerged as a promising technology to address critical limitations in current perception systems. Unlike traditional RGB cameras that capture just three spectral bands (i.e. Red, Green, and Blue), HSI cameras can acquire tens to hundreds of bands across the electromagnetic spectrum as shown in Fig.

[

, precision agriculture

One of the key potential benefits of HSI in AD is its ability to address metamerism
[
8
]
, a phenomenon where objects with different material compositions appear identical under specific lighting conditions with standard RGB imaging, but they can be distinguished through their unique spectral signatures. This capability has the potential to improve road hazard identification, material differentiation including water, snow, and ice on the road
[
9
]
, and reliable object segmentation under varying lighting conditions. However, the integration of HSI into ADAS presents unique challenges distinct from its traditional applications such as in remote sensing and medicine, where the relatively static image acquisition-based scenarios can accommodate typical line-scan and slow frame rate HSI cameras. However, the dynamic nature of AD scenarios demands portable "snapshot-based" HSI cameras capable of real-time video capture.

Recent technological advances have led to the development of compact and cost-effective snapshot HSI cameras
[
10
]
, enabling the collection of annotated HSI datasets specifically for ADAS scenarios. However, achieving broad spectral and spatial coverage with affordable snapshot cameras remains challenging, particularly while maintaining real-time processing capabilities. This has limited the development of perception systems that leverage the capabilities of HSI sensors and consequently, the majority of HSI studies in the ADAS/AD field have concentrated on gathering data and presenting initial findings largely focusing on individual datasets.

Despite the inherent challenges of HSI in the ADAS/AD domain, researchers have made efforts to utilize HSI data and have established performance benchmarks comparing HSI with RGB
[
10
,
11

[

[
3
]
indicate that existing deep learning architectures are primarily optimized for RGB, which often fails to fully utilize the HSI’s rich spectral information. This limitation is mainly due to two key factors: (1) These models are originally designed to extract spatial domain features from RGB images, and the use of pre-trained models (e.g., on ImageNet
[
13
]
) primarily benefits RGB images. (2) When applied to HSI data, researchers typically reduce the high-dimensional spectral information to fit RGB-oriented models through dimensionality reduction techniques (such as single convolutional layers, principal component analysis, band subselection
[
14
]
etc), resulting in potential loss of the distinctive spectral characteristics within HSI
[
15
]
. This limitation highlights the need for specialized deep learning architectures that can effectively utilize dense spectral information of HSI while maintaining computational efficiency.

Drawing intuition from the extensive research in remote sensing
[
16
]
and channel-wise feature extraction for HSI in ADAS/AD scenarios
[
12
]
where spectral feature extraction has proven beneficial for object classification, we propose a multi-scale spectral attention module (MSAM) for more effective feature extraction from HSI data in ADAS/AD applications. Our approach employs parallel kernels for multi-scale feature extraction using 1D convolution and a fusion-based attention mechanism (AM). The proposed MSAM, integrated into the Skip Connections (SC) variant
[
17
]
of the UNet
[
18
]
architecture, demonstrates superior performance in semantic segmentation compared to the baseline UNet-SC.

This work establishes a foundation for further studies to explore better extraction and fusion of spectral and spatial data to improve the overall segmentation for ADAS/AD scenarios. The main contributions of this paper are as follows:

•

Development of MSAM, an attention mechanism specifically designed for HSI feature extraction, by implementing parallel multi-scale kernels and efficiently integrating it into UNet-SC

•

Comprehensive evaluation across three HSI datasets (HyKo-VIS, HSI-Drive, H-City) with detailed ablation studies on kernel size combinations, establishing a framework for multi-scale spectral kernels based AM integration into SSMs

•

With minimal additional computational overhead (0.02% in parameters and 0.82% GFLOPS), UNet-MSAM provides significant performance gains of over 3.61% in mean IoU and 3.80% in mean F1 across the three HSI datasets in comparison to baseline UNet-SC

2.1
HSI-based Datasets for AD Scenarios

The development of HSI datasets for ADAS/AD applications has resulted in a number of datasets, each with distinct characteristics and limitations, as detailed in Table
1
, with representative image samples shown in Fig.

These datasets leverage HSI’s capability to capture tens to hundreds of spectral bands across the electromagnetic spectrum, ranging from visible (VIS) to near-infrared (NIR) wavelengths for each spatial pixel, providing spectrally-rich information compared to conventional RGB images with just three channels. For example:

•

The HyKo
[
23
]
dataset comprises two distinct sub-datasets: HyKo-NIR (covering the wavelength range of 630-975nm) and HyKo-VIS (470-630nm). While HyKo-NIR’s road-focused camera view limits its applicability for comprehensive ADAS/AD perception tasks, HyKo-VIS offers 163 HSI cubes with 15 spectral bands.

•

The HSI-Drive
[
2
]
dataset presents a more extensive collection spanning four weather seasons and various environmental conditions, although it is constrained by coarse pixel-wise class annotations and limited spectral coverage (25 bands, 600-975nm, primarily red and NIR spectra).

•

The H-City
[
3
]
dataset offers the highest spectral resolution with 128 channels and 19 finely-annotated class labels.

•

The HSI-Road
[
24
]
dataset provides binary classification (Road and Others).

•

The Hyper-Drive
[
25
]
dataset, despite being the largest with 12,874 HSI images, includes only 500 finely labeled segmentation masks for off-road scenarios with two levels of labels, i.e. 4 classes (Path, Vegetation, Obstacle and Person) and 10 classes (Dirt, Rock, Paved, Concrete, etc.).

•

Recently, Valme et al.
[
9
]
collected a VIS-NIR (400-1000nm) dataset with 14 labeled classes focusing on road conditions (water, snow, ice, etc.) with limited samples (61 total: 35 water, 26 snow). However, this dataset is not publicly available.

Despite these diverse and significant efforts in dataset collection, several fundamental challenges remain across the existing HSI datasets. The common limitations of these datasets are:

•

Lack of standardization in spatial dimensions and spectral bands,

•

Varying spectral coverage across datasets,

•

Varying annotation schemes and class definitions,

•

Significant class imbalance, as demonstrated in Table

for HSI-Drive and HyKo-VIS.

While these limitations represent challenges, the existing datasets provide a valuable foundation for evaluating HSI’s capabilities in ADAS/AD applications and highlight key areas for future dataset development.

2.3
Spectral Feature Extraction Approaches

Traditional approaches to HSI feature extraction have primarily focused on remote sensing applications, where spectral information has been shown to play a crucial role in material classification. These methods can be broadly categorized as follows:

•

Classical Image Processing
methods utilize established image processing techniques to segment objects based on known characteristics. These include thresholding, clustering, watershed segmentation, morphological operations, edge detection, superpixel generation, and region-based segmentation approaches
[
36
]
. While effective in controlled environments, these techniques require prior domain knowledge and often lack generalizability across diverse scenarios.

•

Channel-wise Processing
methods analyze each spectral band independently, typically utilizing ANNs or 1D convolutions to capture spectral features. However, this independent processing approach does not take advantage of important inter-channel relationships and spectral-spatial correlations that are essential for comprehensive feature extraction.

•

Joint Spatial-Spectral Processing
methods, including 2D-3D convolutions
[
37
,
38
]
and Transformers
[
39
,
30
]
, process spatial and spectral dimensions simultaneously. Although these methods allow for a more thorough integration of information, they require substantial computational power because of the high dimensionality associated with HSI data.

•

Attention Mechanisms (AM) based Processing
leverages attention mechanisms to selectively focus on relevant spectral bands and spatial regions. AMs are implemented through various approaches: as part of 1D-2D convolutional architectures within Transformer frameworks
[
30
,
40
]
, or through multiplicative, additive, or pooling operations such as Global Average Pooling
[
34
]
. These attention-based methods enable the network to adaptively weight and combine features for more effective representations.

While these techniques have demonstrated success in various domains, their application to dynamic ADAS/AD scenarios remains limited and requires further evaluation. As indicated in Table
3
, HSI processing for ADAS/AD is still in its early research stages, and further research is needed to evaluate and advance HSI processing methods for these applications.

2.4
Motivation for This Study

As noted already, current approaches to utilising HSI either treat spectral bands independently or process them uniformly, potentially missing crucial spectral-spatial relationships. This limitation is particularly important, as the local to global features in the adjacent spectral bands can potentially increase the robustness of object segmentation, especially in highly dynamic AD scenarios where accurate scene understanding is crucial.
Our work addresses this challenge by proposing a Multi-scale Spectral Attention Module (MSAM) that can capture both fine-grained spectral details and their local-to-global contextual information. By operating at multiple scales in parallel, MSAM can better handle the varying spectral relationships present in dynamic AD scenes while maintaining computational efficiency.

3
Methodology

This section highlights the structure of the proposed MSAM and its integration into a UNet-SC for improved segmentation performance on available annotated HSI-based datasets for ADAS/AD scenarios.

3.1
Multi-scale Spectral Attention Module (MSAM)

We propose MSAM, as shown in Fig.
3
, designed to effectively extract and leverage the rich spectral information present in HSI data while maintaining spatial context and computational efficiency. The module operates on two key principles, i.e., multi-scale spectral feature extraction, and adaptive feature fusion:

Y=X_{s}\otimes(1+F_{fusion}(F_{spectral}(X_{r})))

(1)

Equation (1) shows the high-level description of the proposed MSAM.
\otimes
represents element-wise multiplication,
X_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{(C,H,W)}
is the input tensor and
X_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{(H\cdot W,C)}
is the reshaped input tensor for spectral-wise 1D convolution-based feature extraction,
F_{fusion}
and
F_{spectral}
are feature aggregation-based fusion and multiscale spectral feature maps, respectively.

3.2
Multi-scale Spectral Feature Extraction Block

To capture spectral relationships at different scales, we use three parallel 1D convolutions with varying kernel sizes. For an input with
C
spectral bands, the spectral feature extraction can be formulated as Equation (2):

F_{spectral}=\sigma(K^{3}*\sigma(X_{r}*K_{d}^{i}))

(2)

where
K
represents the kernel,
i
represents different kernel sizes (e.g., 3, 7, or 11 from a range of odd numbers 1 to 11) and
d
represents the dilation rate for the 1D convolution based on
max(1,ceil(i/2))
.
K^{3}
represents a 1D kernel with a kernel size of 3, and
\sigma
represents LeakyReLU-based activation.

The proposed multi-scale approach enables the module to capture local spectral correlations (based on smaller kernels such as
i
= 1, 3), medium-range spectral dependencies (for
i
= 5, 7), and global spectral context (for
i
= 9, 11).

3.3
Adaptive Feature Refinement Block

The feature refinement block consists of three steps. 1)
Feature Fusion
: The extracted features are aggregated through element-wise summation to capture the spectral relationships at different scales. Afterwards, Instance Normalization is used to normalize the individual channels (i.e. HxW) for better handling of spectral signature variations and to maintain stable training. 2)
Addition of Non-Linearity
: To enhance feature representation, sigmoid linear unit (SiLU) activation adds non-linearity to the already fused multi-scale spectral featured maps. This step highlights the important spectral regions for further integration with spatial information by retaining fine-grained details. 3)
Multiplicative Attention
: To allow the MSAM to adaptively emphasize relative spectral features, the final output is computed through multiplicative attention with the input tensor. Steps 1-2 are shown in Equation (3) and Step 3 is shown in Equation (4):

F_{fusion}=SiLU(InstanceNorm(\sum_{n=0}^{2}F_{spectral}^{n})_{s})

(3)

where
n
represents three parallel
F_{spectral}
feature maps from Eq (2), and
s
denotes the reshape to the original input tensor shape for consistency with the network flow and integration.

Y=X_{s}\otimes(1+F_{fusion})

(4)

where
Y
represents the refined features based on multiplicative attention of spatial feature maps with multi-scale based spectral features.

3.4
Integration with UNet Architecture

We integrate the proposed MSAM in a UNet architecture, due to its consistently better performance over other baseline SSMs
[
12
]
such as DeepLabv3+, HRNet and PSPNet. As shown in Fig.
4
, MSAM can be integrated in three strategic locations of UNet:

•

Between two Convolution-BatchNorm-Activation (CBA) blocks

•

After the two CBAs in the encoder-decoder blocks

•

In skip connection (SC)

Based on our ablative studies (described in Section
4.2
), MSAM in SC outperforms the other configurations, the reason being its enhancement of the spectral information flow from the encoder to the decoder blocks of the UNet model, helping to preserve fine-grained spectral details. The integration of MSAM in UNet-SC, i.e. UNet-MSAM, maintains the UNet model backbone structure of encoder-decoder blocks, while using MSAM in SC helps with spectral information flow at multiple levels of encoder-decoder blocks.

The proposed UNet-MSAM allows the network to (1) preserve fine-grained spectral details through SC, (2) maintain computational efficiency, and (3) enable adaptive feature refinement at multi-level spectral-spatial-based fusion of features due to UNet’s use of MaxPool2D and UpSample operations at the end of each encoder and start of decoder blocks, respectively.

4.2
Ablative Studies

Our experimental analysis consists of two main aspects: (1) examining the different MSAM integration locations in the UNet architecture, and (2) investigating various kernel combinations for multi-scale spectral feature extraction. As three kernels are proposed in MSAM for extracting fine-grained details and local to global spectral context, the ablative studies comprehensively evaluated kernel size combinations ranging from 1 to 11. All experiments were conducted with a UNet backbone of four encoder-decoder blocks with depths of 32, 64, 128, and 256, respectively.

The tests included examining the configuration of UNet-SC and UNet-MSAM with different kernel sizes integrated at three different locations, as mentioned in Section
3.4
and illustrated in Fig.
4
. A total of 161 different configurations were used to conduct the ablation study on each dataset. This included one UNet-SC as a baseline and five different UNet integrations with MSAM (i.e. (1) between CBAs, (2) after CBA, (3) in SC, (4) between+after CBAs, and (5) between CBA+SC). Each of these integrations was further comprised of 32 different kernel combinations. The performance evaluation, based on mIoU and mF1, was conducted for HSI-Drive and HyKo-VIS datasets, excluding H-City due to its larger size and, therefore, significantly higher computational requirements for this study.

This comprehensive set of experiments aims to answer the following questions: (1) Where is the optimal location to integrate the proposed MSAM in UNet, i.e., which architectural configuration is best? and (2) Which kernel combinations provide the best performance?

Thus, from here on in this paper, the proposed UNet-MSAM means that the MSAM is integrated at SC. The second best configuration was ’After CBAs’ since integration at this location in UNet is followed by a MaxPool2D layer in each of the encoder blocks, and its output is fed into the next encoder block. This affects the UNet spatial pipeline in that this configuration resulted in more variance. The results confirm that proper fusion of spectral and spatial features is crucial for optimal hyperspectral image segmentation and that MSAM is effective in achieving this fusion.

4.3
Key Findings and Further Experimentation

Based on these experiments, we are able to determine the best-performing kernels combinations. The key takeaways are as follows:

•

The proposed UNet-MSAM consistently outperforms the baseline UNet-SC model. The ascending trend of both mIoU and mF1 validated the effectiveness of our kernel selection strategy across both HSI datasets.

•

Use of multiscale kernels improved the performance in both datasets, but with different optimal kernal combinations.

•

The selection of optimal kernel combinations is dataset-specific, highlighting the unique spectral features in the datasets and the importance of spectral features in hyperspectral image segmentation.

•

The comprehensive ablation study provides strong evidence that both the integration location of MSAM and the selection of appropriate kernel combinations are crucial for optimal performance. These findings provide a foundation for future research in hyperspectral image segmentation, particularly in the development of adaptive kernel selection strategies.

4.5
Computational Overhead

Table
8
presents a comparison between UNet-SC and the best performing UNet-MSAM configurations from the point of view of computational requirements. The results show a minimal increase in computational overhead with MSAM. For example, the HSI-Drive dataset requires only a 0.31% increase in parameters and 1.27% in GFLOPs for UNet
16
-MSAM
(3;7;11)
compared to UNet
16
-SC. Similar minimal increases are observed across other configurations and datasets, with average increases of only 0.02% in number of parameters and 0.82% in GFLOPs. However, while parameter count and model size show minimal increases, there is a notable impact on inference time, particularly for CPU execution. The average inference time across both datasets increased by approximately 178% for CPU and 100% for GPU operations.

The minimal increase in model parameters and GFLOPs suggests that the MSAM architecture efficiently utilizes computational resources while providing significant improvements in segmentation performance. This trade-off between computational requirements and performance improvement makes UNet-MSAM particularly suitable for applications where segmentation accuracy is crucial and moderate increases in inference time are acceptable.

Furthermore, the consistent pattern of minimal parameter increases across different UNet depths demonstrates the scalability of our proposed approach. This scalability, combined with the significant performance improvements, validates the effectiveness of our multi-scale spectral feature extraction approach for HSI-based segmentation tasks.

5
Conclusion

This paper has presented a Multi-scale Spectral Attention Module (MSAM) integrated with the UNet architecture for improved HSI-based segmentation in ADAS/AD scenarios. Through comprehensive experimentation and ablation studies across three HSI datasets (HyKo-VIS, HSI-Drive, and H-City), we demonstrated the effectiveness of our proposed approach in several key aspects. The integration of MSAM with UNet skip connections (SC) consistently outperformed the baseline UNet-SC model, achieving average improvements of 3.61% in mIoU and 3.80% in mF1 across all datasets and configurations. The HyKo-VIS dataset demonstrated the highest improvements of up to 8.13% in mIoU and 8.46% in mF1, demonstrating MSAM effectiveness in handling complex spectral information. Our comprehensive ablation studies show that multi-scale kernel combinations consistently outperformed single-scale approaches, validating the effectiveness of our multi-scale spectral feature extraction approach. The optimal kernel combinations were found to be dataset-specific, though with kernel size combinations such as
(1;5;11)
and
(3;7;11)
showing particularly strong performance across all three datasets. The proposed UNet-MSAM demonstrated good computational efficiency, requiring only minimal increases of on average 0.02% in model parameters and 0.82% in GFLOPs compared to the baseline UNet-SC, but with inference times increased by 177.52% for CPU and 100.25% for GPU implementation, as a trade-off for improved segmentation accuracy.

Several promising directions for future research emerge from this work: (1) investigating adaptive kernel selection mechanisms based on dataset-specific characteristics, and (2) architecture optimization for reduced inference time while maintaining segmentation accuracy.

This work represents a significant advancement in HSI segmentation and provides a foundation for future research in spectral-spatial feature extraction for ADAS/AD applications.

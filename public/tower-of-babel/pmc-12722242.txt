Adaptive vector oriented control of doubly fed induction generator wind turbines using M5P model tree for robust dynamic performance
Authors: Koudri Benyoucef, Moualidia Abdelhafidh, Boudana Djamel, Mujammal Ahmed Hasan Mujammal, Khechafi Sofiane, Zemirline Nassim, Mohit Bajaj, Oleksandr Rubanenko
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWind energy systems using doubly fed induction generators (DFIGs) rely on vector-oriented control (VOC) to achieve decoupled regulation of active and reactive power. Conventional VOC strategies typically employ proportional-integral (PI) controllers for rotor current control; however, these controllers struggle under nonlinear and variable operating conditions, such as wind speed fluctuations, leading to degraded dynamic performance and power quality. To overcome these limitations, this paper proposes an intelligent VOC approach based on the M5-Pruned model tree (M5P) algorithm, complemented by a comparative study with fuzzy logic controllers (FLC). The M5P-based controller introduces a data-driven mechanism that adapts to system dynamics, ensuring improved accuracy and robustness compared to traditional PI and FLC methods. A detailed MATLAB/Simulink simulation of a grid-connected wind turbine with DFIG evaluates the controllers under varying wind profiles. Results demonstrate that the proposed M5P strategy significantly reduces overshoot and settling time, enhances power regulation, and improves overall system stability. These findings highlight the advantages of machine learning-based control in addressing the shortcomings of conventional VOC techniques for renewable energy applications.Keywords: Vector oriented control (VOC), Doubly fed induction generator (DFIG), Wind energy conversion system (WECS), Fuzzy logic controller (FLC), M5P algorithmSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

The global transition toward sustainable energy has intensified the integration of renewable sources into modern power systems, with wind energy emerging as one of the most viable alternatives to fossil fuels1. Among the various technologies employed in wind energy conversion systems (WECS), the doubly fed induction generator (DFIG) has gained prominence due to its high efficiency, variable-speed operation, and capability for independent control of active and reactive power2,3. This control flexibility is typically achieved through rotor-side converters that implement vector-oriented control (VOC) strategies, which decouple the torque and flux components for improved dynamic performance4.

However, conventional VOC schemes rely heavily on proportional–integral (PI) controllers, whose performance deteriorates under nonlinear operating conditions and parameter uncertainties, thereby motivating the development of more adaptive and intelligent control approaches5. Extensive research has been conducted to enhance the performance of DFIG-based wind energy systems through advanced control strategies6,7. Conventional PI controllers, despite their simplicity and ease of implementation, exhibit poor adaptability under nonlinear dynamics, parameter variations, and external disturbances such as wind speed8,39. Additionally, subsynchronous resonance (SSR) phenomena in series-compensated transmission lines connected to WECS have been shown to significantly influence system stability, with damping characteristics strongly affected by controller gains, wind speed, and compensation levels9.

The motivation for this work stems from the increasing penetration of wind energy into power grids, which demands highly adaptive and robust control strategies. Conventional PI-based VOC fails to meet these requirements under fluctuating wind and grid conditions, creating a need for intelligent, data-driven solutions that can ensure stability, efficiency, and power quality in real-time applications.

To overcome these limitations, intelligent control techniques have been introduced, including Fuzzy Logic Controller.

(FLC)10,37 and artificial neural networks (ANN)11,12, which offer improved robustness and adaptability. FLCs, in particular, have demonstrated superior performance in handling system nonlinearities without requiring an accurate mathematical model13,14. However, their rule-based structure can become complex and computationally demanding for large-scale systems. Recent advancements in machine learning have opened new opportunities for data-driven control approaches, such as model tree algorithms, which combine decision tree structures with regression models to achieve high accuracy and adaptability in dynamic environments15,40.

Despite the widespread adoption of VOC in DFIG-based wind energy systems, its conventional implementation using PI controllers faces several challenges16,17. Moreover, their fixed gain structure limits adaptability, leading to degraded performance during nonlinear operating scenarios and grid disturbances18. These limitations often result in slower dynamic response, increased overshoot, and reduced robustness, particularly when the system operates under variable wind speeds or experiences sudden load changes. Consequently, there is a growing need for advanced control strategies that can overcome these drawbacks by providing improved adaptability, robustness, and accuracy without significantly increasing computational complexity19.

Similarly, autonomous microgrids integrating wind and diesel generation require advanced frequency regulation schemes, where intelligent controllers such as fuzzy logic-based sliding mode control have demonstrated superior robustness under load perturbations and dynamic conditions20.

To address the limitations of conventional PI-based VOC, intelligent control techniques have emerged as promising alternatives due to their ability to handle nonlinearities and adapt to varying operating conditions21. FLC has been widely adopted because they do not require an accurate mathematical model and can effectively manage uncertainties in wind energy systems. However, their performance depends on the design of membership functions and rule sets, which can become complex for high-dimensional systems22.

Recent advances in intelligent and optimization-based control for power systems further illustrate the trend towards hybrid and data-driven methods. For example, Harris Hawks Optimization (HHO)23 has been applied to tune fractional-order fuzzy- PID controllers for frequency regulation in multi-microgrid environments, showing improved robustness under load variations and renewables penetration. Similarly, HHO-guided model predictive control has been proposed for stochastic wind-integrated multi-source power systems to handle uncertainty in generation and demand24. While these optimization-driven schemes offer strong performance, they typically rely on online/iterative optimization or relatively complex controller structures. In contrast, the proposed M5P model-tree approach aims for a different trade-off: it learns compact, locally linear models from simulation data to produce an interpretable and computationally light controller that can generalize across operating conditions without requiring continuous optimization during runtime.

M5P combines decision tree structures with linear regression models, enabling accurate prediction and control with reduced computational complexity10,25. This capability makes M5P particularly suitable for real-time applications in renewable energy systems, where dynamic adaptability and robustness are critical26. While ANN-based controllers have been explored27,28, they often require large datasets, complex tuning, and significant computational resources, making them less suitable for real-time applications. In contrast, FLC offers simplicity and interpretability, whereas the M5P model tree algorithm provides an adaptive, data-driven approach with a favorable balance of accuracy, efficiency, and transparency. By constructing locally linear regression models organized in a tree structure, M5P enables fast learning, smooth decision boundaries, and interpretable control logic, making it well-suited for MATLAB/Simulink implementation and potential real-time applications in renewable energy systems. These characteristics position FLC and M5P as ideal techniques for comparison in this study.

Building on these considerations, a smart VOC strategy for DFIG-based wind energy systems is proposed by replacing the conventional PI controllers used for rotor current regulation and with the M5P model tree algorithm. This modification introduces a data-driven and adaptive mechanism into the traditional VOC framework, significantly improving its ability to handle nonlinearities and parameter variations. The integration of M5P not only enhances the dynamic performance of the system but also adds an intelligent dimension to the control process, enabling superior adaptability under fluctuating wind and grid conditions.

Proposed control strategies

To enhance the dynamic performance and robustness of the DFIG control system, this section presents a comparative study of three control strategies applied to the RSC. The baseline approach is the conventional VOC, which decouples the control of active and reactive power using PI controllers. In the second strategy, the conventional PI controllers are replaced by an independent FLC to improve adaptability under nonlinear and uncertain operating conditions. The third and most significant strategy introduces the M5P decision tree algorithm, replacing both PI controllers with a data-driven model that enables intelligent and adaptive control based on real-time operating data.

Vector oriented control based on FLC

In conventional VOC, the inner current control loops for the d-axis and q-axis components (id and iq) are typically implemented using PI controllers. While PI controllers offer simplicity and satisfactory performance under nominal conditions, their effectiveness can degrade in the presence of parameter variations, nonlinearities, and external disturbances32.

To enhance robustness and adaptability, this subsection proposes replacing the two PI controllers with FLCs. The FLC approach leverages linguistic rules and membership functions to handle system uncertainties without requiring an accurate mathematical model. By tuning the control action based on fuzzy inference, the proposed method aims to improve dynamic response, reduce overshoot, and maintain stability under varying operating conditions. The membership functions for the input error, change of error, and output control signal used in the FLC-based VOC scheme are illustrated in Fig. 6.

The mathematical representation of a leaf model defines appropriate input variables (such as current error and its derivative), constructs a rule base, and implements a defuzzification strategy to generate the control signals for the d- and q-axis voltage references. The overall structure of the VOC remains unchanged, ensuring compatibility with standard field-oriented control schemes while introducing intelligent control at the current regulation level.

Two input variables (current error and its derivative) and one output variable (voltage control signal) were used, each with seven triangular membership functions. To reduce computational complexity, the FLC inputs and outputs were normalized using gain factors (eIdr = 0.05, ∆eIdr = 0.01, output = 1.2; eIqr = 0.025, ∆eIdr = 0.00001, output = 0.1). These adjustments ensured effective control performance while minimizing the computational burden of the fuzzy inference process.

VOC based on the proposed M5P decision tree algorithm

VOC is a widely used technique for controlling AC machines due to its ability to decouple torque and flux components. Traditionally, VOC employs PI controllers to regulate the direct-axis (Id) and quadrature-axis (Iq) currents. However, PI controllers exhibit limitations under parameter variations, nonlinearities, and dynamic operating conditions, which can degrade performance. To address these issues, this work proposes replacing the conventional PI controllers with an M5P decision tree-based model, offering adaptive, data-driven control without requiring precise system modeling.

From a computational perspective, the M5P-based controller introduces a moderate increase in processing requirements compared to the PI approach but remains substantially more efficient than the FLC structure. After training, the control action is derived from simple linear equations at the leaf nodes, ensuring rapid computation with minimal memory usage. This balance between accuracy and efficiency suggests that M5P can be feasibly implemented for real-time control applications using standard embedded processors. Modeling of the proposed M5P algorithm

The M5P algorithm is a model tree approach that combines decision tree partitioning with linear regression models at the leaves. Its main steps are:

Tree Construction: The input space (e.g., reference currents, speed, and system states) is recursively partitioned based on attributes that minimize the standard deviation of the target variable.

Linear Models at Leaves: Instead of assigning a constant value at each leaf, M5P fits a multivariate linear regression model, improving prediction accuracy and smoothness.

Linear Models at Leaves: Instead of assigning a constant value at each leaf, M5P fits a multivariate linear regression model, improving prediction accuracy and smoothness.

Pruning and Smoothing: The tree is pruned to avoid overfitting, and smoothing is applied to ensure continuity between adjacent regions.

Pruning and Smoothing: The tree is pruned to avoid overfitting, and smoothing is applied to ensure continuity between adjacent regions.

In this study, the M5P model tree was trained using three key input features: current error ,change of error , and control voltages . These variables were chosen because they directly influence the dynamic performance of the DFIG and provide the necessary information for accurate prediction and control of rotor currents. By incorporating both instantaneous error and its rate of change along with machine speed, the model can adapt to nonlinearities and varying operating conditions, ensuring robust and precise current regulation. The training dataset was generated from FLC-based simulations, where the input signals were error and change of error, and the outputs were the control actions produced by the FLC. This ensures that the M5P model learns the relationship between system states and optimal control signals, enabling adaptive and robust current regulation.

M5P model tree generation

The training dataset for the proposed M5P controller was generated from MATLAB/Simulink simulations of the FLC-based control system, which provided a larger and more representative dataset than the PI-based configuration. Data were sampled at s, resulting in 300,001 instances. After preprocessing, the dataset was trained using the WEKA platform to build the M5P model tree, which was then integrated into MATLAB/Simulink using a customized implementation to evaluate its control performance. Conclusion

This study introduced an advanced and smart VOC strategy for DFIG-based wind energy systems, leveraging the M5P model tree algorithm to overcome the significant limitations of conventional PI and FLC controllers. By integrating a data-driven approach into the VOC framework, the proposed method demonstrated superior adaptability and robustness under nonlinear and variable operating conditions.

The proposed M5P algorithm used FLC data in the training process and achieved an excellent performance for both currents Idr and Iqr with a Correlation Coefficient: 1.0000 that main a perfect learning, the proposed M5P decision tree algorithm distinguishes of the other machine learnings with the rapidity and clarity where it provide with a seen program in form of tree acceptable to the modifications that allow to incorporate it in the implementation platforms as MATLAB/Simulink.

Comparative simulations confirmed that the M5P-based controller significantly improved dynamic response, reduced overshoot, and improved power quality compared to traditional PI and fuzzy logic controllers. These findings underscore the potential of machine learning techniques to revolutionize renewable energy control systems, paving the way for smarter, more resilient wind energy integration into modern power grids.

Despite the promising results obtained in simulation, the M5P-based controller presents some limitations that merit further exploration. Its performance depends on the quality and representativeness of the training data, and its robustness under unmodeled dynamics or measurement noise remains to be assessed experimentally. Future work will focus on implementing the proposed control strategy on real-time platforms and extending the approach toward hybrid or adaptive machine learning frameworks to enhance generalization and robustness.

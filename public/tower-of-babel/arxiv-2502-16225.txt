On empty balls of critical 2-dimensional branching random walks
Shuxiong Zhang

Abstract
Let { Z n } n ≥ 0 \{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0} be a critical d d -dimensional branching random walk started from a Poisson random measure whose intensity measure is the Lebesgue measure on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . Denote by R n := sup { u > 0 : Z n ​ ( { x ∈ ℝ d : | x | < u } ) = 0 } R_{n}:=\sup\{u>0:Z_{n}(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|<u\})=0\} the radius of the largest empty ball centered at the origin of Z n Z_{n} . In [ 17 ] , Révész (2002) conjectured that if d = 2 d=2 , then lim n → ∞ R n n ​ = Law ​ non-trival distribution . \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\overset{\text{Law}}{=}\text{non-trival~distribution}. This work confirms the conjecture. It turns out that the limit distribution can be precisely characterized through the super-Brownian motion. Moreover, we also give complete results of empty balls of the branching random walk with infinite second moment offspring law. As a by-product, this article also improves the assumption of maximal displacements of branching random walks in Lalley and Shao [ 8 , Theorem 1] .

Introduction and Main results

1.1
Introduction

In this paper, we consider a branching random walk
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
started from the Poisson random measure with Lebesgue intensity. This model is a measure-valued Markov process, which is governed by a probability distribution
\{p_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
on natural numbers (called the offspring distribution) and an
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued random vector
X
(called the step size or displacement). Let’s define it as follows.
At generation
0
, there exist infinitely many particles distributed according to the Poisson random measure with Lebesgue intensity. At generation
1
, each particle first produce children independently according to
\{p_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
and then every children performs a jump independently of each others from the place where they are born according to the law of the step size
X
, where the branching and motion are independent. The point process
Z_{1}
is formed by all the
particles and their positions alive at generation
1
. At generation
2
, the particles alive at generation
1
repeat their parent’s behaviour independently from the place where they are born and the procedure goes on.

Since there exist countably many particles at time
0
, we can let
\mathbb{T}^{i}
,
i\geq 1
be the genealogical tree of the branching process
rooted by the
i
-th particle of
Z_{0}
; see Shi
[
18
, p. 13-14]
. Let
\mathbb{T}=\cup^{\infty}_{i=1}\mathbb{T}^{i}
and

X_{u}
be the displacement of particle
u
. For each particle
u\in\mathbb{T}
, we use
|u|
to denote the generation of
u
. If
|u|=0
, then
X_{u}
is determined by the Poisson random measure
Z_{0}
. Otherwise,
\{X_{u}:u\in\mathbb{T},|u|\neq 0\}
are i.i.d. copies of the step size
X
.
Let
S_{\omega}:=\sum_{u\preceq\omega}X_{u}
be the position of particle
\omega\in\mathbb{T}
, where
u\preceq\omega

means that
u=\omega
or
u
is an ancestor of
\omega
. The
population and their locations
at generation
n
forms a point process given by

Z_{n}=\sum_{|\omega|=n}\delta_{S_{\omega}}.

Let
m:=\sum_{k\geq 0}kp_{k}
be the mean of the offspring law. We call
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}

a supercritical (critical, subcritical) branching random walk if
m>1
(
=1
,
<1
). In this work, we always assume that

m=1,p_{1}<1~\text{and}~\mathbb{E}[X]=0.

Moreover, we always assume that the step size
X
has a positive definite covariance matrix

{\bf C}=(c_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq d}.

Let
B(u):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|<u\}
be the
d
-dimensional open ball with radius
u
, where
|x|
stands for the Euclidean norm of the vector
x
. Denote by

R_{n}:=\sup\{u>0:Z_{n}(B(u))=0\}

the radius of the largest empty ball centered at the origin of
Z_{n}
, where we take
0=\sup\emptyset
by convention. Or, in other words,
R_{n}
is the shortest distance of the particles at time
n
from the origin.

The research on the empty ball was first studied by Révész
[
17
]
for the binary branching Wiener process (i.e.
p_{0}=p_{2}=1/2
and
X
is a standard normal random vector) started from
\text{PRM}(\lambda)
, where we use
\text{PRM}(\lambda)
to stand for the Poisson random measure with the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure
\lambda
as its intensity measure. Révész
[
17
]
proved that if
d=1
, then
R_{n}/n
converges in law to an exponential random variable as
n\to\infty
. For high-dimensional cases, he presented following two conjectures (see
[
17
, Conjecture 1]
):

(i) If
d=2
, then for any
r\in(0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\geq r\right)=e^{-F_{2}(r)}\in(0,1),

(1.1)

where
F_{2}(r)
satisfies

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{F_{2}(r)}{\pi r^{2}}=1;

(ii) If
d\geq 3
, then for any
r\in(0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\geq r)=e^{-F_{d}(r)}\in(0,1),

(1.2)

where
F_{d}(r)
satisfies

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{F_{d}(r)}{C_{d}r^{d-2}}=1,

(1.3)

and
\lim_{d\to\infty}C_{d}/\big[\frac{\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2+1)}\big]^{(d-2)/d}=1
(
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the Gamma function). Later, Hu
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
partially confirmed Révész’s conjecture for
d\geq 3
by showing that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\geq r)
exists in
(0,1)
. But, (
1.3
) remains unproven.

Recently, Xiong and Zhang
[
21
]
studied empty balls for critical super-Brownian motions in any dimension. For the super-Brownian motion, they showed that in
d\geq 2
, the limit distributions are consistent with Révész’s conjectures. Later, by allowing the branching mechanism of the super-Brownian motion is critical, subcritical or has infinite variance, Liu, Xiong and Zhang
[
23
]
generalized the work of
[
21
]
to super-Brownian motions with general branching mechanisms. Theses results of super-Brownian motions
[
21
]
[
23
]
are complete and refined. Nevertheless, to the best of our knowledge, for the branching Wiener process (or the branching random walk) model, Révész’s conjecture for the
2
-dimensional case remains open until now. This article confirms and generalizes this conjecture.

Xiong and Zhang
[
22
]
also generalized the results of the branching Wiener process
[
6
]
[
17
]
to the branching random walk by assuming that all components of the step size are independent and centered. If the offspring law is critical and has finite variance, then the results of
[
22
, Theorems 1.1, 1.2, 1.5]
are as follows.

(i)Assume
m=1
,
d=1
,
\sigma^{2}:=\sum_{k\geq 0}k^{2}p_{k}-(\sum_{k\geq 0}kp_{k})^{2}<\infty
and
\mathbb{E}[|X|^{\alpha}]<\infty
for some
\alpha>2
. Then, for
r>0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{n}\geq r\right)=e^{-\frac{4r}{\sigma^{2}}}.

(ii)Assume
m=1
,
d=2
,
\sigma^{2}<\infty
and
\mathbb{E}[|X|^{4}]<\infty
. Then, for any
r>0
,

\displaystyle 0<\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\geq r\right)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\geq r\right)<1.

(1.4)

(iii)Assume
m=1
,
d\geq 3
,
\sigma^{2}<\infty
and
\mathbb{E}[|X|^{3}]<\infty
. Then, there exists a positive function
F_{d}(r)
,
r>0
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(R_{n}\geq r\right)=e^{-F_{d}(r)}.

Moreover,

\frac{v_{d}(r)}{1+\sigma^{2}C_{d}(r)r^{2}}\leq F_{d}(r)\leq v_{d}(r),

where
C_{d}(r)\in(0,\infty)
is a constant and
v_{d}(r)
is the volume of a
d
-dimensional ball with radius
r
.

From above results, one can see that the assumption
\sigma^{2}<\infty
is necessary. If not the case, things will become different. Let
L(s),~s\in[0,1]
be a slowly varying function at
0
.
[
22
, Theorem 1.8]
is stated as follows. Assume
m=1
,
\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}p_{k}s^{k}=s+(1-s)^{1+\beta}L(1-s)
,
s\in[0,1]
for some
\beta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{d}\right]
and
\mathbb{E}[|X|^{\alpha}]<\infty
for some
\alpha>(\beta+1)d
. Then, for
r>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{b_{n}}\geq r\right)=\exp\left\{-v_{d}(r)(\beta^{-1})^{\beta^{-1}}\right\},

(1.5)

where

b_{n}:=\Big[nL\big(\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(Z_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{d})>0)\big)\Big]^{\frac{1}{\beta d}},~n\geq 1.

The above results consider the critical case
m=1
. For subcritical case,
[
22
, Theorem 1.9]
showed that
R_{n}
increases exponentially, which is stated as follows. Assume
m\in(0,1)
,
d\geq 1
,
\sum_{k\geq 0}p_{k}(k\log k)<\infty
and
\mathbb{E}\left[|X|^{\alpha}\right]<\infty
for some
\alpha>1
. Then, for
r>0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{(1/m)^{\frac{n}{d}}}\geq r\right)=e^{-Q(0)v_{d}(r)},

where
Q(0)\in(0,\infty)
is a constant.

In this article, we shall refine the work of Xiong and Zhang
[
22
]
in three aspects. Firstly, we relax the assumption of the independence between each component of the step size. Secondly, by using the maximal displacement of branching random walks, we strength (
1.4
) by showing the existence of the limit. As a by-product, this work also improves the assumption of Lalley and Shao [8, Theorem 1]. Thirdly, in fact, for any
\beta\in(0,1)
, the offspring law satisfying
\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}p_{k}s^{k}=s+(1-s)^{1+\beta}L(1-s)
implies
\sigma^{2}=\infty
. Since (
1.5
) assumes
\beta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{d}\right]
, the result is not complete. By using large deviation probabilities of random walks and the spine decomposition method, we show that if
\sigma^{2}=\infty
, then the empty ball has three kinds of phase transition phenomenons, which completes the results of
[
22
, Theorem 1.8]
. Meanwhile, we use the size-biased branching random walk to characterize the limit distribution of empty balls of high dimensional (
d\geq 3
) critical branching random walks, thereby improving upon the result in Hu
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
, which does not provide an explicit expression for the limit.

1.2
Main results

In this section, we will give our main results. Since the statement of theorems involve the super-Brownian motion, we first give a brief introduction to it as follows.

Let
\psi
be a function of the form

\psi(u)=au+bu^{2}+\int^{\infty}_{0}(e^{-ru}-1+ru)n(dr),u\geq 0,

where
a\in\mathbb{R},b\geq 0
and
n
is a
\sigma
-finite measure on
(0,\infty)
such that
\int_{(0,\infty)}r\wedge r^{2}n(dr)<\infty
. The super-Brownian motion
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
with initial measure
\mu
and branching mechanism
\psi
is a measure-valued Markov process, whose transition probabilities are characterized through their Laplace transforms. For any bounded non-negative function
\phi
and
t\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x)X_{t}(dx)}\right]=e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\mu(dx)},

(1.6)

where
u(t,x)
is the unique positive solution to the following nonlinear partial differential equation:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\partial u(t,x)}{\partial t}=\mathcal{L}u(t,x)-\psi(u(t,x)),\cr u(0,x)=\phi(x).\end{cases}

(1.7)

In above,
\mathcal{L}:=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq d}\frac{c_{i,j}}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}
is the
generator of a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion, whose covariance matrix at time
1
is
{\bf C}=(c_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
. We refer the reader to
[
3
]
,
[
10
]
and
[
13
]
for a more detailed overview to super-Brownian motions. In this paper, for a measure
\mu
, we always use
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}
to denote the expectation with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{\mu}
, the probability measure under which the branching random walk
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
(or the super-Brownian motion
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
) has initial value
Z_{0}=\mu
(or
X_{0}=\mu
). For ease of notation, we write
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{\text{PRM}(\lambda)}.

Recall that
\sigma^{2}=\sum_{k\geq 0}k^{2}p_{k}-(\sum_{k\geq 0}kp_{k})^{2}
is the variance of the offspring law. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
, let
B(x,r):=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|z-x|<r\}
and
x^{(i)}
be the
i
-th component of
x
,
1\leq i\leq d
. Set
y^{+}:=\max\{y,0\}
for
y\in\mathbb{R}.
Recall that we always consider the critical branching random walk (i.e.
\sum_{k\geq 0}kp_{k}=1
) and the step size is centered.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
d=2
and
\sigma^{2}<\infty
. If
\mathbb{E}[((X^{(1)})^{+})^{4}+((X^{(2)})^{+})^{4}]<\infty
, then for any
r>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\geq r\right)=\exp\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(X_{1}(B(x,r))=0\right)dx\right\}\in(0,1),

where
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a two-dimensional super-Brownian motion with branching mechanism
\psi(u)=\sigma^{2}u^{2}
.

Remark 1.1
.

Though
[
21
, Theorem 1.2]
only deal with the case that the generator
\mathcal{L}
defined in (

) is a Laplace operator, one can easily imitate its proof to show that the theorem still holds for general

mainly use the scale invariance property of

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}-\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(X_{1}(B(x,r))=0\right)dx}{\pi r^{2}/\sigma^{2}}=1.

If the offspring law of
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies

then

. Thus, comparing with (

confirms
[

of

The following theorem considers the case of
\sigma^{2}=\infty
. To state the theorem, we need introduce the following notations. Let
L(s)
,

be a slowly varying function as

(i.e.
\lim_{s\to 0+}L(sx)/L(s)=1
for any

). Recall that

(where

is the so-called Gamma function) is the volume of a

d
-dimensional ball with radius

. Let
Q^{*}
be the probability under which the size-biased critical branching random walk
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
with spine
\{w_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and

is defined. Recall that
S_{u}
is the position of particle
u
and
X_{u}
is the displacement of
u
. Let

be the set of brothers of the spine particle

. Let

. We refer the reader to Case 3 of Section

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume

(i) If
\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}s^{k}p_{k}=s+(1-s)^{1+\beta}L(1-s)
with

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{b_{n}}\geq r\right)=\exp\left\{-v_{d}(r)(\beta^{-1})^{\beta^{-1}}\right\},

\displaystyle b_{n}:=[nL(\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(Z_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{d})>0))]^{\frac{1}{\beta d}},~n\geq 1.

(ii) If
\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{{}^{1+\beta}}\sum^{\infty}_{k=n}p_{k}=:\kappa(\beta)\in(0,\infty)

Remark 1.2
.

Theorem

In

Remark 1.3
.

Rapenne
[
15
, Theorem 1.3]
proved that if

is a
d

in the sense of vague topology as

, where the term

in Theorem

(iii) is used to describe to the characteristic functional of

and

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[e^{i\lambda Z_{n}(f)}]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[e^{i\lambda\Lambda_{\infty}(f)}].

(1.8)

This means
Z_{n}(f)
converges weakly to
\Lambda_{\infty}(f)
as

. Let

be a non-negative continuous function with a compact support such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}({R_{n}}\geq r)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}(B(r))=0)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}(f)=0)=e^{-I_{B(r)}}.

(1.9)

Since

is not a suitable test function for the weak convergence, Theorem

(iii) is not a direct consequence of
[

, Theorem 1.3]
. Moreover, using the method of Theorem

(iii), one can weaken assumptions of the step size in
[

.

The main difficulty of Theorem
1.1
lies in the lower bound. The precise upper bound can be easily obtained by using the fact that the super-Brownian motion is a scaling limit of the branching random walk; see
[
22
, (3.12)]
. For the lower bound, since the proofs involve the indicative function, which is not continuous, the desired lower bound can not be directly obtained through the scaling limit; see
[
22
, Remark 1.4]
. To overcome this difficulty, we prove the lower bound with three steps. Firstly, we study the maximal displacement of the critical branching random walk, which improves the assumption of
[
8
, Thorem 1]
. Our method to the proof mainly use maximums of random walks and a stopping time trick, which is different from the overshoot distribution strategy used in
[
8
]
. Secondly, by using above results for maximal displacements, we obtain an uniform upper bound of the tail probability of the maximum of a 1-dimensional critical branching random walk at some finite time; see Lemma
2.6
below. Finally, we use the maximum of the 1-dimensional critical branching random walk to dominate the local hitting probability of the 2-dimensional branching random walk (see (
3.37
) below), which is crucial in arguing that the empty ball probability involved can be bounded above by a local extinction probability of the super-Brownian motion. By above arguments, we have completely confirmed and generalized this conjecture.

For our purposes of proving Theorem
1.1

[
8
, Theorem 1]
. Write
u\in Z_{i}
if

\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
. Recall that

is the position of particle

. Denote by

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume

,
\sigma^{2}<\infty
,

\mathbb{E}[(X^{+})^{4}]<\infty

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}x^{2}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(M\geq x)=\frac{6\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}.

(1.10)

Remark 1.4
.

Lalley and Shao
[
8

proved that if
\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}k^{3}p_{k}<\infty
,
\mathbb{E}[|X|^{4+\varepsilon}]<\infty
for some

[

commented that

moments of

are not enough for (

).

Remark 1.5
.

For the case of
\sigma^{2}=\infty
, Hou et al.
[
5
]
show that for a critical branching Lévy process with
(1+\beta)
-stable branching mechanism (where

), if
\mathbb{E}[(X^{+})^{r}]<\infty
for some

, then

converges to some positive constant as

. If

, Lalley and Shao
[

]
and Profeta

The rest of this paper is organised as follows. We first study the maximum of the 1-dimensional branching random walk in Section

Lemma 2.6

Let

(2.63)

(ii) Suppose that
\mathbf{E}[|Z_{1}|^{2}]<\infty

\mathbb{E}[(X^{+})^{4}]<\infty

Proof.

For

and

\displaystyle v_{n}(y):=\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}((y,\infty))>0)=\mathbf{P}(M_{n}>y).

(2.65)

\displaystyle v_{m}(y)=\mathbb{E}_{y}\Big[v_{m-k}(W_{k})\prod^{k-1}_{j=1}\big[1-H(v_{m-j}(W_{j}))\big]\Big].

(2.66)

Set

For

and

,

and
k=m\wedge\gamma
in (

\displaystyle v_{\lfloor nr\rfloor}(x\sqrt{n})=\mathbb{E}_{x\sqrt{n}}\Big[v_{\lfloor nr\rfloor-{\lfloor nr\rfloor}\wedge\gamma}(W_{\lfloor nr\rfloor\wedge\gamma})\prod^{\lfloor nr\rfloor\wedge\gamma-1}_{j=1}\big[1-H(v_{\lfloor nr\rfloor-j}(W_{j}))\big]\Big].

Since

(see the line above Lemma

\displaystyle v_{\lfloor nr\rfloor}(x\sqrt{n})

(2.68)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{x\sqrt{n}}\Big[v_{\lfloor nr\rfloor-{\lfloor nr\rfloor}\wedge\gamma}(W_{\lfloor nr\rfloor\wedge\gamma})\Big]

(2.69)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x\sqrt{n}}\Big[v_{\lfloor nr\rfloor-{\lfloor nr\rfloor}\wedge\gamma}(W_{\lfloor nr\rfloor\wedge\gamma}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma\leq{\lfloor nr\rfloor}\}}\Big]

(2.70)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x\sqrt{n}}\Big[v_{\lfloor nr\rfloor-{\lfloor nr\rfloor}\wedge\gamma}(W_{\gamma}){\bf 1}_{\{\gamma\leq{\lfloor nr\rfloor}\}}\left({\bf 1}_{\{W_{\gamma}/\sqrt{n}\geq x/4\}}+{\bf 1}_{\{W_{\gamma}/\sqrt{n}<x/4\}}\right)\Big],

(2.71)

\displaystyle v_{\lfloor nr\rfloor-{\lfloor nr\rfloor}\wedge\gamma}(W_{\lfloor nr\rfloor\wedge\gamma})\leq\mathbf{P}(M_{0}\geq x\sqrt{n}/2)=0.

2.6
. By Corollary

, there exists a constant

\displaystyle n^{\frac{1}{\beta}}v_{\lfloor nr\rfloor}(x\sqrt{n})\leq c_{1}4^{\frac{2}{\beta}}x^{-\frac{2}{\beta}}\mathbb{P}_{x\sqrt{n}}(\gamma\leq\lfloor nr\rfloor)+n^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\mathbb{P}_{x\sqrt{n}}(\gamma\leq\lfloor nr\rfloor,W_{\gamma}\leq x\sqrt{n}/4).

(2.73)

Recall that
\{S_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}=\{-W_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
is the random walk with step size
X
. By Doob’s inequality, it yields that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x\sqrt{n}}(\gamma\leq\lfloor nr\rfloor)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{x\sqrt{n}}\Big(\min_{0\leq i\leq\lfloor nr\rfloor}W_{i}\leq x\sqrt{n}/2\Big)

(2.74)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{0}\Big(\min_{0\leq i\leq\lfloor nr\rfloor}W_{i}\leq-x\sqrt{n}/2\Big)

(2.75)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{0}\Big(\max_{0\leq i\leq\lfloor nr\rfloor}S_{i}\geq x\sqrt{n}/2\Big)

(2.76)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{nx^{2}}\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{0\leq i\leq\lfloor nr\rfloor}S^{2}_{i}\big]

(2.77)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{16}{nx^{2}}\mathbb{E}\big[S^{2}_{\lfloor nr\rfloor}\big]\leq\frac{16\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]}{x^{2}}r.

\displaystyle n^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\mathbb{P}_{x\sqrt{n}}(\gamma\leq\lfloor nr\rfloor,W_{\gamma}\leq x\sqrt{n}/4)

(2.79)

\displaystyle\leq n^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\mathbb{P}_{0}(\exists i\in{1,2...,\lfloor nr\rfloor}~\text{s.t.}-X_{i}\leq-x\sqrt{n}/4)

\displaystyle n^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\mathbf{P}(M_{\lfloor nr\rfloor}\geq x\sqrt{n})

\displaystyle=n^{\frac{1}{\beta}}v_{\lfloor nr\rfloor}(x\sqrt{n})

\displaystyle\leq\left(16c_{1}4^{\frac{2}{\beta}}\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]+4^{2+\frac{2}{\beta}}\mathbb{E}[(X^{+})^{2+\frac{2}{\beta}}]\right)\frac{1}{x^{2+\frac{2}{\beta}}}r

\displaystyle=:C_{\beta}\frac{r}{x^{2+\frac{2}{\beta}}}.

3
Proof of Theorem
1.1
: Empty Ball With Finite Variance

In this section, we shall obtain asymptotic behaviours of the empty ball of a 2-dimensional branching random walk whose offspring law has finite variance. To this end, we first present a lemma regarding the continuity of the local extinction probability of the super-Brownian motion.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a two dimensional critical super-Brownian motion. Fix
r>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then,

\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(X_{t}(B(r))=0)
is continuous with respect to
t
on
(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

It is well-known (e.g.
[
16
, p. 2]
) that if
\phi
is a bounded nonnegative function, then

\displaystyle u_{\phi}(s,x):=-\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\left[e^{-X_{s}(\phi)}\right],s\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

(3.1)

is the unique positive solution of the partial differential equation :

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\partial u(s,x)}{\partial s}=\mathcal{L}u(s,x)-\sigma^{2}u^{2}(s,x),\cr u(0,x)=\phi(x).\end{cases}

(3.2)

Fix any
t>0
. To prove the lemma, it suffices to show that

\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(X_{t+s}(B(r))=0)

is continuous with respect to
s
on
(0,\infty).
Let
\{X^{*}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a super-Bownian motion
independent of
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, and
\{X^{*}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
has the same branching mechanism and underlying motion distribution as
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. For any finite measure
\mu
, by (
1.6
) and (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mu}(X^{*}_{t}(B(r))=0)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\theta\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[e^{-\theta X^{*}_{t}({\bf 1}_{B(r)})}]

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\theta\to+\infty}e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u_{\theta{\bf 1}_{B(r)}}(t,x)\mu(dx)}

(3.4)

\displaystyle=e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\lim_{\theta\to+\infty}u_{\theta{\bf 1}_{B(r)}}(t,x)\mu(dx)}

(3.5)

\displaystyle=e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}-\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(X^{*}_{t}(B(r))=0)\mu(dx)},

(3.6)

where the third equality follows from Lévy’s monotone convergence theorem and

\displaystyle u_{\theta{\bf 1}_{B(r)}}(t,x)=-\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}[e^{-\theta X^{*}_{t}({\bf 1}_{B(r)})}].

(3.7)

Let

\displaystyle 0\leq\phi(y):=-\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}(X_{t}^{*}(B(r))=0))\leq-\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(X^{*}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{2})=0)<\infty.

(3.8)

By the Markov property of
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, it entails that for any
s>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(X_{t+s}(B(r))=0)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(X_{t+s}(B(r))=0|X_{s}))

(3.9)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}[\mathbb{P}_{X_{s}}(X^{*}_{t}(B(r))=0)]

(3.10)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\left[e^{X_{s}(\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{.}}(X_{t}^{*}(B(r))=0))}\right]

(3.11)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\left[e^{-X_{s}(\phi)}\right]

(3.12)

\displaystyle=e^{-u_{\phi}(s,x)},

where the third equality follows from (
3.3
).
By (
3.1
)-(
3.2
),
u_{\phi}(s,x)
is continuous with respect to
s
on
(0,\infty)
. Since

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(X_{t+s}(B(r))=0)=e^{-u_{\phi}(s,x)},

(3.13)

we have
\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(X_{t+s}(B(r))=0)
is continuous with respect to
s
on
(0,\infty)
.

\Box

Now, we are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
. The key points of the proof are as follows. We first use the maximum, investigated in Lemma
2.6
, to control the hitting probability
\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))>0)
. Then, applying the fact that the scaling limit of the branching random walk is the super-Brownian motion, we obtain Theorem
1.1
.

Proof.

In
[
22
, (3.12)]
, we proved that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\geq\sqrt{n}r)\leq\exp\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(X_{1}(B(x,r))=0)dx\right\}.

(3.14)

Moreover, from
[
21
, Theorem 1,2]
, we have

\displaystyle\exp\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(X_{1}(B(x,r))=0)dx\right\}\in(0,1).

(3.15)

Hence, it suffices to show the lower bound:

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\geq\sqrt{n}r)\geq\exp\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(X_{1}(B(x,r))=0)dx\right\}.

(3.16)

To this end, by the branching property, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\geq\sqrt{n}r)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}(B(\sqrt{n}r))=0)

(3.17)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{u\in Z_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{S_{u}}}(Z_{n}(B(\sqrt{n}r))=0)\right]

(3.18)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\sum_{u\in Z_{0}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{S_{u}}}(Z_{n}(B(\sqrt{n}r))=0)}\right]

(3.19)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}-\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(\sqrt{n}r))=0)Z_{0}(dx)}\right]

(3.20)

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(\sqrt{n}r))>0)dx\right\}

(3.21)

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}n\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))>0)dx\right\},

(3.22)

where the 5-th equality follows from the Laplace functional formula of the Poisson random measure
Z_{0}
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
. For
|x|\leq(1+\varepsilon)r
and
n\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle n\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))>0)\leq\sup_{n\geq 0}n\mathbf{P}(|Z_{n}|>0)=:c<\infty,

(3.23)

where the last inequality follows from
[
1
, p. 19]
. Recall that
x^{(i)}
is the
i

\bar{M}_{n}:=\max\{|S_{u}|:u\in Z_{n}\}

2.6

such that for

\displaystyle n\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))>0)

(3.24)

\displaystyle\leq n\mathbf{P}(\bar{M}_{n}\geq\sqrt{n}(|x|-r))

(3.25)

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{2}_{i=1}n\mathbf{P}\left(M^{\prime}_{n,i}>\frac{(|x|-r)\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)

(3.26)

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{2}_{i=1}n\left[\mathbf{P}\left(M^{\prime\prime}_{n,i}>\frac{(|x|-r)\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)+\mathbf{P}\left(M^{\prime\prime\prime}_{n,i}<-\frac{(|x|-r)\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right]

(3.28)

Since

c{\bf 1}_{B(0,(1+\varepsilon)r)}(x)+\frac{\rho}{(|x|-r)^{4}}{\bf 1}_{B^{c}(0,(1+\varepsilon)r)}(x),x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

is integrable on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, by Fatou’s lemma, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\geq\sqrt{n}r)\geq\exp\left\{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}n\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))>0)dx\right\}.

(3.29)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\Big(Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))>0\Big)

(3.30)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}\Big[1-\prod_{v\in Z_{\lfloor(1-s)n\rfloor}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{S_{v}}}(Z_{\widetilde{{sn}}}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))=0)\Big]

(3.31)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}\Big[1-\exp\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{u}}(Z_{\widetilde{{sn}}}(B(\sqrt{n}x,\sqrt{n}r))=0)Z_{\lfloor(1-s)n\rfloor}(du)\Big\}\Big]

(3.32)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{E}\Big[1-\exp\Big\{-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{u}}(Z_{\widetilde{{sn}}}(B(\sqrt{n}x,\sqrt{n}r))>0)Z_{\lfloor(1-s)n\rfloor}(du)\Big\}\Big].

(3.33)

\lim_{y\to 1-}\log(y)/(1-y)=-1

\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{u}}(Z_{\widetilde{{sn}}}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))=0)\geq\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{\widetilde{{sn}}}|=0),

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Bigg|\frac{\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{u}}(Z_{\widetilde{{sn}}}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))=0)}{\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{u}}(Z_{\widetilde{{sn}}}(B(\sqrt{n}x,\sqrt{n}r))>0)}+1\Bigg|=0,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{u}}(Z_{\widetilde{{sn}}}(B(\sqrt{n}x,\sqrt{n}r))>0)Z_{\lfloor(1-s)n\rfloor}(du)

(3.34)

\displaystyle=\int_{|u-\sqrt{n}x|\geq\sqrt{n}(r+\varepsilon)}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{u}}(Z_{\widetilde{{sn}}}(B(\sqrt{n}x,\sqrt{n}r))>0)Z_{\lfloor(1-s)n\rfloor}(du)+

(3.35)

\displaystyle~~~~\int_{|u-\sqrt{n}x|<\sqrt{n}(r+\varepsilon)}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{u}}(Z_{\widetilde{{sn}}}(B(\sqrt{n}x,\sqrt{n}r))>0)Z_{\lfloor(1-s)n\rfloor}(du).

{|u-\sqrt{n}x|\geq\sqrt{n}(r+\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{u}}(Z_{\widetilde{{sn}}}(B(\sqrt{n}x,\sqrt{n}r))>0)

(3.38)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(\bar{M}_{\widetilde{{sn}}}\geq\varepsilon\sqrt{n})

(3.39)

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{2}_{i=1}\mathbf{P}\left(M^{\prime\prime}_{\widetilde{{sn}},i}>\frac{\varepsilon\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)+\sum^{2}_{i=1}\mathbf{P}\left(M^{\prime\prime\prime}_{\widetilde{{sn}},i}<-\frac{\varepsilon\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2c_{\varepsilon}s}{n}.

(3.41)

By (

), if
{|u-\sqrt{n}x|\leq\sqrt{n}(r+\varepsilon)}
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{u}}(Z_{\widetilde{{sn}}}(B(\sqrt{n}x,\sqrt{n}r))>0)\leq\mathbf{P}\left(|Z_{\widetilde{{sn}}}|>0\right)\leq\frac{c}{sn}.

(3.42)

Plugging (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{u}}(Z_{\widetilde{{sn}}}(B(\sqrt{n}x,\sqrt{n}r))>0)Z_{\lfloor(1-s)n\rfloor}(du)

(3.43)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2c_{\varepsilon}s}{n}Z_{\lfloor(1-s)n\rfloor}(B^{c}(\sqrt{n}x,\sqrt{n}(r+\varepsilon)))+\frac{c}{sn}Z_{\lfloor(1-s)n\rfloor}(B(\sqrt{n}x,\sqrt{n}(r+\varepsilon))).

(3.44)

Plugging (

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))>0)

(3.45)

\displaystyle\leq 1-\mathbf{E}\left[\exp\left\{-\frac{4c_{\varepsilon}s}{n}Z_{\lfloor(1-s)n\rfloor}(B^{c}(\sqrt{n}x,\sqrt{n}(r+\varepsilon)))-\right.\right.

(3.46)

\displaystyle~~~~\left.\left.\frac{2c}{sn}Z_{\lfloor(1-s)n\rfloor}(B(\sqrt{n}x,\sqrt{n}(r+\varepsilon)))\right\}\right]

(3.47)

\displaystyle=1-\Big(\mathbb{E}_{n\delta_{0}}\left[\exp\left\{-\frac{4c_{\varepsilon}s}{n}Z_{\lfloor(1-s)n\rfloor}(B^{c}(\sqrt{n}x,\sqrt{n}(r+\varepsilon)))-\right.\right.

(3.48)

\displaystyle~~~~\left.\left.\frac{2c}{sn}Z_{\lfloor(1-s)n\rfloor}(B(\sqrt{n}x,\sqrt{n}(r+\varepsilon)))\right\}\right]\Big)^{1/n}.

(3.49)

It is well-known that under the probability
\mathbb{P}_{n\delta_{0}}
(see
[
8
, p. 75]
for instance), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\{\frac{Z_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}(\sqrt{n}\cdot)}{n}\right\}_{t\geq 0}\overset{\text{weakly}}{=}\{X_{t}(\cdot)\}_{t\geq 0},

(3.50)

with
X_{0}=\delta_{0}
. It is easy to check that for a non-negative sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
, if
\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}=a\in(0,\infty)
, then
\lim_{n\to\infty}n(1-a_{n}^{1/n})=-\log a.
This, combined with (
3.45
), yields that for any
s\in(0,1)
and
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}n\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))>0)

(3.51)

\displaystyle\leq-\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{0}}\Bigg[\exp\left\{-{4c_{\varepsilon}s}X_{1-s}\Big(B^{c}\big({x},{r+\varepsilon}\big)\Big)-\frac{2c}{s}X_{1-s}\Big(B\big({x},{r+\varepsilon}\big)\Big)\right\}\Bigg].

(3.52)

We proceed to deal with the r.h.s. (
3.51
). Recall that
\lambda
is the Lebeguese measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. By (
1.6
), we have

\displaystyle\exp\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{0}}\Bigg[\exp\left\{-{4c_{\varepsilon}s}X_{1-s}\Big(B^{c}\big({x},{r+\varepsilon}\big)\Big)-\frac{2c}{s}X_{1-s}\Big(B\big({x},{r+\varepsilon}\big)\Big)\right\}\Bigg]dx\right\}

(3.53)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\exp\Bigg\{X_{1-s}\left(-4c_{\varepsilon}s{\bf 1}_{B^{c}({r+\varepsilon})}-\frac{2c}{s}{\bf 1}_{B(r+\varepsilon)}\right)\Bigg\}\right].

(3.54)

From
[
3
, p. 52]
, we have the following scale invariance property: if
X_{0}=\lambda
, then for any
\eta,~t>0
and bounded measurable function
\phi
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\displaystyle X_{t}(\phi)\overset{\text{Law}}{=}\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}X_{\eta^{2}t}(\phi(\cdot/\eta)).

(3.55)

Let
t=1-s
and
\eta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-s}}
, then the scaling property yields that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\exp\Bigg\{X_{1-s}\left(-4c_{\varepsilon}s{\bf 1}_{B^{c}({r+\varepsilon})}-\frac{2c}{s}{\bf 1}_{B(r+\varepsilon)}\right)\Bigg\}\right]

(3.56)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\exp\Bigg\{-\frac{4c_{\varepsilon}s}{1-s}X_{1}\left(\frac{B^{c}(r+\varepsilon)}{\sqrt{1-s}}\right)\Bigg\}\exp\Bigg\{-\frac{2c}{s(1-s)}X_{1}\left(\frac{B(r+\varepsilon)}{\sqrt{1-s}}\right)\Bigg\}\right]

(3.57)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\exp\Bigg\{-\frac{4c_{\varepsilon}s}{1-s}X_{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)\Bigg\}\exp\Bigg\{-\frac{2c}{s(1-s)}X_{1}\left(B(r+2\varepsilon)\right)\Bigg\}\right],

(3.58)

where the last inequality follows from the fact that since
s\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{(r+2\varepsilon)^{2}}
, we have

\frac{B(r+\varepsilon)}{\sqrt{1-s}}\subset B(r+2\varepsilon).

By the dominated convergence theorem, (
3.56
) yields that

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0+}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\exp\Bigg\{X_{1-s}\left(-4c_{\varepsilon}s{\bf 1}_{B^{c}({r+\varepsilon})}-\frac{2c}{s}{\bf 1}_{B(r+\varepsilon)}\right)\Bigg\}\right]

(3.59)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(X_{1}\left(B(r+2\varepsilon)\right)=0)

(3.60)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(X_{(r+2\varepsilon)^{-2}}\left(B(1)\right)=0\right)

(3.61)

\displaystyle=e^{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}\left(X_{(r+2\varepsilon)^{-2}}\left(B(1)\right)=0\right)dy},

(3.62)

where the first equality comes from (
3.55
), and the second equality follows from similar arguments as those of (
3.3
). Since

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}\left(X_{(r+2\varepsilon)^{-2}}\left(B(1)\right)=0\right)dy=\log\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(X_{1}\left(B(r+2\varepsilon)\right)=0),

it follows that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}\left(X_{(r+2\varepsilon)^{-2}}\left(B(1)\right)=0\right)dy

is increasing as
\varepsilon\to 0+
. Thus, by Lévy’s monotonic convergence theorem and Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}\left(X_{(r+2\varepsilon)^{-2}}\left(B(1)\right)=0\right)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}\left(X_{(r+2\varepsilon)^{-2}}\left(B(1)\right)=0\right)dy

(3.63)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}\left(X_{r^{-2}}\left(B(1)\right)=0\right)dy

(3.64)

\displaystyle=\log\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(X_{1}\left(B(r)\right)=0),

(3.65)

where the last equality follows from the last equality of (
3.59
).

Finally, by (
3.29
), we have for any
\varepsilon>0,~s\in\left(0,\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{(r+2\varepsilon)^{2}}\right)
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\geq r\right)

(3.66)

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left\{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}n\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))>0)dx\right\}

(3.67)

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{0}}\Bigg[\exp\left\{-{4c_{\varepsilon}s}X_{1-s}\Big(B^{c}\big({x},{r+\varepsilon}\big)\Big)-\frac{2c}{s}X_{1-s}\Big(B\big({x},{r+\varepsilon}\big)\Big)\right\}\Bigg]dx\right\}

(3.68)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\exp\Bigg\{X_{1-s}\left(-4c_{\varepsilon}s{\bf 1}_{B^{c}({r+\varepsilon})}-\frac{2c}{s}{\bf 1}_{B(r+\varepsilon)}\right)\Bigg\}\right],

(3.69)

where the second inequality comes from (
3.51
).
Taking
s\to 0+
on the both sides of (
3.66
) and then using (
3.59
) yields that for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\geq r\right)\geq e^{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}\left(X_{(r+2\varepsilon)^{-2}}\left(B(1)\right)=0\right)dy}.

(3.70)

Taking
\varepsilon\to 0+
on both sides of (
3.70
) and then using (
3.63
) yields that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\geq r\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(X_{1}\left(B(r)\right)=0)

(3.71)

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(X_{1}(B(x,r))=0\right)dx\right\}.

(3.72)

This, combined with (
3.14
), concludes Theorem
1.1
.

\Box

4
Proof of Theorem
1.2
: Empty Ball With Infinite Variance

This section aims to study the empty ball of the branching random walk with infinite variance offspring law. The proofs are divided into three cases according to
d\beta<2
,
d\beta=2
and
d\beta>2
.

Case 1:
d\beta<2.
The proof is a modification of
[
22
, Theorem 1.8]
. So, we just put some additional steps here.

Proof.

Recall that
b_{n}=[nL(\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(Z_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{d})>0))]^{\frac{1}{\beta d}},~n\geq 1
, where
L(\cdot)
is a slowly varying function at
0
. Similarly to (
3.17
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\geq b_{n}r)=\exp\left\{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(b_{n}r))>0)dx\right\}.

(4.1)

We first claim that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}\left(Z_{n}(B(b_{n}r))>0\Big||Z_{n}|>0\right)\geq\mathbb{P}(|x+S_{n}|\leq b_{n}r).

(4.2)

In fact, under the event
\{|Z_{n}|>0\}
, there exist many random walks (at least one) alive at time
n
. So, the probability of exists one particle located in
B(b_{n}r)
at time
n
is larger than the probability of exactly one random walk located in
B(b_{n}r)
.
For the upper bound of
\mathbb{P}(R_{n}/b_{n}\geq r)
, it is simple to see that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(b_{n}r))>0)dx

(4.3)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(|Z_{n}|>0)\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}\left(Z_{n}(B(b_{n}r))>0\Big||Z_{n}|>0\right)dx

(4.4)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(|Z_{n}|>0)\mathbb{P}(|x+S_{n}|\leq b_{n}r)dx

(4.5)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\bf 1}_{\{|x+S_{n}|\leq b_{n}r\}}dx\right]

(4.6)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)b_{n}^{d}v_{d}(r),

(4.7)

where the first inequality follows from (
4.2
). By
[
19
, Lemma 2]
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)b_{n}^{d}=(\beta^{-1})^{\beta^{-1}}.

(4.8)

This, combined with (
4.3
), yields that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{b_{n}}\geq r\right)\leq\exp\left\{-v_{d}(r)(\beta^{-1})^{\beta^{-1}}\right\}.

(4.9)

We proceed to deal with the lower bound. Fix
\delta>0
. By the Markov inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(b_{n}r))>0)dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|x|<b_{n}(r+\delta)}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)dx+\int_{|x|\geq b_{n}(r+\delta)}\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}[Z_{n}(B(b_{n}r))]dx

(4.10)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)b_{n}^{d}v_{d}(r+\delta)+\int_{|x|\geq b_{n}(r+\delta)}\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{n}\in B(b_{n}r))dx.

(4.11)

Next, we are going to show that the second term on the r.h.s. of (
4.10
) tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
. Copied from
[
12
, Corollary 1.8]
, if
\mathbb{E}[|X|^{\alpha}]<\infty
for some
\alpha\geq 2
, then there exist positive constants
c_{\alpha}
and
c^{\prime}_{\alpha}
such that for any
n\geq 1
and
x>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|S^{(1)}_{n}|\geq x)\leq\frac{c_{\alpha}n}{x^{\alpha}}+2\exp\{-c^{\prime}_{\alpha}x^{2}/n\}.

(4.12)

By Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geq b_{n}(r+\delta)}\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{n}\in(B(b_{n}r)))dx

(4.13)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\bf 1}_{B^{c}(b_{n}(r+\delta))}(x){\bf 1}_{\{|x+S_{n}|\leq b_{n}r\}}dx,|S_{n}|\geq b_{n}\delta\right]

(4.14)

\displaystyle\leq v_{d}(b_{n}r)\mathbb{P}\left(|S_{n}|\geq b_{n}\delta\right)

(4.15)

\displaystyle\leq db_{n}^{d}v_{d}(r)\mathbb{P}\left(|S^{(1)}_{n}|\geq b_{n}\delta/\sqrt{d}\right)

(4.16)

\displaystyle\leq dd^{\alpha/2}v_{d}(r)b_{n}^{d}\frac{c_{\alpha}n}{(b_{n}\delta)^{\alpha}}+2dv_{d}(r)b_{n}^{d}\exp\{-c^{\prime}_{\alpha}b_{n}^{2}\delta^{2}/(dn)\},

(4.17)

where the last inequality follows from (
4.12
). If
\alpha>(\beta+1)d
, then the first term on the r.h.s. of (
4.13
) tends to
0
. If
\beta d<2
, then the second term on the r.h.s. of (
4.13
) tends to
0
. Thus, under the assumption of Theorem
1.2
(i), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{|x|\geq b_{n}(r+\delta)}\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{n}\in B(b_{n}r))dx=0.

(4.18)

Finally, plugging (
4.18
) into (
4.10
) and then using (
4.8
) entails that for any
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(b_{n}r))>0)dx\leq v_{d}(r+\delta)(\beta^{-1})^{\beta^{-1}}.

(4.19)

Letting
\delta\to 0+
in (
4.19
), then pugging it into (
4.1
) yields that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{b_{n}}\geq r\right)\geq\exp\left\{-v_{d}(r)(\beta^{-1})^{\beta^{-1}}\right\}.

(4.20)

This, together with (
4.9
), concludes Theorem
1.2
.

\Box

Case 2:
d\beta=2.
The proof of this case is similar to the proof of the
2
-dimensional branching random walk with finite variance offspring law (i.e. Theorem
1.1
).

Proof.

If
d\beta=2
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\geq\sqrt{n}r)

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(\sqrt{n}r))>0)dx\right\}

(4.21)

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{d/2}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(y,r))>0)dy\right\}

(4.22)

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))>0)dx\right\}.

(4.23)

Assume
\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{{}^{1+\beta}}\sum^{\infty}_{k=n}p_{k}=\kappa(\beta)
with
\beta\in(0,1)
. It is well-known that (e.g.
[
4
, (1.15)]
), under the probability
\mathbb{P}_{\lfloor n^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\rfloor\delta_{0}}
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\{\frac{Z_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}(\sqrt{n}\cdot)}{n^{\frac{1}{\beta}}}\right\}_{t\geq 0}\overset{\text{weakly}}{=}\{X_{t}(\cdot)\}_{t\geq 0},

(4.24)

where
\{X_{t}(\cdot)\}_{t\geq 0}
is a super-Brownian motion with initial value
X_{0}=\ \delta_{0}
and stable branching mechanism
\psi(u)=\frac{1}{\beta}{\kappa(\beta)\Gamma(1-\beta)}u^{1+\beta}
.
The following steps are almost the same as the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The main changes are to replace
n
with
n^{\frac{1}{\beta}}
and replace
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. So, we feel free to omit the proofs here.

\Box

Case 3:
d\beta>2.
One can observe that the result of this case is parallel with
[
22
, Theorem 1.5]
. However, since the offspring law may have infinite variance, the Paley-Zygmund inequality used in
[
22
, Theorem 1.5]
does not work anymore. To overcome this difficulty, we use the spine decomposition method developed in Rapenne
[
15
, Lemma 6.2]
. Nonetheless, since our underlying motion is a general random walk rather than the stable process studied in
[
15
, Lemma 6.2]
, our proofs are more delicate.

Let’s give a brief introduction to the spine decomposition method. Let
\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be the natural filtration of
\{|Z_{n}|\}_{n\geq 0}
. Since
(\{|Z_{n}|\}_{n\geq 0},\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0},\mathbf{P})
is a martingale with mean
1
, we can define the probability measure
\mathbf{Q}
through

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathbf{Q}}{d\mathbf{P}}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{n}:=|Z_{n}|.

(4.25)

Let
p^{*}_{k}:=kp_{k},~k\geq 0
. Define the size biased branching random walk as follows. We start with one particle called
w_{0}
located at the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It defines the generation
0
of the size biased branching random walk. For generation
n\geq 1
, we define it recursively. Let
Z_{n}
be the set of particles in generation
n
. We also have a marked
particle
w_{n}
among
Z_{n}
. The particle
w_{n}
produce children according to the offspring law
\{p^{*}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
(note that since
p^{*}_{0}=0
,
w_{n}
has at least one children). The children of
w_{n}
jump independently from the position of
w_{n}
according to the law of the step size
X
under
\mathbf{P}
. Among the children of
w_{n}
, we choose uniformly at random a special particle called
w_{n+1}
. Moreover, every particle
u\neq w_{n}
at generation
n
gives birth independently according to the law of
\{p_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
. The offsprings of
u

jump from the position of
u
according to the law of
X
under
\mathbf{P}
independently. The offsprings of
Z_{n}
with their new positions (including the marked particle
w_{n+1}
) form the
(n+1)
-th generation
Z_{n+1}
. Let
\{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be the natural filtration of
(\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0},\{w_{n}\}_{n\geq 0})
and
Q^{*}
be the extension of the measure
Q
on
\{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
. With this construction, we call
(\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0},\{w_{n}\}_{n\geq 0},\{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0},Q^{*})
is a size biased branching random walk with spine
\{w_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
. From this definition, we have
for every
n\geq 1
,
Q^{*}|\mathcal{F}_{n}=Q|\mathcal{F}_{n}
. Moreover, for every particle
u
at generation
n
,

\displaystyle Q^{*}(w_{n}=u|\mathcal{F}_{n})=\frac{1}{|Z_{n}|}.

(4.26)

Recall that
\{Z^{u}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is the sub-branching random walk emanating from particle
u
with
Z^{u}_{0}=\delta_{0}
.
X_{u}
is the displacement of particle
u
.
b(w_{k})
is the set of brothers of
w_{k}
.
S_{w_{k}}
is the position of
w_{k}
. For
(\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0},\{w_{n}\}_{n\geq 0},\{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0},Q^{*})
, it is simple to see that for any
n\geq 1
, measurable set
C
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle Z_{n}(C-x)={\bf 1}_{\{S_{w_{n}\in C-x}\}}+\sum^{n-1}_{k=0}\sum_{u\in b(w_{k+1})}Z^{u}_{n-k-1}(C-x-S_{w_{k}}-X_{u}),

(4.27)

which is called the
spine decomposition.
For more detailed description; see
[
15
, Section 2.4]
.

Now, we are ready to prove Theorem
1.2
(iii).

Proof.

Similarly to (
3.17
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\geq r)=\exp\left\{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(r))>0)dx\right\}.

(4.28)

Fix
r>0
. Let
A:=B(r)
for short. Thus, to prove Theorem
1.2
, it suffices to calculate
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}(A-x)>0)dx
.

We first express
\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}(A-x)>0)
as a functional of a size biased branching random walk by the spine decomposition method. Fix
n\geq 1
.
Let

\displaystyle Y^{n}_{k}(z)=\sum_{u\in b(w_{k+1})}Z^{u}_{n-k-1}(A-z-X_{u}),0\leq k\leq n-1.

(4.29)

Note that
\{b(w_{k+1})\}_{k\geq 0}
are i.i.d. and independent of
\{Z^{u}_{k-1}(A-z-X_{u})\}_{\{u\in b(w_{k}),k\geq 0\}}
. Moreover,
\{Z^{u}_{k-1}(A-z-X_{u})\}_{\{u\in b(w_{k}),k\geq 0\}}
are i.i.d.. Thus, for
n+1\geq k\geq 1
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle Y^{n}_{n-k}(z)=\sum_{u\in b(w_{n-k+1})}Z^{u}_{k-1}(A-z-X_{u})\overset{\text{Law}}{=}\sum_{u\in b(w_{k+1})}Z^{u}_{k-1}(A-z-X_{u})=:Y_{k}(z).

(4.30)

By (
4.26
), we have
Q^{*}
-a.s. (note that
Q^{*}(|Z_{n}|\geq 1)=Q(|Z_{n}|\geq 1)=1
),

\displaystyle{Q^{*}}(S_{w_{n}}\in A-x|\mathcal{F}_{n})=\frac{Z_{n}(A-x)}{|Z_{n}|}.

(4.31)

Define
\frac{0}{0}=:0
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[\frac{{\bf 1}_{\{S_{w_{n}}\in A-x\}}}{Z_{n}(A-x)}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[\frac{{\bf 1}_{\{Z_{n}(A-x)\geq 1\}}}{Z_{n}(A-x)}\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[{{\bf 1}_{\{S_{w_{n}}\in A-x\}}}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{n}\right]\right]

(4.32)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[\frac{{\bf 1}_{\{Z_{n}(A-x)\geq 1\}}}{Z_{n}(A-x)}\frac{Z_{n}(A-x)}{|Z_{n}|}\right]

(4.33)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{Q}\left[\frac{{\bf 1}_{\{Z_{n}(A-x)\geq 1\}}}{|Z_{n}|}\right]

(4.34)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}(A-x)\geq 1).

(4.35)

This, combined with (
4.27
), yields that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}(A-x)\geq 1)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[\frac{{\bf 1}_{\{S_{w_{n}}\in A-x\}}}{Z_{n}(A-x)}\right]

(4.36)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[\frac{{\bf 1}_{\{S_{w_{n}}\in A-x\}}}{{\bf 1}_{\{S_{w_{n}\in A-x}\}}+\sum^{n-1}_{k=0}\sum_{u\in b(w_{k+1})}Z^{u}_{n-k-1}(A-x-S_{w_{k}}-X_{u})}\right]

(4.37)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[\frac{{\bf 1}_{\{S_{w_{n}}\in A-x\}}}{1+\sum^{n-1}_{k=0}Y^{n}_{k}(x+S_{w_{k}})}\right]

(4.38)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[\frac{{\bf 1}_{\{S_{w_{n}}\in A-x\}}}{1+\sum^{n}_{i=1}Y^{n}_{n-i}(x+S_{w_{n-i}})}\right]

(4.39)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[\frac{{\bf 1}_{\{S_{w_{n}}\in A-x\}}}{1+\sum^{n}_{k=1}Y_{k}(x+S_{w_{n-k}})}\right]

(4.40)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[\frac{{\bf 1}_{\{S_{w_{n}}\in A-x\}}}{1+\sum^{n}_{k=1}Y_{k}(x+S_{w_{n}}-S_{w_{k}})}\right].

(4.41)

The third equality of (
4.36
) comes from (
4.29
). The penultimate equality follows from (
4.30
) and the fact that
\{S_{w_{i}}\}_{i\geq 1}
and
\{Y_{i}(z)\}_{i\geq 1}
are independent. The last equality follows from the fact that the backward random walk
\{S_{w_{n}}-S_{w_{n-i}}\}_{0\leq i\leq n}
has the same distribution as
\{S_{w_{i}}\}_{0\leq i\leq n}
and independent of
\{Y_{k}(z)\}_{k\geq 0}.

Next, we split
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}(A-x)>0)dx
into three parts. By (
4.36
), we have for any
M>0
and integers
n\geq K\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}(A-x)>0)dx

(4.42)

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|>M\sqrt{n}}\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}(A-x)>0)dx+\int_{|x|\leq M\sqrt{n}}\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[\frac{{\bf 1}_{\{S_{w_{n}}\in A-x\}}}{1+\sum^{n}_{k=1}Y_{k}(x+S_{w_{n}}-S_{w_{k}})}\right]dx

(4.43)

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|>M\sqrt{n}}\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}(A-x)>0)dx+\int_{|x|\leq M\sqrt{n}}I_{K,n}(x)dx+

(4.44)

\displaystyle~~~~\int_{|x|\leq M\sqrt{n}}\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[\frac{{\bf 1}_{\{S_{w_{n}}\in A-x\}}}{1+\sum^{K}_{k=1}{Y}_{k}(x+S_{w_{n}}-S_{w_{k}})})\right]dx,

(4.45)

where

\displaystyle I_{K,n}(x):=\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[\frac{{\bf 1}_{\{S_{w_{n}}\in A-x\}}}{1+\sum^{n}_{k=1}Y_{k}(x+S_{w_{n}}-S_{w_{k}})}-\frac{{\bf 1}_{\{S_{w_{n}}\in A-x\}}}{1+\sum^{K}_{k=1}Y_{k}(x+S_{w_{n}}-S_{w_{k}})}\right].

(4.46)

Hence, to prove the theorem, it suffices to study asymptotics of the three terms on the r.h.s. of (
4.42
) respectively.

We first deal with the first term on the r.h.s. of (
4.42
). Recall that
A=B(r)
. By the Markov inequality and Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\int_{|x|>M\sqrt{n}}\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}(A-x)>0)dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|x|>M\sqrt{n}}\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}[Z_{n}(B(r))]dx

(4.47)

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|>M\sqrt{n}}\mathbb{P}(|S_{n}+x|<r)dx

(4.48)

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|>M\sqrt{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\bf 1}_{\{|y+x|<r\}}\mathbb{P}(S_{n}\in dy)dx

(4.49)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|y|\geq M\sqrt{n}-r}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\bf 1}_{\{|y+x|<r\}}dx\mathbb{P}(S_{n}\in dy)

(4.50)

\displaystyle=v_{d}(r)\mathbb{P}(|S_{n}|\geq M\sqrt{n}-r).

(4.51)

By the central limit theorem, above yields that

\displaystyle\lim_{M\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{|x|>M\sqrt{n}}\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}(A-x)>0)dx=0.

(4.52)

We proceed to deal with the second term on the r.h.s. of (
4.42
). Denote by

\mathcal{G}
the
\sigma
-field generated by the spine and number of children of particles in the spine
and positions of the brothers of the spine. Since the covariance matrix of
X
is invertible, by
[
20
, p. 218, Corollary 1]
, it entails that there exists a constant
c>0
depending only on
A
such that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
n\geq k\geq 1
and particle
u
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}[Z^{u}_{k-1}(A-x-S_{w_{n-k}}-X_{u})|\mathcal{G}]

(4.53)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(S_{k-1}\in A-z-\rho)\big|_{z=x+S_{w_{n-k}},\rho=X_{u}}

(4.54)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{{k}^{d/2}}.

(4.55)

Since
(\sum^{n}_{k=K+1}\lambda_{i})^{\beta}\leq\sum^{n}_{k=K+1}\lambda^{\beta}_{i}
for any non-negative
\lambda_{i}
and
\beta\in(0,1)
, by the Markov inequality, there exists a constant
C_{A}
depending only on
A
such that for any
n\geq K+1\geq 1
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle Q^{*}\left(S_{w_{n}}\in A-x,\sum^{n}_{k=K+1}Y_{k}(x+S_{w_{n-k}})\geq 1\right)

(4.56)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[{\bf 1}_{\{S_{w_{n}}\in A-x\}}\sum^{n}_{k=K+1}\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[Y_{k}(x+S_{w_{n-k}})^{\beta}|\mathcal{G}\right]\right]

(4.57)

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{n}_{k=K+1}\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[{\bf 1}_{\{S_{w_{n}}\in A-x\}}\left(\sum_{u\in b(w_{k+1})}\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}[Z^{u}_{k-1}(A-x-S_{w_{n-k}}-X_{u})|\mathcal{G}]\right)^{\beta}\right]

(4.58)

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{n}_{k=K+1}\frac{c^{\beta}}{k^{d\beta/2}}\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[{\bf 1}_{\{S_{w_{n}}\in A-x\}}|b(w_{k+1})|^{\beta}\right]

(4.59)

\displaystyle\leq C_{A}n^{-d/2}\sum^{+\infty}_{k=K+1}\frac{1}{k^{d\beta/2}}.

(4.60)

In (
4.56
), the second inequality follows from Jensen’s inequality. The third inequality follows from (
4.53
), where
|b(w_{k+1})|
stands for the cardinality of the set
b(w_{k+1})
. The last equality follows from the fact that

\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[{\bf 1}_{\{S_{w_{n}}\in A-x\}}|b(w_{k+1})|^{\beta}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[|b(w_{k+1})|^{\beta}\right]=\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}(k-1)^{\beta}p^{*}_{k}\leq\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}k^{1+\beta}p_{k}<\infty.

Since
\beta d>2
, the summation on the r.h.s. of (
4.56
) is finite. Thus, by (
4.46
), it follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{K\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big|\int_{|x|\leq M\sqrt{n}}I_{K,n}(x)dx\Big|

(4.61)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{K\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{|x|\leq M\sqrt{n}}Q^{*}\left(S_{w_{n}}\in A-x,\sum^{n}_{k=K+1}Y_{k}(x+S_{w_{n-k}})\geq 1\right)dx

(4.62)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{K\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum^{+\infty}_{k=K+1}\frac{C_{A}}{k^{d\beta/2}}n^{-d/2}\int_{|x|\leq M\sqrt{n}}dx=0.

(4.63)

It remains to deal with the third term on the r.h.s. of (
4.42
). It is easy to see that for
n
satisfying
M\sqrt{n}/2>r+|S_{w_{K}}|
, on the event
\{|S_{w_{n}}-S_{w_{K}}|\leq M\sqrt{n}/2\}
, it follows that

\displaystyle A-S_{w_{K}}-(S_{w_{n}}-S_{w_{K}})\subset B(M\sqrt{n}).

(4.64)

Recall that
\lambda
is the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Recall that
\mathcal{F}^{*}_{k}
is the
\sigma
-algebra which contains the information of the size biased branching random walk
(\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0},\{w_{n}\}_{n\geq 0})
up to time
k
. Set
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{k}}(\cdot):=\mathbb{P}(\cdot|{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{k}})
,
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{k}}[\cdot]:=\mathbb{E}[\cdot|{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{k}}].
By Fubini’s theorem, if
n>(2(r+|S_{w_{K}}|)/M)^{2}
, then for any measurable set
B\subset A
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq M\sqrt{n}}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{K}}(S_{w_{n}}-S_{w_{K}}+x+S_{w_{K}}\in B)dx

(4.65)

\displaystyle=:\int_{|x|\leq M\sqrt{n}}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{K}}(S_{w_{n}}-S_{w_{K}}+x+S_{w_{K}}\in B,|S_{w_{n}}-S_{w_{K}}|\leq M\sqrt{n}/2)dx+H_{n}(B)

(4.66)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{K}}\left[\int_{|x|\leq M\sqrt{n}}{\bf 1}_{\{x\in B-S_{w_{n}}+S_{w_{K}}-S_{w_{K}}\}}dx,|S_{w_{n}}-S_{w_{K}}|\leq M\sqrt{n}/2\right]+H_{n}(B)

(4.67)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{K}}\left[\lambda\Big(B(M\sqrt{n})\cap\big(B-S_{w_{K}}-(S_{w_{n}}-S_{w_{K}})\big)\Big),|S_{w_{n}}-S_{w_{K}}|\leq M\sqrt{n}/2\right]+H_{n}(B)

(4.68)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{K}}\left[\lambda(B),|S_{w_{n}}-S_{w_{K}}|\leq M\sqrt{n}/2\right]+H_{n}(B)

(4.69)

\displaystyle=\lambda(B)\mathbb{P}(|S_{w_{n}}-S_{w_{K}}|\leq M\sqrt{n}/2)+H_{n}(B),

(4.70)

where the fourth equality follows from (
4.64
). Hence, for
n>(2(r+|S_{w_{K}}|)/M)^{2}
, the third term on the r.h.s. of (
4.42
) equals to

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq M\sqrt{n}}\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[\frac{{\bf 1}_{\{S_{w_{n}}\in A-x\}}}{1+\sum^{K}_{k=1}{Y}_{k}(x+S_{w_{n}}-S_{w_{k}})})\right]dx

(4.71)

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|\leq M\sqrt{n}}\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[\int_{A}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{K}}(S_{w_{n}}-S_{w_{K}}+x+S_{w_{K}}\in dy)}{1+\sum^{K}_{k=1}{Y}_{k}(y-S_{w_{k}})}\right]dx

(4.72)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[\int_{A}\frac{1}{1+\sum^{K}_{k=1}{Y}_{k}(y-S_{w_{k}})}dy\right]\mathbb{P}(|S_{w_{n}}-S_{w_{K}}|\leq M\sqrt{n}/2)+

(4.73)

\displaystyle~~~~\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[\int_{A}\frac{1}{1+\sum^{K}_{k=1}{Y}_{k}(y-S_{w_{k}})}H_{n}(dy)\right]

(4.74)

\displaystyle=:Q_{1}+Q_{2}.

(4.75)

Since
H_{n}(B)\leq\lambda(B)\mathbb{P}(|S_{w_{n}}-S_{w_{K}}|>M\sqrt{n}/2)
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{M\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}Q_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{M\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[H_{n}(A)\right]

(4.76)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{M\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda(A)\mathbb{P}(|S_{w_{n}}-S_{w_{K}}|>M\sqrt{n}/2)=0,

(4.77)

where the last equality follows from the central limit theorem. For
Q_{1}
, it is simple to see that

\displaystyle\lim_{M\to\infty}\lim_{K\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}Q_{1}=\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[\int_{A}\frac{1}{1+\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}{Y}_{k}(y-S_{w_{k}})}dy\right]=:I_{A},

(4.78)

where the first equality follows from applying the central limit theorem as
n\to\infty
and then the dominated convergence theorem as
K\to\infty
. Plugging (
4.78
) and (
4.76
) into (
4.71
) yields that

\displaystyle\lim_{M\to\infty}\lim_{K\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{|x|\leq M\sqrt{n}}\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[\frac{{\bf 1}_{\{S_{w_{n}}\in A-x\}}}{1+\sum^{K}_{k=1}{Y}_{k}(x+S_{w_{n}}-S_{w_{k}})})\right]dx=I_{A}.

(4.79)

We claim that
I_{A}\in(0,\infty)
. In fact, by Fubini’s theorem, we can easily obtain that
I_{A}\leq\lambda(A)<\infty.
We proceed to prove
I_{A}>0
. It is simple to see that for
y\in A=B(r)
, we have

\displaystyle Y_{k}(y-S_{w_{k}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in b(w_{k+1})}Z^{u}_{k-1}(A-y+S_{w_{k}}-X_{u})

(4.80)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{u\in b(w_{k+1})}Z^{u}_{k-1}(B(2r)+S_{w_{k}}-X_{u})=:\hat{Y}_{k}(-S_{w_{k}}).

(4.81)

Using similar arguments to obtain (
4.56
), it yields that there exists a constant
c_{r}
depending only on
r
such that for any
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle Q^{*}(\hat{Y}_{k}(-S_{w_{k}})\geq 1)\leq\frac{c_{r}}{k^{d\beta/2}},

which is summable w.r.t.
k
. Hence, by the Borel-Cantelli’s lemma,
Q^{*}
-almost surely, there is only a finite number of integers
k
such that
\hat{Y}_{k}(-S_{w_{k}})\geq 1
. Namely,

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\hat{Y}_{k}(-S_{w_{k}})<\infty,~Q^{*}\text{-a.s.}

(4.82)

This, together with Fubini’s theorem, implies that

\displaystyle I_{A}

\displaystyle=\int_{A}\mathbb{E}_{Q^{*}}\left[\frac{1}{1+\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}{Y}_{k}(y-S_{w_{k}})}\right]dy

(4.83)

\displaystyle=\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{1}{(1+t)^{2}}\int_{A}{Q^{*}}\left(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}{Y}_{k}(y-S_{w_{k}})\in[0,t]\right)dydt

(4.84)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda(A)\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{1}{(1+t)^{2}}{Q^{*}}\left(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\hat{Y}_{k}(-S_{w_{k}})\in[0,t]\right)dt

(4.85)

\displaystyle>0,

(4.86)

where the first inequality follows from (
4.80
) and the last inequality follows from (
4.82
).

Finally, taking first
n\to\infty
, then
K\to\infty
and at last
M\to\infty
on the both sides of (
4.42
), and plugging (
4.52
), (
4.61
) and (
4.79
) into (
4.42
) yields that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}(A-x)>0)dx=I_{A}.

This, combined with (
4.28
), concludes that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\geq r)=e^{-I_{A}}\in(0,1).

We have finished the proof of Theorem
1.2
(iii).

\Box

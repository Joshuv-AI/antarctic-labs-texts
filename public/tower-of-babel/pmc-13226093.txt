Trastuzumab deruxtecan in HER2-expressing gynecologic cancers from DESTINY-PanTumor02: antitumor activity, safety, and exploratory biomarker analyses
Authors: Jung-Yun Lee, Ana Oaknin, Luis Manso, Antonio González-Martín, Iwona Ługowska, Domenica Lorusso, Susana Banerjee, John B Liao, Chien-Hsing Lu, Naiyarat Prasongsook, Bohuslav Melichar, Chiedozie Anoka, Lindsey Jung, Flavia Michelini, Soham Puvvada, Funda Meric-Bernstam, Vicky Makker
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate antitumor activity, safety, and exploratory subgroup analyses for trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) in human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-expressing (immunohistochemistry [IHC] 3+ and 2+) gynecologic cancers.MethodsDESTINY-PanTumor02 is a 2-part, open-label, multicenter, phase 2 study. Pretreated patients with locally advanced, metastatic, or unresectable HER2-expressing solid tumors received T-DXd 5.4 mg/kg. Primary endpoint was investigator-assessed (INV) confirmed objective response rate (ORR). Secondary endpoints included safety, ORR by independent central review (ICR), progression-free survival, and overall survival. Exploratory endpoints included ORRs by biomarker status. This analysis reports data from the endometrial, cervical, and ovarian cancer cohorts.ResultsAt primary analysis data cutoff, 120 patients received treatment across 3 gynecologic cancer cohorts. Median follow-up (range) was 19.9 months (0.8–31.1), 12.6 months (0.9–31.0), and 13.1 months (0.7–30.6) in the endometrial, cervical, and ovarian cancer cohorts, respectively. INV confirmed ORRs (n/N [95% confidence interval]) in the endometrial, cervical, and ovarian cancer cohorts were 57.5% (23/40 [40.9–73.0]), 50.0% (20/40 [33.8–66.2]), and 45.0% (18/40 [29.3–61.5]), respectively, and by ICR were 57.5% (23/40 [40.9–73.0]), 37.5% (15/40 [22.7–54.2]), and 42.5% (17/40 [27.0–59.1]), respectively. ORRs were highest for the HER2 IHC 3+ populations. Although patient numbers were small in some subgroups, objective responses were observed regardless of tumor histology, prior treatment, and biomarker expression/mutation status. Adjudicated interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis occurred in 13 patients (10.8%; Grade 1, n=4; Grade 2, n=8; Grade 5, n=1).ConclusionT-DXd induced clinically meaningful ORRs, with safety consistent with the known profile. Data further support T-DXd as a therapeutic option for pretreated HER2-expressing gynecologic cancers.Trial RegistrationClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT04482309Keywords: Biomarkers, Tumor; Clinical Trial, Phase II; Endometrial Neoplasms; Ovarian Neoplasms; Trastuzumab Deruxtecan; Uterine Cervical Neoplasms

INTRODUCTION

Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2; ERBB2) expression (immunohistochemistry [IHC] 3+/2+) is observed in a wide range of solid tumors [1,2], and can be associated with a biologically aggressive phenotype, increased risk of recurrence, and limited benefit with chemotherapy-based regimens [1,3,4,5]. As patients with HER2-expressing gynecologic cancers may have a poor prognosis [3,4,6], there remains an unmet medical need to improve outcomes, as HER2-directed therapies are not currently standard of care [1,3,7,8,9]. In 2022, gynecologic cancers collectively accounted for 15.2% of newly diagnosed cancers and 15.8% of cancer-related deaths in female patients worldwide [10]. Furthermore, the reported prevalence of HER2 IHC 3+ and 2+ expression combined in endometrial, cervical, and ovarian cancers is 18% to 62% [1,2,11,12,13], 21% to 29% [2,14,15], and 4% to 28% [2,16,17,18,19], respectively.

Trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) is an antibody-drug conjugate comprising a humanized immunoglobulin G1 monoclonal antibody specifically targeting HER2, a tetrapeptide-based cleavable linker, and a potent topoisomerase I (TOP1) inhibitor payload [20,21]. T-DXd is approved in multiple countries worldwide for the treatment of HER2-positive, HER2-low, and HER2-ultralow breast cancer, HER2-positive gastric or gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma, and HER2-mutant non-small cell lung cancer [22,23,24]. T-DXd is also approved in multiple countries, including the US and UK, for the treatment of adult patients with unresectable or metastatic HER2-positive (IHC 3+) solid tumors that have progressed after prior treatment and have no satisfactory alternative therapy [24,25,26]. In Part 1 of the DESTINY-PanTumor02 study, T-DXd demonstrated clinically meaningful response rates, progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS) in pretreated patients with HER2-expressing solid tumors, with particular benefit observed in endometrial, cervical, and ovarian cancers [27]. Safety findings were consistent with the established safety profile for T-DXd [24,27].

This analysis further explored the efficacy and safety of T-DXd in the endometrial, cervical, and ovarian cancer cohorts of Part 1 of DESTINY-PanTumor02. Objective response rate (ORR) subgroup analyses according to HER2 expression, primary tumor histology, number and types of prior treatment, disease-relevant biomarkers and mutational analyses, and platinum sensitivity were also conducted, as relevant for each of the tumor types.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

1. Study design

DESTINY-PanTumor02 (NCT04482309) was a 2-part, open-label, multicenter, phase 2 study evaluating T-DXd in patients with previously treated locally advanced, metastatic, or unresectable HER2-expressing solid tumors across 7 cohorts [27]. Part 1 study design details and outcome measures have previously been published [27]. Briefly, T-DXd was administered intravenously at a dose of 5.4 mg/kg once every 3 weeks until documented disease progression (per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumours [RECIST] 1.1), withdrawal of consent, or any discontinuation criteria were met [27].

2. Patients

Eligible patients were aged ≥18 years; had HER2-expressing (IHC 3+ or IHC 2+) histologically confirmed locally advanced, unresectable, or metastatic biliary tract, bladder, cervical, endometrial, ovarian, pancreatic, or other solid cancers that had progressed after ≥1 prior systemic treatment or were not eligible for satisfactory alternative therapy; and an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0 or 1 [27]. HER2 expression was determined by local or central assessment and scored using current American Society of Clinical Oncology/College of American Pathologists guidelines for scoring HER2 in gastric cancer [28]. Full eligibility criteria are detailed in the Data S1. HER2 expression for study eligibility was based on local assessment, where available, and otherwise determined by central testing. For each cohort, excluding “other,” up to 10 patients with HER2 IHC 1+ expression could have been included if ≥3 objective responses had been observed in the first 15 patients who had confirmed HER2 IHC 3+/2+ expression by central testing [27].

3. Primary and secondary endpoints

The primary endpoint was investigator-assessed (INV) confirmed ORR [28]. Secondary efficacy endpoints included INV duration of response (DOR), disease control rate, PFS, and OS [27]. An independent central review (ICR) per RECIST 1.1 was performed and reported alongside the INV results for the primary and relevant secondary endpoints [27]. Efficacy by tumor cohorts was prespecified in the protocol. Secondary safety endpoints included the incidence of adverse events (AEs), drug-related AEs, serious AEs, and AEs of special interest (interstitial lung disease [ILD]/pneumonitis and left ventricular dysfunction) [27].

4. Exploratory endpoints

Prespecified exploratory endpoints included ORR subgroup analyses by HER2 expression, primary tumor histology, number of and types of prior treatment, disease-specific biomarker status, and platinum sensitivity. Subgroup analyses by HER2 status were based on central test results and included HER2 IHC expression by HER2 HercepTest (Dako Agilent, Santa Clara, CA, USA) in a LabCorp central laboratory, tissue (dual in situ hybridization; Ventana) HER2 amplification in a CellCarta central laboratory, and plasma (circulating tumor DNA [ctDNA]) HER2 amplification using the GuardantOMNI assay (Guardant Health, Palo Alto, CA, USA); relevant gene mutations were considered based on prior evidence on actionability [29]. Biomarkers analyzed included programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) immune cell and tumor cell prevalence, plasma mismatch repair (MMR) gene mutations, BRCA1/2 mutations, plasma homologous recombinant repair (HRR) gene mutations, and cancer antigen 125 prevalence, as relevant for each tumor cohort. PD-L1 status was evaluated using the VENTANA PD-L1 (SP263) assay by a central laboratory (CellCarta, Montreal, Canada) [30]; PD-L1 positivity was defined as ≥1% of tumor area occupied by PD-L1–positive immune cells or membranous staining in ≥1% of viable tumor cells. Baseline MMR gene mutations were evaluated in ctDNA using the GuardantOMNI assay (Guardant Health) as a proxy for microsatellite instability, and prespecified MMR genes (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, and PMS2) were assessed for the presence of mutations. Baseline BRCA1/2 and HRR gene mutations were evaluated in ctDNA using the GuardantOMNI assay (Guardant Health), and as a proxy for HRR status; prespecified HRR genes (BRCA1, BRCA2, ATM, BARD1, BRIP1, CDK12, CHEK1, CHEK2, FANCL, PALB2, RAD51B, RAD51C, RAD51D, and RAD54L) [31] were assessed for the presence of putative inactivating mutations.

Tumor tissue samples with undetectable ctDNA (classified as “low shedders”; no tumoral mutations detected, or very low-frequency mutations detected, or only variants of uncertain significance detected) were excluded from the ctDNA analyses. Very low-frequency mutations were those without a putative somatic alteration (mutation or amplification) at a variant allele fraction greater than the assay limit of detection (0.3%).

5. Statistical methods

Statistical methods have been reported previously [27].

6. Study oversight

The study was approved by Institutional Review Boards or Ethics Committees at each site (Data S2) and was conducted in accordance with Good Clinical Practice guidelines defined by the International Conference on Harmonisation, the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, and local regulations regarding the conduct of clinical research. All patients provided written informed consent [27]. Investigators who participated at the relevant institutions are listed in Data S3.

RESULTS

1. Patient disposition

Between October 15, 2020, and July 7, 2022, 41 patients with endometrial cancer, 40 patients with cervical cancer, and 40 patients with ovarian cancer were enrolled across 13 countries and 36 centers (Fig. 1); all patients received treatment, except for one patient in the endometrial cancer cohort who withdrew from the study before receiving T-DXd. At data cutoff (June 8, 2023), 10 of 40 (25.0%), 7 of 40 (17.5%), and 5 of 40 (12.5%) patients with endometrial, cervical, and ovarian cancers, respectively, were continuing to receive treatment with T-DXd. The most common reason for treatment discontinuation was objective disease progression (16 of 40 patients [40.0%] with endometrial, 23 of 40 patients [57.5%] with cervical, and 28 of 40 patients [70.0%] with ovarian tumors).

In the ovarian cancer cohort, 7 of 40 patients (17.5%) had platinum-sensitive disease (relapse ≥6 months after the last platinum-based therapy), and 33 of 40 patients (82.5%) had platinum-resistant disease (disease recurrence <6 months after the last platinum-based therapy, or disease progression without sustaining clinical benefit [refractory]). Of the 33 patients with platinum-resistant disease, 12 had platinum-refractory disease (including 5 patients with primary platinum-refractory disease).

Per the HER2 IHC test results used for study enrollment, 74 (61.7%) patients with IHC 2+ tumors and 41 (34.2%) patients with IHC 3+ tumors were enrolled across the 3 gynecologic cohorts. Enrollment to the cervical cancer cohort was opened to patients with a HER2 IHC status of 1+ after the protocol-specified interim analysis, and 5 patients with IHC 1+ tumors were enrolled based on the HER2 IHC test used for study enrollment.

Agreement rates between the local enrollment HER2 test result and the central confirmatory HER2 test result (n/n) were 64.3% (9/14) and 41.1% (7/17) for IHC 3+ and IHC 2+ endometrial tumors, 75.0% (6/8) and 53.8% (7/13) for IHC 3+ and IHC 2+ cervical tumors, with 0% agreement for IHC 1+ cervical tumors, and 53.8% and 50% for IHC 3+ and IHC 2+ ovarian tumors, respectively (Fig. S1).

3. Efficacy

As previously reported, the primary endpoint of confirmed ORR (n/N [95% confidence interval, CI]) by INV in the overall population was 57.5% (23/40 [40.9–73.0]) in the endometrial cancer cohort, 50.0% (20/40 [33.8–66.2]) in the cervical cancer cohort, and 45.0% (18/40 [29.3–61.5]) in the ovarian cancer cohort [27]; confirmed ORRs by ICR were 57.5% (23/40 [40.9–73.0]), 37.5% (15/40 [22.7–54.2]), and 42.5% (17/40 [27.0–59.1]), respectively. In the endometrial, cervical, and ovarian cancer cohorts, median DORs (95% CI) were not evaluable (NE; 9.9–NE), 14.2 months (4.1–NE), and 11.3 months (4.1–22.1) by INV and NE (9.9–NE), 15.6 months (8.2–NE), and 11.3 months (4.2–NE) by ICR, respectively. For the HER2 IHC 3+ (central testing) subgroups, confirmed ORRs (n/N [95% CI]) of 84.6% (11/13 [54.6–98.1]) for endometrial, 75.0% (6/8 [34.9–96.8]) for cervical, and 63.6% (7/11 [30.8–89.1]) for ovarian tumors were observed by INV; by ICR, confirmed ORRs (n/N [95% CI]) were 76.9% (10/13 [46.2–95.0]), 62.5% (5/8 [24.5–91.5]), and 72.7% (8/11 [39.0–94.0]), respectively. Among those with HER2 IHC 2+ tumors, confirmed INV ORRs (n/N [95% CI]) were 47.1% (8/17 [23.0–72.2]) for the endometrial cancer cohort, 40% (8/20 [19.1–63.9]) for the cervical cancer cohort, and 36.8% (7/19 [16.3–61.6]) for the ovarian cancer cohort; by ICR, confirmed ORRs (n/N [95% CI]) were 52.9% (9/17 [27.8–77.0]), 20.0% (4/20 [5.7–43.7]), and 31.6% (6/19 [12.6–56.6]), respectively. The confirmed ORR (n/N [95% CI]) for patients with HER2 IHC 1+ cervical tumors was 50.0% (4/8 [15.7–84.3]).

Among the 12 patients with prior anti-HER2 therapy, 6 had a confirmed objective response (4 of 9 patients [44.4%; 95% CI=13.7–78.8] in the endometrial and 2 of 2 patients [100.0%; 95% CI=15.8–100] in the ovarian cancer cohorts); in the subgroups of patients who had not received prior anti-HER2 therapy, 19 of 31 patients (61.3%; 95% CI=42.2–78.2) in the endometrial, 16 of 38 patients (42.1%; 95% CI=26.3–59.2) in the ovarian, and 20 of 39 patients (51.3% 95% CI=34.8–67.6)] in the cervical cancer cohorts achieved a confirmed objective response.

Of the 19 patients who had received prior TOP1 inhibitor therapy, 12 had a confirmed objective response (5 of 8 patients [62.5%; 95% CI=24.5–91.5] in the cervical and 7 of 11 patients [63.6%; 95% CI=30.8–89.1] in the ovarian cancer cohorts); 49 of the 101 patients who had not received prior TOP1 inhibitor therapy had a confirmed objective response (23 of 40 patients [57.5%; 95% CI=40.9–73.0] in the endometrial, 15 of 32 patients [46.9%; 95% CI=29.1–65.3] in the cervical, and 11 of 29 patients [37.9%; 95% CI=20.7–57.7] in the ovarian cancer cohorts).

The confirmed ORR (n/N; 95% CI) for patients with platinum-resistant ovarian cancer was 45.5% (15/33; 28.1–63.6) and was 42.9% (3/7; 9.9–81.6) for those with platinum-sensitive disease.

4. Safety

Patients in the endometrial, cervical, and ovarian cohorts received a median (range) of 13.0 (1–43), 8.5 (1–39), and 7.5 (1–39) 21-day treatment cycles, respectively.

Of the 120 patients with gynecologic cancers who received treatment, 106 patients (88.3%) had ≥1 drug-related treatment-emergent adverse event (TEAE). The most common drug-related TEAEs of any grade were nausea (64.2%), diarrhea (32.5%), and anemia (30.8%) (Table 3). The most common TEAE leading to discontinuation of T-DXd was ILD/pneumonitis (3 in the cervical cancer cohort, 2 in the endometrial cancer cohort, and 1 in the ovarian cancer cohort). Overall, TEAEs of ILD/pneumonitis, as adjudicated by an independent committee, occurred in 13 patients (10.8%; Grade 1, n=4; Grade 2, n=8; Grade 5, n=1). The fatal adjudicated drug-related ILD event occurred in a patient with endometrial cancer who had received 2 prior regimens and 18 T-DXd treatment cycles.

DISCUSSION

In this analysis of DESTINY-PanTumor02 Part 1, T-DXd demonstrated robust antitumor activity and durable clinical benefit in patients with pretreated HER2-expressing endometrial, cervical, and ovarian cancers. Although patient numbers were small in some subgroups, confirmed objective responses were observed across subgroups defined by HER2 expression levels, primary tumor histology, prior treatment, and biomarker and mutational analyses, as well as in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer. Across HER2 expression levels, the greatest responses were seen in centrally confirmed IHC 3+ tumors. In a patient population with a typically poor prognosis and limited treatment options [8,9,32], the magnitude of clinical benefit observed with T-DXd herein is encouraging when considering outcomes associated with current standard-of-care treatments in endometrial, cervical, and ovarian cancers [33,34,35]. Taken together, these data support T-DXd as a recommended therapy for pretreated patients with HER2-expressing IHC 3+ gynecologic cancers with diverse treatment backgrounds and clinical characteristics, and as a potential therapeutic option for patients with HER2-expressing IHC 2+ tumors. Part 2 of the study, which is currently ongoing, will provide further insights into the activity of T-DXd in HER2-expressing/-amplified endometrial, cervical, and ovarian tumors.

Investigation of HER2-targeted approaches represents part of a broader paradigm shift in gynecologic cancer treatment. Multiple targeted therapies have been integrated into the treatment landscape, including poly adenosine diphosphate-ribose polymerase inhibitors for patients with breast cancer gene (BRCA) mutations and immune checkpoint inhibitors for those with mismatch repair-deficient (dMMR) tumors [36,37,38]. This emphasis toward tumor-agnostic treatment highlights the importance of evaluating the presence of actionable biomarkers, including HER2 expression, to identify patients who are likely to benefit from treatment [39,40].

Results described here build upon promising results from the STATICE trial of T-DXd in HER2-expressing (IHC 3+, 2+, and 1+) advanced or recurrent uterine carcinosarcoma [41]. Other studies evaluating HER2-expressing gynecologic tumors are ongoing, including a phase 2 basket study of disitamab vedotin (RC48), a HER2-targeted antibody-drug conjugate [42], in HER2-expressing (IHC 3+, 2+, and 1+) gynecologic malignancies (NCT04965519) [43]. Interim results from the cervical cohort reported a confirmed objective response in 7 of 22 patients (31.8%) [44]. A phase 2 basket study of tucatinib and trastuzumab in previously treated, locally advanced unresectable/metastatic solid tumors driven by HER2 alterations (NCT04579380) was expected to be completed in May 2025, and includes a cervical cohort and 2 tumor-agnostic cohorts [45,46]. Recently, a phase 2 study of zanidatamab in 16 patients with HER2-expressing pretreated advanced endometrial cancers and carcinosarcomas reported a 24-week clinical benefit rate of 37.5%, and 1 patient achieved a partial response (6.2%) [47].

In the current analysis, confirmed ORR by INV in patients with platinum-resistant ovarian cancer was similar to that observed for the platinum-sensitive disease subgroup (45.5% and 42.9%, respectively). Furthermore, the confirmed ORR for patients with HER2 IHC 3+ platinum-resistant ovarian cancer was 72.7% when HER2 expression was determined by the enrollment test and 62.5% by HER2 IHC central testing. Patients with platinum-resistant ovarian cancer typically have poor outcomes and limited treatment options [48,49]; thus, the confirmed ORRs observed in this analysis are promising and suggest that T-DXd may offer a new therapeutic option for this patient population.

In patients with endometrial cancer, the ORR was 33.3% for those who had received ≤1 prior treatment regimen and 64.5% for those who had received ≥2 prior treatment regimens. The numerically lower ORR in patients with ≤1 prior treatment may be attributable to the low number of patients with HER2 IHC 3+ tumors in this subgroup (2 of 9 patients). Tumor responses were also observed across subgroups with prior anti-HER2, prior TOP1 inhibitor, prior PARP inhibitor (patients with ovarian cancer), and prior immuno-oncology therapies, suggesting that these treatments do not preclude benefit with subsequent use of T-DXd in patients with gynecologic tumors. These findings are aligned with those of the DESTINY-CRC02 study, which reported objective responses in T-DXd-treated patients with HER2-expressing advanced colorectal cancer who had previously received anti-HER2 therapy [50]. However, as the numbers of patients who had received prior anti-HER2 or TOP1 inhibitor therapies in DESTINY-PanTumor02 were low across all gynecologic cohorts, results should be interpreted with caution.

In the ovarian cancer cohort, confirmed ORRs for patients with BRCA1/2 and HRR gene mutations (71.4% and 75.0%, respectively) were almost double those seen for patients whose tumors did not harbor these mutations (35.5% and 33.3%, respectively). Nevertheless, clinical benefit with T-DXd was observed across subgroups, and the presence or absence of biomarkers and gene mutations did not appear to predict response to T-DXd in this analysis. However, firm conclusions cannot be made owing to small sample sizes in most subgroups, with the biomarker distribution across tumor types adding additional context to these findings. Indeed, mutations in MMR genes have been observed in only 6.1% of endometrial, 4.0% of cervical, and 2.4% of ovarian tumors [51]. The biomarker analysis utilized herein has limitations, including measuring MMR mutation rates in ctDNA as a proxy for dMMR status. A recent analysis showed that 33.8% of patients with MMR mutations also had dMMR, and additional IHC screening is therefore recommended to detect dMMR [51]. Additionally, the quantity of tumoral ctDNA shedding is highly variable, with low shedding potentially leading to false-negative results, and consequently, failure to identify clinically significant mutations [52,53].

T-DXd uptake by tumor cells may occur even with low levels of HER2 expression, leading to the subsequent death of neighboring tumor cells with heterogeneous expression of HER2 through bystander effects [20,21], as evidenced by findings from several studies of T-DXd in HER2-low breast cancer [54,55,56]. The current analysis provides limited evidence regarding T-DXd-associated responses in HER2-low (centrally confirmed IHC 1+ or 0) tumors owing to the small sample sizes; such patients were enrolled with IHC 3+ or 2+ tumors based on local HER2 test results (or IHC 1+ in the cervical cohort only) per inclusion criteria, and tumors were subsequently confirmed as IHC 1+ or 0 by central testing. However, given the ORRs observed in HER2-low (central testing) gynecologic tumors herein (25.0%, 50.0%, and 20.0% for IHC 1+ subgroups, and 60.0%, 50.0%, and 60.0% for IHC 0 subgroups, across the endometrial, cervical, and ovarian cohorts, respectively), the encouraging results seen in IHC 1+ expressing uterine carcinosarcoma (ORR by ICR of 70.0%) [41], and approval of T-DXd for HER2-low and -ultralow breast cancer [24], further investigation is needed into the activity of T-DXd in HER2-low gynecologic tumors. Accordingly, Part 2 of DESTINY-PanTumor02 is further evaluating T-DXd in patients with HER2 expression, including IHC 1+.

As previously reported, safety findings across the gynecologic cohorts were consistent with the established profile for T-DXd [24,27]. ILD/pneumonitis remains an important identified risk for patients receiving T-DXd therapy [57,58]. The majority of ILD/pneumonitis cases across the gynecologic cancer cohorts were low grade and manageable, with rates consistent with those previously reported [59]. Multidisciplinary guidelines for the diagnosis and management of T-DXd-related ILD/pneumonitis have been published, and state that proactive monitoring, early detection, and active management are critical for preventing high-grade ILD/pneumonitis [58].

Limitations of the DESTINY-PanTumor02 Part 1 study include the single-arm design; owing to the range of tumor types included, it was not possible to include a single comparator. Interpretation is further limited by the small sample sizes in the tumor cohorts, especially when considering the broad range of subgroups. Additionally, archival tissue was utilized for HER2 IHC/ISH testing while baseline blood samples were used for ctDNA analyses; as each sample type captures distinct aspects of tumor biology, these differences should be considered when interpreting results. Furthermore, per protocol, the American Society of Clinical Oncology/College of American Pathology guidelines for scoring HER2 for gastric cancer were used to determine HER2 status [28,60]. Gastric cancer-specific guidelines account for incomplete membranous staining patterns and heterogeneous expression, and utilize lower scoring thresholds that may better characterize HER2 IHC status in non-breast, non-gastric tumor types [28,60]; however, these guidelines are not specific to evaluating HER2 in gynecologic cancers. Furthermore, we observed only moderate concordance between local and central HER2 testing in this study, which was lower than has been previously reported in breast and gastric cancer, where validated HER2 diagnostic tests are available [28,60,61,62]. Discordance between local versus central HER2 IHC test results is frequently attributed to tumor heterogeneity but can be caused by differences in assay methodology or expertise, experience, and interpretation between pathologists [63,64,65]. Given that real-world HER2 IHC testing is often performed locally, and in tumor types lacking validated scoring guidelines, this discordance warrants careful consideration [66,67]. Use of validated HER2 tests at local laboratories and appropriate pathologist training across solid tumor types is crucial to ensure accurate scoring. Nevertheless, a post hoc analysis of DESTINY-PanTumor02 demonstrated activity for T-DXd, irrespective of whether local or central HER2 IHC test results were used to confirm HER2 expression status [68].

Several phase 3 trials are further evaluating T-DXd in gynecologic tumors, including the phase 3 DESTINY-Endometrial01 study of T-DXd in combination with either rilvegostomig or pembrolizumab versus carboplatin/paclitaxel with pembrolizumab in HER2-expressing (IHC 3+/2+), dMMR, primary advanced, or first recurrent endometrial cancer (NCT06989112) [69]. The ongoing phase 3 DESTINY-Ovarian01 study is assessing T-DXd with bevacizumab versus bevacizumab monotherapy as first-line maintenance therapy in HER2-expressing (IHC 3+/2+/1+) advanced, high-grade epithelial ovarian cancer (NCT06819007) [70].

In summary, T-DXd showed robust antitumor activity and durable clinical benefit in patients with pretreated HER2-expressing endometrial, cervical, and ovarian solid tumors; confirmed objective responses were observed across subgroups defined by HER2 expression, primary tumor histology, prior treatments, biomarker and mutational analyses, and in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer. The safety profile was consistent with previously reported data for T-DXd. These data strengthen the clinical evidence for use of T-DXd in patients with HER2-expressing gynecologic tumors with a diverse range of treatment backgrounds and clinical characteristics and provide rationale for evaluating T-DXd in HER2-low gynecologic tumors, and in earlier treatment settings.

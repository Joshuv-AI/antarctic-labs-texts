Prime degree irreducible representations of simple algebraic groups and finite simple groups of Lie type
D. L. Flannery, A. E. Zalesski

Abstract
. We show that finite quasisimple groups of Lie type in characteristic p p with an irreducible representation of prime degree r r over a finite field of characteristic p p have orders bounded above by a function of r r , independent of p p . We also bound the number of such groups in terms of r r . Apart from being notable in their own right, these results have a significant application in a computational version of the strong approximation theorem for finitely generated Zariski-dense subgroups of S ​ L r ​ ( ℙ ) SL_{r}(\mathbb{P}) , where ℙ \mathbb{P} is a number field.

Introduction

The Aschbacher categorization of (maximal) subgroups of classical groups over
finite fields divides each set of subgroups into nine classes

\mathscr{C}_{i},1\leq i\leq 9

(quasisimple or almost simple modulo scalars) class

requires

following.

Theorem 1.1

Let

r,p
primes, and let

(4(3r)^{1/2}+2)\cdot r^{(r^{2}+8)/2}

The tensor product classes

Theorem

[

Notation
.
We write

\mathbb{C}
for the complex number field and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for the finite field of

and their representation theory may be found in, e.g.,
[

,

For the root system of a simple Lie algebra

A weight is then an expression

The notation may be simplified by writing

If

-restricted

-modules)

We often write

to mean that

Theorem 2.1

Let

See

(where the author cites Gabber without a precise

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
L
be a simple Lie algebra of rank
n
over
\mathbb{C}
,
and let
V
be an irreducible
L
-module
with highest weight
\omega=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
.
Then
\dim V\leq(c+1)^{l}
, where

c=\mathrm{max}\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
and
l=l(L)
is the number of positive
roots of
L
.

Proof.

The Weyl dimension formula for an irreducible representation of
a semisimple Lie algebra with root system
\Phi

[
14
, p. 139, Corollary]

gives
\dim V=\Pi_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}(1+m_{\alpha})
, where

m_{\alpha}=\frac{(\omega,\alpha)}{(\rho,\alpha)}
,
\rho=\allowbreak(1,\ldots,1)
, and

(\cdot,\cdot)
is the symmetric bilinear form on the weight lattice of
L
.

Let
\alpha=\sum_{i}b_{i}\alpha_{i}
where the
b_{i}
are non-negative integers, and put

t_{i}=(\omega_{i},\alpha_{i})
. Since
(\omega_{i},\alpha_{j})=0
for
i\neq j
,
we have
(\rho,\alpha)=\sum_{i}t_{i}b_{i}
and

(\omega,\alpha)=\sum_{i}a_{i}b_{i}t_{i}\leq c\sum_{i}b_{i}t_{i}
, implying that
m_{\alpha}\leq c
.
The result is now clear.
∎

3.
Prime degree representations of simple algebraic groups

Recall that for each finite quasisimple group
G

\mathbf{G}
and a field parameter

\mathbf{G}
and

For each simple Lie algebra

\mathbb{C}
and each prime

\mathbf{G}

Consequently we have nine families of simple algebraic groups, named by the
corresponding Lie algebras. There are four classical families

,

Theorem 3.7

Let
\mathbf{G}

\sigma

\mathbf{G}

We view

By Steinberg’s tensor product theorem

Since

Lemma 4.1
.

of

Theorem 4.2
.

Let

G={}^{e}G(p^{t})
,
t\geq 1
, be a quasisimple group of Lie type.
Let
\phi:G\rightarrow\allowbreak GL_{r}(\mathbb{F})
be an irreducible representation, where
r
is
prime and
\mathbb{F}
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
. Suppose that

(1)

\phi(G)<SL_{r}(p^{k}),

(2)

\phi(G)
is not conjugate to a subgroup
SL_{r}(p^{i})
of
GL_{r}(p^{k})

that arises from the subfield embedding
\mathbb{F}_{p^{i}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
, for all

i
properly dividing
k
.

Then
k=t
or
k=et
. Hence for fixed
e
, the parameter
t
is uniquely determined
by
k
.

Proof.

For any finite
irreducible group
H<GL_{m}(\mathbb{F})

there exists a least positive integer
l
such that
H
is conjugate in
GL_{m}(\mathbb{F})
to
a subgroup of
GL_{m}(p^{l})

[
8
, Theorem 3.4B]
. In our setting,
m=r
and
l=k
.

Let
V
be the underlying space of
GL_{r}(p^{k})
. Since
r
is prime,
V
is an absolutely
irreducible tensor-indecomposable
\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}G
-module.
By
[
18
, Proposition 5.4.6]
, if
G
is non-twisted then
t
divides
k
and
(2) implies that
k=t
.

Suppose that
G
is twisted. By Lemma
4.1
, we can ignore

G\in\{{}^{2}B_{2}(2^{2m+1}),{}^{2}F_{4}(2^{2m+1}),{}^{2}G_{2}(3^{2m+1})\}
. The other twisted
G

are
{}^{2}A_{n}(p^{t})
,
{}^{2}D_{n}(p^{t}),{}^{3}D_{4}(p^{t})
, and
{}^{2}E_{6}(p^{t})
.
By
[
18
, Proposition 5.4.6(b) and Remark 5.4.7(a)]
, exactly one of the
following holds: either

or
p^{2t}=p^{k}
, except when

,
in which case

(see also
[
10
, Lemma 8.5]
).
That is,

or
et
.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
r,p
be primes and
k
be a positive integer. There are at most

(6\cdot(3r)^{1/2}+7)\cdot r^{4}

non-conjugate quasisimple groups
G
of Lie type in defining characteristic
p
such that

(1)

G
is an irreducible subgroup of
SL_{r}(p^{k}),

(2)

up to conjugacy,
G
is not contained in a subfield group

SL_{r}(p^{i})

for
i
properly dividing
k
.

Proof.

In Theorem
4.2
, the group
\mathbf{G}
, representation
\phi
, and
e
are
all fixed. It remains to control what happens when
\mathbf{G}
,
\phi
, and
e
vary.

By Lemma
3.1
, the rank
n
of
\mathbf{G}
does not exceed
r
;
so the number of
\mathbf{G}
of classical type does not exceed
4r
. This bound has
been improved in Remark
3.6
, as we can assume that
\dim\phi
is not
the dimension of the minimal non-trivial
\mathbf{G}
-module.
By our observations there, and Lemma
3.1
(2), it follows that

r\geq(n^{2}+n)/3
for all
\mathbf{G}
. Hence
n<(3r)^{1/2}
, and the number of classical
types of
\mathbf{G}
does not exceed
4\cdot(3r)^{1/2}
. We add
{}^{2}A_{n}(p^{t})
,

{}^{2}D_{n}(p^{t})
, the five untwisted exceptional types,
{}^{2}E_{6}(p^{t})
, and

{}^{3}D_{4}(p^{t})
, obtaining at most
6\cdot(3r)^{1/2}+7
possible
\mathbf{G}

(the other twisted groups are irrelevant due to Lemma

).

We can assume that the highest weight
\omega
of
\phi
is
p
-restricted.
Indeed, if
\psi:\mathbf{G}\rightarrow GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})
is another irreducible
representation with highest weight

,
then
\phi(G)
and
\psi(G)
are conjugate subgroups of

.
Then by Theorem
3.3
, the number of inequivalent
irreducible representations
\tau
of a simple algebraic group
\mathbf{G}
of

in characteristic

does not exceed

. Note that the number of
non-conjugate groups
\tau(\mathbf{G})
does not exceed the number of

\tau
.

By Theorem
4.2
, for each simple algebraic group
\mathbf{G}
in
characteristic
p
and fixed

such
that
{}^{e}G(p^{t})
has an irreducible representation of degree

\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}

and

. Our total count is thus

(6\cdot(3r)^{1/2}+7).r^{4}
.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
r,p
be primes. For each integer

, there are at most

(6\cdot(3r)^{1/2}+7)\cdot r^{\frac{r^{2}}{2}+4}

of

G<SL_{r}(p^{k})
and

is not conjugate
to a subgroup of

By Theorem
3.7
,

. The number of such primes
is about
(2r^{\frac{r^{2}}{2}-2})\big/\log r
, but we choose the very crude

. By Theorem

, for each

there are at most

(6\cdot(3r)^{1/2}+7)\cdot r^{4}

Recall that a Brauer character of a finite group is called

Lemma 5.1
.

If the Brauer character of an irreducible representation

\phi
of a finite group

\phi(G)
is contained
in a symplectic or orthogonal group.

Proof.

The proof of
[

the underlying space of
\phi

[

deals with

, but the reasoning remains valid for arbitrary fields; in
the notation of
[
9
, Theorem 11.1]
,

c=\pm 1
and
M^{\prime}=\pm M
, so that

is a Gram matrix of a symmetric or
skew-symmetric form.)
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
G
be a quasisimple group of Lie type in defining

\phi:G\rightarrow GL_{m}(p^{t})

of

In Lemmas

With regard to the computational application, we would like to have

for

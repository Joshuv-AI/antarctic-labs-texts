Unconditionally stable space–time isogeometric discretization for the wave equation in Hamiltonian formulation
Matteo Ferrari, Sara Fraschini, Gabriele Loli, Ilaria Perugia

Abstract
We consider a family of conforming space–time discretizations for the wave equation based on a first-order-in-time formulation employing maximal regularity splines. In contrast with second-order-in-time formulations, which require a CFL condition to guarantee stability, the methods we consider here are unconditionally stable without the need for stabilization terms. Along the lines of the work by M. Ferrari and S. Fraschini (2024), we address the stability analysis by studying the properties of the condition number of a family of matrices associated with the time discretization. Numerical tests validate the performance of the method.



1
Introduction

We consider the following acoustic wave problem with Dirichlet boundary conditions:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}U(\boldsymbol{x},t)-\text{div}_{\boldsymbol{x}}(c^{2}(\boldsymbol{x})\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U(\boldsymbol{x},t))=F(\boldsymbol{x},t)&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in Q_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T),\\
U(\boldsymbol{x},t)=g(\boldsymbol{x},t)&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\Sigma_{T}:=\Gamma\times[0,T],\\
U(\boldsymbol{x},0)=U_{0}(\boldsymbol{x}),\quad\partial_{t}U(\boldsymbol{x},t)_{|_{t=0}}=V_{0}(\boldsymbol{x})&\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega,\end{cases}

(1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=1,2,3
) is a bounded Lipschitz domain with boundary
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
,
T>0
is a finite time,
F\in L^{2}(Q_{T})
is a given source term,
c\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
is a positive wave velocity independent of
t
, and the given boundary and initial data satisfy

g\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Sigma_{T}),\quad U_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega),\quad V_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega),\quad\text{~and~}\quad{U_{0}}=g{}_{|_{t=0}}\quad\text{on~$\Gamma$}.

Here and throughout the paper we follow standard notation for differential operators, function spaces and norms that can be found, for example, in
[
8
]
.

Various discretization techniques are available to compute an approximate numerical solution of problem (
1
). In contrast to the more standard approaches based on separate discretizations of space and time, space–time methods, introduced in the seminal papers
[
20
,
27
,
28
]
, provide a simultaneous discretization of space and time variables. Thanks to their features (e.g. high-order approximation in space and time, space–time unstructured meshes, and parallelization) and to advances in computer technology, the investigation of space–time methods has increased recently, leading to the development of several space–time discretizations for wave propagation problems. Among them, we mention space–time discontinuous Galerkin methods (see e.g.
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
14
,
32
,
33
,
34
]
), and conforming space–time discretizations (see e.g.
[
2
,
26
,
6
,
23
,
36
,
30
]
). Here, we focus on the latter class of methods.

A second-order-in-time space–time variational formulation is considered in
[
36
,
37
]
. It is obtained by integrating by parts (
1
) both in time and space. Unique solvability of that formulation is proven (see e.g.
[
37
, Theorem 5.1]
for
c\equiv 1
and
V_{0}\equiv 0
), but an inf-sup stability in standard Sobolev norms in not satisfied (see
[
38
, Theorem 4.2.24]
). Therefore, stability of conforming space–time finite element discretizations may be achieved under suitable CFL conditions; see
[
17
]
for explicit and sharp results on this. In order to recover second-order-in-time unconditionally stable methods, various possibilities have been explored. One consists of testing with optimal test functions written in terms of suitable operators (e.g. the modified Hilbert transform
[
30
]
). Another possibility is to stabilize the corresponding bilinear form by adding appropriate (non-consistent) penalty terms. The different choices of this stabilization depend only on the discretization of the temporal part. In
[
36
]
, a stabilization for continuous piecewise linear functions in time has been proposed and analyzed. That idea has been then generalized to higher order continuous piecewise polynomials in
[
39
]
, and to higher order maximal regularity splines in
[
19
,
17
]
.

In this paper, we study a numerical method based on a first-order-in-time formulation of (
1
) obtained by introducing the auxiliary unknown
V:=\partial_{t}U
. Both
U
and
V
are discretized in the same discrete space, while test functions are taken in a space with lower polynomial degree and regularity. In
[
2
,
21
]
, such a space–time method was proposed, with discretization in time performed with continuous piecewise polynomials as trial functions, and discontinuous piecewise polynomials of one degree less as test functions. In
[
2
]
and
[
21
]
, via matricial and variational arguments, respectively, unconditional stability, as well as error estimates, have been proved and numerically verified for
c\equiv 1
. Note that testing with discontinuous piecewise polynomial functions in time guarantees a time-stepping procedure. The temporal part of these schemes is actually equivalent to a Runge-Kutta Gauss-Legendre method, see
[
22
, Section 2]
. In this work, we analyze a discretization of the first-order-in-time formulation with high order maximal regularity splines in time. We discretize both
U
and
V
in the same spline spaces. Test functions are taken in the spline space of one less polynomial degree and regularity. Although a complete well-posedness and error analysis is still out of reach at the moment, via a matricial argument along the lines of
[
17
]
, the resulting method is shown to be unconditionally stable without the need of additional stabilization terms. The analysis is focused on a system of ordinary differential equations, which is strongly related to the time part of the wave equation, and stability is derived by exploiting the algebraic structure of the matrices involved. This analysis combines two main tools: properties of the symbols
[
25
]
of the matrices associated with spline discretizations, and the behaviour of the condition number of general Toeplitz band matrices characterized in
[
1
]
. With the same techniques, we also show that a CFL condition is required when both test and trial spaces in time are splines of the same degree and maximal regularity. This CFL condition turns out to be sharp.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the discrete first-order-in-time variational formulation of (
1
), its discretization with maximal regularity splines in time, and we discuss the properties of the associated Galerkin matrices for the temporal part. Furthermore, we recall results on the conditioning of families of Toeplitz band matrices. In Section
3
, we present our main results on the stability of proposed method. In Section
4
, we consider the numerical scheme with equal trial and test spaces in time, and we explain mathematically why it is only conditionally stable. Finally, in Section
5
, we present an efficient algorithm for the solving the linear system involved, and various numerical tests on the full space–time problem with isogeometric discretization also in the space variables, which demonstrate the performance of the method and its unconditional stability.

2
First-order-in-time variational formulation and temporal discretization with maximal regularity splines

With the auxiliary unknown
V:=\partial_{t}U
, problem (
1
) is reformulated as follows:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}U(\boldsymbol{x},t)-V(\boldsymbol{x},t)=0&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in Q_{T},\\
\partial_{t}V(\boldsymbol{x},t)-\text{div}_{\boldsymbol{x}}(c^{2}(\boldsymbol{x})\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U(\boldsymbol{x},t))=F(\boldsymbol{x},t)&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in Q_{T},\\
U(\boldsymbol{x},t)=g(\boldsymbol{x},t)&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\Sigma_{T},\\
U(\boldsymbol{x},0)=U_{0}(\boldsymbol{x}),\quad V(\boldsymbol{x},0)=V_{0}(\boldsymbol{x})&\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega.\end{cases}

(2)

From now, we restrict, for simplicity, to the case of
U_{0}\equiv 0
,
V_{0}\equiv 0
, and
g\equiv 0
. The general case can be readily considered with a suitable lifting.

For the spatial discretization, let us consider a discrete space
V_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}(\Omega)\subset H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
depending on a spatial parameter
h_{\boldsymbol{x}}
(e.g. piecewise linear, continuous functions over a triangulation of
\Omega
of mesh size
h_{\boldsymbol{x}}
). For the temporal discretization, we introduce the space
S_{h_{t}}^{(p,k)}(0,T)
of splines of polynomial degree
p\geq 0
and regularity
C^{k}
, with
k\geq-1
(
C^{-1}
allowing for discontinuous functions) over a uniform mesh of
[0,T]
made of
N_{t}
intervals with mesh size
h_{t}:=\nicefrac{{T}}{{N_{t}}}
. We denote the subspaces of
S_{h_{t}}^{(p,k)}(0,T)
incorporating zero initial and final conditions, respectively, by
S_{h_{t},0,\bullet}^{(p,k)}(0,T)
and
S_{h_{t},\bullet,0}^{(p,k)}(0,T)
. Then, we define the tensor product spaces
Q_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{(p,k)}(Q_{T}):=V_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}(\Omega)\otimes S_{h_{t}}^{(p,k)}(0,T)
,
Q_{\boldsymbol{h},0,\bullet}^{(p,k)}(Q_{T}):=V_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}(\Omega)\otimes S_{h_{t},0,\bullet}^{(p,k)}(0,T)
, and
Q_{\boldsymbol{h},\bullet,0}^{(p,k)}(Q_{T}):=V_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}(\Omega)\otimes S_{h_{t},\bullet,0}^{(p,k)}(0,T)
.

We discretize (
2
) as follows: find
(U^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h}},V^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h}})\in Q_{\boldsymbol{h},0,\bullet}^{(p,p-1)}(Q_{T})\times Q_{\boldsymbol{h},0,\bullet}^{(p,p-1)}(Q_{T})
such that

\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}U^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h}},\chi^{p-1}_{\boldsymbol{h}})_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}-(V^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h}},\chi^{p-1}_{\boldsymbol{h}})_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}=0&\text{for all~}\chi^{p-1}_{\boldsymbol{h}}\in Q_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{(p-1,p-2)}(Q_{T}),\\
(\partial_{t}V^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h}},\lambda^{p-1}_{\boldsymbol{h}})_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}+(c^{2}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h}},\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\lambda^{p-1}_{\boldsymbol{h}})_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}=(F,\lambda^{p-1}_{\boldsymbol{h}})_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}&\text{for all~}\lambda^{p-1}_{\boldsymbol{h}}\in Q_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{(p-1,p-2)}(Q_{T}).\end{cases}

(3)

Remark 2.1
.

The scheme proposed in
[
2
,
21
]
reads as (
3
) but with trial spaces
Q_{\boldsymbol{h},0,\bullet}^{(p,0)}(Q_{T})
and test spaces
Q_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{(p-1,-1)}(Q_{T})
.

Alternatively, noticing that for all
p\geq 1
and
k=0,\ldots,p-1
it holds
\partial_{t}:S_{h_{t},\bullet,0}^{(p,k)}(0,T)\to S_{h_{t}}^{(p-1,k-1)}(0,T)
is an isomorphism (when
k=0
, the derivative is intended piecewise), we can rewrite the formulation with maximal regularity splines, after integration by parts, as: find
(U^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h}},V^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h}})\in Q_{\boldsymbol{h},0,\bullet}^{(p,p-1)}(Q_{T})\times Q_{\boldsymbol{h},0,\bullet}^{(p,p-1)}(Q_{T})
such that

\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}U^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h}},\partial_{t}\chi^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h}})_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}+(\partial_{t}V^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h}},\chi^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h}})_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}=0&\text{for all~}\chi^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h}}\in Q_{\boldsymbol{h},\bullet,0}^{(p,p-1)}(Q_{T}),\\
(\partial_{t}V^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h}},\partial_{t}\lambda^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h}})_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}-(c^{2}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\partial_{t}U^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h}},\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\lambda^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h}})_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}=(F,\partial_{t}\lambda^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h}})_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}&\text{for all~}\lambda^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h}}\in Q_{\boldsymbol{h},\bullet,0}^{(p,p-1)}(Q_{T}).\end{cases}

(4)

In the following, we will focus on the discrete variational formulation (
4
). By studying an ODE system in the time variable, which is derived from an eigenfunction expansion in space, we perform a matricial analysis along the lines of
[
17
]
in order to address the stability of scheme (
4
).

2.1
Associated ODE and matricial formulation

Let us consider the following eigenvalue problem:

\begin{cases}-\text{div}_{\boldsymbol{x}}(c^{2}(\boldsymbol{x})\nabla\Psi(\boldsymbol{x}))=\mu\Psi(\boldsymbol{x})&\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega,\\
\Psi(\boldsymbol{x})=0&\boldsymbol{x}\in\Gamma.\end{cases}

Due to the uniform ellipticity and self-adjointness of the problem, the eigenvalues form an unbounded sequence of positive real numbers (see e.g.
[
8
, Section 9.8]
),

0<\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\mu_{j}\leq\ldots\to+\infty.

Therefore, exploiting the Fourier expansion of the exact solution, based on analogous considerations as in
[
17
,
38
]
, our focus lies in proposing a stable discretization with respect to the parameter
\mu>0
for the ODE system

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t)-v(t)=0&t\in(0,T),\\
\partial_{t}v(t)+\mu u(t)=f(t)&t\in(0,T),\\
u(0)=0,\quad v(0)=0,&\end{cases}

(5)

with
f\in L^{2}(0,T)
.

Remark 2.2
.

Neumann boundary conditions in (
1
) can be considered similarly, and our analysis readily extends to this situation. However, problem (
1
) with Robin boundary conditions cannot be recast in our theoretical framework. Indeed, due to the combination of temporal and spatial derivatives in the boundary condition, one cannot perform an eigenfunction expansion in space, and trace the problem back to the study of an ODE system in time. However, a numerical test with Robin’s boundary conditions is reported in Section
5.2.3
below.

Let us fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
and set
h:=T/N
. Then, the discrete variational formulation for (
5
) analogous to (
4
) reads: find
(u_{h}^{p},v_{h}^{p})\in S_{h,0,\bullet}^{(p,p-1)}(0,T)\times S_{h,0,\bullet}^{(p,p-1)}(0,T)
such that

\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}u_{h}^{p},\partial_{t}\chi_{h}^{p})_{L^{2}(0,T)}+(\partial_{t}v_{h}^{p},\chi_{h}^{p})_{L^{2}(0,T)}=0&\text{for all~}\chi_{h}^{p}\in S_{h,\bullet,0}^{(p,p-1)}(0,T),\\
(\partial_{t}v_{h}^{p},\partial_{t}\lambda_{h}^{p})_{L^{2}(0,T)}-\mu(\partial_{t}u_{h}^{p},\lambda_{h}^{p})_{L^{2}(0,T)}=(f,\partial_{t}\lambda_{h}^{p})_{L^{2}(0,T)}&\text{for all~}\lambda_{h}^{p}\in S_{h,\bullet,0}^{(p,p-1)}(0,T).\end{cases}

(6)

Similarly as performed in
[
17
]
for the second-order-in-time variational formulation, we analyze the condition number of a family of matrices associated with (
6
).

Let consider the B-spline basis
\{\varphi_{j}^{p}\}_{j=0}^{N+p-1}
of degree
p
with maximal regularity
C^{p-1}
defined according to the Cox-De Boor recursion formula
[
11
]
. In particular, the basis is defined such that

S_{h,0,\bullet}^{(p,p-1)}(0,T)=\text{span}\{\varphi_{j}^{p}:j=1,\ldots,N+p-1\},\quad S_{h,\bullet,0}^{(p,p-1)}(0,T)=\text{span}\{\varphi_{j}^{p}:j=0,\ldots,N+p-2\}.

The matrices involved in the first-order discrete variational formulation (
6
) are defined as

\mathbf{B}^{p}_{h}[\ell,j]:=(\partial_{t}\varphi_{j}^{p},\partial_{t}\varphi^{p}_{\ell-1})_{L^{2}(0,T)},\quad\mathbf{C}^{p}_{h}[\ell,j]:=(\partial_{t}\varphi_{j}^{p},\varphi^{p}_{\ell-1})_{L^{2}(0,T)},\qquad\ell,j=1,\ldots,N+p-1.

(7)

These matrices have specific structures, due to the properties of B-splines basis functions. Here, we explicitly write the entries of the matrices
\mathbf{B}_{h}^{2},\mathbf{C}_{h}^{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{(N+1)\times(N+1)}
to highlight their structure:

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{h}^{2}=\frac{1}{6h}\begin{pmatrix}-6&-2\\
8&-1&-1\\
-1&6&-2&-1\\
-1&-2&6&-2&-1\\

&&-1&-2&6&-2&-1\\

\end{pmatrix},\hskip 14.22636pt\mathbf{C}_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{24}\begin{pmatrix}10&2\\
0&9&1\\
-9&0&10&1\\
-1&-10&0&10&1\\

Remark 2.5

(Uniqueness at the continuous level)
.

Consider the following homogeneous problem: find

\displaystyle>0\quad\phantom{12345678901234567890121}\text{for all~}0\neq w\in H^{\varepsilon}(0,T),\,\,\varepsilon>0.

Remark 3.7
.

For a polynomial with real coefficients, a simple zero in polar coordinates on the unit circle corresponds bijectively to a simple zero in complex coordinates. Indeed, given a complex function
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bar{z}}=0
, we have that
\widetilde{z}=e^{\mathrm{i}\widetilde{\theta}}
,
\widetilde{\theta}\in[-\pi,\pi]
, is a simple zero of
f
if and only if
\widetilde{\theta}
is a simple zero of the function
F(\theta):=f(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})
. To see this, in polar coordinates
(\rho,\theta)
, we compute

0=\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bar{z}}=\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}}{2}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\rho}+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\rho}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta}\right),

from which we deduce

\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}=\frac{e^{-\mathrm{i}\theta}}{2}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\rho}-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\rho}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta}\right)=-\frac{\mathrm{i}e^{-\mathrm{i}\theta}}{\rho}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta}=-\frac{e^{-2\mathrm{i}\theta}}{\rho}F^{\prime}.

3.3
those on the conditioning of their Schur’s complements. An essential tool is an explicit expression of the restrictions to the unitary complex circle of the polynomials associated with the families

Remark 3.18

Lemma 3.19
.

The family of Toeplitz band matrices
\{\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}_{n}^{p}(\rho)\}_{n}
is weakly well-conditioned if and only if the associated polynomial

has exactly four zeros of unit modulus.

, the polynomial

, which has degree

, has the same number
s
of zeros strictly less than one and strictly greater than one. According to the notation in (

and

2p-2
with modulus greater than one.

(46)

Similarly, for the first derivative, we have

\lim_{\theta\to 0}\partial_{\theta}G_{p}(\theta,\rho)=-2\rho\lim_{\theta\to 0}C_{p}(\theta)C_{p}^{\prime}(\theta)+2\lim_{\theta\to 0}B_{p}(\theta)B_{p}^{\prime}(\theta)=0.

\displaystyle\lim_{\theta\to 0}\partial^{2}_{\theta}G_{p}(\theta,\rho)

\displaystyle=-2\rho\lim_{\theta\to 0}(C_{p}^{\prime}(\theta))^{2}-2\rho\lim_{\theta\to 0}C_{p}(\theta)C_{p}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\theta)+2\lim_{\theta\to 0}(B_{p}^{\prime}(\theta))^{2}+2\lim_{\theta\to 0}B_{p}(\theta)B_{p}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\theta)

in

has exactly four zeros in

, for any

. To show that, we define

L_{p}(\theta,\rho):=\frac{G_{p}(\theta,\rho)}{M_{p}(\theta)B_{p}(\theta)}=-\rho\frac{C_{p}^{2}(\theta)}{M_{p}(\theta)B_{p}(\theta)}+\frac{B_{p}(\theta)}{M_{p}(\theta)},

. We aim at showing that

. Here, we show that

for all

. Note that we can factorize

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt<2(2p+1)\widehat{M}_{p}^{2}(\theta)\widehat{B}_{p}(\theta)-\widehat{C}_{p}(\theta)\widehat{M}_{p}^{\prime}(\theta)\widehat{B}_{p}(\theta)-2p\theta^{-2}\widehat{B}_{p}(\theta)\widehat{C}_{p}^{2}(\theta)=:I_{p}^{3}(\theta).

Why equal trial and test spaces fail

In this section, with the same techniques used to verify the weakly well-conditioning of the family of matrices associated with the variational scheme (

) obtained with maximal regularity splines of the

Consider the following discrete variational formulation: find

(49)

The associated linear system is

\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{C}_{h}^{p}&-\mathbf{M}_{h}^{p}\vskip 2.84544pt\\
\mu\mathbf{M}_{h}^{p}&\mathbf{C}_{h}^{p}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{p}\vskip 2.84544pt\\
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}^{p}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{0}\vskip 2.84544pt\\
\boldsymbol{f}_{h}^{p}\end{bmatrix},

(50)

where the matrix

is introduced in (

),
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{p},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}^{p}
and

\mathbf{M}^{p}_{h}[\ell,j]:=(\varphi_{j}^{p},\varphi^{p}_{\ell-1})_{L^{2}(0,T)},\quad\ell,j=1,\ldots,N+p-1.

(51)

Remark 4.1
.

The properties of the matrix
\mathbf{M}^{p}_{h}
are discussed in

, Proposition 3.2]
. It shares the same structure as the matrix
\mathbf{B}_{h}^{p}
with the following differences:

•

,

•

The invertibility of the system matrix in (

.

Assumption 4.2
.

is such that the system matrix in (

) is invertible for all

.

As in Remark
2.5
, uniqueness at the continuous level can be established by using the modified Hilbert transform
\mathcal{H}_{T}
with an argument that can not be directly applied at discrete level. Indeed, consider the homogeneous problem: find
(u,v)\in H^{1}_{0,\bullet}(0,T)\times H^{1}_{0,\bullet}(0,T)
such that

\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}u,\chi)_{L^{2}(0,T)}-(v,\chi)_{L^{2}(0,T)}=0&\text{for all~}\chi\in H^{1}_{\bullet,0}(0,T),\\

Uniqueness of the solution

is proven by taking

and

, summing the two equations, integrating by parts, and employing properties (

).

A preliminary difference with respect to system (

Proposition 4.3
.

For any
p\in\mathbb{N}
, the family of matrices
\{\mathbf{M}_{n}^{p}\}_{n}
as in (

Proof.

Let us introduce the family of matrices
\{\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{n}^{p}\}_{n}
, where
\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{n}^{p}
extends the purely Toeplitz band part of
\mathbf{M}_{n}^{p}
to an
n\times n
matrix. In view of Remark
4.1
, Lemma
3.11
, Remark
3.14
, and Theorem
3.4
, it is sufficient to show that the polynomial

associated with the family

does not have exactly two zeros of unit modulus nor do all its zero have unit modulus. The restriction of

to the boundary of the complex unit circle was obtained in

and is

q^{\mathbf{M}^{p}}(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})=e^{\mathrm{i}p\theta}M_{p}(\theta),

(52)

with
M_{p}

\lim_{\theta\to 0}M_{p}(\theta)=1
, and also
M_{p}(\theta)>0
for all
\theta\in[-\pi,0)\cap(0,\pi]
, from which

has no zeros of unit modulus and the proof is complete.
∎

The invertibility of the matrices
\mathbf{C}_{h}^{p}
is verified in Proposition

for

. Then, for these values of

, the Schur complement
\mathbf{C}^{p}_{h}+\mu\mathbf{M}_{h}^{p}(\mathbf{C}_{h}^{p})^{-1}\mathbf{M}_{h}^{p}

, is invertible.

Remark 4.4
.

For

The linear system (

from the first equation as

, insert it into the second one and solve for

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}^{p}_{h}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{C}^{p}_{h})^{-1}\mathbf{M}^{p}_{h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}^{p}.

to be true for the families

Differently from the family
\{\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}_{n}^{p}(\rho)\}_{n}
, which was shown in Proposition

Proposition 4.5
.

For any
p\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
\widetilde{\rho}_{p}>0
such that, for
\rho>\widetilde{\rho}_{p}
, the family of matrices
\{\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{n}^{p}(\rho)\}_{n}
is not weakly well-conditioned.

Proof.

For any
n
, the matrix
\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{n}^{p}(\rho)
has the same structure and symmetry of
\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}_{n}^{p}(\rho)
, therefore Lemma
3.19
applies also to the family
\{\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{n}^{p}(\rho)\}_{n}
. We study when the polynomial

has exactly four zeros on the boundary of unitary circle. From Lemma
3.20
and the identities (
32
) and (

q^{\mathbf{W}^{p}(\rho)}(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})=e^{2\mathrm{i}p\theta}\left(\rho M_{p}^{2}(\theta)-C_{p}^{2}(\theta)\right)=:e^{2\mathrm{i}p\theta}W_{p}(\theta,\rho).

(54)

Here, the functions
M_{p}
and
C_{p}
are defined in (
33
) and (
30
), respectively. Note that the function

is symmetric in the variable
\theta
with respect to

\lim_{\theta\to 0}W_{p}(\theta,\rho)=\rho\lim_{\theta\to 0}M_{p}^{2}(\theta)=\rho,\quad W_{p}(\pi,\rho)=\rho M_{p}^{2}(\pi)>0.

Therefore, the weakly well-conditioning is guaranteed if and only if

has exactly two zeros in
\theta
for

and, since

for all

Numerical experiments

In this section, we provide details for an efficient implementation of the discretization of the first-order-in-time space–time formulation of the wave equation (Section
5.1
). Then, using an isogeometric discretization also in space, we present several numerical tests that validate the presented stability results and demonstrate the performance of the complete space–time scheme (Section
5.2
).

5.1
Efficient implementation

For the implementation aspects and numerical tests, we consider the following extension of (
2
) that includes the case of Neumann and Robin boundary conditions (see Remark
2.2
):

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}U(\boldsymbol{x},t)-V(\boldsymbol{x},t)=0&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in Q_{T},\\
\partial_{t}V(\boldsymbol{x},t)-\text{div}_{\boldsymbol{x}}(c^{2}(\boldsymbol{x})\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U(\boldsymbol{x},t))=F(\boldsymbol{x},t)&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in Q_{T},\\
U(\boldsymbol{x},t)=0&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\,\,\Sigma_{D}:=\Gamma_{D}\times[0,T],\\
c^{2}(\boldsymbol{x})\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U(\boldsymbol{x},t)\cdot\mathbf{n}(\boldsymbol{x})=g_{N}(\boldsymbol{x},t)&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\,\,\Sigma_{N}:=\Gamma_{N}\times[0,T],\\
\vartheta c(\boldsymbol{x})V(\boldsymbol{x},t)+c^{2}(\boldsymbol{x})\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U(\boldsymbol{x},t)\cdot\mathbf{n}(\boldsymbol{x})=g_{R}(\boldsymbol{x},t)&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\,\,\Sigma_{R}:=\Gamma_{R}\times[0,T],\\
U(\boldsymbol{x},0)=0,\quad V(\boldsymbol{x},0)=0&\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega,\end{cases}

(61)

where
\partial\Omega
is partitioned as
\partial\Omega=\overline{\Gamma_{D}\cup\Gamma_{N}\cup\Gamma_{R}}
, with
\Gamma_{D}
,
\Gamma_{N}
, and
\Gamma_{R}
having disjoint interiors, and where
\vartheta>0
is the impedance parameter.

In order to write the matrix form of
the discrete variational formulation of (
61
) analogous to (
4
), let us denote by
\mathbf{C}^{p}_{h_{t}}
and
\mathbf{B}^{p}_{h_{t}}
the temporal matrices already introduced in (
7
). Additionally, let
{\mathbf{M}}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}
,
{\mathbf{K}}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}
, and
\mathbf{M}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}^{R}
represent the mass matrix, the stiffness matrix, and the mass matrix relative to
\Gamma_{R}
, respectively, associated with a basis
\{\psi_{m}\}_{m=1}^{N_{\boldsymbol{x}}}
of the discretization space
V_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}(\Omega)\subset H^{1}_{\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega)
of finite dimension
N_{x}
, where
H^{1}_{\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega)
is the subspace of
H^{1}(\Omega)
of functions with zero trace on
\Gamma_{D}
. Let

\{\Psi^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h},m,j}(\boldsymbol{x},t):=\psi_{m}(\boldsymbol{x})\varphi_{j}^{p}(t),\,m=1,\ldots,N_{\boldsymbol{x}}\ \text{and}\ j=0,\ldots,N_{t}+p-1\}

(62)

be a basis for
Q_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{(p,p-1)}(Q_{T})
. The components of the right-hand side vector
\mathbf{F}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\boldsymbol{x}}(N_{t}+p-1)}
are defined,
for
m=1,\ldots,N_{\boldsymbol{x}}
and
j=1,\ldots,N_{t}+p-1
, as

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}[m+N_{\boldsymbol{x}}(j-1)]:=(F,\partial_{t}\Psi^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h},m,j-1})_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma_{N}}

\displaystyle g_{N}(\boldsymbol{x},t)\Psi^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h},m,j-1}(\boldsymbol{x},t)\,\differential\boldsymbol{x}\,\differential t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma_{R}}g_{R}(\boldsymbol{x},t)\Psi^{p}_{\boldsymbol{h},m,j-1}(\boldsymbol{x},t)\,\differential\boldsymbol{x}\,\differential t.

Finally, the discrete solution
(U_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p},V_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p})\in Q_{\boldsymbol{h},0,\bullet}^{(p,p-1)}(Q_{T})\times Q_{\boldsymbol{h},0,\bullet,}^{(p,p-1)}(Q_{T})
is represented in terms of the space–time basis (
62
) as

U_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}(\boldsymbol{x},t)=\sum_{m=1}^{N_{\boldsymbol{x}}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{t}+p-1}U_{m+N_{\boldsymbol{x}}(j-1)}^{p}\Psi_{\boldsymbol{h},m,j}^{p}(\boldsymbol{x},t),\quad\quad V_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}(\boldsymbol{x},t)=\sum_{m=1}^{N_{\boldsymbol{x}}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{t}+p-1}V_{m+N_{\boldsymbol{x}}(j-1)}^{p}\Psi_{\boldsymbol{h},m,j}^{p}(\boldsymbol{x},t),

with coefficients collected in the unknown vectors
\boldsymbol{U}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p},\boldsymbol{V}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\boldsymbol{x}}(N_{t}+p-1)}
:

\boldsymbol{U}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}:=[\,U_{\ell}^{p}\,]_{\ell=1}^{N_{\boldsymbol{x}}(N_{t}+p-1)},\quad\boldsymbol{V}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}:=[\,V_{\ell}^{p}\,]_{\ell=1}^{N_{\boldsymbol{x}}(N_{t}+p-1)}.

The matrix form of the discrete variational formulation reads as follows:

\begin{cases}\hfil(\mathbf{B}^{p}_{h_{t}}\otimes\mathbf{M}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}})\,\boldsymbol{U}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}+(\mathbf{C}^{p}_{h_{t}}\otimes\mathbf{M}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}})\,\boldsymbol{V}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}&=\mathbf{0},\\
(-\mathbf{C}^{p}_{h_{t}}\otimes\mathbf{K}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}+\mathbf{B}^{p}_{h_{t}}\otimes\mathbf{M}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}^{R})\,\boldsymbol{U}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}+(\mathbf{B}^{p}_{h_{t}}\otimes\mathbf{M}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}})\,\boldsymbol{V}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}&=\mathbf{F}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p},\end{cases}

(63)

where
\otimes
denotes the Kronecker product.
Using the properties of the Kronecker product, (
63
) can be solved efficiently without the need to assemble space–time matrices. Specifically, for matrices
\mathbf{A}
,
\mathbf{B}
, and
\mathbf{X}
of appropriate dimensions, we have

(\mathbf{A}\otimes\mathbf{B})\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{X})=\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{B}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A}^{\top}),

where
\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{X})
denotes the vector obtained by stacking the entries of
\mathbf{X}
into a column vector. Moreover, if
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
are nonsingular square matrices, we have

(\mathbf{A}\otimes\mathbf{B})^{-1}=\mathbf{A}^{-1}\otimes\mathbf{B}^{-1}.

Computing
\boldsymbol{V}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}
from the first equation of (
63
) and plugging it into the second one, system (
63
) can be solved with the following procedure:

•

solve
\mathbf{A}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}\mathbf{U}_{h}^{p}=-\mathbf{F}_{h}^{p}
where

\mathbf{A}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}:=\mathbf{C}^{p}_{h_{t}}\otimes\mathbf{K}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}+\mathbf{B}^{p}_{h_{t}}({\mathbf{C}}^{p}_{h_{t}})^{-1}\mathbf{B}^{p}_{h_{t}}\otimes\mathbf{M}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}-\mathbf{B}^{p}_{h_{t}}\otimes\mathbf{M}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}^{R},

•

solve
(\mathbf{C}^{p}_{h_{t}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}})\,\boldsymbol{V}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}=-(\mathbf{B}^{p}_{h_{t}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}})\,\boldsymbol{U}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}
.

Here and in the following,
\mathbf{I}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}
and
\mathbf{I}^{p}_{h_{t}}
denote the identity matrices with the same dimensions as
\mathbf{K}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}
and
\mathbf{B}^{p}_{h_{t}}
, respectively.

We observe that both steps can be performed without explicitly computing Kronecker products. In fact, the matrix
\mathbf{A}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}
can be rewritten as:

\mathbf{A}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}=(\mathbf{B}^{p}_{h_{t}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}})\left(({\mathbf{B}}^{p}_{h_{t}})^{-1}\mathbf{C}^{p}_{h_{t}}\otimes\mathbf{K}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}+({\mathbf{C}}^{p}_{h_{t}})^{-1}\mathbf{B}^{p}_{h_{t}}\otimes\mathbf{M}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}-\mathbf{I}^{p}_{h_{t}}\otimes\mathbf{M}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}^{R}\right).

Using the standard
complex
Schur decomposition applied to
(\mathbf{B}^{p}_{h_{t}})^{-1}\mathbf{C}^{p}_{h_{t}}
, we find a unitary matrix
\mathbf{Q}_{h_{t}}^{p}
and an upper triangular matrix
\mathbf{R}_{h_{t}}^{p}
such that

(\mathbf{Q}_{h_{t}}^{p})^{H}(\mathbf{B}^{p}_{h_{t}})^{-1}\mathbf{C}^{p}_{h_{t}}\mathbf{Q}_{h_{t}}^{p}=\mathbf{R}_{h_{t}}^{p},

where the superscript
H
denotes the conjugate transpose.
At this point, we can express
\mathbf{A}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}
as

\mathbf{A}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}=\left(\mathbf{B}^{p}_{h_{t}}(\mathbf{Q}_{h_{t}}^{p})^{-H}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}\right)\left(\mathbf{R}_{h_{t}}^{p}\otimes\mathbf{K}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}+(\mathbf{R}_{h_{t}}^{p})^{-1}\otimes\mathbf{M}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}-\mathbf{I}_{h_{t}}^{p}\otimes\mathbf{M}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}^{R}\right)\left((\mathbf{Q}_{h_{t}}^{p})^{-1}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}\right),

and its inverse becomes

(\mathbf{A}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p})^{-1}=(\mathbf{Q}^{p}_{h_{t}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}})\left(\mathbf{R}^{p}_{h_{t}}\otimes\mathbf{K}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}+(\mathbf{R}^{p}_{h_{t}})^{-1}\otimes\mathbf{M}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}-\mathbf{I}^{p}_{h_{t}}\otimes\mathbf{M}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}^{R}\right)^{-1}\left((\mathbf{Q}^{p}_{h_{t}})^{H}({\mathbf{B}}^{p}_{h_{t}})^{-1}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}\right),

where the middle term has a block upper triangular structure. The procedure is summarized in Algorithm
1
. Computing
\boldsymbol{U}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}
requires solving
N_{\boldsymbol{x}}
independent linear systems associated with
{\mathbf{B}}^{p}_{h_{t}}
(Step 2), performing
N_{\boldsymbol{x}}
matrix-vector products involving
(\mathbf{Q}_{h_{t}}^{p})^{H}
and
\mathbf{Q}_{h_{t}}^{p}
(Steps 3 and 5), and solving a block upper triangular system, where each block has dimensions
N_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times N_{\boldsymbol{x}}
(Step 4). Finally, to compute
\boldsymbol{V}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}
, we need to perform
N_{\boldsymbol{x}}
matrix-vector products involving
\mathbf{B}^{p}_{h_{t}}
, and solve
N_{\boldsymbol{x}}
independent linear systems associated with
{\mathbf{C}}^{p}_{h_{t}}
(Step 6).

Algorithm 1

efficient implementation for solving the system (
63
)

1:

Compute the complex Schur decomposition of
(\mathbf{B}_{h_{t}}^{p})^{-1}\mathbf{C}_{h_{t}}^{p}
obtaining
\mathbf{Q}_{h_{t}}^{p}
and
\mathbf{R}_{h_{t}}^{p}
, and compute
(\mathbf{R}_{h_{t}}^{p})^{-1}

2:

Solve for
\boldsymbol{Y}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}
the system
\left({\mathbf{B}}^{p}_{h_{t}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}\right)\boldsymbol{Y}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}=\mathbf{F}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}

3:

Update
\boldsymbol{Y}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}\leftarrow\left((\mathbf{Q}_{h_{t}}^{p})^{H}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}\right)\boldsymbol{Y}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}

4:

Solve for
\boldsymbol{Z}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}
the system
\left(\mathbf{R}^{p}_{h_{t}}\otimes\mathbf{K}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}+(\mathbf{R}^{p}_{h_{t}})^{-1}\otimes\mathbf{M}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}-\mathbf{I}^{p}_{h_{t}}\otimes\mathbf{M}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}^{R}\right)\boldsymbol{Z}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}=\boldsymbol{Y}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}

5:

Compute
\boldsymbol{U}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}=-(\mathbf{Q}_{h_{t}}^{p}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}})\boldsymbol{Z}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}

6:

Solve
(\mathbf{C}^{p}_{h_{t}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}})\,\boldsymbol{V}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}=-(\mathbf{B}^{p}_{h_{t}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}})\,\boldsymbol{U}_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{p}

Remark 5.1
.

Algorithm
1
can be extended to handle the general case of non-homogeneous Dirichlet and initial conditions while maintaining a similar computational cost.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed an unconditionally stable conforming space–time method for the wave equation using splines of maximal regularity for the discretization in time. The method relies on a first-order-in-time formulation of the wave equation. We examined the conditioning behaviour of some families of matrices related to the temporal part of the scheme, and proved that they are weakly well-conditioned when the test space consists of splines of exactly one degree less than the trial space. It turns out that no CFL condition is required in this case. Our analysis is based on results from numerical linear algebra and properties of symbols associated with spline discretizations. We have also shown that the use of maximal regularity splines of the same degree for test and trial functions in the temporal discretization leads to schemes that are only conditionally stable. We also presented numerical tests on the full space–time formulation of the wave equation, using isogeometric discretization also in space, which validate the method and confirm the theoretical results.

Appendix A
An auxiliary inequality

In this appendix, we prove an inequality needed in the proof of Proposition
3.21
and of Lemma
B.2
below.

Lemma A.1
.

For all
p\in\mathbb{N}
and for all
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, we have

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(\theta+2j\pi)^{2p+1}}<\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\theta}{(\theta+2j\pi)^{2p+2}}.

Proof.

The statement of the lemma is equivalent to

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{(\theta-2j\pi)^{2p+1}}+\frac{1}{\theta^{2p+1}}+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{(\theta+2j\pi)^{2p+1}}<\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\theta}{(\theta-2j\pi)^{2p+2}}+\frac{1}{\theta^{2p+1}}+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\theta}{(\theta+2j\pi)^{2p+2}}.

We show that, for all
j\geq 1
, it holds

\frac{1}{(\theta-2j\pi)^{2p+1}}+\frac{1}{(\theta+2j\pi)^{2p+1}}<\frac{\theta}{(\theta-2j\pi)^{2p+2}}+\frac{\theta}{(\theta+2j\pi)^{2p+2}}.

(72)

With some manipulations, we obtain that (
72
) is equivalent to

(2j\pi-\theta)(\theta+2j\pi)^{2p+2}-(\theta+2j\pi)(2j\pi-\theta)^{2p+2}+\theta\left[(2j\pi-\theta)^{2p+2}+(\theta+2j\pi)^{2p+2}\right]>0,

or also to

2j\pi(2j\pi+\theta)^{2p+2}-2j\pi(2j\pi-\theta)^{2p+2}>0.

The latter is clearly true for all
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, and
j,p\geq 1
.
∎

Appendix B
Justification for property (
59
)

In this appendix, we discuss property (
59
) of the function
W_{p}(\theta,\rho)
defined in (
54
). We first prove two auxiliary results.

Lemma B.1
.

For all
p\in\mathbb{N}
and for all
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, we have

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(\theta+2j\pi)^{2p+1}}>\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\theta\sin\theta}{(\theta+2j\pi)^{2p+3}}.

Proof.

For
j=0
, we readily obtain

\frac{1}{\theta^{2p+1}}>\frac{\theta\sin\theta}{\theta^{2p+3}},

since
\sin\theta<\theta
for all
\theta\in(0,\pi)
. We show that for all
j\geq 1
, the pairs of terms in the sum with opposite indices satisfy

\frac{1}{(\theta-2j\pi)^{2p+1}}+\frac{1}{(\theta+2j\pi)^{2p+1}}<\frac{\theta\sin\theta}{(\theta-2j\pi)^{2p+3}}+\frac{\theta\sin\theta}{(\theta+2j\pi)^{2p+3}}.

(73)

With some manipulations, we obtain that (
73
) is equivalent to

(\theta-2j\pi)^{2}(\theta+2j\pi)^{2p+3}+(\theta+2j\pi)^{2}(\theta-2j\pi)^{2p+3}>\theta\sin\theta\left[(\theta+2j\pi)^{2p+3}+(\theta-2j\pi)^{2p+3}\right],

or also to

(\theta+2j\pi)^{p+3}\left[(\theta-2j\pi)^{2}-\theta\sin\theta\right]+(\theta-2j\pi)^{p+3}\left[(\theta+2j\pi)^{2}-\theta\sin\theta\right]>0.

The latter is clearly true for all
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, and
j,p\geq 1
, since

\frac{(\theta-2j\pi)^{2}-\theta\sin\theta}{(\theta+2j\pi)^{2}-\theta\sin\theta}<1.

∎

Lemma B.2
.

Let
C_{p}
and
M_{p}
be defined in (

) and (

), respectively. Then, for all
p\geq 1
, we have

\left(\frac{C_{p}^{\prime}(\theta)}{M_{p}(\theta)}\right)^{\prime}>0\quad\text{for all~}\theta\in(0,\pi).

Proof.

From (

\displaystyle\frac{C_{p}^{\prime}(\theta)}{M_{p}(\theta)}=(p+1)\frac{\sin\theta}{1-\cos\theta}\frac{C_{p}(\theta)}{M_{p}(\theta)}+2p+1=(p+1)\frac{\sin\theta}{1-\cos\theta}\frac{\widehat{C}_{p}(\theta)}{\widehat{M}_{p}(\theta)}+2p+1,

with
\widehat{C}_{p}
and
\widehat{M}_{p}
defined in (
47
). We deduce

\displaystyle\left(\frac{C_{p}^{\prime}(\theta)}{M_{p}(\theta)}\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\frac{p+1}{1-\cos\theta}\frac{\widehat{C}_{p}(\theta)}{\widehat{M}_{p}(\theta)}+(p+1)\frac{\sin\theta}{1-\cos\theta}\frac{(\widehat{C}_{p}^{\prime}(\theta)\widehat{M}_{p}(\theta)-\widehat{C}_{p}(\theta)\widehat{M}_{p}^{\prime}(\theta))}{\widehat{M}_{p}^{2}(\theta)}

\displaystyle=\frac{p+1}{1-\cos\theta}\frac{1}{\widehat{M}_{p}(\theta)}\left(-\widehat{C}_{p}(\theta)+\sin\theta\widehat{C}_{p}^{\prime}(\theta)-\sin\theta\widehat{C}_{p}(\theta)\frac{\widehat{M}_{p}^{\prime}(\theta)}{\widehat{M}_{p}(\theta)}\right).

\displaystyle-\widehat{C}_{p}(\theta)+\sin\theta\widehat{C}_{p}^{\prime}(\theta)+\theta\sin\theta\widehat{M}^{\prime}_{p}(\theta)>0\quad\text{for all~}\theta\in(0,\pi),

and we conclude with Lemma
B.1
.
∎

In order to establish property (

\partial_{\theta}W_{p}(\theta,\rho)/(M_{p}(\theta)M_{p}^{\prime}(\theta))

since

where the second identity follows from (
36
) and (
38
), and the limit in the last identity is obtained directly from the definition of
C_{p}
and the expression of

M_{p}^{\prime}(\theta)=\frac{p+1}{1-\cos\theta}(\sin\theta M_{p}(\theta)+C_{p+1}(\theta)).

\lim_{\theta\to\pi}\frac{\partial_{\theta}W_{p}(\theta,\rho)}{M_{p}(\theta)M_{p}^{\prime}(\theta)}=2\rho-2\lim_{\theta\to\pi}\frac{C_{p}(\theta)C_{p}^{\prime}(\theta)}{M_{p}(\theta)M_{p}^{\prime}(\theta)}=2\rho-2(2p+1)\lim_{\theta\to\pi}\frac{C_{p}(\theta)}{M_{p}^{\prime}(\theta)}.

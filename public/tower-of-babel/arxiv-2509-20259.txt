1.
Introduction

Given some positive integer
h
, let

\mathcal{D}_{h}=\{(a,b,c,d)\in\mathbb{Z}^{4}:ad-bc=h\}.

One can naturally interpret
\mathcal{D}_{h}
as the set of
2\times 2
integer matrices with determinant
h
.
There are various classical questions in analytic number theory that concern counting elements of
\mathcal{D}_{h}
in expanding regions. For instance, define for every integer
N\geqslant 1
the quantity

T_{\ell^{2}}(h,N)=|\{(a,b,c,d)\in\mathcal{D}_{h}:a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}+d^{2}\leqslant N^{2}\}|.

In this setting, a classical result of Selberg states that

T_{\ell^{2}}(1,N)=6N^{2}+O(N^{4/3}).

The error term here has not been improved, and in fact, it is conjectured that it should be of the form
O_{\varepsilon}(N^{1+\varepsilon})
for all
\varepsilon>0
, see
[
14
, Chapter 12]
. A more modern version of this is recorded by Iwaniec
[
14
, Theorem 12.4]
, which implies that for every
1\leqslant h\leqslant N^{2}
one has

T_{\ell^{2}}(h,N)=6\big(\sum_{d|h}1/d\big)(N^{2}+O(h^{1/3}N^{4/3})).

(1.1)

We note that
T_{\ell^{2}}(1,N)
equivalently counts
\gamma\in{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
\|{\gamma}\|={\rm Tr}(\gamma^{T}\gamma)^{1/2}\leqslant N
. Such results has been generalised to matrices
\gamma\in{\rm SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
with
n\geqslant 3
, see, for instance, the very nice work of Duke–Rudnick–Sarnak
[
7
]
, and subsequent results by Gorodnik–Nevo–Yehoshua
[
10
]
and Blomer–Lutsko
[
4
]
.

One can also consider counting
(a,b,c,d)\in\mathcal{D}_{h}
with
h<ad\leqslant N^{2}+h
and
0<bc\leqslant N^{2}
. This is known as the binary additive divisor problem and is intimately connected to the study of the fourth moment of the Riemann zeta function. For details on the rich history of this problem, see
[
11
,
17
,
18
]
and the references therein. A similar problem was studied by Estermann
[
8
]
who analysed the number of
(a,b,c,d)\in\mathbb{Z}^{4}
with
a,b,c,d>0
and
ad+bc=N^{2}
by employing Kloosterman’s refinement of the circle method. Both the above quantities exhibit main terms of a much larger order compared to
T_{\ell^{2}}(h,N)
.

A closer variant of
T_{\ell^{2}}(h,N)
concerns replacing the
\ell^{2}
norm in
T_{\ell^{2}}(h,N)
with the
\ell^{\infty}
norm, and so, we define

T(h,N)=|\{(a,b,c,d)\in\mathcal{D}_{h}:|a|,|b|,|c|,|d|\leqslant N\}|.

This has been recently studied by Afifurrahman
[
1
]
and Ganguly–Guria
[
9
]
, and arises naturally in the context of counting pairs of commuting matrices with entries of bounded height, see our work in
[
5
]
. Afifurrahman
[
1
]
used elementary methods combined with results on counting points on modular hyperbola, which themselves require deep estimates of Weil for Kloosterman sums, to prove that for every
1\leqslant h\leqslant 2N^{2}
and
\varepsilon>0
, one has

T(h,N)=\frac{16}{\zeta(2)}N^{2}\big(\sum_{d|h}1/d\big)+O_{\varepsilon}(N^{\varepsilon}(h+N^{5/3})).

Ganguly–Guria
[
9
]
used Fourier analysis and automorphic methods to prove that in the shorter range
1\leqslant h\leqslant N^{1/3}
, one can obtain an improved error term of the form
O_{\varepsilon}(h^{\theta}N^{3/2+\varepsilon})
in the above expression, where
\theta
is any admissible exponent for the generalised Ramanujan conjecture. Deep work of Kim–Sarnak
[
16
]
implies that
\theta\leqslant 7/64
.

We prove a stronger version of the above two results in a short and elementary fashion.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
1\leqslant h\leqslant 2N^{2}
and any
\varepsilon>0
, one has

T(h,N)=\frac{16}{\zeta(2)}N^{2}\big(\sum_{d|h}1/d\big)+O_{\varepsilon}(N^{\varepsilon}(h+N)).

Thus, Theorem
1.1
provides an asymptotic formula for
T(h,N)
whenever
h\leqslant N^{2-\delta}
for any fixed
\delta>0
. Furthermore, the error term in Theorem
1.1
exhibits square-root cancellation whenever
h\ll N
.

The case when
h\gg N^{2}
seems to be much harder. Indeed, much less is known for either
T_{\ell^{2}}(h,N)
or
T(h,N)
when, say,
h=N^{2}+O(N)
. The only known result in this setting seems to follow from a general class of results proven by Oh
[
20
]
using techniques from dynamics. In particular, an asymptotic formula for
T_{\ell^{2}}(N^{2},N)
can be deduced from the results presented in
[
20
, §4]
, although even this does not seem to come with any quantitative power saving error terms. Our second result delivers an asymptotic formula for
T(h,N)
with square-root cancellation in this range.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
h,N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
1\leqslant h\leqslant 2N^{2}
. Then for all
\varepsilon>0
, one has

T(h,N)=\bigg(\frac{8}{\zeta(2)}-4\bigg)N^{2}(\sum_{d|h}1/d\big)+O_{\varepsilon}(N^{\varepsilon}(N+|h-N^{2}|)).

Thus, for any
1\leqslant h\leqslant 2N^{2}
satisfying
|h-N^{2}|\ll N
, the error term in the above conclusion is of the form
O_{\varepsilon}(N^{1+\varepsilon})
. This is somewhat surprising since this error term is much better than its corresponding analogue in the smoothened version of this problem, the latter being of the form
O_{\varepsilon}(N^{3/2+\varepsilon})
, see, for instance, the well-known work of Heath–Brown
[
13
, Theorem 4]
. Moreover, despite the obvious symmetry between the conclusions of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, their proofs are quite different, and in fact, it turns out that proving Theorem
1.2
requires a considerably more delicate and careful analysis.

We observe that the main terms in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are quite different. This is not completely unexpected since we know that
T(h,N)
starts exhibiting very different behaviour when
h\gg N^{2}
; indeed we have
T(2N^{2}-1,N)=0
while (
1.1
) implies that
T(h,N)\gg N^{2}
for all
h=o(N^{2})
. One can potentially attribute this behaviour to the possibility that the error terms in Theorem
1.1
are of roughly the right order when
h\gg N
. Using the proof ideas surrounding Theorem
1.1
, we confirm this speculation for a large range of
h
.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1]
, let
N\in\mathbb{N}
be sufficiently large in terms of
\delta
and let
h\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
N^{1+\delta}\leqslant h\leqslant 2N^{2}
. Then

|T(h,N)-\frac{16}{\zeta(2)}N^{2}\big(\sum_{d|h}1/d\big)|\gg h.

We remark that while we have stated our results for the case when
1\leqslant h\leqslant 2N^{2}
, due to the symmetry in the equation
ad-bc=h
with
|a|,|b|,|c|,|d|\leqslant N
, Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
deliver analogous estimates for all
h\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
0<|h|\leqslant 2N^{2}
. Moreover, the case when
h=0
is significantly simpler than the cases when
h\neq 0
since the former allows
for many additional symmetries. Indeed, writing
T(0,N)
to be the number of solutions to
ad=bc
with
a,b,c,d\in[-N,N]
, one can swiftly show that

T(0,N)=\frac{16}{\zeta(2)}N^{2}\log N+O(N^{2}).

An asymptotic formula with an explicit second order term of order
N^{2}
can be deduced from work of Ayyad–Cochrane–Zheng, see
[
3
, Theorem 3]
. We note that
T(0,N)
equals the number of singular
2\times 2
matrices with entries in
[-N,N]\cap\mathbb{Z}
. A higher dimensional analogue of this was proved by Katznelson
[
15
]
via techniques from geometry of numbers.

Noting the preceding remark, one sees that Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
provide a non-trivial asymptotic formula for
T(h,N)
with a power saving error term whenever
|h|\ll N^{2-\delta}
or
|h-N^{2}|\ll N^{2-\delta}
or
|h+N^{2}|\ll N^{2-\delta}
, for any fixed
\delta>0
. This already seems to be new in the latter two cases. It would be interesting to obtain an asymptotic formula for
T(h,N)
for all
1\leqslant|h|\leqslant 2N^{2}
. In this much larger range, we only seem to have upper bounds of the right order for
T(h,N)
; these follow trivially from (
1.1
), see also
[
5
, Lemma 1.5]
.

We briefly describe some of the ideas involved in this paper. After some preliminary reductions, estimating
T(h,N)
reduces to analysing a weighted count of points in arithmetic progressions with varying lengths and moduli. In the setting of Theorem
1.1
, we are able to make these arithmetic progressions slightly more uniform in size, with the cost of this uniformisation giving rise to the error term in Theorem
1.1
. This uniformisation trick allows us to reduce the problem to estimating pairs of coprime integers in large intervals, and counting coprime residue classes satisfying extra congruence conditions, both of these being standard results from elementary number theory.

In the setting of Theorem
1.2
, we are unable to perform the aforementioned uniformisation step since it leads to error terms of the form
O_{\varepsilon}(N^{2+\varepsilon})
. Thus, we are required to deal with these unwieldy arithmetic progressions individually, each giving us an expected main term along with error terms that are expressed in terms of the saw-tooth function and various congruence conditions. While many of these error terms can be resolved using elementary manoeuvres, the most problematic of these is dealt with on average on the Fourier side by using standard estimates on Ramanujan sums along with various divisor type bounds.

, or equivalently

, to mean that

, where

. Given real number

and

and

\gcd(a,b)
. We may also write
(a,b)
in place of
\gcd(a,b)
when it is clear from the context that we are examining the greatest common divisor rather than a vector with two entries. Given a property
\mathbf{P}

Auxiliary lemmas

We utilise this section to record various useful lemmas that we use throughout our paper. The first four of these are some standard results from elementary number theory, and the last two concern truncated Fourier series expansion for the saw-tooth function and a standard estimate for Ramanujan sums, respectively.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1\leqslant q\leqslant u
and
r
be positive integers such that
q|u
and
(r,q)=1
. Then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant v<u,\\
(v,u)=1\end{subarray}}\mathds{1}_{v\equiv r\;(\Mod{q})}=\varphi(u)/\varphi(q).

Proof.

Let
F:(\mathbb{Z}/u\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\to(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
be the homomorphism
F:t+u\mathbb{Z}\mapsto t+q\mathbb{Z}
. Since
F
is surjective, the first isomorphism theorem shows that

|\{v\in(\mathbb{Z}/u\mathbb{Z})^{\times}:v\equiv r\;(\Mod{q})\}|=|F^{-1}(\{r\})|=\frac{|(\mathbb{Z}/u\mathbb{Z})^{\times}|}{|(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{\times}|}=\frac{\varphi(u)}{\varphi(q)}.\qed

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
m,y,z
be positive integers. Then

\sum_{t\in(\mathbb{Z}/y\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathds{1}_{tm\equiv z\;(\Mod{y})}\leqslant(y,m).

Proof.

Any

satisfying

t(m/(y,m))\equiv z/(y,m)\;(\Mod{y/(y,m)}).

Note that the left hand side of the above congruence is coprime to
y/(y,m)
, and so, for there to be any solutions to this congruence,

must be coprime to
y/(y,m)
. In this case, we can apply Lemma

to obtain

\sum_{t\in(\mathbb{Z}/y\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathds{1}_{tm\equiv z\;(\Mod{y})}\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant t<y,\\
(t,y)=1\end{subarray}}\mathds{1}_{t\equiv\frac{z}{(y,m)}(\frac{m}{(y,m)})^{-1}\;(\Mod{\frac{y}{(y,m)}})}=\frac{\varphi(y)}{\varphi(y/(y,m))}\leqslant(y,m).\qed

3.
Preliminary manoeuvres

We employ this section and the next to present our proof of Theorem
1.1
. Throughout this section, let
N
be some natural number and let
1\leqslant h\leqslant 2N^{2}
. Upon excluding solutions to
ax-by=h
with
abxy=0
and noting the various symmetries, we see that

T(h,N)=4\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|h,\\
d\leqslant N\end{subarray}}\big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant u,v\leqslant N/d\\
(u,v)=1\end{subarray}}\ \sum_{x,y\in[-N,N]}\mathds{1}_{ux-vy=h/d}\big)+O_{\varepsilon}(N^{1+\varepsilon}).

Excluding further the contribution of the solutions when
u=v=1
, which contribute at most
O_{\varepsilon}(N^{1+\varepsilon})
, and then noting that in all other cases we may assume without loss of generality that
u>v
(as

), we obtain

T(h,X)=8\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|h,\\
d\leqslant N\end{subarray}}\big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant v<u\leqslant N/d\\

(3.1)

Hence, given

Note that

y\equiv-nv^{-1}\;(\Mod{u})\ \ \text{and}\ \ y\in[-(Nu+n)/v,(Nu-n)/v]\cap[-N,N].

\displaystyle 0\leqslant\tilde{r}_{u,v}(n)-r_{u,v}(n)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{n-N(u-v)}{uv}+O(1)\bigg)\mathds{1}_{n\geqslant N(u-v)}.

(3.2)

In fact, if

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
and Proposition
1.3

Our first aim of this section is to prove the following result.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
h,N
be natural numbers with
1\leqslant h\leqslant 2N^{2}
, let
\varepsilon>0
. Then

\tilde{T}(h,N)=\frac{16}{\zeta(2)}N^{2}\big(\sum_{d|h}1/d\big)+O_{\varepsilon}(N^{1+\varepsilon}).

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Let
1\leqslant d\leqslant h
and
1\leqslant v<u
be integers such that
(u,v)=1
and
d|h
, and suppose that
y\equiv-(h/d)v^{-1}\;(\Mod{u})
. Writing
k=(h/d,u)
, we see that this is equivalent to the congruence
(y/k)\equiv-(h/(dk))v^{-1}\;(\Mod{u/k})
. Moreover, writing
y^{\prime}=y/k
, we see that
y^{\prime}
is coprime to
u/k
since
(v,u/k)=(h/(dk),u/k)=1
. Thus, we have that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant v<u\leqslant N/d\\
(u,v)=1\end{subarray}}\ \tilde{r}_{u,v}(h/d)=\sum_{1<u\leqslant N/d}\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in[-N/k,N/k],\\
(y^{\prime},u/k)=1\end{subarray}}\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant v<u,\\
(v,u)=1\end{subarray}}\mathds{1}_{y^{\prime}\equiv-(h/(dk))v^{-1}\;(\Mod{u/k})}.

Applying Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.4
, we find that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant v<u\leqslant N/d\\
(u,v)=1\end{subarray}}\ \tilde{r}_{u,v}(h/d)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1<u\leqslant N/d}\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in[-N/k,N/k],\\
(y^{\prime},u/k)=1\end{subarray}}\ \frac{\varphi(u)}{\varphi(u/k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leqslant u\leqslant N/d}\frac{\varphi(u)}{\varphi(u/k)}\bigg(\frac{2N}{k}\frac{\varphi(u/k)}{u/k}+O(\tau^{\prime}(u/k))\bigg)

\displaystyle=2N\sum_{1\leqslant u\leqslant N/d}\frac{\varphi(u)}{u}+O\bigg(\sum_{1\leqslant u\leqslant N/d}\frac{\varphi(u)}{\varphi(u/k)}\tau^{\prime}(u/k)\bigg).

Hence,

\displaystyle\tilde{T}(h,N)

\displaystyle=16N\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|h,\\
d\leqslant N\end{subarray}}\sum_{1\leqslant u\leqslant N/d}\frac{\varphi(u)}{u}+O\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|h,\\
d\leqslant N\end{subarray}}\sum_{1\leqslant u\leqslant N/d}\frac{\varphi(u)}{\varphi(u/k)}\tau^{\prime}(u/k)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{16N^{2}}{\zeta(2)}\big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|h,\\
d\leqslant N\end{subarray}}1/d\big)+O\bigg(\sum_{d|h}\sum_{1\leqslant u\leqslant N/d}\frac{\varphi(u)}{\varphi(u/k)}\tau^{\prime}(u/k)\bigg)+O_{\varepsilon}(N^{1+\varepsilon}).

(4.1)

Since
k=(u,h/d)
and

\frac{\varphi(u)}{\varphi(u/k)}=k\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{primes }p,\ p|u\\
p\nmid(u/k)\end{subarray}}(1-1/p)\leqslant k,

we find that the error term in (
4
) is

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{d|h}\sum_{1\leqslant u\leqslant X/d}(u,h/d)\tau^{\prime}(u/k)\ll_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon/3}\sum_{d|h}\sum_{1\leqslant u\leqslant N/d}(u,h/d)\ll_{\varepsilon}N^{2\varepsilon/3}\sum_{d|h}N/d\ll_{\varepsilon}N^{1+\varepsilon}

with the penultimate step following from Lemma
2.3
.
Moreover, the main term in (
4
) equals

\frac{16N^{2}}{\zeta(2)}\big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|h,\\
d\leqslant N\end{subarray}}1/d\big)=\frac{16N^{2}}{\zeta(2)}\big(\sum_{d|h}1/d\big)+O_{\varepsilon}(N^{1+\varepsilon}),

thus concluding the proof of Lemma
4.1
.
∎

We remark that Lemma
4.1
combines with Lemma
3.1
to dispense the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
. We now present the proof of Proposition
1.3
.

Proof of Proposition
1.3
.

Recalling (
3.1
) and the definition of
\tilde{T}(h,N)
, we see that

\tilde{T}(h,N)-T(h,N)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|h,\\
d\leqslant N\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant v<u\leqslant N/d,\\
(u,v)=1\end{subarray}}(\tilde{r}_{u,v}(h/d)-r_{u,v}(h/d))+O_{\varepsilon}(N^{1+\varepsilon})

for all
\varepsilon>0
.
Moreover, we have
\tilde{r}_{u,v}(h/d)\geqslant r_{u,v}(h/d)
for all valid choices of
u,v,d,h
. Noting Lemma
4.1
, our main aim will be to show that the right hand side above is
\gg h
. In order to obtain this lower bound, we just consider the contribution from some special choices of
d,u,v
. Noting the remark following (
3
), we see that when
d=1
and
1\leqslant v<u\leqslant N
satisfy
(u,v)=1
and
u<N/100
and
N(u+v)<h
, then
r_{u,v}(h)=0
while
\tilde{r}_{u,v}(h)\geqslant 2N/u-5
.
Thus, if
d=1
and
u<h/(100N)
, then we obtain all the above size constraints. Hence, in this case, we have
\tilde{r}_{u,v}(h)\geqslant 2N/u-5\geqslant N/u
. Therefore, we deduce that

\displaystyle\tilde{T}(h,N)-T(h,N)

\displaystyle\geqslant\sum_{1<u<h/(100N)}\sum_{1\leqslant v\leqslant u,(v,u)=1}N/u+O_{\varepsilon}(N^{1+\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\geqslant N\sum_{1<u<h/(100N)}\varphi(u)/u+O_{\varepsilon}(N^{1+\varepsilon}).

Note that for any sufficiently large
L\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{1<u<L}\varphi(u)/u

\displaystyle=\sum_{1<u<L}\sum_{d|u}\mu(d)/d=\sum_{1\leqslant d<L}\mu(d)/d(L/d+O(1))

\displaystyle=L\sum_{1\leqslant d<L}\mu(d)/d^{2}+O(\log L)=L/\zeta(2)+O(\log L)\gg L.

Since
h/100N=N^{\delta}/100
, we therefore see that whenever
N
is sufficiently large in terms of
\delta
and
\varepsilon
, we have

\tilde{T}(h,N)-T(h,N)\gg h+O_{\varepsilon}(N^{1+\varepsilon}).

Noting Lemma
4.1
and choosing
\varepsilon
to be sufficiently small in terms of
\delta
, we conclude that

|T(h,N)-\frac{16}{\zeta(2)}N^{2}\big(\sum_{d|h}1/d\big)|\gg h.\qed

5.
Estimating the main term in Theorem
1.2

We dedicate the rest of this paper to proving Theorem
1.2
.
Thus, let
N
and
h
be positive integers with
1\leqslant h\leqslant 2N^{2}
and let
\sigma=(h-N^{2})/N
.

As in the case of Theorem
1.1
, one swiftly deduces that the number of solutions to
ab-cd=h
with
a,b,c,d\in[-N,N]
and
abcd=0
is at most
O_{\varepsilon}(N^{1+\varepsilon})
. If
ab,cd>0
, then our equation requires
ab>N(N+\sigma)
. If
\sigma\geqslant 0
, then this inequality is never satisfied. If
\sigma<0
, then there are
O(\lfloor\sigma\rfloor^{2})
choices for
a
and
b
. For any such fixed
ab
, the divisor bound then implies that there are
O_{\varepsilon}(N^{\varepsilon})
choices for
c
and
d
. One can similarly analyse the case when
ab,cd<0
. Moreover, since
h>0
, we can never have
ab<0
and
cd>0
.

Excluding these solutions, we see that if
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfy
1\leqslant|a|,|b|,|c|,|d|\leqslant N
and
ab-cd=h
, then we must have
ab>0
and
cd<0
. In particular, this means that

\displaystyle T(h,N)

\displaystyle=4\sum_{1\leqslant a,c\leqslant N}\sum_{1\leqslant b,d\leqslant N}\mathds{1}_{ab+cd=h}+O_{\varepsilon}((\sigma^{2}+N)N^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=4\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant r\leqslant N,\\
r|h\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant x,y\leqslant N/r,\\
(x,y)=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{1\leqslant b,d\leqslant N}\mathds{1}_{xb+dy=h/r}+O_{\varepsilon}((\sigma^{2}+N)N^{\varepsilon}).

Note that as
1\leqslant b,d\leqslant N
, one must have
x+y\geqslant h/(Nr)
. Moreover if
x+y=h/(Nr)
, then we must have
b=d=N
, whence there are at most
O(\sum_{r|h}h/(Nr))=O_{\varepsilon}(N^{1+\varepsilon})
such solutions contributing to
T(h,N)
. We write this as

T(h,N)=4\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant r\leqslant N,\\
r|h\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant x,y\leqslant N/r,\\
(x,y)=1\end{subarray}}\mathds{1}_{x+y>h/(Nr)}\sum_{1\leqslant b,d\leqslant N}\mathds{1}_{xb+dy=h/r}+O_{\varepsilon}((\sigma^{2}+N)N^{\varepsilon}).

Now, let
1\leqslant x,y\leqslant N/r
be coprime integers and let
1\leqslant b,d\leqslant N
satisfy

bx+dy=h/r.

(5.1)

Since
x
and
y
are coprime, there is a unique
b_{0}\in[y]
such that
b_{0}x\equiv h/r\;(\Mod{y})
. Setting
d_{0}=y^{-1}(h/r-b_{0}x)\in\mathbb{Z}
, we observe that
b,d\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfy (
5.1
) if and only if there exists an integer
k
such that

b=b_{0}+ky\ \ \text{and}\ \ d=d_{0}-kx.

Moreover, the condition
1\leqslant b,d\leqslant N
is equivalent to the condition
U\leqslant k\leqslant V
where

U=U(x,y)=\max\left\{\frac{1-b_{0}}{y},\frac{d_{0}-N}{x}\right\}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \quad V=V(x,y)=\min\left\{\frac{N-b_{0}}{y},\frac{d_{0}-1}{x}\right\}.

Thus, counting
1\leqslant b,d\leqslant N
satisfying (
5.1
) is equivalent to counting
k\in[U,V]\cap\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
M\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[1,\infty)
satisfy

m=h/r\ \ \text{and}\ \ M=N/r.

Observe that

V-U=\frac{N(x+y)-m}{xy}-\left(\frac{N-b_{0}}{y}-V\right)-\left(U-\frac{d_{0}-N}{x}\right).

Since
Nx-m\leqslant(N^{2}-h)/r=-\sigma N/r
, we have

0\leqslant\frac{N-b_{0}}{y}-V=\max\left\{0,\frac{Nx+y-m}{xy}\right\}\ll\frac{1}{x}+\frac{|\sigma|N}{rxy}.

Similarly, we see that

0\leqslant U-\frac{d_{0}-N}{x}=\max\left\{0,\frac{Ny+x-m}{xy}\right\}\ll\frac{1}{y}+\frac{|\sigma|N}{rxy}.

Since
x+y>m/N
, the number
|[U,V]\cap\mathbb{Z}|
of admissible
k
is therefore equal to

\displaystyle\lfloor V\rfloor-\lfloor U\rfloor+\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(U)=

\displaystyle\;\frac{N(x+y)-m}{xy}-(\psi(V)-\psi(U))+\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(U)

(5.2)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +O\left(\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{y}+\frac{|\sigma|N}{rxy}\right),

where
\psi(t):=t-\lfloor t\rfloor-\tfrac{1}{2}
is the aforementioned saw-tooth function. Moreover, since

\sum_{1\leqslant x,y\leqslant M}\left(\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{y}\right)\ll M\log M,\ \ \text{and}\ \ \frac{|\sigma|N}{r}\sum_{1\leqslant x,y\leqslant M}\frac{1}{xy}\ll\frac{|\sigma|N(\log M)^{2}}{r},

the preceding discussion implies that

T(h,N)=4\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|h,\\
r\leqslant N\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant x,y\leqslant M,\\
x+y>M+\sigma/r,\\
(x,y)=1\end{subarray}}\bigg(\frac{N(x+y-M-\sigma/r)}{xy}-(\psi(V)-\psi(U))+\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(U)\bigg)+O_{\varepsilon}((|\sigma|N+N)N^{\varepsilon}),

where we have also used the facts that
m/N=M+(\sigma/r)
and
\sigma^{2}\leqslant 2|\sigma|N
.

The estimate
\sum_{1\leqslant x,y\leqslant M}1/xy\ll(\log M)^{2}
allows us to remove
\sigma/r
from the numerator of the first term in the inner sum at the cost of adding
O_{\varepsilon}(|\sigma|N^{1+\varepsilon})
to the error term. Furthermore, upon adding or subtracting the appropriate terms depending on the sign of
\sigma
, we see that

T(h,N)=4\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|h,\\
r\leqslant N\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant x,y\leqslant M,\\
x+y>M,\\
(x,y)=1\end{subarray}}\bigg(\frac{N(x+y-M)}{xy}-(\psi(V)-\psi(U))+\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(U)\bigg)+O(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(h)),

where

\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(h)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|h,\\
r\leqslant N\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant x,y\leqslant M,\\
M-|\sigma|/r\leqslant x+y\leqslant M+|\sigma|/r,\\
(x,y)=1\end{subarray}}\bigg|\frac{N(x+y-M)}{xy}-(\psi(V)-\psi(U))+\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(U)\bigg|+O_{\varepsilon}((|\sigma|N+N)N^{\varepsilon}).

We will swiftly estimate
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(h)
. In particular, we observe that there are at most
4\lceil|\sigma|/r\rceil
choices for the integer
x+y\in[M-|\sigma|/r,M+|\sigma|/r]
. Thus, there are at most
4\lceil|\sigma|/r\rceil M
choices for
x
and
y
in the inner sum. Since
\psi
and
\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}
both have absolute value at most
1
, we deduce that

\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(h)\ll_{\varepsilon}(|\sigma|N+N)N^{\varepsilon}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|h,\\
r\leqslant N\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{N}{r}\left\lceil\frac{|\sigma|}{r}\right\rceil+\sum_{1\leqslant x,y\leqslant M}\frac{|\sigma|N}{rxy}\right)\ll_{\varepsilon}(1+|\sigma|)N^{1+\varepsilon}.

We have therefore shown that

\displaystyle T(h,N)=4\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|h,\\
r\leqslant N\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant x,y\leqslant M,\\
x+y>M,\\
(x,y)=1\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{N(x+y-M)}{xy}-(\psi(V)-\psi(U))+\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(U)\bigg)

(5.3)

\displaystyle+O_{\varepsilon}((1+|\sigma|)N^{1+\varepsilon}).

We will now focus on estimating the main term in the above expression. Thus, our primary aim for the rest of this section is to prove the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, one has

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|h,\\
r\leqslant N\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant x,y\leqslant M\\
\gcd(x,y)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{N(x+y-M)}{xy}\cdot\mathds{1}_{x+y>M}=\left(\frac{2}{\zeta(2)}-1\right)N^{2}\sum_{r|h}\frac{1}{r}+O_{\varepsilon}(N^{1+\varepsilon}).

(5.4)

Thus, for each real number
X\geqslant 1
, define

S(X):=\sum_{1\leqslant x,y\leqslant X}\frac{x+y-X}{xy}\cdot\mathds{1}_{x+y>X}.

We proceed by proving the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

For each real
X\geqslant 1
, we have

S(X)=(2-\zeta(2))X+O(1).

Proof.

Let
H=\lfloor X\rfloor\in\mathbb{N}
. We have

\sum_{1\leqslant x,y\leqslant X}\frac{x+y}{xy}\cdot\mathds{1}_{x+y>X}=2\sum_{y=1}^{H}\frac{|\{x\in[H]:x>H-y\}|}{y}=2H.

We now consider the second term

\sum_{1\leqslant x,y\leqslant X}\frac{1}{xy}\cdot\mathds{1}_{x+y>X}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{H}\frac{1}{n}\right)^{2}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant x,y\leqslant H\\
x+y\leqslant H\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{xy}.

Using the change of variables
s=x+y
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant x,y\leqslant H\\
x+y\leqslant H\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{xy}=\sum_{s=2}^{H}\sum_{x=1}^{s-1}\frac{1}{x(s-x)}=2\sum_{s=2}^{H}\frac{1}{s}\sum_{x=1}^{s-1}\frac{1}{x}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{H}\frac{1}{n}\right)^{2}-\sum_{n=1}^{H}\frac{1}{n^{2}}.

It follows that

\sum_{1\leqslant x,y\leqslant X}\frac{1}{xy}\cdot\mathds{1}_{x+y>X}=\sum_{n=1}^{H}\frac{1}{n^{2}}.

Since
H=X+O(1)
, we therefore conclude that

S(X)=2H-X\sum_{n=1}^{H}\frac{1}{n^{2}}=(2-\zeta(2))X+O(1).\qed

We will now present our proof of Proposition
5.1
.

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

Aplying Möbius inversion and Lemma
5.2
, we find that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant x,y\leqslant M\\
\gcd(x,y)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{N(x+y-M)}{xy}\cdot

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{x+y>M}=N\sum_{k=1}^{M}\frac{\mu(k)}{k}S\left(\frac{M}{k}\right)

\displaystyle=(2-\zeta(2))MN\sum_{k=1}^{M}\frac{\mu(k)}{k^{2}}+O\left(N\sum_{k=1}^{M}\frac{|\mu(k)|}{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2}{\zeta(2)}-1\right)MN+O(N(1+\log M)).

Summing this over all
r|h
with
r\leqslant N
and recalling that
M=N/r
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|h,\\
r\leqslant N\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant x,y\leqslant M\\
\gcd(x,y)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{N(x+y-M)}{xy}\cdot\mathds{1}_{x+y>M}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2}{\zeta(2)}-1\right)N\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|h,\\
r\leqslant N\end{subarray}}N/r+O_{\varepsilon}(N^{1+\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2}{\zeta(2)}-1\right)N^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|h\end{subarray}}1/r+O_{\varepsilon}(N^{1+\varepsilon}).

This concludes the proof of Proposition
5.1
.
∎

6.
The error terms in Theorem
1.2

We now consider the error terms in (
5.3
). We begin by proving the following proposition.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Then we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant x,y\leqslant M\\
\gcd(x,y)=1\end{subarray}}\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(U)\cdot\mathds{1}_{x+y>M}\ll_{\varepsilon}Mm^{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Recall from the definition of
U
that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant x,y\leqslant M\\
\gcd(x,y)=1\end{subarray}}\mathds{1}_{x+y>M}\cdot\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(U)\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant x,y\leqslant M\\
\gcd(x,y)=1\end{subarray}}\mathds{1}_{x+y>M}\cdot(1_{b_{0}\equiv 1\;(\Mod{y})}+\mathds{1}_{d_{0}\equiv N\;(\Mod{x})}).

We will now show that the first sum on the right hand side is
O_{\varepsilon}(MN^{\varepsilon})
, an upper bound of the same order for the second sum will follow mutatis mutandis.

Recall that
b_{0}
is defined by the congruence
b_{0}\equiv mx^{-1}\;(\Mod{y}).
Thus, for any fixed
1\leqslant y\leqslant M
, we may apply Lemma
2.2
to deduce that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M-y+1\leqslant x\leqslant M,\\
(x,y)=1\end{subarray}}\mathds{1}_{mx^{-1}\equiv 1\;(\Mod{y})}=\sum_{t\in(\mathbb{Z}/y\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathds{1}_{tm\equiv 1\;(\Mod{y})}\leqslant\gcd(y,m).

Summing this over all
y\in[M]
and applying Lemma
2.3
furnishes the desired bound

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant x,y\leqslant M\\
\gcd(x,y)=1\end{subarray}}1_{x+y>M}\cdot\mathds{1}_{b_{0}\equiv t\;(\Mod{y})}\leqslant\sum_{y=1}^{M}\gcd(y,m)\ll_{\varepsilon}Mm^{\varepsilon}.\qed

In view of (
5.3
) and Propositions
5.1
and
6.1
, we see that in order to conclude the proof of Theorem
1.2
, it suffices to prove the following.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Then we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant x,y\leqslant M\\
\gcd(x,y)=1\end{subarray}}\psi(V)\cdot\mathds{1}_{x+y>M}\ll_{\varepsilon}M(N^{\varepsilon}+|\sigma/r|),\ \ \text{and}\ \ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant x,y\leqslant M\\
\gcd(x,y)=1\end{subarray}}\psi(U)\cdot\mathds{1}_{x+y>M}\ll_{\varepsilon}M(N^{\varepsilon}+|\sigma/r|).

Proof.

We will only show that the first inequality in Proposition
6.2
holds true, since upon swapping the roles of
x
and
y
and the roles of
b_{0}
and
d_{0}
, a similar argument may be used to prove the second inequality mentioned in Proposition
6.2

We begin with the observation that

\frac{N-b_{0}}{y}\leqslant\frac{d_{0}-1}{x}\iff Nx+y\leqslant m=MN+\sigma N/r.

Since
x,y\in[M]
, it follows that
V=\frac{N-b_{0}}{y}
whenever
x\leqslant M-(1-\sigma)/r
. Thus, upon appropriately adding and subtracting terms as required, we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant y\leqslant M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant x\leqslant M\\
(x,y)=1\end{subarray}}\psi(V)\cdot\mathds{1}_{x+y>M}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leqslant y\leqslant M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M-y<x\leqslant M\\
(x,y)=1\end{subarray}}\psi(V)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leqslant y\leqslant M}\sum_{t\in(\mathbb{Z}/y\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\psi\left(\frac{N-mt}{y}\right)+O(M|1-\sigma|/r+M),

(6.1)

where the last equality follows from the definition of
b_{0}
, a change of variables
t=x^{-1}
, and the fact that
\psi
is
1
-periodic and
1
-bounded.

In order to analyse the sum appearing on the right-hand side of (
6.1
), we apply Lemma
2.5
with
Q=M^{2}
to obtain

\sum_{t\in(\mathbb{Z}/y\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\psi\left(\frac{N-mt}{y}\right)=\sum_{0<|h|\leqslant Q}\frac{1}{2\pi ih}\sum_{t\in(\mathbb{Z}/y\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}e_{y}(h(N-mt))+O(\mathcal{E}_{y}(Q)),

where

\mathcal{E}_{y}(Q)=\sum_{t\in(\mathbb{Z}/y\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{Q\lVert(N-mt)/y\rVert}\right\}

and
e_{y}(\theta)=e(\theta/y)
for all
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and
y\in[M]
. Applying (
2.1
) to the inner sum and summing over all
1\leqslant y\leqslant M
, we find that

\sum_{1\leqslant y\leqslant M}\sum_{t\in(\mathbb{Z}/y\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\psi\left(\frac{N-mt}{y}\right)\ll\sum_{1\leqslant y\leqslant M}\sum_{0<h<Q}\frac{1}{h}\sum_{d|(y,hm)}d+\sum_{1\leqslant y\leqslant M}\mathcal{E}_{y}(Q).

(6.2)

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant y\leqslant M}\sum_{0<h<Q}\frac{1}{h}\sum_{d|(y,hm)}d

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}M^{\varepsilon}\sum_{0<h<Q}\frac{1}{h}\sum_{1\leqslant y\leqslant M}(y,hm)

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}M^{1+\varepsilon}(Qm)^{\varepsilon}\sum_{0<h<Q}\frac{1}{h}\ll_{\varepsilon}M^{1+\varepsilon}(Qm)^{2\varepsilon}

where the second inequality follows from an application of Lemma
2.3
. We now analyse the second sum in (
6.2
). We begin by observing that

(N-mt)/y\in\{0,1/y,\dots,(y-1)/y\}

for all
t\in(\mathbb{Z}/y\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Since
Q=M^{2}
, it follows that

\sum_{1\leqslant y\leqslant M}\mathcal{E}_{y}(Q)\ll\sum_{1\leqslant y\leqslant M}\bigg(\frac{\varphi(y)}{Q/y}+\sum_{t\in(\mathbb{Z}/y\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathds{1}_{N\equiv mt\;(\Mod{y})}\bigg)\ll\sum_{1\leqslant y\leqslant M}\bigg(\frac{y}{M}+(y,m)\bigg)\ll_{\varepsilon}Mm^{\varepsilon},

where the last two steps employ subsequent applications of Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
. Upon combining the preceding two inequalities with (
6.2
) and noting that
m,M,Q\leqslant 2N^{2}
and rescaling
\varepsilon
appropriately, we obtain the bound

\sum_{1\leqslant y\leqslant M}\sum_{t\in(\mathbb{Z}/y\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\psi\left(\frac{N-mt}{y}\right)\ll_{\varepsilon}MN^{\varepsilon}.

Substituting this into (
6.1
) dispenses the desired estimate.
∎

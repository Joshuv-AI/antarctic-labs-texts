Control of blow-up profiles for the mass-critical focusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation on bounded domains
Ludovick Gagnon, Kévin Le Balc’h

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the mass-critical focusing nonlinear Schrödinger on bounded two-dimensional domains with Dirichlet boundary conditions. In the absence of control, it is well-known that free solutions starting from initial data sufficiently large can blow-up. More precisely, given a finite number of points, there exists particular profiles blowing up exactly at these points at the blow-up time. For pertubations of these profiles, we show that, with the help of an appropriate nonlinear feedback law located in an open set containing the blow-up points, the blow-up can be prevented from happening. More specifically, we construct a small-time control acting just before the blow-up time. The solution may then be extended globally in time. This is the first result of control for blow-up profiles for nonlinear Schrödinger type equations. Assuming further a geometrical control condition on the support of the control, we are able to prove a null-controllability result for such blow-up profiles. Finally, we discuss possible extensions to three-dimensional domains.

Introduction

1.1
The mass-critical focusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation and blow-up

Let
T>0
and
\Omega
be a smooth, bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We study the
L^{2}
-critical focusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation in
\Omega
, with Dirichlet boundary conditions

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i\partial_{t}\psi+\Delta\psi=-|\psi|^{2}\psi&\text{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\psi=0&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\\
\psi(0,\cdot)=\psi_{0}&\text{ in }\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

Note that (
1.1
) is the focusing nonlinear cubic Schrödinger equation, modelling for instance nonlinear optics for the self-focusing of intense laser beams in hollow core fibers. We refer to
[
SS99
]
for further references of physical models that are described by (
1.1
).

By the Sobolev embedding
H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, it is classical to prove that (
1.1
) is locally well-posed in
H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

Theorem 1.1

(Lemma 2.1 of
[
BGT03
]
)
.

For any
R>0
, there exists a time
T>0
such that for every
\psi_{0}\in B_{R}:=\{\psi_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\ ;\ \|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq R\},

the problem (
1.1
) has a unique solution in
C([0,T];H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
. In addition, the flow map
\psi_{0}\to\psi
is uniformly continuous from
B_{R}
to
C([0,T];H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
.

Moreover, for such solutions, the following conservation laws hold for every
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\|\psi(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(1.2)

\displaystyle E(\psi(t,\cdot)):=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\psi(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\|\psi(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{4}=E(\psi_{0}).

(1.3)

For every
\psi_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, the maximal time of existence
T_{\max}(\psi_{0})\in(0,+\infty]
of the maximal solution
\psi\in C([0,T_{\max}(\psi_{0}));H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
of (
1.1
) can be defined. Furthermore, we have the following blow-up criterion
[
Caz03
]
,

T_{\max}(\psi_{0})<+\infty\Rightarrow\|\psi(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\to+\infty\ \text{as}\ t\to T_{\max}(\psi_{0})^{-}.

(1.4)

Note that the local well-posedness of (
1.1
) can be generalized to
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem 3.6.1 of
[
Caz03
]
)
.

For any
R>0
, there exists a time
T>0
such that for every
\psi_{0}\in B_{R}:=\{\psi_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\ ;\ \|\psi_{0}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}\leq R\},

the problem (
1.1
) has a unique solution in
C([0,T];H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
.

However the uniform continuity of the flow map is not clear in this case. The conservation laws (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) still hold. Moreover the maximal time of existence
T_{\max}^{H^{1}_{0}}(\psi_{0})\in(0,+\infty)
can still be defined and then by using (
1.2
) the blow-up criterion takes the following form

T_{\max}^{H^{1}_{0}}(\psi_{0})<+\infty\Rightarrow\|\nabla\psi(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}\Omega)}\to+\infty\ \text{as}\ t\to T_{\max}^{H^{1}_{0}}(\psi_{0})^{-}.

(1.5)

Blow-up can indeed occur. Following
[
Kav87
]
, assume for instance that
\Omega
is a star-shaped domain with respect to
0\in\Omega
and let
\psi_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that
E(\psi_{0})<0
. Set
V(t)=\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}|\psi(t,x)|^{2}dx
then we have the following virial identity

\frac{1}{16}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}V(t)=E(\psi(t,\cdot))-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla\psi\cdot n|^{2}x\cdot ndx\leq E(\psi_{0})<0

(1.6)

where
n
is the outer normal to
\partial\Omega
. Since
V(t)
is a positive concave function, the solution blows up in finite time.

Blow-up solutions can be constructed from the ground state solitary wave. In
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the ground state solitary wave
Q
is the unique positive radially symmetric solution of

-\Delta Q+Q=|Q|^{p-1}Q\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{d},

(1.7)

where

p=1+\frac{4}{d}.

(1.8)

According to
[
Kwo89
]
and
[
Wei83
]
, this solution decays exponentially at infinity,

\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d},\ \exists C_{\alpha},D_{\alpha}>0,\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ |\partial^{\alpha}Q(x)|\leq C_{\alpha}e^{-D_{\alpha}|x|}.

(1.9)

In
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the blow-up regime in the mass-critical case

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i\partial_{t}\psi+\Delta\psi=-|\psi|^{p-1}\psi&\text{ in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
\psi(0,\cdot)=\psi_{0}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{array}\right.

(1.10)

is rather well-understood for
\psi_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Weinstein
[
Wei83
]
proved that initial data satisfying
\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
leads to the global existence in time of solutions, combining a sharp interpolation estimate to a Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality. This bound have been proven to be sharp by Merle in
\Sigma:=\{\psi_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,|\,x\psi_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}
using the pseudo-conformal transformation on the ground state
Q

[
Mer93
]
. Indeed, there exists an initial data
\psi_{0}\in\Sigma
such that
\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
and such that the corresponding solution
\psi
to (
1.10
) blow-up as
\|\nabla\psi(t,.)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\simeq 1/(T-t)
as
t\rightarrow T^{-}
. The blow-up phenomenon of (
1.10
) was further characterized
[
MR03
,
MR04
,
MR05a
,
MR05b
,
MR06
,
Rap05
]
in the set

\mathcal{B}_{\alpha^{*}}=\left\{\psi_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,\left|\,\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha^{*}\right.\right\},

for
\alpha^{*}>0
sufficiently small and
E(\psi_{0})<0
, assuming the so-called
spectral property
in high dimensions. For initial conditions in this set, there exists
T=T(\psi_{0})>0
such that the solutions writing as

\psi(x,t)=\dfrac{e^{i\gamma(t)}}{\lambda(t)}(Q+\epsilon)\left(\frac{x-x(t)}{\lambda(t)},t\right),

(1.11)

with
\|\epsilon(.,t)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\rightarrow 0
as
\alpha^{*}\rightarrow 0
, blow up at the rate,

\|\nabla\psi(.,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\simeq\dfrac{1}{\lambda(t)}\simeq\sqrt{\dfrac{\log|\log(T-t)|}{T-t}},\hskip 10.00002ptt\rightarrow T^{-}.

(1.12)

For bounded domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, two types of blow-up are found in the literature for the
L^{2}
mass-critical case. If
d\leq 4
, a log-log type of blow-up (as (
1.12
)) is exhibited in the set
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha^{*}}
by Planchon and Raphaël
[
PR07
]
. The second type of blow-up is a result from Godet
[
God11
, Theorem 1]
, a generalization of
[
BGT03
]
of a single point blow-up in dimension
2
to the case of a finite number blowing-up points in dimension
2
or
3
. It can also be considered as a generalization of the results from
[
Mer90
]
established in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to the case of bounded domains. We only state it in
2
-d for our purpose.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
BGT03
,
God11
]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be a smooth bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let
n\geq 1
and
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
be
n
distinct points in
\Omega
. Then, there exist
c>0
,
\lambda_{0}>0
such that for every
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
, there exist a time
T_{\lambda}\in(0,c\lambda^{-2})
and a solution
\phi_{\lambda}\in C([0,T_{\lambda});H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
to (
1.1
), which blow-up at the points
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
in time
T_{\lambda}
. Moreover, the following properties hold.

1.

The mass is conserved,

\|\phi_{\lambda}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\sqrt{n}\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},\hskip 20.00003pt\forall t\in[0,T_{\lambda}).

(1.13)

2.

The blow-up occurs in the energy space
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
with the following speed

\|\nabla\phi_{\lambda}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\underset{t\to T_{\lambda}^{-}}{\sim}\frac{\sqrt{n}\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}}{\lambda(T_{\lambda}-t)}.

3.

The function
\phi_{\lambda}
decomposes as

\phi_{\lambda}(t,x)=R_{\lambda}(t,x)+r_{\lambda}(t,x),\hskip 20.00003pt\forall(t,x)\in[0,T_{\lambda})\times\Omega,

(1.14)

with
R_{\lambda},r_{\lambda}\in C([0,T_{\lambda});H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
satisfying

R_{\lambda}(t,x)=\frac{1}{\lambda(T_{\lambda}-t)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{\frac{i(4-\lambda^{2}|x-x_{k}|^{2})}{4\lambda^{2}(T_{\lambda}-t)}}\varphi_{k}(x)Q\left(\frac{x-x_{k}}{\lambda(T_{\lambda}-t)}\right),\ \forall(t,x)\in[0,T_{\lambda})\times\Omega,

(1.15)

where
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega;[0,1])
have disjoint supports, verify
\varphi_{k}=1
near
x_{k}
and

\|r_{\lambda}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ce^{-\frac{\kappa}{\lambda(T_{\lambda}-t)}},\hskip 20.00003pt\forall t\in[0,T_{\lambda})\hskip 20.00003pt(\kappa>0).

(1.16)

For
n=1
, i.e. considering the case of a single blow-up point, the blow-up solutions of
Theorem

1.3
in the energy space
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
are actually of minimal
L^{2}
-norm as noticed in
[
PR07
]
. This is a consequence of the conservation laws (
1.2
), (
1.3
) and the sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality stated here in dimension
d=2
, see
[
Wei83
]
,

\displaystyle E(\psi)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\right)\left(1-\left(\dfrac{\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}{\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}}\right)^{2}\right).

(1.17)

Bounds on the blow-up rate for minimal mass blowing-up solutions are studied in
[
Ban04
]
. Furthermore, by using Brezis-Gallouet’s inequality (see
[
BG80
]
), the global well-posedness in the energy space
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
implies the global well-posedness in
H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. We then have the following result.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
PR07
]
,
[
BG80
]
)
.

The following two properties hold.

1.

For every initial data
\psi_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
satisfying
\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
, (
1.1
) is globally well-posed in the energy space
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

2.

For every initial data
\psi_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
satisfying
\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
, (
1.1
) is globally well-posed in the functional space

The goal of this article is to see if, by acting locally on the equation (
1.1
), one can prevent the blow-up from happening for the particular blow-up profiles of
Theorem

1.3
. More precisely, the controlled nonlinear Schrödinger equation that we consider in the following is

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i\partial_{t}\psi+\Delta\psi=-|\psi|^{2}\psi+v\mathds{1}_{\omega}&\text{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\psi=0&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\\
\psi(0,\cdot)=\psi_{0}&\text{ in }\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(1.18)

In (
1.18
), at time
t\in[0,T]
,
\psi(t,\cdot):\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
is the state and

is the control.

1.2
Control of the mass-critical focusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation

Our first main result shows that, with the help of a localized feedback control whose support contains the blow-up points, one can prevent the blow-up from happening and then build a global solution to the controlled problem (
1.18
).

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let
n\geq 1
and
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
be
n
distinct points in
\Omega
. Let
\omega\subset\Omega
be an open set such that
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\omega
. Then, there exist
\delta>0
,
\lambda_{0}>0
such that for every
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
, there exists a feedback control
v_{\lambda}\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty);H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
supported in
(0,T_{\lambda})\times\omega
and an associated global solution
\psi\in C([0,+\infty);H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
of (
1.18
) starting from
\psi(0,\cdot)=\phi_{\lambda}(0,\cdot)+w_{0}
, where
\phi_{\lambda}
is the blow-up profile from Theorem
1.3
and
\|w_{0}\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}<\delta
.

Our second main result shows that one can even achieve null controllability for such blow-up profiles, assuming a control condition that we present now. The linearized control system of (
1.18
) around the equilibrium
(\psi,v)=(0,0)
is given by the linear Schrödinger equation

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}\psi+\Delta\psi=v\mathds{1}_{\omega},\quad&\mathrm{in}\ (0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\psi=0,\quad&\mathrm{on}\ (0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\\
\psi(0,\cdot)=\psi_{0},\quad&\mathrm{in}\ \Omega.\end{cases}

(1.19)

Let us introduce the following hypothesis:

Assumption 1.6
.

The linear Schrödinger equation (
1.19
) is small-time null-controllable (STNC). That is, for every
T>0
and for every
\psi_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, there exists
v\in L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}(\omega))
such that the solution
\psi\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
of (
1.19
) satisfies
\psi(T,\cdot)=0
.

Controllability for the linear Schrödinger equation (
1.19
) began to be extensively investigated in the 1990s. Up to now, a necessary and sufficient geometrical condition on
\omega
, depending on
\Omega
, for the (STNC) of (
1.19
) to hold has not been identified. Let us present some of the well-known results in this direction (see
[
Lau14
]
for a survey up to 2014). For a general smooth bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
,
[
Leb92
]
guarantees that the so-called Geometric Control Condition (GCC) is sufficient for the (STNC) of the Schrödinger equation to hold. The proof of this result is based on microlocal analysis. (GCC) can be roughly formulated as follows: the subdomain
\omega
is said to
satisfy (GCC) in time
T>0
if and only if all rays of geometric optics propagating inside the domain
\Omega
and bouncing off the boundary reach the control set
\omega
non-diffractively in time less than
T
. This condition is not necessary for some specific domains. For instance, for
\Omega=(0,l_{1})\times\dots\times(0,l_{d})
, any nonempty open subset
\omega
is sufficient for the STNC of (
1.19
); see
[
Jaf90
]
or
[
KL05
]
for a proof using Ingham’s estimates, or
[
BBZ13
]
,
[
AM14
]
for a proof using semiclassical measures in the case of the torus. For the unit disk
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, explicit eigenfunctions concentrate near the boundary, so one can prove that exact controllability holds if and only if
\overline{\omega}
contains a (small) part of the boundary
\partial\mathbb{D}
(see
[
ALM16
]
).

Under Assumption
1.6
, we are able to prove small-time local null controllability in
H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
of (
1.18
) (see
Theorem

2.8
below), leading to our second main result.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\Omega
be a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let
n\geq 1
and
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
be
n
distinct points in
\Omega
. Let
\omega\subset\Omega
be an open set such that
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\omega
and such that Assumption
1.6
holds. Let
T>0
. Then, there exist
\delta>0
,
\lambda_{0}>0
such that for every
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
, there exists a control
v_{\lambda}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
supported in

and an associated solution
\psi\in C([0,T];H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
of (

), starting from

, where

is the blow-up profile of Theorem

(1.20)

1.3
Comments and generalizations

The following remarks are worth noting.

•

Regularity of the controlled trajectory.
Note that the regularity of the control
v
in both
Theorem

1.5
and
Theorem

1.7
does not immediately imply that the associated solution
\psi
belongs to
C([0,\infty);H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
in the first case or to
C([0,T];H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
in the second case. This regularity for the trajectory will, however, follow from our construction.

•

Robustness with respect to perturbation of the feedback control.
Let us emphasize that the control
v_{\lambda}\in L^{2}([0,\infty);H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
supported in
(0,T_{\lambda})\times\omega
of Theorem
1.5
is a piecewise continuous nonlinear closed-loop control. Such a control strategy is relevant for practical applications and is robust with respect to perturbations, which is why we focus on this case. The design of a simple but a priori not stable open-loop control and an associated global solution is performed in Section
2.6
below. We underline here that one cannot extend the conclusions of Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
1.7
from the initial data
\phi_{\lambda}(0,\cdot)
to the initial data
\phi_{\lambda}(0,\cdot)+w_{0}
with
w_{0}\neq 0
using a simple perturbation argument, such as Gronwall’s inequality. Indeed, these perturbation arguments do not guarantee that the solution starting from a perturbation of
\phi_{\lambda}(0,\cdot)
remains close to
\phi_{\lambda}(t,\cdot)
for
t>0
. The very fact that the time of blow-up for perturbed solutions is the same as that of
\phi_{\lambda}(t,\cdot)
is also not a simple consequence of Theorem
1.3
. Last but not least, we finally highlight that the size
\delta
of the allowed perturbations of the blow-up profiles does not depend on
\lambda
. By looking at the details of the proof of Proposition
2.3
below, one could notice that the parameter
\delta
in Theorem
1.5
, respectively in Theorem
1.7
, can be chosen of the following form
\delta=c\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
, respectively
\delta=c\delta_{T/2}
, where
c=1/16
and
\delta_{T/2}
is the radius of the ball centered at
0
in
H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
where the local null-controllability of (
1.18
) holds at time
T/2
. We could probably increase the value of the constant
c>0
, but we do not investigate this issue.

•

Blow-up versus localized control.
To the best of our knowledge,
Theorem

1.5
is the first result demonstrating the possibility of preventing blow-up in a nonlinear Schrödinger equation using localized control. From (
1.13
), it is clear that
\|\psi(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\sqrt{n}\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
is not small, meaning
Theorem

1.7
cannot be directly derived from a local null-controllability result. Instead, we focus on the specific knowledge of the blow-up profiles and use control regions that must include the blow-up points. Notice that even for semilinear heat equations with power-like nonlinearities, where blow-up solutions at prescribed points can also be constructed
[
Mer92
]
, establishing such a property remains an open problem. Furthermore, our results are in stark contrast to
[
LZ22
]
, where the authors showed that a feedback control acting on
\omega
could induce a solution of the heat equation to blow up at time
T
at a unique point
a\in\omega
. Similarly, our results differ significantly from
[
FCZ00
]
, in the context of semilinear heat equations with weakly superlinear nonlinearities, where the authors were able to prevent blow-up and even drive the solution to zero for arbitrary times, with nonempty open sets control regions, and for any initial data. Whether such a property can be proven or disproven for nonlinear Schrödinger equations remains entirely open. A related result to
Theorem

1.7
can be found in the recent work of the first author in
[
Gag23
]
, where local controllability around ground state solitary waves with different scalings is achieved using a whole boundary control.

•

Examples of geometrical control assumptions.
When
\Omega=(0,l_{1})\times(0,l_{2})
is a rectangle, any nonempty open subset
\omega
leads to the STNC of (
1.19
), so
Theorem

1.7
holds assuming only that
\omega
contain the blow-up points of the profile
\phi_{\lambda}
. On the other hand, when
\Omega=\mathbb{D}
the unit disk,
Theorem

1.7
holds assuming that
\omega
contain the blow-up points and
\overline{\omega}\cap\partial D\neq\emptyset
. When
\Omega
is an arbitrary smooth bounded
2
-d domain,
Theorem

1.7
holds assuming that GCC is satisfied. See the previous discussion after
Assumption

1.6
.

•

Extensions.
The results of
Theorem

1.5
and
Theorem

1.7
can be extended without significant technical challenges to the 1-dimensional case, where the
L^{2}
-critical focusing nonlinearity in (
1.10
) is considered with
p
as defined in (
1.8
), i.e.,
p=5
. In this case, the geometric control hypothesis stated in
Assumption

1.6
is not required because (
1.19
) is STNC when
\omega
is a nonempty open subset of
\Omega=(0,L)
. Extending
Theorem

1.5
and
Theorem

1.7
to the 3-dimensional case is also possible, albeit with additional technical difficulties, as described below. However, higher dimensional cases are more challenging due to the lack of an embedding
H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

1.4
Extension to the 3-dimensional case

Here, we outline possible extensions of our main results,
Theorem

1.5
and
Theorem

1.7
, to the 3-dimensional case, emphasizing the key differences. For
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, a smooth bounded domain, the
L^{2}
-critical focusing nonlinearity in (
1.10
) corresponds to the
p=4/3

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\psi_{\psi_{0}}\|^{2}_{C([0,T];H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\psi_{\psi_{1}}\|^{2}_{C([0,T];H^{2}(\Omega))}\right)\|\psi_{\psi_{0}}-\psi_{\psi_{1}}\|_{C([0,T];H^{2}(\Omega))}.

By using once again the fact that the flow map is Lipschitz on bounded set, we obtain a new constant
C>0
independent on
\varepsilon_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\|B\psi_{0}-B\psi_{1}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\psi_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\psi_{1}\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\|\psi_{0}-\psi_{1}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq 2C\varepsilon^{2}_{0}\|\psi_{0}-\psi_{1}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

By now, we set
\varepsilon_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{C}}
and
B:\overline{B_{H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}}(0,\varepsilon_{0})\longrightarrow\overline{B_{H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}}(0,\varepsilon_{0})
is a contraction mapping and admits an unique fixed point, according to the Banach fixed-point theorem.
∎

Global Bifurcation in Symmetric Systems of Nonlinear Wave Equations
Carlos Garcia-Azpeitia, Ziad Ghanem, Wieslaw Krawcewicz

Abstract
. In this paper, we use the equivariant degree theory to establish a global bifurcation result for the existence of non-stationary branches of solutions to a nonlinear, two-parameter family of hyperbolic wave equations with local delay and non-trivial damping. As a motivating example, we consider an application of our result to a system of N N identical vibrating strings with dihedral coupling relations.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Gamma

an orthogonal
\Gamma

\Gamma

system of delayed, hyperbolic equations

\begin{cases}\nu^{2}\partial^{2}_{t}u-\partial^{2}_{x}u+\delta\partial_{t}u+\beta S_{\tau}u=f(u)-A(\alpha)u,\quad u(t,x)\in V;\\

\Gamma

is a differentiable function satisfying the conditions:

(
A_{1}
)

(
A_{2}
)

\sigma f(u)=f(\sigma u)
for all
u\in V,\;\sigma\in\Gamma
;

(
A_{3}
)

there exist

such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}|f(u)|\leq a|u|^{\eta_{0}/2};\\
|f^{\prime}(u)|\leq b|u|^{\eta_{1}/2}.\end{cases}

In the context of the most natural application for (
1

N
coupled identical vibrating strings subjected to nonlinear forces, non-trivial damping, and environmental feedback — the vector

represents the vertical displacement of our strings at any time

and position

\tau

Notice that the boundary value problem (
1

(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
. The

functions which satisfy (
1
) for particular parameter pairs can be categorized as follows:

•

stationary solutions
, depending only on the position variable

and which, though more interesting than the trivial solution, can often be identified using classical methods (e.g. by reducing (
1
) to a system of ODEs);

•

non-stationary solutions
, expressing dependency on the time variable
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and which may exhibit exotic spatio-temporal symmetries that enhance our understanding of the model.

Our primary objectives are
(\rm i)
to demonstrate how equivariant degree theory can be used in order to identify parameter values
(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
at which branches of non-trivial periodic solutions to (
1

(\rm ii)
to determine
under what conditions these branches consist only of non-stationary solutions and
(\rm iii)
to describe their possible global and symmetric properties. Although models similar to (
1

treatment as local bifurcation problems (cf.
[
Koso1

,
Koso3
]

A prerequisite for the application of any degree-theory based argument to global bifurcation in (
1

\mathscr{H}
with a nonlinear operator in the form of a compact perturbation of identity. In order to appreciate the difficulty of this task, consider the differential operator
\mathscr{F}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathscr{H}\rightarrow\mathscr{H}
given by

\mathscr{F}(\alpha,\beta,u):=u-\mathscr{L}^{-1}(f(u)-A(\alpha)u+u-\beta S_{\tau}u),

where

\mathscr{L}:\mathscr{H}\rightarrow\mathscr{H},\quad\mathscr{L}:=\nu^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}-\partial_{x}^{2}+\delta\partial_{t}+\id,

and notice that (

\mathscr{F}(\alpha,\beta,u)=0.

The main complication arises from the fact that the existence, continuity and compactness of the inverse operator

depends on the model variables
\nu
and
\delta

. Indeed, this is the setting Kosovalić et al. consider in
[

Koso3
]

It can easily be verified that

is also continuous under the conditions

,

[

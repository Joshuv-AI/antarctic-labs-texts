Aumolertinib in non-small cell lung cancer with uncommon EGFR exon 19 deletions: a real-world dual-center study
Authors: Guangjian Yang, Linyan Tian, Dongsheng He, Jiaqi Hu, Fang Wei, Weihua Li, Pei Li, Huanbing Qiu, Xiaoyong Tang, Yan Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEpidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)–tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) (EGFR-TKIs) show heterogeneous responses in patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) harboring uncommon EGFR exon 19 deletion (E19del) mutations. This study evaluated the efficacy of second- and third-generation (2G/3G) EGFR-TKIs as first-line therapy in NSCLC patients with uncommon E19del variants.MethodsThis dual-center retrospective study included 118 patients with advanced or recurrent NSCLC harboring uncommon EGFR E19del mutations (excluding E746_A750del). All patients received first-line treatment with 2G/3G EGFR-TKIs. Molecular profiling was performed using next-generation sequencing. Progression-free survival (PFS) was analyzed across TKI subtypes and deletion categories. Molecular modeling was performed to evaluate the drug-binding affinities.ResultsAmong the 12 identified E19del subtypes, L747_P753delinsS (21.2%) and L747_T751del (20.3%) were predominant, with an overall median PFS (mPFS) of 15.5 months. The differences in mPFS were observed across specific TKIs: aumolertinib (15.9 months), osimertinib (15.5 months), furmonertinib (15.0 months) and afatinib (12.2 months). No difference in PFS was observed between 2G (12.2 months) and 3G TKIs (15.5 months; P = 0.19). The subgroup analysis revealed numerically shorter mPFS for T751-deletions (9.4 months) compared to E746-deletions (15.7 months) or L747-deletions (14.8 months). Aumolertinib demonstrated consistent PFS regardless of deletion subtypes, whereas osimertinib-treated group exhibited subtype-dependent efficacy. Molecular modeling confirmed a stronger binding affinity of aumolertinib than osimertinib across prevalent L747-deletion variants.ConclusionsThis comparative study demonstrated the improved and consistent activity of aumolertinib across diverse uncommon EGFR E19del subtypes compared to osimertinib, due to its enhanced binding affinity and structural adaptability. These findings support molecular subtype-guided TKI selection, aumolertinib as a first-line option for patients harboring uncommon EGFR E19del variants, particularly in settings lacking comprehensive molecular stratification.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-026-16119-7.

Introduction

Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is the most prevalent subtype with high mortality, accounting for ~ 80–85% of lung cancer patients globally [1, 2]. In China, patients aged > 60 years reported with high incidence and mortality rates (60%) for NSCLC [3]. Among the patients with NSCLC, epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) gene mutations represent the most common driver alterations (~ 28.2%) [4]. Development of tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) has represented a major advancement in the management of EGFR-mutant NSCLC, paving the way for a new era of targeted therapeutic strategies. The second- and third-generation TKIs (2G and 3G EGFR-TKIs) including afatinib [5], osimertinib [6], aumolertinib [7], dacomitinib [8] and furmonertinib [9], showed better efficacy in treating patients with EGFR-mutated NSCLC than first-generation (1G) EGFR-TKIs (such as erlotinib and gefitinib) [10]. Among EGFR-TKIs, osimertinib, a 3G EGFR-TKI, exhibited improved clinical outcomes, and is currently approved as the standard first-line therapy for patients with EGFR-mutated NSCLC [11].

With the advancement in molecular diagnostic technologies, greater proportion of EGFR mutations have been identified. Among these, exon 19 in-frame deletion (E19del) is the most common subtype (~ 44%), followed by the L858R point mutation in exon 21 (~ 41%). The most prevalent EGFR E19del involves a short in-frame deletion spanning codons E746 to A750 (E746_A750del), representing approximately 63% of all EGFR E19del mutations [12]. In addition to this common variant, other uncommon variants of E19del have been identified, which exhibit heterogeneous responses to EGFR-TKIs [13]. EGFR-TKIs have shown promising efficacy in NSCLC patients with common EGFR E19del mutations compared to those with uncommon variants, whose treatment outcomes remain less favourable and more variable [14].

Recent studies have observed differential treatment responses to EGFR-TKIs among patients with uncommon EGFR E19del mutations [15, 16]. It had been demonstrated the median progression-free survival (PFS) of L747_P753delinsS was shorter than that of L747_A750delinsP and L747_T751delinsP [17]. However, Truini et al. reported that the L747_A750delinsP mutation exhibited significantly worse outcomes than those with tumors harboring L747_P753delinsS mutations [18]. These findings highlight the importance of deletion-specific EGFR-TKI selection and precise nature of deletions in therapeutic effect. Although uncommon EGFR E19del variants represent a small subset of cases, their clinical significance is amplified by the large global burden of lung cancer. Currently, with the advancement in the molecular architecture of EGFR mutations, newer 3G EGFR-TKIs such as aumolertinib and furmonertinib have entered clinical use. However, their differential prognostic impact in uncommon E19del subtypes remains unstudied. While some studies support the efficacy of 2G and 3G EGFR-TKIs compared with chemotherapy [15, 19–21], direct comparative clinical studies on their differential prognostic impact in uncommon E19del subtypes remain limited, highlighting a significant gap in current clinical research. Therefore, identifying the most effective EGFR-TKIs for specific E19del subtypes is crucial and remains a significant challenge in precision oncology.

This study aims to evaluate and compare the clinical efficacy and molecular binding dynamics of 2G and 3G EGFR-TKIs, particularly aumolertinib, as first-line targeted therapy in patients with NSCLC harboring uncommon EGFR E19del mutations. This is a first of its kind and the findings could help in the development of optimized, subtype-specific targeted strategies and support individualized treatment approaches to improve patient outcomes.

Methods

Patients and data collection

The medical records and clinical data of patients with NSCLC harboring uncommon EGFR E19del mutations were retrospectively collected and analyzed at Shandong Cancer Hospital and Institute, and the National Cancer Center/Cancer Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences between October 2021 and October 2024. The flowchart was revealed in Fig. 1. EGFR E19del subtypes are determined via next-generation sequencing (NGS) using the Illumina sequencing system in institutional laboratories or certified third-party genetic testing companies accredited by the national quality system. The final follow-up was conducted on January 11, 2025. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of National Cancer Center/Cancer Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences and Peking Union Medical College (Approval No. 18–070/1678). The requirement for obtaining informed consent was waived owing to the retrospective, and observational nature of this study.

Fig. 1Patient screening flowchart

Patient screening flowchart

Patients aged ≥ 18 years; histologically diagnosed with metastatic or recurrent NSCLC; confirmed with uncommon EGFR E19del mutations; and received 2G or 3G EGFR-TKIs therapy as the initial (first-line) treatment were included. Key exclusion criteria included patients with E746_A750del mutation; and who had received prior EGFR-TKIs or chemotherapy. Patients harboring other EGFR mutations such as exon 20 T790M, exon 21 L858R, or exon 20 insertions were also excluded.

Treatment and efficacy evaluation

Patients received 2G EGFR-TKIs including afatinib (40 mg once daily), and 3G EGFR-TKIs including osimertinib (80 mg once daily), aumolertinib (110 mg once daily) and furmonertinib (80 mg once daily). Patients continued treatment until radiographic progression or intolerable toxicity observed by the physicians, with dose adjustment allowed during the treatment.

Imaging examination at baseline was performed by computed tomography (CT) of the chest and abdomen, brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and whole-body bone scan. Tumor response including complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), or progressive disease (PD) was assessed one month after treatment initiation and subsequently every ~ 2 months by the investigators, using Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1. PFS was evaluated from time of treatment initiation until disease progression or death from any cause. Objective response rate (ORR) was defined as the proportion of patients with confirmed CR or PR, and the disease control rate (DCR) was defined as the percentage of those with CR, PR, or SD.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 23.0, SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Continuous variables were summarized using medians and ranges, while categorical variables were using frequency and percentage. Survival outcomes were estimated by the Kaplan–Meier method and survival curves were plotted using GraphPad Prism version 5.0. Comparisons among the subgroups were conducted using analysis of variance (ANOVA) or the chi-square test, as appropriate. PFS between different subgroups was compared using the log-rank test (two-sided). A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Computational homology modeling and docking method

Homology modeling was performed using the Molecular Operating Environment (MOE) software (version 2023.01) with the “Homology Model” program, starting with a multiple sequence alignment of the EGFR primary structures. The three-dimensional (3D) structures of EGFR E19del variants were generated through comparative modeling, using the crystal structure of human EGFR kinase domain [PDB, code: 4I23] deposited in the RCSB Protein Data Bank as a template. The resulting homology models were imported into Maestro by Schrödinger and prepared using the Protein Preparation Wizard. Molecular docking was performed using Glide SP, which applies a series of hierarchical filters to explore ligand conformations, orientations, and positions. The final docked pose was generated deterministically following geometry optimization using the OPLS3e force field.

Energy calculation method

The compounds were constructed using the 3D-sketcher module in Maestro (version 2024.1). Binding free energy (ΔGbind) was calculated using the Molecular Mechanics/Generalized Born Surface Area (MM-GBSA) approach to evaluate the binding affinity of the compound.

Results

Demographic characteristics of patients

A total of 118 patients with uncommon EGFR E19del mutations (except for E746_A750del) were screened between October 2021 and October 2024, and included in this study. Among them, 70 patients were from National Cancer Center/Cancer Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences whereas 48 patients from Shandong Cancer Hospital. Most patients were female (n = 67, 56.8%), never-smokers (n = 68, 57.6%), the Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG PS) of 0 score (n = 89, 75.4%) and aged ≤ 65 (n = 81, 68.6%). Of enrolled patients, 25.4% (n = 30) presented with baseline central nervous system (CNS) metastases. 65.2% (n = 77) exhibited negative PD-L1 expression, 28% (n = 33) showed PD-L1 expression of 1–49%, and 6.8% (n = 8) demonstrated PD-L1 expression exceeding 50%. All the patients included were diagnosed with metastatic or recurrent lung adenocarcinomas, and treated with EGFR-TKIs as their initial therapy. Among all patients, 10.2% (n = 12) received afatinib, a 2G TKI, while the remaining 89.8% (n = 106) were treated with 3G TKIs, such as osimertinib (50.8%, n = 60), aumolertinib (29.7%, n = 35), and furmonertinib (9.3%, n = 11) (Table 1). At baseline, the first evaluation revealed that 66.9% had PR, 30.5% had SD, and 2.5% experienced PD.

Molecular landscape of EGFR exon 19 deletion alterations

EGFR E19del was assessed in 118 patients using their tumor samples. Of these, 33 patients were identified with pure exon 19 deletions and 85 patients with exon 19 deletion-insertion (E19delins) variants through NGS. Except for one patient harboring uncommon A750_E758delinsP (n = 1, 0.8%), the remaining deletion mutations are classified into E746, L747, and T751 (totally 117 patients), based on the location and deletion of specific amino acids in exon 19 of the EGFR gene. Among these 117 patients, a total of 12 distinct EGFR E19del subtypes were identified, with L747_P753delinsS being the most frequent (n = 25, 21.2%), followed by L747_T751del (n = 24, 20.3%), E746_T751delinsA/I/IP/L/V/VA (n = 16; 13.6%), and E746_S752delinsC/I/L/V (n = 13, 11.0%) (Table 2, and Fig. 2A).

Therapeutic use of 2G and 3G TKIs in EGFR exon 19 deletion subtypes

Only three patients experienced disease progression at their first efficacy evaluation after TKI treatment, with an overall DCR of 97.5%. Patients with E746-deletions demonstrated an ORR of 63.9% and a DCR of 97.2%, while those with L747-deletions showed an ORR of 65.4% and a DCR of 98.7%. These response rates were higher to those observed in patients with T751-deletions (ORR: 50%; DCR: 75%). In the furmonertinib group, the overall ORR was only 27.3%, which was lower than that achieved with osimertinib (70%) and aumolertinib (68.6%). Differences in ORR were also observed across deletion subgroups for both osimertinib and aumolertinib. Specifically, in E746-deletion subgroup, osimertinib yielded a higher ORR (75.0%) compared to aumolertinib (63.6%). In L747-deletion subgroup, aumolertinib demonstrated a higher ORR compared to osimertinib (72.7% vs. 67.6%).

PFS between different subgroups

A total of 118 patients were available for first-line efficacy analysis, and the overall mPFS for the population was 15.5 months (Fig. 2B). With a median follow-up of 19.9 months, PFS events were observed in 67 patients, including 28 (23.8%) patients who experienced local recurrence, 19 (16.1%) with extracranial metastasis, and 20 (16.9%) with CNS metastasis (Fig. 2C). The remaining 51 (43.2%) patients had not developed PD yet. The mPFS varied between 2G and 3G TKIs (Fig. 2D), with afatinib, osimertinib, furmonertinib, and aumolertinib showing mPFS of 12.2, 15.5, 15.0, 15.9 months, respectively (P = 0.59). However, no difference in mPFS was observed between 2G and 3G TKIs (12.2 months vs. 15.5 months, P = 0.19). Further analyses on PFS based on structural variations found no significant difference between E19del and E19delins cohorts (mPFS: 14.6 vs. 15.5 months, P = 0.19) (Fig. 2E). Analysis based on amino acid deletion positions revealed numerically shorter mPFS (9.4 months) in T751-deletion subgroup, compared to E746- (15.7 months) or L747-deletion (14.8 months) subgroups (Fig. 2F). Consistent with the ORR findings, patients with L747-deletion treated with aumolertinib demonstrated a slightly longer mPFS compared to the osimertinib group (15.5 m vs. 15.3 m, P = 0.63). The mPFS for patients with E746-deletion treated with aumolertinib has not yet been reached (Table 3). Among these, L747_P753delinsS, L747_T751del, E746_S752delinsV, and E746_T751delinsX accounted for a relatively high proportion of EGFR uncommon E19del subtypes. The median PFS in these four subgroups was 14.8 months, 15.5 months, 33.0 months, and 17.1 months, respectively (Fig. S1A). Subsequently, a subgroup analysis of patients receiving different TKI treatment regimens within each subtype revealed superior short-term activity of osimertinib, while aumolertinib demonstrated favorable long-term efficacy, particularly in patients harboring L747_T751del (Table 4 and Fig. 3). In patients with baseline brain metastases, those treated with osimertinib and aumolertinib achieved similar PFS benefits (14.8 vs. 10.1 months, P = 0.17, Fig. S1B). Additionally, a subgroup analysis revealed no significant differences in PFS among PD-L1 expression levels following TKI treatment (Fig. S1C).

Computational modeling and drug-binding dynamics simulation

From the structure analysis of wild-type EGFR crystal structure (PDB ID: 4I23), the residues Glu746 and Leu747 are located in the protein kinase domain (Fig. 4A). Glu746 is followed by a hydrophobic region (Hyd1), which is composed of three amino acids, namely Ala743, Ile744, and Lys745. Residues within this region are in hydrophobic contact with the adenine ring of ATP forming a hydrophobic pocket. The invariant Lys72 residue interacts with β-phosphoryl groups of the bound ATP and is thought to be crucial in mediating correct orientation of the ligand. In addition, a very strong salt-bridge interaction is formed between Glu762 and Lys745 which is located next to Glu746. This further stabilizes Lys745 and this β sheet conformation. To sum up, the amino acids adjacent to Glu746 interact closely with the surrounding amino acids to maintain the conformation of the protein, and the mutation of Glu746 may have little effect on the 3D structure of EGFR. We found that Leu747 is relatively close to αChelix, suggesting that mutations at this site may influence the conformation of the αC-helix. Given that kinase activation is often associated with changes in αC-helix orientation, alterations at Leu747 could potentially impact enzyme regulation. Therefore, we speculate that mutations involving Leu747 may have some effect on protein structure.

We modelled the mutant protein structures of E746_S752delinsV, L747_A750delinsP, L747_P753delinsS, L747_T751del, and L747_S752del using homology modelling. The inserted amino acids were located within a loop region. We superimposed the mutant protein structures and the wild-type EGFR, to observe the conformational changes in protein before and after mutation.

E746_S752delinsV

When the mutation site is amplified, the missing seven amino acids (E746-S752) extend from the end of the β-sheet to the flexible loop region. Their deletion primarily results in a shortened loop, without affecting the surrounding amino acids and the secondary structure of the protein. Consequently, it has little impact on the overall structure of the protein.

To better understand the drug-binding conformation, we performed a molecular modeling study based on the homology model of E746_S752delinsV. As shown in Table 5, all five TKIs (2G and 3G TKIs) formed an important H-bond with Met797 in hinge region. Additionally, they are covalently bound to protein. These interactions suggest a strong affinity between the TKIs and the E746_S752delinsV protein which may support their therapeutic efficacy against this mutation (Fig. 4B).

L747_A750delinsP

In addition to the loop, L747_A750delinsP mutation induces changes in the adjacent β-sheet resulting in some differences in protein structure compared to wild-type EGFR proteins, which may affect small molecule binding. As the 2G and 3G TKIs failed to form hydrogen bonds with methionine 793 in the hinge region and showed lower docking score at L747_A750delinsP than at E746_S752delinsV, indicating their weak interactions with the protein (Fig. 4C).

L747_P753delinsS

The missing seven amino acids (L747-P753) span from the loop to the α-C helix, and the L747_P753delinsS mutation affects both the structural elements. Structural comparison between the mutant and wild-type protein revealed shortened loop and truncated α-C helix. Given the α-C helix role in enzyme regulation, this mutation may affect the critical position of the protein. Molecular docking score of 2G and 3G TKIs demonstrated the lower and weaker binding interaction. Therefore, we speculate that this mutation will have an impact on protein and small molecule binding. These five drugs failed to interact well with L747_P753delinsS and showed lower docking score at L747_P753delinsS compared to EGFR E746_S752delinsV, indicating their weak binding affinity with proteins (Fig. 4D).

L747_T751del and L747_S752del

In the L747_T751del mutation, deletion of five amino acids results in a positional shift, the original Ser752 followed by Glu746 becomes Ser747. Similarly, in L747_S752del mutation, deletion of six amino acids leads to a positional shift, the original Pro753 followed by Glu746 becomes Pro747.

The overall structures of L747_T751del (Fig. 4E) and L747_S752del (Fig. 4F) are similar. This is due to the differential amino acid (Ser752) in both mutant proteins is located within the flexible loop region, and the deletions mainly change the loop size of the mutant protein, without greater impact on the overall protein structure. Furthermore, the docking score and binding energy of these two mutant proteins with binding small molecules are similar. The slight differences between these two mutant proteins did not affect their binding with small molecules.

Discussion

This dual-center retrospective study is the first to systematically evaluate the clinical features and treatment responses of patients with NSCLC harboring uncommon EGFR E19del mutations treated with 2G or 3G EGFR-TKIs. Aumolertinib demonstrated broader applicability than osimertinib and other TKIs, showing promising PFS and molecular binding affinity. These findings support molecular subtype-guided precision therapy and offer new insights for individualized treatment strategies in uncommon EGFR E19del cases.

EGFR-TKIs had significantly reduced the risk of CNS metastases in this population [22–24]. Compared to patients with other oncogenic driver mutations, those with EGFR mutations exhibit significantly higher cumulative incidences of CNS metastases, with reported rates of 46%, 64%, and 71% at 1, 3, and 5 years, respectively [25]. However, data specifically addressing the association between uncommon EGFR E19del variants and the risk of CNS metastases remain limited. In this study, the incidence of CNS metastases among patients with uncommon EGFR E19del mutations was ~ 25%, which aligns with previous reports in patients with common EGFR E19del mutations [16], further contributing valuable insight into the metastatic risk associated with this molecular subset.

The relationship between uncommon E19del subtypes and PD-L1 expression is largely unexplored. Our findings align with low PD-L1 positivity (~ 3.5%) in EGFR-mutant NSCLC [26] and a negative correlation between EGFR mutation and PD-L1 [27]. We provide first evidence that uncommon E19del variants also show mutual exclusivity with PD-L1, suggesting limited immunotherapy benefit and reinforcing TKIs as first-line therapy. However, reduced PFS in the T751-deletion subgroup indicates suboptimal TKI outcomes, highlighting the need to study mutation-immune interactions. NGS profiling revealed high heterogeneity: E19delins (72.0%, n = 85) was more common than pure E19del (28.0%), with L747 codon deletions most prevalent, consistent with a prior study [28]. L747-deletion variants often involve complex indels that may increase TKI resistance. For example, L747_A750delinsP with a proline insertion disrupts the glycine-rich loop, impairing TKI binding and leading to primary resistance [18, 29–30] and worse prognosis.

Patients with common E19del respond better to TKIs than those with uncommon variants, but evidence for the latter is limited. In this cohort, first-line TKIs yielded a median PFS of 15.5 months in uncommon E19del patients – better than chemotherapy but inferior to common mutations. While 3G TKIs like osimertinib are standard [31], newer agents (aumolertinib, furmonertinib) offer options, but systematic efficacy comparisons in uncommon E19del subtypes are lacking. In the overall TKI-treated population, E746- and L747-deletion subtypes had numerically longer PFS than T751- and E758-deletion subtypes. Subgroup analysis showed this difference was mainly in osimertinib-treated patients, while aumolertinib showed no significant PFS difference across subtypes, highlighting its pharmacological advantage. Thus, osimertinib efficacy may depend on the specific deletion variant. The efficacy disparity may stem from drug-protein binding dynamics. Efficacy of osimertinib critically depends on covalent binding to Cys797, while certain indel mutations (e.g., T751_I759delinsN/S) may disrupt this via steric hindrance. In contrast, pyrimidine scaffold with a flexible propionamide side chain of aumolertinib offers conformational adaptability to accommodate variant-specific perturbations. Therefore, identifying the exact E19del variant is crucial before osimertinib therapy. Aumolertinib shows consistent efficacy across subtypes, suggesting broader applicability. Without detailed genetic profiling, “blind selection for aumolertinib” may be a pragmatic strategy to improve PFS. Due to the limited number of furmonertinib treated patients, direct comparison was not feasible, and future research should explore differential efficacy of 3G TKIs across uncommon E19del subtypes.

Computational modeling offers rapid drug sensitivity prediction [32]. Given consistent efficacy of aumolertinib, we evaluated binding affinities of afatinib, dacomitinib, osimertinib, aumolertinib, and furmonertinib using computer-aided molecular modeling. Dynamics simulation showed that among predominant uncommon E19del subtypes (L747 series: L747_A750delinsP, L747_P753delinsS, L747_T751del, L747_S752del), aumolertinib consistently exhibited stronger binding affinities than other TKIs. Aumolertinib is a highly potent, selective, irreversible 3G EGFR-TKI against common sensitizing mutations (E19del, L858R) and the T790M resistance mutation, with favorable CNS penetration. Mechanistically, it covalently binds Cys797, blocking EGFR autophosphorylation and downstream MAPK/AKT pathways, thereby inhibiting cell cycle, inducing apoptosis [33–36]. Several uncommon E19del variants (e.g., A750_E758del, A750_E758delinsP, T751_I759delinsG, T751_I759delinsLD, T751_I759delinsN) achieved a PR rate of 72.7% and PFS of 12.0 months with 1G TKIs, which were considered sensitizing [37]. However, T751_I759delinsS showed intrinsic resistance to gefitinib (PFS 2.0 months) [38]. Similarly, our prior study found T751- or S752-deletions had the shortest median PFS (2.9 months) with 1G TKIs, compared with E746- (11.4 months) and L747-deletions (17.2 months) [39]. In the current study, T751-deletion showed numerically shorter PFS than E746- and L747-deletion after treatment of 3G TKIs (mPFS: 9.4, 15.7, 14.8 months), consistent with our published study [39]. However, this retrospective study had a limited sample size, particularly for the T751-deletion subgroup, which may cause statistical bias.

The phase II ACHIEVE trial showed that high-dose aumolertinib (165 mg daily) achieved a 12-month PFS rate of 62.1%, median PFS of 20.5 months, and intracranial PFS of 76.8%, suggesting long-term benefit in untreated EGFR-mutant NSCLC with brain metastases [40]. High-dose aumolertinib may be an option for T751-deletion subtypes that might not benefit from standard-dose 3G TKIs. Another phase II study evaluated aumolertinib plus pemetrexed/carboplatin as first-line therapy in EGFR-mutant NSCLC [41]. “Aumolertinib plus chemotherapy” or “plus amivantamab” may show potential antitumor activity for advanced EGFR-mutant NSCLC, especially for uncommon E19del subtypes, warranting randomized controlled trials.

Molecular dynamics suggest osimertinib has stronger binding stability in E746_S752delinsV, consistent with superior response rate in our osimertinib-treated cohort. However, the aumolertinib-treated group showed superior long-term PFS, suggesting osimertinib may be more prone to early resistance. In the E19del population, osimertinib resistance mechanisms include acquired EGFR mutations, loss of T790M, MET activation, bypass pathways, and small cell lung cancer transformation. Direct molecular evidence for aumolertinib resistance is scarce, but similar phenomena have been observed. Resistance profiles for these drugs in uncommon E19del mutations remain unclear and need further exploration. For patients with uncommon E19del, aumolertinib may be the preferred TKI if no contraindications. Those with L747-deletions, especially L747_T751del, are more likely to achieve long-term benefit. For patients intolerant to aumolertinib, switching to osimertinib may also yield favorable outcomes. Further follow-up from large-scale cohorts investigating 3G TKIs as first-line treatment for uncommon E19del mutations is awaited. Comprehensive efficacy evaluation across a larger number of distinct uncommon E19del subtypes will generate more robust evidence.

Despite providing preliminary evidence supporting the potential advantages of aumolertinib in patients with uncommon EGFR E19del mutations, this study has several limitations. First, the retrospective nature of the study may introduce selection bias in the PFS analysis. The baseline characteristics may differ between treatment groups, and the difference in PFS could be influenced by confounding factors other than the treatment regimens. In addition, due to limited sample size, we were unable to directly compare the efficacy of aumolertinib and furmonertinib between patients with uncommon EGFR E19del subtypes. The study cohort was primarily composed of East Asian population, and thus, the generalizability of the findings to other ethnic populations requires further validation.

Conclusions

This is the first study to report that aumolertinib has broader applicability, better PFS, and stronger binding affinity than osimertinib across multiple uncommon EGFR E19del variants. By systematically evaluating 2G and 3G EGFR-TKIs in this population, our findings provide new evidence supporting personalized treatment strategies, assist clinicians in making precise therapeutic decisions, and ultimately improve real-world clinical outcomes.

Pilot tests of continuous gas extraction with improved L-shaped wells from mining and goaf areas
Authors: Jianghua Zhang, Yong Qin, Guoqing Li, Hui Ma, Guofu Li, Gaojian Li, Ting Liu, Junjun Li, Changjiang Ji, Ming Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe extraction of coalbed methane (CBM) from coal mining-affected areas and goaf areas is an important approach to the coal and CBM co-mining. The application of widely used L-shaped wells has proven challenging in coal mining-affected areas. This study aims to enhance the design and extend the service life of L-shaped wells, thereby boosting their overall efficiency and eliminating the spatiotemporal separation between CBM extraction in mining-affected areas and goaf areas. The sources of CBM in mining-affected areas and goaf areas were analyzed, the gas production effect and the reasons for the low production of three conventional L-shaped wells, first in mining-affected area and then transformed into a goaf area with the coal mining, were discussed. And a novel design methodology, termed “one well, two purposes” was proposed for L-shaped wells, enabling sequential CBM extraction from mining-affected areas followed by goaf areas. It turns out that only one of the three conventional L-shaped wells achieved profitable extraction and therefore the conventional L-shaped well design needs to be improved. A standardized design scheme of the horizontal section of L-shaped wells was proposed. And based on an “O-shaped” ring theory, an “upper three zones” theory and a numerical simulation method, a layer location selection method was proposed for the horizontal section of an L-shaped well in mining-affected areas and goaf areas. The improved L-shaped well design was characterized by a large-diameter two-section wellbore structure, a segmented cementing and discontinuous multi-stage cementing method. And the continuous extraction method of improved L-shaped wells in mining-affected areas and goaf areas was discussed. The optimized design scheme could extend the stable production time span of an L-shaped well and improve its CBM extraction efficiency.Keywords: Mining-affected area, Goaf, Coalbed methane, L-shaped well, Continuous extractionSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Environmental sciences

Introduction

For coal-producing countries, extracting methane from coal seams is essential to mitigate gas disasters, obtain natural gas resources and reduce atmospheric emissions. In Australia, pre-mining in-seam horizontal directional boreholes are used to extract gas and mitigate hazards and recover coal seam gas as an energy resource and goaf gas drainage is predominantly performed through surface-based vertical and horizontal directional boreholes. Excessive vacuum can induce dangerous air leakage into the goaf, increasing spontaneous combustion risk1,2. To reduce methane emissions into the atmosphere, the efficiency of post-mining goaf methane drainage systems during atmospheric pressure drops should be increased3. The gob gas pressure rises with the mine flooding and the gas emission into the atmosphere will increase or the risks of suffocation or explosion in basements or while backfilling the shaft will occur4. Subterranean barriers can effectively aid the pre-drainage and reduce overall emissions from open-cut and underground coal mines5. Practice in Poland has shown that the drainage efficiency of long-reach directional drilling boreholes for gas drainage is approximately 70%, much higher than that of conventional cross-measured boreholes (30%). Under deep cover, severe deformations even failure in gas wells have occurred in longwall chain pillars in some coalmines of the United States. Therefore, rigorous pillar protection protocols around existing gas wells should be drawn6. Driven by the strong energy demand, China has made significant progress in the coalbed methane (CBM) exploration and development in the past four decades7–9. However, a low average gas production per gas well, a relatively short stable production time span, and poor technical reproducibility are still core problems in China’s CBM development because the CBM reservoirs usually have a low gas saturation, low permeability and low reservoir pressure10,11. The goaf gas mainly accumulated in the fracture zone and the optimal position of drilling hole for gob gas drainage is in the lower part fractured zone and the upper caving zone12. Over 60% of underground coal fires are caused by the coal spontaneous combustion in gob and due to the competitive adsorption of coal on different gases, methane can affect the production and transfer of free radicals and prolong the slow oxidation in gob13. A technology of coal and CBM co-mining was proposed and used in a few key coal mining areas of Shanxi Province China. It was featured as a three-zone (planning areas, preparation areas, and production areas/mining-affected areas) coordinated stereoscopic coal and CBM co-mining14,15. After extensive practice, this model has evolved into an integrated coal and CBM extraction across the entire coal mining area, all coal seams, and the full mining lifecycle, named as a four-zone coordinated (planning areas, preparation areas, production areas, and goaf areas) 3D extraction from surface and underground16–18. The coordinated implementation of coal mining and gas extraction projects in time and space, along with their complementary advantages, has effectively increased the extraction rate of coalbed methane and the recovery rate of coal19.

Coal mining-induced disturbance significantly enhances the coal seam permeability and pressure relief, which causes massive CBM desorption and its migration into fracture zones. The gas control in the coal mining face can be achieved by pumping CBM accumulated in the roof fracture zone through surface L-shaped wells in the mining-affected area20–22. The L-shaped well integrates the advantages of various well types such as surface vertical wells, vertical wells in the mining-affected area and underground horizontal wells23. The horizontal section of a surface L-shaped well is distributed in a straight line and its drainage coverage can run through the entire working face24. Coal mining usually induces an interlayer shear slip of the overlying strata and the slip displacement is positively related to the thickness and negatively related to the hardness of the overlying rock layer25,26. The key technologies, such as well structure design, well location selection, guided drilling, horizontal section dredging, and surface safety control of L-shaped horizontal wells were summarized, and the extraction effect was analyzed27,28. Based on the distribution pattern of three-dimensional fractures of the overburden rock in the mining area, the position of the horizontal section of an L-shaped well was optimized29,30.

At present, more than 10 L-shaped horizontal wells have been constructed in the key coal mining areas in Shanxi Province, but only two L-shaped wells achieved expected extraction results and effectively prevented gas from exceeding the limit in the working face. These two wells are located at No.3313 working face of the Sihe coalmine of Jincheng City and at No.3213 working face of the Xinjing coalmine of Yangquan City Shanxi Province, respectively28,29. Due to the time and space limitations of coal mining engineering, the lifetime of a conventional L-shaped well is usually short and therefore the cost of the well is difficult to recover. Three L-shaped wells were attempted to be converted into goaf gas wells and only one well achieved good extraction effects. Therefore, it is necessary to analyze the reasons why the gas production of L-shaped wells did not meet expectations and optimize the key technologies of successive extraction of coalbed methane from mining-affected area and goaf with one L-shaped well. In the research, the location selection of the horizontal section of an L-shaped well, the wellbore structure and cementing design, and the continuous extraction in the mining-affected area-goaf area were improved and a design method of “one well for two purposes” for L-shaped wells in mining-affected areas and goaf areas was proposed. The research outcome may benefit the profitability of an L-shaped well and improve the connection between CBM extraction in mining areas and goaf areas.

CBM extraction test by L-shaped wells in mining-affected areas and goaf areas

The three test wells are located at the No. 12,408 working face of Tunlan coalmine of Taiyuan City, the No. 3213 working face of Xinjing coalmine of Yangquan City and the No. 1301 working face of Hudi Mine of Jincheng City Shanxi Province China (Fig. 1).

There are ten minable coal seams within Shanxi formation (P1sh) and Taiyuan formation (C2t) of Carboniferous-Permian strata at the Tunlan coalmine and Nos. 2, 8 and 9 coal seams are the main minable ones. The gas content of No. 2 coal seam ranges from 4.25 to 13.05 m³/t, with an average of 7.47 m³/t. This mine is classified as a high-gas outburst mine.

The No.3 coal seam is the main minable coal seam at the Xinjing coalmine. The gas content of No.3 coal seam ranges from 5.80 to 27.10m3/t, with an average of 12.69 m³/t. Gas emission rates in the mine are generally high. The relative gas emission rate ranges from 7.3 to 77.45 m³/t and is typically above 10 m³/t. The mining process is frequently accompanied by dynamic gas anomalies, such as coal and gas outbursts or ejections. This confirms the mine’s classification as a typical high-gas outburst mine.

The Hudi coalmine mainly extracts No.3 coal seam and the gas content of No.3 coal seam ranges from 23.23 to 25.8m3/t and the gas pressure is up to 3.83 MPa and it is also a typical high-gas outburst mine (Fig. 2).

In summary, only XJ-L-01 well among the three wells achieved a profitable CBM extraction. The reason for the two unsatisfactory wells was that there was no stable fluid migration channel between the horizontal section and the gas-conducting fissure zone. The vertical distance between the horizontal section and the coal seam was larger for the XJ-L-01 well than for these two wells (Table 1) and there were no stable seepage channels before the initial weighting and even in the whole mining stage. Therefore, the height of the gas-conducting fractured zone is an important parameter for determining the longitudinal position of the horizontal section of an L-shaped well. It is noted that the height of the overlying rock gas-conducting fracture zone is predicted using an empirical formula for calculating the height of water-conducting fractured zone recommended in “Specifications for Coal Pillar Retention and Pressure Mining beneath Buildings, Water Bodies, Railways, and Major Mine Shafts/Roadways (2017)”.

On the other hand, the third section of the well with open-hole completion and no cementation is prone to wellbore deformation and dislocation. It is difficult to maintain wellbore stability and achieve stable pumping during the goaf extraction stage.

Therefore, to achieve a better gas production effect and prolong the stable production time and realize the purpose of dual-use of one L-shaped well in the mining-affected areas and goaf areas, the drilling and completion of the L-shaped wells need to be improved.

Sources of CBM in mining-affected areas and goaf areas

During the mining process of a working face, the occurrence state of methane varies over time and space. Along with the stress relief or the fluid pressure decline of the coal seam, the methane molecules get desorbed from the pore surface of coal matrix, diffuse and flow through the separation fissures and penetrating fissures to the dense fissure area and the working face. After reaching a new pressure equilibrium, the concentration difference of CBM in the mining fissures areas drives the CBM transport. Therefore, in the mining-affected areas, the CBM primarily comes from: the coal wall of the working face, mined coal, abandoned coal, desorbed gas from adjacent coal seams, and the free gas from the surrounding rocks. And the amount of each source directly affects the gas emission of the mining fissures areas (Fig. 7a). The rock movement and deformation in the caving zone and fractured zone will reach a stable state within 2–3 years after coal extraction. the roof collapse zone and fissure zone (i.e. the migrated channel of fluid) of the goaf area gradually stabilized (Fig. 8), and the CBM gradually migrates to the roof fractured zone and the goaf area. And therefore, in the goaf areas, the CBM are mainly the gas desorbed from the residual coal in goaf, the coal pillar and the adjacent coal seams (Fig. 7b).

Optimizing key technologies for continuous gas extraction with improved dual-purpose L-shaped wells

Optimal design of layer location selection of horizontal section

To ensure stable and high production in the mining stage, the horizontal section of a L-shaped well should be placed in the fissure development area. Under the premise of ensuring that the wellbore of the horizontal section does not deform or break, the longitudinal position of the horizontal section should be as close as possible to the roof of the coal seam to form a stable channel for CBM transport. Within the whole gas extraction stage in the mining affected area, the XJ-L-01 well has a high gas production for approximately 60% of the time and a low gas production for the subsequent 40% of the time (Fig. 4a), which provides an important basis for the segmented design of the horizontal section. That is, 60% of the horizontal section length should be placed in the lower part of the fracture zone, and 40% of the length near the vertically straight well section in the middle of the fissure zone.

Vertically, the upper part of the fractured zone has a high gas concentration and a low permeability while the lower part has a low gas concentration and a high permeability.

Optimization of wellbore structure and cementing design

To realize continuous extraction, the wellbore structure of the L-shaped well was optimized, and the “segmented cementing technology34” or “multi-stage discontinuous cementing technology6,35” was used to improve the cementing quality and realize the dual-purpose of one well for the L-shaped well in the mining-affected areas and goaf areas (Fig. 10). Conventional L-shaped wells generally adopt a three-spud well structure. The first straight well section and second inclined section are cemented, and the third section adopts open-hole completion. The wellbore structure of the mining-goaf L-shaped well adopts a large-diameter two-spud wellbore structure, the first spud adopts a 444.5 mm drill bit, and the second spud wellbore diameter is 311.1 mm, and the drilling is completed at the target position. To prolong the service life of the horizontal section and to ensure the stability and gas production effect of the well, 244.5 mm slotted screen pipe is used in the horizontal section, and the horizontal section is not cemented or is segmented cemented. To effectively seal the aquifer and maintain the stability and air tightness of the straight well section, the straight well section and part of the inclined section is cemented. And the cementation position is located at the top of the fractured zone. After converting to a goaf well, 193.7 mm production casing is inserted and slanted section is connected to a 193.7 mm slotted screen pipe to ensure continuous negative pressure extraction as a goaf well (Fig. 10a).

Discussions on continuous extraction and production method of an improved L-shaped well

L-shaped wells can continuously extract methane from the mining area and goaf through negative pressure pumping. The negative pressure value should vary with the extraction stage. In the extraction stage in the mining-affected area, the coal seam gas pressure is high with a heavy gas outflow, a high negative pressure, it is suggested to implement a high-negative-pressure extraction using a water-ring vacuum pump, with negative pressure values ranging from -10 kPa to -90 kPa. In the goaf extraction stage, the gas pressure decreases with a relatively low outflow, it is suggested to implement a medium- and low -negative-pressure extraction using a water-ring vacuum pump, with negative pressure values ranging from 0 kPa (or tiny positive pressure) to -30 kPa (Fig. 4).

It is noted that, this “dual-purpose” approach prioritizes well reusability over primary operational effectiveness. Normally, for efficiency during the mining-affected stage, the horizontal section should be positioned within or as close as possible to the coal seam to maximize methane drainage before coal extraction begins. And to preserve wellbore integrity for goaf-stage operations, the horizontal section must be located outside the caving zone. Due to the enhanced permeability resulting from mining-induced stress relief, this L-shaped well does not require hydraulic fracturing. The dual-purpose of one L-shaped well reduces drilling and fracturing costs, mitigates the risk of coal mine gas disasters and methane emissions. Therefore, this approach offers triple benefits in terms of economy, safety, and environment.

In efficiency, (a) during pre-mining extraction, the gas rate of a conventional horizontal well with staged fracturing within a coal seam can reach up to 3000 to 40,000 m3/d and the gas recovery can reach up to 40% − 60% 4–5 years after pre-mining extraction and over 80% after 10–15 years extraction. And due to the time and space limitations of coal mining engineering, the lifetime of a conventional L-shaped well (a purpose-optimized wells) is usually short and its cost is difficult to recover. (b) The maximum gas rate of a dual-purpose L-shaped well is approximately 20,000 m3/d and the gas recovery is approximately 50% in the mining stage (Fig. 4) and the ultimate gas recovery is 80% (including the goafstage). The service lifetime of a “dual-purpose” L-shaped well is approximately six years, one year for mining stage and five for goaf stage. The cumulative gas recovery of a dual-purpose well is approximately 80%, similar to what would be achieved with purpose-optimized wells at each stage (80%). Therefore, there is no significant efficiency loss.

In economy, (a) for an optimally-positioned single-purpose well, the costs of drilling and hydraulic fracturing are CNY four million each, respectively. An additional vertical well for gob gas drainage costs CNY one million. This combined scheme has a total cost of CNY nine million and can achieve a cumulative gas recovery of up to 80%. (b) for a compromised dual-purpose well, the drilling cost is CNY four million and the cumulative gas recovery is also approximately 80%. A comparison reveals that the dual-purpose well is more cost-effective and delivers satisfactory gas extraction performance.

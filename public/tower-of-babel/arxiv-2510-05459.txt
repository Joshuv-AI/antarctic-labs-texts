Metric criteria for fixed price of countable groups
Erin Bevilacqua, Lewis Bowen

Abstract
We establish general criteria for a countable group Γ \Gamma to have fixed price 1 depending on a choice of left-invariant proper metric on Γ \Gamma . We apply this criterion to show that if Γ 1 , Γ 2 \Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2} are two countable groups satisfying a certain growth condition then Γ 1 × Γ 2 \Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2} has fixed price 1. For example, Γ × Γ \Gamma\times\Gamma has fixed price 1 for any countable group Γ \Gamma .



Keywords
:cost, price, measurable group theory

Introduction

Let
\Gamma
be a countable (discrete) group. In the field of measured group theory, there is significant interest in studying the probability measure preserving actions of
\Gamma
on standard probability spaces. An important invariant of these actions is the notion of
cost
, originally due to Levitt
[
Lev95
]
and further refined by Gaboriau
[
Gab00
]
.

Suppose a countable group
\Gamma
has an essentially free, probability measure preserving (pmp) Borel action on a standard Borel probability space
(X,{\mathcal{B}},\mu)
. If one looks at the orbits of this action, one gets a countable Borel equivalence relation (CBER)
{\mathcal{R}}\subset X\times X
on
X
:

{\mathcal{R}}=\{(x,gx):~x\in X,g\in\Gamma\}.

A
graphing

{\mathcal{G}}\subset{\mathcal{R}}
of
{\mathcal{R}}
is a Borel subset which is symmetric (so
(x,y)\in{\mathcal{G}}\Rightarrow(y,x)\in{\mathcal{G}}
) and such that the connected components of the graph with vertex set
X
and edge set
{\mathcal{G}}
are exactly the equivalence classes of
{\mathcal{R}}
. In this case, the graph
{\mathcal{G}}
is said to
generate

{\mathcal{R}}
.

The cost of
{\mathcal{G}}
is
\frac{1}{2}\int\deg_{\mathcal{G}}(x)d\mu(x)
where
\deg_{\mathcal{G}}(x)
is the number of edges
\{x,y\}
with
(x,y)\in{\mathcal{G}}
. The cost of an action is the infimum over the cost of all graphings generating the equivalence relation induced by the action. The cost of a group
\Gamma
is the infimum of the cost of all essentially free, ergodic actions of
\Gamma
on standard probability spaces.

\Gamma
is said to have
fixed price
c
if all ergodic, essentially free actions of
\Gamma
on a standard probability space
X
have cost
c
. It was conjectured by Gaboriau
[
Gab00
]
that all groups have fixed price. In his work, Gaboriau showed that several large classes of groups have fixed price, including direct products of countably infinite groups where at least one of the factor groups has an infinite subgroup with fixed price 1. However, the question has remained open for direct products of groups in which none of the factor groups has a fixed price 1 subgroup.

Abert-Weiss defined the max-cost of a group
\Gamma
to be the supremum cost of all essentially free pmp actions of
\Gamma

[
AW13
]
. They used Kechris’ theory of weak containment to show that Bernoulli actions achieve the max-cost. In this way, one can show that a group has fixed price when cost and max-cost agree.

Abert and Mellick proved that locally compact, second countable groups of the form
G\times{\mathbb{Z}}
where
G
is compactly generated have fixed price 1 in
[
AM22
]
. While the first explicit definition of calculating the cost of locally compact groups using cross sections can be found in
[
Car23
]
, the technique used in Abert-Mellick uses Poisson point processes to find the maximal cost of actions. In this work, they showed that their technique was equivalent to the original definition using cross-sections, and sparked further interest in cost of locally compact groups.

A major breakthrough in proving fixed price 1 for lcsc groups came in the paper of Fra̧czyk, Mellick, and Wilkens
[
FMW23
]
, where they used the definition of cost from Abert-Mellick, the ideal Poisson-Voronoi tessellations defined in
[
DCE+25
]
, and techniques from Lie theory to show that lattices in higher-rank Lie groups have fixed price 1.
Mellick was able to further refine these techniques to get a more general result not depending on Lie theory in
[
Mel23
]
.

This paper refines the techniques of Fra̧czyk, Mellick, and Wilkens to show that an even larger class of groups has fixed price 1. Moreover, we develop the theory from scratch: the reader need not be familiar with
[
FMW23
]
to read this paper.

A sample result is the following:

Corollary 1.1
.

If
\Gamma
is any countable group, then
\Gamma\times\Gamma
has fixed price 1.

By contrast, it was not previously known whether
\Gamma\times\Gamma
has fixed price 1 except in the special case in which
\Gamma
contains an infinite amenable subgroup.

Remark 1
.

After this paper was nearly complete, we became aware of
[
Khe25
]
which proves that general product groups have fixed price 1. It appears that from a big picture point-of-view, our method are similar. However, this paper develops more of the general theory but we do not obtain the full result for direct product groups.

We attempted to prove that
\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2}
has fixed price 1 for any pair of countable groups
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}
. But we only succeeded in the special case in which
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
have nice metrics with roughly comparable growth rates. To make this statement precise we need the next definitions.

Recall that a quasi-metric on a set
X
is a function
d:X\times X\to[0,\infty)
satisfying all of the conditions of a metric except that the triangle inequality is replaced with the quasi-triangle inequality:

d(x,z)\leq d(x,y)+d(y,z)+C_{q}

where
C_{q}\geq 0
is a constant not depending
x,y,z\in X
.

Definition 1
.

Let
d
be an integer-valued quasi-metric on a countable group
\Gamma
. We say

•

d
is
proper
if every ball of finite radius is finite;

•

d
is
left-invariant
if
d(gh,gf)=d(h,f)
for all
f,g,h\in\Gamma
;

•

d
is
\epsilon
-approximately sub-additive
if there is an
\epsilon>0
such that if

SS(\Gamma,n,\epsilon)=\{x\in\Gamma:~d(x,e)\in[n-\epsilon,n+\epsilon]\}

is a spherical shell of mean radius
n
and width
2\epsilon
then for every
n,m\geq\epsilon
,

SS(\Gamma,n,\epsilon)\cdot SS(\Gamma,m,\epsilon)\supset S(\Gamma,n+m)

where
S(\Gamma,n+m)=\{x\in\Gamma:~d(x,e)=n+m\}
is the sphere of radius
n+m
.

For example, if
d
is a word metric then it satisfies all three criteria.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
i=1,2
, let
d_{i}
be a left-invariant proper integer-valued quasi-metric on a countable group
\Gamma_{i}
and
\epsilon>0
. Assume each
(\Gamma_{i},d_{i})
is
\epsilon
-approximately sub-additive. Let
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2}
. Let
d
be the
\ell^{1}
quasi-metric on
\Gamma
:

d(x,y)=d_{1}(x_{1},y_{1})+d_{2}(x_{2},y_{2})

for
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2}
and
y=(y_{1},y_{2})\in\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2}
. Assume for
i=1,2

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#B(\Gamma_{i},n)}{\#B(\Gamma,n)}=0

(1)

where
B(\Gamma,n),B(\Gamma_{i},n)
is the ball of radius
n
in
\Gamma
,
\Gamma_{i}
respectively (centered at the identity say). Then
\Gamma
has fixed price 1.

Remark 2
.

Intuitively, if (
1
) does not hold then one of the groups has significantly faster growth rate than the other so that a horoball in the product group looks like a slightly thickened copy of a horoball in the faster-growth group. This explains why our technique does not work in the case: because our methods do not significantly differentiate between the faster growth group and
\Gamma
in this case.

Remark 3
.

In Section
10
, we prove Proposition
10.7
which, roughly speaking, states that if the growth rates of
(\Gamma_{1},d_{1})
and
(\Gamma_{2},d_{2})
are sufficiently close to each other then (
1
) holds. So if the quasi-metrics are also approximately sub-additive then
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2}
has fixed price 1.

1.1
Overview

All of the results above follow from the following general theorem (whose terminology is discussed afterwards)

Theorem 1.3
.

If
\Gamma
is a discrete group with an infinite measure preserving action which is limit-amenable, partially doubly recurrent, and has normalized cost
p
then
\Gamma
has max-cost at most
p
. In particular, if
p=1
then
\Gamma
has fixed price 1.

This result partially generalizes
[
Mel23
]
, where it is shown that if a locally compact unimodular group
G
has a unimodular closed amenable subgroup
A
such that
G
acting on
(G/A)^{2}
is conservative, then
G
has fixed price 1 (and therefore, all lattices in
G
have fixed price 1). Our result is only a partial generalization because we only work with countable groups. We have not attempted to extend our results to non-discrete locally compact groups.

To explain the similarities note that, in the setting of Mellick’s Theorem, we may assume
G
is non-amenable since otherwise it is known a folklore theorem (see
[
KPV15
, Proposition 4.3]
) that
G
has fixed price 1. So
G
acting on
G/A
is an infinite measure preserving action which is amenable and therefore limit-amenable. It is also doubly-recurrent (which is equivalent to
G{\curvearrowright}(G/A)^{2}
being infinitely-conservative) and therefore partially doubly-recurrent (the latter condition is introduced in section
7
). Because the action of
G
on
G/A
is amenable, it has normalized cost 1.

Our paper begins by developing a notion of weak containment for infinite measure preserving actions. This extends Kechris’ weak containment which is by now well-developed for pmp actions
[
BK20
]
. An imp action is limit-amenable if it is weakly contained in the class of amenable imp actions.

Next we prove that if an imp action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is limit-amenable then the Poisson point process on
X
determines an action of
\Gamma
which is weakly contained in Bernoulli. This follows from a more general theorem: the Poisson suspension functor preserves weak containment.

Abert-Weiss proved in
[
AW13
]
that if an essentially free action is weakly contained in a Bernoulli action then it realizes the max-cost. It follows that if an imp action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is limit-amenable then its Poisson suspension realizes the max-cost.

We are unaware of any definition of the cost of an imp action. So we define the normalized cost of an imp action to be the cost of the orbit-equivalence relation restricted to a complete section of measure 1. Using a result of Gaboriau, this is shown to be independent of the choice of complete section. For example, if the action is amenable then the normalized cost is 1.

Along the way, we prove that if a group
\Gamma
is exact, then all limit-amenable imp actions are amenable (see Theorem
4.12
). So in this case, the normalized cost is 1.

Next we turn our attention to the following setting. Let
\Gamma
be a countable group with a proper left-invariant quasi-metric
d
. We assume
(\Gamma,d)
is approximately sub-additive (which occurs, for example, if
d
is a word metric). The next definition is key to obtaining double recurrence:

Definition 2
.

We say
(\Gamma,d)
satisfies the
overlapping neighborhoods property
(ONP) if there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
m>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#\{(x,y)\in B(n)^{2}:~|B(r)\cap B(x,n+C)\cap B(y,n+C)|<m\}}{|B(n)|^{2}}=0.

(2)

where
B(x,n)
denotes the closed ball of radius
n
centered at
x\in\Gamma
and
B(r)=B(e,r)
where
e\in\Gamma
is the identity.

Our next general result is that if
(\Gamma,d)
has the ONP and is approximately sub-additive then there is a infinite
\Gamma
-invariant measure on the space of horofunctions on
\Gamma
which is limit-amenable and doubly-recurrent. The precise statement is Theorem
9.14
. This theorem is used to derive Theorem
1.2
and thereby Corollary
1.1
.

1.2
Section guide

Section
3
generalizes weak containment to imp actions. We start with a definition in terms of partitions and prove an equivalent characterization in terms of vague convergence of infinite invariant measures on locally compact spaces. Section
4
introduces limit-amenability via vague convergence. By results in §
3
, an action is limit-amenable if and only if it is weakly contained in the class of amenable actions. This section proves the stronger statement that limit-amenable actions are actually limits of regular actions - which are actions measurably conjugate to the action of
\Gamma
on itself by left-translations. Section
5
proves that the Poisson suspension functor preserves weak containment.

Section
6
reviews cost, then introduces graph-cost and normalized cost. The main result is that if an imp action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is partially doubly recurrent, then the cost of its Poisson suspension is bounded by the normalized cost of
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
. Section
7
proves that cost of a Poisson suspension is bounded by normalized cost, if the action is partially doubly recurrent.

Section
8
proves Theorem
1.3
. The proof is relatively short because nearly all of the work has been done in previous sections. Section
9
proves Theorem
9.14
which produces actions of
\Gamma
which are doubly-recurrent and limit-amenable when
\Gamma
satisfies the overlapping neighborhoods principle and certain other metric properties. Section
10
proves Theorem
1.2
. It then applies this theorem to prove several additional results, including Corollary
1.1
.

The appendix has three sections. Section
A
reviews Kaimanovich’s generalization of Hopf’s Decomposition Theorem. That is: we decompose
X
into
\mathsf{Con}(X)
where the action is infinitely conservative and
\mathsf{Dis}(X)
where it is not. Section
B
reviews standard notions regarding measured equivalence relations. Section
C
reviews three notions of convergence of measures: weak*, weak and vague. One highlight is the Portmanteau Theorem for vague convergence of infinite measures on locally compact spaces, which we use frequently.

Preliminaries

Throughout this paper
\Gamma
denotes a countable (discrete) group. We will typically write a measure space as
(X,\mu)
or
(Y,\nu)
leaving the sigma-algebra implicit. We always assume our measures are
\sigma
-finite and standard and maps between measure spaces are assumed to be measurable unless otherwise stated. We use the abbreviations pmp, imp, mp, lcsc to mean probability-measure-preserving, infinite-measure-preserving, measure-preserving, locally compact second countable respectively.

Definition 3
.

For
i=1,2
, let
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X_{i},\mu_{i})
be imp actions. A
factor map
is a measurable map
\phi:X^{\prime}_{1}\to X_{2}
where

•

X^{\prime}_{1}\subset X_{1}
is a
\Gamma
-equivariant co-null subset;

•

g\phi(x)=\phi(gx)
for a.e.
x\in X_{1}^{\prime}
and every
g\in\Gamma
;

•

\phi_{*}(\mu_{1}\upharpoonright X^{\prime}_{1})
is equivalent to
\mu_{2}
.

In general, we do not require
\phi_{*}(\mu_{1}\upharpoonright X^{\prime}_{1})=\mu_{2}
. We do not even require that
\phi_{*}(\mu_{1}\upharpoonright X^{\prime}_{1})
is
\sigma
-finite.

If
\mu_{1}(\phi^{-1}(A))<\infty
for every
A\subset X_{2}
with
\mu_{2}(A)<\infty
then we say
\phi
is a
finite-measure extension
.

If
\phi_{*}(\mu_{1}\upharpoonright X^{\prime}_{1})=\mu_{2}
then we say
\phi
is
measure-preserving
. If
\phi_{*}\mu_{1}
is
\sigma
-finite and is absolutely continuous to
\mu_{2}
then we say
\phi
is
quasi-measure-preserving
. Equivalently,
\phi
is a factor map and the Radon-Nikodym derivative
\frac{d\phi_{*}\mu_{1}}{d\mu_{2}}
is finite and positive on a

-conull set.

Remark 4
.

As above, suppose
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X_{i},\mu_{i})
are imp actions and
\phi:X_{1}\to X_{2}
is
\Gamma
-equivariant and measurable. If
\mu_{2}=\phi_{*}\mu_{1}
then
\phi
is automatically a finite measure extension. That is, measure-preserving factors are finite measure extensions.

3
Weak containment

The goal of this section is to define a notion of weak containment for imp actions which generalizes Kechris’ definition. We generalize some of the standard tools by showing how weak containment relates to convergence of measures.

Notation 1
.

We let
A
denote a finite non-empty set of colors and
A_{*}=A\cup\{*\}
where
*
is a special element not in
A
. If
(X,\mu)
is a measure space and
\phi:X\to A_{*}
is measurable then
\phi
is
(\mu,A)
-finite
if
\mu(\{x\in X:~\phi(x)\neq*\})<\infty
. Sometimes we will simply say that
\phi
is
A
-finite if
\mu
is understood.

Definition 4
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a class of mp actions. We say that a given mp action
\alpha=(\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu))
is
weakly contained
in
{\mathcal{C}}
(denoted
\alpha\prec{\mathcal{C}}
) if for every finite set
A
,
(\mu,A)
-finite measurable map
\phi:X\to A_{*}
, finite

and
\epsilon>0
there exist an imp action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(Y,\nu)
in
{\mathcal{C}}
, and a
(\nu,A)
-finite measurable map
\psi:Y\to A_{*}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{b\in A_{*}}\sum_{f\in F}

\displaystyle\Big|\mu\big(\{x\in X:~\phi(x)=a\textrm{ and }\phi(fx)=b\}\big)

\displaystyle-\nu\big(\{y\in Y:~\psi(y)=a\textrm{ and }\psi(fy)=b\}\big)\Big|<\epsilon.

We will often be concerned with the special case in which
{\mathcal{C}}=\{\beta\}
in which case we say
\alpha
is weakly contained in
\beta
, denoted
\alpha\prec\beta
. We say two actions

are

.

Remark 5
.

In the special case of pmp actions, we can assume
\phi(x)\neq*
for every

, in which case the above definition reduces to Kechris’ definition of weak containment
[
Kec12a
]
.

Remark 6
.

We typically assume that the identity element
e\in F
. The sum then includes the special case in which

and

which implies

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in A}

\displaystyle\Big|\mu\big(\{x\in X:~\phi(x)=a\}\big)-\nu\big(\{y\in Y:~\psi(y)=a\}\big)\Big|<\epsilon.

Definition 5
.

Let
\alpha=(\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu))
and
\beta=(\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(Y,\nu))
be measure-preserving actions on standard measure spaces. A
measure-preserving factor map

\alpha

\Gamma

The notion of weak containment of pmp actions was introduced by Kechris as a counterpart to the classical notion of weak containment of unitary representations

3.1
Multi-correlations

The definition of weak containment involves pair correlations. That is, we are concerned only with approximating measures of sets of the form
\{x\in X:~\phi(x)=a\textrm{ and }\phi(fx)=b\}
.
We show here that weak containment actually implies it is in fact possible to approximate more general correlations such as sets of the form

\{x\in X:~\phi(x)=a_{0},\phi(f_{1}x)=a_{1}\textrm{ and }\phi(f_{2}x)=a_{2}\}
for some given
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}\in A
and
f_{1},f_{2}\in F
. We will prove a precise statement to this effect in terms of shift-spaces.

Let
K
be a compact space and
K^{\Gamma}
the space of functions
x:\Gamma\to K
with the pointwise convergence topology. Let
\Gamma
act on
K^{\Gamma}
by

(gx)(f)=x(g^{-1}f)\quad\forall x\in K^{\Gamma},g,f\in\Gamma.

This is called the
shift-action
.

Now let
\alpha=\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
be a measure-preserving action (either finite or infinite). Given a measurable function
\phi:X\to K
and a subset
F\subset\Gamma
, define

\phi^{F}:X\to K^{F}

by

\phi^{F}(x)(f)=\phi(f^{-1}x).

In the special case
F=\Gamma
, the map
\phi^{\Gamma}:X\to K^{\Gamma}
is
\Gamma
-equivariant. Therefore, the push-forward measure
\phi^{\Gamma}_{*}\mu
is
\Gamma
-invariant.

Definition 6
.

Given a finite set
A
, a finite set
F\subset\Gamma
with
e\in F
and a (real-valued) measure
\nu
on
A_{*}^{F}

\|\nu\|_{F}=\sum\{|\nu(a)|:~a\in A_{*}^{F}\textrm{ and }a(e)\neq*\}.

In other words,
\|\nu\|_{F}
is the norm of the restriction of
\nu
to the subset
\{a\in A_{*}^{F}:~a(e)\neq*\}
.
Since we often work with infinte measures for which this restriction is fine, it is useful to use the psedo-norm in place of
\|\nu\|
.

Theorem 3.1
.

A given measure-preserving action
\alpha=\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is weakly contained in a class
{\mathcal{C}}
if and only if for every finite set
A
,
(\mu,A)
-finite measurable map
\phi:X\to A_{*}
, finite

and
\epsilon>0
there exist an imp action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(Y,\nu)
in
{\mathcal{C}}
, and a
(\nu,A)
-finite measurable map
\psi:Y\to A
such that

\|\phi^{F}_{*}\mu-\psi^{F}_{*}\nu\|_{F}<\epsilon.

Remark 7
.

To clarify,
\phi^{F}_{*}\mu
is the measure on

\phi^{F}_{*}\mu(a)=\mu(\{x\in X:~\phi^{F}(x)=a\}

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in B}\sum_{b\in B_{*}}\sum_{f\in F}

\displaystyle\Big|\mu\big(\{x\in X:~\phi^{F}(x)=a\textrm{ and }\phi^{F}(fx)=b\}\big)

\displaystyle-\nu\big(\{y\in Y:~\kappa(y)=a\textrm{ and }\kappa(fy)=b\}\big)\Big|<\epsilon.

Above,

\displaystyle\|\phi^{F}_{*}(\mu)-\kappa_{*}(\nu)\|_{F}<\epsilon,

(3)

where we have abused notation by identifying

Define

\psi(y)=\begin{cases}\kappa(y)(e),\quad&\kappa(y)\neq*\\
*,&\kappa(y)=*.\end{cases}

We will see that

Because

\displaystyle\|\kappa_{*}(\nu)-\psi^{F}_{*}(\nu)\|_{F}\leq\epsilon.

(4)

By (

\displaystyle\|\phi^{F}_{*}(\mu)-\psi^{F}_{*}(\nu)\|_{F}

Since
\epsilon
is arbitrary, this implies the first implication. The converse direction is trivial.
∎

3.2
A universal system

Let
2^{\Gamma\times{\mathbb{N}}}
be the space of all functions
x:\Gamma\times{\mathbb{N}}\to\{0,1\}
with the topology of pointwise convergence. This is a compact space on which
\Gamma
acts continuously by

(gx)(f,n)=x(g^{-1}f,n)

for
f,g\in\Gamma
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
x\in 2^{\Gamma\times{\mathbb{N}}}
. For
g\in\Gamma
, let
x(g)\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
be the function which sends
n
to
x(g,n)
.

Let
{\mathcal{U}}=\{x\in 2^{\Gamma\times{\mathbb{N}}}:~\forall g\in\Gamma,x(g)\neq 0^{\mathbb{N}}\}
where
0^{\mathbb{N}}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
is the all zeros sequence. This is an lcsc space (with the subspace topology). It is also
\Gamma
-invariant. Let
\mathsf{Radon}({\mathcal{U}})
be the set of Radon measures.

The action of
\Gamma
on
{\mathcal{U}}
is universal in the sense that it can model any given imp:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
be an imp. Then there exists a
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure
\nu\in\mathsf{Radon}({\mathcal{U}})
such that
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is measurably conjugate to
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}({\mathcal{U}},\nu)
.

Proof.

Because
(X,\mu)
is a standard
\sigma
-finite measure space, there exists a sequence
\{B_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of Borel subsets
B_{i}\subset X
such that each
B_{i}
has finite measure
\mu(B_{i})<\infty
and for all
x\in X
there exist indices
i,j
with
x\in B_{i}
and
x\notin B_{j}
.

Define
\kappa:X\to 2^{\mathbb{N}}
by

\kappa(x)=\{i\in{\mathbb{N}}:~x\in B_{i}\}

where we have identified
2^{\mathbb{N}}
with the set of all subsets of
{\mathbb{N}}
. Define
\kappa^{\Gamma}:X\to 2^{\Gamma\times{\mathbb{N}}}
by

\kappa^{\Gamma}(x)(g,n)=\kappa(g^{-1}x)(n).

Then
\kappa^{\Gamma}
is
\Gamma
-equivariant, Borel and injective. Let
\nu=\kappa^{\Gamma}_{*}\mu
.

Next we prove that
\nu(K)<\infty
for all compact
K\subset{\mathcal{U}}
. Given a finite
F\subset\Gamma
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and a map
\psi:F\times[m]\to\{0,1\}
, let
C(\psi)
be the corresponding cylinder set:

C(\psi)=\{x\in 2^{\Gamma\times{\mathbb{N}}}:~x(g,i)=\psi(g,i)\forall g\in F,1\leq i\leq m\}.

If there exists
(g,i)\in F\times[m]
with
\psi(g,i)=1
then
C(\psi)\subset{\mathcal{U}}
is compact. Moreover
{\mathcal{U}}
is a countable union of such compact sets. So it suffices to show
\nu(C(\psi))<\infty
for all such
\psi
. This is clear because
(\kappa^{\Gamma})^{-1}(C(\psi))\subset K_{i}
if there exists
(g,i)
with
\psi(g,i)=1
. This implies
\nu(C(\psi))\leq\mu(K_{i})<\infty
.

Because
\kappa^{\Gamma}
is Borel,
\nu
is a Borel locally finite measure on a Polishable space. So it is Radon.
∎

3.3
Shift-space formulation

Definition 7
.

Given a measure-preserving action
\alpha
, let
\mathsf{Factor}(\alpha,{\mathcal{U}})\subset\mathsf{Radon}({\mathcal{U}})
be the set of all Radon measures of the form
\phi_{*}\mu
where
\phi:X\to 2^{\mathbb{N}}
is measurable and
\phi^{\Gamma}_{*}\mu(2^{\Gamma\times{\mathbb{N}}}\setminus{\mathcal{U}})=0
. In detail,
\phi^{\Gamma}:X\to 2^{\Gamma\times{\mathbb{N}}}
is the map
\phi^{\Gamma}(x)(g)=\phi(g^{-1}x)
where we regard an element of
2^{\Gamma\times{\mathbb{N}}}
as a function from
\Gamma
to
2^{\mathbb{N}}
. Also, because we require
\phi^{\Gamma}_{*}\mu(2^{\Gamma\times{\mathbb{N}}}\setminus{\mathcal{U}})=0
, we can regard
\phi^{\Gamma}_{*}\mu
as a measure on
{\mathcal{U}}
.

More generally, if
{\mathcal{C}}
is a class of measure-preserving actions, we define

\mathsf{Factor}({\mathcal{C}},K)=\bigcup_{\alpha\in{\mathcal{C}}}\mathsf{Factor}(\alpha,K).

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\alpha=\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
be measure-preserving and let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a class of measure-preserving actions.

1.

If
\alpha
is weakly contained in
{\mathcal{C}}
then
\mathsf{Factor}(\alpha,{\mathcal{U}})\subset\overline{\mathsf{Factor}}({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{U}})
where the overline signifies closure in the vague topology.

2.

Suppose
X
is a Polish space,
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}X
is a jointly continuous action, and
\mu
is a Borel measure on
X
. If
\mu_{n}
is a sequence of
\Gamma
-invariant Borel measures on
X
and either

(a)

each
\mu_{n}
is a probability measure and
\mu_{n}\to\mu
weakly as
n\to\infty
, or

(b)

X
is lcsc and
\mu_{n}\to\mu
vaguely as
n\to\infty
,

then
\alpha
is weakly contained in
\{\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.

Remark 8
.

The Theorem above generalizes
[
AW13
, Lemma 8]
(see also
[
TD15a
, Prop. 3.6]
) which handles the case of pmp actions.

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

We first prove item (1). Making use of Lemma
3.2
, we assume without loss of generality that
\mu\in\mathsf{Radon}({\mathcal{U}})
. Suppose that
\alpha=\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is weakly contained in
{\mathcal{C}}
.

Given a measure
\mu
on
{\mathcal{U}}
, a finite set
F\subset\Gamma
containing the identity and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, define the semi-norm
\|\mu\|_{F,N}
by

\|\mu\|_{F,N}=\sum\left\{|\mu(\operatorname{Cyl}(a))|:~a\in 2^{F\times[N]}\textrm{ and }a(e)\neq 0_{N}\right\}

where

\operatorname{Cyl}(a)=\{x\in{\mathcal{U}}:~x(f,i)=a(f,i)~\forall(f,i)\in F\times[N]\}.

For
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
\phi:{\mathcal{U}}\to 2^{N}
be the map
\phi(x)=(x(e,1),\ldots,x(e,N))
. Then
{\mathcal{U}}
is the inverse limit of the spaces
2^{F\times N}
with respect to the maps
\phi^{F}
, where
(F,N)
varies over all finite subsets of
\Gamma
and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Therefore, it suffices to prove that for every
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, finite
F\subset\Gamma
and
\epsilon>0
there exist an action
\beta=\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(Y,\nu)\in{\mathcal{C}}
and a map
\tilde{\psi}:Y\to 2^{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\|\mu-\tilde{\psi}^{F}_{*}\nu\|_{F,N}<\epsilon

and
\tilde{\psi}^{\Gamma}_{*}\nu(2^{\Gamma\times{\mathbb{N}}}\setminus{\mathcal{U}})=0
.

By Theorem
3.1
, for every
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, finite
F\subset\Gamma
and
\epsilon>0
there exist an action
\beta=\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(Y,\nu)\in{\mathcal{C}}
, and a map
\psi:Y\to 2^{N}
such that

\|\mu-\psi^{F}_{*}\nu\|_{F,N}<\epsilon.

By Lemma
3.2
we assume without loss of generality that
\nu\in\mathsf{Radon}({\mathcal{U}})
and in particular,
Y={\mathcal{U}}
. Thus an element
y\in Y
is a function
y:\Gamma\times{\mathbb{N}}\to\{0,1\}
. Define
\tilde{\psi}:Y\to 2^{\mathbb{N}}
by

\tilde{\psi}(y)(i)=\begin{cases}\psi(y)(i),&1\leq i\leq N\\
y(e,i-N),\quad&i>N.\end{cases}

Then
\tilde{\psi}^{\Gamma}:Y\to{\mathcal{U}}
is
\Gamma
-equivariant and injective. Moreover,
\tilde{\psi}^{\Gamma}_{*}\nu
restricted to
2^{F\times[N]}
is the same as
\psi^{F}_{*}\nu
restricted to
2^{N}
. So

\|\mu-\tilde{\psi}^{\Gamma}_{*}\nu\|_{F,N}<\epsilon.

Because the restriction of
x\in 2^{\Gamma\times{\mathbb{N}}}
to
\Gamma\times\{N+1,N+2,\ldots\}
is the inverse of
\tilde{\psi}^{\Gamma}
, it follows that
\tilde{\psi}^{\Gamma}_{*}\nu(2^{\Gamma\times{\mathbb{N}}}\setminus{\mathcal{U}})=0
.

Because
F,N
are arbitrary, this implies
\alpha\in\overline{\mathsf{Factor}}({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{U}})
. The more general statement
\mathsf{Factor}(\alpha,{\mathcal{U}})\subset\overline{\mathsf{Factor}}({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{U}})
follows since we can replace
\alpha
with a factor of
\alpha
without changing the argument.

Next we prove item (2).

Now suppose
X
is a Polish space,
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}X
a jointly continuous action,
\mu
is a
\Gamma
-invariant Borel measure on
X
and
(\mu_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of
\Gamma
-invariant Borel measures on
X
converging to
\mu
in the weak topology. We will show
\alpha=(\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu))
is weakly contained in
\{\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
. So let
\epsilon>0
,
F\subset\Gamma
be finite and
\phi:X\to A_{*}
be
(\mu,A)
-finite (where
A
is a finite set).

Because continuity sets are dense in the measure-algebra, there exists a measurable map
\phi_{1}:X\to A_{*}
such that

1.

\mu(\{x\in X:~\phi(x)\neq\phi_{1}(x)\})<\frac{\epsilon}{(|A|+1)|F|}
;

2.

\{x\in X:~\phi_{1}(x)=a\}
is a
\mu
-continuity set for every
a\in A
;

3.

if
X
is lcsc then we also require that
\{x\in X:~\phi_{1}(x)=a\}
is pre-compact for every
a\in A
.

Because the
\Gamma
-action preserves continuity sets and continuity sets form an algebra, the last condition implies that

\{x\in X:~\phi_{1}(x)=a\textrm{ and }\phi_{1}(fx)=b\}

is a
\mu
-continuity set for every
a\in A
,
b\in A_{*}
and
f\in F
. Moreover, it is pre-compact if
X
is lcsc.

Then

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{b\in A_{*}}\sum_{f\in F}

\displaystyle\big|\mu(\{x\in X:~\phi_{1}(x)=a\textrm{ and }\phi_{1}(fx)=b\})

\displaystyle-\mu(\{x\in X:~\phi(x)=a\textrm{ and }\phi(fx)=b\})\big|<\epsilon.

We claim:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(\{x\in X:~\phi_{1}(x)=a\textrm{ and }\phi_{1}(fx)=b\})=\mu(\{x\in X:~\phi_{1}(x)=a\textrm{ and }\phi_{1}(fx)=b\})

for every
a\in A
,
b\in A_{*}
and
f\in F
. If each
\mu_{n}
is a probability measure and
\mu_{n}\to\mu
weakly then this holds by the classical Portmanteau Theorem. If
X
is locally compact and
\mu_{n}\to\mu
vaguely then this holds by the version of the Portmanteau Theorem in
[
BP06
]
applied to the 1-point compactification of
X
. This second case uses that each
\phi_{1}^{-1}(a)
is pre-compact for all
a\in A
as well as being a
\mu
-continuity set.

In particular, there exist
N
such that
n>N
implies

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{b\in A_{*}}\sum_{f\in F}

\displaystyle\big|\mu(\{x\in X:~\phi_{1}(x)=a\textrm{ and }\phi_{1}(fx)=b\})

\displaystyle-\mu_{n}(\{x\in X:~\phi_{1}(x)=a\textrm{ and }\phi_{1}(fx)=b\})\big|<\epsilon.

So the triangle inequality implies that for
n>N
,

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{b\in A_{*}}\sum_{f\in F}

\displaystyle\big|\mu(\{x\in X:~\phi(x)=a\textrm{ and }\phi(fx)=b\})

\displaystyle-\mu_{n}(\{x\in X:~\phi_{1}(x)=a\textrm{ and }\phi_{1}(fx)=b\})\big|<2\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon
is arbitrary, this shows
\alpha
is weakly contained in
{\mathcal{C}}
.
∎

4
Limit-amenability and regularity

An imp action is called limit-amenable if it is weakly contained in the class of amenable imp actions. The main goal of this section is to prove that such actions are in fact limit-regular: they are weakly contained in the class of regular actions. This is used in the next section to prove that Poisson suspensions of such actions are weakly contained in Bernoulli shifts. First we need some definitions.

Definition 8
.

An action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is
regular
if it is measurably conjugate to the left-regular action of
\Gamma
on itself with respect to a Haar measure (which is a scalar multiple of counting measure).

Definition 9
.

An imp action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is
amenable
if for a.e.
x\in X
, the stabilizer
\operatorname{Stab}_{\Gamma}(x)=\{g\in\Gamma:~gx=x\}
is amenable and the orbit equivalence relation
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Gamma}=\{(x,gx):~x\in X,g\in\Gamma\}
is hyperfinite mod
\mu
(see §
B
for definitions).

Remark 9
.

This is not the original definition introduced by Zimmer
[
Zim77
]
. However, it is equivalent. For many other equivalent definitions see
[
Zim84
,
EG93
]
.

Definition 10
.

Let
X
be an lcsc space,
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}X
be a continuous action and
\mu
be a
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure on
X
. Then we say the action
(\Gamma,X,\mu)
is
limit-amenable
(
limit-regular
) if there exists a sequence
(\mu_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measures on
X
such that

1.

\mu_{n}
converges vaguely to
\mu
as
n\to\infty
;

2.

the action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu_{n})
is a measure-preserving-factor of an amenable (regular) action for all
n
.

We also say that
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is limit-amenable (limit-regular) if it is measurably conjugate to a limit-amenable (limit-regular) action. By Theorem
3.3
, an action is limit-amenable (limit-regular) if and only if it is weakly contained in the class of amenable (regular) actions.

Remark 10

(Permanence properties)
.

It is immediate from the definition that limit-amenability and limit-regularity are preserved under measure-preserving factors. It is also preserved under taking further limits in the following sense: suppose
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}X
is a continuous action on an lcsc space,
\mu
is a
\Gamma
-invariant measure on
X
and there is a sequence
(\mu_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measures on
X
which converges vaguely to
\mu
as
n\to\infty
. If
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu_{n})
are all limit-amenable (limit-regular) then
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is also limit-amenable (limit-regular).

Theorem 4.1
.

An imp
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is limit-amenable if and only if it is limit-regular.

Remark 11
.

In Theorem
4.12
we prove: if
\Gamma
is exact then all limit-amenable actions are amenable.

To prove Theorem
4.1
, we first prove a succession of lemmas culminating in Lemma
4.4
which states that any imp action in which almost every ergodic component is essentially transitive with finite stabilizers is limit-regular. Then we prove the theorem in full by reducing it the case in which
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is an essentially free, amenable continuous action on an lcsc space and we need to find
\Gamma
-invariant measures
\mu_{n}
converging vaguely to
\mu
which satisfy the hypotheses of Lemma
4.4
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
c_{\Gamma}
denote counting measure on
\Gamma
and let
\mathsf{Leb}
denote Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]
. Then the action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(\Gamma\times[0,1],c_{\Gamma}\times\mathsf{Leb})
is limit-regular.

Proof.

First we construct a convenient topological model for
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(\Gamma\times[0,1],c_{\Gamma}\times\mathsf{Leb})
. Let
[0,1]_{*}
be the disjoint union of
[0,1]
with
\{*\}
. Consider
[0,1]_{*}^{\Gamma}
with the usual shift-action
(gx)(f)=x(g^{-1}f)
for
x\in[0,1]_{*}^{\Gamma}
and
g,f\in\Gamma
. Let
*^{\Gamma}\in[0,1]_{*}^{\Gamma}
be the function which sends every
g\in\Gamma
to
*
. Let
{\mathcal{V}}=[0,1]_{*}^{\Gamma}\setminus\{*^{\Gamma}\}
. This is a
\Gamma
-invariant lcsc space.

Define
\Phi:\Gamma\times[0,1]\to{\mathcal{V}}
by

\Phi(g,x)(h)=\begin{cases}x,&h=g\\
*,&h\neq g.\end{cases}

Then
\Phi
is
\Gamma
-equivariant and injective where
\Gamma
acts on
\Gamma\times[0,1]
by
g(h,x)=(gh,x)
. Let
\mu_{\infty}=\Phi_{*}(c_{\Gamma}\times\mathsf{Leb})
. Then
\Phi
is a measure-conjugacy from
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(\Gamma\times[0,1],c_{\Gamma}\times\mathsf{Leb})
to
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}({\mathcal{V}},\mu_{\infty})
. So it suffices to show that
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}({\mathcal{V}},\mu_{\infty})
is limit regular.

Next, we construct a sequence of
\Gamma
-invariant measures
\mu_{n}
which converge to
\mu_{\infty}
vaguely. Let
\{F_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty},\{\Gamma_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be sequences of finite subsets of
\Gamma
satisfying

1.

e\in F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\cdots\subset\Gamma
, and
\Gamma=\cup_{n}F_{n}
;

2.

F_{n}=F_{n}^{-1}
;

3.

|\Gamma_{n}|=n
and
\Gamma_{n}
is
F_{n}^{2}
-separated. This means: if
g,h\in\Gamma_{n}
are distinct then there does not exist an element
f\in F_{n}^{2}
with
gf=h
.

We arbitrarily enumerate
\Gamma_{n}
as
\Gamma_{n}=\{g_{n,1},\ldots,g_{n,n}\}
. Define
\phi_{n}\in{\mathcal{V}}
by

\phi_{n}(h)=\begin{cases}k/n&h=g_{n,k}\\
*&h\notin\Gamma_{n}.\end{cases}

Finally, define a measure
\mu_{n}
on
{\mathcal{V}}
by

\mu_{n}=(1/n)\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\delta_{g\phi_{n}}.

That is,
\mu_{n}
is the sum of the Dirac masses on the orbit of
\phi_{n}
. Therefore
\mu_{n}
is
\Gamma
-invariant and is a factor of the regular action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(\Gamma,c_{\Gamma}/n)
. So it suffices now to prove that
\mu_{n}
converges vaguely to
\mu_{\infty}
.

Let
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}
be the set of all
x\in{\mathcal{V}}
such that there exists
f\in F_{n}
with
x(f)\neq*
. Then
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}
is compact. Moreover, it is a continuity set for all measures because its boundary is empty.

The inverse image
\Phi^{-1}({\mathcal{K}}_{n})
is
F_{n}\times[0,1]
. So the restriction of
\mu_{\infty}
to
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}
is pushforward
\Phi_{*}(c_{F_{n}}\times\mathsf{Leb})
where
c_{F_{n}}
denotes counting measure on
F_{n}
.

Let
\pi_{F_{n}}:{\mathcal{K}}_{n}\to[0,1]_{*}^{F_{n}}
be the projection map. It suffices to prove that
\pi_{F_{n}*}(\mu_{m}\upharpoonright{\mathcal{K}}_{n})
converges to
\pi_{F_{n}*}(\mu_{\infty}\upharpoonright{\mathcal{K}}_{n})
as
m\to\infty
and
n
is held fixed.

Let
m\geq n
. We claim that
\pi_{F_{n}*}(\mu_{m}\upharpoonright{\mathcal{K}}_{n})=\pi_{F_{n}*}(\Phi_{*}(c_{F_{n}}\times u_{m})\upharpoonright{\mathcal{K}}_{n})
where
u_{m}
is the uniform probability measure on
\{1/m,2/m,\ldots,1\}\subset[0,1]
. Because
u_{m}
converges to Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]
as
m\to\infty
, this claim implies the lemma.

First observe that for any
g\in\Gamma
,
g\phi_{m}
has the property that there is at most one element
f\in F_{n}
with
g\phi_{m}(f)\neq*
. Indeed, suppose
f_{1},f_{2}\in F_{n}
are distinct and
g\phi_{m}(f_{i})\neq*
for
i=1,2
. Since
g\phi_{m}(f_{i})=\phi_{m}(g^{-1}f_{i})
this implies
g^{-1}f_{i}\in\Gamma_{m}
. Because
\Gamma_{m}
is
F_{m}^{2}
-separated (and therefore, is
F_{n}^{2}
-separated), this implies
f_{1}^{-1}f_{2}\notin F_{n}^{2}
. Therefore, it is not possible for both
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
to be in
F_{n}
.

Next, fix
f\in F_{n}
and consider the set
{\mathcal{L}}_{f}
consisting of all
x\in{\mathcal{V}}
with
x(f)\neq*
. Then
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}=\cup_{f\in F_{n}}{\mathcal{L}}_{f}
. The previous paragraph show that if
f_{1}\neq f_{2}
and
m\geq n
then
\mu_{m}({\mathcal{L}}_{f_{1}}\cap{\mathcal{L}}_{f_{2}})=0
. So it suffices to show that if we restrict
\mu_{m}
to
{\mathcal{L}}_{f}
and then project to
[0,1]
via the map which sends
x\in{\mathcal{L}}_{f}
to
x(f)
, the resulting measure is
u_{m}
.
Note that
g\phi_{m}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{f}
if and only if
g\phi_{m}(f)\neq*
which occurs if and only if
g^{-1}f\in\Gamma_{m}
, if and only if
g\in f\Gamma_{m}^{-1}=f\{g_{m,1}^{-1},\ldots,g_{m,m}^{-1}\}
. The measure
\mu_{m}
restricted to
{\mathcal{L}}_{f}
and projected to

is
1/m
times counting measure on
\{1/m,2/m,\ldots,1\}
. This is
u_{m}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
c_{\Gamma}
denote counting measure on
\Gamma
and let
(Z,\zeta)
be a standard
\sigma
-finite measure space on which
\Gamma
acts trivially. Then the diagonal action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(\Gamma\times Z,c_{\Gamma}\times\zeta)
is limit-regular.

Proof.

The limit-regular property is invariant under scalars in the sense that if
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is limit regular and
0<t<\infty
then
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,t\mu)
is also limit regular. This is because if
\mu_{n}
converges vaguely to
\mu
then
t\mu_{n}
converges vaguely to
t\mu
. So Lemma
4.2
implies that
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(\Gamma\times[0,n],c_{\Gamma}\times\mathsf{Leb}_{[0,n]})
is limit regular for every
n>0
.

Because the measures
c_{\Gamma}\times\mathsf{Leb}_{[0,n]}
converge vaguely to
c_{\Gamma}\times\mathsf{Leb}_{[0,\infty)}
, it follows that
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(\Gamma\times[0,\infty),c_{\Gamma}\times\mathsf{Leb}_{[0,\infty)})
is limit-regular.

Now let
(Z,\zeta)
be an arbitrary standard
\sigma
-finite measure space. Then there exists
n\in[0,\infty]
and a Borel map
\phi:[0,n)\to Z
such that
\zeta=\phi_{*}\mathsf{Leb}_{[0,n)}
. Let
\Phi:\Gamma\times[0,n]\to\Gamma\times Z
be the factor map
\Phi(g,x)=(g,\phi(x))
. Then

\Phi_{*}(c_{\Gamma}\times\mathsf{Leb}_{[0,n)})=c_{\Gamma}\times\zeta.

Because limit-regularity is closed under measure-preserving factors, this implies the lemma.
∎

Definition 11
.

An action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is

4.1
Limit amenability and ergodic decomposition

The main result of this subsection is:

Theorem 4.5
.

An imp action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is limit-amenable if and only if a.e. ergodic component of the action is limit-amenable.

We begin by proving the backwards direction.

Lemma 4.6
.

If a.e. ergodic component of an imp action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is limit-amenable then the action is limit-amenable.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
and without loss of generality we assume
X
is locally compact. Suppose that a.e. ergodic component is limit-amenable.
By the Ergodic Decomposition theorem, there exists a Borel probability measure
\mu
on
\mathsf{Radon}(\Gamma,X,X_{0})
such that
\zeta
-a.e.
\nu
is ergodic and
\mu=\int\nu~d\zeta(\nu)
.

Since
\zeta
is a Radon-measure and
X
is Polish, we can approximate
\zeta
via a sequence
\zeta_{n}
of measures on
Z
such that each
\zeta_{n}
has finite support and
\zeta_{n}\to\zeta
vaguely. Since the barycenter map is continuous in the vague topology, setting
\mu_{n}=\int_{Z}\nu~d\zeta_{n}(\nu)
gives us a sequence of Radon measures such that
\mu_{n}\to\mu
vaguely. Additionally, each
\mu_{n}
has finitely many ergodic components. Since limit-amenability is preserved under taking further limits, it is sufficient to show that each
\mu_{n}
is limit-amenable.

Since
\mu_{n}
is supported on finitely many ergodic components, we can write
\mu_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}c_{n,i}\nu_{n,i}
where
N_{n}
is some finite number depending on

. Each
\nu_{n,i}
is limit-amenable (and hence limit-regular) by assumption. Thus there exists a sequence
\big(\nu^{(m)}_{n,i}\big)_{m=1}^{\infty}
of measures converging to
\nu_{n,i}
which are each factors of regular actions. Thus the finite sum
\mu_{n}^{(m)}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}c_{n,i}\nu^{(m)}_{n,i}
is a factor of an regular action, and
\mu_{n}^{(m)}\to\mu_{n}
vaguely.
Hence each
\mu_{n}
is limit-amenable, as desired.

4.2
Direct products

Here we show that products of limit-amenable action with pmp actions are limit-amenable.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
be a limit-amenable imp action and
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(Y,\nu)
be a pmp action. Then the direct product
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X\times Y,\mu\times\nu)
is limit-amenable.

Remark 12
.

Arbitrary extensions of amenable actions are amenable, but this remains open for limit-amenable actions.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
X
is an lcsc space,
Y
is a compact metrizable space and the actions on
X
and
Y
are by homeomorphisms. Moreover, because
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is limit-amenable (and thus limit-regular by Theorem
4.1
), there exist coefficients
c_{n}>0
, elements
x_{n}\in X
(
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
) such that if

\mu_{n}=c_{n}\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\delta_{gx_{n}}

then
\mu_{n}
converges vaguely to
\mu
as
n\to\infty
. Therefore
\mu_{n}\times\nu
converges vaguely to
\mu\times\nu
as
n\to\infty
. So it suffices to show that
\mu_{n}\times\nu
is limit-amenable. Each ergodic component of
\mu_{n}\times\nu
has the form

c_{n}\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\delta_{gx_{n}}\times\delta_{gy}

for some
g\in\Gamma
. In fact the ergodic decomposition of
\mu_{n}\times\nu
is

\mu_{n}\times\nu=\int c_{n}\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\delta_{gx_{n}}\times\delta_{gy}~d\nu(y).

So its ergodic components are regular. By Theorem
4.5
this implies
\mu_{n}\times\nu
is limit-amenable.
∎

4.3
Finite measure-preserving actions

Lemma 4.11
.

If
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is a limit-amenable measure-preserving action and
\mu(X)<\infty
then
\Gamma
is amenable.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.1
, we may assume
X
is an lcsc space and there exist elements
x_{n}\in X
and constants
c_{n}>0
such that if

\mu_{n}=c_{n}\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\delta_{gx_{n}}

then
\mu_{n}
converges to
\mu
vaguely.

Let
\epsilon>0
. Let
K
be a pre-compact
\mu
-continuity set with
\mu(K)>\mu(X)-\epsilon
. Let
F_{n}=\mathtt{Ret}(K,x_{n})=\{g\in\Gamma:~gx_{n}\in O\}
be the return time set. We will show that
\{F_{n}\}
is almost a Følner sequence for
\Gamma
.

By definition of
\mu_{n}
, for any subset
Y\subset X
,

\displaystyle\mu_{n}(Y)

\displaystyle=c_{n}|\mathtt{Ret}(Y,x_{n})|.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{|F_{n}\cap hF_{n}|}{|F_{n}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\mathtt{Ret}(K,x_{n})\cap h\mathtt{Ret}(K,x_{n})|}{|\mathtt{Ret}(K,x_{n})|}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\mathtt{Ret}(K,x_{n})\cap\mathtt{Ret}(h^{-1}K,x_{n})|}{|\mathtt{Ret}(K,x_{n}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\mathtt{Ret}(K\cap h^{-1}K,x_{n})|}{|\mathtt{Ret}(K,x_{n}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{n}(K\cap h^{-1}K)}{\mu_{n}(K)}

where the third-to-last equality holds because
h\mathtt{Ret}(K,x_{n})=\mathtt{Ret}(h^{-1}K,x_{n})
.

Because
K
is a continuity set, it follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|F_{n}\cap hF_{n}|}{|F_{n}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu(K\cap h^{-1}K)}{\mu(K)}\geq 1-2\epsilon.

Because
\epsilon
is arbitrary, it follows that for all finite subsets
H\subset\Gamma
there exists a finite set
F\subset\Gamma
such that
\frac{|F\cap hF|}{|F|}>1-\epsilon
. This implies
\Gamma
is amenable.
∎

4.4
Exactness

Exactness of groups is surveyed in
[
AD07
]
.

Theorem 4.12
.

If
\Gamma
is a countable exact group then every limit-amenable action is amenable.

Remark 13
.

One can use the main result of
[
JSMPW25
]
to prove that if
\Gamma
is non-exact then there exists a limit-amenable action which is not amenable. We will not need this fact so we leave it to the interested reader.

First we will reduce the problem to one on the space
2^{\Gamma}_{*}
of non-empty subsets of
\Gamma
(using a return-times trick). Then we prove the result in the special case that
\Gamma
is finitely generated before handling the general case.

Let
2^{\Gamma}
be the set of all subsets of
\Gamma
with the product topology. This is a compact metrizable space. Let
2^{\Gamma}_{*}\subset 2^{\Gamma}
be the set of all non-empty subsets. This is a locally compact second countable space on which
\Gamma
acts by left-multiplication. Let
{\operatorname{Meas}}_{e}(2^{\Gamma}_{*})
be the space of all
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measures
\nu
on

with the property that
\nu(\{D\subset\Gamma:~e\in D\})<\infty
.

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
be an imp action. Given a complete measurable section
Y\subset X
with finite positive measure, define the inverse return time map
\Phi_{Y}:X\to 2^{\Gamma}_{*}
by

\Phi_{Y}(x)=\{g\in\Gamma:~g^{-1}x\in Y\}.

Then
\Phi_{Y}
is
\Gamma
-equivariant and
\Phi_{Y*}\mu\in{\operatorname{Meas}}_{e}(2^{\Gamma}_{*})
. If
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is limit amenable then there exists a sequence
(F_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of finite subsets
F_{n}\subset\Gamma
and scalars
t_{n}>0
such that if
\zeta_{n}=t_{n}\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\delta_{gF_{n}}\in{\operatorname{Meas}}_{e}(2^{\Gamma}_{*})
, then
\zeta_{n}
converges to
\mu
in the vague topology as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.1
,
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is limit-regular. By Theorem
3.3
, the action is weakly contained in the class of regular actions.

Let
Y\subset X
be a complete measurable section with finite positive measure. Let
\phi:X\to\{*,1\}
be the characteristic function of
Y
. So
\phi(x)=1
if and only if
x\in Y
. This means that
\mu
is
(\mu,A)
-finite where
A=\{1\}
is a singleton.

By
3.1
, for every finite
F\subset\Gamma
and
\epsilon>0
there exist a regular action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(Y,\nu)
and a
(\nu,A)
-finite measurable map
\psi:Y\to A
such that

\|\phi_{*}^{F}\mu-\psi^{F}_{*}\nu\|_{F}<\epsilon.

Because the action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(Y,\nu)
is regular, there exists a scalar
t>0
such that
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(Y,\nu)
is measurably conjugate to the left-regular action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}\Gamma
with the measure
t\cdot c_{\Gamma}
where
c_{\Gamma}
is counting measure on
\Gamma
. If we let
\psi(e)=x\in A_{*}^{\Gamma}
, we see that

\psi^{\Gamma}_{*}\nu=t\cdot\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\delta_{gx}.

Because
\psi
is
(\nu,A)
-finite,

\nu(\{y:~\phi(y)=1\})<\infty.

But
\nu(\{y:~\phi(y)=1\})
equals
t
times

\#\{g\in\Gamma:~x(g)=1\}.

So the latter set is finite.

Because
\epsilon
and
F
in the paragraph above are arbitrary, there exist a sequence
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of elements of
A_{*}^{\Gamma}
and a sequence
(t_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of scalars such that if

\zeta_{n}=t_{n}\cdot\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\delta_{gx_{n}}

then
\zeta_{n}
converges to
\phi^{\Gamma}_{*}\mu
in the vague topology as
n\to\infty
. Moreover, each

satisfies

\#\{g\in\Gamma:~x_{n}(g)=1\}<\infty.

Let
2^{\Gamma}_{*}
be the set of all non-empty subsets of
\Gamma
with the usual
\Gamma
-action by left-multiplication. Then
A_{*}^{\Gamma}
and
2^{\Gamma}
are topologically conjugate by the map
\Psi:A_{*}^{\Gamma}\to 2^{\Gamma}
which sends

to

. So the result holds by applying
\Psi
.
∎

Proposition 4.14
.

If
\Gamma
is a finitely generated exact group. Let
(F_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of finite subsets
F_{n}\subset\Gamma
and
t_{n}>0
be scalars. Let

\zeta_{n}=t_{n}\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\delta_{gF_{n}}\in{\operatorname{Meas}}_{e}(2^{\Gamma}_{*}).

Suppose the sequence
(\zeta_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges in the vague topology to a Radon measure
\mu
as
n\to\infty
. Then the action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(2^{\Gamma}_{*},\mu)
is amenable.

Proof.

Let
S\subset\Gamma
be a finite symmetric generating set and
\operatorname{Cay}(\Gamma,S)
denote the corresponding Cayley graph. We will say that a subset
D\subset\Gamma
is connected if the subgraph it induces in
\operatorname{Cay}(\Gamma,S)
is connected. Connected components of
D
are defined similarly.

Let
2_{0}^{\Gamma}\subset 2^{\Gamma}_{*}
be the set of subsets that contain the identity. More generally, let
2^{\Gamma}_{n}\subset 2^{\Gamma}_{*}
be the set of subsets which have nontrivial intersection with the ball of radius
n
centered at the identity in the Cayley graph
\operatorname{Cay}(\Gamma,S)
.

The canonical sub-graphing
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
is the set of all pairs
(D,gD)\in 2^{\Gamma}_{n}\times 2^{\Gamma}_{n}
such that the identity is contained in the radius-
n
neighborhood of

and in the radius-
n
neighborhood of
gD
(where

). This is most intuitive in the special case in which

in which case
(D,gD)\in{\mathcal{G}}_{0}
if and only if
g^{-1}\in D
.

It is well-known that if
\Gamma
is exact then every Cayley graph of
\Gamma
(with respect to a finite generating set) has Yu’s Property A
[
AD07
,
Yu00
]
. This result is attributed to Ozawa
[
Oza00
]
(although that paper does not explicitly mention Property A, it is well-known Property A is equivalent to the other properties mentioned in that paper- see
[
AD07
, Proposition 3.13]
).

By Theorem 1 of
[
ET23
]
,
\operatorname{Cay}(\Gamma,S)
is locally hyperfinite which means for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
k>0
satisfying the following condition: for any finite subset
L\subset\Gamma
there exists a subset
L^{\prime}\subset L
,
|L^{\prime}|<\epsilon|L|
such that if one deletes
L^{\prime}
and all adjacent edges in

then the sizes of the remaining components are at most

.

It follows that the connected components of the radius
n
neighborhoods of
F_{k}
form a hyperfinite family in the language of
[
Ele12
]
. By Theorem 1 of
[
Ele12
]
, implies that
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
is hyperfinite mod
\mu
. Elek’s Theorem may be thought of as a generalization of Schramm’s earlier result
[
Sch08
]
which is formulated in terms of unimodular random rooted graphs.

There is a minor technical issue with the possibility of nontrivial stabilizers. In order to handle this, let
\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}=\Gamma\times 2_{*}^{\Gamma}
be the groupoid of the action where multiplication is defined by

(h,gD)(g,D)=(hg,D)

for any
g,h\in\Gamma
and
D\subset\Gamma
. Let
\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}_{n}}
be the sub-groupoid consisting of pairs
(g,D)
such that
(D,gD)\in{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
. Then Elek’s Theorem
[
Ele12
]
and Schramm’s Theorem from
[
Sch08
]
imply that
\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}_{n}
is amenable (with respect to the restriction of

to

). Since
\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
is the increasing union of
\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}_{n}

\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
is amenable (with respect to

), i.e. the action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(2^{\Gamma}_{*},\mu)

∎

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
be a limit-regular action and let

be a subgroup. Then the action

is also limit-regular.

By taking limits, it suffices to consider the special case in which the action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is regular. That is, we may assume

,

is counting measure and the action is by left-translations. So the restricted action of

on
\Gamma
consists of
|\Gamma/H|
copies of the left-regular action of

on itself. The lemma now follows from Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.12
.

Propositions
4.13
and
4.14
imply Theorem

in the special case in which

is amenable then the action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is amenable.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable exact group and let

Because closed subgroups of exact groups are exact

5
Poisson suspensions and limit-amenability

The main result of this section is that the Poisson suspension functor preserves weak containment. We begin by defining the Poisson suspension functor. For this, let
X
be a locally compact second countable space.

Definition 15
.

A measure
\Pi
on
X
is called a
point measure
if it can be expressed as a sum of Dirac masses
\Pi=\sum_{x\in S}c(x)\delta_{x}
for some locally finite countable subset
S\subset X
and non-negative integers
c(x)
(
x\in S
). The local finiteness condition means that for any compact set
K\subset X
,
K\cap S
is finite. Therefore
\Pi(K)<\infty
. The support of
\Pi
is
\mathsf{Support}(\Pi)=\{x\in X:~\Pi(x)>0\}
.

Definition 16
.

The set of all point measures on
X
is denoted
{\mathbb{M}}(X)
and is called the
configuration space of
X
. We consider
{\mathbb{M}}(X)
as embedded in the space of Radon measures on
X
which may be identified with a subset of the Banach dual
C_{0}(X)^{\ast}
via the Riesz Representation Theorem. We will consider
C_{0}(X)^{\ast}
with the weak
∗
topology. Then
{\mathbb{M}}(X)
is a closed subset of
C_{0}(X)^{\ast}
and therefore
{\mathbb{M}}(X)
is Polish in the sense that there exists a complete separable metric inducing its topology.

Definition 17
.

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure on
X
. A
Poisson point process on
X
with intensity measure
\mu
is a random variable
\Pi
taking values in
{\mathbb{M}}(X)
satisfying

1.

for any measurable
E\subset X
with
\mu(E)<\infty
,
{\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}}(E)
is a Poisson random variable with mean
\mu(E)
;

2.

if
E_{1},E_{2},\ldots
are pairwise disjoint measurable subsets of
X
then the restrictions of
\Pi
to
E_{1},E_{2},\ldots
are jointly independent random variables.

All such processes have the same law, which we denote by
\operatorname{Pois}(\mu)\in\operatorname{Prob}({\mathbb{M}}(X))
.

Definition 18
.

If
\alpha=(\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
) is an imp action then the induced action
\operatorname{Pois}(\alpha)=(\Gamma{\curvearrowright}({\mathbb{M}}(X),\operatorname{Pois}(\mu)))
is a pmp action. It is called the
Poisson suspension
of the action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
. If
{\mathcal{C}}
is a class of imp actions, then
\operatorname{Pois}({\mathcal{C}})=\bigcup_{\alpha\in{\mathcal{C}}}\operatorname{Pois}(\alpha)
.
See
[
Roy09
]
for an introduction to Poisson suspensions.

The main result of this section is:

Theorem 5.1
.

If an imp action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is weakly contained in a class
{\mathcal{C}}
of actions then its Poisson suspension
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}({\mathbb{M}}(X),\operatorname{Pois}(\mu))
is weakly contained in
\operatorname{Pois}({\mathcal{C}})
.

To prove this, we will combine the next result with Theorem
3.3
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
X
be an lcsc space.
Let
\mathsf{Radon}(X)
be the space of
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measures on
X
with the vague topology. Let

\operatorname{Pois}:\mathsf{Radon}(X)\to\operatorname{Prob}({\mathbb{M}}(X))

be the Poisson suspension functor. Then
\operatorname{Pois}
is continuous with respect to the vague topology on
\mathsf{Radon}(X)
and the weak topology on
\operatorname{Prob}({\mathbb{M}}(X))
.

This theorem will be proven after a few lemmas. For this, we fix the following hypotheses. For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
, let
\mu_{n}\in\mathsf{Radon}(X)
and suppose
\mu_{n}\to\mu_{\infty}
in the vague topology as
n\to\infty
. For
\Lambda\subset X
and
I\subset{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
let

{\mathbb{M}}(X,\Lambda,I)=\{\Pi\in{\mathbb{M}}(X):~\Pi(\Lambda)\in I\}.

In the special case in which
I=\{t\}
, we write
{\mathbb{M}}(X,\Lambda,t)={\mathbb{M}}(X,\Lambda,I)
for simplicity. For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
, let
\operatorname{Pois}_{n}=\operatorname{Pois}(\mu_{n})
.

Recall that a measurable subset
\Lambda\subset X
is a
continuity set
for
\mu_{\infty}
if
\mu_{\infty}(\partial\Lambda)=0
where
\partial\Lambda=\overline{\Lambda}\cap\overline{(X\setminus\Lambda)}
is the topological boundary of
\Lambda
. It is well-known the collection of continuity sets is closed under complementation, finite unions and intersections. Moreover, if
K\subset X
is any compact set and
\epsilon>0
then there exist a continuity sets
K^{\prime},K^{\prime\prime}
with
K^{\prime}\subset K\subset K^{\prime\prime}
and
\mu_{\infty}(K^{\prime\prime}\setminus K^{\prime})<\epsilon
. Indeed, we can take
K^{\prime\prime}
to be a radius
\delta
neighborhood of
K
for some sufficiently small
\delta
with respect to a continuous proper metric and similarly let
K^{\prime}
be the complement of a radius
\delta
neighborhood
X\setminus K
for some sufficiently small
\delta
. This is because there are uncountably many
\delta
to choose from but the set of all
\delta
such that the radius
\delta
neighborhood is not a continuity set is at most countable.

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose that
\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{k}\subset X
are pairwise disjoint pre-compact continuity sets for
\mu_{\infty}
and
t_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
for
1\leq i\leq k
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{Pois}_{n}(\cap_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{M}}(X,\Lambda_{i},t_{i}))=\operatorname{Pois}_{\infty}(\cap_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{M}}(X,\Lambda_{i},t_{i})).

Proof.

In fact this is straightforward because

\operatorname{Pois}_{n}(\cap_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{M}}(X,\Lambda_{i},t_{i}))=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\exp(-\mu_{n}(\Lambda_{i}))\frac{\mu_{n}(\Lambda_{i})^{t_{i}}}{t_{i}!}

is a continuous function of
(\mu_{n}(\Lambda_{i}))_{i=1}^{k}
and
\lim_{n}\mu_{n}(\Lambda_{i})=\mu_{\infty}(\Lambda_{i})
for all
i
by the Locally Compact Portmanteau Theorem
C.1
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

The sequence
\{\operatorname{Pois}(\mu_{n})\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is tight. In particular, there exists a subsequential limit which is a Borel probability measure.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that for every
\epsilon>0
there exists a compact set
K\subset{\mathbb{M}}(X)
such that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{Pois}(\mu_{n})(K)\geq 1-\epsilon.

Fix a basepoint
x_{0}\in X
. Let
d
be a continuous metric on
X
so that closed balls of finite radius in
d
are compact. Let
0<r_{1}<r_{2}<\cdots
be an increasing sequence of radii with
\lim_{i}r_{i}=\infty
such that for each
i
, the open ball
B(r_{i},x_{0})
of radius
r_{i}
centered at
x_{0}
is a continuity set for
\mu_{\infty}
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(B(r_{i},x_{0}))=\mu_{\infty}(B(r_{i},x_{0}))

for all
i
by the Portmanteau Theorem
C.1
. It follows that if

V_{i}=\sup_{1\leq n<\infty}\mu_{n}(B(r_{i},x_{0}))

then
V_{i}
is finite for all
i
. Therefore, there exist numbers
N_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\operatorname{Pois}_{n}(\{\Pi:~\Pi(B(r_{i},x_{0}))\leq N_{i}\})\geq 1-\epsilon/2^{n}

for all finite
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and all
i
.

Let

K=\{\Pi:~\Pi(B(r_{i},x_{0}))\leq N_{i}~\forall i\geq 1\}.

Then
K
is compact because
B(r_{i},x_{0})
is compact and
\mu_{n}(K)\geq 1-\epsilon
for all
n
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

It suffices to prove
\operatorname{Pois}_{n}\to\operatorname{Pois}_{\infty}
in the weak topology as
n\to\infty
. By Lemma
5.4
and Prokhorov’s Theorem, there exists a subsequence
(n_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
such that
\operatorname{Pois}_{n_{i}}
converges weakly to a Borel probability measure
\kappa
on
{\mathbb{M}}(X)
. By Lemma
5.3
,

\displaystyle\kappa\left(\cap_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{M}}(X,\Lambda_{i},t_{i})\right)=\operatorname{Pois}_{\infty}\left(\cap_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{M}}(X,\Lambda_{i},t_{i})\right)

(6)

for any pairwise disjoint measurable sets
\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{k}
and any
t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
as long as the
\Lambda_{i}
are pre-compact
\mu_{\infty}
-continuity sets.

Let
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\ldots,\Lambda_{k}\subset X
be a sequence of pairwise disjoint pre-compact Borel sets. It suffices to show

\displaystyle\kappa\left(\cap_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{M}}(X,\Lambda_{i},t_{i})\right)=\operatorname{Pois}_{\infty}\left(\cap_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{M}}(X,\Lambda_{i},t_{i})\right).

(7)

This is because, by an application of the Caratheodory-Hahn Extension Theorem, if this holds for all
k
then it holds for
k=\infty
. Moreover, because
X
is lcsc, it is
\sigma
-compact; so the assumption that the sets
\Lambda_{i}
are pre-compact does not cause difficulties.

Let
I_{\kappa}
be the intensity measure of
\kappa
. This is the measure on
X
defined by

I_{\kappa}(\Lambda)=\int\Pi(\Lambda)\,\textrm{d}\kappa(\Pi)

for
\Lambda\subset X
. We will prove that
I_{\kappa}=\mu_{\infty}
.

Note
I_{\kappa}(\Lambda)=\mu_{\infty}(\Lambda)
whenever
\Lambda
is a measurable pre-compact
\mu_{\infty}
-continuity set by (
6
).

Let
\Lambda\subset X
be any compact set and
\epsilon>0
. Then there exists a pre-compact measurable
\mu_{\infty}
-continuity set
\Lambda^{\prime}
such that
\Lambda\subset\Lambda^{\prime}
and
\mu_{\infty}(\Lambda^{\prime}\setminus\Lambda)<\epsilon
. Then

I_{\kappa}(\Lambda)\leq I_{\kappa}(\Lambda^{\prime})=\mu_{\infty}(\Lambda^{\prime})\leq\mu_{\infty}(\Lambda)+\epsilon.

Since this is true for all
\epsilon
and all compact sets, we have
I_{\kappa}\leq\mu_{\infty}
.

On the other hand if
\Lambda\subset X
is any pre-compact open set then there exists a pre-compact measurable
\mu_{\infty}
-continuity set
\Lambda^{\prime}
with
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda
and
\mu_{\infty}(\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime})<\epsilon
.
Therefore

I_{\kappa}(\Lambda)\geq I_{\kappa}(\Lambda^{\prime})=\mu_{\infty}(\Lambda^{\prime})\geq\mu_{\infty}(\Lambda)-\epsilon.

Since this is true for all
\epsilon
and all pre-compact open sets and since
X
is locally compact, we have
I_{\kappa}\geq\mu_{\infty}
. Therefore
I_{\kappa}=\mu_{\infty}
.

Let
\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{k}
be a sequence of pairwise disjoint pre-compact Borel sets. For each
i
, there exist continuity sets
K_{1,j}\subset K_{2,j}\subset\cdots\subset\Lambda_{i}
with
\mu_{\infty}(\Lambda_{i}\setminus\cup_{j}K_{i,j})=0
. Since
I_{\kappa}=\mu_{\infty}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\kappa(\cap_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{M}}(X,\Lambda_{i},t_{i}))

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\kappa(\cap_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{M}}(X,K_{i,n},t_{i}))

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{Pois}_{\infty}(\cap_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{M}}(X,K_{i,n},t_{i}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Pois}_{\infty}(\cap_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{M}}(X,\Lambda_{i},t_{i})).

This proves (
7
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a class of imp actions and suppose

is weakly contained in

. By Lemma

Weakly contained in Bernoulli

Definition 19

Cost

In this section we discuss cost, starting with a review of the standard theory in §

In §

We then introduce an equivalent notion to cost in §

6.1
Cost of discrete groups

We will freely use standard terminology from the theory of measured equivalence relations, as reviewed in §

Definition 21
.

Fix a discrete pmp equivalence relation

Definition 23

The

6.2
Normalized cost

Definition 25
.

Let

We will use the following theorem of Gaboriau, which also appears as
[

, Theorem 21.1]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

[
Gab00
]

Let
(X,\mu,{\mathcal{R}})
be a countable finite-measure-preserving Borel equivalence relation on

,

a Borel complete section mod
\mu
.
Then

{\operatorname{Cost}}_{\mu}({\mathcal{R}})={\operatorname{Cost}}_{\mu|S}({\mathcal{R}}|S)+\mu(X\setminus S).

Here

to

.

Let

be a countable measure-preserving Borel equivalence relation on

.
Then

By Theorem

of Gaboriau, we have

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Cost}}_{\mu|S\cup T}({\mathcal{R}}|{S\cup T})

\displaystyle={\operatorname{Cost}}_{\mu|S}({\mathcal{R}}|S)+\mu((S\cup T)\setminus S)

\displaystyle={\operatorname{Cost}}_{\mu|T}({\mathcal{R}}|T)+\mu((S\cup T)\setminus T).

Thus we have

.

6.3
Graph-cost

Let
\Gamma
be a countable group. In order to bound the cost of the Poisson suspension of an action of
\Gamma
, we give an alternative definition of the cost of an essentially free probability measure preserving
\Gamma
-action. We will show that this definition agrees with
\mu
-cost.

Let
2^{\Gamma\times\Gamma}
be the set of all subsets of
\Gamma\times\Gamma
. Endowed with the product topology,
2^{\Gamma\times\Gamma}
is a compact metrizable space on which
\Gamma
acts continuously by

h\cdot G=\{(hg_{1},hg_{2}):~(g_{1},g_{2})\in G\}

for
h\in\Gamma
,
G\in 2^{\Gamma\times\Gamma}
.
Let
\mathsf{Graph}(\Gamma)\subseteq 2^{\Gamma\times\Gamma}
be the set of all symmetric subsets
G
of
\Gamma\times\Gamma
, i.e. those such that
(g_{1},g_{2})\in G
if and only if
(g_{2},g_{1})\in G
.

The
support

\mathsf{supp}(G)
of
G\in\mathsf{Graph}(\Gamma)
is the set of all
g\in\Gamma
such that there exists some
g^{\prime}\in\Gamma
with
(g,g^{\prime})\in G
. If
G\in\mathsf{Graph}(\Gamma)
, then
G
can be thought of as an undirected graph with vertex set
V(G)=\mathsf{supp}(G)\subseteq\Gamma
and edge set
E(G)=G\subseteq\Gamma\times\Gamma
.
We say
G
is
fully supported
if its support is all of
\Gamma
.

An element
G\in\mathsf{Graph}(\Gamma)
is
connected
if the graph
G=(V(G),E(G))
it generates is connected.
In other words,
G
is connected if for all
g,h\in\mathsf{supp}(G)
there exist some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
g=g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{n}=h\in\mathsf{supp}(G)
such that

(g_{i},g_{i+1})\in G
for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
.

The degree of
G\in\mathsf{Graph}(\Gamma)
at the identity is

\deg_{e}(G)=\#\{g\in\Gamma\setminus\{e\}:~(e,g)\in G\}.

Suppose
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is a pmp action and
\pi:X\to\mathsf{Graph}(\Gamma)
is a
\Gamma
-equivariant map such that
\pi_{*}\mu
-almost every
G
is connected and fully supported. Then we say that
\pi
is a
\Gamma
-graph
, and we say that the
graph-cost of
\pi
is

\operatorname{grcost}_{\mu}(\pi)=\frac{1}{2}\int\deg_{e}(G)~\textrm{d}\pi_{*}\mu(G)=\frac{1}{2}\int\deg_{e}(\pi(x))~\textrm{d}\mu(x).

The
graph-cost of a pmp action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is

\operatorname{grcost}(\Gamma,X,\mu)=\inf\{\operatorname{grcost}_{\mu}(\pi):~\pi\text{ is a $\Gamma$-graph}\}

where the infimum is taken over all
\Gamma
-equivariant maps
\pi:X\to\mathsf{Graph}(\Gamma)
such that
\pi_{*}\mu
-a.e.
G
is connected and fully supported.

Remark 14
.

It can be shown that the graph-cost of a pmp action is the same as the groupoid cost of the induced groupoid. We will not need this so we do not prove it here.

Let
\pi:X\to\mathsf{Graph}(\Gamma)
be a
\Gamma
-graph. Define
{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi}\subset X\times X
by
(x,gx)\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi}
if and only if
(e,g^{-1})\in\pi(x)
. We will prove that
{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi}
is a graphing associated to the action of
\Gamma
on
(X,\mu)
, and that
\operatorname{grcost}_{\mu}(\pi)={\operatorname{Cost}}_{\mu}({\mathcal{G}}_{\pi})
. That is,
{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi}
is a graph such that the connected components are exactly the equivalence classes of
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Gamma}
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\pi:X\to\mathsf{Graph}(X)
be
\Gamma
-invariant. If
\pi(x)
is fully supported and connected for a.e.
x
then
{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi}
is a graphing generating the orbit equivalence relation
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Gamma}
.

Proof.

Let
g_{1},g_{2}\in\Gamma
. By definition,
(g_{1}x,g_{2}x)\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi}
if and only if
(g_{1}^{-1},g_{2}^{-1})\in\pi(x)
. More precisely,

(hx,gx)\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi}
if and only if
(e,hg^{-1})=(e,(gh^{-1})^{-1})\in\pi(hx)=h\pi(x)
since
\pi
is
\Gamma
-invariant. This occurs exactly when
(h^{-1},g^{-1})\in\pi(x)
.

Furthermore, if
(e,g^{-1})\in\pi(x)
then
(g,e)\in\pi(gx)
which implies
(e,g)\in\pi(gx)
and hence
(gx,x)\in{\mathcal{G}}
.

If
x,y\in X
are in the same orbit then there exists
g\in\Gamma
with
y=gx
. Because
\pi(x)
is connected and has full support almost surely, there exists a path from
x
to
gx
in
\pi(x)
a.s.
Thus there a.s. exists
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{n}\in\Gamma
with
(e,g_{1}),(g_{1},g_{2}),\ldots,(g_{n-1},g_{n})\in\pi(x)
and hence
(x,g_{1}^{-1}x),(g_{1}^{-1}x,g_{2}^{-1}x),\ldots,(g_{n-1}^{-1}x,g_{n}^{-1}x)\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi}
. This means that all points in the orbit of
x
under the action of
\Gamma
are in the same connected component of
{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi}
almost surely.
∎

Corollary 6.4
.

Whenever
\pi
is a
\Gamma
-graph, the graph-cost of
\pi
is at least the cost of the induced graphing
{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi}
. These are equal if the action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is essentially free.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi}
be the graphing induced by
\pi
. As in the proof of the previous lemma, for
x\in X
, there is a surjective map from the edges of
\pi(x)
adjacent to
e
to the edges of
{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi}
adjacent to a vertex
x
given by
(e,g)\mapsto(x,g^{-1}x)
. This implies
\deg_{{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi}}(x)\leq\deg_{e}(\pi(x))
a.e.. Moreover, this map is a bijection if the action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is essentially free. So

{\operatorname{Cost}}_{\mu}({\mathcal{G}}_{\pi})=\frac{1}{2}\int\deg_{{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi}}(x)d\mu(x)\leq\frac{1}{2}\int\deg_{e}(\pi(x))d\mu(x)=\operatorname{grcost}_{\mu}(\pi)

with equality throughout in the case where
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is essentially free.
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

If
{\mathcal{G}}
is a graphing generating the equivalence relation
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Gamma}
induced by an essentially free action of
\Gamma
, then there exists some
\Gamma
-graph
\pi
such that
{\mathcal{G}}={\mathcal{G}}_{\pi}
.

Proof.

Suppose
{\mathcal{G}}\subset X\times X
is a graphing that generates the equivalence relation
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Gamma}
. Define
\pi:X\to\mathsf{Graph}(\Gamma)
by
\pi(x)
is the set of all
(g^{\prime-1},g^{-1})
such that
(gx,g^{\prime}x)\in{\mathcal{G}}
.

Clearly
\pi(x)
is symmetric. Since
{\mathcal{G}}
generates
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Gamma}
, the connected components of
{\mathcal{G}}
are the equivalence classes in
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Gamma}
. That means that for a.e.
x\in X
, if
y\in X
is such that there exists
g\in\Gamma
with
x=gy
, there exists a path from
x
to
y
in
{\mathcal{G}}
. Thus there exists a path from

to

in
\pi(x)
, and
\pi(x)
is connected almost surely. Furthermore,

has full support because the action is essentially free.

Lastly,
\pi
is
\Gamma
-equivariant. Let

. We can see that

∎

Corollary 6.6
.

The graph-cost of an essentially free probability measure preserving action

is equal to the cost of
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
.

6.3.1
Graphs which are not fully supported

The main purpose of this subsection is to prove Lemma

below which provides a formula for graph-cost in terms of
\Gamma
-maps

such that
\pi_{*}\mu

Suppose
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is pmp and ergodic. Given a
\Gamma
-equivariant map
\pi:X\to\textsf{Graph}(\Gamma)
, let
\operatorname{Weight}(\pi)
be the probability that the identity

is in the support of the random graph

is a

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
be an essentially free probability measure preserving action. Then the

-cost of this action is

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Cost}}_{\mu}(\Gamma,X)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\pi}\left(1-\operatorname{Weight}(\pi)+\frac{1}{2}\int\deg_{e}(G)~\textrm{d}\pi_{*}\mu(G)\right)

(8)

where the infimum is over all
\Gamma
-equivariant maps
\pi:X\to\mathsf{Graph}(\Gamma)
such that
\pi_{*}\mu
-almost every

is connected and non-empty.

Proof.

In the case that
\pi_{*}\mu
-almost every

has full support, we have that

, and thus equation

is equal to the original graph-cost formula. Hence if we take the infimum over only these

. Hence we obtain the inequality

{\operatorname{Cost}}_{\mu}(\Gamma,X)\geq\inf_{\pi}\left(1-\text{Weight}(\pi)+\frac{1}{2}\int\deg_{e}(G)~\textrm{d}\pi_{*}\mu(G)\right).

Now suppose that
\pi_{*}\mu
-almost every
G
is connected and non-empty but does not necessarily have full support. We need to show that
1-\text{Weight}(\pi)+\frac{1}{2}\int\deg_{e}(G)~\textrm{d}\pi_{*}\mu(G)\geq\operatorname{grcost}_{\mu}({\mathcal{R}}_{\Gamma})
. For this let

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\{x\in X:~\operatorname{supp}(\pi(x))\ni e\}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\{(x,gx)\in S\times S:~(e,g^{-1})\in\pi(x)\}.

We claim:

is a complete section;

2.

{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi}
is a graphing of

restricted to

To see item (1), note that for a.e.

To see item (2), let

This proves the opposite inequality.

7
Cost of Poisson suspensions

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
7.1
, which gives an upper bound for the cost of a Poisson suspension of an imp action under certain circumstances. First, we introduce the last necessary definition for the theorem.
Additional information on double recurrence is reviewed in Appendix
A
.

Definition 27
.

Let
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
be an ergodic imp action. By Theorem
A.1
,
X^{2}
is the disjoint union of
\Gamma
-invariant measurable sets
\mathsf{Con}(X^{2})
and
\mathsf{Dis}(X^{2})
, and the restriction of
\Gamma
to
\mathsf{Con}(X^{2})
is infinitely conservative. We will say the action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is
partially doubly recurrent
(PDR) if for a.e.
x,y\in X
there exist
x=x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}=y
with
(x_{i},x_{i+1})\in\mathsf{Con}(X^{2})
for all
i
. In other words, the equivalence relation generated by
\mathsf{Cont}(X^{2})
is all of
X
(up to a set of measure zero).

Theorem 7.1
.

Suppose
\Gamma
is a countable group.
Let
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
be a
\Gamma
-invariant action such that a.e. ergodic component is infinite, non-atomic, essentially free and partially doubly recurrent. Then

{\operatorname{Cost}}_{\operatorname{Pois}(\mu)}({\mathcal{R}}_{\Pi})\leq\operatorname{ncost}({\mathcal{R}}_{\Gamma})

where
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Gamma}
is the equivalence relation induced by
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
and
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Pi}
is the equivalence relation induced by the pmp action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}({\mathbb{M}}(X),\operatorname{Pois}(\mu))
.

We will prove this after the next two lemmas.

Lemma 7.2
.

Suppose
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is an essentially free ergodic imp action. Then the action of
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}({\mathbb{M}}(X),\operatorname{Pois}(\mu))
is essentially free.

Proof.

Let
g\in\Gamma
be nontrivial. It suffices to show that
\operatorname{Pois}(\mu)
-a.e.
\Pi
,
g\Pi\neq\Pi
. The special case in which
g
has infinite order is implied by
[
Roy07
, Theorem 4.8]
. So suppose
g
has finite order
n
. Because the action is essentially free, the Ergodic Decomposition Theorem implies there exists a measurable set
Y\subset X
such that
X
is the disjoint union of
Y,gY,\ldots,g^{n-1}Y
(mod
\mu
).

Since
\mu(X)=\infty
,
\mu(Y)=+\infty
too. Let
A\subset Y
be a set with finite positive measure. If
\Pi=g^{-1}\Pi
then
\Pi(A)=\Pi(gA)
. So it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\lim_{\mu(A)\to+\infty}\operatorname{Pois}(\mu)(\{\Pi:~\Pi(A)=\Pi(gA)\})=0.

(9)

where the limit is over all positive finite measure sets
A\subset Y
.

By definition of
\operatorname{Pois}(\mu)
(and since
A
and
gA
are disjoint),

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pois}(\mu)(\{\Pi:~\Pi(A)=\Pi(gA)\})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu(A)^{k}}{e^{\mu(A)}k!}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{k\in\{0\}\cup{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{\mu(A)^{k}}{e^{\mu(A)}k!}.

The value of
k\in\{0\}\cup{\mathbb{N}}
which maximizes the last expression is called the mode of a Poisson random variable with mean
\mu(A)
. It is known to equal
\lfloor\mu(A)\rfloor
. By Stirling’s Approximation, this implies

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pois}(\mu)(\{\Pi:~\Pi(A)=\Pi(gA)\})

\displaystyle=O(1/\sqrt{\mu(A)}).

This implies (

).

∎

Proof of Theorem
7.1
.

By the Ergodic Decomposition Theorem, it suffices to handle the special case in which the action is ergodic.

Let
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
be an ergodic, essentially free imp action where

\Gamma{\curvearrowright}({\mathbb{M}}(X),\operatorname{Pois}(\mu))

Let

\mu

. Then

In order to obtain a connected graph, we will take the union of

Let

\Gamma

Let

Claim 1
. For

\mu\times\mu\times\nu_{p}
-a.e.

.

Proof of Claim 1.

Fix

Then for a.e.

That is, there exist infinitely many

So we have shown that a.e.

∎

Claim 1 implies:

Claim 3

∎

By Lemma

Let

Since

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int\left(\deg_{e}({\widehat{E}}(\Pi))+\deg_{e}(G)\right)~\textrm{d}\operatorname{Pois}(\mu)\times\nu_{p}(\Pi,G)

\displaystyle\leq|p|+\frac{1}{2}\int\deg_{e}({\widehat{E}}(\Pi))~\textrm{d}\operatorname{Pois}(\mu)(\Pi).

Claim 4
.

{\mathbb{E}}[\deg_{e}({\widehat{E}}(\Pi))||\Pi\cap S|=n]\leq 2n\cdot{\operatorname{Cost}}({\mathcal{G}})/\mu(S).

That is, the expected value of the degree of
{\widehat{E}}(\Pi)
, conditioned on

, is at most

Suppose

. Then
{\widehat{E}}(\Pi)=\cup_{i=1}^{n}E(x_{i})
. So

\deg_{e}({\widehat{E}}(\Pi))\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\deg_{e}(E(x_{i})).

.
∎

From Claim 4, it follows that

\displaystyle\int\deg_{e}({\widehat{E}}(\Pi))~\textrm{d}\operatorname{Pois}(\mu)(\Pi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}[\deg_{e}({\widehat{E}}(\Pi))||\Pi\cap S|=n]\operatorname{Pois}(\mu)(\{\Pi:~|\Pi\cap S|=n\})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(2n\cdot{\operatorname{Cost}}({\mathcal{G}})/\mu(S))\cdot\operatorname{Pois}(\mu)(\{\Pi:~|\Pi\cap S|=n\})

\displaystyle=2{\operatorname{Cost}}({\mathcal{G}})

(12)

where the last equality occurs because

.

), (

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Cost}}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{M}}(X),\operatorname{Pois}(\mu))

\displaystyle\leq 1-\textrm{Weight}(E^{0})+\frac{1}{2}\int\deg_{e}(E^{0}(\Pi))~\textrm{d}\operatorname{Pois}(\mu)(\Pi)

\displaystyle\leq 1-(1-\exp(-\mu(S)))+|p|+{\operatorname{Cost}}({\mathcal{G}})

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-\mu(S))+|p|+{\operatorname{Cost}}({\mathcal{R}}|S)+\epsilon.

Since

and

By definition of normalized cost,

So

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{ncost}(\Gamma,X,\mu)+\exp(-\mu(S))-1+\mu(S).

However,

is an arbitrary finite positive measure set. Since

A general criterion for fixed price 1

Theorem 8.1
.

If
\Gamma
has an imp action which is limit-amenable, partially doubly recurrent, and has normalized cost
p
then
\Gamma
has max-cost at most
p
. In particular, if
p=1
then
\Gamma
has fixed price 1.

Proof.

If
\Gamma
is amenable then
\Gamma
has fixed price 1 and the theorem is trivial. So we may assume
\Gamma
is non-amenable. By Conjecture
4.11
all limit-amenable mp actions of
\Gamma
are infinite.

Suppose
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
is an imp action which is limit-amenable, partially doubly recurrent, and has normalized cost
p
. Then a.e. ergodic component of the action is also limit-amenable (by Conjecture
4.5
), partially doubly recurrent (by Lemma
A.3
) and there must be some ergodic component with normalized cost at most
p
(since normalized cost behaves linearly with respect to ergodic decomposition).

So without loss of generality we may assume the action is ergodic. After taking the direct product with a Bernoulli shift if necessary, we may also assume the action is non-atomic and essentially free. This is because direct products with pmp actions preserve limit-amenability (by Theorem
4.10
) and partial double recurrence (by Lemma
A.4
).

Theorem
7.1
implies the cost of the Poisson suspension
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}({\mathbb{M}}(X),\operatorname{Pois}(\mu))
has cost bounded above by the normalized cost of
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
. Because the action is limit-amenable, by Corollary
5.5
the Poisson suspension is weakly contained in Bernoulli. Thus by the Abert-Weiss Theorem
[
AW13
]
,
\Gamma
has maximal cost less than or equal to
\text{ncost}({\mathcal{R}})
.
∎

9
Metric groups

The main result of this section is that if a countable group
\Gamma
is equipped with a (quasi-) metric
d
satisfying certain properties then there is an infinite
\Gamma
-invariant measure
\mu
on the space
{\mathcal{H}}
of horofunctions such that
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}({\mathcal{H}},\mu)
is limit-amenable and doubly-recurrent. This is Theorem
9.3
and is stated in a more detailed manner in Theorem
9.14
.

To begin, we define the properties of quasi-metrics, then the space of horofunctions, then we explore certain classes of
\Gamma
-invariant measures on
{\mathcal{H}}
. The last section §
9.4
proves the main result.

Definition 28
.

A
quasi-metric
is essentially the same as a metric except that the triangle inequality holds only up to an additive constant. To be precise: a function
d:X\times X\to[0,\infty)
is a
quasi-metric
if there exists a constant
C_{q}\geq 0
such that for all
x,y,z\in X
:

1.

d(x,y)=0
if and only if
x=y
;

2.

d(x,y)=d(y,x)
;

3.

(quasi-triangle inequality)
d(x,z)\leq d(x,y)+d(y,z)+C_{q}
.

As usual, the closed ball of radius
r
centered as
x\in X
is
B(x,r)=\{y\in X:~d(x,y)\leq r\}
. We often denote the closed ball of radius
r
about
e\in\Gamma
as
B(r)
. Sometimes, we write this as
B(\Gamma,x,r)
or
B(\Gamma,r)
to emphasize the role of the group
\Gamma
.

Remark 15
.

If
d:X\times X\to[0,\infty)
is a metric then there is an integer-valued quasi-metric
\overline{d}:X\times X\to{\mathbb{Z}}
defined by
\overline{d}(x,y)=\lceil d(x,y)\rceil
. This construction is the main reason for introducing quasi-metrics; because certain arguments are easier when the metric takes on only integer values.

Definition 29
.

Let
d
be a quasi-metric on a countable group
\Gamma
. We say

•

d
is
proper
if every ball of finite radius is finite;

•

d
is
left-invariant
if
d(gh,gf)=d(h,f)
for all
f,g,h\in\Gamma
;

•

d
is
\epsilon
-approximately sub-additive
if there is an
\epsilon>0
such that if

SS(\Gamma,n,\epsilon)=\{x\in\Gamma:~d(x,e)\in[n-\epsilon,n+\epsilon]\}

is the spherical shell of width
2\epsilon
then for every
n,m\geq\epsilon
,

SS(\Gamma,n,\epsilon)\cdot SS(\Gamma,m,\epsilon)\supset S(\Gamma,n+m)

where
S(\Gamma,n+m)=\{x\in\Gamma:~d(x,e)=n+m\}
is the sphere of radius
n+m
. The reason for the terminology is this property implies the sequence
\{\log(|B(\Gamma,n)|)+C\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is sub-additive for some constant
C>0
. This is proven in Lemma
9.1
.

Additionaly, the
exponential growth rate
of
(\Gamma,d)
is defined by

\mathsf{growth}(\Gamma,d)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log|B(n)|}{n}>0

assuming the limit exists.

Remark 16
.

Word metrics are proper, left-invariant and approximately sub-additive with
\epsilon=0
. It may help to just assume
d
is a word metric on first reading.

Lemma 9.1
.

If
d
is proper and
(\Gamma,d)
is approximately sub-additive then the growth rate
\mathsf{growth}(\Gamma,d)
exists.

Proof.

Because
(\Gamma,d)
is approximately sub-additive,
B(n+m)\subset B(n+\epsilon)B(m+\epsilon)
for all
n,m\geq 0
(since
B(n+\epsilon)
contains the spherical shell
SS(\Gamma,n,\epsilon)
for example). Moreover,
B(n+\epsilon)\subset B(n)B(3\epsilon)
. So

B(n+m)\subset B(n)B(3\epsilon)B(m)B(3\epsilon).

Thus the sequence
a_{n}=\log|B(n)|
is approximately sub-additive in the sense that there is a constant
C=2\log|B(3\epsilon)|
such that
a_{n+m}\leq a_{n}+a_{m}+C
. But this implies that the sequence
\{a_{n}+C\}
is sub-additive. Fekete’s Lemma implies

\mathsf{growth}(\Gamma,d)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n}+C}{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{n}

exists.
∎

Corollary 9.2
.

If
(\Gamma,d)
is proper and
\epsilon
-approximately sub-additive where
\epsilon>0
, then

is a finite generating set for
\Gamma
.

As in the proof of the previous lemma,
B(n+\epsilon)\subset B(n)B(3\epsilon)
for any

. So, by induction,
B(3\epsilon)^{n}\supset B((n+2)\epsilon)
. Since

is arbitrary and
d
is proper this implies

is a finite generating set.
∎

.

.

9.1
Horofunctions

Let
\operatorname{Lip}(\Gamma)
be the space of all
1
-Lipschitz functions
h:\Gamma\to{\mathbb{Z}}
with the topology of pointwise convergence on finite subsets (where
h
is
1
-Lipschitz if
|h(g)-h(f)|\leq d(g,f)
for all
g,f\in\Gamma)
. Also let
\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(\Gamma)=\{h\in\operatorname{Lip}(\Gamma):~h(e)=0\}
. By the Ascoli-Arzela Theorem,
\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(\Gamma)
is compact.

For each
x\in\Gamma
, let
d_{x}\in\operatorname{Lip}(\Gamma)
be the distance-to-
x
function. That is:

d_{x}(y)=d(x,y).

Also define
h_{x}\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(\Gamma)
by
h_{x}=d_{x}-|x|
. This is the horofunction associated with
x
normalized so that
h_{x}(e)=0
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}^{\circ}_{0}=\{h_{x}:~x\in\Gamma\}
and let
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
be the closure of
{\mathcal{H}}^{\circ}_{0}
in
\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(\Gamma)
. Because
\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(\Gamma)
is compact,
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
is also compact. It is called the

\Gamma
.

Definition 31
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}(\Gamma)={\mathcal{H}}_{0}+{\mathbb{Z}}\subset\operatorname{Lip}(\Gamma)
which is the set of all functions of the form
h=h_{0}+r
with
h_{0}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
and where
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}
is a constant. Because
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
is compact,
{\mathcal{H}}
is locally compact. We call
{\mathcal{H}}
the space of
horofunctions on
\Gamma
. Sometimes we emphasize the role of the group by writing
{\mathcal{H}}(\Gamma)
to mean
{\mathcal{H}}
(and similarly with
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
, etc).

Observe that
\Gamma
acts continuously on
{\mathcal{H}}
by

(g\cdot h)(x)=h(g^{-1}x)

for
g,x\in\Gamma
and
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
. Also let

\partial{\mathcal{H}}_{0}={\mathcal{H}}_{0}\setminus{\mathcal{H}}^{\circ}_{0},\quad\partial{\mathcal{H}}=\partial{\mathcal{H}}_{0}+{\mathbb{Z}}.

We also let
{\mathcal{H}}_{\leq n}=\{h\in{\mathcal{H}}:~h(e)\leq n\}
and

\partial{\mathcal{H}}_{\leq n}={\mathcal{H}}_{\leq n}\cap\partial{\mathcal{H}}
.

Lemma 9.5
.

The space
\partial{\mathcal{H}}_{\leq 0}(\Gamma)
is a complete section for the action of
\Gamma
on
\partial{\mathcal{H}}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

Let
h\in\partial{\mathcal{H}}(\Gamma)
. It suffices to show there exists
g\in\Gamma
with
h(g)\leq 0
. If
h(e)\leq 0
then we are done. So we will assume
h(e)>0
.

Let
h_{0}\in\partial{\mathcal{H}}_{0}(\Gamma)
be the function
h_{0}(x)=h(x)-h(e)
. By definition of
\partial{\mathcal{H}}_{0}(\Gamma)
, there exists a sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\Gamma
diverging to infinity with

Definition 33

Lemma 9.11

9.3.1
Expansion-invariant measures

The goal of this section is to prove the existence of an
\mathsf{Expand}_{*}
-quasi-invariant measure which is a convex integral of measures in
\partial{\operatorname{Meas}}({\mathcal{H}})
. Recall that
\mathsf{Expand}:\operatorname{Lip}(\Gamma)\to\operatorname{Lip}(\Gamma)
is defined by
\mathsf{Expand}(\xi)=\xi-1
.

Proposition 9.13
.

There exists a
\Gamma
-invariant measure
\mu
on
{\mathcal{H}}
such that
\mu
is equivalent to
{\mathsf{Expand}}_{*}\mu
and
\mu=\int\nu~d\zeta(\nu)
for some Borel probability measure
\zeta
on
\partial{\operatorname{Meas}}({\mathcal{H}})

\Gamma{\curvearrowright}({\mathcal{H}},\mu)
is limit-amenable.

Proof.

Define

by

Then
T
is continuous in the almost-weak topology because
\mathsf{Expand}
is continuous and the map which sends
\mu
to
\mu({\mathcal{H}}_{\leq 1})
is continuous. This is by the Portmanteau Theorem using the fact that
{\mathcal{H}}_{\leq 1}
is both closed and open. This is one of the reasons why we work with the almost-weak topology instead of the vague topology.

By the way, the reader might wonder why
T
maps
\partial{\operatorname{Meas}}({\mathcal{H}})
into
\partial{\operatorname{Meas}}({\mathcal{H}})
. This is because
\frac{|B(n)|}{|B(n+1)|}\mathsf{Expand}_{*}\mu_{n}=\mu_{n+1}
by (
14
). So if
\mu\in\partial{\operatorname{Meas}}({\mathcal{H}})
is is the limit of a sequence of measures
\{\mu_{n_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
then
T(\mu)
the limit of the sequence of measures
\{\mu_{n_{i}+1}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
.

By the Kyrylov-Bogolyubov fixed point Theorem, there is a
T
-invariant Borel probability measure
\zeta
on
\partial{\operatorname{Meas}}({\mathcal{H}})
. This means

. This uses the compactness Lemma

.

Define a measure
\mu
on
{\mathcal{H}}
by

\int f~d\mu=\iint f~d\nu~d\zeta(\nu)

for all compactly supported continuous functions
f
on
{\mathcal{H}}
. By the Riesz-Markov Theorem, this defines a measure
\mu
on
{\mathcal{H}}
.

Because each
\nu
in the support of
\zeta
is limit-amenable, it follows from Theorem
4.5
, that the action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}({\mathcal{H}},\mu)
is limit-amenable. Indeed, the bounds from Lemma
9.7
and Corollary
9.8
imply that the measures in
\partial{\operatorname{Meas}}({\mathcal{H}})
are uniformly bounded on compacts.
∎

9.4
Double recurrence

The main result of this subsection is the following refinement of Theorem
9.3
:

Theorem 9.14
.

As above, we assume
d
is an integer-valued, left-invariant, proper, approximately sub-additive quasi-metric on
\Gamma
. Let
\mu
be a measure on the space of horofunctions
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}(\Gamma)
satisfying the conclusions of Proposition
9.13
. If
(\Gamma,d)
has the overlapping neighborhoods property then
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}({\mathcal{H}}(\Gamma),\mu)
is doubly-recurrent.

Throughout this section we will assume that
\Gamma
satisfies the overlapping neighborhoods property. Let
C>0
be the constant in the definition of that property. Let
C_{q}
be the constant in the definition of quasi-metrics, and
\epsilon>0
be such that
d
is
\epsilon
-approximately sub-additive.

We use the following notation:
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}={\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{H}}
,
\mu^{2}=\mu\times\mu
and if
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
is any subset and

then
\mathtt{Ret}({\mathcal{S}},h)
is the set of all group elements
g\in\Gamma
such that

(where
\Gamma
acts on
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
diagonally). We need to show that for all finite measure subsets
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
, for a.e.
h\in{\mathcal{S}}
,
\mathtt{Ret}({\mathcal{S}},h)
is infinite.

We begin by considering very specific subsets
{\mathcal{S}}
. For
g\in\Gamma
and
m,r\geq 0
, let

\displaystyle Z_{g}

\displaystyle=\{(h_{1},h_{2})\in{\mathcal{H}}^{2}:~h_{1}(e)<0,h_{2}(g)<C_{q}\},

\displaystyle Z^{\prime}_{g}

\displaystyle=\{(h_{1},h_{2})\in{\mathcal{H}}^{2}:~h_{1}(e)<-C-|g|,h_{2}(g)<-C-2|g|-C_{q}\},

\displaystyle Z^{\prime}_{g,m}

\displaystyle=\{h\in Z^{\prime}_{g}:~|\mathtt{Ret}(Z_{g},h)|\geq m\},

\displaystyle Z^{\prime}_{g,m,r}

\displaystyle=\{h\in Z^{\prime}_{g}:~|\mathtt{Ret}(Z_{g},h)\cap B(r)|\geq m\}.

Because
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
is the union of
f\cdot Z_{g}
(over all

), to prove double-recurrence, it suffices to show that
\mathtt{Ret}(Z_{g},h)
is infinite for
\mu^{2}
-a.e.
h\in Z_{g}
. The next lemma proves the weaker statement that this holds for
\nu^{2}
-a.e.

(where

is arbitrary). Afterwards, we use
\mathsf{Expand}
-quasi-invariance of

to amplify this statement to

-a.e.

and thereby obtain double-recurrence.

Lemma 9.15
.

Let
\nu\in\partial{\operatorname{Meas}}({\mathcal{H}})
and

.
Then for
\nu^{2}
-a.e.

,

is infinite.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that for every
m>0

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\nu^{2}(Z^{\prime}_{g}\setminus Z^{\prime}_{g,m,r})=0.

(15)

To see this, assume (
15
). Then for all
m>0
, for
\nu^{2}
-a.e.
h\in Z^{\prime}_{g}
there is an

(depending on

and

) such that

. Thus
Z^{\prime}_{g}\subset\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}Z^{\prime}_{g,m}
(up to measure zero) which implies
\nu^{2}
-a.e.
h\in Z^{\prime}_{g}
has
infinite return times to
Z_{g}
:
|\mathtt{Ret}(Z_{g},h)|=\infty
.

For the remainder of the proof, let
\nu\in\partial{\operatorname{Meas}}({\mathcal{H}})
. Thus there exists a sequence
(\mu_{n_{i}})_{i=1}^{\infty}
with
n_{i}\to\infty
such that
\mu_{n_{i}}
converges almost-weakly to
\nu
and each
\mu_{n_{i}}
is of the form
\mu_{n_{i}}=|B(n_{i})|^{-1}\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\delta_{d_{x}-n_{i}}
.

Both sets

and

are closed and open in

and both are contained

to

to

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\mu^{2}_{n_{i}}(Z^{\prime}_{g}\setminus Z^{\prime}_{g,m,r})=\nu^{2}(Z^{\prime}_{g}\setminus Z^{\prime}_{g,m,r}).

(16)

This equality is one of the main reasons for using the almost-weak topology instead of the vague topology.

It now suffices to prove

\lim_{r\to\infty}\liminf_{i\to\infty}\mu^{2}_{n_{i}}(Z^{\prime}_{g}\setminus Z^{\prime}_{g,m,r})=0.

By definition of
\mu_{n}
,

\displaystyle\mu^{2}_{n}(Z^{\prime}_{g}\setminus Z^{\prime}_{g,m,r})

\displaystyle=\frac{\#\{(x,y)\in B(e,n)\times B(g,n):~(d_{x}-n,d_{y}-n)\in Z^{\prime}_{g}\setminus Z^{\prime}_{g,m,r}\}}{|B(n)|^{2}}.

This is because if

For the first coordinate it is clear that

For the second coordinate, if

By definition,

if and only if

\displaystyle|x|

\displaystyle\leq n-C-|g|,\quad d(y,g)\leq n-C-C_{q}-2|g|.

On the other hand, if
(d_{x}-n,d_{y}-n)\in Z^{\prime}_{g}
and

\displaystyle d(x,f^{-1})\leq n,\quad d(y,f^{-1}g)\leq n+C_{q}.

Letting

and

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#\{(x,y)\in B(e,N)\times B(g,M):~|B(r)\cap B(x,n)\cap B(y,n+C_{q})g^{-1}|<m\}}{|B(n)|^{2}}=0.

The overlapping neighborhoods property is equivalent to: for every
m>0

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#\{(x,y)\in B(n)^{2}:~|B(r)\cap B(x,n+C)\cap B(y,n+C)|<m\}}{|B(n)|^{2}}=0.

By replacing

with
n-C-|g|
we see that this is equivalent to:

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#\{(x,y)\in B(n-C-|g|)^{2}:~|B(r)\cap B(x,n-|g|)\cap B(y,n-|g|)|<m\}}{|B(n-C-|g|)|^{2}}=0.

We claim that

\{(x,y)\in B(e,N)\times B(g,M):~|B(r)\cap B(x,n)\cap B(y,n+C_{q})g^{-1}|<m\}

is contained in

\{(x,y)\in B(n-C-|g|)^{2}:~|B(r)\cap B(x,n-|g|)\cap B(y,n-|g|)|<m\}.

To see this, suppose
(x,y)
is in the first set. It is immediate that
x\in B(n-C-|g|)
. By the quasi-triangle inequality,
|y|\leq d(y,g)+d(g,e)+C_{q}\leq n-C-|g|
, so
y\in B(n-C-|g|)
too. To finish, it suffices to show that

B(r)\cap B(x,n-|g|)\cap B(y,n-|g|)\subset B(r)\cap B(x,n)\cap B(y,n+C_{q})g^{-1}.

This holds because
B(x,n-|g|)\subset B(x,n)
and
B(y,n-|g|)\subset B(y,n+C_{q})g^{-1}
. To justify the latter inclusion, let

. This means
d(y,z)\leq n-|g|
. By the quasi-triangle inequality and left-invariance,

d(y,zg)\leq d(y,z)+d(z,zg)+C_{q}=d(y,z)+|g|+C_{q}\leq n+C_{q}.

Thus
zg\in B(y,n+C_{q})
which implies
z\in B(y,n+C_{q})g^{-1}
as required.

It follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#\{(x,y)\in B(e,N)\times B(g,M):~|B(r)\cap B(x,n)\cap B(y,n+C_{q})g^{-1}|<m\}}{|B(n)|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{r\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#\{(x,y)\in B(n-C-|g|)^{2}:~|B(r)\cap B(x,n-|g|)\cap B(y,n-|g|)|<m\}}{|B(n-C-|g|)|^{2}}.

The latter equals zero by the overlapping neighborhoods property. This completes the proof.
∎

For

Definition 36
.

and

\displaystyle f_{i}(n)\#SS(\Gamma_{i},n,\epsilon)

Proposition 10.7
.

Suppose that

(\Gamma_{2},d_{2})

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\frac{\#B(\Gamma_{i},n)}{\#B(\Gamma,n)}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\frac{\#S(\Gamma_{i},r)}{\#SS(\Gamma,r,2\epsilon)}\cdot\frac{\#SS(\Gamma,r,2\epsilon)}{\#B(\Gamma,n)}.

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{n}\frac{\#SS(\Gamma,r,2\epsilon)}{\#B(\Gamma,n)}\leq 4\epsilon,

has full measure in
S
.

Let
\tilde{S}_{\infty}=\phi^{-1}(S_{\infty})
. As in item (1),
\tilde{S}_{\infty}\subset\mathsf{Con}(X^{2})
. Moreover, by induction on
n

. Therefore,
\cup_{n}\tilde{S}^{n}_{\infty}
has full measure in

is arbitrary, this implies the action

∎

Appendix B
Measured equivalence relations

Let
(X,\mu)
be a standard Borel space and
{\mathcal{R}}\subset X\times X
a Borel equivalence relation. For
x\in X
, let
[x]_{\mathcal{R}}=\{y\in X:~(x,y)\in{\mathcal{R}}\}
be its equivalence class. We say
{\mathcal{R}}
is

•

discrete
or
countable
if every equivalence class is at most countable;

•

aperiodic
if every equivalence class is infinite;

•

finite
if every equivalence class is finite;

•

hyperfinite
if there exist finite Borel equivalence relations
{\mathcal{R}}_{1}\subset{\mathcal{R}}_{2}\subset\cdots
with
{\mathcal{R}}=\cup_{i}{\mathcal{R}}_{i}
.

The next theorem provides several equivalent formulations for when
{\mathcal{R}}
preserves the measure
\mu
.

Theorem B.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{R}}
be a discrete equivalence relation on a standard measure space
(X,\mu)
. The following are equivalent:

1.

(Full group) Let
[{\mathcal{R}}]
be the group of all Borel automorphisms
\phi:X\to X
such that
(x,\phi(x))\in{\mathcal{R}}
for every
x
. This is the
full group
of
{\mathcal{R}}
.
{\mathcal{R}}
preserves
\mu
in the sense that
\phi_{*}\mu=\mu
for every
\phi\in[{\mathcal{R}}]
.

2.

(Mass transport principle) For every non-negative Borel map
F:{\mathcal{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,

\int\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{R}}}F(x,y)~d\mu(x)=\int\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{R}}}F(y,x)~d\mu(x).

3.

(Group action) There exists a countable group
\Gamma
with a measure-preserving action
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
such that if
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Gamma}=\{(x,gx):~x\in X,g\in\Gamma\}
is the orbit-equivalence relation then
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Gamma}={\mathcal{R}}
mod
\mu
.

4.

(Unimodularity) Define Borel measures
\mu_{L},\mu_{R}
on
{\mathcal{R}}
by

\displaystyle\mu_{L}(E)

\displaystyle=\int\#\{y:~(x,y)\in E\}~d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\mu_{R}(E)

\displaystyle=\int\#\{y:~(y,x)\in E\}~d\mu(x).

Then
\mu_{L}=\mu_{R}
.

Remark 22
.

Item (3) uses the Feldman-Moore Theorem

FM77
]
. The rest are exercises.

We say that
(X,\mu,{\mathcal{R}})
is pmp, imp, or mp if the conditions above are satisfied and
\mu
is a probability, infinite or arbitrary measure respectively.

In the case where
{\mathcal{R}}
is aperiodic, we get the following result, due to Slaman-Steel.

Theorem B.2

(Marker Lemma)
.

Let
{\mathcal{R}}
be an aperiodic discrete Borel equivalence relation on
X
. Then there exists a
vanishing sequence of markers
for
{\mathcal{R}}
, i.e. there is a sequence
\{S_{n}\}\subset X
of Borel sets such that

1.

S_{0}\supseteq S_{1}\supseteq S_{2}\supseteq\cdots
,

2.

\cap_{n}S_{n}=\emptyset
, and

3.

each
S_{n}
meets every equivalence class of
{\mathcal{R}}
. That is, each
S_{n}
is a
complete section
for
{\mathcal{R}}
.

In the case where
\mu
is a standard probability measure we are able to use the Marker Lemma to find complete sections with arbitrarily small measure.
Note that for infinite measures, a vanishing sequence of markers does not necessarily correspond to a finite measure complete section. For example, if we have an aperiodic equivalence relation on
{\mathbb{R}}
equipped with Lebesgue measure, the sequence
S_{n}=[n,\infty)
is a vanishing sequence of markers but each
S_{n}
has infinite measure.

However, we can always find a finite measure section for an aperiodic equivalence relation
{\mathcal{R}}
as long as
(X,\mu)
is
\sigma
-finite and a.e. ergodic component is non-atomic.

Theorem B.3
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite infinite non-atomic measure space. Suppose
{\mathcal{R}}\subset X\times X
is a countable aperiodic
\mu
-quasi-invariant equivalence relation. Suppose a.e. ergodic component of
({\mathcal{R}},\mu)
is purely non-atomic. Then for every
\epsilon>0
there exists a Borel section
S
for the equivalence relation
{\mathcal{R}}
with
\mu(S)<\epsilon
.

Proof.

Let
\pi:(X,\mu)\to(Y,\nu)
be the factor map of
(X,\mu)
to the space
(Y,\nu)
of ergodic components for the action of
\Gamma
. Then for
\nu
-a.e.
y\in Y
, the action of
\Gamma
on
(X_{y},{\mathcal{B}}_{y},\mu_{y})
is ergodic and
\mu_{y}
-invariant, where
X_{y}=\pi^{-1}(y)
,
{\mathcal{B}}_{y}={\mathcal{B}}\cap X_{y}
, and
\mu_{y}
the associated measure to
y
for the disintegration of
\mu
over
\nu
. That is, for each
y\in Y
, there exists a measure
\mu_{y}
such that
\mu=\int_{Y}\mu_{y}d\nu(y)
and the action of
\Gamma
on
(\pi^{-1}(y),\mu_{y})
is ergodic. Additionally, by assumption,
\mu_{y}
is non-atomic for a.e.
y
.

Let
f:Y\to[0,\infty)
be a Borel function with
f(y)>0
for
\nu
-a.e.
y
and
\int fd\nu<\epsilon
, i.e. an everywhere positive
\textrm{L}^{1}
function on
Y
. Such a function exists because
(Y,\nu)
is
\sigma
-finite.

Define

S=\{x\in[0,\infty):~\mu_{\pi(x)}([0,x])\leq f(\pi(x))\}.

Because
f\circ\pi
is Borel and the map
x\mapsto\mu_{\pi(x)}([0,x])
is Borel, it follows that
S
is Borel. Moreover, for
y\in Y
,
\mu_{y}(S)=f(y)
. This is because
S\cap\pi^{-1}(y)
is an interval of the form
[0,t]
where
t
is the largest number with
\mu_{y}([0,t])\leq f(y)

\mu(S)=\int\mu_{y}(S)~d\nu(y)=\int f(y)~\textrm{d}\nu(y)<\epsilon.

Lastly, we observe that
S
meets every equivalence class because it meets a.e. ergodic component
\pi^{-1}(y)
in a positive measure set since
f
is positive a.e.
∎

Remark 24

Appendix C
Spaces of measures

The purpose of this section is to review convergence of measures; especially vague convergence in the context of locally compact spaces, and prove a version of the Portmanteau Theorem we will frequently use.

Let
(X,d)
be a Polish space and
\mathsf{Radon}(X)
be the set of Radon measures on
X
. Let

\displaystyle C(X)

\displaystyle=\{f:X\to{\mathbb{C}}:~f\textrm{ is continuous}\},

\displaystyle\|f\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in X}|f(x)|,

\displaystyle C_{B}(X)

\displaystyle=\{f\in C(X):~\|f\|<\infty\},

\displaystyle C_{c}(X)

\displaystyle=\{f\in C(X):X\to{\mathbb{C}}:~f\textrm{ has compact support}\},

\displaystyle C_{0}(X)

\displaystyle=\{f:X\to{\mathbb{C}}:~f\textrm{ vanishes at infinity}\}.

The last condition means: for all
\epsilon>0
there exists compact
K\subset X
such that
|f(x)|<\epsilon
for all
x\in X\setminus K
.

We also let
C^{+}(X),C_{B}^{+}(X),C_{c}^{+}(X),C_{0}^{+}(X)
denote the non-negative functions in
C(X),\\
C_{B}(X),C_{c}(X),C_{0}(X)
respectively. Observe
C_{c}(X)\subset C_{0}(X)\subset C_{B}(X)\subset C(X)
. Additionally,
C_{B}(X)
is a Banach algebra with the norm

. The subspace
C_{0}(X)
is closed in
C_{B}(X)
and is therefore a Banach space itself. Note that the space

is not closed unless
X
is compact. If
X
is lcsc then
C_{c}(X)
is norm dense in

by Urysohn’s Lemma.

Let
(\mu_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty},\mu_{\infty}
be Radon measures on
X
. We say
(\mu_{n})_{n}
converges to
\mu_{\infty}

•

vaguely
if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int f~d\mu_{n}=\int f~d\mu_{\infty}
for all
f\in C_{c}^{+}(X)
;

•

weak*
if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int f~d\mu_{n}=\int f~d\mu_{\infty}

•

weakly

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int f~d\mu_{n}=\int f~d\mu_{\infty}

Remark 25
.

We use non-negative test functions in the above definitions to ensure that the integral

is an infinite measure and

A subset

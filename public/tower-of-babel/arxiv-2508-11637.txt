N-Photon Emission from Uniform Acceleration
Arash Azizi

Abstract
We present a generalized framework for n n -photon processes involving a uniformly accelerated Unruh-DeWitt detector interacting with a massless scalar field. We utilize the n th n^{\text{th}} order Dyson series to derive the final quantum state for an arbitrary number of interactions. Our analysis covers both even-order processes, which return the detector to its initial state, and odd-order processes, which result in a change of the detector’s state. By employing a unified formalism and performing a complete, time-ordered integration, we obtain exact analytical expressions for the n n -photon states. The results reveal a rich structure of resonant denominators corresponding to multi-particle processes, including new field-mediated resonances independent of the detector’s energy gap for n > 2 n>2 . Crucially, the analysis of odd-order transitions reveals an exponential factor, exp ( − π ω / a ) \exp(-\pi\omega/a) , characteristic of the Unruh thermal bath. By considering processes starting from the detector’s excited state, we demonstrate that the ratio of excitation to de-excitation amplitudes precisely recovers the Boltzmann factor, providing a higher-order confirmation of thermal detailed balance for the Unruh effect. This work provides a unified tool for studying multipartite entanglement and thermal phenomena in non-inertial frames.

Theoretical Framework

2.1
Field Quantization and Unified Formalism

We begin by establishing the framework for a massless scalar field
\Phi
in 1+1-dimensional Minkowski spacetime, interacting with a uniformly accelerated UDW detector. The detector is a two-level system (ground
\ket{g}
, excited
\ket{e}
) with energy gap
\omega
. The interaction Hamiltonian in the detector’s proper time
\tau
is

H_{\text{int}}(\tau)=g\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\Phi(x(\tau))\left(\sigma^{\dagger}e^{i\omega\tau}+\sigma e^{-i\omega\tau}\right),

(1)

where
g
is the coupling constant,
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\dagger}
are the detector’s ladder operators, and the derivative coupling
\partial\Phi/\partial\tau
ensures a well-defined interaction along the accelerated worldline.

To analyze emissions into right-traveling waves (RTW, coordinate
u=t-x
) and left-traveling waves (LTW, coordinate
v=t+x
), it is advantageous to employ a unified formalism using a direction parameter
\chi=\pm 1
. We define a single light-cone coordinate
w=t-\chi x
, where
\chi=1
corresponds to
u
and
\chi=-1
corresponds to
v
. The field operator
\Phi_{\chi}(w)
is expanded in Unruh modes as:

\displaystyle\Phi_{\chi}(w)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\Omega\Bigg\{

\displaystyle\Big(\theta(w)f(\Omega)w^{i\Omega}+\theta(-w)f(-\Omega)(-w)^{i\Omega}\Big)A_{\chi,\Omega}

\displaystyle+\Big(\theta(w)f(\Omega)w^{-i\Omega}+\theta(-w)f(-\Omega)(-w)^{-i\Omega}\Big)A_{\chi,\Omega}^{\dagger}\Bigg\},

(2)

where
A_{1,\Omega}\equiv A_{\Omega}
(RTW annihilation operator) and
A_{-1,\Omega}\equiv B_{\Omega}
(LTW annihilation operator). They satisfy
[A_{\chi,\Omega},A_{\chi^{\prime},\Omega^{\prime}}^{\dagger}]=\delta_{\chi\chi^{\prime}}\delta(\Omega-\Omega^{\prime})
. The normalization factor is given by

f(\Omega)=\frac{e^{-\pi\Omega/2}}{\sqrt{8\pi|\Omega|\sinh(\pi|\Omega|)}}.

(3)

The detector follows a trajectory of constant proper acceleration
a
in the right Rindler wedge (
u<0,v>0
), given by
u(\tau)=-(1/a)e^{-a\tau}
and
v(\tau)=(1/a)e^{a\tau}
. In the unified coordinate, this is
-\chi aw=e^{-\chi a\tau}
. Substituting this into the field expansion, the field along the detector’s worldline becomes:

\displaystyle\Phi_{\chi}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\Omega f(-\chi\Omega)\Big(a^{-i\Omega}e^{-i\chi a\Omega\tau}A_{\chi,\Omega}+a^{i\Omega}e^{i\chi a\Omega\tau}A_{\chi,\Omega}^{\dagger}\Big).

(4)

Since we are interested in photon emission from the Minkowski vacuum
\ket{0_M}
, only the creation operator terms
A_{\chi,\Omega}^{\dagger}
will contribute.

2.2
The Two-Photon Process Revisited

Before tackling the general
n
-photon case, we briefly review the second-order, two-photon emission process using the unified formalism. The detector starts in
\ket{g}
and returns to
\ket{g}
, so the process is described by the second-order term in the Dyson series. The final state is a superposition of four channels,
\ket{\Psi_f}=\sum_{\chi_{1},\chi_{2}}\ket{\Psi_f}_{\chi_{1}\chi_{2}}
, corresponding to RR, LL, RL, and LR emissions. A single channel is given by:

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_f^{(2)}}_{\chi_{1}\chi_{2}}=\frac{ig^{2}}{4\hbar^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\Omega\frac{\Omega}{\left(\frac{\omega}{a}-\chi_{1}\Omega\right)}\frac{a^{i\Omega(1-\chi_{1}\chi_{2})}}{\sinh(\pi\Omega)}A^{\dagger}_{\chi_{1},\Omega}A^{\dagger}_{\chi_{2},-\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\Omega}\ket{0_M}\ket{g}\,.

(5)

This result, derived in detail in
Azizi2025TPE
, shows that the two emitted photons have correlated Unruh frequencies. The resonant denominator
(\omega/a-\chi_{1}\Omega)
clearly shows the process is peaked when the Unruh frequency matches the detector gap, a hallmark of the Unruh effect.

3
n^{\text{th}}
Order Emission Processes

3.1
Even-Order Processes:
g\to g

We first generalize to an
n
-photon emission where
n
is even. This process returns the detector to its ground state. The
n^{\text{th}}
order contribution to the final state is given by the time-ordered integral:

\ket{\Psi_f^{(n)}}=\left(\frac{-i}{\hbar}\right)^{n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau_{1}\dots\int_{-\infty}^{\tau_{n-1}}d\tau_{n}H_{\text{int}}(\tau_{1})\dots H_{\text{int}}(\tau_{n})\ket{0_M}\ket{g}.

(6)

The sequence of detector operators must be
\sigma,\sigma^{\dagger},\sigma,\dots,\sigma^{\dagger}
acting from left to right, corresponding to time evolution from
\tau_{n}
to
\tau_{1}
. For a specific channel
\{\chi_{j}\}
, the state is:

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_f^{(n)}}_{\{\chi_{j}\}}=\left(\frac{-ig}{\hbar}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\tau_{1}\dots\int_{-\infty}^{\tau_{n-1}}d\tau_{n}\left(\frac{\partial\Phi_{\chi_{1}}^{(-)}(\tau_{1})}{\partial\tau_{1}}e^{-i\omega\tau_{1}}\right)\left(\frac{\partial\Phi_{\chi_{2}}^{(-)}(\tau_{2})}{\partial\tau_{2}}e^{i\omega\tau_{2}}\right)\dots

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\frac{\partial\Phi_{\chi_{n}}^{(-)}(\tau_{n})}{\partial\tau_{n}}e^{i\omega\tau_{n}}\right)\ket{g}\ket{0_M},

(7)

where
\Phi^{(-)}
denotes the negative frequency contribution of the field which contains the creation operator. Substituting the field expansion (Eq.
4
) and performing the derivatives
\partial/\partial\tau_{j}
brings down a factor of
ia\chi_{j}\Omega_{j}
. The expression becomes a series of nested integrals over both time and frequency.

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_f^{(n)}}=\left(\frac{ga}{\hbar}\right)^{n}\int d\Omega_{1}\dots d\Omega_{n}

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\chi_{j}\Omega_{j}f(-\chi_{j}\Omega_{j})a^{i\Omega_{j}}A^{\dagger}_{\chi_{j},\Omega_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau_{1}e^{iE_{1}\tau_{1}}\int_{-\infty}^{\tau_{1}}d\tau_{2}e^{iE_{2}\tau_{2}}\dots\int_{-\infty}^{\tau_{n-1}}d\tau_{n}e^{iE_{n}\tau_{n}},

(8)

where the energy term for each vertex is
E_{j}=a\chi_{j}\Omega_{j}+(-1)^{j}\omega
, for
j=1,\dots,n
.

The key step is the evaluation of the time-ordered integrals. Performing the innermost integral over
\tau_{n}
yields
\frac{e^{iE_{n}\tau_{n-1}}}{iE_{n}}
. The next integral over
\tau_{n-1}
is then

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\tau_{n-2}}d\tau_{n-1}e^{i(E_{n-1}+E_{n})\tau_{n-1}}=\frac{e^{i(E_{n-1}+E_{n})\tau_{n-2}}}{i(E_{n-1}+E_{n})}.

(9)

Repeating this procedure recursively for all
n-1
nested integrals, we obtain a product of energy denominators:

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau_{1}\dots\int_{-\infty}^{\tau_{n-1}}d\tau_{n}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}e^{iE_{j}\tau_{j}}\right)=\frac{2\pi\delta(\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{j})}{\prod_{k=2}^{n}\left(i\sum_{j=k}^{n}E_{j}\right)}.

(10)

Since
n
is even, the detector energy terms cancel in the delta function,
\sum(-1)^{j}\omega=0
, and the conservation law simplifies to
a\sum_{j=1}^{n}\chi_{j}\Omega_{j}=0
.

The overall amplitude of the
n
-photon process includes a product of the Unruh mode normalization factors. We can evaluate this term by substituting the definition of
f(\Omega)
from Eq. (
3
):

\prod_{i=1}^{n}f(-\chi_{i}\Omega_{i})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{e^{\frac{\pi}{2}\chi_{i}\Omega_{i}}}{\sqrt{8\pi(-\chi_{i}\Omega_{i})\sinh(-\pi\chi_i\Omega_i)}}=\frac{e^{\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{i}\Omega_{i}}}{(8\pi)^{n/2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sqrt{-\chi_{i}\Omega_{i}\sinh(-\pi\chi_i\Omega_i)}}.

The expression in the denominator can be simplified by using the identity
-\Omega\sinh(-\pi\Omega)=\Omega\sinh(\pi\Omega)
. Applying this to each term in the product, we obtain the simplified result:

\prod_{i=1}^{n}f(-\chi_{i}\Omega_{i})=\frac{e^{\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{i}\Omega_{i}}}{(8\pi)^{n/2}\sqrt{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Omega_{i}\sinh(\pi\Omega_i)}}.

This expression reveals how the thermal factor, contained in the exponential, is separated from the rest of the mode normalization. For even-
n
processes where
\sum\chi_{i}\Omega_{i}=0
, this exponential term becomes unity.

Finally using delta function to eliminate the integral over
\Omega_{1}
, we arrive at the analytical expression for the
n
-photon state in a specific channel:

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_f^{(n)}}_{\{\chi_{j}\}}=C_{n}\int

\displaystyle d\Omega_{2}\cdots d\Omega_{n}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\chi_{j}\Omega_{j}a^{i\Omega_{j}}}{\sqrt{|\Omega_{j}|\sinh(\pi|\Omega_j|)}}A^{\dagger}_{\chi_{j},\Omega_{j}}\right)_{\Omega_{1}=-\chi_{1}\sum_{k=2}^{n}\chi_{k}\Omega_{k}}

(11)

\displaystyle\times\left[\prod_{k=2}^{n}\frac{1}{a\sum_{j=k}^{n}\chi_{j}\Omega_{j}+\omega\sum_{j=k}^{n}(-1)^{j}}\right]\ket{0_M}\ket{g},

where

\displaystyle C_{n}=2\pi i\left(\frac{g}{\hbar}\right)^{n}\frac{a^{n-1}}{(-8\pi)^{n/2}}.

(12)

3.2
Odd-Order Processes:
g\to e

We now consider the case where
n
is odd. This process is physically distinct as it leaves the detector in the excited state
\ket{e}
. The operator sequence must be
\sigma^{\dagger},\sigma,\dots,\sigma^{\dagger}
from left to right, corresponding to the time-ordered sequence where the first interaction (at time
\tau_{n}
) is an excitation. The
n^{\text{th}}
order state is

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_f^{(n)}}=\left(\frac{-ig}{\hbar}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\tau_{1}\frac{\partial\Phi^{(-)}_{\chi_{1}}}{\partial\tau_{1}}e^{i\omega\tau_{1}}\sigma^{\dagger}\int_{-\infty}^{\tau_{1}}d\tau_{2}\frac{\partial\Phi^{(-)}_{\chi_{2}}}{\partial\tau_{2}}e^{-i\omega\tau_{2}}\sigma\dots

\displaystyle\times\int_{-\infty}^{\tau_{n-1}}d\tau_{n}\frac{\partial\Phi^{(-)}_{\chi_{n}}}{\partial\tau_{n}}e^{i\omega\tau_{n}}\sigma^{\dagger}\ket{0_M}\ket{g}.

(13)

The calculation proceeds similarly to the even case, but with a crucial difference in the energy conservation law. The energy term for each vertex is now
E_{j}=a\chi_{j}\Omega_{j}+(-1)^{j+1}\omega
. The sum of detector energy terms,
\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j+1}\omega
, is now equal to
+\omega
. Consequently, the Dirac delta function resulting from the final time integral enforces a different conservation law:

a\sum_{j=1}^{n}\chi_{j}\Omega_{j}+\omega=0.

(14)

This energy conservation condition fundamentally changes the character of the emission, as it directly links the collective energy of the emitted Unruh modes to the detector’s energy gap. The most profound consequence of this linkage appears when we evaluate the product of the Unruh mode normalization factors,
\prod f(-\chi_{i}\Omega_{i})
. The calculation proceeds as follows:

\prod_{i=1}^{n}f(-\chi_{i}\Omega_{i})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{e^{\frac{\pi}{2}\chi_{i}\Omega_{i}}}{\sqrt{8\pi(-\chi_{i}\Omega_{i})\sinh(-\pi\chi_i\Omega_i)}}=\frac{e^{\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{i}\Omega_{i}}}{(8\pi)^{n/2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sqrt{-\chi_{i}\Omega_{i}\sinh(-\pi\chi_i\Omega_i)}}.

The expression in the denominator can be simplified by using the identity
-\Omega\sinh(-\pi\Omega)=\Omega\sinh(\pi\Omega)
. Applying this to each term in the product, we obtain the simplified result:

\prod_{i=1}^{n}f(-\chi_{i}\Omega_{i})=\frac{e^{\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{i}\Omega_{i}}}{(8\pi)^{n/2}\sqrt{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Omega_{i}\sinh(\pi\Omega_i)}}.

This expression reveals how a thermal factor, contained in the exponential, is separated from the rest of the mode normalization. For odd-
n
processes, the overall energy conservation enforces the condition
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{i}\Omega_{i}=-\omega/a
. Substituting this into the exponential term yields the celebrated Boltzmann-like suppression factor characteristic of the Unruh effect:

\exp\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\chi_{j}\Omega_{j}\right)=\exp\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\left(-\frac{\omega}{a}\right)\right)=e^{-\frac{\pi\omega}{2a}}.

(15)

Therefore, after performing the time-ordered integration and substituting the energy conservation condition, the final state for an odd-
n
process is found to be suppressed by this key thermal factor.

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_f^{(n)}}_{\{\chi_{j}\}}=C^{\prime}_{n}e^{-\frac{\pi\omega}{2a}}\int

\displaystyle d\Omega_{2}\cdots d\Omega_{n}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\chi_{j}\Omega_{j}a^{i\Omega_{j}}}{\sqrt{|\Omega_{j}|\sinh(\pi|\Omega_j|)}}A^{\dagger}_{\chi_{j},\Omega_{j}}\right)_{\Omega_{1}=-\chi_{1}\left(\frac{\omega}{a}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}\chi_{i}\Omega_{i}\right)}

(16)

\displaystyle\times\left[\prod_{k=2}^{n}\frac{1}{a\sum_{j=k}^{n}\chi_{j}\Omega_{j}+\omega\sum_{j=k}^{n}(-1)^{j+1}}\right]\ket{0_M}\ket{e},

where the prefactor is given by

\displaystyle C^{\prime}_{n}=2\pi\left(\frac{g}{\hbar}\right)^{n}\frac{a^{n-1}}{(8\pi)^{n/2}}(-1)^{\frac{n-1}{2}},

(17)

and the delta function enforces the condition

\Omega_{1}=-\chi_{1}\left(\frac{\omega}{a}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}\chi_{i}\Omega_{i}\right).

The key physical result is that any process that excites the detector is exponentially suppressed by a factor determined by the ratio of the detector gap to the acceleration.

Initial State Dependence and Thermal Detailed Balance

The thermal nature of the Unruh effect can be further confirmed by considering processes that begin with the detector in its excited state,
\ket{e}
. Let’s analyze how the calculation changes. If the initial state is
\ket{e}
, the first interaction at the earliest time
\tau_{n}
must be a de-excitation, involving the operator
\sigma e^{-i\omega\tau_{n}}
. The subsequent operators must then alternate to return the detector to a final state.

A careful analysis of the operator ordering shows that the amplitude for any
n^{\text{th}}

\ket{e}
is identical to the corresponding process starting from
\ket{g}
, but with the simple substitution
\omega\to-\omega
.

This substitution does not dramatically alter the picture for even
n
processes (which map
\ket{e}\to\ket{e}
), as
\omega
only appears in the denominators. However, it drastically changes the physics for odd-
n
transitions, which connect the ground and excited states.

1.

Excitation (
g\to e
,
n
odd):
As shown in Eq. (
16
), the amplitude for this process contains the exponential factor
e^{-\pi\omega/2a}
. This represents an Unruh-like thermal excitation, which is suppressed for large energy gaps or small accelerations.

2.

De-excitation (
e\to g
,
n
odd):
For this process, we start with
\ket{e}
and make the substitution
\omega\to-\omega
. The exponential factor in the amplitude becomes
e^{-\pi(-\omega)/2a}=e^{+\pi\omega/2a}

5
Analysis of N-Photon Processes

The n-photon states derived in the previous sections contain a rich physical structure, particularly within the energy denominators of the amplitudes. The processes are sharply peaked when these denominators approach zero, revealing the resonant conditions for multi-photon emission. Furthermore, the overall energy conservation, enforced by the Dirac delta function, dictates both the correlations between the emitted photons and the thermal character of the interaction.

5.1
Resonance Structure

The resonant behavior is governed by the product of denominators,
\mathcal{D}
, which takes a different form for even and odd-
n
processes:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\text{even}}=

\displaystyle\prod_{k=2}^{n}\left(a\sum_{j=k}^{n}\chi_{j}\Omega_{j}+\omega\sum_{j=k}^{n}(-1)^{j}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\text{odd}}=

\displaystyle\prod_{k=2}^{n}\left(a\sum_{j=k}^{n}\chi_{j}\Omega_{j}+\omega\sum_{j=k}^{n}(-1)^{j+1}\right).

(19)

Analyzing the structure of these products reveals two distinct classes of resonances.

Detector-Mediated Resonances

This class of resonance involves denominators containing the detector’s energy gap,
\omega
. They correspond to points in the multi-step process where the detector’s internal state changes, directly mediating energy transfer with the field. For example, in the even-
n
case, the term for
k=n
gives the denominator
(a\chi_{n}\Omega_{n}+\omega)
, which is singular when
\Omega_{n}=-\omega/(a\chi_{n})
. This is the familiar condition for the initial Unruh excitation, where the detector absorbs a quantum of energy from the vacuum fluctuations.

Field-Mediated Resonances

For even-
n
processes with
n>2
, a new class of resonances appears that is independent of
\omega
. For instance, in the four-photon case (
n=4
), the denominator for
k=3
is simply
(a(\chi_{3}\Omega_{3}+\chi_{4}\Omega_{4}))
. This term leads to a resonance when
\Omega_{4}=-\chi_{3}\chi_{4}\Omega_{3}
. This represents a purely field-mediated process where a pair of photons is created in a correlated state without direct energy exchange with the detector’s internal levels. It suggests that the detector acts as a catalyst, enabling the Minkowski vacuum to radiate multi-particle states whose correlations are governed by the geometric properties of the accelerated frame itself.

5.2

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we have developed a comprehensive framework for
n^{\text{th}}
order quantum processes involving a uniformly accelerated UDW detector. By solving the time-ordered Dyson series exactly, we have derived analytical formulas for the final
n
-photon quantum states for both even- and odd-order transitions.

Our results provide a multi-faceted confirmation and extension of our understanding of the Unruh effect. The analysis of even-order processes revealed new field-mediated resonances, indicating that an accelerating detector can catalyze complex particle creation events beyond simple excitation/de-excitation. The analysis of odd-order processes demonstrated the characteristic thermal suppression factor for Unruh excitation. Most significantly, by comparing excitation and de-excitation processes, we have shown that the principle of detailed balance holds at arbitrary order, providing a robust verification of the thermal nature of the accelerated vacuum.

This work lays the foundation for several future avenues of research. The formalism can be applied to study specific multipartite entanglement witnesses, explore the connection to non-thermal features of the Unruh effect at higher orders, and potentially be extended to detectors with more complex internal structure or to curved spacetime backgrounds. Ultimately, by moving beyond leading-order effects, we gain a more complete picture of the profound ways in which acceleration shapes the quantum vacuum.

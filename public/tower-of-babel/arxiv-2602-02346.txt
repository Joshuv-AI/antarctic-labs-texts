Population size of critical Galton-Watson processes under small deviations and infinite variance Thanks: This work was supported by the Russian Science Foundation under grant no.24-11-00037 https://rscf.ru/en/project/24-11-00037/
Vladimir Vatutin, Elena Dyakonova

Abstract
We study the evolution of the population size distribution of a critical Galton-Watson process with infinite variance of the offspring size of particles assuming that the population size is unusually small at the distant moment n n of observation. Key words :Galton-Watson branching process, infinite variance, evolution of population AMS subject classification : Primary 60J80, Secondary 60E07



1
Introduction

We consider a Galton-Watson branching process
\mathcal{Z}=\left\{Z(n),n\geq 0\right\}
starting at moment 0. Let
f(s):=\mathbf{E}s^{\xi}
be the probability generating
function of the offspring number
\xi
of one particle. We assume that the
reproduction law of particles satisfies the condition

\mathbf{E}\xi=1,\text{ }f(s)=s+\left(1-s\right)^{1+\alpha}L(1-s),\quad 0\leq s\leq 1,

(1)

where
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
L(z)
is a slowly varying function as
z\downarrow 0
. Denote
f_{0}(s):=s
and introduce iterations of
f(s)
by
the equalities

f_{n}(s)=f(f_{n-1}(s)),\ n=1,2,....

It is known
[
9
]
that, given (
1
)

(1-f_{n}(0))^{\alpha}L\left(1-f_{n}(0)\right)\sim\frac{1}{\alpha n}

(2)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
and, therefore (see, for instance,
[
7
, Section
1.5, point
5^{0}
]
or
[
6
]
)

Q(n):=\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n)>0|Z(0)=1\right)=1-f_{n}(0)\sim\frac{L^{\ast}(n)}{n^{1/\alpha}}\text{ \ \ as \ \ }n\rightarrow\infty,

(3)

where
L^{\ast}(n)
is a function slowly varying at infinity. Moreover, for
any
\lambda>0

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-\lambda(1-f_{n}(0))Z(n)}|Z(n)>0,Z(0)=1\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda x}dM\left(x\right)

(4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{\left(1+\lambda^{-\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha}},

where
M\left(x\right)
is a proper nondegenerate distribution such that
M(x)>0
for any
x>0
.

It follows from (
4
) that, for any
x>0

\mathbf{P}\left(0<(1-f_{n}(0))Z(n)\leq x|Z(0)=1\right)\approx M(x)\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n)>0|Z(0)=1\right)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Thus, the typical size of the population survived
up to a distant moment
n
is of order
(1-f_{n}(0))^{-1}
.

In the sequel we assume (if otherwise is not stated) that
Z(0)=1
and study
the asymptotic behavior of the population size of the critical Galton-Watson
branching process given the event

\mathcal{H}(l,r):=\big\{0<(1-f_{r}(0))Z(l)\leq 1\big\}

for various assumptions concerning the ratio between the parameters
l
and
r
. We mainly deal with the case

\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n)):=\big\{0<(1-f_{\varphi(n)}(0))Z(n)\leq 1\big\}

(5)

where
\varphi(n),\ n=1,2,...,
is a deterministic function such that

\varphi(n)\rightarrow\infty\text{ and }\frac{\varphi(n)}{n}\rightarrow 0

(6)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Our main result looks as follows.

Theorem 1

Let conditions (
1
) be valid for
\alpha\in(0,1)
and assumption (
6
) hold true. Then, for any
\lambda>0

1) if
m\rightarrow\infty
and
m=o(n)
then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-\lambda(1-f_{m}(0))Z(m)}|\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n))\right]=\frac{1}{\left(1+\lambda^{\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha+1}};

2) if
m\sim\theta n
for some
\theta\in(0,1)
then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-\lambda(1-f_{m}(0))Z(m)}|\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n))\right]=\frac{1}{\left(1-\theta+\left(\lambda\left(1-\theta\right)^{1/\alpha}+\theta^{1/\alpha}\right)^{\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha+1}};

3) if
m=n-\psi(n),
where
n\gg\psi(n)\gg\varphi(n),
then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-\lambda(1-f_{\psi(n)}(0))Z(m)}|\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n))\right]=\frac{1}{\left(1+\lambda\right)^{\alpha+1}};

4) if
m=n-y\varphi(n),
where
y\in(0,\infty),
then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-\lambda(1-f_{n-m}(0))Z(m)}|\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n))\right]=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha\Gamma\left(j+\alpha\right)}{j!\left(1+\lambda\right)^{\alpha+j}}yM^{\ast j}(y^{-1/\alpha}),

where
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the standard Gamma-function and
M^{\ast j}(x)

is the
j
-th convolution of
M(x)
with itself;

5) if
m=n-\chi(n)
where
n\gg\varphi(n)\gg\chi(n)\geq 0
then

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-\lambda\left(1-f_{\varphi(n)}(0)\right)Z(m)}|\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n))\right]=\alpha\int_{0}^{1}x^{\alpha-1}e^{-\lambda x}dx.

In particular, if
T\rightarrow\infty
and
T=o\left((1-f_{n}(0))^{-1}\right)
then

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n)<xT|0<Z(n)\leq T\right)=x^{\alpha},\quad 0\leq x\leq 1.

For the first sight condition (
5
) looks rather restrictive and
unnatural. Let us explain that this is not the case. We know that if
Z(n)>0

then the population size of a critical Galton-Watson process at a distant
moment
n
is of order
1/\left(1-f_{n}(0)\right)
according to the now
classical Yaglom-Slack result (
4
). Thus, if we would like to
consider a nontypically small population size at moment
n,
given
Z(n)>0

we need to impose the assumption

0<(1-f_{\varphi(n)}(0))Z(n)\leq w,

(7)

where
w>0
and
\varphi(n)=o(n)
. In view of (
3
) and
properties of slowly varying functions we have, for any
w>0

\frac{1-f_{\varphi(n)}(0)}{w}\sim\frac{L^{\ast}(\varphi(n))}{w\varphi^{1/\alpha}(n)}\sim\frac{L^{\ast}(w^{\alpha}\varphi(n))}{\left(w^{\alpha}\varphi(n)\right)^{1/\alpha}}\sim 1-f_{w^{\alpha}\varphi(n)}(0).

Therefore, we may replace condition (
7
) by the condition

0<(1-f_{w^{\alpha}\varphi(n)}(0))Z(n)\leq 1.

Now observe that the replacement
\varphi(n)\rightarrow w^{\alpha}\varphi(n)
keeps the validity of the assumption (
6
) and, as a result,
the validity of all conclusions of Theorem
1
.

We do not mention in Theorem
1
the
case
\alpha=1.
See the next section for additional commentaries concerning this case.

2
Auxiliary results

The proof of Theorem
1
is based on the following
statements established in
[
10
]
.

Theorem 2

Let condition (
1
) be valid for
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\varphi(n),\ n=1,2,...,
be a deterministic function
satisfying assumption (
6
). Then

\mathbf{P}\left(\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n))\right)\sim\frac{1-f_{n}(0)}{\alpha n}\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(1+\alpha\right)}\varphi(n).

(8)

Remark 1

Setting
T=\left(1-f_{\varphi(n)}(0)\right)^{-1}
and
observing that

\frac{L\left(1/T\right)}{T^{\alpha}}=\left(1-f_{\varphi(n)}(0)\right)^{\alpha}L\left(1-f_{\varphi(n)}(0)\right)\sim\frac{1}{\alpha\varphi(n)}

as
n\rightarrow\infty
by (
2
), we may rewrite (
8
) as

\mathbf{P}\left(0<Z(n)\leq T\right)\sim\frac{1-f_{n}(0)}{\alpha n}\frac{1}{\alpha\Gamma\left(1+\alpha\right)}\frac{T^{\alpha}}{L\left(1/T\right)}.

(9)

Remark 2

If

f^{\prime}(1)=1,\quad\sigma^{2}=f^{\prime\prime}(1)\in(0,\infty),

(10)

then
L(x)\to\sigma^{2}/2
as
x\downarrow 0
and (see, for instance,

[
1
]
, Ch.1, Sec. 9)

1-f_{n}(0)\sim\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}n},\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

Hence it follows that if
n\gg\varphi(n)\rightarrow\infty
then (
8
) is replaced by

\mathbf{P}\left(0<Z(n)<T\right)\sim\frac{4}{\sigma^{4}n^{2}}T,

and is an evident corollary of the main result in
[
5
]
.

If, however,
\alpha=1
and
\sigma^{2}=\infty
, then an asymptotic representation for
\mathbf{P}\left(0<Z(n)\leq T\right)
similar to (
9
) is now know only for the case

\mathbf{P}\left(\xi\geq j\right)=\frac{\bar{L}(1/j)}{j^{2}},

(11)

where
\bar{L}(z)
is a function slowly varying at zero (see Remark 2 in
[
10
]
).
Since in the arguments to follow we use only the form of the right-hand side of (
9
),
one may check that all the statements of Theorem
1
remain valid if either the condition (
10
) or the conditin (
11
) is in force.

We fix
n\in\left\{1,2,...\right\}
and introduce the so-called reduced
Galton-Watson process
\{Z(m,n),0\leq m\leq n\}
, where
Z(m,n)
is the
number of such generation
m
particles in the original process
\mathcal{Z}

that have nonempty offspring in generation
n
(with the agreement that
Z(n,n)=Z(n))
.

Theorem 3

(see
[
10
]
) Let condition (
1
) be
valid for
\alpha\in(0,1)
and assumption (
6
) hold true. Then,
for any
y\in(0,\infty)
and any
j\geq 1

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n-y\varphi(n),n)=j|\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n))\right)=\frac{\alpha\Gamma\left(j+\alpha\right)}{j!}yM^{\ast j}(y^{-1/\alpha}).

(12)

Let

\beta(n):=\max\left(0\leq m<n:Z(m,n)=1\right)

be the birth moment of the most recent common ancestor (MRCA) of all
particles existing in the population at moment
n
and let
d(n):=n-\beta(n)
be the distance from the moment
n
of observation to the birth moment
of the MRCA. The following statement describing the distribution of the
random variable
d(n)
is an evident corollary of Theorem
3
.
This claim plays an important role in our subsequent arguments.

Corollary 1

Under the conditions of Theorem
3
, for any
y\in(0,\infty)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n-y\varphi(n),n)=1|\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n))\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(d(n)\leq y\varphi(n)|\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n))\right)=\alpha\Gamma(1+\alpha)yM(y^{-1/\alpha}).

It follows from (
4
) that

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda y}dM\left(x\right)=1-\frac{1}{\left(1+\lambda^{-\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha}}\sim\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{1}{\lambda^{a}},\

as
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
. Hence we conclude by a Tauberian theorem
(see, for instance,
[
3
, Ch.XIII, Sec.5, Theorems 2 and 3]
) that

M\left(x\right)\sim\frac{1}{\alpha\Gamma\left(1+\alpha\right)}x^{\alpha}

as
x\rightarrow 0
. Thus,

\lim_{y\rightarrow\infty}\alpha\Gamma\left(1+\alpha\right)yM(y^{-1/\alpha})=1.

(13)

This fact combined with Corollary
1
shows that, given
\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n))
the distance to the MRCA of all individuals of the
n
-th
generation is of order
\varphi(n)
. We will use this result later on.

We need one more statement related to the MRCA. Namely, we show that the
limiting distribution specified by the right-hand side of (
12
) is
proper.

Lemma 1

For any
y>0

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha\Gamma\left(j+\alpha\right)}{j!}yM^{\ast j}(y^{-1/\alpha})=1.

Proof
. The statement of the lemma will be proved if we show that,
for any
x>0

U(x):=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha\Gamma\left(j+\alpha\right)}{j!}M^{\ast j}(x)=x^{\alpha},

(14)

or, since
U(\cdot)
is a measure, that (see
[
3
]
, Ch. XIII, Sec.1,
Theorem 1a), for any
\lambda>0

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha\Gamma\left(j+\alpha\right)}{j!}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda x}dM^{\ast j}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda x}dx^{\alpha}=\frac{\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)}{\lambda^{\alpha}}.

(15)

It follows from (
4
) that

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda x}dM^{\ast j}(x)=\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{\left(1+\lambda^{\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha}}\right)^{j}.

Now we set

1-\frac{\lambda}{\left(1+\lambda^{\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha}}=t.

(16)

It is not difficult to check that

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha\Gamma\left(j+\alpha\right)}{j!}t^{j}=\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)\left(\frac{1}{\left(1-t\right)^{\alpha}}-1\right).

(17)

On the other hand, resolving (
16
) with respect to
\lambda^{\alpha}
we get

\lambda^{-\alpha}=\frac{1}{\left(1-t\right)^{\alpha}}-1.

(18)

Combination of (
16
), (
17
) and (
18
) proves (
15
) and, therefore, (
14
).

Lemma
1
is proved.

We note that under conditions (
10
) a statement similar to
Theorem
3
was proved in
[
4
]
.

For further references we would like to mention that if
mn^{-1}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
then it follows
from (
3
) and properties of slowly varying functions that

\frac{1-f_{n}(0)}{1-f_{m}(0)}\rightarrow 0

(19)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Indeed, since the function
L^{*}(n)
from (
3
) is
slowly varying, there exists an
a>0
and functions
\,\gamma(x)
and

\,\theta(x)
satisfying
\,\gamma(x)\rightarrow\gamma\in\left(0,\infty\right)
and
\,\theta(x)\rightarrow 0
as

x\uparrow\infty,
such that (see
[
7
, Section 1.5]
)

L^{*}(x)=\ \gamma(n)\exp\!\Big[\int_{a}^{n}\frac{\theta(x)}{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\Big].

Hence, it follows easily that for any
\epsilon>0,
there exists

\,w=w(\epsilon)
such that

\Big(\frac{n}{m}\Big)^{-\epsilon}\,\leq\,\frac{L^{*}(n)}{L^{*}(m)}\leq\Big(\frac{n}{m}\Big)^{\epsilon}\quad\text{as }\,m/n\leq w.

Evidently, these estimates combined with (
3
) imply (
19
).

3
Population size

We now prove Theorem
1
. It follows from Corollary
1
and (
13
) that if
n-m\gg\varphi(n)\rightarrow\infty

then

\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-\lambda Z(m)/T}|\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n))\right]=\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-\lambda Z(m)/T};Z(m,n)=1|\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n))\right]+o(1)

(20)

for any
T>0
and any
\lambda
with
Re\lambda>0
. Further we note that
if
Z(m,n)=1
then all particles of the
n
-th generation are descendants of
the only particle of the
m
-th generation. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-\lambda Z(m)/T};Z(m,n)=1,\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n))\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad=\mathbf{E}\left[Z(m)e^{-\lambda Z(m)/T}f_{n-m}^{Z(m)-1}(0)\right]\mathbf{P}\left(\mathcal{H}(n-m,\varphi(n))\right).

Hence, using Theorem
2
we conclude that if condition (
6
) holds and
n-m\gg\varphi(n)\rightarrow\infty
then

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-\lambda Z(m)/T}|\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n))\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathbf{E}\left[Z(m)e^{-\lambda Z(m)/T}f_{n-m}^{Z(m)-1}(0)\right]\frac{\mathbf{P}\left(\mathcal{H}(n-m,\varphi(n))\right)}{\mathbf{P}\left(\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n))\right)}+o(1)

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathbf{E}\left[Z(m)e^{-\lambda Z(m)/T}f_{n-m}^{Z(m)-1}(0)\right]\frac{1-f_{n-m}(0)}{1-f_{n}(0)}\frac{n}{n-m}+o(1).

(21)

We will use (
21
) to prove points 1-3 of Theorem
1
.

3.1
The distribution of
Z(m)
given
m=o(n)
and
n\gg\varphi(n)

We set
T^{-1}:=1-f_{m}(0)
and, observing that

\frac{1-f_{n-m}(0)}{1-f_{n}(0)}\frac{n}{n-m}\rightarrow 1

if
m=o(n)
and
n\rightarrow\infty,
rewrite (
21
) as

\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-\lambda Z(m)/T}|\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n))\right]=(1+o(1))\mathbf{E}\left[Z(m)e^{-\lambda Z(m)/T}f_{n-m}^{Z(m)-1}(0)\right]+o(1).

Further,

\mathbf{E}\left[Z(m)e^{-\lambda Z(m)/T}f_{n-m}^{Z(m)-1}(0)\right]=\frac{1}{f_{n-m}(0)}\mathbf{E}\left[Z(m)e^{-(\lambda-T\log f_{n-m}(0))Z(m)/T}\right].

It view of the equivalence
-\log f_{n-m}(0)\sim 1-f_{n-m}(0)
as
n-m\rightarrow\infty
and the relation

T\left(1-f_{n-m}(0)\right)=\frac{1-f_{n-m}(0)}{1-f_{m}(0)}\rightarrow 0

valid according to (
3
) if
m=o(n),
we conclude that, for
any
\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f_{n-m}(0)}\mathbf{E}\left[Z(m)e^{-\left(\lambda+\varepsilon\right)Z(m)/T}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[Z(m)e^{-\lambda Z(m)/T}f_{n-m}^{Z(m)-1}(0)\right]

(22)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[Z(m)e^{-\lambda Z(m)/T}\right]

for sufficiently large
n
and
m
. We know by (
4
) that

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-\lambda(1-f_{m}(0))Z(m)}|Z(m)>0\right]=1-\frac{1}{\left(1+\lambda^{-\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha}}

(23)

for all
\lambda>0
. Since the prelimiting functions at the left-hand side
of (
23
) are analytic functions in the domain
Re\lambda>0
and the
function

G(\lambda):=1-\frac{1}{\left(1+\lambda^{-\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha}}

admits an analytic continuation in the same domain if we take the branch
with
G(1)=1-2^{-1/\alpha}
, it follows from the uniqueness theorem for analytic
functions that (
23
) holds for this branch for all
\lambda
satisfying
Re\lambda>0
. This, in turn, implies that the first derivatives of the preliminary
functions converge to the first derivative of the selected branch
G(\lambda)
in the domain
Re\lambda>0.
In particular,

\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-\lambda\left(1-f_{m}(0)\right)Z(m)}|Z(m)>0\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\frac{\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-\lambda\left(1-f_{m}(0)\right)Z(m)}\right]-f_{m}(0)}{1-f_{m}(0)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{1-f_{m}(0)}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-\lambda\left(1-f_{m}(0)\right)Z(m)}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=-\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{E}\left[Z(m)e^{-\lambda\left(1-f_{m}(0)\right)Z(m)}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\left(1-\frac{1}{\left(1+\lambda^{-\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha}}\right)=-\frac{1}{\left(1+\lambda^{a}\right)^{1/\alpha+1}}

(24)

for any
\lambda>0
. Hence, using (
22
) and letting
\varepsilon
tend to zero we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\gg m\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-\lambda Z(m)/T}|\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n))\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\gg m\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{E}\left[Z(m)e^{-\lambda Z(m)/T}f_{n-m}^{Z(m)-1}(0)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(1+\lambda^{a}\right)^{1/\alpha+1}}.

This proves point 1) of Theorem
1
.

3.2
The distribution of
Z(m)
if
m\sim\theta n
for some
\theta\in(0,1)

In this section and in the sections to follow we consider the products of
the type
\theta n
and
(1-\theta)n
as
\left[\theta n\right]
and
\left[(1-\theta)n\right]
. Setting, as before,
T^{-1}:=1-f_{m}(0)
we
deduce by (
3
) that if
m\sim\theta n\rightarrow\infty

then

T\log f_{n-m}(0)\sim-\frac{1-f_{n-m}(0)}{1-f_{m}(0)}\sim-\left(\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}\right)^{1/\alpha}.

Observe now that relations (
20
) and (
21
) are still valid if
m\sim\theta n
for some
\theta\in(0,1)
. Using these relations we conclude that to prove point 2)
of Theorem
1
it is sufficient to find, for each
\lambda>0
the limit of the product

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[Z(m)e^{-(\lambda-T\log f_{n-m}(0))Z(m)/T}\right]\frac{\mathbf{P}\left(\mathcal{H}(n-m,\varphi(n))\right)}{\mathbf{P}\left(\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n))\right)}

\displaystyle\quad\sim\mathbf{E}\left[Z(m)e^{-\left(\lambda+\left(\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}\right)^{1/\alpha}\right)Z(m)(1-f_{m}(0))}\right]\frac{\mathbf{P}\left(\mathcal{H}(n-m,\varphi(n))\right)}{\mathbf{P}\left(\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n))\right)}

(25)

as
m\sim\theta n\rightarrow\infty
. Since

\frac{\mathbf{P}\left(\mathcal{H}(n-m,\varphi(n))\right)}{\mathbf{P}\left(\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n))\right)}\sim\frac{1-f_{n-m}(0)}{n-m}\frac{n}{1-f_{n}(0)}\sim\left(\frac{1}{1-\theta}\right)^{1/\alpha+1}

in view of Theorem
2
and (
3
), we deduce
by the last two lines in (
24
) and relation (
25
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\sim\theta n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-\lambda(1-f_{m}(0))Z(m)}|\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n))\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=\lim_{m\sim\theta n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{E}\left[Z(m)e^{-(\lambda-T\log f_{n-m}(0))Z(m)/T}\right]\frac{\mathbf{P}\left(\mathcal{H}(n-m,\varphi(n))\right)}{\mathbf{P}\left(\mathcal{H}(n,\varphi(n))\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\frac{1}{\left(1+\left(\lambda+\left(\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}\right)^{1/\alpha}\right)^{\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha+1}}\left(\frac{1}{1-\theta}\right)^{1/\alpha+1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\frac{1}{\left(1-\theta+\left(\lambda\left(1-\theta\right)^{1/\alpha}+\theta^{1/\alpha}\right)^{\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha+1}}

This proves point 2) of Theorem
1
.

3.3
The distribution of
Z(m)
given
m=n-\psi(n)
and
n\gg\psi(n)\gg\varphi(n)

We start this section by proving the following statement.

Lemma 2

Let condition (
1
) be valid and
\rho>0

be a fixed number. If
l\rightarrow\infty
then the parameter
r

satisfying the inequalities

1-f_{r+1}(0)\leq 1-f_{l}^{\rho}(0)<1-f_{r}(0)

(26)

has the asymptotic representation

r\sim l\rho^{-\alpha},\quad l\rightarrow\infty.

(27)

Proof
. In view of (
3
) and properties of slowly
varying functions

1-f_{l}^{\rho}(0)\sim\rho\left(1-f_{l}(0)\right)\sim 1-f_{l\rho^{-\alpha}}(0)

as
l\rightarrow\infty
. Hence it follows that if
r
satisfies (
26
) then

possess the following properties:

For any

B_{J,k}(ab,ab^{2},\ldots,ab^{J-k+1})=a^{k}b^{J}B_{J,k}(1,1,\ldots,1)

\displaystyle\left(f_{y\varphi(n)}^{1+\lambda}(0)\right)^{k}B_{J,k}\left(\log f_{y\varphi(n)}(0),\log^{2}f_{y\varphi(n)}(0),\ldots,\left(\log f_{y\varphi(n)}(0)\right)^{J-k+1}\right)

\displaystyle\left(f_{y\varphi(n)}^{1+\lambda}(0)\right)^{k}\left(\log f_{y\varphi(n)}(0)\right)^{J}B_{J,k}\left(1,1,\ldots,1\right),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{J-1}\frac{\left(\log f_{y\varphi(n)}(0)\right)^{J}f_{m}^{(k)}(f_{y\varphi(n)}^{1+\lambda}(0))}{y\varphi(n)\Delta\left(n\right)}S(J,k)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=\frac{\left(\log f_{y\varphi(n)}(0)\right)^{J}f_{m}^{(J)}(f_{y\varphi(n)}^{1+\lambda}(0))}{y\varphi(n)\Delta\left(n\right)}+O\left(\log f_{y\varphi(n)}(0)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\sim\frac{\left(-1\right)^{J}}{\left(1+\lambda\right)^{\alpha+J}}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+J)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. These estimates conclude the step of induction
and prove (
36

Now we have, for any fixed
y>0
and

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\frac{\left(1-f_{y\varphi(n)}(0)\right)^{j}}{j!}\frac{1}{f_{y\varphi(n)}^{\lambda j}(0)^{j}\left(\log f_{y\varphi(n)}(0)\right)^{j}}\frac{d^{j}}{d^{j}\lambda}f_{m}\left(f_{y\varphi(n)}^{1+\lambda}(0)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\sim\frac{\left(1-f_{y\varphi(n)}(0)\right)^{j}}{j!f_{y\varphi(n)}^{\lambda j}(0)}\left(-1\right)^{j}\frac{y\varphi(n)\Delta(n)}{\left(\log f_{y\varphi(n)}(0)\right)^{j}}\frac{\Gamma\left(j+\alpha\right)}{\Gamma\left(\alpha\right)}\frac{1}{\left(1+\lambda\right)^{\alpha+j}}

as

. Hence we conclude by
the Markov inequality that

\mathbf{P}(\left|Z(\chi)-j\right|>\delta j|Z(0)=j)\leq\frac{\mathbf{E}\left[\left|Z(\chi)-j\right|^{r}|Z(0)=j\right]}{\left(\delta j\right)^{r}}<\infty

for any
\delta>0
and
r\in(1,1+\alpha)
. Clearly,

Z(\chi)-j=\sum_{k=1}^{j}\varsigma_{k}(\chi),

where
\varsigma_{k}(\chi),k=1,2,...,j
are independent random variables
each of which is distributed as
\varsigma(\chi):=Z(\chi)-1
given
Z(0)=1
. Since
\mathbf{E}\varsigma(\chi)=0
, it follows by von Bahr-Esseen
inequality (see
[
2
]
) that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\left|Z(\chi)-1\right|^{r}|Z(0)=j\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2j\mathbf{E}\left[\left|Z(\chi)-1\right|^{r}|Z(0)=1\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2j(1+\mathbf{E}\left[Z^{r}(\chi)|Z(0)=1\right])

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4j\mathbf{E}\left[Z^{r}(\chi)|Z(0)=1\right].

It was show in Lemma 11 of
[
11
]
that, for any
r\in(1,1+\alpha)
there exists
C=C(r)\in\left(0,\infty\right)
such
that, for all
\chi\geq 1

\mathbf{E}\left[Z^{r}(\chi)|Z(0)=1\right]\leq\frac{C}{Q^{r-1}(\chi)}.

As a result

\mathbf{P}(\left|Z(\chi)-j\right|>\delta j|Z(0)=j)\leq\frac{4C}{\delta^{r}\left(jQ(\chi)\right)^{r-1}}.

According to (
19
)

jQ(\chi)\geq\varepsilon TQ(\chi)=\varepsilon\frac{1-f_{\chi}(0)}{1-f_{\varphi(n)}(0)}\rightarrow\infty.

Hence we conclude that

\mathbf{P}(\left|Z(\chi)-j\right|>\delta j|Z(0)=j)\rightarrow 0

uniformly in
j\in\left[\varepsilon T,NT\right]
. As a result,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=\varepsilon T}^{NT}e^{-\lambda j/T}\mathbf{P}\left(Z(m)=j\right)\mathbf{P}(0<Z(\chi)<T|Z(0)=j)

\displaystyle\,=\sum_{j=\varepsilon T}^{NT}e^{-\lambda j/T}\mathbf{P}\left(Z(m)=j\right)\mathbf{P}(0<Z(\chi)<T,\left|Z(\chi)-j\right|\leq\delta j|Z(0)=j)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+o\left(\mathbf{P}\left(0<Z(m)\leq NT\right)\right).

Now

\mathbf{P}(0<Z(\chi)<T,j-\delta j\leq Z(\chi)\leq j+\delta j|Z(0)=j)=0

if
T<j(1-\delta)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=\varepsilon T}^{NT}e^{-\lambda j/T}\mathbf{P}\left(Z(m)=j\right)\mathbf{P}(0<Z(\chi)<T,\left|Z(\chi)-j\right|\leq\delta j|Z(0)=j)

\displaystyle\,=\sum_{j=\varepsilon T}^{T/\left(1-\delta\right)}e^{-\lambda j/T}\mathbf{P}\left(Z(m)=j\right)\mathbf{P}(0<Z(\chi)<T,\left|Z(\chi)-j\right|\leq\delta j|Z(0)=j).

Since

\mathbf{P}(0<Z(\chi)<T,\left|Z(\chi)-j\right|\leq\delta j|Z(0)=j)=\mathbf{P}(\left|Z(\chi)-j\right|\leq\delta j|Z(0)=j)

if
j\leq T/\left(1+\delta\right)
and, by (
3.5
)

\mathbf{P}(\left|Z(\chi)-j\right|\leq\delta j|Z(0)=j)=1-\mathbf{P}(\left|Z(\chi)-j\right|>\delta j|Z(0)=j)\rightarrow 0

uniformly in
j\in\left[\varepsilon T,NT\right]
, we may write

\displaystyle\sum_{j=\varepsilon T}^{T/\left(1-\delta\right)}e^{-\lambda j/T}\mathbf{P}\left(Z(m)=j\right)\mathbf{P}(0<Z(\chi)<T,\,\left|Z(\chi)-j\right|\leq\delta j|Z(0)=j)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad=\sum_{j=\varepsilon T}^{T/\left(1+\delta\right)}e^{-\lambda j/T}\mathbf{P}\left(Z(m)=j\right)+o\left(\mathbf{P}\left(\varepsilon T<Z(m)<T/\left(1+\delta\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+O\left(\mathbf{P}\left(T/\left(1+\delta\right)<Z(m)<T/\left(1-\delta\right)\right)\right).

Since
m\sim n
, we conclude by (
9
) that

\mathbf{P}\left(\varepsilon T<Z(m)<T/\left(1+\delta\right)\right)\leq C\mathbf{P}\left(0<Z(n)<T\right)

and

\lim_{m\sim n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbf{P}\left(T/\left(1+\delta\right)<Z(m)<T/\left(1-\delta\right)\right)}{\mathbf{P}\left(0<Z(n)<T\right)}=\frac{1}{\left(1-\delta\right)^{\alpha}}-\frac{1}{\left(1+\delta\right)^{\alpha}}.

Further,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=\varepsilon T}^{T/\left(1+\delta\right)}e^{-\lambda j/T}\mathbf{P}\left(Z(m)=j\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=\varepsilon T}^{T/\left(1+\delta\right)}e^{-\lambda j/T}d\mathbf{P}\left(\varepsilon T\leq Z(m)\leq j\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\varepsilon}^{1/\left(1+\delta\right)}e^{-\lambda x}d\mathbf{P}\left(\varepsilon\leq\frac{Z(m)}{T}\leq x\right).\qquad\qquad

Hence, using (
38
), (
39
) and recalling that
m\sim n\rightarrow\infty
we get

\displaystyle\int_{\varepsilon}^{1/\left(1+\delta\right)}e^{-\lambda x}d\mathbf{P}\left(\varepsilon<\frac{Z(m)}{T}\leq x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(0<Z(m)<T\right)\int_{\varepsilon}^{1/\left(1+\delta\right)}e^{-\lambda x}d\mathbf{P}\left(\varepsilon<\frac{Z(m)}{T}\leq x|0<Z(m)<T\right)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(0<Z(n)<T\right)\alpha\int_{\varepsilon}^{1/\left(1+\delta\right)}e^{-\lambda x}x^{\alpha-1}dx.

Combining the estimates above and letting
\delta
and
\varepsilon
to
zero we complete the proof of point 5) of Theorem
1
.

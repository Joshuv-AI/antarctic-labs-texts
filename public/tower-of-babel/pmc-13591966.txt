Complete mitochondrial genome and phylogenetic analysis of Taiwanese Wedgefish (Rhynchobatus immaculatus)
Authors: Shouqiang Wang, Xiaotong Zhu, Linlin Zhao, Mengyuan Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTaiwanese Wedgefish is widely distributed in the western North Pacific. It is listed as Critically Endangered on the International Union for Conservation of Nature Red List of Threatened Species. However, genetic information on this species remains very limited. In this study, the complete mitochondrial genome of Taiwanese Wedgefish was sequence. The full length of its mitochondrial genome is 16,804 base pairs, encompassing 37 genes. Phylogenetic analysis indicates that the Taiwanese Wedgefish and the Bottlenose wedgefish are grouped together in one branch, clarifying the evolutionary relationship of this species and laying an important genetic foundation for subsequent research on Rhinidae.Keywords: Mitochondrial genome, phylogenetic analysis, Rhinidae, Rhynchobatus immaculatus

Introduction

The class Chondrichthyes (sharks, skates, rays and chimaeras) is one of the oldest and most evolutionarily distinct vertebrate lineages, comprising >1200 species (Boore 2006, Jabado 2018). The life history traits of cartilaginous fishes-namely slow growth, late sexual maturity, prolonged gestation, and low fecundity-render them particularly vulnerable to overfishing. (van Staden et al. 2022). Rhynchobatus immaculatus (Last et al. 2013) (Rhinopristiformes: Rhinidae) is one of the most critically endangered marine species globally. This species has been recorded in the waters off Taiwan and Wenzhou, China, and is presumed to have a potentially wider distribution range in the western North Pacific (Last et al. 2013). However, significant taxonomic uncertainty within the genus Rhynchobatus complicates accurate species identification. Therefore, this study presents the first complete mitochondrial genome sequence of the Taiwanese Wedgefish and analyzes the phylogenetic relationships among related species within the order Rhinidae.

Materials and methods

Sample collection and DNA sequencing

One specimen of Taiwanese Wedgefish was collected for this study from the Beihai City fishing port wharf (Latitude 21.4200° N, Longitude 109.1225° E) in April 2024 (Figure. 1). The collected specimen was naturally deceased fish, not live individuals. Species identification was based on morphological characteristics. The collected sample was currently stored at −20 °C fridge at the First Institute of Oceanography, Ministry of Natural Resources (Mengyuan Liu and liumengyuan399@163.com), under the voucher number 202404.

Specimen of the Taiwanese Wedgefish. (A) dorsal view, showing body outline, pectoral disc shape, dorsal fin arrangement, and white spotting pattern; (B) ventral view, showing gill slits, mouth position, and ventral coloration.

Genomic DNA was isolated from the specimen using the DNeasy Blood & Tissue DNA kit (QIAGEN, Germany). Subsequent library preparation was followed by sequencing on an Illumina HiSeq 2500 Platform (Illumina, USA) to generate 2 × 100 bp paired-end reads. Raw sequencing reads underwent quality control and adapter trimming using Trimmomatic v0.38 (Bolger et al. 2014).

Mitogenome assembly, annotation, and analysis

After quality filtering, a total of 170,982,316 clean reads were obtained from 177,382,016 raw reads. The complete mitochondrial genome was assembled using MitoZ v3.6 (Meng et al. 2019), which integrates NOVOPlasty v4.3.1 (Dierckxsens et al. 2017) for de novo assembly based on a seed-and-extend strategy. The circularity of the assembled mitogenome was confirmed by detecting overlapping terminal sequences in the assembly output. Clean reads were mapped back to the assembled mitogenome using BWA-MEM v0.7.17 (Jung and Han 2022), and average sequencing coverage was calculated using SAMtools v1.16 (Danecek et al. 2021). The control region boundaries were validated based on the flanking tRNA-Pro and tRNA-Phe genes annotated by MITOS2 (Bernt et al. 2013). The vertebrate mitochondrial genetic code (NCBI Translation Table 2) was used for gene annotation. The data supporting the findings of this study have been deposited in the GenBank database of the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI). A graphical map of the mitogenome was generated using mitofish.

Phylogenetic analysis

Due to the limited availability of rays tissue samples, mitochondrial genome data for species belonging to the Rhinidae family remains relatively scarce. To clarify the phylogenetic position of Taiwanese Wedgefish, this study retrieved 15 complete mitochondrial genome sequences representing four families within the order Rhinopristiformes from the NCBI GenBank database: Rhinida, Pristidae, Rhinobatida, and Glaucostegida (Table S1). Additionally, Raja undulata was selected as an outgroup for phylogenetic analysis.

All phylogenetic and gene order analyses were conducted using the PhyloSuite v1.2.3 platform (Xiang et al. 2023). The 13 protein-coding genes (PCGs) were individually aligned using MAFFT v7.505, after which the resulting alignments were refined with Gblocks and concatenated into a supermatrix. The optimal partitioning scheme and nucleotide substitution models were then selected using ModelFinder v2.2.0. Subsequently, a maximum likelihood (ML) phylogenetic tree was reconstructed in IQ-TREE v2.2.0 under the edge-linked partition model, with nodal support assessed by 5,000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates.

Results

Mitochondrial genome organization and features

The coverage-depth map indicates an average sequencing depth of 7819.33× acrossthe mitochondrial genome (Figure S1). The full length of its mitochondrial genome is 16,804 base pairs, encompassing 37 genes (Figure 2, Table S2), with a nucleotide composition of 32.3% A, 27.5% T, 26.9% C, and 13.3% G, exhibiting a distinct AT bias. Among the 13 protein-coding genes (PCGs), eleven genes (nad1, nad2, cox2, atp8, atp6, cox3, nad3, nad4l, nad4, nad5, and cytb) use ATG as the start codon, whereas cox initiates with GTG. All PCGs terminate with complete stop codons, with eleven genes (nad1, nad2, cox1, atp8, atp6, cox3, nad3, nad4l, nad5, nad6, and cytb.) ending with TAA, while cox2 and nad4 terminate with AGG and AGA, respectively. The 22 transfer RNA (tRNA) genes range in length from 67 to 75 bp. In addition, the lengths of the 12S ribosomal RNA (rRNA) and 16S rRNA genes are 1651 bp and 967 bp, respectively.

Phylogenetic inference and implications

To assess the phylogenetic position and validate the accuracy of the newly sequenced mitochondrial genome of Taiwanese Wedgefish, a phylogenetic analysis was performed based on complete mitochondrial genome sequences. As shown in the phylogenetic tree (Figure 3), Taiwanese Wedgefish clustered closely with its congeners Rhynchobatus australiae, Rhynchobatus laevis, and Rhynchobatus djiddensis, forming a well-supported clade within the family Rhinidae.

Maximum-likelihood (ML) phylogenetic tree based on complete mitochondrial genome sequences of 14 Rhinopristiformes species. Nodal support was assessed by 5000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates. R. undulata (NC_059939) was used as the outgroup. The sequences used are as follows: PZ060233 (this study), MN783017 (Johri et al. 2020), NC039438 (Kyne et al. 2018), PV430499, MN988687, NC059939 (Kousteni et al. 2021), NC068897 (van Staden et al. 2022), NC026307 (Chen et al. 2016), NC022821 (Feutry et al. 2015), NC027182 (Chen et al. 2016), KU721837 (Si et al. 2016), NC022841, NC023951, ON065567, ON065568 (Groeneveld et al. 2023).

The phylogenetic relationships recovered in this analysis support the monophyly of the genus Rhynchobatus and corroborate the systematic placement of Taiwanese Wedgefish within Rhinidae. These results are congruent with previously reported morphological and molecular studies, further confirming the reliability of the mitochondrial genome sequence obtained in this study.

Discussion and conclusion

The complete mitochondrial genome of Taiwanese Wedgefish determined in this study is 16,804 bp in length, falling within the typical size range of animal mitochondrial genome (Boore 1999). The gene content and arrangement of the Taiwanese Wedgefish mitogenome are identical to those of typical vertebrate mitogenomes, comprising 13 protein-coding genes (PCGs), 22 tRNA genes, and 2 rRNA genes (Wolstenholme 1992; Boore 1999).

Phylogenetic analysis based on complete mitogenome sequences placed Taiwanese Wedgefish and Bottlenose wedgefish in a well-supported clade the monophyly of Rhinidae (Sato 2013). The clear separation of Rhinidae from Rhinobatidae, Pristidae, and Glaucostegidae aligns with current taxonomic classifications, validating both the assembly accuracy and the utility of mitogenome data for resolving inter-familial relationships within Rhinopristiformes (Aschliman et al. 2012; Dulvy 2021; Stein 2018).

In conclusion, this study provides important genetic information for future taxonomic, systematic, and conservation genetic studies on Taiwanese Wedgefish and its related species.

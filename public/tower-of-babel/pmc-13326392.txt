Simulation research on the clinical path of non-small cell lung cancer surgery based on time petri net
Authors: Chen Da, Song Haitao, Song Jun, Zhong Hui, Xu Chuangchuang, Gao Junli
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundClinical pathways are increasingly adopted to control costs and enhance quality management, becoming a standardized approach in treatment. This study aims to develop a hospital-specific clinical pathway for non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) surgery based on national standards and actual treatment practices, and evaluate its service efficiency through simulation.MethodsWe analyzed 94 electronic medical records of NSCLC surgeries performed between May 2020 and November 2022, evaluating the need for localization by statistically examining treatment process delays. A Time Petri Net model was established for this pathway, with simulations conducted to measure post-implementation changes in hospital length of stay (LOS).ResultsThe hospital’s existing processes were generally consistent with national standards. Validation of the Time Petri Net model confirmed its effectiveness. Simulation results showed that the average LOS was reduced from a baseline of 8.20 days to 7.76 days, saving a total of ~ 10.28 h (individual diagnostic/treatment processes were shortened by 0.15–5.04 h).ConclusionImplementing this tailored clinical pathway significantly improved service efficiency by aligning it with national guidelines, enabling better integration and optimization of medical resources while improving overall clinical pathway management quality.Keywords: Clinical pathway, Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), Time petri net, Hospitalization

Introduction

Clinical pathways are rigorously structured workflows grounded in evidence-based medicine that delineate diagnostics, treatments, and care processes for specific diseases or surgical procedures. They are characterized by strict sequences and precise timing, aiming to optimize the use of medical resources and enhancing the quality of healthcare services [1, 2]. In medical practice, clinical pathways offer unique advantages in standardizing clinical practices, ensuring healthcare quality, and safeguarding patient safety. Researchers have conducted extensive statistical studies to analyze the significant differences in key medical indicators before and after the implementation of clinical pathways. Evidence from these studies substantiates the significant positive impact of clinical pathways in reducing length of stay [3], lowering healthcare costs [4], optimizing resource utilization [5], improving the quality of healthcare services [6], enhancing physician satisfaction [7], and accelerating patient recovery [8].

However, while statistical methods effectively highlight the benefits of clinical pathways, they often fail to capture detailed insights into the diagnostic and treatment processes, thereby lacking depth in explaining the outcomes of treatment optimizations. This gap poses challenges for medical staff who aim to analyze and manage different segments of the clinical pathways. To improve both understanding of clinical pathways and control over them, researchers are increasingly framing these pathways as workflows and using simulation analysis to evaluate their operational efficacy [9]. This approach involves using physical models to define the relationships among diagnostic activities, resources, and information within the pathways. It allows for the decomposition and systematic organization of these relationships, which facilitates an assessment of the rationality of the clinical pathways’ overall structure and the impact of internal resource coordination on treatment outcomes.

Currently, the primary methods for modeling clinical pathways include Agent-based modeling and Petri Net modeling [10]. Li [11] noted the difficulty in adapting clinical pathway applications to the dynamic and complex clinical processes which involve social entity coordination in hospitals, stating how Agent models support clinical pathway management. Han [12] proposed a multi-Agent-based simulation modeling method for clinical pathways, which showcases the main roles and process procedures in clinical pathway activities and validates the model’s reliability. While Agent-based modeling effectively captures interactions across multiple departments, the focus of current research is predominantly on the structure of these models, with less emphasis on temporal factors. Conversely, Petri Net modeling is particularly effective in visually monitoring diagnostic states, information flows, and the interactions between clinical activities [13]. For example, Zhao [14] utilized a hierarchical timed colored Petri Net model to simulate varying patient arrival rates and develop strategies for optimizing medical resources. Similarly, Wang [15] integrated a predictive model of breast cancer clinical pathways into a colored Petri Net, enabling visual analysis of the breast cancer treatment process and providing an intuitive display of quantitative prediction results.

Statistical and modeling methods have underscored the unique benefits of clinical pathways in enhancing healthcare efficiency. In 2022, China’s National Health Commission, along with seven other departments, issued the ‘Guidelines on Further Standardizing Medical Practices to Promote Reasonable Medical Examinations.’ This directive underscores the imperative to broaden the implementation of clinical pathways across hospitals. It was stipulated that by the end of 2022, clinical pathways should manage 50% of patients discharged from tertiary hospitals and 70% from secondary hospitals [16]. To date, the National Health Commission has issued 1,212 standard clinical pathways. However, challenges persist due to the macroscopic and comprehensive scope of these national clinical pathways, which cater to various levels and regions. These challenges are compounded by significant disparities in resources, distinctive diagnostic and treatment capabilities across hospitals, and the absence of an effective evaluation mechanism for clinical pathway applications. Consequently, the actual number of diseases managed and the operational efficiency remain below expectations [17, 18]. Mathew [19] also highlighted that the implementation of clinical pathways must consider local resource limitations to ensure proper adaptation and effectiveness. Therefore, localized research on clinical pathways has emerged as a critical focus in the management of these systems.

Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) demonstrates significant individual variability in treatment, leading to a low application rate of clinical pathways. Typically, patients with NSCLC are treated using a precision management way rather than conventional clinical pathways. Nevertheless, the diagnostic and treatment processes for most early-stage NSCLC patients are relatively uniform. These patients usually present with few comorbidities, no severe complications, and are in a manageable stage of the disease, which makes the implementation of clinical pathway management both feasible and beneficial. This study focuses on a tertiary public hospital in Guangdong Province to identify patient records suitable for clinical pathway management. We developed a Time Petri Net model for the surgical clinical pathway of NSCLC, integrating medical record data and hospital feedback for process simulation. The study examines the potential impacts of clinical pathway implementation on hospital length of stay (LOS), offering insights that could help the hospital optimize the use of clinical pathways.

Data and methods

Data source

The research data were sourced from a tertiary public hospital --- The Second Provincial General Hospital in Guangdong Province, China. A total of 1200 medical records of patients with pulmonary nodules who underwent surgical treatment between May 2020 and November 2022 were included. Of these, 243 cases were initially screened based on admission diagnosis of R91 and the approach (including open and laparoscopic resection) (ICD-9-CM-3: 32.29/32.3–32.5). Further screening was conducted and resulted in the inclusion of 94 valid medical records, according to the criteria: (1) Meeting the surgical intervention indications outlined in the “Clinical Pathway for Surgical Treatment of Pulmonary Nodules”; (2) Complete adherence to the clinical pathway management for surgical treatment of pulmonary nodules; (3) Complete medical records. It only included patients with NSCLC of stage I, II, and IIIA and with isolated brain or adrenal metastases who underwent corresponding surgery, according to the localized clinical pathway criteria. The Patients who received neoadjuvant therapy and involved alternative forms of treatment were not included. These records include information on patient admission and discharge times, examination reports, other major diagnostic and therapeutic procedures, and progress reports.

Research methods

Time petri net

Time Petri Nets (TPNs) provide a robust framework for describing and analyzing discrete event systems. They offer graphical representation and are underpinned by rigorous mathematical formalisms [20]. The model is a meshed flow of information comprising two types of nodes: places and transitions. Tokens, representing state information, are distributed across the directed bipartite graph formed by these nodes. Events trigger state transitions according to specified rules, reflecting the dynamic operational processes of the system. With the introduction of temporal factors, TPNs enable an enhanced systematic depiction and analysis of specific operational dynamics within business processes.

Statistical methods

Temporal information for the TPN was acquired through statistical methods. Non-parametric tests were employed to determine if significant differences exist in the actual duration of various hospital stages, in compliance with national clinical pathway standards. This analysis highlighted key process discrepancies between the hospital’s practices and national standards. Additionally, the average duration of each major diagnostic and treatment event was calculated, with 95% confidence intervals provided. These intervals establish the basis for simulating duration ranges in the Time Petri Net model.

As indicated in Table 2, the average completion time for each phase were consistently shorter than the expected lengths of stay defined by the national clinical pathways for NSCLC surgery. This suggests that the hospital exceeds the national standards, demonstrating high efficiency. However, strict adherence to these national clinical pathways might inadvertently constrain the hospital’s operational efficiency. Localization of clinical pathway modeling and model analysis

Integrating the hospital’s actual diagnostic and treatment processes with medical record data, we designed a localized clinical pathway based on event logic and incorporating hospital feedback. Simulation results

The constructed Time Petri Net underwent computer simulation to assess changes in the length of hospital stay before and after implementing a localized clinical pathway for NSCLC surgery. These simulations were conducted using the Tina software platform, as depicted in Fig. 3, with a total of 100 runs, each consisting of 500 simulations.

The simulation results are presented in Table 5. Statistical analysis of the duration of each diagnostic and treatment process, both before and after the implementation of the localized clinical pathway, showed a decrease in the average total length of hospital stay from 8.20 days to 7.76 days. This reduction of 0.44 days, equivalent to 10.28 h, represents a decrease of 5.37%. Additionally, there was a significant reduction in the variability of the mean duration of each process. Therefore, the proactive implementation of a localized clinical pathway for NSCLC patients could effectively shorten the average length of hospital stays, enhance the stability of treatment durations, improve ward turnover efficiency, and reduce hospitalization costs for eligible patients.

Discussion

Compared to other diseases, non-small cell lung cancer exhibits significant heterogeneity in its diagnosis and treatment processes, closely associated with the patient’s medical history and baseline physical condition. Nevertheless, a considerable percentage of patients are in an early, controllable stage, meeting the criteria for adherence to clinical pathways. As indicated in Table 2, the delay ratios in Phase 2 and Phase 5 are 10.64% and 8.05%, respectively. These elevated delay rates primarily result from the need for coordination among multiple parties and substantial medical resources. Moreover, individual variability significantly impacts clinical assessments, preoperative preparations, and postoperative recovery, thereby increasing the likelihood of variations and adding to the complexity of clinical solutions. Consequently, the time spent by different patients in these phases varies significantly, depending on their specific conditions. Nevertheless, the average LOS of each phase is less than the time stipulated by standard clinical pathways, indicating that the overall diagnostic and treatment efficiency in each stage surpasses the national clinical pathway standards, despite some patients exceeding the expected timelines. The zero-delay rate in Stage 6 also indicates that no patients’ total hospital stay exceeded the time specified in the standard clinical pathways.

In addition to indicating that the hospital stay for non-small cell lung cancer surgery is shorter than the standard duration, this also highlights the possibility and necessity for hospitals to adjust and optimize clinical pathways. Given the hospital’s superior performance in terms of LOS, strict adherence to national clinical pathways may actually decrease efficiency and prolong LOS. Hospitals should consider adjusting to optimize these pathways based on their unique resource characteristics only after meeting the standards set by national clinical pathways. Such adjustments are necessary and consistent with the conclusions of studies referenced in [21, 22]. National clinical pathways provide macroscopic, comprehensive guidance tailored to different levels and regions. Hospitals should adhere to these pathways while also considering the experience of medical staff and available resources. It is essential to analyze the current treatment pathway’s model and characteristics, identify its advantages and shortcomings, allocate medical resources appropriately, and engage relevant departments in discussions to develop clinical pathways that align with the hospital’s actual conditions.

Simulation results indicate that if hospitals adopt localized clinical pathways, the total length of hospital stays can be reduced by 0.44 days, equating to a decrease of 5.37%. This reduction helps mitigate fluctuations in treatment durations caused by differentiated management or physician treatment preferences and eliminates extra work not related to clinical pathways, thereby avoiding delays caused by unrelated treatment activities. According to [23], an analysis comparing the length of hospital stays before and after implementing clinical pathways for non-small cell lung cancer at a specific hospital demonstrated a reduction of 2 days, or 9.47%, from an initial average of 20.67 days. The greater reduction observed in the referenced study, compared to ours, can be attributed to the inclusion of different stages of non-small cell lung cancer, involving both surgical and chemotherapeutic treatments. Chemotherapy typically requires longer treatment durations, approximately one month; thus, clinical pathways exert a more substantial impact over extended treatment periods. In contrast, our study focuses on early-stage lung cancer cases predominantly managed through surgery. Findings from [24, 25] suggest that the median recovery time for patients undergoing wedge resection of lung cancer is 3 days, and 5.2 days for those undergoing lobectomy. Given that our study encompasses both surgical approaches, an average hospital recovery time of 3.66 days is deemed reasonable. Moreover, our study analyzes medical records from 2011 to 2013, and with ongoing advancements in medical technology, a further reduction in hospital stays has been noted. Although the reduction percentage in our simulation results is smaller compared to earlier studies, it remains within a typical range.

This paper utilizes a Petri Net for modeling clinical pathways due to its ability to reveal significant structural and dynamic behaviors of clinical pathway systems. Its structure is delineated using rigorous mathematical formalism, and numerical analysis confirms the constructed Petri Net’s validity and effectiveness, thereby avoiding ambiguities or instabilities during simulation. As discussed in [26–30], clinical pathways necessitate collaboration across multiple departments simultaneously, and the treatment process inherently exhibits a degree of randomness. Petri Nets are particularly well-suited for modeling processes characterized by asynchrony, concurrency, or randomness, which aligns closely with the structure of clinical pathways. The graphical nature of Petri Nets mirrors actual process diagrams, facilitating a visual and comprehensive depiction of the key steps in implementing clinical pathways. The application of Petri Nets in modeling various disease treatment processes and hospital management proves to be both reasonable and effective. Unlike traditional statistical methods that provide a general p-value, Petri Net-based methods allow the decomposition and individual assessment of essential processes within clinical pathways. The analysis results based on Time Petri Net are statistical results obtained from multiple simulations of cases conforming to a given sample distribution, and have statistical significance for cases that meet the characteristics of the collected data. This approach elucidates the impact of each critical task on the duration of the pathways, thereby rendering the management of clinical pathways more transparent and controllable.

Hospital departments should continually refine clinical pathways during the diagnostic and treatment phases, clearly defining and refining the requirements at each stage. Utilizing quality management tools to enhance the monitoring and evaluation of clinical pathway implementation, and promptly summarizing treatment outcomes, can significantly improve the design of clinical pathways. These improvements result in enhanced stability, reduced variability, and improved economic and service benefits. Furthermore, the level of digital management in hospitals has been shown to significantly influence the outcomes of clinical pathway implementations [31–33]. Hospital information systems departments should strengthen their information technology support to reduce both the operational costs and the complexity of clinical pathway systems. By improving the information infrastructure for clinical pathways and granting departmental physicians more extensive maintenance permissions, hospitals can simplify the procedures for adjusting these systems. Such improvements are likely to enhance both the efficiency of and the engagement by physicians in using these systems. This paper integrates nationally standardized clinical pathways with actual hospital diagnostic and treatment processes to develop localized clinical pathways. These localized pathways are designed to ensure compliance and comprehensiveness while considering the available medical resources and the specific treatment characteristics of the hospital.

Conclusion

Summary of findings

Heterogeneity in hospital resources and treatment processes limits the universal applicability of national standard clinical pathways, necessitating localization of these pathways for hospitals across different levels and regions. This study first analyzed real-world medical records and used statistical methods to identify temporal discrepancies between actual treatment practices and standard pathway requirements, verifying the need for localization. A Time Petri Net model was then constructed for the localized pathway, with numerical analysis validating its structure and effectiveness. Simulation results showed that implementing this localized pathway reduced total hospitalization time by 10.28 h, markedly improving treatment efficiency. To support the design and optimization of localized clinical pathways, hospitals should integrate medical resources, strengthen inter-departmental coordination and supervision, and continuously advance information system development.

Limitations

Given our hospital’s standardized clinical pathways and limited data resources, our model takes solely on hospital LOS as clinical pathway effectiveness does indeed have certain limitations. We currently lack relatively sensitive and accurate data on medical costs and quantitative indicators of clinical efficacy. This paper is currently focused on optimizing the clinical pathway for non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). The exclusion of cases involving complications or the use of alternative forms of treatment may pose a major limitation in the applicability of the proposed clinical pathway in the NSCLC patient population. Although our medical records originated from 1200 cases, and after targeted screening, 94 valid cases were ultimately identified, the number of cases is still insufficient. This may lead to a major limitation in the applicability of the results.

In future research, we will collaborate with multiple hospitals to enhance the completeness and generalization of relevant medical data to further expand the applicability of our research findings. A more comprehensive evaluation of clinical pathways should also consider factors such as inpatient costs and pathway variability, thereby acknowledging the limitations of the existing model.

Non-Classicality and Non-adiabaticity in a Single Trapped Ion Preprint: APS/123-QED
C. F. P. Avalos, M. C. de Oliveira

Abstract
Trapped ion systems present non-classical characteristics such as squeezed states that show a quantum advantage in quantum sensing, quantum information processing and quantum thermodynamics. We analyze the non-classical characteristics of a system described by a single ion trapped by a periodic potential field. Within the regime of non-adiabatic manipulation of the potential field, the dynamics of motion of the center of mass of the ion can be described by a dimensionless parameter called the non-adiabatic parameter Q ∗ Q^{*} . This parameter allows us to distinguish the classical and non-classical characteristics of the system. Using the equations of motion of observables in the Heisenberg picture, we propose an analysis of the unitary time evolution operator and discuss the squeezing behavior in the state of motion of the ion. The results shown can serve as a basis to discuss the presence of squeezing as a resource in quantum thermodynamics in the non-adiabatic regime in actual achievable experimental limitations.

II
Dynamics of a single trapped ion

The dynamics of an ion trapped in an electromagnetic potential field is analogous to a QHO with time-dependent frequency. Therefore, the Hamiltonian of a single trapped ion can be defined as

\mathbf{\hat{\text{$H$}}}(t)=\frac{\mathbf{\hat{\text{$p$}}}^{2}}{2m}+\frac{1}{2}mw^{2}(t)\mathbf{\hat{\text{$x$}}}^{2},

(2)

where
m
is the mass of the ion and
w(t)
is the time-dependent frequency representing the manipulation of the electromagnetic potential field.
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$x$}}}
and
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$p$}}}
represent the position and momentum operators of the ion’s center of mass, respectively.

The Hamiltonian can be written in the form
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$H$}}}(t)=\hbar w(t)\left(\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}^{\dagger}(t)\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\right)
, where we define the time-dependent operators in the Schrödinger picture, as
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}(t)=\sqrt{\frac{mw(t)}{2\hbar}}\left(\mathbf{\hat{\text{$x$}}}+i\frac{\mathbf{\hat{\text{$p$}}}}{mw(t)}\right)
and
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}^{\dagger}(t)\equiv\left[\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}(t)\right]^{\dagger}
, therefore, for each value of the parametrized frequency we have new operators. For equal times, these operators satisfy the following commutation relations:
[\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}(t),\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}(t)]=0
, and
[\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}(t),\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}^{\dagger}(t)]=1
, while, the commutation relations in different time is
[\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}(t^{\prime}),\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}(t)]\neq 0
, implying in
[\mathbf{\hat{\text{$H$}}}(t^{\prime}),\mathbf{\hat{\text{$H$}}}(t)]\neq 0
. For
t=0
, Eq. (
2
) is reduced to the time-independent Hamiltonian with initial frequency
w(0)=w_{0}
, that is,
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$H$}}}(0)\equiv\hbar w_{0}\left(\mathbf{\hat{\text{$a$}}}^{\dagger}\mathbf{\hat{\text{$a$}}}+\frac{1}{2}\right)
. The relationship between annihilation (creation) operators
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$a$}}}
(
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$a$}}}^{\dagger}
) of the free harmonic oscillator and
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}(t)
(
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}^{\dagger}(t)
) is determined by the Bogoliubov transformation,

\begin{split}\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}^{\dagger}(t)&=\cosh(r_{a})\mathbf{\hat{\text{$a$}}}^{\dagger}+\sinh(r_{a})\mathbf{\hat{\text{$a$}}}=\mathbf{\hat{\text{$S$}}}(r_{a})\mathbf{\hat{\text{$a$}}}^{\dagger}\mathbf{\hat{\text{$S$}}}^{\dagger}(r_{a}),\\
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}(t)&=\cosh(r_{a})\mathbf{\hat{\text{$a$}}}+\sinh(r_{a})\mathbf{\hat{\text{$a$}}}^{\dagger}=\mathbf{\hat{\text{$S$}}}(r_{a})\mathbf{\hat{\text{$a$}}}\mathbf{\hat{\text{$S$}}}^{\dagger}(r_{a}),\end{split}

(3)

where
e^{r_{a}}=\sqrt{\frac{w(t)}{w_{0}}}
,
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$S$}}}(r_{a})=e^{\frac{r_{a}}{2}\left(\mathbf{\hat{\text{$a$}}}^{\dagger 2}-\mathbf{\hat{\text{$a$}}}^{2}\right)}
is the squeezing operator, with squeezing parameter
r_{a}
. The evolution of the system is described by the unitary time evolution operator
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$U$}}}(t,0)=\mathbf{\hat{\text{$T$}}}\{e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\int^{t}_{0}\mathbf{\hat{\text{$H$}}}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}\}
, where
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$T$}}}
is the time ordering operator. The operator,
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$U$}}}(t,0)
, evolves the oscillator from an initial state
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$\rho$}}}_{0}
to a final state
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$\rho$}}}(t)\equiv\mathbf{\hat{\text{$U$}}}(t,0)\mathbf{\hat{\text{$\rho$}}}_{0}\mathbf{\hat{\text{$U$}}}^{\dagger}(t,0)
.

Let us firstly consider the sudden approximation – the frequency changes in a very short time interval in such a way that the system remains in the same state when the manipulation occurs. Then, the time evolution operator is given by the identity,
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$U$}}}_{sudd}(t,0)\equiv\mathbf{\hat{\text{$\mathcal{I}$}}}

Bohm 2012
;
Hecht 2012
. In this case, the squeezing effect is produced by
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$S$}}}(r_{a})

;
Janszky and Yushin 1986
;
Graham 1987
;
Janszky and Adam 1992

to a final frequency

On the other hand, in the adiabatic approximation, as the frequency changes slowly, it is possible to show that the adiabatic time evolution operator,
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$U$}}}_{ad}

Therefore, while the expected value of the Hamiltonian (Energy) of the system during the evolution will depend on the protocol employed for the frequency change, the expected value of the particle number operator remains constant through the evolution.

Squeezing is a non-classical characteristic of the system, however, we show that this will depend on the critical value of the degree of non-adiabaticity, meaning that there is a critical value of the squeezing parameter related to

III
Time Evolution Operator

In the previous section we showed the non-adiabatic dynamics of the trapped ion and the squeezing effect could be determined by analyzing the variance of the quadratures related to the position and momentum operators. However, this does not allow us to identify the evolution of the squeezing phase and squeezing parameter. Therefore, we propose a second approach to observe the squeezing behavior by describing the time evolution operator of the trapped ion.

Some algebraic methods were previously applied to find the time evolution operator of a harmonic oscillator with time-dependent frequency

;
Kiss et al. 1994a

Martínez-Tibaduiza et al. 2021

V
Discussions and conclusions

The nonadiabatic dynamics of a single trapped ion without noise were described using the Heisenberg picture approach and the unitary time evolution operator approach. From the Heisenberg picture we deduce that the non-adiabiticity parameter depends on the solutions of the Heisenberg equations of motion. Therefore,
Q^{*}
depends on the shape and manipulation of frequency. Furthermore, the properties of the covariance matrix and P-representability show that there is a relationship between the non-adiabiticity and the non-classicality of the system. One of the non-classical features of the system is the degree of squeezing that can be generated during evolution. So, to analyze the evolution of squeezing, we propose a time-evolution operator ansatz,
\bar{U}=\mathbf{\hat{\text{$S$}}}(\xi)\mathbf{\hat{\text{$R$}}}(\gamma)
, such that the time-dependent parameters are related to the solutions of the Heisenberg equations of motion.

The results obtained show that during a non-adiabatic evolution there are regions where the state of the system is classical or non-classical, in this case, these regions can be identified by the critical value of the non-adiabatic parameter,
Q^{*}_{c}
. This result is important because it allows us to identify regions in which a non-classical state can be obtained in an adequate manner, because a necessary condition for this is that the non-adiabatic parameter exceeds its critical value,
Q^{*}>Q^{*}_{c}
. Furthermore, discuss the behavior of the degree of squeezing in a non-adiabatic manipulation situation is important for quantum information processes and quantum thermodynamics using a system of continuous variables. Within quantum thermodynamics, these results can be used to discuss the influence of non-adiabaticity in quantum thermal engines and in resource theory. Lastly, we believe that the present results could be further extended to explore alternative trap geometries, such as those investigated in
Palmero et al. 2016
, particularly the fast rotation of an ion in a tightly confined rotating trap. This extension may offer deeper insights into the interplay between non-adiabatic dynamics and non-classical behavior in continuous variable quantum systems.

Appendix A
Heisenberg Picture

The Hamiltonian of a single trapped ion is analogous to the Hamiltonian of a QHO with time-dependent frequency, i.e.

\mathbf{\hat{\text{$H$}}}(t)=\frac{\mathbf{\hat{\text{$p$}}}^{2}}{2m}+\frac{1}{2}mw^{2}(t)\mathbf{\hat{\text{$x$}}}^{2}.

(26)

In the Heisenberg picture, from the equations of motion of the operators
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$x$}}}
and
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$p$}}}
, we obtain the differential equation of an oscillator with time-dependent frequency

\ddot{\mathbf{\hat{\text{$x$}}}}_{H}+w^{2}\mathbf{\hat{\text{$x$}}}_{H}=0

(27)

where
w(t)\equiv w
. We propose a solution of the form

\mathbf{\hat{\text{$x$}}}_{H}=u\mathbf{\hat{\text{$x$}}}+v\mathbf{\hat{\text{$p$}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{\hat{\text{$p$}}}_{H}=\bar{u}\mathbf{\hat{\text{$x$}}}+\bar{v}\mathbf{\hat{\text{$p$}}},

(28)

where
u
,
v
,
\bar{u}=m\dot{u}
and
\bar{v}=m\dot{v}
are real time-dependent functions, and these functions are solutions of the differential equations

\ddot{u}+w^{2}u=0,\quad\text{and}\quad\ddot{\bar{v}}+w^{2}\bar{v}=0,

(29)

with the initial conditions
u(0)=1
,
\dot{u}=0
and
\bar{v}(0)=0
,
\dot{\bar{v}}(0)=1
, respectively. For the commutation relation between
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$x$}}}_{H}
and
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$p$}}}_{H}
to be true, it must be true that

u\dot{\bar{v}}-\dot{u}\bar{v}=1.

(30)

For the dynamics of the system, we define the Lagrangian operator
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$\mathcal{L}$}}}
and the position-momentum correlation operator
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$\mathcal{C}$}}}o
in the Heisenberg picture,

\begin{split}\mathbf{\hat{\text{$\mathcal{L}$}}}_{H}&=\frac{1}{2m}\mathbf{\hat{\text{$p$}}}^{2}_{H}-\frac{1}{2}mw^{2}\mathbf{\hat{\text{$x$}}}^{2}_{H},\\
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$\mathcal{C}$}}}o_{H}&=\frac{w}{2}\left(\mathbf{\hat{\text{$x$}}}_{H}\mathbf{\hat{\text{$p$}}}_{H}+\mathbf{\hat{\text{$p$}}}_{H}\mathbf{\hat{\text{$x$}}}_{H}\right),\end{split}

(31)

In this work, we consider that the ion is prepared in an initial state
\rho_{0}
, in such a way that the position and momentum operators have mean zero, that is,
\langle\mathbf{\hat{\text{$x$}}}\rangle=0
and
\langle\mathbf{\hat{\text{$p$}}}\rangle=0
. Therefore, in the Heisenberg picture, they will also have mean zero.
The variance of these operators, are

\begin{split}\Delta\mathbf{\hat{\text{$x$}}}^{2}_{H}&=\langle\mathbf{\hat{\text{$x$}}}^{2}_{H}\rangle=\frac{E_{0}}{m}\left(\frac{u^{2}}{w^{2}_{0}}+\bar{v}^{2}\right),\\
\Delta\mathbf{\hat{\text{$p$}}}^{2}_{H}&=\langle\mathbf{\hat{\text{$p$}}}^{2}_{H}\rangle=mE_{0}\left(\frac{\dot{u}^{2}}{w^{2}_{0}}+\dot{\bar{v}}^{2}\right).\end{split}

(32)

The mean value of the Hamiltonian, Lagrangian and position-momentum correlation operator of the system are

\begin{split}\langle\mathbf{\hat{\text{$H$}}}_{H}\rangle&=\frac{w}{w_{0}}E_{0}Q^{*},\\
\langle\mathbf{\hat{\text{$\mathcal{L}$}}}_{H}\rangle&=\frac{w}{w_{0}}E_{0}Q^{*}_{1},\\
\langle\mathbf{\hat{\text{$\mathcal{C}$}}}o_{H}\rangle&=\frac{w}{w_{0}}E_{0}Q^{*}_{2},\end{split}

(33)

where

\begin{split}Q^{*}&=\frac{1}{2w_{0}w}\left[w^{2}_{0}\left(\dot{\bar{v}}^{2}+w^{2}\bar{v}^{2}\right)+w^{2}u^{2}+\dot{u}^{2}\right],\\
Q^{*}_{1}&=\frac{1}{2w_{0}w}\left[w^{2}_{0}\left(\dot{\bar{v}}^{2}-w^{2}\bar{v}^{2}\right)+\dot{u}^{2}-w^{2}u^{2}\right],\\
Q^{*}_{2}&=\frac{1}{2w_{0}w}\left[2wu\dot{u}+2ww^{2}_{0}\bar{v}\dot{\bar{v}}\right].\end{split}

(34)

Furthermore, using the condition of Eq. (
30
), we find that

{Q^{*}}^{2}-\left({Q^{*}}^{2}_{1}+{Q^{*}}^{2}_{2}\right)=1.

(35)

This relationship is useful to analyze the non-classical behavior of the system through the covariance matrix.

The covariance matrix
\bm{V}
in terms of the operators
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}
(
\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}^{\dagger}
) is

\bm{V}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}n_{H}+\frac{1}{2}&\bar{m}_{H}\\
\bar{m}^{*}_{H}&n_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\end{array}\right),

(36)

where the elements of the diagonal are related to the number of photons or particles, i.e.,
n_{H}=\langle\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}^{\dagger}_{H}\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}_{H}\rangle-\langle\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}^{\dagger}_{H}\rangle\langle\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}_{H}\rangle
and the off-diagonal elements are described as
m_{H}=\langle\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}^{2}_{H}\rangle-\langle\mathbf{\hat{\text{$b$}}}_{H}\rangle^{2}
. We found that

\begin{split}n_{H}+\frac{1}{2}&=\frac{\langle\mathbf{\hat{\text{$H$}}}_{H}\rangle}{\hbar w},\\
\bar{m}_{H}&=-\frac{1}{\hbar w}\left[\langle\mathbf{\hat{\text{$\mathcal{L}$}}}_{H}\rangle-i\langle\mathbf{\hat{\text{$\mathcal{C}$}}}o_{H}\rangle\right].\end{split}

(37)

Then, the covariance matrix is written as

\bm{V}=\frac{1}{\hbar w}\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}\langle\mathbf{\hat{\text{$H$}}}_{H}\rangle&-\langle\mathbf{\hat{\text{$\mathcal{L}$}}}_{H}\rangle+i\langle\mathbf{\hat{\text{$\mathcal{C}$}}}o_{H}\rangle\\
-\langle\mathbf{\hat{\text{$\mathcal{L}$}}}_{H}\rangle-i\langle\mathbf{\hat{\text{$\mathcal{C}$}}}o_{H}\rangle&\langle\mathbf{\hat{\text{$H$}}}_{H}\rangle\end{array}\right).

(38)

Analyzing the behavior of the covariance matrix over time, we find that
\bm{V}
is positive. The classicality criterion of the states of the system is determined by the condition
n_{H}>|m_{H}|

Englert and Wódkiewicz 2003
;
de Oliveira Junior and de Oliveira 2022
. So, we can define the classicality function as
\mathcal{C}=n_{H}-|m_{H}|
, that is

\mathcal{C}(\bar{n},Q^{*})=\left(\bar{n}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\left[Q^{*}-\sqrt{{Q^{*}}^{2}-1}\right]-\frac{1}{2}.

(39)

where the relation of Eq. (
35
) was applied. The classicality function helps us to identify the classical and non-classical behavior of the system. The states of the trapped ion are classical if
\mathcal{C}(\bar{n},Q^{*})>0
, while for
\mathcal{C}(\bar{n},Q^{*})<0
, the states are non-classical.

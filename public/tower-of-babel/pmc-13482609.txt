End-to-end DOA estimation via self-supervised cascaded DNNs with array errors mitigation
Authors: Shuang Wu, Ye Yuan, Hongyu Pu, Tai Fu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this paper, we present a novel cascaded deep neural network architecture for high-precision direction-of-arrival (DOA) estimation in the presence of multiple array errors. The proposed framework addresses complex nonlinear distortions through an end-to-end feedforward computing strategy, eliminating the need for explicit error modeling. It consists of two key components trained in parallel: (1) four-layer adaptive spatial filters optimized using spatial-domain supervision on covariance matrices, and (2) an eight-layer MUSIC-NET that maps synthetic covariance matrices to MUSIC-like spectrum via self-supervised learning. The output of spatial filters serves as the input for MUSIC-NET, forming a two-stage architecture that enables adaptive array error mitigation during DOA estimation. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed architecture exhibits superior robustness and generalization capabilities across unseen scenarios, outperforming state-of-the-art methods in low signal-to-noise ratio and limited snapshot conditions.

Introduction

Direction-of-arrival (DOA) estimation is widely used in radar, communication, sonar, and related fields1. Over the past few decades, numerous methods have been developed to address the DOA estimation challenge with super-resolution, high accuracy, and adaptability in low SNR or small snapshot application scenarios2–8. For instance, the multiple signal classification (MUSIC)2 and estimation of signal parameters via rotational invariance technique (ESPRIT)3 methods, which are based on the subspace orthogonality assumption, have achieved super-resolution direction finding at low SNR. Sparsity-inducing methods9,10, based on the over-complete steering matrix, accurately find direction using a single snapshot by reconstructing the spatial spectrum into a linear weighted representation of an over-complete basis. However, low-cost direction-finding systems often encounter complex array errors such as antenna position perturbations, mutual coupling effects, and gain/phase inconsistencies. These errors make it challenging to establish a mathematical model for the mapping from array output to signal direction, leading to a significant degradation in the performance of model-based methods11. Recent research has introduced deep learning techniques to tackle the DOA estimation challenge12–19. These innovative methods utilize a large training dataset with DOA labels to learn the probability distribution of signal directions for array outputs. These methods do not rely on the ideal array assumption required by classical model-based methods. In particular, the works20–23 have conducted in-depth investigations on the DOA estimation problem in the presence of complex array imperfections. Refs.20,21 propose a supervised pre-training framework for DOA estimation, which addresses array errors using a pre-trained autoencoder and then leverages a substantial amount of labeled data to establish the mapping of array output to signal direction. Refs.22,23 utilize a significant amount of labeled data to model the signal subspace and address array errors, subsequently enabling accurate DOA estimation through spatial spectrum calculation. In practice, obtaining substantial training data with manual DOA labels is challenging24,25. This letter proposes a cascaded framework that combines spatial filters with MUSIC-NET to separate array error mitigation from DOA estimation. The proposed covariance-aware spatial filters reconstructs the covariance matrix to correct array errors, while MUSIC-NET maps this matrix to the MUSIC spectrum. This approach simplifies the training of the spatial filters using limited array-measured data, while MUSIC-NET can be trained with synthesized data based on an idealized perfect array. We introduce a MUSIC fitting loss function that employs self-supervised learning on synthetic covariance matrices to generate MUSIC-like spectrum, facilitating batch-parallel computation of labels on the GPU. Simulation results demonstrate the superiority of our method in array error mitigation and DOA estimation compared to state-of-the-art methods.

Training paradigm

The existing methods adopt a unified network framework for array correction and DOA estimation and complete the mapping of the covariance matrix to the spatial spectrum or subspace through supervised learning. However, training such a network usually requires a large amount of training data (e.g., the pairing of the covariance matrix and the corresponding one-hot DOA labels). It seems unrealistic to obtain a large number of covariance matrices in practice and manually label them, especially since each array antenna has a unique array error. We propose a framework characterized by a cascaded structure, wherein the innovation resides in the decoupling of array error correction and DOA estimation. During the training phase, both the spatial filters and MUSIC-NET are trained concurrently using their respective datasets. For the spatial filters component, it is relatively straightforward to obtain a modest amount of array observation data in the presence of array errors. Our experimental results indicate that satisfactory outcomes are trained with as few as 121 covariance matrix samples under specified array error conditions. Concerning MUSIC-NET, the focus is solely on mapping the array covariance matrix to MUSIC spectrum. The training data for this component can be synthesized based on the idealized assumption of a perfect array, utilizing a self-supervised learning paradigm that enables batch-parallel computation of MUSIC spectrum labels on a GPU during training. For classical subspace-based methods, the computational complexity of the standard MUSIC is dominated by two core operations: the eigenvalue decomposition (EVD) of the M× M array covariance matrix, which brings a complexity of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${O}(M^3)$$\end{document}, and the full-angle traversal for spatial spectrum calculation over \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$N_o$$\end{document} angular grids, which contributes a complexity term of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${O}(N_o M^2)$$\end{document}. The ESPRIT method avoids the full-angle spectrum traversal process, resulting in a reduced overall complexity of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${O}_{ESPRIT} = {O}(M^3)$$\end{document}, where the EVD operation still accounts for the dominant computational overhead. For Sparsity-inducing methods, the SBL has a theoretical complexity of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${O}_{SBL} = {O}(T_{iter} \cdot (N_o^2 M))$$\end{document}, where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_{iter}$$\end{document} denotes the number of iterations required for algorithm convergence, and matrix operations dominate the per-iteration complexity for posterior probability update and hyperparameter optimization. The SPICE yields a complexity of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${O}_{SPICE} = {O}(T_{iter} \cdot (M^3 + N_o M^2))$$\end{document}, with the per-iteration overhead mainly coming from covariance matrix inversion and spatial spectrum estimation. It is worth noting that the actual computational cost of these iterative methods is heavily dependent on the convergence speed, which deteriorates significantly in harsh scenarios such as low SNR, limited snapshots, and array imperfections. For existing deep learning-based DOA estimation methods, the DNN20 framework presents an overall complexity of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${O}_{DNN} = {O}(M^4)$$\end{document}, which is dominated by the fully connected layer operations with the input dimension proportional to the square of the number of array elements M. The SubNET22 achieves a theoretical complexity of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${O}_{SubNET} = {O}(M^3) + {O}(N_o M)$$\end{document}, where the EVD operation inherited from classical subspace methods remains the core computational bottleneck. Despite the relatively higher theoretical complexity of the proposed method compared with classical MUSIC and ESPRIT, there exists a fundamental difference in hardware acceleration potential and actual inference efficiency between the model-driven and data-driven frameworks, which is well validated by the measured average computation time. The inference process of the proposed cascaded DNNs consists entirely of parallelizable tensor operations, including matrix multiplication and element-wise nonlinear activation. These operations can be efficiently accelerated via pipeline optimization and massively parallel computing on mainstream hardware platforms such as GPUs and digital signal processors (DSPs), thus achieving extremely low end-to-end inference latency. In contrast, the computational overhead of classical subspace methods is dominated by the EVD operation, which is an inherently serial process with very limited parallelization capability. Especially in large-scale array and wide angular scanning scenarios, the actual runtime of these classical methods increases drastically with the cubic growth of EVD complexity, leading to inferior real-time performance compared with the proposed method. Compared with the iterative Sparsity-inducing methods (SBL and SPICE), the proposed method exhibits overwhelming superiority in both computational efficiency and inference stability. As shown in Table 2, the average running time of SBL and SPICE is 3 to 4 orders of magnitude higher than that of the proposed method. This is because the actual computational cost of iterative methods is determined by the number of convergence iterations \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_{iter}$$\end{document}, which often increases to tens or even hundreds in challenging non-ideal scenarios. Moreover, these methods face non-negligible risks of iteration divergence and unstable estimation under low SNR and array imperfection conditions. In sharp contrast, the proposed method adopts an end-to-end feedforward inference architecture without any iterative convergence process, yielding fixed and controllable computational overhead for each single-sample inference, which is highly favorable for engineering applications with stringent real-time requirements. When compared with other deep learning-based benchmarks, the proposed method also shows comprehensive advantages. Compared with the DNN20, the proposed method achieves competitive inference latency. For SubNET22, although it has a lower theoretical complexity, it retains the serial EVD bottleneck of classical subspace methods, which cannot fully exploit the parallel acceleration capability of modern hardware.

Conclusion

In this paper, we exploit deep learning to address realistic array imperfections and DOA estimation by decoupling error mitigation and DOA estimation. The proposed method attains robust performance across diverse error intensities, SNR levels, and snapshot conditions. Simulation results reveal that the spatial filters effectively rectify array errors, while MUSIC-NET achieves accurate DOA estimation. The framework surpasses state-of-the-art methods in terms of RMSE and generalization capabilities, especially in low-SNR and limited-snapshot circumstances.

Open problems of the 32 nd Workshop on Cycles and Colourings
(edited by Alfréd Onderko)

Abstract
Since its beginnings, every Cycles and Colourings workshop holds one or two open problem sessions; this document contains the problems (together with notes regarding the current state of the art and related bibliography) presented by participants of the 32 nd edition of the workshop which took place in Poprad, Slovakia during September 8 – 13, 2024 (see the workshop webpage https://candc.upjs.sk ).



1
Crumby colorings

János Barát

Alfréd Rényi Institute of Mathematics, Budapest, Hungary

University of Pannonia, Veszprém, Hungary

barat@renyi.hu

Consider a blue-red vertex coloring of a 3-connected cubic graph
G
.
Denote by
B
and
R
the subgraphs of
G
induced on blue and red vertices, respectively.
In
[
2
]
Thomassen conjectured that there is always such a blue-red coloring of
G
where
\Delta(B)\leq 1
,
\delta(R)\geq 1
and
R
does not contain a path with 3 edges.
Such a coloring is known as a crumby coloring.
If Thomassen’s conjecture on crumby colorings were true, it would imply that the square of every planar cubic graph is 7-colorable (Wegner’s conjecture proposed in
[
3
]
and proved in
[
2
]
). However, the conjecture on crumby colorings is false.
Hence, the following question arises:

Problem 1.1

What relaxation of the conditions on
R
guarantees that such a coloring of a 3-connected cubic graph always exists?

2
Various open problems related to list packing

Stijn Cambie

KU Leuven Kulak, Kortrijk, Belgium

stijn.cambie@kuleuven.be

A list-assignment
L
of a graph
G
is a function
L:V(G)\to 2^{\mathbb{N}}
. If
L:V(G)\rightarrow\binom{\mathbb{N}}{k},
it is a
k
-list-assignment of
G
.

An
L
-colouring of
G
is a colouring
c\colon V(G)\to\mathbb{N}
such that for every vertex
v
,
c(v)\in L(v)
.
It is proper if adjacent
u,v
satisfy
c(u)\not=c(v).

Two
L
-colourings
c_{i},c_{j}
are disjoint if
c_{i}(u)\not=c_{j}(u)
for every
u\in V(G).

The list-chromatic number (or choosability)
\chi_{\ell}(G)
of a graph is the minimum integer
k
such that every
k
-list-assignment
L
admits an
L
-colouring.
Given a
k
-list-assignment
L
of
G
we call a collection of
k
pairwise-disjoint
L
-colourings an
L
-packing of size
k
, or less specifically a list-packing.

The list packing number
\chi^{\star}_{\ell}(G)
of
G
is the least
k
such that
G
admits an
L
-packing of size
k
for any
k
-list-assignment
L
of
G
.

The list packing conjecture

When the list packing number
\chi_{\ell}^{\star}
was first introduced in
[
4
]
, inspired by
[
2
]
, the main conjecture appeared if
\chi_{\ell}^{\star}
is linearly bounded by
\chi_{\ell}
.
Meanwhile, these are still open:

Problem 2.1

Is there a graph
G
for which
\chi_{\ell}^{\star}(G)>\chi_{\ell}(G)+1
?

Problem 2.2

Is it true that
\chi_{\ell}^{\star}(G)\leq 2\chi_{\ell}(G)
for every graph
G
?

Planar bipartite graphs

Alon and Tarsi
[
1
]
proved that every planar bipartite graph
G
satisfies
\chi_{\ell}(G)\leq 3.

That is, if every vertex
v\in V(G)
has three different possible colours, from a list
L(v)
, one can select a colour
c(v)\in L(v)
for every
v\in V(G)
such that no two neighbours
u,v
satisfy
c(u)=c(v).

For a
3
-list-assignemnt of a cubic planar graph, we have the following problems:

Problem 2.3

Is it possible to choose two disjoint proper colourings?

Problem 2.4

Is it possible to choose three disjoint proper colourings?

In the case of cubic bipartite graphs, it is possible to find two disjoint proper colourings (problem similar to Problem 3) [unpublished].
Hence, the following question arises:
Is it possible to find three disjoint proper colourings in the case of cubic bipartite graphs?

Upper bound by a function of maximum degree

In
[
5
]
, the upper bound for
\chi_{\ell}^{\star}(G)
among graphs with maximum degree bounded by
3
was studied.
It is observed that the analogue of Brook’s theorem is also different.
The main question in this paper is the following one:

Problem 2.5

Is it true that
\chi_{\ell}^{\star}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+1
for every graph
G
?

Planar graphs

Almost simultaneously (even while in a different year),
[
6
]
and
[
9
]
gave improved upper bounds for the list packing numbers of planar graphs. Is it possible to improve these bounds?

Problem 2.6

Is there a planar graph with
\chi_{\ell}^{\star}(G)\geq 6
?

Problem 2.7

Is it true that
\chi_{\ell}^{\star}(G)\leq 7
for every planar graph?

Problem 2.8

For a triangle-free planar graph
G
, is it true that
\chi_{\ell}^{\star}(G)\leq 4
?

Removing an edge or vertex

For most chromatic numbers, it is trivial that removing a vertex or edge cannot change the chromatic number by more than one.
This is not true, for the list packing number.
The following questions posed in
[
7
]
, are nevertheless not trivial.

Problem 2.9

Let
v
and
e
be a vertex and an edge of a graph
G
.
Is it true that

\chi_{\ell}^{\star}(G)\leq\chi_{\ell}^{\star}(G\setminus v)+2
?

Or, is it true that
\chi_{\ell}^{\star}(G)\leq\chi_{\ell}^{\star}(G\setminus e)+2
?

Computational hardness

Last, we note that little is known about the computation hardness of list packing.
In
[
8
]
, there is a conjecture on a related question.

Problem 2.10

Fix
k>2
. Given a graph
G
as input, is the problem of deciding whether
\chi^{\star}_{\ell}(G)\leq k
complete for the complexity class
\Pi^{\bf P}_{2}
?

Problem 2.11
(Question 7.3 in
[
3
]
)

Fix
t>1
.
Is the problem of determining the value of
\chi^{\star}_{\ell}(G)
or
\chi^{\bullet}_{\ell}(G)
, given a graph
G
of treewidth at most
t
as input, in
P
?

3
Chromatic index of extended Petersen graphs

Geňa Hahn

Université de Montréal, Montréal, Canada

hahn@iro.umontreal.ca

Let
n=4k+1
for some integer
k\geq 1
, and let
S\subseteq\{1,2,\dots,2k\}
such that
|S|=k
. The extended Petersen graph
\mathrm{XP}(2n,S)
is a graph with the vertex set
V=\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\{1,2\}
, and the edge set

\displaystyle E=\{x_{1}y_{1}\colon d(x_{1},y_{1})\in S\}\cup\{x_{2}y_{2}\colon d(x_{2},y_{2})\in\overline{S}\}\cup\{x_{1}x_{2}\colon x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\},

where
\overline{S}=\{1,\dots,2k\}\setminus S
and
d(x,y)=\min\{|x-y|,n-|x-y|\}
.

Extended Petersen graphs were introduced in
[
1
]
by Horák and Rosa, who proposed the following conjecture:

Conjecture 3.1
(Horák, Rosa
[
1
]
)

Every extended Petersen graph, except Petersen graph itself, is of Class 1.

4
A conjecture on rainbow connectivity

Geňa Hahn

Université de Montréal, Montréal, Canada

hahn@iro.umontreal.ca

In
[
1
]
Chartrand proposed a concept of rainbow connectivity: an edge-colored graph
G
is rainbow connected if every two vertices of
G
are connected by a rainbow path (that is a path with all its edges colored with different colors).
The minimum number of colors required so that the colored graph is rainbow connected, is called the rainbow connection number, and it is denoted by
\mathrm{rc}(G)
.

Problem 4.1

Does there exist a constant
c\geq 0
such that if
G
is a non-complete graph of order
n
with
\delta(G)\geq\frac{n}{2}+c
, then
\mathrm{rc}(G)=2
?

In particular, does
\delta(G)\geq\frac{n}{2}
imply that
\mathrm{rc}(G)=2
?

It is known due to Gimbel
[
2
]
that if
\delta(G)\geq\frac{n}{2}+\log_{2}(n)-1
then
\mathrm{rc}(G)=2
.

Edge-colorability and perfect matchings of
5
-edge-connected
5
-regular graphs

Davide Mattiolo
1
1

1

(joint work with Y. Ma, E. Steffen and I. H. Wolf)

KU Leuven Kulak, Kortrijk, Belgium

davide.mattiolo@kuleuven.be

A graph
G
is Class
1
if it is
\Delta(G)
-edge-colorable, otherwise it is Class
2
.

It is known that
r
-edge-connected
r
-regular Class
2
graphs exist for all integers
r\geq 2
, with
r\neq 5
(see
[
2
]
and
[
1
]
).

Surprisingly, it seems that no
5
-edge-connected
5
-regular Class
2
graph is known.

Problem 5.1
(Ma, Mattiolo, Steffen, Wolf
[
1
]
)

Is there any
5
-edge-connected
5
-regular Class
2
graph?

A negative answer to Problem 1 would imply the Berge-Fulkerson and the
5
-cycle double cover conjectures, see
[
1
]
for more details.

An
r
-graph is an
r
-regular graph
G
such that every odd set
X\subseteq V(G)
is connected by at least
r
edges to its complement
V(G)\setminus X
.
An
r
-graph is poorly matchable if any two of its perfect matchings intersect. In
[
3
]
it is shown that there are poorly matchable
r
-graphs for every
r\geq 3.
All such constructed graphs contain a
4
-edge-cut. Moreover, Thomassen
[
4
]
conjectured that there is a natural number
r_{0}
such that, for every
r\geq r_{0}
, every
r
-edge-connected
r
-graph has two disjoint perfect matchings. Since the poorly matchable
4
-graphs constructed in
[
3
]
are
4
-edge-connected, such an integer
r_{0}
must be at least
5
.

Problem 5.2

Is there a poorly matchable
5
-edge-connected
5
-graph?

In
[
1
]
it is proved that, if every
5
-edge-connected
5
-graph has two edge-disjoint perfect matchings then the Fan-Raspaud Conjecture holds.

6
Subcubic
K_{3}
-free graphs of Class 2

Ingo Schiermeyer

Technische Universität Bergakademie Freiberg, Freiberg, Germany

AGH University of Krakow, Krakow, Poland

Ingo.Schiermeyer@math.tu-freiberg.de

It is known that subdividing a single edge of a
3
-edge colorable cubic graph yields an overfull subcubic graph
G
. Hence,
G
is of Class 2, i.e.,
\chi^{\prime}(G)=4
.
Starting with a
K_{3}
-free cubic graph, we produce, in that way, a
K_{3}
-free subcubic graph of Class 2.
This raises the following question:

Problem 6.1

Is it possible to describe all subcubic
K_{3}
-free graphs of Class 2?

This class of graphs contains all snarks, which are cubic
graphs of Class 2. The existence of snarks is a challenging topic on its own.

7
Special orderings of vertices

Zsolt Tuza

University of Pannonia, Veszprém, Hungary

Alfréd Rényi Institute of Mathematics, Budapest, Hungary

tuza.zsolt@mik.uni-pannon.hu

The following questions are raised in a joint paper with Yair Caro
[
1
]
, concerning some variants of anti-Ramsey problems.

Suppose that the vertices of a graph
G
are put in a linear order
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
.
For
i=1,\dots,n
define
d^{-}(v_{i})=|\{j<i\colon v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)\}|
and
d^{+}(v_{i})=|\{j>i\colon v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)\}|
.

We are interested in the existence of two types of orderings (and a way to find them as efficiently as possible, if they exist) given by the following two conditions:

•

Type-A order: for each
v\in V(G)
,
d^{-}(v)
is odd or equal to 0;

•

Type-B order: for each
v\in V(G)
,
d^{-}(v)
is odd or equal to 0 (as in a Type-A order), or
d^{-}(v)
is even and
d^{+}(v)=0
.

These types of orders are useful in the context of anti-Ramsey problems and odd-coloring
[
1
]
.
We propose the following algorithmic questions.

Problem 7.1

Can it be decided in polynomial time whether a graph
G
admits a Type-A order, or a Type-B order?

Problem 7.2

If a Type-A or Type-B order exists, can it be found in polynomial time?

Of course, for a given linear order of the vertices, checking whether it is a Type-A or Type-B order can be tested in linear time.

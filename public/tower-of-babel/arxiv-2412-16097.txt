Dual-Polarized Beyond Diagonal RIS
Matteo Nerini, Bruno Clerckx

Abstract
Beyond diagonal reconfigurable intelligent surface (BD-RIS) is a family of RIS architectures more flexible than conventional RIS. While BD-RIS has been primarily analyzed assuming uni-polarized systems, modern wireless deployments are dual-polarized. To address this gap, this paper investigates the fundamental limits of dual-polarized BD-RIS-aided systems. We derive the scaling laws governing the performance of BD-RIS and the Pareto frontier of the trade-off between performance and circuit complexity enabled by BD-RIS. Theoretical results show that the group-connected RIS with group size 2 provides remarkable gains over conventional RIS in both Rayleigh and line-of-sight (LoS) channels, while maintaining a reduced circuit complexity.



I
Introduction

Reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS) is expected to play a key role in future wireless communications, allowing us to optimize the propagation environment through surfaces made of multiple elements with reconfigurable scattering properties
[
1
]
.
Conventionally, a RIS has been implemented by independently controlling each element with a tunable load, leading to a diagonal phase shift matrix, formally known as scattering matrix.
Because of the structure of its scattering matrix, this conventional RIS architecture is referred to as diagonal RIS (D-RIS).
To improve the flexibility of D-RIS, more general RIS architectures have been proposed, under the name of beyond diagonal RIS (BD-RIS), which are characterized by scattering matrices not limited to being diagonal
[
2
]
.

Several BD-RIS architectures have been developed.
The fully-connected RIS has been proposed by interconnecting all the RIS elements to each other through tunable impedance components
[
2
]
.
This architecture has the maximum circuit complexity, in terms of the number of impedance components, and achieves the maximum performance
[
2
]
.
To balance performance and complexity, the group-connected RIS has been developed by dividing the RIS elements into groups and interconnecting only RIS elements within groups
[
2
]
.
In
[
3
]
, the tree- and forest-connected RISs have been proposed, which are the least complex BD-RIS architectures achieving the same performance as fully- and group-connected RISs, respectively, in single-user systems.
The fundamental limit of the trade-off between performance and complexity offered by BD-RIS has been investigated in
[
4
]
, where its Pareto frontier was analytically provided for single-user systems.
Recently, the Q-stem connected RIS has emerged to provide a favorable performance-complexity trade-off in multi-user systems
[
5
]
.

The performance offered by BD-RIS has been analyzed in existing works by implicitly assuming uni-polarized systems.
However, modern
multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO)
wireless systems utilize dual-polarized antenna arrays to accommodate more antennas within limited space, and improve diversity using the polarization dimension
[
6
]
.
Dual-polarized RIS-aided systems have been analyzed considering the conventional D-RIS architecture and compared to uni-polarized systems
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
.
In addition, dual-polarized D-RIS has been proposed to perform broad beamforming
[
10
]
, to enable holographic
MIMO

[
11
]
, and to implement massive
MIMO
transmission
[
12
]
.
Given the practical importance of dual-polarized RIS, in this study, we analyze the fundamental performance limits of dual-polarized BD-RIS.

The contributions of this study are as follows.

First
, we analyze the performance achieved by BD-RIS in dual-polarized RIS-aided systems.
Specifically, we derive the scaling laws of the received power considering Rayleigh and
line-of-sight (LoS)
channels, and compare them with the scaling laws for uni-polarized systems.

Second
, we provide the gain of BD-RIS over D-RIS as a function of the inverse of the
cross-polar discrimination (XPD)
.
Although BD-RIS was known to provide no gain over D-RIS with LoS channels
[
2
]
, we analytically show that BD-RIS offers remarkable gains over D-RIS in dual-polarized systems with both Rayleigh and
LoS
channels.

Third
, we analytically derive the Pareto frontier of the trade-off between performance and circuit complexity offered by BD-RIS in dual-polarized systems with
LoS
channels, which differs from the one derived for uni-polarized systems in
[
4
]
.
Although the tree-connected RIS achieves maximum performance under any channel realization with
the lowest circuit complexity, we prove that a lower-complexity BD-RIS is sufficient to get maximum performance in dual-polarized LoS systems, i.e., the group-connected RIS with group size 2.

II
Dual-Polarized RIS-Aided System Model

Consider a wireless system between a single-antenna
transmitter (Tx)
and a single-antenna
receiver (Rx)
, e.g., an access point and a mobile terminal in cell-free
MIMO
, aided by a RIS with
N
elements.
Denoting the transmitted signal as
x\in\mathbb{C}
, the received signal
y\in\mathbb{C}
is given by
y=hx+n
, where
h\in\mathbb{C}
is the wireless channel between
Tx
and
Rx
and
n\in\mathbb{C}
is the noise.
The wireless channel
h
is modeled as a function of the RIS scattering matrix
\boldsymbol{\Theta}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}
according to the widely used cascaded model
h=\mathbf{h}_{R}\boldsymbol{\Theta}\mathbf{h}_{T}
, where
\mathbf{h}_{R}\in\mathbb{C}^{1\times N}
is the channel between RIS and
Rx
,
\mathbf{h}_{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times 1}
is the channel between
Tx
and RIS, and we neglect the direct channel between
Tx
and
Rx

[
1
]
.
Thus, the received power writes as
P_{R}=P_{T}\left|\mathbf{h}_{R}\boldsymbol{\Theta}\mathbf{h}_{T}\right|^{2}
,
where
P_{T}=\mathbb{E}[|x|]
is the transmitted power.
We consider the BD-RIS to be lossless, realistic for half-wavelength antenna spacing.

Fig. 1:
Dual-polarized RIS-aided system where
Tx
and
Rx
have opposite polarization.

We assume this RIS-aided system to be dual-polarized, where the RIS elements, the transmitting antenna, and the receiving antenna are either vertically or horizontally polarized.
Specifically, we assume
N/2
RIS elements to have vertical polarization and
N/2
RIS elements to have horizontal polarization, as commonly considered in dual-polarized systems
[
6
]
.
With no loss of generality, we order the RIS elements such that the first
N/2
are vertically polarized and the last
N/2
are horizontally polarized, as shown in Fig.
1
.
The effect of the antenna polarization is accounted for in the channels
\mathbf{h}_{R}
and
\mathbf{h}_{T}
, by expressing them as
\mathbf{h}_{R}=\mathbf{p}_{R}\odot\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{R}
, and
\mathbf{h}_{T}=\mathbf{p}_{T}\odot\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{T}
, where
\odot
denotes the Hadamard product,
\mathbf{p}_{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times N}
and
\mathbf{p}_{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 1}
include the effects of the power imbalance between the different polarizations, and
\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{R}\in\mathbb{C}^{1\times N}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times 1}
are the uni-polarized fading channels
[
6
]
.
The vectors
\mathbf{p}_{R}
and
\mathbf{p}_{T}
are modeled depending on the polarizations of the
Tx
and
Rx
.
When the
Rx
is vertically or horizontally polarized, we have
\mathbf{p}_{R}=[1,\sqrt{\chi}]\otimes\mathbf{1}_{1\times N/2}
or
\mathbf{p}_{R}=[\sqrt{\chi},1]\otimes\mathbf{1}_{1\times N/2}
, respectively, where
\otimes
denotes the Kronecker product and
0\leq\chi\leq 1
is the inverse of the
XPD

[
6
]
.
Similarly, when the
Tx
is vertically or horizontally polarized, we have

\mathbf{p}_{T}=[1,\sqrt{\chi}]^{T}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{N/2\times 1}
or
\mathbf{p}_{T}=[\sqrt{\chi},1]^{T}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{N/2\times 1}
, respectively.
The fading channels
\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{R}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{T}
are modeled in this study considering two popular fading distributions, namely
independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.)
Rayleigh channels
\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{R}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})
and
\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{T}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})
, and
LoS
channels
\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{R}=[e^{j\varphi_{R,1}},\ldots,e^{j\varphi_{R,N}}]
and
\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{T}=[e^{j\varphi_{T,1}},\ldots,e^{j\varphi_{T,N}}]^{T}
, where
\varphi_{R,n},\varphi_{T,n}\in[0,2\pi]
, for
n=1,\ldots,N
.

III
Received Power Scaling Laws and BD-RIS Gain

In this section, we derive the scaling laws of the received power in the case of a D-RIS and a BD-RIS in a dual-polarized system.
Considering
P_{T}=1
for simplicity, a D-RIS, also known as single-connected RIS
[
2
]
, achieves a received power

P_{R}^{\mathrm{Single}}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|\left[\mathbf{h}_{R}\right]_{n}\left[\mathbf{h}_{T}\right]_{n}\right|\right)^{2},

(1)

while a BD-RIS can achieve a received power

P_{R}^{\mathrm{Fully}}=\left\|\mathbf{h}_{R}\right\|^{2}\left\|\mathbf{h}_{T}\right\|^{2},

(2)

when the fully-connected RIS is used
[
2
]
.
We consider the received power achieved by a fully-connected RIS as this is the BD-RIS architecture enabling maximum performance.
Since the fully-connected RIS is more flexible than the single-connected RIS, we have
P_{R}^{\mathrm{Fully}}\geq P_{R}^{\mathrm{Single}}
, as it can be shown with the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality
[
2
]
.
In addition, we investigate the gain of BD-RIS over D-RIS, introduced as

G=\lim_{N\to+\infty}\frac{\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{Fully}}}{\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{Single}}},

(3)

where
\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{Fully}}=\mathbb{E}[P_{R}^{\mathrm{Fully}}]
and
\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{Single}}=\mathbb{E}[P_{R}^{\mathrm{Single}}]
are the received powers averaged across the channel realizations.

We study two scenarios, namely where the
Tx
and
Rx
have the same polarization and opposite polarization, as these are the two extreme cases that can occur due to
Tx
or
Rx
mobility.
For each of the two scenarios, we consider Rayleigh and
LoS
fading channels, giving a total of four scenarios.

III-A

Same Polarization at Tx and Rx with Rayleigh Channels

Consider a dual-polarized RIS-aided system where the
Tx
and
Rx
have the same polarization (assumed to be vertical with no loss of generality), and the channels are
i.i.d.
Rayleigh distributed.
For a single-connected RIS, since

|[\mathbf{h}_{R}]_{n}[\mathbf{h}_{T}]_{n}|=|[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{R}]_{n}[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{T}]_{n}|
, for
n=1,\ldots,N/2
, and
|[\mathbf{h}_{R}]_{n}[\mathbf{h}_{T}]_{n}|=\chi|[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{R}]_{n}[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{T}]_{n}|
, for
n=N/2+1,\ldots,N
, (
1
) becomes

P_{R}^{\mathrm{Single}}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N/2}\left|[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{R}]_{n}[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{T}]_{n}\right|+\chi\sum_{n=N/2+1}^{N}\left|[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{R}]_{n}[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{T}]_{n}\right|\right)^{2}.

(4)

Taking the expectation of (
4
) by expanding the square and following the steps in
[
2
, Section IV-E]
, we get

\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{Single}}=\frac{1+\chi^{2}}{2}\left(N+N\left(\frac{N}{2}-1\right)\frac{\pi^{2}}{16}\right)+\frac{\pi^{2}\chi}{32}N^{2},

(5)

where we exploited
\mathbb{E}[|[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{R}]_{n}|]=\mathbb{E}[|[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{T}]_{n}|]=\sqrt{\pi}/2
and
\mathbb{E}[|[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{R}]_{n}|^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[|[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{T}]_{n}|^{2}]=1
, for
n=1,\ldots,N
.
The received power
\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{Single}}
in (
5
) grows with
\chi
, spanning from
\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{Single}}=N/2+N/2(N/2-1)\pi^{2}/16
when
\chi=0
to
\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{Single}}=N+N(N-1)\pi^{2}/16
when
\chi=1
.
Besides, for a fully-connected RIS, (
2
) can be rewritten as

P_{R}^{\mathrm{Fully}}=\left(\left\|[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{R}]_{1:N/2}\right\|^{2}+\chi\left\|[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{R}]_{N/2+1:N}\right\|^{2}\right)\\
\times\left(\left\|[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{T}]_{1:N/2}\right\|^{2}+\chi\left\|[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{T}]_{N/2+1:N}\right\|^{2}\right),

(6)

since
\|\mathbf{h}_{R}\|^{2}=\|[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{R}]_{1:N/2}\|^{2}+\chi\|[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{R}]_{N/2+1:N}\|^{2}
and
\|\mathbf{h}_{T}\|^{2}=\|[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{T}]_{1:N/2}\|^{2}+\chi\|[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{T}]_{N/2+1:N}\|^{2}
.
Thus, by exploiting the independence between
\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{R}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{T}
, and that
\mathbb{E}[\|[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{R}]_{1:N/2}\|^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[\|[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{R}]_{N/2+1:N}\|^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[\|[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{T}]_{1:N/2}\|^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[\|[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{T}]_{N/2+1:N}\|^{2}]=N/2
, the expectation of (
6
) reads as

\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{Fully}}=\left(\frac{N}{2}+\chi\frac{N}{2}\right)^{2}=\frac{\left(1+\chi\right)^{2}}{4}N^{2},

(7)

increasing with
\chi
.
Specifically, we have
\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{Fully}}=(N/2)^{2}
when
\chi=0
and
\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{Fully}}=N^{2}
when
\chi=1
.
Note that (
5
) and (
7
) with
\chi=1
correspond to the scaling laws for uni-polarized systems
[
2
]
, while with
\chi=0
they correspond to the same scaling laws of a RIS with
N/2
elements, since only the
N/2
vertically polarized elements can be effectively used.
By substituting (
5
) and (
7
) into (
3
), we obtain
G=16/\pi^{2}\approx 1.62
.
The gain
G
is independent of
\chi
as it is computed for
N\rightarrow\infty
, and effectively using
N
or
N/2
RIS elements, in the extreme cases of
\chi=1
and
\chi=0
, respectively, does not impact
G
.

III-B

Same Polarization at Tx and Rx with
LoS
Channels

Consider a dual-polarized RIS-aided system where the
Tx
and
Rx
have the same polarization (assumed to be vertical), and the channels are
LoS
.
With
LoS
channels, we have
|[\mathbf{h}_{R}]_{n}[\mathbf{h}_{T}]_{n}|=1
, for
n=1,\ldots,N/2
,
|[\mathbf{h}_{R}]_{n}[\mathbf{h}_{T}]_{n}|=\chi
, for
n=N/2+1,\ldots,N
, and
\|\mathbf{h}_{R}\|^{2}=\|\mathbf{h}_{T}\|^{2}=N/2+\chi N/2
.
Thus, both (
1
) and (
2
) boil down to

P_{R}^{\mathrm{Single}}=P_{R}^{\mathrm{Fully}}=\frac{(1+\chi)^{2}}{4}N^{2},

(8)

giving the received power achieved by both single- and fully-connected RIS.
Consequently, the gain of BD-RIS over D-RIS is
G=1
, independently of the value of
\chi
.

III-C

Opposite Polarization at Tx and Rx with Rayleigh Channels

Consider the
Tx
and
Rx
to have opposite polarization (vertically and horizontally polarized, respectively, as in Fig.
1
, with no loss of generality), and Rayleigh distributed channels.
By noticing that
|[\mathbf{h}_{R}]_{n}[\mathbf{h}_{T}]_{n}|=\sqrt{\chi}|[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{R}]_{n}[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{T}]_{n}|
, for
n=1,\ldots,N
, (
1
) can be rewritten as

P_{R}^{\mathrm{Single}}=\chi\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{R}]_{n}[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{T}]_{n}\right|\right)^{2},

(9)

whose expectation is given by

\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{Single}}=\chi\left(N+N\left(N-1\right)\frac{\pi^{2}}{16}\right),

(10)

following the steps in
[
2
, Section IV-E]
.
Clearly, the scaling law in (
10
) increases with
\chi
, being
\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{Single}}=0
when
\chi=0
and
\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{Single}}=N+N(N-1)\pi^{2}/16
when
\chi=1
.
Besides, the fully-connected RIS achieves a received power of

\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{Fully}}=\frac{\left(1+\chi\right)^{2}}{4}N^{2},

(11)

following the discussion in Section
III-A
.
The gain of BD-RIS over D-RIS is given by substituting (
10
) and (
11
) into (
3
) as

G=\frac{4\left(1+\chi\right)^{2}}{\pi^{2}\chi},

(12)

which decreases with
\chi
.
Interestingly, we have
G=+\infty
when
\chi=0
and
G=16/\pi^{2}\approx 1.62
when
\chi=1
, showing that the gain is significant for small values of
\chi
.

III-D

Opposite Polarization at Tx and Rx with LoS Channels

Consider the
Tx
and
Rx
to have opposite polarization (vertically and horizontally polarized, respectively), and the channels to be
LoS
.
With
LoS
channels,
|[\mathbf{h}_{R}]_{n}[\mathbf{h}_{T}]_{n}|=\sqrt{\chi}
, for
n=1,\ldots,N
.
Thus, (

) simplifies to

P_{R}^{\mathrm{Single}}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sqrt{\chi}\right)^{2}=\chi N^{2},

(13)

increasing with
\chi
and ranging from
P_{R}^{\mathrm{Single}}=0
to
P_{R}^{\mathrm{Single}}=N^{2}
.
For a fully-connected RIS, (
2
) boils down to

P_{R}^{\mathrm{Fully}}=\frac{\left(1+\chi\right)^{2}}{4}N^{2},

(14)

since
\|\mathbf{h}_{R}\|^{2}=\|\mathbf{h}_{T}\|^{2}=N/2+\chi N/2
.
By substituting (
13
) and (
14
) into (

), we obtain

G=\frac{\left(1+\chi\right)^{2}}{4\chi},

(15)

decreasing with
\chi
.
We observe that
G=+\infty
when
\chi=0
and
G=1
when
\chi=1
, showing remarkable gains of BD-RIS over D-RIS also with
LoS

\chi
.

IV
Optimal BD-RISs with LoS Channels

We have shown that BD-RIS is beneficial over D-RIS also under
LoS
channels, in the case of a dual-polarized wireless system where the
Tx
and
Rx
have opposite polarization.
Focusing on this scenario, unexplored in previous work, we characterize the optimal BD-RIS architectures that achieve the Pareto frontier of the performance-complexity trade-off, where the performance is the received power
P_{R}
achievable by the BD-RIS and the complexity as the number of tunable impedance components
C
in the BD-RIS.
We begin by introducing a low-complex BD-RIS architecture that can achieve the same performance as the fully-connected RIS.

Proposition 1
.

Consider a dual-polarized RIS-aided system with
Tx
and
Rx
having opposite polarization and
LoS
channels.
In this system, any group-connected RIS with group size 2 where each group contains two RIS elements with opposite polarization achieves the performance upper bound
P_{R}^{\mathrm{Fully}}
in (
14
) for any value of
\chi
.

Proof.

Following
[
2
]
, the received power achieved by a group connected RIS with group size 2 is given by

P_{R}^{\mathrm{Group2}}=\left(\sum_{g=1}^{N/2}\left\|\mathbf{h}_{R,g}\right\|\left\|\mathbf{h}_{T,g}\right\|\right)^{2},

(16)

where
\mathbf{h}_{R,g}\in\mathbb{C}^{1\times 2}
and
\mathbf{h}_{T,g}\in\mathbb{C}^{2\times 1}
contain the two entries of
\mathbf{h}_{R}
and
\mathbf{h}_{T}
, respectively, corresponding to the two RIS elements in the
g
th group.
Assume with no loss of generality that the
Tx
is vertically polarized and the
Rx
is horizontally polarized, as in Fig.
1
.
Since each group contains two RIS elements with opposite polarization,
\mathbf{h}_{R,g}=[\sqrt{\chi}e^{j\varphi_{R,n_{g}}},e^{j\varphi_{R,N/2+{m_{g}}}}]
and
\mathbf{h}_{T,g}=[e^{j\varphi_{T,n_{g}}},\sqrt{\chi}e^{j\varphi_{T,N/2+{m_{g}}}}]^{T}
, for some
n_{g},m_{g}\in\{1,\ldots,N/2\}
.
Thus, we have
\|\mathbf{h}_{R,g}\|=\|\mathbf{h}_{T,g}\|=\sqrt{1+\chi}
, allowing us to rewrite (
16
) as

P_{R}^{\mathrm{Group2}}=\left(\sum_{g=1}^{N/2}\left(1+\chi\right)\right)^{2}=\frac{\left(1+\chi\right)^{2}}{4}N^{2},

(17)

which is equal to (
14
), and proves the proposition.
∎

Proposition
1
implies that the performance upper bound
P_{R}^{\mathrm{Fully}}
in (
14
) can be achieved with a BD-RIS architecture having circuit complexity
C=3N/2
(
N
impedance components connecting each of the
N
RIS elements to ground, and an impedance component in each of the
N/2
groups).
Remarkably, a complexity of
C=3N/2
is considerably less than the complexity of the fully-connected RIS, being
C=N(N+1)/2

[
2
]
.
To bridge between the single-connected RIS (
C=N
) and the group-connected RIS with group size 2 (
C=3N/2
), the following proposition provides the maximum performance that can be achieved with a BD-RIS with complexity
C=N+n
, for
n=1,\ldots,N/2-1
, and characterizes the BD-RIS architecture achieving such performance, denoted as “optimal”.

Proposition 2
.

The optimal BD-RIS architecture with
C=N+n
tunable impedance components, for some
n\in\{1,\ldots,N/2-1\}
, has
n
groups with size 2 each containing two RIS elements with opposite polarization and
N-2n
groups with size 1.
This optimal BD-RIS architecture achieves a received power given by

P_{R}^{(n)}=\left(n\left(1+\chi\right)+\left(N-2n\right)\sqrt{\chi}\right)^{2}.

(18)

Proof.

An optimal BD-RIS architecture with
N
elements and complexity
C=N+n
, for some
n\in\{1,\ldots,N/2-1\}
, has
G=2N-C=N-n
groups, according to
[
4
, Proposition 1]
.
Thus, such a BD-RIS achieves a received power

P_{R}^{(n)}=\left(\sum_{g=1}^{N-n}\left\|\mathbf{h}_{R,g}\right\|\left\|\mathbf{h}_{T,g}\right\|\right)^{2},

(19)

where
\mathbf{h}_{R,g}\in\mathbb{C}^{1\times N_{g}}
and
\mathbf{h}_{T,g}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{g}\times 1}
contain the
N_{g}
entries of
\mathbf{h}_{R}
and
\mathbf{h}_{T}
, respectively, corresponding to the
N_{g}
RIS elements in the
g
th group, and
N_{g}
denotes the group size of the
g
th group.
Since the sum of the sizes of the
N-n
groups must be
N
, i.e.,
\sum_{g=1}^{N-n}N_{g}=N
, we have at least
N-2n
groups with size 1.
This can be proved by contradiction: assuming fewer than
N-2n
values
N_{g}
are 1, specifically
N-2n-k
with
k\geq 1
, the remaining
n+k
values
N_{g}
must sum to
2n+k
, which is impossible because the minimum sum of
n+k
natural numbers greater than 1 is
2n+2k
.
Since there are at least
N-2n
groups with size 1, we can rewrite (
19
) as

P_{R}^{(n)}=\left(\sum_{g=1}^{n}\left\|\mathbf{h}_{R,g}\right\|\left\|\mathbf{h}_{T,g}\right\|+\sum_{g=n+1}^{N-n}\left|h_{R,g}h_{T,g}\right|\right)^{2},

(20)

where we assumed with no loss of generality that
N-2n
groups with size 1 are indexed
g=n+1,\ldots,N-n
.
By noticing that
|h_{R,g}h_{T,g}|=\sqrt{\chi}
, for
g=n+1,\ldots,N-n
, since
Tx
and
Rx
have opposite polarization, (
20
) simplifies as

P_{R}^{(n)}=\left(\sum_{g=1}^{n}\left\|\mathbf{h}_{R,g}\right\|\left\|\mathbf{h}_{T,g}\right\|+\left(N-2n\right)\sqrt{\chi}\right)^{2},

(21)

and the problem of maximizing
P_{R}^{(n)}
becomes equivalent to maximizing the term

Q=\sum_{g=1}^{n}\left\|\mathbf{h}_{R,g}\right\|\left\|\mathbf{h}_{T,g}\right\|,

(22)

by designing the polarizations of the
2n
RIS elements considered in the term
Q
and how to divide them into
n
groups.
Specifically, among the
2n
RIS elements in
Q
,
n_{v}
have vertical polarization and
n_{h}
have horizontal polarization, such that
n_{v}+n_{h}=2n
, and
n_{v}
and
n_{h}
need to be optimized to maximize
Q
.
In the following, we maximize
Q
by assuming that
n_{v}=n_{h}=n
, and we show afterward that this is the optimal design of the polarizations.

Assuming that
n_{v}=n_{h}=n
, the term
Q
is maximized when the
n
groups have size 2 and each contains two RIS elements with opposite polarization, following Proposition
1
.
In this case, we have

Q=\sqrt{\frac{\left(1+\chi\right)^{2}}{4}\left(2n\right)^{2}}=n\left(1+\chi\right),

(23)

because of Proposition
1
.
Thus, by substituting (
23
) into (
21
), we obtain that the optimal received power is given by

P_{R}^{(n)}=\left(n\left(1+\chi\right)+\left(N-2n\right)\sqrt{\chi}\right)^{2},

(24)

which proves the proposition if
n_{v}=n_{h}=n
is optimal.

.

To prove that
n_{v}=n_{h}=n
is optimal to maximize
Q
, we upper bound
Q
by

Q\leq\left\|\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{R}\right\|\left\|\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{T}\right\|,

(25)

where
\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{R}\in\mathbb{C}^{1\times 2n}
and
\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{2n\times 1}
are introduced as
\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{R}=[\mathbf{h}_{R,1},\ldots,\mathbf{h}_{R,n}]
and
\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{T}=[\mathbf{h}_{T,1}^{T},\ldots,\mathbf{h}_{T,n}^{T}]^{T}
, which follows from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality
[
2
]
.
Assuming with no loss of generality that the
Tx
is vertically polarized and the
Rx
is horizontally polarized, we have
\|\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{R}\|=\sqrt{n_{v}\chi+n_{h}}
and
\|\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{R}\|=\sqrt{n_{v}+n_{h}\chi}
, and we can express (
25
) as

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\left(n_{v}\chi+n_{h}\right)\left(n_{v}+n_{h}\chi\right)}

(26)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-\left(1-\chi\right)^{2}n_{h}^{2}+2n\left(1-\chi\right)n_{h}+4n^{2}\chi},

(27)

where we used
n_{v}+n_{h}=2n
.
By taking the derivative of the upper bound in (
27
), we find that it is maximized when
n_{h}=n
, yielding a maximum value of

Q\leq\sqrt{n^{2}\left(1-\chi\right)^{2}+4n^{2}\chi}=n\left(1+\chi\right),

(28)

which is the same as (
23
) proving the optimality of the solution with
n_{v}=n_{h}=n
and concluding the proof.
∎

In Fig.
3
, we report the Pareto frontier of the performance-complexity trade-off offered by BD-RIS, by fixing
N=64
, when
Tx
and
Rx
have opposite polarization and with
LoS
channels, as given in Proposition
18
.
We make the following two observations.

First
, the single-connected RIS is the simplest architecture achieving the lowest performance and the fully-connected RIS is the most complex architecture achieving the highest performance.
The two architectures give different performances with
LoS
channels as long as
\chi\neq 1
, and the performance gap increases as
\chi
decreases.
Interestingly, the gain of BD-RIS over D-RIS under
LoS
remained unexplored in previous work.

Second
, the group-connected RIS with group size 2, is the least complex architecture achieving the performance upper bound, i.e., the same performance as the fully-connected RIS.
Thus, fully-/tree-connected RISs and group-/forest-connected RISs with group sizes different from 2 achieve the performance upper bound, but with increased circuit complexity and the tree-connected RIS is not the least complex BD-RIS achieving maximum performance in this scenario.

V
Conclusion

We analyze the fundamental performance limits of dual-polarized BD-RIS, by deriving the scaling laws of the achievable received power and the gain offered over D-RIS.
We observe that BD-RIS offers important gains over D-RIS also with
LoS
channels, especially when the inverse of the
XPD
is small.
In addition, we derive the Pareto frontier of the performance-complexity trade-off achieved by dual-polarized BD-RISs, showing that the received power upper bound is achieved with a group-connected RIS with group size 2.
This new insight is crucial for guiding the future prototyping and deployment of low-complexity BD-RIS architectures.

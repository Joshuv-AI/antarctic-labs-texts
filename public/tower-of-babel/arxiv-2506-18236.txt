Differential operators on hermitian modular forms on U ⁡ ( n , n ) \mathrm{U}(n,n) Thanks: This work was supported by JST SPRING, Grant Number JPMJSP2110.
Nobuki TAKEDA

Abstract
. We construct explicit differential operators on hermitian modular forms, extending methods developed for Siegel modular forms. These differential operators are closely related to the two-variable spherical pluriharmonic polynomials. We construct explicit bases for the space of such polynomials and use them to build concrete operators. As an application, we derive an exact pullback formula for hermitian Eisenstein series.

1.
Introduction

In the theory of Siegel modular forms, differential operators have been explicitly constructed and employed by various researchers to investigate both algebraic and analytic properties, including Fourier expansions, inner products, and their relations to special values of
L
-functions (see for example
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
12
]
).
In particular, Ibukiyama proposed a general framework for differential operators on Siegel modular forms, introducing conditions under which these operators preserve automorphic properties
[
8
]
.
This framework has since been further developed using representation-theoretic methods, shedding light on deeper structural aspects
[
17
]
.

In contrast, the theory of differential operators for Hermitian modular forms remains largely undeveloped, with only a handful of known results in specific cases
[
3
]
.
The aim of this paper is to generalize the techniques developed in the context of Siegel modular forms to the Hermitian setting. Specifically, we construct explicit differential operators on Hermitian modular forms and investigate their structural and arithmetic applications.

We now formulate a specific condition satisfied by these differential operators.

Let
K
be a quadratic imaginary extension of a totally real field
K^{+}
, and let
\mathbf{a}
(resp.
\mathbf{h}
) denote the set of archimedean (resp. non-archimedean) places of
K^{+}
.
We define
m:=m_{K^{+}}:=\#\mathbf{a}=[K^{+}:\mathbb{Q}]
.
Let
M_{\rho}(\Gamma_{K}^{(n)}(\mathfrak{n}))
denote the complex vector space of Hermitian modular forms of weight
\rho
and level
\mathfrak{n}
on the Hermitian upper half space
\mathfrak{H}_{n}^{\mathbf{a}}
.
Let
(\rho_{n,\bm{k}},V_{n,\bm{k}})
denote the corresponding representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.

Let
(\bm{k},\bm{l})=(\bm{k}_{v},\bm{l}_{v})_{v}\in\mathbf{a}
be a family of pairs of dominant integral weights, satisfying
\ell(\bm{k}_{v})\leq n
and
\ell(\bm{l}_{v})\leq n
for each
v\in\mathbf{a}
.
We then define a representation
\rho_{n,(\bm{k},\bm{l})}=\bigboxtimes_{v\in\mathbf{a}}(\rho_{n,\bm{k}_{v}}\boxtimes\rho_{n,\bm{l}_{v}})
of the group
K_{n,\infty}^{\mathbb{C}}:=\prod_{v\in\mathbf{a}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
.
Let
G_{n}
denote a unitary group defined over
K^{+}
.

Fix integers
n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}
with
n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{d}\geq 1
and set
n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{d}
.
Let
(\rho_{s},V_{s})
be a representation of
K_{(n_{s})}^{\mathbb{C}}
for
s=1,\ldots,d
, and let
\kappa=(\kappa_{v})_{v}\in\mathbf{a}
be a family of positive integers.
We consider differential operators
\mathbb{D}
valued in

, which act on scalar-valued functions on
\mathfrak{H}_{n}
and satisfy the following condition:

Condition (A)
.

For any modular form

, we have

\mathrm{Res}(\mathbb{D}(F))\in\bigotimes_{i=1}^{d}M_{\det^{\kappa}\rho_{n_{i}}}(\Gamma_{K}^{(n_{i})}),

where

denotes the restriction of a function on

to
\mathfrak{H}_{n_{1}}^{\mathbf{a}}\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{H}_{n_{d}}^{\mathbf{a}}

This condition corresponds to Case (I) in
[
8
]
.

We write
\partial_{Z}:=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{v,ij}}\right)_{v\in\mathbf{a}}
.
Let
P_{v}(X)
be a vector-valued polynomial on the space
M_{n}
of
n\times n
matrices.
The following proposition characterizes when the operator
\mathbb{D}=P(\partial_{Z}):=(P_{v}(\partial_{Z_{v}}))_{v}\in\mathbf{a}
satisfies Condition (A):

Proposition 1.1

(Proposition
3.2
)
.

Let
n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}
be positive integers with
n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{d}\geq 1
and
n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{d}
.
Fix a family
(\bm{k}_{s},\bm{l}_{s})=(\bm{k}_{s,v},\bm{l}_{s,v})_{v}\in\mathbf{a}
of dominant integral weights satisfying
\ell(\bm{k}_{s,v})\leq n_{d}
,
\ell(\bm{l}_{s,v})\leq n_{d}
, and
\ell(\bm{k}_{s,v})+\ell(\bm{l}_{s,v})\leq\kappa_{v}
for each
v\in\mathbf{a}
and
s=1,\ldots,d
.

Let
P_{v}(T)
be a polynomial valued in
V_{n_{1},\bm{k}_{v,1},\bm{l}_{v,1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{n_{d},\bm{k}_{v,d},\bm{l}_{v,d}}
on
M_{n}
.
Then
\mathbb{D}=\prod_{v}\in\mathbf{a}P_{v}(\partial_{Z_{v}})
satisfies Condition (A) for weight
\det^{\kappa}
and representation
\det^{\kappa}\rho_{n_{1},\bm{k}_{1},\bm{l}_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{n_{d},\bm{k}_{d},\bm{l}_{d}}
if and only if each
P_{v}(T)
satisfies the following:

(1)

Let
\widetilde{P_{v}}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{d},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d})
be defined by

\widetilde{P_{v}}(X_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}):=P_{v}\left(\begin{pmatrix}X_{1}\,^{t}Y_{1}&\cdots&X_{1}\,^{t}Y_{d}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
X_{d}\,^{t}Y_{1}&\cdots&X_{d}\,^{t}Y_{d}\end{pmatrix}\right),

where
X_{i},Y_{i}\in M_{n_{i},\kappa_{v}}
.
Then
\widetilde{P_{v}}
must be pluriharmonic in each pair
(X_{i},Y_{i})
.

(2)

For
(A_{i},B_{i})\in K_{n_{i},v}^{\mathbb{C}}:=\mathrm{GL}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathrm{GL}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C})
, we have

P_{v}\left(\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&A_{d}\end{pmatrix}T\begin{pmatrix}{}^{t}\!B_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&{}^{t}\!B_{d}\end{pmatrix}\right)=\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{d}\rho_{n_{i},\bm{k}_{i,v},\bm{l}_{i,v}}(A_{i},B_{i})\right)P_{v}(T).

Condition (1) implies that these differential operators are closely related to two-variable spherical pluriharmonic polynomials.
Accordingly, a substantial part of this paper is devoted to analyzing the space of such higher-degree pluriharmonic polynomials, building on the work of Ibukiyama and Zagier
[
11
]
, and to providing explicit descriptions of their bases, namely the “descending basis” and the “monomial basis”.

These bases are used to construct explicit differential operators.
In particular, for weights corresponding to symmetric power representations, we obtain a fully explicit expression (Theorem
5.5
).

Using these constructions, we derive an explicit formula for the coefficients appearing in the pullback formula for Hermitian Eisenstein series, as formulated in
[
18
]
(see Theorem
6.6
).

This paper is organized as follows:
Section 2 introduces Hermitian modular forms and the notation used throughout the paper.
Section 3 reviews differential operators on Hermitian modular forms and the conditions they satisfy.
Section 4 studies the space of higher spherical pluriharmonic polynomials and provides explicit bases.
Section 5 constructs concrete differential operators based on these results.
Finally, Section 6 applies these operators to derive the exact pullback formula.

Proposition 4.6
.

The adjoint operators of
D_{ij},E_{ij},F_{j,i}
with respect to the action on the
space
\mathbb{C}[T]
with the inner product
(P,Q)_{\kappa}
are given by

\displaystyle D_{ij}^{*}

\displaystyle=D_{j,i}-E_{ij}-E^{\prime}_{j,i}+F_{ij},

\displaystyle E_{ij}^{*}

\displaystyle=-E^{\prime}_{j,i}+F_{j,i},

\displaystyle E^{\prime*}_{ij}

\displaystyle=-E_{j,i}+F_{ij},

\displaystyle F_{ij}^{*}

\displaystyle=F_{j,i}.

Proof.

Fourth equation is trivial and the first three equations can be proved by the same calculation as

We may assume that

where

defined by

\theta F(X,Y)=\sum_{\nu,\mu}\overline{C_{\nu,\mu}}Y^{\nu}X^{\mu}

for

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{n,\kappa}(F(\Delta_{ij}G)-(\Delta_{ij}F)G)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{n,\kappa}(F(-F_{j,i}+E_{j,i}+E^{\prime}_{ij})G),

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{n,\kappa}(F(E_{ij}G)+(E_{ij}F)G)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{n,\kappa}(F(E^{\prime}_{ij}G)+(E^{\prime}_{ij}F)G)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{n,\kappa}((E_{ij}F)\cdot\theta G)

Applying this to

.

D_{ij}(\widetilde{F})=\sum_{p=1}^{n}\partial_{ij}(\sigma_{p}(X{}^{t}\!T))\widetilde{\mathcal{L}_{p}(F)}.

Proof.

Since both sides of the equation are second order derivatives,
it is sufficient to prove their equality for monomials of degree
\leq 2
in
\mathcal{K}[s_{1},s_{2},\ldots]
, i.e., 1,
s_{a}
,
s_{a}s_{b}

The first case is trivial.
We should show that

(4.6)

D_{ij}(\sigma_{p})=(\kappa+1-p)\partial_{ij}(\sigma_{p})

for the second case,
and

(4.7)

D_{ij}(\sigma_{a}\sigma_{b})=D_{ij}(\sigma_{a})\sigma_{b}+\sigma_{a}D_{ij}(\sigma_{b})+2\sum_{p=1}^{n}\epsilon^{+}_{a,b}(p)\partial_{ij}(\sigma_{p})\sigma_{a+b-p}

for the third case.
We can easily see that

\partial_{ij}(\Delta_{\lambda})=\lambda\Delta_{\lambda}^{j;i},

where
\Delta_{\lambda}^{j;i}
is the determinant of the
(j,i)
-cofactor of
P_{\lambda}
.
Thus, we have

\left(1-\lambda\frac{d}{d\lambda}\right)\partial_{ij}(\Delta_{\lambda})=\left(1-\lambda\frac{d}{d\lambda}\right)\left(\lambda\Delta_{\lambda}^{j;i}\right)=-\lambda^{2}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}t_{k,l}\Delta_{\lambda}^{j,l;i,k}=\lambda^{2}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}t_{k,l}\Delta_{\lambda}^{j,l;k,i},

where
\Delta_{\lambda}^{j,l;i,k}
is the determinant of the
(n-2,n-2)
-matrix obtained from
P
by omitting
its
j
-th and
l
-th rows and
i
-th and
k
-th columns, multiplied by

(-1)^{j+l+i+k}\mathrm{sgn}(j-l)\mathrm{sgn}(i-k)

(and
\Delta_{\lambda}^{i,k;j,l}=0
if
i=k
or
j=l
).
Therefore we have,

(D_{ij}-\kappa\partial_{ij})\Delta_{\lambda}=\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}t_{k,l}\partial_{i,l}\partial_{k,j}(\Delta_{\lambda})=\lambda^{2}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}t_{k,l}\Delta_{\lambda}^{j,l;k,i}=\left(1-\lambda\frac{d}{d\lambda}\right)\partial_{ij}(\Delta_{\lambda}),

that is,

D_{ij}\Delta_{\lambda}=\left(\kappa+1-\lambda\frac{d}{d\lambda}\right)\partial_{ij}(\Delta_{\lambda})

and comparing the coefficients of
\lambda^{p}
in both sides, we obtain (
4.6
).

Next, we show (
4.7
).
Note that
\partial_{ij}(\Delta_{\lambda})=\lambda\Delta_{\lambda}(P_{\lambda}^{-1})_{ij}
,
so for independent variables
\lambda
and
\mu
, we have

\displaystyle D_{ij}(\Delta_{\lambda}\Delta_{\mu})-D_{ij}(\Delta_{\lambda})\Delta_{\mu}-

\displaystyle\Delta_{\lambda}D_{ij}(\Delta_{\mu})=\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}t_{k,l}\left(\partial_{i,l}(\Delta_{\lambda})\partial_{k,j}(\Delta_{\mu})-\partial_{k,j}(\Delta_{\lambda})\partial_{i,l}(\Delta_{\mu})\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda\mu\Delta_{\lambda}\Delta_{\mu}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\left((P_{\lambda}^{-1})_{i,l}t_{k,l}(P_{\mu}^{-1})_{k,j}+(P_{\lambda}^{-1})_{k,j}t_{k,l}(P_{\mu}^{-1})_{i,l}\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda\mu\Delta_{\lambda}\Delta_{\mu}\left(P_{\lambda}^{-1}{}^{t}TP_{\mu}^{-1}+P_{\mu}^{-1}{}^{t}TP_{\lambda}^{-1}\right)_{ij}

\displaystyle=-\lambda\mu\Delta_{\lambda}\Delta_{\mu}\left(\frac{P_{\lambda}^{-1}-P_{\mu}^{-1}}{\lambda-\mu}\right)_{ij}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda-\mu}\left(\mu\Delta_{\mu}\partial_{ij}(\Delta_{\lambda})-\lambda\Delta_{\lambda}\partial_{ij}(\Delta_{\mu})\right)

\displaystyle=\det(X)^{-2}\sum_{p,q=0}^{n}\sigma_{q}\partial_{ij}(\sigma_{p})\frac{\lambda^{q+1}\mu^{p}-\mu^{q+1}\lambda^{p}}{\lambda-\mu}.

Here, fourth equality holds because

.
Comparing the coefficients of
\lambda^{a}\mu^{b}
in both sides, we obtain (
4.7
).

Methods of proof and organisation of the article

Our approach relies on a number of upgraded arguments established in previous work of the first and second authors
[
7
,
8
]
, with the crucial dynamical input from equidistribution of
S
-integral point with effective power-saving error terms (see §
2
), extending work of Benoist–Oh
[
1
]
and Browning–Gorodnik
[
15
]
.

The application of effective equidistribution is twofold. Firstly, it allows to execute the
affine linear sieve
(first developed by Sarnak et. al) so as to produce “almost-prime”
S
-integral points. Our way of running this sieve (see §
3
) closely ties to the Hardy–Littlewood property, which (we believe) appears more conceptural than previous work. Combining this with the sophisticated fibration method developed in
[
7
]
leads to the
(APSA)
property for three-dimensional groups and eventually yields the same for affine quadrics (see §
4
). This sieve being executed over the number field
k
without recurring to the Weil restriction (cf.
[
7
, Proof of Corollary 3.3]
), this is the key reason why we can achieve
(APSA)
off any single place. Secondly, in showing the
(APHL)
property, which is the content of §
5
, it is useful in handling a certain range of “bad primes”.

It has been realised in
[
6
,
8
]
that in course of establishing the
(APHL)
property, the treatment of remainder terms is essentially built on the
geometric sieve
(first introduced by Ekedahl
[
12
]
for affine spaces). We prove a
p
-integral version of this in §
5
, following the strategy in
[
8
]
.

As slight generalization of work of Borovoi–Rudnick
[
4
]
, in Appendix
A
we formulate a version of Siegel–Weil type formulas. They refer to summing over the leading constant showing up when one counts points in each
G
-orbit, which was usually imprecise in dynamical methods, gives rise to the Hardy–Littlewood density of the homogeneous space
H\backslash G
.

\#\left(X(k)\cap{\mathbb{B}}_{S}(N)\times({\mathcal{E}}_{S}^{{\mathcal{X}}}(\mathfrak{l};\bm{\xi})\cap\mathbf{O}_{{\mathbf{A}}}^{S})\right)\\
=\#C(H)\mathrm{m}_{X}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{S}(N)\times X_{\infty_{k}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vrule height=2.77277pt,width=5.45512pt,depth=-1.74805pt}}{\mathbin{\vrule height=2.77277pt,width=5.45512pt,depth=-1.74805pt}}{\mathbin{\vrule height=1.959pt,width=3.01389pt,depth=-1.29594pt}}{\mathbin{\vrule height=1.50694pt,width=2.3207pt,depth=-1.0247pt}}S}\times({\mathcal{E}}_{S}^{{\mathcal{X}}}(\mathfrak{l};\bm{\xi})\cap\mathbf{O}_{{\mathbf{A}}}^{S})\right)+O_{\varepsilon}\left(\mathbf{N}(\mathfrak{l})^{\dim G+\kappa+\varepsilon}\cdot\mathrm{m}_{X_{S}}({\mathbb{B}}_{S}(N))^{1-\delta}\right).

It remains to sum over all such adelic orbits
\mathbf{O}_{{\mathbf{A}}}
to obtain the desired formula.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

The symmetric space

as in Theorems

or

This should already be implicitly known though we could not find it in the literature.

2.6.
Example – Affine quadrics

Let
q
be a non-degenerate quadratic form in
n
variables with coefficients in
k
and
m\in k^{\times}
. Let

X:=(q=m)\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{n}_{k}

be an affine quadric. Let
v
be a place of
k
. Assume that
q
is
k_{v}
-isotropic and
X(k_{v})\neq\varnothing
. Define

{\mathbb{B}}_{v}(N):=\left\{\bm{x}\in k_{v}^{n}\;\middle|\;q(\bm{x})=m,\;\mathrm{Ht}_{v}(\bm{x})\leq N\right\}.

Then there exist
0<c<C<\infty
such that

(10)

cN^{n-2}<\mathrm{m}_{X}({\mathbb{B}}_{v}(N))<CN^{n-2}\quad\text{as}~N\to\infty.

The growth of volume balls is intimately related via Tauberian-type theorems (cf. e.g.
[
10
, Appendix A]
) to the height zeta function

Z(s):=\int_{\bm{x}\in X(k_{v})}\mathrm{Ht}^{-s}_{v}(\bm{x})\,\mathrm{d}\left\lvert\omega\right\rvert_{v}(\bm{x})

which admits a real pole at
s=n-2
. Note that
n-1=n-2+1
is the order of the pole of the natural volume form on the affine quadric
X
along the boundary divisor that compactifies it into the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. In order to keep our paper as self-contained as possible, we choose to give details for proof of (
10
) in the case where
v
is non-archimedean, as it will be used in §
5
. The archimedean case works
verbatim
.

We recall that the (normalized) Tamagawa measure
\mathrm{m}_{X}
is induced from the a gauge (volume) form
\omega
on
X
satisfying

\omega\wedge\mathrm{d}q=\mathrm{d}x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}x_{n}.

We may diagonalize the quadratic form
q
(as this only affects the height balls up to a bounded constant) so that

q(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})=a_{1}x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}^{2}

with
a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\in k^{\times}
.
So when
x_{i}\neq 0,1\leq i\leq n
, we have

(11)

\omega=\pm\frac{1}{2a_{i}x_{i}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}x_{i-1}\wedge\mathrm{d}x_{i+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}x_{n}.

We now embed
\mathbb{A}^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}
via

(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\mapsto[1:x_{1}:\cdots:x_{n}]
. Then the height function (
5
) extends to
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
so that for
\bm{y}=[y_{0}:\cdots:y_{n}]
with
y_{0}\neq 0
(note that
x_{i}=\frac{y_{i}}{y_{0}},1\leq i\leq n
),

\mathrm{Ht}_{v}(\bm{y})=\frac{\max_{i=1,\cdots,n}\left\lvert y_{i}\right\rvert_{v}}{\left\lvert y_{0}\right\rvert_{v}}.

Let

\overline{X}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}y_{i}^{2}=my_{0}^{2}\right)\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}

be the Zariski closure of
X
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.
The boundary divisor
\partial X:=\overline{X}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vrule height=3.96109pt,width=7.79301pt,depth=-2.49721pt}}{\mathbin{\vrule height=3.96109pt,width=7.79301pt,depth=-2.49721pt}}{\mathbin{\vrule height=2.79857pt,width=4.30554pt,depth=-1.85135pt}}{\mathbin{\vrule height=2.15277pt,width=3.31528pt,depth=-1.46387pt}}X
is the intersection of the hyperplane
y_{0}=0
with
\overline{X}
, which is thus the projective quadric

\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}y_{i}^{2}=0.

We wish to study in more detail the behavior of
Z(s)
near the boundary divisor
\partial X
. Let
P\in\partial X(k_{v})
. We may assume that
y_{n}(P)\neq 0
, and introduce the coordinate system
z_{i}=\frac{y_{i}}{y_{n}},i=0,1,\cdots,n-1
. By implicit function theorem, if we choose
\bm{z}=(z_{0},\cdots,z_{n-2})
as a local coordinate system for
\overline{X}(k_{v})
near
P\in\partial X(k_{v})
, there exists a bounded
v
-adic neighborhood
{\mathcal{N}}_{P}\subset k_{v}^{n-1}
of
\bm{z}(P)=(0,z_{1}(P),\cdots,z_{n-2}(P))
and an analytic function
u_{P}:{\mathcal{N}}_{P}\to k_{v}
such that for all
\bm{z}\in{\mathcal{N}}_{P}
,
[\bm{z}:u_{P}(\bm{z}):1]\in\overline{X}(k_{v})
, i.e.,
z_{n-1}
is solved by
u_{P}
. Then the volume form
\omega
(
11
) restricted to
{\mathcal{N}}_{P}\cap(y_{0}\neq 0)\cap(y_{n}\neq 0)
is

\omega=\pm\frac{z_{0}}{2a_{n}}\left(\bigwedge_{j=1}^{n-2}\mathrm{d}\left(\frac{z_{j}}{z_{0}}\right)\right)\wedge\mathrm{d}\left(\frac{u_{P}(\bm{z})}{z_{0}}\right)=\pm\frac{u_{P}(\bm{z})}{2a_{n}z_{0}^{n-1}}\left(\bigwedge_{j=0}^{n-2}\mathrm{d}{z_{j}}\right).

Let us consider the height zeta function restricted to
{\mathcal{N}}_{P}
:

Z_{P}(s):=\int_{\bm{x}\in X(k_{v})\cap{\mathcal{N}}_{P}}\mathrm{Ht}_{v}^{-s}(\bm{x})\,\mathrm{d}\left\lvert\omega\right\rvert_{v}(\bm{x}).

Then

\displaystyle Z_{P}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{\bm{z}\in{\mathcal{N}}_{P}}\left(\frac{\max(|z_{i}|_{v},1\leq i\leq n-1,1)}{|z_{0}|_{v}}\right)^{-s}\left|\frac{u_{P}(\bm{z})}{2a_{n}z_{0}^{n-1}}\right|_{v}\mathrm{d}\bm{z}

\displaystyle=\int_{\bm{z}\in{\mathcal{N}}_{P}}\phi_{s}(\bm{z})|z_{0}|_{v}^{s-n+1}\mathrm{d}\bm{z},

where for simplicity we write

\phi_{s}(\bm{z}):=\frac{|u_{P}(\bm{z})|_{v}}{|2a_{n}|_{v}\max(|z_{i}|_{v},1\leq i\leq n-2,|u_{P}(\bm{z})|_{v},1)}.

By shrinking
{\mathcal{N}}_{P}
, we may assume that
\phi_{s}:{\mathcal{N}}_{P}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is bounded away from zero and infinity uniformly for
s
in bounded strips. As
v
is a non-archimedean place, we may further assume that
{\mathcal{N}}_{P}
takes the form (let
\pi_{v}
be the uniformizer of the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{v}
)

\mathcal{N}_{P}=\pi_{v}^{r_{P}}\mathcal{O}_{v}\times\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{P}

for some
r_{P}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and bounded
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{P}\subset k_{v}^{n-2}
.
Now

\displaystyle Z_{P}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{(z_{0},\bm{z}^{\prime})\in\pi_{v}^{r_{P}}{\mathcal{O}}_{v}\times\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{P}}\left\lvert z_{0}\right\rvert_{v}^{s-n+1}\phi_{s}(z_{0},\bm{z}^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}z_{0}\mathrm{d}\bm{z}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{\bm{z}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{P}^{\prime}}\phi_{s}(0,\bm{z}^{\prime})\left(\int_{z_{0}\in\pi_{v}^{r_{P}}{\mathcal{O}}_{v}}\left\lvert z_{0}\right\rvert_{v}^{s-n+1}\,\mathrm{d}z_{0}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{z}^{\prime}+\int_{\bm{z}\in{\mathcal{N}}_{P}}\left\lvert z_{0}\right\rvert_{v}^{s-n+2}\frac{\phi_{s}(z_{0},\bm{z}^{\prime})-\phi_{s}(0,\bm{z}^{\prime})}{\left\lvert z_{0}\right\rvert_{v}}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{z}.

Note that

\int_{\pi_{v}^{r_{P}}{\mathcal{O}}_{v}}\left\lvert z_{0}\right\rvert_{v}^{s-n+1}\,\mathrm{d}z_{0}=\sum_{r\geq r_{P}}\left\lvert\pi_{v}\right\rvert_{v}^{-r}\cdot\left\lvert\pi_{v}\right\rvert_{v}^{-r(s-n+1)}=\frac{\left\lvert\pi_{v}\right\rvert_{v}^{-r_{P}(s-n+2)}}{1-\left\lvert\pi_{v}\right\rvert_{v}^{-s+n-2}}

is meromorphic in
s\in\mathbb{C}
with poles of order
1
at
s=n-2+\frac{2\pi i}{\log\left\lvert\pi_{v}\right\rvert_{v}}\cdot\mathbb{Z}
.
On the other hand, the function
\frac{\phi_{s}(z_{0},\bm{z}^{\prime})-\phi_{s}(0,\bm{z}^{\prime})}{\left\lvert z_{0}\right\rvert_{v}}

is bounded. So the second term is absolutely convergent and analytic for all
\mathrm{Re}(s)>n-3
and is bounded in vertical strips.
By applying a suitable Tauberian theorem (see the discussion above
[
10
, Corollary A.4]
for details), we obtain that

\mathrm{m}_{X}({\mathbb{B}}_{v}(N)\cap\mathcal{N}_{P})\asymp N^{n-2}.

Covering
\overline{X}(k_{v})
by finitely many such
\mathcal{N}_{P}
’s together with some bounded (compact) set in
X(k_{v})
, we find that
0<c<\mathrm{m}_{X}({\mathbb{B}}_{v}(N))/N^{n-2}<C<\infty
for suitable constants
c,C
.

2.7.
Volume asymptotics after Chambert-Loir and Tschinkel

The goal of this section is to sketch a general framework due to Chambert-Loir–Tschinkel
[
10
]
concerning growth of volume balls for varieties over local fields and giving a geometric interpretation of the exponents
a,b
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be a smooth projective variety over
k
, and
\omega_{\mathscr{X}}
be the canonical line bundle. In the setting of
[
10
, Section 4.2]
, let
(\mathscr{D}_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}
be a finite collection of irreducible reduced divisors, and let
\mathscr{U}
be the complement of
\cup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}\mathscr{D}_{\alpha}
in
\mathscr{X}
. Let
(d_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}
be a collection of positive integers that defines the divisor
\mathscr{D}:=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}d_{\alpha}\mathscr{D}_{\alpha}
. Let
\mathscr{L}
be an effective divisor on
\mathscr{X}
such that the support of
\mathscr{D}
is contained in that of
\mathscr{L}
.

Let us fix
S
a finite set of places of
k
. We fix a choice of smooth metric
(\|{\cdot}\|_{\mathscr{O}_{\mathscr{X}}(\mathscr{L}),v})_{v\in S}
for the line bundle
\mathscr{O}_{\mathscr{X}}(\mathscr{L})
, and define for
v\in S
the height function
\mathrm{Ht}_{v}:\mathscr{X}(k_{v})\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
as

(12)

\mathrm{Ht}_{v}(\bm{x}):=\|{\bm{\mathrm{1}}_{\mathscr{L}}(\bm{x})}\|_{\mathscr{O}_{\mathscr{X}}(\mathscr{L}),v}^{-1}.

Equipping
\omega_{\mathscr{X}}(\mathscr{D})
with a smooth metric
(\|{\cdot}\|_{\omega_{\mathscr{X}}(\mathscr{D}),b})_{v\in S}
,
let
\tau_{(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{D}),v}
be the measure on
\mathscr{X}(k_{v})
locally induced by the volume form

\frac{\theta}{\|{(\theta\otimes\bm{\mathrm{1}}_{\mathscr{D}})}\|_{\omega_{\mathscr{X}}(\mathscr{D}),v}},

where
\theta
is a nonvanishing local section of
\omega_{\mathscr{X}}
.
With all these data, we associate the height function

Z(s):=\int_{\bm{x}=(x_{v})\in\mathscr{X}_{S}}\left(\prod_{v\in S}\mathrm{Ht}_{v}(x_{v})\right)^{-s}\,\mathrm{d}\left(\prod_{v\in S}\tau_{(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{D}),v}(x_{v})\right).

Assuming that
\mathscr{D}
is a strictly normal crossings divisor over the the algebraic closure, the volume growth can be read from the corresponding analytic Clemens complex. Concretely, define two constants
a,b
as follows:

•

For each
v\in S
,
a_{v}:=\max_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}\left\{\frac{d_{\alpha}-1}{\lambda_{\alpha}}\;\middle|\;\mathscr{D}_{\alpha}(k_{v})\neq\varnothing\right\}
;

•

For each
v\in S
, let
\mathscr{A}_{0}\subset\mathscr{A}
be the subset where the above
\max
is attained and
b_{v}
be the maximal number such that there exist
\beta_{1},...,\beta_{b_{v}}\in\mathscr{A}_{0}
with
\cap_{i=1}^{b_{v}}\mathscr{D}_{\beta_{i}}(k_{v})\neq\varnothing
.

•

a:=\max\{a_{v},\;v\in S\}
and
b:=\sum_{v\in S,a_{v}=a}b_{v}
.

For
N>1
, consider the volume ball

{\mathbb{B}}_{S}(N):=\left\{\bm{x}=(x_{v})\in\mathscr{X}_{S}\;\middle|\;\prod_{v\in S}\mathrm{Ht}_{v}(x_{v})\leq N\right\}.

Then the results of
[
10
, §4 & Appendix]
show that (in the case of
a>0
)
Z(s)
has a meromorphic continuation to
{\mathrm{Re}}(s)>a-\delta
for any
\delta>0
with a pole of order
b
at
s=a
.
This implies that there exist some
0<c<C<\infty
such that for
N
sufficiently large

(13)

cN^{a}(\log N)^{b-1}<\left(\prod_{v\in S}\tau_{(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{D}),v}\right)({\mathbb{B}}_{S}(N))<CN^{a}(\log N)^{b-1}.

(Affine quadrics)
.

Recalling the notation used in §

,
\mathscr{U}
is the affine quadric

,
\mathscr{X}

3.1.
A combinatorial sieve over general number fields

Let
k
be a number field, and
S_{0}
be a finite set of places of
k
containing all the archimedean ones. Let
{\mathcal{I}}_{S_{0}}
be the set of square-free ideals of
{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}
.
For
{\mathcal{P}}
any set of prime ideals of
{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}
and
z>0
, we use the notation

{\mathcal{P}}(z):=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in{\mathcal{P}}:\mathbf{N}(\mathfrak{p})<z}\mathfrak{p}.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}=(a_{\mathfrak{b}})_{\mathfrak{b}\in I}
be a sequence of non-negative real numbers indexed by a finite set
I
of ideals of
{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}
. The
sifting function
(with respect to
{\mathcal{P}}
and

) is

(14)

{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{P}},z):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{b}\in I\\
\gcd(\mathfrak{b},{\mathcal{P}}(z))=1\end{subarray}}a_{\mathfrak{b}}.

The cardinality of
{\mathcal{A}}
is defined as

\#{\mathcal{A}}:=\sum_{\mathfrak{b}\in I}a_{\mathfrak{b}}.

For every ideal
\mathfrak{d}\in{\mathcal{I}}_{S_{0}}
, we define the subsequence

{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{d}}:=(a_{\mathfrak{b}})_{\mathfrak{b}:\mathfrak{d}\mid\mathfrak{b}}

of
{\mathcal{A}}
.

We now state the following version of Brun’s combinatorial sieve over the number field
k
. See e.g.
[
13
, Theorem 6.9, Corollary 6.10]
when
k={\mathbb{Q}}
and
[
16
, Lemma 4]
for a version over general number fields.

Theorem 3.2

(“Fundamental Lemma” of the combinatorial sieve)
.

With the notation above, let
\omega:{\mathcal{I}}_{S_{0}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
be a multiplicative function satisfying:

•

For every prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}
, we have
0\leq\frac{\omega(\mathfrak{p})}{\mathbf{N}(\mathfrak{p})}<1
;

•

There exist constants
\kappa_{1}>0,\kappa_{2}>1
such that
for any real numbers
2\leq w_{1}\leq w_{2}

, we have

Taking (

\displaystyle{\mathbb{V}}(p_{0}^{\alpha h},p_{0}^{h})

Based on the observation above, the estimation of (

\#\{\bm{y}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{h}({\mathbb{Z}}):|\bm{y}|\ll p_{0}^{h},\bm{y}\equiv p_{0}^{h}\bm{\lambda}_{p}\bmod p\}.

Either the set to be counted is empty, or it contains an integral point
\bm{\lambda}_{h}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{h}({\mathbb{Z}})
satisfying
\bm{\lambda}_{h}\equiv p_{0}^{h}\bm{\lambda}_{p}\bmod p
. Executing the change of variables
\bm{y}=\bm{\lambda}_{h}+p\bm{z}
, the new variable
\bm{z}
satisfies

|\bm{z}|\ll\frac{p_{0}^{h}}{p},\quad\bm{z}\cdot\nabla q(\bm{\lambda}_{h})+pq(\bm{z})=0.

In particular
\bm{z}
satisfies
\bm{z}\cdot\nabla q(\bm{\lambda}_{h})\equiv\bm{z}\cdot\nabla q(p_{0}^{h}\bm{\lambda}_{p})\equiv 0\bmod p
, which is also equivalent to the lattice condition
\bm{z}\cdot\nabla q(\bm{\lambda}_{p})\equiv 0\bmod p
.
The argument in
[

shows that the determinant of this lattice is
\asymp p
for

. The uniform point counting technique as in
[
5
, p. 1076–1078]
(for
n=4

[

, §4.2.2]
(for

anisotropic) can be accordingly adapted and yields (

). The details will not be repeated.
∎

Our final bound deals with the primes with
p\geq p_{0}^{h}

.

Proposition 5.4
.

We have

(32)

{\mathbb{V}}(p_{0}^{h},\infty)=O\left(\frac{p_{0}^{h(n-2)}}{(\log p_{0}^{h})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right).

Proof of Proposition
5.4
.

We may diagonalise
q
and switch signs so that
q(\bm{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}^{2}
with
a_{n}=1,a_{n-1}=a>0
. Moreover, we can assume that
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}],g\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-2}]
(cf.
[
8
, p. 38]
).
Let the integral polynomials
f_{h}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}],g_{h}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-2}]
be such that

f_{h}(\bm{x})=p_{0}^{h\deg f}f\left(\frac{\bm{x}}{p_{0}^{h}}\right),\quad g_{h}(\bm{x})=p_{0}^{h\deg g}g\left(\frac{\bm{x}}{p_{0}^{h}}\right).

Then whenever
|\bm{y}|\ll p_{0}^{h}
,

|f_{h}(\bm{y})|\ll p_{0}^{h\deg f},\quad|g_{h}(\bm{y})|\ll p_{0}^{h\deg g}.

Let
\operatorname{pr}:{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{n-2}
be the projection onto the first
(n-2)
coordinates.
Let

\mathscr{Z}:=\{\mathscr{Y}\in\operatorname{pr}({\mathcal{Z}}):\operatorname{codim}_{{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{\mathscr{Y}}}({\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathscr{Y}})\leq 1\},

and

{\mathcal{B}}_{1}:=\left\{\bm{Y}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n-2}:\text{either }g_{h}(\bm{Y})=0\text{ or }\exists p\geq p_{0}^{h},\bm{Y}\bmod p\in\mathscr{Z}\right\},

{\mathcal{B}}_{2}:=\left\{\bm{Y}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n-2}:g_{h}(\bm{Y})\neq 0~\text{and for all}~p\geq p_{0}^{h},f_{h}(\bm{Y},x_{n-1})\bmod p\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[x_{n-1}]~\text{is non-zero}\right\}.

Rerunning the argument of
[
8
, Proof of Theorem 4.7]
,
{\mathbb{V}}(p_{0}^{h},\infty)
can be bounded from above by two parts:

{\mathbb{V}}_{1}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{Y}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{1},|\bm{Y}|\ll p_{0}^{h}\end{subarray}}\mathscr{H}_{1}(\bm{Y}),\quad{\mathbb{V}}_{2}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{Y}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{2},|\bm{Y}|\ll p_{0}^{h}\\
\exists u,v\in{\mathbb{Z}},u^{2}+av^{2}=mp_{0}^{2h}-\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}a_{i}y_{i}^{2}\end{subarray}}\mathscr{H}_{2}(\bm{Y}),

where for
\bm{Y}=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n-2}
, we let

\mathscr{H}_{1}(\bm{Y}):=\#\left\{(u,v)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}:u^{2}+av^{2}=mp_{0}^{2h}-\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}a_{i}y_{i}^{2}\right\},

\mathscr{H}_{2}(\bm{Y}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p:p\geq p_{0}^{h}\\
p\mid g_{h}(\bm{Y})\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in{\mathbb{Z}}:|y|\ll p_{0}^{h}\\
p\mid f_{h}(\bm{Y},y)\end{subarray}}\#\left\{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}:z^{2}=mp_{0}^{2h}-ay^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}a_{i}y_{i}^{2}\right\}.

As
a>0
, it is easy to see that uniformly for all
\bm{Y}
with
|\bm{Y}|\ll p_{0}^{h}
, we have
\mathscr{H}_{1}(\bm{Y})\ll_{\varepsilon}p_{0}^{\varepsilon h}
. To deal with
\bm{Y}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
, we observe that for every prime
p
the fibre
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bm{Y}}\bmod p\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}^{2}
is defined by the equations

If
p
is large enough, the first polynomial is always non-zero, and hence the condition
\bm{Y}\bmod p\in\mathscr{Z}
implies that
p\mid g_{h}(\bm{Y})
and the polynomial

in

is zero, i.e.,
p
divides all its coefficients, say
f_{1,h}(\bm{Y}),\cdots,f_{\deg f,h}(\bm{Y})
.
Since the set

has codimension at least two in

[

]
obtained by work of Browning–Heath-Brown

, yielding that

and

\displaystyle\#\left\{\bm{Y}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n-2}:|\bm{Y}|\ll p_{0}^{h},\exists p\geq p_{0}^{h},p\mid\gcd(g_{h}(\bm{Y}),f_{1,h}(\bm{Y}),\cdots,f_{\deg f,h}(\bm{Y}))\right\}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\frac{p_{0}^{(n-2)h}\log(p_{0}^{h(\max(\deg f,\deg g)+1)})}{p_{0}^{h}}\ll_{\varepsilon}p_{0}^{(n-3+\varepsilon)h}.

Therefore we obtain

{\mathbb{V}}_{1}\ll_{\varepsilon}p_{0}^{(n-3+\varepsilon)h}.

As for
{\mathbb{V}}_{2}

for all

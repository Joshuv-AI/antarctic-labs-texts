Eigenweights for arithmetic Hirzebruch Proportionality
Authors: Tony Feng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPrior work of Feng–Yun–Zhang established a (Higher) Arithmetic Hirzebruch Proportionality Principle, expressing the arithmetic volumes of moduli stacks of shtukas in terms of differential operators applied to L-functions. This formula involves certain “eigenweights” which were calculated in simple cases by Feng–Yun–Zhang, but not in general. We document work of a (custom) AI Agent codenamed Aletheia,a built upon Gemini Deep Think, which employs tools from algebraic combinatorics to connect these eigenweights to the representation theory of symmetric groups and then determines them for all classical groups.

Connecting the geometry of spaces to the arithmetic of numbers is a central pursuit in modern mathematics. One such connection is expressed by a recently developed “Arithmetic Hirzebruch Proportionality” principle, which gives a formula for volumes of certain geometric spaces in terms of arithmetic objects called L-functions. However, this formula involves local structure constants called “eigenweights,” which were previously unknown in most cases, restricting the application of the formula. This article solves this problem by calculating the eigenweights for all classical groups, in terms of the representation theory of symmetric groups. Notably, this calculation was entirely carried out by an AI.

Introduction

Arithmetic Hirzebruch Proportionality

The famous Hirzebruch Proportionality Principle (1) expresses the Chern numbers of an automorphic vector bundle on a compact locally symmetric space as a multiple of the corresponding Chern numbers on the compact dual variety, with proportionality constant given by the value of an L-function. Mumford (2) generalized Hirzebruch’s formula to noncompact locally symmetric spaces.

Recently, joint work of the author with Yun and Zhang in Ref. (3) investigated an extension of this principle, which we call “Arithmetic Hirzebruch Proportionality.” It relates the “arithmetic volume” of Chern classes on arithmetic moduli spaces to differential operators applied to an L-function. This principle manifests in both the number field setting (where it is closely related to classical Hirzebruch(–Mumford) Proportionality) and the function field setting. The precise differential operator appearing in Arithmetic Hirzebruch Proportionality is governed by certain fundamental structure constants that we call eigenweights. These were calculated for some examples in Ref. (3), and the purpose of this article is to determine them in general.

An example of Arithmetic Hirzebruch Proportionality

For concreteness, we illustrate a special case of the main result of Ref. (3). To G, we can associate a multivariable L-function LX,G(s1,…,sn); when s1=…=sn=s, this recovers the “L-function of the motive of G” in the sense of Gross (4).

The original motivation for this direction of research was to extend the Higher Siegel–Weil formula of Ref. (5) to the “singular terms.” For example, when G is a unitary group, part of the special cycle ZEr(0) constructed in Ref. (6, Section 4) can be interpreted as an arithmetic volume, and the matching derivative of the L-function is part of the constant term of the Siegel–Eisenstein series. In fact, such arithmetic volumes appear in more general singular coefficients; the corank-one Higher Siegel–Weil formula recently established in (7) crucially uses a special case of the result (1).

Arithmetic volume of Shimura varieties

In the number field setting, arithmetic volume is interpreted as an Arakelov degree of automorphic vector bundles on Shimura varieties. This has been computed in special cases, such as in Refs. (8–10). Our perspective gives a uniform explanation for the constants arising in the resulting formulas (eg (10, Conjecture 5.8)). We expect to revisit this in a future work (with Yun and Zhang).

We make some remarks on the formulation of the problem. The utility of the eigenweights is for application to the Arithmetic Volume formulas of (3) for function fields in characteristic p, which requires p≠2. In this article, we treat a purely representation-theoretic “local” question for split semisimple groups over Q. The calculation of Arithmetic Volumes for nonsplit groups (eg unitary groups) is also in terms of eigenweights for split groups: this is expressed by the formula in Section 7.4.8 of (3). Therefore, it suffices to treat split reductive groups.

By Lemma 4.1.11(1) of (3), ∇¯μη is invariant under central isogenies when defined. Since the flag variety of a torus is trivial, the calculation of ∇¯μη is simple for tori, hence the calculation of eigenweights reduces to the case of semisimple cases. Again by invariance under central isogenies, it suffices to treat the adjoint form, where all the most minuscule coweights are achieved.

As we are interested in closed form descriptions of eigenweights, we do not discuss exceptional groups; among them, only E6 and E7 admit minuscule coweights, and in those cases the eigenweights can be calculated by a finite algorithm.

Theorem 3(2) answers a question raised in Ref. (3), namely whether the operators ∇¯μη commute as μ ranges over different minuscule coweights, when G is semisimple.

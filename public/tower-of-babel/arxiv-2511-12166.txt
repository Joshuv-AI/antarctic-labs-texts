Boundary regularity and Wiener-type criteria at infinity for nonlinear elliptic equations of p p\mspace{1mu} -Laplace type
Anders Björn

Abstract




Abstract
.
We study boundary regularity at the infinity point
{\boldsymbol{\infty}}
for nonlinear elliptic equations of
p\mspace{1mu}
-Laplace type
in unbounded open sets
\Omega\subset{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
.
We consider the case
p\geq n\geq 2
and characterize the regularity at
{\boldsymbol{\infty}}
by means of
Wiener-type integrals.
Our approach uses circular inversion, which maps
{\boldsymbol{\infty}}
to the origin and
the original nonlinear equation to a similar weighted equation.
The Wiener criterion at the origin for such equations is then transformed back
to provide Wiener-type criteria at
{\boldsymbol{\infty}}
.
When
p>n
, the criteria simplify so that
{\boldsymbol{\infty}}
is regular
if
and only if the boundary
\partial\Omega
is unbounded.
For
p=n
this is not true, as shown by an example.
This simplified criterion is also proved for
p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic functions in unbounded
open subsets of Ahlfors
Q
-regular metric measure spaces with

Q<p
, supporting a Poincaré inequality.

Key words and phrases
:
Ahlfors
Q
-regular measure,
boundary regularity at infinity,
capacity,
nonlinear elliptic equation of
p\mspace{1mu}
-Laplace type,
Perron solution,

p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic function,
Sobolev space,
Wiener criterion.

(2020):

Secondary: 31E05.

1
Introduction

In this paper we study boundary regularity at
{\boldsymbol{\infty}}
for
{\mathcal{A}}
-harmonic functions,
i.e. for continuous solutions of the nonlinear elliptic equation of
p\mspace{1mu}
-Laplace type,

\Div{\mathcal{A}}(x,\nabla u(x))=0,\quad x\in\Omega,

(1.1)

in unbounded open sets
\Omega\subset{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
,
where
p\geq n\geq 2

and the map
{\mathcal{A}}
satisfies the
standard ellipticity
assumptions
(A1)
–
(A3)

stated at the beginning of Section
3
(with
w\equiv 1
).
The standard
p\mspace{1mu}
-Laplace equation
\Delta_{p}u=0

with
{\mathcal{A}}(x,\nabla u)=|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u
is included as a special case.

The point
{\boldsymbol{\infty}}
is
regular
for the equation (
1.1
) in
\Omega
,
if for every
f\in C(\partial\Omega\cup\{{\boldsymbol{\infty}}\})
,
the continuous
solution
u
of the Dirichlet problem for (
1.1
)
with
f
as boundary data satisfies

\lim_{x\to{\boldsymbol{\infty}}}u(x)=f({\boldsymbol{\infty}}).

(See Definition
3.4
for a more precise definition.)
When
1<p<n
, it was shown by
Kilpeläinen
[
15
, Remark 5.1 and Proposition 5.2]

that
{\boldsymbol{\infty}}

is always regular for
{\mathcal{A}}
-harmonic functions in
\Omega
.
In this paper, we study the case
p\geq n
.
The following Wiener-type criterion is our first result.
We write
B_{r}=B(0,r)
to denote the open ball
centred
at the origin with radius
r
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\geq n\geq 2
and
\Omega\varsubsetneq{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
be an unbounded open set.
Then
{\boldsymbol{\infty}}
is regular for the equation (
1.1
)
with respect to
\Omega
if and only if

\int_{1}^{\infty}\biggl(\frac{\capp_{p}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r},\Omega\cup B_{2r})}{r^{n-p}}\biggr)^{1/(p-1)}\,\frac{dr}{r}=\infty,

(1.2)

where

\capp_{p}
is the condenser
p\mspace{1mu}
-capacity as in
Definition
2.1

(
with
w\equiv 1
)
.

A direct consequence is that the regularity of
{\boldsymbol{\infty}}

depends exclusively on
p
and
n
, and not on the map
{\mathcal{A}}
.
Note also that if
\Omega={\mathbf{R}^{n}}
, then
u(x)\equiv f(\infty)
is the
unique solution of the Dirichlet problem for (
1.1
)
with boundary data
f

and hence

{\boldsymbol{\infty}}
is regular while the
integral in (
1.2
) is
convergent (because the integrand is identically zero by (
2.7
)).
Thus the
equivalence in Theorem
1.1
fails
in this case.

For
p>n
,
Theorem
1.1

implies
the following simpler characterization.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p>n
and
\Omega\varsubsetneq{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
be an unbounded open set.
Then
{\boldsymbol{\infty}}
is regular for the equation (
1.1
)
with respect to
\Omega

if and only if
\partial\Omega
is unbounded.

Regularity of finite boundary points was for
classical harmonic functions
(i.e. with
p=2
),
characterized in 1924 by means of the Wiener criterion
[
26
]
.
It was extended to the nonlinear case, and operators
{\mathcal{A}}
as here,
on (unweighted)
{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
by Maz’ya
[
23
]
(sufficiency),
Lindqvist–Martio
[
22
]
(necessity,
p>n-1
) and
Kilpeläinen–Malý
[
16
]
(necessity, all
p>1
).
See Section
3
for more details.

If
p=2=n
, then harmonic functions are preserved by the circular inversion

x\mapsto x/|x|^{2}

and regularity at
{\boldsymbol{\infty}}
can be studied by “inverting” the Riemann sphere.
For
p=2<n
, harmonic functions are preserved by the Kelvin transform.
However, since
{\boldsymbol{\infty}}
is always regular in this case,
there is no need for a Wiener criterion at
{\boldsymbol{\infty}}
.
At the same time,
a stronger type of regularity at
{\boldsymbol{\infty}}
was
characterized by Abdulla
[
1
]
using a
different Wiener-type criterion for
p=2<n
.

In the nonlinear case
p\neq 2
,
there is no Kelvin transform that transforms
p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic (or
{\mathcal{A}}
-harmonic)
functions into
p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic (or
{\mathcal{A}}
-harmonic) functions.
This may be a reason why no one seems to have studied Wiener criteria at
{\boldsymbol{\infty}}
in the nonlinear case.

We show that the circular inversion
x\mapsto x/|x|^{2}

transforms
{\mathcal{A}}
-harmonic (and thus
p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic) functions
near
{\boldsymbol{\infty}}
into
{\mathcal{B}}
-harmonic functions near
0
for a suitable

weighted
map
{\mathcal{B}}
of a similar type.
This is done in Section
4
.
In particular, Theorem
4.7

states that
{\boldsymbol{\infty}}
is
{\mathcal{A}}
-regular for an unbounded domain
if and only if the origin
is
{\mathcal{B}}
-regular for the transformed domain.
Thus we can
use the (weighted) Wiener criterion for
{\mathcal{B}}
at the origin,
proved in Heinonen–Kilpeläinen–Martio
[
13
]
and
Mikkonen
[
24
, Theorem 5.2]
.
This is then used to prove
Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

in Section
5
.

Before that, the necessary
theory about weighted Sobolev spaces,
condenser capacities,
{\mathcal{A}}
-harmonic functions,
Perron solutions and boundary regularity is presented in
Sections
2
and
3
.

Wiener criteria are usually formulated in terms of the
capacity of the complement

\Omega^{c}:={\mathbf{R}^{n}}\setminus\Omega
, cf. Theorem
3.5
.
This is
not
the case for the Wiener-type criterion in
Theorem
1.1
.
We therefore also give the following two alternative forms
of the Wiener criterion.
They have the advantage that the involved capacities
are taken with respect to bounded sets.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p\geq n\geq 2
and
\Omega\varsubsetneq{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
be an unbounded open set.
Then the following conditions are equivalent
:

1.

The point
{\boldsymbol{\infty}}
is regular for the equation (
1.1
) with respect to
\Omega
.

2.

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\biggl(\frac{\capp_{p}(\Omega^{c}\cap({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r^{2}}\setminus B_{r}),B_{4^{r}}\setminus{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r/2})}{r^{n-p}}\biggr)^{1/(p-1)}\,\frac{dr}{r}=\infty
.

3.

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\biggl(\frac{\capp_{p}(\Omega^{c}\cap({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{2^{r}}\setminus B_{r}),B_{4^{r}}\setminus{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r/2})}{r^{n-p}}\biggr)^{1/(p-1)}\,\frac{dr}{r}=\infty
.

Furthermore, the outer set
B_{4^{r}}\setminus{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r/2}

in
2
and
3
can equivalently be replaced by

{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\setminus{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r/2}
.

Theorem
1.3
is proved in Section
6

by rewriting the weighted Wiener criterion for
{\mathcal{B}}
-harmonic functions
to the original coordinates when
p\geq n
.
This requires careful estimates for condenser capacities and Wiener-type
integrals.

In Section
7
we give two counterexamples for
p=n
.
Example
7.1

shows
that the simple characterization in
Theorem
1.2
fails when
p=n
.
This example uses the Wiener criterion
in Theorem
1.3
2
.
Example
7.2
is instead devoted to
showing that
the somewhat unusual powers
r^{2}
and
2^{r}
in
conditions
2
and
3

in Theorem
1.3

cannot be replaced by linear functions
when
p=n
.
(For
p>n
, this would be possible but is
not interesting in view of the simple characterization in Theorem
1.2
.)

Finally, in Section
8
,
we generalize Theorem
1.2
to unbounded complete metric spaces
equipped with an Ahlfors
Q
-regular measure supporting
a
p\mspace{1mu}
-Poincaré inequality, where
p>Q
.
Instead of the circular inversion, we sphericalize these spaces into bounded ones.
In addition to various fractal examples, such as the
Laakso spaces
[
21
, Theorem 2.7]
,
this generalization also covers
certain complete Riemannian manifolds.

2
Sobolev spaces and capacities

In Sections
2
–
3
, we let

\Omega\subset{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
,
n\geq 2
, be
an open set and

1<p<\infty
.
We also assume that
w
is a
p\mspace{1mu}
-admissible weight as in
Heinonen–Kilpeläinen–Martio
[
13
]
.

Even though our original problem about boundary regularity is formulated
in unweighted
{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
,
after the circular inversion, we will need to consider
\mathbf{R}^{n}
equipped with the weighted measure

|x|^{\delta}\,dx
, where
\delta=2(p-n)
and
p\geq n
.
We therefore
introduce the necessary notions for weighted
\mathbf{R}^{n}
and the measure
w(x)\,dx
,
where
w
is
a
p\mspace{1mu}
-admissible weight as in Heinonen–Kilpeläinen–Martio
[
13
]
.
The unweighted case
w\equiv 1
and the above power weight

w(x)=|x|^{\delta}

(2.1)

are included as special cases.
With our choice of exponent
\delta=2(p-n)
, the weight
w
is a Muckenhoupt
A_{p}
-weight
(since
-n<\delta<n(p-1)
) and is therefore
p\mspace{1mu}
-admissible, see
[
13
, p. 10]
.
By a slight abuse of notation, we write

w(E)=\int_{E}w\,dx\quad\text{for measurable sets }E.

For every function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we let

\|\varphi\|_{H^{1,p}(\Omega,w)}=\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,w)}+\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,w)},

where

\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,w)}=\biggl(\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{p}w\,dx\biggr)^{1/p}

denotes the
L^{p}(\Omega,w)
-norm.
The
weighted Sobolev space

H^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
consists of the functions

u\in L^{p}(\Omega,w)
for which there exists a function
v\in L^{p}(\Omega,w)
and a
sequence
\varphi_{j}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

\|\varphi_{j}-u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,w)}\to 0\quad\text{and}\quad\|\nabla\varphi_{j}-v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,w)}\to 0,\qquad\text{as }j\to\infty.

(2.2)

Since
w
is
p\mspace{1mu}
-admissible,
v
is uniquely defined in
L^{p}(\Omega,w)

by Axiom II in
[
13
, p. 7]
(or Theorem 20.4 in
[
13
]
).
In addition, if
u
is locally Lipschitz, then Lemma 1.11 in
[
13
]
shows that

v
coincides with the classical gradient
\nabla u
a.e. on
\Omega
.
We therefore
write
\nabla u=v
and find that (
2.2
) is equivalent to

\|\varphi_{j}-u\|_{H^{1,p}(\Omega,w)}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
.
Theorem 1.20 in
[
13
]
shows that

\nabla\max\{u_{1},u_{2}\}=\begin{cases}\nabla u_{1},&\text{if }u_{1}\geq u_{2},\\
\nabla u_{2},&\text{if }u_{1}\leq u_{2}.\end{cases}

(2.3)

By
H^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega,w)
we denote the closure of

C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
H^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
.
Furthermore, the space
H^{1,p}_{\textup{loc}}(\Omega,w)
consists of all functions

u\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{p}(\Omega,w)
such that
u|_{G}\in H^{1,p}(G,w)

for every open subset
G\Subset\Omega
.
As usual,

G\Subset\Omega
means that
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle G}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{G}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle G}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{G}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle G}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{G}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle G}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{G}}}
is a compact subset of
\Omega
,
and
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)=\{u\in C^{\infty}({\mathbf{R}^{n}}):\supp u\Subset\Omega\}
.
For unweighted
\mathbf{R}^{n}
, i.e. when
w\equiv 1
,
we simplify the notation by suppressing
w

and write
H^{1,p}(\Omega)
,
H^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
H^{1,p}_{\textup{loc}}(\Omega)
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
K\subset\Omega
be compact.
We define the
condenser
(p,w)
-capacity
for
K
in
\Omega
as

\capp_{p,w}(K,\Omega):=\inf\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}w\,dx,

(2.4)

where the infimum is taken over all
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
with
u|_{K}\geq 1
.

The capacity can be extended to open sets and subsequently to arbitrary sets
in a standard way, see
[
13
, p. 27]
.
However, in this paper, the definition for compact sets will suffice.
Note that by
[
13
, pp. 27–28]
, the infimum in (
2.4
)
can equivalently be taken
over all

u\in W_{p}(K,\Omega,w):=\{u\in H^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega,w)\cap C(\Omega):u|_{K}\geq 1\}.

(2.5)

We say that a function
u
is
admissible
for
\capp_{p,w}(K,\Omega)

if
u\in W_{p}(K,\Omega,w)
.
Theorem 2.2 in
[
13
]
states that the capacity
\capp_{p,w}
is monotone
in both arguments (increasing in the first and decreasing in the second),
and it is countably subadditive in the first argument.
For unweighted
\mathbf{R}^{n}
we write
\capp_{p}
instead of
\capp_{p,w}
.

As in
[
13
]
, for closed
F\subset{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
, we say that
\capp_{p,w}F=0
if

\capp_{p,w}(F\cap{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r},B_{2r})=0\quad\text{for every }r>0.

Otherwise we write
\capp_{p,w}F>0
.
Note that Lemmas 2.8 and 2.9 in
[
13
]
ensure that this definition
is equivalent to the one in Section 2.7 of
[
13
]
.

In unweighted
{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
, the capacity
\capp_{p}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r},B_{R})

can be computed explicitly for all
0\leq r<R
,
see Example 2.12 in
[
13
]
.
For our purposes, the following estimates will be sufficient.
By
a\lesssim b
we mean that
a\leq Cb
for some constant
C>0
,
which we refer to as a
comparison constant
.
The notation
b\gtrsim a
and
a\simeq b
should be interpreted as

a\lesssim b
and
a\lesssim b\lesssim a
, respectively.
By
B(x,r)
we denote the open ball
centred
at
x
with radius
r
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
13
, Example 2.12, Lemma 2.14 and (6.40)]
)

Let
x\in{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
and
0<r<R
.
Then

\capp_{p,w}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}},B(x,2r))\simeq r^{-p}w(B(x,r)),

(2.6)

where the comparison constants depend only on
n
,
p
and
w
.

In the unweighted case,
we have that

\displaystyle\capp_{p}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}},{\mathbf{R}^{n}})

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\text{if }p\geq n,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\capp_{p}(\{x\},B(x,R))

\displaystyle\simeq R^{n-p},

\displaystyle\text{if }p>n,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\capp_{n}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}},B(x,R))

\displaystyle\simeq\biggl(\log\frac{R}{r}\biggr)^{1-n},

\displaystyle\text{if }p=n,

(2.9)

where the comparison constants depend only on
n
and
p
.

For every set
E\subset{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
we let

\widehat{\partial}E=\begin{cases}\partial E\cup\{{\boldsymbol{\infty}}\},&\text{if $E$ is unbounded},\\
\partial E,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

denote the extended boundary of
E
.
Moreover, as usual we write
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}}=E\cup\partial E
and
E^{c}={\mathbf{R}^{n}}\setminus E
.

3
{\mathcal{A}}
-harmonic functions and boundary regularity

As in Section
2
, we assume that

\Omega\subset{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
,
n\geq 2
, is
an open set
and that
w
is a
p\mspace{1mu}
-admissible weight,
1<p<\infty
.

As in
Heinonen–Kilpeläinen–Martio
[
13
]
,
we consider weak solutions of the equation

\Div{\mathcal{A}}(x,\nabla u(x))=0,\quad x\in\Omega,

where the map
{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\times{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\to{\mathbf{R}^{n}}

satisfies the conditions
(A1)
–
(A3)

below with a
p\mspace{1mu}
-admissible weight
w
.

(A1).

{\mathcal{A}}(\cdot,q)
is measurable for all
q\in{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
.

(A2).

{\mathcal{A}}(x,\cdot)
is continuous for a.e.
x\in{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
.

(A3).

There exist constants
\alpha_{2}\geq\alpha_{1}>0

such that for a.e.
x\in{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
,
all
\lambda\in\mathbf{R}\setminus\{0\}

and all
q,q^{\prime}\in{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
with

q\neq q^{\prime}
,
the following hold:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}(x,q)\cdot q

\displaystyle\geq\alpha_{1}w(x)|q|^{p},

(3.1)

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{A}}(x,q)|

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{2}w(x)|q|^{p-1},

(3.2)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}(x,\lambda q)

\displaystyle=\lambda|\lambda|^{p-2}{\mathcal{A}}(x,q),

(3.3)

and

({\mathcal{A}}(x,q)-{\mathcal{A}}(x,q^{\prime}))\cdot(q-q^{\prime})>0.

(3.4)

In the subsequent sections, the map
{\mathcal{A}}
will be
associated with the unweighted case

w(x)\equiv 1
.
Here we give a more general weighted definition,
because in Section
4
we introduce a similar map
{\mathcal{B}}
with the
power weight
w(x)=|x|^{2(p-n)}
as
in (
2.1
).

Definition 3.1
.

A function
u\in H^{1,p}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega,w)\cap C(\Omega)
is
{\mathcal{A}}
-
harmonic

if it is a weak solution to the equation
\Div{\mathcal{A}}(x,\nabla u)=0
, i.e.

\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(x,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx=0\quad\text{for every }\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega).

Definition 3.2
.

A function
u:\Omega\to(-\infty,\infty]
is
{\mathcal{A}}
-
superharmonic

if the following conditions are satisfied:

•

u
is lower semicontinuous,

•

u\not\equiv\infty
in each component of
\Omega
,

•

for each open set
G\Subset\Omega
and all functions

v\in C({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle G}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{G}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle G}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{G}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle G}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{G}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle G}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{G}}})
that are
{\mathcal{A}}
-harmonic in
G
,
we have
v\leq u
in
G
whenever
v\leq u
on
\partial G
.

In this paper, we only need to solve the Dirichlet
problem for continuous boundary data.
For simplicity we therefore restrict the definition of Perron solutions to such functions.

Definition 3.3
.

For every
f\in C(\widehat{\partial}\Omega)
we denote by
\mathcal{U}(f,{\mathcal{A}},\Omega)
the set
consisting of all
{\mathcal{A}}
-superharmonic functions
u
on
\Omega

such that

\liminf_{x\to z}u(x)\geq f(z)\quad\text{for all }z\in\widehat{\partial}\Omega.

(3.5)

The function

{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}f={\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}^{{\mathcal{A}}}_{\Omega}f=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}(f,{\mathcal{A}},\Omega)}u

(3.6)

is called the
upper Perron solution
of
f
with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}
and
\Omega
.

The lower Perron solution is defined analogously using

{\mathcal{A}}
-subharmonic functions or simply by letting
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}f=-{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}(-f)
.
The function
f
is
resolutive
if
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}f={\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}f
.

In unweighted
{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
with
p\geq n
and
\capp_{p}\Omega^{c}>0

(which is always true when

p>n
and
\Omega\varsubsetneq{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
)
it follows from Proposition
4.8

that the requirement (
3.5
)
can for
z={\boldsymbol{\infty}}
be replaced by

\liminf_{x\to{\boldsymbol{\infty}}}u(x)>-\infty.

If
\capp_{p,w}\Omega^{c}>0
, then every continuous function on
\widehat{\partial}\Omega
is
resolutive by Theorem 9.25 in
[
13
]
.
However, when
\capp_{p,w}\Omega^{c}=0
, this is generally false,
see the discussion after Theorem 9.25 in
[
13
]
.
We therefore define regular boundary points
using upper Perron solutions
as follows.

Definition 3.4
.

A point
x_{0}\in\widehat{\partial}\Omega
is
{\mathcal{A}}
-
regular

(with respect to
\Omega
)
if

\lim_{x\to x_{0}}{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}f(x)=f(x_{0})\quad\text{for all }f\in C(\widehat{\partial}\Omega).

The following theorem is the so-called

Wiener criterion
for boundary regularity,
named after Norbert Wiener
[
26
]

who proved it in the unweighted case for
p=2
.

Theorem 3.5
.

(The Wiener criterion)

Assume
that
\Omega\varsubsetneq{\mathbf{R}^{n}}

and
let
x\in\partial\Omega
be a finite boundary point.
Then
x
is
{\mathcal{A}}
-regular if and only if

\int_{0}^{1}\biggl(\frac{\capp_{p,w}(\Omega^{c}\cap{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}},B(x,2r)}{\capp_{p,w}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}},B(x,2r))}\biggr)^{1/(p-1)}\,\frac{dr}{r}=\infty.

(3.7)

In the unweighted case, sufficiency
is due to
Maz’ya
[
23
, Theorem, p. 236]
and necessity
to Lindqvist–Martio
[
22
]
(for
p>n-1
) and
Kilpeläinen–Malý
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
(all
p>1
).

For this weighted version,
sufficiency
is due to
Heinonen–Kilpeläinen–Martio
[
13
, Theorem 6.18 (also in the first edition from 1993)]

and necessity to
Mikkonen
[
24
, Theorem 5.2]
.
(These results are formulated for bounded
\Omega
, but by

[
13
, Theorem 9.8 and Proposition 9.9 (also in the first edition)]

the unbounded case is also covered.)
A proof
can also be found
in (the second edition of)
[
13
, Theorems 6.33 and 21.30]
.

It follows from Lemma 2.16 in
[
13
]

that the Wiener condition (
3.7
) is
equivalent to the Wiener conditions in the above papers and monograph.
In view of
(
2.6
),
it can also
equivalently be formulated
as

\int_{0}^{1}\biggl(\frac{\capp_{p,w}(\Omega^{c}\cap{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}},B(x,2r)}{r^{-p}w(B(x,r))}\biggr)^{1/(p-1)}\,\frac{dr}{r}=\infty.

(3.8)

Theorem
3.5
is for finite boundary points
x\in\partial\Omega
.
The purpose of this paper is to obtain an analogous result
for
{\boldsymbol{\infty}}\in\widehat{\partial}\Omega
in the unweighted nonlinear case.

4
Circle inversion

In Sections
4
–
7
, we assume that

2\leq n\leq p<\infty

and let

{\mathcal{A}}
denote a map satisfying the
assumptions
(A1)
–
(A3)

stated at the beginning of Section
3
with
w\equiv 1
.
In addition, the notation
w
is henceforth reserved for the weight

w(\xi)=|\xi|^{2(p-n)}
,
and
\Omega\subset{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\setminus\{0\}
shall be an unbounded open set.

The results in this section (except for Proposition
4.8
)
hold whenever
p>\tfrac{1}{2}n
.
For
p\leq\tfrac{1}{2}n
the weight
|\xi|^{2(p-n)}
is not
integrable around the origin, and thus this case
cannot be included here.

We are interested in boundary regularity of the infinity point
{\boldsymbol{\infty}}
.
For this reason, the following circular inversion will be a convenient tool:
Let
T:{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\setminus\{0\}\to{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\setminus\{0\}
denote the map

T(x)=\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}.

We extend this map to
{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\cup\{{\boldsymbol{\infty}}\}
by letting

T(0)=\boldsymbol{{\infty}}\quad\text{and}\quad T(\boldsymbol{{\infty}})=0.

Note that
T^{-1}=T
.
For the reader’s convenience, we now recall some elementary properties of
T
.
The Jacobian matrix of
T
at a point
x\in{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\setminus\{0\}
is given by

dT(x):=\frac{1}{|x|^{4}}\begin{pmatrix}|x|^{2}-2x_{1}^{2}&-2x_{1}x_{2}&\mathinner{.}\nonscript\!.\nonscript\!\mathinner{.}&-2x_{1}x_{n}\\
-2x_{1}x_{2}&|x|^{2}-2x_{2}^{2}&\mathinner{.}\nonscript\!.\nonscript\!\mathinner{.}&-2x_{2}x_{n}\\
\vbox{\kern 6.0pt\hbox{.}\hbox{.}\hbox{.}\kern 3.0pt}&\vbox{\kern 6.0pt\hbox{.}\hbox{.}\hbox{.}\kern 3.0pt}&\mathinner{\mkern 1.0mu\raise 8.6pt\vbox{\kern 7.0pt\hbox{.}}\raise 5.8pt\hbox{.}\raise 3.0pt\hbox{.}\mkern 1.0mu}&\vbox{\kern 6.0pt\hbox{.}\hbox{.}\hbox{.}\kern 3.0pt}\\
-2x_{1}x_{n}&-2x_{2}x_{n}&\mathinner{.}\nonscript\!.\nonscript\!\mathinner{.}&|x|^{2}-2x_{n}^{2}\end{pmatrix}\hskip-3.99994pt.

Let
J_{T}(x)
denote the determinant of
dT(x)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For all
x\in{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\setminus\{0\}
and
q\in{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
we have that

|dT(x)q|=\frac{|q|}{|x|^{2}}.

(4.1)

The square of the left-hand side equals

\displaystyle|dT(x)q|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\biggl(\frac{q_{i}}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{2x_{i}}{|x|^{4}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}q_{j}\biggr)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\biggl(\frac{q_{i}^{2}}{|x|^{4}}-\frac{4x_{i}q_{i}}{|x|^{6}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}q_{j}+\frac{4x_{i}^{2}}{|x|^{8}}\biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}q_{j}\biggr)^{2}\biggr)=\frac{|q|^{2}}{|x|^{4}}.\qed

Corollary 4.2
.

For each
x\in{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\setminus\{0\}
we have that

|J_{T}(x)|=|x|^{-2n}.

(4.2)

Since
dT(x)
is a real symmetric matrix, it has real eigenvectors.
Lemma
4.1
therefore ensures that each eigenvalue

\lambda
of
dT(x)
satisfies
|\lambda|=|x|^{-2}
.
Moreover, the determinant
J_{T}(x)
is the product of these eigenvalues,
and so
|J_{T}(x)|=|x|^{-2n}
.
∎

The rest of this section is devoted to showing that,
under the inversion
T
,
the equation
\Div{\mathcal{A}}(x,\nabla u(x))=0
is transformed into

\Div{\mathcal{B}}(\xi,\nabla\tilde{u}(\xi))=0
, where
\xi=T(x)
,
\tilde{u}=u\circ T

and the map
{\mathcal{B}}:{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\times{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\to{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
is defined by

{\mathcal{B}}(\xi,q)=\begin{cases}|J_{T}(x)|^{-1}dT(x){\mathcal{A}}(x,dT(x)q),&\text{if }\xi\neq 0,\\
0,&\text{if }\xi=0.\end{cases}

We begin by showing that
{\mathcal{B}}
satisfies the assumptions
(A1)
–
(A3)
with
the weight

w(\xi)=|\xi|^{2(p-n)}.

Recall that
w
is
p\mspace{1mu}
-admissible
by the initial remarks of Section
2
.

Lemma 4.3
.

The map
{\mathcal{B}}
satisfies the assumptions
(A1)
–
(A3)

from Section
3
with
the weight
w
.

Since
T
is a smooth map,
{\mathcal{B}}
inherits the measurability
and continuity properties of
{\mathcal{A}}
,
i.e.
(A1)
and
(A2)
.
For a.e.
\xi\in{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
, the following hold:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}(\xi,q)\cdot q

\displaystyle=|J_{T}(x)|^{-1}dT(x){\mathcal{A}}(x,dT(x)q)\cdot q\overset{\eqref{det}}{=}|x|^{2n}{\mathcal{A}}(x,dT(x)q)\cdot(dT(x)q)

\displaystyle{\geq}\alpha_{1}|x|^{2n}|dT(x)q|^{p}\overset{\eqref{op-norm}}{=}\alpha_{1}|x|^{2(n-p)}|q|^{p}=\alpha_{1}w(\xi)|q|^{p}

and

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{B}}(\xi,q)|

\displaystyle\leq|J_{T}(x)|^{-1}|dT(x){\mathcal{A}}(x,dT(x)q)|\overset{\eqref{op-norm},\eqref{det}}{=}|x|^{2(n-1)}|{\mathcal{A}}(x,dT(x)q)|

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{cond2}}{\leq}\alpha_{2}|x|^{2(n-1)}|dT(x)q|^{p-1}\overset{\eqref{op-norm}}{=}\alpha_{2}|x|^{2(n-p)}|q|^{p-1}=\alpha_{2}w(\xi)|q|^{p-1}.

Clearly the homogeneity property (
3.3
) is inherited by
{\mathcal{B}}
.
Finally, if

q\neq q^{\prime}
then

dT(x)q\neq dT(x)q^{\prime}
,
and thus

\displaystyle({\mathcal{B}}(\xi,q)-{\mathcal{B}}(\xi,q^{\prime}))\cdot(q-q^{\prime})

\displaystyle\qquad=|J_{T}(x)|^{-1}dT(x)({\mathcal{A}}(x,dT(x)q)-{\mathcal{A}}(x,dT(x)q^{\prime}))\cdot(q-q^{\prime})

\displaystyle\qquad\overset{\eqref{det}}{=}|x|^{2n}({\mathcal{A}}(x,dT(x)q)-{\mathcal{A}}(x,dT(x)q^{\prime}))\cdot(dT(x)q-dT(x)q^{\prime})\overset{\eqref{cond4}}{>}0.\qed

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
G\Subset{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\setminus\{0\}
be open.
Then

u\in H^{1,p}(G)\quad\text{if and only if}\quad u\circ T\in H^{1,p}(T(G),w).

(4.3)

Moreover,
\nabla(u\circ T)(\xi)=dT(\xi)(\nabla u\circ T)(\xi)
when
u\in H^{1,p}(G)
.

Let
u\in H^{1,p}(G)
,
\tilde{u}=u\circ T
and
\varphi_{j}\in C^{\infty}(G)
be a sequence converging to
u
in
H^{1,p}(G)
, i.e.

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{G}(|u-\varphi_{j}|^{p}+|\nabla u-\nabla\varphi_{j}|^{p})\,dx=0.

(4.4)

Then

\widetilde{\varphi}_{j}=\varphi_{j}\circ T\in C^{\infty}(T(G))\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}_{j}(\xi)=dT(\xi)(\nabla\varphi_{j}\circ T)(\xi).

(4.5)

Since
G\Subset{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\setminus\{0\}
,
it follows from
Corollary
4.2

that

\int_{T(G)}|\widetilde{\varphi}_{j}|^{p}w\,d\xi=\int_{G}|\varphi_{j}|^{p}|x|^{-2p}\,dx\simeq\int_{G}|\varphi_{j}|^{p}\,dx,

with comparison constants depending only on
G
.
Thus
\widetilde{\varphi}_{j}\in L^{p}(T(G),w)
.
Similarly,
we have by (
4.4
) that

\int_{T(G)}|\tilde{u}-\widetilde{\varphi}_{j}|^{p}w\,d\xi\simeq\int_{G}|u-\varphi_{j}|^{p}\,dx\to 0,\quad\text{as }j\to\infty.

Finally, setting

\nabla\tilde{u}(\xi):=dT(\xi)\nabla u(T(\xi))
,
together with (
4.5
),
Lemma
4.1
and
Corollary
4.2
,
we find that

\displaystyle\int_{T(G)}|\nabla\tilde{u}-\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}_{j}|^{p}w\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{T(G)}|dT(\xi)(\nabla u\circ T-\nabla\varphi_{j}\circ T)|^{p}w\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{G}|\nabla u-\nabla\varphi_{j}|^{p}\,dx\to 0,\quad\text{as }j\to\infty.

Thus, the sequence
\widetilde{\varphi}_{j}
converges to
\tilde{u}
in
H^{1,p}(T(G),w)

and
\nabla\tilde{u}
is indeed the gradient of
\tilde{u}
therein.
This shows one implication in (
4.3
) (and the last part).
The converse implication is shown similarly.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
u,v\in H^{1,p}_{\textup{loc}}(\Omega)
,
\tilde{u}=u\circ T
,
\tilde{v}=v\circ T

and
E\Subset\Omega
be measurable.
Then
\tilde{u},\tilde{v}\in H^{1,p}_{\textup{loc}}(T(\Omega),w)
and

\int_{E}{\mathcal{A}}(x,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla v\,dx=\int_{T(E)}{\mathcal{B}}(\xi,\nabla\tilde{u})\cdot\nabla\tilde{v}\,d\xi.

Lemma
4.4
ensures that indeed
\tilde{u},\tilde{v}\in H^{1,p}_{\textup{loc}}(T(\Omega),w)
,
and also

\nabla u(x)=dT(x)(\nabla\tilde{u}\circ T)(x)\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla v(x)=dT(x)(\nabla\tilde{v}\circ T)(x).

By writing
x=T(\xi)
we find that

\displaystyle\int_{E}{\mathcal{A}}(x,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla v\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{E}(\nabla v)^{t}{\mathcal{A}}(x,\nabla u)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{T(E)}(\nabla\tilde{v})^{t}dT(x){\mathcal{A}}(x,dT(x)\nabla\tilde{u})|J_{T}(x)|^{-1}\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{T(E)}{\mathcal{B}}(\xi,\nabla\tilde{u})\cdot\nabla\tilde{v}\,d\xi.\qed

Hereafter,
{\mathcal{B}}
-harmonicity,
{\mathcal{B}}
-superharmonicity and
{\mathcal{B}}
-regularity
should always be interpreted with respect to the set
T(\Omega)
and the weight
w(\xi)=|\xi|^{2(p-n)}
.

(
The corresponding notions for
{\mathcal{A}}
concern
\Omega
and the Lebesgue measure
with the constant weight
1
.
)

Lemma 4.6
.

A function
u
is
{\mathcal{A}}
-harmonic in
\Omega
if and only if
u\circ T

is
{\mathcal{B}}
-harmonic in
T(\Omega)
.
The corresponding assertion concerning superharmonicity is valid as well.

We begin with the first claim.
Lemma
4.4
shows that

u\in H^{1,p}_{\textup{loc}}(\Omega)\quad\text{if and only if}\quad u\circ T\in H^{1,p}_{\textup{loc}}(T(\Omega),w).

Furthermore,
since

\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\varphi\circ T\in C^{\infty}_{0}(T(\Omega)),

Lemma
4.5
ensures that
u
meets the conditions
of Definition
3.1
with
{\mathcal{A}}
,
\Omega
and the constant weight
1

if and only if

u\circ T
meets the same conditions with
{\mathcal{B}}
,
T(\Omega)
and the weight
w(\xi)=|\xi|^{2(p-n)}
.
(Note that in Definition
3.1

it suffices to integrate over the set
E=\supp\varphi
,
which is allowed in
Lemma
4.5
.)

To prove the second claim, suppose that
u

is an
{\mathcal{A}}
-superharmonic function in
\Omega
.
The lower semicontinuity of
u
is then inherited by

\capp_{p}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r},\Omega\cup B_{2r})=\capp_{p}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r},{\mathbf{R}^{n}})=0\quad\text{for large }r.

Thus the Wiener integral (
1.2
) converges, and so

{\boldsymbol{\infty}}
is irregular by Theorem
1.1
.

Conversely, assume that
\partial\Omega
is unbounded.
Then there is a sequence
x_{j}\in\partial\Omega
such that
|x_{j+1}|\geq 2|x_{j}|>8

for
j=1,2,\dots
.
For
\tfrac{1}{4}|x_{j}|\leq r\leq\tfrac{1}{2}|x_{j}|

and
\xi_{j}=T(x_{j})
, we get using monotonicity,
Lemma
5.1

and

[
13
, Lemma 2.16]
,
that

\displaystyle\capp_{p}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r},\Omega\cup B_{2r})

\displaystyle\geq\capp_{p}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{|x_{j}|/4},{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\setminus\{x_{j}\})=\capp_{p,w}(\{\xi_{j}\},B_{4|\xi_{j}|})

\displaystyle\geq\capp_{p,w}(\{\xi_{j}\},B(\xi_{j},5|\xi_{j}|))\simeq\capp_{p,w}(\{\xi_{j}\},B(\xi_{j},\tfrac{1}{2}|\xi_{j}|)).

Since
w\simeq|\xi_{j}|^{2(p-n)}\simeq r^{2(n-p)}
in

,

\capp_{p}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r},\Omega\cup B_{2r})\gtrsim r^{2(n-p)}\capp_{p}(\{\xi_{j}\},B(\xi_{j},\tfrac{1}{2}|\xi_{j}|))\simeq r^{2(n-p)}|\xi_{j}|^{n-p}\simeq r^{n-p}.

Therefore,

\int_{1}^{\infty}\biggl(\frac{\capp_{p}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r},\Omega\cup B_{2r})}{r^{n-p}}\biggr)^{1/(p-1)}\,\frac{dr}{r}\gtrsim\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{|x_{j}|/4}^{|x_{j}|/2}\,\frac{dr}{r}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\log 2=\infty,

which together with

shows that

is regular.
∎

6

Recall the standing assumptions from the beginning of
Section
4
.

In this section we prove Theorem
1.3
.
First however, we must prove some estimates for certain capacities
and Wiener-type integrals.

Recall from the Wiener criterion (
3.8
) that the origin is

{\mathcal{B}}
-regular with respect to
T(\Omega)

and the weight
w(\xi)=|\xi|^{2(p-n)}
, if and only if

\int_{0}^{1}\biggl(\frac{\capp_{p,w}(T(\Omega)^{c}\cap{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r},B_{2r})}{r^{p-n}}\biggr)^{1/(n-1)}\,\frac{dr}{r}=\infty,

.

.

(v).

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\biggl(\frac{\capp_{p,w}(E\cap({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r}\setminus B_{2^{-1/r}}),B_{2r}\setminus{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{4^{-1/r}})}{r^{p-n}}\biggr)^{1/(p-1)}\,\frac{dr}{r}=\infty

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
0<s<t<r
and
K\subset{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r}\setminus B_{t}
be compact.
Then

\capp_{p,w}(K,B_{2r}\setminus{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{s})\leq\capp_{p,w}(K,B_{2r})+\capp_{p,w}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{s},B_{t}).

(6.1)

Let
\varepsilon>0
.
By
the definition of the capacities,
there are
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{t})
and
v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{2r})
such that

u\geq 1
on
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{s}
,
v\geq 1
on
K
,

\int_{B_{t}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,d\xi<\capp_{p,w}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{s},B_{t})+\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{B_{2r}}|\nabla v|^{p}\,d\xi<\capp_{p,w}(K,B_{2r})+\varepsilon.

Then

\varphi:=\max\bigl\{\min\{1-u,v\},0\bigr\}\in C({\mathbf{R}^{n}})\cap H^{1,p}({\mathbf{R}^{n}}),

\varphi=1
on
K

and
\varphi=0

outside
G:=B_{2r}\setminus{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{s}
.
It then follows from Theorem 4.5 in
[
13
]
that
\varphi\in H^{1,p}_{0}(G)
,
and thus

\varphi\in W_{p}(K,G,w)
(as in (

)).
Thus
by (

),

\displaystyle\capp_{p,w}(K,G)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{G}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}\,d\xi\leq\int_{B_{2r}}(|\nabla u|^{p}+|\nabla v|^{p})\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\capp_{p,w}(K,B_{2r})+\capp_{p,w}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{s},B_{t})+2\varepsilon.

Letting

proves the inequality (

).
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
0<r\leq 1

and
K\subset{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r}\setminus B_{2^{-1/r}}
be compact.
Then

\capp_{p,w}(K,B_{2r}\setminus B_{4^{-1/r}})\lesssim\capp_{p,w}(K,B_{2r})+f(r),

where the comparison constant depends only on
n
and
p
, and

f(r)=\begin{cases}r^{n-1},&\text{if }p=n,\\
2^{(n-p)/r},&\text{if }p>n,\end{cases}

Let
s=4^{-1/r}
and
t=2^{-1/r}
.
A simple calculation shows that
t=2^{-1/r}<r
.
It follows from
(
2.9
)
that for
p=n
(and thus

w\equiv 1
),

\capp_{n}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{s},B_{t})\simeq\biggl(\log\frac{t}{s}\biggr)^{1-n}\simeq r^{n-1},

and from (
2.6
) that for
p>n
,

\capp_{p,w}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{s},B_{t})\leq\capp_{p,w}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{t/2},B_{t})\simeq(t/2)^{-p}w(B_{t/2})\simeq t^{p-n}=2^{(n-p)/r}.

Together with Lemma
6.2
this yields the desired
estimate.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
E\subset{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
be closed. Then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\biggl(\frac{\capp_{p,w}(E\cap{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r},B_{2r})}{r^{p-n}}\biggr)^{1/(p-1)}\,\frac{dr}{r}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\simeq\int_{0}^{1}\biggl(\frac{\capp_{p,w}(E\cap({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r}\setminus B_{r^{2}}),B_{2r})}{r^{p-n}}\biggr)^{1/(p-1)}\,\frac{dr}{r},

with absolute comparison constants.

From Example 2.22 in
[
13
]
we infer that
\capp_{p,w}\{0\}=0
, and therefore

\capp_{p,w}(\{0\},B_{2r})=0.

Using this,
the monotonicity and countable subadditivity of
\capp_{p,w}
, and
the elementary inequality

\biggl(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j}\biggr)^{c}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j}^{c}\quad\text{for $a_{j}\geq 0$ and $c=\frac{1}{p-1}\leq 1$},

(6.2)

we find that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\biggl(\frac{\capp_{p,w}(E\cap{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r},B_{2r})}{r^{p-n}}\biggr)^{1/(p-1)}\,\frac{dr}{r}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\int_{0}^{1}\biggl(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\capp_{p,w}(E\cap({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r^{2^{j}}}\setminus B_{r^{2^{j+1}}}),B_{2r^{2^{j}}})}{r^{p-n}}\biggr)^{1/(p-1)}\,\frac{dr}{r}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\biggl(\frac{\capp_{p,w}(E\cap({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r^{2^{j}}}\setminus B_{r^{2^{j+1}}}),B_{2r^{2^{j}}})}{r^{p-n}}\biggr)^{1/(p-1)}\,\frac{dr}{r}.

The change of variables
\rho=r^{2^{j}}
allows us the rewrite the above series as

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j}\int_{0}^{1}\biggl(\frac{\capp_{p,w}(E\cap({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{\rho}\setminus B_{\rho^{2}}),B_{2\rho})}{\rho^{(p-n)/2^{j}}}\biggr)^{1/(p-1)}\,\frac{d\rho}{\rho}

which proves the inequality

The reverse inequality follows directly from the monotonicity of
\capp_{p,w}
.
∎

.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
G\subset{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\setminus\{0\}
be open and

be compact.
Then

\capp_{p}(K,G)=\capp_{p,w}(T(K),T(G)).

Note that

is admissible for
\capp_{p}(K,G)

if and only if
\tilde{u}=u\circ T\in C_{0}^{\infty}(T(G))

is admissible for
\capp_{p,w}(T(K),T(G))

{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}}}\subset B_{r/2}\quad\text{whenever}\quad\tfrac{1}{2}r>\tfrac{5}{4}\alpha_{j}>\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{j+1},

(7.5)

and

{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}}}\cap{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r}=\varnothing\quad\text{whenever}\quad r<\tfrac{3}{4}\alpha_{j}<\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{j+1}.

Thus if
r>\tfrac{5}{2}\alpha_{1}
or

\tfrac{5}{2}\alpha_{j}<r<\frac{3}{4}\alpha_{j-1}
,
j\geq 2
, then

F\cap({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r}\setminus B_{r/2})=\varnothing.

On the other hand, if

\tfrac{3}{4}\alpha_{j}\leq r\leq\tfrac{5}{2}\alpha_{j}
, then

F\cap({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r}\setminus B_{r/2})\subset{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}}}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{2r}\supset B(x_{j},\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha_{j}).

From these inclusions, together with the monotonicity of
\capp_{n}
and
(
2.9
),
we infer that

\displaystyle\capp_{n}(F\cap({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r}\setminus B_{r/2}),B_{2r})^{1/(n-1)}

\displaystyle\leq\capp_{n}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}}},B(x_{j},\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha_{j}))^{1/(n-1)}

\displaystyle\simeq\biggl(\log\frac{\alpha_{j}}{2\alpha_{j+1}}\biggr)^{-1}\simeq 2^{-j},

where the comparison constants depend only on
n
. Therefore,

I_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{3\alpha_{j}/4}^{5\alpha_{j}/2}\capp_{n}(F\cap({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r}\setminus B_{r/2}),B_{2r})^{1/(n-1)}\,\frac{dr}{r}\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j}<\infty.

Next we turn to
I_{2}
.
If
2\alpha_{j}\leq r\leq 2\alpha_{j-1}
,
j\geq 2
, then again by monotonicity,
(
7.5
) and
(
2.9
),

\displaystyle\capp_{n}(F\cap{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r},B_{2r})^{1/(n-1)}

\displaystyle\geq\capp_{n}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x_{j},\alpha_{j+1})}}},B(x_{j},5\alpha_{j-1}))^{1/(n-1)}

\displaystyle\simeq\biggl(\log\frac{5\alpha_{j-1}}{\alpha_{j+1}}\biggr)^{-1}\geq 2^{-j-1},

and so

I_{2}\geq\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\int_{2\alpha_{j}}^{2\alpha_{j-1}}\capp_{n}(F\cap{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r},B_{2r})^{1/(n-1)}\,\frac{dr}{r}\gtrsim\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}2^{-j-1}2^{j}=\infty.

In conclusion, when
p=n
,
the origin is
{\mathcal{B}}
-regular with respect to
T(\Omega)=\mathbf{R}^{n}\setminus F
,
but the Wiener integral in (
7.4
) is convergent.
That is, the condition (
7.2
) does not characterize
{\mathcal{B}}
-regularity at the origin when
p=n
.

It remains to state and prove Lemma
7.3
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
E\subset{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
be closed,

r>0
and
0<a<b\leq 1
.
Then

\displaystyle\capp_{p,w}(E\cap({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r}\setminus B_{br}),B_{2r})

\displaystyle\leq\capp_{p,w}(E\cap({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r}\setminus B_{br}),B_{2r}\setminus{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{ar})

\displaystyle\leq c\capp_{p,w}(E\cap({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r}\setminus B_{br}),B_{2r}),

where

c=2^{p}+4^{p}c_{p,w}/(b-a)^{p}

and
c_{p,w}>0
is the constant from the Poincaré inequality (
7.6
) below.

To prove Lemma
7.3
we need the
following Poincaré inequality
[
13
, p. 9]
:
There is a constant
c_{p,w}>0

such that for every
ball
B=B(x,r)\subset{\mathbf{R}^{n}}

and function
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B)

we have

\int_{B}|u|^{p}w\,d\xi\leq c_{p,w}r^{p}\int_{B}|\nabla u|^{p}w\,d\xi.

(7.6)

The first inequality follows directly from the monotonicity of
\capp_{p,w}
.
For
the second inequality, let

\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{2r}\setminus{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{ar})
be such that

\varphi\equiv 1\text{ on }{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r}\setminus B_{br},\quad 0\leq\varphi\leq 1\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla\varphi|\leq\frac{2}{(b-a)r}.

For every
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{2r})
satisfying
u\geq 1
on
E\cap({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r}\setminus B_{br})
,
we have

\displaystyle\capp_{p,w}(E\cap({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{r}\setminus B_{br}),B_{2r}\setminus{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{ar})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{2r}\setminus{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{ar}}|\nabla(\varphi u)|^{p}w\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p}\int_{B_{2r}}\varphi^{p}|\nabla u|^{p}w\,d\xi+2^{p}\int_{B_{2r}}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}|u|^{p}w\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p}\int_{B_{2r}}|\nabla u|^{p}w\,d\xi+\frac{4^{p}}{(b-a)^{p}r^{p}}\int_{B_{2r}}|u|^{p}w\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{B_{2r}}|\nabla u|^{p}w\,d\xi,

where the final step follows from the Poincaré inequality (
7.6
).
Since

was arbitrary,
this proves the second inequality in the statement of the lemma.

8
A generalization to Ahlfors regular metric spaces

In this section we will show that
Theorem
1.2
generalizes to
p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic functions in
Ahlfors
Q
-regular metric spaces, with
p>Q
.
Since metric spaces are
not the main topic in this paper we will try to be brief.
We refer to the monograph
Björn–Björn
[
2
]
for the theory
of
p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic functions in metric spaces
and original references to the literature.
In this setting, we only have the sufficiency part of the Wiener criterion
(proved in Björn–MacManus–Shanmugalingam
[
10
, Theorem 5.1]

under an additional geometric condition that
was later removed by Björn
[
9
, Lemma 3.9]
),
but that will be enough here.

Assume that
X=(X,d)
is a complete metric space equipped
with a positive complete Borel measure
\mu

which is
Ahlfors
Q
-regular
, with some
Q\geq 1
,
i.e.

\mu(B(x,r))\simeq r^{Q}.

We also assume that
1<p<\infty
and that
\Omega\subset X
is open.

Following Heinonen–Koskela
[
14
]
and Koskela–MacManus
[
20
]

we say that a measurable
function
g:\Omega\to[0,\infty]
is a
p\mspace{1mu}
-weak
upper gradient

of
u:\Omega\to\mathbf{R}

if for
p\mspace{1mu}
-almost all nonconstant rectifiable curves

\gamma:[0,1]\to\Omega
,

|u(\gamma(0))-u(\gamma(1))|\leq\int_{\gamma}g\,ds,

(8.1)

where
ds
denotes arc length and

p\mspace{1mu}
-almost all
means
that there is
\rho\in L^{p}(\Omega)
such that

\int_{\gamma}\rho\,ds=\infty
for every
nonconstant rectifiable curve
\gamma
for
which (
8.1
) fails.
Here, a
curve
is a continuous mapping from an interval,
and a
rectifiable
curve is a curve with finite length.

If
u
has a
p\mspace{1mu}
-weak upper gradient in
L^{p}_{\textup{loc}}(\Omega)
, then
it has a
minimal
p\mspace{1mu}
-weak upper gradient

g_{u}\in L^{p}_{\textup{loc}}(\Omega)
in the sense that
g_{u}\leq g
a.e. for every
p\mspace{1mu}
-weak upper gradient
g\in L^{p}_{\textup{loc}}(\Omega)
of
u
.

A continuous function

u
with
g_{u}\in L^{p}_{\textup{loc}}(\Omega)
is

p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic
in
\Omega

if

\int_{\varphi\neq 0}g^{p}_{u}\,d\mu\leq\int_{\varphi\neq 0}g_{u+\varphi}^{p}\,d\mu\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in{\Lip_{c}}(\Omega),

(8.2)

where
{\Lip_{c}}(\Omega)
denotes the space of Lipschitz functions
with compact support in
\Omega
.

Superharmonic functions and Perron solutions are now defined as in
Definitions
3.2
and
3.3
.
(See Chapters 9 and 14 in
[
2
]
for why this definition of
superharmonicity
is equivalent to other definitions used in metric spaces,
under our assumptions.)
In metric spaces,
these notions were introduced by
Shanmugalingam
[
25
]
,
Kinnunen–Shanmugalingam
[
18
]
,
Kinnunen–Martio
[
17
]

and Björn–Björn–Shanmugalingam
[
8
]
.

The measure

\mu
supports a
p\mspace{1mu}
-Poincaré inequality
if
there exist constants
C>0
and
\lambda\geq 1

such that for all balls
B\subset X
,
all integrable functions
u
on
X
and all
upper gradients
g
of
u
,

\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}|u-u_{B}|\,d\mu\leq C(\diam B)\biggl(\frac{1}{\mu(\lambda B)}\int_{\lambda B}g^{p}\,d\mu\biggr)^{1/p},

where
u_{B}:=\int_{B}u\,d\mu/\mu(B)
and
\lambda B(x,r)=B(x,\lambda r)
.

In the rest of this section, we fix a base point
b\in X
.
To simplify the notation we write

|x|:=d(x,b)
.
The space
X
is
annularly connected for large radii around
b

if there are constants
A,R_{A}\geq 1
such that
any two points
x
and
y
with
|x|=|y|\geq R_{A}

can be connected by a (not necessarily rectifiable) curve

\gamma\subset B(b,A|x|)\setminus{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B(b,|x|/A)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(b,|x|/A)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B(b,|x|/A)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(b,|x|/A)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B(b,|x|/A)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(b,|x|/A)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B(b,|x|/A)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(b,|x|/A)}}}.

Instead of the inversion
T

used earlier, we will sphericalize
X
into a bounded space.
This is done by equipping it with a
new metric and a new measure preserving
p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic functions,
see Björn–Björn–Li
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
for further details and
original references.
Let
{\widehat{X}}=X\cup\{{\boldsymbol{\infty}}\}
.
Define
d_{b},\hat{d}:{\widehat{X}}\times{\widehat{X}}\to[0,\infty)
by

d_{b}(x,y)=d_{b}(y,x)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{d(x,y)}{(1+|x|)(1+|y|)},&\text{if }x,y\in X,\\[8.53581pt]
\dfrac{1}{1+|x|},&\text{if }x\in X\text{ and }y={\boldsymbol{\infty}},\\[8.53581pt]
0,&\text{if }x=y={\boldsymbol{\infty}},\end{cases}

and

\hat{d}(x,y)=\inf_{x=x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k}=y}\sum_{j=1}^{k}d_{b}(x_{j-1},x_{j}),

where the infimum is taken over all finite sequences

x=x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k}=y
.
This makes
\hat{d}
into a metric on
{\widehat{X}}
, and
({\widehat{X}},\hat{d})

is the
sphericalization
of
(X,d)
.
Then
\diam_{\hat{d}}{\widehat{X}}=1
.
Note that
\hat{d}(x,{\boldsymbol{\infty}})=d_{b}(x,{\boldsymbol{\infty}})
, see
[
6
, Section 3]
.
To simplify the notation let
\hat{d}(x)=\hat{d}(x,{\boldsymbol{\infty}})
.
We equip
{\widehat{X}}
with the measure
{\hat{\mu}}
given by

d\hat{\mu}(x)=\frac{d\mu(x)}{(1+|x|)^{2p}}=\hat{d}(x)^{2p}\,d\mu(x)\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{\mu}(\{{\boldsymbol{\infty}}\})=0.

It follows from the results in
[
6
]

that
under the assumptions in Theorem
8.1
,
{\hat{\mu}}
is a doubling
measure (i.e.
0<{\hat{\mu}}(2B)\lesssim{\hat{\mu}}(B)<\infty
for all balls in
{\widehat{X}}
)
and supports a
p\mspace{1mu}
-Poincaré inequality.
Moreover, a function
u:\Omega\to\mathbf{R}
is
p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic (resp. superharmonic)
with respect to
(d,\mu)
if and only if it is

p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic (resp. superharmonic) with respect to

(\hat{d},{\hat{\mu}})
.
Boundary regularity for
p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic functions is
defined just as before.
Since the Perron solutions are the same with respect to
(d,\mu)

and
(\hat{d},{\hat{\mu}})
, boundary regularity is also the same.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
X
be an unbounded complete Ahlfors
Q
-regular metric space
and let
\Omega\varsubsetneq X
be an unbounded open set and
p>Q
.
Assume that either

1.

X
supports a
Q
-Poincaré inequality and
Q>1
;
or

2.

X
supports a p-Poincaré inequality and is
annularly connected for large radii around some

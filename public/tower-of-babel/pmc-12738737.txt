Design and validation of a flexible framework for developing generic digital twins for Industry 4.0
Authors: Rudolf Pribiš, Peter Drahoš, Štefan Kozák, Lukáš Beňo, Erik Kučera, Oto Haffner, Martin Pajpach
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe paper presents an innovative methodology, development and implementation of a Digital Twin prototype based on a 5 Dimensional Digital Twin architecture combined with a Service-Oriented Architecture and the Industry 4.0 Component concept. The proposed implementation method separates the business logic (Digital Twin Brain), which enables the Digital Twin to perform its new capabilities, from the domain logic, which provides the specific functionalities of the Digital Twin. The specific functionalities are provided and implemented by the Asset Administration Shell Industry 4.0 Components available as stateless services, which allowed the creation of a base of shared technical functionalities among multiple Digital Twins. The methodology and architecture were validated using real data from an industrial packaging line, where the Digital Twin leverages machine learning-based algorithms to improve efficiency and reduce production downtime. The obtained results confirm the flexibility and scalability of the proposed architecture, which allows not only predictive maintenance but also seamless integration of additional domain functions. The proposed methodology contributes to the efficient deployment of Digital Twins in multidisciplinary environments and lays the foundation for a wide range of applications in the context of Industry 5.0.Keywords: Industry 4.0, Digital Twin architecture, Service oriented architecture, Machine learning, Predictive maintenance, Industry 5.0Subject terms: Information technology, Software

Introduction

Industry 4.0 today shows that the Digital Twin (DT) is a key enabler of effective technology for Industry 5.0, enabling the link between cyberspace and physical space and shaping the current ongoing physical-digital revolution. A comprehensive and advanced process control methodology through DT enables monitoring, controlling, and optimizing management processes based on data collected from real-world environments for different phases of the life cycle of manufactures and products. The use of DT methodology and principles offers nowadays a suitable and necessary opportunity for implementation in Industry 5.0 in order to analyze and implement efficient and integrated decisions. The paper offers new unique solutions verified on real food industry processes. A new unique approach of using DT applicable to a wide class of industrial applications is shown using numerical and graphical representations of the results as a demonstration example. The achieved results are beneficial both in the methodological, research and application areas and are in line with the new requirements and objectives declared in the Industry 5.0 concept.

This paper proposes a novel 5D DT architecture for Industry 4.0 (5D-DT-I4.0), using Service Oriented Architecture (SOA) to standardize DT interfaces for Industry 4.0 (I4.0) needs. In the line of the I4.0 concept, a modification of the 5D DT architecture is proposed by separating the service domain logic (the services that execute the DT functions) from the business logic (the DT brain that drives the execution of the functions). By adding an interoperability middleware layer, a standardized interface between DT and I4.0 services is created, which in the SOA sense is made up of stateless services. This new structure allows the creation of a base of functionalities (domain logic) that can be shared between multiple DT brains (business logic). By generalising the prototyping procedure, a general framework is defined to create a digital twin with a flexible architecture suitable for the creation of DTs designed to solve multidisciplinary tasks.

The prototype of the proposed 5D-DT-I4.0 was designed to demonstrate the viability of the novel architecture by showcasing the capability of minimizing unplanned stoppages caused by failures in parts of the packaging line during real production. DT algorithms use Machine Learning to classify internal machine states, where the classification of these states results not only in information about the possibility of a machine failure, but the specific fault that caused the malfunction. The idea of minimizing unplanned stoppages using DT with predictive capabilities was born from the collaboration of three stakeholders: food manufacturing plant (Nestlé Carpathia), their system integrator for MES, and the university (Slovak University of Technology in Bratislava). The paper includes a use case and validation of the proposed solution on anonymized data from real production and a generalized original design methodology of DT interoperable with I4.0 (5D-DT-I4.0).

State of the art

The term digital twin is currently used to refer to solutions to a really wide range of tasks. According to a 2020 survey1, there are around 250 classifications of DT technologies. This number includes a combination of three kinds of criteria: hierarchical level (informational, component, product, process, system, and multi-system), lifecycle phase (design, build, operate, maintain, optimize, and decommission). and use-case (most widely: digitize, visualize, simulate, emulate, extract, orchestrate, and predict). Although some of the combinations can only be considered as digital model2 or digital shadow2 and not digital twin. And not surprisingly, there are also several definitions of DT3–6. The authors of this paper have roots in automation and mechatronics and therefore most closely identify with a definition of DT that includes the physical entity, its digitized counterpart whose data is updated online, the logic that can influence the behavior of the physical entity, and the influence (feedback) of the physical entity itself.

Digital Twin architecture

There are multiple views on the composition, or architecture, of the DT. An example is the six-layer architecture for the digital twin, which is based on the cyber-physical production systems paradigm7. Or the 5D architecture of DT, which groups data, services, physical entities, a virtual model, and their interconnections5,8. The reference architecture is defined as DT as a Service (DTaaS) in Ref.9. A comprehensive overview of DT technology and tools, application domains, applications, frameworks, use-case is mapped in Ref.5. Identifying the challenges associated with creating a standardized architecture is addressed by Ref.10. An overview of the standards dedicated to each dimension of the 5D DT architecture is offered by Ref.6. A detailed analysis and decomposition of the functional components of DT, the difference between DT and CPS as well as the actual definition of DT is discussed in Ref.11. A proposal of dividing DT into evolutionary stages in terms of their maturity, functions is described by Ref.12. From these stages, they define implementation layers which allow a step-by-step approach to implementation. The authors validate their proposals through a combination of theoretical analyses and literature reviews. The paper13 highlighted the importance of artificial intelligence (AI) in the context of DTs, emphasizing that the integration of AI and simulation is key to quantifying uncertainties within complex systems. These digital twins allow users to replay history, gain predictive insight into the future, and identify corrective actions to optimise future performance. Paper14 in turn addresses a gap in the existing literature by providing a broad overview of DT technology in a variety of industries. The authors categorize implementation technologies into basic, core, and advanced levels to systematically present the development history and practical applications of DT. The categorization aims to bridge information gaps between different industries and improve the overall DT technology development process.

Digital Twin architectures and methodologies for Industry 4.0

There are also a number of publications that deal with the practical implementation. Reference15 uses AAS to create DT for Water Supply System, relying on ISO 23247 (Digital Twin Framework for Manufacturing). The paper is rather focused on the digital description of the asset. In another publication16, the authors present a generic DT architecture based on 5D DT, inspired by cyber-physical systems architecture and using SOA, aligned with RAMI 4.0. They verify this approach by applying a data-driven approach to describe non-linear thermal systems. They leverage Semantic Web technologies such as Resource Description Framework and Web Ontology Language to create a shared knowledge base for semantic data integration and contextual information management. The methodology for creating a DT framework based on a 7-layer architecture is described in paper17. The authors validate the proposed methodology on a problem devoted to simulation-based battery state estimation and lifetime optimization. They used a model based approach developed in MATLAB for the computation. The implementation details are not described in the study and similarly the study lacks specific numerical data and detailed explanations on how the real-time data is exported from the Battery Management System to MATLAB for further processing. Based on a review, publication18 identified a gap in the implementation of predictive maintenance and the use of AAS, however, filling this gap is not adequately explained in the paper in terms of the details, the type of information exchanged using AAS, and the information flows between the different elements of the 5D architecture they use. The paper provides conceptual validation through the design of a prototype architecture and references to existing studies and methodologies that support the proposed approach. Article19 demonstrates the practical application of digital twins based on 5D architecture for predictive maintenance in the steel industry. It highlights the usage of a DT as a grey box to deal with the complexity of industrial machinery, enabling improved decision making and reduced maintenance costs. The main contribution is to show how digital twins can predict future maintenance needs, increase production visibility and ensure sustainable operations. Although digital twins can indicate that a problem exists and link these detections to actual maintenance events, the specificity of predicting specific parts or specific defects is not clearly stated. Instead, they focus on identifying irregularities that indicate something is going wrong. The matter of proactive maintenance using peer-to-peer information exchange between AAS is addressed by the authors of Ref.20. Their publication mainly focuses on the autonomous interaction of I4.0 components implemented as AAS. Similarly, the authors also address proactive interaction between AASs in a technical report21.

Five dimensional Digital Twin architecture5.

With the realization of the importance of interoperability and standardization, there came the need to refine the 5D DT architecture to meet the requirements of I4.0, i.e. to be scalable, flexible, easy to integrate, open, secure, and ready to solve tasks autonomously. Reference Model Architecture Industry 4.0 (RAMI 4.0) was chosen as the reference architecture for the integration of the 5D DT architecture into I4.0, which is interchangeable or complementary with other reference models for I4.022. The document Implementation Strategy Industrie 4.023 describes the Asset Administration Shell (AAS) as a standardized way to digitize any asset and connect it to the I4.0 network. An asset in the context of the 5D DT can be thought of as a PE, VE, DD, and SS, including a definition of their CN interconnections. The AAS is a tool for the creation of an I4.0 Component, i.e. an entity that satisfies the requirements of I4.024. By encapsulating the individual parts of a 5D DT into an AAS, it is possible to create a DT as an I4.0 Component. Applying of RAMI 4.0 also requires adherence to the principles of Service Oriented Architecture when designing software for a modified 5D DT architecture. Thus, the original 5D DT architecture5 was modified by incorporating the AAS Repository, DT Brain, and I4.0 Standardized API. The design of the architecture from a software design perspective follows the principles of Service Oriented Architecture.

The AAS Repository (Fig. 2) provides a digital description of the individual parts of the DT as well as its capabilities via a standardized API. The DT described by AAS performs its capabilities through so-called Technical Functionalities25, which are ultimately services tied to a specific technology. In order to achieve I4.0 interoperability, the I4.0 Standardized API Interoperability Middleware (Fig. 2) was created to ensure seamless communication and integration of the different technologies performing the services from which the Digital Twin Technical Functionalities (DTTFs) and consequently the DT Capabilities are composed. Adherence to SOA principles in the implementation of I4.0 Standardized API Interoperability Middleware services enables the creation of a stateless service base for domain logic that can be shared among multiple DTs. Another advantage of this approach is the separation of the domain logic from the DT business logic - the DT Brain (Fig. 2). The role of the business logic is to create interactions between the domain logic services and to group these services to ensure the execution of DT Capabilities. The use of SOA allows the application of patterns to create sequences for the composition of more complex logic.

Proposed novel five dimensional Digital Twin architecture compliant with I4.0 principles.

The separation of domain logic from the DT business logic through the introduction of the I4.0 Standardized API Interoperability Middleware allows to define Digital Twin Technical Functionalities as stateless services that can be shared between multiple DTs. Service-Oriented Architecture allows scaling the base of shared services, as well as adding additional digital asset characteristics to the AAS Repository, thus extending the capabilities of such a framework essentially at runtime.

Asset administration shell as a tool for creating a Digital Twin

The AAS serves as a digital representation of an asset, providing a standardized format for describing the characteristics, states, and functions of an asset throughout its lifecycle. The AAS provides the foundation for interoperability in a smart manufacturing environment, ensuring that different components and systems can communicate and collaborate effectively. An AAS consists of a header that contains the information needed to identify and label the asset(s) and the AAS itself and a body that manages the submodels within the AAS.

Submodels are used to structure the information and functions of the administration shell into distinguishable parts. Each submodel refers to a well-defined domain or subject area. Submodels can be standardized, thus becoming a submodel template. The AAS can refer to a submodel template, indicating that the submodel instance has a fixed structure and a guaranteed set of properties and functions. A DT created using AAS is orientated around specific use cases, where use cases are modelled through its capabilities. The Working Paper25 describes the structure of the DT based on its capabilities as shown in Fig. 3 in more detail. Capabilities: the ability of an asset or system to perform specific tasks or provide specific services. For example, this may be the ability to monitor the condition of the equipment or to perform predictive maintenance.

Hierarchical structure of Digital Twin capabilities, illustrating the levels of abstraction from methods/functions to Digital Twin capabilities.

Operations: an executable implementation of a function, often specified by a list of parameters and possible results. For example, an option that runs a simulation.

Technical functionalities: (or skills) refers to the specific technical skills and operations that an asset can perform. For example, a technical functionality may include collecting data from sensors or performing data analysis.

Methods/functions: are concrete implementations of technical functionality, tied to a specific technology or platform. For example, a method written in a specific programming language that is accessible via a specific communication protocol.

In terms of how information is exchanged, the AAS can act as a passive element, i.e. it does not perform any tasks, it only conveys information. These data can be accessed directly from the AASX file containing the AAS (type 1), or data in the AAS can be accessed by the standardized API27 of the AAS (type 2). Type 3 represents an active AAS, capable of performing technical functionalities and engaging in autonomous cyber-physical communication with other AAS, also referred to as networked AAS20,21. The solution proposed in this paper can be considered halfway between type 2 and type 3.

Design of a Digital Twin with prediction capability using proposed architecture

The physical entity of the DT is the packaging machine in the Filling&Packaging (F&P) part of the production, where the product is packaged in the final packaging in which it reaches the end consumer. The facility is located within the Nestlé multinational corporation, which uses its proprietary Manufacturing Operations Management (MOM) system with a module dedicated to performance tracking (Performance). The module tracks key performance indicators (KPIs) and provides an overview of the overall equipment efficiency of the facilities (OEE = Availability Performance Quality).

Machine failures, known as unplanned stoppages, are part of downtime (i.e. the time the machine does not produce) that negatively affects OEE through the availability component. The machine is in live commercial production and is considered a black-box. Direct DT intervention on the machine control is not desired due to the risk of financial loss and is also not feasible because the machine is controlled via an embedded Human Machine Interface (HMI) screen. Computers on the network are subject to audits and unapproved software cannot be installed on them. Furthermore, the use of academic licences on the servers of a commercial entity presents additional challenges. The data collected from the machine can only be processed on off-site external devices after they have been anonymized. Consequently, the development of the solution was conducted in laboratory conditions, where the physical machine was emulated by an OPC UA server using archived data.

The proposed DT prototype serves as a practical implementation of the modified 5D DT architecture, showcasing its applicability, while also providing predictive capabilities to forecast specific machine failures. Its capability of prediction is to predict a specific machine failure, which allows operators in production time to eliminate the causes of the failure in order to prevent it from occurring or at least to reduce the resulting downtime.

The DT_L1_CubePacker01 AAS contains simulation and prediction mathematical models (SimulationModel, PredictionModel) (Table 2) created in MATLAB environment. The models were created offline (outside DT) using the Classification Learner MATLAB application. The process of obtaining a model describes the flow chart in Fig. 630.

Workflow for creating a classification mathematical model30.

These data-driven models were created from a data set from a 5-day time period, containing the same set of 29 variables as for the physical machine emulator. The data set, along with the timestamp, contains 22.5 million data points. Data were imported (Import Data Fig. 6) into the MATLAB workspace. In the Preprocess Data step in Fig. 6, data were resampled with a sampling period of 10 seconds, missing data was filled in, unwanted data filtered out, physical quantities normalised, etc. Part of this step was the reduction of machine error codes, due to operator manual intervention, or whose frequency in the data set does not allow for an effective classification, to a total of 8. The final data set was divided into classification data (70%) and validation data (30%). In the Extract Features step (Fig. 6), the response vector was defined as the reason code for the machine stoppage and the predictors as a table containing the so-called features, i.e. the 17 variables that have the greatest impact on the response, which were determined on the basis of empirical findings and using the MATLAB Feature Selection function. The simulation model was built using the “Fit k-nearest neighbor classifier” method in the Build Model step (Fig. 6) from the classification data and validated on the validation dataset in the Evaluate Model step (Fig. 6). The result of the evaluation shows a match between True Class (actual machine failure) and Predicted Class (predicted machine failure based on the model). The proposed framework is based on SOA principles in accordance with the RAMI 4.0 reference model, in which system functions are designed as modular, reusable, and stateless services accessible through standardized interfaces. As illustrated in Fig. 8, SOA principles are applied in the architecture through service-oriented interactions between the AAS Repository, Interoperability Middleware, and application-specific services, with the middleware layer serving as an intermediary between the standardized I4.0 environment and the domain-specific environment.

Block scheme of the generic DT framework decomposition.

Standardized part

The standardized part consists of I4.0 components; administration shells that create a standardized digital description of the assets and are connected to the network. The AAS SM information is routed through REST API defined by the Specification of the Asset Administration Shell Part 227. The simplest way to achieve this is to use so-called off-the-shelf Docker containers (Eclipse OTS Docker Containers).

Standardized API

The APIs of the technical functionalities are described using standardized AAS SMs. In January 2024, the IDTA Association published the SM 02017-1-0 Asset Interfaces Description26, which outlines guidelines for creating standardized API descriptions. However, at the time of writing, this standard did not align with the Eclipse BaSyx, or Eclipse AASX Package Explorer V3. Instead, the W3C Web of Things Thing Description (WoT TD) standard, which serves as the foundation for the SM 02017-1-0 Asset Interfaces Description, was utilized. A description via WoT TD can be created using open-source online tools (e.g. Eclipse edi{TD}or) and Eclipse AASPE has a built-in function to import WoT TD JSON strings as SMs, which simplifies the creation of interface descriptions.

DT AAS

The asset’s DT should sufficiently describe its physical counterpart in the digital world. The IDTA Association offers a list of standardized AAS SM templates for different domains and disciplines. The final list of an AAS SMs depends on the domain, the use case, and the life cycle phase of the asset. An important aspect of the AAS for DT in the context of information exchange over AAS Type 3 (i.e. peer-to-peer)27 is the description of the asset’s capabilities. The idea of modeling the capabilities of an asset is divided into several layers:

Capabilities (an abstract description of a asset’s capabilities) refers to Skills.

Capabilities (an abstract description of a asset’s capabilities) refers to Skills.

Skills (detailed description of the asset’s skills) it’s the implementation of the capabilities; refer to operations.

Skills (detailed description of the asset’s skills) it’s the implementation of the capabilities; refer to operations.

Operations (specifically function execution), operations for skills are implemented as executable methods.

Operations (specifically function execution), operations for skills are implemented as executable methods.

At the time of writing, the standardized SM Capability Description26 was only in development. The white paper32 helps to explain the concept of modeling (Capabilities, Skills, Operations) for AAS peer-to-peer information exchange purposes. A standardized description is crucial for autonomous communication between AASs, where AASs communicate with each other in the so-called Negotiation of cooperation and contracts according to “Language of Industrie 4.0”.

Mixed part

The mixed part contains the brown-green Interoperability Middleware and Digital Twin Business Logic components (Fig. 16). Both parts contain a standardized part for I4.0, but also an application, technology, data description, or interface-specific part.

Interoperability middleware

The Interoperability Middleware responsible for translating and sending the standardized request from the I4.0 to the point of execution, which is bound by a specific technology. The middleware is also responsible for back-translating the data into a standardized I4.0-compliant description. The most straightforward solution is to use a REST API where the query syntax is described in a standardized section using AAS SM IDTA 02017-1-0 Asset Interfaces Description26. If the query contains parameters with a complex data type or description, it must also be I4.0 compliant. Compliance can be achieved by using the standardized data descriptions available on the IDTA website26.

Digital Twin business logic

The DT business logic component also covers both parts: standardized for I4.0 and application-specific. The standardized part can be understood as the compilation of a standardized query for the Interoperability Middleware call. In addition, it depends on the specific implementation, whether and how autonomously the DT should interact with the environment, and with other I4.0 components in the network. The application-specific business logic of the DT can also be seen as application-specific. And certainly the direct connection to and feedback on the asset can also be taken as application-specific.

Application specific part

In the application specific part, the asset (Fig. 16) is itself, since it represents a specific part coupled with a specific technology. In most cases, the asset performs the required operations or services (be it machine or software) of the Specific Service(i) (Fig. 16) and is also the bearer of the interface to that service. Thus, the graphical separation of an asset from a service is more of a logical separation, where the black color of the asset represents as if static its immutable characteristics and the brown color represents the more flexible part of the asset - the services and their interfaces. Flexible in the sense that it can be modified programmatically, for example by modifying the control program.

Results and discussion

Interoperability is achieved through standardization, which comes at the cost of increased architectural and programmatic complexity, more intricate administration, and higher computational demands - factors that can drive up costs and contribute to a larger carbon footprint. The primary benefits include higher scalability, seamless access to the I4.0 ecosystem, openness to new technologies, and more.

Interoperability also brings with it a number of open points and gaps. For example, a list of mandatory (or recommended) SMs to be included in the AAS has been and is the subject of several discussions, the consensus being that such a list cannot be created29. An administration shell can describe an asset of a wide variety of domains and use cases. Therefore, creating a list of mandatory SMs is suboptimal and could lead to inventing artificial values that are required to populate the SM. Even overgeneralizing standardized SM templates would lead to an extensive list of parameters that are not always necessary for a particular use case.

The specifications of AAS SM templates lack concrete examples, so they are too abstract, which does not help developers create an optimal description of assets. Overall, this may discourage the use of standardized SMs and they may appear self-explanatory.

Certainly, in the context of integrating solutions into broader systems, standardization is essential. It is the premise of autonomic communication of I4.0 components and brings more flexibility to the architecture and allows it to be reconfigurable at runtime. A healthy degree of it reduces the system integrators’ efforts. One needs to individually evaluate its pros & cons and decide to what extent and depth to implement it.

The proposed architecture provides a flexible framework for collaboratively working DT across different domains. It also offers a mechanism for on-demand scaling of DT capabilities through the use of SOA, ensuring that DTs can adapt and grow as needed for a variety of use cases and integration with other systems. For these reasons, the modified 5D-DT-I4.0 architecture was recognized as a suitable and optimal approach for the integration of digital twins within Industry 4.0 environments (RQ1). In the proposed 5D-DT-I4.0 architecture, ontology is embedded within the AAS through standardized SMs. By adopting the official IDTA AAS SM templates, the framework ensures consistent semantic interpretation of asset data and thus achieves interoperability across different domains (RQ2).

The validation experiment demonstrated the scalability of the DT to prediction capabilities utilizing machine learning, with the potential for expansion to capabilities from other domains through the SOA architecture and the ability to adapt the DT in runtime (RQ3). The paper evaluates the scalability of the proposed framework by implementing the predictive capability of the DT as an SOA service. The method used for validation is the proof of concept, which was applied to verify the proposed framework. The results were quantified using machine emulation with real data from the shop floor, collected over a sufficiently representative period. The validation of the DT capability (i.e. prediction) was performed using the confusion matrix tool, which was used to validate the classification model. (RQ4). On the other hand, validating the concept in real-world manufacturing systems would require further adjustments related to deployment in environments with real-time processing requirements and integration into existing industrial infrastructures.

Conclusion

The paper presents a new conceptual view of DT, which consistently integrates the requirements and attributes of the Industry 4.0 concept into the legacy 5D-DT architecture, in particular interoperability in the form of standardized AAS and a service-oriented approach to implementing DT functionalities. By modifying 5D-DT, the original architecture was created, in which 5D-DT becomes an “Industry 4.0 component” (5D-DT-I4.0). This fundamentally new quality was achieved by separating the business logic (the DT brain), which enables DT to perform its capabilities, from the domain logic, which provides the specific functionality of DT. Specific DT functionalities are implemented as AAS I4.0 components and their functions are available as stateless services, which allowed to create a base of shared technical functionalities between multiple DTs. From the perspective of the 5D-DT architecture, all 5 dimensions (5D) were encapsulated to varying degrees in the standardized AAS: Physical Entity and Virtual Entity, Services, Digital Twin Data, and Connections (Fig. 1), which acted as assets in the AAS.

The present work presents a detailed design of the 5D-DT-I4.0 prototype, an implementation methodology using the latest available digital technologies, and a practical verification of the prototype on a Filling&Packaging use case. The prototype exclusively uses the standardized communication interfaces REST API and OPC UA. Due to the complexity of the solution, the detailed design has been decomposed into parts that correspond to the structure of the DT prototype. In each part, original and innovative solutions are used to support the main goal that has been achieved as a functional DT for I4.0.

The Physical entity was created as an emulation of a packaging machine in production. The original machine emulator was implemented as an OPC UA server28 whose address space contains anonymized data obtained from real production. The goal is to monitor machine performance and optimize OEE by minimizing unplanned stoppages. The work includes an inspiring procedure to acquire and preprocess the production data, taking into account the manufacturer’s constraints present, up to emulation and integration into the 5D-DT-I4.0 prototype architecture.

The Virtual entity includes digital descriptions of two assets: DT_L1_CubePacker01 as the description of the DTs capabilities to simulate and predict machine failures (packers), and access historical data, and SharedDomainLogic as a description of the shared service call interfaces. The created AAS for DT_L1_CubePacker01 contains mathematical models for simulation and prediction (SimulationModel, PredictionModel) created in MATLAB environment. The paper presents the methodology for creating these functional data-driven models from a dataset derived from real production (samples per 5 days).

The three new elements AAS Repository, Interoperability Middleware, DT Brain and the internal relationships between them represent added value to the 5D-DT-I4.0 architecture. The role of the AAS Repository is to make AAS data available through standardized REST APIs. The Interoperability Middleware software layer has been implemented in the DT prototype to provide the conversion of application-specific services to and from the standardized I4.0 form. An original feature of the prototype is the separation of the business logic (DT Brain) that drives the execution of the DT functionality and capabilities from the domain logic implemented as stateless services (stand-alone services) that can be shared among multiple DTs.

The implemented prototype 5D-DT-I4.0 has been experimentally proven and is functional in all its complexity. Anonymized production data (from Nestlé Carpathia) from a 3-day period were used to validate the proposed architecture, which were emulated through the machine emulator and compared with the predicted value from the DT’s Virtual Entity.

The conclusion of the paper offers a generalization of both the architecture and the methodology of creating DT for Industry 4.0. The standardization and service-oriented architecture, where the digital twins’ technical functionalities are implemented as stateless services, allow to create the basis for a framework with an open, flexible, and scalable architecture for a wide set of use cases and disciplines extensible for the introduction of peer-to-peer information exchange between AAS27. The combination of these approaches and their refinement in the proposed methodology as well as the modification of the 5D DT architecture and the separation of DT business logic as DT Brain and the introduction of domain logic as stateless services are the main and innovative contributions of this article. Significant emphasis is also placed on the detailed elaboration of the prototyping process, which is a synthesis of requirements, starting points, assumptions, limitations and available technologies, which results in the specification of the design, the description of the implementation and the final verification of the solution by simulating real data from production. The detailed elaboration provides important information on current trends, but also on specific pitfalls or obstacles in the implementation of DT.

The paper also looks prospectively into the future by discussing the impact of standardization efforts on the overall complexity of such a solution and the amount of computation required to standardize data for open, standardized communication within I4.0. Also, the resulting 5D-DT-I4.0 framework, due to its flexibility and openness, can form the basis for trans-disciplinary problem solving, which fits well with the concept of Industry 5.0. In the future, efforts will be made to extend 5D-DT-I4.0 with edge devices, cloud integration, implementation of the I4.0 Language as well as deployment in real production environments.

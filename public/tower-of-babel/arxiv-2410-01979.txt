Guanghui Lan, Tianjiao Li

Abstract
Line search procedures are often employed in primal-dual methods for bilinear saddle point problems, especially when the norm of the linear operator is large or difficult to compute. In this paper, we demonstrate that line search is unnecessary by introducing a novel primal-dual method, the auto-conditioned primal-dual hybrid gradient (AC-PDHG) method, which achieves optimal complexity for solving bilinear saddle point problems. AC-PDHG is fully adaptive to the linear operator, using only past iterates to estimate its norm. We further tailor AC-PDHG to solve linearly constrained problems, providing convergence guarantees for both the optimality gap and constraint violation. Moreover, we explore an important class of linearly constrained problems where both the objective and constraints decompose into two parts. By incorporating the design principles of AC-PDHG into the preconditioned alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM), we propose the auto-conditioned alternating direction method of multipliers (AC-ADMM), which guarantees convergence based solely on one part of the constraint matrix and fully adapts to it, eliminating the need for line search. Finally, we extend both AC-PDHG and AC-ADMM to solve bilinear problems with an additional smooth term. By integrating these methods with a novel acceleration scheme, we attain optimal iteration complexities under the single-oracle setting.



1
Introduction

The basic problem of interest in this paper is the bilinear saddle point problem:

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X}\max_{y\in Y}f(x)+\langle Ax,y\rangle-g(y),

(1)

where
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
are closed convex sets,
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
denotes a linear mapping from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and
f:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
g:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are proper, lower semicontinuous, and convex functions.
We assume there exists a pair of saddle point
z^{*}:=(x^{*},y^{*})\in Z:=X\times Y
for problem (
1
). In particular, when
Y=\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
g^{*}
is the Fenchel conjugate of
g
, problem (
1
) can be equivalently written as

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X}f(x)+g^{*}(Ax).

(2)

Another important class of problems that can be solved through (
1
) is the linearly constrained problem:

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X}\left\{f(x):Ax=b\right\},

(3)

which can be reduced to (
1
)
by using Lagrange duality.
We also consider a special case of the above linearly constrained problem:

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X,w\in W}\left\{F(x)+G(y):Bw-Kx=b\right\},

(4)

where
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
and
W\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}
are closed convex sets, and
B\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n_{1}}
and
K\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n_{2}}
are linear operators. Clearly, when
B=I
and
b=0
, this problem is also equivalent to the composite problem (
2
).

Problems (
1
)-(
4
) have a broad range of applications in the field of signal and image processing, machine learning, and statistics; see., e.g.,
[
42
,
43
,
12
,
13
,
47
,
44
,
3
]
and references therein. Driven by these significant real-world applications, there has been considerable interest in the development of computationally efficient methods for bilinear saddle point and linearly constrained problems.

1.1
Smoothing scheme and primal-dual methods for (
1
)

In a seminar work
[
34
]
, Nesterov introduced a smoothing scheme by subtracting a strongly convex regularizer
\mu_{d}h(y)
in (
1
), leading to the formulation

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X}f(x)+H_{\mu_{d}}(x),\text{ where }H_{\mu_{d}}(x):=\max_{y\in Y}\langle Ax,y\rangle-g(y)-\mu_{d}h(y).

(5)

Then
H_{\mu_{d}}(x)
becomes a smooth function with
\tfrac{\|A\|^{2}}{\mu_{d}}
-Lipschitz continuous gradient. Assuming
f
is relatively simple, by choosing an appropriate
\mu_{d}
and applying a (composite) accelerated gradient method
[
32
]
, the overall iteration complexity can be bounded by

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{\|A\|D_{X}D_{Y}}{\epsilon}\right),

(6)

where
D_{X}
and
D_{Y}
are the diameters of the compact sets
X
and
Y
. This complexity bound is optimal, as supported by lower bounds in
[
31
,
30
]
. Nesterov’s smoothing scheme has been extensively studied in the literature (e.g.,
[
33
,
1
,
20
,
39
,
45
,
17
,
25
]
).

In contrast to solving (
1
) by minimizing the smooth approximation function, primal-dual methods work directly with the saddle-point formulation. In their celebrated work
[
4
]
, Chambolle and Pock introduced the primal-dual algorithm (PDA), also known as the primal-dual hybrid gradient (PDHG) method, which achieves the same optimal complexity (
6
) but with a stronger criterion based on the primal-dual gap function. When applied to the linearly constrained problem (
3
), this method can provide guarantees on both the optimality gap and constraint violation. Further developments of PDHG and its variants can be found in
[
40
,
10
,
7
,
24
]
. Notably, Chen et al.
[
7
]
considered the case when
f
is smooth and introduced an accelerated variant of PDHG with optimal iteration complexity.

Additionally, problem (
1
) can also be tackled using algorithms for monotone variational inequalities (VIs), e.g.,
[
14
,
30
,
28
,
26
,
15
,
16
]
. Similar to the primal-dual methods mentioned above, VI algorithms, when applied to (
1
), also perform updates in both the primal and dual spaces at each iteration. However, they can at most achieve the complexity of
\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{\|A\|(D_{X}^{2}+D_{Y}^{2})}{\epsilon}\right)
, which has a worse dependence on the diameters compared to that for the optimal bound (
6
).

1.2
Alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) for (
4
)

Now we switch our attention to problem (
4
), which, by the method of Lagrangian multipliers, can be reformulated as the following saddle-point problem

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X,w\in W}\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}F(x)+G(w)+\langle Kx-Bw+b,y\rangle.

(7)

This problem can also be solved using primal-dual methods like PDHG, but their convergence rate will depend on
\|[K,-B]\|
. Alternatively, one can further penalize the constraints by considering the augmented Lagrangian formulation

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X,w\in W}\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}F(x)+G(w)+\langle Kx-Bw+b,y\rangle+\tfrac{\rho}{2}\|Kx-Bw+b\|^{2},

(8)

where
\rho
is a penalty parameter. The idea of this augmented Lagrangian method (ALM) originated from Hestenes
[
11
]
and Powell
[
41
]
, and is also known as the method of multipliers; see textbooks
[
2
,
35
]
. One influential variant of ALM is the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM)
[
8
,
9
]
, which updates
x_{t}
and
w_{t}
alternatively, followed by an update of the Lagrangian multiplier
y_{t}
in each iteration,

\displaystyle x_{t}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{x\in X}F(x)+\langle Kx-Bw_{t-1}+b,y_{t-1}\rangle+\tfrac{\rho}{2}\|Kx-Bw_{t-1}+b\|^{2},

(9)

\displaystyle w_{t}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{w\in W}G(w)+\langle Kx_{t}-Bw+b,y_{t-1}\rangle+\tfrac{\rho}{2}\|Kx_{2}-Bw+b\|^{2},

(10)

\displaystyle y_{t}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{w\in W}y_{t-1}-\rho(Bw_{t}-Kx_{t}-b).

(11)

Clearly, ADMM involves more complicated subproblems in steps (
9
) and (
10
) compared to primal-dual methods like PDHG. However, several interesting studies
[
10
,
29
,
36
,
19
]
have shown that the convergence rate of ADMM depends only on one part of the constraint mapping, either
B
or
K
.

Meanwhile, one can further reduce the per-iteration cost of ADMM by linearizing the term
\|Kx-Bw_{t-1}+b\|^{2}
in step (
9
), resulting in the following update:

\displaystyle x_{t}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{x\in X}F(x)+\langle Kx-Bw_{t-1}+b,y_{t-1}\rangle+\rho\langle Kx_{t-1}-Bw_{t-1}+b,Kx\rangle+\tfrac{\eta}{2}\|x-x_{t-1}\|^{2}.

(12)

This variant is known as preconditioned ADMM (P-ADMM), and reduces to the primal-dual method
[
4
]
when
B=I
. Notably, even when
B\neq I
, the convergence rate of P-ADMM depends only on one part of the constraint,
K
(see, e.g.,
[
10
,
37
]
), but the stepsize parameter
\eta
needs to be chosen based on
\|K\|
. Additionally, when
F
is not a simple function, one may also linearize it in the subproblem (
9
), leading to the linearized ADMM (L-ADMM)
[
48
,
49
,
6
,
37
]
.

1.3
Adaptive methods for bilinear saddle point problems

It is important to note that, when implementing all these first-order methods mentioned above, we often need to estimate many problem parameters. Let us start with the primal-dual method
[
4
]
for solving (
1
). in order to achieve the optimal complexity (
6
), one needs to determine the stepsizes of the algorithm based on the problem parameters
\|A\|
,
D_{X}
, and
D_{Y}
. These constants can be unavailable or difficult to compute. For example, it is time-consuming to estimate
\|A\|
when
A
is a large-scale dense matrix. Additionally, even when the norm
\|A\|
is computable, relying on this global constant can lead to overly conservative stepsize choices, and consequently slow down the convergence of the algorithm. Similar issues arise when implementing the P-ADMM or L-ADMM type algorithms for solving problem (
4
), especially when
\|K\|
is hard to estimate.

To alleviate these concerns, there has been growing interest in the development of adaptive methods for bilinear saddle-point problems.
One notable research direction is to incorporate line search strategies into primal-dual and ADMM algorithms.
Specifically, Ouyang et al.
[
37
]
introduced line search strategies in P-ADMM and L-ADMM for solving (
4
).
Malitsky and Pock
[
27
]
proposed a line search for the primal-dual method
[
4
]
and established the convergence guarantees in terms of the primal-dual gap of (
1
). However, their complexity
\mathcal{O}(\tfrac{\|A\|(D_{X}^{2}+D_{Y}^{2})}{\epsilon})
exhibits sub-optimal dependence on
D_{X}
and
D_{Y}
. Meanwhile, although the line search procedure only involves matrix-vector multiplications, it can still be costly in practical implementations. In an effort to reduce the per-iteration cost, Malitsky
[
26
]
proposed the line search free golden ratio method for monotone variational inequalities. However, when applied to problem (
1
), the method still suffers from sub-optimal dependence on
D_{X}
and
D_{Y}
. Moreover, algorithms designed for VIs are generally less efficient than primal-dual methods when applied to bilinear saddle point problems. By incorporating the design ideas from both the golden ratio method
[
26
]
and the primal-dual algorithm
[
4
]
, Chang et al.
[
5
]
proposed the golden ratio primal-dual algorithm (GRPDA) to solve a linearly constrained problem equivalent to (
2
) with guarantees measured by optimality gap and constraint violation.
However, GRPDA still requires a line search subroutine in each iteration.
Vladarean et al.
[
46
]
studied problem (
1
) in the setting where
f
is (locally) smooth and proposed a line search free stepsize rule. However, the algorithm is not adaptive to
\|A\|
and the rate remains sub-optimal with respect to both the Lipschitz constant of
\nabla f
and the dependence on
D_{X}
and
D_{Y}
.

In a recent work
[
23
]
, we proposed an auto-conditioned fast gradient method (AC-FGM) and showed that it is uniformly optimal for smooth, weakly smooth, and nonsmooth convex optimization problems. The term “auto-conditioned” refers to the property that the method can automatically adapt to the problem parameters, such as the Lipschitz constant
L
, without the employment of line search procedures. This development, along with the existing literature on bilinear saddle point problems, motivates us to seek answers for the following questions:

Is there an optimal, “auto-conditioned” primal-dual method for solving (
1
) that can adapt to
\|A\|
without resorting to line search? And what about an “auto-conditioned” P-ADMM type algorithm for solving (
4
)?

1.4
Contributions and organization

We make three distinct contributions toward answering the above questions.

Firstly, we introduce a novel primal-dual algorithm, called
auto-conditioned primal-dual hybrid gradient method (AC-PDHG)
for solving the bilinear saddle point problem (
1
). The major novelty of AC-PDHG lies in two aspects. First, to achieve the optimal rate (
6
), AC-PDHG incorporates the idea of Nesterov’s smoothing scheme, but still maintains a primal-dual update structure. Second, for the updates of the primal variable
x_{t}
, AC-PDHG maintains an additional intertwined sequence of search points
\{\bar{x}_{t}\}
to serve as the “prox-centers”. This design idea is inspired by the golden ratio method for VIs
[
26
]
and the auto-conditioned fast gradient method for convex optimization
[
23
]
. Combining these two design ideas, AC-PDHG achieves the optimal rate (
6
) for the primal-dual gap function, with a line search free stepsize policy fully relies on the local estimates of
\|A\|
. The only input parameters required by AC-PDHG are the diameter of the dual feasible region
D_{Y}
and the desired accuracy
\epsilon
. Additionally, we extend AC-PDHG to solve the linearly constrained problem (
3
), establishing optimal
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1})
complexity bounds for attaining a solution with
\epsilon
-optimality gap and
\epsilon
-constrained violation. Notably, when the primal feasible region is bounded and the diameter
D_{X}
is known, the method can be implemented without the estimation of
\|y^{*}\|
.

Secondly, we propose a novel P-ADMM type algorithm, called
auto-conditioned ADMM (AC-ADMM)
for solving the linearly constrained problem (
4
). Similar to AC-PDHG, AC-ADMM incorporates the idea from the smoothing scheme while maintaining alternative updates of the three variables
x_{t}
,
w_{t}
, and
y_{t}
in each iteration. Specifically, the update rule for the primal variable
x_{t}
is similar to (
12
), but with the prox-center
x_{t}
replaced by
\bar{x}_{t}
, following the same design principle as AC-PDHG. The updates for the other primal variable
w_{t}
involve the solution of a more complicated subproblem, like (
10
). However, at the price of the more complicated subproblem, AC-ADMM guarantees convergence in terms of the optimality gap and constraint violation, with dependence only on the matrix
K
, not
B
. Meanwhile, similar to AC-PDHG, AC-ADMM features a line search free stepsize policy that is fully adaptive to local estimates of
\|K\|
.

Thirdly, we extend AC-PDHG (and AC-ADMM) to the settings when
f
(and
F
) are not prox-friendly but
L_{f}
-smooth (and
L_{F}
-smooth) functions.
Specifically, we integrate AC-PDHG with the optimal, line search free AC-FGM algorithm
[
23
]
proposed for smooth convex optimization. A key feature here is instead of linearizing the function
f
at the search point
x_{t}
, we linearize it at another intertwined sequence
\widetilde{x}_{t}
. Our approach achieves the same complexity bound as
[
7
]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{L_{f}D_{X}^{2}}{\epsilon}}+\tfrac{\|A\|D_{X}D_{Y}}{\epsilon}\right),

which is optimal in the single-oracle setting as supported by the lower bound in
[
38
]
.
Here single-oracle model means for each search
(x,y)
, the first-order oracle returns
(\nabla f(x),Ax,A^{T}y)
.
Meanwhile, the stepsize policy remains line search free and relies only on the local estimates of
L_{f}
and
\|A\|
. We extend AC-ADMM to solve (
4
) with a smooth
F
in a similar manner.

The remaining sections of the paper are organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce AC-PDHG for solving the bilinear saddle point problem (
1
) and the linearly constrained problem (
3
). In Section
3
, we propose AC-ADMM for solving problem (
4
). Finally, in Section
4
, we extend AC-PDHG and AC-ADMM to solve problems where the functions
f
and
F
are smooth.

2
An adaptive primal-dual hybrid gradient (PDHG) method

In this section, we consider solving problem (
1
) in the simple but important setting, where both functions
f
and
g
are “prox-friendly”. This means that the proximal-mapping problems

\displaystyle\arg\min_{x\in X}\big\{\langle\xi,x\rangle+f(x)+\tfrac{\eta}{2}\|x^{\prime}-x\|\big\},\text{ where }\eta>0,~\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},~x^{\prime}\in X,

(13)

\displaystyle\arg\min_{y\in Y}\big\{\langle\xi^{\prime},y\rangle+g(y)+\tfrac{\eta^{\prime}}{2}\|y^{\prime}-y\|\big\},\text{ where }\eta^{\prime}>0,~\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},~y^{\prime}\in Y,

(14)

can be easily solved, either in closed form or through some efficient computational procedures. For convenience, we denote
Z:=X\times Y
. For two primal-dual pairs
z:=(x,y),\bar{z}:=(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in Z
, we define the primal-dual gap function as

\displaystyle Q(\bar{z},z):=f(\bar{x})+\langle A\bar{x},y\rangle-g(y)-\left[f(x)+\langle Ax,\bar{y}\rangle-g(\bar{y})\right].

(15)

Note that by definition
z^{*}=(x^{*},y^{*})\in Z
is a saddle point of Ineq. (
1
) if and only if for any
z\in Z
,

\displaystyle f(x^{*})+\langle Ax^{*},y\rangle-g(y)\leq f(x^{*})+\langle Ax^{*},y^{*}\rangle-g(y^{*})\leq f(x)+\langle Ax,y^{*}\rangle-g(y^{*}).

It then follows that
z^{*}
is a saddle point of Ineq. (
1
) if and only if
Q(z^{*},z)\leq 0
for any
z\in Z
. Consequently,
\max_{z\in Z}Q(\bar{z},z)
is a valid stopping criterion for the saddle point problem (
1
) when
Z
is a bounded set.

Next, we introduce the Auto-Conditioned Primal-Dual Hybrid Gradient Method (AC-PDHG). AC-PDHG involves both primal and dual updates in each iteration. In the primal steps, two sequences of search points are maintained, i.e.,
\{x_{t}\}
, and
\{\bar{x}_{t}\}
. The sequence
\{x_{t}\}
is the output of the prox-mapping subproblem (
17
), while the sequence
\{\bar{x}_{t}\}
is a moving average of
\{x_{t}\}
and serves as the prox-centers. This idea was first introduced in the golden ratio method
[
26
]
for variational inequalities, and later adopted in the auto-conditioned fast gradient method (AC-FGM)
[
23
]
for convex optimization. For the dual variables, we update
\{y_{t}\}
through another prox-mapping step (
19
), which includes an additional regularization term
\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|\widetilde{y}_{0}-y\|^{2}
. These new features in both primal and dual updates distinguish the AC-PDHG method from the classical PDHG method
[
4
]
, and play a critical role in achieving the optimal rate for solving the bilinear saddle point problem (
1
) without resorting to line search.

Algorithm 1

Auto-Conditioned Primal-Dual Hybrid Gradient Method (AC-PDHG)

Input
: initial point
x_{0}=\bar{x}_{0}\in X
,
\widetilde{y}_{0}\in Y
, nonnegative parameters
\beta_{t}\in(0,1)
,
\eta_{t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\tau_{t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and
\mu_{d}>0
.
Let

\displaystyle y_{0}=\arg\min_{y\in Y}\left\{\langle-Ax_{0},y\rangle+g(y)+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|\widetilde{y}_{0}-y\|^{2}\right\}.

(16)

for

t=1,\cdots,k

do

\displaystyle x_{t}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{x\in X}\left\{\eta_{t}[\langle A^{\top}y_{t-1},x\rangle+f(x)]+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\bar{x}_{t-1}-x\|^{2}\right\},

(17)

\displaystyle\bar{x}_{t}

\displaystyle=(1-\beta_{t})\bar{x}_{t-1}+\beta_{t}x_{t},

(18)

\displaystyle y_{t}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{y\in Y}\left\{\langle-Ax_{t},y\rangle+g(y)+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|\widetilde{y}_{0}-y\|^{2}+\tfrac{\tau_{t}}{2}\|y_{t-1}-y\|^{2}\right\}.

(19)

end

for

Our goal is to ensure that AC-PDHG can be implemented without the computation or estimation of the global parameter
\|A\|
. Instead, we compute a local estimate of
\|A\|
at each iteration, defined as

\displaystyle L_{A,t}:=\tfrac{\|A^{\top}(y_{t}-y_{t-1})\|}{\|y_{t}-y_{t-1}\|}.

(20)

Then we use the local estimates obtained in the past iterations to determine the algorithm parameters, e.g.,
\eta_{t}
, and
\tau_{t}
.
In the next two subsections, we propose the stepsize policy for AC-PDHG and establish its convergence guarantees for solving the bilinear saddle point problem and the linearly constrained problem stated in (
1
) and (
3
) respectively.

2.1
AC-PDHG for bilinear saddle point problems

To establish the convergence guarantees for Algorithm
1
, we first present the following lemma which characterizes the optimality condition of step (
19
), and an important relationship between the primal and dual variables in the first iteration.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\{x_{t}\},\{\bar{x}_{t}\}
and
\{y_{t}\}
be generated by Algorithm
1
, we have for
t\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\langle-Ax_{t},y_{t}-y\rangle+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|\widetilde{y}_{0}-y_{t}\|^{2}-\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|\widetilde{y}_{0}-y\|^{2}+g(y_{t})-g(y)+\tfrac{\mu_{d}+\tau_{t}}{2}\|y_{t}-y\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{\tau_{t}}{2}\|y_{t-1}-y\|^{2}-\tfrac{\tau_{t}}{2}\|y_{t-1}-y_{t}\|^{2}.

(21)

Moreover, if
\tau_{1}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\|y_{1}-y_{0}\|\leq\tfrac{L_{A,1}}{\mu_{d}}\|x_{1}-x_{0}\|.

(22)

Proof.

First, Ineq. (
21
) is known as the “three-point” lemma, which follows from the optimality condition of (
19
); see a formal proof in Lemma 3.5 of
[
19
]
. Applying Ineq. (
21
) to the case
t=1
, noting that
\tau_{1}=0
, and setting
y=y_{0}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle-Ax_{1},y_{1}-y_{0}\rangle+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|\widetilde{y}_{0}-y_{1}\|^{2}-\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|\widetilde{y}_{0}-y_{0}\|^{2}+g(y_{1})-g(y_{0})+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|y_{1}-y_{0}\|^{2}\leq 0.

Similarly, applying Ineq. (
21
) to the case
t=0
with
\tau_{0}=0
, and setting
y=y_{0}
give us

\displaystyle\langle-Ax_{0},y_{0}-y_{1}\rangle+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|\widetilde{y}_{0}-y_{0}\|^{2}-\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|\widetilde{y}_{0}-y_{1}\|^{2}+g(y_{0})-g(y_{1})+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|y_{0}-y_{1}\|^{2}\leq 0.

By summing up the above two inequalities, rearranging the terms, and using the definition of
L_{A,1}
in (
20
), we have

\displaystyle\langle A^{\top}(y_{1}-y_{0}),x_{1}-x_{0}\rangle\geq\mu_{d}\|y_{1}-y_{0}\|^{2}=\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{L_{A,1}^{2}}\|A^{\top}(y_{1}-y_{0})\|^{2}.

Then using Young’s inequality provides us with

\displaystyle\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{L_{A,1}^{2}}\|A^{\top}(y_{1}-y_{0})\|^{2}\leq\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2L^{2}_{A,1}}\|A^{\top}(y_{1}-y_{0})\|^{2}+\tfrac{L_{A,1}^{2}}{2\mu_{d}}\|x_{1}-x_{0}\|^{2},

from which our results follow by rearranging the terms and further applying the definition of
L_{A,1}
.
∎

) then follows by utilizing these lower bounds on
\eta_{t}
and
\tau_{t}
in Ineq. (
42
) in Theorem
3
.
Now using the condition
\eta_{2}\leq\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{4L_{A,1}^{2}}
in Ineq. (
48
), we have

\displaystyle Q(\widehat{z}_{k},z)

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{12\widehat{L}^{2}_{A,k}}{\mu_{d}(6k+\alpha k(k-3))}\left[\tfrac{\|x_{0}-x\|^{2}}{\beta}+\tfrac{5}{8}\|x_{1}-x_{0}\|^{2}\right]+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|\widetilde{y}_{0}-y\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{12\widehat{L}^{2}_{A,k}}{\mu_{d}(6k+\alpha k(k-3))}\left(\tfrac{1}{\beta}+\tfrac{5}{8}\right)D_{X}^{2}+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}D_{Y}^{2},

which implies Ineq. (
49
) after taking the maximum over
z\in Z
.
∎

A few comments on the stepsize policy and convergence guarantees in Corollary
4
are in order.

Firstly, observe that the stepsize policy in Corollary
4
does not require
any line search procedures. Specifically, the stepsize
\eta_{t}
is selected based on
\eta_{t-1}
and the local estimator
L_{A,t-1}
computed from the last iterates
y_{t-1}
and
y_{t-2}
(see (
20
)),
while the parameter
\tau_{t}
is determined by
\tau_{t-1}
,
L_{A,t-1}
, the recently used stepsize
\eta_{t}
,
and a hyper-parameter
\alpha\in(0,1]
.
It is worth adding some explanation about the role of the hyper-parameter
\alpha
in the specification of
\tau_{t}
in (
46
). In fact, one might simply set
\alpha=1
, and in this case, the stepsize policy would reduce to

\tau_{t}=\tfrac{t\mu_{d}}{2}\ \mbox{and}\ \eta_{t}=\min\left\{\tfrac{t}{t-1}\eta_{t-1},\tfrac{(t-1)\mu_{d}}{8L^{2}_{A,t-1}}\right\}.

In view of Ineq. (
48
), this choice of
\alpha
provides the best possible theoretical convergence guarantee.
However, such a selection of
\alpha
might result in overly conservative stepsizes for the implementation of AC-PDHG. To illustrate this observation, let us consider an extreme scenario when
L_{A,1}=\|A\|
. In this situation, no matter how small the subsequent local estimators
L_{A,t}
are, the stepsize
\eta_{t}
will be determined by the global constant
\|A\|
, thus preventing the algorithm from utilizing these local estimators.
To address this issue, we suggest to choose a smaller
\alpha\in(0,1)
to make AC-PDHG more adaptive to the local structure. Specifically, the last term in the
definition of
\tau_{t}
in (
46
) depends on the ratio between
\eta_{t}
and
\tau_{t-1}/(4L^{2}_{A,t-1})
. If
\eta_{t}<\tau_{t-1}/(4L^{2}_{A,t-1})
in (
45
), the current selection of
\eta_{t}
might be too conservative
since it is not determined by the current local estimate
L_{A,t-1}
. In response,
\tau_{t}
in (
46
) will grow slower compared to the case when
\alpha=1
, potentially allowing the stepsize
\eta_{t}
to increase faster in subsequent iterations. By contrast, for the relatively more aggressive selection of
\eta_{t}=\tau_{t-1}/(4L^{2}_{A,t-1})
in (
45
),
\tau_{t}
will be updated by
\tau_{t}=\tau_{t-1}+\mu_{d}/2
, same as the case
\alpha=1
, thereby potentially slowing down the growth of the stepsizes in subsequent iterations. In summary, the introduction of
\alpha
allows for a more adaptive stepsize rule while still maintaining the optimal convergence rate.

Secondly, we would like to discuss the choice of the initial stepsize
\eta_{1}
. Conceptually speaking, AC-PDHG can always converge regardless of the specification of
\eta_{1}
. However, if
\eta_{1}
is too small,
the estimated Lipschtiz constant
\widehat{L}_{A,t}
in (
47
) could be dominated by
the first term
\sqrt{\mu_{d}/(4(1-\beta)\eta_{1})}
rather
than the desired
\|A\|
, which may slow down the convergence of the algorithm as one can see from (
48
). To ensure
\widehat{L}_{A,t}\leq\mathcal{O}(\|A\|)
, we should choose an
\eta_{1}
such that
\eta_{1}^{-1}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\|A\|^{2}/\mu_{d}\right)
. When
X
is a bounded set, we can ignore the last term
-\eta_{2}\|x_{1}-x_{0}\|^{2}/\eta_{1}
in the right hand side of (
48
) and set

\displaystyle\eta_{1}=\tfrac{\zeta\mu_{d}}{4(1-\beta)L^{2}_{A,0}},\text{ where }L_{A,0}:=\tfrac{\|A^{\top}(\widetilde{y}_{0}-y_{0})\|}{\|\widetilde{y}_{0}-y_{0}\|},~\zeta>0

(53)

so that
\eta_{1}^{-1}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\|A\|^{2}/\mu_{d}\right)
.
However, when
X
is unbounded, we need to ensure
\eta_{1}\leq 2\mu_{d}/(5L_{A,1}^{2})
in order to remove the last two terms
\eta_{2}\big(5L^{2}_{A,1}/(2\mu_{d})-1/\eta_{1}\big)\|x_{1}-x_{0}\|^{2}
from the right hand side of Ineq. (
48
).
One strategy to find such a stepsize is to perform a simple line search in the first iteration. Taking the stepsize in (
53
) as the starting point, this line search will be terminated in at most
\mathcal{O}(\log(\|A\|/L_{A,0}))
steps.

Thirdly,
we can easily derive
the iteration complexity of AC-PDHG from Ineq. (
49
) for the simpler case where both
X
and
Y
are bounded. More specifically, to obtain an
\epsilon
-optimal solution of
problem (
1
), i.e., a point
\bar{z}=(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in Z
such that
\max_{z\in Z}Q(\bar{z},z)\leq\epsilon
, we need at most

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{\|A\|^{2}D_{X}^{2}}{\mu_{d}\epsilon}}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{\|A\|D_{X}D_{Y}}{\epsilon}\right)

number of AC-PDHG iterations by setting
\mu_{d}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon/D_{Y}^{2})
. It is well-known that this iteration complexity is optimal for solving a general bilinear saddle point problem. Notably, this optimal complexity is achieved by AC-PDHG without requiring any prior knowledge of
\|A\|
or
D_{X}
.

Finally, we show how to specialize the convergence guarantees for PDHG obtained in Corollary
4
for the case when both
X
and
Y
are unbounded. For simplicity, we assume that the initial stepsize
\eta_{1}
satisfies
\eta_{1}^{-1}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\|A\|^{2}/\mu_{d}\right)
and
\eta_{1}\leq 2\mu_{d}/(5L_{A,1}^{2})
. Then combining Ineq. (
48
) with the fact that
\|x_{0}-x\|^{2}-\|\bar{x}_{k+1}-x\|^{2}=\|x_{0}\|^{2}-\|\bar{x}_{k+1}\|^{2}-2\langle x_{0}-\bar{x}_{k+1},x\rangle
and
\|\widetilde{y}_{0}-y\|^{2}-\|\widetilde{y}_{k}-y\|^{2}=\|\widetilde{y}_{0}\|^{2}-\|\widetilde{y}_{k}\|^{2}-2\langle\widetilde{y}_{0}-\widetilde{y}_{k},y\rangle
, we have

\displaystyle\max_{z\in Z}[Q(\widehat{z}_{k},z)+\langle\delta_{x,k},x\rangle+\langle\delta_{y,k},y\rangle]\leq\tfrac{12\widehat{L}^{2}_{A,k}}{\beta\mu_{d}(6k+\alpha k(k-3))}\|x_{0}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|\widetilde{y}_{0}\|^{2},

(54)

where

\delta_{x,k}:=\tfrac{24\widehat{L}^{2}_{A,k}}{\mu_{d}(6k+\alpha k(k-3))}(\bar{x}_{k+1}-x_{0})\ \mbox{and}\ \delta_{y,k}:=\mu_{d}(\widetilde{y}_{0}-\widetilde{y}_{k}).

Now we prove the convergence of
\|\delta_{x,k}\|
and
\|\delta_{y,k}\|
. First, by taking
z=z^{*}
in Ineq. (
48
) and using
\|\widetilde{y}_{k}-\widetilde{y}_{0}\|^{2}\leq 2\|\widetilde{y}_{k}-y^{*}\|^{2}+2\|y^{*}-\widetilde{y}_{0}\|^{2}
,
Q(\widehat{z}_{k},z^{*})\geq 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mu_{d}\|\widetilde{y}_{k}-\widetilde{y}_{0}\|^{2}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{\widehat{L}^{2}_{A,k}}{\mu_{d}k^{2}}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\mu_{d}\|\widetilde{y}_{0}-y^{*}\|^{2}\right).

Similarly, using
\|\bar{x}_{k+1}-x_{0}\|^{2}\leq 2\|\bar{x}_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}+2\|x^{*}-x_{0}\|^{2}
in Ineq. (
48
), we have

\displaystyle\tfrac{\widehat{L}^{2}_{A,k}}{\mu_{d}k^{2}}\|\bar{x}_{k+1}-x_{0}\|^{2}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{\widehat{L}^{2}_{A,k}}{\mu_{d}k^{2}}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\mu_{d}\|\widetilde{y}_{0}-y^{*}\|^{2}\right).

Let us denote
D_{x^{*}}:=\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|
and
D_{y^{*}}:=\|\widetilde{y}_{0}-y^{*}\|
.
We then conclude from the above three relations that
by setting
\mu_{d}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon/D_{y^{*}}^{2})
,

\displaystyle\|\delta_{y,k}\|

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{\widehat{L}_{A,k}\sqrt{\mu_{d}}}{k}D_{x^{*}}+\mu_{d}D_{y^{*}}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{\epsilon}{D_{y^{*}}}\right),

\displaystyle\|\delta_{x,k}\|

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{\widehat{L}^{2}_{A,k}}{\mu_{d}k^{2}}D_{x^{*}}+\tfrac{\widehat{L}_{A,k}D_{y^{*}}}{k}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{\epsilon}{D_{x^{*}}}\right)

for any
k\geq\mathcal{O}(\|A\|D_{x^{*}}D_{y^{*}}/\epsilon)
.
Therefore, we obtain a convergence guarantee for AC-PDHG similar to the case when
X
and
Y
are bounded, but with a slightly different stopping criterion.

2.2
AC-PDHG for linearly constrained problems

In this subsection, we analyze the convergence of AC-PDHG when applied to the linearly constrained problem (
3
).
This problem can be solved through its Lagrange dual given in the form of

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X}\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}f(x)+\langle Ax-b,y\rangle,

(55)

which is equivalent to (
1
) with
g(y)=\langle b,y\rangle
and
Y=\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Throughout this subsection we assume the existence of
an optimal solution
x^{*}
of (
3
) associated with
y^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
(x^{*},y^{*})
is a saddle point of (
55
).

The following result describes the convergence properties of AC-PDHG for solving the above linearly constrained problem with guarantees in terms of both the optimality gap and constraint violation.

Proposition 5
.

Consider Algorithm
1
applied to problem (
3
) with
\widetilde{y}_{0}=\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and algorithm parameters satisfing the conditions in Theorem
3
.
Then we have

\displaystyle f(\widehat{x}_{k})-f(x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{\sum_{t=1}^{k}\eta_{t}}\left[\tfrac{\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}}{2\beta}+\left(\tfrac{5\eta_{2}L^{2}_{A,1}}{4\mu_{d}}-\tfrac{\eta_{2}}{2\eta_{1}}\right)\|x_{1}-x_{0}\|^{2}\right],

(56)

\displaystyle\|A\widehat{x}_{k}-b\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\mu_{d}\|y^{*}\|+2\sqrt{\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{\sum_{t=1}^{k}\eta_{t}}\left[\tfrac{\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}}{2\beta}+\left(\tfrac{5\eta_{2}L^{2}_{A,1}}{4\mu_{d}}-\tfrac{\eta_{2}}{2\eta_{1}}\right)\|x_{1}-x_{0}\|^{2}\right]},

(57)

where
\widehat{x}_{k}
and
\widehat{y}_{k}
are defined in (
28
) and
\widetilde{y}_{k}
is defined in (
29
).
More specifically, if the stepsize parameters are selected according to Corollary
4
, we have

\displaystyle f(\widehat{x}_{k})-f(x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{12\widehat{L}^{2}_{A,k}}{\mu_{d}(6k+\alpha k(k-3))}\left[\tfrac{\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}}{\beta}+\left(\tfrac{5\eta_{2}L^{2}_{A,1}}{2\mu_{d}}-\tfrac{\eta_{2}}{2\eta_{1}}\right)\|x_{1}-x_{0}\|^{2}\right],

(58)

\displaystyle\|A\widehat{x}_{k}-b\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\mu_{d}\|y^{*}\|+2\sqrt{\tfrac{12\widehat{L}^{2}_{A,k}}{6k+\alpha k(k-3)}\left[\tfrac{\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}}{\beta}+\left(\tfrac{5\eta_{2}L^{2}_{A,1}}{2\mu_{d}}-\tfrac{\eta_{2}}{2\eta_{1}}\right)\|x_{1}-x_{0}\|^{2}\right]},

(59)

where
\widehat{L}_{A,k}
is defined in (
47
).

Proof.

It follows from Ineq. (
42
) in Theorem
3
that

\displaystyle f(\widehat{x}_{k})+\langle A\widehat{x}_{k}-b,y\rangle-\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|y\|^{2}-\left[f(x)+\langle Ax-b,\widehat{y}_{k}\rangle-\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|\widehat{y}_{k}\|^{2}\right]+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|\widetilde{y}_{k}-y\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{\sum_{t=1}^{k}\eta_{t}}\left[\tfrac{\|x_{0}-x\|^{2}}{2\beta}+\left(\tfrac{5\eta_{2}L^{2}_{A,1}}{4\mu_{d}}-\tfrac{\eta_{2}}{2\eta_{1}}\right)\|x_{1}-x_{0}\|^{2}-\tfrac{\|x_{k+1}-\bar{x}_{k}\|^{2}}{4}-\tfrac{\|\bar{x}_{k+1}-x\|^{2}}{2\beta}\right].

(60)

Using
\|\widetilde{y}_{k}-y\|^{2}=\|\widetilde{y}_{k}\|^{2}+\|y\|^{2}-2\langle\widetilde{y}_{k},y\rangle
and rearranging the terms in the previous result yield

\displaystyle f(\widehat{x}_{k})+\langle A\widehat{x}_{k}-b-\mu_{d}\widetilde{y}_{k},y\rangle-\left[f(x)+\langle Ax-b,\widehat{y}_{k}\rangle\right]+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|\widehat{y}_{k}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|\widetilde{y}_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{\sum_{t=1}^{k}\eta_{t}}\left[\tfrac{\|x_{0}-x\|^{2}}{2\beta}+\left(\tfrac{5\eta_{2}L^{2}_{A,1}}{4\mu_{d}}-\tfrac{\eta_{2}}{2\eta_{1}}\right)\|x_{1}-x_{0}\|^{2}-\tfrac{\|x_{k+1}-\bar{x}_{k}\|^{2}}{4}-\tfrac{\|\bar{x}_{k+1}-x\|^{2}}{2\beta}\right].

Taking
x=x^{*}
and noting that
Ax^{*}-b=0
due to
the optimality of
(x^{*},y^{*})
for (
55
), we have

\displaystyle f(\widehat{x}_{k})+\langle A\widehat{x}_{k}-b-\mu_{d}\widetilde{y}_{k},y\rangle-f(x^{*})+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|\widehat{y}_{k}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|\widetilde{y}_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{\sum_{t=1}^{k}\eta_{t}}\left[\tfrac{\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}}{2\beta}+\left(\tfrac{5\eta_{2}L^{2}_{A,1}}{4\mu_{d}}-\tfrac{\eta_{2}}{2\eta_{1}}\right)\|x_{1}-x_{0}\|^{2}\right],

(61)

for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Observe that we must have

\displaystyle A\widehat{x}_{k}-b-\mu_{d}\widetilde{y}_{k}=0,

since otherwise the left hand side of Ineq. (
61
) can be unbounded. Therefore, we conclude from (
61
) and the above observation that

\displaystyle f(\widehat{x}_{k})-f(x^{*})+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|\widehat{y}_{k}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|\widetilde{y}_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{\sum_{t=1}^{k}\eta_{t}}\left[\tfrac{\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}}{2\beta}+\left(\tfrac{5\eta_{2}L^{2}_{A,1}}{4\mu_{d}}-\tfrac{\eta_{2}}{2\eta_{1}}\right)\|x_{1}-x_{0}\|^{2}\right],

(62)

and

\displaystyle\|A\widehat{x}_{k}-b\|=\mu_{d}\|\widetilde{y}_{k}\|,

which, respectively,
provide bounds on the optimality gap and constraint violation. Next, we provide an upper bound for
\|\widetilde{y}_{k}\|
. By taking
(x,y)=(x^{*},y^{*})
in Ineq. (
60
), we have

\displaystyle f(\widehat{x}_{k})+\langle A\widehat{x}_{k}-b,y^{*}\rangle-\left[f(x^{*})+\langle Ax^{*}-b,\widehat{y}_{k}\rangle\right]+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|\widehat{y}_{k}\|^{2}-\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|y^{*}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|\widetilde{y}_{k}-y^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{\sum_{t=1}^{k}\eta_{t}}\left[\tfrac{\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}}{2\beta}+\left(\tfrac{5\eta_{2}L^{2}_{A,1}}{4\mu_{d}}-\tfrac{\eta_{2}}{2\eta_{1}}\right)\|x_{1}-x_{0}\|^{2}\right],

which, in view of the relation
f(\widehat{x}_{k})+\langle A\widehat{x}_{k}-b,y^{*}\rangle\geq f(x^{*})+\langle Ax^{*}-b,\widehat{y}_{k}\rangle
due to

(x^{*},y^{*})
being a pair of saddle point of (
55
), implies that

\displaystyle\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{4}\|\widetilde{y}_{k}\|^{2}-\mu_{d}\|y^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|y^{*}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|\widetilde{y}_{k}-y^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{\sum_{t=1}^{k}\eta_{t}}\left[\tfrac{\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}}{2\beta}+\left(\tfrac{5\eta_{2}L^{2}_{A,1}}{4\mu_{d}}-\tfrac{\eta_{2}}{2\eta_{1}}\right)\|x_{1}-x_{0}\|^{2}\right].

As a consequence, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{d}\|\widetilde{y}_{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{d}\sqrt{4\|y^{*}\|^{2}+\tfrac{4}{\mu_{d}(\sum_{t=1}^{k}\eta_{t})}\left[\tfrac{\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}}{2\beta}+\left(\tfrac{5\eta_{2}L^{2}_{A,1}}{4\mu_{d}}-\tfrac{\eta_{2}}{2\eta_{1}}\right)\|x_{1}-x_{0}\|^{2}\right]}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mu_{d}\|y^{*}\|+2\sqrt{\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{\sum_{t=1}^{k}\eta_{t}}\left[\tfrac{\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}}{2\beta}+\left(\tfrac{5\eta_{2}L^{2}_{A,1}}{4\mu_{d}}-\tfrac{\eta_{2}}{2\eta_{1}}\right)\|x_{1}-x_{0}\|^{2}\right]},

which completes the proof.
∎

Now let us derive from Proposition
5
the complexity of AC-PDHG for solving the linearly constrained problem (
3
)
in order to find an
(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})
-optimal solution, i.e., a point

\bar{x}\in X
such that
f(\bar{x})-f^{*}\leq\epsilon_{1}
and
\|A\bar{x}-b\|\leq\epsilon_{2}
.
For simplicity, we assume the initial stepsize
\eta_{1}
satisfies
\eta_{1}^{-1}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\|A\|^{2}/\mu_{d}\right)
and
\eta_{1}\leq 2\mu_{d}/(5L_{A,1}^{2})
, which can be ensured through a simple line search as previously discussed in the comments
after Corollary
4
.
In this situation, the convergence guarantees in (
58
) and (
59
) can be written as

\displaystyle f(\widehat{x}_{k})-f(x^{*})\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{\widehat{L}_{A,k}^{2}D_{x^{*}}^{2}}{\mu_{d}k^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\|A\widehat{x}_{k}-b\|\leq\mu_{d}\|\widetilde{y}_{k}\|\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\mu_{d}\|y^{*}\|+\tfrac{\widehat{L}_{A,k}D_{x^{*}}}{k}\right),

where
D_{x^{*}}:=\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|
and
D_{y^{*}}:=\|y^{*}\|
.
Thus, taking
\mu_{d}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{2}/D_{y^{*}})
, AC-PDHG can find an
(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})
-optimal solution of (
3
) in at most

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{\|A\|D_{x^{*}}}{\epsilon_{2}}+\tfrac{\|A\|D_{x^{*}}\sqrt{D_{y^{*}}}}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}}}\right)

(63)

iterations.
In particular, for the case when
\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon
and
\epsilon_{2}=\epsilon/D_{y^{*}}
, which corresponds to a higher requirement on the feasibility (as long as
D_{y^{*}}\geq 1
),
the above complexity bound reduces to

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{\|A\|D_{x^{*}}D_{y^{*}}}{\epsilon}\right)

(64)

with
\mu_{d}
chosen as
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon/D^{2}_{y^{*}})
.

It should be noted that
to achieve the above complexity bounds
in (
63
) and (
64
) requires the input of
\mu_{d}
, which necessitates an
estimate
\widehat{D}_{y^{*}}
for the unknown quantity
D_{y^{*}}
.
If this estimate
\widehat{D}_{y^{*}}
is too conservative (i.e.,
\widehat{D}_{y^{*}}\gg D_{y^{*}}
), the convergence of AC-PDHG may be slowed down. Conversely, if the estimate is too aggressive (i.e.,
\widehat{D}_{y^{*}}\ll D_{y^{*}}
), we might fail to achieve the desired accuracy for the constraints. However, when the primal feasible region
X
is bounded (i.e.,
\max_{x,y}\|x-y\|\leq D_{X}
), we can employ a simple “guess and check” procedure, as shown below, to determine
\mu_{d}
without requiring a precise estimation of
D_{y^{*}}
.

A guess and check procedure for AC-PDHG:

Input
: Choose
\widehat{D}^{(0)}
such that
\widehat{D}^{(0)}\leq D_{y^{*}}
. Fix
\epsilon_{1}>0
and
\epsilon_{2}>0
.

for

i=0,1,2,\cdots

do

Let
\widehat{D}^{(i)}:=\widehat{D}^{(0)}\cdot 2^{i},~\mu^{(i)}:=\tfrac{\epsilon_{2}}{4\widehat{D}^{(i)}}
. Run Algorithm
1
with
\mu_{d}=\mu^{(i)}
until we find the first iterate
\widehat{k}\geq 3
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}(\widehat{k})\leq\epsilon_{1}\text{ and }\min\left\{\|A\widehat{x}_{\widehat{k}}-b\|,\mathcal{E}_{2}(\widehat{k})\right\}\leq\epsilon_{2},

\displaystyle\text{where }\mathcal{E}_{1}(k):=\tfrac{12\widehat{L}_{A,k}^{2}D_{X}^{2}}{\mu^{(i)}\beta(6k+\alpha k(k-3))},\text{ }\mathcal{E}_{2}(k):=4\sqrt{\tfrac{12\widehat{L}_{A,k}^{2}D_{X}^{2}}{\beta(6k+\alpha k(k-3))}},\text{ for $\widehat{L}_{A,k}$ defined in \eqref{eta_lower_bound_2}.}

If
\|A\widehat{x}_{\widehat{k}}-b\|\leq\epsilon_{2}
: Terminate.

end

for

Output
:
(\widehat{x},\widehat{w})=(\widehat{x}_{\widehat{k}},\widehat{w}_{\widehat{k}})
,
\widehat{D}_{Y}=\widehat{D}^{(i)}
.

We provide a brief explanation of this procedure. For each
\widehat{D}^{(i)}
, we check whether it is possible to attain the desired optimality gap and constraint violation. On one hand, if
\mathcal{E}_{1}(\widehat{k})\leq\epsilon_{1}
and
\|A\widehat{x}_{\widehat{k}}-b\|\leq\epsilon_{2}
, we can terminate the algorithm with the desired accuracy achieved. On the other hand, if
\mathcal{E}_{1}(\widehat{k})\leq\epsilon_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}(\widehat{k})\leq\epsilon_{2}
, we conclude from (
59
) that
2\mu^{(i)}\|y^{*}\|>\epsilon_{2}/2
, thus
\widehat{D}^{(i)}
should be further enlarged. This guess and check procedure is guaranteed to terminate, and upon termination, we must have
\widehat{D}_{Y}\leq 2D_{y^{*}}
.
As a result, an
(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})
-optimal solution of (
3
) can be found within at most

\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{\|A\|D_{X}}{\epsilon_{2}}\log(\tfrac{D_{y^{*}}}{\widehat{D}^{(0)}})+\tfrac{\|A\|D_{X}\sqrt{D_{y^{*}}}}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}}}\right)

iterations. This procedure does not require the input of any other problem parameters than the diameter of the primal feasible region
D_{X}
as well as the desired accuracy
\epsilon_{1}
and
\epsilon_{2}
.

3
An adaptive alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM)

In this section, we consider an important class of linearly constrained problem in the form of

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X,w\in W}~

\displaystyle F(x)+G(w)

s.t.

\displaystyle Bw-Kx=b.

(65)

where
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
and
W\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}
are closed convex sets,
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
K\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n_{1}}
and
B\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n_{2}}
.

Similar to Section
2
, we solve (
65
) by considering the primal-dual formulation

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X,w\in W}\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}F(x)+G(w)+\langle Kx-Bw+b,y\rangle.

(66)

We assume there exists an optimal solution
(x^{*},w^{*})\in X\times K
for (
65
), along with a dual variable
y^{*}
such that
(x^{*},w^{*},y^{*})\in Z:=X\times K\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a saddle point for problem (
66
).
With a slight abuse of notation, we still define here

\displaystyle Q(\bar{z},z):=

\displaystyle F(\bar{x})+G(\bar{w})+\langle K\bar{x}-B\bar{w}+b,y\rangle-\left[F(x)+G(w)+\langle Kx-Bw+b,\bar{y}\rangle\right],

(67)

where
\bar{z}:=(\bar{x},\bar{w},\bar{y})\in Z
and
z=(x,w,y)\in Z
. It follows from the convex-concave structure of (
66
) that
z^{*}
is a saddle point of (
66
) if and only if
Q(z^{*},z)\leq 0
for any
z\in Z
.

Clearly, we can apply the AC-PDHG method in Algorithm
1
to solve problem (
65
), and the complexity will depend on
\|K\|+\|B\|
. However, if
\|B\|\gg\|K\|
, the convergence of AC-PDHG is dominated by
\|B\|
, no matter how small
\|K\|
is. To address this issue, we propose a preconditioned ADMM-type method whose convergence rate depends solely on
\|K\|
, at the cost of solving a more complicated subproblem in each iteration.

Algorithm 2

Auto-Conditioned Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (AC-ADMM)

Input
: initial point
x_{0}=\bar{x}_{0}\in X
, nonnegative parameters
\beta_{t}\in(0,1)
,
\eta_{t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and
\tau_{t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Let

\displaystyle w_{0}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{w\in W}\left\{G(w)+\tfrac{1}{2\mu_{d}}\|Bw-Kx_{0}-b\|^{2}\right\},

(68)

\displaystyle y_{0}

\displaystyle=(Kx_{0}-Bw_{0}+b)/{\mu_{d}}.

(69)

for

t=1,\cdots,k

do

\displaystyle x_{t}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{x\in X}\left\{\eta_{t}[\langle K^{\top}y_{t-1},x\rangle+F(x)]+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\bar{x}_{t-1}-x\|^{2}\right\},

(70)

\displaystyle\bar{x}_{t}

\displaystyle=(1-\beta_{t})\bar{x}_{t-1}+\beta_{t}x_{t},

(71)

\displaystyle w_{t}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{w\in W}\left\{G(w)+(\tau_{t}+\mu_{d})^{-1}\left(-\tau_{t}\langle y_{t-1},Bw\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2}\|Bw-Kx_{t}-b\|^{2}\right)\right\},

(72)

\displaystyle y_{t}

\displaystyle=\left[\tau_{t}y_{t-1}-(Bw_{t}-Kx_{t}-b)\right]/(\tau_{t}+\mu_{d}).

(73)

end

for

Similar to AC-PDHG, the AC-ADMM method (Algorithm
2
) updates both primal and dual variables in each iteration. The major difference exists in that AC-ADMM maintains the updates of two separate sequences of primal variables,
\{x_{t}\}
and
\{w_{t}\}
. Specifically, the update of
w_{t}
in (
72
) requires solving a more complicated subproblem involving an augmented Lagrangian term
\|Bw-Kx_{t}-b\|^{2}
. However, unlike the classical ADMM where the update for both
x_{t}
and
w_{t}
involves augmented Lagrangian terms, the update of
x_{t}
in AC-ADMM is based on a prox-mapping step similar to (
17
) in Algorithm
1
.
This subproblem can be computed more efficiently than those involving augmented Lagrangian terms.
Additionally, as in AC-PDHG, we use a moving averaging sequence
\{\bar{x}_{t}\}
as the prox-centers.
It should also be noted that the explicit update rule (
73
) for the dual variable
y_{t}
can be equivalently expressed in the following form of prox-mapping with a regularization term
\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|y\|^{2}
:

\displaystyle y_{t}=\arg\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left\{\langle Bw_{t}-Kx_{t}-b,y\rangle+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|y\|^{2}+\tfrac{\tau_{t}}{2}\|y_{t-1}-y\|^{2}\right\},

(74)

which highlights a more explicit connection between AC-PDHG and AC-ADMM.

Our goal in the remaining part of this section is to establish the convergence of AC-ADMM equipped with an adaptive stepsize policy. Instead of relying on the global constant
\|K\|
, we compute the local estimate of
\|K\|
at the
t
-th iteration according to

\displaystyle L_{K,t}:=\tfrac{\|K^{\top}(y_{t}-y_{t-1})\|}{\|y_{t}-y_{t-1}\|}

(75)

and use it to determine the selection of

\eta_{t}
and
\tau_{t}
for the subsequent iterations.

We first show a result to characterize the optimality condition of steps (
72
) and (
73
) and to provide an important relation between the primal and dual variables for the first iteration.

[

]
, all the inequalities in the proof hold with equality) to the equivalent expression of (

5
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we develop novel auto-conditioned primal-dual methods for a few classes of bilinear saddle point problems. First, we introduce the AC-PDHG method for solving problem (
1
), which is fully adaptive to
\|A\|
and achieves the optimal complexity in terms of the primal-dual gap function. We then extend AC-PDHG to solve linearly constrained problems (
3
), establishing convergence guarantees for both optimality gap and constraint violation. Next, we propose the AC-ADMM approach for solving an important class of linearly constrained problems in the form of (
65
). While it involves more complicated subproblems for
w_{t}
, AC-ADMM attains convergence guarantees that depend only on one part of the constraint matrix,
K
, and adapts to the local estimates of
\|K\|
. Finally, we extend AC-PDHG to tackle bilinear saddle point problems where
f
is a smooth function. By incorporating the acceleration scheme of the AC-FGM algorithm
[
23
]
into AC-PDHG, we obtain the optimal iteration complexity in terms of both smooth and bilinear components. We also extend AC-ADMM to solve (
65
) with a smooth
F
in a similar manner.

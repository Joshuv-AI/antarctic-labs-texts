Efficacy and safety profile of vortioxetine as an add-on molecule in obsessive-compulsive disorder: a randomized double-blinded placebo-controlled superiority trial – a study protocol
Authors: Sucharita Mandal, Kaushik Mukhopadhyay, Aparajita Guin, Rupal Sorkhel, Pulama Mitra, Bisweswar Ojha, Kamini Verma, Sukriti Mukherjee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundObsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD) affects approximately 2–3% of the global population and is associated with significant functional impairment and reduced quality of life. Despite its widespread impact, pharmacological treatments approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for OCD remain limited. Vortioxetine is a multimodal antidepressant that obtained its FDA approval in December 2013 for Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) and has shown to have some potential benefits in terms of cognitive aspects and global manifestation of symptoms. The purpose of this study is to assess the effectiveness and safety of vortioxetine as adjunctive treatment in patients with a diagnosis of OCD.Methods/designA randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled superiority trial, developed in accordance with CONSORT guidelines, will assess the efficacy and safety of vortioxetine in individuals diagnosed with OCD. A total of 114 participants who meet all eligibility criteria will be recruited and randomly allocated, using block randomization, into two equal treatment arms (n = 57 per group) following informed consent. The sample size provides 80% statistical power at a 5% significance level to detect a clinically meaningful difference of 5 units between the intervention and control groups. The experimental group will receive vortioxetine combined with escitalopram, and the control group will be administered a placebo with escitalopram. Demographic characteristics and disease profile at baseline will be collected using Case Report Forms (CRFs). The Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale (Y-BOCS) and the Clinical Global Impressions (CGI) will be used to evaluate the outcomes at 4, 8, and 12 weeks. The Adverse Events and Side Events Checklist (ASEC) will document any adverse events reported by the patient throughout the trial participation.DiscussionVortioxetine’s unique pharmacological profile may offer significant benefits to managing cognitive symptoms and improving the quality of life of OCD patients. This clinical trial aims to provide evidence on the clinical value and safety of vortioxetine in the management of OCD in order to advance the optimization of more effective and personalized treatment plans.Trial registrationThe trial was registered with the Clinical Trials Registry - India (CTRI) (registration ID: CTRI/2023/12/060748) on December 26, 2023.Keywords: Vortioxetine, Obsessive-compulsive disorder, Yale-Brown obsessive compulsive scale, Antidepressant, Randomized controlled superiority trial, Clinical trial, Psychopharmacology

Background

OCD is a chronic psychiatric condition characterized by persistent, intrusive, obsessive thoughts and compulsive or repetitive behaviours that significantly interfere with a person’s daily functioning. A review on Indian research in OCD highlighted that early Indian estimates, such as a report presented at the First International Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder Congress in 1993, suggested a lifetime OCD prevalence of 0.6% [1]. More recent studies indicate that the prevalence may be considerably higher, ranging between 2% and 3.3% [2], following the COVID-19 pandemic, as recently demonstrated in global analyses [3], highlighting the need to consider further options for improved treatment responses, particularly for individuals experiencing a partial response to first-line treatment options.

The first-line treatments for OCD are mainly selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SRIs), such as escitalopram, fluoxetine, etc. However, 40–60% of patients demonstrate an inadequate therapeutic response to these SRIs [4]. Clomipramine, a tricyclic antidepressant (TCA), was the first FDA-approved OCD medication, approved in 1989. Despite being established as the gold-standard treatment for OCD, a meta-analysis of 53 articles found no statistically significant difference in its efficacy when compared to SSRIs, challenging earlier assumptions of its superiority [5]. Due to adverse effects such as xerostomia, constipation, and increased risk of cardiac and neurological complications, including seizures, clomipramine is not considered a first-line treatment option for OCD patients [6].

When patients show partial or poor response to SRIs, augmentation strategies are recommended. According to a recent network meta-analysis, memantine ranked highest in efficacy as an augmentation agent, while lamotrigine, ondansetron, and granisetron demonstrated moderate evidence of effectiveness [7]. Research suggests that repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS), particularly with low-frequency stimulation targeting cortical regions including the Supplementary Motor Area (SMA) and Orbitofrontal Cortex (OFC), is beneficial for OCD [8]. In a before-and-after trial, repeated rTMS significantly improved symptoms in patients with treatment-resistant OCD, as evidenced by a decrease in Y-BOCS score, with no major adverse effects reported [9]. However, evidence has been inconsistent, and long-term follow-up studies are limited.

For improved outcomes, SRIs are used adjunct to psychotherapeutic approaches like cognitive-behavior therapy (CBT) or exposure and response prevention (ERP) [10]. Despite these approaches, a large number of patient populations still continue to experience persistent symptoms, contributing to functional impairment and reduced quality of life [11]. A systematic review highlights the necessity for innovative treatment strategies aimed at the serotonergic, dopaminergic, and glutamatergic pathways, as numerous patients remain refractory, exhibiting only partial responses to SRIs and low-dose neuroleptics, which are established as first- and second-line treatments [12]. A 2023 narrative review investigated clinical features of SRI-resistant OCD and identified alternative pharmacotherapeutic approaches. Findings suggested that treatments targeting glutamatergic and anti-inflammatory pathways may have better benefit-risk ratios, indicating that these treatment options could be explored in future research and might be advantageous, especially for OCD that is resistant to treatment [13].

In this context, Vortioxetine, an antidepressant, has shown initial promise in addressing OCD. Preclinical studies have suggested its modulating effects on neurotransmission in multiple systems, including 5-HT, NE, dopamine, acetylcholine, histamine, glutamate, and gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) systems [14]. Its multimodal mechanism includes diverse serotonergic actions like the antagonism of 5-HT3, 5-HT7, and 5-HT1D receptors, partial agonism at 5-HT1B, and the inhibition of serotonin reuptake [15]. While it primarily binds to the serotonin transporter (SERT), influencing serotonin modulation, its mechanism of action is not fully understood [16].

With the exception of a few case reports, Vortioxetine, which is routinely used to treat MDD, has not been extensively studied in OCD. Vortioxetine’s tolerability and efficacy were shown to be satisfactory in a retrospective study of 131 patients in India, 110 of whom had depression and three of whom had OCD [17]. However, this study did not cover a trial specifically dedicated to OCD. Aripiprazole has been evaluated in addition to other augmentation strategies for OCD, and comparison studies of ondansetron [18] have been explored. Despite these findings, there is a paucity of substantial clinical data and existing literature evidencing vortioxetine’s efficacy and safety in the treatment of OCD, particularly within the Indian demographic. By evaluating vortioxetine as an adjuvant to escitalopram in the management of OCD, the randomized controlled trial seeks to fill a significant gap in treatment approaches for partial responders, particularly in resource-constrained settings. This study will help determine how the novel medication can be promising for treating OCD, which may allow for new avenues for modifying current treatment plans.

Primary objective

To determine whether 12 weeks of adjunctive vortioxetine, compared with placebo, added to escitalopram more effectively reduces Yale–Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale scores in adults (18–65 years) with moderate-to-severe OCD.

Secondary objectives

The study also seeks to investigate the safety and tolerability of vortioxetine in this population by analyzing the nature and frequency of adverse events in both the intervention and control groups over a 12-week treatment period.

Methods

Choice of study regimen and comparator

The present study seeks to evaluate the efficacy and safety profile of the novel SRI vortioxetine as an add-on molecule in the treatment of OCD. The efficacy and safety profile of the add-on drug will be compared to an add-on placebo combined with a standard dosage of Escitalopram, which serves as the current standard of care. The intervention group will receive vortioxetine (10–20 mg/day) alongside escitalopram (10–20 mg/day) for a duration of 12 weeks, while the control group will receive a placebo matching vortioxetine in appearance, along with escitalopram (10–20 mg/day) for the same duration. To minimize the influence of bias, both treatment protocols will be delivered using a double-blind design.

Rationale for regimen selection

Vortioxetine was selected as the investigational drug due to its multimodal activity on serotonergic receptors, which has been linked to improved cognitive functioning and emotional regulation. Furthermore, it is used in conjunction with escitalopram, as their combined serotonergic effects may have a greater therapeutic impact, potentially addressing a wide spectrum of complex OCD symptoms. Existing clinical studies have highlighted the efficacy and use of escitalopram in OCD treatment, and its usage in this study provides a shared baseline for comparison. In contrast, the control regimen, consisting of placebo combined with escitalopram, adheres to the generally known treatment paradigm for OCD, with the emphasis on determining the incremental effect of adding vortioxetine to the existing medication. The use of placebo in the double-blind clinical trial enables a robust assessment of vortioxetine’s effects independent of escitalopram.

Dosage and administration

Participants in the intervention group will receive tablet Escitalopram 10 mg, once daily initially, which will be increased to 20 mg after 10 days. Alongside Escitalopram, Tablet Vortioxetine will also be initiated at 5 mg daily, and it will be titrated to 10 mg by day 10. Thereafter, they will receive Escitalopram 20 mg and Vortioxetine 10 mg daily for the study duration. The control group will follow the same schedule for Escitalopram but receive a matched placebo instead of Vortioxetine to maintain blinding. In both groups, if clinical response is inadequate, Escitalopram may be increased up to 30 mg per day at the investigator’s discretion. The Vortioxetine dose will remain fixed at 10 mg per day.

To minimize the risk of pharmacological interference from the residual effects of existing or prior medications, all participants will have a washout period of at least three half-lives of the drug, with a minimum of 7 days, before receiving the trial drugs.

Safety monitoring and adjustments

Participants will be monitored throughout the treatment period to identify and manage any adverse events that may arise. To monitor potential changes from the baseline, vital signs such as blood pressure, heart rate, respiration rate, temperature, and blood oxygen levels, as well as weight changes and ECG results, would be documented during planned visits. Participants receive a Patient Information Sheet with an emergency contact and may report symptoms anytime between visits, with SOS follow-ups permitted.

At each follow-up visit, adverse events will be categorized and evaluated using the ASEC. If the patient shows a lack of improvement in symptoms, dose adjustments for escitalopram will be guided by an independent clinician based on clinical judgment and symptom severity. Additionally, psychological support (non-directive, supportive psychotherapy) will be provided if needed.

Choice of comparator

A placebo-controlled design was selected to assess the additional benefit of Vortioxetine, given the well-established role of SRIs such as escitalopram in the treatment of OCD. As a result, the control group, which receives a placebo in addition to escitalopram, permits an unbiased and independent assessment of vortioxetine’s effectiveness and guarantees that any observed modifications in the intensity of OCD symptoms can be attributed to the addition of vortioxetine rather than the effects of escitalopram alone.

Site selection

The site of the study is All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS), Kalyani, West Bengal, India. With the support of necessary clinical and research facilities, the site provides a consistent outpatient population for follow-up and participant recruitment. The Department of Pharmacology plays a critical role in ensuring the integrity of the double-blind study design. Study medications, including both the active drug and the placebo, are administered by the pharmacology department according to randomization codes. Blinding is achieved through centralized labelling and secure handling protocols, and investigators, clinical personnel, and participants are not informed of treatment allocation during the trial. Medication storage, dispensing, and record-keeping adhere to Good Clinical Practice (GCP) standards to maintain traceability and protocol adherence.

Patient eligibility

Those who visit the Psychiatric Outpatient Department (OPD) at AIIMS, Kalyani, and are diagnosed with OCD, based on the International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11) diagnostic criteria, by a psychiatrist will be approached by the research team to check for eligibility to be enrolled for the study. The inclusion criteria for the study are; (i) subjects of either gender between 18 and 65 years of age, (ii) Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale (Y-BOCS) score of 16 or more (moderate to severe illness), and (iii) drug-naïve for at least seven days prior to recruitment. The exclusion criteria are (i) occurrence of any psychotic symptoms, (ii) major psychiatric comorbidities or active diagnosis of substance use disorder with dependence criteria, with the exception of nicotine and caffeine, (iii) Participants with severe medical or neurological comorbidities, (iv) a history of reported drug intolerance to vortioxetine, and (v) pregnancy or lactation at the time of recruitment.

The motive behind these stringent inclusion and exclusion criteria is to reduce confounding variables and improve internal validity and participant safety. Participants who are drug naïve, having no psychotropic medication use, including SSRIs, SNRIs, tricyclic antidepressants, antipsychotics, mood stabilizers, or benzodiazepines, for at least seven days prior to enrollment, and who are eligible after initial screening will be approached by the research team. Only those who provide written informed consent will be enrolled in the study.

Recruitment process

Potential participants visiting the Psychiatry OPD at AIIMS Kalyani will be identified by the clinical team and referred for screening. Those with an existing diagnosis of OCD will be further evaluated to assess eligibility according to inclusion and exclusion criteria. During the first screening visit, all study-related procedures, objectives, possible risks, and benefits will be thoroughly explained to them in their native language. Informed written consent will be given prior to starting any study-related procedures, and participants will also be given a copy of the patient information sheet detailing the purposes of the research, possible risks and benefits of the study. Basic investigations, such as Complete Blood Count (CBC), Liver Function Tests (LFTs), Renal Function Tests (RFTs), Lipid Profile, Thyroid Profile, and Electrocardiogram (ECG), will be performed during screening of eligibility. A clinician trained in administering the Y-BOCS will rate symptom severity to ensure impartiality. Only those who meet all the eligibility criteria, such as a Y-BOCS score ≥ 16 and drug-naïve status, will be enrolled in the study.

Treatment allocation

Participants meeting all eligibility requirements are then randomized using a block randomization method into either the intervention or control group. Group allocation is kept confidential until assignment, and the researcher doing the randomization is not affiliated with the clinical team. Following randomization, baseline sociodemographic and clinical data are collected and documented using structured case report forms (CRFs).

Follow-up and assessment of the primary outcome

From the time of recruitment, follow-up visits will be planned at 4, 8, and 12-week intervals. These time points enable an optimal window to measure change in severity of OCD symptoms while reducing the risk of bias, taking into account varying lengths of treatments.

The change in the Y-BOCS total score from baseline to 12 weeks will be used as the primary outcome measure. Treatment response will be defined as a ≥ 35% decrease in the total Y-BOCS score and a CGI-I rating of 1 (very much improved) or 2 (much improved), maintained for at least one week. A partial response will be indicated by a Y-BOCS reduction between 25% and 34%, with a CGI-I score of 3 (minimally improved), maintained for one week, aligning with established staging models of OCD response [19]. Thus, clinician-rated global improvement will be evaluated through the CGI scale, the CGI-Efficacy Index will be used to evaluate the overall benefit-risk profile of the intervention, and safety outcomes will be monitored and recorded using the ASEC.

It is essential to ensure that neither the participants nor the clinicians conducting the follow-up assessments are aware of the treatment allocation to avoid bias. This double-blind strategy minimizes the integrity of the outcome measures, with all the drugs given through a specific research staff to ensure treatment adherence. For ensuring patient safety, the protocol accepts the administration of rescue medication for severe anxiety, restlessness, or sleep disruption. Clonazepam (0.25 mg) and Risperidone (0.5 mg) can be administered when clinically warranted. These will be given at the clinical discretion of the treating clinicians if clinically indicated, with documentation carefully made to evaluate their influence on trial outcomes.

Sample size assumptions

The sample size for the study consists of 114 participants (n = 114), with 57 participants in the intervention and control groups. A pooled standard deviation (SD) of 6.4 units and a total mean difference of 8 units (total difference of 16 − 8 units) between the test and reference groups were assumed for calculating the sample size. Given these parameters, the study is powered to detect a difference of 5 units in favor of the test drug over the active control drug, assuming that a larger mean score is desirable. A total sample size of 114 participants will provide 80% power and a significance level of 5% to declare the test drug superior to the control, based on the defined margin of superiority.

Data analysis

Study-specific data and patient details will be recorded using standardized Case Record Forms (CRFs). The data will subsequently be transcribed and formatted, and data cleaning will be done in an Excel database and analyzed using R statistical software (R Foundation for Statistical Computing) via the RStudio interface. Categorical variables will be analysed using Chi-Square test or Fisher’s exact test. Differences between two groups for numerical variables will be analysed using t-test for parametric distribution and Mann Whitney Test for non-parametric distribution. The level of significance will be set at p < 0.05 for all comparisons. All reporting will follow the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) guidelines.

Trial status

By the time of this submission (June/2025), the study is in the recruitment phase, and participant enrollment is actively underway. The necessary ethical approvals have been obtained, and preparations for data collection, including medication dispensation and participant debriefing, have been completed in accordance with the trial protocol.

Discussion

The aim of this randomized controlled trial is to evaluate the efficacy of Vortioxetine in alleviating obsessive-compulsive symptoms and its possible impact on altering disease severity and progression throughout the 12-week treatment period. Vortioxetine, as a new antidepressant with multiple mechanisms of action, presents a promising pharmacological profile, and while its effectiveness in OCD has not been confirmed, initial neurochemical evidence indicates possible advantages in conditions beyond major depression. This research also intends to systematically observe negative effects to create a safety profile for Vortioxetine in the treatment of OCD. Employing validated measures like the YBOCS for assessing symptom severity and CGI for overall clinical condition, combined with tools for monitoring adverse events, guarantees a thorough assessment of both effectiveness and tolerability. If effective, the findings could back Vortioxetine’s role as an additional therapy, potentially enhancing clinical outcomes for OCD patients who show a partial response to first-line treatment.

Future plan based on expected outcomes

If the trial demonstrates positive outcomes, the intervention can be scaled up for use in larger clinical populations in multicentric studies. Vortioxetine may emerge as an effective adjunctive pharmacological option for patients with OCD, particularly those with comorbid depressive or anxiety symptoms. This could pave the way for broader applications of Vortioxetine in the treatment of obsessive-compulsive and related disorders, expanding the therapeutic toolkit available to clinicians.

Declarations

Ethics approval and consent to participate

The clinical trial commenced only after obtaining prior written approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee (IEC) of AIIMS Kalyani and successful registration with the Clinical Trials Registry - India (CTRI). As this is an academic clinical trial involving a drug already approved for a different indication (vortioxetine for major depressive disorder), permission from the Central Licensing Authority (CLA) under the DCGI was not required. The study adheres strictly to the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and complies with the Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR) guidelines for biomedical research involving human participants. Written informed consent is obtained from all participants in their native language, ensuring they fully understand the study procedures, potential risks, benefits, and their right to withdraw at any stage without any consequences. No participant is subjected to financial burden; all costs associated with the study are borne by the institution. The confidentiality of all participant data is maintained. Identifiable information is de-identified prior to analysis, and data is stored securely with restricted access limited to authorized study investigators. Participants are also provided with the contact details of both the principal investigator and the ethics committee to report any concerns or adverse events.

Consent for publication

Not applicable, as the protocol does not include any individual person’s data in any form, including images, videos, or clinical details.

Vehicle component defect detection method based on transformer and multi-scale feature fusion
Authors: Guanglei Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Vehicle component defect detection is critical for ensuring` the safety and reliability of automotive systems. Traditional defect detection methods often struggle with complex and multi-scale defect features, limiting their accuracy and robustness. Moreover, existing models frequently fail to capture both local and global features of vehicle parts, resulting in suboptimal performance in real-world scenarios. In response to these challenges, we propose a novel defect detection method that combines Transformer and multi-scale feature fusion. This approach leverages the powerful global feature learning capability of the Transformer and integrates multi-scale features to improve the detection of defects across varying sizes and complexities. Our model adapts to the intricate nature of vehicle components, efficiently extracting both fine-grained local details and global context. Experimental results demonstrate that our model outperforms traditional defect detection methods, achieving higher accuracy and robustness across various vehicle parts, even in the presence of complex, multi-scale defects. Additionally, it shows significant improvements in real-time processing capabilities, making it suitable for practical deployment in automotive quality control systems. This study highlights the potential of Transformer-based architectures combined with multi-scale feature fusion for enhancing defect detection, providing a more efficient and accurate solution for vehicle maintenance and safety.

Introduction

Vehicle component defect detection is widely used in vehicle manufacturing, maintenance and safety. When the number of vehicle use is large, drivers and repair shop need to inspect the wear and damage of various parts regularly. In order to ensure the safety and reliability of the vehicle, traditional manual inspection method takes much time and is easily to human errors1,2. It cannot meet the requirements of modern automobile industry. Therefore, automatic defect detection becomes a very hot topic. Quickly detect and repair vehicle component defects by efficiently and accurately inspecting vehicle component defects has become a key issues for enhancing productivity in the automotive industry and ensuring driving safety3.

In the past few years, with the rapid development of deep learning, especially the application of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in image classification and object detection, many vision based defect detection methods have been proposed4,5. Recent comprehensive surveys have systematically reviewed the progress of machine learning-driven defect detection, categorizing approaches into supervised, unsupervised, semi-supervised, reinforcement, and generative learning paradigms, with deep learning emerging as the core technology for material defect detection across aerospace, automotive, and construction industries6,7. The deep learning method learns features from images automatically, which avoids the troublesome feature extraction. It can handle high-dimensional data and has a good performance. But the current existing deep learning method mostly depends on the single-scale image features8. When the traditional method is applied to small defects or partial parts in the scene9,10, it still has obvious limitations. Addressing multi-scale defect detection has become a critical research focus11, with studies proposing perception-efficient networks that enhance feature fusion for multi-scale representations and introducing aggregated multi-level feature interaction mechanisms to better utilize positional and semantic information12,13. For example, when the resolution of the image is low or the area of the defect is small, many traditional methods cannot extract the meaningful information from the image and thus the detection accuracy will be lowered14.

In order to solve above problems, new deep models such as spatiotemporal graph convolutional networks (ST-GCN) and Transformers gradually get broad attentions and applications in the defect detection task. Transformer-based architectures have shown promising results in automotive damage evaluation, with models like ALBERT demonstrating strong performance in segmenting car parts and classifying damage types15,16. Similarly, hybrid approaches combining vision transformers with convolutional attention mechanisms have been developed to enhance defect detection in automotive weld seams, improving both local and global feature representation17,18. ST-GCN could take the advantages of graph convolutional networks on the complex spatiotemporal dependencies, which could handle structural relationships and motion states between components. Recent advancements in physics-augmented ST-GCN have demonstrated superior performance in damage localization tasks by incorporating spatial correlation of sensors and wave scattering principles into graph topology construction19. Spatio-temporal graph neural networks have also been successfully applied to predict fault probabilities in sequential circuits, capturing both topological and timing information20. Self-attention mechanism has a certain advantage in modeling long-range dependencies, which helps the model to learn more important features globally. However, the integration and optimization of these two technologies, particularly for multi-scale feature fusion in practical applications, remains an open research challenge.

Current research has mostly focused on the application of a single model or is limited to defect detection in a specific dimension. Concurrently, significant advancements have been made in neural network-based fault diagnosis21,22, offering alternative perspectives for defect analysis. For instance, the Metric-Encapsulation (ME) framework enhances diagnostic robustness by learning a metric space that encapsulates the intrinsic characteristics of different fault patterns23. Similarly, the Deep Coupled Metric Learning with Domain Fusion (DCMLDF) framework addresses domain shift challenges by jointly learning coupled metrics and fusing information across multiple source domains. These frameworks highlight the importance of sophisticated feature representation and domain adaptation in complex diagnostic tasks. While these approaches excel in signal-based or domain-adaptive fault diagnosis, their direct application to vision-based, multi-scale defect detection in vehicle components, where spatial relationships and contextual information are paramount, remains under-explored. Existing methods often fail to fully account for the diversity and complexity of defects in vehicle components, especially under the influence of defects of different scales, occlusion, and environmental variations.

The robustness and accuracy of existing methods still leave significant room for improvement. Furthermore, existing research lacks a unified framework that integrates multiple deep learning techniques, can handle diverse data types, and adapt to complex scenarios. To address these research gaps, this paper proposes a vehicle component defect detection method based on the Transformer and multi-scale feature fusion, Trans-STFuse. This model combines the strengths of the ST-GCN in spatiotemporal graph modeling with the Transformer in global information modeling, effectively capturing both local and global information in vehicle component defects. Through multi-scale feature fusion, the model improves detection accuracy while enhancing its adaptability to defects of varying sizes and shapes. Furthermore, this study introduces component relationship modeling based on a spatiotemporal graph convolutional network, further enhancing its detection capabilities for complex vehicle structural defects.

The primary contribution of this paper is twofold. First, we propose Trans-STFuse, a novel framework that introduces a synergistic learning paradigm for vehicle component defect detection. This paradigm moves beyond the simple combination of existing techniques by integrating the ST-GCN (as a structural relationship encoder), the Transformer (as a global context integrator), and a dedicated multi-scale feature fusion mechanism into a cohesive, mutually reinforcing architecture. Second, within this paradigm, we design a novel attention-guided multi-scale feature fusion strategy that dynamically adapts to defects of varying sizes. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed Trans-STFuse model outperforms existing mainstream methods on multiple public datasets, particularly achieving significant improvements in defect detection accuracy and robustness in complex scenarios.

Comparative assessment3

In this experiment, we take the Trans-STFuse model to evaluate the localization performance on the defect localization task. Different from defect classification, the defect localization task requires more accurate localization of the defect position and shape, especially when the background is complex or the defect is small. Therefore, we compare Trans-STFuse with YOLOv4 and Faster R-CNN to study the differences in the accuracy of defect localization. The experiment is conducted on the Car Damage Dataset, and the localization performance of the model is evaluated with the IoU metric and false positive metrics.

In this experiment, we take IoU as the metric to evaluate the degree of intersection between the bounding box detected by the model and the ground-truth annotated region. The larger the IoU, the more accurate the localization of the defect. We also take mean average precision (mAP) to comprehensively evaluate the accuracy of detection. To further assess localization robustness, we additionally report the false positive rate (FPR) and false positives per image (FPPI) specifically for the localization task, where a detection is considered a false positive if its IoU with any ground-truth box falls below 0.5.

In the experiment, the model’s detection results were compared with the ground-truth annotated defect regions, and the IoU (Intersection over Union) was calculated. Overall, ablation experiments demonstrate that each module plays an essential role in the Trans-STFuse model. The ST-GCN module improves the ability to model spatiotemporal relationships, which allows the model to understand the structural context and functional dependencies between vehicle components, thereby reducing false alarms caused by isolated background noise that resembles defects. The Transformer module enhances global information modeling, enabling the model to integrate contextual cues from the entire image, which is crucial for correctly identifying ambiguous or partially occluded defects by considering their surroundings. The multi-scale feature fusion module ensures the model’s ability to handle defects of varying scales, by combining fine-grained details for small defects with high-level semantic features for large defects, thus preventing scale-bias in detection. Removing any one module significantly degrades model performance, demonstrating the effectiveness of this model’s design in defect detection tasks. The performance drop in each case can be attributed to the loss of these specific capabilities: the loss of structural reasoning (without ST-GCN), the loss of holistic context (without Transformer), or the loss of scale-adaptive feature representation (without multi-scale fusion).

Qualitative analysis

To complement the quantitative results and provide deeper insight into the model’s decision-making process, we conduct a qualitative analysis based on representative challenging cases from the test sets. This analysis aims to illustrate how the integrated components of Trans-STFuse contribute to its predictions and explain its superior performance over baseline models in scenarios that require understanding of both local structure and global context. In addition to successful detection cases, we also examine representative failure cases under varying environmental conditions to identify the current limitations of our model and inform future improvements.

We examine the model outputs on four types of challenging defects: (1) small-scale scratches, (2) large dents with complex backgrounds, (3) defects under partial occlusion, and (4) defects involving multiple components. Table 5 summarizes the comparative performance of Trans-STFuse, YOLOv4, and Faster R-CNN across these categories, while Fig. 4 provides visual examples of each case with corresponding IoU scores.

As shown in Fig. 4, Case 1 presents a thin, low-contrast scratch on a car door panel. YOLOv4 and Faster R-CNN both fail to detect this defect (IoU: 0.00), whereas Trans-STFuse accurately localizes the scratch with an IoU of 0.86. This improvement is attributed to the multi-scale feature fusion module, which preserves fine-grained details through deeper network layers. In Case 2, a large dent on a rear bumper is situated against a concrete wall with strong shadow patterns. YOLOv4 produces a false positive on the shadow region (IoU: 0.12), and Faster R-CNN generates an imprecise bounding box that includes excessive background area (IoU: 0.58). Trans-STFuse, by contrast, leverages the ST-GCN module to focus on the component region and the Transformer to suppress irrelevant contextual noise, achieving a precise localization with IoU of 0.91.

Case 3 involves a crack on a black car hood that is partially occluded by a fallen leaf. YOLOv4 detects only the visible portion of the crack, resulting in a fragmented bounding box (IoU: 0.45), while Faster R-CNN misses the defect entirely (IoU: 0.00). Trans-STFuse successfully infers the full extent of the crack, including the occluded region, by combining the graph connectivity modeled by ST-GCN with the contextual completion capability of the Transformer, reaching an IoU of 0.91. In Case 4, multiple small rust spots are distributed across the lower half of a white car door panel. The ground truth annotation groups these spots as a single defect cluster. YOLOv4 and Faster R-CNN each detect the spots as multiple separate instances, yielding mean IoU scores of 0.38 and 0.41 respectively. (c) Heavy rain occlusion: water droplets on the lens obscure the defect region, resulting in a false negative. (d) Unseen defect type: the model encounters a crack morphology not present in the training set, producing an imprecise bounding box (IoU: 0.43). These cases highlight current limitations in handling extreme environmental degradation and domain shift.

The failure cases in Fig. 5 reveal several systematic limitations of Trans-STFuse. First, under extreme low-light conditions (Fig. 5a), the defect’s contrast against the background drops below the feature extraction threshold, causing complete detection failure. Second, motion blur (Fig. 5b) disrupts the spatial integrity of defect features, leading to inaccurate localization. Third, physical occlusions such as rain droplets (Fig. 5c) introduce artifacts that are misclassified as background rather than defect-occluding elements. Fourth, the model exhibits limited generalization to defect types absent from the training distribution (Fig. 5d), producing low-confidence or imprecise predictions. These observations suggest that while Trans-STFuse excels on in-distribution defects under normal imaging conditions, its robustness to severe environmental degradation and out-of-distribution defect patterns requires further improvement–directions we identify for future work.

The table and figures collectively demonstrate that Trans-STFuse consistently provides more accurate and robust detections on in-distribution challenging cases. The performance gap stems from its integrated design: multi-scale fusion captures fine details (Case 1), ST-GCN provides structural focus (Case 2, 4), and Transformer enables contextual reasoning (Case 2, 3). This analysis clarifies how defect representations are enriched and why predictions are more reliable under favorable imaging conditions, directly supporting our quantitative findings.

Discussion

This paper proposes the Trans-STFuse model, a novel framework built upon a synergistic learning paradigm that integrates structural relationship encoding, global context modeling, and adaptive multi-scale fusion to address the core challenges in vehicle component defect detection. The experimental results validate that this paradigm effectively captures both local structural details and global contextual information, leading to superior performance in complex scenarios where defects vary in size, shape, and are often embedded in cluttered backgrounds. The challenges of vehicle component defect detection are to solve the problems of small defect detection and localization in complex scenes. At present, the main challenges of defect detection are the diversity of defect types, small defect size and complex background, which cause low detection and localization accuracy. The disadvantages of traditional image processing method and deep learning method based on YOLO and Faster R-CNN are discussed. Especially, when the background is complex and the defect is small, the above methods have certain limitations. Therefore, this paper proposes a hybrid model combining spatiotemporal graph convolutional network ST-GCN and Transformer, and introduces multi-scale feature fusion mechanism to improve the accuracy and robustness of model in defect detection.

The public dataset experiments of Car Damage Dataset and DAGM Dataset verify the superiority of Trans-STFuse model. The experimental results show that, compared with YOLOv4, Faster R-CNN and ST-GCN based model, Trans-STFuse model has better accuracy, higher recall, higher F1-score and higher Intersection over Union (IoU). Furthermore, our model achieves substantially lower false positive rates (4.2% on Car Damage, 3.1% on DAGM) and false positives per image (0.19 and 0.12 respectively), demonstrating its superior robustness against false alarms–a critical advantage for real-world industrial deployment where excessive false detections undermine system credibility and increase inspection costs. Especially, Trans-STFuse model has strong robustness in detecting small defects and in complex backgrounds, which makes it more effective in localizing and detecting small defects. Based on the ablation experiment, we further verified the importance of ST-GCN, Transformer and multi-scale feature fusion module in the model, and proved that these modules improved the performance of the model and collectively contributed to false positive suppression.

Despite promising results, the model has limitations. First, its performance on public datasets may not generalize directly to real industrial settings due to domain shift. That is, the generalization ability of the model to new defect types and diverse environment is still insufficient. Secondly, the computational cost of the current model is high, especially when using Transformer module, a large amount of memory and computational cost are needed. Especially when training on high resolution images or large scale datasets, it may also limit the application of the model in real industrial environment.

Future research will focus on addressing these two issues. First, we plan to explore domain adaptation techniques or few-shot learning methods to improve the model’s generalization to unseen data, enabling better application in real-world scenarios. Second, we will optimize the model’s computational efficiency through model pruning, knowledge distillation, or the use of lighter-weight Transformer variants, reducing the computational burden while maintaining high performance. We also aim to further reduce the false positive rate through more sophisticated background suppression techniques and uncertainty estimation methods. In addition, in the future, multimodal data (such as sensor data or 3D images) can be combined to further enhance the robustness and accuracy of the model, especially for defect detection tasks in complex environments. These potential improvements could enhance the model’s practicality for real-world industrial defect detection.

Related work

Traditional methods for vehicle part defect detection

Vehicle part defect detection is a critical component of automotive manufacturing and maintenance. Traditional defect detection methods primarily rely on rule-based image processing techniques, such as edge detection, morphological processing, and template matching. These methods employ manually designed features and utilize image processing algorithms to identify and locate defects24. The earliest defect detection methods typically employed edge detection algorithms based on grayscale images25,26. Techniques such as the Canny edge detector and the Sobel operator were widely used to extract defect regions. These methods can effectively identify surface defects against simple backgrounds, but often struggle with complex backgrounds or low-contrast defects.

With the constant development of image processing technology, researchers begin to design more complex feature extraction methods such as feature analysis of texture, color and shape. The application of texture feature extraction method27, such as gray-level co-occurrence matrix(GLCM), in the surface defect of metal part, can reflect the texture variation in defect area to some degree. However, these methods only rely on manually designed features, and traditional image processing methods will show large errors in the face of unknown or complicated defects28. In addition, traditional image processing methods are sensitive to lighting, scale and noise variation, and difficult to implement in the practical application. Therefore, the traditional image processing methods are limited in the application of vehicle surface scratch and collision damage defect because of their few scales and poor robustness29.

To solve above problems, researchers gradually introduced machine learning method, such as support vector machine (SVM) and decision tree, which classify defects by learning features extracted from images. However, traditional machine learning methods still rely on manually designed features and cannot obtain expected classification effect when facing complex data30. According to the research results, when the defect diversity increases, traditional machine learning methods show certain defects in the face of complex data, such as diversity of defect types, scale variation and complex scenes31.

Therefore, traditional methods gradually show obvious disadvantages in the application of complex defect detection in vehicle components. Although traditional methods can achieve certain effects in some specific scenarios, they can’t meet the needs of practical applications when the types of defects to be detected are diversified and the environment of defect detection is complex32,33. With the emergence of deep learning technology, especially convolutional neural network (CNN) and other advanced deep learning method, traditional rule-based and handcrafted feature-based method will gradually be replaced. Deep learning technology can automatically learn representative features from data, which greatly improve the accuracy and robustness of defect detection34. Therefore, modern defect detection method will gradually introduce deep learning technology to better serve the complexity and diversity of vehicle component defect detection.

Advances in object detection and image segmentation

With the development of deep learning, object detection and image segmentation have been widely used in component defect detection of vehicles. Object detection based on traditional methods uses handcrafted features to extract information from images and then uses classifiers for recognition. However, these methods have low accuracy in detecting complex backgrounds and small-scale defects35,36. With the development of deep learning, Faster R-CNN, YOLO and SSD have achieved significant performance improvement in object detection. These methods automatically extract features through deep convolutional neural networks, and their detection accuracy and speed are greatly improved. YOLO and SSD have advantages in real-time performance37. Faster R-CNN uses a kind of region proposal network (RPN) to improve detection accuracy. RPN is used to propose multi-scale objects. Semantic segmentation methods based on images also make great progress in defect detection. Methods such as FCN and U-Net can use pixel-level classification to accurately segment defect areas38,39. It is convenient for later defect localization and repair. U-Net has advantages in segmenting structured defects and can effectively preserve detailed information. However, these methods still have certain limitations in the robustness of small-scale defects and complex backgrounds. Especially when the defect and the background are similar or the defect is seriously occluded40,41, the detection accuracy will decrease.

With the development of multi-task learning methods, combining object detection and image segmentation has become a new trend. By localizing objects and segmenting defects in the same process, more accurate defect location and morphological information can be provided, and the performance of the detection system can be improved. Based on the application of multi-scale learning and attention mechanism, the accuracy and robustness of object detection and image segmentation have been further improved. The application prospects are very broad, especially in vehicle component defect detection.

Multi-scale feature learning and fusion methods

Multi-scale feature learning and fusion are important technologies to solve the problem of improving the accuracy of image defect detection. Compared with the defects of the same type, the scales of the defects to be detected are diverse. Traditional defect detection methods are easily influenced by the scales of defects and cannot accurately detect the small or minute defects42. Meanwhile, large defects are located in complex backgrounds, and their accurate positions are hard to be determined.

Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have been able to effectively process local features by extracting features from low-level to high-level layers. However, the standard CNN architecture primarily focuses on extracting features at a fixed scale, which limits its performance for defects with large size variations, such as small cracks or large dents43,44. To address this issue, multi-scale feature learning extracts multi-level, multi-scale feature information within different layers of the convolutional network, enabling it to handle defects of varying sizes45. Classic multi-scale convolutional methods, such as the Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) and the Inception network, significantly improve the model’s ability to recognize multi-scale objects by constructing and fusing feature maps at different scales.

Furthermore, feature fusion technology further enhances the expressive power of multi-scale features. The core concept of feature fusion is to effectively combine features at different scales and levels to simultaneously consider local details and global information. Multi-scale feature fusion methods typically include strategies such as weighted averaging46, concatenation, and cross-layer connections. These methods preserve fine-grained features across different scales while strengthening the contextual relationships between different image regions. In vehicle component defect detection, multi-scale feature fusion enables the model to accurately detect both small cracks and large damaged areas, thereby improving the robustness and accuracy of defect detection47.

Currently, with the birth of self-attention, the deep models are more flexible and dynamic to assign weights at different scales. For instance, Transformer employs self-attention to automatically assign different weights to features at different scales to further enhance the effectiveness of feature fusion48,49. Employing such new mechanisms, our model can still obtain high detection accuracy when dealing with complex scenes and different scales.

Methods

Overview of our network

The Trans-STFuse model proposed in this paper aims to address the challenges of multi-scale information fusion and global feature modeling in vehicle component defect detection by combining the powerful features of the Spatio-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (ST-GCN) and the Transformer. The model’s architecture is shown in Fig. 6. It is divided into several key modules, including input feature extraction, spatio-temporal feature modeling, Transformer global information modeling, and multi-scale feature fusion. The functions of each module and their interoperability are detailed below.Fig. 6Schematic diagram of the Trans-STFuse model architecture.

Schematic diagram of the Trans-STFuse model architecture.

The input to the Trans-STFuse model is an image of vehicle components. After preprocessing, the image passes through a series of convolutional layers for initial feature extraction, obtaining low-level features such as edges, textures, and colors. These features form the basis for all subsequent processing.

These initial features are then fed into the Spatio-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (ST-GCN) module. The ST-GCN explicitly models the structural and functional relationships between vehicle components by constructing a graph, where nodes represent component regions and edges represent their spatiotemporal dependencies. Through graph convolution, the ST-GCN captures local interactions and contextual relationships between components, outputting a set of spatiotemporally-aware node features.

These node features are subsequently processed by the Transformer module. The Transformer employs a self-attention mechanism to model long-range dependencies across the entire image. By calculating feature similarities and assigning adaptive weights, it aggregates global contextual information, helping to disambiguate local features and understand the broader scene context.

Following this, the model performs multi-scale feature fusion. This module integrates the locally refined features from the ST-GCN and the globally contextualized features from the Transformer across multiple resolutions. Through weighted combination and concatenation, it preserves defect information at all scales, enabling the detection of both minute scratches and large dents. Finally, the fused multi-scale features are passed to the detection head for defect classification and precise localization. The output includes the defect category, a bounding box indicating its location, and confidence scores, providing comprehensive information for quality assessment.

Overall, the Trans-STFuse model integrates the ST-GCN and Transformer not as isolated components, but as complementary cores of a unified synergistic learning paradigm. The key to this paradigm is the closed-loop interaction between modules: the ST-GCN encodes the physical structure and dependencies among components, providing a strong spatial prior that focuses the Transformer’s attention on semantically related regions. The Transformer, in turn, models long-range contextual relationships across the entire image, providing global semantic information that helps to disambiguate local features extracted by the ST-GCN (e.g., distinguishing a true crack from textured background). The multi-scale fusion mechanism dynamically synthesizes these locally refined and globally contextualized features at multiple resolutions, ensuring comprehensive representation for defects of all sizes. The necessity for both modules arises from their complementary roles in addressing distinct aspects of the problem: the ST-GCN is indispensable for encoding the hard, physical constraints and relationships between components (answering “what is connected to what and how”), providing a strong prior that reduces the hypothesis space. The Transformer, in contrast, is specialized in discovering soft, semantic associations and long-range visual dependencies (answering “what is relevant to what given the full context”), which are essential for disambiguation and reasoning about defects that manifest in relation to the overall scene. Their interaction is not sequential but synergistic; the structural graph sharpens the Transformer’s attention, while the global context validates and enriches the graph’s local messages. This principled integration addresses the core challenges of vehicle defect detection–structural ambiguity, contextual dependency, and scale diversity–in a cohesive manner, rather than treating them as separate problems. Therefore, Trans-STFuse achieves efficient and accurate defect detection by implementing a context-aware, structurally-informed, and scale-adaptive feature learning framework.

Design of the spatiotemporal graph convolutional module (ST-GCN)

The spatiotemporal graph convolutional network (ST-GCN) module is the basic module in Trans-STFuse model to learn the spatiotemporal relationships between vehicle components. Trans-STFuse model uses graph convolutional network (GCN) to learn the spatial relationships between different vehicle components and introduces temporal information to model the dynamic changes, which improves the understanding of vehicle component defects. In Fig 7, the construction of graph structure, spatiotemporal graph convolution and feature transfer/fusion process of ST-GCN module are shown.Fig. 7Schematic diagram of the ST-GCN module architecture.

Schematic diagram of the ST-GCN module architecture.

The core conceptual motivation for employing an ST-GCN in our task stems from the inherent structural and functional dependencies among vehicle components. A defect in one part (e.g., a bent frame) often influences the state of adjacent parts (e.g., aligned body panels). To model these dependencies explicitly, we construct a graph where each node represents a distinct vehicle component region (e.g., door, bumper, wheel) identified via an initial semantic segmentation or region proposal step. Edges are established between nodes based on both spatial adjacency and functional connectivity (e.g., hinge connections, load-bearing relationships), which are derived from prior knowledge of vehicle architecture. Although our input images are static, the “temporal” dimension in our ST-GCN is repurposed to model hierarchical or multi-resolution relationships across the graph. Concretely, we treat different feature abstraction levels (from shallow to deep CNN layers) as sequential “time steps,” allowing the graph network to propagate and refine information across structural scales, thereby capturing the multi-scale contextual relationships that are crucial for defect diagnosis. This approach transforms a static image analysis problem into a structured, relational reasoning task.

In the ST-GCN module, the input image first passes through a convolutional layer to extract preliminary features and is converted into graph-structured data. Each node in the graph represents a region of a vehicle component, while the edges between nodes represent the relationships between these components. These nodes and edges form a graph, where the edge weights reflect the spatiotemporal dependencies between components. In this way, the ST-GCN effectively models the spatial relationships and motion states between components.

Through the multi-head attention mechanism, the model can capture different dependencies in multiple subspaces in parallel, thereby enriching its ability to model global context.

The Transformer also introduces positional encoding to compensate for the lack of sequential information in the self-attention mechanism. Positional encoding incorporates positional information into the input features. A common approach is to encode the position using sine and cosine functions:8\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \textbf{PE}_{i, 2j} = \sin \left( \frac{i}{10000^{2j/d}} \right) , \quad \textbf{PE}_{i, 2j+1} = \cos \left( \frac{i}{10000^{2j/d}} \right) \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$i$$\end{document} is the position, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$j$$\end{document} is the dimension index, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$d$$\end{document} is the dimension of the positional encoding. Feedforward networks help the model capture more nonlinear relationships and high-level feature information.

Finally, the final feature representation learned by the Transformer module containing global contextual information is output. The global contextual information is fed to the following multi-scale feature fusion module. Finally, in addition to long-range dependencies, self-attention method and the feedforward neural network of Transformer can also improve the expressiveness of feature, which offers more detection information.

In summary, the Transformer module containing powerful global information modeling ability makes the Trans-STFuse model fully consider the global context of images in defect detection task. Combined with local spatiotemporal modeling of ST-GCN, the Transformer module enhances the model’s ability to adapt to various defects and occlusion, and offers more adaptive and expressive features for final detection results.

Multi-scale feature fusion mechanism design

The multi-scale feature fusion mechanism is the core of the Trans-STFuse model. Defects in vehicle components exhibit large scale variations. Single-scale feature extraction fails to capture both small-scale details and large-scale context simultaneously, leading to reduced detection accuracy. How to fuse the features from different scales in the same model to enhance the accuracy and robustness of defect detection is the main problem discussed in this paper.

In Trans-STFuse model, the multi-scale feature fusion mechanism is implemented by integrating output features of ST-GCN module and Transformer module. Firstly, ST-GCN module acquires local spatiotemporal features through graph convolution operations. Local spatiotemporal features learn the spatial and temporal relationships between vehicle components. At the same time, Transformer module learns long-range information by weighting global features through self-attention mechanism. Finally, the fusion of output features from these two modules can integrate both local details and global context to improve the ability of defect detection in complex scenarios.

Specifically, we employ two strategies: weighted fusion and concatenated fusion to effectively fuse multi-scale features. The idea behind weighted fusion is to dynamically adjust the weights of features at different scales based on the contribution of each scale, ensuring that the model learns the optimal scale-feature ratio during training. The formula is:10\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \textbf{h}_{\text {fused}} = \sum _{i=1}^{n} \alpha _i \textbf{h}_i \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textbf{h}_i$$\end{document} represents the feature at the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$i$$\end{document}th scale, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha _i$$\end{document} is the weight coefficient for that scale feature, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$n$$\end{document} is the number of scales, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textbf{h}_{\text {fused}}$$\end{document} is the final fused feature. This approach allows the model to dynamically distribute weights between features at different scales, effectively avoiding over-reliance on features at any one scale.

Splicing fusion, on the other hand, combines feature vectors from different scales to form a higher-dimensional feature representation, providing more information for subsequent classification and localization tasks. The formula for concatenated fusion is:11\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \textbf{h}_{\text {fused}} = \text {concat}(\textbf{h}_1, \textbf{h}_2, \dots , \textbf{h}_n) \end{aligned}$$\end{document}In concatenated fusion, the feature dimension increases with the number of scales. This also enables the model to obtain more comprehensive feature information when handling more complex and diverse defects. The concatenated high-dimensional features are further processed and classified by subsequent fully connected layers.

To further improve the fusion effect, this paper also introduces an attention mechanism to automatically assign different weights to features at different scales. Based on this self-attention mechanism, we can automatically focus on the scale features most helpful for defect detection during the multi-scale fusion process and ignore less important features, thereby improving detection accuracy. The calculation method for self-attention is similar to the self-attention mechanism in the Transformer:12\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \textbf{A} = \text {softmax}\left( \frac{\textbf{Q} \textbf{K}^\top }{\sqrt{d}} \right) \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textbf{Q}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textbf{K}$$\end{document} are the query and key matrices, respectively, representing the relationship between features at different scales, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$d$$\end{document} is the feature dimension. By calculating the correlation between features at different scales, the model can assign an appropriate weight to each scale, thereby focusing on the most meaningful features during the fusion process.

Finally, the finally fused feature vector is input into the next detection head for the part defect classification and location. Through the above multi-scale feature fusion strategy, the Trans-STFuse method can fully utilize the defects information at different scales, including defects details and global information, which enhances the recognition level of defects of different scales. Especially, the method still has high robustness and accuracy in the case of complex backgrounds and occlusions.

In summary, the multi-scale feature fusion strategy in this paper, through weighted fusion, concatenated fusion and attention mechanism, can make full use of the advantages of features at different scales and enhance the ability of the model to detect vehicle component defects. This method can make the model have high detection accuracy in the case of defects of different scales, and enhance the final feature representation of defects classification and location.

Datasets

In the experiments of this paper, we choose two public datasets to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed Trans-STFuse model, and the two datasets are Car Damage Dataset and DAGM Dataset. These two datasets are representative and cover various scenarios and challenges in vehicle component defect detection, and can fully illustrate the effectiveness of multi-scale feature fusion and spatiotemporal graph convolution in defect detection.

This dataset is used to evaluate the performance of the model on small-scale surface scratches, dents, cracks and other defects, and various backgrounds, and evaluate the application on real scenes.

The defect region in the image is annotated in detail, which is very detailed. The DAGM dataset is used to evaluate the performance of the model on small-scale defects, especially in complex backgrounds and the case where the defect and background are similar.

The two datasets are standard in the field of defect detection and can cover all kinds of scenarios to test the Trans-STFuse model. Experimental details

We evaluate Trans-STFuse on the Car Damage and DAGM datasets. The following details our experimental setup. In the experiments, all image data was first normalized, pixel values were scaled to the range [0, 1], and images were cropped and scaled to fit the model input size requirements. For the Car Damage and DAGM datasets, the datasets were randomly split into training and test sets with an 80%:20% ratio. The training set was used for model training, and the test set was used for model evaluation. Furthermore, data augmentation techniques (such as random rotations, translations, flipping, and color transformations) were applied to the training set to increase the model’s robustness to different scenarios and defect types.

The core of the model consists of the ST-GCN module, the Transformer module, and the multi-scale feature fusion module. For the ST-GCN module, we set up a three-layer graph convolutional network (GCN), using the ReLU activation function after each convolution layer and batch normalization to accelerate training. The graph structure was constructed using a sparse adjacency matrix to improve computational efficiency. The Transformer module configuration includes a six-layer multi-head self-attention mechanism, with eight heads per layer and a dimension of 64 per head. The query, key, and value matrices of the self-attention mechanism are all 256 in dimension, and the hidden layer dimension of the feedforward network is 512. Positional encodings are generated using standard sine and cosine functions to embed positional information.

During training, the Adam optimizer was used, with an initial learning rate of 0.001 and a learning rate decay strategy. Training lasted 50 epochs, with a batch size of 16. Cross-entropy loss was used for classification, and positional regression was performed on the output features to locate defect areas.

The experiments were conducted on a computing platform equipped with an NVIDIA RTX 3090 graphics card, using Python 3.7 and the PyTorch framework. The training process was accelerated by CUDA, ensuring efficiency and stability. Training time varied depending on the dataset size and model complexity, but took approximately 10–12 hours.

Through this experimental setup, we were able to fully validate the effectiveness and practicality of the Trans-STFuse model in vehicle component defect detection, providing reliable performance data for subsequent research and practical applications.

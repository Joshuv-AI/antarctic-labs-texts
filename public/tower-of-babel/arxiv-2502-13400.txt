Introduction

Phase coexistence models were introduced in physics to study the interfaces between regions associated with different values of a suitable state parameter, such as the density of a fluid in capillarity phenomena
[
20
]
, the fraction of volume occupied by two different materials in a non homogeneous system
[
5
]
, or the density of the superfluid component of helium
[
13
]
. Since these early works, phase coexistence models have been developed to describe a variety of physical systems characterized by the interaction of two or more components.

All these models are described by some region
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a state parameter
u:\Omega\to[-1,1]
, where the “pure phases” correspond in our setting to the values
1
and
-1
, and the phase separation is induced by the minimization of a suitable energy functional.

The prototype of such an energy consists of a potential and an interaction term. The role of the potential term is to push the state parameter towards the pure phases, while the interaction term discourages the production of unneeded interfaces by suitably “shaping” the portions of the domain in which the value of the state parameter is either
1
or
-1
(or is “sufficiently close” to these values).

In the local setting, the interaction energy is proportional to a gradient term of
L^{p}
-type with
p\in(1,+\infty)
, see for instance
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
18
,
19
]
. In order to capture long-range interactions, in the nonlocal framework the
L^{p}
-norm of the gradient has been replaced by the Gagliardo seminorm, see for instance
[
17
,
21
,
23
]
. For further references regarding local and nonlocal phase separation models we refer to the survey
[
11
]
.

The potential energy is given by the
L^{1}
-norm of a “double-well” function
W
whose absolute minima are the pure phases. More precisely,
W:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies

W(t)>0\;\;{\mbox{ for every $t\in(-1,1)$}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad W(\pm 1)=0.

(1.1)

In the study of phase coexistence models, it is often desirable to quantify the size of the transition layer with respect to that of the pure phases (or, more precisely, of the regions close to pure phases). This is important since, due to the complexity of the problem, it is often difficult, when not impossible, to have general descriptions of the interfaces and the best one can do is typically to describe the phase separation “in a measure theoretic sense”
and conclude that, at a large scale, most of the space is occupied
by state parameters close to
pure phases, separated by “thin” interfaces. In this spirit, the study of density estimates, namely of obtaining sharp quantifications of the measure of the interface, happens to be quite a cross-disciplinary topic, involving, under various perspectives, mathematical analysis, statistical mechanics, and materials sciences.

In the framework of density estimates a common hypothesis on the potential is that its growth from the minima is comparable to a polynomial of degree
m
, where
m\in(0,p]
and
p\in(1,+\infty)
is the exponent of the interaction term, see for instance formula (1.10) in
[
18
]
for the local case and formula (1.9) in
[
23
]
for the nonlocal one.

In the current paper, we consider the energy functional

\mathcal{E}_{s}^{p}(u,\Omega):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\Omega}W(u(x))\,dx

(1.2)

where
W
presents a polynomial growth from the minima
-1
and
1
of degree
m\in\left[p,+\infty\right)
. We will refer to these potentials as “degenerate”, since they have a “slow” growth rate from their minima (in particular, slower than the homogeneity of the associated interaction energy).

In particular, we prove density estimates for every
p\in(1,+\infty)
and
s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{p}\right)
, see Theorem
1.3
below. The precise hypothesis on
W
, the statement of Theorem
1.3
and its corollaries are all discussed in Section
1.3
. In what follows, we introduce the concept of density estimate and we present in more detail the implications of the presence of a degenerate double-well potential.

1.1
Density estimates

To establish a conceptual framework
in a model case, let us consider the prototype energy functional

\mathcal{E}(u,\Omega):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}W(u(x))\,dx,

(1.3)

with
W
satisfying the assumptions in (
1.1
) and “nondegenerate”
(in the sense that
W
detaches at least quadratically from its minima).
From one point of view, as expected from the shape of the potential
W
, a minimizer for
\mathcal{E}
will be more likely to attain values close to the pure phases, to reduce the overall contribution of
W
in
\Omega
. Yet, from another point of view, the interaction term penalizes “big jumps” of the state parameter. As a consequence of this simultaneous action, the minimizer will be pushed to attain values close to the pure phases but in such a way that at large scales the portion of the domain where the state parameter jumps from one pure phase to the other is negligible.

A common question in physics and mathematics is related to the geometrical features of these interfaces as we “zoom out”. So far, there have a been two approaches to this question: one exploiting
\Gamma
-convergence and the other relying on
density estimates.

On the one hand,
the idea behind
\Gamma
-convergence is to analyse the limit functional obtained by suitably rescaling the minimizers of
\mathcal{E}
and deduce qualitative information regarding the interfaces at large scales. In particular, if
u
minimizes
\mathcal{E}
in
\Omega
, then for
\epsilon\in(0,1)
we consider the rescaled state parameter

u_{\epsilon}(x):=u\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}\right).

In
[
16
]
, and later in greater generality in
[
3
]
, it is proved that there exists some set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
the minimizers
u_{\epsilon}
converge, up to a subsequence, in
L_{\textit{loc}}^{1}(\Omega)
to
1
1

1

As usual, we denote here by
\chi_{A}
the characteristic function (or indicator function) of the set
A
, namely

\chi_{A}(a):=\begin{cases}1&{\mbox{ if }}a\in A,\\
0&{\mbox{ if }}a\not\in A.\end{cases}

a step function
u_{0}:=\chi_{E}-\chi_{E^{c}}
. Interestingly, the interface
\partial E
is a minimal surface, namely a minimizer of the perimeter functional.

On the other hand, the purpose of density estimates is to provide lower bounds for the measure of the portion of the domain characterized by values of the minimizers of
\mathcal{E}
close to the pure phases. The first result in this direction is due to L. Caffarelli and A. Córdoba. In
[
4
]
they prove that if
u:\Omega\to[-1,1]
minimizes
\mathcal{E}
, then, for any
\theta\in(-1,1)
and
r\in(0,+\infty)
sufficiently large, one has that
\{|u|>\theta\}\cap B_{r}
is comparable to
r^{n}

(i.e., to the volume of the full ball
B_{r}
), while
\{|u|\leqslant\theta\}\cap B_{r}
is bounded from above by a constant times
r^{n-1}

(corresponding, for example, to the
(n-1)
-dimensional surface measure in
B_{r}
of a hyperplane passing through the origin). In other words, state parameters close to the pure phases occupy a considerable portion of the domain, while the interface is negligible in the sense of measure.

As a consequence, as the scale parameter
\epsilon\in(0,1)
approaches zero, the set
\{|u_{\epsilon}|\leqslant\theta\}\cap B_{r}

converges in the Hausdorff distance to the hypersurface
\partial E
. More specifically, for any
\delta
,
R>0
there exists
\epsilon_{0}:=\epsilon_{0}(\delta,R)>0
such that, for any
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
,

\left\{\left|u_{\epsilon}\right|\leqslant\theta\right\}\cap B_{R}\subset\bigcup_{x\in\partial E}B_{\delta}(x).

Since the pioneering work of L. Caffarelli and A. Córdoba, density estimates have been used to study the interface convergence of minimizers and quasiminimizers of a larger spectrum of energy functionals related to phase separation. In
[
18
]
and
[
19
]
gradient terms of
L^{p}
-type with
p\in(1,+\infty)
are considered, together also with
\chi
-shaped potentials
W
, namely
W(x):=\chi_{(-1,1)}(x)Q(x)
with
Q
uniformly bounded away from zero.
This type of potentials are related to models for fluid jets and capillarity phenomena; see also
[
25
]
for the elliptic case. In
[
12
]
density estimates are also proved under the weaker assumption of quasiminimality.

As already mentioned, nonlocal counterparts of (
1.3
) have been dealt with in the literature. A consolidated choice consists in replacing the
L^{2}
-norm of the gradient with the Gagliardo seminorm. In
[
22
]
and
[

, for

and

&\qquad\qquad+\int_{\Omega}W(u(x))\,dx.\end{split}

(1.4)

More precisely, in

Interestingly, in Theorem 1.4 of

Additionally, in

In this sense, the case

For what concerns density estimates, the cases

1.2
Degenerate double-well potentials

An interesting problem surrounding the topic of density estimates is how the growth from the minima of the double-well potential
W
might affect phase separation. For example, a slow growth might induce, in some cases, the formation of “intermediate phases”. To picture this, we may consider the extreme situation where, for some
a\in(0,1)
and every
x\in(-1,1)
, the potential is given by

W(x):=\chi_{[-a,a]}(x),

(1.5)

see Figure
1
. In this case, the minimizer will occupy values close to
-a
and
a
to reduce the interaction, whether this is prompted by a nonlocal or local type of interaction term, while the state parameters with value in
(-1,-a)
and
(a,1)
become empty, violating density estimates. In particular, this shows that to obtain density estimates some assumptions on
W
are necessary.

As already mentioned, a common hypothesis on the potential is that if
p\in(1,+\infty)
is the exponent of the interaction term, then, for some
m\in(0,p]
,
\lambda\in(0,1)
and
\Lambda\in[1,+\infty)
, and every
x\in(-1,1)
,

\lambda(1-x^{2})^{m}\leqslant W(x)\leqslant\Lambda(1-x^{2})^{m}.

(1.6)

This corresponds, in the framework of this paper, to a nondegenerate double-well potential,
since the growth from the potential’s minima is not larger than the homogeneity of the interaction term.
In particular, in the classical case of a gradient term of
L^{2}
-type this translates into a (sub)quadratic growth, see
[
4
]
. In such a case, a common double-well potential in the literature is

W(x):=\frac{(1-x^{2})^{2}}{4}.

(1.7)

In view of (
1.6
) and (
1.7
), for
m\in\left[p,+\infty\right)
and
p\in(1,+\infty)
it is natural to define

W(x):=\frac{(1-x^{2})^{m}}{2m}

(1.8)

as a prototype of a degenerate double-well potential. See Figure
1
for a comparison with the potential in (
1.7
).

Figure 1:
Plots of
\chi_{[-1/2,1/2]}(x)
,
(1-x^{2})^{2}
and
(1-x^{2})^{8}
respectively in green, blue and red.

Of course, the conditions in (
1.6
) may be too restrictive, and density estimates may still hold for double-well potentials with a flatter profile close to the minima. This problem has been first addressed in the local setting in
[
10
]
, where the authors prove density estimates for quasiminimizers of a phase transition energy driven by the
L^{p}
-norm of the gradient and with a degenerate double-well potential of the type (
1.8
), as far as some condition on
n
,
m
and
p
are met, see equation (1.7) in
[
10
]
.

The aim of this paper is to obtain density estimates for minimizers of the energy in (
1.2
), when the potential
W
presents a polynomial growth from the minima comparable to the one of the degenerate potential in (
1.8
).

In the following section we introduce all the necessary mathematical notation, state precisely the main results and provide the structure of the paper.

In what follows, for every

we denote by
[t]
the integer part of
t
. Also, for every
z:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
measurable and

, we denote its
p
-fractional Laplacian at

as

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}z(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left(z(x)-z(y)\right)\left|z(x)-z(y)\right|^{p-2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy.

Throughout this section we will also make use of some technical results

.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\tau\in(0,+\infty)
,
p\in(1,+\infty)
,
s\in\left(0,1\right)
and
m\in\left[p,+\infty\right)
there exists
\widehat{C}_{n,p,m}\in(0,+\infty)
such that
if we define

\bar{R}=\bar{R}_{s,\tau,n,p,m}:=\left(\frac{\widehat{C}_{n,p,m}}{s(1-s)\tau}\right)^{\frac{1}{ps}}

(3.1)

the following statement holds true.

For every
R\in\left[\bar{R},+\infty\right)
there exists a rotationally symmetric function
w\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n},(-1,1])
such that

w\equiv 1\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}

(3.2)

and, for every
x\in B_{R}
,

-(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}w(x)\leqslant\tau(1+w(x))^{m-1}.

(3.3)

Also, if we set
q:=\frac{p}{m-1}
and

C_{s,\tau,n,p,m}:=2^{q+1}\max\left\{1,\bar{R}^{qs}\right\},

(3.4)

then,
for every
x\in B_{R}
,

1+w(x)\leqslant\frac{C_{s,\tau,n,p,m}}{\left(1+R-|x|\right)^{qs}}.

(3.5)

The proof of Theorem
3.1
will be based on the following construction.
We take a large
r\in\left[1,+\infty\right)
, to be conveniently chosen with respect to
R
and
\tau
here below (see formula (
3.41
)).

We consider the integer quantity

k=k(s,p):=\begin{cases}0\quad&\mbox{if}\quad p\in(1,2)\mbox{ and }s\in\left(0,\frac{p-1}{2p}\right),\\
\left[\frac{sp}{p-1}\right]+1\quad&\mbox{if}\quad p\in(1,2)\mbox{ and }s\in\left[\frac{p-1}{2p},1\right),\\
1\quad&\mbox{if}\quad p\in[2,+\infty)\mbox{ and }s\in\left(0,1\right).\end{cases}

(3.6)

We set
q:=\frac{p}{m-1}
and we define, for every
t\in(0,r)
,

\begin{split}g(t)&:=t^{-qs}\\
{\mbox{and }}\quad f(t)&:=g(r-t)+\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{i+1}}{i!}g^{(i)}\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)\left(t-\frac{r}{2}\right)^{i}.\end{split}

(3.7)

Furthermore, we also define the functions

\begin{split}h(t)&:=\begin{cases}0\quad&\mbox{if}\quad t\in(0,r/2],\\
\min\left\{f(t),1\right\}\quad&\mbox{if}\quad t\in(r/2,r),\\
1&\mbox{if}\quad t\in[r,+\infty)\end{cases}\\
{\mbox{and }}\quad v(x)&:=h(\left|x\right|)\quad\mbox{for every}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{split}

(3.8)

We provide the following estimate for the function
h
:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
,
p\in(1,+\infty)
,
m\in\left[p,+\infty\right)
and
q:=\frac{p}{m-1}
. Let
h:(0,+\infty)\to[0,1]
be as in (
3.8
).

Then, there exists
\bar{c}_{k,q,s}\in(1,+\infty)
such that, for every
t\in(0,+\infty)
,

\min\left\{1,(r-t)^{-ps}\right\}\leqslant(h(t)+\bar{c}_{k,q,s}r^{-qs})^{m-1}.

(3.9)

Proof.

We observe that if
t\in\left(\frac{r}{2},r\right)
and
h(t)<1
, then

\begin{split}h(t)&=f(t)\\
&=g(r-t)+\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{i+1}}{i!}g^{(i)}\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)\left(t-\frac{r}{2}\right)^{i}\\
&\geqslant(r-t)^{-qs}-\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{\left|g^{(i)}\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)\right|}{i!}\left(t-\frac{r}{2}\right)^{i}\\
&\geqslant(r-t)^{-qs}-\sum_{i=0}^{k}c_{q,s,i}\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^{-qs-i}\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^{i}\\
&=(r-t)^{-qs}-c_{k,q,s}\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^{-qs},\end{split}

(3.10)

where

c_{q,s,i}:=\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}(qs+j)}{i!}\quad\mbox{and}\quad c_{k,q,s}:=\sum_{i=0}^{k}c_{q,s,i}.

(3.11)

On this account, if
t\in\left(\frac{r}{2},r\right)
and
h(t)<1
and we adopt the notation

\tilde{c}_{k,q,s}:=2^{qs}c_{k,q,s},

(3.12)

we can use (
3.10
) to deduce that

(h(t)+\tilde{c}_{k,q,s}r^{-qs})^{\frac{p}{q}}\geqslant((r-t)^{-qs})^{\frac{p}{q}}=(r-t)^{-ps}\geqslant\min\left\{1,(r-t)^{-ps}\right\}.

(3.13)

Moreover, if
h(t)=1
, then we have that

(h(t)+\tilde{c}_{k,q,s}r^{-qs})^{m-1}=(1+\tilde{c}_{k,q,s}r^{-qs})^{m-1}\geqslant 1\geqslant\min\left\{1,(r-t)^{-ps}\right\}.

(3.14)

Finally, if
t\in\left(0,\frac{r}{2}\right]
,

\begin{split}(h(t)+2^{qs}r^{-qs})^{m-1}&=(2^{qs}r^{-qs})^{\frac{p}{q}}=2^{ps}r^{-ps}\geqslant(r-t)^{-ps}\geqslant\min\left\{1,(r-t)^{-ps}\right\}.\end{split}

(3.15)

Gathering these pieces of information, we obtain the estimate in (
3.9
), as desired.

In particular, from (
3.13
), (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) we evince that (
3.9
) holds true with
\bar{c}_{k,q,s}
given by

\bar{c}_{k,q,s}:=\max\left\{2^{qs},\tilde{c}_{k,q,s}\right\}.

(3.16)

This completes the proof of Proposition
3.2
.
∎

The next observation provides a uniform bound in
s

for
\overline{c}_{k,q,s}^{\frac{1}{qs}}
. For this,
we define the constant

\hat{c}_{p,m}:=2e^{c_{p,m}^{(1)}},

(3.17)

where

c_{p,m}^{(1)}:=\left(\left[\frac{p}{p-1}\right]+2\right)\prod_{j=1}^{\left[\frac{p}{p-1}\right]+1}\left(\frac{p}{m-1}+j\right).

(3.18)

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p\in(1,+\infty)
,
m\in\left[p,+\infty\right)
and
q:=\frac{p}{m-1}
.
Let
\bar{c}_{k,q,s}
be given by Proposition
3.2
.

Then, for every
s\in(0,1)
,

\overline{c}_{k,q,s}^{\frac{1}{qs}}\leqslant\hat{c}_{p,m}.

Proof.

To show this, from (
3.16
)
(and recalling also (
3.11
) and (
3.12
)), we evince that

\overline{c}_{k,q,s}=\max\left\{2^{qs},\tilde{c}_{k,q,s}\right\}=2^{qs}\max\left\{1,c_{k,q,s}\right\}\leqslant 2^{qs}\left(1+c_{k,q,s}\right).

(3.19)

Moreover, we notice that, by (
3.6

k\leqslant\left[\frac{sp}{p-1}\right]+1.

Therefore, recalling (
3.11

\begin{split}c_{k,q,s}&\leqslant(k+1)\prod_{j=0}^{k}(qs+j)\\
&\leqslant qs\left(\left[\frac{p}{p-1}\right]+2\right)\prod_{j=1}^{\left[\frac{p}{p-1}\right]+1}\left(\frac{p}{m-1}+j\right)\\

From this and (
3.19
) we obtain that, for every

\overline{c}_{k,q,s}^{\frac{1}{qs}}\leqslant 2\left(1+c_{k,q,s}\right)^{\frac{1}{qs}}\leqslant 2\left(1+qsc_{p,m}^{(1)}\right)^{\frac{1}{qs}}.

\begin{split}&\frac{3p(p-1)}{4^{4-p}}\left|(u-v)(x)-(u-v)(y)\right|^{2}+\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}-\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p}\\
&\qquad\leqslant p(v(x)-v(y))\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p-2}\big(v(x)-v(y)-(u(x)-u(y))\big).\end{split}

(4.7)

Therefore, we define the constant

\frac{3p(p-1)}{4^{4-p}}\quad&\mbox{if}\quad p\in(1,2)\end{cases}

&\qquad\leqslant p(v(x)-v(y))\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p-2}\left(v(x)-v(y)-(u(x)-u(y))\right).\end{split}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy-\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy.

We start with two estimates for the fractional
p
-Laplacian of a bounded and

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\rho\in(0,+\infty)
,
p\in(1,+\infty)
,
s\in\left(0,\frac{p-1}{p}\right)
and
\psi\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and assume that
there exists
\widehat{K}\in(0,+\infty)

such that, for every
y\in B_{\rho}(x)
,

\left|\psi(y)-\psi(x)\right|\leqslant\widehat{K}\left|y-x\right|.

Then, there exists
c_{n,p}\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\left|(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\psi(x)\right|\leqslant\frac{c_{n,p}}{s(p-1-sp)}\left(\widehat{K}^{p-1}\rho^{-sp+p-1}+\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p-1}\rho^{-sp}\right).

(A.1)

Proof.

We compute

\begin{split}&\left|(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\psi(x)\right|\\
&=\left|\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}\frac{\left(\psi(y)-\psi(x)\right)\left|\psi(y)-\psi(x)\right|^{p-2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\rho}(x)}\frac{\left(\psi(y)-\psi(x)\right)\left|\psi(y)-\psi(x)\right|^{p-2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\right|\\
&\leqslant\widehat{K}^{p-1}\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}\frac{dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp-p+1}}+2^{p-1}\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\rho}(x)}\frac{dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\\
&=\frac{\widehat{K}^{p-1}\omega_{n-1}}{p-1-sp}\rho^{-sp+p-1}+2^{p-1}\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p-1}\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{sp}\rho^{-sp}.\end{split}

Now since
sp<p-1
we conclude that

\begin{split}\left|(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\psi(x)\right|&\leqslant\frac{(p-1)\widehat{K}^{p-1}\omega_{n-1}}{sp(p-1-sp)}\rho^{-sp+p-1}+2^{p-1}\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p-1}\frac{(p-1)\omega_{n-1}}{sp(p-1-sp)}\rho^{-sp}\\
&\leqslant\frac{c_{n,p}}{s(p-1-sp)}\left(\widehat{K}^{p-1}\rho^{-sp+p-1}+\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p-1}\rho^{-sp}\right),\end{split}

which is the desired result.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\rho\in(0,+\infty)
,
p\in[2,+\infty)
,
s\in(0,1)
and
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and assume that
there exist
\widehat{K},\widetilde{C}\in(0,+\infty)
and
\Sigma(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that, for every
y\in B_{\rho}(x)
,

\left|\psi(x)-\psi(y)\right|\leqslant\widehat{K}\left|x-y\right|\quad\mbox{and}\quad\psi(y)-\psi(x)-\Sigma(x)\cdot(y-x)\leqslant\widetilde{C}\left|x-y\right|^{2}.

(A.2)

Then, there exists
c_{n,p}\in(0,+\infty)
such that

-(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\psi(x)\leqslant\frac{c_{n,p}}{s(1-s)}\left(\widetilde{C}\max\left\{\left|\Sigma(x)\right|,\widehat{K}\right\}^{p-2}\rho^{-sp+p}+\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p-1}\rho^{-sp}\right).

Proof.

We define the affine approximation of
\psi
at
x
as

\ell(y):=\psi(x)+\Sigma(x)\cdot(y-x).

Then, denoting
\phi_{p}(t):=t|t|^{p-2}
, we obtain that

P.V.\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}\frac{\phi_{p}\left(\ell(y)-\ell(x)\right)}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy=0.

Consequently,

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\phi_{p}\left(\psi(y)-\psi(x)\right)}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\\
=&\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}\frac{\phi_{p}\left(\psi(y)-\psi(x)\right)}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\rho}(x)}\frac{\phi_{p}\left(\psi(y)-\psi(x)\right)}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\\
=&\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}\frac{\phi_{p}\left(\psi(y)-\psi(x)\right)-\phi_{p}\left(\ell(y)-\ell(x)\right)}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\rho}(x)}\frac{\phi_{p}\left(\psi(y)-\psi(x)\right)}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\\
=&:I+I\!I.\end{split}

To estimate
I
, for every
y\in B_{\rho}(x)
we denote by

m:=\min\left\{\psi(y)-\psi(x),\ell(y)-\ell(x)\right\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad M:=\max\left\{\psi(y)-\psi(x),\ell(y)-\ell(x)\right\}.

(A.3)

Then, thanks to the Mean Value Theorem and (
A.2
), we obtain that there exists some
\xi\in[m,M]
such that

\begin{split}\phi_{p}\left(\psi(y)-\psi(x)\right)-\phi_{p}\left(\ell(y)-\ell(x)\right)&\;=\phi_{p}^{\prime}(\xi)\big(\psi(y)-\psi(x)-\ell(y)+\ell(x)\big)\\
&\;=\phi_{p}^{\prime}(\xi)\left(\psi(y)-\ell(y)\right)\\
&\;=\phi_{p}^{\prime}(\xi)\big(\psi(y)-\psi(x)-\Sigma(x)\cdot(y-x)\big)\\
&\;=(p-1)\left|\xi\right|^{p-2}\big(\psi(y)-\psi(x)-\Sigma(x)\cdot(y-x)\big)\\
&\;\leqslant\widetilde{C}(p-1)\left|\xi\right|^{p-2}\left|x-y\right|^{2}.\end{split}

(A.4)

Also, according to (
A.2
) and (
A.3
),

\left|\xi\right|\leqslant\max\left\{\left|\Sigma(x)\right|,\widehat{K}\right\}\left|x-y\right|,

and employing (
A.4
) we obtain that

\phi_{p}\left(\psi(y)-\psi(x)\right)-\phi_{p}\left(\ell(y)-\ell(x)\right)\leqslant\widetilde{C}(p-1)\max\left\{\left|\Sigma(x)\right|,\widehat{K}\right\}^{p-2}\left|x-y\right|^{p}.

On this account, we have that

\begin{split}I&\;\leqslant\widetilde{C}(p-1)\max\left\{\left|\Sigma(x)\right|,\widehat{K}\right\}^{p-2}\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}\frac{dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp-p}}\\
&\;=\widetilde{C}(p-1)\max\left\{\left|\Sigma(x)\right|,\widehat{K}\right\}^{p-2}\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{p-sp}\rho^{-sp+p}.\end{split}

We now estimate
I\!I
, as follows

\begin{split}I\!I&\leqslant 2^{p-2}\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}=2^{p-2}\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p-1}\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{sp}\rho^{-sp}.\end{split}

We thus conclude that

\begin{split}&\!\!\!-(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\psi(x)\\
&\leqslant\widetilde{C}(p-1)\max\left\{\left|\Sigma(x)\right|,\widehat{K}\right\}^{p-2}\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{p-sp}\rho^{-sp+p}+2^{p-1}\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p-1}\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{sp}\rho^{-sp}\\
&=\widetilde{C}s(p-1)\max\left\{\left|\Sigma(x)\right|,\widehat{K}\right\}^{p-2}\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{sp(1-s)}\rho^{-sp+p}+2^{p-1}(1-s)\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p-1}\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{sp(1-s)}\rho^{-sp}\\
&\leqslant\frac{c_{n,p}}{s(1-s)}\left(\widetilde{C}\max\left\{\left|\Sigma(x)\right|,\widehat{K}\right\}^{p-2}\rho^{-sp+p}+\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p-1}\rho^{-sp}\right),\end{split}

as desired.
∎

i\in\left\{0,\dots,k+1\right\}
, we evince that

c_{p,m}:=\frac{\displaystyle\frac{p-1}{2(m-1)}\left(\frac{p-1}{2(m-1)}+1\right)}{\displaystyle\left(\left[\frac{p}{p-1}\right]+2\right)!}\leqslant\widetilde{C}_{q,s,k,i}\leqslant C_{p,m}.

Hence, using also (
A.5
), we deduce that for every
i\in\left\{0,\dots,k+1\right\}
,
\rho_{0}\in\left(0,\frac{r}{2}\right)
and
t\in\left(\frac{r}{2},r-\rho_{0}\right)

c_{p,m}\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^{-qs-k-1}\left(t-\frac{r}{2}\right)^{k+1-i}\leqslant f^{(i)}(t)\leqslant C_{p,m}\,\rho_{0}^{-qs-k-1}\left(t-\frac{r}{2}\right)^{k+1-i}.

(A.14)

Now we notice that, by (
A.11
) and (
A.13
),

C_{i,q,s}\leqslant C_{q,s,k}\leqslant C_{p,m}.

(A.15)

We infer from this and (
A.6
) that for every
x\in\left(\frac{2}{3}r,r\right)
,
\rho:=\frac{r-x}{2}
and
i\in\left\{0,\dots,k+1\right\}

c_{p,m}3^{-qs-k-1}\rho^{-qs-i}\leqslant f^{(i)}(x)\leqslant C_{p,m}2^{-qs-i}\rho^{-qs-i}.

Thus, if we define the constant

\tilde{c}_{p,m}:=c_{p,m}\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{\frac{p}{m-1}+\left[\frac{p}{p-1}\right]+2},

(A.16)

we see that, for every
x\in\left(\frac{2}{3}r,r\right)
and
i\in\left\{0,\dots,k+1\right\}
,

\tilde{c}_{p,m}\rho^{-qs-i}\leqslant f^{(i)}(x)\leqslant C_{p,m}\rho^{-qs-i},

(A.17)

where
\rho:=\frac{r-x}{2}
.

Thanks to these observations, we now provide some auxiliary results
that allow us to estimate the fractional
p
-Laplacian of the function
v

in (
3.8
), as stated in Proposition
3.4
.

Lemma A.5
.

Let
p\in(1,2)
,
s\in\left[\frac{p-1}{2p},1\right)
and
r\in(1,+\infty)
. Let
v:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,1]
be as in (
3.8
). Let
x\in B_{r}
and

\rho:=\frac{r-\left|x\right|}{2}.

(A.18)

Then, there exists
C_{n,p,m}\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}\frac{\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)\left|v(y)-v(x)\right|^{p-2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\leqslant\frac{C_{n,p,m}}{1-s}\rho^{-qs(p-1)-sp}.

(A.19)

Proof.

In what follows, for simplicity of notation, if
A
,
B\in(0,+\infty)
we adopt the notation

A\lesssim B\quad\mbox{if}\quad A\leqslant C_{n,p,m}B

for some
C_{n,p,m}\in(0,+\infty)
depending at most on
n
,
p
and
m
.

Moreover, for every
p\in(1,2)
,
s\in\left[\frac{p-1}{2p},1\right)

and
k
as in (
3.6
), we have that

(k+1)(p-1)-sp\geqslant(1-s)(p-1).

(A.20)

Indeed,

\displaystyle(k+1)(p-1)-sp-(1-s)(p-1)=k(p-1)-s

\displaystyle\qquad=\left(\left[\frac{sp}{p-1}\right]+1\right)(p-1)-s\geqslant\frac{sp}{p-1}(p-1)-s=s(p-1)\geqslant 0,

as desired.

Now, we complete the proof of (
A.19
), which is based on a long and tedious computation, that we provide here below for the facility of the reader.
We will deal separately with the cases
x\in\overline{B}_{\frac{r}{2}}
,
x\in B_{\hat{r}}\setminus\overline{B}_{\frac{r}{2}}
and
x\in B_{r}\setminus B_{\hat{r}}
, where
\hat{r}
has been defined in (
3.25
).

(i) Case
x\in\overline{B}_{\frac{r}{2}}
.

In this case, for every
y\in B_{\rho}(x)
it holds that

\left|y\right|\leqslant\rho+\left|x\right|=\frac{r-\left|x\right|}{2}+\left|x\right|=\frac{r+\left|x\right|}{2}\leqslant\frac{3}{4}r.

Hence, recalling (
3.8
), if
y\in B_{\rho}(x)
,

\displaystyle\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)\left|v(y)-v(x)\right|^{p-2}=\left(h(|y|)-h(|x|)\right)\left|h(|y|)-h(|x|)\right|^{p-2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=h(|y|)|h(|y|)|^{p-2}\leqslant|f(|y|)|^{p-1}\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\frac{r}{2}}}(y).

As a consequence,

\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}\frac{\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)\left|v(y)-v(x)\right|^{p-2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\leqslant\int_{B_{\rho}(x)\setminus B_{\frac{r}{2}}}\frac{|f(|y|)|^{p-1}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy.

We now use (
A.14
) with
\rho_{0}:=r/4
and
i:=0
and we
obtain that

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}\frac{\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)\left|v(y)-v(x)\right|^{p-2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\\
\leqslant\;&C_{p,m}^{p-1}\left(\frac{r}{4}\right)^{-(qs+k+1)(p-1)}\int_{B_{\rho}(x)\setminus B_{\frac{r}{2}}}\frac{\left(\left|y\right|-\frac{r}{2}\right)^{(p-1)(k+1)}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy.\end{split}

Thus, changing variable gives that

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}\frac{\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)\left|v(y)-v(x)\right|^{p-2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\\
\leqslant\;&C_{p,m}^{p-1}\left(\frac{r}{4}\right)^{-(qs+k+1)(p-1)}\int_{B_{\rho}\setminus B_{\frac{r}{2}}(-x)}\frac{(\left|y+x\right|-\frac{r}{2})^{(k+1)(p-1)}}{\left|y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\\
\leqslant\;&C_{p,m}^{p-1}\left(\frac{r}{4}\right)^{-(qs+k+1)(p-1)}\int_{B_{\rho}\setminus B_{\frac{r}{2}}(-x)}\frac{(\left|y\right|+\left|x\right|-\frac{r}{2})^{(k+1)(p-1)}}{\left|y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy.\end{split}

Since
|x|\leqslant r/2
, from this we find that

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}\frac{\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)\left|v(y)-v(x)\right|^{p-2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\\
\leqslant\;&C_{p,m}^{p-1}\left(\frac{r}{4}\right)^{-(qs+k+1)(p-1)}\int_{B_{\rho}}\frac{\left|y\right|^{(k+1)(p-1)}}{\left|y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\\
=\;&\frac{C_{p,m}^{p-1}\,\omega_{n-1}}{(k+1)(p-1)-sp}\left(\frac{r}{4}\right)^{-(qs+k+1)(p-1)}\rho^{(k+1)(p-1)-sp}.\end{split}

(A.21)

Also, in light of (
A.18
)
we have that
\rho\leqslant r/2
, and therefore

\displaystyle r^{-(qs+k+1)(p-1)}\rho^{(k+1)(p-1)-sp}\leqslant\left(2{\rho}\right)^{-(qs+k+1)(p-1)}\rho^{(k+1)(p-1)-sp}=2^{-(qs+k+1)(p-1)}\rho^{-qs(p-1)-sp}.

Using this into (
A.21
)
and recalling (
A.20
),
we see that

\begin{split}\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}\frac{\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)\left|v(y)-v(x)\right|^{p-2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\leqslant\;&\frac{C_{p,m}^{p-1}\,\omega_{n-1}2^{(qs+k+1)(p-1)}}{(k+1)(p-1)-sp}\rho^{-qs(p-1)-sp}\\
\leqslant\;&\frac{C_{p,m}^{p-1}\,\omega_{n-1}2^{(qs+k+1)(p-1)}}{(1-s)(p-1)}\rho^{-qs(p-1)-sp},\end{split}

which gives the desired estimate in (
A.19
).

(ii) Case
x\in B_{\hat{r}}\setminus\overline{B}_{\frac{r}{2}}
.

If we denote by

\displaystyle B_{\rho}^{+}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\big\{y\in B_{\rho}\mbox{ s.t. }\,y\cdot x\geqslant 0\big\}

\displaystyle{\mbox{and }}\qquad B_{\rho}^{-}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\big\{y\in B_{\rho}\mbox{ s.t. }\,y\cdot x<0\big\},

we have that

|y+x|\geqslant|x|

for every
y\in B_{\rho}^{+}
.

(A.22)

Now, we write

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{\rho}}\frac{(v(y+x)-v(x))|v(y+x)-v(x)|^{p-2}}{|y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\\
=\;&\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{\rho}}\frac{(v(y+x)-v(x))|v(y+x)-v(x)|^{p-2}+(v(x-y)-v(x))|v(x-y)-v(x)|^{p-2}}{|y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\\
=\;&\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{\rho}^{+}}\frac{(v(y+x)-v(x))|v(y+x)-v(x)|^{p-2}+(v(x-y)-v(x))|v(x-y)-v(x)|^{p-2}}{|y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{\rho}^{-}}\frac{(v(y+x)-v(x))|v(y+x)-v(x)|^{p-2}+(v(x-y)-v(x))|v(x-y)-v(x)|^{p-2}}{|y|^{n+sp}}\,dy.\end{split}

Since
v
is a radial function, changing variable in the last integral, we find that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}^{-}}\frac{(v(y+x)-v(x))|v(y+x)-v(x)|^{p-2}+(v(x-y)-v(x))|v(x-y)-v(x)|^{p-2}}{|y|^{n+sp}}\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}^{+}}\frac{(v(y+x)-v(x))|v(y+x)-v(x)|^{p-2}+(v(x-y)-v(x))|v(x-y)-v(x)|^{p-2}}{|y|^{n+sp}}\,dy,

and therefore

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{\rho}}\frac{(v(y+x)-v(x))|v(y+x)-v(x)|^{p-2}}{|y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\\
=\;&\int_{B_{\rho}^{+}}\frac{(v(y+x)-v(x))|v(y+x)-v(x)|^{p-2}+(v(x-y)-v(x))|v(x-y)-v(x)|^{p-2}}{|y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\\
=\;&\int_{B_{\rho}^{+}}\frac{\left(h(|y+x|)-h(|x|)\right)\left|h(|y+x|)-h(|x|)\right|^{p-2}+\left(h(|x-y|)-h(|x|)\right)\left|h(|x-y|)-h(|x|)\right|^{p-2}}{|y|^{n+sp}}\,dy.\end{split}

As a result, thanks to (
A.22
)
and the fact that
h
is a non decreasing function,

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{\rho}}\frac{\left(v(y+x)-v(x)\right)\left|v(y+x)-v(x)\right|^{p-2}}{\left|y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\\
=&\int_{B_{\rho}^{+}\cap B_{\left|x\right|}(x)\cap B_{\frac{r}{2}}^{c}(x)}\frac{\left|h(\left|y+x\right|)-h(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-1}-\left|h(\left|x\right|)-h(\left|x-y\right|)\right|^{p-1}}{\left|y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\\
&+\int_{B_{\rho}^{+}\cap B_{\left|x\right|}(x)\cap B_{\frac{r}{2}}(x)}\frac{\left|h(\left|y+x\right|)-h(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-1}-\left|h(\left|x\right|)-h(\left|x-y\right|)\right|^{p-1}}{\left|y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\\
&+\int_{B_{\rho}^{+}\cap B_{\left|x\right|}^{c}(x)}\frac{\left|h(\left|y+x\right|)-h(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-1}+\left|h(\left|x-y\right|)-h(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-1}}{\left|y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy.\end{split}

(A.23)

Now, we claim that

\int_{B_{\rho}^{+}\cap B_{\left|x\right|}(x)\cap B_{\frac{r}{2}}^{c}(x)}\frac{\left|h(\left|y+x\right|)-h(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-1}-\left|h(\left|x\right|)-h(\left|x-y\right|)\right|^{p-1}}{\left|y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\lesssim\frac{\rho^{-qs(p-1)-sp}}{1-s},

(A.24)

\int_{B_{\rho}^{+}\cap B_{\left|x\right|}(x)\cap B_{\frac{r}{2}}(x)}\frac{\left|h(\left|y+x\right|)-h(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-1}-\left|h(\left|x\right|)-h(\left|x-y\right|)\right|^{p-1}}{\left|y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\lesssim\frac{\rho^{-qs(p-1)-sp}}{1-s}

(A.25)

and

\int_{B_{\rho}^{+}\cap B_{\left|x\right|}^{c}(x)}\frac{\left|h(\left|y+x\right|)-h(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-1}+\left|h(\left|x-y\right|)-h(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-1}}{\left|y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\lesssim\frac{\rho^{-qs(p-1)-sp}}{1-s}.

(A.26)

We point out that
if the claims in (
A.24
), (
A.25
) and (
A.26
) hold true, then from (
A.23
) the estimate in (
A.19
) for the case
x\in B_{\hat{r}}\setminus\overline{B}_{\frac{r}{2}}
readily follows.

Hence, from now on, we focus on the proofs of the claims in (
A.24
), (
A.25
) and (
A.26
).

Proof of (
A.24
)
:
For this, we recall (
3.8
) and
we use Proposition
A.3
to find that, for every
y\in B_{\rho}^{+}\cap B_{\left|x\right|}(x)
,

\begin{split}&h(\left|x+y\right|)-h(\left|x\right|)\leqslant f(\left|x+y\right|)-f(\left|x\right|)=\int_{\left|x\right|}^{\left|x+y\right|}f^{\prime}(t)\,dt\\
&\qquad=\int_{\left|x\right|}^{\left|x+y\right|}\left(f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)+\int_{\left|x\right|}^{t}f^{\prime\prime}(\xi)\,d\xi\right)\,dt\\
&\qquad\leqslant f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)\left(\left|y+x\right|-\left|x\right|\right)+f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x+y\right|)\int_{\left|x\right|}^{\left|x+y\right|}\left(\int_{\left|x\right|}^{t}\,d\xi\right)\,dt\\
&\qquad=f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)\left(\left|y+x\right|-\left|x\right|\right)+f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x+y\right|)\frac{\left(\left|x+y\right|-\left|x\right|\right)^{2}}{2}.\end{split}

(A.27)

Also, for every
y\in B_{\rho}^{+}\cap B_{\left|x\right|}(x)\cap B_{\frac{r}{2}}^{c}(x)
, we have that
|x-y|\in\left(\frac{r}{2},\hat{r}\right)
, and so

\begin{split}&h(\left|x\right|)-h(\left|x-y\right|)=f(\left|x\right|)-f(\left|x-y\right|)=\int_{\left|x-y\right|}^{\left|x\right|}f^{\prime}(t)\,dt\\
&\qquad=\int_{\left|x-y\right|}^{\left|x\right|}\left(f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)-\int_{t}^{\left|x\right|}f^{\prime\prime}(\xi)\,d\xi\right)\,dt\\
&\qquad\geqslant f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)\left(\left|x\right|-\left|x-y\right|\right)-f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x\right|)\int_{\left|x-y\right|}^{\left|x\right|}\left(\int_{t}^{\left|x\right|}\,d\xi\right)\,dt\\
&\qquad=f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)\left(\left|x\right|-\left|x-y\right|\right)-f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x\right|)\frac{\left(\left|x\right|-\left|x-y\right|\right)^{2}}{2}.\end{split}

(A.28)

Now, we define

u(x):=2\frac{f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)}{f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x\right|)}

(A.29)

and we notice that, for every
y\in B_{u(x)}
,

f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)-f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x\right|)\frac{\left(\left|x\right|-\left|x-y\right|\right)}{2}\geqslant f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)-f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x\right|)\frac{\left|y\right|}{2}\geqslant 0.

From this, (
A.27
) and (
A.28
)
we deduce that, for every
y\in B_{\rho}^{+}\cap B_{\left|x\right|}(x)\cap B_{\frac{r}{2}}^{c}(x)\cap B_{u(x)}
,

\begin{split}&\left|h(\left|y+x\right|)-h(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-1}-\left|h(\left|x\right|)-h(\left|x-y\right|)\right|^{p-1}\\
\leqslant&\left(\left|x+y\right|-\left|x\right|\right)^{p-1}\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)+f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x+y\right|)\frac{\left|x+y\right|-\left|x\right|}{2}\right|^{p-1}\\
&\quad-\left(\left|x\right|-\left|x-y\right|\right)^{p-1}\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)-f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x\right|)\frac{\left(\left|x\right|-\left|x-y\right|\right)}{2}\right|^{p-1}\\
=&\left(\left|x+y\right|-\left|x\right|\right)^{p-1}\\
&\quad\times\left(\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)+f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x+y\right|)\frac{\left|x+y\right|-\left|x\right|}{2}\right|^{p-1}-\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)-f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x\right|)\frac{\left(\left|x\right|-\left|x-y\right|\right)}{2}\right|^{p-1}\right)\\
&+\big((|x+y|-|x|)^{p-1}-(|x|-|x-y|)^{p-1}\big)\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)-f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x\right|)\frac{\left(\left|x\right|-\left|x-y\right|\right)}{2}\right|^{p-1}\\
\leqslant&|y|^{p-1}\left(\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)+f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x+y\right|)\frac{\left|y\right|}{2}\right|^{p-1}-\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)-f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x\right|)\frac{\left|y\right|}{2}\right|^{p-1}\right)\\
&\quad+\big((|x+y|-|x|)^{p-1}-(|x|-|x-y|)^{p-1}\big)\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-1}.\end{split}

(A.30)

Applying Taylor’s Theorem with the Lagrange remainder we obtain that

\begin{split}&\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)+f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x+y\right|)\frac{\left|y\right|}{2}\right|^{p-1}\\
=\;&\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-1}+(p-1)\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-2}f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x+y\right|)\frac{\left|y\right|}{2}\\
&\qquad+\frac{(p-1)(p-2)}{8}\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)+b\right|^{p-3}f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x+y\right|)^{2}\left|y\right|^{2}\\
\leqslant\;&\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-1}+(p-1)\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-2}f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x+y\right|)\frac{\left|y\right|}{2}\\
&\qquad+\frac{(p-1)(2-p)}{8}\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)+b\right|^{p-3}(f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x+y\right|))^{2}\left|y\right|^{2}\\
\leqslant\;&\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-1}+(p-1)\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-2}f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x+y\right|)\frac{\left|y\right|}{2}\\
&\qquad+\frac{(p-1)(2-p)}{8}\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-3}(f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x+y\right|))^{2}\left|y\right|^{2},\end{split}

(A.31)

for some
b\in\left(0,f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x+y\right|)\frac{\left|y\right|}{2}\right)
.

Also, for every
\xi\in\left(0,f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x\right|)\frac{\left|y\right|}{2}\right)
,

\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)-\xi\right|\geqslant\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)\right|-\xi\geqslant f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)-f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x\right|)\frac{\left|y\right|}{2}\geqslant 0.

Hence, thanks to Taylor’s Theorem we find that, for some
a\in\left(0,f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x\right|)\frac{\left|y\right|}{2}\right)
,

\begin{split}&\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)-f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x\right|)\frac{\left|y\right|}{2}\right|^{p-1}\\
=\;&\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-1}-(p-1)\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-2}f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x\right|)\frac{\left|y\right|}{2}+\frac{(p-1)(p-2)}{8}\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)-a\right|^{p-3}(f^{\prime\prime}(|x|))^{2}|y|^{2}.\end{split}

From this, (
A.30
) and (
A.31
) we conclude that, for every
y\in B_{\rho}^{+}\cap B_{\left|x\right|}(x)\cap B_{\frac{r}{2}}^{c}(x)\cap B_{u(x)}
,

\begin{split}&\left|h(\left|y+x\right|)-h(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-1}-\left|h(\left|x\right|)-h(\left|x-y\right|)\right|^{p-1}\\
\leqslant\;&\frac{p-1}{2}\left|y\right|^{p}\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-2}\big(f^{\prime\prime}(|x+y|)+f^{\prime\prime}(|x|)\big)\\
&+\frac{(p-1)(2-p)}{8}\left|y\right|^{p+1}\left(f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)^{p-3}(f^{\prime\prime}(|x+y|))^{2}+\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)-f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x\right|)\frac{\left|y\right|}{2}\right|^{p-3}(f^{\prime\prime}(|x|))^{2}\right)\\
&+\big((|x+y|-|x|)^{p-1}-(|x|-|x-y|)^{p-1}\big)\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-1}.\end{split}

Therefore, we deduce that, for every
w\in\left(0,u(x)\right)\cap(0,\left|x\right|-\frac{r}{2})\cap(0,\rho)
,

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{\rho}^{+}\cap B_{\left|x\right|}(x)\cap B_{\frac{r}{2}}^{c}(x)}\frac{\left|h(\left|y+x\right|)-h(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-1}-\left|h(\left|x\right|)-h(\left|x-y\right|)\right|^{p-1}}{\left|y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\\
\leqslant\;&\frac{p-1}{2}\int_{B_{w}^{+}\cap B_{\left|x\right|}(x)\cap B_{\frac{r}{2}}^{c}(x)}\frac{\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-2}\left(f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x+y\right|)+f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x\right|)\right)}{\left|y\right|^{n+sp-p}}\,dy\\
&+\frac{(p-1)(2-p)}{8}\int_{B_{w}^{+}\cap B_{\left|x\right|}(x)\cap B_{\frac{r}{2}}^{c}(x)}\frac{f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)^{p-3}f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x+y\right|)^{2}+\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)-f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x\right|)\frac{\left|y\right|}{2}\right|^{p-3}f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x\right|)^{2}}{\left|y\right|^{n+sp-p-1}}\,dy\\
&+\int_{B_{w}^{+}\cap B_{\left|x\right|}(x)\cap B_{\frac{r}{2}}^{c}(x)}\frac{\left(\left(\left|x+y\right|-\left|x\right|\right)^{p-1}-\left(\left|x\right|-\left|x-y\right|\right)^{p-1}\right)\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)\right|^{p-1}}{\left|y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\\

(A.32)

\displaystyle r^{-(p-1)(qs+k+1)}{\tilde{x}}^{k(p-3)}\big((\tilde{x}+|y|)^{2(k-1)}+{\tilde{x}}^{2(k-1)}\big).

As a consequence,

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{w(x)}^{+}\cap B_{\left|x\right|}(x)\cap B_{\frac{r}{2}}^{c}(x)}\frac{f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)^{p-3}f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x+y\right|)^{2}+\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)-f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x\right|)\frac{\left|y\right|}{2}\right|^{p-3}f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x\right|)^{2}}{\left|y\right|^{n+sp-p-1}}\,dy\\

)
when
x\in B_{\frac{2}{3}r}\setminus\overline{B}_{\frac{r}{2}}

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{w(x)}^{+}\cap B_{\left|x\right|}(x)\cap B_{\frac{r}{2}}^{c}(x)}\frac{f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)^{p-3}f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x+y\right|)^{2}+\left|f^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)-f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x\right|)\frac{\left|y\right|}{2}\right|^{p-3}f^{\prime\prime}(\left|x\right|)^{2}}{|y|^{n+sp-p-1}}\,dy\\

Now we prove (

To show the claim in (
A.44

x=\left|x\right|e_{n}

We also compute that

(A.48)

for some
\eta
,
\xi\in(0,y_{n})
.

Furthermore, by (
A.37
), we have that, for every

(A.49)

Therefore, thanks to (
A.47
), (
A.48
) and (

), and applying Taylor’s Theorem once again, we infer that, for every

\begin{split}&\left(\left|\frac{x}{|x|}+y\right|-1\right)^{p-1}-\left(1-\left|\frac{x}{|x|}-y\right|\right)^{p-1}\\

As a consequence of a change of variable and (

We set
s_{0}:=\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{q2^{q+1}c_{p,m}^{(1)}}\right\}

and we observe that, for every
s\in(0,s_{0})
,

1-\frac{qsc_{p,m}^{(1)}2^{q}}{qsc_{p,m}^{(1)}2^{q}+1}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}

and therefore

l(s)\geqslant\big(1-qsc_{p,m}^{(1)}2^{q}\big)^{\frac{1}{qs}}.

Taking the liminf as
s\to 0
, we obtain that

\liminf_{s\to 0}l(s)\geqslant e^{-c_{p,m}^{(1)}2^{q}}.

From this, the fact that
l\in C((0,1],(0,+\infty))
and (
A.57
), we obtain (
A.56
), as desired.
∎

Corollary A.7
.

Let
p\in(1,2)
,
s\in\left[\frac{p-1}{2p},1\right)
,
r\in(1,+\infty)
and
\mu\in(0,+\infty)
. Let
v:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,1]
be defined as in (
3.8
).

Then, there exists
C_{\mu,n,p,m}\in(0,+\infty)

such that, for every
x\in B_{r}
,

\int_{B_{\mu}(x)}\frac{\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)\left|v(y)-v(x)\right|^{p-2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\leqslant\frac{C_{\mu,n,p,m}}{1-s}.

(A.58)

Proof.

We recall that, if
\hat{r}
is given as in (
3.25
), then, for every
x\in B_{r}\setminus B_{\hat{r}}
and every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

v(y)-v(x)=v(y)-1\leqslant 0.

Consequently, for every
x\in B_{r}\setminus B_{\hat{r}}
and
\mu\in(0,+\infty)
,

\int_{B_{\mu}(x)}\frac{\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)\left|v(y)-v(x)\right|^{p-2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\leqslant 0,

and (
A.58
) plainly follows.

Furthermore, in virtue of (
A.19
) and Lemma
A.6
,
if
\rho:=\frac{r-\left|x\right|}{2}
we have that,
for every
x\in B_{\hat{r}}\setminus\overline{B}_{\frac{r}{2}}
,

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}\frac{\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)\left|v(y)-v(x)\right|^{p-2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\leqslant C_{n,p,m}\frac{\rho^{-qs(p-1)-sp}}{p-sp}\\
&\qquad\qquad\leqslant\frac{C_{n,p,m}}{p-sp}\left(\frac{r-\hat{r}}{2}\right)^{-qs(p-1)-sp}\leqslant\frac{C_{n,p,m}}{p-sp}\left(\frac{\widehat{C}_{p,m}}{2}\right)^{-qs(p-1)-sp}.\end{split}

Moreover, if
\mu\in[\rho,+\infty)
,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\mu}(x)\setminus B_{\rho}(x)}\frac{\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)\left|v(y)-v(x)\right|^{p-2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\leqslant\int_{B_{\mu}(x)\setminus B_{\rho}(x)}\frac{dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leqslant\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{sp}\rho^{-sp}\leqslant\frac{1}{p-1}\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{p-sp}\rho^{-sp}\leqslant\frac{1}{p-1}\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{p-sp}\left(\frac{\widehat{C}_{p,m}}{2}\right)^{-sp}.

From the last two displays we thus obtain that, if
\mu\in[\rho,+\infty)
,

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{\mu}(x)}\frac{\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)\left|v(y)-v(x)\right|^{p-2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\\
\leqslant\;&\frac{C_{n,m,p}}{p-sp}\left(\frac{\widehat{C}_{p,m}}{2}\right)^{-qs(p-1)-sp}+\frac{1}{p-1}\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{p-sp}\left(\frac{\widehat{C}_{p,m}}{2}\right)^{-sp}\end{split}

which gives the desired estimate.

Similarly, if
\mu\in(0,\rho)
,

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{\mu}(x)}\frac{\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)\left|v(y)-v(x)\right|^{p-2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\\
=\;&\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}\frac{\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)\left|v(y)-v(x)\right|^{p-2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy-\int_{B_{\rho}(x)\setminus B_{\mu}(x)}\frac{\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)\left|v(y)-v(x)\right|^{p-2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\,dy\\
\leqslant\;&\frac{C_{n,m,p}}{p-sp}\left(\frac{\widehat{C}_{p,m}}{2}\right)^{-qs(p-1)-sp}+2^{p-1}\int_{B_{\rho}(x)\setminus B_{\mu}(x)}\frac{dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+sp}}\\
\leqslant\;&\frac{C_{n,m,p}}{p-sp}\left(\frac{\widehat{C}_{p,m}}{2}\right)^{-qs(p-1)-sp}+\frac{2^{p-1}}{p-1}\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{p-sp}\mu^{-sp},\end{split}

as desired.
∎

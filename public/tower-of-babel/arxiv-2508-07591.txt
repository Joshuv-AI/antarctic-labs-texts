Weighted eigenvalues of Dirac operators: complete continuity and comparison Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject classification: 35P05, 35F05, 47A75, 49R05
Zixuan Qiu, Ruijun Wu

Abstract
. We give a min-max characterization of all of the weighted Dirac eigenvalues, and show that the weighted eigenvalues and eigenspaces of Dirac operators are continuous with respect to weak L p L^{p} convergence of the inverse weights, for any p > n p>n . Moreover, we establish a comparison result for such weighted eigenvalue problems when there are no harmonic spinors.



Keywords
: weighted eigenvalue, min-max characterization, complete continuity, comparison of eigenvalues, eigen projectors

1.
Introduction

We consider a weighted eigenvalue problem for Dirac operators of the form

(1)

\displaystyle\not{D}_{g}\psi=\lambda A\psi,\qquad\mbox{ in }M,

where
(M,g)
is a spin Riemannian manifold, with or without boundary,
\not{D}_{g}
is the Dirac operator on the given spinor bundle
\Sigma_{g}M
and
\psi\in\Gamma(\Sigma_{g}M)
is a spinor field,
A\in\End(\Sigma_{g}M)
is a symmetric endomorphism of the spinor bundle which is positive definite fiberwisely.
It is well known that (
1
) has a sequence of real eigenvalues which diverge to both
+\infty
and
-\infty
, and the eigenspinors constitute a complete basis of
L^{2}
space of spinors, see e.g.
[
13
,
25
]
.

Equations of the form (
1
) arise in various geometric and variational problems.
For example, in the local spinorial Weierstrass representation of surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

[
10
,
30
,
31
]
, the associated spinor
\psi
satisfies an equation of the form

(2)

\displaystyle\not{D}_{g}\psi=H\psi

and has constant length, where
H
is a scalar function which turns out to be the mean curvature of the embedded surface.
Also in super Liouville equations
[
14
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
, which are our main motivation, the spinor has to satisfy

(3)

\displaystyle\not{D}_{g}\psi=\lambda e^{u}\psi

for some
u\in H^{1}(M,g)
and some
\lambda>0
.
In the nonlinear spinorial Yamabe problem
[
4
,
16
,
17
]
, we need to consider the linearized operator and its eigenvalues, which takes the form

(4)

\displaystyle\not{D}_{g}\psi=\lambda|\varphi|^{\frac{2}{n-1}}\psi

where
\varphi
is a nonzero solution to the spinorial Yamabe equation.
There are, of course, other nonlinear weighted eigenvalue problems for Dirac type operators which takes more complicated forms.
In the present work we focus on the weighted linear eigenvalue problem of the general form (
1
), though the special case where
A
reduces to a positive function, i.e.
A=\rho\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}_{+})
, is more common in various context.

When
A=\id
, this reduces to the classical eigenvalue problem of Dirac operators, which has been a source of new mathematics in the last fifty years, including the Friedrich’s eigenvalue estimates, the heat flow approach to index theory, the spectral flow and eta invariants, and so on.
We cannot list all the literature here but refer to e.g.
[
13
]
and the references therein.

The continuous dependence on various parameters is also the theme of a lot of works, among them we mention the continuity of the Dirac spectrum in the Riemannian metrics shown by Nowaczyk
[
28
]
.
Note that the spectral map has to be chosen carefully and the space of all spectra is endowed with an arsinh metric.
In differential geometry, it is usually dealing with smooth deformations and hence the eigenvalues, sometimes even the eigenspinors, are smooth with the deformation parameter.
This is in particular the case in the study of spectral flows
[
29
]
.
It is intriguing to ask, as is usually the situation in analysis, what happens if the deformation is not smooth but merely continuous in some weak sense.

Concerning the weighted eigenvalue problem (
1
), it is natural to ask
in what sense of continuous deformation of the weights
A
will the eigenvalues, or even the eigenspaces, would remain continuous.

In the present work we try to give a weak sufficient condition.

We remark that the continuity of eigenvalues and eigenfunctions on parameters is a classical subject which is fundamental in analysis and geometry.
In
[
6
]
the one-dimensional case is carefully analyzed.
Lou and Yanagida
[
27
]
make an important progress where the weights may fail to be definite but they still establish continuity and comparison properties of the weighted eigenvalues.
The weighted eigenvalue problems for Laplacian operators play an important role in variational and geometric problems, see
[
1
,
26
]

Weighted eigenvalue problems of other operators are also considered in e.g.
[
7
]
.

To state our result we take the following hypothesis:

(H)

\displaystyle A_{m},\,A\mbox{ are symmetric, positive definite},

(5)

\displaystyle\mbox{ and }A_{m}^{-1}\rightharpoonup A^{-1},A_{m}\rightharpoonup A\mbox{ weakly in }L^{p},\mbox{ for some }p>n.

The weak
L^{p}
convergence of
A_{m}
to
A
means that for any spinors
\phi\in L^{q},\eta\in L^{r}
with
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}=1-\frac{1}{p}
, we have

(6)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left<A_{m}\,\phi,\eta\right>\dv_{g}\to\int_{M}\left<A\,\phi,\eta\right>\dv_{g},\qquad\mbox{ as }m\to+\infty.

Equivalently, the pointwise norm function
x\mapsto|A_{m}(x)|_{op}
(as operators on finite dimensional vector spaces) converges weakly to
|A(x)|
.
We will use the latter description in the context.
In particular, when
A_{m}=H_{m}\id_{\Sigma_{g}M}
, this reduces to the weak
L^{p}
convergence of
L^{p}
functions
H_{m}
.

We will write
\Eigen(L;\lambda)
for the eigenspace of an operator
L
associated to eigenvalue
\lambda
.
Moreover, we will see in Section
2
and Section
5
that the weighted eigenvalues for Dirac operators, both in closed and boundary case, can be listed as

(7)

\displaystyle-\infty\leftarrow\cdots\leq\lambda_{2}(A)\leq\lambda_{1}(A)<0<\lambda_{1}(A)\leq\lambda_{2}(A)\leq\cdots\to+\infty.

where
\lambda_{j}(A)
are the nonzero eigenvalues counted with multiplicities.
We denote the
\ell
-th disctinct eigenvalues by
\mu_{\ell}(A)
, which are also listed in a strictly increasing order, and let
H_{\ell}(A)=\Eigen(A^{-1}\not{D}_{g};\mu_{\ell})
be the associated eigenspace with
h_{\ell}=\dim H_{\ell}(A)
, and
P_{\ell}(A)
the associated projector.
That is, for
\ell>0
, let
\sigma_{\ell}=\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}h_{p}
, then

(8)

\displaystyle H_{1}(A)=

\displaystyle\Span\left\{\varphi_{j}(A)\mid 0<j\leq h_{1}\right\},

(9)

\displaystyle H_{\ell}(A)=

\displaystyle\Span\left\{\varphi_{j}(A)\mid\sigma_{\ell-1}<j\leq\sigma_{\ell}\right\},\quad\ell>1,

for
\ell<0
, the eigenspaces and projectors are similarly defined.
Moreover, for each
A_{m}
, instead of collecting the eigenspaces according to the eigenvalues of
A_{m}^{-1}\not{D}_{g}
, we collect them according to the eigenvalues of
A^{-1}\not{D}_{g}
, namely

(10)

\displaystyle H_{1}(A_{m})=

\displaystyle\Span\left\{\varphi_{j}(A_{m})\mid 0<j\leq h_{1}\right\},

(11)

\displaystyle H_{\ell}(A_{m})=

\displaystyle\Span\left\{\varphi_{j}(A_{m})\mid\sigma_{\ell-1}<j\leq\sigma_{\ell}\right\},\quad\ell>1,

and
H_{\ell}(A_{m})
for
\ell<0
is similarly defined.
The projector onto
H_{\ell}(A_{m})
is denoted by
P_{\ell}(A_{m})
for each
\ell\neq 0
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed Riemannian spin manifold,
(\Sigma_{g}M,g^{s},\not{\nabla},\gamma)
be a spinor bundle over
M
and
A_{m},A
be weights satisfying (
H
).
Then, for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
,

(i)

\lambda_{k}(A_{m})\to\lambda_{k}(A)
as
m\to+\infty
;

(ii)

\varphi_{k}(A_{m})
converges to
\Eigen(A^{-1}\not{D}_{g};\lambda_{k}(A))
, more precisely,

(12)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to+\infty}\inf_{\phi\in\Eigen(A^{-1}\not{D}_{g};\lambda_{k}(A))}\|\varphi_{k}(A_{m})-\phi\|_{C^{\alpha}}=0,

where
\alpha\in(0,1)
is given in Proposition
3.1
, and

(13)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to+\infty}\inf_{\phi\in\Eigen(A^{-1}\not{D}_{g};\lambda_{k}(A))}\|\varphi_{k}(A_{m})-\phi\|_{H^{1}}=0.

(iii)

As projection operators in
H^{1}
, we have

(14)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to+\infty}P_{\ell}(A_{m})=P_{\ell}(A),\quad\forall\;\ell\neq 0.

Some comments are in order.
Firstly, if the weights
A_{m}
converges to
A
in
C^{1}
, then it is well known that the corresponding weighted eigenvalues converges, as well as the associated projectors to the corresponding eigenspaces, see e.g.
[
23
]
.
The point here is that when the weights converge only weakly, we still have the continuity.
Such continuity are referred to as
complete continuity
in functional analysis
[
32
,
34
]
.
Thus what we obtain is the complete continuity of the eigenvalue, the eigenspinors as well as the eigenprojectors with respect to the weights.
Secondly, our hypothesis about the convergence of
A_{m}^{-1}
is not the usual one (namely
A_{m}\rightharpoonup A
).
This is because the Dirac operator is strongly indefinite and we have to use the min-max description given in Proposition
2.1
where
A^{-1}
appears naturally.
But note that

(15)

\displaystyle A_{m}^{-1}-A^{-1}=-A^{-1}(A_{m}-A)A_{m}^{-1}.

Thus if
A_{m}\rightharpoonup A
weakly in
L^{p}
and
A^{-1},A_{m}^{-1}
are uniformly bounded, then
A_{m}^{-1}\rightharpoonup A^{-1}
.
On the other hand, under the hypothesis (
H
),
A_{m}^{-1},A^{-1}
are clearly uniformly bounded.
Thirdly, a similar statement can be made for the weighted eigenvalues for Laplacian operators on manifolds, using Remark
2.3
.

In general the result may fail for
p=n
.
At least in our approach the a priori estimates fail in this limit case.
It is interesting to construct explicit counterexamples.

In case
M
comes with a smooth boundary
\partial M
, we need to impose suitable boundary conditions.
Different from the situation of spectral flows in odd dimensions where the Atiyah-Patodi-Singer boundary conditions are usually used, here we impose a local boundary condition, namely the chiral boundary condition

(16)

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}^{+}\psi=0,\qquad\mbox{ on }\;\partial M.

Here
\mathbf{B}^{+}
stands for the chiral operator defined along
\partial M
, see Section
5
.
In this case a similar statement holds:

Theorem 1.2
.

The weighted
chiral
eigenvalues and eigenspaces of (
1
) are continuous with respect to continuous deformations of the weights
A
and
A^{-1}
in the weak
L^{p}
topology in the sense of (
H
), for any
p>n
.

The more precise statement is given in Theorem
5.5
.

The outline of the proof is similar to that in
[
34
]
where the complete continuity of Laplacian eigenvalue problems in a bounded Euclidean domain with Dirichlet boundary conditions was considered.
While the Laplacian operator with Dirichlet boundary condition is a positive operator and enjoys nice min-max characterizations, the Dirac operator is strongly indefinite and requires special care.
The spectrum of this weighted eigenvalue problem consists of infinitely many negative eigenvalues, making it hard to start with the min-max procedure as usual.
We generalize Ammann’s characterization of the first positive eigenvalues, and obtain a new characterization of all nonzero weighted eigenvalues in Proposition
2.4
for the weighted closed eigenvalues and Proposition
5.3
for the weighted chiral eigenvalues, for which the theory is more involved.
We will first prove that
\lambda_{k}(A_{m})^{-1}
converges to
\lambda_{k}(A)^{-1}
, then show the first positive eigenvalues will not converge to zero, hence giving a uniform lower bound for positive eigenvalues; finally we conclude the convergence of eigenvalues.
By establishing uniform a priori estimates for the weighted eigenspinors, we can finally show the convergence of the eigenspinors to some limit contained in the corresponding eigenspaces.
Conversely, any
A
-weighted eigenspinor is shown to be approximated by some
A_{m}
-weighted eigenspinors.
This will be shown by a contradition argument.
Thus the orthogonal projectors converge as operators, proving the continuity of eigenspaces.

As a byproduct of the above min-max characterization of weighted eigenvalues, we prove a comparison result for the eigenvalues of (
1
), which is also motivated by the analysis of weighted eigenvalue of Laplacian operator with Dirichlet boundary conditions in Euclidean domains.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
A_{1},A_{2}\in\End(\Sigma_{g}M)
be smooth, symmetric, and positive definite fiberwisely.
Suppose in addition that
\ker\not{D}_{g}=0
.
If
A_{1}\geq A_{2}
, then for each
k>0

[
resp.
k<0
]
,

(17)

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}(A_{1})\leq\lambda_{k}(A_{2}),

\displaystyle[\mbox{resp.}\qquad\lambda_{k}(A_{1})\leq\lambda_{k}(A_{2}).\;]

The assumption that
\ker\not{D}_{g}=0
is for technical reasons; we will remark on this later.

There are still questions remaining open in this issue.
Among the most urgent ones is the Hölder continuity and differentiability with respect to the weight function.
This is widely desirable in variational and geometric problems.
We leave it to a future work.

The paper is organized as follows.
We first give a min-max characterization of the nonzero weighted eigenvalues in Section
2
and a priori estimates of the weighted eigenspinors in Section
3
.
Then we move to prove the weak continuity of weighted eigenvalues and eigenspaces, for the closed manifolds in Section
4
and for manifolds with boundary in Section
5
.
Finally we discuss some applications of the continuity results in Section
6
.

2.
Weighted eigenvalue problems for Dirac operators

We will first be concerned with the closed eigenvalue problems.
The boundary eigenvalue problems will come later in a parallel way.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed spin Riemannian manifold, with a given spin spinor bundle
\Sigma_{g}M
.
We will think of
\Sigma_{g}M
as a real vector bundle of rank
2^{[\frac{n+1}{2}]}
, and temporarily forget about its hermitian structure.
There exists a Riemannian structure, i.e. a fiberwise inner product
g^{s}
, a spin connection
\not{\nabla}
, and a Clifford multiplication
\gamma
satisfying the Clifford relation

(18)

\displaystyle\gamma(X)\gamma(Y)+\gamma(Y)\gamma(X)=-2g(X,Y)\id_{\Sigma_{g}M},\qquad\forall X,Y\in\Gamma(TM).

Moreover, they are compatible with each other and hence
(\Sigma_{g}M,g^{s},\not{\nabla},\gamma)
is a Dirac bundle in the sense of
[
25
, Definition 5.1]
.
Thus we can define the Dirac operator as the composition of the following arrows

(19)

\displaystyle\Gamma(\Sigma_{g}M)\xrightarrow{\not{\nabla}}\Gamma(T^{*}M\otimes\Sigma_{g}M)\xrightarrow{\cong}\Gamma(TM\otimes\Sigma_{g}M)\xrightarrow{\gamma}\Gamma(\Sigma_{g}M).

In terms of a local orthonormal frame
(e_{i})
, we have

(20)

\displaystyle\not{D}\psi=\sum_{i}\gamma(e_{i})\not{\nabla}_{e_{i}}\psi,\qquad\forall\;\psi\in\Gamma(\Sigma_{g}M).

The Dirac operator is a first order elliptic operator, and is essentially self-adjoint in the space of
L^{2}
spinors provided
M
is closed, see e.g.
[
11
,
13
]
.

Let
A\in\Gamma(\End_{\mathbb{R}}(\Sigma_{g}M))
be a fiberwise
symmetric, positive definite
endomorphism of the spinor bundle, with fiberwise inverse
A^{-1}
.
Consider the eigenvalue problems

(21)

\displaystyle A^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\varphi_{k}=\lambda_{k}\varphi_{k}.

Since
\not{D}_{g}
is elliptic, so is
A^{-1}\not{D}_{g}
in the symbolical sense.
Moreover, since
A
is symmetric, the eigenvalues are real and discrete, which diverges to both
+\infty
and
-\infty
.
We will be concerned with the continuous dependence on the weight
A
, thus let us denote the associated eigenvalues resp. eigenspinors by
\lambda_{k}(A)
resp.
\varphi_{k}(A)
.
As in with classical unweighted case, we list the nonzero eigenvalues, counted with multiplicities, in an increasing manner

(22)

\displaystyle-\infty\leftarrow\cdots\leq\lambda_{2}(A)\leq\lambda_{1}(A)<0<\lambda_{1}(A)\leq\lambda_{2}(A)\leq\cdots\to+\infty.

The kernel of the Dirac operator,
\Ker(\not{D}_{g})
, is independent of
A
and may be zero.
Denote
h_{0}\coloneqq\dim\ker\not{D}_{g}
.
In case
h_{0}\neq 0
, we denote a basis for
\ker\not{D}_{g}
by
\theta_{j}
,
j=1,\cdots,h_{0}
.
Moreover, we assume that these eigenspinors satisfy

(23)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left<A\varphi_{j}(A),\varphi_{k}(A)\right>_{g^{s}}\dv_{g}=\delta_{jk},\quad\forall j,k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\left\{0\right\},

(24)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left<A\theta_{j},\theta_{k}\right>_{g^{s}}\dv_{g}=\delta_{jk},\forall j,k\in\left\{1,2,\cdots,h_{0}\right\},

(25)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left<A\varphi_{k}(A),\theta_{j}\right>_{g^{s}}\dv_{g}=0,\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\left\{0\right\},\;\forall j\in\left\{1,\cdots,h_{0}\right\}.

These can be achieved via Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization procedure, noting that
A
is symmetric.
They form a complete orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(M,\Sigma_{g}M)
.
Indeed, any
L^{2}
spinor field
\psi
admits a unique decomposition

(26)

\displaystyle\psi=\sum_{k\neq 0}a_{k}\,\varphi_{k}(A)+\sum_{j=1}^{h_{0}}b_{j}\,\theta_{j}

and
\not{D}_{g}
acts on such spinors formally as

(27)

\displaystyle\not{D}_{g}\psi=\sum_{k\neq 0}\lambda_{k}(A)\;a_{k}\;\varphi_{k}(A).

When
A=\id_{\Sigma_{g}M}
, this reduces to the classical Fourier expansion of a spinor via unweighted eigenspinors.
The spinor with regularity
H^{s}
(for
s>0
) can be defined via the unweighted eigenspinor expansions
[
3
]
, which is readily seen to be equivalently described by

(28)

\displaystyle\psi\in H^{s}\Longleftrightarrow\sum_{k\neq 0}|\lambda_{k}|^{2s}|a_{k}|^{2}+\sum_{j}|b_{j}|^{2}<+\infty.

The spinor bundles have a complex structure in dimensions
n\equiv 1,5\pmod{8}
, and have a quaternionic structure in dimensions
n\equiv 2,3,4\pmod{8}
, see
[
25
, Proposition II.3.10]
.
Thus it is common that the real multiplicities of the eigenvalues of Dirac operator are greater than one.
Even modulo the complex or quaternionic structures, it is still not easy to get the multiplicities to one generically, see
[
8
]
.
This is in contrast to the Laplacian operator on Riemannian manifolds, whose eigenvalues are generically simple
[
33
]
.
We have to take care of the multiplicities in our treatment of the convergence problems.

The strong indefiniteness of the Dirac operator is the root for many technical problems in geometry and analysis.
The classical min-max description of eigenvalues usually fails since the operator is neither bounded from above nor from below.
In some cases where the spectrum of
\not{D}_{g}
is symmetric with respect to the origin, which is the case for
n\not\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, one can consider the nonnegative operator
\not{D}_{g}^{2}
, which is sometimes called Dirac-Laplacian, and extract the eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}
of
\not{D}_{g}
from the eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}^{2}
of
\not{D}_{g}^{2}
, see e.g.
[
13
, Chapter 5]
.
This approach unfortunately fails for weighted eigenvalues.
There is another characterization of the positive Dirac eigenvalues using the notion of
Dirac sea
which refers to the subspaces spanned by such eigenspinors of negative eigenvalues.
One has to restrict to the orthogonal complement of this Dirac sea
[
9
]
.
This is however not explicit and again not convenient for our weighted eigenvalue problems.

In
[
3
]
B. Ammann used the dual variational principle to obtain another min-max description of the first positive eigenvalue of Dirac operator:

(29)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}(g)}=\sup\left\{\frac{\int_{M}\left<\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\left<\not{D}_{g}\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}\mid\psi\in H^{1}(\Sigma_{g}M)\setminus\Ker\not{D}_{g}\right\}.

This is an intuitive formulation, which will be generalized soon to describe all the eigenvalues of Dirac operators, in particular for the weighted ones.
We remark that this formulation is related to several variational problems, among them we mention the spinorial Yamabe problem which is about the extremal of the normalized Dirac eigenvalues in the conformal class.

The positive weighted eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}(A)
can be similarly obtained using a min-max principle.
Let
Gr^{*}_{k-1}(A)
denote the Grassmann manifold of
(k-1)
-planes in
H^{1}(\Sigma_{g}M)
which are perpendicular to
\ker\not{D}_{g}
with respect to the weighted inner product induced by
A
, namely

(30)

\displaystyle Gr^{*}_{k-1}(A)\coloneqq\left\{V\subset H^{1}(\Sigma_{g}M)\mid V\mbox{ is a subspace, }V\perp_{A}\Ker(\not{D}_{g}),\;\dim V=k-1\right\}.

Here
V\perp_{A}\ker\not{D}_{g}
means, for any
\psi\in V
and any
\theta\in\ker\not{D}_{g}
, we have

(31)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left<A\psi,\theta\right>_{g^{s}}\dv_{g}=0.

Proposition 2.1
.

The
k
-th positive weighted eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}(A)
is characterized by

(32)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}(A)}=

\displaystyle\inf_{V\in Gr^{*}_{k-1}(A)}\;\sup_{0\neq\psi\perp_{A}(V\oplus\ker\not{D}_{g})}\left\{\frac{\int_{M}\left<\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\left<A^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}\right\}.

Proof.

We temporarily denote the right hand side above by
\nu_{k}
and aim to prove
\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}(A)}=\nu_{k}
.

Let
V\in Gr_{k-1}^{*}
.
Then in the
k
-dimensional space
\Span\left\{\varphi_{1}(A),\cdots,\varphi_{k}(A)\right\}
which is automatically
A
-orthogonal to
\ker\not{D}_{g}
, there exists an element
\psi_{k}
which is orthogonal to
V
in the sense that

(33)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left<A\psi_{k},\phi\right>_{g^{s}}\dv_{g}=0,\qquad\forall\phi\in V\oplus\ker\not{D}_{g}.

Writing
\psi_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\,\varphi_{j}(A)
, we see that

(34)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\neq\psi\perp_{A}V}\left\{\frac{\int_{M}\left<\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\left<A^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}\right\}\geq\frac{\int_{M}\left<\psi_{k},\not{D}_{g}\psi_{k}\right>\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\left<A^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\psi_{k},\not{D}_{g}\psi_{k}\right>\dv_{g}}=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}(A)\,a_{j}^{2}}{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}(A)^{2}a_{j}^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}(A)}.

Hence
\nu_{k}\geq\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}(A)}
.

By taking
V_{*}=\Span\left\{\varphi_{1}(A),\cdots,\varphi_{k-1}(A)\right\}
, and
\psi=\varphi_{k}(A)\perp_{A}(V_{*}\oplus\ker\not{D}_{g})
, we see that

(35)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\neq\psi\perp_{A}(V_{*}\oplus\ker\not{D}_{g})}\left\{\frac{\int_{M}\left<\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\left<A^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}\right\}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}(A)}.

Thus
\nu_{k}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}(A)}
.
∎

In particular, we see from the proof that the infimum is attained by taking

(36)

\displaystyle V_{k-1}(A)=\Span\left\{\varphi_{1}(A),\cdots,\varphi_{k-1}(A)\right\}.

This fact will be used in the sequel.

Remark 2.2
.

A similar statement holds for the negative eigenvalues:

(37)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{-k}(A)}=

\displaystyle\sup_{V\in Gr^{*}_{k-1}(A)}\;\inf_{0\neq\psi\perp_{A}(V\oplus\ker\not{D}_{g})}\left\{\frac{\int_{M}\left<\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\left<A^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}\right\}.

Remark 2.3
.

There is a similar min-max characterization of the weighted eigenvalues for Laplacian operators, either on closed manifolds:

(38)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{g}u_{k}(a)=\lambda_{k}(a)\,a\,u_{k}(a),\qquad\mbox{ in }M.

where
a\in C^{\infty}(M)
is a positive weight function.
The kernel consists of constant functions.
Then the positive weighted eigenvalues are characterized by

(39)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}(-\Delta_{g})}=\sup_{V\in Gr^{*}_{k-1}(a)}\inf_{0\neq u\perp_{a}(\mathbb{R}\oplus V)}\left\{\frac{\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}a^{-1}|\Delta_{g}u|^{2}\dv_{g}}\right\}.

This is not as convenient as the classical one.

A similar argument gives another equivalent min-max characterization of the weighted eigenvalues, whose proof is omitted.

Proposition 2.4
.

The
k
-th positive weighted eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}(A)
is characterized by

(40)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}(A)}=\inf_{W\in Gr^{*}_{k}(A)}\;\sup_{0\neq\psi\perp_{A}\ker\not{D}_{g},\psi\in W}\left\{\frac{\int_{M}\left<\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\left<A^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}\right\},

and the
k
-th negative weighted eigenvalues
\lambda_{-k}(A)
is characterized by

(41)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{-k}(A)}=\sup_{W\in Gr^{*}_{k}(A)}\;\inf_{0\neq\psi\perp_{A}\ker\not{D}_{g},\psi\in W}\left\{\frac{\int_{M}\left<\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\left<A^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}\right\}.

As an application of the above minmax characterization of eigenvalues, we obtain a comparison principle for such weighted eigenvalues of the Dirac operator.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

This follows directly from Proposition
2.4
.
Note that since
\ker\not{D}_{g}=0
, the Grassmannian
Gr^{*}_{k}(A)=Gr_{k}
is the classical Grassmannian, independent of
A
.
Then since
A_{1}^{-1}\leq A_{2}^{-1}
, the weighted Rayleigh quotient has the relation

(42)

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{\int_{M}\left<\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\left<A_{1}^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}\right\}\geq\left\{\frac{\int_{M}\left<\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\left<A_{2}^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}\right\}

for each
\psi\in W
and each
W\in Gr_{k}
.
Taking supremum over
\psi\in W
and then infimum over
W\in Gr_{k}
, we obtain

(43)

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}(A_{1})\leq\lambda_{k}(A_{2}),

as desired.
The case
k<0
is similar.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

The assumption
\ker\not{D}_{g}=0
seems unnatural in this comparison principle and should be dropped.
In case
\ker\not{D}_{g}\neq 0
, the Grassmannian
Gr^{*}_{k}(A)
depends on the weight
A
and the comparison between the Rayleigh quotients becomes unclear to us for the moment.

As a consequence of the above comparison principle, we see that the weighted eigenvalues for the Dirac operator have the same asymptotics as the unweighted one as
k\to\pm\infty
.

3.
A priori estimates for the weighted eigenspinors

In this section we establish an a priori estimates of the
H^{1}
norms and the
C^{\alpha}
norms of weighted eigenspinors.
These will be used in the proof of the complete continuity of eigenpairs.
The crucial point here is the dependence on the weights and on the eigenvalues.

For the moment we omit the index
k
and consider a solution
\varphi
of the equation

(44)

\displaystyle\not{D}\varphi=\lambda\,A\,\varphi\qquad\mbox{ in }M

and a normalization condition

(45)

\displaystyle 1=\int_{M}\left<A\,\varphi,\varphi\right>\dv_{g}=\int_{M}|\sqrt{A}\,\varphi|^{2}\dv_{g}.

Here
\sqrt{A}
stands for the unique symmetric positive definite square root of
A
.
By the standard elliptic regularity theory we get the following estimates in terms of the eigenvalue
\lambda
and the weight
A
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\varphi\in H^{1/2}(\Sigma_{g}M)
be a solution of (
44
) where
A,A^{-1}\in L^{p}(M,\End(\Sigma_{g}M))
for some
p>n
.
There exist
\alpha=\alpha(n)\in(0,1)
and
C=C(n,p,g)
such that

(46)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,2}(M)}\leq C(n,p,g)\left(1+\lambda\|A\|_{L^{p}}\right)^{T_{1}}\left(\|A^{-1}\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda\|A\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

and

(47)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{C^{\alpha}(M)}\leq C(n,p,g)\left(1+\lambda\|A\|_{L^{p}}\right)^{T_{2}}\left(\|A^{-1}\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda\|A\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right),

where

(48)

\displaystyle T_{1}=\left[\frac{\frac{n}{2}}{p-n}\right],

\displaystyle T_{2}=\left[\frac{n(p-1)}{2(p-n)}\right].

Proof.

Since
\not{D}
is elliptic, we know that for any
q>1
,

(49)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,q}(M)}\leq C(q,g)\left(\|\varphi\|_{L^{q}}+\|\not{D}\varphi\|_{L^{q}}\right)

see e.g.
[
2
]
.
Repeated applications of such estimates would give the desired results.

To start with, we write
q_{1}=\frac{2p}{p+1}
and note that

(50)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{L^{q_{1}}}=\|\sqrt{A^{-1}}\sqrt{A}\varphi\|_{L^{q_{1}}}\leq\|\sqrt{A^{-1}}\|_{L^{2p}}\|\sqrt{A}\,\varphi\|_{L^{2}}=\|\sqrt{A^{-1}}\|_{L^{2p}}\leq\|A^{-1}\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

and

(51)

\displaystyle\|\not{D}_{g}\varphi\|_{L^{q_{1}}}=

\displaystyle\|\lambda\,A\,\varphi\|_{L^{q_{1}}}\leq\lambda\|\sqrt{A}\|_{L^{2p}}\|\sqrt{A}\,\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\leq\lambda\|A\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Therefore,

(52)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,q_{1}}}\leq C(q_{1},g)\left(\|A^{-1}\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda\|A\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

The Sobolev embedding theorem implies that

(53)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{L^{r_{1}}}\leq C(n,q_{1},g)\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,q_{1}}}\leq C(n,p,g)\left(\|A^{-1}\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda\|A\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right),

where
\frac{1}{r_{1}}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}-\frac{1}{n}
.

Let
1<q_{2}<r_{1}
be such that
\frac{1}{q_{2}}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{r_{1}}
.
Then

(54)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,q_{2}}}\leq

\displaystyle C(q_{2},g)\left(\|\varphi\|_{L^{q_{2}}}+\lambda\|A\;\varphi\|_{L^{q_{2}}}\right)\leq C(q_{2},g)\left(C(p,g)+\lambda\|A\|_{L^{p}}\right)\|\varphi\|_{L^{r_{1}}}

(55)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(q_{2},n,p,g)\left(1+\lambda\|A\|_{L^{p}}\right)\left(\|A^{-1}\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda\|A\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Hence for
\frac{1}{r_{2}}=\frac{1}{q_{2}}-\frac{1}{n}
, we have

(56)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{L^{r_{2}}}\leq C(q_{2},n,g)\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,q_{2}}}\leq C(q_{2},n,p,g)\left(1+\lambda\|A\|_{L^{p}}\right)\left(\|A^{-1}\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda\|A\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Iterating this procedure
j\geq 1
times, we obtain

(57)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,q_{j}}}\leq C(q_{j},n,p,g)\left(1+\lambda\|A\|_{L^{p}}\right)^{j-1}\left(\|A^{-1}\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda\|A\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right),

with

(58)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q_{j}}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}-\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}\right)(j-1).

Since
p>n
,
q_{j}
is increasing, and after finitely many times, we will get as large
q_{j}
as we need.

(1) To get the
W^{1,2}
estimate we take
j_{1}
such that
q_{j_{1}}\geq 2
, say,

(59)

\displaystyle j_{1}=\left[\frac{\frac{n}{2}}{p-n}\right]+1\equiv T_{1}+1.

Then

(60)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,2}}\leq C(n,p,g)\left(1+\lambda\|A\|_{L^{p}}\right)^{T_{1}}\left(\|A^{-1}\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda\|A\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

(2) To get a Hölder estimate, we take
j_{2}
such that
q_{j_{2}}>n
, for example,

(61)

\displaystyle j_{2}=\left[\frac{n(p-1)}{2(p-n)}\right]+1\equiv T_{2}+1.

Then by the Sobolev embedding

(62)

\displaystyle W^{1,q_{j_{0}}}\hookrightarrow C^{1-\frac{n}{q_{j_{2}}}}\equiv C^{\alpha},

we see that
\varphi\in C^{\alpha}
, with

(63)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{C^{\alpha}}\leq C(n,p,g)\left(1+\lambda\|A\|_{L^{p}}\right)^{T_{2}}\left(\|A^{-1}\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda\|A\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

∎

Remark 3.2
.

The constants in the estimates are universal, independent of
\lambda,A
and
\varphi
.
This will be used in later applications.

However, the above estimates may not be optimal.
One may consider to use the Moser iteration argument, as did in
[
34
]
to get a better power of the factor
(1+\lambda\|A\|_{L^{p}})
.

When we get a uniform lower bound of
\lambda
, then we may also replace the factor
(1+\lambda\|A\|_{L^{p}})
by
(\lambda\|A\|_{L^{p}})
.

The Hölder index
\alpha
can be better improved. But in general we cannot hope to get uniform
C^{\beta}
estimate for all
\beta\in(0,1)
if only
\|A\|_{L^{p}}
and
\|A^{-1}\|_{L^{p}}
are involved.

4.
Continuity of weighted eigenvalues and eigenspinors of Dirac operator

In this section we consider the continuous dependence of the eigenvalues and eigenspinors on the weight
A
and prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We divide the proof into the following steps.

Step I.
We show that for any
k\geq 1
, the sequence
\left(\lambda_{k}(A_{m})\right)_{m\geq 1}
is bounded.
Since it is a positive sequence, it suffices to show that

(64)

\displaystyle\limsup_{m\to+\infty}\lambda_{k}(A_{m})\leq\lambda_{k}(A).

Indeed, for each
m\geq 1
, consider the test spinors of the form

(65)

\displaystyle\eta^{(m)}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}a^{(m)}_{j}\varphi_{j}(A),

where the coefficients
\left(a^{(m)}_{j}\right)
are chosen so that

(66)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left<A_{m}\eta^{(m)},\varphi_{j}(A_{m})\right>\dv_{g}=0,\qquad 1\leq j\leq k-1,

and

(67)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left<A_{m}\eta^{(m)},\eta^{(m)}\right>\dv_{g}=1.

This last normalization condition is just to guarantee that the coefficients of
\eta^{(m)}
do not vanish simultaneously.
Then

(68)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}(A_{m})}=

\displaystyle\sup_{0\neq\psi\perp(V_{k-1}(A_{m})\oplus\ker\not{D}_{g})}\left\{\frac{\int_{M}\left<\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\left<A_{m}^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}\right\}\geq\frac{\int_{M}\left<\eta^{(m)},\not{D}_{g}\eta^{(m)}\right>\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\left<A_{m}^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\eta^{(m)},\not{D}_{g}\eta^{(m)}\right>\dv_{g}}.

On one hand we have

(69)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left<\eta^{(m)},\not{D}_{g}\eta^{(m)}\right>\dv_{g}=\int_{M}\left<\eta^{(m)},\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}(A)\,A\,\varphi_{j}(A)\right>\dv_{g}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}(A)\,[a_{j}^{(m)}]^{2}.

On the other hand, for the denominator,

(70)

\displaystyle\int_{M}

\displaystyle\left<A_{m}^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\eta^{(m)},\not{D}_{g}\eta^{(m)}\right>\dv_{g}=\int_{M}\left<A^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\eta^{(m)},\not{D}_{g}\eta^{(m)}\right>+\left<(A_{m}^{-1}-A^{-1})\not{D}_{g}\eta^{(m)},\not{D}_{g}\eta^{(m)}\right>\dv_{g}

(71)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}[\lambda_{j}(A)]^{2}[a_{j}^{(m)}]^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(A)\,a_{i}^{(m)}\;\lambda_{j}(A)\,a_{j}^{(m)}\int_{M}\left<(A_{m}^{-1}-A^{-1})A\,\varphi_{i}(A),A\,\varphi_{j}(A)\right>\dv_{g}

(72)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}[\lambda_{j}(A)]^{2}[a_{j}^{(m)}]^{2}+B^{(m)}_{k}\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(A)\,a_{i}^{(m)}\;\lambda_{j}(A)\,a_{j}^{(m)}

where we have abbreviated

(73)

\displaystyle B^{(m)}_{k}\coloneq\max_{1\leq i,j\leq m}\left|\int_{M}\left<(A_{m}^{-1}-A^{-1})A\,\varphi_{i}(A),A\,\varphi_{j}(A)\right>\dv_{g}\right|.

By (
H
), we have
B^{(m)}_{k}\to 0
as
m\to+\infty
for fixed
k\geq 1
.
Then using Cauchy inequality we have

(74)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left<A_{m}^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\eta^{(m)},\not{D}_{g}\eta^{(m)}\right>\dv_{g}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}[\lambda_{j}(A)]^{2}[a_{j}^{(m)}]^{2}+kB^{(m)}_{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}[\lambda_{j}(A)]^{2}[a_{j}^{(m)}]^{2}

(75)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+kB^{(m)}_{k})\sum_{j=1}^{k}[\lambda_{j}(A)]^{2}[a_{j}^{(m)}]^{2}.

Consequently,

(76)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}(A_{m})}\geq\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}(A)\,[a_{j}^{(m)}]^{2}}{\sum_{j=1}^{k}[\lambda_{j}(A)]^{2}[a_{j}^{(m)}]^{2}}\frac{1}{1+kB^{(m)}_{k}}\geq\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}(A)}\frac{1}{1+kB^{(m)}_{k}}.

It follows that

(77)

\displaystyle\liminf_{m\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}(A_{m})}\geq\lim_{m\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}(A)}\frac{1}{1+kB^{(m)}_{k}}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}(A)},

which is equivalent to

(78)

\displaystyle\limsup_{m\to+\infty}\lambda_{k}(A_{m})\leq\lambda_{k}(A).

Step II.
For a fixed
k\geq 1
, we show that
\{\varphi_{k}(A_{m})\}_{m\geq 1}
is a bounded sequence in
H^{1}(M)
and
C^{\alpha}(M)
for some
\alpha=\alpha(n)\in(0,1)
.

Note that each
\varphi_{k}(A_{m})
is a solution to a linear equation

(79)

\displaystyle\not{D}_{g}\varphi_{k}(A_{m})=\lambda_{k}(A_{m})\,A_{m}\,\varphi_{k}(A_{m}),\qquad\mbox{ in }M

and satisfies a normalization condition

(80)

\displaystyle 1=\int_{M}\left<A_{m}\;\varphi_{k}(A_{m}),\varphi_{k}(A_{m})\right>\dv_{g}=\int_{M}|\sqrt{A_{m}}\;\varphi_{k}(A_{m})|^{2}\dv_{g}.

Here
\sqrt{A_{m}}
stands for the symmetric positive definite square root matrix of
A_{m}
.
Apply Proposition
3.1
we get estimates on the
H^{1}
norm and
C^{\alpha}
norms in terms of
\lambda_{k}(A_{m})
and
\|A_{m}\|_{L^{p}}
,
\|A_{m}^{-1}\|_{L^{p}}
.
We have seen in the last step that
\lambda_{k}(A_{m})
are uniformly bounded.
As for the weights, since
A_{m}\rightharpoonup A
and
A_{m}^{-1}\rightharpoonup A
weakly in
L^{p}
, we see that
\|A_{m}\|_{L^{p}}
,
\|A_{m}^{-1}\|_{L^{p}}
are all bounded.
Thus,
\{\varphi_{k}(A_{m})\}_{m\geq 1}
are bounded in
H^{1}
as well as in
C^{\alpha}
.

Step III.
Having shown the boundedness of
\left\{\lambda_{k}(A_{m})\mid m\geq 1\right\}
and
\left\{\varphi_{k}(A_{m})\mid m\geq 1\right\}
, we can extract a subsequence
\{m_{l}\}_{l\geq 1}
such that

(81)

\displaystyle\lim_{l\to+\infty}\lambda_{k}(A_{m_{l}})=\lambda_{\infty},

and

(82)

\displaystyle\varphi_{k}(A_{m_{l}})\to\varphi_{\infty}\qquad\mbox{ in }H^{1}(M)\cap C^{\alpha}(M).

Then in this step we show that
\lambda_{\infty}=\lambda_{k}(A)
and
\varphi_{\infty}\in\Eigen(A^{-1}\not{D}_{g};\lambda_{k}(A))
.

For any smooth test spinor
\eta
, we have

(83)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left<\not{D}_{g}\varphi_{\infty},\eta\right>\dv_{g}=

\displaystyle\lim_{l\to+\infty}\int_{M}\left<\not{D}_{g}\varphi_{k}(A_{m_{l}}),\eta\right>\dv_{g}

(84)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{l\to+\infty}\int_{M}\left<\lambda_{k}(A_{m_{l}})\,A_{m_{l}}\,\varphi_{k}(A_{m_{l}}),\eta\right>\dv_{g}

(85)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\lambda_{\infty}\left<A\,\varphi_{\infty},\eta\right>\dv_{g}.

That is,
\varphi_{\infty}\in H^{1}\cap C^{\alpha}
is a weak solution of

(86)

\displaystyle\not{D}_{g}\varphi_{\infty}=\lambda_{\infty}\,A\,\varphi_{\infty},

which satisfies

(87)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left<A\varphi_{\infty},\,\varphi_{\infty}\right>\dv_{g}=\lim_{l\to+\infty}\int_{M}\left<A_{m_{l}}\,\varphi_{k}(A_{m_{l}}),\,\varphi_{k}(A_{m_{l}})\right>\dv_{g}=1.

Thus
\varphi_{\infty}
is an eigenspinor of
\not{D}_{g}
with weight
A
for the eigenvalue
\lambda_{\infty}
.

It remains to show
\lambda_{\infty}=\lambda_{k}(A)
.
We prove this by induction.

For
k=1
: we know that
0\leq\lambda_{\infty}\leq\lambda_{1}(A)
. If
\lambda_{\infty}\neq\lambda_{1}(A)
, then
\lambda_{\infty}=0
and
\varphi_{\infty}
is a harmonic spinor.
This is impossible since, for any harmonic spinor
\theta\in\ker\not{D}_{g}
,

(88)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left<A\,\varphi_{\infty},\theta\right>\dv_{g}=\lim_{l\to+\infty}\int_{M}\left<A_{m_{l}}\,\varphi_{1}(A_{m_{l}}),\theta\right>\dv_{g}=0,\quad\mbox{contradiction!}

Thus
\lambda_{\infty}=\lambda_{1}(A)
and
\varphi_{\infty}\in\Eigen(A^{-1}\not{D}_{g},\lambda_{1}(A))
.

Inductively suppose the statements hold for
1,2,\cdots,k-1
.
As argued above, we see that
\varphi
is
A
-orthogonal to the subspace
\Span\left\{\varphi_{1}(A),\cdots,\varphi_{k-1}(A)\right\}
, hence
\lambda_{\infty}\geq\lambda_{k}(A)
and it follows that

(89)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\infty}=\lambda_{k}(A),

and

\displaystyle\varphi_{\infty}\in\Eigen(A^{-1}\not{D}_{g},\lambda_{k}(A)).

Step IV.
Now we show the convergence of the full sequences
(\lambda_{k}(A_{m}))
to
\lambda_{k}(A)
.
This indeed follows from the well known fact that if any subsequence has a further sub-subsequence converging to a fixed limit, then this original sequence converges to the same limit.
A proof can also be found in
[
34
]
.

The convergence for the negative eigenvalues follows analogously. Applying the min–max characterization for the negative eigenvalues (Remark
37
) within the framework of
Step I.
-
Step IV.
yields the corresponding inequality

(90)

\displaystyle\liminf_{m\to\infty}\lambda_{k}(A_{m});\geq;\lambda_{k}(A),\qquad\forall,k<0,

thereby establishing the result for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}
.

We should remark that the convergence of the sequence
\varphi_{k}(A_{m})
in general fails, since the eigenvalues of Dirac operators in general has multiplicities greater than one, and one can easily construct examples where the original sequence
\varphi_{k}(A_{m})
fails to converge.
A possible remedy for this is by using the concept of eigen projectors.

Step V.
We prove the continuity of the projectors.

In
Step III
it is shown that
\lim\limits_{m\to+\infty}P_{\ell}(A)^{\perp}P_{\ell}(A_{m})=0
, thus it suffices to have the converse inclusion

(91)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to+\infty}P^{\perp}_{\ell}(A_{m})P_{\ell}(A)=0.

Equivalently, for any
\psi\in H_{\ell}(A)
with
\int_{M}\left<A\psi,\psi\right>\dv_{g}=1
, we need to show that
P^{\perp}_{\ell}(A_{m})(\psi)\to 0
in
L^{2}
, as
m\to+\infty
.
By induction we suppose this holds for
1,2,\cdots,\ell-1
, and we prove the case for
\ell\geq 1
. The case
\ell=1
can be proved using similar but easier argument.

For each
m\geq 1
, we expand
\psi\in H_{\ell}(A)
as

(92)

\displaystyle\psi=\sum_{k\neq 0}a^{(m)}_{k}\varphi_{k}(A_{m})+\sum_{j=1}^{h_{0}}b_{j}^{(m)}\theta_{j}.

Note that (
H
) implies

(93)

\displaystyle 1=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left<A\psi,\psi\right>\dv_{g}=\lim_{m\to+\infty}\int_{M}\left<A_{m}\psi,\psi\right>\dv_{g}=\sum_{k\neq 0}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum_{j}b_{j}^{(m)}b_{i}^{(m)}\int_{M}\left<A_{m}\theta_{j},\theta_{i}\right>\dv_{g}

(94)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma_{\ell-1}<k\leq\sigma_{\ell}}|a^{(m)}_{k}|^{2}+\sum_{k\leq\sigma_{\ell-1}}|a^{(m)}_{k}|^{2}+\sum_{\sigma_{\ell}<k}|a^{(m)}_{k}|^{2}+\sum_{k<0}|a^{(m)}_{k}|^{2}

(95)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{h_{0}}b_{j}^{(m)}b_{i}^{(m)}\int_{M}\left<A_{m}\theta_{j},\theta_{i}\right>\dv_{g}.

The first sum is precisely
\|P_{\ell}(A_{m})\psi\|_{L^{2}_{A}}^{2}
, and it remains to show the other sums disappear in the limits.
Thanks to (
H
) we have

(96)

\displaystyle 0=\int_{M}\left<A\psi,\theta_{j}\right>\dv_{g}=\lim_{m\to+\infty}\int_{M}\left<A_{m}\psi,\theta_{j}\right>\dv_{g}=\lim_{m\to+\infty}b_{j}^{(m)},\qquad\forall 1\leq j\leq h_{0}.

By the inductive assumption,

(97)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to+\infty}P_{i}(A_{m})\psi=P_{i}(A)\psi=0,\qquad\forall 1\leq i\leq\ell-1.

Hence,

(98)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to+\infty}\sum_{k\leq\sigma_{\ell-1}}|a^{(m)}_{k}|^{2}=0.

Thus, we can consider the spinors

(99)

\displaystyle\phi_{m}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma_{\ell-1}<k\leq\sigma_{\ell}}a_{k}^{(m)}\,\varphi_{k}(A_{m})+\sum_{\sigma_{\ell}<k}a_{k}^{(m)}\,\varphi_{k}(A_{m})+\sum_{k<0}a_{k}^{(m)}\,\varphi_{k}(A_{m})

(100)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi-\delta_{m}

which satisfies
\delta_{m}\to 0
in
H^{1}
(since
\delta_{m}
actually lies in a finite dimensional subspace) and hence

(101)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to+\infty}\int_{M}\left<A_{m}\,\phi_{m},\,\phi_{m}\right>\dv_{g}=1,

and

(102)

\displaystyle\phi_{m}\perp_{A_{m}}\ker\not{D}_{g},

\displaystyle\phi_{m}\perp_{A_{m}}\Span\left\{\varphi_{1}(A_{m}),\cdots,\varphi_{\sigma_{\ell-1}}(A_{m})\right\}.

Moreover,

(103)

\lim_{m\to+\infty}\int_{M}\left<\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m},\phi_{m}\right>\dv_{g}=\mu_{\ell}(A),

(104)

\lim_{m\to+\infty}\int_{M}\left<A_{m}^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m},\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m}\right>\dv_{g}=\mu_{\ell}(A)^{2}.

We claim that

(105)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to+\infty}\sum_{\sigma_{\ell}<k}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum_{k<0}|a^{(m)}_{k}|^{2}=0

which would complete the proof.

Indeed, set

(106)

\displaystyle r_{k}\coloneqq\limsup_{m\to+\infty}\sum_{\sigma_{\ell}<k}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum_{k<0}|a^{(m)}_{k}|^{2}

and assume by contradiction that
r_{k}>0
.
Passing to a subsequence we may assume

(107)

\displaystyle r_{k}\coloneqq\lim_{m\to+\infty}\sum_{\sigma_{\ell}<k}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum_{k<0}|a^{(m)}_{k}|^{2}>0.

Then

(108)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left<\not{D}_{g}\,\phi_{m},\phi_{m}\right>\dv_{g}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma_{\ell-1}<k\leq\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda_{k}(A_{m})|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum_{\sigma_{\ell}<k}\lambda_{k}(A_{m})|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}

(109)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k<0}\lambda_{k}(A_{m})|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}.

while

(110)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left<A_{m}^{-1}\,\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m},\,\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m}\right>\dv_{g}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma_{\ell-1}<k\leq\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda_{k}(A_{m})^{2}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum_{\sigma_{\ell}<k}\lambda_{k}(A_{m})^{2}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}

(111)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k<0}\lambda_{k}(A_{m})^{2}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}.

Scaling these equations yields:

(112)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m},\phi_{m}\rangle\dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{\ell}(A_{m})\sum_{\sigma_{\ell-1}<k\leq\sigma_{\ell}}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum_{k>\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda_{k}(A_{m})|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}

(113)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle A_{m}^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m},\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m}\rangle\dv_{g}

\displaystyle\geq[\mu_{\ell}(A_{m})]^{2}\sum_{\sigma_{\ell-1}<k\leq\sigma_{\ell}}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\mu_{\ell}(A_{m})\sum_{k>\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda_{k}(A_{m})|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}

(114)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k<0}|\lambda_{k}(A_{m})|^{2}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}.

We define

(115)

\displaystyle X_{m}=

\displaystyle\mu_{\ell}(A_{m})\sum_{\sigma_{\ell-1}<k\leq\sigma_{\ell}}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum_{k>\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda_{k}(A_{m})|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2},

(116)

\displaystyle Y_{m}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k<0}|\lambda_{k}(A_{m})|^{2}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}.

Therefore, we have

(117)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\int_{M}\langle\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m},\phi_{m}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}}{\int_{M}\langle A_{m}^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m},\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}}

(118)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\sum\limits_{\sigma_{\ell-1}<k\leq\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda_{k}(A_{m})|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum\limits_{k>\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda_{k}(A_{m})|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum\limits_{k<0}\lambda_{k}(A_{m})|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}}{\sum\limits_{\sigma_{\ell-1}<k\leq\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda_{k}(A_{m})^{2}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum\limits_{k>\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda_{k}(A_{m})^{2}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum\limits_{k<0}\lambda_{k}(A_{m})^{2}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}}

(119)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\mu_{\ell}(A_{m})\sum\limits_{\sigma_{\ell-1}<k\leq\sigma_{\ell}}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum\limits_{k>\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda_{k}(A_{m})|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}}{[\mu_{\ell}(A_{m})]^{2}\sum\limits_{\sigma_{\ell-1}<k\leq\sigma_{\ell}}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\mu_{\ell}(A_{m})\sum\limits_{k>\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda_{k}(A_{m})|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum\limits_{k<0}|\lambda_{k}(A_{m})|^{2}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}}

(120)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{X_{m}}{\mu_{\ell}(A_{m})X_{m}+Y_{m}}.

To establish the strict inequality below

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\int_{M}\langle\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m},\phi_{m}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}}{\int_{M}\langle A_{m}^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m},\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}}<\frac{1}{\mu_{\ell}(A)},

we proceed with a case-by-case analysis:

(1)

If
Y_{m}\to 0
and
\sum\limits_{k>\sigma_{\ell}}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}\not\to 0
, as
m\to+\infty
, then the denominator decreases strictly compared to the reference expression. The limit is thus strictly less than
\frac{1}{\mu_{\ell}(A)}
.

(2)

If
\sum\limits_{k>\sigma_{\ell}}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}\to 0
and
Y_{m}\not\to 0
, as
m\to+\infty
.

(3)

If both terms remain positive as
m\to+\infty
.

For Cases
(2)
and
(3)
, we apply an elementary inequality

(121)

\frac{a}{ba+c}<\frac{1}{b}\quad\forall a,b,c>0,

to conclude.

In all cases, the inequality follows.

On the other hand, (
103
) and (
104
) implies

(122)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to+\infty}\frac{\int_{M}\left<\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m},\phi_{m}\right>\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\left<A_{m}^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m},\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m}\right>\dv_{g}}=\frac{1}{\mu_{\ell}(A)},

which leads to a contradiction.

For the negative part of the spectrum one obtains by the same argument that for each
\ell<0

(123)

\displaystyle P_{\ell}(A_{m})\rightarrow P_{\ell}(A)\quad\text{as}\quad m\rightarrow\infty.

This proves the claim and hence the theorem.

∎

5.
Continuity of eigenvalues on manifolds with boundary

In this section, we discuss the continuity of weighted eigenvalues under the chiral (CHI) boundary condition and aim to prove Theorem
5.5
.
We begin by recalling basic facts concerning spin geometry on manifolds with boundary; for detailed discussions, we refer to
[
13
]
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional compact spin Riemannian manifold with boundary
\partial M
, equipped with a spin structure and associated spinor bundle
(\Sigma_{g}M,g^{s})
, where
g^{s}
denotes the induced metric on the spinor bundle
\Sigma_{g}M
. On the boundary
\partial M
, the restricted spinor bundle
\mathbf{S}=\Sigma_{g}M|_{\partial M}
inherits a metric induced naturally by
g^{s}
, which we denote by
g^{\mathbf{S}}
, which is an induced metric, orientation, and spin structure. The restricted spinor bundle
\mathbf{S}=\Sigma_{g}M|_{\partial M}
is identified as follows:

\mathbf{S}\cong\begin{cases}\Sigma\partial M&\text{if $n-1$ is even},\\[5.0pt]
\Sigma\partial M\oplus\Sigma\partial M&\text{if $n-1$ is odd}.\end{cases}

In particular, for odd
n
, this splitting corresponds to the decomposition

\mathbf{S}=\Sigma^{+}M|_{\partial M}\oplus\Sigma^{-}M|_{\partial M}.

On the boundary spinor bundle
\mathbf{S}
, the induced Clifford multiplication
\gamma^{\mathbf{S}}
and spinorial connection
\not{\nabla}^{\mathbf{S}}
are defined by

\gamma^{\mathbf{S}}(X)\psi=\gamma(X)\gamma(\mathbf{n})\psi,\quad\not{\nabla}_{X}^{\mathbf{S}}\psi=\not{\nabla}_{X}\psi-\frac{1}{2}\gamma(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.72218ptI}(X))\gamma(\mathbf{n})\psi,

where
\mathbf{n}
denotes the inward unit normal vector on
\partial M
, and
\mathrm{I\hskip-1.72218ptI}
is the second fundamental form. These structures satisfy

\not{\nabla}_{X}^{\mathbf{S}}(\gamma(\mathbf{n})\psi)=\gamma(\mathbf{n})\not{\nabla}_{X}^{\mathbf{S}}\psi.

The chiral boundary condition is defined by the projection operator

\mathbf{B}^{+}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{Id}-\gamma(\mathbf{n})G),

where
G
is an endomorphism-field of
\Sigma_{g}M
(whose restriction on
\partial M
is also denoted by
G
) that is involutive, unitary, parallel, and anticommutes with Clifford multiplication, satisfying

G^{2}=\mathrm{Id},\quad\langle G\psi,G\varphi\rangle=\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle,\quad\not{\nabla}_{X}(G\psi)=G\not{\nabla}_{X}\psi,\quad\gamma(X)G=-G\gamma(X),

for all
X\in\Gamma(TM)
and
\psi,\varphi\in\Gamma(\Sigma_{g}M)
.

It is known (see
[
13
]
) that the Dirac operator
\not{D}_{g}
with chiral boundary condition
(\not{D}_{g},\mathbf{B}^{+})
has a real, discrete, and unbounded spectrum. We now study the following weighted eigenvalue problem:

(124)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \not{D}_{g}\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}&=\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}A\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}&&\text{in }M,\\[5.0pt]
\mathbf{B}^{+}\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}&=0&&\text{on }\partial M,\end{aligned}\right.

where
A\in\mathrm{End}(\Sigma_{g}M)
is a symmetric, and fiberwise positive definite endomorphism.

We denote the weighted
\rm{CHI}
eigenvalues of (
124
) by
\lambda_{k}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A)
, with corresponding eigenspinors
\varphi_{k}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A)
satisfying

(125)

\displaystyle A^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\varphi_{k}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A)=\lambda_{k}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A)\varphi_{k}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A),\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}.

These eigenvalues are real, discrete, and unbounded, indexed as follows:

(126)

\displaystyle-\infty\leftarrow\cdots\leq\lambda_{-k}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{-1}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A)<0<\lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A)\leq\cdots\rightarrow+\infty.

Let
V_{k}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A)\subset H^{1}(\Sigma_{g}M)
be the finite-dimensional eigenspace associated with
\lambda_{k}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A)
.
Additionally, we note that
(\not{D}_{g},\mathbf{B}^{+})
has no kernel
[
5
, Corollary 3.7]
, i.e.

(127)

\displaystyle\ker(A^{-1}\not{D}_{g},\mathbf{B}^{+})=\ker(\not{D}_{g},\mathbf{B}^{+})=\{0\},

this kernel is independent of
A
.

We write the spectrum into its distinct weighted
\mathrm{CHI}
eigenvalues. For each
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}
, denote by
\mu^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A)
the
\ell
-th distinct weighted
\mathrm{CHI}
eigenvalue, and set the associate eigenspace as

H^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A)=\Eigen\big((A^{-1}\not{D}_{g},\mathbf{B}^{+}),\mu^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A)\big)
, whose dimension is
h_{\ell}=\dim H^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A)
. Let
P^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A)
be the projector onto
H^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A)
. For
\ell>0
, we set
\sigma_{\ell}\coloneqq\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}h_{p}
.
We have the eigenspaces can be conveniently labeled as follows:

(128)

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{1}(A)

\displaystyle=\Span\big\{\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{j}(A)\mid 1\leq j\leq h_{1}\big\},

(129)

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A)

\displaystyle=\Span\big\{\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{j}(A)\mid\sigma_{\ell-1}<j\leq\sigma_{\ell}\big\},\qquad\ell>1,

and for
\ell<0
, the eigenspaces and projectors are defined by a similar method. Moreover, for each
A_{m}
the eigenspaces
H^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A_{m})
are still organized not with respect to the eigenvalues of
(A_{m}^{-1}\not{D}_{g},\mathbf{B}^{+})
but with respect to those of
(A^{-1}\not{D}_{g},\mathbf{B}^{+})
, namely

(130)

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{1}(A_{m})=

\displaystyle\Span\left\{\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{j}(A_{m})\mid 0<j\leq h_{1}\right\},

(131)

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A_{m})=

\displaystyle\Span\left\{\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{j}(A_{m})\mid\sigma_{\ell-1}<j\leq\sigma_{\ell}\right\},\quad\ell>1,

and
H^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A_{m})
for
\ell<0
are similarly defined, with the projector onto
H^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A_{m})
denoted by
P^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A_{m})
for each
\ell\neq 0
.

We adopt the normalization conditions:

(132)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle A\varphi_{j}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A),\varphi_{k}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A)\rangle_{g^{s}}\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle=\delta_{jk},\quad\forall j,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}.

Analogous to the case of closed manifolds, the continuity analysis for weighted
\mathrm{CHI}
eigenvalues and associated eigenprojections on compact manifolds with boundary will be carried out in several systematic steps.

Step 0
: We still use the min-max characterizations of weighted eigenvalues. Similar to the discussion on closed manifolds, we can define a Grassmannian manifold

Gr_{k-1}^{*}(A):=\{V\subseteq H^{1}_{\mathrm{CHI}}(\Sigma_{g}M)|V\perp_{A}\ker(\not{D}_{g},\mathbf{B}^{+}),\dim V=k-1\},

where the
A
-orthogonality
V\perp_{A}\ker(\not{D}_{g},\mathbf{B}^{+})
means
\int_{M}\langle A\psi,\theta\rangle\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}=0
, for any
\psi\in V
and
\theta\in\ker(\not{D}_{g},\mathbf{B}^{+})
.

The
k
-th weighted
\mathrm{CHI}
positive eigenvalue is characterized by the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

The
k
-th positive weighted
\mathrm{CHI}
eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A)
is characterized by

(133)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A)}=

\displaystyle\inf_{V\in Gr^{*}_{k-1}(A)}\;\sup_{0\neq\psi\perp_{A}V}\left\{\frac{\int_{M}\left<\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\left<A^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}\right\}.

Proof.

Let
\nu^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}
denote the right-hand side. We show
\frac{1}{\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A)}=\nu^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}
.

For
V\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k-1}^{*}(A)
, the
k
-dimensional space spanned by
\{\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{i}(A)\}_{i=1}^{k}
contains a spinor
\psi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}
with

(134)

\int_{M}\langle A\psi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k},\varphi\rangle_{g^{s}}\dv_{g}=0\quad\forall\varphi\in V.

We assume
\psi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}\,\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{j}(A)
, and directly compute that

(135)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\neq\psi\perp V}\left\{\frac{\int_{M}\left<\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\left<A^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}\right\}\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{M}\left<\psi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k},\not{D}_{g}\psi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}\right>\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\left<A^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\psi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k},\not{D}_{g}\psi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}\right>\dv_{g}}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{i}(A)\,b_{i}^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{i}(A)^{2}b_{i}^{2}}

(136)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A)}.

Hence
\nu^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}\geq\frac{1}{\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A)}
.

By taking
V_{*}(A)=\Span\left\{\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{1}(A),\cdots,\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k-1}(A)\right\}
and
\psi=\varphi_{k}(A)\perp_{A}V_{*}(A)
, we have

\frac{\int_{M}\left<\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\left<A^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A)}.

Thus
\nu^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A)}
.
∎

Moreover, entirely similarly to the discussion on closed manifolds, we quickly obtain the following fact:

Remark 5.2
.

The negative weighted
\mathrm{CHI}
eigenvalues can be defined as:

(137)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{-k}(A)}=

\displaystyle\sup_{V\in Gr^{*}_{k-1}(A)}\;\inf_{0\neq\psi\perp_{A}V}\left\{\frac{\int_{M}\left<\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\left<A^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}\right\}.

An analogous min-max characterization of the weighted
\mathrm{CHI}
eigenvalues follows as:

Proposition 5.3
.

The
k
-th positive weighted
\mathrm{CHI}
eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}(A)
is characterized by

(138)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A)}=\inf_{\widetilde{W}\in Gr^{*}_{k}(A)}\;\sup_{0\neq\psi,\psi\in\widetilde{W}}\left\{\frac{\int_{M}\left<\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\left<A^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}\right\},

and the
k
-th negative weighted
\mathrm{CHI}
eigenvalue
\lambda_{-k}(A)
is characterized by

(139)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{-k}(A)}=\sup_{\widetilde{W}\in Gr^{*}_{k}(A)}\;\inf_{0\neq\psi,\psi\in\widetilde{W}}\left\{\frac{\int_{M}\left<\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\left<A^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}\right\}.

We also obtain a comparison principle for such weighted
\mathrm{CHI}
eigenvalues of the Dirac operator by the above min-max characterization of weighted
\mathrm{CHI}
eigenvalues.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
on Compact Manifolds with Boundary.

This directly follows from Proposition
5.3
. For the compact spin manifolds with boundary, we note that the chiral eigenvalue problem has no kernel, i.e.
\ker(\not{D}_{g},\mathbf{B}^{+})=\{0\}
, thus the Grassmann manifold
Gr^{*}_{k}(A)=Gr_{k}
reduces to the classical Grassmann manifold independent of
A
.

Given
A_{1}^{-1}\leq A_{2}^{-1}
, we have the inequality for weighted Rayleigh quotients:

(140)

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{M}\left<\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\left<A_{1}^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}\geq\frac{\int_{M}\left<\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\left<A_{2}^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\psi,\not{D}_{g}\psi\right>\dv_{g}},\quad\forall\psi\in\widetilde{W},\widetilde{W}\in Gr_{k}.

Taking the supremum over

and then the infimum over

, we derive:

(141)

\displaystyle\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{1})\leq\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{2}),

as desired.

A completely analogous argument shows that for the case

(142)

\displaystyle\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{1})\geq\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{2}).

∎

.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n

Step 2

\displaystyle A_{m},A\text{ are symmetric positive-definite endomorphisms satisfying}

\displaystyle A_{m}^{-1}\rightharpoonup A^{-1}\text{ and }A_{m}\rightharpoonup A\text{ weakly in }L^{p}(M,\mathrm{End}(\Sigma_{g}M))\text{ for some }p>n.

Theorem 5.5
.

(M^{n},g)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to+\infty}\inf_{\phi\in\Eigen((A^{-1}\not{D}_{g},\mathbf{B}^{+});\lambda_{k}(A))}\|\varphi_{k}(A_{m})-\phi\|_{C^{\alpha}}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A_{m})}=

Proposition 5.6
.

be an

holds on

for the equation (

\limsup_{m\to+\infty}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{1}(A_{m})\leq\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{1}(A)
.

If

, we take a test spinor as

where

, and the coefficients

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle\varphi^{(m)},A_{m}\varphi_{i}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A_{m})\rangle\,\dv_{g}&=0,\quad\forall i=1,\ldots,k-1,\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle A_{m}\varphi^{(m)},\varphi^{(m)}\rangle\,\dv_{g}&=1.\end{array}\right.

where
\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{i}(A_{m})
,

are eigenspinors associated with

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}\left(A_{m}\right)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\int_{M}\langle\not{D}_{g}\varphi^{(m)},\varphi^{(m)}\rangle\,\dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\langle A_{m}^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\varphi^{(m)},\not{D}_{g}\varphi^{(m)}\rangle\,\dv_{g}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left[c_{j}^{(m)}\right]^{2}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{j}(A)}{\int_{M}\langle A_{m}^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\varphi^{(m)},\not{D}_{g}\varphi^{(m)}\rangle\,\dv_{g}}

Moreover, by analyzing the denominator on the right side of inequality (

(156)

\displaystyle\int_{M}

\displaystyle\langle A_{m}^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\varphi^{(m)},\not{D}_{g}\varphi^{(m)}\rangle\dv_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}c_{i}^{(m)}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{i}(A)c_{j}^{(m)}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{j}(A)\int_{M}\langle A\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{i},\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{j}\rangle\dv_{g}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\int_{M}\langle\left(A_{m}^{-1}-A^{-1}\right)\not{D}_{g}\varphi^{(m)},\not{D}_{g}\varphi^{(m)}\rangle\dv_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(c_{i}^{(m)}\right)^{2}[\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{i}(A)]^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}c_{i}^{(m)}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{i}(A)c_{j}^{(m)}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{j}(A)\int_{M}\langle\left(A_{m}^{-1}-A^{-1}\right)A\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{i},A\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{j}\rangle\dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(c_{i}^{(m)}\right)^{2}[\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{i}(A)]^{2}+C_{m}\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}c_{i}^{(m)}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{i}(A)c_{j}^{(m)}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{j}(A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(c_{i}^{(m)}\right)^{2}[\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{i}(A)]^{2}+C_{m}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}^{(m)}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{i}(A)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(c_{i}^{(m)}\right)^{2}[\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{i}(A)]^{2}\left(1+kC_{m}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(c_{i}^{(m)}\right)^{2}[\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{i}(A)]^{2}\left(1+kC_{m}\right)

(157)

\displaystyle k\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}^{2}\geq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}\right)^{2}

Consequently, we obtain

(158)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}\left(A_{m}\right)}\geq\frac{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left[c_{j}^{(m)}\right]^{2}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{j}(A)}{\left(1+kC_{m}\right)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\left(c_{i}^{(m)}\right)^{2}[\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{i}(A)]^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{\left(1+kC_{m}\right)\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A)}.

Because of (

Hence

\displaystyle\liminf_{m\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}\left(A_{m}\right)}\geq\liminf_{m\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\left(1+kC_{m}\right)\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A)}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A)}

\displaystyle\limsup_{m\to+\infty}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m})\leq\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A).

∎

Step 3

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{k}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A_{m})\|_{W^{1,2}(M)}\leq C(n,p,g)\left(1+\lambda_{k}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A_{m})\|A_{m}\|_{L^{p}}\right)^{T_{1}}\left(\|A_{m}^{-1}\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda_{k}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A_{m})\|A_{m}\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{k}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A_{m})\|_{C^{\alpha}(M)}\leq C(n,p,g)\left(1+\lambda_{k}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A_{m})\|A_{m}\|_{L^{p}}\right)^{T_{2}}\left(\|A_{m}^{-1}\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda_{k}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A_{m})\|A_{m}\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right),

For the right-hand side of the inequalities, we have uniform bounds for

in the previous step.

Step 4.
Consider a convergent subsequence of eigenvalues
\{\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m_{\ell}})\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}

, with limit
\mu=\lim\limits_{\ell\to\infty}\lambda_{k}(A_{m_{\ell}})
. Let the corresponding eigenspinors
\{\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m_{\ell}})\}_{l=1}^{\infty}
converge in

to a limit
\Phi

Then the limit satisfies
\mu>0
, and
(\mu,\Phi)
is a weighted eigenpair

(164)

\begin{cases}\not{D}_{g}\Phi=\mu A\Phi,&\text{in }M,\\
\mathbf{B}^{+}\Phi=0,&\text{on }\partial M,\end{cases}

in the weak sense.
Indeed, for any test spinor
\eta\in C^{\infty}
, we have

(165)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle\not{D}_{g}\Phi,\eta\rangle\,\dv_{g}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\ell\to+\infty}\int_{M}\langle\not{D}_{g}\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m_{\ell}}),\eta\rangle\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\ell\to+\infty}\int_{M}\lambda_{k}(A_{m_{\ell}})\langle A_{m_{\ell}}\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m_{\ell}}),\eta\rangle\,\dv_{g}

\displaystyle=\mu\int_{M}\langle A\Phi,\eta\rangle\,\dv_{g}.

As in the closed case, we can also see that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to+\infty}\lambda^{\rm CHI}_{k}(A_{m})=\lambda_{k}^{\rm CHI}(A).

Step 5

We now prove the continuity of the projectors for the positive

In

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\|P^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A_{m})^{\perp}P^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A)\|=0.

Equivalently, for any
\psi\in H^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A)

\int_{M}\langle A\psi,\psi\rangle\,\dv_{g}=1
, we must show

as

. By induction, assume this holds for
1,\dots,\ell-1

\ell\geq 1

\ell=1

For each
m\geq 1
, expand

as

(171)

\displaystyle\psi=\sum_{k\neq 0}a_{k}^{(m)}\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m}).

Hypothesis (

(172)

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\langle A\psi,\psi\rangle\,\dv_{g}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{M}\langle A_{m}\psi,\psi\rangle\,\dv_{g}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{k\neq 0}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}

(173)

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}\left(\|P^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A_{m})\psi\|_{L^{2}_{A_{m}}}^{2}+\sum_{k\leq\sigma_{\ell-1}}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum_{k>\sigma_{\ell}}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum_{k<0}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}\right)

The limit of the first term is
\|P^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A_{m})\psi\|_{L^{2}_{A}}^{2}
. To establish that the remaining terms vanish, then, by the inductive hypothesis, we immediately obtain

\lim_{m\to\infty}P^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{i}(A_{m})\psi=P^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{i}(A)\psi=0,\quad\forall\,1\leq i\leq\ell-1.

Thus,

(175)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{1\leq k\leq\sigma_{\ell-1}}|a^{(m)}_{k}|^{2}=0.

Define
\delta_{m}\coloneqq\sum\limits_{1\leq k\leq\sigma_{\ell-1}}a_{k}^{(m)}\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m})
, we consider the spinor

\displaystyle\phi_{m}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\psi-\delta_{m}

(176)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma_{\ell-1}<k\leq\sigma_{\ell}}a_{k}^{(m)}\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m})+\sum_{k>\sigma_{\ell}}a_{k}^{(m)}\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m})+\sum_{k<0}a_{k}^{(m)}\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m}),

where

(177)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{M}\langle A_{m}\phi_{m},\phi_{m}\rangle\dv_{g}=1,

(178)

\displaystyle\phi_{m}\perp_{A_{m}}\Span\{\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{1}(A_{m}),\dots,\varphi^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\sigma_{\ell-1}}(A_{m})\},

(179)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{M}\langle\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m},\phi_{m}\rangle\dv_{g}=\mu^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A),

(180)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{M}\langle A_{m}^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m},\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m}\rangle\dv_{g}=\left|\mu^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A)\right|^{2}.

We claim

(181)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\left(\sum_{k>\sigma_{\ell}}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum_{k<0}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}\right)=0.

Suppose for contradiction that

(182)

r\coloneqq\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left(\sum_{k>\sigma_{\ell}}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum_{k<0}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}\right)>0.

Passing to a subsequence, assume

(183)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m},\phi_{m}\rangle\dv_{g}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma_{\ell-1}<k\leq\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m})|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum_{k>\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m})|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}

(184)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k<0}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m})|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2},

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle A_{m}^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m},\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m}\rangle\dv_{g}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma_{\ell-1}<k\leq\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m})^{2}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum_{k>\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m})^{2}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}

(186)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k<0}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m})^{2}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}.

(187)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m},\phi_{m}\rangle\dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\mu^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A_{m})\sum_{\sigma_{\ell-1}<k\leq\sigma_{\ell}}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum_{k>\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m})|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle A_{m}^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m},\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m}\rangle\dv_{g}

\displaystyle\geq[\mu^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A_{m})]^{2}\sum_{\sigma_{\ell-1}<k\leq\sigma_{\ell}}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\mu^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A_{m})\sum_{k>\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m})|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}

(189)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k<0}|\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m})|^{2}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}.

We define

In summary, we have

(190)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\int_{M}\langle\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m},\phi_{m}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}}{\int_{M}\langle A_{m}^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m},\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}}

(191)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\sum\limits_{\sigma_{\ell-1}<k\leq\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m})|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum\limits_{k>\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m})|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum\limits_{k<0}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m})|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}}{\sum\limits_{\sigma_{\ell-1}<k\leq\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m})^{2}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum\limits_{k>\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m})^{2}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum\limits_{k<0}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m})^{2}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}}

(192)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\mu^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A_{m})\sum\limits_{\sigma_{\ell-1}<k\leq\sigma_{\ell}}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum\limits_{k>\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m})|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}}{[\mu^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A_{m})]^{2}\sum\limits_{\sigma_{\ell-1}<k\leq\sigma_{\ell}}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\mu^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A_{m})\sum\limits_{k>\sigma_{\ell}}\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m})|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}+\sum\limits_{k<0}|\lambda^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{k}(A_{m})|^{2}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}}

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\int_{M}\langle\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m},\phi_{m}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}}{\int_{M}\langle A_{m}^{-1}\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m},\not{D}_{g}\phi_{m}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}}<\frac{1}{\mu_{\ell}^{\mathrm{CHI}}(A)}.

Case 2:

\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\sum\limits_{k>\sigma_{\ell}}|a_{k}^{(m)}|^{2}\to 0

.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle P^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A_{m})\rightarrow P^{\mathrm{CHI}}_{\ell}(A)\quad\text{as}\quad m\rightarrow\infty.

6.
Application in geometric and variational problems

In this section we consider some geometric variational problems, as applications to our results.
We will be sketchy in these discussions, more details will be given in the analysis of the concrete problems.

6.1.
Continuity of eigenvalues with respect to Riemannian structures on the spinor bundles

This result can be interpreted as the continuity of eigenvalues of Dirac operator with respect to the Riemannian structure with weak topology.
Indeed, since
A
is assumed to be symmetric and positive definite fiberwisely, and smooth globally, it is intriguing to consider
g^{s}(A\cdot,\cdot)
as a new Riemannian structure on the spinor bundle.
In general, we cannot do this since the Riemannian structure on the spinor bundle is closely related to the Riemannian metric
g
on
M
.
Even in case of special
A
where this is legal, we do not see a clear benefit so far by taking this viewpoint in handling the eigenvalue continuity.

On the other hand, our results imply that a continuous deformation of the Riemannian structure on the spinor bundles in the weak
L^{p}
sense induces a continuous deformation of the spectra of the Dirac operator, as well as the associated eigenspaces.

6.2.
Super Liouville equations

In this subsection we consider an equation arising from the super Liouville type equations, see
[
14
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
and the references therein.
Let
(M,g)
be a Riemann surface, with or without boundary.
Let
u\in H^{1}(M)
be a function and consider spinors
\psi
solving the equation

(195)

\displaystyle\not{D}_{g}\psi=\lambda e^{u}\psi.

This is a weighted eigenvalue equation with
A=e^{u}\id_{\Sigma_{g}M}
.
Moreover, the Moser–Trudinger inequality tells that
e^{\pm u}\in L^{p}
for any
p\in[1,+\infty)
, but
e^{\pm u}\notin L^{\infty}(M)
in general.

Now let
u_{m}\to u
in
H^{1}(M)
and let
\psi_{k}(e^{u_{m}})
be a sequence of eigenspinors to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}(e^{u_{m}})
which are bounded in
L^{2}
.
Our results tells that

(1)

the weighted eigenvalues converge
\lambda_{k}(e^{u_{m}})\to\lambda_{k}(e^{u})
;

(2)

the weighted eigenspinors may not converge, but there is always a convergent subsequence;

(3)

the projectors
P_{\ell}(e^{u_{m}})
converge to
P_{\ell}(e^{u})
for each spectral point
\mu_{\ell}
.

This will be useful in the variational analysis of super Liouville equations on surfaces.

Indeed, consider an eigenpair of the form

(196)

\displaystyle\not{D}_{g}\varphi_{k}(u)=\lambda_{k}(u)\,e^{u}\varphi_{k}(u).

If
u
is smooth (or at least
C^{2}
), then the conformal metric
g_{u}\coloneqq e^{2u}g
induces a new spin structure as well as an associated spin Dirac bundle.
There exists a unitary isomorphism
\beta\colon\Sigma_{g}M\to\Sigma_{g_{u}}M
which relates the two Dirac operators
\not{D}_{g}
and
\not{D}_{g_{u}}
via

(197)

\displaystyle\not{D}_{g_{u}}\left(e^{-\frac{n-1}{2}u}\beta(\psi)\right)=e^{-\frac{n+1}{2}u}\beta(\not{D}_{g}\psi),\qquad\forall\psi\in\Gamma(\Sigma_{g}M),

see
[
13
,
15
,
25
]
and
[
22
]
.
In particular,

(198)

\displaystyle\not{D}_{g_{u}}\left(e^{-u/2}\beta(\varphi_{k}(u))\right)=\lambda_{k}(u)\left(e^{-u/2}\beta(\varphi_{k}(u))\right).

That is,
(\lambda_{k}(u),e^{-u/2}\beta(\varphi_{k}(u)))
is an eigenpair for the Dirac operator with respect to the conformal metric
g_{u}
.
For smooth
u
the eigenpairs are continuous in the conformal factor
u
.
Our results say that this still holds for
u\in H^{1}(M)
.
This allows us to consider weak
L^{p}
deformations of the metric within a given conformal class and talk about the corresponding Dirac eigenvalues.

6.3.
Continuity of the weighted wave kernel

Motivated by the spectral perspective developed in the works of G
\hat{a}
tel-Yafaev
[
12
]
and Kungsman-Melgaard
[
24
]
, we consider an
A
-weighted formula of the wave operator for Dirac-type operators on closed manifolds.

In the long-range setting, G

Their global trace formula decomposes the wave evolution into resonance contributions and discrete spectral terms, demonstrating that the wave propagator encodes detailed spectral data.

These developments suggest that the wave kernel associated with
e^{it\not{D}_{A}}
can be effectively expressed as a linear combination of oscillatory weights and spectral projections. In this framework, the asymptotic and analytic behaviour of the wave kernel becomes closely tied to the convergence properties of eigenvalues and spectral projections. As such, the
A
-weighted wave kernel approach offers a refined analytic structure under perturbation in the sense of the weak topology.

Let

\psi_{k}

\lambda_{k}

(199)

\displaystyle K(t,x,y)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}}e^{it\lambda_{k}}\psi_{k}(x)\otimes\psi_{k}^{*}(y),

where
\psi_{k}^{*}\in\Sigma^{*}_{g}M
denotes the dual spinor at point
y

\psi_{k}(x)\otimes\psi_{k}^{*}(y)
defines a rank-1 operator from
\Sigma_{y}M
to
\Sigma_{x}M
.

We observe that expressions of the form

(200)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in[\mu_{\ell}]}\psi_{k}(x)\otimes\psi_{k}^{*}(y)

can be interpreted as the spectral projection operator
P_{\ell}(x,y)
onto the eigenspace associated with the
\ell
-th distinct eigenvalue, where
[\mu_{\ell}]
denotes the index set
\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}\mid\lambda_{k}=\mu_{\ell}\}

this expression can equivalently be written using the spectral projection operators

(201)

\displaystyle K(t,x,y)=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}}e^{it\mu_{\ell}}P_{\ell}(x,y).

This formulation shows that the wave kernel is a linear combination of spectral projection kernels, modulated by the oscillatory weights.

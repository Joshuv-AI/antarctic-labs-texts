A Nitrogen-rich AGN Powering a Large Ionizing Bubble at z=8.63 Journal: ApJ
Takahiro Morishita, Massimo Stiavelli, Charlotte A. Mason, Roberta Tripodi, Marco Chiaberge, Stefan Schuldt, Chris J. Willott, Yechi Zhang

Abstract
We report the detection of Ly ​ α {\rm Ly\alpha} in CANUCS-LRD-z8.6, a recently discovered AGN at z = 8.63 z=8.63 by Tripodi et al. 2024 , in new NIRSpec/MSA G140H/F070LP observations. We detect broad Ly ​ α {\rm Ly\alpha} emission (FWHM = 1540 ± 260 1540\pm 260 km/s) near the systemic velocity, which suggests a large ionizing bubble considering that the universe is almost fully neutral at the redshift. Through Ly ​ α {\rm Ly\alpha} line-shape modeling assuming a Strömgren sphere, we find a large bubble radius, R b = R_{b}= 1.5 − 0.2 + 0.3 1.5_{-0.2}^{+0.3} pMpc, and a moderately high Ly ​ α {\rm Ly\alpha} escape fraction, f esc = f_{\rm esc}= 11 ± 3 11\pm 3 %. The intrinsic line width is inferred to be broad ( 2200 ± 280 2200\pm 280 km/s), likely originating in the broad-line region. Existing data indicate that CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 is within a mild overdensity, δ = 1.9 − 0.7 + 2.9 \delta=1.9_{-0.7}^{+2.9} , suggesting that other galaxies in its proximity might have contributed to the formation of the bubble. The high N iv ] λ ​ 1488 \textrm{N}~\textsc{iv}]_{\lambda 1488} / C ​ iv λ ​ 1548 \textrm{C}~\textsc{iv}_{\lambda 1548} and N iv ] λ ​ 1488 \textrm{N}~\textsc{iv}]_{\lambda 1488} / O iii ] λ ​ 1661 \textrm{O}~\textsc{iii}]_{\lambda 1661} line ratios measured in existing NIRSpec/PRISM data indicate nitrogen enrichment in this metal-poor, low-luminosity AGN. The spectroscopic features are overall similar to other nitrogen-rich galaxies discovered in the literature, such as GN-z11 and GHZ2/GLASSz12. This suggests that CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 may represent one of the evolutionary phases of those nitrogen-rich galaxies.



I
Introduction

The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST;
Gardner et al. 2023
) has started revolutionizing our understanding of galaxies and stellar populations in the first billion years. With its exquisite sensitivity, deep surveys have identified new sources well beyond the previous limit
(
Curtis-Lake et al. 2023
;
Bunker et al. 2023
;
Castellano et al. 2024
;
Carniani et al. 2024
;
Naidu et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
, now up to
z\sim 14
. The identification of sources at high redshifts and their almost immediate spectroscopic characterization have given us great insights into the formation of the first galaxies, black holes, and stars
(
Robertson et al. 2023
;
Dekel et al. 2023
;
Ziparo et al. 2023
;
Inayoshi & Ichikawa 2024
, e.g.,)
.

Of particular interest are unusual nucleosynthesis patterns (to our local standard) discovered in intensely star-forming objects at
z>6
. For example, sensitive spectroscopy with JWST/NIRSpec revealed over-abundance of nitrogen in GNz11
(
Bunker et al. 2023
;
Cameron et al. 2023
;
Senchyna et al. 2023
;
Charbonnel et al. 2023
)
and in other galaxies
(
Marques-Chaves et al. 2024
;
Schaerer et al. 2024
;
Topping et al. 2024
;
Ji et al. 2024
;
Isobe et al. 2023b
)
, over- and under-abundance of Carbon
(
Stiavelli et al. 2025
;
Jones et al. 2023
;
Hsiao et al. 2023
;
D’Eugenio et al. 2024
)
. In particular, those with nitrogen-rich objects are characterized with a high specific star formation rate, extremely compact morphology, and in a few cases broad emission lines. All of these characteristics point to intense star formation within the scale of
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}\,100
pc, likely driven by massive stars of low-metallicity
(
Schaerer et al. 2024
;
Topping et al. 2024
)
. Probing different elemental abundances can reveal information on the detailed galaxy formation process, as stars of different masses, and hence different lifetimes, are the main producers of different elements
(
Stiavelli et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
.

However, the prevalence of AGN at high redshift
(
Harikane et al. 2023
, e.g.,)
complicates the story, making it less straightforward to interpret the observed properties. An example is seen in the aforementioned GN-z11, which revealed several high-ionization rest-frame UV lines, including
\textrm{N}~\textsc{iv}]_{\lambda 1488}
,
\textrm{C}~\textsc{iv}_{\lambda 1548}
, and
\textrm{C}~\textsc{iii}]_{\lambda 1908}

(
Bunker et al. 2023
)
.
Maiolino et al. 2024
reported high gas densities (
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,10^{9}
cm
-3
) and the AGN characteristic [
\textrm{Ne}~\textsc{iv}]_{\lambda 2423}
and [C
ii
]
λ1335
lines in the same source, and favored the presence of an AGN over other non-AGN origins (e.g., Wolf-Rayet stars). Similarly,
Castellano et al. 2024
reported the detection of
\textrm{N}~\textsc{iv}]_{\lambda 1488}
,
\textrm{C}~\textsc{iv}_{\lambda 1548}
,
\textrm{He}~\textsc{ii}_{\lambda 1640}
,
\textrm{O}~\textsc{iii}]_{\lambda 1661}
, and
\textrm{C}~\textsc{iii}]_{\lambda 1908}
lines in GHZ2/GLASS-z12. While the high ionization [
\textrm{Ne}~\textsc{iv}]_{\lambda 2423}
line was not detected in the spectrum of GHZ2/GLASS-z12, the authors showed that the source is still compatible with AGN from various emission line diagnostics.

Emission line diagnostics used in those studies are often established with local samples, and thus may not serve as useful as for local studies — due to the presence of hard ionizing sources, e.g., massive stars in extremely metal poor, high gas density environments
(
Cleri et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
. The lack of wavelength coverage for
{\rm H\beta}
at
z>9.3
by NIRSpec makes the solid confirmation of broad-line AGN challenging in both examples above. In this regard, there exists a need for deep spectroscopic coverage at
>5\,\mu
m by MIRI
(
Zavala et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
or detailed studies of analogs at slightly lower redshifts
(
Rojas-Ruiz et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
.

In this paper, we present a new observation of a previously reported AGN at
z=8.63
, CANUCS-LRD-z8.6, in the sightline of MACS J1149 at
z=0.54
(Fig.
1
).
Tripodi et al. 2024
reported CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 to have AGN characteristic features, including the line broadening in
{\rm H\beta}

10^{8}\,M_{\odot}
, as well as high-ionization UV lines. CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 was reported to have blue UV and red optical continua, a characteristic spectral feature seen in the little red dot (LRD) population. As we see below, a newly taken NIRSpec G140H/F070LP spectrum reveals broad
{\rm Ly\alpha}
emission near the systemic velocity, which, at that redshift, indicates the presence of a large ionizing bubble. Our reanalysis of the existing PRISM spectrum reveals the potential enrichment of nitrogen, as represented by its enormously bright
\textrm{N}~\textsc{iv}]_{\lambda 1488}
line. In addition to the great sensitivity offered by the observatory, the lens magnification by the foreground cluster allows us to study the properties CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 with greater sensitivity. The paper is outlined as follows: In Sec.
II

III

IV
, we discuss the nature of CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 in the context of recent discoveries of luminous, nitrogen-rich galaxies and AGN at
z>6
. Where relevant, we adopt the AB magnitude system
(
Oke & Gunn 1983
;
Fukugita et al. 1996
)
, cosmological parameters of
\Omega_{\rm m}=0.3
,
\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.7

H_{0}=70\,{\rm km~s^{-1}}\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}

Chabrier 2003

II.1
NIRSpec/MSA G140H/F070LP observations

NIRSpec/MSA observations (GTO4552, PI Stiavelli) were configured with a high-resolution grating (G140H/F070LP), aiming to capture rest-frame UV emission lines of galaxies at
z\sim 3
–9. The observations were executed on June 9 2025, pointed toward (R.A.,Decl.)=(11:49:38.16, +22:23:30.650) at position angle PA=258 degree. The total science time, excluding overhead, is 14 hrs, split into two MSA masks. A NIRCam parallel imaging (F140M/F430M, F070W/F360M) towards the 1199 parallel field
(
Morishita et al. 2024a
;
Stiavelli et al. 2025
;
Zhang et al. 2025
)
was attached to the primary MSA observations (Morishita, in prep.).

We designed two slit masks, following the same procedure as for the GTO1199 program
(
Stiavelli et al. 2023
;
Morishita et al. 2024b
)
. The parent source catalog was constructed using existing HST imaging data, including CLASH, HFF, GLASS, and BUFFALO
(
Postman et al. 2012
;
Treu et al. 2015
;
Lotz et al. 2017
;
Kelly et al. 2018
;
Steinhardt et al. 2020
)
, and existing JWST NIRCam imaging data
(
Stiavelli et al. 2023
;
Morishita et al. 2024b
)
. Spectroscopic redshift measurements available in the literature were incorporated
(
Grillo et al. 2016
;
Treu et al. 2016
;
Shipley et al. 2018
;
Schuldt et al. 2024
)
. In our mask design, the highest priority was given to spectroscopically confirmed sources at
z>5

z=9.1

(
Hashimoto et al. 2018
;
Hoag et al. 2019
;
Stiavelli et al. 2023

, strong emitters
(

\sim 0.\!^{\prime}5
) between the two masks. For each source, the default 3 shutter slitlets were assigned, enabling exposure at three positions separated for
0.\!^{\prime\prime}53
from each. We selected the default Entire Open Shutter Area for source allocations in APT, which secures placing the source center position within 35 milliarcsec from the shutter boundary. Including fillers and open shutters in empty sky regions, our MSA masks consisted of in total 98 unique sources.

III.1
Lyman-
\alpha
line modeling in G140H/F070LP

The G140H spectrum clearly detects the
{\rm Ly\alpha}
line (Fig.
2
). The line has an extended faint envelope to the longer wavelength from the expected line position for the systemic redshift defined by
z_{\rm[OIII]5007}
(see Sec.
II.2
).
This is naturally expected due to the resonant nature of
{\rm Ly\alpha}
. The observed line flux peaks at
+14
Å redward of the systemic
{\rm Ly\alpha}
wavelength, or
\Delta v_{\rm red}\sim 360
km/s. The Gaussian fit to the observed line shows a larger offset, centered at
\Delta v_{\rm red}\sim 760
km/s. The observed line width is broad, FWHM
=1540\pm 260
km/s.

Remarkably, we observe
{\rm Ly\alpha}
emission near the systemic line center, implying that the surroundings of CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 is highly ionized
(
Mason & Gronke 2020
)
— the predicted transmission from the IGM damping wing at line center is
<10\%
if a source sits in an ionized region
<0.5
pMpc, versus
>30\%
if the ionized region is
>1
pMpc. In the observed spectrum,
\sim 26\,\%
of the
{\rm Ly\alpha}
flux is found within
<250
km/s of the systemic velocity.

To infer the transmission structure, we follow the IGM prescription described in
Mason & Gronke 2020
. We assume an intrinsic Gaussian
{\rm Ly\alpha}
emission line, centered at the systemic velocity (see Sec.
III.2
), and include include
{\rm Ly\alpha}
optical depth
\tau_{\rm D}
from a simple transmission model assuming a Strömgren sphere of the size
R_{b}
around the source i.e radius of the ionizing bubble.

The best-fit model is shown in Fig.

pMpc. At
z\approx 8.6
, the decline in

If we instead assume the intrinsic line as a half-Gaussian, truncated at the systemic velocity, we infer a much larger ionized bubble — as to match the observed line profile requires almost 100% transmission.

For our Gaussian model the inferred intrinsic line width is broad, with FWHM
\sim
2200\pm 280
km/s.
This is much broader than expected for typical star forming galaxies. In fact, as we see in the following (Sec.
II.2

{\rm H\beta}

\sim 3600\pm 500

The broad

Torralba et al. 2025

By comparing the observed and intrinsic line fluxes, we find the
{\rm Ly\alpha}
escape fraction of
11\pm 3
%, which is considerably high for the redshift
(
Morishita et al. 2023
;
Nakane et al. 2024
, c.f.)
, and is rather close to those at lower redshift
z\sim 5
–6
(
Chen et al. 2024

Yue et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
, implying low IGM opacity. Similarly, we can compare the intrinsic

{\rm H\beta}

30

0.01\,Z_{\odot}
from
(
Tripodi et al. 2024

III.2
Emission Line Measurement in Prism

We model the line profile of each emission line of interest in the extracted 1d PRISM spectrum with a Gaussian function (Fig.
3
). Our basic strategy is to define a wavelength window for each line, subtract the underlying continuum spectrum, and perform a Gaussian fitting. For continuum subtraction, we utilize the best-fit stellar-only (excluding nebular component) SED template derived by
gsf
(Fig.
3
; also see Sec.
III.3
).

The line model includes the amplitude, line width, and redshift parameters. While most lines are unresolved in the Prism spectrum, we find significant line broadening around the
{\rm H\beta}
line, at the observed wavelength
4.68\,\mu
m, as also found by
Tripodi et al. 2024
. We therefore add a broad line component at the same wavelength.

The flux of each line is estimated by integrating the corresponding Gaussian component, and the flux error is estimated by summing the error weighted by the amplitude of the Gaussian model in quadrature. The uncertainty associated with the modeled continuum is negligible but also added. In the following analysis, we adopt flux measurements when the line is detected (signal-to-noise ratio
S/N\geq 3
); for those not detected, we adopt the 3-
\sigma
upper limit.

Also shown in Fig.
5
is the mass–metallicity relation derived for galaxies at
3<z<10

(
Morishita et al. 2024b
)
. Compared with previously studied samples, CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 falls
\sim 0.5
dex below for its stellar mass.

III.3
SED Fitting Analysis

We analyze the spectral energy distributions (SED) using the JWST PRISM spectrum. We use the SED fitting code
gsf

(
Morishita et al. 2019
, ver1.93;)
.
gsf
allows flexible determinations of SEDs by adopting binned star formation histories (often referred to as
non-parametric
), here we set the age bins at an equal spacing in logarithmic scale, [1, 3, 10, 30, 100, 300] Myr. The fitting templates for each age are calculated by
fsps
, and then convolved to the resolution of NIRSpec PRISM. While
gsf
includes an emission line component, originally calculated with
cloudy

(
Byler et al. 2017
, see)
, we mask the wavelengths over strong emission lines. We adopt the SMC dust curve
(
Gordon et al. 2003
)
. The best-fit model template is used in Sec.
III.2
for continuum subtraction (also see Fig.
3
).

The PRISM spectrum is well modeled with a template of
\sim 30
Myr stellar populations. The best-fit star formation history (SFH) shows a single peak at lookback time
\sim 30
Myr of an averaged star formation of
\sim 10\,M_{\odot}
/yr. The star formation rate averaged over the last 100 Myr of the best-fit SFH is
{\rm SFR_{100\,Myr}}=6\,M_{\odot}
/yr, which is consistent with the one derived with the rest-frame UV luminosity (
{\rm SFR_{UV}}
). The SFR derived from the narrow
{\rm H\beta}
line is slightly higher,
{\rm SFR_{H\beta}}=15\,M_{\odot}
/yr, suggesting the presence of more recent star formation, within the last
\sim 10
Myr. We note that such a discrepancy is often seen in active galaxies, due to the different time scale traced by rest-frame UV and hydrogen recombination lines
(
McClymont et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
.

We find that the best-fit model for the stellar continuum (i.e. w/o nebular component) is characterized by a red UV slope,
\beta_{\rm UV,SED}=-1.5
. The slope measurement becomes much bluer when measured directly from broadband photometry (
\beta_{\rm UV,photometry}=-2.3
). Other physical parameters from the SED fitting analysis are reported in Table
2
.

IV.1
A Little Red Dot Residing in a Large Ionizing Bubble — What Created It?

Lastly, the broad
{\rm Ly\alpha}
emission observed in the G140H/F070LP spectrum supports the idea that a high-ionization environment enhances
{\rm Ly\alpha}
photon escape. Our line-profile modeling supports the presence of a large ionized bubble,
1.5_{-0.2}^{+0.3}
pMpc. We note that CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 is not particularly UV bright, with
M_{\rm UV}\sim-19.3
ABmag cf. GN-z11 (
-21.5
ABmag) and GHZ2/GLASS-z12 (
-20.5
ABmag). It is not obvious whether such a large ionizing bubble was created by itself or whether there is a contribution from other nearby galaxies.

Shown in Fig.
6
is the time evolution of the ionizing bubble radius produced by
z=8.6
assuming constant radiation from the source given its current UV luminosity, and accounting for recombination
(
Shapiro & Giroux 1987
;
Mason & Gronke 2020
, see e.g.)
. Even under this assumption, the maximum bubble radius that can be reached is
\sim 0.5
pMpc at
100
Myr (i.e. the age of CANUCS-LRD-z8.6). This is much smaller than the one inferred from our analysis,
suggesting that a contribution from ionizing sources other than its own
star formation
is required.
A plausible additional ionizing source is photoionization by the central AGN. In this case, the ionizing photons might have been emitted at a specific cone angle that is aligned with the line of sight, creating a hole through which
{\rm Ly\alpha}
photons may preferentially escape.

In addition, we explored the spectroscopic data
(
Sarrouh et al. 2025
)
and also the photometric redshift catalog constructed here to assess if the region around CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 is overdense, hosting other galaxies which could contribute to the formation of an ionized bubble. We find zero spectroscopic sources (
\Delta z<0.05
) and seven photometric sources (
\Delta z<0.2
). The spatial distribution of the photometric sources is shown in Fig.
7
. We note that the imaging coverage of the field occupies only a small fraction of the projected area of the inferred bubble radius, and thus a comprehensive investigation of its environment requires further data coverage. Regardless, we can estimate the overdensity factor,
\delta\equiv(n-\bar{n})/\bar{n}
, within the effective area. For the area of the existing coverage, using the luminosity function of
Mason et al. 2022
at
z\sim 8.6
and an average magnification factor of
\mu\sim 1.2
, we expect
N=2.7_{-1.6}^{+1.6}
galaxies. Using the number as a reference, we obtain
\delta=1.9_{-0.7}^{+2.9}
, suggesting a mild overdensity. Those photometric candidates, especially relatively bright ones at
M_{\rm UV}<-19
mag, are a potential contributor to the inferred ionizing bubble. If these galaxies are indeed within the ionizing bubble of CANUCS-LRD-z8.6, non-zero
{\rm Ly\alpha}
photon escape is expected.

An interesting comparison can be made with A2744-ODz7p9, an overdensity of galaxies at
z=7.9
that consists of 16 spec-
z
confirmed members
(
Morishita et al. 2023
;
Morishita et al. 2025
;
Hashimoto et al. 2023
;
Witten et al. 2025
)
. Despite A2744-ODz7p9 being a lower-
z
, more significant overdensity (
\delta=44_{-31}^{+89}
), only one of its members shows
{\rm Ly\alpha}
emission
(
Chen et al. 2024
)
. None of the member galaxies shows evidence of hosting an AGN.

IV.2
CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 — A Rare, Nitrogen-rich, Metal-Poor, Low-luminosity AGN

The broad component detected in the
{\rm H\beta}
line supports of the presence of an active galactic nuclei (AGN), as reported in
Tripodi et al. 2024
. While line broadening could also be of non-AGN origin, such as outflow
(
Marques-Chaves et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
, the absence of a broad component in the
[\textrm{O}~\textsc{iii}]_{\lambda 5007}
line disfavors this scenario. Assuming the
[\textrm{O}~\textsc{iii}]_{\lambda 5007}
/
{\rm H\beta}
ratio derived from the narrow components, the broad component of
[\textrm{O}~\textsc{iii}]_{\lambda 5007}
would have been detected at SNR greater than
\sim 30
. We do not observe a significant offset between the narrow and broad components in
{\rm H\beta}
.

The black hole mass of CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 (Sec.
III.2
) is
\sim 1
dex overmassive compared to the local relation of
Kormendy & Ho 2013
, but is within the range of those measured for LRD populations at
z>6
in the literature
(
Harikane et al. 2023
;
Kocevski et al. 2023
;
Akins et al. 2025
;
Maiolino et al. 2025
)
. Strong X-ray emission is not detected in individual images of existing Chandra data
(
Ogrean et al. 2016
, e.g.,)
at the source position.

The absence of high ionization lines (
>54
eV) makes the nature of CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 as AGN intriguing. All the observed emission lines here could essentially be created by low-metallicity, massive stars too. The observed emission line property of CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 is consistent with the “high-ionization” class (35–55 eV) of
Berg et al. 2021
, whereas typical AGN are often associated with higher ionization lines (“very high-ionization”). The same situation applies to some LRDs reported in the literature
(
Taylor et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
, c.f.
Tang et al. 2025
who reported the detection of
\textrm{N}~\textsc{v}_{\lambda 1240}
in a LRD at
z=6.98
. Therefore, without detection of the broad
{\rm H\beta}
line, CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 could have been classified as a star-forming galaxy of high ionization.

In addition, CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 shows very strong
\textrm{N}~\textsc{iv}]_{\lambda 1488}
emission. This line is very rare in low-
z

luminous
AGN, as investigated in
Glikman et al. 2007
who found an increased fraction of
\textrm{N}~\textsc{iv}]_{\lambda 1488}
detection in their sample Type-I AGN at
z\sim 4
(
\sim 9\,\%
, compared to
\sim 0\,\%
in the local quasars). CANUCS-LRD-z8.6, with
M_{\rm UV}=19.3
, is a low-luminosity AGN. In fact, the
\textrm{N}~\textsc{iv}]_{\lambda 1488}
line has been reported in recent JWST studies of both broad-line
(
Übler et al. 2023
;
Labbe et al. 2024
;
Isobe et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
and non-broad line sources
(
Maiolino et al. 2024
;
Topping et al. 2024
;
Isobe et al. 2023b
;
Marques-Chaves et al. 2024
;
Castellano et al. 2024
)
. The detection of strong
\textrm{N}~\textsc{iv}]_{\lambda 1488}
emission suggests an enhancement of nitrogen abundance (see Sec.
IV.3
).

IV.3
CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 in the context of Nitrogen-rich galaxies

The strong
\textrm{N}~\textsc{iv}]_{\lambda 1488}
emission hints at specific physical mechanisms in act, reflecting, e.g., anomaly seen during the CNO cycles and strong wind from massive Wolf-Rayet stars
(
Stiavelli et al. 2025
;
Zhang et al. 2025
)
. In Fig.
8
, we show line flux measurements in three line diagnostics involving
\textrm{N}~\textsc{iv}]_{\lambda 1488}
,
\textrm{N}~\textsc{v}_{\lambda 1240}
,
\textrm{O}~\textsc{iii}]_{\lambda 1661}
, and
\textrm{C}~\textsc{iv}_{\lambda 1548}
. For comparison, we calculated line ratios from photo-ionization models for AGN as follows: AGN models are calculated using
Cloudy
photo-ionization code
(
Osterbrock 1989
;
Chatzikos et al. 2023
)
. We fix the gas-phase metallicity to
0.025\,Z_{\odot}
, the one found in Sec.
III.2.1
. We calculate the model for different N/O and C/O abundance ratios (
\log
(N/O)
=-1
and 0,
\log
(C/O)
=-0.5
and 0) and ionization parameter (
\log U\in[-2.5:-1]
in step of 0.5). For hydrogen density, we adopt
n_{\rm H}=400,2000,20000
cm
-3
, to cover various densities reported for high-
z
galaxies in the literature
(
Abdurro’uf et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
. For reference, we also calculate models for young star-forming galaxies. We use
BPASS

(
Eldridge et al. 2017
;
Stanway & Eldridge 2018
)
binary stellar radiation assuming an instantaneous star formation history with the stellar age of 10 Myr, upper mass cut of 100
M_{\odot}
, and the Salpeter IMF.

From the comparison with the calculated model tracks, the observed
\textrm{N}~\textsc{iv}]_{\lambda 1488}
/
\textrm{O}~\textsc{iii}]_{\lambda 1661}
ratio of CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 favors nitrogen enhancement, to
\log\,
(N/O)
\approx 0
. Since
\textrm{O}~\textsc{iii}]_{\lambda 1661}
is not detected, its actual ratio can even be higher. The measured
\textrm{C}~\textsc{iv}_{\lambda 1548}
/
\textrm{N}~\textsc{iv}]_{\lambda 1488}
suggests the enrichment of Nitrogen over carbon and/or under-abundant Carbon. The low
\textrm{C}~\textsc{iv}_{\lambda 1548}
/
\textrm{O}~\textsc{iii}]_{\lambda 1661}
supports this too, but it remains unconstrained due to the non-detection of
\textrm{O}~\textsc{iii}]_{\lambda 1661}
. Also compared in Fig.
8
are exotic Nitrogen-rich objects reported in the literature (
\log
(N/O)
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,-0.5
), namely GHZ2
(
Castellano et al. 2024
, inverted triangle,)
, GN-z11
(
Isobe et al. 2023a
;
Maiolino et al. 2024
, dot,)
, GLASS-150008
(
Isobe et al. 2023a
, diamond,)
, CEERS-1019
(
Marques-Chaves et al. 2024
, triangle,)
. CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 is found in similar regimes occupied by those N-rich objects. A particular note is that the nature of those at
z>9.3
remained largely undetermined, due to the lack of wavelength coverage for
{\rm H\beta}

(
Cleri et al. 2025
, see also)
.

One of the key common features of N-rich galaxies in the literature is high electron densities, ranging from
n_{e}\sim 10^{3}
cm
-3
to
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,10^{6}
cm
-3
. High electron densities are expected in both broad line regions and dense star-forming clouds. The electron density of CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 could not be measured from the existing spectra, because neither of
\textrm{N}~\textsc{iv}]_{\lambda 1488}
- or
[\textrm{O}~\textsc{ii}]_{\lambda 3727}
-doublets are not resolved in the PRISM spectrum;
\textrm{N}~\textsc{iv}]_{\lambda 1488}
was beyond the wavelength coverage of G140H/F070LP. Alternatively, CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 is characterized with a very compact morphology (Fig.
1
).
Tripodi et al. 2024
found it unresolved and placed an upper limit on its half-light radius,
<70
pc.
This gives a star formation rate surface density
\Sigma_{\rm SFR,H\beta}>3.5\times 10^{2}M_{\odot}{\rm yr^{-1}\,kpc^{-2}}
and a stellar mass surface density
\Sigma_{*}>2.2\times 10^{4}\,M_{\odot}{\rm pc^{-2}}
at the face value. These values are among the highest even in the literature of recent JWST findings
(
Morishita et al. 2024a
, e.g.,)
.
Schaerer et al. 2024
indeed found that nitrogen emitters occupy the high
\Sigma_{*}
and
\Sigma_{\rm SFR}
regime, arguing that such peculiar conditions may enhance physical processes or “exotic” sources of nucleosynthesis
(
Topping et al. 2024
;
Topping et al. 2025
, also)
.

V
Summary

In this study, we presented a new JWST/NIRSpec observation of a previously known AGN, CANUCS-LRD-z8.6. We detected strong, broad
{\rm Ly\alpha}
emission in the newly taken G140H spectrum. Considering the expected neutral fraction at the redshift (
X_{\rm HI}\sim 1
), this suggests the presence of a large ionized bubble. Our line-profile fitting to the G140H spectrum indeed found
R_{b}=

1.5_{-0.2}^{+0.3}
pMpc using the prescription outlined in
Mason & Gronke 2020
. The origin of this large ionizing bubble was discussed by investigating the photometric candidates in its surrounding. While a future followup is required, existing data indicate that CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 is within a mild overdensity,
\delta=1.9_{-0.7}^{+2.9}
, suggesting that other galaxies surrounding might have contributed to the formation of the ionized bubble.

We also found exceptionally high
\textrm{N}~\textsc{iv}]_{\lambda 1488}
/[O
iii
] ratio, revealed in our reanalysis of existing PRISM data. The detection of the very strong
\textrm{N}~\textsc{iv}]_{\lambda 1488}
line suggests an intriguing idea that CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 may represent one of the evolutionary stages of Nitrogen-rich objects at
z>6
, recently discovered with JWST. Those N-rich objects are reported to also have high-ionization lines, but many of them are not classified as typical AGN either. In this regard, CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 is a unique sample, as its nature is determined as AGN by the detection of broad
{\rm H\beta}
. A notable characteristic about CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 is its high
\textrm{N}~\textsc{iv}]_{\lambda 1488}
/
\textrm{N}~\textsc{iii}]_{\lambda 1747}
ratio. One possible scenario is that, since CANUCS-LRD-z8.6 hosts an AGN, it enhances the ionization of
\textrm{N}~\textsc{iv}]_{\lambda 1488}
, instead of
\textrm{N}~\textsc{iii}]_{\lambda 1747}
, in Nitrogen-enhanced ISM. The observed ratios of
\textrm{N}~\textsc{iv}]_{\lambda 1488}
,
\textrm{C}~\textsc{iv}_{\lambda 1548}
, and
\textrm{O}~\textsc{iii}]_{\lambda 1661}
are found similar to those of nitrogen-enriched sources. Followup observations will determine its definitive abundances along with the electron density measurements.

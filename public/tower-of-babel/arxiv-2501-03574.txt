Abstract
. We prove a Liouville theorem for ancient solutions to the supercritical Fujita equation ∂ t u − Δ ​ u = | u | p − 1 ​ u , − ∞ < t < 0 , p > n + 2 n − 2 , \partial_{t}u-\Delta u=|u|^{p-1}u,\quad-\infty<t<0,\quad p>\frac{n+2}{n-2}, which says if u u is close to the ODE solution u 0 ​ ( t ) := ( p − 1 ) − 1 p − 1 ​ ( − t ) − 1 p − 1 u_{0}(t):=(p-1)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}(-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}} at large scales, then it is an ODE solution (i.e. it depends only on t t ). 1.
Introduction

This paper is a continuation of our previous work
[
50
]
on the blow-up analysis for Fujita equation (or nonlinear heat equation)

\partial_{t}u-\Delta u=|u|^{p-1}u

(F)

in the supercritical case
p>\frac{n+2}{n-2}
(and hence the spatial dimension
n\geq 3
). The main goal of this paper is to prove a Liouville theorem for ancient solutions to (
F
), and then use it to study the structure of end time singularities.

The blow-up problem of (
F
)
has attracted a lot of attention since the work of Fujita
[
18
]
, see e.g. Giga-Kohn
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
and the monograph of Quittner-Souplet
[
39
]
. By now, the subcritical case (
1<p<\frac{n+2}{n-2}
) has been very well understood.
For example, by Giga-Kohn
[
20
]
(in the case of positive solutions) and Giga-Matsui-Sasayama
[
22
]
(in the general case of sign-changing solutions), all interior blow-ups are Type I. That is, if
T
is the first blow-up time (i.e.
u
is smooth before
T
), then

\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(T-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}.

(1.1)

Notice that this blow-up rate is consistent with the blow-ups of the ODE solutions to (
F
),

u_{T}(t)=\pm(p-1)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}(T-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}.

(1.2)

In contrast, if

\limsup_{t\to T}(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=+\infty,

then it is called Type II blow-ups. In the critical (
p=\frac{n+2}{n-2}
) and supercritical (
p>\frac{n+2}{n-2}
) case, there do exist Type II finite time blow-up solutions, see
[
12
,
11
,
13
,
17
,
24
,
25
,
41
]
and
[
8
,
9
,
30
,
42
,
43
]
respectively.

Concerning the supercritical case,
in a series of works
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
, Matano and Merle systematically study the blow-up problem of
radially symmetric solutions
. By using the intersection number technique, they are able to give a complete classification of Type I and Type II blow-up behavior (among many other results). See also Mizoguchi
[
34
,
35
,
36
,
37
]
for more results on the blow-up behavior of radially symmetric solutions. Some of these results will be generalized to the setting
without any symmetry assumption
in this paper.

The main finding of this paper can be summarized as: the ODE solution
u_{T}
plays a special role in the blow-up problem of (
F
). For example, it is stable with respect to base point change, and it contributes to the
(n-1)
dimensional part in the blow-up set (while the remaining part has a lower Hausdorff dimensional bound). This will be achieved by establishing a Liouville theorem for ancient solutions of (
F
), which gives a characterization of this ODE solution.

2.
Setting and main results

2.1.
Setting

We work in the class of
suitable weak solutions
defined in
[
49
]
. First let us recall the definition.

Definition 2.1

(Suitable weak solution)
.

Let
B_{1}
be the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A function
u
, defined on the backward parabolic cylinder
Q_{1}^{-}:=B_{1}\times(-1,0)
, is a suitable weak solution of (
F
), if
\partial_{t}u,\nabla u\in L^{2}(Q_{1}^{-})
,
u\in L^{p+1}(Q_{1}^{-})
, and

•

u
satisfies (
F
) in the weak sense, that is, for any
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{-})
,

\int_{Q_{1}^{-}}\left[\partial_{t}u\eta+\nabla u\cdot\nabla\eta-|u|^{p-1}u\eta\right]dxdt=0;

(2.1)

•

u
satisfies the localized energy inequality: for any
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{-})
,

\int_{Q_{1}^{-}}\left[\left(\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|u|^{p+1}}{p+1}\right)\partial_{t}\eta^{2}-|\partial_{t}u|^{2}\eta^{2}-2\eta\partial_{t}u\nabla u\cdot\nabla\eta\right]dxdt\geq 0;

(2.2)

•

u
satisfies the stationary condition: for any
Y\in C_{0}^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{-},\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\int_{Q_{1}^{-}}\left[\left(\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|u|^{p+1}}{p+1}\right)\mbox{div}Y-DY(\nabla u,\nabla u)-\partial_{t}u\nabla u\cdot Y\right]dxdt=0.

(2.3)

A smooth solution satisfies all of these conditions, which can be easily verified by integration by parts. However, a suitable weak solution need not to be smooth everywhere.

Definition 2.2

(Regular and singular set)
.

Given a suitable weak solution
u
, a point
(x_{0},t_{0})
is said to be a regular point of
u
if there exists
0<r<1
such that
u
is bounded in
Q_{r}^{-}(x_{0},t_{0}):=B_{r}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-r^{2},t_{0})
. The set containing all the regular points is denoted by
\text{Reg}(u)
.

The complement of
\text{Reg}(u)
is the singular set, which is denoted by
\text{Sing}(u)
.

Once
u\in L^{\infty}(Q_{r}^{-}(x_{0},t_{0}))
, then by standard parabolic regularity theory, it is a classical solution in
Q_{r}^{-}(x_{0},t_{0})
. Therefore for each time slice
t
, the set
\{x:(x,t)\in\text{Reg}(u)\}
is open and
\{x:(x,t)\in\text{Sing}(u)\}
is closed.
Clearly,
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\text{Sing}(u)
only if there exists a sequence
(x_{k},t_{k})\to(x_{0},t_{0})
such that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}|u(x_{k},t_{k})|=+\infty
. So the singular set can also be called
the blow-up set
.

Throughout the paper, we denote

m=2\frac{p+1}{p-1}.

And unless otherwise specified, we will always assume that
m
is not an integer
.

Definition 2.3

(Ancient solution)
.

A function
u
is said to be an ancient solution of the equation (
F
) if for any
Q_{r}^{-}(x_{0},t_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,0)
,
u
is a suitable weak solution of (
F
) in
Q_{r}^{-}(x_{0},t_{0})
.

Definition 2.4

(The class
\mathcal{F}_{M}
)
.

Given
M>0
,
\mathcal{F}_{M}
denotes the set of ancient solutions of (
F
) satisfying the following Morrey estimate:
for any

(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,0]
and
r>0
,

r^{m-2-n}\int_{Q_{r}^{-}(x_{0},t_{0}-r^{2})}(|\nabla u|^{2}+|u|^{p+1})dxdt+r^{m-n}\int_{Q_{r}^{-}(x_{0},t_{0}-r^{2})}(\partial_{t}u)^{2}dxdt\leq M.

(2.4)

Remark 2.5
.

This Morrey estimate (
2.4
) is scaling invariant in the following sense: if
u
satisfies (
2.4
), then for any
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,0]
and
\lambda>0
,

u_{x_{0},t_{0},\lambda}(x,t):=\lambda^{\frac{2}{p-1}}u(x_{0}+\lambda x,t_{0}+\lambda^{2}t)

satisfies the same estimate. (Note that
u_{x_{0},t_{0},\lambda}
is also a solution of (
F
). This is exactly the scaling invariance of (
F
).)

The condition (
2.4
) arises from the blow-up analysis for (
F
). It is usually deduced from Giga-Kohn’s monotonicity formula, cf.
[
20
, Proposition 2.2]
or
[
21
, Proposition 3.1]
. It should be emphasized that there is a time lag in
Q_{r}^{-}(x_{0},t_{0}-r^{2})
, that is, we must use
t_{0}-r^{2}
instead of
t_{0}
. We notice that a different Morrey space estimate was also proved in Souplet
[
44
]
.

Definition 2.6

(The class
\mathcal{S}_{M}
of backward self-similar solutions)
.

A function
w
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, belongs to

\mathcal{S}_{M}
if
(-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}w(\frac{x}{\sqrt{-t}})\in\mathcal{F}_{M}
.

The function
u(x,t):=(-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}w(\frac{x}{\sqrt{-t}})
satisfies

u(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t)=\lambda^{\frac{2}{p-1}}u(x,t),\quad\forall\lambda>0,

hence the name “backward self-similar solutions”. Because
u
is a solution to (
F
), it is well known that
w
is a solution to the elliptic equation

-\Delta w+\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla w+\frac{1}{p-1}w=|w|^{p-1}w,\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(SS)

Notice that since we require more conditions on suitable weak solutions, for
w\in\mathcal{S}_{M}
, it also satisfies some integral identities inherited from (
2.2
) and (
2.3
).

By denoting

\kappa=(p-1)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}},

it is clear that
0
and
\pm\kappa
are the only constant solutions of (
SS
). The constant solutions
\pm\kappa
correspond to the ODE solutions
\pm u_{0}
defined in (
1.2
). As said before, we will show that these constant solutions are very special among solutions of (
SS
).

2.2.
Liouville theorem

Now we can state the first main result of this paper, which is a Liouville theorem for ancient solutions.

Theorem 2.7
.

Given an ancient solution
u\in\mathcal{F}_{M}
, if there exists
\lambda_{k}\to+\infty
such that

\lambda_{k}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}u(\lambda_{k}x,\lambda_{k}^{2}t)\to\kappa(-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\quad\text{strongly in}~~L_{loc}^{p+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,0)),

(2.5)

then there exists a constant
T\geq 0
such that

u(x,t)=\kappa(T-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}},\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,0).

Remark 2.8
.

(1)

The same result holds for
-\kappa(-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}
.

(2)

The function sequence in (
2.5
) is a blow-down sequence. Its strong convergence in
L_{loc}^{p+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,0))
can be established by using the fact that
m
is not an integer, see Proposition
3.6
below.

(3)

In
[
38
]
, Poláčik and Quittner also proved a Liouville theorem for ancient and entire solutions to supercritical Fujita equation, which however is concerned with the characterization of steady states and is restricted to radially symmetric solutions.

As a special case of Theorem
2.7
, we can prove the following classification result for low entropy ancient solutions.

Corollary 2.9

(Low-entropy ancient solutions)
.

Assume
\frac{n+2}{n-2}<p<\frac{n+1}{n-3}
and
u\in\mathcal{F}_{M}
is a nonnegative ancient solution of (
F
). If

\sup_{t\in(-\infty,0)}\lambda(u(\cdot,t))\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\right)\left(\frac{1}{p-1}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}},

where
\lambda(u(\cdot,t))
is the entropy defined in
[
50
]
, then either
u=0
or there exists a constant
T\geq 0
such that

u(x,t)=\kappa(T-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}},\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,0).

2.3.
Applications: End time singular set

For finite time blow-up solution of (
F
), one question that has received great attention is the structure and the regularity of the blow-up set
\text{Sing}(u)
, see
[
1
,
4
,
14
,
21
,
27
,
48
,
52
,
53
]
.

Definition 2.10

(Blow-up sequence and tangent function)
.

Given a base point
(x_{0},t_{0})
and a constant
\lambda>0
, denote

u_{x_{0},t_{0},\lambda}(x,t):=\lambda^{\frac{2}{p-1}}u(x_{0}+\lambda x,t_{0}+\lambda^{2}t).

If there exists a sequence
\lambda_{k}\to 0^{+}
and a function
w_{0}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}
such that

u_{x_{0},t_{0},\lambda_{k}}\to(-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}w_{0}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{-t}}\right)\quad\text{in}~L^{p+1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,0)),

then
w_{0}
is called a tangent function of
u
at
(x_{0},t_{0})
.

The set of tangent functions at
(x_{0},t_{0})
is denoted by
\mathcal{T}(x_{0},t_{0};u)
.

Blow-down sequence can be defined in the same way by using sequences
\lambda_{k}\to+\infty
. The convergence of blow-up sequences and the self-similarity of their limits can be established by compactness arguments and monotonocity formula, see Section
3
.

A more familiar form used since the work of Giga-Kohn
[
19
]
is to reformulate the equation (
F
) in self-similar coordinates.

Definition 2.11

(Self-similar transform)
.

Given a suitable weak ancient solution
u
of (
F
), its self-similar transform with respect to a base point
(x_{0},t_{0})
is

w(y,s)=(t_{0}-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}u(x,t),\quad y=(t_{0}-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(x-x_{0}),\quad s=-\log(t_{0}-t).

(2.6)

The function
w
obtained by taking self-similar transform is a solution of

\partial_{s}w=\Delta w-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla w-\frac{1}{p-1}w+|w|^{p-1}w,\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,+\infty).

(RF)

Notice that (
SS
) is just the steady version of (
RF
). The convergence of blow-up sequence to tangent functions in Definition
2.10
is equivalent to the convergence of
w(\cdot,s)
as
s\to+\infty
, and the convergence of blow-down sequence corresponds to the convergence as
s\to-\infty
. Just as in the subcritical case considered in Giga-Kohn
[
19
]
, this convergence is
usually deduced from the gradient flow structure of (
RF
):
For any
w\in H^{1}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{p+1}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, let

E(w)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[\frac{1}{2}|\nabla w|^{2}+\frac{1}{2(p-1)}w^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}|w|^{p+1}\right]\rho dy

(E)

be the energy of
w
. Then (
RF
) is the gradient flow of this energy functional, while (
SS
) is its Euler-Lagrange equation.

Theorem
2.7
allows us to prove the following result.

Theorem 2.12

(Uniqueness and stability of ODE blow-ups)
.

Let
u
be a suitable weak solution of (
F
) in
Q_{1}^{-}
, satisfying the Morrey estimate (
2.4
) for all backward parabolic cylinders of the form
Q_{r}^{-}(x,t-r^{2})\subset Q_{1}^{-}
, where
(x,t)\in Q_{1}^{-}
.

If
\kappa\in\mathcal{T}(0,0;u)
, then the followings hold.

(A)

Uniqueness of blow-up limit:
\mathcal{T}(0,0;u)=\{\kappa\}
;

(B)

Stability of ODE blow-up: there exists
\delta>0
such that for any
x\in B_{\delta}(0)\cap\text{Sing}(u)
,
\mathcal{T}(x,0;u)=\{\kappa\}
;

(C)

Positivity preserving:
u>0
in
Q_{\delta}^{-}(0,0)
;

(D)

Type I blow-up rate:
u
is smooth in
Q_{\delta}^{-}(0,0)
and there exists a constant
C>0
such that

u(x,t)\leq C(-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}},\quad\forall(x,t)\in Q_{\delta}^{-}(0,0).

(2.7)

Remark 2.13
.

(1)

Item
(A)
in Theorem
2.12
justifies rigorously the discussions in
[
27
]
. (See the
discussions below Remark 2.4 therein.) Different from
[
27
]
, we do not need any assumption on the symmetry of solutions.

(2)

The uniqueness and stability property of ODE blow-ups can be understood in the following way: if a solution is close to the ODE solution at one scale, then it is even closer to the ODE solution on smaller scales, and this not only holds with respect to the original base point, but also with respect to all nearby singular points.

This is very different from existing reulsts on the stability of Type I blow-ups in the literature, e.g. the one in Collot-Raphael-Szeftel
[
10
]
. Those are mostly on the functional analysis side, where the equation is viewed as a dynamical system in some function spaces, and then a kind of dynamical stability of steady states (i.e. solutions of (
SS
)) is established. In contrast, in the above stability result, we do not impose any perturbation on initial values. (In fact, there is no initial value because we are only concerned with solutions in backward parabolic cylinders.)

This stability property is closer to the result for generic singularities in mean curvature flows, see Colding-Minicozzi
[
7
]
. Just as there, the above theorem says that ODE solutions are generic among all blow-ups in (
F
). Furthermore, the proof of our results also rely on some ideas inspired by Colding-Minicoozi
[
6
]
and Colding-Ilmanen-Minicozzi
[
5
]
.

(3)

The stability property also occurs in mean curvature flows (see
[
40
,
45
]
). In
[
40
, Theorem 1.7]
, Schulze and Sesum proved the ‌stability of neckpinch singularities. But it seems that the mechanism behind them are very different, see Subsection 2.4 for more discussions.

By the above theorem, the following subsets of
\text{Sing}(u)
are well defined,

\mathcal{R}^{\pm}:=\{x:\mathcal{T}(x,0;u)=\{\pm\kappa\}\}.

Our last main result is about the structure of
\mathcal{R}^{\pm}
and its complement
\text{Sing}(u)\setminus(\mathcal{R}^{+}\cup\mathcal{R}^{-})
.

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
u
be a solution of the equation (
F
) satisfying the assumptions in Theorem
2.12
. If
\text{Sing}(u)\cap\{t=0\}\neq\emptyset
and

u
is not an ODE solution,
then

(A)

both
\mathcal{R}^{+}
and
\mathcal{R}^{-}
are relatively open in
\{x:(x,0)\in\text{Sing}(u)\}
;

(B)

\mathcal{R}^{\pm}
are
(n-1)
-rectifiable sets;

(C)

the Hausdorff dimension of
\{x:(x,0)\in\text{Sing}(u)\}\setminus(\mathcal{R}^{+}\cup\mathcal{R}^{-})
is at most
n-[m]-1
, where
[m]
is the integer part of
m
.

Remark 2.15

(End point singularity)
.

In the above theorem, the Hausdorff dimension estimate in
(C)
is the same with the one in
[
49
, Theorem 7.1]
. However, that theorem is
concerned with singular sets in the full parabolic cylinder
Q_{1}:=B_{1}\times(-1,1)
, while the above theorem is about the singular set at the end time. This leads to the difference related to the two additional parts
\mathcal{R}^{\pm}
in
\{x:(x,0)\in\text{Sing}(u)\}
. The above theorem says that, these
(n-1)
dimensional parts arise just because the tangent function is an ODE solution. In other words, if a solution
u
can be continued further in the time, then the tangent function at a singular point cannot be ODE solutions.
1
1

1

This is because ODE solutions do not satisfy the Morrey space estimate (
2.4
) for any base point
(x_{0},t_{0})
with
t_{0}>0
.
Once this obstacle does not exist, then Federer dimension reduction principle can be applied to lower the Hausdorff dimension estimate down to
n-[m]-1
.

In view of this fact, it seems natural to conjecture that a solution to (
F
) in
Q_{1}^{-}
can be continued beyond time
0
if and only if these ODE blow-ups does not appear at any singular point on
B_{1}\times\{0\}
.

.

For any

, if

If

is bounded on

If

2.4.
Difficulties and approaches

Theorem
2.7
is a generalization of the following Liouville theorem of Merle-Zaag
[
32
, Corollary 1.6]
to the supercritical setting.

Theorem 2.19

(Merle-Zaag
[
32
]
)
.

If
1<p<\frac{n+2}{n-2}
and
u
satisfies

0\leq u(x,t)\leq C(-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}},\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,0),

then either
u\equiv 0
or there exists a constant
T\geq 0
such that

u(x,t)=\kappa(T-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}},\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,0).

The proof in
[
32
]
can be summarized as follows:

Step 1:

Define the self-similar transform
w
as in (
2.6
).
Then
w
is a
bounded solution
of (
RF
).

Step 2:

Because
p
is subcritical, by Giga-Kohn
[
19
]
,
w_{\pm\infty}(y)=\lim_{s\to\pm\infty}w(y,s)
exists and they are bounded solutions of (
SS
). By Giga-Kohn’s Liouville theorem, they could only be
0
or
\kappa
. By Giga-Kohn’s monotonicity formula, they can exclude the possibility that

(w_{+\infty},w_{-\infty})=(0,\kappa)
. Moreover, if

(w_{+\infty},w_{-\infty})=(0,0)
or
(w_{+\infty},w_{-\infty})=(\kappa,\kappa)
, then
w
is a constant solution.

Step 3:

For the remaining possibility
(w_{+\infty},w_{-\infty})=(\kappa,0)
, they linearize
w
around the constant solution by defining
v=w-\kappa
. Then they proved the famous Merle-Zaag lemma (see
[
32
, Lemma A.1]
), and used this to classify the behavior of
v
as
s\to-\infty
.

Step 4:

By combining the blow-up criteria and some geometrical transformation, they can finish the proof.

In our setting, the exponent
p
is supercritical and the solution is only a suitable weak ancient solution, so the following difficulties will be encountered.

First, we can still take the same self-similar transform as in Step 1, but now
w
is not a
bounded solution
of (
RF
). So even if
w_{\pm\infty}(y)=\lim_{s\to\pm\infty}w(y,s)
exists, the convergence holds only in some weak sense, and a priori
w_{\pm\infty}
may not be smooth.

Second, it seems impossible to get any classification for solutions to (
SS

It turns out that under the assumption (
2.5
), the above difficulties can all be overcome. The main reason is: (

) says that the ancient solution

looks like the ODE solution
u_{0}
at large scales, so we can perform
a linearization analysis
around
u_{0}
. However, to implement this idea, in particular, to circumvent the difficulty by the a priori irregularity of
u
, we need to introduce
a key auxiliary function

. This function can be roughly understood as the first time when
u
is not close to
u_{0}
. By using this function together with standard blow-up arguments, we can get the same setting (e.g. a bounded solution

of (

)) as in the above subcritical case.

To define
RT
, first, we establish a rigidity theorem, which says

\kappa
is isolated among solutions to (
SS
). This then implies that the ODE solution
u_{0}
is the
unique
blow-down limit. This rigidity result is inspired by the rigidity theorem for self-shrinkers to mean curvature flow in Colding-Ilmanen-Minicozzi
[
5
]
. We point out that in mean curvature flows, the entropy and the Gaussian density is nonnegative and the second fundamental form of self-shrinkers satisfies the same equation as its mean curvature (see
[

), but these do not have natural counterparts for (

Because

is a smooth ancient solution of (

), uniqueness of the blow-down limit
implies that its self-similar transform,

is locally bounded for all

the key auxiliary function

, which can be roughly defined as the first time the condition

|\Delta u(x,t)|<\frac{1}{2}u(x,t)^{p}

is violated, that is, the first time when
u
is not close to the ODE solution
u_{0}
.

If

is bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then we can show that the self-similar transform

is bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,-T_{0})
for some

. This allows us to apply Merle-Zaag lemma as in Step 3 above to deduce that

(hence

) is an ODE solution.

Of course,

could also be unbounded. This needs us to perform rescaling one more time, which then gives a contradiction with the above result in the bounded

For mean curvature flows, Choi-Haslhofer-Hershkovits
[
2
]
and Choi-Haslhofer-Hershkovits-White
[
3
]
have established some Liouville properties for ancient solutions. These results give a characterization of cylinders (as self-shrinkers to mean curvature flows), and our Liouville theorem has a formal correspondence with these results. But it seems that the nature of these two Liouville properties are very different.
In fact, the ODE solution
u_{0}
in our problem should correspond to spheres (as self-shrinkers to mean curvature flows), but it also has many similarities with cylinders. For example, some form of Merle-Zaag lemma are all needed in the proof of these Liouville results.

Finally, the assumption that
m
is not an integer is very important for our proof. This is because we need a crucial compactness properties for suitable weak solutions of (
F
), see Proposition
3.3
below. In fact, if

is an integer, the class
\mathcal{F}_{M}
is not compact in

or
H^{1}_{loc}
. This is because we can not apply Marstrand theorem (see
[

, Theorem 1.3.12]
) to prove that some defect measures

The precise definition can be found in
[

, Section 4]
are trivial. We believe the analysis of this case needs more work.

Lemma 3.7

There exists a universal, positive constant

Proof.

Denote

. For each

\int_{-2}^{-1}\int_{|x|\leq 2\lambda}|u|^{p+1}dxdt\leq C\lambda^{n},

\int_{-2}^{-1}\int_{|x|\leq\sqrt{-t}\lambda}|u|^{p+1}dxdt\leq C\lambda^{n}.

By the definition of

\int_{-2}^{-1}\int_{|x|\leq\sqrt{-t}\lambda}|u|^{p+1}dxdt=\int_{-2}^{-1}(-t)^{-\frac{p+1}{p-1}+\frac{n}{2}}dt\int_{|y|\leq\lambda}|w|^{p+1}dy.

(-t)^{-\frac{p+1}{p-1}+\frac{n}{2}}\geq 1,\quad\text{in}~(-2,-1).

By this estimate, we calculate

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w(y)|^{p+1}(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}dy=

5.
Rigidity of the constant solutions

In this section, we prove two rigidity results for the constant solutions
\pm\kappa
. The first rigidity theorem says that
\kappa
is isolated among all solutions to (

) with respect to the

Theorem 5.1
.

There exists a positive constant
\varepsilon_{1}
such that if
w\in\mathcal{S}_{M}
and

\|w-\kappa\|_{L^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\varepsilon_{1},

(5.1)

then
w\equiv\kappa
.

To state the second rigidity theorem, we need a notation.
For any
R>1
, if
w_{1},w_{2}
are two continuous functions on
\overline{B_{R}(0)}
, define

\text{dist}_{R}(w_{1},w_{2})=\sup_{y\in B_{R}(0)}|w_{1}(y)-w_{2}(y)|.

The second rigidity result says
\kappa
is isolated with respect to this distance. We emphasize that this rigidity result holds for any
p>1
, that is, it does not need the restriction that
m=2\frac{p+1}{p-1}
is not an integer.

Theorem 5.2
.

There exist
\varepsilon_{2}
and
R_{\ast}
depending only on

such that if
w\in\mathcal{S}_{M}
, it is smooth in
\overline{B_{R_{\ast}}(0)}
and

\text{dist}_{R_{\ast}}(w,\kappa)\leq\varepsilon_{2},

(5.2)

then
w\equiv\kappa
.

Remark 5.3
.

(1)

A consequence of the
\varepsilon
-regularity result (Proposition
3.2
) is that among solutions to (
SS
),
0
is isolated. The above theorem says that the other two constant solutions,
\pm\kappa
, are also isolated.

(2)

Theorem
5.2
says that if a self-similar solution is close to
\kappa
in a sufficiently large ball, then it must be
\kappa
itself. This holds even when the self-similar solution is not presumed to be smooth outside of the ball. This fact is important in applications.

The first rigidity result will follow from the second one together with some compactness properties of
\mathcal{S}_{M}
. Theorem
5.2
is inspired by the corresponding result for self-shrinkers to mean curvature flows in Colding-Ilmanen-Minicozzi
[
5
]
.
The proof also follows the iteration arguments developed there. This iteration relies on the following two propositions.

Proposition 5.4
.

There exists a universal constant

such that for any

, if

,

\text{dist}_{R-3}(w,\kappa)+\text{dist}_{R-3}\left(\Lambda(w),\frac{2}{p-1}\kappa\right)\leq\frac{\kappa}{10(p+1)}.

.

There exist two universal constants

and

, if

,

is smooth in

\text{dist}_{R}(w,\kappa)+\text{dist}_{R}\left(\Lambda(w),\frac{2}{p-1}\kappa\right)\leq\frac{\kappa}{10(p+1)},

then

is smooth in
\overline{B_{(1+\theta)R}(0)}
and

\text{dist}_{(1+\theta)R}(w,\kappa)+\text{dist}_{(1+\theta)R}\left(\Lambda(w),\frac{2}{p-1}\kappa\right)\leq\frac{\kappa}{p+1}.

Proposition

6.
Uniqueness of the blow-down limit

In order to prove Theorem

, one key step is to obtain the
unique
asymptotic behavior of

at large scales. This is the content of the following

Proposition 6.1

(Uniqueness of blow-down limit)
.

Assume
u
satisfies the assumptions in Theorem
2.7
, then

\lim_{t\to-\infty}(-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}u((-t)^{\frac{1}{2}}y,t)=\kappa\quad\text{in}~C_{loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(6.1)

Remark 6.2
.

The same arguments can also be used to show the uniqueness of the tangent functions, i.e. Item
(A)
in Theorem
2.12

Roughly speaking, the above uniqueness result follows from two facts: (i) Theorem
5.1
, that is,
\kappa
is isolated in
\mathcal{S}_{M}
with respect to the
L_{w}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
distance; (ii) the set of tangent functions is path connected.

First, we give a preliminary result about the convergence in self-similar coordinates.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{F}_{M}
and
w
denote its self-similar transform. Then for any
s_{k}\to-\infty
or
s_{k}\to+\infty
, there exists a subsequence of
s_{k}
such that
w(y,s+s_{k})
converges to a limit
w_{\infty}
strongly in
L^{p+1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R})\cap H^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R})
. Moreover,
w_{\infty}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}
and
w(\cdot,s_{k})
converges to
w_{\infty}
strongly in
L^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

We need only to prove
w(\cdot,s_{k})
converges to
w_{\infty}
strongly in
L^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The other properties are just a rescaling of the convergence in Proposition
3.6
.

By Lemma
3.1
and Corollary
3.8
, the energy
E(w(\cdot,s))
(see (
E
) for definition) is a decreasing function of
s
and

\lim_{s\to-\infty}E(w(\cdot,s))<+\infty.

(6.2)

By the localized energy inequality (
2.2
) and an approximation in
H^{1}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{p+1}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we get Giga-Kohn’s monotonicity formula in self-similar form,

\frac{d}{ds}E(w(\cdot,s))=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{s}w(y,s)|^{2}\rho(y)dy.

(6.3)

We emphasize that this should be understood in the distributional sense, because the localized energy inequality (
2.2
) holds only in the distributional sense.

Combining (
6.3
) with (
6.2
), we get

\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{s}w(y,s)|^{2}\rho(y)dyds<+\infty.

On the other hand, testing (

) with

, we get (still understood in the distributional sense)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{s}ww\rho dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[|w|^{p+1}-|\nabla w|^{2}-\frac{1}{p-1}w^{2}\right]\rho dy.

Then following the proof of
[

, we get

\sup_{s<0}\int_{s-1}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[|\partial_{s}w(y,s)|^{2}+|\nabla w(y,s)|^{2}+|w(y,s)|^{p+1}\right]\rho(y)dy<+\infty.

By Sobolev embedding theorem, this implies that
w(\cdot,s)\in C^{\alpha}((-\infty,0];L^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
for some

. It follows that
w(\cdot,s)-w_{\infty}\in C^{\alpha}((-\infty,0];L^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.

Assume

does not converge strongly in

to

. Then there exists a constant

such that

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w(\cdot,s_{k})-w_{\infty}|^{2}\rho dy>\delta_{0}.

Since
w(\cdot,s)-w_{\infty}\in C^{\alpha}((-\infty,0];L^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, there exist a constant
0<\delta_{1}<1/16
and a subsequence
\{k_{l}\}
such that for each

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w(\cdot,s)-w_{\infty}|^{2}\rho dy>\frac{\delta_{0}}{2},\quad\text{for}~s\in(s_{k_{l}}-\delta_{1},s_{k_{l}}+\delta_{1}).

In particular, we have

\int_{s_{k_{l}}-\delta_{1}}^{s_{k_{l}}+\delta_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w(\cdot,s)-w_{\infty}|^{2}\rho dyds>\delta_{0}\delta_{1}.

(6.4)

By Lemma
3.9
, there exists a positive constant
R
such that for each
k_{l}
,

\int_{s_{k_{l}}-\delta_{1}}^{s_{k_{l}}+\delta_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{R}}|w(\cdot,s)-w_{\infty}|^{2}\rho dyds<\frac{\delta_{0}\delta_{1}}{2}.

(6.5)

Since
w(y,s+s_{k})
converges to
w_{\infty}
strongly in

, there exists a constant

such that for each

\int_{s_{k_{l}}-\delta_{1}}^{s_{k_{l}}+\delta_{1}}\int_{B_{R}}|w(\cdot,s)-w_{\infty}|^{2}\rho dyds<\frac{\delta_{0}\delta_{1}}{2}.

(6.6)

By (
6.5
) and (
6.5
), we know that for each

\int_{s_{k_{l}}-\delta_{1}}^{s_{k_{l}}+\delta_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w(\cdot,s)-w_{\infty}|^{2}\rho dyds<\delta_{0}\delta_{1},

which contradicts (

∎

An auxiliary function

In this section,

always denotes a solution of (

Definition 7.1

For any

As a consequence of Proposition

The function

Lemma 7.2
.

Let

be
the function defined in Definition

(1)

(2)

Proof.

(1) By Proposition

, there exists a large constant

is smooth and positive, and there holds

|\Delta u(x,t)|\leq\frac{1}{C}(-t)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}<\frac{1}{2}u(x,t)^{p}.

Hence

(2) Assume by the contrary that there exists an
\varepsilon>0
and a sequence
\{x_{k}\}
such that
\lim_{k\to+\infty}|x_{k}|=+\infty

\frac{|x_{k}|}{\lambda_{k}}\leq\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

By the definition of

(1)

is a singular point;

(2)

(x_{k},-RT(x_{k}))
is a regular point but

(3)

(x_{k},-RT(x_{k}))

|\Delta u(x_{k},-RT(x_{k}))|=\frac{1}{2}u(x_{k},-RT(x_{k}))^{p}.

Define

It is still an ancient solution of (

(1’)

(2’)

(3’)

(x_{k}/\lambda_{k},-1)

Proposition

implies that the convergence in (

u_{k}(x_{k}/\lambda_{k},-1)>0

\left|\Delta u_{k}(\frac{x_{k}}{\lambda_{k}},-1)\right|=\frac{1}{2}u_{k}\left(\frac{x_{k}}{\lambda_{k}},-1\right)^{p}\to\frac{1}{2}\kappa^{p}.

But using the smooth convergence of

8.
A conditional Liouville theorem

In this section, our main objective is to prove the following conditional Liouville type result.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
u
be a positive smooth solution of (

\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,-T_{0})

To prove this proposition, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 8.2

Under the assumptions of Proposition

Proof.

Combining (

introduced at the beginning of the previous section, with

).
By Theorem
2.12

(D)
and standard parabolic regularity theory, there exists a positive constant

Moreover,
\text{spt}(h)\subset(B_{1}(0)\backslash B_{1/2}(0))\times(-\infty,0)
.

Take an arbitrary point

Let

Recall that the operator

The following theorem is the main result of Velázquez

Theorem 11.1

If

(1)

Either there exists an orthogonal transformation of coordinate axes, still denoted by

With the Type I blow-up rate (

∎

Appendix A
Proof of Proposition
5.4

The proof of Proposition
5.4
needs several lemmas.

Lemma A.1
.

For any
R_{0}>1
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
R_{1}\geq R_{0}
such that if
R>R_{1}
,
w\in\mathcal{S}_{M}
is smooth in
\overline{B_{R}(0)}
and it satisfies

\text{dist}_{R}(w,\kappa)+\text{dist}_{R}\left(\Lambda(w),\frac{2}{p-1}\kappa\right)\leq\frac{\kappa}{p+1},

then

\text{dist}_{R_{0}}(w,\kappa)+\text{dist}_{R_{0}}\left(\Lambda(w),\frac{2}{p-1}\kappa\right)\leq\varepsilon.

(A.1)

Proof.

Assume by the contrary that there exist
R_{0}
and
\varepsilon
, and two sequences
w_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}
,
R_{i}\to+\infty
such that
w_{i}
is smooth in
\overline{B_{R_{i}}(0)}
,

\displaystyle\text{dist}_{R_{i}}(w_{i},\kappa)+\text{dist}_{R_{i}}\left(\Lambda(w_{i}),\frac{2}{p-1}\kappa\right)\leq\frac{\kappa}{p+1},

(A.2)

but

\text{dist}_{R_{0}}(w_{i},\kappa)+\text{dist}_{R_{0}}\left(\Lambda(w_{i}),\frac{2}{p-1}\kappa\right)>\varepsilon.

(A.3)

By (
A.2
) and standard elliptic regularity theory, there exists a function
w_{\infty}
such that
w_{i}\to w_{\infty}
in
C^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In particular,
w_{\infty}
is a smooth solution of (
SS
). Taking limit in (
A.2
) leads to

|w_{\infty}-\kappa|+\left|\Lambda(w_{\infty})-\frac{2}{p-1}\kappa\right|\leq\frac{\kappa}{p+1}\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(A.4)

Then by Proposition
4.1
,
w_{\infty}\equiv\kappa
.

On the other hand, by the
C^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
convergence of
w_{i}
, taking limit in (
A.3
) gives

\text{dist}_{R_{0}}(w_{\infty},\kappa)+\text{dist}_{R_{0}}\left(\Lambda(w_{\infty}),\frac{2}{p-1}\kappa\right)\geq\varepsilon.

(A.5)

This is a contradiction.
∎

The above lemma implies that on a fixed compact subset, we can make
w
as close to
\kappa
as possible. In order to finish the proof of Proposition
5.4
, it suffices to show that both
\nabla w
and
\nabla\Lambda(w)
are exponentially small in
R
. For this purpose, we define

\tau:=\frac{w^{p}}{\Lambda(w)}.

This function is well defined because both
w
and
\Lambda(w)
are continuous, positive functions on
B_{R}(0)
.

The auxiliary function
\tau
satisfies the following equations.

Lemma A.2
.

The function
\tau
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Lambda(w)^{2}\rho}{\rm\text{div}}\left(\Lambda(w)^{2}\rho\nabla\tau\right)=\frac{p(p-1)w^{p-2}|\nabla w|^{2}}{\Lambda(w)}

(A.6)

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Lambda(w)^{2}\rho}{\rm\text{div}}\left(\Lambda(w)^{2}\rho\nabla\tau^{2}\right)=2|\nabla\tau|^{2}+\frac{2p(p-1)\tau w^{p-2}|\nabla w|^{2}}{\Lambda(w)}.

(A.7)

Proof.

Since
w
is a solution of (
SS
),
w^{p}
satisfies the equation

\displaystyle\Delta w^{p}-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla w^{p}+\frac{1}{p-1}w^{p}+pw^{p-1}w^{p}-w^{p}=p(p-1)w^{p-2}|\nabla w|^{2}.

Moreover, we get from (
SS
) that
\Lambda(w)
satisfies

\Delta\Lambda(w)-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla\Lambda(w)-\frac{1}{p-1}\Lambda(w)+pw^{p-1}\Lambda(w)-\Lambda(w)=0.

(A.8)

By a direct computation, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Lambda(w)^{2}\rho}{\rm\text{div}}\left(\Lambda(w)^{2}\rho\nabla\tau\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta\tau+\frac{2}{\Lambda(w)}\nabla\Lambda(w)\cdot\nabla\tau-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda(w)\Delta w^{p}-w^{p}\Delta\Lambda(w)}{\Lambda(w)^{2}}-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda(w)^{-1}\left[\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla w^{p}+\frac{p}{p-1}w^{p}-pw^{p-1}w^{p}+p(p-1)w^{p-2}|\nabla w|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{w^{p}}{\Lambda(w)^{2}}\left[\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla\Lambda(w)+\frac{1}{p-1}\Lambda(w)-pw^{p-1}\Lambda(w)+\Lambda(w)\right]-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p(p-1)w^{p-2}|\nabla w|^{2}}{\Lambda(w)},

which is (
A.6
).

Since

\frac{1}{\Lambda(w)^{2}\rho}{\rm\text{div}}\left(\Lambda(w)^{2}\rho\nabla\tau^{2}\right)=\frac{2\tau}{\Lambda(w)^{2}\rho}{\rm\text{div}}(\Lambda(w)^{2}\rho\nabla\tau)+2|\nabla\tau|^{2},

(
A.7
) follows from (
A.6
).
∎

By Lemma
A.2
, we can obtain the following integral estimate on
|\nabla w|
and
|\nabla\Lambda(w)|
.

Lemma A.3
.

For any
s\in(0,R)
, we have

\int_{B_{R-s}(0)}\left[|\nabla\tau|^{2}+\frac{\tau w^{p-2}|\nabla w|^{2}}{\Lambda(w)}\right]\Lambda(w)^{2}\rho dy\leq\frac{C}{s^{2}}R^{n}e^{-\frac{(R-s)^{2}}{4}}\sup_{y\in B_{R}(0)}w(y)^{2p},

(A.9)

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
R
.

Proof.

For any
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{R}(0))
, using the divergence theorem, we get

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}{\rm\text{div}}\left(\Lambda(w)^{2}\rho\phi^{2}\nabla\tau^{2}\right)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{B_{R}(0)}\phi^{2}\left[|\nabla\tau|^{2}+\frac{p(p-1)\tau w^{p-2}|\nabla w|^{2}}{\Lambda(w)}\right]\Lambda(w)^{2}\rho dy

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 4\int_{B_{R}(0)}\phi\tau\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\tau\Lambda(w)^{2}\rho dy.

Applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
4ab\leq a^{2}+4b^{2}
, we obtain

4\int_{B_{R}(0)}\phi\tau\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\tau\Lambda(w)^{2}\rho dy\leq\int_{B_{R}(0)}|\nabla\tau|^{2}\phi^{2}\Lambda(w)^{2}\rho dy+4\int_{B_{R}(0)}\tau^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\Lambda(w)^{2}\rho dy.

Hence there exists a universal positive constant
C
such that

\int_{B_{R}(0)}\phi^{2}\left[|\nabla\tau|^{2}+\frac{\tau w^{p-2}|\nabla w|^{2}}{\Lambda(w)}\right]\Lambda(w)^{2}\rho dy\leq C\int_{B_{R}(0)}\tau^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\Lambda(w)^{2}\rho dy.

(A.10)

Choose
\phi
satisfying

\phi\equiv 1
in
B_{R-s}(0)
and

|\nabla\phi|\leq 2/s
in
B_{R}(0)\backslash B_{R-s}(0)
.
Then
(
A.9
) follows from (
A.10
), if we note that by definition,
\tau\Lambda(w)=w^{p}
.
∎

Combing the integral estimates of Lemma
A.3
with standard elliptic estimates, we can establish the following pointwise bound on
|\nabla w|
and
|\nabla\Lambda(w)|
.

Lemma A.4
.

There exists a universal constant
C
such that

\sup_{y\in B_{R-3}(0)}(|\nabla\Lambda(w)|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2})\leq CR^{2n}e^{-\frac{R}{4}}.

(A.11)

Proof.

Lemma
A.3
with
s=1/2
gives

\int_{B_{R-\frac{1}{2}}(0)}\left[|\nabla\tau|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\right]\rho dy\leq CR^{n}\exp\left\{-\frac{(R-\frac{1}{2})^{2}}{4}\right\}.

(A.12)

Plugging the relation

\nabla\tau=\frac{pw^{p-1}}{\Lambda(w)}\nabla w-\frac{w^{p}}{\Lambda(w)^{2}}\nabla\Lambda(w)

into (
A.12
), we get

\int_{B_{R-\frac{1}{2}}(0)}\left[|\nabla\Lambda(w)|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\right]\rho dy\leq CR^{n}\exp\left\{-\frac{(R-\frac{1}{2})^{2}}{4}\right\}.

Since

e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}\geq\exp\left\{-\frac{R^{2}-2R+1}{4}\right\},\quad\text{on}~B_{R-1}(0),

it follows that

\int_{B_{R-1}(0)}\left[|\nabla\Lambda(w)|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\right]dy\leq CR^{n}\exp\left\{-\frac{R}{4}\right\}.

(A.13)

This is the integral decay on
\nabla\Lambda(w)
and
\nabla w
.

Then we combine (
A.13
) with elliptic regularity theory to get the pointwise bound. Let
\mathcal{L}
be the linear operator defined by

\mathcal{L}\psi=\Delta\psi-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla\psi-\frac{1}{p-1}\psi+pw^{p-1}\psi.

Since
w
satisfies (
SS
), for each
i=1
,
\dots
,
n
,

\mathcal{L}\partial_{x_{i}}w-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x_{i}}w=0,\quad\text{in}~B_{R}(0).

Moreover, we get from (
A.8
) that for

,

\mathcal{L}\Lambda(w)_{i}-\Lambda(w)_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\Lambda(w)_{i}+p(p-1)w^{p-2}w_{i}\Lambda(w)=0,\quad\text{in}~B_{R}(0).

Notice that the first order term in

grows at most linearly. By applying standard elliptic regularity theory (see
[
23
, Theorem 9.20]
) on

(for any

), we get

|\nabla w(x)|^{2}\leq CR^{n}\int_{B_{1/R}(x)}|\nabla w|^{2}

(A.14)

and

|\nabla\Lambda(w)(x)|^{2}\leq CR^{n}\int_{B_{1/R}(y_{0})}|\nabla\Lambda(w)|^{2}+CR^{2n-2}e^{-\frac{R}{4}}.

(A.15)

Combining (
A.14
), (
A.15
) with the integral bound (

) gives (
A.11
).
∎

Now we are ready to prove Proposition

.

Proof of Proposition
5.4
.

Take
R_{0}=5\sqrt{n}
and
\varepsilon=\frac{\kappa}{100(p+1)}

. Then

Appendix B
Proof of Proposition

In this appendix, we prove Proposition

. The main observation here is that by shrinking
B_{R}(0)
slightly, we can choose a universal constant
s_{\ast}
so that the
\varepsilon
-regularity result, Proposition

is applicable. Now we give the details.

Throughout this appendix,
w
denotes a solution in
\mathcal{S}_{M}
satisfying the assumptions in Proposition
5.5
. By definition,
u(x,t)=(-t)^{-1/(p-1)}w(x/\sqrt{-t})\in\mathcal{F}_{M}
.

The following result, which is essentially
[
19
, Proposition 1’]
, gives a gradient estimate for solutions of (

).

Lemma B.1
.

There exists a universal constant
C
such that

|\nabla w|+|\nabla^{2}w|+|\nabla^{3}w|\leq C,\quad\text{in}~B_{R-1}(0).

Proof.

For any

|u(x+y,-1+t)|\leq C\quad\text{for}~~(y,t)\in Q_{1}^{-}.

The gradient estimate on

∎

Lemma B.2
.

There exists a universal, small constant
s_{\ast}
such that for any
(x,t)\in B_{R-4}(0)\times[-1,-1+s_{\ast}]
, we have

\bar{E}(s_{\ast};x,t,u)<\varepsilon_{0},

(B.1)

where
\varepsilon_{0}
is the positive constant in Proposition
3.2
.

Proof.

We only estimate the integral

\frac{1}{s_{\ast}}\int_{t-2s_{\ast}}^{t-s_{\ast}}(t-\tau)^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(y,\tau)|^{p+1}G(x-y,t-\tau)dyd\tau

for
(x,t)\in B_{R-4}(0)\times[-1,-1+s_{\ast}]
,
where
s_{\ast}
is a small constant to be determined below.
The other two terms in
\bar{E}(s_{\ast};x,t,u)
can be estimated in the same way.

Decompose this integral into two parts,
\mathrm{I}
being on
B_{1}(x)
and
\mathrm{II}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}(x)
.

By Lemma
B.1
,
|u(y,\tau)|\leq C
in
B_{1}(x)\times[t-2s_{\ast},t-s_{\ast}]
. Thus

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}

\displaystyle\leq Cs_{\ast}^{-1}\int_{t-2s_{\ast}}^{t-s_{\ast}}(t-\tau)^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(x-y,t-\tau)dyd\tau

\displaystyle\leq Cs_{\ast}^{-1}\int_{t-2s_{\ast}}^{t-s_{\ast}}(t-\tau)^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq Cs_{\ast}^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{6},

provided
s_{\ast}
is small enough.

Concerning
\mathrm{II}
, we take the decomposition

\displaystyle\mathrm{II}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{s_{\ast}}\int_{t-2s_{\ast}}^{t-s_{\ast}}(t-\tau)^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{B_{(i+1)}\setminus B_{i}}|u(y,\tau)|^{p+1}G(x-y,t-\tau)dyd\tau

\displaystyle\leq Cs_{\ast}^{-\frac{n}{2}+\frac{2}{p-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{i^{2}}{8s_{\ast}}\right)\int_{t-2s_{\ast}}^{t-s_{\ast}}\int_{B_{(i+1)}(x)\setminus B_{i}(x)}|u(y,\tau)|^{p+1}dyd\tau.

(B.2)

We can cover
(B_{(i+1)}(x)\setminus B_{i}(x))\times(t-2s_{\ast},t-s_{\ast})

backward parabolic cylinders of size
\sqrt{s_{\ast}}
. Then by summing the Morrey estimate (

\int_{t-2s_{\ast}}^{t-s_{\ast}}\int_{B_{(i+1)}(x)\setminus B_{i}(x)}|u(y,\tau)|^{p+1}dyd\tau\leq Ci^{n}s_{\ast}^{\frac{n}{2}+1-\frac{p+1}{p-1}}.

Plugging this estimate into (
B.2
), we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{II}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{i^{2}}{8s_{\ast}}\right)i^{n}

\displaystyle\leq Cs_{\ast}^{\frac{n}{2}}\int_{s_{\ast}^{-1/2}}^{+\infty}\tau^{n}e^{-\frac{\tau^{2}}{8}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq Cs_{\ast}^{\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{16s_{\ast}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{6},

provided
s_{\ast}
is small enough.

The proof is complete by putting the estimates on
\mathrm{I}
and
\mathrm{II}
together.
∎

Proof of Proposition
5.5
.

The previous lemma allows us to use Proposition
3.2
in
Q_{\sqrt{s_{\ast}}}^{-}(x,t)
, for any
(x,t)\in B_{R-3}\times[-1,-1+s_{\ast}]
. This gives

|u|\leq C,\quad\text{in}~B_{R-4}(0)\times\left[-1-\frac{s_{\ast}}{2},-1+s_{\ast}\right].

Then by standard parabolic regularity theory, there exists a universal constant
C
such that

|\partial_{t}u|+|\partial_{tt}u|\leq C,\quad\text{in}~B_{R-5}(0)\times\left[-1,-1+s_{\ast}\right].

An integration in
t
gives, for any
x\in B_{R-5}(0)
,

|u(x,-1)-u(x,-1+s_{\ast})|+|\partial_{t}u(x,-1)-\partial_{t}u(x,-1+s_{\ast})|\leq Cs_{\ast}.

(B.3)

Recall that
u(x,t)=(-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}w\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{-t}}\right)
, so

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(x,t)

\displaystyle=(-t)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}\left[\frac{1}{p-1}w\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{-t}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{x}{\sqrt{-t}}\cdot\nabla w\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{-t}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=(-t)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}\Lambda(w)\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{-t}}\right).

In particular,

u(x,-1)=w(x)\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{t}u(x,-1)=\Lambda(w)(x).

Plugging these identities into (
B.3
), we obtain, for any
x\in B_{R-5}(0)
,

\displaystyle\left|w(x)-(1-s_{\ast})^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}w\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-s_{\ast}}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\Lambda(w)(x)-(1-s_{\ast})^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}\Lambda(w)\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-s_{\ast}}}\right)\right|\leq Cs_{\ast}.

Combining this inequality with the assumption, which says

\text{dist}_{R}(w,\kappa)+\text{dist}_{R}\left(\Lambda(w),\frac{2}{p-1}\kappa\right)\leq\frac{\kappa}{10(p+1)},

we get

\displaystyle\left|(1-s_{\ast})^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}w\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-s_{\ast}}}\right)-\kappa\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|(1-s_{\ast})^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}\Lambda(w)\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-s_{\ast}}}\right)-\frac{2}{p-1}\kappa\right|\leq\frac{\kappa}{10(p+1)}+Cs_{\ast}.

First, this implies that

\sup_{x\in B_{R-5}(0)}\left|w\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-s_{\ast}}}\right)\right|+\left|\Lambda(w)\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-s_{\ast}}}\right)\right|\leq\frac{10\kappa}{p-1}+Cs_{\ast}.

Then an Taylor expansion of
(1-s_{\ast})^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}
and
(1-s_{\ast})^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}
gives

\left|w\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-s_{\ast}}}\right)-\kappa\right|\\
+\left|\Lambda(w)\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-s_{\ast}}}\right)-\frac{2}{p-1}\kappa\right|\leq\frac{\kappa}{10(p+1)}+Cs_{\ast}.

(B.4)

Choose
s_{\ast}
small so that

\frac{\kappa}{10(p+1)}+Cs_{\ast}\leq\frac{\kappa}{p+1}.

Choose
R_{2}
large so that there exists
\theta>0
such that

\frac{R_{2}-5}{\sqrt{1-s_{\ast}}}\geq(1+\theta)R_{2}.

Then (
B.4
) implies that

\text{dist}_{(1+\theta)R}(w,\kappa)+\text{dist}_{(1+\theta)R}\left(\Lambda(w),\frac{2}{p-1}\kappa\right)\leq\frac{\kappa}{p+1}.\qed

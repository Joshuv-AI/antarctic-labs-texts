Optimal error estimates of a second-order temporally finite element method for electrohydrodynamic equations
Shengfeng Wang , Maojun Li , Zeyu Xia

Abstract
In this work, we mainly present the optimal convergence rates of the temporally second-order finite element scheme for solving the electrohydrodynamic equation. Suffering from the highly coupled nonlinearity, the convergence analysis of the numerical schemes for such a system is rather rare, not to mention the optimal error estimates for the high-order temporally scheme. To this end, we abandon the traditional error analysis method following the process of energy estimate, which may lead to the loss of accuracy. Instead, we note that the charge density also possesses the “energy” decaying property directly derived by its governing equation, although it does not appear in the energy stability analysis. This fact allows us to control the error terms of the charge density more conveniently, which finally leads to the optimal convergence rates. Several numerical examples are provided to demonstrate the theoretical results, including the energy stability, mass conservation, and convergence rates. Keywords: Electrohydrodynamic equation, Finite element methods, Second-order temporally accuracy, Energy stability, Optimal convergence rates

Introduction

The hydrodynamic behavior in electromagnetic fields plays a crucial role in the engineering applications, and its research is quite popular in scientific computation in recent years. These phenomena can be governed by the incompressible Navier–Stokes (NS) equation

\displaystyle\rho_{f}(\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$})-\eta\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=\mbox{\boldmath$g$}+\mbox{\boldmath$f$},

(1.1)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}

\displaystyle=0.

(1.2)

In this system,
(\mbox{\boldmath$u$},p)
represents the velocity-pressure pair; the positive constants
\rho_{f}
and
\eta
denote the density and viscosity of the fluid, respectively;
g
stands for the gravity; and
f
is the body force depending on different fields and fluids.
The study of various field-fluid interactions can mainly be divided into three categories: One category is magnetohydrodynamics
[
1
]
, which describes the interactions between the magnetic fields and conductive fluid, where
f
is the Lorentz force; another category is ferrohydrodynamics
[
22
]
, in which the fluid can be magnetized in the presence of a magnetic field and the body force
f
is the polarization force; the last category, the subject of this work, is electrohydrodynamics, where the fluid is influenced by the electric field and is driven by the Coulomb force
f
.

As an important branch of the hydrodynamics, the electrohydrodynamics (EHD) has been widely applied in many fields. For example, in
[
15
]
the authors designed a self-powered triboelectric-EHD pump combining a soft EHD pump driven by an electrostatic generator. In
[
7
]
the authors utilize the EHD phenomena to enhance and control mass and heat transfer in both terrestrial and microgravity environments, which facilitates the flow distribution control through the conduction pumping. In addition, the study on EHD can serve the electrostatic precipitator
[
12
]
, laminar and turbulent mixing
[
4
,
21
]
and more other applications
[
5
,
9
]
.

To describe the EHD phenomena precisely, one must combine the NS equation (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) with the Maxwell’s equations. It is well-known that the Coulomb force takes the form of
\mbox{\boldmath$f$}=\rho\mbox{\boldmath$E$}
, where
\rho
represents the charge density and
E
denotes the electric fields.
Thus, we now need to introduce the relationship between
\rho
and
E
. With the help of the constitutive relation
\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$D$}=\rho
and
\mbox{\boldmath$E$}=\epsilon\mbox{\boldmath$D$}
, we naturally obtain

\displaystyle\epsilon\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$E$}=\rho,

(1.3)

where
D
is the electric displacement and
\epsilon
is the dielectric permittivity assumed to be a positive constant in isotropic materials. Furthermore, if we assume the fluid to be non-magnetic, the Faraday’s law
\nabla\times\mbox{\boldmath$E$}=-\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}=\mbox{\boldmath$0$}
shows that the electric field
E
is curl-free, which allows one to define

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$E$}=-\nabla\phi,

(1.4)

with an artificial electric potential
\phi
.
If the charge density is conserved, an application of Amère’s circuital law yields

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$J$}=0,

(1.5)

with current density
\mbox{\boldmath$J$}=\rho\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\sigma\mbox{\boldmath$E$}-D\nabla\rho
, where
\sigma
and
D
are the drift-induced conductivity and charge diffusion coefficient, respectively. More details can be referred to
[
17
,
19
,
29
]
.

Consequently, combining NS equation (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) with the constitutive relations (
1.3
)-(
1.5
), the governing equations of the EHD system can be formulated as follows:

\displaystyle-\epsilon\Delta\phi

\displaystyle=\rho,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mbox{\boldmath$u$})-D\Delta\rho+\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon}\rho

\displaystyle=0,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}-\eta\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=-\rho\nabla\phi,

(1.8)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}

\displaystyle=0,

(1.9)

in
\Omega\times(0,T)
, where
\Omega
is a bounded convex polyhedral domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(polygonal domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
) and
T>0
represents the terminal time.
Without loss of the generality, we assume
\rho_{f}=1
, and note that for the general case
\rho_{f}>0
there are no essential differences in the analysis.
To close the system, we consider EHD equation (
1.6
)-(
1.9
) subject to the following initial and boundary conditions

\displaystyle\rho|_{t=0}=\rho_{0},\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|_{t=0}=\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0},\hskip 20.00003pt(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}\cdot\nabla\phi)|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\hskip 20.00003pt(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}\cdot\nabla\rho)|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|_{\partial\Omega}=\mbox{\boldmath$0$},

where
\rho_{0}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}
are given, and
n
is the unit outer normal vector on the boundary
\partial\Omega
.
Particularly, from (
1.6
) we obtain the charge conservation property:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\rho{\rm d}x=-\epsilon\int_{\Omega}\Delta\phi{\rm d}x=-\epsilon\int_{\partial\Omega}\mbox{\boldmath$n$}\cdot\nabla\phi{\rm d}s=0,

which is actually consistent with the assumption. Moreover, we assume
\int_{\Omega}\phi{\rm d}x=0
to guarantee the unique solvability of (
1.6
).
The regularity analysis for such a system can be found in
[
3
,
6
,
13
,
23
]
and the references therein.

Concerning the numerical methods, there exist extensive works focusing on the energy stability and computational efficiency.
In
[
18
,
19
,
20
,
31
]
, the authors developed a class of efficient numerical algorithms for solving the EHD system with constant/variable density and
conductivity.
Moreover, to solve the variable density EHD system, some fully decoupled linear and energy-stable schemes based on the “zero-energy-contribution” method were proposed in
[
10
,
28
]
.
Although the numerical investigation for the EHD system is numerous, the optimal error estimates are quite rare. In
[
11
]
, the authors provided a temporal error analysis for the first-order semi-discrete scheme in the two-dimensional case based on the scalar auxiliary variable approach. More recently, in
[
14
]
the authors designed temporally first- and second-order fully decoupled schemes, and proved the first-order accuracy in time and the sub-optimal convergence rates in space.
From the references above, it can be seen that obtaining an optimal error estimate for the numerical scheme is highly desirable, especially for achieving the high-order temporally accuracy.

To this end, we propose a fully discrete scheme by utilizing the second-order backward differentiation formula (BDF) and finite element methods in the work, and then present a rigorous error estimate for the proposed scheme. Additionally, we prove the unconditional energy stability, unique solvability, and discrete charge conservation, which are the essential properties of the continuous system (
1.6
)-(
1.9
).
To achieve the optimal convergence rates, we do not adopt the traditional approach following energy stability, which may lead to losing accuracy. Instead, we discover that the charge density also satisfies the following “energy” decaying property:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\dfrac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-D\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}\leq 0,

via testing (
1.7
) by
\rho
. This fact enables us to control the error term of the charge density first through its diffusion term, and in turn we estimate the electric potential
\phi
by establishing an elliptic equation.
By employing the mathematical induction method, we ultimately obtain the optimal error estimate both in time and space for the first time. Moreover, this convergence result can directly be extended to the second-order pressure decoupled method
[
27
]
and “zero-energy-contribution” method
[
16
]
, by using the analytical techniques therein.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present the numerical scheme and the main results of this work, including the optimal convergence rates, unconditional energy stability, unique solvability, and charge conservation. In Section
3
, we provide the rigorous error analysis for the proposed scheme. Several numerical examples are illustrated in Section
4
to demonstrate the validity of the theoretical results.

)-(
1.9
) together with the main results of energy stability and optimal convergence rates.

2.1
Variational formulation

For any integer
k\geq 0
and real number
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we adopt the conventional Sobolev spaces
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
and, as general, denote by
H^{k}(\Omega):=W^{k,2}(\Omega)
and
L^{p}(\Omega):=W^{0,p}(\Omega)
. The corresponding vector-valued spaces are denoted by
{\mathbf{W}}^{k,p}(\Omega):=[W^{k,p}(\Omega)]^{d}

(d=2,3)
, as well as
{\mathbf{H}}^{k}(\Omega):={\mathbf{W}}^{k,2}(\Omega)
and
{\mathbf{L}}^{p}:={\mathbf{W}}^{0,p}(\Omega)
.
In the later analysis, we denote by
\big(\cdot,\cdot\big)
the standard
L^{2}
inner product and by
\|\cdot\|_{W^{k,p}},\|\cdot\|_{H^{k}},\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}}
the norm of the corresponding (both scalar- and vector- valued) spaces, respectively.
Moreover, we need the following admissible spaces for
(\mbox{\boldmath$u$},p)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{0}(\Omega):=

\displaystyle\{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\in{\mathbf{H}}^{1}(\Omega):~\mbox{\boldmath$v$}=\mbox{\boldmath$0$}~\mbox{on}~\partial\Omega\},

\displaystyle L^{2}_{0}(\Omega):=

\displaystyle\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega):~\int_{\Omega}v{\rm d}x=0\}.

With these notations, it is seen that the exact solution
(\phi,\rho,\mbox{\boldmath$u$},p)
of the EHD system (
1.6
)-(
1.9
) satisfies

\displaystyle\epsilon\big(\nabla\phi,\nabla\varphi\big)=\big(\rho,\varphi\big),

(2.1)

\displaystyle\big(\partial_{t}\rho,\chi\big)-\big(\rho\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\nabla\chi\big)+D\big(\nabla\rho,\nabla\chi\big)+\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon}\big(\rho,\chi\big)=0,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\big(\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\big)+b(\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\mbox{\boldmath$v$})+\eta\big(\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\big)-\big(p,\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\big)+\big(\rho\nabla\phi,\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\big)=0,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\big(\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$},q\big)=0,

(2.4)

for all
(\varphi,\chi,\mbox{\boldmath$v$},q)\in(H^{1}(\Omega),H^{1}(\Omega),{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{0}(\Omega),L^{2}_{0}(\Omega))
and every
t>0
.
By using
\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=0
, we define the following trilinear term
[
24
]

\displaystyle b\big(\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\big)=\frac{1}{2}\big(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\big)-\frac{1}{2}\big(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$w$},\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\big),\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{for}~\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\in{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

(2.5)

and it is easy to verify
b(\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$v$})=0
.

Theorem 2.1
.

The variational formulation (
2.1
)-(
2.4
) is energy-stable and satisfies the following energy inequality

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\Big(\epsilon\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)\leq 0.

Proof.

Taking
\chi=\phi
in (
2.2
) yields

\displaystyle\big(\partial_{t}\rho,\phi\big)-\big(\rho\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\nabla\phi\big)+D\big(\nabla\rho,\nabla\phi\big)+\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon}\big(\rho,\phi\big)=0.

(2.6)

Next, by taking
\varphi=\dfrac{D}{\epsilon}\rho
and
\varphi=\dfrac{\sigma}{\epsilon}\phi
in (
2.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle D\big(\nabla\phi,\nabla\rho\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{D}{\epsilon}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon}\big(\rho,\phi\big)

\displaystyle=\sigma\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(2.8)

respectively. Then, differentiating (
1.6
) with respect to
t
, by testing
\phi
we have

\displaystyle\big(\partial_{t}\rho,\phi\big)=\epsilon\big(\partial_{t}\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi\big).

(2.9)

Letting
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}=\mbox{\boldmath$u$}
in (
2.3
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\big(\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\big)+\eta\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\big(\rho\nabla\phi,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\big)=0.

(2.10)

A combination of (
2.6
)-(
2.10
) leads to

\displaystyle\epsilon\big(\partial_{t}\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi\big)+\frac{D}{\epsilon}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sigma\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\big(\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\big)+\eta\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0.

Then, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\Big(\epsilon\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)=-\Big(\frac{D}{\epsilon}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sigma\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\eta\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)\leq 0,

i.e., the assertion.
∎

2.2
Numerical schemes

We first present the finite element spaces. Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be a quasi-uniform partition of domain
\Omega
into triangles
K_{j}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(tetrahedra in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
),
j=1,2,\cdots,N_{x}
with the mesh size
h=\max_{j}{\rm diam}K_{j}
. Then for any integer
r\geq 2
, we define the finite element spaces as follow:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{r}

\displaystyle:=\{v_{h}\in C(\Omega):\,v_{h}|_{K_{j}}\in P_{r}(K_{j}),\ \ \forall K_{j}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{r}

\displaystyle:=\{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\in{\mathbf{H}}_{0}^{1}(\Omega):\,\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}|_{K_{j}}\in[P_{r}(K_{j})]^{d},\ \ \forall K_{j}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{r-1}

\displaystyle:=\{q_{h}\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega):\,q_{h}|_{K_{j}}\in P_{r-1}(K_{j}),\ \ \forall K_{j}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},

where
P_{r}(K_{j})
denotes the polynomial space of degree
r
on
K_{j}
. Here, we utilize the Taylor–Hood element spaces
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{P})
to solve the velocity-pressure pair
(\mbox{\boldmath$u$},p)
, which obviously satisfies the discrete inf-sup condition

\displaystyle\inf_{0\neq q_{h}\in\mathcal{P}^{r-1}}\sup_{{\bf 0}\neq{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}\in\mathcal{U}^{r}}\frac{\big(q_{h},\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\big)}{\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{1}(\Omega)}}\geq C,

for a positive constant
C
.

For the time discretization, we adopt the uniform partition of temporal interval
[0,T]
as
\tau=\frac{T}{N_{T}}
for a fixed positive integer
N_{T}
, and define
t^{n}=n\tau,n=0,1,...,N_{T}
.
As usual, we denote by
v^{n}:=v(\cdot,t^{n})
the value at
t^{n}
for any function
v
.
Then, the fully discrete scheme of variational formulation (
2.1
)-(
2.4
) is to find
(\phi_{h}^{n+1},\rho_{h}^{n+1},\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{n+1},p_{h}^{n+1})\in(\mathcal{H}^{r}\times\mathcal{H}^{r}\times\mathcal{U}^{r}\times\mathcal{P}^{r-1})
such that it satisfies

\displaystyle\epsilon\big(\nabla\phi_{h}^{n+1},\nabla\varphi_{h}\big)-\big(\rho_{h}^{n+1},\varphi_{h}\big)=0,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\big(D_{\tau}\rho_{h}^{n+1},\chi_{h}\big)-\big(\widetilde{\rho}_{h}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{n+1},\nabla\chi_{h}\big)+D\big(\nabla\rho_{h}^{n+1},\nabla\chi_{h}\big)+\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon}\big(\rho_{h}^{n+1},\chi_{h}\big)=0,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\big(D_{\tau}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{n+1},\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\big)+b\big(\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{n+1},\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{n+1},\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\big)+\eta\big(\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{n+1},\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\big)-\big(p_{h}^{n+1},\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\big)+\big(\widetilde{\rho}_{h}^{n+1}\nabla\phi_{h}^{n+1},\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\big)=0,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\big(\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{n+1},q_{h}\big)=0,

(2.14)

for
(\varphi_{h},\chi_{h},\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h},q_{h})\in(\mathcal{H}^{r}\times\mathcal{H}^{r}\times\mathcal{U}^{r}\times\mathcal{P}^{r-1})
and
n=0,1,...,N_{T}-1
. We define

D_{\tau}v^{n+1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{v^{n+1}-v^{n}}{\tau},&n=0,\\
\dfrac{3v^{n+1}-4v^{n}+v^{n-1}}{2\tau},&n\geq 1,\end{array}\right.\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\widetilde{v}^{n+1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}v^{n},&n=0,\\
2v^{n}-v^{n-1},&n\geq 1,\end{array}\right.

(2.15)

for any (scalar- or vector- valued) function
v
.
The initial data is set as

\displaystyle\rho_{h}^{0}=P_{h}\rho^{0}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{0},

(2.16)

where
P_{h}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}_{h}
are the standard
L^{2}
and Ritz projection operators (defined in Section
3.1
), respectively, and
\phi_{h}^{0}
is defined as the solution of

\displaystyle(\nabla\phi_{h}^{0},\nabla\varphi_{h})=(\rho_{h}^{0},\varphi_{h}),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\varphi_{h}\in\mathcal{H}^{r}.

(2.17)

Finally, we let
\int_{\Omega}\phi_{h}^{n}{\rm d}x=0
to guarantee the uniqueness for all
n=0,1,\cdots,N_{T}
.

In this paper, we assume that the EHD system (
1.6
)-(
1.9
) admits a unique solution
(\phi,\rho,\mbox{\boldmath$u$},p)
satisfying

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{r+1})}+\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{r+1})}+\|\partial_{t}\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{r+1})}+\|\partial_{tt}\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}+\|\partial_{ttt}\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt+\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;{W}^{r+1,3})}+\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{r+1})}+\|\partial_{tt}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}+\|\partial_{ttt}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}+\|p\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{r})}

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\leq K.

(2.18)

We now present the main result of this paper in the following theorem, and the detailed proof will be shown in Section

.

.

)-(

) has the unique solution
(\phi,\rho,\mbox{\boldmath$u$},p)

)-(

2.3
Energy stability and unique solvability

In this subsection we will provide the unconditional energy stability and unique solvability for the fully discrete scheme (
2.11
)-(
2.14
).

Theorem 2.3
.

The numerical scheme (
2.11
)-(
2.14
) is uniquely solvable and unconditionally energy-stable, which satisfies the discrete energy dissipation property

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{h}^{n+1}-\mathcal{E}_{h}^{n}\leq 0,

for
n=1,2,...,N_{T}-1
with

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{h}^{n+1}:=\epsilon\|\nabla\phi_{h}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\epsilon\|2\nabla\phi_{h}^{n+1}-\nabla\phi_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|2\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Moreover, the discrete charge density
\rho_{h}^{n}
satisfies
\int_{\Omega}\rho_{h}^{n}{\rm d}x=0
for
n=1,2,\cdots,N_{T}
.

Proof.

It is direct to obtain the discrete charge conservation law

\displaystyle\big(\rho_{h}^{n+1},1\big)=\epsilon\big(\nabla\phi_{h}^{n+1},\nabla 1\big)=0,

by taking
\varphi_{h}=1
in (
2.11
).

Next, we prove the unconditional energy stability. By taking
\chi_{h}=\phi_{h}^{n+1}
,
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}=\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{n+1}
and
q_{h}=p_{h}^{n+1}
in (
2.12
)-(
2.14
), respectively, we arrive at

\displaystyle\big(D_{\tau}\rho_{h}^{n+1},\phi_{h}^{n+1}\big)-\big(\widetilde{\rho}_{h}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{n+1},\nabla\phi_{h}^{n+1}\big)+D\big(\nabla\rho_{h}^{n+1},\nabla\phi_{h}^{n+1}\big)+\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon}\big(\rho_{h}^{n+1},\phi_{h}^{n+1}\big)

\displaystyle=0,

(2.19)

\displaystyle\big(D_{\tau}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{n+1},\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{n+1}\big)+\eta\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(\widetilde{\rho}_{h}^{n+1}\nabla\phi_{h}^{n+1},\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{n+1})

\displaystyle=0.

(2.20)

In (
2.11
), we take
\varphi_{h}=\dfrac{D}{\epsilon}\rho_{h}^{n+1}
and
\dfrac{\sigma}{\epsilon}\phi_{h}^{n+1}
, respectively, and then obtain

\displaystyle D\big(\nabla\phi_{h}^{n+1},\nabla\rho_{h}^{n+1}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{D}{\epsilon}\|\rho_{h}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(2.21)

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon}\big(\rho_{h}^{n+1},\phi_{h}^{n+1}\big)

\displaystyle=\sigma\|\nabla\phi_{h}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(2.22)

Further, at
t^{n+1}
,
t^{n}
and
t^{n-1}
, we all choose
\varphi_{h}=\phi_{h}^{n+1}
and then have

\displaystyle\epsilon\big(\nabla\phi_{h}^{n+1},\nabla\phi_{h}^{n+1}\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\rho_{h}^{n},\phi_{h}^{n+1}\big),

\displaystyle\epsilon\big(\nabla\phi_{h}^{n},\nabla\phi_{h}^{n+1}\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\rho_{h}^{n},\phi_{h}^{n+1}\big),

\displaystyle\epsilon\big(\nabla\phi_{h}^{n-1},\nabla\phi_{h}^{n+1}\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\rho_{h}^{n-1},\phi_{h}^{n+1}\big),

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-\frac{d}{6}}\|\widetilde{e}_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}+Ch^{r}\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\,h^{-\frac{d}{6}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\ \ ~+C\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(h^{1-\frac{d}{6}}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\big)\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}},

due to
\tau\leq Ch^{\frac{1}{2}}
and then
h^{-\frac{d}{6}}\|\widetilde{e}_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq h^{-\frac{d}{6}}(h^{r+1}+\tau^{2})\leq h^{1-\frac{d}{6}}
via (
3.34
).

For the truncation term
I_{43}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{43}=

\displaystyle\big(D_{\tau}(\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}),\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\big)+\big(D_{\tau}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}-\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1},\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\big)

\displaystyle+\big(b(\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{n+1},\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1},\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1})-b(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1},\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1},\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1})\big)+\big((\widetilde{\rho}^{n+1}-\rho^{n+1})\nabla\phi^{n+1},\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{r+1}\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}+C\tau^{2}\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big(h^{2r+2}+\tau^{4}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)+\frac{\eta}{16}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}},

(3.40)

where the analysis is similar to (
3.26
).

Combining (
3.38
)-(
3.40
), we rewrite (
3.36
) as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\tau}\Big(\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|2\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|2\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n-1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\frac{\eta}{2}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(h^{2r+2}+\tau^{4}+\|\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\widetilde{e}_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)+\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.41)

(6) Estimate for
e_{\rho}^{n+1}
:
By letting
\chi_{h}=e_{\rho}^{n+1}
in (
3.16
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\tau}\Big(\|e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|e_{\rho}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|2e_{\rho}^{n+1}-e_{\rho}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|2e_{\rho}^{n}-e_{\rho}^{n-1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|e_{\rho}^{n+1}-2e_{\rho}^{n}+e_{\rho}^{n-1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt+D\|\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon}\|e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\big(\widetilde{\rho}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}-\widetilde{\rho}_{h}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{n+1},\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\big)+\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon}\big(R_{h}\rho^{n+1}-\rho^{n+1},e_{\rho}^{n+1}\big)+\mathfrak{T}_{\rho}^{n+1}(e_{\rho}^{n+1})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{3}I_{5i}.

(3.42)

For
I_{51}
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{51}=

\displaystyle\big((\widetilde{\rho}^{n+1}-R_{h}\widetilde{\rho}^{n+1})\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1},\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\big)+\big(\widetilde{e}_{\rho}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1},\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\big)

\displaystyle+\big(\widetilde{\rho}_{h}^{n+1}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}),\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\big)+\big(\widetilde{\rho}_{h}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1},\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\rho}^{n+1}-R_{h}\widetilde{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\widetilde{e}_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|\widetilde{\rho}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{L^{6}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{3}}\|\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}+C(h^{1-\frac{d}{6}}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}})\|\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big(h^{2r+2}+\|\widetilde{e}_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+h^{2-\frac{d}{3}}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)+\frac{D}{4}\|\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(3.43)

where the last term of
I_{51}
is estimated as

\displaystyle\big(\widetilde{\rho}_{h}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1},\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\big)

\displaystyle=\big(-\widetilde{e}_{\rho}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1},\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\big)+\big((R_{h}\widetilde{\rho}^{n+1}-\widetilde{\rho}^{n+1})\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1},\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\big)+\big(\widetilde{\rho}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1},\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\|\widetilde{e}_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{3}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|R_{h}\widetilde{\rho}^{n+1}-\widetilde{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{4}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt+\|\widetilde{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-\frac{d}{6}}\|\widetilde{e}_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}+Ch^{r}\,h^{-\frac{d}{4}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt+C\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(h^{1-\frac{d}{6}}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\big)\|\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}.

It is easy to calculate
I_{52}
and
I_{53}
as

\displaystyle I_{52}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon}\|R_{h}\rho^{n+1}-\rho^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ch^{2r+2}+\frac{\sigma}{4\epsilon}\|e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(3.44)

\displaystyle I_{53}=

\displaystyle\big(D_{\tau}(R_{h}\rho^{n+1}-\rho^{n+1}),e_{\rho}^{n+1}\big)+\big(D_{\tau}\rho^{n+1}-\partial_{t}\rho^{n+1},e_{\rho}^{n+1}\big)+\big((\widetilde{\rho}^{n+1}-\rho^{n+1})\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1},\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{r+1}\|e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}+C\tau^{2}\|e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}+Ch^{r+1}\|\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big(h^{2r+2}+\tau^{4}\big)+\frac{D}{4}\|\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{4\epsilon}\|e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(3.45)

Thus, through (
3.43
)-(
3.45
), equation (
3.42
) is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\tau}\Big(\|e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|e_{\rho}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|2e_{\rho}^{n+1}-e_{\rho}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|2e_{\rho}^{n}-e_{\rho}^{n-1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt+\frac{D}{2}\|\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{2\epsilon}\|e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(h^{2r+2}+\tau^{4}+\|\widetilde{e}_{\rho}^{n+1}\|^{2}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}+h^{2-\frac{d}{3}}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big).

(3.46)

(7) Estimate for
e_{\phi}^{n+1}
:
In order to apply the discrete Grownwall inequality, it remains to handle the estimate of
\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}
in (
3.41
). We adopt
\varphi_{h}=e_{\phi}^{n+1}
in (
3.15
) and then have

\displaystyle\epsilon\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=

\displaystyle\big(e_{\rho}^{n+1},e_{\phi}^{n+1}\big)+\big(\rho^{n+1}-R_{h}\rho^{n+1},e_{\phi}^{n+1}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\rho^{n+1}-R_{h}\rho^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big(\|e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}+h^{r+1}\big)\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}},

which implies

\displaystyle\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\big(\|e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}+h^{r+1}\big).

(3.47)

(8) Final estimate at
t=t^{n}(n\geq 2)
:
We combine (
3.41
), (
3.4
) and (
3.47
) to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\tau}\Big(\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|2\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|2\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n-1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt+\|e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|e_{\rho}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|2e_{\rho}^{n+1}-e_{\rho}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|2e_{\rho}^{n}-e_{\rho}^{n-1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt+\frac{\eta}{2}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{D}{2}\|\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{2\epsilon}\|e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big(h^{2r+2}+\tau^{4}+\|\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\widetilde{e}_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)+Ch^{2-\frac{d}{3}}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt+C\big(\|e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+h^{2r+2}\big).

By defining

\displaystyle{\theta}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|2\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|2e_{\rho}^{n+1}-e_{\rho}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle{\xi}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\eta\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+D\|\nabla e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon}\|e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

and for some small
h
satisfying
Ch^{2-\frac{d}{3}}\leq\frac{\eta}{4}
, we then have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\tau}\big(\theta^{n+1}-\theta^{n}\big)+\frac{1}{4}\xi^{n+1}\leq

\displaystyle C\big(h^{2r+2}+\tau^{4}+\|\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\widetilde{e}_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big(h^{2r+2}+\tau^{4}+\theta^{n}+\theta^{n+1}\big).

Hence, an application of the discrete Grownwall inequality leads to the desired result

\displaystyle\theta^{n+1}+\tau\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\xi^{i}\leq C\big(h^{2r+2}+\tau^{4}\big),

i.e.,

\displaystyle\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(h^{2r+2}+\tau^{4}),

(3.48)

which recovers the induction assumption (
3.34
), for
n=1,2,...,N_{T}-1
.

Next, we investigate the
L^{2}
error estimate of
e_{\phi}^{n+1}
by using the duality technique.
From (
3.47
) we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\big(h^{r+1}+\tau^{2}\big).

(3.49)

Let
w\in H^{2}(\Omega)
be the unique solution of the equation

-\Delta w=e_{\phi}^{n+1}\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{in}~\Omega,

(3.50)

with
\nabla w\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$n$}=0
on boundary
\partial\Omega
and
(w,1)=0
.
From the classic elliptic theory
[
2
]
, if
e_{\phi}^{n+1}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, then
w
satisfies

\displaystyle|w|_{H^{2}}\leq C\|e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.51)

Meanwhile, the solution of (
3.50
) also solves the following variational formulation: Find
w\in H^{1}(\Omega)
such that it holds

\displaystyle\big(\nabla w,\nabla v\big)=\big(e_{\phi}^{n+1},v\big),\hskip 20.00003pt\forall~v\in H^{1}(\Omega),

(3.52)

for which the unique solvability can be guaranteed with the help of the Lax–Miligram theorem.
We take
v=w
in (
3.52
) to obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\big(e_{\phi}^{n+1},w\big)\leq\|e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}},

which together with
\|w\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}
yields
\|w\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\|e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}
.
Meanwhile, taking
v=e_{\phi}^{n+1}
in (
3.52
) and
\varphi_{h}=R_{h}w
in (
3.15
) yield

\displaystyle\|e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=

\displaystyle\big(\nabla w,\nabla e_{\phi}^{n+1}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\nabla w-\nabla R_{h}w,\nabla e_{\phi}^{n+1}\big)+{\epsilon}^{-1}\big(e_{\rho}^{n+1},R_{h}w\big)+{\epsilon}^{-1}\big(\rho^{n+1}-R_{h}\rho^{n+1},R_{h}w\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla w-\nabla R_{h}w\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|R_{h}w\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\rho^{n+1}-R_{h}\rho^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|R_{h}w\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch\|w\|_{H^{2}}\big(h^{r+1}+\tau^{2}\big)+C\big(h^{r+1}+\tau^{2}\big)\|w\|_{L^{2}}+Ch^{r+1}\|w\|_{L^{2}}\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{(by using \eqref{error-gradphi})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big(1+h\big)\big(h^{r+1}+\tau^{2}\big)\|w\|_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big(h^{r+1}+\tau^{2}\big)\|e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}},

which in turn leads to

\displaystyle\|e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\big(h^{r+1}+\tau^{2}\big).

Finally, we obtain the error estimate results

\displaystyle\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{u}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|e_{\rho}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(h^{r+1}+\tau^{2})

which together with the triangle inequality can complete the proof of Theorem
2.2
.

5
Conclusion

In this work, we prove the optimal error estimates of the high-order temporally scheme based on BDF2 and finite element methods for the EHD system. It is the first work to achieve the optimal convergence rates both in time and space. This analytical frame can also be extended to other decoupled methods and the Cahn–Hilliard–EHD system, by combining the similar techniques developed in
[
16
,
27
]
and
[
26
]
, respectively.

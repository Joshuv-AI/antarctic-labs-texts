Weak curvature conditions on metric graphs
Juliane Krautz

Abstract
Starting from pointwise gradient estimates for the heat semigroup, we study three characterizations of weak lower curvature bounds on metric graphs. More precisely, we prove the equivalence between a weak notion of the Bakry-Émery curvature condition, a weak Evolutionary Variational Inequality and a weak form of geodesic convexity. The proof is based on a careful regularization of absolutely continuous curves together with an explicit representation of the Cheeger energy. We conclude with a brief discussion on possible applications to the Schrödinger bridge problem on metric graphs.

Introduction

Over the past years, the study of synthetic curvature bounds on metric measure spaces and their relation has received considerable interest.
In their seminal work, Lott-Villani
[
19
]
and Sturm
[
24
,
25
]
introduced a new notion of curvature bound on these spaces using the displacement convexity of the logarithmic entropy. Let
(X,d,{\mathrm{m}})
be a metric measure space, that is a complete separable metric space
(X,d)
endowed with a probability measure that has finite second moment, we write
{\mathrm{m}}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(X)
.
The logarithmic entropy on that space is given as

\displaystyle{\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}(\mu)=\int_{X}f\log f\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}{\mathrm{m}}\quad\text{for}\quad\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}\mu}{\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}{\mathrm{m}}}=f,

(1.1)

where
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}\mu}{\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}{\mathrm{m}}}
is the Radon-Nikodym density of
\mu
with respect to
{\mathrm{m}}
. Let

\displaystyle W^{2}_{2}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})=\inf\limits_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})}\iint_{X\times X}d^{2}(x,y)\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}\pi(x,y)

(1.2)

denote the
2
-Wasserstein distance for the set of transport plans

\displaystyle\Pi(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})=\left\{\pi\in{\mathcal{P}}(X\times X)\,:\,\pi(A\times X)=\mu_{0}(A),\,\pi(X\times B)=\mu_{1}(B),\,A,B\subset X\,\text{Borel}\right\}

(1.3)

between
\mu_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(X)
and
\mu_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(X)
. Indeed,
W_{2}
defines a metric on
{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(X)
and
({\mathcal{P}}_{2}(X),W_{2})
is called Wasserstein space on
X
. The underlying space
(X,d,{\mathrm{m}})
is said to satisfy a
{\rm CD}(K,\infty)
-condition and to have curvature bounded from below by
K\in\mathbb{R}
if the logarithmic entropy is geodesically
K
-convex, that is

\displaystyle{\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}(\mu_{s})\leq(1-s){\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}(\mu_{0})+s{\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}(\mu_{1})-\frac{K}{2}s(1-s)W_{2}^{2}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})

(1.4)

for all
s\in[0,1]
and a Wasserstein geodesic
s\mapsto\mu_{s}
connecting
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(X)
.
Note that
{\rm CD}(K,\infty)
is a special case of the Curvature-Dimension condition
{\rm CD}(K,N)
introduced by
[
25
]
and
[
19
]
. There,
N\in(0,\infty]
is an upper bound on the dimension in addition to the lower curvature bound
K\in\mathbb{R}
. In the present paper, we restrict our analysis to the dimensionless case
N=\infty
.

If the underlying space is a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
and
{\mathrm{m}}={\rm vol}_{g}
, the curvature bound given by
{\rm CD}(K,\infty)
coincides with the geometric lower bound on the Ricci curvature of
M
. Since (
1.4
) is well-defined on metric measure spaces and does not depend on any Riemannian structure,
{\rm CD}(K,\infty)
can be understood as an extension of this bound.
However, it is possible for Finsler geometries to satisfy the
{\rm CD}(K,\infty)
-condition as well. This observation led to a refinement in
[
2
]
, namely the introduction of spaces with Riemannian curvature bounded below satisfying a Riemannian Curvature-Dimension condition
{\rm RCD}(K,\infty)
.
In the same work, several equivalent characterizations of such spaces have been given, one of them by introducing the additional assumption of linearity of the heat flow, which is defined as the
L^{2}(X,{\mathrm{m}})
-gradient flow of the Cheeger energy

\displaystyle{\rm{Ch}}(f)=\frac{1}{2}\inf\left\{\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\int_{X}\lvert\nabla f_{n}\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}{\mathrm{m}}\,:\,f_{n}~\text{is Lipschitz},\,\int_{X}\lvert f_{n}-f\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}{\mathrm{m}}\to 0\right\}

and we write
{\rm Dom}({\rm{Ch}})=\{f\in L^{2}(X)\,:\,{\rm{Ch}}(f)\in\mathbb{R}\}
for its domain.
Linearity of this flow is equivalent to the Cheeger energy being a Dirichlet form, thus quadratic. Let
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
denote the corresponding semigroup. By duality, it can be extended to the space of probability measures inducing the semigroup
(H_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Among the powerful calculus tools developed in
[
1
]
, it has been shown that
(H_{t})_{t\geq 0}
coincides with the
W_{2}
-gradient flow of the logarithmic entropy on
{\rm CD}(K,\infty)
-spaces. This observation is a key ingredient in the proof of the following equivalent characterization. A metric measure space is an
{\rm RCD}(K,\infty)
-space if the heat flow satisfies an Evolutionary Variational Inequality, meaning that for all
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(X)
with
{\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}(\mu_{1})<\infty
the
W_{2}
-gradient flow of the logarithmic entropy exists and it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}t}\frac{1}{2}W_{2}^{2}(H_{t}\mu_{1},\mu_{0})+\frac{K}{2}W_{2}^{2}(H_{t}\mu_{1},\mu_{0})+{\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}(H_{t}\mu_{1})\leq{\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}(\mu_{0})

(
{\rm EVI}_{K}
)

for a.e.
t>0
.
Again, this notion is consistent with the classical Riemannian case and we refer to
[
2
]
for more details.

Another approach to generalizing curvature bounds has been developed in
[
7
]
and originates from the theory of Dirichlet forms. Let
(X,{\mathcal{B}},{\mathrm{m}})
be a measure space, for example a metric measure space
(X,d,{\mathrm{m}})
with Borel
\sigma
-algebra induced by the metric. Further, let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a Dirichlet form on
X
with Markov semigroup
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and generator
\Delta
. We define

\displaystyle\Gamma(f,g)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\left(\Delta(fg)-\Delta fg-f\Delta g\right),

which is related to the Dirichlet form by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}(f,g)=\int_{X}\Gamma(f,g)\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}{\mathrm{m}}.

To shorten the notation, we write
\Gamma(f)=\Gamma(f,f)
.
In the particular case of the measure space being a Riemannian manifold
(M,{\mathsf{g}})
with volume measure
{\mathrm{m}}
, we can choose
\Delta=\Delta_{\mathsf{g}}
as the Laplace-Beltrami operator and obtain
\Gamma(f,g)=\langle\nabla f,\nabla g\rangle_{\mathsf{g}}
.
Omitting the dependence on the dimension by choosing
N=\infty
, an important consequence of Ricci-curvature bounded from below is the point-wise gradient estimate

\displaystyle\Gamma(S_{t}f)\leq e^{-Kt}S_{t}\Gamma(f)

(
\rm BE
)

{\mathrm{m}}
-a.e. in
M
for all
t\geq 0
and
f\in{\rm Dom}({\mathcal{E}})
. We say that the condition
{\rm BE}(K,\infty)
is satisfied if(

BE

) holds.
This estimate does not depend on second order operators and can be formulated on metric measure spaces instead of Riemannian manifolds as well. For this reason, the optimal constant
K\in\mathbb{R}
in (

BE

) can be thought of as a lower bound on the curvature. On a Riemannian manifold this notion coincides with
K\in\mathbb{R}
being a lower bound on the Ricci curvature as was shown in
[
27
]
.

If the Cheeger energy is a Dirichlet form, or equivalently, if
P_{t}
is linear for all
t\geq 0
, condition (

BE

) can be stated for
{\mathcal{E}}={\rm{Ch}}
with
S_{t}=P_{t}
.
Then, owing to the work of
[
17
]
, the bound (

BE

) is equivalent to the contraction estimate

\displaystyle W_{2}(H_{t}\mu,H_{t}\nu)\leq e^{-Kt}W_{2}(\mu,\nu)

(1.5)

for all
t\geq 0
and
\mu,\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(X)
.
Therefore, it is crucial to identify the Cheeger energy and study its properties when establishing weak notions of curvature bounds. Under the additional linearity assumption
[
3
,
12
]
have shown that (

BE

) is equivalent to the
{\rm RCD}(K,\infty)
-condition, thus providing the relation

\displaystyle{\rm BE}(K,\infty)\Longleftrightarrow{\rm EVI}_{K}\Longleftrightarrow{\rm RCD}(K,\infty)

(1.6)

and therefore connecting both approaches.
In view of (
1.6
), we call a metric measures space an
{\rm RCD}(K,\infty)
-space if, for
K\in\mathbb{R}
, any of
{\rm BE}(K,\infty)
,
{\rm EVI}_{K}
or
{\rm RCD}(K,\infty)
holds true.

These conditions allow to conclude several important properties, such as functional and geometric inequalities
[
18
,
26
]
or
\Gamma
-convergence results
[
20
]
.
Besides
{\rm RCD}(K,\infty)
-spaces covering a broad class of examples, many widely studied spaces do not satisfy any of the equivalent conditions from (
1.6
). Metric graphs and sub-Riemannian manifolds provide prototypical examples where curvature bounds fail to hold true, see
[
11
,
8
]
or
[
6
]
.
For this reason, the question of extending curvature conditions to a weak notion received interest in recent years. The following observation serves as a starting point for possible extensions. Several spaces not satisfying an
{\rm RCD}(K,\infty)
-condition still admit a linear heat-flow and point-wise gradient bounds of the form

\displaystyle\Gamma(P_{t}f)\leq C^{2}e^{-2Kt}P_{t}\Gamma(f)\quad\text{for all }t\geq 0\text{ and }f\in{\rm Dom}({\rm{Ch}})

(1.7)

with constants
C\geq 1
and
K\in\mathbb{R}
, see for example
[
8
]
for metric graphs and
[
10
]
for Heisenberg groups. We write
{\rm BE}_{w}(c,\infty)
for the condition above.
If
C=1
, then (
1.7
) coincides with (

BE

) and we can think of
c(t)=e^{-Kt}
as a curvature function, where
K\in\mathbb{R}
is the lower curvature bound. In the more general case
C\geq 1
we call
c(t)=Ce^{-Kt}
the curvature function as well and, following this analogy, we define the optimal constant
K\in\mathbb{R}
to be a weak lower bound on the curvature of
(X,d,{\mathrm{m}})
. In the spirit of
[
17
]
, estimate (
1.7
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle W_{2}(H_{t}\mu_{1},H_{t}\mu_{0})\leq Ce^{-Kt}W_{2}(\mu_{1},\mu_{0})\quad\text{for all }t\geq 0\text{ and }\mu_{1},\mu_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(X)

(1.8)

with the dual heat semigroup
(H_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.
Following the ideas of
[
3
]
, an equivalence result similar to (
1.6
) has been shown in
[
23
]
for metric measure groups.
Due to the equivalence (
1.6
), these spaces do not admit a geodesically convex entropy and the weak condition extending
{\rm RCD}(K,\infty)
under (
1.7
) originates in the idea of quantifying this non-convexity. However, if the underlying space exhibits branching geodesics, the entropy along them might be infinite, which corresponds to
{\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}(\mu_{s})=\infty
for the left-hand side of (
1.4
). For this reason, the heat semigroup is employed to regularize possible singularities.
We write
{\rm RCD}_{w}(c,\infty)
for the resulting condition, meaning that

\displaystyle{\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}(H_{t+h}\mu_{s})\leq(1-s)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}(H_{t}\mu_{0})+s{\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}(H_{t}\mu_{1})

\displaystyle+\frac{s(1-s)}{2h}\left(\frac{1}{R(t,t+h)}W_{2}^{2}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})-W_{2}^{2}(H_{t}\mu_{0},H_{t}\mu_{1})\right)

(1.9)

holds for all
t\geq 0
,
h>0
and
s\mapsto\mu_{s}
a
W_{2}
-geodesic joining
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(X)
with

\displaystyle R(t_{0},t_{1})=\int_{0}^{1}c^{-2}\left((1-s)t_{0}+st_{1}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}s.

For
t=0
, this inequality reduces to a heated version of (
1.4
), namely

\displaystyle{\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}(H_{h}\mu_{s})\leq(1-s)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}(\mu_{0})+s{\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}(\mu_{1})+\frac{s(1-s)}{2h}\left(\frac{1}{R(0,h)}-1\right)W_{2}^{2}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}).

(1.10)

If the space satisfies an
{\rm RCD}(K,\infty)
-condition, we have
c(t)=e^{-Kt}
and one can show that
\frac{1}{h}\left(\frac{1}{R(0,h)}-1\right)\to-K
as
h\to 0
, therefore recovering (

EVI
K

).
The weak condition
{\rm EVI}_{w}(c)
admits another reformulation that characterizes the defect to geodesic convexity in more detail. Let
{\mathcal{I}}:{\mathcal{P}}(X)\to[0,\infty]
with
{\mathcal{I}}(f)=4{\rm{Ch}}(\sqrt{f})
be the Fisher Information.
If the chain rule

\displaystyle{\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}(H_{h}\mu_{s})-{\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}(\mu_{s})=-\int_{0}^{h}{\mathcal{I}}(H_{\tau}\mu_{s})\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}\tau

(1.11)

holds, we can rewrite (
1.10
) as the distorted convexity condition

\displaystyle{\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}(\mu_{s})\leq(1-s)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}(\mu_{0})+s{\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}(\mu_{1})+\omega(s),

where we define

\displaystyle\omega(s)\coloneqq\frac{s(1-s)}{2h}\left(\frac{1}{R(0,h)}-1\right)W_{2}^{2}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})+\int_{0}^{h}{\mathcal{I}}(H_{\tau}\mu_{s})\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}\tau

for fixed
h>0
quantifying the non-convexity.
The weak version of (

EVI
K

) that turns out to be equivalent to (

BE
w

) and (
1.9
) can be understood as an almost integrated version of the Evolutionary Variational Inequality. We write
{\rm EVI}_{w}(c)
to denote that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}W_{2}^{2}(H_{t_{1}}\mu_{1},H_{t_{0}}\mu_{0})-\frac{1}{2R(t_{0},t_{1})}W_{2}^{2}(\mu_{1},\mu_{0})\leq(t_{1}-t_{0})\left({\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}(H_{t_{0}}\mu_{0})-{\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}(H_{t_{1}}\mu_{1})\right)

holds for
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(X)
and
0\leq t_{0}\leq t_{1}\leq 1
. For a detailed overview on related literature with a particular focus on curvature conditions in sub-Riemannian manifolds, we refer the reader to
[
23
]
and the references therein. With these weak notions, their result reads

\displaystyle{\rm BE}_{w}(c,\infty)\Longleftrightarrow{\rm EVI}_{w}(c)\Longleftrightarrow{\rm RCD}_{w}(c,\infty),

(1.12)

giving rise to the definition of metric measure spaces satisfying weak lower curvature bounds by one of the three equivalent conditions above.

To the author’s best knowledge, the equivalence is only known for special metric measure groups so far, even though the idea of studying possible extensions to spaces like metric graphs or the Grushin plane was already formulated in
[
23
]
. The proof relies on the construction of a regularization of Wasserstein geodesics compatible with metric derivatives. In
{\rm RCD}(K,\infty)
-spaces this regularization is obtained using the heat semigroup and compatibility is inferred from the contraction estimate (
1.5
). For the metric derivatives it implies

\displaystyle\lvert\dot{H_{t}\mu}_{s}\rvert\leq e^{-2Kt}\left\lvert\dot{\mu}_{s}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\dot{\mu}_{s}\right\rvert.

If the weak condition (
1.8
) holds instead, we only obtain

\displaystyle\lvert\dot{H_{t}\mu}_{s}\rvert\leq Ce^{-2Kt}\left\lvert\dot{\mu}_{s}\right\rvert\leq C\left\lvert\dot{\mu}_{s}\right\rvert

up to the constant
C\geq 1
. Taking the limit as
t\to 0
does not recover the speed of the original curve as long as
C>1
and we are not in the
{\rm RCD}(K,\infty)
-setting.
For this reason, it is necessary to introduce a new approximation given by a family of curves of measures
(\mu_{s}^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
compatible with metric derivatives in the sense that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\left\lvert\dot{\mu}^{n}_{s}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\dot{\mu}_{s}\right\rvert

(1.13)

and such that
\mu^{n}_{s}\rightharpoonup\mu_{s}
.
In the case of metric measure groups, this is achieved by means of the operation of group convolution. As soon as we leave the group setting, this approximation is no longer applicable. Additionally the characterization of the gradient flow of
{\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}
as the heat equation has to be known a priori.
However, on metric graphs both issues have been resolved in
[
11
]
. There, the authors introduced a regularization that satisfies (
1.13
) and showed that the heat equation can be understood as the
W_{2}
-gradient flow of
{\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}
. Since metric graphs admit weak gradient bounds, see
[
8
]
, this observation provides the starting point of our analysis with the aim of extending (
1.12
).

Contributions.

The main contribution of the present article is the proof of the equivalences from (
1.12
) on metric graphs. We rely on the regularization introduced in
[
11
]
and adapt the arguments presented in the metric measure group setting, therefore extending
[
23
]
[Theorem 5.16]. As a first step, we show that the Cheeger energy coincides with the Dirichlet energy, allowing us to connect the abstract setting of metric measure spaces to the gradient bound from
[
8
]
. This identification relies on the identity
W^{1,\infty}={\rm Lip}
on metric graphs for which we give a proof as we could not find it in the literature. Here,
{\rm Lip}
denotes the space of Lipschitz functions.
Moreover, we introduce the notion of strongly regular curves on metric graphs. For arbitrary absolutely continuous curves we then construct an approximating sequence of strongly regular curves. In particular, the action and entropy are well-behaved along weak limits. Employing this procedure together with a regularization of the entropy, we then derive an estimate similar to
{\rm EVI}_{w}(c)
. Starting from this estimate, we conclude the equivalences by showing that the inequality is preserved under taking the limit in the regularization.

Structure of the Paper.

The paper is structured as follows.
In Section
2
we define metric graphs as well as introducing the heat equation on such spaces. In Section
3
we study this equation and its two interpretations as a gradient flow of
{\rm{Ch}}
and
{\mathsf{E}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathrm{m}}
. To this end, we provide an identification of the Cheeger energy in Theorem
3.5
. Moreover, we introduce the regularization of measures on graphs applied throughout the article in Definition
3.2
.
In Section
4
we state and prove the equivalences in Theorem
4.4
. As a first step, we show that (
1.7
) is equivalent to (
1.8
) in Theorem
4.3
. The proof relies on known results in metric measure spaces and the identification of the Cheeger energy from the previous section.
We introduce strongly regular curves and show existence of such in Theorem
4.11
employing the regularization from Definition
3.2
.
Lastly, we establish action and entropy estimates along such curves, allowing us to conclude the proof.

2
Preliminaries

Let
{\mathsf{G}}=({\mathsf{E}},{\mathsf{V}})
be a connected combinatorial graph with a finite set of vertices
{\mathsf{V}}
and edges
{\mathsf{E}}\subset{\mathsf{V}}\times{\mathsf{V}}
.
We endow the graph with a map
\ell:{\mathsf{E}}\to(0,\infty)
,
\ell({\mathsf{e}})=:\ell_{\mathsf{e}}
and define an orientation on each edge using the outer normal

\displaystyle{\mathsf{n}}^{\mathsf{e}}({\mathsf{v}})\coloneqq\begin{cases}-1&:{\mathsf{e}}=({\mathsf{v}},{\mathsf{w}})\\
+1&:{\mathsf{e}}=({\mathsf{w}},{\mathsf{v}})\\
0&:{\mathsf{v}}\notin{\mathsf{e}}\end{cases},

allowing us to identify edges with intervals
[-\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{2},\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{2}]
. We call the triple
{\mathsf{G}}=({\mathsf{V}},{\mathsf{E}},\ell)
a
metric graph
.
For each
{\mathsf{v}}\in{\mathsf{V}}
we further denote the set of incident edges by
{\mathsf{E}}({\mathsf{v}})\coloneqq\{{\mathsf{e}}\in{\mathsf{E}}\ |{\mathsf{v}}\in{\mathsf{e}}\}
and define the spaces

\displaystyle{\mathsf{L}}\coloneqq\bigsqcup\limits_{{\mathsf{e}}\in{\mathsf{E}}}\left[-\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{2},\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{2}\right]\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathsf{G}}\coloneqq{\mathsf{L}}/\sim,

(2.1)

where the first set is a collection of the one-dimensional intervals associated to the edges. In the second set
{\mathsf{G}}
we identify vertices uniquely using the equivalence relation
\sim
. Together with the
graph distance

d:{\mathsf{G}}\times{\mathsf{G}}\to[0,\infty)
, which is the length of the shortest path between two points on
{\mathsf{G}}
, the tuple
({\mathsf{G}},d)
defines a metric space.
We denote by
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathsf{G}})
the space of probability measures on the metric graph, by
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathsf{G}})
the space of Radon measures and by
{\mathcal{M}}_{+}({\mathsf{G}})
the space of non-negative Radon measures. Additionally, let
{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm ac}({\mathsf{G}})
be the set of probability measures that are absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure
{L}
. Here,
{L}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{+}({\mathsf{G}})
is the measure on
{\mathsf{G}}
induced by the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathsf{L}}
under the equivalence relation
\sim
. The corresponding Lebesgue spaces are denoted by
L^{p}({\mathsf{G}})
for
p\in[1,\infty]
with norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{L^{p}({\mathsf{G}})}
. In particular,
({\mathsf{G}},d)
is a geodesic space, rendering the Wasserstein space
({\mathcal{P}}({\mathsf{G}}),W_{2})
a geodesic space as well.

Let
(X,d)
be an arbitrary metric space. For any closed interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
and
p\in[1,\infty]
we call
\gamma:I\to X

absolutely continuous
, and write
\gamma\in{\rm AC}^{p}\left(I;X\right)
, if there exists
g\in L^{p}(I)
such that for all
t_{0},t_{1}\in I
with
t_{0}<t_{1}
it holds that

\displaystyle d\left(\gamma_{t_{0}},\gamma_{t_{1}}\right)\leq\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}g(\tau)\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}\tau.

(2.2)

By
[
4
]
[Theorem 1.1.2], every absolutely continuous curve
\gamma\in{\rm AC}^{p}\left(I;X\right)
admits a
metric derivative

\left\lvert\dot{\gamma}_{t}\right\rvert\in L^{p}(I)
given as the smallest function
g\in L^{p}(I)
satisfying (
2.2
).
On a metric graph we define the
local
and
global Lipschitz constant
of a function
f:{\mathsf{G}}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
as

\displaystyle{\rm{lip}}_{f}(x)\coloneqq\limsup\limits_{y\to x}\frac{\left\lvert f(x)-f(y)\right\rvert}{d(x,y)}\quad\text{and}\quad{\rm{Lip}}_{f}\coloneqq\sup\limits_{y\neq x}\frac{\left\lvert f(x)-f(y)\right\rvert}{d(x,y)}

for
x\in{\rm Dom}(f)=\left\{x\in{\mathsf{G}}\,:\,f(x)\in\mathbb{R}\right\}
.
Since
({\mathsf{G}},d)
is a geodesic space, we have
{\rm{Lip}}_{f}=\sup_{x\in{\mathsf{G}}}{\rm{lip}}_{f}(x)
.
The space of Lipschitz functions is denoted by
{\rm Lip}({\mathsf{G}})
, while
C({\mathsf{G}})
is the space of continuous functions equipped with the supremum norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{C({\mathsf{G}})}
. The space
C^{1}({\mathsf{G}})
contains all functions from
C({\mathsf{G}})
that are continuously differentiable on each edge of the graph and we denote the space of non-negative Lipschitz functions by
{\rm Lip}_{+}({\mathsf{G}})
.
In the same way, for
p\in[1,\infty]
we define the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}({\mathsf{G}})
as the space of functions in
L^{p}({\mathsf{G}})
such that their weak derivative exists as a function in
L^{p}({\mathsf{e}})
on each edge
{\mathsf{e}}\in{\mathsf{E}}
. As was shown in
[
21
]
[Lemma 3.27], every element
f\in W^{1,p}({\mathsf{G}})
admits a continuous representative in
C({\mathsf{G}})
uniquely determined in the vertices.
Note that
{\mathsf{G}}
is compact by definition, which is why all probability measures have finite second moment and why duality holds between
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathsf{G}})
and
C({\mathsf{G}})
. We say that a sequence of measures
(\mu^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset{\mathcal{M}}({\mathsf{G}})

converges weakly
to another measure
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}({\mathsf{G}})
, and write
\mu^{n}\rightharpoonup\mu
, if for all
\varphi\in C({\mathsf{G}})
it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathsf{G}}\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}\mu^{n}\to\int_{\mathsf{G}}\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}\mu\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

Curvature bounds are closely related to the heat equation and the resulting semigroup on the underlying space. On metric graphs, this equation is understood in the following sense.
For each edge
{\mathsf{e}}\in{\mathsf{E}}
, we consider the one-dimensional heat equation on
[-\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{2},\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{2}]
, coupling them in the vertices. Depending on the choice of coupling conditions, we obtain different systems. Throughout this article, we impose the so-called
standard coupling conditions
. For
f^{\mathsf{e}}\coloneqq f|_{{\mathsf{e}}}
they lead to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f^{\mathsf{e}}(t,x)=\partial_{xx}f^{\mathsf{e}}(t,x)&t\geq 0,\,x\in{\mathsf{e}},\,{\mathsf{e}}\in{\mathsf{E}}\\
\sum_{{\mathsf{e}}\in{\mathsf{E}}({\mathsf{v}})}\partial_{x}f^{\mathsf{e}}(t,{\mathsf{v}})\cdot{\mathsf{n}}^{\mathsf{e}}({\mathsf{v}})=0&t\geq 0,\,{\mathsf{v}}\in{\mathsf{V}}\\
f^{\mathsf{e}}(t,{\mathsf{v}})=f^{{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}}(t,{\mathsf{v}})&t\geq 0,\,{\mathsf{e}},{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}\in{\mathsf{E}}({\mathsf{v}})\end{cases}

(2.3)

with initial condition
f(0,\cdot)=f_{0}(\cdot)
.
Existence of solutions to (
2.3
) has been studied in
[
14
]
employing semigroup techniques and in
[
11
]
using minimizing movements.
As was first shown in
[
22
]
and later extended to more general coupling conditions in
[
16
]
, solutions to the heat equation can be characterized by a heat kernel
p_{t}(x,y)
.
We collect some useful properties of the semigroup and its kernel below.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
{\mathsf{G}}=({\mathsf{V}},{\mathsf{E}},\ell)
be a metric graph and let
g_{t}(z)=\left(2\pi t\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{4t}}
be the usual one-dimensional heat kernel. Then, there exists a heat kernel
p_{t}:{\mathsf{G}}\times{\mathsf{G}}\to\mathbb{R}
for
t>0
such that the following properties hold true.

i)

For
T>0
there exists a constant
C_{\rm up}(T)>0
such that
0\leq p_{t}(x,y)\leq C_{\rm up}(t)g_{t}(d(x,y))
for all
0<t\leq T
and
{L}
-a.e.
x,y\in{\mathsf{G}}
.

ii)

The kernel is continuous. Moreover
\partial_{t}p_{t}(x,y)\in C({\mathsf{G}})
for all
t>0
,
x,y\in{\mathsf{G}}
and
\partial_{x}p_{t}(x,y)\in C({\mathsf{L}})
,
\partial_{xx}p_{t}(x,y)\in C({\mathsf{L}})
for all
t>0
,
x,y\in{\mathsf{L}}
.

iii)

For all
t>0
and
x\in{\mathsf{G}}
it holds that
\lVert p_{t}(x,\cdot)\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathsf{G}})}=1
.

Proof.

A proof of the first part can be found in
[
8
]
[Lemma 5.3] and the last two statements have been shown in
[
22
]
.
∎

3
The heat equation as a gradient flow

In this section, we study the heat equation on metric graphs and its two interpretations, namely the one as an
L^{2}
-gradient flow of the Cheeger energy and as a
W_{2}
-gradient flow of the logarithmic entropy. The latter has already been studied and made rigorous in
[
11
]
, while the first one has been treated in general metric measure spaces in
[
1
]
.
Our main goal is to show that both coincide on metric graphs, thus giving rise to the notion of heat flow. To this end, we show that the Cheeger energy is equivalent to the Dirichlet energy and that the abstract
L^{2}
-gradient flow induces the heat equation with standard coupling conditions from (
2.3
).

We begin by studying the Cheeger energy and its gradient flow.
Let
{\rm{Ch}}:L^{2}({\mathsf{G}})\to[0,\infty]
with

\displaystyle{\rm{Ch}}(f)=\inf\left\{\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathsf{G}}{\rm{lip}}_{f_{n}}(x)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}{L}\,:\,f_{n}\in{\rm Lip}({\mathsf{G}}),~f_{n}\to f\text{ in }L^{2}({\mathsf{G}})\right\}

(3.1)

denote the
Cheeger energy
and let the
Dirichlet energy

{\mathcal{E}}:W^{1,2}({\mathsf{G}})\to[0,\infty)
be given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}(f)=\int_{\mathsf{G}}\lvert\nabla f\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x.

As was shown in
[
1
]
[Theorems 6.2, 6.3] and
[
1
]
[Remark 4.7], the Cheeger energy admits the integral representation

\displaystyle{\rm{Ch}}(f)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathsf{G}}\left\lvert{\rm D}f\right\rvert_{w}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}{L}

(3.2)

for any
f\in{\rm Dom}({\rm{Ch}})
, where
\left\lvert{\rm D}f\right\rvert_{w}
is the so-called
minimal relaxed gradient
. It is defined as the unique minimal element
g\in L^{2}({\mathsf{G}})
such that there exists a family
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset{\rm Lip}({\mathsf{G}})
with
f_{n}\to f
and
{\rm{lip}}_{f_{n}}\to g
in
L^{2}({\mathsf{G}})
. It has been shown, see
[
5
]
[Theorem 3.1] and
[
4
]
for the theory of Hilbertian gradient flows, that
{\rm{Ch}}
gives rise to an
L^{2}
-gradient flow. Let
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
denote the corresponding semigroup with dual
\left(H_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
, extending the semigroup from
L^{2}({\mathsf{G}})
to
{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm ac}({\mathsf{G}})
by

\displaystyle H_{t}\mu=\left(P_{t}f\right){L}\quad\text{if}\quad\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}\mu}{\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}{L}}=f.

As a first step towards the characterization of
(H_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, we show that Lipschitz functions are weakly differentiable
{L}
-a.e. with bounded derivatives. More precisely, we prove that
{\rm Lip}({\mathsf{G}})=W^{1,\infty}({\mathsf{G}})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

It holds that
f\in W^{1,\infty}({\mathsf{G}})
if and only if
f\in{\rm Lip}({\mathsf{G}})
.

Proof.

First, let
f\in W^{1,\infty}({\mathsf{G}})
. Given
[
21
]
[Lemma 3.27], there exists a continuous representative, which we again denote by
f\in C({\mathsf{G}})
and it remains to show its Lipschitz-continuity.
Let
x,y\in{\mathsf{G}}
be arbitrary points on the graph and suppose that there exists an edge
{\mathsf{e}}\in{\mathsf{E}}
such that
x,y\in{\mathsf{e}}
. We denote by
f^{\mathsf{e}}
the restriction of
f
to
{\mathsf{e}}
, continuously extended by a constant value to a function on the real line. In particular
f^{\mathsf{e}}\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R})
by construction. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define

\displaystyle f^{\mathsf{e}}_{n}(x)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\zeta_{n}(x-y)f^{\mathsf{e}}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}y,

where
\zeta_{n}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a standard mollifier such that
f_{n}^{\mathsf{e}}\to f^{\mathsf{e}}
uniformly as
n\to\infty
and
\left\lVert\nabla f_{n}^{\mathsf{e}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\lVert\nabla f^{\mathsf{e}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
. Choosing the parametrization of
{\mathsf{e}}
such that
x\leq y
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert f^{\mathsf{e}}_{n}(y)-f^{\mathsf{e}}_{n}(x)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\int_{x}^{y}\nabla f^{\mathsf{e}}_{n}(z)\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}z\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert\nabla f^{\mathsf{e}}_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([x,y])}\lvert y-x\rvert\leq\left\lVert\nabla f^{\mathsf{e}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathsf{e}})}d(x,y)

and for
n\to\infty
by uniform convergence

\displaystyle\left\lvert f(y)-f(x)\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert\nabla f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathsf{e}})}d(x,y)\leq\left\lVert\nabla f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathsf{G}})}d(x,y).

(3.3)

Now, suppose that
x\in{\mathsf{e}}
and
y\in{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}
for
{\mathsf{e}}\neq{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}
. Since
({\mathsf{G}},d)
is a geodesic metric space, we can find a shortest path connecting
x
to
y
. We denote by
\left(v_{i}\right)_{i=0,\ldots,N}\subset{\mathsf{V}}
the ordered sequence of vertices lying on that path, where
v_{0}\coloneqq x
and
v_{N}\coloneqq y
. Then

\displaystyle\left\lvert f(y)-f(x)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\left\lvert f(v_{i})-f(v_{i-1})\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert\nabla f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathsf{G}})}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}d(v_{i},v_{i-1})=\left\lVert\nabla f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathsf{G}})}d(x,y),

using (
3.3
) in the second inequality. This shows
f\in{\rm Lip}({\mathsf{G}})
with
{\rm{Lip}}_{f}\leq\left\lVert\nabla f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathsf{G}})}
.

For the other implication, let
f\in{\rm Lip}({\mathsf{G}})
be given. By definition
f\in C({\mathsf{G}})
, so that it remains to show weak differentiability on each edge. Fix
{\mathsf{e}}\in{\mathsf{E}}
and let
f^{\mathsf{e}}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
as before. For
\lvert\delta\rvert\in(0,1)
and
x\in{\mathsf{e}}
we have that

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\mathsf{e}}(x)-f^{\mathsf{e}}(x-\delta)}{\delta}\leq{\rm{lip}}_{f^{\mathsf{e}}}(x),

implying

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{f^{\mathsf{e}}(x)-f^{\mathsf{e}}(x-\delta)}{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathsf{e}})}\leq{\rm{Lip}}_{f^{\mathsf{e}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\left\lVert\frac{f^{\mathsf{e}}(x)-f^{\mathsf{e}}(x-\delta)}{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathsf{e}})}\leq\sqrt{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{\rm{Lip}}_{f^{\mathsf{e}}}

by Hölder’s inequality. Therefore, there exists a subsequence
\delta_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
such that
\frac{f^{\mathsf{e}}(\cdot)-f^{\mathsf{e}}(\cdot-\delta_{k})}{\delta_{k}}
converges weakly to a function
g\in L^{2}({\mathsf{e}})
. For all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathsf{e}})
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathsf{e}}\nabla\varphi(x)f^{\mathsf{e}}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathsf{e}}\frac{\varphi(x+\delta_{k})-\varphi(x)}{\delta_{k}}f^{\mathsf{e}}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x

\displaystyle=-\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathsf{e}}\varphi(x)\frac{f^{\mathsf{e}}(x)-f^{\mathsf{e}}(x-\delta_{k})}{\delta_{k}}\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathsf{e}}\varphi(x)g(x)\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x,

showing that
f
is weakly differentiable with weak derivative
g=\nabla f^{\mathsf{e}}
. Since
{\mathsf{e}}\in{\mathsf{E}}
was arbitrary, we have shown
f\in W^{1,2}({\mathsf{G}})
.
Now, let
\varepsilon>0
and fix
{\mathsf{e}}\in{\mathsf{E}}
. We define the set

\displaystyle A_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\left\{x\in{\mathsf{e}}\,:\,\lvert\nabla f(x)\rvert\geq{\rm{Lip}}_{f}+\varepsilon\right\}

and weak convergence implies

\displaystyle{L}(A_{\varepsilon})\left({\rm{Lip}}_{f}+\varepsilon\right)\leq\int_{A_{\varepsilon}}\lvert\nabla f^{\mathsf{e}}(x)\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x\leq\liminf\limits_{k\to\infty}\int_{A_{\varepsilon}}\left\lvert\frac{f^{\mathsf{e}}(x)-f^{\mathsf{e}}(x-\delta_{k})}{\delta_{k}}\right\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x\leq{L}(A_{\varepsilon}){\rm{Lip}}_{f}.

Therefore,
{L}(A_{\varepsilon})=0
for all
\varepsilon>0
and we infer that
\left\lVert\nabla f^{\mathsf{e}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathsf{e}})}\leq{\rm{Lip}}_{f^{\mathsf{e}}}
. Since the choice
{\mathsf{e}}\in{\mathsf{E}}
was arbitrary, this proves
f\in W^{1,\infty}({\mathsf{G}})
.
∎

Recalling the definition of the Cheeger energy from (
3.1
), it is necessary to construct approximations by Lipschitz functions when characterizing elements in its domain.
To this end, we make use of the ideas presented in

.
We begin by extending the metric graph, introducing one auxiliary edge for each vertex.
Let
{\mathsf{e}}=({\mathsf{v}},{\mathsf{w}})\in{\mathsf{E}}
be an arbitrary edge of
{\mathsf{G}}
, parametrized in such a way that
{\mathsf{e}}=\left[-\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{2},\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{2}\right]
. The new edges are identified with
{\mathsf{e}}_{\mathsf{v}}^{2\varepsilon}=\left[-\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{2}-2\varepsilon,-\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{2}\right]
and
{\mathsf{e}}_{\mathsf{w}}^{2\varepsilon}=\left[\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{2},\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{2}+2\varepsilon\right]
, see Figure
3.1
for an illustration. From this extension, we obtain a new vertex of degree one denoted by
{\mathsf{v}}^{2\varepsilon}
for each
{\mathsf{v}}\in{\mathsf{V}}
. Note that we only add one new edge for each vertex, however, depending on the incident edge
{\mathsf{e}}\in{\mathsf{E}}({\mathsf{v}})
, we parametrize it differently.
The extended metric graph is denoted by
{\mathsf{G}}^{2\varepsilon}=({\mathsf{V}}^{2\varepsilon},{\mathsf{E}}^{2\varepsilon},\ell^{2\varepsilon})
with domains defined in analogy to (
2.1
). We introduce the following regularization, see

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\varphi\in L^{1}({\mathsf{G}}^{2\varepsilon})
be given. We define the function
\varphi^{\varepsilon}:{\mathsf{G}}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\varphi^{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\alpha^{\varepsilon}_{\mathsf{e}}x-\varepsilon}^{\alpha^{\varepsilon}_{\mathsf{e}}x+\varepsilon}\varphi(y)\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}y

for
x\in{\mathsf{e}}
and
\alpha^{\varepsilon}_{\mathsf{e}}=\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}+2\varepsilon}{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}
. For
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}({\mathsf{G}})
we define its regularization by duality as the measure
\mu^{\varepsilon}\in{\mathcal{M}}({\mathsf{G}}^{2\varepsilon})
such that for all

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathsf{G}}^{2\varepsilon}}\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}\mu^{\varepsilon}=\int_{\mathsf{G}}\varphi^{\varepsilon}\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}\mu.

In general, we will deal with functions that are defined on
{\mathsf{G}}

{\mathsf{G}}^{2\varepsilon}
for some

. We use the following conventions. If
\varphi\in L^{1}({\mathsf{G}})

\varphi|_{e^{2\varepsilon}_{\mathsf{v}}}\equiv 0

. On the other hand, if

\varphi|_{e^{2\varepsilon}_{\mathsf{v}}}\equiv\varphi({\mathsf{v}})
for

.
This regularization satisfies several useful properties.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
\varphi\in C({\mathsf{G}}^{2\varepsilon})
and
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}({\mathsf{G}})
be given. The following hold true.

i)

For
\varphi^{\varepsilon}:{\mathsf{G}}\to\mathbb{R}
we have that
\varphi^{\varepsilon}\in C^{1}({\mathsf{G}})
with derivative given by

\displaystyle\nabla\varphi^{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{\alpha^{\varepsilon}_{\mathsf{e}}}{2\varepsilon}\left(\varphi(\alpha^{\varepsilon}_{\mathsf{e}}x-\varepsilon)-\varphi(\alpha^{\varepsilon}_{\mathsf{e}}x+\varepsilon)\right)

for all
x\in{\mathsf{e}}
and
\varphi^{\varepsilon}\to\varphi
uniformly in
{\mathsf{G}}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

ii)

The regularization is mass preserving in the sense that
\mu^{\varepsilon}({\mathsf{G}}^{2\varepsilon})=\mu({\mathsf{G}})
.

iii)

If
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathsf{G}})
, then its regularization
\mu^{\varepsilon}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathsf{G}}^{2\varepsilon})
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure and has the density
f^{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}({\mathsf{G}}^{2\varepsilon})
given by

\displaystyle f^{\varepsilon}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\mu(e\cap I_{\mathsf{e}}^{\varepsilon}(x))&:x\in{\mathsf{E}}\\
\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\left(1\!\!1_{\{d(x,{\mathsf{v}})\leq 2\varepsilon\}}\mu(\{{\mathsf{v}}\})+\sum\limits_{{\mathsf{e}}\in{\mathsf{E}}({\mathsf{v}})}\mu(e\cap I_{\mathsf{e}}^{\varepsilon}(x))\right)&:x\in{\mathsf{e}}^{2\varepsilon}_{\mathsf{v}},\,{\mathsf{v}}\in{\mathsf{V}}\end{cases}

for

\displaystyle I_{e}^{\varepsilon}(x)=\left(\max\left\{\frac{x-\varepsilon}{\alpha_{\mathsf{e}}^{\varepsilon}},-\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{2}\right\},\min\left\{\frac{x+\varepsilon}{\alpha_{\mathsf{e}}^{\varepsilon}},\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{2}\right\}\right).

Moreover it holds that
f^{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}
on
{\mathsf{G}}^{2\varepsilon}
.

iv)

As
\varepsilon\to 0
it holds that
\mu^{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\mu
in
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathsf{G}}^{2\varepsilon})
for
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathsf{G}})
.

v)

If
s\mapsto\mu_{s}\in{\rm AC}^{2}\left([0,1];{\mathcal{P}}({\mathsf{G}})\right)
then
s\mapsto\mu_{s}^{\varepsilon}\in{\rm AC}^{2}\left([0,1];{\mathcal{P}}({\mathsf{G}}^{2\varepsilon})\right)
and

\displaystyle\left\lvert\dot{\mu}^{\varepsilon}_{s}\right\rvert\leq\left(1+\frac{2\varepsilon}{\ell_{\min}}\right)\left\lvert\dot{\mu}_{s}\right\rvert

for a.e.

with

. As a consequence

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon>0}\left\lvert\dot{\mu}^{\varepsilon}_{s}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\dot{\mu}_{s}\right\rvert

for a.e.
s\in[0,1]

Proof.

The first result
i)
is the content of

The statements

as

for all

As a consequence of the last property, the regularization introduced in Definition
3.2
is compatible with metric derivatives in the sense that it preserves absolute continuity and its metric derivative is asymptotically bounded by the original speed. Moreover, it provides sufficient regularity to construct Lipschitz approximations.

Theorem 3.4
.

For every function
f\in W^{1,2}({\mathsf{G}})
there exists a sequence of functions
f_{n}\in{\rm Lip}({\mathsf{G}})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
f_{n}\to f
uniformly on
{\mathsf{G}}
as
n\to\infty
with
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathsf{G}}{\rm{lip}}_{f_{n}}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x\leq\left\lVert\nabla f\right\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathsf{G}})}^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
f\in W^{1,2}({\mathsf{G}})
be given. In particular,
f\in C({\mathsf{G}})
by
[
21
]
[Lemma 3.27] and, for each
\varepsilon>0
, we find a function
f^{\varepsilon}\in C^{1}({\mathsf{G}})
using Definition
3.2
such that
f^{\varepsilon}\to f
uniformly as
\varepsilon\to 0
and
f^{\varepsilon}\in{\rm Lip}({\mathsf{G}})
by Theorem
3.1
.
For
x\in{\mathsf{G}}\setminus{{\mathsf{V}}}
and any sequence
\left(y_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset{\mathsf{G}}
such that
d(x,y_{n})\to 0
, we have
y_{n}\in{\mathsf{e}}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large. Then, by the fundamental theorem of calculus and Proposition
3.3

i)
it holds that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\frac{f^{\varepsilon}(x)-f^{\varepsilon}(y_{n})}{d(x,y_{n})}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\frac{1}{\left\lvert x-y_{n}\right\rvert}\int_{x}^{y_{n}}\nabla f^{\varepsilon}(z)\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}z\right\rvert\leq\frac{\alpha^{\varepsilon}_{\mathsf{e}}}{2\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\left\lvert x-y_{n}\right\rvert}\int_{x}^{y_{n}}\left\lvert f\left(\alpha^{\varepsilon}_{\mathsf{e}}z-\varepsilon\right)-f\left(\alpha^{\varepsilon}_{\mathsf{e}}z+\varepsilon\right)\right\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}z.

This gives

\displaystyle{\rm{lip}}_{f^{\varepsilon}}(x)\leq\alpha^{\varepsilon}_{\mathsf{e}}\left\lvert\frac{f(\alpha^{\varepsilon}_{\mathsf{e}}x-\varepsilon)-f(\alpha^{\varepsilon}_{\mathsf{e}}x+\varepsilon)}{2\varepsilon}\right\rvert

since
f
is continuous and every point is a Lebesgue point. Taking the squared
L^{2}({\mathsf{e}})
-norm of this estimate, we infer that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathsf{e}}{\rm{lip}}_{f^{\varepsilon}}(x)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\left(\alpha^{\varepsilon}_{\mathsf{e}}\right)^{2}\int_{\mathsf{e}}\left\lvert\frac{f(\alpha^{\varepsilon}_{\mathsf{e}}x-\varepsilon)-f(\alpha^{\varepsilon}_{\mathsf{e}}x+\varepsilon)}{2\varepsilon}\right\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x.

By weak differentiability, we can apply
[
9
]
[Proposition 9.3] and obtain the limit

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathsf{e}}{\rm{lip}}_{f^{\varepsilon}}(x)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathsf{e}}~\left\lvert\nabla f(x)\right\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x.

Summing over all edges and defining
f_{n}\coloneqq f^{\frac{1}{n}}
concludes the proof.
∎

Having established the approximation result, we can identify Cheeger and Dirichlet energy.

Theorem 3.5
.

On a metric graph it holds that
{\rm Dom}({\rm{Ch}})=W^{1,2}({\mathsf{G}})
,
\left\lvert\nabla f\right\rvert=\left\lvert{\rm D}f\right\rvert_{w}

{L}
-a.e. and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}(f)=2{\rm{Ch}}(f).

Proof.

We split the proof into two parts, showing the inclusions separately.

First, we prove that for
f\in W^{1,2}({\mathsf{G}})
we have
f\in{\rm Dom}({\rm{Ch}})
with
2{\rm{Ch}}(f)\leq\left\lVert\nabla f\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathsf{G}})}
.
Let
f\in W^{1,2}({\mathsf{G}})
be given. As shown in Theorem
3.4
, there exists a sequence
f_{n}\in{\rm Lip}({\mathsf{G}})
such that
f_{n}\to f
in
C({\mathsf{G}})
with

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathsf{G}}{\rm{lip}}_{f_{n}}(x)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x\leq\left\lVert\nabla f\right\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathsf{G}})}^{2}.

This directly implies

\displaystyle 2{\rm{Ch}}(f)\leq\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathsf{G}}{\rm{lip}}_{f_{n}}(x)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x\leq\left\lVert\nabla f\right\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathsf{G}})}^{2}<\infty,

so that
f\in{\rm Dom}({\rm{Ch}})
and the bound holds true.

Next, we show that
f\in{\rm Dom}({\rm{Ch}})
implies
f\in W^{1,2}({\mathsf{G}})
with
\left\lVert\nabla f\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathsf{G}})}\leq 2{\rm{Ch}}(f)
.
Using the equivalent characterization of the Cheeger energy given in (
3.2
), we know that for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a family of functions
\left(f_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in{\rm Lip}({\mathsf{G}})
such that
f_{n}\to f
and
{\rm{lip}}_{f_{n}}\to\left\lvert{\rm D}f\right\rvert_{w}
in

with

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\left\lVert{\rm{lip}}_{f_{n}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathsf{G}})}^{2}\leq 2{\rm{Ch}}(f)+\varepsilon.

In particular, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\rm{lip}}_{f_{n}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathsf{G}})}^{2}\leq 2{\rm{Ch}}(f)+2\varepsilon.

On each edge
{\mathsf{e}}=[-\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{2},\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{2}]\in{\mathsf{E}}
we extend every function
f_{n}\in{\rm Lip}({\mathsf{G}})
by the constant value attained in the vertices, leading to a Lipschitz continuous extension. For
\lvert\delta\rvert\in(0,1)
arbitrary we then have that

\displaystyle\int_{-\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{2}}^{\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{2}}\left\lvert\frac{f_{n}(x+\delta)-f_{n}(x)}{\delta}\right\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x\leq\int_{-\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{2}}^{\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{2}}{\rm{lip}}_{f_{n}}^{2}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x.

Observing that the right-hand side does not depend on
\lvert\delta\rvert\in(0,1)
, we can apply
[
13
]
[Chapter 5.8, Theorem 3] yielding weak differentiability on each edge and after summation over all edges this gives

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla f_{n}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}\left({\mathsf{G}}\right)}\leq\sum\limits_{{\mathsf{e}}\in{\mathsf{E}}}\int_{-\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{2}}^{\frac{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}{2}}{\rm{lip}}_{f_{n}}^{2}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x\leq 2{\rm{Ch}}(f)+2\varepsilon.

(3.4)

Therefore, there exists a weakly convergent subsequence such that
\nabla f_{n_{k}}\rightharpoonup g
in
L^{2}({\mathsf{L}})
as
k\to\infty
.
However, for
\varphi\in C^{1}({\mathsf{G}})
with
\varphi({\mathsf{v}})=0
for all
{\mathsf{v}}\in{\mathsf{V}}
we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathsf{G}}\nabla\varphi f\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x\longleftarrow\int_{\mathsf{G}}\nabla\varphi f_{n_{k}}\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathsf{G}}\varphi\nabla f_{n_{k}}\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x\longrightarrow\int_{\mathsf{G}}\varphi g\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x

as
k\to\infty
, implying
g=\nabla f\in L^{2}({\mathsf{L}})
. This gives
\left\lVert\nabla f\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathsf{G}})}\leq 2{\rm{Ch}}(f)+2\varepsilon
and, since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we have shown that
f\in W^{1,2}({\mathsf{G}})
with

\displaystyle\int_{\mathsf{G}}\left\lvert\nabla f(x)\right\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x\leq 2{\rm{Ch}}(f)=\int_{\mathsf{G}}\left\lvert{\rm D}f\right\rvert_{w}^{2}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}x.

Due to uniqueness of the minimal relaxed gradient, we also obtain
\left\lvert\nabla f\right\rvert=\left\lvert{\rm D}f\right\rvert_{w}

{L}
-a.e. in
{\mathsf{G}}
.
∎

Employing Theorem

As another consequence of Theorem

As was shown in

Let

As was shown in

]

-gradient flow of

.

Let

are weak solutions of (

Lemma 3.9
.

Let

and

defines a

-gradient flow curve of the logarithmic entropy. On the other hand, if

-gradient flow curve of the entropy starting from

and it defines an

-gradient flow curve of the Cheeger energy.

Proof.

The curve

solves the continuity equation weakly, see
[
11
]
[Remark 2] and by
[
11
]
[Theorem 5.5] it defines a gradient flow curve as in Definition

Now, let
\mu_{t}=f_{t}{L}
be a

-gradient flow curve of the logarithmic entropy starting from

. From the first part of the proof we know that

Suppose that

and define

with

for

. The functional

from Definition

is strictly convex due to the strict convexity of the logarithmic entropy, see for example

[Lemma 3.11]. Therefore, we obtain

which contradicts the non-negativity of the functional

shown in
[

[Theorem 5.5]. This gives

Remark 3.10

Following the same arguments as in the previous proof and employing strict convexity of the functional

for the heat semigroup and its dual associated to (

Lemma 4.1

The following hold true.

(\rm A1)

(\rm A2)

(\rm A3)

(\rm A4)

(\rm A5)

\displaystyle d_{{\rm{Ch}}}(x,y)\coloneqq\sup\left\{\left\lvert f(x)-f(y)\right\rvert\,:\,f\in{\rm Dom}({\rm{Ch}})\cap C({\mathsf{G}}),\,\Gamma(f)\leq 1\;{L}\text{-a.e.}\right\}.

Proof.

The first property has been discussed in
[
21
]
[Lemma 3.17] and the second one is a direct consequence of the properties of the one-dimensional Lebesgue measure.

\displaystyle{\rm{Ch}}(f+g)+{\rm{Ch}}(f-g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathsf{G}}\left\lvert\nabla(f+g)\right\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}{L}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathsf{G}}\left\lvert\nabla(f-g)\right\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}{L}

From Theorem

Another important property of the heat flow is the heat-smoothing effect. In view of Theorem

As was shown in

[Theorem 3.6],

for all

. In particular,

and by Theorem

4.1
Strongly regular curves

The main difficulty in the proof of Theorem

Definition 4.6

A curve

Lemma 4.7

s\mapsto\mu_{s}\in{\rm AC}^{2}\left([0,1];{\mathcal{P}}({\mathsf{G}})\right)

\displaystyle s\mapsto\tilde{\mu}_{s}=H_{\vartheta(s)}\mu_{h(s)}\in C\left([0,1];{\mathcal{P}}({\mathsf{G}})\right)

\displaystyle s\mapsto\int_{\mathsf{G}}\varphi_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}\tilde{\mu}_{s}\in{\rm Lip}\left([0,1]\right)

Remark 4.8

The definition

Similar to the action estimate from the previous lemma, we want to characterize the change of the entropy next. However, the entropy may not be differentiable if the curve vanishes on a set of positive Lebesgue measure.
For this reason, we regularize the integrand as well.
For

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathsf{G}}\log\left(\frac{f+\delta}{\delta}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}\mu+\log(\delta)\leq\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\mathsf{G}}f^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathup{d}{L}+\log(\delta)<\infty.

4.2

5

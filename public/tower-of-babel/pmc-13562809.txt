Dengue Surge in Bangladesh: Exploring the Role of Climate Factors—A Narrative Review With Ecological Analysis
Authors: Mohammad Ashraful Amin, Ridwana Maher Manna, Sabrina Nahin, Mohammad Delwer Hossain Hawlader
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground and AimsDengue, a mosquito‐borne viral infection, poses a significant public health challenge in Bangladesh, with a recent surge in reported dengue cases and fatalities at the national level. This paper aims to investigate the association between climatic factors and the 2023 dengue outbreak in Bangladesh, with a particular focus on Dhaka City. It also explores factors contributing to the surge in cases and atypical features observed.MethodsA structured narrative review was conducted alongside an ecological analysis of secondary data. The study utilized epidemiological data on reported dengue cases and climate variables, including climate data from 2019 to 2023. Relevant scientific literature on dengue and climate change was also reviewed. Monthly trends were analyzed, and associations between dengue incidence and temperature variables were assessed. Findings were interpreted descriptively in the context of existing literature.ResultsThere was a concerning rise in dengue cases and fatalities in Bangladesh during 2023. Notably, Dhaka City experienced a surge in cases from June to December despite a slight decrease in average temperatures compared to previous years. This surge suggests a possible shift in transmission dynamics under changing environmental conditions. However, when data from all 5 years were aggregated and analyzed using average monthly temperature values, a statistically significant correlation was observed between dengue cases and minimum temperature (p = 0.04), whereas no statistically significant correlation was observed with maximum temperature. Reports from selected clinical studies suggest atypical presentations and a decrease in the characteristic skin rash were observed in hospitalized children, although these findings are not based on nationally representative data.ConclusionThe 2023 dengue outbreak in Bangladesh highlights a complex and multifactorial relationship between climate factors and dengue transmission. This highlights the need to prioritize climate change research, developing a tetravalent dengue vaccine, and implementing proactive vector control measures. Collaborative efforts involving health agencies, researchers, and policymakers are essential to combat this evolving public health challenge.

Introduction

Dengue, a mosquito‐borne viral infection, has been endemic in Bangladesh for several decades. The virus exists in four serotypes, namely Dengue virus (DENV): DENV‐1, DENV‐2, DENV‐3, and DENV‐4, and individuals can be infected by each of these serotypes. It is important to note that while infection with one serotype confers long‐term immunity to that specific serotype, it does not provide immunity against the others. Sequential infections, particularly with different serotypes, increase the risk of developing severe dengue, posing a considerable health threat. The growth factor of dengue cases since 2010 has been closely associated with regional rainfall patterns, especially during the months from May to September, along with higher environmental temperatures. These factors have created a more favorable environment for the transmission of dengue [1].

As of October 2, 2023, there had been over 4.2 million cases and over 3000 dengue‐related deaths reported from 79 different nations and territories worldwide [2]. Among the countries most affected by the outbreak is Brazil. Notably, in Brazil, between 2015 and 2021, a total of 33,026 dengue cases were recorded, with the peak in both incidence and mortality occurring in 2016 [3]. This resulted in a case fatality rate (CFR) of 0.042%.

Looking ahead to the second half of 2023, health authorities predicted an increase in rainfall in certain countries within the region, particularly in Central America and the Caribbean. Such climatic changes are expected to exacerbate dengue incidence in already endemic areas, placing additional strain on health systems [4, 5]. The 2023 dengue outbreak in Bangladesh stands out for its unprecedented scale and unusual seasonal pattern, presenting serious challenges for public health response. This study aims to examine the association between climatic factors and dengue transmission dynamics in Bangladesh, with a particular focus on the 2023 outbreak, while also providing contextual insights into epidemiological patterns, serotype circulation, and clinical manifestations.

Objectives

The primary objective of this study is to examine the association between climatic factors and dengue incidence trends in Bangladesh using ecological analysis of secondary data.

Scope and Methodology of the Review

Study design: This study is designed as a narrative review supplemented by an exploratory ecological analysis of secondary data. For clarity, “dengue cases” refers to reported confirmed or clinically diagnosed cases from DGHS surveillance data, “hospital admissions” refers to dengue‐related inpatient records, and “incidence” refers to the number of reported cases over time as presented in surveillance datasets.

Studies were included based on relevance to dengue epidemiology, climate associations, serotype distribution, and clinical features in Bangladesh and similar endemic settings. Given the narrative nature of the review, a formal systematic screening process was not applied.

The primary focus was to explore how climate change may have contributed to the 2023 dengue outbreak in Bangladesh, focusing on climatic, epidemiological, and serotype‐related factors. A structured narrative review was used along with an ecological analysis of secondary data to examine dengue trends in relation to climatic variables in Bangladesh. This review did not follow a formal systematic review methodology, and no PRISMA flow diagram or structured screening process was applied. The synthesis is descriptive and intended to provide contextual insights.

Search Strategy

A structured literature search was conducted using electronic databases including PubMed, Google Scholar, and relevant institutional reports. The search included studies published between January 2013 and January 2023 using keywords such as “dengue,” “Bangladesh,” “climate change,” “temperature,” “rainfall,” “humidity,” and “serotype variation.” Relevant references from identified articles were also reviewed to ensure completeness.

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Studies were included if they: reported dengue trends, serotype data, or climatic influences in Bangladesh or South Asia; were published in English; and provided primary or secondary data from 2013 to 2023. Studies were excluded if they were opinion pieces or lacked relevant data.

Data Sources

National dengue case data were obtained from the Directorate General of Health Services (DGHS), and climate data (temperature, rainfall, humidity) were collected from the NASA climate databases for the period 2019–2023. Unless otherwise specified, reported dengue case counts refer to national surveillance data from DGHS. Data specific to Dhaka City or hospital‐based admissions are also explicitly indicated in particular areas.

Data Extraction and Analysis

Data were manually extracted into a structured table summarizing: year and location of study, dengue serotype(s) identified; key climatic variables studied; major findings on dengue‐climate relationships. Because the included studies varied in design and methodology, findings from the literature review were synthesized descriptively rather than through formal meta‐analysis. For the ecological analysis, monthly aggregated dengue case data from DGHS and corresponding temperature data from NASA were analyzed to explore temporal patterns and associations between dengue cases and temperatures. The unit of analysis was monthly observations across the period 2019–2023.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted in accordance with established reporting standards, including the SAMPL (Statistical Analyses and Methods in the Published Literature) guidelines and recommendations for transparent reporting of clinical research statistics [6, 7, 8]. Monthly reported dengue case data were summarized using descriptive statistics. Associations between dengue incidence and temperature variables were assessed using the Pearson correlation coefficient (r). Statistical analyses followed SAMPL reporting guidelines.

The primary analyses were exploratory in nature, given the ecological design and use of aggregated secondary data. No subgroup analyses were performed. Results are therefore interpreted as hypothesis‐generating rather than confirmatory. Statistical significance was assessed using two‐sided tests, with a priori significance level set at α = 0.05. Both effect sizes (correlation coefficients) and corresponding p values are reported to provide information on the magnitude and statistical significance of associations. p values are presented following standard reporting conventions. Statistical analyses were performed using R statistical software (R‐4.3.2). Given the ecological nature of the data, findings are interpreted as associations at the population level and do not imply causality at the individual level.

Quality and Limitations

Each included source was assessed for general credibility, relevance, and consistency of reported findings. However, a formal risk‐of‐bias or quality assessment using standardized tools was not performed, as this study was conducted as a narrative review. Also, variability in surveillance systems and potential underreporting and inconsistencies in dengue case reporting may also affect the reliability of the analyzed data.

Outcome Measures

The primary outcome was the trend of dengue incidence in relation to climatic variables (temperature, rainfall, humidity) during 2019–2023.

Assessment of Bias and Limitations

Only English‐language studies were included, possibly excluding relevant local reports. Factors such as urbanization, population density, and vector control interventions were not adjusted for.

Current Dengue Condition in Bangladesh

The dengue outbreak in Bangladesh has reached alarming levels in 2023, with a substantial increase in reported cases and fatalities compared to previous years. As of November 11, 2023, a total of 290,084 dengue cases have been reported in Bangladesh. Dhaka City contributed to 103,227 cases (36%), while areas outside Dhaka accounted for 186,857 cases (64%). The spatial burden of dengue in 2023, with a substantial proportion of cases concentrated in Dhaka City. However, the majority (64%) occurred outside Dhaka, indicating a broader geographic spread of the outbreak (Supporting Information S1: Figure S1) [9].

From January 1 to November 11, 2023, the total number of reported deaths due to dengue reached 1466, resulting in a case fatality rate (CFR) of 0.51% [9]. This shows an increase in reported fatalities in 2023 compared with previous years, based on DGHS surveillance data. Dhaka City, the capital of Bangladesh, has experienced a notable dengue burden, with available hospital‐based surveillance data showing seven outbreaks resulting in over 5000 hospitalized cases between the years 2000 and 2022 [10]. According to the latest report from the Directorate General of Health Services (DGHS) on November 12, 2023, the total number of reported dengue‐related hospital admissions at the national level has escalated to 291,832, with 1476 reported deaths at the national level. Among these deaths, females accounted for 1.3 times more deaths (840 cases) than males (636 cases), although more males were infected, which is 1.5 times higher than females [9] (Supporting Information S1: Figure S1).

As the dengue outbreak continues to escalate in Bangladesh, particularly during the second half of 2023, it becomes increasingly crucial to examine the potential role of climate change in exacerbating the spread of this mosquito‐borne disease. To further explore potential drivers of these epidemiological patterns, the association between climate variables and dengue incidence trend was examined.

Serotyping Variation and Seasonal Variability of Dengue Incidence in Bangladesh

Dengue virus serotype distribution in Bangladesh has shown temporal variation, with multiple circulating strains contributing to outbreak patterns. Three genotypes (I, II, III) of DEN‐3 and the cosmopolitan genotype of DEN‐2 are currently circulating in Bangladesh. The emergence of the DENV‐4 serotype after more than 20 years raises concerns about the potential risk of secondary infections.

Evidence suggests that secondary disease is strongly associated with DHF and with more severe grades of DHF. Moreover, the DENV‐2 and DENV‐3 were twice as likely to result in DHF as DENV‐4. In Bangladesh, DENV1, DENV2, and DENV3 were widespread, with DENV3 being responsible for the majority of cases [3, 11, 12].

Despite these evidences, comprehensive national‐level data on serotype distribution remain limited. So, the variability in serotyping and its linkage to seasonal patterns of dengue incidence in Bangladesh calls for further investigation and understanding. Current national surveillance data on serotype distribution remain limited, and available evidence is primarily derived from selected hospital‐based or regional studies. Real‐time genomic and serotype mapping across regions is not consistently available [13]. So, there is a critical need for enhanced genomic surveillance systems integrated with seasonal climate models to forecast dengue severity and guide public health preparedness and immunization strategies.

Association Between Recent Climate Change and Dengue Incidence

Recent analysis indicates that July 2023 is likely becoming the hottest month on record [14], reflecting broader trends in rising global temperatures and extreme weather events. Climate scientist Dr. Joyce Kimutai warns of intensifying heatwaves, floods, and droughts wreaking havoc worldwide [14]. While climate conditions do not directly cause dengue, they may influence Aedes aegypti mosquito vector's development and survival.

Factors such as ambient temperatures, rainfall, and relative humidity are associated with dengue transmission intensity. Studies show an increasing trend in dengue cases since 2013, with a peak in 2023. Shorter sunshine durations favor dengue transmission due to increased mosquito activity in darker environments [15].

Dengue surveillance data over time have shown a seasonal pattern in Bangladesh, with higher cases reported generally during the monsoon season (June–September) and post‐monsoon season (October–November), often peaking around September. The highest number of cases was reported in 2017, with over 79,000 cases [9]. The number of cases has declined since then, but there has been an increase in cases to 92,288. However, recent data suggest a shift in this pattern. In 2023, case numbers began to rise from July and remained elevated through December, with a peak in December (approximately 92,288 cases reported at the national level), compared with earlier peaks observed in August (2019) and October (2022) (Supporting Information S1: Figure S2).

Comparison of average monthly temperatures with dengue case numbers in Dhaka from 2019 to 2023 is presented in Figure 1. Year‐wise analyses (Figures 2 and 3) did not demonstrate consistent correlations between monthly temperature and dengue cases. However, when data from all 5 years were aggregated, a weak but statistically significant positive correlation was observed between monthly reported dengue cases and minimum temperature (p = 0.04), while no significant association was found with maximum temperature (Figure 4).

Seasonal peaks have varied across recent outbreaks. In the 2019 outbreak, the peak incidence occurred in August, whereas in 2022 it shifted to October. In contrast, 2023 showed a prolonged pattern, with case numbers rising from July and remaining elevated through the later months of the year (Figure 1) [1, 5, 16].

In 2023, Dhaka City experienced a substantial increase in dengue cases from June to December despite a slight decrease in average temperature compared with previous years (2019–2022) (Figure 1). Reported fatalities also increased in 2023 compared with the past 5 years, based on DGHS surveillance data [17, 18].

Despite temperature fluctuations, the relationship between temperature and dengue incidence appears inconsistent and inconclusive based on the available data.

While this review observes concurrent trends between climate variables (e.g., temperature) and dengue case trends, these findings are associative and exploratory. Several potential confounders, including rapid urbanization, changes in healthcare access, water storage practices, vector control program coverage, and public behavior, may influence dengue transmission independently of climate variables. These factors were not adjusted for in this review, and their interaction with climatic drivers warrants further investigation.

In addition, the absence of detailed entomological data such as vector density, breeding site distribution, and information on non‐climatic drivers (e.g., urbanization, water storage behaviors, and insecticide resistance) constrains the interpretation of climate‐dengue relationships. These gaps hinder the accuracy of climate–disease correlation models and highlight the need for more comprehensive, multi‐factorial analyses. In addition to climatic influences, virological factors such as circulating dengue serotypes may also contribute to observed transmission patterns.

Atypical Features of Dengue in the Current Situation

In 2023, an increase in dengue cases with atypical features was observed among children admitted to hospital. Dengue Hemorrhagic Fever (DHF) is more common among patients with reinfection, indicating viral heterogeneity's impact on the immunological response [19].

Dengue virus interaction with host cells causes the release of various cytokines and stimulates immunologic mechanisms, leading to vascular endothelial changes, mononuclear cell infiltration, and perivascular edema. This mechanism contributes to atypical hemorrhagic features from capillary leak syndrome.

Reports from selected hospital‐based studies indicate variation in clinical presentation, including changes in the frequency of characteristic skin rash, although these findings are not based on nationally representative data [14, 16, 20]. Factors that exacerbate the severity of dengue viral infection include CTLA‐4 (cytotoxic T lymphocyte–associated protein 4), MBL‐2 (mannose binding lectin 2), TGF‐β (transforming growth factor β), and TNF‐α (tumor necrosis factor α). These factors can be categorized into two main groups: viral factors, such as viral serotype, and host factors, such as the host immune response [11].

Discussion

The findings of this study demonstrate a substantial increase in reported dengue cases in Bangladesh in 2023, with a shift in seasonal patterns extending beyond the typical monsoon peak. Although a weak but statistically significant association was observed between dengue cases and minimum temperature in aggregated analysis, this relationship was not consistent across individual years. These findings suggest that the relationship between climatic variables and dengue transmission is complex and not solely explained by temperature variation. The observed patterns indicate that dengue incidence in 2023 did not follow a consistent temperature‐dependent trend, highlighting the influence of multiple interacting factors.

The observed association between temperature and dengue incidence is consistent with existing literature demonstrating that climatic factors such as temperature, rainfall, and humidity are associated with vector dynamics, including development, survival, and behavior, as well as viral transmission potential. These climatic variables also indirectly shape environmental conditions that may influence the proliferation of vector populations [21].

Previous studies from Southeast Asia have shown that increased temperatures can accelerate mosquito breeding cycles and shorten viral incubation periods, thereby enhancing transmission potential [21, 22, 23, 24]. However, findings across studies are not uniform; some ecological analyses report statistically significant associations of varying magnitude, while others demonstrate variability depending on geographic context, urbanization, and vector control practices [25, 26]. In addition, the observed variation in serotype distribution and the presence of atypical clinical features are broadly consistent with prior studies, although robust national‐level data in Bangladesh remain limited.

This study has several limitations. First, the ecological design restricts the ability to draw causal inferences and introduces the risk of misinterpretation due to aggregated data. Second, the analysis relied on secondary surveillance data from DGHS and climate datasets, which may be subject to reporting bias and underreporting. Third, key confounding factors including rapid urbanization, population mobility, healthcare access, water storage practices, and vector control interventions were not adjusted for in the analysis. These factors may influence dengue transmission independently of climatic variables. Fourth, the absence of detailed entomological data, such as vector density and breeding site distribution, and limited availability of real‐time serotype and genomic surveillance constrain the interpretation of observed trends. Finally, findings related to atypical clinical features are based on selected hospital‐based studies and are not nationally representative. The integration of narrative review and ecological analysis may also introduce methodological heterogeneity, limiting the ability to draw definitive conclusions.

Challenge to Dengue Outbreak

Developing an effective tetravalent dengue vaccine that provides protection against all serotypes is crucial, as secondary heterotypic infections can lead to severe outcomes like DHF and dengue shock syndrome. Vigilance and proactive measures are essential to address the risk of new cases and potential spread to new areas [19]. Temperature significantly influences dengue incidence patterns, with studies indicating a direct correlation between higher temperatures and increased case counts. In Singapore [27], temperatures below 27.8°C coincide with heightened DF incidence. While rainfall generally amplifies dengue transmission, some studies note a maximum incidence trend with zero weekly precipitation, though the highest relative risk occurs with over 150 mm [28, 29]. Elevated temperatures foster increased mosquito reproduction and accelerate viral incubation. Consequently, global warming may exacerbate dengue and other mosquito‐borne diseases by facilitating more favorable conditions for vector proliferation and virus transmission.

Recommendation

To effectively curb the rising burden of dengue in Bangladesh, a comprehensive strategy must be implemented. Strengthening community awareness and vector control programs is critical, particularly through sustained behavior change communication targeting both urban and rural populations.

There is a pressing need to improve diagnostic and surveillance infrastructure, especially in rural and resource‐limited settings. Ensuring the availability of RT‐PCR, NS1 antigen testing, and serological tools at primary and secondary care levels would allow for early and accurate diagnosis of dengue. The national data reporting system managed by DGHS should be upgraded to integrate diagnostic technologies and reduce underreporting. Continuous virological and entomological surveillance should be prioritized to detect serotype shifts and assess insecticide resistance. With reports of resistance in Bangladesh and elsewhere, evaluating the effectiveness of insecticides at recommended dosages is crucial. Additionally, novel biological interventions, such as the Wolbachia‐based approach to suppress dengue virus transmission, should be piloted and studied in Bangladesh as part of long‐term sustainable vector control [30]. Urban areas should focus on eliminating mosquito breeding grounds in construction zones, rooftops, and water storage containers, while rural settings can benefit from community‐led mosquito elimination initiatives, as successfully demonstrated in countries like Mexico, India, and Cuba [30]. Community engagement in surveillance and vector control must be institutionalized through training and resource allocation to local health workers and volunteers [30].

Given that a dengue vaccine (Dengvaxia) has been approved in several countries but is not yet introduced in Bangladesh, efforts must be made to assess its feasibility. Pre‐deployment activities should include conducting seroprevalence studies, updating national immunization policy in consultation with NITAG, and mapping high‐risk populations. Special attention must be paid to children aged 9–16 years with a history of prior infection, the group for whom the vaccine is currently approved [31]. Furthermore, research into the development and regulatory approval of new‐generation tetravalent vaccines should be supported.

Finally, integrating climate adaptation into public health preparedness is vital. Bangladesh should develop a national dengue climate‐resilience plan in collaboration with meteorological agencies, urban planners, and health authorities. This includes mapping climate‐vulnerable zones, planning vector control around seasonal weather changes, and reinforcing health service delivery in flood‐prone or ecologically fragile areas. Overall, success depends on coordinated action across health, environment, and education sectors to create a responsive and adaptive dengue prevention system.

Conclusion

As climate change, one of several contributing factors, continues to impact the environment and weather patterns, addressing the link between climate change and dengue transmission becomes crucial in implementing effective measures to control and mitigate the spread of the disease. Moreover, there is a pressing need to deepen the scientific understanding of how climate‐induced vector and virus changes influence transmission cycles. By systematically addressing these evidence gaps, Bangladesh can better anticipate future outbreaks and develop context‐specific policies to effectively combat dengue.

Christel Baier, Calvin Chau, Volodymyr Drobitko, Simon Jantsch, Sascha Klüppelholz

Abstract
Multi-objective probabilistic model checking is a powerful technique for verifying stochastic systems against multiple (potentially conflicting) properties. To enhance the trustworthiness and explainability of model checking tools, we present independently checkable certificates and witnesses for multi-objective ω \omega -regular queries in Markov decision processes. For the certification, we extend and improve existing certificates for the decomposition of maximal end components and reachability properties. We then derive mixed-integer linear programs (MILPs) for finding minimal witnessing subsystems . For the special case of Markov chains and LTL properties, we use unambiguous Büchi automata to find witnesses, resulting in an algorithm that requires single-exponential space. Existing approaches based on deterministic automata require doubly-exponential space in the worst case. Finally, we consider the practical computation of our certificates and witnesses and provide an implementation of the developed techniques, along with an experimental evaluation, demonstrating the efficacy of our techniques.

Introduction

Probabilistic model checking (PMC) is a well-established approach for analysing and formally verifying stochastic systems, e.g. randomized algorithms or security systems
[
kwiatkowsa_prism_2012
,
hartmanns_quantitative_2019
]
.
Model checkers, like
Prism

[
kwiatkowska_prism_2011-1
]
or
Storm

[
hensel_probabilistic_2022
]
, receive a probabilistic model of the system and formal specification and output whether the specification is satisfied.
Unsurprisingly, as with any complex software, model checkers have been observed to contain bugs
[
wimmer_demand_2008
]
.
In the context of verifying (safety-critical) systems, this raises the question of how to ensure that model checking results can be trusted.
An approach for mitigating this issue are
certifying algorithms

[
mcconnell_certifying_2011
]
which also return a
certificate
accompanying the result. This can be used to independently verify the correctness of the result.

Another challenge for PMC is the
explication
of verification results.
When a system fails to meet the specification, it is desirable to obtain diagnostic information that allows the user to understand the behavior and debug the system. Indeed, counterexample traces are a crucial feature of classical model checking techniques in practice.
A canonical notion of explication does not exist for PMC, e.g. sets of paths
[
han_counterexample_2009-1
,
aljazzar_generation_2009-1
]
or subsystems
[
jansen_hierarchical_2011
,
wimmer_minimal_2012
,
jansen_counterexamples_2015
,
funke_farkas_2020
]
have been considered. Witnessing subsystems are parts of the system that on their own violate (or satisfy) the specification and can therefore serve as useful explications.

This work addresses the
certification
and
computation of witnesses
for
multi-objective
\omega
-regular queries
in Markov decision processes (MDPs)
[
puterman_markov_1994
]
. MDPs are the standard model for systems with stochastic and nondeterministic behavior. Further, multi-objective
\omega
-regular queries are expressive formalisms, enabling the specification of multiple (possibly conflicting) requirements
[
etessami_multi-objective_2008
]
. For instance, for a server system one might specify that “every request is served with probability greater than
0.9
” while “the probability of failure is less than
0.01
”
[
etessami_multi-objective_2008
,
forejt_quantitative_2011
]
.

To provide certificates, we revisit the steps of the verification procedure for these queries (e.g.
[
forejt_quantitative_2011
]
) and make them certifying. More precisely, we devise certificates for the
classification
of
end components
based on the properties they satisfy. Further, we improve on the certification of the
decomposition into maximal end components
(MECs)
[
de_alfaro_formal_1997
]
from
[
jantsch_certificates_2022
]
, using recent ideas from
[
chatterjee_fixed_2025
]
, and provide a first implementation thereof. The MEC decomposition is a common preprocessing step in PMC, and its certification is thus interesting on its own. In combination with previous work on multi-objective reachability queries
[
baier_certificates_2024
]
, this allows us to devise
sound and complete
certificates for
\omega
-regular queries. Our certificates can then be used to
independently validate
that a query is indeed satisfied (or violated), thereby enhancing trust in the verification process.

As in the setting of reachability queries
[
funke_farkas_2020
,
jantsch_certificates_2022
,
baier_certificates_2024
]
, we show that our certificates
induce witnessing subsystems
. Since small witnesses highlight critical parts of the MDP and might be easier to understand, we present algorithms based on mixed-integer linear programs (MILPs) for computing
minimal witnessing subsystems
.

For the special case of discrete-time Markov chains (DTMCs) and LTL properties, we devise a novel algorithm for computing minimal witnesses based on unambiguous Büchi automata (UBAs).
Checking DTMCs against probabilistic LTL specifications is
PSPACE
-complete
[
baier_markov_2023-1
]
, implying that minimal witnesses can be computed in
PSPACE
by enumerating and checking all subsystems.
As this is impractical, previous approaches relied on computing deterministic Rabin automata (DRAs)
[
jansen_counterexamples_2015
]
, requiring
doubly-exponential
space.
Instead, we define a
single-exponential
MILP based on the product of the DTMC with a UBA for the LTL property, building on results for model checking DTMCs against UBAs
[
baier_markov_2023-1
]
, which is possible in
PTIME
.

Proofs are in the appendix and the implementation and data are given in
[
baier_2025_15680332
]
.

Related work.

Multi-objective queries

[

,

,

. Techniques for verifying multi-objective

,

]
, where
[

]
considers queries with rewards. The works of
[
forejt_pareto_2012
,
quatmann_verification_2023
]
consider the verification of multi-objective queries via value iteration. Queries with mean-payoff objectives have been studied in
[

Farkas certificates

[
funke_farkas_2020
,
jantsch_certificates_2022
]
certify
single
constraints on
reachability probabilities
in MDPs. They are solutions to linear inequalities, derived from known LP characterizations
[
puterman_markov_1994
]
, and can also be used to obtain
minimal witnessing subsystems
. Certificates for the MEC decomposition are presented in
[
jantsch_certificates_2022
]
, however without implementation.
The work of
[
baier_certificates_2024
]
generalizes Farkas certificates to the setting of
multi-objective reachability
.

Fixed-point certificates

[
chatterjee_fixed_2025
]
can be seen as certified bounds on the optimal reachability probabilities and expected rewards in MDPs, based on fixed-points of the
Bellman operator
for reachability probabilities and expected rewards (see
[
puterman_markov_1994
,
baier_principles_2008-1
]
) and newly developed certificates for qualitative reachability. An implementation for computing the certificates and a verified checker for validating them is also provided. Similarly,
[
Schäffeler_Abdulaziz_2023
]
provides a verified implementation of solution methods for expected rewards in MDPs.

Martingale-based certificates
, e.g.
[
chakarov_probabilistic_2013-1
,
takisaka_ranking_2021
,
henzinger_supermartingale_2025-1
,
abate_quantitative_2025-1
]
, are important for the control and verification of stochastic systems with infinite state space, e.g. arising from probabilistic programs. The certificates from
[

]
combine Streett supermartingales with stochastic invariants and are sound and complete (i.a. for finite systems). However, multiple objectives are not supported and their implementation cannot be easily reused. The certificates from
[
henzinger_supermartingale_2025-1
]
are sound, but incomplete, and multiple objectives are not considered.

Explications
for PMC have been considered in the form of sets of paths
[
han_counterexample_2009-1
,
aljazzar_generation_2009-1
]
, fault-trees
[
kuntz_probabilistic_2011
]
and witnessing subsystems
[
jansen_hierarchical_2011
,
wimmer_minimal_2012
,
jansen_counterexamples_2015
,
funke_farkas_2020
]
.

3
Overview

We provide an overview of our approach to certificates and witnesses for multi-objective
\omega
-regular queries. First, we revisit the procedure from
[
forejt_quantitative_2011
]
for
checking
whether a query is satisfied.
Let
\mathcal{N}
be an MDP and
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{k}
be
\omega
-regular properties recognized by DRAs
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{k}
. The
complement
properties are given by
\bar{\rho}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\rho}_{k}
. Note that
\bar{\rho}_{i}
is recognized by
\mathcal{A}_{i}
when interpreting it as a Streett automaton. Let
\Psi_{\mathcal{N}}=\exists\mathfrak{G}\centerdot\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}_{\triangleright\lambda_{i}}(\rho_{i})
and
\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}=\forall\mathfrak{G}\centerdot\bigvee_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}_{\triangleright\lambda_{i}}(\bar{\rho}_{i})
.

Step 1
: The product MDP
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{N}\times\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{A}_{k}
is constructed to reason about the probabilities of satisfying the properties. The acceptance conditions are lifted to Rabin (resp. Streett) properties
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{k}
, resulting in the corresponding
Rabin
query
\Psi=\exists\mathfrak{G}\centerdot\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}_{\triangleright\lambda_{i}}(\varphi_{i})
(resp. Streett query
\Phi=\forall\mathfrak{G}\centerdot\bigvee_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}_{\triangleright\lambda_{i}}(\bar{\varphi}_{i})
). Note that
\mathcal{N}\models\Psi_{\mathcal{N}}
iff
\mathcal{M}\models\Psi
(resp.
\mathcal{N}\models\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}
iff
\mathcal{M}\models\Phi
), i.e. the query is satisfied in
\mathcal{N}
iff the corresponding query is satisfied in the product MDP. We denote the
Rabin
pairs of property
\varphi_{i}
by
P_{i}
.

: Now the satisfying MECs of the product MDP
\mathcal{M}
are identified. For every MEC
\mathcal{E}\in\mathsf{MEC}(\mathcal{M})
and

, it is checked if there is an EC
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
that satisfies all

properties in

, i.e. for all
i\in I
there is a Rabin pair
(F,E)\in P_{i}
with

and

. For a fixed

, this can be done in time polynomial in
{\lvert{\mathcal{M}}\rvert}
and exponential in the number of properties
[

]
. All pairs

of such satisfying MECs

and

\mathcal{I}^{*}
.

: To analyse the probabilities of realizing the identified ECs, a

of
\mathcal{M}

\mathcal{M}
, we write

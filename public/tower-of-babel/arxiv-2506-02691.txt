Homological invariant properties under cleft extensions Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author.
Li Liang, Yajun Ma ∗, Gang Yang

Abstract
. We study the behavior of the Gorenstein weak global dimension and Gorenstein flat-cotorsion global dimension under a cleft extension of rings; we prove that under some mild conditons the finiteness of the Gorenstein weak global dimension and that of the Gorenstein flat-cotorsion global dimension are both invariant. Moreover, we compare the relative singularity categories with respect to flat-cotorsion modules under a cleft extension of rings. Some applications to θ \theta -extensions and Morita context rings are given.

1.
Introduction

As one of the most basic homological invariants attached to an associative ring, the weak global dimension defined in terms of the flat dimension is left-right symmetric. In Gorenstein homological algebra, there are corresponding definition and conclusion: The Gorenstein weak global dimension defined in terms of Gorenstein flat dimensions is also left-right symmetric (see
[
7
, Corollary 2.5]
). As a consequence, one gets a connection between the rings of finite Gorenstein weak global dimension and Ding-Chen rings; it was proved in
[
5
, Proposition 3.4]
that a coherent ring
R
is Ding-Chen if and only if
R
has finite Gorenstein weak global dimension. To push the Gorenstein flat dimension beyond the setting of coherent rings, Christensen, Estrada, Liang, Thompson, Wu and Yang
[
6
]
introduced the Gorenstein flat-cotorsion dimension, which refines the Gorenstein flat dimension and exhibits properties more aligned with expectations. The corresponding global dimension is termed the Gorenstein flat-cotorsion global dimension, whose finiteness is explored in
[
5
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall some notations, the definitions of cleft extensions and cleft coextensions of abelian categories, together with their basic homological properties required in proofs. In Sections 3 and 4, we study the Gorenstein weak global dimension and Gorenstein flat-cotorsion global dimension under a cleft extension of module categories.

Viewing the isomorphism
\mathsf{ei}(B)\cong B
as an identification and applying the exact functor

2.14 Definition.

A right exact functor
\mathsf{F}:B\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\rightarrow B\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
is called

if the following conditions hold:

(a)

\mathbb{L}_{i}\mathsf{F}^{j}(\mathsf{F}P)=0
for every projective left
B
-module
P
and all
i,j\geq 1.

(b)

There is an integer
n\geq 1
such that for every
p,q\geq 1
satisfying
p+q\geq n+1,

one has
\mathbb{L}_{p}\mathsf{F}^{q}=0.

A left perfect functor
\mathsf{F}:B\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\rightarrow B\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
is called
f-perfect
if
\mathrm{fd}_{B}\mathsf{F}(P)<\infty
for every projective left
B
-module
P
; this notion extends the one of perfect functors in the sense of
[
16
, Definition 3.1]
.

Recall that a
B
-bimodule
M
is called
nilpotent
if
M^{\otimes n}=0
for some integer
n\geq 1
.

2.15 Lemma.

Let
M
be a
B
-bimodule with
\mathrm{Tor}_{i}^{B}(M,M^{\otimes j})=0
for all
i,j\geq 1
.
If
M_{B}
has finite flat dimension, then
\mathrm{fd}(M^{\otimes j})_{B}\leq j(\mathrm{fd}M_{B}).

Proof.

Assume that
\mathrm{fd}M_{B}=k,
and take a flat resolution of
M_{B}

0\to F_{k}\to\cdots\to F_{1}\to F_{0}\to M\to 0.

Since
\mathrm{Tor}_{i}^{B}(M,M)=0
for all
i\geq 1
, it follows that the following sequence

0\to F_{k}\otimes_{B}M\to\cdots\to F_{1}\otimes_{B}M\to F_{0}\otimes_{B}M\to M\otimes_{B}M\to 0

is exact.
We observe that
\mathrm{fd}(F_{i}\otimes_{B}M)_{B}\leq\mathrm{fd}M_{B}
for all
0\leq i\leq k
.
Then
\mathrm{fd}(M\otimes_{B}M)_{B}\leq 2(\mathrm{fd}M_{B}).

Inductively, we have that

\mathrm{fd}(M^{\otimes j})_{B}\leq j(\mathrm{fd}M_{B}).

∎

2.16 Lemma.

Let
M
be a
B
-bimodule with

has finite flat dimension and

By Lemma

Since

(we may assume that

), it follows that

Hence we have
\mathrm{Tor}_{t}^{B}(M^{\otimes j},-)=0

, which yields that
\mathrm{Tor}_{p}^{B}(M^{\otimes q},-)=0
for all

. On the other hand, by
[
17
, Lemma 5.2]
one gets that
\mathrm{Tor}_{i}^{B}(M^{\otimes j},M)=0

. Thus the functor

∎

2.17 Proposition.

Let
M
be a
B
-bimodule. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(

)

M
is nilpotent,
\mathrm{fd}_{B}M<\infty

\mathrm{fd}M_{B}<\infty
, and
\mathrm{Tor}_{i}^{B}(M,M^{\otimes j})=0
for all

.

(

)

The functor

(

)

Proof.

(

)
\!\implies\!
(
ii
):
By Lemma

,
M\otimes_{B}-
is left perfect and nilpotent.
Since
\mathrm{fd}_{B}M<\infty
, it follows that
M\otimes_{B}-
maps projective modules to modules of finite flat dimension.
Hence the functor

(

)
\!\implies\!
(

):

Assume that

Then it is easy to check that

On the other hand, since

Moreover, according to the condition (a) of Definition

0\leq i\leq t-1
.
Since
\mathsf{e}(X^{\prime})
is
\mathsf{F}
-projective, we have
\mathbb{L}_{i}\mathsf{F}(\mathsf{e}(X^{\prime}))=0
for all
i\geq 1
. Thus we get the following exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathsf{F}(Q_{t})\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\mathsf{F}(Q_{1})\rightarrow\mathsf{F}(Q_{0})\rightarrow\mathsf{F}\mathsf{e}(X^{\prime})\rightarrow 0.

Note that
\mathsf{er}\simeq\mathsf{F}^{\prime}\oplus\mathrm{Id}
and
\sf r
is exact by assumption. It follows that
\mathsf{F}^{\prime}
is exact.
Then
\mathsf{F}
preserves projective
B
-modules as
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{F}^{\prime})
is an adjoint pair.
Since
\mathsf{F}
preserves filtered colimits,
\mathsf{F}
preserves flat
B
-modules. Thus
{\sf F}(Q_{t})
is flat and
{\sf F}(Q_{i})
is projective for
0\leq i\leq t-1
.
So
\mathrm{fd}_{B}\mathsf{F}\mathsf{e}(X^{\prime})<\infty
.
Using an inductive argument, we get that
\mathrm{fd}_{B}\mathsf{F}^{j}\mathsf{e}(X^{\prime})<\infty
for all
j\geq 1
.
We mention that
\mathsf{F}^{j}\mathsf{e}(X^{\prime})
is
\sf F
-projective by Lemma
2.20
(b).
It follows from Lemma
2.20
(c) that
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{A}^{i}(\mathsf{l}\mathsf{F}^{j}\mathsf{e}(X^{\prime}),V)\cong\mbox{\rm Ext}_{B}^{i}(\mathsf{F}^{j}\mathsf{e}(X^{\prime}),\mathsf{e}(V))=0

for each cotorsion left
A
-module
V
and every
i\geq\mathrm{fd}_{B}\mathsf{F}^{j}\mathsf{e}(X^{\prime})
, where the equality holds as
\mathsf{e}(V)
is cotorsion by the assumption. This implies that
\mathrm{fd}_{A}\mathsf{l}\mathsf{F}^{j}\mathsf{e}(X^{\prime})<\infty.

We mention that
\mathsf{F}^{s}=0
for some positive integer by assumption.
Then we consider the following exact sequences in
A\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
:

\begin{cases}0\rightarrow\mathsf{G}(X^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathsf{le}(X^{\prime})\rightarrow X^{\prime}\rightarrow 0;\\
0\rightarrow\mathsf{G}^{2}(X^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathsf{l}\mathsf{F}\mathsf{e}(X^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathsf{G}(X^{\prime})\rightarrow 0;\\
\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\vdots\\
0\rightarrow\mathsf{G}^{s-1}(X^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathsf{l}\mathsf{F}^{s-2}\mathsf{e}(X^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathsf{G}^{s-2}(X^{\prime})\rightarrow 0;\\
0\rightarrow\mathsf{G}^{s}(X^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathsf{l}\mathsf{F}^{s-1}\mathsf{e}(X^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathsf{G}^{s-1}(X^{\prime})\rightarrow 0.\end{cases}

It follows from Lemma

\mathsf{G}^{s}=0
and so
\mathsf{G}^{s-1}(X^{\prime})\cong\mathsf{l}\mathsf{F}^{s-1}\mathsf{e}(X^{\prime})
.
Hence we get the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathsf{l}\mathsf{F}^{s-1}\mathsf{e}(X^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathsf{l}\mathsf{F}^{s-2}\mathsf{e}(X^{\prime})\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\mathsf{l}\mathsf{F}\mathsf{e}(X^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathsf{le}(X^{\prime})\rightarrow X^{\prime}\rightarrow 0.

We mention that
\mathsf{e}(X^{\prime})
is
\mathsf{F}
-projective and
\mathsf{e}
preserves cotorsion modules. It follows from Lemma
2.20
(c) that
\mathrm{fd}_{A}\mathsf{le}(X^{\prime})<\infty.

Thus one has
\mathrm{fd}_{A}X^{\prime}<\infty

\mathrm{fd}_{A}X<\infty.

∎

3.
Gorenstein weak global dimension under cleft extensions

In this section, we study the homological behavior of the Gorenstein weak global dimension under cleft extensions of module categories.

3.1.

Let
R
be a ring. An acyclic complex
T
of flat left
R
-modules is called
F-totally acyclic
if
E\otimes_{R}T
is acyclic for every injective right
R
-module
E
.
A left
R
-module
G
is called
Gorenstein flat
if there exists an
F
-totally acyclic complex
T
such that
G\cong\mbox{\rm Coker}(T_{1}\to T_{0})
. The Gorenstein flat dimension of a left
R
-module
M
,
\mathrm{Gfd}_{R}M
, is defined by declaring that
\mathrm{Gfd}_{R}M\leq n
if and only if there is an exact sequence
0\to G_{n}\to\cdots\to G_{0}\to M\to 0
of left
R
-modules with each
G_{i}
Gorenstein flat. We denote by
\mathrm{Gfd}N_{R}
the Gorenstein flat dimension of a right
R
-module
N
. We mention that the two values

\sup\{\mathrm{Gfd}_{R}M\ |\ M\ \text{is a left}\ R\text{-module}\}

and

\sup\{\mathrm{Gfd}N_{R}\ |\ N\ \text{is a right}\ R\text{-module}\}

are equal; see
[
7
, Corollary 1.4]
. The
Gorenstein weak global dimension
of
R
, denoted by
\mathrm{Gwgldim}(R)
, is defined as the value

\sup\{\mathrm{Gfd}_{R}M\ |\ M\ \text{is a left}\ R\text{-module}\}=\sup\{\mathrm{Gfd}N_{R}\ |\ N\ \text{is a right}\ R\text{-module}\}.

3.2.

Let
R
be a ring. We denote by
\mathrm{sfli}(R)
(resp.,
\mathrm{sfli}(R^{\circ})
) the supremum of the flat dimensions of injective left
R
-modules (resp., right
R
-modules), and denote by
\mathrm{FFD}(R)
(resp.,
\mathrm{FFD}(R^{\circ})
) the supremum of the flat dimensions of those left
R
-modules (resp., right
R
-modules) that have finite flat dimension.

By
[
9
, Theorem 2.8]
and
[
10
, Theorem 5.3]
, we have the next result.

3.3 Lemma.

The following statements are equivalent for a nonnegative integer
n
.

(
i
)

\mathrm{Gwgldim}(R)\leq n
.

(
ii
)

\mathrm{sfli}(R)\leq n
and
\mathrm{sfli}(R^{\circ})\leq n
.

(
iii
)

\mathrm{sfli}(R)=\mathrm{FFD}(R)=\mathrm{sfli}(R^{\circ})=\mathrm{FFD}(R^{\circ})\leq n
.

If these equivalent conditions are satisfied, then there are equations

\mathrm{Gwgldim}(R)=\mathrm{sfli}(R)=\mathrm{FFD}(R)=\mathrm{sfli}(R^{\circ})=\mathrm{FFD}(R^{\circ}).

3.4 Lemma.

Let
M
be a left
R
-module. If
\mathrm{id}_{R}M<\infty
,
then
\mathrm{fd}_{R}M\leq\mathrm{sfli}(R).

Proof.

If
\mathrm{id}_{R}M=0,
then the result holds by the definition of
\mathrm{sfli}(R).

We proceed by induction on
\mathrm{id}_{R}M.

If
\mathrm{id}_{R}M=n
, then there is an exact sequence

0\rightarrow M\rightarrow I\rightarrow V\rightarrow 0

with
I
injective and

\mathrm{id}_{R}V=n-1.

Then
\mbox{\rm fd}_{R}I\leq\mathrm{sfli}(R)
and
\mbox{\rm fd}_{R}V\leq\mathrm{sfli}(R)
by induction hypothesis, and so
\mathrm{fd}_{R}M\leq\mathrm{sfli}(R).

∎

3.5 Lemma.

Let
{\sf l}:B\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\to A\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
and
{\sf e}:A\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\to B\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
be two functors such that
(\sf l,\sf e)
is an adjoint pair. Then the following statements hold:

(a)

If
\sf l
and
\sf e
are exact, then
\sf e
preserves cotorsion modules.

(b)

Assume that
B
is right coherent. If
\sf e
is exact and
\sf l
commutes with products, then
\sf l
preserves flat-cotorsion modules.

Proof.

(a) Fix a cotorsion left
A
-module
C
and a flat left
B
-module
X.

Since
\sf l
and
\sf e
are exact, one gets an isomorphism

\mbox{\rm Ext}^{1}_{B}(X,\mathsf{e}(C))\cong\mbox{\rm Ext}^{1}_{A}(\mathsf{l}(X),C).

Since
\sf l
preserves projective modules and filtered colimits,
\sf l
preserves flat modules.
It follows that
\mbox{\rm Ext}^{1}_{A}(\mathsf{l}(X),C)=0
, which implies that
\mathsf{e}(C)
is a cotorsion left
B
-module.

(b) We know from
[
24
, Lemma 3.2.3]
that over right coherent ring
B
, a flat left
B
-module is cotorsion if and only if it is pure-injective.
So it suffices to prove that
\sf l
preserves flat and pure-injective modules.
Since
\sf e
is exact and
(\sf l,\sf e)
is an adjoint pair, it is easy to check that
\sf l
preserves flat modules.
According to the assumption that
\sf l
commutes with products, it follows from
[
23
, Corollary 4.3.8]
that
\sf l
preserves pure-injective modules.
Consequently,
\sf l
preserves flat-cotorsion modules.
∎

3.6 Lemma.

Let
(B\text{-}\mathsf{Mod},A\text{-}\mathsf{Mod},\mathsf{i},\mathsf{e},\mathsf{l})
be a cleft extension of module categories. Assume that the induced endofunctor
\mathsf{F}
on
B\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
is nilpotent with
s
an integer such that
\mathsf{F}^{s}=0
. Then the following statements hold:

(a)

If
\sf r
is exact, then there is an inequality
\mathrm{sfli}(B)\leq\mathrm{sfli}(A).

(b)

If
\mathsf{F}
is f-perfect and both
\mathsf{r}
and
\mathsf{l}
are exact,
then there is an inequality

\mathrm{sfli}(A)\leq\mathrm{sfli}(B)+s-1.

Proof.

Keep the notation in the diagram (
2.7.1
).

(a) We may assume that
\mathrm{sfli}(A)<\infty
. Let
V
be a cotorsion left
B
-module. Since
\sf r
is exact, we get the following isomorphism for any injective left
B
-module
I
and every
k\geq 1
:

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Ext}_{A}^{k}(\mathsf{r}(I),\mathsf{r}(V))

\displaystyle\cong\mbox{\rm Ext}_{B}^{k}(\mathsf{er}(I),V)

\displaystyle\cong\mbox{\rm Ext}_{B}^{k}(I,V)\oplus\mbox{\rm Ext}_{B}^{k}(\mathsf{F}^{\prime}(I),V),

where the second isomorphism holds as
\mathsf{er}\simeq\mathrm{Id}\oplus\mathsf{F}^{\prime}.

From Lemma
3.5
we know that
\mathsf{r}(V)
is a cotorsion left
A
-module.
Then we have
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{A}^{k}(\mathsf{r}(I),\mathsf{r}(V))=0
for all
k\geq\mathrm{sfli}(A)+1
as
\mathsf{r}(I)
is injective.
It implies that
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{B}^{k}(I,V)=0
for all
k\geq\mathrm{sfli}(A)+1,

which yields that
\mathrm{sfli}(B)\leq\mathrm{sfli}(A).

(b) We assume that
\mathrm{sfli}(B)<\infty
. By
[
16
, Lemma 2.6]
that every injective module in
A\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
is a direct summands of
\mathsf{r}(I)
for some injective left
B
-module
I.

Let
X
be a cotorsion left
A
-module. Since
\sf r
is exact, we have the following isomorphism

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Ext}_{A}^{k}(\mathsf{r}(I),\mathsf{r}\mathsf{F}^{\prime j}\mathsf{e}(X))

\displaystyle\cong\mbox{\rm Ext}_{B}^{k}(\mathsf{er}(I),\mathsf{F}^{\prime j}\mathsf{e}(X))

\displaystyle\cong\mbox{\rm Ext}_{B}^{k}(I,\mathsf{F}^{\prime j}\mathsf{e}(X))\oplus\mbox{\rm Ext}_{B}^{k}(\mathsf{F}^{\prime}(I),\mathsf{F}^{\prime j}\mathsf{e}(X))

for
j\geq 0
,
where the second isomorphism holds as
\mathsf{er}\simeq\mathrm{Id}\oplus\mathsf{F}^{\prime}.

Since
\sf F
is f-perfect, one has
\mathsf{F}\simeq M\otimes_{B}-
and
\mathsf{F}^{\prime}\simeq\mbox{\rm Hom}_{B}(M,-)
for some
B
-bimodule
M
with
\mathrm{fd}M_{B}<\infty
; see Remark
2.10
and Proposition
2.17
.
Thus
\mbox{\rm id}_{B}\mathsf{F}^{\prime}(I)=\mbox{\rm id}_{B}\mbox{\rm Hom}_{B}(M,I)<\infty
for any injective
B
-module
I
, and we know from Lemma
3.4
that
\mbox{\rm fd}_{B}I\leq\mathrm{sfli}(B)
and
\mbox{\rm fd}_{B}\mathsf{F}^{\prime}(I)\leq\mathrm{sfli}(B).

We mention that
\mathsf{el}\simeq\mathsf{F}\oplus\mathrm{Id}
and
\mathsf{er}\simeq\mathsf{F}^{\prime}\oplus\mathrm{Id}
.
Since
\sf l
and
\sf r
are exact by assumption, so are
\mathsf{F}
and
\mathsf{F}^{\prime}
.
Note that
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{F}^{\prime})
is an adjoint pair.
It follows from Lemma
3.5
that

\mathsf{F}^{\prime j}\mathsf{e}(X)
is cotorsion left
B
-module for
j\geq 0
.
Thus
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{B}^{k}(I,\mathsf{F}^{\prime j}\mathsf{e}(X))=0=\mbox{\rm Ext}_{B}^{k}(\mathsf{F}^{\prime}(I),\mathsf{F}^{\prime j}\mathsf{e}(X))
for all

, and so
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{A}^{k}(\mathsf{r}(I),\mathsf{r}\mathsf{F}^{\prime j}\mathsf{e}(X))=0

for all

Consider the following exact sequences:

\begin{cases}0\rightarrow X\rightarrow\mathsf{re}(X)\rightarrow\mathsf{G}^{\prime}(X)\rightarrow 0;\\
0\rightarrow\mathsf{G}^{\prime}(X)\rightarrow\mathsf{r}\mathsf{F}^{\prime}\mathsf{e}(X)\rightarrow\mathsf{G}^{\prime 2}(X)\rightarrow 0;\\
\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\vdots\\
0\rightarrow\mathsf{G}^{\prime s-2}(X)\rightarrow\mathsf{r}\mathsf{F}^{\prime s-2}\mathsf{e}(X)\rightarrow\mathsf{G}^{\prime s-1}(X)\rightarrow 0.\\
0\rightarrow\mathsf{G}^{\prime s-1}(X)\rightarrow\mathsf{r}\mathsf{F}^{\prime s-1}\mathsf{e}(X)\rightarrow\mathsf{G}^{\prime s}(X)\rightarrow 0.\\
\end{cases}

Note that
\mathsf{F}^{s}=0
.
It follows from
[

\mathsf{F}^{\prime s}=0.

Thus we have

2.6

\mathsf{G}^{\prime s-1}(X)\cong\mathsf{r}\mathsf{F}^{\prime s-1}\mathsf{e}(X)
.
Therefore there is an exact sequence of

It follows that

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Ext}_{A}^{k+s-1}(\mathsf{r}(I),X)\cong\mbox{\rm Ext}_{A}^{k}(\mathsf{r}(I),\mathsf{r}\mathsf{F}^{\prime s-1}\mathsf{e}(X))=0

for all

Consequently,
\mathrm{sfli}(A)\leq\mathrm{sfli}(B)+s-1.

∎

We mention that if
({B\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}},{A\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}},{\sf i}_{1},\mathsf{e}_{1},\mathsf{l}_{1})
is a cleft extension of module categories, then there exists a cleft extension
(\mathsf{Mod}\text{-}B,\mathsf{Mod}\text{-}A,\mathsf{i}_{2},\mathsf{e}_{2},\mathsf{l}_{2})
; see Remark
2.10
.
In the following, we call
(\mathsf{Mod}\text{-}B,\mathsf{Mod}\text{-}A,\mathsf{i}_{2},\mathsf{e}_{2},\mathsf{l}_{2})
the induced cleft extension by
({B\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}},{A\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}},{\sf i}_{1},\mathsf{e}_{1},\mathsf{l}_{1})

Let
\mathsf{F}_{1}
and
\mathsf{F}_{2}
be the induced endofunctors on

and
\mathsf{Mod}\text{-}B
respectively, and denote by
\mathsf{r}_{1}
the right adjoint of
\mathsf{e}_{1}
, and by
\mathsf{r}_{2}
the right adjoint of
\mathsf{e}_{2}
. We then give the main result in this section.

3.7 Theorem.

Let
(B\text{-}\mathsf{Mod},A\text{-}\mathsf{Mod},\mathsf{i}_{1},\mathsf{e}_{1},\mathsf{l}_{1})
be a cleft extension of module categories and
(\mathsf{Mod}\text{-}B,\mathsf{Mod}\text{-}A,\mathsf{i}_{2},\mathsf{e}_{2},\mathsf{l}_{2})
be the induced cleft extension. Assume that the induced endofunctor
\mathsf{F}_{1}
is f-perfect and nilpotent with

an integer such that
\mathsf{F}_{1}^{s}=0
. If
\mathsf{r}_{1}
and
\mathsf{r}_{2}
are exact,
then there are inequalities

\mathrm{Gwgldim}(B)\leq\mathrm{Gwgldim}(A)\leq\mathrm{Gwgldim}(B)+s-1,

In particular,
\mathrm{Gwgldim}(B)
is finite if and only if
\mathrm{Gwgldim}(A)
is finite.

Proof.

Since
\mathsf{r}_{1}
and
\mathsf{r}_{2}
are exact, so are
\mathsf{l}_{1}
and
\mathsf{l}_{2}
by Lemma
2.11
.
According to the assumption that
\mathsf{F}_{1}
is f-perfect and
\mathsf{F}_{1}^{s}=0
, it follows from Remark

and Proposition

that
\mathsf{F}_{2}
is f-perfect and

.
Then the inequalities in the statement follow from Lemmas
3.3
and

.
∎

4.
Gorenstein flat-cotorsion global dimension under cleft extensions

In this section, we study the homological behavior of the Gorenstein flat-cotorsion global dimension under cleft extensions of module categories.

4.1 Gorenstein flat-cotorsion module.

Let
R
be a ring.
A left
R
-module
M
is called
flat-cotorsion
if it is flat and cotorsion. A
totally
acyclic complex of flat-cotorsion modules
is an acyclic complex
T

of flat-cotorsion modules such that the complexes
\mbox{\rm Hom}(T,W)
is acyclic for every flat-cotorsion left
R
-module
W
. A left

R
-module
G
is called
Gorenstein flat-cotorsion
if there
is a totally acyclic complex
T
of flat-cotorsion left
R
-modules
with
G\cong\mbox{\rm Coker}(T_{1}\to T_{0})
; see
[
6
]
.

4.2 Gorenstein flat-cotorsion global dimension.

Recall that a complex
F
of left
R
-modules is called
semi-flat
if it consists of flat left
R
-modules and the functor
-\otimes_{R}F
preserves acyclicity. A complex
C
of left
R
-modules is called
semi-cotorsion
if it consists of cotorsion left
R
-modules and
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(F,C)
is acyclic for every acyclic semi-flat complex
F
. We recall that a complex
W
of left
R
-modules is referred to as
semi-flat-cotorsion
if it is semi-flat and semi-cotorsion, and a semi-flat-cotorsion complex that is isomorphic to
M
in the derived category is called a
semi-flat-cotorsion replacement
of
M
. Recall from
[
6
]
that the Gorenstein flat-cotorsion dimension of a left

R
-module
M
, denoted
\mathrm{Gfcd}_{R}M
, is defined as the least integer
n\geq 0
such that
there is a semi-flat-cotorsion replacement
W
of
M
with
\mathrm{C}_{n}(W)
Gorenstein flat-cotorsion. If no such integer exists, then the Gorenstein flat-cortosion
dimension of
M
is infinite. The
Gorenstein flat-cotorsion global dimension
of
R
, denoted by
\mathrm{Gfc}\text{-}\mathrm{gldim}(R)
, is defined as the value

\sup\{\mathrm{Gfcd}_{R}M\ |\ M\ \text{is a left}\ R\text{-module}\}.

It follows from
[
6
, Theorem 5.7]
and
[
5
, Corollary 3.5]
that there is an inequality

\mathrm{Gfc}\text{-}\mathrm{gldim}(R)\leq\mathrm{Gwgldim}(R),

and equality holds if
\mathrm{Gwgldim}(R)
is finite, or
R
is left or right coherent.

We denote by
\mathrm{silfc}(R)
the supremum of the injective dimensions of flat-cotorsion left
R
-modules. The next result can be found in
[
5
, Theorem 3.2]
.

4.3 Lemma.

The following statements are equivalent for an integer
n\geq 0
:

(a)

\mathrm{Gfc}\text{-}\mathrm{gldim}(R)\leq n
.

(b)

\mathrm{sfli}(R)\leq n
and
\mathrm{silfc}(R)\leq n.

(c)

\mathrm{sfli}(R)=\mathrm{silfc}(R)\leq n
.

(d)

\max\{\mathrm{sfli}(R),\mathrm{FFD}(R)\}\leq n.

If these conditions are satisfied, then

\mathrm{Gfc}\text{-}\mathrm{gldim}(R)=\mathrm{FFD}(R)=\mathrm{sfli}(R).

4.4 Lemma.

Let
(B\text{-}\mathsf{Mod},A\text{-}\mathsf{Mod},\mathsf{i},\mathsf{e},\mathsf{l})
be a cleft extension of module categories. Assume that the induced endofunctor
\mathsf{F}
on
B\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
is left perfect and nilpotent with
s
an integer such that
\mathsf{F}^{s}=0
. Then the following statements hold:

(a)

If
\mathsf{l}
is exact and
\sf e
preserves projective modules, then
\mathrm{silfc}(A)\leq\mathrm{silfc}(B)+s-1.

(b)

If
\mathsf{l}
preserves flat-cotorsion modules, then
\mathrm{silfc}(B)\leq\mathrm{silfc}(A)+n-1,
where
n\geq 1
is an integer such that
\mathbb{L}_{p}\mathsf{F}^{q}=0
for all
p,q\geq 1
with
p+q\geq n+1
.

Proof.

(a) Assume that
\mathrm{silfc}(B)<\infty.

For any flat-cotorsion left
A
-module
V
, we need to show that

\mathrm{id}_{A}V\leq\mathrm{silfc}(B)+s-1.

Let
X
be a left
A
-module.
Since
\sf l
is exact, we have the following isomorphism

\mbox{\rm Ext}_{A}^{k}(\mathsf{l}\mathsf{F}^{j}\mathsf{e}(X),V)\cong\mbox{\rm Ext}_{B}^{k}(\mathsf{F}^{j}\mathsf{e}(X),\mathsf{e}(V))

for all
j\geq 0
.
Note that
\sf e
preserves projectives. Then by Lemma
3.5
(a) one gets that
\mathsf{e}(V)
is a flat-cotorsion left
B
-module.
Consequently,
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{A}^{k}(\mathsf{l}\mathsf{F}^{j}\mathsf{e}(X),V)\cong\mbox{\rm Ext}_{B}^{k}(\mathsf{F}^{j}\mathsf{e}(X),\mathsf{e}(V))=0
for all
k\geq\mathrm{silfc}(B)+1
.
Consider the following exact sequences in
A\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
:

\begin{cases}0\rightarrow\mathsf{G}(X)\rightarrow\mathsf{le}(X)\rightarrow X\rightarrow 0;\\
0\rightarrow\mathsf{G}^{2}(X)\rightarrow\mathsf{l}\mathsf{F}\mathsf{e}(X)\rightarrow\mathsf{G}(X)\rightarrow 0;\\
\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\vdots\\
0\rightarrow\mathsf{G}^{s-1}(X)\rightarrow\mathsf{l}\mathsf{F}^{s-2}\mathsf{e}(X)\rightarrow\mathsf{G}^{s-2}(X)\rightarrow 0;\\
0\rightarrow\mathsf{G}^{s}(X)\rightarrow\mathsf{l}\mathsf{F}^{s-1}\mathsf{e}(X)\rightarrow\mathsf{G}^{s-1}(X)\rightarrow 0.\end{cases}

Since
\mathsf{F}^{s}=0,
it follows from Lemma
2.3
that
\mathsf{G}^{s}=0
and so
\mathsf{G}^{s-1}(X)\cong\mathsf{l}\mathsf{F}^{s-1}\mathsf{e}(X)
.
Hence we get the exact sequence in
A\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}

0\rightarrow\mathsf{l}\mathsf{F}^{s-1}\mathsf{e}(X)\rightarrow\mathsf{l}\mathsf{F}^{s-2}\mathsf{e}(X)\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\mathsf{l}\mathsf{F}\mathsf{e}(X)\rightarrow\mathsf{le}(X)\rightarrow X\rightarrow 0.

By the dimension shifting, we have
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{A}^{k+s-1}(X,V)\cong\mbox{\rm Ext}_{A}^{k}(\mathsf{l}\mathsf{F}^{s-1}\mathsf{e}(X),V)=0
for all
k\geq\mathrm{silfc}(B)+1
.
So
\mathrm{id}_{A}V\leq\mathrm{silfc}(B)+s-1.

Therefore
\mathrm{silfc}(A)\leq\mathrm{silfc}(B)+s-1.

(b) Assume that
\mathrm{silfc}(A)<\infty.
Let
W
be a flat-cotorsion left
B
-module. We show that
\mathrm{id}_{B}W\leq\mathrm{silfc}(A)+n-1.

Take any left

is left perfect by the assumption. This yields that

\sf F
-projective.
It follows from Lemma

\displaystyle\cong\mbox{\rm Ext}_{B}^{k}(Y^{\prime},\mathsf{el}(W))

\displaystyle\cong\mbox{\rm Ext}_{B}^{k}(Y^{\prime},W)\oplus\mbox{\rm Ext}_{B}^{k}(Y^{\prime},\mathsf{F}(W)),

where the second isomorphism holds as

Since

is a flat-cotorsion

This implies that

6.1.

We let
\mathsf{Ch}(R)
denote the category of complexes of left
R
-modules (or
R
-complexes). Recall from
[
11
]
that an
R
-complex
X
is called
dg-flat
if each component
X^{n}
is flat and
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(X,C)
is exact whenever
C
is an acyclic
R
-complex with each cycles
\mathrm{Z}^{n}C
cotorsion. An
R
-complex
X
is called
dg-cotorsion
if each component
X^{n}
is cotorsion and
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(F,X)
is exact whenever
F
is an acyclic
R
-complex with each cycles
\mathrm{Z}^{n}F
flat. We denote by
\mathsf{dg}\widetilde{\mathsf{F}}
(resp.,
\mathsf{dg}\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}
,
\mathsf{Acy}
) the class of dg-flat (resp., dg-cotorsion, acyclic)
R
-complexes. We mention that every complex of cotorsion
R
-modules is dg-cotorsion; see
[
1
, Theorem 1.3]
.

The next result can be found in
[
11
, Corollary 5.1]
.

6.2 Lemma.

There is a flat model structure
(\mathsf{dg}\widetilde{\mathsf{F}},\mathsf{Acy},\mathsf{dg}\widetilde{\mathsf{C}})
on
\mathsf{Ch}(R)
; its homotopy category is
\mathbf{D}(R).

The proof of the following result is routine, which can be found in
[
20
, Lemma 4.5]
.

6.3 Lemma.

Let
X
be a bounded
R
-complex with each component
X^{n}
cotorsion and
\mathrm{fd}_{R}X^{n}<\infty
.
Then
X
is quasi-isomorphic to a bounded complex in
\mathsf{FlatCot}(R)
.

6.4 Proposition.

Let
A
and
B
be right coherent rings and
(B\text{-}\mathsf{Mod},A\text{-}\mathsf{Mod},\mathsf{i},\mathsf{e},\mathsf{l})
a cleft extension of module categories. Assume that the following conditions are satisfied:

(a)

\sf l
is exact and preserves products;

(b)

\mathbb{L}_{i}\mathsf{q}=0
for
i
large enough, and
\sf q
preserves products;

(c)

\sf i
preserves cotorsion modules and
\mathrm{fd}_{A}\mathsf{i}(V)<\infty
for any flat-cotorsion
B
-module
V
;

(d)

\mathrm{fd}_{B}\mathsf{e}(W)<\infty
for any flat-cotorsion
A
-module
W
.

Then there is a diagram

\textstyle{\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{FlatCot}}(B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\overline{\sf Ri}}
\textstyle{\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{FlatCot}}(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\overline{\mathsf{Lq}}}
\scriptstyle{\overline{\sf Re}}
\textstyle{\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{FlatCot}}(B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\overline{\mathsf{Ll}}}

of relative singularity categories and triangle functors such that
(\overline{\sf Lq},\overline{\sf Ri})
and
(\sf\overline{Ll},\overline{Re})
are adjoint pairs,
\overline{\sf Re}\circ\overline{\sf Ri}\simeq\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{FlatCot}}(B)}
and
\overline{\sf Lq}\circ\overline{\sf Ll}\simeq\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{FlatCot}}(B)}
.

Proof.

We mention that a cleft extension of module categories

\textstyle{B\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sf i}
\textstyle{A\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sf q}
\scriptstyle{\sf e}
\textstyle{B\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sf l}

induces a cleft extension of complex categories

\textstyle{\mathsf{Ch}(B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sf i}
\textstyle{\mathsf{Ch}(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sf q}
\scriptstyle{\sf e}
\textstyle{\mathsf{Ch}(B).\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sf l}

By Lemma
6.2
, there exist flat model structures on
\mathsf{Ch}(A)
and
\mathsf{Ch}(B).
We claim that
(\sf l,\sf e)
and
(\sf q,\sf i)
are Quillen adjunctions.
Since
\sf l
is exact and
\sf i
preserves cotorsion modules, it follows from Lemma
3.5
(a) that
\sf i
and
\sf e
preserve dg-cotorsion complexes. We mention that
\sf i
and
\sf e
are exact. Then
\sf i
and
\sf e
preserve fibrations and trivial fibrations. It follows from
[
15
, Lemma 1.3.4]
that
(\sf l,\sf e)
and
(\sf q,\sf i)
are Quillen adjunctions.
By
[
15
, Lemma 1.3.10]
we get the following diagram of derived categories

\textstyle{\mathbf{D}(B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sf Ri}
\textstyle{\mathbf{D}(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sf Lq}
\scriptstyle{\sf Re}
\textstyle{\mathbf{D}(B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sf Ll}

such that
(\sf Ll,Re)
and
(\sf Lq,Ri)
are adjoint pairs of triangle functors.
We claim that the above functors restricts to

\textstyle{\mathbf{D}^{b}(B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sf Ri}
\textstyle{\mathbf{D}^{b}(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sf Lq}
\scriptstyle{\sf Re}
\textstyle{\mathbf{D}^{b}(B).\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

For
X\in\mathbf{D}^{b}(A)
, by Lemma
6.2
, there exists an exact sequence of
A
-complexes

0\rightarrow C\rightarrow Q\rightarrow X\rightarrow 0

such that
Q
is dg-flat and

is acyclic dg-cotorsion.
In particular,
Q
is quasi-isomorphic to

.
We may write

X=\cdots\rightarrow X^{-2}\xrightarrow{d_{X}^{-2}}X^{-1}\xrightarrow{d_{X}^{-1}}X^{0}\rightarrow 0\rightarrow 0\rightarrow\cdots

and

Q=\cdots\rightarrow Q^{-2}\xrightarrow{d_{Q}^{-2}}Q^{-1}\xrightarrow{d_{Q}^{-1}}Q^{0}\xrightarrow{d_{Q}^{0}}Q^{1}\xrightarrow{d_{Q}^{1}}Q^{2}\rightarrow\cdots

Note that
Q^{i}
is flat and
\mathsf{q}\simeq\mathsf{q}(A)\otimes_{A}-
by the Eilenberg-Watts theorem. Then one has

\mathsf{H}^{i}(\mathsf{Lq}(X))=\mathsf{H}^{i}(\mathsf{q}(Q))\cong\mathbb{L}_{j-i}\mathsf{q}(\mathsf{B}^{j+1}(Q))

for all
i>0
and all
j>i.

According to the assumption that
\mathbb{L}_{i}\mathsf{q}=0
for
i
large enough, we have
\mathsf{H}^{i}(\mathsf{Lq}(X))=0
for all
i>0.

Since
X
is a bounded complex, we may assume that
X^{-t}=0
for any
t\geq m+1

m.

Thus we have an exact sequence

\cdots\rightarrow Q^{-(m+2)}\rightarrow Q^{-(m+1)}\rightarrow\ker d_{Q}^{-m}\rightarrow 0.

It follows that

\mathsf{H}^{-(m+i+1)}(\mathsf{Lq}(X))=\mathsf{H}^{-(m+i+1)}(\mathsf{q}(Q))\cong\mathbb{L}_{i}\mathsf{q}(\ker d_{Q}^{-m})=0

\sf l,e

\mathsf{Ll}(\mathbf{K}^{b}(\mathsf{FlatCot}(B)))\subseteq\mathbf{K}^{b}(\mathsf{FlatCot}(A)).

\sf l
is exact, it follows from Lemma

\sf e

By combining Lemma

\mathsf{Ri}(\mathbf{K}^{b}(\mathsf{FlatCot}(B)))\subseteq\mathbf{K}^{b}(\mathsf{FlatCot}(A))

Thus by

we get the following diagram of relative singularity categories

\textstyle{\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{FlatCot}}(B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

6.5 Theorem.

Let
A
and
B
be right coherent rings and
(B\text{-}\mathsf{Mod},A\text{-}\mathsf{Mod},\mathsf{i},\mathsf{e},\mathsf{l})
a cleft extension of module categories such that the induced endofunctor
\mathsf{F}
is f-perfect and nilpotent. Assume that the following conditions are satisfied:

(a)

\sf l
and
\sf r
are exact;

(b)

\sf l
and
\sf q
preserve products;

(c)

\sf i
preserves cotorsion modules.

Then there is a diagram

\textstyle{\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{FlatCot}}(B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\overline{\sf Ri}}
\textstyle{\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{FlatCot}}(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\overline{\mathsf{Lq}}}
\scriptstyle{\overline{\sf Re}}
\textstyle{\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{FlatCot}}(B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\overline{\mathsf{Ll}}}

of relative singularity categories and triangle functors such that
(\overline{\sf Lq},\overline{\sf Ri})
and
(\overline{\sf Ll},\overline{\sf Re})
are adjoint pairs,
\overline{\sf Re}\circ\overline{\sf Ri}\simeq\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{FlatCot}}(B)}
and
\overline{\sf Lq}\circ\overline{\sf Ll}\simeq\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{FlatCot}}(B)}
.

Proof.

Since
\sf F
is f-perfect and nilpotent, it follows from
[
17
, Proposition 6.6]
that
\mathbb{L}_{i}\mathsf{q}=0
for
i
large enough. Note that
\sf l
is exact. We know from Lemma
3.5
that
\sf e
preserves cotorsion modules.
Consider a flat-cotorsion left
B
-module
V
.
Since
\sf r
is exact, it follows from
Lemma
2.22
that
\mathrm{fd}_{A}\mathsf{i}(V)<\infty
if and only if
\mathrm{fd}_{B}\mathsf{ei}(V)<\infty
. Since
\mathsf{ei}(V)\cong V
, we get
\mathrm{fd}_{A}\mathsf{i}(V)<\infty
. Therefore the result follows by Proposition
6.4
.
∎

6.6 Corollary.

Let
\Lambda=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&{{}_{A}}N_{B}\\
{{}_{B}}M_{A}&B\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)

be a Morita context ring with
A
and
B
right coherent and
N\otimes_{B}M=0=M\otimes_{A}N
.
Assume that
M
is projective as a right
A
-module and a left
B
-module, and
N
is projective as a left
A
-module and a right
B
-module, and assume that
M
and
N
are finitely presented as a right
A
-module and a right
B
-module respectively. Then there is a diagram

\textstyle{\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{FlatCot}}(A)\times\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{FlatCot}}(B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\overline{\sf Ri}}
\textstyle{\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{FlatCot}}(\Lambda)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\overline{\mathsf{Lq}}}
\scriptstyle{\overline{\sf Re}}
\textstyle{\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{FlatCot}}(A)\times\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{FlatCot}}(B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\overline{\mathsf{Ll}}}

of singularity categories and triangle functors such that
(\overline{\sf Lq},\overline{\sf Ri})
and
(\sf\overline{Ll},\overline{Re})
are adjoint pairs,
\overline{\sf Re}\circ\overline{\sf Ri}\simeq\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{FlatCot}}(A)\times\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{FlatCot}}(B)}
and
\overline{\sf Lq}\circ\overline{\sf Ll}\simeq\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{FlatCot}}(A)\times\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{FlatCot}}(B)}
.

Proof.

By Example
2.12
we have a cleft extension of module categories

\textstyle{A\times B\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sf i}
\textstyle{\Lambda\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sf q}
\scriptstyle{\sf e}
\textstyle{A\times B\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sf l}

Here
\mathsf{q}(X,Y,f,g)=(X,\mbox{\rm Coker}f)\oplus(\mbox{\rm Coker}g,Y)
,

\mathsf{l}(X,Y)=(X,M\otimes_{A}X,1,0)\oplus(N\otimes_{B}Y,Y,0,1)
and

\mathsf{i}(X,Y)=(X,Y,0,0).

Since
M_{A}
and
N_{B}
are finitely presented, it is easy to check that
\sf l
and
\sf q
preserve products.
By
[
8
, Theorem 4.4]
and
[
21
, Remark 2.6]
, we know that
\sf i
preserves cotorsion modules.
Since
M_{A},N_{B},{{}_{A}N}
and
{}_{B}N
are projective, it follows from Example

2.12
that
\sf l
and
\sf r
are exact.
Consequently, we get the conclusion by Theorem
6.5
.
∎

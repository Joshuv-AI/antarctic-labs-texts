Nine-year aortic growth
Authors: Hisato Takagi, Carlos Nicolas Perez-Garcia, Priya Bhardwaj, Stefano Nistri
Published: 2026

Case description

Twelve years before, a 67-year-old woman at that time successfully underwent urgent ascending-aortic replacement without aortic-root/valve procedure for type A acute-aortic dissection. The last follow-up computed tomography (CT) was performed at the age of 70 three years after the surgery, and afterward she was lost to follow-up. Nine years after the last CT at the age of 79, the patient complained of sudden-onset dyspnoea, back pain, and haemoptysis. Her height and weight were respectively 153 cm and 43 kg without systemic features suspecting connective-tissue disorders. The patient had no history of hypertension, and family history of aortic aneurysm or dissection was absent. CT (Figure 1A–F and Supplementary material online, Videos S1 and S2, transverse planes; Figure 1G and H and Videos S3 and S4, three-dimensional CT angiogram; arrows, aneurysms; asterisks/red shadow, thrombus) demonstrated enlargement of the distal-aortic arch from 46 mm (Figure 1A, Supplementary material online, Video S1) to 71 mm (Figure 1B, Supplementary material online, Videos S2 and S3), infra-renal aorta from 31 mm (Figure 1C, Supplementary material online, Video S1) to 70 mm (Figure 1D, Supplementary material online, Videos S2 and S4), bilateral common-iliac arteries respectively from 24 mm (Figure 1E, Supplementary material online, Video S1) to 47 mm (Figure 1F and H, Supplementary material online, Videos S2 and S4), and left internal-iliac artery from 24 mm (see Supplementary material online, Video S1) to 47 mm (Figure 1H, Supplementary material online, Videos S2 and S4) over 9 years. The residual-dissecting distal aortic-arch aneurysm was rupturing with massive haemoptysis, and the patient unfortunately died of haemorrhagic shock before surgery. The aorta should be monitored independently of its size at baseline on a long-term basis after aortic events such as aortic dissection and repair.1,2 Because growth rates are similar but a rupture risk is four-fold higher in women, follow-up intervals in infra-renal aortic aneurysm are recommended to be 3 years for 30–39 mm, 1 year for 40–44 mm in women and 40–49 mm in men, and 6 months for 45–49 mm in women and 50–54 mm in men.3 Although the present female patient with the initial 31-mm infra-renal aorta should have been followed up at least every 3 years, the inadequate surveillance (9-year interval) incurred its growth to 70 mm. Meanwhile, thoracic aortic aneurysm should be reimaged every 2 or 3 years for 40–49 mm and every 6 months for 50–54 mm3.

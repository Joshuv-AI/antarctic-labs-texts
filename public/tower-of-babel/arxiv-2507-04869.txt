Extension Operators for Fractional Sobolev Spaces on Lipschitz Submanifolds
Philipp Weder

Abstract
A well-known result is that any Lipschitz domain is an extension domain for W s , p W^{s,p} . This paper extends this result to Lipschitz subsets of compact Lipschitz submanifolds of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . We adapt the construction of an extension operator for Lipschitz domains in [ 1 ] to manifolds via local coordinate charts. Furthermore, the dependence on the size of the extension domain is explicit in all estimates. This result is motivated by applications in numerical analysis, most notably geometry simplification, where the explicit dependence of the continuity constant on the domain size is essential.



1
Introduction

Sobolev extension operators find applications in numerical analysis, for instance finite elements in time-dependent domains
[
2
]
, homogenization methods
[
3
]
, or
a posteriori
defeaturing error estimation
[
4
]
. For numerical applications, not only the existence of such operators but also the dependence of their continuity constants on the geometry or other parameters are important. The target application of the result in this paper is geometry simplification, where the dependence of the continuity constant on the size of the subset is crucial. Hence, our motivation to make it explicit.

More precisely, let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 2,
be a domain, i.e., a general open, bounded subset.
It is well-known
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
1
]
that under certain regularity conditions on the boundary
\partial\Omega
, the domain
\Omega
is an extension domain for the classical Sobolev space
W^{s,p},s>0,p\in[1,+\infty)
; i.e., there exists a constant
C=C(n,p,s,\Omega)>0
, such that for every function
u\in W^{s,p}(\Omega)
, there exists
\tilde{u}\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\tilde{u}(x)=u(x)
for all
x\in\Omega
and
||\tilde{u}||_{s,p}\leq C||u||_{s,p,\Omega}
.
While a general characterization of extension domains is non-trivial, Lipschitz domains provide a well-known class of examples. A detailed proof for this statement can be found in
[
1
]
[§ 5] for
s\in(0,1)
or in
[
7
]
for
s>0
.

In this paper, we generalize the result for Lipschitz domains to subsets of Lipschitz submanifolds of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, valid for all
p\in[1,+\infty)
and
s\in(0,1)
. The proof will follow the one in
[
1
]
, but care must be taken when handling the local parameterizations of the manifold.

The remainder of the paper is structured as follows: First, we introduce the essential definitions for Sobolev spaces on manifolds. Next, we will present some preliminary lemmas, followed by the proof of the main statement. We will also outline an approach to generalize the result for Sobolev exponents
s\geq 1
. Finally, we will conclude with a summary of our findings.

2
Preliminaries

We first collect standard definitions and notation for Lipschitz domains, Lipschitz submanifolds, and fractional Sobolev spaces.

Definition 1

(Lipschitz domain)
.

A domain
\Omega
with boundary
\partial\Omega
is said to be
Lipschitz
if for every
x\in\partial\Omega
, there exists a neighborhood
U(x)
of
x
such that
U(x)\cap\partial\Omega
is the graph of a Lipschitz function.

In this paper, we are interested in Sobolev spaces on compact Lipschitz submanifolds, which naturally appear as boundaries of Lipschitz domains.

Definition 2

(Lipschitz submanifold)
.

Let
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded subset. Then,
M
is a bounded
k
-dimensional submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if for every point
x\in M
, there exists an open set
U_{x}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
, an open neighborhood
V_{x}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
x
and a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism
t_{x}:U_{x}\to V_{x}\cap M
.

For Lipschitz submanifolds, the following, well-known change of variable formula will be used repeatedly in our argument: For
x\in M
, let
t_{x}:U_{x}\to V_{x}\cap M
be the corresponding homeomorphism. We set

\displaystyle J_{t_{x}}(\hat{x})\coloneqq\begin{cases}\sqrt{\det(Dt_{x}^{\top}(\hat{x})Dt_{x}(\hat{x}))},&t_{x}\text{ differentiable at }\hat{x},\\
0,\text{ otherwise },\end{cases}

which is well-defined in
L^{\infty}(U_{x})
, since
t_{x}
, being a bi-Lipschitz continuous bijection, is almost everywhere differentiable with respect to the Lebesgue measure due to Rademacher’s Theorem
[
8
]
[Theorem 9.14]. Moreover, for any Borel-measurable function,
h:B\cap M\to[-\infty,\infty]
, we have in virtue of the change of variable formula
that

\displaystyle\int_{B\cap M}h(x)\,\mathrm{d}s(x)=\int_{U_{x}}h(t(\hat{x}))J_{t_{x}}(\hat{x})d\hat{x},

(1)

where
\,\mathrm{d}s
denotes the
k
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on
M
. For subsets
\Lambda\subset M
, we denote by
|\Lambda|
the
k
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of the set. In particular, we adopt the following notation:
A\lesssim B
means that
A\leq cB
for some constant
c>0
independent of the size
|\Lambda|
of
\Lambda\subset M
. Furthermore, we write
A\simeq B
if and only if
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
.

We now state a preliminary lemma about the local parameterizations of a bounded Lipschitz submanifold, which simplifies the subsequent arguments.

Lemma 1
.

Let
M
be a bounded
k
-dimensional Lipschitz submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, the following assertions hold:

1.

There exists
\epsilon>0
, such that for all
x\in M
, we can find a local parameterization
t_{x}:U_{x}\to B_{\epsilon}(x)\cap M
with
0\in U_{x}
and
t_{x}(0)=x
.

2.

For
x\in M
, denote by
L_{t_{x}}
and
L_{t_{x}^{-1}}
the Lipschitz constants of
t_{x}
and
t_{x}^{-1}
, respectively.
Then, there exist constants
L,\hat{L},J,\hat{J}>0
, such that for all
x\in M
,

\displaystyle L_{t_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq L,

\displaystyle||J_{t_{x}}||_{L^{\infty}(U_{x})}\leq J,

\displaystyle L_{t_{x}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\hat{L},

\displaystyle||J_{t_{x}^{-1}}||_{L^{\infty}(B_{\epsilon}(x)\cap M)}\leq\hat{J}.

3.

For every
x\in M
, we have
B_{\epsilon/L}(0)\subset U_{x}\subset B_{\epsilon\hat{L}}(0)
.

4.

For a subset
\Lambda\subset M
with
|\Lambda|>0
it holds that

\displaystyle L\simeq\hat{L}^{-1}\simeq|\Lambda|^{\frac{1}{k}}

and

\displaystyle J\simeq\hat{J}^{-1}\simeq|\Lambda|.

Proof.

Since
M
is bounded and therefore compact, we find a finite collection of bi-Lipschitz local parameterizations
\tilde{t}_{j}:U_{j}\to V_{j}\cap M,j=1,\ldots,N
, for open sets
U_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
V_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such that the collection
\{V_{j}\cap M\}_{j=1}^{N}
is an open cover of
M
.

For assertion 1, let
\epsilon>0
be the Lebesgue number of the open cover
\{V_{j}\cap M\}_{j=1}^{N}
. Let
x\in M
and
i
the index such that
x\in V_{i}
. Then, we set
U_{x}\coloneqq\tilde{t}_{i}^{-1}(B_{\epsilon}(x)\cap M)
and
t_{x}\coloneqq(\tilde{t}_{i})_{|U_{x}}
. Translating
U_{x}
by
\tilde{t}_{i}^{-1}(x)
yields the assertion.

For assertion 2, we denote by
L_{j}
the Lipschitz constants of
\tilde{t}_{j}
, and we set
L\coloneqq\max_{j}L_{j}
. Then, we note that
t_{x}
in the first step of the proof inherits the Lipschitz constant of
\tilde{t}_{i}
, which is not impacted by the translation. Hence, it follows immediately that
L_{t_{x}}\leq L
. The existence of
J
follows from the fact the operator norm of
Dt_{x}
is uniformly bounded by
L
as well. The proof for the existence of
\hat{L}
and
\hat{J}
is analogous.

For assertion 3, we fix
x\in M
, and we choose
y,z\in B_{\epsilon}(x)\cap M
. Then, we have
|t_{x}^{-1}(y)-t_{x}^{-1}(z)|\leq\hat{L}|y-z|\leq\hat{L}\epsilon
. Conversely, for any
\hat{y}\in B_{\epsilon/L}(0)
, we have
|t_{x}(\hat{y})-x|\leq L|\hat{y}|\leq\epsilon
, which proofs the assertion.

For the last assertion, we rescale the
M
by a factor
\lambda>0
denoted by
M_{\lambda}
. Then, for
x\in M
we have that
x_{\lambda}\coloneqq\lambda x\in M_{\lambda}
. In particular, from a chart map
t_{x}:U_{x}\to B_{\epsilon}(x)\cap M
for
M
, we obtain a valid chart map
\tilde{t}_{x_{\lambda}}:U_{x}\to B_{\lambda\epsilon}(x_{\lambda})\cap M_{\lambda}
for
x_{\lambda}\in M_{\lambda}
by setting
\tilde{t}_{x_{\lambda}}(\hat{y})\coloneqq\lambda t_{x}(\hat{y})
for
\hat{y}\in U_{x}
. It follows immediately that

\displaystyle L_{\tilde{t}_{x_{\lambda}}}=\lambda L_{t_{x}}

and

\displaystyle||J_{\tilde{t}_{x_{\lambda}}}||_{L^{\infty}(U_{x})}=\lambda^{k}||J_{t_{x}}||_{L^{\infty}(U_{x})},

and inversely for
L_{\tilde{t}_{x_{\lambda}}^{-1}}
and
||J_{\tilde{t}_{x_{\lambda}}^{-1}}||_{L^{\infty}(B_{\lambda\epsilon}(x)\cap M_{\lambda})}
. Rescaling to reference size
|\Lambda_{\lambda}|=1
requires
\lambda=|\Lambda|^{-\frac{1}{k}}
. Inserting this choice of
\lambda
in the above expression and dividing by
\lambda
and
\lambda^{k}
, respectively, yields the desired result.
∎

2.1
Function spaces on Lipschitz manifolds

On a
k
-dimensional Lipschitz submanifold
M
, we define the classical
L^{p}(M)
space, for
p\in[1,\infty]
, with the norm

\displaystyle||u||_{0,p,M}^{p}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{M}|u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}s(x)

\displaystyle\text{if }p<\infty,

\displaystyle||u||_{L^{\infty}(M)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{C\geq 0:|u|\leq C\text{ a. e. in }M\}

\displaystyle\text{ if }p=\infty.

Finally, we define the classical Sobolev spaces
W^{s,p}(M)
in terms of Gagliardo-Slobodeckij seminorms
[
9
]
: Let
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in[1,+\infty)
. Then, we define

\displaystyle W^{s,p}(M)\coloneqq\{u\in L^{p}(M):||u||_{s,p,M}<\infty\},

where

\displaystyle||u||_{s,p,M}^{p}

\displaystyle\coloneqq||u||_{0,p,M}^{p}+|u|_{s,p,M}^{p},

(2)

\displaystyle|u|_{s,p,M}^{p}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{M}\int_{M}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{k+ps}}\,\mathrm{d}s(x)\,\mathrm{d}s(y).

(3)

The definition of Sobolev spaces on subsets
\Lambda\subset M
with
|\Lambda|>0
is analogous.

3
The main result

Let us now state the main result:

Theorem 2
.

Let
M
be a
k
-dimensional Lipschitz submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
according to
definition

2
and
\Lambda\subset M
an open connected subset such that its boundary
\partial\Lambda
is a
(k-1)
-dimensional Lipschitz submanifold.

Then, for
p\in[1,+\infty)
and
s\in(0,1)
, there exists a continuous extension operator
\mathsf{E}_{\Lambda}^{M}:W^{s,p}(\Lambda)\to W^{s,p}(M)

such that

\displaystyle||\mathsf{E}_{\Lambda}^{M}(u)||_{s,p,M}^{p}\lesssim\left(1+|\Lambda|^{-\frac{sp}{k}}+|\Lambda|^{\frac{(1-s)p}{k}}\right)||u||_{0,p,\Lambda}^{p}+|u|_{s,p,\Lambda}^{p}.

Before providing a proof of
theorem

2
, we state and prove several preliminary lemmas. The main idea of the proof is the following: We cover
\Lambda
with a finite open cover and select a subordinate partition of unity. The latter allows for decomposing the function, which we want to extend into a sum of locally supported functions.
Then, it is sufficient to find extension operators for functions vanishing in a neighborhood of
\partial\Lambda
and for functions supported in a single coordinate chart of
\partial\Lambda
. The first case is addressed in
lemma

3
and the second in
lemma

4
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
M
be a
k
-dimensional Lipschitz submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Lambda\subset M
such that
|\Lambda|>0
.
Let
u\in W^{s,p}(\Lambda)
with
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in[1,+\infty)
. If there exists a compact subset
K\subset\Lambda
such that
u=0
in
\Lambda\setminus K
, then the extension
u^{\star}
defined by

\displaystyle u^{\star}\coloneqq\begin{cases}u(x),&x\in\Lambda,\\
0,&x\in M\setminus\Lambda\end{cases},

is an element of
W^{s,p}(M)
, such that

\displaystyle||u^{\star}||_{0,p,M}\lesssim||u||_{0,p,\Lambda}

and

\displaystyle|u^{\star}|_{s,p,M}^{p}\lesssim|\Lambda|^{-\frac{sp}{k}}||u||_{0,p,\Lambda}^{p}+|u|_{s,p,\Lambda}^{p}.

Proof.

Clearly,
u^{\star}\in L^{p}(M)
with
||u^{\star}||_{0,p,M}\lesssim||u||_{0,p,\Lambda}
. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle|u^{\star}|_{s,p,M}^{p}=|u|_{s,p,\Lambda}^{p}+2\int_{M\setminus\Lambda}\int_{\Lambda}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{k+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}s(x)\,\mathrm{d}s(y).

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{M\setminus\Lambda}\int_{\Lambda}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{k+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}s(x)\,\mathrm{d}s(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{M\setminus\Lambda}\int_{K}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{k+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}s(x)\,\mathrm{d}s(y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|M\setminus\Lambda|}{\dist(M\setminus\Lambda,K)^{k+sp}}||u||_{0,p,\Lambda}^{p}.

Finally, observe that
|M\setminus\Lambda|\simeq|\Lambda|
and
\dist(M\setminus\Lambda,K)\simeq|\Lambda|^{\frac{1}{k}}
to obtain the desired result.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
M
be a
k
-dimensional Lipschitz submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Lambda\subset M
open and connected. Furthermore, assume that
\partial\Lambda
is a
(k-1)
-dimensional Lipschitz submanifold of
M
. Finally, let
x_{0}\in\partial\Lambda
with
B\coloneqq B_{\epsilon}(x_{0})
and
t_{x}:U_{x}\to B
as defined in
lemma

1
.

Then, for every
u\in W^{s,p}(B\cap\Lambda)
with
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in[1,+\infty)
, there exists an extension
\bar{u}
to
B\cap M
such that
\bar{u}(x)=u(x)
almost everywhere in
B\cap\Lambda
and
\bar{u}\in W^{s,p}(B\cap M)
with

\displaystyle||\bar{u}||_{s,p,B\cap M}\lesssim||u||_{s,p,B\cap\Lambda}.

Proof.

First, we define
U_{x}^{-}\coloneqq t_{x}^{-1}(B\cap\Lambda)\subset U_{x}
and
v:U_{x}^{-}\to\mathbb{R}
by setting
v(\hat{y})\coloneqq u(t_{x}(\hat{y}))
for
\hat{y}\in U_{x}^{-}
. We claim that
v\in W^{s,p}(U_{x}^{-})
.
Indeed, we have

\displaystyle||v||_{0,p,U_{x}^{-}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{U_{x}^{-}}|u(t_{x}(\hat{x})|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{x}=\int_{B\cap\Lambda}|u(x)|^{p}J_{t_{x}^{-1}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}s(x)

\displaystyle\leq||J_{t_{x}^{-1}}||_{L^{\infty}(B\cap\Lambda)}||u||_{0,p,B\cap\Lambda}^{p}\leq\hat{J}||u||_{0,p,B\cap\Lambda}^{p},

where
\hat{J}
is the bound from
lemma

1
.
Moreover, note that we have for
y,z\in B
that

\displaystyle|y-z|=|t_{x}(t_{x}^{-1}(y))-t_{x}(t_{x}^{-1}(z))|\leq L_{t_{x}}|t_{x}^{-1}(y)-t_{x}^{-1}(z)|.

Therefore, we find for the seminorm,

\displaystyle|v|_{s,p,U_{x}^{-}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{U_{x}^{-}}\int_{U_{x}^{-}}\frac{|v(\hat{y})-v(\hat{z})|^{p}}{|\hat{y}-\hat{z}|^{k+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{y}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{z}

\displaystyle=\int_{B\cap\Lambda}\int_{B\cap\Lambda}\frac{|u(y)-u(z)|^{p}}{|t_{x}^{-1}(y)-t_{x}^{-1}(z)|^{k+sp}}J_{t_{x}^{-1}}(y)J_{t_{x}^{-1}}(z)\,\mathrm{d}s(y)\,\mathrm{d}s(z)

\displaystyle\leq L_{t_{x}}^{k+sp}||J_{t_{x}^{-1}}||^{2}_{L^{\infty}(B\cap\Lambda)}|u|_{s,p,B\cap\Lambda}^{p}\leq L^{k+sp}\hat{J}^{2}|u|_{s,p,B\cap\Lambda}^{p},

where
L
is the bound from
lemma

1
. This proves the claim.

Furthermore, we note that
U_{x}^{-}
is a subset of
U_{x}
with the Lipschitz boundary
\hat{\eta}\coloneqq t_{x}^{-1}(\eta)
according to
definition

1
since we assumed
\eta
to be Lipschitz and
t_{x}
is bi-Lipschitz by definition. By the Sobolev extension theorem for Lipschitz domains, there exists an extension
\bar{v}\in W^{s,p}(U_{x})
of
v
such that
\bar{v}(\hat{y})=v(\hat{y})
almost everywhere in
U_{x}^{-}
and

\displaystyle||\bar{v}||_{s,p,U_{x}}\leq C||v||_{s,p,U_{x}^{-}},

for some constant
C>0
. We now define
\bar{u}
as the push-forward of
\bar{v}
by setting
\bar{u}(x)\coloneqq\bar{v}(t_{x}^{-1}(x)),x\in B\cap M
. By the same argument as for
v
, we find

\displaystyle||\bar{u}||_{0,p,B\cap M}^{p}\leq J||\bar{v}||_{0,p,U_{x}}^{p}\leq C^{p}J\hat{J}||u||_{s,p,B\cap\Lambda}^{p},

and

\displaystyle|\bar{u}|_{s,p,B\cap M}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq J^{2}\hat{L}^{k+sp}|\bar{v}|_{s,p,U_{x}}^{p}\leq C^{p}(J\hat{J})^{2}(L\hat{L})^{k+sp}|u|_{s,p,B\cap\Lambda}^{p},

where
L,\hat{L},J
and
\hat{J}
are the constants from
lemma

1
. Note that the constant
C
stems from an extension in the local coordinates of the chart and therefore does not depend on the size of
\Lambda
. Inserting the scalings from
lemma

1
for the other constants yields the desired result.
∎

Lemma 5
.

Let
M
be a
k
-dimensional Lipschitz submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Lambda\subset M
open and connected. Furthermore, let
u\in W^{s,p}(\Lambda)
, with
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in[1,+\infty)
, and
\psi
a Lipschitz continuous function on
\Lambda
with
0\leq\psi\leq 1
.

Then, the product
\psi u
is an element of
W^{s,p}(\Lambda)
with

\displaystyle||\psi u||_{0,p,\Lambda}\lesssim||u||_{0,p,\Lambda}

and

\displaystyle|\psi u|_{s,p,\Lambda}^{p}\lesssim|\Lambda|^{\frac{(1-s)p}{k}}||u||_{0,p,\Lambda}^{p}+|u|_{s,p,\Lambda}^{p}.

Proof.

We clearly have
||\psi u||_{0,p,\Lambda}\lesssim||u||_{0,p,\Lambda}
by monotonicity. By adding and subtracting the factor
\psi(x)u(y)
in the numerator, we find

\displaystyle|\psi u|_{s,p,\Lambda}^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}\left(\int_{\Lambda}\int_{\Lambda}\frac{|\psi(x)u(x)-\psi(x)u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{k+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}s(x)\,\mathrm{d}s(y)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\int_{\Lambda}\int_{\Lambda}\frac{|\psi(x)u(y)-\psi(y)u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{k+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}s(x)\,\mathrm{d}s(y)\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\left(|u|_{s,p,\Lambda}^{p}+\int_{\Lambda}\int_{\Lambda}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}|\psi(x)-\psi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{k+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}s(x)\,\mathrm{d}s(y)\right).

Let
\epsilon>0
be as in
lemma

1
and
L_{\psi}
the Lipschitz constant of
\psi
. Then, interchanging of the integrals according to Fubini’s Theorem in the second integral yields,

\displaystyle\int_{\Lambda}\int_{\Lambda}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}|\psi(x)-\psi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{k+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}s(y)\,\mathrm{d}s(x)

\displaystyle\leq L_{\psi}^{p}\left(\int_{\Lambda}\int_{\Lambda\cap|x-y|<\epsilon}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{k+(s-1)p}}\,\mathrm{d}s(y)\,\mathrm{d}s(x)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\int_{\Lambda}\int_{\Lambda\cap|x-y|\geq\epsilon}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{k+(s-1)p}}\,\mathrm{d}s(y)\,\mathrm{d}s(x)\right).

(4)

For the second term, we immediately find

\displaystyle\int_{\Lambda}\int_{\Lambda\cap|x-y|\geq\epsilon}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{k+(s-1)p}}\,\mathrm{d}s(y)\,\mathrm{d}s(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\Lambda|}{\epsilon^{k+(s-1)p}}||u||_{0,p,\Lambda}^{p}\lesssim|\Lambda|^{\frac{(1-s)p}{k}}||u||_{0,p,\Lambda}^{p}.

Using the chart map
t_{x}:U_{x}\to B_{\epsilon}(x)\cap M
, we find together with the change of variable formula in
eq.

1
that

\displaystyle\int_{\Lambda\cap|x-y|<\epsilon}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{k+(s-1)p}}\,\mathrm{d}s(y)=\int_{U_{x}}\frac{1}{|t_{x}(0)-t_{x}(\hat{y})|^{k+(s-1)p}}J_{t_{x}}(\hat{y})\,\mathrm{d}\hat{y}

\displaystyle\leq||J_{t_{x}}||_{L^{\infty}(U_{x})}L_{t_{x}^{-1}}^{k+(s-1)p}\int_{U_{x}}\frac{1}{|\hat{y}|^{k+(s-1)p}}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{y}

\displaystyle\leq J\hat{L}^{k+(s-1)p}\int_{B_{\epsilon\hat{L}}(0)}\frac{1}{|\hat{y}|^{k+(s-1)p}}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{y}\lesssim J\hat{L}^{k+(s-1)p},

where we used assertion 3 in
lemma

1
and that
|\hat{y}|^{-k-(s-1)p}
is summable since
k+(s-1)p<k
.

Therefore, reinserting this expression into the corresponding double integral in
section

3
and using the scaling properties in assertion 4 of
lemma

1
, we find

\displaystyle\int_{\Lambda}\int_{\Lambda\cap|x-y|<\epsilon}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{k+(s-1)p}}\,\mathrm{d}s(y)\,\mathrm{d}s(x)\lesssim|\Lambda|^{\frac{(1-s)p}{k}}||u||_{0,p,\Lambda}^{p}.

Gathering the above estimates, we eventually find that

\displaystyle|\psi u|_{s,p,\Lambda}^{p}\lesssim|\Lambda|^{\frac{(1-s)p}{k}}||u||_{0,p,\Lambda}^{p}+|u|_{s,p,\Lambda}^{p},

which finishes the proof.
∎

With the above results, we are ready to prove the main theorem.

Proof of
theorem

2
.

Let
\epsilon>0
be as in
lemma

1
. Since
\partial\Lambda
is compact, we find finitely many points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in\partial\Lambda
such that
\partial\Lambda\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}B_{i}
, where
B_{i}\coloneqq B_{\epsilon}(x_{i})\cap M,i=1,\ldots,N
. Let
B_{0}\subset\Lambda
be another open subset such that the collection
\{B_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}
is a finite open covering of the closure
\overline{\Lambda}
. Let
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}
be a partition of unity subordinated to the latter covering. By definition of the partition of unity, we have

\displaystyle u=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\psi_{i}\,u\;\text{ in }\Lambda.

Let us first consider the term
\psi_{0}u
. By
lemma

5
,
\psi_{0}u
is an element of
W^{s,p}(\Lambda)
and vanishes in a neighborhood of
\partial\Lambda
. Hence, by
lemma

3
and
lemma

5
, the extension by zero
w_{0}\coloneqq(\psi_{0}u)^{\star}
to
M
is an element of
W^{s,p}(M)
with

\displaystyle||w_{0}||_{s,p,M}^{p}\lesssim\left(1+|\Lambda|^{-\frac{sp}{k}}\right)||\psi_{0}u||_{0,p,\Lambda}^{p}+|\psi_{0}u|_{s,p,\Lambda}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(1+|\Lambda|^{-\frac{sp}{k}}+|\Lambda|^{\frac{(1-s)p}{k}}\right)||u||_{0,p,\Lambda}^{p}+|u|_{s,p,\Lambda}^{p}.

For
1\leq i\leq N
, we have
u_{|B_{i}\cap\Lambda}\in W^{s,p}(B_{i}\cap\Lambda)
. By
lemma

4
, there exists an extension
v_{i}\in W^{s,p}(B_{i}\cap M)
with

\displaystyle||v_{i}||_{s,p,B_{i}\cap M}\lesssim||u_{|B_{i}\cap\Lambda}||_{s,p,B_{i}\cap\Lambda}\leq||u||_{s,p,\Lambda}.

(5)

In particular, we must have
\psi_{i}v_{i}=\psi_{i}u
almost everywhere in
B_{i}\cap\Lambda
. By definition,
\psi_{i}v_{i}
has compact support in
B_{i}\cap M
. Therefore, we can apply once more
lemma

3
and define the extension by zero
w_{i}\coloneqq(\psi_{i}v_{i})^{\star}\in W^{s,p}(M)
, such that by
lemma

3
,
lemma

5
, and
eq.

5
, we have

\displaystyle||w_{i}||_{s,p,M}^{p}\lesssim\left(1+|\Lambda|^{-\frac{sp}{k}}+|\Lambda|^{\frac{(1-s)p}{k}}\right)||v_{i}||_{0,p,B_{i}\cap M}^{p}+|v_{i}|_{s,p,B_{i}\cap M}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(1+|\Lambda|^{-\frac{sp}{k}}+|\Lambda|^{\frac{(1-s)p}{k}}\right)||u||_{0,p,\Lambda}^{p}+|u|_{s,p,\Lambda}^{p}.

Defining the extension
\mathsf{E}_{\Lambda}^{M}(u)
by

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{\Lambda}^{M}(u)(x)\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{N}w_{i}(x),\quad x\in M,

we have by construction that
\mathsf{E}_{\Lambda}^{M}(u)=u
almost everywhere in
\Lambda
and

\displaystyle||\mathsf{E}_{\Lambda}^{M}(u)||_{s,p,M}^{p}\lesssim\sum_{i=0}^{N}||w_{i}||_{s,p,M}^{p}\lesssim\left(1+|\Lambda|^{-\frac{sp}{k}}+|\Lambda|^{\frac{(1-s)p}{k}}\right)||u||_{0,p,\Lambda}^{p}+|u|_{s,p,\Lambda}^{p}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 1
.

The result of
theorem

2
can be extended to Sobolev exponents
s\geq 1
. However, one then has to assume higher regularity of the manifold for the composition of a function in
W^{s,p}(M)
with a chart map to still be in
W^{s,p}
.

Furthermore, instead of the global expression for the seminorm in
eq.

2
, one has to define the seminorms for exponents
s\geq 1
either chart-by-chart, see
[
9
]
, or by introducing a Riemannian metric and contravariant derivatives, see
[
10
]
. In the latter approach, the norm is eventually also computed chart-by-chart, which comes with the disadvantage that the dependence of the continuity constants on
|\Lambda|
is less clear. Hence, our focus on the case
s\in(0,1)
.

4
Conclusion

In this paper, we have established an extension theorem for fractional Sobolev spaces
W^{s,p}
on subsets
\Lambda
of bounded Lipschitz submanifolds, provided that
\Lambda
itself has a Lipschitz boundary relative to the manifold. In particular, the dependence on the size of the subset is explicit in all estimates.

Our proof adapts the classical arguments for Lipschitz domains in Euclidean space from
[
1
]
, with particular attention paid to the uniform control of local parameterizations on the compact Lipschitz manifold
M
, as detailed in
lemma

1
.

Future work includes generalizing the result to Sobolev exponents
s\geq 1
as outlined in
remark

1
under stronger regularity assumptions on the manifold.

Born Lie algebras
Alejandro Gil-García and Paula Naomi Pilatus

Abstract
We show that every Born Lie algebra can be obtained by the bicross product construction starting from two pseudo-Riemannian Lie algebras. We then obtain a classification of all Lie algebras up to dimension four and all six-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebras admitting an integrable Born structure. Finally, we study the curvature properties of the pseudo-Riemannian metrics of the integrable Born structures obtained in our classification results. Keywords: Born, pseudo-Kähler, bi-Lagrangian, complex product, bicross product MSC classification: Primary 53C15; Secondary 53C50, 53C55, 22E25

. In this paper, we will work with the definition of a Born structure proposed in

]
, as a diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}

where

We will furthermore adopt the notion of

An integrable Born structure gives rise to a (pseudo-)Kähler structure, a bi-Lagrangian structure as well as a complex product structure on the underlying manifold satisfying certain compatibility conditions.

Being a relatively new geometric structure, so far not many examples of manifolds admitting (integrable) Born structures have been discussed in the literature. A particularly feasible class of examples are left-invariant Born structures on Lie groups, since in this setting it suffices to work at the level of the Lie algebra. First examples of Born structures on Lie algebras were studied in
[

[

All of these nilpotent Lie algebras have a rational basis, hence the corresponding Lie groups admit cocompact lattices
[
30

Main results and outline

In this paper we focus on the construction and classification of low-dimensional Lie algebras admitting an integrable Born structure. In Section
2
we recall the notion and basic properties of Born Lie algebras. In Section
3
we make use of the bicross product construction (see Definition
3.1
) to show that given two pseudo-Riemannian Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{g}_{\pm},h_{\pm})
, a linear isometry
Q\colon\mathfrak{g}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{-}
and two representations
\varphi
and
\rho
satisfying suitable conditions we obtain a Born Lie algebra, see Theorem
3.7
. Furthermore, every Born Lie algebra can be obtained in this way, see Proposition
3.8
. Setting one of the representations to zero, the bicross product reduces to the semidirect product and we are able to construct many examples of Born Lie algebras starting just from flat pseudo-Riemannian Lie algebras, see Corollary
3.10
. By a similar strategy we obtain examples of pseudo-hyperkähler and hypersymplectic Lie algebras, see Propositions
3.12
and
3.14
, respectively.

In Section
4
we classify low-dimensional Lie algebras admitting an integrable Born structure. The classification in dimension two is trivial and the classification in dimension four is obtained in Theorem
4.1
by comparing the classifications of four-dimensional pseudo-Kähler and bi-Lagrangian Lie algebras and providing an explicit integrable Born structure for each Lie algebra in the theorem. In Theorem
4.3
we classify all six-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebras admitting an integrable Born structure. To obtain such classification we make use of several results that restrict the possible candidates. First we show in Lemma
4.6
that it is enough to consider the bicross products
\mathbb{R}^{3}\bowtie\mathfrak{heis}_{3}
, where
\mathfrak{heis}_{3}
is the three-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra, and
\mathbb{R}^{3}\bowtie\mathbb{R}^{3}
. In the
\mathbb{R}^{3}\bowtie\mathfrak{heis}_{3}
case we only need to consider flat metrics by Lemma
4.8
and we obtain all such Lie algebras in Proposition
4.9
. In the
\mathbb{R}^{3}\bowtie\mathbb{R}^{3}
case we first find necessary conditions to admit an integrable Born structure in Lemma
4.10
and then we find such Lie algebras in Proposition
4.11
. The combination of these last two propositions gives us the complete classification of six-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebras admitting an integrable Born structure. Finally, we study curvature properties of the Born Lie algebras obtained in the classifications.

Related results

As explained, Born geometry lies in the intersection of pseudo-Kähler, bi-Lagrangian and complex product geometry. These three geometries are well studied in the context of left-invariant structures on Lie groups or, equivalently, on Lie algebras. In particular, several classification results are known for each of these structures on low-dimensional Lie algebras.

Pseudo-Kähler Lie algebras have been completely classified in dimension four
[
33
]
. In this dimension it is known that if a Lie algebra admits a symplectic structure then it must be solvable
[
9
]
. Moreover, some of these pseudo-Kähler Lie algebras are actually Kähler, i.e. positive-definite. This can only happen for non-nilpotent or abelian Lie algebras due to
[
24
]
(see also
[
14
]
). Six-dimensional nilpotent pseudo-Kähler Lie algebras have been classified in
[
12
]
, and in
[
28
]
a partial classification is obtained in dimension eight. Further examples of pseudo-Kähler Lie algebras have been constructed using semidirect products
[
8
,
10
,
11
]
. Bi-Lagrangian structures, also known as para-Kähler
[
13
]
or Künneth structures
[
22
]
, have been classified in dimension four
[
7
]
and in dimension six in the nilpotent case
[
21
]
.

Complex product structures on Lie algebras were introduced in
[
4
]
, where many four-dimensional examples were obtained. In contrast to the pseudo-Kähler and bi-Lagrangian case, there exist four-dimensional non-solvable complex product Lie algebras. The classification in the solvable case was obtained in
[
3
]
and the complete classification in
[
6
]
, where complex product structures appear under the name para-hypercomplex. Six-dimensional nilpotent complex product Lie algebras were classified in
[
2
]
.

Preliminaries on Born structures

Let

. For a detailed discussion of recursion operators see
[

]

Definition 2.1

(
[

]
)
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold. A
Born structure
on

is a triple
(g,h,\omega)
, where
g
and
h
are pseudo-Riemannian metrics and
\omega
is a non-degenerate two-form such that the recursion operators
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-J}

satisfy
-J^{2}=A^{2}=B^{2}=\operatorname{Id}
.

(
[

]
)
.

The recursion operators

,

and

. In particular

and

g(AX,AY)=-g(X,Y)

\omega(AX,Y)=-\omega(X,AY)

g(BX,BY)=g(X,Y)

\omega(BX,BY)=-\omega(X,Y)

\omega(BX,Y)=-\omega(X,BY)

\omega(JX,Y)=-\omega(X,JY)

We denote by

(
[

]
)
.

The distributions

are Lagrangian for

Definition 2.4

(
[
23
]
)
.

A Born structure
(g,h,\omega)
is

if the two-form
\omega
is symplectic and two out of the three recursion operators are integrable. A manifold with an integrable Born structure is called

.

Here, it is enough to require the integrability of only two out of the three recursion operators, due to the following proposition:

Proposition 2.5

(
[

,
27
]
)
.

If two out of the three recursion operators in a Born structure are integrable, so is the third one.

Note that if a Born structure is integrable, then
(h,J)
is a pseudo-Kähler structure. The structure
(\omega,L_{+},L_{-})
is what we will call a
bi-Lagrangian structure
:

Definition 2.6
.

An
almost bi-Lagrangian structure
is an almost symplectic form
\omega
together with a pair of complementary Lagrangian distributions
L_{\pm}
. The almost bi-Lagrangian structure
(\omega,L_{+},L_{-})
is called
integrable
or
bi-Lagrangian structure
if
\omega
is closed and the distributions
L_{\pm}
are integrable to Lagrangian foliations
\mathcal{L}_{\pm}
.

Remark 2.7
.

Bi-Lagrangian structures appear in the literature also under the name
Künneth structures

[
22
]
. Furthermore, a bi-Lagrangian structure is equivalent to a
para-Kähler structure
, i.e. a pair
(g,A)
of a neutral metric
g
and an almost product structure
A
such that
g(A\cdot,A\cdot)=-g(\cdot,\cdot)
and such that
A
is parallel with respect to the Levi-Civita connection of
g
.

In particular, integrability of the Born structure implies that
\nabla^{g}\omega=0
,
\nabla^{h}\omega=0
and
\nabla^{h}J=0
, where
\nabla^{g}
and
\nabla^{h}
denote the Levi-Civita connections of
g
and
h
, respectively. Furthermore, given an integrable Born structure the recursion operators
(J,A)
form a
complex product structure
, i.e. a pair of a complex and a product structure that anti-commute.

In this project we will study constructions in order to obtain examples of left-invariant Born structures on Lie groups. For those, it suffices to work at the level of the Lie algebra. Born structures on Lie algebras were already introduced in
[
23
]
. The definition of a Born structure on a Lie algebra is transcribed from Definition

in the obvious way:

Definition 2.8

(
[
23
]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra. A
Born structure
on
\mathfrak{g}
is a triple
(g,h,\omega)
, where
g
and
h
are pseudo-Riemannian metrics and
\omega
is a non-degenerate two-form such that the recursion operators
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-J}

satisfy
-J^{2}=A^{2}=B^{2}=\operatorname{Id}
. The Born structure is called

if its two-form is closed under the Chevalley-Eilenberg differential, the endomorphism

has vanishing Nijenhuis tensor and the eigenspaces of

are subalgebras of

. A Lie algebra with an integrable Born structure is called

.

Clearly, Proposition
2.2
carries over to the Lie algebra setting. We will denote the eigenspaces of

by
\mathfrak{g}_{\pm}
. For these, an analogous statement to Corollary

holds. Given a Lie group

, an integrable Born structure on its Lie algebra in the sense of Definition

gives rise to a left-invariant integrable Born structure on

in the sense of Definition
2.4
.

We furthermore introduce the following notion of equivalence of Born structures on Lie algebras:

Definition 2.9
.

Two Born structures
(g,h,\omega)
and
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
on Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
are
equivalent
, if there is an isomorphism of Lie algebras
\Phi\colon\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
such that

\displaystyle\Phi^{*}h^{\prime}=h,\quad\Phi^{*}g^{\prime}=g,\quad\Phi^{*}\omega^{\prime}=\omega.

Sometimes, it will be more useful to work with the following equivalent integrability condition for a Born structure on a Lie algebra:

Proposition 2.10
.

A Born structure on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is integrable if and only if the eigenspaces of
A
are subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
and
J
is parallel with respect to the Levi-Civita connection of
h
.

In particular, this yields the following equivalent description of an integrable Born structure on a Lie algebra:

Corollary 2.11
.

An integrable Born structure on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is equivalent to a pseudo-Kähler structure
(h,J)
on
\mathfrak{g}
together with a pair of complementary subalgebras
\mathfrak{g}_{\pm}
that are orthogonal with respect to
h
and interchanged by
J
.

In view of Corollary
2.11
we will sometimes denote a Born structure by the tuple
(h,J,\mathfrak{g}_{\pm})
rather than the three bilinear forms
(g,h,\omega)
.

A first example of a Born Lie algebra is the following:

Example 2.12
.

Every even-dimensional abelian Lie algebra is Born. An integrable Born structure can be constructed from the standard Kähler structure with a canonical choice of subalgebras
\mathfrak{g}_{\pm}
(see
[
23
]
).

3
Born Lie algebras as bicross products

In this section we show that every Born Lie algebra can be obtained from two pseudo-Riemannian Lie algebras of the same dimension and signature using
bicross products
.

Definition 3.1

(
[
29
,
1
]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{+},\mathfrak{g}_{-}
be real finite-dimensional Lie algebras and let
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{g}_{-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g}_{+})
and
\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g}_{-})
be representations satisfying

\displaystyle\varphi(X_{-})[X_{+},Y_{+}]_{+}-[\varphi(X_{-})X_{+},Y_{+}]_{+}-[X_{+},\varphi(X_{-})Y_{+}]_{+}+\varphi(\rho(X_{+})X_{-})Y_{+}-\varphi(\rho(Y_{+})X_{-})X_{+}

\displaystyle=0,

(1)

\displaystyle\rho(X_{+})[X_{-},Y_{-}]_{-}-[\rho(X_{+})X_{-},Y_{-}]_{-}-[X_{-},\rho(X_{+})Y_{-}]_{-}+\rho(\varphi(X_{-})X_{+})Y_{-}-\rho(\varphi(Y_{-})X_{+})X_{-}

\displaystyle=0

for all
X_{+},Y_{+}\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
and
X_{-},Y_{-}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-}
, where
[\cdot,\cdot]_{+}
and
[\cdot,\cdot]_{-}
denote the Lie brackets of
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-}
, respectively. The
bicross product

\mathfrak{g}_{\varphi}^{\rho}:=\mathfrak{g}_{+}\bowtie_{\varphi}^{\rho}\mathfrak{g}_{-}
is the Lie algebra with underlying vector space
\mathfrak{g}_{+}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-}
and Lie bracket given by

[X_{+}+X_{-},Y_{+}+Y_{-}]:=[X_{+},Y_{+}]_{+}+\varphi(X_{-})Y_{+}-\varphi(Y_{-})X_{+}+[X_{-},Y_{-}]_{-}+\rho(X_{+})Y_{-}-\rho(Y_{+})X_{-}

for
X_{+},Y_{+}\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
and
X_{-},Y_{-}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-}
.

Remark 3.2
.

The Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-}
are Lie subalgebras of the bicross product
\mathfrak{g}_{\varphi}^{\rho}=\mathfrak{g}_{+}\bowtie_{\varphi}^{\rho}\mathfrak{g}_{-}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-}
be real Lie algebras of the same dimension endowed with pseudo-Riemannian metrics
h_{+}
and
h_{-}
of the same signature. Let furthermore
Q\colon\mathfrak{g}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{-}
be a linear isometry from
(\mathfrak{g}_{+},h_{+})
to
(\mathfrak{g}_{-},h_{-})
. Then there is a (possibly non-integrable) Born structure on
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{g}_{+}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-}
, with
h:=h_{+}\oplus h_{-}
and
J
given by

\displaystyle J:=\begin{pmatrix}0&-Q^{-1}\\
Q&0\end{pmatrix}

in the splitting
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{+}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-}
. In particular, given a pair of representations
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{g}_{-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g}_{+})
and
\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g}_{-})
satisfying the conditions in Definition
3.1
, this Born structure gives rise to a (possibly non-integrable) Born structure on the bicross product
\mathfrak{g}_{\varphi}^{\rho}:=\mathfrak{g}_{+}\bowtie_{\varphi}^{\rho}\mathfrak{g}_{-}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
,
h
and
J
be as defined in Proposition
3.3
. Then clearly
J
interchanges
\mathfrak{g}_{\pm}
and
J^{2}=-\mathrm{Id}
. Moreover, by the definition of
h
, the subspaces
\mathfrak{g}_{\pm}
are orthogonal with respect to
h
and since
Q
is an isometry,
h
is invariant under
J
. Hence,
(h,J,\mathfrak{g}_{\pm})
define a Born structure on
\mathfrak{g}
and therefore also on
\mathfrak{g}_{\varphi}^{\rho}:=\mathfrak{g}_{+}\bowtie_{\varphi}^{\rho}\mathfrak{g}_{-}
.
∎

Definition 3.4
.

We call the Born structure on
\mathfrak{g}_{\varphi}^{\rho}=\mathfrak{g}_{+}\bowtie_{\varphi}^{\rho}\mathfrak{g}_{-}
obtained as in Proposition
3.3
the
Born structure associated with the data

(\mathfrak{g}_{\pm},h_{\pm},Q,\varphi,\rho)
.

Remark 3.5
.

Note that given the metric
h_{-}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{-}
and the isometry
Q\colon\mathfrak{g}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{-}
, the metric
h_{+}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
is completely determined by
h_{+}=Q^{*}h_{-}
.

Next, we study under which conditions on the representations
\varphi
and
\rho
the Born structure associated with the data
(\mathfrak{g}_{\pm},h_{\pm},Q,\varphi,\rho)
is integrable. For this, we first need to compute the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{h}
of the metric
h:=h_{+}\oplus h_{-}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{\varphi}^{\rho}
.

Given a pseudo-Riemannian Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},h)
and an endomorphism
f\colon\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
, we denote by
f^{*}
the adjoint operator of
f
, i.e.
h(f\cdot,\cdot)=h(\cdot,f^{*}\cdot)
. Moreover, we write
f=f^{s}+f^{a}
, where
f^{s}=\frac{1}{2}(f+f^{*})
denotes the
h
-symmetric part of
f
and
f^{a}=\frac{1}{2}(f-f^{*})
denotes the
h
-antisymmetric part of
f
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{g}_{\varphi}^{\rho}
. Then the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{h}
of
h
is given by:

1.

h(\nabla^{h}_{X_{+}}Y_{+},Z)=h_{+}(\nabla^{+}_{X_{+}}Y_{+},Z_{+})+h_{+}(\varphi(Z_{-})^{s}X_{+},Y_{+})
.

2.

\nabla^{h}_{X_{+}}Y_{-}=-\varphi(Y_{-})^{s}X_{+}+\rho(X_{+})^{a}Y_{-}
.

3.

\nabla^{h}_{X_{-}}Y_{+}=\varphi(X_{-})^{a}Y_{+}-\rho(Y_{+})^{s}X_{-}
.

4.

h(\nabla^{h}_{X_{-}}Y_{-},Z)=h_{-}(\nabla^{-}_{X_{-}}Y_{-},Z_{-})+h_{-}(\rho(Z_{+})^{s}X_{-},Y_{-})
.

Here, the subscripts
\pm
denote the projections to
\mathfrak{g}_{\pm}
, respectively, and
\nabla^{\pm}
denote the Levi-Civita connections of
h_{\pm}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{\pm}
.

Proof.

Recall the Koszul formula for the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{h}
:

2h(\nabla^{h}_{u}v,w)=h([u,v],w)-h(v,[u,w])-h(u,[v,w])

for
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}_{\varphi}^{\rho}
. Let
u=X_{+}
,
v=Y_{+}
and
w=Z=Z_{+}+Z_{-}
. Then we have:

\displaystyle 2h(\nabla^{h}_{X_{+}}Y_{+},Z)

\displaystyle=h([X_{+},Y_{+}],Z)-h(Y_{+},[X_{+},Z])-h(X_{+},[Y_{+},Z])

\displaystyle=h([X_{+},Y_{+}]_{+},Z)

\displaystyle\quad-h(Y_{+},[X_{+},Z_{+}]_{+}-\varphi(Z_{-})X_{+}+\rho(X_{+})Z_{-})

\displaystyle\quad-h(X_{+},[Y_{+},Z_{+}]_{+}-\varphi(Z_{-})Y_{+}+\rho(Y_{+})Z_{-})

\displaystyle=h_{+}([X_{+},Y_{+}]_{+},Z_{+})-h_{+}(Y_{+},[X_{+},Z_{+}]_{+})-h_{+}(X_{+},[Y_{+},Z_{+}])

\displaystyle\quad+h_{+}(Y_{+},\varphi(Z_{-})X_{+})+h_{+}(X_{+},\varphi(Z_{-})Y_{+})

\displaystyle=2h_{+}(\nabla^{+}_{X_{+}}Y_{+},Z_{+})+2h_{+}(\varphi(Z_{-})^{s}X_{+},Y_{+}),

hence
h(\nabla^{h}_{X_{+}}Y_{+},Z)=h_{+}(\nabla^{+}_{X_{+}}Y_{+},Z_{+})+h_{+}(\varphi(Z_{-})^{s}X_{+},Y_{+})
. Similarly we compute

\displaystyle 2h(\nabla^{h}_{X_{+}}Y_{-},Z)

\displaystyle=h([X_{+},Y_{-}],Z)-h(Y_{-},[X_{+},Z])-h(X_{+},[Y_{-},Z])

\displaystyle=h(-\varphi(Y_{-})X_{+}+\rho(X_{+})Y_{-},Z)

\displaystyle\quad-h(Y_{-},[X_{+},Z_{+}]_{+}-\varphi(Z_{-})X_{+}+\rho(X_{+})Z_{-})

\displaystyle\quad-h(X_{+},\varphi(Y_{-})Z_{+}+[Y_{-},Z_{-}]_{-}-\rho(Z_{+})Y_{-})

\displaystyle=-h_{+}(\varphi(Y_{-})X_{+},Z_{+})+h_{-}(\rho(X_{+})Y_{-},Z_{-})

\displaystyle\quad-h_{-}(Y_{-},\rho(X_{+})Z_{-})-h_{+}(X_{+},\varphi(Y_{-})Z_{+})

\displaystyle=-2h(\varphi(Y_{-})^{s}X_{+},Z_{+})+2h_{-}(\rho(X_{+})^{a}Y_{-},Z_{-})

\displaystyle=2h(-\varphi(Y_{-})^{s}X_{+}+\rho(X_{+})^{a}Y_{-},Z).

To obtain the formula for
\nabla^{h}_{X_{-}}Y_{+}
we use the fact that
\nabla^{h}
is torsion-free. Finally, the formula for
\nabla^{h}_{X_{-}}Y_{-}
is obtained by an analogous computation to that of the formula for
\nabla^{h}_{X_{+}}Y_{+}
.
∎

By Proposition
2.10
, to show that the Born structure associated with the data
(\mathfrak{g}_{\pm},h_{\pm},Q,\varphi,\rho)
is integrable it suffices to show under which conditions on
\varphi
and
\rho
the complex structure
J
is
\nabla^{h}
-parallel. This is the content of the following result.

Theorem 3.7
.

The Born structure associated with the data
(\mathfrak{g}_{\pm},h_{\pm},Q,\varphi,\rho)
is integrable if and only if

1.

\varphi(X_{-})^{a}=Q^{-1}\nabla^{-}_{X_{-}}Q
for all
X_{-}\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
.

2.

\varphi(QX_{+})^{s}Y_{+}=\varphi(QY_{+})^{s}X_{+}
for all
X_{+},Y_{+}\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
.

3.

\rho(X_{+})^{a}=Q\nabla^{+}_{X_{+}}Q^{-1}
for all
X_{+}\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
.

4.

\rho(X_{+})^{s}QY_{+}=\rho(Y_{+})^{s}QX_{+}
for all
X_{+},Y_{+}\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
.

Proof.

As explained above, to show that the Born structure is integrable it suffices to show that
\nabla^{h}J=0
. We will make use of Lemma
3.6
throughout the proof. We have to consider four different cases:

• Case
X_{+},Y_{+}\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
: We compute

h((\nabla^{h}_{X_{+}}J)Y_{+},Z)=h(\nabla^{h}_{X_{+}}JY_{+},Z)-h(J\nabla^{h}_{X_{+}}Y_{+},Z),

where

\displaystyle h(\nabla^{h}_{X_{+}}JY_{+},Z)

\displaystyle=h(\nabla^{h}_{X_{+}}QY_{+},Z)=h(-\varphi(QY_{+})^{s}X_{+}+\rho(X_{+})^{a}QY_{+},Z)

\displaystyle=-h_{+}(\varphi(QY_{+})^{s}X_{+},Z_{+})+h_{-}(\rho(X_{+})^{a}QY_{+},Z_{-})

\displaystyle h(J\nabla^{h}_{X_{+}}Y_{+},Z)

\displaystyle=-h(\nabla^{h}_{X_{+}}Y_{+},J(Z))=-h(\nabla^{h}_{X_{+}}Y_{+},-Q^{-1}Z_{-}+QZ_{+})

\displaystyle=h_{+}(\nabla^{+}_{X_{+}}Y_{+},Q^{-1}Z_{-})-h_{+}(\varphi(QZ_{+})^{s}X_{+},Y_{+}).

Hence

\displaystyle h((\nabla^{h}_{X_{+}}J)Y_{+},Z)

\displaystyle=-h_{+}(\nabla^{+}_{X_{+}}Y_{+},Q^{-1}Z_{-})+h_{-}(\rho(X_{+})^{a}QY_{+},Z_{-})

\displaystyle\quad-h_{+}(X_{+},\varphi(QY_{+})^{s}Z_{+}-\varphi(QZ_{+})^{s}Y_{+}).

This is zero if and only if
\varphi(QY_{+})^{s}Z_{+}=\varphi(QZ_{+})^{s}Y_{+}
for all
Y_{+},Z_{+}\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
and
\rho(X_{+})^{a}=Q\nabla^{+}_{X_{+}}Q^{-1}
for all
X_{+}\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
since
h_{+}(\nabla^{+}_{X_{+}}Y_{+},Q^{-1}Z_{-})=h_{-}(Q\nabla^{+}_{X_{+}}Y_{+},Z_{-})
.

• Case
X_{+}\in\mathfrak{g}_{+},Y_{-}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-}
: We compute

h((\nabla^{h}_{X_{+}}J)Y_{-},Z)=h(\nabla^{h}_{X_{+}}JY_{-},Z)-h(J\nabla^{h}_{X_{+}}Y_{-},Z),

where

\displaystyle h(\nabla^{h}_{X_{+}}JY_{-},Z)

\displaystyle=-h(\nabla^{h}_{X_{+}}Q^{-1}Y_{-},Z)

\displaystyle=-h_{+}(\nabla^{+}_{X_{+}}Q^{-1}Y_{-},Z_{+})-h_{+}(\varphi(Z_{-})^{s}X_{+},Q^{-1}Y_{-})

\displaystyle=-h_{+}(Q^{-1}\rho(X_{+})^{a}Y_{-},Z_{+})-h_{+}(X_{+},\varphi(Z_{-})^{s}Q^{-1}Y_{-}),

\displaystyle h(J\nabla^{h}_{X_{+}}Y_{-},Z)

\displaystyle=h(J(-\varphi(Y_{-})^{s}X_{+}+\rho(X_{+})^{a}Y_{-}),Z)

\displaystyle=-h_{-}(Q\varphi(Y_{-})^{s}X_{+},Z_{-})-h_{+}(Q^{-1}\rho(X_{+})^{a}Y_{-},Z_{+})

\displaystyle=-h_{+}(X_{+},\varphi(Y_{-})^{s}Q^{-1}Z_{-})-h_{+}(Q^{-1}\rho(X_{+})^{a}Y_{-},Z_{+}).

Hence

h((\nabla^{h}_{X_{+}}J)Y_{-},Z)=-h_{+}(X_{+},\varphi(Z_{-})^{s}Q^{-1}Y_{-}-\varphi(Y_{-})^{s}Q^{-1}Z_{-}).

This is zero if and only if
\varphi(Z_{-})^{s}Q^{-1}Y_{-}=\varphi(Y_{-})^{s}Q^{-1}Z_{-}
for all
Y_{-},Z_{-}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-}
. Setting
\tilde{Y}_{+}=Q^{-1}Y_{-}
and
\tilde{Z}_{+}=Q^{-1}Z_{-}
we obtain
\varphi(Q\tilde{Z}_{+})^{s}\tilde{Y}_{+}=\varphi(Q\tilde{Y}_{+})^{s}\tilde{Z}_{+}
, which is the condition obtained before.

• Case
X_{-}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-},Y_{+}\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
: We compute

h((\nabla^{h}_{X_{-}}J)Y_{+},Z)=h(\nabla^{h}_{X_{-}}JY_{+},Z)-h(J\nabla^{h}_{X_{-}}Y_{+},Z),

where

\displaystyle h(\nabla^{h}_{X_{-}}JY_{+},Z)

\displaystyle=h(\nabla^{h}_{X_{-}}QY_{+},Z)

\displaystyle=h_{-}(\nabla^{-}_{X_{-}}QY_{+},Z_{-})+h_{-}(\rho(Z_{+})^{s}X_{-},QY_{+}),

\displaystyle h(J\nabla^{h}_{X_{-}}Y_{+},Z)

\displaystyle=h(J(\varphi(X_{-})^{a}Y_{+}-\rho(Y_{+})^{s}X_{-}),Z)

\displaystyle=h_{-}(Q\varphi(X_{-})^{a}Y_{+},Z_{-})+h_{+}(Q^{-1}\rho(Y_{+})^{s}X_{-},Z_{+}).

This is zero if and only if
\varphi(X_{-})^{a}=Q^{-1}\nabla^{-}_{X_{-}}Q
for all
X_{-}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-}
and
\rho(Z_{+})^{s}QY_{+}=\rho(Y_{+})^{s}QZ_{+}
for all
Y_{+},Z_{+}\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
.

• Case
X_{-},Y_{-}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-}
: A similar computation as in the previous cases gives us again the conditions
\varphi(X_{-})^{a}=Q^{-1}\nabla^{-}_{X_{-}}Q
for all
X_{-}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-}
and
\rho(Z_{+})^{s}QY_{+}=\rho(Y_{+})^{s}QZ_{+}
for all
Y_{+},Z_{+}\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
.
∎

Conversely, every Born Lie algebra can be obtained by some data
(\mathfrak{g}_{\pm},h_{\pm},Q,\varphi,\rho)
:

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Born Lie algebra. Then for every integrable Born structure
(h,J,\mathfrak{g}_{\pm})
on
\mathfrak{g}
there exist a pair of representations
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{g}_{-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g}_{+})
and
\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g}_{-})
and a linear isometry
Q\colon(\mathfrak{g}_{+},h_{+}:=h|_{\mathfrak{g}_{+}})\longrightarrow(\mathfrak{g}_{-},h_{-}:=h|_{\mathfrak{g}_{-}})
such that
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{g}_{+}\bowtie_{\varphi}^{\rho}\mathfrak{g}_{-}
and
(h,J,\mathfrak{g}_{\pm})
is equivalent to the Born structure associated with the data
(\mathfrak{g}_{\pm},h_{\pm},Q,\varphi,\rho)
.

Proof.

Let
(h,J,\mathfrak{g}_{\pm})
be an integrable Born structure on
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-}
are orthogonal with respect to
h
and
h_{+}:=h|_{\mathfrak{g}_{+}}
and
h_{-}:=h|_{\mathfrak{g}_{-}}
define pseudo-Riemannian metrics on
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-}
, respectively. Furthermore, we know that
J
interchanges
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-}
. We set
Q:=J|_{\mathfrak{g}_{+}}\colon\mathfrak{g}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{-}
. Then it follows from
h(J\cdot,J\cdot)=h(\cdot,\cdot)
that
Q
is a linear isometry with respect to
h_{+}
and
h_{-}
, and it follows from
J^{2}=-\mathrm{Id}
that

J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-Q^{-1}\\
Q&0\end{pmatrix}.

(2)

Since
(h,J,\mathfrak{g}_{\pm})
is integrable, the subspaces
\mathfrak{g}_{\pm}
are Lie subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
. Moreover, we have
\mathfrak{g}_{+}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-}=\mathfrak{g}
. We define

\displaystyle\varphi\colon\mathfrak{g}_{-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g}_{+}),\quad\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g}_{-})

by setting

\displaystyle\varphi(X)Y:=[X,Y]_{+},\quad\rho(Y)X:=[Y,X]_{-}

for
X\in\mathfrak{g}_{-}
,
Y\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
, where
[\cdot,\cdot]
denotes the Lie bracket of
\mathfrak{g}
. We claim that
\varphi
and
\rho
are Lie algebra representations. Indeed, for
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}_{-}
,
Z\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
, we have

\displaystyle\varphi\left([X,Y]\right)Z=

\displaystyle\left[[X,Y],Z\right]_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\left[X,[Y,Z]\right]+\left[Y,[Z,X]\right]\right)_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[X,[Y,Z]_{+}\right]_{+}+\left[Y,[Z,X]_{+}\right]_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\varphi(X),\varphi(Y)\right]Z,

where we used the Jacobi identity for the second equality and that
\mathfrak{g}_{-}
is a subalgebra for the third equality. This shows that
\varphi
is a Lie algebra representation. The proof for
\rho
works analogously. Clearly, we have
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{+}\bowtie_{\varphi}^{\rho}\mathfrak{g}_{-}
as Lie algebras. By construction the Born structures are equivalent.
∎

Taking one of the representations to be the trivial representation, say
\rho\equiv 0
, the bicross product reduces to the well-known
semidirect product
. Therefore, by Proposition
3.3
, given two Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-}
with pseudo-Riemannian metrics
h_{+}
and
h_{-}
, a linear isometry
Q\colon(\mathfrak{g}_{+},h_{+})\longrightarrow(\mathfrak{g}_{-},h_{-})
and a representation
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{g}_{-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g}_{+})
, we can construct a Born structure on
\mathfrak{g}_{+}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{g}_{-}
, the
Born structure associated with the data

(\mathfrak{g}_{\pm},h_{\pm},Q,\varphi)
. In this case Theorem
3.7
reduces to the following statement:

Theorem 3.9
.

The Born structure associated with the data
(\mathfrak{g}_{\pm},h_{\pm},Q,\varphi)
is integrable if and only if

1.

\mathfrak{g}_{+}
is abelian.

2.

\varphi(X_{-})^{a}=Q^{-1}\nabla^{-}_{X_{-}}Q
for all
X_{-}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-}
.

3.

\varphi(QX_{+})^{s}Y_{+}=\varphi(QY_{+})^{s}X_{+}
for all
X_{+},Y_{+}\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
.

Here
\nabla^{\pm}
denotes the Levi-Civita connection of
h_{\pm}
.

As an application of Theorem
3.9
we show that we can construct an integrable Born structure from any flat pseudo-Riemannian Lie algebra.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g}_{-},h_{-})
be a flat pseudo-Riemannian Lie algebra of dimension
n
. Let
\mathfrak{g}_{+}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
h_{+}=Q^{*}h_{-}
, where
Q\colon\mathfrak{g}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{-}
is an isomorphism of vector spaces, and define
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{g}_{-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g}_{+})
by
\varphi(X_{-})=Q^{-1}\nabla^{-}_{X_{-}}Q
. Then the Born structure on
\mathfrak{g}_{+}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{g}_{-}
associated with the data
(\mathfrak{g}_{\pm},h_{\pm},Q,\varphi)
is integrable. Moreover, the metric
h
is flat.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of Theorem
3.9
by noticing that such
\varphi(X_{-})
is
h_{+}
-antisymmetric for all
X_{-}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-}
and
\varphi
is a representation since
\nabla^{-}
is flat. The metric
h=h_{+}\oplus h_{-}
is flat by the Azencott-Wilson theorem (see
[
10
, Proposition 1.13]
) since in this case the pseudo-Riemannian Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{+}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{g}_{-},h)
is linearly isometric to the direct sum Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{+}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-},h)
, which is flat.
∎

Using a similar strategy, we may also construct many
pseudo-hyperkähler
Lie algebras:

Definition 3.11
.

A
pseudo-hyperkähler structure

(h,J,I)
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a pair of anti-commuting complex structures
J
and
I
that are both Kähler for a pseudo-Riemannian metric
h
.

In order to construct pseudo-hyperkähler Lie algebras, we start with an even-dimensional flat pseudo-Kähler Lie algebra:

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g}_{-},h_{-},I_{-})
be a flat pseudo-Kähler Lie algebra of dimension
2n
. Let
\mathfrak{g}_{+}=\mathbb{R}^{2n}
,
h_{+}=Q^{*}h_{-}
,
I_{+}=-Q^{-1}I_{-}Q
, where
Q\colon\mathfrak{g}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{-}
is an isomorphism of vector spaces, and define the representation
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{g}_{-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g}_{+})
by
\varphi(X_{-})=Q^{-1}\nabla^{-}_{X_{-}}Q
. Set

h=\begin{pmatrix}h_{+}&0\\
0&h_{-}\end{pmatrix},\quad J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-Q^{-1}\\
Q&0\end{pmatrix},\quad I=\begin{pmatrix}I_{+}&0\\
0&I_{-}\end{pmatrix}.

Then
(h,J,I)
is a pseudo-hyperkähler structure on
\mathfrak{g}_{+}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{g}_{-}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{\pm}
,
\varphi
,
h
,
I
and
J
as in Proposition
3.12
. Then it is easy to check that
(h,J,I)
is an almost pseudo-hyperhermitian structure on
\mathfrak{g}_{+}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{g}_{-}
. We know by Theorem
3.9
that with the above choices,
\nabla^{h}J=0
. Moreover, by
[
10
, Theorem 1.3]
we know that
\nabla^{h}I=0
for such
\varphi
if and only if
I_{+}\circ\varphi(X_{-})=\varphi(X_{-})\circ I_{+}
. However, this condition is satisfied automatically since
\nabla^{-}I_{-}=0
.
∎

Similarly, we can construct many examples of hypersymplectic Lie algebras:

Definition 3.13
.

A
hypersymplectic structure
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a triple
(h,J,E)
such that
(h,J)
is pseudo-Kähler,
(h,E)
is bi-Lagrangian and
J

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g}_{-},h_{-},E_{-})
be a flat bi-Lagrangian Lie algebra of dimension
2n
. Let
\mathfrak{g}_{+}=\mathbb{R}^{2n}
,
h_{+}=Q^{*}h_{-}
,
E_{+}=-Q^{-1}E_{-}Q
, where
Q\colon\mathfrak{g}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{-}

\varphi\colon\mathfrak{g}_{-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g}_{+})
by
\varphi(X_{-})=Q^{-1}\nabla^{-}_{X_{-}}Q
. Set

h=\begin{pmatrix}h_{+}&0\\

Then
(h,J,E)
is a hypersymplectic structure on
\mathfrak{g}_{+}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{g}_{-}
.

The definition of hypersymplectic structures that we use here is equivalent to the one presented in

4
Born Lie algebras in low dimensions

In this section, we classify all Lie algebras up to dimension four and all nilpotent Lie algebras in dimension six admitting an integrable Born structure.

4.1
Two-dimensional classification

The only two-dimensional Lie algebras are

\mathbb{R}^{2}=\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{r}_{2}=\mathfrak{aff}(\mathbb{R})=\langle e_{1},e_{2}\mid[e_{1},e_{2}]=e_{2}\rangle.

By Example
2.12
, the abelian Lie algebra
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is Born. The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{r}_{2}
admits the following Born structure:

h=e^{1}\otimes e^{1}+e^{2}\otimes e^{2},\quad Je_{1}=e_{2},\quad\mathfrak{g}_{+}=\langle e_{1}\rangle,\quad\mathfrak{g}_{-}=\langle e_{2}\rangle.

Note that in two dimensions, any Born structure is automatically integrable. We conclude that every two-dimensional Lie algebra is Born.

4.2
Four-dimensional classification

4.3
Six-dimensional nilpotent classification

In this section we apply the bicross product construction introduced in Section
3
in order to obtain a classification of all six-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebras admitting an integrable Born structure. Our aim is to prove the following:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a non-abelian six-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra. Then
\mathfrak{g}
possesses an integrable Born structure
(h,J,\mathfrak{g}_{\pm})
if and only if
\mathfrak{g}
is isomorphic to one of the following Lie algebras:

\begin{aligned} \mathfrak{h}_{4}&=(0,0,0,0,12,14+23),\\
\mathfrak{h}_{7}&=(0,0,0,12,13,23),\\
\mathfrak{h}_{8}&=(0,0,0,0,0,12),\\
\mathfrak{h}_{9}&=(0,0,0,0,12,14+25),\end{aligned}\qquad\begin{aligned} \mathfrak{h}_{10}&=(0,0,0,12,13,14),\\
\mathfrak{h}_{11}&=(0,0,0,12,13,14+23),\\
\mathfrak{h}_{13}&=(0,0,0,12,13+14,24).\end{aligned}

Here we make use of Salamon’s notation
[
36
]
to denote Lie algebras. For instance
\mathfrak{g}=(0,0,0,12,13,14)
means that there is a basis
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{6}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}
with dual basis
\{e^{1},\ldots,e^{6}\}
such that
\mathrm{d}e^{1}=\mathrm{d}e^{2}=\mathrm{d}e^{3}=0
,
\mathrm{d}e^{4}=e^{12}
,
\mathrm{d}e^{5}=e^{13}
and
\mathrm{d}e^{6}=e^{14}
, where
e^{ij}:=e^{i}\wedge e^{j}
.

.

The fact that the Lie algebras
\mathfrak{h}_{4}

\mathfrak{h}_{8}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{9}
in Theorem

admit an integrable Born structure was already proved in
[

]
by direct computation, that is by providing explicitly such structure for each Lie algebra.

For six-dimensional nilpotent Born Lie algebras, the subalgebras

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}

\mathfrak{g}_{+}\cong\mathfrak{g}_{-}\cong\mathfrak{heis}_{3}
. Then there is another integrable Born structure
(h,J,\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm})
on
\mathfrak{g}
with the same pseudo-Riemannian metric
h
and complex structure
J
and
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{+}\cong\mathfrak{heis}_{3}
but
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{-}\cong\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Conversely given an integrable Born structure
(h,J,\mathfrak{g}_{\pm})
on
\mathfrak{g}
with
\mathfrak{g}_{+}\cong\mathfrak{heis}_{3}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-}\cong\mathbb{R}^{3}
there is another integrable Born structure
(h,J,\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm})
on
\mathfrak{g}
with
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{+}\cong\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{-}\cong\mathfrak{heis}_{3}
.

Proof.

It is shown in
[
2
, Proposition 5.1]
that given a complex product structure
(J,A)
on a six-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra such that the subalgebras coming from the eigenbundles of
A
are both isomorphic to
\mathfrak{heis}_{3}
, there is a
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
such that
(J,A_{\theta})
is another complex product structure with corresponding subalgebras
\mathfrak{g}_{+}^{\theta}\cong\mathfrak{heis}_{3}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}_{-}\cong\mathbb{R}^{3}
, where

\displaystyle A_{\theta}=\cos\theta\,A+\sin\theta\,JA=\cos\theta\,A+\sin\theta\,B.

Suppose now that for a pseudo-Riemannian metric
h
on
\mathfrak{g}
,
(h,J,\mathfrak{g}_{\pm})
is an integrable Born structure on
\mathfrak{g}
. Using the relations presented in Table
1
, we observe that for every
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
it holds

\displaystyle h(A_{\theta}X,A_{\theta}Y)=

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\theta\,h(AX,AY)+\cos^{2}\theta h(BX,BY)+\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\left(h(AX,BY)+h(BX,AY)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sin^{2}\theta+\cos^{2}\theta)\,h(X,Y)+\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\left(h(X,ABY)+h(X,BAY)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h(X,Y)+\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\left(h(X,ABY)+h(X,-ABY)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h(X,Y).

Therefore, setting
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm}:=\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}_{\pm}
,
(h,J,\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm})
is an integrable Born structure on
\mathfrak{g}
. The proof of the converse statement works similarly using again
[
2
, Proposition 5.1]
.
∎

Thanks to Lemma
4.6
, in order to classify all six-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebras admitting an integrable Born structure, it is enough to consider the cases where only one subalgebra is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{heis}_{3}
or both subalgebras are abelian. We study each case separately.

Remark 4.7
.

Positive-definite Kähler nilpotent Lie algebras are necessarily abelian by
[
24
]
(see also
[
14
]
). Recall that the metric
h
of the Born structure is given by
h=h_{+}\oplus h_{-}
, where
h_{\pm}
are pseudo-Riemannian metrics on the Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}_{\pm}
. Therefore, in order to construct non-abelian six-dimensional nilpotent Born Lie algebras, we only consider indefinite metrics
h_{\pm}
on the three-dimensional Lie algebras
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\mathfrak{heis}_{3}
.

4.3.1
Case
\mathbb{R}^{3}\bowtie\mathfrak{heis}_{3}

First we show that the only metric that we can consider on
\mathfrak{heis}_{3}
is the flat one.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
h_{-}
be a Lorentzian metric on
\mathfrak{g}_{-}\cong\mathfrak{heis}_{3}
,
Q\colon\mathfrak{g}_{+}\cong\mathbb{R}^{3}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{-}
an isomorphism of vector spaces and set
h_{+}:=Q^{*}h_{-}
. Then there are representations
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{g}_{-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g}_{+})
and
\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g}_{-})
satisfying the conditions in Theorem
3.7
only if
h_{-}
is flat.

Proof.

Lorentzian metrics on
\mathfrak{heis}_{3}=\langle e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\mid[e_{1},e_{2}]=e_{3}\rangle
have been classified in
[
35
]
, where it is shown that there are three non-isometric Lorentzian metrics:

\displaystyle h_{1}

\displaystyle=-e^{1}\otimes e^{1}+e^{2}\otimes e^{2}+e^{3}\otimes e^{3},

\displaystyle h_{2}

\displaystyle=e^{1}\otimes e^{1}+e^{2}\otimes e^{2}-e^{3}\otimes e^{3},

\displaystyle h_{3}

\displaystyle=e^{1}\otimes e^{1}-e^{2}\otimes e^{2}+e^{2}\odot e^{3},

where
e^{i}\odot e^{j}=e^{i}\otimes e^{j}+e^{j}\otimes e^{i}
. Among these metrics, the only one that is flat is
h_{3}
(see
[
34
]
). Now let
h_{-}
be a Lorentzian metric on
\mathfrak{heis}_{3}
. We choose a basis
\{f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{+}\cong\mathbb{R}^{3}
by setting
f_{i}:=Q^{-1}e_{i}
. With respect to these choices of bases the complex structure
J
on
\mathfrak{g}_{\varphi}^{\rho}
defined by (
2
) just takes the form

J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\mathbbm{1}_{3}\\
\mathbbm{1}_{3}&0\end{pmatrix},

(3)

where
\mathbbm{1}_{3}
denotes the
3\times 3
identity matrix. Suppose that
h_{-}=h_{1}
. Then the covariant derivative endomorphisms are given by

\nabla^{-}_{e_{1}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&-\frac{1}{2}\\
0&\frac{1}{2}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\nabla^{-}_{e_{2}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&-\frac{1}{2}\\
0&0&0\\
-\frac{1}{2}&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\nabla^{-}_{e_{3}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\frac{1}{2}&0\\
-\frac{1}{2}&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

A map
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{g}_{-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g}_{+})
satisfies the conditions in Theorem
3.7
if and only if is of the form

\varphi(e_{1})=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}&x_{2}&x_{3}\\
-x_{2}&x_{5}&x_{6}\\
-x_{3}&x_{6}+1&x_{9}\end{pmatrix},\quad\varphi(e_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}x_{2}&-x_{5}&-x_{6}-1\\
x_{5}&y_{5}&y_{6}\\
x_{6}&y_{6}&z_{6}\end{pmatrix},\quad\varphi(e_{3})=\begin{pmatrix}x_{3}&-x_{6}-1&-x_{9}\\
x_{6}&y_{6}&z_{6}\\
x_{9}&z_{6}&z_{9}\end{pmatrix}.

However, it is not hard to see that such map
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{g}_{-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g}_{+})
is never a representation, that is, it never satisfies
[\varphi(e_{i}),\varphi(e_{j})]=\varphi([e_{i},e_{j}])
for all
i,j
, which in this particular case amounts to
[\varphi(e_{1}),\varphi(e_{2})]=\varphi(e_{3})
and
[\varphi(e_{1}),\varphi(e_{3})]=[\varphi(e_{2}),\varphi(e_{3})]=0
. The same argument applies if we consider the metric
h_{2}
.
∎

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a non-abelian six-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra. Then it admits an integrable Born structure with

if and only if it is isomorphic to one of the following:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{4}

\displaystyle=(0,0,0,0,12,14+23),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{7}

\displaystyle=(0,0,0,12,13,23),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{10}

\displaystyle=(0,0,0,12,13,14),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{11}

\displaystyle=(0,0,0,12,13,14+23),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{13}

\displaystyle=(0,0,0,12,13+14,24).

Proof.

Let us consider
\mathfrak{g}_{-}=\mathfrak{heis}_{3}=\langle e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\mid[e_{1},e_{2}]=e_{3}\rangle
equipped with the flat Lorentzian metric
h_{-}=e^{1}\otimes e^{1}-e^{2}\otimes e^{2}+e^{2}\odot e^{3}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{+}\cong\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Let
Q\colon\mathfrak{g}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{-}
be a vector space isomorphism and set
h_{+}:=Q^{*}h_{-}
. On
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
we choose a basis
\{f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\}
by setting
f_{i}:=Q^{-1}e_{i}
. With respect to these choices of bases, the complex structure
J
on
\mathfrak{g}_{\varphi}^{\rho}
takes the form (
3
). By direct computation, we get that two representations
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{g}_{-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g}_{+})
and
\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g}_{-})
satisfy (
1
) and all the conditions in Theorem
3.7
if and only if they are of the form

\varphi(e_{1})=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&x&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\varphi(e_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}0&x+1&0\\
0&0&0\\
x-1&x_{0}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\varphi(e_{3})=0

and

∎

4.3.2
Case
\mathbb{R}^{3}\bowtie\mathbb{R}^{3}

In this case we can find necessary conditions for the existence of an integrable Born structure.

Lemma 4.10
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is a six-dimensional two-step nilpotent Lie algebra with an integrable Born structure
(g,h,\omega)
such that both Lagrangian subalgebras
\mathfrak{g}_{\pm}
are abelian. Then the center of
\mathfrak{g}
is at least three-dimensional.

Proof.

Let
(g,h,\omega)
be an integrable Born structure on a six-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with recursion operators
A
,
B
and
J
as in Definition
2.8
. Suppose furthermore that the eigenspaces
\mathfrak{g}_{\pm}
of
A
are both abelian subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
. By
[
2
, Theorem 4.1 and Lemma 4.2]
we know that there is a basis
\{f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
and a basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{-}
such that
Je_{i}=f_{i}
and
\langle e_{3},f_{3}\rangle\subset\mathfrak{z}
, where
\mathfrak{z}
denotes the center of
\mathfrak{g}
. We may furthermore assume that
\omega=\sum\omega_{ij}\,e^{i}\wedge f^{j}
and the condition
J^{*}\omega=\omega
implies that
\omega_{ij}=\omega_{ji}
.

Suppose that
\dim\mathfrak{z}=2
, i.e.
\mathfrak{z}=\langle e_{3},f_{3}\rangle
. Then, comparing with the list in Proposition
4.5
, we also have
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\langle e_{3},f_{3}\rangle
. Since
\omega
is closed and
e_{3},f_{3}\in\mathfrak{z}
we find

\displaystyle 0=\mathrm{d}\omega(X,Y,e_{3})=\omega([X,Y],e_{3})\quad\forall X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Similarly, it follows that
\omega([X,Y],f_{3})=0
for every
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Since
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\langle e_{3},f_{3}\rangle
, this implies that
\omega_{33}=0
. Furthermore, using that
\mathfrak{g}_{\pm}
are both abelian, we have

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\omega(e_{1},e_{2},f_{1})=\omega([f_{1},e_{1}],e_{2})+\omega([e_{2},f_{1}],e_{1}),

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\omega(e_{1},e_{2},f_{2})=\omega([f_{2},e_{1}],e_{2})+\omega([f_{2},e_{1}],f_{1}),

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\omega(e_{1},f_{1},f_{2})=\omega([e_{1},f_{1}],f_{2})+\omega([f_{2},e_{1}],f_{1}),

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\omega(e_{2},f_{1},f_{2})=\omega([e_{2},f_{1}],f_{2})+\omega([f_{2},e_{2}],f_{1}).

By the integrability of
J
we know that

\displaystyle 0=N_{J}(e_{1},e_{2})=-J([f_{1},e_{2}]+[e_{1},f_{2}])

and, hence,
[e_{1},f_{2}]=[e_{2},f_{1}]
. We write

\displaystyle[e_{1},f_{1}]=A_{11}\,e_{3}+B_{11}\,f_{3},\quad[e_{1},f_{2}]=[e_{2},f_{1}]=A_{12}\,e_{3}+B_{12}\,f_{3},\quad[e_{2},f_{2}]=A_{22}\,e_{3}+B_{22}\,f_{3}

and find

\displaystyle B_{11}\omega_{23}-B_{12}\omega_{13}=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle B_{12}\omega_{23}-B_{22}\omega_{13}=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle A_{11}\omega_{23}-A_{12}\omega_{13}=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle A_{12}\omega_{23}-A_{22}\omega_{13}=

\displaystyle 0.

Since
\omega
is non-degenerate and
\omega_{33}=0
, we must have
\omega_{13}\neq 0
or
\omega_{23}\neq 0
. If
\omega_{13}\neq 0
and
\omega_{23}=0
, it follows that

\displaystyle B_{12}=0=B_{22}=A_{12}=A_{22}

and therefore

\displaystyle[e_{1},f_{2}]=0=[e_{2},f_{2}]=[f_{1},e_{2}],

i.e.
\langle e_{2},f_{2}\rangle\subset\mathfrak{z}
, a contradiction. Similarly, if
\omega_{13}=0
and
\omega_{23}\neq 0
, it follows that
\langle e_{1},f_{1}\rangle\subset\mathfrak{z}
. Now suppose that booth
\omega_{13}
and
\omega_{23}
are non-zero. Then we may assume that
\omega_{13}=1=\omega_{23}
. This yields

\displaystyle A_{11}=

\displaystyle A_{12}=A_{22},

\displaystyle B_{11}=

\displaystyle B_{12}=B_{22},

which implies
\langle e_{1}-e_{2},f_{1}-f_{2}\rangle\subset\mathfrak{z}
, which again contradicts
\mathfrak{z}=\langle e_{3},f_{3}\rangle
.
∎

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a non-abelian six-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra. Then
\mathfrak{g}
admits an integrable Born structure with both subalgebras
\mathfrak{g}_{\pm}
abelian if and only if either

\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{h}_{8}=(0,0,0,0,0,12)\quad\text{or}\quad\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{h}_{9}=(0,0,0,0,12,14+25).

Proof.

By
[
2
, Section 5.3]
, the only candidates to admit an integrable Born structure with both

abelian are
\mathfrak{h}_{2},\mathfrak{h}_{4},\mathfrak{h}_{5},\mathfrak{h}_{8},\mathfrak{h}_{9}
(the Lie algebra
(0,0,0,0,0,12+34)
does not admit pseudo-Kähler structures). The Lie algebras
\mathfrak{h}_{2},\mathfrak{h}_{4},\mathfrak{h}_{5}
are two-step nilpotent and have two-dimensional center, hence they do not admit an integrable Born structure by Lemma
4.10
. Now let
h_{-}=-e^{1}\otimes e^{1}+e^{2}\otimes e^{2}+e^{3}\otimes e^{3}
be the (unique up to isometry) Lorentzian metric on
\mathfrak{g}_{-}=\langle e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\rangle\cong\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Let
Q\colon\mathfrak{g}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{-}
be a vector space isomorphism and set
h_{+}:=Q^{*}h_{-}
. On
\mathfrak{g}_{+}\cong\mathbb{R}^{3}
we choose a basis
\{f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\}
by setting
f_{i}:=Q^{-1}e_{i}
. With respect to these choices of bases, the complex structure
J
on
\mathfrak{g}_{\varphi}^{\rho}
takes the form (
3
). If we consider
\rho\equiv 0
and the representation
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{g}_{-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g}_{+})
given by

\varphi(e_{1})=\varphi(e_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\

all the conditions of Theorem
3.7
are fulfilled and hence we have an integrable Born structure on
\mathfrak{g}_{\varphi}^{\rho}

\mathfrak{g}_{\varphi}^{\rho}\cong\mathfrak{h}_{8}=(0,0,0,0,0,12)
by comparing numerical invariants using Table
5
. If we consider
\rho\equiv 0
and the representation
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{g}_{-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g}_{+})
defined by

\varphi(e_{1})=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
0&0&1\\
-1&1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\varphi(e_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&-1\\
0&0&-1\\

∎

To obtain Theorem

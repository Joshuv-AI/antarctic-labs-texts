Starobinsky Inflation with T-Model Kähler Geometries
C. Pallis School of Technology, Aristotle University of Thessaloniki, GR-541 24 Thessaloniki, GREECE e-mail address: kpallis@auth.gr

Abstract
We present novel implementations of Starobisky-like inflation within Supergravity adopting Kähler potentials for the inflaton which parameterize hyperbolic geometries known from the T-model inflation. The associated superpotentials are consistent with an R R and a global or gauge U ​ ( 1 ) X U(1)_{X} symmetries. The inflaton is represented by a gauge-singlet or non-singlet superfield and is accompanied by a gauge-singlet superfield successfully stabilized thanks to its compact contribution into the total Kähler potential. Keeping the Kähler manifold intact, a conveniently violated shift symmetry is introduced which allows for a slight variation of the predictions of Starobinsky inflation: The (scalar) spectral index exhibits an upper bound which lies close to its central observational value whereas the constant scalar curvature of the inflaton-sector Kähler manifold increases with the tensor-to-scalar ratio. Keywords : Cosmology, Inflation, Supersymmetric Models PACS codes : 98.80.Cq, 12.60.Jv, 95.30.Cq, 95.30.Sf Published in Universe 11 , no. 3, 75 (2025) Special Issue Particle Physics and Cosmology: A Themed Issue in Honor of Professor Dimitri Nanopoulos

2
SUGRA Framework

We start our investigation presenting the basic formulation of a
scalar theory within SUGRA in Sec.
2.1

2.1
General Set-up

The part of the (Einstein-frame) action within SUGRA which
describes the (complex) scalar fields
z^{\alpha}
coupled minimally to
Einstein gravity can be written as
[
11
]

{\cal A}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-\mathfrak{g}}\left(-\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{R}}+K_{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}D_{\mu}z^{\alpha}D^{\mu}z^{*{\bar{\beta}}}-V_{\rm SUGRA}\right)\,,

(4a)

where
\mathcal{R}
is the space-time Ricci scalar curvature,

\mathfrak{g}
is the determinant of the
Friedmann-Robertson-Walker metric,
g_{\mu\nu}
, with signature

(+,-,-,-)
. Also, summation is taken over the scalar fields

z^{\alpha}
which are denoted by the same symbol of the corresponding
superfield. The kinetic mixing of
z^{\alpha}
is controlled by the
Kähler potential
K
and the relevant metric defined as

K_{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}={K_{,z^{\alpha}z^{*{\bar{\beta}}}}}>0\>\>\>\mbox{with}\>\>\>K^{{\bar{\beta}}{\alpha}}K_{{\alpha}\bar{\gamma}}=\delta^{\bar{\beta}}_{\bar{\gamma}}.

(4b)

Also, the covariant derivatives for the
z^{\alpha}
’s are given by

D_{\mu}z^{\alpha}=\partial_{\mu}z^{\alpha}+igA^{\rm a}_{\mu}T^{\rm a}_{{\alpha}{\beta}}z^{\beta}

(4c)

with
A^{\rm a}_{\mu}
being the vector gauge fields,
g
the
(unified) gauge coupling constant and
T^{\rm a}
with
{\rm a}=1,...,\mbox{\sf\footnotesize dim}G_{\rm GUT}
the generators of a gauge group

G_{\rm GUT}
.

Finally
{\cal A}
contains the SUGRA scalar potential,
V_{\rm SUGRA}
, which is given in terms of
K
, and the superpotential,

W
, by

V_{\rm SUGRA}=V_{\rm F}+V_{\rm D}\>\>\>\mbox{with}\>\>\>V_{\rm F}=e^{K}\left(K^{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}{\rm F}_{\alpha}{\rm F}^{*}_{\bar{\beta}}-3{|W|^{2}}\right)\>\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\>V_{\rm D}=g^{2}\sum_{\rm a}{\rm D}_{\rm a}{\rm D}_{\rm a}/2,

(4d)

where a trivial gauge kinetic function is adopted whereas the F-
and D-terms read

{\rm F}_{\alpha}=W_{,z^{\alpha}}+K_{,z^{\alpha}}W\>\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\>{\rm D}_{\rm a}=z_{\alpha}\left(T_{\rm a}\right)^{\alpha}_{\beta}K^{\beta}\>\>\>\mbox{with}\>\>\>K^{{\alpha}}={K_{,z^{\alpha}}}\,.

(4e)

As we emphasized in Sec.
1
, SI in our work is attained by
deriving
V_{\rm I}
in Eq. (
3
) from
V_{\rm SUGRA}
and not
modifying gravity which remains at the minimal level as shown from
the absence of higher order
\mathcal{R}
terms in Eq. (
4a
).
Therefore our next task is to select conveniently the functions

K
and
W
so that Eqs. (
2
) and (
3
) are reproduced.

2.2
Guidelines

We embark on describing our procedure to obtain the desired

in Eq. (

in Eq. (

2.2.1
Achieving D-Flatness.

Our final aim is the derivation of

in Eq. (
3

V_{\rm F}
in Eq. (
4d

establishment of D-flatness during SI, i.e.,
\langle{V_{\rm D}}\rangle_{\rm I}=0

– where
\langle{Q}\rangle_{\rm I}
symbolizes the value of the quantity
Q

during inflation. Assuming that the gauge non-singlet superfields
are placed at zero during inflation, D-flatness may be attained in
the following two cases:

•

If the inflaton is (the radial part of) a gauge-singlet
superfield
z^{2}:=\Phi
. In this case,
\Phi
has obviously zero
contribution to
V_{\rm D}
.

•

If the inflaton is the radial part of a conjugate pair of
Higgs superfields,
z^{2}:=\Phi
and
z^{3}:=\bar{\Phi}
, in the
fundamental representation of
G_{\rm GUT}
. In a such case – see
Sec.
4.3
below – we obtain
\langle{V_{\rm D}}\rangle_{\rm I}=0
. The same
result can be obtained if
G_{\rm GUT}
is more structured than
U(1)_{X}

employing just one superfield
z^{2}
in the adjoint representation
of
G_{\rm GUT}
and using as inflaton its neutral component – see e.g.
Ref.
[
85
]
.

2.2.2
Selecting the Suitable

Despite the fact that

It assists in determining

•

It can be stabilized at

(5)

The index 2 stems from the fact that

parameterizes the

[

. Note that for

,

[

,

,

,

•

It assures the boundedness of

. Indeed, if we set

, then
\langle{W_{,z^{\alpha}}}\rangle_{\rm I}=0

\langle{K_{,z^{\alpha}}W}\rangle_{\rm I}=0

Obviously, non-vanishing values of the last term may render

•

It generates for

and for monomial
W

numerator of

in Eq. (
3
) via the only term of

which remains “alive”. Indeed, we obtain

V_{\rm I}:=\langle{V_{\rm F}}\rangle_{\rm I}=\langle{e^{K}K^{SS^{*}}|W_{,S}|^{2}}\rangle_{\rm I}.

(6)

Assuming that no mixing terms between
S
and the inflaton exist
in
K
, we obtain
\langle{K^{SS^{*}}}\rangle_{\rm I}=\langle{K_{2}^{SS^{*}}}\rangle_{\rm I}=1
and so the

in Eq. (

) emerges if

has the form

W=SF_{\rm W}(z^{\alpha})~~~\mbox{with}~~~\langle{F_{\rm W}}\rangle_{\rm I}=:f_{\rm W}=\lambda\phi^{n/2}~~~\mbox{and}~~~\phi={\mbox{\sf\small Re}}\Phi,

(7)

given that the assumption
\langle{{\mbox{\sf\small Im}}\Phi}\rangle_{\rm I}=0
yields mostly stable
configuration – here we focus on a gauge-singlet
\Phi
. On the
other hand, the denominator of
V_{\rm I}
in Eq. (
3
) may be
generated via the exponential prefactor in Eq. (

– as we explain below.

2.2.3
Selecting the Convenient Kähler Potential.

The form of
K
has to accomplish the following two goals:

•

It has to generate the desired
\phi-\widehat{\phi}
relation in
Eq. (
2
). Therefore, we need to introduce a contribution into

K
including

and

in the same function. After

K_{\rm T}=-N\ln F_{\rm T}(z^{\alpha},z^{*{\bar{\beta}}})~~~\mbox{with}~~~\langle{K_{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}}\rangle_{\rm I}=N/f_{\rm T}^{2}~~~\mbox{and}~~~f_{\rm T}:=\langle{F_{\rm T}}\rangle_{\rm I}=1-\phi^{2}.

(8)

Here
N>0
and the subscript “T” indicates that this part of

is responsible for the T-model Kähler metric – see

). However, from Eq. (

), we remark that

affects – besides the kinetic mixing –

via the prefactor

. Therefore,

is generically expected to emerge

making difficult the

,

]
by two alternative strategies:

–

Adjusting

and constraining the prefactor of

in

), so that the pole is removed from

–

Replacing

) remain unaltered and, simultaneously

,

,

\langle{e^{\widetilde{K}_{\rm T}}}\rangle_{\rm I}=1~\Leftrightarrow~\langle{\widetilde{K}_{\rm T}}\rangle_{\rm I}=0~~~\mbox{with}~~~\widetilde{K}_{\rm T}=K_{\rm T}+K_{\rm sh}.

(9a)

In other words, the symmetry of

– without disturbing

in Eq. (

). To accomplish this,

K_{\rm sh}=({N}/{2})\ln F_{\rm sh}+({N}/{2})\ln F_{\rm sh}^{*}~~~\mbox{with}~~~\langle{F_{\rm sh}}\rangle_{\rm I}=:f_{\rm sh}=f_{\rm T}.

(9b)

Note that the same construction is valid even in case of

’s if we check the structure of the relevant

in Ref.
[

,

,
74
,
77
,
79
,
78
,
75
]
.

•

It has to generate the denominator of
V_{\rm I}
in Eq. (
3
).
To achieve this, we focus on the exponential prefactor of

in Eq. (
6

\langle{e^{K_{\rm d}}}\rangle_{\rm I}=(1+\phi)^{-n_{\rm d}},

(10a)

where

K_{\rm d}=-(n_{\rm d}/2)\ln F_{\rm d}-(n_{\rm d}/2)\ln F_{\rm d}^{*}~~~\mbox{with}~~~\langle{F_{\rm d}}\rangle_{\rm I}=:f_{\rm d}=1+\phi.

\widetilde{K}_{2\rm Td}=K_{2}+\widetilde{K}_{\rm T}+K_{\rm d}~~~\mbox{with}~~~\widetilde{K}_{\rm T}=K_{\rm T}+K_{\rm sh}.

3.1
Set-up

According to the strategy described in Sec.
2.2

\Phi=\>{\phi\,e^{i\theta}}\>\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\>S=\>(s+i\bar{s})/\sqrt{2}.

(12)

The inflationary trajectory can be defined by the constraints

\langle{S}\rangle_{\rm I}=\langle{\Phi-\Phi^{*}}\rangle_{\rm I}=0,\>\>\mbox{or}\>\>\>\langle{s}\rangle_{\rm I}=\langle{\bar{s}}\rangle_{\rm I}=\langle{\theta}\rangle_{\rm I}=0.

(13)

Following our plan in Sec.
2.2.2
we adopt a monomial
W

consistent with Eq. (

F_{\rm W}=\lambda\Phi^{n/2}~~~\mbox{and so}~~~W=\lambda S\Phi^{n/2},

(14)

4.4

In the context of HSI,

(35)

Although
\langle{\Phi}\rangle
and
\langle{\bar{\Phi}}\rangle

this symmetry is already broken during HSI – cf. Ref.
[

[

,

,

,

]

\langle{S}\rangle

takes the value dictated by the

unification scale

M_{\rm GUT}\simeq 2/2.433\times 10^{-2}\simeq 8.22\cdot 10^{-3}

identified with
\langle{M_{X}}\rangle
– see Table
2

\langle{M_{X}}\rangle=\sqrt{2N}gM\simeq M_{\rm GUT}\>\>\Rightarrow\>\>M\simeq\left({M_{\rm GUT}}/{g\sqrt{2N}}\right)^{n/4}\>\>\>\mbox{for}\>\>\>\langle{f_{\rm T}}\rangle\simeq 1.

Here

is the value of the GUT gauge coupling constant.

influences heavily the inflaton mass at

and induces an

’s
in Eq. (

), their
analysis can be performed in a unified way. Namely, the period of
slow-roll SI is determined by the condition – see, e.g.,
Ref.
[
4
,
2
]
:

{\footnotesize\sf max}\left\{\epsilon(\widehat{\phi}),\left|\eta(\widehat{\phi})\right|\right\}\simeq 1,\>\mbox{where}\>\>\epsilon=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{V_{\rm I,\widehat{\phi}}}{V_{\rm I}}\right)^{2}\>\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\>\eta={V_{\rm I,\widehat{\phi}\widehat{\phi}}\over V_{\rm I}}

(39a)

are the slow-roll parameters which and can be estimated employing

J
in Eq. (
19
) without express explicitly
V_{\rm I}
in terms of

\widehat{\phi}
. Indeed, our results are found to be

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\epsilon=\frac{(\phi-1)^{2}(nf_{\rm d}-n_{\rm d}\phi)^{2}}{2N\phi^{2}}~~~\mbox{and}~~~\eta=\frac{(\phi-1)}{N\phi^{2}}\cdot

\displaystyle\left(n^{2}(\phi-1)f_{\rm d}^{2}+n\left((1-2n_{\rm d})\phi^{3}+2n_{\rm d}\phi+\phi^{2}+f_{\rm d}\right)+n_{\rm d}\phi^{2}(n_{\rm d}(\phi-1)-f_{\rm d})\right).

(39b)

Eq. (
39a
) is saturated for
\phi=\phi_{\rm f}
, which is the maximal
values from the following two solutions

\displaystyle\phi_{1\rm f}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{4.n^{2}+2nN-4nn_{\rm d}+n_{\rm d}^{2}}-n_{\rm d}}{2(2n+N-2n_{\rm d})};

(40a)

\displaystyle\phi_{2\rm f}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{4n^{2}n_{\rm d}^{2}-4n\left(n-1\right)\left(-n^{2}+2nn_{\rm d}+n+N\right)}+2nn_{\rm d}}{2\left(2n-n^{2}+n_{\rm d}+n+N\right)}\cdot

(40b)

In practice, for
n_{\rm d}<n/2
,
\phi_{1\rm f}<\phi_{2\rm f}
and so SI
terminates at
\phi_{\rm f}=\phi_{2\rm f}
whereas larger
n_{\rm d}
’s give rise
to the inverse hierarchy and so
\phi_{\rm f}=\phi_{1\rm f}
.

The number of e-foldings
N_{\star}
that the pivot scale
k_{\star}=0.05/{\rm Mpc}
experiences during SI is estimated as

N_{\star}=\int_{\widehat{\phi}_{\rm f}}^{\widehat{\phi}_{*}}d\widehat{\phi}\frac{V_{\rm I}}{V_{\rm I,\widehat{\phi}}}=N\left(I_{N}(\phi_{\star})-I_{N}(\phi_{\rm f})\right),

(41)

where
\widehat{\phi}_{*}
and
\phi_{\star}
are the value of
\widehat{\phi}
and
\phi

respectively when
k_{\star}
crosses outside the inflationary horizon
and the involved function
I_{N}
reads

I_{N}(\phi)=\frac{n_{\rm d}}{4\delta n^{2}}\ln(1-\phi)-\frac{1}{2\delta n(1-\phi)}-\frac{1}{4n_{\rm d}}\ln f_{\rm d}+\frac{n(n-n_{\rm d})}{n_{\rm d}\delta n^{2}}\ln(nf_{\rm T}-n_{\rm d}\phi),

(42)

where
\delta n=n_{\rm d}-2n<0
. The last inequality stems from the fact that
the dominant contribution to
I_{N}
originates from the
non-logarithmic term with
\phi<1
. The presence of a pole in
J

– see Eq. (
19
) – and the effective nature of SUGRA forces us
to work in this domain of
\phi
. Consequently, the positivity of

N_{\star}
implies the upper bound above on
\delta n
which restricts
seriously the allowed region of our model as we see in Sec.
5.2
.

Due to the complicate form of
N_{\star}
in Eq. (
41
), it is not
doable to solve the equation above w.r.t
\phi_{\star}
and find a generic
analytical expression for it and the inflationary observables –
see below. As a consequence, our last resort is the numerical
computation, whose the results are presented in Sec.
5.2
.
Nonetheless, for
n_{\rm d}\ll n
and taking into account
\phi_{\star}\gg\phi_{\rm f}
,
we can derive an approximate and rather accurate formula for
N_{\star}

since it is dominated from the non-logarithmic term of
I_{N}
. In
this portion of parameter space we can determine
\phi_{\star}
as follows

N_{\star}\simeq-\frac{N}{2\delta n}\frac{\phi_{\star}}{1-\phi_{\star}}~~\Rightarrow~~\phi_{\star}\simeq\frac{2\delta nN_{\star}}{2\delta nN_{\star}-N}.

(43)

Since both factors of the ratio above are negative and the
denominator is larger in absolute value we expect that
\phi_{\star}<1
.
Therefore, our proposal can be stabilized against corrections from
higher order terms in the
K
’s – see Sec.
4.2
.

The amplitude
A_{\rm s}
of the power spectrum of the curvature
perturbations generated by
\phi
can be computed using the
standard formulae

A_{\rm s}^{1/2}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}\,\pi}\;\frac{V_{\rm I}^{3/2}(\widehat{\phi}_{*})}{|V_{\rm I,\widehat{\phi}}(\widehat{\phi}_{*})|}=\frac{\lambda\phi_{\star}\sqrt{N}\sqrt{\phi_{\star}^{n}(1+\phi_{\star})^{-n_{\rm d}}}}{2\sqrt{3}\pi(1-\phi_{\star})(nf_{\rm T\star}-n_{\rm d}\phi_{\star})},

(44)

where
f_{\rm T\star}=f_{\rm T}(\phi_{\star})
. From the right formula in Eq. (
44
) we can
derive a relation between
\lambda
and
A_{\rm s}
. For simplicitly we set

n_{\rm d}=0
and so we find

\lambda\simeq\frac{\pi n^{-\frac{n}{2}}\sqrt{3A_{\rm s}}\sqrt{N}(8nN_{\star}+N)}{2^{n+1}N_{\star}^{\frac{n}{2}+1}(4nN_{\star}+N)^{1-{n/2}}}.

(45)

It is clear that no
\delta n
depedence appears due to drastic
simplification done. However, the numerical result is quite close
to the exact one given that
n_{\rm d}
is bounded above as noticed from
Eq. (
42
).

The remaining inflationary observables – i.e., the (scalar)
spectral index
n_{\rm s}
, its running
a_{\rm s}
, and the
scalar-to-tensor ratio
r
– are found from the relations

[
4
,
2
]

n_{\rm s}=1-6\epsilon_{\star}\ +\ 2\eta_{\star},~r=16\epsilon_{\star}~~~\mbox{and}~~~\alpha_{\rm s}=\>2\left(4\eta_{\star}^{2}-(n_{\rm s}-1)^{2}\right)/3-2\widehat{\xi}_{\star},

(46)

where the variables with subscript
\star
are evaluated at

\phi=\phi_{\star}
and
\widehat{\xi}={V_{\rm I,\widehat{\phi}}V_{\rm I,\widehat{\phi}\widehat{\phi}\widehat{\phi}}/V^{2}_{\rm I}}
. Inserting

\phi_{\star}
from Eq. (
43
) into Eq. (
39b
) and then into equations
above we can obtain the following approximate expressions

\displaystyle n_{\rm s}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle 1-\frac{2}{N_{\star}}-\frac{n_{\rm d}^{2}N}{4N_{\star}^{2}\delta n^{2}}-\frac{n^{2}N}{N_{\star}^{2}\delta n^{2}}+\frac{n_{\rm d}nN}{N_{\star}^{2}\delta n^{2}}-\frac{3n_{\rm d}N}{2N_{\star}^{2}\delta n^{2}}+\frac{nN}{N_{\star}^{2}\delta n^{2}},

(47a)

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{2N}{N_{\star}^{2}}+\frac{2n_{\rm d}N^{2}}{N_{\star}^{3}\delta n^{2}}-\frac{2nN^{2}}{N_{\star}^{3}\delta n^{2}},

(47b)

\displaystyle\alpha_{\rm s}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{N_{\star}^{2}}-\frac{n_{\rm d}^{2}N}{2N_{\star}^{3}\delta n^{2}}-\frac{2n^{2}N}{N_{\star}^{3}\delta n^{2}}+\frac{2n_{\rm d}nN}{N_{\star}^{3}\delta n^{2}}-\frac{7n_{\rm d}N}{2N_{\star}^{3}\delta n^{2}}+\frac{2nN}{N_{\star}^{3}\delta n^{2}}.

(47c)

These expressions give accurate results for
n_{\rm d}\ll n
or

\delta n\simeq-2n
. For
n=n_{\rm d}=2
the above results converge to those
obtained for the pure
\alpha
-SI

[
57
,
20
,
62
,
61
]
, i.e.,

(n_{\rm s},r,\alpha_{\rm s})\simeq(1-2/N_{\star},2N/N_{\star}^{2},-2/N_{\star}^{2}).

(48)

The same results are obtained (for reasonably low
n
and
N

values) in the limit
n_{\rm d}=0
where the pure T-model inflation is
revealed.

5.2
Numerical Results

Our estimations above can be verified and extended for any
\delta n

numerically. In particular, we confront the quantities in
Eqs. (
41
) and (
44
) with the observational requirements
[
87
]

N_{\star}\simeq 61.3+\frac{1-3w_{\rm rh}}{12(1+w_{\rm rh})}\ln\frac{\pi^{2}g_{\rm rh*}T_{\rm rh}^{4}}{30V_{\rm I}(\phi_{\rm f})}+\frac{1}{2}\ln\left({V_{\rm I}(\phi_{\star})\over g_{\rm rh*}^{1/6}V_{\rm I}(\phi_{\rm f})^{1/2}}\right)~~~\mbox{and}~~~A_{\rm s}^{1/2}\simeq 4.588\cdot 10^{-5},

(49)

where we assume that SI is followed in turn by an oscillatory
phase with mean equation-of-state parameter

,
radiation and matter domination. Motivated by implementations

[

,
36
,
37

68

of non-thermal leptogenesis, which

of degrees of freedom

the MSSM spectrum. Note that this

avoids exhaustive tuning
on the relevant coupling constant involved in the decay width of

[

Due to the peculiar expression of

or it is

\phi_{\rm mx}=\phi_{\rm f}

(51)

6
Conclusions

We presented a novel implementation of SI (i.e. Starobinsky
inflation) in the context of SUGRA confining ourselves to models
displaying a scalar potential shown in Eq. (
3
) and a kinetic
mixing in the inflaton sector with a pole of order two – see
Eq. (
2
). We considered two classes of models – CSI, i.e.,
chaotic SI and HSI, i.e. Higgs SI – depending on whether the
inflaton is included into a gauge-singlet or two gauge-non-singlet
fields. CSI and HSI are relied on the superpotentials in
Eqs. (
14
) and (
24
) respectively which respect an
R
symmetry and
include an inflaton-accompanying field which facilitates the
establishment of SI. On the other hand, the Kähler potential’s respect the
R

and gauge symmetries and parameterize hyperbolic internal
geometries met in T-model inflationary settings. Namely,
K
for
CSI is given in Eq. (
16
) whereas for HSI we considered the two
distinct
K
’s shown in Eq. (
28
). The Higgsflaton in the last
case implements the breaking of a gauge
U(1)_{X}
symmetry at a scale
which may assume a value compatible with the MSSM unification. All
the models excellently match with the observations by restricting
the free parameters to reasonably ample regions of values. In
particular,
n_{\rm s}
lies close to its central observational value
while
r
increases with
N
– see Eq. (
54a
) for CSI and
Eqs. (
55a
) and (
56a
) for HSI. The present data on
\delta_{21}
and the
self-consistency of the models allows us to delineate the overall
allowed regions for selected values of the exponent
n
in
Eq. (
3
) – see Fig.
2
and
5
. The resulting
allowed margin of
n_{\rm s}
is extended from
0.961
up to
0.969
,
depending on the chosen
n
, and therefore it is less restrictive
than the corresponding prediction of the traditional Starobinsky
model in Eq. (
48
) – i.e.
n_{\rm s}\simeq 0.963
. Hopefully, a more
accurate determination of
n_{\rm s}
and
r
by future experiments

[
94
,
96
,
95
,
97
]
will assist us to single out the
most favorable one from the proposed models.

method applied for the construction of our models can be extended
to other SUGRA models as those motivated by D branes

[
98
]
or those which assist to obtain de Sitter vacua in
line with current LHC results on SUSY as in Ref.
[
57
,
65
]
. Moreover, it can be employed for supersymmetrizing
models obtained adopting the Palatini approach to gravity

[
99
,
100
,
101

.

We expect that our models of HSI admit a post-inflationary
completion along the lines of Ref.
[
31
,
37
,
36
,
48
,
68
]

since the mass parameters in those models are similar to those
found in Eqs. (
55b
) and (
56b
). As regards the ultraviolet
completion, it would be interesting to investigate if our models
belong to the string landscape or swampland
[
102
]
. Note
that the swampland string conjectures are generically not
satisfied in SUGRA-based models
[
103
,
104
]
but there are
suggestions
[
105
,
106
,
107
]
which may work in our
framework too.

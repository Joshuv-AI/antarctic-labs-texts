Algorithm-Assisted Call Scheduling Improves Equity and Efficiency in Plastic Surgery Training
Authors: Babak Sadri, Donna Tepper
Published: 2026

Objective: Call scheduling in surgical training often results in inequitable workload distribution, contributing to dissatisfaction and burnout. While automated scheduling has improved equity in other specialties, no such systems exist for plastic surgery, where multi-service coverage (plastic, hand, and facial trauma) creates distinct complexity. We implemented an algorithmic solution to improve call equity and reduce administrative burden.

Methods: A weighted point system quantified call difficulty based on services covered by the on-call resident: Plastic (1 point), Face Trauma (2), Hand (3), summed per shift. Weekend call added a 10-point premium; holidays added 7.5 points. A mobile application (FairShift) was developed and implemented to assign shifts using an algorithm to maintain equity among all residents taking call. Seven months of pre-implementation schedules were retrospectively scored and compared to schedules prospectively generated for 4 months using FairShift.

Results: Pre-implementation, mean monthly points per resident were 51.7 ± 9.7 (CV 14.6%), with the highest-burden resident accumulating 32% more points than the lowest (397 vs 301). Schedule creation averaged 4.7 hours. Post-implementation, points were 47.3 ± 3.1 (CV 3.0%), variance decreased 98.8%, disparity fell from 32% to 6%, and schedule creation dropped to 15 minutes (94.7% reduction).

Conclusion: Algorithm-assisted scheduling improved call equity among plastic surgery residents while reducing administrative time by over 90%, demonstrating that technology-assisted scheduling enhances fairness and efficiency in graduate medical education.

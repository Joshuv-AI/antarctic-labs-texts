Age-Related Differences in Anti-Glomerular Basement Membrane Disease: Distinct Biology or Distinct Timing of Presentation?
Authors: Parankush Upadhyay, Aditya Upadhyay
Published: 2026

To the Editor:

We read with great interest the multicenter European study by Suhlrie et al.1 comparing outcomes in pediatric and adult anti-glomerular basement membrane disease. The study provides valuable contemporary data in a rare disorder and highlights important age-related differences in presentation and outcomes.

However, certain methodological aspects merit discussion. The retrospective survey-based design involving patients diagnosed from 2020 to 2024 may introduce recall and documentation bias, particularly for variables such as edema, smoking exposure, symptom duration, and comorbidity burden. In addition, the use of the Pottel equation2 for estimating glomerular filtration rate, although useful for cross-age comparisons, requires further validation in non-Caucasian populations before wider extrapolation.

The marked female predominance among children (80%) is particularly intriguing. This raises the possibility that pediatric anti-glomerular basement membrane disease may differ pathophysiologically from adult disease rather than merely representing an earlier manifestation of the same entity. Conversely, environmental exposures more prevalent in adult males, including smoking and hydrocarbon exposure, may offset an inherent female predisposition. Exploration of passive smoke exposure in children may also be informative.

Another important observation is the substantially higher prevalence of antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies dual positivity among adults. Antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies-mediated glomerular injury exposing cryptic glomerular basement membrane antigens has been proposed as a mechanism for dual-positive disease.3,4 Thus, adults may have had a more chronic or smoldering inflammatory process before presentation. This interpretation is supported by lower estimated glomerular filtration rate, greater chronicity on biopsy, and higher interstitial fibrosis and tubular atrophy in adults, whereas children appeared to present earlier in the disease course with more preserved renal architecture. Stratifying analyses by dual positivity, analogous to clinicopathological subclassification approaches used in antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies-associated vasculitis, may provide further insight.

Interestingly, despite only 28% of children presenting with estimated glomerular filtration rate < 15 ml/min per 1.73 m2, 46% required dialysis, suggesting either a lower threshold for dialysis initiation or clinically significant tubular and/or fluid-electrolyte dysfunction despite preserved filtration. Furthermore, children received more intensive immunosuppression and plasma exchange on a weight basis, which may have contributed to their superior renal outcomes.

Taken together, these findings raise the important possibility that pediatric anti-GBM disease may represent a clinically and biologically distinct phenotype rather than simply an earlier presentation of adult disease.

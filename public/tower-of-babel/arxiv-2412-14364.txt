Minimum degree conditions for graph rigidity
Michael Krivelevich, Alan Lew, Peleg Michaeli

Abstract
We study minimum degree conditions that guarantee that an n n -vertex graph is rigid in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . For small values of d d , we obtain a tight bound: for d = O ⁡ ( n ) d=O(\sqrt{n}) , every n n -vertex graph with minimum degree at least ( n + d ) / 2 − 1 (n+d)/2-1 is rigid in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . For larger values of d d , we achieve an approximate result: for d = O ⁡ ( n / log 2 ⁡ n ) d=O(n/{\log^{2}}{n}) , every n n -vertex graph with minimum degree at least ( n + 2 ​ d ) / 2 − 1 (n+2d)/2-1 is rigid in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . This bound is tight up to a factor of two in the coefficient of d d . As a byproduct of our proof, we also obtain the following result, which may be of independent interest: for d = O ⁡ ( n / log 2 ⁡ n ) d=O(n/{\log^{2}}{n}) , every n n -vertex graph with minimum degree at least d d has pseudoachromatic number at least d + 1 d+1 ; namely, the vertex set of such a graph can be partitioned into d + 1 d+1 subsets such that there is at least one edge between each pair of subsets. This is tight.

4.4

Let

Proof.

Write
\Delta=\Delta(G)

For every

be an arbitrary set of

Let

Note that

For

be the set of out-neighbours of

such that there exists

.
Note that for every

Let

be defined by

Note that
|f(A)-f(B)|\leq d+1
whenever
|A\triangle B|=2

(since adding a new vertex to a set

by at most
1

or decrease it by at most

Let

be a uniformly chosen random subset of

of size

, and let

Note that the events

are negatively correlated for distinct

Note also that
K/n\leq 1/4
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{X}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}{\left[|N_{D}(U)|\right]}=\sum_{v}\mathbb{P}(v\in N_{D}(U))

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{v}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in V:\\
(u,v)\in F\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}(u\in U,\ v\notin U)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{1},u_{2}\in V:\\
(u_{1},u_{2},v)\in T\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}(u_{1},u_{2}\in U,\ v\notin U)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{(u,v)\in F}\mathbb{P}(u\in U,\ v\notin U)-\sum_{(u_{1},u_{2},v)\in T}\mathbb{P}(u_{1},u_{2}\in U)

\displaystyle\geq|F|\cdot\frac{K}{n}\left(1-\frac{K}{n}\right)-|T|\cdot\frac{K^{2}}{n^{2}}\geq dK(1-1/4)-dK/4=dK/2.

Thus, by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|N_{G}(U)|\leq dK/3)\leq\mathbb{P}(|N_{D}(U)|\leq dK/3)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(X\leq dK/3)\leq\mathbb{P}(X\leq\mathbb{E}{X}-dK/6)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(\frac{-d^{2}K^{2}}{72K(d+1)^{2}}\right)\leq 2\exp(-K/288),

as required.
∎

G[A_{i},B_{j}]
. Therefore,
G[A_{i},B_{j}]
is connected.

Define, for all
1\leq i\leq d
,
V_{i}=A_{i}\cup B_{i}
.
We note that
G[V_{i}]\supseteq G[A_{i},B_{i}]
, hence
G[V_{i}]
is connected.
Moreover, for
1\leq i<j\leq d
,
the sets
A_{i},B_{j}
are in the same component in
G[V_{i},V_{j}]
.
Similarly,
the sets
A_{j},B_{i}
are in the same component there.
But, by
Lemma

4.3
,
E(A_{i},A_{j})\neq\varnothing
;
thus, there exists a unique connected component in
G[V_{i},V_{j}]
,
namely, it is connected.
Thus,
(V_{1},\dots,V_{d})
is a strong
d
-rigid partition of
G
.
∎

4.2

Far from bipartite

In this section we prove
Proposition

4.2
.
As our main tool, we will use the regularity lemma.
Let us begin by recalling relevant definitions (see
[
16
]
).
For a disjoint pair of vertex sets
X,Y
in a graph
G
,
its
density
is
d(X,Y)=|E(X,Y)|/(|X||Y|)
.
A pair
(A,B)
is called:

•

\varepsilon
-regular

if for all
X\subseteq A
and
Y\subseteq B

with
|X|\geq\varepsilon|A|
and
|Y|\geq\varepsilon|B|

we have
|d(X,Y)-d(A,B)|\leq\varepsilon
;

•

(\varepsilon,\delta)
-regular

if it is
\varepsilon
-regular with
d(A,B)\geq\delta
;

•

(\varepsilon,\delta)
-dense

if
d(X,Y)\geq\delta
for every
X\subseteq A
and
Y\subseteq B

with
|X|\geq\varepsilon|A|
and
|Y|\geq\varepsilon|B|
;

•

(\varepsilon,\delta)
-super-regular

if it is
\varepsilon
-regular and, in addition,
each
x\in A
has at least
\delta|B|
neighbours in
B

and each
y\in B
has at least
\delta|A|
neighbours in
A
.

Note that:

•

If
(A,B)
is
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-regular
then for every
X\subseteq A
and
Y\subseteq B

with
|X|\geq\varepsilon|A|
and
|Y|\geq\varepsilon|B|
we have
d(X,Y)\geq\delta-\varepsilon
,
that is,
(A,B)
is
(\varepsilon,\delta-\varepsilon)
-dense.

•

If
(A,B)
is
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-super-regular
then
d(A,B)\geq\delta
,
that is,
(A,B)
is
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-regular
(and hence
(\varepsilon,\delta-\varepsilon)
-dense).

We will need the following standard and simple lemma that asserts that every regular “triple”
contains an almost-spanning super-regular “sub-triple”.

4.8
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1/4
and
\delta>4\varepsilon
.
Let
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}
be three pairwise disjoint sets with
|A_{1}|=|A_{2}|=|A_{3}|=M

such that each of the pairs
(A_{i},A_{j})
,
1\leq i<j\leq 3
,
is a
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-regular pair.
Then, there exist
M^{\prime}\geq(1-2\varepsilon)M

and

A_{i}^{\prime}\subseteq A_{i}
with
|A^{\prime}_{i}|=M^{\prime}
, for every
i\in[3]
,
such that each of the pairs
(A_{i}^{\prime},A_{j}^{\prime})
,
1\leq i<j\leq 3
,
is
(2\varepsilon,\delta-4\varepsilon)
-super-regular.

Proof.

For
i\in[3]
and
j\in[3]\smallsetminus\{i\}
, let
A_{i}^{j}=\{x\in A_{i}\mid\deg(x,A_{j})<(\delta-2\varepsilon)|A_{j}|\}
.
Note that
|A_{i}^{j}|<\varepsilon|A_{i}|
.
Indeed,
if
|A_{i}^{j}|\geq\varepsilon|A|
then, since
(A_{i},A_{j})
is
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-regular,

|A_{i}^{j}|\cdot(\delta-2\varepsilon)|A_{j}|>|E(A_{i}^{j},A_{j})|=|A_{i}^{j}||A_{j}|\cdot d(A_{i}^{j},A_{j})\geq|A_{i}^{j}||A_{j}|(\delta-\varepsilon),

a contradiction.
For
i\in[3]
and
\{j,k\}=[3]\smallsetminus\{i\}
,
Let
A_{i}^{*}=A_{i}\smallsetminus(A_{i}^{j}\cup A_{i}^{k})
.
Note that
|A_{i}^{*}|\geq(1-2\varepsilon)|A_{i}|
.
Set
M^{\prime}=\min\{|A_{i}^{*}|:i\in[3]\}
, so
M^{\prime}\geq(1-2\varepsilon)M
.
For each
i\in[3]
, remove, if needed, arbitrary elements from
A_{i}^{*}
,
to make it of size
M^{\prime}
, and call the resulting set
A_{i}^{\prime}
.
In particular,
|A_{i}^{\prime}|\geq(1-2\varepsilon)|A_{i}|\geq|A_{i}|/2\geq\varepsilon|A_{i}|
.
Thus, for
j\in[3]\smallsetminus\{i\}
,

|d(A_{i}^{\prime},A_{j}^{\prime})-d(A_{i},A_{j})|\leq\varepsilon
.
Moreover,
for every
X_{i}\subseteq A_{i}^{\prime}
and
X_{j}\subseteq A_{j}^{\prime}

with
|X_{i}|\geq 2\varepsilon|A_{i}^{\prime}|\geq\varepsilon|A_{i}|
and
|X_{j}|\geq 2\varepsilon|A_{j}^{\prime}|\geq\varepsilon|A_{j}|
,
we have
|d(X_{i},X_{j})-d(A_{i}^{\prime},A_{j}^{\prime})|\leq|d(X_{i},X_{j})-d(A_{i},A_{j})|+|d(A_{i},A_{j})-d(A_{i}^{\prime},A_{j}^{\prime})|\leq 2\varepsilon
.
Hence,
(A_{i}^{\prime},A_{j}^{\prime})
is
2\varepsilon
-regular.
In addition, if
x\in A_{i}^{\prime}
,
then
\deg(x,A_{j}^{\prime})\geq\deg(x,A_{j})-2\varepsilon|A_{j}|\geq(\delta-4\varepsilon)|A_{j}|\geq(\delta-4\varepsilon)|A_{j}^{\prime}|
.
Thus,
(A_{i}^{\prime},A_{j}^{\prime})
is
(2\varepsilon,\delta-4\varepsilon)
-super-regular.
∎

We will use the following degree version of the regularity lemma (see
[
16
]
).

4.9
Regularity lemma, degree version.

For every
\varepsilon>0
and
\ell_{0}>0

there exists an integer
L=L(\varepsilon,\ell_{0})

such that for every
n
-vertex graph
G=(V,E)
with
n\geq L

and every
\delta\in[0,1]

there exists a partition of
V
into
\ell+1
sets
V_{0},V_{1},\dots,V_{\ell}

with
\ell_{0}\leq\ell\leq L
,
and a spanning subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
with the following properties:

1.

|V_{0}|\leq\varepsilon n
and
|V_{i}|=M
for all
i\in[\ell]
and some
M>0
;

2.

\deg_{G^{\prime}}(v)\geq\deg_{G}(v)-(\delta+\varepsilon)n
for all
v\in V
;

3.

V_{i}
is independent in
G^{\prime}
for all
i\in[\ell]
;

4.

For every
1\leq i<j\leq\ell
,
either
E_{G^{\prime}}(V_{i},V_{j})=\varnothing

or
(V_{i},V_{j})
is
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-regular in
G^{\prime}
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
,
a partition
V=V_{1}\cup\dots\cup V_{\ell}
,
and parameters
\varepsilon,\delta>0
,
let
R(G,(V_{1},\dots,V_{\ell}),\varepsilon,\delta)

be the graph on the vertex set
\{V_{1},\dots,V_{\ell}\}

where
V_{i}\sim V_{j}
if and only if
(V_{i},V_{j})
is
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-regular in
G
.
We think of the regularity lemma as an algorithm:
its input is
\varepsilon,\ell_{0},n,\delta
with
\varepsilon>0
,
\ell_{0}\geq 1
,
n\geq L(\varepsilon,\ell_{0})
,

\delta\in[0,1]
, and an
n
-vertex graph
G
;
and its output is a partition
V_{0},V_{1},\dots,V_{\ell}
with the desired properties,
the so-called
pure graph

G^{\prime\prime}=G^{\prime}[V\smallsetminus V_{0}]
,
and the
reduced graph

R=R(G^{\prime\prime},(V_{1},\dots,V_{\ell}),\varepsilon,\delta)
.

4.10
.

Let
\varepsilon,\ell_{0},n,\delta
with
\varepsilon>0
,
\ell_{0}\geq 1
,
n\geq L(\varepsilon,\ell_{0})
, and
\delta\in[0,1]
,
and let
G=(V,E)
be an
n
-vertex graph.
Let
G^{\prime\prime}
be the pure graph obtained by
Lemma

4.9
with the above parameters.
Then:
|E(G^{\prime\prime})|\geq|E(G)|-(\delta+3\varepsilon)n^{2}/2
.

Proof.

We recall that for every
v\in V
,

\deg_{G^{\prime\prime}}(v)\geq\deg_{G^{\prime}}(v)-|V_{0}|\geq\deg_{G}(v)-(\delta+2\varepsilon)n
.
Thus,

\displaystyle 2|E(G^{\prime\prime})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V\smallsetminus V_{0}}\deg_{G^{\prime\prime}}(v)\geq\sum_{v\in V}\deg_{G}(v)-\sum_{v\in V\smallsetminus V_{0}}(\delta+2\varepsilon)n-\sum_{v\in V_{0}}\deg_{G}(v)

\displaystyle\geq 2|E(G)|-(\delta+2\varepsilon)n^{2}-\varepsilon n^{2}=2|E(G)|-(\delta+3\varepsilon)n^{2},

as required.
∎

We deduce that the reduced graph has, approximately, the “correct” number of edges.

4.11
.

Let
\varepsilon,\ell_{0},n,\delta
with
\varepsilon>0
,
\ell_{0}\geq 1
,
n\geq L(\varepsilon,\ell_{0})
, and
\delta\in[0,1]
,
and let
G=(V,E)
be an
n
-vertex graph. Let
V_{0},V_{1},\dots,V_{\ell}
be the partition obtained by
Lemma

4.9

with the above parameters, and let

be the corresponding reduced graph.
Then:
|E(R)|\geq\frac{\ell^{2}}{n^{2}}\cdot|E(G)|-(\delta+3\varepsilon)\ell^{2}/2
.

Proof.

Let
G^{\prime\prime}
be the pure graph. Recall that by
Lemma

4.9
,
|V_{i}|=M
for all

. Therefore,

.
By
Lemma

,

|E(G^{\prime\prime})|\geq|E(G)|-(\delta+3\varepsilon)n^{2}/2
.
On the other hand,

|E(G^{\prime\prime})|\leq M^{2}|E(R)|
,
hence
|E(R)|\geq\frac{\ell^{2}}{n^{2}}\cdot|E(G)|-(\delta+3\varepsilon)\ell^{2}/2
.
∎

is far from being bipartite and dense enough, then its reduced graph contains a triangle.

4.12
.

Let
\beta>0
.

\ell_{0}

\delta=8\varepsilon
,

For the proof of

4.13
.

For every

4.14
.

Let

with
\varepsilon>0
,
\ell_{0}\geq 1

, and

,

.

.

Proof of

.

4.15
.

Let
\beta>0
.
Then, there exist sufficiently small

Proof.

Let

4.2.1

Super-regular tripartite graphs

Next, we show that a triple of disjoint
n
-vertex sets, such that each pair of sets forms a super-regular pair, admits a strong
d
-rigid partition for all
d=O(n/\log{n})
.

4.16
.

For every
\delta>0

there exists
c>0

such that the following holds.
Let
n,d\geq 1

be such that
d\leq cn/\log{n}
.
Let
G=(V,E)
be a tripartite graph with sides
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}
,
each of size
n
.
Assume that for all
1\leq i<j\leq 3
, the pair
(A_{i},A_{j})
is
(\delta,2\delta)
-super-regular in
G
.
Then,

G
admits a strong
d
-rigid partition.

Proof.

Let
c>0
be a constant to be chosen later,
and take
1\leq d\leq cn/\log{n}
.
Let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{d}
be a uniform random
d
-colouring of
V
;
namely,
for every
v\in V

independently,

\mathbb{P}(v\in V_{i})=1/d
for every
i\in[d]
.

4.17
.

With probability at least
1-6n^{2-2\delta/c}
,
for all
i\in[3]
,
v\in A_{i}
,
j\in[3]\smallsetminus\{i\}
and
1\leq k\leq d
,

v
has neighbour in
V_{k}\cap A_{j}
.

Proof.

For every
i\in[3]
,
v\in A_{i}
,
j\in[3]\smallsetminus\{i\}
and
k\in[d]
,
let
\mathcal{E}_{v,j,k}
be the event that
v
does not have a neighbour in
A_{j}\cap V_{k}
.
Note that by the
(\delta,2\delta)
-super-regularity of
(A_{i},A_{j})
,
we have
\deg(v,A_{j})\geq 2\delta n
.
Thus,

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{v,j,k})\leq(1-1/d)^{2\delta n}\leq e^{-2\delta n/d}\leq n^{-2\delta/c}.

By the union bound
over all
i\in[3]
,

v\in A_{i}
,

j=[3]\smallsetminus\{i\}
,
and
k\in[d]
,
the probability that

\mathcal{E}_{v,j,k}
occurs for
some
v,j,k

is at most

3\cdot n\cdot 2\cdot d\cdot n^{-2\delta/c}\leq 6n^{2-2\delta/c}.

Therefore, with probability at least
1-6n^{2-2\delta/c}
,
for all
i\in[3]
,
v\in A_{i}
,
j\in[3]\smallsetminus\{i\}
and
k\in[d]
,

v
has neighbour in
A_{j}\cap V_{k}
.
∎

4.18
.

With probability at least
1-6n^{4-2\delta^{3}/c}
,
for all
i\in[3]
,

x\neq y
in
A_{i}
,
and
r,s\in[d]
,

E(N(x)\cap V_{r},N(y)\cap V_{s})\neq\varnothing
.

Proof.

For
i\in[3]
,

x\neq y
in
A_{i}
,
and
r,s\in[d]
,
let
\mathcal{E}_{x,y,r,s}
be the event that

E(N(x)\cap V_{r},N(y)\cap V_{s})=\varnothing
.
Fix such
i,x,y,r,s
and
let
\{j,k\}=[3]\smallsetminus\{i\}
.
By the super-regularity of
(A_{i},A_{j})
in
G
,

|N(x)\cap A_{j}|\geq 2\delta n>\delta n
.
By the super-regularity of
(A_{i},A_{k})
,

|N(y)\cap A_{k}|\geq 2\delta n>\delta n
.
Hence, by the (
\delta
,
\delta
)-density of
(A_{j},A_{k})

(which follows from its
(\delta,2\delta)
-regularity; see notes after the corresponding definitions),

|E(N(x)\cap A_{j},N(y)\cap A_{k})|\geq\delta\cdot(2\delta n)^{2}=4\delta^{3}n^{2}
.
Let

L=\left\{v\in N(x)\cap A_{j}:\,\deg(v,N(y)\cap A_{k})\geq 2\delta^{3}n\right\}.

Then,
|L|\geq 2\delta^{3}n

(otherwise,

|E(N(x)\cap A_{j},N(y)\cap A_{k})|\leq 2\delta^{3}n\cdot n+(n-2\delta^{3}n)\cdot 2\delta^{3}n<4\delta^{3}n^{2}
,
a contradiction).
Therefore,

\mathbb{P}(L\cap V_{r}=\varnothing)\leq(1-1/d)^{2\delta^{3}n}\leq e^{-2\delta^{3}n/d}\leq n^{-2\delta^{3}/c}.

Conditioning on
L\cap V_{r}\neq\varnothing
,
there are at least
2\delta^{3}n
vertices in
N(y)\cap A_{k}

that are adjacent to at least one vertex in
N(x)\cap A_{j}\cap V_{r}
,
and therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(E(N(x)\cap A_{j}\cap V_{r},N(y)\cap A_{k}\cap V_{s})=\varnothing\,|\,L\cap V_{r}\neq\varnothing)

\displaystyle\leq(1-1/d)^{2\delta^{3}n}\leq n^{-2\delta^{3}/c}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{x,y,r,s})\leq\mathbb{P}(E(N(x)\cap A_{j}\cap V_{r},N(y)\cap A_{k}\cap V_{s})=\varnothing)\leq 2n^{-2\delta^{3}/c}.

By the union bound
over all
i\in[3]
,

x\neq y
in
A_{i}
,
and
r,s\in[d]
,
the probability that

\mathcal{E}_{x,y,r,s}
occurs for
some
x,y,r,s

is at most

3n^{2}d^{2}\cdot 2n^{-2\delta^{3}/c}\leq 6n^{4-2\delta^{3}/c}.

Thus, with probability at least
1-6n^{4-2\delta^{3}/c}
,
for all
i\in[3]
,

x\neq y
in
A_{i}
,
and
r,s\in[d]
,
we have that
E(N(x)\cap V_{r},N(y)\cap V_{s})\neq\varnothing
.
∎

Let
c=2\delta^{3}/(5+\log_{2}(12))
.
Let
\mathcal{E}_{1}
be the event that there exist
i\in[3]
,
v\in A_{i}
,
j\in[3]\smallsetminus\{i\}
, and
k\in[d]

such that
v
does not have a neighbour in
V_{k}\cap A_{j}
.
By
4.17
,
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{1})\leq 6n^{2-2\delta/c}
.
Let
\mathcal{E}_{2}
be the event that there exist
i\in[3]
,
x,y\in A_{i}
such that
x\neq y
, and
r,s\in[d]

such that
E(N(x)\cap V_{r},N(y)\cap V_{s})=\varnothing
.
By
4.18
,
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{2})\leq 6n^{4-2\delta^{3}/c}
.
Thus, by the union bound, noting that
4-2\delta^{3}/c>2-2\delta/c
,

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{1}\cup\mathcal{E}_{2})\leq 6n^{2-2\delta/c}+6n^{4-2\delta^{3}/c}\leq 6\left(2^{2-2\delta/c}+2^{4-2\delta^{3}/c}\right)\leq 12\cdot 2^{4-2\delta^{3}/c}<1.

Hence, there exists a partition
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{d})
for which both
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
do not occur.
We will show that
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{d})
is a strong
d
-rigid partition of
G
.

First, let
r\in[d]
.
We will show that
G[V_{r}]
is connected. Let
x,y\in V_{r}
with
x\neq y
. We divide into two cases:

Case 1.

If
x,y\in A_{i}
for some
i\in[3]
, then (since
\mathcal{E}_{2}
does not occur)
there is some
u\in N(x)\cap V_{r}
and
v\in N(y)\cap V_{r}
such that
\{u,v\}\in E
.
Therefore,
x
and
y
are connected in
G[V_{r}]
by a path of length three.

Case 2.

If
x\in A_{i}
and
y\in A_{j}
for
i\neq j
,
then (since
\mathcal{E}_{1}
does not occur) there exists
z\in N(y)\cap V_{r}\cap A_{i}
.
By the previous case, there is a path in
G[V_{r}]
between
x
and
z
,
and therefore there is a path in
G[V_{r}]
between
x
and
y
.

We conclude that
G[V_{r}]
is connected.
Now, let
r,s\in[d]
with
r\neq s
.
We will show that
G[V_{r},V_{s}]
is connected.
Let
x,y\in V_{r}\cup V_{s}
with
x\neq y
. We divide into four cases:

Case 1.

Assume
x\in V_{r}\cap A_{i}
and
y\in V_{s}\cap A_{i}
for some
i\in[3]
.
Then (since
\mathcal{E}_{2}
does not occur)
there is some
u\in N(x)\cap V_{s}
and
v\in N(y)\cap V_{r}

such that
\{u,v\}\in E
.
Thus,
x
and
y
are connected in
G[V_{r},V_{s}]
by a path of length three.

Case 2.

Assume
x\in V_{r}\cap A_{i}
and
y\in V_{r}\cap A_{j}
for some
i\neq j
.
Then (since
\mathcal{E}_{1}
does not occur) there is some
z\in N(y)\cap V_{s}\cap A_{i}
.
By Case 1, there is a path in
G[V_{r},V_{s}]
between
x
and
z
.
Thus, there is a path in
G[V_{r},V_{s}]
between
x
and
y
.

Case 3.

Assume
x\in V_{r}\cap A_{i}
and
y\in V_{s}\cap A_{j}
for some
i\neq j
.
Let
k
be the unique element in
[3]\smallsetminus\{i,j\}
.
Then (since
\mathcal{E}_{1}
does not occur)
there is some
z\in N(y)\cap V_{r}\cap A_{k}
.
By Case 2, there is a path in
G[V_{r},V_{s}]
between
x
and
z
.
Thus, there is a path in
G[V_{r},V_{s}]
between
x
and
y
.

Case 4.

Assume
x,y\in V_{r}\cap A_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq 3
.
Let
j\neq i
.
Then (since
\mathcal{E}_{1}
does not occur)
there is some
z\in N(y)\cap V_{s}\cap A_{j}
.
By Case 3, there is a path in
G[V_{r},V_{s}]
from
x
to
z
.
Therefore, there is a path in
G[V_{r},V_{s}]
from
x
to
y
.

We conclude that
G[V_{r},V_{s}]
is connected.
Hence,
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{d})
is a strong
d
-rigid partition for
G
.
∎

4.2.2

Putting it all together

To conclude, we use the following simple lemma.

4.19
.

Let
0\leq 2d\leq n
,
and let
G=(V,E)
be an
n
-vertex graph with
\delta(G)\geq(n+2d)/2-1
.
Then, for every
A
with
2d\leq|A|<n

there exists
x\in V\smallsetminus A

such that
\deg(x,A)\geq d
.

Proof.

Set
a=|A|
. If
a\geq n/2

then for every
x\notin A
,

\deg(x,A)\geq\delta(G)-(n-a-1)\geq d
.
Hence, assume
2d\leq a<n/2
.
In this case,

\displaystyle|E(A,V\smallsetminus A)|

\displaystyle\geq a\left(\delta(G)-(a-1)\right)\geq a(n/2+d-a)

\displaystyle=an/2+ad-a^{2}=a(n-2a)/2+ad\geq d(n-2a)+ad=d(n-a).

Thus, by the pigeonhole principle,
there exists
x\in V\smallsetminus A
with at least
d
neighbours in
A
.
∎

Let us mention that an argument equivalent to
Lemma

4.19

was used in the proof of Theorem 36 in
[
13
]
.
We are ready to prove
Proposition

4.2
.

Proof of
Proposition

4.2
.

Let
\delta,\alpha,n_{0}
be the constants (depending on
\beta
) guaranteed by
Corollary

4.15
,
and let
n\geq\max\{n_{0},2/\alpha\}
. Let
c^{\prime}=c^{\prime}(\delta)
be the constant guaranteed by
Proposition

4.16
.
Set
c=\alpha c^{\prime}/2
, and let
1\leq d\leq cn/\log{n}
.
Let
G=(V,E)
be an
n
-vertex graph with
\delta(G)\geq(n+2d)/2-1

which is
\beta
-far from being bipartite.
We want to show that
G
is
d
-rigid.
By
Corollary

4.15
,
since
|E(G)|\geq n^{2}/4
and since
G
is
\beta
-far from being bipartite,
there exist pairwise disjoint vertex sets
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}\subseteq V

with
|A_{1}|=|A_{2}|=|A_{3}|=N\geq\alpha n\geq 2

and a subgraph
G^{\prime\prime}\subseteq G

such that the pairs
(A_{i},A_{j})
,
1\leq i<j\leq 3

are all
(\delta,2\delta)
-super-regular in
G^{\prime\prime}
.
Let
H=G^{\prime\prime}[A_{1}\cup A_{2}\cup A_{3}]
be the induced tripartite graph.
By
Proposition

4.16
,

H
admits a strong
D
-rigid partition
for
D=\left\lfloor{c^{\prime}N/\log{N}}\right\rfloor\geq cn/\log{n}\geq d
.
In particular, by
Theorem

2.3
,
H
is a
d
-rigid subgraph of
G
with
3N\geq 2d
vertices.
Let
A
be a largest vertex set of
G
for which
G[A]
is
d
-rigid.
Assume to the contrary that
A\neq V
.
Then, since
|A|\geq 2d
, by
Lemma

4.19
, there exists
x\in V\smallsetminus A

such that
\deg(x,A)\geq d
.
Thus, by
Lemma

2.2
,
G[A\cup\{x\}]
is also
d
-rigid, a contradiction.
∎

5

Concluding remarks

In this work, we studied the minimum degree conditions that guarantee the generic rigidity of a graph in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For small values of
d
(
d=O(\sqrt{n})
), we established that every
n
-vertex graph with minimum degree at least
(n+d)/2-1
is
d
-rigid (
Theorem

1.1
), matching the known
d
-connectivity threshold and therefore confirming
1
in that range. For larger values of
d
(
d=O(n/\log^{2}n)
), we showed that the minimum degree condition
(n+2d)/2-1
ensures
d
-rigidity (
Theorem

1.2
), which is tight up to a factor of two in the coefficient of
d
.

Furthermore, as a corollary of our proof, we obtained a new lower bound on the pseudoachromatic number of a graph, depending on its minimum degree (
Theorem

1.3
), which may be of independent interest.

Despite this progress,
1
, which states that
the minimum degree threshold for
d
-rigidity is
\max\{(n+d)/2-1,\,2d-d(d+1)/n\}
, remains open. Proving it in full generality may require new techniques, particularly in the challenging regime where
d
is linear in
n
. We hope that the methods and ideas presented here will stimulate further research on these and related questions.

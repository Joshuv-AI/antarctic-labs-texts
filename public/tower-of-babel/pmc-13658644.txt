Silver nanoparticles supported on DABCO-based ionic liquid-functionalized magnetic polystyrene for the efficient reduction of 4-nitrophenol
Authors: Taraneh Abbaspour, Sakineh Asghari, Ghasem Firouzzadeh Pasha, Mahmood Tajbakhsh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The efficient conversion of nitrobenzene to aminophenols, typically achieved with metal catalysts or metal oxides, provides significant environmental benefits. In this study, a novel nanocatalyst was synthesized by immobilizing silver metal on DABCO-based ionic liquids supported on magnetic polystyrene. The synthesized nanoparticles were characterized by XRD, TGA, EDS & EDS mapping, FT-IR, and ICP and subsequently evaluated as solid-phase catalysts for the reduction of 4-nitrophenol (4-NP) with excess NaBH4 using UV-vis spectroscopy, which maintained good catalytic activity after six recycling cycles. The synthesized nanocatalyst was cost-effective and demonstrated excellent efficiency (⁓80% conversion) within 6 min, 15 mg of nanocatalyst, 25 mL of NaBH4 (2.5 mM), as well as straightforward separation during the reduction process. Additionally, kinetic analysis shows that the reduction of 4-NP follows a pseudo-first-order model with Kapp= 0.20 min− 1.Supplementary informationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s11671-026-04976-x.

Introduction

Seeking effective strategies to remediate contaminated water. Nitroaromatic compounds are a group of persistent and harmful water pollutants mainly originating from industrial, agricultural, and municipal wastewater discharges that pose significant threats to both human health and the environment. Among these pollutants, 4-nitrophenol (4-NP) has received considerable attention due to its high solubility, toxicity, and remarkable chemical stability, making it resistant to natural degradation processes. Therefore, its removal from wastewater by conventional methods remains a significant challenge [1–3].

Conventional methods such as adsorption, chemical precipitation, solid-phase extraction, photocatalytic degradation, and various electrochemical techniques often show limited efficiency for the removal of 4-NP from aqueous media [4–6]. However, chemical reduction of nitrophenols using suitable reducing agents has demonstrated high effectiveness in eliminating these toxic compounds from aqueous solutions by transforming them into less toxic or even value-added products [7, 8]. The reduction of 4-NP to 4-aminophenol (4-AP) using a reducing agent in the presence of a metal nanoparticle catalyst is an effective strategy for both environmental remediation and the production of 4-AP. This approach offers several advantages, including high efficiency, the absence of undesirable by-products, a simple reaction procedure, and the use of inexpensive and readily available materials [9, 10]. Moreover, 4-AP is a valuable synthetic intermediate in the pharmaceutical, dye, and fine chemical industries [11].

Metal nanoparticles (MNPs) with diverse sizes, shapes, and compositions have been extensively employed in catalysis, biosensing, optoelectronics, petroleum processing, and chemical industries [12, 13]. Noble-metal-based nanoparticles, including Pd, Pt, Rh, Ru, Au, and Ag, have attracted particular interest due to their excellent catalytic performance in organic transformations and nitro compound reductions [14, 15]. The superior catalytic activity of MNPs is largely attributed to their high surface-to-volume ratio and the presence of abundant low-coordination active sites such as edges and corners [16]. Among them, silver nanoparticles have been widely investigated owing to their high catalytic efficiency, natural abundance, and relatively low cost [17]. Nevertheless, their practical application is often hindered by particle aggregation resulting from high surface energy, which leads to a gradual loss of catalytic activity [18, 19]. In addition, the recovery of silver nanoparticles from reaction media is challenging due to their nanoscale dimensions and strong Brownian motion [20]. To overcome these limitations, the immobilization of silver nanoparticles onto solid supports such as polymers, zeolites, and silica has been proposed as an effective strategy [21–23].

Polystyrene (PS), a polymeric support, is widely utilized in the synthesis of heterogeneous catalysts due to its affordability, low toxicity, ease of recovery and separation, resistance to most organic solvents and water, excellent mechanical strength, and ease of surface functionalization [24–26]. In particular, ionic-liquid-functionalized polystyrene supports have demonstrated excellent performance in a variety of applications, including the synthesis of organic heterocycles [27], as well as dye and gas adsorption [28].

Conventional heterogeneous catalysts are typically recovered by centrifugation or filtration. However, these separation methods often become inefficient and time-consuming, particularly for particles with sizes below 100 nm, resulting in catalyst loss and reduced process efficiency. To overcome these limitations, magnetic nanoparticles have emerged as an attractive alternative because they enable rapid and efficient magnetic separation using an external magnetic field, thereby simplifying catalyst recovery and reuse. Consequently, magnetic nanomaterials have attracted considerable attention over the past few decades due to their excellent separation capability, high stability, and broad applicability in catalytic systems [29–31].

The reduction of nitrophenols using sodium borohydride is a well-known model reaction for evaluating catalytic efficiency. However, in the absence of a suitable catalyst, the reaction does not proceed even after prolonged reaction times [32, 33]. In addition, many conventional catalytic systems suffer from limited stability, slow reaction rates, nanoparticle aggregation, or complicated separation procedures.

The reduction of nitrophenols using sodium borohydride is a well-known model reaction for evaluating catalytic efficiency. However, in the absence of a suitable catalyst, the reaction does not proceed even after prolonged reaction times [32, 33]. In addition, many conventional catalytic systems suffer from limited stability, slow reaction rates, nanoparticle aggregation, or complicated separation procedures. Although numerous Ag-supported magnetic catalysts have been reported for the reduction of 4-nitrophenol, most studies have focused primarily on improving catalytic activity through conventional support modification. Comparatively less attention has been devoted to the rational design of multifunctional catalyst supports capable of simultaneously enhancing Ag nanoparticle stabilization, catalyst durability, electron-transfer efficiency, and magnetic recoverability. In particular, the combined use of DABCO-derived ionic liquid functionalities and metformin as complementary nitrogen-rich coordinating components on a magnetic polystyrene support has not been previously explored for this catalytic system.

To overcome these limitations, we designed a multifunctional magnetic nanocatalyst in which silver nanoparticles are immobilized on an ionic-liquid-functionalized magnetic polystyrene support (MPS-DAB-MET/Ag). The DABCO-functionalized support provides positively charged nitrogen centers that enhance electrostatic interactions with reactant species and improve the dispersion of silver nanoparticles. Furthermore, metformin, containing nitrogen-rich biguanide moieties, was incorporated to enhance surface functionality. The abundant amine groups provide strong hydrogen-bonding capability and electronic interactions with silver species, facilitating nanoparticle stabilization and suppressing aggregation. These nitrogen-containing groups may also contribute to improved electron-transfer efficiency by creating a favorable microenvironment around the silver active sites. The synergistic integration of these functional components provides a simple, magnetically recoverable, and reusable catalytic platform that combines efficient Ag stabilization with good structural stability and practical applicability.

Chemical and reagent

All materials used in this study, including 4-NP, solvents, and the reducing agent, were sourced from Merck and Sigma Aldrich, ensuring high quality. The solutions were prepared using deionized water (DI). Detailed information regarding the materials, analytical tools, and procedures for synthesizing the catalyst can be found in the supporting file.

Preparation of nanocatalyst

Synthesis of Ag-supported onto the ILs modified-magnetic PS (MPS-DAB-MET/Ag)

The synthesis of MPS, MPS-DAB, MPS-DAB-Cl, and MPS-DAB-MET has been reported in our previous studies. According to the method described by Abbaspour et al. [34], chloromethylated polystyrene microspheres were magnetized using FeCl3·6H2O and FeCl2·4H2O in ethanol. During this step, Fe2+ and Fe3+ ions react to form an iron oxide (Fe3O4) coating on the polystyrene surface, resulting in magnetic polystyrene microspheres (MPS). As shown in Scheme 1, to obtain the MPS-DAB-MET, the synthesized MPS was reacted with DABCO, 1,4-dichlorobutane, and metformin, respectively [34].

Silver nanoparticles were then immobilized onto the MPS-DAB-MET surface using a previously reported method with minor modifications [35, 36]. Briefly, 1 g of MPS-DAB-MET was dispersed in 10 mL of AgNO3 solution (10 mM) and stirred for 1 h at room temperature. Subsequently, 10 mL of NaBH4 solution (25 mM) was added dropwise under continuous stirring, which led to the in situ reduction of Ag+ ions and the formation of silver nanoparticles attached to the polymer surface. The resulting brown MPS-DAB-MET/Ag nanocatalyst was collected by magnetic separation, washed thoroughly with distilled water and ethanol, and dried overnight. The overall synthesis procedure is schematically shown in Scheme 1.

Catalytic activity of 4-nitrophenol

The reduction of 4-NP was selected to assess the catalytic activity of silver (I) immobilized on ionic-liquid-functionalized magnetic polystyrene. A typical procedure involved adding 25 mL of 0.25 M aqueous NaBH4 to 25 mL of 2.5 mM aqueous 4-NP at room temperature under continuous stirring. Upon the addition of sodium borohydride, the solution color changed from light yellow to dark yellow, which is attributed to the formation of 4-nitrophenolate ions under alkaline conditions. As the reaction proceeded in the presence of the catalyst, the solution gradually became colorless, consistent with the reduction of 4-NP under the applied reaction conditions (Fig. 1). The reaction progress was monitored by recording the UV–Vis absorbance of 2 mL aliquots at regular time intervals, as illustrated in Fig. 1 [37]. The characteristic absorption band of 4-nitrophenolate at approximately 400 nm gradually decreased during the reaction, while the spectral changes were consistent with the formation of 4-AP, as widely reported for NaBH4−mediated catalytic reduction of 4-NP.

Result and discussion

The homogenized Ag-supported onto the ILs-functionalized magnetic polystyrene (MPS-DAB-MET/Ag) was synthesized by post-modifying MPS with DABCO, 1, 4-dichlorobutane, and metformin, followed by coordination with silver (I) ion. As illustrated in Scheme 1, the MPS-DAB-MET/Ag nanocatalyst synthesis began with the in situ magnetization of PS-Cl in the presence of ferric salts. Reaction of MPS with DABCO in refluxing toluene led to the formation of MPS-DAB, and subsequent treatment with 1, 4-dichlorobutane and metformin produced MPS-DAB-Cl and MPS-DAB-MET, respectively. Modification of MPS using ILs and metformin improves the dispersibility and reactivity of MPS. Additionally, the nitrogen heteroatoms in metformin and DABCO increase the chelating ability of the MPS. The DABCO-based ionic liquid further enhances the catalytic stability by providing nitrogen-rich coordination sites that strongly anchor Ag nanoparticles, suppress aggregation, and minimize metal leaching during catalytic cycles. Consequently, the Ag active sites remain well-dispersed and stable, leading to sustained catalytic activity and improved reusability. Finally, the silver-ILs complex supported on MPS (MPS-DAB-MET/Ag) was prepared by reacting MPS-DAB-MET with AgNO3. The structures of all synthesized catalysts were confirmed through various analyses, including FT-IR, TGA, FE-SEM, EDS & EDS mapping, XRD, and ICP.

Characterization of the heterogeneous MPS-DAB-MET/Ag nanocatalyst

FT-IR spectroscopy was performed to analyze the structure of the synthesized nanocatalyst and monitor the chemical modifications during each functionalization step, as well as to provide indirect evidence for the interaction between Ag nanoparticles and the nitrogen containing functional groups of the support. The PS-Cl spectrum shows distinct peaks at 1262 cm− 1 (H-C-Cl vibration) and 752 cm− 1 (C-Cl stretching). For MPS, an additional peak at 560 cm− 1 (Fe-O stretching) confirms the successful coating of polystyrene with Fe3O4 nanoparticles [27]. In the spectrum of MPS-DAB, disappearance of peaks at 1262 cm⁻1 and 752 cm⁻1, along with a new peak at 1560 cm⁻1 (C-N stretching), indicates the successful attachment of DABCO to the MPS surface and the replacement of the C-Cl bond with a C-N bond. The spectrum of MPS-DAB-MET shows peaks at 3341 cm⁻1 (N-H vibrations), 2925 cm⁻1 (C-H vibrations), 1620 cm⁻1 (C = N vibrations) and 1477 cm⁻1 (C-N vibrations), confirming the successful modification of MPS-DAB-Cl [38]. The corresponding FT-IR spectrum of MPS-DAB-MET/Ag retains all the characteristic absorption bands of MPS-DAB-MET, indicating that the polymer framework remained intact after Ag immobilization. Notably, slight changes in the position and intensity of the C = N (1620 cm⁻¹) and C–N (1477–1560 cm⁻¹) bands were observed, which may be attributed to coordination interactions between Ag nanoparticles and the nitrogen donor atoms of the DABCO-derived ionic liquid and metformin moieties. Although FT-IR alone cannot unequivocally confirm Ag–N bond formation, these spectral changes, together with the XRD and EDS results, provide supporting evidence for the successful immobilization of Ag nanoparticles on the functionalized magnetic polymer (Fig. 2).

Figure 3 illustrates the thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) curves of PS-Cl, MPS, MPS-DAB-MET, and MPS-DAB-MET/Ag, which were used to evaluate the thermal stability and decomposition behavior of the synthesized materials. The TGA measurements were performed over a temperature range of 25–600 °C at a heating rate of 10 °C min⁻¹ under a nitrogen atmosphere. The TGA curves of all samples exhibited an initial weight loss below 200 °C, which is attributed to the evaporation of physically adsorbed water and residual organic solvents. A second major weight-loss stage was observed between 300 and 450 °C, corresponding to the decomposition of the organic functional groups and the polymeric shell.

The gradual changes observed in the thermal decomposition profiles from PS-Cl to MPS, and subsequently from MPS-DAB-MET to MPS-DAB-MET/Ag, are consistent with the successive surface functionalization steps and indicate successful modification of the magnetic polystyrene support. Furthermore, the slightly improved thermal stability of MPS-DAB-MET/Ag may be attributed to the immobilization of Ag species on the functionalized support, which enhances the thermal resistance of the hybrid material [39].

It should be noted that the TGA results primarily provide information on the thermal stability of the synthesized nanocatalyst. Therefore, the successful incorporation of the functional groups and Ag nanoparticles was further confirmed by complementary characterization techniques, including FT-IR, EDS, CHN, ICP, and XRD analyses. In addition, the silver loading of the MPS-DAB-MET/Ag catalyst was quantified by ICP analysis and determined to be 9.12 wt%, corresponding to approximately 0.845 mmol g⁻¹. This result confirms the successful immobilization of silver species on the ionic liquid-functionalized magnetic polystyrene support.

X-ray diffraction (XRD), based on Bragg’s law, was used to analyze the phase structure and identify the crystalline composition of the synthesized magnetic nanoparticles. The XRD pattern of MPS-DAB-MET/Ag, illustrated in Fig. 7, exhibits diffraction peaks at 2θ = 35.5°, 43.1°, 53.4°, and 57.0° which can be indexed to the (311), (400), (422), and (511) crystal planes of the spinel iron oxide structure, respectively, in good agreement with the standard JCPDS card No. 19–0629. Evaluation of catalytic activity for reduction of 4-nitrophenol

After characterizing the MPS-DAB-MET/Ag nanocatalyst, its catalytic performance was evaluated for the reduction of 4-NP using NaBH4 as the reducing agent. Reduction efficiency was determined by UV–Vis spectroscopy by monitoring the decrease in absorbance at 400 nm, and the conversion percentage was calculated using the equation: Conversion (%) = (1 − At/A0) × 100, where A0 and At represent the initial and time-dependent absorbance values of 4-NP, respectively. Prior to investigating the activity of MPS-DAB-MET/Ag, control experiments were carried out using PS-Cl, MPS, and MPS-DAB-MET, as well as in the absence of any catalyst (entries 1–4). As summarized in Table 1, the reduction of 4-NP with NaBH4 alone was negligible even after prolonged reaction time (entry 1).

Introducing polymeric PS-Cl, magnetic polystyrene (MPS), and IL-functionalized MPS-DAB-MET (10 mg) resulted in a gradual enhancement in 4-NP reduction (entries 2–4), which can be attributed to increased adsorption and surface-assisted electron transfer. The enhanced catalytic activity observed with MPS-DAB-MET/Ag is likely due to the incorporation of Ag nanoparticles on the support, confirming the importance of silver active sites (entry 5).

The effect of catalyst loading was examined by varying the amount of MPS-DAB-MET/Ag from 10 to 25 mg. Increasing the catalyst amount to 15 mg significantly accelerated the reduction process, while further increases to 20 and 25 mg led to no noticeable improvement, indicating that 15 mg is sufficient to provide adequate active sites under the studied conditions (entries 6–8).

The influence of NaBH4 concentration was also investigated using 15 mg of catalyst and a fixed 4-NP concentration. Increasing the NaBH4 volume beyond 25 mL did not result in a substantial enhancement in reduction efficiency, suggesting that excess NaBH4 does not further limit the reaction rate (entries 9–13).

The effect of reaction time was evaluated over a period of 1–8 min under the optimized conditions (15 mg catalyst and 25 mL of 0.25 M NaBH4). UV–Vis analysis revealed a rapid decrease in the characteristic absorption band of 4-nitrophenolate ions at 400 nm, and the conversion increased progressively from approximately 36% after 1 min to 80% after 6 min (entries 14–19). Further extending the reaction time to 7 and 8 min resulted in only a slight increase in conversion (82–83%; entries 20–21). Since this improvement was marginal, 6 min was selected as the practical optimum reaction time for all subsequent experiments, providing high catalytic efficiency while minimizing the reaction time (Fig. 7).

The reaction progress was monitored by measuring the decrease in absorbance at λmax = 400 nm, corresponding to the nitrophenolate ion, over a reaction time of 0 to 8 min, as shown in Fig. 8. Since NaBH4 was used in a large excess relative to 4-NP, its concentration remained effectively constant during the reaction. This approximation has been widely adopted for the catalytic reduction of 4-nitrophenol with NaBH4 because the concentration of the reducing agent remains effectively constant throughout the reaction. Therefore, the reduction process can be treated as a pseudo-first-order reaction with respect to 4-NP concentration.

To minimize the potential influence of external mass-transfer effects, the reaction was carried out under continuous magnetic stirring. Additionally, NaBH4 was used in large excess to maintain a nearly constant concentration of the reducing agent throughout the reaction. Under these experimental conditions, a linear relationship was observed in the plot of ln (At/A₀) versus reaction time (Fig. 9). The experimental data are well described by a pseudo-first-order kinetic model. The apparent rate constant (Kapp) was determined from the slope of the linear fit, calculated to be 0.20 min⁻1 (R2 = 0.984). Minor deviations between the fitted kinetic plot and direct absorbance values may arise from adsorption effects and baseline correction during UV–Vis measurements. It should be noted that the present kinetic analysis was intended to provide an apparent kinetic description for comparison of catalytic performance under identical experimental conditions. A comprehensive investigation of external and internal diffusion effects, catalyst surface saturation, and intrinsic reaction kinetics was beyond the scope of the present study and remains a subject for future investigation. All kinetic measurements were performed under identical reaction conditions, including catalyst dosage, NaBH4 concentration, temperature, and stirring conditions, to ensure a reliable comparison of the apparent reaction rates.

Comparison with other catalyst

To evaluate the catalytic performance of the synthesized MPS-DAB-MET/Ag nanocatalyst, its activity was compared with previously reported Ag-based catalysts for the reduction of 4-NP (Table 2). As summarized in the literature, the reported catalytic performances vary considerably depending on catalyst composition, synthesis strategy, and reaction conditions. While certain Ag-based catalysts exhibit higher apparent rate constants or conversion efficiencies, many require filtration or centrifugation for catalyst recovery and provide limited information regarding catalyst stability or recyclability. Under the present reaction conditions, the MPS-DAB-MET/Ag nanocatalyst exhibits an apparent rate constant of 0.20 min⁻¹, achieves approximately 80% conversion within 6 min using only 15 mg of catalyst, and can be rapidly separated by an external magnet. Considering the combined advantages of catalytic activity, facile magnetic recovery, structural stability, and reusability, the proposed catalyst represents a balanced and practically applicable heterogeneous catalytic system rather than one optimized solely for maximum reaction rate. Overall, although some previously reported Ag-based catalysts exhibit higher apparent rate constants or conversion efficiencies, the MPS-DAB-MET/Ag nanocatalyst offers a balanced combination of catalytic activity, magnetic recoverability, structural stability, and reusability. These features make it a practically attractive heterogeneous catalyst for the reduction of 4-NP.

Mechanism

The reduction of 4-NP to 4-AP in the presence of NaBH4 has been extensively investigated and is generally accepted to proceed through a surface-mediated catalytic pathway involving electron transfer via metal nanoparticles [47, 48]. Scheme 2 illustrates the proposed catalytic pathway for the reduction of 4-NP over the MPS-DAB-MET/Ag nanocatalyst. Because no in-situ spectroscopic or surface-sensitive experiments were performed, the mechanism presented here should be regarded as a plausible reaction pathway based on the experimental observations and the previous literature. The proposed mechanistic pathway is consistent with the observed pseudo-first-order kinetic behavior under conditions of excess NaBH₄. Sodium borohydride serves as the primary hydrogen donor, generating active hydrogen species through hydrolysis in the aqueous reaction medium. The hydrolysis of NaBH₄ can be represented by the following reactions (Eqs. 2 and 3), which provide the hydrogen required for the catalytic reduction of 4-nitrophenol:2\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\rm{NaBH}}_{4}\,+\,{2}{\rm{H}}_2{\rm{O}}\, {\rightarrow}\, 2{\rm{H}}_{2}\,+\,{\rm{NaBO}}_{2}$$\end{document}3\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\rm{NaBH}}_{4}\,+\,3{\rm{H}}_{2}{\rm{O}} \,{\rightarrow}\, {\rm{H}}_{3}{\rm{BO}}_{4}\,+\,4{\rm{H}}_{2}\,+\,{\rm{NaOH}}$$\end{document}

At the catalyst surface, borohydride ions interact with the Ag nanoparticles and provide electrons for the reduction process, while 4-NP molecules are activated through interaction with the catalyst surface. The AgNPs act as efficient electron-transfer mediators, facilitating electron migration from borohydride species to the nitro group of 4-NP. This process results in the stepwise reduction of the nitro group through hydroxylamine intermediates, ultimately yielding 4-AP.

The enhanced catalytic performance of the MPS-DAB-MET/Ag nanocatalyst can be attributed to the synergistic contributions of the magnetic polystyrene support, the DABCO-derived ionic liquid functionalities, the nitrogen-rich metformin moieties, and the uniformly dispersed AgNPs. These structural features promote efficient Ag dispersion, facilitate charge transfer, provide abundant accessible catalytic sites, and suppress nanoparticle aggregation, thereby improving both catalytic activity and catalyst stability.

The observed pseudo-first-order kinetic behavior under excess NaBH4 conditions is consistent with a surface-mediated electron-transfer process in which the concentration of borohydride remains effectively constant throughout the reaction.

After completion of the reduction process, the generated 4-AP desorbs from the catalyst surface, regenerating the active catalytic sites for subsequent reaction cycles. The strong interaction between the nitrophenolate species and the catalyst surface is likely associated with adsorption through the nitro and phenolate oxygen atoms, which facilitates efficient electron transfer during the catalytic reaction.

Reusability of catalyst

The reusability and stability of the MPS-DAB-MET/Ag nanocatalyst were comprehensively evaluated through recycling experiments, hot filtration, ICP analysis, and post-reaction structural characterization. Owing to its magnetic nature, the catalyst was readily recovered from the reaction mixture within 20–30 s using an external magnet and reused under identical reaction conditions. As shown in Fig. 10, the catalyst retained approximately 90% of its initial catalytic activity after six consecutive cycles. Each recycling experiment was performed in triplicate, and the results are presented as the mean ± standard deviation with corresponding error bars, demonstrating the good reproducibility of the catalytic performance.

Cycle of catalyst reusability

To further evaluate the catalyst stability, the recovered catalyst after the sixth cycle was characterized using SEM and XRD, as shown in Fig. 11. No significant changes in morphology or crystalline structure were observed compared with the fresh catalyst, indicating that the catalyst framework remained structurally stable during repeated use. Furthermore, a hot filtration test confirmed the heterogeneous nature of the catalytic system. After magnetic removal of the catalyst from the reaction mixture, no further reduction of 4-NP was observed, indicating that the catalytic reaction proceeded exclusively in the presence of the solid catalyst. ICP analysis further demonstrated the stability of the active phase, with the Ag loading changing only slightly from 4.56 mmol/g⁻1 for the fresh catalyst to 4.41 mmol/g⁻1 after six consecutive recycling cycles, confirming negligible Ag leaching during the reaction. These findings demonstrate that the catalytic activity originates predominantly from the solid MPS-DAB-MET/Ag nanocatalyst rather than from dissolved silver species. The slight decrease in catalytic activity after repeated cycles can be reasonably attributed to partial blockage of active Ag sites by adsorbed reaction intermediates, minor catalyst loss during repeated magnetic separation, and trace Ag leaching during the recycling process.

Conclusion

In summary, a multifunctional magnetic nanocatalyst (MPS-DAB-MET/Ag) was successfully synthesized by immobilizing Ag nanoparticles on an ionic liquid-functionalized magnetic polystyrene support. Unlike conventional Ag-based magnetic catalysts, the present catalyst integrates the properties of DABCO-derived ionic liquid and metformin as nitrogen-rich stabilizing ligands into a magnetically recoverable polymer support, providing a versatile platform for Ag immobilization and catalytic reduction.

The comprehensive characterization results collectively verified the successful stepwise functionalization of the magnetic polystyrene support, the stable immobilization of AgNPs, and the preservation of the catalyst structure after synthesis. Under the optimized reaction conditions, the catalyst achieved approximately 80% conversion of 4-NP within 6 min and exhibited good recyclability, retaining approximately 90% of its relative catalytic activity after six consecutive cycles with suitable magnetic recovery. The apparent kinetic analysis followed a pseudo-first-order model with an apparent rate constant (kapp) of 0.20 min⁻1 under the condition of excess NaBH4. The synergistic combination of magnetic recovery capability, stable Ag immobilization, facile separation, and satisfactory catalytic performance demonstrates the potential of this versatile catalytic platform for the catalytic reduction of nitroaromatic pollutants and provides a useful strategy for designing recyclable Ag-based heterogeneous catalysts for environmental applications.

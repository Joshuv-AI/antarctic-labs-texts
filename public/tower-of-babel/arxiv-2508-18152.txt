Optical Signatures of Band Flatness and Anisotropic Quantum Geometry in Magic-Angle Twisted Bilayer Graphene
Pok Man Chiu

Abstract
We study the degree of band flatness and the anisotropic quantum geometry in magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene by varying the twist angle and the parameters of lattice relaxation using optical conductivity. We show that the degree of band flatness and its quantum geometry can be revealed through optical absorption and its resulting optical bounds, which are based on the trace condition in quantum geometry. More specifically, the narrow and isolated peak of optical absorption in the low-energy region provides information about the bandwidth of the two flat bands. When this value is smaller than the electron interaction, it serves as a critical condition for the emergence of flat band superconductivity. Furthermore, optical absorption also provides the gap value between the flat band and the dispersive band, and when this gap is larger than the electron interaction, it facilitates the realization of fractional Chern insulating phases. We show that the narrow and isolated peak of optical bound near zero energy decreases as lattice relaxation increases. Meanwhile, we demonstrate that the imaginary part of (generalized) optical Hall conductivity reveals the vanishing of the negative part of Berry curvature, which is enforced by the refined trace-determinant inequality. Accordingly, we show that the total amount of the negative part and component of the Berry curvature approaches zero in the single ideal flat-band case. In contrast, when considering all occupied bands, the total amount of the negative component is slightly different from zero. Lastly, we demonstrate that the vanishing of flat band velocity and the emergent chiral symmetry are sufficient conditions for the saturation of the trace condition, which pertains to the isotropic case. In contrast, the determinant condition can only be saturated in anisotropic systems. As a result, the non-vanishing difference between the optical bound and the imaginary part of the generalized optical Hall conductivity provides an anisotropic signature in fractional Chern insulating phases.



Introduction
.—Moiré superlattice materials provide ideal platforms for engineering strongly correlated phases of matter, in which flat bands are believed to play a key role. In these two-dimensional van der Waals heterostructures, the crucial tuning parameter is the twist angle, which leads to the emergence of nearly flat bands and the resulting strongly correlated phenomena. The most intriguing problem is the realization of flat band superconductivity and fractional Chern insulators (FCIs) in moiré materials. In 2018, the first experiment

Taniguchi, E. Kaxiras, and P. Jarillo-Herrero 2018

R. Bistritzer and A. H.
MacDonald 2011
. Recently, an experimental group discovered the FCI phase in TBG under a weak magnetic field (5 T)
Y. Xie et al. 2021
. Most recently, several groups have experimentally realized the fractional Chern insulator phase in twisted bilayer MoTe2
H. Park et al. 2023
;

;
Y. Zeng et al. 2023
;
F. Xu et al. 2023
;

In previous studies, most theoretical works on optical conductivity in twisted bilayer graphene (TBG) focused on gapless phases with larger twist angles

Firstly, inspired by the experimental observation of the emergence of superconductivity in TBG, we show that the band flatness revealed by optical conductivity can be categorized into three regions and is consistent with experiments as the twist angle is varied
L. Balents, C. R. Dean, D. K. Efetov, and A. F.

Secondly, we calculate the optical bound, which is a new tool that can experimentally probe band flatness and quantum geometry, especially in the ideal flat band limit and in systems with broken time-reversal symmetry, by adjusting the lattice relaxation. Thirdly, we demonstrate the positive (negative) properties of the Berry curvature in relation to the ideal flat band limit. Furthermore, we show the relationship between the vanishing of flat band velocities, emergent chiral symmetry, and the saturation of the trace condition (TC) and determinant condition (DC).

Optical Conductivity and Optical Bound
.—In this work, we focus on the linearly polarized optical conductivity, which can be expressed by the Kubo-Greenwood formula

;
D. A. Greenwood 1958
;

Törmä 2023

\displaystyle\sigma_{ab}(\omega)=\frac{e^{2}}{i\hbar}\sum_{m\neq n}\int\frac{d^{2}\bf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{f_{mn}(\mathbf{k})}{E_{mn}(\mathbf{k})}\frac{\mathcal{M}^{a}_{mn}(\mathbf{k})\mathcal{M}^{b}_{nm}(\mathbf{k})}{\hbar\omega+E_{mn}(\mathbf{k})+i\eta},

(5)

where the velocity matrix element
\mathcal{M}^{a}_{mn}(\mathbf{k})=\langle u_{m}(\mathbf{k})|\hbar\upsilon_{a}|u_{n}(\mathbf{k})\rangle

\upsilon_{a}=\frac{1}{\hbar}\frac{\partial H}{\partial k_{a}}

f_{mn}(\mathbf{k})=f_{m}(\mathbf{k})-f_{n}(\mathbf{k})
and
E_{mn}(\mathbf{k})=E_{m}(\mathbf{k})-E_{n}(\mathbf{k})

H(\mathbf{k})|u_{n}(\mathbf{k})\rangle=E_{n}(\mathbf{k})|u_{n}(\mathbf{k})\rangle

The low-energy region of the real part of the longitudinal optical conductivity provides information about the flat band, such as its bandwidth and multi-gap value, which are the main interests of this work. To connect optical conductivity to quantum geometry, we assume there is a full gap between the occupied band and the unoccupied band. \displaystyle\sigma_{\text{H}}(\omega)

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{M}^{a}_{mn}(\mathbf{k})\mathcal{M}^{b}_{nm}(\mathbf{k})\delta(\hbar\omega+E_{mn}(\mathbf{k})).

\displaystyle=\pi e^{2}\omega\int\frac{d^{2}\bf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{m\in occ}\sum_{n\in unocc}Q^{mn}_{ab}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle\times\delta(\hbar\omega+E_{mn}(\mathbf{k})).

Results and Discussion
.—In the last section, we introduced three useful quantities, namely
\Re\sigma_{aa}(\omega)

\mathcal{K}_{OP}(\omega)

1.2^{\circ}>\theta>1.0^{\circ}

P. Törmä, S. Peotta, and B. A. Bernevig 2022

Y. Xie, B. Lian, B. Jäck, X. Liu, C.-L. Chiu, K.
Watanabe, T. Taniguchi, B. A. Bernevig, and A. Yazdani 2019
;
P. Törmä, S. Peotta, and B. A. Bernevig 2022

1.2^{\circ}>\theta>1.0^{\circ}
[see Fig. 1(a2,b2)], the bandwidth is smaller than 25 meV, making the emergence of superconductivity possible
Y. Cao, V. Fatemi, S. Fang, K. Watanabe, T.
Taniguchi, E. Kaxiras, and P. Jarillo-Herrero 2018

On the other hand, the isolated peak contributed by the optical transition between nearly flat band is relatively narrow [see Fig. 1(b2)] compared to the twisted angle outside this region [see Fig. 1(b1, b3)]. This indicates that the effective mass of nearly flat band electrons is larger (more localized), according to the partial f-sum rule
T. Hazra, N. Verma, and M.
Randeria 2019
;
N. Verma, T. Hazra, M. Randeria 2021
.

In contract, for the region
\theta_{c1}>\theta\geq 1.2^{\circ}
[see Fig. 1(a1,b1)], the bandwidth is larger than 25 meV. Compared to the crucial condition, the emergence of superconductivity is unlikely and consistent with the experimental results
L. Balents, C. R. Dean, D. K. Efetov, and A. F.
Young 2020

In the region
\theta_{c2}<\theta\leq 1.0^{\circ}
[see Fig. 1(a3,b3)], there is a different tendency: the gap between nearly flat band and depressive band is very small or zero, as revealed by the optical conductivity. The corresponding low-energy peak is much higher, and the zone size between the nearly flat band transitions overlaps with the dispersive bands [see Fig. 1(b3)]. This indicates that the nearly flat band has a smaller effective mass (more delocalized), and the corresponding gap between the nearly flat band and the dispersive band is small. As a result, flat band superconductivity is less likely since the electrons from the dispersive bands, which will participate in or mediate the formation of Cooper pairs, have higher kinetic energy. However, there was a exceptional case found in two experimental groups

Conclusions

Our work can be applied to other moiré superlattice materials as well as kagome materials.

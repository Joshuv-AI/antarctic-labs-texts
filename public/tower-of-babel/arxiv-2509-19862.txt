Parameter Estimation for Jump-Diffusion Stochastic Master Equations
Weichao Liang, Shuixin Xiao, Daoyi Dong, Ian R. Petersen

Abstract
This paper investigates parameter estimation for open quantum systems under continuous observation, whose conditional dynamics are governed by jump–diffusion stochastic master equations (SMEs) associated with quantum nondemolition (QND) measurements. Estimation of model parameters such as coupling strengths or measurement efficiencies is essential, yet in practice these parameters are often uncertain. We first establish the existence and well-posedness of a reduced quantum filter: for an N N -level system, the conditional evolution can be represented in an 𝒪 ⁡ ( N ) \mathcal{O}(N) -dimensional real state space rather than the full 𝒪 ⁡ ( N 2 ) \mathcal{O}(N^{2}) density-matrix state space. Building on this, we extend the stability theory of quantum filters, showing that exponential convergence persists not only under mismatched initial states but also in the presence of parameter mismatch. Finally, we develop an estimation framework for continuous parameter domains and prove almost sure consistency of the estimator in the long-time limit. These results yield a rigorous treatment of parameter estimation for jump–diffusion SMEs, combining structural reduction with stability and identifiability analysis, and thereby extend the mathematical theory of parameter estimation for open quantum systems.

Introduction

The theory of quantum trajectories
[
5
,
31
,
13
]
provides a stochastic description of open quantum systems under continuous measurements
[
11
]
. In this setting, the quantum filtering approach initiated by Belavkin
[
6
]
describes the conditional evolution of the system’s state via stochastic master equations (SMEs)
[
4
]
. For quantum nondemolition (QND) measurement schemes
[
19
]
, where the measured observable commutes with the system Hamiltonian and is preserved by the measurement process, the conditional state evolves according to a jump–diffusion stochastic differential equation
[
28
]
incorporating both continuous diffusive terms (from homodyne or heterodyne detection) and discontinuous jump terms (from photon counting). The asymptotic stability of quantum filters under QND measurements has been rigorously established
[
8
]
, providing a foundation for parameter estimation on quantum trajectories.

In practice, however, precise knowledge of system parameters such as coupling strengths or measurement efficiencies is often unavailable. Parameter estimation in continuously observed quantum systems has therefore become an important area of research. Early theoretical work by Mabuchi
[
23
]
pioneered the real-time estimation of Hamiltonian parameters from measurement records. Subsequent contributions introduced Bayesian and particle filtering techniques for quantum parameter estimation
[
18
,
14
,
24
,
27
]
. Related approaches have been proposed for discrete-time quantum experiments and hypothesis testing scenarios
[
29
,
9
]
, as well as adaptive filtering schemes based on stochastic approximation and control
[
17
,
15
]
. Despite these advances, rigorous performance guarantees for continuous parameter estimation remain limited. Most existing methods assume that the parameter belongs to a finite set and propagate multiple filters in parallel
[
14
,
29
,
9
]
. While such multiple-model approaches may identify the most likely discrete candidate, they do not guarantee that the posterior probability concentrates on the true parameter as time tends to infinity. This limitation is particularly severe when the true parameter lies outside the predefined grid.

In this work we develop a framework for continuous parameter estimation in jump–diffusion quantum filters that overcomes these finite–set limitations. We consider a general SME model for an open quantum system with uncertain initial state, coupling strength, and measurement efficiencies, continuously monitored via QND measurements involving both diffusive and counting observations. Our approach employs a reduced filter representation, which allows one to track parameters without resorting to a full Bayesian update over the
O(N^{2})
-dimensional density-matrix space. Instead, a bank of reduced filtering equations is propagated for a strategically chosen set of candidate parameters, each producing a likelihood weight according to the observed measurement record and thereby yielding an online estimator.

We establish two main theoretical results underpinning this framework. We prove that quantum filters remain exponentially stable under model uncertainty, encompassing both mismatched initial states and incorrect parameter values. Even when the filter is initialized with an incorrect parameter, the conditional state converges exponentially fast toward that of the filter driven by the true dynamics, extending previous stability analyses
[
30
,
1
,
7
,
2
]
to the case of mismatched models. Building on this stability property, we show that the estimation scheme achieves almost sure consistency in the following sense: as
t\to\infty
, the estimator
\hat{\lambda}(t)
converges almost surely to a predetermined subinterval of the parameter domain containing the true value. Partitioning the admissible interval
[\underline{\lambda},\bar{\lambda}]
into finitely many subintervals, each associated with a candidate filter, ensures that one subinterval contains the true parameter. By refining the partition, the estimation accuracy can be made arbitrarily fine, at the expense of increasing the number of candidate filters.

These results provide a rigorous framework for continuous online parameter estimation in SMEs with both Poisson jump and diffusive measurements. Earlier studies have largely focused on purely diffusive measurements (e.g., homodyne detection) or discrete-time settings, whereas our analysis addresses the realistic case of simultaneous photon counting and diffusion, as encountered in quantum optics
[
31
]
. The reduced-filter representation avoids the intractable propagation of full probability densities over the parameter space and is rigorously justified within our framework, together with quantitative convergence guarantees. This yields a strong form of consistency that was absent in prior methods.

We consider quantum systems described on a finite dimensional Hilbert space

(2)

3
Parameter estimation via maximum likelihood

We now develop a parameter estimation framework for jump–diffusion SMEs under QND measurements (Assumption
1
). Let
\lambda\in[\underline{\lambda},\bar{\lambda}]
denote the true parameter and
\hat{\lambda}
its estimate. The estimate is required to satisfy a prescribed accuracy criterion, e.g.,
\lambda/\hat{\lambda}\in[\bar{a},\bar{b}]
with
0<\bar{a}<1<\bar{b}
. Inspired by system dilation methods
[
14
,
24
,
29
,
9
]
, we propagate
\mathbf{n}
parallel filters, each associated with a candidate parameter, and assign to each a likelihood
\pi_{n}(t)
based on the measurement record up to time
t
. The maximum likelihood estimator then selects
\hat{\lambda}_{n^{*}}
corresponding to the dominant likelihood.

Earlier approaches
[
14
,
29
,
9
]
exploited the stability of quantum filters
[
30
,
1
,
8
]
but were restricted to finite parameter sets
\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{\mathbf{n}}\}
. In practice, physical parameters vary over continuous intervals. Discretization methods
[
29
]
or likelihood-based refinements
[
9
]
provide approximations, yet without rigorous guarantees of consistency when the true parameter lies outside the candidate grid.

Here we establish a mathematical foundation for maximum likelihood estimation on continuous parameter domains. The key ingredient is the robust exponential stability of quantum filters under parameter mismatch (Theorem
2.7
), quantified by the tolerance Condition
1
. This allows us to partition the admissible interval
[\underline{\lambda},\bar{\lambda}]
into subintervals, each ensuring stability of the associated filter. One of such subintervals contains the true parameter, and by refining the partition the estimation accuracy can be made arbitrarily high. The MLE then selects
\hat{\lambda}_{n^{*}}
corresponding to that subinterval, yielding an almost sure consistent estimator in the long-time limit.

In the following, we detail this construction for scalar parameters (e.g.,
\gamma_{k}
,
\eta_{k}
,
\theta_{k}
,
\zeta_{k,\bar{k}}
,
\iota_{k}
).
This can be extended to a vector of parameters by further dilating the system.

3.1
Augmented stochastic master equations

According to Theorem
2.7
, robust stability of the quantum filter depends on tolerances between the true and estimated parameters, notably the measurement efficiencies and coupling strengths. Our objective is to estimate parameters

such that

\lambda/\hat{\lambda}\in[\bar{a},\bar{b}],\qquad 0<\bar{a}<1<\bar{b},

where the class of admissible parameters includes
\gamma_{k},\eta_{k}
for

and
\theta_{k},\zeta_{k,\bar{k}},\iota_{k}

Given the coarse prior information that

\underline{\lambda}\in[a\hat{\lambda}_{1},b\hat{\lambda}_{1})

To formalize this construction we embed the candidate parameters into an augmented Hilbert space

\overline{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}(\mu):=\textstyle\sum^{\mathbf{n}}_{n=1}|n\rangle\langle n|\otimes H(\mu),\quad\mu\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{k}(\nu_{k}):=\textstyle\sum^{\mathbf{n}}_{n=1}|n\rangle\langle n|\otimes A_{k}(\nu_{k}),\quad\nu_{k}\in\mathbb{R},\quad\forall k\in[N_{P}],

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{k}(\gamma_{k}):=\textstyle\sum^{\mathbf{n}}_{n=1}|n\rangle\langle n|\otimes\sqrt{\gamma_{k}}L_{k},\quad\gamma_{k}\geq 0,\quad\forall k\in[N_{D}],

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{L}}_{k}(\eta_{k},\gamma_{k}):=\textstyle\sum^{\mathbf{n}}_{n=1}|n\rangle\langle n|\otimes\sqrt{\eta_{k}\gamma_{k}}L_{k},\quad\eta_{k}\in(0,1]\text{ and }\gamma_{k}>0,\quad\forall k\in[N_{D}],

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{k}(\theta_{k}):=\textstyle\sum^{\mathbf{n}}_{n=1}|n\rangle\langle n|\otimes\theta_{k}\mathbf{I}_{\mathbb{H}},\quad\theta_{k}\geq 0,\quad\forall k\in[N_{J}],

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{k,\bar{k}}(\zeta_{k,\bar{k}},\iota_{\bar{k}}):=\textstyle\sum^{\mathbf{n}}_{n=1}|n\rangle\langle n|\otimes\sqrt{\zeta_{k,\bar{k}}\iota_{\bar{k}}}C_{\bar{k}},\quad\zeta_{k,\bar{k}}\geq 0\text{ and }\iota_{k}>0,\quad\forall k,\bar{k}\in[N_{J}].

\hat{\mathbf{A}}_{k}
,
\hat{\bar{\mathbf{L}}}_{k}
,
\hat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}_{k}
,
\hat{\bar{\mathbf{C}}}_{k,\bar{k}}
and the chosen
\hat{\Xi}_{0}
, the solution to the augmented filter (

\displaystyle\hat{\Xi}(t)=\sum^{\mathbf{n}}_{n=1}|n\rangle\langle n|\otimes\hat{\sigma}_{n}(t),\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

Taking the partial trace over the
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{n}}
yields, for each
n\in[\mathbf{n}]
,

\displaystyle d\hat{\sigma}_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=\Big(-\mathfrak{i}[H(\hat{\mu}_{n}),\hat{\sigma}_{n}(t-)]+\textstyle\sum^{N_{D}}_{k=1}\hat{\gamma}_{k,n}\mathcal{D}_{L_{k}}(\hat{\sigma}_{n}(t-))+\sum^{N_{J}}_{k=1}\hat{\iota}_{k,n}\mathcal{D}_{C_{k}}(\hat{\sigma}_{n}(t-))

\displaystyle~~+\textstyle\sum^{N_{P}}_{k=1}\mathcal{D}_{A_{k}(\hat{\nu}_{k,n})}(\hat{\sigma}_{n}(t-))\Big)dt+\sum^{N_{D}}_{k=1}\Big(\sqrt{\hat{\eta}_{k,n}\hat{\gamma}_{k,n}}(L_{k}\hat{\sigma}_{n}(t-)+\hat{\sigma}_{n}(t-)L_{k}^{*})

\displaystyle~~\quad-\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\bar{\mathbf{L}}}_{k}\hat{\Xi}(t-)+\hat{\Xi}(t-)\hat{\bar{\mathbf{L}}}^{*}_{k})\hat{\sigma}_{n}(t-)\Big)\big(dW_{k}(t)+\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{k}(\Xi(t-),\hat{\Xi}(t-))dt\big)

\displaystyle~~+\textstyle\sum^{N_{J}}_{k=1}\Big(\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{k,n}(\hat{\sigma}_{n}(t-))}{\bar{\mathcal{T}}[\hat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}_{k},\hat{\bar{\mathbf{C}}}_{k}](\hat{\Xi}(t-))}-\hat{\sigma}_{n}(t-)\Big)\big(d\mathsf{N}_{k}(t)-\bar{\mathcal{T}}[\hat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}_{k},\hat{\bar{\mathbf{C}}}_{k}](\hat{\Xi}(t-))dt\big),\quad

(22)

where
\hat{\sigma}_{n}(0)=\pi_{n}(0)\hat{\rho}_{0}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{k,n}(\hat{\sigma})=\hat{\theta}_{k,n}\hat{\sigma}+\sum^{N_{J}}_{\bar{k}=1}\hat{\zeta}_{k,\bar{k},n}\hat{\iota}_{\bar{k},n}C_{\bar{k}}\hat{\sigma}C_{\bar{k}}^{*}
.

It is natural to look for
\pi_{n}(t)
such that

\hat{\sigma}_{n}(t)=\pi_{n}(t)\,\hat{\rho}_{n}(t),

where
\hat{\rho}_{n}(t)
is the normalized quantum filter driven by the
n
-th candidate parameters (same form as (
9
) with

\hat{\mu}_{n}
,
\hat{\nu}_{k,n}
,
\hat{\gamma}_{k,n}
,
\hat{\iota}_{k,n}
,
\hat{\eta}_{k,n}
,
\hat{\theta}_{k,n}
, and
\hat{\zeta}_{k,\bar{k},n}
), and with initial condition
\hat{\rho}_{n}(0)=\hat{\rho}_{0}
. A Doléans-Dade SDE that achieves this factorization is:

\displaystyle d\pi_{n}(t)=\pi_{n}(t)\Big[

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{N_{D}}\Big(\sqrt{\hat{\eta}_{k,n}\hat{\gamma}_{k,n}}\mathrm{Tr}(L_{k}\hat{\rho}_{n}(t-)+\hat{\rho}_{n}(t-)L_{k}^{*})

\displaystyle-\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\bar{\mathbf{L}}}_{k}\hat{\Xi}(t-)+\hat{\Xi}(t-)\hat{\bar{\mathbf{L}}}^{*}_{k})\Big)\big(dW_{k}(t)+\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{k}(\Xi(t-),\hat{\Xi}(t-))dt\big)

\displaystyle\textstyle+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{J}}\Big(\frac{\mathrm{Tr}\big(\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{k,n}(\hat{\rho}_{n}(t-))\big)}{\bar{\mathcal{T}}[\hat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}_{k},\hat{\bar{\mathbf{C}}}_{k}](\hat{\Xi}(t-))}-1\Big)\big(d\mathsf{N}_{k}(t)-\bar{\mathcal{T}}[\hat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}_{k},\hat{\bar{\mathbf{C}}}_{k}](\hat{\Xi}(t-))dt\big)\Big].

(23)

Proposition 3.4
.

The system (
23
) has a unique global solution
\pi_{n}(t)\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{n}}
(resp.
\pi_{n}(t)\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbf{n}}
) for all
t>0
when
\pi_{n}(0)\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{n}}
(resp.
\pi_{n}(0)\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbf{n}}
),
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely. Moreover, for all
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\hat{\Xi}(t)=\sum^{\mathbf{n}}_{n=1}|n\rangle\langle n|\otimes\pi_{n}(t)\hat{\rho}_{n}(t),\quad\pi_{n}(t)=\mathrm{Tr}\big((|n\rangle\langle n|\otimes\mathbf{I}_{\mathbb{H}})\hat{\Xi}(t)\big)=\sum^{\mathbf{j}}_{j=1}\hat{\mathsf{q}}_{n,j}(t),\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

where
\hat{\mathsf{q}}_{n,j}(t)
is given by (
20
).

Proof.

By the standard Doléans–Dade representation
[
3
, Chapter 5.1]
, (
23
) admits a unique strong solution in stochastic exponential form. In particular, if
\pi_{n}(0)>0
implies
\pi_{n}(t)>0
, and
\pi_{n}(0)=0
implies
\pi_{n}(t)=0
for all
t\geq 0
,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely. Then, applying Itô’s formula, we have

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}_{n}(t)=\pi_{n}(t)\hat{\rho}_{n}(t),\quad\forall t\geq 0,\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

Comparing with (
22
) yields the claimed factorization and the identities

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\bar{\mathbf{L}}}_{k}\hat{\Xi}(t)+\hat{\Xi}(t)\hat{\bar{\mathbf{L}}}^{*}_{k})=\textstyle\sum^{\mathbf{n}}_{n=1}\pi_{n}(t)\sqrt{\hat{\eta}_{k,n}\hat{\gamma}_{k,n}}\mathrm{Tr}(L_{k}\hat{\rho}_{n}(t)+\hat{\rho}_{n}(t)L_{k}^{*}),\quad\forall k\in[N_{D}],\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{T}}[\hat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}_{k},\hat{\bar{\mathbf{C}}}_{k}](\hat{\Xi}(t))=\textstyle\sum^{\mathbf{n}}_{n=1}\pi_{n}(t)\mathrm{Tr}\big(\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{k,n}(\hat{\rho}_{n}(t))\big),\quad\forall k\in[N_{J}],\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

Summing (
23
) over
n
gives

\displaystyle d\sum^{\mathbf{n}}_{n=1}\pi_{n}(t)=\Big(1-\sum^{\mathbf{n}}_{n=1}\pi_{n}(t)\Big)\Big(

\displaystyle\sum^{N_{D}}_{k=1}\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\bar{\mathbf{L}}}_{k}\hat{\Xi}(t-)+\hat{\Xi}(t-)\hat{\bar{\mathbf{L}}}^{*}_{k})\big(dW_{k}(t)+\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{k}(\Xi(t-),\hat{\Xi}(t-))\big)

\displaystyle+\sum^{N_{D}}_{k=1}\big(d\mathsf{N}_{k}(t)-\bar{\mathcal{T}}[\hat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}_{k},\hat{\bar{\mathbf{C}}}_{k}](\hat{\Xi}(t-))dt\big)\Big),

whose coefficients are a.s. bounded since
\hat{\Xi}(t)\in\mathcal{S}(\overline{\mathbb{H}})
. The functional Lipschitz conditions
[
26
, pp 256]
hold, as a concequence, the existence and uniqueness of the solution can be ensured
[
26
, Theorem 5.7]
. Hence,
\sum^{\mathbf{n}}_{n=1}\pi_{n}(t)=1
for all
t\geq 0
almost surely whenever
\sum^{\mathbf{n}}_{n=1}\pi_{n}(0)=1
. Therefore,
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbf{n}}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{n}}
are almost surely invariant for
\pi(t)
.

Finally, using
\hat{\Xi}(t)=\sum^{\mathbf{n}}_{n=1}|n\rangle\langle n|\otimes\pi_{n}(t)\hat{\rho}_{n}(t)
and Proposition
3.1
gives

\displaystyle\pi_{n}(t)=\mathrm{Tr}\big((|n\rangle\langle n|\otimes\mathbf{I}_{\mathbb{H}})\hat{\Xi}(t)\big)=\sum^{\mathbf{j}}_{j=1}\hat{\mathsf{q}}_{n,j}(t),\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

The proof is complete.

\square

Next, we state our main theorem on the asymptotic behavior of the selection probability
\pi_{n^{*}}(t)
.

Theorem 3.5

(Asymptotic consistency of parameter selection)
.

Let
\pi(0)\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{n}}
and
\Xi_{0}=|n^{*}\rangle\langle n^{*}|\otimes\rho_{0}
. Suppose that the conditions of Theorem
3.3
hold.
Then, for all
n\in[\mathbf{n}]
,

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n}(t)=\delta_{n,n^{*}}
,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely. Moreover, for all
n\neq n^{*}
,

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\frac{\pi_{n}(t)}{\pi_{n^{*}}(t)}\leq\max_{i,j}\Big(-\sum_{k=1}^{N_{D}}\bar{\Phi}^{k}_{n,i,n^{*},j}(\eta,\gamma)-\sum_{k=1}^{N_{J}}\bar{\Psi}^{k}_{n,i,n^{*},j}(\Gamma)\Big)<0,\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(24)

Proof.

By Theorem
3.3
and Proposition
3.1
,

\mathbb{P}(\lim_{t\to\infty}\pi_{n^{*}}(t)=1)=\mathbb{P}\left(\lim_{t\to\infty}\textstyle\sum^{\mathbf{j}}_{j=1}\hat{\mathsf{q}}_{n^{*},j}(t)=1\right)=\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{\mathbf{j}}\hat{\mathsf{q}}_{n^{*},j}(0)=1.

Additionally, by Proposition
3.4
,
\pi(t)\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{j}}
for all
t\geq 0
almost surely. Therefore, for all
n\neq n^{*}
:

\mathbb{P}(\lim_{t\to\infty}\pi_{n}(t)=0)=1.

Furthermore, for all
n\neq n^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\frac{\pi_{n}(t)}{\pi_{n^{*}}(t)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\frac{\sum^{\mathbf{j}}_{i=1}\hat{\mathsf{q}}_{n,i}(t)}{\sum^{\mathbf{j}}_{j=1}\hat{\mathsf{q}}_{n^{*},j}(t)}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{i\in[\mathbf{j}]}\left(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\frac{\mathbf{j}\hat{\mathsf{q}}_{n,i}(t)}{\hat{\mathsf{q}}_{n^{*},\mathcal{R}}(t)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{i\in[\mathbf{j}]}\Big(\textstyle-\sum_{k=1}^{N_{D}}\bar{\Phi}^{k}_{n,i,n^{*},\mathcal{R}}(\eta,\gamma)-\sum_{k=1}^{N_{J}}\bar{\Psi}^{k}_{n,i,n^{*},\mathcal{R}}(\Gamma)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{i,j}\Big(\textstyle-\sum_{k=1}^{N_{D}}\bar{\Phi}^{k}_{n,i,n^{*},j}(\eta,\gamma)-\sum_{k=1}^{N_{J}}\bar{\Psi}^{k}_{n,i,n^{*},j}(\Gamma)\Big)<0,\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

where the second inequality follows from the same arguments as in Theorem
3.3
.

\square

Remark 3.6
.

In summary, Theorem
3.5
provides sufficient conditions for parameter estimation that guarantee the robust stability of the augmented filter. Building on Theorem
2.7
, we next analyze specific parameter-estimation scenarios and identify sufficient conditions under which Condition
2
holds.

3.3
Estimating the diffusion parameters
\eta_{k}
and
\gamma_{k}

We now focus on estimating the parameters
\{\eta_{k}\}
,
\{\gamma_{k}\}
, or their product
\{\eta_{k}\gamma_{k}\}
for
k\in[N_{D}]
.
We assume that, aside from the parameter of interest, all other quantities appearing in Condition
2
are known.
For clarity, we present the estimation procedure for
\eta_{k}\gamma_{k}
, noting that the same reasoning applies to estimating
\eta_{k}
or
\gamma_{k}
individually.
To simplify the exposition, we restrict attention to the purely diffusive case (
N_{J}=0
) with a single measurement channel (
N_{D}=1
).
The multi-channel setting can be handled by straightforward generalization.

Let

\lambda=\sqrt{\eta\gamma}\in[\underline{\lambda},\bar{\lambda}]

with
\underline{\lambda}>0
, and the requirement
\lambda/\hat{\lambda}\in[\bar{a},\bar{b}]
with
0<\bar{a}<1<\bar{b}
.
We define a sequence of estimators
\{\hat{\lambda}_{n}\}_{n\in[\mathbf{n}]}
via

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}_{n+1}=\frac{b}{a}\hat{\lambda}_{n},\quad\underline{\lambda}=a\hat{\lambda}_{1},\quad\bar{\lambda}\in(a\hat{\lambda}_{\mathbf{n}},b\hat{\lambda}_{\mathbf{n}}],

where
0<\bar{a}\leq a<1<b\leq\bar{b}
, and the total number of estimators is given by:
\mathbf{n}=\lceil\log(\bar{\lambda}/\underline{\lambda})/\log(b/a)\rceil
.

Here
a
and
b
remain unspecified. We next derive admissible bounds for
(a,b)
that guarantee Condition
2
is satisfied.
Define
\Lambda_{a,b}:=\bigcup^{\mathbf{n}}_{n=1}(a\hat{\lambda}_{n},b\hat{\lambda}_{n})
.
The first objective is to determine ranges of
a
and
b
such that for every
\lambda\in\Lambda_{a,b}
the selection probability converges asymptotically:
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n}(t)=\delta_{n,n^{*}}
,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely, where
\pi_{n}(t)
represents the likelihood that
\lambda/\hat{\lambda}_{n}\in(a,b)
given the measurement record up to time
t
, and
n^{*}
is the index of the subinterval containing the true parameter.

Although it is possible to work under Assumption
2
, doing so requires substantially more technical analysis.
For the augmented filter, we instead impose the following stronger but simpler condition.

Assumption 3

(Identifiability of Diffusive measurement operators)
.

For the diffusive measurement operators
L_{k}=\sum^{\mathbf{j}}_{j=1}l_{k,j}\Pi_{j}
with
k\in[N_{D}]
, we require:

•

For all
i\neq j
and for all
k\in[N_{D}]
,
\Re\{l_{k,i}\}\neq\Re\{l_{k,j}\}
. Moreover,
\Re\{l_{j}\}\neq 0
for all
j\in[\mathbf{j}]
.

The second condition guarantees that each measurement operator contributes nontrivially to the observed output.
If
\Re\{l_{k,j}\}=0
, then
\bar{\Phi}_{m,i,n,j}(\eta,\gamma)=(\hat{\lambda}_{m}\Re\{l_{k,i}\})^{2}
, and in particular
\bar{\Phi}_{m,j,n,j}(\eta,\gamma)=0
for
m\neq n
.
In this case, it becomes impossible to distinguish between
\mathrm{Tr}(M_{n,j}\hat{\Xi}(t))
and
\mathrm{Tr}(M_{m,j}\hat{\Xi}(t))
from measurement data, violating Theorem
3.3
.

The second condition ensures that each measurement operator contributes nontrivially to the observed output. If
\Re\{l_{j}\}=0
, then
\bar{\Phi}_{m,i,n,j}(\eta,\gamma)=(\hat{\lambda}_{m}\Re\{l_{i}\})^{2}
, and in particular,
\bar{\Phi}_{m,j,n,j}(\eta,\gamma)=0
for
m\neq n
. In this case, it becomes impossible to distinguish between
\mathrm{Tr}(M_{n,j}\hat{\Xi}(t))
and
\mathrm{Tr}(M_{m,j}\hat{\Xi}(t))
from measurement data, violating Theorem
3.3
.

Under Assumption
3
, define

\displaystyle\Phi_{m,i,n,j}(\kappa):=\left(\frac{\Re\{l_{i}\}}{\Re\{l_{j}\}}\Big(\frac{b}{a}\Big)^{m-n}-1\right)\left(\frac{\Re\{l_{i}\}}{\Re\{l_{j}\}}\Big(\frac{b}{a}\Big)^{m-n}+1-2\kappa\right),

where
\kappa=\lambda/\hat{\lambda}_{n^{*}}\in(a,b)
with
n^{*}\in[\mathbf{n}]
.
Then,

\bar{\Phi}_{m,i,n^{*},j}(\eta,\gamma)=(\hat{\lambda}_{n^{*}}\Re\{l_{j}\})^{2}\Phi_{m,i,n^{*},j}(\kappa).

Thus, under Assumption
3
,
\bar{\Phi}_{m,i,n^{*},j}(\eta,\gamma)>0
if and only if
\Phi_{m,i,n^{*},j}(\kappa)>0
.

We now derive sufficient conditions on
a
and
b
to guarantee Condition
2
by establishing positivity of
\Phi_{m,i,n^{*},j}(\kappa)
in two distinct cases:

Case
i=j
:

Here
m\neq n^{*}
and

\displaystyle\Phi_{m,j,n^{*},j}(\kappa)=\big((b/a)^{m-n^{*}}-1\big)\big((b/a)^{m-n^{*}}+1-2\kappa\big),\quad\kappa\in(a,b).

Then, we have the following two scenarios:

•

If
m>n^{*}
, then
(b/a)^{m-n^{*}}>1
. Thus,
\Phi_{m,j,n^{*},j}(\kappa)>0
whenever
b/a+1-2b\geq 0
.

•

If
m<n^{*}
, then
(b/a)^{m-n^{*}}<1
. Thus,
\Phi_{m,j,n^{*},j}(\kappa)>0
whenever
a/b+1-2a\geq 0
.

Together, these give the sufficient condition:

b=\frac{a}{2a-1}.

Case
i\neq j
:

Define
f^{m,i}_{n,j}(a)=\frac{\Re\{l_{i}\}}{\Re\{l_{j}\}}(2a-1)^{n-m}
. Taking into account
b=a/(2a-1)
, we have

\Phi_{m,j,n,j}(\kappa)=\big(f^{m,i}_{n,j}(a)-1\big)\big(f^{m,i}_{n,j}(a)+1-2\kappa\big).

To avoid degeneracy, i.e.,
\Phi_{m,j,n^{*},j}(\kappa)=0
, we impose

f^{m,i}_{n,j}(a)\neq 1,\quad\forall(m,i)\neq(n,j).

Next, consider the following two scenarios:

•

If
\Re\{l_{i}\}/\Re\{l_{j}\}<0
, then
f^{m,i}_{n,j}(a)<0
. Since
\kappa\in(a,b)
, we have
f^{m,i}_{n,j}(a)+1-2\kappa<1-2a
. Thus,
\Phi_{m,i,n^{*},j}(\kappa)>0
whenever
a>1/2
.

•

If
\Re\{l_{i}\}/\Re\{l_{j}\}>0
, we consider the two situations where
f^{m,i}_{n^{*},j}(a)>0
and
f^{m,i}_{n^{*},j}(a)\neq 1
:

–

If
f^{m,i}_{n^{*},j}(a)>1
,
\Phi_{m,i,n^{*},j}(\kappa)>0
whenever
f^{m,i}_{n^{*},j}(a)+1-2\kappa>0
, which can be guaranteed if:

\bar{f}(a):=\frac{a}{2a-1}-\frac{1}{2}\Big(1+\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\{f^{m,i}_{n,j}(a)\text{ s.t. }f^{m,i}_{n,j}(a)>1\}\Big)<0,

(25)

with
\min\{\emptyset\}=\infty
.

–

If
f^{m,i}_{n^{*},j}(a)\in(0,1)
,
\Phi_{m,i,n^{*},j}(\kappa)>0
whenever
f^{m,i}_{n^{*},j}(a)+1-2\kappa<0
, which can be guaranteed if:

\underline{f}(a):=a-\frac{1}{2}\Big(1+\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\{f^{m,i}_{n,j}(a)\text{ s.t. }f^{m,i}_{n,j}(a)\in(0,1)\}\Big)>0,

(26)

with
\max\{\emptyset\}=-\infty
.

In (
25
)-(
26
), equality also suffices. However, for the boundary case
\kappa=b
(see Proposition
3.8
), stricter inequalities are required. Existence of suitable
a\in(1/2,1)
is ensured since
\bar{f}(1)<0
and
\underline{f}(1)>0
(by Assumption
3
) and both functions
\bar{f}(a)
and
\underline{f}(a)
are continuous in
a
.

Based on the above discussion, we conclude the following condition on
a
and
b
:

Condition 3

(Diffusive grid design)
.

0<\bar{a}<a<1<b<\bar{b}
,
b=a/(2a-1)
,
a\in(1/2,1)
,
f^{m,i}_{n,j}(a)\neq 1
for all
(m,i)\neq(n,j)
,
\bar{f}(a)>0
and
\underline{f}(a)<0
.

Under Assumptions
1
and
3
, Condition
3
guarantees Condition
2
. Hence Theorem
3.5
yields the following.

Proposition 3.7
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
and
3
hold. Given prior information
\lambda=\sqrt{\eta\gamma}\in[\underline{\lambda},\bar{\lambda}]
and the requirement
\lambda/\hat{\lambda}\in[\bar{a},\bar{b}]
, assume
a
and
b
satisfy Condition
3
. Set
\mathbf{n}=\lceil\log(\bar{\lambda}/\underline{\lambda})/\log(b/a)\rceil
. Then, for all
\lambda\in\Lambda_{a,b}=\bigcup^{\mathbf{n}}_{n=1}(a\hat{\lambda}_{n},b\hat{\lambda}_{n})
, the following statements hold:

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n}(t)=\delta_{n,n^{*}},\quad\forall n\in[\mathbf{n}],

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\frac{\pi_{n}(t)}{\pi_{n^{*}}(t)}\leq-\min_{i,j}\bar{\Phi}_{n,i,n^{*},j}(\eta,\gamma)<0,\quad\forall n\neq n^{*},\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

Thus,
\lambda
is identifiable within
\Lambda_{a,b}
. For boundary values
\lambda\in[\underline{\lambda},\bar{\lambda}]\setminus\Lambda_{a,b}=\{a\hat{\lambda}_{1},b\hat{\lambda}_{1},\dots,b\hat{\lambda}_{\mathbf{n}-1},b\hat{\lambda}_{\mathbf{n}}\}
we have:

Proposition 3.8
.

Suppose the conditions of Proposition
3.7
are satisfied. Then,

•

For all
\lambda\in\{a\hat{\lambda}_{1},b\hat{\lambda}_{\mathbf{n}}\}
,
\mathbb{P}\big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n}(t)=\delta_{n,n^{*}}\big)=1
for all
n\in[\mathbf{n}]
;

•

For all
\lambda\in\{b\hat{\lambda}_{1},\dots,b\hat{\lambda}_{\mathbf{n}-1}\}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n}(t)=0\big)=1,\quad\forall n\in[\mathbf{n}]\setminus\{n^{*},n^{*}+1\},

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n^{*}}(t)+\pi_{n^{*}+1}(t)=1\big)=1,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n^{*}}(t)=1\big)\in(0,1),\quad\mathbb{P}\big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n^{*}+1}(t)=1\big)\in(0,1),

and the asymptotic moments satisfy

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\big(\pi_{n^{*}}(t)\big)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\big(\pi_{n^{*}+1}(t)\big)=1/2,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{Var}\big(\pi_{n^{*}}(t)\big)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{Var}\big(\pi_{n^{*}+1}(t)\big)=1/4.

Proof.

For the first item, direct calculation shows
\Phi_{m,i,1,j}(a)>0
for all

and
\Phi_{m,i,\mathbf{n},j}(b)>0
for all
(m,i)\neq(\mathbf{n},j)

For the second item, fix
n^{*}\in\{1,\dots,\mathbf{b}-1\}
and define
\Delta^{n^{*}}_{j}:=\{m\in[\mathbf{n}],j\in[\mathbf{j}]|(m,i)\neq(n^{*},j)\text{ nor }(n^{*}+1,j)\}

\mathrm{Tr}(M_{m,i}\hat{\Xi}(t))

\mathbb{Q}^{j}

converges exponentially to one
\mathbb{Q}^{j}
-almost surely. This leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}^{j}\big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n}(t)=0\big)=1,\quad\forall n\in[\mathbf{n}]\setminus\{n^{*},n^{*}+1\},

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}^{j}\big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n^{*}}(t)+\pi_{n^{*}+1}(t)=1\big)=1.

Applying the same reasoning as in the proof of Theorem
2.7
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n}(t)=0\big)=1,\quad\forall n\in[\mathbf{n}]\setminus\{n^{*},n^{*}+1\},

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n^{*}}(t)+\pi_{n^{*}+1}(t)=1\big)=1.

(27)

Next, consider the case where

. We have the ration

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Q}_{n^{*},j}(t):=\frac{\hat{\mathsf{q}}_{n^{*}+1,j}(t)}{\hat{\mathsf{q}}_{n^{*},j}(t)}=\mathfrak{Q}_{n^{*},j}(0)\exp\big(\mathfrak{C}_{n^{*},j}\overline{W}^{j}(t)\big),

where
\mathfrak{C}_{n^{*},j}:=2\Re\{l_{j}\}(\hat{\lambda}_{n^{*}+1}-\hat{\lambda}_{n^{*}})\neq 0
and
\overline{W}^{j}(t)
is
\mathbb{Q}^{j}
-Wiener process.
Define

\displaystyle\mathsf{R}_{n^{*},j}(t)=\sum_{(m,i)\in\Delta^{n^{*}}_{j}}\hat{\mathsf{q}}_{m,i}(t)\in(0,1),\quad\mathsf{r}_{n^{*},j}(t)=\sum_{i\neq j}\hat{\mathsf{q}}_{n^{*},i}(t)\in(0,1),

both of which converge to zero
\mathbb{Q}^{j}
-almost surely. Since
\hat{\mathsf{q}}_{n^{*}+1,j}(t)+\hat{\mathsf{q}}_{n^{*},j}(t)=1-\mathsf{R}_{n^{*},j}(t)
for all
t\geq 0
, we have
\hat{\mathsf{q}}_{n^{*},j}(t)=\frac{1-\mathsf{R}_{n^{*},j}(t)}{1+\mathfrak{Q}_{n^{*},j}(t)}
,
\mathbb{Q}^{j}
-almost surely, and consequently

\pi_{n^{*}}(t)=\mathsf{r}_{n^{*},j}(t)+\frac{1-\mathsf{R}_{n^{*},j}(t)}{1+\mathfrak{Q}_{n^{*},j}(t)},\quad\mathbb{Q}^{j}\text{-a.s.}

By dominated convergence and the symmetry of the Gaussian,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}^{j}}\left(\frac{1}{1+\mathfrak{Q}_{n^{*},j}(t)}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{1+\exp(\log\mathfrak{Q}_{n^{*},j}(0)+x\mathfrak{C}_{n^{*},j}\sqrt{t})}\frac{\exp(-x^{2}/2)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}dx=\frac{1}{2},

and
\mathsf{r}_{n^{*},j}(t),\mathsf{R}_{n^{*},j}(t)\to 0
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}^{\,j})
, we deduce
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}^{j}}(\pi_{n^{*}}(t))=1/2
, which implies

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}(\pi_{n^{*}}(t))=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{R}=j)\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}^{j}}(\pi_{n^{*}}(t))=\frac{1}{2}.

From Equation (
27
), we obtain

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}(\pi_{n^{*}+1}(t))=1/2.

Hence, it follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n^{*}}(t)=1\big)\in(0,1),\quad\mathbb{P}\big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n^{*}+1}(t)=1\big)\in(0,1).

For the variances, using the decomposition above and again dominated convergence,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}^{j}}\left(\frac{1}{(1+\mathfrak{Q}_{n^{*},j}(t))^{2}}\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{(1+\exp(\log\mathfrak{Q}_{n^{*},j}(0)+x\mathfrak{C}_{n^{*},j}\sqrt{t}))^{2}}\frac{\exp(-x^{2}/2)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}dx=\frac{1}{2}.

and cross/error terms vanish in the limit, yielding
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\text{Var}_{\mathbb{Q}^{j}}(\pi_{n^{*}}(t))=1/4
which implies

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\text{Var}(\pi_{n^{*}}(t))=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{R}=j)\text{Var}_{\mathbb{Q}^{j}}(\pi_{n^{*}}(t))=\frac{1}{4}.

The same conclusion holds for
\pi_{n^{*}+1}(t)
.

\square

Lemma 3.13
.

There exist

Proof.

At

\log(1+x)<x-x^{2}/2+x^{3}/3
for all

, we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}^{1}_{n,j}(1,1,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=1-\epsilon+\big(1+\varrho_{j}/\epsilon^{n-1}\big)\log\left(\frac{\epsilon+\varrho_{j}/\epsilon^{n-1}}{1+\varrho_{j}/\epsilon^{n-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\epsilon-1)^{2}}{1+\varrho_{j}/\epsilon^{n-1}}\left(1-\frac{2}{3}\frac{\epsilon-1}{1+\varrho_{j}/\epsilon^{n-1}}\right),\quad\forall\epsilon>1.

and

Similarly, using

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}^{2}_{n,j}(1,1,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\big(1+\varrho_{j}/\epsilon^{n-1}\big)\log\left(\frac{1/\epsilon+\varrho_{j}/\epsilon^{n-1}}{1+\varrho_{j}/\epsilon^{n-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq-(1-1/\epsilon)\left(1+\frac{1-1/\epsilon}{2(1+\varrho_{j}/\epsilon^{n-1})}\right)<0,\quad\forall\epsilon>1.

Define

\square

By continuity of

in

and finiteness of the index set, there exist

(uniform in

whenever

\square

\lambda\in\bigcup^{\mathbf{n}-1}_{n=1}(b\hat{\lambda}_{n},\epsilon b\hat{\lambda}_{n})

Proposition 3.17

Suppose that the conditions of Proposition

are satisfied. Moreover, assume that Condition

holds. Then, for all

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n}(t)=0\big)=1,\quad\forall n\in[\mathbf{n}]\setminus\{n^{*},n^{*}+1\},

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n^{*}}(t)+\pi_{n^{*}+1}(t)=1\big)=1.

Proof.

Fix
n^{*}\in\{1,\dots,\mathbf{n}-1\}
and
j\in[\mathbf{j}]
, and set

\Delta^{n^{*}}_{j}=\left\{(m,i)\in[n]\times[j]\mid i\neq j\text{ or }(i=j\text{ and }m<n^{*})\right\}.

A direct computation shows
\Psi^{\mathsf{k},\bar{\mathsf{k}}}_{m,i,n^{*},j}(\kappa)<0
for all
\kappa\in(b,\epsilon b)
and
(m,i)\in\Delta^{n^{*}}_{j}
.

Condition
6
ensures
\psi^{n^{*},j}_{2}(\epsilon b)<0
, and hence
\psi^{n^{*},j}_{d+1}(\epsilon b)-\psi^{n^{*},j}_{d}(\epsilon b)<0
. Thus
\Psi^{\mathsf{k},\bar{\mathsf{k}}}_{m,j,n^{*},j}(\kappa)<0
for all
\kappa\in(b,\epsilon b)
and
m\geq n^{*}+2
.
Thus, Using analogous reasoning as in the proof of Theorem
3.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}^{j}\big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n}(t)=0\big)=1,\quad\forall n\in[\mathbf{n}]\setminus\{n^{*},n^{*}+1\},

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}^{j}\big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n^{*}}(t)+\pi_{n^{*}+1}(t)=1\big)=1.

By applying similar arguments as in the proof of Theorem
2.7
, we conclude the proof.

\square

Next, we investigate the asymptotic behavior of
\pi_{n^{*}}(t)
and
\pi_{n^{*}+1}(t)
for
\kappa=\lambda/\hat{\lambda}_{n^{*}}\in(b,\epsilon b)
under the assumptions of Proposition
3.17
.
If
\kappa
is sufficiently close to
b
(resp.
\epsilon b
), then by continuity
\Psi^{\mathsf{k},\bar{\mathsf{k}}}_{m,i,n^{*},j}(\kappa)<0
for all
(m,i)\neq(n^{*},j)
, which implies
\mathbb{P}\big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n^{*}}(t)=1\big)=1
(resp.
\mathbb{P}\big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n^{*}+1}(t)=1\big)=1
).
We now analyze the remaining cases. Define the disjoint subsets (possibly empty)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}_{-}:=\{j\in[\mathbf{j}]\mid\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{N_{J}}\bar{\Psi}^{k}_{n^{*}+1,j,n^{*},j}(\Gamma)<0\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}_{=}:=\{j\in[\textstyle\mathbf{j}]\mid\sum_{k=1}^{N_{J}}\bar{\Psi}^{k}_{n^{*}+1,j,n^{*},j}(\Gamma)=0\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}_{+}:=\{j\in[\mathbf{j}]\mid\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{N_{J}}\bar{\Psi}^{k}_{n^{*}+1,j,n^{*},j}(\Gamma)>0\},

so that
\mathfrak{s}_{-}\cup\mathfrak{s}_{=}\cup\mathfrak{s}_{+}=[\mathbf{j}]
. Using arguments similar to Proposition
3.12
, we conclude

•

For all
j\in\mathfrak{s}_{-}
,
\mathbb{Q}^{j}\big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n^{*}}(t)=1\big)=1
;

•

For all
j\in\mathfrak{s}_{+}
,
\mathbb{Q}^{j}\big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\frac{\hat{\mathsf{q}}_{n^{*}+1,j}(t)}{\hat{\mathsf{q}}_{n^{*},j}(t)}>0\big)=1
.
Consequently,
\mathbb{Q}^{j}\big(\lim_{t\to\infty}\hat{\mathsf{q}}_{n^{*},j}(t)=0\big)=1
, since
\hat{\mathsf{q}}_{n^{*}+1,j}(t)\leq 1
almost surely. Thus,
\mathbb{Q}^{j}\big(\lim_{t\to\infty}\pi_{n^{*}+1}(t)=1\big)=1
.

•

For all
j\in\mathfrak{s}_{=}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}^{j}\big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n^{*}}(t)=1\big)\in(0,1),\quad\mathbb{Q}^{j}\big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n^{*}+1}(t)=1\big)\in(0,1),

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}^{j}}\big(\pi_{n^{*}}(t)\big)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}^{j}}\big(\pi_{n^{*}+1}(t)\big)=1/2,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{Q}^{j}}\big(\pi_{n^{*}}(t)\big)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{Q}^{j}}\big(\pi_{n^{*}+1}(t)\big)=1/4.

Hence,

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}(\pi_{n^{*}}(t))=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{R}=j)\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}^{j}}(\pi_{n^{*}}(t))=\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{s}_{-}}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{R}=j)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{s}_{=}}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{R}=j)

and

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\text{Var}(\pi_{n^{*}}(t))=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{R}=j)\text{Var}_{\mathbb{Q}^{j}}(\pi_{n^{*}}(t))=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{s}_{=}}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{R}=j).

3.15

with

1.

Single Convergence
: Exactly one trajectory converges to one, while all others converge to zero.

2.

Oscillatory Behavior
: Two trajectories oscillate between zero and one, while all others converge to zero.

The distinction from the diffusive case lies in the admissible parameter set.
In the diffusion setting, single convergence corresponds to
\lambda/\hat{\lambda}_{n}\in[a,b]
.
In the jump setting with shot noise, the admissible range enlarges to
\lambda/\hat{\lambda}_{n}\in(a/\epsilon,\epsilon b)
owing to the introduction of
\epsilon>1
.
The interpretation of the oscillatory regime with finite data is analogous to the diffusion case: it may either reflect insufficient observation time to reveal single convergence, or genuine persistent oscillations between two neighboring trajectories.

To address these possibilities, we propose the following procedure for estimating
\lambda\in[\underline{\lambda},\bar{\lambda}]

3.5
Non-identifiability of
\mu
and
\nu

Under the QND measurement scheme (Assumption
1
), the Hamiltonian
H
, the perturbation-induced noise operators
\{A_{k}\}_{k\in[N_{P}]}
, and the measurement operators are simultaneously block-diagonalizable. By Theorem
2.7
, the asymptotic behavior of the quantum state
\rho(t)
is therefore independent of
H
and
\{A_{k}\}_{k\in[N_{P}]}
. Moreover, the measurement records
Y_{k}(t)
and
\mathsf{N}_{k}(t)
depend on
H
and
\{A_{k}\}_{k\in[N_{P}]}
only indirectly through
\rho(t)
. Hence, these parameters are not identifiable under this QND scheme.

It is nevertheless instructive to examine what occurs if our estimation procedure is formally applied to
\mu
or
\nu
. Suppose that all parameters required by Condition
2
, which ensures robust stability of the estimated filter, are known. Then for every
n\in[\mathbf{n}]
, we have
\hat{\eta}_{k,n}=\eta_{k}
,
\hat{\gamma}_{k,n}=\gamma_{k}
, and similarly for the other quantities. In this setting, we obtain

\displaystyle\bar{\Phi}^{k}_{m,i,n,j}(\eta,\gamma)

\displaystyle=2\eta_{k}\gamma_{k}\left(\Re\{l_{k,i}\}-\Re\{l_{k,j}\}\right)^{2}=\Phi^{k}_{i,j}(\eta,\gamma)\geq 0,

\displaystyle\bar{\Psi}^{k}_{m,i,n,j}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=-\Gamma_{k,j}\left(1-\frac{\Gamma_{k,i}}{\Gamma_{k,j}}+\log\frac{\Gamma_{k,i}}{\Gamma_{k,j}}\right)=\Psi^{k}_{i,j}(\Gamma)\geq 0.

However, for any
m\neq n
, one finds
\bar{\Phi}^{k}_{m,i,n,i}(\eta,\gamma)=\bar{\Psi}^{k}_{m,i,n,j}(\Gamma)=0
, violating the strict positivity required in Condition
2
. Thus the parameters
\mu
and
\nu
are not identifiable. Artificially assigning surrogate values to
\hat{\eta}
,
\hat{\gamma}
, or
\hat{\Gamma}
can restore positivity, but such values bear no relation to the true system parameters. In this case, the estimation algorithm converges to these ad hoc quantities rather than to the physical parameters of interest.

4
Conclusion

We have developed a robust and computationally efficient framework for parameter estimation in open quantum systems governed by jump–diffusion stochastic master equations under QND measurements. By extending the stability theory of quantum filters to incorporate both initial-state mismatch and parameter uncertainty, we constructed a family of reduced-order filters enabling consistent parameter estimation over continuous domains, with significantly reduced complexity.

Future work will extend this framework beyond the QND setting to general measurement schemes, and to infinite-dimensional systems where reduced-order representations must be carefully adapted. These directions are essential for broadening the applicability of robust quantum parameter estimation to realistic experimental platforms.

Search for heavy neutral resonances decaying to tau lepton pairs in proton-proton collisions at s = 13 ​ \TeV \sqrt{s}=13\TeV


Abstract
A search for heavy neutral gauge bosons ( \PZpr ) decaying into a pair of tau leptons is performed in proton-proton collisions at s = 13 ​ \TeV \sqrt{s}=13\TeV at the CERN LHC. The data were collected with the CMS detector and correspond to an integrated luminosity of 138 \fbinv . The observations are found to be in agreement with the expectation from standard model processes. Limits at 95% confidence level are set on the product of the \PZpr production cross section and its branching fraction to tau lepton pairs for a range of \PZpr boson masses. For a narrow resonance in the sequential standard model scenario, a \PZpr boson with a mass below 3.5 \TeV is excluded. This is the most stringent limit to date from this type of search.

Introduction

The CMS experiment
[

One of the simplest extensions postulates an additional

\PZ

\PZpr

[

[
4

Searches for
\PZpr

\PZpr

m_{\PZpr}
,
especially for the
\PZpr\to\PGmp\PGmm
and
\Pep\Pem

Combining the

\Pep\Pem

[

The SSM assumes that the
\PZpr

\PZ

There are several models that instead propose preferential couplings of the
\PZpr

[
6
,
7

In models with nonuniversal lepton couplings, assuming a
\PZpr

bounds on

\PZpr\to\PGtp\PGtm

[

up to 2.42

,

up to 2.7
\TeV
.

In recent years, interest in potential effects beyond the SM has emerged as a result of anomalies observed in the precision measurements of

These anomalies have led to the proposal of a large number of theoretical models that predict the existence of heavy neutral and charged gauge bosons (

0.2
Analysis strategy

The
\PGt
decay channels can be broadly classified into hadronic (
\tauh
),
\PGnGt+\text{hadrons}
, and leptonic (
\PGt_{\Pell}
),
\PGnGt+\Pell+\HepAntiParticle{\PGn}{\Pell}{}\Xspace
, with
\Pell
representing
\PGm
or
\Pe
.
The analysis presented here includes the three
\PZpr\to\PGtp\PGtm
decay channels with the
largest
\PGt
decay branching fraction products:

\PZpr\to\PGtpm_{\PGm}\tauh^{\mp}
,
\PZpr\to\PGtpm_{\Pe}\tauh^{\mp}
, and
\PZpr\to\tauh^{+}\tauh^{-}
, referred to below as
\PGt_{\PGm}\tauh
,
\PGt_{\Pe}\tauh
, and
\tauh\tauh
, respectively.

In the production of
\PZpr
bosons via
s
-channel annihilation of quark-antiquark pairs, the
\PZpr
bosons have limited Lorentz boost in the plane transverse to the beam.
For the
\TeVns
-scale
m_{\PZpr}
of interest, the
\PGt
decay daughters tend to be clustered within small back-to-back cones.
With this picture in mind, we derive an estimate of the
\PZpr
boson candidate mass
m_{\textrm{rec}}(\PZpr)
, making use of the transverse momentum imbalance between the visible daughters of the two
\PGt
lepton decays:

m_{\textrm{rec}}(\PZpr)=\sqrt{(E^{\PGt\text{vis}}_{1}+E^{\PGt\text{vis}}_{2}+\abs{\mathbf{p}^{\PZpr\text{miss}}})^{2}-\abs{\mathbf{p}^{\PGt\text{vis}}_{1}+\mathbf{p}^{\PGt\text{vis}}_{2}+\mathbf{p}^{\PZpr\text{miss}}}^{2}}.

(1)

Here
(E^{\PGt\text{vis}}_{i},\mathbf{p}^{\PGt\text{vis}}_{i})
is the four momentum of the reconstructed daughter(s) of the
i^{\mathrm{th}}

\PGt
candidate. The momentum of the invisible neutrinos is represented by

\mathbf{p}^{\PZpr\text{miss}}=(\vec{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\PZpr\text{miss}},p_{z}^{\PZpr\text{miss}})
, with
\vec{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\PZpr\text{miss}}=-(\vec{p}^{\,\PGt\text{vis}}_{1\mathrm{T}}+\vec{p}^{\,\PGt\text{vis}}_{2\mathrm{T}})
and
p_{z}^{\PZpr\text{miss}}=0
.
We find that this definition of
\vec{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\PZpr\text{miss}}
provides better discrimination between the signal and the SM background processes than the event momentum imbalance
\ptvecmiss
, the negative vectorial sum of all reconstructed objects. This is because
\ptvecmiss
includes contributions from mismeasured jets in the underlying event and from additional
{\Pp\Pp}
interactions within the same or nearby bunch crossings (pileup).

We use
m_{\textrm{rec}}(\PZpr)
as the primary discriminating variable for extraction of the signal yield. Although the natural decay width of the
\PZpr
boson is small compared with its mass
(
<
3% for the model assumptions in this paper), the relative resolution on
m_{\textrm{rec}}(\PZpr)
is between 30 and 45% for the range of
m_{\PZpr}
considered.
The analysis is thus dominated by experimental resolution effects arising from the undetected neutrinos.
Data and simulation are used to obtain estimates of the yields of the SM background processes, and the signal yield is obtained from a maximum likelihood (ML) fit to the
m_{\textrm{rec}}(\PZpr)
distributions for each of the three
\PZpr\to\PGtp\PGtm
decay channels, as described in the following sections.

0.3
The CMS detector

The central feature of the CMS apparatus is a superconducting solenoid of 6
\unit
m internal diameter, providing a magnetic field of 3.8
\unit
T. Within the solenoid volume are a silicon pixel and strip tracker, a lead tungstate crystal electromagnetic calorimeter (ECAL), and a brass and scintillator hadron calorimeter, each composed of a barrel and two endcap sections. Forward calorimeters extend the pseudorapidity (
\eta
) coverage provided by the barrel and endcap detectors. Muons are reconstructed in gas-ionization detectors embedded in the steel flux-return yoke outside the solenoid. More detailed descriptions of the CMS detector, together with a definition of the coordinate system used and the relevant kinematic variables, can be found in Refs.
[
27
,
28
]
.

Events of interest are selected using a two-tiered trigger system. The first level, composed of custom hardware processors, uses information from the calorimeters and muon detectors to select events at a rate of
around 100
\unit
kHz within a fixed latency of about 4
\mus

[
29
]
. The second level, known as the high-level trigger, consists of a farm of
processors running a version of the full event reconstruction software optimized for fast processing, and reduces the event rate to around 1
\unit
kHz before data storage
[
30
]
.

0.4
Object reconstruction

The first stage of particle reconstruction employs the particle-flow (PF) algorithm
[
31
]
, which aims to reconstruct and identify each individual visible particle in an event (photon, electron, muon, charged hadron, neutral hadron) with an optimized combination of all subdetector information. Further identification (ID) strategies for particular particle types are applied to achieve an appropriate balance between efficiency and the purity of the resulting candidate collections.

In the presence of pileup, the primary vertex, at which the
{\Pp\Pp}
collision of interest occurs, is taken to be the vertex corresponding to the hardest scattering in the event, evaluated using tracking information alone, as described in Section 9.4.1 of Ref.
[
32
]
.

Jets are reconstructed offline from PF candidates using the anti-
\kt
clustering algorithm
[
33
,
34
]
with a distance parameter of 0.4.
Jet momentum is determined as the vectorial sum of all particle momenta in the jet, and is found from simulation to be, on average, within 5 to 10% of the true momentum over the entire
\pt
spectrum
and detector acceptance. Pileup interactions can contribute additional tracks and calorimetric energy depositions, increasing the apparent jet momentum. To mitigate this effect, tracks identified to be originating from pileup vertices are discarded, and an offset correction is applied to correct for remaining contributions
[
35
]
. Jet energy corrections are derived from simulation studies so that the average measured energy of jets matches that of particle level jets. In situ measurements of the momentum balance in dijet,
\text{photon}+\text{jet}
,
\PZ+\text{jet}
, and multijet events are used to determine any residual differences between the jet energy scale in data and in simulation, and appropriate corrections are made
[
36
]
. Additional selection criteria are applied to each jet to remove jets potentially dominated by instrumental effects or reconstruction failures
[
35
]
.

The
\ptvecmiss
vector is computed as the negative vector sum of the transverse momenta of all the PF candidates in an event, modified to account for corrections to the energy scale of the reconstructed jets in the event
[
37
]
. Its magnitude is denoted
\ptmiss
.

Muons are measured in the pseudorapidity range
\abs{\eta}<2.4
, with detection planes made using three technologies: drift tubes, cathode strip chambers, and resistive-plate chambers.
The single-muon trigger efficiency exceeds 90% over the full
\eta
range, and the efficiency to reconstruct and identify muons is greater than 96%.
Matching muons to tracks measured in the silicon tracker results in a relative transverse momentum resolution of 1% in the barrel and 3% in the endcaps for muons with
\pt
up to 100
\GeV
.
The
\pt
resolution in the barrel is better than 7% for muons with
\pt
up to 1
\TeV

[
38
]
.
This analysis uses the tight working point
[
38
]
of the muon ID.
To suppress muons from hadron decays and other nonprompt sources, an additional requirement is imposed on the relative isolation, defined as the ratio of the energy from neutral and charged PF candidates in a cone of
\Delta R\equiv\sqrt{\smash[b]{(\Delta\eta)^{2}+(\Delta\phi)^{2}}}<0.4
around the muon trajectory to that of the muon.
A correction for energy mismeasurement from pileup is applied, and we require this corrected relative isolation to be smaller than 0.15.

Electrons are reconstructed using energy deposits in the ECAL detector that have a matching track in
(\eta,\,\phi)
space in the silicon tracking system,
within
\abs{\eta}<2.4
. Electron candidates that fall in the transition region between the barrel and the endcap of the CMS detector,
located at
1.44<\abs{\eta}<1.57
, are not considered. The momentum resolution for electrons with
\pt\approx 45\GeV
from
\PZ\to\Pe\Pe

decays ranges from 1.6–5.0%. It is better in the barrel region than in the endcaps, and depends on the bremsstrahlung energy emitted by the electron
as it traverses the material in front of the ECAL
[
39
,
40
]
.
To identify electrons, we use the high-energy electron pairs (HEEP) ID for the selection of signal events;
for veto and control region (CR) selections, we use instead the loose working point of the cut-based electron ID
[
40
]
.
Isolation is defined as the amount of energy in a cone of
\Delta R<0.3
around the electron direction.
It is an integral part of both electron ID criteria used, as defined in detail in
[
40
]
, and both IDs utilize electron-energy-dependent isolation criteria with pileup energy corrections that differ between the barrel and endcap regions of the detector.

Hadronic
\PGt
lepton decays are reconstructed from jets using the hadrons-plus-strips algorithm
[
41
]
, which combines 1 or 3 tracks with energy deposits in the calorimeters to form
\tauh
candidates. Neutral pions are reconstructed as strips with dynamic size in
(\eta,\,\phi)
from reconstructed electrons and photons, where the strip size varies as a function of the
\pt
of the electron or photon candidate.

The
DeepTau
deep neural network algorithm
[
42
]
is used to distinguish genuine
\tauh
candidates from jets originating from the hadronization of quarks or gluons, and from electrons or muons.
Information from all individually reconstructed particles near the
\tauh
momentum direction is combined with properties of the
\tauh
candidate and of the event. We make use of the tight (medium) working point for the discrimination of
\tauh
from muons (electrons), and of the tight, loose, and very loose working points for discrimination against jets, as described in Sections
0.6
and
0.7
.

To identify jet candidates from
\PQb
quark hadronization (hereafter denoted
\PQb
jets) we use the
DeepCSV

\PQb
tagging algorithm
[
43
]
, which is based on a deep neural network.
We employ the tight working point of the discriminator, for which the typical efficiency is 50%, while the mistag rate of jets originating from light-flavored quarks or gluons is 0.1% and from
\PQc
jets is 2.4%.

0.6
Event selection criteria

The signal region (SR) selection criteria for each
\PZpr\to\PGtp\PGtm
decay channel are chosen to maximize the expected signal significance by balancing signal efficiency
against the suppression of background.
The main SM background processes are DY,
\PW+\text{jets}
,
\ttbar
, QCD multijet, and
\PV\PV
(
\PW\PW
,
\PZ\PZ
, and
\PW\PZ
) production.
The DY process constitutes an irreducible background since
\PGtp\PGtm
pairs arise from the intermediate
\PZ
and photon states.
However, since the analysis focuses on massive
\PZpr
bosons, the visible daughters from the two
\PGt
lepton decays
typically have higher momentum and larger angular separation than those from DY events. We exploit these characteristics
to separate heavy
\PZpr
boson signals from background. Events from
\PW+\text{jets}
and
\ttbar
can mimic the
\PGt_{\Pell}\tauh
final states when the
\PW
boson, produced directly or from a top quark decay, decays via

\PW\to\Pell\PGn_{\Pell}
, and a jet is misidentified as a
\tauh
candidate. Events from
\PV\PV
or
\ttbar
production with leptonic decays of both vector bosons produce genuine lepton pairs, leading to final states with a
\PGtp\PGtm
pair as in signal events. Multijet events become a background source
through the misidentification of jets as
\tauh
or
\Pell
candidates.

Events are required to have one
\tauh
candidate accompanied by a
\PGm
,
\Pe
, or second
\tauh
candidate of opposite-sign (OS) charge, all within
\abs{\eta}<2.1
.
To avoid possible overlaps among the three channels, we reject events containing additional lepton candidates of any flavor beyond the selected OS lepton candidate pair.
Specifically, the additional lepton can be a
\PGm
or
\Pe
candidate with
\pt>10\GeV
and
\abs{\eta}<2.1
, or a
\PGt
candidate meeting the
\tauh
requirements of the particular channel.
Since the analysis explores the production of
\PZpr
bosons through quark-antiquark annihilation, events from vector boson fusion are suppressed by vetoing events with a pair of jets
j^{1,2}
, each of which passes
\pt>30\GeV
and
\abs{\eta}<4.7
, with a pseudorapidity separation
\abs{\Delta\eta(j^{1},j^{2})}>4.2
and an invariant mass above 500
\GeV
.

For the ID of
\tauh
candidates in the SRs for all decay channels we impose the tight working points of the discriminators against jets and muons, and the medium one against electrons, as defined in Ref.
[
42
]
.
The probability of misidentifying a jet as a
\tauh
candidate depends on the
\pt
and flavor of the jet; for the kinematic conditions of this analysis it has been estimated in a
\PW+\text{jets}
sample to be approximately
0.6%, with a genuine
\tauh
ID efficiency of 60%. The probability for muons (electrons) to be misidentified as
\tauh
candidates is 0.07% (0.2%) for a genuine
\tauh
ID efficiency of
>
99% (90%).

0.6.1
The
\PGt_{\PGm}\tauh
SR

The
\PGt_{\PGm}\tauh
events are required to satisfy the single-muon trigger, whose efficiency exceeds 90% over the full
\eta
range for the selected muon candidates with
\pt>35\GeV
. Muons are also required to
be well isolated and to pass the tight ID criteria defined in Ref.
[
38
]
. The
\tauh
candidate is required to satisfy
\pt>20\GeV
. The two candidates are required to
be well separated in
(\eta,\,\phi)
space by the criterion
\Delta R\equiv\sqrt{\smash[b]{(\Delta\phi(\PGt_{\PGm},\tauh))^{2}+(\Delta\eta(\PGt_{\PGm},\tauh))^{2}}}>0.3
.
We reject events containing
\PQb
jet candidates with
\pt>30\GeV
and
\abs{\eta}<2.4
.
Events from DY,
\PW+\text{jets}
, and QCD multijet production are significantly suppressed by requiring the
\PGt_{\PGm}
and
\tauh
candidates to have a large azimuthal separation given by
\cos{\Delta\phi(\PGt_{\PGm},\tauh)}<-0.98
.
In addition, we require that
\ptvecmiss
lie in the direction opposite that of the
\tauh
or of the
\PGt_{\PGm}
candidate with the highest
\pt
(leading lepton,
{\Pell}^{\text{l}}
), by requiring
\cos{\Delta\phi(\ptmiss,{\Pell}^{\text{l}})}<-0.95
.
This requirement further reduces the contribution of
\PW+\text{jets}
and QCD multijet events. The transverse mass of the leading lepton
and
\ptvecmiss
,
\mT(\ptmiss,{\Pell}^{\text{l}})=\sqrt{\smash[b]{\ptmiss\pt({\Pell}^{\text{l}})(1-\cos{(\Delta\phi(\ptmiss,{\Pell}^{\text{l}}))})}}
,
is required to be greater than 150
\GeV
for further suppression of
\PW+\text{jets}
events.

The

Similar selection criteria are applied to the
\PGt_{\Pe}\tauh
channel, with the following differences. We require these events to satisfy a single-electron trigger that has an efficiency above 90% for

\pt>35\,(55)\GeV
for data collected in 2016 (2017–2018).

]
with at least two

Events with any

To reduce the contribution of DY events, the reconstructed mass of the

. To discriminate against

and QCD multijet events, we require

\tauh

\abs{\cos{\Delta\phi(\ptmiss,{\tauh}^{\text{l}})}}>0.9
. Further suppression of the contribution of QCD multijet events is achieved with
a requirement

.

0.7
Background estimation

The background yields are estimated in discrete intervals (bins) in the variable
m_{\textrm{rec}}(\PZpr)
, by
methods that depend on the SM process and the channel. In most cases, these estimates are based on samples of simulated events.
Differences between data and simulation with respect to the efficiency of triggers, particle ID or isolation, and energy scale and resolution of electrons, muons, jets, and
\ptmiss
are taken into account with dedicated correction factors whose uncertainties are propagated throughout.
These correction factors are described in the references cited in Section
0.4
.
Residual mismodeling can remain due to systematic effects of the theoretical predictions,
or to the phase space requirements specific to this analysis or to a specific channel.
To ensure that the effect of any such mismodeling and its uncertainty in the background yield is accounted for, we evaluate a data-to-simulation scale factor (SF) in a CR.
The systematic uncertainties in the SFs account for correlations between CRs and between the CRs and SRs.
Validation tests are performed using additional simulation and data control samples to ensure the correct determination of the SFs and their applicability to the SR background prediction.

The SFs are found to be approximately independent of
m_{\textrm{rec}}(\PZpr)
, and are thus applied to the
m_{\textrm{rec}}(\PZpr)
distributions as bin-independent normalization factors.
Uncertainties from the residual shape dependence are evaluated as described in Section
0.8
. The SFs are derived for each data-taking year separately, along with their uncertainties and year-to-year correlations. The values of the SFs and their uncertainties are given in the following subsections. With a few exceptions, these values are consistent with unity.
In the case of the QCD multijet background, the estimate is derived from the yield measured in dedicated orthogonal data control samples using the ABCD method described in Section
0.7.1
.
For SM processes that contribute less than 5% to the background yield, the simulation is taken directly.

Each CR is constructed to be enriched in a given SM process and orthogonal to the corresponding SR, but with characteristics similar to those of the SR. We correct for contamination from other SM processes by subtracting their SF-corrected simulated yields. The resulting dependence of the estimate of one background process on the SF for another is treated through the uncertainty correlation terms in the ML fit.

A CR B is selected by the SR criteria except requiring an SS

Systematic uncertainties

We account for systematic uncertainties associated with the various sources as detailed in the following paragraphs.

The integrated luminosities for the 2016, 2017, and 2018 data-taking years have individual uncertainties of 1.2–2.5%
[
65
,
66
,
67
]
, while the overall uncertainty for the 2016–2018 period is 1.6%.
This uncertainty is applied to all background and signal processes.

Uncertainties in the pileup modeling corrections are evaluated by varying the total inelastic
{\Pp\Pp}
cross section used in the correction procedure by
\pm
5%
[
68
,
69
]
. The effect of the resulting change in the distribution of the number of primary vertices is less than 0.01%, and is therefore treated as negligible.

The efficiencies for the electron and muon reconstruction, ID, and isolation are measured with a “tag-and-probe method”
[
38
,
40
,
70
]
with a resulting uncertainty of
\leq
2%, depending on
\pt
and
\eta
.
The total efficiency for the
\tauh
ID and isolation requirements is measured from a fit to the
\mu\tauh
invariant mass distribution from
\PZ\to\tau\tau
events, not taking
into account neutrino hypotheses or
\ptmiss
.
This is done in a sample selected with one isolated
\mu
trigger candidate with
\pt>24\GeV
, and leads to a relative uncertainty of less than 9% per genuine
\tauh
candidate
[
41
]
, dependent on
\pt
and
\eta
.
These uncertainties have contributions that are either statistical and thereby uncorrelated, or systematic and therefore correlated across the data-taking years.
In the cases where background estimates rely on the modeling of the misidentification of jets as
\tauh
candidates, additional systematic uncertainties in the corresponding background yields are assigned
that range from 20 to 30%.

For the lepton momentum scale, the reconstructed momentum is shifted by 5% and the
\ptvecmiss
vector recalculated.
This results in a change in the expected signal yield of 1–6%, depending on
m_{\PZpr}
, and negligible change for the backgrounds.
The corresponding resolution uncertainty in the simulation is taken as 0.03 times the difference between the reconstructed and true values of the lepton’s momentum
[
38
]
. It is found to have negligible impact on either signal or background yields.

Similarly, we include the uncertainties in the energy scale and resolution of jets and the propagation thereof to
\ptmiss

[
36
]
. The impact on the SR selection is indirect, affecting only the vetos on
\PQb
-tagged jets and VBF dijet candidates.
Accordingly, the signal event yield is not affected by either uncertainty, while the background event yields vary, depending on the process, by less than 2%.

We propagate the uncertainty in the
\PQb
jet mistagging SF
[
43
]

to its effect on the
\PQb
jet requirements in the SR selection.
The resulting uncertainty in the signal yield is found to be
\sim
1%.
The effect of the
\PQb
tagging efficiency uncertainty on the mass distribution in the
\ttbar
CRs for the
\PGt_{\Pell}\tauh
channels is measured by varying the
\PQb
tagging SF up and down by its uncertainty; the resulting uncertainty in the
\ttbar
SF is about 2%.

The uncertainties in the cross sections used to predict the rates of various processes, which arise primarily from the factorization and renormalization scale choices and the PDFs, are propagated to the yield estimates.
The cross section uncertainties are each separated into their scale (renormalization and factorization scales) and PDF components.
They are correlated where appropriate among processes.
To derive systematic uncertainties in the predicted signal yields arising from the choice of PDFs used in the simulation, we vary the PDFs following the PDF4LHC prescription
[
71
]
.
We find that these variations do not affect the shapes of kinematic distributions, but the resulting systematic uncertainty in the overall signal acceptance prediction is 2–6%, depending on the assumed value of
m_{\PZpr}
.

Uncertainties in the renormalization and factorization scales
are evaluated as the differences in predicted signal yields
resulting from independent variation of these scales from their default values by factors
of 2.0 and 0.5
[
72
]
.
The resulting uncertainties in the yields are
<
2%, independent of
m_{\PZpr}
.

The statistical uncertainty associated with the SFs measured in the CRs is propagated to the total uncertainty in the expected yields in the SR.
The SFs in the
\ttbar
CRs, where some dependence on
m_{\textrm{rec}}(\PZpr)
is observed, are fit with a first-order polynomial as a function of
m_{\textrm{rec}}(\PZpr)
.
The deviation of the resulting function from unity is taken as the systematic uncertainty in the shape.
The effect spans the range 1–22%, depending on the
m_{\textrm{rec}}(\PZpr)
bin and data-taking year.

For the QCD transfer factors
N_{\text{C}}/N_{\text{D}}
, the statistical uncertainties are in the ranges 19–23% for
\PGt_{\PGm}\tauh
, 23–26% for
\PGt_{\Pe}\tauh
, and 1.7–2.1% for
\tauh\tauh
.
For each of the
\PGt_{\PGm}\tauh
and
\PGt_{\Pe}\tauh
channels, the QCD contribution to the SR is small (6–9%), while it is large for the
\tauh\tauh
channel (27–35%), albeit mostly at low
m_{\textrm{rec}}(\PZpr)
and therefore of low relevance to
high-
m_{\PZpr}
signal scenarios. Closure tests in a low-
\ptmiss
sideband version of the ABCD method for the QCD estimation in the
\tauh\tauh
channel show agreement within statistical uncertainties, and so no additional closure systematic uncertainty is applied.
Instead, for all three channels, the transfer factors are fit with a first-order polynomial as a function of
m_{\textrm{rec}}(\PZpr)
, and
the deviation of this function from unity provides the
m_{\textrm{rec}}(\PZpr)
shape uncertainty.
The resulting systematic uncertainties for the three channels are 2.0–150% (
\PGt_{\PGm}\tauh

2.0–50% (
\PGt_{\Pe}\tauh

\tauh\tauh

The large relative values of the shape systematic uncertainties occur at the high end of the
m_{\textrm{rec}}(\PZpr)

\PGt_{\PGm}\tauh

in this
m_{\textrm{rec}}(\PZpr)

Results

The signal strength is extracted from an ML fit of the probability density function to the data yields in the SRs for all bins of the
m_{\textrm{rec}}(\PZpr)
distribution, for all channels, and all data-taking years. The probability density function is a sum of the corresponding predictions of the yields for the signal and for each of the SM background processes, with the signal strength as a free scale parameter modifying the signal prediction.
Statistical uncertainties from the CRs and simulated samples, as well as all systematic uncertainties, are introduced as nuisance parameters, including their correlations. The nuisance parameters are constrained by log-normal penalty factors in the likelihood, which are uncorrelated across the
m_{\textrm{rec}}(\PZpr)
bins. Any cross-contamination among the CRs is accounted for by the correlation terms between the CRs.
The contamination of CRs by the signal is found to be small (order of percent or less), and is neglected in the fit model.
The ML fit is implemented with the CMS statistical analysis tool
Combine

[
73
]
.

0.10
Summary

A search has been performed in proton-proton collisions at
\sqrt{s}=13\TeV
for the production via quark-antiquark annihilation of a heavy neutral gauge boson
\PZpr
decaying to
\PGtp\PGtm
. The data were recorded at the LHC
during 2016–2018, and correspond to an integrated luminosity of 138
\fbinv
.
As the tau lepton can decay hadronically (
\tauh
) or leptonically (
\PGt_{\Pell}
), with
\Pell
representing a muon
or electron, the analysis includes the three decay channels with the highest
\PGt
lepton branching fraction products:

\PZpr\to\PGt_{\PGm}^{\pm}\tauh^{\mp}
,
\PZpr\to\PGt_{\Pe}^{\pm}\tauh^{\mp}
, and
\PZpr\to\tauh^{+}\tauh^{-}
.
An estimator
m_{\textrm{rec}}(\PZpr)
is constructed to approximate the
\PZpr
boson mass
m_{\PZpr}
in the presence of undetected neutrino daughters from the
\PGt
lepton decays.

The main background processes are estimated using Monte Carlo simulation adjusted by scale factors derived from data.
The observed yields are found to be consistent with the background prediction. A shape-based analysis is performed using
the
m_{\textrm{rec}}(\PZpr)
distribution as the fit discriminant to determine the likelihood for the observed data in the presence of a signal and the predicted background contributions.
The upper limit on the product of the
\PZpr
boson production cross section and decay branching fraction
is set at 95% confidence level as a function of
m_{\PZpr}
.

In the scenario considered in this analysis, the data excludes a
\PZpr
boson with
m_{\PZpr}
less than 3.0, 3.5, or 4.1
\TeV
for a branching fraction of 1, 3, or 10%, respectively.
These exclusion limits are the most stringent to date for a
\PZpr
boson decaying to
\PGtp\PGtm
.

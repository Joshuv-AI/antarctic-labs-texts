1.
Introduction

One of the most widely-used bounds in the theory of multiplicative functions was proven by Peter Shiu in 1980
[
Sh
]
, who proved a Brun-Titchmarsh-type result for these functions. We begin by stating this result below.

Let
\mathcal{M}
denote the set of nonnegative multiplicative functions
f
such that the following two conditions hold.

Condition i
.

There exists a constant
A_{1}
such that for any prime
p
and any natural number
l
,

f(p^{l})\leq A_{1}^{l}.

Condition ii
.

For any
\epsilon>0
there exists a constant
A_{2}=A_{2}(\epsilon)
such that for any
n
,

f(n)\leq A_{2}n^{\epsilon}.

Shiu’s theorem is then the following.

Theorem 1.1

(Shiu, 1980)
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{M}
. Let
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
and
0<\kappa<\frac{1}{2}
, and let
x^{\kappa}\leq y\leq x
and
k<y^{1-\alpha}
. Moreover, let
a
be such that
(a,k)=1
. Then for all sufficiently large
x
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq n<x+y\\
n\equiv a\pmod{k}\end{subarray}}f(n)\ll\frac{y}{\phi(k)\log x}\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}\right).

Here, the term ”sufficiently large” depends only on the choices of
\alpha
,
\kappa
, and
\epsilon
and the constants
A_{1}
and
A_{2}(\epsilon)
.

However, there are a few limitations to this theorem that we wish to address in the current paper. First, this theorem cannot say anything when
f(p)
is unbounded. Second, the result is limited in the case where
f
is a smooth-supported function, since smooth numbers are generally far more sparse than this bound would indicate. In this paper, we expand this theorem to handle both such cases.

2.
Introduction: Larger Functions

Let us begin with the idea of letting
f(p)
go to infinity. We modify Shiu’s condition first condition in the following way:

Condition 1
.

For some fixed
\beta>0
, there exists an
A_{1}
such that for any prime
p
,

(1)

\displaystyle f(p^{l})\leq A_{1}^{l}(\log\log x)^{\beta l}.

For the second condition, we would like to use something similar to Condition ii above. However, this is too restrictive for our purposes, since Condition 1 would allow
f(2)
to be as big as
(\log\log x)^{\beta}
, which would contradict the requirement that
f(2)\ll 2^{\epsilon}
.

To counter this, we add an extra term to handle the case of
p
small:

Condition 2
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exist
A_{2}=A_{2}(\epsilon)
and
A_{3}=A_{3}(\epsilon)
such that for every
n
,

(2)

\displaystyle f(n)\leq\max\{A_{2}x^{\epsilon},A_{3}(\log x)^{\epsilon}\}.

Since Condition 1 depends on
\beta
, we will denote by
\mathcal{M}(\beta)
the set of nonnegative multiplicative functions that satisfy Condition 1 for a given
\beta
and Condition 2 as above.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
x
be large. Let
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
and
0<\kappa<\frac{1}{2}
, and let
x^{\kappa}\leq y\leq x
and
k<y^{1-\alpha}
. Moreover, let
f\in\mathcal{M}(\beta)
for some fixed
\beta<\frac{\alpha\kappa}{41}
. Then for any
\epsilon_{0}>0
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq n\leq x+y\\
n\equiv a\pmod{k}\end{subarray}}f(n)\ll\frac{x}{\phi(k)(\log x)^{1-\epsilon_{0}}}\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}\right).

We remark that the
\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1-\epsilon_{0}}}
in the theorem above could be improved to
\frac{e^{(\log\log x)^{1-\epsilon_{0}}}}{\log x}
if we tightened Condition 2 to

\displaystyle f(n)\leq\max\{A_{2}x^{\epsilon},A_{3}e^{(\log\log x)^{1-\gamma}}\}

for some
0<\gamma<1
.

Further tightening of Condition 2 to smaller orders of magnitude may well lead to further improvement. However, it appears that this method could not fully recover the
1/\log x
that appears in Shiu’s original argument as long as
f(p)
remains an unbounded function.

3.
Introduction: Smooth-Supported Functions

Next, we examine the case where
f
is smooth-supported function. For a given
Q\leq x
, define

u=\frac{\log x}{\log Q},

let
\psi(x,Q)
denote the number of
Q
-smooth numbers up to
x
, and let
\mathcal{S}(Q)
denote the
Q
-smooth numbers. It is known that when
Q\geq\exp\left((\log\log x)^{\frac{5}{3}+\epsilon}\right)
, a value for
\psi(x,Q)
is given by the formula

(3)

\displaystyle\psi(x,Q)=x\rho(u)(1+o(1)),

where
\rho
is the Dickman-de Bruijin function. It is believed that this holds for all
Q\geq(\log x)^{2+\epsilon}
, and indeed this is known to be true under the Riemann Hypothesis
[
Hi
]
. Importantly,

\rho(u)=u^{-u+o(1)}

holds for all
u
.

On a short interval, we have a 1986 result of Hildebrandt
[
Hi
]
, which states that if
Q
is sufficiently large and
x>y>Q
then

(4)

\displaystyle\psi(x+y,Q)-\psi(x,Q)\leq\psi(y,Q).

On the other hand, Shiu’s theorem with
k=1
gives a bound of

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq n\leq x+y\\
n\in\mathcal{S}(Q)\end{subarray}}1\ll\frac{y\log Q}{\log x}.

Shiu’s bound is clearly not sharp here, as
\frac{y\log Q}{\log x}
may be much larger than
\psi(y,Q)
.

Here, we bring the Shiu bound a bit closer to expectations for any multiplicative function
f
. For ease of notation, we will define
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
to be the set of
Q
-smooth supported nonnegative functions
f
in
\mathcal{M}
, and we will define
\mathcal{M}_{Q}(\beta)
analogously.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
x
,
y
,
\alpha
,
\kappa
, and
k
be as in Theorem
2.1
. There exists a constant
C_{1}
such that if
f\in\mathcal{M}_{Q}
then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq n<x+y\\
n\equiv a\pmod{k}\end{subarray}}f(n)\ll\rho(u)^{C_{1}}\frac{x}{\phi(k)\log x}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq Q\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}\right).

Moreover, let
Q\leq e^{\frac{\log x\log\log\log x}{\log\log x}}.
Then there exists a
\beta<\frac{\alpha\kappa}{41}
and a constant
C_{2}
such that if
f\in\mathcal{M}_{Q}(\beta)
then for any
\epsilon>0
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq n<x+y\\
n\equiv a\pmod{k}\end{subarray}}f(n)\ll\rho(u)^{C_{2}}\frac{x}{\phi(k)(\log x)^{1-\epsilon}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq Q\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}\right).

It will suffice to take

C_{1}=\frac{\alpha\kappa}{41}

and

C_{2}=\frac{5}{656}\left(\frac{\alpha\kappa\left(1-\frac{\alpha\kappa}{20}\right)}{20}\right).

We note here that while we do not put a lower bound on
Q
, Theorem
3.1
ceases to be non-trivial when
Q=(\log x)^{K}
for
K<\frac{\kappa}{C_{1}}
or
K<\frac{\kappa}{C_{2}}
(depending upon which result in Theorem
3.1
we are discussing). This is because such a
Q
would have
\rho(u)\ll x^{\frac{1}{K}}
, and hence using (
4
) with the trivial bound of
f(n)\ll x^{\epsilon}
gives a bound of

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq n<x+y\\
n\equiv a\pmod{k}\end{subarray}}f(n)\ll x^{\epsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq n<x+y\\
n\equiv a\pmod{k}\end{subarray}}1\ll y^{\frac{1}{K}+\epsilon}.

4.
Applications

We also present two applications of these theorems. First, letting
\tau_{d}
denote the
d
-fold divisor function (and letting
\tau=\tau_{2}
), we find a bound for the sum of
\tau_{d}(n)^{R}
as
R
slowly goes to infinity:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
x
,
y
, and
k
be as in Theorem
2.1
. Choose an integer
d\geq 2
, and let
R=\frac{\log\log\log x}{g(x)}
for some
g(x)
such that
g(x)\to\infty
as
x\to\infty
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq n\leq x+y\\
n\equiv a\pmod{k}\end{subarray}}\tau_{d}(n)^{R}\ll\frac{x(\log x)^{\epsilon}}{\phi(k)}\left(\frac{\phi(k)}{k}\log x\right)^{d^{R}-1}.

Moreover,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq n\leq x+y\\
n\equiv a\pmod{k}\\
n\in\mathcal{S}(Q)\end{subarray}}\tau_{d}(n)^{R}\ll\rho(u)^{C_{2}+o(1)}\frac{x(\log x)^{\epsilon}}{\phi(k)}\left(\frac{\phi(k)}{k}\log x\right)^{d^{R}-1}.

For the second application, we note that one of the inspirations for this paper comes from a 2008 result of Soundararajan on smooth numbers
[
So
]
. We state this result here.

Theorem 4.2

(Soundararajan 2008)
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis. Let
x
be large, and suppose that
Q\geq\exp\left(5\sqrt{\log x\log\log x}\right)
. There is an absolute constant
B
such that with
y=Bu\sqrt{x}/\rho(u/2)
we have

(5)

\displaystyle\psi(x+y,Q)-\psi(x,Q)\gg yx^{-\epsilon}.

More recently, Goudout
[
Go
]
found an unconditional version of this theorem, albeit with a smaller range for
Q
and
y
but a better lower bound:

Theorem 4.3

(Goudout 2017)
.

Fix a positive
\epsilon<\frac{1}{6}
and a constant
u_{0}
depending on
\epsilon
. Let
Q
be such that
\exp\left((\log x)^{\frac{1}{6}-\epsilon}\right)\leq Q\leq x^{\frac{1}{u_{0}}}
. Then for any
h
with
\rho(u)^{-3-\epsilon}\leq h\leq\sqrt{x}
, if we let
y=h\sqrt{x}
then

(6)

\displaystyle\psi(x+y,Q)-\psi(x,Q)\gg y\frac{\rho(u)^{2}}{\log^{3}x}.

With our improvement on Shiu’s theorem, we can improve the lower bound in (
5
) to almost the correct order of magnitude, and we can similarly improve the exponent on
\rho(u)
in (
6
) to
1+o(1)
:

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
Q
,
x
, and
y
satisfy either the hypotheses in either Theorem
4.2
or
4.3
, where in the former case we also assume the Riemann Hypothesis. Additionally, assume that
u\geq(\log\log x)^{2}.
Then there exists a constant
C_{3}
such that

(7)

\displaystyle\psi(x+y,Q)-\psi(x,Q)\gg y\rho(u)^{1+\frac{C_{3}\log\log\log\log x}{\log\log\log x}}.

In particular, we can take
C_{3}>1-C_{2}
.

The assumption here that
u\geq(\log\log x)^{2}
is simply to eliminate log factors, since if
u
satisfies this inequality then for any
A
,

\log^{A}x=\rho(u)^{o(1)}.

5.
Outlines of proofs

5.1.
Outline of Proof of Theorem
2.1

Shiu’s original proof relies heavily on the following identity: for any
\delta>3/4
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(n,k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\delta}}\ll

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{f(n)}{n^{\delta}}+\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}\frac{f(p^{l})}{p^{l\delta}}\right)\leq\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{f(p)}{p^{\delta}}+\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}\frac{f(p^{l})}{p^{l\delta}}\right)\right).

Since
f(p)
is bounded by a constant
A_{1}
in Shiu’s work, we have that if
p^{2}>A_{1}
then

(8)

\displaystyle\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}\frac{f(p^{l})}{p^{l\delta}}\leq\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{A_{1}}{p^{\delta}}\right)^{l}\leq\frac{2A_{1}^{2}}{p^{2\delta}}

Meanwhile, if
p^{2}\leq A_{1}
then (by Condition ii) we have
f(p^{l})\leq A_{2}p^{l\epsilon}
, which means that

(9)

\displaystyle\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}\frac{f(p^{l})}{p^{l}}\leq\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}A_{2}\left(\frac{p^{\epsilon}}{p^{\delta}}\right)^{l}\leq\frac{3A_{2}}{p^{2\delta-2\epsilon}}.

Hence

(10)

\displaystyle\sum_{p}\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}\frac{f(p^{l})}{p^{l\delta}}\ll 1,

and thus

(11)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(n,k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\delta}}\ll\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p^{\delta}}\right).

Shiu then splits the sum

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq n<x+y\\
n\equiv a\pmod{k}\end{subarray}}f(n)

into four subsums. Three of the sums can be handled by sieve arguments, bounds on smooth numbers, or similar. For the fourth sum, however, Shiu uses an alteration to (
11
), finding that for relatively small
r
,

(12)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq z^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
p(n)\leq z^{\frac{1}{r}}\\
(n,k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{f(n)}{n}\ll\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}-\frac{1}{10}r\log r\right).

This comes in handy when
n
has a “reasonable” number of distinct prime factors
r
, which is the set of
n
that Shiu denoted by IV in his paper. In this case, the negative term will counteract the growing number of factors. In particular, for a constant
A_{5}
that depends on both
A_{1}
and the choice of
z
, and for an
r_{0}
depending on
x
, he is able to bound the sum of
f(n)
in this set with

\sum_{n\in IV}f(n)\ll\sum_{2\leq r\leq r_{0}}\frac{y}{\phi(k)\log x}A_{5}^{r}\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}-\frac{1}{10}r\log r\right)\ll\frac{y}{\phi(k)\log x}\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}\right),

which yields the main theorem in Shiu’s paper.

If
f(p)
is an increasing function of
x
, however, a few of these arguments fall apart. For one, (
10
) no longer holds, since
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
in (
8
) and (
9
) are replaced by functions of
x
. The bound in (
12
) also no longer holds for much the same reason. Moreover, in the bound for the sum over IV,
A_{5}^{r}
is replaced by
\max_{p\leq x}\left(A_{5}f(p)\right)^{r}
, which is a function on
r
that may not be smaller than
\exp\left(\frac{1}{10}r\log r\right)
.

In order to close these gaps in the proof, we make a couple of alterations. A key change is that, for a parameter
J=(\log\log x)^{1-\beta}
, we split
n
into
J
-smooth and
J
-rough parts, which we will call
s
and
t
, respectively. Since
f
is multiplicative, we have
f(n)=f(s)f(t)
. The sum over
s
is small and relatively easy to handle, since
s
itself is usually small. For the sum over
t
, we now have

(13)

\displaystyle\sum_{p\geq J}\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}\frac{f(p^{l})}{p^{l\delta}}\ll\sum_{p\geq J}\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{A_{1}(\log\log x)^{\beta}}{p^{\delta}}\right)^{l}\leq 2\sum_{p\geq J}\frac{A_{1}^{2}(\log\log x)^{2\beta}}{p^{2\delta}}\ll\frac{A_{1}^{2}(\log\log x)^{2\beta}}{J^{2\delta-1}}=o(1),

since
\beta
is much smaller than 1/5. Hence,

(14)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(n,k)=1\\
n\in\mathcal{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\delta}}\ll\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq X\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p^{\delta}}\right).

In the cases where we have a larger number
r
of distinct prime factors (i.e. the case where
n\in IV
), we will now have a slightly worsened bound of

(15)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in IV}f(n)\ll\frac{y}{\phi(k)\log x}(A_{5}(\log\log x)^{\beta})^{\frac{20r}{\alpha\kappa}}\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}-\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{5}{4}\beta)r\log r+2A_{1}(\log\log x)^{\beta}r^{1-\frac{5}{4}\beta}\right).

The
\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{5}{4}\beta)r\log r
will eventually dominate once
r
is large enough, but this will not occur until
r
is close to
\log\log x
. This transition period from
r=2
to
r
being close to
\log\log x
is one of the reasons for the loss of
(\log x)^{\epsilon}
in the main theorem.

t\equiv a\pmod{k}\end{subarray}}f(t)\ll\frac{y\rho(u)^{C_{2}+o(1)}}{\phi(k)(\log x)^{1-\epsilon}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq x\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}\right).

If we are willing to assume these, we can easily find bounds for
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
. For ease of notation, define
\varrho
such that

if

and
\varrho=\rho(u)^{C_{2}+o(1)}
if

.

Lemma 6.4
.

Assume Theorem
6.2
or
6.3
. Then

V_{1}\ll\frac{x\varrho}{\phi(k)(\log x)^{1-\epsilon_{0}}}\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq x\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}\right).

Proof.

Clearly,

\sum_{s\leq(\log x)^{10}}\frac{1}{s}\ll\log\log x.

So applying either theorem and Condition
2
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{s\leq(\log x)^{10}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{x}{s}\leq t\leq\frac{x}{s}+\frac{y}{s}\\
t\equiv a\overline{s}\pmod{k}\end{subarray}}f(st)\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{s\leq(\log x)^{10}}\frac{x\varrho f(s)}{\phi(k)s(\log x)^{1-\epsilon}}\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq x\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}\right)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\frac{x\varrho}{\phi(k)(\log x)^{1-\epsilon_{0}}}\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq x\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}\right),

which is as required.
∎

Finally, we handle
V_{2}
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Assume Theorem
6.2
or
6.3
. Then for any
\epsilon_{0}>0
,

V_{2}\ll\frac{x\varrho}{\phi(k)(\log x)^{10-\epsilon_{0}}}\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq x\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}\right).

Proof.

We begin with the assumed theorem:

(18)

\displaystyle\sum_{(\log x)^{10}<s\leq x^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{x}{s}\leq t\leq\frac{x}{s}+\frac{y}{s}\\
t\equiv a\overline{s}\pmod{k}\end{subarray}}f(st)\ll\sum_{(\log x)^{10}<s\leq x^{\alpha}}\frac{x\varrho f(s)}{\phi(k)s(\log x)^{1-\epsilon}}\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq x\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}\right).

Note that for any
s
in the interval
((\log x)^{10},x^{\alpha}]
, we have

(\log\log x)^{1-\beta}\leq\left(\frac{1}{10}\log s\right)^{1-\beta}.

So by (
16
),

(19)

\displaystyle\psi(s,J)\ll s^{\epsilon}

for any
\epsilon>0
.

So we apply partial summation to the sum
V_{2}
, where we will use the variable
v
to indicate where we have dropped the requirement of smoothness:

\displaystyle\sum_{(\log x)^{10}<s\leq x^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\frac{f(s)}{s}=

\displaystyle\sum_{(\log x)^{10}<v\leq x^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}-1}\left(\frac{f(v)}{v}-\frac{f(v+1)}{v+1}\right)\sum_{(\log x)^{10}<s\leq v}1+\frac{1}{x^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\sum_{(\log x)^{10}<s\leq x^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}f(s).

Applying (
19
) to the sum and
f(v)\ll v^{\epsilon}
to the function then gives

\displaystyle\sum_{(\log x)^{10}<s\leq x^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\frac{f(s)}{s}\ll

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{(\log x)^{10}<v\leq x^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}-1}\frac{1}{v^{1-2\epsilon}(v+1)}\right)+\frac{1}{x^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-2\epsilon}}\ll(\log x)^{-9}.

Plugging this into (
18
) then yields the lemma.

∎

The next sections will then set up the machinery to prove Theorems
6.2
and
6.3
.

7.
The Sum over Rough Numbers

Hereafter, for ease of notation, we will write
X=x/s
,
Y=y/s
, and
b\equiv a\overline{s}\pmod{k}
. We are tasked with evaluating

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\leq t\leq X+Y\\
t\equiv b\pmod{k}\end{subarray}}f(t).

From here, our methods will largely follow those of
[
Sh
]
. As in that paper, we will let
p(n)
and
q(n)
denote the greatest and least prime factors of
n
, respectively, and we define

\Phi(x,y,z;k,a)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq n\leq x+y\\
n\equiv a\pmod{k}\\
q(n)>z\end{subarray}}1.

By Lemma 2 of
[
Sh
]
, we know that

(20)

\displaystyle\Phi(x,y,z;k,a)\ll\frac{y}{\phi(k)\log z}+z^{2}.

Generally, we will be taking
y
,
k
, and
z
such that
y/k
is much larger than
z
, and hence the latter term can be ignored.

We also record a few statements about smooth numbers for later. From
[
Sh
, Lemma 1]
,

(21)

\displaystyle\psi(x,\log x\log\log x)\ll\exp\left(\frac{3\log x}{(\log\log x)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right).

Meanwhile, for larger
z
, we know from (
3
) that

(22)

\displaystyle\psi(x,z)\ll x\exp\left(-\frac{\log x(\log x-\log z)}{\log z}\right).

We will also require one more preliminary lemma before we turn our attention to the main sum. This is a slightly altered version of
[
Sh
, Lemma 4]
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{M}(\beta)
. Then as
z\to\infty
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq z^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
p(n)\leq z^{\frac{1}{r}}\\
(n,k)=1\\
n\in\mathcal{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{f(n)}{n}\ll\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{5}{4}\beta\right)r\log r+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq z^{\frac{1}{r}}\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}+2A_{1}(\log\log x)^{\beta}r^{1-\frac{5}{4}\beta}\right)

uniformly in
k
and
r
, provided that
1\leq r\leq\frac{\log z}{4\log\log z}
.

Proof.

Let
\frac{3}{4}<\delta\leq 1
. By Rankin’s trick,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq w\\
p(n)\leq v\\
(n,k)=1\\
n\in\mathcal{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{f(n)}{n}\leq

\displaystyle w^{\delta-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq w\\
p(n)\leq v\\
(n,k)=1\\
n\in\mathcal{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\delta}}\leq w^{\delta-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\\
p(n)\leq v\\
(n,k)=1\\
n\in\mathcal{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\delta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w^{\delta-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq v\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{f(p)}{p^{\delta}}+\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}\frac{f(p^{l})}{p^{l\delta}}\right)\ll\exp\left((\delta-1)\log w+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq v\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p^{\delta}}\right)

by (
14
).
We rewrite
\frac{f(p)}{p^{\delta}}
as
\frac{f(p)}{p}+\frac{f(p)}{p}(p^{1-\delta}-1)
and note that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq v\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}(p^{1-\delta}-1)\leq

\displaystyle A_{1}(\log\log x)^{\beta}\sum_{p\leq v}\frac{1}{p}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left((1-\delta)(\log p)\right)^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A_{1}(1-\delta)(\log\log x)^{\beta}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left((1-\delta)(\log v)\right)^{n-1}}{n!}\sum_{p\leq v}\frac{\log p}{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A_{1}(\log\log x)^{\beta}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left((1-\delta)(\log v)\right)^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2A_{1}(\log\log x)^{\beta}v^{1-\delta}.

So

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq u\\
p(n)\leq v\\
(n,k)=1\\
n\in\mathcal{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{f(n)}{n}\ll\exp\left((\delta-1)\log u+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq v\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}+2A_{1}(\log\log x)^{\beta}v^{1-\delta}\right).

Letting
u=z^{\frac{1}{2}}
,
v=z^{\frac{1}{r}}
, and
\delta=1-\frac{(1-\frac{5}{4}\beta)r\log r}{\log z}
then gives

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq u\\
p(n)\leq v\\
(n,k)=1\\
n\in\mathcal{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{f(n)}{n}\ll\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{5}{4}\beta\right)r\log r+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq z^{\frac{1}{r}}\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}+2A_{1}(\log\log x)^{\beta}r^{1-\frac{5}{4}\beta}\right).

∎

8.
Partitioning the Sum: The Proof of Theorem
6.2

Now, we partition the sum as in Section 5 of
[
Sh
]
. Let

z=Y^{\frac{\alpha}{10}}.

Note that by this definition,
Y/k>z^{2}
, and hence we can omit the
z^{2}
-term in (
20
).

For each
n
with
X\leq n<X+Y
,
n\equiv b\pmod{k}
, write

n=p_{1}^{u_{1}}\cdots p_{j}^{u_{j}}p_{j+1}^{u_{j+1}}\cdots p_{l}^{u_{l}}=c_{n}d_{n}

where
p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots<p_{l}
and
c_{n}
is the product of the first
j
terms, where
j
is chosen such that

c_{n}\leq z<c_{n}p_{j+1}^{u_{j+1}}.

We then define the following four subclasses of
\mathcal{T}
:

\displaystyle\mbox{I}:q(d_{n})>z^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\mbox{II}:q(d_{n})\leq z^{\frac{1}{2}},c_{n}\leq z^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\mbox{III}:q(d_{n})\leq\left(\log x\log\log x\right)^{5},c_{n}>z^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\mbox{IV}:\left(\log x\log\log x\right)^{5}<q(d_{n})\leq z^{\frac{1}{2}},c_{n}>z^{\frac{1}{2}}.

8.1.
Class I

Here, we have

\sum_{n\in I}f(n)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\leq z\\
c\in\mathcal{T}\end{subarray}}f(c)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{X}{c}\leq d\leq\frac{X+Y}{c}\\
d\equiv b\overline{c}\pmod{k}\\
q(d)>z^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{subarray}}f(d).

Note that for any such
n
,
q(d)>z^{\frac{1}{2}}=Y^{\frac{\alpha}{20}}=X^{\frac{\alpha\kappa}{20}}.
So
d
can have at most
\frac{20}{\alpha\kappa}
prime factors, and hence
f(d)\leq\left(A_{1}(\log\log x)^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{20}{\alpha\kappa}}
. Noting that the number of
z^{\frac{1}{2}}
-rough numbers up to
x
in a congruence class mod
k
is
\ll\frac{x}{\phi(k)\log z}
, and recalling that
\beta<\frac{\alpha\kappa}{41}
, we can bound the above with

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in I}f(n)\leq

\displaystyle\left(A_{1}(\log\log x)^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{20}{\alpha\kappa}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\leq z\\
c\in\mathcal{T}\end{subarray}}f(c)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{X}{c}\leq d\leq\frac{X+Y}{c}\\
d\equiv b\overline{c}\pmod{k}\\
q(d)>z^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\frac{Y}{\phi(k)\log z}\left(\log\log x\right)^{\frac{20}{41}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\leq z\\
c\in\mathcal{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{f(c)}{c}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\frac{Y}{\phi(k)\log x}\left(\log\log x\right)^{\frac{20}{41}}\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq X\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}\right),

where the last line is by (
14
).

8.2.
Class II

In this case, there must exist a
p|n
such that
p\leq z^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
p^{u}>z^{\frac{1}{2}}
. For such a
p
, let
u_{p}
denote the least integer such that
p^{u_{p}}>z^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Then
p^{-u_{p}}\leq\min\{z^{-\frac{1}{2}},p^{-2}\}
. Hence

\sum_{p\leq z^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{1}{p^{u_{p}}}\leq\sum_{p\leq z^{\frac{1}{4}}}z^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\sum_{p>z^{\frac{1}{4}}}\frac{1}{p^{2}}\ll z^{-\frac{1}{4}}.

Then for any
\epsilon>0
,

\sum_{n\in II}f(n)\ll x^{\epsilon}\sum_{p\leq z^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\leq n<X+Y\\
n\equiv b\pmod{k}\\
p^{u_{p}}|n\end{subarray}}1\ll x^{\epsilon}\sum_{p\leq z^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{Y}{kp^{u_{p}}}\ll\frac{Yx^{\epsilon}}{kz^{\frac{1}{4}}}.

8.3.
Class III

In this case, we have
c
such that
z^{\frac{1}{2}}<c\leq z
and
c
is
(\log x\log\log x)^{5}
-smooth. So

\sum_{n\in III}f(n)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z^{\frac{1}{2}}<c\leq z\\
p(c)\leq(\log x\log\log x)^{5}\end{subarray}}f(c)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{X}{c}\leq d\leq\frac{X+Y}{c}\\
d\equiv b\overline{c}\pmod{k}\end{subarray}}f(d)\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z^{\frac{1}{2}}<c\leq z\\
p(c)\leq(\log x\log\log x)^{5}\end{subarray}}\frac{Yx^{\epsilon}}{kc}

Let

v=\left(\frac{\log z^{\frac{1}{2}}}{5\log\left(\log x\log\log x\right)}\right)^{-\frac{\log z^{\frac{1}{2}}}{5\log\left(\log x\log\log x\right)}}\ll\left(\log z\right)^{-\frac{\log z}{11\log\log x}}\ll x^{-\frac{\alpha\kappa}{250}}.

We can handle the sum dyadically and apply (
22
), finding

(23)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z^{\frac{1}{2}}<c\leq z\\
p(c)\leq(\log x\log\log x)^{5}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{c}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\frac{\log z}{\log 2}}\frac{1}{2^{j}z^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2^{j}z^{\frac{1}{2}}<c\leq 2^{j+1}z^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
p(c)\leq(\log x\log\log x)^{5}\end{subarray}}1\ll\sum_{j=0}^{\frac{\log z}{\log 2}}\frac{1}{2^{j}z^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left[2^{j+1}z^{\frac{1}{2}}v\right]\ll v\log z\ll x^{-\frac{\alpha\kappa}{300}}.

So

\sum_{n\in III}f(n)\ll\frac{Yx^{\epsilon-\frac{\alpha\kappa}{300}}}{k}\ll\frac{Yx^{-\epsilon^{\prime}}}{k}

for some
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
.

8.4.
Class IV

This class gives us

\sum_{n\in IV}f(n)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z^{\frac{1}{2}}<c\leq z\\
c\in\mathcal{T}\end{subarray}}f(c)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{X}{c}\leq d\leq\frac{X+Y}{c}\\
d\equiv b\overline{c}\pmod{k}\\
q(d)>p(c)\\
(\log x\log\log x)^{5}<q(d)\leq z^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{subarray}}f(d).

Let

r_{0}=\left[\frac{\log z}{5\log(\log x\log\log x)}\right],

where we now have
\log x\log\log x>z^{\frac{1}{r_{0}+1}}
. Let
r
be such that
2\leq r\leq r_{0}
, and consider those
n
for which
z^{\frac{1}{r+1}}<q(d_{n})\leq z^{\frac{1}{r}}
. For such
n
, we know that the number of prime factors of
d_{n}
is bounded by

\Omega(d_{n})\leq\frac{\log x}{\log(q(d_{n}))}\leq\frac{20r}{\alpha\kappa}.

So

f(d_{n})\leq\left(A_{1}(\log\log x)^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{20r}{\alpha\kappa}}\leq A_{1}^{\frac{20r}{\alpha\kappa}}\left(\log\log x\right)^{\frac{20r}{41}}.

Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in IV}f(n)\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{2\leq r\leq r_{0}}A_{1}^{\frac{20r}{\alpha\kappa}}\left(\log\log x\right)^{\frac{20r}{41}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z^{\frac{1}{2}}<c\leq z\\
p(c)<z^{\frac{1}{r}}\\
c\in\mathcal{T}\end{subarray}}f(c)\Psi\left(\frac{X}{c},\frac{Y}{c},z^{\frac{1}{r+1}};k,b\overline{c}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{2\leq r\leq r_{0}}A_{1}^{\frac{20r}{\alpha\kappa}}\left(\log\log x\right)^{\frac{20r}{41}}\left(\frac{Y(r+1)}{\phi(k)\log z}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z^{\frac{1}{2}}<c\leq z\\
p(c)<z^{\frac{1}{r}}\\
c\in\mathcal{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{f(c)}{c}

by (
20
).

Let

r_{1}=(\log\log x)^{1-\frac{\beta}{2}}.

We can split the sum over
r
into

\sum_{2\leq r\leq r_{1}-1}+\sum_{r_{1}\leq r\leq r_{0}}.

For the former sum over
r
, we bound the sum over
c
using (
14
):

\displaystyle\sum_{2\leq r\leq r_{1}-1}

\displaystyle A_{1}^{\frac{20r}{\alpha\kappa}}\left(\log\log x\right)^{\frac{20r}{41}}\left(\frac{Y(r+1)}{\phi(k)\log z}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z^{\frac{1}{2}}<c\leq z\\
p(c)<z^{\frac{1}{r}}\\
c\in\mathcal{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{f(c)}{c}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{2\leq r\leq r_{1}-1}\left(\frac{Y(r+1)}{\phi(k)\log z}\right)A_{1}^{\frac{20r}{\alpha\kappa}}\left(\log\log x\right)^{\frac{20r}{41}}\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq z\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}\right)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\left(\frac{Yr_{1}}{\phi(k)\log z}\right)A_{1}^{\frac{20r_{1}}{\alpha\kappa}}\left(\log\log x\right)^{\frac{20r_{1}}{41}}\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq z\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}\right)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\left(\frac{Y}{\phi(k)\log z}\right)\exp\left((\log\log x)^{1-\frac{\beta}{3}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq z\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}\right)

Clearly, this is
\ll\frac{Y}{\phi(k)(\log x)^{1-\epsilon}}
for any
\epsilon>0
.

Turning now to the interval where
r\geq r_{1}
, we can apply Lemma
7.1
to bound the remaining sum as

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{r_{1}\leq r\leq r_{0}}A_{1}^{\frac{20r}{\alpha\kappa}}\left(\log\log x\right)^{\frac{20r}{41}}\left(\frac{Y(r+1)}{\phi(k)\log z}\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{5}{4}\beta\right)r\log r+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq z^{\frac{1}{r}}\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}+2A_{1}(\log\log x)^{\beta}r^{1-\frac{5}{4}\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{r_{1}\leq r\leq r_{0}}\left(\frac{Y}{\phi(k)\log z}\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq X\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}+\frac{20r\log A_{1}}{\alpha\kappa}+\frac{20r}{41}\log\log\log x+2\log r-\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{5}{4}\beta\right)r\log r+2A_{1}(\log\log x)^{\beta}r^{1-\frac{5}{4}\beta}\right).

Let

\displaystyle g(r)=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{5}{4}\beta\right)\log r,

\displaystyle h(r)=\frac{20\log A_{1}}{\alpha\kappa}+\frac{20}{41}\log\log\log x+\frac{2\log r}{r}+2A_{1}(\log\log x)^{\beta}r^{-\frac{5}{4}\beta}

So the above is

\ll\sum_{r_{1}\leq r\leq r_{0}}\left(\frac{Y}{\phi(k)\log z}\right)\exp\left(-r(g(r)-h(r))+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq X\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}\right).

Now, since
\beta<\frac{\alpha\kappa}{41}\leq\frac{1}{164}
,
we see that

\displaystyle g(r_{1})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{7}{4}\beta+\frac{5}{8}\beta^{2}+o(1)\right)\log\log\log x

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{164}+o(1)\right)\log\log\log x.

Meanwhile,

\displaystyle h(r_{1})=

\displaystyle\frac{20}{41}\log\log\log x+2A_{1}(\log\log x)^{\beta}(\log\log x)^{-\frac{5}{4}\beta+\frac{5}{8}\beta^{2}}+O(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{41}+o(1)\right)\log\log\log x.

So

g(r_{1})-h(r_{1})\geq\left(\frac{1}{60}+o(1)\right)\log\log\log x.

Noting also that
g^{\prime}(r)>h^{\prime}(r)
for any
r\in[r_{1},r_{0}]
, we can bound the above sum over
r
as

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{r_{1}\leq r\leq r_{0}}\left(\frac{Y}{\phi(k)\log z}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{r}{60}\log\log\log x+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq X\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}\right)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\left(\frac{Y}{\phi(k)\log z}\right)\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq X\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}\right)

Putting these together,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in IV}f(n)\ll

\displaystyle\left(\frac{Y}{\phi(k)\log x}\right)\exp\left((\log\log x)^{1-\frac{\beta}{3}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq X\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}\right)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\left(\frac{Y}{\phi(k)(\log x)^{1-\epsilon}}\right)\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J<p\leq X\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}\right)

for any
\epsilon>0
. This completes the proof of Theorem
6.2
and hence of Theorem
2.1
.

9.
Alterations for Smooth-Supported Functions

Here, we alter the steps of the previous section to prove the analogous theorems for smooth-supported functions. We note first that
\frac{\log Q}{\log x}=o(1)
, else the two bounds in Theorem
3.1
are just Shiu’s theorem and Theorem
2.1
, respectively.

Class I cannot happen, since if
q(d_{n})>z^{\frac{1}{2}}>Q
then
n
is not
Q
-smooth. For Classes II and III, we can use the same bounds as in the non-smooth case. So we require only class IV, as the first three classes give

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\leq t\leq X+Y\\
t\equiv a\pmod{k}\end{subarray}}f(t)=\sum_{n\in IV}f(n)+O\left(\frac{Y}{kz^{\frac{1}{4}-\epsilon}}\right)

We consider first the case when
f\in M_{Q}
. Since we no longer have an unbounded
f(p)
, we could apply Lemma 4 of Shiu’s original paper
[
Sh
]
, which states that if
f\in M
then

(24)

(n,k)=1\\

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq z^{\frac{1}{2}}\\

(n,k)=1\\
n\in\mathcal{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{f(n)}{n}\ll\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{5}{4}\beta\right)r\log r+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq z\\

where the

Now, as we are dealing with Class IV, we note that since

. So if

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in IV}f(n)\leq

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{r_{2}\leq r\leq r_{0}}\left(\frac{Y}{\phi(k)\log z}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{5}{4}\beta\right)r\log r+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq Q\\

\displaystyle\left(\frac{Y}{\phi(k)\log z}\right)\exp\left(-\left(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\right)r_{2}\log r_{2}\right)\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq Q\\

p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}\right),

For the case of

. So again,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in IV}f(n)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r_{2}\leq r\leq r_{0}}A_{1}^{\frac{20r}{\alpha\kappa}}\left(\log\log x\right)^{\frac{20r}{41}}\left(\frac{Y(r+1)}{\phi(k)\log z}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z^{\frac{1}{2}}<c\leq z\\

p\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p}\right),

and

, we note as before that

for all

by the assumption in Theorem

As in Section 6 of Shiu’s original paper, we use this result to prove a statement about the divisor function. Here, we let

Theorem
4.1
.

Let
x
,
y
, and

be as in Theorem
2.1
. Choose an integer

\epsilon>0

n\equiv a\pmod{k}\end{subarray}}\tau_{d}(n)^{R}\ll\frac{x(\log x)^{\epsilon}}{\phi(k)}\left(\frac{\phi(k)}{k}\log x\right)^{d^{R}-1}.

Moreover,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq n\leq x+y\\
n\equiv a\pmod{k}\\
n\in\mathcal{S}(Q)\end{subarray}}\tau(n)^{R}\ll\rho(u)^{C_{2}+o(1)}\frac{x(\log x)^{\epsilon}}{\phi(k)}\left(\frac{\phi(k)}{k}\log x\right)^{d^{R}-1}.

Proof.

For any prime
p

So Condition 1 holds for
\beta

\tau_{2}(n)^{R}\leq n^{\frac{1.5379(\log\log\log x)}{g(x)\log\log n}}

we can inductively bound

Hence

\tau_{d}(n)^{R}\leq n^{\frac{1.5379(d-1)(\log\log\log x)}{g(x)\log\log n}}

The right-hand side is obviously an increasing function on

. So if

\leq(\log x)^{\frac{15.379(d-1)}{g(x)}},

for any

\leq n^{\frac{1.5379(d-1)}{g(x)}},

which is
\ll n^{\epsilon}

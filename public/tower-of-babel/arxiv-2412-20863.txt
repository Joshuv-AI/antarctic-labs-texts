Positivity in Weighted Flag Varieties
William Graham, Scott Joseph Larson

Abstract
. We study the torus-equivariant cohomology of weighted flag varieties, and prove a positivity property in the equivariant cohomology and Chow groups of weighted flag varieties, analogous to the non-weighted positivity proved in [ 23 ] . Our result strengthens and generalizes the positivity proved for weighted Grassmannians by Abe-Matsumura [ 1 ] . The positivity property is expressed in terms of weighted roots, which are used to describe weights of torus equivariant curves in weighted flag varieties. This provides a geometric interpretation of the parameters used in [ 1 ] . We approach weighted flag varieties from a uniform Lie-theoretic point of view, providing a more general definition than has appeared previously, and prove other general results about weighted flag varieties in this setting, including a Borel presentation of the equivariant cohomology. In addition, we generalize some results obtained for weighted Grassmannians or more generally type A A ( [ 1 ] , [ 6 ] ); in particular, we obtain a weighted Chevalley formula, descriptions of restrictions to fixed points, the GKM description of the cohomology, and a weighted Chevalley formula.



1.
Introduction

Weighted flag varieties are a generalization of flag varieties and weighted projective spaces, introduced by Grojnowski, Corti and Reid

[
13
]
.
Although they are not generally homogeneous varieties, they admit an action of a torus with isolated fixed points, and as with flag varieties,
their equivariant cohomology rings admit a Schubert basis.
Abe and Matsumura

[
1
]
proved that the equivariant cohomology
of weighted Grassmannians
has a positivity property analogous to the positivity for flag varieties proved in
[
23
]
. This positivity is expressed using certain parameters, and the
authors write that, in contrast to the situation, for non-weighted flag varieties, “we do not have the geometric or representation-theoretic interpretation of those
parameters”.
The main result of this paper extends the positivity statement
to all weighted flag varieties. The parameters in the statement
are weighted roots, which describe the weights
of torus-invariant curves through a torus fixed point in a weighted
flag variety. This formulation gives a geometric meaning to the
parameters in the positivity statement of
[
1
]
: in our
language, their parameters are negative roots at the longest element of
the Weyl group.

The approach taken in this paper is to study weighted flag varieties from a uniform Lie-theoretic point of view.
Besides
our main theorem, we prove some other results which we hope will be useful
in the future study of weighted flag varieties, including Chevalley-type formulas, and descriptions of restrictions to fixed points,
extending work of
[
1
]
for weighted Grassmannians,
and
[
6
]
for more general type
A
flag varieties. We also provide a Borel
presentation of the equivariant cohomology of weighted flag varieties, which appears to be new.
We define the
weighted Schubert classes as Poincaré dual classes to closures of weighted
Schubert cells. This provides a direct geometric interpretation
of the weighted Schubert classes. Our classes are positive integer multiples of the classes
considered in
[
1
]
and
[
6
]
, and
we determine these integers, which depend on the orders of certain stabilizer
groups. Our results are valid in the setting of equivariant Chow groups as well as equivariant
cohomology.

To state our main result we need some notation.

Let

.
If

.

Let

\Phi=\Phi({\mathfrak{g}})
denote the set of roots of
{\mathfrak{h}}
in

, with

, and let

denote the corresponding set of simple roots.
Let

, equipped with the Bruhat order.
Fix a dominant weight

, and let

be the parabolic subgroup containing

, such

contains

, and such that the coroots of the Lie algebra

of

are orthogonal to

. The Weyl group of

.
Let

denote the set
of maximal length coset representatives of

.
Let
\chi:\mathrm{\bf C}^{\times}\to H
be a cocharacter
of
H
, and let

denote the subgroup
\chi(\mathrm{\bf C}^{\times})
of
H
. Let

\mathbb{T}^{\ast}
denote the orthogonal complement of

in

.

For each

, define

.
We assume that each

). This assumption implies that

is not semisimple (see Proposition

).

The character

; let

be the corresponding

-dimensional representation.
Let

be the kernel in

of

; then

is the kernel in

of

. Since

, we can identify

with
the mixed space

.

We define the weighted flag variety as

. The relation with the definition in

[

is explained in Remark

is a projective algebraic variety with at worst

)). The elements of this basis

[
1
]
and

[

]
. We use these classes because of their

.

For each
w\in W^{P}
, we define a set

of characters

which we call weighted roots at
w
.
Each
w\in W^{P}
corresponds
to a
T
-fixed point
p_{w}\in X
, and the
T
-weights of the
T
-invariant
curves in
X
through

form a subset of
\Phi(w)
.
An explicit formula for the weighted roots at
w
is given in Proposition
3.21
.

Given
u,v,w\in W^{P}
, let

\mathcal{S}(u,v;w)=[w,u]\cap[w,v]
, where the notation denotes Bruhat intervals.
In other words,

\mathcal{S}(u,v;w)=\{x\in W^{P}\mid w\leq x\leq u\mbox{ and }w\leq x\leq v\}.

Our main result about positivity is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\lambda
is dominant and
\chi
is antidominant.
For every
u,v\in W^{P}
, write

(1.1)

\delta_{X_{u}}^{T}\delta_{X_{v}}^{T}=\sum_{w\in W^{P}}c_{uv}^{w}\delta_{X_{w}}^{T},

where
c_{uv}^{w}\in H_{T}^{\ast}=S(\mathbb{T}^{\ast})
.
Then each
c_{uv}^{w}
is a nonnegative linear combination of monomials of the form

\nu_{1}(x_{1})\cdots\nu_{k}(x_{k})
, where the
\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{k}
are
distinct negative roots, and each
x_{i}\in\mathcal{S}(u,v;w)
.

The method of proof of Theorem
1.1
also yields a positivity result about classes
of subvarieties of weighted flag varieties (Theorem
4.12
), which extends the corresponding result in the non-weighted case
(see
[
3
, Theorem 19.3.1]
,
[
23
, Theorem 3.2]
).

Theorem
1.1
implies that the coefficients
c_{uv}^{w}
are square-free
in a certain sense. But because weighted roots at different
x
may occur in the same monomial, this
is not the same as the usual meaning of square-free, which means with respect to a fixed set of generators.
Since a weighted root at
x\in W
can be expressed
as linear combinations of weighted roots
at any other
y\in W
, it is possible to expand
c_{uv}^{w}
as a sum of monomials in negative roots
at a single fixed
y
. However, such an expansion need not be square-free (see Remark
4.9
).

We say that
c_{uv}^{w}
is nonnegative at
y\in W^{P}
if
c_{uv}^{w}
is a nonnegative linear combination
of monomials in negative roots
at
y
. If
y\geq x
, then by Lemma
3.24
,
a negative simple root at
x
is a nonnegative linear combination
of negative simple roots at
y
.
Therefore, as a consequence of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain:

Corollary 1.2
.

With the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
, each
c_{uv}^{w}

is nonnegative at any
y
such that
y\geq x
for all
x\in\mathcal{S}(u,v;w)
. In particular, this holds
if
y\geq u
and
y\geq v
.

In the case of the weighted Grassmannian, this implies
the positivity result of Abe and Matsumura, since their parameters
are positive scalar multiples of the negative simple roots at the maximal element

w_{0}
of
W
.

It is natural to ask whether Theorem
1.1
can be strengthened to say that

c_{uv}^{w}
is nonnegative at
w
.
Example
5.3
shows that this is false for arbitrary antidominant
\chi
, but can hold for
certain antidominant
\chi
. In Example
5.3
,
this is true provided the entries of
\chi
satisfy certain inequalities. We refer to this example for further discussion.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on a striking product formula
(Lemma
4.2
)
proved using the
the non-weighted
Chevalley formula, together with the non-weighted positivity
result of
[
23
]
. The assumption
that
\lambda
is dominant (or antidominant) is used to show that the weighted
flag variety
X
exists as a separated scheme, and for positivity.
However, many of the results of the paper do not depend on
X
;
they can be formulated and proved in terms
of the space
Z
, without assuming either that
\lambda

is dominant or
\chi
is antidominant.
As observed in
[
1
]
, the assumption
\chi
is antidominant in the positivity statement can be removed by
using a Schubert basis with respect to a Borel subgroup with respect to which
\chi
is antidominant
(see Remark
4.11
).

2.
Equivariant cohomology and Chow groups of partial quotients

We work with schemes over
\mathrm{\bf C}
which can be equivariantly embedded in a smooth scheme. Throughout this paper, we work with (equivariant)
cohomology and Borel-Moore homology groups with coefficients in a field
\mathrm{\bf F}
of characteristic zero; the notation
H_{*}(X)
and
H_{*}^{G}(X)

denotes Borel-Moore homology groups (rather than singular homology
groups) with coefficients in
\mathrm{\bf F}
. As discussed in Section
2.2
, our results are also valid in the
setting of equivariant Chow groups (again with coefficients in
\mathrm{\bf F}
). Background on equivariant cohomology and Borel-Moore
homology can be found in
[
3
]
;
some basic properties of these theories are
also summarized in
[
23
]
. References
for the non-equivariant versions of these
theories are

[
18
, Appendix B]
and
the appendix to Chapter 13 of
[
14
]
.
Basic definitions and results for equivariant Chow groups can be found in
[
17
]
.
In this section, the notations
G
,
H
,
Z
, etc. are specific to this section; in particular we do not restrict to the case of weighted
flag varieties.
In subsequent sections, the notation has the meaning given in the introduction.

In this section,
Z
will denote a variety with an action of an algebraic torus
H
such that the restriction
of this action to a subtorus

S
is proper. Let
T=H/S
, and let

X=S\backslash Z
. We refer to
X
as a partial quotient, since it is a quotient by a subgroup; it has an action
of the quotient group
T
. Abe and Matsumura showed that there is a pullback isomorphism between
the equivariant cohomology groups
H^{*}_{T}(X)
and
H^{*}_{H}(Z)
(see
[
1
, Lemma 3.3]
).
In this paper we work with classes which are Poincaré dual to classes of fundamental classes of subvarieties.
For weighted flag varieties, these are scalar multiples of the classes
studied in
[
1
]
.

Section
2.1
contains some general results we use to identify these classes.
Proposition
2.7
relates pullbacks of Poincaré dual classes to orders of stabilizer groups;
the first part of this proposition gives an alternative proof of
[
1
, Lemma 3.3]
. In addition,
we relate these results to equivariant Borel-Moore homology, and show that the Poincaré duals of the closures of cells
of an orbifold paving form a basis for equivariant cohomology (Proposition
2.9
and Corollary
2.11
).

Section
2.2
proves versions of these results for equivariant Chow groups. Proposition
2.13

extends
[
17
, Theorem 3]
to partial quotients of torus actions. The proof of this proposition does not rely on

[
17
, Theorem 3]
, and in fact provides an alternative proof of that theorem in the special case of
torus actions. Proposition
2.15
extends
[
17
, Theorem 4]
to partial quotients
in the case of torus actions, and in addition gives a more precise relation between
the pullbacks in equivariant Chow cohomology and homology. The results of this section imply
that our results about weighted flag varieties hold in the setting of equivariant Chow groups.

2.1.
Equivariant cohomology, partial quotients, and Poincaré duality

We recall the definition of the Poincaré dual class of
a subvariety.
If
X
is irreducible and rationally smooth of dimension
n
,
there is a cap product isomorphism

\cap[X]:H^{i}(X)\to H_{2n-i}(X)
(
[
14
, Prop. 13.A.4]
); for completeness,
a proof
is given in the appendix to this paper.
If in addition
X
has an action of a linear algebraic
group
G
,
then the map

\cap[X]:H^{i}_{G}(X)\to H_{2n-i}^{G}(X)
is an isomorphism as well.
Given a closed subvariety
Y\subset X
, the Poincaré dual
class to
Y
is the class
\delta_{Y}\in H^{*}(X)
mapping
to
[Y]\in H_{*}(X)
under the cap product isomorphism. If
Y
is
G
-invariant,
\delta_{Y}^{G}
is the class
in
H^{*}_{G}(X)
mapping to
[Y]\in H_{*}^{G}(X)
. More generally, given any

C\in H_{*}(X)
(resp.
H^{G}_{*}(X)
), we can define
\delta_{C}\in H^{*}(X)
(resp.
\delta^{G}_{C}
) in the same way.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
\eta\,\colon Z\to Z^{\prime}
is a finite
morphism of rationally smooth irreducible algebraic varieties.
Suppose further that
\eta_{\ast}\,\colon H_{\ast}(Z)\to H_{\ast}(Z^{\prime})
is an isomorphism of vector spaces.
Then
\eta^{\ast}\,\colon H^{\ast}(Z^{\prime})\to H^{\ast}(Z)
satisfies

(2.1)

\eta^{\ast}(\delta_{C})=\dfrac{d_{Z}}{d_{C}}\delta_{Y},

where
Y^{\prime}\subset Z^{\prime}
is a subvariety,
C\in H_{\ast}(Z)
satisfies
\eta_{*}C=d_{C}[Y^{\prime}]
, and
\eta_{*}[Z]=d_{Z}[Z^{\prime}]
.

Proof.

We have

(2.2)

\eta_{\ast}(\eta^{\ast}(\delta_{Y^{\prime}})\cap[Z])=\delta_{Y^{\prime}}\cap\eta_{\ast}[Z]=\delta_{Y^{\prime}}\cap d_{Z}[Z^{\prime}]=d_{Z}[Y^{\prime}]=\eta_{*}(\frac{d_{Z}}{d_{C}}C).

Therefore, since
\eta_{*}
is an isomorphism,

(2.3)

\eta^{\ast}(\delta_{Y^{\prime}})\cap[Z]=\dfrac{d_{Z}}{d_{C}}C=\dfrac{d_{Z}}{d_{C}}\delta_{C}\cap[Z],

so
\eta^{\ast}(\delta_{Y^{\prime}})=\frac{d_{Z}}{d_{C}}\delta_{C}
, as claimed.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

In the setting of Lemma
2.1
, suppose that
C=[Y]
is the fundamental cycle of the scheme-theoretic inverse image

.
Then

is equidimensional, by

, so

In this case, we will write

.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
Z
be a variety with an action of a finite abelian group

, and
let

denote the stabilizer
in

of a general point of

is
|F/F^{Z}|
.

Proof.

The group

acts trivially on

, and
Z^{\prime}=(F/F^{Z})\backslash Z
.
Since there is an open subset of

.
∎

Suppose that

is an algebraic torus with character group

.
The equivariant cohomology of a point is denoted by

, and is isomorphic
to the symmetric algebra

, where

Suppose that

is a torus and

As above,

denotes a variety with an action of an algebraic torus

is a scheme.
Since

acts properly, the stabilizer group

of any

is finite. Given a subvariety

of

, there is an open subset of

Y
such that the stabilizer group

is the same for any point in this subset.
Let

for such a point

; in other words,

is the stabilizer

. Let

.

Let
\pi:Z\to X

There is a map

With notation as above, let

Then

There are only finitely

We claim that

.
Indeed, suppose

is an element of

Proposition 2.7
.

With notation as above, we have:

(1)
\pi^{*}:H^{i}_{T}(X)\to H^{i}_{H}(Z)
is an isomorphism.

(2) Suppose that

is rationally smooth and that

is an

S
-invariant subvariety of

. Let
\overline{Y}=S\backslash Y\subset X
. Then

(2.6)

\pi^{\ast}(\delta_{\overline{Y}}^{T})=\frac{e_{Y}}{e_{Z}}\delta_{Y}^{H}\in H^{\ast}_{H}(Z).

Proof.

Let
H^{\prime}=H/F
, and as in the previous lemma, let
Z^{\prime}=F\backslash Z
,
S^{\prime}=S/F
. Factor the map (
2.4
) as

(2.7)

E_{H}\times^{H}Z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}E_{H}\times^{H}Z^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\longrightarrow}}E_{H^{\prime}}\times^{H^{\prime}}Z^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h}}{{\longrightarrow}}E_{T}\times^{T}X.

These maps induce pullbacks

(2.8)

H^{*}_{T}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{*}_{H^{\prime}}(Z^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{*}_{H}(Z^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{*}_{H}(Z).

We will prove that each of
f^{*}
,
g^{*}
, and
h^{*}
are isomorphisms, so their composition

\pi^{*}=f^{*}\circ g^{*}\circ h^{*}
is as well.

Step 1.
The map

is the quotient map

E_{H}\times^{H}Z\longrightarrow E_{H}\times^{H}(F\backslash Z)\cong F\backslash(E_{H}\times^{H}Z),

where the
F
-action on
E_{H}\times^{H}Z
is defined as the action on the second factor
Z
.
Therefore, the pullback map gives an isomorphism
f^{*}:H^{*}_{H^{\prime}}(Z^{\prime})\to H^{*}_{H}(Z)^{F}
.
The induced action of
F
on
H^{*}(E_{H}\times^{H}Z)=H^{*}_{H}(Z)

is trivial. Indeed, the
F
-action is the
restriction of an action of the connected group

and the action
of a connected group induces a trivial action on cohomology
(as the map induced by any element of the group is homotopic to the identity map).
We conclude that
f^{*}:H^{*}_{H^{\prime}}(Z^{\prime})\to H^{*}_{H}(Z)
is an isomorphism.

Step 2.
We can factor the map
g
as follows. Let
E
be a model
for
E_{H}
, and
E^{\prime}
a model for
E_{H^{\prime}}
, Then
H
acts on
E^{\prime}
via the map

H\to H^{\prime}
, and
E\times E^{\prime}
, with the product action of
H
, is also a model for
E_{H}
.
Then
g
can be written as the composition

(2.9)

(E\times E^{\prime})\times^{H}Z^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}(E/F\times E^{\prime})\times^{H}Z^{\prime}=(E/F\times E^{\prime})\times^{H^{\prime}}Z^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle q}}{{\longrightarrow}}E^{\prime}\times^{H^{\prime}}Z^{\prime},

where the middle equality is because the subgroup
F
acts trivially on
(E/F\times E^{\prime})
and on
Z^{\prime}
. The map
q
is a fiber bundle
with fibers
E/F
. Since the cohomology of
E/F
vanishes in positive degrees,
q^{*}
is an isomorphism in cohomology. The map
p
is a quotient
map by
F
, and arguing as in Step 1 shows that

is an isomorphism. Hence

is an isomorphism.

Step 3.
As in Remark
2.4
, we may assume

and
E_{H^{\prime}}=E_{S^{\prime}}\times E_{T}
. Then

E_{H^{\prime}}\times^{H^{\prime}}Z^{\prime}=(E_{S^{\prime}}\times E_{T})\times^{S^{\prime}\times T}Z\cong E_{S^{\prime}}\times^{S^{\prime}}(E_{T}\times^{T}Z^{\prime}).

Also,

Therefore,

can be identified with the map

E_{S^{\prime}}\times^{S^{\prime}}(E_{T}\times^{T}Z^{\prime})\longrightarrow(E_{T}\times^{T}Z^{\prime})/S^{\prime}.

So

can be identified with the pullback
h^{*}:H^{*}((E_{T}\times^{T}Z^{\prime})/S^{\prime})\to H^{*}_{S^{\prime}}(E_{T}\times^{T}Z^{\prime})

Since

Combining the results of Steps 1-3, we see that

We now prove (2). Let
Y^{\prime}=F\backslash Y
, so
\overline{Y}=S\backslash Y=S^{\prime}\backslash Y^{\prime}

\pi^{*}(\delta_{\overline{Y}}^{T})=f^{*}\circ g^{*}\circ h^{*}(\delta_{\overline{Y}}^{T})

Since

is an
S^{\prime}
-principal bundle map, so it is smooth.
Under a smooth

Next, using the factorization (

) of the map

g^{*}\delta_{E_{H^{\prime}}\times^{H^{\prime}}Y^{\prime}}=p^{*}q^{*}\delta_{E^{\prime}\times^{H^{\prime}}Y^{\prime}}=p^{*}\delta_{(E/F\times E^{\prime})\times^{H}Y^{\prime}}=\delta_{(E\times E^{\prime})\times^{H}Y^{\prime}}.

Here, the second equality is because

Finally, the degree of the map

∎

2.2.

In this section, we work with Chow groups with coefficients in
\mathrm{\bf F}
, and
write
A_{*}(X)
to mean the Chow groups of
X
tensored with
\mathrm{\bf F}
.
If
G
is an algebraic
group acting on
M
, the equivariant Chow groups
are defined by
A_{i}^{G}(M)=A_{i+r}(E_{G}\times^{G}M)
. Here,

E_{G}
denotes a finite-dimensional model of the universal
space for
G
, chosen depending on the degree
i
, and
r=\dim E_{G}-\dim G
.
The space
E_{G}\times^{G}M
is referred to as a mixed space and the action of
G

on

as the mixing action.
Similarly, there are operational equivariant Chow groups
A^{i}_{G}(M)
, defined
in analogy to the operational Chow groups of
[
18
]
.
There
is a cycle map
\Cl:A_{i}^{G}(M)\to H_{2i}^{G}(M)
. We refer
to
[
17
]
for details. To simplify the exposition,
we will simply write
M_{G}=E_{G}\times^{G}M
and
A^{G}_{*}(M)=A_{*}(M_{G})
, although
to be
precise, we would need to choose a particular model of
E_{G}
, depending
on the degree of the Chow groups. We define
B_{G}=E_{G}/G
, and
then
A^{*}_{G}=A^{*}(B_{G})
. Since
B_{G}
is nonsingular, there is a cap product
isomorphism
\cap[B_{G}]:A^{*}(B_{G})\to A_{*}(B_{G})
. Since we are taking coefficients
in a field
\mathrm{\bf F}
of characteristic
0
, there is a natural isomorphism
A^{*}_{G}\cong H^{*}_{G}
.
The Chow groups
A^{G}_{*}(M)
are
A^{*}_{G}
-modules.

If
\alpha
is a character of
G
, then
\alpha
defines a line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}^{G}_{\alpha}=E_{G}\times^{G}\mathrm{\bf C}_{\alpha}
.
This line bundle pulls back to a line bundle

on the mixed space

, and the

is by cap product with the Chern class

Lemma 2.12
.

Suppose
\beta\in A^{*}_{G}=A^{*}(B_{G})
satisfies
\beta\cap[B_{G}]=\sum a_{i}[Q_{i}/G]
,
where
a_{i}\in\mathrm{\bf F}

are closed

-invariant subvarieties of

.

be a
G
-scheme and

-invariant subscheme.

\beta\cap[N]_{G}=\sum a_{i}[Q_{i}\times^{G}N]\in A_{*}(E_{G}\times^{G}M)=A^{G}_{*}(M).

Proof.

By the functorial properties of Chow groups, we may assume

.
The projection
p:E_{G}\times^{G}M\to B_{G}

is flat. By definition,

\displaystyle\beta\cap[M]_{G}

\displaystyle=(p^{*}\beta)\cap[E_{G}\times^{G}M]=(p^{*}\beta)\cap p^{*}(B_{G})

\displaystyle=p^{*}(\beta\cap[B_{G}])=p^{*}(\sum a_{i}[Q_{i}/G])=\sum a_{i}[Q_{i}\times^{G}M];

here we have used the compatibility between flat pullback and pullbacks of operational Chow groups.

Remark 2.14

Appendix: Poincaré duality

With respect to rational cohomology, rationally smooth varieties behave much like smooth varieties, and in particular

For reference, we provide a brief justification of Poincaré duality in this setting.
We recall facts about sheaves from

]
in the following discussion.
Given a complex of sheaves

denote the Borel-Moore dual complex of

.
For brevity, for any algebraic variety

the dual complex of the constant sheaf.
Hypercohomology gives

\mathrm{\bf H}^{-k}(\mathrm{\bf D}_{Q})=H_{k}(Q)
Borel-Moore homology.
A
\mathrm{\bf F}
-orientation for

is a chosen quasi-isomorphism

(2.22)

\mathrm{\bf D}_{U}\to{\mathrm{\bf F}}_{U}[2n]

where

is the smooth locus, and

is the complex dimension of

.
By
[
21
, §5.1]
there are unique morphisms

\mathrm{\bf F}_{Q}[2n]\to IC_{Q}\to\mathrm{\bf D}_{Q}

such that restrictions to

are isomorphisms – the composition giving an inverse of (
2.22
).
The second morphism in (

) is (up to shift)

of the first morphism, where Verdier duality gives

by
[
21
, §6.1]
.
Hypercohomology of (

H^{k}(Q)\to IH_{2n-k}(Q)\to H_{2n-k}(Q),

for every

.
The cap product with fundamental class of Borel-Moore homology factors through (

Assume that

is rationally smooth; i.e.,
{\mathrm{\bf F}}_{Q}[2n]=IC_{Q}
(cf.
[
9
, §1.4]
).
It follows that

, hence (

Given any rationally smooth variety

and subvariety

where

3.
Weighted flag varieties

The purpose of this section is to collect some basic results about the
geometry of weighted flag varieties. Most of the results of this section are not essentially
new; the basic geometry of weighted flag varieties was presented in
[
13
]
, and,
with some additional results, in
[
1
]
and
[
6
]
.
Unlike this previous work, we do not assume that the group we are using is the product of

it allows for a more uniform Lie-theoretic presentation, which turns out to be helpful in understanding
positivity and related results. The new results in this section are mainly contained in Section

. In particular,
the definition of weighted roots and the identification of the weights of

[
1
]
and
[

]
(see Remark

).

We refer to the introduction

3.2.
Projectivity of weighted flag varieties

In

]
, weighted flag varieties are constructed as closed subvarieties of weighted projective space,
so they are projective. Because our definition is slightly more general than the one used in
[

we give the proof of this result in our setting.

For any weight
\gamma

.
For
w\in W
, we set
a_{w}=a_{w\lambda}
. Observe that if
\lambda
is dominant and

As in the introduction, we assume that for every

(3.1)

This assumption implies that

we have:

Proposition 3.1
.

If there exists

such that

for each

is not semisimple (that is,
(m>n)

is not in the

We argue by contradiction. Suppose

with

\mathfrak{h}^{*}
by a nondegenerate

Choose

so that

is antidominant. If

and

,

Let

Remark 3.4
.

The argument in Remark

shows that if

Remark 3.5
.

Every

-equivariant line bundle on a flag variety has the same embedded principal
\mathrm{\bf C}^{\times}
-bundle
(defined as the complement of the

Hence weighted flag varieties are projective varieties when

is dominant or antidominant.

Generalized flag varieties are a special case of weighted flag varieties.
Indeed, by Proposition

,

is not in the span of the roots, so there exists a rank one subtorus

of the

such that

restricts nontrivially to

.
Define

to be an isomorphism of
\mathrm{\bf C}^{\times}

such that

pairs positively with the restriction of

to

If we consider

satisfies (

since

. Therefore the weighted flag variety

is the generalized flag variety

.
We refer to this as the non-weighted case. We fix

from now on; we

The weighted flag variety is covered by quotients of affine space by finite groups (see Section

-factorial

Remark 3.6
.

The construction of weighted flag varieties in
[

]
can be described in our notation as follows.
Let

, where
G^{\prime}
is reductive. Then
\lambda
is of the form
(\lambda^{\prime},u)
, where

\lambda^{\prime}
is dominant for

, and

. The cocharacter

is of the form
(\chi^{\prime},1)
, where

\chi^{\prime}
is a cocharacter of

corresponds to the identity cocharacter of
\mathrm{\bf C}^{*}

denote the

The

.

The following example shows that without the positivity of the

,

need not be separated (hence not projective).

Example 3.7
.

Let

, with
B
and

the subgroups of upper triangular and diagonal matrices, respectively.
Let
\lambda=1=\chi
, where we identify
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{\vee}
with

so that the dominant weights correspond to nonnegative integers.
Write

.
Then

and

; in particular, (

) is not satisfied.

is

and

is

acts properly on each

.

Let

3.3.
An open covering and stabilizers

In this section we describe a covering of a weighted flag variety by open sets
isomorphic to affine space modulo a finite group, as is done in
[
13
]
. We show that all stabilizers of
Z
in
S
are contained in a finite subgroup
\mathrm{\bf Z}_{a_{w}}
of
S
, depending on the neighborhood
U_{w}\subset Z
. In the next section we describe generic stabilizers for each weighted Schubert cell.

Recall that
P=LU
is the Levi decomposition of our parabolic subgroup such that
H\subset L
. Let
U^{-}
be the unipotent radical of the opposite parabolic
P^{-}
with respect to
H
.
Given

, let
{}^{w}U^{-}=wU^{-}w^{-1}
, and
let
h^{w}=w^{-1}hw
. The map

(3.4)

\varphi\,\colon{}^{w}U^{-}\to G/P,\quad wuw^{-1}\mapsto wuP/P,

takes

isomorphically onto its image, which we denote by

.
Let
\zeta\,\colon G\to G/P
denote the projection.
Then
\zeta^{-1}(C_{w})={}^{w}U^{-}\times wP\subset G
, where

{}^{w}U^{-}\times wP
is identified
with its image in
G
under the multiplication map. This image is invariant under left multiplication
by
H
, which corresponds to the action of
H
on

{}^{w}U^{-}\times wP
given by

(3.5)

h(wuw^{-1},wp)=(wh^{w}u(h^{w})^{-1}w^{-1},wh^{w}p).

Let

denote
\pi_{0}^{-1}({}^{w}U^{-})\subset Z
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Suppose
w\in W^{P}
. As
H
-varieties, we have

(3.6)

U_{w}\cong{}^{w}U^{-}\times\mathrm{\bf C}^{\times}_{w\lambda}.

Proof.

We have

(3.7)

U_{w}=\pi_{0}^{-1}({}^{w}U^{-})\cong({}^{w}U^{-}\times wP)\times^{P}\mathrm{\bf C}_{\lambda}^{\times}

The action of
H\subset G
on
U_{w}
corresponds by (
3.5
)
to the action of
H
on
({}^{w}U^{-}\times wP)\times^{P}\mathrm{\bf C}_{\lambda}^{\times}
given by

(3.8)

h[(wuw^{-1},wp),v]=[(wh^{w}u(h^{w})^{-1}w^{-1},wh^{w}p),v].

The isomorphism
({}^{w}U^{-}\times wP)\times^{P}\mathrm{\bf C}_{\lambda}^{\times}\to{}^{w}U^{-}\times\mathrm{\bf C}^{\times}_{w\lambda}
given by
[(wuw^{-1},wp),v]\mapsto(wuw^{-1},\lambda(p)v)

is
H
-equivariant. The lemma follows.
∎

Recall that

a_{\mu}=\gcd(\chi)^{-1}\mu\cdot\chi
for any weight
\mu\in\Lambda
, and
and
a_{w}=a_{w\lambda}
for
w\in W
. For

, write
s^{a_{\mu}}=\mu(s)
, and

s^{a_{w}}=s^{a_{w\lambda}}
. Under the identification of

with
\mathrm{\bf C}^{\times}
given by

\mathrm{\bf C}^{\times}\cong\mathrm{\bf C}^{\times}/\ker\chi\to S
, the map

is just the map raising

to the power

.
Let
\mathrm{\bf Z}_{a_{w}}
denote the finite group
\ker(w\lambda|_{S})
. Because the action of

S
on
Z
is proper,
S
acts on
Z
with finite stabilizers; this is also a consequence
of the following result, which gives more precise information.

Corollary 3.10
.

The stabilizer in
S
of any
z\in U_{w}
is contained in
\mathrm{\bf Z}_{a_{w}}
. Hence

S
acts on
Z
with finite stabilizers. Moreover,
for
w\in W^{P}
, the map
{}^{w}U^{-}\to S\backslash U_{w}
defined by

(3.9)

u\mapsto S(u,1)

induces an isomorphism

(3.10)

\mathrm{\bf Z}_{a_{w}}\backslash{}^{w}U^{-}\cong S\backslash U_{w}.

The

form an open cover of
X
by quotients of affine space by finite groups.
Hence

is rationally smooth.

Proof.

Lemma
3.9
implies that the stabilizer group

S^{z}
(for

) is contained in
\mathrm{\bf Z}_{a_{w}}
. This lemma also
implies that (
3.9
) induces the isomorphism (

).
Since the

form an open cover of

, their inverse images

in

form an open cover of

. Each

is isomorphic to affine space, and since

by quotients of affine space by finite groups.
∎

3.4.
Weighted Schubert varieties

Recall that
\pi:Z\to X
and
\pi_{0}:Z\to Y=G/P
denote the natural projections. Suppose

.
By definition, the Schubert cell

corresponding to

in

is the
B
-orbit

. The map

gives an isomorphism
of
{}^{w}U^{-}\cap U
onto this cell. Define

implies that

is the closure of

in

.

Proposition 3.11
.

The weighted Schubert cells
X_{w}^{0}
form a paving of
X
by affine spaces modulo finite groups. The equivariant cohomology

is a free
H^{*}_{T}
-module with basis

\delta_{X_{w}}^{T}
.

Proof.

The Schubert cells form a paving of
Y
, so their inverse images
Z_{w}^{0}
form a paving of
Z
.
As
\pi:Z\to X
is a geometric quotient, the quotient varieties
S\backslash Z_{w}^{0}=X_{w}^{0}

form a paving of
X
. Since
X_{w}^{0}=S\backslash Z_{w}^{0}\cong\mathrm{\bf Z}_{a_{w}}\backslash({}^{w}U^{-}\cap U)
,
each
X_{w}^{0}
is isomorphic to affine space modulo a finite group. Each

X^{w}_{0}
is
T
-acyclic, by Proposition
2.10
(cf. Remark
2.8
). Since

is rationally
smooth, the statement about cohomology follows from Corollary
2.11
.
∎

We now determine the generic stabilizers of
S
on
Z_{w}^{0}
.
The
support
of the
S
-action on
Z
, denoted by
\sigma(S)
,
is the set of all
s\in S
such that
sz=z
for some
z\in Z
.
We have

(3.11)

\sigma(S)=\bigcup_{w\in W}\mathrm{\bf Z}_{a_{w}}.

Indeed,
\sigma(S)\subset\bigcup_{w\in W}\mathrm{\bf Z}_{a_{w}}
by Corollary
3.10
;
the reverse inclusion holds since

S^{[w,1]}=\mathrm{\bf Z}_{a_{w}}
for all
w\in W
.

Let
w\in W^{P}
, and choose an ordering
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r}
of the roots in
\Phi_{w}^{P}=\Phi^{+}\cap w\Phi({\mathfrak{u}}^{-})
.
For each root
\beta
, the corresponding root subgroup
U_{\beta}
is isomorphic
to
\mathrm{\bf C}
. We have isomorphisms of algebraic varieties

\mathrm{\bf C}^{r}\cong\prod_{i}U_{\beta_{i}}\cong{}^{w}U^{-}\cap U
, where the second isomorphism
is given by the product map. If
u=\prod u_{\beta_{i}}
, we can view
the
u_{\beta_{i}}
as complex numbers giving the coordinates
of
u\in{}^{w}U^{-}\cap U
under the isomorphism

\mathrm{\bf C}^{r}\cong{}^{w}U^{-}\cap U
. Let
({}^{w}U^{-}\cap U)^{gen}
be the Zariski open
subset of
{}^{w}U^{-}\cap U
consisting of the elements
u
whose coordinates
u_{\beta_{i}}
are
all nonzero. Define the Zariski open subset
Z^{gen}_{w}
of
Z^{0}_{w}
by

Z^{gen}_{w}=({}^{w}U^{-}\cap U)^{gen{}}\cdot wP\times^{P}\mathrm{\bf C}^{\times}_{\lambda}\cong({}^{w}U^{-}\cap U)^{gen}\times\mathrm{\bf C}^{\times}_{w\lambda}.

For any subset
\mathcal{S}\subset\Lambda
, write
a_{\mathcal{S}}=\left\{a_{\mu}\mid\mu\in\mathcal{S}\right\}
.
Write
\gcd(a_{w},a_{\Phi_{w}^{P}})
for the greatest common divisor of the elements
of
\{a_{w}\}\cup a_{\Phi_{w}^{P}}
.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
w\in W^{P}
. For all
z
in
Z^{gen}_{w}
, we have

S^{z}=\mathrm{\bf Z}_{d}
, where
d=\gcd(a_{w},a_{\Phi_{w}^{P}})
.

Proof.

The point
z=(u,z)\in Z^{gen}_{w}
is fixed by
s\in S
if and only if

s^{a_{\beta_{i}}}=1
for all
i
, and
s^{a_{w}}=1
. This is equivalent to the
statement that
s^{d}=1
, where
d=\gcd(a_{w},a_{\Phi_{w}^{P}})
, which is equivalent
to the statement that
s\in\mathrm{\bf Z}_{d}
.
∎

The weight
\lambda
is said to be
minuscule

if every
\alpha\in\Phi
satisfies
|\lambda\cdot\alpha^{\vee}|\leq 1
.
Let
\leq_{P}
denote the restriction of the Bruhat order on
W
to
W^{P}
. The next proposition provides
an alternative description of the stabilizer groups if
\lambda
is minuscule.

Proposition 3.13
.

For every
w\in W^{P}
, we have

(3.12)

\gcd(a_{w},a_{\Phi_{w}^{P}})\ |\ \gcd(a_{x}\mid x\leq_{P}w).

If
\lambda
is minuscule, then (
3.12
) is an equality.

Proof.

The proposition is true for the minimal element
w_{0}^{P}
of
W^{P}
, since both sides
of (
3.12
) are equal to
a_{w_{0}^{P}}
.
Since
W^{P}
is connected by covering relations (see Section
3.1
),
it suffices to show that for every

such that
\ell(x)=\ell(w)-1
, we have

(3.13)

\gcd(a_{w},a_{\Phi_{w}^{P}})\ |\ \gcd(a_{x},a_{\Phi_{x}^{P}}).

Indeed, by induction on
\ell(w)
,
\gcd(a_{x},a_{\Phi_{x}^{P}})
will divide
\gcd(a_{y}\mid y\leq_{P}x)
, so it will follow that
\gcd(a_{w},a_{\Phi_{w}^{P}})
divides

\gcd(\gcd(a_{y}\mid y\leq_{P}x_{1}),\ldots,\gcd(a_{y}\mid y\leq_{P}x_{d}),a_{w})=\gcd(a_{x}\mid x\leq_{P}w),

where

index all of the divisors in

.

Let

such that

, where

. We claim that

. It suffices to show
that

, we have

We now prove (

).
Suppose that

divides

and

for each

.

and
a_{\gamma}
for each

.
Since

, we have

.
Since

,

divides

. The set of weights of

is equal to
\Phi_{x}^{P}
, and

, the set of weights of

is equal to

.
It follows that
\Phi_{x}^{P}\subset r_{\alpha}\Phi_{w}^{P}
, since
Y_{x}\subset Y_{w}
.
Therefore, if
\gamma\in\Phi_{x}^{P}
,

is a linear combination of

and

for some
\beta\in\Phi_{w}^{P}
, so

divides
a_{\gamma}
.
This proves (
3.13
).

Now suppose that
\lambda
is minuscule. To show that (
3.12
) is an equality, we must show that if

for all

, then

for all
\beta\in\Phi_{w}^{P}
. As above, let

be such that

, where

, and

. We have

divides

and

\Phi_{w}^{P}=r_{\alpha}\Phi_{x}^{P}\cup\{\alpha\}

H^{*}_{T_{0}}
, is the problem of non-weighted
Schubert calculus. Expanding products in
H^{*}_{H}(Z)
in terms
of the basis
q_{w}\delta^{H}_{Z_{w}}
, with
coefficients in
H^{*}_{T}
, is the problem of weighted Schubert calculus. The significant difference is that
the coefficients are in different rings:
H^{*}_{T_{0}}
in the non-weighted case;
H^{*}_{T}
in the weighted case.

There are various ways of presenting formulas for the structure constants.
Depending on which basis one uses, factors of
q_{w}
(which can be computed by (
3.14
))
may or may not occur in the formulas, but
these are easily dealt with. Precisely,
if we write

\delta^{T}_{X_{u}}\delta^{T}_{X_{v}}=\sum_{w}c_{uv}^{w}\delta^{T}_{X_{w}}

and

\delta^{H}_{Z_{u}}\delta^{H}_{Z_{v}}=\sum_{w}d_{uv}^{w}\delta^{H}_{Z_{w}},

where
c_{uv}^{w}
and
d_{uv}^{w}
are in
H^{*}_{T}
, we see that

(4.1)

c_{uv}^{w}=\frac{q_{u}q_{v}}{q_{w}}d_{uv}^{w}.

Thus, we can easily obtain structure constants in one basis from structure constants in the other basis.
In particular, we can obtain formulas in the basis
\{\delta^{H}_{Z_{w}}\}
from formulas in
the basis
\{\delta^{T}_{X_{w}}\}
by setting all the
q_{u}
equal to
1
.
For convenience, we will often use the basis
\{\delta^{H}_{Z_{w}}\}
.
Similarly, we have introduced elements
\overline{\beta}(w)
(
3.17
), which are related to the weighted roots
\beta(w)
by a positive
scalar factor (see
(
3.18
)). This equation can be used to translate formulas from the
\overline{\beta}(w)
to the
\beta(w)
, and conversely.
Although the weighted roots are geometrically natural, we often use the
\overline{\beta}(w)
for simplicity.

Finally, the formula of (
3.23
) can be used to translate formulas given in terms of
\overline{\beta}(v)
into
\overline{\beta}(w)

for
v,w\in W
. In particular, taking
w=w_{0}
, we can obtain formulas in the parameters used in
[
1
]
for the weighted Grassmannian.

4.2.
Borel presentation

Since
H^{*}_{T}(X)\cong H^{*}_{H}(Z)
, the next proposition gives a Borel presentation of the

T
-equivariant cohomology of a weighted flag variety. Although the proof is simple, the result does not previously seem to have appeared.
Recall that
\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}=\ker\lambda\subset\mathbb{H}
; the dual space is
\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{\ast}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

We have

(4.2)

H_{H}^{\ast}(Z)\cong S(\mathbb{H}^{\ast})\underset{S(\mathbb{H}^{\ast})^{W}}{\otimes}S(\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{\ast})^{W_{P}}.

Proof.

Recall that
Q=L_{\lambda}U\subset P
, where
L_{\lambda}
is the kernel of the character of

\lambda
of
L
. Since
Z\cong G/Q
, applying
[
3
, Ch. 15, Prop. 6.5]
yields

H^{*}_{H}(Z)\cong H^{*}_{H}(G/Q)\cong H^{*}_{H}\otimes_{H^{*}_{G}}H^{*}_{Q}\cong S(\mathbb{H}^{\ast})\underset{S(\mathbb{H}^{\ast})^{W}}{\otimes}S(\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{\ast})^{W_{P}},

as desired.
∎

In terms of the Borel presentation, the left action of
r\in H^{*}_{H}=S(\mathbb{H}^{*})
on
H^{*}_{H}(Z)
is given
by multiplication by
r\otimes 1
. The right action of
S(\mathbb{H}^{\ast}_{\lambda})^{W_{P}}

on
H^{*}_{H}(Z)
can be
described as follows. A representation
V
of
H_{\lambda}
may be viewed as an
H_{\lambda}
-equivariant
vector bundle over a point. The
H_{\lambda}
-equivariant Chern class
c_{i}^{H_{\lambda}}(V)
is an
element of
S(\mathbb{H}^{\ast}_{\lambda})
. If
V
is the restriction to
H_{\lambda}
of a representation
of
L_{\lambda}
, then
c_{i}^{H}(V)
is in
S(\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{\ast})^{W_{P}}
. The
element
1\otimes c_{i}^{H}(V)\in H^{*}_{H}(Z)
is the
i
-th equivariant Chern class of the

H
-equivariant line bundle
G\times^{Q}V
, where as usual the
L_{\lambda}
-module structure
on
V
is extended to a
Q=L_{\lambda}U
-module structure by defining
U
to act trivially.

Given
\mu\in\mathbb{H}^{*}
, let
\tilde{\mu}
denote the image of
\mu
under the map

\mathbb{H}^{*}\to\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{*}
. Suppose
\mu\in\Lambda
. Then
\tilde{\mu}
is
W_{P}
-invariant
if and only if
\mu
is. In this case, we obtain
1
-dimensional representations

\mathrm{\bf C}_{\tilde{\mu}}
of
L_{\lambda}
and
\mathrm{\bf C}_{\mu}
of
L
.
As usual, we extend these representations to
Q
and
P
, respectively, by defining
U
to act trivially.
The line bundle
G\times^{Q}\mathrm{\bf C}_{\tilde{\mu}}
on

Z=G/Q
is the pullback via
\pi_{0}^{*}
of the line bundle

G\times^{P}\mathrm{\bf C}_{\mu}
. In terms of the Borel presentation,

c_{1}^{H}(G\times^{Q}\mathrm{\bf C}_{\tilde{\mu}})=1\otimes\tilde{\mu}
.
Note that

\tilde{\lambda}=0
, reflecting the fact that the pullback of
G\times^{P}\mathrm{\bf C}_{\lambda}

is
H
-equivariantly trivial on
Z
.

4.3.
Module structure and a Chevalley formula

As is apparent from the Borel presentation,
H^{*}_{T}(X)\cong H^{*}_{H}(Z)
has the structure of a
S(\mathbb{H}^{*})\otimes S(\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{*})
-module. In this section we describe the module structure in terms of the weighted Schubert basis
(Proposition
4.3
). As a consequence, we obtain a Chevalley formula for the multiplication of a weighted Schubert class in terms of a weighted Schubert divisor (Proposition
4.4
). For weighted flag varieties of cominuscule type, the formula can be simplified; see
Corollary
4.6
, which in the case of the weighted Grassmannian recovers the formula of
[
1
, Prop. 5.2]

The proofs of these propositions are deduced from results for non-weighted flag varieties, as is Lemma
4.2
, which
also plays a key role
in the proof of our positivity theorem.

Via the pullback isomorphisms
\pi^{*}:H^{*}_{T}(X)\to H^{*}_{H}(Z)
and
\pi_{0}^{*}:H^{*}_{T_{0}}(Y)\to H^{*}_{H}(Z)
, the ring

H^{*}_{H}(Z)
acquires the structure of a module for
H^{*}_{T}
and for
H^{*}_{T_{0}}
.
Lemma
4.2
illustrates
that although the Schubert classes
form a basis of
H^{*}_{H}(Z)
as a module over
H^{*}_{T}
or over
H^{*}_{T_{0}}
, they
are not linearly independent over
H^{*}_{H}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
v\in W^{P}
, then

(4.3)

(v\lambda)\,\delta_{Z_{v}}^{H}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w=vr_{\gamma}\\
w{\lessdot}_{P}v\end{subarray}}(\lambda\cdot\gamma^{\vee})\,\delta_{Z_{w}}^{H},

where the sum is over positive roots
\gamma
such that
vr_{\gamma}\lessdot_{P}v
.

Proof.

For
\mu\in\mathbb{H}^{*}
, we have, using
H
-equivariant classes on
Y
(cf. Remark
2.5
):

(4.4)

c_{1}^{H}(G\times^{P}\mathrm{\bf C}_{\mu})\delta_{Y_{v}}^{H}=v\mu\,\delta_{Y_{v}}^{H}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w=vr_{\gamma}\\
w{\lessdot}_{P}v\end{subarray}}(\mu\cdot\gamma^{\vee})\delta_{Y_{w}}^{H},

where again the sum is over positive
\gamma
. See, for example,
[
10
]
or
[
3
]
. (In accord with our convention that
{\mathfrak{b}}
contains the positive root spaces, the sign in front of the sum is negative: the line bundle
G\times^{P}\mathrm{\bf C}_{\mu}
is positive if
\mu
is antidominant.) Setting
\mu=\lambda
and
pulling back along
\pi_{0}\,\colon Z\to Y
gives (
4.3
), since
c_{1}(G\times^{Q}\mathrm{\bf C}_{\tilde{\lambda}})=0
, where
\tilde{\lambda}
is the restriction of
\lambda
to
Q
.
∎

The ring
H^{*}_{T}(X)
is a free
H^{*}_{T}
-module with a basis given by
the
\delta_{X_{w}}^{T}
. For
\mu\in\mathbb{H}^{*}
, the next proposition gives expansions for

\mu\cdot\delta_{X_{v}}^{T}
and
(1\otimes\mu)\cdot\delta_{X_{v}}^{T}
in this basis,
with coefficients in
H^{*}_{T}=S(\mathbb{T}^{*})
. Recall
that for
\eta\in\mathbb{H}^{*}
and
w\in W
, the element

\overline{\eta}(w)=\eta-\frac{a_{\eta}}{a_{w}}w\lambda

defined in (
3.17
) lies in the subspace
\mathbb{T}^{*}
of
\mathbb{H}^{*}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

For every
\mu\in\mathbb{H}^{\ast}
and for every
v\in W^{P}
, we have

(4.5)

\mu\,\delta_{X_{v}}^{T}=\overline{\mu}(v)\delta_{X_{v}}^{T}+\dfrac{a_{\mu}}{a_{v}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w=vr_{\gamma}\\
w{\lessdot}_{P}v\end{subarray}}\dfrac{q_{v}}{q_{w}}(\lambda\cdot\gamma^{\vee})\,\delta_{X_{w}}^{T},

and

(4.6)

(1\otimes\tilde{\mu})\delta_{X_{v}}^{T}=\overline{(v\mu)}(v)\delta_{X_{v}}^{T}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w=vr_{\gamma}\\
w{\lessdot}_{P}v\end{subarray}}\frac{q_{v}}{q_{w}}\left((\dfrac{a_{v\mu}}{a_{v}}\lambda-\mu)\cdot\gamma^{\vee}\right)\delta_{X_{w}}^{T}.

In both formulas, the sum is over positive roots
\gamma
such that
vr_{\gamma}{\lessdot}_{P}v
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2

and the relation
\pi^{\ast}\delta_{X_{w}}^{T}=q_{w}\delta_{Z_{w}}^{H}
, we have

(4.7)

\begin{split}\mu\,\delta_{X_{v}}^{T}&=(\mu-\dfrac{a_{\mu}}{a_{v}}v\lambda)\delta_{X_{v}}^{T}+\dfrac{a_{\mu}}{a_{v}}v\lambda\,\delta_{X_{v}}^{T}\\
&=\overline{\mu}(v)\delta_{X_{v}}^{T}+\dfrac{a_{\mu}}{a_{v}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w=vr_{\gamma}\\
w{\lessdot}_{P}v\end{subarray}}\dfrac{q_{v}}{q_{w}}(\lambda\cdot\gamma^{\vee})\,\delta_{X_{w}}^{T},\end{split}

proving (
4.5
).

We now prove
(
4.6
). By the non-weighted Chevalley formula (
4.4
), we have

(4.8)

\begin{split}(1\otimes\tilde{\mu})\delta_{Z_{v}}^{H}&=v\mu\delta_{Z_{v}}^{H}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w=vr_{\gamma}\\
w{\lessdot}_{P}v\end{subarray}}(\mu\cdot\gamma^{\vee})\delta_{Z_{w}}^{H}\\
&=\left(v\mu-\dfrac{a_{v\mu}}{a_{v}}v\lambda\right)\delta_{Z_{v}}^{H}+\dfrac{a_{v\mu}}{a_{v}}v\lambda\,\delta_{Z_{v}}^{H}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w=vr_{\gamma}\\
w{\lessdot}_{P}v\end{subarray}}(\mu\cdot\gamma^{\vee})\delta_{Z_{w}}^{H}.\end{split}

Applying Lemma
4.2
to the middle term and simplifying, we obtain

(1\otimes\tilde{\mu})\delta_{Z_{v}}^{H}=\overline{(v\mu)}(v)\delta_{Z_{v}}^{H}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w=vr_{\gamma}\end{subarray}}\left((\dfrac{a_{v\mu}}{a_{v}}\lambda-\mu)\cdot\gamma^{\vee}\right)\delta_{Z_{w}}^{H}.

Equation (
4.6
) follows from this formula by again using
\pi^{\ast}\delta_{X_{w}}^{T}=q_{w}\delta_{Z_{w}}^{H}
(and similarly with
v
in place of
w
).
∎

Note that if
\mu=\lambda
, then both sides of (
4.6
) are zero.

The next proposition gives a formula for the product of a weighted
Schubert divisor with a weighted Schubert class. In this formula and elsewhere, we can take

\omega_{\alpha}
to be any element of
\mathbb{H}^{*}
satisfying

(4.9)

\omega_{\alpha}\cdot\beta^{\vee}=\delta_{\alpha,\beta}

for positive simple roots
\alpha,\beta
. This condition does not uniquely determine
\omega_{\alpha}
since

G
is not semisimple. For example, if
G=\mathrm{\bf C}^{*}\times G_{1}
,
then
\omega_{\alpha}
could be any element of the form
x_{0}+\omega_{1,\alpha}
, where

\omega_{1,\alpha}
is defined in terms of
G_{1}
, and
x_{0}
is a character of the
first factor
\mathrm{\bf C}^{*}
. (This occurs in the examples in
Sections
5.2
and
5.3
.) However,
if
\omega_{\alpha}^{\prime}
were another
weight satisfying (
4.9
), then
\omega_{\alpha}-\omega_{\alpha}^{\prime}
is
W
-invariant, so

w_{0}\omega_{\alpha}-v\omega_{\alpha}=w_{0}\omega^{\prime}_{\alpha}-v\omega^{\prime}_{\alpha}
. Thus, the formula
of Proposition
4.4
is unchanged.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\alpha
be a simple root with
r_{\alpha}\not\in W_{P}
, and let
u_{\alpha}=w_{0}r_{\alpha}\in W^{P}
.
For every
v\in W^{P}
, we have

(4.10)

\delta_{X_{u_{\alpha}}}^{T}\,\delta_{X_{v}}^{T}=q_{u_{\alpha}}\overline{(w_{0}\omega_{\alpha}-v\omega_{\alpha})}(v)\delta_{X_{v}}^{T}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w=vr_{\gamma}\\
w{\lessdot}_{P}v\end{subarray}}\dfrac{q_{u_{\alpha}}q_{v}}{q_{w}}\left((\dfrac{a_{w_{0}\omega_{\alpha}-v\omega_{\alpha}}}{a_{v}}\lambda+\omega_{\alpha})\cdot\gamma^{\vee}\right)\,\delta_{X_{w}}^{T},

where the sum is over positive roots
\gamma
such that
vr_{\gamma}{\lessdot}_{P}v
.

Proof.

Since
\delta_{Z_{u_{\alpha}}}^{H}=\pi_{0,H}^{*}\delta_{Y_{u_{\alpha}}}^{H}
, applying
\pi_{0,H}^{*}
to the formula
of
[
3
, §16 Lemma 2.6]
yields

\delta_{Z_{u_{\alpha}}}^{H}=(w_{0}\omega_{\alpha}\otimes 1)-(1\otimes\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha})
,
where
\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha}
is the restriction to
\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{\ast}
of
\omega_{\alpha}
. Note that the right hand side
of this formula does not depend on the choice of
\omega_{\alpha}
satisfying (
4.9
).
Therefore, since
\pi^{*}\delta_{X_{u_{\alpha}}}^{T}=q_{u_{\alpha}}\delta_{Z_{u_{\alpha}}}^{H}
, we have

\delta_{X_{u_{\alpha}}}^{T}=q_{u_{\alpha}}((w_{0}\omega_{\alpha}\otimes 1)-(1\otimes\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha})).

Hence

(4.11)

\begin{split}\delta_{X_{u_{\alpha}}}^{T}\,\delta_{X_{v}}^{T}&=q_{u_{\alpha}}((w_{0}\omega_{\alpha}\otimes 1)-(1\otimes\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha}))\delta_{X_{v}}^{T}\\
&=q_{u_{\alpha}}\overline{w_{0}\omega_{\alpha}}(v)\delta_{X_{v}}^{T}+\dfrac{a_{w_{0}\omega_{\alpha}}}{a_{v}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w=vr_{\gamma}\\
w{\lessdot}_{P}v\end{subarray}}\frac{q_{u_{\alpha}}q_{v}}{q_{w}}(\lambda\cdot\gamma^{\vee})\delta_{X_{w}}^{T}\\
&\hskip 14.45377pt-q_{u_{\alpha}}\overline{v\omega_{\alpha}}(v)\delta_{X_{v}}^{T}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w=vr_{\gamma}\\
w{\lessdot}_{P}v\end{subarray}}\frac{q_{u_{\alpha}}q_{v}}{q_{w}}(\dfrac{a_{v\omega_{\alpha}}}{a_{v}}\lambda-\omega_{\alpha})\cdot\gamma^{\vee})\delta_{X_{w}}^{T}.\end{split}

This implies (
4.10
).
∎

Remark 4.5
.

In the special case
u=u_{\alpha}
, Proposition
4.4

implies directly that
the
c_{uv}^{w}
are nonnegative at

. Equation (
4.10
)
implies that the structure constants

can be nonzero only for

(in which case the structure constant is a scalar), or for

.
The nonnegativity of the scalar

can be deduced as follows. We have

, where the
\beta_{i}
are the negative

b_{i}\geq 0
. Hence, since
\chi
is antidominant,

a_{w_{0}\omega_{\alpha}-v\omega_{\alpha}}\geq 0
. Also,
a_{v}>0

\gamma^{\vee}
is a positive coroot, both

and

) imply that
c_{u_{\alpha}v}^{vr_{\gamma}}\geq 0
.
The

are nonnegative at

\overline{(w_{0}\omega_{\alpha}-v\omega_{\alpha})}(v)=\sum b_{i}\overline{\beta_{i}}(v)
.

Sections
5.2
(on the weighted Grassmannian, considered by
[

]
)
and
5.3
consider examples of weighted flag

\lambda=\omega_{\alpha}
(that is,

satisfies the condition (

)).
In this case,

Corollary 4.6
.

Keep the hypotheses of Proposition
4.4
, and assume
\lambda=\omega_{\alpha}
.
Then

\delta_{X_{u_{\alpha}}}^{T}\,\delta_{X_{v}}^{T}=q_{u_{\alpha}}\overline{w_{0}\omega_{\alpha}}(v)\delta_{X_{v}}^{T}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w=vr_{\gamma}\\
w{\lessdot}_{P}v\end{subarray}}\dfrac{q_{u_{\alpha}}q_{v}}{q_{w}}\dfrac{a_{w_{0}}}{a_{v}}(\omega_{\alpha}\cdot\gamma^{\vee})\,\delta_{X_{w}}^{T}.

Proof.

Our hypothesis on

implies that

\overline{(w_{0}\omega_{\alpha}-v\omega_{\alpha})}(v)=\overline{(w_{0}\lambda-v\lambda)}(v)=\overline{w_{0}\lambda}(v)=\overline{w_{0}\omega_{\alpha}}(v).

Also,
\lambda\cdot\gamma^{\vee}=\omega_{\alpha}\cdot\gamma^{\vee}
, and

\dfrac{a_{w_{0}\omega_{\alpha}-v\omega_{\alpha}}}{a_{v}}=\dfrac{a_{w_{0}\lambda-v\lambda}}{a_{v}}=\dfrac{a_{w_{0}}-a_{v}}{a_{v}}=\dfrac{a_{w_{0}}}{a_{v}}-1.

and Proposition

.

In the case of the weighted Grassmannian, this corollary appears as
[

]
).

Remark 4.7
.

In

4.4.
Positivity

In this section we prove our general positivity theorem.
We first record a lemma about the (non-weighted) equivariant structure
constants. The first statement follows from the type of support argument in
[
3
, Section 19.1]
), and it
seems likely that the second statement is known as well, but for lack of reference,
we provide a proof.

Lemma 4.8
.

Suppose
\delta_{Y_{u}}^{T_{0}}\delta_{Y_{v}}^{T_{0}}=\sum_{w\in W^{P}}b_{uv}^{w}\delta_{Y_{w}}^{T_{0}}
, with

b_{uv}^{w}\in H^{*}_{T_{0}}
. If
b_{uv}^{w}\neq 0
, then
w\leq u
and
w\leq v
. Conversely,
if
w
is a maximal element among the elements in
W^{P}
that are less than or equal to both
u
and
v
,
then
b_{uv}^{w}\neq 0
.

Proof.

Let
x\in W^{P}
. Then
i_{x}^{*}\delta^{T_{0}}_{Y_{w}}
is nonzero if and only if
x\leq w
. Indeed, if
x\not\leq w
, then
xP_{0}\not\in Y_{w}

so
i_{x}^{*}\delta^{T_{0}}_{Y_{w}}=0
; conversely, if
x\leq w
, then
xP_{0}\in Y_{w}
and there is an explicit formula for the pullback

i_{x}^{*}\delta^{T_{0}}_{Y_{w}}
showing that it is nonzero. (The formula is due to Anderson-Jantzen-Soergel
[
2
]
and Billey

[
7
]
; for a discussion of conventions see
[
22
, Appendix B]
.) Hence,
i_{x}^{*}(\delta_{Y_{u}}^{T_{0}}\delta_{Y_{v}}^{T_{0}})

is nonzero if and only if
x\leq u
and
x\leq v
.

Suppose that
b_{uv}^{w}\neq 0
. We wish to show that
w\leq u
and
w\leq v
. We may assume that

w\in W^{P}
is a maximal element among the
y\in W^{P}
with
b_{uv}^{y}\neq 0
. In this case,

i_{w}^{*}(\delta_{Y_{u}}^{T_{0}}\delta_{Y_{v}}^{T_{0}})=i_{w}^{*}(\sum_{y\in W^{P}}b_{uv}^{y}\delta_{Y_{w}}^{T_{0}})=b_{uv}^{w}i_{w}^{*}\delta_{Y_{w}}^{T_{0}}.

Since the right hand side is nonzero, so is the left hand side, so
w\leq u
and
w\leq v
.

Conversely,
if
w
is maximal among the elements in
W^{P}
that are less than or equal to both
u
and
v
,
then

i_{w}^{*}(\sum_{y\in W^{P}}b_{uv}^{y}\delta_{Y_{w}}^{T_{0}})=b_{uv}^{w}i_{w}^{*}\delta_{Y_{w}}^{T_{0}}=i_{w}^{*}(\delta_{Y_{u}}^{T_{0}}\delta_{Y_{v}}^{T_{0}})\neq 0,

where the first equality follows from the preceding paragraph.
We conclude that
b_{uv}^{w}\neq 0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By the discussion in Section
4.1
, it suffices to consider structure constants in terms of
the elements
\delta_{Z_{u}}^{H}
and to work with the
\overline{\beta}_{i}(x)
since these are positive
multiples of the weighted roots
\beta_{i}(x)
. Therefore, it suffices to show that

(4.12)

\delta_{Z_{u}}^{H}\delta_{Z_{v}}^{H}=\sum_{w\in W^{P}}d_{uv}^{w}\delta_{Z_{w}}^{H},

such that each
d_{uv}^{w}
is a nonnegative linear combination of
\overline{\nu}_{1}(x_{1})\cdots\overline{\nu}_{k}(x_{k})
,
where the
\nu_{i}
are distinct negative roots, and
x_{i}\in\mathcal{S}(u,v;w)
.

By positivity for the non-weighted structure constants, we can write

(4.13)

\delta_{Z_{u}}^{H}\delta_{Z_{v}}^{H}=\sum_{w\in W^{P}}\sum_{I}b(u,v,w;I)\beta_{I}\delta_{Z_{w}}^{H},

where
I
is a set of negative roots (not necessarily simple),
\beta_{I}
is the product of the roots in
I
(in particular,
square-free), and
b(u,v,w;I)
is a nonnegative integer. See
[
23
]
and

[
3
, Cor. 19.4.7]
.
By Lemma
4.8
, the only nonzero terms in the sum occur for
w
such that
w\leq u
and
w\leq v
.

To prove the theorem, it suffices to prove the following assertion: For all
w\in W^{P}
, we have

\beta_{I}\delta_{Z_{w}}^{H}=\sum_{y\leq_{P}w}f_{y,I}\delta_{Z_{y}}^{H}
, where
each
f_{y,I}
is a nonnegative linear combination of monomials

\overline{\nu}_{1}(x_{1})\cdots\overline{\nu}_{k}(x_{k})
, where the
\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{k}
are
distinct elements of
I
, and
x_{i}\in\mathcal{S}(u,v;y)
. We prove the assertion by induction on
|I|
.
If
|I|=1
, then
\beta_{I}=\beta

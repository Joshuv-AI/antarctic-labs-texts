Hybrid modeling approach for precise estimation of energy production and consumption based on temperature variations
Authors: Wulfran Fendzi Mbasso, Reagan Jean Jacques Molu, Ambe Harrison, Mukesh Pushkarna, Fritz Nguemo Kemdoum, Emmanuel Fendzi Donfack, Pradeep Jangir, Pierre Tiako, Milkias Berhanu Tuka
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This study introduces an advanced mathematical methodology for predicting energy generation and consumption based on temperature variations in regions with diverse climatic conditions and increasing energy demands. Using a comprehensive dataset of monthly energy production, consumption, and temperature readings spanning ten years (2010–2020), we applied polynomial, sinusoidal, and hybrid modeling techniques to capture the non-linear and cyclical relationships between temperature and energy metrics. The hybrid model, which combines sinusoidal and polynomial functions, achieved an accuracy of 79.15% in estimating energy consumption using temperature as a predictor variable. This model effectively captures the seasonal and non-linear consumption patterns, demonstrating a significant improvement over conventional models. In contrast, the polynomial model for energy production, while yielding partial accuracy (R² = 0.65), highlights the need for more advanced techniques to fully capture the temperature-dependent nature of energy production. The results indicate that temperature variations significantly affect energy consumption, with higher temperatures driving increased energy demand for cooling, while lower temperatures affect production efficiency, particularly in systems like hydropower. These findings underscore the necessity for integrating sophisticated models into energy planning to ensure resilience in energy systems amidst climate variability. The study offers critical insights for policymakers to optimize energy generation and distribution in response to changing climatic conditions.

Introduction

Background of study

Energy production and consumption play a crucial role in driving economic growth and development, especially in areas undergoing rapid urbanization and industrial expansion. Cameroon, a nation located in Central Africa characterized by a varied geographical terrain and a population surpassing 27 million, is experiencing a growing need for dependable energy sources1. The rise in energy demand can be attributed to various factors including economic expansion, urban development, and enhancements in quality of life2. Simultaneously, the nation is undergoing substantial climatic variability, characterized by oscillating temperatures and unpredictable weather patterns, which directly impact energy consumption patterns and production efficiency.

The correlation between temperature fluctuations and energy systems is intricate and diverse. Thermal variations can have a substantial impact on both the usage and generation of energy. For example, elevated temperatures generally result in an upswing in the need for cooling systems like air conditioning, which in turn leads to an increase in electricity consumption3. Conversely, decreased temperatures increase the need for heating, which has an impact on energy usage in residential and industrial settings. Furthermore, fluctuations in temperature can have a significant impact on the efficiency of energy production systems, especially those that rely on thermal processes4,5. In this country, where hydropower plays a substantial role in the energy portfolio, alterations in water availability and flow due to temperature fluctuations can impact the capacity of hydropower generation. This presents difficulties in maintaining a reliable energy supply.

Contemporary energy modeling methodologies frequently depend on linear or oversimplified non-linear techniques, which may not sufficiently encompass the intricacy of the relationships between climatic variables and energy systems6,7. These conventional models have a tendency to oversimplify the dynamics, neglecting to consider the non-linear and cyclical characteristics of temperature effects on energy consumption and production. In addition, numerous current models are not customized to the particular climatic and socio-economic conditions of this nation, resulting in inaccuracies in forecasting and planning for energy demand. The necessity for precise models that accurately represent the distinct characteristics of this country’s energy landscape is of utmost importance for efficient energy management and the formulation of policies.

In order to overcome the constraints of conventional models, there is an urgent requirement for sophisticated mathematical modeling techniques that can offer a more precise and comprehensive comprehension of the correlation between temperature fluctuations and energy dynamics. These sophisticated models should possess the ability to accurately capture the non-linear and periodic impacts of temperature fluctuations on energy consumption and production. By integrating advanced mathematical functions, such as polynomial, sinusoidal, and hybrid models, it becomes feasible to attain enhanced precision in energy predictions. This is crucial for the purpose of strategizing and optimizing energy infrastructure in response to climatic fluctuations8,9.

The energy sector in regions with diverse climatic conditions offers a distinctive setting for examining the impact of temperature on energy generation and usage, owing to its varied geography and climate10. The nation’s energy infrastructure is predominantly dependent on hydroelectric power, which is highly susceptible to climatic variables such as temperature and precipitation. The reliance on energy sources that are affected by climate conditions highlights the significance of creating precise models for forecasting the impact of temperature fluctuations on energy supply and demand. By placing emphasis on regions with varied climatic conditions as the central focus, this study aims to address a notable gap in energy modeling and provide valuable insights that can be extrapolated to similar regions dealing with climate-related challenges.

Literature review

The influence of temperature on energy production and consumption is a thoroughly researched field, with the development of numerous models and methodologies to comprehend and forecast these interconnections11,12. Nevertheless, there is an increasing demand for sophisticated, region-specific models that can precisely depict the distinct climatic and socio-economic circumstances of various areas, especially in regions such as Cameroon. This literature review provides a comprehensive analysis of recent findings and methodologies in the field, focusing on the shortcomings of current models and the potential of advanced techniques to enhance accuracy and practicality. The temperature is a crucial variable that has a significant impact on energy consumption, especially in residential and commercial sectors. This is because the heating and cooling requirements vary depending on the prevailing climatic conditions. Research has indicated that the energy requirements for heating and cooling are greatly influenced by fluctuations in temperature, exhibiting notable differences in regions with both warm and cold climates13. For example, a study13 discovered that there is a projected significant increase in global residential energy demand as a result of rising temperatures. This is due to the fact that warmer regions will require more cooling, while colder areas will have increased heating needs. In this country, the correlation between temperature and energy consumption exhibits distinct attributes as a result of the country’s varied climatic zones. The study conducted by14 emphasized the substantial influence of temperature on energy consumption patterns in urban regions with varied climatic condition. It revealed that elevated temperatures result in escalated utilization of air conditioning and cooling systems, consequently leading to a surge in electricity demand. This discovery highlights the necessity for models that can precisely capture the non-linear and region-specific impacts of temperature on energy consumption. Within the sphere of photovoltaic production, temperature is more than an important parameter as it can dictate the production yield of the photovoltaic system15–17. The performance of a photovoltaic (PV) module is significantly affected by its operating temperature, with changes in performance being characterized by the module’s temperature coefficients. These coefficients provide a quantitative measure of how the electrical parameters of the module, such as current and voltage, respond to temperature variations. In detail, both the electrical current and the voltage produced by a PV module are independently influenced by temperature fluctuations. As the temperature rises, the current generated by the module tends to increase, albeit slightly. Conversely, the voltage output typically decreases more substantially with increasing temperature18. This behavior is due to the intrinsic properties of semiconductor materials used in PV cells, where higher temperatures lead to increased carrier recombination rates, thereby reducing the open-circuit voltage more significantly than the increase in short-circuit current. The temperature coefficients for current and voltage are crucial for accurately predicting the performance of PV module under different environmental conditions. Understanding these coefficients allows for better system design and optimization, ensuring that the PV modules operate efficiently across a range of temperatures. Consequently, managing the thermal environment of PV installations, through cooling systems or strategic placement, becomes essential to maximize their overall energy yield and reliability. This further shows the crucial role of temperature in energy production through photovoltaics. To further illustrate this increasing relevance of temperature in PV energy production, some studies have studied the temperature dependece of PV systems. For instance19, examines how temperature affects the performance of PV systems. Two models were developed using the simulation programs ; Model A assumes constant efficiency regardless of temperature, while Model B incorporates temperature variations using the nominal operating cell temperature approach. The study was conducted across 236 cities in the USA using Typical Meteorological Year data. The findings highlight that lower PV-cell temperatures can lead to increased efficiency and power generation, particularly in colder climates. In cold regions, power outputs were higher in winter (16-20%) and slightly lower in summer (-4%). Conversely, in hot regions, power outputs were significantly lower in summer (-12-15%) and marginally higher in winter (5%). The study underscores the importance of considering temperature variations in PV system models to improve accuracy and optimize performance, demonstrating that ignoring temperature effects can lead to significant discrepancies in power output predictions. This emphasizes the limitations of conventional linear regression models that do not account for temperature’s impact on PV efficiency.

In terms of modelling of energy production, several approaches have been studied in different contexts. For instance20, examines the use of linear regression models to predict the additional electricity produced in cogeneration when new consumers are connected to district heating systems. The study highlights that while linear regression is a straightforward and quick method, it has limitations, particularly when data is insufficient or when the relationship between variables is more complex than a simple linear dependency. The authors demonstrate a direct and linear correlation between supplied thermal energy and additional electricity produced, but they also note that the regression model’s accuracy varies across different heating seasons, indicating potential shortcomings of using linear regression for complex energy systems modeling. This underscores the need for more sophisticated modeling techniques to account for the multifaceted interactions in energy production and consumption. Also21, discusses the use of linear regression models to predict indoor air temperature, relative humidity, and energy consumption in buildings. The study demonstrates that while linear regression offers a simpler and faster alternative to complex building simulation tools, it has inherent limitations, particularly in accurately capturing the dynamic interactions within building systems. The research validates the regression models against detailed simulations, showing good agreement but highlighting that such simplified models may not always capture all variables influencing building performance, thus limiting their predictive accuracy22. explores the use of linear regression to predict the cooling load of a research office. The study utilizes the Radiant Time Series (RTS) method to account for various influential factors such as building materials and environmental conditions. While linear regression models provide a straightforward approach to prediction, the study acknowledges their limitations, especially in capturing complex interactions and dependencies. The authors highlight that while the model shows good correlation and low error rates, further improvements could be achieved by incorporating additional parameters and occupancy behaviors to enhance predictive accuracy. This underscores the simplicity and limitations of conventional modeling techniques like linear regression.

Conventional energy models frequently utilize linear regression methods to estimate the correlation between temperature and energy metrics. Although these models are simple and can be implemented easily, they frequently do not adequately represent the intricacies of the interactions involved. For instance, a study by23 showed that linear models have a tendency to oversimplify the dynamics of energy consumption, especially in areas that experience significant climatic variability. In addition, linear models fail to consider the periodic and non-linear characteristics of temperature effects, resulting in inaccurate predictions and suboptimal energy planning.

Recent advancements in non-linear modeling have been made in an effort to overcome these limitations. For example, polynomial regression models have been employed to capture intricate relationships between temperature and energy consumption. Nevertheless, these models may encounter challenges related to overfitting and might not exhibit strong generalization capabilities when applied to diverse regions or climatic conditions. In order to enhance the precision of energy models, researchers have investigated sophisticated mathematical and computational methods that are capable of capturing the complex connections between climatic variables and energy dynamics. One promising approach is the utilization of hybrid models that integrate multiple mathematical functions to more accurately represent the non-linear and cyclical characteristics of temperature impacts. For instance, researchers24 have proposed a hybrid model that combines polynomial and sinusoidal functions to effectively capture the periodic fluctuations in energy consumption caused by seasonal temperature variations. The aforementioned methodology was discovered to have a substantial positive impact on the precision of energy consumption forecasts, especially in areas characterized by noticeable seasonal fluctuations. Similarly, research conducted by25 utilized a hybrid modeling approach to predict electricity demand in China. This approach combined machine learning techniques with traditional statistical models, resulting in a high level of accuracy. In the context of regions with diverse climatic conditions, there is a paucity of studies that have specifically addressed the need for utilizing advanced modeling techniques to predict energy metrics based on temperature fluctuations. Nevertheless, the study conducted by26 emphasizes the potential of hybrid models in delivering more precise and dependable energy planning forecasts in the region. This study emphasizes the significance of creating customized models that account for the distinct climatic and socio-economic circumstances in this country, which may vary significantly from other areas.

Machine learning algorithms have gained significant traction in the field of energy modeling due to their ability to efficiently handle and analyze large datasets, enabling the identification of complex patterns. Several machine learning algorithms, such as Random Forests, Gradient Boosting, and Neural Networks, have been employed to accurately predict energy consumption27. These methodologies, such as non-linear modeling and interaction analysis, are particularly beneficial for capturing and analyzing complex relationships and interactions between multiple variables. As a result, they are well-suited for predicting the impact of temperature on energy metrics. Hybrid models, which integrate machine learning techniques with conventional statistical methods, have demonstrated potential in enhancing the precision of energy forecasts. For example, researchers28 proposed a hybrid model that combines Support Vector Machines with polynomial regression to forecast residential energy consumption. The hybrid model outperformed traditional models in terms of performance. This approach exemplifies the potential of hybrid models to exploit the advantages of various techniques, offering a resilient framework for energy prediction.

Research gap and contributions

Notwithstanding the advancements achieved in modeling techniques, there are still notable shortcomings in the current body of literature when it comes to their implementation in regions such as Cameroon. Several existing models are developed and assessed in regions with extensively documented climatic data and established energy infrastructures, which may not have direct applicability to countries with distinct climatic and socio-economic circumstances. Notable gaps in the current research include the absence of models specifically designed for Africa, insufficient representation of non-linear and periodic impacts, limited use of hybrid models, and inadequate incorporation of climatic variability. Existing models frequently struggle to accurately represent the intricate and recurring patterns of temperature effects on energy metrics, resulting in less dependable forecasts29,30.

This research paper introduces advanced hybrid models that have been specifically designed for the purpose of addressing the unique climatic conditions found in diverse regions. These models aim to fill the existing gaps in energy forecasting and provide precise and dependable predictions. This study addresses the significant research gap in accurately predicting energy production and consumption in regions with diverse climatic conditions, particularly in countries like Cameroon, where conventional energy models fail to capture the complex non-linear and cyclical relationships between temperature and energy metrics. Most existing models are either too simplistic, relying on linear regression techniques, or are developed for regions with extensive data and well-established energy infrastructures, which do not account for the unique climatic and socio-economic conditions of developing regions. The novelty of this work lies in the development of a hybrid model that combines polynomial and sinusoidal functions to capture both non-linear and cyclical variations in energy consumption and production. This hybrid approach significantly improves the accuracy of energy forecasting, with the model achieving an accuracy of 79.15% for energy consumption predictions, compared to conventional models that perform significantly worse under similar conditions. Moreover, this is one of the few studies that focus on integrating temperature variability into energy forecasting models specific to Cameroon, a country whose energy infrastructure is heavily reliant on hydropower, which is highly sensitive to climatic factors. The hybrid model’s ability to accurately predict energy demand based on temperature changes addresses a critical need for more resilient energy systems in the face of climate variability.

The outcomes of this study are expected to offer substantial knowledge for energy policymakers and planners, facilitating the development of resilient and flexible energy systems that effectively adapt to climate variations. By utilizing polynomial, sinusoidal, and hybrid modeling methodologies, this research surpasses the constraints of existing models and offers a customized resolution to tackle the particular obstacles encountered by the energy industry in areas with varied climatic conditions. The dataset was divided into training and testing sets to evaluate the models’ performance. The training set comprised 70% of the data, while the testing set comprised the remaining 30%. The training set was used to fit the models and estimate the parameters, ensuring that the models were well-calibrated with the majority of the data. The testing set, on the other hand, was employed to validate the models and assess their predictive accuracy, providing an unbiased evaluation of how well the models could generalize to new, unseen data. This division allowed for a thorough assessment of the models’ robustness and their ability to make accurate predictions based on temperature variations.

In this study, the models (polynomial, sinusoidal, and hybrid) were fitted to the data using the Least Squares Estimation (LSE) algorithm. LSE is widely used in regression modeling due to its efficiency in minimizing the sum of squared residuals, which is the difference between observed and predicted values. The algorithm iteratively adjusts the model parameters to minimize the error, ensuring that the fitted model provides the best approximation of the relationship between temperature and energy metrics. The LSE algorithm is particularly suitable for this study because it allows for straightforward optimization of polynomial and sinusoidal models, both of which involve non-linear relationships. Additionally, LSE enables the hybrid model to combine both periodic and non-linear components effectively, resulting in a robust predictive model for energy production and consumption.

In addition, K-fold cross-validation was used to ensure the robustness of the models. The dataset was divided into k equal parts, and the model was trained and tested k times, each time using a different part as the testing set and the remaining parts as the training set. In addition, a sensitivity analysis was conducted to evaluate the models’ performance under different temperature scenarios. This involved varying the temperature inputs and assessing the impact on the predicted energy metrics.

Simulation setup

The dataset included monthly temperature, energy production, and energy consumption data from 2010 to 2020. The parameters for each model were optimized using grid search and cross-validation to achieve the best fit for the data. The final models were used to simulate energy production and consumption based on temperature variations.

Validation of results

To validate the accuracy and robustness of the models, a 5-fold cross-validation approach was employed. This involved dividing the dataset into five equal parts, with each part serving as a testing set once while the remaining four parts were used for training. The cross-validation process provided a comprehensive evaluation of the models’ performance across different subsets of the data, ensuring that the models were not overfitting and were generalizing well to unseen data.

The validation of the models developed in this study goes beyond simply applying cross-validation metrics. We employed a 5-fold cross-validation to ensure the robustness of the models by training and testing the models on different subsets of the data. This method provides an unbiased estimate of the models’ performance and prevents overfitting, confirming their ability to generalize well to unseen data. In addition to using standard metrics such as R², MSE, and RMSE, which were introduced in Section 2.3, this section focuses on residual analysis and sensitivity testing to provide deeper insights into model performance. Residual analysis is crucial for identifying any patterns or biases in the predictions. By plotting the residuals against the predicted values, we ensured that the models do not exhibit systematic errors, which would indicate poor model fit. Randomly distributed residuals around zero, as observed in the results, confirm that the models are capturing the underlying relationships without significant bias. We also conducted sensitivity analysis to test how the models respond to variations in temperature inputs. By adjusting the temperature by ± 5 °C, we observed significant changes in energy production and consumption, highlighting the sensitivity of the models to climatic fluctuations. This analysis is particularly important in regions experiencing extreme weather conditions, as it provides insights into the resilience of energy systems under varying temperature scenarios.

Model replicability and comparative study

While the hybrid modeling framework was developed with Cameroon’s unique climatic conditions in mind, its structure and methodology are robust and adaptable to other regions with different climate profiles. The model’s combination of polynomial and sinusoidal components allows it to capture both non-linear and cyclical energy consumption and production patterns, which are characteristic of many regions influenced by temperature fluctuations and seasonal changes. To assess the replicability of the framework, we applied it to Sweden, a country with a temperate to cold climate where energy consumption is largely driven by heating demands during winter months. The seasonal energy consumption patterns in Sweden differ significantly from those in Cameroon, where cooling needs dominate during hot seasons. Despite these differences, after adjusting the temperature ranges and seasonal parameters in the model, the hybrid framework achieved an accuracy of 78.5% in predicting energy consumption in Sweden.

In terms of comparative study, in Sweden, energy demand peaks during the cold winter months, driven by heating needs. By adjusting the model to account for these regional-specific energy demands and climatic conditions, the hybrid framework demonstrated its versatility. The model successfully captured the cyclical energy consumption patterns tied to temperature fluctuations, achieving results comparable to those observed in Cameroon. This comparative analysis highlights the flexibility and robustness of the hybrid model, demonstrating its potential for application in regions with vastly different energy consumption drivers. Whether applied to regions where cooling or heating needs dominate, the model can be adjusted to provide accurate energy forecasts, making it a valuable tool for energy planning and policy development in both developing and developed regions.

Compared to traditional linear models, the advanced mathematical models developed in this study demonstrated significantly better performance in capturing the complex relationships between temperature and energy metrics. The hybrid model, in particular, provided a more accurate and reliable framework for predicting energy consumption, highlighting the limitations of linear approaches in handling non-linear and cyclical data. The advanced models’ predictions closely matched actual energy production and consumption data, confirming their applicability for real-world energy planning and management. The models’ ability to generalize across different climatic scenarios and their high accuracy in predicting energy metrics make them valuable tools for energy stakeholders in various regions with diverse climatic conditions.

Limitations and areas for improvement

One of the primary limitations of this study is the availability and quality of historical temperature and energy data. While the collected dataset provided a comprehensive basis for model development, additional data on other climatic variables, such as humidity and precipitation, could further enhance the models’ accuracy and robustness. Future research should focus on refining the models by incorporating more sophisticated machine learning techniques and additional climatic factors. Enhancing the models to account for more complex interactions between multiple variables could improve their predictive performance and applicability in diverse settings. The existing models are designed to accommodate the unique climatic conditions found in regions with a wide range of climates. Expanding the scope of the study to include other regions with different climatic characteristics could validate the models’ generalizability and provide insights into their applicability in broader contexts.

Recommendations for future research

Future research should explore the integration of additional climatic variables, such as wind speed, solar radiation, and humidity, to develop more comprehensive models that capture the full range of factors influencing energy production and consumption. The application of advanced machine learning techniques, such as neural networks and ensemble learning, could enhance the models’ ability to handle large datasets and complex relationships. These techniques can provide more accurate and reliable predictions, supporting better energy planning and management. Given the diversity of climatic conditions across different regions, developing region-specific models that account for local climatic and socio-economic factors can provide more accurate predictions and insights. Tailoring models to the unique characteristics of each region can enhance their relevance and utility for local energy stakeholders.

The examination of results derived from the sophisticated mathematical models formulated in this research offers valuable insights into the influence of temperature fluctuations on energy generation and usage in areas characterized by varying climatic conditions. The models’ high accuracy and robustness highlight their potential as tools for energy forecasting, policy development, and resource management. By addressing the limitations and expanding the scope of future research, these models can contribute to the development of sustainable and resilient energy systems that are better equipped to handle climatic variability and ensure a stable energy supply.

Conclusions and future work

This research formulated and verified sophisticated mathematical models aimed at forecasting energy generation and usage in relation to temperature variations. The hybrid model, which integrates sinusoidal and polynomial functions, demonstrated a notable accuracy rate of 79.15% in predicting energy consumption, markedly surpassing conventional models. This model accurately represents the complex non-linear and cyclical interactions between temperature and energy metrics, establishing a strong framework for energy management in areas with varied climatic conditions. The results indicate that temperature significantly influences energy generation and consumption, with elevated temperatures leading to heightened cooling requirements and reduced temperatures impacting production, especially in hydropower systems. The findings highlight the necessity of integrating climatic factors into energy planning and policy development to guarantee system resilience against climate variability. The study emphasizes the necessity of incorporating supplementary climatic variables, including humidity and wind speed, into the models to improve their predictive capabilities. Furthermore, utilizing sophisticated machine learning methodologies, including neural networks, has the potential to enhance model precision and flexibility across diverse climatic conditions. In summary, the models created in this study present considerable opportunities for enhancing energy forecasting and informing policy development. Through the provision of precise, climate-informed forecasts, they can enhance the design of robust energy systems capable of enduring climatic fluctuations, optimizing the allocation of energy resources, and advancing sustainable development.

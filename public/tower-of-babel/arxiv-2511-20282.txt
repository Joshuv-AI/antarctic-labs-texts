Solubility of a family of conics with polynomial coefficients in many variables
Mathieu Da Silva

Abstract




We study the proportion of conics given by
(\mathcal{C}_{\bm{F},\bm{y}}):F_{0}(\bm{y})x_{0}^{2}+F_{1}(\bm{y})x_{1}^{2}=F_{2}(\bm{y})x_{2}^{2}
which have a rational point
\bm{x}=(x_{0}:x_{1}:x_{2})\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{Q})
, where
\bm{y}=(y_{0}:\dots:y_{n})\in\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{Q})
and
F_{0},F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{0},\ldots,X_{n}]
are homogeneous polynomials in many variables of the same degree
d
. We provide an asymptotic formula for the number of
\bm{y}
of bounded height such that the corresponding conic
(\mathcal{C}_{\bm{F},\bm{y}})
has a rational point. In particular, our result agrees with the Loughran–Smeets and the Loughran–Rome–Sofos conjectures. Our strategy is based on a recent result of Destagnol–Lyczak–Sofos relying on the circle method to estimate the average of an arithmetic function over polynomials in many variables. To this end, we study the proportion of conics
t_{0}x_{0}^{2}+t_{1}x_{1}^{2}+t_{2}x_{2}^{2}=0
having a rational point, and coefficients
t_{0},t_{1},t_{2}
in arithmetic progressions.

Introduction

Motivations and first definitions.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{Q})
. We recall that the anti-canonical height on
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{Q})
is defined by

H({\bm{y}}):=\max(\left|y_{0}\right|,\ldots,\left|y_{n}\right|)^{n+1},

where
(y_{0}:\cdots:y_{n})
is a representative of
\bm{y}
with coprime coordinates. The integer
H(\bm{y})
does not depend on the choice of such a representative of
\bm{y}
.

Let
X
be a smooth and proper projective variety over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\pi\penalty\ :\penalty\ X\penalty\ \to\penalty\ \mathbb{P}^{n}
a dominant morphism whose generic fibre is geometrically integral. The fibres of
\pi
define a family of varieties. The problem we are interested in consists in estimating, when
B
goes to
+\infty
, the quantity

N_{\mathrm{loc}}(\pi,B):=\#\left\{\bm{y}\in\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{Q}):H(\bm{y})\leqslant B,\bm{y}\in\pi(X(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}))\right\},

which is a natural upper-bound for the quantity

N_{\text{glob}}(\pi,B):=\#\left\{\bm{y}\in\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{Q}):H(\bm{y})\leqslant B,\bm{y}\in\pi(X(\mathbb{Q}))\right\}.

The Loughran–Smeets conjecture.

In 2016, Loughran and Smeets
[
15
, th. 1.2 and 1.3]
used the large sieve and the Ekedahl sieve to obtain an upper-bound for
N_{\mathrm{loc}}(\pi,B)
(and hence for
N_{\mathrm{glob}}(\pi,B)
), which is valid for every dominant morphism
\pi:X\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
whose generic fibre is geometrically integral, where
X
is a smooth and proper projective variety over a number field. The Loughran–Smeets conjecture
[
15
, 1.6]
states that this upper-bound is sharp for
N_{\mathrm{loc}}(\pi,B)
under some mild assumptions. More precisely, it predicts that

N_{\mathrm{loc}}(\pi,B)\underset{B\to+\infty}{\sim}c{B\over(\log B)^{\Delta(\pi)}}

for some constant
c>0
and
\Delta(\pi)\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
defined by
[
15
, (1.3)]
. This generalises a previous work of Serre
[
21
]
from 1990 where, using the large sieve as well, the author obtained a sharp upper-bound for the quantity

N(B):=\#\left\{{\bm{t}}\in\left[-B,B\right]^{3}:t_{0}x_{0}^{2}+t_{1}x_{1}^{2}=t_{2}x_{2}^{2}\text{ has a solution $\bm{x}\in\mathbb{Q}^{3}\setminus\{(0,0,0)\}$}\right\}\quad(B\geqslant 2).

Note that, here, studying the quantity
N(B)
is equivalent to studying the quantity
N_{\mathrm{loc}}(\pi,B)
in the case of the family described by

X:\quad t_{0}x_{0}^{2}+t_{1}x_{1}^{2}=t_{2}x_{2}^{2}\quad\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2},

with
\pi:(\bm{x},\bm{t})\in X\mapsto\bm{t}\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Between 1990 and 2000 matching lower bounds for
N(B)
were obtained independently by Guo
[
8
]
and Hooley
[
10
,
11
]
. In 2023, Loughran, Rome and Sofos promoted those bounds to an asymptotic by showing the estimate

N(B)\underset{B\to+\infty}{\sim}c{B^{3}\over(\log B)^{3/2}}

where
c
is an explicit positive constant. They also give a prediction for the shape of the constant
c
in general.

in
\{0,1,2\}
. Each of these cases contributes at most
p^{4}
to
\#Y_{p}
, so we deduce that

\#Y_{p}\geqslant{3\over 2}p^{3}(p-1)^{2}+O(p^{4})\geqslant{3\over 2}p^{5}-3p^{4}.

Hence, the function
F
in lemma
6.5
satisfies

F(L)\gg\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leqslant L\\
\gcd(2c_{012},a)=1\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}(a)\prod_{p\mid a}{{3\over 2}\left(p-2\right)\over p^{2}-{3\over 2}\left(p-2\right)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leqslant L\\
\gcd(2c_{012},a)=1\end{subarray}}{\mu^{2}(a)u(a)\over a}\left({3\over 2}\right)^{\omega(a)},

with
u
the multiplicative function defined by

u(a)=\prod_{p\mid a}\left({p^{2}-2p\over p^{2}-{3p\over 2}+3}\right)=\prod_{p\mid a}\left(1-{{p\over 2}+3\over p^{2}-{3p\over 2}+3}\right).

To estimate the right hand-side, we write uniquely each integer
k
as
k=ab^{2}
with
a
squarefree, which yields

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leqslant L\\
\gcd(k,2c_{012})\end{subarray}}{1\over k}\left({3\over 2}\right)^{\omega(k)}\!\!\!\!u(k)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}ab^{2}\leqslant L\\
\gcd(ab^{2},2c_{012})=1\end{subarray}}{\mu^{2}(a)\over ab^{2}}\left({3\over 2}\right)^{\omega(ab^{2})}\!\!\!\!\!u(ab^{2})\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leqslant L\\
\gcd(2c_{012},a)=1\end{subarray}}{\mu^{2}(a)\over a}\left({3\over 2}\right)^{\omega(a)}\!\!\!\!\!u(a).

Then, we apply the Selberg–Delange method to estimate the sum

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leqslant L\\
\gcd(k,2c_{012})\end{subarray}}\left({3\over 2}\right)^{\omega(k)}\!\!\!\!\!u(k).

Let
G(s)
denote the product

G(s):=\prod_{p}\left(1-{1\over p^{s}}\right)^{3/2}\left(\sum_{\nu\geqslant 0}\left({3\over 2}\right)^{\nu}{u(p^{\nu})\over p^{\nu s}}\right)

which is convergent for
\sigma>1/2
, since
u(p)=1+O(1/p)
. Similarly to
[
26
, exercise 203, p181]
, it follows that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leqslant L\\
\gcd(k,2c_{012})\end{subarray}}\left({3\over 2}\right)^{\omega(k)}\!\!\!\!\!u(k)=L(\log L)^{1/2}\left({G(1)\over\Gamma(3/2)}\prod_{p\mid 2c_{012}}\left(\sum_{\nu\geqslant 0}\left({3\over 2}\right)^{\nu}{u(p^{\nu})\over p^{\nu}}\right)^{-1}+O\left({1\over\log L}\right)\right).

Again, since
u(p)=1+O(1/p)
, we have

\prod_{p\mid 2c_{012}}\left(\sum_{\nu\geqslant 0}\left({3\over 2}\right)^{\nu}{u(p^{\nu})\over p^{\nu}}\right)^{-1}\gg\prod_{p\mid 2c_{012}}\left(1+{3\over 2p}\right)^{-1}\geqslant\left({\varphi(2c_{012})\over 2c_{012}}\right)^{3/2}.

Hence

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leqslant L\\
\gcd(k,2c_{012})\end{subarray}}\left({3\over 2}\right)^{\omega(k)}\!\!\!\!\!u(k)\gg\left({\varphi(2c_{012})\over 2c_{012}}\right)^{3/2}L(\log L)^{1/2}.

In particular, it follows from a partial summation that

F(L)\gg\left({\varphi(2c_{012})\over 2c_{012}}\right)^{3/2}\int_{2}^{L}{\log(v)^{1/2}\over v}\mathrm{d}v\gg\left({\varphi(2c_{012})\over 2c_{012}}\log L\right)^{3/2}.

We can therefore conclude with lemma
6.5
and
L=(\min x_{i})^{5/24}\leqslant\min(x_{i}/c_{i})^{1/4}
.

∎

We remove here the large valuations and impose the coprimality condition to recover the assumptions from Theorem
4.2
.

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
z>2
be a real number. The quantity
\varepsilon(z)
is defined in
6.1
and goes to
0
when the parameter
z
goes to
+\infty
. If
z\ll\log_{2}B
, we have the estimate

\displaystyle N_{\bm{\epsilon}}^{\prime}(\pi_{\bm{F}},B)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n+1}}\widetilde{N}_{\bm{\epsilon},z}(\pi_{\bm{F}},B,\mathcal{K}_{i})+O\left(\left(\varepsilon(z)+{1\over z\log z}\right){B^{n+1}\over(\log B)^{3/2}}+{B^{n+1/2}\over(\log B)^{1/2}}\right),

where

[-1,1]^{n+1}=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{2^{n+1}}\mathcal{K}_{i}

is the partition defined in Remark
6.4
.

Proof.

It suffices to work with
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}_{i}
fixed. Recall that
d
is the common degree of the polynomials
F_{i}
and
b
denotes the real number
2\max_{i}(\max\left|F_{i}(\mathcal{K})\right|)
. We start by dealing with the large valuations. The condition
\max(v_{p}(n_{0}n_{2}),v_{p}(n_{1}n_{2}))\geqslant m_{p}(z)
implies that
\max(v_{p}(n_{0}),v_{p}(n_{1}),v_{p}(n_{2}))\geqslant m_{p}(z)/2
so we can apply lemma
6.3
to the set

\mathcal{A}:=\left\{\bm{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap B\mathcal{K}:(t_{0}t_{2},t_{1}t_{2})_{\mathbb{Q}}=1,\;\exists p\leqslant z\text{ such that }\max v_{p}(t_{i})\geqslant m_{p}(z)/2\right\}.

The result follows from Lemma
6.6
applied for instance with
c_{0}=p^{m_{p}(z)/2}
,
c_{1}=c_{2}=1
for which we have

c_{012}=p^{m_{p}(z)/2}\leqslant W_{z}\leqslant\mathrm{e}^{m_{p}(z)\left(z+{z\over 2\log z}\right)}\ll B^{1/6},

since
z\ll\log_{2}(B)
. Thus,

{\#\mathcal{A}\over B^{3d}}\ll\sum_{p\leqslant z}{1\over p^{m_{p}(z)/2}}{1\over(\log B)^{3/2}}\ll{\varepsilon(z)\over(\log B)^{3/2}}.

To deal with the coprimality condition, we follow
[
5
, lemma 4.1]
when
R=3
which provide a bound of size

\ll{1\over z\log z}{B^{n+1}\over(\log B)^{3/2}}+{B^{n+1/2}\over(\log B)^{1/2}}.

∎

We now focus on estimating
\widetilde{N}_{\bm{\epsilon},z}(\pi_{\bm{F}},B,\mathcal{K}_{i})
. Recall that we denote by
Q_{\bm{t}}
the conic of equation

t_{0}x_{0}^{2}+t_{1}x_{1}^{2}-t_{2}x_{2}^{2}=0
, with
\bm{t}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{>0})^{3}
. Thanks to theorem
6.2
and Corollary
6.7
, it suffices to provide an asymptotic formula for the quantity

N({\bm{n}},W_{z},B)\!:=\!\#\left\{{\bm{t}}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{>0})^{3}\!:\begin{tabular}[]{c}$\max t_{i}\leqslant B,\gcd(t_{0},t_{1},t_{2})=1$\\
$Q_{\bm{t}}$ has a solution in $\mathbb{Q}\smallsetminus\{(0,0,0)\}$, $\bm{t}\equiv\bm{n}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ W_{z})$\end{tabular}\right\},

where
W_{z}:=\displaystyle\prod_{p\leqslant z}p^{m_{p}(z)}
with
m_{p}(z)
given by (

6.2

), and
\bm{n}\in[0,W_{z})^{3}
is such that
\gcd(n_{0},n_{1},n_{2},W_{z})=\penalty\ 1
,
\max(v_{2}(n_{0}n_{2}),v_{2}(n_{1}n_{2}))\leqslant m_{2}(z)-3
and
\max(v_{p}(n_{0}n_{2}),v_{p}(n_{1}n_{2}))<m_{p}(z)
for all
2<p\leqslant z
. A satisfying estimate for
N(\bm{n},W_{z},B)
is provided by Theorem
4.2
.

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
z>2
be a real number and let
W_{z}
be as in Definition
6.1
with
m_{p}(z)
given by
(

6.2

)
. Let
\bm{n}\in[0,W_{z})^{3}
be such that
\gcd(n_{0},n_{1},n_{2},W_{z})=1
,
\max v_{2}(n_{i})\leqslant m_{2}(z)-3
and
\max v_{p}(n_{i})<m_{p}(z)
for all
2<p\leqslant z
. If
z\leqslant\sqrt{{1\over 14}\log_{2}B}
, then, when
B
goes to
+\infty

N({\bm{n}},W_{z},B)=\rho({\bm{n}},W_{z}){B^{3}\over(\log B)^{3/2}}+O\left({(\log_{3}B)^{3/2}B^{3}\over W_{z}^{3}\log_{2}B(\log B)^{3/2}}\right),

where

\rho({\bm{n}},W_{z}):={2\over\pi^{3/2}}{1\over W_{z}^{3}}\prod_{p\leqslant z}\left(1-{1\over p}\right)^{-3/2}\vartheta_{p}(\bm{n}).

Proof.

The assumption
z\leqslant\sqrt{{1\over 14}\log_{2}B}
ensures that
W_{z}\leqslant(\log B)^{1/7}
so that we can apply Theorem
4.2
, leading to

\displaystyle N({\bm{n}},W_{z},B)=

\displaystyle{2\over\pi^{3/2}\varphi(W_{z})^{3}}\left(\prod_{p\leqslant z}\left(1-{1\over p}\right)^{3/2}\vartheta_{p}(\bm{n})\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\prod_{p>z}\left(1-{1\over p}\right)^{3/2}{\left(1+{1\over p}+{1\over p^{2}}\right)\left(1+{1\over 2p}+{1\over p^{2}}\right)\over\left(1-{1\over p^{2}}\right)^{2}}\right){B^{3}\over(\log B)^{3/2}}+O\left({B^{3}\log_{2}(B)W_{z}^{3}\over\varphi(W_{z})^{3}(\log B)^{5/2}}\right).

Now, we use the estimate

\displaystyle\prod_{p>z}\left(1-{1\over p}\right)^{3/2}{\left(1+{1\over p}+{1\over p^{2}}\right)\left(1+{1\over 2p}+{1\over p^{2}}\right)\over\left(1-{1\over p^{2}}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p>z}\left(1+O\left({1\over p^{2}}\right)\right)=1+O\left({1\over z}\right)

in order to write

\displaystyle N({\bm{n}},W_{z},B)=

\displaystyle\;\rho(\bm{n},W_{z}){B^{3}\over(\log B)^{3/2}}+O\left({B^{3}\log_{2}(B)W_{z}^{3}\over\varphi(W_{z})^{3}(\log B)^{5/2}}\right)

\displaystyle+O\left({1\over z\varphi(W_{z})^{3}}{B^{3}\over(\log B)^{3/2}}\prod_{p\leqslant z}\left(1-{1\over p}\right)^{3/2}\right).

The last error term is admissible since

\prod_{p\leqslant z}\left(1-{1\over p}\right)^{3/2}={\varphi(W_{z})^{3/2}\over W_{z}^{3/2}}\quad\text{ and }\quad{1\over\varphi(W_{z})}\ll{\log z\over W_{z}},

which leads us to the estimate

{1\over z\varphi(W_{z})^{3}}\prod_{p\leqslant z}\left(1-{1\over p}\right)^{3/2}{B^{3}\over(\log B)^{3/2}}\ll{(\log z)^{3/2}\over zW_{z}^{3}}{B^{3}\over(\log B)^{3/2}}\ll{(\log_{3}B)^{3/2}B^{3}\over W_{z}^{3}\log_{2}B(\log B)^{3/2}}.

∎

Let
g
be the arithmetic function defined by

g(\bm{t}):=\mathds{1}\left(\begin{tabular}[]{l}$\forall p\in(2,z],\;\max(v_{p}(t_{0}t_{2}),v_{p}(t_{1}t_{2}))<m_{p}(z)$ ;\\

and
\bm{t}\equiv\bm{n}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ W_{z})
, we have

g(\bm{t}):=\mathds{1}\left(\begin{tabular}[]{l}$\forall p\in(2,z],\;\max(v_{p}(t_{0}t_{2}),v_{p}(t_{1}t_{2}))<m_{p}(z)$ ;\\
$\max(v_{2}(t_{0}t_{2}),v_{2}(t_{1}t_{2}))\leqslant m_{2}(z)-3$ ;\\
$\gcd(t_{0},t_{1},t_{2})=1,\text{ and }\vartheta_{\mathbb{Q}}(\bm{t})=1$\end{tabular}\right).

Lemma
6.8
provides a non trivial upper-bound for the quantity

defined by (

Lemma 6.11

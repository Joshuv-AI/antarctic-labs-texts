Federated k -Means via Generalized Total Variation Minimization
Alexander Jung

Abstract
We consider the problem of federated clustering, where inter-connected devices have access to private local datasets and need to jointly cluster the overall dataset without sharing their local dataset. Our focus is on hard clustering based on the k k -means principle. We formulate federated k k -means clustering as an instance of GTVMin. This formulation lends naturally to a federated k k -means algorithm where each device updates local cluster centroids by solving a modified local k k -means problem. The modification consists of adding a penalty term that measures the discrepancy between the cluster centroid of neighbouring devices. Our federated k k -means algorithm is privacy-friendly as it only requires to share aggregated information among inter-connected devices.

Introduction

Clustering is one of the most fundamental and widely-used techniques for data analysis and
unsupervised machine learning (ML)
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.
The goal of clustering is to decompose a given dataset into coherent sub-groups
or clusters
[
1
,
2
,
4
]
. In what follows, our
focus will be on hard clustering where the clusters are non-overlapping, i.e., they
form a partition of the dataset.

A widely-used clustering principle for data points characterized by Euclidean feature vectors
is
k
-means
[
1
, Sec. 8.1]
. Mathematically,
k
-means amounts to partitioning the
data point into
k
non-overlapping clusters such that the resulting clustering error is
minimal. The clustering error is measured by the average squared Euclidean distance between
each data point and its nearest cluster centroid.

It turns out that solving the
k
-means problem is NP-hard in general
[
5
]
.
Nevertheless, we can obtain approximate solutions by an iterative scheme which is referred to as
Lloyd’s method
[
6
,
7
]
. This method alternates between updating the cluster
assignments for given cluster centroids and, in turn, updating the cluster centroids. Trivially,
each of these steps can never increase the clustering error which, in turn, ensures that
Lloyd’s method is guaranteed to converge in terms of clustering error.

While conceptually straightforward, a direct implementation of Lloyd’s method for

k
-means requires access to the entire dataset. A naive gathering (or pooling) of local datasets
at a central location (e.g., a server) might not be feasible for technical or legal reasons
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
.
Instead, we can use distributed algorithms that combine computational resources of each
devices with communication links between some of them.

Distributed algorithms are privacy-friendly since they do not exchange raw data but only
the results of local computations. One trivial extreme case of such an distributed algorithm consists
of running a separate
k
-means method independently at each device. This approach
is not useful for small local dataset that do not carry sufficient information individually.
Another, less trivial, example for a distributed algorithm is obtained by coupling the local
k
-means
instances by some consensus protocol that enforces identical cluster centroids at each device.
However, enforcing identical cluster centroid can be suboptimal when local datasets are heterogeneous statistically.

As a compromise between fully independent device-wise and global server-based
k
-means,
we couple the local
k
-means instances by requiring similar cluster centroids at
devices connected via a bi-directional link. This coupling arises from penalizing
a measure for the dis-agreement between local cluster centroids at connected devices.
The resulting federated clustering methods is an instance of GTVMin
[
15
]
.

In contrast to existing federated clustering methods that compute a single (global) set of
cluster centroids, our method delivers a separate set of cluster centroids for
each well-connected subset of devices. This behaviour is appealing for applications
where local datasets have varying probability distributions and the connectivity between
devices somewhat reflects the similarity between probability distributions of local datasets.

Related work

A main theme of this paper is the formulation of federated clustering as an instance GTVMin.
Generalized total variation minimization (GTVMin) unifies and generalizes distributed learning methods such as network Lasso
[
16
]
,
MOCHA
[
17
]
or CoCoA
[
18
]
. Our recent work studies GTVMin for
clustered federated learning (CFL)
[
19
]
. In contrast to CFL, this paper studies decentralized methods
for clustering data points collected by different devices instead of clustering devices
themselves as in CFL.

Deng et al. propose FKmeansCB
[
20
]
, a federated
k
-means method where each
device implements a privacy-friendly variant of
k
-means to obtain local cluster centroids.
These local cluster centroids are sent to a central server. The server then applies

k
-means to the union of these local cluster centroids to obtain a single (global)
set of cluster centroids, which are broadcasted back to each device.

Conceptually similar to our federated
k
-means method is the distributed
k
-means method in

[
21
]
. The approach of
[
21
]
consists of formulating the
distributed clustering problem as an optimization problem which is solved using a distributed
optimization method. In contrast to , which uses a primal-dual formulation and method, our
optimization problem is formulated directly in terms of the local cluster centroids (which
are the primal variables). Moreover, our optimization method operates directly on these cluster centroids.

Balcan et.al.,
[
22
]
propose a distributed
k
-means method where
each device runs a local
k
-means implementation to construct a weighted subset,
referred to as a coreset, of data points. The local coresets are then communicated
over links to allow each device to construct a global coreset. In contrast, we formulate federated

k
-means as an instance of GTVMin directly in
terms of local cluster centroids, i.e., without an intermediate coreset construction as
in
[
22
]
.

Contribution

Our main contributions can be summarized as

•

(conceptual) we formulate federated
k
-means as an instance of GTVMin.

•

(algorithmic) we obtain a novel federated clustering method by solving the
above GTVMin instance using a distributed non-linear Jacobi iteration.

We denote Euclidean vectors (matrices) by bold-face lower (upper) letters.
The set consisting of the first

.

Given a node

\mathcal{G}=\left(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}\right)
, we denote

\mathcal{N}^{(i)}:=\big\{i^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}:\{i,i^{\prime}\}\in\mathcal{E}\big\}
.
The node degree is denoted

.
For a matrix

.

II
Problem Formulation

We consider an arbitrary but finite number
n
of devices, which we
index by natural numbers
i\in[n]
. Each device
has its own local dataset

\mathcal{D}^{(i)}=\big\{{\bf x}^{(i,1)},\ldots,{\bf x}^{(i,m_{i})}\big\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The local datasets constitute the entire pooled dataset

\mathcal{D}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{D}^{(i)}=\big\{{\bf x}^{(1)},\ldots,{\bf x}^{(m)}\big\}.

(1)

In what follows, we assume that local datasets are pair-wise disjoint and therefore, in turn,

m=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}
.

Our ultimate goal is to decompose the pooled dataset (
1
) into a pre-scribed
number
k
of clusters by solving the
k
-means problem

\displaystyle\min_{{\bf w}^{(1)},\ldots,{\bf w}^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\;\sum_{r=1}^{m}\min_{c^{\prime}\in[k]}\left\|{{\bf x}^{(r)}-{\bf w}^{(c^{\prime})}}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

(2)

A solution of the
k
-means problem (
2
) consists of
k
cluster centroids

\widehat{{\bf w}}^{(1)},\ldots,\widehat{{\bf w}}^{(k)}
, which we stack into the matrix

\widehat{{\bf W}}:=\big(\widehat{{\bf w}}^{(1)},\ldots,\widehat{{\bf w}}^{(k)}\big).

A naive approach would be to collect the entire dataset at a single computational unit (e.g., a server)
and then solve (
2
). However, this “centralized
k
-means” approach is
infeasible when local datasets contain sensitive information and cannot be shared easily. Instead,
we propose a privacy-friendly decentralized federated clustering method.

Our federated clustering method requires devices to only share aggregated data (model parameter updates)
across bidirectional communication links
\mathcal{E}
. Each link
\{i,i^{\prime}\}\!\in\!\mathcal{E}

connects two different devices
i,i^{\prime}\in[n]
.

Section
III
presents a de-centralized method for computing approximation of
the columns in
\widehat{{\bf W}}
. This method assigns each device
i\in[n]

a set of local cluster centroids
{\bf w}^{(1,i)},\ldots,{\bf w}^{(k,i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
which we stack into the matrix

{\bf W}^{(i)}:=\big({\bf w}^{(1,i)},\ldots,{\bf w}^{(k,i)}\big)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}.

This method is iterative: In each iteration, every device updates their local cluster centroids
using their local dataset
\mathcal{D}^{(i)}
and the information received via the links
\mathcal{E}
.

The goal of our federated
k
-means method is that each device learns the
same set of cluster centroids as those obtained by a centralized
k
-means
method that solves (
2
). To this end, we require

{\rm cols}\left({\bf W}^{(i)}\right)\!=\!{\rm cols}\left({\bf W}^{(i^{\prime})}\right)\mbox{ for any two devices }i,i^{\prime}\!\in\![n].

(3)

Note that (
3
) can hold even
if
{\bf W}^{(i)}\neq{\bf W}^{(i^{\prime})}
for some
i,i^{\prime}\in[n]
.
In particular, the validity of (
3
) does not change
if we arbitrarily permute the columns of the matrices
{\bf W}^{(i)}
, for
i=1,\ldots,n
.

We can measure the extent by which (
3
) is violated via the GTV

\displaystyle\sum_{\{i,i^{\prime}\}\in\mathcal{E}}d^{(i,i^{\prime})}\mbox{ with }

\displaystyle d^{(i,i^{\prime})}\!:=\!\underbrace{\sum_{c\in[k]}\min_{c^{\prime}\in[k]}\left\|{{\bf w}^{(i,c)}\!-\!\mathbf{w}^{(i^{\prime},c^{\prime})}}\right\|_{2}^{2}}_{\rm I}\!+\!

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\underbrace{\sum_{c\in[k]}\min_{c^{\prime}\in[k]}\left\|{{\bf w}^{(i^{\prime},c)}\!-\!{\bf w}^{(i,c^{\prime})}}\right\|_{2}^{2}}_{\rm II}.

(4)

Note that, for any link
\{i,i^{\prime}\}
, we can ensure
d^{(i,i^{\prime})}=0

only if each of the two components
{\rm I}
and
{\rm II}
vanishes. Component
{\rm I}
vanishes
if and only if
{\rm cols}\left({\bf W}^{(i)}\right)\subseteq{\rm cols}\left({\bf W}^{(i^{\prime})}\right)
. Similarly,
component
{\rm II}
vanishes if and only if
{\rm cols}\left({\bf W}^{(i^{\prime})}\right)\subseteq{\rm cols}\left({\bf W}^{(i)}\right)
. Thus,
we have

d^{(i,i^{\prime})}=0\mbox{ if and only if }{\rm cols}\left({\bf W}^{(i)}\right)\!=\!{\rm cols}\left({\bf W}^{(i^{\prime})}\right).

From now on, we assume that the links
\mathcal{E}
are such that the resulting undirected graph

\mathcal{G}=\left([n],\mathcal{E}\right)
is connected. In this case, enforcing a vanishing
GTV (
II
) implies that each device
i\in[n]

carries the same set of local cluster centroids. Thus, by enforcing a vanishing GTV (
II
),
the centralized
k
-means problem (
2
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\min_{{\bf W}^{(1)},\ldots}

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[n]}L_{i}\left({\bf W}^{(i)}\right)\mbox{s.t.\ }\sum_{\{i,i^{\prime}\}\in\mathcal{E}}d^{(i,i^{\prime})}=0.

(5)

Here, we used the local loss function

\displaystyle L_{i}\left({\bf W}^{(i)}\right)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{r\in[m_{i}]}\min_{c^{\prime}\in[k]}\left\|{{\bf x}^{(i,r)}-{\bf w}^{(i,c^{\prime})}}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

(6)

III
A Federated
k
-means Method

Section
II
reformulated the basic
k
-means problem
(
2
) as solving (
5
) under
the constraint of vanishing GTV (
II
). Instead of enforcing a vanishing
GTV, we add it as a penalty term to the objective function in (
5
).
This results an instance of GTVMin,

\min_{{\bf W}^{(1)},\ldots}\hskip-2.84526pt\sum_{i\in[n]}\hskip-2.84526ptL_{i}\left({\bf W}^{(i)}\right)\!+\!\alpha\hskip-8.53581pt\sum_{\{i,i^{\prime}\}\in\mathcal{E}}\hskip-5.69054ptd^{(i,i^{\prime})}.

(7)

In general, (
7
) is a non-convex optimization problem. For the special
case
\alpha=0
, (
7
) decomposes into separate
k
-means
problems for each device
i
.

While solving (
7
) exactly is NP-hard in general
[
23
,
5
]
,
we can use a non-linear Jacobi method to solve (
7
) approximately.
The idea is as follows: During a new iteration, we update
{\bf W}^{(i)}
by minimizing (
7
)
with all other
{\bf W}^{(i^{\prime})}
,
i^{\prime}\in[n]\setminus\{i\}
held fixed.
More formally, we synchronously execute the following update at each device
i
,

\displaystyle\min_{{\bf W}^{(i)}}L_{i}\left({\bf W}^{(i)}\right)\!+\!\alpha\hskip-8.53581pt\sum_{i^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}^{(i)}}\hskip-5.69054ptd^{(i,i^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{equ_def_local_loss},\eqref{equ-def_gtv}}}{{=}}\min_{{\bf w}^{(i,1)},\ldots,{\bf w}^{(i,k)}}\sum_{r\in[m_{i}]}\min_{c^{\prime}\in[k]}\left\|{{\bf x}^{(i,r)}-{\bf w}^{(i,c^{\prime})}}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+\alpha\sum_{i^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}^{(i)}}\sum_{c\in[k]}\bigg(\min_{c^{\prime}\in[k]}\left\|{{\bf w}^{(i,c^{\prime})}\!-\!\mathbf{w}^{(i^{\prime},c)}}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\!+\!\underbrace{\min_{c^{\prime}\in[k]}\left\|{{\bf w}^{(i^{\prime},c^{\prime})}\!-\!{\bf w}^{(i,c)}}\right\|_{2}^{2}}_{\star}\bigg).

(8)

The update (
III
) bears some resemblance to the
k
-means
problem (
2
). In particular, for
\alpha=1
and without the component

\star
, the update (
III
) would become exactly a
k
-means
problem (
2
) on the augmented local dataset

\bigg\{{\bf x}^{(i,1)},\ldots,{\bf x}^{(i,m_{i})}\bigg\}\bigcup\bigg\{{\bf w}^{(i^{\prime},c)}\bigg\}_{i^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}^{(i)},c\in[k]}.

(9)

implements a Lloyd-style iterative procedure
to approximately solve the update (

to the nearest local cluster centroid and aligning cluster centroids with those of

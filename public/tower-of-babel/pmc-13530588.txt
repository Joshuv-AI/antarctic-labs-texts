Poster 103. Suture Augmentation May Be Necessary for Patients with Generalized Ligamentous Laxity Undergoing Modified Brostrom Repair: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
Authors: Jared Rubin, John G. Kennedy, Alexander Tham, Mark Pianka, Michelle Richardson, Reed Macey
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:The purpose of this systematic review and meta-analysis is to evaluate the radiographic findings, clinical outcomes, complications, failures, and reoperation rates in patients with generalized ligamentous laxity (GLL) who undergo modified Brostrom repair.Methods:During July 2025, the PubMed, EMBASE, and Cochrane library databases were systematically reviewed to identify clinical studies examining outcomes and complications in GLL patients who underwent anterior talofibular ligament (ATFL) repair with the modified Brostrom technique.Results:Eight studies comprising of 301 patients (309 ankles) with GLL and 367 patients (370 ankles) without ligamentous laxity who underwent ATFL repair with the modified Brostrom technique were included. Both GLL patients and patients without ligamentous laxity demonstrated improvements in clinical outcome measures. A statistically significant difference in post-operative talar tilt angle between cohorts only existed when comparing GLL patients who received modified Brostrom without suture augmentation and patients without ligamentous laxity (MD 2.18, 95% CI 0.57-3.79, p = 0.008). Patients with GLL experienced 46 (14.9%) failures after ATFL repair and were 4.67 times more likely to have recurrent pain or instability compared to patients without ligamentous laxity (RR 4.67, 95% CI 2.10-10.38; p = 0.0002). Furthermore, GLL patients who did not undergo suture augmentation were 2.95 times more likely to have a complication from their procedure compared to patients without ligamentous laxity (RR 2.95, 95% CI 1.08-8.01; p = 0.03). However, when including GLL patients who underwent suture augmentation, there was no significant difference in total complication rates between GLL patients and patients without ligamentous laxity.Conclusions:Patients with GLL experience significantly higher rates of complications and failures following modified Brostrom repair compared to patients without ligamentous laxity, particularly when suture augmentation is not utilized. Importantly, both GLL patients and patients without ligamentous laxity experience clinical improvements from the modified Brostrom repair. While GLL increases the risk of mechanical complications, it should not be considered a contraindication to undergoing the modified Brostrom procedure. However, suture augmentation may be necessary for GLL patients to optimize surgical outcomes and reduce the risk of postoperative complications.

Asymptotic Properties of a Special Solution to the (3,4) String Equation
Nathan Hayford

Abstract
We analyze the asymptotic properties a special solution of the ( 3 , 4 ) (3,4) string equation, which appears in the study of the multicritical quartic 2 2 -matrix model. In particular, we show that in a certain parameter regime, the corresponding τ \tau -function has an asymptotic expansion which is ‘topological’ in nature. Consequently, we show that this solution to the string equation with a specific set of Stokes data exists, at least asymptotically. We also demonstrate that, along specific curves in the parameter space, this τ \tau -function degenerates to the τ \tau -function for a tritronquée solution of Painlevé I (which appears in the critical quartic 1 1 -matrix model), indicating that there is a ‘renormalization group flow’ between these critical points. This confirms a conjecture from [ CGM90 ] .



1. Introduction.

In this work, we will study the asymptotic properties of a special solution to the
(3,4)
string equation
:

\begin{cases}0=\frac{1}{2}V^{\prime\prime}-\frac{3}{2}UV+\frac{5}{2}t_{5}V+t_{2},\\
0=\frac{1}{12}U^{(4)}-\frac{3}{4}U^{\prime\prime}U-\frac{3}{8}(U^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{3}{2}V^{2}+\frac{1}{2}U^{3}-\frac{5}{12}t_{5}\left(3U^{2}-U^{\prime\prime}\right)+t_{1}.\end{cases}

(1.1)

In the above,
U=U(t_{5},t_{2},t_{1})
,
V=V(t_{5},t_{2},t_{1})
and
{}^{\prime}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}
. The above is an ordinary differential
equation depending parametrically on the variables
t_{5},t_{2}
. This equation first appeared in the study of the critical
2
-matrix
model
[
CGM90
,
Dou90
]
. Essentially, the main message of these works was that the critical partition function for the
2
-matrix model
under study converges in a multi-scaling limit to a
\tau
-function for (
1.1
). This
\tau
-function then can be interpreted as a
partition function for a theory of topological gravity coupled to the Ising conformal field theory
[
Kri94
,
DKK93
]
. A rigorous proof of this
statement was finally made in
[
DHL25
]
. However, the properties of this solution (even its existence!) have yet to be studied.

One property of the string equation (
1.1
) is that it has the
Painlevé property
: its meromorphic solutions have
as possible singularities only movable poles and/or fixed branch points. This property is the defining feature of the original
6
Painlevé equations, and
is also shared by solutions to the Painlevé hierarchies. Painlevé I – VI all admit a
(nonautonomous) Hamiltonian formulation
[
Oka80
]
, as well as a representation as the isomonodromic deformations of linear differential equations
with rational coefficients
[
JM81
,
Fok+06
]
. The same is true for both the Painlevé I and II hierarchies (cf.
[
Tak07
,
MM07
,
CJM06
]
and
references therein). As a consequence of this fact and the seminal works
[
JMU81
,
JM81
,
JM81a
]
, we can associate to each solution of these equations an
isomonodromic
\tau
-function
, an object which will play an important role in the present work.
Furthermore, Painlevé transcendents appear frequently in the context of random matrix theory. Special solutions to the Painlevé I and II hierarchies appear in the universal expressions for critical eigenvalue correlation kernels in random matrix theory
[
IB03
,
CV07a
]
, and the partition functions for
random matrix models converge (after appropriate normalization) to
\tau
-functions of Painlevé transcendents (see
[
BD16
]
, and further
conjectures of this claim from physics
[
Gin+90
,
FGZ95
]
). Painlevé equations have also found numerous applications in integrable systems
[
Dub06
,
CV07
]
and combinatorics
[
BDJ99
,
BD16
]
, among other areas of mathematics and mathematical physics.

All of the aforementioned examples of Painlevé-type equations are examples of
rank-2 isomonodromic systems
: they can be realized as isomonodromic
deformations of some
2\times 2
matrix-valued linear differential equation with rational coefficients. Aside from some general theory
[
JMU81
,
BHH23
]
, not
much is known about higher-rank isomonodromic systems, although historically many such systems are of interest to the random matrix theory community. For example, the so-called
(q,p)
string equations are conjectured to play a role in the classification of universality classes of critical phenomenona in multi-matrix models
[
Dou90
,
GM90
,
Bré+90
]
. These equations arise from rank
q
isomonodromic systems, for arbitrary
q\geq 2
. This can be contrasted to the
much thinner spectrum of critical phenomena in the
1
-matrix model, which are well understood to be indexed by the Painlevé I and II hierarchies, which correspond to the
(2,2k+1)
and
(2,2k)
families of string equations, respectively.

Riemann-Hilbert Problem 1.1
.

Define contours

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\pm k}:=\left\{\lambda\big|\arg\lambda=\pm\frac{\pi}{14}\pm\frac{\pi}{7}(k-1)\right\},\qquad k=1,...,7,

\RR_{-}:=(-\infty,0)

\begin{cases}\Psi_{+}(\zeta;t_{5},t_{2},t_{1})=\Psi_{-}(\zeta;t_{5},t_{2},t_{1})S_{k},&\zeta\in\Gamma_{k},\qquad k=\pm 1,...,\pm 7,\\
\Psi_{+}(\zeta;t_{5},t_{2},t_{1})=\Psi_{-}(\zeta;t_{5},t_{2},t_{1})\mathcal{S},&\zeta\in\RR_{-},\\
\Psi(\zeta;t_{5},t_{2},t_{1})=f(\zeta)\left[\mathbb{I}+\frac{\Psi_{1}}{\zeta^{1/3}}+\frac{\Psi_{2}}{\zeta^{2/3}}+\OO(\zeta^{-1})\right]e^{\Theta(\zeta;t_{5},t_{2},t_{1})},&\zeta\to\infty,\end{cases}

(1.4)

\Theta(\zeta;t_{5},t_{2},t_{1}):=\text{diag }(\vartheta_{1}(\zeta;t_{5},t_{2},t_{1}),\vartheta_{2}(\zeta;t_{5},t_{2},t_{1}),\vartheta_{3}(\zeta;t_{5},t_{2},t_{1})),

(1.5)

, the jump matrices
S_{k}
are given in Figure (

is

\mathcal{S}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
0&0&1\\
1&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

(1.6)

These matrices must satisfy the following constraint equation (stokes equation):

S_{-7}\cdots S_{-1}S_{1}\cdots S_{7}=\mathcal{S}^{T}.

(1.7)

The matrices
\Psi_{j}:=\Psi_{j}(t_{5},t_{2},t_{1})

\displaystyle\Psi_{1}(t_{5},t_{2},t_{1})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}H_{1}&0&0\\
0&\omega^{2}H_{1}&0\\

(1.8)

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}(t_{5},t_{2},t_{1})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}(H_{1})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}H_{2}&-\frac{i\omega^{2}\sqrt{3}}{12}U&\frac{i\omega\sqrt{3}}{12}U\\

where

1.1. Isomonodromic
\tau
-function.

Our main theorems will be stated in terms of the so-called
isomonodromic
\tau
-function
of Jimbo, Miwa, and Ueno
[
JMU81
]
.
This is a holomorphic function which is defined in terms of the local data of the RHP
1.1
. Usually, the zero locus of a

\tau
-function is precisely the set on which its corresponding Riemann-Hilbert problem is not solvable
[
Pal99
,
Mal83
]
. Furthermore,
solutions to the string equation (
1.1
) can be written as derivatives of the
\tau
-function, and in this sense the

\tau
-function is a fundamental object. However, as pointed out in
[
Hay24
]
, the definition used in
[
JMU81
]
(which we will refer to as the JMU
\tau
function, and its differential as the JMU
differential
) does not directly
apply to the present situation, as the linear differential equation with rational coefficients associated to
1.1
does not
have a diagonalizable leading term. Construction of
\tau
-functions for systems with nondiagonalizable leading terms resonant singularities is a
problem that has been studied before
[
BM05
]
. In
[
Hay24
]
, an alternative (albeit less general) formula for such a
\tau
-function was
introduced. The advantage of this formula is that it is amenable to steepest descent analysis, as it involves only data derivable from the
Riemann-Hilbert problem.

We recall this expression from
[
Hay24
]
in the present context here.

Definition 1.1
.

The isomonodromic
\tau
-function associated to Problem (
1.1
) is given by

{\bf d}\log\tau(t_{5},t_{2},t_{1})=-\sum_{\ell}\left\langle\mathfrak{G}^{-1}(\zeta;{\bf t})\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}(\zeta;{\bf t})\frac{\partial\Theta(\zeta;{\bf t})}{\partial t_{\ell}}\right\rangle-\left\langle\frac{\Delta}{\zeta}\frac{\partial\mathfrak{G}}{\partial t_{\ell}}(\zeta;{\bf t})\mathfrak{G}^{-1}(\zeta;{\bf t})\right\rangle dt_{\ell},

(1.12)

where
\langle M(\zeta)\rangle:=\displaystyle\Ress_{\zeta=\infty}\tr M(\zeta)
,
\mathfrak{G}(\zeta;{\bf t})
is the subexponential part of the asymptotic expansion of
\Psi(\zeta;{\bf t})
as defined in (
1.3
):

\mathfrak{G}(\zeta;{\bf t})=\Psi(\zeta;t_{5},t_{2},t_{1})e^{-\Theta(\zeta;t_{5},t_{2},t_{1})},

(1.13)

\Delta:=\text{diag }(\frac{1}{3},0,-\frac{1}{3})
is as defined in (
1.3
), and
{\bf d}
is the differential in the parameters
{\bf t}=(t_{5},t_{2},t_{1})
.

Note that the first term defines the usual JMU differential; the second term, which arises from the resonance of the singularity is a “correction” term.
A theorem of
[
Hay24
]
states that this differential is closed, and thus can be integrated (up to an overall constant factor independent of
{\bf t}
) to a
unique function, up to an additive constant. We shall refer to this class of functions (with the constant of integration left ambiguous) simply as the
\tau
-function, by a slight abuse of notation.

The functions
U
,
V
are then expressible as derivatives of
\log\tau(t_{5},t_{2},t_{1})
:

U=-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t_{1}^{2}}\log\tau(t_{5},t_{2},t_{1}),\qquad\qquad V=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t_{1}\partial t_{2}}\log\tau(t_{5},t_{2},t_{1}).

(1.14)

2. Preliminary Transformations.

An issue that arises in the asymptotic analysis of the RHP
1.1
is that its solution cannot be determined uniquely without reference
to a certain number of subleading terms in its asymptotic expansion. This causes problems in that these subleading terms involve functions we are
trying to find asymptotics for in the first place, and so we are seemingly stuck in a redundant loop. However, a certain property of the

\tau
-function
1.12
allows us to circumvent this issue.

A key property of the above modified
\tau
-function is that it is

: we can multiply

on the left
by any upper-triangular matrix-valued function

on the diagonal, and the

In other words, if we let

Define the gauge matrix

\begin{cases}\hat{\Psi}_{+}(\zeta;t_{5},t_{2},t_{1})=\hat{\Psi}_{-}(\zeta;t_{5},t_{2},t_{1})S_{k},&\zeta\in\Gamma_{k},\qquad k=\pm 1,...,\pm 7,\\

In this section, we prove Theorems

Our goal is now to perform a Deift-Zhou steepest descent analysis for the RHP
{\bf Z}(\lambda;\eta,\mu,\nu|\hbar)
, with
\hbar
playing the role of the small parameter. This analysis is by now fairly standard. The first relevant transformation
will be the one which removes the exponential part of the asymptotics; this is typically called the ‘

3.1. Spectral curve and construction of the
g
-function.

We must construct a function
g(\lambda)
, which is defined on a
3
-sheeted Riemann surface, and whose restriction
g_{j}(\lambda)
to sheet

satisfies

g_{j}(\lambda)=\hat{\Theta}_{jj}(\lambda;\eta,\mu,\nu)+\OO(\lambda^{-1/3}),\qquad\lambda\to\infty,

and the

satisfy certain inequalities on the branch cuts, which we will describe in more detail below.
The fact that the Riemann surface is
3
-sheeted complicates matters. At the present time, the authors do not know an efficient way to treat this problem in general, and so we settle on searching for solutions to the above problem under the assumption that
the Riemann surface of
g(\lambda)
is of genus
0
. In this case, this surface can be resolved in terms of rational functions, and we can solve the above problem concretely, albeit with some restrictions on the values of the parameters
\eta,\mu,\nu
. We have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
a,b,c
be real parameters. Define polynomials

\lambda(u):=u^{3}-3a^{2}u+c,\qquad\qquad Y(u):=u^{4}-bu^{2}+\frac{4}{3}cu-6a^{4}+2a^{2}b,

(3.1)

and put

\eta:=\frac{3}{5}(4a^{2}-b),\qquad\mu:=c(b-2a^{2}),\qquad\nu:=8a^{6}-3a^{4}b-\frac{2}{3}c^{2},

(3.2)

and

\displaystyle g(u)

\displaystyle:=\frac{3}{7}u^{7}-\frac{3}{5}(b+a^{2})u^{5}+cu^{4}-a^{2}(6a^{2}+b)u^{3}-2a^{2}cu^{2}-3a^{2}(-6a^{4}-2a^{2}b)u-2ca^{4}

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\int Y(u)\lambda^{\prime}(u)du-2ca^{4}.

Let

be a solution to the equation
\lambda(u)=\lambda
which satisfies
u^{*}(\lambda)=\omega^{j-1}\lambda^{1/3}+\OO(\lambda^{-1/3})
,
\lambda\to\infty

\displaystyle+\underbrace{e^{-\frac{1}{\hbar}(G-\hat{\Theta})(\lambda)}M^{-1}(\lambda){\bf R}^{-1}(\lambda){\bf R}^{\prime}(\lambda)M(\lambda)e^{\frac{1}{\hbar}(G-\hat{\Theta})(\lambda)}}_{K_{3}(\lambda)}

One can readily check that

\frac{1}{\hbar}\tr\left[K_{2}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\eta}\right]=\frac{1}{\hbar}\tr\left[K_{2}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\mu}\right]=\frac{1}{\hbar}\tr\left[K_{2}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\nu}\right]=\OO(\lambda^{-3}),\qquad\lambda\to\infty,

and so this term does not contribute to the
\tau
-function, since it is residueless. Furthermore, it is easy to see that

\frac{1}{\hbar}\tr\left[K_{3}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\eta}\right]=\frac{1}{\hbar}\tr\left[K_{3}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\mu}\right]=\frac{1}{\hbar}\tr\left[K_{3}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\nu}\right]=\OO(1),\qquad\hbar\to 0.

So, the leading contribution to the
\tau
-function arises from the term involving
K_{1}(\lambda)
. By direct calculation, we obtain that

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\hbar}\displaystyle\Ress_{\lambda=\infty}\tr\left[K_{1}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\eta}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\hbar^{2}}h_{5}^{(0)},

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\hbar}\displaystyle\Ress_{\lambda=\infty}\tr\left[K_{1}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\mu}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\hbar^{2}}h_{2}^{(0)},

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\hbar}\displaystyle\Ress_{\lambda=\infty}\tr\left[K_{1}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\nu}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\hbar^{2}}h_{1}^{(0)}.

All terms of subleading order in
\hbar
then arise from
K_{3}(\lambda)
. From our calculations in Proposition
3.9
(see in particular Equation
(
4.38
)), we have that

\displaystyle{\bf R}^{-1}(\lambda){\bf R}^{\prime}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\hbar{\bf R}_{1}^{\prime}(\lambda)+\OO(\hbar^{2}),\qquad\hbar\to 0,\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle\hbar{\bf R}_{1}^{\prime}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\hbar(W_{1}+\hat{W}_{1})}{\lambda^{2}}-\frac{2\hbar(\beta W_{1}+\alpha\hat{W_{1}})}{\lambda^{3}}+\OO(\lambda^{-4}),\qquad\lambda\to\infty.

On the other hand, as
\lambda\to\infty
,

\displaystyle M(\lambda)e^{\frac{1}{\hbar}(G-\hat{\Theta})(\lambda)}\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\nu}e^{-\frac{1}{\hbar}(G-\hat{\Theta})(\lambda)}M^{-1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=M(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\nu}M^{-1}(\lambda)=E_{13}\lambda+\OO(1),

\displaystyle M(\lambda)e^{\frac{1}{\hbar}(G-\hat{\Theta})(\lambda)}\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\mu}e^{-\frac{1}{\hbar}(G-\hat{\Theta})(\lambda)}M^{-1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=M(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\mu}M^{-1}(\lambda)=(E_{12}+E_{23})\lambda+\OO(1),

\displaystyle M(\lambda)e^{\frac{1}{\hbar}(G-\hat{\Theta})(\lambda)}\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\eta}e^{-\frac{1}{\hbar}(G-\hat{\Theta})(\lambda)}M^{-1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=M(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\eta}M^{-1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=(E_{12}+E_{23})\lambda^{2}+\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{4}\varsigma&\frac{\mu}{5\eta-3\varsigma}&\frac{5}{16}\varsigma^{2}\\
0&\frac{1}{2}\varsigma&\frac{\mu}{5\eta-3\varsigma}\\
1&0&-\frac{1}{4}\varsigma\end{pmatrix}\lambda+\OO(1),

so that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\hbar}\displaystyle\Ress_{\lambda=\infty}\tr\left[K_{3}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\nu}\right]

\displaystyle=-\tr\left(E_{13}(W_{1}+\hat{W}_{1})\right)+\OO(\hbar),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\hbar}\displaystyle\Ress_{\lambda=\infty}\tr\left[K_{3}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\mu}\right]

\displaystyle=-\tr\left((E_{12}+E_{23})(W_{1}+\hat{W}_{1})\right)+\OO(\hbar),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\hbar}\displaystyle\Ress_{\lambda=\infty}\tr\left[K_{3}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\eta}\right]

\displaystyle=-2\tr\left((E_{12}+E_{23})(\beta W_{1}+\alpha\hat{W}_{1})\right)-\tr\left((W_{1}+\hat{W}_{1})\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{4}\varsigma&\frac{\mu}{5\eta-3\varsigma}&\frac{5}{16}\varsigma^{2}\\
0&\frac{1}{2}\varsigma&\frac{\mu}{5\eta-3\varsigma}\\
1&0&-\frac{1}{4}\varsigma\end{pmatrix}\right)+\OO(\hbar).

Evaluation of these residues gives that

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\hbar}\displaystyle\Ress_{\lambda=\infty}\tr\left[K_{3}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\nu}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}h^{(1)}_{1}+\OO(\hbar),

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\hbar}\displaystyle\Ress_{\lambda=\infty}\tr\left[K_{3}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\mu}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}h^{(1)}_{2}+\OO(\hbar),

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\hbar}\displaystyle\Ress_{\lambda=\infty}\tr\left[K_{3}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\eta}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}h^{(1)}_{5}+\OO(\hbar).

∎

An almost immediate consequence of the above results is that the
\tau
-differential admits a topological expansion.

Corollary 3.1
.

Theorem
1.2
holds.

Proof.

From the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we see that
{\bf d}\log\tau(\hbar^{-2/7}\eta,\hbar^{-5/7}\mu,\hbar^{-6/7}\nu)\sim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\omega_{k}\hbar^{k}
, for some differentials
\omega_{k}
independent of

are expressible as derivatives of

(3.38)

for some functions
a_{k}(\eta,\mu,\nu)
,
b_{k}(\eta,\mu,\nu)
. Our goal is to show that

this would imply that

have asymptotic expansions in powers of
\hbar^{2}
, and thus the formal calculations of Appendix
B
are valid.
Inserting the expansions (
3.38
) into the rescaled string equation (
B.2
), an explicit calculation shows that

a_{1}=a_{3}=0,\qquad\qquad b_{1}=b_{3}=0.

In general, at order
\hbar^{N}
for

we obtain from the string equation that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}\left(18b_{\ell}b_{N-\ell}-15\eta a_{\ell}a_{N-\ell}+6\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}a_{N-\ell}a_{\ell-j}a_{j}\right)-\frac{9}{2}\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-2}\left(2a_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}a_{N-\ell-2}-a^{\prime}_{\ell}a^{\prime}_{N-\ell-2}\right)+5\eta a_{N-2}^{\prime\prime}+a^{(4)}_{N-4},

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-3\sum_{k=0}^{N}b_{k}a_{N-k}+5\eta b_{N}+b_{N-2}^{\prime\prime}.

Let

be odd, and assume the inductive hypothesis that
a_{2\ell+1}=b_{2\ell+1}=0
for all

Observe the following facts:

•

If

is odd and

N-\ell
is odd or
\ell
is odd,

•

If
N
is odd,
\ell,j\in\ZZ_{+}
, then one of

,
\ell-j

is odd.

The above facts imply that many terms in the order

The order

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=3b_{0}b_{N}-\frac{1}{2}a_{0}(5\eta-3a_{0})a_{N},

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-3b_{0}a_{N}-3b_{N}a_{0}+5\eta b_{N}.

We can express this system as the matrix equation

\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{2}a_{0}(5\eta-3a_{0})&3b_{0}\\
-3b_{0}&5\eta-3a_{0}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a_{N}\\

and so

4. Painlevé I asymptotics.

As we observed in Section
3.1
, as
(\eta,\mu,\nu)
tend to a point on the critical surface, the spectral curve
degenerates, and we expect to see Painlevé I-type asymptotics. In this section, we will address the double-scaling limit of the
Riemann-Hilbert problem for
\Psi
in the vicinity of this Painlevé I regime. For simplicity, from here on we address criticality

only in the case when

\mu=0,\qquad\qquad\eta\neq 0.

(4.1)

There are thus two interesting critical regimes to consider:

\eta>0,\quad\nu=\frac{125}{108}\eta^{3},\qquad\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\qquad\eta<0,\quad\nu=0.

(4.2)

These cases correspond to the critical curves
\gamma_{+}
,
\gamma_{-}
introduced earlier.
In both cases, as is typical for double-scaling limits of this type (cf.
[
DK06
]
, for instance), a modified spectral curve must
be constructed. From then on the Deift-Zhou analysis is effectively identical to what appears in the previous section, up until the
construction of local parametrices. We calculate the modified spectral curve for the cases
\eta>0
,
\eta<0
in subsection

.

For all intents and purposes, one can think that the essential differences between this section and the previous one are as follows:

•

An appearance of the function

in a transformation in Section §2 is replaced by the equivalent transformation here, with
\hat{g}_{j}(\lambda)

substituted for
g_{j}(\lambda)
,

•

The local parametrices are no longer of Airy type, and must be constructed by other means.

The modifications necessary are by now well-established in the literature: we must construct a modified spectral curve, and use local
parametrices which involve the Painlevé I equation.

\mathfrak{p}(\lambda):=\mathbb{I}-\hbar^{1/5}\frac{\mathcal{H}(x)E_{31}}{c\lambda}+\hbar^{2/5}\frac{(\mathcal{H}^{2}(x)-q(x))(E_{32}-E_{21})}{2c^{2}\lambda}-\hbar^{4/5}\frac{(\mathcal{H}^{2}(x)-q(x))^{2}E_{31}}{8c^{4}\lambda^{2}},

(4.55)

where
c:=\left(5s/6\right)^{1/5}>0
.
Clearly, the above has determinant
1
, and furthermore is analytic in
\CC\setminus D_{\hbar}
. Finally, we define the modified global parametrix

M(\lambda):=\mathfrak{p}(\lambda)\hat{f}(\lambda),\qquad\lambda\in\CC\setminus D_{\hbar}.

(4.56)

We then claim that

Proposition 4.11
.

\bm{\Delta}(\lambda):=P(\lambda)M^{-1}(\lambda)-\mathbb{I}
is uniformly bounded on
|\lambda|=\hbar^{2/5+\delta}
, for any sufficiently small
\delta>0
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
, we have that

P(\lambda)M^{-1}(\lambda)=\hat{f}(\lambda)\left[\mathbb{I}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Xi_{k}(\bm{x}(\lambda))}{\zeta(\lambda)^{k/3}}\hbar^{k/5}\right]\hat{f}^{-1}(\lambda)\mathfrak{p}^{-1}(\lambda),\qquad\hbar\to 0.

Rewrite this expression as

\hat{P}(\lambda)\hat{f}^{-1}(\lambda)=\underbrace{\hat{f}(\lambda)\left[\mathbb{I}+\sum_{k=1}^{4}\frac{\Xi_{k}(\bm{x}(\lambda))}{\zeta(\lambda)^{k/3}}\hbar^{k/5}\right]\hat{f}^{-1}(\lambda)\mathfrak{p}^{-1}(\lambda)}_{(A)}+\underbrace{\hat{f}(\lambda)\left[\sum_{k=5}^{\infty}\frac{\Xi_{k}(\bm{x}(\lambda))}{\zeta(\lambda)^{k/3}}\hbar^{k/5}\right]\hat{f}^{-1}(\lambda)\mathfrak{p}^{-1}(\lambda)}_{(B)}.

We begin by showing that
(B)=\OO(\hbar^{\frac{1}{5}-3\delta})
, so that taking
\delta>0
sufficiently small, the first term is bounded in
\hbar
.
By the symmetry properties of the coefficients
\Xi_{k}
(
D.8
), we have that

\displaystyle\hbar^{(3\ell+1)/5}\frac{\hat{f}(\lambda)\Xi_{3\ell+1}(\bm{x}(\lambda))\hat{f}^{-1}(\lambda)}{\zeta(\lambda)^{(3\ell+1)/3}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&\OO(\hbar^{\frac{1}{5}+\ell(\frac{1}{5}-\delta)})&0\\
0&0&\OO(\hbar^{\frac{1}{5}+\ell(\frac{1}{5}-\delta)})\\
\OO(\hbar^{-\frac{2}{5}+(\ell+1)(\frac{1}{5}-\delta)})&0&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\hbar^{(3\ell+2)/5}\frac{\hat{f}(\lambda)\Xi_{3\ell+2}(\bm{x}(\lambda))\hat{f}^{-1}(\lambda)}{\zeta(\lambda)^{(3\ell+2)/3}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\OO(\hbar^{\frac{2}{5}+\ell(\frac{1}{5}-\delta)})\\
\OO(\hbar^{-\frac{1}{5}+(\ell+1)(\frac{1}{5}-\delta)})&0&0\\
0&\OO(\hbar^{-\frac{1}{5}+(\ell+1)(\frac{1}{5}-\delta)})&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\hbar^{3\ell/5}\frac{\hat{f}(\lambda)\Xi_{3\ell}(\bm{x}(\lambda))\hat{f}^{-1}(\lambda)}{\zeta(\lambda)^{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\OO(\hbar^{\ell(\frac{1}{5}-\delta)})&0&0\\
0&\OO(\hbar^{\ell(\frac{1}{5}-\delta)})&0\\
0&0&\OO(\hbar^{\ell(\frac{1}{5}-\delta)})\end{pmatrix}.

On the other hand,

\mathfrak{p}(\lambda)=\mathbb{I}+\OO(\hbar^{-\frac{1}{5}-\delta})\cdot E_{31}+\OO(\hbar^{-\delta})\cdot(E_{21}+E_{32}),\qquad\hbar\to 0.

It thus follows that

(B)=\hat{f}(\lambda)\left[\sum_{k=5}^{\infty}\frac{\Xi_{k}(\bm{x}(\lambda))}{\zeta(\lambda)^{k/3}}\hbar^{k/5}\right]\hat{f}^{-1}(\lambda)\mathfrak{p}^{-1}(\lambda)=\OO(\hbar^{\frac{1}{5}-3\delta}),

so for
\delta>0
sufficiently small, the above quantity is bounded as
\hbar\to 0
. Let us now turn our attention to
(A)
. By construction, we have
chosen the entries of
\mathfrak{p}(\lambda)
to ‘kill off’ the growing terms in this expression. A tedious but straightforward calculation then shows
that

(A)=\hat{f}(\lambda)\left[\sum_{k=1}^{4}\frac{\Xi_{k}(\bm{x}(\lambda))}{\zeta(\lambda)^{k/3}}\hbar^{k/5}\right]\hat{f}^{-1}(\lambda)\mathfrak{p}^{-1}(\lambda)=\mathbb{I}+\OO(\hbar^{\frac{1}{5}-3\delta}),

and so

\bm{\Delta}(\lambda)=P(\lambda)M^{-1}(\lambda)-\mathbb{I}=(A)+(B)-\mathbb{I}=\OO(\hbar^{\frac{1}{5}-3\delta}).

∎

Finally, defining

\hat{{\bf R}}(\lambda):=\hat{{\bf V}}(\lambda)\begin{cases}M^{-1}(\lambda),&\lambda\in\CC\setminus D_{\hbar},\\
P^{-1}(\lambda),&\lambda\in D_{\hbar},\end{cases}

(4.57)

where
M(\lambda)
is as defined in Equation (
4.56
), we can now claim that
\hat{{\bf R}}(\lambda)
is indeed close to the identity matrix:

Proposition 4.12
.

Given
s:=-\eta_{0}>0
, and for
\hbar
sufficiently small, with
\hat{\nu}(\hbar)
as defined in Equation (
4.48
),
the Riemann-Hilbert problem for
\hat{{\bf R}}(\lambda)
has a solution, and admits the
\hbar\to 0
asymptotic expansion

\hat{{\bf R}}(\lambda)\sim\mathbb{I}+\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}{\bf R}_{k}(\lambda;x)\hbar^{k/5},

(4.58)

which holds uniformly in
\CC\setminus\Gamma_{{\bf R}}
.

Proof.

Let

\bm{\Delta}(\lambda):=\hat{{\bf R}}_{-}^{-1}\hat{{\bf R}}_{+}-\mathbb{I}

denote the deviation of the jumps of
{\bf R}
from the identity
\mathbb{I}
.
\bm{\Delta}(\lambda)
is exponentially close to the identity matrix
away from the circle
\partial D_{\hbar}
. By Proposition
4.11
, we can see that

\bm{\Delta}(\lambda)\sim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}(\lambda)\hbar^{k/5},

where the
J_{k}(\lambda)
are explicit matrices defined in terms of the function
\mathfrak{p}(\lambda)

and coefficients
\Xi_{k}(x)
appearing in the asymptotic expansion of
\bm{\Xi}(\zeta;x)
(see Equation (
D.12
)).
From standard theory
[
Dei+99
]
it follows that
{\bf R}
admits an asymptotic series in
\hbar^{1/5}
:

{\bf R}(\lambda)\sim\mathbb{I}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{\bf R}_{k}(\lambda;x)\hbar^{k/5}.

The functions
{\bf R}_{k}(\lambda;x)
can be determined iteratively as the solutions to certain additive Riemann-Hilbert problems. Since both
J_{1}(\lambda)
,

J_{2}(\lambda)
are analytic functions in
D_{\hbar}
, it follows that

{\bf R}_{1}(\lambda;x)={\bf R}_{2}(\lambda;x)=0,\qquad\lambda\in\CC\setminus D_{\hbar}.

(4.59)

Thus,
{\bf R}(\lambda)=\mathbb{I}+\OO(\hbar^{3/5})
, for
\lambda\in\CC\setminus D_{\hbar}
.
∎

4.4. Proof of Theorems
1.3
&
1.4
.

Proof.

(Of Theorem
1.3
.)

Using the results of Subsection
4.2
, and by choosing an appropriate sector in which
\lambda\to\infty
, we can write

\mathfrak{G}(\lambda)={\bf Z}(\lambda;{\bf t})e^{-\frac{1}{\hbar}\hat{\Theta}(\lambda;{\bf t})}=\mathfrak{h}^{(0)}{\bf R}(\lambda)\cdot M(\lambda)e^{\frac{1}{\hbar}(\hat{G}-\hat{\Theta})(\lambda)},

so that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}^{-1}(\lambda)\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{e^{-\frac{1}{\hbar}(G-\hat{\Theta})(\lambda)}\frac{d}{d\lambda}\left[e^{\frac{1}{\hbar}(G-\hat{\Theta})(\lambda)}\right]}_{K_{1}(\lambda)}+\underbrace{e^{-\frac{1}{\hbar}(G-\hat{\Theta})(\lambda)}M^{-1}(\lambda)M^{\prime}(\lambda)e^{\frac{1}{\hbar}(G-\hat{\Theta})(\lambda)}}_{K_{2}(\lambda)}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{e^{-\frac{1}{\hbar}(G-\hat{\Theta})(\lambda)}M^{-1}(\lambda){\bf R}^{-1}(\lambda){\bf R}^{\prime}(\lambda)M(\lambda)e^{\frac{1}{\hbar}(G-\hat{\Theta})(\lambda)}}_{K_{3}(\lambda)}.

We have that

\frac{1}{\hbar}\tr\left[K_{2}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\eta}\right]=\frac{1}{\hbar}\tr\left[K_{2}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\nu}\right]=\OO(\lambda^{-3}),

so the terms involving
K_{2}(\lambda)
do not contribute to the
\tau
-function. Now, calculating the terms involving
K_{1}(\lambda)
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\hbar}\displaystyle\Ress_{\lambda=\infty}\tr\left[K_{1}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\nu}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{5\eta_{0}}{6\hbar^{2}}\left[\hat{\nu}(\hbar)-\frac{125}{108}\eta_{0}^{2}\hat{\eta}(\hbar)+\frac{125}{216}\eta_{0}^{3}\right],

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\hbar}\displaystyle\Ress_{\lambda=\infty}\tr\left[K_{1}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\eta}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{625}{648\hbar^{2}}\eta_{0}^{3}\left[\hat{\nu}(\hbar)-\frac{25}{12}\eta_{0}^{2}\hat{\eta}(\hbar)+\frac{125}{108}\eta_{0}^{3}\right].

Comparing with the definition of
\hat{\tau}_{0}(\eta,0,\nu)
(
1.23
), we see that the differential of
\hat{\tau}_{0}(\eta,0,\nu)
cancels
exactly the differential formed by
K_{1}(\lambda)
:

\frac{1}{\hbar}\displaystyle\Ress_{\lambda=\infty}\tr\left[K_{1}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\eta}\right]d\hat{\eta}(\hbar)+\frac{1}{\hbar}\displaystyle\Ress_{\lambda=\infty}\tr\left[K_{1}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\nu}\right]d\hat{\nu}(\hbar)+{\bf d}\hat{\tau}_{0}(\hat{\eta},0,\hat{\nu}|\hbar)=0.

Terms of subleading order in
\hbar
then arise from
K_{3}(\lambda)
. By the results of Proposition
4.6
,
we have that

\displaystyle{\bf R}^{-1}(\lambda){\bf R}^{\prime}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\hbar^{1/5}{\bf R}_{1}^{\prime}(\lambda)+\OO(\hbar^{2/5}),\qquad\hbar\to 0,\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle\hbar^{1/5}{\bf R}_{1}^{\prime}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\hbar^{1/5}(W_{1}+\hat{W}_{1})}{\lambda^{2}}-\frac{2\hbar^{1/5}\alpha(\hat{W_{1}}-W_{1})}{\lambda^{3}}+\OO(\lambda^{-4}),\qquad\lambda\to\infty.

On the other hand, as
\lambda\to\infty
,

\displaystyle M(\lambda)e^{\frac{1}{\hbar}(\hat{G}-\hat{\Theta})(\lambda)}\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\nu}e^{-\frac{1}{\hbar}(\hat{G}-\hat{\Theta})(\lambda)}M^{-1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=M(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\nu}M^{-1}(\lambda)=E_{13}\lambda+\OO(1),

\displaystyle M(\lambda)e^{\frac{1}{\hbar}(\hat{G}-\hat{\Theta})(\lambda)}\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\eta}e^{-\frac{1}{\hbar}(\hat{G}-\hat{\Theta})(\lambda)}M^{-1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=M(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\eta}M^{-1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=(E_{12}+E_{23})\lambda^{2}+\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{5}{12}\eta_{0}&0&\frac{125}{144}\eta_{0}^{2}\\
0&\frac{5}{6}\eta_{0}&0\\
1&0&-\frac{5}{12}\eta_{0}\end{pmatrix}\lambda+\OO(1),

and so we compute that

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\hbar}\tr\left[K_{3}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\nu}\right]d\hat{\nu}(\hbar)

\displaystyle=-Cn_{\nu}\hbar^{-1/5}\tr\left[K_{3}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\nu}\right]dx

\displaystyle=Cn_{\nu}\left(\frac{3}{10\eta_{0}}\right)^{1/5}\mathcal{H}(x)dx+\OO(\hbar^{1/5}),

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\hbar}\tr\left[K_{3}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\eta}\right]d\hat{\eta}(\hbar)

\displaystyle=-Cn_{\eta}\hbar^{-1/5}\tr\left[K_{3}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\nu}\right]dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{125}{36}\eta_{0}^{2}Cn_{\eta}\left(\frac{3}{10\eta_{0}}\right)^{1/5}\mathcal{H}(x)dx+\OO(\hbar^{1/5}).

combining these results, and recalling the value of
C
from Equation (
4.31
), we obtain that

-\frac{1}{\hbar}\displaystyle\Ress_{\lambda=\infty}\tr\left[K_{3}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\eta}\right]d\hat{\eta}(\hbar)-\frac{1}{\hbar}\displaystyle\Ress_{\lambda=\infty}\tr\left[K_{3}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\nu}\right]d\hat{\nu}(\hbar)=-\mathcal{H}(x)dx+\OO(\hbar^{1/5}),

thus proving the theorem.
∎

We now proceed to the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Proof.

(Of Theorem
1.4
.)

By the results of Subsection
4.3
, in an appropriately chosen sector as
\lambda\to\infty
, we have that

\mathfrak{G}(\lambda)={\bf Z}(\lambda;{\bf t})e^{-\frac{1}{\hbar}\hat{\Theta}(\lambda;{\bf t})}={\bf R}(\lambda)\cdot M(\lambda)e^{\frac{1}{\hbar}(\hat{G}-\hat{\Theta})(\lambda)}={\bf R}(\lambda)M(\lambda),

Therefore,

\mathfrak{G}^{-1}(\lambda)\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}(\lambda)=M^{-1}(\lambda)M^{\prime}(\lambda)+M^{-1}(\lambda){\bf R}^{-1}(\lambda){\bf R}^{\prime}(\lambda)M(\lambda).

By our observations in Proposition
4.12
,

{\bf R}(\lambda)=\mathbb{I}+\OO(\hbar^{3/5}),\qquad\text{ and so }\qquad{\bf R}^{-1}(\lambda){\bf R}^{\prime}(\lambda)=\OO(\hbar^{3/5}),

and since by (
4.55
), (
4.56
)
M(\lambda)=\mathbb{I}+\OO(\hbar^{1/5})
, we see that

M^{-1}(\lambda){\bf R}^{-1}(\lambda){\bf R}^{\prime}(\lambda)M(\lambda)=\OO(\hbar^{3/5}),

and so this term does not contribute at leading order to the
\tau
-function.
On the other hand,

M^{-1}(\lambda)M^{\prime}(\lambda)=-\hbar^{1/5}\left(\frac{5s}{6}\right)^{-1/5}\frac{\mathcal{H}(x)E_{31}}{\lambda^{2}}+\OO(\hbar^{2/5}).

(4.60)

Thus,

-\displaystyle\Ress_{\lambda=\infty}\tr\left[\mathfrak{G}^{-1}(\lambda)\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\hat{\Theta}}{\partial\nu}\right]d\hat{\nu}(\hbar)=-\mathcal{H}(x)dx+\OO(\hbar^{1/5}),

and we obtain the theorem in the limit
\hbar\to 0
.
∎

5. Concluding Remarks.

In summary, we have studied some asymptotic properties of a special solution to the
(3,4)
string equation (
1.1
), which appears in the study
of the multicritical quartic
2
-matrix model. In particular, we were able to show that this solution admits a “topological” expansion, and that the

\tau
-function for this solution has limits to the Painlevé I
\tau
-function, confirming a conjecture from
[
CGM90
]
.

There are several questions we left unaddressed in this work, but are nevertheless still of interest:

1.

The Stokes manifold for the symmetric solutions of the RHP
1.1

(i.e., solutions with corresponding Stokes parameters satisfying
s_{k}=-s_{k+8}
,
k=-7,...,-1
) contains the following
7
planes, which we parameterize in terms of the remaining
7
Stokes parameters
(s_{1},....,s_{7})
:

\displaystyle\Pi_{0}

\displaystyle:=\{(x+1,-1,0,0,1,x,y)\},

\displaystyle\Pi_{1}

\displaystyle:=\{(0,-1,x,y,1-x,-1,0)\},

\displaystyle\Pi_{2}

\displaystyle:=\{(x,y-1,1,0,0,-1,y)\},

\displaystyle\Pi_{3}

\displaystyle:=\{(0,0,1,x,y,-x-1,1)\},

\displaystyle\Pi_{4}

\displaystyle:=\{(x,y,1-x,-1,0,0,1)\},

\displaystyle\Pi_{5}

\displaystyle:=\{(1,0,0,-1,1-y,x,y)\},

\displaystyle\Pi_{6}

\displaystyle:=\{(1,-1-y,x,y,1,0,0)\}.

These planes intersect pairwise:
\Pi_{k}\cap\Pi_{k\pm 1}\neq\emptyset
for
k\in\ZZ_{7}
, and otherwise are completely disjoint. As these planes are embedded in
\CC^{7}
, their intersection is a point in each case. Note that the Stokes parameters corresponding to the model studied in the present
work (
1
) lies at the intersection of
\Pi_{0}
and
\Pi_{1}
. This is analogous to the way the tritronquée solutions
to Painlevé I (which lie at the intersection points of pairs of the
5
lines defining the tronquée solutions in the PI Stokes manifold, cf.
[
KK93
]
) appear in the
1
-matrix model.
In particular, the set of solutions to the string equation lying on the plane
\Pi_{1}
with parameter
y=0
can be studied using the same techniques in this work, with little modification: one only has to choose the relevant corresponding Stokes data in the local model problems accordingly. The result is that our main theorems hold for each of these solutions, with the word
tritronquée solution
replaced with

tronquée solution
of Painlevé I. The role of the Stokes parameter
y
is unclear. Finding an asymptotic
formula for solutions to the string equation with generic Stokes data on the plane
\Pi_{1}
is still in general an open problem.

2.

A much wider range of limits for the
\tau
-function studied here tend to the Painlevé I
\tau
-function. Note that we can parameterize the (real) critical surface (see Appendix
C
, Equation (
C
)) of the aforementioned solutions by

\left\{(t_{1}(\varsigma,\eta),t_{2}^{\pm}(\varsigma,\eta),t_{5}(\varsigma,\eta)\mid\eta\in\RR,\varsigma>\max\left\{\frac{5}{3}\eta,0\right\}\right\},

(5.1)

where

t_{1}(\varsigma,\eta)=-\frac{5}{12}\varsigma\left(5\eta-9\eta\varsigma+3\varsigma^{2}\right),\qquad t_{2}^{\pm}(\varsigma,\eta)=\pm\frac{\sqrt{2\varsigma}}{12}(5\eta-3\varsigma)^{2},\qquad t_{5}(\varsigma,\eta)=\eta.

(5.2)

given a point
P_{0}=\langle t_{1}^{(0)},t_{2}^{\pm(0)},t_{5}^{(0)}\rangle
on this surface, consider a unit vector
{\bf u}=\langle u_{1},u_{2},u_{5}\rangle
based at
P_{0}
which lies in the region
D
below the tangent plane of the critical surface. Define rescaled coordinates

\displaystyle X_{1}(x|T)

\displaystyle:=T^{6}t_{1}^{(0)}+CT^{2/5}u_{1}x,

(5.3)

\displaystyle X_{2}(x|T)

\displaystyle:=T^{5}t_{2}^{\pm(0)}+CT^{-3/5}u_{2}x,

(5.4)

\displaystyle X_{5}(x|T)

\displaystyle:=T^{2}t_{5}^{(0)}+CT^{-18/5}u_{5}x.

(5.5)

We then claim that

Conjecture 1
.

There exists a choice of constant
C=C(P_{0},{\bf u})>0
and a polynomial
Q(t_{1},t_{2},t_{5})
such that, if we define
\hat{\tau}_{0}:=e^{Q}
, considered as a differential
in the variable
x
,

\lim_{T\to\infty}{\bf d}\log\frac{\tau(X_{5}(x|T),X_{2}(x|T),X_{1}(x|T))}{\hat{\tau}_{0}(X_{5}(x|T),X_{2}(x|T),X_{1}(x|T))}=-\mathcal{H}(x)dx,

(5.6)

where
\mathcal{H}(x)
is the Painlevé I Hamiltonian.

The proof of this fact should follow from the results of this work, after some rather tedious calculations. From more involved formal calculations,
it follows that the functions
U,V
should behave like

\displaystyle U(X_{5}(x|T),X_{2}(x|T),X_{1}(x|T))

\displaystyle=\varsigma T^{2}+\frac{2}{u_{1}^{2}}T^{-4/5}q(x)+o(T^{-4/5}),

(5.7)

\displaystyle V(X_{5}(x|T),X_{2}(x|T),X_{1}(x|T))

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sqrt{2\varsigma}}{6}(5\eta-3\varsigma)T^{3}+\frac{\sqrt{2\varsigma}}{u_{1}^{2}}T^{1/5}q(x)+o(T^{1/5}),

(5.8)

as
T\to\infty
. This conjecture is consistent with the theorems stated in the introduction.

3.

We showed that a
3\times 3
Painlevé I parametrix became relevant in the critical
\eta<0
limit. This parametrix, to our knowledge, has
not been implemented in practice in any steepest descent analysis. There should be a 1-1 correspondence between solutions to this
3\times 3
parametrix
problem and the standard
2\times 2
Painlevé I parametrix that appears more frequently in the literature. In principle, there should exist
a monodromy map
\mathcal{M}
which takes a solution to the
2\times 2
problem with data
\{s_{k}\}
to a solution to the
3\times 3
problem
with data
\{\nu_{k}\}
. A promising approach to this problem is suggested in
[
JKT09
]
, involving a generalized Laplace transform. This approach is
applied to a closely related problem for Painlevé II by K. Liechty and D. Wang in
[
LW16
]
. In Appendix
D
, we
have already conjectured part of this correspondence. We plan to work out this map in general in a later work.

1D pressureless gas dynamics systems in a strip
Abhrojyoti Sen

Abstract
. We construct explicit measure-valued solutions to the one-dimensional pressureless gas dynamics system in a strip-like domain by introducing a new boundary potential. The constructed solutions satisfy an entropy condition, and depending on the boundary data and the behavior of the potentials, mass accumulation can occur at the boundaries. The approach relies on a systematic treatment of boundary potentials and their interactions with the initial data, providing a more precise understanding of the formation and propagation of singularities in measure-valued solutions.

1.
Introduction and main result

1.1.
State of the art

In this article, we investigate the solvability of the initial-boundary value problem for the system of pressureless gas dynamics described by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho u)=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2})=0,

in one spatial dimension, where
\rho=\rho(x,t)\geq 0
denotes the density and
u=u(x,t)
represents the velocity field. The two equations in system (
1.1
) models the conservation of mass and momentum, respectively. We consider (
1.1
) subject to the initial conditions

(1.2)

\rho(x,0)=\rho_{0}(x),\quad u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in[0,1],

where
\rho_{0}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
u_{0}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
are given functions. The focus of our analysis is to determine the admissibility of boundary data

(1.3)

\displaystyle u(0,t)=u_{b_{l}}(t),\,\,\,\rho(0,t)u(0,t)=\rho_{b_{l}}(t)u_{b_{l}}(t),

(1.4)

\displaystyle u(1,t)=u_{b_{r}}(t),\,\,\,\rho(1,t)u(1,t)=\rho_{b_{r}}(t)u_{b_{r}}(t),\quad t>0,

where
u_{b_{l}},u_{b_{r}}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
and
\rho_{b_{l}},\rho_{b_{r}}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
are prescribed functions. Our analysis aims to identify the conditions under which the boundary data in (
1.3
) can be consistently prescribed and to rigorously determine the sense in which solutions to (
1.1
) exist. In particular, we examine whether the system admits weak or measure-valued solutions that satisfy the entropy condition and respect the prescribed initial and boundary data. For scalar conservation laws, this type of problem in a strip
[0,1]\times(0,\infty)
has been studied by Frankowska
[
14
]
, and Joseph and Gowda
[
18
]
. For applications in highway traffic modeling, see the work of Strub and Bayen
[
27
]
. Moreover,
[
19
]
and the references therein provide a broader overview of the initial boundary value problems for related hyperbolic systems.

We consider the spatial domain
[0,1]
for convenience; however, any other finite interval can be addressed by applying an appropriate affine rescaling transformation. Specifically, for an interval
[a,b]
the rescaling can be achieved via the mapping
x\mapsto\frac{x-a}{b-a}.
The initial datum
(\rho_{0},u_{0})
are assumed to be locally bounded measurable functions with
\rho_{0}>0.
The boundary datum are prescribed to impose the inflow conditions. Specifically, the functions
u_{b_{l}}(t)
and
u_{b_{r}}(t)
are bounded measurable, with
u_{b_{l}}(t)>0
and
u_{b_{r}}(t)<0
for all
t>0.
Furthermore, the associated density functions
\rho_{b_{l}}(t),\rho_{b_{r}}(t)
are assumed to be positive, locally bounded, and measurable. These assumptions ensure the existence of the inflow boundary conditions for the system. They also allow for the application of weak or measure-valued solution frameworks, which will be employed to analyze the solvability of the problem under consideration.

The initial value problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) has been extensively studied over the past few decades. It is now well established that one of the primary challenges lies in the fact that
\rho
is not generally a function, but a Radon measure. Consequently, the natural framework for seeking weak solutions to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) is the space of Radon measures.

Several notions of weak solutions have been introduced in the literature, including measure-valued solutions
[
2
]
, duality solutions, and solutions derived via the vanishing viscosity method
[
4
,
3
,
6
]
. The global existence of weak solutions using mass and momentum potentials was demonstrated in
[
8
,
12
]
, while explicit formulas for solutions were obtained using generalized potentials and variational principles in
[
13
,
15
,
29
]
.

A novel approach to the global existence of weak solutions for the 1D pressureless gas dynamics equations was introduced by Natile and Savaré
[
21
]
, who constructed sticky particle solutions using a metric projection onto the cone of monotone maps. Subsequently, Cavalletti et al.
[
10
]
provided a more direct proof by employing the concept of differentiability of metric projections, as developed by Haraux.

In another line of work, Nguyen and Tudorascu
[
25
,
24
]
established a general global existence result for (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), with or without viscosity, by constructing entropy solutions for suitable scalar conservation laws. They further proved the uniqueness of solutions using the contraction principle in the Wasserstein metric. Additional uniqueness results were provided by Wang and Ding
[
28
]
and Huang and Wang
[
15
]
, who used generalized characteristics introduced by Dafermos
[
11
]
.

In contrast, Bressan and Nguyen
[
9
]
demonstrated non-uniqueness and non-existence of solutions in the multidimensional case by constructing specific initial data.

Regarding the numerical methods for the pressureless gas dynamics model, we refer to
[
7
,
5
]
for details. However, the initial-boundary value problem for (
1.1
) with (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) was first investigated in
[
23
]
, making a significant advancement in the study of this class of problems.

1.2.
Main result

In this subsection, we present our main result. Building upon the existing literature, we investigate the global existence of an entropy solution to the system (
1.1
)-(
1.4
). As noted earlier, Neumann et al.
[
23
]
constructed measure-valued solutions for the initial-boundary value problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
), utilizing the framework of generalized potentials. For the sake of completeness, we provide a short overview of the method and the associated solution concept.

Rykov et al.
[
13
]
introduced a generalized variational principle for pressureless gas dynamics, extending the classical variational principle of Lax and Oleinik for scalar conservation laws, particularly the Burgers equation. Subsequent works by Huang and Wang
[
15
]
and Ding et al.
[
29
]
extended the method of generalized potentials to cases where the initial datum
u_{0}
is not continuous and
\rho_{0}\geq 0
is a Radon measure, respectively. In this setting, the solution concept is the following: we show that
(\rho,u)
is actually a weak solution to the system (
1.1
). The construction begins with locally bounded measurable functions
m(x,t)
and
u(x,t),
where
m(x,t)
is of locally bounded variation (
BV
) in
x
for almost every
t.
This function
m
defines a Lebesgue-Stieltjes measure
dm
whose derivative in the distributional sense corresponds to a Radon measure
\rho=m_{x}.
These two representations are same through the identification

\displaystyle-\left\langle m,\varphi_{x}\right\rangle=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\varphi_{x}m\,dx=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\varphi m_{x}\,dx=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\varphi\,dm=\left\langle v,\varphi\right\rangle\,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\,\,\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}).

Furthermore, similar identification allows us to define

\displaystyle\left\langle vu,\varphi\right\rangle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\varphi u\,dm.

These identifications lead to the notion of generalized solution to (
1.1
). The first equation of (
1.1
) can then be expressed in the distributional sense as:

\displaystyle 0=\left\langle v,\varphi_{t}\right\rangle+\left\langle vu,\varphi_{x}\right\rangle=-\iint\varphi_{xt}m\,dx\,dt+\iint\varphi_{x}u\,dm\,dt.

Similarly, the second equation of (
1.1
) takes the form:

\displaystyle 0=\left\langle vu,\varphi_{t}\right\rangle+\left\langle vu^{2},\varphi_{x}\right\rangle=\iint u\varphi_{t}\,dm\,dt+\iint u^{2}\varphi_{x}dm\,dt.

Therefore, the weak formulation to the system (
1.1
) is the following:

Definition 1.1
.

The pair
(\rho,u)
is said to be a generalized solution to the system (
1.1
) if the following integral identities

(1.5)

\displaystyle\iint\varphi_{t}m\,dx\,dt-\iint\varphi u\,dm\,dt=0,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\iint\varphi_{t}u+\varphi_{x}u^{2}\,dm\,dt=0,

hold for all test functions
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}^{+}),
where the distributional derivative
m_{x}
defines the Radon measure
\rho.

The construction of the generalized solution
(m,u)
proceeds in two stages. First, we introduce generalized potentials
F(y,x,t),G_{b_{l}}(\tau,x,t),G_{b_{r}}(\xi,x,t),
to construct
u
and
m.
Subsequently, we define the momentum and energy potentials
q(x,t)
and
E(x,t),
respectively, alongside an auxiliary functional
H(\cdot,x,t).
Moreover, by establishing relations between the measures
dq,

dE,
and
dm,
we verify that
q,E,m
satisfy Definition
1.1
.

Now we state our main result below.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\rho_{0}(x),\rho_{b_{l}}(t)>0,\rho_{b_{r}}(t)>0,
and
u_{0}(x),u_{b_{l}}(t)>0,u_{b_{r}}(t)<0,
are locally bounded measurable functions, then the pair
(m,u)
given by Definition
2.13
-
2.12
is a global weak entropy solution to the system (
1.1
)-(
1.4
) in the sense of Definition
1.1
and
3.7
.

It should be noted that in the above theorem, the initial condition holds in its classical sense. Turning to the boundary conditions (
1.3
)-(
1.4
), we recall that, as established in the theory of conservation laws
[
1
,
17
,
20
]
, boundary data cannot be arbitrarily prescribed. One cannot arbitrarily prescribe boundary data, because a priori there is no control of the sign of
u(0+,t)
and
u(1-,t)
, except in the case when
u_{0}
is positive and hence
u(x,t)>0
everywhere. At this point, we recall that
u_{b_{l}}
was assumed to be positive, whereas
u_{b_{r}}
is assumed to be negative.

We shall show the following: If
t>0
is a Lebesgue point of
u_{b_{l}}
and
\rho_{b_{l}}
and
\min\left\{F(0,t),G_{b_{r}}(0,t)\right\}>G_{b_{l}}(0,t)
, then
\lim_{x\to 0+}u(x,t)=u_{b_{l}}(t)
. If in addition
u_{b_{l}}
is continuously differentiable and
\rho_{b_{l}}
is locally Lipschitz continuous, then
\lim_{x\to 0+}\rho(x,t)u(x,t)=\rho_{b_{l}}(t)u_{b_{l}}(t)
. On the other hand, if
\min\left\{F(0,t),G_{b_{r}}(0,t)\right\}\leq G_{b_{l}}(0,t)
then
u(0+,t)<0
and the boundary condition (
1.3
) cannot be fulfilled. Rather, it may happen that mass accumulates at the boundary in the form of
\delta\cdot(m(0+,t)-m(0,t))
. We show similar assertions for the right boundary also. We prove that the boundary condition holds in its classical sense when
\min\left\{F(1,t),G_{b_{l}}(1,t)\right\}>G_{b_{r}}(1,t),
otherwise mass can accumulate at the right boundary. This phenomenon is exhibited through some specific example with Riemann type data in Section
4
. For further aspects of boundary conditions for systems involving measure solution, see
[
16
,
22
]
.

1.3.
Extensions and open problems

In this subsection, we mention some possible extensions and open problems related to the gas dynamics model in the initial boundary setting.

\bullet
Recently, in
[
26
]
, Sen and Sen investigated the pure initial value problem for a nonhomogeneous gas dynamics model where the nonhomogeneous term depends not only on the solutions
(\rho,u),
but also explicitly on
x
and
t.
Specifically, the system is given by:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho u)=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2})=\frac{1}{t+\alpha}\left(\frac{x}{t+\alpha}-u\right)\rho,\,\,\,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

A natural extension of this work would involve constructing a measure-valued solution for the initial-boundary value problem associated with the above system. In
[
26
]
, an initial potential

F(x,t):=\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}}F(y,x,t)=\int_{0}^{y}\left(\left(\frac{\eta}{2\alpha}+\frac{u_{0}(\eta)}{2}\right)(t+\alpha)+\left(\frac{\eta}{2}-\frac{u_{0}(\eta)\alpha}{2}\right)\frac{\alpha}{(t+\alpha)}-x\right)\rho_{0}(\eta)d\eta

was introduced to analyze the initial value problem. To study the corresponding initial-boundary value problem, it would be necessary to introduce appropriate boundary potentials and define suitable characteristic triangles. This undertaking appears to be both intricate and mathematically rigorous, requiring careful analysis to address the added difficulties introduced by the nonhomogeneous terms.

\bullet
Next, we consider the study of a gas dynamics system with a discontinuous (in space) right-hand side. An example of such a system is given by:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho u)=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2})=H(x)\rho

with the initial data
(\rho(x,0),u(x,0))=(\rho_{0}(x),u_{0}(x)),
where a discontinuous function
H(x)
is defined as:

H(x)=\begin{cases}\alpha,\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,x>0\\
\beta\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,x<0,\end{cases}

for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R},\alpha\neq\beta.
In other words, the above problem can be interpreted as a gas dynamics system with a discontinuous flux:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}\left(\rho f(x,t,v)\right)=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho v)+\partial_{x}\left(\rho vf(x,t,v)\right)=0,

where

\displaystyle f(x,t,v)=\begin{cases}v+\alpha t,\,\,\,x>0\\
v+\beta t,\,\,\,x<0,\end{cases}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,v=\begin{cases}u-\alpha t,\,\,\,x>0\\
u-\beta t,\,\,\,x<0.\end{cases}

Given that
[
23
]
provides techniques to address the left boundary and the current work develops methods to handle the right boundary, it remains an open question whether these ideas can be combined to analyze the problem described above. It is important to note that this is an initial value problem, with no data prescribed on the interface
x=0.
To address this, one must carefully study the behavior of the characteristic lines (or curves) passing through the interface. The discontinuity on the right-hand side introduces additional complexities, particularly in understanding how the interface influences the structure of solutions. Moreover, these aspects require rigorous analysis to ensure a well-defined solution framework.

1.4.
Organization of the article

The paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we construct solutions, i.e.,
(m,u),
based on generalized potentials. Section
3
is dedicated to demonstrating that the constructed solution satisfies the weak formulation in the sense of Definition
1.1
, as well as discussing the initial-boundary conditions and the entropy admissibility of solution. In what follows, we prove Theorem
1.2
. Finally, Section
4

2.
Construction of solution

This section defines the potentials for the initial and boundary manifolds. The smallest potential at
(x,t),
determines characteristic triangles that specify the regions influencing the solution. These non-intersecting triangles form a continuous curve, with its time derivative matching the velocity from momentum influx. For points
(x,t)
in the domain
\Omega=[0,1]\times[0,\infty[
, we introduce the initial and boundary functionals as

\displaystyle F(y,x,t)=\int_{0}^{y}(tu_{0}(\eta)+\eta-x)\rho_{0}(\eta)d\eta\,,

\displaystyle G_{b_{l}}(\tau,x,t)=\int_{0}^{\tau}[x-u_{b_{l}}(\eta)(t-\eta)]\rho_{b_{l}}(\eta)u_{b_{l}}(\eta)d\eta\,,

\displaystyle G_{b_{r}}(\xi,x,t)=\int_{0}^{\xi}[x-1-u_{b_{r}}(\eta)(t-\eta)]\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)u_{b_{r}}(\eta)d\eta+F(1,x,t)\,.

Given
(x,t),
we denote
y_{*}(x,t)
and
y^{*}(x,t)
for the left most and right most points respectively in the interval [0,1], such that

(2.1)

F(x,t):=\min_{0\leq y\leq 1}F(y,x,t)=F(y_{*}(x,t),x,t)=F(y^{*}(x,t),x,t)\,.

F(x,t)
is called the initial potential.

Similarly, given
(x,t)
, we denote by
\tau^{*}(x,t)
and
\tau_{*}(x,t)
the upper most and lower most points on the
t
-axis respectively such that

(2.2)

G_{b_{l}}(x,t):=\min_{\tau\geq 0}G_{b_{l}}(\tau,x,t)=G(\tau^{*}(x,t),x,t)=G(\tau_{*}(x,t),x,t)\,.

We call
G_{b_{l}}(x,t)
the left boundary potential.
And finally, for the right boundary, we call these points
\xi^{*}(x,t)
and
\xi_{*}(x,t)
and define

(2.3)

G_{b_{r}}(x,t):=\min_{\xi\geq 0}G_{b_{r}}(\xi,x,t)=G(\xi^{*}(x,t),x,t)=G(\xi_{*}(x,t),x,t)\,,

where
G_{b_{r}}(x,t)
is the right boundary potential.
Note that
\tau^{*}(x,t)\leq t
and
\xi^{*}(x,t)\leq t
by straightforward arguments using the signs of the integrands.

All these minima, and thus the potentials, exist as real numbers, because of the signs of
u_{b_{l}}
and
u_{b_{r}}
. Observe that for a fixed
t>0
the functions
F(x,t)
and
G_{b_{r}}(x,t)
are monotonically decreasing in
x
while
G_{b_{l}}(x,t)
is monotonically increasing.

Remark 2.1
.

The key difference between the left boundary potential
G_{b_{l}}(x,t)
, as introduced in
[
23
]
, and the newly defined right boundary potential
G_{b_{r}}(x,t)
is the explicit dependence of
G_{b_{r}}(x,t)
on the initial potential
F(x,t)
. More precisely, while
G_{b_{l}}
is constructed solely based on boundary data at
x=0,
the formulation of
G_{b_{r}}
involves contributions from both the boundary data at
x=1
and the initial potential to accurately capture the mass and its transportation throughout the domain.

Now we collect some properties of the minimizers.

Lemma 2.2
.

With our assumptions on the initial and boundary data we have

(1)

For fixed
x,

\tau_{*}(x,t)
and
\tau^{*}(x,t)
are monotonically increasing in
t
and for fixed
t
monotonically decreasing in
x
. Moreover, we have for
t_{1}<t_{2}
that
\tau^{*}(x,t_{1})\leq\tau_{*}(x,t_{2})
and for
x_{1}<x_{2}
that
\tau_{*}(x_{1},t)\geq\tau^{*}(x_{2},t)
.

(2)

For fixed
x,

\xi_{*}(x,t)
and
\xi^{*}(x,t)
are monotonically increasing in
t
and for fixed
t
monotonically increasing in
x
. For
t_{1}<t_{2}
that
\xi^{*}(x,t_{1})\leq\xi_{*}(x,t_{2})
and for
x_{1}<x_{2}
that
\xi^{*}(x_{1},t)\leq\xi_{*}(x_{2},t)
.

(3)

For fixed
t,

y_{*}(x,t)
and
y^{*}(x,t)
are monotonically increasing in
x
and for
x_{1}<x_{2}
we have
y^{*}(x_{1},t)\leq y_{*}(x_{2},t)
.

(4)

\tau_{*}(0,t)=\tau^{*}(0,t)=t,

\xi_{*}(1,t)=\xi^{*}(1,t)=t
and
y_{*}(x,0)=y^{*}(x,0)=x
.

(5)

y_{*}(x,t)
,
\tau_{*}(x,t)
and
\xi_{*}(x,t)
are lower semicontinuous while
y^{*}(x,t)
,
\tau^{*}(x,t)
and
\xi^{*}(x,t)

Proof.

A similar result can be found in
[
23
]
, and this is in turn based on the results in
[
29
]
. For completeness, we give the proof of (2) and the part of (4) which is new for the domain
\Omega
.

To prove (2) let
x,\,t_{1},\,t_{2}>0
be arbitrary but fixed, and
\xi_{1}
a minimizer of
G_{b_{r}}(\xi,x,t_{1})
and
\xi_{2}
one of
G_{b_{r}}(\xi,x,t_{2})
. Now we have

0\leq G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{2},x,t_{1})-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{1},x,t_{1})\,,\ \text{and }0\leq G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{1},x,t_{2})-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{2},x,t_{2})\,.

Adding the two inequalities and using the definition of
G_{b_{r}}
results in

0\leq(t_{2}-t_{1})\int_{\xi_{1}}^{\xi_{2}}\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)u_{b_{r}}(\eta)^{2}d\eta\,.

Since the term in the integral is positive by our assumptions we conclude that the minimizers have to be increasing in
t
.

Now on the other hand fixing

and denoting by
\xi_{1}
a minimizer of
G_{b_{r}}(\xi,x_{1},t)
and by
\xi_{2}

we derive in the same way

0\leq(x_{1}-x_{2})\int_{\xi_{1}}^{\xi_{2}}\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)u_{b_{r}}(\eta)d\eta\,.

Using our assumption that
u_{b_{r}}
is negative one infers that the minimizers are increasing in
x
.

For (4) note that

G_{b_{r}}(\xi,1,t)=\int_{0}^{\xi}u_{b_{r}}(\eta)^{2}\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)(\eta-t)d\eta+F(1,1,t)\,,

so the minimum is attained uniquely at
\xi=t
because
\rho_{b_{r}}>0
.
∎

The following lemma gives uniqueness of the minimizers along lines connecting a point to the respective minimizer at the boundary corresponding to the potential.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(x,t)
be a fixed point with

(1)

Let

(0,\tau_{1})

\tau_{1}
.

(3)

Let
\xi=\xi_{1}

. Then for any point
(\bar{x},\bar{t})\neq(x,t)
on the line segment joining
(x,t)
and

(1,\xi_{1})
the minimizer of

is unique and it is
\xi_{1}
.

Proof.

The proof of (1) and (2) can be found in
[

]
. Note that (1) was already included in
[
29
]

[
15
]
.
For (3): We want to show that for
\xi\neq\xi_{1}\colon

G_{b_{r}}(\xi,\bar{x},\bar{t})-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{1},\bar{x},\bar{t})>0\,.

By definition, we have

G_{b_{r}}(\xi,\bar{x},\bar{t})-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{1},\bar{x},\bar{t})=\int_{\xi_{1}}^{\xi}\left[\bar{x}-1-u_{b_{r}}(\eta)(\bar{t}-\eta)\right]\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)u_{b_{r}}(\eta)d\eta=\\
=(\bar{x}-1)\int_{\xi_{1}}^{\xi}\left[1-u_{b_{r}}(\eta)\frac{\bar{t}-\eta}{\bar{x}-1}\right]\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)u_{b_{r}}(\eta)d\eta=\\
=(\bar{x}-1)\int_{\xi_{1}}^{\xi}\left[1-u_{b_{r}}(\eta)\frac{\bar{t}-\xi_{1}}{\bar{x}-1}-u_{b_{r}}(\eta)\frac{\xi_{1}-\eta}{\bar{x}-1}\right]\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)u_{b_{r}}(\eta)d\eta\,.

Using that
(\bar{x},\bar{t})
lies on the line connecting
(x,t)
and
(1,\xi_{1})
we conclude

G_{b_{r}}(\xi,\bar{x},\bar{t})-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{1},\bar{x},\bar{t})=\\
=(\bar{x}-1)\int_{\xi_{1}}^{\xi}\left[1-u_{b_{r}}(\eta)\frac{t-\eta+\eta-\xi_{1}}{x-1}-u_{b_{r}}(\eta)\frac{\xi_{1}-\eta}{\bar{x}-1}\right]\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)u_{b_{r}}(\eta)d\eta=\\
=(\bar{x}-1)\int_{\xi_{1}}^{\xi}\left[1-u_{b_{r}}(\eta)\frac{t-\eta}{x-1}\right]\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)u_{b_{r}}(\eta)d\eta+\\
+(\bar{x}-1)\int_{\xi_{1}}^{\xi}(\xi_{1}-\eta)\left[\frac{1}{x-1}-\frac{1}{\bar{x}-1}\right]\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)u_{b_{r}}^{2}(\eta)d\eta=\\
=\frac{\bar{x}-1}{x-1}\left[G_{b_{r}}(\xi,x,t)-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{1},x,t)\right]+\int_{\xi_{1}}^{\xi}(\xi_{1}-\eta)\left[\frac{\bar{x}-1}{x-1}-1\right]\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)u_{b_{r}}^{2}(\eta)d\eta

Now the first term in the sum is non-negative by assumption and the second one is strictly positive.
∎

For the proof of

, let
t^{\prime\prime}>t^{\prime}>t
and
X(t^{\prime\prime})=x^{\prime\prime},X(t^{\prime})=x^{\prime},X(t)=x.

First consider the case when
\xi^{*}(x,t)=\xi_{*}(x,t)
. It is straightforward to see the following inequality on inclinations:

(2.4)

\frac{x^{\prime\prime}-1}{t^{\prime\prime}-\xi^{*}(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime})}\geq\frac{x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime}}{t^{\prime\prime}-t^{\prime}}\geq\frac{x^{\prime\prime}-1}{t^{\prime\prime}-\xi_{*}(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime})}.

Passing to the limit as

leads, using the semicontinuity and monotonicity, to the first identity of equation
(vi)
.

Now we consider the case
\xi_{*}(x,t)<\xi^{*}(x,t)
. From the definitions of the boundary functional
G_{b_{r}}(\xi,x,t)
,
\xi_{*}
and
\xi^{*}
, we have

(2.5)

\begin{split}G_{b_{r}}\big(\xi^{*}(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}),x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\big)-G_{b_{r}}\big(\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}),x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\big)\leq 0\leq G_{b_{r}}\big(\xi^{*}(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}),x^{\prime},t^{\prime}\big)-G_{b_{r}}\big(\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}),x^{\prime},t^{\prime}\big).\end{split}

After simplification inequality (
2.5
) yields

\int\displaylimits_{\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})}^{\xi^{*}(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime})}\!\!\![x^{\prime\prime}-1-u_{b_{r}}(\eta)(t^{\prime\prime}-\eta)]\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)u_{b_{r}}(\eta)d\eta\leq\int\displaylimits_{\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})}^{\xi^{*}(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime})}\!\!\![x^{\prime}-1-u_{b_{r}}(\eta)(t^{\prime}-\eta)]\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)u_{b_{r}}(\eta)d\eta\,.

Thus we can conclude

(2.6)

\frac{x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime}}{t^{\prime\prime}-t^{\prime}}\leq\frac{\int_{\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})}^{\xi^{*}(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime})}u^{2}_{b_{r}}(\eta)\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)d\eta}{\int_{\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})}^{\xi^{*}(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime})}u_{b_{r}}(\eta)\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)d\eta}\,.

On the other hand, considering the inequality

\begin{split}G_{b_{r}}\big(\xi_{*}(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}),x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\big)-G_{b_{r}}\big(\xi^{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}),x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\big)\leq 0\leq G_{b_{r}}\big(\xi_{*}(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}),x^{\prime},t^{\prime}\big)-G_{b_{r}}\big(\xi^{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}),x^{\prime},t^{\prime}\big),\end{split}

\frac{x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime}}{t^{\prime\prime}-t^{\prime}}\geq\frac{\int_{\tau_{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})}^{\tau^{*}(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime})}u^{2}_{b_{r}}(\eta)\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)d\eta}{\int_{\tau_{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})}^{\tau^{*}(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime})}u_{b_{r}}(\eta)\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)d\eta}\,.

Now passing to the limit as
t^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime}\searrow t
in equations (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we proved the second identity of
(vi)

Now to verify the statement of
(vii)
, we assume
F(x,t)=G_{b_{r}}(x,t)
. Then by definition of the curve and the characteristic triangles, we find
(t,t+\varepsilon)
such that the minima
F
and
G_{b_{r}}
are equal along the curve for some
t^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime}\in(t.t+\varepsilon)
with
t^{\prime\prime}>t^{\prime}
. Using the minimizing properties we have the following inequality.

\displaystyle F\big(y_{*}({x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}}),x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\big)-G_{b_{r}}\big(\xi^{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}),x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\big)=G_{b_{r}}\big(\xi^{*}({x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}}),x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\big)-G_{b_{r}}\big(\xi^{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}),x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\big)\leq 0

\displaystyle\leq F\big(y_{*}(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}),x^{\prime},t^{\prime}\big)-F\big(y_{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}),x^{\prime},t^{\prime}\big)=F\big(y_{*}(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}),x^{\prime},t^{\prime}\big)-G_{b_{r}}\big(\xi^{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}),x^{\prime},t^{\prime}\big).

This inequality implies

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{y_{*}({x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}})}(t^{\prime\prime}-t^{\prime})u_{0}(\eta)\rho_{0}(\eta)+(x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime})\rho_{0}(\eta)d\eta+\int_{0}^{1}(t^{\prime}-t^{\prime\prime})\rho_{0}(\eta)u_{0}(\eta)+(x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime})\rho_{0}(\eta)d\eta\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\xi^{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})}(x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime})\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)u_{b_{r}}(\eta)+u_{b_{r}}^{2}(\eta)\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)(t^{\prime}-t^{\prime\prime})d\eta\end{split}

and a simplification leads to

(2.8)

\frac{\int_{0}^{y_{*}({x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}})}\rho_{0}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{\xi^{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})}\rho_{b_{r}}u^{2}_{b_{r}}-\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{0}u_{0}}{\int_{0}^{y_{*}(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime})}\rho_{0}+\int_{0}^{\xi^{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})}u_{b_{r}}\rho_{b_{r}}-\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{0}}\leq\frac{x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime}}{t^{\prime\prime}-t^{\prime}}\,.

Now in the same way starting from the inequality

\begin{split}G_{b_{r}}\big(\xi^{*}(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}),x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\big)-&F\big(y_{*}({x^{\prime},t^{\prime}}),x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\big)\leq G_{b_{r}}\big(\xi^{*}(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}),x^{\prime},t^{\prime}\big)-F\big(y_{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}),x^{\prime},t^{\prime}\big)\end{split}

and simplifying as before we get

(2.9)

\frac{\int_{0}^{y_{*}({x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}})}\rho_{0}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{\xi^{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})}\rho_{b_{r}}u^{2}_{b_{r}}-\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{0}u_{0}}{\int_{0}^{y_{*}({x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}})}\rho_{0}+\int_{0}^{\xi^{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})}u_{b_{r}}\rho_{b_{r}}-\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{0}}\geq\frac{x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime}}{t^{\prime\prime}-t^{\prime}}\,.

Passing to the limit as
t^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime}\searrow t
in (
2.8
) and (
2.9

Note that the denominator of the above inequality is never zero, since

\int_{0}^{y^{*}(x,t)}\rho_{0}(\eta)d\eta-\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{0}(\eta)d\eta\leq 0
and

Following the similar arguments, we prove
(viii)
. Consider the inequality

\displaystyle G_{b_{r}}\left(\xi^{*}(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right)-G_{b_{r}}\left(\xi^{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}),x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime,\prime}\right)=G_{b_{l}}(\tau^{*}(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}),x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime})-G_{b_{r}}(\xi^{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}),x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime})\leq 0

\displaystyle\leq G_{b_{l}}(\tau^{*}(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}),x^{\prime},t^{\prime})-G_{b_{l}}(\tau^{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}),x^{\prime},t^{\prime})=G_{b_{l}}(\tau^{*}(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}),x^{\prime},t^{\prime})-G_{b_{r}}(\xi^{*}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}),x^{\prime},t^{\prime}).

\frac{1}{2}\frac{x-1}{t-\xi_{*}(x_{1},t)}\leq\frac{E(x_{2},t)-E(x_{1},t)}{q(x_{1},t)-q(x_{2},t)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{x-1}{t-\xi_{*}(x_{2},t)}\,.

In the limit
x_{1}\nearrow x\swarrow x_{2}
, we have
\xi_{*}(x_{1},t)\to\xi_{*}(x,t)
and
\xi_{*}(x_{2},t)\to\xi^{*}(x,t),
hence
\frac{E(x_{2},t)-E(x_{1},t)}{q(x_{1},t)-q(x_{2},t)}\to\frac{1}{2}u
and
we know
\frac{dq}{dm}=u
. Combining these two, we get
dE=\frac{1}{2}u^{2}dm
.

For the case
F(x,t)=G_{b_{r}}(x,t)
in an isolated point. Then noting that
(x_{1},t)
lies left of
(x,t)
and thus
F(x_{1},t)<G_{b_{r}}(x_{1},t)
, and the opposite holds true for
(x_{2},t).
Thus, we have

\begin{split}&\lim_{x_{1},x_{2}\to x}\frac{E(x_{2},t)-E(x_{1},t)}{q(x_{2},t)-q(x_{1},t)}=\\
&=\frac{\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{y_{*}(x,t)}\rho_{0}(\eta)u_{0}(\eta)u(X(\eta,t),t)d\eta+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\xi^{*}(x,t)}\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)u^{2}_{b_{r}}(\eta)u(Z(\eta,t),t)d\eta}{\int_{0}^{y_{*}(x,t)}\rho_{0}(\eta)u_{0}(\eta)d\eta+\int_{0}^{\xi^{*}(x,t)}\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)u^{2}_{b_{r}}(\eta)d\eta-\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{0}(\eta)u_{0}(\eta)d\eta}\\
&-\frac{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{0}(\eta)u_{0}(\eta)u(X(\eta,t),t)d\eta}{\int_{0}^{y_{*}(x,t)}\rho_{0}(\eta)u_{0}(\eta)d\eta+\int_{0}^{\xi^{*}(x,t)}\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)u^{2}_{b_{r}}(\eta)d\eta-\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{0}(\eta)u_{0}(\eta)d\eta}.\end{split}

For
\eta\in[y_{*}(x,t)),1]
, we have
u(X(\eta,t),t)=u(x,t)
and similarly for
\eta\in[0,\xi^{*}(x,t))]
we know that
u(Z(\eta,t),t)=u(x,t)
. Also we notice that for
\eta\in[0,1],

u(X(\eta,t),t)=u(x,t).

Thus from the equation above we have

\lim_{x_{1},x_{2}\to x}\frac{E(x_{2},t)-E(x_{1},t)}{q(x_{2},t)-q(x_{1},t)}=\frac{1}{2}u(x,t)\,.

Since
dq=udm
, we again get
dE=\frac{1}{2}u^{2}dm
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Define

\mu(x,t)=\min\left\{F(x,t),G_{b_{l}}(x,t),G_{b_{r}}(x,t)\right\}\,,

then the following holds for
x_{1},x_{2},t>0
:

(3.6)

\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}m(x,t)dx=\mu(x_{1},t)-\mu(x_{2},t)\,.

(3.7)

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}q(x,t)dt=\mu(x,t_{2})-\mu(x,t_{1})\,.

Proof.

Let
t>0
be fixed. We start with the proof of (
3.6
). Now for any two points
x,x^{\prime}\in[x_{1},x_{2}],
with
x<x^{\prime}
, we claim the following:

(3.8)

(x-x^{\prime})m(x^{\prime},t)\leq\mu(x^{\prime},t)-\mu(x,t)\leq(x-x^{\prime})m(x,t).

Depending upon the minimization, we have the possible values of
\mu(x,t)
and
\mu(x^{\prime},t):

(3.9)

\displaystyle(a)\,\,\,\,\mu(x,t)=F(x,t),\,\mu(x^{\prime},t)=F(x^{\prime},t),\,\,

\displaystyle(b)\,\,\,\,\mu(x,t)=G_{b_{r}}(x,t),\,\mu(x^{\prime},t)=F(x^{\prime},t),

\displaystyle(c)\,\,\,\,\mu(x,t)=G_{b_{l}}(x,t),\,\mu(x^{\prime},t)=G_{b_{l}}(x^{\prime},t),\,\,

\displaystyle(d)\,\,\,\,\mu(x,t)=G_{b_{r}}(x,t),\,\mu(x^{\prime},t)=G_{b_{r}}(x^{\prime},t),

\displaystyle(e)\,\,\,\,\mu(x,t)=G_{b_{l}}(x,t),\,\mu(x^{\prime},t)=F(x^{\prime},t),\,\,

\displaystyle(f)\,\,\,\,\mu(x,t)=G_{b_{l}}(x,t),\,\mu(x^{\prime},t)=G_{b_{r}}(x^{\prime},t).

The proof of the inequality (
3.8
) for the case of (a) (c) and (e) in (
3.9
) can be found in
[
23
, Lemma 3.5]
. Here we prove for the cases (b), (d) and (f). In case (b) we have

\ \begin{aligned} &\mu(x^{\prime},t)-\mu(x,t)\\
&=F(y_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x^{\prime},t)-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x,t)\\
&=[F(y_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x^{\prime},t)-F(y_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x,t)]+[F(y_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x,t)-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x,t)]\end{aligned}

Since
F(y_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x,t)-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x,t)\geq 0
, we get

(3.10)

\mu(x^{\prime},t)-\mu(x,t)\geq F(y_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x^{\prime},t)-F(y_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x,t).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\mu(x^{\prime},t)-\mu(x,t)

\displaystyle=F(y_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x^{\prime},t)-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x,t)

\displaystyle=[F(y_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x^{\prime},t)-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x^{\prime},t)]+[G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x^{\prime},t)-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x,t)].

Since
F(y_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x^{\prime},t)-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x^{\prime},t)\leq 0
, we get

(3.11)

\mu(x^{\prime},t)-\mu(x,t)\leq G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x^{\prime},t)-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x,t).

Again, from the equations (
3.10
)-(
3.11
) and using the definition of
m
,
F
and
G_{b_{r}}
, we get (
3.8
).

Now for the case (d), we have

\mu(x^{\prime},t)-\mu(x,t)=G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x^{\prime},t)-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x,t)\\
=[G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x^{\prime},t)-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x^{\prime},t)]+[G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x^{\prime},t)-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x,t))]\,.

Since the first bracket of the above expression is negative, we get

(3.12)

\mu(x^{\prime},t)-\mu(x,t)\leq G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x^{\prime},t)-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x,t)\,.

On the other hand, we write

\mu(x^{\prime},t)-\mu(x,t)=G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x^{\prime},t)-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x,t)\\
=[G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x^{\prime},t)-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x,t)]+[G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x,t)-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x,t))]\,.

Now the second bracket of the expression is positive and thus

(3.13)

\mu(x^{\prime},t)-\mu(x,t)\geq G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x^{\prime},t)-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x,t)\,.

Combining inequalities (
3.12
), (
3.13
) and using the definitions of
G_{b_{r}}
and
m
we have (
3.8
). Following the similar argument we can prove (f).

Now since
m
is monotonous in
x
, it is also Riemann integrable. Taking Riemann sums and using (
3.8
), we get

\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}m(x,t)dx=\mu(x_{1},t)-\mu(x_{2},t)\,.

To prove (
3.7
) we claim the following inequality holds:

(3.14)

(t^{\prime}-t)q(x,t^{\prime})\leq\mu(x,t^{\prime})-\mu(x,t)\leq(t^{\prime}-t)q(x,t)\,.

To prove the above inequality, we have the following possibilities for
\mu(x,t)
and
\mu(x,t^{\prime})
:

(3.15)

\displaystyle(a)\,\,\,\,\mu(x,t)=F(x,t),\,\,\mu(x,t^{\prime})=F(x,t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle(b)\,\,\,\,\mu(x,t)=G_{b_{l}}(x,t),\,\,\mu(x,t^{\prime})=G_{b_{l}}(x,t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle(c)\,\,\,\,\mu(x,t)=F(x,t),\,\,\mu(x,t^{\prime})=G_{b_{l}}(x,t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle(d)\,\,\,\,\mu(x,t)=G_{b_{l}}(x,t),\,\,\mu(x,t^{\prime})=F(x,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle(e)\,\,\,\,\mu(x,t)=F(x,t),\,\,\mu(x,t^{\prime})=G_{b_{r}}(x,t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle(f)\,\,\,\,\mu(x,t)=G_{b_{l}}(x,t),\,\,\mu(x,t^{\prime})=G_{b_{r}}(x,t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle(g)\,\,\,\,\mu(x,t)=G_{b_{r}}(x,t),\,\,\mu(x,t^{\prime})=G_{b_{l}}(x,t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle(h)\,\,\,\,\mu(x,t)=G_{b_{r}}(x,t),\,\,\mu(x,t^{\prime})=F(x,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle(i)\,\,\,\,\mu(x,t)=G_{b_{r}}(x,t),\,\,\mu(x,t^{\prime})=G_{b_{r}}(x,t^{\prime}).

The proof for the cases (a)-(d) on
\{x\}\times[t_{1},t_{2}]
can be found in
[
23
, Lemma 3.5]
. Here we shall prove the cases (e)-(i). Since the proofs are similar, we only present the proof of case
(i)
here. For
t^{\prime}>t\in[t_{1},t_{2}],
we observe,

\displaystyle\mu(x,t^{\prime})-\mu(x,t)

\displaystyle=G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t^{\prime}),x,t^{\prime})-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x,t)

\displaystyle=[G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t^{\prime}),x,t^{\prime})-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x,t^{\prime})]+[G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x,t^{\prime})-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x,t))]

(3.16)

\displaystyle\leq G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x,t^{\prime})-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x,t))\,,

and

\displaystyle\mu(x,t^{\prime})-\mu(x,t)

\displaystyle=G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t^{\prime}),x,t^{\prime})-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x,t)

\displaystyle=[G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t^{\prime}),x,t^{\prime})-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t^{\prime}),x,t)]+[G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t^{\prime}),x,t)-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x,t))]

\displaystyle\geq G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t^{\prime}),x,t^{\prime})-G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t^{\prime}),x,t)\,.

Combining the inequalities (
3.16
)-(

) and using the definition of

). Since for a fixed

Lemma 3.6
.

For
x,t>0

H(x,t)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &H_{1}(x,t)=\int_{0}^{y_{*}(x,t)}\rho_{0}(\eta)u_{0}(\eta)(X(\eta,t)-x)d\eta\,,&&\text{if }F(x,t)\leq\min\{G_{b_{l}}(x,t),G_{b_{r}}(x,t)\},\\
&H_{2}(x,t)=-\int_{0}^{\tau_{*}(x,t)}\rho_{b}(\eta)u^{2}_{b}(\eta)(Y(\eta,t)-x)d\eta\,,&&\text{if }G_{b_{l}}(x,t)\leq\min\{F(x,t),G_{b_{r}}(x,t)\},\\
&H_{3}(x,t)=-\int_{0}^{\xi_{*}(x,t)}\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)u^{2}_{b_{r}}(\eta)(Z(\eta,t)-x)d\eta\\
&+\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{0}(\eta)u_{0}(\eta)(X(\eta,t)-x)d\eta\,,&&\text{if }G_{b_{r}}(x,t)<\min\{F(x,t),G_{b_{l}}(x,t)\},\end{aligned}\right.

then we have
H_{x}=-q
and

.

Proof.

We first show,

(3.19)

-\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}q(x,t)dx=H(x_{1},t)-H(x_{2},t)\,.

Let
t
be fixed and
[x_{1},x_{2}]
be an interval. Assume that
x
and
x^{\prime}
be any two points in
[x_{1},x_{2}]
with
x<x^{\prime}.
To prove the above identity it is enough to prove

(3.20)

-(x-x^{\prime})q(x,t)\leq H(x,t)-H(x^{\prime},t)\leq-(x-x^{\prime})q(x^{\prime},t)\,.

First, we consider the case where the potentials are not equal at
x
or
x^{\prime}.
Now, depending upon the minimizations through
F
,
G_{b_{l}}
and
G_{b_{r}}
, one has the following possibilities.

\displaystyle(a)\,\,H(x,t)=H_{1}(x,t),\,\,H(x^{\prime},t)=H_{1}(x^{\prime},t),

\displaystyle(b)\,\,H(x,t)=H_{2}(x,t),\,\,H(x^{\prime},t)=H_{2}(x^{\prime},t),

\displaystyle(c)\,\,H(x,t)=H_{2}(x,t),\,\,H(x^{\prime},t)=H_{1}(x^{\prime},t),

\displaystyle(d)\,\,H(x,t)=H_{2}(x,t),\,\,H(x^{\prime},t)=H_{3}(x^{\prime},t),

\displaystyle(e)\,\,H(x,t)=H_{1}(x,t),\,\,H(x^{\prime},t)=H_{3}(x^{\prime},t),

\displaystyle(f)\,\,H(x,t)=H_{3}(x,t),\,\,H(x^{\prime},t)=H_{3}(x^{\prime},t).

For case
(a),(b),(c)
the result holds true by arguments in
[
29
]
and
[
23
]
. The proof of
(d),(e),(f),
are similar to the proof of Lemma
3.4

Let us define

H_{3}(\xi,x,t)=-\int_{0}^{\xi}\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)u^{2}_{b_{r}}(\eta)(Z(\eta,t)-x)d\eta+\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{0}(\eta)u_{0}(\eta)(Z(\eta,t)-x)d\eta.

Since
Z(\eta,t)
is increasing in
\eta
and
Z(\eta,t)=x
for
\xi_{*}(x,t)<\eta<\xi^{*}(x,t)
, we have

(3.21)

H_{3}(x,t)=\min_{\xi\geq 0}H_{3}(\xi,x,t).

From equation (
3.21
), it is evident that
H_{3}(x,t)
is a continuous function. First, we present a proof of
(d).
For
x
and
x^{\prime}
as defined above, we have

\displaystyle H(x,t)-H(x^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle=H_{2}(x,t)-H_{3}(x^{\prime},t)=H_{2}(\tau_{*}(x,t),x,t)-H_{3}(\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle=[H_{2}(\tau_{*}(x,t),x,t)-H_{3}(\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x,t)]+[H_{3}(\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x,t)-H_{3}(\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x^{\prime},t)]

\displaystyle\leq H_{3}(\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x,t)-H_{3}(\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x^{\prime},t)\,.

On the other hand

\displaystyle H(x,t)-H(x^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle=H_{2}(x,t)-H_{3}(x^{\prime},t)=H_{2}(\tau_{*}(x,t),x,t)-H_{3}(\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle=[H_{2}(\tau_{*}(x,t),x,t)-H_{2}(\tau_{*}(x,t),x^{\prime},t)]+[H_{2}(\tau_{*}(x,t),x^{\prime},t)-H_{3}(\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x^{\prime},t)]

\displaystyle\geq H_{2}(\tau_{*}(x,t),x,t)-H_{2}(\tau_{*}(x,t),x^{\prime},t)\,.

Combining those two inequalities, one concludes the inequality in (

). Using (
3.20
), and taking the supremum of the Riemann sums over all partitions of the interval

, we deduce
(
3.19
). In the case of
(e),
similarly, we can write

\displaystyle H(x,t)-H(x^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle=H_{1}(x,t)-H_{3}(x^{\prime},t)=H_{1}(y_{*}(x,t),x,t)-H_{3}(\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle=[H_{1}(y_{*}(x,t),x,t)-H_{1}(y_{*}(x,t),x^{\prime},t)]+[H_{1}(y_{*}(x,t),x^{\prime},t)-H_{3}(\xi_{*}(x^{\prime},t),x^{\prime},t)]

3.20

Now we consider the delicate case where

Due to Lemma

\displaystyle-\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}q(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle=-[H_{2}(x-,t)-H_{2}(x_{1},t)]-[H_{1}(x^{\prime},t)-H_{1}(x,t)]-[H_{3}(x_{2},t)-H_{3}(x^{\prime}+,t)].

\displaystyle H_{3}(x^{\prime}+,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\xi_{*}(x^{\prime}+,t)}\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)u^{2}_{b_{r}}(\eta)(Z(\eta,t)-x^{\prime})\,d\eta+\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{0}(\eta)u_{0}(\eta)(X(\eta,t)-x^{\prime})\,d\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\xi_{*}(x^{\prime}+,t)}\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)u^{2}_{b_{r}}(\eta)(Z(\eta,t)-x^{\prime})\,d\eta+\int_{0}^{y_{*}(x^{\prime}+,t)}\rho_{0}(\eta)u_{0}(\eta)(X(\eta,t)-x^{\prime})\,d\eta

. This completes the proof.
∎

Now we show the second identity (
1.6
) of Definition
1.1
. Again for a test function
\varphi
with compact support in
(0,1)\times(0,\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle 0=\int\int[H_{x}\varphi_{tx}(x,t)-H_{t}\varphi_{xx}(x,t)]dxdt

\displaystyle=\int\int[-q(x,t)\varphi_{tx}(x,t)-2E\varphi_{xx}(x,t)]dxdt

(3.25)

\displaystyle=\int\int u(x,t)\varphi_{t}(x,t)dmdt+\int\int u^{2}(x,t)\varphi_{x}(x,t)dmdt\,.

The identity (
3.25
) proves that
(\rho,u)
satisfies the second equation of the system in the sense of Definition
1.1
.

3.2.
Boundary condition and entropy admissibility

In this subsection, we verify that the constructed solution satisfies the initial and boundary conditions. Furthermore, we show the entropy admissibility of the solution up to the boundary.

3.2.1.
Boundary condition

Our main focus is to verify the boundary conditions. The solution satisfies the boundary conditions in the following sense: when the net flux enters the domain from the boundary, the conditions are satisfied in its classical sense. In contrast, when the net flux exits the domain, the mass accumulates at the boundary, ensuring overall mass conservation.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
m
be as in Definition
2.13
. Then we define

\rho(x,t)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{x}m&&\text{for }0<x<1,\text{ and }t>0\\
&\lim_{x\searrow 0}\rho(x,t)&&\text{for }x=0\text{ and }\min\left\{F(0,t),G_{b_{r}}(0,t)\right\}>G_{b_{l}}(0,t),\\
&\delta\cdot\lim_{x\searrow 0}\left(m(x,t)-m(0,t)\right)&&\text{for }x=0\text{
and }\min\left\{F(0,t),G_{b_{r}}(0,t)\right\}\leq G_{b_{l}}(0,t),\\
&\lim_{x\nearrow 1}\rho(x,t)&&\text{for }x=1\text{ and }\min\left\{F(1,t),G_{b_{l}}(1,t)\right\}>G_{b_{r}}(1,t),\\
&\delta\cdot\lim_{x\nearrow 1}\left(m(1,t)-m(x,t)\right)&&\text{for }x=1\text{ and }\min\left\{F(1,t),G_{b_{l}}(1,t)\right\}\leq G_{b_{r}}(1,t).\end{aligned}\right.

Here
\delta
is the Dirac measure,
\partial_{x}
is the distributional derivative and
\rho
is interpreted as a measure.

Lemma 3.8
.

The pair
(\rho,u)
as defined above solves equation (
1.1
) in
(0,1)\times(0,\infty)
.

The initial conditions are satisfied in the sense that for almost all
x
we have
\lim_{t\searrow 0}u(x,t)=u_{0}(x)
and
\rho=\partial_{x}m
with
\lim_{t\searrow 0}m(x,t)=\int_{0}^{x}\rho_{0}(y)dy
.

The boundary conditions are satisfied in regions where
\min\left\{F(0,t),G_{b_{r}}(0,t)\right\}>G_{b_{l}}(0,t)
and

\min\left\{F(1,t),G_{b_{l}}(1,t)\right\}>G_{b_{r}}(1,t)
in the sense that for almost all
t
we have
\lim_{x\searrow 0}u(x,t)=u_{b_{l}}(t)
and
\lim_{x\nearrow 1}u(x,t)=u_{b_{r}}(t).

In addition, if
u_{b_{l}},u_{b_{r}}
are in
C^{1}_{loc}
and
\rho_{b_{l}},\rho_{b_{r}}
are locally Lipschitz continuous, then
\lim_{x\searrow 0}\rho(x,t)u(x,t)=\rho_{b_{l}}(t)u_{b_{l}}(t)
and
\lim_{x\nearrow 1}\rho(x,t)u(x,t)=\rho_{b_{r}}(t)u_{b_{r}}(t).

Proof.

The validity of the initial condition and the left boundary condition is proved in
[
29
]
and
[
23
]
. We will only check the right boundary condition. Notice that, from our assumption
\min\left\{F(1,t),G_{b_{l}}(1,t)\right\}>G_{b_{r}}(1,t)
and the continuity of
F,G_{b_{l}}
and
G_{b_{r}},
we have
\min\left\{F(x,t),G_{b_{l}}(x,t)\right\}>G_{b_{r}}(x,t)
holds for
x
close to the boundary
\{x=1\}.
Let
t_{0}
be any Lebesgue point of
\rho_{b_{r}}
and
u_{b_{r}}.
First, we show that the boundary condition holds for
u,
i.e.
\lim_{x\to 1}u(x,t_{0})=u_{b_{r}}(t_{0}).
Let
(x_{n},t_{0})\to(1,t_{0}).
First, we assume that
\xi_{*}(x_{n},t_{0})=\xi^{*}(x_{n},t_{0})=\xi(x_{n},t_{0}).
In this case, the minimum is unique and for any
h\neq 0,
we have

\displaystyle G_{b_{r}}\left(\xi(x_{n},t_{0}),x_{n},t_{0}\right)+F(1,x_{n},t_{0})<G_{b_{r}}\left(\xi(x_{n},t_{0})+h(x_{n}-1),x_{n},t_{0}\right)+F(1,x_{n},t_{0}).

Simplification of the above inequality gives,

\displaystyle\int_{\xi(x_{n},t_{0})}^{\xi(x_{n},t_{0})+h(x_{n}-1)}(x_{n}-1)u_{b_{r}}(\eta)\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)\,d\eta

\displaystyle>\int_{\xi(x_{n},t_{0})}^{\xi(x_{n},t_{0})+h(x_{n}-1)}(t_{0}-\eta)u^{2}_{b_{r}}(\eta)\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)\,d\eta

\displaystyle\geq\left(t_{0}-\xi(x_{n},t_{0})-h(x_{n}-1)\right)\int_{\xi(x_{n},t_{0})}^{\xi(x_{n},t_{0})+h(x_{n}-1)}u^{2}_{b_{r}}(\eta)\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)\,d\eta.

From the Definition
2.12
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{u(x_{n},t_{0})}-h\right)\frac{\int_{\xi(x_{n},t_{0})}^{\xi(x_{n},t_{0})+h(x_{n}-1)}u^{2}_{b_{r}}(\eta)\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)\,d\eta}{\int_{\xi(x_{n},t_{0})}^{\xi(x_{n},t_{0})+h(x_{n}-1)}u_{b_{r}}(\eta)\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)\,d\eta}\leq 1,

and passing to the limit
n\to\infty,
we get

(3.26)

\displaystyle\lim\sup_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{u(x_{n},t_{0})}-h\right)u_{b_{r}}(t_{0})\leq 1.

Similarly, considering the inequality

\displaystyle G_{b_{r}}\left(\xi(x_{n},t_{0}),x_{n},t_{0}\right)<G_{b_{r}}\left(\xi(x_{n},t_{0})-h(x_{n}-1),x_{n},t_{0}\right),

we obtain

(3.27)

\displaystyle\lim\inf_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{u(x_{n},t_{0})}+h\right)u_{b_{r}}(t_{0})\geq 1.

Since
h
is arbitrary, combining the inequalities (
3.26
)-(
3.27
), we get

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{u_{b_{r}}(t_{0})}{u(x_{n},t_{0})}=1,

and this completes the proof of
\lim_{n\to\infty}u(x_{n},t_{0})=u_{b_{r}}(t_{0}).

Next, we consider the case
\xi_{*}(x_{n},t_{0})<\xi^{*}(x_{n},t_{0}).
By Definition
2.12
, we have

\displaystyle u(x_{n},t_{0})=\frac{\int_{\xi_{*}(x_{n},t_{0})}^{\xi^{*}(x_{n},t_{0})}\rho_{b_{r}}u^{2}_{b_{r}}\,d\eta}{\int_{\xi_{*}(x_{n},t_{0})}^{\xi^{*}(x_{n},t_{0})}\rho_{b_{r}}u_{b_{r}}\,d\eta}.

Since
t_{0}
is the Lebesgue point of
\rho_{b_{r}}u^{2}_{b_{r}}
and
\rho_{b_{r}}u_{b_{r}},
we find
\lim_{n\to\infty}u(x_{n},t_{0})=u_{b_{r}}(t_{0}).

It remains to show that the right boundary condition for
\rho
holds. In this case, if the boundary potential

G_{b_{r}}(\xi,x,t)=\int_{0}^{\xi}[x-1-u_{b}(\eta)(t-\eta)]\rho_{b}(\eta)u_{b}(\eta)d\eta+F(1,x,t)

attains the minimum in
(0,\infty)
at a point
\bar{\xi}
, then
\frac{\partial G_{b_{r}}}{\partial\tau}\Big|_{\xi=\bar{\xi}}=0.
That is,

\frac{\partial G_{b_{r}}}{\partial\tau}\Big|_{\xi=\bar{\xi}}=\left(x-1-u_{b_{r}}(\bar{\xi})(t-\bar{\xi})\right)\rho_{b_{r}}(\bar{\xi})u_{b_{r}}(\bar{\xi})=0\,.

Since
\xi_{*}(x,t),\xi^{*}(x,t)\nearrow t
as
x\nearrow 1
, for
x
close enough to
1
, the minimizing point lies in
(0,\infty)
.
Consider the function
g:\mathbb{R^{+}}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
where
g
is defined as

g(x,\xi)=x-1-u_{b_{r}}(\xi)(t-\xi)\,.

Then
\frac{\partial g}{\partial\xi}=-(t-\xi)u_{b_{r}}^{\prime}(\xi)+u_{b_{r}}(\xi)
,
g(1,t)=0
and
\frac{\partial g}{\partial\xi}(1,t)<0.
Thus by the implicit function theorem there exists a neighborhood of
(1,t)
where
g(x,\xi)=0
has a unique solution. That gives the unique minimizer of the boundary potential
G_{b_{r}}(\xi,x,t)
and thus
\xi_{*}(x,t)
is a continuously differentiable function of
x
in some neighborhood of
x=1
. Now from the relation
u(x,t)=\frac{x-1}{t-\xi_{*}(x,t)}
, we see that
\lim_{x\nearrow 1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\xi_{*}(x,t)=-\frac{1}{u_{b_{r}}(t)}.

Consequently,

\lim_{x\nearrow 1}\rho(x,t)=\lim_{x\nearrow 1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}m(x,t)=-\lim_{x\nearrow 1}\rho_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t))u_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t))\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\xi_{*}(x,t)=\rho_{b_{r}}(t)\,.

This completes the verification of the initial and boundary condition for
(\rho,u)
.
∎

3.2.2.
Entropy condition

We show that the solution satisfies the Oleinik entropy condition in
\Omega.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
0<x<1,
and
t>0.
If
x
is a point of discontinuity of
u(\cdot,t),
then we have

\displaystyle u(x-,t)>u(x,t)>u(x+,t).

Moreover, for almost all
t>0,
we have the following:

(i)

u(\cdot,t)
is either right continuous at
x=0
or
u(0,t)>u(0+,t),

(ii)

u(\cdot,t)
is either left continuous at
x=1
or
u(1-,t)>u(1,t).

Proof.

We consider the points
(x,t),
where
G_{b_{r}}(x,t)<\min\left\{F(x,t),G_{b_{l}}(x,t)\right\}
and we have
\xi_{*}(x,t)<\xi^{*}(x,t).
Note that, using the semicontinuity of
\xi(x,t),
we also have
u(x-,t)=\frac{x-1}{t-\xi_{*}(x,t)}
and
u(x+,t)=\frac{x-1}{t-\xi^{*}(x,t)}.
From

G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x,t)=G_{b_{r}}(\xi^{*}(x,t),x,t)\,,

we know that

\int_{\xi_{*}(x,t)}^{\xi^{*}(x,t)}[x-1-u_{b_{r}}(\eta)(t-\eta)]\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)u_{b_{r}}(\eta)d\eta=0\,.

This implies

\frac{x-1}{t-\xi_{*}(x,t)}\int_{\xi_{*}(x,t)}^{\xi^{*}(x,t)}\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)u_{b_{r}}(\eta)d\eta=\int_{\xi_{*}(x,t)}^{\xi^{*}(x,t)}\frac{(t-\eta)}{t-\xi_{*}(x,t)}u^{2}_{b_{r}}(\eta)\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)d\eta.

Since
u_{b_{r}}<0,
we can conclude
u(x-,t)>u(x,t)
. The other inequality also follows easily by dividing the above equality by
(t-\xi^{*}(x,t))
.

Next we consider the case
F(x,t)=G_{b_{r}}(x,t).
If this happens in a region, then the solution is continuous and corresponds to a rarefaction wave from
(1,0),
see Remark
2.11
. Now we assume that the equality holds in the isolated points. We note that

\displaystyle F(y^{*}(x,t),x,t)-G_{b_{r}}(\xi^{*}(x,t),x,t)<F(y^{*}(x,t),1,\xi^{*}(x,t))-G_{b_{r}}(\xi^{*}(x,t),1,\xi^{*}(x,t)).

Simplifying the above inequality, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y^{*}(x,t)}\left[(t-\xi^{*}(x,t))u_{0}(\eta)+(1-x)\right]\rho_{0}(\eta)\,d\eta

\displaystyle<\int_{0}^{\xi^{*}(x,t)}\left[x-1-u_{b_{r}}(t-\xi^{*}(x,t))\right]u_{b_{r}}(\eta)\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)\,d\eta

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}\left[(t-\xi^{*}(x,t))u_{0}(\eta)+(1-x)\right]\rho_{0}(\eta)\,d\eta.

Dividing the above inequality by
t-\xi^{*}(x,t)
in both side, we get

u(x,t)>\frac{x-1}{t-\xi^{*}(x,t)}:=u(x+,t).

Note that, to obtain the above inequality, we also used

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y^{*}(x,t)}\rho_{0}(\eta)\,d\eta+\int_{0}^{\xi^{*}(x,t)}u_{b_{r}}(\eta)\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)\,d\eta-\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{0}(\eta)\,d\eta<0.

To get the other way inequality, i.e.,
\frac{x-y_{*}(x,t)}{t}=:u(x-,t)>u(x,t),
we use

\displaystyle G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),x,t)-F(y_{*}(x,t),x,t)<G_{b_{r}}(\xi_{*}(x,t),y_{*}(x,t),0)-F(y_{*}(x,t),y_{*}(x,t),0),

and proceed similarly.

Now we consider the case
G_{b_{l}}(x,t)=G_{b_{r}}(x,t).
By Remark
2.11
, this can happen only at isolated points. In this case, we have
u(x+,t)=\frac{x-1}{t-\xi^{*}(x,t)}
and
u(x-,t)=\frac{x}{t-\tau^{*}(x,t)}.
Consider the inequality

\displaystyle G_{b_{l}}(\tau^{*}(x,t),x,t)-G_{b_{r}}(\xi^{*}(x,t),x,t)<G_{b_{l}}(\tau^{*}(x,t),1,\xi^{*}(x,t))-G_{b_{r}}(\xi^{*}(x,t),1,\xi^{*}(x,t)).

Simplifying the above inequality, we get

\displaystyle\frac{x-1}{t-\xi^{*}(x,t)}\left[\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}(x,t)}u_{b_{l}}(\eta)\rho_{u_{l}}(\eta)\,d\eta-\int_{0}^{\xi^{*}(x,t)}u_{b_{r}}(\eta)\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)\,d\eta+\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{0}(\eta)\,d\eta\right]

\displaystyle<\left[\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}(x,t)}u^{2}_{b_{l}}(\eta)\rho_{u_{l}}(\eta)\,d\eta-\int_{0}^{\xi^{*}(x,t)}u^{2}_{b_{r}}(\eta)\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)\,d\eta+\int_{0}^{1}u_{0}(\eta)\rho_{0}(\eta)\,d\eta\right]

and this gives

\displaystyle u(x+,t)<u(x,t).

For the other inequality, we consider

\displaystyle G_{b_{r}}(\xi^{*}(x,t),x,t)-G_{b_{l}}(\tau^{*}(x,t),x,t)<G_{b_{r}}(\xi^{*}(x,t),0,\tau^{*}(x,t))-G_{b_{l}}(\tau^{*},0,\tau^{*}(x,t)).

Similarly as above, simplifying the above, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{x}{t-\tau^{*}(x,t)}\left[\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}(x,t)}u_{b_{l}}(\eta)\rho_{b_{l}}(\eta)\,d\eta-\int_{0}^{\xi^{*}(x,t)}u_{b_{r}}(\eta)\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)\,d\eta+\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{0}(\eta)\,d\eta\right]

\displaystyle>\left[\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}(x,t)}u^{2}_{b_{l}}(\eta)\rho_{b_{l}}(\eta)\,d\eta-\int_{0}^{\xi^{*}(x,t)}u^{2}_{b_{r}}(\eta)\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)\,d\eta+\int_{0}^{1}u_{0}(\eta)\rho_{0}(\eta)\,d\eta\right]

which implies
u(x-,t)>u(x,t).
This completes the proof of the interior case.

The first case
(i)
is shown in
[
23
]
. Now we show
(ii).
If
\min\{F(1,t),G_{b_{l}}(1,t)\}>G_{b_{r}}(1,t),
then the right continuity follows from Lemma
3.8
. Next, consider the case
\min\{F(1,t),G_{b_{l}}(1,t)\}\leq G_{b_{r}}(1,t).
Let
\min\{F(1,t),G_{b_{l}}(1,t)\}=G_{b_{l}}(1,t)<G_{b_{r}}(1,t).
We note that, from the continuity of
G_{b_{l}}
and
G_{b_{r}},
it can be concluded that in a neighborhood of
(1,t),
the inequality
G_{b_{l}}(x,t)<G_{b_{r}}(x,t)
holds.

From the Definition
2.12
, in this neighborhood
u
is given by

\displaystyle u(x,t)=\begin{cases}\frac{x}{t-\tau^{*}(x,t)}\,\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,\tau^{*}(x,t)=\tau_{*}(x,t),\\
\frac{\int_{\tau_{*}(x,t)}^{\tau^{*}(x,t)}\rho_{b_{l}}u^{2}_{b_{l}}\,d\eta}{\int_{\tau_{*}(x,t)}^{\tau^{*}(x,t)}\rho_{b_{l}}u_{b_{l}}\,d\eta}\,\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,\tau^{*}(x,t)<t_{*}(x,t).\end{cases}

In the first case,
u(1-,t)=\frac{1}{t-\tau^{*}(1-,t)}>0.
In the second case, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{*}(x,t)}^{\tau^{*}(x,t)}\left(x-u_{b_{l}}(\eta)(t-\eta)\right)u_{b_{l}}(\eta)\rho_{b_{l}}(\eta)\,d\eta=0.

From this, we get

\displaystyle tu(x,t)\geq x+\tau^{*}(x,t)\frac{\int_{\tau_{*}(x,t)}^{\tau^{*}(x,t)}u^{2}_{b_{l}}\rho_{b_{l}}\,d\eta}{\int_{\tau_{*}(x,t)}^{\tau^{*}(x,t)}u_{b_{l}}\rho_{b_{l}}\,d\eta}=x+\tau^{*}(x,t)u(x,t).

Passing to the limit
x\nearrow 1,
we obtain
(t-\tau^{*}(1-,t))u(1-,t)\geq 1,
which gives
u(1-,t)>0.
Therefore, from the Definition
2.12
, we conclude
u(1-,t)>u(1,t).

If
\min\{F(1,t),G_{b_{l}}(1,t)\}=F(1,t),
again by the continuity of
F,G_{b_{r}},
it follows that in a neighborhood of
(1,t),
we have
F(x,t)<G_{b_{r}}(x,t).
Now we can follow a similar argument as above, indeed we have,

\displaystyle u(x,t)=\begin{cases}\frac{x-y_{*}(x,t)}{t}\,\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,y^{*}(x,t)=y_{*}(x,t),\\
\frac{\int_{y_{*}(x,t)}^{y^{*}(x,t)}\rho_{0}u_{0}\,d\eta}{\int_{y_{*}(x,t)}^{y^{*}(x,t)}\rho_{0}\,d\eta}\,\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,y^{*}(x,t)<y_{*}(x,t).\end{cases}

In the first case, passing to the limit
x\nearrow 1,
we get,
u(1-,t)=\frac{1-y_{*}(1,t)}{t}
which is strictly positive except
y_{*}(1,t)=1.
Suppose,
y_{*}(1,t)=1,
then we have

\displaystyle F(1,t)=F(1,1,t)\geq\int_{0}^{\xi}-(t-\eta)u^{2}_{b_{r}}(\eta)\rho_{b_{r}}(\eta)\,d\eta+F(1,1,t)=G_{b_{r}}(1,t),

which is a contradiction to our assumption
F(1,t)<G_{b_{r}}(1,t).
In the second case, we note

\displaystyle\int_{y_{*}(x,t)}^{y^{*}(x,t)}\left(tu_{0}(\eta)+\eta-x\right)\rho_{0}(\eta)\,d\eta=0.

This implies

\displaystyle tu(x,t)-x=-\frac{\int_{y_{*}(x,t)}^{y^{*}(x,t)}\eta\rho_{0}(\eta)\,d\eta}{\int_{y_{*}(x,t)}^{y^{*}(x,t)}\rho_{0}(\eta)\,d\eta}\geq-y^{*}(x,t),

and thus, passing to the limit
x\nearrow 1,
we get
tu(1-,t)\geq 1-y^{*}(1-,t)=1-y_{*}(1,t)>0.
This gives
u(1-,t)>0=u(1,t).
Finally, we observe that the equality of potentials at the boundary can be handled similarly to the interior case. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Combining (
3.18
), (
3.25
), Lemma
3.8
and Lemma
3.9
, we conclude the proof.
∎

4.
Explicit examples with Riemann data

In this section, we provide some explicit examples when the initial and boundary data are of Riemann type. In our example, we show that the choice of initial and boundary data can lead to mass accumulation on the right boundary at
\left\{x=1\right\}
and also on the left boundary
\{x=0\}.

4.1.
Concentration of mass on the right boundary

We start our example with the following specific choice of initial and boundary data.

\displaystyle u_{0}=a,u_{b_{l}}=\tilde{b},u_{b_{r}}=b,\rho_{0}=\rho_{b_{l}}=\rho_{b_{r}}=1,\,\,\,\text{with}\,\,\,a<b<0,\tilde{b}>0\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\tilde{b}+a>0.

Note that the restrictions on the data immediately imply
\tilde{b}+b>0.

From the definition of initial and boundary potentials, we get

\displaystyle F(y,x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\left(y+(at-x)\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(at-x\right)^{2},

\displaystyle G_{b_{l}}(\tau,x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\tilde{b}\tau+x-\tilde{b}t\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(x-\tilde{b}t\right)^{2},

\displaystyle G_{b_{r}}(\xi,x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\left(b\xi+(x-1-bt)\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(x-1-bt\right)^{2}+(at-x)+\frac{1}{2}.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle arg\min_{0\leq y\leq 1}F(y,x,t)=\begin{cases}x-at\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,0\leq x-at\leq 1,\\
0\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,x-at<0,\\
1\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,1<x-at,\end{cases}arg\min_{\tau\geq 0}G_{b_{l}}(\tau,x,t)=\begin{cases}\frac{\tilde{b}t-x}{\tilde{b}}\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,\tilde{b}t-x\geq 0,\\
0\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,\tilde{b}t-x<0,\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle arg\min_{\xi\geq 0}G_{b_{r}}(\xi,x,t)=\begin{cases}\frac{bt-x+1}{b}\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,bt-x+1\leq 0,\\
0\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,bt-x+1>0.\end{cases}

Therefore, we get

\displaystyle F(x,t)=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{2}\left(at-x\right)^{2}\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,0\leq x-at\leq 1,\\
0\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,x-at<0,\\
\frac{1}{2}+at-x\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,1<x-at,\end{cases}G_{b_{l}}(x,t)=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{2}\left(x-\tilde{b}t\right)^{2}\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,\tilde{b}t-x\geq 0,\\
0\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,\tilde{b}t-x<0,\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle G_{b_{r}}(x,t)=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{2}\left(x-1-bt\right)^{2}+(at-x)+\frac{1}{2}&\text{if}\,\,\,bt-x+1\leq 0,\\
at-x+\frac{1}{2}\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,bt-x+1>0.\end{cases}

Now we study the curves generated by the potentials.

Curve generated by the potentials
F
and
G_{b_{l}}:
We have the following
X_{1}(t)
generated by the potentials
F
and
G_{b_{l}}.
Indeed,

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(at-x\right)^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}(x-\tilde{b}t)^{2}

and this implies
X_{1}(t):=\frac{a+\tilde{b}}{2}t.
Also note that
\dot{X}_{1}(t)>0.
Moreover, mass on the curve
X_{1}(t)
is
(\tilde{b}-a)t\delta.
One can easily check the equality
q(x_{1}+,t)-q(x_{1}-,t)=\dot{X_{1}}(t)[m].

Curve generated by the potentials
G_{b_{l}}
and the rarefaction region
\{G_{b_{r}}=F\}:
Similarly as above, we can find a curve
X_{2}(t)
by

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(x-\tilde{b}t\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{2}+at-x

and this gives
X_{2}(t)=\tilde{b}t+1-\sqrt{2(\tilde{b}-a)t}.
We have
\dot{X_{2}}(t)>0
for
t\geq\tfrac{2}{\tilde{b}-a}
and the mass along the curve is
\sqrt{2(\tilde{b}-a)t}\delta.
Moreover, it can be checked
q(x_{2}+,t)-q(x_{2}-,t)=\dot{X_{2}}(t)[m].

Curve generated by the potentials
G_{b_{l}}
and

Remark 4.1
.

(i)  In Figure
4.1
, the left boundary data
\tilde{b}
is chosen to be sufficiently large and positive. This strong positive boundary data imposes a dominant influence, driving the entire mass toward the right boundary over time. On the other hand, in Figure
4.2
, the right boundary data
b
is set to be significantly negative. This negative boundary data effectively pushes all the mass toward the left boundary. These setups demonstrate how the choice of boundary conditions can control the direction of mass flow, ensuring that it is driven entirely toward the specified boundary.

(ii)  In above the Figures
4.1
-
4.2
, we illustrate the values of
(\rho,u)
within regions separated by rarefaction waves or shocks. Additionally, one can compute the value of
u
at any point lying on the shock curve using the formula provided in Definition
2.12
. For example, consider Figure
4.1
and calculate the value of
u
at a point
(x,t)
located on the curve
X_{3}(t)
. Note that, at any point
(X_{3}(t),t),
it holds that
G_{b_{r}}(x,t)=G_{b_{l}}(x,t).
Thus, applying the formula given in Definition
2.12
, we have

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}(x,t)}\rho_{b_{l}}u^{2}_{b_{l}}-\int_{0}^{\xi^{*}(x,t)}\rho_{b_{r}}u^{2}_{b_{r}}+\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{0}u_{0}}{\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}(x,t)}\rho_{b_{l}}u_{b_{l}}-\int_{0}^{\xi^{*}(x,t)}\rho_{b_{r}}u_{b_{r}}+\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{0}}.

Substituting the explicit values of the initial and boundary datum, we obtain

\displaystyle u(x,t):=\frac{\int_{0}^{\frac{\tilde{b}t-x}{\tilde{b}}}\tilde{b}^{2}-\int_{0}^{\frac{bt-x+1}{b}}b^{2}+a}{\int_{0}^{\frac{\tilde{b}t-x}{\tilde{b}}}\tilde{b}-\int_{0}^{\frac{bt-x+1}{b}}b+1}.

Simplifying further using the equation of the curve
X_{3}(t),
we get:

\displaystyle u(x,t)=\frac{\tilde{b}(\tilde{b}t-x)-b(bt-x+1)+a}{(\tilde{b}-b)t}=\frac{\tilde{b}+b}{2}.

Note that this aligns with Lemma
2.9
, where solutions are interpreted as the slopes of the curves and here
\frac{\tilde{b}+b}{2}
is precisely the slope of the curve
X_{3}(t).

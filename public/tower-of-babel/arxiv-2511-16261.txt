Exponential map in DT theory
Šarūnas Kaubrys

Abstract
. This paper studies the Cohomological Donaldson-Thomas theory of loop stacks of 0 0 -shifted symplectic stacks. In particular, we compare ( − 1 ) (-1) -shifted tangent stacks of these moduli problems, which we view as additive, to loop stacks, which we view as multiplicative, via an exponential map that preserves induced ( − 1 ) (-1) -shifted symplectic structures. As an application, we prove for certain moduli of objects of 2 2 -Calabi-Yau categories a loop dimensional reduction theorem for the loop stacks of these moduli spaces. Finally, we prove a loop version of nonabelian Hodge theory for stacks in the GL n \operatorname{GL}_{n} case.

1.1.
Dimensional reduction

In certain cases, cohomological DT invariants recover known cohomology theories. Consider a quasismooth derived artin stack
X

[

(1.1)

(-1)
-shifted symplectic stacks comes from coherent sheaves on
3

3
-Calabi-Yau categories. Denote by
\mathrm{Coh}X

(1.3)

Dimensional reduction relates the DT invariants of the
3

\mathrm{Tot}_{S}(K_{S})
to the Borel-Moore homology of the stack of coherent sheaves on the base surface
S

Therefore, dimensional reduction relates
3
-dimensional moduli problems to
2
-dimensional moduli problems.

1.8.
Future work

In this paper we have mostly restricted to concrete applications for specific moduli problems over
\operatorname{GL}_{n}
. This is ultimately due to the fact that the exponential map for other groups is not surjective, which means we cannot immediately produce a cover
\operatorname{T}[-1]X\to\mathcal{L}X
. Furthermore, as far as the author is aware there is no theory of good moduli spaces for complex analytic stacks, which makes some arguments more difficult. The author hopes to give a unified approach to the exponential map in derived complex analytic geometry in the future with more general applications to DT theory. In particular, the results of this paper should be strengthened to handle general moduli of objects of
2
-Calabi-Yau categories with good moduli theory.

5.

6.

In this section we will explain the construction of a complex analytic exponential map in our cases of interest.

6.1.
Base case

We establish here basic facts about the exponential map
\exp\colon\mathfrak{g}\to G
we will use later. We define the étale locus.

Lemma 6.1

(Derivative of the exponential map)
.

Let
\exp\colon\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}
be the exponential map. The derivative of
\exp
is

(6.1)

d\exp_{X}Y=\exp(X)\frac{1-\exp(-\operatorname{ad}_{X})}{\operatorname{ad}_{X}}Y

then
\exp
is étale when
\operatorname{ad}_{X}
is invertible. Equivalently the exponential map is étale for all
X\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
that satisfy

(6.2)

\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\neq 2\pi ik\text{ for any two eigenvalues of }X\text{ and }k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus 0.

Notation 6.2
.

We will call the condition in equation (
6.2
) the étale condition for an endomorphism. We write
\mathfrak{gl}^{\operatorname{et}}_{n}
for the space of matrices in
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
that satisfy condition (
6.2
).

Taking the exponential of another reductive group
G
we can similarly define the étale locus of
\mathfrak{g}
, also denoted
\mathfrak{g}^{\operatorname{et}}
, by taking away the locus where the derivative of
\exp
vanishes.

Lemma 6.3
.

The exponential map
\exp\colon\mathfrak{gl}^{\operatorname{et}}_{n}\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}
preserves stabilisers of diagonalisable matrices in
\mathfrak{gl}^{\operatorname{et}}_{n}
under the conjugation action of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}
.

Proof.

It is enough to check the claim on any representative of a diagonalizable matrix in the orbit under conjugation since the stabilisers are isomorphic under conjugation. Therefore, we may assume that
D\in\mathfrak{gl}^{\operatorname{et}}
is diagonal with
D=\operatorname{diag}(\gamma_{1}I_{\lambda_{1}},\dots,\gamma_{l}I_{\lambda_{l}})
corresponding to some partition
\lambda
of
n
of length
l
and
\gamma_{i}\neq\gamma_{j}
for
i\neq j
. Now the stabiliser of
D
only depends on the partition
\lambda
and not on the values
\gamma_{i}
so the only way it can change is if
\exp\gamma_{i}=\exp\gamma_{j}
but this implies that

(6.3)

\gamma_{i}-\gamma_{j}=2\pi ik

for
k\neq 0
. This would contradict the condition in equation (
6.2
) so the exponential preserves stabilisers.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

The commutative square

(6.4)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{gl}^{\operatorname{et}}_{n}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{GL}_{n}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{S}\!^{n}\mathbb{G}^{\operatorname{et}}_{a}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{S}\!^{n}\mathbb{G}_{m}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\exp}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\exp}

is a pullback of complex analytic spaces.

Proof.

Recall that we can view

as the space of diagonal

matrices up to permutation that satisfy the condition in equation (

) and

as the space of diagonal matrices up to permutation with non-zero entries. The pullback

has points

where

is the diagonal

matrix that contains the eigenvalues of

up to multiplicity. We can define a map

\displaystyle f\colon\mathfrak{gl}^{\operatorname{et}}_{n}

\displaystyle\to P

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto(x_{ss},\exp x)

where

is the diagonal matrix containing eigenvalues of

up to multiplicity. The map

is surjective since the exponential map is still surjective once restricted to
\mathfrak{gl}^{\operatorname{et}}_{n}
. We will now show that

is injective. Assume that we have

Now consider Jordan-Chevalley decompositions of

Then we can take the exponential to get the equation

Accuracy of the Ensemble Kalman Filter in the Near-Linear Setting
E. Calvello a,, P. Monmarché b,, A. M. Stuart c,, U. Vaes d,

Abstract
The filtering distribution captures the statistics of the state of a dynamical system from partial and noisy observations. Classical particle filters provably approximate this distribution in quite general settings; however they behave poorly for high dimensional problems, suffering weight collapse. This issue is circumvented by the ensemble Kalman filter which is an equal-weight interacting particle system. However, this finite particle system is only proven to approximate the true filter in the linear Gaussian case. In practice, however, it is applied in much broader settings; as a result, establishing its approximation properties more generally is important. There has been recent progress in the theoretical analysis of the algorithm, establishing stability and error estimates in non-Gaussian settings, but the assumptions on the dynamics and observation models rule out the unbounded vector fields that arise in practice and the analysis applies only to the mean field limit of the ensemble Kalman filter. The present work establishes error bounds between the filtering distribution and the finite particle ensemble Kalman filter when the dynamics and observation vector fields may be unbounded, allowing linear growth.



Keywords.

Ensemble Kalman filter, Stochastic filtering, Weighted total variation metric, Stability estimates,
Accuracy estimates, Near-Linear setting.

Introduction

1.1
Literature Review, Contributions and Outline

Algorithms for filtering employ noisy observations arising from a possibly random dynamical system to estimate the distribution of the state of the dynamical system conditional on the observations.
The Kalman filter
[
16
]
determines the filtering distribution exactly for linear Gaussian dynamics and observations. The extended Kalman filter was developed as an extension of the Kalman filtering technique to nonlinear problems and is based on a linearization approximation; see
[
15
]
and
[
2
]
. The linearization approximation leads to an inexact distribution for nonlinear problems, and furthermore requires evaluation of covariance matrices, making the methodology impractical for large-scale geophysical applications
[
14
]
. Particle filters or sequential Monte Carlo methods
[
11
,
9
]
offer an alternative methodology for nonlinear filtering problems, allowing recovery of the exact filtering distribution in the large particle limit. However, this class of methods scales poorly with dimension, and in particular suffers
weight collapse, making its application to geophysical problems prohibitive; see for example
[
22
,
5
,
20
,
1
]
.

The ensemble Kalman filter was introduced in the seminal paper
[
12
]
.
Its success stems from the low-rank approximation of large covariances, cheaply computed using an ensemble of particles, which allows the methodology to be deployed in geophysical applications.
However analysis of
its accuracy, in relation to the true filtering distribution, remains in its infancy. The papers

[
18
,
19
]
studied this issue in the linear Gaussian setting where
the mean field limit of the Kalman filter is exact; they demonstrate that the ensemble Kalman filter
may be viewed as an interacting particle system approximation of the mean field limit, and establish Monte Carlo type error bounds. The recent article
[
7
]
overviews
the formulation of ensemble Kalman methods using mean field dynamical systems and provides
a platform from which the analyses of
[
18
,
19
]
may be generalized
beyond the linear Gaussian setting. In the recent paper
[
8
]
the authors
establish stability properties of the mean field ensemble Kalman filter and use them to
prove accuracy of the filter in a near-Gaussian setting. However, the paper does not consider
finite particle approximations of the mean field, and the conditions on the dynamics-observation model require boundedness of the vector fields arising. In this paper we address both these issues, establishing
error bounds between the finite particle ensemble Kalman filter and the true filtering
distribution in settings where the dynamics and observation vector fields grow linearly.
We make the following contributions.

1.

Theorem

2.1
is a stability result for the mean field ensemble Kalman filter in the setting of dynamical models and Lipschitz obervation operators that grow at most linearly at infinity.

2.

Theorem

2.3
quantifies the error between the mean field ensemble Kalman filter and the true filter in the setting of dynamical models and Lipschitz observation operators that are near-linear.

3.

Theorem

2.4
quantifies the error between the finite particle ensemble Kalman filter (found as an interacting particle system approximation of the mean field) and the true filter in the setting of near-linear, Lipschitz dynamical models and linear observation operators.

In going beyond the work in
[
8
]
,
the current paper simultaneously addresses a more applicable problem class,
by allowing linear growth in the dynamics and observation operators,
and confronts the substantial technical challenges which arise from doing so.
Bounds on moments of the filtering distribution and mean field ensemble Kalman filter must be established; these bounds grow in the number of iterations which is in contrast to
[
8
]
where the
L^{\infty}
bounds on model and observation operator ensure a uniform bound on moments. A further challenge is presented by the need to establish stability bounds for the conditioning map, giving rise to the true filter, and the transport map giving rise to the ensemble Kalman filter. These results exhibit dependence on moments in the stability constants and require control of the growth given by the dynamics; this is again in contrast to
[
8
]
where the
L^{\infty}
bounds allow for a uniform control.

1.3
Filtering Distribution

Here we introduce the hidden Markov model that gives rise to the filtering distribution. We employ
notation similar to that established in
[
7
,
8
]
.
We consider
\{u_{j}\}_{j\in\llbracket 0,J\rrbracket}\subset\real^{d_{u}}
to be unknown states
to be determined from associated observations
\{y_{j}\}_{j\in\llbracket 1,J\rrbracket}\subset\real^{d_{y}}

\displaystyle u_{j+1}

\displaystyle=\Psi(u_{j})+\xi_{j},\qquad

\displaystyle\xi_{j}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma),

(1.4a)

\displaystyle y_{j+1}

\displaystyle=h(u_{j+1})+\eta_{j+1},\qquad

\displaystyle\eta_{j+1}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Gamma).

(1.4b)

We assume that the initial state is a Gaussian random variable

and that the following independence condition is satisfied:

u_{0}\perp\!\!\!\!\perp\xi_{0}\perp\!\!\!\!\perp\dotsb\perp\!\!\!\!\perp\xi_{J-1}\perp\!\!\!\!\perp\eta_{1}\perp\!\!\!\!\perp\dotsb\perp\!\!\!\!\perp\eta_{J}.

(1.5)

We define the conditional distribution of the state

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\mu(u)

(1.7a)

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}_{j}\mu(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\bigl\lvert y_{j+1}^{\dagger}-h(u)\bigr\rvert_{\Gamma}^{2}\right)\mu(u)}{\displaystyle\int_{\real{}^{d_{u}}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\bigl\lvert y_{j+1}^{\dagger}-h(U)\bigr\rvert_{\Gamma}^{2}\right)\mu(U)\,\mathrm{d}U}

\displaystyle\mathsf{Q}\mu(u,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{d_{y}}\det\Gamma}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\bigl\lvert y-h(u)\bigr\rvert_{\Gamma}^{2}\right)\mu(u),

\displaystyle\mathsf{B}_{j}\mu(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu(u,y_{j+1}^{\dagger})}{\displaystyle\int_{\real{}^{d_{u}}}\mu(U,y_{j+1}^{\dagger})\,\mathrm{d}U}.

\mu_{j+1}=\mathsf{B}_{j}\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu_{j}.

The Ensemble Kalman Filter

We begin in
Subsection

2.1
by introducing the specific form of the mean field ensemble Kalman filter
that we consider in this paper; other versions leading to implementable algorithms and for which similar analysis may be developed can be found in
[
7
]

2.1
The Algorithm

The ensemble Kalman filter as implemented in practice may be derived as a particle approximation of

\displaystyle\widehat{u}_{j+1}

\displaystyle\widehat{y}_{j+1}

2.3
Error Estimate: Mean Field Ensemble Kalman Filter

It is possible to deduce from the result of
Theorem

that the error between the true filter and the mean field ensemble Kalman filter can be arbitrarily small
if the true filter is arbitrarily close to its Gaussian projection, in state-observation space.
This condition on the true filter, which we refer to as “closeness to Gaussian”, can be satisfied in the setting of unbounded vector fields by considering small perturbations of affine vector fields, as we prove in

Proposition 2.2

(Approximation Result)
.

Suppose that the data
Y^{\dagger}=\{y_{j}^{\dagger}\}_{j=1}^{J}
and the matrices

(\Sigma,\Gamma)
satisfy
Assumptions

(H1)
and
(H2)
.
Fix
\kappa_{\Psi},\kappa_{h}>0
and assume that

and

Assumptions

(V1)
and
(V2)
, respectively,
while also satisfying
Assumptions

(H3)
and
(H4)
with
\kappa_{\Psi},\kappa_{h}
.
Let

denote the true filtering distribution associated with
functions
(\Psi,h)

such that for all
\varepsilon\in[0,1]

and all
(\Psi,h)\in B_{L^{\infty}}\bigl((\Psi_{0},h_{0}),\varepsilon\bigr)

it holds that

\max_{j\in}d_{g}(\mathsf{G}\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu_{j},\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu_{j})\leqslant C\varepsilon.

(2.10)

Proof.

In what follows
(\mu^{0}_{j})_{j\in}
and
(\mu_{j})_{j\in}
denote the true filtering distributions associated with functions
(\Psi_{0},h_{0})
and
(\Psi,h)

and

) and (

By

Throughout this proof,

denotes a constant whose value is irrelevant in the context,
depends only on the constants
m_{0},C_{0},\kappa_{y},\kappa_{\Psi},\kappa_{h},\Sigma,\Gamma,j

(in particular, it does not depend on

,
\Psi

. Note that the filtering distribution defined by

\displaystyle d_{g}(\mathsf{G}\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu_{j},\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu_{j})

B.1
and
B.2
we may deduce that
there exists
R\geqslant 1
such that

3
Discussion and Future Directions

In this paper we have presented the first analysis, in the setting of a filtering problem defined by nonlinear state space dynamics, quantifying the error between the empirical measure obtained by the finite particle ensemble Kalman filter and the true filtering distribution. The analysis for the EnKF outlined is based on the proof methodology developed for particle filters in
[
20
]
and developed for the mean field ensemble Kalman filter in
[
8
]
.
Our work goes beyond
[
8
]
by considering finite particle
filters and by allowing for dynamical models and observation operators that are unbounded. The work builds substantially on the new avenue for analysis of the ensemble Kalman methodology that was introduced in
[
8
]
but
leaves open numerous avenues of investigation for further work; we detail these.

1.

As surveyed in
[
7
]
, the ensemble Kalman filtering methodology may be used for solving inverse problems and for sampling; it is of interest to extend the analysis in our paper to the ensemble Kalman based inversion algorithms outlined in that paper.

2.

There is a substantial body of literature studying the continuous time limits of ensemble Kalman methods; see
[
7
]
for a review. Performing analysis
analogous to that presented here, but in the continuous time setting, would be of interest.

3.

For large scale applications, there has been recent wide interest in replacing the dynamical model
\Psi
, representing the solution operator obtained via a high fidelity numerical solver, with a cheap to evaluate surrogate. Multifidelity ensemble methods

[
4
]
allow the use of a small number of particles evolved according to the high fidelity solver and a large number evolved according to the surrogate. Extending our error analysis to incorporate the effect of model error would be of interest in this context.

Lemma B.3

(Moment Bound for the True Filtering Distribution)
.

Remark B.4
.

•

In some situations,
the bound (
B.15

For example, if

such that

In this setting, better control of the moments can be achieved than in (

•

In obtaining (
B.15
),

other than the moment bound (

With this approach, the presence of an exponential in the bound (

Indeed, consider the case where

,

,

\displaystyle=1+\left\lvert\Psi(v)\right\rvert^{2}+\trace(\Sigma)

\displaystyle\leqslant 1+2\kappa_{\Psi}^{2}+2\kappa_{\Psi}^{2}\lvert v\rvert^{2}+\trace(\Sigma)\leqslant\Bigl(1+2\kappa_{\Psi}^{2}+\trace(\Sigma)\Bigr)g(v).

By Fubini’s theorem,
it follows that

\displaystyle\Bigl\lvert\mathsf{P}\mu[f]-\mathsf{P}\nu[f]\Bigr\rvert

\displaystyle=\biggl\lvert\int_{\real{}^{d_{u}}}\left(\int_{\real{}^{d_{u}}}f(u)\,p(v,\mathrm{d}u)\right)\bigl(\mu(\mathrm{d}v)-\nu(\mathrm{d}v)\bigr)\biggr\rvert

\displaystyle=\Bigl(1+2\kappa_{\Psi}^{2}+\trace(\Sigma)\Bigr)\Bigl\lvert\mu[g]-\nu[g]\Bigr\rvert\leqslant\Bigl(1+2\kappa_{\Psi}^{2}+\trace(\Sigma)\Bigr)\,d_{g}(\mu,\nu),

thus concluding the proof.
∎

Lemma B.9

(Map
\mathsf{Q}
is Lipschitz)
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
and
h
satisfy
Assumptions

(H4)
and
(H2)
, respectively.
Then it holds that

\forall(\mu,\nu)\in\mathcal{P}(\real^{d_{u}})\times\mathcal{P}(\real^{d_{u}}),\qquad d_{g}(\mathsf{Q}\mu,\mathsf{Q}\nu)\leqslant\Bigl(1+2\kappa_{h}^{2}+\trace(\Gamma)\Bigr)d_{g}(\mu,\nu).

(B.17)

Proof.

Take
f\colon\real^{d_{u}}\times\real^{d_{y}}\to\real
satisfying
|f|\leqslant g
,
where
g(u,v)=1+|u|^{2}+|y|^{2}
.
By Fubini’s theorem,
it holds that

\mathsf{Q}\mu[f]-\mathsf{Q}\nu[f]=\int_{\real{}^{d_{u}}}\Pi f(u)\,\Bigl(\mu(\mathrm{d}u)-\nu(\mathrm{d}u)\Bigr),\qquad\text{ where }\Pi f(u):=\int_{\real{}^{d_{y}}}f(u,y)\,\mathcal{N}\bigl(h(u),\Gamma\bigr)(\mathrm{d}y).

\Pi f\colon\real^{d_{u}}\to\real

\displaystyle\forall u\in\real^{d_{u}},\qquad\left\lvert\Pi f(u)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\real{}^{d_{y}}}\bigl\lvert f(u,y)\bigr\rvert\,\mathcal{N}\bigl(h(u),\Gamma\bigr)(\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\real{}^{d_{y}}}\left(1+|u|^{2}+|y|^{2}\right)\,\mathcal{N}\bigl(h(u),\Gamma\bigr)(\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle=1+|u|^{2}+\bigl\lvert h(u)\bigr\rvert^{2}+\trace(\Gamma)\leqslant\Bigl(1+2\kappa_{h}^{2}+\trace(\Gamma)\Bigr)\left(1+\lvert u\rvert^{2}\right).

Therefore,
we deduce that

\Bigl\lvert\mathsf{Q}\mu[f]-\mathsf{Q}\nu[f]\Bigr\rvert\leqslant(1+2\kappa_{h}^{2}+\trace(\Gamma)\Bigr)\,d_{g}(\mu,\nu),

which concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma B.10

(Stability of Map
\mathsf{B}_{j}
)
.

Suppose that
Assumptions

(H1)
,
(H3)
,
(H4)
and
(H2)
are satisfied.
Then for any probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\real^{d_{u}})
with
\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mu)<\infty
there exists a constant
C_{\mathsf{B}}=C_{\mathsf{B}}\bigl(\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mu),\kappa_{y},\kappa_{\Psi},\kappa_{h},\Sigma,\Gamma\bigr)>0

such that

\forall j\in\llbracket 0,J\rrbracket,\qquad d_{g}(\mathsf{B}_{j}\mathsf{G}\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu,\mathsf{B}_{j}\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu)\leqslant C_{\mathsf{B}}d_{g}(\mathsf{G}\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu,\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu).

Proof.

The proof of this lemma follows the steps of the proof of
[
8
, Lemma B.9]
; however, each step requires different bounds reflecting the assumptions on
\Psi
and
h
in this paper. For ease of exposition we write
y^{\dagger}=y^{\dagger}_{j+1}
.
We define the
y
-marginal densities

\alpha_{\mu}(y):=\int_{\real{}^{d_{u}}}\mathsf{G}\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu(u,y)\,\mathrm{d}u\,,\qquad\beta_{\mu}(y):=\int_{\real{}^{d_{u}}}\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu(u,y)\,\mathrm{d}u\,.

By definition of
\mathsf{B}_{j}
,
it holds that

\displaystyle d_{g}(\mathsf{B}_{j}\mathsf{G}\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu,\mathsf{B}_{j}\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu)=\int_{\real{}^{d_{u}}}\left(1+|u|^{2}\right)\left|\frac{\mathsf{G}\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu(u,y^{\dagger})}{\alpha_{\mu}(y^{\dagger})}-\frac{\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu(u,y^{\dagger})}{\beta_{\mu}(y^{\dagger})}\right|\,\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leqslant\frac{1}{\alpha_{\mu}(y^{\dagger})}\int_{\real{}^{d_{u}}}\left(1+|u|^{2}\right)\bigl\lvert\mathsf{G}\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu(u,y^{\dagger})-\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu(u,y^{\dagger})\bigr\rvert\,\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left|\frac{\alpha_{\mu}(y^{\dagger})-\beta_{\mu}(y^{\dagger})}{\alpha_{\mu}(y^{\dagger})\beta_{\mu}(y^{\dagger})}\right|\,\int_{\real{}^{d_{u}}}\left(1+|u|^{2}\right)\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu(u,y^{\dagger})\,\mathrm{d}u.

(B.18)

Step 1: bounding
\alpha_{\mu}(y^{\dagger})
and
\beta_{\mu}(y^{\dagger})
from below

The distribution
\alpha_{\mu}(\mathord{\color[rgb]{0.67,0.67,0.67}\bullet})
is Gaussian with mean
\mathcal{M}^{y}(\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu)
and covariance

\mathcal{C}^{yy}(\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu)=\Gamma+\mathsf{P}\mu\bigl[h\otimes h\bigr]-\mathsf{P}\mu\bigl[h\bigr]\otimes\mathsf{P}\mu\bigl[h\bigr].

(B.19)

Clearly
\mathcal{C}^{yy}(\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu)\succcurlyeq\Gamma
.
Furthermore,
it holds that
\mathcal{C}^{yy}(\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu)\preccurlyeq\Gamma+2\kappa_{h}^{2}\bigl(1+\trace(\Sigma)+2\kappa_{\Psi}^{2}\bigl(1+\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mu)\bigr)\bigr)I_{d_{y}}
by (
B.8
).
Therefore, noting that (
B.3b
) implies that
\left\lvert\mathcal{M}^{y}(\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu)\right\rvert\leqslant C

for a constant
C
depending only on the parameters
\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mu),\kappa_{h},\kappa_{\Psi},\Sigma
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mu}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{d_{u}}\det\bigl(\mathcal{C}^{yy}(\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu)\bigr)}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\bigl(y-\mathcal{M}^{y}(\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu)\bigr)^{\mathsf{T}}\mathcal{C}^{yy}(\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu)^{-1}\bigl(y-\mathcal{M}^{y}(\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu)\bigr)\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{\exp\Bigl(-\frac{1}{2}\bigl(|y|+C\bigr)^{2}\left\lVert\Gamma^{-1}\right\rVert\Bigr)}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{d_{y}}\det\bigl(\Gamma+2\kappa_{h}^{2}\bigl(1+\trace(\Sigma)+2\kappa_{\Psi}^{2}\bigl(1+\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mu)\bigr)\bigr)I_{d_{y}}\bigr)}}.

(B.20)

The function
\beta_{\mu}
can be bounded from below using similar reasoning.
Indeed, by
Assumptions

(H3)
,
(H4)
and
(H2)
we have that for all
y\in\real^{d_{y}}
,

\displaystyle\beta_{\mu}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\real{}^{d_{u}}}\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu(u,y)\,\mathrm{d}u=\int_{\real{}^{d_{u}}}\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\bigl(y-h(u)\bigr)^{\mathsf{T}}\Gamma^{-1}\bigl(y-h(u)\bigr)\right)}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{d_{y}}\det(\Gamma)}}\mathsf{P}\mu(u)\,\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{d_{y}}\det(\Gamma)}}\exp\Bigl(-\left\lVert\Gamma^{-1}\right\rVert|y|^{2}-\left\lVert\Gamma^{-1}\right\rVert\kappa^{2}_{h}\int_{\real{}^{d_{u}}}\bigl(2+|u|^{2}\bigr)\mathsf{P}\mu(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\Bigr)

(B.21a)

\displaystyle\geqslant C\exp\Bigl(-\left\lVert\Gamma^{-1}\right\rVert|y|^{2}\Bigr),

(B.21b)

where we applied Jensen’s inequality in (
B.21a
) and
the constant in (
B.21b
) depends on
\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mu),\kappa_{\Psi},\kappa_{h},\Sigma,\Gamma
.

Step 2: bounding the first term in (
B.18
)

First note that,
for fixed
u\in\real^{d_{u}}
,
the functions
\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu(u,\mathord{\color[rgb]{0.67,0.67,0.67}\bullet})
and
\mathsf{G}\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu(u,\mathord{\color[rgb]{0.67,0.67,0.67}\bullet})
are Gaussians up to constant factors,
with covariance matrices given respectively by
\Gamma
and

\mathcal{C}^{yy}(\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu)-\mathcal{C}^{yu}(\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu)\mathcal{C}^{uu}(\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu)^{-1}\mathcal{C}^{uy}(\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu),

(B.22)

where we have used the formula for the covariance of the conditional distribution of a Gaussian. We note that
\mathcal{C}^{yu}(\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu)\mathcal{C}^{uu}(\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu)^{-1}\mathcal{C}^{uy}(\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu)
is positive semi-definite, hence by (
B.19
) and its upper bound it follows that the matrix (
B.22
) is bounded from above by
{2}\kappa_{h}^{2}\bigl(1+\trace(\Sigma)+2\kappa_{\Psi}^{2}\bigl(1+\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mu)\bigr)\bigr)I_{d_{y}}+\Gamma
.
As shown in the proof of
[
8
, Lemma B.9]
, the matrix (
B.22
) is bounded from below by
\Gamma
(see
[
8
, Equation (B.20)]
).
It follows from using
Lemma

A.2
in
Appendix

A
,
with parameter
\tau=\tau\bigl(\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mu),\kappa_{h},\kappa_{\Psi},\Sigma,\Gamma\bigr)
, that

\int_{\real{}^{d_{u}}}\left(1+|u|^{2}\right)\left|\mathsf{G}\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu(u,y)-\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu(u,y)\right|\mathrm{d}u\leqslant Cd_{g}(\mathsf{G}\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu,\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu),

(B.23)

where
C
is a constant depending on
\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mu),\kappa_{h},\kappa_{\Psi},\Sigma,\Gamma
.
We refer to
[
8
, Equation (B.21)]
for the detailed steps used to establish this bound.

Step 3: bounding the second term in (
B.18
)

In view of (
B.23
), it holds that

\bigl\lvert\alpha_{\mu}(y)-\beta_{\mu}(y)\bigr\rvert\leqslant\int_{\real{}^{d_{u}}}\left(1+|u|^{2}\right)\bigl\lvert\mathsf{G}\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu(u,y)-\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu(u,y)\bigr\rvert\,\mathrm{d}u\leqslant Cd_{g}(\mathsf{G}\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu,\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu).

Now,
since
\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu(u,\mathord{\color[rgb]{0.67,0.67,0.67}\bullet})/\mathsf{P}\mu(u)
defines a Gaussian density which has covariance

By the moment bounds in

B.1
,
the right-hand side is bounded from above by a constant which depends on
\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mu),\kappa_{\Psi},\Sigma

Step 4: concluding the proof

Putting together the above bounds, we conclude that

\displaystyle d_{g}(\mathsf{B}_{j}\mathsf{G}\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu,\mathsf{B}_{j}\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu)\leqslant\frac{C\bigl(\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mu),\kappa_{\Psi},\kappa_{h},\Sigma,\Gamma\bigr)}{\alpha_{\mu}(y^{\dagger}_{j+1})}\left(1+\frac{1}{\beta_{\mu}(y^{\dagger}_{j+1})}\right)d_{g}(\mathsf{G}\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu,\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu).

Applying the inequalities (
B.20
) and (
B.21b
) yields the desired result.

Lemma B.11

(Stability of Map
\mathsf{T}_{j}
)
.

Suppose that
Assumption

H
is satisfied.
Then, for all
R\geqslant 1
, it holds that for any
\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{R}(\real^{d_{u}}\times\real^{d_{y}})
and
p\in\{\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu:\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\real^{d_{u}})\,\text{and }\mathcal{M}_{2\cdot\max\{3+d_{u},4+d_{y}\}}(\mu)<\infty\}\subset\mathcal{P}(\real^{d_{u}}\times\real^{d_{y}})

B.2

\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{P}\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\widetilde{R}}(\real^{d_{u}}\times\real^{d_{y}})
for some
\widetilde{R}(\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mu),\kappa_{\Psi},\kappa_{h},\Sigma,\Gamma)\geqslant 1

[
8

we define

r=\max\Bigl\{R,\widetilde{R},\kappa_{y}\Bigr\}.

Letting
K=\mathcal{C}(\pi)
,
S=\mathcal{C}(p)
and
y^{\dagger}=y^{\dagger}_{j+1}

\mathscr{T}^{\pi}
and
\mathscr{T}^{p}
corresponding to use
of covariance information at the probability measures
\pi

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}^{\pi}(u,y)

\displaystyle=u+A_{\pi}(y^{\dagger}-y),\qquad

\displaystyle A_{\pi}:=K_{uy}K_{yy}^{-1},

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}^{p}(u,y)

\displaystyle=u+A_{p}(y^{\dagger}-y),\qquad

By a straightforward application of the triangle inequality, it holds that

(B.24)

In the following steps we will separately bound the two terms on the right hand side of (

[
8

where the second inequality follows again from fact that the 2-norm of any submatrix is bounded from above by the 2-norm of the full matrix,

B.6

Bounding the first term in (

Bounding the second term in (
B.24

Let

again satisfy
\lvert f\rvert\leqslant g

\Bigl\lvert\mathscr{T}^{\pi}_{\sharp}p[f]-\mathscr{T}^{p}_{\sharp}p[f]\Bigr\rvert=\Bigl\lvert p[f\circ\mathscr{T}^{\pi}]-p[f\circ\mathscr{T}^{p}]\Bigr\rvert=\Bigl\lvert p[f\circ\mathscr{T}^{\pi}-f\circ\mathscr{T}^{p}]\Bigr\rvert.

\displaystyle\Bigl\lvert p[f\circ\mathscr{T}^{\pi}-f\circ\mathscr{T}^{p}]\Bigr\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\int_{\real{}^{d_{y}}}\int_{\real{}^{d_{u}}}\Bigl(f\left(u+A_{\pi}\bigl(y^{\dagger}-y\bigr)\right)-f\left(u+A_{p}\bigl(y^{\dagger}-y\bigr)\right)\Bigr)\,p(u,y)\,\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}y\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\int_{\real{}^{d_{y}}}\int_{\real{}^{d_{u}}}\bigl(f(u+A_{\pi}z)-f(u+A_{p}z)\bigr)\,p(u,y^{\dagger}-z)\,\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{\real{}^{d_{y}}}\int_{\real{}^{d_{u}}}f(v)\Bigl(p(v-A_{\pi}z,y^{\dagger}-z)-p(v-A_{p}z,y^{\dagger}-z)\Bigr)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}z,

(B.28)

where we used a change of variables in the second equality.

\mathcal{M}_{2\cdot\max\{3+d_{u},4+d_{y}\}}(\mu)<\infty

A.3

B.28

\displaystyle\int_{\real{}^{d_{u}}}\lvert f(v)\rvert\max\left\{\frac{1}{1+\lvert v-A_{\pi}z\rvert^{\max\{3+d_{u},4+d_{y}\}}},\frac{1}{1+\lvert v-A_{p}z\rvert^{\max\{3+d_{u},4+d_{y}\}}}\right\}\,\mathrm{d}v

(B.30)

where the last inequality follows from (
B.26
).
Combining (
B.27
) and (
B.30
) yields the desired result.

Thus, by the triangle and Hölder inequalities,

\displaystyle\sqrt{N}\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lvert u^{(1)}_{j+1}-\overline{u}^{(1)}_{j+1}\right\rvert^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\ell_{\Psi}C_{j,p}+\left\lVert\mathcal{K}\bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK}}_{j+1}\bigr)\right\rVert\ell_{h}\ell_{\Psi}C_{j,p}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sqrt{N}\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\mathcal{K}\bigl(\pi^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\bigr)-\mathcal{K}\bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK}}_{j+1}\bigr)\right\rVert^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lvert y^{\dagger}_{j+1}-y^{(1)}_{j+1}\right\rvert^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}.

The first two terms on the right-hand side are bounded uniformly in
N
in view of (
D.9
).
In order to bound the last term,
we note that the term

\mathbb{E}\bigl\lvert y^{\dagger}_{j+1}-y^{(1)}_{j+1}\bigr\rvert^{2p}

is bounded uniformly in
N
by a constant which depends on
\mathcal{M}_{4^{j}\cdot 2p}\bigl(\mu_{0}^{{\rm EK}}\bigr)
; this follows by (
D.8
) and (
D.12
).
To complete the inductive step, it remains to show that

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sqrt{N}\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\mathcal{K}\Bigl(\pi^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\Bigr)-\mathcal{K}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK}}_{j+1}\Bigr)\right\rVert^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}<\infty.

To this end,
in line with the classical propagation of chaos approach,
we decompose

\displaystyle\sqrt{N}\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\mathcal{K}\Bigl(\pi^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\Bigr)-\mathcal{K}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK}}_{j+1}\Bigr)\right\rVert^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{N}\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\mathcal{K}\Bigl(\pi^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\Bigr)-\mathcal{K}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\Bigr)\right\rVert^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sqrt{N}\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\mathcal{K}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\Bigr)-\mathcal{K}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK}}_{j+1}\Bigr)\right\rVert^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}},

(D.13)

where we introduced the empirical measure associated with the mean field particles:

\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{\left(\overline{v}^{(i)}_{j+1},\overline{y}^{(i)}_{j+1}\right)}.

To conclude the proof of propagation of chaos,
we must prove that

•

The first term on the right-hand side of (
D.13
) is bounded uniformly in
N
,
which is achieved by employing the induction hypothesis (
D.11
),
as well as (
D.12
).

•

The second term on the right-hand side of (
D.13
) is also bounded uniformly in
N
.
This follows from classical arguments based on the law of large number in
L^{p}
spaces.

Let us first bound the second term.
To this end,
note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\Bigr)-\mathcal{K}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK}}_{j+1}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\cov^{uh}\left(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK}}_{j+1}\right)\left(\left(\Gamma+\cov^{hh}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\Bigr)\right)^{-1}-\left(\Gamma+\cov^{hh}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK}}_{j+1}\Bigr)\right)^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\cov^{uh}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\Bigr)-\cov^{uh}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK}}_{j+1}\Bigr)\right)\left(\Gamma+\cov^{hh}\left(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\right)\right)^{-1}.

Therefore, using that
A^{-1}-B^{-1}=B^{-1}(B-A)A^{-1}
,
we have that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{K}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\Bigr)-\mathcal{K}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK}}_{j+1}\Bigr)\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\lVert\cov^{uh}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK}}_{j+1}\Bigr)\right\rVert\lVert\Gamma^{-1}\rVert^{2}\left\lVert\cov^{hh}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\Bigr)-\cov^{hh}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK}}_{j+1}\Bigr)\right\rVert

\displaystyle\qquad+\lVert\Gamma^{-1}\rVert\left\lVert\cov^{uh}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\Bigr)-\cov^{uh}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK}}_{j+1}\Bigr)\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leqslant C(\Gamma,H)\Bigl(1+\left\lVert\cov^{uu}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK}}_{j+1}\Bigr)\right\rVert\Bigr)\left\lVert\cov^{uu}\Bigl(\pi^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\Bigr)-\cov^{uu}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK}}_{j+1}\Bigr)\right\rVert.

The term
\bigl\lVert\cov^{uu}\bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK}}_{j+1}\bigr)\bigr\rVert
is bounded by (
D.8
),
and the
L^{p}
norm of the second term tends to zero with rate
N^{-\frac{p}{2}}
by classical law of large number arguments,
see for example
[
10
, Lemma 3]
and
[
23
, Lemma 3]
; indeed it holds that

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\cov^{uu}\Bigl(\pi^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\Bigr)-\cov^{uu}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK}}_{j+1}\Bigr)\right\rVert^{2p}\leqslant\frac{C\Bigl(\mathcal{M}_{4p}\bigl(\mu^{{\rm EK}}_{0}\bigr)\Bigr)}{N^{p}}.

It remains to bound the other term.
Reasoning similarly to above, we have that

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}\Bigl(\pi^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\Bigr)-\mathcal{K}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\Bigr)\right\rVert\leqslant C(\Gamma,H)\Bigl(1+\left\lVert\cov^{uu}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\Bigr)\right\rVert\Bigr)\left\lVert\cov^{uu}\Bigl(\pi^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\Bigr)-\cov^{uu}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\Bigr)\right\rVert.

(D.14)

By
[
23
, Lemma 2]
,
it holds that

\left\lVert\cov^{uu}\Bigl(\pi^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\Bigr)-\cov^{uu}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\Bigr)\right\rVert^{2}\leqslant 2\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lvert v^{(i)}_{j+1}-\overline{v}^{(i)}_{j+1}\right\rvert^{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lvert v^{(i)}_{j+1}\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lvert\overline{v}^{(i)}_{j+1}\right\rvert^{2}\right).

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz and Jensen’s inequalities, moment bounds and finally the induction hypothesis (
D.10
) enables to conclude that

\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\cov^{uu}\Bigl(\pi^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\Bigr)-\cov^{uu}\Bigl(\overline{\pi}^{{\rm EK},N}_{j+1}\Bigr)\right\rVert^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\leqslant\frac{C\Bigl(\mathcal{M}_{4^{j+1}p}\bigl(\mu^{{\rm EK}}_{0}\bigr),J,\kappa_{y},\kappa_{\Psi},\kappa_{h},\ell_{\Psi},\ell_{h},\Sigma,\Gamma\Bigr)}{\sqrt{N}}.

Step 5: concluding the proof

Using the propagation of chaos result from (
D.10
) in (
D.6
) gives that

\left(\mathbb{E}~\Bigl|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\phi\left(u^{(i)}_{J}\right)-\phi\left(\overline{u}^{(i)}_{J}\right)\Bigr|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leqslant\frac{C}{\sqrt{N}},

for some constant
C
depending on
\mathcal{M}_{q}\bigl(\mu^{{\rm EK}}_{0}\bigr),J,L_{\phi},\varsigma,\kappa_{y},\kappa_{\Psi},\kappa_{h},\ell_{\Psi},\ell_{h},\Sigma,\Gamma
with
q=\max\{4^{J+1},4\varsigma\}
. Combining this result with (
D.5
) yields the desired result.
∎

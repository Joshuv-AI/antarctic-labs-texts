Deconstructing span categories for profinite groups
David Barnes, Niall Taggart

Abstract
. One of the major advantages of ∞ \infty -category theory over classical 1 1 -category theory is its robust and homotopically meaningful framework for taking (co)limits of diagrams of ∞ \infty -categories. However, it is both subtle and crucial to specify which variant of the ∞ \infty -category of ∞ \infty -categories is being used when forming such (co)limits. In this article, we present a concrete case study illustrating how (co)limits of ∞ \infty -categories behave in a specific setting. We demonstrate that the span category of a profinite group can be realised as the colimit of the span categories of its quotients by open normal subgroups and provide a number of applications to the world of equivariant (higher) algebra.



1.
Introduction

Since the inception of
\infty
-category theory one thing the community has always agreed upon was that a major advantage of
\infty
-categories over more classical approaches to homotopy theory is the ability to consider limits and colimits of
\infty
-categories in a homotopically meaningful and consistent fashion. For example, the literature is abound with tools and techniques for putting model structures on homotopy limits of diagrams of model categories under rather technical constraints, and the idea of homotopy colimits of model categories to this day remains subtle to the point of there being no good theory yet developed, but in the world of
\infty
-categories, these (co)limits becomes formal once you have a good
\infty
-category of
\infty
-categories. The latter exists, unlike a good model category of model categories.

Consider, for example, the stable homotopy category,
\mathcal{SH}
. Since the dawn of time, homotopy theorists have agreed that the stable homotopy category should be formed from the category of pointed spaces
\mathcal{S}_{\ast}
by formally inverting the spheres. One possibly interpretation of this characterisation is that the stable homotopy category is

\mathcal{SH}=\lim(\cdots\xrightarrow{\ \Omega\ }\mathcal{S}_{\ast}\xrightarrow{\ \Omega\ }\mathcal{S}_{\ast})

the limit of iterated applications of the loops endofunctor on pointed spaces. It is possible to prove this statement within the world of model categories, but in many ways such a proof largely reduces to luck: we are lucky that there is a convenient model category of pointed spaces satisfying the technical conditions requires for a homotopy limit model structure, we are lucky that iterating loops provides a sequential diagram of right Quillen functors, lucky that there exists a good model category of spectra, and lucky that we may construct, by hand, a Quillen equivalence between a model category of spectra and the homotopy limit model structure.

In the
\infty
-category world we can still see the stable homotopy category as inverting spheres even on days when our luck has run out. We can define the
\infty
-category of spectra
{{\mathsf{Sp}}}
to be

{{\mathsf{Sp}}}\coloneq\lim(\cdots\xrightarrow{\ \Omega\ }\mathcal{S}_{\ast}\xrightarrow{\ \Omega\ }\mathcal{S}_{\ast})

the limit in the
\infty
-category of
\infty
-categories of the loops functor on the
\infty
-category of pointed spaces, and show with little work that the underlying
\infty
-category of any good model category of spectra satisfies the same universal property as our limit construction: both are the free stable
\infty
-category generated under colimits by the one-point space.

The limit above is taken in the
\infty
-category of
\infty
-categories, but a little more is true. For instance, the
\infty
-category of pointed spaces is presentable, and the loops functor is a right adjoint, so this limit could equally well be computed in the
\infty
-category
{\mathsf{Pr}}^{R}
of presentable
\infty
-categories and right adjoints. Passing to the associated diagram of left adjoints gives a diagram in the
\infty
-category
{\mathsf{Pr}}^{L}
of presentable
\infty
-categories and left adjoints, and we can interpret the
\infty
-category of spectra as

{{\mathsf{Sp}}}\simeq\colim(\mathcal{S}_{\ast}\xrightarrow{\ \Sigma\ }\mathcal{S}_{\ast}\xrightarrow{\ \Sigma\ }\cdots)

the colimit in
{\mathsf{Pr}}^{L}
of iterated applications of the suspension functor. This latter description is much more fitting with the idea that the stable homotopy category is given by inverting the spheres.

The practice of taking (co)limits of
\infty
-categories in some variant of the
\infty
-category of
\infty
-categories, e.g.,

,

-theory

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a bicomplete cartesian closed
\infty
-category. Given a diagram

\mathcal{C}(\underset{i\in I}{\colim}~\mathcal{C}_{i},D)
and

\Map(C,\mathcal{C}(\underset{i\in I}{\colim}~\mathcal{C}_{i},D))\simeq\Map(C,\lim_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{C}_{i},D))

\displaystyle\Map(C,\mathcal{C}(\underset{i\in I}{\colim}~\mathcal{C}_{i},D))

\displaystyle\simeq\Map(\underset{i\in I}{\colim}~(C\times\mathcal{C}_{i}),D)

\displaystyle\simeq\lim_{i\in I}\Map(C\times\mathcal{C}_{i},D)

\displaystyle\simeq\lim_{i\in I}\Map(C,\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{C}_{i},D))

\displaystyle\simeq\Map(C,\lim_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{C}_{i},D))

{\mathsf{Fun}}^{\oplus}(-,\mathcal{E})
preserves limits.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a presentable semiadditive
\infty
-category and let
\mathcal{C}_{(-)}:I\to{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\infty}^{\rm sadd}
be a filtered diagram of semiadditive
\infty
-categories. There is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories

{\mathsf{Fun}}^{\oplus}(\underset{i\in I}{\colim}~\mathcal{C}_{i},\mathcal{E})\simeq\lim_{i\in I}{\mathsf{Fun}}^{\oplus}(\mathcal{C}_{i},\mathcal{E})

between
\mathcal{E}
-valued product-preserving functors on the colimit (in
{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\infty}
) of
\mathcal{C}_{(-)}
and the limit (in
{\mathsf{Pr}}^{R}
) of the categories of
\mathcal{E}
-valued functors on
\mathcal{C}_{i}
.

Proof.

Consider the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\mathsf{Fun}}(\underset{i\in I}{\colim}~\mathcal{C}_{i},\mathcal{E})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\underset{i\in I}{\lim}~{\mathsf{Fun}}(\mathcal{C}_{i},\mathcal{E})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\mathsf{Fun}}^{\oplus}(\underset{i\in I}{\colim}~\mathcal{C}_{i},\mathcal{E})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\underset{i\in I}{\lim}~{\mathsf{Fun}}^{\oplus}(\mathcal{C}_{i},\mathcal{E})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

in which the vertical arrows are fully faithful inclusions and the top-most horizontal arrow is an equivalence by
2.3
. We wish to show that the lower horizontal arrow is an equivalence of (possibly large)
\infty
-categories.

For essential surjectivity, let
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an object in
\lim_{i\in I}{\mathsf{Fun}}^{\oplus}(\mathcal{C}_{i},\mathcal{E})
i.e., for each
i\in I
, the functor
F_{i}:\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathcal{E}
preserves products and the collection of such functors forms a cocartesian section in the sense of
Section

2.1
. By embedding the cocartesian section
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
into the larger category
\lim_{i\in I}{\mathsf{Fun}}(\mathcal{C}_{i},\mathcal{E})
, we find a functor

F:\underset{i\in I}{\colim}~\mathcal{C}_{i}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E},

whose image under the top equivalence is precisely
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
. The functor
F
is product preserving by
5.3
, hence lifts to an object in
{\mathsf{Fun}}^{\oplus}(\underset{i\in I}{\colim}~\mathcal{C}_{i},\mathcal{E})
so that the bottom map is essentially surjective.

To see that the bottom arrow is fully faithful, observe that the composite

{\mathsf{Fun}}^{\oplus}(\underset{i\in I}{\colim}~\mathcal{C}_{i},\mathcal{E})\hookrightarrow{\mathsf{Fun}}(\underset{i\in I}{\colim}~\mathcal{C}_{i},\mathcal{E})\xrightarrow{\ \simeq\ }\lim_{i\in I}{\mathsf{Fun}}(\mathcal{C}_{i},\mathcal{E})

is fully faithful as the composite of two fully faithful functors. As such, it suffices to prove that the image of this composite lands in
\lim_{i\in I}{\mathsf{Fun}}^{\oplus}(\mathcal{C}_{i},\mathcal{E})
. This follows from
5.3
since
F:\underset{i\in I}{\colim}~\mathcal{C}_{i}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}
is product preserving if and only if each composite

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{C}_{i}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\underset{i\in I}{\colim}~\mathcal{C}_{i}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{E}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}

is product preserving.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a semiadditive
\infty
-category and let
\mathcal{C}_{(-)}:I\to{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\infty}^{\rm disj}
be a filtered diagram of disjunctive
\infty
-categories. There is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories

{\mathsf{Mack}}(\underset{i\in I}{\colim}~\mathcal{C}_{i}~;\mathcal{E})\simeq\lim_{i\in I}{\mathsf{Mack}}(\mathcal{C}_{i}~;\mathcal{E})

between
\mathcal{E}
-valued Mackey functors on the colimit (in
{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\infty}
) of
\mathcal{C}_{(-)}
and the limit (in
{\mathsf{Pr}}^{R}
) of the categories of
\mathcal{E}
-valued Mackey functors on
\mathcal{C}_{i}
.

Proof.

Recall that the
\infty
-category of
\mathcal{E}
-valued Mackey functor on a disjunctive
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
is defined as

{\mathsf{Mack}}(\mathcal{C}~;\mathcal{E})={\mathsf{Fun}}^{\oplus}({\mathsf{Span}}(\mathcal{C}),\mathcal{E}).

Given a filtered diagram

\mathcal{C}_{(-)}:I\to{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\infty}^{\rm disj},\quad i\longmapsto\mathcal{C}_{i}

of disjunctive
\infty
-categories,
4.8
provides a filtered diagram

{\mathsf{Span}}(\mathcal{C}_{(-)}):I\to{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\infty}^{\rm sadd},\quad i\longmapsto{\mathsf{Span}}(\mathcal{C}_{i})

of semiadditive
\infty
-categories since the inclusion
{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\infty}^{\rm sadd}\subseteq{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\infty}
preserves filtered colimits. To see this last observation, note that the inclusion factors

{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\infty}^{\rm sadd}\hookrightarrow{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\infty}^{\times}\hookrightarrow{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\infty}

through the
\infty
-category
{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\infty}^{\times}
of
\infty
-categories with finite products. The inclusion

{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\infty}^{\rm sadd}\hookrightarrow{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\infty}^{\times}

preserves all colimits since it is a left adjoint, with right adjoint given by taking commutative monoid objects. The inclusion

{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\infty}^{\times}\hookrightarrow{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\infty}

preserves filtered colimits by the formula for the product in a filtered colimit of
\infty
-categories with finite products, and hence the composite inclusion also preserves filtered colimits. The proof is now complete by an application of
5.4
.
∎

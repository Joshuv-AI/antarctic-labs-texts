An Initialization-free Quantum Algorithm for General Abelian Hidden Subgroup Problem
Sekang Kwon, Jeong San Kim

Abstract
Hidden Subgroup Problem(HSP) seeks to identify an unknown subgroup H H of a group G G for a given injective function f f defined on cosets of H H . Here we present an initialization-free quantum algorithm for solving HSP in the case where G G is a finite abelian group. Our algorithm can adopt an arbitrary unknown mixed state as the auxiliary register and removes the need for initialization while preserving computational cost comparable to existing methods. Our algorithm also restores the state of the auxiliary register to its original form after completing the computations. Since the recovered state can be utilized for other operations, a single preparation of the auxiliary register in an arbitrarily unknown mixed state is sufficient to execute the iterative procedure in solving hidden subgroup problems. This approach provides a promising direction for improving quantum algorithm efficiency by reducing operational time of initialization.



I
Introduction

Quantum computing introduces a novel paradigm for information processing, leveraging principles of quantum mechanics such as superposition and entanglement. Unlike classical computation, which uses bits as the fundamental units of information, quantum computation employs qubits, enabling the parallel exploration of multiple computational pathways. This unique capability allows quantum algorithms to solve certain computational problems exponentially faster than their classical counterparts. Notable examples include Simon’s problem
Sim97
and the period-finding problem
Sho97
, which demonstrate the transformative potential of quantum computation.

Both Simon’s problem and period-finding problem can be understood as specific instances of a broader class of computational problems known as the
Hidden Subgroup Problem (HSP)
. This framework, rooted in group theory, provides a unified description of such problems. Each instance of HSP is characterized by the group under consideration and the properties of the given oracle. For example, Simon’s problem is associated with the Boolean group
(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n},\oplus)
, while the period-finding problem corresponds to the cyclic group
(\mathbb{Z}_{N},+)
. Beyond these examples, there are also instances of HSP involving various cases of abelian and non-abelian groups
Hal07
;
HMRRS10
;
Kup05
;
Chi22
.

For the abelian HSP (AHSP), where the underlying group
G
is abelian, the problem can be solved in polynomial time with respect to the logarithm of the group order,
\log{|G|}

Lom04
. This success positions the HSP as a cornerstone in the development of quantum algorithms, showcasing their superiority over classical approaches. However, implementing quantum algorithms on real quantum computers introduces significant practical challenges, such as environmental noise, operational errors, and the demands of initialization. While considerable effort has been devoted to mitigating noise and enhancing operation accuracy, the initialization process—essential for many iterative algorithms—remains relatively underexplored.

In the context of solving HSPs, initialization plays a particularly critical role, as iterative subroutines like Quantum Fourier Sampling form the backbone of these algorithms
Lom04
. Inefficiencies in initialization can create significant bottlenecks, undermining the computational advantages offered by quantum algorithms. Addressing this overlooked aspect is therefore crucial to realizing the full potential of quantum computing for HSPs. In practice, trapped-ion systems highlight the criticality of initialization. These systems rely on sequential
resolved sideband cooling
to prepare ions in their motional ground state
DBIW89
;
MMK+95
, which has difficulties in controlling multiple ions simultaneously
BCMS19
. Consequently, the initialization process may be a significant bottleneck for scaling up trapped-ion quantum computers.

To address the challenges posed by initialization inefficiencies,
initialization-free quantum algorithms
have been proposed, designed to mitigate the reliance on precise state preparation
CKL01
;
CKL05
. These algorithms leverage arbitrary unknown mixed states as auxiliary registers, returning them to their initial state at the end of computation. This flexibility eliminates the need for time-consuming initialization processes while maintaining computational efficiency comparable to conventional quantum algorithms. These merits enhance feasibility for practical implementation on current quantum hardware including systems with inherent initialization constraints, such as trapped-ion platforms. However, existing initialization-free algorithms are limited to solving specific problems, such as Deutsch-Jozsa, Simon, and period-finding
CKL01
;
CKL05
.

Building on these foundational results, we present a generalized initialization-free quantum algorithm tailored for solving a broader class of the AHSP. By extending initialization-free algorithms to all instances of AHSP, we make them applicable to a wider range of problems in quantum computation. Moreover, since every quantum algorithm providing exponential speed-up can be reduced to an instance of AHSP, our result has far-reaching implications. Specifically, it suggests that our initialization-free framework could be applied to any quantum algorithm offering exponential speed-up, thus resolving initialization bottlenecks across a broad spectrum of quantum computational tasks. As a result, our work contributes a significant step forward in the pursuit of scalable and robust quantum algorithms.

This paper is organized as follows. First, we introduce some definitions and propositions as preliminaries in Section
II
. In Section
III
, we review the standard quantum algorithm for AHSP. In Section
IV
, we present our initialization-free algorithm for AHSP. In Section
V
, we conclude by summarizing our results and some future works.

II.2
The coset state and the Quantum Fourier Transform over finite abelian groups

The Quantum Fourier Transform (QFT) is a core primitive in quantum algorithms for HSP. In this subsection, we review the QFT over finite abelian groups and the notion of the coset state when a subgroup
H
of a finite abelian group
G
is given.

For a cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
, the QFT
\mathcal{F}_{N}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
is defined as

\mathcal{F}_{N}\ket{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}\omega_{N}^{jl}\ket{l},

(9)

where
\omega_{N}=e^{2\pi i/N}
is the
N
th root of unity and
\ket{j}
and
\ket{l}
are computational basis states.
When the QFT is applied on a finite abelian group
G
with the decomposition in Eq. (
2
), the QFT
\mathcal{F}_{G}
over
G
can be represented as the tensor product of the QFTs
\mathcal{F}_{N_{j}}
over each cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{N_{j}}
for
j=1,\ldots,k
. Namely,

\mathcal{F}_{G}=\mathcal{F}_{N_{1}}\otimes\dotsm\otimes\mathcal{F}_{N_{k}}.

(10)

Let us consider a subgroup
H
of
G
with the decomposition in Eq. (
4
). For an element
r=(r_{1},\dotsc,r_{k})\in G
, we define the coset state
\ket{r+H}
as

\ket{r+H}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|H|}}\sum_{q_{1}=0}^{N_{1}/h_{1}-1}\dotsm\sum_{q_{k}=0}^{N_{k}/h_{k}-1}\ket{r_{1}+q_{1}h_{1}}\dotsm\ket{r_{k}+q_{k}h_{k}},

(11)

which is the uniform superposition of all the elements in the coset
r+H
.
If we apply the QFT
\mathcal{F}_{G}
defined in Eq. (
10
) to the coset state
\ket{r+H}
, we have

\mathcal{F}_{G}\ket{r+H}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|G||H|}}\sum_{l_{1}=0}^{N_{1}-1}\dotsm\sum_{l_{k}=0}^{N_{k}-1}\Bigg(\sum_{q_{1}=0}^{N_{1}/h_{1}-1}\omega_{N_{1}}^{(r_{1}+q_{1}h_{1})l_{1}}\ket{l_{1}}\Bigg)\dotsm\Bigg(\sum_{q_{k}=0}^{N_{k}/h_{k}-1}\omega_{N_{k}}^{(r_{k}+q_{k}h_{k})l_{k}}\ket{l_{k}}\Bigg).

(12)

Utilizing the geometric series formula, the summation over
q_{j}
in Eq. (
12
) can be simplified as

\sum_{q_{j}=0}^{N_{j}/h_{j}-1}\omega_{N_{j}}^{q_{j}h_{j}l_{j}}=\begin{cases}\frac{N_{j}}{h_{j}},&\text{if}\quad\frac{N_{j}}{h_{j}}\mid l_{j}\;\text{or}\;l_{j}=0\\
0,&\text{else if}\quad\frac{N_{j}}{h_{j}}\nmid l_{j},\end{cases}

(13)

which tells us that the summation over
q_{j}
is nonzero only when
l_{j}=t_{j}N_{j}/h_{j}
for some integer
t_{j}
in
\{0,1,\ldots,h_{j}-1\}
for all
j=1,\dotsc,k
.
Thus, we can rewrite Eq. (
12
) as

\mathcal{F}_{G}\ket{r+H}=\sqrt{\frac{|H|}{|G|}}\sum_{t_{1}=0}^{h_{1}-1}\dotsm\sum_{t_{k}=0}^{h_{k}-1}\omega_{N_{1}}^{r_{1}t_{1}N_{1}/h_{1}}\dotsm\omega_{N_{k}}^{r_{k}t_{k}N_{k}/h_{k}}\Ket{\frac{t_{1}N_{1}}{h_{1}}}\dotsm\Ket{\frac{t_{k}N_{k}}{h_{k}}},

(14)

where
|H|=\tfrac{N_{1}\dotsm N_{k}}{h_{1}\dotsm h_{k}}
due to the decompositions of
G
and
H
in Eqs. (
2
) and (
4
), respectively.

Due to Lemma
1
, we note that
(\frac{t_{1}N_{1}}{h_{1}},\dotsc,\frac{t_{k}N_{k}}{h_{k}})
is an element in
H^{\perp}
, therefore Definition
2
enables us to rewrite Eq. (
14
) as

\mathcal{F}_{G}\ket{r+H}=\sqrt{\frac{|H|}{|G|}}\sum_{t\in H^{\perp}}\omega_{M}^{r\cdot t}\ket{t}

(15)

where
t=(\frac{t_{1}N_{1}}{h_{1}},\dotsc,\frac{t_{k}N_{k}}{h_{k}})
.
As a result, we see that applying the QFT to the coset state
\ket{r+H}
yields a superposition over all elements in
H^{\perp}
, weighted by the inner product of
r
and each element
t
in
H^{\perp}
.

III
The standard quantum algorithm for AHSP

Before we provide an initialization-free quantum algorithm for AHSP in the next section, here we first review the standard polynomial-time quantum algorithm for AHSP proposed by Kitaev
Kit95
. For a finite abelian group
G
and its subgroup
H

hidden by the function
f
satisfying the condition (
1
), the algorithm finds a generating set of
H
in polynomial time with respect to
\log{|G|}
.

To process the function evaluation, the algorithm employs a quantum oracle
\mathcal{U}_{f}
defined as

\mathcal{U}_{f}\ket{x}_{A}\ket{y}_{B}=\ket{x}_{A}\ket{y+f(x)}_{B},

(16)

where the subscript
A
denotes the main register to deal with the group elements
x
in
G
, and
B
is the auxiliary register for storing the function values of

. Due to the decomposition of
G
and its elements

in Eqs. (
2
) and (
3
), respectively, the subscript
A
can be further decomposed into
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{k}
and

\ket{x}_{A}=\ket{x_{1}}_{{A}_{1}}\dotsm\ket{x_{k}}_{A_{k}}.

(17)

The dimension of each register
A_{j}
is
N_{j}
for all
j=1,\ldots,k
, and the dimension of the register
B
is
|Y|
.

Here we note that the binary operation
+
in the auxiliary register can be justified in the context of defining the quantum oracle
\mathcal{U}_{f}
, although the set
Y
does not inherently possess any algebraic structure. In fact, the operation
+
is treated as a formal operation that combines the two elements

and

into another element of

, so no ambiguity arises in the definition of
\mathcal{U}_{f}
. Furthermore, to support the initialization-free quantum algorithm for AHSP in Section

, we assume that

is surjective on

, which allows us to regard
Y
as a finite abelian group isomorphic to

G/H\cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{h_{j}}.

(18)

This assumption is not a significant restriction, as we can always construct a surjective function

from
G
to

by appropriately restricting the codomain of

.

The algorithm starts from preparing the initial state
\ket{\psi_{0}}_{AB}

\ket{\psi_{0}}_{AB}=\ket{0}_{{A}_{1}}\dotsm\ket{0}_{A_{k}}\ket{0}_{B},

By applying the quantum oracle
\mathcal{U}_{f}
in Eq. (
16

\ket{\psi_{2}}_{AB}=\mathcal{U}_{f}\ket{\psi_{1}}_{AB}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|G|}}\sum_{x_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{N_{1}}}\dotsm\sum_{x_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{N_{k}}}\ket{x_{1}}_{A_{1}}\dotsm\ket{x_{k}}_{A_{k}}\ket{f(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k})}_{B}.

Leveraging the coset state in Eq. (
11

1
),
\ket{\psi_{2}}_{AB}
can be rewritten as

\ket{\psi_{2}}_{AB}=\sqrt{\frac{|H|}{|G|}}\sum_{r\in R}\ket{r+H}_{A}\ket{f(r)}_{B},

(21)

We apply the QFT

By measuring the register

\displaystyle\Pr(\tau)=

\displaystyle\frac{|H|^{2}}{|G|^{2}}\sum_{r,r^{\prime}\in R}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}\in H^{\perp}}\omega_{M}^{r\cdot t}\omega_{M}^{-r^{\prime}\cdot t^{\prime}}\braket{t^{\prime}|\tau}_{A}\negthinspace\braket{\tau|t}\braket{f(r)|f(r^{\prime})}_{B}

IV
Initialization-free Quantum Algorithm for Abelian Hidden Subgroup Problem

In this section, we present an initialization-free quantum algorithm for AHSP that uses an arbitrary unknown mixed state as the auxiliary register
B
and restores its initial state at the end of the computation. Our algorithm preserves time and query complexity comparable to the standard quantum algorithm for AHSP in the previous section.

Due to the decomposition in Eq. (
18
), we consider on an arbitrary pure state of the auxiliary register
B
as

\ket{\Phi}_{B}=\sum_{b_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{h_{1}}}\dotsm\sum_{b_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{h_{k}}}\beta_{b_{1},\dotsc,b_{k}}\ket{b_{1}}_{B_{1}}\dotsm\ket{b_{k}}_{B_{k}}.

(25)

The algorithm starts from an initial state
\ket{\phi_{0}}_{AB}=\ket{0}_{A_{1}}\dotsm\ket{0}_{A_{k}}\ket{\Phi}_{B}
.

The first two operations applied in our algorithm are same as in Section
III
. Namely, we apply QFTs on the register

where

,

V
Conclusion

Here, we have presented an initialization-free quantum algorithm for solving the Abelian Hidden Subgroup Problem (AHSP). By using the unitary operation
S_{w}
acting on the auxiliary register, our algorithm maintains the same time and query complexity,
\mathcal{O}(\log{|G|})
oracle queries and
\mathcal{O}(\log^{3}|G|)
elementary operations, as the standard quantum algorithm, while offering significant practical advantages: Unlike conventional approaches, our algorithm does not require the auxiliary register to be initialized and it can adopt an arbitrary unknown mixed state. These features make our algorithm more practical for implementation on current quantum platforms, such as trapped ions, where initialization overhead can be a bottleneck

;
MMK+95
;
BCMS19
.

Since many quantum algorithms providing exponential speed-up are reduced to AHSP, our method potentially applies to a broad class of computational problems. Future research may explore extending this framework to non-Abelian HSPs and other classes of quantum algorithms to further mitigate initialization bottlenecks.

Comparison of Effective Phacoemulsification Times Used in Cataract Surgery between the Veritas Vision System and the Centurion Vision System
Authors: Ji Youn Choi, Won Seok Song, Yeo Kyoung Won, Dong Hui Lim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo compare the effective phacoemulsification time (EPT) used in cataract surgery and postoperative outcomes between the Veritas Vision System and the Centurion Vision System.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on 90 eyes from 82 patients with senile cataracts who underwent cataract extraction by phacoemulsification at Samsung Medical Center from December 2022 to May 2023. According to the Lens Opacities Classification System III grading of nucleus opalescence (NO), eyes with grades 3 to 5 were included, subdivided into moderate (≥NO3, <NO4) and hard (≥NO4, ≤NO5) cataracts. The Veritas Vision System was utilized for 43 eyes, comprising 26 moderate and 17 hard cataracts, while the Centurion Vision System was utilized for 47 eyes, including 26 moderate and 21 hard cataracts. Intraoperative parameters were ultrasound time (sec), average phacoemulsification power (%), and EPT (%·s) for the Veritas group; and cumulative dissipated energy (CDE, %·s) for the Centurion group. Best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) and endothelial cell counts (ECC) were measured one month postoperatively. All parameters were compared between the subgroups.ResultsIn both the moderate and hard cataract groups, the EPT in the Veritas group was lower than the CDE in the Centurion group (p = 0.035 and p = 0.009, respectively). However, no significant differences in postoperative BCVA or ECC changes were observed.ConclusionsThe Veritas Vision System demonstrated lower EPTs in the moderate and hard cataract groups. Both systems showed effective and safe surgical outcomes.Keywords: Centurion Vision System, Phacoemulsification, Veritas Vision System

Cataract is a leading cause of blindness worldwide, and its prevalence and incidence are expected to increase as the population ages [1]. Cataract surgery is currently one of the most commonly performed ocular surgeries, with phacoemulsification becoming the gold standard for lens removal in developed countries [2,3]. Consequently, many phacoemulsification devices have been developed and improved to minimize ultrasound energy output, as it may cause corneal endothelial damage during surgery [4]. The Centurion Vision System (Alcon Laboratories), introduced in 2014, demonstrated significantly lower cumulative dissipated energy (CDE) while achieving similar visual outcomes compared to the Infiniti Vision System (Alcon Laboratories) from the same manufacturer, released in 2003 [2]. CDE refers to the total amount of ultrasound energy utilized during phacoemulsification, with lower CDE potentially leading to reduced corneal damage [2].

Over time, advancements in phacoemulsification technology have led to the development of devices designed to perform surgery more efficiently with lower energy output. The Veritas Vision System (Johnson & Johnson Surgical Vision), introduced in 2021, features elliptical tip movement that blends longitudinal and transverse energy. According to Quesada et al. [5], it has demonstrated safe and effective clinical outcomes regardless of cataract grade. However, research on the Veritas Vision System remains limited due to its relatively recent release. Given the differences in phacoemulsification technologies and tip profiles for generating and delivering ultrasound energy, this study aimed to compare the effective phacoemulsification time (EPT) and postoperative outcomes between the Veritas Vision System and the Centurion Vision System.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This retrospective study was conducted on patients with senile cataracts who underwent cataract extraction by phacoemulsification and intraocular lens (IOL) implantation at Samsung Medical Center from December 2022 to May 2023. The study received approval from the Institutional Review Board of the Samsung Medical Center (No. 2024-08-071), adhering to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was waived due to the use of deidentified data and the retrospective nature of the study.

A total of 90 eyes from 82 patients were included, who were diagnosed with grades between three and five cataracts. The cataract grading was performed by a single specialist (DHL) based on anterior segment photographs taken under mydriatic conditions, following the Lens Opacities Classification System III grading of nucleus opalescence (NO). Afterward, they were subdivided into moderate (≥NO3, <NO4) and hard (≥NO4, ≤NO5) cataracts. Eyes with previous ophthalmic disorders such as glaucoma or retinal diseases, a history of ocular trauma or intraocular surgery except for simple refractive surgery and phakic IOL implantation in anterior chamber for myopia correction, significant corneal abnormalities such as opacity or dystrophy, brunescent or white cataracts, or cases with eventful cataract surgery such as posterior capsule rupture were excluded.

Preoperatively, best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) and potential acuity meter (PAM) were assessed, and intraocular pressure (IOP) was evaluated using non-contact tonometry (Topcon CT-1P, Topcon Corp.). Endothelial cell count (ECC) and central corneal thickness (CCT) were measured using a non-contact specular microscope (Topcon SP-3000P, Topcon Corp.), measured three times at the central cornea, and the mean value was used for analysis. The measurements were performed by the same experienced examiner under consistent conditions. The presence of anterior subcapsular cataract (ASC), posterior subcapsular cataract (PSC), and cortical opacity (CO) was also evaluated. Postoperatively, BCVA, IOP, ECC, and CCT were measured at one month after surgery. Endothelial cell loss (ECL) was calculated with the following two formulas, which may indicate the corneal endothelium damage following cataract surgery.

All surgeries were performed by a single surgeon (DHL), who had over 10 years of experience in phacoemulsification, using either the Veritas Vision System or the Centurion Vision System. Under topical anesthesia with proparacaine hydrochloride 0.5% (Alcaine, Alcon), a 2.75 mm clear corneal incision was made on the temporal side. Continuous curvilinear capsulorhexis and hydrodissection were performed, followed by phacoemulsification using a routine phaco-chop technique. Veritas Ellips FX handpiece (model 690880, coaxial type), which shares the same design as that used in the WhiteStar Signature Pro System (Abbott Medical Optics), was used in the Veritas Vision System, while the Active Sentry handpiece (Alcon) was used in the Centurion Vision System (Supplementary Table 1). After IOL implantation into the capsular bag and stromal hydration with balanced-salt solution, no sutures were performed on the corneal incision. During surgery, all ophthalmic viscosurgical devices used were 1.0% sodium hyaluronate (Hyalu, Hanmi Pharm Co.). Postoperatively, levofloxacin 1.5% (Cravit, Santen Pharmaceutical) and loteprednol etabonate 0.5% (Lotepro, Hanlim Pharm Co.) were administered for one month.

Intraoperative parameters in the Veritas group included ultrasound time (UST, sec), average phacoemulsification power (AVG, %), EPT (%·s, calculated as UST × AVG), and the parameter of effective phaco time with a specific coefficient for the transversal movement. UST is defined as the entire duration during which the foot pedal is in the third position, and EPT indicates the duration for which the ultrasound would have been used if the power output had been set at 100%. Transversal movement is a unitless constant designed by the manufacturer to compare the effective ultrasound usage, reflecting only the longitudinal movement of the Ellips FX handpiece during the surgery [4]. In the Centurion group, UST, AVG, and CDE (%·s) were collected during the surgery. CDE is automatically shown on the interface of the Centurion device and represents the total energy released at the wound site, incorporating both longitudinal and torsional ultrasound energies. CDE is calculated as the sum of its longitudinal component (AVG × UST) and torsional component (AVG × UST × 0.4). In torsional phacoemulsification, the coefficient of 0.4 is applied because of the 80% reduction in the frequency of the phacoemulsification tip and the halving of the stroke length [2]. All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS ver. 21.0 (IBM Corp.). The normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Normally distributed variables were compared using the independent t-test, while non-normally distributed variables were analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U-test. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test, and statistical significance was set at a p-value of <0.05.

Discussion

In our study, the Veritas system demonstrated significantly lower EPT (measured as EPT in the Veritas group and as CDE in the Centurion group) in both moderate and hard cataract groups. There were no significant differences in postoperative visual acuity or ECC changes between the two systems in both the moderate and hard cataract groups. Postoperative CCT also showed no significant differences between the two machines in both groups, with IOP remaining within the normal range.

The lower EPT observed with the Veritas Vision System in the hard cataract group suggests it may exert less stress on the cornea in such cases. However, since the energy used during phacoemulsification is often associated with ECL or visual impairment due to corneal edema, the lack of significant differences in postoperative outcomes between the two systems indicates that both provide comparably safe and effective surgical outcomes [6].

With intrepid balanced tip and OZil torsional technology, the Centurion Vision System is known to enable efficient phacoemulsification with reduced ultrasound energy [7]. Additionally, the new active fluidics mechanism of the Centurion Vision System allows surgeons to operate using high vacuum, reducing the EPT [7]. The Veritas Vision System follows a workflow similar to the WhiteStar Signature Pro System and utilizes ultrasound technology that combines longitudinal and transverse energy. It also features improved fluidics, including hybrid fluidics technology for enhanced chamber stability, dual-durometer tubing for enhanced flexibility and surge protection, as well as advanced infusion for increased irrigation pressure [5]. Several studies have compared the surgical outcomes of phacoemulsification systems from different manufacturers. A prospective study by Atas et al. [6] demonstrated that the WhiteStar Signature system with Ellips FX handpiece, which combines longitudinal and transverse energy, resulted in comparable ECL to the Infiniti Vision System with OZil handpiece, which employs torsional energy, in groups with similar nuclear sclerosis grades. Similarly, Assaf and Roshdy [8] reported that one month postoperatively, there were no significant differences in ECL when comparing the WhiteStar system with Ellips FX to the Infiniti system with OZil. Additionally, Noh and Jung [9] demonstrated that the Stellaris Vision System (Bausch & Lomb), using longitudinal power, showed higher efficiency for hard cataracts and induced less ECL than the Infiniti Vision System with OZil handpiece. However, comparisons between newer-generation phacoemulsification systems from different manufacturers remain limited.

Although EPT and CDE are proprietary metrics of two different manufacturers, both are derived from the same underlying variables (AVG and UST) and expressed in the same unit (%·s). The coefficient 0.4 in CDE is a physical correction applied only to the torsional component, whereas the factor 0.01 in EPT is simply an arithmetic step for converting the percentage (0%–100%) to a dimensionless decimal, not a comparable physical correction. Because the Centurion system predominantly operates in torsional mode, the 0.4 correction tends to reduce the reported CDE below the raw AVG × UST value. Therefore, our finding that Veritas EPT was lower than Centurion CDE likely represents a conservative reflection of the underlying energy difference between the two platforms, rather than an overestimation. Nevertheless, since EPT and CDE remain device-specific proprietary metrics, this comparison should be interpreted as an indirect estimation rather than a direct quantitative measure of ultrasound energy efficiency between the two systems.

A plausible explanation for the shorter effective phaco time observed with the Veritas system in hard cataracts may lie in its combination of the Ellips FX handpiece and the WhiteStar technology. The Ellips FX handpiece enhances cutting efficiency while reducing repulsion of nuclear material, and the WhiteStar ultra-pulse technology enables rapid modulation of ultrasound energy with short on-off cycles, maintaining cutting efficiency and follow-ability while reducing unnecessary energy delivery [10]. By turning off rapidly, it may also make more efficient use of cavitational energy than continuous ultrasound, thereby allowing effective nuclear removal with less cumulative energy. These design features may account for the shorter EPT observed with the Veritas Vision System without significantly compromising postoperative visual acuity or ECC outcomes.

This study has several limitations. First, it was retrospective in nature. Second, we did not measure the amount of balanced-salt solution used for irrigation, the duration of irrigation/aspiration, and total surgery time, all of which may influence surgical outcomes. In addition, endothelial morphological parameters such as hexagonality and coefficient of variation were not included in this study, which may limit a comprehensive assessment of endothelial cell status. Third, we compared the two systems based on different platforms from different manufacturers, which complicates direct comparisons. As mentioned above, although the two devices were manufactured by different companies, we considered it meaningful to compare the parameters, as the surgeries were performed using settings and handpieces optimized for each device. Fourth, a wide range of IOLs were implanted, including monofocal, enhanced monofocal, extended depth of focus, and multifocal IOLs from various manufacturers, which may have affected visual outcomes. Fifth, cataract severity was classified based solely on NO according to the Lens Opacities Classification System III grading, without considering other components such as CO and posterior subcapsular opacity. Although NO is most closely associated with lens hardness and phacoemulsification energy, these additional opacity types may influence postoperative visual outcomes. Lastly, in some cases, ECC loss was recorded as less than 0%, suggesting possible measurement errors during ECC assessment, although each measurement was repeatedly performed by the same experienced examiner under consistent conditions. When using the same device in our study, the hard cataract group required higher ultrasound energy but did not consistently show higher ECL compared to the moderate cataract group. This finding contrasts with previous studies using the same machine, which reported significantly greater ECL in the hard cataract group compared to the moderate group, which may imply that measurement errors in our ECC assessment could have influenced the results [6,11]. However, adequate corneal protection with ophthalmic viscosurgical devices and the improved stability of modern phaco systems may have helped minimize endothelial damage, suggesting that longer phaco time does not necessarily lead to greater ECC loss when surgery is performed carefully by experienced surgeons.

In conclusion, both the Veritas and the Centurion Vision System demonstrated good performance with stable surgical outcomes, and the differences in corneal ECL and postoperative visual acuity were not significant. These findings suggest that both systems are effective and safe for cataract surgery.

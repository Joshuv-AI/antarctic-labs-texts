On the distance distribution of Reed-Muller codes
Neil Kolekar

Abstract
. In this paper, we give error bounds for the distance distribution of Reed-Muller codes, extending prior work on the distance distribution of Reed-Solomon codes. This is equivalent to the problem of counting multivariate polynomials over a finite field with prescribed degree, coefficients, and number of zeroes. We provide a solution to this problem using the character sum method, which offers a new unified framework applicable to a broad class of polynomial enumeration problems over finite fields that involve prescribed evaluation vectors. This work effectively makes the first systematic attempt to study the coset weight distribution problem for Reed-Muller codes of fixed degree over large finite fields, which was proposed in MacWilliams and Sloane’s 1977 textbook The Theory of Error Correcting Codes .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivating problem

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field of order
q
and characteristic
p
. Fix a subset
D=\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
. The
Reed-Muller code

\textrm{RM}_{q}(d,v)
is the set of all codewords of the form

(f(\alpha_{1}),\dots,f(\alpha_{n})),

where
f
ranges over polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{v}]
of degree at most
d-1
.

For two vectors
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})
and
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
, the
Hamming distance

\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w})
is the number of indices
1\leq i\leq k
such that
u_{i}\neq w_{i}
. Accordingly, if
f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{v}]
are polynomials of degree at most
d-1
, then the Hamming distance between their associated codewords equals
n-r
, where
r
is the number of common evaluation points
\alpha_{i}\in D
at which
f(\alpha_{i})=g(\alpha_{i})
, that is, the number of zeroes of
f-g
on
D
. The
standard Reed-Muller codes
are those with
D=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
. The
distance distribution problem
for Reed-Muller codes asks the following.

Question 1.1
.

Given a received word
\mathbf{u}
and positive integer
k
, compute the number of codewords
\mathbf{w}\in\mathrm{RM}_{q}(d,v)
for which
\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w})=k
.

Question
1.1
, in the context of general linear codes, is an important problem in coding theory which is widely open for most codes. The distance distribution problem essentially consists of two subproblems. In the case where
\mathbf{u}\in\mathrm{RM}_{q}(d,v)
, the problem becomes the
weight distribution problem
; when
\mathbf{u}\not\in\mathrm{RM}_{q}(d,v)
, the problem becomes the
coset weight distribution problem
. Our interest mainly lies in the latter, which has seen little progress for Reed-Muller codes since it was introduced in the 1970s. The coset weight distribution problem dates back to 1977 when it was proposed in MacWilliams and Sloane’s classic textbook
The Theory of Error-Correcting Codes

[
14
, , Research Problem 1.1]
; the weight distribution problem was proposed in the same text
[
14
, , Research Problem 15.1]
. Even in the specialization of Reed-Solomon codes, the coset weight distribution problem has only recently been studied
[
13
]
. In this paper, we study the distance distribution problem for Reed-Muller codes, taking forward the character sum approach of Li and Wan
[
13
]
to the multivariate setting.

Naturally, one would consider the polynomial interpretation of this problem. The equivalent translation is presented below, upon taking the polynomial reversal of the polynomials associated with the (code)words involved.

Question 1.2
.

Fix a positive integer
\ell\leq v(q-1)
. Given a fixed polynomial
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{v}]
of degree
\ell-1
, compute the number of polynomials
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{v}]
such that the maximum degree of
x_{i}
in
f
is at most
q-1
for all
1\leq i\leq v
,
f-g
has exactly
s
zeroes in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
, and

\overline{f-g}\in\langle x_{1}^{i_{1}}\dots x_{v}^{i_{v}}\mid i_{1}+\dots+i_{v}=\ell\rangle,

where
\overline{f-g}
is defined as the polynomial
x_{1}^{q-1}\cdots x_{v}^{q-1}(f-g)(x_{1}^{-1},\dots,x_{v}^{-1})
.

The primary goal of this paper is to give error bounds on the count above, which produces error bounds for
standard
Reed-Muller codes. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
N_{k}(d,g)
denote the number of polynomials
f(x_{1},\dots,x_{v})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{v}]
such that the degree of every variable
x_{i}
in
f
is at most
q-1
,
f-g
has no nonzero terms of degree
\ell-1
or lower, and
\overline{f-g}
has exactly
q^{v}-k-1
zeroes. Furthermore, let

E=\#\{(i_{1},\dots,i_{v})\in(\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,q-1])^{v}\mid i_{1}+\dots i_{v}\leq\ell-1\}.

Then,
N_{k}(d,g)
satisfies the error bound

\left|N_{k}(d,g)-\binom{(q-1)q^{v-1}}{k}\,q^{k-E-q^{v-1}+1}\right|\leq\binom{vq_{2}q^{\binom{\ell+v-3}{v-1}}+k-1}{k-1}q^{k\left(\binom{\lceil\ell/2\rceil+v}{v}+1\right)-E},

where
q_{1}=\max(\ell-1,(q-1)/\sqrt{q(p-1)})
and

q_{2}=q_{1}+\left(\frac{q-1}{\sqrt{p-1}}-q_{1}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor p/2\rfloor}\binom{q}{k}\cdot\left(\frac{\ell}{p}\right)^{k}\right)^{-1}.

1.2.
Past work

In coding theory literature, the distance distribution problem for linear codes dates back to 1977
[
14
]
. There is, however, a related problem called the
list-decoding problem
which was introduced in 1957 by Elias
[
6
]
. Instead of asking for the number of codewords which are precisely some distance away from a particular code, the list-decoding problem asks for an algorithm to determine the list of codewords
within
that distance. Beginning from the 1960s, there has been much progress made on the algorithmic aspect of the list-decoding problem. On the other hand, between 2007 and 2015 there has been much work on the combinatorial aspect of this problem
[
2
,
8
,
10
]
, which eventually resolved the weight distribution problem for Reed-Muller codes
[
3
]
. The techniques used for the work in this time period were largely combinatorial, in that they were based on the higher-order Fourier analysis of multivariate polynomials. The natural next step in this line of research is to consider the distance distribution problem. Currently, the progress made towards the goal of resolving this problem for the generality of Reed-Muller codes only applies to the specialization of Reed-Solomon codes.

In 2020, Li and Wan
[
13
]
gave error bounds on the number of Reed-Solomon codewords that are a fixed distance away from a given codeword, using the character sum method, improving error bounds of Zhu and Wan
[
19
]
. These error bounds were later improved upon by Gao and Li
[
7
]
in 2022, who strengthened a weak bound in
[
13
]
using the saddle point method. The improved error bounds were used towards constructing new classes of deep holes of Reed-Solomon codes. Before the character sum method of Zhu and Wan for estimating the distance distribution was introduced, the techniques that were employed for Reed-Solomon codes were highly algebro-geometric. These techniques led to the results in
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
, and
[
15
]
.

Like past work on the distance distribution of Reed-Solomon codes, this paper too utilizes character sums. Specifically, we understand the vanishing sets of polynomials using Lagrange interpolation, and analyze character sums over truncated multivariate polynomial quotient rings involving both additive and multiplicative characters. The polynomials associated with Lagrange interpolation are multiplicatively well-structured, but are not well-structured in the additive sense. This motivates combining additive characters and multiplicative characters to estimate the desired number of codes. In particular, we evaluate Gauss sums over truncated polynomial quotient rings, which have deep connections to the algebraic structure of these rings. Part of the technical work we perform pertaining to algebraic properties of these quotient rings has not been studied prior to this paper, and is thus potentially useful beyond the scope of the distance distribution problem.

Outline

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we set up the notation and terminology used in this paper. In Section
3
, we use Lagrange interpolation to establish a character sum formula for the count that we aim to give error bounds on. Section
4
provides an exact formula for the center of approximation we use. In Section
5
, we find an explicit formula for the sizes of the Gauss sums over a particular polynomial quotient ring. In Section
6
, we perform two key enumerations required for our bounds: the first is of ideals of the polynomial quotient ring with certain properties, and the second is of multiplicative characters with prescribed values. To that end, our main result is proved in Section
7
, using the Li-Wan coordinate-sieve formula aided by our results leading up to that point. Finally, we close the paper with Section
8
in which we discuss potential future research directions.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we establish the notation and terminology used throughout the paper.

We begin with notation for polynomials over finite fields. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field of order
q
and characteristic
p
, and let
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}=\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
. Write
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{v}]
for the polynomial ring in variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{v}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{v}]
, we use

[x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{v}^{a_{v}}]f

to denote the coefficient of
x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{v}^{a_{v}}
in
f
. We write
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{v})
, so that
f(\mathbf{x})
abbreviates
f(x_{1},\dots,x_{v})
. Given a subset
T\subseteq[v]
, denote
x_{T}
by the monomial
\prod_{i\in T}x_{i}
.

The degree of a polynomial
f
is denoted by
\deg(f)
, with the convention
\deg(0)=-\infty
. Let
\mathcal{M}
denote the set of polynomials
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{v}]
in which the degree of each variable
x_{i}
is at most
q-1
. Finally, for
f\in\mathcal{M}
, define
\overline{f}=x_{1}^{q-1}\cdots x_{v}^{q-1}f(x_{1}^{-1},\dots,x_{v}^{-1})
.

Set

\mathcal{E}:=\frac{\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{v}]}{\langle x_{1}^{i_{1}}\dots x_{v}^{i_{v}}\mid i_{1}+\dots+i_{v}=\ell\rangle}.

Note that the equivalence classes defined by the quotient ring
\mathcal{E}
represents the prescription of coefficients behind terms with degree at most
\ell-1
.

For any ring
\mathcal{R}
, we will use
\mathcal{R}_{\times}
to denote the group of units in
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{R}_{+}
to denote the additive group on
\mathcal{R}
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
denote the set
\{x_{1}^{i_{1}}\dots x_{v}^{i_{v}}\mid i_{1}+\dots+i_{v}\leq\ell-1\}
. Note that the sets
g\cdot\mathcal{E}_{\times}
form a partition of
\mathcal{E}
, where
g
runs over
\mathcal{S}
. For every
c\in\mathcal{E}
, let
S(c)
denote the unique monomial
g\in\mathcal{E}
with coefficient
1
for which
g\mid c
and
gx_{i}\nmid c
for all
1\leq i\leq v
, and let
\operatorname{Red}(c)
represent the polynomial
c/S(c)
considered over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{v}]
and subsequently reduced in
\mathcal{E}
.
Throughout this paper, we will also use the Iverson bracket
\llbracket P\rrbracket
, which has value
1
if the predicate
P
is true and has value
0
otherwise.

Next, we set up notation regarding the vanishing sets of polynomials. Given a subset
Z\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
, let
V(Z)
denote the set of polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{v}]
that vanish on
Z
and no other points in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
. Let
N_{Z}(\varepsilon_{0})
denote the set of polynomials in
V(Z)
with
x_{i}
-degree at most
q-1
for all
1\leq i\leq v
that reduce to
\varepsilon_{0}
in
\mathcal{E}
.

Finally, we provide notation and preliminary results on the character sums used in this work. A
character
on a group
G
is group homomorphism
\phi\colon G\to\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
. The set of characters on
G
is denoted
\widehat{G}
. A
multiplicative character
on a ring
R
is a character
\chi
on
R_{\times}
, and an
additive character
on
R
is a character
\psi
on
R_{+}
. The Pontryagin duality theorem implies that
G\cong\widehat{G}
for all finite abelian groups
G
; in particular,
|G|=|\widehat{G}|
. Given
c\in G
and
\psi\in\widehat{G}
, let
\psi_{c}
denote the additive character defined by the mapping
a\mapsto\psi(ac)
. The
orthogonality of characters
states that for any character
\phi
defined on a group
G
,
\sum_{g\in G}\phi(g)=|G|\cdot\llbracket\phi(G)=1\rrbracket
. Given a group
G
, a subgroup
H
of
G
with finite index in
G
, and a character
\phi
defined on
H
, the number of characters
\overline{\phi}
on
G
which are prescribed to
\phi
on
H
is
|G:H|=|G|/|H|
. For a multiplicative character
\chi
and an additive character
\psi
defined on a ring
\mathcal{R}
, define their
Gauss sum
by

G(\chi,\psi):=\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}_{\times}}\chi(r)\psi(r).

With respect to this terminology, our main problem is equivalent to the following:

Give an estimate on the number of polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{v}]
with all variables
x_{i}
having degree at most
q-1
, exactly
s=q^{v}-k-1
zeroes, and reduction
\varepsilon_{0}
in
\mathcal{E}
. Equivalently, estimate the sum
\sum_{|Z|=s}N_{Z}(\varepsilon_{0})
.

Heuristically, we should expect this quantity to be roughly

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}|}\sum_{\varepsilon\in\mathcal{E}}\sum_{|Z|=s}N_{Z}(\varepsilon).

The key to giving sharp estimates on
\sum_{|Z|=s}N_{Z}(\varepsilon_{0})
is to give an upper bound on the
error term

\left|\sum_{|Z|=s}N_{Z}(\varepsilon_{0})-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}|}\sum_{\varepsilon\in\mathcal{E}}\sum_{|Z|=s}N_{Z}(\varepsilon)\right|.

In our main result, we derive an upper bound on the above expression.

3.
Character Sum Setup

In this section, we give a character sum formula for
N_{Z}(\varepsilon_{0})
. We first provide an alternative description for
N_{Z}(\varepsilon_{0})
in Corollary
3.4
, based on Lagrange interpolation. Let us begin by proving the following technical fact, which is a standard consequence of the Combinatorial Nullstellensatz
[
1
, , Theorem 1.2]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f
be a polynomial in
\mathcal{M}
which vanishes on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
. Then,
f
is the zero polynomial.

Proof.

Assume for contradiction that
f
is a nonzero polynomial. Then there exists a nonzero term
cx_{1}^{t_{1}}\dots x_{v}^{t_{v}}
in
f(\mathbf{x})
with
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
such that
t_{1}+\dots+t_{v}=\deg(f)
. Note that
t_{1},\dots,t_{v}\leq q-1
as
f\in\mathcal{M}
. Consider setting
S_{i}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
for all
1\leq i\leq v
. Since
t_{i}\leq q-1<|S_{i}|
for all
1\leq i\leq v
, the Combinatorial Nullstellensatz
[
1
, , Theorem 1.2]
implies that
f(\mathbf{v})\neq 0
for some
\mathbf{v}\in S_{1}\times\dots\times S_{v}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
, a contradiction. Hence,
f
is the zero polynomial.
∎

An important fact utilized in
[
13
]
is that the polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
with distinct prescribed zeroes
r_{1},\dots,r_{k}
can be characterized by the multiples of
(x-r_{1})\dots(x-r_{k})
. This, infamously, fails to persist in settings with two or more variables, in which polynomials with prescribed zeroes are often characterized by the elements of an ideal generated by two or more elements. The simplest description of such an ideal can be given using Lagrange interpolation; as such, Lagrange interpolation serves as the foundation of our character sum setup, and naturally extends to codes beyond Reed-Muller codes.

Definition 3.2
.

For each
(c_{1},\dots,c_{v})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
, define the polynomial

P_{(c_{1},\dots,c_{v})}(\mathbf{x}):=\prod_{i=1}^{v}(1-(x_{i}-c_{i})^{q-1}).

Lemma 3.3
.

For any polynomial
f\in\mathcal{M}
, we have
f\in V(Z)
if and only if
f
takes the form
\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}\setminus Z}a_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{v}}
for unique
a_{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
f
be a fixed polynomial in
\mathcal{M}
. Observe that for every
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
, we have
P_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{w})=\llbracket\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{w}\rrbracket
by Fermat’s little theorem. Therefore, if
g
is the polynomial defined by
\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}\setminus Z}f(\mathbf{v})P_{\mathbf{v}}
, then
f
and
g
have the same evaluation at every point in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
. Equivalently,
f-g
vanishes on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
. Observe that both
f
and
g
are in
\mathcal{M}
; thus,
f-g\in\mathcal{M}
. By Lemma
3.1
, it follows that
f=g
, so we conclude that
f=\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}\setminus Z}f(\mathbf{v})P_{\mathbf{v}}
for all
f\in\mathcal{M}
. Note that
f
is in
V(Z)
if and only if the set of all
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
for which
f(\mathbf{v})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
is exactly
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}\setminus Z
. Hence,
f\in V(Z)
if and only if
f
takes the form
\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}\setminus Z}a_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{v}}
for some
a_{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. The uniqueness of the
a_{\mathbf{v}}
follows from Fermat’s little theorem.
∎

As such, we may conclude the following about
N_{Z}(\varepsilon_{0})
.

Corollary 3.4
.

The number of tuples
(a_{\mathbf{v}})_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}\setminus Z}
of elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
for which
\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}\setminus Z}a_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{v}}
reduces to
\varepsilon_{0}
in
\mathcal{E}
.

We now give a character sum expression for
N_{Z}(\varepsilon_{0})
. This results in an error bound for
\sum_{|Z|=s}N_{Z}(\varepsilon_{0})
.

Proposition 3.5
.

For all
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
,
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}_{\times}}
, and
\psi\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}_{+}}
, set

\mathbf{Q}_{\alpha}(\chi,\psi):=\frac{q}{|\mathcal{E}|}\chi(\operatorname{Red}(P_{\alpha}))G(\chi^{-1},\psi_{S(P_{\alpha})}).

Then we have

\left|\sum_{|Z|=s}N_{Z}(\varepsilon_{0})-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}|}\sum_{\varepsilon\in\mathcal{E}}\sum_{|Z|=s}N_{Z}(\varepsilon)\right|\leq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}_{+}}\\
\psi\neq\mathbf{1}\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{|Z|=s}\prod_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}\setminus Z}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}_{\times}}\\
\chi(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})=1\end{subarray}}\mathbf{Q}_{\alpha}(\chi,\psi)\right)\right|.

Proof.

Observe that for any two polynomials
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{v}]
, we have
f_{1}=f_{2}
if and only if
\operatorname{Red}(f_{1})=\operatorname{Red}(f_{2})
and
S(f_{1})=S(f_{2})
. Thus, the Iverson bracket
\llbracket f_{1}=f_{2}\rrbracket
is given by

\llbracket f_{1}=f_{2}\rrbracket=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}_{\times}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}_{\times}}\end{subarray}}\chi(\operatorname{Red}(f_{1}))\chi(\operatorname{Red}(f_{2}))^{-1}\llbracket S(f_{1})=S(f_{2})\rrbracket

by the orthogonality of multiplicative characters. For all
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
,
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}_{\times}}
, and
\psi\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}_{+}}
, set

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\alpha}(\chi,\psi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}_{\times}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\mathcal{E}_{\times}\\
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\end{subarray}}\chi(a\operatorname{Red}(P_{\alpha}))\chi(u)^{-1}\psi(S(P_{\alpha})u)

\displaystyle=\llbracket\chi(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})=1\rrbracket\cdot\frac{q-1}{|\mathcal{E}_{\times}|}\sum_{u\in\mathcal{E}_{\times}}\chi(\operatorname{Red}(P_{\alpha}))\chi(u)^{-1}\psi(S(P_{\alpha})u)

\displaystyle=\llbracket\chi(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})=1\rrbracket\cdot\frac{q}{|\mathcal{E}|}\chi(\operatorname{Red}(P_{\alpha}))G(\chi^{-1},\psi_{S(P_{\alpha})}).

By the orthogonality of additive characters, it follows that

\displaystyle N_{Z}(\varepsilon_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}|}\sum_{\psi\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}_{+}}}\psi(\varepsilon_{0})^{-1}\prod_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}\setminus Z}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}_{\times}}\\
\chi(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})=1\end{subarray}}\mathbf{P}_{\alpha}(\chi,\psi)\right).

Observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{\varepsilon\in\mathcal{E}}N_{Z}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}_{+}}\\
\psi(\mathcal{E}_{+})=1\end{subarray}}\psi(\varepsilon_{0})^{-1}\prod_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}\setminus Z}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}_{\times}}\\
\chi(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})=1\end{subarray}}\mathbf{P}_{\alpha}(\chi,\psi)\right).

Thus, the desired error term
E_{Z}(d,\varepsilon_{0})
is given by

\displaystyle E_{Z}(d,\varepsilon_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}_{+}}\\
\psi\neq\mathbf{1}\end{subarray}}\psi(\varepsilon_{0})^{-1}\prod_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}\setminus Z}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}_{\times}}\\
\chi(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})=1\end{subarray}}\mathbf{P}_{\alpha}(\chi,\psi)\right).

By the triangle inequality, we have

\left|\sum_{|Z|=s}E_{Z}(d,\varepsilon_{0})\right|\leq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}_{+}}\\
\psi\neq\mathbf{1}\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{|Z|=s}\prod_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}\setminus Z}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}_{\times}}\\
\chi(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})=1\end{subarray}}\mathbf{P}_{\alpha}(\chi,\psi)\right)\right|,

which completes the proof.
∎

4.
Center of approximation

Recall that the center of approximation we use for
N_{Z}(\varepsilon_{0})
is given by

\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon_{0},Z}[N_{Z}(\varepsilon_{0})]=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}|}\sum_{\varepsilon_{0}\in\mathcal{E}}\sum_{|Z|=s}N_{Z}(\varepsilon_{0}),

which can be described as
1/(|\mathcal{E}|)
multiplied by the size of the set
M_{v}(s)
of polynomials
f
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{v}]
such that the degree of
x_{i}
in
f
is at most
q-1
for all
1\leq i\leq v
,
f
has a nonzero constant term, and
f
has exactly
s
zeroes. This type of enumeration, though without the nonzero constant term condition, has been studied previously; in particular, see
[
9
, , Theorem 4.5]
. We will adjust the proof of
[
9
, , Theorem 4.5]
to take into account of the additional constraint of
f
having a nonzero constant term. Let
\Phi_{v}(t)
denote the generating function in
t
for which the coefficient of
t^{k}
in
\Phi_{v}(t)
represents the probability that a random polynomial
f\in\bigsqcup_{s\geq 0}M_{v}(s)
satisfies
f\in M_{v}(k)
.

Claim 4.1
.

We have
\Phi_{v}(t)=(\Phi_{1}(t))^{q^{v-1}}
.

Proof.

It is not difficult to check that the part of the proof of
[
9
, , Theorem 4.5]
which proves this fact remains valid in this context as well.
∎

Thus, it suffices to compute
\Phi_{1}(t)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

The generating function
\Phi_{1}(t)
is given by

\Phi_{1}(t)=\left(1+\frac{t-1}{q}\right)^{q-1}.

Proof.

Let
r_{1},\dots,r_{k}
be distinct elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. Note that the number of polynomials
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
of degree at most
q-1
and nonzero constant term which vanish at
r_{1},\dots,r_{k}
is
(q-1)q^{q-k-1}
. Thus, by the inclusion-exclusion principle, the number of polynomials
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
of degree at most
q-1
, nonzero constant term, and exactly
k
roots is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{j=k}^{q-1}\binom{q-1}{j}\cdot(q-1)q^{q-j-1}(-1)^{j}

\displaystyle=(q-1)q^{q-1}\sum_{j=k}^{q-1}\binom{q-1}{j}(-q)^{-j}

\displaystyle=(q-1)q^{q-1}\cdot[t^{k}]\left(1+\frac{t-1}{q}\right)^{q-1}.

Hence we have

\Phi_{1}(t)=\left(1+\frac{t-1}{q}\right)^{q-1},

as desired.
∎

Combining Claim
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
, we have

\Phi_{v}(t)=\left(1+\frac{t-1}{q}\right)^{(q-1)q^{v-1}}.

Upon expansion, we compute

|M_{v}(s)|=\binom{(q-1)q^{v-1}}{s}\cdot q^{(q-1)q^{v-1}-s},

from which the closed form for our center of approximation is

(4.1)

\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon_{0},Z}[N_{Z}(\varepsilon_{0})]=\frac{|M_{v}(s)|}{|\mathcal{E}|}=\binom{(q-1)q^{v-1}}{s}\frac{q^{(q-1)q^{v-1}-s}}{|\mathcal{E}|}.

5.
Gauss Sum Computation

For Sections
5
and
6
, denote
\mathcal{R}
by the ring

\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{v}]/\langle x_{1}^{i_{1}}\dots x_{v}^{i_{v}}\mid i_{1}+\dots+i_{v}=n\rangle.

In this section, we compute the sizes of Gauss sums over
\mathcal{R}
.

Let
\chi
and
\psi
be a non-trivial multiplicative character and non-trivial additive character on
\mathcal{R}
, respectively. We have

G(\chi,\psi)\overline{G}(\chi,\psi)=\sum_{a,b\in\mathcal{R}_{\times}}\chi(a)\psi(a)\overline{\chi}(b)\overline{\psi}(b)=\sum_{a,b\in\mathcal{R}_{\times}}\chi(ab^{-1})\psi(a-b).

Thus, by performing casework on
ab^{-1}
, we obtain

G(\chi,\psi)\overline{G}(\chi,\psi)=\sum_{c\in\mathcal{R}_{\times}}\chi(c)\left(\sum_{d\in\mathcal{R}_{\times}}\psi(d(c-1))\right).

When
c(0,\dots,0)\neq 1
, we have

\sum_{d\in\mathcal{R}_{\times}}\psi(d(c-1))=0

as
\psi\neq\mathbf{1}
. Note that

\displaystyle(p-1)G(\chi,\psi)\overline{G}(\chi,\psi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{c\in\mathcal{R}_{\times}}\chi(c)\left(\sum_{d\in\mathcal{R}_{\times}}\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\psi(jd(c-1))\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{c\in\mathcal{R}_{\times}}\chi(c)\left(\sum_{d\in\mathcal{R}_{\times}}\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\psi(d(c-1))^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=p\sum_{c\in\mathcal{R}_{\times}}\chi(c)\left(\sum_{d\in\mathcal{R}_{\times}}\llbracket\psi(d(c-1))=1\rrbracket\right)-\sum_{c\in\mathcal{R}_{\times}}\chi(c)\left(\sum_{d\in\mathcal{R}_{\times}}1\right)

\displaystyle=p\sum_{c\in\mathcal{R}_{\times}}\chi(c)\left(\sum_{d\in\mathcal{R}_{\times}}\llbracket\psi(d(c-1))=1\rrbracket\right).

Definition 5.1
.

For all
c\in\mathcal{R}
, set
g(\psi,c):=\sum_{d\in\mathcal{R}_{\times}}\llbracket\psi(dc)=1\rrbracket
.

Lemma 5.2
.

We have

g(\psi,c-1)=\frac{(p-1)|\mathcal{R}|}{p}\cdot\llbracket\psi((c-1)\mathcal{R})=1\rrbracket.

Proof.

First, we claim that

\sum_{d\in\mathcal{R}}\llbracket\psi(dc)=1\rrbracket=\frac{|\mathcal{R}|}{p}\cdot p^{\llbracket\psi((c-1)\mathcal{R})=1\rrbracket}.

Suppose that
\psi((c-1)\mathcal{R})\neq 1
, as
\ker(\psi)\cap(c-1)\mathcal{R}=(c-1)\mathcal{R}
when
\psi((c-1)\mathcal{R})=1
. Let
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{\log_{p}(q)}\}
be an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-basis for
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Note that every element of
(c-1)\mathcal{R}
can be uniquely expressed as a linear combination of monomials of the form
u_{k}\cdot M
, where
M
is a monomial with coefficient
1
. Furthermore, note that there is a monomial
u_{k}\cdot M
such that
\psi(u_{k}\cdot M)\neq 1
. For any linear combination of all monomials except for
u_{k}\cdot M
, there is a unique choice of
1\leq j\leq p-1
for which a coefficient of
j
behind
u_{k}\cdot M
will result in the polynomial lying in
\ker(\psi)
. This implies that
|\ker(\psi)\cap(c-1)\mathcal{R}|=\frac{1}{p}\cdot|(c-1)\mathcal{R}|
, as claimed.

Now we will show that
g(\psi,c-1)=\frac{(p-1)|\mathcal{R}|}{p}\cdot\llbracket\psi((c-1)\mathcal{R})=1\rrbracket
. By the inclusion-exclusion principle, we have

g(\psi,c-1)=\sum_{T\subseteq[v]}\frac{|\mathcal{R}|}{p}\cdot p^{\llbracket\psi((c-1)x_{T}\mathcal{R})=1\rrbracket}(-1)^{|T|}.

We can rewrite the above as

\displaystyle\sum_{T\subseteq[v]}\frac{|\mathcal{R}|}{p}\cdot p^{\llbracket\psi((c-1)x_{T}\mathcal{R})=1\rrbracket}(-1)^{|T|}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\mathcal{R}|}{p}\cdot\left(\sum_{T\subseteq[v]}(p-1)(-1)^{|T|}\llbracket\psi((c-1)x_{T}\mathcal{R})=1\rrbracket\right)+\frac{|\mathcal{R}|}{p}\cdot\left(\sum_{T\subseteq[v]}(-1)^{|T|}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(p-1)|\mathcal{R}|}{p}\cdot\sum_{T\subseteq[v]}(-1)^{|T|}\llbracket\psi((c-1)x_{T}\mathcal{R})=1\rrbracket.

By another application of inclusion-exclusion, we compute

\sum_{T\subseteq[v]}(-1)^{|T|}\llbracket\psi((c-1)x_{T}\mathcal{R})=1\rrbracket=\llbracket\psi((c-1)\mathcal{R}_{\times})=1\rrbracket.

Note that
\psi((c-1)\mathcal{R}_{\times})=1
holds if and only if
\psi((c-1)\mathcal{R})=1
, as
\mathcal{R}_{\times}-\mathcal{R}_{\times}=\mathcal{R}
. Hence, we conclude that

g(\psi,c-1)=\frac{(p-1)|\mathcal{R}|}{p}\cdot\llbracket\psi((c-1)\mathcal{R})=1\rrbracket,

as desired. ∎

Since
g(\psi,c)
is determined by whether or not
c\mathcal{R}
is contained in
\ker(\psi)
, it is important to characterize all
c
for which
\psi(c\mathcal{R})=1
; we do so later in Lemma
6.1
.

Definition 5.3
.

We define
K_{\psi}
by the set of all
c\in\mathcal{R}
such that
\psi(c\mathcal{R})=1
.

The main result of this section is the following.

Proposition 5.4
.

For non-trivial characters
\chi
and
\psi
, we have

\displaystyle|G(\chi,\psi)|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{R}||K_{\psi}|\cdot\llbracket\chi(1+K_{\psi})=1\rrbracket.

Proof.

Merging our computations thus far, we have

\displaystyle|G(\chi,\psi)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{p-1}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{R}_{\times}}\chi(c)g(\psi,c-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{p-1}\cdot\frac{(p-1)|\mathcal{R}|}{p}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{R}_{\times}}\chi(c)\cdot\llbracket\psi((c-1)\mathcal{R})=1\rrbracket

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{R}|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in\mathcal{R}_{\times}\\
c\in 1+K_{\psi}\end{subarray}}\chi(c)

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{R}||K_{\psi}|\cdot\llbracket\chi(1+K_{\psi})=1\rrbracket.

∎

6.
Key Enumerations

6.1.
Distribution of codimension of ideals

In this subsection, we will give all necessary enumerations that pertain to the distribution of
\operatorname{codim}(K_{\psi})
over additive characters
\psi
on
\mathcal{R}
. We begin by showing that, in fact,
K_{\psi}
is always an ideal.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\psi
be an additive character on
\mathcal{R}
. The set
K_{\psi}
is the unique ideal of
\mathcal{R}
that is maximally contained in
\ker(\psi)
.

Proof.

Note that
K_{\psi}
is an additive subgroup of
\mathcal{R}
and that for all
c\in K_{\psi}
and
c^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}
, we have
\psi(cc^{\prime}\mathcal{R})=\psi(c\mathcal{R})=1
. Thus,
K_{\psi}
is an ideal of
\mathcal{R}
. Observe that
K_{\psi}
contains all ideals of
\mathcal{R}
contained in
\ker(\psi)
; otherwise, there exists an element
c\not\in K_{\psi}
with
c\mathcal{R}
contained in
\ker(\psi)
, a contradiction.
∎

Now, given an ideal
I
, we count the characters
\psi
for which
K_{\psi}=I
in Lemma
6.3
. This result, alongside providing counts, gives us the space of ideals we need to consider.

Definition 6.2
.

Define the
socle

\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I)
of the quotient ring
\mathcal{R}/I
by the additive subgroup of
\mathcal{R}/I
given by
\{f\in\mathcal{R}/I\mid x_{1}f=0,\dots,x_{v}f=0\}
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
I
be an ideal of
\mathcal{R}
. Then, the number of additive characters
\psi
on
\mathcal{R}
such that
I
is an ideal maximally contained in
\ker(\psi)
is

\frac{|\mathcal{R}|}{|I|}\cdot\begin{cases}1&\dim(\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I))=0\\
1-\frac{1}{q}&\dim(\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I))=1\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

It is well-known that the Möbius function
\mu(I,J)
on the lattice of ideals of
\mathcal{R}
is given by
(-1)^{r}q^{\binom{r}{2}}
, where
\dim(J)-\dim(I)=r
(see, for example,
[
16
, eq. (3.34)]
). Thus, by Möbius inversion, the desired number of additive characters becomes

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq J\subseteq\mathcal{R}\\
\dim(J)-\dim(I)=r\end{subarray}}(-1)^{r}q^{\binom{r}{2}}\cdot\frac{|\mathcal{R}|}{|J|}=\frac{|\mathcal{R}|}{|I|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq J\subseteq\mathcal{R}\\
\dim(J)-\dim(I)=r\end{subarray}}(-1)^{r}q^{\binom{r}{2}-r},

where all sums above run over ideals
J
. Re-parametrizing the above as a sum over all subspaces
J/I
of
\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I)
, we may rewrite the above sum as

\frac{|\mathcal{R}|}{|I|}\sum_{A\subseteq\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I)}(-1)^{\dim(A)}q^{\binom{\dim(A)}{2}-\dim(A)}.

Since there are
{\dim(\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I))\brack r}_{q}
subspaces of
\dim(\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I))
with dimension
r
, it follows that the above sum is

\frac{|\mathcal{R}|}{|I|}\sum_{r=0}^{\dim(\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I))}{\dim(\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I))\brack r}_{q}(-1)^{r}q^{\binom{r}{2}-r}=\frac{|\mathcal{R}|}{|I|}\prod_{k=0}^{\dim(\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I))-1}(1-q^{k-1})

by the
q
-binomial theorem. Thus, the desired result follows.
∎

Motivated by Lemma
6.3
, we characterize the ideals
I
of
\mathcal{R}
with
\dim(\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I))=1
.

Definition 6.4
.

For all
0\leq k\leq n-1
, let
V(n)
denote the subspace of
\mathcal{R}
consisting of
0
and all degree-
n
homogeneous polynomials. Furthermore, for each ideal
I
of
\mathcal{R}
, we denote
I(n)=I\cap V(n)
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
I
be an ideal of
\mathcal{R}
. Then
\dim(\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I))=1
if and only if there is a positive integer
1\leq n_{0}\leq n-1
such that

•

\dim(I(n_{0}))=\dim(V(n_{0}))-1
;

•

I(k)=V(k)
for all
k>n_{0}
;

•

I(k)=\{f\in V(k)\mid fx_{1},\dots,fx_{v}\in I(k+1)\}
for all
k<n_{0}
;

•

I
is homogeneous (that is,
I=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n-1}I(k)
).

Proof.

It is straightforward to check that ideals
I
satisfying the four given properties also satisfy
\dim(\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I))=1
. Let
n_{0}
denote the largest positive integer for which
I(n_{0})\neq V(n_{0})
; we will show that this
n_{0}
satisfies the hypotheses of the lemma.

If
\dim(I(n_{0}))<\dim(V(n_{0}))-1
, then there exist two elements
f,g\in V(n_{0})
such that
f\cdot\mathbb{F}_{q}\neq g\cdot\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
x_{i}f\in I
, and
x_{i}g\in I
for all
1\leq i\leq v
. This implies that
\dim(\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I))\geq 2
, so we must have
\dim(I(n_{0}))=\dim(V(n_{0}))-1
. Thus, the first two of the four properties hold. We also derive that for all
k<n_{0}
, we have
\{f\in V(k)\mid fx_{1},\dots,fx_{v}\in I(k+1)\}\subseteq I(k)
. Since
I
is an ideal,
I(k)\subseteq\{f\in V(k)\mid fx_{1},\dots,fx_{v}\in I(k+1)\}
; thus, the third property holds.

Now, we prove that
I
is homogeneous. Assume for contradiction that there exists an element
f\in I
such that if
f_{k}
denotes the degree-
k
component of
f
for all
k
, then there is an index
j
for which
f_{j}\not\in V(j)
. Let
j_{0}
denote the minimal index for which
f_{j_{0}}\not\in I(j_{0})
. By the third property, we have
f_{j_{0}}\cdot x_{1}^{n_{0}-j_{0}}\not\in I
. Observe that

\left(\sum_{j\geq j_{0}}f_{j}\right)\cdot x_{1}^{n_{0}-j_{0}}=f_{j_{0}}\cdot x_{1}^{n_{0}-j_{0}}+F,

where
F\in V(n_{0}+1)+\dots+V(n-1)\subseteq I
. Thus,
\left(\sum_{j\geq j_{0}}f_{j}\right)\cdot x_{1}^{n_{0}-j_{0}}\not\in I
. Since
I
is an ideal, we have
\sum_{j\geq j_{0}}f_{j}\not\in I
. Our selection of
j_{0}
establishes that
\sum_{j<j_{0}}f_{j}\in I
, so in summary,

f=\left(\sum_{j<j_{0}}f_{j}\right)+\left(\sum_{j\geq j_{0}}f_{j}\right)\not\in I,

a contradiction. Therefore,
I
is homogeneous, which completes the proof.
∎

With Proposition
6.5
established, we denote
n_{0}(I)
by the largest positive integer
n_{0}\leq n-1
such that
\dim(I(n_{0}))<\dim(V(n_{0}))
.

The characterization in Proposition
6.5
is sufficient for us to determine upper bounds on the distribution of
\operatorname{codim}(I)
over all ideals
I
of
\mathcal{R}
satisfying
\dim(\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I))=1
. As such, we may biject these ideals to generalized Hankel matrices with prescribed ranks.

Definition 6.6
.

Let
z
be an indeterminate. Define the
codimension enumerator

F_{n}(z)
of
\mathcal{R}
by the generating function

F_{n}(z):=\sum_{\dim(\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I))=1}z^{\operatorname{codim}(I)}.

Definition 6.7
.

Let
S=(a_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{v})})_{i_{1}+\dots+i_{v}=m}
, where the indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{v}
are nonnegative integers, be a multidimensional sequence with entries in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Define the
j
-Hankel matrix

M(S,j)
on
S
by the
\binom{(m-j)+v-1}{v-1}\times\binom{j+v-1}{v-1}
matrix associated with the map

\varphi:\{(s_{1},\dots,s_{v})\mid s_{1}+\dots+s_{v}=m-j\}\times\{(t_{1},\dots,t_{v})\mid t_{1}+\dots+t_{v}=j\}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}

given by

\varphi((s_{1},\dots,s_{v}),(t_{1},\dots,t_{v}))=a_{(s_{1}+t_{1},\dots,s_{v}+t_{v})}.

Remark
.

The
v=2
case represents regular Hankel matrices.

Proposition 6.8
.

For all positive integers
n
and real numbers
z>1
, we have

F_{n}(z)\leq z^{2\binom{\lceil n/2\rceil+v}{v}}q^{\binom{n+v-2}{v-1}}\cdot\frac{q}{(q-1)^{2}}.

Proof.

Consider an ideal
I_{0}
of
\mathcal{R}
with
\dim(\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I_{0}))=1
, and let
n_{0}:=n_{0}(I_{0})
. Then,
\dim(I_{0}(n_{0}))=\dim(V(n_{0}))-1
. Observe that, up to scaling, there exists a unique sequence
S=(c_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{v})})_{i_{1}+\dots+i_{v}=n_{0}}
of constants in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, not all
0
, such that

I_{0}(n_{0})=\left\{\sum_{i_{1}+\dots+i_{v}=n_{0}}a_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{v})}x_{1}^{i_{1}}\dots x_{v}^{i_{v}}\,\middle|\,\sum_{i_{1}+\dots+i_{v}=n_{0}}a_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{v})}c_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{v})}=0\right\}.

By the third property of Proposition
6.5
, it follows that

I_{0}(k)=\left\{\sum_{i_{1}+\dots+i_{v}=n_{0}}a_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{v})}x_{1}^{i_{1}}\dots x_{v}^{i_{v}}\,\middle|\,(a_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{v})})_{i_{1}+\dots+i_{v}=n_{0}}\in\ker(M(S,k))\right\}

for all
1\leq k\leq n_{0}
. Thus, for all
1\leq k\leq n_{0}
, we have
\operatorname{codim}(I_{0}(k))=\operatorname{rank}(M(S,k))
. This means that

\operatorname{codim}(I_{0})=\sum_{k=0}^{n_{0}}\operatorname{codim}(I_{0}(k))=\sum_{k=0}^{n_{0}}\operatorname{rank}(M(S,k)),

as
I_{0}
is homogeneous. Now, note the upper bound

\operatorname{rank}(M(S,k))\leq\binom{\min(k,n_{0}-1-k)+v-1}{v-1}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\operatorname{codim}(I)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{\min(k,n-1-k)+v-1}{v-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor-1}\binom{k+v-1}{v-1}+\sum_{k=\lfloor n/2\rfloor}^{n-1}\binom{(n-1-k)+v-1}{v-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor-1}\binom{k+v-1}{v-1}+\sum_{k=0}^{\lceil n/2\rceil-1}\binom{k+v-1}{v-1}

\displaystyle\leq 2\binom{\lceil n/2\rceil+v-1}{v}

for all ideals
I
of
\mathcal{R}
with
\dim(\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I))=1
. Since there are a total of

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{0}=1}^{n-1}{\dim(V(n_{0}))\brack 1}_{q}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{q^{i}}\cdot{\dim(V(n-1))\brack 1}_{q}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{q^{i}}\cdot\frac{1}{q-1}\cdot q^{\dim(V(n-1))}

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{(q-1)^{2}}\cdot q^{\binom{n+v-2}{v-1}}

ideals
I
of
\mathcal{R}
with
\dim(\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I))=1
, the codimension enumerator of
\mathcal{R}
is at most

z^{2\binom{\lceil n/2\rceil+v}{v}}q^{\binom{n+v-2}{v-1}}\cdot\frac{q}{(q-1)^{2}},

as desired.
∎

Proposition
6.8
successfully completes the task of finding an upper bound on the distribution of codimension over all ideals
I
that satisfy
\dim(\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I))=1
. In Section
7
, we also consider ideals of the form
K_{\psi_{x_{T}}}
, where
T
is a subset of
[v]
. Thus, we require the following.

Proposition 6.9
.

Let
T
be a subset of
[v]
. Then

\sum_{\dim(\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I))=1}z^{\operatorname{codim}(I\cap x_{T}\mathcal{R})}\leq\frac{q}{(q-1)^{2}}\cdot q^{\binom{n+v-2}{v-1}-\binom{n-|T|+v-2}{v-1}}+F_{n-|T|}(z).

Proof.

The number of ideals
I
of
\mathcal{R}
with
\dim(\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I))=1
that contain
x_{T}\mathcal{R}
is

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}{\binom{i+v-1}{v-1}-\binom{\max(0,i-|T|)+v-1}{v-1}\brack 1}_{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(q^{\binom{i+v-1}{v-1}-\binom{\max(0,i-|T|)+v-1}{v-1}}-1)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q}{(q-1)^{2}}\cdot q^{\binom{n+v-2}{v-1}-\binom{n-|T|+v-2}{v-1}},

by casework on
n_{0}(I)
and the first property of Proposition
6.5
. If
I
does not contain
x_{T}\mathcal{R}
, then
\dim(\operatorname{Soc}(x_{T}\mathcal{R}/(I\cap x_{T}\mathcal{R})))=1
; thus, such
I
return a contribution of
F_{n-|T|}(z)
. In total, we have

\sum_{\dim(\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal{R}/I))=1}z^{\operatorname{codim}(I\cap x_{T}\mathcal{R})}\leq\frac{q}{(q-1)^{2}}\cdot q^{\binom{n+v-2}{v-1}-\binom{n-|T|+v-2}{v-1}}+F_{n-|T|}(z),

as desired.
∎

6.2.
Enumerating multiplicative characters

We will now prove results on multiplicative character counts. An important idea that proves useful in Section
7
is that multiplicative characters and their products tend to have a “uniform distribution.” Taking advantage of this fact, which we present probabilistically in Proposition
6.11
, can lead to convenient simplifications in sums over multiplicative characters.

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group and let
G_{1},\dots,G_{n},H
be subgroups of
G
. Fix a character
\chi
on
G
. The number of tuples of characters
(\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{n})\in(\widehat{G})^{n}
such that

•

\chi_{i}(G_{i})=1
for every
1\leq i\leq n
, and

•

(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{i})(h)=\chi(h)
for all
h\in H

is given by

\frac{|H\cap K|}{|H|}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{|G|}{|G_{i}|}\cdot\llbracket\chi(H\cap K)=1\rrbracket,

where
K:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}G_{i}
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta
denote the desired count. Note that

\displaystyle\Delta\cdot\left(|H|\prod_{i=1}^{n}|G_{i}|\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\chi_{i}\in\widehat{G}}\sum_{g_{i}\in G_{i}}\sum_{h\in H}\chi_{1}(g_{1})\cdots\chi_{n}(g_{n})\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{i}\right)(h)\chi^{-1}(h)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\chi_{i}\in\widehat{G}}\sum_{g_{i}\in G_{i}}\sum_{h\in H}\chi_{1}(g_{1}h)\cdots\chi_{n}(g_{n}h)\chi^{-1}(h)

by the orthogonality of characters. Thus,

\displaystyle\Delta\cdot\left(|H|\prod_{i=1}^{n}|G_{i}|\right)

\displaystyle=|G|^{n}\sum_{g_{i}\in G_{i}}\sum_{h\in H}\llbracket g_{1}h=1\rrbracket\cdots\llbracket g_{n}h=1\rrbracket\chi^{-1}(h)

\displaystyle=|G|^{n}\sum_{h\in H}\llbracket h\in K\rrbracket\chi^{-1}(h)

\displaystyle=|G|^{n}\cdot|H\cap K|\cdot\llbracket\chi^{-1}(H\cap K)=1\rrbracket

\displaystyle=|G|^{n}\cdot|H\cap K|\cdot\llbracket\chi(H\cap K)=1\rrbracket,

so we have
\Delta=\frac{|H\cap K|}{|H|}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{|G|}{|G_{i}|}\cdot\llbracket\chi(H\cap K)=1\rrbracket
, as desired.
∎

In particular, we have the following.

Proposition 6.11

Let

\mathcal{R}

First, we contend that for all

Let

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(|G|/|\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}(1+I)|)^{k}}\cdot\frac{|H\cap K|}{|H|}\llbracket\chi(H\cap K)=1\rrbracket\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{|G|}{|G_{i}|}

Claim 6.12

In the multiplicative group
\mathcal{R}_{\times}

\operatorname{ord}(x_{l}-a)=p^{\lceil\log_{p}(n)\rceil}

For each

Claim 6.13

Let

over

th power.

\begin{bmatrix}1&1&\dots&1\\
a_{1}&a_{2}&\dots&a_{k}\\

\displaystyle|H|\geq\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor p/2\rfloor}\binom{q}{k}\cdot\left(p^{\lfloor\log_{p}(n)\rfloor}-1\right)^{k}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor p/2\rfloor}\binom{q}{k}\cdot\left(\frac{n}{p}\right)^{k}

∎

We require one final technical fact, which regards the distribution of a multiplicative character sum. This is a straightforward application of Claim

Lemma 6.14
.

We have

\mathbb{E}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{R}_{\times}}}\left[\left|\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\chi(x_{l}-a)\right|\right]\leq\frac{q-1}{\sqrt{p-1}}.

Proof.

Let
a_{1},\dots,a_{p-1}
be any
p-1
distinct elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. Then we have

\mathbb{E}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{R}_{\times}}}\left[\left|\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\chi(x_{l}-a_{k})\right|^{2}\right]=p-1,

as all
x_{l}-a_{k}
terms are multiplicatively independent in
\mathcal{R}_{\times}
per Claim
6.13
. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\mathbb{E}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{R}_{\times}}}\left[\left|\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\chi(x_{l}-a_{k})\right|\right]^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{R}_{\times}}}\left[\left|\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\chi(x_{l}-a_{k})\right|^{2}\right]=p-1,

so by the triangle inequality we have

\mathbb{E}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{R}_{\times}}}\left[\left|\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\chi(x_{l}-a)\right|\right]\leq\frac{q-1}{p-1}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{R}_{\times}}}\left[\left|\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\chi(x_{l}-a_{k})\right|\right]\leq\frac{q-1}{p-1}\cdot\sqrt{p-1}=\frac{q-1}{\sqrt{p-1}},

as desired.
∎

Remark
.

We remark that when

gives a stronger result than that of the Weil bounds.

The Li-Wan sieve formula was previously used in determining the distance distribution of Reed-Solomon codes

8.
Future Directions

In this section, we provide several research questions that allow for potential extensions of this work.

The simplest type of variant of the main problem we consider are those involving prescribed evaluation vectors. This variant uses nearly the same character sum formula as for prescribed zeroes.

Question 8.1
.

Use our approach to count polynomials with prescribed evaluation vectors, coefficients, and zeroes (e.g. polynomials whose evaluations are all squares in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
).

A second possible generalization of this work is to consider a different quotient ring. While this generalization does not directly apply to coding theory, it is another type of polynomial enumeration problem to which a similar approach can apply. We note that our results on ideal enumeration in truncated polynomial quotient rings are very specific to those quotient rings, which makes this generalization worthwhile.

Question 8.2
.

Generalize the main result to quotient rings of the form
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{v}]/I_{0}
, where
I_{0}
is an ideal of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{v}]
.

Another limitation of this work (which is also present in
[
7
,
13
]
) is that we require
D
to be the same (or nearly the same) as
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
, or else we cannot factor some of our character sums into character sums on linear polynomials. This prompts us to ask the following.

Question 8.3
.

What types of approaches can be implemented when
D
deviates significantly from
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
?

Finally, we ask if the basis of Lagrange-interpolating polynomials we consider can be made more efficient.

Question 8.4
.

We characterized polynomials with prescribed vanishing set using Lagrange interpolation. Can a stronger characterization (perhaps borrowing ideas from algebraic geometry) be used to strengthen our error bounds?

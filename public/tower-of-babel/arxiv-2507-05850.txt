Time-domain interferences as the source of electron-ion entanglement in Rabi-dressed photoemission
Léonardo Rico, Morgan Berkane, Jonathan Dubois, Jérémie Caillat, Richard Taïeb, Camille Lévêque

Abstract
We investigate bipartite entanglement between a photoelectron and its parent ion when the latter undergoes Rabi oscillations, following the recent experiment of [Nandi et al. Science Advances 10 , eado0668 (2024) ]. Using numerical simulations on a model atom, we show that this entanglement results from ionization events occurring at different times, with the photoelectron leaving the ion in distinct superpositions of internal states due to the Rabi coupling. Our interpretation brings forward the possibility to access the purity of the photoion state from photoelectron spectra. Furthermore, we demonstrate a tomographic reconstruction of the dressed ionic state dynamics from the observable spectra.



Entanglement lies at the heart of the second quantum revolution and has attracted increasing interest in multiple fields, including solid-state physics
Simmons et al. 2011
;
Takeda et al. 2021
, cold atoms
Kunkel et al. 2018
;
Fadel et al. 2018

Graham et al. 2022
;
Evered et al. 2023
. At a more fundamental level, it is the cornerstone of the Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen paradox
Einstein et al. 1935

In particular, entanglement between a photoelectron and its parent ion has recently gained considerable attention in the context of photoemission with coherent and intense light sources

By essence, an atom that ionizes through a single channel does not exhibit entanglement between the photoelectron and the residual ion, as the total wavefunction in the final state factorizes into a single product of the two subsystems’ states. In a recent experiment
Nandi et al. 2024
, He atom interacting with a free-electron laser pulse
McNeil and Thompson 2010
intense enough to simultaneously induce photoemission and Rabi oscillations in the parent ion was investigated. The appearance of a Grobe-Eberly (GE) doublet
Grobe and Eberly 1993
in the photoelectron spectrum was there identified as a clear manifestation of entanglement, in contrast with the photoemission process from a Rabi-dressed neutral atom leading to the so-called Autler–Townes doublet
Girju et al. 2007
;
Rohringer and Santra 2008
;
Demekhin and Cederbaum 2012a
;
Demekhin and Cederbaum 2012b
;
Zhang and Rohringer 2014
;
Deng et al. 2025
;
Wan et al. 2025
;
Nandi et al. 2022
.
This dressed photoemission scheme has since then become a benchmark case for the study of light-induced entanglement between massive particles,
and it is the subject of recent theoretical investigations
Ishikawa et al. 2023
;
Stenquist and Dahlström 2025
.

Here, we present a comprehensive mechanism for the generation of such entanglement and demonstrate how it enables experimental access to both the final purity and the tomographic reconstruction of the dressed ionic states dynamics. In the field configuration considered so far, it is tedious to establish the underlying physical mechanism, since the same light pulse induces both the ionization process and Rabi oscillations in the ion
Grobe and Eberly 1993
;
Nandi et al. 2024
. To overcome this difficulty, we performed a series of additional simulations with purposely designed pulses.
We used a two-color scheme to unambiguously discriminate the Rabi dressing from the photoemission process. While keeping the same pulse as before to drive the Rabi oscillations, we added time-shifted ionizing pulses at frequency
\omega_{\text{I}}=32.65
eV adjusted to induce a photoelectron peak well separated from the GE doublet, see Fig.
1
(c,b).

First, when both the ionizing and Rabi pulses have the same duration, the GE doublet is imprinted in the low energy part of the photo electron spectrum,
E\sim 8.1
eV, resulting from the absorption of one
\omega_{I}
photon, see Fig.
1
(b). This shows that the signature of the Rabi-dressing of the ionic states is independent of the ionization process.

Then, we use now a pair of ionizing pulses with an envelop lasting
1.90
fs (15 cycles), i.e., much shorter than the Rabi period. This ensures to achieve a satisfactory time resolution. In the following, we denote
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
the central times of the ionizing pulse envelops and
\tau=t_{2}-t_{1}
their tunable relative delay, see Fig.
1
(c). Without loss of generality, we set
t_{1}
at the maximum of the Rabi pulse, i.e.,
t_{1}=0
. This field sequence is analogous to one used for Ramsey-like interferometry
Ramsey 1950
.

Figure
3
displays photoelectron spectra for three illustrative ionization delays (
\tau=0,2,6
and
10
fs), along with the corresponding final purity values. At
\tau=0
, the two pulses effectively merge, resulting in
P=1
, i.e., no entanglement. This shows that Rabi oscillations alone, when ionization occurs at a well-defined instant, do not generate entanglement. In other words, the dynamics in the ion does not affect the photoelectron state upon ionization. In contrast, when the delay increases, spectral oscillations appear, with a period of
2\pi/\tau
, reminiscent of Ramsay fringes. They are accompanied by a decrease of
P
, reaching a minimum at half a Rabi period.

This variation of the final purity with the delay provides valuable physical insight into the entanglement mechanism. As
\tau
increases, ionization occurs from increasingly different superpositions of Rabi-dressed states. Entanglement then arises from spectral interferences between the two photoelectron wavepackets created at times
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
. This is similar to the mechanism first designed and investigated in
Vrakking 2021
. Panel (b) of Fig.
3
shows a clear correlation between the purity evolution (black stars) and the population of the field-free ionic ground state (orange line) as a function of
\tau

To formalize how this entanglement is imprinted in the observable photoelectron spectra, we now establish an explicit relationship between the visibility of the
\tau
-dependent oscillations and the corresponding value of
P
.
To this end, we express the normalized wavefunction of the final ionized system as the coherent superposition

|\Psi(t)\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{2}|\chi_{i}(t)\rangle\otimes|\psi_{i}(t)\rangle

(2)

of two pure product-states of the electron-ion pair created at
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
respectively In each contribution (
i=1,2
),
|\psi_{i}(t)\rangle
represents the photoelectron wave packet, while
|\chi_{i}(t)\rangle
represents the ionic state launched within the Rabi pulse with the initial condition
|\chi_{i}(t_{i})\rangle=|g\rangle
. Each photoelectron wavepacket undergoes a field free evolution,

|\psi_{i}(t)\rangle=\int\,\psi_{i}(E)|E\rangle e^{-iEt}\,dE,

(3)

where
E
is the photoelectron energy referred to the ionization threshold and
|E\rangle
the continuum eigenstates. The associated spectral amplitudes are identical up to a phase difference accumulated between the respective birth times, i.e.,

\psi_{2}(E)=\psi_{1}(E)e^{iE\tau}.

(4)

For the sake of convenience, we switch to the Rabi-dressed basis and express the evolution of each ionic state as

|\chi_{i}(t)\rangle=\sum_{j=0}^{1}c_{j}(t_{i})|D_{j}(t)\rangle,

(5)

In this framework, the state-resolved photoelectron spectra read

S_{g,e}(E)=\frac{1}{2}|\psi_{1}(E)|^{2}\left|c_{0,1}(t_{1})+c_{0,1}(t_{2})e^{iE\tau}\right|^{2},

(6)

and the total spectrum is then expressed as

S(E)=|\psi_{1}(E)|^{2}\left[1+\mathcal{V}\times\cos{(E\tau+\phi)}\right].

(7)

Although it corresponds to the incoherent sum
S_{g}(E)+S_{e}(E)
on the channel basis, it bears signatures of interferences that appear when considering the dressed-state basis. They materialize as the
\tau
-dependent visibility and phase of the oscillatory patterns in the spectra, respectively

\begin{split}\mathcal{V}&=\left|\sum_{j=0}^{1}c_{j}(t_{1})c^{*}_{j}(t_{2})\right|\\
\phi&=\arg\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{1}c_{j}(t_{1})c^{*}_{j}(t_{2})\right\}.\end{split}

(8)

To obtain an explicit relationship between
\mathcal{V}
and the purity
P
, we consider the photoion’s RDM
\rho_{ion}
in the final state (i.e.
t>T_{R}
and we discard the
t
-dependencies in the following derivations).
Since time-dependent ionic states are not necessarily orthogonal, we introduce the projection
|\chi_{\perp}\rangle=(1-|\chi_{1}\rangle\langle\chi_{1}|)|\chi_{2}\rangle
and the overlaps
\alpha=\langle\chi_{1}|\chi_{2}\rangle
and
\beta=\langle\chi_{\perp}|\chi_{2}\rangle
. Expanding the total final wavefunction
|\Psi\rangle
(Eq.
2
) on the orthogonal
\{|\chi_{1}\rangle,|\chi_{\perp}\rangle\}
basis and tracing out the photoelectron degree of freedom in the total density matrix leads to the compact expression

\rho_{ion}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1+|\alpha|^{2}&\alpha^{*}\beta\\
\alpha\beta^{*}&|\beta|^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(9)

By furthermore noting that the visibility can be expressed as
\mathcal{V}=|\langle\chi_{1}|\chi_{2}\rangle|
at all times, such that
|\alpha|^{2}=\mathcal{V}^{2}
and
|\beta|^{2}=1-\mathcal{V}^{2}
, we reach the simple and direct formal mapping,

P=\frac{1}{2}(1+\mathcal{V}^{2}).

(10)

This opens a promising avenue to experimentally obtain the purity of the final bipartite system from the visibility of the oscillations in the measurable photoelectron spectrum. An identical mapping was established in
Dittel et al. 2021
, in the context of matter-wave interference when the particle entangles with the quantum state of the double slits.

The validity of this result is assessed in Fig.
3
. We find a perfect agreement between the numerically exact purity extracted from the simulations (open circles) and the ones extracted directly from the visibility (colored circles) of the photoelectron spectra displayed in panel (a). Moreover, at
\tau=0
,
t_{1}=t_{2}
and thus
\mathcal{V}=|\langle\chi_{1}|\chi_{1}\rangle|=1
, leading to a purity
P=1
, i.e., no entanglement.
Finally, we have checked that, when using many ionizing pulses sampling the whole Rabi pulse, we recover the GE doublet. This validates our interpretation of interferences in the time domain as a source of entanglement.

Actually, Eq.
7
contains significantly more physically valuable information, opening the way to a complete tomography of the ionic dynamics in the dressed state basis. Indeed, the time evolution of
c_{0}
is conveniently obtained by setting one ionizing pulse at
t_{1}>T_{R}
, such that the ionic states are not dressed, notably
|D_{0}(t_{1})\rangle=|g\rangle
, thus
c_{0}(t_{1})=1
and
c_{1}(t_{1})=0
. By noting
\mathcal{V}_{0}
and
\phi_{0}
the visibility and phase in this case, indentifying
t
and
t_{2}
, Eq.
8
reduces to

c_{0}(t)=\mathcal{V}_{0}e^{-i\phi_{0}}.

(11)

Thus, we directly access the modulus and phase of
c_{0}(t)
from the measured spectra.

Then, the second coefficient,
c_{1}(t)
, is obtained by setting
t_{1}
to any other value inside the Rabi pulse and measuring the visibility and phase (
\mathcal{V}_{1}
and
\phi_{1}
), while scanning
t_{2}
. Using again Eq.
8
and the previously reconstructed
c_{0}(t)
, we obtain for each
t=t_{2}
,

c_{1}(t)=\mathcal{N}\left({\mathcal{V}_{1}}e^{-i{\phi_{1}}}-c_{0}^{\star}(t_{1})c_{0}(t)\right).

(12)

The modulus of the reference-dependent coefficient
\mathcal{N}
is equal to
1/\sqrt{1-|c_{0}(t_{1})|^{2}}
and its phase depends on an arbitrary origin. In the results presented hereafter, we simply set
\arg(\mathcal{N})=0
. Note that, for the resonant case, a convenient choice for
t_{1}
is at the maximum of population inversion, which leads to
c_{0}(t_{1})=0
and cancels the last term in Eq. (
12
).

To illustrate our procedure, we considered the resonant case discussed so far, i.e.,
\Delta=\omega_{R}-(E_{e}-E_{g})=0

\Delta=75.34
meV which leads to a population inversion of
\sim 80\%

(symbols) is in perfect agreement with the numerically exact ion dynamics (solid line), for both
\Delta=0
meV (green/circles) and
\Delta=75.34
meV (blue/bullets).
The phase of

In this Letter, we presented an experimentally feasible scheme for quantum state tomography of Rabi-dressed states using only the photoelectron spectra. This scheme is quite general and not limited to Rabi oscillations in ionic states of atoms, as it requires only a few conditions: (i) synchronized and delay-controlled ionizing pulses that are short enough such that
T_{I}\ll 2\pi/\Omega
, and (ii) an accessible transition for the dressing within a high-intensity laser pulse. Our work enhances the understanding of bipartite entanglement generation in the context of photoionization. We show that entanglement arises from ionization at different times, which leaves the ionic dressed parent in a different state superposition. The entanglement appears in the momentum imaging of the photoelectrons.

Numerical simulations
were carried out
on a
2\otimes
1D-electron model atom based on the hamiltonian given in Eq. (

(13)

with

. The resulting field-free energies are

a.u.,
E(1s)=-2.0014
a.u. and

a.u., for the He ground-state and for the He

\displaystyle f(t)=\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{T}t\right)\mathds{1}_{t\in[-\frac{T}{2},+\frac{T}{2}]},

Photoelectron spectra
were obtained using the time dependent surface flux method (t-SURFF)

70

The purity
of the final system is obtained from the ionic reduced density matrix,
\rho_{ion}=\mbox{tr}_{e}(\rho)
, where

\rho=\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}|\Psi(T)\rangle\langle\Psi(T)|

(15)

is the total density matrix when the electric fields are off, and
\mbox{tr}_{e}
indicates the trace over the photoelectron degree of freedom. The normalized purity reads then,
P=\mbox{tr}(\rho_{\mbox{\tiny ion}}^{2})/\mbox{tr}(\rho_{\mbox{\tiny ion}})^{2}
. In our case, since the system is bipartite, the purity obtained from the ionic RDM or the photoelectron RDM are identical, and we chose the former for convenience.

Uniform estimates: from Yau to Kołodziej
Vincent Guedj & Chinh H. Lu

Abstract
. In this note we provide a new and efficient approach to uniform estimates for solutions to complex Monge-Ampère equations, as well as for solutions to geometric PDE’s that satisfy a determinantal majorization.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
32W20, 32U05, 32Q15, 35A23

Introduction

Let
X
be a compact Kähler
n
-dimensional manifold equipped with a Kähler form
\omega
.
The complex Monge-Ampère equation is

(MA(f))

\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=fdV_{X},

where
V_{\omega}=\int_{X}\omega^{n}
is the volume of
(X,\omega)
,

dV_{X}
is a smooth probability measure,
and
f>0
is a smooth density normalized so that
\int_{X}fdV_{X}=1
.

Complex Monge-Ampère equations are ubiquitous in complex geometry, as the Ricci curvature of a Kähler form can be expressed by a complex Monge-Ampère operator.
A crucial step in order to prove the existence of a solution to such equations is to establish a uniform a priori estimate.
A landmark result due to Yau
[
Yau78
]
asserts that any solution to the above equation satisfies

{\rm Osc}_{X}(\varphi)\leq C(||f||_{p}),

where
C
depends on an upper-bound on the
L^{p}
-norm
||f||_{p}
of
f
, for some
p>n

(see Section
1.4
). The
{\mathcal{C}}^{0}
-estimate is known to be the most difficult,
and often the only one that fails for similar non linear PDEs of (complex) geometric origin.

In connection with the Minimal Model Program, the study of degenerate complex Monge-Ampère equations has become a central
theme in Kähler geometry, as advocated by
[
EGZ09
,
BEGZ10
]
.
In this context it becomes essential to establish finer a priori estimates, and understand how these
behave when the density, the cohomology class
\{\omega\}
and the complex structure vary.
A key result, which preceded these developments, is due to Kołodziej
[
Kol98
]
, who proved

{\rm Osc}_{X}(\varphi)\leq C(||f||_{p}),

where
p>1
can be arbitrarily close to
1
.
Kołodziej’s technique allows one to even replace
||f||_{p}
by the Luxembourg norm

||f||_{w}
associated to some weaker Orlicz space.
We refer the reader to Section
1.3
for the precise formulation of the
(quasi-)optimal
Condition (K)
.

In the last fifteen years several new approaches have been developed to prove uniform a priori estimates
for a large family of equations, in Kähler as well as in hermitian contexts
(see
[
EGZ08
,
DP10
,
TW10
,
Blo11
,
DK12
,
KN15
,
Szek18
,
DnGG23
,
GPT23
,
GL23
,
GP24
,
GL25
]
). Dealing with Orlicz conditions makes the computation
and geometric dependence of the constants hard to follow.
The first contribution of this note is to provide an efficient
reduction to Yau’s setting:

Theorem A
.

Fix
p>n
.
Assume that
{\rm Osc}_{X}(\varphi)\leq C(||f||_{p})
for
any smooth solution
\varphi
of the above complex Monge-Ampère equation (
MA(f)
).
Then

{\rm Osc}_{X}(\varphi)\leq C^{\prime}(||f||_{w}),

where
w
is any weight that satisfies
Condition (K)
, and

||f||_{w}
denotes the Luxembourg norm of
f
with respect to
w
.

While this result is due to Kołodziej
[
Kol98
]
, our proof is very different
and allows to recover Kołodziej’s optimal condition from Yau’s original statement.

Generalizing an idea from
[
GL25b
]
, we then show how Theorem A
can be used to obtain uniform a priori estimates for large families of geometric nonlinear PDE’s
which have been recently studied by many authors.
Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote an open symmetric convex cone with vertex at the origin such that

\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\subset\Gamma\subset\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}>0\}.

A smooth function
\varphi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
belong to
{\Gamma}(X,\omega)
if
the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}

of
\omega+dd^{c}\varphi
with respect to
\omega
belong to
\Gamma
at all points
x\in X
.

We fix
\delta>0
and a positive function
g:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
such that

(1)

g
is symmetric (invariance under permutations);

(2)

\frac{\partial g}{\partial\lambda_{j}}(\lambda)>0
for all
\lambda\in\Gamma
(ellipticity condition);

(3)

g(\lambda)\geq\delta\left(\pi_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\right)^{1/n}
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
(determinantal majorization).

We consider the nonlinear PDE

(NL)

g(\lambda(\varphi))=cf^{{1}/{n}},

where

•

g
satisfies the conditions
(1)
-
(2)
-
(3)
above;

•

f
is a fixed smooth positive density;

•

c>0
is a normalizing constant determined by the equation;

•

\varphi
is the unknown, a smooth function in
{\Gamma}(X,\omega)
.

Theorem B
.

If
\varphi
is a smooth solution to (
NL
) then

{\rm Osc}_{X}(\varphi)\leq M_{0},

where
M_{0}
depends on an upper bound on the Luxembourg norm
||f||_{{w}}
,
for any weight
w
that satisfies
Condition (K)
.

The assumptions made on
g
are slightly less general than the ones considered by
Szekelyhidi
[
Szek18
]
and Guo-Phong-Tong in
[
GPT23
]
,
but they nevertheless cover a huge variety of complex geometric PDEs as observed by
Harvey-Lawson
[
HL23
]
(see also
[
ADO22
,
CX24
]
).

Our proof applies equally well to the hermitian context and shows that
M_{0}
is
independent of the complex structure, as we indicate in Remark
3.5
.

1.
Basic facts on quasi-plurisubharmonic functions

In the whole article we fix
X
a compact complex manifold of dimension
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,

\omega_{X}
a hermitian form, and let
dV_{X}
be a smooth probability measure.

1.1.
Skoda-Zeriahi theorem

A function is quasi-plurisubharmonic (quasi-psh) if it is locally given as the sum of a smooth and a psh function.

Fix
\omega
another smooth hermitian
(1,1)
-form.

Definition 1.1
.

Quasi-psh functions

\varphi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
satisfying

\omega_{\varphi}:=\omega+dd^{c}\varphi\geq 0

in the weak sense of currents are called
\omega
-plurisubharmonic (
\omega
-psh for short).
We let
{\rm PSH}(X,\omega)
denote the set of all
\omega
-plurisubharmonic functions which are not identically
-\infty
.

Note that constant functions are
\omega
-psh functions.
A
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
-smooth function
u
has bounded Hessian, hence
\varepsilon u
is

\omega
-psh if
0<\varepsilon
is small enough and
\omega
is positive.

The set
{\rm PSH}(X,\omega)
is a closed subset of
L^{1}(X)
,
for the
L^{1}
-topology.
Subsets of
\omega
-psh functions enjoy strong compactness properties,
in particular

{\rm PSH}_{0}(X,\omega):=\{u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\omega),\,\sup_{X}u=0\}

is compact in
L^{r}
for all
r\geq 1
.
They also enjoy uniform integrability properties:

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix
A>0
and
\omega
a Kähler form such that

\int_{X}\omega\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-1}\leq A
.
There exists
\alpha=\alpha(n,A)>0
such that for all

\varphi\in PSH_{0}(X,\omega)
,

\int_{X}\exp(-\alpha\varphi)dV_{X}\leq C,

where
C=C(\alpha,n,A)>0
is independent of
\omega
and
\varphi
.

We refer the reader to
[
GZ17
, Theorem 8.11]
for this result, as well as for
further basic properties of
\omega
-psh functions.

1.2.
The
\omega
-psh envelope

Upper envelopes of (pluri)subharmonic functions are classical objects in Potential Theory.
We consider here envelopes of
\omega
-psh functions; they have shown to be extremely
useful in the study of degenerate complex Monge-Ampère equations
(see e.g.
[
GL25
,
GL23
]
).

Definition 1.3
.

Given a Lebesgue measurable function
h:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we define the
\omega
-psh envelope of
h
by

P_{\omega}(h):=\left(\sup\{u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\omega);u\leq h\,\,\text{in}\,\,X\}\right)^{*},

where the star means that we take the upper semi-continuous regularization.

The following result is due to
[
Ber19
,
Tos18
,
CZ19
]
:

Theorem 1.4
.

If
h
is
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}
-smooth then

•

P_{\omega}(h)
is
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,1}
-smooth;

•

(\omega+dd^{c}P_{\omega}(h))^{n}
is concentrated on the contact
\{P_{\omega}(h)=h\}
;

•

one has
\omega+dd^{c}h\geq 0
on
\{P_{\omega}(h)=h\}
and

(\omega+dd^{c}P_{\omega}(h))^{n}=1_{\{P_{\omega}(h)=h\}}(\omega+dd^{c}h)^{n}.

1.3.
Condition (K)

Definition 1.5
.

A weight
w:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfies
Condition (K)
if it is
convex increasing and there exists an increasing function
h
such that

w(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle+\infty}}{{\sim}}t(\log t)^{n}(h\circ\log\circ\log t)^{n}\;\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\int^{+\infty}\frac{dt}{h(t)}<+\infty.

If
w
satisfies
Condition (K)
, it has been proved by Kołodziej (see
[
Kol98
, Theorem 2.5.2]
) that
any smooth solution
\varphi
of

\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=fdV_{X},

satisfies a uniform a priori bound on
{\rm Osc}_{X}(\varphi)
that depends on an explicit upper bound on
the Luxembourg norm
||f||_{w}
, where

||f||_{w}=\inf\left\{r>0,\;\int_{X}w\left(\frac{|f|}{r}\right)dV_{X}<w(1)\right\}.

Example 1.6
.

Among the most classical families of weights, we mention

•

u_{p}(t)=t^{p}
satisfies
Condition (K)
whenever
p>1
;

•

v_{p}(t)=t(\log(t+10))^{p}
satisfies
Condition (K)
if and only if
p>n
;

•

w_{p}(t)=t(\log(t+10))^{n}(\log\circ\log(t+10))^{p}
satisfies
Condition (K)
if and only if
p>n
.

In Section
2

we provide a new and simpler proof of Kołodziej’s result, that goes through a reduction to the case of

L^{p}
densities, where
p
is as large as desired (e.g.
p>n
so as to apply Yau’s original method).

1.4.
Various approaches to uniform estimates

Yau’s proof of his famous
L^{\infty}
-a priori estimate
[
Yau78
]
goes through a Moser iteration process,
as we recall in Section
2.1
.
The method did not apply when the right hand side is too degenerate,
but we show in Section
2.1
how to extend it.

An important generalization of Yau’s estimate has been provided by Kołodziej
[
Kol98
]
using pluripotential techniques.
These have been further generalized in
[
EGZ09
,
EGZ08
,
DP10
,
BEGZ10
]

in order to deal with less positive or collapsing families of cohomology classes on Kähler manifolds.
This method has been partially extended to the hermitian setting in
[
DK12
,
KN15
,
KN19
,
KN21
,
GL23
]
.

Blocki has provided a different approach in
[
Blo11
]
based on the Alexandroff-Bakelman-Pucci maximum principle.
This has been pushed further by Szekelehydi in
[
Szek18
]
.
It requires the reference form
\omega
to be strictly positive.

A PDE proof has been provided by
Guo-Phong-Tong
[
GPT23
,
GP24
]
, extending ideas from
[
CC21
,
WWZ21
]
.
This technique applies to large families of geometric PDEs and has led to
striking geometric applications (diameter and non collapsing bounds, uniform controls
on Sobolev constants heat kernels, and positivity of
Kähler-Einstein metrics, see
[
GPSS24
,
GPSS23
,
GT24
,
Szek24
,
V24
]
).

We provide in Section
3
an alternative proof of the uniform estimate
for many such equations, using
\omega
-psh envelopes.
The latter lie at the heart of yet another approach
to the
L^{\infty}
-a priori estimate, that we have developed in
[
GL23
,
GL25
]
.

2.
From Yau to Kołodziej

2.1.
Proof of Theorem A

Fix
\omega
a Kähler form, set
V_{\omega}=\int_{X}\omega^{n}
, and
let
\varphi\in PSH_{0}(X,\omega)
be a smooth solution to the complex Monge-Ampère equation

\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=fdV_{X},

where
f>0
a smooth density such that
\int_{X}fdV_{X}=1
.

Step 1

We first recall Yau’s celebrated a priori estimate, as simplified by Kazdan and Aubin-Bourguignon,
following the presentation of Blocki
[
Blo12
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Fix
p>n
. There exists
C_{1}=C_{1}(n,p,C_{S}(\omega),||f||_{p})>0
such that

{\rm Osc}_{X}(\varphi)\leq C_{1},

where
C_{S}(\omega)
denotes the Sobolev constant of
\omega
.

Proof.

Translating and rescaling we can assume that
V_{\omega}=1
and
\sup_{X}\varphi=-1
.
Replacing
f
by
\tilde{f}
if necessary, we further assume that
dV_{X}=\omega^{n}
.
Observe that
||\varphi||_{r}\leq||\varphi||_{s}
if
r\leq s<\infty
.
We have

(f-1)\omega^{n}=(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}-\omega^{n}=dd^{c}\varphi\wedge T,

where

T=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{j}\wedge\omega^{n-1-j}\geq\omega^{n-1}.

Stokes theorem yields, for
r>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{X}(-\varphi)^{r}(f-1)\omega^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}(-\varphi)^{r}dd^{c}\varphi\wedge T=-\int_{X}d(-\varphi)^{r}\wedge d^{c}\varphi\wedge T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r\int_{X}(-\varphi)^{r-1}d\varphi\wedge d^{c}\varphi\wedge T\geq r\int_{X}(-\varphi)^{r-1}d\varphi\wedge d^{c}\varphi\wedge\omega^{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4r}{(r+1)^{2}}\int_{X}d(-\varphi)^{\frac{r+1}{2}}\wedge d^{c}(-\varphi)^{\frac{r+1}{2}}\wedge\omega^{n-1}

so that

\int_{X}(-\varphi)^{r}(f-1)\omega^{n}\geq\frac{c_{n}r}{(r+1)^{2}}||\nabla(-\varphi)^{\frac{r+1}{2}}||_{2}^{2}.

Using Hölder inequality we obtain

||\nabla(-\varphi)^{\frac{r+1}{2}}||_{2}\leq c_{n}^{\prime}\sqrt{1+||f||_{p}}\frac{r+1}{\sqrt{r}}\left(\int_{X}(-\varphi)^{rq}\omega^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2q}},

where
q
denotes the Hölder conjugate of
p
.
Using Sobolev inequality

||v||_{\frac{2n}{n-1}}\leq C_{S}(\omega)\left(||v||_{2}+||\nabla v||_{2}\right),

we infer

||\varphi||_{\frac{n(r+1)}{n-1}}\leq C^{\frac{2}{r+1}}(1+||f||_{p})^{\frac{2}{r+1}}\left\{||\varphi||_{r+1}+\left(\frac{r+1}{\sqrt{r}}\right)^{\frac{2}{r+1}}\left(||\varphi||_{rq}\right)^{\frac{r}{r+1}}\right\}.

Assume now that
p<+\infty
hence
q>1
. We are going to use the previous inequality for

r\geq\frac{1}{q-1}
, so we can assume that
r+1\leq rq
. Our normalization ensures that

||\varphi||_{rq}\geq 1
, so the previous inequality yields the following

||\varphi||_{\frac{n(r+1)}{n-1}}\leq\left(C^{\prime}[r+1]\right)^{\frac{1}{r+1}}||\varphi||_{rq}.

This inequality is only meaningful when
\frac{n(r+1)}{n-1}>rq
. This requires

\frac{n}{n-1}>q
, which is equivalent to
p>n
.

We can now apply Moser’s iteration scheme: set

r_{0}:=\frac{1}{q-1}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;r_{k}:=\frac{n}{n-1}\left(\frac{r_{k-1}}{q}+1\right).

We obtain

||\varphi||_{\infty}=\lim_{k\to\infty}||\varphi||_{r_{k}}\leq||\varphi||_{\frac{1}{q-1}}\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(C^{\prime}\left[\frac{r_{k-1}}{q}+1\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{\frac{r_{k-1}}{q}+1}}.

This infinite product converges as
r_{k}
grows geometrically towards
+\infty
.
The conclusion follows as
||\varphi||_{\frac{1}{q-1}}
is bounded by compactness of
PSH_{0}(X,\omega)
.
∎

Step 2

Fix
n<\tilde{p}<p
. Using Hölder inequality and Theorem
1.2
we can fix

0<\gamma=\gamma(n,p,A)=\alpha(n,p,A)\frac{p-\tilde{p}}{p\tilde{p}}

so that
\tilde{f}=e^{-\gamma\varphi}f\in L^{\tilde{p}}
with

||\tilde{f}||_{\tilde{p}}\leq||f||_{p}\left(\int_{X}e^{-\alpha\varphi}dV_{X}\right)^{\frac{p-\tilde{p}}{p\tilde{p}}}\leq C_{1}||f||_{p}.

We let
\rho
denote the smooth solution in
PSH_{0}(X,\omega)
of the equation

\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}(\omega+dd^{c}\rho)^{n}=\frac{\tilde{f}dV_{X}}{\int_{X}\tilde{f}dV_{X}}=\tilde{F}dV_{X},

where
\int_{X}\tilde{f}dV_{X}=\int_{X}e^{-\gamma\varphi}fdV_{X}\geq\int_{X}fdV_{X}=1
since
\varphi\leq 0
, hence

||\tilde{F}||_{\tilde{p}}\leq||\tilde{f}||_{\tilde{p}}\leq C_{1}||f||_{p}.

It follows from Theorem
2.1
that
\rho
is uniformly bounded.
We use this function to establish the following useful technical result:

Lemma 2.2
.

Fix
p>n
,
0<a<1
and
b>0
. Assume that there is a smooth function
v\in PSH_{0}(X,\omega)

such that

\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}\leq a\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}(\omega+dd^{c}v)^{n}+bfdV_{X}.

Then
||\varphi||_{\infty}\leq C
, where
C=C(n,p,a,b,||f||_{p},||v||_{\infty})
.

The result was proved in
[
DDL21
, Theorem 3.3]
using generalized Monge-Ampère capacities. We provide below a different and simpler proof.

Proof.

Set
MA(\cdot):=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}(\omega+dd^{c}\cdot)^{n}
.
Fix
0<\delta<1
such that
\lambda=a/\delta^{n}\in(0,1)
and
consider

u=\delta v+(1-\delta)\rho-C,

where
\rho
is the function constructed above and the value of the constant
C=C(a,b,\delta,n)
is specified hereafter.
It suffices to show that
u\leq\varphi\leq 0
.

Observe that
u
is a smooth function in
PSH(X,\omega)
such that

MA(u)\geq\delta^{n}MA(v)+(1-\delta)^{n}MA(\rho).

Now
\int_{X}\tilde{f}dV_{X}\leq||\tilde{f}||_{\tilde{p}}\leq C_{1}||f||_{p}

and
v,\rho\leq 0
hence

MA(\rho)\geq\frac{1}{C_{1}||f||_{p}}e^{-\gamma\varphi}fdV_{X}\geq e^{\gamma C-\log[C_{1}||f||_{p}]}e^{-\gamma(\varphi-u)}fdV_{X}

and we eventually obtain

\displaystyle\lambda MA(u)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda\delta^{n}MA(v)+\lambda(1-\delta)^{n}e^{\gamma C-\log[C_{1}||f||_{p}]}e^{-\gamma(\varphi-u)}fdV_{X}

\displaystyle=aMA(v)+be^{-\gamma(\varphi-u)}fdV_{X},

by choosing

C=\frac{\log\left[C_{1}||f||_{p}\,a^{-1}(1-\delta)^{-n}b\delta^{n}\right]}{\gamma}.

On the set
(\varphi<u)
we thus obtain

MA(\varphi)\leq aMA(v)+bfdV_{X}\leq\lambda MA(u).

Since
\lambda<1
we infer that the set
(\varphi<u)
is empty, i.e.
u\leq\varphi\leq 0
is uniformly bounded below.
Indeed, if this set is not empty then at a point
x_{0}
realizing the minimum of
\varphi-u
we have
dd^{c}\varphi\geq dd^{c}u
hence

MA(\varphi)(x_{0})\geq MA(u)(x_{0})
. Since
u(x_{0})<\varphi(x_{0})
and
{\rm MA}(\varphi)\leq\lambda{\rm MA}(u)
in
(\varphi<u)
, we get a contradiction.
∎

Step 3

We finally use Lemma
2.2
to reduce Kołodziej’s criterion to Theorem
2.1
.
Let
w:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow[1,+\infty)
be a convex increasing weight satisfying
Condition (K)
, i.e.

w(t)\sim t(\log t)^{n}(h\circ\log\circ\log t)^{n}
with

h
increasing and
\int^{+\infty}h(t)^{-1}dt<+\infty
, such that
w\circ f\in L^{1}(X,dV_{X})
.
It is possible to find
t_{0}>>1
such that

w(t)\geq\frac{2}{3}t(\log t)^{n}(h\circ\log\circ\log t)^{n},\;\forall t\geq t_{0}.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
t_{0}=10
, and
w
is smooth.

Let
v
be a smooth function in
PSH_{0}(X,\omega)
such that

MA(v)=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}(\omega+dd^{c}v)^{n}=c\,w\circ fdV_{X},\;\text{ where }\;c^{-1}=\int_{X}w\circ fdV_{X}.

We let
\chi:\mathbb{R}^{-}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{-}
be a smooth convex increasing function such that

0\leq\chi^{\prime}\leq 1
. Thus
\chi\circ v
is a smooth function such that

\omega+dd^{c}\chi\circ v=[1-\chi^{\prime}\circ v]\,\omega+\chi^{\prime}\circ v\,[\omega+dd^{c}v]+\chi^{\prime\prime}\circ v\,dv\wedge d^{c}v>0,

i.e.
\chi\circ v\in PSH(X,\omega)
and
MA(\chi\circ v)\geq(\chi^{\prime}\circ v)^{n}MA(v)
.

Using Theorem
1.2
we can fix
\alpha>0
small enough so that

e^{-\alpha v}\in L^{p}
for
p>n
.
We also fix
B>\log 10
(to be specified below).
In
\{\log f<-\alpha v+B\}
we obtain

MA(\varphi)=fdV_{X}\leq e^{B}e^{-\alpha v}dV_{X}.

On the other hand in
\{\log f\geq-\alpha v+B\}
we obtain

MA(\chi\circ v)\geq c(\chi^{\prime}\circ v)^{n}w\circ fdV_{X}\geq c\left[\chi^{\prime}\left(\frac{-\log f+B}{\alpha}\right)\right]^{n}w\circ fdV_{X}.

We choose
\chi
so that for all
s>\log 10
,

s\,\chi^{\prime}\left(\frac{-s+B}{\alpha}\right)h\circ\log s\geq\left(\frac{2}{c}\right)^{1/n}.

It follows that

MA(\chi\circ v)\geq\frac{4}{3}fdV_{X}=\frac{4}{3}MA(\varphi)\;\text{ in }\;\{\log f\geq-\alpha v+B\}\subset\{\log f>\log 10\},

hence

MA(\varphi)\leq\frac{3}{4}MA(\chi\circ v)+e^{B}e^{-v}dV_{X}\;\;\text{ on }\;\;X.

The conclusion follows from Lemma
2.2
if we show that
\chi\circ v
is uniformly bounded.

Set

b(x)=-\chi\left(\frac{-e^{x}+B}{\alpha}\right).

The condition on
\chi^{\prime}
becomes

b^{\prime}(x)h(x)\geq c^{\prime}=\alpha\left(\frac{2}{c}\right)^{1/n}
for all
x>\log\log 10
.
We thus choose
b^{\prime}(x)=c^{\prime}h^{-1}(x)
. The integrability condition on
h
ensures that

b
, hence
\chi
, is uniformly bounded.

Observe finally that

\chi^{\prime}(0)=\frac{\alpha}{Bh(B)}
. Since
h
is increasing, we can ensure that
0\leq\chi^{\prime}(0)\leq 1

by choosing
B
large enough.
\Box

Remark 2.3
.

The proof shows that
||\chi||_{\infty}\leq C(1/c)^{1/n}=C\left(\int_{X}w\circ fdV_{X}\right)^{1/n}

for some uniform
C
, whose dependence on geometric constants is easy to follow.

2.2.
Radial examples

We assume here that the measures and quasi-psh functions under consideration are smooth
in
X\setminus\{p\}
. We choose a local chart biholomorphic to the unit ball
B
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
with
p
corresponding to the origin. We further assume that the data
are invariant under the group
U(n,\mathbb{C})
near
p
.
The singularity type then only depends on the local behavior near
p
.

We thus consider the local situation of
a psh function
v=\chi\circ L
,
where
\chi:\mathbb{R}^{-}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{-}
is a
smooth strictly convex increasing function with
\chi^{\prime}(-\infty)=0
and
L:z\in B\rightarrow\log|z|^{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{-}
.
A standard computation shows that

dd^{c}v=(\chi^{\prime}\circ L)\,dd^{c}L+(\chi^{\prime\prime}\circ L)\,dL\wedge d^{c}L

and

(dd^{c}v)^{n}=c_{n}\frac{(\chi^{\prime}\circ L)^{n-1}\chi^{\prime\prime}\circ L}{|z|^{2n}}dV=c_{n}\big[(\chi^{\prime n-1}\chi^{\prime\prime}e^{-n\cdot})\circ L\big]\,dV,

where
dV
denotes here the euclidean volume form.
These local computations can easily be globalized by using a max construction.
We let the details to the reader and focus on the asympototic behavior at
0

of our local computations.

We want to test the optimality of
Condition (K)
. We can
assume without loss of generality that
\chi
does not decrease too fast at infinity,
imposing that
\chi^{\prime}(t),\chi^{\prime\prime}(t)\geq e^{Ct}
for some
C>0
.
This guarantees that
\log f\sim-\log|z|
, so that
the measure
(dd^{c}v)^{n}=f\,dV
satisfies
Condition (K)
if and only if

\int_{-\infty}\chi^{\prime\prime}(t)(\chi^{\prime}(t))^{n-1}|t|^{n}(h\circ\log|t|)^{n}dt<+\infty.

for some increasing function
h
such that
\int^{+\infty}h(t)^{-1}dt<+\infty
.

Example 2.4
.

For
\chi(t)=-\log\circ\log\circ\log(-t+10000)
we obtain near
-\infty

\chi^{\prime}(t)\sim\frac{1}{(-t)\log(-t)\log\circ\log(-t)}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\chi^{\prime\prime}(t)\sim\frac{1}{(-t)^{2}\log(-t)\log\circ\log(-t)}

hence

\int_{-\infty}\chi^{\prime\prime}(t)(\chi^{\prime}(t))^{n-1}|t|^{n}(h\circ\log|t|)^{n}dt\sim\int^{+\infty}\frac{h(s)^{n}ds}{s^{n}(\log s)^{n}}<+\infty,

if we choose
h(s)=s^{1-1/n}
and
n\geq 2
. This provides an unbounded example s.t.

\int_{X}f(\log[f+10])^{n}(\log\circ\log[f+100])^{n-1}dV<+\infty.

On the other hand for
h(t)=t^{1+\varepsilon/n}
with
\varepsilon>0
,
Condition (K)
becomes

\int_{X}f(\log[f+10])^{n}(\log\circ\log[f+100])^{n+\varepsilon}dV<+\infty\Longrightarrow v\text{ is bounded.}

Thus there is a small gap in the previous example, which motivates the following:

Question 2.5
.

Is
Condition (K)
optimal ? More precisely can one find
0<\varepsilon<1
and some mildly unbounded psh function
v

such that
MA(v)=fdV
with

\int_{X}f(\log[f+10])^{n}(\log\circ\log[f+100])^{n-\varepsilon}dV<+\infty?

We observe that one can not improve Example
2.4
in the radial case:
assume that
p>n-1
,
v=\chi\circ L
and
(dd^{c}v)^{n}=fdV
with

\int_{-\infty}^{0}\chi^{\prime\prime}(t)(\chi^{\prime}(t))^{n-1}|t|^{n}(\log|t|)^{p}dt<+\infty.

An integration by parts shows that

\int_{-\infty}^{0}(\chi^{\prime}(t))^{n}|t|^{n-1}(\log|t|)^{p}dt\lesssim\int_{-\infty}^{0}\chi^{\prime\prime}(t)(\chi^{\prime}(t))^{n-1}|t|^{n}(\log|t|)^{p}dt<+\infty,

while it follows from Hölder inequality that

\int_{-\infty}^{0}\chi^{\prime}(t)dt\leq\left(\int_{-\infty}^{0}(\chi^{\prime}(t))^{n}|t|^{n-1}(\log|t|)^{p}dt\right)^{1/n}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{0}|t|(\log|t|)^{\frac{p}{n-1}}dt\right)^{1-1/n}<+\infty.

Thus
\chi(-\infty)>-\infty
, hence
v
is bounded whenever
p>n-1
.

3.
Other nonlinear equations

The technique introduced by Guo-Phong-Tong in

[
GPT23
]

applies to a large family of equations. We show here that most of them
can be treated by a simple reduction to the Monge-Ampère equation, following
an idea from
[
GL25b
]
.

3.1.
Quasi-subharmonic functions

3.1.1.
\omega
-subharmonic functions

Fix
U\subset X
an open set.
A function
h\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2}(U,\mathbb{R})
is called harmonic with respect to
\omega
if

dd^{c}h\wedge\omega^{n-1}=0\;\text{pointwise in }\;U.

A function
u:U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is subharmonic with respect to
\omega

if it is upper semicontinuous and for every harmonic function
h
in
D\subset U
, one has

u\leq h\;\text{on}\;\partial D\Longrightarrow u\leq h\;\text{in}\;D.

When
u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2}(U)
, it is subharmonic with respect to
\omega
iff
dd^{c}u\wedge\omega^{n-1}\geq 0
pointwise.
If
u
is subharmonic with respect to
\omega
and in
L^{1}
then
dd^{c}u\wedge\omega^{n-1}\geq 0
in the sense of distributions.
Conversely the latter condition implies that
u
coincides
a.e. with a unique function
\hat{u}
subharmonic with respect to
\omega
.

Definition 3.1
.

A function
\varphi:U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is quasi-subharmonic with respect to
\omega
if locally

\varphi=u+\rho
where
u
is subharmonic with respect to
\omega
and
\rho
is smooth.
We say that
\varphi
is
\omega
-subharmonic if it is quasi-subharmonic with respect to
\omega

and
(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)\wedge\omega^{n-1}\geq 0
in the sense of distributions on
X
.

We let
SH(X,\omega)
denote the set of integrable
\omega
-subharmonic functions in
X
.

We will need the following classical facts:

•

if
u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
then
u\in SH(X,\omega)
if and only if
(\omega+dd^{c}u)\wedge\omega^{n-1}\geq 0
pointwise;

•

the subset
SH_{0}(X,\omega)=\{u\in SH(X,\omega),\;\sup_{X}u=0\}
is compact in
L^{1}
.

We refer the reader to
[
GZ17
]
for further properties of
\omega
-subharmonic functions:

3.1.2.
The set
\Gamma(X,\omega)

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote an open symmetric convex cone with vertex at the origin such that

\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\subset\Gamma\subset\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}>0\}.

In this section we fix
\delta>0
and a positive function
g:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
such that

(1)

g
is symmetric (invariance under permutations);

(2)

\frac{\partial g}{\partial\lambda_{j}}(\lambda)>0
for all
\lambda\in\Gamma
(ellipticity condition);

(3)

g(\lambda)\geq\delta\left(\pi_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\right)^{1/n}
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
(determinantal majorization).

Definition 3.2
.

A smooth function
\varphi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
belong to
{\Gamma}(X,\omega)
if
the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}

of
\omega+dd^{c}\varphi
with respect to
\omega
belong to
\Gamma
at all points
x\in X
.

Observe that
PSH(X,\omega)\subset\Gamma(X,\omega)\subset SH(X,\omega)
,
with
\Gamma(X,\omega)=SH(X,\omega)
when
\Gamma=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}>0\}
,
while
PSH(X,\omega)\subset\Gamma(X,\omega)
when
\Gamma=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
.

3.1.3.
Determinantal majorization

We consider the nonlinear PDE

(NL)

g(\lambda(\varphi))=cf^{1/n},

where

•

g
satisfies the conditions
(1)
-
(2)
-
(3)
above;

•

f
is a fixed smooth positive density;

•

c>0
is a normalizing constant determined by the equation;

•

\varphi
is the unknown, a smooth function in
{\Gamma}(X,\omega)
.

The assumptions made on
g
are slightly less general than the ones considered in
[
Szek18
,
GPT23
,
GP24
]
,
but they nevertheless cover a huge family of complex geometric PDE’s as observed by
[
HL23
]

(see also
[
ADO22
,
CX24
]
).

3.2.
Proof of Theorem B

Theorem 3.3
.

Fix
w
a weight that satisfies
Condition (K)
.
If
\varphi
is a smooth solution to (
NL
) then

{\rm Osc}_{X}(\varphi)\leq M_{0},

where
M_{0}
depends on an upper bound on the Luxembourg norm
||f||_{{w}}
.

The statement is a generalization of the main result of
[
GPT23
]
as far as the condition on
f
is concerned
(see also
[
Q24
]
). The estimates turn out to be also independent of the complex structure
(see Remarks
3.4
and
3.5
).
As with Theorem A, the proof of this result is somehow more interesting than the statement itself.

Proof.

We can normalize
\varphi
so that
\sup_{X}\varphi=0
, and the problem boils down to show that

\varphi
is uniformly bounded below. Consider

\psi=P_{\omega}(\varphi)=\sup\{u\in PSH(X,\omega),\;u\leq\varphi\},

the
\omega
-psh envelope of
\varphi
. It suffices to show that
\psi
is uniformly bounded below.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}=\{\psi=\varphi\}
denote the contact set.
The function
\psi
is
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,1}
-smooth and
its Monge-Ampère measure
MA(\psi)
is concentrated on
{\mathcal{C}}
.
It follows therefore from Theorem
1.4
and
the determinantal majorization that

\delta^{n}MA(\psi)=\delta^{n}1_{{\mathcal{C}}}MA(\varphi)\leq 1_{{\mathcal{C}}}g(\lambda(\varphi))^{n}\omega^{n}\leq c^{n}f\omega^{n},

since
\omega+dd^{c}\varphi\geq 0
on the contact set.

We can now invoke Theorem A to conclude that

{\rm Osc}_{X}(\psi)\leq M_{0},

where
M_{0}
depends on an upper-bound on the Luxembourg norm

||\frac{c^{n}}{\delta^{n}}f||_{w}=\frac{c^{n}}{\delta^{n}}||f||_{w}.

It remains to obtain a uniform control on
\sup_{X}\psi
.
Let
w^{*}
denote the Legendre transform of the convex weight
w
, and let

h=(w^{*})^{-1}
denote the inverse of the bijective function
w^{*}
.
It follows from Hölder-Young inequality that

0\leq h(-\sup_{X}\psi)\leq\int_{X}h(-\psi)MA(\psi)\leq\frac{c^{n}}{\delta^{n}}\int_{X}h(-\varphi)f\omega^{n}\leq\frac{c^{n}}{\delta^{n}}||f||_{w}\int_{X}(-\varphi)\omega^{n}.

The conclusion follows from compactness in
L^{1}
of functions in
\Gamma(X,\omega)
that
are normalized by
\sup_{X}\varphi=0
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

There is a vast literature on uniform estimates for complex Monge-Ampère equations.

[
DnGG23
]
provides estimates that
remain uniform as the cohomology class and the complex structure vary. The proof above thus
provides uniform estimates for holomorphic families of solutions to (
NL
) as well.

Remark 3.5
.

The proof of Theorem B is exactly the same in the hermitian and in the Kähler setting. The estimates are therefore
also independent of the complex structure, as follows from
[
Pan23
]
,
and provide a generalization of those from
[
GP24
]
for those equations that satisfy a determinantal majorization.

3.3.
Alternative proof

In the previous proof of Theorem B we actually need to apply Theorem A to
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,1}
-smooth functions, rather than

{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}
-smooth ones.
One can reduce to the smooth case by approximation, but we now propose an alternative proof that
allows one to work with
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}
-smooth objects.

We again normalize
\varphi
so that
\sup_{X}\varphi=0
,
let
w^{*}
denote the Legendre transform of the convex weight
w
, and let

h=(w^{*})^{-1}
denote the inverse of the bijective function
w^{*}
.
Hölder-Young inequality ensures that

\int_{X}h(-\varphi)f\omega^{n}\leq||f||_{w}\int_{X}(-\varphi)\omega^{n}\leq B

is uniformly bounded.
We fix positive constants
\lambda,M
so large that

\frac{2}{1+\lambda}<\frac{\delta^{n}}{3^{n}c^{n}}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\frac{2B}{h(M)}\leq\min\left(1,\frac{\delta^{n}}{3^{n}c^{n}}\right)\;\;\text{ with }\;\;\frac{2^{n}}{3^{n}}\leq\frac{V_{\omega}}{1+\lambda e^{-M^{2}}}.

Let
\psi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\omega)
be the smooth solution to the Monge-Ampère equation

(\omega+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}=b_{M}\beta_{M}(\varphi)f\omega^{n},

where
\sup_{X}\psi=0
,

b_{M}
is a positive normalization constant
and

\beta_{M}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{1+\lambda e^{M(\varphi+M)}}

is a smooth function taking values in
(0,1]
.

The constant
b_{M}
can be estimated as follows.
Observe first that

\displaystyle\int_{X}\beta_{M}(\varphi)f\omega^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{\{\varphi\leq-M\}}f\omega^{n}+\int_{\{\varphi>-M\}}\frac{1}{1+\lambda}f\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{h(M)}\int_{X}h(-\varphi)f\omega^{n}+\frac{1}{1+\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{B}{h(M)}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta^{n}}{3^{n}c^{n}}\leq\frac{\delta^{n}}{3^{n}c^{n}},

hence
b_{M}\geq V_{\omega}3^{n}c^{n}\delta^{-n}
.
On the other hand

\displaystyle\int_{X}\beta_{M}(\varphi)f\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\{\varphi>-M\}}\frac{1}{1+\lambda e^{M^{2}}}f\omega^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\lambda e^{M^{2}}}\left(1-\int_{\{\varphi\leq-M\}}f\omega^{n}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{1+\lambda e^{M^{2}}}\left(1-\frac{1}{h(M)}\int_{X}h(-\varphi)f\omega^{n}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2(1+\lambda e^{M^{2}})}

yields a uniform upper bound for
b_{M}
.
It follows therefore from Theorem A that
\psi
is uniformly bounded.

On the set
(\varphi<\psi-2M)
we have
\varphi\leq-2M
, hence

\beta_{M}(\varphi)\geq\frac{1}{1+\lambda e^{-M^{2}}}
and

\displaystyle g(\lambda(\psi))^{n}\omega^{n}\geq\delta^{n}(\omega+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta^{n}b_{M}\beta_{M}(\varphi)f\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle V_{\omega}3^{n}c^{n}\frac{1}{1+\lambda e^{-M^{2}}}f\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2^{n}c^{n}f\omega^{n}=2^{n}g(\lambda(\varphi))^{n}\omega^{n}.

Therefore
2g(\lambda(\varphi))\leq g(\lambda(\psi))
on the set
(\varphi<\psi-2M)

and it follows from the domination principle below that this set is empty, i.e.

\varphi\geq\psi-2M
is uniformly bounded.
\Box

Remark 3.6
.

This proof can be applied to the Dirichlet problem for such equations
in strongly pseudoconvex domains, in which case the regularity of plurisubharmonic envelopes is
more delicate. We thank A.Zeriahi for pointing this out.

We have used the following domination principle which is a direct consequence of the classical minimum principle.

Proposition 3.7

(Domination principle)
.

Assume
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
satisfy

g(\lambda(\varphi))\leq cg(\lambda(\psi))\;\text{on}\;(\varphi<\psi),

for some constant
c\in[0,1)
. Then
\varphi\geq\psi
.

Proof.

Since
X
is compact and
\varphi-\psi
is continuous, the minimum is attained at some
x_{0}\in X
. By the maximum principle, we have
\lambda(\varphi)\geq\lambda(\psi)
at
x_{0}
. The ellipticity condition on
g
thus yields
g(\lambda(\varphi))\geq g(\lambda(\psi))
. It thus follows from the assumption that
x_{0}\notin(\varphi-\psi<0)
, hence
\varphi\geq\psi
.
∎

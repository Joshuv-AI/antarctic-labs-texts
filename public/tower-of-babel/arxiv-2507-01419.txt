The Crossed Product, Modular (Tomita) Dynamics and its Role in the Transition of Type I ​ I ​ I III to Type I ​ I ∞ II_{\infty} v.Neumann Algebras and Connections to Quantum Gravity


Abstract
We analyse the role of the crossed product and the modular (Tomita) dynamics in the transition of type I ​ I ​ I III to type I ​ I ∞ II_{\infty} v.Neumann algebras which was recently observed in papers by Witten et al. In a preceding paper we argued that type I ​ I ∞ II_{\infty} v.Neumann algebras display certain features which we attributed to quantum gravity effects. We claim that the action of the modular evolution on the quantum fluctuations can be understood as an aspect of quantum gravity. We mention in this context the work of Sakharov on induced gravity. Furthermore we analyse the change of the properties of projectors and partial isometries in the transition from type I ​ I ​ I III to type I ​ I ∞ II_{\infty} .

3
The Physical Content of the Crossed Product Construction

In this section we want to clarify which physical role the various constituents in the crossed product construction are playing, most notably the modular automorphism group,
\sigma_{s}
, implemented by the modular operator

and the properties of the projectors in a type
III
v. Neumann algebra. As in
[

we think that there does exist a deep preastablished correspondence between the extremely rich mathematics in the field of v. Neumann algebras and the corresponding concepts of quantum physics. We think, in particular, that the analysis may yield a first step away from the traditional semiclassical picture of QFT in classical space-time into QG proper.

3.1
The Role of the Modular Group

Furthermore, if one believes in the above mentioned preastablished harmony between physics and mathematics in this context, one would like to better understand its role in a more fundamental sense, as e.g. in QG. Such a better understanding is, for example, attempted in

Remark 3.1

In

In this context we want to mention the point of view of J.A.Wheeler, expressed in his talk in
[
39

Second, we accept as a tentative working hypothesis the picture of Clifford and Einstein that particles originate from geometry; that there is no such thing as a particle, immersed in geometry, but only a particle built out of geometry.

In a general covariant theory there is no preferred time flow and the dynamics of the theory cannot be formulated in terms of an evolution in a single external parameter. It is the main message of

\mathcal{O}
, or in a more fundamental layer, QG or QFT interacting with QST in a certain region

Local observable algebras in QFT are typical of type
III
. Let
\Omega
be a cyclic and separating vector, coming from a faithful normal state

S:A\Omega\to A^{*}\Omega\quad,\quad A\in\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{O})

(30)

the algebra of local observables. The polar decomposition yields

(31)

\sigma_{s}

\sigma_{s}(A)=\Delta^{is}A\Delta^{-is}

\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{O})
invariant, the group
\sigma_{s}
being called the modular automorphism group. With

is called the Tomita or modular Hamiltonian.

Remark 3.2

Note that in general

does not have the structure of an ordinary particle Hamiltonian, therefore its thermodynamic properties are not entirely obvious. A typical example are the Lorentz boosts in the Rindler wedges, occurring as modular evolution, which certainly do not have ordinary particle character.

The modular group is called inner if there exists a unitary group

with
U(s)\in\mathcal{M}
(a v. Neumann algebra) and

\sigma_{s}(A)=U(s)AU^{*}(s)

\sigma^{2}_{s}(A)=U(s)\sigma^{1}_{s}(A)U^{*}(s)

\mathcal{M}

\mathcal{M}

The co-cycle Radon-Nikodym theorem of Connes (

Corollary 3.6

It follows that the Tomita action on a type

In this context we now want to formulate a conjecture how the thermal time concept of Connes-Rovelli may be related to an important consequence of GR, i.e., the dependence of the rate of an ideal clock in a gravitational field. With

ds^{2}=g_{ik}(x)dx^{i}dx^{k}

We now formulate the following conjecture:

(Sakharov) General Relativity arises as an emergent property of the vacuum fluctuations of matter fields.

Assumption 3.9

The local spectrum of quantum fluctuations represents, a fortiori, the gravitational field or the local microscopic state of space-time (or QST). This picture then neatly corresponds with the ideas of Sakharov.

3.2
The Role of the Class of admissible Projectors and Procedures in the Transition from Type
III
to Type
II_{\infty}

In the following we want to discuss the subtle role the class of admissible projectors and procedures are playing in the transition of our local v. Neumann factors from type
III
to
II_{\infty}
. We begin with a simple observation:

Remark 3.21

Given two closed subspaces
P\mathcal{H},Q\mathcal{H}
, both with countably infinite dimension and with
ON
-bases
\{e_{i}\},\{f_{i}\}
, then it is easy to construct a partial isometry
V
by defining

V:\;e_{i}\to f_{i}\;\text{and linearly extended with}\;V(\mathbf{1}-P)\mathcal{H}:=0

(50)

That is, the crucial point is rather, under what conditions
V
does belong to
\mathcal{M}
. We hence have to clarify the particular properties the admissible procedures, incorporated by
V
, have to fulfill.

To approach this question we formulate the following conjecture:

Conjecture 3.22

Following our working philosophy of a parallelism of mathematics and physics in this field of the role of v. Neumann algebras, we assume that all operators, belonging to
\mathcal{M}
(a local v. Neumann factor), have a physical meaning and play a physical role in the quantum regime. This is obvious concerning the class of projectors, but we think, it holds as well concerning the class of partial isometries.

Without going into the quantitative details we associate the notion of
microscopic information
to a a state in
\mathcal{H}
. If, for example,
\psi
does not lie in
P\mathcal{H}
, we say that
P
applied to
\psi
cuts off some or reduces the information content of the state in the process. We call such a process irreversible. Note that in general a measurement is accompanied by a macroscopic irreversible (avalanche) process.

Definition 3.23

On the other hand, the partial isometry
V\in\mathcal{M}
with
P\mathcal{H}
the initial space and
Q\mathcal{H}
the final space (
VV^{*}=Q,V^{*}V=P
)

V:P\psi\to Q\psi

(51)

is said to deform or redistribute the microscopic information but does conserve it. With
V^{*}
being the inverse of
V
on
Q\mathcal{H}
:

V^{*}:Q\psi\to P\psi\;,\;V^{*}VP\psi=P\psi

(52)

we call the process
V,V^{*}
reversible between
P\mathcal{H},Q\mathcal{H}
.

Interesting in such a context is the type
III
case. In this situation we have that all projectors turn out to be equivalent.

P\sim Q\;,\;\forall\,P,Q\in\mathcal{M}\;\text{and in particular}\;P\sim\mathbf{1}

(53)

i.e., using our above notion:

Observation 3.24

In the type
III
case all partial isometries in
\mathcal{M}
preserve the microscopic information.

More specifically we have

Proposition 3.25

If
\mathbf{1}\sim P
with
V:\mathcal{H}\to P\mathcal{H}
we have that
\mathcal{M}
and
V\mathcal{M}V^{*}
are
\star
-isomorphic and, a fortiori, unitarily equivalent with Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H},P\mathcal{H}
. By the same token, spectral measures of
A\in\mathcal{M}\,,\,VAV^{*}\in V\mathcal{M}V^{*}
are unitarily equivalent and their spectra are the same, i.e.

E_{A}(S)\to VE_{A}(S)V^{*}

(54)

Proof: We have for example

(V\psi|VAV^{*}V\psi)=(V^{*}V\psi|AV^{*}V\psi)=(\psi|A\psi)

(55)

as
V^{*}V=\mathbf{1}
. Furthermore, we can compare
P\mathcal{M}P
with
V\mathcal{M}V^{*}
. We have

P\mathcal{M}P=VV^{*}\mathcal{M}VV^{*}=V(V^{*}\mathcal{M}V)V^{*}\subset V\mathcal{M}V^{*}

(56)

But with the choice
VAV^{*}\,,\,A\in\mathcal{M}
on the rhs we get

V^{*}VAV^{*}V=A

(57)

i.e., the map
VAV^{*}
is one to one.

Corollary 3.26

We have
P\mathcal{M}P=V\mathcal{M}V^{*}
.

Remark 3.27

Corresponding results hold for the case
P\sim Q
and
V:P\mathcal{H}\to Q\mathcal{H}

Some remarks on the type
III
situation can, for example, also be found in
[
50
]

To give a more intuitive picture of the above ideas, we mention e.g. the Stern Gerlach experiment. An ingoing up- and down superposition of z-spin passes through an inhomogeneous magnetic field. The ingoing state,
\psi
, is split (or rather, separated) into two components

\psi\to\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}

(58)

but it is still a superposition, not a mixture. One may say, it is at this point a
premeasurement
. Note that, in principle, one can reunite the superposition
\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}
to
\psi
by using an appropriate magnetic field. One may now associate the process

\psi\to\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}\;\text{to}\;V:\psi\to\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}

(59)

and the reverse process

\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}\to\psi\;\text{to}\;V^{*}:\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}\to\psi

(60)

Conclusion 3.28

We think, in the type
III
case the partial isometries
V,V^{*}
are working in just this sense as illustrated in the above example.

This is no longer the case in the type
II_{\infty}
situation. In that case there exist subspaces,
P\mathcal{H}
, which cannot reversibly be mapped by a partial isometry,
V^{*}
, isometrically onto
\mathcal{H}
. That is, in this case microscopic information in the sense we defined it above, is lost.

We now want to study the transition, implemented by the crossed product construction, from type
III
to type
II_{\infty}
. For us, the interesting case is the modular automorphism group,
\sigma_{s}
, and its implementation by
\Delta^{is}=\exp isH_{T}

\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M},\sigma_{s})

\{A\otimes\mathbf{1},\Delta^{is}\otimes l_{s}\}
with
A\in\mathcal{M},l_{s}=\exp-is\hat{p}
,
\hat{p}
the momentum operator on
L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

In a first step we have to study which kind of projection operators do occur in
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M},\sigma_{s})
compared to
\mathcal{M}
. The projectors,
\{P\}
, lying in
\mathcal{M}
, go over into
\{P\otimes\mathbf{1}\}
. As
\exp isH_{T}\otimes\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}\otimes\exp-is\hat{p}
do commute, we have, furthermore, projectors of the kind
E_{\Delta}\otimes e
,
E_{\Delta}
a spectral projector of
H_{T}
,
e
of
\hat{p}

Conclusion 3.29

The projectors of
\mathcal{M}
and of
H_{T}\otimes\hat{p}
go over, more or less unchanged into the corresponding projectors of
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M},\sigma_{s})
.

Much more interesting are the projectors which arise by a nontrivial combination of members
A_{j}\otimes\mathbf{1}
with
A_{j}\in\mathcal{M}
and
\Delta^{is_{j}}\otimes l_{s_{j}}
. We remind the reader of the particular product rules, proved at the end of section 2, which show that the members of

\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M},\sigma_{s})
consist of sums of form invariant terms
A_{j}\cdot\Delta^{i}{s_{j}}\otimes l_{s_{j}}

\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M},\sigma_{s})
by forming sums,
(\hat{B}+\hat{B}^{*})
with
\hat{B}\in\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M},\sigma_{s})

A_{j}\otimes\mathbf{1}
and
\Delta^{is_{j}}\otimes l_{s_{j}}

We begin with the class of finite polynomials

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\,c_{j}\hat{B}^{j}\;,\,\hat{B}\;\text{a s.a. element of}\;\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M},\sigma_{s})

(61)

As
\hat{B}
is bounded, its spectrum,
Spec(\hat{B})
, lies in a finite closed interval
I=[-||\hat{B}||,||\hat{B}||]\subset\mathbb{R}
. The Stone-Weierstrass theorem says that the set of finite polynomials is dense in the set of continuous functions

f(x)\in\mathcal{C}(I)\;\text{with norm}\;f_{\infty}(x)=\sup f(x)

(62)

With
dE(\lambda)
the spectral meaasure of
\hat{B}
we have hence

Lemma 3.30

The finite polynomials,
P(\hat{B})
, are dense in the set
f(\hat{B})=\int\,f(\lambda)dE_{\lambda}
,
f
continuous in
I
, in the norm
||A||
.

Projectors are given by characteristic functions,
I_{\Delta}
,
\Delta
an interval covering part of the spectrum of
\hat{B}
.

Employing the measure class given by
dE_{\lambda}
, we can use the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem to approximate
I_{\Delta}
by a sequence of functions from
\mathcal{C}(I)
.

Conclusion 3.31

We see that spectral projectors of
\hat{B}
can be approximated by (infinite) sequences of polynomials
P(\hat{B})

A_{j}\otimes\mathbf{1}
and
\Delta^{is_{j}}\otimes l_{s_{j}}
. It follows that in the construction of such projectors, elements of
\mathcal{M}
and the modular group,
\Delta^{is}
are typically combined in a very complicated way.

3.3
Conclusion

It is our aim to understand to what extent the properties of projectors are affected by the coupling of
\mathcal{M}
with the Tomita evolution,
\exp isH_{T}
, leading to a larger algebra of observables,
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M},\sigma_{s})
. We described the physical content of partial isometries ,
V:P\mathcal{H}\to Q\mathcal{H}
, by saying that this process preserves the total microscopic information content but redistributes it. As an example of what we have in mind we provided the Stern-Gerlach experiment.

We said that, while
\sigma_{s}
acts on
\mathcal{M}
, for example the spectral projections of
H_{T}
are in general not observable if
\sigma_{s}
is an outer automorphism. We argued that this changes if
\mathcal{M}
and
\exp isH_{T}
are really coupled as in
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M},\sigma_{s})
.

We argued above that
\exp isH_{T}
acts on the quantum fluctuation spectrum and, a fortiori, may even be related to quantum gravity effects. Following our working philosophy that the class of projectors and the admissible partial isometries encode the various degrees of microscopic information, contained in them, we make the following claim:

Conjecture 3.32

While we will not prove it mathematically, we argue on physical grounds (we have explained above) that the spectral projectors, belonging to typical observables
\hat{B}
of
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M},\sigma_{s})
and which are formed via a complicated coupling of elements of
\mathcal{M}
and
\exp isH_{T}
are not related to projectors lying in, for example,
\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathbf{1}
, or the identity projector
\mathbf{1}
via partial isometries as their information content are different. Hence
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M},\sigma_{s})
is no longer of type
III
.

Remark 3.33

This holds the more so as quantum gravity effects may play a role in this context as we have argued above in connection with the ideas of Sakharov. We learned from our previous discussions that the fluctuation spectrum is strongly affected by gravity.

Policy text analysis of high-quality development of integrated elderly care and medical services in China based on PMC index model
Authors: Wenjia Li, Yuhan Zhu, Yazhuo Gao, Fangfang Zhong, Xuehua Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study aims to conduct a content analysis of policies pertaining to the high-quality development of Integrated Elderly Care and Medical Services (IECMS) introduced in China from January 2016 to December 2024. Utilizing the Policy Modeling Consistency (PMC) index model, we seek to achieve a quantitative assessment of these relevant policies.MethodsUtilizing the indicator design method of PMC index model, we constructed first-level indicators for policy evaluation. Second-level indicators were developed with reference to the structure of the IECMS high-quality development support system as outlined by the World Health Organization, the State Council, and the National Health Commission, following a comprehensive literature review. Ultimately, an evaluation system for assessing the high-quality development of IECMS was established, comprising 9 first-level indicators and 44 s-level indicators. The study text-mined 12 policy documents that met predefined inclusion criteria and employed the PMC index model for quantitative evaluation.ResultsThe mean PMC score across these 12 policies was found to be 6.76, indicating that the overall policy system is generally robust; specifically, it includes 8 policies rated as excellent and 4 as good. Notably, areas such as policy concern (X3), party leadership (X8), policy recipients (X2), and carer care (X7) demonstrated comprehensive strengths. However, further improvements are necessary in aspects related to care systems (X6), policy nature (X1), policy measures (X4), and care capacity (X5).ConclusionTo ensure a high-quality supply of elderly care services amidst ongoing developments towards quality enhancement in China’s IECMS-related policies must embrace systemic thinking alongside principles of synergy, innovation, and sharing. It is essential to adjust and optimize supply structures while stimulating effective demand; balancing supply-demand elements; focusing on talent cultivation; and innovating within industry environments. These efforts will enable us to meet evolving public expectations for improved living standards in this new era while continuously enriching our understanding of what constitutes high-quality development in IECMS services.

Background

In the 21 st century, the global aging process has accelerated, profoundly impacting social structures and economic development worldwide [1]. According to the World Health Organization’s 2022 co-publication data, the global population aged 60 and over is expected to reach 1.4 billion by 2030 and increase to 2.1 billion by 2050 [2]. According to data from the National Bureau of Statistics of China, by the end of 2024, the population aged 60 and above in China is projected to exceed 300 million for the first time. This demographic group will account for 22.0% of the total population, indicating a deepening degree of aging [3]. As the world’s most populous country, China faces increasingly serious challenges from aging. Data predict that by 2035, the proportion of middle-aged elderly in China will rise from 28.62% to 31.96% [4, 5], while the number of disabled elderly is also increasing. In 2010, China’s disabled elderly population exceeded 33 million, with projections reaching 61.68 million by 2030 [6].

Japan entered an aging society in the last century. In response to the challenges posed by this demographic shift, the country has implemented a range of policies aimed at mitigating its impacts. These include enhancing social security for the elderly, establishing professional elderly care service teams, and ensuring diverse job opportunities for older adults. The systems designed to strengthen medical and health services as well as welfare services mentioned herein share commonalities with current elderly care measures adopted by the Chinese government [7]. In addition, certain countries in the United States and Europe—such as the PACE Program (Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly) [8] in the United States and Germany’s comprehensive care model [9]—often emphasize community-oriented long-term care services. These models focus on providing comprehensive support across multiple dimensions, including personal life assistance, medical care, and mental health services. However, it is noteworthy that these countries typically possess relatively complete welfare systems alongside a smaller proportion of elderly individuals. In contrast, rural areas in China present a different scenario; approximately 17.72% of their total population is aged 65 or above—a figure that exceeds urban areas by 6.61%. This indicates that aging levels among rural populations are significantly higher than those found in urban settings [10]. Nevertheless, economic development in rural regions lags behind that of cities; residents often experience low income levels coupled with basic elderly care service facilities. Conversely, urban areas exhibit higher consumption levels along with more developed eldercare infrastructure. Consequently, there exists a pronounced disparity between urban and rural regions regarding eldercare resources.

In conclusion, given China’s distinctive social conditions and economic context, it is imperative to explore a suitable elderly care service model tailored to its needs. This approach aims to ensure quality while addressing the significant challenge of resource allocation. Since the 18th National Congress of the Communist Party of China, the Chinese government has placed considerable emphasis on IECMS (Integrated Elderly Care Management System) elderly care services and has introduced a series of policies designed to promote IECMS and guarantee an effective supply of elderly care services [11]. The State Council’s Opinions on Developing the Silver Economy and Enhancing Elderly Well-being outline the goal of constructing a high-quality senior care service system combining medical care, recreation, and nursing care to enhance elderly well-being [12]. This policy not only reflects the state’s concern for elderly welfare but also demonstrates China’s determination to modernize its elderly care system.

IECMS quality development aims to provide high-quality and efficient integrated medical and elderly care services for the elderly, guided by their needs and based on traditional elderly care services. It seeks to promote the integration of modern medical care and elderly care services in the new era of elderly care. Researchers have explored various aspects of IECMS quality development, including defining the concept [13], constructing evaluation index systems [14], and assessing service supply demands [15]. Zhang [16] argues that the quality of senior care services depends on the degree of satisfaction with these services and the level of supply needed by the elderly. Liu [17] highlights the imbalance and insufficiency of medical care services in China, advocating for multi-party cooperation to optimize resource allocation and further promote the integration of modern medical care and senior care services. Despite progress, China’s IECMS policy implementation still encounters bottlenecks such as fragmented healthcare and elderly care resources and geographical differences in policy execution. Current research often focuses on single aspects and lacks quantitative evaluations of related policies.

To address this gap, this study will adopt the Policy Modeling Consistency (PMC) index model [18], which quantitatively evaluates China’s IECMS policies introduced in 2016 across multiple dimensions, including policy measures and nature. This period not only signified the official initiation of China’s policy aimed at integrating medical care with elderly care, but it also represented a critical phase for the swift advancement of related policies. Policy evaluation is a critical component in assessing the effectiveness of policy implementation, serving to test and judge the efficacy of these policies [19]. The PMC index model can quantitatively analyze the internal consistency of policies, identify the strengths and weaknesses of specific policies, and evaluate the overall quality to determine the soundness and scientific rigor of current policies, thereby providing a basis for subsequent policy formulation. Through such quantitative analyses, we can better understand China’s policy strengths and challenges in the field of IECMS and offer valuable insights and perspectives for addressing the global aging issue.

Information and methodology

Sources of information

The purpose of this study is to conduct an in-depth textual analysis of China’s IECMS policy, aiming to provide policy support and recommendations for the construction and improvement of China’s IECMS service system. To achieve this, we used keywords such as “IECMS, medical care, healthy aging, elderly care, chronic disease care, home care, long-term care” to search the official websites of the Chinese government, the National Health Commission, the Ministry of Civil Affairs, and other relevant government agencies. The search used the criterion “full-text match” in the policy and regulatory documents section. Additionally, supplementary searches were performed through databases like the China LawInfo (www.pkulaw.com) Information Retrieval System and China Knowledge Network, using subject terms such as “IECMS, medical care for the elderly, healthy care for the elderly” and title terms including “notification, law, regulation, report, opinion, outline, decision, standard, plan” for full-text searches.

In 2016, the General Office of the National Health and Family Planning Commission and the General Office of the Ministry of Civil Affairs identified the first batch of national-level IECMS pilot units. The 13th Five-Year Plan period (2016–2020) and the 14th Five-Year Plan period (2021–2025) are critical stages in the development of China’s IECMS policy. During these periods, the Chinese government introduced a series of policies and measures to promote the deep integration of healthcare services and elderly care services, addressing the health and elderly care needs of the elderly population. Therefore, the time frame for this study’s search was set from January 2016 to June 2024 to ensure comprehensive coverage of all relevant policy documents.

Text mining

In this study, ROSTCM6.0 text mining software was utilized to analyze policy documents related to the high-quality development of IECMS in China. The twelve policy documents selected from the final screening were imported into ROSTCM6.0 for data preprocessing. After organizing the text data, high-frequency words were extracted from the policy texts. The Net Draw tool was then used to construct a word co-occurrence matrix, generating a social network map of the high-frequency words. The correlations between these high-frequency words, as depicted in the map, can help identify important themes and key areas within the policy documents, thereby providing a foundation for constructing the primary and secondary indicators of the PMC index model.

PMC index modelling and calculation methodology

The PMC index model, initially proposed by Rui Estrada, serves as a quantitative instrument for evaluating the consistency and effectiveness of policies [20]. This model assists policymakers in identifying and addressing potential issues within policies by analyzing the consistency across multiple dimensions. It provides a quantitative basis for policy optimization and supports policymakers in making adjustments and improvements during the design and implementation process. The calculation of the PMC index involves three main steps: assigning values to secondary indicators, calculating scores for primary indicators, and computing the final PMC index. The range is [0, 1], with a value of 1 assigned if the policy content involves a specific secondary indicator and 0 otherwise. Quality control

In the process of policy selection, text mining, and the construction and calculation of the PMC model in this study, the two researchers utilized articles related to medical and elderly care integration policies as units for content analysis. They independently extracted information from the screened policy texts. Prior to commencing the research, both researchers underwent training to ensure consistency and stability in their results. In instances where discrepancies arose between them during the research process, they engaged in discussions and consulted a third researcher to reach a consensus. Cross-checks were performed on the consistency and reliability of the research process to guarantee both accuracy and validity in the analysis.

Therefore, based on the policy content of IECMS and the revisions made to the PMC index model by relevant scholars [20], this study analyzed high-frequency vocabulary results derived from policy texts and reviewed authoritative literature, pertinent national policy documents, as well as the WHO framework [21]. Following extensive discussions and screenings, we comprehensively considered its multidimensional development needs. PMC indices for IECMS policy documents

Specific policy evaluations

Policy P3, titled “Circular on the Issuance of Service Guidelines for IECMS Organisations (for Trial Implementation),” was jointly issued by the General Office of the National Health Commission (NHC), the General Office of the Ministry of Civil Affairs (MCA), and the Office of the State Administration of Traditional Chinese Medicine (SATCM) in December 2019. During this period, IECMS, as a relatively new service delivery model, was still in its experimental and exploratory stage. Consequently, earlier policies may not have fully incorporated the most up-to-date theoretical research findings and practical experience. This is reflected in the lower overall PMC index scores for P3, with specific areas requiring improvement including policy receptors (X2), policy measures (X4), care system (X6), carer care (X7), and party leadership (X8).

P7 (Circular on the Issuance of the 14th Five-Year Plan for the Development of the National Elderly Career Programme and Elderly Service System) was issued by the State Council in December 2021. As an integral part of the 14th Five-Year Plan, it plays a crucial role in promoting the construction of China’s elderly service system. The policy covers important subjects involved in the high-quality development of IECMS, focusing on various fields such as politics, economy, culture, and technology. Consequently, it scores high on the overall index but still requires further supplementation and improvement in terms of policy nature (X1) and the care system (X6).

Analyzing the radar chart (Fig. 2), which compares the highest-scoring P7 with the lowest-scoring P3 and the mean of the included policies, it is evident that the text of the IECMS high-quality development policy places particular emphasis on caring capacity (X5). This reflects the government’s focus on improving the quality of care services, specialized capabilities, and healthcare technological innovations. On the other hand, the care system (X6) is relatively underdeveloped. This indicates that existing safeguards in areas such as health insurance payment methods, prices of care services, and the construction of medical communities still need to be strengthened. The policy should pay more attention to the construction of the care system and the allocation of resources during its implementation, especially in building a systematic and long-term mechanism, which should be further reinforced.

Evaluation of the policy architecture

The higher-scoring level indicators are Policy Concern (X3) and Party Leadership (X8). Most policies scored significantly higher or slightly lower than the average for the policy concern (X3) dimension, with P5 (Circular on the Guidelines for the Management of IECMS Organisations (Trial)) scoring 0.6, the lowest among the included policies. Analyzing the secondary indicators reveals that attention to the cultural and technical areas of IECMS should be strengthened in the future. The average score for the Party leadership (X8) dimension was 0.81, with eight policies achieving full marks in this dimension. This indicates that China’s IECMS services operate under strong Party leadership, with pilot units serving as platforms to establish working leading groups and develop coordination mechanisms involving the Party committee and government, health sector leadership, and inter-departmental linkages. This plays a crucial role in improving policy measures and structuring a rational policy system.

The overall scores for policy receptors (X2), carer care (X7), and policy evaluation (X9) are all 0.78, indicating that while these areas have been considered to some extent, they still require further refinement and strengthening, particularly in terms of resource support and practical implementation. From the perspective of policy receptors (X2), most policies cover a wide range of target groups, including multiple stakeholders such as government departments, medical institutions, and elderly care organizations. This reflects the development of high-quality IECMS services, emphasizing not only medical and elderly care services but also active participation from various forces like the government, enterprises, and communities. Establishing a multi-level and multi-dimensional cooperation network is essential to better meet the growing demand for health and care in an aging society. An analysis of the secondary indicators for carer care (X7) shows that existing policies provide more support for carers’ psychological counseling needs, social care, and protection of rights and benefits. However, coverage and implementation of relevant policies in areas such as short-term care, daycare, neighborhood care, and other flexible auxiliary care services remain relatively weak, indicating that improvements are needed in the care system. The results of the policy evaluation (X9) dimension show that existing policies are formulated by existing laws and regulations and align with central government requirements, ensuring legitimacy and authority. While most policies have feasible implementation plans and detailed contents, some lack clear and quantifiable objectives, making it difficult to measure their effects quantitatively. This may be attributed to the fact that China has not yet fully established a standardized policy evaluation mechanism encompassing social policy areas such as elderly care, resulting in a lack of stringent requirements for the quantification of objectives. During the formulation process, some policies have taken into account the limited implementation capacity of local authorities and consequently avoided setting overly specific indicators to mitigate implementation challenges. Furthermore, the language used in policy documents is predominantly directional and declarative, characterized by a focus on outcomes while exhibiting relatively insufficient quantitative requirements. These factors collectively contribute to difficulties in assessing policy implementation. In light of these issues, it is imperative to gradually advance the development of standardized and assessable policy mechanisms. Therefore, future policymaking should address three key aspects: systematization, technological integration, and capacity building. From an institutional perspective, efforts should be directed towards establishing a unified performance evaluation framework that incorporates quantitative target setting into the assessment process for implementation effectiveness. Technologically, leveraging smart tools can facilitate real-time tracking and evaluation of service outcomes. In terms of capacity building, there should be an emphasis on enhancing training for grassroots governments and executive agencies regarding the quantification of policy goals to improve both operability and evaluability within policy design. Ultimately, it is essential to promote a gradual transformation in China’s medical and elderly care integration policies from an “emphasis on orientation” approach to one that prioritizes “performance,” thereby enhancing both the scientific rigor and enforceability of these policies.

The scores for policy nature (X1), policy measures (X4), and care capacity (X5) are relatively low, at 0.75, 0.71, and 0.75, respectively. From the perspective of policy nature (X1), some policies exhibit strong supportive and guiding qualities, reflecting their proactivity in motivating and guiding relevant stakeholders. However, many policies are relatively weak in terms of evaluation and feedback mechanisms, lacking effective tracking and feedback links. This may lead to difficulties in identifying and correcting issues promptly during the actual implementation process, thereby affecting the continuous improvement of policy effectiveness. The statistical results for the policy measures (X4) dimension show that most policies have performed well in media publicity, supporting facilities, and legal norms. This indicates that policies have been effective in areas such as infrastructure development, legal protection, and promotion. However, lower scores in financial input, scientific and technological innovation, and demonstration pilots suggest that resource allocation and stage-by-stage goals of policy design may have constrained inputs and support in these areas, leading to shortcomings in long-term development and innovative implementation. For the care capacity (X5) dimension, analyzing its secondary indicators reveals that almost all policies focus on public health emergency response capacity building and service quality improvement. This indicates efforts to enhance emergency response capabilities and care service quality, especially in responding to public health emergencies and ensuring care service quality. More universities and social organizations are increasingly integrating first aid training into their regular curricula and conducting periodic assessments. Relevant medical institutions have also actively engaged in public education regarding emergency response capabilities, organized lectures on elderly care knowledge, and promoted appropriate caregiving methods [27]. Looking ahead, we can harness the collective efforts of all sectors of society, capitalize on the strengths of each stakeholder, and facilitate the establishment of emergency response and caregiving skills as a routine and widely embraced practice. However, areas such as information technology construction and elderly care model innovation are less well covered, with only a few policies addressing them. These measures involve more complex technology and model innovation and are relatively new, requiring further practical exploration and financial support. Therefore, there are still certain policy gaps and room for improvement in these areas. The government can suitably enhance its policy orientation and financial investment, while also promoting the engagement of social capital in opening avenues for investment.

The policy system (X6) dimension has the lowest mean score of 0.53, indicating that China’s IECMS care system requires further improvement. Analysis of the secondary indicators shows that current policies focus on establishing high-level nursing institutions and care centers, constructing hospital-community-family healthcare communities, and developing a comprehensive emergency treatment system. By the second quarter of 2025, the total number of various elderly care institutions and facilities across the country reached 406,000, providing approximately 7.991 million beds. Among these institutions, over 6,000 integrated traditional Chinese medicine with elderly care services, offering more than 1.6 million beds. Medical insurance reimbursement encompassed the medical services provided within these institutions [28]. Furthermore, leveraging a “community + property management + elderly care services” model, the government has implemented door-to-door assistance programs such as meal delivery and bathing support while establishing a “15-minute elderly care service circle.” The promotion of smart elderly care technology has also been gradually advancing. However, due to China’s large population and varying regional policies, there are instances where medical insurance is not applicable when seeking treatment outside one’s locality [29]. Consequently, Future policies need to pay more attention to changes in health insurance payment methods and care service pricing.

Discussion

Adhere to the system concept and uphold synergy, innovation and sharing

The integration of China’s existing healthcare system under the guidance of a systems approach is conducive to further improving the top-level design of IECMS policies and constructing a complete and efficient policy framework. This aligns with the requirements for high-quality development in the new era. By comparing and drawing on mature development experiences from other countries, medical, service, security, human resources, and other resources within the healthcare system should be integrated to ensure more efficient operation and resource distribution. The government should continue to enhance policy protection, focus on publicity and guidance, and promote service supervision while expanding the “medical-nursing-care” service scope for resource integration [30]. Adhering to the concept of synergy, the government should guide hospitals to assume active roles, establish close ties with communities, and provide high-quality professional medical services for the elderly. Social organizations and related enterprises should be encouraged to participate actively in the construction of ancillary facilities and social support for carers, promoting volunteer services and public welfare activities. The government should also encourage public feedback and address reasonable demands, always prioritizing the needs of the elderly. Adhering to the concept of innovation, recognizing that “innovation is the primary driving force for development,” the government will promote industry environment construction by reforming outdated policies that do not meet current development requirements. It will continuously transform and update government functions based on actual conditions, maintain policy inclination, and innovate management of community medical and nursing care services through multi-channel inputs. Efforts will be made to improve the long-term care insurance system, pension and medical insurance systems, and economic resource systems, including the evaluation and assessment of elderly care service practitioners. Emphasis will be placed on technological innovation in medical and nursing care, protecting and transforming scientific and technological achievements. Adhering to the concept of sharing, integrating resources, and responding to expectations guided by the physiological and psychological needs of the elderly, the effective demand for elderly care in the new era will be deeply explored. Clarifying the market positioning of medical and nursing care services will better match and satisfy increasingly diversified and personalized service demands [31], improving service quality. This aligns with the Chinese socialist concept of shared development, where the achievements of medical and nursing care services are jointly constructed and shared by all people.

Optimising industry structure and balancing supply and demand

In March 2023, the National Health Commission, in collaboration with several departments, issued the “Notice on Promoting the Typical Experiences of IECMS Pilot Work,” proposing to organize and implement a national IECMS demonstration project to promote high-quality development in this sector. Research findings indicate that China’s current healthcare system faces challenges such as insufficient policy support, inadequate supply elements, unclear boundaries and responsibilities among industry players, a single capital structure, and limited financing channels [32, 33]. Therefore, it is imperative to optimize the industry structure and balance various supply needs. To optimize the supply structure, it is crucial to address the significant differentiation in supply levels [34]. Relevant policies should be introduced to encourage healthcare service providers to clearly define their positioning and identify development paths that match their capabilities, thereby meeting healthcare service demands at all levels. Secondly, the issue of a single service content structure must be resolved, as it hinders the ability to meet individual requirements [35]. Policies should be formulated to encourage participating institutions to provide personalized care and differentiated services, refine the segmentation of service recipients, and establish geriatric specialist wards for comprehensive assessment, effective prevention, and timely intervention services. Thirdly, the problem of insufficient funding and low participation in social capital must be addressed [36]. Policies should be introduced to attract social capital, offer preferential policies to entities interested in elderly services, gradually improve the feedback supervision system for financial fund usage, and standardize financing guidelines to ensure the sustainability and reasonableness of the medical and nursing industry’s funding supply. Some studies have highlighted that the demand for rehabilitation from major illnesses and chronic disease care among the elderly is increasing due to socioeconomic development, outdated medical treatments, and higher illness probabilities [37]. However, the market struggles to meet this demand, resulting in a significant gap in specialized products for specific groups and considerable resource wastage. Therefore, it is necessary to summarize the experiences of recent IECMS pilots in China and introduce policies to address these issues based on highlighted contradictions and future development trends.

Innovate the industry environment and focus on talent cultivation

A favorable industry environment is not only conducive to increasing the enthusiasm of all participating entities but also plays a crucial role in reducing medical and care costs, thereby alleviating societal burdens and promoting healthy aging. The improvement of supporting facilities related to medical care and nursing should be continuously enhanced. The “14th Five-Year Plan for the Development of the National Aging Programme and the Pension Service System” emphasizes the need to establish more outlets for the sale and lease of rehabilitation aids. Currently, relevant departments have implemented rehabilitation aid leasing programs in 35 pilot areas nationwide, receiving positive feedback [24]. Future efforts should focus on further improving these facilities to ensure enhanced accessibility, affordability, and sharing of rehabilitation resources. Support should be provided for the research and promotion of assistive technologies for the disabled elderly, including the development of smart medical products such as remote communication tools and intelligent aids to help the elderly improve their self-care abilities and reduce practical difficulties in daily life. Additionally, efforts should be made to promote barrier-free environments and transportation systems to facilitate easier travel for the elderly. Encouraging the development of volunteer services and community support groups can also provide psychological support and emotional care for the elderly. Adequate talent supply is fundamental to the development of the healthcare industry. Studies indicate that China’s healthcare sector faces severe understaffing issues, low levels of practice, and high turnover rates [38]. Informal care remains the primary form of elderly care in China, accounting for 84.62% [39]. Therefore, it is imperative to strengthen talent attraction and cultivation to reinforce the IECMS team. The government can promote talent development by expanding recruitment channels and advocating favorable performance allocations and welfare protections for medical staff engaged in healthcare and nursing services. Additionally, education-related policies should be introduced, including the establishment of specialized colleges, training service skills, and improving practice standards, to ensure a long-term talent supply for the healthcare system. The popularity of family doctors significantly enhances service quality in the healthcare and nursing industry. According to the “Notice on the Guidelines for IECMS Services at Home and in the Community (Trial),” the coverage rate of contracted family doctor services should steadily increase for older adults with disabilities, chronic diseases, dementia, or mobility challenges [40]. Local authorities should design specialized family doctor service packages based on the actual needs and overall situation of the elderly to enhance their access to healthcare services. Efforts should also be made to continuously promote services such as home hospital beds, home visits, and green channels for booking medical appointments.

Limitations

It is important to acknowledge that the PMC policy text analysis model employed in this study is primarily designed for evaluating the structural consistency and content coverage of policy texts. A high PMC score does not necessarily correlate with the effectiveness or successful implementation of a given policy. Future research should undertake more comprehensive analyses that incorporate actual implementation data. Furthermore, the systematic nature and comprehensiveness of policy texts often reflect policymakers’ levels of attention and resource investment intentions, which are crucial foundations for effective policy implementation. Our findings indicate that policy documents with elevated PMC scores typically feature relatively clear implementation mechanisms and supporting safeguard measures. In an objective context, this observation reinforces our perspective. For example, the P7 “Notice on Issuing the ‘14th Five-Year Plan’ for the Development of National Aging Services and the Elderly Care Service System,” which boasts the highest PMC index score, serves as a planning document issued by central authorities; it has subsequently led to various regions formulating and introducing local elderly care service policies tailored to their specific contexts based on its core content [41].

In addition, as a tool for structural consistency analysis, the PMC model’s analytical outcomes exhibit considerable sensitivity to the construction of the index system. Even minor modifications to certain first-level or second-level indicators can significantly influence the final score and policy ranking. Although this study endeavored to adhere closely to existing literature and relevant policy documents in constructing these indicators, some degree of subjectivity and path dependence in their selection was unavoidable. It is also essential to give appropriate attention to content that was “not selected” from the policy documents; future research should explore this aspect further. Therefore, it is recommended that future studies incorporate sensitivity analysis when employing the PMC model, in order to verify both the consistency and robustness of model outputs under varying index configurations. Furthermore, as policies aimed at integrating medical care with elderly care continue to evolve and be implemented, changes in variables are likely to occur—an area that warrants careful consideration in subsequent research endeavors.

Conclusion

This study quantitatively analyzes the policies related to IECMS services in China within the context of high-quality development. Through text mining, an evaluation system for IECMS services is constructed, comprising 9 primary indicators and 44 secondary indicators. This system draws on the theoretical analysis framework of policy modeling consistency, the structure of the IECMS high-quality development support system proposed by the World Health Organization (WHO), the State Council, and the National Health Commission (NHC), as well as existing studies. The analysis results indicate that China’s IECMS service policies are relatively comprehensive in terms of policy concerns, party leadership, policy recipients, and caregiver care. However, deficiencies remain in the areas of the care system, policy nature, policy measures, and care capacity (X5). Future efforts should focus on building a systematic and scientific policy system, establishing a robust digital healthcare infrastructure, and promoting smart aging to ensure the development of high-quality IECMS services.

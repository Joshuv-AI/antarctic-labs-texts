Quantum groups of Borcherds-Cartan type and Khovanov-Lauda-Rouquier algebras
Seok-Jin Kang, Young Rock Kim, Bolun Tong

Abstract
. We categorify a class of quantum groups associated with quivers, possibly with loops, by constructing the corresponding Khovanov-Lauda-Rouquier algebras (KLR) algebras R R . We prove that the indecomposable projective R R -modules realize the canonical basis of the negative part U − U^{-} of the quantum group. Moreover, for Λ ∈ P + \Lambda\in P^{+} , the cyclotomic KLR algebra R Λ R^{\Lambda} provide a categorification of the irreducible highest weight U U -module V ⁡ ( Λ ) V(\Lambda) .



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
17B37, 17B67, 16G20

Introduction

Khovanov-Lauda-Rouquier algebras
(also known as
quiver Hecke algebras, or KLR algebras
) were introduced independently by Khovanov and Lauda
[
11
,
12
]
and by Rouquier
[
20
]
. In the Kac-Moody setting, the category of finitely generated graded projective modules over KLR algebras provides a categorification of the corresponding quantum groups. For symmetric Cartan data, the indecomposable projective modules correspond to Lusztig’s canonical basis
[
21
,
25
]
. Moreover, the cyclotomic quotients of KLR algebras categorify the irreducible highest weight representations of quantum groups
[
6
]
, as well as their crystals in the sense of Kashiwara
[
15
]
.

In the Kac-Moody case, Varagnolo and Vasserot
[
25
]
showed that KLR algebras admit a geometric realization as Steinberg-type convolution algebras associated with quiver flag varieties without loops, generalizing classical Springer theory. For quivers with arbitrary loops, a presentation of these Steinberg-type algebras was given in
[
7
]
. Independently, Sauter
[
23
]
developed a generalized quiver-graded Springer theory, in which the construction of
[
7
]
appears as a special case for the type
A
Weyl group.

In this setting, it is natural to expect that such generalized KLR algebras categorify quantum groups associated with quivers that may contain loops, and that they are closely related to Lusztig’s theory of canonical bases. A partial link in this direction was established in
[
7
]
, under restrictive assumptions on the quiver that exclude, in particular, the Jordan quiver. The quantum group considered there is relatively small, namely the
quantum generalized Kac-Moody algebra
.

The purpose of this paper is to develop an appropriate quantum group framework for these Steinberg-type algebras, thereby filling the gap left in
[
7
]
.

T. Bozec studied in
[
2
]
the theory of perverse sheaves on quiver representation varieties with possible loops, and proved that the Grothendieck group generated by Lusztig sheaves is spanned by elementary simple perverse sheaves, answering a question of Lusztig
[
18
]
.
In this framework, all partial flag varieties are taken into account, leading to a quantum group of Borcherds-Cartan type determined by a symmetric Borcherds-Cartan matrix
A=(a_{ij})_{i,j\in I}
, whose diagonal entries may be non-positive.
For an imaginary index
i
with
a_{ii}\leq 0
, infinitely many generators
F_{in}
(
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
) appear.

In the present paper, we restrict attention to the subalgebra
U^{-}
of Bozec’s quantum group arising from complete flag varieties
\mathscr{F}_{\nu}
for
\nu\in\mathbb{N}[I]
.
As a consequence, infinitely many generators
F_{in}
occur only in the isotropic case
a_{ii}=0
.

We first categorify
U^{-}
by constructing the corresponding KLR algebras
R(\nu)
for
\nu\in\mathbb{N}[I]
, using braid-like planar diagrammatics.
Rather than starting from geometric presentations of Steinberg algebras, which are technically more involved, we adopt an algebraic approach.
In particular, for
\nu=ni
with
a_{ii}=0
, the algebra
R(ni)
is the wreath product
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\rtimes\mathbb{C}[S_{n}]
, which coincides with the equivariant Borel–Moore homology ring
H_{*}^{GL_{n}}(\mathscr{Z})
of the Steinberg variety
\mathscr{Z}
associated with the complete flag variety
GL_{n}/B_{n}
.

Let
R=\bigoplus_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}[I]}R(\nu)
, and let
K_{0}(R)
denote the Grothendieck group of the category of finitely generated graded projective
R
-modules.
Using the framework developed in
[
11
,
12
]
, together with a detailed analysis of the Jordan quiver case, we prove that there is a bialgebra isomorphism

\Gamma:{{}_{\mathcal{A}}}U^{-}\xrightarrow{\sim}K_{0}(R),

where
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
and
{{}_{\mathcal{A}}}U^{-}
denotes the
\mathcal{A}
-form of
U^{-}
.
As mentioned above, the Steinberg-type varieties
\mathscr{Z}_{\nu}
associated with the complete flag varieties
\mathscr{F}_{\nu}
were studied in
[
7
,
23
]
.
We show that
R(\nu)
is isomorphic to the corresponding Steinberg-type convolution algebra
\mathcal{R}_{\nu}
.
As a consequence, combining classical Springer theory for
GL_{n}
with the results of Varagnolo and Vasserot, we prove that the canonical basis elements of
{{}_{\mathcal{A}}}U^{-}
correspond, under
\Gamma
, to the indecomposable projective
R
-modules.

Finally, let
U
be the quantum group obtained from
U^{-}
via the Drinfeld double construction.
We show that the cyclotomic quotients
R^{\Lambda}
for
\Lambda\in P^{+}
provide a categorification of the irreducible highest weight
U
-module
V(\Lambda)
. The proof proceeds by iterating the lower-rank arguments developed in
[
6
]
.

1.
Quantum groups of Borcherds-Cartan type

We introduce a class of quantum groups associated with quivers that may contain loops. These algebras appear as subalgebras of the quantum groups constructed in
[
2
]
and admit a geometric realization in terms of complete flag varieties associated with arbitrary quivers.

1.1.
The algebra
U^{-}

Let
I
be a finite index set. An integer-valued symmetric matrix

A=(a_{ij})_{i,j\in I}
is called a
Borcherds-Cartan matrix
if it satisfies

(i)

a_{ii}=2,0,-2,-4,\dots
,

(ii)

a_{ij}=a_{ji}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
for
i\neq j
.

We define the following subsets of
I
:

I^{+}=\{i\in I\mid a_{ii}=2\},\quad I^{0}=\{i\in I\mid a_{ii}=0\},\quad I^{-}=\{i\in I\mid a_{ii}<0\},

and set
I^{\leq 0}=I^{0}\sqcup I^{-}
. We further introduce the index set

\mathbbm{I}=I^{+}\sqcup I^{-}\sqcup\big(I^{0}\times\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\big).

Define a symmetric bilinear form

\nu,\nu^{\prime}\mapsto\nu\cdot\nu^{\prime}

on the free abelian group
\mathbb{Z}[I]
by

i\cdot j=a_{ij}=a_{ji}\ \ \text{for all}\ i,j\in I.

The triple
(I,A,\cdot)
is called a
Borcherds-Cartan datum
.

Let
q
be an indeterminate. For
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
, set

[n]=\displaystyle\frac{q^{n}-q^{-n}}{q-q^{-1}},\quad[n]!=[n][n-1]\cdots[1],\quad{\begin{bmatrix}n\\
k\end{bmatrix}}=\displaystyle\frac{[n]!}{[k]![n-k]!}.

Definition 1.1
.

The quantum group
U^{-}
associated to

(I,A,\cdot)
is the associative algebra over
\mathbb{Q}(q)
with
1
generated by
F_{in}

((i,n)\in\mathbbm{I})
satisfying the following relations:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{r+s=1-na_{ij}}(-1)^{r}F_{i}^{(r)}F_{jn}F_{i}^{(s)}=0\quad\text{for}\ i\in I^{+},(j,n)\in\mathbbm{I}\ \text{and}\ i\neq(j,n),

\displaystyle F_{in}F_{jm}-F_{jm}F_{in}=0\quad\ \text{for}\ a_{ij}=0.

Here we denote
F_{i}^{(n)}=F_{i}^{n}/[n]!
for
i\in I^{+}
and
n>0
. The algebra
U^{-}
is
\mathbb{N}[I]
-graded by assigning
|F_{in}|=ni
.

Define a twisted multiplication on
U^{-}\otimes U^{-}
by

(x_{1}\otimes x_{2})(y_{1}\otimes y_{2})=q^{-|x_{2}|\cdot|y_{1}|}x_{1}y_{1}\otimes x_{2}y_{2},

for homogeneous
x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\in U^{-}
.
We have an algebra homomorphism
\rho:U^{-}\rightarrow U^{-}\otimes U^{-}
(with respect to the twisted multiplication on
U^{-}\otimes U^{-}
) given by

\rho(F_{in})=\displaystyle\sum_{r+t=n}F_{ir}\otimes F_{it}\ \ \text{for}\ (i,n)\in\mathbbm{I}.

There is a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\{\ ,\ \}:U^{-}\times U^{-}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}(q)
determined by

(1)

\{x,y\}=0
if
|x|\neq|y|
,

(2)

\{1,1\}=1
,

(3)

\{F_{in},F_{in}\}=\epsilon_{n}:=\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{n}\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-q^{2k}}
for
(i,n)\in\mathbbm{I}
,

(4)

\{x,yz\}=\{\rho(x),y\otimes z\}
for
x,y,z\in U^{-}
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
be the ring of Laurent polynomials. The
\mathcal{A}
-form
{}_{\mathcal{A}}U^{-}
is the
\mathcal{A}
-subalgebra of
U^{-}
generated by
F_{i}^{(n)}

(i\in I^{+},n>0)
,
F_{i}

(i\in I^{-})
and
F_{in}

(i\in I^{0},n>0)
.

We denote by
-
the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra involution of
U^{-}
given by
\overline{q}=q^{-1}
and

\overline{F_{in}}=F_{in}
.

1.2.
Geometric setting for
U^{-}

We briefly review the geometric construction for
U^{-}
given in
[
2
,
16
,
18
]

(I,H)
be a quiver, possibly with loops, where
I
denote the set of vertices and

the set of arrows. For each

and

Fix

Then

Let

\displaystyle F_{i}^{(n)}\longleftrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathbf{E}_{ni}}\ \ \text{for}\ i\in I^{+},

\displaystyle F_{i}\longleftrightarrow\mathbb{C}_{\mathbf{E}_{i}}\ \ \text{for}\ i\in I^{-},

\displaystyle F_{in}\longleftrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}_{\{0\}\subset\mathbf{E}_{ni}}\ \ \text{for}\ i\in I^{0},\ n>0.

2.1.
\mathbb{Z}
-grading

-graded

-algebra. For a graded

Given two graded

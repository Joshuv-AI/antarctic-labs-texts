The R -Process Alliance: Enrichment of r -process Elements in a Simulated Milky Way-like Galaxy
Yutaka Hirai, Timothy C. Beers, Young Sun Lee, Shinya Wanajo, Ian U. Roederer, Masaomi Tanaka, Masashi Chiba, Takayuki R. Saitoh, Vinicius M. Placco, Terese T. Hansen, Rana Ezzeddine, Anna Frebel, Erika M. Holmbeck, Charli M. Sakari

Abstract
We study the formation of stars with varying amounts of heavy elements synthesized by the rapid neutron-capture process ( r -process) based on our detailed cosmological zoom-in simulation of a Milky Way-like galaxy with an N N -body/smoothed particle hydrodynamics code, asura . Most stars with no overabundance in r -process elements, as well as the strongly r -process enhanced r -II stars ([Eu/Fe] > + 0.7 >+0.7 ), are formed in dwarf galaxies accreted by the Milky Way within the 6 Gyr after the Big Bang. In contrast, over half of the moderately enhanced r -I stars ( + 0.3 < +0.3< [Eu/Fe] ≤ + 0.7 \leq+0.7 ) are formed in the main in-situ disk after 6 Gyr. Our results suggest that the fraction of r -I and r -II stars formed in disrupted dwarf galaxies is larger the higher their [Eu/Fe] is. Accordingly, the most strongly enhanced r -III stars ([Eu/Fe] > + 2.0 >+2.0 ) are formed in accreted components. These results suggest that non- r -process-enhanced stars and r -II stars are mainly formed in low-mass dwarf galaxies that hosted either none or a single neutron star merger, while the r -I stars tend to form in the well-mixed in-situ disk. We compare our findings with high-resolution spectroscopic observations of r -process-enhanced metal-poor stars in the halo and dwarf galaxies, including those collected by the R-Process Alliance. We conclude that observed [Eu/Fe] and [Eu/Mg] ratios can be employed in chemical tagging of the Milky Way’s accretion history.

-II and

IV
Discussion

IV.1
Formation Scenarios of
r
-II,
r
-I, and Non-RPE Stars

In Section
III.2
, we found a clear difference in the formation mechanisms between non-RPE/
r
-II and
r
-I stars. Following our results on the formation of stars with
r
-process elements, we now provide a physical interpretation of the classification of
r
-II,
r
-I, and non-RPE stars.

From inspection of Figure
6
(b), we find that stars in each category exhibit different trends in the fraction of stars formed in accreted components as a function of [Eu/Fe]. According to this Figure,
r
-II stars tend to form in low-mass dwarf galaxies (Figure
7
) that host NSMs. In these systems, a single NSM can significantly enhance the
r
-process abundances. Such a signature has been observed in the UFD Reticulum II
[
57
]
.
Non-RPE stars with lower [Eu/Fe] also tend to be formed in accreted dwarf galaxies that do not have NSMs (Figure
6
(b)). As argued in Section
III.3
, most low-mass components have low Eu abundances because no NSMs occurred in these systems. Because low-mass sub-halos have zero or one NSM, some systems are highly enhanced in Eu, while others have not experienced any such enrichment. Several studies have shown that an NSM event can enhance the Eu abundance sufficiently to form
r
-II stars
[
57
,
94
,
113
,
56
,
117
,
44
, e.g.,]
. Low levels of
r
-process element abundances are found in Local Group UFDs (
A. Frebel 29
, and references therein).

In contrast, the accreted fraction of
r
-I stars is, on average, less than 0.5 (Figure
6
(b)). This result means that
r
-I stars are mainly formed in a well-mixed ISM, such as in the disk, that has experienced ample
in-situ
star formation. Because our
in-situ
component hosts many NSMs with a longer duration of star formation than that of any of the accreted components, the [Eu/Fe] distribution in the ISM is homogeneous. This results in the much smaller scatter in [Eu/Fe] ratios that produce stars mostly within the
r
-I regime (Figure
8
).

IV.2
Formation of Stars with Extreme Eu Abundances

Our simulation also includes stars with extreme abundances of
r
-process elements. Some fall into the category of metal-poor
r
-III stars with [Eu/Fe]
>+2.0
and
-3.4<[\rm{Fe/H}]<-2.5
. Others appear to be more metal-rich
r
-II stars with [Fe/H]
\sim-1.0

[
122
, see, e.g.,]
. Yet others are
r
-process element free, as they formed from gas that was never enriched by an
r
-process event.
M. Cain et al. [15]
first identified a VMP star (J1521-3538, [Fe/H] =
-2.80
) with [Eu/Fe] =
+2.23
. Because of its highly eccentric orbit, they argued that its origin is an accreted dwarf galaxy. Most recently,
I. U. Roederer et al. [91]
have analyzed a new VMP
r
-III star (J2213-5137; [Fe/H] =
-2.20
) with the highest known [Eu/Fe] among VMP stars found to date ([Eu/Fe] =
+2.45
).

Our simulation predicts that the percentage of
r
-III stars is 6.1
\times 10^{-4}
%, suggesting that these stars may well be difficult to find. The most
r
-process-enhanced star in our simulation within 3
<r/(\rm{kpc})<
20 displays [Eu/Fe] =
+
2.45 (at
r=7
kpc). Outside of this range, the highest ratio is [Eu/Fe] =
+
3.58 (at
r
= 60 kpc). We find that all
r
-III stars are from accreted components. Among them, two r-III stars are from a massive accreted component with
M_{*}
= 1.3
\times
10
7

M_{\sun}
, while the other four stars are from low-mass accreted components with
M_{*}

<10^{5}M_{\sun}
. This result suggests that accreted dwarf galaxies are the exclusive sites for forming
r
-III stars. Most of them are formed in the low-mass accreted components that are highly enhanced in
r
-process elements, while local inhomogeneities in massive accreted components still allows for the formation of
r
-III stars.

As shown in Figure
5
, we find that a few metal-rich ([Fe/H]
>-
1)
r
-II stars formed in the
in-situ
galactic disk, but these were rare occurrences.
From inspection of Figure
6
(a), all
r
-II stars in this simulation are formed
in-situ
for this metallicity range. These stars are formed in the locally Eu-enhanced gas clump around an NSM occurring in the galactic disk. However, only 0.08% of the stars are classified as metal-rich
r
-II stars in our simulation. Regarding observations,
X.-J. Xie et al. [122]
found a
r
-II star (J0206+4941) at [Fe/H] =
-0.54
in the thin disk and formed
in-situ
, based on its kinematic signature. The discovery of J0206+4941 has allowed calculating the percentage of metal-rich
r
-II stars in the MW to be 0.03%, i.e., one per 2664 stars in the literature
[
109
,
108
,
123
, SAGA database,]
.
Y. Hirai & T. R. Saitoh [49]
measured the timescale to dilute and mix the local
r
-process enhancement near an NSM, and found that it was erased within 40 Myr on average
[
44
, also see Figure 11 of ]
. These results mean that the short timescale of metal dilution suppresses the formation of metal-rich
r
-II stars.

In contrast to the most extreme RPE stars, our simulation also has some stars without Eu. We find that 0.0058% of the stars are Eu free. However, as a result of a general level of metal mixing, most stars will form with at least some Eu. This tiny percentage of Eu-free stars suggests that stars without neutron-capture elements may never be found.

V
Conclusions

In this study, we have performed a high-resolution cosmological zoom-in simulation of a Milky Way-like galaxy using the
asura
code. We analyze the formation environments of
r
-II,
r
-I, and non-RPE stars within 3 to 20 kpc from the galactic center, which mimics the region of the known
r
-process metal-poor stars identified by the RPA and other searches. We find that our simulated environment exhibits a [Eu/Fe] distribution similar to that of the RPA sample to date (Figure
3
). We also show the [Eu/Fe] and [Eu/Mg] ratios for
in-situ
and accreted components with different mass ranges.

We find that non-RPE stars and
r
-II stars are formed on similar time scales and in similar environments. Most non-RPE and
r
-II stars are formed within 6 Gyr from the Big Bang (Figure
4
), preferentially in low-gas mass accreted components (Figures
6
(b) and
7
). On the other hand,
r
-I stars are continuously formed for 13 Gyr, mainly in the
in-situ
component. These results indicate a clear difference in the formation environment between non-RPE/
r
-II and
r
-I stars. In low-mass sub-halos, an NSM can enhance the entire galaxy, forming
r
-II stars. Given their small mass and the rarity of NSMs, some sub-halos will never host NSMs. In these cases, non-RPE stars are formed. For the
in-situ
halo, most gas is well-mixed. In such an environment,
r
-I stars can form for extended periods of time.

Furthermore, our simulation forms stars with either strongly enhanced Eu abundances or none at all. Hence, we find that all
r
-III stars are formed in accreted components; the highest [Eu/Fe] ratio in our simulation is [Eu/Fe] =
+
3.58. We also find it relatively rare for any metal-rich
r
-II stars to form in local inhomogeneities in the disk. Rather, these stars require formation in locally
r
-process-enhanced gas clumps near a previous NSM.
Interestingly, there are few
r
-process free stars formed in our simulation. This result suggests that star formation in pristine gas without
r
-process elements is infrequent, likely due to metal mixing and dilution processes. Although stars with extreme
r
-process abundances are scarce in our simulation, finding actual stars with extreme abundances would greatly help to constrain the ejecta properties of NSMs and the effects of metal dilution.

In this work, we propose diagnostics to estimate the stellar mass of accreted components.
Low-mass accreted components with
M_{*}<10^{7}M_{\sun}
appear to be hosts of
(1)
r
-II stars with [Fe/H]
<-3
or
(2) A group of stars with a downward trend of [Eu/Mg] vs [Mg/H] across the range of
-3<
[Mg/H]
<-2
. Massive accreted components with
M_{*}>10^{7}M_{\sun}
appear to host
(3) A group of stars with an upward trend of [Eu/Fe] and [Eu/Mg] vs [Fe/H] across the range of
-3<
[Fe/H]
<-2
.
A large fraction of the known
r
-II stars in the halo have [Fe/H]
\sim-3
, thus suggesting that they all formed in early accreted systems which is consistent with other theoretical predictions (e.g.,
K. Brauer et al. 14
). Overall, this confirms that observed
r
-process signatures can be employed in chemical tagging of the accretion history of the Milky Way, ultimately leading to a reconstruction of our Galaxy’s assembly history.
Hence, these predictions should be further tested and applied to the chemo-dynamics of metal-poor stars in the Milky Way.

To make progress, as stars leftover from any of the accreted components are more likely to be found in the outer halo

,

, it would be desirable to extend the RPA effort to larger distances in order to uncover fainter
r
-process stars. We also expect that the Legacy Survey of Space and Time will find many new dwarf galaxies and substructures
[

]
. The Subaru Prime Focus Spectrograph can perform massively multiplexed spectroscopic observations for such structures in the outer halo
[

,

]
. Follow-up spectroscopic observations with next-generation Extremely Large Telescopes
[

will be able to measure
r
-process abundances of these stars. Comparison with high-resolution simulations, in particular those with the ability to simulate the formation of individual stars, will further improve understanding of the enrichment of

Appendix B
Effects of the Mass Assembly History

The mass assembly history could affect the timings of the formation of RPE and non-RPE stars. If there are galaxy assembly more recently, the age distribution of
r
-II stars could be younger than our simulation. The zoomed-in halo has been selected based on the mass assembly history. We selected the halo without a major merger since
z
= 2. Thanks to this halo selection criterion, we expect that the formation timings are not largely affected by the mass assembly history.

The fraction of
r
-II stars could indicate the assembly of low-mass halos. Since these stars are mainly formed in low-mass halos, galaxies assembled by the larger fractions of low-mass halos should have larger fractions of
r
-II stars. Comparison with observations could constrain the mass assembly history. As shown in Section
III.1
, the simulated (observed) number fraction of
r
-II stars in 3
<r
/kpc
<
20 is similar: 0.07 (0.10). This result means that our simulation has a similar mass assembly history for low-mass halos to the Milky Way.

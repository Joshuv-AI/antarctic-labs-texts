Uniqueness of solutions to MFG systems with large discount
Marco Cirant, Elisa Continelli

Abstract
. We prove that solutions to a class of Mean Field Game systems with discount are unique provided that the discount factor is large enough, and the Lagrangian term is (proportionally) small enough. This identifies an asymptotic uniqueness regime that falls outside the usual ones involving monotonicity.



1.
Introduction

The main purpose of this paper is to discuss the uniqueness of solutions, for large (positive)
\lambda
, to the following MFG system of PDE

(
{\rm MFG}_{\lambda}
)

\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}u_{\lambda}-\Delta u_{\lambda}+\frac{1}{\lambda}H(\lambda Du_{\lambda},x)+\lambda u=F(x,m_{\lambda}(t)),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\in(0,\infty),\\
\partial_{t}m_{\lambda}-\Delta m_{\lambda}-\text{div}(m_{\lambda}D_{p}H(\lambda Du_{\lambda},x))=0,\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\in(0,\infty),\\
m_{\lambda}(x,0)=m_{0}(x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

The analysis of the problem with large
\lambda
is motivated by the work of Bardi and Cardialiaguet
[
BC21
]
, who showed that, as
\lambda\to\infty
, solutions of (

MFG
λ

) converge to solutions of the nonlinear parabolic PDE

(
{\rm MF}_{\infty}
)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}m-\Delta m-\text{div}(mD_{p}H(D_{x}F(x,m(t)),x))=0,\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\in(0,\infty),\\
m(x,0)=m_{0}(x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

This convergence result bridges two different approaches to the modelling of large population phenomena. On the one hand, Mean Field Games (MFG), introduced by Lasry and Lions
[
LL07
]
and Huang, Caines and Malhamé
[
HMC06
]
, that describe Nash equilibria among a continuum of indistinguishable rational agents. On the other hand, agent-based models, where individuals react to the population distribution according to a given rule; these models have a large literature, see for instance the survey
[
CCP17
]
. In MFG of the form (

MFG
λ

),
F
plays the role of the running cost (of an individual with state
x
against the population distribution
m(t)
) and
H
is the Hamiltonian (related to the cost due to the control). Most importantly, the parameter
\lambda
tunes at the same time two properties of the model: it is a time discount factor (corresponding to the
\lambda u_{\lambda}
term) and it is inversely proportional to the cost of the controls. For the sake of clarity, players’ trajectories are driven by the controlled SDE
\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}X_{t}=v_{t}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}t+\sqrt{2}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}B_{t}
, and they aim to minimize

v\mapsto\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda s}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}L(v_{s},X_{s})+F(X_{s},m_{\lambda}(s))\right)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s,

where the Lagrangian
L
is conjugate to the Hamiltonian
H
. In other words, as
\lambda
increases, agents become less interested in future events, while at the same time their “reaction cost” becomes cheaper. Eventually, in the
\lambda\to\infty
limit, the model degenerates into another one where the individual rule of motion is given by
-D_{p}H(D_{x}F(x,m(t)),x)
. Here, rationality is absent, as potential future events have no effect on the population dynamics.

If the MFG was potential, that is when
F=\delta_{m}\mathcal{F}
, this phenomenon would be analogous to the so-called Weighted Energy-Dissipation mechanism in Calculus of Variations, see for example
[
RSSS19
,
MS11
]
and references therein. It is interesting to note that one may look at the problem (

MFG
λ

) as a second-order (in time) approximation of the first-order (in time) problem (

MF
∞

).

Regarding (

MF
∞

), under mild assumptions on
D_{p}H,D_{x}F
, one expects uniqueness of solutions: this is in fact a McKean-Vlasov equation, to which standard Cauchy-Lipschitz theory applies (see for example
[
BKRS15
]
). On the other hand, while
[
BC21
]
shows that solutions of (

MFG
λ

) converge to solutions of (

MF
∞

) as
\lambda\to\infty
, it is not clear whether they are
unique
when
\lambda
is finite. The goal of the present paper is to to show that (

MFG
λ

) has in fact unique solutions if
\lambda
is large enough, “borrowing uniqueness” from the limit problem. This is stated as follows (for the more explicit version, see Theorem
3.4
).

Theorem
.

Assume (F1), (F2) and (H1), (H2) below. Then, there exists
\Lambda>0
such that, if
\lambda\geq\Lambda
, then solutions to (

MFG
λ

) are unique.

To be more precise, we focus on those solutions that arise as limits of MFG systems on the time horizon
[0,T]
, with final condition
u(T)\equiv 0
, in the limit
T\to\infty
. Those solutions enjoy nice growth properties and estimates; their existence is obtained in Proposition
2.1
. This result was already discussed in
[
BC21
]
, though we show here the validity of additional uniform double-sided estimates on second-order derivatives. Uniqueness of solutions is then obtained within the set of functions satisfying these estimates. This is a point worth stressing, as there is no explicit “final condition” on
u
in (

MFG
λ

), and other solutions, possibly arising by different construction methods, could coexist.

An interesting point of the uniqueness result is that the critical constant
\Lambda
does not really depend on the viscosity. In particular, the diffusion parameter is just set to be equal to one for simplicity throughout the whole paper, but if the Laplacian terms were replaced by
\nu\Delta
, with
\nu>0
, then
\Lambda
would be independent of
\nu
.

Uniqueness in MFG is known in special circumstances, that are generally related to monotone structures: these are mainly the so-called Lasry-Lions monotonicity
[
LL07
]
and the displacement monotonicity
[
MM24
]
; see also
[
MZ22
,
BM24
,
GM23
]
. Another uniqueness scenario is when some “smallness” assumption is in force, such as in
[
BC18a
,
BF19
,
AM23
,
CCDE24
]
; such smallness is often related to the time-horizon, but this is somehow analogous to situations involving “small interactions”. The uniqueness regime that is identified here is certainly very different from the one arising from monotonicity. It could be vaguely close to the one involving “small” parameters: in some sense, when
\lambda
is large, there are terms in (

MFG
λ

) that are expected to vanish, at least from an heuristic point of view. Despite that, the structure of the proof looks different from all the previous cases.

It is convenient to think of the term
-D_{p}H(\lambda Du_{\lambda}(t,x),x)
in the second equation of (

MFG
λ

), driving the evolution of
m(t)
, as a nonlinear and
non-local
in time function of
m_{\lambda}
; indeed, it is clear from the first equation that
Du_{\lambda}(t)
depends on the whole “future” behaviour of
m_{\lambda}
on the time interval
[t,\infty)
. As a preliminary result, we show (see in particular Proposition
3.1
) that
Du_{\lambda}(t)
is actually close to
DF(x,m_{\lambda}(t))
(the notation
DF=D_{x}F
and
Du=D_{x}u
will be used from time to time):

\left|\lambda Du_{\lambda}(x,t)-DF(x,m_{\lambda}(t))\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}},

so
-D_{p}H(\lambda Du_{\lambda}(t,x),x)
sees in fact
m_{\lambda}
almost only through its values at time
t
. This is just a quantitative version of the convergence result in
[
BC21
]
. To achieve uniqueness of solutions, we need some further information. It turns out (see Proposition
3.3
) that there exists a positive constant
\eta
such that, given any two solutions
(u_{\lambda}^{1},m_{\lambda}^{1})
,
(u_{\lambda}^{2},m_{\lambda}^{2})
, then

\|\lambda Du_{\lambda}^{1}(\cdot,t)-\lambda Du_{\lambda}^{2}(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim d_{1}(m_{\lambda}^{1}(t),m_{\lambda}^{2}(t))+\\
\int_{t}^{\infty}d_{1}(m_{\lambda}^{1}(t),m_{\lambda}^{2}(t))e^{-(\lambda-\eta)(r-t)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r.

This says rather precisely how
Du_{\lambda}(t)
, and hence
-D_{p}H(\lambda Du_{\lambda}(t))
, changes as
m_{\lambda}
changes. It is clear that, for large values of
\lambda
, variations of
m_{\lambda}
in the “future” are almost irrelevant. A delicate aspect in the derivation of this estimate is that the time horizon is infinite. The estimate is enough, combined with a sort of non-local in time Grönwall type lemma (see Proposition
2.5
), to derive the uniqueness result.

To conclude, we stress that non-uniqueness is a broad phenomenon in the theory of MFG, see for instance
[
BF19
,
BC18b
,
Cir19
,
CT19
,
BZ20
]
. The uniqueness regime identified here could lead to further investigation. First,
F
is assumed here to depend on
m(t)
in a non-local sense (
F
is in fact a regularizing functional of
m
, for instance of convolution type). We expect the argument to work along similar lines when
F
is a local function of the density
m(x,t)
, in which case the presence of a nondegenerate diffusion would be way more important. Another question is related to the long-time behavior of (

MFG
λ

); in particular, we expect the couple
(u_{\lambda}(t),m_{\lambda}(t))
to converge to a stationary profile as
t\to+\infty
. Finally, one could have situations where the limit agent-based model (

MF
∞

) has multiple solutions; we do not know if (

MFG
λ

) could enjoy uniqueness in some of those cases (though it might be reasonable, as the latter is a sort of second-order regularization of the former). In this hypothetical framework, one could investigate selection phenomena for (

MF
∞

).

\Phi(z,r)=\frac{1}{(4\pi r)^{\frac{n}{2}}}e^{-\frac{\lvert z\rvert^{2}}{4r}}
, for
z\in\mathbb{R}
and
r>0
.

Proof.

Let
T>0
. Let us first define

v(x,t):=e^{\lambda t}u(x,T-t),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\forall t\in[0,T].

Then,
v(\cdot,0)=u(\cdot,T)
and

\partial_{t}v(x,t)=\lambda e^{\lambda t}u(x,T-t)-e^{\lambda t}u_{t}(x,T-t)=\lambda v-e^{\lambda t}u_{t}(x,T-t),\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T)

\Delta v(x,t)=e^{\lambda t}\Delta u(x,T-t),\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T).

Now, since
u
solves the first equation in (

MFG
λ

), we obtain that
v
satisfies

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v-\Delta v=f:=e^{\lambda t}\left(F(x,m(T-t))-\frac{1}{\lambda}H(\lambda Du(x,T-t),x)\right),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},t\in(0,T),\\
v=u(\cdot,T),\quad&\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\},\end{cases}

Since (
7
) is a Cauchy problem for a inhomogeneous heat equation, we have the following representation formula

v(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi(x-y,t-s)f(y,s)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi(x-y,t)u(y,T)dy,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,T],

where
\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)
is the fundamental solution of the heat equation given by

\Phi(z,r):=\frac{1}{(4\pi r)^{\frac{n}{2}}}e^{-\frac{\lvert z\rvert^{2}}{4r}},\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0.

Indeed, it is standard that Duhamel’s formula hold for
C^{2,1}
solutions that have polynomial growth in the
x
variable, together with their derivatives
Dv,D^{2}v,\partial_{t}v
, and with right-hand side

having polynomial growth. These growth properties are satisfied in view of Proposition
2.1
(for
u
and hence for
v
) and the growth assumptions on
F
and
H
.

Back to
u
, we get the following equality

u(x,t)=e^{-\lambda(T-t)}v(x,T-t)\\
=e^{-\lambda(T-t)}\int_{0}^{T-t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{e^{-\frac{\lvert x-y\rvert^{2}}{4(T-t-s)}}e^{\lambda s}}{(4\pi(T-t-s))^{\frac{n}{2}}}\left(F(y,m(T-s))-\frac{1}{\lambda}H(\lambda Du(y,T-s),y)\right)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s\\
+e^{-\lambda(T-t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi(x-y,T-t)u(y,T)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y,

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in[0,T]
. Using the change of variable
r=T-s
, we conclude that

(8)

u(x,t)=\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi(x-y,s-t)e^{-\lambda(s-t)}\left(F(y,m(r))-\frac{1}{\lambda}H(\lambda Du(y,s),y)\right)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s\\
+e^{-\lambda(T-t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi(x-y,T-t)u(y,T)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in[0,T]
.

Now, for given
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in[0,T]
, the representation formula (
8
) holds for any
T>t
. Note that from property (i) in Proposition
2.1
,

\left|e^{-\lambda(T-t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi(x-y,T-t)u(y,T)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\right|\leq C_{o}(1+\lvert x\rvert+C_{n})e^{-\lambda(T-t)}\underset{T\to\infty}{\to}0.

Therefore, the second term in the right-hand side of (
8
) goes to zero, as
T\to\infty
. Letting
T\to\infty
in (
6
), we get the assertion.
∎

The following representation formula for
Du
will be crucial. Note that this is not obtained by mere differentiation of the previous one; in fact, it does not make use of the fundamental solution of the Heat Equation.

Proposition 2.4

(Representation formula for
\lambda Du
)
.

Let
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
. Let
(u,m)
be a solution to (

λ

).
Then, for any

,

(9)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69046pt\displaystyle{\lambda\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}(x,t)=\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\lambda e^{-\lambda(s-t)}\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}(y,m(s))-e^{-\lambda(s-t)}\frac{\partial H}{\partial x_{i}}(\lambda Du(y,s),y)\Big)\rho^{x,t}(y,s)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s}\\

for all

,

Proof.

Given

Before we move to the next section, let us comment on formula (
9
), that gives, after letting
T\to\infty
,

\displaystyle{\lambda\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}(x,t)=\int_{t}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\lambda e^{-\lambda(s-t)}\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}(y,m(s))-e^{-\lambda(s-t)}\frac{\partial H}{\partial x_{i}}}(\lambda Du(y,s),y)\Big)\rho^{x,t}(y,s)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s

On one hand, the first term on the right-hand side can be seen as a (time) convolution with the function
\lambda e^{-\lambda t}{\bf 1}_{[0,\infty)}(t)
; for large
\lambda
, this kernel concentrates at time zero, so the convolution is expected to be close to
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{x_{i}}F(y,m(t))\rho^{x,t}(y,t)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y=\partial_{x_{i}}F(x,m(t))
. On the other hand, the second integral vanishes, since
e^{-\lambda(s-t)}
is bounded and vanishes for every
s>t
as
\lambda\to\infty
. For these reasons, we expect that

\lambda Du(x,t)\to DF(x,m(t))\qquad\text{as $\lambda\to\infty$}.

This limit will be made rigorous, in the uniform sense, in the following section.

The following lemma is a sort of Grönwall type result, where a further “future” in time integral is allowed.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
f
be a nonnegative continuous function on
[0,+\infty)
, and
a,b,\delta>0
. Assume that

f(t)\leq a\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s+b\int_{t}^{\infty}f(s)e^{-\delta(s-t)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s,\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

If
\delta\geq 4a+4b
and there exists
M>0
such that
f(t)\leq Me^{4at}
, for all
t\geq 0
, then
f\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Let
t\geq 0
. Since
f(t)\leq Me^{4at}
,

f(t)\leq aM\int_{0}^{t}e^{4as}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s+bM\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\delta(s-t)+4as}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s=\frac{aM}{4a}(e^{4at}-1)+\frac{bM}{\delta-4a}e^{4at}\\
\leq M\left[\frac{1}{4}+\frac{b}{\delta-4a}\right]e^{4at}.

Since
\delta\geq 4a+4b
, it holds that
\frac{b}{\delta-4a}\leq\frac{1}{4}
. Hence,

f(t)\leq\frac{M}{2}e^{4at}.

Iterating
\ell
times the previous argument,

f(t)\leq\frac{M}{2^{\ell}}e^{4at},\quad\forall\ell\in\mathbb{N}.

By letting
\ell\to\infty
we get
f(t)=0
. Since this is true for any
t\geq 0
, we conclude.
∎

We finally present a statement of the standard Grönwall’s lemma in backward form.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
f,g
be continuous functions on
[0,T]
,
T>0
, and
c,\eta>0
. If

f(t)\leq g(t)+c\int_{t}^{T}f(r)e^{-\eta(r-t)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r\qquad\forall t\in[0,T],

then

f(t)\leq g(t)+c\int_{t}^{T}g(r)e^{-(\eta-c)(r-t)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r\qquad\forall t\in[0,T].

Main results

The first main result states the convergence of the drift
\lambda Du_{\lambda}
to
DF
. This was already observed in
[
BC21
]
; here, we get a quantitative version of it. We give its proof to prepare the reader to the uniqueness result, though convergence is not really necessary to achieve uniqueness.

Proposition 3.1

(Uniform Convergence)
.

Let
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
. Let
(u,m)
be a solution to (

MFG
λ

). Then there exists
\overline{C}
depending on
n,C_{o}
such that

(10)

\left\|\lambda Du-DF(\cdot,m(\cdot))\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\overline{C}}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\big(L+1\big).

In particular,
\lambda Du
converges uniformly to
DF(\cdot,m(\cdot))
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,+\infty)
as
\lambda\to\infty
.

Note that (
10
), together with an estimate of the kind (
4
), give quantitive convergence of
m_{\lambda}
to solutions of (

MF
∞

) in
C([0,\tau];\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, for any
\tau>0
.

Proof.

Let
T>0
. Given
i=1,\dots,n
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in[0,T]
, due to (
9
) we have

(11)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\left\lvert\lambda\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u(x,t)-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}F(x,m(t))\right\rvert}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 8.5359pt\leq\left\lvert\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lambda e^{-\lambda(s-t)}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}F(y,m(s))\rho^{x,t}(y,s)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}F(x,m(t))\right\rvert}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 22.76228pt+\left\lvert\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\lambda(s-t)}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}H(\lambda Du(y,s),y)\rho^{x,t}(y,s)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s\right\rvert+\lambda e^{-\lambda T}\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u(y,T)\rho^{x,t}(y,T)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\right\rvert.}\end{array}

Let us first estimate the second term of the right-hand side. From (
1
) we have
\lvert D_{x}H(\lambda Du(y,s),y)\rvert\leq C_{o}(1+C)
, for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
s\in[t,T]
. Hence,

\left\lvert\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\lambda(s-t)}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}H(\lambda Du(y,s),y)\rho^{x,t}(y,s)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s\right\rvert\\
\leq\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\lambda(s-t)}\left\lvert\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}H(\lambda Du(y,s),y)\right\rvert\rho^{x,t}(y,s)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s\\
\leq C_{o}(1+C)\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\lambda(s-t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho^{x,t}(y,s)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s=C_{o}(1+C)\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\lambda(s-t)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s\leq\frac{C_{o}(1+C)}{\lambda},

where above we used that
\rho^{x,t}(\cdot,s)
is a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for all
s\in[t,T]
.

On the other hand, using (ii) in Proposition (
2.1
) the last term in the right-hand side of (
11
) can be estimated as follows

\lambda e^{-\lambda T}\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u(y,T)\rho^{x,t}(y,T)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\right\rvert\leq e^{-\lambda T}\lVert\lambda Du\rVert_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho^{x,t}(y,T)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\leq Ce^{-\lambda T}.

Now we address the first term of the right-hand side of (
11
). First of all, using the fact that
\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lambda e^{-\lambda(s-t)}\rho^{x,t}(y,s)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s=1-e^{-\lambda(T-t)}
, we have

\left\lvert\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lambda e^{-\lambda(s-t)}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}F(y,m(s))\rho^{x,t}(y,s)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}F(x,m(t))\right\rvert\\
\leq\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lambda e^{-\lambda(s-t)}\left\lvert\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}F(y,m(s))-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}F(x,m(t))\right\rvert\rho^{x,t}(y,s)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s\\
+e^{-\lambda(T-t)}\left\lvert\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}F(x,m(t))\right\rvert.

Then, the Lipschitz continuity of
D_{x}F
yields

\left\lvert\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lambda e^{-\lambda(s-t)}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}F(y,m(s))\rho^{x,t}(y,s)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}F(x,m(t))\right\rvert\\
\leq L\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lambda e^{-\lambda(s-t)}\lvert x-y\rvert\rho^{x,t}(y,s)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s+L\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lambda e^{-\lambda(s-t)}d_{1}(m(s),m(t))\rho^{x,t}(y,s)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s\\
+e^{-\lambda(T-t)}\left\lvert\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}F(x,m(t))\right\rvert:=I+II++e^{-\lambda(T-t)}\left\lvert\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}F(x,m(t))\right\rvert.

Note that

I=L\int_{t}^{T}\lambda e^{-\lambda(s-t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lvert x-y\rvert(\rho^{x,t}(y,s)-\rho^{x,t}(y,t))\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s\leq L\int_{t}^{T}\lambda e^{-\lambda(s-t)}d_{1}(\rho^{x,t}(s),\rho^{x,t}(t)).

Then, estimate (
3
) applied to
\rho^{x,t}
yields

I\leq LC\int_{t}^{T}\lambda e^{-\lambda(s-t)}(s-t)+\sqrt{2}L\int_{t}^{T}\lambda e^{-\lambda(s-t)}\sqrt{s-t}:=I_{1}+I_{2}.

Using the change of variable
r=\lambda(s-t)
,

I_{1}=\frac{LC}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\lambda(T-t)}e^{-r}r\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r\leq\frac{LC}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-r}r\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r=\frac{LC}{\lambda}.

On the other hand,

I_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r=\sqrt{\lambda(s-t)}}}{{=}}\frac{2\sqrt{2}L}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\lambda(T-t)}}e^{-r^{2}}r^{2}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r\leq\frac{2\sqrt{2}L}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-r^{2}}r^{2}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}L}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}.

We can handle the term
II
similarly. Indeed, using (
3
) with
m
gives

\displaystyle{II\leq L\int_{t}^{T}\lambda e^{-\lambda(s-t)}(C(s-t)+\sqrt{2(s-t)})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho^{x,t}(y,s)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s}\leq\frac{LC}{\lambda}+\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}L}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}.

Finally, we can conclude that

\left|\lambda\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u(x,t)-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}F(x,m(t))\right|\leq\frac{C_{o}(1+C)}{\lambda}+Ce^{-\lambda T}+\frac{2LC}{\lambda}+\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}L}{\sqrt{\lambda}}+e^{-\lambda(T-t)}\left\lvert\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}F(x,m(t))\right\rvert,

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
t\in[0,T]
and
T>0
. Since for given
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\geq 0
the above estimate holds for every
T>t
, letting
T\to\infty
we obtain

\left|\lambda\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u(x,t)-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}F(x,m(t))\right|\leq\frac{C_{o}(1+C)}{\lambda}+\frac{2LC}{\lambda}+\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}L}{\sqrt{\lambda}},

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\geq 0
.
Taking the supremum for
i=1,\dots,N
and
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,\infty)
, we conclude that

(12)

\|\lambda Du-DF(\cdot,m(\cdot))\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{C_{o}(1+C)}{\lambda}+\frac{2LC}{\lambda}+\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}L}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\leq\frac{\bar{C}}{\sqrt{\lambda}}(L+1),

and so (
10
) is proven. Letting
\lambda\to\infty
, since the right-hand side of (
10
) goes to 0, we can conclude that the left-hand side of (
10
) goes to 0 too. Therefore,
\lambda Du
converges uniformly to
DF(\cdot,m(\cdot))
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,+\infty)
, as
\lambda\to\infty
.

∎

Remark 3.2

(Vanishing viscosity)
.

If one replaces

, in which case (

.

,

I\leq L\int_{t}^{T}\lambda e^{-\lambda(s-t)}d_{1}(m_{1}(s),m_{2}(s))\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{1}^{x,t}(y,s)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s=L\int_{t}^{T}\lambda e^{-\lambda(s-t)}d_{1}(m_{1}(s),m_{2}(s))\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s,

where we have used above that
\rho_{1}^{x,t}(\cdot,s)
is a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Using now (
4
) on the time interval
(s,t)
we get

d_{1}(m_{1}(s),m_{2}(s))\leq\left(d_{1}(m_{1}(t),m_{2}(t))+\tilde{L}\int_{t}^{s}\|\lambda Du_{1}(\cdot,r)-\lambda Du_{2}(\cdot,r)\|_{\infty}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r\right)e^{\tilde{L}(C+1)(s-t)}.

Plugging this estimate in the previous inequality we obtain

I\leq L\lambda\int_{t}^{T}e^{-(\lambda-\tilde{L}(C+1))(s-t)}d_{1}(m_{1}(t),m_{2}(t))\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s\\
+L\tilde{L}\lambda\int_{t}^{T}\int_{t}^{s}\|\lambda Du_{1}(\cdot,r)-\lambda Du_{2}(\cdot,r)\|_{\infty}e^{-(\lambda-\tilde{L}(C+1))(s-t)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s.

Note that, assuming
\lambda>\tilde{L}(C+1)
,

(16)

\lambda\int_{t}^{T}\int_{t}^{s}\|\lambda Du_{1}(\cdot,r)-\lambda Du_{2}(\cdot,r)\|_{\infty}e^{-(\lambda-\tilde{L}(C+1))(s-t)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s\\
=\lambda\int_{t}^{T}\left(\|\lambda Du_{1}(\cdot,r)-\lambda Du_{2}(\cdot,r)\|_{\infty}\int_{r}^{T}e^{-(\lambda-\tilde{L}(C+1))(s-t)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s\right)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r\\
\leq\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-\tilde{L}(C+1)}\int_{t}^{T}\|\lambda Du_{1}(\cdot,r)-\lambda Du_{2}(\cdot,r)\|_{\infty}e^{-(\lambda-\tilde{L}(C+1))(r-t)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r.

Therefore, for
\lambda\geq 2\tilde{L}(C+1)
,

I\leq\frac{L\lambda}{\lambda-\tilde{L}(C+1)}d_{1}(m_{1}(t),m_{2}(t))\\
+\frac{L\tilde{L}\lambda}{\lambda-\tilde{L}(C+1)}\int_{t}^{T}\|\lambda Du_{1}(\cdot,r)-\lambda Du_{2}(\cdot,r)\|_{\infty}e^{-(\lambda-\tilde{L}(C+1))(r-t)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r\\
\leq 2Ld_{1}(m_{1}(t),m_{2}(t))+2L\tilde{L}\int_{t}^{T}\|\lambda Du_{1}(\cdot,r)-\lambda Du_{2}(\cdot,r)\|_{\infty}e^{-(\lambda-\tilde{L}(C+1))(r-t)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r.

Regarding the term
II
, we use the Lipschitz regularity of
D_{x}H
from the assumption (H2) and from (
1
) to get

II\leq C_{o}(1+C)\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\lambda(s-t)}\|\lambda Du_{1}(\cdot,s)-\lambda Du_{2}(\cdot,s)\|_{\infty}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s.

To handle
III
, we first observe that, by the uniform bounds on
D^{2}_{xx}F
,
D^{2}_{xx}H
,
D^{2}_{xp}H
and
\lambda D^{2}u
, there exists a constant
\tilde{C}>0
depending on
C,C_{o}
such that (we use here that
\lambda\geq 1
)

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}F(y,m_{2}(s))-\lambda^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}H(\lambda Du_{2}(y,s),y)\Big)(\rho_{1}^{x,t}(y,s)-\rho_{2}^{x,t}(y,s))\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\right|\\
\leq\|D\big(\partial_{x_{i}}F(\cdot,m_{2}(s))-\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x_{i}}H(\lambda Du_{2}(\cdot,s),\cdot)\big)\|_{\infty}d_{1}\big(\rho_{1}^{x,t}(s),\rho_{2}^{x,t}(s)\big)\leq\tilde{C}d_{1}\big(\rho_{1}^{x,t}(s),\rho_{2}^{x,t}(s)\big).

Moreover, using (
4
) with
\rho_{1}^{x,t}
and
\rho_{2}^{x,t}
that share the same initial condition, and so
d_{1}(\rho_{1}^{x,t}(t),\rho_{2}^{x,t}(t))=0
, we find

d_{1}(\rho_{1}^{x,t}(s),\rho_{2}^{x,t}(s))\leq\ \tilde{L}e^{\tilde{L}(C+1)(s-t)}\int_{t}^{s}\|\lambda Du_{1}(\cdot,r)-\lambda Du_{2}(\cdot,r)\|_{\infty}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r.

Therefore,

III\leq\tilde{C}\tilde{L}\lambda\int_{t}^{T}\int_{t}^{s}e^{-(\lambda-\tilde{L}(C+1))(s-t)}\|\lambda Du_{1}(\cdot,r)-\lambda Du_{2}(\cdot,r)\|_{\infty}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}s.

Arguing as in (
16
), we get that

III\leq 2\tilde{C}\tilde{L}\int_{t}^{T}\|\lambda Du_{1}(\cdot,r)-\lambda Du_{2}(\cdot,r)\|_{\infty}e^{-(\lambda-\tilde{L}(C+1))(r-t)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r.

As far as the term
IV
is concerned, thanks to the estimates on
\|\lambda Du^{i}\|
,

IV\leq\lambda e^{-\lambda T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u_{1}(y,T)\Big|\rho_{1}^{x,t}(y,T)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y+\lambda e^{-\lambda T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u_{2}(y,T)\Big|\rho_{2}^{x,t}(y,T)\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}y\leq 2Ce^{-\lambda T}.

Finally, putting the estimates on
I,II,III,IV
together in (
15
) we obtain

\left|\lambda\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u_{1}(x,t)-\lambda\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u_{2}(x,t)\right|\leq 2Ld_{1}(m_{1}(t),m_{2}(t))+2Ce^{-\lambda T}\\
+(2L^{2}+C_{o}(1+C)+2\tilde{C}L)\int_{t}^{T}\|\lambda Du_{1}(\cdot,r)-\lambda Du_{2}(\cdot,r)\|_{\infty}e^{-(\lambda-L(C+1))(r-t)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r.

Taking then the supremum with respect to
i=1,\dots,n
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
gives

\|\lambda Du_{1}(\cdot,t)-\lambda Du_{2}(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}\leq 2Ld_{1}(m_{1}(t),m_{2}(t))+2Ce^{-\lambda T}\\
+(2L\tilde{L}+C_{o}(1+C)+2\tilde{C}L)\int_{t}^{T}\|\lambda Du_{1}(\cdot,r)-\lambda Du_{2}(\cdot,r)\|_{\infty}e^{-(\lambda-L(C+1))(r-t)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r.

The application of Grönwall’s Lemma
2.6
gives now the desired assertion (
13
)

\|\lambda Du_{1}(\cdot,t)-\lambda Du_{2}(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}\leq 2Ld_{1}(m_{1}(t),m_{2}(t))+(1+T(2L^{2}+C_{o}(1+C)+2\tilde{C}L))2Ce^{-\lambda T}\\
+2L(2L^{2}+C_{o}(1+C)+2\tilde{C}L)\int_{t}^{T}d_{1}(m_{1}(r),m_{2}(r))e^{-(\lambda-\tilde{L}(C+1)-2L\tilde{L}-C_{o}(1+C)-2\tilde{C}\tilde{L})(r-t)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r,

where
\eta
is chosen as the maximum between
\tilde{L}(C+1)+2\tilde{L}^{2}+\tilde{L}+2\tilde{C}\tilde{L}
and
2\tilde{L}(C+1)
; we also use here that
Te^{-\lambda T}\leq C^{\prime}e^{-\lambda T/2}
for some universal constant
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}(\lambda_{0})
. Finally, for all
t\geq 0
, letting
T\to\infty
in (
13
), we conclude that (
14
) is fulfilled.
∎

We are now ready to state and prove the main uniqueness result.

Theorem 3.4
.

There exists a constant
\eta^{\prime}
depending on
L,C_{o}
such that, if
\lambda\geq\Lambda=\max\{\lambda_{0},\eta^{\prime}\}
, then (

MFG
λ

) has a unique solution, in the following sense: given two couples
(u_{1},m_{1})
,
(u_{2},m_{2})
solving (

MFG
λ

), and satisfying the estimates (i)–(iii) of Proposition
2.1
, then
u_{1}\equiv u_{2}
and
m_{1}\equiv m_{2}
.

Proof.

We start by assuming that
\lambda>\max\{\lambda_{0},\eta\}
, where
\lambda_{0},\eta
are as in previous propositions. Let
T>0
, and
(u_{1},m_{1})
,
(u_{2},m_{2})
be two solutions to (

MFG
λ

). Let
\xi
be a random variable in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\mathcal{L}(\xi)=m_{0}
, and let
X^{1}
,
X^{2}
be solutions to the SDEs

\begin{cases}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}X^{i}_{t}=-D_{p}H(\lambda Du_{i}(X^{i}_{t},t),X^{i}_{t})\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}t+\sqrt{2}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}B_{t}\\
X^{i}_{0}=\xi\qquad i=1,2.\end{cases}

Note that
\mathcal{L}(X^{i}_{t})=m_{i}(t)
for all
t\in[0,T]
and
i=1,2
. Arguing as in the proof of Proposition
2.2
and using that
X^{1}_{0}=\xi=X^{2}_{0}
, we get, for all
s\in[0,T]
,

\mathbb{E}|X^{1}_{s}-X^{2}_{s}|\leq\tilde{L}(C+1)\int_{0}^{s}\lvert X^{1}_{t}-X^{2}_{t}\rvert\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}t+\tilde{L}\int_{0}^{s}\|\lambda Du_{1}(\cdot,t)-\lambda Du_{2}(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}t.

Since
d_{1}(m_{1}(t),m_{2}(t))\leq\mathbb{E}|X^{1}_{t}-X^{2}_{t}|
, by the estimate (
13
) in Proposition
3.3
we have

(17)

\mathbb{E}|X^{1}_{s}-X^{2}_{s}|\leq\tilde{L}(C+1+K)\int_{0}^{s}\mathbb{E}\lvert X^{1}_{t}-X^{2}_{t}\rvert\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}t+\tilde{L}K\int_{0}^{s}\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}\lvert X^{1}_{r}-X^{2}_{r}\rvert e^{-(\lambda-\eta)(r-t)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}t\\
+KTe^{-\lambda T/2}.

We focus now on the second term of the right-hand side, that can be manipulated as follows

\int_{0}^{s}\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}\lvert X^{1}_{r}-X^{2}_{r}\rvert e^{-(\lambda-\eta)(r-t)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}t\\
=\int_{0}^{s}\mathbb{E}\lvert X^{1}_{r}-X^{2}_{r}\rvert\int_{0}^{r}e^{-(\lambda-\eta)(r-t)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}t\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r+\int_{s}^{T}\mathbb{E}\lvert X^{1}_{r}-X^{2}_{r}\rvert\int_{0}^{s}e^{-(\lambda-\eta)(r-t)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}t\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r\\
\leq\frac{1}{\lambda-\eta}\int_{0}^{s}\mathbb{E}\lvert X^{1}_{r}-X^{2}_{r}\rvert\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r+\frac{1}{\lambda-\eta}\int_{s}^{T}\mathbb{E}\lvert X^{1}_{r}-X^{2}_{r}\rvert e^{-(\lambda-\eta)(r-s)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r,\\
\leq\int_{0}^{s}\mathbb{E}\lvert X^{1}_{r}-X^{2}_{r}\rvert\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r+\int_{s}^{T}\mathbb{E}\lvert X^{1}_{r}-X^{2}_{r}\rvert e^{-(\lambda-\eta)(r-s)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r,

where in the last inequality we used that
\lambda\geq\eta+1
. Plugging the previous estimate into (
17
) we find that

\mathbb{E}|X^{1}_{s}-X^{2}_{s}|\leq\tilde{L}(C+1+2K)\int_{0}^{s}\mathbb{E}\lvert X^{1}_{t}-X^{2}_{t}\rvert\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}t+\tilde{L}K\int_{s}^{T}\mathbb{E}\lvert X^{1}_{r}-X^{2}_{r}\rvert e^{-(\lambda-\eta)(r-s)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r\\
+KTe^{-\lambda T/2},

for all
t\in[0,T]
and for any
T>0
. Taking the limit
T\to\infty
we finally obatin

\mathbb{E}|X^{1}_{s}-X^{2}_{s}|\leq\tilde{L}(C+1+2K)\int_{0}^{s}\mathbb{E}\lvert X^{1}_{t}-X^{2}_{t}\rvert\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}t+\tilde{L}K\int_{s}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\lvert X^{1}_{r}-X^{2}_{r}\rvert e^{-(\lambda-\eta)(r-s)}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}r,

for all
s\geq 0
.

We now check that Lemma
2.5
applies to
f(t)=\mathbb{E}|X^{1}_{t}-X^{2}_{t}|
. Assuming that

\lambda-\eta\geq 4\tilde{L}(C+1+2K)+4\tilde{L}K,

we just need to show that
\mathbb{E}|X^{1}_{t}-X^{2}_{t}|\leq Me^{4\tilde{L}(C+1+2K)t}
for all
t
. This is a consequence of Proposition
2.2
(see in particular (
5
)) and the estimates on
\lambda Du^{i}
: for all
t\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}|X^{1}_{t}-X^{2}_{t}|\leq\tilde{L}e^{\tilde{L}(C+1)}t\int_{0}^{t}\|\lambda Du_{1}(\cdot,t)-\lambda Du_{2}(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}\hskip 0.7pt{\rm d}t\leq 2C\tilde{L}te^{\tilde{L}(C+1)t}\leq 2\tilde{C}\tilde{L}e^{2\tilde{L}(C+1)t}.

Therefore,
d_{1}(m_{1}(t),m_{2}(t))\leq\mathbb{E}|X^{1}_{t}-X^{2}_{t}|=0
for all
t
, hence
m_{1}\equiv m_{2}
. To get this final step, we need to assume that
\lambda\geq\eta^{\prime}:=\eta+4\tilde{L}(C+1+2K)+4\tilde{L}K
.

Now, estimate (
14
) in Proposition
3.3
together with
m_{1}=m_{2}
yields
\|\lambda Du_{1}(\cdot,t)-\lambda Du_{2}(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}=0
, for all
t\geq 0
. Hence,
\lambda Du_{1}=\lambda Du_{2}
. From the representation formula (
8
), using that
m_{1}=m_{2}
and
\lambda Du_{1}=\lambda Du_{2}
, we obtain
u_{1}-u_{2}=0
, namely
u_{1}=u_{2}
.

∎

Remark 3.5

(Uniqueness for finite horizon problems)
.

Uniqueness of solutions is obtained for (

MFG
λ

), that is set on the time horizon
[0,\infty)
. Solutions are naturally those that come from limits of the same system of PDE on
[0,T]
, where the final condition
u(T)=0
is imposed, see (
2
). We observe that the same uniqueness result holds for such a family of problems, and the uniqueness threshold for
\lambda
is in fact independent of
T
; this can be obtained arguing in the very same way (there is no need to take
T\to\infty
in the previous proofs). On the other hand, the uniform convergence result stated in Proposition
3.1
holds on time intervals
[0,\tau]\subsetneqq[0,T]
; this is expected, since
\lambda Du
needs to deviate from
DF
close to
T
to reach the final condition.

One could certainly replace the final condition on
u(T)
with nonzero functions. We cannot exclude the possibility that other choices may lead to a completely different behavior of the system.

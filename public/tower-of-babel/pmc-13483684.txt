Predicting sublingual immunotherapy efficacy in allergic rhinitis
Authors: Jiayue Wang, Xiaoning Zhu, Zan Ding
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSublingual immunotherapy (SLIT) efficacy for allergic rhinitis (AR) varies considerably, with 30%–40% of patients showing poor response. A reliable tool integrating multidimensional factors for individualized efficacy prediction remains lacking. This study aimed to construct an optimal prediction model incorporating clinical characteristics, environmental exposure factors, and immune-inflammatory indicators to predict SLIT efficacy in AR patients, and further establish a nomogram as an auxiliary interpretable tool for intuitive clinical application.Materials and methodsA total of 346 AR patients receiving SLIT were included and randomly allocated to training (n = 242) and validation (n = 104) cohorts at a 7:3 ratio. Baseline data included demographics, clinical features, symptom scores, environmental exposures, and immune-inflammatory indicators. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were performed to screen independent predictive factors. Three models, including random forest, support vector machine, and conventional logistic regression, were developed for performance comparison. Model performance was evaluated using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), calibration curves, and decision curve analysis (DCA). On the basis of independent predictors, a nomogram was constructed for visual interpretation. Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) analysis was further applied to interpret feature importance of the optimal model.ResultsMultivariate logistic regression confirmed these same seven variables as independent predictors of SLIT clinical efficacy in AR: disease duration, baseline symptom score, baseline medication score, air conditioning usage time, specific immunoglobulin E/total immunoglobulin E (sIgE/tIgE) ratio, interleukin (IL)-4, and IL-10 (all P < 0.05). The random forest model yielded optimal predictive performance, while the nomogram built on these predictors achieved acceptable discrimination with AUCs of 0.757 (95% CI: 0.676–0.837) in the training cohort and 0.729 (95% CI: 0.596–0.862) in the validation cohort, and showed better efficacy than the support vector machine (0.739) and conventional logistic regression (0.709) models. Calibration curves demonstrated good agreement between predicted probabilities and observed risks for the nomogram. DCA indicated that the model provided a high clinical net benefit across a wide range of threshold probabilities. SHAP analysis identified disease duration, sIgE/tIgE ratio, and baseline medication score as the three most influential features contributing to model predictions.ConclusionThe developed random forest model presents good discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility for predicting SLIT efficacy. The corresponding nomogram further enables intuitive individualized clinical assessment, providing a quantitative basis for personalized SLIT decision-making in AR patients.Keywords: Allergic rhinitis, Sublingual immunotherapy, Efficacy prediction, Predictive model, Nomogram, Random forest

Introduction

Allergic rhinitis (AR) is one of the most common chronic inflammatory diseases in otorhinolaryngology–head and neck surgery, with a global prevalence ranging from 10% to 40% that is increasing annually, resulting in a substantial disease burden [1]. Sublingual immunotherapy, as the only currently available etiological treatment capable of modifying the natural course of AR, induces immune tolerance and significantly improves patient symptoms, reduces the use of symptomatic medication, and enhances quality of life [2]. Studies have demonstrated that patients completing a full course of standardized sublingual immunotherapy achieve long-term efficacy lasting several years. However, significant individual variability exists in treatment response, with approximately 30% to 40% of patients showing no substantial symptom improvement. Currently, reliable tools for accurately predicting the efficacy of sublingual immunotherapy are lacking in clinical practice, potentially subjecting some patients to 2–3 years of ineffective treatment and delaying optimal intervention [3].

The efficacy of sublingual immunotherapy for AR is influenced by a complex interplay of factors, including genetic background, clinical characteristics, and immune status [4]. Disease duration, which reflects the chronicity of the disease, has been confirmed by multiple studies to be closely associated with sublingual immunotherapy efficacy, with earlier intervention potentially leading to better treatment responses [5]. Baseline symptom scores and baseline medication scores, which reflect disease severity, evaluate the patient’s pre-treatment disease control status from different dimensions [6]. Regarding environmental exposure factors, such as air conditioning usage duration, a recent study demonstrated a negative correlation between air conditioning usage duration and the short-term efficacy of sublingual immunotherapy in pediatric AR patients [7]. The ratio of specific immunoglobulin E to total immunoglobulin E (sIgE/tIgE ratio), which reflects the intensity of the allergen-specific immune response, has recently been confirmed as a core predictor of sublingual immunotherapy efficacy, exhibiting superior predictive performance compared to a single sIgE level [8]. At the immuno-inflammatory level, the baseline level of interleukin (IL)-4, a key effector cytokine of the Th2-type immune response, has been associated with sublingual immunotherapy efficacy [9]. Furthermore, baseline levels of IL-10, a functional marker of regulatory T (Treg) cells, were significantly lower in patients with effective response to SLIT [10]. Lower baseline IL-10 and Treg levels suggest that the immune regulatory system is not exhausted by chronic allergic inflammation, which enables SLIT to efficiently induce immune tolerance and thus predict better treatment efficacy. Nonetheless, the combined impact of the aforementioned clinical characteristics, environmental factors, and immuno-inflammatory markers on the final efficacy outcome of sublingual immunotherapy is complex, and a comprehensive prediction model integrating multidimensional information is currently lacking.

Machine learning algorithms can efficiently process complex clinical data and capture potential nonlinear relationships and interactions among variables, offering unique advantages in constructing high-dimensional prediction models. Consequently, this study aims to systematically integrate multidimensional data, including clinical characteristics, environmental exposure factors, and immuno-inflammatory markers, to establish an optimal prediction model using machine learning methods, with a nomogram further constructed as an interpretable auxiliary tool for predicting the clinical efficacy of sublingual immunotherapy in patients with AR. The goal is to provide a quantitative decision-making tool for early clinical risk stratification and individualized treatment planning.

Materials and methods

Study population

A total of 346 AR patients who received sublingual immunotherapy at the Department of Otorhinolaryngology–Head and Neck Surgery in our hospital between January 2023 and December 2024 were included. Inclusion criteria were: (1) age 5–65 years; (2) diagnosis of moderate-to-severe persistent AR according to the diagnostic criteria outlined in the Guidelines for Diagnosis and Treatment of AR [11]; (3) confirmation of house dust mite as the primary allergen via skin prick test or serum-specific immunoglobulin E (IgE) testing (single dust mite allergy or dust mite-dominated multiple allergies). In this study, primary allergen is defined as the dominant allergen mainly triggering clinical symptoms with the highest sensitization titer; major allergen refers to all sensitized allergen categories including house dust mite, pollen and others; dust mite-dominated multiple allergy indicates concomitant sensitization to pollen or other allergens, while house dust mite remains the dominant symptomatic and sensitizing allergen. Serum specific IgE and specific IgG4 were exclusively detected against the main house dust mite allergens, Dermatophagoides pteronyssinus (Der p 1) and Dermatophagoides farinae (Der f 1). (4) completion of at least one year of standardized house dust mite allergen sublingual immunotherapy; (5) availability of complete clinical assessment data before and after treatment. Exclusion criteria were: (1) presence of comorbid conditions potentially affecting efficacy assessment, such as asthma, nasal polyps, or severe nasal septum deviation; (2) prior receipt of any form of immunotherapy; (3) history of autoimmune diseases, immunodeficiency disorders, or malignancy; (4) pregnancy or lactation; (5) incomplete clinical data precluding accurate group assignment. Based on the rule of 10 events per predictor variable (EPV) for prediction model development, with seven candidate predictors and an anticipated ineffective rate of 30%–40%, a minimum of 175–234 patients was required. The final 346 patients (73 ineffective cases in the training cohort) met this requirement.

Outcome definition

The primary outcome of this study was clinical efficacy after one year of sublingual immunotherapy. Clinical efficacy was defined as a ≥ 50% reduction in the total nasal symptom score (TNSS) from baseline after one year of treatment [12]. The TNSS comprises four nasal symptoms: rhinorrhea, nasal congestion, sneezing, and nasal itching. Each symptom is rated from 0 to 3 (0 = none, 1 = mild, 2 = moderate, 3 = severe), yielding a total score ranging from 0 to 12. Notably, this efficacy evaluation was based solely on nasal symptom improvement and does not include medication score, which differs from the conventional combined symptom and medication score widely used in immunotherapy studies. Symptom scores and medication scores were evaluated by the same trained physicians for all patients at baseline and at the 1-year follow-up. All patients were further divided into effective and ineffective groups according to the degree of nasal symptom improvement.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using SPSS 26.0, R 4.2.3, and Python 3.8.5 software. Normally distributed continuous data were presented as mean ± standard deviation, with comparisons between groups using independent samples t-tests. Categorical data were presented as counts (percentages), with comparisons between groups using chi-squared (χ²) tests.

The total sample was randomly divided into a training set (242 cases) and a validation set (104 cases) at a 7:3 ratio. Baseline characteristics were compared between the two sets using t-tests and χ² tests to ensure they were balanced and comparable (P > 0.05). In the training set, univariate analysis was initially performed to identify potential predictors with P < 0.05. Subsequently, LASSO regression was applied for further variable selection, and the retained variables were incorporated into multivariate logistic regression analysis to determine independent predictors of clinical efficacy following sublingual immunotherapy.

A nomogram prediction model was constructed based on the results of the multivariate logistic regression analysis. Internal validation was performed using the Bootstrap method (1000 resamples). The model’s discriminatory ability was assessed by calculating the concordance index (C-index), and its calibration was evaluated by plotting calibration curves.

Three machine learning algorithms—random forest, support vector machine, and logistic regression—were subsequently applied to construct comparative prediction models. The predictive performance of each model was compared between the training and validation sets using the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) to identify the optimal prediction model. Pairwise comparisons of AUC between models were performed using DeLong’s test, with Bonferroni correction applied for multiple comparisons. The calibration of each prediction model was assessed using the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test, with P > 0.05 indicating adequate calibration. Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) analysis was used to interpret the contribution of each feature and the prediction logic within the optimal machine learning model. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was employed to evaluate the clinical net benefit of the model across different threshold probabilities, compared with the extreme strategies of “treating all” or “treating none.” All hypothesis tests were two-sided, and a P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Comparison of baseline characteristics between the training and validation sets

No statistically significant differences were observed in any baseline characteristics between the training set (n = 242) and the validation set (n = 104) (P > 0.05). Specifically, the two groups demonstrated good balance in demographic characteristics (age, sex, BMI), disease-related characteristics (disease duration, allergen type, major allergen, presence of comorbid allergic diseases, family history of allergy), clinical symptom scores (baseline symptom score, baseline medication score, quality of life score), environmental factors (air conditioning usage duration, passive smoking exposure), and laboratory findings (serum total IgE, specific IgE, sIgE/tIgE ratio, specific IgG4, IL-4, IL-10, IL-35). These findings indicate that the random split of the dataset was successful, and the baseline characteristics of the training and validation sets were highly comparable, allowing for subsequent construction and internal validation of the prediction model (Table 1).

Multivariate logistic regression analysis of factors influencing the efficacy of sublingual immunotherapy for AR

Using whether patients achieved clinical effectiveness after sublingual immunotherapy (symptom score improvement ≥ 50%) as the dependent variable (0 = effective group, 1 = ineffective group), the seven indicators with statistical significance from the univariate analysis were all incorporated into a multivariate logistic regression model (Fig. 1). Notably, the sIgE/tIgE ratio was retained as a composite indicator, whereas its component variables (sIgE and tIgE) did not enter the multivariate model due to nonsignificant univariate results, thereby avoiding potential multicollinearity. The results of the multivariate logistic regression analysis indicated that all seven variables were independent predictors of SLIT efficacy in AR (P < 0.05) (Table 3).

Performance Evaluation of Machine Learning Models

Based on the seven key predictive variables identified by multivariate logistic regression analysis, this study further constructed three machine learning prediction models: Random Forest, Support Vector Machine, and Logistic Regression. This approach was undertaken to comprehensively evaluate and optimize the predictive performance for sublingual immunotherapy efficacy in AR.

Pairwise comparisons of AUC using DeLong’s test with Bonferroni correction revealed that in the training set, the Random Forest model (AUC = 0.757, 95% CI: 0.676–0.837) achieved a significantly higher AUC than the Logistic Regression model (AUC = 0.709, 95% CI: 0.623–0.795, P = 0.041), while no statistically significant difference was observed between Random Forest and Support Vector Machine (AUC = 0.739, 95% CI: 0.653–0.825,P = 0.312). In the validation set, no significant differences were detected among the three models after correction (all P > 0.05). The Random Forest model demonstrated superior discriminative ability in both the training and validation sets, exhibiting stable performance without significant overfitting. Consequently, it was designated as the optimal prediction model for this study (Fig. 2).

Receiver operating characteristic curve analysis of the prediction model in the training (A) and validation (B) sets

Calibration was further quantified using the Hosmer–Lemeshow test. In the training set, the Random Forest model demonstrated adequate calibration (χ²=8.34, P = 0.401), whereas the Support Vector Machine (χ²=15.67, P = 0.047) and Logistic Regression (χ²=18.22, P = 0.019) showed evidence of poor calibration. In the validation set, similar trends were observed, with only the Random Forest model maintaining adequate calibration (χ²=9.12, P = 0.332). These findings were consistent with the calibration curve analysis presented in Fig. 3, collectively indicating that the Random Forest model possessed superior predictive calibration.

Additionally, DCA (Fig. 4) confirmed that across a wide range of threshold probabilities, the clinical net benefit of applying the Random Forest prediction model was consistently significantly higher than the extreme strategies of “treating all” or “treating none,” and was markedly superior to the Support Vector Machine and Logistic Regression models. This demonstrates its outstanding clinical utility.

Interpretability assessment of model predictions

Utilizing the seven core predictive variables identified by multivariate logistic regression analysis, his study further constructed a nomogram model based on the multivariate logistic regression results. This model was developed to visually represent the contribution of each predictive factor to the clinical effectiveness of sublingual immunotherapy for AR. As shown in Fig. 5, the nomogram intuitively displays the direction and weight of the impact of each clinical characteristic and immune-inflammatory indicator on the probability of treatment benefit.

SHAP analysis based on the Random Forest model further quantified the relative importance of each feature, with the order of influence from largest to smallest being: disease duration (X1), sIgE/tIgE ratio (X5), baseline medication score (X3), duration of air conditioner use (X4), IL-10 (X7), IL-4 (X6), and baseline symptom score (X2). Notably, disease duration, reflecting the chronicity of the disease, contributed most prominently to efficacy prediction, with its SHAP value significantly exceeding that of other variables. The sIgE/tIgE ratio, reflecting the intensity of the allergen-specific immune response, and the baseline medication score, reflecting disease severity, followed closely, together forming the core indicator combination for efficacy prediction.

The results of the SHAP analysis were highly consistent with the conclusions of the multivariate logistic regression, collectively confirming the central role of disease duration, sIgE/tIgE ratio, and baseline medication score in predicting the response to sublingual immunotherapy. Furthermore, this analysis revealed the significant impact of environmental factors such as duration of air conditioner use and immune-inflammatory markers including IL-10 and IL-4 on treatment outcomes.

Discussion

This study successfully developed and validated a random forest model for predicting the clinical efficacy of SLIT in AR, utilizing multidimensional data encompassing clinical characteristics, environmental exposure factors, and immuno-inflammatory markers. The model demonstrated robust discriminatory ability in both the training and validation cohorts, achieving an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.757 in each, which outperformed support vector machine (0.739) and logistic regression (0.709) models. Through multivariate logistic regression analysis, seven independent predictors were identified: disease duration, baseline symptom score, baseline medication score, duration of air conditioner use, sIgE/tIgE ratio, IL-4, and IL-10. Furthermore, by integrating nomogram and SHAP analyses for model interpretability, this study not only quantified the relative importance of each predictor but also elucidated the direction of its association with SLIT clinical efficacy.

The seven core predictors ultimately identified in this study encompass multiple dimensions, including the degree of disease chronicity, disease severity, environmental exposure factors, and immune-inflammatory status. These factors collectively reflect the complex biological processes that influence SLIT efficacy in AR [13]. SHAP analysis revealed the predictors in descending order of impact: disease duration, sIgE/tIgE ratio, baseline medication score, duration of air conditioner use, IL-10, IL-4, and baseline symptom score. Disease duration emerged as the most influential contributor to the model’s predictions [14]. A longer disease duration correlated with a lower probability of achieving clinical efficacy. This association may be attributed to immune tolerance or exhaustion induced by long-term chronic inflammation [15]. Specifically, chronic persistent allergen exposure may lead to regulatory T cell dysfunction or the consolidation of Th2 immune responses, thereby diminishing the therapeutic effect of SLIT [16]. This finding suggests that early intervention may represent a critical strategy for enhancing SLIT efficacy [17].

The sIgE/tIgE ratio, an indicator of the intensity of allergen-specific immune responses, demonstrated significant predictive value in both univariate analysis and the multivariate model. Its importance in the SHAP analysis was second only to disease duration. This study found that a higher sIgE/tIgE ratio was associated with a greater probability of achieving clinical efficacy, a finding consistent with previous research [18]. Compared with sIgE levels alone, the sIgE/tIgE ratio more accurately reflects the proportion of allergen-specific immune responses within the total immunoglobulin profile, serving as a superior indicator for assessing patient allergic status. A high sIgE/tIgE ratio may suggest a more robust immune recognition capacity towards specific allergens, potentially rendering patients more likely to benefit from SLIT.

Baseline medication score, which reflects the degree of medication dependence required for symptom control, was identified as a significant independent predictor in this study. Higher medication scores indicated a greater medication burden needed to control symptoms, signifying more severe disease and greater management difficulty. Such patients often exhibit poorer outcomes with SLIT [19]. This finding underscores the importance of considering patients’ medication dependence, in addition to symptom severity, when evaluating candidacy for SLIT.

Duration of air conditioner use, an environmental exposure factor, was confirmed as an independent predictor of SLIT efficacy. Prolonged air conditioner use correlated with a lower probability of achieving clinical efficacy. This relationship may be associated with indoor air dryness, increased allergen concentration, and impaired respiratory mucosal barrier function resulting from air conditioner use [20]. Consequently, this finding suggests that patient education during SLIT should incorporate environmental control recommendations, and that reducing air conditioner use may potentially improve treatment outcomes.

IL-10 and IL-4, representative cytokines of Treg cells and Th2 cells, respectively, both exhibited significant predictive value. Higher baseline IL-10 levels were associated with a greater probability of achieving clinical efficacy, consistent with its role in promoting immune tolerance [21]. Conversely, higher baseline IL-4 levels were associated with a lower probability of clinical efficacy, aligning with its function as a core Th2 effector cytokine driving allergic inflammation [22]. These findings support the classical understanding that a favorable Treg/Th2 balance at baseline may predict better SLIT outcomes. Notably, although baseline symptom score achieved statistical significance (P < 0.05) in the multivariate model, its confidence interval upper limit approached 1, and SHAP analysis indicated its relative contribution was the lowest among the seven factors, suggesting a comparatively weaker effect within the multivariate context.

A core methodological strength of this study lies in its effective integration of traditional statistical methods with modern machine learning techniques. Candidate variables were initially screened using univariate analysis, followed by confirmation of their independent predictive value via multivariate logistic regression. This approach ensured statistical rigor and clinical interpretability throughout the model development process. Subsequently, a random forest algorithm was employed as the primary predictive model, leveraging its capacity to handle complex nonlinear relationships and high-dimensional interactions, and it achieved superior predictive accuracy compared with the support vector machine and logistic regression models. Meanwhile, the nomogram was adopted as an auxiliary visualized tool to improve clinical interpretability. Critically, this study applied the SHAP interpretability framework to elucidate the “black box” machine learning model. This analysis not only objectively quantified the global contribution ranking of each feature but also revealed the specific direction and magnitude of each feature’s impact on individual case predictions. This “white-box” approach enhances clinicians’ understanding and trust in the model’s decision-making logic, representing a crucial step towards translating predictive models from research settings to clinical practice.

Several limitations should be acknowledged in this study. First, as a single-center investigation, despite rigorous internal validation, the generalizability of the model to broader populations, different allergen types, and diverse healthcare settings requires further validation through multicenter, prospective external cohorts. Second, this study only included patients who completed at least one year of standardized SLIT with complete clinical data, while those with early treatment discontinuation, poor medication compliance, adverse events, or loss to follow-up were excluded. This enrollment design may introduce selection bias and tend to overestimate the predictive performance of the model. Moreover, the current cohort lacks real-world patients with heterogeneous compliance and treatment persistence, and further prospective multicenter cohorts covering all newly diagnosed AR patients are required to externally verify and optimize the model for early pretreatment prediction in routine clinical practice. Third, the model incorporated only baseline static variables, failing to capture dynamic changes in immune-inflammatory markers over the course of treatment, which may contain critical information for predicting efficacy. Future research could explore developing dynamic prediction models that integrate early treatment-related changes. Finally, the sample size in this study was relatively limited, and subgroup analyses based on different allergen types (e.g., monosensitization vs. polysensitization) or age groups (children vs. adults) were not performed. Given that immune response mechanisms and treatment response characteristics may vary across subgroups, the development of subgroup-specific, refined models represents a promising direction for future research.

In summary, this study successfully developed and validated a random forest prediction model for SLIT efficacy in AR by integrating clinical characteristics, environmental exposure factors, and immune-inflammatory markers, with a nomogram applied for auxiliary visual interpretation. The model exhibits favorable discrimination and calibration, and interpretability analysis revealed the contribution weights of each predictor. These findings underscore the necessity of considering multidimensional information—including disease chronicity, severity, environmental exposure, and immune-inflammatory status—in evaluating SLIT efficacy for AR. This model, serving as a promising clinical decision-support tool, provides an objective quantitative basis for precisely identifying patients most likely to benefit from SLIT, formulating individualized treatment plans, and optimizing therapeutic strategies. Future efforts should focus on prospective external validation and explore deeper integration of dynamic monitoring data with multi-omics information to continuously enhance the model’s clinical utility and application value.

NasalSeg: A Dataset for Automatic Segmentation of Nasal Cavity and Paranasal Sinuses from 3D CT Images
Authors: Yichi Zhang, Jing Wang, Tan Pan, Quanling Jiang, Jingjie Ge, Xin Guo, Chen Jiang, Jie Lu, Jianning Zhang, Xueling Liu, Mei Tian, Yuan Qi, Yuan Cheng, Chuantao Zuo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractModern facial surgical planning and therapeutic strategies rely heavily on the precise segmentation of the nasal cavity and paranasal sinuses from computed tomography (CT) images for quantitative analysis. Nevertheless, manual segmentation is labor-intensive and prone to inconsistencies, highlighting the need for automatic segmentation methods. A significant challenge in this field is the lack of publicly available clinical datasets for research. To address this issue, we introduce NagalSeg, the first large-scale, publicly available dataset for nasal cavity and paranasal sinus segmentation. In comparison to existing nasal structure segmentation datasets, which are either private or small-scale, NagalSeg stands out as the first publicly accessible dataset. It provides an order of magnitude more labeled data, consisting of 130 3D CT scans with pixel-wise annotations of five anatomical structures: the left nasal cavity, right nasal cavity, nasopharynx, left maxillary sinus, and right maxillary sinus. The NagalSeg dataset serves as an open-access resource to facilitate the development and evaluation of segmentation algorithms and promote future in-depth research towards the clinical application of artificial intelligence methods.Subject terms: Medical research, Diseases, Anatomy

Background & Summary

Understanding the morphology of the nasal cavity and paranasal sinuses is crucial for clinical practice. It improves the detection of paranasal sinus disease, provides information on therapeutic approaches, and facilitates the planning and simulation of endonasal surgery1,2. Additionally, the segmentation data is of great importance for diagnosis, surgical preparation and determining the areas of robot-assisted systems. Quantifying the volume or surface area of the nasal cavity is essential to assess its health status and understand the effects of inhaled therapeutic or toxic substances3. Detailed anatomical knowledge is essential for effective endoscopic sinus surgery, as 3D structures are required for the reconstruction of the lateral nasal wall and septum and for the objective evaluation of surgical outcomes4,5. To this end, accurate segmentation to delineate the outlines of nasal cavity and paranasal sinuses from Computed tomography (CT) images is an essential procedure for quantitative evaluation and analysis, so as to assist in automated diagnosis and treatment planning.

Nevertheless, manual and semi-automatic segmentation of these structures is time-consuming and labor-intensive, especially for 3D medical images where experts need to delineate the target from the volume slice by slice, which is often considered impractical in clinical settings6,7. To enhance efficiency and accuracy, developing automatic segmentation methods plays a crucial role and has gained increasing attention in clinical research. Recently, many deep learning-based methods have been widely developed and achieved cutting-edge performance in many medical image segmentation tasks8–10. However, due to the complexity of the anatomy of the nasal cavity and paranasal sinuses, it is unknown how these deep-learning methods perform in this task. Since the development and evaluation of deep learning-based segmentation methods heavily rely on access to large-scale annotated datasets11, the major challenge lies in the lack of publicly available clinical datasets for research. Therefore, developing large-scale high-quality datasets and building benchmarks for the nasal structure segmentation task is vital to boost these unsolved problems.

Existing works on nasal segmentation were evaluated on the limited number of in-house datasets and kept their annotations private. As summarized in Table 1, Huang et al.12 evaluated the segmentation framework on 30 CT scans with annotation of the nasal cavity and paranasal sinuses. Laura et al.13 conducted experiments on 10 CT images acquired for diagnosis in the nasal cavity and paranasal sinuses region. Choi et al.14 trained a convolutional neural network using cone-beam CT (CBCT) images acquired from 45 patients for maxillary sinus segmentation. These methods are all evaluated on small number of private datasets and there is not an existing large-scale publicly available annotated dataset for this task.

To address these challenges and accelerate the research progress, we introduce NasalSeg, the first large-scale open-access annotated dataset for developing segmentation algorithms for nasal cavities and paranasal sinuses from 3D CT images. The NasalSeg dataset consists of 130 CT scans with pixel-wise manual annotation of 5 nasal structures in great detail, including the left nasal cavity, right nasal cavity, nasal pharynx, left maxillary sinus, and right maxillary sinus. An example of an image and annotation from the NasalSeg dataset is shown in Fig. 1. Moreover, we split into five pre-defined folds for cross-validation. This NasalSeg dataset is expected to serve as a valuable resource to facilitate the development and evaluation of algorithms for automatic segmentation of nasal cavities and paranasal sinuses, ultimately contributing to automated diagnosis and treatment planning in real clinical scenarios.

Methods

Data collection

The dataset was retrospectively collected in accordance with the relevant ethical regulations established by the Institutional Review Board of Huashan Hospital, Fudan University (No. KY2024-1345). Given that this dataset was gathered retrospectively and all sensitive patient information was anonymized using the 3D Slicer tool (https://www.slicer.org/), thereby ensuring that the rights and health of the subjects were not adversely affected, the ethics committee waived the necessity for researchers to obtain informed consent from patients.

The NasalSeg dataset consists of 130 head CT scans collected from 130 independent patients, including 74 males and 56 females. The age range of the patients is from 24 to 82 (Avg ± Std = 54.6 ± 12.1). All of the images are scanned using a Biograph 64 scanner (Siemens, Erlangen, Germany) at the Department of Nuclear Medicine/PET center, Huashan Hospital. The dataset has a median shape of 148 × 512 × 512, with an in-plane resolution of 0.586 mm × 0.586 mm and slice spacing of 1.5 mm.

Image annotation

To enable the development and evaluation of automatic segmentation algorithms, the CT images of NasalSeg dataset have undergone meticulous pixel-wise manual annotation performed by three highly trained junior annotators (with one to five years of experience) and then checked with two senior annotators (with five to ten years of experience). Finally, the annotations are verified by four experienced experts specializing in radiology and otolaryngology (with more than 20 years of experience).

In the NasalSeg dataset, all the CT scans are annotated with 5 anatomical structures including the left nasal cavity, right nasal cavity, nasal pharynx, left maxillary sinus, and right maxillary sinus. The annotation process involved pixel-wise annotation, where each pixel was carefully labelled to identify to different structures or background. The manual annotation and refined procedure of segmentation is adopted in 3D Slicer 5.6.2 (Asan Medical Center Nuclear Medicine Image Quantification Toolkit of Excellence), a software tool available at https://www.slicer.org/. Air thresholding was set at −400 to 1000 Hounsfield units (HU) for all scans. Then, the filter module (i.e. LaplacianSharpeningImageFilter) was used to highlight regions with rapid intensity changes and thus enhance the edges, followed by the Segment Editor module for segmentation.

Due to the high annotation cost of annotating large-scale 3D medical image segmentation datasets, we adopt a human-in-the-loop procedure to facilitate the annotation procedure. As shown in Fig. 2, the annotation procedure can be organized into the following key steps:

The workflow of image annotation procedure of the establishment of NasalSeg dataset.

Step 1. Manually annotate a small subset

To begin, we manually annotate 21 cases from total data from scratch for the development of the initial segmentation model. Three highly trained junior annotators perform the annotation.

Step 2. Develop the initial version of the segmentation model

Based on the annotated subset, we train a segmentation model based on nnUNet15 as the initial segmentation model. Then the initial segmentation model is utilized to infer the remaining cases and generate pseudo-segmentation masks.

Step 3. Refine the coarse segmentation masks

Building upon the output of the initial segmentation model, the coarse segmentation masks of remaining cases are then manually refined by the annotators.

Step 4. Re-train the model for cross-validation

After acquiring the annotation of all 130 cases, we split the data into five folds for cross-validation.

Step 5. Checking and correcting by senior experts

To ensure the accuracy of annotation, after cross validation, cases with lower scores are double-checked by the senior experts.

Data Records

All data records16 are available on the official Zenodo repository at https://zenodo.org/records/13893419. Each CT scan and corresponding label are stored as single NNRD files. The unzipped file is organized into 2 folders, named “images” and “labels”, that contain the original CT images and corresponding ground truth images, respectively. As illustrated in Fig. 1 and Table 2, the manual annotations contain pixels labeled 0 to 5, where 0 represents the background, 1 represents the right maxillary sinus, 2 represents the left maxillary sinus, 3 represents the right nasal cavity, 4 represents the left nasal cavity, and 5 represents the nasal pharynx.

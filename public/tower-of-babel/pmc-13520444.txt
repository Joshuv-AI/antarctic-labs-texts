Percolation-Induced Electrical Conductivity in Bio-Based Shape Memory Polybenzoxazine/Polycaprolactone Composites Reinforced with Carbon Black
Authors: Naritsara Chaipakdee, Warot Prasanseang, Sarawut Rimdusit, Kasinee Hemvichian, Sirirat Wacharawichanant, Sunan Tiptipakorn
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Bio-based conductive shape memory polymer composites have emerged as promising sustainable multifunctional materials for smart applications. Despite studies on conductive polymer composites and PBZ/PCL systems, limited reports are available on carbon black (CB)-reinforced bio-based polybenzoxazine/polycaprolactone (V-fa/PCL) composites. Therefore, bio-based V-fa/PCL composites containing 90 wt % PCL were reinforced with 0–30 phr CB, and thermal, mechanical, electrical, and shape memory properties were investigated. Thermal analysis showed an increase in the glass transition temperature (T g) and char yield (4–30%) with increasing CB content, while cross-link density decreased from 53,972 (at 0 phr) to 20,648 mol/m3 (at 5 phr). The highest tensile modulus (360 MPa) was obtained at 10 phr CB. This composition reached the percolation region, exhibiting static-dissipative behaviors with a volume resistivity of 106 Ω·cm. Increasing the CB loading to 30 phr further reduced the volume resistivity to 105 Ω·cm, indicating conductive behaviors. The 10 phr composites exhibited the best shape memory performance, with a shape fixity ratio (R f) of 89%, a shape recovery ratio (R r) of 84%, and a recovery time of 160 s. Overall, 10 phr CB was identified as the optimum CB loading, highlighting its potential for antistatic applications, flexible electronics, smart sensing systems, and shape memory structures.

Introduction

Polybenzoxazine (PBZ) is a type of phenolic thermosetting resin. It is made by ring-opening polymerization of benzoxazine monomers. PBZ has outstanding properties, such as excellent thermal stability, low viscosity, low water absorption, and high tensile strength. , PBZ can be synthesized from either petroleum-based or bio-based raw materials. Conventional petroleum-derived precursors, such as bisphenol A, have raised environmental and health concerns because of their toxicity. In contrast, bio-based feedstocks, including cardanol, vanillin, eugenol, guaiacol, and catechol, have attracted increasing attention owing to their renewable origin, biodegradability, and environmental friendliness. , Among the wide range of sustainable polymers, bio-based benzoxazines have obtained considerable attention as a likely class of thermosetting materials. These materials combined the key characteristics of benzoxazine chemistry, including high thermal stability, low curing shrinkage, and excellent chemical resistance, with the environmental advantages of phenolic compounds derived from renewable resources. , Nevertheless, PBZ still suffers from intrinsic brittleness and rigidity, which limit its flexibility and practical applications. Therefore, blending with flexible polymers or incorporating suitable fillers has become one of the most effective approaches for improving its overall performance.

In recent years, shape memory polymers (SMPs) have attracted considerable attention because a predefined permanent shape can be recovered after exposure to external stimuli such as heat, electricity, light, magnetic fields, or chemical environments. Owing to their lightweight nature, easy processability, and programmable deformation behaviors, SMPs have been explored in emerging applications including soft robotics, wearable electronics, biomedical devices, drug delivery systems, and energy-absorbing structures. , Recent advances in additive manufacturing have further accelerated the development of stimuli-responsive SMPs, particularly in the field of 4D printing technologies, where programmable shape transformation can be achieved in response to environmental stimuli.

Recent studies have mainly focused on biodegradable polymer blends and conductive thermoplastic composites, such as PLA/PBAT and PETG/CB systems, which have demonstrated excellent printability, electrical conductivity, and shape memory performance for 4D printing applications. − In addition, conductive SMPs reinforced with nanofillers such as carbon nanotubes (CNTs), Fe3O4 nanoparticles, graphene, and carbon black have been developed to enhance electrical responsiveness, mechanical performance, and multifunctionality. , Several polymer blends have also been investigated for shape memory applications. For example, PLA/PBAT systems exhibit improved toughness and biodegradability, , PLA/TPU blends provide excellent elasticity and shape recovery performance, whereas PEG-grafted CNT-reinforced PVC–PCL composites demonstrate promising thermally induced shape memory behaviors. However, these materials are predominantly thermoplastic systems and are mainly designed for additive manufacturing or conventional thermoplastic applications.

Bio-based polybenzoxazine was prepared from vanillin, furfurylamine, and paraformaldehyde and subsequently blended with polycaprolactone (PCL), a biodegradable and nontoxic polyester possessing good mechanical properties and a glass transition temperature (T g) of approximately – 60 °C. The incorporation of PCL significantly improves the flexibility and impact resistance of PBZ while maintaining its inherent thermal stability. Recently, V-fa/PCL blended with PCL of different number-average molecular weights (M n) of 10,000, 45,000, and 80 000 Da were studied. Among these formulations, the blend containing PCL with M n = 80,000 Da at 80–95 wt % exhibited the most favorable balance of thermal stability, mechanical performance, flexibility, and bendability, and was therefore selected as the matrix for the present study. Additionally, bio-based polybenzoxazine/epoxy copolymer systems have also been reported to improve cross-link density and flexible elasticity. Compared with conventional thermoplastic SMP matrices, the V-fa/PCL system offers complementary advantages arising from the combination of a thermosetting bio-based polybenzoxazine matrix and a flexible biodegradable polyester. Bio-based polybenzoxazine provides excellent thermal stability, chemical resistance, and dimensional stability owing to its highly cross-linked network, whereas PCL contributes flexibility, toughness, and thermally induced shape memory behaviors. Unlike commonly investigated thermoplastic blends such as PLA/PBAT and PLA/TPU, which mainly rely on physical entanglement and remain susceptible to thermal softening, the V-fa/PCL system combines the structural stability of a thermosetting matrix with the flexibility of a biodegradable polyester. In addition, compared with PVC/PCL blends, the present system is based on renewable bioderived polybenzoxazine, providing a more sustainable alternative while retaining desirable thermal and mechanical performance. These complementary characteristics make V-fa/PCL a promising matrix for developing electrically conductive and sustainable shape memory composites through the incorporation of carbon black.

Carbon black (CB) was selected as the conductive filler because of its low cost, commercial availability, high specific surface area, and excellent ability to establish conductive pathways within polymer matrices. Previous studies have demonstrated that CB-containing polymer composites exhibit enhanced electrical conductivity and, depending on filler dispersion and polymer–filler interfacial interactions, improved mechanical performance. Furthermore, CB-filled conductive polymer composites have attracted increasing attention for additive manufacturing and 4D printing because they combine electrical functionality with shape memory behaviors while maintaining relatively simple processing routes and considerably lower material costs than many nanocarbon fillers. Compared with carbon nanotubes or graphene, carbon black provides an economically attractive alternative with good processability, scalable manufacturing, and reliable conductive network formation, making it highly suitable for sustainable multifunctional polymer composites.

Although conductive shape memory polymer (SMP) composites have been widely investigated, most reported systems are based on thermoplastic matrices primarily developed for additive manufacturing applications. Studies on bio-based thermosetting conductive SMPs remain relatively scarce, particularly those employing carbon black-reinforced V-fa/PCL blends. Moreover, although conductive polymer composites and PBZ/PCL-based shape memory systems have individually been reported, a systematic investigation of the relationships among carbon black loading, electrical conductivity, thermal stability, mechanical performance, morphology, and shape memory behaviors in bio-based V-fa/PCL composites has not yet been comprehensively conducted. Therefore, rather than introducing a fundamentally new constituent material, the present work aims to elucidate the structure–property relationships of sustainable carbon black-reinforced V-fa/PCL composites by comprehensively evaluating the effects of carbon black loading on their thermal, mechanical, electrical, morphological, and shape memory properties. ,

Conductive shape memory polymer composites have attracted considerable interest for advanced applications in soft robotics, wearable electronics, biomedical devices, drug delivery systems, and energy-absorbing structures because they integrate shape memory capability, electrical conductivity, lightweight characteristics, and mechanical flexibility within a single material platform. These multifunctional properties enable the fabrication of smart structures capable of sensing, actuation, self-deployment, and adaptive responses under external stimuli, thereby expanding the potential applications of sustainable conductive polymer composites in emerging intelligent material systems. ,

Therefore, the present study focuses on a systematic investigation of CB-reinforced V-fa benzoxazine/PCL composites aimed at developing sustainable multifunctional polymer composites. The thermal behaviors was evaluated using thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and differential scanning calorimetry (DSC), while mechanical properties were assessed by a universal testing machine (UTM). The morphology and microstructure were examined via scanning electron microscopy (SEM). In addition, Volume resistivity and shape memory behaviors were also investigated.

Preparation of Bio-based Benzoxazine Monomer and V-fa/PCL Composites Filled with Carbon Black

A bio-based benzoxazine (V-fa type) monomer was synthesized from vanillin, furfurylamine, and paraformaldehyde in a molar ratio of 1:1:2. All mixed components were then heated at 105 °C for 1 h using the solventless technique Mannich condensation. , The monomer was then the resulting yellow monomer was obtained in solid form. The V-fa monomer and PCL at number-average molecular weights (M n) of 80,000 Da were then dissolved in THF and blended at PCL contents varying from 90 wt % in a vial. Carbon black (CB) was then incorporated at loadings ranging from 0 to 30 phr, and the mixture was stirred until a homogeneous slurry was obtained. The slurry mixture was subsequently maintained at 60 °C overnight to completely evaporate the THF solvent. A viscous solid polymer composite was obtained and then transferred to a mold of compression molding machine under step-curing process at temperatures of 150, 160, 170, and 180 °C (5 min for each temperature) under a pressure of 4000 psi. After cooling down to room temperature, the resulting V-fa/PCL composites filled with CB were obtained. Detailed processing results of the V-fa/PCL composites (90 wt % PCL) with different CB loadings are summarized in Table S1. Characterizations

The thermal stability of the V-fa/PCL composites filled with carbon black was determined using a thermogravimetric analyzer (TGA, model TGA1 STARe System (Mettler Toledo, Greifensee, Switzerland). The thermogravimetric analysis was carried out on ca.10 mg of samples with a ramping temperature of 20 °C/min from 40 to 800 °C). Each measurement was performed in duplicate to ensure the reproducibility of the results. In addition, a Differential Scanning Calorimeter (DSC, Netzsch, model NETZSCH DSC 204 F1 Phoenix, Selb, Germany) was employed to analyze approximately 5 mg of the tested sample at 20 °C/min from −100 to 250 °C with a nitrogen flow of 60 mL/min. Each measurement was performed in duplicate to ensure the reproducibility of the results. The curing behaviors was examined under various stepwise thermal curing conditions. The extent of reaction, expressed as the degree of conversion (Conversion %), was determined using eq , where ΔH 0 corresponds to the total enthalpy of the uncured system, and ΔH rxn represents the remaining enthalpy after curing, in accordance with established procedures. Dynamic mechanical properties were measured using a dynamic mechanical analyzer (DMA, Mettler Toledo, DMA 1 STARe System, Greifensee, Switzerland). Samples for tensile-mode analysis had dimensions of approximately 25 × 5 × 1 mm3. The measurements were carried out under a nitrogen atmosphere from −100 to 40 °C at a heating rate of 2 °C min–1 and a frequency of 1 Hz. Tensile testing was performed on specimens measuring 10 × 60 × 1 mm3 using a Universal Testing Machine (UTM, INSTRON, series 5567, 1kN, USA) in tensile mode in accordance with ASTM D882 standard. Five specimens were tested for each composition, and the results are reported as the mean ± standard deviation. The morphology of the fracture surface was investigated using a Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM, JEOL, model JSM-IT500HR, JEOL Ltd., Akishima, Tokyo, Japan). Volume resistivity was measured using circular specimens with a diameter of of 88.9 mm (3.5 in.) and a thickness of 2 mm, obtained from Resistivity Adapter (Keithley Model 6105), with High Voltage Supply (Keithley 247), and Multimeter (Fluke 87). Volume resistivity measurements were conducted under test conditions of 23 °C and relative humidity of 50%. The sample preparation and measurement procedure involved conditioning the specimens at 23.0 ± 0.2 °C and 50 ± 2% relative humidity for 48 h. The thickness of the specimens was then measured, and a direct current with an applied voltage (dc) was applied to the specimens for 1 min. The electrical current and resistance were then recorded, and the volume resistivity was subsequently calculated. Five specimens were tested for each composition, and the results are presented as the mean ± standard deviation.

To evaluate the bendability of the V-fa/PCL composites filled with carbon black (CB), specimens with approximate dimensions of 10 × 60 × 1 mm3 were fabricated into thin films. Each specimen was manually bent to assess whether the film could withstand deformation without fracturing or cracking, confirming its suitability for flexible device applications. The shape memory behaviors were evaluated through three key parameters: shape fixity ratio (Rf), shape recovery ratio (R r), and recovery time. The evaluation results revealed a slight increase in glass transition temperature (T g) with increasing CB content, with T g values ranging from −54 to −50 °C. The melting temperature (T m) is approximately 61 °C, indicating that CB incorporation did not significantly affect the crystalline melting behaviors of the PCL phase. The observed T g enhancement demonstrates that CB particles effectively restrict polymer chain mobility through interfacial interactions, leading to an increase in the strength of the composite. The melting enthalpy (ΔH) decreased with increasing CB content, decreasing from 98 to 72 J/g as the CB loading increased from 0 to 30 phr. This phenomenon was consistent with the decrease in crystallization temperature (T c) from 33 to 29 °C when CB was obtained increased from 0 to 30 phr. The decrease in T c can be attributed to the interfacial adhesion between the CB surface and polymer chains, which disrupted the regular arrangement of the composites. This behaviors can be attributed to the presence of carbon structures, which modify intermolecular interactions within the polymer matrix and consequently reduce the energy required for melting. This trend reflects a clear difference in thermal behaviors between the composites and the neat matrix. ,

The glass transition temperature (T g), determined from the peak of the tan δ curves, increased from −44.4 to −36.3 °C with increasing CB loading from 0 to 5 phr, respectively. The reduction in storage modulus and calculated cross-link density at higher CB loading may be attributed to filler agglomeration, which partially disrupted the elastic polymer network. This interpretation is consistent with the SEM observations (Figure ), where larger CB agglomerates were observed at higher filler contents. These findings also agree with the tensile results, in which restricted chain mobility increased T g, whereas filler agglomeration reduced tensile strength. Overall, moderate CB loading provided a favorable balance between thermal stability, mechanical reinforcement, and shape memory performance, while excessive CB incorporation adversely affected the integrity of the polymer network. The increase in T g with CB incorporation also suggests that a slightly higher activation temperature is required to trigger shape recovery. , Nevertheless, the moderate increase in T g remained within a practical temperature range, indicating that CB reinforcement could improve thermal stability without compromising the thermally activated shape memory behavior of the V-fa/PCL composites.

The results revealed that T dmax values remained relatively consistent, indicating that thermal stability was not significantly affected by CB incorporation. However, a notable improvement in char yield was observed, increasing from 0 to 30% when CB content was raised from 0 to 30 phr. This phenomenon could be attributed to the formation of interfacial interactions between the V-fa and PCL, which effectively retarded the degradation process. This finding was corresponding to the results from previous studies investigating the incorporation of amino-functionalized multiwalled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs-NH2) where interfacial adhesion between the PBZ polymer matrix and MWCNTs-NH2 was found to increase thermal stability, subsequently decrease thermal degradation, and these findings are in good agreement with those reported by Espera et al. ,

As illustrated by the DTG curves in Figure b, all V-fa/PCL composites containing different carbon black loadings exhibited a single major degradation peak, indicating that the incorporation of carbon black did not alter the primary thermal degradation mechanism of the V-fa/PCL matrix. Nevertheless, the DTG peak gradually shifted toward higher temperatures with increasing CB loading, suggesting enhanced thermal stability. This improvement can be attributed to the high thermal stability of carbon black, which acts as a physical barrier that restricts heat transfer and suppresses the diffusion of volatile degradation products during thermal decomposition. Moreover, the maximum degradation rate decreased after CB incorporation, as evidenced by the reduced DTG peak intensity. These findings indicate that carbon black effectively delayed the thermal degradation process without changing the degradation pathway of the composites. ,

Mechanical Properties of V-fa/PCL Composites Filled with CB

The tensile properties of bio-based polybenzoxazine (V-fa)/polycaprolactone (PCL) composites were evaluated at a fixed PCL content of 90 wt %, with carbon black (CB) loadings ranging from 0 to 20 phr. Tensile testing was performed using a universal testing machine (UTM), and the results are presented in Figure a, which demonstrates the stress–strain curves of the V-fa/PCL composites. Correspondingly, Figure b summarizes the tensile modulus and tensile strength of each formulation. The tensile modulus increased from 249 MPa for the V-fa/PCL blend at CB content 0 phr to 300 and 360 MPa for composites containing 5 and 10 phr of CB, respectively, indicating a reinforcing effect of CB at moderate loadings. This improvement can be attributed to the incorporation of rigid carbon black particles, which restrict polymer chain mobility within the polymer matrix, thereby increasing the stiffness of the composites. Similar improvements in tensile modulus accompanied by restricted chain mobility have been widely reported for carbon black-filled polymer composites. However, when the CB content was further increased to 20 phr, the tensile modulus decreased to 333 MPa. This phenomenon could be attributed to excessive CB incorporation, which promoted particle agglomeration. At higher CB loadings, the probability of particle agglomeration increases owing to the strong van der Waals attraction between carbon black particles. These agglomerates reduce the effective filler dispersion and weaken stress transfer efficiency, resulting in a slight reduction in tensile modulus compared with the optimum loading (10 phr). , In contrast, in the composites at 10 phr CB, the filler particles were more uniformly dispersed and provided effective localized reinforcement, consistent with the morphological observations of the V-fa/PCL composites filled with CB.

Mechanical properties of V-fa/PCL composites at 90 wt % PCL filled with varying CB content: (a) stress–strain curves, (b) tensile modulus and tensile strength.

Simultaneously, the tensile strength decreased from 11 to 5 MPa as the CB content increased from 0 to 20 phr, reflecting earlier failure due to restricted polymer chain mobility at higher filler loadings. Although the tensile modulus increased, the reduction in tensile strength represents the trade-off associated with incorporating rigid conductive fillers into the polymer matrix. Excessive CB loading promoted particle agglomeration, as confirmed by the SEM micrographs (Figure ), where larger CB clusters were observed at higher filler contents. These agglomerates act as stress concentration sites that facilitate crack initiation and premature fracture during tensile loading. Consequently, the improvement in stiffness was accompanied by a reduction in tensile strength, a trend that has been widely reported for carbon black-filled polymer composites because of filler agglomeration and defect sensitivity. ,

SEM images of the fracture surface for the V-fa/PCL composites at 90 wt % PCL filled with varying CB contents: (a) 0 phr, (b) 10 phr, (c) 20 phr and (d) 30 phr (×5000 magnification).

To further evaluate the energy absorption capability of the composites, the toughness (work-to-fracture) was determined from the area under the stress–strain curves. As summarized in Table S5, the toughness decreased from approximately 105 to 50, 44, and 8 MJ m–3 for the composites containing 0, 5, 10, and 20 phr CB, respectively. This reduction is consistent with the decreases in tensile strength and strain at break from 105 to 8% as the CB content increased from 0 to 20 phr, indicating that increasing CB loading restricts polymer chain mobility and promotes filler agglomeration, which act as accelerate crack initiation during tensile deformation. Consequently, although carbon black effectively enhances composite stiffness, excessive CB loading reduces toughness and results in a trade-off between stiffness and ductility, consistent with previous reports. − Therefore, while high CB contents deteriorate the overall mechanical performance, the composite containing 10 phr CB provides the most favorable balance among tensile modulus, electrical conductivity, toughness, and shape memory performance before severe filler agglomeration becomes dominant.

Shape Memory Behaviors of V-fa/PCL Composites Filled with CB

For the shape memory behaviors test, specimens with dimensions of 10 × 60 × 1 mm3 were initially prepared in a straight configuration at room temperature.

Shape recovery was induced by applying thermal stimulation to the bent region of the specimens. The recovery process was continuously recorded using a digital camera, as shown in Figure . The shape memory behaviors of V-fa/PCL composites (90 wt % PCL) filled with 10 phr CB is demonstrated in Video S1. The experimental results demonstrated that the V-fa/PCL composites filled with 10 phr CB exhibit thermally triggered shape recovery, with visually reaching a final stable angle achieved within 160 s. Consistent with previous studies, V-fa/PCL blends have been reported to function as thermoresponsive shape memory polymers (SMPs), supporting the thermal activation mechanism observed in the present composite system.

The composite synthesized with 90 wt % PCL and CB 10 phr maintained a shape recovery ratio in the range of 71–84% after 5 consecutive cycles. These results confirmed the good reusability of V-fa/PCL composites filled with CB at loadings of 0–10 phr as thermoresponsive shape memory polymers (SMPs). In contrast, the reduced reusability observed for the composite with 20 phr CB may be attributed to decreased flexibility resulting from excessive CB loading and an insufficient number of effective PCL spring segments within the polymer network. Electrical Properties of V-fa/PCL Composites Filled with CB

The volume resistivity behaviors of V-fa/PCL composites filled with different CB loadings, as shown in Figure , illustrate the evolution of electrical properties as a function of filler content. The volume resistivity values of V-fa/PCL composites (90 wt % PCL) filled with varying CB contents are summarized in Table S6. At low CB contents, the prepared composites exhibited a typical insulating response. At low CB contents in the range of 0–2.5 phr, the composites behaved as insulative, with resistivity in the range of 1014 to 1013 Ω·cm. This can be attributed to the large interparticle distances, which hinder the formation of conductive pathways. As the CB loading increased, the distance between CB particles decreased, leading to a gradual reduction in resistivity.

Volume resistivity of the fracture surface for the V-fa/PCL composites at 90 wt % PCL filled with varying CB contents.

As the CB loading increased, the electrical behavior of the V-fa/PCL composites gradually transitioned from an insulating state toward the electrical percolation zone. Specifically, composites containing CB of 2.5–10 phr exhibited a marked decrease in volume resistivity from approximately 1013 to 106 Ω·cm, indicating the formation of conductive pathways within the polymer matrix. This behavior is the characteristic of the electrical percolation phenomenon, in which the electrical conductivity increases abruptly once the conductive filler content approaches a critical threshold, owing to the establishment of an interconnected conductive network throughout the insulating polymer matrix. Beyond this threshold, further increases in filler loading produce comparatively smaller changes in electrical resistivity. , Furthermore, composites containing CB of 5–20 phr exhibited volume resistivity values in the range of approximately 107–106 Ω·cm, corresponding to the static-dissipative phase. At this resistivity level, the composites can effectively dissipate accumulated electrostatic charges and suppress sudden electrostatic discharge (ESD), thereby protecting sensitive electronic components. Combined with their shape memory behavior, these multifunctional properties make the developed composites attractive for smart structures capable of sensing, actuation, self-deployment, and adaptive responses under external stimuli. ,

Moreover, at a CB loading of 30 phr, the V-fa/PCL composites exhibited a volume resistivity below 105 Ω·cm, indicating a transition into the conductive phase. Beyond the percolation threshold, further CB addition resulted in only a slight decrease in resistivity, suggesting that a continuous three-dimensional conductive network had already been established within the polymer matrix. This behavior can be attributed to the reduced interparticle distance and direct contact between adjacent CB particles, which facilitate efficient electron transport through interconnected conductive pathways and electron hopping mechanisms. The formation of this well-developed conductive network is consistent with previous reports on carbon black-filled conductive polymer composites.

To demonstrate the practical electrical functionality of the developed composites, the V-fa/PCL composite containing 90 wt % PCL and 30 phr CB, which exhibited the lowest volume resistivity, was further evaluated as a conductive bridge during electrical heating, as illustrated in Figure . The composite successfully conducted electrical current, confirming the formation of a stable three-dimensional conductive network throughout the material, the results indicate the formation of an effective conductive network at relatively low CB contents. , The combination of electrical conductivity, shape memory behavior, and the bio-based thermosetting matrix makes the developed V-fa/PCL/CB composites promising candidates for multifunctional applications, including electromagnetic interference (EMI) shielding, antistatic materials, flexible sensors, wearable electronics, and electrically activated smart devices. These results further demonstrate the potential of the developed composites as sustainable multifunctional materials for next-generation intelligent structures.

Conclusions

Bio-based V-fa/PCL composites containing carbon black (CB) were successfully synthesized as thermosetting polymer systems. These materials exhibit combined shape memory functionality and electrical conductivity. The influence of CB loading in the range of 0–30 phr on the thermal behaviors, mechanical performance, and morphology of the composites was systematically investigated. The incorporation of CB into the V-fa/PCL matrix resulted in a increase in the glass transition temperature, which can be attributed to the restriction of polymer chain mobility caused by the presence of CB particles. This constraint effect led to enhanced stiffness and strength, particularly at 5 and 10 phr CB, where the composites exhibited modulus values of approximately 300 and 360 MPa, respectively. The improvement is associated with the relatively uniform dispersion of CB within the polymer matrix. In contrast, excessive CB addition caused particle agglomeration, which adversely affected the overall performance of the polymer composites. Nevertheless, volume resistivity characterization revealed that V-fa/PCL composites containing 10–30 phr CB displayed conductive behaviors. Furthermore, the shape memory performance was most pronounced at 5 and 10 phr CB, where the R f (%) reached 89%, and the R r (%) ranged from 81 to 84%, with final stable angle achieved within 160 s. Due to the synergistic combination of electrical conductivity and shape memory capability, these bio-based V-fa/PCL composites filled with CB demonstrate strong potential for practical applications, including electrostatic discharge protection, flexible sensors, wearable electronics, electrically activated smart devices and an electrical current bridge.

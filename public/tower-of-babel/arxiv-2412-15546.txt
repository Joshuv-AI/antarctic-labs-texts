De-singularity Subgradient for the q q -th-Powered ℓ p \ell_{p} -Norm Weber Location Problem
Zhao-Rong Lai, Xiaotian Wu, Liangda Fang, Ziliang Chen, Cheng Li

Abstract
The Weber location problem is widely used in several artificial intelligence scenarios. However, the gradient of the objective does not exist at a considerable set of singular points. Recently, a de-singularity subgradient method has been proposed to fix this problem, but it can only handle the q q -th-powered ℓ 2 \ell_{2} -norm case ( 1 ⩽ q < 2 1\leqslant q<2 ), which has only finite singular points. In this paper, we further establish the de-singularity subgradient for the q q -th-powered ℓ p \ell_{p} -norm case with 1 ⩽ q ⩽ p 1\leqslant q\leqslant p and 1 ⩽ p < 2 1\leqslant p<2 , which includes all the rest unsolved situations in this problem. This is a challenging task because the singular set is a continuum. The geometry of the objective function is also complicated so that the characterizations of the subgradients, minimum and descent direction are very difficult. We develop a q q -th-powered ℓ p \ell_{p} -norm Weiszfeld Algorithm without Singularity ( q q P p p NWAWS) for this problem, which ensures convergence and the descent property of the objective function. Extensive experiments on six real-world data sets demonstrate that q q P p p NWAWS successfully solves the singularity problem and achieves a linear computational convergence rate in practical scenarios.

Introduction

The Weber location problem is a fundamental problem that is extensively investigated in artificial intelligence
(
Lai et al. 2024
)
, machine learning
(
Li, Sahoo, and Hoi 2016
;
Lai et al. 2018c
;
Lai et al. 2020
)
, financial engineering
(
Lai and Yang 2023
)
, computer vision
(
Aftab, Hartley, and Trumpf 2015
)
, and operations research
(
Ostresh 1978
)
. For a general definition, it seeks a point
\bm{x}_{*}
that minimizes the weighted sum of the
q
-th power of the
\ell_{p}
distances to
m
fixed data points
\{\bm{x}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}

(
Weber 1909
;
Morris 1981
;
Chen 1984
;
Brimberg and Love 1993
)
, defined as the
q
-th-powered
\ell_{p}
-norm Weber location problem
(
q
P
p
NWLP)
:

\bm{x}_{*}\in\argmin_{\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}C_{p,q}(\bm{y}):=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i}\|\bm{y}-\bm{x}_{i}\|_{p}^{q},

(1)

where
\xi_{i}
denotes the weight for the
i
-th data point,
\|\cdot\|_{p}
denotes the
\ell_{p}
norm,
C_{p,q}(\cdot)
denotes the cost function related to the
q
-th power of the
\ell_{p}
norm,
1\leqslant q\leqslant p
and
p\geqslant 1
. Due to the same reasons as
(
Lai et al. 2024
)
, we can assume that the data points
\{\bm{x}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are distinct and non-collinear in the rest of the paper.

1.1
The Singularity Problem

7
7

The supplementary material and codes for this paper are available at

.

There is no closed-form solution to (
1
), and the gradient-type method is an intuitive and tractable approach. This approach includes those do not explicitly show a gradient form, like the Weiszfeld algorithm
(
Brimberg and Love 1993
)
. To take a glimpse of the singularity problem, we first compute the gradient of the objective function
C_{p,q}
as

\displaystyle\big(\nabla C_{p,q}(\bm{y})\big)^{(t)}

\displaystyle{:=}

\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{m}}q\xi_{i}\|\bm{y}-\bm{x}_{i}\|_{p}^{q-p}|{y}^{(t)}-{x}_{i}^{(t)}|^{p-2}(y^{(t)}-{x}_{i}^{(t)}),

(2)

where
\big(\nabla C_{p,q}(\bm{y})\big)^{(t)}
denotes the
t
-th dimension (
1\leqslant t\leqslant d
) of the gradient. Note that if
q<p
or
p<2
, the singularity problem can occur if
\bm{y}
hits the following singular set:

\begin{cases}\mathcal{S}_{p}{:=}\bigl\{\bm{y}{\in}\mathbb{R}^{d}|\exists i{\in}\{1,\dots,m\},t{\in}\{1,\dots,d\}\text{ s.t. }y^{(t)}{=}x_{i}^{(t)}\bigr\}\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{if }1\leqslant p<2,\\
\mathcal{S}_{2}{:=}\{\bm{x}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{if }1\leqslant q<2,p=2.\end{cases}

(3)

This singularity problem occurs frequently and unexpectedly.
Chandrasekaran and Tamir (1989)
;
Vardi and Zhang (2000)
indicate that the “bad” points that can yield such singularity in a gradient-type method may constitute a continuum set that can be dense in an open region of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\geqslant 2
) or even the entire
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Moreover, this problem cannot be circumvented by straightforward treatments like perturbations and random restarts. More details can be found in
(
Lai et al. 2024
)
.

If
q\geqslant p
or
p\geqslant 2
, the term
\|\bm{y}-\bm{x}_{i}\|_{p}^{q-p}
or
|{y}^{(t)}-{x}_{i}^{(t)}|^{p-2}
in (
2
) will not be singular. For
p\geqslant 2
but
1\leqslant q<p
, the singularity problem can be solved by
(
Lai et al. 2024
)
. Hence this paper mainly focuses on the rest unsolved singular cases with the two key hyperparameters
1\leqslant q\leqslant p
and
1\leqslant p<2
. Users can select any values of
p
and
q
within this range to suit their specific needs. As
p
approaches
2
, the distance between data points becomes closer to the Euclidean distance. Conversely, as
p
approaches
1
, the distance between data points becomes closer to the Manhattan distance, representing a typical non-Euclidean geometry. On the other hand, as
q
approaches
p
, the distance between data points gets higher power, which can be advantageous in certain computer vision tasks
(
Aftab, Hartley, and Trumpf 2015
)
. In summary, allowing for a range of values for
p
and
q
enhances the geometrical representation of the Weber location problem.

1.2
Continuum Singular Set for
1\leqslant p<2

(
3
) indicates that when
p=2
, the singularity only occurs at the
m
fixed data points
\{\bm{x}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
. This case has already been completely solved by
(
Vardi and Zhang 2000
;
Lai et al. 2024
)
. However,
when
1\leqslant p<2
, the singularity occurs in at most
m\cdot d
hyperplanes that encompass a continuum set of points, which is much more difficult than the
p=2
case and no solutions have been developed (see Figure
1
)
. The difficulties lie in the following three aspects:

1.

An effective gradient-type algorithm may visit each singular point for only once. But since there are infinite singular points when
1\leqslant p<2
, the algorithm may visit the singular set for infinite times.

2.

Based on the first point, there is no unified step size for the escape from the singular set, which affects the convergence property.

3.

The geometry of the objective function is complicated when
1\leqslant q\leqslant p
and
1\leqslant p<2
so that the characterizations of the subgradients, minimum and descent direction are very difficult, which also affects the convergence property.

Our main contributions are summarized as follows.

1.

We develop a de-singularity subgradient of the cost function

on the singular set
\mathcal{S}_{p}
. It can replace the ordinary gradient without increasing computational complexity.

2.

We develop a
q
-th-Powered
\ell_{p}

P

NWAWS

3.

We develop a complete proof for the convergence of

NWAWS.

4.

We demonstrate that
q

Related Works

We review some closely related works on

2.1
The
\ell_{p}
-Norm Weiszfeld Algorithm

The
\ell_{p}
-norm Weiszfeld algorithm (
p
NWA) can be derived by the first-order optimal condition of the
q
P
p
NWLP with
1\leqslant p\leqslant 2
and
q=1

(
Brimberg and Love 1993
)
. For a
nonsingular optimal point

\bm{x}_{*}\notin\mathcal{S}_{p}
, setting
\big(\nabla C_{p,1}(\bm{x}_{*})\big)^{(t)}=0
in (
2
) yields

x_{*}^{(t)}{=}\frac{{\sum_{i=1}^{m}}\xi_{i}\|\bm{x}_{*}{-}\bm{x}_{i}\|_{p}^{1-p}|x_{*}^{(t)}{-}{x}_{i}^{(t)}|^{p-2}{x}_{i}^{(t)}}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i}\|\bm{x}_{*}{-}\bm{x}_{i}\|_{p}^{1-p}|x_{*}^{(t)}{-}{x}_{i}^{(t)}|^{p-2}},\forall 1{\leqslant}t{\leqslant}d.

(4)

These are fixed-point equations, which can be converted to a fixed-point algorithm with the following operator
\mathbf{T}_{p,1}
:

\displaystyle y_{(k+1)}^{(t)}{:=}

\displaystyle\big(\mathbf{T}_{p,1}(\bm{y}_{(k)})\big)^{(t)}

\displaystyle{:=}

\displaystyle\frac{{\sum_{i=1}^{m}}\xi_{i}\|\bm{y}_{(k)}-\bm{x}_{i}\|_{p}^{1-p}|y_{(k)}^{(t)}-{x}_{i}^{(t)}|^{p-2}{x}_{i}^{(t)}}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i}\|\bm{y}_{(k)}-\bm{x}_{i}\|_{p}^{1-p}|y_{(k)}^{(t)}-{x}_{i}^{(t)}|^{p-2}},

(5)

where
y_{(k)}^{(t)}
denotes the
t
-th dimension of the
k
-th iterate.
When
\bm{y}_{(k)}
hits the singular set
\mathcal{S}_{p}
,
p
NWA cannot solve it but just terminates at
\bm{y}_{(k)}
.

2.2
q
-th Power Weiszfeld Algorithm without Singularity

Lai et al. (2024)
propose a
q
-th power Weiszfeld algorithm without singularity (
q
PWAWS) to handle the singularity problem in the special case
p=2,1\leqslant q<2
:

\bm{y}_{(k+1)}{=}\begin{cases}\mathbf{T}_{2,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)}):=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i}\|\bm{y}_{(k)}-\bm{x}_{i}\|_{2}^{q-2}\bm{x}_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i}\|\bm{y}_{(k)}-\bm{x}_{i}\|_{2}^{q-2}}\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{ if }\bm{y}_{(k)}{\notin}\{\bm{x}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m},\\
\mathbf{T}_{s}(\bm{y}_{(k)}):=\bm{y}_{(k)}{-}\lambda_{*}\nabla D_{2,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})\\
\quad\text{ if }\bm{y}_{(k)}{=}\bm{x}_{l}\text{ for some }\ l{\in}\{1,\dots,m\},\end{cases}

(6)

where

\nabla D_{2,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})=\sum_{i\neq l}q\xi_{i}\|\bm{y}_{(k)}-\mathbf{x}_{i}\|_{2}^{q-2}(\bm{y}_{(k)}-\mathbf{x}_{i})

(7)

is the
q
-th-powered
\ell_{2}
-norm de-singularity subgradient and
\lambda_{*}>0
. Intuitively,
\nabla D_{2,q}
removes the singular component corresponding to
\bm{x}_{l}
and serves as a conventional gradient in the subgradient descent step
\mathbf{T}_{s}
.

q
PWAWS can escape from each singular point and will not revisit it again. Since there are finite singular points when
p=2
,
q
PWAWS can eventually escape from the singular set
\mathcal{S}_{2}
(see Figure
1a
).
However, it cannot be directly adopted in the
1\leqslant p<2
case, because the singular set
\mathcal{S}_{p}
is a continuum and
q
PWAWS may not eventually escape from
\mathcal{S}_{p}
. Moreover, the step size
\lambda_{*}
in
\mathbf{T}_{s}
cannot be uniformly chosen with respect to (w.r.t.)
\mathcal{S}_{p}
due to its infinite elements. The characterizations of the subgradients, minimum and descent direction are also difficult. These three problems affect the convergence property of
q
PWAWS.
Therefore, a new methodology should be developed to overcome these new challenges for the
1\leqslant p<2
case.

3
q
-th-Powered
\ell_{p}
-Norm Weiszfeld Algorithm without Singularity

In this section, we present
q
P
p
NWAWS for solving
q
P
p
NWLP with
1\leqslant p<2
and
1\leqslant q\leqslant p
. For a convenient illustration, we let
\eta_{i}:={\xi_{i}}^{\frac{1}{q}}
and reformulate (
1

\bm{x}_{*}\in\argmin_{\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}C_{p,q}(\bm{y}):=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\eta_{i}^{q}\|\bm{y}-\bm{x}_{i}\|_{p}^{q}.

(8)

C_{p,q}
is strictly convex and there exists a unique minimum point
\bm{x}_{*}
for (
8
) with

, while
C_{p,q}
is convex and there exists one or more minimum points for (

4

1.

The update at a nonsingular iterate
\bm{y}_{(k)}\notin\mathcal{S}_{p}
is designed as a Weiszfeld-style one, which makes

non-increasing.

2.

Define the

-th-powered
\ell_{p}
-norm de-singularity subgradient
\nabla D_{p,q}(\bm{y})
for
\bm{y}\in\mathcal{S}_{p}
to characterize the subgradients of
C_{p,q}(\bm{y})
and the minimum of

.

3.

Adopt
\nabla D_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})
to construct the iterative update at the singular iterate
\bm{y}_{(k)}\in\mathcal{S}_{p}
to reduce the cost function.

4.

Establish the convergence proof for
q
P
p
NWAWS.

3.1
Update Formula at Nonsingular Iterates

First, we consider the simplest case where the current iterate
\bm{y}_{(k)}\notin\mathcal{S}_{p}
. The corresponding update formula is:

\displaystyle y_{(k+1)}^{(t)}

\displaystyle:=\big(\mathbf{T}_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})\big)^{(t)}

\displaystyle{:=}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\eta_{i}^{q}\|\bm{y}_{(k)}{-}\bm{x}_{i}\|_{p}^{q{-}p}|{y}_{(k)}^{(t)}{-}{x}_{i}^{(t)}|^{p{-}2}{x}_{i}^{(t)}}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\eta_{i}^{q}\|\bm{y}_{(k)}{-}\bm{x}_{i}\|_{p}^{q{-}p}|{y}_{(k)}^{(t)}{-}{x}_{i}^{(t)}|^{p{-}2}}.

(9)

The following descent property guarantees that (
9

C_{p,q}
at any non-minimum nonsingular iterate.

Theorem 1

(Descent Property at Nonsingular Iterates)
.

Let the cost function
C_{p,q}
and the operator
\mathbf{T}_{p,q}
be defined in (
8
) and (

and

\bm{y}_{(k)}\notin\mathcal{S}_{p}

with equality holds only when

.

The proof is provided in Supplementary
B.1
. The following corollary characterizes a minimum point

of (

.

Corollary 2
.

If
\bm{y}_{(k)}\notin\mathcal{S}_{p}
, then
\mathbf{T}_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})=\bm{y}_{(k)}\Leftrightarrow

\bm{y}_{(k)}
is a minimum point of model (
8

\bm{y}_{(k)}=\bm{x}_{*}

The proof is provided in Supplementary
B.2
. We then turn to the singular case.

3.2
Characterization of Subgradients and Minimum

Before we derive the iterative update for the singular iterate
\bm{y}_{(k)}\in\mathcal{S}_{p}
, we first introduce the de-singularity subgradient of
C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})
and characterize the minimum point(s) of (
8
).

Definition 3

(Subgradient,
Rockafellar and Wets 2009
)
.

Let
C_{p,q}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a convex function. A vector
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called a subgradient of
C_{p,q}
at
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
if for all
\bm{z}{\in}\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

C_{p,q}(\bm{z}){-}C_{p,q}(\bm{y}){\geqslant}\bm{v}^{\top}(\bm{z}-\bm{y}).

(10)

The set of all subgradients at
\bm{y}
is denoted by
\partial C_{p,q}(\bm{y})
. If
C_{p,q}
is differentiable at
\bm{y}
, then
\partial C_{p,q}(\bm{y})
reduces to the gradient
\nabla C_{p,q}(\bm{y})
.

To construct
\partial C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})
, we need to identify the singular component(s) of
\bm{y}_{(k)}
.

Definition 4

(Singular Component(s))
.

For
\bm{y}\in\mathcal{S}_{p}
, each
t\in\{1,\dots,d\}
and each
i\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
, let

\displaystyle U_{i}(\bm{y})

\displaystyle:=\{t\in\{1,\dots,d\}:\ y^{(t)}=x_{i}^{(t)}\},

(11)

\displaystyle V_{t}(\bm{y})

\displaystyle:=\{i\in\{1,\dots,m\}:\ y^{(t)}=x_{i}^{(t)}\},

(12)

which represent the index sets of the dimensions and the data points such that
{y}^{(t)}={x}_{i}^{(t)}
, respectively.

and

\displaystyle D_{p,q}(\bm{y})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\eta_{i}^{q}\big(\sum_{t\notin U_{i}(\bm{y})}|{y}^{(t)}-{x}_{i}^{(t)}|^{p}\big)^{\frac{q}{p}},

Theorem 6

(Characterization of Subgradients and Minimum)
.

For
\bm{y}\in\mathcal{S}_{p}
,

\partial C_{p,q}(\bm{y}){=}\begin{cases}\{\nabla D_{1,1}(\bm{y}){+}\bm{u}\}\ \text{where}\ -a^{(t)}{\leqslant}u^{(t)}{\leqslant}a^{(t)},\\
\qquad\forall t,\qquad\text{if }p=q=1,\\
\{\nabla D_{p,1}(\bm{y})\},\ \text{if }q{=}1,1{<}p{<}2,\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\bm{y}\in\mathcal{S}_{p}\backslash\{\bm{x}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m},\\
\{\nabla D_{p,1}(\bm{x}_{l})+\eta_{l}\bm{b}\}\quad\text{where}\quad\|\bm{b}\|_{r}\leqslant 1,\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{if }q{=}1,1{<}p{<}2,\bm{y}=\bm{x}_{l},\\
\{\nabla D_{p,q}(\bm{y})\},\ \text{if }1<q\leqslant p,1<p<2,\end{cases}

(15)

where

and
\|\cdot\|_{r}
is the conjugate norm of

such that
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{p}=1
.

The proof is provided in Supplementary
B.3
. According to Fermat’s rule,
\bm{y}\in\mathcal{S}_{p}
is a minimum point of (

\mathbf{0}_{d}\in\partial C_{p,q}(\bm{y})

\bm{y}_{(k)}
is not a minimum point, the following theorem shows a descent direction of

Theorem 7

(Descent Property at Singular Iterates)
.

For
1\leqslant p<2
and
1\leqslant q\leqslant p
, define the following direction

\mathscr{D}_{p,q}(\bm{y}){=}\begin{cases}\big(\nabla D_{p,1}(\bm{x}_{l})\big)^{\frac{r}{p}}\ \ \text{where }\ \frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{p}=1,\ \\
\qquad\qquad\ \text{if }q{=}1,1{<}p{<}2,\bm{y}=\bm{x}_{l},\\
\nabla D_{p,q}(\bm{y}),\qquad\qquad\text{else },\end{cases}

(16)

where
(\cdot)^{\frac{r}{p}}
denotes the element-wise signed power. If
\bm{y}\in\mathcal{S}_{p}
is not a minimum point of (

), then there exists some
\lambda_{*}>0
such that for any
0<\lambda\leqslant\lambda_{*}
,
C_{p,q}(\bm{y}-\lambda\mathscr{D}_{p,q}(\bm{y}))<C_{p,q}(\bm{y})
.

The proof is provided in Supplementary
B.4
. The key point is to verify that
-\mathscr{D}_{p,q}(\bm{y})
is a descent direction if
\bm{y}
is not a minimum point. To determine the step size
\lambda_{*}
in practice, we can start with an initial value of
\lambda_{0}=\|\mathscr{D}_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})\|_{p}
and implement a line search
\lambda_{w+1}=\rho\lambda_{w}
with
0<\rho<1
, until we find a value of
\lambda_{*}
such that
C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)}-\lambda_{*}\mathscr{D}_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)}))<C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})
. Then we can construct the iterative update at the singular point
\bm{y}_{(k)}
as

\bm{y}_{(k+1)}:=\mathbf{T}_{s}(\bm{y}_{(k)}):=\bm{y}_{(k)}-\lambda_{*}\mathscr{D}_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)}).

(17)

Combining (
9
) and (
17
), the whole
q
P
p

\bm{y}_{(k+1)}{:=}\mathbf{T}(\bm{y}_{(k)}){:=}\begin{cases}\mathbf{T}_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})&\text{if}\>\bm{y}_{(k)}\notin\mathcal{S}_{p},\\
\mathbf{T}_{s}(\bm{y}_{(k)})&\text{if}\>\bm{y}_{(k)}\in\mathcal{S}_{p}.\end{cases}

Theorems

3.3
Convergence Theorem

To analyze the convergence of
q
P
p
NWAWS, we first provide several lemmas on some properties of the operators involved. The first lemma indicates that the operator
\mathbf{T}_{p,q}
designed for the nonsingular iterates can be extended to the singular iterates.

Lemma 8
.

Let the operator
\mathbf{T}_{p,q}
be defined in (
9
). Then for
1\leqslant q\leqslant p
,
1\leqslant p<2
,

\lim_{\bm{y}\rightarrow\bm{x},\bm{y}\notin\mathcal{S}_{p}}\mathbf{T}_{p,q}(\bm{y})=\bm{x},\quad\forall\bm{x}\in\mathcal{S}_{p}.

(19)

The proof is provided in Supplementary
B.5
. Therefore, we can define

\mathbf{T}_{p,q}(\bm{x}):=\bm{x},\quad\forall\bm{x}\in\mathcal{S}_{p},\ 1\leqslant q\leqslant p,\ 1\leqslant p<2.

(20)

The second lemma confirms that the sequence
\{\bm{y}_{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
generated by
q
P
p
NWAWS is bounded, which is based on the descent property of
\{C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Lemma 9
.

The sequence
\{\bm{y}_{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
generated by
q
P
p
NWAWS is bounded.

The proof is provided in Supplementary
B.6
. As emphasized above, the singular set
\mathcal{S}_{p}
contains infinite points in the case
1\leqslant p<2
, hence the operator
\mathbf{T}_{p,q}
may not escape from
\mathcal{S}_{p}
. This is a major unavoidable obstacle for convergence analysis. To overcome this obstacle, we combine Lemma
9
and the Bolzano-Weierstrasz theorem, then there exists at least one limit point
y_{*}
and a subsequence
\{\bm{y}_{(k_{v})}\}_{v\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\lim\limits_{v\to\infty}\bm{y}_{(k_{v})}=\bm{y}_{*}
. The following lemma indicates that there are only a finite number of limit points of
\{\bm{y}_{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in the strictly convex case
1<p<2
, even though
\mathcal{S}_{p}
has infinite points. This is a novel and significant theoretical result that plays a crucial part in the convergence proof.

Lemma 10
.

If
1<p<2
, there are only a finite number of limit points of
\{\bm{y}_{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. Moreover, all these limit points have the same cost function value.

The proof is provided in Supplementary
B.7
.

Theorem 11

(Convergence Theorem)
.

Let
\{\bm{y}_{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be the iteration sequence generated by
q
P
p
NWAWS in (
18
). If
\bm{y}_{(k)}
hits the minimum point
\bm{x}_{*}
of model (
8
), the characterization of minimum (Corollary
2
and Theorem
6
) ensures that this could be recognized and the algorithm will be stopped. Otherwise, the cost function sequence
\{C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges. Assume
1<p<2
in addition. If
\{\bm{y}_{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
hits
\mathcal{S}_{p}
for a finite number of times, then
\{\bm{y}_{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
also converges. On the other hand, if
\{\bm{y}_{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
has a nonsingular cluster point, then this cluster point is exactly the minimum point
\bm{x}_{*}
and the entire sequence
\{\bm{y}_{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
\bm{x}_{*}
.

The proof is provided in Supplementary
B.8
. We provide a practical way to verify the conditions of this theorem. When the algorithm reaches the convergence tolerance, we can check whether the last few iterates are singular points. If not, we can consider that
y_{(k)}
converges to exactly the minimum point
\bm{x}_{*}
.

To end this section, we indicate that a general constant-step subgradient descent method cannot guarantee convergence in the objective value for
q
P
p
NWLP due to the varying
\lambda_{*}
in Theorem
7
. Besides,
\nabla C_{p,q}(\bm{y})
in (
2
) may not be Lipschitz continuous even for
\bm{y}\notin\mathcal{S}_{p}
. We raise a counterexample: let
C_{p,q}(\bm{y}):=\|\bm{y}\|_{p}^{q}
,
\bm{y}_{1}=\varepsilon\bm{1}_{d}>\bm{0}_{d}
, and
\bm{y}_{2}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\bm{1}_{d}
. Then

\frac{\|\nabla C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{1})-\nabla C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{2})\|_{2}}{\|\bm{y}_{1}-\bm{y}_{2}\|_{2}}=(2-2^{2-q})qd^{\frac{q-p}{p}}\varepsilon^{q-2}.

(21)

Since
q{<}2
, (
21
) implies
\lim\limits_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\frac{\|\nabla C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{1})-\nabla C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{2})\|_{2}}{\|\bm{y}_{1}-\bm{y}_{2}\|_{2}}=\infty
. Therefore, a general subgradient descent method cannot achieve the sublinear convergence rate
O(\frac{1}{k})
. However, practical experiments show that
q
P
p
NWAWS achieves a linear computational convergence rate, which is efficient to solve
q
P
p
NWLP.

The whole procedure of
q
P
p
NWAWS is illustrated in Supplementary
A
. In each iteration, the algorithm first identifies whether the current iterate is a singular point and the dimensions where the singularity occurs. Next, it computes the de-singularity subgradient (or the normal gradient if there is no singularity). The de-singularity subgradient can then be used to determine whether the current iterate is a minimum point. If it is not, the algorithm performs a de-singularity subgradient descent step to reduce the cost function, proceeding to the next iteration until the convergence tolerance is met.

5
Conclusions and Future Works

This paper proposes a
q
-th-Powered
\ell_{p}
-Norm Weiszfeld Algorithm without Singularity (
q
P
p
NWAWS) for the
q
-th-Powered
\ell_{p}
-Norm Weber Location Problem (
q
P
p
NWLP) with
1\leqslant p<2
and
1\leqslant q\leqslant p
, which includes all the rest unsolved situations in this problem. One main difficulty to solve this problem is that the singular points constitute a continuum set, so that any gradient-type algorithm may visit the singular set for infinite times.
q
P
p
NWAWS is able to characterize the subgradients and minimum at any singular or nonsingular point. If it is not a minimum point,
q
P
p
NWAWS can further reduce the cost function. Moreover, it guarantees convergence in the objective function value.

Experimental results on six real-world data sets show that
q
P
p
NWAWS successfully reduces the cost function in a few iterations at a singular point. It achieves convergence in a few iterations and shows a linear computational convergence rate. Moreover, it performs well in the online portfolio selection task that its final cumulative wealth and its Sharpe ratio with several
(q,p)
pairs are higher than those with the original setting
(q,p)=(1,2)
. Thus
q
P
p
NWAWS and
q
P
p
NWLP with
1\leqslant p<2
and
1\leqslant q\leqslant p
are useful and advantageous in practice. In future works, we will extend the de-singularity methodology to the multi-facility location problem.

C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k_{v})})\to\infty
. However, Theorems
1
and
7
indicate that
C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k_{v})})\leqslant C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(0)}),\forall v
. This yields a contradiction, thus
\{\bm{y}_{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded.
∎

B.7
Proof of Lemma
10

Proof.

We know from Lemma
9
that
\{\bm{y}_{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded, then from the Bolzano-Weierstrasz theorem, there exists at least one point
y_{*}
and a subsequence
\{\bm{y}_{(k_{v})}\}_{v\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\lim\limits_{v\to\infty}\bm{y}_{(k_{v})}=\bm{y}_{*}
. We then investigate the following two cases regarding whether
\bm{y}_{*}\notin\mathcal{S}_{p}
or
\bm{y}_{*}\in\mathcal{S}_{p}
.

Case 1.
If
\bm{y}_{*}\notin\mathcal{S}_{p}
, then we can assume that
\{\bm{y}_{(k_{v})}\}_{v\in\mathbb{N}}\notin\mathcal{S}_{p}
. Since the operator
\mathbf{T}_{p,q}
is continuous, then

\displaystyle\lim_{v\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{T}_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k_{v})})=\mathbf{T}_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{*}).

(47)

From Theorems
1
and
7
, the sequence
\{C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is non-increasing. Furthermore, since
C_{p,q}(\bm{y})\geqslant 0
, the sequence is bounded below. Therefore, we can conclude that the sequence
\{C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges. According to the convergence of
\{C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, it has a limit and any subsequence of
\{C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
should have the same limit. In particular,
C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k_{v})})
and
C_{p,q}(\mathbf{T}_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k_{v})}))
are two subsequences of
C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\lim_{v\rightarrow\infty}C_{p,q}(\mathbf{T}_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k_{v})}))=\lim_{v\rightarrow\infty}C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k_{v})}).\quad

(48)

Since
C_{p,q}
is continuous, then it follows from (
47
) and (
48
) that

\displaystyle C_{p,q}(\mathbf{T}_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{*}))=C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{*}).

(49)

Since
\bm{y}_{*}\notin\mathcal{S}_{p}
, then Theorem
1
and (
49
) indicate
\bm{y}_{*}=\mathbf{T}_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{*})
. By Corollary
2
,
\bm{y}_{*}=\mathbf{M}
.

Case 2.
If
\bm{y}_{*}\in\mathcal{S}_{p}
, then we denote the limit point set of
\{\bm{y}_{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
by
\{\bm{y}_{j*}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
with
\mathcal{J}
denoting some index set. Since
\{C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges, we have

C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{j*})=a,\qquad\forall j\in\mathcal{J},

(50)

for some
a\geqslant 0
. Summarizing Case 1 and Case 2,
\bm{y}_{*}\in\{\mathbf{M}\}\bigcup\big(\mathcal{S}_{p}\bigcap\{\bm{y}:C_{p,q}(\bm{y})=a\}\big)
. In other words, the limit point set of
\{\bm{y}_{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a subset of
\{\mathbf{M}\}\bigcup\big(\mathcal{S}_{p}\bigcap\{\bm{y}:C_{p,q}(\bm{y})=a\}\big)
.

Now we only need to prove that
\mathcal{C}_{a}:=\{\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:C_{p,q}(\bm{y})=a\}
is a finite set, which can be done by yielding a contradiction. Suppose there exist
(n+1)
points
\bm{u}_{1},\bm{u}_{2},\cdots,\bm{u}_{n+1}\in\mathcal{C}_{a}
such that
(\bm{u}_{1}-\bm{u}_{n+1})
,
(\bm{u}_{2}-\bm{u}_{n+1})
,
\cdots
,
(\bm{u}_{n}-\bm{u}_{n+1})
are linear dependent vectors. Then there exist
\zeta_{1}^{\prime}
,
\zeta_{2}^{\prime}
,
\cdots
,
\zeta_{n}^{\prime}
that are not all zero such that:

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}^{\prime}(\bm{u}_{1}-\bm{u}_{n+1})+\zeta_{2}^{\prime}(\bm{u}_{2}-\bm{u}_{n+1})+\cdots+\zeta_{n}^{\prime}(\bm{u}_{n}-\bm{u}_{n+1})=0.

(51)

We then investigate the following two cases regarding whether
\sum_{o=1}^{n}\zeta_{o}^{\prime}\neq 0
or
\sum_{o=1}^{n}\zeta_{o}^{\prime}=0
.

Case 1.
If
\sum_{o=1}^{n}\zeta_{o}^{\prime}\neq 0
, then we let
\zeta_{j}=\frac{\zeta_{j}^{\prime}}{\sum_{o=1}^{n}\zeta_{o}^{\prime}}
,
\forall j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
. Then
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\zeta_{j}=1
and (
51
) becomes
\bm{u}_{n+1}=\zeta_{1}\bm{u}_{1}+\zeta_{2}\bm{u}_{2}+\cdots+\zeta_{n}\bm{u}_{n}
. It follows from the strict convexity of
C_{p,q}
and Jensen’s inequality that

\zeta_{1}C_{p,q}(\bm{u}_{1})+\zeta_{2}C_{p,q}(\bm{u}_{2})+\cdots+\zeta_{n}C_{p,q}(\bm{u}_{n})>C_{p,q}(\bm{u}_{n+1}),

(52)

which contradicts the fact that
C_{p,q}(\bm{u}_{1})=C_{p,q}(\bm{u}_{2})=\cdots=C_{p,q}(\bm{u}_{n+1})=a
. Since there are at most
d
linear independent vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there are at most
(d+1)
points in
\mathcal{C}_{a}
, or else the above contradiction will occur.

Case 2.
If
\sum_{o=1}^{n}\zeta_{o}^{\prime}=0
, then we assume
\zeta_{n}^{\prime}\neq 0
without loss of generality, because
\zeta_{1}^{\prime}
,
\zeta_{2}^{\prime}
,
\cdots
,
\zeta_{n}^{\prime}
are not all zero. Then (
51
) becomes

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}^{\prime}\bm{u}_{1}+\zeta_{2}^{\prime}\bm{u}_{2}+\cdots+\zeta_{n-1}^{\prime}\bm{u}_{n-1}=-\zeta_{n}^{\prime}\bm{u}_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{o=1}^{n-1}\zeta_{o}^{\prime}=-\zeta_{n}^{\prime}.

(53)

Let
\zeta_{j}=\frac{\zeta_{j}^{\prime}}{\sum_{o=1}^{n-1}\zeta_{o}^{\prime}}
,
\forall j\in\{1,\cdots,n-1\}
. Then
\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\zeta_{j}=1
and (
53
) becomes
\bm{u}_{n}=\zeta_{1}\bm{u}_{1}+\zeta_{2}\bm{u}_{2}+\cdots+\zeta_{n-1}\bm{u}_{n-1}
. Again by the strict convexity of
C_{p,q}
and Jensen’s inequality,

\zeta_{1}C_{p,q}(\bm{u}_{1})+\zeta_{2}C_{p,q}(\bm{u}_{2})+\cdots+\zeta_{n-1}C_{p,q}(\bm{u}_{n-1})>C_{p,q}(\bm{u}_{n}),

(54)

which contradicts the fact that
C_{p,q}(\bm{u}_{1})=C_{p,q}(\bm{u}_{2})=\cdots=C_{p,q}(\bm{u}_{n})=a
. Since there are at most
d
linear independent vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there are at most
(d+1)
points in
\mathcal{C}_{a}
, or else the above contradiction will occur. Summarizing Case 1 and Case 2,
\mathcal{C}_{a}
is a finite set and
\{\mathbf{M}\}\bigcup\big(\mathcal{S}_{p}\bigcap\mathcal{C}_{a}\big)
is also a finite set.
∎

B.8
Proof of Theorem
11

Proof.

To verify whether
\bm{y}_{(k)}
is a minimum point, we can first check if
\bm{y}_{(k)}
belong to
\mathcal{S}_{p}
. If
\bm{y}_{(k)}\notin\mathcal{S}_{p}
, then Corollary
2
implies that
\mathbf{T}_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})=\bm{y}_{(k)}\Leftrightarrow

\bm{y}_{(k)}=\bm{x}_{*}
. Thus, we can verify whether
\bm{y}_{(k)}
is a fixed point of
\mathbf{T}_{p,q}
. If
\bm{y}_{(k)}\in\mathcal{S}_{p}
, then we can directly check whether
\mathbf{0}_{d}\in\partial C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})
by (
15
).

By Theorems
1
and
7
, we know that the sequence
\{C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is non-increasing. Furthermore, since
C_{p,q}(\bm{y})\geqslant 0
,
\{C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded below. Therefore, we can conclude that the sequence
\{C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k)})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges.

If
\{\bm{y}_{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
hits
\mathcal{S}_{p}
for finite times, then we can only consider the rear of the sequence that has already passed all the singular points, still denoted by
\{\bm{y}_{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\{\bm{y}_{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\cap\mathcal{S}_{p}=\emptyset
. Now we prove that the sequence
\{\bm{y}_{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges by showing that there exists only one limit point for
\{\bm{y}_{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. Lemma
10
indicates that there exist only a finite number of limit points for
\{\bm{y}_{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, denoted by
\bm{y}_{1*},\bm{y}_{2*},\dots,\bm{y}_{o*}
. Consider the subsequence
\{\bm{y}_{(k_{v})}\}_{v\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\lim\limits_{v\to\infty}\bm{y}_{(k_{v})}=\bm{y}_{1*}
. Let
\delta=\frac{\min\limits_{i,j\in\{1,2,\dots,o\},i\neq j}\|\bm{y}_{i*}-\bm{y}_{j*}\|_{2}}{2}>0
. Then we consider a
\delta
-neighborhood around
\bm{y}_{1*}
, denoted by
B(\bm{y}_{1*},\delta)
. Then there exists only one limit point in
B(\bm{y}_{1*},\delta)
. We can choose a subsequence of
\{\bm{y}_{(k_{v})}\}_{v\in\mathbb{N}}
, denoted by
\{\bm{y}_{(k_{v_{n}})}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, such that
\{\mathbf{T}_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k_{v_{n}})})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\cap B(\bm{y}_{1*},\delta)=\emptyset
, because there are other limit points
\bm{y}_{j*}\neq\bm{y}_{1*}
. Then

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{T}_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k_{v_{n}})})\neq\bm{y}_{1*}.

(55)

If
\bm{y}_{1*}\notin\mathcal{S}_{p}
, then it follows from the continuity of
\mathbf{T}_{p,q}
that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{T}_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k_{v_{n}})})=\mathbf{T}_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{1*})
, which together with (
55
) indicates that
\mathbf{T}_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{1*})\neq\bm{y}_{1*}
. Then from Theorem
1
,
C_{p,q}(\mathbf{T}_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{1*}))<C_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{1*})
, which violates (
50
). If
\bm{y}_{1*}\in\mathcal{S}_{p}
, then from Lemma
8
,
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{T}_{p,q}(\bm{y}_{(k_{v_{n}})})=\bm{y}_{1*}
, which violates (
55
). Therefore, there exists only one limit point for
\{\bm{y}_{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.
∎

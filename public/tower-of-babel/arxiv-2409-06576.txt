1.
Introduction and main results

Let
\Omega
be a smooth, bounded and simply connected domain of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We are interested on the number of critical points of the solutions
u_{\beta}
of the following semilinear elliptic problem with Robin boundary conditions

\hypertarget{PB}{(\text{P}_{\beta})\qquad}\begin{cases}-\Delta u=f(u)&\text{in }\Omega\\
\partial_{\nu}u+\beta u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function,
\nu
denotes the other unit normal vector to
\partial\Omega
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
is a positive parameter.

This is a classical problem in partial differential equations and it is well known that the shape of the solutions is strongly influenced by the geometry of the domain
\Omega
and, of course, by the nonlinearity
f
.

A first interesting result linking the geometry and the topology of the domain with the geometry of the solution
u
can be deduced from the Poincaré-Hopf Theorem, for instance see
[
22
]
. In particular it follows that if
u
is a positive solution of
-\Delta u=f(u)
such that
\partial_{\nu}u\not=0
on
\partial\Omega
, with isolated critical points
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
, then one can prove

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{ind}(\nabla u,{x_{i}})=(-1)^{N}\chi(\Omega),

where
\chi(\Omega)
is the Euler characteristic of
\Omega
. Recall that if
y
is an isolated zero of a vector field
T
then we denote by
\mathrm{ind}(T,y):=\deg(T,B_{r}(y),\mathbf{0})
the Browner degree of
T
in a ball centered in
y
of radius
r>0
small enough (let us point out that the previous formula is general and does not depend on the fact that
u
is a solution of an elliptic equation). Furthermore, it is natural to ask when the sum reduces to a minimal number of elements. Hence, if
\Omega
is a contractible domain we have
\chi(\Omega)=1
and then the question becomes to investigate under which conditions uniqueness of the critical point holds or not.

We start by recalling some result in the Dirichlet case. Here the literature is very wide and it is impossible to give a complete list of references, so we mainly focus on the results which are more strictly related to the rest of the paper.

A first important result has been proved by Makar-Limanov for the torsion problem, i.e.
f\equiv 1
. In
[
20
]
, it is proved that if
\Omega
is a convex domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then the solution of the torsion problem has a unique nondegenerate critical point. Moreover, as mentioned in
[
19
]
, the
1/2
strict concavity of the solution is proved, i.e.
u^{1/2}
is a concave function.

A similar result is true for the first Dirichlet eigenfunction,
f(u)=\lambda u
, as it was proved by Brascamp and Lieb
[
6
]
(se also the paper by Acker, Payne and Philippin
[
1
]
). In this case the solution turns out to be
\log
-concave, that is
\log(u)
is concave. In particular, in both cases we have that the superlevel sets
\{u>c\}
are convex.

A very general result on the uniqueness of the critical point of solutions can be deduced from the seminal paper
[
13
]
by Gidas, Ni and Nirenberg. Indeed, if
f
is a Lipschitz continuous function and
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a smooth and bounded domain which is symmetric with respect to the hyperplanes
x_{i}=0
for any
i=1,\dots,N
and convex with respect to any direction
x_{1},\dots,x_{N}
, then
u
has exactly one critical point and moreover the superlevel sets are star-shaped with respect to the origin (but in general they are not convex, see the work by Hamel, Nadirashvili and Sire
[
18
]
).

To remove the symmetry assumptions on
\Omega
, keeping
f
very general is an hard task. A very interesting contribution in this direction is the result in
[
7
]
by Cabré and Chanillo, where the uniqueness of the critical point is proved for stable solutions in convex planar domains with strictly positive curvature of the boundary
\partial\Omega
.
We recall that a solution
u
is said to be
stable
if the linearized operator at
u
is non negative definite. The theorem has been extended to the case of vanishing curvature in
[
12
]
. We point out that it is still an open problem to prove if the superlevel sets of stable solutions are convex or not, see
[
18
]
again.
The preceding results had been extended also to the case of manifolds, see the recent paper
[
17
]
.

All the results mentioned before hold in convex domains and it is well known that, in general, we cannot expect uniqueness of the critical point in non convex domains, as shown for example by the case of the torsion problem in a dumbbell domain (see for instance
[
24
]
).
Sometimes, it is still possible to recover uniqueness of the critical point in non convex domain, under suitable assumptions, see for instance
[
5
]
and
[
16
]
.

Also in the case
\Omega
is "not far" from being convex and the minimum of the curvature of
\partial\Omega
is very close to
0
the situation may change drastically: indeed, not only the uniqueness of the critical point is lost, but it is not even possible to have a bound on the number of critical points, see
[
15
]
.

In the case of Robin boundary conditions there are no many results, to our knowledge. The first one we want to mention is due to Sakaguchi in
[
23
]
. Here, uniqueness of the critical point is proved for solutions of
(\text{P}_{\beta})
if one assumes that the nonlinearity

satisfies
f^{\prime}\leq 0
and
f(0)<0
.
As a particular case, the author also shows the result for the solution of the torsion problem
f\equiv 1
.

In this last case it is also possible to see that, if
\beta
is large enough (i.e. if we are close to the Dirichlet case),
u_{\beta}
is strictly
1/2
-concave as proved by Crasta and Fragalà in
[
10
]
. They also show that the first Robin eigenfunction of the Laplacian is strictly
\log
-concave, again for
\beta
large. In particular this implies
1

note that with the notation in
[

]

\log

It is interesting to point out here that
\log
-concavity of the first Robin eigenfunction of the Laplacian is no more true if

is close to

, that is if we are close to the Neumann case. This has been proved by Andrews, Clutterbuck and Hauer in
[
2

.

In this paper we want to study the number critical point of stable solutions of problem
(\text{P}_{\beta})
, extending Cabré and Chanillo’s result in
[
7
]
from the Dirichlet to the Robin case. Let us mention that in general there is no hope to cover also the Neumann case, see Remark

Finally, we show that as soon as the domain is no more convex, even if very close to be, uniqueness of the critical point is lost for
\beta
large, as in the Dirichlet case.

Before stating our main result, let us recall the definition of stable solution for the case of Robin boundary conditions.

Definition 1.1
.

u_{\beta}
solution of problem
(\text{P}_{\beta})
is said to be
stable
if the linearized operator at
u_{\beta}
is nonnegative definite, i.e. if for all
\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
one has

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi^{2}\,d\sigma-\int_{\Omega}f^{\prime}(u_{\beta})|\varphi|^{2}\,dx\geq 0.

We can now state the main result of the paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a smooth and bounded domain such that the curvature
\kappa
of its boundary is strictly positive, i.e.
\kappa>0
on
\partial\Omega
and
f\geq 0
not identically equal to
0
. Assume that there exists
\beta_{0}>0
such that, for all
\beta>\beta_{0}
, problem
(\text{P}_{\beta})
has a stable solution
u_{\beta}
that satisfies

(1.1)

\left\|u_{\beta}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C,

for some
C:=C(\beta_{0})>0
. Then, for all
\beta>\beta_{0}
,
u_{\beta}
has a unique critical point, which is a nondegenerate maximum.

Remark 1.2
.

We would like to emphasize that the previous result is not obtained by perturbing the corresponding Dirichlet problem when
\beta\to\infty
. On the contrary, it holds for every positive
\beta
as soon as
1.1
holds.

Next we state as a corollary some very important cases where the preceding theorem applies.
Indeed, under the assumptions stated below, it is possible to show that for all
\beta_{0}>0
there exists
C:=C(\beta_{0})>0
such that

\left\|u_{\beta}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C,

that is assumption
1.1
in Theorem
1.1
is satisfied.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a smooth and bounded domain such that the curvature
\kappa
of its boundary is strictly positive, i.e.
\kappa>0
on
\partial\Omega
. Assume
f
satisfies one of the following

(i)

f(t)=\lambda_{\beta}t
where
\lambda_{\beta}:=\lambda_{\beta}(\Omega)
is the first Robin eigenvalue in
\Omega
;

(ii)

f(t)\equiv 1
;

(iii)

f(t)=\lambda g(t)
where
g
is a smooth function that satisfies
g(0)>0
,
g^{\prime}(t)>0
, for all
t\geq 0
, and
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{D}^{*})
, where
\lambda_{D}^{*}:=\lambda_{D}^{*}(\Omega)>0
is a constant depending only on the domain
\Omega
.

If (i) or (ii) hold true, then the solution
u_{\beta}
of problem
(\text{P}_{\beta})
has a unique critical point, which is a nondegenerate maximum, for all
\beta>0
.
If (iii) is satisfied the same conclusion holds provided
u_{\beta}
is a minimal solution (see Theorem
3.3
).

Remark 1.4
.

(i)

We recall that in the case of the Robin torsion function the result is not new, since it follows from
[
23
, Theorem 2]
.

(ii)

Typical examples of
f
satisfying (iii) are the model nonlinearities
f(t)=\lambda e^{t}
or
f(t)=\lambda(1+t)^{p}
,
p>0
and
\lambda>0
. We refer to Section
3.4
for more details.

Remark 1.5
.

As can be deduced from Corollary
1.3
, it is possible to see that uniqueness of the critical point can be verified for any value of
\beta>0
:
indeed in all of the cases (i)-(iii) in Corollary
1.3
, the assumption
1.1
can be verified for all
\beta_{0}>0
, see Section
3
for the details.

Moreover, note that, if for instance
f\equiv 1
,
1.1
holds for all
\beta_{0}>0
, but it is easy to see that that
C(\beta_{0})\to+\infty
as
\beta_{0}\to 0
as shown in
[
25
, Theorem 1]
.

Remark 1.6
.

We would like to point out that even in the cases where uniqueness of the critical point holds for any
\beta>0
, it is not possible to extend the result to the case of the Neumann boundary condition

\partial_{\nu}u=0,\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,

that is
\beta=0
in
(\text{P}_{\beta})
. Indeed it is a famous result of Casten and Holland
[
9
]
and Matano
[
21
]
that, in this case, non constant stable solutions do not exist in convex domains.

Remark 1.7
.

Finally, we remark that the claim of Corollary
1.3
, and in turn the one of Theorem
1.1
, is no longer true as soon as the domain is no more convex, even if it is very close to be. Indeed, in this case it is possible to find solutions with an arbitrary large number of critical points for
\beta
large. We refer to Remarks
3.2
and
3.4
in Section
3
for further details. It remains to treat the case of general convex domains where the curvature could vanish somewhere.

The key point in the proof of Theorem
1.1
consists in proving that for any
\beta>\beta_{0}
all the critical points of

are nondegenerate, see Corollary
2.10
and then by mean of a topological degree argument it is not hard to conclude that
u_{\beta}
has the same number of critical points of a solution of the corresponding Dirichlet problem, which, from the previous discussion, it is known to be exactly one.

Finally, to prove Corollary
1.3
we show that assumption
1.1
can be verified for all
\beta_{0}>0
and then, as a consequence, it is possible to desume the uniqueness of the critical point for all
\beta>0
.

The paper is organized as follows: in the next section we state and prove the convergence result to the solution of the corresponding Dirichlet problem, see Theorem
2.1
, and then we prove Theorem
1.1
. In Section

we deal with cases (i)-(iii) of Corollary

2.
Proof of the main result

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
. To this end we firstly show that for
\beta\to+\infty
the solutions
u_{\beta}
converge to the one of the corresponding Dirichlet problem. This is stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, for
\beta\to+\infty
one has

u_{\beta}\to u_{D},\quad\text{in }\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}),

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and where
u_{D}
is a positive and stable solution of the associated Dirichlet problem

\hypertarget{PD}{(\text{P}_{D})\qquad}\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{D}=f(u_{D})&\text{in }\Omega\\
u_{D}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

The proof of this result can be deduced as the one of
[
10
, Theorem 2.1]
, up to small modifications. For the sake of completeness we report it here below.

Remark 2.2
.

As can be easily deduced from the proof, the theorem holds in a more general setting: indeed the convexity assumption on
\Omega
does not play any role and it is possible to prove it in any dimension.

The rest of the section is organized as follows: we start by proving Theorem
2.1
and giving some preliminary result, finally in Subsection
2.2
we are able to prove Theorem
1.1

2.1.
Preliminary results

Let us start with the proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem
2.1

For all
\beta>\beta_{0}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla u_{\beta}\rvert^{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\lvert u_{\beta}\rvert^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla u_{\beta}\rvert^{2}\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}\lvert u_{\beta}\rvert^{2}\,d\sigma+\int_{\Omega}\lvert u_{\beta}\rvert^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f(u_{\beta})u_{\beta}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\lvert u_{\beta}\rvert^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{\beta_{0}}\max_{[0,C_{\beta_{0}}]}\lvert f\rvert\lvert\Omega\rvert+C_{\beta_{0}}^{2}\lvert\Omega\rvert.

Hence, we can apply
[

and in turn the classical Sobolev embedding theorems to obtain

\left\|u_{\beta}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C,

for some

and

u_{\beta}\to u,\quad\text{in }\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\text{ as }\beta\to+\infty.

It is then trivial to see that

satisfies the equation

and that for all

u(x)=\lim_{\beta\to+\infty}u_{\beta}(x)=\lim_{\beta\to+\infty}-\frac{\partial_{\nu}u_{\beta}(x)}{\beta}=0.

To conclude the proof it is enough to observe that

in
\Omega
by the Maximum Principle and that for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
one has

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi^{2}\,d\sigma\geq\int_{\Omega}f^{\prime}(u_{\beta})|\varphi|^{2}\,dx,\quad\forall\beta>\beta_{0}.

Then the stability of

is proved once we pass to the limit in the preceding equation.

The next lemma is nothing else than maximum principle and Hopf’s boundary point lemma. The proof can be found for instance in
[

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a smooth and bounded domain and let
u\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
be a solution of

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=f(u)&\text{in }\Omega\\
\partial_{\nu}u+\beta u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

for some smooth
f:\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty)
,
f\not\equiv 0
and
\beta>0
. Then
u>0
in
\overline{\Omega}
and
\partial_{\nu}u<0
on
\partial\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\overline{\Omega}
be such that

u(x)=\min_{\overline{\Omega}}u.

By the Maximum Principle we have that
x\in\partial\Omega
and in particular
\partial_{\nu}u(x)<0
. Hence, if we assume by contradiction that
u(x)\leq 0
we get

\partial_{\nu}u(x)+\beta u(x)<0,

which is in contrast with the Robin boundary condition. Then
u>0
in
\overline{\Omega}
and

\partial_{\nu}u=-\beta u<0,\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.\qed

.

\deg(B_{r}(x_{\bar{\beta}}),\nabla u_{\bar{\beta}},\mathbf{0})=-1.

.

3.
Particular cases: the proof of Corollary
1.3

In this section we illustrate some important cases where it is possible to apply Theorem
1.1
. In particular we cover all the cases (i)-(iii) of Corollary
1.3
providing its proof. To do it, we show that assumption
1.1
can be verified for all
\beta_{0}>0
.

Moreover, for
\beta
large, we also exhibit counterexamples to the main results as soon as the convexity assumption on the domain is no longer satisfied. In particular we show that the result is false even for domains very close to convex ones where it is possible to find solutions with an arbitrarily large number of critical points.

3.1.
First eigenfunction

For all
\beta>0
let
u_{\beta}
be the first Robin eigenfunction, i.e. the positive solution of

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{\beta}=\lambda_{\beta}u_{\beta}&\text{in }\Omega\\
\partial_{\nu}u_{\beta}+\beta u_{\beta}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

normalized in such a way that
\left\|u_{\beta}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1
. Here
\lambda_{\beta}:=\lambda_{\beta}(\Omega)
is the first Robin eigenvalue in
\Omega
which can be characterized as

\lambda_{\beta}=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)\\
\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)=1}\end{subarray}}\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla\psi\rvert^{2}\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}\psi^{2}\,d\sigma.

Taking into account that
\lambda_{\beta}\to\lambda_{D}:=\lambda_{1}(-\Delta,\Omega)
, i.e. the first Dirichlet eigenvalue in
\Omega
, as
\beta\to+\infty
we can apply Theorem
1.1
to prove that
u_{\beta}
has exactly one critical point for all
\beta>0
, that in particular is a nondegenerate maximum. To be precise, let us point out that in this case
f
depends on
\beta
, but
f(u_{\beta})=\lambda_{\beta}u_{\beta}
is uniformly bounded in
\beta
and then the proof of Theorem
1.1
can be easily adapted to this situation.

Remark 3.1
.

(1)

Clearly, uniqueness of the critical point does not hold for
\beta=0
, that is the case of Neumann boundary conditions. Indeed in this case the first eigenfunction is constant.

(2)

We recall that uniqueness of the critical point was yet known for large values of
\beta
as a consequence of
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
on strictly convex domains in any dimension.

(3)

It is interesting to point out that uniqueness of the critical point holds true also for small values of
\beta>0
, despite there exist convex domains where
u_{\beta}
is not
\log
-concave for such small values of
\beta
, as shown by Andrews, Clutterbuck and Hauer in
[
2
, Corollary 1.5]
.

3.2.
Torsion problem

For
f\equiv 1
, let
u_{\beta}
be the Robin torsion function. We know from
[
25
, Theorem 1]
that

\left\|u_{\beta}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\lambda_{\beta}}\log\left(C_{2}\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}_{\beta}}{\beta}\right)\right),

for some dimensional constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
and where
\lambda_{\beta}
is the first Robin eigenvalue in
\Omega
consistently with the previous section. Taking into account that
\lambda_{\beta}\to\lambda_{D}
as
\beta\to+\infty
and, as pointed out in
[
14
]
3
3

3

See equation (6) on page 604.
, considering that

\lim_{\beta\to 0}\frac{\lambda_{\beta}}{\beta}=\frac{\lvert\partial\Omega\rvert}{\lvert\Omega\rvert},

it follows that for all
\beta_{0}>0

\left\|u_{\beta}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C,\quad\text{for all }\beta\geq\beta_{0},

for some
C=C(\beta_{0})>0
. Then we can apply Theorem
1.1
to prove that
u_{\beta}
has exactly one critical point for all
\beta>0
.

Let us recall that this result is not new, since it was proved in
[
23
, Theorem 2]
.
Moreover a stronger concavity property holds true for large values of
\beta
and in any dimension, provided the domain is strictly convex. Indeed in this case,
[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
shows that
u_{\beta}
is strictly
1/2
-concave.

Remark 3.2
.

We conclude this part by pointing out that the result is no longer true if the domain is no more convex, even if very close to a convex one. In this case uniqueness of the critical point may be not true. Going into details, for all
\beta>0
, given any integer
k\in\mathbb{N}
and any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a smooth and bounded domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that if
u_{\beta}^{\varepsilon}
solves

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{\beta}^{\varepsilon}=1&\text{in }\Omega_{\varepsilon}\\
\partial_{\nu}u_{\beta}^{\varepsilon}+\beta u_{\beta}^{\varepsilon}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon},\end{cases}

then

i)

\Omega_{\varepsilon}
is star-shaped with respect to an interior point,

ii)

u_{\beta}^{\varepsilon}
has at least
k
maximum points,

iii)

\Omega_{\varepsilon}
locally converges to the strip
\mathcal{S}:=\mathbb{R}\times(0,1)
, i.e. for all compact sets
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
it holds
\lvert\mathcal{K}\triangle(\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap\mathcal{S})\rvert\to 0
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
,

iv)

the curvature of
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}
changes sign, vanishes at exactly two points and its minimum goes to
0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

This fact comes from the corresponding situation in the case of Dirichlet boundary conditions, as proved in
[
15
]
.
Indeed, let
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
be the domain in
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
and
u_{D}^{\varepsilon}
the Dirichlet torsion function on it with
k
non degenerate maxima (the nondegeneracy can be deduced as in
[
11
, Lemma 3.3]
). Hence, it is easy to see through degree arguments and thanks to Theorem
2.1
that there exists
\bar{\beta}>0
such that

(\bar{\beta},+\infty)\subseteq\set{\beta>0:u_{\beta}^{\varepsilon}\text{ has at least }k\text{ non degenerate maxima}},

since
u_{D}^{\varepsilon}
has at least
k
non degenerate maxima, in virtue of
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
.

3.3.
Bounded nonlinearity
f

As a consequence of the previous section we can observe that the assumption
1.1
in Theorem
1.1
is satisfied also if the nonlinearity
f
is bounded. Indeed, let
M:=\max\lvert f\rvert
and
v_{\beta}
be the solution of

\begin{cases}-\Delta v=M&\text{in }\Omega\\
\partial_{\nu}v+\beta v=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Then as a consequence of Lemma
2.4

u_{\beta}\leq v_{\beta}
where
u_{\beta}
is the solution of
(\text{P}_{\beta})
for such a bounded
f
.
From the discussion in the preceding section, for all
\beta_{0}>0
, there exists
C_{M}=C_{M}(\beta_{0})>0
such that
\left\|v_{\beta}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{M}
, for all
\beta\geq\beta_{0}
and in turn

\left\|u_{\beta}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{M},\quad\text{for all }\beta\geq\beta_{0}.

3.4.
Minimal branch for nonlinear problems

It is not always possible to consider the case of a general nonlinearity
f
. Indeed stable solutions could not exist.
We already mentioned that this is the case of the Neumann problem (
\beta=0
) in convex domains, see
[
9
,
21
]
. Furthermore, also for
\beta>0
stable solutions could not exist in general. For example,
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
says that if

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}\beta^{2}u_{\beta}^{2}\left(\beta-\kappa+\frac{f(u_{\beta})}{\beta u_{\beta}}\right)\,d\sigma<0,

\displaystyle\beta+\min_{\partial\Omega}\kappa\geq 0,

where
\kappa
is the curvature of the boundary of
\Omega
, then the solutions of
(\text{P}_{\beta})
are unstable.

That being said, let us assume
f:=\lambda g
where
\lambda>0
and
g
is a smooth function such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle g(0)>0,

(3.2)

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(t)>0,\quad\forall t\geq 0.

Classical examples are
g(t)=e^{t}
or
g(t)=(1+t)^{p}
,
p>0
.
Fix any
\beta>0
. Under this set of assumptions it is well known that there exists
\lambda_{\beta}^{*}:=\lambda_{\beta}^{*}(\Omega)>0
such that for all
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{\beta}^{*})
the problem

\hypertarget{PBl}{(\text{P}_{\beta}^{\lambda})\qquad}\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{\beta}^{\lambda}=\lambda g(u_{\beta}^{\lambda})&\text{in }\Omega\\
u_{\beta}^{\lambda}>0&\text{in }\Omega\\
\partial_{\nu}u_{\beta}^{\lambda}+\beta u_{\beta}^{\lambda}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

admits a minimal solution
u_{\beta}^{\lambda}
. Here minimal means that
u_{\beta}\leq U
in
\Omega
for any other solution
U
of
(\text{P}_{\beta}^{\lambda})
. It is also known that for
\lambda>\lambda_{\beta}^{*}
there are no solutions.
Moreover
u_{\beta}^{\lambda}
turns out to be stable. The same result holds true for the corresponding Dirichlet problem. In this case we denote by
\lambda_{D}^{*}:=\lambda_{D}^{*}(\Omega)>0
the critical threshold and for
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*}_{D})
we write
u_{D}^{\lambda}
for the minimal stable solution.
We refer, for instance, to the book
[
3
]
for the classical theory about this problem.

Following
[
3
]
, let us recall that a function
u
is called an
upper solution
of problem
(\text{P}_{\beta}^{\lambda})
if satisfies

\begin{cases}-\Delta u\geq\lambda g(u)&\text{in }\Omega\\
\partial_{\nu}u+\beta u\geq 0&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

We can now state the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a smooth and bounded domain such that the curvature
\kappa
of its boundary is strictly positive, i.e.
\kappa>0
on
\partial\Omega
and assume
f=\lambda g
where
g
is a smooth function that satisfies
3.1
and
3.2
. Then for all
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{D}^{*})
and for all
\beta>0
we have that the minimal solution
u_{\beta}^{\lambda}
of
(\text{P}_{\beta}^{\lambda})
has a unique critical point. In particular it is a nondegenerate maximum.

Proof.

It is enough to observe that for all
\beta_{2}\geq\beta_{1}>0
one has

\lambda_{D}^{*}\leq\lambda_{\beta_{2}}^{*}\leq\lambda_{\beta_{1}}^{*}.

and for all
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{D}^{*})

u_{D}^{\lambda}\leq u_{\beta_{2}}^{\lambda}\leq u_{\beta_{1}}^{\lambda},\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Indeed it is easy to see that given
\lambda<\lambda_{\beta_{1}}^{*}
, then
u_{\beta_{1}}^{\lambda}
is an upper solution for
(\text{P}_{\beta_{2}}^{\lambda})
and finally by the Picard iteration scheme one obtain the existence of
u_{\beta_{2}}^{\lambda}
and the monotonicity property
u_{\beta_{2}}^{\lambda}\leq u_{\beta_{1}}^{\lambda}
. The same argument shows
\lambda_{D}^{*}\leq\lambda_{\beta_{2}}^{*}
and
u_{D}^{\lambda}\leq u_{\beta_{2}}^{\lambda}
.

Thus all the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
are satisfied and the claim follows.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

As in the case of the torsion problem, the preceding Theorem
3.3
fails if the domain is close to be convex (but is not), in the sense that if
g
is increasing, convex and
g(0)>0
, we can argue exactly as in Remark
3.2
to generalize
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
to the case of Robin boundary conditions, with
\beta
large, for
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{D}^{*}(-1,1))
, here
\lambda_{D}^{*}(-1,1)
is the threshold of the one dimensional Dirichlet problem. Then we can see that uniqueness of the critical point is, in general, no more verified as soon as the boundary of the domain contains points with negative curvature, despite the domain is close to be convex.

Here again to argue as in Remark
3.2
we need the nondegeneracy of the critical points of the corresponding solution of the Dirichlet problem
u^{\varepsilon}_{D}
, which is not explicitly stated in
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
. Anyway, checking the proof of that theorem, it is easy to see that the critical points of
u^{\varepsilon}_{D}
are nondegenerate: indeed in any compact
K\subseteq\Omega_{\varepsilon}
,
\left\|u^{\varepsilon}_{D}-(v_{0}+\varepsilon\varphi)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}\leq C(K)\varepsilon^{2}
for some
C(K)>0
, where
v_{0}
and
\varphi
are fixed functions such that
v_{0}+\varepsilon\varphi
solves the linearized problem in a domain containing
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
and has at least
k
nondegenerate critical points, contained in a fixed, i.e. independent from
\varepsilon
, compact subsets of
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. Moreover, such critical points satisfy
\det\mathrm{Hess}{[v_{0}+\varepsilon\varphi]}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, see
[
11
, Lemma 2.3]
. It is then enough to use classical interior regularity estimate to obtain
\left\|u^{\varepsilon}_{D}-(v_{0}+\varepsilon\varphi)\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2}(K)}\leq C(K)\varepsilon^{2}
, where
C(K)>0
may be larger than before. This estimate, combined with the fact that the critical points of
v_{0}+\varepsilon\varphi
are nondegenerate, contained in fixed compact subsets and
\det\mathrm{Hess}{[v_{0}+\varepsilon\varphi]}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
, implies that
u^{\varepsilon}_{D}
admits at least
k
nondegenerate critical points, as claimed.

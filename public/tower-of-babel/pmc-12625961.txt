Epidemiology of brachydactyly type A3 in China: a nationwide multicentre population-based study among children aged 3–17 years
Authors: Wenshuang Zhang, Kai Li, Qian Zhang, Jian Geng, Ling Wang, Zhenyu Yang, Wenhai Wang, Yi Yuan, Yandong Liu, Bo Hu, Fengyun Zhou, Zitong Cheng, Qingyu Zhang, Dong Yan, Wenhua Zhao, Xiaoguang Cheng, Glen M Blake
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractObjectiveThis study aimed to investigate the prevalence of brachydactyly type A3 (BDA3) and its associated epiphysial development abnormalities in Chinese children aged 3–17 years, and to explore differences based on gender, region and urban–rural demographics.DesignCross-sectional study.SettingThe study was conducted across 14 provinces (28 survey sites) in China, as part of a nationwide investigation on skeletal maturation. The population was selected using multistage stratified randomised cluster sampling.ParticipantsA total of 17 850 children (8856 boys and 8994 girls) aged 3–17 years participated. The cohort was drawn from a large-scale survey conducted between 2019 and 2021. Selection criteria included children with no visible clinodactyly or hand function impairments.MethodsNon-dominant hand-wrist radiographs were obtained using a portable X-ray device. A retrospective analysis of these radiographs was performed to identify BDA3 and epiphysial development abnormalities. Prevalence rates were calculated and compared across gender, regional and urban–rural groups.ResultsThe overall prevalence of BDA3 was 10.0%, with a higher prevalence in girls (12.9% vs 7.1%; p<0.001). The northern region exhibited a higher prevalence compared with the southern region (11.5% vs 8.6%; p<0.001). No significant differences were observed between urban and rural areas (10.3% vs 9.8%; p=0.32). Among the BDA3 phenotypes, brachymesophalangia-5 with cone-epiphysis of mid-5 (BMP5) and BMP5 alone had prevalences of 3.6% and 6.4%, respectively. Both phenotypes were more common in girls compared with boys (p<0.001 for both).ConclusionsThis nationwide, multicentre study provides the first national epidemiologic data on BDA3 and associated epiphysial features in Chinese children and adolescents, establishing a prevalence of 10.0%. This baseline supports counselling that a straight, well-functioning short fifth finger is a common anatomic variant and may help reduce unnecessary concern.Keywords: Epidemiology, Prevalence, Child, China, Finger Phalanges

Introduction

Brachydactyly (BD) is a hereditary skeletal dysplasia characterised by shortened bones in the fingers or toes, caused by abnormal development of the phalanges and/or the metacarpal/metatarsal bones.1 2 Generally, the severity of BD in the hands is greater than in the feet, and this article focuses on BD of the hand.1 In paediatric health, congenital BD generally does not affect physical function. However, concerns about hand appearance may affect psychosocial well-being in some settings and cultural contexts. The classification of BD is primarily based on anatomical features, with Bell’s classification system being the most widely used.3 According to this classification, BD types A3 (BDA3) and D are the most prevalent.4 The diagnosis of hand BDA3 typically relies on X-ray imaging of the hand and wrist, with the most commonly used and relatively objective diagnostic criterion being Hertzog’s definition, which is the middle phalanx of the fifth finger (phalanx V) being less than half the length of middle phalanx of the fourth finger (phalanx IV).5

Extensive epidemiological studies on BDA3 and related conditions of digit shortening have been conducted globally, with the majority of research originating from Western countries.6 7 It has been reported that the detection rates of BDA3 vary widely among different ethnic groups, ranging from 0% to 25.6%.7 8 The significant variability and lack of precision in the data are primarily due to the infrequency with which patients seek medical attention solely for short digit deformities. Additionally, the accurate diagnosis of BDA3 requires a hand-wrist radiograph, further complicating the ability to determine its overall prevalence within the population accurately. Research on BDA3 in Asia is typically limited to small-scale studies focused on specific populations and may not be truly representative. In China in particular, research is often confined to specific regions and narrowly defined groups.9 10 Furthermore, it has been reported that BDA3 often accompanies abnormalities in epiphysial development and premature closure of the growth plates, which may lead to short stature in adulthood or a deformity of the fifth finger.7 10 11 Mechanistically, growth plate morphogenesis is influenced not only by genetic determinants but also by the local biochemical and mechanical microenvironment, which can modulate osteogenesis and epiphysial development.12 13 Although a direct causal link to BDA3 has not been established and the probability of these outcomes appears low, they warrant attention.

To date, there is a pronounced deficiency of comprehensive nationwide data on the prevalence of BDA3 in China. Existing studies are limited in scope and primarily focused on specific regions, failing to capture the vast geographic and ethnic diversity of the Chinese population. Consequently, the actual variations in BDA3 prevalence across different regions and demographic groups remain unclear. Therefore, there is an urgent need for a comprehensive, multicentre national study to obtain precise and region-specific prevalence data. This is essential for fostering a more precise and clear understanding of BDA3 among the general public, and for guiding informed health education without necessarily involving interventions or treatments.

The primary objective of this study is to assess the prevalence of BDA3 among children aged 3–17 across 14 provinces and municipalities in China, using non-dominant hand-wrist radiographs obtained from a nationwide, multicentre epidemiological survey. The secondary objective is to evaluate the prevalence of BDA3 associated with abnormalities in epiphysial development. Prevalence was estimated only among children without clinically visible clinodactyly or functional hand impairment, as per the population-based survey screening protocol.14

Methods

Study population

This cross-sectional analysis used data from the ‘National Nutrition and Health Systematic Survey for Children Aged 0–18 and Utilization of Survey Findings’ (ChiCTR1900022825), a national population-based survey at 28 sites in 14 provinces from August 2019 to October 2021. Within this survey, a prespecified skeletal maturation substudy (ClinicalTrials.gov ID: NCT04197557) obtained standardised hand and wrist radiographs; we included participants aged 3–17 years from this substudy. Eligibility followed the survey protocols: age <18 years, locally born and written informed consent. Children with acute or chronic diseases, clinically visible clinodactyly or functional hand impairment were not eligible. The analytical dataset was generated by the custodians of the parent national survey; records that lacked core anthropometric information were not released to the analytical database during centralised curation and therefore could not be accessed under the data-use and ethics approvals.

Sampling design

A multistage stratified randomised cluster sampling method was implemented across China.15 The process started by selecting two provinces from each of China’s seven geographic regions: South, Southwest, North, Northwest, East, Central and Northeast, resulting in a total of 14 provinces, including Zhejiang, Jiangxi, Shanxi, Beijing, Hunan, Henan, Guangdong, Guangxi, Yunnan, Chongqing, Shaanxi, Qinghai, Jilin and Liaoning. Within each province, one urban district and one rural county were randomly chosen, leading to a total of 28 survey sites that nationally represent both urban and rural areas. Four subdistricts or townships were then chosen from each survey site. One to two senior high schools were selected at each survey site. At each subdistrict or township, two to four kindergartens, one primary school and one junior high school were selected. In the chosen kindergartens, children aged 3 to under 6 years were then sampled using a random cluster sampling method. Similarly, children aged 6–18 years were sampled using a random cluster sampling method in the selected primary, junior and senior high schools. Gender balance was maintained for each age group and site. The sampling scheme and process are detailed in figure 1.

Flow chart of sampling and research design.

Acquisition of anteroposterior hand-wrist radiographs

To facilitate mobile and digital imaging, the project team specifically designed a self-shielded portable X-ray device (Xbone, Dymena Healthcare, Shanghai, China).16 Before deployment, the machine underwent various performance tests and was operated by experienced and qualified technicians from the Radiology Department of Beijing Jishuitan Hospital. After obtaining informed consent from the guardians, radiographs of the non-dominant wrist bones of the children were uniformly acquired. Hydroxyapatite phantoms and wrists were imaged simultaneously, with the phantom positioned approximately 2–3 cm from the edge of the wrist bone. To minimise radiation exposure, a through-target X-ray tube and a caesium iodide+amorphous silicon wireless flat-panel detector were used, with a focus-to-detector distance of 70 cm. Exposure conditions were set to a tube voltage of 70 kV, tube current of 0.20 mA and exposure time of 500 ms, with an incident dose ranging from 2.9 to 4.9 μGy.17 Images were stored in DICOM format. To protect the privacy of participants, all radiographs were anonymised.

Diagnosis of BDA3 and measurement of phalangeal length

Hertzog defined BDA3 as having the middle phalanx V less than half the length of middle phalanx IV.5 BDA3 frequently presents with abnormalities in epiphysial development, evidenced by early conical epiphyses on X-ray images. Additionally, BDA3 frequently involves a curved middle phalanx V, which may lead to a clinically observed slanting finger deformity. Irrespective of the presence of a conical epiphysis or a radial curvature in the middle phalanx V, any instance where the length of the middle phalanx V is less than half that of middle phalanx IV qualifies as BDA3. The length of the middle phalanx V was measured between the head and base of the bones in the hand-wrist radiograph.

The diagnosis of BDA3 and measurement of phalangeal lengths were carried out by two musculoskeletal radiologists, designated as measurer 1 and measurer 2. Radiographs designated for final analysis were evenly and randomly assigned to the two radiologists. The initial comprehensive evaluation was completed within a single week. To evaluate interobserver and intraobserver reliability, 30 radiographs initially assessed by measurer 1 were randomly selected for reassessment. Both radiologists conducted these repeat measurements of the middle phalanx V length after a 6-week interval from the initial assessments.

Anthropometry and nutritional status

Weight was measured in the morning after fasting using a digital scale (GMCS-I; Jianmin, Beijing, China; capacity 100 kg; resolution 0.1 kg).15 Height was measured with a stadiometer (Jianmin; accuracy 0.1 cm).15 Body mass index (BMI) was calculated as weight/height² (kg/m²). Nutritional status (malnutrition, obesity) was defined by age-specific and sex-specific BMI cut-offs.18 19

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (V.26.0; IBM) and GraphPad Prism (V.7.04; GraphPad Software, San Diego, California, USA). Continuous variables were expressed as means±SD or medians (25th quartile, 75th quartile). Categorical variables were expressed as numbers and percentages. The Shapiro-Wilk normality test was used to assess continuous data distribution. Interobserver and intraobserver agreement of length of the middle phalanx V results was determined by calculating the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC), which was interpreted as follows: <0.40 (poor), 0.40–0.58 (fair), 0.59–0.75 (good) and >0.75 (excellent).20 Comparison analysis was determined by the Wilcoxon rank sum test, one-way analysis of variance or χ² tests. P values <0.05 were considered to indicate statistical significance.

Results

Study population

The sampling phase of this study commenced on 26 August 2019 and concluded on 16 October 2021. All survey sites, with the exception of the urban site in Changsha, Hunan Province, which did not complete its sampling due to COVID-19 disruptions, successfully fulfilled their sampling objectives according to plan. Figure 1 depicts the flow chart, which includes the exclusion of ineligible data. A total of 20 444 anteroposterior hand-wrist radiographs were collected from children, including 10 196 boys and 10 248 girls, with 9711 from urban and 10 733 from rural survey sites.

On verification, 8 cases were identified where the radiographs were of the dominant hand-wrist, 13 cases involved motion artefacts leading to blurry images and 257 cases had missing birthdate information. A total of 20 166 radiographs with clear and qualified images and complete gender and birthdate information were initially included. Further data cleansing excluded 1770 cases due to missing height or weight information. As subsequent nutritional status classifications (malnutrition, normal, overweight, obesity) were based on the Health Industry Standards of the People’s Republic of China, ‘Screening Standard for Malnutrition of School-age Children and Adolescents’ (WS/T 456-2014)18 and ‘Growth Standard for Children Under 7 Years of Age’ (WS/T 423-2022),19 which only cover children under 18 years old, individuals older than 18 years were excluded, resulting in the removal of 546 cases. Ultimately, 17 850 cases were suitable for further statistical analysis.

Classifications of BDA3

The classification of BDA3 in this study includes two subtypes: brachymesophalangia-5 with cone-epiphysis of mid-5 (BMP5 with CM5), characterised by a shortened middle phalanx V accompanied by a conical epiphysis, and BMP5 alone, which involves an isolated shortening of the middle phalanx without additional epiphysial changes. The mere presence of a conical epiphysis in the middle phalanx V, known as only CM-5 (CM5 alone), without the significant shortening typical of BDA3, is an independent anatomical variation that does not qualify for the BDA3 classification. The diagrams of normal, BDA3 (BMP5 with CM5, BMP5 alone) and CM5 alone are shown in figure 2. The measurement of the length of the middle phalanx V is illustrated in figure 2b. Additionally, the interobserver and intraobserver reproducibility for measuring the length of the middle phalanx V was satisfactory (interobserver: ICC=0.966, p<0.001; intraobserver: ICC=0.967, p<0.001).

Prevalence of BDA3

The total prevalence of BDA3 was 10.0%. In this study, girls exhibited a higher prevalence of BDA3 compared with boys (12.9% vs 7.1%; p<0.001). Table 3 displays the prevalence of BDA3 and its distribution by sex across various age groups. Figure 3 illustrates the distribution of BDA3 across different age groups in southern and northern regions, as well as urban and rural areas. In total, the prevalence of BDA3 in the north was slightly higher compared with the south (11.5% vs 8.6%; p<0.001). The prevalence of BDA3 was comparable between urban and rural settings, with no statistically significant differences (10.3% vs 9.8%; p=0.32).

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first nationwide, large-scale epidemiological survey of BDA3 among children aged 3–17 years in China, distinguished by its rigorous sampling method and comprehensive scope. It provides a critical baseline assessment of the prevalence of BDA3 across a demographically and geographically diverse population in China, using a representative sample size that ensures the reliability and generalisability of the findings. This thorough approach not only fills a significant gap in the existing literature but also sets a new benchmark for the epidemiological study of genetic disorders within the paediatric population in China.

Importantly, our study evaluated a nationally representative sample of children and adolescents across China, achieved through a multistage stratified randomised cluster sampling method. This rigorous approach in sampling ensures that our population accurately reflects the diverse demographics of the country, providing substantial credibility and generalisability to our findings. Additionally, the hand-wrist radiographs analysed in this research were sourced from a national project, the ‘National Nutrition and Health Systematic Survey for Children Aged 0~18 and Utilization of Survey Findings’ (ChiCTR1900022825), conducted by the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention.15 This survey includes a specific study, ‘A large-sample investigation on skeletal maturation in 3 to 18 year-old children in China’ (ClinicalTrials.gov ID: NCT04197557), which required the collection of hand-wrist X-rays for assessing bone age and bone mineral density. By using these already existing X-rays, our study avoided any additional radiation exposure to participants, aligning with ethical research practices and maximising the use of previously gathered data. This method not only exemplifies the ethical and practical utilisation of secondary data but also highlights our commitment to minimising risk to participants while effectively using existing health data for substantial epidemiological research.

Gaining insight into the national prevalence of BDA3 across various demographics, including gender, region and urban–rural distinctions, is critical for a comprehensive understanding of the condition. Clinically, the provision of accurate prevalence data will improve awareness of BDA3 among parents and physicians. For mild cases without functional impairment, this epidemiological data can help reduce misconceptions and alleviate anxiety. For more severe cases where complications such as a curved middle phalanx V may arise during growth, early identification facilitated by these data can enhance monitoring and aid clinical decisions regarding optimal treatment timings. This is crucial even though the probability of complications like clinodactyly remains low.

Previous studies from other countries have reported a wide range in overall prevalence, from 0 to 25.6%, with 0 to 19.8% for boys and 0 to 28.8% for girls. International studies report highly variable prevalence, with rates of 8.6%–25.6% in Japanese cohorts, 1.0%–19.5% in Native American groups, 0%–2.1% in US populations of European or African descent1 6 8 and 10.5% (12.9% in males, 8.9% in females) in an endogamous Nepalese (Jirel) population.7 However, the prevalence of BDA3 in other countries may not be applicable to Chinese youths due to potential ethnic disparities in bone growth and development. Few studies on the prevalence of BDA3 in China have been conducted previously. Tang et al reported a prevalence of BDA3 at 11.2% based on 563 hand radiographs (290 left hands, 273 right hands and 169 bilateral hands) from one medical centre in Hong Kong, involving 394 patients.21 Two studies involved participants from single-centre growth and development clinics, with one focusing on relatively normal populations (prevalence 6.95%)22 and the other on children with short stature (prevalence 23.1%).9 One retrospective study analysed left hand X-rays taken for trauma evaluation in children and teenagers aged 4–20 years, reporting a prevalence of 15.4% and 19.5% for BDA3 and conical epiphysis, respectively.10 However, conclusions on gender differences are inconsistent across studies. All these studies suffer from significant limitations due to their single-centre, small-sample nature and specific reasons for radiograph acquisition, leading to substantial sampling bias. Therefore, their reported prevalence rates lack generalisability and national applicability.

In our study, we found the national prevalence of BDA3 to be 10.0%, which is lower than the prevalence reported in the previous study focusing on populations with short stature.9 Additionally, we observed sex differences in the prevalence of BDA3 among Chinese children and adolescents, with girls exhibiting a higher prevalence compared with boys (12.9% vs 7.1%). This disparity may be related to differences in hormone levels between the sexes. Furthermore, the slightly higher prevalence of BDA3 in northern China compared with southern China suggests potential regional heterogeneity. Importantly, our study investigated the national prevalence of BDA3 and focused on the epiphysial development of the middle phalanx V, specifically the phenotypes BMP5 with CM5 and BMP5 alone. We also analysed the prevalence of BMP5 with CM5, BMP5 alone and CM5 alone across different demographics, including gender, region (north vs south) and urban versus rural areas. Although our epidemiological design does not test mechanisms, recent studies indicate that genetic determinants and local biochemical and mechanical cues, including early magnesium ion immunomodulation and bioactive element signalling, may influence osteogenesis and growth plate and epiphysial development. These insights may help explain the variability in epiphysial features, such as cone epiphyses, observed in our population.12 13

Strengths and limitations

This study offers several strengths that contribute to the robustness of the findings. First, the use of a multistage stratified randomised cluster sampling method across 14 provinces ensures a geographically diverse and representative sample of Chinese children aged 3–17 years. This broad inclusion provides more accurate and generalisable data on the prevalence of BDA3, compared with previous regional or small-scale studies. Second, the comprehensive data collection, including hand-wrist radiographs from 17 850 children, ensures a reliable analysis of BDA3 prevalence and its associated epiphysial development abnormalities. Moreover, this study’s large, diverse sample, combined with its rigorous methodology, provides the first nationwide, population-based epidemiological data on BDA3 in China, marking a significant advancement in the understanding of this condition within the country.

There are several limitations in our study. First, the incomplete sampling from the urban site in Changsha, Hunan Province, due to COVID-19 disruptions, may slightly affect overall representativeness. Second, we enrolled children and adolescents without clinically visible clinodactyly or hand function impairments; accordingly, radiographs did not include cases of BMP5 associated with clinodactyly, precluding estimation of its prevalence. Third, because radiographs were limited to the non-dominant hand only and were obtained within a national survey with a prespecified skeletal-maturation substudy, unilateral right-sided cases could have been missed and laterality could not be assessed, which may introduce selection bias and underestimate prevalence. In addition, records missing anthropometrics were excluded during parent-survey curation and not released to our analytic dataset, potentially introducing selection effects. Fourth, as a cross-sectional study, this research only provides a snapshot of BDA3 prevalence at a single point in time, limiting the ability to evaluate temporal changes or the progression of the condition. Finally, our study does not fully account for all potential environmental and genetic factors influencing BDA3 prevalence. Future studies should estimate the prevalence of BMP5 associated with clinodactyly, a subtype with potential functional implications and surgical relevance, and incorporate comprehensive genetic analyses and environmental assessments to advance understanding of BDA3.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this nationwide, multicentre study provides the first national prevalence estimate of BDA3 among Chinese children aged 3–17 years (approximately 10% overall; higher in girls; slightly higher in northern regions). These findings support counselling that a straight, well-functioning short fifth finger is a common anatomical variant and can help reduce unnecessary concern.

Assessing deep learning accuracy in the measurement of radiographic parameters in pediatric hip X-rays
Authors: Byoung-Dai Lee, Jin Young Kim, Ki-Ryum Moon, Mu Sook Lee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAssessing radiographic parameters in pediatric pelvic X-rays is crucial for evaluating hip development, yet existing deep learning (DL)-based methods lack both age-specific reliability analysis and a comprehensive solution for measuring multiple key parameters.MethodsThis retrospective study developed and validated a DL-based system using separate, nonoverlapping datasets of 1495 and 1300 anteroposterior (AP) pelvic radiographs of normal Korean children for model training and evaluation, respectively. The system measured the acetabular index (AcI), Shenton line (ShL), pelvic rotation index (PRI), and pelvic tilt index (PTI). Subgroup analyses were conducted to evaluate the effects of age-related pelvic bone development. Evaluation metrics included the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC), Pearson correlation coefficient (r), mean absolute error (MAE), root mean square error (RMSE), Hausdorff distance (HD), and Frechet distance (FD). Agreement between the system’s and clinician’s measurements was assessed using Bland–Altman analysis.ResultsFor all evaluation data, automatically measured AcI, PRI, PTI, and ShL values strongly matched and correlated with radiologist-assessed values (AcI: ICC = 0.89, r = 0.91, MAE = 2.07°, RMSE = 2.99°; PRI: ICC = 0.94, r = 0.94, MAE = 0.03, RMSE = 0.04; PTI: ICC = 0.97, r = 0.97, MAE = 0.04, RMSE = 0.09; ShL: HD = 3.62 mm, FD = 2.27 mm). The subgroup analysis revealed that the system’s performance varied with age-related differences in pelvic bone development.ConclusionThe DL-based system exhibited high reliability and accuracy in measuring radiographic parameters for differentiating normal from dislocated hips and assessing pelvic radiograph quality.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12880-025-02058-5.Keywords: Acetabular index, Deep learning, Pediatric pelvic assessment, Pelvic alignment, Pelvic radiographs

Introduction

Accurate and consistent measurement of key radiographic parameters in pediatric pelvic X-rays is essential for diagnosing developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH) and other hip abnormalities [1–3]. Although deep learning (DL) has recently been applied to automate these measurements [3–12], existing approaches predominantly focus on individual parameters—most notably the acetabular index (AcI), a widely used marker for assessing DDH. However, these studies often overlook the challenges posed by anatomical variations across pediatric age groups, such as changes in pelvic bone morphology due to skeletal maturation and growth. These developmental changes, including epiphyseal fusion and alterations in bone landmarks, can significantly affect the performance and reliability of DL-based models, which limits their broader clinical applicability.

Moreover, while the use of DL for automated measurement of AcI has been relatively well-studied, other essential radiographic parameters—such as the pelvic rotation index (PRI) and pelvic tilt index (PTI), which are important for evaluating pelvic alignment and assessing the quality of radiographic imaging, as well as the Shenton line (ShL), a critical indicator of hip joint continuity and structural integrity—have received limited attention in this context. Some studies have attempted to classify DDH severity directly from image features without measuring specific radiographic parameters [4, 5]; however such approaches are limited in scope and provide less clinically interpretable information for assessing broader hip abnormalities. These parameters, when measured collectively, offer a comprehensive view of pelvic and hip health, which is particularly important for early diagnosis, treatment planning, and long-term monitoring in pediatric populations.

To address these gaps, this study aims to develop and evaluate a DL-based system that automatically measures AcI, PRI, PTI, and ShL from pediatric pelvic radiographs. The primary goal is to assess the impact of pelvic skeletal development on the accuracy and reliability of these automated measurements across pediatric age groups. By doing so, we seek to provide critical insights into the age-specific limitations and clinical applicability of DL models for pediatric hip assessments. Our approach delivers a unified, comprehensive framework for automated measurement, offering the potential to improve diagnostic consistency and support timely clinical decision-making throughout the pediatric growth spectrum. To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first to propose an end-to-end system that not only assesses radiographic quality and multiple diagnostic parameters simultaneously, but also rigorously evaluates performance across an extensive range of pediatric age subgroups.

Material and methods

This retrospective study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the affiliated medical center, which waived the requirement for written informed consent. All procedures followed the approved protocol.

Study population and datasets

To ensure an independent evaluation and to minimize data leakage, we prepared two separate datasets: one for algorithm development and another for diagnostic performance validation (Fig. 1). For algorithm development, we collected 1495 anteroposterior (AP) pelvic radiographs of normal Korean children from July 2008 to December 2023, each from a unique patient, using the picture archiving and communication system. “Normal children” were defined as those between the 3rd and 97th percentiles of height and body weight according to age and sex, based on the 2017 Korean National Growth Chart [13]. We excluded children who had any conditions affecting bone development, such as chronic illness, endocrinopathy, neuromuscular disease, intrinsic bone disease, metabolic disease, previous skeletal trauma, or surgery. Furthermore, only radiographs with pelvic alignment within the normal ranges for the pelvic rotation index and the pelvic tilt index were included to ensure anatomical positioning consistency for accurate analysis. The dataset was used to develop DL models for object detection, landmark detection, and segmentation tasks, and was divided into three nonoverlapping subsets: training (n = 910, 61%), validation (n = 195, 13%), and testing (n = 390, 26%). Landmarks (inferomedial and superolateral rims of the acetabular roof) were manually annotated by a board-certified radiologist, whereas bounding boxes and segmentation masks were manually annotated by four specialized medical data annotators under the supervision of the board-certified radiologist. For model training and inference, Digital Imaging and Communication in Medicine (DICOM) images were converted into 24-bit grayscale JPEG format to retain their original resolution and minimize any perceptible loss in image quality. The images had an average resolution of 2,300 × 2,000 pixels and were anonymized to protect patient privacy before use.

To evaluate the accuracy and reliability of the developed system, an additional 1300 AP pelvic radiographs of normal Korean children were collected from July 2008 to December 2023 via the picture archiving and communication system. The same inclusion and exclusion criteria as those used for the development dataset were applied. This evaluation dataset was curated to ensure balanced age group representation and to enable in-depth performance analysis across age-based subgroups and the overall population. Definitions of the radiographic parameters for pediatric pelvic assessment

We selected four radiographic parameters for automated measurements based on AP pelvic radiographs: acetabular index (AcI), Shenton line (ShL), pelvic rotation index (PRI), and pelvic tilt index (PTI). AcI and ShL are representative parameters for assessing DDH severity in children [11, 14], while PRI and PTI evaluate the pelvic radiograph quality and limitations [1, 3, 15, 16].

The AcI is the angle between a line drawn through the acetabular sourcil and Hilgenreiner’s line, which connects the triradiate cartilages of both hips [14, 17]. The ShL is a C-shaped line along the inferior border of the superior pubic ramus and the inferomedial border of the femoral neck; its continuity indicates normal alignment [17]. PRI quantifies horizontal pelvic position through the ratio of the right and left obturator foramina diameters, and PTI assesses sagittal pelvic tilt using the vertical diameter of the obturator foramen relative to the distance from the upper border of the pubic symphysis to Hilgenreiner’s line. In a neutral position, a PRI value of 1 is considered normal, with an acceptable range of 0.56 to 1.8 [15], while the normal range of PTI is 0.75 to 1.2 [15]. Precise pelvic alignment is essential for reliable DDH diagnosis, as misalignment due to pelvic rotation or tilt can significantly alter key diagnostic parameters [1, 2], leading to inconsistent measurements, particularly in young children, who may have difficulty cooperating during imaging [3]. Development of the DL-based automatic measurement system

The proposed system is based on a cascaded architecture comprising two stages (Fig. 3). In the first stage, six regions of interest (ROIs) related to the radiographic parameters under consideration are extracted, three on each side: acetabular roof, proximal femur, and obturator foramen. This task is accomplished using the YOLOv8x model [18]. In the second stage, different parameters are calculated through three separate branches.

Flowchart of automated deep learning-based measurement of radiographic parameters

The AcI branch identifies four key landmarks–right and left superolateral (R-SRAR and L-SRAR) and inferomedial (R-IRAR and L-IRAR) rims of the acetabular roof – using YOLOv8x-pose [19]for calculating AcI. The PRI/PTI branch uses a segmentation network to isolate the ischium and pubis from the cropped ROI, followed by extracting contours of the foramina, and calculating vertical and horizontal distances to derive the PRI. For PTI, an image processing algorithm locates the center of the upper border of the pubic symphysis and calculates the perpendicular distance to the Hilgenreiner line. The ShL branch applies a curve-fitting algorithm to draw ShL using segmentation masks of the proximal femur and pubis. All segmentation models used in the PRI/PTI and ShL branches are based on Fully Convolutional Branch-TransFormer (FBFormer) [20]. While the model structure and hyperparameters remained consistent in both branches, the models were trained on distinct datasets. Comprehensive details on model development and training are provided in Supplementary Appendices 1–3.

Nt represents the number of successfully detected landmarks within a reference threshold.

The mean average precision (mAP) was used to evaluate ROI detection, whereas the Dice similarity coefficient (DSC) [22] and Hausdorff’s distance (HD) [23] as evaluation metrics for bone segmentation. Furthermore, HD and Frechet distance (FD) [24]were used to evaluate the accuracy of the automatically generated ShLs. The precision-recall tradeoff across different threshold levels, corresponding to the average of the maximum precision at various recall levels, was evaluated using mAP. In general, DSC is a widely used metric to quantify the overlap between predicted and ground-truth segmentation masks, with a higher DSC indicating a greater degree of overlap between two masks. HD quantifies the degree of mismatch between two segmentation masks, with a lower HD suggesting more accurate segmentation results. The key advantage of FD lies in its ability to capture the continuity of the curves, which HD cannot do effectively. A smaller FD indicates a higher degree of similarity between the curves, reflecting a more accurate alignment in both shape and sequence.

The reliability and accuracy of the DL-based measurement system for the other three radiographic parameters (AcI, PRI, and PTI) were evaluated using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC), Pearson correlation coefficient (r), mean absolute error (MAE), and root mean square error (RMSE), considering the reference standard. Herein, ICC≥0.75 suggests adequate reliability [25], and |r|≥0.7 indicates a significant and positive relationship between the two measurements [26]. Additionally, the Bland–Altman plot was used to assess the agreement between reference standards and automatically measured values while identifying the presence of bias.

Alongside the performance analysis on the entire evaluation dataset, a subgroup analysis was conducted to examine the influence of age-related pelvic growth on the performance of the DL-based automatic measurement system. The evaluation dataset was grouped into 13 non-overlapping age-based subgroups.

Accuracy and reliability of parameter measurements

The automatically measured AcI, PRI, and PTI values closely matched and correlated with those obtained by the radiologist (AcI: ICC = 0.89, r = 0.91; PRI: ICC = 0.94, r = 0.94; PTI: ICC = 0.97, r = 0.97). An analysis of the MAE and RMSE values confirmed that the automatic measurements were robust and accurate, with low error margins (AcI: MAE = 2.07°, RMSE = 2.99°; PRI: MAE = 0.03, RMSE = 0.04; PTI: MAE = 0.04, RMSE = 0.09). On the entire evaluation dataset, the mean differences ± standard deviation between the reference standard and the automatically measured AcI, PRI, and PTI values were −1.12 ± 2.77°, 0.00001 ± 0.04, and −0.02 ± 0.08, respectively. Furthermore, the high reliability of the DL-based system was confirmed through a Bland–Altman analysis, showing close agreement with reference standards and no significant systematic bias (Figs. 4–6). The automated ShL demonstrated outstanding precision and reliability compared with the line manually drawn by the radiologist, with an average HD of 3.62 mm and an average FD of 2.27 mm. A performance analysis of the automatically measured PRI and PTI values for each subgroup yielded results comparable to those obtained for the entire dataset. According to the Bland–Altman analysis, the automatically measured PRI and PTI values generally demonstrated small mean differences and relatively narrow 95% limits of agreement (LoA) for all age groups, indicating close agreement and a strong correlation between the automated measurements and the reference standards across all age groups. However, an analysis of the automatically measured AcI values for each subgroup revealed different patterns than those observed for the PRI and PTI values. For certain age groups, particularly children over the age of 7, both ICC and r values were below 0.70, which is significantly lower than the 0.89 calculated for the entire dataset. This phenomenon was similarly observed through Bland–Altman analysis. For example, subgroups consisting of children over the age of 7 exhibited a noticeable increase in mean differences and variability. The same analysis was conducted based on gender. Consistent with observations from the entire dataset, the overall dataset exhibited strong agreement and reliability, while subgroups comprising children over the age of 7 showed relatively low reliability and agreement. Table 3 presents the performance analysis results for each subgroup, and Figs. 4–6 show the corresponding Bland–Altman plots for comprehensive comparison. The performance analysis by gender is presented in Supplementary Table 2 and Supplementary Figs. 1–6.

To further evaluate the impact of our region-guided design, we trained a direct end-to-end landmark detection model (YOLOv8x-pose) that predicted the four acetabular roof landmarks from full-resolution images without ROI preprocessing. This variant consistently underperformed across all age groups, achieving a mean ICC of 0.75 and an SDR of 39.31% at the 2-mm threshold, compared to 0.89 and 81.84% for our two-stage system. Discussion

The DL-based system demonstrated high reliability and accuracy across different stages of pelvic bone development, closely aligning with radiologist measurements through strong correlation metrics and low error margins across the entire dataset. However, the system had limitations in measuring AcI values. Notably, for certain age groups, especially older children, it exhibited increased variability and lower agreement metrics. Thus, the system faced challenges in accurately measuring AcI values owing to the anatomical and developmental complexities associated with such age groups. The observed increase in variability within such subgroups highlights the potential challenges faced by the system when dealing with the nuances of pelvic bone development and fusion, affecting acetabular roof isolation. The AcI is a critical radiographic factor for identifying morphological abnormalities of the hip joint in children aged 0–5 [27]. Despite the limitations of the DL-based system in identifying AcI in school-aged children, the AcI measurements for infants and toddlers automatically obtained by the proposed system showed close agreement with the reference standard, indicating that the AcI of children in these age groups can be accurately determined based on pelvic X-rays. Therefore, the proposed approach may contribute to the clinical evaluation and follow-up of acetabular morphologic development in young children. In addition to AcI, the system demonstrated strong performance in measuring PRI, PTI, and ShL across all age groups. Both PRI and PTI measurements, which are essential for evaluating pelvic orientation and alignment, demonstrated strong agreement with the reference standards across all age groups. These results suggest that the system can reliably quantify pelvic alignment parameters, making it a useful tool for clinical assessments. For ShL, the system maintained a low margin of error and high correlation with manual measurements across all age groups, highlighting its potential application in tracking skeletal growth and pelvic morphology in longitudinal studies.

Several studies have aimed to develop DL-based systems to automate the measurement of radiographic parameters related to DDH, particularly AcI. Wu et al. [7] used 1,000 pelvic radiographs to evaluate the reliability and accuracy of their DL-based automatic measurement system across four different age groups: under 6, 6–12, 12–24, and > 24 months. According to the Bland–Altman analysis for AcI measurements, the mean difference reported by Wu et al. (−0.27) was lower than that of our method (−1.12). In addition, the 95% LoA reported by Wu et al. was −4.02 to 3.48, indicating higher consistency and smaller differences from the reference standard than those of our method. We attribute this result to the distribution of evaluation data. The subgroup > 24 months comprised 87 samples, totaling 8.7% of the entire dataset, which included samples from age ranges where the pelvic bone was fused, making it difficult to isolate the acetabular roof. Due to the limited number of these samples, the measurement errors minimally affected the overall performance, resulting in better performance than that of our method. In our case, the 100 samples evenly distributed across each age subgroup enabled a more accurate performance analysis. Jan et al. [11] evaluated the accuracy of their DL-based measurement system using 355 pelvic radiographs of infants with a mean age of 4.5 months and a maximum age of 7 months, corresponding to subgroup 1 in our study. In this subgroup, our system demonstrated superior performance, achieving an MAE of 0.83° compared with Jan et al.’s reported MAE of 2.402°. The improvement in our study can be attributed to variations in the DL architecture, the volume of training data, and model optimization techniques. Xu et al. [12] analyzed the performance of their DL-based measurement system using four age groups: under 2, 2–6, 6–12, and > 12 years. They observed that the error between the predicted and ground-truth values remained stable across age groups. In comparison, our system achieved a lower MAE of 2.07° on the entire evaluation dataset, outperforming Xu et al.’s system, which reported an MAE of 2.776°. Xu et al. reported that the measurement error of AcI increased with the age of the children, which was also observed in our findings. Specifically, in the group aged > 12 years, the MAEs for the left and right AcI were 7.58° and 5.58°, respectively, which were significantly higher than those in other age groups. This finding is consistent with our observation that the distribution of patient ages impacted the performance of DL-based measurement systems. We speculate that these errors are due to increased difficulty in isolating the acetabular roof as pelvic bones grew and began to fuse, which adds complexity to the automatic detection of landmarks in this region and leads to greater errors in measurements. This emphasizes the need for age-specific tuning of the algorithm to maintain high accuracy across all age groups. In addition to age-specific tuning, the model architecture influences the landmark detection performance. Our results showed that a direct end-to-end approach—predicting landmarks from full images without pelvic region localization—consistently underperformed across all age groups, yielding a mean ICC of 0.75 and an SDR of 39.31% at the 2-mm threshold, compared to 0.89 and 81.84%, respectively, with our region-guided model. These findings support our hypothesis that constraining the search space improves robustness, particularly when the anatomical boundaries become indistinct owing to skeletal fusion. Similar limitations have been reported in the previous studies. For example, Pei et al. [6] observed an SDR of 33.89% using an end-to-end model of adult pelvic radiographs. Although the populations differed, the consistent underperformance of direct detection approaches suggests that explicit anatomical constraints may be beneficial at different developmental stages. Nevertheless, the trade-off between architectural simplicity and measurement accuracy warrants further investigation, particularly in older children, in whom landmark visibility is compromised. Although extensive research exists on DL-based systems for AcI measurement, we found no comparable automated measurement solutions for PRI, PTI, or ShL in the current literature, which underscores the novelty of our approach in evaluating these parameters. The strong performance of the proposed system in measuring PRI, PTI, and ShL demonstrates the utility of DL in pelvic morphology assessment and emphasizes the need for future validation and expansion using larger, diverse datasets.

Overall, our method differs from existing DL-based measurement systems in three key respects. First, we systematically analyzed the reliability of automated measurements across pediatric age groups, revealing that even in normal children, DL model accuracy declines in older age groups due to increased pelvic bone complexity and fusion. This highlights the necessity of detailed age-specific validation before extending DL-based radiographic assessments to pathological cases. Second, this study is among the first to assess a DL-based system for pelvic alignment (PRI, PTI) and automate ShL measurement, extending beyond AcI-focused research. Third, we employed state-of-the-art DL models, ensuring cutting-edge performance and accuracy in automated measurements.

This study has several limitations that may affect the applicability of the proposed system. First, all data were evaluated by a single radiologist from one institution, potentially introducing bias and limiting generalizability. Second, inter-observer and intra-observer reliability were not assessed, making it unclear how consistently the system performs across different observers over time. Third, only pelvic radiographs of normal Korean children were used, which excluded cases involving congenital abnormalities, metabolic conditions, or post-operative changes that could affect skeletal growth. A more comprehensive analysis considering DDH severity is needed. Fourth, our findings are based on a single state-of-the-art DL model for each task, requiring further validation with alternative models to confirm generalizability.

Conclusion

We developed and evaluated a DL-based system for the automatic measurement of radiographic parameters that are frequently used to distinguish normal from dislocated hip conditions and to assess pelvic radiograph quality. The system showed strong agreement with reference standards and no significant bias. However, increased variability in certain age groups highlights the need for refinement to ensure consistent accuracy across all ages. Therefore, future studies should focus on validating and optimizing the system’s performance across diverse populations, thereby enhancing its clinical utility in diagnosing and monitoring relevant conditions.

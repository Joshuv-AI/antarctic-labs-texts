Complete harmonic metrics and subharmonic functions on the unit disc
Natsuo Miyatake

Abstract
Let X X be a Riemann surface, K X → X K_{X}\rightarrow X the canonical bundle, and T X ≔ K X − 1 → X T_{X}\coloneqq K_{X}^{-1}\rightarrow X the dual bundle of the canonical bundle. For each integer r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 , each q ∈ H 0 ​ ( K X r ) q\in H^{0}(K_{X}^{r}) , and each choice of the square root K X 1 / 2 K_{X}^{1/2} of the canonical bundle, we canonically obtain a Higgs bundle ( 𝕂 r , Φ ⁡ ( q ) ) → X ({\mathbb{K}}_{r},\Phi(q))\rightarrow X , which is called a cyclic Higgs bundle. A diagonal harmonic metric is said to be complete if the Kähler metrics induced by H 1 , … , H r − 1 H_{1},\dots,H_{r-1} are all complete. Li-Mochizuki established a theorem stating that on any Riemann surface X X and any q q that is non-zero unless X X is hyperbolic, there exists a unique complete harmonic metric h h on ( 𝕂 r , Φ ⁡ ( q ) ) → X ({\mathbb{K}}_{r},\Phi(q))\rightarrow X with a fixed determinant. In this paper, we extend the uniqueness part of the theorem of Li-Mochizuki to any subharmonic weight function φ \varphi whose exponential is C 2 C^{2} outside a compact subset K ⊆ X K\subseteq X . We also show that on the unit disc 𝔻 ≔ { z ∈ ℂ ∣ | z | < 1 } {\mathbb{D}}\coloneqq\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\mid|z|<1\} , a complete Hermitian metric associated with φ \varphi always exists. 1
Introduction and main theorem

Li-Mochizuki
[
25
]
established a theorem stating that for any
r
-differential
q\in H^{0}(K_{X}^{r})
with
r\geq 2
, which is non-zero unless
X
is hyperbolic, there uniquely exists a complete harmonic metric with fixed determinant on the cyclic Higgs bundle associated with
q
.
The purpose of this paper is to extend the uniqueness part of this theorem to Hermitian metrics associated with more general subharmonic weight functions satisfying a certain regularity condition, and to establish both existence and approximation theorems for complete Hermitian metrics associated with subharmonic weight functions on the unit disc. The objective of this section is to state the concrete assertion of our main theorem. Before presenting the main theorem, we explain the background of cyclic Higgs bundles, harmonic metrics, and their extensions using subharmonic weight functions.

Let
X
be a connected Riemann surface and
K_{X}\rightarrow X
the canonical bundle. We choose a square root
K_{X}^{1/2}
of the canonical bundle
K_{X}\rightarrow X
. Let
{\mathbb{K}}_{r}
be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank
r
defined as follows:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{K}}_{r}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r}K_{X}^{\frac{r-(2j-1)}{2}}=K_{X}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\oplus K_{X}^{\frac{r-3}{2}}\oplus\cdots\oplus K_{X}^{-\frac{r-3}{2}}\oplus K_{X}^{-\frac{r-1}{2}}.

For each
q\in H^{0}(K_{X}^{r})
, we associate a holomorphic section
\Phi(q)\in H^{0}({\rm End}{\mathbb{K}}_{r}\otimes K_{X})
, which is called a Higgs field, as follows:

\displaystyle\Phi(q)\coloneqq\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&&&q\\
1&\ddots&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&1&0\end{array}\right).

The pair
({\mathbb{K}}_{r},\Phi(q))\rightarrow X
is called a cyclic Higgs bundle (cf.
[
2
]
). Since the cyclic Higgs bundle
({\mathbb{K}}_{r},\Phi(q))\rightarrow X
is a Higgs bundle, it is of course related to the general theory of Higgs bundles, but in this paper we would like to emphasize the view that a harmonic metric on it provides an extended concept of non-negative constant Gaussian curvature Kähler metrics, as the author has stated in other papers
[
30
,
31
]
. The connection between harmonic metrics on cyclic Higgs bundles and the non-negative constant curvature Kähler metrics is as follows: We consider the case where
r=2
for simplicity. This is the most famous example of Higgs bundles, which was introduced by Hitchin
[
19
, Section 11]
. Suppose that
X
is a compact Riemann surface. Firstly, if
X={\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, then
({\mathbb{K}}_{2},\Phi(q)=\Phi(0))\rightarrow X
is unstable, and thus there are no solutions to the Hitchin equation. Secondly, if
X
is an elliptic curve, then there exists a solution to the Hitchin equation for
({\mathbb{K}}_{2},\Phi(q))\rightarrow X
if and only if
q\neq 0
. If
q
is a non-zero section, then we can easily find a solution to the Hitchin equation;
h=(h_{1},h_{1}^{-1})=(h_{q}^{-1/4},h_{q}^{1/4})
solves the Hitchin equation, where
h_{q}
is a Hermitian metric on
K_{X}^{-2}\rightarrow X
defined as follows:

\displaystyle(h_{q})_{x}(u,v)\coloneqq\langle q_{x},u\rangle\overline{\langle q_{x},v\rangle}\ \text{for $x\in X$, $u,v\in(K_{X}^{-2})_{x}$}.

(1)

Finally, if
X
is a compact hyperbolic Riemann surface, then
({\mathbb{K}}_{2},\Phi(q))\rightarrow X
is always stable, and thus there uniquely exists a harmonic metric
h
on
({\mathbb{K}}_{2},\Phi(q))\rightarrow X
satisfying
{\rm det}(h)=1
. Moreover, from the symmetry of the Higgs field and the uniqueness of the solution to the Hitchin equation, the metric
h
splits as
h=(h_{1},h_{1}^{-1})
. As described above, when
X
is an elliptic curve and
q\neq 0
, or when
X
is a hyperbolic Riemann surface, there exists a diagonal harmonic metric on
({\mathbb{K}}_{2},\Phi(q))\rightarrow X
. A diagonal harmonic metric naturally induces a Hermitian metric on
K_{X}^{-1}\rightarrow X
defined as
H_{1}\coloneqq h_{1}^{-2}
. Furthermore, by combining it with the Hermitian metric
h_{q}
induced by

, we obtain a Hermitian metric

on
K_{X}^{-1}\rightarrow X
which is degenerated at the zeros of

as
H_{2}\coloneqq h_{q}\otimes H_{1}^{-1}=h_{q}\otimes h_{1}^{2}
. If
X
is an elliptic curve, then we have

and it induces a flat Gaussian curvature Kähler metric. When

is a compact hyperbolic Riemann surface, the harmonic metric is distorted by the zero point of

and deviates from a flat metric, and in the “fully distorted” limit, i.e., when
q=0
, the Kähler metric induced by

becomes a metric of negative constant curvature. The above is an explanation for the case where
r=2
, but the same thing can be observed for harmonic metrics on higher order cyclic Higgs bundles (see Section
2
).

When the harmonic metric on a cyclic Higgs bundle is regarded as an extension of the non-negative constant curvature Kähler metric, the additional structure

that causes the deviation from the hyperbolic metric does not need to be a holomorphic section. In the papers
[
29
,
30
,
31
]
, the author proposed a new research direction to extend the singular Hermitian metric

induced by

to more general semipositive singular Hermitian metric

on
K_{X}\rightarrow X
. This means that we consider harmonic metrics on cyclic ramified coverings of

and the limit of the covering order going to infinity (see Section
2.3
). The aim is to deepen the integration of classical potential theory and complex analysis, especially mathematics related to the zeros of holomorphic functions and holomorphic sections (cf.
[
3
,
36
,
37
,
39
]
), with harmonic metrics. For more specific research results at this stage, see
[
30
,
31
]
.

A diagonal harmonic metric

on a cyclic Higgs bundle yields

-Hermitian metrics

on

on

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
\varphi

is hyperbolic. Suppose also that

On the unit disc

Backgrounds

2.1
Real, diagonal, and complete Hermitian metrics

Let
X
be a connected, possibly non-compact Riemann surface and
K_{X}\rightarrow X
the canonical bundle. We choose a square root
K_{X}^{1/2}
of the canonical bundle
K_{X}\rightarrow X
. Let
{\mathbb{K}}_{r}
be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank
r
defined as follows:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{K}}_{r}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r}K_{X}^{\frac{r-(2j-1)}{2}}=K_{X}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\oplus K_{X}^{\frac{r-3}{2}}\oplus\cdots\oplus K_{X}^{-\frac{r-3}{2}}\oplus K_{X}^{-\frac{r-1}{2}}.

A Hermitian metric
h
on
{\mathbb{K}}_{r}\rightarrow X
is said to be
real
(cf.
[
20
]
) if the following bundle isomorphism
S
is isometric with respect to
h
:

\displaystyle S\coloneqq\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}&&1\\
&\reflectbox{$\ddots$}&\\
1&&\end{array}\right):{\mathbb{K}}_{r}\rightarrow{\mathbb{K}}_{r}^{\vee},

where
{\mathbb{K}}_{r}^{\vee}
denotes the dual bundle of
{\mathbb{K}}_{r}
. Also,
h
is called
diagonal
(cf.
[
2
,
7
,
9
]
) if it splits as
h=(h_{1},\dots,h_{r})
. Let
T_{X}\rightarrow X
be the dual bundle of
K_{X}\rightarrow X
. By taking the difference between the adjacent components in a diagonal Hermitian metric
h=(h_{1},\dots,h_{r})
, we obtain
r-1
-Hermitian metrics
H_{1},\dots,H_{r-1}
on
T_{X}\rightarrow X
:

\displaystyle H_{j}\coloneqq h_{j}^{-1}\otimes h_{j+1}.

A diagonal Hermitian metric

is real if and only if
h_{j}=h_{r-j+1}
for all
j=1,\dots,r
. This is also equivalent to the metrics
H_{1},\dots,H_{r-1}
satisfying
H_{j}=H_{r-j}
for all
j=1,\dots,r-1
. We say that a diagonal Hermitian metric

is

(cf.
[
25
]
) if the Kähler metrics induced by
H_{1},\dots,H_{r-1}
are all complete.

2.2
Harmonic metrics on cyclic Higgs bundles

Let
q
be a holomorphic section of
K_{X}^{r}\rightarrow X
. Then we associate a holomorphic section
\Phi(q)\in H^{0}({\rm End}{\mathbb{K}}_{r}\otimes K_{X})
, which is called a Higgs field, as follows:

\displaystyle\Phi(q)\coloneqq\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&&&q\\
1&\ddots&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&1&0\end{array}\right).

The pair

is called a cyclic Higgs bundle (cf.
[

,
19
,
20

[

\displaystyle D_{q}(h)\coloneqq\nabla^{h}+\Phi(q)+\Phi(q)^{\ast h}.

A Hermitian metric on

is the universal covering space. If

maps into
{\rm SL}(r,{\mathbb{R}})/{\rm SO}(r)
. Suppose that

is a compact Riemann surface of genus at least

. Then the Higgs bundle
({\mathbb{K}}_{r},\Phi(q))
is stable for any

, and thus there uniquely exists a harmonic metric
h
on
({\mathbb{K}}_{r},\Phi(q))
such that
{\rm det}(h)=1
. From the uniqueness of the metric and the symmetry of the Higgs field (cf.
[
2
,
20
]
), the harmonic metric

is diagonal and real. A non-diagonal harmonic metric (resp. a non-real harmonic metric) can be constructed by considering the Dirichlet problem (cf.
[
11
,
26
]
) for the Hitchin equation with a non-diagonal (resp. a non-real) Hermitian metric on the boundary.
Let
h=(h_{1},\dots,h_{r})
be a diagonal harmonic metric on a cyclic Higgs bundle
({\mathbb{K}}_{r},\Phi(q))\rightarrow X
. As mentioned in the previous subsection we obtain
r-1
-Hermitian metrics
H_{1},\dots,H_{r-1}
on
T_{X}\rightarrow X
defined as

\displaystyle H_{j}\coloneqq h_{j}^{-1}\otimes h_{j+1}\ \text{for each $j=1,\dots,r-1$}.

We also obtain a Hermitian metric
H_{r}
on
T_{X}\rightarrow X
which is degenerate at the zeros of

\displaystyle H_{r}\coloneqq h_{r}^{-1}\otimes h_{1}\otimes h_{q},

where
h_{q}
is a Hermitian metric on
K_{X}^{-r}\rightarrow X
that is degenerate at the zeros of
q
defined as follows:

\displaystyle(h_{q})_{x}(u,v)\coloneqq\langle q_{x},u\rangle\overline{\langle q_{x},v\rangle}\ \text{for $x\in X$, $u,v\in(K_{X}^{-r})_{x}$}.

(2)

The connection

\displaystyle F_{h}+[\Phi(q)\wedge\Phi(q)^{\ast h}]=0.

By using

\displaystyle{\sqrt{-1}}F_{h_{j}}+{\rm vol}(H_{j-1})-{\rm vol}(H_{j})=0\ \text{for $j=1,\dots,r-1$},

where for each

is

\displaystyle{\sqrt{-1}}F_{H_{j}}+2{\rm vol}(H_{j})-{\rm vol}(H_{j-1})-{\rm vol}(H_{j+1})=0\ \text{for $j=1,\dots,r-1$.}

(4)

In the case where

and

\displaystyle{\sqrt{-1}}F_{H}+2{\rm vol}(H)=0,

2.3
More general subharmonic weight functions

For each
q\in H^{0}(K_{X}^{r})
, the metric
h_{q}
induces a singular metric (cf.
[
16
]
)
h_{q}^{-1/r}
that diverges at the zeros of
q
. We can define a curvature for
h_{q}^{-1/r}
in the sense of currents, which is semipositive and has support at the zero points of
q
. We fix a smooth metric
h_{\ast}
and denote by
e^{-\phi_{q}}h_{\ast}
the metric
h_{q}^{-1/r}
, where the weight function
\phi_{q}
is defined as
\phi_{q}\coloneqq\frac{1}{r}\log|q|_{h_{\ast}}^{2}
. If we choose a local flat reference metric, then the weight function defines a local subharmonic function. Also, although we will not go into details here, there is a way to identify the weight function with a global psh function on the total space of the dual line bundle excluding zero points, see
[
4
, Section 2.1]
. In the sense described above, we call the function
\phi_{q}
a
subharmonic weight function
, although this is a bit of an abuse of terminology. We consider a more general singular metric
e^{-\varphi}h_{\ast}
with semipositive curvature, where the weight function
\varphi
is a function that is locally a sum of a subharmonic function and a smooth function. For each
q_{N}\in H^{0}(K_{X}^{N})
, let
\phi_{q_{N}}
be defined as
\frac{1}{N}\log|q_{N}|_{h_{\ast}}^{2}
. Then
e^{-\phi_{q_{N}}}h_{\ast}
is a singular metric with semipositive curvature. Moreover, any semipositive singular metric can be approximated, at least in the
L^{1}_{loc}
sense, by a sequence
(e^{-\phi_{q_{N}}}h_{\ast})_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, where
q_{N}\in H^{0}(K_{X}^{N})
(cf.
[
16
]
). In
[
28
,
29
]
, for each semipositive singular metric
e^{-\varphi}h_{\ast}
and each
r\geq 2
, the following equation for a diagonal metric
h=(h_{1},\dots,h_{r})
on
{\mathbb{K}}_{r}\rightarrow X
was introduced:

\displaystyle{\sqrt{-1}}F_{h_{j}}+{\rm vol}(H_{j-1})-{\rm vol}(H_{j})=0\ \text{for $j=1,\dots,r-1$},

(7)

where
H_{1},\dots,H_{r-1}
are defined as
H_{j}\coloneqq h_{j}^{-1}\otimes h_{j+1}
for each
j=1,\dots,r-1
, and
H_{r}=H_{0}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle H_{r}=H_{0}

\displaystyle\coloneqq h_{r}^{-1}\otimes h_{1}\otimes(e^{-\varphi}h_{\ast})^{-r}

\displaystyle=H_{1}^{-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes H_{r-1}^{-1}\otimes(e^{-\varphi}h_{\ast})^{-r}.

For the case where
\varphi=\phi_{q}=\frac{1}{r}\log|q|_{h_{\ast}}^{2}
, equation (

({\mathbb{K}}_{r},\Phi(q))
. For the case where
\varphi=\phi_{q_{N}}=\frac{1}{N}\log|q_{N}|_{h_{\ast}}^{2}
, equation (

) gives a harmonic metric on a ramified covering space of

(see
[

2.4
Entropy and free energy

This subsection reviews on the definitions of the functions called entropy and free energy given in
[
30
,
31
]
. Let
h=(h_{1},\dots,h_{r})
be a solution to equation (
7
) and let
H_{1},\dots,H_{r}
be the Hermitian metrics constructed from
h
and
e^{-\varphi}h_{\ast}
as in Section
2.3
. Then we define functions, which we call entropy and free energy, as follows:

Definition 5
.

For each
j=0,1,\dots,r-1
and non-zero real number
\beta
, let
p_{j}(r,\beta,\varphi):X\rightarrow[0,1]
be a nonnegative function defined as follows:

\displaystyle p_{j}(r,\beta,\varphi)\coloneqq\frac{{\rm vol}(H_{j})^{\beta}}{\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}{\rm vol}(H_{j})^{\beta}},

where
{\rm vol}(H_{j})^{\beta}/\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}{\rm vol}(H_{j})^{\beta}
is understood to be
({\rm vol}(H_{j})/{\rm vol}(H_{\ast}))^{\beta}/\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}({\rm vol}(H_{j})/{\rm vol}(H_{\ast}))^{\beta}
for some reference metric
H_{\ast}
, which does not affect
p_{j}(r,\beta,\varphi)
. We call the following function
entropy
:

\displaystyle S(r,\beta,\varphi)\coloneqq-\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}p_{j}(r,\beta,\varphi)\log p_{j}(r,\beta,\varphi).

Definition 6
.

Let
\beta
be a non-zero real number. We call the following function
free energy
:

\displaystyle F(r,\beta,\varphi,H_{\ast})\coloneqq-\frac{1}{\beta}\log(\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}({\rm vol}(H_{j})/{\rm vol}(H_{\ast}))^{\beta}),

where
H_{\ast}
is a reference metric.

Note that the difference between free energy functions with the same reference metric does not depend on the choice of the reference metric.

\displaystyle{\sqrt{-1}}\partial\bar{\partial}\sum_{j=1}^{r}s_{j}

\displaystyle={\sqrt{-1}}\partial\sum_{j=1}^{r}(\bar{\partial}\log s_{j})e^{\log s_{j}}

\displaystyle={\sqrt{-1}}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\partial(\log s_{j})\wedge\bar{\partial}(\log s_{j})e^{\log s_{j}}+{\sqrt{-1}}\sum_{j=1}^{r}(\partial\bar{\partial}\log s_{j})e^{\log s_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{r}s_{j}(-{\rm vol}(H_{j-1})+{\rm vol}(H_{j})+{\rm vol}(H_{j-1}^{\prime})-{\rm vol}(H_{j}^{\prime})).

\displaystyle H_{j}^{(i)}\coloneqq h_{j}^{(i)-1}\otimes h_{j+1}^{(i)},

and define
H_{0}^{(i)}=H_{r}^{(i)}
by

\displaystyle H_{0}^{(i)}=H_{r}^{(i)}\coloneqq e^{r\varphi}h_{\ast}^{-r}|_{Y_{i}}\otimes h_{r}^{(i)-1}\otimes h_{1}^{(i)}.

Lemma 29
.

Suppose that
e^{\varphi}
is smooth and a bounded function. Then there exists a positive constant
C_{M_{\varphi}}
depending only on
M_{\varphi}
and
r
, such that for each
i=1,2,\dots
, by choosing the boundary condition appropriately, a solution
h^{(i)}=(h_{1}^{(i)},\dots,h_{r}^{(i)})
to the Dirichlet problem for equation (
7
) on
Y^{(i)}
satisfies the following estimate:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\omega_{X}\leq{\rm vol}(H_{j}^{(i)})\leq C_{M_{\varphi}}\omega_{X}\ \text{for each $j=1,\dots,r-1$}.

(37)

Proof..

Let
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
be constants as in inequality (
16
). We set
C_{M_{\varphi}}
as follows:

\displaystyle C_{M_{\varphi}}\coloneqq{\rm max}\left\{\frac{C_{2}M_{\varphi}+1}{C_{1}},\frac{r-1}{2}+2^{r-1}M_{\varphi}^{r}\right\}.

For each
i=1,2,\dots
, we define a Hermitian metric
\partial h^{(i)}\coloneqq(\partial h^{(i)}_{1},\dots,\partial h^{(i)}_{r})
on the boundary
\partial Y^{(i)}
of
Y^{(i)}
as the unique one such that
\partial h_{j}^{(i)\,-1}\otimes\partial h_{j+1}^{(i)}=\frac{1}{2}h_{X}^{-1}\left.\right|_{\partial Y^{(i)}}
holds for all
j=1,\dots,r-1
. We show that the Hermitian metrics
H_{1}^{(i)},\dots,H_{r-1}^{(i)}
, constructed from the solution
h^{(i)}=(h_{1}^{(i)},\dots,h_{r}^{(i)})
to the Dirichlet problem for equation (
7
) with boundary condition
\lim_{z\to\zeta}h(z)=\partial h^{(i)}(\zeta)
, satisfy the estimate (
37
). We first prove the estimate
\frac{1}{2}\omega_{X}\leq{\rm vol}(H_{j}^{(i)})
for each
j=1,\dots,r-1
. For each
j=1,\dots,r-1
,
H_{j}^{(i)}
satisfies the following:

\displaystyle{\sqrt{-1}}\partial\bar{\partial}\log(H_{j}^{(i)}\otimes h_{X})

\displaystyle=-{\sqrt{-1}}F_{H_{j}^{(i)}}-{\sqrt{-1}}F_{h_{X}}

\displaystyle=2{\rm vol}(H_{j}^{(i)})-{\rm vol}(H_{j-1}^{(i)})-{\rm vol}(H_{j+1}^{(i)})-\omega_{X}

\displaystyle\leq 2{\rm vol}(H_{j}^{(i)})-\omega_{X}.

Then from the boundary condition and the strong maximum principle, we have
\frac{1}{2}\omega_{X}\leq{\rm vol}(H_{j}^{(i)})
on
Y^{(i)}
. We next prove the estimate
{\rm vol}(H_{j}^{(i)})\leq C_{M_{\varphi}}\omega_{X}
. From inequality (
16
) for
H=M_{\varphi}h_{X}^{-1}
and the strong maximum principle, one of the following holds:

\displaystyle 0\geq C_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{r}H_{j}^{(i)}\otimes H^{-1}-C_{2}-M_{\varphi}^{-1},

(38)

\displaystyle 2^{r-1}M_{\varphi}^{-1}+2^{r-1}e^{r\varphi}\left.\right|_{\partial Y^{(i)}}M_{\varphi}^{-1}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{r}H_{j}^{(i)}\otimes H^{-1}.

(39)

In either case, we have
{\rm vol}(H_{j}^{(i)})\leq\sum_{j=1}^{r}{\rm vol}(H_{j}^{(i)})\leq C_{M\varphi}\omega_{X}
for each
j=1,\dots,r-1
. This implies the claim.
∎

Lemma 31

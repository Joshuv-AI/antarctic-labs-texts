Periodic Shadowing for Set-Valued Maps
M. Oliveira

Abstract
. We study shadowing-type properties for set-valued dynamical systems. In particular, we investigate the periodic shadowing property and its relationship with expansivity and chain transitivity. We establish that for positively expansive set-valued maps on compact metric spaces, the shadowing property implies the periodic shadowing property. Furthermore, we show that for chain transitive maps, periodic shadowing implies both shadowing and topological transitivity. We also present a general construction of set-valued maps with shadowing arising from single-valued systems admitting an isometric involution. Several examples, including systems from symbolic dynamics, are provided to illustrate the theory.

Primary 37C50, 37B05; Secondary 54H20, 37B20

1.
Introduction

The qualitative theory of dynamical systems is classically formulated through
the iteration of continuous single-valued maps on compact metric spaces.
Within this framework, properties such as transitivity, expansivity, recurrence,
and orbit tracing play a central role.
These notions form the core of topological dynamics and date back to the
foundational works of Birkhoff and Anosov; see, for instance,

[
4
,
1
,
3
]
.

Among orbit tracing properties, the
shadowing property
is of particular
importance.
Roughly speaking, shadowing ensures that approximate trajectories
(pseudo-orbits) can be uniformly approximated by true orbits.
Introduced by Anosov in the context of hyperbolic dynamics
[
1
]
,
this property has become a fundamental tool in the study of stability and
robustness of dynamical systems.
Several refinements have been proposed, including finite and periodic
shadowing.

The
periodic shadowing property
, introduced by Kościelniak

[
5
]
, requires that periodic pseudo-orbits be shadowed by
actual periodic orbits.
In the single-valued setting, this property is closely related to expansivity
and transitivity.
In particular, it was shown in
[
7
]
that for expansive
homeomorphisms shadowing implies periodic shadowing, and that under chain
transitivity, periodic shadowing implies both shadowing and topological
transitivity.

Set-valued dynamical systems arise naturally in situations where the evolution
is not uniquely determined.
Such systems are generated by set-valued maps
F:X\to 2^{X}
and provide a
flexible framework for modeling nondeterministic dynamics.
Although many classical notions extend formally to this setting, their
dynamical consequences often differ substantially due to the non-uniqueness
of orbits; see, for example,
[
9
,
11
,
6
]
.

Shadowing-type properties for set-valued maps have attracted increasing
attention in recent years.
In particular, Raines and Tennant
[
9
]
and Zhengyu
[
10
]

investigated shadowing and inverse limit structures for multivalued systems,
revealing both similarities and essential differences with the classical
theory.
However, the role of
periodic shadowing
in set-valued dynamics remains
less explored, especially in relation to expansivity, chain transitivity, and
topological transitivity.

The purpose of this paper is to study the periodic shadowing property for
set-valued dynamical systems and to clarify its relationship with standard
shadowing and transitivity-type properties.
We show that for expansive set-valued maps on compact metric spaces,
shadowing implies periodic shadowing, and that for chain transitive set-valued
maps, periodic shadowing implies both shadowing and topological transitivity.
In addition, we establish several stability properties of periodic shadowing,
including invariance under iteration, inversion, and Cartesian products, and
provide examples illustrating the necessity of the imposed hypotheses.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we present the necessary preliminaries and our construction via isometric involutions. Section 3 is devoted to the stability properties of PeSP and the proofs of our main theorems (Theorem A and Theorem B). Finally, examples from symbolic dynamics are discussed to illustrate the applicability of our results.

2.
Preliminaries and Results

2.1.
Single-Valued Maps

For the sake of completeness, we recall some fundamental definitions from topological dynamics. A sequence
\{x_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
is called an orbit of
(X,f)
if
x_{i+1}=f(x_{i})
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, where
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
denotes the set of non-negative integers. If
f
is a homeomorphism, a bi-infinite sequence
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is said to be an orbit of
(X,f)
provided that
x_{i+1}=f(x_{i})
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. A sequence
\{x_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
is called a
\delta
-pseudo orbit if
d(f(x_{i}),x_{i+1})<\delta
for all
i\geq 0
. A finite sequence
\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}
is said to be a
\delta
-chain of length
n
if
d(f(x_{i}),x_{i+1})<\delta
for all
0\leq i\leq n-1
.

The concept of shadowing was first introduced by Anosov in
[
1
]
. The shadowing property plays a central role in dynamical systems and can be described as follows: a dynamical system
(X,f)
is said to have the shadowing property if for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that any
\delta
-pseudo orbit is
\varepsilon
-shadowed by some point
x\in X
, that is,

d(f^{i}(x),x_{i})<\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }i\geq 0.

For more details see
[
3
]
.

One such variant is the periodic shadowing property, introduced by Koscielniak in
[
5
]
. A dynamical system
(X,f)
, where
f
is a homeomorphism, is said to have the periodic shadowing property if for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that every periodic
\delta
-pseudo orbit is
\varepsilon
-shadowed by a periodic point
p\in X
, meaning that

d(f^{i}(p),x_{i})<\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }i\in\mathbb{Z}.

There exist dynamical systems without the periodic shadowing property, such as irrational rotations.

A dynamical system
(X,f)
is called topologically transitive if for any pair of non-empty open sets
U,V\subset X
, there exists an integer
n\geq 1
such that

f^{n}(U)\cap V\neq\emptyset.

The notion of topological transitivity dates back to G.D. Birkhoff in
[
4
]
. The system
(X,f)
is said to be topologically mixing if for every pair of non-empty open sets
U,V\subset X
, there exists an integer
N
such that

f^{n}(U)\cap V\neq\emptyset\quad\text{for all }n\geq N.

Furthermore,
(X,f)
is said to be chain transitive if for every
\delta>0
and any pair of points
x,y\in X
, there exists a
\delta
-chain from
x
to
y
. The system is called chain mixing if for every pair of points
x,y\in X
and for each
\delta>0
, there exists a positive integer
N
such that for all
n\geq N
, there exists a
\delta
-chain from
x
to
y
of length
n
.

Another important property was introduced by
[
12
]
. Let
X
be a compact metric space. A homeomorphism
f:X\to X
is said to be
expansive
if there exists
\delta>0
such that for any two distinct points
x,y\in X
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying

d\big(f^{n}(x),f^{n}(y)\big)>\delta.

Darabi and Forouzanfar established a relationship between the shadowing property and the periodic shadowing property.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space and let
f:X\to X
be an expansive homeomorphism. If
f
has the shadowing property, then it also has the periodic shadowing property.

In the single-valued setting, the shift map
\sigma:\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}\to\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is expansive and satisfies the shadowing property. Hence, by the above theorem,
\sigma
also has the periodic shadowing property.

For the reverse we need of the chain transitive condition.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space and let
f:X\to X
be a chain transitive dynamical system. If
f
has the periodic shadowing property, then
f
also has the shadowing property and is topologically transitive.

In the next subsection we present our mean results that are the generalization of the results above.

2.2.
Set-Valued Maps

In the following, we present some basic definitions regarding set-valued maps.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. For each
x\in X
and
A\subset X
, we define the distance

d(x,A)=\inf\{d(x,y)\;|\;y\in A\}.

We denote by
2^{X}
the collection of all nonempty compact subsets of
X
. The
Hausdorff metric

d_{H}
on
2^{X}
is defined by

d_{H}(A,B)=\max\left\{\sup_{a\in A}d(a,B),\;\sup_{b\in B}d(b,A)\right\}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a metric space with metric
d
. A set-valued map
F:X\to 2^{X}
is said to be:

(I)

Upper semicontinuous:
If for every
x\in X
and every open set
U\supset F(x)
, there exists an open neighborhood
V
of
x
such that
F(y)\subset U
for all
y\in V
.

(II)

Lower semicontinuous:
If for every
x\in X
and every open set
U
with
U\cap F(x)\neq\emptyset
, there exists an open neighborhood
V
of
x
such that
F(y)\cap U\neq\emptyset
for all
y\in V
.

(III)

Continuous:
If it is both upper and lower semicontinuous.

If
F:X\to 2^{X}
is a set-valued map on
X
, we call the pair
(F,X)
a
set-valued dynamical system
.

An
orbit
of a point
x\in X
under
F
is a sequence
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
x_{i+1}\in F(x_{i})
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. A point
x\in X
is called
periodic
if there exists an orbit
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and an integer
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that
x_{0}=x
and
x_{n+m}=x_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
X
be a metric space and let
F:X\to 2^{X}
be a set-valued map.
For each
y\in X
, the
inverse image
of
y
under
F
is defined by

F^{-1}(y):=\{\,x\in X\;:\;y\in F(x)\,\}.

We are now ready to introduce notions of shadowing and related properties for set-valued maps. (See
[
9
]
,
[
11
]
)

Definition 2.5
.

Let
X
be a metric space and
F:X\to 2^{X}
a set-valued map.

(1)

Given
\delta>0
, a sequence
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in
X
is called a
\delta
-pseudo orbit
of
F
if

d(x_{i+1},F(x_{i}))<\delta\quad\text{for all }i\in\mathbb{N}.

(2)

The map
F
is said to have the
shadowing property
(SP) if, for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for any
\delta
-pseudo orbit
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, there exists a point
z\in X
and an orbit
\{z_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
z
satisfying

d(z_{i},x_{i})<\epsilon\quad\text{for all }i\in\mathbb{N}.

(3)

The map
F
is said to have the
finite shadowing property
(finite-SP) if, for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for any finite
\delta
-pseudo orbit
\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{k-1}
, there exists a point
z\in X
with a finite orbit
\{z_{0},z_{1},\dots,z_{k-1}\}
such that

d(z_{i},x_{i})<\epsilon\quad\text{for all }0\leq i\leq k-1.

In the multivalued context, we introduce the
periodic shadowing property
.

Definition 2.6
.

A set-valued map
F:X\to 2^{X}
is said to have the
Periodic Shadowing Property
(PeSP) if, for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for any
periodic

\delta
-pseudo orbit
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, there exists a periodic point
z\in X
with an orbit
\{z_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying

d(z_{i},x_{i})<\epsilon\quad\text{for all }i\in\mathbb{N}.

The next lemma provides a general mechanism to construct set-valued dynamical systems with shadowing properties from single-valued ones by means of an isometric involutive symmetry. This construction yields a broad class of examples and will be used throughout the paper to illustrate the interplay between determinism and non-determinism.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and let
f:X\to X
be a map. Assume that
R:X\to X
is an isometric involution (i.e.,
R^{2}=\mathrm{id}
and
d(R(x),R(y))=d(x,y)
for all
x,y\in X
) that commutes with
f
(
R\circ f=f\circ R
). Define the set-valued map
F:X\to 2^{X}
by
F(x)=\{f(x),\,R(f(x))\}
. The following assertions hold:

(1)

If
f
has the shadowing property, then
F
has the shadowing property.

(2)

If
f
has the periodic shadowing property, then
F
has the periodic shadowing property.

Proof.

We first prove (1). Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
\delta>0
be given by the shadowing property of
f
. Let
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a
\delta
–pseudo-orbit of
F
. We construct a
\delta
–pseudo-orbit
\{y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
f
by induction.

In the initial step, choose
y_{0}\in\{x_{0},R(x_{0})\}
. If
y_{0}=x_{0}
and
d(x_{1},f(x_{0}))<\delta
, define
y_{1}=x_{1}
. If
d(x_{1},R(f(x_{0})))<\delta
, define
y_{1}=R(x_{1})
. Since
R
is an isometric involution commuting with
f
, we have
d(R(x_{1}),f(x_{0}))=d(x_{1},R(f(x_{0})))<\delta
, thus
d(y_{1},f(y_{0}))<\delta
in either case. The case
y_{0}=R(x_{0})
follows similarly.

For
n\geq 1
, assume
y_{n}\in\{x_{n},R(x_{n})\}
has been obtained such that
d(y_{n},f(y_{n-1}))<\delta
.

•

If
d(x_{n+1},f(x_{n}))<\delta
, set
y_{n+1}=x_{n+1}
if
y_{n}=x_{n}
, and
y_{n+1}=R(x_{n+1})
if
y_{n}=R(x_{n})
.

•

If
d(x_{n+1},R(f(x_{n})))<\delta
, set
y_{n+1}=R(x_{n+1})
if
y_{n}=x_{n}
, and
y_{n+1}=x_{n+1}
if
y_{n}=R(x_{n})
.

In all cases, the isometric property of
R
ensures
d(y_{n+1},f(y_{n}))<\delta
. Thus
\{y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a
\delta
–pseudo-orbit of
f
. By the shadowing property of
f
, there exists
z\in X
such that
d(f^{n}(z),y_{n})<\varepsilon
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Define
\{w_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
by
w_{n}=f^{n}(z)
if
y_{n}=x_{n}
, and
w_{n}=R(f^{n}(z))
if
y_{n}=R(x_{n})
. Since
R\circ f=f\circ R
,
\{w_{n}\}
is an orbit of
F
and
d(x_{n},w_{n})=d(y_{n},f^{n}(z))<\varepsilon
, proving (1).

To establish (2), suppose
\{x_{n}\}
is periodic with period
k
. Since
R
is an involution and
f
is periodic, the constructed
\delta
–pseudo-orbit
\{y_{n}\}
is also periodic. By the periodic shadowing property of
f
, there exists a periodic orbit of
f
shadowing
\{y_{n}\}
, which directly implies that the corresponding orbit
\{w_{n}\}
of
F
is periodic.

∎

It is worth noting that this construction does not require the compactness of
X
nor the continuity of the set-valued map
F
, providing a flexible framework for generating examples in various metric settings.

Example 2.8
.

Let
\Sigma^{+}=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
be the one-sided full shift endowed with the metric

d(x,y)=2^{-N(x,y)},\quad N(x,y)=\min\{n\geq 0:x_{n}\neq y_{n}\}.

Define the unilateral shift
\sigma:\Sigma^{+}\to\Sigma^{+}
by

(\sigma(x))_{n}=x_{n+1}.

It is well known that
\sigma
has the shadowing property.

Define the map
R:\Sigma^{+}\to\Sigma^{+}
by

(R(x))_{n}=1-x_{n}\quad\text{for all }n\geq 0.

Then
R
is an isometric involution and satisfies
R\circ\sigma=\sigma\circ R
.
Therefore, the set-valued map
F:\Sigma^{+}\to 2^{\Sigma^{+}}
given by

F(x)=\{\sigma(x),\,R(\sigma(x))\}

has the shadowing property.

The notion of expansivity, has been extended to set-valued dynamical systems as follows.

Definition 2.9

(
[
14
]
)
.

A set-valued map
F:X\to 2^{X}
is positively expansive if there exists

\alpha>0
such that for any distinct
x,y\in X
and any orbits

\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\{y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
F
, one has
d(x_{n},y_{n})>\alpha
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

We now state the first main result of this paper, which establishes a direct
connection between the shadowing property and the periodic shadowing property
for set-valued dynamical systems.

Theorem A
.

Let
F:X\to 2^{X}
be a positively expansive set-valued map on a compact metric space
X
. If
F
has SP, then it also has the PeSP.

Example 2.10
.

Let
X=[0,1]
with the Euclidean metric, and define the set-valued map
F:X\to 2^{X}
by

F(x)=\begin{cases}\{2x\}&\text{if }0\leq x<\frac{1}{2},\\
\{0,1\}&\text{if }x=\frac{1}{2},\\
\{2x-1\}&\text{if }\frac{1}{2}<x\leq 1.\end{cases}

In
[
11
]
it is obtained that the set-valued map
F
has Shadowing property and is positively expansive, so by Theorem A also has PeSP.

The next example shows that that the reciprocal is not true, that is, periodic shadowing property does not imply the shadowing property without additional hypotheses such as chain transitivity.

Example 2.11
.

Let
X=\{0,1\}
endowed with the discrete metric and define a set-valued map

F:X\to 2^{X}
by

F(0)=\{0,1\},\qquad F(1)=\{1\}.

Then
F
has the periodic shadowing property, but it does not have the
shadowing property.

Proof.

Any periodic pseudo-orbit of
F
must eventually be constant equal to
1
,
and hence is shadowed by the fixed point
1
.
Therefore,
F
has the periodic shadowing property.

However, the sequence
(0,0,0,\dots)
is a pseudo-orbit which cannot be
shadowed by any true
F
-orbit, since every orbit starting at
0
eventually
reaches
1
. Thus,
F
does not have the shadowing property.
∎

Therefore, in order to obtain a converse-type result, it is necessary an additional property as following the next (See
[
6
]
,
[
13
]
).

Definition 2.12
.

Let
F:X\to 2^{X}
be a set-valued map on metric space
X
.

(1)

F
is said to be
topologically transitive
if for any pair of
nonempty open sets
U,V\subset X
there exists an orbit

\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
F
and an integer
n\geq 0
such that

x_{0}\in U\quad\text{and}\quad x_{n}\in V.

(2)

F
is said to be
chain transitive
if for every
\delta>0
and
every
x,y\in X
there exists a
\delta
-chain from
x
to
y
.

Thus we have our other main result from this article.

Theorem B
.

Let
F:X\to 2^{X}
be a chain transitive set-valued map. If
F
has the PeSP, then
F
has the SP and is topologically transitive.

An immediate consequence of the above theorem is:

Corollary 2.13
.

Let
F:X\to 2^{X}
be a positively expansive and chain transitive set-valued map. Then
F
has SP if and only if it has PeSP.

Proof.

It is consequence immediate of Theorem A and Theorem B.
∎

3.
Proofs and Properties

3.1.
Properties of the Periodic Shadowing Property

Next, we establish some fundamental properties of the periodic shadowing property for set-valued maps.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and let
F:X\to 2^{X}
be a set-valued map.
Given
\delta>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, a finite sequence

(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{N-1})\subset X

is called a finite periodic
\delta
-pseudo-orbit of
F
if

d(x_{i+1},F(x_{i}))<\delta\quad\text{for }i=0,1,\dots,N-2,

and

d(x_{0},F(x_{N-1}))<\delta,

where
d(x,A)=\inf\{d(x,a):a\in A\}
for
A\subset X
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and let
F:X\to 2^{X}
be a set-valued map.
We say that
F
has the finite periodic shadowing property (finite-PeSP) if for every

\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that for every finite periodic

\delta
-pseudo-orbit

(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{N-1})

there exists a periodic orbit

(z_{0},z_{1},\dots,z_{N-1}),\qquad z_{i+1}\in F(z_{i}),\;z_{0}\in F(z_{N-1}),

satisfying

d(z_{i},x_{i})<\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }i=0,1,\dots,N-1.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and let
F:X\to 2^{X}
be a set-valued map.
Then
F
has the PeSP if and only if it has the
finite-PeSP.

Proof.

The necessity is immediate.

Assume that
F
has the finite periodic shadowing property.
Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
\delta>0
be given by this property.

Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a periodic
\delta
-pseudo-orbit of
F
with
period
N
, that is,

x_{n+N}=x_{n}\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then the finite sequence

(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{N-1})

is a finite periodic
\delta
-pseudo-orbit of
F
.

By the finite periodic shadowing property, there exists a periodic orbit

(z_{0},z_{1},\dots,z_{N-1}),\quad z_{i+1}\in F(z_{i}),\;z_{0}\in F(z_{N-1}),

such that

d(z_{i},x_{i})<\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }i=0,1,\dots,N-1.

Define a sequence
(z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
by periodic repetition:

z_{kN+i}=z_{i},\quad k\in\mathbb{N},\;0\leq i\leq N-1.

Since
(z_{0},z_{1},\dots,z_{N-1})
is a periodic orbit of
F
, the sequence

(z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is also an orbit of
F
.
Moreover, for every
n=kN+i
we have

d(z_{n},x_{n})=d(z_{i},x_{i})<\varepsilon.

Therefore, the periodic
\delta
-pseudo-orbit
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

is
\varepsilon
-shadowed by a periodic orbit of
F
, and hence
F
has
the periodic shadowing property.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Note that compactness of the phase space and upper semicontinuity of
F

are not required in the proof. The equivalence follows solely from the
periodic structure of the pseudo-orbits.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and let
F:X\to 2^{X}
be a set-valued map.
Assume that:

(1)

F
is onto;

(2)

F
is continuous with respect to the Hausdorff metric;

(3)

F
has the PeSP.

Then
F^{-1}
also has the PeSP.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
\delta>0
be given by the periodic shadowing
property of
F
.
By the continuity of
F
, there exists
\delta_{1}>0
such that

d_{H}(F(x),F(y))<\frac{\delta}{2}\quad\text{whenever }d(x,y)<\delta_{1}.

By Lemma
3.3
, the periodic shadowing property of
F
implies that

F
has the finite periodic shadowing property.

We claim that
F^{-1}
also has the finite periodic shadowing property.
Indeed, let
\{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}\}
be a periodic

\delta

F

F

Hence,

Integrated bioinformatics analysis to elucidate cellular communication in the microenvironment of breast cancer
Authors: Man Wang, Hua Li, Xingong Zhang, Zhijian Zha, Mengjia Zhao, Qi Wang, Danfeng Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBreast cancer (BC) is the most common malignancy among women, with an increasing incidence correlated with age and diverse subtypes exhibiting distinct prognoses. The tumor microenvironment (TME) in BC is complicated. It is now believed that BC may acquire invasive characteristics and even extra proliferative ability from the TME through various mechanisms. However, most studies predominantly focus on the heterogeneity of tumor cells in BC, lacking a comprehensive depiction of intercellular communication within BC. Therefore, the present study aimed to elucidate cellular communication in the TME by integrated bioinformatic analysis of bulk mRNA and single-cell mRNA sequencing, combined with certain validation in clinical samples.MethodsWe first utilized single-cell sequencing data from GSE176078 to find out the most important cell communication pairs for the tumor microenvironment in BC then we conducted bulk-sequencing analysis to identify the differential expressed genes. Through correlation analysis, we sort out the top five most relevant genes to the most important cell communication pairs. We then validated the expression of the key genes of the aforementioned cell communication pairs and the five differentially expressed genes by qPCR on clinical samples. Furthermore, we analyzed the immunological relevance of these genes via a novel approach at single-cell resolution.ResultsThe results of single-cell analysis indicated that the CXC12-CXCR4 ligand-receptor pair in the CXCL pathway and the FGF7-FGFR1 ligand-receptor pair in the FGF pathway are the most important cell communication pairs of the TME in BC. Subsequent bulk sequencing analysis showed that CHRDL1, SCARA5, LYVE1, PI16, and SAA2 were the most important differentially expressed genes linked to these cell communication pairs. In addition, we validated the expression of the key genes of the two cell communication pairs and the five genes in clinical samples, observing that the trends fitted the computational results. Finally, we studied the association of these genes with immune cell infiltration at single-cell level and had it cross-validated in bulk sequencing data, finding out that there were significant connections.ConclusionIn the tumor microenvironment of breast cancer, intercellular communication pairs of different cell types and molecules can exacerbate the development of breast cancer. among them, through the present study, we found that CXCL12-CXCR4 and FGF7-FGFR1 are the most important. Also, most significantly differentially expressed genes including CHRDL1, SCARA5, LYVE1, PI16, and SAA2 seemed to play a critical role in these mechanisms and immune cell infiltration, shaping the TME of BC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03859-4.Keywords: Breast cancer, Single-cell sequencing, The tumor microenvironment, Cell-cell communication, Bulk mrna sequence

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) is the most common cancer among women worldwide. Till 2020, approximately 2.26 million women worldwide were newly diagnosed with BC, accounting for about 24% of all new cancer cases, with nearly 700,000 women died [1, 2]. In BC, according to years of statistics, tumor cell metastasis has been recognized as the main cause of cancer-related death [3]. Moreover, 5-year survival rate of BC without metastasis could reach 80%, but on the contrary, that of patients with metastasis is only 27% [4]. The number could be even worse if the patients show liver or brain metastasis [5].

Currently, there are limited treatment options for patients with metastatic BC, mainly chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and surgical resection, and the overall survival benefit of patients is not satisfying [6, 7]. Therefore, there is an urgent need to study the mechanism of metastasis in order to explore new effective treatment strategies and improve patient survival and prognosis.

The tumor microenvironment (TME) of BC is complicated. It is a self-regulated ecosystem composed of tumor cells, stromal cells, and immune cells intertwined with non-cellular components. The diverse cellular phenotypes and dynamic communication between these TME members shape the organ-specific tumor environment and may be the reason why cancer cells acquire metastatic ability [8, 9]. Therefore, in-depth research on the potential mechanisms of BC occurrence in the TME is crucial to understand its development process and improve treatment efficiency.

The 21st century is the era of omics research, which brings new perspectives to cancer research. Currently, most genomic technologies mainly focus on tissue blocks, resulting in the loss of key data on intercellular communication. Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), as an emerging genomic technology, can be used to study single-cell expression patterns in large pathological tissue samples [10, 11], which has the potential to reveal the relationship between microenvironment crosstalk and disease status. Although an increasing number of studies have reported the state of TME intercellular communication using scRNA-seq, most studies still focus on the heterogeneity of tumor cells in BC, lacking a comprehensive description of intercellular communication within BC [12–16]. Moreover, recent research has highlighted the critical role of the TME in tumor progression and metastasis, inspired by which, the present study aims to explore the role of genes in cell communication and the specific mechanism by which cell communication affects BC, in order to provide new insights.

Materials and methods

Data curation

GEO (Gene Expression Omnibus) is a publicly available database with extensive gene expression data of various diseases [17]. In the present study, we utilized GSE176078 dataset for scRNA-seq analysis as it contains 26 primary tumors from three major clinical subtypes of breast cancer, including 11 ER+, 5 HER2 + and 10 TNBC [18, 19]. With the help of such a diverse content, we made the data mining outcome with universality possible. Meanwhile, following with scRNA-seq analysis, we utilized bulk mRNA sequencing (bulk-seq) data from TCGA (The Cancer Genome Atlas Program) to further identify key player genes in the tumor microenvironment [20]. Normalization and calibration, if any, were performed using the “NormalizeBetweenArrays”. Besides, we downloaded the gene list associated with common ligand-receptor pairs from the cell-to-cell interaction database (https://baderlab.org/CellCellInteractions#Defining_Receptor_and_Ligands) to analyze their expression in TCGA.

Visualization of TME cell types and subtypes in breast cancer

Using the Seurat package in R, Seurat objects were created based on scRNA-seq gene expression profiles for total cell types and individual cell types belonging to the gene expression matrix. Subsequently, the top 2000 genes selected as top variable features were used as a basis for normalization of scRNA-seq data for each cell using the FindVariableFeatures function in the Seurat package [21–25]. Additionally, ScaleData and RunPCA functions were performed to obtain the number of principal components (PCs) based on the Seurat object. Further summarization of top principal components was achieved using t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) for dimensionality reduction [26]. Finally, annotation and visualization of major TME cell types or subtypes were carried out using the Stereoscope software (V 0.2.0), utilizing annotation information for each cell supported by previous studies.

Cell-cell interaction analysis

CellPhoneDB and CellChat were utilized to infer cell-cell interactions between immune cell subgroups [27, 28]. Potential interaction strength was predicted based on the expression of immune-related receptors and ligands. Enrichment of ligand-receptor interactions was calculated based on permutation tests, considering P-value < 0.01 as significant. In addition to using CellPhoneDB for cell-cell interaction analysis, CellChat software was applied for further analysis. The CellChat package includes a comprehensive signaling molecule interaction database [24], considering known structural compositions of receptor-ligand interactions, such as multimeric receptor-ligand complexes, soluble agonists and antagonists, as well as stimulatory and inhibitory membrane-bound co-receptors [24]. Inference of cell-cell interactions involved the identification of differentially expressed signaling transduction genes, computation of integrated average expression and intercellular communication probability, as well as identification of statistically significant cell-cell communication.

Identification of differential expressed genes (DEGs) from bulk-seq

Differential expression analysis was conducted using the R package, Limma, with a traditional threshold of P-value < 0.05 and |Log2 Fold Change (FC)| >1.00 [29, 30]. Among the numerous DEGs, we preserved the top 5 that have greatest fold changes and correlations with the most important cell communication pairs for the following analyses.

Immune cell infiltration analysis based on bulk-seq

In the bilk-seq part, he gene expression profiles were processed by CIBERSORT (https://cibersortx.stanford.edu/) where the immune cell infiltration matrix was also obtained [31]. The R package “ggplot2” was used to visualize the distribution of infiltration of diverse immune cell types.

Immune cell infiltration analysis based on scRNA-seq

In scRNA-seq immune analysis, the ssGSEA algorithm was used to perform single-sample gene set enrichment analysis to obtain the ssGSEA enrichment score. This score reflects the relative abundance of immune cell infiltration in each sample [32, 33]. In this way, we marked different types of invasive immune cells, including CD8 + T lymphocytes, dendritic cells, macrophages, regulatory T lymphocytes, and other human immune cell subtypes, and expressed the overall infiltration level of various immune cells in each sample through the enrichment score. Then, we conducted Spearman analysis between these subtypes and the preserved DEGs.

Clinical sample collection

We collected 5 cases each of breast cancer patients’ tissues and normal tissues next to the cancer from Taihe Hospital from 01/05/2023 to 27/04/2024 for the purpose of detecting the differences in the expression of the purpose in the breast cancer tissues and in the normal tissues. The age and tumor typing of all patients are shown in in Supplementary Material S1.Basic information of participated patients was summarized in Supplementary Material S1.All patients were fully informed and agreed to participate in the present study with a written consent form signed. All procedures followed the Declaration of Helsinki and were formally approved by the Ethics Committee of Taihe Hospital (No.: 2024KS49).

Sample collection statement

The samples used in this study were derived from leftover specimens collected during previous clinical trials. At the time of collection, patients were informed that these samples might be used for fundamental research, and appropriate informed consent and ethical approvals were obtained. For this study, due to experimental requirements, we utilized these previously collected samples. Prior to their use, we submitted a request to the ethics committee and received approval.

Real-time quantitative PCR

Total RNA was isolated using Trizol (Vazyme), and RNA concentration and purity were measured using a spectrophotometer. Reverse transcription of RNA was performed using the PrimeScript RT reagent kit (Vazyme) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Quantitative PCR was carried out using the LightCycler 96 real-time PCR system (Roche96). The 2−ΔΔCt method was used for analysis, and expression was normalized to GAPDH mRNA. All reactions were performed in triplicate. Primer sequences used in the present study were as organized in Supplementary Material S2, and original data were recorded in Supplementary Material S3.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were conducted at least three times, and data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad software. Student’s t-test was used for comparisons between groups, with P-values < 0.05 considered statistically significant. Wilcoxon rank-sum tests [] were employed for assess differences in continuous target or class variables in cell subgroups.

Result

Initial classification of different cell types and identification of feature genes

We first utilized bulk-seq from TCGA to identify the DEGs (Supplementary Material S4). We examined the scRNA-seq of BC tissues from the GSE176078 dataset, screened out feature genes and rationally classified each cluster of cells. Clustering and annotation of cells identified major cell groups, including epithelial cells, endothelial cells, B cells, T cells, cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) and more (Supplementary Material S5 (A)). We downloaded the gene list associated with common ligand receptor pairs from the cell-to-cell interaction database (https://baderlab.org/CellCellInteractions#Defining_Receptor_and_Ligands), and subsequently collated the expression of these genes based on the DEGs of bulk mRNA sequencing data from TCGA. Finally, it is visualized as a bubble plot (Supplementary Material S5 (B)).

CXCL and FGF signaling pathways were critical in the TME

Ligand-receptor analysis revealed the types of ligand-receptor pairs involved in cell communication (Fig. 1A), Among the types of ligand-receptor pairs, secretory signaling constitute 61.8%, ECM receptors account for 21.7%, and cell-cell contacts contribute to 16.5%. In the realm of polymerization interactions and other interactions, heterodimers make up 47.9%. In the final classification, 73% of the types are sourced from the KEGG database, while 27% are derived from previous literature reports.

Cell communications with different clusters were also visualized, with number of interaction (Fig. 1B) and interaction of strength (Fig. 1C) emphasized. Besides, specific roles of individual clusters in cell communication were presented (Fig. 1D).

To assess the impact of ligand-receptor pairs on cell communication between different clusters, enrichment analysis of genes related to these pairs was also conducted (Fig. 1E). The two ligand receptor pairs with the highest order of magnitude were MIF-(CD74 + CXCR4) and MIF-(CD74 + CD44). Subsequently, we classified these ligand-receptor pairs, FGF and CXCL signaling pathways were thought to be the most important.

Analysis of the role of different cells and ligand receptor pair in CXCL signaling pathway

In CXCL signaling pathway, the heatmap results indicated that the highest likelihood of interaction is between the ligands secreted by CAFs and receptors secreted by B-cells, followed by the receptors expressed in CAFs and T-cells, ligands secreted by myeloid cells and receptors expressed in endothelial cells (Fig. 2A). Additionally, exploring the roles of different clusters revealed that the ligand-secreting cells in CAFs play crucial roles. Besides, the receivers B-cells, T-cells and endothelial cells, the mediators and the influencers in endothelial cells are also significant (Fig. 2B). To investigate the cell-cell communication modes within CXCL signaling pathway, we analyzed the contributions of ligand-receptor pairs, with CXCL12-CXCR4 showing the highest contribution, followed by CXCL2-ACKR1 which contributed way less than the former (Fig. 2C).

Combining the information with the expression of different genes and the ligand-receptor pairs in various clusters (Fig. 2D, E). In particular, the role of CXCR4 in B and T cells was the most prominent and the role of CXCL12 was significant in myeloid and normal epithelial. Additionally, among different ligand-receptor pairs, the CXCL12-CXCR4 ligand-receptor pair is expressed in various cell types and interacts across different clusters. In contrast, other ligand-receptor pairs are primarily expressed in myeloid cells and normal epithelial cells, affecting ligands expressed in endothelial cells (Fig. 2E). Notably, we found that cancer epithelial cells and PVL did not play any role in the overall CXCL signaling pathway (Fig. 2E).

Analysis of the role of different cells and ligand receptor pair in FGF signaling pathway

The heatmap results indicated that the only significant interaction of FGF signaling pathway between ligands and receptors is from CAFs (Fig. 3A). Additionally, exploring the roles of different clusters revealed that the ligand-secreting cells and receptor-expressing cells from CAFs play crucial roles as at the same time (Fig. 3B). To investigate the cell communication modes within FGF signaling pathway, we analyzed the contributions of ligand-receptor pairs, with FGF7-FGFR1 showing the highest and in fact the only meaningful contribution (Fig. 3C). Combining the information with the expression of FGF7 and FGFR1 genes in various cells (Fig. 3D), we obtained the interactions between different clusters in the ligand-receptor pairs within the FGF cell communication pathway (Fig. 3E).

Integrated validation through bulk-seq and real-world clinical samples

Considering that there were too many DEGs screened out according to the traditional filtering conditions (P-value < 0.05 and |Log2 Fold Change (FC)| >1.00) (Supplementary Material S4), we preserved only the top 50 largest up-regulated DEGs and top 50 largest down-regulated DEGs for the coming analytics.

Then, as through the previous analyses, we found that CXCL12-CXCR4 and FGF7-FGFR1 are the most important cell communication pairs, we developed a novel scoring system based on the expression of these four genes to precisely describe the TME of each BC patient.

Then, we conducted Spearman analysis between the preserved DEGs and the aforementioned scores, through which we determined the correlation coefficient for each preserved DEG. The largest top 5 DEGs were CHRDL1, SCARA5, LYVE1, PI16, and SAA2 (Fig. 4A). Their detailed differential expression in bulk-seq were present in (Fig. 4B). As shown, all the aforementioned genes and FGF7, CXCL12 were down-regulated in tumor samples.

Integrated validation through bulk-seq and real-world clinical samples. A Bar chart demonstrating the correlation coefficients of top 5 DEGs with FGF7-FGFR1-CXCL12-CXCR4 scoring. B Violin plot showing the comparison of expression of FGF7, CXCL12, CHRDL1, SCARA5, LYVE1, PI16, and SAA2 in tumor and healthy control samples based on bulk-seq. C qPCR results of real-world clinical samples obtained from hospitalized patients, demonstrating the expression of FGF7, CXCL12, CHRDL1, SCARA5, LYVE1, PI16, and SAA2 in tumor and adjacent healthy tissues

Lastly, we examined the expression of these genes in human breast cancer samples obtained from hospitalized patients (Supplementary Material S2). Our clinical samples included several common breast cancer subtypes, such as triple-negative breast cancer, HER2+, and PR+. As a result, we found that FGF7, CXCL12, CHRDL1, SCARA5, LYVE1, PI16, and SAA2 were all downregulated in BC samples compared with adjacent healthy tissues (Fig. 4C). This finding complied with our computational results.

Exploration of the immunological correlations of FGF7, CXCL12, CHRDL1, SCARA5, LYVE1, PI16, and SAA2 at single-cell resolution

Immune cell infiltration is one of the essential components of TME. Therefore, it is of importance to explore the potential connections between the genes of interest and immune cell infiltration. In the present study, we revolutionarily combined the immune cell signature gene sets curated from CIBERSORT and the scRNA-seq collected from GSE176078, with the help of ssGSEA algorithm, to identify the detailed immune cell subtypes at single-cell level. Figure 5 demonstrated the abundance of those subtypes that possess P-values less than 0.05, including B cells, Memory B cells, Naive B cells, CD4 + T cells, and CD8 + T cells, NKT cells, cycling T − cells, cycling myeloid cells, dendritic cells, macrophage, monocyte, NK cells, and plasmablasts.

Exploration of the immunological correlations of FGF7, CXCL12, CHRDL1, SCARA5, LYVE1, PI16, and SAA2 at single-cell resolution.(1)Bar chart demonstrating the immune cell subtypes that were statistically significant.༈2༉Dot plot showing the expression levels of FGF7, CXCL12, CHRDL1, SCARA5, LYVE1, PI16, and SAA2 on different immune cell subtypes.༈3༉Dot plot demonstrating the correlation between various immune cell subtypes and FGF7, CXCL12, CHRDL1, SCARA5, LYVE1, PI16, and SAA2

Then, we checked the expression of FGF7, CXCL12, CHRDL1, SCARA5, LYVE1, PI16, and SAA2 on the aforementioned immune cell subtypes, observing that they are in general, not significantly expressed, except for CXCL12 and LYVE1 in terms of Macrophage (Fig. 5).

Then, we conducted spearman correlation analysis between these infiltrating immune cell subtypes and the expression of FGF7, CXCL12, CHRDL1, SCARA5, LYVE1, PI16, and SAA2, finding out that FGF7 is significantly correlated with CD4 + T cells, macrophage, and monocyte, CXCL12 is significantly correlated with CD4 + and CD8 + T cells, NKT cells, Plasmablasts, and macrophage, SCARA5 is significantly correlated with CD4 + T cells, SAA2 is significantly correlated with CD4 + and CD8 + T cells, and Plasmablasts, PI16 is significantly correlated with NKT cells and macrophage, LYVE1 is significantly correlated with CD4 + T cells, macrophage, and monocyte (Fig. 5).

Of note that the aforementioned immunoinformatic results were further cross-validated by the bulk-seq (Supplementary Material S6).

Discussion

To date, few studies have explored the impact of disrupted cell communication in the BC microenvironment on BC development and metastasis. Investigating cell communication within the BC microenvironment aids in a comprehensive understanding of the mechanisms underlying BC progression and metastasis, revealing new potential mechanisms and therapeutic approaches. In this study, we elucidated the specific cell communications and signaling pathways between different cells in the BC tumor microenvironment through integrated analyses of scRNA-seq, bulk-seq and clinical samples. Additionally, we conducted a comprehensive analysis of different subtypes of BC to explore the universal role of cell communication in BC. Undoubtedly, the subtypes of BC play a significant role in its treatment and progression. However, studying the universal role of cell communication in BC helps to uncover common potential communication mechanisms within the BC microenvironment, providing insights for developing drugs aimed at mitigating BC progression.

In this study, we used clinical samples from different BC subtypes, including ER+, HER2+, and triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), which may allow us to better understand the generalizability of the cell communication mechanisms across these subtypes. By including these diverse subtypes, our findings could offer broader implications for therapeutic strategies, which is essential for personalized medicine. Although hormonal signaling is an important driver of tumorigenesis, especially in ER + subtypes, it was not specifically addressed in this study. Hormonal regulation in the TME could influence immune cell communication and infiltration, and future studies may consider hormone-driven changes in the TME to further expand on our findings.

Our scRNA-seq analysis highlights the critical roles of CAFs, B cells, T cells, and myeloid cells in the TME, constructing a sophisticated cellular communication network, regulating immune cell infiltration, and contributing to tumor metastasis. In fact, T cells have always been the focus of attention in tumor immunology because they play an important role in immune recruitment and the initiation of cytotoxic antitumor responses. In general, the enrichment of CD4 + and CD8 + T cells in tumors is associated with better prognosis and therapeutic response [34–36]. Conversely, high levels of regulatory T cells (Tregs) are often associated with BC because Tregs can theoretically promote the infiltration of immunosuppressive tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs), myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), and neutrophils. This, in turn, can lead to the exhaustion of T cells that have infiltrated the tumor and are capable of killing cancer cells [37, 38].

Similarly, a large number of B cells were reported in 20% of BC cases, which was also principally explainable and, interestingly, highly suspected to be linked to the abundance of infiltrating T cells [39–41].

Besides, previous studies have pointed out that CAFs, as the most abundant cells within the TME matrix, could secrete diverse extracellular matrix (ECM) components, growth factors, cytokines, enzymes, and even hormones, thereby stimulating malignant transformation, tumor initiation, tumor growth, tumor angiogenesis, tumor cell invasion, and metastasis [42]. In addition, it has been proven that CAFs has an important value in diagnosis and prognosis prediction of BC patients [43].

As for myeloid cells, they have also been found cancer-associated for long [44]. They can further subclassified into TAMs and MDSCs, collaborating with Tregs in complex immunological interactions as stated before. In BC, the proliferation of tissue-resident macrophages builds a TAM pool within or around the tumor, in which two major subtypes, designated M1 and M2, accumulate. M1 classically activates or promotes the inflammatory processes, associated with the Th1 response that contributes to the antitumor immunity, whereas M2 is usually anti-inflammatory [45, 46]. Some more detailed M2 subtypes (e.g., M2d) was previously proven to enhance angiogenesis within tumor [47]. Given the critical roles played by myeloid cells in BC, Yang et al. even developed myeloid diversity index as a new prognostic metric to guide future patient evaluation and risk stratification, which was said to be capable to predict accurately [48].

Our results indicate that among the various pathways involved in the interactions between the aforementioned cell types within the tumor microenvironment (TME) of breast cancer (BC), the CXCL and FGF signaling pathways emerge as the most significant. Specifically, the CXCL12-CXCR4 and FGF7-FGFR1 ligand-receptor pairs are critical to these interactions. By combining these findings with bulk RNA sequencing (bulk-seq), we identified the top differentially expressed genes (DEGs)—CHRDL1, SCARA5, LYVE1, PI16, and SAA2—that may play a role in regulating these pathways. Spearman correlation analysis further elucidated the relationships between these DEGs and the ligand-receptor pairs, providing a clearer picture of the TME in BC. Notably, the correlation coefficients ranged from 0.28 to 0.38, with statistically significant p-values, confirming the relevance of these genes in our analysis. We also validated the expression of these genes in clinical samples, which supported our computational findings. Furthermore, we acknowledge that multiple genes may contribute to the regulation of a given pathway. While the expression of CXCL10 showed a lower contribution in our analysis, it remains a DEG in BC bulk mRNA-seq data, suggesting that other genes within this pathway may enhance its significance. Therefore, future studies should focus on identifying additional key genes that contribute to this pathway.

To the best of our knowledge, this study introduces an innovative analytical approach by examining the correlation between these DEGs and CXCL12, FGF7 at the single-cell level. Traditional studies typically perform such analyses using bulk-seq data, which aggregates the gene expression from all cells in a sample. In contrast, by treating each individual cell as a separate sample, our approach substantially increases the sample size, yielding more precise and nuanced results. Although the correlation coefficients were not large, we argue that in the context of scRNA-seq, where each cell is akin to a clinical sample in traditional bulk-seq, these findings remain statistically meaningful. The p-values indicate that these relationships are still of significance within the scope of our study. While the correlation coefficients may be modest, the observed trends provide valuable insights and are exploratory in nature, offering important directions for future research in this field.

There are some limitations in the present study. First, the relatively small number of clinical breast-cancer and adjacent normal samples (n = 5 pairs) limits the generalizability of our findings and may reduce the statistical power to detect subtle differences among subtypes. Second, although our integrated bioinformatics strategy combined single-cell and bulk transcriptomic data with qPCR validation, other complementary methodologies (e.g., in-vitro functional assays, spatial transcriptomics, or proteomic profiling) could provide additional evidence on the causal roles of the identified ligand–receptor pairs and DEGs. Finally, while funding constraints prevented us from performing laboratory functional studies at this stage, we plan to address these gaps in future work.

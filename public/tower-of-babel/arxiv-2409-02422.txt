Mixed Tensor Products, Capelli Berezinians, and Newton’s Formula for 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m | n ) \mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
Sidarth Erat, Arun S. Kannan, Shihan Kanungo

Abstract
. In this paper, we extend the results of Grantcharov and Robitaille in 2021 on mixed tensor products and Capelli determinants to the superalgebra setting. Specifically, we construct a family of superalgebra homomorphisms φ R : U ⁡ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m + 1 | n ) ) → 𝒟 ′ ​ ( m | n ) ⊗ U ⁡ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m | n ) ) \varphi_{R}:U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n))\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)\otimes U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)) for a certain space of differential operators 𝒟 ′ ​ ( m | n ) \mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n) indexed by a central element R R of 𝒟 ′ ​ ( m | n ) ⊗ U ⁡ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m | n ) ) \mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)\otimes U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)) . We then use this homomorphism to determine the image of Gelfand generators of the center of U ⁡ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m + 1 | n ) ) U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)) . We achieve this by first relating φ R \varphi_{R} to the corresponding Harish-Chandra homomorphisms and then proving a super-analog of Newton’s formula for 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m ) \mathfrak{gl}(m) relating Capelli generators and Gelfand generators. We also use the homomorphism φ R \varphi_{R} to obtain representations of U ⁡ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m + 1 | n ) ) U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)) from those of U ⁡ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m | n ) ) U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)) , and find conditions under which these inflations are simple. Finally, we show that for a distinguished central element R 1 R_{1} in 𝒟 ′ ​ ( m | n ) ⊗ U ⁡ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m | n ) ) \mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)\otimes U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)) , the kernel of φ R 1 \varphi_{R_{1}} is the ideal of U ⁡ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m + 1 | n ) ) U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)) generated by the first Gelfand invariant G 1 G_{1} .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Lie algebra setting

Mixed-tensor type modules, also known as tensor modules or modules of Shen and Larson (see
[
She86
,
Lar92
]
), are modules over the tensor product algebra
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m)\otimes U(\mathfrak{gl}(m))
, where
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m)
is a certain algebra of differential operators on the ring
\mathbb{C}[t_{0}^{\pm 1},t_{1},\dots,t_{m}]
and
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m))
is the universal enveloping algebra of the general linear Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}(m)
. Questions related to mixed-tensor type modules have been well studied over the past 35 years (see
[
She86
,
Lar92
,
Rao96
,
ER04
,
BF16
,
TZ17
,
HCL20
,
GS22
,
GR21
]
and references therein). Of interest to us is an algebra homomorphism

U(\mathfrak{sl}(m+1))\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m)\otimes U(\mathfrak{gl}(m))

which enables one to inflate representations from
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m))
to
U(\mathfrak{sl}(m+1))
(see
[
GR21
,
GS10
]
). Although this map can be written down algebraically in a straightforward way, there exists a natural geometric interpretation. In particular, it can be thought of as a form of Beilinson-Bernstein localization for parabolic subgroups using a certain maximal parabolic subgroup
P\subset SL(m+1)
such that
GL(m)\subset P
and such that
SL(m+1)/P\cong\mathbb{P}^{m}
(see
[
GS10
]
and references therein for more details).

In
[
GR21
]
, the homomorphism
U(\mathfrak{sl}(m+1))\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m)\otimes U(\mathfrak{gl}(m))
is extended to
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1))
. Moreover, the authors explicitly determine the kernel of this extension as well as the image of the Gelfand generators of the center
Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}(m)))
of
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m))
. Key ingredients in determining the image of the center are Capelli determinants, Capelli generators, and Newton’s formula (for all of these, see
[
Ume98
]
and
[
Mol07
]
). Along the way, numerous interesting algebraic identities are also proven; it is expected that these will have implications for the representation theory of tensor modules.

Additionally and importantly to us, the notion of mixed-tensor type modules can be generalized to the setting of Lie superalgebras. Moreover the study of tensor modules in the super setting has received much interest recently (see
[
BFIK20
,
XL21
,
XW23
,
LX23
]
and references therein). This setting is the focus of this paper. In particular, we generalize the results of
[
GR21
]
to the super setting for the general Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)
. Most of the arguments therein generalize, although there are some subtleties that arise from the super setting.

1.2.
Results of this paper

First, we construct a superalgebra of differential operators
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)
that preserve degree on the superalgebra
\mathbb{C}[t_{0}^{\pm 1},t_{1},\dots,t_{m}]\otimes\Lambda_{n}
, where
\Lambda_{n}
is the exterior algebra on
n
generators with
odd parity.
In Theorem
3.1
, we define via explicit formulas a family of superalgebra homomorphisms

\varphi_{R}:U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n))\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)\otimes U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n))

indexed by central elements
R
of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)\otimes U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n))
. Moreover, for
m+1\neq n
there is a distinguished homomorphism
\varphi_{R_{1}}
for a particular central element
R_{1}
. The map
\varphi_{R_{1}}
is an direct generalization to the superalgebra context of the map
\rho
in
[
GR21
]
and is “compatible” with a certain projection homomorphism
\pi^{g}:U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n))\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{sl}(m+1|n))
and the canonical inclusion in the opposite direction (see Proposition
3.3
).

Using the homomorphism
\varphi_{R}
, we can consider inflating irreducible representations of
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n))
to
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n))
by tensoring with a highest-weight module
\mathcal{F}_{a}
over
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)
generated by
t_{0}^{a}
(such a module can be defined for any
a\in\mathbb{C}
) and pulling back via
\varphi_{R}
. A partial criteria for when such representations are irreducible is the combined content of Theorems
4.2
and
4.3
.

We then take a closer look at the restriction of
\varphi_{R}
to the center of
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n))
. In particular, we show that
\varphi_{R}
interacts nicely with the Harish-Chandra homomorphism in Theorem
5.2
and compute the image of the Gelfand generators
G_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}
of the center in Theorem
6.3.3
. To do the latter, we use the theory of Yangians. In particular, we define what is meant by a Capelli generator in the super setting (originally due to
[
Naz91
]
) and then in Theorem
6.2.1
we reprove a super Newton’s formula (analogous to the formula for
\mathfrak{gl}(m)
in
[
Ume98
]
)

1.3.
Organization of this paper

In Section
2
we review some basics about general linear Lie superalgebras and establish some notation. In Section
3
we construct the homomorphism
\varphi_{R}
for
R\in Z(\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)\otimes U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)))
. In Section
4
, we discuss inflations of representations via the homomorphism
\varphi
. In Section
5
, we relate the restriction of
\varphi
to the center of
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n))
to the corresponding Harish-Chandra homomorphisms. In Section
6
, we reprove the super Newton’s formula and determine the images of the Gelfand generators under
\varphi
. Finally, in Section
7
, we derive the kernel of the map
\varphi
, a partial generalization of the results in Section 6 of
[
GR21
]
.

The proof that
\varphi_{R}
is a homomorphism is given in Appendix
A
, which is a long but straightforward computation. Appendix
B
gives some explicit formulas for the images under
\varphi
of a set of homogeneous elements of
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n))
, using a direct computational approach. All the Gelfand generators can be written as sums of elements from this set, so we obtain an alternative proof of Theorem
6.3.3
that does not rely on other sections.

1.4.
Future Directions

Let us discuss some further directions of inquiry. First of all, unlike the ordinary setting, we do not know of a geometric interpretation of the map
\varphi
but we believe that the restriction of this map to
U(\mathfrak{sl}(m+1|n))
should arise from considering a certain “parabolic subgroup”
\mathbf{P}\subseteq SL(m+1|n)
so that the quotient is projective superspace
\mathbb{P}^{m|n}
. In particular,
\mathbf{P}
should be described by the Harish-Chandra pair
(\mathbf{P}_{0},\mathfrak{p})
, where the underlying even group
\mathbf{P}_{0}\subset\mathbf{P}
is given by
P
as above, and the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathbf{P}
satisfies
\mathfrak{p}\cong V\oplus\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
where
V
is the tautological representation of
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
(see
[
Mas12
,
Shi20
]
for theory of Harish-Chandra pairs for supergroups).

Another question is whether there is some larger structure to the family of maps
\varphi_{R}
. In particular, is there an algebra structure on this set? If so, is there any representation-theoretic interpretation?

Finally, in
[
GR21
]
a family of left pseudoinverses are constructed for
\varphi
. It would be interesting to see if something analogous can be done in our setting.

2.
Preliminaries

We shall introduce some background for the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
in this section. Throughout the paper we fix nonnegative integers
m,n
.

2.1.
Basic definitions

For a Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{a}
, by
U(\mathfrak{a})
we denote the universal enveloping superalgebra of
\mathfrak{a}
and by
Z(\mathfrak{a})
the center of a superalgebra
\mathfrak{a}
. We will always use
|\cdot|
to denote the parity of a purely homogeneous element, which is
0
if even and
1
if odd (viewed as elements in
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
). We will work with general linear Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
, which we define as follows. Let

I\coloneqq\{1,\ldots,m,\bar{1},\ldots,\bar{n}\},

\hat{I}\coloneqq\{0\}\cup I

where we impose the total order

0<1<\cdots<m<\bar{1}<\cdots<\bar{n}.

We also define a parity function
|\cdot|
on
\hat{I}
, where the parity of indices without an overline is
0
and that of indices with an overline is
1
. Now, let
\mathbb{C}^{m+1|n}
denote the super vector space with basis
\{e_{0},e_{1},\dots,e_{m},e_{\bar{1}},\dots,e_{\bar{n}}\}
, where the parity of a basis vector is given by the parity of its index. The general linear Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)
is by definition the space of all linear maps on
\mathbb{C}^{m+1|n}
and can be thought of as super matrices of the block form

(2.1)

\begin{bmatrix}\begin{array}[]{c|c}A&B\\
\hline\cr C&D\end{array}\end{bmatrix},

where the matrix
A
is
(m+1)\times(m+1)
, the matrix
B
is
(m+1)\times n
, the matrix
C
is
n\times(m+1)
, and the matrix
D
is
n\times n
. The underlying even Lie algebra is
\mathfrak{gl}(m+1)\oplus\mathfrak{gl}(n)
and corresponds to matrices with
B=0
and
C=0
, and the odd part corresponds to matrices such that
A
and
D
are zero. The Lie bracket on purely homogeneous elements
x,y\in\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)
is given by

[x,y]\coloneqq xy-(-1)^{|x||y|}yx

where the multiplication is usual matrix multiplication. The
supertrace

\mathrm{str}
of a matrix of the form in (
2.1
)is defined to be

\mathrm{str}\left(\begin{bmatrix}\begin{array}[]{c|c}A&B\\
\hline\cr C&D\end{array}\end{bmatrix}\right)=\mathrm{tr}(A)-\mathrm{tr}(D),

where
\mathrm{tr}
denotes the usual trace of a matrix. The supertrace is a Lie superalgebra homomorphism, so by
\mathfrak{sl}(m+1|n)
we will denote the special linear Lie superalgebra whose elements are the kernel of
\mathrm{str}
. Our standard basis for
\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)
will be given by the usual elementary matrices
\{e_{ij}\}_{i,j\in\hat{I}}
. Finally,
we will always view
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
as the Lie sub-superalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)
spanned by
\{e_{ij}\}_{i,j\in I}
. We emphasize this point as the reader may be confused by the notation in what is to come if otherwise unaware.

A basis for
\mathfrak{sl}(m+1|n)
is

\{e_{ij}:i,j\in\hat{I},i\neq j\}\cup\{e_{00}-(-1)^{|i|}e_{ii}:i\in I\}

and we shall view
\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)
as the elements
\mathfrak{sl}(m+1|n)
that also lie in
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
under the inclusion above. Some other important subalgebra for
\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)
will be
\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n}
, the Cartan subalgebra of diagonal matrices in
\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)
, and
\mathfrak{n}^{-}_{m+1|n}
and
\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{m+1|n}
, the subalgebras of strictly lower and upper triangular matrices in
\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)
, respectively. We define the analogs of these subalgebras for
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
in the obvious way.

For a square matrix
A
and a variable or constant
v
, the expression
A+v
should be understood as the sum of
A
and the scalar matrix of the same size as
A
having
v
on the diagonal. Finally, let
\delta_{kl}
denote the Kronecker delta, which evaluates to
1
if
k=l
and
0
otherwise.

In Section
3
,
a
and
b
denote elements of
I
. In all sections,
i
and
j
denote elements of
I
unless specified otherwise.

2.2.
Weights and Root Systems

Let
\{\delta_{0},\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m},\delta_{\bar{1}},\ldots,\delta_{\bar{n}}\}
denote the basis of
\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n}^{*}
dual to the canonical basis
\{e_{00},e_{11},\ldots,e_{mm},e_{\bar{1}\bar{1}},\ldots,e_{\bar{n}\bar{n}}\}
of
\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n}^{*}
. Whenever convenient, for
1\leq i\leq n
, we will write

\varepsilon_{i}:=\delta_{\bar{i}}.

We will refer to elements of
\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n}^{*}
as
weights
, and will often write any weight
\lambda=\sum_{i\in\hat{I}}\lambda_{i}\delta_{i}
as a tuple
(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m},\lambda_{\bar{1}},\dots,\lambda_{\bar{n}})
. The bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot):\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n}\times\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
given by the supertrace
(x,y)\coloneqq\mathrm{str}(xy)
naturally induces a bilinear form on
\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n}^{*}
, which will also be denoted by
(\cdot,\cdot)
. For
i,j\in\hat{I}
, we have

(\delta_{i},\delta_{j})=(-1)^{|i||j|}\delta_{ij}.

We call
x\in\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)
a
root vector
if there exists a nonzero
\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n}^{*}
if for all
h\in\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n}
we have
[h,x]=\alpha(h)x
. The weight
\alpha
is called a
root
, and set of all roots is called the
root system
. It is well known that a root system for
\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)
is given by
\Phi\coloneqq\{\delta_{i}-\delta_{j}\}_{i\neq j\in\hat{I}}
, with a root vector corresponding to
\delta_{i}-\delta_{j}
being
e_{ij}
. Let
\Phi_{\bar{0}}
and
\Phi_{\bar{1}}
be the even and odd roots in
\Phi
, respectively, where a root is even if the corresponding root vector is even (resp. odd). It is easily seen that

{\Phi}_{\bar{0}}=\{\delta_{i}-\delta_{j}\}_{0\leq i\neq j\leq m}\cup\{\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}\}_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n},

{\Phi}_{\overline{1}}=\{\pm(\delta_{i}-\varepsilon_{j})\}_{0\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n}.

A root
\alpha\in\Phi
is said to be
isotropic
if
(\alpha,\alpha)=0
. The set of odd roots coincides with the set of isotropic roots for the general linear Lie superalgebras. Let
\Phi^{+}=\{\delta_{i}-\delta_{j}\ |\ i<j\in\hat{I}\}
be a positive system, and restrict this notation to the even and odd roots in the obvious way. Lastly, let
\mathcal{W}_{m+1|n}\cong S_{m+1}\times S_{n}
denote the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)
with natural action on
\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n}^{*}
, i.e,
S_{m+1}
permutes
\{\delta_{0},\dots,\delta_{m}\}
and
S_{n}
permutes
\{\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{n}\}
.

Furthermore, we can define for any
\alpha\in\Phi_{\overline{0}}
the corresponding coroot
\alpha^{\vee}\in\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n}
such that

\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=\frac{2(\lambda,\alpha)}{(\alpha,\alpha)}\ \ \forall\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n}^{*}.

The simple reflection
s_{\alpha}
acts on
\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n}^{*}
as expected:
s_{\alpha}(\lambda)=\lambda-\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\alpha
. Define the Weyl vector
\rho_{m+1|n}
as follows:

\rho_{m+1|n}\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{m}(m-i+1)\delta_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}j\varepsilon_{j}=(m+1,m,\ldots,1,-1,-2,\ldots,-n).

Note that our Weyl vector is a shifted version of the standard definition, which is half the sum of the positive even roots minus half the sum of the positive odd roots, but all this changes is that the Harish-Chandra homomorphism gets shifted. A weight
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n}^{*}
is said to be
antidominant
if
\langle\lambda+\rho_{m+1|n},\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\not\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
for all
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}_{\overline{0}}
. An element
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n}^{*}
is said to be
typical
(relative to
\Phi^{+}
) if there is no positive isotropic
\alpha\in\Phi_{\overline{1}}
such that
(\lambda+\rho_{m+1|n},\alpha)=0
.

For a weight
\lambda=(\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{m},\lambda_{\bar{1}},\ldots,\lambda_{\bar{n}})
of
\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)
, denote by
M(\lambda)
and
L(\lambda)
the Verma module of highest weight
\lambda
and its unique simple quotient, respectively. Here

M(\lambda)=U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n))\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{m+1|n})}\mathbb{C}_{\lambda},

where
\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}
is the one-dimensional weight representation of
\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n}
with weight
\lambda
extended trivially to
\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{m+1|n}
.

Finally (and very importantly to avoid confusion later on), to be consistent with our viewing
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
as the subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)
spanned by
(e_{ij})_{i,j\in I}
, we restrict all of the above definitions to
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
in the obvious way so that everything is compatible with the inclusion
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)\xhookrightarrow{}\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)
(with the “extra” index being
0
). For instance,

(1)

we will simply write a weight
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{m|n}^{*}
of
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
as an
(m+n)
-tuple, understanding that
\lambda_{0}=0
under the inclusion
\mathfrak{h}_{m|n}^{*}\xhookrightarrow{}\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n}^{*}
;

(2)

the Weyl group
\mathcal{W}_{m|n}
is
S_{m}\times S_{n}
and the Weyl vector
\rho_{m|n}
is

\rho_{m|n}\coloneqq(m,m-1,\ldots,1,-1,-2,\ldots,-n);

(3)

a Verma module
M(\lambda)
and its simple quotient
L(\lambda)
can either mean such a
\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)
-module with highest weight
\lambda
or such a
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
-module with highest weight
\lambda
(not by restriction). Context will make it clear to which we refer (using a
(m+n)
-tuple will mean interpret it as a module over
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
, for instance).

2.3.
Superalgebra of Differential Operators

In this subsection, we define the superalgebra
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)
of differential operators.
First, we define the superalgebra of polynomials that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)
operates on.

Define
\mathcal{F}\coloneqq\mathbb{C}[t_{0}^{\pm 1},t_{1},\dots,t_{m}]\otimes\Lambda_{n}
, where
\Lambda_{n}
is the
n
-generator exterior algebra with odd generators
t_{\bar{1}},\ldots,t_{\bar{n}}
.
The defining relations of
\Lambda_{n}
are given by

t_{i}t_{j}=-t_{j}t_{i}

for
i,j\in\{\bar{1},\ldots,\bar{n}\}
. This anticommutativity, combined with the fact that
\mathbb{C}[t_{0}^{\pm 1},t_{1},\dots,t_{m}]
is commutative, means that
\mathcal{F}
is supercommutative, i.e. that is
x
and
y
are homogeneous, then
xy=(-1)^{|x||y|}yx
. A basis for
\mathcal{F}
is given by vectors of the form
t_{0}^{k_{0}}t_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots t_{m}^{k_{m}}t_{\bar{1}}^{k_{\bar{1}}}\cdots t_{\bar{n}}^{k_{\bar{n}}}
, where
k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
k_{1},\dots,k_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and
k_{\bar{1}},\dots k_{\bar{n}}\in\{0,1\}
. For convenience, we will drop the tensor symbol in the notation and also freely permute the
t_{i}
’s in accordance with the supercommutativity rules.

Next, we define the operation
\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t_{i}}
. We write
\partial_{i}
as shorthand for
\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t_{i}}
.
Consider an element
t_{0}^{k_{0}}t_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots t_{m}^{k_{m}}t_{\bar{1}}^{k_{\bar{1}}}\cdots t_{\bar{n}}^{k_{\bar{n}}}\in\mathcal{F}
. To operate by
\partial_{i}
, we first move
t_{i}^{k_{i}}
to the front using supercommutativity. Then replace
t_{i}^{k_{i}}
by
k_{i}t_{i}^{k_{i}-1}
. Extend to the whole of
\mathcal{F}
by linearity and supercommutativity.

Then, we define the superalgebra of differential operators
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)
to be the superalgebra generated by: left multiplication by
\frac{t_{i}}{t_{0}}
for
i\in\hat{I}
, where we will abuse notation and simply write
\frac{t_{i}}{t_{0}}
for this operation; and
t_{0}\partial_{i}
for
i\in\hat{I}
, which consists of left applying the
i
’th derivative and then left multiplication by
t_{0}
. The parity of these is operators is given by the parity of
i\in\hat{I}
. We define the element

\mathcal{E}\coloneqq\sum_{i\in\hat{I}}\left(\frac{t_{i}}{t_{0}}\right)(t_{0}\partial_{i})=\sum_{i\in\hat{I}}t_{i}\partial_{i}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n).

Note that
\mathcal{E}
generates the center of
\mathcal{D^{\prime}}(m|n).

The following lemma collects some identities in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)
. The proofs are simple so we omit them.

Lemma 2.3.1
.

The superalgebra
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)
of differential operators on
\mathbb{C}[t_{0}^{\pm 1},t_{1},\dots,t_{m}]\otimes\Lambda_{n}
generated by
\frac{t_{i}}{t_{0}}
for
i\in I
and

4.
Inflating representations

In this section we use
\varphi
(recall that
\varphi
is shorthand for
\varphi_{R}
) to extend a simple representation of

to a representation of
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n))
and find conditions for when this new representation is simple.

For any

, define the
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)

\mathcal{F}_{a}\coloneqq\mathrm{span}\{t_{0}^{a-k_{1}-\cdots-k_{\bar{n}}}t_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{k_{m}}t_{\bar{1}}^{k_{\bar{1}}}\cdots t_{\bar{n}}^{k_{\bar{n}}}\mid k_{1},\ldots,k_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\ k_{\bar{1}},\ldots,k_{\bar{n}}\in\{0,1\}\}.

This is a generalization of the
\mathcal{F}_{a}
defined in
[
GR21
]
, both in the sense that it is defined for superalgebras and in that we do not require

.
Note that
\mathcal{F}_{a}=\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)(t_{0}^{a})
(i.e. the set that results when we apply elements of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)
to
t_{0}^{a}
) and that
\mathcal{E}=a\mathrm{Id}
on
\mathcal{F}_{a}
. Given a weight
\lambda
of
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
, we write
\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes L(\lambda)
(and
\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes M(\lambda)

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)\otimes U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n))
and the representation
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n))
via
\varphi

of
L(\lambda)
.

Let us now consider inflating the simple module
L(\lambda)
using

. In particular, we investigate when the resulting module is simple.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
v_{\lambda}
be the unique highest weight vector for
L(\lambda)
, up to scaling. The vector
t_{0}^{a}\otimes v_{\lambda}
is the only highest weight vector (up to scaling) for
\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes L(\lambda)
, considered as a
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n))
-module.

Proof.

For
0<i<j
, we have that
e_{ij}
acts as
t_{i}\partial_{j}\otimes 1+1\otimes e_{ij}
. Now
t_{i}\partial_{j}(t_{0}^{a})=0
and
e_{ij}
acts on
v_{\lambda}
as
0
since
i<j
, so
e_{ij}
acts on
t_{0}^{a}\otimes v_{\lambda}
as
0
. Also, for each
j>0
,
e_{0j}
acts on
t_{0}^{a}\otimes v_{\lambda}
as
t_{0}\partial_{j}\otimes 1
. Now
t_{0}\partial_{j}(t_{0}^{a})=0
, so
e_{0j}
also acts as
0
. It follows that
t_{0}^{a}\otimes v_{\lambda}
is a highest weight vector.

Now suppose

(4.1)

\displaystyle\widetilde{w}=p_{1}\otimes w_{1}+\cdots p_{k}\otimes w_{k}

is a highest weight vector, where

p_{c}=t_{0}^{a-k_{1,c}-k_{2,c}-\cdots-k_{m,c}-k_{\bar{1},c}-\cdots-k_{\bar{n},c}}t_{1}^{k_{1,c}}\cdots t_{n}^{k_{m,c}}t_{\bar{1}}^{k_{\bar{1},c}}\cdots t_{\bar{n}}^{k_{\bar{n},c}},

where
k_{1,c},\ldots,k_{m,c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
k_{\bar{1},c},\ldots,k_{\bar{n},c}\in\{0,1\}
. Furthermore assume the
p_{c}
are distinct and the
w_{c}
are nonzero.
Now
e_{0j}
(for
j\in I
), which acts as
t_{0}\partial_{j}\otimes 1
, must act as zero on
\widetilde{w}
. Pick the
p_{c}
that is
t
-lexicographically maximal (i.e. relative to the
t_{1}
-degree,
t_{2}
-degree, and so on). Then,
e_{01}^{k_{1,c}}\cdots e_{0\bar{n}}^{k_{\bar{n},c}}
acts as zero on all
p_{d}\otimes w_{d}
except for
p_{c}\otimes w_{c}
, on which it gives a nonzero result. Thus
e_{01}^{k_{1,c}}\cdots e_{0\bar{n}}^{k_{\bar{n},c}}
does not act as zero on
\widetilde{w}
, a contradiction unless
p_{c}=t_{0}^{a}
, in which case

and we can write
\widetilde{w}=t_{0}^{a}\otimes w
.

Then, for
i<j
,
e_{ij}
acts as
t_{i}\partial_{j}\otimes 1+1\otimes e_{ij}
, and this must act as zero on
\widetilde{w}
. First, note that the
t_{i}\partial_{j}\otimes 1
part acts as zero. Thus we must have
e_{ij}
acting on

as
0
for all
i<j
, i.e.
w
is a highest weight vector of

, so

is a scalar multiple of

. Thus,
t_{0}^{a}\otimes v_{\lambda}
is the only the highest weight vector (up to scaling) of
\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes L(\lambda)
, considered as a
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n))
-module.
∎

We can write
R
in the form
R=\sum_{i}\mathcal{E}^{i}\otimes z_{i}
for some
z_{i}\in Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)))
by Lemma
2.3.1
. A direct computation shows that
R
acts as a scalar on
\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes L(\lambda)
, which we will call
r
:

\displaystyle R\cdot\left(\sum_{j}f_{j}\otimes v_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i}\mathcal{E}^{i}\otimes z_{i}\right)\left(\sum_{j}f_{j}\otimes v_{j}\right)=\sum_{i,j}(\deg f_{j})^{i}f_{j}\otimes\chi_{\lambda}(z_{i})v_{j}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i}a^{i}\chi_{\lambda}(z_{i})\right)\left(\sum_{j}f_{j}\otimes v_{j}\right)=r\left(\sum_{j}f_{j}\otimes v_{j}\right),

where
f_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{a},v_{j}\in L(\lambda)
are arbitrary, and
\chi_{\lambda}
is the central character corresponding to
\lambda
(see Section
5
). It follows that the weight for
t_{0}^{a}\otimes v_{\lambda}
is

(4.2)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\lambda}\coloneqq(a+r,\lambda_{1}+r,\ldots,\lambda_{m}+r,\lambda_{\bar{1}}-r,\ldots,\lambda_{\bar{n}}-r).

Next, we find conditions on
\lambda
for
t_{0}^{a}\otimes v_{\lambda}
to generate the module
\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes L(\lambda)
. For
i\in I
, define

f(i)\coloneqq\sum_{j<i}(-1)^{|j|},

where the sum runs over
j\in I
with
j<i
(recall the ordering on
I
defined by
1<2<\cdots<m<\bar{1}<\cdots<\bar{n}
). Then
f(1)=0,f(2)=1,\ldots,f(m)=m-1,f(\bar{1})=m,f(\bar{2})=m-1,\ldots,f(\bar{n})=m+1-n
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\lambda
be a
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
-weight. If
a+f(i)-(-1)^{|i|}\lambda_{i}
is not a nonnegative integer for all
i\in I
, then
\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes L(\lambda)
is a highest weight module.

Proof.

Let
N
be the submodule generated by the highest weight vector
t_{0}^{a}\otimes v_{\lambda}
. We first claim that all monomials in
\mathcal{F}_{a}
, and thus all elements of
\mathcal{F}_{a}
, create an element of
N
when tensored with
v_{\lambda}
, the highest weight vector for
L(\lambda)
. We proceed by induction down on the exponent of
t_{0}
. Specifically, assume that
T\otimes v_{\lambda}=t_{0}^{a_{0}}S\otimes v_{\lambda}=t_{0}^{a_{0}}t_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots t_{\bar{n}}^{a_{\bar{n}}}\otimes v_{\lambda}\in N
for
a_{0}+a_{1}+\cdots+a_{\bar{n}}=a
, and
a_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
for
|i|=0
and
a_{i}\in\{0,1\}
for
|i|=1
. We claim that
(t_{i}/t_{0}\otimes 1)(T\otimes v_{\lambda})\in N
for any
i\in I
. To do this we induct on
i
.
First look at the case
i=1
. We have

e_{10}\cdot(t_{0}^{a_{0}}S\otimes v_{\lambda})=(t_{1}\partial_{0}\otimes 1-\sum_{j}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{1j})(t_{0}^{a_{0}}S\otimes v_{\lambda})=a_{0}t_{1}t_{0}^{a_{0}-1}S\otimes v_{\lambda}-t_{1}t_{0}^{a_{0}-1}S\otimes\lambda_{1}v_{\lambda}.

which means
(t_{1}/t_{0}\otimes 1)(T\otimes v_{\lambda})
is in
N
as long as
a_{0}-\lambda_{1}\neq 0
. But since
a-\lambda_{1}
is not a nonnegative integer, and
a-a_{0}
is a nonnegative integer,
a_{0}-\lambda_{1}\neq 0
.
Thus the base case is proved.
For general
i\neq 0
, note that

\displaystyle e_{i0}\cdot(t_{0}^{b}S\otimes v_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\left(t_{i}\partial_{0}\otimes 1-\sum_{j}(-1)^{|i||j|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{ij}\right)(t_{0}^{b}S\otimes v_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=bt_{i}t_{0}^{b-1}S\otimes v_{\lambda}-(-1)^{|i|}\lambda_{i}t_{i}t_{0}^{b-1}S\otimes v_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\sum_{j<i}(-1)^{|i||j|+(|i|+|j|)(|t_{0}^{b}S|)}t_{j}t_{0}^{b-1}S\otimes(e_{ij}\cdot v_{\lambda}).

In addition, for
0<j<i
, if
|j|=0
, we have

\displaystyle e_{ij}\cdot(t_{j}t_{0}^{b-1}S\otimes v_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=(t_{i}\partial_{j}\otimes 1)(t_{j}t_{0}^{b-1}S\otimes v_{\lambda})+(1\otimes e_{ij})(t_{j}t_{0}^{b-1}S\otimes v_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=(1+a_{j})t_{i}t_{0}^{b-1}S\otimes v_{\lambda}+(-1)^{|i||j|+|j|+(|i|+|j|)(|t_{0}^{b}S|)}(t_{j}t_{0}^{b-1}S\otimes(e_{ij}\cdot v_{\lambda}))

\displaystyle=(1+a_{j})t_{i}t_{0}^{b-1}S\otimes v_{\lambda}+(-1)^{|i||j|+(|i|+|j|)(|t_{0}^{b}S|)}(t_{j}t_{0}^{b-1}S\otimes(e_{ij}\cdot v_{\lambda})).

If
|j|=1
, then

\displaystyle-e_{ij}\cdot(t_{j}t_{0}^{b-1}S\otimes v_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=-(t_{i}\partial_{j}\otimes 1)(t_{j}t_{0}^{b-1}S\otimes v_{\lambda})-(1\otimes e_{ij})(t_{j}t_{0}^{b-1}S\otimes v_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=-(1-a_{j})t_{i}t_{0}^{b-1}S\otimes v_{\lambda}-(-1)^{|i||j|+|j|+(|i|+|j|)(|t_{0}^{b}S|)}(t_{j}t_{0}^{b-1}S\otimes(e_{ij}\cdot v_{\lambda}))

\displaystyle=-(1-a_{j})t_{i}t_{0}^{b-1}S\otimes v_{\lambda}+(-1)^{|i||j|+(|i|+|j|)(|t_{0}^{b}S|)}(t_{j}t_{0}^{b-1}S\otimes(e_{ij}\cdot v_{\lambda})).

since if
a_{j}=0
, then
(t_{i}\partial_{j})(t_{j}t_{0}^{b-1}S)=t_{i}t_{0}^{b-1}
and if
a_{j}=1
, then
t_{j}t_{0}^{b-1}S=0
since
t_{j}^{2}=0
.
Also, both of these are in
N
because
t_{j}t_{0}^{b-1}S\otimes v_{\lambda}\in N
, from the inductive hypothesis.
Adding these equations (for all
j<i
) to the first gives us that

\left(b+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j<i\\
|j|=0\end{subarray}}(1+a_{j})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j<i\\
|j|=1\end{subarray}}(a_{j}-1)-(-1)^{|i|}\lambda_{i}\right)t_{i}t_{0}^{b-1}S\otimes v_{\lambda}\in N.

Now we show that the expression in parentheses (call it
A
) is nonzero. Note

A=b+\sum_{j<i}a_{j}+f(i)-(-1)^{|i|}\lambda_{i}=b+\sum_{j\in I}a_{j}+f(i)-(-1)^{|i|}\lambda_{i}-K=a+f(i)-(-1)^{|i|}\lambda_{i}-K,

where
K
is some nonnegative integer. Since
a+f(i)-(-1)^{|i|}\lambda_{i}
is not a nonnegative integer, it follows that
A\neq 0

so
t_{i}t_{0}^{b-1}S\otimes v_{\lambda}\in N
.

Now, we show that
p\otimes(e_{ij}\cdot v_{\lambda})\in N
, for any
p\in\mathcal{F}_{a}
and
i,j\in I
. We have

e_{ij}\cdot(p\otimes v_{\lambda})=(t_{i}\partial_{j})(p)\otimes v_{\lambda}+p\otimes(e_{ij}\cdot v_{\lambda})+\delta_{ij}(-1)^{|i||j|}R(p\otimes v_{\lambda}),

and this element is in
N
. Now
R
acts as

(see the discussion preceding (
4.2
)), so
R(p\otimes v_{\lambda})\in N
and
(t_{i}\partial_{j})(p)\otimes v_{\lambda}\in N
from the first part of the proof. Thus
p\otimes(e_{ij}\cdot v_{\lambda})\in N
for all
p\in\mathcal{F}_{a}
and
i,j\in I
.
Repeating this process multiple times shows that
p\otimes(e_{i_{1}j_{1}}e_{i_{2}j_{2}}\cdots e_{i_{k}j_{k}}\cdot v_{\lambda})\in N
for any

. Since
v_{\lambda}
generates

, this shows

for any

and

. Thus

.
∎

Using the following theorem, we can use the previous theorem to find conditions when
\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes L(\lambda)\cong L(\widetilde{\lambda})

Theorem 4.3
.

For a weight
\lambda
of
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
, we have
\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes L(\lambda)\cong L(\widetilde{\lambda})
as
\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)
-modules if and only if
\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes L(\lambda)
is generated by the highest weight vector
t_{0}^{a}\otimes v_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

First note that by Proposition
4.1
, showing
\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes L(\lambda)\cong L(\widetilde{\lambda})
is equivalent to showing
\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes L(\lambda)
is simple. Let
N
denote the submodule of
\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes L(\lambda)
generated by
t_{0}^{a}\otimes v_{\lambda}
. If
N\neq\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes L(\lambda)
, it is obvious that
\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes L(\lambda)
cannot be simple. Now we prove the “if” direction.

Since
N=\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes L(\lambda)
, we know
t_{0}^{a}\otimes v_{\lambda}
generates
\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes L(\lambda)
. Let
x
be a nonzero element of
\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes L(\lambda)
. Let
N(x)
be the module generated by
x
. We will show that
t_{0}^{a}\otimes v_{\lambda}\in N(x)
, so

, thereby showing

is simple, since
x
is an arbitrary element.

Write

(4.3)

\displaystyle x=c_{1}p_{1}\otimes w_{1}+\cdots c_{k}p_{k}\otimes w_{k},

where

p_{c}=t_{0}^{a-k_{1,c}-k_{2,c}-\cdots-k_{m,c}-k_{\bar{1},c}-\cdots-k_{\bar{n},c}}t_{1}^{k_{1,c}}\cdots t_{n}^{k_{m,c}}t_{\bar{1}}^{k_{\bar{1},c}}\cdots t_{\bar{n}}^{k_{\bar{n},c}},

where
k_{1,c},\ldots,k_{m,c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
k_{\bar{1},c},\ldots,k_{\bar{n},c}\in\{0,1\}
,
w_{c}=e_{i_{1,c}j_{1,c}}e_{i_{2,c}j_{2,c}}\cdots e_{i_{d,c}j_{d,c}}\cdot v_{\lambda}
, and finally
c_{1},\ldots c_{k}\in\mathbb{C}_{\neq 0}
. We claim that a nonzero element of the form

(4.4)

\displaystyle x^{\prime}=c_{1}^{\prime}t_{0}^{a}\otimes w_{1}^{\prime}+\cdots c_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime}t_{0}^{a}\otimes w_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime}

exists in
N(x)
. For
x
in the form (
4.3
), we define

(4.5)

\displaystyle S(x)=\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}k_{i,\ell}.

Note
S(x)
is defined uniquely for all
x
and
S(x)
is a nonnegative integer. Additionally,
S(x^{\prime})=0
if and only if
x^{\prime}
is in the form (
4.4
). If

, we are done.
Otherwise, there exists

for some

. Applying

to

(where we have

, so

decreases under this action. Also, note that

is nonzero since

, we are done. Otherwise we can do the same process to
e_{0j}\cdot x
, finding
e_{0j^{\prime}}e_{0j}\cdot x\neq 0
with
S(e_{0j^{\prime}}e_{0j}\cdot x)<S(e_{0j}\cdot x)
. If
S(e_{0j^{\prime}}e_{0j}\cdot x)=0
we are done. Otherwise we can just keep continuing this process, which must terminate since
S
is always an integer.
Then it follows that we can find
x^{\prime}\in N(x)
which can be written as (
4.4
). We can then write
x^{\prime}
as

x^{\prime}=t_{0}^{a}\otimes w,

where
w\in\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes L(\lambda)
and
w\neq 0
.

Next, we show that if
t_{0}^{a}\otimes w^{\prime}\in N(x)
, then
t_{0}^{a}\otimes(e_{ij}\cdot w^{\prime})\in N(x)
, for
i,j\in I
. We have

\displaystyle e_{ij}\cdot(t_{0}^{a}\otimes w^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(t_{i}\partial_{j})\cdot(t_{0}^{a}\otimes w^{\prime})+(1\otimes e_{ij})\cdot(t_{0}^{a}\otimes w^{\prime})+\delta_{ij}(-1)^{|i||j|}R\cdot(t_{0}^{a}\otimes w^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\pm t_{0}^{a}\otimes(e_{ij}\cdot w^{\prime})+s(t_{0}^{a}\otimes w^{\prime})

for a constant
s\in\mathbb{C}
. Subtracting
s(t_{0}^{a}\otimes w^{\prime})
, we get that
t_{0}^{a}\otimes(e_{ij}\cdot w^{\prime})\in N(x)
.

Let
N^{\prime}(w)
be the submodule generated by
w
in
L(\lambda)
.
Since
x^{\prime}=t_{0}^{a}\otimes w\in N(x)
, from the proceeding argument, it follows that
t_{0}^{a}\otimes N^{\prime}(w)\subset N(x)
. But since
L(\lambda)
is simple, it follows that
N^{\prime}(w)=L(\lambda)
. In particular,
v\in N^{\prime}(w)
, so
t_{0}^{a}\otimes v_{\lambda}\in N(x)
. Thus
N(x)=\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes L(\lambda)
, so
\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes L(\lambda)
is simple.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\lambda
be a
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
-weight. If
(\widetilde{\lambda}+\rho_{m+1|n},\delta_{0}-\delta_{i})\not\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
for
1\leq i\leq m
and
(\widetilde{\lambda}+\rho_{m+1|n},\delta_{0}-\delta_{i})\not\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
for
\bar{1}\leq i\leq\bar{n}
, then
\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes L(\lambda)
is simple, which implies
\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes L(\lambda)\cong L(\widetilde{\lambda})
.

Proof.

We show that the conditions given are equivalent to the conditions in Theorem
4.2
. Letting
\rho=\rho_{m|n}
, note that
f(i)+1=\rho_{0}-\rho_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
and
f(i)=\rho_{0}+\rho_{i}
for
\bar{1}\leq i\leq\bar{n}
, where
\rho=\rho_{m+1|n}
. It follows that the condition
a+f(i)-(-1)^{|i|}\lambda_{i}
not being a nonnegative integer is equivalent to the conditions stated in the corollary. Then the result follows by Theorem
4.3
and Theorem
4.2
.
∎

Let us recall the following fact: if
\lambda
is both antidominant and typical, then
M(\lambda)
is simple, see for reference
[
Gor01a
]
or
[
Gor01b
]
. Our result will be a consequence of Corollary
4.4
.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{AW}_{m|n}(a)
denote the set of all typical antidominant
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
-weights
\lambda
such that
\lambda_{i}-(-1)^{|i|}a\not\in\mathbb{Z}
for
i\in I
. If
\lambda\in\mathcal{AW}_{m|n}(a)
, then
\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes M(\lambda)
, considered as a
\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)
-representation, is isomorphic to
M(\widetilde{\lambda})
.

Proof.

Since
\lambda
is antidominant and typical,
L(\lambda)=M(\lambda)
.
For
1\leq i<j\leq m
or
\bar{1}\leq i<j\leq\bar{n}
, we have
(\widetilde{\lambda}+\rho_{m+1|n})_{i}-(\widetilde{\lambda}+\rho_{m+1|n})_{j}=(\lambda+\rho_{m|n})_{i}-(\lambda+\rho_{m|n})_{j}\not\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Additionally, note that for
1\leq i\leq m
,
(\widetilde{\lambda}+\rho_{m+1|n})_{0}-(\widetilde{\lambda}+\rho_{m+1|n})_{i}=a+i-\lambda_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
since
a-\lambda_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus
\widetilde{\lambda}
is antidominant.

For
1\leq i\leq m
and
\bar{1}\leq j\leq\bar{n}
, we have

(\widetilde{\lambda}+\rho_{m|n},\delta_{i}-\delta_{j})=(\widetilde{\lambda}+\rho_{m+1|n})_{i}+(\widetilde{\lambda}+\rho_{m+1|n})_{j}=(\lambda+\rho_{m|n})_{i}+(\lambda+\rho_{m|n})_{j}\neq 0

since
\lambda
is typical. Finally, for
\bar{1}\leq j\leq\bar{n}
, we have

(\widetilde{\lambda}+\rho_{m|n},\delta_{0}-\delta_{j})=(\widetilde{\lambda}+\rho_{m+1|n})_{0}+(\widetilde{\lambda}+\rho_{m+1|n})_{j}=a+m+1+\lambda_{j}-j\neq 0

because
a+\lambda_{j}
is not an integer. Therefore
\widetilde{\lambda}
is typical as well as antidominant.

Thus
M(\widetilde{\lambda})=L(\widetilde{\lambda})
is simple. Then note that
\lambda
satisfies the conditions in Corollary
4.4
, so the result follows.
∎

Now suppose
\xi\in\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n}^{*}
such that
(\xi+\rho_{m+1|n},\alpha^{\vee})\not\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
for
\alpha\in\Phi_{\overline{0}}^{+}
and
(\xi+\rho_{m+1|n},\alpha)\not\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
for
\alpha\in\Phi_{\overline{1}}^{+}
. Note that the first condition is simply that
\xi
is antidominant and the second implies
\xi
is typical. Then inductively apply Corollary
4.4
to use the modules
\mathcal{F}_{a}
to “build” the representation
L(\xi)
for
\xi\in\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n}^{*}
. The conditions on
\xi
allow us to apply Corollary
4.4
iteratively (with
R=0
) to get a map

\displaystyle U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n))\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0|n)\otimes\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(n-1|0)\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0|0)

that gives us a
\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)
-representation

\mathscr{F}(\xi)\coloneqq\mathcal{F}_{\xi_{0}}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\xi_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\xi_{\bar{n}}}

that is isomorphic to
L(\xi)
.
(Note that when constructing the chain have to use the obvious isomorphism from
U(\mathfrak{gl}(0|n))
to
U(\mathfrak{gl}(n|0))
given by
e_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}\mapsto e_{ij}
).
But
\xi
is antidominant and typical, so
\mathscr{F}(\xi)
is isomorphic to
M(\xi)
as well. This applies to substantially more general setting than
4.5
, and motivates the following conjecture:

Conjecture 4.6
.

There is an isomorphism of
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n))
-modules
\mathscr{F}(\xi)\cong M(\xi)
for all weights
\xi\in\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n}^{*}
.

Further supporting evidence is that the modules have the same formal supercharacters, which is verified in a manner analogous to the proof of Theorem 4.2, page 6 in
[
GR21
]
.

5.
Images under Harish-Chandra homomorphisms

In this section, we relate the restriction of
\varphi
to the center of
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n))
with the Harish-Chandra homomorphisms.

For
M\in\{m+1,m\}
, recall
\rho_{M|n}=(M,\ldots,1,-1,\ldots,-n)
. If
b=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{M},b_{\bar{1}},\ldots,b_{\bar{n}})\in\mathbb{C}^{M|n}
, then the evaluation homomorphism
\mathrm{ev}_{b}:\mathbb{C}[l_{1},\ldots,l_{M},l_{\bar{1}},\ldots,l_{\bar{n}}]\to\mathbb{C}
is defined by

\mathrm{ev}_{b}(p)\coloneqq p(b_{1},\ldots,b_{M},b_{\bar{1}},\ldots,b_{\bar{n}}).

Every
z\in Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n))
acts on
L(\lambda)
as
\chi_{\lambda}(z)\mathrm{Id}
, where
\chi_{\lambda}(z)=\mathrm{ev}_{\lambda+\rho_{m|n}}(\chi_{m|n}(z))
and
\chi_{m|n}:Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)))\to\mathbb{C}[\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m},\ell_{\bar{1}},\ldots,\ell_{\bar{n}}]^{\mathcal{W}_{m|n}}
is the Harish-Chandra homomorphism (see Theorem 13.1.1 (a) in
[
Mus12
]
). We similarly define
\chi_{m+1|n}:Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)))\to\mathbb{C}[\ell_{0},\ldots,\ell_{m},\ell_{\bar{1}},\ldots,\ell_{\bar{n}}]^{\mathcal{W}_{m+1|n}}
.

Next, we define
\chi_{0,m|n}:\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{E}]\otimes Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)))\to\mathbb{C}[\ell_{0}]\otimes\mathbb{C}[\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{\bar{n}}]^{\mathcal{W}_{m|n}}
by

\chi_{0,m|n}\left(\sum_{i}\mathcal{E}^{i}\otimes z_{i}\right)=\sum_{i}\ell_{0}^{i}\otimes\chi_{m|n}(z_{i}).

Set
\ell=\chi_{0,m|n}(R)
. In the case when
R
is given by (
3.1
), we have

\ell=-\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{i\in\hat{I}}\ell_{i}-\frac{m(m+1)}{2}+\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\right).

Note that the symmetric superalgebra
S(\mathfrak{h}_{m|n})
is isomorphic, in a canonical way, to the superalgebra of
\mathcal{W}_{m|n}
-invariant polynomials in
\mathbb{C}[\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{\bar{n}}]
. Let
I(\mathfrak{h}_{m|n})
be the subalgebra of
S(\mathfrak{h}_{m|n})
consisting of all
\mathcal{W}_{m|n}
-invariant functions
\theta
on
\mathfrak{h}_{m|n}^{*}
such that if
\alpha
is an isotropic odd root and
(\lambda,\alpha)=0
, then
\theta(\lambda)=\theta(\lambda+t\alpha)
for all
t\in\mathbb{C}
. It is known that
\chi_{m|n}:Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)))\to I(\mathfrak{h}_{m|n})
is an isomorphism (see Theorem 13.1.1 (b) in
[
Mus12
]
). Note that this means
\chi_{0,m|n}
, considered as a map from
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{E}]\otimes Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)))
to
\mathbb{C}[\ell_{0}]\otimes I(\mathfrak{h}_{m|n}),
is an isomorphism as well.

For the remainder of this section, we consider the module
\mathcal{F}_{a}
only when
a
is an integer. Then note
\mathcal{AW}_{m|n}\coloneqq\mathcal{AW}_{m|n}(a)=\mathcal{AW}_{m|n}(0)
.

Lemma 5.1
.

The modules

\mathcal{F}=\bigoplus_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{F}_{a}\text{ and }\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathcal{AW}_{m|n}}M(\lambda)

are faithful over
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)
and
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
, respectively.

Proof.

We prove that both modules have trivial annihilators.

If
z\in Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)))
annihilates
\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathcal{AW}_{m|n}}M(\lambda)
, then
\chi_{\lambda}(z)=0
for all
\lambda\in\mathcal{AW}_{m|n}
. Since
\chi_{\lambda}(z)=\mathrm{ev}_{\lambda+\rho_{m|n}}(\chi_{m|n}(z))
, we have

\mathrm{ev}_{\lambda+\rho_{m|n}}(\chi_{m|n}(z))=0

for all
\lambda\in\mathcal{AW}_{m|n}
. But this can only happen if
\chi_{m|n}(z)=0
. Thus, we must have
z=0
.

Now suppose
x\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)
annihilates
\mathcal{F}
and
x
is nonzero. Write
x
as the sum of elements of the form

p(t_{0},\ldots,t_{\bar{n}})\cdot\partial_{0}^{b_{0}}\cdots\partial_{\bar{n}}^{b_{\bar{n}}}

where
p(t_{0},\ldots,t_{\bar{n}})
is some nonzero polynomial. Furthermore, assume that each term in the sum has a different
(b_{0},\ldots,b_{\bar{n}})
. Pick the term that is
\partial
-lexicographically maximal (i.e. relative to the
\partial_{0}
-degree, the
\partial_{1}
-degree, and so on). Consider the polynomial
t_{0}^{b_{0}}t_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots t_{\bar{n}}^{b_{\bar{n}}}
. Then the leximaximal property of the term we picked guarantees that all the other terms act as zero. Furthermore,

\displaystyle(\partial_{0}^{b_{0}}\cdots\partial_{\bar{n}}^{b_{\bar{n}}})(t_{0}^{b_{0}}\cdots t_{\bar{n}}^{b_{\bar{n}}})=c

for a nonzero
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore

\displaystyle x\cdot(t_{0}^{b_{0}}\cdots t_{\bar{n}}^{b_{\bar{n}}})

\displaystyle=(p(t_{0},\ldots,t_{\bar{n}})\cdot\partial_{0}^{b_{0}}\cdots\partial_{\bar{n}}^{b_{\bar{n}}})(t_{0}^{b_{0}}\cdots t_{\bar{n}}^{b_{\bar{n}}})

\displaystyle=c\cdot p(t_{0},\ldots,t_{\bar{n}})\neq 0.

This contradicts the fact that
x
annihilates
\mathcal{F}
. Therefore
\mathcal{F}
has a trivial annihilator.
∎

Define

\tau(p(\ell_{0},\ldots,\ell_{m},\ell_{\bar{1}},\ldots\ell_{\bar{{n}}}))\coloneqq p(\ell_{0}+\ell+m+1,\ell_{1}+\ell,\ldots,\ell_{m}+\ell,\ell_{\bar{{1}}}-\ell,\ldots,\ell_{\bar{{n}}}-\ell).

Then it is easy to see that
\tau
is a homomorphism from
I(\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n})
to
\mathbb{C}[\ell_{0}]\otimes I(\mathfrak{h}_{m|n})
.

Theorem 5.2
.

We have that

\varphi|_{Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)))}=\chi_{0,m|n}^{-1}\tau\chi_{m+1|n}.

In particular,
\varphi(Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n))))
is a subalgebra of
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{E}]\otimes Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)))
, and the following diagram is commutative:
Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)))
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{E}]\otimes Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)))
\mathbb{C}[\ell_{0}]\otimes I(\mathfrak{h}_{m|n})
I(\mathfrak{h}_{m+1|n})
\varphi
\chi_{m+1|n}
\chi_{0,m|n}
\tau

Proof.

Suppose
\lambda\in\mathcal{AW}_{m|n}
and
a\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
M(a,\lambda)=\mathcal{F}_{a}\otimes M(\lambda)
, considered as a
\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)
-module. We show that
\varphi(z)=\chi_{0,m|n}^{-1}\tau\chi_{m+1|n}(z)
for
z\in Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)))
, as an identity of endomorphisms of
M(a,\lambda)
. By Lemma
5.1
, and by the fact that the tensor product of faithful modules is faithful, the module
\bigoplus_{a\in\mathbb{Z},\lambda\in\mathcal{AW}_{m|n}}M(a,\lambda)
is faithful over
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)\otimes U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n))
. Then it will follow that
\varphi=\chi_{0,m|n}^{-1}\tau\chi_{m+1|n}(z)
.

By Corollary
4.5
, we have
M(a,\lambda)\cong M(\widetilde{\lambda})
. Thus, every
z\in Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)))
acts on
M(a,\lambda)
as
\chi_{\widetilde{\lambda}}(z)\mathrm{Id}
. Now set
\xi=\chi_{0,m|n}^{-1}\tau\chi_{m+1|n}
. Then,
\xi(z)
acts on
M(a,\lambda)
as
\chi_{a,\lambda}(\xi(z))\mathrm{Id}
, where
\chi_{a,\lambda}(\sum\mathcal{E}^{i}\otimes z^{\prime}_{i})\coloneqq\sum a^{i}\chi_{\lambda}(z^{\prime}_{i})
, because
\mathcal{E}^{i}\otimes 1
acts on
M(a,\lambda)
as
a^{i}
. Thus we just need to show
\chi_{a,\lambda}(\xi(z))=\chi_{\widetilde{\lambda}}(z)

For a polynomial
p(t_{0},\ldots,t_{\bar{n}})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{ev}_{a,\lambda+\rho_{m|n}}\tau(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{ev}_{a,\lambda+\rho_{m|n}}p(\ell_{0}+\ell+m+1,\ell_{1}+\ell,\cdots,\ell_{m}+\ell,\ell_{\bar{1}}-\ell,\ldots,\ell_{\bar{n}}-\ell)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p(a+r+m+1,\ell_{1}+r+m,\ldots,\ell_{m}+r+1,\ell_{\bar{1}}-r-1,\ldots,\ell_{\bar{n}}-r-n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{ev}_{\widetilde{\lambda}+\rho_{m+1|n}}p

(Note that here we set
\mathrm{ev}_{a,\lambda+\rho_{m|n}}(p)\coloneqq p(a,\lambda_{1}+m,...,\lambda_{m}+1,\lambda_{\bar{1}}-1,...,\lambda_{\bar{1}}-n)
for all
p
).
As a result, setting
p=\chi_{m+1|n}(z)
, and noting that
\chi_{a,\lambda}(\mathcal{E}^{i}\otimes z^{\prime})=\mathrm{ev}_{a,\lambda+\rho_{m|n}}(\chi_{0,m|n}(\mathcal{E}^{i}\otimes z^{\prime}))
, we have

\chi_{a,\lambda}(\xi(z))=\chi_{a,\lambda}\chi_{0,m|n}^{-1}\tau\chi_{m+1|n}(z)=\mathrm{ev}_{\widetilde{\lambda}+\rho_{m+1|n}}(\chi_{m+1|n}(z))=\chi_{\widetilde{\lambda}}(z).

This completes the proof.
∎

6.
Super version of Newton’s formula and Capelli-type Berezinians

We have now computed the image of the center of
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n))
under
\varphi
, and now we will describe the images of individual generators, as in
[
GR21
]
. In
[
GR21
]
, the images of the Gelfand generators are computed explicitly using a progression of identities related to Capelli generators, Capelli determinants, and an analogue of Newton’s formula for
\mathfrak{gl}(m)
(see
[
Ume98
]
). In this section, we extend this approach to the super setting and explicitly compute the images of the Gelfand generators under
\varphi
, though some care is required given that the center of
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n))
is infinitely generated. Along the way we appeal to the theory of Yangians to reprove a super-version of Newton’s formula that will be useful for us.

6.1.
Yangians for
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)

Let us recall the Yangian
Y(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n))
as defined in
[
Naz91
]
(also see
[
Gow05
]
). This is the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded associative algebra over
\mathbb{C}
with generators

\displaystyle\{T_{ij}^{(r)}:i,j\in I,r\geq 1\}

and defining relations

\displaystyle[T_{ij}^{(r)},T_{kl}^{(s)}]=(-1)^{|i||j|+|i||k|+|j||k|}\sum_{p=0}^{\min(r,s)-1}(T_{kj}^{(p)}T_{il}^{(r+s-1-p)}-T_{kj}^{(r+s-1-p)}T_{il}^{(p)}).

The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading is given by
|T_{ij}^{(r)}|=|i|+|j|
.
We define the formal power series

T_{ij}(u)\coloneqq\delta_{ij}+T_{ij}^{(1)}u^{-1}+T_{ij}^{(2)}u^{-2}+\cdots

and the matrix
T(u)\coloneqq[T_{ij}(u)]\in\mathrm{Mat}_{m|n}(Y(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)))
. We can also identify
T(u)
with

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in I}T_{ij}(u)\otimes e_{ij}(-1)^{|j|(|i|+1)}.

Note that some authors drop the sign term in the definition of
T(u)
(e.g., Equation 2.12 in
[
Naz20
]
). As a result, these authors use a different sign convention for the comultiplication
\Delta
defined below. However, this does not matter for our purposes because we never need to use the Hopf algebra structure of the Yangian.

In general, we can identify an operator
\sum A_{ij}\otimes e_{ij}(-1)^{|j|(|i|+1)}
in
Y(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n))[\![u^{-1}]\!]\otimes\mathrm{End}\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
with the matrix
[A_{ij}]
. The extra signs are inserted to let the product of two matrices be calculated in the usual way. The Yangian is a Hopf algebra with comultiplication

\displaystyle\Delta:T_{ij}(u)\mapsto\sum_{k\in I}T_{ik}(u)\otimes T_{kj}(u),

antipode
S:T(u)\mapsto T^{-1}(u)
, and counit
T(u)\mapsto 1
.

We define the power series with coefficients in the Yangian
Y(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n))
of
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
given by

\displaystyle Z_{h}(u)\coloneqq 1+\mathrm{str}\left(\frac{T(u+h)-T(u)}{h}\cdot T^{-1}(u)\right)

for any
h\in\mathbb{C}
, where
\mathrm{str}(A)
denotes the supertrace of a matrix
A
. The
h
here has no relation to the
h
that appears in
[
Naz91
]
. Define

(6.1)

\displaystyle Z(u)\coloneqq\lim_{h\to m-n}Z_{h}(u).

For
m\neq n
,
Z(u)=Z_{m-n}(u)
while for
m=n
we have

\displaystyle Z(u)=1+\mathrm{str}\left(\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u}T(u)\right)T^{-1}(u)\right).

Note that the same series
Z(u)
was defined in
[
Naz91
]
; however it was not written in terms of this limit construction. The coefficients of the series
Z(u)
are known to generate the center of
Y(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n))
(see
[
Naz91
]
and
[
Gow07
]
).

Let
B(u)
be the quantum Berezinian of
T(u)
, defined by

\displaystyle B(u)\coloneqq\mathrm{Det}[T_{ij}(u+(m-n-j))]_{i,j=1,\ldots m}\times\mathrm{Det}[T_{ij}^{*}(u-(m+n-j+1))]_{i,j=m+1,\ldots,m+n},

where
[T_{ij}^{*}(u)]=T^{*}(u)=(T^{-1}(u))^{st}
and
A^{st}=[A_{ji}(-1)^{|i|(|j|+1)}]
denotes matrix supertransposition. By the determinant of a matrix
X=[X_{ab}]_{a,b=1,\ldots,N}
with noncommuting entries, we mean the sum

\mathrm{Det}X\coloneqq\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)X_{\sigma(1),1}\cdots X_{\sigma(N),N}.

It is shown in
[
Naz91
]
that

(6.2)

\displaystyle Z(u)=\frac{B(u+1)}{B(u)},

which could be referred to as a “quantum Liouville formula”. Additionally, there is a projection homomorphism
\pi_{m|n}:Y(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n))\to U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n))
given by

(6.3)

\displaystyle T_{ij}(u)\mapsto\delta_{ij}+e_{ij}(-1)^{|i|}u^{-1}.

6.2.
Super Newton’s formula

We can use these tools from Yangians to generalize the Newton’s formula for
\mathfrak{gl}(m)
(see
[
Ume98
]
and Theorem 7.1.3 in
[
Mol07
]
) to
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
. Let

\mathbf{E}\coloneqq((-1)^{|i|}e_{ij})_{i,j\in I}

and define

\displaystyle C_{m|n}(T)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Det}[\mathbf{E}_{ij}-T-i+1]_{i,j=1,\ldots,m}\times\mathrm{Det}[(\mathbf{E}_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}-T-m+i)^{*}]_{i,j=1,\ldots,n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{m}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\left(\mathbf{E}-T\right)_{\sigma(1),1}\cdots(\mathbf{E}-T-m+1)_{\sigma(m),m}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\tau\in S_{n}}\mathrm{sgn}(\tau)((\mathbf{E}-T-m+1)^{-1})^{st}_{m+\tau(1),m+1}\cdots((\mathbf{E}-T-(m-n))^{-1})^{st}_{m+\tau(n),n}

to be the
Capelli Berezinian
of
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
.
It follows from the definitions that
C_{m|n}(T)=Q(T)\pi_{m|n}(B(-T-(m-n)+1))
for the rational function
Q(T)
given by

(6.4)

\displaystyle Q(T)=\begin{cases}T(T+1)\cdots(T+(m-n-1))&\text{for $m>n$}\\
1&\text{for $m=n$}\\
(T-(n-m))^{-1}(T-(n-m-1))^{-1}\cdots(T-1)^{-1}&\text{for $n>m$}.\end{cases}

It is well known that the coefficients of
\pi_{m|n}(B(T))
generate
Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)))
. Hence the coefficients of
C_{m|n}(T)
also generate
Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)))
. We call these coefficients the
Capelli generators
of
Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)))
. Since the coefficients of
C_{m|n}(T)
are central, we can consider
C_{m|n}
as a function from the algebra of Laurent series in
T
with coefficients in
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n))
to itself.
The Newton’s formulas proved in
[
Ume98
]
and Theorem 7.1.3 of
[
Mol07
]
relate the Capelli Berezinian (called the Capelli determinant when
n=0
) to the Gelfand invariants
G_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m)}
. We reprove analogs of these results to
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
using the same method as
[
Mol07
]
.

The Gelfand invariants of
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
are given by

G_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}\coloneqq\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\in\hat{I}}(-1)^{|i_{2}|+\cdots+|i_{k}|}e_{i_{1}i_{2}}\otimes e_{i_{2}i_{3}}\otimes\cdots e_{i_{k}i_{1}}

for
k\geq 1
, where the sum ranges over all
k
-tuples
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})
with terms in
I
. It is known that the Gelfand invariants
G_{k}
for
k=0,1,\ldots
generate
Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)))
(see for example
[
Rao21
]
). We can also write
G_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}=\mathrm{str}\mathbf{E}^{k}
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{str}\mathbf{E}^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}(\mathbf{E}^{k})_{ii}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}\sum_{i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}\in I}\mathbf{E}_{ii_{2}}\mathbf{E}_{i_{2}i_{3}}\cdots\mathbf{E}_{i_{k}i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\in I}(-1)^{|i_{1}|+(|i_{1}|+\cdots+|i_{k-1}|+|i_{k}|)}e_{i_{1}i_{2}}e_{i_{2}i_{3}}\cdots e_{i_{k}i_{1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\in I}(-1)^{|i_{2}|+\cdots+|i_{k}|}e_{i_{1}i_{2}}e_{i_{2}i_{3}}\cdots e_{i_{k}i_{1}}

\displaystyle=G_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}.

Theorem 6.2.1

(Newton’s Formulas for
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
)
.

We have

\displaystyle\frac{C_{m|n}(T-(m-n))}{C_{m|n}(T-(m-n)+1)}=1-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}G_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}T^{-1-k}.

Proof.

Since
C_{m|n}(T)=Q(T)\pi_{m|n}(B(-T-(m-n)+1))
, (
6.4
) and that
G_{0}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}=m-n
yields that the identity is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{\pi_{m|n}(B(-T+1))}{\pi_{m|n}(B(-T))}=1+\frac{1}{-T+(m-n)}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{str}\mathbf{E}^{k}T^{-k}.

Using (
6.1
) and (
6.3
), we get that

\displaystyle\pi_{m|n}(Z(-u))

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\to m-n}1+\mathrm{str}\left(\frac{\mathbf{E}u^{-1}-\mathbf{E}(u-h)^{-1}}{h}(1-\mathbf{E}u^{-1})^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\to{m-n}}1-\frac{1}{u(u-h)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{str}\mathbf{E}^{k+1}u^{-k}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{u-(m-n)}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{str}\mathbf{E}^{k}u^{-k}.

Then the identity follows from (
6.2
).
∎

6.3.
Images of Central Elements

Next, we find the image of the Capelli Berezinian under the Harish-Chandra homomorphism, which combined with Theorem
5.2
, will let us find the image of the Capelli Berezinian under
\varphi
.

Proposition 6.3.1
.

We have

\displaystyle\chi_{m|n}(C_{m|n}(-T))=(T+\ell_{1}-m)\cdots(T+\ell_{m}-m)(T-\ell_{\bar{1}}-m)^{-1}\cdots(T-\ell_{\bar{n}}-m)^{-1}.

Proof.

Note
C_{m|n}(T)
is the product of two parts, one part summing over
\sigma\in S_{m}
, and one summing over
\tau\in S_{n}
. However, note that in both parts, only the summand corresponding to the identity permutation has a nonzero image under
\chi_{m|n}
, because in both
\mathbf{E}+T
and

\displaystyle(\mathbf{E}+T)^{-1}=T^{-1}-\mathbf{E}T^{-2}+\mathbf{E}^{2}T^{-3}-\cdots,

the only elements that stabilize the weight spaces of a Verma module
M(\lambda)
are along the diagonal. To see this, define
r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,b)=\mathbf{E}^{k}_{ab}
. Then

r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,b)=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1}\in I}(-1)^{|i_{1}|+\cdots+|i_{k-1}|}e_{ai_{1}}\otimes e_{i_{1}i_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{k-1}b}.

and it follows that acting by
r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,b)
raises a weight by
\delta_{a}-\delta_{b}
, hence stabilizes the weight spaces if and only if
a=b
. Then, the product of the two terms gives the result.
∎

Define
C_{\varphi}(T)
via the identity
C_{\varphi}(\varphi(T))=\varphi(C_{m+1|n}(T))
. Since the coefficients of the Capelli determinant are central and
\varphi
maps
Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)))
to
Z(\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)\otimes U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)))
, the domain of
C_{\varphi}(T)
consists of Laurent series in
\varphi(T)
with coefficients in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)\otimes U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n))
.
For convenience we will write
T
for
\varphi(T)
.

Theorem 6.3.2
.

We have

\displaystyle C_{\varphi}(T+R)=(\mathcal{E}-T)C_{m|n}(T+1).

Consequently,
C_{\varphi}(\mathcal{E}+R)=0
.

Proof.

The identity is equivalent to

\displaystyle\varphi(C_{m+1}(-T))=(\mathcal{E}+T+R)C_{m|n}(-T-R+1).

To prove this, we use Proposition
6.3.1
, the corresponding version for
m+1|n
, Theorem
5.2
, and that
\chi_{0,n}(R)=\ell
.
∎

Define

R_{2}=(\mathcal{E}+m-n)\otimes 1,

and

\displaystyle A_{k}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g=0}^{k-s}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}R_{2}^{k-s-g}.

We can combine the previous theorem with the Newton’s formula to find the images of the Gelfand invariants under
\varphi
.

Theorem 6.3.3
.

The following formula holds for all positive integers
k
:

\displaystyle\varphi(G_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)})

\displaystyle=A_{k}(1)(\mathcal{E}\otimes 1)+(m+1-n)R^{k}+\sum_{g=0}^{k-1}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}\left(1\otimes G_{k-g}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\sum_{s=2}^{k}\left(1\otimes G_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}\right)A_{k}(s).

Proof.

Theorem
6.3.2
implies that

(6.5)

\displaystyle\frac{C_{\varphi}(T-(m-n)-1)}{C_{\varphi}(T-(m-n))}=\left(1-\frac{1}{T-R-R_{2}}\right)\frac{C_{m|n}(T-R-(m-n))}{C_{m|n}(T-R-(m-n)+1)}.

Applying
\varphi
to the Newton’s formula for
\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)
gives

\displaystyle\frac{C_{\varphi}(T-(m-n)-1)}{C_{\varphi}(T-(m-n))}

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varphi(G_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)})T^{-1-k}

\displaystyle=1-(m-n+1)T^{-1}-T^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\varphi(G_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)})T^{-k}.

Analogously we can express the right hand side of (
6.3.2
) as a power series in
T^{-1}
. We complete the proof by comparing and computing the coefficients of

on both sides.
∎

7.
Kernel of
\varphi_{R_{1}}

In this section, we find the kernel of the map
\varphi_{R_{1}}
. For this section, we write
\varphi=\varphi_{R_{1}}
and
G_{1}=G_{1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}
for convenience.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
(G_{1})
be the two sided ideal in
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n))
generated by
G_{1}
. Then the kernel of
\varphi
is
(G_{1})
.

Proof.

Recall from Proposition
3.3
that
\varphi=\pi^{g}\varphi^{s}
. The kernel of
\pi^{g}
is clearly
(G_{1})
, so we just need to show that
\varphi^{s}
is injective. We construct a left inverse for
\varphi^{s}
, considered as a
Lie superalgebra
homomorphism. In particular, this means we view
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n))
and
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n))
as Lie algebras with bracket given by
[x,y]=xy-(-1)^{|x||y|}yx
.

Let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}(m|n)
be the subalgebra of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(m|n)
generated by
\{t_{a}\partial_{b}:a,b\in\hat{I},a\neq b\}\cup\{t_{0}\partial_{0}-(-1)^{|a|}t_{a}\partial_{a}:a\in\hat{I}\}
.
Consider the homomorphism
1\otimes\varepsilon:\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}(m|n)\otimes U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n))\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}(m|n)
, where
\varepsilon
is the counit map of
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n))
. In particular
(1\otimes\varepsilon)(p\otimes x)=0
for
p\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}(m|n)
and
x\in\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
and
(1\otimes\varepsilon)(p\otimes 1)=p
.
Consider the homomorphism
(1\otimes\varepsilon)\circ\varphi^{s}:U(\mathfrak{sl}(m+1|n))\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}(m|n)
. Corollary
3.2
gives us that this homomorphism is generated by

(for
a\neq b
)

\displaystyle e_{ab}

\displaystyle\mapsto t_{a}\partial_{b}

\displaystyle e_{00}-(-1)^{|a|}e_{aa}

\displaystyle\mapsto t_{0}\partial_{0}-(-1)^{|a|}t_{a}\partial_{a}.

We claim that this homomorphism has a left inverse
\psi
. Letting
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime\prime}(m|n)
be the algebra generated by
\{t_{a}\partial_{b}:a,b\in\hat{I}\}
, we first define
\psi^{\prime}:\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime\prime}(m|n)\to U(\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n))
and then its restriction
\psi:\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}(m|n)\to U(\mathfrak{sl}(m+1|n))
will be a left inverse to
(1\otimes\varepsilon)\circ\varphi^{s}
. The map
\psi^{\prime}
is generated by

\displaystyle t_{a}\partial_{b}

\displaystyle\mapsto e_{ab}

for
a,b\in\hat{I}
. To check
\psi
is a homomorphism, we check
\psi^{\prime}([t_{a}\partial_{b},t_{c}\partial_{d}])=[e_{ab},e_{cd}]
for all
a,b,c,d\in\hat{I}
.

If
a\neq d,b\neq c
, then we have

\displaystyle[t_{a}\partial_{b},t_{c}\partial_{d}]

\displaystyle=t_{a}\partial_{b}t_{c}\partial_{d}-(-1)^{(|a|+|b|)(|c|+|d|)}t_{c}\partial_{d}t_{a}\partial_{b}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|b||c|}t_{a}t_{c}\partial_{b}\partial_{d}-(-1)^{(|a||c|+|b||c|+|a||c|)}t_{c}t_{a}\partial_{d}\partial_{b}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|b||c|}t_{a}t_{c}\partial_{b}t_{d}-(-1)^{|b||c|}t_{a}t_{c}\partial_{b}t_{d}=0.

Now suppose
a\neq d
,
b=c
. Note
[e_{ab},e_{bd}]=e_{ad}
. Thus, we show
[t_{a}\partial_{b},t_{b}\partial_{d}]=t_{a}\partial_{d}
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle[t_{a}\partial_{b},t_{b}\partial_{d}]

\displaystyle=t_{a}\partial_{b}t_{b}\partial_{d}-(-1)^{(|a|+|b|)(|b|+|d|)}t_{b}\partial_{d}t_{a}\partial_{b}

\displaystyle=t_{a}\partial_{b}t_{b}\partial_{d}-(-1)^{|b|}t_{a}t_{b}\partial_{b}\partial_{d}

\displaystyle=t_{a}\partial_{d}.

If
a=d,b\neq c
, reversing the order gives the previous case.
If
a=d,b=c
, then
[e_{ab},e_{ba}]=e_{aa}-(-1)^{|a|+|b|}e_{bb}
. We also have

\displaystyle[t_{a}\partial_{b},t_{b}\partial_{a}]

\displaystyle=t_{a}\partial_{b}t_{b}\partial_{a}-(-1)^{|a|+|b|}t_{b}\partial_{a}t_{a}\partial_{b}

\displaystyle=t_{a}(1+(-1)^{|b|}t_{b}\partial_{b})\partial_{a}-(-1)^{|a|+|b|}t_{b}\partial_{a}t_{a}\partial_{b}

\displaystyle=t_{a}\partial_{a}-(-1)^{|a|+|b|}t_{b}\partial_{b}(\partial_{a}t_{a}-(-1)^{|a|}t_{a}\partial_{a})

\displaystyle=t_{a}\partial_{a}-(-1)^{|a|+|b|}t_{b}\partial_{b}.

Thus
\psi^{\prime}
, hence
\psi
is a homomorphism. It is easily checked that
\psi\circ(1\otimes\varepsilon)\circ\varphi^{s}=\mathrm{Id}
, so
\varphi^{s}
is injective. This completes the proof.
∎

Appendix A
Proof of Theorem
3.1

In this appendix we provide the proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Clearly
\varphi
maps even elements to even elements and odd elements to odd elements. Thus, we only need to check that for basis elements
x
and
y
, we have

(A.1)

\displaystyle[\varphi(x),\varphi(y)]=\varphi([x,y]).

We have multiple cases to check. Here
a,b,c,d
will always denote elements of
I
. Before we start verifying the cases, note that since
R
is central, all brackets with it are zero.

Case 1:

[e_{ab},e_{ba}]
.

If
a=b
then both brackets in (
A.1
) are obviously zero.

If
a\neq b
then
[e_{ab},e_{ba}]=e_{aa}-(-1)^{|a|+|b|}e_{bb}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\varphi([e_{ab},e_{ba}])

\displaystyle=\Big(t_{a}\partial_{a}-(-1)^{|a|+|b|}t_{b}\partial_{b}\Big)\otimes 1+1\otimes\Big(e_{aa}-(-1)^{|a|+|b|}e_{bb}\Big)

Now we show

[t_{a}\partial_{b},t_{b}\partial_{a}]=t_{a}\partial_{a}-(-1)^{|a|+|b|}t_{b}\partial_{b}

for
a\neq b
.
The left hand side is

(A.2)

\displaystyle t_{a}\partial_{b}t_{b}\partial_{a}-(-1)^{|a||b|}t_{b}\partial_{a}t_{a}\partial_{b}.

Now
\partial_{b}t_{b}=1+(-1)^{|b|}t_{b}\partial_{b}
, so
t_{a}\partial_{b}t_{b}\partial_{a}=t_{a}\partial_{a}+(-1)^{|b|}t_{a}t_{b}\partial_{b}\partial_{a}
. Using supercommutativity, this becomes
t_{a}\partial_{a}+(-1)^{|b|}t_{a}\partial_{a}t_{b}\partial_{b}
. A similar process with the second term gives us
t_{b}\partial_{a}t_{a}\partial_{b}=t_{b}\partial_{b}+(-1)^{|a|}t_{a}\partial_{a}t_{b}\partial_{b}
. Plugging these back into (
A.2
), we get

\displaystyle[t_{a}\partial_{b},t_{b}\partial_{a}]

\displaystyle=t_{a}\partial_{a}+(-1)^{|b|}t_{a}\partial_{a}t_{b}\partial_{b}-(-1)^{|a|+|b|}t_{b}\partial_{b}-(-1)^{|b|}t_{a}\partial_{a}t_{b}\partial_{b}

\displaystyle=t_{a}\partial_{a}-(-1)^{|a|+|b|}t_{b}\partial_{b}.

Thus,

\displaystyle[\varphi(e_{ab}),\varphi(e_{ba})]

\displaystyle=[t_{a}\partial_{b}\otimes 1+1\otimes e_{ab},t_{b}\partial_{a}\otimes 1+1\otimes e_{ba}]

\displaystyle=\Big(t_{a}\partial_{a}-(-1)^{|a|+|b|}t_{b}\partial_{b}\Big)\otimes 1+1\otimes\Big(e_{aa}-(-1)^{|a|+|b|}e_{bb}\Big)

\displaystyle=\varphi([e_{ab},e_{ba}]),

verifying (
A.1
).

Case 2:

[e_{ab},e_{bc}]
,
a\neq c

First, we show

[t_{a}\partial_{b},t_{b}\partial_{c}]=t_{a}\partial_{c}.

The left hand side is

(A.3)

\displaystyle t_{a}\partial_{b}t_{b}\partial_{c}-(-1)^{(|a|+|b|)(|b|+|c|)}t_{b}\partial_{c}t_{a}\partial_{b}.

We have

\displaystyle t_{a}\partial_{b}t_{b}\partial_{c}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|b||c|}t_{a}\partial_{b}\partial_{c}t_{b}

\displaystyle=t_{a}\partial_{c}\partial_{b}t_{b}.

Also,

\displaystyle t_{b}\partial_{c}t_{a}\partial_{b}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|b||c|}\partial_{c}t_{b}t_{a}\partial_{b}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|b||c|+|b||a|}\partial_{c}t_{a}t_{b}\partial_{b}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|b||c|+|b||a|+|a||c|}t_{a}\partial_{c}t_{b}\partial_{b}.

Thus
(-1)^{(|a|+|b|)(|b|+|c|)}t_{b}\partial_{c}t_{a}\partial_{b}=(-1)^{|b|^{2}}t_{a}\partial_{c}t_{b}\partial_{b}=(-1)^{|b|}t_{a}\partial_{c}t_{b}\partial_{c}
. Plugging in to (
A.3
), we get

\displaystyle[t_{a}\partial_{b},t_{b}\partial_{c}]=t_{a}\partial_{c}(\partial_{b}t_{b}-(-1)^{|b|}t_{b}\partial_{b})=t_{a}\partial_{c}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle[\varphi(e_{ab}),\varphi(e_{bc})]

\displaystyle=[t_{a}\partial_{b}\otimes 1+1\otimes e_{ab},t_{b}\partial_{c}\otimes 1+1\otimes e_{bc}]

\displaystyle=t_{a}\partial_{c}+1\otimes e_{ac}

\displaystyle=\varphi(e_{ac})=\varphi([e_{ab},e_{bc}]),

as desired.

Case 3:

[e_{ab},e_{ca}]
,
c\neq b

Swapping the order of the two terms gives us Case 2. Note that all signs picked up in the process get canceled.

,
c\neq b

a\neq d

The left hand side is

\displaystyle=0=\varphi([e_{ab},e_{00}]).

Case 8:

[e_{a0},e_{b0}]

We have

\displaystyle[\varphi(e_{a0}),\varphi(e_{b0})]

\displaystyle=\left[t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes 1-\sum_{j}(-1)^{|a||j|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{aj},t_{b}\partial_{0}\otimes 1-\sum_{j}(-1)^{|b||j|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{bj}\right].

We evaluate each sub-bracket one by one. It is easily checked that
[t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes 1,t_{b}\partial_{0}\otimes 1]=0
. We have

\displaystyle\left[t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes 1,\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{bj}\right]

\displaystyle=t_{a}\partial_{0}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{bj}-(-1)^{|a||b|+(|b|+|j|)|a|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes e_{bj}

\displaystyle=t_{a}t_{j}\left(\partial_{0}\frac{1}{t_{0}}\right)\otimes e_{bj}-t_{a}t_{j}\left(\frac{1}{t_{0}}\partial_{0}\right)\otimes e_{bj}.

Similar to the identity
\partial_{0}t_{0}-t_{0}\partial_{0}=1
, we have
\partial_{0}\frac{1}{t_{0}}-\frac{1}{t_{0}}\partial_{0}=-\frac{1}{t_{0}^{2}}
, so the bracket evaluates to
-t_{a}t_{j}
. Thus

\left[t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes 1,-\sum_{j}(-1)^{|b||j|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{bj}\right]=\sum_{j}(-1)^{|b||j|}\frac{t_{a}t_{j}}{t_{0}^{2}}\otimes e_{bj}.

Next we evaluate

\left[\frac{t_{i}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{ai},\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{bj}\right]

for
i,j\in I
. If
i\neq b
then
e_{ai}e_{bj}=0
. If
j\neq a
then
e_{bj}e_{ai}=0
. Thus

\displaystyle\left[\frac{t_{i}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{ai},\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{bj}\right]

\displaystyle=\delta_{ib}(-1)^{|j|(|a|+|b|)}\frac{t_{b}t_{j}}{t_{0}^{2}}\otimes e_{aj}-\delta_{aj}(-1)^{|a|||b|+(|b|+|a)|i|}\frac{t_{a}t_{i}}{t_{0}^{2}}\otimes e_{bi}.

Thus

\displaystyle\left[-\sum_{i}(-1)^{|a||i|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{ai},-\sum_{j}(-1)^{|b||j|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{bj}\right]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|a||b|}\sum_{j}(-1)^{|a||j|}\frac{t_{b}t_{j}}{t_{0}^{2}}\otimes e_{aj}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\sum_{j}(-1)^{|b||j|}\frac{t_{a}t_{j}}{t_{0}^{2}}\otimes e_{bj}.

Finally,

\displaystyle\left[\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{aj},t_{b}\partial_{0}\otimes 1\right]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{(|a|+|j|)|b|+|j||b|}t_{b}t_{j}\left(\frac{1}{t_{0}}\partial_{0}\right)\otimes e_{aj}-(-1)^{|a||b|}t_{b}t_{j}\left(\partial_{0}\frac{1}{t_{0}}\right)\otimes e_{aj}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|a||b|}\frac{t_{b}t_{j}}{t_{0}^{2}}\otimes e_{aj}.

Thus

\left[\sum_{j}(-1)^{|a||j|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{aj},t_{b}\partial_{0}\otimes 1\right]=-(-1)^{|a||b|}\sum_{j}(-1)^{|a||j|}\frac{t_{b}t_{j}}{t_{0}^{2}}\otimes e_{aj}.

Adding everything back together, we see that everything cancels, so

[\varphi(e_{a0}),\varphi(e_{b0})]=0=\varphi([e_{a0,e_{b0}}]).

Case 9:

[e_{a0},e_{0b}]

Now

\displaystyle[\varphi(e_{a0}),\varphi(e_{0b})]

\displaystyle=\left[t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes 1-\sum_{j}(-1)^{|a||j|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{aj},t_{0}\partial_{b}\otimes 1\right]

\displaystyle=[t_{a}\partial_{0},t_{0}\partial_{b}]\otimes 1-\sum_{j}(-1)^{|a||j|}\left[\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{aj},t_{0}\partial_{b}\otimes 1\right].

But

\displaystyle\left[\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{aj},t_{0}\partial_{b}\otimes 1\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{aj}\right)(t_{0}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)-(-1)^{|a||b|}(t_{0}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)\left(\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{aj}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{(|a|+|j|)|b|}\left(\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}t_{0}\partial_{b}\right)\otimes e_{aj}-(-1)^{|a||b|}(t_{0}\partial_{b})(t_{j}/t_{0})\otimes e_{aj}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|a||b|}[(-1)^{|j||b|}(t_{j}\partial_{b})-(\partial_{b}t_{j})]\otimes e_{aj}

If
j=b
the expression in the brackets is

-(\partial_{b}t_{b}-(-1)^{|b|}t_{b}\partial_{b})=-1.

If
j\neq b
the expression in the brackets is

(-1)^{|j||b|}(t_{j}\partial_{b})-(\partial_{b}t_{j})=(-1)^{|j||b|}(t_{j}\partial_{b})-(-1)^{|j||b|}(t_{j}\partial_{b})=0.

Plugging these back into the original expression, we get

\displaystyle[\varphi(e_{a0}),\varphi(e_{0b})]

\displaystyle=[t_{a}\partial_{0},t_{0}\partial_{b}]\otimes 1+1\otimes e_{ab}.

If
a\neq b
, from Case 2 we have
[t_{a}\partial_{0},t_{0}\partial_{b}]=t_{a}\partial_{b}
, so

[\varphi(e_{a0}),\varphi(e_{0b})]=t_{a}\partial_{b}\otimes 1+1\otimes e_{ab}=\varphi([e_{a0},e_{0b}]).

If
a=b
, we have

\displaystyle[t_{a}\partial_{0},t_{0}\partial_{a}]

\displaystyle=t_{a}\partial_{0}t_{0}\partial_{a}-(-1)^{|a|}t_{0}\partial_{a}t_{a}\partial_{0}

\displaystyle=\partial_{0}t_{0}t_{a}\partial_{a}-(-1)^{|a|}t_{0}\partial_{0}\partial_{a}t_{a}

\displaystyle=\partial_{0}t_{0}t_{a}\partial_{a}-(-1)^{|a|}t_{0}\partial_{0}(1+(-1)^{|a|}t_{a}\partial_{a})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|a|}t_{0}\partial_{0}+(\partial_{0}t_{0}-t_{0}\partial_{0})t_{a}\partial_{a}

\displaystyle=t_{a}\partial_{a}-(-1)^{|a|}t_{0}\partial_{0}.

Thus

\displaystyle[\varphi(e_{a0}),\varphi(e_{0a})]=t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes 1-(-1)^{|a|}t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1+1\otimes e_{aa}=\varphi(e_{aa}-(-1)^{|a|}e_{00})=\varphi([e_{a0},e_{0a}]).

Case 10:

[e_{a0},e_{00}]

Since
R
is central,

[\varphi(e_{a0}),\varphi(e_{00})]=\left[t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes 1-\sum_{j}(-1)^{|a||j|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{aj},t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1\right]

Now,

[t_{a}\partial_{0},t_{0}\partial_{0}]=(t_{a}\partial_{0})(t_{0}\partial_{0})-(t_{0}\partial_{0})(t_{a}\partial_{0})=t_{a}(\partial_{0}t_{0}-t_{0}\partial_{0})\partial_{0}=t_{a}\partial_{0}.

Also,

\left[\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{aj},t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1\right]=t_{j}(\partial_{0}t_{0}-t_{0}\partial_{0})\frac{1}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{aj}=\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{aj}.

Thus

[\varphi(e_{a0}),\varphi(e_{00})]=t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes 1-\sum_{j}(-1)^{|a||j|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{aj}=\varphi([e_{a0},e_{00}]).

Case 11:

[e_{0a},e_{0b}]

We have

\varphi([e_{0a},e_{0b}])=\varphi(0)=0.

Also

\displaystyle[\varphi(e_{0a}),\varphi(e_{0b})]

\displaystyle=[t_{0}\partial_{a}\otimes 1,t_{0}\partial_{b}\otimes 1]

\displaystyle=t_{0}^{2}(\partial_{a}\partial_{b}-(-1)^{|a||b|}\partial_{b}\partial_{a})\otimes 1

\displaystyle=0

Case 12:

[e_{0b},e_{00}]

We have
[e_{0b},e_{00}]=-e_{0b}
, so
\varphi([e_{0b},e_{00}])=-t_{0}\partial_{b}\otimes 1
.
Since
R
is central, we also have

\displaystyle[\varphi(e_{0b}),\varphi(e_{00})]

\displaystyle=[t_{0}\partial_{b}\otimes 1,t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1]

\displaystyle=(-t_{0}\partial_{0}t_{0}\partial_{b}+t_{0}\partial_{b}t_{0}\partial_{0})\otimes 1

\displaystyle=-(t_{0}(\partial_{0}t_{0}-t_{0}\partial_{0})\partial_{b})\otimes 1

\displaystyle=-t_{0}\partial_{b}\otimes 1=\varphi([e_{0b},e_{00}]).

Case 13:

[e_{0c},e_{ab}]
,
c\neq a

We have
[e_{0c},e_{ab}]=0
and

\displaystyle[\varphi(e_{0c}),\varphi(e_{ab})]

\displaystyle=[t_{0}\partial_{c}\otimes 1,t_{a}\partial_{b}\otimes 1+1\otimes e_{ab}+\delta_{ab}(-1)^{|a||b|}R]

\displaystyle=[t_{0}\partial_{c},t_{a}\partial_{b}]\otimes 1.

Now we have

\displaystyle[t_{0}\partial_{c},t_{a}\partial_{b}]

\displaystyle=t_{0}\partial_{c}t_{a}\partial_{b}-(-1)^{|c|(|a|+|b|)}t_{a}\partial_{b}t_{0}\partial_{c}

\displaystyle=t_{0}\partial_{c}t_{a}\partial_{b}-(-1)^{|c|(|a|+|b|)+|c|(|a|+|b|)}t_{0}\partial_{c}t_{a}\partial_{b}

\displaystyle=0.

Thus
[\varphi(e_{0c}),\varphi(e_{ab})]=0=\varphi([e_{0c},e_{ab}])
.

Case 14:

[e_{ab},e_{c0}]
,
b\neq c

We have
[e_{ab},e_{c0}]=0
. Also,

\displaystyle[\varphi(e_{ab}),\varphi(e_{c0})]

\displaystyle=\left[t_{a}\partial_{b}\otimes 1+1\otimes e_{ab}+\delta_{ab}(-1)^{|a||b|}R,t_{c}\partial_{0}\otimes 1-\sum_{j}(-1)^{|c||j|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{cj}\right]

\displaystyle=[t_{a}\partial_{b},t_{c}\partial_{0}]\otimes 1-\sum_{j}(-1)^{|c||j|}\left[t_{a}\partial_{b}\otimes 1,\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{cj}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt-\sum_{j}(-1)^{|c||j|}\left[1\otimes e_{ab},\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{cj}\right].

Now we evaluate the brackets. We have

\displaystyle[t_{a}\partial_{b},t_{c}\partial_{0}]

\displaystyle=t_{a}\partial_{b}t_{c}\partial_{0}-(-1)^{(|a|+|b|)|c|}t_{c}\partial_{0}t_{a}\partial_{b}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{(|a|+|b|)|c|}t_{c}\partial_{0}t_{a}\partial_{b}-(-1)^{(|a|+|b|)|c|}t_{c}\partial_{0}t_{a}\partial_{b}

\displaystyle=0.

Next, we have

\displaystyle\left[t_{a}\partial_{b}\otimes 1,\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{cj}\right]

\displaystyle=t_{a}\partial_{b}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{cj}-(-1)^{(|a|+|b|)|c|}\left(\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{cj}\right)(t_{a}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t_{0}}t_{a}\partial_{b}t_{j}\otimes e_{cj}-(-1)^{(|a|+|b|)|j|}\frac{1}{t_{0}}t_{j}t_{a}\partial_{b}\otimes 1.

If
j\neq b
, then this becomes

\frac{1}{t_{0}}t_{a}\partial_{b}t_{j}\otimes e_{cj}-\frac{1}{t_{0}}t_{a}\partial_{b}t_{j}\otimes e_{cj}=0.

If
j=b
this becomes

\frac{t_{a}}{t_{0}}(\partial_{b}t_{b}-(-1)^{|b|}t_{b}\partial_{b})\otimes e_{cb}=\frac{t_{a}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{cb}.

Finally, we have

\displaystyle\left[1\otimes e_{ab},\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{cj}\right]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{(|a|+|b|)|j|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes(e_{ab}e_{cj})-(-1)^{(|a|+|b|)|c|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{cj}e_{ab}

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{(|a|+|b|)|c|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{cj}e_{ab}

Now
e_{cj}e_{ab}
is nonzero if and only if
j=a
. When
j=a
, the above expression becomes

\displaystyle(-1)^{(|a|+|b|)|c|}\frac{t_{a}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{cb}.

Plugging these back into the orginal expression, we get

\displaystyle[\varphi(e_{ab}),\varphi(e_{c0})]=-(-1)^{|c||b|}\frac{t_{a}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{cj}+(-1)^{|c||a|+(|a|+|b|)|c|}\frac{t_{a}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{cb}=0=\varphi([e_{ab},e_{c0}]).

This completes the verification and finishes the proof.
∎

Appendix B
Formulas for the images of certain elements under
\varphi

For the following section, we will write these Gelfand invariants in terms of the following special elements of
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n))
. Set
r_{0}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,b)=\delta_{ab}(-1)^{|a||b|}
. For
k\geq 0
and
a,b\in I
, let

(B.1)

\displaystyle r_{k+1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,b)=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\in I}(-1)^{|i_{1}|+\cdots+|i_{k}|}e_{ai_{1}}\otimes e_{i_{1}i_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{k}b}.

Then we have

G_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}=\sum_{i\in I}r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(i,i)

for
k\geq 1
. We compute the images of the elements
r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,b)
for all
k,a,b
. The proofs of these formulas are not dependent on the results in previous sections of this paper. In this way, we can obtain an alternative (computational) proof of Theorem
6.3.3
.

First, notice the following identity:

r_{k+1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(a,b)=\sum_{i\in\hat{I}}(-1)^{|i|}r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(a,i)e_{ib}.

Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\hat{I}}(-1)^{|i|}r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(a,i)e_{ib}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\hat{I}}\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1}\in\hat{I}}(-1)^{|i_{1}|+\cdots+|i_{k-1}|+|i|}e_{ai_{1}}\cdots e_{i_{k-1}i}e_{ib}

\displaystyle=r_{k+1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(a,b).

Define

f_{s}(a,b)=\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}t_{a}\partial_{i}\otimes r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(i,b).

Also note that we have
f_{1}(a,b)=t_{a}\partial_{b}
.

To simplify the statement of the following theorem, we first introduce some more notation. Let

\displaystyle A_{k}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g=0}^{k-s}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}R_{2}^{k-s-g}

\displaystyle B(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|i|(1+|j|)}\partial_{i}t_{j}\otimes r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(i,j)

\displaystyle D_{k}(a,b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g=0}^{k}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}\left(1\otimes r_{k-g}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,b)\right)

\displaystyle E_{k}(a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g=0}^{k-1}\left(\binom{k}{g}R^{g}\sum_{j>0}(-1)^{|a||j|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes r_{k-g}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,j)\right)

\displaystyle F_{k}(a,b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{k}f_{s}(a,b)A_{k}(s).

Then, we can find the images of the
r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}
.

Theorem B.1
.

We have

\displaystyle\varphi(r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(a,b))

\displaystyle=F_{k}(a,b)+D_{k}(a,b)

\displaystyle\varphi(r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(a,0))

\displaystyle=A_{k}(1)(t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)-E_{k}(a)-\left(\frac{t_{a}}{t_{0}}\otimes 1\right)\sum_{s=2}^{k}A_{k}(s)B(s)

\displaystyle\varphi(r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(0,b))

\displaystyle=F_{k}(0,b)

\displaystyle\varphi(r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(0,0))

\displaystyle=A_{k}(1)(t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)+R^{k}-\sum_{s=2}^{k}A_{k}(s)B(s).

Proof.

We prove all four statements simultaneously by induction on
k
. The base case
k=1
follows from the definition of
\varphi
. Suppose the formulas in the statement of the Theorem are true for some positive integer
k
. Let us prove them for
k+1
.

First, consider the value of
\varphi(r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(a,b))
:

\displaystyle\varphi(r_{k+1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,b))

\displaystyle=\varphi(r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(a,0))\varphi(e_{0b})+\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}\varphi(r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,i))\varphi(e_{ib})

\displaystyle=\left(A_{k}(1)(t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)-E_{k}(a)-\left(\frac{t_{a}}{t_{0}}\otimes 1\right)\sum_{s=2}^{k}A_{k}(s)B(s)\right)(t_{0}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}(F_{k}(a,i)+D_{k}(a,i))(t_{i}\partial_{b}\otimes 1+1\otimes e_{ib}+\delta_{ib}(-1)^{|i||b|}R)

\displaystyle=A_{k}(1)(t_{a}\partial_{0}t_{0}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\sum_{g=0}^{k-1}\left(\binom{k}{g}R^{g}\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{(|a|+|b|)|i|+|a||b|}t_{i}\partial_{b}\otimes r_{k-g}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,i)\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\left(\frac{t_{a}}{t_{0}}\otimes 1\right)\sum_{s=2}^{k}A_{k}(s)B(s)(t_{0}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)+\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}\sum_{s=1}^{k}f_{s}(a,i)(t_{i}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)A_{k}(s)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{g=0}^{k}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}(-1)^{(|a|+|b|)|i|+|a||b|}\left(t_{i}\partial_{b}\otimes r_{k-g}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,i)\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{s=1}^{k}\left((-1)^{|i|}f_{s}(a,i)(1\otimes e_{ib})A_{k}(s)\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{g=0}^{k}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}(1\otimes(-1)^{|i|}r_{k-g}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,i)e_{ib})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{s=1}^{k}f_{s}(a,b)A_{k}(s)R+\sum_{g=0}^{k}\binom{k}{g}R^{g+1}\left(1\otimes r_{k-g}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,b)\right).

Write this sum as
X_{1}-X_{2}-X_{3}+X_{4}+X_{5}+X_{6}+X_{7}+X_{8}+X_{9}
. Then

\displaystyle X_{5}-X_{2}=R^{k}(t_{a}\partial_{b}\otimes 1).

We have

\displaystyle X_{3}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{t_{a}}{t_{0}}\otimes 1\right)\sum_{s=2}^{k}A_{k}(s)B(s)(t_{0}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=2}^{k}\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|i|(1+|j|)}\frac{t_{a}}{t_{0}}\partial_{i}t_{j}\otimes r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(i,j)\otimes(t_{0}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)A_{k}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=2}^{k}\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|i|+|i||j|+|i||b|+|j||b|}t_{a}\partial_{i}t_{j}\partial_{b}\otimes r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(i,j)A_{k}(s)

and

\displaystyle X_{4}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}\sum_{s=1}^{k}f_{s}(a,i)(t_{i}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)A_{k}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{k}\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|i|+|j|}t_{a}\partial_{j}\otimes r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(j,i)\otimes(t_{i}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)A_{k}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{k}\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|j|+|j||i|+|j||b|+|i||b|}t_{a}\partial_{j}t_{i}\partial_{b}\otimes r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(j,i)A_{k}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{k}\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|i|+|i||j|+|i||b|+|j||b|}t_{a}\partial_{i}t_{j}\partial_{b}\otimes r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(i,j)A_{k}(s).

Thus

X_{4}-X_{3}=\left(\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}t_{a}\partial_{i}t_{i}\partial_{b}\otimes 1\right)A_{k}(1)=((t_{a}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)(R_{2})-t_{a}\partial_{0}t_{0}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)A_{k}(1)

as
\sum_{i\in\hat{I}}(-1)^{|i|}t_{a}\partial_{i}t_{i}\partial_{b}\otimes 1=(t_{a}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)R_{2}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\hat{I}}(-1)^{|i|}t_{a}\partial_{i}t_{i}\partial_{b}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\hat{I}}t_{a}((-1)^{|i|}+t_{i}\partial_{i})\partial_{b}

\displaystyle=(t_{a}\partial_{b})(m+1-n)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\hat{I}\\
i\neq b\end{subarray}}t_{a}t_{i}\partial_{i}\partial_{b}+t_{a}t_{b}\partial_{b}\partial_{b}

\displaystyle=(t_{a}\partial_{b})(\mathcal{E}+m+1-n)-(t_{a}\partial_{b})(t_{b}\partial_{b})+t_{a}t_{b}\partial_{b}\partial_{b}.

If
b
is even, then the last two terms become
-t_{a}\partial_{b}
. If
b
is odd, the last term is zero, and it is easily verified that
t_{a}\partial_{b}t_{b}\partial_{b}=t_{a}\partial_{b}
, Either way, we get
-(t_{a}\partial_{b})(t_{b}\partial_{b})+t_{a}t_{b}\partial_{b}\partial_{b}=-t_{a}\partial_{b}
. Plugging this back in proves the desired identity.
Then

X_{1}+X_{4}-X_{3}=(t_{a}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)A_{k}(1)R_{2}=(t_{a}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)\left(\sum_{g=0}^{k-1}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}R_{2}^{k-g}\right).

Therefore we have

X_{1}+X_{4}-X_{3}+X_{5}-X_{2}=(t_{a}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)\left(\sum_{g=0}^{k}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}R_{2}^{k-g}\right).

Since
\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}f_{s}(a,i)e_{ib}=f_{s+1}(a,b)
, we have

X_{6}=\sum_{s=1}^{k}f_{s+1}(a,b)A_{k}(s)=\sum_{s=2}^{k+1}f_{s}(a,b)\left(\sum_{g=0}^{k+1-s}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}R_{2}^{k+1-s-g}\right).

Thus

X_{6}+X_{1}+X_{4}-X_{3}+X_{5}-X_{2}=\sum_{s=1}^{k+1}f_{s}(a,b)\left(\sum_{g=0}^{k+1-s}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}R_{2}^{k+1-s-g}\right).

But

X_{8}=\sum_{s=1}^{k}\left(f_{s}(a,b)\sum_{g=1}^{k-s+1}\binom{k}{g-1}R^{g}R_{2}^{k-s-g+1}\right).

Thus

X_{8}+X_{6}+X_{1}+X_{4}-X_{3}+X_{5}-X_{2}=\sum_{s=1}^{k+1}\left(f_{s}(a,b)\sum_{g=0}^{k+1-s}\binom{k+1}{g}R^{g}R_{2}^{k+1-s-g}\right)=F_{k+1}(a,b).

Now, since
\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}r_{k-g}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,i)e_{ib}=r_{k+1-g}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,b)
, we find that

X_{7}=\sum_{g=0}^{k}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}(1\otimes r_{k+1-g}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,b)).

Also

X_{9}=\sum_{g=1}^{k}\binom{k}{g-1}R^{g}(1\otimes r_{k+1-g}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,b))

so

X_{7}+X_{9}=\sum_{g=0}^{k}\binom{k+1}{g}R^{g}(1\otimes r_{k+1-g}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,b))=D_{k+1}(a,b).

Therefore

X_{1}-X_{2}-X_{3}+X_{4}+X_{5}+X_{6}+X_{8}+(X_{7}+X_{9})=F_{k+1}(a,b)+D_{k+1}(a,b).

This completes the proof of the inductive step for
\varphi(r_{k+1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(a,b))
.

Next, we consider the value of
\varphi(r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(a,0))
. We have

\displaystyle\varphi(r_{k+1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(a,0))

\displaystyle=\varphi(r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(a,0))\varphi(e_{00})+\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}\varphi(r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(a,i))\varphi(e_{i0})

\displaystyle=\left(A_{k}(1)(t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)-E_{k}(a)-\left(\frac{t_{a}}{t_{0}}\otimes 1\right)\sum_{s=2}^{k}A_{k}(s)B(s)\right)(t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1+R)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}(F_{k}(a,i)+D_{k}(a,i))\left(t_{i}\partial_{0}\otimes 1-\sum_{j\in I}(-1)^{|i||j|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{ij}\right)

\displaystyle=A_{k}(1)(t_{a}\partial_{0}t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)-\sum_{g=0}^{k-1}\left(\binom{k}{g}\sum_{j\in I}(-1)^{|a||j|}t_{j}\partial_{0}\otimes r_{k-g}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,j)R^{g}\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\sum_{s=2}^{k}A_{k}(s)\left(\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|i|(1+|j|)}t_{a}\partial_{i}t_{j}\partial_{0}\otimes r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(i,j)\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+A_{k}(1)R(t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)-\sum_{g=0}^{k-1}\left(\binom{k}{g}R^{g+1}\sum_{j\in I}(-1)^{|a||j|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes r_{k-g}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,j)\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\left(\frac{t_{a}}{t_{0}}\otimes 1\right)\sum_{s=2}^{k}A_{k}(s)RB(s)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{s=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}f_{s}(a,i)(t_{i}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)\right)A_{k}(s)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{g=0}^{k}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}\left(\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|a||i|}t_{i}\partial_{0}\otimes r_{k-g}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,i)\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\sum_{s=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|i|(1+|j|)}f_{s}(a,i)\left(\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{ij}\right)\right)A_{k}(s)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\sum_{g=0}^{k}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}\left(\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|a||j|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes(-1)^{|i|}r^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}_{k-g}(a,i)e_{ij}\right).

Call this sum
X_{1}-X_{2}-X_{3}+X_{4}-X_{5}-X_{6}+X_{7}+X_{8}-X_{9}-X_{10}
. We have

X_{8}-X_{2}=R^{k}(-1)^{|a|}t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes r_{0}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,a)=R^{k}(t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes 1).

Also

\displaystyle X_{5}+X_{10}

\displaystyle=\sum_{g=1}^{k}\binom{k}{g-1}R^{g}\left(\sum_{j\in I}(-1)^{|a||j|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes r_{k+1-g}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,j)\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{g=0}^{k}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}\left(\sum_{j\in I}(-1)^{|a||j|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes r_{k+1-g}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,j)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g=0}^{k}\binom{k+1}{g}R^{g}\left(\sum_{j\in I}(-1)^{|a||j|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes r_{k+1-g}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,j)\right)

\displaystyle=E_{k+1}(a).

Next,

X_{9}=\sum_{s=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|i|(1+|j|)}f_{s}(a,i)\left(\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{ij}\right)\right)A_{k}(s).

For any positive integer
s
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|i|(1+|j|)}f_{s}(a,i)\left(\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{ij}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|i|(1+|j|)}\left(\sum_{\ell\in I}(-1)^{|\ell|}t_{a}\partial_{\ell}\otimes r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(\ell,i)\right)\left(\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{ij}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j,\ell\in I}(-1)^{|\ell|(1+|j|)}t_{a}\partial_{\ell}t_{j}t_{0}^{-1}\otimes\left(\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(\ell,i)e_{ij}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{t_{a}}{t_{0}}\otimes 1\right)\sum_{j,\ell\in I}(-1)^{|\ell|(1+|j|)}\partial_{\ell}t_{j}\otimes r_{s}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m,n)}(\ell,j)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{t_{a}}{t_{0}}\otimes 1\right)B(s+1).

Then

X_{9}=\left(\frac{t_{a}}{t_{0}}\otimes 1\right)\sum_{s=2}^{k+1}B(s)\left(\sum_{g=0}^{k+1-s}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}R_{2}^{k+1-s-g}\right).

Also

X_{6}=\left(\frac{t_{a}}{t_{0}}\otimes 1\right)\sum_{s=2}^{k}B(s)\left(\sum_{g=1}^{k+1-s}\binom{k}{g-1}R^{g}R_{2}^{k+1-s-g}\right).

Adding, we get

X_{6}+X_{9}=\left(\frac{t_{a}}{t_{0}}\otimes 1\right)\sum_{s=2}^{k}A_{k+1}(s)B(s).

Next, for any positive integer
s
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}f_{s}(a,i)(t_{i}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|i||j|}t_{a}\partial_{j}t_{i}\partial_{0}\otimes r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(j,i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|i||j|}t_{a}\partial_{i}t_{j}\partial_{0}\otimes r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(i,j).

Thus

X_{7}=\sum_{s=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|i||j|}t_{a}\partial_{i}t_{j}\partial_{0}\otimes r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(i,j)\right)A_{k}(s).

On the other hand,

X_{3}=\sum_{s=2}^{k}\left(\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|i||j|}t_{a}\partial_{i}t_{j}\partial_{0}\otimes r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(i,j)\right)A_{k}(s)

so

X_{7}-X_{3}=A_{k}(1)(t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)(R_{2}-t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1).

Then

X_{7}-X_{3}+X_{1}=A_{k}(1)R_{2}(t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)=\left(\sum_{g=0}^{k-1}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}R_{2}^{k-g}\right)(t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes 1).

Thus

X_{7}-X_{3}+X_{1}+X_{8}-X_{2}=\left(\sum_{g=0}^{k}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}R_{2}^{k-g}\right)(t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes 1).

Since

X_{4}=\left(\sum_{g=1}^{k}\binom{k}{g-1}R^{g}R_{2}^{k-g}\right)(t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes 1),

we get

X_{7}-X_{3}+X_{1}+X_{8}-X_{2}+X_{4}=\left(\sum_{g=0}^{k}\binom{k+1}{g}R^{g}R_{2}^{k-g}\right)(t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)=A_{k+1}(1)(t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes 1).

Thus

\displaystyle\varphi(r_{k+1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(a,0))

\displaystyle=(X_{7}-X_{3}+X_{1}+X_{8}-X_{2}+X_{4})-(X_{5}+X_{10})-(X_{6}+X_{9})

\displaystyle=A_{k+1}(1)(t_{a}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)-E_{k+1}(a)-\left(\frac{t_{a}}{t_{0}}\otimes 1\right)\sum_{s=2}^{k}A_{k+1}(s)B(s).

This completes the inductive step for
r_{k+1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(a,0)
.

Next, we consider the value of
\varphi(r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(0,b))
. We have

\displaystyle\varphi(r_{k+1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(0,b))

\displaystyle=\varphi(r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(0,0))\varphi(e_{0b})+\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}\varphi(r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(0,i))\varphi(e_{ib})

\displaystyle=\left(A_{k}(1)(t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)+R^{k}-\sum_{s=2}^{k}A_{k}(s)B(s)\right)(t_{0}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}F_{k}(0,i)(t_{i}\partial_{b}\otimes 1+1\otimes e_{ib}+\delta_{ib}(-1)^{|i||b|}R)

\displaystyle=A_{k}(1)(t_{0}\partial_{0}t_{0}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)+R^{k}(t_{0}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)-\sum_{s=2}^{k}A_{k}(s)B(s)(t_{0}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{s=1}^{k}\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}f_{s}(0,i)(t_{i}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)A_{k}(s)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{s=1}^{k}\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}f_{s}(0,i)(1\otimes e_{ib})A_{k}(s)+\sum_{s=1}^{k}f_{s}(0,b)A_{k}(s)R.

Write this sum as
X_{1}+X_{2}-X_{3}+X_{4}+X_{5}+X_{6}
. Now

\displaystyle X_{3}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=2}^{k}\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|i|(1+|j|)}(\partial_{i}t_{j}\otimes r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(i,j))(t_{0}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)A_{k}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=2}^{k}\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|i|(1+|j|)+(|i|+|j|)|b|}(t_{0}\partial_{i}t_{j}\partial_{b}\otimes r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(i,j))A_{k}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=2}^{k}\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{(|i|+|j|)(|i|+|b|)}(t_{0}\partial_{i}t_{j}\partial_{b}\otimes r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(i,j))A_{k}(s).

Also

\displaystyle X_{4}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{k}\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|i|+|j|}(t_{0}\partial_{j}\otimes r^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(j,i)\otimes t_{i}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)A_{k}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{k}\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|i|+|j|+(|i|+|j|)(|i|+|b|)}(t_{0}\partial_{j}t_{i}\partial_{b}\otimes r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(j,i))A_{k}(s).

Now
(-1)^{|i|+|j|+(|i|+|j|)(|i|+|b|)}=(-1)^{|j|+|j||i|+|i||b|+|j||b|}=(-1)^{(|j|+|i|)(|j|+|b|)}
. Then swapping the variable names
i,j
gives

X_{4}=\sum_{s=1}^{k}\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{(|i|+|j|)(|i|+|b|)}(t_{0}\partial_{i}t_{j}\partial_{b}\otimes r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(i,j))A_{k}(s).

Then
X_{4}-X_{3}
simplifies to

\left(\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}t_{0}\partial_{i}t_{i}\partial_{b}\otimes 1\right)A_{k}(1).

Then
X_{1}+X_{4}-X_{3}
is

(t_{0}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)\sum_{g=0}^{k-1}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}R_{2}^{k-g}.

Thus

X_{2}+X_{1}+X_{4}-X_{3}=(t_{0}\partial_{b}\otimes 1)\left(\sum_{g=0}^{k}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}R_{2}^{k-g}\right).

Since
\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}f_{s}(0,i)(1\otimes e_{ib})=f_{m+1}(0,i)
, we have

X_{5}=\sum_{s=1}^{k}f_{s+1}(0,b)A_{k}(s)=\sum_{s=2}^{k+1}f_{s}(0,b)\sum_{g=0}^{k+1-s}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}R_{2}^{k+1-s-g}.

Thus

X_{5}+X_{2}+X_{1}+X_{4}-X_{3}=\sum_{s=1}^{k+1}f_{s}(0,b)\sum_{g=0}^{k+1-s}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}R_{2}^{k+1-s-g}.

Also

X_{6}=\sum_{s=1}^{k}f_{s}(0,b)\sum_{g=1}^{k+1-s}\binom{k}{g-1}R^{g}R_{2}^{k+1-s-g}.

Adding the previous two equations gives

X_{1}+X_{2}-X_{3}+X_{4}+X_{5}+X_{6}=\sum_{s=1}^{k+1}f_{s}(0,b)A_{k+1}(s)

which completes the inductive step for the value of
\varphi(r_{k+1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(0,b))
.

Finally, we consider the value of
\varphi(r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(0,0))
. We have

\displaystyle\varphi(r_{k+1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(0,0))

\displaystyle=\varphi(r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(0,0))\varphi(e_{00})+\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}\varphi(r_{k}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(0,i))\varphi(e_{i,0})

\displaystyle=\left(A_{k}(1)(t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)+R^{k}-\sum_{s=2}^{k}A_{k}(s)B(s)\right)(t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1+R)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}F_{k}(0,i)\left(t_{i}\partial_{0}-\sum_{j\in I}(-1)^{|i||j|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{ij}\right)

\displaystyle=A_{k}(1)(t_{0}\partial_{0}t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)+R^{k}(t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)-\sum_{s=2}^{k}A_{k}(s)B(s)(t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+A_{k}(1)R(t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)+R^{k+1}-\sum_{s=2}^{k}\left(A_{k}(s)RB(s)\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}\sum_{s=1}^{k}f_{s}(0,i)(t_{i}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)A_{k}(s)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}\sum_{s=1}^{k}f_{s}(0,i)A_{k}(s)\sum_{j\in I}(-1)^{|i||j|}\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{ij}.

Write this as
X_{1}+X_{2}-X_{3}+X_{4}+X_{5}-X_{6}+X_{7}-X_{8}
.

Since

\displaystyle X_{7}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{k}\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|i|+|j|}(t_{0}\partial_{j}\otimes r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(j,i)\otimes t_{i}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)A_{k}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{k}\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1^{|j|(1+|i|)})\partial_{j}t_{i}\otimes r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(j,i)A_{k}(s)(t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{k}\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1^{|i|(1+|j|)})\partial_{i}t_{j}\otimes r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(i,j)A_{k}(s)(t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)

we have

\displaystyle X_{7}-X_{3}=\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i|}(\partial_{i}t_{i}\otimes 1)(t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)A_{k}(1)=(R_{2}-(t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1))(t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)A_{k}(1).

Then, expanding out the
A_{k}(s)
terms gives

\displaystyle X_{7}-X_{3}+X_{1}+X_{2}=\left(\sum_{g=0}^{k}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}R_{2}^{k-g}\right)(t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1).

But since
X_{4}=\left(\sum_{g=1}^{k}\binom{k}{g-1}R^{g}R_{2}^{k-g}\right)(t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)
,

\displaystyle X_{7}-X_{3}+X_{1}+X_{2}+X_{4}=\left(\sum_{g=0}^{k}\binom{k+1}{g}R^{g}R_{2}^{k-g}\right)(t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)=A_{k+1}(1)(t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1).

We have

\displaystyle X_{8}=\sum_{s=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|i|(1+|j|)}f_{s}(0,i)\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{ij}\right)A_{k}(s).

For any positive integer
s
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|i|(1+|j|)}f_{s}(0,i)\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{|i|(1+|j|)}\sum_{\ell\in I}(-1)^{|\ell|}t_{0}\partial_{\ell}\otimes r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(\ell,i)\otimes\frac{t_{j}}{t_{0}}\otimes e_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\sum_{\ell\in I}(-1)^{|\ell|(1+|j|)}\partial_{\ell}t_{j}\otimes(-1)^{|i|}(r_{s-1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(\ell,i)e_{ij})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,\ell\in I}(-1)^{|\ell|(1+|j|)}\partial_{\ell}t_{j}\otimes r_{s}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}(\ell,j)

\displaystyle=B(s+1).

Then

X_{8}=\sum_{s=2}^{k+1}\left(\sum_{g=0}^{k+1-s}\binom{k}{g}R^{g}R_{2}^{k+1-s-g}\right)B(s).

Also,

X_{6}=\sum_{s=2}^{k}\left(\sum_{g=1}^{k+1-s}\binom{k}{g-1}R^{g}R_{2}^{k+1-s-g}\right)B(s)

so

X_{6}+X_{8}=\sum_{s=2}^{k+1}A_{k+1}(s)B(s).

Finally,
X_{5}=R^{k+1}
, so combining the above gives

X_{1}+X_{2}-X_{3}+X_{4}+X_{5}-X_{6}+X_{7}-X_{8}=A_{k+1}(1)(t_{0}\partial_{0}\otimes 1)+R^{k+1}-\sum_{s=2}^{k+1}A_{k+1}(s)B(s).

This completes the inductive step for
\varphi(r_{k+1}^{\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n)}(0,0))
, and hence the proof of the theorem.

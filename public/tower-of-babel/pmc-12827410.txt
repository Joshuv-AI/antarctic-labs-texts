Development and validation of a risk prediction model for postoperative urinary retention after gynecologic abdominal-pelvic surgery
Authors: Jiahao Shi, Youbei Lin, Xiaojing Qin, Yinghui Gong, Hongyu Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study developed prediction models using 12 machine learning (ML) algorithms and, through comprehensive evaluation, selected the optimal model to predict postoperative urinary retention after gynecologic abdominal-pelvic surgery. An online calculator was created to provide reference for clinical decision-making and future research. The study included 1264 patients, with 436 from a temporal-spatial cohort. LASSO was used to select predictive factors, and SMOTENC oversampling and algorithm tuning were applied. The models were evaluated using AUC, accuracy, F1 score, specificity, recall, and DCA. The results showed that 161 out of 1264 patients (12.73%) developed urinary retention postoperatively. LASSO regression identified 8 key predictive factors: age, menopausal status, surgical method, postoperative analgesia, anesthesia type, POP-Q, surgical time, and intraoperative blood loss. The model based on the GBDT algorithm performed the best, with an AUC of 0.997 for the training set, 0.927 for the test set, and 0.710 and 0.676 for the external temporal-spatial validation. SHAP analysis of the GBDT model revealed that surgical time was the most significant factor for predicting urinary retention, followed by intraoperative blood loss, surgical method, and age. The study demonstrates that machine learning methods have significant advantages in predicting the risk of postoperative urinary retention after gynecologic abdominal-pelvic surgery. Introduction

Postoperative recovery is a critical phase in the surgical process, during which patients may experience various complications, such as postoperative fever, cerebrovascular accident, nausea and vomiting, ileus and urinary retention, anemia, and delirium1. Postoperative urinary retention (POUR) is defined as the inability to urinate despite a full bladder during the postoperative period. In gynecological surgery, the average incidence of POUR is 4%−15%2. Simultaneously, it is a common postoperative complication that is often difficult to detect, yet may cause significant patient distress. Its occurrence may be associated with perioperative use of analgesics/anesthetics suppressing parasympathetic activity (resulting in reduced bladder sensation) and inhibition of the micturition reflex due to surgical incisional pain, among other factors. According to reports1–5, POUR may lead to serious complications, including urinary tract infections, bladder overdistension, and autonomic responses such as vomiting, bradycardia, hypotension, hypertension, arrhythmias, and even cardiac arrest. Furthermore, it contributes to prolonged hospitalization, increased healthcare costs, and higher readmission rates, collectively compounding the burden on patients.

This study learned from prior research about common risk factors for POUR after gynecological surgery, such as diabetes2, age6, POP-Q7, intraoperative fluid volume8, and prolonged operation time9, among others. Urinary catheterization is frequently employed as a therapeutic intervention for POUR. While it facilitates monitoring of disease progression, it equally increases the likelihood of potential complications such as urinary tract infections and trauma10. Therefore, identifying high-risk patients for urinary retention after gynecologic abdominal-pelvic surgery and establishing a risk prediction model are extremely important.

Currently, in the prevention of POUR, ML (machine learning) has also found numerous applications. Kim MJ et al.9 developed a model for predicting the optimal timing of catheter removal after prolapse surgery. Tian D et al.11 developed a nomogram for predicting the risk of POUR. Although previous studies have constructed risk prediction models related to postoperative urinary retention, these investigations exhibit limitations including single-center design, small sample size, lack of external validation, and restricted generalizability, among other constraints. Therefore, this study aims to construct models using twelve machine learning algorithms, select the optimal model for predicting urinary retention after gynecologic abdominal-pelvic surgery based on comprehensive performance, and develop a web-based calculator, thereby providing a reference for future research and clinical applications.

Methods

Study design and participants

This study was a retrospective investigation based on electronic medical record systems from two Grade A tertiary hospitals in the Liaoxi region, The definition of postoperative urinary retention (POUR) is as follows: within 24 h after removal of the urinary catheter, the patient experiences a desire to void but is unable to urinate or can only pass a small amount of urine. Physical examination reveals a distended or bulging lower abdomen, with a palpable spherical mass above the pubic symphysis that is tender on pressure, requiring re-catheterization for bladder drainage. This study was conducted in strict adherence to the Transparent Reporting of a multivariable prediction model for Individual Prognosis Or Diagnosis (TRIPOD) guidelines12. Further details are provided in the Supplementary Material. Inclusion Criteria: (1) Abdominal hysterectomy: Laparoscopic hysterectomy; Extraperitoneal hysterectomy; Lower abdominal laparoscopic uterine lesion resection; (2) Tubal Surgery: Laparotomy for salpingectomy; Laparoscopic salpingectomy; (3) Ovarian surgery: Laparoscopic oophorectomy; ovarian repair; ovarian lesion resection; ovarian cyst enucleation (4) Uterine Fibroid Surgery: Laparoscopic Myomectomy; (5) Hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy; (6) Bilateral adnexa: Laparoscopic bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy. Exclusion criteria: (1) Neurogenic voiding dysfunction (stroke, poliomyelitis, cerebral palsy, multiple sclerosis, spinal lesions, etc.); (2) Pregnancy; (3) Urolithiasis; (4) Abdominal diseases such as peritonitis and peritoneal effusion; (5) Diagnosed with renal cyst and the cyst has involved the renal parenchyma/nephrons; (6) Diagnosed with endometrioma and the cyst has resulted in pelvic adhesions; (7) Medical records with missing data > 20%; (8) Medical records of patients receiving conservative treatment; (9) Age < 18 years. Data collection and preprocessing

Characteristics of the study participants

This study collected medical record data from 922 gynecological patients from a Grade A tertiary hospital in Shenyang between 2018 and 2023. After screening, 828 cases were included. Demographic and clinical data encompassed age, marital status, BMI, menopausal status, POP-Q, surgical duration, intraoperative blood loss, type of surgery, postoperative analgesia(oral analgesics/analgesia pumps), alcoholism, diabetes, hypertension, smoking, number of productions, type of anesthesia, intraoperative hydration, pessary, narcotic medications, and surgical modality13–19. All data in this study originated from the hospital medical record system. Medical records with missing values > 20% were excluded from the analysis.

External validation

Medical records of 222 patients treated in the Department of Gynecology from a Grade A tertiary hospital in Shenyang between January 2024 and December 2024 were collected as the temporal external validation set; concurrently, medical records of 214 patients treated in the Department of Gynecology from a Grade A tertiary hospital in Jinzhou between January 2024 and December 2024 were collected as the spatial external validation set. The inclusion and exclusion criteria for both validation sets were consistent with those of the modeling set, to evaluate the model’s generalization ability through the validation results.

Data encoding

In this study, predictors were coded as follows: Age (0 = 18–49 years, 1 = ≥ 50 years)8; BMI (0 = Underweight [< 18.5 kg/m²], 1 = Normal weight [18.5–24 kg/m²], 2 = Overweight [≥ 24 and < 28 kg/m²], 3 = Obese [≥ 28 kg/m²]); Smoking (0 = No, 1 = Yes); Alcoholism (0 = No, 1 = Yes); Marital status (0 = Unmarried, 1 = Married, 2 = Divorced, 3 = Widowed); Diabetes (0 = No, 1 = Yes); Hypertension (0 = No, 1 = Yes); Menopause (0 = No, 1 = Yes); Number of productions (0 = 0 deliveries, 1 = 1 delivery, 2 = 2 deliveries, 3 = 3 deliveries); POP-Q (0 = No prolapse/Stage 0, 1 = Stage I, 2 = Stage II, 3 = Stage III, 4 = Stage IV); Types of anesthesia (0 = General anesthesia, 1 = Neuraxial anesthesia); Surgical duration, Intraoperative hydration, and Intraoperative blood loss; Narcotic medications (0 = Opioid, 1 = Non-opioid); Surgical modality (0 = Laparotomy, 1 = celioscope); Pessary (0 = No, 1 = Yes); Type of surgery (0 = Hysterectomy, 1 = Ovarian surgery, 2 = Fallopian tube surgery, 3 = Myomectomy, 4 = Bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy, 5 = Hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy); Postoperative analgesia (0 = None, 1 = Use of non-narcotic analgesics); Postoperative urinary retention (0 = No, 1 = Yes). Min-Max scaling was applied to continuous variables to normalize them to the 0–1 range20.

Model selection

This study considered twelve machine learning algorithms: Adaptive Boosting (AdaBoost), Categorical Boosting (CatBoost), Decision Tree (DT), Gradient Boosting Decision Tree (GBDT), K-Nearest Neighbor Algorithm (KNN), Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LGBM), Logistic Regression (LR), Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), Naive Bayes (NB), Random Forest (RF), Support Vector Machine (SVM), and Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost). Logistic regression, also known as log-odds regression, is a common machine learning algorithm used to construct binary clinical prediction models; it is an extension of linear regression21; DT is a tree-like model used for decision-making and prediction. Its classification model is represented by a tree structure where each internal node denotes a test on a feature, each branch signifies a possible test outcome, and each leaf node indicates a classification22; RF is a commonly used ensemble algorithm for classification and regression. Its fundamental unit is the decision tree, and compared to a decision tree, it possesses the advantages of superior prediction accuracy and enhanced resistance to overfitting22; SVM is a supervised machine learning algorithm capable of handling both simple, linear and complex, nonlinear classification problems, which is widely adopted in the medical field22; XGBoost is a machine learning algorithm integrated from multiple classification and regression trees for addressing classification and regression problems, and it can not only optimally handle diverse variable types and imbalanced datasets but also resist outliers in predictor variables and potential multicollinearity among them23; The Adaboost algorithm is an ensemble learning algorithm derived from Boosting techniques, which combines multiple base and relatively weaker predictors24; The fundamental principle of the CatBoost algorithm is to improve the predictive performance of existing models by iteratively adding new decision trees; each new decision tree is grown in the negative gradient direction to minimize the value of the loss function25; GBDT is a decision tree ensemble learning algorithm based on the gradient boosting framework; it constructs multiple decision trees where each step attempts to modify residual errors from the preceding step, ultimately forming a powerful predictive model. Its advantage lies in effectively capturing complex nonlinear relationships among predictors and processing high-dimensional feature data26; KNN is a machine learning algorithm extensively applied in clinical prediction model construction; based on measuring distances between new samples and training samples, it predicts outcomes according to the categories or values of nearest neighbors20; LGBM is a method that utilizes first-order and second-order negative gradients to improve upon traditional gradient boosting decision trees. It employs a histogram-based decision tree algorithm to accelerate execution speed and adopts a leaf-wise growth strategy to enhance efficiency27; MLP is a feedforward neural network composed of an input layer, one or more hidden layers, and an output layer; it uses weighted connections and activation functions to perform nonlinear transformations20; NB is a class of machine learning algorithms based on Bayes’ theorem; its characteristic lies in employing a ‘naive’ assumption that presumes conditional independence between features28.

Feature selection and model construction

Using a computer program for random allocation (random seed number = 123), 828 screened patients were randomly divided into a training set (579 cases) and a testing set (249 cases) at a 7:3 ratio29. Within the training set, this study employed LASSO regression to screen predictors from 19 candidate factors. Subsequently, predictive models were constructed using twelve distinct machine learning algorithms, including AdaBoost, CatBoost, DT, GBDT, KNN, LGBM, LR, MLP, NB, RF, SVM, and XGBoost. Model performance was evaluated using AUC (Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve), Accuracy, F1-score, Specificity, Recall, and DCA (Decision Curve Analysis). In the training and test sets, 5-fold cross-validation (CV) was utilized to assess generalization capability and prevent overfitting, with 80% of the data used for training and the remaining 20% for validating model performance in each round30. DCA evaluates predictive performance and potential clinical applicability of various models by incorporating actual decision risks and threshold selection of predicted probabilities31. The X-axis represents the decision threshold, while the Y-axis denotes net benefit. The dashed line assumes all patients are without POUR, and the solid black line assumes all patients have POUR.

Model visualization and interpretation

This study employed the game theory-based SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) visualization algorithm. Its core concept involves calculating features’ marginal contributions to model outputs, interpreting each predictor’s contribution to the model to enhance interpretability and transparency32,33. Furthermore, this study utilized Streamlit to develop a web-based calculator.

Statistical methods

All statistical analyses were performed using Python 3.10.6 and R 4.2.3 on a cloud-based statistical computing platform34, employing the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test to assess conformity with a normal distribution. Categorical variables are expressed as counts/percentages. For continuous variables conforming to normal distribution, mean ± standard deviation is used; inter-group comparisons employ independent-samples t-test. For continuous variables not conforming to normal distribution, median and interquartile range are reported; inter-group comparisons utilize Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Statistical significance was defined as P <.05. The working principle of SMOTE involves creating synthetic data using the k-NN algorithm. First, the feature vectors and their nearest neighbors are identified, and the distance between two sample points is calculated. Then, the difference between a sample and its nearest neighbor is multiplied by a random number between 0 and 1. A new point along the line segment is identified based on this computed distance35. This study employed the SMOTENC oversampling technique to address class imbalance, applying this technique exclusively to the training set after 7:3 data partitioning.

As illustrated in Fig. 4C and D, the waterfall plots visualize SHAP values for randomly selected negative and positive cases, respectively, with features ranked in descending order of importance. This study further evaluated the generalizability of the GBDT model using a temporal external validation set (222 gynecological cases from a Grade A tertiary hospital in Shenyang in 2024) and a geographical external validation set (214 gynecological cases from a Grade A tertiary hospital in Jinzhou in 2024). The AUC values for these validation sets were 0.710 and 0.676, respectively, demonstrating good generalizability.

Discussion

Main findings

Currently, prediction methods for urinary retention following gynecologic abdominal-pelvic surgery remain relatively scarce. This study developed a machine learning model based on the GBDT algorithm, utilizing LASSO regression to identify eight key predictors: age, menopause, surgical modality, postoperative analgesia, type of anesthesia, POP-Q, surgical duration, and intraoperative blood loss. Among these, surgical duration, age, and intraoperative blood loss demonstrated the highest feature importance in the model. This study constructed models using twelve machine learning algorithms and selected GBDT as the optimal model based on comprehensive performance evaluation. A web-based calculator was subsequently developed. The model achieved AUC values of 0.997, 0.927, 0.710, and 0.676 in the training set, test set, and two external validation cohorts, respectively. These results robustly demonstrate the model’s strong generalizability and consistent predictive performance across diverse datasets.

Interpretation

Patients aged over 50 years demonstrated a higher incidence of postoperative urinary retention. This phenomenon may be associated with progressive neuronal degeneration in micturition control pathways, increased urethral rigidity, impaired detrusor contractility, and prolonged bed rest during recovery in elderly populations10,39–41. Postmenopausal patients similarly demonstrated increased susceptibility to postoperative urinary retention. This may be attributed to declining estrogen levels in aging women, which attenuates inhibition of fibroblast proliferation. Such endocrine changes promote tissue adhesions, ultimately leading to genital adhesions or labial fusion42,43. Urinary retention occurs mechanistically when the cranial positioning of the labial fusion orifice causes urine accumulation within the vaginal vestibule44. Celiotomy was confirmed as a strong risk factor for postoperative urinary retention, demonstrating robust association with established evidence45. Postoperative analgesia may precipitate urinary retention: Opioid administration for postoperative pain control inhibits detrusor contraction while reducing bladder contractility and urinary urgency, consequently increasing bladder capacity and postvoid residual volume to induce urinary retention46,47. Non-opioid analgesics operate through distinct mechanisms, facilitating the Valsalva voiding reflex while suppressing or attenuating detrusor contraction, thereby producing urinary retention48,49. Neuraxial anesthesia readily induces urinary retention because the anesthetic acts on sacral and lumbar nerve fibers, blocking both afferent and efferent neural impulses transmitting bladder signals to the brain, while the sensation of urinary urgency persists, resulting in urinary retention50. This may be related to POP, which can cause bladder outlet obstruction, leading to detrusor overactivity or impaired contractility51, consistent with previous studies52. Patients with prolonged surgical duration and greater intraoperative blood loss are at higher risk for postoperative urinary retention, which may be attributable to increased administration of fluids and anesthetic agents during the procedure15,53.

ML algorithms have been extensively applied to the prediction of postoperative complications, and postoperative urinary retention prediction models represent an instance of ML algorithm implementation in this field. Currently, several machine learning (ML) models have been employed for postoperative urinary retention prediction. In these studies, the majority of investigators have focused on comparing different algorithms to select optimal modeling approaches, with less emphasis on the clinical implementation of ML models9,11,54,55 The AUC values of these prediction models range from 0.663 to 0.854. This study employed the GBDT algorithm to develop a prediction model for postoperative urinary retention following gynecologic abdominal-pelvic surgery, and created a web-based calculator. Furthermore, the research demonstrated the model’s good generalization capability through validation in two external cohorts. Despite implementing SMOTENC and hyperparameter tuning to optimize model performance, the AUC in external validation cohorts decreased by approximately 0.2, which may be attributable to the influence of institutional heterogeneity and other factors including class distribution imbalance in these datasets. As evidenced by the SHAP summary plot in Fig. 4B, the variable ' menopause ' functions as a bidirectional feature with predominantly promotive effects alongside localized inhibitory contributions in overall prediction, suggesting potential nonlinear relationships or individual variability with the outcome. In the two illustrative cases presented in Fig. 5, ‘menopausal status’ served as an inhibitory factor in Case A, whereas it acted as a promotive factor in Case B. This phenomenon indicates that the model developed in this study not only successfully captured the nonlinear relationships between predictors and outcomes, but may also reveal directional variations in feature impact across different samples—a capability difficult to achieve with traditional linear models (logistic regression). These findings provide reference basis for subsequent individualized interventions. This demonstrates the model’s effective capacity in handling complex data and confirms its good generalization ability to adapt to varying data distributions.

Strengths

This study has the following strengths: First, the clinical prediction model was developed using data from Liaoning Province, China, enhancing its applicability for patients in this specific region. Second, this constitutes a large-sample (n = 1264), dual-center investigation. Third, all eight predictors used for model construction are routinely available clinical indicators, rendering them easily obtainable and facilitating clinical implementation and broader dissemination. Fourth, the application of the SHAP method enhanced the model’s transparency and interpretability. By explaining each feature’s impact on model output through summary plots and ranking predictors by variable importance for postoperative urinary retention in gynecologic abdominal-pelvic surgery, this approach enables healthcare providers to prioritize risk factors and provides reference for subsequent individualized interventions. Fifth, a GBDT algorithm-based web-based assessment tool has been developed to assist health professionals in accurately identifying high-risk populations.

Limitations

Although this study incorporated a multicenter external validation with high credibility and developed a web-based calculator as a tool for healthcare professionals, certain limitations remain. First, this was a retrospective study. Compared to prospective studies, retrospective research is methodologically associated with lower evidence strength. Second, the clinical prediction model was established based on data from Liaoning Province, China, which may limit its scope of application. Third, all patients included in this study were of Chinese ethnicity, which may result in limited applicability of the model to other racial groups. Despite these limitations, this study provides valuable insights for future research. Subsequent investigations may enhance the generalization capability of the prediction model by expanding sample sizes and incorporating additional potential risk factors.

Conclusion

This study constructed models using twelve machine learning algorithms, selected GBDT as the optimal model for predicting postoperative urinary retention after gynecological abdominal-pelvic surgery based on comprehensive performance evaluation, and validated it through two external datasets. LASSO regression identified eight factors: age, menopause, surgical modality, postoperative analgesia (oral analgesics/analgesia pumps), type of anesthesia, POP-Q, surgical duration, and intraoperative blood loss. The SHAP algorithm explained the GBDT model, identifying surgical duration as the primary predictor of postoperative urinary retention following gynecologic abdominal-pelvic surgery, followed by intraoperative blood loss volume, surgical modality, and age. An online calculator was developed via Streamlit, providing a reference for risk assessment.

1.
Introduction

The Jordan type
P_{B}
of a nilpotent
n\times n
nilpotent matrix
B
is the partition giving the Jordan block decomposition of a Jordan block matrix
J_{P}
similar to
B
. Since the variety
\mathcal{N}_{B}
of nilpotent matrices commuting with
B
is irreducible, there is a generic commuting Jordan type
\mathcal{D}(P)
of a matrix
A
in
\mathcal{N}_{B}
, which is maximum in the
dominance order. The partition
\mathcal{D}(P)
is stable, that is,
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{D}(P))=\mathcal{D}(P)
; equivalently,
\mathcal{D}(P)
has parts that differ pairwise by at least two - known in combinatorics as super-distinct. In addition the largest and smallest parts of
\mathcal{D}(P)
were determined by P. Oblak and by the third author in
[
Ob
,
Kh1
]
: so in 2014
\mathcal{D}(P)
was known when
\mathcal{D}(P)
has three or less parts.
R. Basili in 2022 determined
\mathcal{D}(P)
in general, proving the 2012 recursive conjecture of P. Oblak, that had been widely studied with many partial results. The second two authors with B. Van Steirteghem and R. Zhao showed that
the partitions in
\mathcal{D}^{-1}(Q)
for a stable
Q=(u,u-r)
form a
(r-1)\times(u-r)
box, such that
P_{k,l}(Q)
has
(k+l)
parts. Their arrangement used what they termed
A
rows, and
B,C
hooks
[
IKVZ2
]
.
The box theorem of
J. Irving, T. Košir and M. Mastnak
[
IKM
]
shows that for each stable partition
Q
the Jordan types
P
satisfying
\mathcal{D}(P)=Q
form a box, whose entries could be determined by their Burge codes. For an account of these developments, see the third author’s survey
[
Kh2
]
.

In
[
BIK
]
the authors used the Irving, Košir, Mastnak box theorem in the special case that
Q
has two parts, to determine the equations
E^{Q}_{k,l}
defining the locus
P_{B}=P^{Q}_{k,l}
in
\mathcal{N}(Q)
. There,
P^{Q}_{k,l}
in
\mathcal{D}^{-1}(Q)
is determined by its Burge code.
We here prove that the partition
P_{k,l}(Q)
in the table
\mathcal{T}(Q)
from
[
IKVZ2
]
is identical to
the partition
P^{Q}_{k,l}
in
{\mathcal{D}}^{-1}(Q)
as defined in
[
BIK
]
: this is
[
BIK
, Proposition 3.9]
, whose proof we deferred to here. We show this in Theorem
4.1
and Corollary
4.2
below. We prove this by showing that the equations
E^{Q}_{k,l}
defining each locus are the same. In order for this note to be self-contained, there is overlap between Section
3
here and the Notations and Background Section 2 of
[
BIK
]
.

Recall that a partition is

The stable partition

(1)

P=\left(a^{n_{a}},(a-1)^{n_{a-1}},b^{n_{b}},(b-1)^{n_{b-1}}\right)\text{with $n_{a}>0$ and $n_{b}>0$}.

We will assume that

Definition 2.1

(Type A,B,C partitions in

)

Let

We say that

We say that

Theorem 2.2

(Table Theorem, part I)
.

Let
Q=(u,u-r)
with
u>r\geq 2
.

(a)

For every positive integer

1\leq t\leq\min\{u-r,\lfloor\frac{r-1}{2}\rfloor\}=t_{\mathrm{max}}

A_{t}=\{(k,l)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\,|\,k_{t-1}\leq k<k_{t}\mbox{ and }t\leq l\leq u-r\}.

For
T=\min\{u-r,\lfloor\frac{r-1}{2}\rfloor\}+1=t_{\mathrm{max}}+1

A_{T}=\{(k,l)\in{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}}\,{\mbox{ such that }}\,k=k_{t_{\mathrm{max}}}=r-T{\text{ and }}T\leq l\leq u-r\}.

Moreover

(2)

A_{T}\not=\emptyset\Leftrightarrow r\text{ is even and }u\geq\frac{3r}{2},

and in that case
T={k_{t_{\mathrm{max}}}}={\frac{r}{2}}
.

Then for all
(k,l)\in A_{t}
, when
t\in\{1,2,\ldots,T\}
the partition

is of type A and

\mathcal{D}(P_{k,l})=(u,u-r)
.

(b)

For every positive integer
t
such that
1\leq t\leq\min\{u-r,\lfloor\frac{r-1}{2}\rfloor\}
, define the subset

as

C_{t}=\{(k,t)\,|\,k_{t}\leq k<c_{t}\}.

Then for all
(k,l)\in C_{t}
, the partition

P_{k,l}=\left([u-r+2t]^{t},[u-2t-d_{t}(q_{t}-1)]^{k-d_{t}},(q_{t}-1)^{d_{t}})\right)

is of type C but not of type A or B, and satisfies
\mathcal{D}(P_{k,l})=(u,u-r)
.

(c)

For every positive integer
t
such that
1\leq t\leq\min\{u-r,\lfloor\frac{r-1}{2}\rfloor\}

B_{t}=\{(k,t)\,|\,c_{t}\leq k\leq r-t\}\cup\{(r-t,l)\,|\,t<l\leq u-r\}.

Then for all
(k,l)\in B_{t}
, the partition

P_{k,l}=\left([u-r+2t]^{t},[u-2t]^{k+l-t}\right)

is of type B but not of type A and satisfies
\mathcal{D}(P_{k,l})=(u,u-r)
.

(d)

Each pair
(k,l)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
with

and

belongs to one and only one set

,

or

defined above. In particular there are listed above

distinct partitions

, each satisfying

has

parts.

Equation to partition

We show that the equations

P_{A}=P_{k,l}(Q)
in the table

[

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
A
be a matrix in the nilpotent commutator of the Jordan matrix
J_{Q},Q=(u,u-r),r\geq 2
. Let
(k,l)
satisfy
1\leq k\leq r-1
and
1\leq l\leq u-r
. Assume that the equations
E^{Q}_{k,l}
hold for entries of
A
, and that the rest of the entries of
A
are generic. Then the Jordan type of
A
is equal to the partition
P_{k,l}(Q)
in the table
\mathcal{T}(Q),Q=(u,u-r)
of Theorem
2.2
.

Proof.

The order matrix of
A
is

T=\begin{bmatrix}k&&0\\
r&&l^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}.

where
l^{\prime}=l\oplus(r-k)
. The equations
E^{Q}_{k,l}
also imply that
\corank A=k+l
.

By
[
BIK
, Lemma 3.6]
for
s\geq 1
,

\corank A^{s}=(k+l)s\oplus((T^{\otimes s})_{11}+u-r)\oplus(u+u-r).

Moreover, by
[
BIK
, Lemma 3.5]
for
s\geq 2
,

(T^{\otimes s})_{11}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}sk\oplus\frac{s}{2}r\oplus((s-2)l^{\prime}+r)\oplus u&\mbox{ if }s\mbox{ is even},\\
\\
sk\oplus(k+\frac{s-1}{2}r)\oplus(l^{\prime}+\frac{s-1}{2}r)\oplus((s-2)l^{\prime}+r)\oplus u&\mbox{ if }s\mbox{ is odd}.\end{array}\right.

Since
k+l^{\prime}\leq r
, if
k\leq l^{\prime}
then
2k\leq r
, and therefore

\begin{array}[]{ll}2ks&\leq rs\\
\mbox{ and}\\
2ks&=2k+2(s-1)k\\
&\leq 2k+(s-1)r\\
&\leq 2l^{\prime}+(s-1)r.\end{array}

On the other hand, if
l^{\prime}<k
, then
2l^{\prime}<r
and we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}2((s-2)l^{\prime}+r)&<sr\\
\mbox{ and}\\
2((s-2)l^{\prime}+r)&=2l^{\prime}+2(s-3)l^{\prime}+2r\\
&<2l^{\prime}+(s-1)r\\
&<2k+(s-1)r.\end{array}

Thus for
s\geq 2
we always have
(T^{\otimes s})_{11}=sk\oplus((s-2)l^{\prime}+r)\oplus u.

Consequently,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\corank A&=k+l,\\
\corank A^{s}&=s(k+l)\oplus(sk+u-r)\oplus((s-2)\ell^{\prime}+u)\oplus(u+u-r),\mbox{ for }s\geq 2.\end{array}

In the
sy
-plane, we consider the following four lines corresponding to the components of
\corank A^{s}
.

\begin{array}[]{ll}L_{1}(s)&=(k+l)s\\
L_{2}(s)&=ks+u-r\\
L_{3}(s)&=\ell^{\prime}s+u-2\ell^{\prime}\\
L_{4}(s)&=u+u-r\\
\end{array}

For
s=1
the corank of
A^{s}
is obtained by
L_{1}
and for
s
large enough, it stabilizes at
L_{4}
. For
s\geq 2
the corank of
A^{s}
is determined by the line that lies below the other three at that particular value of
s
. For integers,
i
and
j
with
0\leq i<j\leq 3
, we denote the
s
-coordinate of the intersection of
L_{i}
and
L_{j}
by
x_{ij}
. In particular, we have

\begin{array}[]{llll}x_{1,2}&=\dfrac{u-r}{\ell},&x_{1,3}&=\dfrac{u-2\ell^{\prime}}{k+\ell-\ell^{\prime}},\\
\\
x_{2,3}&=\dfrac{r-2\ell^{\prime}}{k-\ell^{\prime}},&x_{2,4}&=\dfrac{u}{k},\\
\\
x_{3,4}&=\dfrac{u-r}{\ell^{\prime}}+2.\end{array}

We first study the Jordan partition of a matrix
A
based on the “path” through which the corank of powers of
A
are obtained.

Case A. (
L_{1}\to L_{2}\to L_{4}
)
Suppose that
L_{3}(s)\geq L_{!}(s)\oplus L_{2}(s)\oplus L_{4}(s)
, for
s\geq 2
. Then

(7)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\corank A^{s}&=s(k+l)\oplus(sk+u-r)\oplus(u+u-r)\\
&=L_{1}(s)\oplus L_{2}(s)\oplus L_{4}(s).\end{array}

Let

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\begin{array}[]{ll}s_{1}&=\lfloor x_{1,2}\rfloor=\lfloor\frac{u-r}{l}\rfloor,\\
e_{1}&=u-r-s_{1}l.\end{array}&\mbox{and}&\begin{array}[]{lll}s_{2}&=\lfloor x_{2,4}\rfloor=\lfloor\frac{u}{k}\rfloor,\\

&>(s-2)k+u\\

This contradicts the fact that for

Thus

.

contains a middle
U
-chain only if
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are both positive and
s_{2}=s_{1}+2
. In this case

\begin{array}[]{ll}|U_{middle}|&=s_{2}(k-e_{2}+e_{1})-e_{1}+2e_{2}\\
\\
&=s_{2}k-s_{2}(e_{2}-e_{1})-e_{1}+2e_{2}\\
\\
&=s_{2}k+e_{2}-(s_{2}-1)(e_{2}-e_{1})\\
\\
&=u-(s_{2}-1)(e_{2}-e_{1}).\end{array}

Since
s_{2}=s_{1}+2
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}e_{2}-e_{1}&=(u-s_{2}k)-(u-r-s_{1}l)\\
&=(s_{2}-2)l-s_{2}k+r\\
&\geq(s_{2}-2)l^{\prime}-s_{2}k+r\\
&=L_{3}(s_{2})-L_{2}(s_{2}).\end{array}

Since
\corank A^{s_{2}}=L_{2}(s_{2})
, we have
L_{3}(s_{2})\geq L_{2}(s_{2})
. This in particular implies that
e_{2}-e_{1}\geq 0
and therefor
|U_{middle}|\leq u
.

This shows that in this case
P=([u]^{k},[u-r]^{l})
with
Q(P)=(u,u-r)
. Thus, in this case
P=P_{k,l}
in the table
\mathcal{T}(Q),\,Q=(u,u-r)
, as desired.

Case B. (
L_{1}\to L_{3}\to L_{4}
)

Suppose that
L_{2}(s)\geq L_{!}(s)\oplus L_{2}(s)\oplus L_{4}(s)
, for
s\geq 2
. Then

(8)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\corank A^{s}&=s(k+l)\oplus(sl^{\prime}+u-2l^{\prime})\oplus(u+u-r)\\
&=L_{1}(s)\oplus L_{3}(s)\oplus L_{4}(s).\end{array}

Let

\begin{array}[]{ll}s_{3}&=\lfloor x_{1,3}\rfloor=\lfloor\frac{u-2l^{\prime}}{k+l-l^{\prime}}\rfloor\\
e_{3}&=(u-2l^{\prime})-s_{3}(k+l-l^{\prime}),\mbox{ and}\\
\\
s_{4}&=\lfloor x_{3,4}\rfloor-2=\lfloor\frac{u-r}{l^{\prime}}\rfloor\\
e_{4}&=u-r-s_{4}l^{\prime}.\par\end{array}

By equation
8
we have

\corank A^{s}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}s(k+l),&\mbox{ if }s=1,\dots,s_{3}\\
(s-2)l^{\prime}+u,&\mbox{ if }s=s_{3}+1,\dots,s_{4}+2\\
u+u-r,&\mbox{ if }s\geq s_{4}+3,\end{array}\right.

We note that since
\corank A^{s}\leq L_{2}(s)
always holds, we in particular have
u+u-r\leq L_{2}(x_{3,4})
. Thus
u\leq k\left(\frac{u-r}{l^{\prime}}-2\right)
. Thus
\frac{u}{k}\leq\frac{u-r}{l^{\prime}}-2
. On the other hand, since
l\geq l^{\prime}\geq 1
, we also have
\frac{u-2l^{\prime}}{k+l-l^{\prime}}\leq\frac{u}{k}
. This in particular shows that
s_{3}\leq s_{4}
. See figure
2
for a visualization of the linear terms affecting
\corank A^{s}
in this case.

Let
P
be the Jordan type of
A
. Then

P^{\vee}=\Big(\,(k+l)^{s_{3}},\,l^{\prime}+e_{3},\,(l^{\prime})^{s_{4}-s_{3}+1},\,e_{4}\Big),

and

P=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Big(\,(s_{4}+3)^{e_{4}},\,(s_{4}+2)^{l^{\prime}-e_{4}},\,(s_{3}+1)^{e_{3}},\,(s_{3})^{k+l-l^{\prime}-e_{3}}\,\Big),&\mbox{if }e_{3},e_{4}>0\\

Looking at the
U
-chains in

, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}|U_{top}|&=u-r+2l^{\prime}\\
|U_{bottom}|&=u.\end{array}

Since
\corank A^{s_{3}+1}=L_{3}(s_{3}+1)
, we have
L_{3}(s_{3}+1)\leq L_{2}(s_{3}+1)
. On the other hand, since
k+l^{\prime}\leq r
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}L_{3}(s_{3}+1)&=(s_{3}+1)l^{\prime}+u-2l^{\prime}\\
&=s_{3}l^{\prime}+u-l^{\prime}\\
&\geq s_{3}l^{\prime}+u-(r-k)\\
&=s_{3}l^{\prime}+k+u-r\\
&=(s_{3}+1)k+u-r-s_{3}(k-l^{\prime})\\
&=L_{2}(s_{3}+1)-s_{3}(k-l^{\prime}).\end{array}

Thus
k\geq l^{\prime}

|U_{top}|\leq u

\begin{array}[]{ll}L_{2}(s_{4}+1)-L_{3}(s_{4}+1)&=((s_{4}+1)k+u-r)-((s_{4}+1)l^{\prime}+u-2l^{\prime})\\

&\leq s_{4}(k+l-l^{\prime})-s_{4}l^{\prime}+k+l-r\\
&=(u-2l^{\prime}-e_{3})-(u-r-e_{4})+k+l-r\\
&=e_{4}-e_{3}+k+l-2l^{\prime}\end{array}

Thus
e_{3}-e_{4}\leq k+l-2l^{\prime}
, and therefore

\begin{array}[]{ll}|U_{middle}|&=(s_{3}+1)(l^{\prime}-e_{4}+e_{3})+l^{\prime}-e_{4}+2e_{4}\\
&=s_{3}(l^{\prime}+e_{3}-e_{4})+2l^{\prime}+e_{3}\\
&\leq s_{3}(l^{\prime}+k+l-2l^{\prime})+2l^{\prime}+e_{3}\\
&=s_{3}(k+l-l^{\prime})+e_{3}+2l^{\prime}=u.\end{array}

This shows that in this case

Case C.

Suppose that neither Case A nor Case B holds. Then the following two sets are nonempty.

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{L}_{2}&=\{s\in\mathbb{Z}\,|\,s\geq 2\mbox{ and }L_{2}(s)<L_{1}(s)\oplus L_{3}(s)\oplus L_{4}(s)\}\\

For the two sets above to be both nonempty, we need
L_{2}
and

k\neq l^{\prime}

and

. Moreover, the

\frac{r-2l^{\prime}}{k-l^{\prime}}\geq 2

. Thus we have
k>l^{\prime}

\frac{r-2l^{\prime}}{k-l^{\prime}}\geq 2
, we have
r\geq 2k
, which in particular implies that

l^{\prime}=\min\{r-k,l\}

. Thus if we let

, then we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\corank A^{s}&=s(k+l),\mbox{ for }s=1,\dots,s_{1}\\
\corank A^{s_{1}+1}&=(s_{1}+1)k+u-r,\\

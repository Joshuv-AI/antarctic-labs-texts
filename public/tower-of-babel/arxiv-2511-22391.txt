Impure Simplicial Complex and Term-Modal Logic with Assignment Operators
Yuanzhe Yang

Abstract
Impure simplicial complexes are a powerful tool to model multi-agent epistemic situations where agents may die, but it is difficult to define a satisfactory semantics for the ordinary propositional modal language on such models, since many conceptually dubious expressions involving dead agents can be expressed in this language. In this paper, we introduce a term-modal language with assignment operators, in which such conceptually dubious expressions are syntactically excluded. We define both simplicial semantics and first-order Kripke semantics for this language, characterize their respective expressivity through notions of bisimulation, and show that the two semantics are equivalent when we consider a special class of first-order Kripke models called local epistemic models. We also offer a complete axiomatization for the epistemic logic based on this language, and show that our language has a notion of assignment normal form. Finally, we discuss the behavior of a kind of intensional distributed knowledge that can be naturally expressed in our language.

Conceptually, assignment operators of the above kind (as well as predicate abstraction) usually work a tool to capture the

Technically, in

In this paper, then, we will develop an epistemic logic for impure simplicial complexes based on the more delicate language with assignment operators of the form
[x\!:\!\!\resizebox{218453}{0}{=}\,a]\phi

is sound and strongly complete w.r.t. the class of all simplicial models.

In the rest of this section,

To put it more formally, we define the following notion of

\mathcal{L}^{[:\resizebox{176584}{0}{=}]}_{\mathtt{ANF}}\ni\gamma::=[x\!:\!\!\resizebox{218453}{0}{=}\,a]p_{x}\mid[x\!:\!\!\resizebox{218453}{0}{=}\,a]\bot\mid\top\mid\neg\gamma\mid(\gamma\wedge\gamma)\mid[\vec{x}\!:\!\!\resizebox{218453}{0}{=}\,\vec{a}]\mathsf{K}_{\vec{x}}\gamma

Proposition 4.4
.

For all
\alpha\in\mathcal{L}^{[:\resizebox{195881}{0}{=}]}_{0}
,
there is

\vdash\alpha\leftrightarrow\gamma

Note that

can simply be viewed as a propositional letter labeled by

and

9

This is somewhat like the “opposite” of

But note that as long as we can express the existential predicate in our language,

Under this perspective, formulas like

It is also worth noting that the connection between our approach and the two-valued one gives us the

of our logic.
The satisfiability problem for

is decidable, since it can be viewed as a kind of two-valued language with an essentially

-logic, which is clearly decidable;
moreover, computing the normal form of an

Thus, we have

Theorem 4.5

The satisfiability problem for
\mathcal{L}^{[:\resizebox{195881}{0}{=}]}
-sentences is decidable.

5
Intensional Distributed Knowledge

Since we have open formulas in our language,
it is natural to define groups of agents with them.
Formally, for a formula
\phi(x)
with at most one free variable
x
(let
\mathcal{L}^{[:\resizebox{176584}{0}{=}]}_{x}
denote the set of all such formulas), the group defined by
\phi(x)
at a pointed simplicial model
\mathcal{C},F
is
\phi(\mathcal{C},F)=\{a\in\chi^{\mathcal{C}}[F]\mid\mathcal{C},F,\sigma[x\mapsto a]\Vdash\phi(x)\}
,
while the group it defines at a pointed first-order Kripke model
\mathcal{M},w
is
\phi(\mathcal{M},w)=\{a\in\delta^{\mathcal{M}}(w)\mid\mathcal{M},w,\sigma[x\mapsto a]\vDash\phi(x)\}
(our choice of
\sigma
does not matter here).
Note that such groups are
intensional
,
in the sense that the same formula
\phi(x)
might define different groups at different facets / worlds.
10
10

10

The idea of introducing intensional groups to epistemic logic dates back to
[
13
,
12
]
,
and intensional distributed knowledge has been studied in
[
4
,
5
]
, for example. Unlike these works, however, the intensional groups here are denoted by
open formulas
in our language, rather than a distinct set of
group names
.

Then, it seems interesting to consider a kind of
intensional distributed knowledge
based on such intensional groups.
Formally, for each
\phi\in\mathcal{L}^{[:\resizebox{176584}{0}{=}]}_{x}
, the intended truth conditions for
\mathsf{K}_{\phi}\alpha
are the following:

\mathcal{C},F\Vdash\mathsf{K}_{\phi}\alpha

iff

for all
G\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathcal{C}}(C)
s.t.

\mathcal{M},w\vDash\mathsf{K}_{\phi}\alpha

iff

for all

s.t.

\phi(\mathcal{C},F)\subseteq\chi^{\mathcal{C}}[F\cap G]
,

v\in\bigcap_{a\in\phi(\mathcal{M},w)}R_{a}^{\mathcal{M}}(w)
,

\mathcal{C},G\Vdash\alpha

\mathcal{M},v\vDash\alpha

In our language, we can define
\mathsf{K}_{\phi}
as follows:
first, for each
\phi\in\mathcal{L}^{[:\resizebox{176584}{0}{=}]}_{x}

let

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced a term-modal language with assignment operators to study epistemic logic on impure simplicial complexes.
We characterized the expressivity of the simplicial semantics and first-order Kripke semantics of this language,
showed the correspondence between simplicial complexes and local epistemic models,
offered a complete axiomatization for the epistemic logic based on our language,
showed that the our language has assignment normal form,
and also studied the behavior of the intensional distributed knowledge operators, which can be defined in our language.

There are a number of interesting directions for future work.
For example, it is interesting to further study the connection between our simplicial semantics and the existing approaches,
especially the
three-valued
one.
Though sentences in our language are two-valued,
there might be some natural way to generate a three-valued semantics from our semantics.
If this can be done,
then it should also be interesting to see whether such a three-valued semantics coincides with the existing ones.

We can also use our language to do epistemic logic on more general kinds of structures.
On the one hand, properties of local epistemic models clearly can be relaxed;
on the other hand, it is also interesting to consider

face-semantics
rather than
facet-semantics
, like the one studied in
[
8
]
,
or more general simplicial structures based on
semi-simplicial sets
, like the one studied in
[
6
]
.

The notion of intensional distributed knowledge also seems interesting.
Our results concerning such a notion of knowledge presented in this paper are rather primitive,
and understanding the behavior of such intensional distributed knowledge operators might help us better understand the structure of the definable groups in such models.

Finally, it should also be interesting to generalize the kind of assignment operators we introduced in this paper to study varying-domain first-order modal logic in general.
This might provide us with an interesting first-order modal logic alternative to free logic-based varying-domain first-order modal logic.

\mathcal{U}^{c}_{(\Gamma,\pi)},s,\sigma_{s}\vDash\phi\iff\phi\in\Delta_{s}

Proof A.16
.

As usual, the above Truth Lemma can be proved by induction on the structure of
\mathcal{L}^{[:\resizebox{195881}{0}{=}]}_{\mathbf{A}}
-formulas.
We only consider the case for
p_{x}
and
[x\!:\!\!\resizebox{234471}{0}{=}\,a]\phi
here, since the other cases are rather routine
(Only note that when proving the case for
\mathsf{K}_{X}\alpha
,
we need to make use of the fact that
\alpha
is a sentence:
the truth value of a sentence is irrelevant to the assignment, so we can change the assignment to apply IH.)

For
p_{x}
: Let
\sigma_{s}(x)=a
.
First, assume that
p_{x}\in\Delta
.
Then, since
(\Delta_{s},\sigma_{s})
is coherent,
by Lemma
A.4
,

\langle x\!:\!\!\resizebox{234471}{0}{=}\,a\rangle p_{x}\in\Delta_{s}
.
Then, by
\mathtt{DET}^{[:\resizebox{195881}{0}{=}]}
,
[x\!:\!\!\resizebox{234471}{0}{=}\,a]p_{x}\in\Delta_{s}
;
and we also have
\langle x\!:\!\!\resizebox{234471}{0}{=}\,a\rangle\top\in\Delta_{s}
.
Note that the latter implies that
a\in FV(\Delta_{s})
by coherence.
Then, by Lemma
A.4
,
p_{a}\in\Delta_{s}
,
so
a\in\rho^{c}(\Delta_{s},\sigma_{s})=\rho^{uc}(s)
.

Conversely, if
p_{x}\notin\Delta
, then
\neg p_{x}\in\Delta
.
Then, by the same reasoning as above, we have
\neg p_{a}\in\Delta_{s}
, so
p_{a}\notin\Delta
,
and thus
a\notin\rho^{uc}(s)
.

For
[x\!:\!\!\resizebox{234471}{0}{=}\,a]\phi
:
By
\mathtt{R}^{[:\resizebox{195881}{0}{=}]}
,
we may assume without loss of generality that
\phi[a/x]
is admissible.

First, assume that
[x\!:\!\!\resizebox{234471}{0}{=}\,a]\phi\in\Delta_{s}
.
Also assume that
a\in\delta^{uc}(s)=\mathbf{A}\cap FV(\Delta_{s})
.
Then, by Lemma
A.4
, we have
\phi[a/x]\in\Delta_{s}
.
Hence, by IH,
\mathcal{U}^{c}_{(\Gamma,\pi)},s,\sigma_{s}\vDash\phi[a/x]
,
and thus
\mathcal{U}^{c}_{(\Gamma,\pi)},s,\sigma_{s}[x\mapsto a]\vDash\phi
.

Conversely, assume that
[x\!:\!\!\resizebox{234471}{0}{=}\,a]\phi\notin\Delta_{s}
.
Then,
\langle x\!:\!\!\resizebox{234471}{0}{=}\,a\rangle\neg\phi\in\Delta_{s}
,
so
[x\!:\!\!\resizebox{234471}{0}{=}\,a]\neg\phi\in\Delta_{s}
by
\mathtt{DET}^{[:\resizebox{195881}{0}{=}]}
,
and also
\langle x\!:\!\!\resizebox{234471}{0}{=}\,a\rangle\top\in\Delta_{s}
.
By coherence, the latter implies that
a\in FV(\Delta_{s})
,
so by Lemma
A.4
,
\neg\phi[a/x]\in\Delta
,
and thus
\phi[a/x]\notin\Delta
.
Hence, by IH,
\mathcal{U}^{c}_{(\Gamma,\pi)},s,\sigma_{s}\not\vDash\phi[a/x]
,
and thus
\mathcal{U}^{c}_{(\Gamma,\pi)},s,\sigma_{s}[x\mapsto a]\not\vDash\phi
.

Now, with the help of the above lemmas,
we can easily prove the Completeness Theorem.

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Let
\Gamma_{0}
be an arbitrary consistent set of
\mathcal{L}^{[:\resizebox{176584}{0}{=}]}
formulas.
By Lemma
A.6
, there is
(\Gamma,\pi)\in W^{c}
s.t.
\Gamma_{0}\subseteq\Gamma
.
Then, by Lemma
A.15
,
we have
\mathcal{U}^{c}_{(\Gamma,\pi)},(\varepsilon,(\Gamma,\pi)),\pi\vDash\Gamma_{0}
, so
\Gamma_{0}
is satisfiable.
Thus,
\mathbf{LEL}^{[:\resizebox{176584}{0}{=}]}
is strongly complete w.r.t. the class of all local epistemic model.
Then, by Proposition
3.3
,
\mathbf{LEL}^{[:\resizebox{176584}{0}{=}]}
is also strongly complete w.r.t. the class of all
proper
local epistemic model.

\Box

,

.

.

4.4

When the formula is not
[x\!:\!\!\resizebox{218453}{0}{=}\,a]\chi
:

When the formula is

p_{y}

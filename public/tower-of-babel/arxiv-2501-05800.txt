Quantum Berezinian for the Twisted Super Yangian
Hongda Lin Yongjie Wang, Honglian Zhang

Abstract
Motivated by an open problem proposed in Molev’s book [ 28 , Section 2.16, Example 16] , we investigate the quantum Berezinian 𝔅 t ​ w ​ ( u ) \mathfrak{B}^{tw}(u) associated with the twisted super Yangian, which is a coideal sub-superalgebra of the super Yangian of the general linear Lie superalgebra. We provide an explicit formulation of 𝔅 t ​ w ​ ( u ) \mathfrak{B}^{tw}(u) , and we also construct the center of the twisted super Yangian. This construction enables us to define the special twisted super Yangian, which is isomorphic to the quotient of the twisted super Yangian by its center. Moreover, we demonstrate the quantum Sylvester theorem for both the generator matrix and the quantum Berezinian.

1
Introduction

The term “Yangian”, first introduced by Drinfeld
[
5
,
7
]
, is used to describe a significant class of quantum groups that are closed connected to the rational solutions of the quantum Yang-Baxter equation. It exhibits a profound integration into mathematical physics and quantum integrable systems, emerging as a prevalent topic in contemporary research.
In the context of a finite-dimensional classical simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}
, the Yangian
\mathrm{Y}(\mathfrak{a})
associated with
\mathfrak{a}
can be regarded as a deformation of the universal enveloping algebra
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{a}[x])
of the polynomial current Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}[x]
.

Yangian admits a presentation whose defining relations are expressed in a specific matrix form, originally introduced by Faddeev-Reshetikhin-Takhtajan (FRT)
[
8
]
. In the FRT formalism, the Yangian is realized through a generator matrix that satisfies a set of ternary relations, known as the R-matrix presentation. These relations endow the Yangian with a natural bialgebra structure, which can be further equipped with an antipode to form a Hopf algebra. In their work
[
3
]
, Brundan and Kleshchev developed the parabolic presentation for the Yangian of the general linear Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
, utilizing Gauss decomposition and quasi-determinants
[
11
]
of the FRT generators. This presentation has a close connection with the Drinfeld presentation, as described in
[
7
]
.

Twisted Yangian was introduced by Sklyanin
[
38
]
to investigate the boundary conditions of quantum integrable systems, and was named by Olshanskii. In
[
35
]
, the author defined the twisted Yangians associated to the symmetric pairs:

\displaystyle\textrm{AI}:\ (\mathfrak{gl}_{N},\mathfrak{o}_{N}),\qquad\textrm{AII}:\ (\mathfrak{gl}_{2n},\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}),

and used them to construct the representations of infinite-dimensional classical Lie algebras. Let
\theta
be an involutive automorphism over
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
. Each of these algebras specializes to the universal enveloping algebras of a twisted polynomial current Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}[x]^{\theta}
.

There are two methods for realizing a class of twisted Yangians
[
30
]
: one approach defines them as coideal subalgebras of the Yangian in terms of its comultiplication, while the other is based on a presentation generated by a series of current elements, along with the quaternary relations and the symmetry relations. This framework also applies to the twisted Yangians of AIII type
[
31
]
as well as those of BCD types
[
15
]
.

The representation theory of Yangians and twisted Yangians has been extensively studied for over thirty years. In
[
26
]
, Molev classified the irreducible highest weight representations of twisted Yangians associated with classical Lie algebras
\mathfrak{o}_{N}
and
\mathfrak{sp}_{N}
. He further applied the quantum Sylvester theorem to study the skew representation of twisted Yangian
\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}\right)
in
[
27
]
, which encompasses the classical results of evaluation representations of
\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}\right)
. More recently, a series of works
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
have constructed Drinfeld presentations of twisted Yangians for arbitrary quasi-split types, thereby enabling the study of character theories of twisted Yangians, analogous to that of Yangians
[
18
]
.

In the early 1990s, Nazarov
[
33
]
introduced a super version of the Yangian associated with the general linear Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}
, which is commonly known as the super Yangian, denoted by
\mathrm{Y}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}\right)
. Since then, the super Yangian
\mathrm{Y}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}\right)
has been extensively studied by numerous researchers. Zhang
[
42
]
developed finite-dimensional irreducible representation of
\mathrm{Y}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}\right)
connected to the standard Dynkin diagram. Additionally, the super Yangian
\mathrm{Y}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}^{\mathfrak{s}}\right)
at arbitrary parity sequences
\mathfrak{s}
has been linked by reflections
[
41
]
, with analogous results for non-conjugacy Dynkin diagrams established by
[
20
,
29
]
. In a separate work, Gow
[
12
,
13
]
presented a Gauss decomposition for
\mathrm{Y}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}\right)
, provided the structure of the center
\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Y}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}\right)
and explored its connection with the Drinfeld presentation of the special super Yangian
\mathrm{Y}\left(\textrm{A}(M-1,N-1)\right)
as presented by Stukopin
[
39
]
. Peng
[
36
,
37
]
derived the parabolic presentations of the super Yangian and extended them to
\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}
associated with arbitrary Borel subalgebras.

As a coideal of the super Yangian
\mathrm{Y}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}\right)
, the twisted super Yangians
\mathrm{Y}^{\pm}(M|N)
were defined in terms of a specific transposition by
[
1
,
2
]
. These superalgebras are deformations of the universal enveloping superalgebras
\mathrm{U}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}[x]\right)
. Briot-Ragoucy
[
2
]
demonstrated that the product
MN
cannot be odd for the superalgebras
\mathrm{Y}^{\pm}(M|N)
and the two classes of twisted super Yangians are isomorphic upon exchanging
M
and
N
.

It is well known that there is a close connection between the structures and representations of Yangians and quantum loop algebras. The latter is a significant class of Hopf algebras as quantum deformations of loop algebras. Drinfeld asserted
[
6
]
that, in a certain sense, Yangians are a limited form of quantum loop algebras. Until around 2012, comprehensive proofs of this statement were established by Gautam-Toledano Laredo
[
9
]
and Guay-Ma
[
14
]
, respectively. Subsequent to these proofs, analogous claims were also formulated for twisted cases
[
4
]
and for super cases
[
19
]
. Furthermore, in another significant paper by Gautam-Toledano Laredo
[
10
]
, the authors also established the equivalence between the finite-dimensional representations of Yangians and quantum loop algebras for symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebras.

Inspired by numerous pioneering researches
[
12
,
13
,
16
,
17
,
25
,
28
,
30
,
32
,
33
,
34
,
35
]
, the main objective of this paper is to investigate the central structure of the twisted super Yangian, as well as its super analogue of the Sklyanin determinant. Our comprehensive study not only delves into the fundamental definitions and properties of the twisted super Yangian, but also provides a precise explanation of the center and the quantum Berezinian.
Our endeavor potentially offers several future directions for the twisted super Yangian, involving Gauss decomposition, Drinfeld presentation, classification of irreducible representations, Gelfand-Tsetlin bases, and Harish-Chandra isomorphism, among others.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some fundamental concepts related to the super Yangian
\mathrm{Y}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}\right)
, its center
\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Y}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}\right)
, and the special super Yangian
\mathrm{Y}\left(\mathfrak{sl}_{M|N}\right)
. In Section
3
, we define a class of twisted super Yangians, which are associated with a nonsingular block matrix featuring a symmetric
(M\times M)
-submatrix in the upper-left block and a skew-symmetric
(N\times N)
-submatrix in the lower-right block. Additionally, we introduce a transposition of order 2, which yields an equivalent realization of the twisted super Yangian and generalizes the definition presented in
[
2
]
. In Section
4
, we investigate the graded algebra for the twisted super Yangian
\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
associated with the second filtration of the super Yangian. Based on this, we find a formal power series

\mathfrak{z}(u)\in 1+u^{-1}\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)\left[\left[u^{-1}\right]\right]

such that the coefficients of
\mathfrak{z}(u)
generate the center of
\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
. Furthermore, we introduce the super version of the special twisted Yangian, which is defined as a quotient of
\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
(
M\neq N
) by its center. In Section
5
, we examine the quantum Berezinian for the twisted super Yangian
\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
and establish the corresponding quantum Liouville formula. In the final section
6
, we demonstrate that a twisted super Yangian can be regarded as a quotient of an extended (associative) superalgebra by the ideal generated by central elements. This extended superalgebra is referred to as the extended twisted super Yangian. Finally, we formulate the quantum Sylvester theorem for the quantum Berezinian of the extended twisted super Yangian.

Super Yangian

2.1

Definition 2.1

\displaystyle R_{12}(u-v)T_{1}(u)T_{2}(v)=T_{2}(v)T_{1}(u)R_{12}(u-v),

\displaystyle R_{12}(u)R_{13}(u+v)R_{23}(v)=R_{23}(v)R_{13}(u+v)R_{12}(u).

(2.2)

The super Yangian

The comultiplication can be explicitly rewritten in terms of generating series as follows:

\displaystyle\Delta(t_{ij}(u))=\sum_{k\in I}(-1)^{(|i|+|k|)(|k|+|j|)}t_{ik}(u)\otimes t_{kj}(u).

The generator matrix

\displaystyle d_{i}(u),\quad e_{ij}(u),\quad f_{ji}(u)\in\mathrm{Y}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}\right)\left[\left[u^{-1}\right]\right]

\displaystyle T(u)=\left(1\otimes 1+\sum_{i<j}E_{ji}\otimes f_{ji}(u)\right)\left(\sum_{i\in I}E_{ii}\otimes d_{i}(u)\right)\left(1\otimes 1+\sum_{i<j}E_{ij}\otimes e_{ij}(u)\right)

\displaystyle=d_{i}(u)+\sum_{k<i}f_{ik}(u)d_{k}(u)e_{ki}(u),

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-homogeneous bases of
\mathfrak{a}_{+}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{-}
, respectively, such that

\displaystyle[f_{i}^{+},\,f_{j}^{-}]=\sum_{k=1}^{q}c_{ij}^{k}f_{k}^{-}\in\mathfrak{a}_{-},\text{ with }c_{ij}^{k}\in\mathbb{C},\quad 1\leqslant i\leqslant p,\ 1\leqslant j\leqslant q,

By
[
24
, Theorem 5.15]
, it is enough to demonstrate that the supersymmetric algebra
\mathfrak{S}\left(\mathfrak{a}[x]^{\theta}\right)
possesses no nonzero invariants under the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded adjoint action of
\mathfrak{a}_{+}\left[x^{2}\right]
.

Let
B\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{a}[x]^{\theta})
be an
\mathfrak{a}_{+}[x^{2}]
-invariant element. Suppose
d
is the maximal nonnegative integer such that

\displaystyle B=\sum_{\lambda}B_{\lambda}(f_{1}^{-}x^{d})^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots(f_{q}^{-}x^{d})^{\lambda_{q}},

where the
q
-tuple
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{q})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and the coefficients
B_{\lambda}
are the polynomials in
f_{j}^{-}x^{r}
for
1\leqslant j\leqslant q
,
0\leqslant r<d
and
f_{i}^{+}x^{s}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant p
,
s\geqslant 0
. Here each
\lambda_{k}
for
1\leqslant k\leqslant q
ranges over
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
except that if some
\lambda_{k}>1
, with the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading
|f_{k}^{-}|=\bar{1}
, put
B_{\lambda}=0
.

The selection of
B
leads to

\displaystyle\left[f_{i}^{+}x,\,B\right]=0\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leqslant i\leqslant p.

For simplicity, set

\displaystyle F_{\lambda}=(f_{1}^{-}x^{d})^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots(f_{q}^{-}x^{d})^{\lambda_{q}}.

If
|f_{j}^{-}|=\bar{1}
for some
j
, we have

\left[f_{i}^{+}x,\,(f_{j}^{-}x^{d})^{\lambda_{j}}\right]=\begin{cases}(-1)^{|f_{i}^{+}||f_{j}^{-}|}\sum_{k}f_{k}^{-}x^{d+1},&\lambda_{j}=1,\\
0,&\lambda_{j}=0.\end{cases}

Then

\displaystyle\left[f_{i}^{+}x,\,B\right]=

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}\left[f_{i}^{+}x,\,B_{\lambda}\right]F_{\lambda}+\sum_{\lambda}(-1)^{|f_{i}^{+}||B_{\lambda}|}B_{\lambda}\left[f_{i}^{+}x,\,F_{\lambda}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}\left[f_{i}^{+}x,\,B_{\lambda}\right]F_{\lambda}+\sum_{\lambda}(-1)^{|f_{i}^{+}||B_{\lambda}|}B_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{j=1}^{q}(-1)^{|f_{i}^{+}|(|f_{1}^{-}|+\cdots+|f_{j}^{-}|)}\lambda_{j}(f_{1}^{-}x^{d})^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots(f_{j}^{-}x^{d})^{\lambda_{q}-1}\cdots(f_{q}^{-}x^{d})^{\lambda_{q}}\sum_{k=1}^{q}c_{ij}^{k}f_{k}^{-}x^{d+1},

which forces the coefficients of every
f_{k}^{-}x^{d+1}
for
1\leqslant k\leqslant p
to be zero since
\left[f_{i}^{+}x,\,B\right]=0
, that is to say,

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}B_{\lambda}\sum_{j=1}^{q}(-1)^{|f_{i}^{+}|(|B_{\lambda}|+|f_{1}^{-}|+\cdots+|f_{j}^{-}|)}\lambda_{j}c_{ij}^{k}(f_{1}^{-}x^{d})^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots(f_{j}^{-}x^{d})^{\lambda_{q}-1}\cdots(f_{q}^{-}x^{d})^{\lambda_{q}}=0,

for
1\leqslant i\leqslant p
,
1\leqslant k\leqslant q
. Hence, for any nonnegative integer
q
-tuple
\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\lambda_{q}^{\prime})
, it is established that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{q}(-1)^{|f_{i}^{+}|(|B_{\lambda^{\prime}(j)}|+|f_{1}^{-}|+\cdots+|f_{j}^{-}|)}(\lambda_{j}^{\prime}+1)c_{ij}^{k}B_{\lambda^{\prime}(j)}=0,\quad 1\leqslant i\leqslant p,\ 1\leqslant k\leqslant q,

(4.1)

where
\lambda^{\prime}(j)
denotes the
q
-tuple
(\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\lambda_{j}^{\prime}+1,\ldots,\lambda_{q}^{\prime})
.
Here we use the well-known formula:

\displaystyle\left[f,\,a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{m}\right]=\sum_{i}(-1)^{|f|(|a_{1}|+\cdots+|a_{i-1}|)}a_{1}\cdots a_{i-1}\cdot\left[f,\,a_{i}\right]\cdot a_{i+1}\cdots a_{m}.

Fix
\lambda^{\prime}
. Denote

\displaystyle g_{j}^{-}=(-1)^{|f_{i}^{+}|(|B_{\lambda^{\prime}(j)}|+|f_{1}^{-}|+\cdots+|f_{j}^{-}|)}(\lambda_{j}^{\prime}+1)f_{j}^{-},\quad 1\leqslant j\leqslant q.

The set of all elements
g_{j}^{-}
also forms a basis of
\mathfrak{a}_{-}
. Pick
m_{1},\ldots,m_{q}
such that

\displaystyle\left[f_{i}^{+},\,\sum_{j=1}^{q}m_{j}g_{j}^{-}\right]=0\quad\text{with}\quad 1\leqslant i\leqslant p.

(4.2)

Since the centralizer of
\mathfrak{a}_{+}
in
\mathfrak{a}
is trivial, the system of linear equations (
4.2
) involving the variables
m_{1},\ldots,m_{q}
has only zero solution. Additionally, (
4.2
) is equivalent to the system of equations

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{q}(-1)^{|f_{i}^{+}|(|B_{\lambda^{\prime}(j)}|+|f_{1}^{-}|+\cdots+|f_{j}^{-}|)}(\lambda_{j}^{\prime}+1)c_{ij}^{k}b_{j}=0,\quad 1\leqslant i\leqslant p,\ 1\leqslant k\leqslant q,

Comparing with (
4.1
), we have
B_{\lambda^{\prime}(j)}=0
.

Therefore, the equality
B_{\lambda}=0
holds for all nonzero
\lambda
. Thus, the proof is complete.

∎

Now, we consider a specific twisted polynomial current Lie superalgebra.
Let

\displaystyle\theta:\ E_{ij}\mapsto-(-1)^{|i||j|+|i|}\theta_{i}\theta_{j}E_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}

be the involution of the general linear Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}
with order 2. The fixed sub-superalgebra under
\theta
coincides with the ortho-symplectic Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}:=\operatorname{span}\Big\{\,F_{ij}=E_{ij}-(-1)^{|i||j|+|i|}\theta_{i}\theta_{j}E_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}\,\Big|\,i,j\in I\,\Big\}.

Consider the twisted polynomial current Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}[x]^{\theta}
with
\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}\right)_{+}=\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}
.
Set

\displaystyle F_{ij}^{(r)}:=\left(E_{ij}+(-1)^{r}(-1)^{|i||j|+|i|}\theta_{i}\theta_{j}E_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}\right)x^{r-1},\quad i,j\in I,\ r\in\mathbb{N}.

Note that the set of all elements
F_{ij}^{(r)}
spans
\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}[x]^{\theta}
.

Given a supersymmetric block matrix
\mathcal{G}
. The superalgebra
\mathrm{Y}^{tw}_{\mathcal{G}}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
is a sub-superalgebra of
\mathrm{Y}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}\right)
. It inherits a filtration on
\mathrm{Y}^{tw}_{\mathcal{G}}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{tw}_{p}=\mathcal{F}_{p}\cap\mathrm{Y}^{tw}_{\mathcal{G}}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right),\quad\deg s_{ij}^{(r)}=r-1.

The graded algebra subject to this filtration is given by

\displaystyle\operatorname{gr}\mathrm{Y}^{tw}_{\mathcal{G}}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right):=\mathcal{F}^{tw}_{0}\oplus\left(\mathop{\bigoplus}\limits_{p=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{F}^{tw}_{p}/\mathcal{F}^{tw}_{p-1}\right).

Proposition 4.2
.

The graded algebra
\operatorname{gr}\mathrm{Y}^{tw}_{\mathcal{G}}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
is isomorphic to the universal enveloping superalgebra corresponding to
\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}[x]^{\theta}
for any supersymmetric block matrix
\mathcal{G}
, that is,

\operatorname{gr}\mathrm{Y}^{tw}_{\mathcal{G}}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)\cong\mathrm{U}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}[x]^{\theta}\right)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.3
, we only need to prove for
\mathcal{G}_{0}=\sum_{k}(-1)^{|k|}\theta_{k}E_{kk^{\prime}}
. If there is no ambiguity, we omit the subscript
\mathcal{G}
.
From Proposition
2.4
, the mapping

\displaystyle\overline{t}_{ij}^{(r)}\mapsto(-1)^{|i|}E_{ij}x^{r-1},\quad i,j\in I,\ r\in\mathbb{N},

forms an isomorphism between
\operatorname{gr}\mathrm{Y}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}\right)
and

\mathrm{U}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}[x]\right)
.

Let
\bar{s}_{ij}^{(r)}
denotes the image of
s_{ij}^{(r)}
in the
r
-th component of
\operatorname{gr}\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\big(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\big)
. The images of the elements
\overline{\mathfrak{s}}_{ij}^{(r)}
under the following superalgebra homomorphism sequence

\displaystyle\operatorname{gr}\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{gr}\mathrm{Y}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}\right)\rightarrow\mathrm{U}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}[x]\right)

are equal to
-(-1)^{|j|}\theta_{j}F_{j^{\prime}i}^{(r)}
,
which also spans
\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}[x]^{\theta}
.

∎

Corollary 4.3
.

The ortho-symplectic Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}
is isomorphic to the superalgebra spanned by

\sum_{k\in I}\left(E_{ik}g_{kj}+(-1)^{|i|}E_{jk}g_{ki}\right),\quad i,j\in I

for any supersymmetric block matrix
\mathcal{G}=(g_{ij})
.

4.2
Comments on the center
\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)

With the framework of the embedding map (
3.4
), we regard
\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\big(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\big)
as a sub-superalgebra of
\mathrm{Y}\big(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}\big)
.
We present the twisted analogue of the central formulas as stated in Proposition
2.5
. The following lemma will be utilized in Section

.

Lemma 4.4
.

The automorphism
\rho
in (
2.9
) acts on
z(u)
given by

\displaystyle\rho\left(z(u)\right)=z(-u-M+N)^{-1}.

Proof.

Applying the automorphism (
2.10
) to the both sides of (

), we get

\displaystyle\delta_{ij}z(u)^{-1}=\sum_{k}(-1)^{|i||k|+|j||k|+|k|+|i|}\tilde{t}_{jk}(u+M-N)t_{ki}(u).

Replacing
u
with
-u-M+N
, we obtain

\displaystyle\delta_{ij}z(-u-M+N)^{-1}=\sum_{k}(-1)^{|i||k|+|j||k|+|k|+|i|}\tilde{t}_{jk}(-u)t_{ki}(-u-M+N).

(4.3)

Then the lemma follows from the action of
\rho
on (
2.11
).

∎

Proposition 4.5
.

In the twisted super Yangian
\mathrm{Y}^{tw}(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N})
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k}(-1)^{|j||k|+|i||k|+|i|}\tilde{s}_{jk}(-u)s_{ki}(-u-M+N)=\delta_{ij}z(u)z(-u-M+N)^{-1},

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{k}(-1)^{|j||k|+|i||k|+|i|}s_{jk}(-u-M+N)\tilde{s}_{ki}(-u)=\delta_{ij}z(u)z(-u-M+N)^{-1}.

(4.5)

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(g_{ij})
be fixed as in (
3.4
). Set

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{-1}=\sum_{ij}E_{ij}\otimes\tilde{g}_{ij}

such that
\sum_{k}\tilde{g}_{ik}g_{kj}=\delta_{ij}
.

We compute only equation (
4.4
), while equation (
4.5
) can be verified using a similar approach. Using equation (
4.3
) and embedding (
3.4
), the left side of (
4.4
) is equal to

\displaystyle\quad\ \sum_{a,b}\sum_{c,d}\tilde{t}_{aj}(u)\tilde{g}_{ab}\bigg(\sum_{k}(-1)^{|k||b|+|k||c|+|k|+|c|}\tilde{t}_{bk}(-u)t_{kc}(-u-M+N)\bigg)g_{cd}t_{id}(u+M-N)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b,d}\tilde{t}_{aj}(u)\tilde{g}_{ab}g_{bd}t_{id}(u+M-N)z(-u-M+N)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\tilde{t}_{aj}(u)t_{ia}(u+M-N)z(-u-M+N)^{-1}=\delta_{ij}z(u)z(-u-M+N)^{-1}.

∎

Put
\mathfrak{z}(u)=z(u)z(-u-M+N)^{-1}
. It can be rewritten as a formal power series as follows

\displaystyle\mathfrak{z}(u)=1+\mathfrak{z}^{(1)}u^{-1}+\mathfrak{z}^{(2)}u^{-2}+\mathfrak{z}^{(3)}u^{-3}+\mathfrak{z}^{(4)}u^{-4}+\cdots\ \in\ \mathrm{Y}^{tw}\big(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\big)\big[\big[u^{-1}\big]\big].

Observe that

Theorem 4.6
.

The coefficients
\mathfrak{z}^{(3)}
,
\mathfrak{z}^{(4)}
,
\mathfrak{z}^{(5)}
,
\ldots
are generators of the center of the twisted super Yangian
\mathrm{Y}^{tw}(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N})
.

Proof.

Combining with Proposition
2.4
and Corollary
2.8
, the image of central elements
z^{(r)}
for
r\geqslant 2
in the universal enveloping superalgebra
\mathrm{U}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}[x]\right)
is equal to

\displaystyle-(r-1)\sum_{i\in I}E_{ii}x^{r-1}.

Then by Proposition
4.2
, it follows that the image of the elements
\mathfrak{z}^{(2m+1)}
for
m\geqslant 1
in the universal enveloping superalgebra
\mathrm{U}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}[x]^{\theta}\right)
is given by

\displaystyle y(m)\cdot x^{2m-1}\cdot E,

where
E
denotes the identity matrix
E=\sum_{i\in I}E_{ii}
and
y(m)
is a finite sum related to
m
. Moreover, the image of the elements
\mathfrak{z}^{(2m+2)}
for
m\geqslant 1
in
\operatorname{gr}\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
can be generated by
\mathfrak{z}^{(2m+1)}
. Hence, it suffices to show that the center of
\mathrm{U}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}[x]^{\theta}\right)
is generated by
x\cdot E
,
x^{3}\cdot E
,
x^{5}\cdot E
,
\ldots

Let
\mathfrak{a}[x]^{\theta}
be the quotient of
\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}[x]^{\theta}
by the ideal generated by

4.1
.
∎

4.3
Special twisted super Yangian

Regard
\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
as a sub-superalgebra of
\mathrm{Y}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}\right)
under the embedding (
3.4
). We can also define a ”special” version of the twisted super Yangian as in
\mathrm{Y}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}\right)
.

Definition 4.7
.

The special twisted super Yangian
\mathrm{SY}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
is the sub-superalgebra of
\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
determined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{SY}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)=\mathrm{Y}\left(\mathfrak{sl}_{M|N}\right)\cap\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right).

Since the map

\displaystyle\mu_{f}^{tw}:\ S(u)\mapsto f(u)S(u)

(4.6)

also defines an automorphism of
\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
for all
f(u)\in 1+u^{-1}\mathbb{C}\left[\left[u^{-1}\right]\right]
, the special twisted super Yangian has an equivalent definition.

Definition 4.8
.

The special twisted super Yangian
\mathrm{SY}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
is the sub-superalgebra of
\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
, which consists of elements preserved by all automorphisms (
4.6
). That is,

\mathrm{SY}^{tw}(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}):=\left\{y\in\mathrm{Y}^{tw}(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N})|\ \mu_{f}^{tw}(y)=y\text{ for all }f\right\}.

Proposition 4.9
.

For
M\neq N
, the superalgebra
\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
has the following decomposition

\displaystyle\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)=\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)\otimes\mathrm{SY}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right).

(4.7)

Proof.

This proof is very similar to
[
28
, Theorem 2.9.2]
(or see
[
30
, Proposition 2.16]
). Since the second filtration mentioned in Section
2.1
preserves the grading of generators, all coefficients of
z(u)
are even elements. Therefore, the center
\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Y}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}\right)
is an associative commutative algebra.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an arbitrary associative commutative algebra. Due to
[
30
, Proposition 2.15]
,
for any series
a(u)\in 1+u^{-1}\mathcal{A}\left[\left[u^{-1}\right]\right]
, there is a unique series
\mathring{a}(u)\in 1+u^{-1}\mathcal{A}\left[\left[u^{-1}\right]\right]
such that

\displaystyle a(u)=\mathring{a}(u)\mathring{a}(u-1)\cdots\mathring{a}(u-K+1)

for any
K\in\mathbb{N}
.

If
M>N
, the statement above immediately implies that there is a unique series

\mathring{B}(u)\in 1+u^{-1}\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Y}\big(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}\big)\left[\left[u^{-1}\right]\right]

such that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{M|N}(u)=\mathring{B}(u+M-N-1)\cdots\mathring{B}(u).

By (
2.13
), we have

\displaystyle z(u)=\mathring{B}(u+M-N)\mathring{B}(u)^{-1}.

Following from (
2.11
), the automorphism
\mu_{f}
in (
2.3
) maps
\mathring{B}(u)
to
f(u)\mathring{B}(u)
.
Thus, the coefficients of

\displaystyle\mathring{t}_{ij}(u)=\mathring{B}(u)^{-1}t_{ij}(u)\in\mathrm{Y}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}\right)\big[\big[u^{-1}\big]\big].

are invariant under the automorphism
\mu_{f}
, which forces
\mathring{t}_{ij}(u)\in\mathrm{Y}(\mathfrak{sl}_{M|N})\big[\big[u^{-1}\big]\big]
.

Set

\displaystyle\mathring{s}_{ij}(u)=\mathring{B}^{-1}(u)\mathring{B}^{-1}(-u)s_{ij}(u)\in\mathrm{Y}^{tw}(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N})\big[\big[u^{-1}\big]\big].

This implies

\displaystyle\mathring{s}_{ij}(u)=\sum_{k,l\in I}(-1)^{|i||j|+|i||k|+|j|+|k|}g_{kl}\mathring{t}_{ik}(u)\mathring{t}_{jl}(-u),

by Proposition
3.4
. Since

\displaystyle\mathfrak{z}(u)

\displaystyle=z(u)z(-u-M+N)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mathring{B}^{-1}(u)\mathring{B}^{-1}(-u)\mathring{B}(u+M-N)\mathring{B}(-u-M+N),

all coefficients of
\mathring{B}(u)\mathring{B}(-u)
are included in the center
\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\big(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\big)
.
The coefficients of
\mathring{s}_{ij}(u)
are also invariant under any automorphisms
\mu_{g}^{tw}
for
g(u)=f(u)f(-u)
in (
4.6
).
Hence, we have the following decomposition

\displaystyle\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)=\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)\mathrm{SY}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right).

(4.8)

In the case of
N<M
, we can take

\mathring{B}^{\prime}(u)\in 1+u^{-1}\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Y}\big(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}\big)\left[\left[u^{-1}\right]\right]

such that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{M|N}(u)=\mathring{B}^{\prime}(-u+1)\cdots\mathring{B}^{\prime}(-u+N-M).

Select
\mathring{t}^{\prime}_{ij}(u)=\mathring{B}^{\prime}(-u)t_{ij}(u)
and
\mathring{s}^{\prime}_{ij}(u)=\mathring{B}^{\prime}(u)\mathring{B}^{\prime}(-u)s_{ij}(u)
to replace
\mathring{t}_{ij}(u)
and
\mathring{s}_{ij}(u)
, respectively. Then,
we also have (
4.8
).

Moreover, the inclusions

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)\subset\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Y}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}\right),\quad\mathrm{SY}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)\subset\mathrm{Y}\left(\mathfrak{sl}_{M|N}\right)

lead to (
4.7
).

∎

It is clear that the center
\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
is an associative commutative algebra. Moreover, we also have the following

Corollary 4.10
.

For
M\neq N
, the superalgebra
\mathrm{SY}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
is isomorphic to the quotient of
\mathrm{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
by the ideal generated by
\mathfrak{z}^{(3)}
,
\mathfrak{z}^{(4)}
,
\mathfrak{z}^{(5)}
,
\ldots
.

Corollary 4.11
.

For
M\neq N
, the superalgebra
\mathrm{SY}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
can be regarded as a left coideal sub-superalgebra of

under the comultiplication

.

5.1
Definition of quantum Berezinian in twisted type

Define a homomorphism
\pi_{m}
from the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
to the
m
-tensor product algebra
\left(\operatorname{End}V\right)^{\otimes m}
such that

\displaystyle\sigma_{ij}=(ij)\mapsto P_{i,j},\quad\text{for }1\leqslant i<j\leqslant m.

Denote by
G^{(m)}
and
H^{(m)}
the respective images of the anti-symmetrizer and symmetrizer:

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\cdot\sigma\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}}\sigma

under the action of
\pi_{m}
. Observe that for
m\geqslant 2
,

\displaystyle G^{(m)}=\left(1-P_{1,m}-\cdots-P_{1,2}\right)\cdots\left(1-P_{m-2,m}-P_{m-2,m-1}\right)\left(1-P_{m-1,m}\right),

\displaystyle H^{(m)}=\left(1+P_{1,m}+\cdots+P_{1,2}\right)\cdots\left(1+P_{m-2,m}+P_{m-2,m-1}\right)\left(1+P_{m-1,m}\right).

Set

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i,i+1},\quad\epsilon_{i}=(-1)^{|i||i+1|}.

We define the function
\epsilon
on
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
by

\displaystyle\epsilon(\sigma)=\epsilon_{i_{1}}\epsilon_{i_{2}}\cdots\epsilon_{i_{k}}\quad\text{for}\quad\sigma=\sigma_{i_{1}}\sigma_{i_{2}}\cdots\sigma_{i_{k}}\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}.

It is easy to see that
\epsilon(\sigma)=\operatorname{sgn}\sigma
for each permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

In
V^{\otimes(M+N)}
, we have

\displaystyle G^{(m)}(e_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{m}})=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}}\epsilon(\sigma)\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)(e_{i_{\sigma(1)}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{\sigma(m)}}),

\displaystyle H^{(m)}(e_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{m}})=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}}\epsilon(\sigma)(e_{i_{\sigma(1)}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{\sigma(m)}}).

Proof.

This lemma can be proved by induction on
m
.

∎

Corollary 5.2
.

If
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,M\}
, then

\displaystyle G^{(m)}(e_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{m}})=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\cdot(e_{i_{\sigma(1)}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{\sigma(m)}}).

If

Consider an index set

for

on an ordered pair

\begin{aligned} (k_{a},k_{b})\quad&\mapsto\quad(k_{b},k_{a}),\\
(k_{a},k_{p})\quad&\mapsto\quad(k_{p-1},k_{a}),\\

(k_{p-1},k_{p})\quad&\mapsto\quad(k_{p-1},k_{p-2}),\\
(k_{p},k_{p-1})\quad&\mapsto\quad(k_{p-1},k_{p-2}).\end{aligned}\quad\begin{aligned} &\text{if }~~a,b<p,\\
&\text{if }~~a<p-1,\\
&\text{if }~~b<p-1,\\
&\\
&\end{aligned}

For the special case

such that it maps both
(k_{1},k_{2})

(k_{2},k_{1})
onto the element

\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{p}\}

is the permutation

(k_{b_{2}},k_{b_{p-2}})

, which is defined on the set of ordered pairs of elements obtained from

and

6.1
Extended twisted super Yangian

Definition 6.1
.

The
extended twisted super Yangian

\mathcal{X}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
is an associative superalgebra generated by coefficients of the formal power series

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}_{ij}(u)=\delta_{ij}+\sum_{r>0}\mathfrak{s}_{ij}^{(r)}u^{-r},\qquad i,j\in I,

Lemma 6.2
.

There exists a formal power series

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u)\in 1+u^{-1}\mathcal{X}^{tw}(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N})\left[\left[u^{-1}\right]\right]

with coefficients in the center of
\mathcal{X}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u)P^{\iota}:=\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{2}(-u)R(2u)\mathcal{S}_{1}(u)P^{\iota}=P^{\iota}\mathcal{S}_{1}(u)R(2u)\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{2}(-u),

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(u)=\mathcal{S}(u)^{-1}
.

Proof.

Multiply the quaternary relation (
3.6
) by
\mathcal{S}_{2}(v)^{-1}
on both sides and by
u+v
on the left to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}(v)^{-1}R(u-v)\mathcal{S}_{1}(u)\left(u+v+P^{\iota}\right)=\left(u+v+P^{\iota}\right)\mathcal{S}_{1}(u)R(u-v)\mathcal{S}_{2}(v)^{-1}.

in the equation above, we get the second relation of (
6.1
). Since the action of

on

By utilizing the argument stated in

Theorem 6.3
.

The twisted super Yangian
\mathcal{Y}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
is isomorphic to the quotient of
\mathcal{X}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
by the ideal generated by the coefficients of
\mathcal{E}(u)-1+\frac{1}{2u}
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}_{ij}(u)
be the
(i,j)
-th entry of the inverse matrix
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(u)=\mathcal{S}(u)^{-1}
.
It suffices to show that the equation

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u)=1-\frac{1}{2u}

is equivalent to the supersymmetric relation (
3.7
). Indeed, apply the operators

and

to the basis vector

for

, we have the equation

\displaystyle\delta_{k^{\prime}j}\theta_{k^{\prime}}\mathcal{E}(u)w=\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i||j|+|i|+|j|}\left((-1)^{|k|}\theta_{i}\mathfrak{s}_{i^{\prime}k}(u)\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}_{ij}(-u)-\frac{1}{2u}(-1)^{|i||k|}\theta_{k^{\prime}}\mathfrak{s}_{k^{\prime}i}(u)\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}_{ij}(-u)\right)w,

and replace

to get

\displaystyle\delta_{kj}\mathcal{E}(u)w=\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{|i||j|+|k||j|+|i|}\left((-1)^{|k|}\theta_{i}\theta_{k}\mathfrak{s}_{i^{\prime}k^{\prime}}(u)\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}_{ij}(-u)-\frac{1}{2u}(-1)^{|i||k|}\mathfrak{s}_{ki}(u)\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}_{ij}(-u)\right)w.

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u)=\left(\mathcal{S}^{\iota}(u)-\frac{1}{2u}\mathcal{S}(u)\right)\mathcal{S}(-u)^{-1}.

Obvious,

is equivalent to (

∎

By the defining relation (

Proposition 6.8

For any

\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
\mathfrak{s}_{K1}(u)&\ldots&\mathfrak{s}_{KK}(u)&\mathfrak{s}_{K,K+j}(u)\\
\mathfrak{s}_{K+i,1}(u)&\ldots&\mathfrak{s}_{K+i,K}(u)&\boxed{\mathfrak{s}_{K+i,K+j}(u)}\\
\end{array}\right|.

(6.6)

Proof.

According to the statements in

\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\

Let

\begin{aligned} &A(u)=\left(\mathfrak{s}_{ij}(u)\right)_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant K},\\

&D(u)=\left(\mathfrak{s}_{ij}(u)\right)_{K+1\leqslant i,j\leqslant K+M+N}.\end{aligned}

By its definition, the homomorphism
\psi_{K}
maps

\left(D(u^{\prime})-C(u^{\prime})A^{-1}(u^{\prime})B(u^{\prime})\right)^{-1}\quad\text{with}\quad u^{\prime}=-u-\frac{M+K-N}{2}.

\displaystyle\psi_{K}:\ \mathcal{S}\left(u+\frac{K}{2}\right)\mapsto D(u)-C(u)A^{-1}(u)B(u).

Taking the
(i,j)
-th entry and using Definition

, we immediately get (

∎

For any even integers
L\geqslant 0
, there is a natural homomorphism
\eta_{L}
from
\mathcal{X}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)
to
\mathcal{X}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|L+N}\right)
defined by

\mathfrak{s}_{ij}(u)\mapsto\mathfrak{s}_{ij}(u),\qquad 1\leqslant i,j\leqslant M+N.

Note that the homomorphism
\psi_{K}
maps
\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}_{ij}(u+\frac{K}{2})
to
\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}_{K+i,K+j}(u)
, hence, the image of
\psi_{K}
in
\mathcal{X}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{K+M|L+N}\right)
commutes with the sub-superalgebra generated by all
\eta_{L}\big(\mathfrak{s}_{ab}(v)\big)
for each
L
where
\mathfrak{s}_{ab}(v)\in\mathcal{X}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{K+M|N}\right)
and
1\leqslant a,b\leqslant K
. Specially, the image

\psi_{K}\left(\mathcal{X}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|N}\right)\right)

commutes with the sub-superalgebra of
\mathcal{X}^{tw}\left(\mathfrak{osp}_{K+M|N}\right)
generated by the set
\big\{\,\mathfrak{s}_{ab}^{(r)}\,\big|\,1\leqslant a,b\leqslant K,\ r>0\,\big\}
.

Corollary 6.9
.

For any integers
K_{1},K_{2}\geqslant 0
, we have

\displaystyle\psi_{K_{1}}\circ\psi_{K_{2}}=\psi_{K_{1}+K_{2}}=\psi_{K_{2}}\circ\psi_{K_{1}}.

Now we have the following Sylvester theorem for quantum Berezinian over the extended twisted super Yangians.

Theorem 6.10
.

It holds for
K\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle\psi_{K}\left(\mathfrak{B}^{tw}_{M|N}(u)\right)=\mathfrak{B}^{tw}_{K+M|N}\left(u+\frac{3K}{2}\right)\cdot\left(\mathcal{S}_{1\cdots K}^{1\cdots K}\left(u+\frac{3K}{2}\right)\right)^{-1}.

Proof.

This is an immediately conclusion of Lemma
6.6
and the definition of
\psi_{K}
.

Integrating NMR and machine learning for pore-type driven rock classification in the heterogeneous Asmari carbonate reservoirs
Authors: Maryam Veysi, Ali Kadkhodaie, Mehran Arian, Mohsen Aleali, Zahra Maleki, Pooria Kianoush
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The Asmari Formation’s complex heterogeneity presents fundamental challenges for reservoir characterization, where conventional lithology-based methods inadequately capture dynamic fluid behavior and pore-scale productivity controls. This study develops an integrated NMR-electrofacies framework combining Combinable Magnetic Resonance (CMR) logging with explainable machine learning to overcome such limitations. High-resolution CMR data were analyzed to quantify pore structure characteristics, fluid mobility parameters, and permeability indicators, with electrofacies classification performed using optimized machine learning algorithms incorporating SHAP interpretability. Three key innovations are presented: (1) a novel pore-connectivity index derived from T₂ distribution analysis, (2) dynamic permeability corrections accounting for clay-bound fluid effects, and (3) a lithology-independent rock typing system based on pore functionality. Results demonstrate that intervals with similar porosity (11% average) exhibit 3–5× variations in hydrocarbon transmissibility, confirming pore architecture’s dominant control. Notably, sandstone subunits representing just 15–20% of thickness contributed 60% of productive capacity through superior macroporosity (T₂ >100 ms). The machine learning framework achieved 92% prediction accuracy for flow units, with SHAP analysis identifying T₂ geometric mean (47% contribution) as the primary controlling parameter. These findings necessitate recharacterization of the Asmari Formation as a pore-connectivity governed system rather than lithology-dependent. Validation through comprehensive core-log integration (R²=0.89, RMSE = 0.32) and production data verification (84% PLT agreement) confirms method reliability. Practical applications include optimized well completion strategies and enhanced recovery in mature fields, while future adaptations encompass real-time implementation and CO₂ storage potential evaluation in analogous carbonate reservoirs. Introduction

The pursuit of sustainable energy production requires a profound understanding of reservoir heterogeneity and rock fabric variability to maximize hydrocarbon recovery1,2. The Asmari Formation, among Iran’s most prolific carbonate reservoirs, exhibits complex diagenetically altered lithofacies, diverse depositional environments, and intricate porosity structures that challenge conventional characterization methods3–6. Traditional rock-typing approaches, while fundamental, often fail to capture dynamic pore geometry-fluid behavior interactions, particularly in carbonates with multi-scale heterogeneity7–9.

The theoretical foundation of this research builds upon established pore system classifications10–12, while introducing critical adaptations for dynamic reservoir conditions. The methodology advances predictive modeling through NMR petrophysics and explainable machine learning integration, demonstrating significant improvements over conventional composition-based approaches13,14. These developments prove particularly valuable for mature fields where legacy data interpretations frequently overlook dynamic reservoir behavior and where precise flow unit identification is essential for recovery optimization.

While conventional petrophysical logs provide valuable volumetric and textural insights, they often cannot resolve complete pore architectures governing flow capacity9,15, particularly in formations with complex wettability16. Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) logging offers superior microstructural characterization through direct quantification of pore throat distributions and fluid mobility via T₂ relaxation measurements. Recent advances in NMR-seismic integration have enhanced fluid typing capabilities, though carbonate-specific resolution remains limited17–20. NMR technology enables rock type differentiation based on pore system characteristics rather than bulk lithology, providing quantitative assessment of pore size distribution and permeability without lithologic assumptions21–23.

In mature hydrocarbon provinces such as the Dezful Embayment, where decades of production have altered pressure regimes and fluid distributions within reservoir systems, conventional petrophysical interpretations often depend on outdated static models that cannot account for evolving pore structures and wettability contrasts24–26. This limitation necessitates a paradigm shift toward dynamic rock typing approaches where reservoirs are evaluated not as homogeneous entities but as spatiotemporal continua of evolving petrophysical properties. The Asmari Formation, characterized by its multi-scale heterogeneity arising from cyclic shallow marine carbonate sedimentation interspersed with siliciclastic incursions and diagenetic overprints, serves as an ideal case study for implementing such innovative approaches.

The fundamental challenge lies in quantifying subtle yet critically important variations in pore architecture across different facies - an endeavor for which NMR logging proves indispensable27,28. Unlike conventional porosity logs that provide only bulk averages, NMR technology enables discrimination of porosity into distinct size classes (micro-, meso-, and macro-pores), thereby revealing the true nature of fluid flow networks that govern both permeability and storage potential29,30. These capabilities become particularly valuable when evaluating mixed carbonate-clastic systems where traditional log interpretation methods struggle to distinguish between matrix effects and true reservoir quality indicators.

By analyzing T₂ distribution signatures from NMR data, this study establishes a reservoir-aware classification of rock types that simultaneously captures depositional signatures, diagenetic imprints, and pore connectivity within a unified model. This model achieves geological realism through integration with stratigraphic stacking patterns, electrofacies analysis, and core-log calibrations - effectively transforming abstract log responses into geologically consistent, production-relevant insights. The workflow holds particular significance for reservoirs approaching secondary recovery phases, where accurate flow unit delineation and optimized stimulation strategies become increasingly critical for maintaining production efficiency.

The geological and petrophysical complexity of the Asmari Formation has been extensively documented in regional studies. Gharechelou, et al31,32. demonstrated the superior hydrocarbon potential of sandstone-rich subunits within the predominantly carbonate sequence, while Honarmand and Amini33 established important relationships between flow units, facies distribution and diagenetic features34,35. Recent work by Saffari and Kianoush36 provided comprehensive petrophysical benchmarks for Zagros reservoirs, highlighting the need for improved pore-scale characterization methods. Recent technological advances in NMR applications37,38 and machine learning implementations39,40 have created new opportunities to overcome longstanding limitations in carbonate reservoir characterization.

Notably, Varkouhi, et al41,42. investigated pore water typing in Japanese reservoirs using NMR and machine learning, demonstrating the critical role of fluid mobility in pore system classification. Their work highlighted the limitations of traditional lithology-based methods in capturing dynamic fluid behavior, strongly supporting the pore type-based approach developed in this study. Similarly, Eftekhari, et al43,44. applied electrofacies and rock typing in the Mansouri Oilfield (SW Iran), emphasizing the need for high-resolution pore-scale diagnostics in heterogeneous carbonate-clastic systems. Their findings revealed that conventional facies models often overlook sandstone-rich intervals with superior flow potential - a key gap addressed through NMR-derived pore architecture analysis in the current research.

The conceptual innovation of this research lies in treating rock types as dynamic, pore-structured systems rather than static lithofacies labels. Methodologically, this represents the first integration of explainable AI (SHAP) with NMR petrophysics for transparent electrofacies classification, coupled with empirical demonstration of sandstone subunits as critical flow conduits—fundamentally challenging traditional carbonate-centric reservoir models. By integrating NMR-derived pore-scale insights with conventional logs, geological data, and core-calibrated benchmarks, a transformative framework is developed for identifying functional rock units directly tied to reservoir quality, zonation, and development strategy. Through application of digital log modeling, multi-scale data fusion, and sedimentological correlation, this study improves resolution of reservoir heterogeneity while enabling more predictive and tailored decision-making in field development.

The practical importance of this research is underscored by its implications for reservoir simulation, enhanced recovery planning, and geostatistical modeling. Reliable prediction of production potential depends fundamentally on precise classification of rock types - particularly in aging fields where dynamic reservoir behavior may diverge from initial models. By applying this NMR-guided methodology to such a complex and economically significant field, the study contributes to both theoretical advancement and operational optimization in carbonate reservoir management.

Reservoir formation of interest: the Asmari carbonate system

The Asmari Formation is the youngest and most prolific carbonate reservoir in the Zagros Fold and Thrust Belt, serving as the primary hydrocarbon-bearing unit in numerous onshore oilfields of southwestern Iran37,58,59. Lithologically, the formation consists of platform carbonates (biomicrites, dolomitized limestones) with minor argillaceous layers, shale seams, and sandy carbonates55,60,61. Regionally, the Asmari exhibits considerable heterogeneity, including the Ahvaz Sandstone Member in the southwest and the Kalhur Evaporite Member in the Lurestan region62,63. A basal anhydrite unit further complicates the stratigraphy in fields like Masjed Soleyman, Haftgel, and Pars64,65.

Chrono-stratigraphically, the Asmari spans the late Oligocene (Rupelian-Chattian) to early Miocene (Aquitanian-Burdigalian), representing a major transgressive-regressive cycle driven by regional tectonics and eustatic sea-level changes48,53,56. These complexities result in highly variable pore fabrics (interparticle, moldic, fracture) that are strongly influenced by diagenetic processes such as cementation, dolomitization, and pressure solution66–68. As a classic example of a multiscale, diagenetically modified carbonate reservoir32,33,69, the Asmari Formation is an ideal subject for pore-scale studies using NMR logging to link depositional features, diagenetic overprints, and dynamic fluid flow behavior.

Study area

The study area located approximately 52 km northeast of Dehluran in Ilam Province, occupies a strategic position within the central sector of Iran’s prolific Dezful Embayment. The field was discovered in 1967 following exploratory drilling initiatives that targeted the Asmari Formation8,31.

To date, twelve wells have been drilled across the structure, with one additional well currently under drilling to further delineate the reservoir extent and productivity. The field demonstrates a production capacity exceeding 81,000 barrels of oil per day, with a current operational output of over 66,000 barrels per day. Commercial production commenced in 1977, and since then, crude oil extracted from the reservoir has been processed. Following separation and treatment, the crude is transported via a dedicated 153-kilometer pipeline to the Ahvaz-3 Production Unit for export or downstream utilization12,56.

Ongoing expansion efforts, including the proposed drilling of eight additional wells by 2025, are aimed at sustaining or potentially increasing production to nominal capacity levels. Development strategies were informed by ongoing geomechanical studies, reservoir simulation, and enhanced data integration—of which the present study is a significant component. The geospatial positioning of the field and its neighboring reservoirs is illustrated in Fig. 1, while the structural framework and terrain attributes derived from satellite imagery are detailed in Fig. 2. The study area provides an ideal laboratory for advanced reservoir studies owing to its accessible data infrastructure, active production history, and well-preserved core and log records. These attributes render it especially well-suited for pore-scale reservoir characterization using NMR log data, as pursued in the current research50,70.

Methodology

Data acquisition and preprocessing

The empirical foundation incorporated multiple data streams71–74: conventional wireline logs (gamma ray, density, neutron, sonic, and resistivity) provided baseline petrophysical parameters; NMR logs enabled T₂ relaxation analysis for pore-scale characterization; and core measurements from 32 plugs served as calibration benchmarks. Geological and stratigraphic context was established through well reports and regional studies50,75,76.

All data processing and analysis were conducted using the Geolog software environment, beginning with essential preprocessing steps including depth alignment via stratigraphic markers, environmental corrections for borehole effects, and rigorous quality control excluding intervals with signal-to-noise ratios below 3:1 or caliper readings exceeding 12 inches. Feature selection identified 17 predictive parameters through mutual information scoring (> 0.3), including NMR-derived metrics (T₂ geometric mean, bound/free fluid volumes, pore connectivity index), conventional log measurements, and core-calibrated permeability values, all normalized using z-score standardization.

The complete workflow, illustrated in Fig. 3, progresses systematically from raw data acquisition through to reservoir quality prediction. Particular attention was given to the NMR data processing chain, where multiple inversion algorithms were evaluated to optimize T₂ distribution reconstruction, ultimately selecting singular value decomposition (SVD) for its superior performance in heterogeneous carbonate intervals. The complete initial feature set considered for machine learning is provided in Supplementary Table 5.

Conventional log analysis for lithological context

To provide a lithological framework for NMR pore-type integration, we employed neutron-density and M-N crossplot analyses94–96. Matrix lines for standard lithologies were calibrated and overlaid on environmentally corrected crossplots (Fig. 5). The neutron-density analysis revealed the formation’s inherent heterogeneity, with data clusters indicating carbonate-dominant intervals interspersed with sandstone-rich zones and localized anhydrite.

Machine learning methodology

The integration of machine learning (ML) techniques - including Random Forest (RF), Support Vector Machines (SVM), and SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) - provides a transformative approach for refining the pore type-based rock typing framework. These methods analyze NMR-derived pore system attributes, electrofacies classification, and petrophysical parameters, enabling data-driven validation of the hybrid NMR-electrofacies model7,76,93,98.

The machine learning framework follows a structured workflow (Fig. 4) that integrates multi-scale data sources and maintains geological consistency throughout the classification process. This systematic approach ensures transparent model development from feature selection to interpretable prediction output.

Pore system characterization from NMR analysis

In this study, T₂ data were acquired throughout the reservoir interval of the studied oilfield. Rigorous quality control protocols were applied to the raw CMR data prior to interpretation. These steps ensured that the derived T₂ distributions accurately reflected formation-level porosity and fluid partitioning.

The Bound Fluid Volume (BFV) and its volumetric spectrum—expressed as the Spectral Bound Fluid Volume (SBFV)—were derived from CMR T₂ distribution analysis across the depth profile. These parameters were computed using appropriate T₂ cutoff values calibrated to local core data and mineralogy. The results revealed average BFV and SBFV values of 3.9% and 2.2%, respectively, across the Asmari interval, highlighting zones of low-permeability facies, particularly in fine-grained dolomitic units and shale-dominated laminations.

Figure 7 presents the processed CMR log data, illustrating multiple tracks of key reservoir parameters, including total and effective porosity, free and bound fluid volumes, and CMR-derived permeability estimates. The spatial variation of T₂ spectra along depth intervals provides insights into the vertical architecture of the pore system, revealing zones of micro-, meso-, and macro-porosity that directly inform the rock typing workflow.

The processed CMR data delineated three distinct pore architecture regimes across the Asmari Formation (Fig. 8). Microporous zones (T₂ < 10 ms), comprising 18–23% of total porosity, were predominantly associated with dense micritic intervals, exhibiting bound fluid volumes (BFV) exceeding 85% of total porosity. Mesoporous zones (10 ≤ T₂ ≤ 100 ms) dominated the productive intervals, accounting for 62% of hydrocarbon-bearing zones and showing strong correlation with dolomitized units (R² = 0.79), with an average permeability of 8.7 mD (range: 1.2–24.3 mD). Macroporous zones (T₂ > 100 ms) were concentrated in sandstone subunits (Fig. 7), representing only 15% of total porosity but contributing 73% of total flow capacity, consistently demonstrating pore connectivity indices (PCI) greater than 1.8. Core-log integration confirmed these NMR-derived classifications, with vuggy macroporosity at 3564 m showing excellent agreement between NMR predictions and core photographs (Table 3; Supplementary Tables 2 and 3).

Permeability Estimation through NMR-integrated petrophysics

The CMR-based approach integrates porosity, fluid mobility, and pore size distribution into a coherent framework for dynamic flow assessment. Permeability (k), defined as the rock’s capacity to transmit fluids, is intimately related to the average size, distribution, and connectivity of its pore throats. In carbonate reservoirs—especially those with diagenetically altered textures like the Asmari Formation—these factors vary vertically and laterally, necessitating a resolution-rich, physics-based estimation method. This study employs two widely accepted NMR-derived permeability models:

Permeability Estimation via the Ktim model

To complement the KSDR permeability model, the Ktim model was applied, utilizing lithology-specific T₂ cutoff times to partition the T₂ relaxation spectrum. This approach distinguishes free and bound fluid components based on pore connectivity and capillary retention, independent of hydrocarbon presence94,102.

In the Ktim method, fluid classification hinges on predefined T₂ cutoffs—set at 33 ms for sandstone and 100 ms for carbonate formations. Signals with T₂ values above these thresholds are attributed to Free Fluid Index (FFI), representing moveable fluids residing in macro- and mesopores, while those below correspond to the Bulk Volume Irreducible (BVI), indicative of capillary-bound fluids retained in micropores, often due to surface tension effects or mineral-fluid interactions.

This fluid distribution anomaly manifests most prominently in the 3590–3709 m interval, where favorable porosity-permeability characteristics coincide with elevated water saturations. Multiple mechanisms may explain this phenomenon, including capillary entrapment in microporous networks, structural positioning within the hydrocarbon column, or the presence of bimodal pore systems where hydrocarbons preferentially occupy larger pore spaces while water remains bound in smaller throats. Track correlations between CMR-derived pore size distributions and conventional saturation logs provide compelling evidence for this complex fluid partitioning behavior.

Mineralogical analysis reveals these productive intervals correlate strongly with sandstone-rich lithofacies, a finding that substantially modifies the traditional view of the Asmari Formation’s reservoir characteristics. Machine learning integration for enhanced reservoir characterization

Random forest (RF) for pore system classification

Random Forest, an ensemble learning method, was utilized to classify pore systems based on NMR T₂ relaxation spectra and conventional log data. The model was trained on labeled datasets from the studied well, where pore types (micro-, meso-, and macro-pores) were identified using NMR-derived cutoffs. Feature importance analysis revealed that T₂ log-mean (T₂LM), bound fluid volume (BFV), and effective porosity (PHIE) were the most influential predictors. The RF model achieved an accuracy of 92% in pore-type classification, with a cross-validated correlation coefficient (R²) of 0.89 between predicted and observed pore distributions. This high accuracy confirms the reliability of NMR-derived pore typing and its alignment with geological facies.

SVM and SHAP analysis for electrofacies prediction

SVM was applied to predict electrofacies using a combination of NMR and conventional log inputs44,109. The kernel-based algorithm effectively handled the non-linear relationships between petrophysical parameters and facies boundaries. The SVM model achieved an overall accuracy of 88%, with distinct clusters corresponding to sandstone-rich (high permeability) and carbonate-dominated (variable porosity) intervals. Notably, the model identified subtle transitions in pore connectivity that were overlooked by traditional lithology-based methods, reinforcing the novelty of the pore type-based framework.

Machine learning classification identified five electrofacies (EF1–EF5) with distinct petrophysical and geological characteristics. Tight carbonates (EF1) exhibited minimal porosity (4.2%) and permeability (0.3 mD), while dolomitized packstones (EF2) showed moderate reservoir quality (14.7% porosity, 9.1 mD permeability). The sandstone facies (EF3), though representing only 15% of total thickness, contained 58% of movable hydrocarbons due to superior macroporosity (18.2% porosity, 54.6 mD permeability). SHAP interpretability analysis revealed T₂ geometric mean as the primary electrofacies discriminator (42% contribution).

The ML results corroborate the study’s core findings, particularly the superior hydrocarbon potential of sandstone-rich intervals within the Asmari Formation. The RF and SVM models confirmed that traditional lithology-based classifications often misrepresent flow potential, whereas pore type-based metrics (e.g., T₂LM, BFV) provide a more accurate basis for reservoir zonation. Discussion

Reservoir characterization in heterogeneous formations like the Asmari requires moving beyond conventional lithology-based approaches94,106. The NMR-electrofacies integration addresses these challenges through direct pore-scale characterization, establishing a dynamic rock typing framework grounded in pore functionality rather than mineral composition.

Pore type-based reservoir characterization

The integrated petrophysical visualization (Fig. 12) redefines reservoir evaluation by prioritizing fluid response and pore accessibility over compositional labels. Electrofacies classification via T₂ relaxation dynamics departs from traditional gamma-ray or lithology-based schemes, categorizing intervals as flow-conductive, storage-dominated, or non-contributing. For instance, high SBFV and low T₂LM in deeper sections signify shifts to microporous domains, likely from compaction or dolomitization. This approach directly links pore geometry to permeability trends and saturation-height functions, enhancing predictive capacity for reservoir engineering.

Figure 13 extends this paradigm by correlating mineralogy with fluid behavior. Calcite-rich layers with SWE-SWI convergence denote optimal connectivity, whereas clay-anhydrite associations signal capillary barriers.

Implications for heterogeneous reservoir modeling

The dual-log analysis in Fig. 14 resolves ambiguities in hydrocarbon distribution, revealing that high porosity does not universally equate to producibility. Sandstone-rich intervals emerge as critical flow units within the Asmari’s carbonate-clastic heterogeneity, underscoring the need for lithology-sensitive saturation models. The convergence of CMR and conventional logs validates NMR-derived pore-scale insights, enabling high-resolution electrofacies mapping.

This methodology advances reservoir characterization by unifying static properties (porosity, mineralogy) with dynamic fluid behavior. It demonstrates that pore architecture, shaped by depositional and diagenetic processes, ultimately governs reservoir performance—a principle applicable to other complex carbonate systems.

Pore type-based rock typing: a paradigm shift

The hybrid NMR-electrofacies framework challenges conventional lithology-driven models by prioritizing pore connectivity over mineral composition. Intervals with comparable porosity (e.g., 3540–3560 m vs. 3738–3748 m) exhibited marked divergence in hydrocarbon occupancy, underscoring the limitations of lithofacies models. Sandstone subunits, though comprising only 15% of thickness, emerged as primary flow units due to macroporous networks (PCI > 1.8)—a finding later validated by production correlations (Fig. 19; Supplementary Table 6) and prior studies31,32,35.

NMR-electrofacies integration: resolving heterogeneity

The integration of NMR T₂ distributions with electrofacies classification resolved vertical heterogeneity more effectively than conventional logs. For instance, bimodal T₂ distributions in mid-depth intervals (e.g., 3590–3709 m) identified dual-porosity systems (micro- and macro-pores), while diffuse spectra in clay-rich zones confirmed capillary-bound fluids. This echoes Kadkhodaie, et al17., who linked T₂ signatures to dynamic flow units. Discrepancies between Ktim (5.1 mD) and KSDR(87 mD) permeability estimates highlight the need for model calibration to pore-throat geometry, as noted by Al-Sudani, et al94..

Stratigraphic controls and diagenetic impacts

The preferential hydrocarbon accumulation in sandstone intervals (3540–3625 m) fundamentally challenges the traditional carbonate-dominated reservoir model of the Asmari Formation. Three key stratigraphic factors explain this phenomenon:

Depositional architecture

Sandstone bodies within the Asmari represent tidally influenced deltaic incursions during Oligo-Miocene sea-level lowstands32,33. Their sheet-like geometries (3–5 m thick, 200–500 m lateral continuity based on well correlations) create interconnected high-permeability pathways (avg. 54.6 mD vs. 9.1 mD in carbonates).

Synergy between ML and NMR-electrofacies analysis

The synergy between ML and NMR-electrofacies analysis introduces a paradigm shift in reservoir characterization. By leveraging RF and SVM, this study transcends subjective lithological labels, instead classifying rocks based on measurable pore functionality. SHAP analysis further illuminates the geological relevance of ML predictions, ensuring that the model’s decisions align with petrophysical principles. This dual approach—combining advanced ML with NMR diagnostics—redefines reservoir evaluation as a dynamic, data-rich science, offering a scalable template for heterogeneous carbonate systems worldwide. The novelty lies in the methodological integration and also in its capacity to reconcile machine learning with geological realism, setting a new standard for predictive reservoir modeling.

Implications for reservoir development

The framework’s predictive capacity was demonstrated by correlating SBFV (2.2%) and T₂LM values with production data, enabling targeted stimulation of high-FFI (Free Fluid Index) zones. This approach mitigates risks in mature fields, where legacy models often misrepresent dynamic fluid behavior (Fig. 19). The methodology’s transferability to analogous carbonate-clastic systems (e.g., Ghawar Field) is supported by Wang, et al100. and Lin, et al73, who advocated NMR-based pore typing for Middle East reservoirs.

Sandstone intervals (blue) exhibit a stronger linear trend (R² = 0.82) compared to carbonates (red), reinforcing their dominance as flow conduits despite their minority presence in the Asmari Formation. Comparative results with prior works

The novelty of this study is best contextualized by comparing its methodology and findings with prior research (Table 9). While Varkouhi, et al42. and Eftekhari, et al43. advanced fluid typing and electrofacies classification, respectively, their approaches relied heavily on static lithological descriptors. In contrast, this work redefines rock typing through dynamic pore connectivity metrics (e.g., T₂LM, BFV) and ML-aided interpretability, offering a more predictive and adaptable framework.

For example, Zhang, et al74. achieved high accuracy in pore size prediction but did not link these results to reservoir-scale flow units. Here, the integration of SVM-based electrofacies and SHAP explanations bridges this gap, correlating pore geometry with production potential. Similarly, while Jamshidi Gohari, et al110. and Murtaza, et al111. used SHAP for facies interpretation, their study lacked NMR validation—a limitation overcome in this work through core-calibrated T₂ distributions (Table 9). This multi-parameter integration advances upon techniques like the joint inversion of spontaneous potential and resistivity for saturation assessment112, and frequency-domain electrical dispersion methods73,74,113. The NMR-ML framework directly quantifies pore-scale fluid mobility, thereby overcoming the limitations posed by complex wettability16 and static lithological models45,55.

Limitations and future work

While NMR data resolved microporosity, core calibration remains critical for validating clay-bound fluid volumes. Future studies could incorporate micro-CT imaging to refine pore-network models. Additionally, the framework’s reliance on Schlumberger’s CMR tool necessitates adaptation for other NMR tools in legacy datasets.

Conclusions and recommendations

This study established that pore architecture, rather than lithology alone, governs fluid flow behavior in the heterogeneous Asmari Formation. The hybrid NMR-machine learning framework successfully quantified three distinct pore systems (micro-, meso-, and macroporous) and demonstrated that sandstone intervals, despite comprising only 15% of the reservoir thickness, contribute 58% of movable hydrocarbons and 73% of total flow capacity due to superior pore connectivity (PCI > 1.8). The explainable AI approach achieved 89% accuracy in electrofacies classification, with SHAP analysis confirming T₂ geometric mean (42% contribution) and bound fluid volume (−0.38 correlation) as dominant controls on permeability distribution. These findings fundamentally challenge traditional carbonate-centric reservoir models and provide quantitative evidence that pore-scale characterization should supersede lithological classification in heterogeneous systems.

This study introduces three fundamental advances in carbonate reservoir characterization: (1) a NMR-derived pore connectivity index (PCI) that quantifies flow potential independent of lithology, (2) the first integration of SHAP-interpretable machine learning with NMR petrophysics for electrofacies classification, and (3) dynamic permeability corrections that reduce prediction errors by 37% in clay-rich zones. The workflow’s validation against core and production data demonstrates unprecedented accuracy, with 89% agreement in flow unit identification and < 0.32 log unit permeability errors. Most significantly, the results overturn conventional models by proving sandstone subunits (15% of thickness) dominate production (58% of hydrocarbons) due to superior pore connectivity - a finding with immediate implications for redevelopment strategies in mature fields.

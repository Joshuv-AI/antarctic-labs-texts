Affordance-First Decomposition for Continual Learning in Video–Language Understanding
Mengzhu XuUniversity of Sydney, Hanzhi LiuUniversity of California, Santa BarbaraNingkang PengNanjing Normal UniversityQianyu ChenNanyang Technological University, Canran XiaoShenzhen Campus of Sun Yat-sen University

Abstract
Continual learning for video–language understanding is increasingly important as models face non-stationary data, domains, and query styles, yet prevailing solutions blur what should stay stable versus what should adapt, rely on static routing/capacity, or require replaying past videos. We aim to explicitly specify where stability lives and where plasticity should be focused under realistic memory and privacy constraints. We introduce Affordance-First Decomposition (AFD): videos are mapped to slowly varying affordance tokens that form a shared, time-aligned substrate, while a lightweight, query-routed, conflict-aware scheduler concentrates adaptation and grows capacity only when needed. The substrate is stabilized via weak alignment and teacher consistency, and training uses question-only replay. AFD achieves state-of-the-art across protocols: 51.6% average accuracy with − 1.8 % -1.8\% forgetting on domain-incremental VideoQA, ViLCo R@1@0.5 of 29.6% (MQ) and 20.7% (NLQ) with 18.4% stAP@0.25 (VQ), and 39.5% accuracy with − 1.6 % -1.6\% forgetting on time-incremental iVQA. Overall, AFD offers an explicit, interpretable split between a stable interaction-centered substrate and targeted adaptation.

Introduction

Figure 1:
Under a stream of video–language reasoning tasks, existing methods rely on prompt/adapter add-ons with static routing and post-hoc stabilization, leaving the stability–plasticity trade-off implicit. Video understanding
1
underpins assistive analytics, retrieval, and embodied agents, where models must parse long, multi-event sequences and align visual dynamics with natural language
15
. Yet real deployments seldom operate in a stationary world: data, domains, and query styles evolve over time
25
.
Continual video–language reasoning
is therefore central to assistants that must keep learning from non-stationary streams while answering open-form queries, and step reasoning in videos.
Recent studies underscore both the opportunity and difficulty: long egocentric protocols mix heterogeneous tasks and language forms
27
, and time-continual pretraining shows that naïve fine-tuning quickly drifts while replay-heavy strategies raise cost and privacy concerns
8
;
28
. Robust solutions must retain prior skills, acquire new ones, and remain efficient without relying on storing old videos
30
.

Despite rapid progress, two shortcomings persist:
(i) Objectives for stability vs. adaptability are under-specified.
Existing lines either specialize with prompts/adapters or preserve geometry via distillation/topology constraints, but rarely articulate
which structures should remain task-invariant and which should adapt
along a stream—making stability largely incidental and hard to diagnose
2
;
4
;
25
;
40
;
42
.
(ii) Plasticity is budgeted heuristically.
Capacity and routing are commonly fixed or task-indexed, while interference is mitigated post hoc by merging or global regularizers. Few approaches use online signals to decide
when/where
to change
32
;
24
;
36
;
26
;
10
.

This paper asks:
Can continual video–language learning be anchored in a slowly varying, interaction-centered substrate that separates stability from adaptation?
As shown in Fig.
1
, we adopt an
affordance-first decomposition(AFD)
: videos are mapped into affordance evidence, while adaptation is concentrated in a lightweight, query-routed reasoning module. The affordance evidence provides time-aligned, reusable signals that remain stable across tasks. The routed module focuses plastic updates only where conflict arises, preserving past capabilities without broad parameter drift.

Our contributions are as follows:

•

We introduce an
affordance-first decomposition
that separates a
slowly varying
shared substrate from a
plastic
routed scheduler, clarifying where stability vs. adaptability should live in continual video–language learning.

•

We operationalize conflict-aware adaptation by query-conditioned per-layer routing and selective capacity growth, and adopt a question-only replay strategy that is privacy- and memory-friendly.

•

Across ViLCo
27
and standard VideoQA suites, our approach achieves state-of-the-art results with substantially lower forgetting compared to strong baselines and recent SOTAs, while remaining order-robust and compute-efficient.

Related Work

2.1
Continual video–language learning.

Early multimodal CL revealed strong order sensitivity and forgetting in linguistically structured VQA and captioning

3
Method

We address continual video–language reasoning where tasks arrive as a stream and both domains and query formats evolve over time. Our approach is
Affordance-First Decomposition
(AFD). A shared head converts a video into temporally grounded affordance tokens. A plastic LLM-backbone scheduler consumes the query tokens together with these affordance tokens and performs event-level reasoning through per-layer routed low-rank adapters. Stability acts only on the shared head. Plasticity and task specialization are absorbed by the LLM scheduler. Two compact memories enable practical rehearsal. Figure
2
presents the pipeline.

3.1
Problem setup

We work in a streaming setting where tasks arrive over time. Each task provides labeled triples
(V,q,y)
and an unlabeled clip pool for stability. A video
V=\{F_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
is the visual input. A query
q
is free text. The target
y
is either an open answer or a temporal span or a step sequence.

The model has two parts that play different roles. The
shared affordance head

h_{\psi}
converts the video into continuous affordance tokens that are linearly projected into the LLM hidden space to form keys and values
(K,V)
. The
LLM-backbone scheduler

g_{\phi}^{\text{LLM}}
embeds the query with the same LLM to obtain
U
and attends to
(K,V)
to produce the task-appropriate prediction. The overall predictor is

f_{\Theta}(V,q)=g_{\phi}^{\text{LLM}}\!\big(U,K,V\big)

(1)

where
U=E_{\text{LLM}}[\mathrm{Tok}(q)]
,
(K,V)=\Pi(h_{\psi}(V))
.
We maintain two small memories that serve training only.
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
stores diverse past questions for replay distillation.
\mathcal{M}_{A}
stores affordance prototypes for diagnostics. By design, stability constraints are applied to the shared head
h_{\psi}
and task plasticity is absorbed by the scheduler
g_{\phi}^{\text{LLM}}
.

3.2
Architecture

Shared affordance head
h_{\psi}

Affordances are object–action regularities that vary slowly across domains and tasks. A stable affordance space reduces gradient conflict for downstream reasoning.

Let
X_{1:T}=\mathrm{Enc}_{v}(V)
with
X_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{v}}
. The head produces an affordance distribution

\displaystyle z_{t}

\displaystyle=f_{\text{st}}(X_{t}),

(2)

\displaystyle s_{t}(a)

\displaystyle=\langle w_{a},z_{t}\rangle,

\displaystyle P_{t}(a)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{softmax}_{a\in\mathcal{V}_{A}}\!\big(s_{t}(a)/\tau\big).

We form a sparse renormalized distribution on the top
L
categories,

q_{t}(a)=\frac{\mathbf{1}[a\in\text{Top-}L]\cdot P_{t}(a)}{\sum_{a^{\prime}\in\text{Top-}L}P_{t}(a^{\prime})},

(3)

then build a continuous token with an embedding table
E_{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{V}_{A}|\times d_{a}}
,

A_{t}=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{V}_{A}}q_{t}(a)\,E_{A}[a]\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{a}}.

(4)

To interface with the LLM hidden space we project

K_{t}=W_{K}A_{t},\quad V_{t}=W_{V}A_{t},

(5)

where stack
K=\mathrm{stack}_{t}(K_{t})
and
V=\mathrm{stack}_{t}(V_{t})
.
W_{K},W_{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}}\times d_{a}}
.

LLM tokenizer and text interface

The scheduler is an LLM. Queries must be embedded in its native space for unified conditioning and generation.
Let
\mathrm{Tok}
be the LLM tokenizer with vocabulary
\mathcal{V}_{\text{LLM}}
and
E_{\text{LLM}}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{V}_{\text{LLM}}|\times d_{q}}
its input embedding matrix. We compute

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tok}(q),

(6)

\displaystyle U=[u_{1},\dots,u_{L}]

\displaystyle\text{with}\ u_{\ell}=E_{\text{LLM}}[w_{\ell}],

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Pool}(U).

where
U\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times d_{q}}
are token embeddings.
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{q}}
is a pooled query state used by the router.

Per-layer routing and adapter injection

Heterogeneous tasks require different reasoning skills. Instance-wise routing focuses plastic capacity and reduces interference.
At each adapterized LLM layer
\ell\in\mathcal{S}
the router computes mixture weights

\alpha^{(\ell)}=\mathrm{softmax}\!\big(W_{r}^{(\ell)}\,u\big)\in\Delta^{m-1},

(7)

and injects a mixture of LoRA experts into the linear map
W^{(\ell)}

\widetilde{W}^{(\ell)}=W^{(\ell)}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}^{(\ell)}\,\frac{B_{j}^{(\ell)}A_{j}^{(\ell)}}{s_{j}^{(\ell)}},

(8)

where
A_{j}^{(\ell)}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{r_{j}^{(\ell)}\times d_{\text{in}}}
and
B_{j}^{(\ell)}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}}\times r_{j}^{(\ell)}}
are low-rank factors with rank
r_{j}^{(\ell)}
and
s_{j}^{(\ell)}
is a scale.

We measure conflict by a clamped negative cosine with numerical stabilization

c_{j}^{(k)}=\left[-\frac{\langle g_{j}^{(k)},\,\bar{g}_{j}^{(1:k-1)}\rangle}{\|g_{j}^{(k)}\|_{2}\,\|\bar{g}_{j}^{(1:k-1)}\|_{2}+\varepsilon}\right]_{+},\qquad\varepsilon>0.

(9)

The LoRA rank grows discretely by the excess conflict above a threshold and is capped

\displaystyle\Delta r_{j}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\min\!\Big\{r_{\max}-r_{j}^{(k-1)},\ \big\lfloor\gamma\,(c_{j}^{(k)}-\tau_{c})_{+}\big\rfloor\Big\},

(10)

\displaystyle r_{j}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=r_{j}^{(k-1)}+\Delta r_{j}^{(k)}.

Here
\tau_{c}\in[0,1)
is the threshold,
\gamma>0
is a gain, and
(\cdot)_{+}=\max\{\cdot,0\}
. Initialization of new columns can follow a truncated SVD of the projected gradient with Tikhonov-regularized inverses, detailed in the Supplementary Materials.

LLM-backbone scheduler with affordance cross-attention

The LLM composes language evidence with affordance evidence and outputs the final reasoning result while insulating the shared head from frequent changes.

At layers
\ell\in\mathcal{S}
the LLM attends to affordances

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=UW_{Q},

(11)

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Attn}(Q,K,V),

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Pool}(H,U).

and task heads support three query formats with a unified supervision

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{I}_{\text{gen}}\!\left[-\sum_{m}\log p(y_{m}\mid y_{<m},U,K,V)\right]

(12)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{I}_{\text{span}}\!\left[-\log p_{s}(t_{s})-\log p_{e}(t_{e})+\lambda_{\mathrm{u}}\big(1-\mathrm{tIoU}\big)\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{I}_{\text{step}}\!\left[-\sum_{m}\log p(\pi_{m}\mid\pi_{<m},U,K,V)\right].

Here
t_{s},t_{e}\in\{1,\dots,T\}
and
\mathrm{tIoU}
14
is computed on discrete frame intervals. Each sample activates exactly one head indicated by the selector
\mathbb{I}_{\text{gen}}
or
\mathbb{I}_{\text{span}}
or
\mathbb{I}_{\text{step}}
.

3.3
Training objective

Affordance stability on
h_{\psi}

We blend weak alignment with teacher consistency

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{aff}}

\displaystyle=\beta\left[-\sum_{\ell}\log\Big(\sum_{t\in S_{\ell}}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell}}P_{t}(a)\Big)\right]

(13)

\displaystyle+(1-\beta)\,\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathrm{KL}\!\left(\bar{P}_{t}\,\|\,P_{t}\right),

where
S_{\ell}
is an ASR span with verb candidates
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
.
P_{t}(\cdot)
is the current affordance distribution and
\bar{P}_{t}(\cdot)
is the frozen teacher from the previous task. The scalar
\beta\in[0,1]
balances the two terms. Gradients of
\mathcal{L}_{\text{aff}}
update
\psi
only.

Question-only replay distillation on
g_{\phi}^{\text{LLM}}

We store diverse past questions and distill on current clips with temperature
T_{\mathrm{kd}}>0
and optional confidence masking
\rho\in(0,1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{replay}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{q^{(u)},V}\ \mathrm{KL}\!\Big(\bar{p}_{T}(\cdot\mid V,q)\,\|\,p_{T}(\cdot\mid V,q)\Big),

(14)

\displaystyle\text{where }p_{T}=\mathrm{softmax}(z/T_{\mathrm{kd}}).

and we include only pairs whose teacher maximum probability exceeds
\rho
to suppress noisy supervision. Here, the expectation is over
q^{(u)}\in\mathcal{M}_{Q}
and
V
, and
p_{T}=\mathrm{softmax}(z/T_{\mathrm{kd}})
denotes the temperature-scaled probability distribution. Gradients of
\mathcal{L}_{\text{replay}}
update
\phi
.

Full objective

At task
k
we minimize the three-term objective

\mathcal{L}^{(k)}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}^{(k)}+\lambda_{\text{aff}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{aff}}^{(k)}+\lambda_{\text{rep}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{replay}}^{(k)}

(15)

with positive scalars
\lambda_{\text{aff}}
and
\lambda_{\text{rep}}
. Gradients of
\mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\text{replay}}
update the LLM scheduler with routed adapters and rank growth.

Conclusion

We addressed the central question of
where stability should live and where plasticity should adapt
in continual video–language learning, proposing an
affordance-first decomposition
with a slowly varying substrate and a query-routed, conflict-aware scheduler.

Appendix B
Theoretical Analysis

We give a stylized analysis of AFD in a continual-learning setting,
with the goal of making the separation between a stable
affordance head and a plastic routed scheduler mathematically explicit. To keep notation compact in a double-column layout, we first introduce shorthands that will be used throughout.

Shorthands.

Recall that tasks arrive sequentially
k=1,\dots,K
. After finishing
task
k
the model parameters are
(\psi^{(k)},\phi^{(k)})
. We write

\displaystyle h^{k}

\displaystyle\;\triangleq\;h_{\psi^{(k)}},\qquad\phi^{k}\;\triangleq\;\phi^{(k)},

(16)

\displaystyle\Delta h^{k}

\displaystyle\;\triangleq\;h^{k}-h^{k-1},\qquad\Delta\phi^{k}\;\triangleq\;\phi^{k}-\phi^{k-1}.

(17)

For task
i
, the expected risk under
(\psi^{(k)},\phi^{(k)})
is
denoted

R_{i}^{k}\;\triangleq\;R_{i}(\psi^{(k)},\phi^{(k)}).

(18)

Gradients of
R_{i}
w.r.t. the scheduler at step
k
are abbreviated as

g_{i}^{k}\;\triangleq\;\nabla_{\phi}R_{i}(\psi^{(k)},\phi^{(k)}).

(19)

The scheduler update direction at task
k
is

d^{k}\;\triangleq\;P_{k}\nabla_{\phi}L_{k}(\psi^{(k-1)},\phi^{(k-1)}),

(20)

where
P_{k}
is the orthogonal projector onto the routed LoRA subspace
for task
k
and
L_{k}
is the task
+
replay loss. The effective update
is

\Delta\phi^{k}\;=\;-\eta\,d^{k},\qquad\eta>0.

(21)

Forgetting measure and path length.

For a past task
i\leq K
, we define the forgetting at time
K
as

F_{i}^{K}\;\triangleq\;R_{i}^{K}-R_{i}^{i}.

(22)

We also introduce the cumulative affordance drift and scheduler
path length after task
i
:

\displaystyle B_{h}^{i:K}

\displaystyle\;\triangleq\;\sum_{k=i+1}^{K}\big\|\Delta h^{k}\big\|_{\mathrm{op}},

(23)

\displaystyle S_{i:K}

\displaystyle\;\triangleq\;\sum_{k=i+1}^{K}\big\|\Delta\phi^{k}\big\|_{2}^{2}.

(24)

Here
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}}
is the operator norm induced by the
Euclidean norm.

Assumptions.

We adopt standard regularity conditions.

(A1) Lipschitz loss and bounded predictions.

There exist
L_{\ell},B_{f}>0
such that for any
y
and predictions

\hat{y}_{1},\hat{y}_{2}
,

\displaystyle\big|\ell(\hat{y}_{1},y)-\ell(\hat{y}_{2},y)\big|

\displaystyle\leq L_{\ell}\,|\hat{y}_{1}-\hat{y}_{2}|,

(25)

\displaystyle|f_{\Theta}(V,q)|

\displaystyle\leq B_{f},\quad\forall(V,q).

(26)

(A2) Lipschitz in the affordance operator.

There exists
L_{h}>0
such that for any task
i
and any

(\psi,\psi^{\prime},\phi)
,

|R_{i}(\psi^{\prime},\phi)-R_{i}(\psi,\phi)|\;\leq\;L_{h}\,\big\|h_{\psi^{\prime}}-h_{\psi}\big\|_{\mathrm{op}}.

(27)

(A3) Smoothness and bounded gradients in
\phi
.

For each
i
and
\psi
, the map
\phi\mapsto R_{i}(\psi,\phi)
is

L_{\phi}
-smooth:

\displaystyle R_{i}(\psi,\phi^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq R_{i}(\psi,\phi)+\langle g_{i}(\psi,\phi),\phi^{\prime}-\phi\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\tfrac{L_{\phi}}{2}\|\phi^{\prime}-\phi\|_{2}^{2},

(28)

and gradients are uniformly bounded:

\|g_{i}(\psi,\phi)\|_{2}\leq G_{\phi},\quad\forall i,(\psi,\phi).

(29)

(A4) Conflict-aware routing.

The question-only replay and conflict-aware router in AFD are designed
so that the update direction
d^{k}
is not strongly anti-aligned with
any past task gradient. We encode this via a cosine bound: there
exists
\rho\in[0,1)
such that for all
k>i
,

\langle g_{i}^{k-1},d^{k}\rangle\;\geq\;-\rho\,\|g_{i}^{k-1}\|_{2}\,\|d^{k}\|_{2}.

(30)

B.1
Single-Step Effect on a Past Task

We first bound the change in
R_{i}
incurred by a single update at step

k>i
. To keep notation uncluttered, set

\begin{split}h^{k-1}&=h^{\mathrm{old}},\quad h^{k}=h^{\mathrm{new}},\\
\phi^{k-1}&=\phi^{\mathrm{old}},\quad\phi^{k}=\phi^{\mathrm{new}}.\end{split}

(31)

Lemma 1

(Single-step bound)
.

Under (A1)–(A4), for any
k>i
,

\displaystyle R_{i}^{k}-R_{i}^{k-1}

\displaystyle\leq L_{h}\,\big\|\Delta h^{k}\big\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\quad+\rho\,G_{\phi}\,\|\Delta\phi^{k}\|_{2}+\frac{L_{\phi}}{2\eta}\,\|\Delta\phi^{k}\|_{2}^{2}.

(32)

Proof.

We split the increment into a representation part and a scheduler part:

\displaystyle R_{i}^{k}-R_{i}^{k-1}

\displaystyle=\big[R_{i}(h^{\mathrm{new}},\phi^{\mathrm{new}})-R_{i}(h^{\mathrm{old}},\phi^{\mathrm{new}})\big]

\displaystyle\quad+\big[R_{i}(h^{\mathrm{old}},\phi^{\mathrm{new}})-R_{i}(h^{\mathrm{old}},\phi^{\mathrm{old}})\big].

(33)

Representation term.

By (
27
),

R_{i}(h^{\mathrm{new}},\phi^{\mathrm{new}})-R_{i}(h^{\mathrm{old}},\phi^{\mathrm{new}})\leq L_{h}\,\|\Delta h^{k}\|_{\mathrm{op}}.

(34)

Scheduler term.

Apply (
28
) with

(\psi,\phi,\phi^{\prime})=(\psi^{(k-1)},\phi^{\mathrm{old}},\phi^{\mathrm{new}})
:

\displaystyle R_{i}(h^{\mathrm{old}},\phi^{\mathrm{new}})

\displaystyle\leq R_{i}(h^{\mathrm{old}},\phi^{\mathrm{old}})+\langle g_{i}^{k-1},\Delta\phi^{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\tfrac{L_{\phi}}{2}\|\Delta\phi^{k}\|_{2}^{2}.

(35)

Rearranging,

\displaystyle R_{i}(h^{\mathrm{old}},\phi^{\mathrm{new}})-R_{i}(h^{\mathrm{old}},\phi^{\mathrm{old}})

\displaystyle\quad\leq\langle g_{i}^{k-1},\Delta\phi^{k}\rangle+\tfrac{L_{\phi}}{2}\|\Delta\phi^{k}\|_{2}^{2}.

(36)

Using (
21
) and

\Delta\phi^{k}=-\eta d^{k}
, equation
(
30
) gives

\displaystyle\langle g_{i}^{k-1},\Delta\phi^{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\eta\,\langle g_{i}^{k-1},d^{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\eta\,\rho\,\|g_{i}^{k-1}\|_{2}\,\|d^{k}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{\rho\,G_{\phi}}{\eta}\,\|\Delta\phi^{k}\|_{2},

(37)

where we used
\|d^{k}\|_{2}=\|\Delta\phi^{k}\|_{2}/\eta
in the last step.
Substituting (
37
) into
(
36
) yields

\displaystyle R_{i}(h^{\mathrm{old}},\phi^{\mathrm{new}})-R_{i}(h^{\mathrm{old}},\phi^{\mathrm{old}})

\displaystyle\quad\leq\tfrac{\rho\,G_{\phi}}{\eta}\,\|\Delta\phi^{k}\|_{2}+\tfrac{L_{\phi}}{2}\|\Delta\phi^{k}\|_{2}^{2}.

(38)

Combining (
34
) and
(
38
) with
(
33
) gives
(
32
).
\square

∎

B.2
Task-wise Forgetting Bound

We now sum Lemma
1
over all updates after
task
i
and control the linear path-length term via
Cauchy–Schwarz.

Theorem 1

(Task-wise forgetting)
.

Under
(A1)–(A4)
, for any
1\leq i\leq K
,

\displaystyle F_{i}^{K}

\displaystyle\leq L_{h}\,B_{h}^{i:K}

\displaystyle\quad+\rho\,G_{\phi}\,\sqrt{K-i}\,\sqrt{S_{i:K}}+\frac{L_{\phi}}{2\eta}\,S_{i:K}.

(39)

Proof.

By telescoping,

\displaystyle F_{i}^{K}

\displaystyle=R_{i}^{K}-R_{i}^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=i+1}^{K}\big(R_{i}^{k}-R_{i}^{k-1}\big).

(40)

Applying Lemma
1
term-wise,

\displaystyle F_{i}^{K}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=i+1}^{K}L_{h}\,\|\Delta h^{k}\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\quad+\rho\,G_{\phi}\sum_{k=i+1}^{K}\|\Delta\phi^{k}\|_{2}+\frac{L_{\phi}}{2\eta}\sum_{k=i+1}^{K}\|\Delta\phi^{k}\|_{2}^{2}.

(41)

The first sum is exactly
L_{h}B_{h}^{i:K}
. For the second, Cauchy–Schwarz
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{k=i+1}^{K}\|\Delta\phi^{k}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{K-i}\,\Big(\sum_{k=i+1}^{K}\|\Delta\phi^{k}\|_{2}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{K-i}\,\sqrt{S_{i:K}}.

(42)

The last sum is
S_{i:K}
by definition. Substituting these into
(
40
) gives
(
39
).
\square

∎

The bound (
39
) separates three
contributions:

•

L_{h}B_{h}^{i:K}
: forgetting due to
affordance drift
,
controlled by the stability loss
\mathcal{L}_{\text{aff}}
.

•

\rho\,G_{\phi}\sqrt{K-i}\sqrt{S_{i:K}}
:
a
first-order interference term
coupling the worst-case
negative cosine
\rho
with the scheduler path length

S_{i:K}
.

•

(L_{\phi}/(2\eta))S_{i:K}
:
a
second-order curvature term
that is small when the
loss is smooth and updates are moderate.

In the ideal regime where (i) the affordance head is nearly frozen
(
B_{h}^{i:K}\approx 0
) and (ii) conflict-aware routing makes gradient
subspaces almost orthogonal (
\rho\approx 0
), the dominant term is the
curvature term, which vanishes as the effective path length

S_{i:K}
shrinks.

B.3
Regret of the Routed Scheduler

We briefly relate the scheduler dynamics to online regret. Consider the
sequence of convex losses

\ell_{k}(\phi)\;\triangleq\;R_{k}(\psi^{(k-1)},\phi),

(43)

and the projected OGD update

\phi^{k}\;=\;\Pi\big(\phi^{k-1}-\eta d^{k}\big),

(44)

where
\Pi
is projection onto a convex set of diameter at most
D
.
For a comparator
\phi^{\star}
, the static regret is

\mathrm{Reg}_{K}\;\triangleq\;\sum_{k=1}^{K}\ell_{k}(\phi^{k-1})-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\ell_{k}(\phi^{\star}).

(45)

Proposition 1

(Scheduler regret)
.

Assume each
\ell_{k}
is convex, gradients are bounded by
G_{\phi}
, and
the feasible set has diameter
D
. If

\eta=D/(G_{\phi}\sqrt{K})
, then

\mathrm{Reg}_{K}\;\leq\;DG_{\phi}\sqrt{K},

(46)

and the squared path length satisfies

S_{0:K}\leq D^{2}
.

Proof.

This is the standard analysis of projected OGD. The key inequality is

\displaystyle\|\phi^{k}-\phi^{\star}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi^{k-1}-\phi^{\star}\|_{2}^{2}-2\eta\,\langle\nabla\ell_{k}(\phi^{k-1}),\phi^{k-1}-\phi^{\star}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\eta^{2}G_{\phi}^{2},

(47)

which, after rearranging, using convexity, and summing over
k
, yields

\mathrm{Reg}_{K}\leq\frac{\|\phi^{0}-\phi^{\star}\|_{2}^{2}}{2\eta}+\frac{\eta}{2}G_{\phi}^{2}K\;\leq\;\frac{D^{2}}{2\eta}+\frac{\eta}{2}G_{\phi}^{2}K.

(48)

Choosing
\eta=D/(G_{\phi}\sqrt{K})
minimizes the RHS and gives the
claimed regret bound; with this choice the total movement is at most

D
, hence
S_{0:K}\leq D^{2}
.
\square

∎

Combining Theorem
1
and
Proposition
1
, we see that the same mechanisms
that control the scheduler’s online regret—bounded gradients and
short path length in routed low-rank subspaces—also control
catastrophic forgetting once the affordance substrate is stabilized.
This provides a theoretical underpinning for the design of AFD: by
confining plasticity to conflict-aware low-rank updates and making the
affordance head slowly varying, AFD simultaneously enjoys low regret on
a nonstationary stream and tight forgetting guarantees on past tasks.

C.2
Drift–Forgetting Diagnostics for the Affordance Substrate

To empirically validate the theoretical decomposition in
Sec.
B
, we study how task-wise forgetting correlates
with the drift of the shared affordance head across tasks. We focus on
the 6-step domain-incremental VideoQA protocol
and the 4-slice time-incremental iVQA protocol (S1
\rightarrow
S4),
yielding
10
tasks in total. For each task
i
and method, we compute:

•

an empirical proxy for the cumulative affordance drift

\widehat{B}_{h,i}
; and

•

the magnitude of forgetting
\widehat{F}_{i}
on that task.

We report results for Full AFD and three key ablations:
❶ w/o affordance tokens,
❺ w/o ASR alignment in
\mathcal{L}_{\text{aff}}
, and
❹ w/o question-only replay.
For each task
i
, we approximate the cumulative affordance drift as a
prototype-level cosine distance across successive tasks. Let

\mathcal{P}
denote the set of affordance prototypes, and let

\mu_{p}^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{a}}
be the learned embedding of prototype

p\in\mathcal{P}
after training on task

\widehat{B}_{h,i}\;\triangleq\;\sum_{k=i+1}^{K}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\Big(1-\cos\big(\mu_{p}^{(k)},\mu_{p}^{(k-1)}\big)\Big),

(49)

which serves as a task-wise surrogate for the operator-norm drift

B_{h}^{i:K}
in Eq. (
23
). Larger

\widehat{B}_{h,i}
indicates a less stable affordance substrate for
task
i

Following the standard CL literature, we define forgetting on task
i

as the drop between its best and final performance. Let

A_{i}^{\max}
be the maximum validation accuracy observed on task
i

over the training trajectory, and let
A_{i}^{\text{final}}
be the
accuracy after finishing all
K
tasks. We then use the magnitude

\widehat{F}_{i}\;\triangleq\;A_{i}^{\max}-A_{i}^{\text{final}},

(50)

which is non-negative and directly comparable across methods
(smaller is better).

For each of the
10
tasks (6 domain-incremental datasets and 4
time-slices), we collect
(\widehat{B}_{h,i},\widehat{F}_{i})
pairs for:

•

AFD (full)
— our full model;

•

❶w/o affordance tokens
— direct frame tokens
to the LLM scheduler;

•

❺w/o ASR alignment
—
\beta=0
in

\mathcal{L}_{\text{aff}}
(teacher consistency only);

•

❹w/o replay
—
\lambda_{\text{rep}}=0

(no question-only replay).

This yields
40
points in the
(\widehat{B}_{h,i},\widehat{F}_{i})
plane.
We additionally approximate the scheduler path-length proxy

\widehat{S}_{i}
by accumulating the squared Frobenius norm of LoRA
updates on task
i
, in line with
S_{i:K}
in
Eq. (
24
), and use it in a 3D diagnostic plot.

C.3
Scheduler Path-Length vs Forgetting and Backward Transfer

The second term in Theorem
1
shows that the
scheduler path-length
S_{i:K}
—i.e., the cumulative norm of
low-rank updates—directly contributes to forgetting. We now
empirically probe this effect by correlating a practical proxy

\widehat{S}^{(m)}
with method-level forgetting and backward transfer
(BWT) in the domain-incremental VideoQA setting.

For a given method
m
trained on the 6-step domain-incremental
VideoQA sequence, we denote by

\{\phi^{k,(m)}\}_{k=0}^{K}
the scheduler parameters after each
task, and by

\Delta\phi^{k,(m)}=\phi^{k,(m)}-\phi^{k-1,(m)}
the effective
update at step
k
. We define the method-level path-length proxy as

\widehat{S}^{(m)}\;\triangleq\;\sum_{k=1}^{K}\big\|\Delta\phi^{k,(m)}\big\|_{F}^{2},

(51)

which is an empirical counterpart of
S_{i:K}
in
Eq. (
24
). Larger
\widehat{S}^{(m)}
indicates a
longer path in parameter space.

For forgetting, we aggregate the standard CL metric over tasks. Let

A_{i}^{\max,(m)}
be the best validation accuracy achieved on task
i

for method
m
, and
A_{i}^{\text{final},(m)}
the accuracy on task
i

after training all
K
tasks. We define the method-level forgetting
magnitude

|\widehat{F}^{(m)}|\;\triangleq\;\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\big(A_{i}^{\max,(m)}-A_{i}^{\text{final},(m)}\big),

(52)

in percentage points (p.p.). Similarly, we approximate the backward
transfer as

\widehat{\mathrm{BWT}}^{(m)}\;\triangleq\;\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\big(A_{i}^{\text{final},(m)}-A_{i}^{\max,(m)}\big),

(53)

and use its magnitude

|\widehat{\mathrm{BWT}}^{(m)}|
(smaller is better) for plotting.

We instantiate this diagnostic on the domain-incremental VideoQA
experiment (6 datasets) for the following methods, each averaged over
three random seeds:

•

AFD (full)
— full model;

•

w/o Router
— uniform adapter mixing
(no instance-wise routing, cf. ❷);

•

Fixed Rank
— LoRA ranks fixed at
r{=}8

(no rank growth, cf. ❸);

•

w/o Replay
—
\lambda_{\text{rep}}{=}0

(no question-only replay, cf. ❹).

For each (method, seed) pair we record the proxy path-length

\widehat{S}^{(m)}
, the average forgetting magnitude

|\widehat{F}^{(m)}|
, the average backward transfer magnitude

|\widehat{\mathrm{BWT}}^{(m)}|
, and the average accuracy.

C.4
Cross-Task CKA of Affordance vs. Visual Features

To directly probe the “stable substrate” hypothesis behind AFD, we
compare how fast the raw visual tokens and the affordance tokens drift across tasks using centered kernel alignment (CKA). The goal is to show that, under the same continual protocol, the
affordance space evolves significantly more slowly than the backbone
feature space.

We use the 6-step domain-incremental VideoQA protocol
(iVQA
\rightarrow
MSVD
\rightarrow
MSRVTT
\rightarrow
LSMDC

\rightarrow
ANet
\rightarrow
TGIF). We fix a set of
N{=}1024

validation clips sampled uniformly across datasets and reuse them for
all tasks. For each task index
k\in\{1,\dots,6\}
, after finishing
training on task
k
, we extract:

•

the backbone frame features

X^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d_{v}}
from the frozen ViT-based
encoder (mean-pooled over time); and

•

the corresponding affordance tokens

A^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d_{a}}
(mean-pooled over time)
from the shared affordance head.

CKA similarity.

Given two feature matrices

Z^{(k)},Z^{(k^{\prime})}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
(either backbone or
affordance), we compute the linear CKA similarity as

\mathrm{CKA}(Z^{(k)},Z^{(k^{\prime})})\;=\;\frac{\left\|(Z^{(k)})^{\top}Z^{(k^{\prime})}\right\|_{F}^{2}}{\left\|(Z^{(k)})^{\top}Z^{(k)}\right\|_{F}\,\left\|(Z^{(k^{\prime})})^{\top}Z^{(k^{\prime})}\right\|_{F}}

(54)

For the 6 tasks, this yields two
6\times 6
symmetric matrices:

•

M_{\text{back}}(k,k^{\prime})=\mathrm{CKA}(X^{(k)},X^{(k^{\prime})})
for backbone features;

•

M_{\text{aff}}(k,k^{\prime})=\mathrm{CKA}(A^{(k)},A^{(k^{\prime})})
for affordance tokens.

We then form a composite matrix
M_{\text{comb}}\in\mathbb{R}^{6\times 6}

by placing
M_{\text{aff}}
in the upper triangle and
M_{\text{back}}

in the lower triangle:

(M_{\text{comb}})_{kk^{\prime}}\;=\;\begin{cases}M_{\text{aff}}(k,k^{\prime}),&k<k^{\prime},\\
M_{\text{back}}(k,k^{\prime}),&k>k^{\prime},\\
1.0,&k=k^{\prime}.\end{cases}

(55)

Fig.
9
shows the results. Tasks are ordered as

i
VQA, MSVD, MSRVTT, LSMDC, ANet, TGIF. The upper triangle
summarizes the affordance-token CKA
M_{\text{aff}}
, and the lower
triangle the backbone CKA
M_{\text{back}}
.
The mean off-diagonal CKA is
\approx 0.91
for affordance tokens and

\approx 0.70
for backbone features, indicating that the affordance
space is much more invariant across tasks.
Two observations stand out:

•

Affordance space is substantially more stable.

The affordance CKA stays above
0.88
for all cross-task pairs,
whereas backbone CKA drops to
\approx 0.64
for distant
dataset pairs (e.g., iVQA vs. TGIF). This confirms that
affordance tokens form a slowly varying shared substrate, in
line with the theoretical assumption on
B_{h}^{i:K}
.

•

Stability is global, not just local.

Backbone CKA tends to decrease with task distance in the
sequence (e.g., iVQA
\leftrightarrow
MSRVTT vs. iVQA
\leftrightarrow
TGIF), while affordance CKA remains
uniformly high. This suggests that affordance tokens capture
cross-domain, interaction-centered regularities that are reused
throughout the continual stream.

To further probe the interference term in

\rightarrow

Mucosal responses in the skin and gill filaments of Atlantic salmon (Salmo salar) infected by Neoparamoeba perurans
Authors: Grigory V Merkin, Albert Girons, Carlo C Lazado, Karin Pittman
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Mucosal mapping reveals that amoebic gill disease (AGD) affects mucous cells in gill filaments but does not alter mucosal parameters in the skin.•Exposure to AGD led to a significant increase in mucous cell size and abundance in gill filaments 4 weeks post-treatment.•The findings reinforce the importance of careful use of PAA in aquaculture to balance therapeutic effects with potential impacts on mucosal health.•This research underscores the role of mucosal homeostasis in the immune response to parasitic infections in Atlantic salmon.Keywords: Amoebic gill disease (AGD), Peracetic acid (PAA), Mucous cells (MCs), Gills, Skin

Introduction

Amoebic gill disease (AGD), primarily affecting salmonids, is caused by Neoparamoeba perurans, which colonizes the fish gills and is a significant cause of reduced growth and compromised welfare [1]. Treatment of fish affected by AGD with the potent oxidant peracetic acid (PAA) increased the risk of toxicity, highlighting the need for careful consideration in the use of this oxidant in aquaculture [[2], [3], [4], [5]]. This research is a continuation of a previously published study on the immune and stress responses of Atlantic salmon exposed to AGD and treated with peracetic acid (PAA) [4]. In that study, a broad range of physiological and molecular parameters were evaluated, including those related to blood plasma markers, gill pathology, and fish welfare [4]. AGD, whether treated or untreated, resulted in larger and more numerous mucous cells (MCs) in the gill lamellae (secondary lamella), where most respiration occurs [4]. The current follow-up research focuses solely on the assessment of MC parameters in gill filaments (primary lamella) and skin, reinforcing the evidence-based application of PAA in aquaculture.

The experiment conducted at the Tromsø Aquaculture Research Station in Norway, where AGD was induced in Atlantic salmon (Salmo salar) and fish were treated with PAA, has been detailed in the previous study by Lazado et al. [4]. In brief, AGD was induced by exposing the fish to Neoparamoeba perurans for 1 h: the water flow was closed, and amoeba culture was added directly to the tank to reach 1500 parasites per liter. The uninfected group underwent the same process without the parasite [4]. Twenty-five days post-infection with the parasite, the fish were treated with PAA 5 ppm for 30 min and 10 ppm for 15 min [4]. For the present follow-up study, we conducted a targeted analysis of MC parameters in the fish's skin and gill filament sampled at 24 h and 4 weeks post-treatment. All other experimental data, including blood plasma analysis, gill scores, and quantification of the amoeba load, were obtained from a previously published paper [4].

A highly sensitive method for the evaluation of mucosal tissues, mucosal mapping (Appendices), developed in academia [6,7] and now trademarked as Veribarr™ [4,[8], [9], [10], [11]], was applied in the current study. This technique, using tangential sections approximately 90° from the perspective of traditional histology providing 1–2 square centimeters of surface area for analysis (Appendices) was used to measure the MC area (MCA, μm²), the volumetric density of MCs in the epithelium (MCD, amount of the epithelia occupied by MCs, in %), and the abundance of the MCs in the tissue (defense activity, previously called barrier status; 1000*(1/MC area/MC volumetric density) (1000*(N MCs* μm−2)) [4,8,9].

A linear mixed-effects model (LME) was fitted using the lme() function (nlme package) to evaluate the effect of the groups on MC parameters in the gill filament [12]. Estimated marginal means were calculated (emmeans package), and Dunnett's post-hoc test was applied to compare each treatment with the control group; in addition, diagnostic plots, including a Q-Q plot of the normalized residuals, were used to assess model assumption [12]. Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed on selected blood and mucosal parameters using the princomp function in R [12].

One of the earlier studies revealed that therapeutic doses of PAA (0.6 and 2.4 ppm) induced only marginal morphomolecular changes in the salmon skin [3]. In good agreement with these findings, MCA and defense activity in the skin were not significantly changed between the groups at 24 hours (Fig. 1A) and 4 weeks (Fig. 1B) after the PAA treatment (5 and 10 ppm) in the current study. Hence, PAA treatment at a dose that reduces parasite load (5 ppm) [4] does not significantly affect the skin mucosa.

It has been demonstrated previously that gill lamella MC parameters increased within 24 hours post-treatment and were significantly higher at termination, especially in AGD-affected fish [4]. In the gill filaments, 24 hours after treatment, MCA and defense activity increased insignificantly in the AGD-exposed groups (Fig. 1C). After 4 weeks compared to the control group (“No AGD/PAA 0 ppm”), the fish given “AGD/PAA 0 ppm” had significantly larger MCs and higher defense activity while those with “AGD/PAA 10 ppm” had significantly higher defense activity. Importantly the mucosa of “AGD/PAA 5 ppm” was not significantly different from that of the control group (Fig. 1D). According to the Quantidoc database [8] the gill filament MC values of the control group fell within the most common range (mean ± 0.5 standard deviation) for salmon (green zone, Fig. 1C, Fig. 1D). “AGD/PAA 0 ppm” had the largest MCs at the highest abundance and was transitioning out of homeostasis area 4 weeks after treatment (red zone, Fig. 1D).

The analysis of gill lamellae and filaments across fish species revealed distinct patterns in MC parameters: the MC area, volumetric density, and abundance are lower in the gill lamellae compared to the gill filaments [8]. The consistently measurable differences in site-specific MCs support the hypothesis that body site matters [6,7] and that the gill has two functionally separate compartments with the gill lamella primarily involved in gas exchange and the gill filament having a structural function and possibly reflecting systemic microbiota regulation [13]. However, despite these differences, the AGD exposure and PAA treatment led to the disturbance of gill mucosa in both gill lamella and filament.

The PCA plot visually groups samples based on their treatment conditions (AGD exposure and PAA treatment levels), suggesting that these treatments influence the measured variables (Fig. 1E, Fig. 1F). Individual points representing different treatment groups (e.g., “No AGD/PAA 0 ppm” and “AGD/PAA 0 ppm”) are distributed along PCA1 (Fig. 1F), illustrating that variations in treatment severity differentially affect mucosal parameters. The higher values of mucosal parameters for both the gill lamella [4] and filament were observed in the AGD groups (particularly “AGD/PAA 0 ppm”) compared to the control group, indicating changes in the gill mucosal activity especially 4 weeks after treatment (Fig. 1F). There was a clear separation of the different treatment groups at 4 weeks primarily along PCA1, which explained 34.1 % of the variance in the PCA biplot (Fig. 1F). This indicated that the groups differed significantly based on the variables included in the analysis, particularly the MC parameters in both segments of the gills.

As reported in the previous publication on the experiment [4], all treatment groups exhibited a similar distribution of gross gill scores (GS) 24 hours after the treatment. However, four weeks post-treatment, all fish in the “AGD/PAA 0 ppm” group showed AGD pathology scores of 2 or higher [4]. In the “AGD/PAA 5 ppm” group, 80 % of the evaluated fish continued to exhibit AGD pathology scores of 2 or higher [4]. Similarly, 90 % of the fish in the “AGD/PAA 10 ppm” group displayed the same level of severity [4].

Elevated mucus production is commonly associated with AGD [1] and is identified specifically in the gill lamellae [4] and, in the current study, in the gill filament. The hyperplasia and hypertrophy of MCs in the lamellae and filament, along with the proliferation and fusion of the lamellar epithelium in AGD-affected fish, increase the diffusion distance across the water-blood barrier for oxygen transfer [4]. This results in respiratory disturbances and potentially leads to secondary hypoxic changes in the liver [1]. Moreover, the current insignificant increase in MC parameters in “AGD/PAA 5 ppm” 4 weeks after treatment was supported by Lazado et al. [4], where the lowest parasite load was found in this group, demonstrating that 5-ppm PAA could reduce the severity of infection without a significant disturbance in the gill filament mucosa homeostasis.

To summarize, using mucosal mapping, we demonstrated that AGD, whether treated or untreated, did not affect skin MC parameters but resulted in MC hypertrophy and hyperplasia in the gill filaments mucosa of infected fish. Disturbances of mucosal homeostasis in the gill filaments could potentially influence the immune response to the parasite. These disturbances correspond to typical findings in complex gill histopathology, where MC hyperplasia is one of the most frequently observed abnormalities [1,14].

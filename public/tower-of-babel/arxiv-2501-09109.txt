An explicit theta lift to Siegel paramodular forms
Jennifer Johnson-Leung, Nina Rupert

Abstract
. Let E / L E/L be a real quadratic extension of number fields. We construct an explicit map from an irreducible cuspidal automorphic representation of GL ( 2 , E ) \mathop{\rm GL}(2,E) which contains a Hilbert modular form with Γ 0 \Gamma_{0} level to an irreducible automorphic representation of GSp ( 4 , L ) \mathop{\rm GSp}(4,L) which contains a Siegel paramodular form and exhibit local data which produces a paramodular invariant vector for the local theta lift at every finite place, except when the local extension has wild ramification.



1.
Introduction

The theta correspondence, introduced by Roger Howe in 1979
[
How79
]
, provides a robust and explicit method of relating automorphic forms on pairs of reductive groups. In general, the theta correspondence is a tool for relating automorphic adelic representations (or admissible local representations) of certain pairs of subgroups
(S_{1},S_{2})
of a symplectic group
\mathop{\rm Sp}(X)
defined over a number field (or a local field of characteristic zero). Such a map is generally called a theta lift, and this theory has been carefully developed over the past forty-five years by the work of many people. See, for example,
[
Gan23
]
for a modern introduction.

The global theta lift from
S_{1}
to
S_{2}
takes as input a cusp form
f
on the adeles of
S_{1}
and defines an automorphic form on the adeles of
S_{2}
by integrating
f
against a theta kernel; this theta kernel depends on a choice of a certain local Schwartz function
\varphi
. In the archimedean case Schwartz functions are rapidly decreasing away from
0
, and the in non-archimedean case Schwartz functions are locally constant and compactly supported. Determining a choice for
\varphi
, which gives automorphic forms with desirable qualities, requires a theory of local theta lifts that are commensurable with the global theory.

While the global theta lift has a natural construction as an integral operator derived from the work of Weil
[
Wei64
]
, there is nothing so ubiquitous in the local theory. In
[
Wal80
]
, the author studies the local and global theta lift when
W
is a 2-dimensional symplectic space,
V
is a rank
3
quadratic space, and
X=W\otimes V
. In
[
Wal81
]
, the author investigated the correspondence between modular forms of half integral weight and those with integral weight and computed special values of
L
-functions in terms of Fourier coefficients of half integral weight modular forms.

In this work, we consider an analogous problem when
W
is a 4-dimensional symplectic space,
V
is a rank
4
quadratic space, and
X=W\otimes V
. The key technical result is the construction an explicit theta lift from
{\rm GO}(X)
to
{\rm GSp}(4)
. In this setting, the local theta correspondence was studied by Roberts in a series of papers, culminating in
[
Rob01
]
. An integral map for the local theta lift was studied in
[
JLR12
]
, and the authors proved that for every Hilbert cusp form of level
\Gamma_{0}({\mathfrak{N}})
there exists a Siegel paramodular newform with weight, level, Hecke eigenvalues, epsilon factor and
L
-function determined explicitly by the data of the Hilbert modular form.

We continue this work by calculating explicit input data for the local theta lifts. In particular, we study an integral realization for the local theta lifts and give an explicit recipe for Schwartz functions at all places that are not wildly ramified. This allows for explicit construction of the Siegel paramodular forms appearing in this correspondence as lifts of Hilbert modular forms over any real quadratic field, except
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})
. These paramodular forms correspond to abelian surfaces which are Weil restrictions of elliptic curves over real quadratic fields as a consequence of the results of
[
FLHS15
]
. This provides further evidence for the paramodular conjecture of Brumer and Kramer
[
BK14
]
. Our result opens up a new approach to calculate Fourier coefficients of these paramodular lifts.

This paper is organized as follows: in Section
2
we set up the language of theta correspondence with similitudes which is required to state our main results. Section
3
contains known results about Weil representations and Whittaker models which are required for the proof. In Section
4
we study the intertwining maps which give the local integral map of the theta lift, and finally in Section
5
we complete the proof of the Main Theorem by specifying specific Schwartz functions to produce the prescribed paramodular vector at every prime.

2.
Statement of the Main Theorem

We define the algebraic group
\mathop{\rm GSp}(4)
with respect to the symplectic form
J=[\begin{smallmatrix}&I\\
-I&\end{smallmatrix}]
to be the subgroup of
g\in{\rm GL}(4)
such that
{}^{t}gJg=\lambda(g)J
, for some
\lambda(g)\in{\rm GL}(1)
. The Siegel upper half space
\mathcal{H}
is the space of elements of
[\begin{smallmatrix}\tau&z\\
z&\tau^{\prime}\end{smallmatrix}]\in\mathrm{M}(2,\mathbb{C})
whose imaginary part is positive definite. For natural numbers
N
and
n
and a rational prime
p
, we define global and local paramodular groups of level
N
and
p^{n}
, respectively, to be

{\rm K}(N)=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Z}&N^{-1}\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Z}\\
N\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Z}\\
N\mathbb{Z}&N\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Z}&N\mathbb{Z}\\
N\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Z}\end{bmatrix}\cap\mathop{\rm Sp}(\mathbb{Q})\quad{\rm K}(p^{n})=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{Z}_{p}&\mathbb{Z}_{p}&p^{-n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}&\mathbb{Z}_{p}\\
p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}&\mathbb{Z}_{p}&\mathbb{Z}_{p}&\mathbb{Z}_{p}\\
p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}&p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}&\mathbb{Z}_{p}&p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\\
p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}&\mathbb{Z}_{p}&\mathbb{Z}_{p}&\mathbb{Z}_{p}\end{bmatrix}\cap{\rm GSp}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}).

A Siegel paramodular form is a holomorphic function on
\mathcal{H}
such that
F(\gamma\langle Z\rangle)=j(\gamma,Z)^{-k}F(Z)
for all
\gamma\in{\rm K}(N)
and
Z\in\mathcal{H}
, where
\gamma\langle Z\rangle
is the action by fractional linear transformation. By Theorem 1.4.1 of
[
JLRS23
]
there is a bijection between cuspidal automorphic representations of
{\rm GSp}(4,{\mathbb{A}})
of weight
k
which are paramodular with conductor
N
and cuspidal Siegel eigenforms with respect to
{\rm K}(N)
that are new of level
N
.

For any even-dimensional non-degenerate symmetric bilinear space
X
over a field of characteristic not equal to 2, we let
{\rm GO}(X)
be the group of

h\in{\rm GL}(X)
such that there exists a
\lambda\in{\rm GL}(1)
such that
\langle hx,hy\rangle=\lambda\langle x,y\rangle

for all
x,y\in X
. The scalar
\lambda
is unique, and will be denoted by
\lambda(h)
. We let
{\rm O}(X)
be the subgroup of
h\in{\rm GO}(X)
such that
\lambda(h)=1
, and we let
{\rm SO}(X)
be the subgroup of
h\in{\rm O}(X)
such that
\det(h)=1
. We see that for
h\in{\rm GO}(X)
we have
\det(h)^{2}=\lambda(h)^{\dim X}
. We set
{\rm GSO}(X)
to be the subgroup of
h\in{\rm GO}(X)
such that
\det(h)=\lambda(h)^{\dim X/2}
and
{\rm SO}(X)={\rm O}(X)\cap{\rm GSO}(X)
.

Let
L
be a number field, let
E
be a real quadratic extension of
L
, let
{\mathbb{A}}_{L}
be the adeles of
L
, and let
{\mathbb{A}}_{E}
be the adeles of
E
. Let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
{\rm GL}(2,{\mathbb{A}}_{E})
that has trivial central character and is not Galois invariant. The Jacquet-Langlands correspondence
[
JL70a
]
produces a cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi^{\prime}
of a quaternion algebra
B^{\times}({\mathbb{A}}_{E})
, from the data of
\pi
. Let
X({\mathbb{A}}_{L})
be a certain symmetric bilinear 4-dimensional
{\mathbb{A}}_{L}
-subspace of
B({\mathbb{A}}_{E})
. For
x,y\in X
, the symmetric product on
X
is given by
\langle x,y\rangle=\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}(xy^{*})
. The following sequence is exact (
[
Knu91
]
):

(2.1)

1\to{\mathbb{A}}_{E}^{\times}\to{\mathbb{A}}_{L}^{\times}\times B({\mathbb{A}}_{E})^{\times}\xrightarrow{\ \ \rho\ \ }\mathop{\rm GSO}(X({\mathbb{A}}_{E}))\to 1.

So, from the data of
\pi^{\prime}
, it is simple to produce a cuspidal automorphic representation of
{\rm GSO}(X({\mathbb{A}}_{L}))
, also denoted by
\pi^{\prime}
. Let
\sigma
be the associated cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathop{\rm GO}(X)
, as in Theorem 7 of
[
Rob01
]
. Since
{\rm GO}(X)
and
{\rm GSp}(4)
form a dual reductive pair, we have the existence of the Weil representation
\omega
on the group
R=\{(g,h)\in{\rm GSp}(4,{\mathbb{A}}_{L})\times{\rm GO}(X({\mathbb{A}}_{L}))\mid\lambda(g)=\lambda(h)\}
and hence the existence of the theta lift, which is the focus of this paper.

The space of
\omega
is the space of Schwartz functions on
X({\mathbb{A}}_{L})^{2}
, which we denote by
\mathcal{S}(X({\mathbb{A}}_{L})^{2})
. The key to determining an explicit theta lift is choosing a
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(X({\mathbb{A}}_{L})^{2})
wisely, but we can define the theta lift for any choice of
\varphi
. Let
f
be a cusp form on
{\rm GO}(X,{\mathbb{A}}_{L})
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(X({\mathbb{A}}_{L})^{2})
. Let
{\rm GSp}(4,{\mathbb{A}}_{L})^{+}
be the subgroup of
g\in{\rm GSp}(4,{\mathbb{A}}_{L})
such that
\lambda(g)\in\lambda({\rm GO}(X({\mathbb{A}}_{L})))
. For
g\in{\rm GSp}(4,{\mathbb{A}}_{L})^{+}
define:

\theta(f,\varphi)(g)=\int_{\mathop{\rm O}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\setminus\mathop{\rm O}(X,{\mathbb{A}}_{L})}\vartheta(g,h_{1}h;\varphi)f(h_{1}h)dh_{1}

where
h\in{\rm GO}(X,{\mathbb{A}}_{L})
is any element such that
(g,h)\in R({\mathbb{A}}_{L})
and
\vartheta
is the global theta kernel given by

\vartheta(g,h;\varphi)=\sum_{x\in X(L)^{2}}(\omega(g,h)\cdot\varphi)(x).

Then
\theta(f,\varphi)
can be extended uniquely to all of
{\rm GSp}(4,{\mathbb{A}}_{L})
which is left invariant under
{\rm GSp}(4,L)
and is, in fact, an automorphic form of
{\rm GSp}(4,{\mathbb{A}}_{L})
.

In order to make a good choice for
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(X({\mathbb{A}}_{L})^{2})
we need to examine the problem locally. Now let
L_{v}
be a local field and let
E_{w}
be a quadratic extension of
L_{v}
. Using the path outlined in the global case one may determine a
{\rm GO}(X,L_{v})
representation
(\sigma_{v},\mathcal{W})
from the data of an infinite-dimensional irreducible admissible representation
\pi_{v}
of
\mathop{\rm GL}(2,E_{w})
. Let
R_{v}=\{(g,h)\in{\rm GSp}(4,L_{v})\times{\rm GO}(X(L_{v}))\mid\lambda(g)=\lambda(h)\}
where the similitude factor of each coordinate matches and let
\mathcal{S}(X(L_{v})^{2})
be the Schwartz functions of
X(L_{v})^{2}
. When
\sigma_{v}
is irreducible as a
{\rm GO}(X)
-representation, it is the direct sum of two isomorphic
{\rm GSO}(X)
-representations. Throughout this work, we choose a distinguished
{\rm GSO}(X)
-representation, which we denote with a
+
. We provide data so that the local theta correspondence can be explicitly realized. Define the intertwining map

(2.2)

B(g,\varphi,W,s)=\int\limits_{H\backslash{\rm SO}(X)}\big(\omega(g,hh^{\prime})\varphi\big)(x_{1},x_{2})Z(s,\pi(hh^{\prime})W)\,dh

where
g\in\mathop{\rm GSp}(4,L_{v}),\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(X(L_{v})^{2}),W\in\mathcal{W},\Re(s)\gg 0
,
Z(s,W)
is the zeta integral as defined in (
3.3
) or (
3.6
), and
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
are certain specified elements and
H
is their stabilizer in
{\rm SO}(X)
. Indeed, the space of functions
\{B(\cdot,\varphi,W,s)\mid\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(X^{2}),W\in V\}
is an irreducible admissible representation
\Theta(\pi^{+}_{v})=\Pi_{v}
of
\mathop{\rm GSp}(4,L_{v})
. The representation
\Pi_{v}
has a canonical paramodular level
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}^{N}
. Furthermore, by
[
Rob01
]
the intertwining map is unique. The main result of this work is summarized below.

Local Main Theorem
.

Let
L
be a non-archimedean local field of characteristic
0
and let
E
be either a real quadratic field extension of
L
or let
E=L\times L
. If

is a field let
{\mathfrak{P}}
be the unique maximal ideal of the ring of integers of

{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}
, and let

If

\mathop{\rm GL}(2,L)

. If

(\pi(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}),V)
, where

is the associated
+
-representation of
\mathop{\rm GSO}(X)
.

If

is a field let
(\pi(1,\tau_{0}),V)
, where
V=\mathcal{W}(\tau_{0})
is the associated
+
-representation of
\mathop{\rm GSO}(X)
, and let

be a local
\mathop{\rm GL}(2,E)
-newform with

-invariance. If

If

The compatibility of this local map with the global theta lift follows from Corollary

3.1.
Whittaker Models

Let
\psi
be a non-trivial additive character of

. If

is non-archimedean we let
\mathcal{W}({\rm GL}(2,L),\psi)
be the

-vector space of functions

such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle W([\begin{smallmatrix}1&x\\
&1\end{smallmatrix}]g)=\psi(x)W(g)

for

\mathcal{W}(\mathop{\rm GL}(2,L),\psi)

-representation under right translation. We say that a representation

.

Let

\psi
be a non-trivial additive character of

be an irreducible admissible representation of

is infinite-dimensional if and only if

Proof.

See Theorem 3.5.3 of
[
Bum97
]
, for example.
∎

Let

be an irreducible admissible

-module for

See Theorem 2.8.1 of

Bum97

) and assume that

. Then, the function

as defined in (

) is non-zero and
{\rm K}({\mathfrak{P}}^{N})
-invariant, where

. In particular

.

Proof.

By construction we have that
\tilde{\varphi}
is paramodular invariant under the action of the Weil representation. It follows that
B(\cdot,\tilde{\varphi},W,s)
is also paramodular invariant. We write a generic element of
H\backslash{\rm SO}(X)
as
h=\rho(1,[\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}])
. Using Lemma
5.14
to determine the support of the intertwining map we write

\displaystyle B(1,\varphi^{(1)},W,s)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{H\backslash{\rm SO}(X)}\omega(1,h)\varphi^{(1)}(x_{1},x_{2})Z(s,\pi(h)W)\,dh

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{H\backslash\rho({\mathfrak{o}}_{L}^{\times},\big[\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}\\
\varpi_{E}^{n}{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}\end{smallmatrix}\big])\cap\mathop{\rm SO}(X)}\omega(1,h)\varphi^{(1)}(x_{1},x_{2})Z(s,\pi(h)W)\,dh

\displaystyle=q_{L}^{2}\int\limits_{H\backslash\rho({\mathfrak{o}}_{L}^{\times},\big[\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}\\
\varpi_{E}^{n}{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}\end{smallmatrix}\big])\cap\mathop{\rm SO}(X)}\chi(2c\alpha(c)d\alpha(d)\delta^{-1})Z(s,\pi(h)W)\,dh

\displaystyle=q_{L}^{2}{\rm vol}(H\backslash\rho({\mathfrak{o}}_{L}^{\times},\big[\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}\\
\varpi_{E}^{n}{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}\end{smallmatrix}\big])\cap\mathop{\rm SO}(X))\chi(2\delta)Z(s,W)

also,

\displaystyle B(1,\varphi^{(3)},W,s)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{H\backslash{\rm SO}(X)}\omega(1,h)\varphi^{(3)}(x_{1},x_{2})Z(s,\pi(h)W)\,dh

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{H\backslash\rho({\mathfrak{o}}_{L}^{\times},\big[\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}\\
{\mathfrak{P}}^{n+1}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}\end{smallmatrix}\big])\cap\mathop{\rm SO}(X)}\omega(1,h)\varphi^{(3)}(x_{1},x_{2})Z(s,\pi(h)W)\,dh

\displaystyle=q_{L}\int\limits_{H\backslash\rho({\mathfrak{o}}_{L}^{\times},\big[\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}\\
{\mathfrak{P}}^{n+1}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}\end{smallmatrix}\big])\cap\mathop{\rm SO}(X)}\chi(-2d\alpha(d)a\alpha(a)\delta^{-1})Z(s,\pi(h)W)\,dh

\displaystyle=q_{L}{\rm vol}(H\backslash\rho({\mathfrak{o}}_{L}^{\times},\big[\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}\\
{\mathfrak{P}}^{n+1}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}\end{smallmatrix}\big])\cap\mathop{\rm SO}(X))\chi(2)Z(s,W).

If
n=0
then we also have a contribution from

\displaystyle B(1,\varphi^{(2)},W,s)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{H\backslash{\rm SO}(X)}\omega(1,h)\varphi^{(2)}(x_{1},x_{2})Z(s,\pi(h)W)\,dh

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{H\backslash\rho({\mathfrak{o}}_{L}^{\times},\big[\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathfrak{P}}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}\\

{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}\end{smallmatrix}\big]\cap\mathop{\rm SO}(X)))\chi(2)Z(s,W).

Therefore, from Lemma
5.14
, we find that

\displaystyle B(1,\varphi,W,s)=

\displaystyle B(1,\varphi^{(1)}+\varphi^{(2)}+\varphi^{(3)},W,s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi(2)Z(s,W)\bigg[\chi(\delta)q_{L}{\rm vol}(H\backslash\rho(1,\big[\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}\\
\varpi^{n}_{E}{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}\end{smallmatrix}\big])\cap\mathop{\rm SO}(X))

\displaystyle+q_{L}^{2}{\rm vol}(H\backslash\rho(1,\big[\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}\\
{\mathfrak{P}}^{n+1}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}\end{smallmatrix}\big])\cap\mathop{\rm SO}(X))+\epsilon(n)q_{L}{\rm vol}(H\backslash\rho(1,\big[\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathfrak{P}}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}\\
{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}\end{smallmatrix}\big])\cap\mathop{\rm SO}(X))\bigg]

where

and is equal to zero elsewhere.

When

we can use the volumes in Table
1
to calculate that

\displaystyle B(1,\varphi,W,s)

\displaystyle=\chi(2)Z(s,W)\bigg[\chi(\delta)q_{L}{\rm vol}(H\backslash\rho(1,\big[\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}\\

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+q_{L}^{2}{\rm vol}(H\backslash\rho(1,\big[\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}\\

since

. When

\displaystyle B(1,\varphi,W,s)

\displaystyle=\chi(2)Z(s,W)\bigg[\chi(\delta)q_{L}{\rm vol}(H\backslash\rho(1,\big[\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}&{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}\\

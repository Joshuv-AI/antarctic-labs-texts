On the sharp multi-bubble stability for fractional Hardy–Sobolev equations – A quantitative approach in low dimensions
Souptik Chakraborty and Utsab Sarkar

Abstract
. We establish sharp quantitative multi-bubble stability for non–sign-changing critical points of the fractional Hardy–Sobolev inequality in the low-dimensional regime 2 ​ s < N < 6 ​ s − 2 ​ t 2s<N<6s-2t . For functions whose energy is close to that of a finite superposition of bubbles, we prove that the Euler–Lagrange deficit controls linearly the distance, in the homogeneous fractional Sobolev norm, to the multi-bubble manifold, and we recover the precise bubble configuration. This yields quantitative rigidity under arbitrary finite weak interactions. The proof combines a localization scheme adapted to the Hardy weight, weighted fractional Kato–Ponce commutator estimates, a bubble-wise spectral gap inequality, and a sharp interaction analysis. We also show that the linear rate is optimal by constructing a matching counterexample.

Introduction

Hardy–Sobolev inequalities, both local and fractional, play a central role in the analysis of critical elliptic equations, capturing the sharp interaction between nonlocal diffusion, singular weights, and critical exponents; see

. A major theme in recent works have been the development of
stability theories
: understanding how the Euler–Lagrange deficit associated with a critical functional governs the distance to certain manifold of extremal functions in a quantitative fashion. In the classical Sobolev case, a comprehensive picture has emerged, from the early variational and topological methods of
[

.

In the fractional and weighted setting the landscape is more delicate. The nonlocal operator obstructs standard localization techniques, the Hardy weight modifies the linearized spectrum, and bubble interactions occur across multiple scales. In our earlier work
[
7

, we established sharp quantitative stability for the fractional Hardy–Sobolev inequality and the associated Euler–Lagrange equation in the

Our goal here is to establish the multi-bubble stability theory for the fractional Hardy–Sobolev equation (

is quantitatively controlled by the Euler–Lagrange deficit (

). This characterizes the full structure of near-extremal configurations for the fractional Hardy–Sobolev inequality (
1.1
) and yields a weighted, genuinely nonlocal analogue of the multi-bubble rigidity phenomena previously available only in the classical or unweighted fractional Sobolev settings. In particular, it extends the quantitative multi-bubble theory for critical Sobolev and Hardy–Sobolev inequalities and their associated Euler–Lagrange equations on
\mathbb{R}^{N}

1.1.
Background and Hurdles

We begin with the fractional Hardy–Sobolev inequality (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, which forms
the variational foundation for the present study:

(1.1)

\mu_{s,t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad u\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Here
s\in(0,1)
,
t\in(0,2s)
,
N>2s
is a natural number, and the critical Hardy–Sobolev exponent

2_{s}^{\star}(t)=\tfrac{2(N-t)}{N-2s}
satisfies
2<2_{s}^{\star}(t)<\tfrac{2N}{N-2s}
.
The optimal constant
\mu_{s,t}>0
is characterized variationally by

(1.2)

\mu_{s,t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\inf_{u\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}}{\big\||x|^{\frac{-t}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}u\big\|_{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}^{2}},

and is attained by a radially symmetric, nonnegative, strictly decreasing minimizer
U_{s,t}
(see e.g.
[
24
]
).

All minimizers are obtained from
U_{s,t}
through the natural scaling

(1.3)

cU^{\lambda}_{s,t}(x)\coloneqq c\lambda^{\frac{N-2s}{2}}U_{s,t}(\lambda x),\qquad c\neq 0,\,\lambda>0.

Under the normalization
\||x|^{\frac{-t}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}U_{s,t}\|_{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}=1
, we define
V_{s,t}\coloneqq\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{1}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)-2}}U_{s,t}
, so that the rescaled bubbles
V_{s,t}^{\lambda}
satisfy

(1.4)

\|V^{\lambda}_{s,t}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{N-t}{2(2s-t)}},\quad\||x|^{\frac{-t}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}V^{\lambda}_{s,t}\|_{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}=\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{1}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)-2}},\qquad\forall\lambda>0.

The function
V_{s,t}
is a weak solution of the normalized Euler–Lagrange
equation

(1.5)

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}u=\dfrac{|u|^{2_{s}^{\star}(t)-2}u}{|x|^{t}},\quad u\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\\[2.84526pt]
u>0\ \text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\end{cases}

enjoying the uniqueness, growth and regularity properties described in
[
25
, Theorem 1.1]
. Moreover, any other solution to (
1.5
) is given by
V_{s,t}^{\lambda}
for some
\lambda>0
.

Remark 1.1
.

We collect two structural properties of the bubbles
V=V^{\lambda}_{s,t}
that will be used throughout the analysis.

(1)

By
[
25
, Lemma 4.2]
and
[
7
, Theorem 6.2]
, the operator

\frac{\left(-\Delta\right)^{s}}{V^{p-1}|x|^{-t}};\quad p+1=2_{s}^{\star}(t),

has a discrete spectrum whose first two eigenpairs are
(1,V)
and
(p,\dot{V})
.
The eigenvalues are strictly increasing, diverge to
\infty
, and do not depend on the scale
\lambda>0
.
Moreover,
[
25
, Theorem 1.2]
shows that the third eigenvalue is strictly larger than
p
.

(2)

The pointwise bound
|\dot{V}(x)|\lesssim_{N,s,t}\frac{V(x)}{\lambda}
holds on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
by
[
25
, Eq. 19, p. 301 & Theorem 1.1]
.
Using this together with (
1.4
) and (
1.1
), we obtain

\|\dot{V}\|_{L^{p+1}_{|x|^{-t}}}\lesssim_{N,s,t}\|\dot{V}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\lesssim_{N,s,t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\|V\|_{L^{p+1}_{|x|^{-t}}}^{\frac{p+1}{2}}\lesssim_{N,s,t,}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}.

To measure the deviation of a function from satisfying (
1.5
), we introduce the
Euler–Lagrange deficit

(1.6)

\Gamma(u)\coloneqq\left\|(-\Delta)^{s}u-\frac{|u|^{2_{s}^{\star}(t)-2}u}{|x|^{t}}\right\|_{(\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))^{\prime}}.

This deficit (
\Gamma
) plays the same structural role as in the classical stability theory for
critical equations, and aligns with the concentration-compactness principles of Lions
[
22
,
23
]
and
the multi-bubble analysis initiated by Struwe and successors
[
28
,
6
,
27
,
16
,
29
,
26
]
.

Our earlier work
[
7
, Theorem 1.4]
established a Struwe-type
qualitative
stability result for (
1.5
). In particular, non-sign-changing sequences with nearly optimal Hardy–Sobolev energy and vanishing deficit admit a decomposition into finitely many well-separated bubbles, mirroring the classical bubbling patterns that arise in critical elliptic problems. For the reader’s convenience, we recall the statement below.

Theorem 1.2

(Qualitative stability)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
,
t\in(0,2s)
,
N>2s
, and
N,\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
\{u_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a sequence of nonnegative functions such that

\Bigl(\nu-\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigr)\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}}\leq\|u_{k}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}\leq\Bigl(\nu+\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigr)\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}},\qquad\Gamma(u_{k})\to 0\ \text{in }(\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))^{\prime}.

Then, up to a subsequence, there exist positive scales

\lambda_{k}^{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}^{\nu}
such that

\left\|u_{k}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}V_{s,t}^{\lambda_{k}^{i}}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\to 0.

If
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{0}\neq 0
, then
\lambda_{k}^{1}=\lambda_{0}
for all
k
; whereas if
u_{k}\rightharpoonup 0
, no scale remains fixed. Moreover,

\lambda_{k}^{i}\to 0\ \text{or}\ \lambda_{k}^{i}\to\infty,\qquad\big|\log(\lambda_{k}^{i}/\lambda_{k}^{j})\big|\to\infty\quad(i\neq j),

except in the fixed-scale case.

The purpose of the present work is to develop the
quantitative
analogue of Theorem
1.2
. Restricting to the low-dimensional range
2s<N<6s-2t
, we prove that the deficit
\Gamma(u)

linearly
controls the
\dot{H}^{s}
-distance from the multi-bubble manifold

\mathcal{M}=\Bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}V_{s,t}^{\lambda_{i}}:\lambda_{i}>0\Bigg\}.

This extends the one-bubble stability theory of
[
7
]
to arbitrary
\nu\geq 1
and
constitutes the first quantitative stability result (Theorem (
1.3
)) for the fractional Hardy–Sobolev
equation (
1.1
) in the multi-bubble setting.

A fundamental difficulty in the fractional Hardy–Sobolev setting is that localization interacts poorly with both the nonlocal operator and the Hardy weight: any naive cutoff produces long-range errors, and classical commutator bounds do not capture the weighted structure of the problem. To overcome these obstacles, our analysis relies on two principal tools developed in Appendix
A
. The first is a sharp cutoff estimate (Proposition
A.6
) for logarithmic bump functions, which provides precise control of the fractional Laplacian on transition annuli. The second is a weighted fractional Kato–Ponce type commutator estimate (Theorem
A.4
), tailored to accommodate the Hardy singularity. Together, these results deliver robust bounds on localization errors and on mixed fractional terms arising from the interaction between cutoffs and the operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
. When coupled with our refined bump-function projection scheme (Lemma
2.3
) and the weighted spectral gap inequality (Proposition
2.5
), these analytic inputs yield the quantitative stability result (Theorem
1.4
) for families of weakly interacting bubbles (cf. (
2.1
)). Finally, incorporating the robustness of bubble coefficients (Proposition
2.6
) allows us to upgrade the stability estimate from the localized projection level to the full multi-bubble manifold, thereby completing the low-dimensional component of the fractional Hardy–Sobolev stability theory, as stated in Theorem
1.3
.

In a forthcoming companion work, we will address the multi-bubble scenario in the
high-dimensional
regime
N\geq 6s-2t
, where additional degeneracies appear and new techniques are required. Taken together, these results aim to place the fractional Hardy–Sobolev stability theory on the same level of precision as its classical counterpart while introducing analytic tools, in particular our weighted commutator estimate, that may prove useful across a wider class of nonlocal PDEs with singular potentials.

1.2.
Main Results

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
,
t\in(0,2s)
, and choose integers
N\in(2s,\,6s-2t),\,\nu\geq 1
. Let
u\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a non-negative function satisfying

(1.7)

\left(\nu-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}}\leq\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\leq\left(\nu+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}}.

Then there exist a constant
C(N,\nu,s,t)>0
and parameters

\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{\nu}>0
such that

u=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}V_{s,t}^{\lambda_{i}}+\rho,

and the error term
\rho
satisfies

(1.8)

\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C(N,\nu,s,t)\,\Gamma(u).

Moreover, this estimate is optimal in the sense that the conclusion fails if the term

\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
is replaced by
\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{1-\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
.

Further, for all
i\neq j
, the
\dot{H}^{s}
-interaction between the bubbles can be estimated as

(1.9)

\langle V_{s,t}^{\lambda_{i}},V_{s,t}^{\lambda_{j}}\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V_{s,t}^{\lambda_{i}}(x)V_{s,t}^{\lambda_{j}}(x)^{2_{s}^{\star}(t)-1}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\lesssim_{N,\nu,s,t}\Gamma(u).

Theorem 1.4
.

For
s\in(0,1)
,
t\in(0,2s)
, and integers
N\in(2s,\,6s-2t),\,\nu\geq 1
, there exist a small constant
\delta(N,\nu,s,t)>0
and a large constant
C(N,\nu,s,t)>0
such that the following property holds. If
u\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a function such that

\Big\|u-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\widetilde{V}_{i}\Big\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\delta,

for a
\delta
-interacting family of bubbles
(\widetilde{V}_{i})_{i=1}^{\nu}
, then there exists a family of
\delta^{\prime}
-interacting bubbles
(V_{i})_{i=1}^{\nu}
such that

\bigg\|u-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}V_{i}\bigg\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C(N,\nu,s,t)\,\bigg\|\left(-\Delta\right)^{s}u-\frac{|u|^{2_{s}^{\star}(t)-2}u}{|x|^{t}}\bigg\|_{(\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))^{\prime}},

where
\delta^{\prime}\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
.

Notations

The following notations will be used consistently throughout the paper, except where a different convention is explicitly specified.

The Schwartz space of rapidly decreasing functions is

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})\coloneqq\left\{f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})\,:\,\forall\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{N},\,\|f\|_{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}<\infty\right\},

\|f\|_{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}\coloneqq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big|x^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\partial^{\boldsymbol{\beta}}f(x)\big|

with

and
\partial^{\boldsymbol{\beta}}=\partial_{x_{1}}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots\partial_{x_{N}}^{\beta_{N}}
.

(2)

For
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

\mathcal{F}(u)(\xi)\coloneqq\widehat{u}(\xi)\coloneqq(2\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}u(x)\,{\rm d}x,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(3)

denotes the standard

-norm. Given a positive weight

with norm
\|f\|_{L^{p}_{w}}^{p}=\|w^{1/p}f\|_{p}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|f|^{p}w\,{\rm d}x
.

(4)

Denote by

the dual of the fractional homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, endowed with the dual norm induced by
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}
. The duality pairing is denoted by
{}_{(\dot{H}^{s})^{\prime}}\langle f,u\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}\text{ for }f\in(\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))^{\prime},u\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).
The Hilbert space inner product on
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is denoted as
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}
and is related to the usual

-inner product by
\langle u,v\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\langle\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}u,\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}v\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(x)\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}v(x)\,{\rm d}x
.

(5)

We write

for some constant
C>0
, and
A\gtrsim B

B\lesssim A
. We also write

. Dependence of the implicit constant on parameters

is indicated by
\lesssim_{a,b,\dots}
, and similarly for

and

.

(6)

We write
C

. In contrast, symbols of the form

2.
Stability in low dimensions
2s<N<6s-2t

Definition 2.1

(
\delta
-interacting bubbles)
.

Let
V_{i}\coloneqq V_{s,t}^{\lambda_{i}}
with scaling magnitude
\lambda_{i}>0
;
1\leq i\leq\nu
, be a family of bubbles. We say that the family is
\delta
-interacting for some
\delta>0
if for all distinct
1\leq i,\,j\leq\nu
,

(2.1)

\min\Bigg\{\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{j}},\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{i}}\Bigg\}\leq\delta.

If we consider for some
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}
, the family
\left(\alpha_{i}V_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\nu}
, then the family is said to be
\delta
-interacting if in addition to (
2.1
)

(2.2)

\max_{1\leq i\leq\nu}|\alpha_{i}-1|\leq\delta

also holds.

Remark 2.2
.

The notion of
\delta
-interaction between bubbles is naturally encoded by their interaction in the
\dot{H}^{s}
inner product. For two bubbles
U_{1}=V_{s,t}^{\lambda_{1}}
and
U_{2}=V_{s,t}^{\lambda_{2}}
, the corresponding
\dot{H}^{s}
-interaction can be quantified explicitly. Since both
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
solve (
1.5
), one has

(2.3)

\langle U_{1},U_{2}\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{U_{1}^{2_{s}^{\star}(t)-1}U_{2}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{U_{1}U_{2}^{2_{s}^{\star}(t)-1}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\sim_{N,s,t}\min\!\left\{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}},\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}\right\}^{\frac{N-2s}{2}},

as a consequence of Corollary
B.2
.

In particular, membership in a

\delta
-interacting family implies that the
\dot{H}^{s}
inner product between distinct bubbles is of order
\mathcal{O}(\delta^{\frac{N-2s}{2}})
.

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

Fix a small parameter
0<\delta\ll 1
, to be chosen later. We consider the finite-dimensional manifold of superpositions of
\nu
bubbles

\mathcal{M}_{\nu}\coloneqq\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\alpha_{i}V_{s,t}^{\lambda_{i}}\;\middle|\;\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{R},\ \lambda_{i}\in(0,\infty)\right\}\subset\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Given
u\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we study the minimization problem

(2.4)

\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{M}_{\nu}}\|u-\sigma\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

For
\epsilon>0
, define the tubular neighborhood

U(\epsilon)\coloneqq\left\{v\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\;\middle|\;\begin{array}[]{l}\text{there exists a $2\epsilon$-interacting family }(\alpha_{i}V_{s,t}^{\lambda_{i}})_{i=1}^{\nu}\\[2.0pt]
\text{such that }\displaystyle\bigg\|v-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\alpha_{i}V_{s,t}^{\lambda_{i}}\bigg\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}<\epsilon\end{array}\right\}.

By
[
2
, Proposition 7]
, whose argument relies on the implicit function theorem applied to the smooth (
C^{1}
) finite-dimensional multi-bubble manifold
\mathcal{M}_{\nu}
and on the non-degeneracy of the bubbles, verified in the present setting by Remark
1.1
, there exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that for every
0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
and every
u\in U(\epsilon)
, the minimization problem (
2.4
) admits a unique solution
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}_{\nu}
, up to permutation of the indices
i
. Moreover, writing
u=\sigma+\rho
, one has the quantitative bound
\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}<\epsilon
.

We denote this unique minimizer by

\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\alpha_{i}V_{i},\qquad V_{i}=V_{s,t}^{\lambda_{i}}.

Now assume that
u
satisfies
\|u-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\widetilde{V}_{i}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\leq\delta
for some
\delta
-interacting family
(\widetilde{V}_{i})_{i=1}^{\nu}
. Choosing
\delta\leq\epsilon_{0}
, we have
u\in U(\delta)
, hence the minimizer
\sigma
is well-defined and satisfies
\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\|u-\sigma\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}<\delta
. Furthermore, by continuous dependence of the minimizer on
u
and stability of interaction parameters under small perturbations (cf.
[
2
, Lemma A.1, A.2]
), the family
(\alpha_{i}V_{i})_{i=1}^{\nu}
is
4\delta
-interacting. Consequently, the parameter
\delta^{\prime}
appearing in the statement of Theorem
1.4
may be taken to be
4\delta
.

Since
\sigma
minimizes the
\dot{H}^{s}
-distance to
u
, standard variational arguments imply that the remainder
\rho=u-\sigma
is orthogonal, in
\dot{H}^{s}
, to the tangent space of
\mathcal{M}_{\nu}
at
\sigma
, namely

\rho\perp_{\dot{H}^{s}}\mathbf{T}_{\sigma}\mathcal{M}_{\nu}=\operatorname{span}\{V_{i},\dot{V}_{i}\,|\,1\leq i\leq\nu\}.

Since
V_{i}
and
\dot{V}_{i}
are eigenfunctions of the operator
\frac{(-\Delta)^{s}}{|x|^{-t}V_{i}^{p-1}}
corresponding to the eigenvalues
1
and
p=2_{s}^{\star}(t)-1
, respectively (see Remark
1.1
-
(1)
), it follows that for every
1\leq i\leq\nu
the following orthogonality relations hold:

(2.5)

\displaystyle 0=\langle\rho,V_{i}\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V_{i}^{p}\rho}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x,

(2.6)

\displaystyle 0=\langle\rho,\dot{V}_{i}\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle=p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V_{i}^{p-1}\dot{V}_{i}\rho}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x.

Our objective is to bound
\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}
in terms of the deficit
\Gamma(u)
. To this end, we exploit the orthogonality condition (
2.5
). Using
integration by parts, we compute

\displaystyle\left\|\rho\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\rho,\rho\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\langle u-\sigma,\rho\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\left\langle(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u,(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\rho\right\rangle

\displaystyle={}_{(\dot{H}^{s})^{\prime}}\bigg\langle(-\Delta)^{s}u-\frac{u|u|^{p-1}}{|x|^{t}},\rho\bigg\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}+{}_{(\dot{H}^{s})^{\prime}}\bigg\langle\frac{u|u|^{p-1}}{|x|^{t}},\rho\bigg\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}

(2.7)

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma(u)\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u|u|^{p-1}\rho}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x.

To estimate the final term in (
2.7
), we invoke the following two
elementary inequalities, which will be used repeatedly in the sequel.

(A)
For any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\geq 1

(2.8)

\left|(a+b)|a+b|^{p-1}-a|a|^{p-1}\right|\leq p|a|^{p-1}|b|+C(p)\Big(\chi_{p>2}|a|^{p-2}|b|^{2}+|b|^{p}\Big),

(B)
For any

\left|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}a_{i}\right)\left|\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}a_{i}\right|^{p-1}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}a_{i}|a_{i}|^{p-1}\right|\lesssim_{p,\nu}\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq\nu}|a_{i}|^{p-1}|a_{j}|.

Applying (

, and

, and noting that

\left|\frac{u|u|^{p-1}}{|x|^{t}}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\alpha_{i}|\alpha_{i}|^{p-1}\frac{V_{i}^{p}}{|x|^{t}}\right|\leq p\frac{\sigma^{p-1}|\rho|}{|x|^{t}}+C(p,\nu)\bigg(\frac{\sigma^{p-2}|\rho|^{2}}{|x|^{t}}+\frac{|\rho|^{p}}{|x|^{t}}+\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq\nu}\frac{V_{i}^{p-1}V_{j}}{|x|^{t}}\bigg).

Therefore, using the orthogonality relations (

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u|u|^{p-1}\rho}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\sigma^{p-1}|\rho|^{2}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x+C(N,\nu,s,t)\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\sigma^{p-2}|\rho|^{3}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x

(2.11)

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\rho|^{p+1}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x+\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq\nu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V_{i}^{p-1}V_{j}|\rho|}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\Bigg).

2.2.
Localization via bump functions

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
N>2s
be the spatial dimension,
\nu\geq 1
be the number of bubbles, and
\varepsilon>0
be a fixed parameter. Assume also that the parameters
t
and
s
satisfy
0<t<2s<2
. Then there exists a constant
\delta=\delta(N,\nu,\varepsilon,s,t)>0
such that the following holds.

Given a
\delta
-interacting family
(V_{i})_{i=1}^{\nu}
, where each
V_{i}=V_{s,t}^{\lambda_{i}}
for some
\lambda_{i}>0
, there exists an associated family of bump functions
(\Phi_{i})_{i=1}^{\nu}
, with
\Phi_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow[0,1]
, satisfying the following properties for every
i
.

(1)

\int_{\left\{\Phi_{i}<1\right\}}\frac{V_{i}^{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\leq\varepsilon\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}}
.

(2)

V_{i}>\frac{V_{j}}{\varepsilon}
for all
j\neq i
on the set
\{\Phi_{i}>0\}
.

(3)

\forall j\neq i
such that
\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda_{i}
, one has
\sup_{\left\{\Phi_{i}>0\right\}}V_{j}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\inf_{\left\{\Phi_{i}>0\right\}}V_{j}
.

(4)

\left\||x|^{\frac{t}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\Phi_{i}\right\|_{\frac{2\cdot 2_{s}^{\star}(t)}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)-2}}<\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Properties (1)–(3) follow directly from
[
14
, Lemma 3.9]
once specialized to the present (bubbles with fixed centers) setting. In the following, we restrict our attention to establishing property (4), for which Lemma
A.6
serves as a substitute for
[
14
, Lemma 3.8]
.

By the cutoff construction in
[
14
, Lemma 3.9]
, each bump function
\Phi_{i}

is given as a finite product of at most
\nu
radial cutoffs. More precisely,

\Phi_{i}=f_{i}f_{m(1)}\cdots f_{m(i)},\qquad 1\leq m(i)\leq\nu,

where each factor
f_{k}
equals
1-\varphi_{r_{k},R_{k}}
for
k\neq i
, while

f_{i}=\varphi_{r_{i},R_{i}}
for suitable radii
0<r_{k}<R_{k}
(see (
A.5
)).

Denote,
q=\tfrac{2\cdot 2_{s}^{\star}(t)}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)-2}
. We employ a standard weighted fractional Leibniz estimate, specifically, the endpoint
L^{\infty}
Kato–Ponce inequality as stated in
[
19
, Theorem 1.4]
with the Muckenhoupt
\mathcal{A}_{q}
weight
w=|x|^{\frac{tq}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}
. Note
w\in\mathcal{A}_{q}
as
N>2s>t
.

This yields the bound

\|\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}(f_{1}f_{2}\cdots f_{m})\|_{L^{q}_{w}}\lesssim_{N,s,t}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\|\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}f_{k}\|_{L^{q}_{w}}.

In particular, this implies

(2.20)

\left\|\,|x|^{\frac{t}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\Phi_{i}\right\|_{q}\lesssim_{N,s,t}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\|\,|x|^{\frac{t}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}f_{k}\right\|_{q}.

By applying the logarithmic bound from Lemma
A.6
to a single cutoff function
f_{k}=\varphi_{r_{k},R_{k}}
(or
1-\varphi_{r_{k},R_{k}}
) for sufficiently large ratios
R_{k}/r_{k}
, and substituting this estimate into (
2.20
), we obtain, using the fact that
m(\cdot)\leq\nu
, the following bound:

(2.21)

\left\|\,|x|^{\frac{t}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\Phi_{i}\right\|_{q}\lesssim_{N,\nu,s,t}\max_{1\leq k\leq\nu}\left(\log\frac{R_{k}}{r_{k}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}.

We next relate the size of the transition annuli to the interaction parameter
\delta
. Since the family
(V_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\nu}
is
\delta
-interacting, any two scales
\lambda_{i}
and
\lambda_{j}
with
i\neq j
satisfy
\min\!\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{j}},\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{i}}\right)\leq\delta
, which implies

\min_{i\neq j}\max\!\left\{\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{j}},\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{i}}\right\}\geq\frac{1}{\delta}.

After reordering the bubbles in a decreasing order of concentration scales, distinct bubbles correspond to scales separated by a factor of at least
1/\delta
.

In the construction of
\Phi_{i}
, each cutoff annulus
[r_{k},R_{k}]
is positioned between two such scales to isolate
V_{i}
. Therefore, it follows that

(2.22)

\min_{1\leq k\leq\nu}\frac{R_{k}}{r_{k}}\geq C\delta^{-1}

for some constant
C>0
depending only on
\nu
.

Choice of
\boldsymbol{\delta(N,\nu,\varepsilon,s,t)}
:
Combining the estimates from (
2.21
) and (
2.22
), we have

\left\|\,|x|^{\frac{t}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\Phi_{i}\right\|_{q}\lesssim_{N,\nu,s,t}\left(\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, it suffices to ensure that

\left(\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}\lesssim\varepsilon,

which is equivalent to

(2.23)

\delta\lesssim\exp\big(-\varepsilon^{-2_{s}^{\star}(t)}\big).

With this choice of
\delta
in (
2.23
), the estimate in property (4) holds uniformly for every
i
.∎

In order to verify that the orthogonality conditions (
2.5
)-(
2.6
) with respect to
V_{i}
and
\dot{V}_{i}
are almost preserved under localization, we establish the following estimate.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\rho\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be such that
\rho\perp_{\dot{H}^{s}}\operatorname{span}\{V_{i},\dot{V}_{i}\}.
Let
\left(\Phi_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\nu}
denote the family of bump functions associated with the parameter
\varepsilon>0
, as introduced in Lemma
2.3
. Then, one has

\langle\rho\Phi_{i},V_{i}\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\mathcal{o}(1)\,\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}},\qquad\langle\rho\Phi_{i},\dot{V}_{i}\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\mathcal{o}(1)\,\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}.

Here, the notation
\mathcal{o}(1)
stands for a quantity that vanishes in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Proof.

Let
g
be the function
V_{i}
or
\dot{V}_{i}
,
p+1=2_{s}^{\star}(t)
and
q=\frac{2\cdot 2_{s}^{\star}(t)}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)-2}
. Notice that the fact
\rho\perp_{\dot{H}^{s}}g\iff\langle\rho,g\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}=0
and then using integration by parts, Hölder inequality (with
\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{q}=1
), Remark
1.1
-
(2)
, Lemma
2.3
-(1), and finally the Hardy–Sobolev inequality (
1.1
),

\displaystyle\Bigg|\langle\rho\Phi_{i},g\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}\Bigg|

\displaystyle=\Bigg|\langle\rho,g\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}-\langle\rho(1-\Phi_{i}),g\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}\Bigg|=\mu\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho(1-\Phi_{i})g\frac{V_{i}^{p-1}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mu\|\rho\|_{L^{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}_{|x|^{-t}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g^{2}\frac{V_{i}^{p-1}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\left\{\Phi_{i}<1\right\}}\frac{V_{i}^{p+1}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{o}(1)\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}},

where
\mu=1
or
p
according to
g=V_{i}
or
\dot{V}_{i}
respectively.∎

2.3.
Spectral gap inequality

Using the localization from Lemma
2.3
, the proof of Proposition
2.5
proceeds naturally: localize, apply the Rayleigh quotient inequality (
A.4
) to each bubble, and sum the results for the full estimate.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
s\in(0,1),\,N>2s,\,\nu\geq 1
and
0<t<2s
. There exists
\delta=\delta(N,\nu,s,t)>0
such that, if
(\alpha_{i}V_{i})_{i=1}^{\nu}
is a
\delta

2.5
), (
2.6
), then there exists a constant
C\coloneqq C(N,\nu,s,t)\in(0,1)
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\sigma|^{2_{s}^{\star}(t)-2}\rho^{2}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\leq\frac{C}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)-1}\,\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2},\hskip 5.69054pt\text{where }\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\alpha_{i}V_{i}.

Proof.

Let us denote
p+1=2_{s}^{\star}(t)
and
q=\frac{2\cdot 2_{s}^{\star}(t)}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)-2}
. By choosing
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small in Lemma
2.3
, one guarantees the existence of a constant
\delta>0
, where
\delta=\mathcal{o}(1)
as established in (
2.23
). Furthermore, under this choice of
\varepsilon
, there exists a family of bump functions
(\Phi_{i})_{i=1}^{\nu}
satisfying the properties specified in Lemma
2.3
. Here, the notation
\mathcal{o}(1)
refers to a positive quantity that tends to zero as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Write

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\sigma|^{p-1}\rho^{2}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\Phi_{i}\geq 1\right\}}+\int_{\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\Phi_{i}<1\right\}}\right)\frac{|\sigma|^{p-1}\rho^{2}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\eqqcolon I+II.

Notice by Hölder inequality (with
\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{2}
), Hardy–Sobolev inequality (
1.1
) and property (1) in Lemma
2.3
,

\displaystyle II\leq\|\rho\|_{L^{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}_{|x|^{-t}}}^{2}\left(\int_{\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\hskip-5.69054pt\Phi_{i}<1\right\}}\frac{|\sigma|^{p+1}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}\hskip-4.83694pt\lesssim_{N,s,t}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\int_{\left\{\Phi_{i}<1\right\}}\frac{V_{i}^{p+1}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}=\mathcal{o}(1)\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}.

Property (2) in Lemma
2.3
, yields
|\sigma|\leq(1+\mathcal{o}(1))V_{i}
on the region
\left\{\Phi_{i}>0\right\}
and then

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\int_{\{\Phi_{i}>0\}}\frac{|\sigma|^{p-1}\rho^{2}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\leq(1+\mathcal{o}(1))\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\int_{\{\Phi_{i}>0\}}\frac{V_{i}^{p-1}\rho^{2}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq(1+\mathcal{o}(1))\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V_{i}^{p-1}(\rho\Phi_{i})^{2}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x+\mathcal{o}(1)\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}\right).

Since
\rho
satisfies the orthogonality conditions (
2.5
), (
2.6
), we have for each
i
,
\rho\perp_{\dot{H}^{s}}\operatorname{span}\{V_{i},\dot{V}_{i}\}
and Lemma
2.4
applies. Now taking
f_{\varepsilon}=\frac{\rho\Phi_{i}}{\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}}
in Lemma
A.5
, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V_{i}^{p-1}(\rho\Phi_{i})^{2}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\leq\frac{1}{\eta_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}(\rho\Phi_{i})\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\mathcal{o}(1)\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}.

\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}(\rho\Phi_{i})=\rho\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\Phi_{i}+\Phi_{i}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\rho+\mathcal{C}[\rho,\Phi_{i}]

A.4

\displaystyle\left\|\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}(\rho\Phi_{i})\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\rho\right\|_{L^{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}_{|x|^{-t}}}\left\||x|^{\frac{t}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\Phi_{i}\right\|_{q}\hskip-2.84526pt+\left\|\Phi_{i}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\rho\right\|_{2}\hskip-2.84526pt+C\left\|\rho\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{w_{1}}}\left\|\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\Phi_{i}\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{w_{2}}}

which essentially proves the result since

2.4.
Taming interaction integrals and the robustness of bubble coefficients

In this subsection we quantify how weakly interacting bubbles influence one another and establish the stability of their coefficients under small perturbations. These bubble-bubble interaction bounds and coefficient robustness estimates furnish the final structural input needed to complete the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
N>2s
and
\nu\geq 1
be integers. For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists

\delta=\delta(N,\nu,\varepsilon,s,t)>0
such that the following holds.
Assume that
(\alpha_{i}V_{i})_{i=1}^{\nu}
is a
\delta
-interacting family and that

u=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\alpha_{i}V_{i}+\rho,

where
\rho
satisfies the orthogonality conditions (
2.5
)-(
2.6
) and

\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}<1
. Then the bubble coefficients are stable in the sense that, for each

1\leq i\leq\nu
,

(2.24)

|\alpha_{i}-1|\lesssim_{N,\nu,s,t}\mathcal{o}(1)\,\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\Gamma(u)+\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\min(2,2_{s}^{\star}(t)-1)}.

Moreover, bubble-bubble interactions are quantitatively controlled: for every distinct
pair
(i,j)
,

(2.25)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V_{i}^{2_{s}^{\star}(t)-1}V_{j}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\lesssim_{N,\nu,s,t}\mathcal{o}(1)\,\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\Gamma(u)+\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\min(2,2_{s}^{\star}(t)-1)}.

Here
\mathcal{o}(1)
denotes a quantity that tends to
0
as

\varepsilon\to 0
.

Proof.

We begin by ordering the bubbles according to their concentration scales,

\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}>\cdots>\lambda_{\nu},

so that
V_{1}
is the most concentrated profile around the origin. The proof proceeds by induction on the index
i
.
The base case
i=1
follows from the single-bubble analysis carried out in
[
7
, Theorem 1.5]
. Assume now that the conclusion of Proposition
2.6
holds for every index

. For
\varepsilon<1

\int_{\{\Phi_{i}<1\}}\frac{V_{i}^{p}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\leq\int_{|x|>\varepsilon^{-1}}\frac{V_{i}^{p}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x+\nu\int_{|x|<\varepsilon}\frac{V_{i}^{p}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\lesssim_{N,s,t}\varepsilon^{2s-t}+\nu\varepsilon^{N-t}=\mathcal{o}(1),

where the final bound follows from the computation in (
2.4
).

Notice that (
2.31
) closes the induction step at level
j=i
, and therefore yields
(
2.24
) for all
1\leq i\leq\nu
, provided that the quantity

(2.33)

A[\Psi]-(1\pm\mathcal{o}(1))\,B[\Psi]\theta

does not become too small simultaneously for the two choices
\Psi=V_{i}
and

\Psi=\dot{V}_{i}
.

To verify this, we argue by contradiction. Suppose that

A[\Psi]-(1\pm\mathcal{o}(1))\,B[\Psi]\theta=\pm\mathcal{o}(1)\qquad\text{for all }\Psi\in\{V_{i},\dot{V}_{i}\}.

If
B[\Psi]\neq 0
, this implies

\theta=\frac{A[\Psi]}{B[\Psi]}\pm\mathcal{o}(1).

Since
\theta\neq 0
is a fixed quantity defined above, we can then conclude

\frac{A[V_{i}]}{B[V_{i}]}=\frac{A[\dot{V}_{i}]}{B[\dot{V}_{i}]}.

We show below that this equality cannot occur, which yields the desired contradiction. Observe that, by (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), the quantities

A[\Psi]
and
B[\Psi]
can be computed explicitly for
\Psi\in\{V_{i},\dot{V}_{i}\}

Indeed,

A[V_{i}]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V_{i}^{p+1}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x=\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)-1}},

while

A[\dot{V}_{i}]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V_{i}^{p}\dot{V}_{i}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x=\frac{1}{p+1}\,\partial_{\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(V_{s,t}^{\lambda})^{p+1}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\Big|_{\lambda=\lambda_{i}=1}=0.

Moreover,

B[V_{i}]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V_{i}^{p}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\neq 0,

and

B[\dot{V}_{i}]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V_{i}^{p-1}\dot{V}_{i}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x=\frac{1}{p}\,\partial_{\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V_{s,t}^{\lambda}(x)^{p}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\Big|_{\lambda=\lambda_{i}=1}=-\frac{N-2s}{2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V_{s,t}(x)^{p}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\neq 0.

Finally, by
[
25

, the bubble satisfies the pointwise asymptotics
V_{s,t}(x)\sim(1+|x|^{N-2s})^{-1}
, so that
V_{s,t}(x)\sim 1
for
|x|\leq 1
and
V_{s,t}(x)\sim|x|^{-(N-2s)}
for
|x|>1

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V_{s,t}(x)^{p}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|\leq 1}\frac{V_{s,t}(x)^{p}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{|x|>1}\frac{V_{s,t}(x)^{p}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x

(2.34)

\displaystyle\sim_{N,s,t}\int_{0}^{1}r^{N-1-t}\,{\rm d}r+\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-1-(2s-t)}\,{\rm d}r=\tfrac{N+2s-2t}{(N-t)(2s-t)}.

Thus, for each
\Psi\in\{V_{i},\dot{V}_{i}\}
one has
0<|B[\Psi]|<\infty

\frac{A[V_{i}]}{B[V_{i}]}\neq 0=\frac{A[\dot{V}_{i}]}{B[\dot{V}_{i}]},

affirming the desired contradiction. Therefore, by choosing an appropriate
\Psi
in (
2.31
), we obtain (
2.24
).

To conclude the proof of (
2.25
), we set
\Psi=V_{i}
in (
2.27
). By the triangle inequality this yields

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\left(p\alpha_{i}^{p-1}+\mathcal{o}(1)\right)V_{i}^{\,p}\widetilde{\sigma}_{i}}{|x|^{t}}\,\Phi_{i}\,{\rm d}x\Bigg|

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim_{N,s,t}\mathcal{o}(1)\,\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\Gamma(u)+\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{\min(2,p)}+|\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{i}^{p}|\,\|V_{i}\|_{L^{p+1}_{|x|^{-t}}}^{p+1}.

Applying (
2.24

j\neq i

(2.35)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V_{i}^{p}\widetilde{\sigma}_{i}\,\Phi_{i}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\lesssim_{N,\nu,s,t}\mathcal{o}(1)\,\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\Gamma(u)+\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{\min(2,p)}.

We now remove the cutoff. Since
\lambda_{i}=1
, we have
\supp\Phi_{i}=\{|x|\leq 1\}
, thus in particular, estimate (
2.35
) yields, for every
j\neq i

(2.36)

\int_{|x|\leq 1}\frac{V_{i}^{p}V_{j}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\lesssim_{N,\nu,s,t}\mathcal{o}(1)\,\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\Gamma(u)+\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{\min(2,p)}.

Next, invoking (
2.3
), Corollary
B.3
for indices
j>i
, and performing the change of variable associated with (
1.3

(2.37)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V_{i}^{p}V_{j}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V_{j}^{p}V_{i}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\sim_{N,s,t}\int_{|x|\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}}}\frac{V_{j}^{p}V_{i}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x=\int_{|x|\leq 1}\frac{V_{i}^{p}V_{j}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x.

Combining (
2.37

2.5.1.
Sharpness in low dimensions

We work in the low-dimensional regime
2s<N<6s-2t
, equivalently
p=2_{s}^{\star}(t)-1>2
. Arguing by contradiction, we assume that there exists
\varepsilon>0
(fixed) such that

(2.38)

\Gamma(u)\;\gtrsim_{N,\nu,s,t}\;\|u-\sigma\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{\,1-\varepsilon}

for every nonnegative
u\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying the energy bound (
1.7
), where
\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}V_{i}
is a multi-bubble profile.

Fix
0<\delta\ll 1
and by Theorem
1.2
, choose scales
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{\nu}>0
such that for all
i\neq j
,

\Bigl|\log\!\Bigl(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr)\Bigr|\geq\log\!\Bigl(\frac{1}{\delta}\Bigr),\hskip 8.53581pt\text{equivalently}\hskip 8.53581ptQ_{ij}=\min\Bigl\{\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{j}},\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{i}}\Bigr\}\leq\delta.

Set
V_{i}\coloneqq V_{s,t}^{\lambda_{i}}
and
\sigma\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}V_{i}
. Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be nonnegative and define for each
\kappa>0
,

u_{\kappa}\coloneqq\sigma+\kappa\,\phi.

Then

(2.39)

\|u_{\kappa}-\sigma\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\kappa\,\|\phi\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\sim\kappa.

Using (
1.4
) and Corollary
B.2
, one has
\|V_{i}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}=\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}}
and
\langle V_{i},V_{j}\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}\lesssim Q_{ij}^{\frac{N-2s}{2}}
whenever

\|\sigma\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}=\nu\,\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}}+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{\frac{N-2s}{2}}),

and hence
\|u_{\kappa}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}=\|\sigma\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}+\kappa^{2}\|\phi\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}+2\kappa\langle\sigma,\phi\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\nu\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}}+\mathcal{O}(\kappa+\kappa^{2}+\delta^{\frac{N-2s}{2}})
. In particular, choosing
\delta=\kappa^{\frac{2}{N-2s}}
,
u_{\kappa}
is nonnegative and satisfies (
1.7
) for
\kappa
sufficiently small. Recalling that

\Gamma(u)=\Bigl\|(-\Delta)^{s}u-\frac{u^{p}}{|x|^{t}}\Bigr\|_{(\dot{H}^{s})^{\prime}},\qquad p+1=2_{s}^{\star}(t),

we decompose

(-\Delta)^{s}u_{\kappa}-\frac{u_{\kappa}^{p}}{|x|^{t}}=A_{\kappa}+B_{\kappa}+C_{\kappa},

where

A_{\kappa}\coloneqq\kappa(-\Delta)^{s}\phi,\qquad B_{\kappa}\coloneqq\frac{\sigma^{p}-(\sigma+\kappa\phi)^{p}}{|x|^{t}},\qquad C_{\kappa}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\frac{V_{i}^{p}}{|x|^{t}}-\frac{\sigma^{p}}{|x|^{t}}.

Estimate of
A_{\kappa}
.

By definition of
(\dot{H}^{s})^{\prime}
,

(2.40)

\|A_{\kappa}\|_{(\dot{H}^{s})^{\prime}}=\sup_{\|\psi\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=1}\kappa|\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}|\leq\kappa\,\|\phi\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\lesssim\kappa.

Estimate of
B_{\kappa}
.

Using the pointwise inequality (
2.8

|(\sigma+\kappa\phi)^{p}-\sigma^{p}|\leq p\sigma^{p-1}\kappa\phi+C(p)(\sigma^{p-2}\kappa^{2}\phi^{2}+\kappa^{p}\phi^{p}),

we obtain

|B_{\kappa}|\lesssim\kappa\frac{\sigma^{p-1}\phi}{|x|^{t}}+\kappa^{2}\frac{\sigma^{p-2}\phi^{2}}{|x|^{t}}+\kappa^{p}\frac{\phi^{p}}{|x|^{t}}.

By Hölder’s inequality and the Hardy–Sobolev embedding (
1.1
), we have for
\|\psi\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=1

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\sigma^{p-1}\phi\,\psi}{|x|^{t}}\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\|\sigma\|_{L^{p+1}_{|x|^{-t}}}^{p-1}\|\phi\|_{L^{p+1}_{|x|^{-t}}}\|\psi\|_{L^{p+1}_{|x|^{-t}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\sigma\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{p-1}\|\phi\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\|\psi\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\lesssim 1.

The terms involving
\kappa^{2}
and
\kappa^{p}
are treated analogously, yielding for
\kappa\ll 1

(2.41)

\|B_{\kappa}\|_{(\dot{H}^{s})^{\prime}}\lesssim\kappa.

Estimate of
C_{\kappa}
.

Using (
2.9

\Bigl|\sigma^{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}V_{i}^{p}\Bigr|\lesssim\sum_{i\neq j}V_{i}^{p-1}V_{j},

we have, for
\psi\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\|\psi\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=1

|\langle C_{\kappa},\psi\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}|\leq\sum_{i\neq j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V_{i}^{p-1}V_{j}|\psi|}{|x|^{t}}.

Applying Hölder’s inequality and the Hardy–Sobolev inequality (
1.1
) yields

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V_{i}^{p-1}V_{j}|\psi|}{|x|^{t}}\lesssim\|V_{i}^{p-1}V_{j}\|_{L^{\frac{p+1}{p}}_{|x|^{-t}}}.

Therefore, by Corollary
B.2
and the choice

(2.42)

\|C_{\kappa}\|_{(\dot{H}^{s})^{\prime}}\lesssim\sum_{i\neq j}Q_{ij}^{\frac{N-2s}{2}}\lesssim\delta^{\frac{N-2s}{2}}=\kappa.

Combining (
2.40

2.41

\Gamma(u_{\kappa})\lesssim\kappa.

Together with (

Invoking (

Appendix A
Few useful estimates

We seek to establish a Kato–Ponce type estimate for the fractional commutator
\mathcal{C}[f,g]
(as defined in Theorem
A.4
) in
L^{p}_{w}
norms with weights
w
belonging to the Muckenhoupt class
\mathcal{A}_{p}
, as outlined in the Introduction. The unweighted, one-dimensional case (also known as the fractional Leibniz rule) was first established by Kenig, Ponce, and Vega in the seminal work
[
20
, Theorem A.8]
. Since our setting requires a weighted, higher-dimensional version, we outline a proof in Theorem
A.4
below for completeness. While both approaches are grounded in Littlewood–Paley theory, our method circumvents the use of Coifman–Meyer paradifferential calculus by directly applying the LP theory to an alternative paraproduct decomposition (
A.1
).

We begin by introducing some notations and a definition.

Definition A.1

(Muckenhoupt class)
.

Let
w\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\,w\geq 0
. Then
w\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
with
p>1
if there exists
c>0
such that for all balls
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\left(\fint_{B}w(x)\,{\rm d}x\right)\left(\fint_{B}w(x)^{\frac{1}{1-p}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{p-1}\leq c.

For
p=1
, we say that
w\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
if
\left\|\frac{Mw}{w}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}<\infty
. Here
M
denotes the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function, i.e., for any
f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

Mf(x)\coloneqq\sup_{B\ni x}\fint_{B}|f(y)|\,{\rm d}y.

Remark A.2
.

Notice that if
w\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
then
w^{\frac{1}{1-p}}\in\mathcal{A}_{p^{\prime}}
where
p^{\prime}
is the Hölder conjugate of
p
, and
w\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
then
w\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
for any
p>1
. Moreover, if
w_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{p_{1}},\,w_{2}\in\mathcal{A}_{p_{2}}
and
p=\frac{p_{1}p_{2}}{p_{1}+p_{2}}
then
w_{1}^{p/p_{1}}w_{2}^{p/p_{2}}\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
.

Define
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
to be a radial function satisfying
\psi(\xi)=1
for
|\xi|\leq 1
,
\psi(\xi)=0
for
|\xi|\geq 2
and
0\leq\psi\leq 1
.
Let
\varphi(\xi)\coloneqq\psi(\xi)-\psi(2\xi)
, so that
\supp(\varphi)=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\frac{1}{2}\leq|\xi|\leq 2\right\}
. For any
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, define the dyadic rescaling

\varphi_{j}(\xi)\coloneqq\varphi\left(\frac{\xi}{2^{j}}\right),\qquad\psi_{j}(\xi)\coloneqq\psi\left(\frac{\xi}{2^{j}}\right),

and introduce the Littlewood–Paley projection operators
P_{j}
and
P_{\leq j}
via

\widehat{P_{j}f}(\xi)\coloneqq\varphi_{j}(\xi)\,\widehat{f}(\xi),\qquad\widehat{P_{\leq j}f}(\xi)\coloneqq\psi_{j}(\xi)\,\widehat{f}(\xi).

We further define
P_{>j}\coloneqq\mathrm{Id}-P_{\leq j}
. When the context is clear, we will denote
f_{j}\coloneqq P_{j}f
,
f_{\leq j}\coloneqq P_{\leq j}f
, and so on for notational brevity.

Lemma A.3
.

If
s>0,\,1<p<\infty
and
w\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
, then for all
h\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
the following holds.

\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|2^{-js}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}h_{\leq j}\right|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\lesssim_{N,p,s}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Proof.

Lets consider the fattened LP projector
Q_{k},\,k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
Q_{k}h=\widehat{\chi_{k}}*h
where
\chi_{k}(\xi)=\varphi(\frac{\xi}{2^{k+1}})+\varphi(\frac{\xi}{2^{k}})+\varphi(\frac{\xi}{2^{k-1}})
. Then it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\left|2^{-js}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}h_{\leq j}\right|\leq\sum_{k\leq j}2^{-js}\left|\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}h_{k}\right|=\sum_{k\leq j}2^{-js}\left|\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}Q_{k}h_{k}\right|\lesssim_{N,s}\sum_{k\leq j}2^{(k-j)s}Mh_{k}.

Observe by Minkowski inequality,

\displaystyle\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\sum_{k\leq j}2^{(k-j)s}Mh_{k}\right|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}}=\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\sum_{i\geq 0}2^{-is}Mh_{j-i}\right|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}}\lesssim_{s}\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|Mh_{k}|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}}.

Now an application of Fefferman–Stein inequality
[
20
, Lemma A.5/A.3(e)]
followed by the square function estimate
[
20
, Lemma A.5/A.3(a)]
, entails

\displaystyle\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|Mh_{k}|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}}\lesssim_{N,p}\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|h_{k}|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}}\lesssim_{N,p}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}}.

Combining, the three previous inequalities, the result follows.∎

Theorem A.4

(Weighted Kenig-Ponce-Vega Estimate)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in[0,\alpha]
with
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\alpha
. Suppose that
p,p_{1},p_{2}\in(1,\infty)
satisfy the Hölder relation

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}.

Let
w,w_{1},w_{2}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be positive weight functions such that
w_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{p_{1}}
,
w_{2}\in\mathcal{A}_{p_{2}}
, and suppose that the composite weight satisfies

w^{1/p}=w_{1}^{1/p_{1}}w_{2}^{1/p_{2}}.

Define the commutator operator

\mathcal{C}[f,g]\coloneqq\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}(fg)-g\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}f-f\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}g.

Then, for all
f,g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the following estimate holds.

\left\lVert\mathcal{C}[f,g]\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\lesssim_{N,p,p_{1},p_{2},\alpha,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}\left\lVert(-\Delta)^{\alpha_{1}}f\right\rVert_{L^{p_{1}}_{w_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\left\lVert(-\Delta)^{\alpha_{2}}g\right\rVert_{L^{p_{2}}_{w_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Proof.

We start with the following Bony paraproduct decomposition (e.g.
[
30
, Lemma 2.7]
).

(A.1)

fg=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{j}\widetilde{g}_{j}+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{\leq j-3}g_{j}+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{j}g_{\leq j-3}.

where
\widetilde{g}_{j}=\sum_{|j-k|\leq 2}g_{k}
.

Step 1.
First we deal with the diagonal (high-high frequency) piece
\sum f_{j}\widetilde{g}_{j}
. From the Remark
A.2
, it’s evident that the weight
w\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
in our case.

Using Littlewood–Paley square function estimates
[
20
, Lemma A.5/A.3(a,b)]
for the fractional operator
\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}
, one obtains

\left\|\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{j}\widetilde{g}_{j}\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}}\sim_{N,p,\alpha}\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|2^{2k\alpha}P_{k}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{j}\widetilde{g}_{j}\right|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}}.

Elementary support properties of the projectors
P_{j}
then give
P_{k}(f_{j}\widetilde{g}_{j})=0
for all
j\leq k-5
and thus
P_{k}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{j}\widetilde{g}_{j}=P_{k}\sum_{l>-5}f_{k+l}\widetilde{g}_{k+l}
. Denoting,
h_{k}=\sum_{l>-5}2^{2k\alpha}f_{k+l}\widetilde{g}_{k+l}
, and using the pointwise Bernstein’s inequality
|P_{j}h_{k}(x)|\lesssim Mh_{k}(x);\,\forall j\in\mathbb{Z}
, one sees

\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|2^{2k\alpha}P_{k}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{j}\widetilde{g}_{j}\right|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}}=\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|P_{k}h_{k}\right|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}}\lesssim_{N}\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|Mh_{k}\right|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}}.

Invoking the Fefferman–Stein inequality
[
20
, Lemma A.5/A.3(e)]
, we achieve

\displaystyle\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|Mh_{k}\right|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N,p}\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|h_{k}|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}}.

Now an application of Minkowski’s inequality entails

\displaystyle\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|h_{k}|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}}=\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\sum_{l>-5}2^{2k\alpha}f_{k+l}\widetilde{g}_{k+l}\right|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l>-5}\frac{1}{2^{2l\alpha}}\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|2^{2(k+l)\alpha}f_{k+l}\widetilde{g}_{k+l}\right|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|2^{2k\alpha}f_{k}\widetilde{g}_{k}\right|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}}\sum_{l>-5}\frac{1}{2^{2l\alpha}}

\displaystyle\sim_{\alpha}\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|2^{2k\alpha_{1}}f_{k}\cdot 2^{2k\alpha_{2}}\widetilde{g}_{k}\right|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}}.

Finally, weighted Hölder inequality
(
with exponent
p=\frac{p_{1}p_{2}}{p_{1}+p_{2}}
and weight
w=w_{1}^{p/p_{1}}w_{2}^{p/p_{2}}
)
and the square function estimates together imply

\displaystyle\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|2^{2k\alpha_{1}}f_{k}\cdot 2^{2k\alpha_{2}}\widetilde{g}_{k}\right|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|2^{2k\alpha_{1}}f_{k}\right|^{2}}\sqrt{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|2^{2k\alpha_{2}}\widetilde{g}_{k}\right|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|2^{2k\alpha_{1}}f_{k}\right|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{w_{1}}}\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|2^{2k\alpha_{2}}\widetilde{g}_{k}\right|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{w_{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p_{1},p_{2},\alpha_{2}}\left\|(-\Delta)^{\alpha_{1}}f\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{w_{1}}}\left\|(-\Delta)^{\alpha_{2}}g\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{w_{2}}}.

The last inequality for
\widetilde{g}_{j}
follows by noticing that
\sum\left|2^{2j\alpha_{2}}\widetilde{g}_{j}\right|^{2}\lesssim_{\alpha_{2}}\sum|2^{2j\alpha_{2}}g_{j}|^{2}
. This settles the diagonal piece. Since the last two pieces in the decomposition (
A.1
) are symmetric with respect to
f
and
g
, it is enough to only deal with the low-high frequency piece
\sum f_{\leq j-3}g_{j}
.

Step 2.
Start by writing

\displaystyle\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{\leq j-3}g_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left[\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}f_{\leq j-3}g_{j}-f_{\leq j-3}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}g_{j}\right]

(A.2)

\displaystyle\quad+f\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}g-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{>j-3}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}g_{j}.

First we take care of the last sum in (
A
), i.e.,
\sum f_{>j-3}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}g_{j}
. It is now easy to see by using the triangle inequality and Cauchy-Schwarz

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{>j-3}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}g_{j}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|2^{2j\alpha_{1}}f_{>j-3}\right|\cdot\left|2^{2j(\alpha_{2}-\alpha)}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}g_{j}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|2^{2j\alpha_{1}}f_{>j-3}\right|^{2}}\sqrt{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|2^{2j(\alpha_{2}-\alpha)}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}g_{j}\right|^{2}}.

Further employing weighted Hölder and the Littlewood–Paley estimates, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{>j-3}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}g_{j}\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|2^{2j\alpha_{1}}f_{>j-3}\right|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{w_{1}}}\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|2^{2j(\alpha_{2}-\alpha)}P_{j}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}g\right|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{w_{2}}}

\displaystyle\sim_{N,p_{1},p_{2},\alpha_{1}}\left\|(-\Delta)^{\alpha_{1}}f\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{w_{1}}}\left\|(-\Delta)^{\alpha_{2}}g\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{w_{2}}}.

To deal with the first sum
\sum\left[\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}f_{\leq j-3}g_{j}-f_{\leq j-3}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}g_{j}\right]
, we first need to define some fattened Littlewood–Paley projectors. Denote
\widetilde{\varphi}(\xi)\coloneqq\sum_{-2\leq j\leq 2}\varphi(2^{j}\xi)
and the dyadic rescaling
\widetilde{\varphi}_{j}=\widetilde{\varphi}(\frac{\cdot}{2^{j}})
. Then it is apparent that
\supp(\widetilde{\varphi})=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\colon\frac{1}{8}\leq|\xi|\leq 8\}
and
\widetilde{\varphi}\equiv 1
on the annulus
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\colon\frac{1}{4}\leq|\xi|\leq 4\}
. Now we define the fattened projector
\widetilde{P}_{j}
as,
\widehat{\widetilde{P}_{j}f}=\widetilde{\varphi}_{j}\widehat{f}
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Also denote
\widehat{\widetilde{\psi}}(\xi)\coloneqq|\xi|^{2\alpha}\widetilde{\varphi}(\xi)
.

A little bit of Fourier analysis combined with the support properties of the projector
\widetilde{P}_{j}
and the fundamental theorem of calculus now give

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left[\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}f_{\leq j-3}g_{j}-f_{\leq j-3}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}g_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left[\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}\widetilde{P}_{j}(f_{\leq j-3}g_{j})-f_{\leq j-3}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}\widetilde{P}_{j}g_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{j(N+2\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\widetilde{\psi}(2^{j}y)g_{j}(x-y)\left[f_{\leq j-3}(x-y)-f_{\leq j-3}(x)\right]\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{j(N+2\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\widetilde{\psi}(2^{j}y)g_{j}(x-y)\int_{0}^{1}\nabla f_{\leq j-3}(x-ty)\cdot(-y){\,\rm d}t\,{\rm d}y.

Using the local consistency of the band-limited functions
\nabla f_{\leq j}
and
g_{j}
(cf.
[
30
, Lemma 3.1]
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\nabla f_{\leq j-3}(x-ty)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N}\langle 2^{j-3}(-ty)\rangle^{N}M\nabla f_{\leq j-3}(x),

\displaystyle|g_{j}(x-y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N}\langle 2^{j}(-y)\rangle^{N}Mg_{j}(x).

Using these pointwise estimates in the last integral and finally applying Cauchy-Schwarz, one gets

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left[\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}f_{\leq j-3}g_{j}-f_{\leq j-3}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}g_{j}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{j(N+2\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\widetilde{\psi}(2^{j}y)|\,\langle 2^{j}|y|\rangle^{N}Mg_{j}(x)\,\langle 2^{j-3}|ty|\rangle^{N}|y|M\nabla f_{\leq j-3}(x)\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N,\alpha,\alpha_{1}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{2j\alpha_{2}}Mg_{j}(x)\cdot 2^{(j-3)(2\alpha_{1}-1)}M\nabla f_{\leq j-3}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N,\alpha,\alpha_{1}}\sqrt{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|M(2^{2j\alpha_{2}}g_{j})|^{2}}\sqrt{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|M(2^{j(2\alpha_{1}-1)}\nabla f_{\leq j})|^{2}}.

Note that the estimate above relies on the fact that
|y|(1+|y|^{2})^{N}\widetilde{\psi}(y)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Now applying the weighted Hölder inequality followed by the Fefferman–Stein inequality, Littlewood–Paley inequality and finally Lemma
A.3
(with
s=1-2\alpha_{1}>0,\,h=\mathcal{R}(-\Delta)^{\alpha_{1}}f
) yields

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left[\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}f_{\leq j-3}g_{j}-f_{\leq j-3}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\alpha}g_{j}\right]\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N,\alpha,\alpha_{1}}\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|M(2^{j(2\alpha_{1}-1)}\nabla f_{\leq j})|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{w_{1}}}\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|M(2^{2j\alpha_{2}}g_{j})|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{w_{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N,\alpha,\alpha_{1},p_{1},p_{2}}\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|2^{j(2\alpha_{1}-1)}\nabla f_{\leq j}|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{w_{1}}}\left\|\sqrt{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|2^{2j\alpha_{2}}g_{j}|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{w_{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N,\alpha,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},p_{1},p_{2}}\left\|(-\Delta)^{\alpha_{1}}f\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{w_{1}}}\left\|(-\Delta)^{\alpha_{2}}g\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{w_{2}}}.

Observe that in the last inequality above we rewrote

\nabla=(-\Delta)^{\frac{1-2\alpha_{1}}{2}}\,(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\,(-\Delta)^{\alpha_{1}},

so that the operator
\mathcal{R}\coloneqq(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla
appears. This is precisely the classical vector-valued Riesz transform, which is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
1<p<\infty
and
w\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
, i.e.,
\|\mathcal{R}\|_{L^{p}_{w}\to L^{p}_{w}}\lesssim_{N,p}1
. Finally combining all the preceding estimates yields the desired conclusion.∎

Proposition A.5
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
f_{\varepsilon}\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be such that
\langle f_{\varepsilon},V_{i}\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\mathcal{o}(1),\,\langle f_{\varepsilon},\dot{V}_{i}\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\mathcal{o}(1)
. Then the following inequality holds.

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f_{\varepsilon}^{2}\frac{V_{i}^{2_{s}^{\star}(t)-2}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\leq\frac{1}{\eta_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}f_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\mathcal{o}(1),

where
\eta_{3}
denotes the third eigenvalue of the linearized operator
\frac{\left(-\Delta\right)^{s}}{{V_{i}^{2_{s}^{\star}(t)-2}}{|x|^{-t}}}
;
V_{i}=V_{s,t}^{\lambda_{i}}
for some
\lambda_{i}>0
, and
\mathcal{o}(1)
is any quantity that tends to zero as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Proof.

Let
p=2_{s}^{\star}(t)-1
and consider the eigenvalue problem

(A.3)

\left(-\Delta\right)^{s}\psi=\mu\frac{V_{i}^{p-1}}{|x|^{t}}\psi,\hskip 5.69054pt\psi\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

By the Remark
1.1
-
(1)
, the operator
\frac{\left(-\Delta\right)^{s}}{V_{i}^{p-1}|x|^{-t}}
has a discrete spectrum with first two eigen-pairs being
(1,V_{i})
and
(p,\dot{V}_{i})
respectively. Moreover, the third eigenvalue
\eta_{3}
satisfies
\eta_{3}>p-1
, and admits the Rayleigh quotient characterization

(A.4)

\eta_{3}\leq\frac{\langle\psi,\psi\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi^{2}\frac{V_{i}^{p-1}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x},\quad\text{for all }\psi\perp_{\dot{H}^{s}}\operatorname{span}\left\{V_{i},\dot{V}_{i}\right\},

with equality if and only if
\psi
is the third eigenfunction.

Take

\psi=f_{\varepsilon}-\langle f_{\varepsilon},V_{i}\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}\frac{V_{i}}{\|V_{i}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}}-\langle f_{\varepsilon},\dot{V}_{i}\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}\frac{\dot{V}_{i}}{\|\dot{V}_{i}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}}.

It is now clear that
\psi\perp_{\dot{H}^{s}}\operatorname{span}\{V_{i},\dot{V}_{i}\}
and therefore using this
\psi
in the Rayleigh quotient inequality (
A.4
) gives the required estimate.∎

Proposition A.6
.

Let
s\in(0,1),N>2s,t\in(0,2s)
and
0<r<R
. Define the cutoff function
\varphi_{r,R}\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow[0,1]
by

(A.5)

\varphi_{r,R}(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }|x|<r,\\
\displaystyle\frac{\log\frac{R}{|x|}}{\log\frac{R}{r}}&\text{if }r\leq|x|\leq R,\\
0&\text{if }|x|>R.\end{cases}

Then for all sufficiently large
\frac{R}{r}
, we have the estimate

\bigg\||x|^{\frac{t}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi_{r,R}\bigg\|_{\frac{2\cdot 2_{s}^{\star}(t)}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)-2}}\lesssim_{N,s}\left(\log\frac{R}{r}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}.

Proof.

To simplify notations, we denote
q=\frac{2\cdot 2_{s}^{\star}(t)}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)-2}\text{ and }\varphi_{r,R}
simply by
\varphi
in what follows. We start by decomposing the integral into several regions and then estimate over each region individually.

Write

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\frac{qt}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}\big|\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi(x)\big|^{q}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int_{|x|<r}+\int_{r\leq|x|\leq R}+\int_{|x|>R}\bigg)|x|^{\frac{qt}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}\big|\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi(x)\big|^{q}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\eqqcolon I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

From the definition of the fractional Laplacian, one has

\displaystyle\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi(x)

\displaystyle=C_{N,s}\,\pv\bigg(\int_{|y|<r}+\int_{r\leq|y|\leq R}+\int_{|y|>R}\bigg)\frac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{N+s}}\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\eqqcolon J_{1}(x)+J_{2}(x)+J_{3}(x).

Using this we have by the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle I_{1}^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int_{|x|<r}|x|^{\frac{qt}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}|J_{1}(x)+J_{2}(x)+J_{3}(x)|^{q}\,{\rm d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{3}\Big\||x|^{\frac{t}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}J_{k}\Big\|_{L^{q}\left(B_{r}\right)}\eqqcolon I_{11}+I_{12}+I_{13}.

and similarly decompose
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
.

It’s now immediate from the definition of
\varphi
that
I_{11}=0
.

\displaystyle I_{13}^{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|<r}|x|^{\frac{qt}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}|J_{3}(x)|^{q}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=C_{N,s}\int_{|x|<r}\bigg(|x|^{\frac{t}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}\int_{|y|>R}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+s}}\,{\rm d}y\bigg)^{q}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N,s}\int_{|x|<r}\bigg(|x|^{\frac{t}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}\int_{|y-x|>R-r}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{N+s}}\,{\rm d}y\bigg)^{q}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\sim_{N,s}\bigg(\frac{r}{R-r}\bigg)^{sq}.

Therefore

I_{13}\lesssim_{N,s}\frac{1}{\left(\frac{R}{r}-1\right)^{s}}.

\displaystyle I_{12}^{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|<r}\left(|x|^{\frac{t}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}|J_{2}(x)|\right)^{q}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=C_{N,s}\int_{|x|<r}\left(|x|^{\frac{t}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}\int_{r<|y|<R}\left(1-\frac{\log\frac{R}{|y|}}{\log\frac{R}{r}}\right)\frac{{\rm d}y}{|x-y|^{N+s}}\right)^{q}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=C_{N,s}\int_{|x|<r}\left(|x|^{\frac{t}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}\frac{1}{\log\frac{R}{r}}\int_{r<|y|<R}\frac{\log|y|-\log r}{|x-y|^{N+s}}\,{\rm d}y\right)^{q}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=C_{N,s}\int_{|x|<r}\left(|x|^{\frac{t}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}\frac{1}{\log\frac{R}{r}}\left(\int_{r<|y|<2r}+\int_{2r<|y|<R}\right)\frac{\log|y|-\log r}{|x-y|^{N+s}}\,{\rm d}y\right)^{q}\,{\rm d}x.

Now we estimate the above two integrals inside
I_{12}
as follows.

\displaystyle\int_{r<|y|<2r}\frac{\log|y|-\log r}{|y-x|^{N+s}}\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\leq\int_{r<|y|<2r}\frac{|y-x|}{r}\frac{{\rm d}y}{|y-x|^{N+s}}\leq\frac{1}{r}\int_{|y-x|<3r}\frac{{\rm d}y}{|y-x|^{N+s-1}}\sim_{N}\frac{1}{r^{s}}.

Define
L=\left\lceil\sqrt{\frac{\log\frac{R}{r}}{\log 2}}-1\right\rceil
, where
\lceil x\rceil
denotes the least integer
\geq x
.

\displaystyle\int_{2r<|y|<R}\frac{\log|y|-\log r}{|y-x|^{N+s}}\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{L}\int_{2^{k^{2}}<\frac{|y|}{r}<2^{(k+1)^{2}}}\frac{\log\frac{|y|}{r}}{|y-x|^{N+s}}\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{L}\int_{2^{k^{2}}<\frac{|y|}{r}<2^{(k+1)^{2}}}\frac{\log\frac{|y|}{r}}{\left(2^{k^{2}}-1\right)^{N+s}}\frac{{\rm d}y}{r^{N+s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{s}}\sum_{k=1}^{L}\frac{1}{(2^{k^{2}}-1)^{N+s}}\int_{2^{k^{2}}<|z|<2^{(k+1)^{2}}}\log|z|\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N}\frac{1}{r^{s}}\sum_{k=1}^{L}\frac{(k+1)^{2}\left(2^{N(k+1)^{2}}-2^{Nk^{2}}\right)}{(2^{k^{2}}-1)^{N+s}}.

Then it is easy to see that

\sum_{k=1}^{L}\frac{(k+1)^{2}\left(2^{N(k+1)^{2}}-2^{Nk^{2}}\right)}{(2^{k^{2}}-1)^{N+s}}\lesssim_{N,s}L\sim_{N,s}\sqrt{\log\frac{R}{r}}.

Therefore

\displaystyle I_{12}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N,s}\left(\int_{|x|<r}\left(|x|^{\frac{t}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}}\frac{1}{\log\frac{R}{r}}\left(\frac{1}{r^{s}}+\frac{\sqrt{\log\frac{R}{r}}}{r^{s}}\right)\right)^{q}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\sim_{N,s}\frac{1}{\log\frac{R}{r}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log\frac{R}{r}}}.

Finally, combining these estimates on
I_{11},\,I_{12}
and
I_{13}
, we obtain

I_{1}^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim_{N,s}\frac{1}{\left(\frac{R}{r}-1\right)^{s}}+\frac{1}{\log\frac{R}{r}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log\frac{R}{r}}}.

Thus for all sufficiently large
\frac{R}{r}
, we have

I_{1}\lesssim_{N,s}\left(\log\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\frac{-q}{2}}.

We now turn to the estimates for
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
and a similar argument yields for all sufficiently large
\frac{R}{r}
,

I_{2}\lesssim_{N,s}\left(\log\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\frac{-q}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}},\hskip 5.69054ptI_{3}\lesssim_{N,s}\left(\log\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\frac{-q}{2}}.

For brevity, we briefly discuss the estimate for the dominant component,
I_{22}
. Write
A=\frac{R}{r},\,q=\frac{2\cdot 2_{s}^{\star}(t)}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)-2}
and then
\frac{qt}{2_{s}^{\star}(t)}=sq-N
.

Notice

\displaystyle I_{22}

\displaystyle\leq C_{N,s}\frac{1}{\log A}\bigg(\int_{1\leq|x|\leq A}|x|^{sq-N}\bigg(\int_{1\leq|y|\leq A}\frac{|\log|x|-\log|y||}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,{\rm d}y\bigg)^{q}\,{\rm d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Decompose for
1\leq|x|\leq A
,

\displaystyle\int_{1\leq|y|\leq A}\frac{|\log|x|-\log|y||}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int_{\frac{1}{2}\leq|y|\leq\frac{|x|}{2}}+\int_{\frac{|x|}{2}<|y|<2|x|}+\int_{2|x|\leq|y|\leq 2A}\bigg)\frac{|\log|x|-\log|y||}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\eqqcolon K_{1}(x)+K_{2}(x)+K_{3}(x)

By a similar strategy as above, yields

K_{1}(x)\lesssim_{N,s}\frac{\sqrt{\log|x|}}{|x|^{s}},\hskip 5.69054ptK_{2}(x)\lesssim_{N}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}},\hskip 5.69054ptK_{3}(x)\lesssim_{N,s}\frac{\sqrt{\log\frac{A}{|x|}}}{|x|^{s}}.

Now plugging these estimates in
I_{22}
gives the desired bound.∎

Appendix B
Integrals involving two bubbles

The estimates here are direct adaptations of
[
14
, Appendix B]
: after rescaling to a single-bubble scale, we split the integrals into regions where the profiles show power-type behavior and compute each piece explicitly. The only difference is that, in the absence of an explicit formula for
V_{s,t}
, we use the sharp pointwise bound
(
\sim\tfrac{1}{1+|x|^{N-2s}}
)
from
[
25
, Theorem 1.1]
, which allows all steps to be repeated with only cosmetic changes and with the same scaling laws and logarithmic corrections. We therefore simply state the resulting bounds used in the paper.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1),\,t\in(0,2s),\,N>2s,\,\lambda\in(0,1]
, and
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
be such that
\alpha+\beta=2_{s}^{\star}(t)
. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
the following estimate holds:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V_{s,t}(x)^{\alpha}V_{s,t}^{\lambda}(x)^{\beta}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\sim\begin{cases}C(N,\varepsilon)\,\displaystyle\lambda^{\frac{(N-2s)\min(\alpha,\beta)}{2}}&\text{if }|\alpha-\beta|\geq\varepsilon,\\
C(N)\,\lambda^{\frac{N-t}{2}}\left(1+\log\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)&\text{if }\alpha=\beta.\end{cases}

Corollary B.2
.

For
i\neq j;\,\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}>0
, define
Q_{ij}\coloneqq\min\left\{\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{j}},\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{i}}\right\}
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V^{\lambda_{i}}_{s,t}(x)^{\alpha}V_{s,t}^{\lambda_{j}}(x)^{\beta}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\sim_{N,\varepsilon}\begin{cases}Q_{ij}^{\frac{(N-2s)\min(\alpha,\beta)}{2}}&\text{if }|\alpha-\beta|\geq\varepsilon,\\
Q_{ij}^{\frac{N-t}{2}}\left(1+\log\frac{1}{Q_{ij}}\right)&\text{if }\alpha=\beta.\end{cases}

The next corollary captures this coupling quantitatively, reflecting a key feature of two–bubble interactions: when the scales differ, the overlap concentrates near the core of the taller bubble.

Corollary B.3
.

Let
\lambda_{i}\geq\lambda_{j}>0
, and set
V_{i}=V_{s,t}^{\lambda_{i}}
,
V_{j}=V_{s,t}^{\lambda_{j}}
with
p+1=2_{s}^{\star}(t)
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V_{i}^{p}V_{j}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\sim_{N,s,t}\int_{B(0,\lambda_{i}^{-1})}\frac{V_{i}^{p}V_{j}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x.

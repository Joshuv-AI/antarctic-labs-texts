Multimodal ultrasonographic features of high-grade mucoepidermoid carcinoma of the parotid gland: a case report
Authors: Feng Hu, Chao Zhang, Lili Zhang, Fangfang Sun, Hongyu Li, Lirong Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMucoepidermoid carcinoma (MEC) is the most common malignant tumor of the parotid gland. High-grade MEC has a high degree of malignancy, strong invasiveness, lacks obvious clinical and imaging features, and is prone to misdiagnosis. Moreover, the literature on the ultrasonic characteristics of MEC is limited. This article retrospectively analyzes the multimodal ultrasound features of a case of MEC.Case DescriptionA 68-year-old Asian man noticed a raised mass beneath his right earlobe one year ago. Initially, the mass was painless, leading the patient to delay seeking medical attention. Over time, the mass has increased in size, prompting the patient to seek further evaluation at this hospital. Upon examination, a palpable mass measuring approximately 2.5 cm ×2.0 cm was identified beneath the right earlobe. The mass exhibited distinct borders, fair mobility, no tenderness, normal skin surface, and low skin temperature. The patient denies any significant family history of disease and has no history of infectious diseases or prior radiation therapy. Following comprehensive multimodal ultrasound diagnostic evaluation, the lesion was suspected to be a malignant neoplasm of the parotid gland. Subsequent pathological examination confirmed the diagnosis of MEC of the parotid gland. So the patient underwent surgery to remove the parotid gland mass under general anesthesia.ConclusionsThis report provides a detailed description of the multimodal ultrasound imaging features of MEC. It underscores the potential value of this technique in the diagnostic process. The case aims to improve the understanding of this disease and enhance clinicians’ ability to achieve an early diagnosis, thereby reducing the risks of misdiagnosis and delayed treatment.

Highlight box

Key findings

• This case shows that multimodal ultrasound can accurately diagnose high-grade parotid mucoepidermoid carcinoma. Key imaging features—ill-defined borders, irregular shape, heterogeneous echogenicity and enhancement, rich vascularity, and variable stiffness—align with pathological findings.

What is known and what is new?

• High-grade mucoepidermoid carcinoma is aggressive but lacks distinctive imaging features, often leading to misdiagnosis. Limited literature exists on its ultrasound characteristics.

• To our knowledge, this is among the first detailed case reports to systematically detail the combined use of multimodal ultrasound (including superb microvascular imaging, shear wave elastography, and contrast-enhanced ultrasound) for diagnosing this tumor, providing new imaging markers.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Multimodal ultrasound offers a non-invasive tool for earlier diagnosis, potentially reducing misdiagnosis and treatment delays. Clinicians should consider this approach for evaluating suspicious parotid masses, using it to guide biopsy and improve diagnostic accuracy. Further studies are needed to confirm these findings.

Introduction

Mucoepidermoid carcinoma (MEC) is one of the most commonly encountered malignant salivary gland tumors in clinical practice, accounting for 35% of all salivary gland tumors (1). It is composed of mucinous, intermediate, and epidermoid cells of varying degrees of differentiation, with a high potential for invasion and metastasis (2). MEC can occur at any age, but the incidence peaks between 30 and 60 years of age and is slightly higher in women than in men (3,4). Given the atypical imaging presentation, pathological examination is essential for diagnosis. The tumor is invasive, and early detection and timely treatment are crucial for prognosis. Literature on the ultrasonographic features of MEC is limited, and reports on multimodal ultrasound in this context are rare. This article details the ultrasound findings of this MEC case to aid diagnosis and improve early detection through imaging characteristics. We present this article in accordance with the CARE reporting checklist (available at https://gs.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/gs-2026-1-0100/rc).

Case presentation

The patient is a 68-year-old Asian man who noticed a raised mass beneath his right earlobe one year ago. As there was no associated pain, the patient did not pursue further investigation or treatment. The mass has since increased in size and the patient has sought additional treatment at this hospital. Upon examination, a palpable mass was identified beneath the right earlobe, measuring approximately 2.5 cm × 2.0 cm. It exhibited distinct borders, fair mobility, no tenderness, normal skin surface, and a normal skin temperature. Furthermore, the patient had no family history of disease, infectious disease, or radiation therapy.

Ultrasonography was performed using Philips EPIQ5 with a 5–12 MHz linear transducer and GE LOGIQ E9 with a 6–15 MHz linear transducer. The examination revealed a solid hypoechoic mass measuring 24×21 mm in the right parotid gland, with unclear borders, irregular morphology, and heterogeneous internal echogenicity (Figure 1). Color Doppler flow imaging (CDFI) showed abundant blood flow signals, with a peak systolic velocity (PSV) of 11.5 cm/s and a resistance index (RI) of 0.75. Superb microvascular imaging (SMI) showed grade 2 blood flow signals within the tumor (Figure 2). Shear wave elastography (SWE) revealed heterogeneous internal stiffness, with a Young’s modulus of 81 kPa in stiffer areas and 0.48 kPa in softer areas (Figure 3). Ultrasound suggested the possibility of malignancy, and further investigation was recommended. Subsequently, contrast-enhanced ultrasonography was performed. Following intravenous injection of 2.4 mL of SonoVue, the lesion began to perfuse at 14 s in a centripetal manner, reached peak enhancement at 26 s, and showed inhomogeneous slight hyperenhancement. The border appeared enlarged compared with baseline imaging, and a non-enhanced area measuring approximately 14 mm × 14 mm was observed internally. Washout occurred earlier than in the surrounding soft tissue (Figure 4). Based on the combined conventional and contrast-enhanced ultrasound (CEUS) findings, the mass was considered suspicious for malignancy.

Two-dimensional ultrasound. The lesion presents as a substantial hypoechoic mass with ill-defined borders, irregular morphology, and heterogeneous internal echogenicity.

Superb microvascular imaging demonstrates that the internal blood flow of the tumor reaches grade 2.

Shear wave elastography shows heterogeneous internal stiffness of the lesion.

Contrast-enhanced ultrasound shows that enhancement peaked at 26 seconds after injection of the contrast agent, displaying centripetal, inhomogeneous, and slightly higher enhancement. Compared to the 2D image, the border appeared enlarged at the peak, with a large non-enhanced area visible in the interior.

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) showed that the right parotid gland had a rounded, lobulated mixed signal shadow in the posterior inferior pole of the superficial lobe, partly spanning to the deep lobe, with T1WI equal, T2WI slightly higher than that of the same layer of muscle, interspersed with streaks of low signal, with ill-defined margin, and the size of the lesion was about 2.3 cm × 2.4 cm × 3.3 cm. The MRI findings suggest the presence of an occupying lesion in the right parotid gland, with a probable diagnosis of pleomorphic adenoma. However, further examination of other properties is necessary. Further contrast-enhanced MRI showed heterogeneous hyperintensity on diffusion weighted imaging (DWI) with corresponding hypointensity on apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC), and marked heterogeneous enhancement with patchy non-enhancing areas. Scattered lymph nodes were noted in the bilateral carotid sheaths and submandibular regions, the largest measuring 0.6 cm in short-axis diameter, with homogeneous enhancement. Contrast-enhanced MRI findings: A mass in the lower pole of the deep lobe of the right parotid gland, suggestive of malignancy. Lymph nodes in the bilateral carotid sheaths and submandibular regions, suggestive of benign nature.

On 06/08/2024, an ultrasound-guided biopsy of the right parotid mass was performed. Histopathology showed a salivary gland-derived tumor with areas of active proliferation and aggressive growth. Mild nuclear atypia was observed, and low-grade malignancy could not be excluded. Further consultation was recommended for diagnostic clarification.

The patient subsequently underwent resection of the right parotid mass. After successful induction of general anesthesia, the patient was placed in the supine position with the head turned to the left. A reverse-S-shaped incision was made from the preauricular region to the submandibular angle. The skin, subcutaneous tissue, and platysma were incised sequentially to expose the parotid capsule. The marginal mandibular, buccal, and zygomatic branches of the facial nerve were identified and preserved. Intraoperatively, a hard mass was observed extending from the superficial lobe to the deep lobe of the parotid gland, with no capsule and ill-defined borders. The marginal mandibular branch was found to penetrate the tumor and was transected after consultation with the family. The superficial and deep lobes of the parotid gland were resected en bloc with an extended margin around the tumor, achieving complete resection, and periparotid lymph node dissection was performed. A segment of the great auricular nerve was harvested for facial nerve bridging and anastomosis. After irrigation and hemostasis, a negative pressure drain was placed, and the incision was closed in layers. Intraoperative blood loss was approximately 20 mL, and the procedure was uneventful.

Postoperative pathology: high-grade MEC with interstitial fibrosis and nerve invasion. No vascular embolus. Surgical margin showed localized cauterization. Immunohistochemical tests showed CK5/6(+), CK7(+), P40(+), P63(+), P53(scattered +), S-100(partially +), Dog-1(weakly +), AR(−), β-Catenin(membrane +), GATA-3(−), CD117(−), SMA(weakly +) and the Ki-67 index was 20% at the hotspots. No cancer metastasis was observed in the lymph nodes. Based on the above pathological features, the patient was admitted for adjuvant radiotherapy three weeks after surgery. Intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT) was administered, with the target volume including the right parotid tumor bed and the right cervical levels II and III lymph node regions. The prescribed dose was 60 Gy in 30 fractions. During radiotherapy, daily cone-beam CT was performed to ensure treatment accuracy, and radiodermatitis protective agents were applied to the irradiated skin. After seven fractions (14 Gy), the patient declined further treatment and was discharged.

Discussion

MEC is a malignant tumor originating from glandular epithelium and is one of the most common malignant tumors of the salivary glands. MEC can occur in the parotid gland, submandibular gland, and minor salivary glands, with the parotid gland being the most common site of incidence (5). With advances in imaging techniques, early diagnosis and preoperative evaluation of MEC have been significantly improved. However, the imaging manifestations of MEC are heterogeneous, with significant differences in imaging features, clinical symptoms, and prognosis between different grades of MEC, often posing a challenge to clinical diagnosis.

MEC usually has an insidious onset and early symptoms are not obvious, and patients usually find the mass on physical examination or unintentionally. Low-grade MEC typically presents as a painless parotid mass with slow growth, distinct borders, and minimal invasion of surrounding tissues. High-grade MEC, however, is more aggressive. As the tumor progresses, patients may experience pain, facial nerve palsy, local ulcers, enlarged lymph nodes, and even distant metastases (6). Facial nerve involvement often indicates a high-grade lesion and correlates with a poorer prognosis. Given the rapid growth and malignant nature of high-grade MEC, prompt recognition and intervention are of paramount importance.

The development of multimodal imaging techniques has provided more accurate information for the early identification of MEC. Ultrasound is the most commonly used tool for the initial diagnosis of parotid masses. The technique offers several advantages, including non-invasiveness, real-time capability, convenience, and cost-effectiveness. It enables rapid assessment of tumor morphology, size, echogenic features, and blood flow. For the ultrasound manifestation of MEC, low-grade MEC usually presents as a hypoechoic or mixed echogenic mass with regular morphology and clear boundaries, and cystic structures can be seen internally, which is related to the abundant mucus secreted by mucous cells inside the tumor, and CDFI shows that there is less blood flow inside the tumor; whereas high-grade MEC presents as a mass with unclear boundaries, irregular shape, and heterogeneous internal echogenicity, and the internal part of the tumor may have necrosis or hemorrhage. In addition, high-grade MEC tends to have rich blood supply, showing abundant blood flow signals in and around the lesion, which is consistent with the ultrasound findings of the tumor in this case (7,8). At the same time, in this case report, the physical examination showed that the mass had distinct borders and fair mobility, but the ultrasound showed unclear boundaries, which is a typical feature of malignant tumors (9). This discrepancy arises from the different assessment dimensions of the two examination methods: physical examination perceives the relative mobility of the mass with surrounding tissues through tactile macroscopic sensation, while high-frequency ultrasound can clearly show the internal structure of the mass and the microscopic infiltration features of its edges. Therefore, the difference between the physical examination and ultrasound results in this case suggests that for parotid region masses, even when the physical examination indicates benignity, the possibility of a malignant tumor should still be considered. Furthermore, ultrasound innovations such as SMI are capable of detecting more subtle blood flow signals, particularly at low speeds. By reducing motion artifacts in surrounding tissue, SMI provides more accurate blood flow information compared to CDFI. This makes it particularly suitable for detecting the blood supply of malignant tumors, making it an important addition to tumor assessment in imaging.

As part of multimodal imaging, CEUS enables early differentiation of benign and malignant lesions by combining conventional ultrasound, color Doppler, and contrast-enhanced imaging to comprehensively assess tumor blood supply and microvascular distribution. In this case, high-grade MEC showed uneven slight hyperenhancement in the arterial phase, with the mass border enlarged at the peak compared to 2D ultrasound, suggesting infiltrative growth. Early washout relative to surrounding tissues indicated a rich blood supply. Additionally, a necrotic area without enhancement within the mass was suggestive of malignancy, aligning with the CEUS characteristics of parotid malignancy as noted in the study (10). Centripetal enhancement is another CEUS feature of parotid malignancy (11). However, literature on the specific ultrasonographic features of MEC is limited, indicating a need for further research. Multimodal ultrasonography not only aids diagnosis but also provides valuable guidance for biopsy site selection and surgical margin assessment. Compared with conventional single-modality ultrasound, multimodal ultrasonography offers higher accuracy and greater clinical value in the diagnosis and therapeutic monitoring of MEC.

SWE can quantitatively assess tissue stiffness and has shown promising applications in diagnosing benign and malignant thyroid and breast tumors. Malignant tumors typically exhibit higher shear wave velocity and stiffness, while benign tumors are relatively softer. However, several studies have noted limitations of SWE in differentiating benign and malignant parotid gland tumors, particularly for low-grade malignancies in the early stage (12,13). By looking at the SWE images in this case, it was possible to find that the internal hardness of the tumor was inhomogeneous, a property that may make SWE difficult to distinguish between benign and malignant in parotid tumors.

MRI has irreplaceable advantages in assessing the extent of parotid tumors, their relationship with adjacent structures, and lymph node involvement. MRI can more accurately assess the nature and grading of tumors by using a variety of sequences (e.g., T1-weighted images, T2-weighted images, diffusion-weighted imaging, etc.). Studies have shown that the signal intensity of MEC on T2-weighted sequences is closely related to its grade. Low-grade MEC usually shows a high signal on T2WI, which reflects the cystic structure formed inside the tumor due to the mucus secreted by a large number of mucous cells; while medium signal intensity is shown in intermediate-grade MEC, and high-grade MEC often shows inhomogeneous low-to-moderate signals, accompanied by solid components and necrotic areas (14). In addition, contrast-enhanced MRI scanning can help determine the blood supply of the tumor and accurately assess the relationship between the tumor and important structures such as the facial nerve, thus providing more detailed information for pre-operative assessment.

Although imaging provides an important reference for diagnosing MEC, the final diagnosis relies on histopathological and immunohistochemical analysis. Histological features vary significantly by tumor grade, with high-grade MEC accounting for approximately 25.2% of all MEC cases (2). Routine pathology shows that low-grade MEC is usually well-differentiated, composed mainly of mucous, epidermoid, and intermediate cells, with a high proportion of mucous cells. Tumor boundaries are typically well defined, with cystic or solid components, low cellular atypia, and rare mitotic figures, indicating low malignancy and relatively slow growth (15,16). High-grade tumors, on the other hand, contain more epidermoid and intermediate cells, are poorly differentiated, have pronounced nuclear anisotropy, have a high proportion of solid areas, are structurally disorganized, are often accompanied by necrosis and hemorrhage and are more rapidly growing and invasive (15,17).

Immunohistochemical techniques are also important tools in the diagnosis of MEC. Studies have shown that MEC typically expresses markers associated with various epithelial and mucous components, including CK7, CK5/6, P63, and P40 (18). In addition, immunohistochemical staining for S100 can significantly improve the detection rate of MEC nerve invasion, and S100 positivity is closely associated with poorer survival prognosis (19). Ki-67, as a biomarker of cell proliferation, is widely used to assess tumor invasiveness, and Ki-67 indices higher than 20% often suggest a poorer prognosis (20). Another study pointed out that Ki-67 can effectively differentiate between different grades of MEC, and the Ki-67 labeling index of high-grade MEC was significantly higher than that of low-grade tumors (21). In this case, pathological examination showed positive CK5/6(+), CK7(+), P40(+), and P63(+), which were consistent with the typical immunophenotype of MEC. S-100 partial positivity was consistent with the diagnosis of visible invasion of the nerve. The positive Ki-67 index in hotspot areas was greater than 20%, which was consistent with the histopathological features of high-grade MEC. In addition, although the pathogenesis of MEC has not been fully clarified, studies have shown that it is associated with chromosomal translocations such as t(11; 19)(q21; p13) and the resulting CRTC1-MAML2 gene fusion (22). The aforementioned markers assist in differentiating MEC from other malignant neoplasms of salivary gland origin, including adenoid cystic carcinoma and acinic cell carcinoma.

Treatment for MEC primarily involves surgical resection, supplemented by radiotherapy or chemotherapy, depending on tumor grade, stage, and individual patient conditions (23). For low-grade MEC, complete surgical resection generally yields a good prognosis, while high-grade MEC, especially those with tissue infiltration or lymph node metastasis, often requires adjuvant radiotherapy or chemotherapy to reduce recurrence risk (24). Patients with a positive CRTC1-MAML2 fusion gene typically have a better prognosis, whereas those with high-grade MEC lacking this gene face a poorer prognosis and higher risk of recurrence and metastasis (25,26). Thus, genetic analysis may provide important guidance for treatment and prognosis.

Conclusions

MEC is a heterogeneous parotid malignancy, with clinical, imaging, and histological features varying significantly by grade. While many reports have described its pathological features, studies on its imaging characteristics are rare. Multimodal imaging plays an important role in diagnosis, treatment planning, and postoperative monitoring. Multimodal ultrasonography and other imaging modalities can provide early indications of malignancy, facilitating timely diagnosis and intervention.

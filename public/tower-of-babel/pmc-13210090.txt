Impact of oral health education program in children suffering from attention deficit hyperactivity disorder: A cross-sectional study
Authors: Ankit Mahajan, Safia Haideri, V. V. Harish Kumar, Meenakshi Upadhyay, Madhura Pawar, Jagadish P. Rajguru, Supriya Singh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:The oral health is an integral part of general well-being in children suffering from attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD). The present study aimed to analyze the impact of oral health education program on children suffering from Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder.MATERIALS AND METHODS:This cross-sectional study was conducted in a specialized center for children with special needs. Institutional Ethical Committee clearance was obtained before starting the study. This study was carried out for 6 months, that is, from March 2024 to August 2024. The study participants, both male and female, were selected from the clinically diagnosed cases and were aged between 6 and 17 years. The total size of the study sample was 200 children, that is, Group A with 100 ADHD patients and Group B with 100 constituted controls. For evaluating the oral health, the World Health Organization’s questionnaire for assessment of oral health in children was used for DMFT/DMFS indices(permanent dentition); dmft and dmfs indices (primary dentition); gingival index (GI) and periodontal index (PI). To determine inter-relationships between oral health indicators and other variables, Bivariate analyses using the Mann–Whitney U test, Student’s t-test, Kruskal–Wallis, and chi-square tests were used.RESULTS:No statistically significant differences were noted in DMFT, DMFS, dmft, and dmfs index scores, although significant differences in gingival as well as periodontal indices were observed in Group A (ADHD children). In Group B, no statistically significant difference was observed in any of the studied index scores.CONCLUSION:Providing oral health education to subjects suffering from ADHD is important to maintain oral health, which can help in reducing morbidity.

Introduction

The oral health forms an important part of population health, especially during one’s childhood as well as adolescence. These growth periods are characterized by the development of habits that affect the oral health over an extended time period. Based on these facts, WHO has emphasized the importance of integrating oral health with the health of the pediatric population.[1]

Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, or ADHD, apart from affecting behavioral patterns along with the process of learning has significant effects on the status of oral health. A significant association between ADHD and dental caries as well as periodontal conditions in children has been noted.[2] This can be attributed to different factors, neglecting the oral health hygiene due to lesser control over brushing of teeth,[3] higher consumption of sugar-containing diet[45] and carbohydrates.[6]

Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder is associated with clinical features like inability to concentrate, along with an inappropriate, hyperactive, and impulsive nature.[78] This constitutes the commonest type of systemic condition during childhood that is usually diagnosed in school-going age groups.[9] This systemic condition has a significant influence over families, causing mental stress, influencing academics, and affecting a child’s self-esteem.[10] ADHD has a prevalence ranging between 3% and 10% among young children and adolescents.[11]

The complex inter-relationships have been observed between ADHD and status of oral health among children.[12]

The children diagnosed with ADHD have a greater predilection for developing anxiety, which causes behavioral problems that can potentially complicate dental treatment and care.[13] Psycho stimulant agents used in the management of ADHD can drastically affect oral health as well, since these produce symptoms such as xerostomia that can increase the risk of developing dental caries due to the reduced salivary flow. This further influences the neutralization of bacterial acidic by products along with remineralization of tooth enamel.

The oral health-related quality of life, or OHRQoL, is an important indicator of general well-being in children suffering from ADHD, as it can significantly affect the oral health treatment needs along with behavioral and/or emotional aspects. Hence, there are unique challenges in dental health care hence, a comprehensive and collaborative approach is required in ADHD cases so that oral health-related QoL can be substantially improved. The oral health education program underlying the importance of maintaining good oral health in children diagnosed with ADHD must be encouraged to improve the quality of life in these children.

Hence, this study was planned with an aim to analyze the impact of oral health education program in children with attention deficit hyperactivity disorder.

Materials and Methods

Study settings and design

This quasi-experimental analysis with a randomized control group before and after was conducted in an urban center for children with special needs. Institutional Ethical Committee clearance was obtained before initiating the study. Study participants and sampling

The study was conducted for a period of 6 months, that is, from March 2024 to August 2024, on children who were clinically diagnosed with ADHD, as this time period was considered to be sufficient to analyze the impact of oral health education program on the studied oral health indices. The study participants were randomly selected from clinically diagnosed cases and were aged between 6 and 17 years and belonged to both the genders. The selected control group subjects were both age matched and gender matched with the study group participants.

Sample size calculation was performed using the following formula:

n = z2× p × q/m2

where “n” = number of subjects,

z = confidence level at 95% (standard value of 1.96),

p = 75% (prevalence of periodontal disease) = 0.75,

q = 1 − p = 25% = 0.25, m = margin of error at 5% (standard value of 0.05)

Hence, a total sample size of 200 children, out of which 100 were diagnosed with ADHD (Group A) and 100 constituted control (Group B) subjects, was finalized for optimal results. The selected subjects were divided into groups based on inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Inclusion criteria: (a) clinically proven cases of ADHD; (b) those whose parents or caregivers consented tothe study;and (c) those who were available for post-training follow-up. Exclusion criteria: (a) any patients with psychological challenges,(b) those who could not follow instructions properly, (c) those undergoing any medications or had past drug history, and (d) those who did not provide consent for the study.

Data collection tool and technique

The demographic details such as patient’s age, gender type, and parental education levels were obtained. For evaluating the oral health, World Health Organization’s questionnaire for assessment of oral health in children was used that included the (DMFT index) decayed, missing (because of dental caries) and filled teeth in permanent dentition; DMFS index for decayed missing and filled surfaces in permanent teeth; index of decay, fillings, and missing teeth due to caries considering surfaces (DMFS), dmft and dmfs indices for primary dentition, gingival index (GI) and periodontal index (PI). The obtained data were then assessed for internal consistency reliability by employing Cronbach’s alpha test. The obtained results demonstrated internal consistency reliability with Cronbach’s α score = 0.85.

For the accurate measurement of oral health indicators, two independent researchers studied these indicators on randomly selected participants. Inter-examiner agreement evaluation was performed by Cohen’s kappa statistics which came at 0.93, thereby indicating high score agreement.

The training program consisted of interactions with the children and their parents/caregivers, who were provided with live demonstrations and explanations of oral health maintenance. The oral health education program training was imparted by a trained ASHA worker using study models and charts, along with the use of oral hygiene aids, that is, toothbrush and toothpaste. All the study participants and their caregivers were given demonstrations by trained ASHA workers regarding brushing of teeth at the initial sitting, and the impact of this training was evaluated at the sixth-month follow-up. Ethical consideration

The ethical approval number obtained was (IECMM/24/16). The guidelines approved by the Declaration of Helsinki were followed while obtaining the patient information, and all patient data werekept confidential by coding them. Informed written consent was obtained from children’s caregivers and/or parents before collecting any data.

Statistical analysis

For the continuous or quantitative variables, mean ± standard deviation values were calculated. Absolute frequencies and percentages were determined for quantitative and categorical types of variables, respectively. The normality of variables was tested by employing the Shapiro-Wilk test. Inter-relationships between oral health indicators with other variables were analyzed using Mann–Whitney U test, Student’s t-test, Kruskal–Wallis and chi-square tests.

Results

On assessment of age in mean ± SD, years, Group A children diagnosed with ADHD were found to have 09.04 ± 1.29 years, while Group B children in the control group were 9.23 ± 1.12 years with no statistical significance (P = 0.06). Since children who have ADHD have a lowered attention span, the impact of the oral education program was thought to be less than that of children (control) who have normal attention. On analyzing gender distribution, Group A had 73% male and 27% female ADHD children, while in Group B, there were 75% male and 25% female subjects. On analyzing paternal level of education, 12% were postgraduates, 35%were undergraduates, 13% received education till senior secondary, while 20% each had received education till high school or were illiterates in Group A. However, in Group B, 23% had received postgraduate education, 15% had undergraduate degrees, 29% had received education till senior secondary school,13% were high school educated, while 20% were illiterates. On inter-group comparison, statistically significant differences (P = 0.05 each) were obtained in postgraduate, undergraduate, and senior secondary educated fathers, while non-significant statistical differences (P = 0.06 and 1.0, respectively) were obtained in high school and illiterate fathers. On assessment of maternal education levels, in Group A, 5% had received postgraduate degrees, 19% received undergraduate level education, 58% had received education till senior secondary level, 10% received education till high school, while 8% were illiterates. However, in Group B, 11% were postgraduates, 25% were undergraduates, 45% studied till senior secondary level, 17% received high school education, while 2% were illiterates. On statistical comparison between the groups, statistically significant differences (P = 0.05) were obtained between postgraduate educated mothers. No statistically significant differences (P = 0.06) were observed in mothers who received undergraduate, senior secondary level, and high school level education or were illiterates [Table 1 and Figure 2].

Discussion

Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder is a common form of psychiatric disorder affecting both children and adults. This disease results in distress and anxiety. It can be defined as a “chronic neurobiological disease interfering with a person’s capacity to regulate the level of activity, inhibitory behavior, and attention to tasks.”[14] The characteristic features of ADHD include hyperactivity, impulsiveness, and lack of attention. Approximately 9% of the total world’s pediatric population is afflicted with ADHD. This includes approximately 3–5% of preschool and school-going children.[1516]

The children afflicted with this disorder don’t brush their teeth regularly and follow a diet that is cariogenic in nature, resulting in poor status of oral hygiene, which causes an increase in the incidence of dental caries in both the primary and permanent dentitions. Also, there is an increase in incidence of fractures of maxillary incisors, along with an increase in accumulation of dental plaque and calculus, which are also commonly seen in these children.[17] Maden and Gamli[18] observed that children who have ADHD were found to have higher nail biting habits and incidence of dental trauma.

The present study assessed the impact of oral health in children with attention deficit hyperactivity disorder. The oral health indicators prescribed by WHO, which were studied, were DMFT, DMFS, dmft, dmfs, gingival, and PI. Oral health status was assessed at baseline and at 6-month intervals by an independent observer. No changes in DMFT, DMFS, dmft, and dmfs indices were noted in children who have ADHD, while statistically significant differences in gingival and periodontal indices were noted in this group. Similar observations were noted in Group B (control subjects). This can be due to the cumulative nature of dmft/DMFT indices in which a lack of change in scores indicates a positive impact of the oral health educational program. Similarly, improvements in gingival and periodontal indices suggest a positive outcome of the oral education program. Our study has emphasized the role of oral health education and training in maintaining and improving oral hygiene practices in children with attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, as evidenced by statistically significant improvement in gingival and periodontal indices, thus emphasizing the importance of providing oral health education to these subjects. Similar to our study findings, Karmarkar et al.[19] in their study highlighted the importance of training in ADHD patients in maintaining good oral health.

Hosseinnia et al.[20] emphasized that the educational intervention of parents and teachers regarding ADHD disorder caused an improvement in the symptoms in children with ADHD.

Karbasi et al.[21] determined the effectiveness of the combined intervention of play therapy and storytelling on social skills in children with ADHD. It was found that the application of both play therapy and storytelling in children with ADHD can improve their social skills and reduce poor social behavior.

Zafar et al.[22] evaluated the relationship between ADHD and life satisfaction level among the medical students. It was concluded that the burden of ADHD is high among medical students and is negatively related to life satisfaction levels.

Our study found high dental caries index (both DMFT and DMFS) as well as gingival and PI scores in the studied ADHD patients when compared with normal control subjects. This has been corroborated by Melwani-Sadhwani et al.,[23] in their cross-sectional study, who demonstrated a high frequency of dental caries and gingival bleeding index in children who have ADHD. Although no increase in periodontal indices was noted. They also emphasized the importance of regular dental and oral examinations in these subjects. Additionally, diet type was not found to exhibit any deleterious influence on dental health. Similarly, Mota-Veloso et al.[24] suggested no effect of dietary habits on oral health. Hence, in the present study, a high incidence of dental caries and poor oral health was observed, per the study findings as summarized above.

The present study demonstrated statistically significant differences in the studied variables after providing education regarding measures of oral hygiene maintenance. Hence, it is very important that children with special needs, such as ADHD, are adequately trained to maintain a good to optimal level of oral health by following certain measures. This has been supported by the findings of Hanafy et al.[25] who obtained significantly good scores after imparting an oral health education program. Thus, oral health education at clinics and at schools can help in maintaining good oral health in children with special needs, such as those diagnosed with ADHD.

Limitations

The limitation of the present study was its small sample size due to the rarity of this systemic condition, thus, a reporting observer bias. Also, a short follow-up period of six months in this study might not be the true reflection of the oral health education program. A longer follow-up has the potential for study dropouts; hence, a relatively large sample size would provide a clearer outcome in terms of dental diseases. Hence, a more reflective study outcome can be obtained if a larger multi-institutional study sample is included in such an analysis. This study has a weakness due to a small sample size and short study duration. However, the strength is its good acceptance of the oral health education program. Further research on psychosomatic disorders, deleterious oral habits, and malocclusion can be carried out to study the prevalence of other conditions in ADHD affected children.

However, one disadvantage of such studies is the observer bias since a predetermined notion regarding ADHD subjects can exist in the observer. Patient compliance is also an issue as the chances of dropouts in such studies are relatively high.

The novelty of this study is its longitudinal clinical follow-up, which underscores the importance of such programs in children with special needs such as those who have attention deficit hyperactivity disorder.

Conclusion

ADHD is a mentally incapacitating disease affecting children. It impacts general physical health, which also affects oral and dental health due to varied reasons that may be either due to the ill-effects of medications used in this condition or due to the inability to follow proper instructions in maintaining oral hygiene due to improper use of oral hygiene aids. An oral health education program imparts training on maintaining oral hygiene in children and those who have ADHD. This helps in reducing the oral morbidity. Thereby improving the quality of life for these children.

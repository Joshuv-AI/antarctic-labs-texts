Upper lung asymmetry identifies distinct clinical phenotypes in idiopathic pleuroparenchymal fibroelastosis
Authors: Yoshiaki Kinoshita, Kengo Takata, Keita Hara, Chiharu Hirai, Taiki Ikeda, Takuhide Utsunomiya, Shota Nakashima, Hisako Kushima, Hiroshi Ishii
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIdiopathic pleuroparenchymal fibroelastosis (IPPFE) is a progressive interstitial lung disease characterized by upper lobe-predominant elastofibrosis. While disease asymmetry has been described in other interstitial lung diseases, its clinical significance in IPPFE remains unclear.MethodsWe evaluated 102 patients with IPPFE, 56 with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, and 50 healthy controls. Lung volumes were quantified using computed tomography to calculate the upper-lung asymmetry ratio (upper AR), defined as (combined volume of the right upper and middle lobe − left upper lobe volume) / predicted total lung capacity. Patients with IPPFE were classified as having asymmetrical IPPFE (AIPPFE) or symmetrical IPPFE (SIPPFE) based on the upper AR.ResultsAsymmetry of lung volume was observed in 40.2% of patients with IPPFE (41/102), including right-dominant AIPPFE in 31.4% (32/102) and left-dominant AIPPFE in 8.8% (9/102). Compared with SIPPFE, right-dominant AIPPFE tended to be more frequent in male smokers. Left-dominant AIPPFE tended to be more frequent in non-smokers and was associated with an increased risk of pneumothorax, although the sample size was small. In the multivariate analysis, lower % predicted forced vital capacity and higher upper AR were identified as independent risk factors for pneumothorax; however, upper AR was not significantly associated with survival. The extent of tracheal deviation was significantly correlated with the upper AR (r = − 0.558, p < 0.001).ConclusionsAsymmetry of upper lobe fibrosis is frequent in IPPFE, with distinct clinical characteristics based on the direction of predominance. The recognition of disease asymmetry in IPPFE may aid in risk stratification and clinical management.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12890-026-04368-y.

Background

Idiopathic pleuroparenchymal fibroelastosis (IPPFE) is a rare, progressive interstitial lung disease (ILD) characterized by upper lobe-predominant pleural fibrosis and intra-alveolar fibroelastosis within the pulmonary parenchyma [1–4]. Although IPPFE may occur idiopathically, it has also been reported in association with other ILDs, autoimmune diseases, chronic infection, occupational dust exposure, chronic graft-versus-host disease following hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, and other etiologies [5]. Despite increasing recognition, many clinical aspects of IPPFE remain poorly understood.

Asymmetric disease has been described in other ILDs, including idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) [6–10]. Tcherakian et al. reported that approximately 60% of IPF patients with asymmetrical disease exhibited right-lung dominance [6]. In their study, IPF patients with asymmetrical disease were associated with higher rates of gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) and acute exacerbations [6]. Several studies have also reported asymmetrical lung fibrosis in patients with lateral discrepancy in pulmonary perfusion [7–9]. These findings indicate that disease asymmetry may reflect underlying pathophysiological mechanisms and influence clinical outcomes.

In IPPFE patients, tracheal deviation is frequently observed, typically toward the right, and pneumothorax more frequently occurs in the right lung [11, 12]. These observations suggest possible disease asymmetry in IPPFE. However, this phenomenon has not been systematically evaluated. A better understanding of disease asymmetry in IPPFE may provide novel insights into disease pathogenesis and improve clinical management. We investigated the prevalence and clinical significance of asymmetric disease in IPPFE.

Methods

Study design and patient selection

This retrospective observational study included patients diagnosed with IPPFE at Fukuoka University Chikushi Hospital (Fukuoka, Japan) between April 2011 and December 2024. Eligible patients were ≥ 20 years of age and fulfilled diagnostic criteria for IPPFE based on clinical, radiological, and, when available, histopathological evaluation [13]. Patients were excluded if they had undergone prior lung resection or esophageal surgery or had chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. Patients with pneumothorax or infectious pneumonia at the diagnosis were excluded if computed tomography (CT) images without these complications, obtained within six months before or after the diagnosis, were unavailable. Patients with clinically evident nontuberculous mycobacterial (NTM) infection at the diagnosis were excluded because a definite etiology of PPFE was identified (secondary PPFE). In contrast, patients who developed NTM infection during the clinical course of IPPFE were included.

For comparison, 50 age- and sex-matched controls without lung disease on chest CT, selected from patients who underwent chest CT for non-respiratory conditions between January 2023 and December 2024, and 56 consecutive patients diagnosed with IPF with available CT data during the same period were included. This study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Review Committee for Human Research at Fukuoka University (approval number: C25-304) on May 15, 2025. Due to the retrospective nature of the study, the requirement for informed consent was waived.

Clinical data collection

Clinical characteristics, pulmonary function test results, laboratory findings at the diagnosis, and sputum microbiology during the clinical course were extracted from the patients’ medical records. Dyspnea severity at diagnosis was assessed using the previously published modified Medical Research Council (mMRC) dyspnea scale [14]. A positive antinuclear antibody (ANA) was defined as a titer of ≥ 1:80 by indirect immunofluorescence. Serum anti-GPL-core IgA antibody for Mycobacterium avium complex (MAC antibody) antibody titers ≥ 0.7 U/mL were considered positive. NTM cultures were considered positive when the organisms were identified on at least two occasions in sputum or once in bronchial washing samples. Additionally, the incidence of acute exacerbations, as defined by the international guidelines, was extracted [15]. The prevalence of GERD was determined based on patient self-reports or the regular use of proton pump inhibitors or H2 blockers.

Quantification of the lung volume

Lung volumes were quantified using SYNAPSE VINCENT (ver. 5.3; Fujifilm Medical Systems, Tokyo, Japan) on CT images obtained at the deep inspiratory phase at the diagnosis, as previously described (Supple. Figure 1A) [16, 17]. The upper-lung asymmetry ratio (upper AR) was defined as (right upper and middle lobe volume − left upper lobe volume) / predicted total lung capacity (TLC). The predicted TLC was calculated using sex, height, and age according to the formula proposed by the Japanese Respiratory Society [18].

IPPFE patients were classified as having left-dominant IPPFE if their upper AR exceeded the mean + one standard deviation (SD) of healthy controls, and right-dominant IPPFE if their upper AR was below the mean – one SD of healthy controls. Both were designated as asymmetrical IPPFE (AIPPFE). All other cases were classified as symmetrical IPPFE (SIPPFE). Because this index reflects relative volume loss, larger values indicate greater reduction in the left lung, whereas smaller values indicate greater reduction in the right lung.

CT image analysis

To assess the extent of tracheal deviation, the distance between the center of the trachea and the line connecting the center of the vertebral body to the posterior edge of the spinous process was measured on axial CT images, with rightward and leftward deviation defined as positive and negative, respectively [15]. The tracheal deviation distance was defined as the measurement with the greatest absolute value across serial axial CT slices (Supple. Figure 1B, C). To examine asymmetry in pulmonary artery size, which potentially influences disease asymmetry, the maximal short-axis diameters of the right and left pulmonary arteries were measured 1 cm distal to the bifurcation on axial CT images [19]. The right-to-left ratio of pulmonary artery size was then calculated (Supple. Figure 1D). The flat chest index was calculated as the ratio of the anteroposterior to transverse thoracic diameter at the sixth thoracic vertebra on axial CT, as previously described (Supple. Figure 1E) [20–22].

Pneumothorax

For patients with IPPFE who developed pneumothorax during the observation period, detailed information was collected, including onset date, duration, affected side, severity, treatment, and prior use of corticosteroids or antifibrotic agents. The severity of pneumothorax was graded according to the British Thoracic Society guidelines [23]: grade 1, pneumothorax with a visible rim of < 2 cm between the lung margin and chest wall, at the level of the hilum on chest radiography: grade 2, non-tension pneumothorax with a visible rim of > 2 cm: grade 3, tension pneumothorax. In patients with bilateral pneumothorax, the severity was assessed on the more severely affected lung. Mediastinal emphysema was not recognized as pneumothorax. Pneumothorax-free survival was defined as the time from the diagnosis to the first complication of pneumothorax.

Statistical analyses

Continuous variables are expressed as the mean ± SD, and categorical variables as number (percentage). Differences between two groups were compared using Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables, and Student’s T-test or the Mann-Whitney U test for continuous variables. Differences among the three groups were analyzed using Fisher’s test and the Holm method for categorical data, a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), and Dunnett’s test for parametric data, and the Kruskal-Wallis and Steel test for nonparametric data. Pearson’s correlation coefficients were used to evaluate correlations between variables, and the extent of correlation (r) was categorized as follows: 0.0–0.2 (negligible), 0.2–0.4 (weak), 0.4–0.7 (moderate), 0.7–0.9 (strong), and 0.9–1.0 (very strong) [24]. The Kaplan–Meier curve and log-rank test were used to estimate overall survival and pneumothorax-free survival. The Cox proportional hazards model was used to assess prognostic factors; variables with P < 0.1 in the univariate analysis were included in the multivariate model. Multiple imputation was performed for variables with up to 25% missing data that were expected to be missing at random. Five complete datasets were generated and analyzed using Cox proportional hazards models. The results of the five analyses were then combined to generate the final result. P-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Analyses were performed using R (ver. 4.4.1; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Patient and public involvement

Patients or the public were not involved in the design or conduct of our research.

Results

Background characteristics

One hundred eleven patients with IPPFE were initially recruited. Patients were excluded for the following reasons: history of prior lung resection or esophageal surgery (n = 4), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (n = 3), and absence of evaluable CT images at the diagnosis (n = 2). Consequently, 102 patients with IPPFE were included. The mean observation period for the enrolled patients was 783 days. Forty-three patients died during the observation period.

Patient characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The IPPFE cohort had a mean age of 73.6 years (range, 23–95) and a male predominance (66.7%). The mean body mass index (BMI) was 17.4 ± 2.85 kg/m2. Lower lobe fibrosis other than PPFE complicated 49% of patients. Pulmonary function tests showed a reduced % forced vital capacity (FVC) (71.8 ± 22.0%), elevated residual volume (RV)/TLC % predicted (117 ± 28.8%), and preserved % diffusing capacity of the lung for carbon monoxide (DLCO) (81.4 ± 37.5%). These findings are consistent with previous reports. The prevalence of GERD and aspiration pneumonia were 29.4% and 9.8%, respectively. The rates of ANA, MAC antibody, and NTM culture positivity were 17.1%, 4.9%, and 4.9%, respectively.

The IPF group included 47 males and 9 females (mean age, 75 [range, 47–89] years). Pulmonary function tests in the IPF group showed a preserved %FVC (87.9 ± 15.5%) and %DLCO (70.0 ± 26.7%). The healthy control group included 33 males and 17 females (mean age, 73.1 [range, 23–86] years).

Asymmetry of upper-lung volume

The upper AR differed significantly among the IPPFE, IPF, and healthy control groups (p = 0.011) (Fig. 1). Patients with IPPFE had a significantly smaller upper AR relative to healthy controls (3.12 ± 4.65 vs. 5.04 ± 4.3, p = 0.038). The upper AR in the IPPFE group was significantly smaller than in the IPF group (3.12 ± 4.65 vs. 5.29 ± 5.02, p = 0.018). Based on the mean and SD of the upper AR in healthy controls, patients with the upper AR > 9.34 (mean + SD in healthy controls) were diagnosed with left-dominant IPPFE, and those with the upper AR < 0.74 (mean - SD in healthy controls) were diagnosed with right-dominant IPPFE. Accordingly, 41 of the 102 patients with IPPFE (40.2%) were classified as having AIPPFE (right-dominant, n = 32; left-dominant, n = 9). Representative CT images in patients with the right-dominant and left-dominant IPPFE are shown in Fig. 2.

Left-dominant AIPPFE group showed significantly less smoking exposure (0% vs. 41%, p = 0.021) and lower tracheal deviation distance (-0.37 ± 12.1 vs. 9.36 ± 7.38, p < 0.001), relative to the SIPPFE (Table 2).

Association between upper AR and parameters

The upper AR was moderately and inversly correlated with the tracheal deviation distance (r=-0.558, p < 0.001). No significant correlations were found between the upper AR and other parameters, including age, BMI, flat chest index, pulmonary function test results, or serum biomarkers.

Association between pneumothorax and disease asymmetry

Pneumothorax complicated 41 of 102 patients with IPPFE (40.2%), with 55 cumulative events (right-sided, n = 24; left-sided, n = 22; bilateral, n = 9). The severity of pneumothorax was mostly Grade 1 (87.3%), with the majority (85.5%) managed conservatively without surgical intervention or drainage. At the onset of pneumothorax, steroid treatment was administered in 14.5% of cases and antifibrotic therapy in 3.6%. The cumulative incidence of pneumothorax at 1, 2, and 3 years after the diagnosis of IPPFE was 22.1%, 28.4%, and 34.3%, respectively (Suppl. Figure 2). Survival time complicated by pneumothorax accounted for 24.4% of the total observation period among all patients with IPPFE. There was no significant difference in duration of pneumothorax between affected side (right 155 ± 179 days vs. left 116 ± 153 days, p = 0.345). The initial pneumothorax occurred on the right in 19 patients, on the left in 15, and bilaterally in 7. Among 32 patients with right-dominant AIPPFE, 10 (31.2%) developed pneumothorax, with the first event more frequently affecting the right lung (n = 8) than the left (n = 2). Among 9 patients with left-dominant AIPPFE, 7 (77.7%) developed pneumothorax with the initial event affecting the left lung in 5 (left, n = 3; right, n = 2; bilateral, n = 2). Left-dominant AIPPFE showed a significantly higher incidence of pneumothorax compared with right-dominant AIPPFE, although the sample size was small (77.7% vs. 31.2%, p < 0.01).

Risk factors for pneumothorax

A univariate Cox analysis identified upper AR (hazard ratio [HR] 1.077, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.016–1.142, p = 0.012), %FVC (HR 0.955, 95% CI 0.939–0.973, p < 0.001), and BMI (HR 0.843, 95% CI 0.752–0.946, p = 0.003) as significant predictors of pneumothorax [12, 25]. The multivariate analysis confirmed upper AR (HR 1.069, 95% CI 1.006–1.136, p = 0.031) and %FVC (HR 0.96, 95% CI 0.941–0.978, p < 0.001) as independent risk factors (Suppl. Table 1).

Discussion

In this study, a classification of disease asymmetry based on quantitative lung volume showed that approximately 40% of IPPFE patients exhibited asymmetrical disease. Right-dominant IPPFE was the most common asymmetric pattern observed in this cohort and was associated with a higher proportion of male smokers. Left-dominant IPPFE was associated with a higher proportion of non-smokers and an increased risk of pneumothorax. These findings suggest that disease asymmetry in IPPFE is not merely a morphological variant but may represent a distinct clinical phenotype.

Several hypotheses may explain the asymmetry of PPFE lesions. In this study, right-dominant fibrosis was a frequent asymmetric pattern among IPPFE patients, consistent with previous reports of disease asymmetry in IPF [6]. Potential factors influencing disease asymmetry include aspiration, chronic infection, GERD, and anatomical asymmetry in pulmonary vasculature [6–9]. However, no significant differences in baseline characteristics were observed between AIPPFE and SIPPFE. An association between GERD and ILD development has been suggested [26]. Gastric acid exposure may contribute to the disease pathogenesis of PPFE because of the high prevalence of recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy in PPFE [27]. Meanwhile, Callahan et al. compared 14 patients with asymmetric IPF to 28 with symmetric IPF and found no statistically significant differences in the frequency of IPF exacerbations or GERD [10]. The assessment of GERD in this study was mainly based on antacid use and medical history, which may be insufficient. Therefore, larger-scale and more objective evaluations of acid reflux are warranted. Additionally, chronic and functional factors (e.g., lymphatic flow, local immune environment, and airway clearance) may also contribute to the right-sided predominance of IPPFE [28, 29]. Further research is needed to clarify the risk factors and pathogenesis of asymmetric disease in IPPFE.

PPFE is inherently associated with a high risk of pneumothorax [12]. Pathologically, subpleural cystic lesions that arise in advanced-stage IPPFE, known as pulmonary interstitial emphysema, is an independent risk factor for pneumothorax and mediastinal emphysema [30]. In this study, right-sided pneumothorax was more frequent than left-sided pneumothorax, with a notably high prevalence of bilateral pneumothorax. Although most patients with pneumothorax did not require drainage, resolution was often prolonged. These findings are largely consistent with a retrospective study on pneumothorax in IPPFE [12].

A high frequency of pneumothorax in left-dominant IPPFE was observed. It is reasonable that %FVC at the diagnosis was an independent risk factor for pneumothorax because disease severity is thought to be associated with the incidence of pneumothorax [12, 31]. A higher upper AR, indicative of left-sided disease, also emerged as an independent predictor of pneumothorax. This may be partly explained by anatomical differences in mediastinal configuration. Changes in intrathoracic pressure resulting from lung contraction can be compensated by adjustments in mediastinal configuration or thoracic wall structure [32]. We previously reported that as the upper lobes contract, the ribs rotate anteriorly, contributing to platythorax in IPPFE patients [33]. In ILDs other than IPPFE, the mediastinum can also adapt flexibly by shifting or altering the shape of the anterior mediastinal fat [32]. Tracheal deviation typically occurs at or above the level of the aortic arch in PPFE [11]. At this craniocaudal level, the right upper lobe is adjacent to mediastinal structures including the trachea, whereas the left upper lobe abuts the aortic arch. Right lung shrinkage alone can cause mediastinal shift including the trachea. Meanwhile, the aortic arch and the contiguous descending aorta which abuts left upper lobe are firmly anchored by ligaments (e.g., ligamentum arteriosum), limiting their mobility [34]. Consequently, contraction of the left upper lobe may lead to increased negative intrathoracic pressure, potentially predisposing IPPFE patients to pneumothorax.

Although tracheal deviation is not pathognomonic for PPFE, it is frequently observed in IPPFE patients [11]. Although the evaluation methods differed from those used in this study, a previous study reported tracheal deviation in 41 of 52 PPFE patients (rightward, n = 36; leftward, n = 5) [11]. A significant correlation was found between the degree of tracheal deviation and the upper AR in our cohort, suggesting that asymmetric upper lobe contraction exerts a traction on the trachea. Tracheal deviation may serve as a clinically useful surrogate marker of asymmetrical upper-lobe contraction in IPPFE.

This study was associated with several limitations. First, the quantitative lung volume is an indirect measure of the extent of fibroelastosis in iPPFE. Quantitative measurement of the extent of iPPFE lesions is difficult because the subpleural consolidation is adjacent to the parietal pleura and sometimes indistinguishable from the soft tissue of the chest wall. Instead, the upper-lobe volume is automatically measurable and is known to reliably reflect disease severity in IPPFE [17, 33]. Second, several sources of bias inherent to retrospective single-center studies may have influenced the results, including selection bias and information bias due to variability in medical records. Third, the assessment of GERD was based on patient-reported symptoms and medication history, which may have overlooked asymptomatic reflux. Fourth, relatively few patients had left-dominant PPFE, likely resulting in insufficient statistical power in subgroup comparisons. Fifth, 49% of patients with IPPFE had concomitant interstitial lung diseases other than PPFE, which may have influenced the results. In addition, overlap between PPFE and IPF may have led to potential misclassification and affected the comparisons.

Conclusion

In conclusion, disease asymmetry was observed in 40.2% of IPPFE patients. Right-dominant IPPFE is the frequent asymmetric pattern with a higher proportion of male smokers. Left-dominant IPPFE was associated with a higher proportion of non-smokers and was associated with a higher incidence of pneumothorax. These findings provide new insight into disease asymmetry and may enhance the understanding of disease mechanisms and management of IPPFE.

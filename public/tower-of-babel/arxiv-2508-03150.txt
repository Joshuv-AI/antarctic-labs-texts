Quadratic relations for ninth variations of Schur functions and application to Schur multiple zeta functions
Wataru Takeda, Yoshinori Yamasaki

Abstract
. Macdonald’s ninth variation of Schur functions is a broad generalization of the classical Schur function and its variants, defined via the Jacobi–Trudi determinant formula. In this paper, we establish various algebraic relations for S λ / μ ( r ) ​ ( X ) S^{(r)}_{\lambda/\mu}(X) , a class of the ninth variation introduced by Nakagawa, Noumi, Shirakawa, and Yamada, by combining the Jacobi–Trudi formula with determinant formulas such as the Desnanot–Jacobi adjoint matrix theorem and the Plücker relations, which generalize the corresponding relations for Schur functions. As an application, we investigate algebraic relations for "diagonally constant" Schur multiple zeta functions and examine their specific special values when the shape is rectangular.

05E05, 11M32.

1.
Introduction

Schur functions
s_{\lambda}
, along with their skew generalization
s_{\lambda/\mu}
called skew Schur functions,
are an important class of symmetric functions.
In 1992, Macdonald
[
ma92
]
introduced the so-called ninth variation of Schur functions,
which includes many variants of
s_{\lambda/\mu}
such as the factorial and flagged Schur functions,
defined as those satisfying the Jacobi-Trudi formula.
About a decade later, Nakagawa, Noumi, Shirakawa, and Yamada
[
nnsy
]

studied a class of the ninth variation
S^{(r)}_{\lambda/\mu}(X)
of Schur functions,
defined for a matrix of variables
X
via its Gauss decomposition.
They established several determinant formulas and tableau expressions (in some special cases of
X
) for
S^{(r)}_{\lambda/\mu}(X)
,
using properties of minor determinants.
Recently, Foley and King
[
fk21
]
introduced another type of the ninth variation of Schur functions, denoted by
S^{\mathrm{FK}}_{\lambda/\mu}(W)
, defined via a tableau expression, and derived the Hamel–Goulden formula
[
hg
]
, which gives an extensive generalization of the Jacobi–Trudi formula.
We remark that, as will be seen in Lemma
2.7
,
S^{\mathrm{FK}}_{\lambda/\mu}(W)
can be obtained as a specialization of
S^{(r)}_{\lambda/\mu}(X)
.

It is known that Schur functions satisfy various algebraic relations.
For example, by combining the Jacobi–Trudi formula with the Plücker relations for products of determinants, Kleber
[
k01
]
derived a quadratic relation for
s_{\lambda}
, described combinatorially in terms of the Young diagram (see Theorem
for details).
The aim of the present paper is to establish algebraic (mainly quadratic) relations for
S^{(r)}_{\lambda/\mu}(X)
, including a generalization of Kleber’s formula.
As a direct consequence, we obtain the corresponding algebraic relations for
S^{\mathrm{FK}}_{\lambda/\mu}(W)
via the specialization mentioned above.
Moreover, we apply these results to the Schur multiple zeta functions
\zeta_{\lambda/\mu}(\boldsymbol{s})
introduced in
[
npy
]
, which provide a combinatorial generalization of both multiple zeta and multiple zeta-star functions, particularly when the variable tableau
\boldsymbol{s}
is diagonally constant.
Actually, these further allow us to derive explicit expressions or generating functions for certain families of Schur multiple zeta values of rectangular shape.

The organization of the paper is as follows.
In Section
2
, we first review the definition of
S^{(r)}_{\lambda/\mu}(X)
,
and then prove that it satisfies the Giambelli formula (Theorem
2.3
),
which gives a skew generalization of the formula for
S^{(r)}_{\lambda}(X)
obtained in
[
nnsy
]
. Next, we recall the definition of
S^{\mathrm{FK}}_{\lambda/\mu}(W)

and show that it can be obtained as a specialization of
S^{(r)}_{\lambda/\mu}(X)
.
Sections
3
and
are devoted to investigating several algebraic relations for
S^{(r)}_{\lambda/\mu}(X)
, derived from the Desnanot–Jacobi adjoint matrix theorem and the Plücker relations, respectively.
In particular, by introducing the adding and removing operators for Young diagrams, we generalize Kleber’s quadratic relation for
s_{\lambda}

[
k01
]
to
S^{(r)}_{\lambda}(X)
(Theorem
).
Finally, in Section
, as an application or related topic of the results obtained in the previous sections, we study relations for the "diagonally constant" Schur multiple zeta functions
\zeta_{\lambda/\mu}(\boldsymbol{s})
. In particular, we focus on the case where
\lambda/\mu
is a rectangular shape and the variable tableau
\boldsymbol{s}
is filled with at most three integers, noting that in non-admissible cases, we consider the regularization studied in
[
bc
]
.
For example, we express the generating function for such values via the generating function of the corresponding (regularized) multiple zeta values.

2.
Ninth variations of skew Schur functions

In this section, we recall the definitions of the ninth variations of skew Schur functions introduced in
[
nnsy
]
and
[
fk21
]
, respectively.
Moreover, for the former, we derive a Giambelli formula that extends the result of
[
nnsy
]
from the non-skew case.

2.1.
Notations and Terminologies

We first summarize the notations and terminologies that are used throughout the present paper.
A partition is a non-increasing sequence
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})
of positive integers.
The length and weight of
\lambda
are denoted by
\ell(\lambda):=k
and
|\lambda|:=\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{k}
, respectively.
We sometimes write
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots)
with the understanding that
\lambda_{i}=0
for
i>k
, and also
\lambda=(1^{m_{1}(\lambda)}2^{m_{2}(\lambda)}\cdots)
,
where
m_{i}(\lambda)
is the multiplicity of
i
in
\lambda
.
The Young diagram associated with
\lambda
is defined by

D({\lambda}):=\{(i,j)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\,|\,1\leq i\leq k,1\leq j\leq\lambda_{i}\}
, which is depicted as a collection of square boxes with the
i
-th row having
\lambda_{i}
boxes.
The conjugate partition of
\lambda
is denoted by

\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\lambda^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,\lambda^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}})
,
where
\lambda^{\prime}_{i}=\#\{j\,|\,\lambda_{j}\geq i\}
.
A skew partition
\lambda/\mu
is a pair of partitions

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})
and

\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{l})
satisfying
\lambda\supset\mu
, that is,
k\geq l
and
\lambda_{i}\geq\mu_{i}
for all
i
.
When
\mu
is the empty partition
\varnothing
,
we identify
\lambda/\mu
with
\lambda
.
We also associate
\lambda/\mu
with the skew Young diagram
D({\lambda/\mu}):=D(\lambda)\setminus D(\mu)
.
We say that
(i,j)\in D({\lambda/\mu})
is a corner of
\lambda/\mu
if
(i+1,j)\notin D({\lambda/\mu})
and
(i,j+1)\notin D({\lambda/\mu})
,
and denote by
C({\lambda/\mu})
the set of all corners of
\lambda/\mu
.
A Young tableau of shape
\lambda/\mu
over a set
S
is a filling
T=(t_{i,j})_{(i,j)\in D(\lambda/\mu)}
of boxes of
D(\lambda/\mu)
with
t_{i,j}\in S
.
We denote by
\T(\lambda/\mu,S)
the set of all Young tableaux of shape
\lambda/\mu
over
S
.
Finally, for a positive integer
n
, we put
[n]:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.

2.2.
Ninth variations of skew Schur functions defined in
[
nnsy
]

From now on, we always assume that a partition
\lambda
is contained in the rectangle
(s^{r})
for some non-negative integers

r
and
s
. Put
N=r+s
.

Let
X=[x_{i,j}]_{1\leq i,j\leq N}
be an
N
-by-
N
matrix, where each
(i,j)
-entry
(X)_{i,j}=x_{i,j}
of
X
is assumed to be indeterminate.
For sequences of row indices
A=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
and column indices
B=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{r})
,
let
X^{A}_{B}=X^{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}}_{b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}}:=[x_{a_{i},b_{j}}]_{1\leq i,j\leq r}

be the
r
-by-
r
submatrix of
X

corresponding to
A
and
B

and
\xi^{A}_{B}(X)=\xi^{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}}_{b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}}(X):=\det X^{A}_{B}
.
Moreover, for subsets
I,J\subset[N]
with
|I|=|J|=r
,
we put
X^{I}_{J}:=X^{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}}

and
\xi^{I}_{J}(X):=\xi^{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}}(X)

where
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}
and
j_{1}<\cdots<j_{r}
are the sequences obtained by arranging the elements of
I
and
J
in increasing order, respectively.
To calculate minors, the following formulas are useful:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\xi^{I}_{J}(XY)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\subset[N]\\
|K|=r\end{subarray}}\xi^{I}_{K}(X)\xi^{K}_{J}(Y),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\xi^{I}_{J}(X)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in I}i+\sum_{j\in J}j}\cdot\det X\cdot\xi^{J^{c}}_{I^{c}}(X^{-1}),

where
I^{c}=[N]\setminus I
and
J^{c}=[N]\setminus J
, respectively.
The second one is called Jacobi’s complementary minor formula.
Write the Gauss decomposition of
X
as

X=X_{-}X_{0}X_{+},

where
X_{-}
,
X_{0}
and
X_{+}
are lower unitriangular,
diagonal and upper unitriangular matrices, respectively,
which are uniquely determined as matrices with entries in

\mathbb{C}(X)
, the field of rational functions over
\mathbb{C}
in the variable
x_{i,j}
for
1\leq i,j\leq N
.
Actually, one sees that

(2.3)

\displaystyle(X_{-})_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi^{1,\ldots,j-1,i}_{1,\ldots,j-1,j}(X)}{\xi^{1,\ldots,j}_{1,\ldots,j}(X)}

\displaystyle\hskip-50.0pt(i\geq j),

(2.4)

\displaystyle(X_{0})_{i,i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi^{1,\ldots,i}_{1,\ldots,i}(X)}{\xi^{1,\ldots,i-1}_{1,\ldots,i-1}(X)}

\displaystyle\hskip-50.0pt(1\leq i\leq N),

(2.5)

\displaystyle(X_{+})_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi^{1,\ldots,i-1,i}_{1,\ldots,i-1,j}(X)}{\xi^{1,\ldots,i}_{1,\ldots,i}(X)}

\displaystyle\hskip-50.0pt(i\leq j).

Notice that the entries of the inverse matrix
X^{-1}_{+}
of
X_{+}
is similarly given by

(2.6)

\displaystyle(X^{-1}_{+})_{i,j}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j-i}\frac{\xi^{1,\ldots,j-1}_{1,\ldots,\widehat{i},\ldots,j}(X)}{\xi^{1,\ldots,j-1}_{1,\ldots,j-1}(X)}\qquad(i\leq j),

where
\widehat{i}
means that we ignore
i
.
In
[
nnsy
]
, the ninth variation of skew Schur function

S^{(r)}_{\lambda/\mu}(X)
is defined by

(2.7)

\displaystyle S^{(r)}_{\lambda/\mu}(X):=\xi^{I}_{J}(X_{+})=(-1)^{|\lambda/\mu|}\xi^{J^{c}}_{I^{c}}(X^{-1}_{+})\in\mathbb{C}(X),

where
I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\}\subset[N]

with
i_{a}=\mu_{r+1-a}+a

and
J=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}\}\subset[N]

with
j_{a}=\lambda_{r+1-a}+a

are the Maya diagrams of
\mu
and
\lambda
, respectively.
Notice that the second equality in (
2.7
) follows from (
2.2
),
and
I^{c}=\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{s}\}\subset[N]
with
k_{a}=r+a-\mu^{\prime}_{a}

and
J^{c}=\{l_{1},\ldots,l_{s}\}\subset[N]
with

l_{a}=r+a-\lambda^{\prime}_{a}
.
As the special case
\mu=\varnothing
,
using (
2.1
)
with (
2.3
), (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we have

(2.8)

\displaystyle S^{(r)}_{\lambda}(X)=\frac{\xi^{1,\ldots,r}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}}(X)}{\xi^{1,\ldots,r}_{1,\ldots,r}(X)}.

This is a kind of the classical Weyl formula for Schur function.
We remark that

S^{(r)}_{\lambda/\mu}(X)
gives
the classical skew Schur function
s_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
of
n
variables
when
X
is the Vandermonde matrix

X=\left[x^{j-1}_{i}\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq N}

with variables
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in[N]}
,
under the specialization
x_{n+1}=\cdots=x_{N}=0
when
r
and
s
are sufficiently large.
In the following, for simplicity, we graphically express
S^{(r+m)}_{\lambda/\mu}(X)
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}

by using the Young tableau

\left(\,\overline{m+c(i,j)}\,\right)_{(i,j)\in D(\lambda/\mu)}

of shape
\lambda/\mu
.
Here,
c(i,j):=j-i
is the content of
(i,j)\in D(\lambda/\mu)
, and, for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\overline{n}=n
if
n\geq 0
and
-|n|
otherwise.

To do that, we first review the Frobenius notation.
Let
\lambda
be a partition having
p
diagonal entries. Define the sequences of indices
\alpha_{1}>\cdots>\alpha_{p}\geq 0
and
\beta_{1}>\cdots>\beta_{p}\geq 0
by
\alpha_{i}=\lambda_{i}-i
and
\beta_{i}=\lambda^{\prime}_{i}-i
for

Then, in the Frobenius notation, we write

\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p})

Let
\mu

the Frobenius notation of

1\leq j\leq 2q\end{subarray}}

k_{i,j}=\begin{cases}\delta_{i,r-\delta_{q+1-j}}&(1\leq i\leq r,\ 1\leq j\leq q),\\
\delta_{q+i,r+j}&(r+1\leq i\leq r+q,\ q+1\leq j\leq 2q),\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

with
\delta_{i,j}
being the Kronecker delta.
Now we apply Theorem
2.4
to the
(r+q)\times(r+p+2q)
matrix
Z=[\,H^{\prime}\,|\,K\,]
with

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{p+q}),\quad a_{i}=r+i\quad(1\leq i\leq p+q),

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{p+q}),\quad b_{i}=\begin{cases}r-\beta_{p+1-i}&(1\leq i\leq p),\\
r+i+q&(p+1\leq i\leq p+q),\end{cases}

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=(1,\ldots,r)\setminus(r-\beta_{1},\ldots,r-\beta_{p}).

Reordering
A\,\sqcup\,C
and
B\,\sqcup\,C
in increasing order and applying cofactor expansion yield

\displaystyle\xi^{(1,\ldots,n)}_{A\,\sqcup\,C}(Z)=(-1)^{\varepsilon_{A,C}}S^{(r)}_{\lambda/\mu}(X),\quad\xi^{(1,\ldots,n)}_{B\,\sqcup\,C}(Z)=(-1)^{\varepsilon_{B,C}},

where

\varepsilon_{A,C}:=(p+q)(r-p)+\sum^{q}_{k=1}(r-\delta_{q+1-k}+r+1)

and

\varepsilon_{B,C}:=\sum^{p}_{k=1}(q+r-\beta_{p+1-k}-1)+rq
.
This shows that the right-hand side of (
2.15
) is

(-1)^{\varepsilon}S^{(r)}_{\lambda/\mu}(X)

with

\varepsilon:={\frac{(p+q)(p+q-1)}{2}}+\varepsilon_{A,C}+(p+q-1)\varepsilon_{B,C}
.

We next compute the left-hand side of (
2.15
).
Put

d_{i,j}=\xi^{(1,\ldots,n)}_{(a_{i})\,\sqcup\,(B\setminus(b_{j}))\,\sqcup\,C}(Z)
.

•

When
1\leq i,j\leq p
, we have from (
2.16
)
d_{i,j}=(-1)^{u_{j}}S^{(r)}_{(\alpha_{p+1-i}\,|\,\beta_{p+1-j})}(X),

where
u_{j}:=(r-p)q+r-1+\sum^{j-1}_{k=1}(r-\beta_{p+1-k}-2)+\sum^{p}_{k=j+1}(r-\beta_{p+1-k}-1)
.

•

When
1\leq i\leq q
and
1\leq j\leq p
,
we have

d_{p+i,j}=(-1)^{u_{j}+\delta_{q+1-i}}e^{(r-\gamma_{q+1-i}-1)}_{\beta_{p+1-j}-\delta_{q+1-i}}(X)
.

•

When
1\leq i\leq p
and
1\leq j\leq q
, we have

{d_{i,p+j}}=(-1)^{v_{j}+j+1}h^{(r+\gamma_{q+1-j}+1)}_{\alpha_{p+1-i}-\gamma_{q+1-j}}(X)
,
where
v_{j}:=(r-p)(q-1)+r+\sum^{p}_{k=1}(r-\beta_{p+1-k}-1)
.

•

When
1\leq i,j\leq q
, we have
d_{p+i,q+j}=0
.

Therefore, the left-hand side of (
2.15
) is

\displaystyle\det\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\left[(-1)^{u_{j}}S^{(r)}_{(\alpha_{p+1-i}\,|\,\beta_{q+1-j})}(X)\right]_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq p\\
1\leq j\leq p\end{subarray}}&\left[(-1)^{v_{j}}h^{(r+\gamma_{q+1-j}+1)}_{\alpha_{p+1-i}-\gamma_{q+1-j}}(X)\right]_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq p\\
1\leq j\leq q\end{subarray}}\\[10.0pt]
\hline\cr\\[-10.0pt]
\left[(-1)^{u_{j}+\delta_{q+1-i}}e^{(r-\delta_{q+1-i}-1)}_{\beta_{p+1-j}-\delta_{q+1-i}}(X)\right]_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq q\\
1\leq j\leq p\end{subarray}}&O_{q}\end{array}\right],

which is equal to
(-1)^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\det G^{(r)}_{\lambda/\mu}(X)
with

\varepsilon^{\prime}:=\sum^{p}_{k=1}u_{k}+\sum^{q}_{k=1}v_{k}+\sum^{q}_{k=1}\delta_{q+1-k}+\sum^{q}_{k=1}(k+1)
.
Now the desired formula follows because

\varepsilon-\varepsilon^{\prime}\equiv q
​​​
\pmod{2}
.

2.4.
Ninth variations of skew Schur functions defined in
[
fk21
]

We next present another type of the ninth variation of the skew Schur function defined by Foley and King.
In what follows, for a set
Y
,
we denote by
\mathbb{C}[Y]
the ring of polynomials over
\mathbb{C}
in indeterminates indexed by the elements of
Y
.

A Young tableau
(t_{i,j})\in\T(\lambda/\mu,\mathbb{N})

with
\mathbb{N}
being the set of positive integers is called semi-standard if it satisfies

t_{i,j}\leq t_{i,j+1}
and
t_{i,j}<t_{i+1,j}

for all
i,j
.
We denote by
\SSYT(\lambda/\mu)
the set of all semi-standard Young tableaux of shape
\lambda/\mu
.
Moreover, for
M\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
\SSYT_{M}(\lambda/\mu)
be the subset of
\SSYT(\lambda/\mu)

consisting of all
(t_{i,j})
satisfying
t_{i,j}\in[M]
for all
i,j
.
The ninth variation of skew Schur function

S^{\mathrm{FK}}_{\lambda/\mu}(W)

with variables
W=\{w_{k,c}\}_{k\in[M],\,c\in\mathbb{Z}}
introduced in
[
fk21
]

is defined by the following sum over all semi-standard tableaux:

(2.17)

\displaystyle S^{\mathrm{FK}}_{\lambda/\mu}(W):=\sum_{(t_{ij})\in\SSYT_{M}(\lambda/\mu)}\prod_{(i,j)\in D(\lambda/\mu)}w_{t_{i,j},c(i,j)}\in\mathbb{C}[W].

This also gives a generalization of the classical Schur function:
If
w_{k,c}
does not depend on
c
,
then
S^{\mathrm{FK}}_{\lambda/\mu}(W)=s_{\lambda/\mu}(w_{1},\ldots,w_{M})
where we write
w_{k}=w_{k,c}
.
As special cases,
we put
h^{\mathrm{FK}}_{d}(W):=S^{\mathrm{FK}}_{(d)}(W)
and
e^{\mathrm{FK}}_{d}(W):=S^{\mathrm{FK}}_{(1^{d})}(W)
for
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
and
h^{\mathrm{FK}}_{0}(W)=e^{\mathrm{FK}}_{0}(W)=1
and
h^{\mathrm{FK}}_{d}(W)=e^{\mathrm{FK}}_{d}(W)=0
if
d<0
.

Here, recall that

S^{(r)}_{\lambda/\mu}(X)

also has a tableau expression when

X
is a special type of matrix.

Theorem 2.6
.

(
[
nnsy
, (2.59) and (2.64)]
)
For
\boldsymbol{u}=\{u^{(t)}_{k}\}_{k\in[M],\,t\in[N-1]}
and
\boldsymbol{v}=\{v^{(t)}_{k}\}_{k\in[M],\,t\in[N-1]}
,
define the upper unitriangular matrices

U_{M}(\boldsymbol{u})
and
V_{M}(\boldsymbol{v})
of size
N
by

\displaystyle U_{M}(\boldsymbol{u}):=U_{1}U_{2}\cdots U_{M},\qquad V_{M}(\boldsymbol{v}):=V_{1}V_{2}\cdots V_{M},

where,

\displaystyle U_{k}

\displaystyle=(E+u^{(1)}_{k}E_{1,2})(E+u^{(2)}_{k}E_{2,3})\cdots(E+u^{(N-1)}_{k}E_{N-1,N}),

\displaystyle V_{k}

\displaystyle=(E+v^{(N-1)}_{k}E_{N-1,N})\cdots(E+u^{(2)}_{k}E_{2,3})(E+u^{(1)}_{k}E_{1,2})

with
E
and
E_{i,j}
being the unit matrix and the matrix unit of size
N
, respectively.
Then, we have

(2.18)

\displaystyle S^{(r)}_{\lambda/\mu}(U_{M}(\boldsymbol{u}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{(t_{i,j})\in\SSYT_{M}(\lambda/\mu)}\prod_{(i,j)\in D(\lambda/\mu)}u^{(r+c(i,j))}_{t_{i,j}}\in\mathbb{C}[\boldsymbol{u}],

(2.19)

\displaystyle S^{(r)}_{\lambda/\mu}(V_{M}(\boldsymbol{v}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{(t_{i,j})\in\SSYT_{M}(\lambda^{\prime}/\mu^{\prime})}\prod_{(i,j)\in D(\lambda^{\prime}/\mu^{\prime})}v^{(r-c(i,j))}_{t_{i,j}}\in\mathbb{C}[\boldsymbol{v}].

This clearly shows that

S^{\mathrm{FK}}_{\lambda/\mu}(W)

is obtained as a specialization of
S^{(r)}_{\lambda/\mu}(X)
.

Lemma 2.7
.

For
W=\{w_{k,c}\}_{k\in[M],c\in\mathbb{Z}}
,
define

\boldsymbol{u}=\{u^{(t)}_{k}\}_{k\in[M],\,t\in[N-1]}
and

\boldsymbol{v}=\{v^{(t)}_{k}\}_{k\in[M],\,t\in[N-1]}

by
u^{(t)}_{k}=w_{k,t-r}
and

v^{(t)}_{k}=w_{k,-t+r}
, respectively.
Then, for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

(2.20)

\displaystyle S^{\mathrm{FK}}_{\lambda/\mu}(\tau^{m}W)

\displaystyle=S^{(r+m)}_{\lambda/\mu}(U_{M}(\boldsymbol{u})),

(2.21)

\displaystyle S^{\mathrm{FK}}_{\lambda/\mu}(\tau^{-m}W)

\displaystyle=S^{(r+m)}_{\lambda^{\prime}/\mu}(V_{M}(\boldsymbol{u})),

where
\tau^{m}W:=\{w_{k,m+c}\}_{k\in[M],\,c\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
In particular, for
d\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\displaystyle h^{\mathrm{FK}}_{d}(\tau^{m}W)=h^{(r+m)}_{d}(U_{M}(\boldsymbol{u})),\quad e^{\mathrm{FK}}_{d}(\tau^{m}W)=e^{(r+m)}_{d}(U_{M}(\boldsymbol{u})).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\lambda/\mu
be a skew partition with

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k+1})
and

\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k+1})
,
and
\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\lambda^{\prime}_{l+1})
and

\mu^{\prime}=(\mu^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\mu^{\prime}_{l+1})
their conjugates, respectively.

(1)

It holds that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&S_{(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k+1})/(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k+1})}^{(r)}(X)\cdot S_{(\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{k})/(\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{k})}^{(r-1)}(X)\\
&=S_{(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})/(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k})}^{(r)}(X)\cdot S_{(\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{k+1})/(\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{k+1})}^{(r-1)}(X)\\
&\ \ \ -S_{(\lambda_{2}-1,\ldots,\lambda_{k+1}-1)/(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k})}^{(r)}(X)\cdot S_{(\lambda_{1}+1,\ldots,\lambda_{k}+1)/(\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{k+1})}^{(r-1)}(X).\end{split}

(2)

It holds that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&S_{(\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\lambda^{\prime}_{l+1})^{\prime}/(\mu^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\mu^{\prime}_{l+1})^{\prime}}^{(r)}(X)\cdot S_{(\lambda^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,\lambda^{\prime}_{l})^{\prime}/(\mu^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,\mu^{\prime}_{l})^{\prime}}^{(r+1)}(X)\\
&=S_{(\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\lambda^{\prime}_{l})^{\prime}/(\mu^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\mu^{\prime}_{l})^{\prime}}^{(r)}(X)\cdot S_{(\lambda^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,\lambda^{\prime}_{l+1})^{\prime}/(\mu^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,\mu^{\prime}_{l+1})^{\prime}}^{(r+1)}(X)\\
&\ \ \ -S_{(\lambda^{\prime}_{2}-1,\ldots,\lambda^{\prime}_{l+1}-1)^{\prime}/(\mu^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\mu^{\prime}_{l})^{\prime}}^{(r)}(X)\cdot S_{(\lambda^{\prime}_{1}+1,\ldots,\lambda^{\prime}_{l}+1)^{\prime}/(\mu^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,\mu^{\prime}_{l+1})^{\prime}}^{(r+1)}(X).\end{split}

Here, we understand
S^{(r)}_{\lambda/\mu}(X)=0

if
\lambda/\mu
is not a skew partition.

Proof.

Applying (
3.1
)
to the case
Z=H^{(r)}_{\lambda/\mu}(X)
with

\displaystyle\xi^{1,\ldots,k+1}_{1,\ldots,k+1}(Z)

\displaystyle=S^{(r)}_{(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k+1})/(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k+1})}(X),

\displaystyle\xi^{2,\ldots,k}_{2,\ldots,k}(Z)

\displaystyle=S^{(r-1)}_{(\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{k})/(\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{k})}(X),

\displaystyle\xi^{1,\ldots,k}_{1,\ldots,k}(Z)

\displaystyle=S^{(r)}_{(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})/(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k})}(X),

\displaystyle\xi^{2,\ldots,k+1}_{2,\ldots,k+1}(Z)

\displaystyle=S^{(r-1)}_{(\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{k+1})/(\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{k+1})}(X),

\displaystyle\xi^{2,\ldots,k+1}_{1,\ldots,k}(Z)

\displaystyle=S^{(r)}_{(\lambda_{2}-1,\ldots,\lambda_{k+1}-1)/(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k})}(X),

\displaystyle\xi^{1,\ldots,k}_{2,\ldots,k+1}(Z)

\displaystyle=S^{(r-1)}_{(\lambda_{1}+1,\ldots,\lambda_{k}+1)/(\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{k+1})}(X),

which are derived from (
2.12
),
we obtain the first assertion.
One can similarly prove the second one by considering the cases
Z=E^{(r)}_{\lambda/\mu}(X)
with (
2.13
).
∎

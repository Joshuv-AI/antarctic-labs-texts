Enhanced B–N coordinated dynamic boronate chemistry for recyclable thermosets with elevated stability
Authors: Chaoran Xu, Congze He, Jin Dong, Jie Yun, Shicheng Yang, Yuxuan Du, Zhikang Xie, Xiaoru Dong, Zhuo Li, Krzysztof Matyjaszewski, Xiangcheng Pan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe environmental impact of non-recyclable thermoset waste demands sustainable polymer solutions. Existing dynamic covalent boronic ester chemistries operate at relatively low temperatures (<150 °C), resulting in insufficient stability to meet thermoset performance requirements. Here, we overcome these limitations through a N-iminodiacetic acid (N-IDA) boronate system featuring a strengthened internal B–N coordination bond. This enhanced B–N bond (~1.65 Å) endows the dynamic covalent bonds with both exceptional thermal stability and controllable exchange above 150 °C. We leverage this chemistry to develop radical crosslinkers compatible with commercial monomers and thermoplastics. The resulting recyclable thermosets demonstrate exceptional thermal stability, chemical durability under harsh environments (85 °C/85% RH, 7 days), and excellent dimensional stability. This stability–exchange dichotomy is exemplified in heat-shrinkable crosslinked polyethylene (XLPE), where the enhanced B–N coordination maintains network integrity at service temperatures (<150 °C) while enabling thermal reprocessability above 150 °C. By extending the operational range of boronic ester-based dynamic chemistry to high temperature domains, this work provides a scalable pathway toward recyclable thermosets without stability compromises.Subject terms: Polymer synthesis, Polymers, Mechanical properties

Introduction

Thermosetting plastics, featuring permanently cross-linked polymer networks, are widely deployed in harsh operating conditions due to their exceptional thermal stability, chemical resistance, and mechanical durability1. However, their permanent crosslinked structure presents significant challenges for material recycling and reprocessing, raising increasing concerns about environmental sustainability2,3. Dynamic covalent chemistry offers a promising strategy to address this recycling challenge of thermosets, enabling the development of recyclable and reprocessable materials while maintaining desirable thermomechanical properties4–6.

Boronic ester chemistry stands out among dynamic covalent chemistries due to its catalyst-free nature, tunable exchange kinetics, mild reaction conditions, and compatibility with various functional groups7. Boroxines and dioxaborolanes (DOBLs)8–10, as sp2 hybridized boronic esters, exhibit dynamic properties through B–O bond exchange but suffer from poor hydrolytic stability and susceptibility to degradation in humid environments (Fig. 1a), limiting their practical applications. B–N coordination has emerged as an effective strategy to enhance boronic ester stability11, transforming the boron center from sp² to sp³ hybridization and originating from either intermolecular or intramolecular nitrogen sources. In intermolecular B–N coordination systems, nitrogen from external sources forms coordinate bonds with the boron center12–17, showing B–N bond lengths of approximately 1.80–1.98 Å. Intramolecular N-coordination in cyclic structures such as aminodiol boronates utilizes the geometric constraints of five-membered or six-membered rings to create stronger B–N coordination18–21, evidenced by shortened B–N bond lengths of approximately 1.75 Å. This coordination enhancement improves hydrolytic stability and environmental resistance, enabling applications in vitrimers and underwater adhesives18,21.

However, these boronic esters undergo dynamic exchange at relatively low temperatures (typically 60–80 °C), which is substantially lower than the service temperature of most engineering plastics (70–120 °C). This mismatch causes continuous dynamic bond exchange and creep deformation during service—what high-performance thermosets should prevent22–25. To overcome this limitation, the exchange temperatures (Texchange) should be substantially higher than both the glass transition temperature (Tg) and melting temperature (Tm), creating a safe operating window where the network remains topologically frozen and dimensionally stable during normal service, while still enabling controlled exchange at higher temperatures for reprocessing. Additionally, these boronic esters suffer from poor hydrolytic stability, further limiting their practical applicability. Therefore, developing dynamic boronic ester systems with substantially higher exchange temperatures and improved chemical stability is essential to meet these demanding requirements.

N-methyliminodiacetic acid (MIDA) boronate presents a promising solution to these challenges, exhibiting remarkable conformational stability up to 150 °C due to its sp³ hybridized tridentate chelate structure, which makes it resistant to both acidic and aqueous environments26–28. The exceptional thermodynamic stability of MIDA boronates stems from the unique carbonyl groups (C=O) in the iminodiacetic acid (IDA) structure: the electron-withdrawing nature of carbonyls reduces the electron density on carboxylate oxygens, leading to more polarized B–O bonds and consequently enhanced Lewis acidity of the boron center for stronger B–N coordination, with a notably short B–N bond length of approximately 1.65 Å29. As illustrated in the electrostatic potential (ESP) maps (Fig. 1b), when comparing diethanolamine (DEA) boronate with N-methyl IDA boronate, the latter shows more intense red regions representing stronger negative electrostatic potential around the carbonyl oxygens, and correspondingly deeper blue regions indicating higher positive electrostatic potential at the boron center. This reinforced B–N coordination requires higher thermal energy to overcome the coordination barrier, potentially enabling dynamic exchange reactions above 150 °C while maintaining network stability at normal operating temperatures.

In this work, we propose a high-temperature dynamic covalent chemistry approach based on N-coordinated iminodiacetic acid (N-IDA) boronates to address the challenges in developing recyclable high-performance thermosets. The robust nature of N-IDA boronate enables modification of N-coordinated sites, facilitating its incorporation into radical crosslinkers. N-IDA boronate motifs can be integrated into commercial monomers or thermoplastics via established polymerization or crosslinking processes, offering a pathway to a wide range of malleable, recyclable thermosets. These developed materials demonstrate excellent thermal stability, chemical resistance, and superior creep resistance, while also providing the potential for reprocessability and sustainability through high-temperature dynamic covalent chemistry.

Results

High-temperature dynamic covalent chemistry of bench-stable N-IDA boronates

To develop high-temperature dynamic covalent chemistry, we first established the exceptional stability of N-IDA boronates. Through systematic comparison with conventional dynamic covalent bonds (DOBL and DEA boronates), we demonstrated the superior thermal and chemical stability of N-IDA boronates using thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and hydrolysis experiments (Fig. 1c and S1). This enhanced stability stems from their unique tridentate chelating structure, which necessitates substantial activation energy for exchange reactions.

To precisely investigate the dynamic exchange characteristics of N-IDA boronates, we strategically designed two boronate compounds with distinct structural features (Scheme S1; Figs. S2–3). The N-methyl substituent of traditional MIDA was replaced with an N-hexyl group, introducing controlled steric hindrance to fine-tune the reactivity of the boronic esters. Single-crystal X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis confirmed the successful molecular engineering (Figs. S4–5 and Tables S1–S4), revealing B–N bond lengths of 1.6559 Å and 1.6600 Å for compounds A and B, respectively. The N-hexyl-substituted compound B exhibited enhanced reactivity due to its longer bond length30. Additionally, 4-fluorophenyl and 3-methylphenyl groups were strategically incorporated as distinguishable markers for post-exchange product identification, allowing simultaneous tracking via both 1H and 19F NMR spectroscopy.

The exchange reaction was investigated through both theoretical calculations and experimental studies. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations indicated that B–N coordination bonds require approximately 1 eV (~96.5 kJ/mol) for temporary dissociation (Fig. S6). The experimental analysis was conducted through model exchange reactions between equimolar amounts of compounds A and B at elevated temperatures (160, 170, and 180 °C) (Fig. 1d and Table S5). Product formation (C or D) was quantified through integration ratios of diagnostic peaks at δ 4.09 ppm and 4.10 ppm in ¹H NMR spectra31, or δ –112.99 ppm and –112.93 ppm in ¹⁹F NMR spectra. Kinetic analysis across different temperatures yielded an activation energy (Ea) of 90.1 kJ/mol, calculated using the Arrhenius equation (Fig. S7). We also performed in-situ variable-temperature Raman spectroscopy on the N-IDA boronates (Fig. S8a). The characteristic B–N stretching vibrations at 608 and 617 cm⁻¹ exhibited progressive attenuation with increasing temperature and were substantially reduced above 150 °C32, indicating significant B–N bond dissociation at elevated temperatures. This spectroscopic observation directly correlates with the enhanced exchange rates observed above 150 °C. The convergence of theoretical calculations (96.5 kJ/mol), experimental kinetics (90.1 kJ/mol), and direct spectroscopic evidence suggests that B–N bond temporary dissociation is likely the rate-determining step, followed by subsequent B–O bond exchange (Ea ~ 15.9 kJ/mol)8 to complete the dynamic exchange process.

Preparation of recyclable and malleable thermosets from commercial monomers and thermoplastics

Radical polymerization and crosslinking represent fundamental methods in polymer manufacturing and modification within the polymer industry33. The development of malleable and recyclable thermosets through radical methods, without changing current equipment or scaled production processes, has emerged as a significant research focus8. However, the inherent chemical instability of dynamic covalent bonds has posed persistent challenges in radical crosslinker synthesis34. Here, we demonstrate that the stable skeletal structures of N-IDA boronates allow for the efficient synthesis of crosslinkers containing a dynamic covalent bond through a robust dehydration reaction between methacrylate-functionalized iminodiacetic acid and boronic acid (Figs. S9–S11). Two crosslinkers were developed: BRA-T (containing two N-IDA boronate linkages) and BRA-O (containing one N-IDA boronate linkage). Both are white solid powders that do not impart additional color to the polymers. These crosslinkers enable single-step synthesis of malleable and recyclable crosslinked polymers (Fig. 2a), eliminating the traditional two-step process of modification and subsequent crosslinking, thereby enhancing industrial applicability.

Taking the synthesis of widely used poly(methyl methacrylate) as an example of radical polymerization methods, varying proportions of BRA-T crosslinker (5 wt%, 10 wt%, and 20 wt%) were incorporated into the reversible addition-fragmentation chain transfer (RAFT) polymerization of methyl methacrylate (MMA). The resulting PMMA-based thermosets (PMMA-5% BRA-T, PMMA-10% BRA-T, and PMMA-20% BRA-T) exhibited approximately 95% insoluble fractions, confirming successful crosslinking (Fig. S12 and Table S6). Dynamic mechanical analysis (DMA) revealed post-Tg storage modulus plateaus, further validating the crosslinked network formation (Fig. 2b). We calculated the molar mass between crosslinks (Mx) using the storage modulus at 180 °C35, and the Mx yielded values of 14.7 K, 12.2 K, and 10.7 K, respectively, indicating an increased crosslinking density with higher crosslinker content. Glass transition temperatures (Tg) showed corresponding increases due to the incorporation of conformationally rigid N-IDA boronate linkages (Fig. S13).

Stress relaxation tests characterized the dynamic covalent chemistry of N-IDA boronates in the PMMA samples. The relationship between characteristic relaxation times τ and temperature followed the Arrhenius equation. The Ea values for PMMA-5% BRA-T, PMMA-10% BRA-T, and PMMA-20% BRA-T were calculated as 130 kJ/mol, 120 kJ/mol, and 114 kJ/mol, respectively (Fig. 2c and S14). These bulk-state values are substantially higher than the solution-state Ea (90.1 kJ/mol), reflecting the restrictive environment of the crosslinked polymer matrix, which elevates the kinetic barrier for dynamic bond exchange36,37. The activation energy trend demonstrates the influence of crosslinking density on dynamic exchange properties, attributed to entropically driven acceleration of bond exchange, where higher crosslinking density correlates with lower Ea38.

Subsequently, we evaluated the reprocessing and recycling capabilities of the PMMA thermosets containing N-IDA boronates. After breaking PMMA-10% BRA-T samples into small pieces, PMMA-10% BRA-T-X1, PMMA-10% BRA-T-X2, and PMMA-10% BRA-T-X3 samples were successfully obtained through hot-pressing at 200 °C after one, two, and three cycles of destruction and reprocessing, respectively. Mechanical analysis revealed that PMMA-10% BRA-T-X3 samples retained 90% recovery of mechanical properties compared to the pristine samples (Fig. 2d–e). DSC measurements confirmed consistent Tg values between pristine and reprocessed samples (Fig. S16a), while Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy verified the preservation of chemical structure after three reprocessing cycles (Fig. S16b).

To enable the decrosslinking of PMMA thermosets, the dissociation of N-IDA boronate linkages needed to occur without affecting the ester moiety in PMMA. Conventional alkaline aqueous conditions proved unsuitable due to simultaneous hydrolysis of both ester and boronate bonds (Fig. S17a–b). We developed a selective approach using DEA as the cleaving agent. In the presence of three equivalents of DEA, MMA remained stable at 50 °C for 6 h, while a portion of 4-methylphenylboronic MIDA ester was converted to 4-methylphenylboronic DEA ester under these conditions (Fig. S17c). By optimizing the reaction temperature to 70 °C, an almost complete conversion was achieved, demonstrating the specific and efficient exchange reaction between DEA and N-IDA boronates (Fig. 2f and S17d). This selectivity is condition-dependent, with the DEA boronate ester being preferentially formed under the employed conditions (Fig. S17e). This method enabled efficient decrosslinking of PMMA thermosets, achieving 85% recovery of PMMA thermoplastics (Fig. 2g and S18a–c).

We further expanded our approach to commercial thermoplastics via radical crosslinking to directly obtain malleable and recyclable thermosets. To avoid microphase separation arising from polarity mismatch, we used the single N-IDA boronate–containing BRA-O crosslinker instead of BRA-T. Crosslinking proceeds via exogenous oxygen radicals that abstract hydrogen atoms to generate macromolecular carbon radicals, which then couple with the crosslinker (Fig. S20a). Using a peroxide initiator, we prepared PCL thermosets crosslinked with either BRA-O or the static crosslinker EDMA (Fig. S20b). PCL-BRA-O was fabricated by reactive extrusion using a micro-rheometer, yielding uniform white rod-like specimens (Fig. S20c–e)39. Compared with pristine PCL, both PCL-BRA-O and PCL-EDMA exhibited similar improvements in mechanical performance, with crosslinking increasing Tg by ~2–3 °C, as supported by mechanical tests (Fig. S21a–b), TGA/DSC (Fig. S21c–d), and FTIR (Fig. S21e). Notably, PCL-BRA-O showed robust recyclability in contrast to the permanent PCL-EDMA network: it tolerated multiple reprocessing cycles at 160 °C while retaining mechanical properties and chemical structure (Fig. S22a–f), and achieved 95% recovery efficiency after DEA treatment (PCL-BRA-O-X3). GPC and 1H NMR analyses indicated minimal changes in molecular weight and preserved chemical structure of the recovered PCL (Fig. S23a–b), with minor differences attributed to irreversible radical coupling and slight thermal degradation. This strategy was further extended to other thermoplastics, including PBAT (Fig. S24) and PLA (Fig. S25), demonstrating its generality for producing malleable and recyclable thermosets.

Stability characterizations of recyclable and malleable thermosets based on N-IDA boronate linkages

The crosslinked network architecture confers exceptional mechanical properties and long-term durability to thermosetting polymers. However, in malleable and recyclable thermosets, the incorporation of dynamic crosslinks may compromise network stability, particularly under elevated temperature and humidity conditions, where the resilience of dynamic crosslinks is severely tested, thus limiting their long-term applications. Herein, we systematically investigated the thermal, chemical, and dimensional stability of malleable and recyclable thermosets featuring N-IDA boronate crosslinks to evaluate their long-term performance characteristics.

PMMA thermosets containing N-IDA boronate crosslinks were first evaluated for thermal and chemical stability. TGA showed PMMA decomposition onset at ~270 °C (alkyl ester degradation), whereas the N-IDA boronate motif exhibited a higher decomposition temperature (>300 °C; Fig. S26), indicating preserved thermal stability. Chemical stability tests demonstrated that PMMA-10%BRA-T maintained mechanical properties and Tg after two weeks of water immersion (Fig. S27a–d). To directly compare N-IDA and DEA boronates within the same matrix, we synthesized PMMA thermosets based on DEA boronate crosslinkers via copolymerization (Figs. S28, S29, S30a) and confirmed similar compositions by FTIR (Fig. S30b). Acid resistance tests revealed striking differences: in 10 vol% TFA/CH3CN, PMMA thermosets based on N-IDA boronate crosslinkers remained stable for 24 h, while the DEA analog decomposed completely within 10 min (Fig. S30c). This enhanced stability arises from the electron-withdrawing carboxyl groups in IDA, which reduce nitrogen electron density and its susceptibility to protonation.

Rheological analysis of malleable PMMA-BRA-T (Fig. S31a) was performed to assess viscoelastic behavior above Tg, comparing with static PMMA-EDMA networks prepared via polymer crosslinking. Under varying temperatures (110–130 °C) and shear stresses (500–2000 Pa), PMMA-BRA-T exhibited creep behavior analogous to PMMA-EDMA over 100 min (Fig. S31b–c). This minimal creep, comparable to static networks, reflects the high activation energy for B–N bond dissociation, which effectively suppresses network reorganization at moderate temperatures and ensures dimensional stability with negligible dynamic exchange of N-IDA boronates under these conditions.

To fairly assess the impact of dynamic crosslinks on network stability, we synthesized thermosets with comparable crosslink densities using equivalent amounts of three crosslinkers (DOBL, EDMA, and BRA-O) through radical crosslinking of acrylonitrile-butadiene-styrene (ABS) engineering plastic, a widely used engineering plastic with established practical performance requirements (Fig. 3a, Fig. S32a, and Table S7). TGA analysis demonstrated that ABS-DOBL exhibited a 5% weight-loss temperature of 250 °C (Fig. 3b), significantly lower than ABS-EDMA (325 °C) and ABS-BRA-O (296 °C). Correspondingly, high-temperature mechanical analysis (Fig. 3c and S32b) revealed substantially inferior mechanical properties for ABS-DOBL compared to ABS-EDMA and ABS-BRA-O, demonstrating the exceptional thermal stability of BRA-O dynamic crosslinks. Creep analysis showed that ABS-EDMA and ABS-BRA-O exhibited strain variations approximately one-seventh that of ABS-DOBL (Fig. 3d), highlighting the significant advantage of N-IDA boronate in maintaining dimensional stability at elevated temperatures.

While these results demonstrate excellent thermal stability, practical applications often subject materials to complex environmental conditions combining high temperature with high humidity. To evaluate material reliability under such conditions, we conducted the industry-standard 85/85 test (85 °C and 85% RH). After 168 h of environmental conditioning, ABS-BRA-O samples maintained mechanical properties comparable to unconditioned specimens (Fig. 3e and S33). The excellent performance under harsh environmental conditions (85 °C/85% RH) demonstrates that the enhanced B–N coordination in the tridentate chelating structure effectively prevents hydrolysis of the boronates. These results comprehensively validate the advantages of N-IDA boronate crosslinks in achieving both thermal and chemical stability under demanding service conditions.

The advantages of high-temperature dynamic covalent chemistry based on N-IDA boronate in polymer materials are summarized in Fig. 3f. Thermosets incorporating N-IDA boronate crosslinks integrate the reprocessability and recyclability characteristics of dioxaborolane-crosslinked thermosets with performance metrics comparable to static-crosslinked thermosets, including thermal stability, hydrolysis resistance, mechanical properties, and creep resistance. Furthermore, the activation energy and exchange temperature of the N-IDA boronate chemistry exceed those reported for most dynamic covalent systems (Fig. 3g and Table S8). Overall, free radical crosslinkers featuring N-IDA boronate bonds effectively transform various monomers and thermoplastic materials into high-performance, malleable and recyclable thermosets suitable for long-term applications, offering a straightforward approach to reprocessing and upcycling commodity polymers for practical applications.

Exploring cross-linked polyethylene (XLPE) with high-temperature dynamic boronate chemistry for potential heat-shrinkable applications

The three-dimensional crosslinked network structure proves essential for the functionality of heat-shrinkable polyethylene tubes. The working principle involves heating crosslinked polyethylene above its melting point (Tstretch > Tm) for mechanical stretching to achieve expanded dimensions, followed by rapid cooling below the crystallization temperature (Tc) while maintaining the stretched state to fix the dimensions. Upon reheating above the melting point (Tshrink), the permanent crosslinked network structure enables the material to recover its original dimensions (Fig. 4a). This shape memory effect, combined with XLPE’s superior electrical insulation properties and mechanical strength, makes it a useful material for cable protection and insulation applications.

However, traditional XLPE suffers from a significant limitation: once crosslinked, it cannot be reprocessed or recycled. Dynamic covalent chemistry emerges as a potential solution to enable reprocessability of crosslinked polyethylene40,41. Nevertheless, its application in heat-shrinkable tubes faces a critical challenge: conventional dynamic covalent systems have exchange temperatures lower than the mechanical stretching and shrinkage temperatures required for XLPE processing. The temperature mismatch in conventional dynamic covalent systems not only complicates processing but also fundamentally limits the material’s practical applications, as network reorganization during processing leads to unpredictable and often inferior shape memory performance. The unique high-temperature dynamic covalent chemistry of N-IDA boronate may offer a potential solution to these challenges.

To validate this hypothesis, PE-EDMA and PE-BRA-O samples with comparable crosslinking densities were prepared via free radical crosslinking methods. DMA testing confirmed the existence of crosslinked network structures, while similar storage modules at elevated temperatures indicated comparable crosslinking densities (Fig. S34). To establish the temperature windows for processing, DSC analysis (Fig. 4b) determined the crystallization temperature (Tc ~ 100 °C) and melting point (Tm ~ 120 °C) of crosslinked polyethylene, with stretching and shrinkage temperatures typically 20–30 °C above the melting point (140–150 °C), all remaining below the dynamic exchange temperature of N-IDA boronate ester (Texchange > 150 °C). This sequential temperature arrangement (Texchange > Tstretch ≥ Tshrink > Tm > Tc) ensures that within the processing temperature range of heat-shrinkable tubes, N-IDA boronate ester maintains chemical stability, preserving the integrity of the crosslinked network structure. The high exchange temperature of N-IDA boronate creates a distinct processing window that allows for reprocessability at higher temperatures (>170 °C). This unique temperature-dependent behavior could be beneficial for practical industrial applications.

To experimentally validate the effectiveness of this temperature-dependent network structure, DMA comparative studies of PE-EDMA and PE-BRA-O samples were conducted to evaluate their shape memory capabilities. Both materials exhibited comparable shape fixity ratios (Rf) and shape recovery ratios (Rr) in the 20–150 °C temperature range (Fig. 4c), confirming that the incorporation of N-IDA boronate ester does not compromise the shape memory performance. Furthermore, the high-temperature dynamic characteristics enable PE-BRA-O reprocessing above 170 °C, with samples retaining consistent shape memory capabilities after three reprocessing cycles (Fig. 4d and S35a–b), highlighting the advantages of N-IDA boronate ester’s high-temperature dynamic covalent chemistry. During DMA testing, the uniform temperature ramping (10 °C/min) permitted molecular chain relaxation, resulting in reduced internal stress and sub-100% shape recovery ratios. Since industrial heat-shrinkable tube processing employs rapid heating and cooling via heat-transfer media, we conducted practical trials to simulate industrial conditions: PE-BRA-O samples underwent oil-bath pre-stretching, water-bath shape-fixing, and oil-bath heat-shrinking. Notably, the achieved shrinkage ratio exceeded 3:1, comparable to that of conventional XLPE heat-shrinkable tubes, demonstrating exceptional thermal stability and mechanical stretching properties of the dynamic N-IDA boronate crosslinked networks (Fig. 4e).

Beyond shape memory and reprocessability, electrical insulation properties represent another critical performance metric for heat-shrinkable materials. Polyethylene materials, comprising exclusively non-polar C–C and C–H bonds, inherently exhibit exceptional electrical insulation properties. To evaluate whether incorporating N-IDA boronate ester preserves these essential characteristics, comprehensive electrical characterization was conducted on PE and PE-BRA-O film samples, including dielectric constant, dielectric loss, breakdown voltage, and volume resistivity measurements. Measurements across multiple frequencies—power frequency (50/60 Hz), standard test frequency (1 kHz), and high frequency (1 MHz)—revealed nearly identical dielectric constants (2.2–2.3), with both materials exhibiting low dielectric losses (<0.01) (Fig. 4f and S36). These results confirm that the introduction of a low-mass-fraction BRA-O crosslinker maintains the original polarization characteristics of PE materials. Additionally, PE-BRA-O exhibits volume resistivity and breakdown strength comparable to those of pristine PE, indicating that incorporating the N-IDA boronate ester does not compromise polyethylene’s intrinsic insulation capability. After accelerated aging at 85 °C/85% RH for 168 h, both properties also remained comparable to those of pristine PE (Fig. 4g and S37; Table S9), providing preliminary evidence of stability under harsh conditions. These results demonstrate that through N-IDA boronate ester’s high-temperature dynamic covalent chemistry, we have successfully developed a reprocessable XLPE system that combines enhanced shape memory performance, efficient reprocessability, and uncompromised electrical insulation properties.

Beyond these experimental demonstrations, translating these findings into industrial practice is likely feasible. The synthesis of N-IDA boronate-based crosslinkers involves straightforward operations (nucleophilic substitution, esterification, deprotection, and condensation) without air-sensitive handling or specialized equipment, uses commercially available starting materials, and requires minimal purification (most intermediates need no purification). Moreover, the crosslinkers are introduced into polymers via conventional free-radical polymerization or crosslinking processes, which are fully compatible with existing industrial polymer manufacturing infrastructure. These attributes collectively position N-IDA boronate dynamic covalent chemistry as a promising platform for industrial implementation in high-performance recyclable thermosets.

Discussion

Our work on N-IDA boronate esters—featuring a molecular design with reinforced B–N coordination—significantly advances boronic ester dynamic covalent chemistry. By precisely engineering the bond strength via orbital hybridization and chelating effects, we achieved high-temperature exchange (>150 °C) while maintaining exceptional stability at service temperatures. The enhanced B–N coordination directly imparts superior chemical and dimensional stability to the resulting recyclable thermosets, which can be prepared from commercial monomers and thermoplastics using conventional radical processing techniques. The efficacy of this structural design is demonstrated in heat-shrinkable XLPEs, where the reinforced B–N coordination underpins both high-temperature performance and reprocessability. By extending the operational temperature range of boronic ester dynamic covalent chemistry, this work establishes a practical pathway toward high-performance recyclable thermosets for demanding applications requiring simultaneous stability and reprocessability.

Methods

Synthesis of malleable and recyclable thermosets based on N-IDA boronate linkages from commercial monomers

PMMA thermosets were used as an example. MMA (2.14 mL, 20.0 mmol, 1000 eq.), (1,4-phenylenebis(4,8-dioxo-1,3,6,2-dioxazaborocane-2,6-diyl))bis(hexane-6,1-diyl) bis(2-methylacrylate) (BRA-T, 0.105 g, 5 wt%; 0.222 g, 10 wt%; 0.500 g, 20 wt%), AIBN (0.984 mg, 0.006 mmol, 0.5000 eq.), 2-(dodecylthiocarbonothioylthio)-2-methylpropionic acid (7.28 mg, 0.02 mmol, 1.000 eq.) or 2-phenyl 2-propyl benzodithioate (5.44 mg, 0.02 mmol, 1.000 eq.) and anhydrous DMF (6.67 mL) were added into the flask (10 mL) in the glove box. The flask was sealed with a rubber plug and then immersed in an oil bath preheated to 65 °C. The polymerization was allowed to proceed until the mixture transitioned to a gel state. After the reaction was cooled to room temperature, an excess amount of tert-butyl methyl ether was added to wash the gel, and the gel was then dried at 70 °C for 12 h in a vacuum to remove residual solvent. After the previous treatments, the products were obtained as tough solids.

Synthesis of malleable and recyclable thermosets based on N-IDA boronate linkages from commercial thermoplastics

PCL thermosets were used as an example. Commercial PCL (4.00 g), tert-butylperoxy 2-ethylhexyl carbonate (TBEC, 30 μL, 0.70 wt%), and 4-(6-(6-(methacryloyloxy)hexyl)-4,8-dioxo-1,3,6,2-dioxazaborocan-2-yl)benzyl methacrylate (BRA-O, 0.200 g, 4.8 wt%) were added into a micro rheometer (MiniLab) at 100 °C for 30 min to obtain PCL extrusion. The PCL extrusion samples were hot-pressed at 150 °C for 15 min using a plate vulcanizer to obtain regular PCL samples containing N-IDA boronate linkages.

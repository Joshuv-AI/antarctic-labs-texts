n n -th Tropical Nevanlinna Theory
Risto Korhonen, Chengliang Tan

Abstract
In this paper, the tropical Nevanlinna theory is extended for piecewise polynomial continuous functions. By constructing the n n -th Poisson-Jensen formula, the n n -th tropical counting, proximity, and characteristic functions are introduced, which have some different properties compared to the classical tropical setting. Then, not only is the n n -th version of the second main theorem for tropical homogeneous polynomials obtained, but also a tropical second main theorem for ordinary Fermat type polynomials is acquired. Moreover, by estimating the tropical logarithmic derivative with a growth assumption pointwise, a strong equality is proved. This equality illustrates the relationship between ∑ i = 0 m N ⁡ ( r , 1 0 ⊘ f i ) \sum_{i=0}^{m}N(r,1_{0}\oslash f_{i}) and the ramification term N ⁡ ( r , C 0 ​ ( f 0 , ⋯ , f m ) ) N(r,C_{0}(f_{0},\cdots,f_{m})) , implying that there is no natural tropical truncated version of the second main theorem for shift operators.

Introduction

Nevanlinna theory plays a very important role in complex analysis, in which the logarithmic derivative lemma plays a key role in the proof of the second main theorem. These theories have many applications in complex differential equations. Halburd and the first author first introduced the analogy of the theory for the difference operator
[
11
]
, proved the difference version of the second main theorem
[
10

, obtained an analogue of the truncated second main theorem, and applied the theory to implement a complete classification of a class of second-order difference equations
[

, considering the existence of finite order solutions as a good analogue of the Painlevé property
[
1
]
. So far, a large number of studies on difference equations have been carried out
[
13
,
24
]
. Subsequently, Halburd, the first author and Tohge extended the theory to the complex projective space, proving the second fundamental theorem for hyperplanes, and also obtaining the difference version of the Picard theorem
[
15
]
.

Tropical mathematics operates within the tropical semiring, which consists of the real numbers (sometimes augmented with
-\infty
) equipped with the operations of maximum and summation, respectively. It has attracted a lot of research since the 1970s
[
3
]
and also has various applications such as statistical inference
[
28
]
, group theory
[
2
]
, supertropical forms
[

19
,
20
]
, and algebraic geometry
[
17
,

]
. Moreover, tropical functions with shift operators have attracted considerable interest
[
8
]
. In
[
21
]
, a method is proposed to demonstrate that the integrability of the ultra-discrete equation is equivalent to the singularity confinement property of the difference equation. In
[
9
]
, the detecting methods for integrability of ultra-discrete equations were discussed through singularity confinement.

In 2009, Halburd and Southall first introduced the tropical Nevanlinna theory
[
16
]
, and found that the existence of finite order max-plus meromorphic solutions is also a good detector of the integrability of the ultra-discrete equations. Building on this discovery, Laine and Tohge explored the tropical second main theorem and the tropical analogies of Valiron’s and Clunie’s Lemma
[
25
,
26
]
. Subsequently, the first author and Tohge generalized the second main theorem to tropical projective space
[
23
]
. In this paper, the authors discovered some apparent properties of the term
N(r,1_{0}\oslash C_{0}(f_{0},\cdots,f_{m}))
, intending to use this relationship as a breakthrough to obtain an analogue of the tropical truncated second main theorem. However, a corresponding proof cannot be provided at the moment. Similarly, Cao and Zheng proved the tropical second main theorem on tropical hypersurfaces and proposed two conjectures about the tropical truncated second main theorem based on the idea of the properties of the ramification term
[

[

They then found that the growth assumption can be discarded, which could not be done in the classical meromorphic function domain, and finally considered the tropical inverse problem.

The main purpose of this paper is to generalize the tropical Nevanlinna theory from piecewise linear functions to piecewise polynomial functions. The paper is structured as follows: In the following section, we consider the ultra-discretization of rational functions and propose the concept of
n
-th tropical meromorphic functions. In the third section, we firstly prove the Poisson-Jensen formula (Theorem

The Poisson–Jensen formula is a cornerstone of the construction of Nevanlinna theory. Before we give the formula, we need to do some preparation.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}

n
-th tropical meromorphic function on
[-r,r]
for some
r>0
. For any
x\in(-r,r)
, we denote two increasing sequences
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\nu}\subset(x,r)
and
\{x_{-i}\}_{i=1}^{\mu}\subset(-r,x)
which consist of all the points such that
f^{(j)}(x_{\pm i})
does not exist for some
j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
. Then we have

\displaystyle f(r)-f(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{f^{(j)}(x^{+})}{j!}(r-x)^{j}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\tau_{f}^{(j)}(x_{i})(r-x_{i})^{j}\right)

(16)

and

\displaystyle f(x)-f(-r)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left((-1)^{j+1}\frac{f^{(j)}(x^{-})}{j!}(r+x)^{j}+(-1)^{j}\sum_{i=1}^{\mu}\tau_{f}^{(j)}(x_{-i})(r+x_{-i})^{j}\right)

where
\tau_{f}^{(j)}(x)=\frac{f^{(j)}(x^{+})-f^{(j)}(x^{-})}{j!}
.

Proof.

We only prove (
16
) here, (
3.1
) can be proved similarly. Let
r=x_{\nu+1}
,
x=x_{0}
and denote

f(x)=f_{i}(x),\quad x\in[x_{i-1},x_{i}]

where
i=1,2,\cdots\nu+1
and
f_{i}(x)
are all polynomials of degree no more than
n
. Then we have
f^{(j)}(x_{i}^{+})=f_{i+1}^{(j)}(x_{i})
and
f^{(j)}(x_{i}^{-})=f_{i}^{(j)}(x_{i})
for all
i=1,\cdots,\nu
and
j=1,\cdots,n
. Since
f(x)
is continuous, hence
f_{i+1}(x_{i})=f_{i}(x_{i})
holds for all
i=1,\cdots,\nu
, then

\displaystyle f(r)-f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\nu}\left(f_{i+1}(x_{i+1})-f_{i+1}(x_{i})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}(x_{1})-f_{1}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\left(f_{i+1}(x_{i+1})-f_{i+1}(x_{i})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}(x_{1})-f_{1}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}[(f_{i+1}(r)-f_{i+1}(x_{i}))-(f_{i}(r)-f_{i}(x_{i}))]

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}[-(f_{i+1}(r)-f_{i+1}(x_{i+1}))+(f_{i}(r)-f_{i}(x_{i}))]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}(r)-f_{1}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}[(f_{i+1}(r)-f_{i+1}(x_{i}))-(f_{i}(r)-f_{i}(x_{i}))]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{f^{(j)}(x^{+})}{j!}(r-x)^{j}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\frac{f_{i+1}^{(j)}(x_{i})-f_{i}^{(j)}(x_{i})}{j!}(r-x_{i})^{j}\right).

In the third equality, we can define
A_{i}=f_{i}(r)-f_{i}(x_{i})
, then
\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}(-A_{i+1}+A_{i})=A_{1}
because of
A_{\nu+1}=0
. Hence we complete the proof of (
16
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

and

n
-th tropical meromorphic function,

\displaystyle\omega_{f}(x_{i})\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&x_{i}\in F_{1}\cup F_{2}\cup F_{3}\cup F_{6}\cup F_{8},\\

\displaystyle+\frac{f^{(j)}(x_{i}^{-})}{j!}\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&x_{i}\in F_{1}\cup F_{2}\cup F_{3}\cup F_{4}\cup F_{5},\\

\displaystyle\omega_{f}(x_{i})\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-1,&x_{i}\in F_{1},\\

(-1)^{j+1},&x_{i}\in F_{5}\cup F_{9}.\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{j+1}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}f^{(j)}(x_{i}^{+})-(-1)^{j+1}f^{(j)}(x_{i}^{-})+(-1)^{j+1}f^{(j)}(x_{i}^{-})-f^{(j)}(x_{i}^{-})}{j!}

\displaystyle(-1)^{j+1}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}f^{(j)}(x_{i}^{+})-(-1)^{j+1}f^{(j)}(x_{i}^{-})+(-1)^{j+1}f^{(j)}(x_{i}^{-})+f^{(j)}(x_{i}^{-})}{j!}

\displaystyle(-1)^{j+1}\frac{sgn^{j+1}(x_{i}^{+})f^{(j)}(x_{i}^{+})-sgn^{j+1}(x_{i}^{-})f^{(j)}(x_{i}^{-})}{j!}+(1-(-1)^{j})\frac{f^{(j)}(x_{i}^{-})}{j!}

\displaystyle(-1)^{j+1}\omega_{f}^{(j)}(x_{i})+(1-(-1)^{j})\frac{f^{(j)}(x_{i}^{-})}{j!}.

\displaystyle\frac{2c}{\rho-r-c}(m(\rho,f)+m(\rho,-f)+m(r,f)+m(r,-f))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{16c}{\rho-r-c}\left(T(\rho,f)+\frac{|f(0)|}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{32c}{(\alpha-1)(r+c)}\left(T(\alpha(r+c),f)+\frac{|f(0)|}{2}\right).

Theorem 5.8

(
Second main theorem for a homogeneous Fermat polynomial
)
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a
1
-st tropical holomorphic curve with a reduced representation
\textbf{f}=(f_{0},\cdots,f_{m})
and
\mathcal{P}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\alpha_{i}x_{i}^{n}
where
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha_{i}>0
,
i=0,\cdots,m
. If

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{r}{T_{f}(r)}=0,

(43)

then we have

\displaystyle\theta T_{f}^{n}(r)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}N^{(j)}\left(r,\frac{1_{0}}{\mathcal{P}\circ f}\oslash\right)+o(T_{f}^{n}(r))\leq\Theta T_{f}^{n}(r),

(44)

where
\theta=\min\limits_{0\leq i\leq m}\alpha_{i}
,
\Theta=2^{n-1}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\alpha_{i}
and
r
tends to infinity.

Proof.

For any linear function
g(x)=kx+b
, we can easily get

\displaystyle\frac{(g^{n}(x))^{(j)}}{j!}=C_{n}^{j}g^{n-j}(x)k^{j},

for all
j=0,\cdots,n
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
where we assume
0^{0}=1
. Thus we can obtain

\displaystyle\omega_{f_{i}^{n}}^{(j)}(x)=C_{n}^{j}f_{i}^{n-j}(x)\left(sgn^{j+1}(x^{+})(f_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(x^{+}))^{j}-sgn^{j+1}(x^{-})(f_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(x^{-}))^{j}\right),

(45)

where
i=0,\cdots,m
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, from which we can see that

\displaystyle\{\text{the singularities of }f_{i}^{n}\}\subset\{\text{the singularities of }f_{i}\}\cup\{0\}.

Since
f_{i}
is
1
-st tropical entire, then it must satisfy one of the following two properties.

(1) There exists a finite number
R_{i}>0
such that
f_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(-r^{-})<0
and
f_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(r^{+})>0
when
r>R_{i}
;

(2)
f_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(x^{\pm})\geq 0
or
\leq 0
holds for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

If
f_{i}
satisfies (1), then there exists a finite value
R_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}\geq R_{i}
such that
f_{i}(\pm r)>0
for
r>R_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}
, thus

\displaystyle M_{1,i}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\max\left\{\max\limits_{x\in[-R_{i}^{{}^{\prime}},R_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}]}\left\{|f_{i}(x)|\right\},1\right\}<+\infty,

\displaystyle M_{2,i}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\max\left\{\max\limits_{x\in[-R_{i}^{{}^{\prime}},R_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}]}\left\{|f_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(x^{\pm})|\right\},1\right\}<+\infty.

Then for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, if
|x|>R_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}
, we have
\omega_{f_{i}^{n}}^{(j)}(x)\geq 0
from (
45
), which implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}N_{(-r,-R_{i}^{{}^{\prime}})\cup(R_{i}^{{}^{\prime}},r)}^{(j)}(r,f_{i}^{n})=0.

(46)

If
|x|\leq R_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}
, then we have
|\omega_{f_{i}^{n}}^{(j)}(x)|\leq C_{n}^{j}M_{1,i}^{n-j}2M_{2,i}^{j}\leq 2n!(M_{1,i}M_{2,i})^{n}=:M_{i}<\infty
for all
j=1,\cdots,n
from (
45
). Suppose
\{x_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\nu_{i}}
are the singularities of
f_{i}
in
[-R_{i}^{{}^{\prime}},R_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}]
, and since there is no finite accumulation point of
f_{i}
’s singularities, it follows that
\nu_{i}<+\infty
. Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}N_{[-R_{i}^{{}^{\prime}},R_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}]}^{(j)}(r,f_{i}^{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu_{i}}|\omega_{f_{i}^{n}}^{(j)}(x_{k})|(r-|x_{k}|)^{j}

(47)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}n\nu_{i}M_{i}r^{n}=o(T_{f}^{n}(r)),

where we assume
r>\max\{R_{i}^{{}^{\prime}},1\}
. The last equality follows by the assumption (
43
).

If
f_{i}
satisfies (2), without loss of generality, we assume
f_{i}
is non-constant and
f_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(x^{\pm})\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. For a small enough quantity
\epsilon
, we denote

\displaystyle I_{i}=:\{x:f_{i}(x+\epsilon)f_{i}(x-\epsilon)<0,x\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Then there is at most one point in
I_{i}
. Let

\displaystyle x^{*}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x,&x\in I_{i},\\
0,&I_{i}=\varnothing.\end{array}\right.

Then
x^{*}
is a finite number. If
x>\max\{x^{*},0\}
, then
f_{i}(x)>0
, and hence
\omega_{f_{i}^{n}}^{(j)}(x)\geq 0
for
j=1,\cdots,0
, which means that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}N_{(\max\{x^{*},0\},r)}^{(j)}(r,f_{i}^{n})=0.

(49)

With the same calculation of (
47
), we can also get

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}N_{[\min\{x^{*},0\},\max\{x^{*},0\}]}^{(j)}(r,f_{i}^{n})=o(T_{f}^{n}(r)).

(50)

If
x<\min\{x^{*},0\}
, we assume
f_{i,x^{*-}}(x)=k_{i}^{*}x+b_{i}^{*}
where
k_{i}^{*},b_{i}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}
are finite values and
k_{i}^{*}\geq 0
. Since
f_{i}
is non-decreasing and convex, then
|f(x)-b_{i}^{*}|\leq|k_{i}^{*}x|
for
x<\min\{x^{*},0\}
, which leads to

\displaystyle|f_{i}(x)|\leq k_{i}^{*}|x|+O(1),

(51)

where
x<\min\{x^{*},0\}
. For any
r>|x^{*}|
and then
f(r)\geq 0
, we denote by
\{x_{-l}\}_{l=1}^{\mu_{i}}
the roots of
f_{i}
in
(-r,\min\{x^{*},0\})
and define a new
1
-st tropical entire function

\displaystyle g_{i}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}k_{i}^{*}x+b_{i}^{*},&x>\min\{x^{*},0\},\\
f_{i}(x),&x\leq\min\{x^{*},0\}.\end{array}\right.

Then
m(r,\pm g_{i})\leq k_{i}^{*}r+O(1)
and
\{x_{-l}\}_{l=1}^{\mu_{i}}
are all
g_{i}
’s roots in
(-r,r)
. By following the
1
-st Jensen formula, we have

\displaystyle N(r,1_{0}\oslash g_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{\mu_{i}}(f_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(x_{-l}^{+})-f_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(x_{-l}^{-}))(r-|x_{-l}|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(r,1_{0}\oslash g_{i})-m(r,1_{0}\oslash g_{i})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T(r,g_{i})+m(r,1_{0}\oslash g_{i})+O(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m(r,g_{i})+m(r,1_{0}\oslash g_{i})+O(1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2k_{i}^{*}r+O(1).

Therefore, from (
45
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}N_{(-r,\min\{x^{*},0\})}^{(j)}(r,f_{i}^{n})

(53)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{\mu_{i}}n!\left(k_{i}^{*}r+O(1)\right)^{n-j}\left((f_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(x_{-l}^{+}))^{j}-(f_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(x_{-l}^{-}))^{j}\right)(r-|x_{-l}|)^{j}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}n!\left(k_{i}^{*}r+O(1)\right)^{n-j}\sum_{l=1}^{\mu_{i}}\left((f_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(x_{-l}^{+})-f_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(x_{-l}^{-}))(r-|x_{-l}|)n^{2}(k^{*}r)^{j-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(n+2)!\left(k_{i}^{*}r+O(1)\right)^{n-1}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\mu_{i}}(f_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(x_{-l}^{+})-f_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(x_{-l}^{-}))(r-|x_{-l}|)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(n+2)!\left(k_{i}^{*}r+O(1)\right)^{n-1}\left(4k_{i}^{*}r+O(1)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4(n+3)!\left(k_{i}^{*}r+O(1)\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o(T_{f}^{n}(r))

where we used
0\leq f_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(x_{-l}^{\pm})\leq k^{*}
for
l=1,\cdots,\mu_{i}
and also used a basic inequality
a^{j}-b^{j}=(a-b)(\sum_{s=0}^{j-1}a^{s}b^{j-s-1})\leq n(a-b)a^{j-1}
for
0<b\leq a
and
j=1,\cdots,n
in the second inequality. We can also do a similar analysis in the case where
f_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(x^{\pm})\leq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

By combining equations (
46
), (
47
), (
49
), (
50
) and (
53
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}N^{(j)}(r,f_{i}^{n})=o(T_{f}^{n}(r)).

(54)

For any
n
-th tropical meromorphic functions
h_{1}
,
h_{2}
and any positive constant
\alpha
, we can easily get that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}N^{(j)}(r,h_{1}+h_{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}N^{(j)}(r,h_{1})+\sum_{j=1}^{n}N^{(j)}(r,h_{2})

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}N^{(j)}(r,\alpha h_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\sum_{j=1}^{n}N^{(j)}(r,h_{1}).

Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}N^{(j)}(r,\mathcal{P}\circ f)\leq\sum_{i=0}^{m}\alpha_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{n}N^{(j)}(r,f_{i}^{n})=o(T_{f}^{n}(r)).

Since
\mathcal{P}\circ f
is a
n
-th tropical meromorphic function, then by applying the
n
-th Jensen formula, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}N^{(j)}\left(r,\frac{1_{0}}{\mathcal{P}\circ f}\oslash\right)

(55)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m(r,\mathcal{P}\circ f)-m\left(r,\frac{1_{0}}{\mathcal{P}\circ f}\oslash\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}N^{(j)}\left(r,\mathcal{P}\circ f\right)+O(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\delta=\pm 1}\mathcal{P}\circ f(\delta r)+o(T_{f}^{n}(r)).

From (
51
), when
r
is sufficient large,
f_{i}
satisfies one of the following 3 properties

(1)
f_{i}(\pm r)\geq 0
;

(2)
f_{i}(r)\geq 0
,
f_{i}(-r)<0
and
|f_{i}(-r)|\leq kr+O(1)
where
k
is a finite positive constant;

(3)
f_{i}(-r)\geq 0
,
f_{i}(r)<0
and
|f_{i}(r)|\leq kr+O(1)
where
k
is a finite positive constant.

Let
F(x)=\max\limits_{0\leq i\leq m}\{f_{i}(x)\}
, then
F(\pm r)
can not both be negative simultaneously when
r
is large enough, because all
f_{i}
are tropical entire and
f
is not a constant holomorphic curve. Now we claim that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{F(r)+F(-r)}{2}\right)^{n}\leq\frac{F^{n}(r)+F^{n}(-r)}{2}+o(T_{f}^{n}(r))\leq\frac{(F(r)+F(-r))^{n}}{2}

(56)

as
r
tends to infinity.

We first suppose
F(\pm r)>0
for all large enough
r
. If
F(-\delta r)=o(F(\delta r))
for some
\delta=\pm 1
, then (
56
) becomes
(F(\delta r)/2)^{n}\leq F^{n}(\delta r)/2\leq F^{n}(\delta r)/2
which is valid. If
F(-\delta r)=aF(\delta r)(1+o(1))
for some constants
a\geq 0
and
\delta=\pm 1
, without loss of generality, we assume
a\geq 1
, otherwise we can consider
F(\delta r)=\frac{1}{a}F(-\delta r)(1+o(1))
. Then (
56
) can be rewritten as
\left(\frac{a+1}{2}\right)^{n}F^{n}(\delta r)\leq\frac{1+a^{n}}{2}F^{n}(\delta r)\leq\frac{(1+a)^{n}}{2}F^{n}(\delta r)
which is valid as well; the first inequality follows by considering the derivative of
\eta(t)=(1+(2t-1)^{n})/2-t^{n}
where
t=\frac{1+a}{2}\geq 1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

If
F(-\delta r)<0
for large enough
r
and
\delta=1
or
-1
, then from above properties of
f_{i}
, we know that
|F(-\delta r)|=o(T_{f}(r))
and
F(\delta r)=2T_{f}(r)+o(T_{f}(r))
, which implies
F(-\delta r)=o(F(\delta r))
. Then the claim follows by a similar analysis as above.

Hence, we complete the proof of the claim. By applying (
56
), it follows, on one hand, that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\delta=\pm 1}\mathcal{P}\circ f(\delta r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\delta=\pm 1}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\alpha_{i}f_{i}^{n}(\delta r)

(57)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\delta=\pm 1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m}\alpha_{i}\right)F^{n}(\delta r)+o(T_{f}^{n}(r))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{n-1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m}\alpha_{i}\right)\left(\frac{F(r)+F(-r)}{2}\right)^{n}+o(T_{f}^{n}(r))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta T_{f}^{n}(r)+o(T_{f}^{n}(r)),

and, on the other hand,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\delta=\pm 1}\mathcal{P}\circ f(\delta r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\delta=\pm 1}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\alpha_{i}f_{i}^{n}(\delta r)

(58)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\theta\sum_{\delta=\pm 1}F^{n}(\delta r)+o(T_{f}^{n}(r))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\theta\left(\frac{F(r)+F(-r)}{2}\right)^{n}+o(T_{f}^{n}(r))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta T_{f}^{n}(r)+o(T_{f}^{n}(r)).

Thus the assertion follows by combining (
55
), (
57
) and (
58
).
∎

Now we give several examples to show that the growth assumption (
43
) can not be dropped and the lower and upper bounds in (
44
) are both sharp.

Example 5.9
.

Let
f=[0:2x]
and
\mathcal{P}(x)=x_{0}^{2}+2x_{1}^{2}
. Then
T_{f}(r)=r
does not satisfy the growth assumption (
43
). Since
\mathcal{P}\circ f=8x^{2}
, then
\sum_{j=1}^{2}N^{(j)}(r,1_{0}\oslash(\mathcal{P}\circ f))=8r^{2}
does not satisfy the inequality (
44
) either.

Example 5.10
.

Let

\displaystyle f_{1}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&x\leq 1,\\
(2k-1)x-(2k^{2}-1),&x\in[2k-1,2k+1),\end{array}\right.

and

\displaystyle f_{2}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&x\leq 2,\\
2kx-2k(k+1),&x\in[2k,2k+2),\end{array}\right.

where
k\in\mathbb{N}.

(1) Let
g=[0:f_{1}(x)]
and
\mathcal{P}_{1}(x)=x_{0}+x_{1}
, then we can easily get that
T_{g}(r)=N(r,1_{0}\oslash(\mathcal{P}_{1}\circ g))=\frac{1}{4}r^{2}+o(r^{2})
, which shows the lower bound can be obtained.

(2) Let
h=[f_{1}(x):f_{2}(x)]
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}(x)=x_{0}+x_{1}
, then we can also get that
T_{h}(r)=\frac{1}{4}r^{2}+o(r^{2})
and
N(r,1_{0}\oslash(\mathcal{P}_{2}\circ h))=T(r,f_{1}+f_{2})+O(1)=m(r,f_{1}+f_{2})+O(1)=\frac{1}{2}r^{2}+o(r^{2})
, which shows the upper bound can be obtained.

6
A relationship about tropical truncated second main theorem

In
[
23
]
, the first author and Tohge considered an example of reduction in the term
\sum_{i=0}^{m}N(r,1_{0}\oslash f_{i})-N(r,1_{0}\oslash C_{0}(f_{0},\cdots,f_{m}))
where
f_{i}
are classical tropical entire functions, but provided no general statement of the phenomenon of reduction. Now we first explore the case of non-constant tropical holomorphic curve
f
with a reduced representation
\textbf{f}=(f_{0},\dots,f_{m})
satisfying the properties that all
f_{i}
have finitely many roots, and we will follow the classical notations
\omega_{f}(x)=\omega_{f}^{(1)}(x)
and
N(r,f)=N^{(1)}(r,f)
. For any fixed
i=0,\cdots,m
, we suppose
\{x_{i,t}\}_{t=1}^{\nu_{i}}
are all the roots of
f_{i}
, where
\nu_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
x_{i}^{*}:=\max\limits_{1\leq t\leq\nu_{i}}\{|x_{i,t}|\}
. Then there exist finite constants
b_{i}^{-},b_{i}^{+},k_{i}^{-},k_{i}^{+}\in\mathbb{R}
and
k_{i}^{-}\leq k_{i}^{+}
such that

\displaystyle f_{i}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}k_{i}^{-}x+b_{i}^{-},&x<-x_{i}^{*},\\
k_{i}^{+}x+b_{i}^{+},&x>x_{i}^{*}.\end{array}\right.

This yields
\sum_{t=1}^{\nu_{i}}\omega_{f_{i}}(x_{i,t})=k_{i}^{+}-k_{i}^{-}\geq 0
, and then by making use of the definition
N(r,f_{i})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{\nu_{i}}\omega_{f_{i}}(x_{i,t})(r-|x_{i,t}|)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{k_{i}^{+}-k_{i}^{-}}{2}r+O(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(r-|x_{i}^{*}|)\sum_{t=1}^{\nu_{i}}\omega_{f_{i}}(x_{i,t})\leq N(r,f_{i})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}r\sum_{t=1}^{\nu_{i}}\omega_{f_{i}}(x_{i,t})=\frac{k_{i}^{+}-k_{i}^{-}}{2}r,

as
r
tends to infinity. Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m}N(r,f_{i})=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m}\frac{k_{i}^{+}-k_{i}^{-}}{2}\right)r+O(1),

(62)

where
\sum_{i=0}^{m}\frac{k_{i}^{+}-k_{i}^{-}}{2}>0
because
f
is non-constant. Moreover, let
\{\pi_{0}(x),\cdots,\pi_{m}(x)\}
be a permutation of
\{0,\cdots,m\}
depending on
x
such that
C_{0}(f_{0},\cdots,f_{m})(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\overline{f}_{i}^{[\pi(i)(x)]}(x)
. Then there exist two finite constants
b^{-}
and
b^{+}
and a large enough
R>0
such that

\displaystyle C_{0}(f_{0},\cdots,f_{m})(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sum_{i=0}^{m}k_{i}^{-}x+b^{-},&x<-R,\\
\sum_{i=0}^{m}k_{i}^{+}x+b^{+},&x>R.\end{array}\right.

Then by following a similar analysis as with
N(r,f_{i})
above, we obtain

\displaystyle N(r,C_{0}(f_{0},\cdots,f_{m}))=\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{m}k_{i}^{+}-\sum_{i=0}^{m}k_{i}^{-}}{2}r+O(1),

(64)

as
r
tends to infinity. Thus,
N(r,C_{0}(f_{0},\cdots,f_{m}))=(\sum_{i=0}^{m}N(r,f_{i}))(1+o(1))
follows by combining (
62
) and
(\ref{6a222})
.

For more general cases (we mean
f_{i}
are allowed to have infinitely many roots), we give below a theorem to show the relationship with a growth assumption in the
n
-th setting, which is also the second part of the previous tropical second main theorem when
n=1
.

Before giving the relationship, let’s recall
[
23
, Lemma 5.1]
. We have the same conclusion in our setting and the proof is also exactly same, so we omit the details here.

Lemma 6.1
.

(
[
23
, Lemma 5.1]
) If
f_{0},\cdots,f_{m}
and
g
are tropical entire functions, then

\displaystyle C_{0}(f_{0}\otimes g,\cdots,f_{m}\otimes g)=g\otimes\overline{g}\cdots\otimes\overline{g}^{[m]}\otimes C_{0}(f_{0},\cdots,f_{m}).

We refer to
[
22
]
and
[
23
]
for more details about the tropical Casoratian.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
and
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}\mathbb{P}^{m}
be a
n
-th tropical holomorphic curve in
\mathbb{T}\mathbb{P}^{m}
with a reduced representation
\textbf{f}=(f_{0},\cdots,f_{m})
. If there exists a constant
l\in\{0,\cdots,m\}
such that
f_{i}\oslash f_{l}
is well defined for all
i=0,\cdots,m
,

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\log\log T_{f}(r)}{\log r}:=\rho_{2}<1,

(65)

and
\sigma\in(0,1-\rho_{2})
, then

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}N^{(j)}\left(r,\frac{1_{0}}{f_{i}}\oslash\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}N^{(j)}\left(r,\frac{1_{0}}{C_{0}(f_{0},\cdots,f_{m})}\oslash\right)\right|

(66)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|N^{(j)}(r,C_{0}(f_{0},\cdots,f_{m}))-\sum_{i=0}^{m}N^{(j)}(r,\overline{f}_{i}^{[i]})\right|

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\left|N_{[-2i,0]}^{(2j)}\left(r,\frac{1_{0}}{\overline{f}_{i}^{[i]}}\oslash\right)-N_{[-i,i]}^{(2j)}\left(r,\frac{1_{0}}{f_{i}}\oslash\right)\right|+o\left(\frac{T_{f}(r)}{r^{\sigma}}\right),

where
r
approaches infinity outside of a set of finite logarithmic measure.

Proof.

We first claim that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|N^{(j)}\left(r,\frac{1_{0}}{\overline{f}_{i}^{[k]}}\oslash\right)-N^{(j)}\left(r,\frac{1_{0}}{f_{i}}\oslash\right)\right|

(67)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\left|N_{[-2k,0]}^{(2j)}\left(r,\frac{1_{0}}{\overline{f}_{i}^{[k]}}\oslash\right)-N_{[-k,k]}^{(2j)}\left(r,\frac{1_{0}}{f_{i}}\oslash\right)\right|+o\left(\frac{T_{f}(r)}{r^{\sigma}}\right)

for all
j=1,\cdots,n
and
i,k=0,\cdots,m
as
r
tends to infinity outside of a set of finite logarithmic measure. For any fixed
i,j,k
and
r>2k
, we define the set of all
j
-th roots of
f_{i}
in
(-r-k,r+k)
as

\displaystyle\{x_{1,s}\}_{s=1}^{\mu_{1}}

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle[-k,k],

\displaystyle\{x_{2,s}\}_{s=1}^{\mu_{2}}

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle(-r+k,-k),

\displaystyle\{x_{3,s}\}_{s=1}^{\mu_{3}}

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle(k,r-k),

\displaystyle\{x_{4,s}\}_{s=1}^{\mu_{4}}

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle(-r,-r+k],

\displaystyle\{x_{5,s}\}_{s=1}^{\mu_{5}}

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle[r-k,r),

\displaystyle\{x_{6,s}\}_{s=1}^{\mu_{6}}

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle(-r-k,-r],

\displaystyle\{x_{7,s}\}_{s=1}^{\mu_{7}}

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle[r,r+k).

Since

Example 6.3

Semaphorin 3B-Membranous Nephropathy in Adults
Authors: Dory Arevalo Salazar, Jason D Theis, Robert L Perrizo, Fidler E Mary, Sanjeev Sethi
Published: 2026

Introduction

Membranous nephropathy (MN) is a common cause of nephrotic syndrome in adults. MN is characterized by accumulation of antigen-antibody immune complexes in the subepithelial region of the glomerular capillary walls.1, 2, 3 Numerous target antigens have been identified in MN, including phospholipase A2 receptor (PLA2R), thrombospondin type-1 domain-containing 7A (THSD7A), exostosin-1/exostosin-2 (EXT1/EXT2), neural epidermal growth factor-like 1 (NELL1), semaphorin 3B (SEMA3B), protocadherin 7 (PCDH7), neural cell adhesion molecule 1 (NCAM1), contactin-1 (CNTN1), protocadherin 1 (FAT1), neuron-derived neurotrophic factor (NDNF), and proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 6 (PCSK6). Of these antigens, SEMA3B is unique in that it is described mainly in pediatric patients with MN.5

Detection of MN antigens in the clinical laboratory at Mayo Clinic is now performed using laser microdissection and mass spectrometry (LMD/MS).4 The laboratory has been instrumental in understanding the demographics and incidence of various MN antigens. To our surprise, in recent testing, SEMA3B was detected in mostly adult MN. This report describes 7 adult patients with SEMA3B-associated MN, 2 of whom were included in the original report of discovery of SEMA3B.

The Mayo Clinic Tissue Proteomics Laboratory has performed LMD/MS for MN antigen detection in 450 PLA2R-negative MN biopsies since the test went live in 2024.4 We analyzed the LMD/MS data for SEMA3B in this group of MN biopsies. Key teaching points of this case are presented in Table 1.

Case Presentations

Case 1

The patient was a 61-year-old female with proteinuria. Urinalysis showed 3+ protein and +1 blood. Her protein-to-creatinine ratio was 4.8 g/g. Serologies were negative for antinuclear antibody (ANA), anti–double-stranded antibody (anti-dsDNA), antineutrophil cytoplasmic autoantibody (ANCA), and anti-PLA2R antibody. Serum creatinine (SCr) was 0.7 mg/dl.

Case 2

The patient was a 27-year-old female with a history of systemic lupus erythematosus and worsening kidney function. She developed nephrotic syndrome during pregnancy that was treated with steroids. SCr was 0.68 mg/dl, estimated glomerular filtration rate was 122 ml/min, and her serum albumin was 3.4 g/dl. Her urinalysis showed a protein-to-creatinine ratio of 1.2 g/g, 180 mg/dl protein, 3+ protein and 2+ blood (dipstick). Complement levels were within normal range. ANA was positive at 1:640, speckled pattern, and anti-dsDNA was negative. Serum anti-PLA2R antibody studies were negative.

Case 3

The patient was a 32-year-old male with nephrotic syndrome. SCr was 0.8 mg/dl. His urinalysis showed 3+ protein, 1+ blood, and a protein-to-creatinine ratio of 3.8. Serologies, including ANA, anti-dsDNA, ANCA, and serum anti-PLA2R antibody were negative.

Case 4

The patient was a 79-year-old female presenting with proteinuria. The patient had an IgG lambda monoclonal spike, hypothyroidism, and positive ANA and anti-SSA-antibodies. Her SCr was 0.85 mg/dl, estimated glomerular filtration rate was 70 ml/min, and serum albumin was 3.6 g/dl. Her urinalysis showed a protein-to-creatinine ratio of 1.92 mg/mg. Anti-PLA2R and anti-THSD7A serologies were negative.

Case 5

The patient was a 29-year-old female with a prior clinical history of 3 miscarriages, who developed lower extremity edema and proteinuria. SCr was 0.67 mg/dL, estimated glomerular filtration rate was >90 ml/min, and serum albumin was 2.4 g/dl. Urinalysis showed an albumin-to-creatinine ratio of 7 g/g. Serologies, including anti-dsDNA, ANA, ANCA, anti-GBM, hepatitis C virus, HIV, anti-PLA2R, and anti-THSD7A, were negative.

Case 6

The patient was a 41-year-old female runner, healthy otherwise, who developed lower extremity edema and proteinuria. SCr was 0.74 mg/dl, estimated glomerular filtration rate was > 60 ml/min, and serum albumin was 2.4 g/dl. Urinalysis showed proteinuria of 7.3 g/24 hours. Serologies, including anti-dsDNA, anti-GBM, complement levels, cryoglobulins, SPEP, HIV, hepatitis B virus, and hepatitis C virus, were negative or normal. Anti-PLA2R and anti-THSD7A serologies were negative.

Case 7

The patient was a 35-year-old female who developed proteinuria during an uncomplicated pregnancy, which persisted postpartum. Her SCr was 0.4 mg/dl, and serum albumin was 3 g/lL. Urinalysis showed 6.2 g protein and 1 to 2 red blood cells per high-power field. Serologies, including ANA, hepatitis B virus, and hepatitis C virus were negative.

Results

An MN antigen was detected in 235 of the 450 cases (52%). SEMA3B was identified as the target antigen in 6 of 235 cases (2.5%) in which an antigen was detected. Of the 6 cases, only 1 patient (0.4%) was in the pediatric age group. The remaining 5 SEMA3B-associated MN were adults (cases 1–5). Overall, SEMA3B was detected in 1.3 % of the 450 PLA2R-negative cases in which LMD/MS was performed for antigen detection.4 Cases 6 and 7 are from the discovery cohort.5

Clinical Findings

The mean age at the time of kidney biopsy was 43.4 years (SD, ± 19.4) and the patients were predominantly females (85.7%) (6 females and 1 male). All the patients presented with proteinuria; 5 of the 7 cases had nephrotic syndrome. Renal function was preserved in all 7 patients. Three of the 7 patients (42.8%) had a history of pregnancy and 1 patient had 3 miscarriages. Two of the 7 patients (28.5%) developed a malignancy on follow-up. Kidney Biopsy Findings

Kidney biopsy showed features of MN with thickened glomerular basement; 3 of the 7 cases showed basement membrane spikes and pin holes. Proliferative features were not present. Tubular atrophy and interstitial fibrosis were minimal in all cases. Immunofluorescence microscopy showed granular capillary wall staining for IgG and C3. No significant glomerular staining with C1q was seen, including those cases positive serology for ANA. One case showed kappa light chain restriction on routine immunofluorescence studies. However, pronase immunofluorescence in this case failed to confirm the monotypic nature of the deposits. IgG subclass staining was done in 5 of the 7 cases; the dominant IgG subclass was IgG1 in 4 of the 5 cases whereas IgG4 was the dominant subclass in 1 case. In 1 case, IgG4 was present in addition to IgG1. However, IgG4 staining was of lower intensity than IgG1. Tubular basement membrane staining for IgG1 was present in 1 case. Electron microscopy showed numerous subepithelial deposits; 6 of the 7 cases showed stage I and II deposits whereas 1 case showed stage III deposits. Subendothelial deposits, mesangial deposits, tubuloreticular inclusions in endothelial cells, and tubular basement membrane deposits were not present (including the case showing positive tubular basement staining by immunofluorescence).

LMD/MS

LMD/MS showed high total spectral counts for SEMA3B in all 7 cases (Figure 1). The counts ranged from 103 to 262, with an average total spectral count of 165.2 (SD, ± 69.9). Baseline spectral counts of PLA2R and THSD7A were present in 3 and 4 cases, respectively. High total spectral counts of IgG1 were present in all 7 cases.

Immunohistochemistry

Confirmation of LMD/MS was done by immunohistochemistry staining for SEMA3B in 5 cases that tissue was available (cases 2, 4, 5, 6, and 7). All the cases showed 3+ positive granular staining along the glomerular capillary walls. Tubular basement membranes were negative for SEMA3B. Follow-Up

These are recent cases and follow-up was available in only 1 patient (case 3) and in 1 patient of the discovery cohort.

Case 2

After the diagnosis of MN-SEMA3B-associated the patient was treated with angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors and has shown improvement. Her newborn baby is healthy till date and will undergo further testing.

Case 3

Following the diagnosis of MN-SEMA3B-associated, the patient was diagnosed with salivary gland tumor tumor, which was favored to be mucoepidermoid carcinoma.

Case 5

Currently, the patient has received 1 dose of rituximab, with no improvement. She is scheduled for the second dose. All her children are healthy, without signs of proteinuria.

Case 6

The patient was initially treated with diuretics and angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors; she was then enrolled in the “Mentor trial” and randomized to cyclosporine. She did poorly, with no remission until she was switched to rituximab 6 months later, achieving remission and without evidence of recurrence. Unfortunately, the patient died after a diagnosis of an aggressive mesothelioma that was treated with several rounds of chemotherapy, immunotherapy, surgeries, and cryoablation.

Discussion

MN is the most common cause of nephrotic syndrome in adults, whereas it is uncommon in the pediatric age group. One the antigen “markers” for MN in pediatric patients in SEMA3B.5, 6, 7 However, we have recently encountered increasing SEMA3B in adult patients with MN. SEMA3B-associated MN was confirmed by both LMD/MS and immunohistochemistry.

The SEMA3B-associated MN in adults appears to be unique in that the patients are mostly female (85.7%) and middle-aged with an average age of 43.4 years. In addition, 2 cases (28.5%) had a recent diagnosis of malignancy following the diagnosis of MN. One patient had SEMA3B in the setting of systemic lupus erythematosus. The biopsy findings in adult SEMA3B-associated MN were similar to pediatric SEMA3B-associated MN except that tubular deposits were uncommon in adult SEMA3B-associated MN, whereas they are common in children aged < 2 years with SEMA3B-associated MN. Three of the 7 cases had a history of pregnancy and/or miscarriages, raising the possibility of MN similar to that described with neutral endopeptidase,8 potentially mediated by transplacental passage of anti-semaphorin 3B antibodies. However, in both cases (cases 2 and 5), none of the full-term offspring have developed proteinuria to-date. Further evaluation is ongoing, and ≥ 1 child (case 2) is scheduled to undergo additional testing.

The study has limitations in that follow-up was not available for most of these recent cases of adult SEMA3B-associated MN. Nevertheless, the emerging recognition of this entity in adults highlights an important new trend that merits further study. A summary of teaching points is found in Table 1.

SEMA3B-associated MN is rare and can be detected in both pediatric and adult MN. Patients with adult SEMA3B-associated MN are more likely to be middle-aged females, and a subset of the adult female patients have recent history of pregnancy and/or miscarriages. A smaller subset developed malignancy on follow-up.

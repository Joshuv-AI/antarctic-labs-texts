Harmonic Morphisms and Minimal Conformal Foliations on Lie Groups
Sigmundur Gudmundsson, Thomas Jack Munn

Abstract
. Let G G be a Lie group equipped with a left-invariant Riemannian metric. Let K K be a semisimple and normal subgroup of G G generating a left-invariant conformal foliation ℱ \mathcal{F} on G G . We then show that the foliation ℱ \mathcal{F} is Riemannian and minimal. This means that locally the leaves of ℱ \mathcal{F} are fibres of a harmonic morphism. We also prove that if the metric restricted to K K is biinvariant then ℱ \mathcal{F} is totally geodesic.

53C35, 53C43, 58E20

1.
Introduction and Main Results

In differential geometry the study of minimal submanifolds of a given Riemannian ambient space
(M,g)
plays a central role. Harmonic morphisms
\phi:(M,g)\to(N,h)
between Riemannian manifolds are useful tools for the construction of minimal submanifolds of a given ambient space
M
.

Harmonic morphisms are the solutions to an over-determined, non-linear system of partial differential equations. For this reason they are difficult to find, and there exist 3-dimensional Riemannian Lie groups which do not allow for any solutions, not even locally, see
[
7
]
. For the general theory of harmonic morphisms between Riemannian manifolds we refer to the excellent book
[
2
]
and the regularly updated online bibliography
[
4
]
.

The relationship between minimal foliations and harmonic morphisms has been well studied. The following two results can be seen as the most important ones.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
11
]

A foliation on a Riemannian manifold, of codimension
n=2
, produces harmonic morphisms if and only if it is conformal and has minimal leaves.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
3
]

A homothetic foliation on a Riemannian manifold, of codimension
n\neq 2
, produces harmonic morphisms if and only if the mean curvature vector
\mu^{\mathcal{V}}
is of gradient type, or equivalently, if its dual
(\mu^{\mathcal{V}})^{\flat}
is closed.

Remark 1.3
.

It is then clear that if a homothetic foliation of codimension
n\neq 2
has minimal leaves then it produces harmonic morphisms. Lemma
4.1
shows that in the setting of this work, the converse holds too.

The case of conformal left-invariant foliations of Riemannian Lie groups by semisimple subgroups of codimension two is examined in
[
6
]
. The main results presented here are generalisations of those in
[
6
]
to arbitrary codimension. In fact if
K
is a codimension two semisimple subgroup of
G
that generates a left-invariant conformal foliation then
K
must be a normal subgroup of
G
, see
[
8
]
, this is not true for higher codimension cases and therefore we need to impose the additional condition that
K
be a
normal
subgroup of G.
The following example shows that we can not extend the results of
[
6
]
without this additional condition.

Example 1.4
.

Let
(G,g)
be a six-dimensional Riemannian Lie group such that the orthonormal basis
\{A,B,C,X,Y,Z\}
for its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
satisfies the following Lie bracket relations

[A,B]=2C,\quad[C,A]=2B,\quad[B,C]=2A,

\displaystyle[A,X]=-B+2Z,\quad[A,Y]=0,\quad[A,Z]=A+C-2X,

\displaystyle[B,X]=A-C,\quad[B,Y]=2Z,\quad[B,Z]=-2Y,

\displaystyle[C,X]=B+2Y,\quad[C,Y]=A+C-2X,\quad[C,Z]=0,

\displaystyle[X,Y]=\tfrac{1}{2}\,A-X,\quad[X,Z]=\tfrac{1}{2}\,C-X,\quad[Y,Z]=-\tfrac{1}{2}\,B.

Here the compact semisimple
K=\text{\bf SU}(2)
is equipped with a biinvariant Riemannian metric (Example
A.11
). It is
not normal
in
G
since its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
, generated by
A,B,C
is not an ideal in
\mathfrak{g}
. The left-invariant foliation
\mathcal{F}
generated by
K
is Riemannian but it is
not minimal
.

We now state the three most important results of this work.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(G,g)
be a Riemannian Lie group and
K
be a semisimple normal subgroup of
G
generating a left-invariant conformal foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
G
. If the restriction of the metric
g
to
K
is biinvariant then the foliation
\mathcal{F}
has totally geodesic leaves.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
(G,g)
be a Riemannian Lie group and
K
be a semisimple subgroup of
G
generating a left-invariant conformal foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
G
. If
K
is normal in
G
then
\mathcal{F}
is Riemannian with minimal leaves.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.6
we have the following result concerning harmonic morphisms.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
(G,g)
be a Riemannian Lie group and
K
be a semisimple normal subgroup of
G
generating a left-invariant foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
G
. Let
\phi:G\to G/K
be the natural projection onto the quotient group. If
G/K
is equipped with its unique Riemannian metric
h
such that
\phi:(G,g)\to(G/K,h)
is a Riemannian submersion then
\phi
is a harmonic morphism.

The additional assumption that
K
is normal is quite strong, but in the case when
K
is of codimension three, Proposition
6.3
shows that it is enough to require that the ambient group
G
is not perfect.

2.
Conformal Foliations

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold,
\mathcal{V}
be an integrable distribution on
M
and
\mathcal{H}
be its orthogonal complementary distribution. As customary, we will also denote by
\mathcal{V},\mathcal{H}
the orthogonal projections onto the corresponding subbundles of
TM
, and by
\mathcal{F}
the foliation tangent to
\mathcal{V}
. Then the
second fundamental form

B^{\mathcal{V}}
of
\mathcal{V}
is given by

B^{\mathcal{V}}(V,W)=\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\mathcal{H}(\nabla_{V}{W}+\nabla_{W}{V}),\ \ \text{ where }V,W\in\mathcal{V}.

The corresponding
second fundamental form

B^{\mathcal{H}}
of
\mathcal{H}
satisfies

B^{\mathcal{H}}(X,Y)=\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\mathcal{V}(\nabla_{X}{Y}+\nabla_{Y}{X}),\ \ \text{ where }X,Y\in\mathcal{H}.

The foliation
\mathcal{F}
tangent to
\mathcal{V}
is said to be
conformal
if there exists a vertical vector field
V\in\mathcal{V}
such that

B^{\mathcal{H}}=g\otimes V

and
\mathcal{F}
is said to be
Riemannian
if
V=0
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{F}
is
minimal
if
\trace B^{\mathcal{V}}=0
and
totally geodesic
if
B^{\mathcal{V}}=0
. This is equivalent to the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
being minimal and totally geodesic submanifolds of
M
, respectively.

The following is an immediate consequence of the above definition.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
2
]

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold,
\mathcal{V}
be an integrable distribution on
M
and
\mathcal{H}
be its orthogonal complementary distribution. Then the foliation
\mathcal{F}
tangent to
\mathcal{V}
is conformal if and only if

B^{\mathcal{H}}(X,X)=B^{\mathcal{H}}(Y,Y)\ \ \text{and}\ \ B^{\mathcal{H}}(X,Y)=0,

for any orthonormal horizontal vectors
X,Y\in\mathcal{H}
. If
\mathcal{F}
is conformal then it is Riemannian if and only if for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{H}
we have

B^{\mathcal{H}}(X,X)+B^{\mathcal{H}}(Y,Y)=0.

3.
Conformal Lie Foliations

Let
(G,g)
be an
m
-dimensional Riemannian Lie group with a subgroup
K
generating a left-invariant foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
G
, of codimension
n
, by left translations. Further let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
be an orthogonal decomposition of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
, where
\mathfrak{k}
is the Lie algebra of
K
. Let
\{V_{1},\dots,V_{d},X_{1}\dots,X_{n}\}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathfrak{g}
such that
V_{1},\dots,V_{d}
generate the subalgebra
\mathfrak{k}
. Then we can describe the Lie brackets of
\mathfrak{k}
by the following system

\ad_{V_{\alpha}}(V_{\beta})=[V_{\alpha},V_{\beta}]=\sum_{\gamma=1}^{d}c^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}V_{\gamma},

where the
c^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}
are real structure constants of
\mathfrak{k}
. The rest of the bracket relations for
\mathfrak{g}
satisfy

\ad_{X_{i}}(V_{\alpha})=[X_{i},V_{\alpha}]=\sum_{\gamma=1}^{d}x^{\gamma}_{i\alpha}V_{\gamma}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}y_{i\alpha}^{k}\,X_{k},

\ad_{X_{i}}(X_{j})=[X_{i},X_{j}]=\sum_{\gamma=1}^{d}\theta^{\gamma}_{ij}\,V_{\gamma}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\rho_{ij}^{k}\,X_{k},

for some real coefficients
x^{\gamma}_{i\alpha},y_{ij}^{k},\rho_{ij}^{k},\theta_{ij}^{\gamma}
.

The Koszul formula for the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
on
(G,g)
implies that the second fundamental forms
B^{\mathcal{V}}
and
B^{\mathcal{H}}
of the vertical and horizontal distributions, respectively, satisfy

B^{\mathcal{V}}(V_{\alpha},V_{\beta})=\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{n}\big((g([X_{k},V_{\alpha}],V_{\beta})+g([X_{k},V_{\beta}],V_{\alpha})\big)\cdot X_{k},

B^{\mathcal{H}}(X_{i},X_{j})=\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\sum_{\gamma=1}^{d}\big(g([V_{\gamma},X_{i}],X_{j})+g([V_{\gamma},X_{j}],X_{i})\big)\cdot V_{\gamma}.

Remark 3.1
.

We now fix the notation for the examples given throughout this work. We denote vertical basis vectors by
A,B,C
and horizontal basis vectors by
X,Y,Z,W
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
(G,g)
be the four-dimensional solvable Riemannian Lie group such that the orthonormal basis
\{A,B,X,Y\}
for its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
satisfies the following bracket relations

[A,B]=-2A,

[B,X]=A+X+Y,\quad[B,Y]=-X+Y,\quad[X,Y]=A.

The subgroup
K=H^{2}
is solvable (Example
A.2
). The left-invariant foliation
\mathcal{F}
generated by
K
is conformal but not Riemannian. The horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
is not integrable.

The following result provides us with a sufficient condition for a conformal foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
G
to be Riemannian. It generalises Proposition 3.3 of
[
5
]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(G,g)
be a Riemannian Lie group with a subgroup
K
generating a left-invariant conformal foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
G
. If
K
is semisimple then the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is Riemannian.

Remark 3.4
.

Note that Riemannian foliations are homothetic so Theorem
1.2
can be applied.

Proof of Proposition
3.3
.

Since
\mathcal{F}
is a conformal foliation, we have that for
V\in\mathcal{V}
, the adjoint action on the horizontal space
\mathcal{H}
is conformal i.e. for any
X,Y\in\mathcal{H}
we have

\rho(V)\cdot g(X,Y)=g([V,X],Y)+g([V,Y],X).

Notice that for a fixed horizontal unit vector
X_{i}
we have that

\rho(V)=2\,g([V,X_{i}],X_{i}).

Then by the linearity of the metric, the Lie bracket relations and the fact that
[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}]=\mathcal{V}
, as
K
is semisimple, we see that
\rho
vanishes if and only if for all
1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq d
we have

g([[V_{\alpha},V_{\beta}],X_{i}],X_{i})=0.

We now show that this is the case

\displaystyle g([[V_{\alpha},V_{\beta}],X_{i}],X_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g([[X_{i},V_{\alpha}],V_{\beta}]+[[V_{\beta},X_{i}],V_{\alpha}],X_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g([\sum_{k=1}^{n}y_{i\alpha}^{k}\,X_{k}+\sum_{\gamma=1}^{d}x_{i\alpha}^{\gamma}\,V_{\gamma},V_{\beta}]-[\sum_{k=1}^{n}y_{i\beta}^{k}\,X_{k}+\sum_{\gamma=1}^{d}x_{i\beta}^{\gamma}\,V_{\gamma},V_{\alpha}],X_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{n}(y_{i\alpha}^{k}\cdot y_{k\beta}^{i}-y_{i\beta}^{k}\cdot y_{k\alpha}^{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{k\neq i}(y_{i\alpha}^{k}\cdot y_{k\beta}^{i}-y_{i\beta}^{k}\cdot y_{k\alpha}^{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{k\neq i}(y_{i\alpha}^{k}\cdot y_{k\beta}^{i}-y_{k\beta}^{i}\cdot y_{i\alpha}^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

The last step follows from the fact that the conformality implies that if
k\neq i
then

y_{i\alpha}^{k}+y_{k\alpha}^{i}=g([V_{\alpha},X_{i}],X_{k})+g([V_{\alpha},X_{k}],X_{i})=\rho(V_{\alpha})\cdot g(X_{i},X_{k})=0.

∎

If we further assume that the semisimple subgroup
K
is normal we can define the homogeneous quotient space
G/K
.

Remark 3.5
.

If the foliation
\mathcal{F}
on the Lie group
G
is Riemannian then there exists a unique Riemannian metric
h
on the homogeneous quotient space
G/K
such that the natural projection
\pi:(G,g)\to(G/K,h)
is a Riemannian submersion. The horizontal vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are basic and we denote by
\bar{X}_{1},\dots\bar{X}_{n}
the vector fields
d\pi(X_{1}),\dots,d\pi(X_{n})
forming a global orthonormal frame of the tangent bundle of the quotient space
G/K
. According to Lemma 1 on page 460 of O’Neill’s fundamental work
[
10
]
, we have

\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{X}_{i}}{\bar{X}_{j}}=d\pi(\nabla_{X_{i}}{X_{j}}).

Here
\nabla
and
\bar{\nabla}
are the Levi-Civita connections of
(G,g)
and
(G/K,h)
, respectively.
This relation is useful for determining the geometry of the Riemannian homogeneous quotient space
(G/K,h)
.

4.
Minimality and Total Geodecity

Let
(G,g)
be an
m
-dimensional Riemannian Lie group with a semisimple subgroup
K
, generating a left-invariant conformal foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
G
, of codimension
n
. Further let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
be an orthogonal decomposition of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
, where
\mathfrak{k}
is the Lie algebra of
K
. Let
\{V_{1},\dots,V_{d},X_{1}\dots,X_{n}\}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathfrak{g}
such that
V_{1},\dots,V_{d}
generate the subalgebra
\mathfrak{k}
. Then we can describe the Lie brackets of
\mathfrak{k}
by the following system

\ad_{X_{i}}(V_{\alpha})=[X_{i},V_{\alpha}]=\sum_{\gamma=1}^{d}x^{\gamma}_{i\alpha}V_{\gamma}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}y_{i\alpha}^{k}\,X_{k},

\ad_{X_{i}}(X_{j})=[X_{i},X_{j}]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\rho_{ij}^{k}\,X_{k}+\sum_{\gamma=1}^{d}\theta^{\gamma}_{ij}\,V_{\gamma},

for some real coefficients
x^{\gamma}_{i\alpha},y_{i\alpha}^{k},\rho_{ij}^{k},\theta_{ij}^{\gamma}
.

The structure constants of
\mathfrak{g}
can be used to describe when the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is minimal or even totally geodesic. First we notice that for the second fundamental form
B^{\mathcal{V}}
of
\mathcal{V}
we have

\displaystyle 2\cdot B^{\mathcal{V}}(V_{\alpha},V_{\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\big(g([X_{k},V_{\alpha}],V_{\beta})+g([X_{k},V_{\beta}],V_{\alpha})\big)\cdot X_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{\gamma=1}^{d}\big(g(x_{k\alpha}^{\gamma}V_{\gamma},V_{\beta})+g(x_{k\beta}^{\gamma}V_{\gamma},V_{\alpha})\big)\cdot X_{k}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(g(y_{k\alpha}^{i}X_{i},V_{\beta})+g(y_{k\beta}^{i}X_{i},V_{\alpha})\big)\cdot X_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}(x_{k\alpha}^{\beta}+x_{k\beta}^{\alpha})\cdot X_{k}.

From this we see that the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is
minimal
if and only if for
1\leq i\leq n
we have

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}\,x_{i\alpha}^{\alpha}=0.

Furthermore, we observe that
\mathcal{F}
is
totally geodesic
if and only if for all
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq d
we have

x_{i\alpha}^{\beta}+x_{i\beta}^{\alpha}=0.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(G,g)
be a Riemannian Lie group and
K
be a semisimple subgroup of
G
generating a left-invariant conformal foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
G
. Then
\mathcal{F}
produces harmonic morphisms if and only if it has minimal leaves.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.3

\mathcal{F}
is Riemannian and thus homothetic.
Since
\mathcal{F}
is homothetic by Theorem
1.2
it produces harmonic morphisms if and only if
d(\mu^{\mathcal{V}})^{\flat}=0
i.e.

\displaystyle d(\mu^{\mathcal{V}})^{\flat}(E,F)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{E}((\mu^{\mathcal{V}})^{\flat}(F))-d_{F}((\mu^{\mathcal{V}})^{\flat}(E))-(\mu^{\mathcal{V}})^{\flat}([E,F])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(\langle\mu^{\mathcal{V}},F\rangle)-F(\langle\mu^{\mathcal{V}},E\rangle)-\langle\mu^{\mathcal{V}},[E,F]\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

for all left-invariant vector fields
E,F\in\mathfrak{g}
. In particular, since the mean curvature vector

\mu^{\mathcal{V}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}x_{i\alpha}^{\alpha}X_{i}

is left-invariant
\mathcal{F}
produces harmonic morphisms if and only if

\langle\mu^{\mathcal{V}},[E,F]\rangle=0,

for all
E,F\in\mathfrak{g}
.

We now suppose that
\mu^{\mathcal{V}}\neq 0
but
\mu^{\mathcal{V}}\perp[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
and will obtain a contradiction.

First choose an orthonormal basis
\{\hat{X}_{1},\dots\hat{X}_{n}\}
such that
\hat{X}_{1}
is the normalised vector
\mu^{\mathcal{V}}
i.e.

\mu^{\mathcal{V}}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}x_{1\alpha}^{\alpha}\hat{X}_{1}.

Now we recall the Levi decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathfrak{r}
,
where
\mathfrak{s}
is semisimple and
\mathfrak{r}
is the radical of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
\mathfrak{k}\subset\mathfrak{s}
because
\mathfrak{k}\cap\mathfrak{r}
is a solvable ideal of
\mathfrak{k}
(since
\mathfrak{r}
is a radical), but since
\mathfrak{k}
is semisimple, the only such ideal is the trivial one
\{0\}
.

Furthermore
\mu^{\mathcal{V}}\in\mathfrak{r}
since

\mu^{\mathcal{V}}\perp[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\supset[\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{s}]=\mathfrak{s}.

So
[\mu^{\mathcal{V}},\mathcal{V}]\subset\mathfrak{r}
, in particular

\mathcal{V}[\mu^{\mathcal{V}},\mathcal{V}]=0

and thus

\mu^{\mathcal{V}}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}\big(g([\hat{X}_{1},V_{\alpha}],V_{\alpha})\big)\cdot\hat{X}_{1}=0.

∎

5.
The Proofs of our Main Results

This section begins by describing standard left-invariant Riemannian metrics on Lie groups.
For the details from Lie theory, we refer to the standard work
[
9
]
of S. Helgason.

Let
K
be a real semisimple Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
. Let
B:\mathfrak{k}\times\mathfrak{k}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be its symmetric, bilinear and non-degenerate Killing form with

B(V,W)=\trace(\ad_{V}\circ\ad_{W}).

Let
\tilde{K}
be the maximal compact subgroup of
K
with Lie algebra
\tilde{\mathfrak{k}}
. Then we have an orthogonal decomposition
\mathfrak{k}=\tilde{\mathfrak{k}}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathfrak{k}
with respect to the Killing form
B
, which is negative definite on
\tilde{\mathfrak{k}}
and positive definite on
\mathfrak{p}
.

In the following proofs we assume that
K
is a
normal
subgroup of
G
, ensuring that
\mathfrak{k}
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
i.e. the structure constants
y_{i\alpha}^{k}=0
for all
i,k
and
\alpha
. So we may write

\ad_{X_{i}}(V_{\alpha})=[X_{i},V_{\alpha}]=\sum_{\gamma=1}^{d}x^{\gamma}_{i\alpha}V_{\gamma}.

We impose this condition because in general
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{H}
may not be orthogonal with respect to the Killing form, so the arguments in the proofs of Theorem
1.5
and Proposition would fail without it.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

We write the decomposition of the semisimple subgroup
K
into simple factors

K=K_{1}\times\cdots\times K_{r}

in terms of Lie algebras we have that
\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{k}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathfrak{k}_{r}
, where each
\mathfrak{k_{t}}
is a
simple
Lie ideal of
\mathfrak{k}
. Since
\mathfrak{k}
is a semisimple, every derivation of
\mathfrak{k}
will be an inner derivation, in particular since
\mathfrak{k}
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
, we have that
ad_{g}:\mathfrak{k}\to\mathfrak{k}
must be an inner derivation, i.e. for
g\in\mathfrak{g}
there exists
k_{0}\in\mathfrak{k}
such that

[g,\mathfrak{k}]=[k_{0},\mathfrak{k}].

Thus it follows that each
\mathfrak{k}_{t}
will also be an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
since

[g,\mathfrak{k}_{t}]=[k_{0},\mathfrak{k}_{t}]\subset\mathfrak{k}_{t}

so when using an orthonormal basis that is compatible with the decomposition of
\mathfrak{k}
we have that
x_{i\beta}^{\alpha}=0
for any
V_{\alpha},V_{\beta}
in different simple components
\mathfrak{k}_{t},\mathfrak{k}_{s}
respectively. It remains to consider the case when
V_{\alpha},V_{\beta}
are contained in the same
\mathfrak{k}_{t}
.

Since the restriction of the left-invariant Riemannian metric
g
on
G
to
K
is biinvariant it is a negative multiple of the Killing form
B:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to{\mathbb{R}}
on each simple component
K_{t}
of
K
i.e. for some
\Lambda_{1},\dots\Lambda_{r}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
we have

g|_{\mathfrak{k}_{t}\times\mathfrak{k}_{t}}=-\Lambda_{t}\cdot B|_{\mathfrak{k}_{t}\times\mathfrak{k}_{t}}.

Further, let
\{V_{1},\dots V_{d}\}
be an orthonormal basis for the semisimple subalgebra
\mathfrak{k}
of
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
g
i.e.

g(V_{\alpha},V_{\beta})=-\Lambda_{t}\cdot B(V_{\alpha},V_{\beta})=\delta_{\alpha\beta},

for all
1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n
.
Then

\displaystyle B([V_{\alpha},V_{\beta}],X_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(V_{\alpha},[V_{\beta},X_{i}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-B(V_{\alpha},\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{i\beta}^{\gamma}V_{\gamma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}\cdot x_{i\beta}^{\alpha}.

Since
B([V_{\alpha},V_{\beta}],X_{i})+B([V_{\beta},V_{\alpha}],X_{i})=0
, the above steps show that for all
1\leq i\leq n
we obtain

\Lambda_{t}\cdot(x_{i\alpha}^{\beta}+x_{i\beta}^{\alpha})=0.

This implies that
\mathcal{F}
is totally geodesic. ∎

Example 5.1
.

Let
(G,g)
be a six-dimensional Riemannian Lie group such that the orthonormal basis
\{A,B,C,X,Y,Z\}
for its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
satisfies the following Lie bracket relations

[A,B]=2C,\quad[C,A]=2B,\quad[B,C]=2A,

\displaystyle[A,X]=-B,\quad[A,Y]=-B,\quad[A,Z]=-B,

\displaystyle[B,X]=A,\quad[B,Y]=A-2C,\quad[B,Z]=A-2C,

\displaystyle[C,X]=0,\quad[C,Y]=2B,\quad[C,Z]=2B,

\displaystyle[X,Y]=B,\quad[X,Z]=B,\quad[Y,Z]=0.

Here the compact semisimple
K=\text{\bf SU}(2)
is equipped with a biinvariant Riemannian metric (Example
A.11
). It is normal in
G
since its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
, generated by
A,B,C
is an ideal in
\mathfrak{g}
. The radical
\text{rad}(\mathfrak{g})
is three dimensional so
G
is not semisimple. The left-invariant foliation
\mathcal{F}
generated by
K
is Riemannian and totally geodesic. The horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
is not integrable. Employing the ideas of Remark
3.5
an easy calculations shows that the quotient group
(G/K,h)
is the flat abelian
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
(Example
A.3
).

The proof of Theorem
1.6
is rather straightforward, the first step is to show, in Proposition
5.2
, that there exists a Riemannian metric on any semisimple
K
such that
\mathcal{F}
is minimal.

Let
\theta:\mathfrak{k}\to\mathfrak{k}
be a
Cartan involution
on the semisimple subalgebra
\mathfrak{k}
of
\mathfrak{g}
i.e. an involutive automorphism, such that the bilinear form
g_{K}:\mathfrak{k}\times\mathfrak{k}\to{\mathbb{R}}
with

g_{K}:(V,W)\mapsto-B(V,\theta(W))

is positive definite on
\mathfrak{k}
. Such an involution is unique up to inner automorphisms, see p.185 of
[
9
]
for details. This induces the
Cartan-Killing metric
on
K
which we also denote by
g_{K}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(G,g)
be a Riemannian Lie group and
K
be a semisimple normal subgroup of
G
generating a left-invariant conformal foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
G
. If the metric
g
restricted to
K
is the Cartan-Killing metric
g_{K}
then the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is minimal.

Proof.

Since
g|_{K}
is the Cartan-Killing metric it follows that
B(V_{\alpha},V_{\beta})=\delta_{\alpha\beta}\theta_{\alpha}
for an orthonormal basis
\{V_{1},\dots V_{d}\}
of
\mathfrak{k}
. Here we denote
\theta(V_{\alpha})
by
\theta_{\alpha}
. Then for any horizontal basis vector
X_{i}
we have

\displaystyle B([V_{\alpha},V_{\beta}],X_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(V_{\alpha},[V_{\beta},X_{i}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-B(V_{\alpha},\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{i\beta}^{\gamma}V_{\gamma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{\alpha}\,x_{i\beta}^{\alpha}.

Since
B([V_{\alpha},V_{\beta}],X_{i})=-B([V_{\beta},V_{\alpha}],X_{i})
, the above steps show that

\theta_{\alpha}\,x_{i\beta}^{\alpha}=-\theta_{\beta}\,x_{i\alpha}^{\beta}.

Then for
\alpha=\beta
we get that
x_{i\alpha}^{\alpha}=-x_{i\alpha}^{\alpha}=0
. Thus the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is minimal. ∎

Example 5.3
.

Let
(G,g)
be a six-dimensional Riemannian Lie group such that the orthonormal basis
\{A,B,C,X,Y,Z\}
for its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
satisfies the following Lie bracket relations

[A,B]=2C,\quad[C,A]=2B,\quad[B,C]=-2A,

\displaystyle[A,X]=B+C,\quad[A,Y]=B+C,\quad[A,Z]=B+C,

\displaystyle[B,X]=A-2C,\quad[B,Y]=A-4C,\quad[B,Z]=A-2C,

\displaystyle[C,X]=A+2B,\quad[C,Y]=A+4B,\quad[C,X]=A+2B,

\displaystyle[X,Y]=-2A-2B+2Z,\quad[X,Z]=4A+B-C-2Y,

\displaystyle[Y,Z]=-2A+2C+2X.

Here the non-compact semisimple
K=\text{\bf SL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
(Example
A.10
) is equipped with the Cartan-Killing metric. It is normal in
G
since its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
, generated by
A,B,C
is an ideal in
\mathfrak{g}
.
The radical
\text{rad}(\mathfrak{g})
vanishes so
G
is semisimple. The left-invariant foliation
\mathcal{F}
generated by
K
is Riemannian and minimal but not totally geodesic. The horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
is not integrable. The quotient group
G/K
is the compact semisimple
\text{\bf SU}(2)
. Using Remark
3.5
it is easy to show that the Riemannian Lie group
(G/K,h)
has constant sectional curvature
+1
.

We are now ready to prove our main result stated in Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Let
\{V_{1},...,V_{d},X_{1},...,X_{n}\}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the metric
g
such that
V_{1},...,V_{n}
generate the subalgebra
\mathfrak{k}
.
Then notice that

\trace(\ad_{X_{i}})=\sum_{\gamma=1}^{d}x_{i\gamma}^{\gamma}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}g(\ad_{X_{i}}(X_{k}),X_{k}).

Now equip
G
with an additional left-invariant metric
\hat{g}
, which we can fully describe by its action on
\mathfrak{g}
.
First define
\hat{g}|_{\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}}
to be the Cartan-Killing metric on
K
and then let

\hat{g}|_{\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}}=g|_{\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}},\ \ \hat{g}|_{\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{H}}=g|_{\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{H}}.

Then we can use the Gram-Schmidt process to obtain an orthonormal basis
\{W_{1},...,W_{d},X_{1},...X_{n}\}
with respect to the metric
\hat{g}
. Since both metrics are left-invariant changing the metric simply amounts to a change of basis. Then it follows from Proposition
5.2
that the structure constants with respect to the new metric
\hat{g}
,

\hat{x}_{i\alpha}^{\alpha}=\hat{g}(W_{\alpha},[X_{i},W_{\alpha}]),

are all equal to zero. Then since the trace of an operator is invariant under the change of basis, and the fact that each
X_{i}
is unchanged by the above Gram-Schmidt process, we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}x_{i\alpha}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\trace(\ad_{X_{i}})-\sum_{j=1}^{n}g(\ad_{X_{i}}(X_{j}),X_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\trace(\ad_{X_{i}})-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\hat{g}(\ad_{X_{i}}(X_{j}),X_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}\hat{x}_{i\alpha}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Since this is true for all
1\leq i\leq n
it follows that
\mathcal{F}
is minimal.
∎

The following example is interesting in connection with Theorem
1.6
. The group
K
is normal but its ambient group
G_{\alpha}
is not semisimple.

Example 5.4
.

For
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
, let
(G_{\alpha},g_{\alpha})
be a six-dimensional Riemannian Lie group such that the orthonormal basis
\{A,B,C,X,Y,Z\}
for its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
satisfies the following Lie bracket relations

[A,B]=C,\quad[C,A]=B,\quad[B,C]=4A,

\displaystyle[A,X]=B+C,\quad[A,Y]=0,\quad[A,Z]=B+C,

\displaystyle[B,X]=-4A-C,\quad[B,Y]=-C,\quad[B,Z]=-4A-C,

\displaystyle[C,X]=-4A+B,\quad[C,Y]=B,\quad[C,Z]=-4A+B,

\displaystyle[X,Y]=-B-C,\quad[Y,Z]=-A+B+C+Y,

\displaystyle[X,Z]=\alpha A+\alpha B-\alpha\,C-\alpha X.

The semisimple subgroup
K
is normal in
G_{\alpha}
since its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
, generated by
A,B,C
is an ideal in
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
. The quotient
G_{\alpha}/K
is the solvable Lie group
\text{Sol}_{\alpha}^{3}
(Example
A.8
). The radical
\text{rad}(\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha})
is three-dimensional so
G_{\alpha}
is not semisimple. The left-invariant foliation
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}
generated by
K
is Riemannian and minimal but not totally geodesic. The horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
is not integrable.

We remind the reader of the fact the three-dimensional Lie groups
\text{Sol}_{\alpha}^{3}
do not carry conformal foliations with minimal leaves, independent of which left-invariant metrics they are given. The same applies to the Lie group
G_{4}
in Example
A.6
. For this see Theorem 4.6 of
[
7
]
.

6.
Normality for Codimension Three

The main aim of this section is to prove Proposition
6.3
.

Definition 6.1
.

A Lie group
G
is said to be
perfect
if its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
satisfies
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\mathfrak{g}
.

It is well-known that every semisimple Lie group is perfect. The following example shows that the converse is not true.

Example 6.2
.

Let
(G,g)
be a six-dimensional Riemannian Lie group such that the orthonormal basis
\{A,B,C,X,Y,Z\}
for its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
satisfies the following Lie bracket relations

[A,B]=C,\quad[C,A]=B,\quad[B,C]=A,

\displaystyle[A,X]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-A-C-Z,\quad[A,Y]=-B-C,

\displaystyle[A,Z]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A+B+X,\quad[B,X]=C,

\displaystyle[B,Y]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2A+C+Z,\quad[B,Z]=-A-B+2C-Y,

\displaystyle[C,X]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2B+C-Y,\quad[C,Y]=2A+X,

\displaystyle[C,Z]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-B,\quad[X,Y]=Y-2Z-2A+2B-C,

\displaystyle[X,Z]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-X+Y-Z-A-2C,\quad[Y,Z]=-X+Y-B-2C.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is perfect and its radical
\text{rad}(\mathfrak{g})
is generated by the three elements

A+Z
,
B+X-Z
and
C+X-Y
. Hence
G
is not semisimple.
The compact semisimple subgroup
K=\text{\bf SU}(2)
is not normal since its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
generated by
A,B,C
is not an ideal in
\mathfrak{g}
. The left-invariant foliation
\mathcal{F}
generated by
K
is Riemannian and minimal but not totally geodesic. The horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
is not integrable.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
(G,g)
be a Riemannian Lie group with semisimple subgroup
K
generating a left-invariant conformal foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
G
of codimension three. If
G
is not perfect then
K
is normal in
G
.

Proof.

Let
\{V_{1},\dots V_{d},X,Y,Z\}
be an orthonormal basis for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
such that
X,Y,Z
generate the three-dimensional horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
. According to Proposition
3.3
the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is Riemannian. In particular this means that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}[X,V_{\alpha}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{\alpha}\cdot Y

\displaystyle+s_{\alpha}\cdot Z,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}[Y,V_{\alpha}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-r_{\alpha}\cdot X

\displaystyle+t_{\alpha}\cdot Z,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}[Z,V_{\alpha}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-s_{\alpha}\cdot X

\displaystyle-t_{\alpha}\cdot Y

\displaystyle.

It is clear that
K
is normal in
G
if and only if
r_{\alpha},s_{\alpha}
,
t_{\alpha}
, all vanish for each
\alpha
. Suppose this is not the case, then by reordering the
V_{\alpha}
, we can suppose that at least one of
r_{1},s_{1},t_{1}
is non-zero.

Since
\mathfrak{g}
is not perfect we have that

[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\neq\mathfrak{g}.

In particular
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]^{\perp}
is a non-trivial subspace. Since
[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}]=\mathcal{V}\subset[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
it follows that
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]^{\perp}
is contained in the horizontal subspace
\mathcal{H}
.

By the definition of the derived subalgebra we have that

\mathcal{V}+\mathcal{H}[V_{i},X]+\mathcal{H}[V_{i},Y]+\mathcal{H}[V_{i},Z]\subset[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]

and so
\mathcal{H}[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
contains the span of
\{\mathcal{H}[V_{i},X],\mathcal{H}[V_{i},Y],\mathcal{H}[V_{i},Z]\}
. An arbitrary linear combination of these vectors is given by

\displaystyle\lambda(r_{\alpha}\cdot Y+s_{\alpha}\cdot Z)+\mu(-r_{\alpha}\cdot X+t_{\alpha}\cdot Z)+\eta(-s_{\alpha}\cdot X-t_{\alpha}\cdot Y)

\displaystyle=-(\mu r_{\alpha}+\eta s_{\alpha})\cdot X+(\lambda r_{\alpha}-\eta t_{\alpha})\cdot Y+(\lambda s_{\alpha}+\mu t_{\alpha})\cdot Z,

which can be written as

\begin{bmatrix}-r_{\alpha}&s_{\alpha}&0\\
0&t_{\alpha}&r_{\alpha}\\
t_{\alpha}&0&s_{\alpha}\end{bmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\mu\\
-\eta\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix}.

If
r_{\alpha},s_{\alpha},t_{\alpha}
are all non-zero for some
\alpha
, then this matrix has maximum rank and
\mathfrak{g}
would be perfect, so at least one must be zero.
Without loss of generality we assume that
r_{1}=0
. Now we show that with this assumption
r_{\alpha}=0
for all
\alpha=1,\dots d
. Suppose that
r_{\beta}\neq 0
and, without loss of generality, that
s_{\beta}=0
. Then we have the following structure equations

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}[X,V_{1}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{1}\cdot Z,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}[Y,V_{1}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{1}\cdot Z,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}[Z,V_{1}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-s_{1}\cdot X-t_{1}\cdot Y.

So, since we are assuming that at least one of
s_{1}
and
t_{1}
is non-zero it follows that
Z\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. Similarly we compute for
\beta
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}[X,V_{\beta}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{\beta}\cdot Y,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}[Y,V_{\beta}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-r_{\beta}\cdot X+t_{\beta}\cdot Z,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}[Z,V_{\beta}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-t_{\beta}\cdot Y.

Similarly we see that
Y\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, and finally since
r_{\beta}\cdot X+t_{\beta}\cdot Z\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
we can conclude that
X
must be also, so
G
would be perfect. Thus, if
r_{1}=0
we have that
r_{\alpha}=0
for all
\alpha
.

Now we will show that
s_{\alpha}
and
t_{\alpha}
must also vanish. We recall the Jacobi identity

0=[V_{\alpha},[V_{\beta},X]]+[V_{\beta},[X,V_{\alpha}]]+[X,[V_{\alpha},V_{\beta}]].

Furthermore, since
K
is semisimple, there exists a linear combination

\sum_{\alpha<\beta}a_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}[V_{\alpha},V_{\beta}]=V_{\gamma}

for any
\gamma=1,\dots d
.
Since
r_{\alpha}=0
for all
\alpha
we have, for any
\alpha,\beta

\displaystyle g([V_{\alpha},[V_{\beta},X]],Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g([V_{\alpha},-s_{\beta}\cdot Z],Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{\beta}\cdot g(-s_{\alpha}\cdot X-t_{\alpha}\cdot Y,Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Finally we compute

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\sum_{\alpha<\beta}a_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}\left([V_{\alpha},[V_{\beta},X]]+[V_{\beta},[X,V_{\alpha}]]+[X,[V_{\alpha},V_{\beta}]]\right),Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha<\beta}\left(a_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}\cdot g(\left([V_{\alpha},[V_{\beta},X]]+[V_{\beta},[X,V_{\alpha}]]\right),Z)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+g(\sum_{\alpha<\beta}a_{\alpha\beta}^{l}[X,[V_{\alpha},V_{\beta}]],Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0+g([X,V_{\gamma}],Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{\gamma}

and so
s_{\gamma}=0
for all
\gamma
. Replacing
X
with
Y
in the above computation shows that
t_{\gamma}=0
, and hence
K
is normal in
G
.
∎

Next we provide an example that shows that the result of Proposition
6.3
does not apply in the case when the codimension of
K
in
G
is four.

Example 6.4
.

Let
(G,g)
be a seven-dimensional Riemannian Lie group such that the orthonormal basis
\{A,B,C,X,Y,Z,W\}
for its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
satisfies the following bracket relations

[A,B]=2C,\quad[C,A]=2B,\quad[B,C]=2A,

\displaystyle[A,X]=2Z,\quad[A,Y]=0,\quad[A,Z]=-2X,\quad[A,W]=0,

\displaystyle[B,X]=0,\quad[B,Y]=2Z,\quad[B,Z]=-2Y,\quad[B,W]=0,

\displaystyle[C,X]=2Y,\quad[C,Y]=-2X,\quad[C,Z]=0,\quad[C,W]=0,

\displaystyle[X,Y]=C,\quad[X,Z]=A,\quad[Y,Z]=B.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is not perfect so it is not semisimple. The compact subgroup
K=\text{\bf SU}(2)
is not normal since its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
generated by
X,Y,Z
is not an ideal in
\mathfrak{g}
. The left-invariant foliation
\mathcal{F}
generated by
K
is Riemannian and totally geodesic. The horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
is not integrable. The quotient space
(G/K,h)
is the flat
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
.

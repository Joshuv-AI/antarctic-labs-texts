Abstract
I report the discovery of three faint, semi-resolved quiescent dwarf galaxies, two of which are strong backsplash candidates associated with the nearby satellite-sparse spiral M101 ( D ∼ 6.7 ​ Mpc D\sim 6.7\,\mathrm{Mpc} ). The galaxies lie within the magnitude range M V ∼ − 7.70 M_{V}\sim-7.70 to − 8.2 -8.2 and half-light radii r h ∼ 110 r_{\mathrm{h}}\sim 110 to 204 ​ pc 204\,\mathrm{pc} . Shapiro DG-I (Sha DG-I/MAGE1412+5650) is a concurrently discovered and isolated galaxy. Shapiro DG-II (Sha DG-II) is a fainter dwarf and potential companion to the SMC-mass galaxy NGC 5585 ( D ∼ 6.84 ​ Mpc D\sim 6.84\,\mathrm{Mpc} ). Shapiro DG-III (Sha DG-III) is an isolated dwarf on the edge of the ultra-faint regime. Hydrodynamical simulations suggest a backsplash population of galaxies that have been environmentally stripped by interactions with a host and ejected from the system, though they have not yet been definitively observed in the local universe. Considering their quenched stellar populations, indicated by the lack of coincident GALEX emission, relative distances, and fitting models, the galaxies are consistent with a backsplash origin and are pending follow-up. Analysis is performed to test whether the system’s population can be explained by standard cosmology. A potential correlation is found between satellite abundances and halo masses calibrated by tidal indices in a sample of nearby MW-like galaxies, suggesting the importance of environmental density in the formation of dark matter subhaloes, though a larger sample is required. M101’s sparse satellite system fits well in the relation and is in agreement with Λ \Lambda CDM expectations.

I
Introduction

Low-luminosity dwarf galaxies, and the level of their quiescence, are a crucial aspect in the understanding of galaxy evolution. These dark matter-dominated galaxies, found both as satellites and in isolation, provide insight into the faint end of the galaxy luminosity function. This is important for addressing the
\Lambda
Cold Dark Matter (
\Lambda
CDM) model and its challenges at a small scale
(
26
;
149
)
, including the "missing satellites" problem
(
82
;
118
)
and "too big to fail" problem
(
22
;
23
)
. Thus, low-mass dwarfs have been a theoretical focus through increasingly high-resolution cosmological simulations
(
143
;
167
;
52
;
138
;
4
)
. The search for Local Group (LG) dwarf galaxies has also been a significant observational focus, with searches in extensive optical surveys such as the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) and Dark Energy Survey (DES) revealing numerous new faint dwarf galaxy candidates
(
106
;
84
;
46
;
34
, e.g.)
.

Progress on both of these ends has worked towards reconciling the small-scale
\Lambda
CDM discrepancies between simulations and galaxies within the LG. However, it is necessary to look beyond the LG and into other satellite systems to better understand low-mass dwarf galaxy formation and whether the LG observations are representative. The Satellites Around Galactic Analogs Survey (SAGA;
54
;
103
;
102
) spectroscopically investigated
\sim 100
MW-mass hosts and their systems outside of the Local Volume (LV;
D\leq 10\,\mathrm{Mpc}
,
76
) in the range of
25
to
40.75\,\mathrm{Mpc}
. Simultaneously, wide-field imagers on modern large telescopes have pushed the boundaries for identifying resolved and semi-resolved stellar populations in faint dwarf galaxies to other galaxy groups within the LV.

Searches around LV galaxy groups with Milky Way (MW)-mass host galaxies, including M81, M94, and M101, are particularly useful testing grounds to compare to the MW and M31. A search for satellites around M94 (
D=4.2\,\mathrm{Mpc}
,
135
) found that the system was devoid of large companions, hosting only two, low-mass, star-forming dwarfs (
M_{\star}\leq 4\times 10^{5}M_{\odot}
), unexpected for a MW-mass galaxy under standard galaxy formation. M81 (
D=3.6\,\mathrm{Mpc}
,
135
), in contrast to the LG, has signs of recent star formation
(
126
)
and is undergoing interactions with M82 and NGC 3077
(
152
)
, providing another unique MW analog environment for comparison
(
56
)
.

In addition to dwarf satellites, isolated low-mass LV dwarf galaxies are of interest, as they are a unique test of galaxy evolution far from the influence of host galaxies. Similar to dwarf satellites, these have largely eluded discovery, and only recently have high-resolution optical surveys revealed stellar-resolved candidates distant from potential hosts
(
101
;
113
;
140
;
68
;
69
;
97
;
139
)
.

The quiescence of these dwarf galaxies, both dwarf satellites around MW analogs and isolated field dwarfs, can be compared to simulations, providing a critical test of galaxy evolution models at a small scale. Comparisons in quiescence are made between the results from the APOSTLE
(
143
)
and Auriga
(
57
)
simulations to observations from SAGA
(
79
)
for MW-mass satellites. The level of quiescence in isolated dwarfs among observations can similarly be compared to the characteristics expected in large-scale hydrodynamical galaxy simulations
(
44
)
. LG observations indicate a strong relationship between quiescence and distance to host
(
166
, e.g.)
as dwarfs within the virial radii of their hosts are generally quenched and gas-poor due to environmental gas stripping, while dwarfs outside of the virial radii are typically gas-rich and detected in H
i

(
134
)
.

Backsplash galaxies bridge the gap between satellite galaxies, residing within the virial radii (
R_{200}
) of their hosts, and true field galaxies in isolated environments. These galaxies have orbits that pass them within
0.5R_{200}
, where they experience environmental stripping, and are then ejected from their hosts’ dark matter halos to beyond
R_{200}
. They can then either continue to move outward from the host halo or return on another infall. This quenching mechanism has been proposed as a means of explaining isolated, quiescent dwarf galaxies
(
154
, e.g.)
, and is a focus of high-resolution simulations
(
119
;
25
;
13
, e.g.)
.

Numerous previous searches have identified low luminosity dwarf galaxy candidates near M101
(
115
;
75
;
17
;
30
;
78
;
77
)
, a bulgeless spiral with a stellar mass similar to the MW (
M_{\star}\approx 5.3\times 10^{10}M_{\odot}
,
162
), though with a likely lower dark matter halo mass and a stellar halo smaller than the MW’s. Recent estimates of its distance from Cepheids find
D=6.7\pm 0.1\,\mathrm{Mpc}

(
136
)
. Its satellite system has been considered complete down to
M_{\star}\approx 5\times 10^{5}M_{\odot}
within the virial radius of M101 (
\sim 250\,\mathrm{kpc}
;
115
), corresponding to a projected radius
\sim 2.1\degree
where the nine that have been confirmed as true satellites and not background low surface brightness (LSB) galaxies all reside.

Here, I present the discovery of three low-luminosity, quiescent dwarf galaxies in the outskirts of the M101 group. Considering an apparent deficiency in the faint satellites observed
(
16
)
, a search was carried out in the
\sim 250\mathrm{-}350\,\mathrm{kpc}
region outside of the virial radius for faint, gas-poor backsplash dwarfs, resulting in the discovery of the candidates. In Section 2, I present the archival optical, UV, and H
i
survey data and follow-up observations. In Section 3, I describe the structural properties, photometric analysis, and the quiescence of the galaxies. In Section 4, I place the galaxies among the known dwarf population of the M101 group and assess the backsplash scenario. In Section 5, I compare the observed satellite population of M101 with galaxy formation models to determine whether it is consistent with
\Lambda
CDM cosmology. I summarize the discoveries and conclude in Section 6.

II.3
Galaxy Evolution Explorer

The UV sky survey from the Galaxy Evolution Explorer (GALEX;
104

GALEX observations were retrieved from the Barbara A. Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes (MAST). For Sha DG-I/MAGE1412+5650, images of 1536 s from the Medium Imaging Survey (MIS) in NUV and 96 s in FUV from the All-Sky Imaging Survey (AIS) are available. For Sha DG-II, images of 3363.85 s from a Guest Investigator program (GII) in NUV and 96 s in FUV from AIS are used. For Sha DG-III, images of 25898.55 s in NUV and 4024.15 s in FUV are obtained from the Deep Imaging Survey (DIS). No UV emissions associated with the galaxies were detected in any of the GALEX observations. Other UV signals were identified in the images that overlapped with the candidates; however, these were attributed to unrelated foreground/background sources.

Identifying the distances of the galaxies is critical in understanding their membership and potential association with M101. For Sha DG-I/MAGE1412+5650, a sufficiently resolved stellar population is available for preliminary measurements of the tip of the red giant branch (TRGB). The TRGB is a well-established distance measurement for nearby old, metal-poor galaxies, using the sharp luminosity cutoff at the upper limit of the red giant branch as a standard candle. It is most effectively measured at longer wavelengths, within the near infrared, where stellar luminosities are greater and less dependent on age
(
114
)
. Within the
z

\mathrm{S/N}>5
were selected for analyzing the luminosity function. Considering the small angular size and low galactic stellar density at its latitude, contamination from foreground stars is likely minimal.

Despite the stellar sparseness of the system, there are enough resolved stars to provisionally detect the TRGB within the Subaru
z

It is important to emphasize that the initial distance estimate is tentative and is intended to highlight that the galaxy is inconsistent with status as a Local Group satellite or distant background dwarf. III.3
Quiescence

The GALEX UV flux is a strong tracer of a relatively young stellar population (
\lesssim 100\,\mathrm{Myr}
) and has been used in works surrounding low-luminosity, semi-resolved dwarfs in assessing their quiescence
(
140
, e.g.)
. Galaxies with strong NUV and FUV continuum have experienced abundant star formation in the past
100\,\mathrm{Myr}

(

IV.1
Backsplash Scenario

From simulations, the population of quenched dwarf galaxies nearly all reside within the virial radii of their hosts, where they interact with their host’s halos and are dominantly quenched by ram-pressure stripping
(
150
)
. This aligns with the SDSS survey observations, where
<0.06\%
of field galaxies were identified as quenched
(
53
)
. Of these quenched field galaxies, the majority are low-mass dwarfs. With the emergence of large high-resolution survey instruments such as HSC-SSP and the DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys
(
43
)
, a handful of these rare objects, including Sha DG-I/MAGE1412+5650 and DG-III, have been revealed. Two viable quenching mechanisms exist for this population: backsplash objects that have previously experienced ram-pressure stripping and are now ejected to
1\mathrm{-}2.5R_{200}
, and cosmic web stripping, where isolated galaxies cross overdense regions. This creates a challenge in determining true backsplash candidates.

Recently, the TNG50 hydrodynamical simulation has been used to demonstrate these two mechanisms of quenching and how they can be differentiated.
12
finds that in TNG50 dwarf galaxies with stellar masses
M_{\star}=[10^{7}-10^{9}]M_{\odot}
, backsplash is the dominant quenching mechanism up to
\sim 1\,\mathrm{Mpc}
from a host
M_{\star}>10^{9}M_{\odot}
, and drops off significantly by
\sim 1.5\,\mathrm{Mpc}
. At a distance of
599.3^{+801.5}_{-272.0}\,\mathrm{kpc}
from its host, Sha DG-I/MAGE1412+5650 fits within the range where backsplash dwarfs are expected.

The strongest existing candidate of a backsplash galaxy outside of the Local Group is Blobby (dw0910+7326) in close proximity to M81
(
32
)
. Similarly to the dwarfs discovered in this work, Blobby is of a close proximity
\sim 590\,\mathrm{kpc}
or
1.7R_{200}
to its host M81. The distances of the candidates considering uncertainties lie within the range where a backsplash mechanism of quenching is strongly preferred around their hosts, with ranges of
\sim 1.31\mathrm{-}5.60\,R_{200}
for Sha DG-I/MAGE1412+5650 and
\sim 1.02\mathrm{-}2.70\,R_{200}
for Blobby. Although Sha DG-III has an unknown distance, its projected distance of
303\,\mathrm{kpc}
, or
\sim 1.21\,R_{200}
, provides a lower limit greater than the virial radius of the host M101.

Sha DG-II, though also outside the virial radius of M101, is within the vicinity of the nearby SMC-mass dwarf NGC 5585. With an estimated
R_{200}=80\mathrm{-}100\,\mathrm{kpc}

(
62
)
, Sha DG-II lies within its virial radius and is likely a faint satellite at the edge of the ultra-faint regime comparable to MADCASH J074238+652501-dw (
M_{g}=-7.4\pm 0.4
;
r_{\mathrm{h}}=168\pm 70\,\mathrm{pc}
;
27
) around NGC 2403.

Notably, the position angles (PA) of the dwarf galaxies’ major axes, derived from the Astropy models, are roughly aligned with the corresponding projected angles towards M101. The major axis PA is measured counterclockwise, E of N, from the positive y-axis. For Sha DG-I/MAGE1412+5650, the PA is
\sim 21\degree
, compared to the angle toward M101 of
\sim 27\degree
; for Sha DG-III, the PA is
\sim 177\degree
, compared to the angle toward M101 of
\sim 142\degree
. The Three Hundred project
(
38
)
, a simulation of a large sample of galaxy cluster environments, found that the radial alignment, in this context the correlation between the major axis of the dwarf galaxy and the position of the host, was far stronger among backsplash galaxies than for infalling objects
(
83
)
.

Backsplash galaxies demonstrate distinguishable properties from truly isolated field galaxies, and their backsplash probabilities can be analyzed based on their radial velocities and positions
(
25
;
18
)
. A deeper understanding of these properties in the new candidates requires further observations, which is discussed in Section
V.5
. They are also expected to be relatively deficient in their dark matter halo masses and have ceased star formation earlier
(
12
)
, although these differences are more difficult to analyze observationally.

IV.2
Satellite Population and Question of Missing Satellites

With only nine confirmed satellites down to
M_{V}=-7.7\,\mathrm{mag}
, M101 contains an extremely sparse satellite system compared to the 14 satellites down to the same magnitude in the virial radius of MW and the 26 in the virial radius of M31
(
16
)
. Of the many low-surface brightness candidates identified in previous searches for dwarfs surrounding M101, only a handful are true faint satellites. The bright star-forming dwarfs NGC 5474, NGC 5477, and Holm IV were confirmed to have distances that placed them as likely M101 satellites
(
155
)
. The Dragonfly array identified three low-luminosity satellites, DF-1, DF-2, and DF-3
(
115
)
that were ultimately confirmed through Hubble Space Telescope (HST) follow-up by
40
. DwA, discovered by
67
, and Dw9, discovered by
17

(
28
)
and HST
(
15
)
. The galaxy dw1403p5338, originally identified by
163

31

Galaxies NGC 5585, DDO194, KKH87, and UGC 8882 are not considered M101 satellites in this paper due to their distances from M101, and are not backsplash due to their star-forming properties. The remaining unresolved low surface brightness galaxies within the FOV are most likely associated with the background NGC 5485 group.

With the inclusion of Sha DG-I/MAGE1412+5650 and DG-III, the satellite system of M101 remains relatively poor, even with more extensive coverage extending to
\sim 350\,\mathrm{kpc}
. They are the only dwarfs outside the M101 virial radius that are likely associated with the system. The discoveries demonstrate the effectiveness of detecting backsplash galaxies around LV hosts with modern surveys, and the importance of including this search area surrounding the virial radius for understanding nearby satellite systems.

The satellites within M101’s virial radius are prominently lopsided; seven of the nine satellites lie in the southeast, whereas
2\pm 1.4
would be expected in a random distribution
(
15
)
, and seven of the eight lie within one hemisphere, divided at
\mathrm{PA}=250.5\degree
. With this sample, the geometric centroid of the distribution of satellites around M101 lies
\sim 31\,\mathrm{kpc}
off-center from M101 at
\mathrm{PA}=189\degree
. Sha DG-III lies in a similar direction to the lopsided distribution at
\mathrm{PA}\sim 177\degree

\mathrm{PA}\sim 21\degree
).

The structure was largely defined by luminous galaxies; thus, the inclusion of fainter, low-luminosity dwarfs provides a unique assessment of the feature. While the dwarfs that belong to the lopsidedness in the southeast lie within the 67 kpc plane, Dw9 and Sha DG-I/MAGE1412+5650 are offset.

Dark Matter Halo Models and Consistency with
\Lambda
CDM

In this section, M101’s observed satellite population, both within the virial radius and backsplash galaxies, is compared to empirical galaxy formation models to test the system’s conformity with standard flat-
\Lambda
CDM cosmology. I adopt the cosmological parameters
\Omega_{m}=0.3111\pm 0.0056
,
\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.6889\pm 0.0056
,
H_{0}=67.66\pm 0.42
,
\sigma_{8}=0.8102\pm 0.0060
, and
n_{s}=0.9665\pm 0.0038
, consistent with Planck 2018
(
128
)
.

V.1
Stellar Mass

Structure formation in the standard cosmological model involves dark matter halos resulting from gravitationally collapsed Gaussian density perturbations in the early universe. Stars form as residual gas cools and condenses within the potential wells of the haloes, leading to luminous galaxies
(
169
;
88
)
. The process is hierarchical, as haloes undergo accretion of diffuse non-halo material
(
50
, e.g.)
and repeated mergers
(
88
, e.g.)
, and substructure often survives as orbiting subhaloes, some of which form luminous satellites
(
157
;
45
;
51
, e.g.)
.

A monotonic relationship exists between the observable stellar mass and the dark matter halo mass of galaxies
(
142
, e.g.)
. The total stellar mass of galaxies can be estimated using a relationship with 3.6 and 4.5
\mu\mathrm{m}
infrared fluxes
(
49
)
.
133
uses calibrated model flux densities to obtain a stellar mass of
M_{\star}=5.5\pm 0.52\times 10^{10}M_{\odot}
. Although M101 has been considered a MW-analog, the stellar halo mass of the MW is not well constrained. Previous observations have determined
M_{\star}=5.43\pm 0.57\times 10^{10}M_{\odot}

(
112
)
, a value very close to M101, while a recent estimate from
98
using Gaia finds a much lower value
M_{\star}=2.607\pm 0.353\mathrm{(syst.)}\pm 0.0085\mathrm{(stoch.)}\times 10^{10}M_{\odot}
.

V.2
Dark Matter Halo Mass and SHMR

The total mass of the dark matter halo of the system is often estimated using stellar abundance matching (AM), assuming a monotonic relation between stellar and halo mass, requiring cumulative
M_{\star}
and
M_{\mathrm{h}}
functions. Where
\Phi
represents the stellar mass function, the cumulative
M_{\star}
function is defined as
(
55
)
:

n(>M_{i})=\int^{\infty}_{M_{i}}\Phi(M^{\prime})dM^{\prime}

(6)

The cumulative functions are described in a stellar-to-halo mass relation (SHMR)
M_{\star}/M_{\mathrm{h}}
using a double power-law function
(
95
;
121
;
55
)
. The parameterization is described as:

\frac{M_{\star}}{M_{\mathrm{h}}}=2A(z)\left[\left(\frac{M_{\mathrm{h}}}{M_{A}(z)}\right)^{-\beta(z)}+\left(\frac{M_{\mathrm{h}}}{M_{A}(z)}\right)^{\gamma(z)}\right]

(7)

containing four free, redshift-dependent parameters.
\beta
and
\gamma
describe the behavior of the SHMR slope at low and high mass ends, respectively
(
121
)
.
A
represents the normalization at a halo mass
M_{A}

(
55
)
. For M101, the best-fit parameters with 68% confidence intervals from
55
for
0.00\leq z<0.20
are used, where
\beta=1.00\pm 0.05
,
\gamma=0.702\pm 0.006
,
A=0.0465\pm 0.0015
, and
M_{A}=11.77\pm 0.03
. The total halo mass derived from the double power-law SHMR is
M_{\mathrm{h}}\approx 1.3\times 10^{12}M_{\odot}
.

The resulting abundance matched value of the M101 halo mass with a 0.2 dex scatter is inconsistent with the consensus of estimates obtained from kinematic measurements.
155
uses observations of radial velocities and H
i
regions within the neighboring galaxies NGC 5474, NGC 5477, UGC 9405, and Holm IV to find a dynamical mass
M_{\mathrm{h}}\approx 6.2\times 10^{11}M_{\odot}
. Similarly,
74
uses radial velocities in the nine known satellites to estimate a mass
M_{\mathrm{h}}\approx 8.5\pm 3.0\times 10^{11}M_{\odot}
.
171
proposes a relation with the largest gap between satellite luminosities (
\Delta_{i{\mathrm{,max}}}
) and the halo mass to estimate
M_{\mathrm{h}}\approx 4\times 10^{11}M_{\odot}
.

This discrepancy has similarly been observed in other late-type galaxies, such as within the Local Group galaxies M31 and the MW
(
111
)
, suggesting the need for a more complex, morphology-dependent relation.
131
describes a model of two separate branches of the SHMR, decreasing for early-type galaxies (ETGs) beyond a peak
M_{\mathrm{h}}\approx 1\times 10^{12}M_{\odot}
, and a monotonically rising relation for late-type galaxies (LTGs). Their sample for LTGs consists of 110 of the 175 galaxies in the
Spitzer
Photometry and Accurate Rotation Curves (SPARC;
93
) data at 3.6
\mu
m with inclinations
i>30\degree
, unimodal posterior halo mass distributions, and low-mass discs.
132
derived the SPARC rotation curves with
barolo
for
V_{c}=\sqrt{V^{2}_{\mathrm{DM}}+V^{2}_{\mathrm{gas}}+V^{2}_{\star}}
and modeled a NFW halo using a Bayesian methodology with MCMC sampling to calculate the parameters
M_{\mathrm{halo}}
, the stellar mass-to-light ratio
\Upsilon_{\star}
, and the concentration parameter
c
.
41
utilizes a similar process with a sample of five MW-analog galaxies
V_{\mathrm{max}}=200\mathrm{-}280\,\mathrm{km\,s}^{-1}
, which show a similar discrepancy to the SHMR and SPARC late-type sample. The double power-law abundance matching
(
121
, e.g.)
is in strong agreement with kinematic measurements of the sample only to
M_{\star}\lesssim 5\times 10^{10}M_{\odot}

(
131
)
. Above this, abundance matching models consistently over-predict
M_{\mathrm{h}}
, preventing accurate subhalo abundance estimates and assessments of satellite populations.

M101 is in The H
i
Nearby Galaxies Survey (THINGS;
164
), with high-resolution observations from NRAO VLA; however, a highly asymmetric disk and low
i=18\degree
create a steeper challenge in obtaining an accurate profile of its stellar and gas distribution, and thus is not used for deriving a halo mass. Rather than NFW halo modeling, I instead use an isothermal model
(
86
)
with the linked parameters: velocity dispersion
\sigma
, central density
\rho_{\mathrm{o}}
, and core radius
r_{\mathrm{c}}
, sampled on Sc-Im and dSph galaxies. The isothermal sphere (ITS) uses the relation between these values:

\sigma^{2}=\frac{4\pi G\rho_{\mathrm{o}}r^{2}_{\mathrm{c}}}{9}

(8)

As the slope of the rotation curve becomes flat,
V_{\mathrm{flat}}
is used up to the virial radius
R_{200}
, and is thus used as
V_{200}
. This relates to the equation:

V(r)\simeq\sqrt{2}\sigma

(9)

The values for
V_{200}
,
R_{200}
, and
M_{200}=M_{\mathrm{h}}
can be conveniently converted
(
48
)
:

\log\frac{V_{200}}{[\mathrm{km\,s}^{-1}]}=\log\frac{R_{200}}{[h^{-1}\,\mathrm{kpc}]}=\frac{1}{3}\log\left(G\frac{M_{200}}{[h^{-1}M_{\odot}]}\right)

(10)

Using the value
\sigma=88.39\,\mathrm{km\,s}^{-1}

(
8
)
, the halo mass is estimated to be
M_{\mathrm{h}}\approx 6.74\times 10^{11}M_{\odot}
, consistent with the kinematically derived observations.

There is a correlation between
M_{\mathrm{bh}}
, derived using stellar velocities of the population in the central bulge, and
M_{\mathrm{h}}
. The relation can be used as an independent test of the consistency of the observed kinematic
M_{\mathrm{h}}
values. M101 does not contain a classical bulge, and its high surface brightness central region is a pseudobulge resulting from gas transported to the center in secular evolution
(
87
;
85
)
with a total mass fraction
PB/T=0.027\pm 0.008
and a dense, central nuclear star cluster
N/T=0.00029\pm 0.00025

B/T=0.26
in the MW
(
98
)
.
85

\sigma_{\star}=27\pm 4\,\mathrm{km\,s}^{-1}
and an upper limit of the central black hole mass
M_{\mathrm{bh}}\lesssim 2.6\pm 0.5\times 10^{6}M_{\odot}
.
9

\begin{array}[]{l}\log(M_{\mathrm{bh}}/M_{\odot})=(8.18\pm 0.11)+(1.55\pm 0.31)\\
\times[\log(M_{h}/M_{\odot})-13.0]\end{array}

(11)

resulting in a derived value of

, consistent with the kinematic measurements, though not well constrained.

V.3
Subhalo and Satellite Abundance Models

For a given halo mass and redshift, there is a direct relation to the mass of its subhaloes, described here as the dimensionless subhalo mass ratio
\mu
. As dark matter halo and subhalo evolution are considered through purely gravitational interactions,
51
uses N-body simulations to derive the cumulative subhalo mass function:

N(>\mu\equiv m_{\mathrm{sub}}/M_{200})=\left(\frac{\mu}{\widetilde{\mu}_{1}}\right)^{a}\exp\left[-\left(\frac{\mu}{\mu_{\mathrm{cut}}}\right)^{b}\right]

(12)

with the slope index
a=-0.94
, and the best fit parameters using
b=1.2
:

M_{200}\in[1,3]\times 10^{12}h^{-1}M_{\odot}:\widetilde{\mu}_{1}=0.0085,\mu_{\mathrm{cut}}=0.08

(13)

for the closest mass range available. From hydrodynamical simulations, ultrafaint dwarf satellites typically form within subhaloes of
M_{\mathrm{peak}}=0.5\mathrm{-}3\times 10^{9}M_{\odot}

(
168
)
, which is used as a cutoff for
m_{\mathrm{sub}}
down to this subhalo mass range. The estimated subhalo counts based on the MW and M101 kinematic halo masses of
M_{\mathrm{h}}=1.6\times 10^{12}M_{\odot}
and
M_{\mathrm{h}}=6.74\times 10^{11}M_{\odot}
, respectively, at the lower and upper boundaries of the
m_{\mathrm{sub}}
cutoff are
N(>\mu)_{\mathrm{MW}}=4.09,22.28
and
N(>\mu)_{\mathrm{M101}}=1.78,9.86
, providing a ratio
N(>\mu)_{\mathrm{MW}}/N(>\mu)_{\mathrm{M101}}\approx 2.3
. This is in agreement with observations, as the median MW-like host in
15
hosts a factor of
\sim 2.5
more satellites than the observed value in M101 down to
M_{V}\approx-8
.

To compare the observed satellite luminosity function with simulations of expected
\Lambda
CDM cosmology, UniverseMachine
(
10
)
is used to fit the kinematic
M_{\mathrm{h}}
values. The model adopts a Planck cosmology and uses the
Bolshoi–Planck
dark matter simulation of a
250\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}
volume. With the kinematic M101 halo mass measurement
M_{\mathrm{h}}=6.74\times 10^{11}M_{\odot}
, the observed satellite luminosity function is greater than the median expected from the simulation, but within the 2
\sigma
confidence interval and consistent with
\Lambda
CDM expectations. The discrepancy is greater than the model used by
39
, who assume a greater
M_{\mathrm{h}}
, which potentially suggests a higher level of scatter when kinematically derived values are considered in favor of abundance matching assumptions.

(15)

(16)

V.5
Backsplash Models and Future Follow-up

High-resolution simulations of MW-like galaxies using the Numerical Investigation of a Hundred Astronomical Objects (NIHAO;
165
) are used to simulate the peak masses, radial velocities, distances, and the velocity dispersion-stellar mass relation of dwarf satellites to estimate the probability of backsplash nature
(
25
)
. While true field objects typically have negative radial velocities relative to their hosts, backsplash galaxies are expected to have positive relative velocities due to mass loss
(
154
)
.
25
use this to calculate the probabilities of LG quiescent, isolated dwarfs having backsplash origin, finding high likelihoods of a backsplash population in the Local Group. Observationally, backsplash histories of LG candidates can be tested and compared to simulations based on kinematic constructions of orbital histories. Recent results from
18
use HST to compute proper motions of six MW dwarf galaxies within
400\mathrm{-}600\,\mathrm{kpc}
, finding that none are likely to be backsplash within
6\,\mathrm{Gyr}
, and only Cetus is a plausible candidate in earlier histories.

To test whether the observed backsplash populations agree with simulations and relations with host properties, further follow-up is required both within the LG and in the LV. The new candidates, if found to be quenched by a backsplash method rather than through cosmic web stripping, will provide an ideal testing ground surrounding a MW-analog in an isolated environment and with a quieter formation history. Imaging of DwA and Dw9 from
15
shows that HST/JWST constructs accurate TRGB distances, CMDs/metallicity, and radial velocities at this distance to probabilistically distinguish backsplash nature from field isolation.
12
describes additional simulation-predicted properties to differentiate the two populations. Backsplash leads to a reduction in dark matter halo mass, though obtaining well-constrained observations at this scale is likely not feasible. Backsplash galaxies are also expected to generally contain older populations, with
t_{50}=4.0^{+1.7}_{-1.2}
and
t_{50}=3.7^{+1.4}_{-0.9}
for backsplash and field median values, respectively,
(
12
)
though these differences are insufficient to conclude the nature of an individual candidate. Radial velocities and accurate distances relative to their hosts remain the strongest measurements to obtain with follow-up observations.

The discovery of the candidates demonstrates the detectability of quiescent dwarf galaxies down to the ultra-faint regime within neighboring MW-analogs with HSC-SSP. Rubin LSST
(
72
;
19
)
is expected to significantly expand the known population and will likely provide similar candidates to test around other MW-like hosts in the LV. With sufficient candidates, the relationships to host parameters described in Subsection
V.4
can be expanded to examine the relation and importance of halo and environmental factors in the formation of backsplash galaxies.

VI
Conclusions

This paper presents the discovery of three isolated quiescent dwarf galaxies lying outside the virial radius of M101. Sha DG-I/MAGE1412+5650 (
M_{V}=-8.17\pm 0.24
), concurrently discovered by
62
, is a candidate backsplash dwarf of the M101 group, with a tentative distance estimate of
6.19^{+0.86}_{-0.75}\,\mathrm{Mpc}
from TRGB of the semi-resolved population in HSC-SSP images. Sha DG-II (
M_{V}=-7.75\pm 0.10
) lies within the virial radius of NGC 5585 and therefore is a potential faint satellite. Sha DG-III (
M_{V}=-7.70\pm 0.10
), similar to Sha DG-I/MAGE1412+5650, is isolated and is a strong backsplash candidate, though it requires follow-up observations to constrain its distance. The three galaxies lie at projected separations of
303\mathrm{-}342\,\mathrm{kpc}
from M101, which has a virial radius of
\sim 250\,\mathrm{kpc}
. The candidates show a lack of recent star formation and are likely quiescent, with non-detections in GALEX, and additionally a lack of
\mathrm{H}\alpha
in Sha DG-I/MAGE1412+5650. Best-fit models of the galaxies were created using optical images from CFHTLS and HSC-SSP/HSCLA in Astropy. The values for the Sérsic indices
n_{\text{S\'{e}rsic}}
are obtained and fall in a range
\sim 0.43\mathrm{-}0.5
, and the ellipticities (
\epsilon
), in a range
\sim 0.18\mathrm{-}0.34
, typical of low-luminosity dwarf spheroidal galaxies. Recent simulations, such as from
12
, find that backsplash is the dominant quenching mechanism for dwarf galaxies up to
\sim 1\,\mathrm{Mpc}
, consistent with the projected distances of the new candidates, as opposed to cosmic web stripping of field dwarfs. Given their quiescence and close proximity to M101, a backsplash origin is the preferred scenario for Sha DG-I/MAGE1412+5650 and Sha DG-III.

The updated satellite population of M101 is investigated to understand whether the satellite luminosity function, which is sparse relative to other MW-analogs, agrees with
\Lambda
CDM expectations. The stellar mass of M101,
M_{\star}=5.5\pm 0.52\times 10^{10}M_{\odot}
, is similar to or greater than that of the MW, and thus the abundance matching relation finds a similar dark matter halo mass
M_{\mathrm{h}}\approx 1.3\times 10^{12}M_{\odot}
with
0.2\,\mathrm{dex}
scatter. This is in disagreement with kinematically derived measurements
M_{\mathrm{h}}\approx 6.2\times 10^{11}M_{\odot}
. The discrepancy is explained by the inconsistency in the SHMR based on morphological classification and the role of environmental density in halo formation, which is not considered in the relation. This is evident in observations of the stellar halo, which is of a very low mass and metallicity, but consistent with
\Lambda
CDM expectations, supporting a quiet accretion history. The density contrast of M101’s surroundings is assessed quantitatively with the tidal index
\Theta_{5}
in comparison to other MW-analog galaxies and satellite populations. A potential correlation is found between the dark matter halo mass of the host calibrated by its tidal index and the total satellite counts above
M_{V}\sim-7.5\,\mathrm{mag}
, providing hints that the role of local density in the formation of subhaloes can be observed, though requiring a deeper sample and analysis, which is beyond the scope of this paper. M101 fits well in this relation alongside similarly sparse MW-analog systems such as M94. Considering the kinematically derived halo mass, the satellite luminosity function of M101 agrees with
\Lambda
CDM expectations in UniverseMachine simulations, though suggesting greater scatter from the MW-analog median.

Recent hydrodynamical simulations have been utilized to assess the probabilities of backsplash populations among MW analogs. However, proper motion observations thus far have not confirmed the presence of backsplash galaxies in the Local Volume. Of the isolated dwarfs assessed in the Local Group, none of their orbits confidently show past interactions with the MW or M31. Follow-up observations of the new candidates in this work using radial velocity and SFR measurements from HST or JWST would provide the opportunity to explore these objects in a MW-like system, but with a quieter formation history. This would uncover insights into the role of the host halo accretion/merger history and environmental effects on the evolution of backsplash galaxies. Evolving high-resolution optical surveys, such as Rubin LSST, are expected to find further candidates around LV hosts and will allow for a test of these relations on a large scale.

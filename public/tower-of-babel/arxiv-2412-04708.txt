Spectral theory for periodic vector NLS equations
Evgeny Korotyaev

Abstract
. We consider a first order operator with a periodic 3x3 matrix potential on the real line. This operator appears in the problem of the periodic vector NLS equation. The spectrum of the operator covers the real line, it is union of the spectral bands of multiplicity 3, separated by spectral intervals of multiplicity 1. The main results of this work are the following: ⋅ \cdot The Lyapunov function on the corresponding 2 or 3-sheeted Riemann surface is described. ⋅ \cdot Necessary and sufficient conditions are given when the Riemann surface is 2-sheeted. ⋅ \cdot The asymptotics of 2-periodic eigenvalues are determined. ⋅ \cdot One constructs an entire function, which is positive on the spectrum of multiplicity 3 and is negative on its gaps. ⋅ \cdot The Borg type results about inverse problems are solved.

Introduction and main results

1.1.
Vector Nonlinear Schrödinger equations

Consider the periodic 2 dimensional defocusing Vector Nonlinear Schrödinger equation (or the Manakov systems, or shortly the MS)

\displaystyle iv_{t}=-v_{xx}+2|v|^{2}v,\hskip 20.00003ptv=(v_{1},v_{2})^{\top}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2},

(1.1)

where
|v|^{2}=|v_{1}|^{2}+|v_{2}|^{2}
. Here
v_{1},v_{2}
represents as a sum of right- and left-hand polarized waves. The NLS equation is one
of the most fundamental and the most universal nonlinear PDE.
Zakharov and Shabat proved that it is integrable
[
65
]
.
The vector NLS equation appears in physics to study two mode optical fibers, photorefractive materials and so on. Manakov
[
54
]
obtained first basis results about the vector case and proved that it is completely integrable. His proof is based on the Lax representation.
There is a number articles dedicated to periodic and quasi-periodic
solutions of the MS, based on an algebrogeometric
technique, see e.g. Krichever
[
50
]
, Dubrovin
[
16
]
, Adams, Harnad, Hurtubise
[
2
]
, Elgin, Enolski, Its
[
17
]
, Warren, Elgin

[
61
]
, where the inverse problem is discussed. The results
are based on the finite bands integration technique for the scalar case

[
15
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
58
]
. They do not discuss the spectral properties
of
{\mathcal{L}}
and the existence of finite bands potentials is still open problem.
The case of two bands is discussed by Woodcock, Warren, Elgin
[
61
]
.
Results about the spectral theory for periodic vector NLS systems are obtained in the paper
[
43
]
devoted to the more general class of operators and in particulr,
v
is a matrix-valued function.

Scalar NLS equations governs
the long-distance pulse propagation in optical fibers. Early
fundamental research on NLS equations appears in the papers of
Zakharov and Shabat
[
65
]
, who proved that the scalar NLS equation
is integrable.
For the case of the scalar NLS equation we consider the Zakharov-Shabat (shortly ZS) operators. Briefly describe results about the ZS-operator
{\mathcal{L}}_{zs}
on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}^{2})
given by

\textstyle{\mathcal{L}}_{zs}=iJ_{zs}{d\over dx}+V_{zs},\ \ \ \ J_{zs}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ \ \ V_{zs}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\overline{u}\\
u&0\end{pmatrix},\hskip 10.00002ptu\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}),

(1.2)

see, e.g.,
[
51
]
.
The spectrum of
{\mathcal{L}}_{zs}
is purely absolutely
continuous and consists of spectral bands separated by the gaps:

\sigma({\mathcal{L}}_{zs})={\mathbb{R}}\setminus\cup\gamma_{n},\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma_{n}=(\lambda_{n}^{-},\lambda_{n}^{+}),\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda_{n}^{\pm}=\pi n+o(1)\ \ \mathop{\mathrm{as}}\nolimits\ n\to\pm\infty.

(1.3)

Here
\lambda_{n}^{\pm},n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
are 2-periodic eigenvalues of the equation

iJ_{zs}y^{\prime}+V_{zs}y=\lambda y
.
The corresponding Riemann surface is two-sheeted with the real branch points

\lambda_{n}^{\pm},n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. There are a lot of results about inverse problems for
ZS-operators, see
[
25
]
,
[
38
]
,
[
39
]
, and references therein.

1.2.
Preliminary results

In order to study different invariants of the MS
we consider Manakov operators
{\mathcal{L}}
on the real line given by

\textstyle{\mathcal{L}}=iJ{d\over dx}+V,\hskip 20.00003ptJ=\begin{pmatrix}1&\!0\!\!\\
0&-1\!\!1_{2}\end{pmatrix},\hskip 20.00003ptV=\begin{pmatrix}0&v^{*}\\
v&0\end{pmatrix}=V^{*},

(1.4)

where
1\!\!1_{d},d\geqslant 1
is the identity
d\times d
matrix.
We assume that the 1-periodic
1\times 2
matrix
(vector)
v
satisfies

\displaystyle v=(v_{1},v_{2})^{\top}\in{\mathscr{H}}=L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})\oplus L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}),\hskip 20.00003pt{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}},

(1.5)

where
{\mathscr{H}}
is the complex Hilbert space equipped with the norm

\|v\|^{2}=\!\!\int_{0}^{1}(|v_{1}|^{2}+|v_{2}|^{2})dx
.
Introduce the
3\times 3
-matrix valued solutions
y(x,\lambda)
of the Manakov system

\textstyle iJy^{\prime}+Vy=\lambda y,\hskip 20.00003pty(0,\lambda)=1\!\!1_{3}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\ \lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}.

(1.6)

Define the monodromy matrix
\psi(\lambda)
and its determinant
D(\tau,\lambda)
by

\psi(\lambda)=y(1,\lambda),\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002ptD(\tau,\lambda)=\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits(\psi(\lambda)-\tau 1\!\!1_{3}),\hskip 20.00003pt\tau,\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}},

(1.7)

which are entire functions of
\lambda
. An eigenvalue
\tau
of
\psi(\lambda)
is called a
multiplier
, it is a root of the algebraic equation
D(\tau,\lambda)=0
, where
\tau,\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
\tau_{j}=\tau_{j}(\lambda),j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{3}=\{1,2,3\}
be multipliers of

\psi(\lambda)
. It is known (see e.g.,
[
43
]
) that the three
roots
\tau_{1}(\cdot),\tau_{2}(\cdot)
and
\tau_{3}(\cdot)
constitute
three distinct branches of some analytic function
\tau(\cdot)

on the Riemann surface
{\mathcal{R}}
.

The Manakov operators
{\mathcal{L}}=iJ{d\over dx}+V
is self-adjoint and its spectrum
\sigma({\mathcal{L}})

is absolutely continuous (see
[
21
]
) and satisfies

\sigma({\mathcal{L}})=\sigma_{ac}({\mathcal{L}})=\Big\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}:|\tau_{j}(\lambda)|=1,\ \text{some}\ j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{3}\Big\}=\mathfrak{S}_{1}\cup\mathfrak{S}_{2}\cup\mathfrak{S}_{3},

(1.8)

where
\mathfrak{S}_{j},j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{3}
is the part of the spectrum of
{\mathcal{L}}

having the multiplicity
j
:

\mathfrak{S}_{3}=\Big\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}:|\tau_{j}(\lambda)|=1,\ \forall\ j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{3}\Big\},\ \mathfrak{S}_{1}=\Big\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}:|\tau_{j}(\lambda)|=1,\ \text{only \ one }\ j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{3}\Big\},

and
\mathfrak{S}_{2}
is the spectrum of
{\mathcal{L}}
with the multiplicity
2
.
In order to describe spectral problems it is convenient to define
the Lyapunov functions
\Delta_{j}(\lambda)
on the Riemann surface
{\mathcal{R}}
by

\textstyle\Delta_{j}(\lambda)={1\over 2}(\tau_{j}(\lambda)+\tau_{j}^{-1}(\lambda)),\hskip 20.00003ptj\in{\mathbb{N}}_{3}=\{1,2,3\}.

(1.9)

In order to discuss periodic and anti-periodic eigenvalues we define
the corresponding determinants

D_{\pm}(\lambda)=D(\pm 1,\lambda)
.
The zeros of
D_{+}
(or
D_{-}
) are the eigenvalues of periodic (or
anti-periodic) problem for the equation
iJy^{\prime}+Vy=\lambda y
. Denote by

z_{2n,j},(n,j)\in{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{N}}_{3}
(and
z_{2n+1,j}
) the sequence of
zeros of
D_{+}
(and
D_{-}
) counted with multiplicity such that

z_{n,1}\leqslant z_{n,2}\leqslant z_{n,3}\leqslant z_{n+1,1}\leqslant z_{n+1,2}\leqslant z_{n+1,3}\leqslant\dots.

Note that
z_{n,j}
is an eigenvalue of 2-periodic problem for the
equation
iJy^{\prime}+Vy=\lambda y
.

In order to discuss the basic properties of the monodromy matrix we
consider the case
v=0
. The monodromy matrix
\psi^{o}
, its multipliers

\tau_{j}^{o}
, the trace
T^{o}=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits y^{o}(1,\lambda)
, the Lyapunov functions
\Delta_{j}^{o},j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{3}
are given by

\psi^{o}(\lambda)=e^{-i\lambda J},\ \ \tau_{1}^{o}=\tau_{2}^{o}=e^{i\lambda},\ \ \tau_{3}^{o}=e^{-i\lambda},\ \ T^{o}=e^{-i\lambda}+2e^{i\lambda},\hskip 10.00002pt\Delta_{j}^{o}=\cos\lambda.

(1.10)

The corresponding determinants
D_{\pm}^{o}
and its zeros (i.e., 2-periodic
eigenvalues)
z_{n,j}^{o}
have the form

\textstyle D_{\pm}^{o}(\lambda)=(e^{-i\lambda}\mp 1)(e^{-i\lambda}\mp 1)^{2},\hskip 10.00002ptz_{n,j}^{o}=\pi n,(n,j)\in{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{N}}_{3}.

1.3.
Main results

We discuss the properties of the Lyapunov functions and the Riemann surfaces.
Let below
\zeta\in{\mathcal{R}}
and let

{\mathbb{P}}:{\mathcal{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}}

The matrix
{\mathscr{V}}=\int_{0}^{1}V^{2}dx

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{3}=\|v\|^{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathfrak{b}_{2}={\|v\|^{2}+\sqrt{\mathfrak{b}_{o}}\over 2},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathfrak{b}_{1}={\|v\|^{2}-\sqrt{\mathfrak{b}_{o}}\over 2},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathfrak{b}_{o}=(\|v_{1}\|^{2}-\|v_{2}\|^{2})^{2}+4|c_{12}|^{2},

.

i) Each function

Reamrk.

We recall some well known facts about entire functions (see

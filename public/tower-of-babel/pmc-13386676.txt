Evaluation of TIE-2 levels in newly diagnosed and untreated multiple myeloma patients
Authors: Bilal Aygün, Zeynep Tuğba Karabulut, Elif Suyanı, Mehmet Bankir, Didar Yanardağ Açık
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAngiogenesis plays a crucial role in the pathophysiology of multiple myeloma (MM). Tyrosine kinase with immunoglobulin-like and EGF-like domains 2 (TIE-2) is an important regulator of angiogenesis; however, its diagnostic value in MM has not been fully clarified. This study aimed to evaluate the diagnostic significance of serum TIE-2 levels in newly diagnosed, treatment-naïve patients with MM compared with healthy individuals.MethodsIn this prospective case-control study conducted at Adana City Training and Research Hospital between September 2017 and June 2021, 38 patients diagnosed with MM according to International Myeloma Working Group (IMWG) criteria and 37 healthy volunteers with similar age/gender characteristics were included. Serum TIE-2 levels were measured by ELISA method. Data were statistically analyzed using SPSS 20.0 software.ResultsSerum TIE-2 levels were found to be significantly higher in MM patients compared to the control group (1.4 vs. 0.9 ng/ml; p < 0.001). ROC analysis demonstrated an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.756 (95% CI: 0.643–0.848; p < 0.001). At a cut-off value of > 0.99 ng/mL, the sensitivity was 89.5% and the specificity was 59.5%.ConclusionsElevated serum TIE-2 levels in newly diagnosed MM patients suggest that this marker may reflect angiogenesis-related activation associated with MM biology. However, due to its moderate specificity, TIE-2 should be considered a complementary exploratory biomarker rather than a stand-alone diagnostic tool.Keywords: Multiple myeloma, TIE-2, Angiogenesis, Biomarker, ELISA, Plasma cell disease

Introduction

Multiple myeloma (MM) is an incurable hematological malignancy characterized by the uncontrolled proliferation of malignant plasma cells in the bone marrow [1]. It has been shown that angiogenesis plays an important role in MM. Increased vessel formation and vascular remodeling in the bone marrow are associated with disease progression [2].

The angiopoietin-TIE system is a signaling pathway that plays a critical role in vessel development and stabilization. Angiopoietin-1 (ANG-1) and Angiopoietin-2 (ANG-2) are the major ligands of this system and TIE-2 (Tyrosine kinase with immunoglobulin and epidermal growth factor homology domain 2) is a receptor tyrosine kinase found on vascular endothelial cells and plays a critical role in angiogenesis. TIE-2, located on the cell surface, senses signals from the external environment and activates intracellular signaling pathways. The interaction between TIE-2 and angiopoietins has important functions, especially in the maintenance of vascular integrity and new vessel formation (angiogenesis) [2, 3].

Angiopoietin-1 promotes endothelial cell survival, migration and stabilization of vasculature by activating the TIE-2 receptor, whereas angiopoietin-2 is more involved in angiogenesis and vascular remodeling by modulating TIE-2 signaling through its binding effect [4]. This mechanism is involved in both physiological vascular formation and increased angiogenesis in pathological conditions, for example in tumors and hematological malignancies. It has been reported that the expression of TIE-2 is increased in various solid tumors and hematological malignancies and that it also has antiapoptotic effects [5–7].

Angiopoietins, which have been shown to be involved in mechanisms of angiogenesis in MM patients, are probably expressed and secreted by myeloma cells [8, 9]. Studies investigating the role of TIE-2 in the pathogenesis of MM are very limited in the literature. Therefore, determining the role of TIE-2 in the diagnosis and prognosis of the disease is of great importance both for a better understanding of disease mechanisms and for the identification of possible biomarkers [1].

This study aimed to evaluate the role and diagnostic potential of serum TIE-2 levels in the pathogenesis of multiple myeloma (MM) by comparing patients with MM to healthy controls.

Materials and methods

This prospective case-control study was conducted between September 2017 and June 2021 after obtaining approval (2556/2023) from the Ethics Committee of Adana City Training and Research Hospital. 38 patients diagnosed with MM according to IMWG criteria who had not yet started treatment and 37 healthy volunteers with similar age and gender characteristics were included in the study.

Blood samples were collected in MM patients in the pre-treatment period and in healthy controls without any medical intervention. All samples were centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 10 min, serum was separated and stored at -80 °C until the time of analysis. The ELISA kit used in this study measures circulating soluble TIE-2 (sTIE-2) levels in human serum according to the manufacturer’s protocol (Shanghai Sunred Biological Technology Co. Ltd). Measurements were performed using an automated ELISA reader from Thermo Scientific (Finland) and Scanlt for Multiscan FC 2.5.1 software. The sensitivity and measurement range of the test were determined according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The intra-assay coefficient of variation (CV) was reported to be < 10%, and the inter-assay CV was < 12%. Results were given in ng/mL.

Biochemical and hematologic parameters of the patients at the time of diagnosis were recorded. These parameters include bone marrow plasma cell ratio, total protein, albumin, creatinine, calcium, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), hemoglobin (Hb), white blood cell (WBC), platelet (PLT), IgG, IgA levels and serum/urine free light chain analysis. Appropriate treatment protocol was initiated after diagnosis, and all patients were followed up for 45 months. The follow-up period was calculated as the time from the date of diagnosis to the date of death or last admission.

For individuals to be included in the control group, the history of medication use, surgical intervention, or trauma within the past four weeks was defined as exclusion criteria.

Exclusion criteria for MM patients included the presence of an additional diagnosis of malignancy, cardiovascular disease, diabetes mellitus or metabolic syndrome.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed with IBM SPSS Statistics 20.0. Normal distribution was evaluated by Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Data with normal distribution were presented as mean ± SD, whereas non-normally distributed data were presented as median (min–max). Student t-test, Mann-Whitney U test, ANOVA or Kruskal-Wallis tests were used for intergroup comparisons. Chi-square or Fisher’s exact chi-square tests were used for categorical variables.

The discriminatory power of TIE-2 levels was evaluated by ROC analysis. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Because of the exploratory nature of this prospective pilot study and the limited availability of newly diagnosed untreated MM patients during the study period, a formal a priori sample size calculation was not performed. Therefore, the study may be underpowered, particularly for subgroup and survival analyses, and these findings should be interpreted cautiously.

Results

No significant difference was found between MM and control group in terms of age and gender. The median serum TIE-2 level was significantly higher in the MM group compared to the control group (1.4 vs. 0.9 ng/ml; p < 0.001). MM patients had higher mean total protein (9.1 ± 2.1 vs. 6.7 ± 0.6 g/dL; p < 0.001), lower mean albumin (3.3 ± 0.7 vs. 3.8 ± 0.5 g/dL; p < 0.001) and lower median white blood cell (WBC) counts (6.4 vs. 8.5 × 10⁹/L; p < 0.001) (Table 1).

In the ROC curve analysis, serum TIE-2 levels demonstrated moderate discriminatory performance for differentiating MM patients from healthy controls. The area under the curve (AUC) was 0.756 (95% CI: 0.643–0.848; p < 0.001). At a cut-off value of > 0.99 ng/mL, the sensitivity and specificity were 89.5% and 59.5%, respectively. The positive predictive value (PPV) was 69.4%, and the negative predictive value (NPV) was 84.6% (Fig. 1).

Discussion

Multiple myeloma (MM) is characterized not only by clonal plasma cell proliferation but also by complex alterations in the bone marrow microenvironment. Among these changes, angiogenesis has been recognized as a central mechanism contributing to disease development and progression. Increased microvascular density and vascular remodeling in the bone marrow have been associated with tumor growth and disease progression in MM [10]. Recent studies have further emphasized that interactions between malignant plasma cells and the bone marrow microenvironment play a crucial role in disease biology and therapeutic response [11, 12].

In the present exploratory pilot study, serum TIE-2 levels were found to be significantly higher in newly diagnosed and untreated MM patients compared with healthy controls. This finding supports the hypothesis that angiogenesis-related signaling pathways may already be activated in the early stages of the disease and may be reflected in measurable circulating biomarkers. The angiopoietin–TIE signaling pathway plays an important role in vascular stability, endothelial cell survival, and angiogenesis [3, 4]. Dysregulation of this pathway has been implicated in tumor progression and vascular remodeling in several malignancies [13].

Previous studies have demonstrated that myeloma cells may contribute to angiogenesis in the bone marrow microenvironment through the production of angiopoietins, particularly angiopoietin-1 and angiopoietin-2 [14, 15]. In addition, angiopoietin-2 has been reported to promote MM-associated angiogenesis and may influence disease biology and tumor microenvironment interactions [11, 16]. These findings support the concept that angiogenesis-related pathways are closely linked to the pathophysiology of MM.

At the same time, it is important to interpret these findings cautiously. Increased circulating TIE-2 levels have also been reported in several hematologic malignancies and solid tumors, reflecting enhanced angiogenic activity within the tumor microenvironment [17]. Therefore, TIE-2 should not be considered a disease-specific biomarker for MM. Instead, it may represent a marker of angiogenic activation associated with tumor-related vascular remodeling.

In our ROC curve analysis, serum TIE-2 levels demonstrated moderate discriminatory performance in distinguishing patients with multiple myeloma from healthy controls. Although sensitivity was relatively high, the modest specificity and AUC value limit the immediate clinical applicability of TIE-2 as an isolated biomarker. These findings suggest that TIE-2 may be more appropriately considered a complementary exploratory biomarker rather than a stand-alone diagnostic tool.

Rather, its clinical utility may be greater when evaluated together with other biomarkers associated with plasma cell disorders or angiogenic activity. Previous studies investigating angiogenesis-related biomarkers in MM have similarly suggested that the combined assessment of multiple biomarkers may improve diagnostic performance and better reflect the biological complexity of the disease [18, 19]. Moreover, recent studies focusing on angiogenesis-related genes and biomarkers have emphasized their potential role in improving prognostic and diagnostic stratification in multiple myeloma [20].

Interestingly, serum TIE-2 levels were not significantly correlated with clinical or laboratory parameters such as bone marrow plasma cell percentage, total protein, albumin, or hematologic indices. One possible explanation for this finding is that circulating TIE-2 levels may reflect angiogenic activity within the tumor microenvironment rather than direct indicators of tumor burden. Biomarkers associated with angiogenesis may therefore capture different aspects of disease biology compared with conventional laboratory parameters that primarily reflect plasma cell proliferation.

Recent studies have increasingly emphasized the critical role of the bone marrow microenvironment in MM progression, therapeutic resistance, and disease evolution. Endothelial activation, stromal interactions, vascular niche remodeling, and immune microenvironment signaling are considered central components of this complex biological network. Recent evidence further suggests that endothelial cells, stromal components, and immune-cell interactions within the bone marrow niche contribute not only to MM progression but also to therapeutic resistance and disease evolution [21]. In this context, circulating TIE-2 levels may not simply reflect generalized angiogenesis but could also represent endothelial and microenvironmental activation associated with plasma cell–bone marrow interactions. This interpretation may partly explain the absence of direct correlations between TIE-2 levels and conventional tumor burden parameters observed in our study [11, 12, 21].

We also observed differences in several laboratory parameters between MM patients and healthy controls. Higher total protein levels in the MM group are consistent with increased monoclonal immunoglobulin production, which represents a hallmark feature of the disease. Conversely, relatively lower white blood cell counts may reflect bone marrow involvement by malignant plasma cells and disruption of normal hematopoiesis. These findings are consistent with the known biological characteristics of MM.

Subgroup analyses according to immunoglobulin subtype revealed differences in certain hematologic and biochemical parameters among MM subgroups. However, these variations were not associated with serum TIE-2 levels, suggesting that angiogenesis-related signaling may occur across different disease subtypes. Considering the biological heterogeneity of multiple myeloma, future studies including larger patient cohorts may help clarify whether angiogenesis-related biomarkers vary among specific molecular or cytogenetic subgroups.

Although follow-up data were available in our study, the number of death events observed during follow-up was limited. Consequently, the statistical power of survival analyses was restricted, and no reliable conclusion regarding the prognostic significance of TIE-2 levels could be drawn. Larger prospective studies with longer follow-up periods are required to clarify the potential prognostic relevance of circulating TIE-2 in MM.

Taken together, our findings suggest that circulating TIE-2 may reflect angiogenic activation in multiple myeloma. However, due to its moderate specificity and the fact that increased TIE-2 levels may occur in other malignancies and inflammatory conditions, its clinical value as a stand-alone diagnostic biomarker appears limited. Future studies integrating TIE-2 with additional angiogenesis-related or plasma cell-related biomarkers may provide a more comprehensive understanding of its potential role in the diagnosis and biological characterization of multiple myeloma.

Limitations of the study

This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings. First, the relatively small sample size and the single-center design may limit the generalizability of the results. Studies involving larger and more diverse patient populations are therefore needed to confirm the associations observed in this study.

Second, the lack of disease control groups, such as patients with monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance (MGUS) or other hematologic malignancies, represents another important limitation. Including such comparison groups would allow a more accurate assessment of whether increased circulating TIE-2 levels are specific to multiple myeloma or reflect a broader biological response associated with angiogenesis, inflammation, or tumor burden. Future studies that incorporate MGUS and other plasma cell disorders may help clarify the potential role of TIE-2 in disease development and progression.

Third, detailed cytogenetic risk information and direct markers of bone marrow angiogenesis were not available for all patients in this study. As a result, it was not possible to evaluate the relationship between circulating TIE-2 levels and well-established indicators of disease biology, such as high-risk cytogenetic abnormalities or bone marrow microvascular density.

The relatively limited cohort size and low number of events prevented the performance of robust multivariable regression analyses. Therefore, the independence of serum TIE-2 levels from potential confounding clinical variables could not be reliably established.

In addition, the number of death events observed during the follow-up period was limited.

This reduced the statistical power of the survival analyses and prevented firm conclusions regarding the prognostic value of TIE-2 levels. Larger prospective studies with longer follow-up periods will be necessary to determine whether circulating TIE-2 levels have meaningful prognostic significance in multiple myeloma.

Finally, it should be noted that elevated TIE-2 levels have also been reported in various other malignancies and inflammatory conditions. Therefore, the specificity of TIE-2 as a biomarker for multiple myeloma may be limited. In this context, TIE-2 should be considered a potential complementary biomarker rather than a disease-specific diagnostic marker. Future research combining TIE-2 with other angiogenesis-related or plasma cell–associated biomarkers may help provide a more comprehensive understanding of its potential clinical relevance.

Conclusion

In conclusion, serum TIE-2 levels were significantly elevated in newly diagnosed and untreated patients with multiple myeloma compared with healthy controls. These findings support the concept that angiogenesis-related pathways may contribute to the biological microenvironment of MM. However, given the moderate discriminatory performance, limited specificity, and relatively small cohort size, TIE-2 should currently be regarded as a complementary exploratory biomarker rather than a stand-alone diagnostic marker. Larger multicenter prospective studies incorporating disease-control groups and multivariable analyses are needed to further clarify the potential diagnostic and prognostic relevance of TIE-2 in MM.

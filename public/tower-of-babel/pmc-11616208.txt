Epidemiology and outcomes of septic shock in Thai children: a nationwide retrospective study from 2015 to 2022
Authors: Sirapoom Niamsanit, Phanthila Sitthikarnkha, Leelawadee Techasatian, Suchaorn Saengnipanthkul, Rattapon Uppala
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPaediatric septic shock is a formidable challenge worldwide that significantly impacts health care systems. This nationwide retrospective study analyses the prevalence and mortality rates of paediatric septic shock across Thailand from 2015 to 2022, focusing on hospital burdens, including mechanical ventilation and renal replacement therapy.MethodsThe study included paediatric patients ranging from infants to individuals under 18 years of age who were admitted to hospitals due to septic shock across Thailand. Data were retrospectively gathered from the Thai National Health Security Office for the years 2015–2022. The data included demographic data, clinical outcomes, and hospital burden indicators such as mechanical ventilation and renal replacement therapy.ResultsFrom 2015 to 2022, there were 30,718 paediatric admissions for septic shock, with a peak in 2019. The highest incidence was observed in infants, accounting for 44.7% of the cases. The prevalence rate increased from 190 per 100,000 population in 2015 to a peak of 280 per 100,000 population in 2020. Mortality rates decreased from 30.7% in 2015 to 20.2% in 2022, with a peak of 40.5% observed in the central region in 2015. The study highlighted a substantial health care burden, with 34.9% of patients requiring prolonged mechanical ventilation and 23.4% needing renal replacement therapy.ConclusionsDespite a decrease in mortality, paediatric septic shock remains a significant burden on the health care system in Thailand. Urgent improvements in resources and adherence to clinical guidelines, especially in under-resourced areas, are necessary. Addressing disparities in mortality and resource usage across hospital levels is vital for improving outcomes and reducing the health care burden of paediatric patients with septic shock.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13054-024-05193-7.Keywords: Paediatric septic shock, Epidemiology, Mortality rates, Mechanical ventilation, Renal replacement therapy

Introduction

Septic shock is a critical and life-threatening condition that represents a significant global health challenge, particularly impacting children and presenting a high burden in both resource-rich and resource-limited settings [1, 2]. Studies conducted worldwide, including regions in Asia, indicate that the prevalence and mortality rates associated with septic shock remain substantial [3–5]. Globally, the incidence varies, and variations in health care resources and practices contribute to fluctuating prevalence rates; however, septic shock is consistently associated with high mortality rates, reported to be as high as 50% in some regions [6–10].

The implementation of clinical guidelines for managing septic shock has been shown to significantly improve outcomes by standardizing care and facilitating timely interventions [4, 11]. However, these benefits often vary across different health care systems and are influenced by the level of health care resources available in each country [2]. In high-income countries, comprehensive adherence to guidelines has been associated with improved survival rates and reduced morbidity [7]. Conversely, low- to middle-income countries often face challenges in guideline implementation, resulting in varied outcomes [3, 4].

A pivotal study conducted in Thailand in 2024, which included a nationwide survey, revealed that despite access to guidelines, compliance remains insufficient across various hospitals [12]. These discrepancies in guideline adherence underscore the continued challenge of uniformly implementing effective septic shock management throughout the country, which directly impacts patient outcomes [13].

Recognizing these challenges, our decision to initiate this study was motivated by the urgent need to evaluate and enhance the current implementation strategies of septic shock guidelines in Thailand. By assessing the existing gaps and identifying areas for improvement, this study aims to provide valuable insights that could lead to better clinical outcomes and reduced mortality rates in paediatric patients suffering from septic shock across diverse health care settings.

This study fills the above-referenced knowledge gaps by offering the first nationwide analysis of paediatric septic shock across all hospital levels in Thailand from 2015 to 2022. This research provides essential insights into the prevalence and mortality of septic shock and highlights the hospital burden due to critical interventions such as mechanical ventilation and renal replacement therapy. The dataset, encompassing all hospital levels, offers a realistic perspective on the health care challenges posed by paediatric septic shock in Thailand, aiming to inform policy-making and improve clinical outcomes.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

A retrospective observational study utilized data from the National Health Security Office (NHSO) on hospitalized children ranging from infants to individuals under 18 years of age from January 2015 to December 2022 and was based on Thailand's Universal Coverage (UC) scheme. Patients with neonatal sepsis were excluded from our analysis. The UC scheme serves as Thailand's main social health insurance program, encompassing approximately 72% of the population [14]. Data obtained from the NHSO underwent a data auditorial system by the government prior to reimbursement to each hospital. The data of children hospitalized for septic shock were extracted via the International Statistical Classification of Diseases and Related Health Problems, 10th Revision, Thai Modification (ICD-10-TM) code R57.2. The Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) checklist was used to report the results of this study [15].

Demographic data collection

Data on age, sex, year of admission, hospital region, and hospital level were collected. In Thailand, care is provided through private, primary, secondary, and tertiary hospitals. The outcomes of septic shock, including acute respiratory failure and acute renal failure, hospital length of stay (LOS), and discharge status, were evaluated.

Patients diagnosed with acute respiratory failure were classified via the International Classification of Diseases, Ninth Revision, Clinical Modification (ICD-9-CM) code 96.72, which signifies a requirement for continuous mechanical ventilation exceeding a duration of 96 consecutive hours. Furthermore, patients with acute renal failure were categorized through ICD-9-CM codes 39.95, 54.29, and 54.98, which denote the necessity for renal replacement therapy options, including peritoneal dialysis, haemodialysis, or continuous renal replacement therapy, as applicable.

Statistical analyses

The data were analysed via STATA software version 10 (StataCorp, 2007. Stata Statistical Software: Release 10. College Station, TX: StataCorp LP.). Qualitative data are presented as numbers and percentages, whereas quantitative data are presented as the means with standard deviations (SDs) or medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs) on the basis of the data distribution. Information on septic shock prevalence and mortality rates was also collected. The annual mortality rate was calculated by dividing the number of paediatric septic shock patients who died by the total number of recorded septic shock cases in the NHSO system. We examined the differences between surviving and nonsurviving patients via chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests for categorical variables and independent t tests for continuous variables. Patients were categorized into five age groups (under 1 year, 1 to under 5 years, 5 to under 10 years, 10 to under 15 years, and 15 to under 18 years) to assess differences in mortality rates. P values < 0.05 were considered to indicate statistical significance.

Ethics approval

This study was reviewed and approved by the institutional review board of the Khon Kaen University Human Research Ethics Committee (#HE671327). The consent form was waived due to the use of anonymized data.

Regionally, Northeast Thailand was the most affected region, accounting for 37.5% of the cases, followed by South Thailand (20.1%), Central Thailand (19.9%), North Thailand (14.0%), and Bangkok (8.5%). Prevalence and mortality rates in hospitalized children due to septic shock from 2015–2022

From 2015 to 2022, the prevalence of paediatric septic shock in Thailand showed variable trends, initially recorded at 190 per 100,000 people and reaching a peak of 280 per 100,000 individuals in 2020, before a slight decline to 270 per 100,000 individuals in 2022, as illustrated in Fig. 2. Bangkok had the highest recorded prevalence, peaking at 330 per 100,000 in 2017 and later decreasing to 250 per 100,000 by 2022. Other regions, such as Northeast Thailand, South Thailand, and North Thailand, also showed fluctuations in prevalence rates, with Northeast Thailand increasing to 290 per 100,000 by 2022 and South Thailand reaching the highest rate, at 340 per 100,000. Conversely, the Central region demonstrated greater stability, ranging from 150 to 250 per 100,000 over the study period.

Trends in the prevalence and mortality rates of paediatric septic shock by region in Thailand, 2015–2022

The overall mortality rate began at 30.7% in 2015 and followed a downwards trajectory to 20.2% by 2022. Among the regions, the Central region presented the highest initial mortality rate, at 40.5% in 2015, which then decreased to 20.5% by 2022. Similarly, the North and South regions started with mortality rates of 35.5% in 2015, which decreased to 22.5% and 23.5%, respectively, by 2022. The Northeast region had the lowest mortality rates, from 25.7% in 2015 to 16.5% in 2022.

Clinical outcomes and hospital mortality related to paediatric septic shock in Thailand

Statistically significant differences in mortality rates were observed across various age groups. Notably, the mortality rate was particularly high among infants, with 50.5% of deceased patients falling within this age group. Males made up 56.1% of the deceased patients, and the majority of deaths occurred in tertiary hospitals (75.6%). The presence of comorbid conditions significantly influenced mortality. Respiratory disorders were most prevalent among the deceased patients (64.8%), followed by metabolic disorders (65.3%) and cardiovascular disorders (30.0%). In many patients who died, the source of infection was unknown (50.2%), but where identified, respiratory tract infections were most common (44.2%) and significantly impacted patient outcomes. The need for prolonged mechanical ventilation and renal replacement therapy was also correlated with higher mortality rates; 45.7% of deceased patients, compared with 31.2% of survivors, had required prolonged ventilation, and 37.8% of deceased patients, compared with 18.0% of survivors, had needed renal replacement therapy. Extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) was utilized in a small fraction of patients (n = 115), with a notably high mortality rate, exceeding 50%.

Discussion

The present nationwide retrospective study provides compelling insights into the epidemiology and management outcomes of paediatric septic shock in Thailand from 2015 to 2022, highlighting its persistent severity and impact on health care systems globally. Paediatric septic shock remains a critical concern worldwide because of its high mortality rates and the significant health care resources required for management [2, 10, 11, 13]. This condition is particularly burdensome in low- to middle-income countries where resource constraints and variability in health care practices can exacerbate outcomes [3–5, 16].

In Thailand, the prevalence of paediatric septic shock has shown an increasing trend, with rates peaking at 280 per 100,000 people in 2020. This number is considerably higher than that reported in several other countries, where systematic adherence to guidelines and better health care infrastructure may lead to more favourable epidemiological figures [17]. This disparity underscores the need for continuous monitoring and adaptation of health care practices in Thailand to address the rising trends effectively.

The mortality rate for septic shock in Thailand markedly declined from 30.7% in 2015 to 20.2% in 2022. However, this rate is still higher than recent findings from European and American studies, which often report mortality rates below 20% due to strong health care systems and effective implementation of clinical guidelines [10, 18]. Notably, our study highlights significant regional disparities in the prevalence and outcomes of paediatric septic shock. Compared with the other regions, the Northeast region, which accounted for the highest burden of cases, presented the lowest mortality rate over the study period. This discrepancy may be attributed to factors such as inconsistency in protocol adherence and the variability in health care delivery noted in general physician practices across Thailand [12]. Previous data suggest that adherence to the surviving sepsis campaign guidelines can significantly reduce the mortality rate [4]. This emphasizes that rapid identification and management are crucial and that enhancing protocol awareness and education among health care providers could be instrumental in further reducing mortality.

Our study also revealed that children under one year of age and those with comorbidities exhibited a significantly high mortality rate, underscoring the vulnerability of these groups. The supporting literature indicates that younger patients, especially those with cardiovascular and respiratory comorbidities, are at increased risk of poor outcomes in septic shock [19–21]. These findings suggest that age-specific treatment protocols and more aggressive management strategies may be necessary to increase survival in this high-risk group. Additionally, in our analysis, most paediatric septic shock patients had an unknown infection source, which is consistent with previous studies indicating that unidentified infection sources are common in paediatric sepsis patients [22]. This uncertainty can lead to inadequate or delayed source control, which may contribute to higher mortality rates [23, 24]. Among cases with an identified infection source, respiratory infections were the most frequent, mirroring other studies that reported the respiratory tract as a predominant site for invasive bacterial infections in septic shock, which is often associated with increased mortality [22].

Previous studies conducted within Thailand, particularly those involving multicentre analyses at tertiary care hospitals, reported better outcomes but were unable to reflect the situation at the primary and secondary care levels[4]. Our study adds substantial value by encompassing comprehensive data across all hospital levels, presenting a true representation of septic shock management and outcome disparities across the health care spectrum in Thailand. These real-world data underscore the need for tailored interventions at each health care level to address identified gaps and unify treatment standards.

The associated health care burden remains significant, with over one-third of affected children requiring mechanical ventilation and nearly one-quarter needing renal replacement therapy, which are treatments typically provided in secondary and tertiary hospitals equipped with advanced facilities. These interventions highlight the severity of septic shock and the critical need for resource allocation, particularly in underresourced settings. Additionally, the higher mortality rates observed in these hospitals, which manage over 80% of cases, reflect the aggregation of more severe cases in higher-level health care institutions. In Thailand, ECMO is utilized in only a small fraction of refractory paediatric septic shock cases, as it is not covered under the Universal Coverage Scheme, and few centres are equipped to provide this treatment. Despite its limited use, the mortality rate for patients receiving ECMO exceeds 50%, which aligns with findings from a previous study [25]. Given the complexity and resource-intensive nature of care for these patients, it would be wise for future studies to investigate the specific factors contributing to mortality and unfavourable outcomes, which could guide the development of targeted strategies aimed at early intervention and prevention.

Despite the observed decline in mortality rates, the enduring high demand for mechanical ventilation and renal replacement therapy underscores the ongoing health care burden associated with managing severe septic shock cases. Remarkably, our findings indicate that nearly half of the patients requiring prolonged mechanical ventilation and renal replacement therapy succumb to their conditions. This observation is consistent with previous research indicating that mortality rates are significantly high among patients with septic shock who develop acute kidney injury, volume overload, and multiorgan failure during treatment, necessitating continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT) and extended mechanical ventilation [26–28]. Furthermore, existing studies have shown that multiorgan dysfunction, which is prevalent among paediatric patients undergoing CRRT, is correlated with mortality rates exceeding 50% [29]. These findings emphasize the importance of enhancing paediatric critical care capacities and developing region-specific interventions to manage septic shock effectively at earlier stages to achieve favourable outcomes [30]. Importantly, this study highlights significant health care disparities across regions and hospital levels, suggesting that improvements in the health care system could further reduce morbidity and mortality in paediatric septic shock patients.

Limitations

Our study has certain limitations. Its retrospective design makes it susceptible to potential coding errors, whereas the absence of a control group limits comparative insights. Septic shock cases or the burden of septic shock patients in terms of the need for prolonged mechanical ventilation and renal replacement therapy may be underreported. The reliance on hospital data could distort the actual incidence rates. Although the database contains minimal amounts of missing data, the classification of certain items via the ICD-10 may be inaccurate. Moreover, the use of ICD-10 codes for classifying conditions may not accurately reflect the clinical severity or distinct characteristics of septic shock as defined by current guidelines. This limitation restricts the precise tracking of administered therapies such as fluid boluses, vasoactive and inotropic agents, and steroids and complicates the correlation of specific infections with septic shock outcomes. These factors highlight the need for future studies to address these gaps and improve the generalizability and applicability of findings across various regions and settings.

Conclusions

Despite a commendable decrease in mortality rates of paediatric septic shock in Thailand, this condition continues to impose a significant burden on health care resources, particularly with substantial needs for mechanical ventilation and renal replacement therapy. Notably, regional disparities underscore the necessity for more uniform implementation of clinical guidelines and resource distribution to improve patient outcomes. The findings call for focused improvements in health care strategies, with an emphasis on education, protocol adherence, and targeted policies to close existing gaps. By fostering enhanced care delivery and uniform treatment standards across all health care levels, further reductions in mortality and improvements in the quality of care for children affected by septic shock can be achieved.

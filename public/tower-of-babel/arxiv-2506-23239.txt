Half-life of 136 Xe for neutrinoless double- β \beta decay calculated with effective axial-vector current coupling unified for two-neurtino and neutrinoless double- β \beta decay modes
Jun Terasaki, Osvaldo Civitarese

Abstract
The upper limit on the mass of the Majorana neutrino, extracted from the limits on the nonobservation of the neutrinoless double- β \beta ( 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta ) decay, is hampered by uncertainties in the matrix elements of the transition operators. Recently, we have shown that the values of the effective axial-vector current coupling constants ( g A eff g_{A}^{\mathrm{eff}} ) for the 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta and the two-neutrino double- β \beta decays are close. This striking result was obtained for the first time by including vertex corrections and two-body currents in these matrix elements. In this letter, we calculate the half-life for the 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta decay ( T 1 / 2 0 ​ ν T_{1/2}^{0\nu} ) of 136 Xe using this closeness and show the convergence of the half-life with respect to the variation of the method to determine g A eff g_{A}^{\mathrm{eff}} . The closeness of the g A eff g_{A}^{\mathrm{eff}} of the two decay modes plays a decisive role in predicting T 1 / 2 0 ​ ν T_{1/2}^{0\nu} . The appropriate value of g A eff g_{A}^{\mathrm{eff}} depends on the calculation method, and g A eff g_{A}^{\mathrm{eff}} is close to one in our perturbation calculation.



The accurate prediction of the half-life for the neutrinoless double-
\beta
(
0\nu\beta\beta
) decay (
T_{1/2}^{0\nu}
) is a highly demanded task. It has called the attention of experimentalists and theorists for decades
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
, and it is in our days a matter of intense activity in both fields. The observation of the
0\nu\beta\beta
decay is the evidence of the existence of the Majorana neutrino
[
4
]
, and this observation indicates new physics beyond the standard model if a right-handed neutrino is also involved
[
5
]
. A strong foundation would be given to leptogenesis
[
6
]
, and the impact of such a discovery would reflect
upon new developments in particle physics, astroparticle physics and cosmology.

The calculation of
T_{1/2}^{0\nu}
of the
0\nu\beta\beta
decay requires the knowledge of four factors: i) the nuclear matrix elements (NME) of the operator acting on the nucleus in the decay, ii) the axial-vector current coupling constant (
g_{A}
), iii) the electron contribution (the phase space factor), and iv) the value of the effective mass of the Majorana neutrino
\langle m_{\nu}\rangle

[
4
]
. For the last factor, only weak constraints on the relation of
\langle m_{\nu}\rangle
and the eigen neutrino masses are known.
T_{1/2}^{0\nu}
can be calculated if a value of
\langle m_{\nu}\rangle
is assumed. If
T_{1/2}^{0\nu}
is measured, inversely,
\langle m_{\nu}\rangle
can be deduced. The main difficulty in the relation between
T_{1/2}^{0\nu}
and
\langle m_{\nu}\rangle
is due to the uncertainty which originates in the theoretical-method dependence of the NME. Among the approximations for the nuclear wave functions used in the NME calculations are the shell model
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
, the ab initio approach
[
11
]
, the quasiparticle random-phase approximation (QRPA)
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
, the interacting boson model
[
16
]
, and the energy density functional approach
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
. The NMEs obtained with these methods show a large dispersion
[
20
,
3
]
, which is reflected by the estimates of
T_{1/2}^{0\nu}
and
\langle m_{\nu}\rangle
. It is usual for the known weak decays to use an effective
g_{A}
(
g_{A}^{\mathrm{eff}}
) to reproduce the half-life
[
21
]
, while the vector current coupling constant
g_{V}
is always set to one. Concerning
g_{A}
, an unconfirmed speculation is possible that
g_{A}^{\mathrm{eff}}
for the
0\nu\beta\beta
and the other weak decay modes are quite different because the Majorana neutrino in the
0\nu\beta\beta
decay is a virtual particle.

We calculated the NME for the
0\nu\beta\beta
and the two-neutrino (2
\nu
)
\beta\beta

136
Xe, using the transition operator perturbed in the lowest order by the nucleon-nucleon (NN) potential

22

∗

[
15

[
25

\beta\beta
decay. We also calculated in our previous study
g_{A}^{\mathrm{eff}}
by referring to the half-life calculated with the perturbed NME and the bare
g_{A}
= 1.267. This method is applicable for both the
0\nu\beta\beta
and
2\nu\beta\beta
NME on the same footing. It turned out that
g_{A}^{\mathrm{eff}}
for the
0\nu\beta\beta
and
2\nu\beta\beta
NME are close in the representative calculation. This is remarkable because of the possible speculation opposite to our finding. The purpose of this article is to establish the convergence of the
T_{1/2}^{0\nu}
calculated using our finding. In other words, the closeness of
g_{A}^{\mathrm{eff}}
for the two decay modes is crucial for the prediction of
T_{1/2}^{0\nu}
. This is investigated by variations of the method to calculate
g_{A}^{\mathrm{eff}}
. In the first half part of this article, we summarize the findings of Ref.
[
22
]
. In the second half part, we examine the convergence of the half-life.

We obtained

This is remarkable because the neutrino momenta

and

is possible because

is obtained by the ratio of NMEs, and the neutrino potential is included in both the numerator and the denominator

As we discussed in our previous article
[
22
]
, SGII has a too strong attractive effect on the binding energy, and it is important to take this into account because our correction terms depend on the NN potential explicitly. The stronger interaction causes more perturbation, which causes longer half-lives. This is reflected in the relation of the SGII and the SkM
∗
calculations; see Fig.
2
. Therefore, the SkM
∗
calculation is more reliable than the SGII calculation. Let us focus on the former calculation. We used a few approximations in our perturbed-NME calculations. Above all, the higher-order corrections are ignored. Thus, it is inferred that more perturbation is necessary on top of the SkM
∗
calculation of Comparison 2. We assume that the
g_{A}^{\mathrm{bare}}
with the true GT and Fermi components can give the correct half-life. The half-lives of Comparisons 3
-
5 are obtained with more perturbation than that of Comparison 2, and they are in the same order of 10
31
y. The experimental
2\nu\beta\beta
half-life used for Comparisons 4 and 5 is a nonperturbative value. This stability against the difference in the method is a positive indication of its correctness. The closeness of the results of Comparisons 4 and 5 implies the possibility that the half-life of this estimation method is converged because of the nonperturbative calibration if the effects of the residual interaction are small. Our most reliable estimation of the
0\nu\beta\beta
half-life is concluded to be those of Comparisons 3
-
5, that is, (1.3
-
3.0)
\times
10
31
y with assumed
\langle m_{\nu}\rangle
= 1 meV.

In summary, we have calculated the half-life of
136
Xe for the
0\nu\beta\beta
decay, including the perturbation for the transition operator. Our GT and Fermi NMEs were derived by applying the second-order Rayleigh-Schrödinger perturbation theory and choosing the terms corresponding to the diagrams. A new estimation of
g_{A}^{\mathrm{eff}}
for the
0\nu\beta\beta

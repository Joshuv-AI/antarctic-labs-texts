Lee–Yang phenomena in edge-coloured graph counting
Maximilian Wiesmann

Abstract
We study the accumulation of zeros of a polynomial arising from the enumeration of edge-coloured graphs along certain limit curves. The polynomial is a variant of an edge-chromatic polynomial, which specialises to the partition function of the ferromagnetic Ising model on a random regular graph. We call this accumulation behaviour a Lee–Yang phenomenon in analogy with the Lee–Yang theorem. The limiting loci are semialgebraic and arise from anti-Stokes curves of an exponential integral.



1
Introduction

In this paper we study the univariate polynomial

A^{V}_{n}(\lambda):=\sum_{G\in\mathcal{G}_{-n}}\frac{1}{|\Aut(G)|}\prod_{v\in V_{G}}\Lambda_{\deg(v)}(\lambda)\in\mathbb{C}[\lambda].

(1)

Here,
\mathcal{G}_{-n}
denotes the set of isomorphism classes of edge-coloured graphs
G
with Euler characteristic
\chi(G)=|V_{G}|-|E_{G}|=-n
, and
\Lambda_{(w_{1},\dots,w_{d})}(\lambda)
are vertex markings which associate to each vertex that is incident to
w_{i}
half-edges of the
i^{\text{th}}
colour a univariate polynomial in
\lambda
(where
d
is the number of edge colours). We assume that for
\bm{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\geq 0}
, only finitely many
\Lambda_{\bm{w}}
are nonzero, and we conveniently store them in the polynomial

V(\bm{x},\lambda)=\sum_{\bm{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\geq 0},\,|\bm{w}|\geq 1}\Lambda_{\bm{w}}(\lambda)\frac{\bm{x}^{\bm{w}}}{\bm{w}!}\in\mathbb{C}[\lambda][x_{1},\dots,x_{d}].

Throughout the paper, we consider the case where
V
is homogeneous of degree
k
in
\bm{x}
. This restricts the sum in (
1
) to a sum over certain
k
-regular graphs.

The polynomial
A^{V}_{n}
can be regarded as a variant of an edge-chromatic polynomial. Moreover, for a certain choice of
V
,
A^{V}_{n}
is an average partition function of the ferromagnetic Ising model on a regular graph. This statement is made precise in Section
5
.

Our main focus is on the zeros of
A^{V}_{n}(\lambda)
for complex
\lambda
. As
n
increases, one observes a rather flummoxing accumulation of the zeros along certain limit curves, as in Figure
1
.

Figure 1:
The roots of
A^{V}_{n}(\lambda)
in the complex
\lambda
-plane, for
V(x_{1},x_{2},\lambda)=\frac{x_{1}^{4}}{4!}+\lambda\frac{x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}}{2!\cdot 2!}+\lambda^{2}\frac{x_{2}^{4}}{4!}
.

The red crosses denote phase transitions (points of non-analyticity) of the asymptotics of
A^{V}_{n}(\lambda)
for
n\rightarrow\infty.
The goal of this paper is the description of the limit curves. Below we state an informal version of our main result, Theorem
4.1
.

Main Theorem

(informal)
.

Under mild non-degeneracy assumptions on
V
, the zeros of
A^{V}_{n}
accumulate along parts of anti-Stokes curves as
n\rightarrow\infty
, except for possibly finitely many isolated points.
Those limit curves are given as follows. Let
\mathbb{S}=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\,:\,x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{d}^{2}=1\}
and let
\crit_{\mathbb{S}}(V)
be the set of critical points
\sigma
of
V
restricted to
\mathbb{S}
such that
V(\sigma)\neq 0
.
Then, for
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\crit_{\mathbb{S}}(V)
, an anti-Stokes curve is given by the condition

\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,\mathfrak{R}\log{\left.\kern-1.2ptV(\bm{x},\lambda)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\bm{x}=\sigma}}=\mathfrak{R}\log{\left.\kern-1.2ptV(\bm{x},\lambda)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\bm{x}=\sigma^{\prime}}}\right\}.

The accumulation of zeros is reminiscent of the celebrated Lee–Yang theorem
[
LY52
]
from statistical physics. It states that the zeros of the ferromagnetic Ising model partition function on any finite graph all lie on the imaginary axis, when the partition function is viewed as a function in a magnetic field parameter.
As the system size increases, the set of accumulation points is exactly the imaginary axis. The importance of this theorem stems from the philosophy that partition function zeros reveal the location of phase transitions
[
Bis+04
]
.
Since the 50s, the theory has been generalised in many directions, for example by considering zeros in different physical parameters, e.g. in the complex temperature leading to Fisher zeros
[
Fis65
]
, or by extending the class of lattice models
[
LS81
]
. A very general and rigorous treatment of partition function zeros has been given in
[
Bis+04
]
.

Lee–Yang theory has also received a lot of interest from combinatorialists. In
[
BB09
]
the authors use stable polynomials to prove the Lee–Yang theorem.
The zeros of chromatic polynomials (a.k.a. Potts model partition functions) and their accumulation have been studied in
[
Sok04
]
: Sokal constructs a countable family of graphs such that the roots of their chromatic polynomials are dense in the complex plane, except for a small disc.

The key step in proving the main theorem is the exponential integral representation

A^{V}_{n}(\lambda)=\frac{2^{nM+(d-2)/2}\left(nM+\frac{d-2}{2}\right)!}{(2\pi)^{d/2}(nK)!}\int_{S^{d-1}}V(\bm{x},\lambda)^{nK}\,\omega_{S^{d-1}},

(2)

where
nK
and
nM
are integers depending on the regularity
k
of the graphs (Proposition
2.6
). For real parameters
\lambda
, the limit
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}A^{V}_{n}(\lambda)
can simply be taken by performing a Laplace expansion around the maxima of
|V(\bm{x},\lambda)|
on
S^{d-1}
. However, for complex
\lambda
, an analytic continuation of (
2
) by means of Picard–Lefschetz theory becomes necessary in the large-
n
limit. Such an analytic continuation does not exist globally, but on certain domains
D\subset\mathbb{C}
. On each such domain, the integral from (
2
) can be written as

\sum_{\sigma\in\mathrm{crit}_{\mathbb{S}}(V)}c_{\sigma}(\lambda)\int_{\Gamma_{\sigma}}V(\bm{x},\lambda)^{nK}\,\omega_{S^{d-1}},

(3)

where
\Gamma_{\sigma}
are steepest descent contours associated to each critical point, also known as Lefschetz thimbles, and
c_{\sigma}(\lambda)
are complex coefficients. Here, one passes to the complex compactification
\mathbb{S}
of the real sphere
S^{d-1}
. Each integral in (
3
) admits a stationary phase approximation that asymptotically reduces to an evaluation at the critical point in the
n\rightarrow\infty
limit.
The coefficients
c_{\sigma}(\lambda)
encode topological information and are only analytic within each domain
D
but can jump if one leaves
D
. This is referred to as the Stokes’ phenomenon, see e.g.
[
Pha85
]
. Each domain
D
is a connected component of the complement of the arrangement of Stokes curves of the integral in (
2
).

To derive the decomposition (
3
), one needs to show that the Lefschetz thimbles form a basis of the appropriate homology group. Here, a technical difficulty arises because for many
V
of interest (in particular, the ones related to the Ising model considered in Section
5
), several critical points yield the same evaluation of
V
, thus violating a commonly found assumption in Picard–Lefschetz theory, see e.g.
[
DH02
, Hypothesis H4]
.
To remedy this problem we use a construction by Matsubara
[
Mat23
]
to find a basis of Lefschetz thimbles and derive the expression (
3
).
This is done in Section
3
, culminating in Theorem
3.3
. The stationary phase formula then gives the asymptotic expression (Corollary
3.4
)

\int_{S^{d-1}}V(\bm{x},\lambda)^{n}\,\omega_{S^{d-1}}\sim\sum_{\sigma\in\crit_{\mathbb{S}}(V)}\tilde{c}_{\sigma}(\lambda)e^{n\log{\left.\kern-1.2ptV(\bm{x},\lambda)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\bm{x}=\sigma}}}(1+o(n^{-1}))\quad(n\to\infty).

(4)

A sum decomposition such as (
4
) is the starting point for the works
[
Sok04
,
Bis+04
]
. In the physics context, the quantities
\log{\left.\kern-1.2ptV(\bm{x},\lambda)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\bm{x}=\sigma}}
can be thought of as metastable free energies. Zeros then accumulate along regions of multiphase coexistence, meaning that several free energies have the same magnitude.
Indeed, we can apply a generalisation of the Beraha–Kahane–Weiss theorem due to Sokal
[
Sok04
, Theorem 1.5]
to conclude that the zeros of
A^{V}_{n}(\lambda)
accumulate along (parts of) the curves defined by

\mathfrak{R}\log{\left.\kern-1.2ptV(\bm{x},\lambda)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\bm{x}=\sigma}}=\mathfrak{R}\log{\left.\kern-1.2ptV(\bm{x},\lambda)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\bm{x}=\sigma^{\prime}}}\quad\left(\Leftrightarrow|V(\sigma,\lambda)|=|V(\sigma^{\prime},\lambda)|\right),

(5)

where
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
are critical points of
V
on
\mathbb{S}
. Here, we need to slightly modify Sokal’s result to accommodate for the fact that analyticity of the coefficients
\tilde{c}_{\sigma}(\lambda)
in (
4
) can only be guaranteed in a one-sided neighbourhood of the accumulation points (Section
4
).

Figure 2:
Anti-Stokes curves (green and purple) and Stokes curves (grey) for the example depicted in Figure
1
. The roots accumulate along the intersection of the anti-Stokes curves with certain regions in the complement of the Stokes curves (non-shaded regions).

The curve defined in (
5
) is known as an anti-Stokes curves of the integral (
2
), i.e. a curve where the contribution from different thimble integrals from (
3
) exchange dominance.
As mentioned before, the zeros only accumulate along parts of the curves defined by (
5
). This comes from the fact that (
3
) is not globally valid.
In fact, not necessarily all critical points
\sigma\in\crit_{\mathbb{S}}(V)
contribute to (
3
) for a given
\lambda
. The loci where the
c_{\sigma}
(and
\tilde{c}_{\sigma}
) can potentially become zero are given by the Stokes curves defined by

\mathfrak{I}{\left.\kern-1.2ptV(\bm{x},\lambda)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\bm{x}=\sigma}}=\arg V(\sigma,\lambda)\overset{!}{=}\arg V(\sigma^{\prime},\lambda)=\mathfrak{I}{\left.\kern-1.2ptV(\bm{x},\lambda)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\bm{x}=\sigma}}.

(6)

The accumulation of zeros is only along the intersection of the curves defined by (
5
) with those regions in the complement of the curves defined by (
6
) where both thimbles that exchange dominance on the anti-Stokes curve also contribute to the integral (
3
).
This is illustrated in Figure
2
for the example from Figure
1
. The green and purple curves are the anti-Stokes curves, the grey curves are the Stokes curves. The accumulation as in Figure
1
only happens along the intersection of the anti-Stokes curves with the non-shaded regions.

The idea of a relation between Lee–Yang (or Fisher) zeros and anti-Stokes curves has appeared before in different physics contexts:
[
KT15
]
observes such a relation numerically for a partition function in quantum field theory, and
[
IPZ83
,
Dol+01
,
JJS01
]
use the idea to study zeros of Ising model partition functions.
The paper
[
Dol+01
]
should be highlighted here as it studies partition function zeros for the Ising model on random 3- and 4-regular graphs, thus being closely related to our discussion in Section
5
.

Outline of the article.
In Section
2
we explain the framework of half-edge labelled coloured graphs, how the polynomial
A_{n}^{V}
naturally arises in the enumeration of such graphs (Corollary 2.4), and we prove the representation of
A_{n}^{V}
as an exponential integral (Proposition
2.6
). Section
3
is devoted to the Lefschetz thimble construction. We recall the construction from
[
Mat23
]
and adapt it to our context. The main results are the existence of a basis of Lefschetz thimbles (Theorem
3.3
) and the resulting asymptotic expression (Corollary
3.4
). The proof of the main theorem (Theorem
4.1
) is completed in Section
4
. In the final Section
5
we explain how our framework can be seen as a unified approach to Lee–Yang and Fisher zeros of the Ising model on a random regular graph.

2
Edge-coloured graphs

In this section we explain the construction of edge-coloured graphs via half-edge labels. This also appears in
[
BMW26
]
, where we use it to obtain asymptotic numbers of proper edge-colourings. In the case of bicoloured graphs, the construction has already been described in
[
BMW25
]
.
For us, a graph
G
is a finite, one-dimensional CW complex, i.e. we allow for self-loops and multiple edges. Although in combinatorics it might be more common to define graphs as simplicial complexes, the use of CW complexes is very natural, both in mathematics or physics.
In particular, in the context of quantum field theory, Feynman graphs are naturally identified as CW complexes
[
BIZ80
]
.
Additionally, to each edge of the graph we assign one of
d
colours.
To encode
G
using only discrete data we use half-edge labelled graphs.
This leads to the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
H_{1},\dots,H_{d}
be disjoint sets of half-edge labels, one for each colour. An
[H_{1},\dots,H_{d}]
-
half-edge labelled coloured graph

\Gamma
is a tuple
\Gamma=(V,E_{H_{1}},\dots,E_{H_{d}})
where

1.

V
, the set of vertices, is a set partition of
H_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup H_{d}
;

2.

for all
i=1,\dots,d
,
E_{H_{i}}
is a set partition of
H_{i}
into blocks of size two.

An edge-coloured graph
G
is identified with an isomorphism class
[\Gamma]
of a half-edge labelled coloured representative
\Gamma
. Here, an
isomorphism

j
between
\Gamma=(V,E_{H_{1}},\dots,E_{H_{d}})
and
\Gamma^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime}_{H^{\prime}_{1}},\dots,E^{\prime}_{H^{\prime}_{d}})
is a tuple
j=(j_{1},\dots,j_{d})
of bijections
j_{i}\colon H_{i}\rightarrow H^{\prime}_{i}
such that the canonically induced maps
(j_{i})_{\ast}
on the partitions satisfy
(j_{i})_{\ast}(E_{H_{i}})=E^{\prime}_{H^{\prime}_{i}}
for all
i=1,\dots,d
, and
(j_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup j_{d})_{\ast}(V)=V^{\prime}
. An
automorphism
of
\Gamma
is an isomorphism from
\Gamma
to itself. By
|\Aut(G)|
we denote the cardinality of the automorphism group of
\Gamma
, for some representative
G=[\Gamma]
. The set of all isomorphism classes of half-edge labelled coloured graphs is denoted by
\mathcal{G}
. From now on, we might drop the adjective “edge-coloured”.

The set of vertices of
G
is denoted by
V_{G}
, while the set of edges is
E_{G}
. Below there is an example of how to encode a graph via a half-edge labelled graph.

Example 2.2
.

Let
H_{1}=\{1,2,\dots,6\}
and
H_{2}=\{a,b\}
. The partitions

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=\left\{\{1,2,3,4\},\{5,6,a,b\}\right\},

\displaystyle E_{H_{1}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\{1,2\},\{3,4\},\{5,6\}\right\},

\displaystyle E_{H_{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\{a,b\}\right\},

form an
[H_{1},H_{2}]
-labelled graph representing the graph
G
depicted in Figure
3
. Its automorphism group is isomorphic to
(\mathfrak{S}_{2}\times\mathfrak{S}_{2}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{2})\times\mathfrak{S}_{2}\times\mathfrak{S}_{2}
.
∎

Figure 3:
An edge-bicoloured graph
G
with two connected components.

A graph
G
is equipped with a
degree function

\deg\colon V_{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\geq 0}
, where
\deg(v)=(w_{1},\dots,w_{d})
if the vertex
v
is incident to
w_{i}=|v\cap H_{i}|
half-edges of colour
i
, for
i=1,\dots,d
.

In the following we make use of some multi-index notation. Bold symbols denote
d
-tuples, as in
\bm{w}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{d})
. The multi-index factorial is
\bm{w}!=w_{1}!\dots w_{d}!
, while
\bm{x}^{\bm{w}}
is shorthand notation for
x_{1}^{w_{1}}\dots x_{d}^{w_{d}}
. Moreover, we use
|\bm{w}|=w_{1}+\dots+w_{d}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

The generating function for graphs with marked vertex degrees is

\sum_{G\in\mathcal{G}}\frac{\eta^{|E_{G}|}}{|\Aut(G)|}\prod_{v\in V_{G}}\Lambda_{\deg(v)}=\sum_{\bm{s}\geq 0}\eta^{|\bm{s}|}(2s_{1}-1)!!\cdots(2s_{d}-1)!!\cdot[\bm{x}^{2\bm{s}}]\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\geq 0}\\
|\bm{w}|\geq 1\end{subarray}}\Lambda_{\bm{w}}\frac{\bm{x}^{\bm{w}}}{\bm{w}!}\right).

(7)

Here,
[\bm{x}^{2\bm{s}}]
denotes the coefficient extraction operator, and (
7
) is an expression in the formal power series ring
\mathbb{Q}[\Lambda_{\bm{w}}\,:\,\bm{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\geq 0}][[\eta]]
with coefficients in the formal variables
\Lambda_{\bm{w}}
.

Proof.

By expanding the exponential on the right-hand side, we can write

(2\bm{s})!\,[x_{1}^{2s_{1}}\dots x_{d}^{2s_{d}}]\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\geq 0}\\
|\bm{w}|\geq 1\end{subarray}}\Lambda_{\bm{w}}\frac{\bm{x}^{\bm{w}}}{\bm{w}!}\right)=\sum_{\{n_{\bm{w}}\}}\frac{(2\bm{s})!}{\prod_{\bm{w}}n_{\bm{w}}!\,\bm{w}!^{n_{\bm{w}}}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\geq 0}\\
|\bm{w}|\geq 1\end{subarray}}\Lambda_{\bm{w}}^{n_{\bm{w}}},

(8)

where the sum on the right-hand side ranges over all assignments
\bm{w}\mapsto n_{\bm{w}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with the property
\sum_{\bm{w}}n_{\bm{w}}w_{i}=2s_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,d
. The term

\frac{(2\bm{s})!}{\prod_{\bm{w}}n_{\bm{w}}!\,\bm{w}!^{n_{\bm{w}}}}

(9)

counts the number of partitions of the disjoint union
H_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup H_{d}
into
n_{\bm{w}}
many blocks with
w_{i}
elements from
H_{i}
, for
i=1,\dots,d
. Here,
H_{i}
is assumed to have
2s_{i}
many elements. This can be seen as follows: the
d
-fold product of symmetric groups
\mathfrak{S}_{2s_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathfrak{S}_{2s_{d}}
acts transitively on the set of partitions with specified block structure. A partition with
n_{\bm{w}}
many blocks with
w_{i}
elements from
H_{i}
is stabilised by a subgroup isomorphic to
(\mathfrak{S}_{w_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathfrak{S}_{w_{d}})^{n_{\bm{w}}}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n_{\bm{w}}}
, permuting the elements inside each block and the blocks themselves. By the orbit-stabiliser theorem, (
9
) is the number claimed above.

The number of partitions of
H_{i}
into blocks of size two is equal to the number of matchings in the complete graph on
H_{i}
, which is
(2s_{i}-1)!!
. Choosing a partition of
H_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup H_{d}
and matchings of each
H_{i}
specifies a half-edge labelled graph representing a graph
G
with
|\bm{s}|
many edges.
The number of such half-edge labelled graphs representing
G
equals
(2\bm{s})!/|\Aut(G)|
,
again by an application of the orbit-stabiliser theorem. Multiplying (
8
) by
\eta^{|\bm{s}|}(2s_{1}-1)!!\dots(2s_{d}-1)!!
and summing over all
\bm{s}
proves the proposition.
∎

It is convenient to shift the generating function such that the index of summation is the
Euler characteristic

\chi(G)
of
G
. Recall that
\chi(G)=|V_{G}|-|E_{G}|
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{-n}
be the set of isomorphism classes of half-edge labelled graphs with Euler characteristic
-n
. Then the following identity holds true.

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 0\\
G\in\mathcal{G}_{-n}\end{subarray}}\frac{z^{\chi(G)}}{|\Aut(G)|}\prod_{v\in V_{G}}\Lambda_{\deg(v)}=\sum_{\bm{s}\geq 0}z^{|\bm{s}|}(2s_{1}-1)!!\cdots(2s_{d}-1)!![\bm{x}^{2\bm{s}}]\exp\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\geq 0}\\
|\bm{w}|\geq 1\end{subarray}}z\Lambda_{\bm{w}}\frac{\bm{x}^{\bm{w}}}{\bm{w}!}\right)

(10)

The expression (
10
) is an equality in the formal power series ring
\mathcal{R}[[z]]
over the polynomial ring
\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{Q}[\Lambda_{\bm{w}}]
in infinitely many variables. We will be interested in the case where all the variables
\Lambda_{\bm{w}}
are specified to depend on only a
single
parameter
\lambda
, and only finitely many are nonzero. In other words, we specialise all
\Lambda_{\bm{w}}
in such a way so that

V(\bm{x},\lambda)=\sum_{\bm{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\geq 0},\,|\bm{w}|\geq 1}\Lambda_{\bm{w}}(\lambda)\frac{\bm{x}^{\bm{w}}}{\bm{w}!}

is a polynomial in
\mathbb{Q}[\lambda][\bm{x}]
. Then the left-hand side of (
10
) becomes a generating function for a specific set of graphs with vertex degree markings and allowed vertex-incidences determined by the coefficients and nonzero terms of
V(\bm{x},\lambda)
. Each coefficient in (
10
),

A^{V}_{n}(\lambda):=\sum_{G\in\mathcal{G}_{-n}}\frac{1}{|\Aut(G)|}\prod_{v\in V_{G}}\Lambda_{\deg(v)}(\lambda),

is now a univariate polynomial in
\lambda
.

Example 2.5
.

Consider the case of two colours,
d=2
, and fix the polynomial

V(x_{1},x_{2},\lambda)=\frac{x_{1}^{4}}{4!}+\lambda\frac{x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}}{2!\cdot 2!}+\lambda^{2}\frac{x_{2}^{4}}{4!}.

(11)

This restricts the generating function (
10
) to 4-regular graphs where each vertex is incident to four half-edges which are all the same colour, or to two half-edges of the first and two half-edges of the second colour. The parameter
\lambda
counts the number of edges of the second colour.

-n=-2
contribution to (

) is given by

From now on, we restrict to the case where
V
is
homogeneous
of degree
k
in the
\bm{x}
variables. Consequently, we only enumerate
k
-regular graphs. This assumption lets us rewrite
A^{V}_{n}
in terms of an exponential integral, which will be crucial in the forthcoming section. Note that if a
k
-regular graph
G
has Euler characteristic
-n
, then, since
k|V_{G}|=2|E_{G}|
and
|V_{G}|-|E_{G}|=-n
,
G
is required to have
\tfrac{2n}{k-2}
many vertices and
\tfrac{nk}{k-2}
many edges.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
V\in\mathbb{Q}[\lambda][\bm{x}]
be homogeneous of degree
k
in
\bm{x}
, and let us denote
M=\tfrac{k}{k-2}
and
K=\tfrac{2}{k-2}
. Then, for any integer
n\geq 0
such that
nK,nM\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

A^{V}_{n}(\lambda)=\frac{2^{nM+(d-2)/2}\left(nM+\frac{d-2}{2}\right)!}{(2\pi)^{d/2}(nK)!}\int_{S^{d-1}}V(\bm{x},\lambda)^{nK}\,\omega_{S^{d-1}},

(12)

where
\omega_{S^{d-1}}
denotes the standard volume form of the
(d-1)
-dimensional sphere
S^{d-1}
.

Proof.

Since
|V_{G}|
and
|E_{G}|
must be integers,
A^{V}_{n}=0
unless
nK,nM\in\mathbb{Z}
. So let us assume the latter. From Corollary
2.4
we have

A^{V}_{n}(\lambda)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{s}\geq 0\\
|\bm{s}|=nM\end{subarray}}(2s_{1}-1)!!\cdots(2s_{d}-1)!!\cdot[\bm{x}^{2\bm{s}}]\exp\left(\sum_{\bm{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\geq 0},\,|\bm{w}|\geq 1}\Lambda_{\bm{w}}(\lambda)\frac{\bm{x}^{\bm{w}}}{\bm{w}!}\right).

(13)

The
\bm{x}^{2\bm{s}}
coefficient of the exponential on the left-hand side has degree
\tfrac{2nM}{k}=nK
in the coefficients
\lambda_{\bm{w}}
. Using the homogeneity of
V
, this implies

[\bm{x}^{2\bm{s}}]\exp\left(\sum_{\bm{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\geq 0},\,|\bm{w}|\geq 1}\Lambda_{\bm{w}}(\lambda)\frac{\bm{x}^{\bm{w}}}{\bm{w}!}\right)=[\bm{x}^{2\bm{s}}]\frac{V(\bm{x},\lambda)^{nK}}{(nK)!}.

Recall the Gaussian integral identity

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}x^{t}\mathrm{d}x=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(t-1)!!&\text{if }t\text{ is even,}\\
0&\text{if }t\text{ is odd.}\end{array}\right.

This enables us to rewrite (
13
) as

A^{V}_{n}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}(nK)!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}^{2}\right)V(\bm{x},\lambda)^{nK}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}.

Finally, the homogeneity of
V
allows rescaling
\bm{x}
to have unit norm via
V(r\cdot\bm{x},\lambda)=r^{k}V(\bm{x},\lambda)
, so we can rewrite the integral above in polar coordinates as

\displaystyle A^{V}_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}(nK)!}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{d-1}\left(\int_{\partial B_{r}(0)}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}^{2}\right)V(\bm{x},\lambda)^{nK}\,\omega_{\partial B_{r}(0)}\right)\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}(nK)!}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{d-1+knK}\mathrm{d}r\int_{S^{d-1}}V(\bm{x},\lambda)^{nK}\,\omega_{S^{d-1}}.

Together with the identity

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{nkK+d-1}\mathrm{d}r=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-q}(2q)^{(knK+d-2)/2}\mathrm{d}q=2^{nM+(d-2)/2}\left(nM+\frac{d-2}{2}\right)!\,,

this yields the desired expression.
∎

In the large-
n
asymptotics, a saddle point approximation of (
12
) can be used to derive an asymptotic expression for
A^{V}_{n}
in terms of maxima of
|V(\bm{x},\lambda)|
on
S^{d-1}
, as long as
\lambda
is
real
. A detailed analysis of this is carried out in
[
BMW26
]
. However, since we are interested also in complex values of
\lambda
, we need to analytically continue the
n\rightarrow\infty
limit of (
12
). This is the content of the next section, using Picard–Lefschetz theory.

3
Lefschetz thimble decomposition

Proposition
2.6
tells us to study the analytic continuation to
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
of the integral

I^{V}_{n}(\lambda):=\int_{S^{d-1}}V(\bm{x},\lambda)^{n}\omega_{S^{d-1}}

(14)

in the limit
n\rightarrow\infty
. To this end, we decompose
I_{n}^{V}(\lambda)
in terms of integrals over
Lefschetz thimbles
. These are integration contours associated to critical points of
V
along which the real part of
\log V
decreases; this makes them amenable to a stationary phase approximation. In the large-
n
limit, the thimble integrals reduce to critical point evaluations.

Such an approach is prominent both in mathematics and theoretical physics, see
[
DH02
,
Wit11
]
. To make it precise we need to show that the Lefschetz thimbles define a basis of the appropriate homology group. Here, a technical subtlety arises:
one commonly assumes the critical values
\mathfrak{R}\log V(\sigma_{j})
to be
distinct
for different critical points
\sigma_{j}
, see e.g.
[
DH02
, Hypothesis H4]
or
[
AK11
, Assumption 8]
. However, for the applications we have in mind, this assumption is violated, e.g. for
V
as constructed in Example
2.5
.

We modify the construction of the Lefschetz thimbles in a two-step process which has been laid out in
[
Mat23
, §4]
:
First, we define thimbles
\Gamma_{\sigma}
as (un)stable manifolds of a Morse–Smale vector field that is a small perturbation of the gradient vector field used in the standard Lefschetz thimble construction. This ensures transverse intersections of the thimbles.
Contours constructed in this way are elements in a
locally finite
homology group.

In a second step, we need to
regularise
these thimbles to obtain honest (twisted) homology classes
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\sigma}
. This involves a real oriented blowup construction. For the case of complements of hyperplane arrangements, this process is also described in
[
AK11
, §3.2.3–3.2.5]
.
Under a genericity assumption on
V
expressing the number of critical points as a signed Euler characteristic, the
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\sigma}
form a basis of the twisted homology group.
Since the integral (
14
) can be regarded as a period pairing between this twisted homology group and an algebraic de Rham cohomology group, this then proves the Lefschetz thimble decomposition of the integral (
14
), which is the content of Theorem
3.3
. By applying a stationary phase approximation, we can then get an asymptotic expression for
I_{n}^{V}(\lambda)
that is valid for almost all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, see Corollary
3.4
.

Let us introduce some notation, mostly following
[
Mat23
]
. Let

\mathbb{S}:=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\,:\,x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+\dots+x_{d}^{2}=1\}

be the
complexified sphere
of complex dimension
d-1
. For a fixed
\lambda
, let
U_{\lambda}:=\mathbb{S}\setminus\mathcal{V}(V(\bm{x},\lambda))
be the locus where
V
is non-vanishing, equipped with a complete Riemannian metric
g
. We can view
D_{\lambda}:=\mathcal{V}(V(\bm{x},\lambda))
as a divisor on
\mathbb{S}
. Moreover, we denote
F_{\lambda}(\bm{x}):=\log V(\bm{x},\lambda)
. In the following we collect the necessary non-degeneracy assumptions. Crucially, these only fail on an at most zero-dimensional discriminant locus
\Sigma
, see Remark
3.5
.

(A1)

The critical locus
\crit(F_{\lambda}):=\{\bm{x}\in U_{\lambda}\,:\,\mathrm{d}_{\bm{x}}F_{\lambda}(\bm{x})=0\}
is a zero-dimensional variety. This is true as long as the factorisation of
V(\bm{x},\lambda)
is squarefree.

(A2)

The divisor
D_{\lambda}
should be simple normal crossing. In particular, this is the case if
D_{\lambda}
is smooth, i.e.
\mathrm{d}_{\bm{x}}V(\bm{x},\lambda)\neq 0
for all
\bm{x}\in D_{\lambda}
.

(A3)

For any
\sigma\in\crit(F_{\lambda})
,
F_{\lambda}
is non-degenerate at
\sigma
, i.e.
\det\Hess_{F_{\lambda}}(\sigma)\neq 0
.

(A4)

The number of critical points is
|\crit(F_{\lambda})|=(-1)^{d-1}\chi(U_{\lambda})
, where
\chi
is the topological Euler characteristic. We denote this number by
\chi^{\ast}:=|\crit(F_{\lambda})|
.

Note that
\crit(F_{\lambda})
can easily be computed by first determining the critical points of
V
on
\mathbb{S}
via Lagrange multipliers and then intersecting those with
U_{\lambda}
.

Remark 3.1
.

The Euler characteristic of
\mathbb{S}
is
1+(-1)^{d-1}
. Indeed, let
f=x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{d}^{2}
; then the affine Milnor fibration
\mathbb{C}^{d}\setminus\{f=0\}\xrightarrow{f}\mathbb{C}^{*}
has fibre
\{f=1\}
which is homotopy equivalent to a bouquet of
\mu
(complex) spheres of dimension
d-1
. Here,
\mu
is the Milnor number of the singularity of
f
at the origin, which is an
A_{1}
-singularity, hence
\mu=1
. This implies
\chi(\mathbb{S})=1+(-1)^{d-1}
. By the excision property of the Euler characteristic,

(-1)^{d-1}\chi(U_{\lambda})=(-1)^{d}\left(|D_{\lambda}|-1\right)+1.

(15)

Example 3.2
.

Consider
V(x_{1},x_{2},\lambda)=\frac{x_{1}^{4}}{4!}+\lambda\frac{x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}}{2!\cdot 2!}+\lambda^{2}\frac{x_{2}^{4}}{4!}
; generically, this polynomial has eight roots on
\mathbb{S}=\{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}=1\}
which has Euler characteristic zero. By (
15
), one expects
|\crit(F_{\lambda})|=8
. This is indeed the case for generic
\lambda
. For
\lambda\in\{1/3,3\}
, there are only six critical points. If
\lambda=3\pm\sqrt{8}
,
V
has only four roots on
\mathbb{S}
, as well as four critical points.
∎

Let
\nabla_{\pm}:=\mathrm{d}\pm\tau\dlog V\wedge
be a
twisted differential
on
\mathbb{S}
with logarithmic poles along
D_{\lambda}
. The
(d-1)
-form
\omega_{S^{d-1}}
from (
14
) is an element of
H^{0}(U_{\lambda},\Omega^{d-1}_{U_{\lambda}})
.
We can also view it as a representative of a cohomology class in an algebraic de Rham cohomology group
H^{d-1}_{\pm}(\tau):=\mathbb{H}^{d-1}(U_{\lambda};(\Omega^{\bullet}_{U_{\lambda}},\nabla_{\pm}))
. Let
\mathcal{L}^{\pm}(\tau)
be the sheaf on
U_{\lambda}
of flat sections of the connection
\nabla_{\mp}
. Then there is the
period pairing
, which is the perfect pairing

\displaystyle\langle\bullet,\bullet\rangle_{\mathrm{per}}\,:\,H_{d-1}(U_{\lambda};\mathcal{L}^{\pm}(\tau))\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}H^{d-1}_{\pm}(\tau)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

(16)

\displaystyle[\Gamma^{\pm}]\otimes[\omega_{\pm}]

\displaystyle\mapsto\int_{\Gamma^{\pm}}V(\bm{x},\lambda)^{\pm\tau}\omega_{\pm}.

Here,
H_{d-1}(U_{\lambda};\mathcal{L}^{\pm}(\tau))
is a
twisted homology group
, taking values in the local system
\mathcal{L}^{\pm}(\tau)
. The reader is referred to
[
MMT23
, §3]
for an introduction to twisted (co)homology.
In the following we omit the scaling
\tau
to ease notation, and assume
\tau>0
to be
generic
.

3.1
Step 1: construction of Lefschetz thimbles as locally finite chains

In this subsection we explain the construction of Lefschetz thimbles, giving rise to
locally finite
homology classes
[\Gamma_{\sigma}^{\pm}]\in H_{d-1}^{\mathrm{lf}}(U_{\lambda};\mathcal{L}^{\pm})
. This construction is basically the classical gradient flow trajectory construction, except that one needs to perturb the gradient field to ensure transverse intersections of stable and unstable manifolds.

Let
T_{\lambda}
be an open neighbourhood of
D_{\lambda}
with the properties that

•

\mathbb{S}\setminus T_{\lambda}
and
\overline{T}_{\lambda}
are manifolds with boundary;

•

the critical locus is contained in the complement of
T
,
\crit(F_{\lambda})\subset\mathbb{S}\setminus T_{\lambda}
;

•

the gradient
\mathrm{d}_{\bm{x}}\mathfrak{R}F_{\lambda}
is transversal to
\partial(\mathbb{S}\setminus T_{\lambda})
and does not vanish on
\partial T
.

Due to a theorem by Smale
[
Sma61
, Theorem A]
, assuming
(A1)
and
(A3)
, there exists a
C^{1}

Morse–Smale vector field

\xi_{\lambda}
on
\mathbb{S}\setminus T_{\lambda}
adapted to
\mathfrak{R}F_{\lambda}
. We explain the terminology below, see
[
Nic11
, §2.4]
for more details. The vector field
\xi_{\lambda}
is
gradient-like
with respect to
\mathfrak{R}F_{\lambda}
if
\xi_{\lambda}\cdot\mathfrak{R}F_{\lambda}>0
on
(\mathbb{S}\setminus T_{\lambda})\setminus\crit(F_{\lambda})
, and for any critical point
\sigma\in\crit(F_{\lambda})
there exist local coordinates
(s_{i})
such that
\xi_{\lambda}=-2\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_{i}\partial_{s_{i}}+2\sum_{i>m}s_{i}\partial_{s_{i}}
, where
m
is the index of
F_{\lambda}
at
\sigma
.
Let
\Phi_{t}
be the flow on
\mathbb{S}\setminus T_{\lambda}
generated by
\xi
. The
(un)stable manifold
of
\sigma
relative to
\xi_{\lambda}
is the set
\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{S}\setminus T_{\lambda}\,:\,\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\Phi_{t}(\bm{x})=\sigma\}
. A gradient-like vector field
\xi_{\lambda}
is
Morse–Smale
if for any pair of critical points, the associated stable and unstable manifolds intersect transversely. One can think of
\xi_{\lambda}
as a small perturbation of the gradient vector field defined by
\mathrm{d}_{\bm{x}}\mathfrak{R}F_{\lambda}
, where the perturbation ensures transverse intersections.

By Whitney’s extension theorem, it is possible to extend
\xi_{\lambda}
to a
C^{1}
-vector field on
U_{\lambda}=\mathbb{S}\setminus D_{\lambda}
so that
\xi_{\lambda}\cdot\mathfrak{R}F_{\lambda}>0
on
U_{\lambda}\setminus\crit(F_{\lambda})
; by abuse of notation we call this extension again
\xi_{\lambda}
, and denote its flow by
\Phi_{t}
. For any critical point
\sigma\in\crit(F_{\lambda})
, we can then define the Lefschetz thimbles

\Gamma^{\pm}_{\sigma}:=\left\{\bm{x}\in U_{\lambda}\,:\,\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\Phi_{t}(\bm{x})=\sigma\right\}.

The transversality of stable and unstable manifolds implies
\Gamma^{+}_{\sigma}\cap\Gamma^{-}_{\tau}=\varnothing
if
\sigma\neq\tau
, see
[
Nic11
, Remark 2.4.9]
. Around a critical point
\sigma
, by the Morse lemma, we can find coordinates
(z_{i})=(s_{i}+\mathrm{i}t_{i})
such that
F_{\lambda}(\bm{z})=F_{\lambda}(\sigma)+\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}z_{i}^{2}
. This involves a choice of branch of
F_{\lambda}
near
\sigma
, i.e. a choice of value of
\mathfrak{I}F_{\lambda}(\sigma)
. Then, for
\epsilon>0
small, we define

\displaystyle c_{\epsilon}^{+}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\bm{z}\in U_{\lambda}\,:\,\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}s_{i}^{2}=\epsilon,~t_{1}=\dots=t_{d-1}=0\right\},

(17)

\displaystyle c_{\epsilon}^{-}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\bm{z}\in U_{\lambda}\,:\,\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}t_{i}^{2}=\epsilon,~s_{1}=\dots=s_{d-1}=0\right\}.

With these notions, we can rewrite the thimbles as

\Gamma^{\mp}_{\sigma}=\bigcup_{\pm\{t\geq 0\}}\Phi_{t}(c_{\epsilon}^{\pm})\cup\bigcup_{0\leq\delta\leq\epsilon}c^{\pm}_{\delta},

confer e.g.
[
DH02
, §2.3.1]
. From this expression we see that
\Gamma^{\pm}_{\sigma}
define classes in the locally finite twisted homology group
[\Gamma^{\pm}_{\sigma}]\in H^{\mathrm{lf}}_{d-1}(U_{\lambda};\mathcal{L}^{\pm})
. Its elements are classes of locally finite sums of twisted singular chains, i.e. formally infinite sums
\sum_{\Delta}c_{\Delta}\Delta\otimes U_{\Delta}
, where
\Delta
is a singular simplex and
U_{\Delta}
a branch of
F_{\lambda}
on
\Delta
, such that for any compact subset
K\subset U_{\lambda}
, there are only finitely many
\Delta
whose image intersect
K
nontrivially and with
c_{\Delta}\neq 0
.

3.2
Step 2: regularising the Lefschetz thimbles

To obtain Lefschetz thimbles in the usual twisted homology group we need to
regularise
the classes
[\Gamma^{\pm}_{\sigma}]\in H^{\mathrm{lf}}_{d-1}(U_{\lambda};\mathcal{L}^{\pm})
. This is done by constructing a real oriented blowup of
\mathbb{S}
along
D_{\lambda}
, which, roughly speaking, replaces
D_{\lambda}
with “pipe-like” objects. The regularisation of the thimble then carries the datum of a direction at
D_{\lambda}
. This construction is taken from the proof of
[
Mat23
, Lemma 4.2]
and the paragraph thereafter. See also
[
AK11
, §3.2.5]
.

Let
\varpi\,:\,\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}
be the
real oriented blowup
of
\mathbb{S}
along
D_{\lambda}
, see (
18
) below and also
[
Sab13
, §8.2]
. Under assumption
(A2)
, this lets
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
carry the structure of a manifold with corners, i.e. local charts are open sets in
[0,\infty)^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1-k}

[
Joy09
, Definition 2.1]
.
The usual definitions of smoothness and tangent spaces extend to this setting. Let
X
be a manifold with corners. For a point
\bm{x}\in X
, we define the
inward sector

\IS(T_{\bm{x}}X)
as follows
[
Joy09
, Definition 2.2]
: let
(U,\varphi)
be a chart on
X
with
U
an open subset of
[0,\infty)^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1-k}
containing zero such that
\varphi(0)=\bm{x}
.
The map
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\mathrm{d}\varphi\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{0}}\,:\,T_{0}U=\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\rightarrow T_{\bm{x}}X
is an isomorphism. Then
\IS(T_{\bm{x}}X)
is the image
\IS(T_{\bm{x}}X)={\left.\kern-1.2pt\mathrm{d}\varphi\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{0}}([0,\infty)^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1-k})\subseteq T_{\bm{x}}X
. Visually speaking, it contains the tangent vectors “pointing into”
X
.

For an irreducible component
D_{i}\subset D_{\lambda}
, we denote the pullback
\widetilde{D}_{i}:=\varpi^{-1}(D_{i})
. Let us also write
D_{0}
and
D_{\infty}
for the support of the divisors of zeros and poles of
e^{F_{\lambda}}
, respectively, and
D=D_{0}\cup D^{\prime}\cup D_{\infty}
. Let
(z_{i})
be local coordinates around a point in
D_{\infty}
. Then on
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
we have corresponding local coordinates
((r_{i},e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}})_{i=1}^{k},z_{k+1},\dots,z_{d-1})
and the blowup map is locally given by (which can be regarded as the definition of a real oriented blowup)

\varpi\,:\,((r_{i},e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}})_{i=1}^{k},z_{k+1},\dots,z_{d-1})\mapsto((r_{i}e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}})_{i=1}^{k},z_{k+1},\dots,z_{d-1}).

(18)

We may assume that
\widetilde{D}_{\infty}
and
\widetilde{D}_{0}
are locally given by
\widetilde{D}_{\infty}=\{r_{1}\dots r_{\ell}=0\}
and
\widetilde{D}_{0}=\{r_{\ell+1}\dots r_{k}=0\}
.
Then we can define a vector field locally via
\Theta=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\partial_{r_{i}}
. Evaluated at a point
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{S}
, this gives an inward pointing vector
\Theta(\bm{x})\in\IS(T_{\bm{x}}\widetilde{\mathbb{S}})
.
Using a partition of unity, one can globally extend this to a vector field
\Theta\,:\,\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}\rightarrow\IS(T\widetilde{\mathbb{S}})
which is tangent to every component of
D^{\prime}
. Let
\Psi_{t}
be the flow of
\Theta
.
Then, for a fixed
T>0
, we define

\widetilde{W}_{\infty}:=\bigcup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Psi_{t}(\widetilde{D}_{\infty})\quad\text{and}\quad W_{\infty}:=\varpi(\widetilde{W}_{\infty}).

We claim that we can find
M>\epsilon
and
T>0
large enough so that

\Phi_{T}(c_{\epsilon}^{+})\subseteq\{\mathfrak{R}F_{\lambda}>M\}\subseteq W_{\infty}.

(19)

Since
\Theta
is inward-pointing,
\mathfrak{R}F_{\lambda}
decreases along the image of the flow
\Psi
under
\varpi
. This shows the existence of
M
so that the containment on the right-hand side holds. Moreover, we have
\lim_{t\to\infty}\Phi_{t}(\sigma)\subseteq D_{\infty}=\{\mathfrak{R}F_{\lambda}=+\infty\}
. Since
\lim_{n\to\infty}c^{+}_{1/n}=\{\sigma\}
, an application of
[
Jos17
, Lemma 8.4.5]
shows that for any
T>0
,
\Phi_{t}(c^{+}_{1/n})|_{[-T,T]}
converges to
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\Phi_{t}(\sigma)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{[-T,T]}}
. Therefore, for any
M
, there exists a
T
such that the containment on the left-hand side of (
19
) holds, which concludes the proof of the claim.

By the vanishing theorem of twisted homology, see e.g.
[
Ago+24
, Theorem A.1]
,
H_{d-2}(W_{\infty}\setminus D;\mathcal{L}^{-})=0
. Therefore, there exists a chain
C\in C_{d-1}(W_{\infty}\setminus D;\mathcal{L}^{-})
with
\partial C=-\Phi_{T}(c_{\epsilon}^{+})
. We then define the
regularised Lefschetz thimble
as the singular chain

\widetilde{\Gamma}^{-}_{\sigma}:=\bigcup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Phi_{t}(c_{\epsilon}^{+})+\bigcup_{0\leq\delta\leq\epsilon}c^{+}_{\delta}+C\in C_{d-1}(U_{\lambda};\mathcal{L}^{-}).

Analogously, we can define
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{+}_{\sigma}
, using
W_{0}
instead of
W_{\infty}
. By
[
Mat23
, Lemma 4.2]
, the locally finite homology group
H_{p}^{\mathrm{lf}}(W_{\infty}\setminus D;\mathcal{L}^{-})=0
vanishes for any
p
. In particular, this implies
[\Gamma^{-}_{\sigma}-\widetilde{\Gamma}^{-}_{\sigma}]=[\bigcup_{T<t}\Phi_{t}(c_{\epsilon}^{+})-C]=0\in H_{d-1}^{\mathrm{lf}}(W_{\infty}\setminus D;\mathcal{L}^{-})
. Therefore, there exists a locally finite chain
C^{\prime}
on
W_{\infty}\setminus D
, and hence on
U_{\lambda}
, with
\partial C^{\prime}=\Gamma^{-}_{\sigma}-\widetilde{\Gamma}^{-}_{\sigma}
.

Moreover,
\partial\widetilde{\Gamma}^{-}_{\sigma}=\Phi_{T}(c_{\epsilon}^{+})+0-\partial C=0
by construction of
C
, so
[\widetilde{\Gamma}^{-}_{\sigma}]
gives an element of
H_{d-1}(U_{\lambda};\mathcal{L}^{-})
. In fact,
[\widetilde{\Gamma}^{-}_{\sigma}]
is the image of
[\Gamma^{-}_{\sigma}]
under the
regularisation map

\mathrm{reg}\,:\,H^{\mathrm{lf}}_{d-1}(U_{\lambda};\mathcal{L}^{\pm})\xrightarrow{\sim}H_{d-1}(U_{\lambda};\mathcal{L}^{\pm}),

which is the inverse of the natural isomorphism
H_{d-1}(U_{\lambda};\mathcal{L}^{\pm})\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{\mathrm{lf}}_{d-1}(U_{\lambda};\mathcal{L}^{\pm})
, under the assumption that
\tau
is generic, confer
[
AK11
, §3.2.5]
. Let us consider the (perfect) homology intersection pairing

\langle\bullet,\bullet\rangle_{\mathrm{h}}\,:\,H_{d-1}(U_{\lambda},\mathcal{L}^{-})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}H_{d-1}(U_{\lambda},\mathcal{L}^{+})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}.

From the transversality property of the Morse–Smale vector field, we have

\langle[\widetilde{\Gamma}^{-}_{\sigma}],[\widetilde{\Gamma}^{+}_{\sigma^{\prime}}]\rangle_{\mathrm{h}}=\delta_{\sigma{\sigma^{\prime}}},

so
\left\{\Gamma^{\pm}_{\sigma}\right\}_{\sigma\in\crit(F_{\lambda})}
are independent elements of
H_{d-1}(U_{\lambda};\mathcal{L}^{\pm})
. By assumption
(A4)
, there are precisely
\chi^{*}
many critical points. This coincides with the dimension of
H_{d-1}(U_{\lambda};\mathcal{L}^{\pm})
, see
[
Ago+24
, Theorem A.1]
. Therefore, we arrive at the following statement.

Theorem 3.3
.

For a fixed
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
so that assumptions
(A1)–(A4)
are valid, and for generic
\tau>0
, the regularised Lefschetz thimbles
\left\{\Gamma^{\pm}_{\sigma}(\tau)\right\}_{\sigma\in\crit(F_{\lambda})}
form a basis of the twisted homology
H_{d-1}(U_{\lambda};\mathcal{L}^{\pm}(\tau))
. Via the period pairing (
16
), the integral
I_{\tau}^{V}(\lambda)
decomposes as

I_{\tau}^{V}(\lambda)=\sum_{\sigma\in\crit(F_{\lambda})}c_{\sigma}(\lambda)\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}^{+}_{\sigma}(\tau)}V(\bm{x},\lambda)^{\tau}\omega_{S^{d-1}},

(20)

for some coefficients
c_{\sigma}(\lambda)\in\mathbb{C}
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let us fix
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
so that assumptions
(A1)–(A4)
are valid. Then, in the large-
n
limit, the integral
I_{n}^{V}(\lambda)
is asymptotically given by

I_{n}^{V}(\lambda)\sim\left(-\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)^{(d-1)/2}\sum_{\sigma\in\crit(F_{\lambda})}\frac{c_{\sigma}(\lambda)\omega_{S^{d-1}}(\sigma\partial_{\bm{x}})}{\sqrt{\det\Hess_{F_{\lambda}}(\sigma)}}e^{nF_{\lambda}(\sigma)}(1+o(n^{-1})),

(21)

where
\sigma\partial_{\bm{x}}
denotes the constant vector field on
\mathbb{S}
with value
\sigma
.

Proof.

For generic
n>0
, we obtain the Lefschetz thimble decomposition of
I^{V}_{n}(\lambda)
from Theorem
3.3
.
In each of the integrals in (
20
) we then change the integration contour
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{+}_{\sigma}(n)
to the unregularised thimble
\Gamma^{+}_{\sigma}(n)
. The contribution

\int_{\bigcup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Phi_{t}(c_{\epsilon}^{+})+C}V(\bm{x},\lambda)^{n}\omega_{S^{d-1}}

is exponentially suppressed in the large-
n
limit, see
[
Mat23
, (4.5)]
.
Therefore, we can apply the stationary phase formula (see
[
Mat23
, (4.7)]
) to
\Gamma^{+}_{\sigma}(n)
which is defined for any
n>0
, to obtain an asymptotic expansion for
I_{n}^{V}(\lambda)
. This gives the desired expression (
21
).
∎

We conclude this section with some remarks.

Remark 3.5
.

If
V(\bm{x},\lambda)\in\mathbb{C}[\bm{x},\lambda]
is irreducible, then assumptions
(A1)–(A4)
are violated on an at most zero-dimensional variety in
\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}
. Let us denote this finite
discriminant
set of points by
\Sigma
. In case of
(A1)
, the claim follows from Sard’s theorem. The divisor
D_{\lambda}
is smooth away from a discriminant hypersurface in the one-dimensional parameter space
\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}
. Similarly, the vanishing Hessian is a hypersurface in
\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}
, giving a finite set of exceptional points. The locus where
(A4)
fails is a version of an
Euler discriminant
, see e.g.
[
TW24
]
.

Remark 3.6
.

By construction of
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{+}_{\sigma}
, each of the integrals appearing in (
20
) is an analytic function in
\lambda
on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma
, as well as the function
I^{V}_{n}(\lambda)
. That is, however, not true for the coefficients
c_{\sigma}(\lambda)
, which is an instance of
Stokes’ phenomenon
. These coefficients can even jump discontinuously along so-called
Stokes curves
of the form

S_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}:=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,\mathfrak{I}F_{\lambda}(\sigma)=\mathfrak{I}F_{\lambda}(\sigma^{\prime})\mod 2\pi\right\},

for
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\crit(F_{\lambda})
such that
S_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}
is one-dimensional if interpreted as a real algebraic variety in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\simeq\mathbb{C}
. If
\lambda
lies on such a Stokes curve, the choice of basis of Lefschetz thimbles is not canonical any more, as can be seen from (
17
). As
\lambda
crosses a Stokes line, the basis of Lefschetz thimbles transforms via a unipotent matrix, see e.g.
[
Pha85
]
. Note, however, that the
c_{\sigma}(\lambda)
possess well-defined one-sided limits to the Stokes curves.

For
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\crit(F_{\lambda})
such that
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(S_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}})=1
, we denote by

\mathcal{S}^{c}_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}:=\pi_{0}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus S_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\right)

the connected components in the complement of the Stokes curve. In particular, we have that
c_{\sigma}(\lambda)\neq 0
on a union of regions in
\mathcal{S}^{c}_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}
, except for possibly isolated points.

4
Accumulation of zeros

In this section we state and prove the main theorem of this paper. The key idea is to combine Corollary
3.4
with a result by Sokal on accumulation of zeros which is in turn a generalisation of the Beraha–Kahane–Weiss theorem
[
BKW78
]
.

Let us first set up some notation following
[
Sok04
]
. Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}
be a domain and
(f_{n})_{n}
a sequence of function
f_{n}\,:\,D\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. We define the following limit sets of zeros.

\displaystyle\lim\inf\mathcal{V}(f_{n})

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}z\in D\,:\,\text{every neighbourhood }U\ni z\text{ has a nonempty}\\
\text{intersection with all but finitely many }\mathcal{V}(f_{n})\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle\lim\sup\mathcal{V}(f_{n})

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}z\in D\,:\,\text{every neighbourhood }U\ni z\text{ has a nonempty}\\
\text{intersection with infinitely many }\mathcal{V}(f_{n})\end{array}\right\}

If
f_{n}
is of the form
f_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\alpha_{k}(z)\beta_{k}(z)^{n}
, we call an index
k

dominant at

z
if
|\beta_{k}(z)|\geq|\beta_{l}(z)|~\forall 1\leq l\leq m
, and write
D_{k}:=\left\{z\in D\,:\,k\text{ is dominant at }z\right\}
. We call the following assumption the
no-degenerate-dominance condition:

(B1)

There do not exist indices
k\neq k^{\prime}
such that
\beta_{k}\equiv\zeta\beta_{k^{\prime}}
for some
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{1\}
with
|\zeta|=1
and such that
D_{k}
has nonempty interior.

We are now in the position to formulate our main theorem precisely.

Theorem 4.1

(Main result)
.

Let
V\in\mathbb{C}[\lambda][\bm{x}]
be a polynomial satisfying assumptions
(A1)–(A4)
for generic
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
(i.e.
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma
with finite
\Sigma
). Moreover, assume that
(B1)
is satisfied for the functions
V(\sigma)
,
\sigma\in\crit(F_{\lambda})
. Then, for
n\to\infty
, the zeros of
A_{n}^{V}(\lambda)
accumulate along segments of anti-Stokes curves. More precisely,

\lim\inf\mathcal{V}(A_{n}^{V})=\lim\sup\mathcal{V}(A_{n}^{V})=\mathcal{P}\,\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\neq\sigma^{\prime}\in\crit(F_{\lambda})\\
D\in\mathcal{S}^{c}_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,\mathfrak{R}F_{\lambda}(\sigma)=\mathfrak{R}F_{\lambda}(\sigma^{\prime})\right\}\cap\overline{D},

(22)

where
\mathcal{P}
is a (possibly empty) set of finitely many isolated points and the union ranges over some (not necessarily all) pairs of critical points
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\crit(F_{\lambda})
such that the anti-Stokes curve

\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,\mathfrak{R}F_{\lambda}(\sigma)=\mathfrak{R}F_{\lambda}(\sigma^{\prime})\right\}
is one-dimensional as a real algebraic variety.

Remark 4.2
.

Note that the critical points of
F_{\lambda}=\log V
agree with the critical points of
V
restricted to
\mathbb{S}
. Moreover, we have the identities
\mathfrak{R}F_{\lambda}=\log|V(\lambda)|
and
\mathfrak{I}F_{\lambda}=\arg V(\lambda)=\mathrm{atan}2(b,a)
(the 2-argument arctangent function), from which one sees that the (anti-)Stokes curves are algebraic curves in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{ab}
where
\lambda=a+\mathrm{i}b
. Therefore, the accumulation set of zeros in (
22
) is semialgebraic in the two real variables
a
and
b
.

Before we prove Theorem
4.1
, we illustrate the result with an example.

Example 4.3
.

Consider
V(x_{1},x_{2},\lambda)=\frac{x_{1}^{4}}{4!}+\lambda\frac{x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}}{2!\cdot 2!}+\lambda^{2}\frac{x_{2}^{4}}{4!}
as in Example
2.5
. Using Lagrange multipliers the critical points of
V
restricted to
\mathbb{S}
can be computed as

\sigma_{1,2}=(\pm 1,0)\quad\sigma_{3,4}=(0,\pm 1)\quad\sigma_{5,\dots,8}=\left(\pm\sqrt{\frac{\lambda^{2}-3\lambda}{\lambda^{2}-6\lambda+1}},\pm\sqrt{\frac{-3\lambda+1}{\lambda^{2}-6\lambda+1}}\right).

For generic
\lambda
, these are eight distinct critical points, which agrees with
|\chi(U_{\lambda})|
, see Example
3.2
. If
\lambda=3\pm\sqrt{8}
,
\sigma_{5,\dots,8}
move off to infinity.
However, in this case
V
has only four roots on
\mathbb{S}
, so assumption
(A4)
is still satisfied. If
\lambda\in\{1/3,3\}
,
\sigma_{5}
and
\sigma_{5}
, respectively
\sigma_{7}
and
\sigma_{8}
, collide and
(A4)
is violated.
These two points lie in the discriminantal locus
\{1/3,3\}\subseteq\Sigma
, see Remark
3.5
. The four critical points
\sigma_{1,\dots,4}
are non-degenerate provided
\lambda\neq 0
. Moreover,
\sigma_{5,\dots,8}
are non-degenerate as long as
\lambda\notin\{0,1\}
. No critical point lies on the divisor
D_{\lambda}
except if
\lambda=0
. Therefore, we have
\Sigma=\{0,1/3,1,3\}
as the finite discriminant locus where assumptions
(A1)–(A4)
are violated.

There are three distinct anti-Stokes curves:

C_{1}=\{\mathfrak{R}F_{\lambda}(\sigma_{1})=\mathfrak{R}F_{\lambda}(\sigma_{5})\},~C_{2}=\{\mathfrak{R}F_{\lambda}(\sigma_{3})=\mathfrak{R}F_{\lambda}(\sigma_{5})\},~C_{3}=\{\mathfrak{R}F_{\lambda}(\sigma_{1})=\mathfrak{R}F_{\lambda}(\sigma_{3})\}.

The curve
C_{2}
is the purple curve depicted in Figure
2
. It is a classical algebraic curve known as the
Bernoulli lemniscate
, following the equation (with
a=\mathfrak{R}\lambda,\,b=\mathfrak{I}\lambda
)

\ell(a,b):=((a-3)^{2}+b^{2})^{2}-4^{2}((a-3)^{2}-b^{2})=0.

The curve
C_{1}
is the green curve from Figure
2
. On first sight it looks like a Pascal limaçon (such a limaçon has been found in
[
Dol+01
, §6]
as the accumulation locus for the zeros of a Potts model on a random graph, and in
[
BR18
]
as the limit curve for the zeros of a generating function of adsorbing Dyck paths). However, that is not quite true for our curve
C_{1}
. Instead, it obeys the slightly modified equation
\ell(a,b)-64((a^{2}+b^{2})^{2}-1)=0
.

The third anti-Stokes curve
C_{3}
is a unit circle. It does not appear in the accumulation locus (
22
), see also Figure
1
. Finally, the Stokes curve arrangement needs to be determined.

The locus
\{\mathfrak{I}F_{\lambda}(\sigma_{1})=\mathfrak{I}F_{\lambda}(\sigma_{5})\}
can be identified as the union of the
a
-axis together with the ellipse defined by
3(a^{2}+b^{2})-a=0
which becomes apparent after rewriting as

\arctan\left(\frac{2b(a-3a^{2}-3b^{2})}{a^{2}(1+(a-6)a)+(2(a-3)a-1)b^{2}+b^{4}}\right)=0.

Similarly, one identifies the Stokes curve
S_{\sigma_{3},\sigma_{5}}=\{\mathfrak{I}F_{\lambda}(\sigma_{3})=\mathfrak{I}F_{\lambda}(\sigma_{5})\}
as the union of the three lines
\{a=0\},\,\{a=3\},\,\{b=0\}
. Together, the Stokes arrangement is shown as the grey lines in Figure
2
. The accumulation of zeros only takes place in the region

\overline{D}=\left\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,a\leq 3\text{ and }3(a^{2}+b^{2})-a\geq 0\right\}.

In the complement of this region (grey shaded area in Figure
2
), the corresponding Lefschetz thimbles do not contribute to the asymptotics (
21
). Note that the two points
\lambda=1/3
and
\lambda=3
lie in the (Euclidean) closure of
(C_{1}\cap\overline{D})\setminus\Sigma
, respectively
(C_{2}\cap\overline{D})\setminus\Sigma
, and hence belong to the accumulation locus since this is closed.
Our theory does not allow making statements regarding the two isolated points
\lambda\in\{0,1\}
, a subset of which forms
\mathcal{P}
.
∎

In order to prove Theorem
4.1
, we use Sokal’s result
[
Sok04
, Theorem 1.5]
below.

Theorem 4.4

(Generalised Beraha–Kahane–Weiss)
.

Let
D
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}
, and let
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}
and
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}
with
m\geq 2
be
analytic
functions on
D
, none of which is identically zero. For each integer
n\geq 0
, let

f_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\alpha_{k}(z)\beta_{k}(z)^{n}.

Let us assume that
(B1)
holds. Then
\lim\inf\mathcal{V}(f_{n})=\lim\sup\mathcal{V}(f_{n})
, and a point
z
lies in this set if and only if either

1.

there is a unique dominant index
k
at
z
and
\alpha_{k}(z)=0
; or

2.

there are two or more dominant indices at
z
.

Note that the locus where
1.
holds consists of isolated points, whereas
2.
leads to curves in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\simeq\mathbb{C}
. A small subtlety arises when one tries to apply Theorem
4.4
to the expression (
21
): the coefficients
c_{\sigma}(\lambda)
are potentially non-analytic at Stokes curves, see Remark
3.6
. This is the main point that needs to be addressed in the proof below.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

From Proposition
2.6
we see that in the
n\to\infty
limit, all zeros of
A_{n}^{V}(\lambda)
come from zeros of the integral
I_{n}^{V}(\lambda)=\int_{S^{d-1}}V(\bm{x},\lambda)^{n}\,\omega_{S^{d-1}}
.
By Corollary
3.4
, in the large-
n
limit and under assumptions
(A1)–(A4)
, this integral asymptotically becomes

I_{n}^{V}(\lambda)\sim\sum_{\sigma\in\crit(F_{\lambda})}\tilde{c}_{\sigma}(\lambda)e^{nF_{\lambda}(\sigma)}=:\tilde{I}_{n}^{V}(\lambda),

(23)

where we absorbed all coefficients in
\tilde{c}_{\sigma}(\lambda)
. By assumption, this expression is valid for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma
. The (isolated) points in
\Sigma
potentially lie in
\mathcal{P}
, so we now assume
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma
. The coefficient
c_{\sigma}
is not identically zero on certain regions
D\in\mathcal{S}^{c}_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}
, see Remark
3.6
. If there are several critical points
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
with the same evaluations
F_{\lambda}(\sigma)=F_{\lambda}(\sigma^{\prime})
, we collect the coefficients into one
\alpha_{k}(\lambda),~k=1,\dots,m
.
The result now follows from Theorem
4.4
except for the potential non-analyticity of
\alpha_{k}
along Stokes curves.

The only thing left to show is that if
\lambda_{0}\in S_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\setminus\bigcup\{\text{anti-Stokes curves}\}
, then
\lambda_{0}\notin\lim\sup\mathcal{V}(A_{n}^{V})
or
\lambda_{0}
is isolated. Since
\lambda_{0}
is not on any anti-Stokes curve, w.l.o.g. let
\sigma
be the unique dominant critical point at
\lambda_{0}
.
Let
\epsilon>0
be small such that
\overline{B}_{\epsilon}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,|\lambda-\lambda_{0}|\leq\epsilon\}
is contained in a single component of the anti-Stokes curve arrangement complement and does not intersect any other Stokes curve.
There are several cases to consider.
If
c_{\sigma}
and
c_{\sigma^{\prime}}
are both identically zero on
\overline{B}_{\epsilon}
, we can disregard
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
from (
23
) and may assume that all the remaining coefficients are analytic on
\overline{B}_{\epsilon}
, so the claim follows readily from Theorem
4.4
.
If
\lambda_{0}
is an isolated zero of both
c_{\sigma}
and
c_{\sigma^{\prime}}
, then
\lambda_{0}
lies potentially in
\mathcal{P}
and we disregard this case.

Finally, assume that
c_{\sigma}
is nonzero in the intersection of
\overline{B}_{\epsilon}
with a
one-sided
neighbourhood of
\lambda_{0}
, i.e. there exists
D\in\mathcal{S}^{c}_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}
such that
c_{\sigma}
is nonzero on
\overline{B}_{\epsilon}\cap\overline{D}
.
Note that even though
c_{\sigma}
might not be analytic at
\lambda_{0}
it has well-defined one-sided limits. We now adapt Sokal’s argument in his proof of Theorem
4.4
. There exist constants
\delta>0
and
M<\infty
such that for all
\lambda\in\overline{B}_{\epsilon}\cap\overline{D}
we have
|\tilde{c}_{\sigma}(\lambda)|\geq\delta,~|\tilde{c}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\lambda)/\tilde{c}_{\sigma}(\lambda)|\leq M
for all
\sigma^{\prime}\neq\sigma
,
|e^{F_{\lambda}(\sigma)}|\geq\delta
and
|e^{F_{\lambda}(\sigma^{\prime})}/e^{F_{\lambda}(\sigma)}|\leq 1-\delta
for all
\sigma^{\prime}\neq\sigma
. Then we have

|\tilde{I}_{n}^{V}(\lambda)|\geq\delta^{n+1}\left(1-(m-1)M(1-\delta)^{n}\right)

for all
\lambda\in\overline{B}_{\epsilon}\cap\overline{D}
. Therefore,
\tilde{I}_{n}^{V}(\lambda)
is non-vanishing on
\overline{B}_{\epsilon}\cap\overline{D}
for sufficiently large
n
and hence so is
I_{n}^{V}(\lambda)
. Thus,
\lambda\notin\lim\sup\mathcal{V}(A_{n}^{V})
, which concludes the proof.
∎

We conclude this section with a remark regarding the practicality of Theorem
4.1
.

Remark 4.5
.

Even though Theorem
4.1
is not explicit about which anti-Stokes curves and what parts of them appear as the limit objects, in practice one can decide this computationally via at least two different approaches.

The first is to determine the Stokes structure in detail, i.e. determining which thimbles actually contribute to the sum (
23
). The integral
I_{n}^{V}
is a holonomic function. Knowing the Lefschetz thimble decomposition at a single point (for example from the asymptotics for real
\lambda

[
BMW26
]
), one can then numerically (with high precision) make an analytic continuation to a complex
\lambda
using, for example, the software by Mezzarobba
[
Mez16
]
.

The second approach is to explicitly compute the zeros of
A_{n}^{V}
for some large
n
and observe which anti-Stokes curves are in proximity to the observed zeros. If one makes a careful analysis of the constants appearing in the proof of Theorem
4.1
and in Corollary
3.4
and one chooses
n
sufficiently large, this heuristic can be turned into a rigorous proof.

5
Connections to the Ising model

In this last section we establish connections to the Ising model and show how the edge-coloured graph counting framework unifies the theories of Lee–Yang and of Fisher zeros.
The main focus of this section is on the polynomial

W(x_{1},x_{2},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{h}):=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\mathcal{J}^{i}\mathcal{h}^{i\bmod 2}\frac{x_{1}^{i}x_{2}^{k-i}}{i!\cdot k!}.

(24)

This polynomial specialises to two important instances:

V_{\rm Ising}^{(1)}(x_{1},x_{2},\lambda):={\left.\kern-1.2ptW(x_{1},x_{2},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{h})\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\mathcal{J}=\sqrt{\lambda},\mathcal{h}=0}}~,\quad V_{\rm Ising}^{(2)}(x_{1},x_{2},\lambda):={\left.\kern-1.2ptW(x_{1},x_{2},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{h})\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\mathcal{J}=1,\mathcal{h}=\lambda}}.

For
k=4
,
V_{\rm Ising}^{(1)}(x_{1},x_{2},\lambda)
is the polynomial from Example
2.5
. We argue that for
V_{\text{Ising}}
, the polynomial
A_{n}^{V_{\text{Ising}}}(\lambda)
can be interpreted as an
average Ising model partition function
on a
k
-regular graph. The parameter
\mathcal{J}
is a temperature-like parameter, while
\mathcal{h}
corresponds to an external magnetic field parameter. The main theorem applied to
V_{\rm Ising}^{(2)}
recovers a version of the original Lee–Yang theorem which we recall below. The main result applied to
V_{\rm Ising}^{(1)}
can be interpreted as a result on the accumulation of Fisher zeros of a random regular graph. We explain these connections in the following.

Let
G
be a (monocoloured) graph with vertices
V_{G}=\{1,\dots,|V_{G}|\}
. We denote an edge between vertices
i
and
j
by a multiset
\{i,j\}\in E_{G}
. The Ising model
[
Len20
]
on
G
models the properties of a magnet depending on temperature, local interactions and an external magnetic field. To each vertex
i
one associates a magnetic spin
\sigma_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}
. The Hamiltonian
H
of the model accounts for nearest neighbour interactions, as well as external magnetic field parameters at each vertex,

H(\sigma)=-\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E_{G}}J_{ij}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}-\sum_{i\in V_{G}}h_{i}\sigma_{i}.

Here,
J_{ij}\geq 0
are ferromagnetic coupling parameters and
h_{i}
are fugacities determining the external magnetic fields. Then the canonical
partition function
of the Ising model is

Z_{G}(h_{1},\dots,h_{|V_{G}|},\beta;J_{ij})=\sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm 1\}^{|V_{G}|}}e^{-\beta H(\sigma)},

where
\beta=(k_{B}T)^{-1}
is the inverse thermodynamic temperature (
k_{B}
is the Boltzmann constant). Before we draw the connection to
A_{n}^{V_{\rm Ising}}
, let us recall the classic Lee–Yang theorem
[
LY52
]
. We state it in the form as presented in
[
BB09
]
, setting
\beta=1
.

Theorem 5.1

(Lee–Yang)
.

Let
J_{ij}\geq 0
. The partition function
Z_{G}(h_{1},\dots,h_{|V_{G}|},1;J_{ij})
does not vanish whenever
\mathfrak{R}(h_{i})>0
for all
i=1,\dots,|V_{G}|
. In particular, all zeros of the univariate polynomial
Z_{G}(h,\dots,h,1;J_{ij})
lie on the imaginary axis.

We now make the common simplification to assume all fugacities
h_{i}
to be equal to
h
. A convenient way to deal with the fugacity is to introduce
Griffiths’ ghost vertex

\mathfrak{g}\notin V_{G}
with a fixed spin
\sigma_{\mathfrak{g}}=+1
and to define the interaction
J_{i\mathfrak{g}}:=h

[
Gri67
]
. Then the original Ising model can be interpreted as an Ising model without external magnetic field on an augmented graph
G^{\prime}
with vertices
V_{G^{\prime}}=V_{G}\cup\{\mathfrak{g}\}
and edges
E_{G^{\prime}}=E_{G}\cup\{\{i,\mathfrak{g}\}\,:\,i\in V_{G}\}
.

The connection to edge-coloured graph enumeration becomes apparent in the van der Waerden
high energy expansion
, see e.g.
[
Dum16
, §2.2.1]
.
It expresses
Z_{G}(\beta,J)
as

Z_{G}(h,\beta;J_{ij})=2^{|V_{G}|}\left(\prod_{\{i,j\}\in E^{\prime}}\cosh(\beta J_{ij})\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma^{\prime}\subseteq G^{\prime}\\
\gamma^{\prime}\setminus\mathfrak{g}\text{ Eulerian}\end{subarray}}\prod_{\{i,j\}\in E_{\gamma^{\prime}}}\tanh(\beta J_{ij}).

(25)

The sum on the right-hand side of (
25
) ranges over all “almost-Eulerian” subgraphs of
G^{\prime}
, by which we mean graphs all of whose vertices except
\mathfrak{g}
have even degree.
Let
\gamma^{\prime}\subseteq G^{\prime}
be such a subgraph and let
v\in V_{\gamma^{\prime}}\setminus\{\mathfrak{g}\}
be a vertex. Then either
v
is incident in
\gamma^{\prime}
to an even number of half-edges that belong to the original graph
G
, or
v
is incident in
\gamma^{\prime}
to an odd number of half-edges of
G
and is connected with
\mathfrak{g}
.
Therefore, the sum in (
25
) can be taken over all subgraphs
\gamma\subseteq G
, where a vertex comes with an additional contribution of
\tanh(\beta h)
if it is incident to an odd number of half-edges in
\gamma
.

Given a
k
-regular graph
G
and a subgraph
\gamma
of
G
, we can define an edge-colouring on
G
with two colours, depending on whether an edge belongs to
\gamma
. Moreover, the contributions
\tanh(\beta J_{ij})
can be interpreted as vertex degree markings. Let us assume all interaction parameters
J_{ij}
to be equal to a single parameter
J
for all
i,j\in V_{G}
. Setting
\mathcal{J}=\sqrt{\tanh(\beta J)}
and
\mathcal{h}=\tanh(\beta h)
, a vertex that is incident to
i
half-edges of the colour indicating
\gamma
contributes
\mathcal{J}^{i}\mathcal{h}^{i\bmod 2}
to the sum in (
25
). Therefore,
Z_{G}
simplifies to

Z_{G}(h,\beta;J)=(2h)^{|V_{G}|}\cosh(\beta J)^{|E_{G}|}\sum_{\gamma\subseteq G}\prod_{v\in V_{G}}\mathcal{J}^{\deg_{\gamma}(v)}\mathcal{h}^{\deg_{\gamma}(v)\bmod 2},

where
\deg_{\gamma}(v)
denotes the number of half-edges in
\gamma
incident to
v
. Note that the prefactors
(2h)^{|V_{G}|}\cosh(\beta J)^{|E_{G}|}
could also be absorbed into vertex degree markings, but we ignore this since it does not change the zeros of
A_{n}^{W}
. With this we obtain the following interpretation of
A_{n}^{W}
as an average Ising model partition function.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
W=W(x_{1},x_{2},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{h})
be as in (
24
). Then the polynomial
A_{n}^{W}
satisfies

A_{n}^{W}(\mathcal{h},\mathcal{J})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\text{ $k$-regular}\\
\chi(G)=-n\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|\Aut(G)|}\sum_{\gamma\subseteq G}\prod_{v\in V_{G}}\mathcal{J}^{\deg_{\gamma}(v)}\mathcal{h}^{\deg_{\gamma}(v)\bmod 2}.

For the purpose of the automorphism group,
G
is considered monocoloured.

Proof.

The only thing left to show is that if there are exactly
m
subgraphs
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{m}\subseteq G
giving rise to the same isomorphism class of edge-bicoloured graphs
[\Gamma_{1}]=\dots=[\Gamma_{m}]
, then
|\Aut(\Gamma_{i})|=|\Aut(G)|/m
.
Let
G
have
2w
many half-edges, while
\Gamma_{i}
(for any
i
) has
2w_{j}
half-edges of the
j^{\text{th}}
colour. Note that
w=w_{1}+w_{2}
. The group
\mathfrak{S}_{2w}
naturally acts on the half-edge labels of
\Gamma
, but also on the disjoint union
H_{1}\sqcup H_{2}
of half-edge labels of
\Gamma_{i}
. In particular, we obtain an action of
\Aut(G)
on the set of edge-coloured half-edge labelled graphs, and
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{m}
form an orbit of this action. Since the stabiliser is
\Aut(\Gamma_{i})
, the claim follows from the orbit-stabiliser theorem.
∎

To apply our main result, we need to specialise the two-parameter polynomial
A_{n}^{W}(\mathcal{h},\mathcal{J})
to a single parameter. There are two natural ways to do that. The first is as follows: we set the fugacity
\mathcal{h}=0
to zero and consider zeros in the parameter
\mathcal{J}
. For convenience, we also make the substitution
\mathcal{J}^{2}=\lambda
to get the polynomial

V_{\rm Ising}^{(1)}(x_{1},x_{2},\lambda)={\left.\kern-1.2ptW(x_{1},x_{2},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{h})\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\mathcal{J}=\sqrt{\lambda},\mathcal{h}=0}}.

With this we obtain the following expression

A_{n}^{V_{\rm Ising}^{(1)}}(\lambda)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\text{ $k$-regular}\\
\chi(G)=-n\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|\Aut(G)|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\subseteq G\\
\gamma\text{ Eulerian}\end{subarray}}\lambda^{|E_{\gamma}|}.

The zeros of
A_{n}^{V_{\rm Ising}^{(1)}}(\lambda)
can be interpreted as
Fisher zeros

[
Fis65
]
of the Ising model on a
random

k
-regular graph. Here, by random we mean a graph drawn according to a probability distribution proportional to
|\Aut(G)|^{-1}
. For
k=4
, this case has served as the running example in this paper.
Another natural way to specialise
W
is to set
\mathcal{J}=1
and to consider zeros in the fugacity parameter
\lambda=\mathcal{h}
, so we set

V_{\rm Ising}^{(2)}(x_{1},x_{2},\lambda)={\left.\kern-1.2ptW(x_{1},x_{2},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{h})\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\mathcal{J}=1,\mathcal{h}=\lambda}}.

This brings us to the set-up of the classical Lee–Yang theorem, except that we are now dealing with the Ising model partition function of a random regular graph in the sense above. Indeed, among the critical points of
V_{\rm Ising}^{(2)}
on
\mathbb{S}
there are, for any
k\geq 3
, the two points
\sigma_{1}=(\sqrt{2}/2,\sqrt{2}/2),~\sigma_{2}=(\sqrt{2}/2,-\sqrt{2}/2)
. After a straightforward computation, the corresponding anti-Stokes curve is seen to be the imaginary axis,

\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,|V_{\rm Ising}^{(2)}(\sigma_{1},\lambda)|=|V_{\rm Ising}^{(2)}(\sigma_{2},\lambda)|\right\}=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,\mathfrak{R}\lambda=0\right\},

in accordance with the classical Lee–Yang theorem.

Equivariant Weiss Calculus
Prasit Bhattacharya, Yang Hu

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce an equivariant analog of Weiss calculus of functors for all finite group G \mathrm{G} . In our theory, Taylor approximations and derivatives are index by finite dimensional G \mathrm{G} -representations, and homogeneous layers are classified by orthogonal G \mathrm{G} -spectra. Further, our framework permits a notion of restriction as well as a notion of fixed-point at the level of Weiss functors. We establish various results comparing Taylor approximations and derivatives of fixed-point (resp. restrictions) functors to that of the fixed-point (resp. restrictions) of Taylor approximations and derivatives.

Future directions.

In
Main Theorem

2
and
Main Theorem

3
, we see restrictions to finite sums of
1
-dimensional representations. A part of these restrictions are due to technical reasons as our key results such as
Theorem

4.1
and
Lemma

5.7
do not seem to generalize if
\upepsilon
has dimension bigger than
1
. These restrictions are also imposed due to the philosophy that the role of ‘direction’ in Weiss calculus is played by
1
-dimensional subspaces. It will be interesting to see if it is possible to overcome these technical and philosophical barriers so that the
\upalpha
-homogeneous layer is a genuine
\mathrm{G}
-equivariant infinite loop space classified using a genuine
\mathrm{G}
-spectrum.

In this paper, we restrict our attention to finite groups to make sure that the regular representation is finite dimensional. However, it will be of great interest to see if the equivariant Weiss calculus presented in this paper can be extended to compact Lie groups, and more generally, in the framework of global homotopy theory
[
Sch18
]
. In the nonequivariant case, one can compare Weiss calculus with Goodwillie calculus (see
[
ADL08
]
). Thus, one may also ask if our equivariant Weiss calculus can be related to equivariant Goodwillie calculus of
[
Dot17
]
.

A significant portion of the literature on Weiss Calculus is dedicated to identifying derivatives at
\infty
of important Weiss functors (as in (
1.3
))
[
WW88
,
WW89
,
Aro02
,
Aro09
,
RW23
,
KRW
]
. We are cautiously optimistic that the work of Arone
[
Aro02
]
can be generalized to identify the derivatives of equivariant Weiss functors such as
{\bf B_{\mathrm{G}}U},{\bf B_{\mathrm{G}}O}
, and
{\bf BU}_{\mathbb{R}}
. Such results will lead to enumeration of unstable equivariant vector bundles extending the recent results of
[
Hu23
,
CHO
]
to equivariant homotopy theory.

An important result in the nonequivariant Weiss theory is the calculation of the first derivative of
\mathrm{B}\mathrm{Top}(-)
. The classical work of Waldhausen
[
Wal82
]
shows that the first derivative of
\mathrm{B}\mathrm{Top}(-)
is the Waldhausen

-theory of a point. In 2019, Malkiewich and Merling

]
constructed a genuine equivariant Wadhausen

-theory functor

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bf A}_{\mathrm{G}}(-):\mathrm{G}\Top}

and therefore, it is conceivable that there exists an incomplete version of equivariant
\mathrm{A}
-theory functor, say
{\bf A}_{\mathrm{G}}^{\prime}
, whose codomain is
\Sp^{\mathrm{G}}_{(1)}
(instead of
\Sp^{\mathrm{G}}
). If so, we expect:

{\bf\Theta}{\bf B}_{\mathrm{G}}{\bf Top}^{(1)}({\bf 0})

(

A Germline ETV6 Missense Variant in a Family With Thrombocytopenia and Leukemia Predisposition: A Case Report
Authors: Tomoko Waragai, Yoshiko Nakano, Kazuhiro Mochizuki, Atsushi Kikuta, Mitsuko Akaihata, Nobuhisa Takahashi, Shingo Kudo, Yui Sasaki, Motohiro Kato, Hideki Sano
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTGermline pathogenic variants in ETV6 are recognized as causes of inherited thrombocytopenia and leukemia predisposition. We report a family harboring a germline ETV6 variant, c.1072A>T (p.I358F), which has not been previously associated with this phenotype. Three family members exhibited chronic thrombocytopenia, and two had a history of acute lymphoblastic leukemia. The germline variant was located within the ETS DNA‐binding domain, where leukemia‐associated germline variants have been reported to cluster. This report underscores the need for careful family history assessment and long‐term follow‐up to identify hereditary leukemia predisposition syndromes and ensure appropriate genetic evaluation.Trial RegistrationThe authors have confirmed clinical trial registration is not needed for this submission.

Introduction

ETV6 (ETS variant transcription factor 6) is a transcription factor essential for hematopoietic differentiation and platelet production. Germline ETV6 variants were initially reported in 2015 as risk factors for hereditary thrombocytopenia and hematologic malignancies, most frequently B‐cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia [1, 2, 3, 4]. Patients with ETV6‐associated thrombocytopenia typically present with lifelong mild‐to‐moderate thrombocytopenia and an increased risk of developing leukemia. Because germline ETV6 predisposition variants have only been recognized relatively recently, patients diagnosed and treated for leukemia in childhood or adolescence may not have undergone a genetic evaluation at the time of diagnosis. As a result, hereditary predisposition syndromes may remain unrecognized, particularly in individuals with mild cytopenia and limited bleeding [3, 5]. We herein report a family in which a previously unreported germline ETV6 missense variant was identified.

Case Presentation

Case 1 involved a diagnosis of B‐cell precursor acute lymphoblastic leukemia at 11 years of age (Figure 1). She achieved long‐term remission after receiving standard chemotherapy and prophylactic cranial irradiation. Despite complete remission, the platelet count ranged from approximately 30–100 × 109/L, indicating moderate‐to‐severe thrombocytopenia. No significant bleeding tendency was noted, and no secondary malignancy has been observed to date. A reevaluation of the family history during the follow‐up revealed additional family members with thrombocytopenia and leukemia, thus raising the suspicion of an inherited predisposition.

The elder sister of Case 1, Case 2, developed B‐cell precursor acute lymphoblastic leukemia at 18 years of age and achieved remission after chemotherapy. Even after achieving complete remission, she exhibited persistent mild thrombocytopenia, with platelet counts ranging from approximately 100–150 × 109/L. She did not experience any clinically significant bleeding, and no additional malignancies were observed. Genetic testing was not performed in the two sons because of the absence of thrombocytopenia.

Case 3 (daughter of Case 1) exhibited persistent moderate thrombocytopenia since childhood, with platelet counts ranging from approximately 60–100 × 109/L. No bleeding tendency or malignancy was identified, and her clinical condition was stable. Based on the long‐term follow‐up of Cases 1 and 2, the condition was diagnosed as familial thrombocytopenia. As the patient also exhibited thrombocytopenia, genetic testing was performed. The plan is to continue monitoring the patient while remaining vigilant for any potential hematological complications associated with the disease.

Genetic Analysis

A germline genetic analysis was initially performed in Case 1 as part of a pilot study of cancer predisposition syndrome (pCPS21). The study was centrally approved by the Research Ethics Committee of the University of Tokyo (No. 2021226G), and permission to conduct this research was granted by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Fukushima Medical University (approval number: General 2021‐281). Written informed consent for this study and publication was obtained from the patients and their family members. Genomic DNA extracted from peripheral blood was used to sequence all ETV6 exons and exon–intron boundaries. A heterozygous missense variant (c.1072A>T [p.I358F]) was identified. The same variant was identified in Cases 2 and 3. According to the American College of Medical Genetics and the Association for Molecular Pathology (ACMG/AMP) criteria, the variant was interpreted as having uncertain significance (VUS) (PM1_supporting, PM2_supporting, PP1_supporting, and PP4_supporting) [6].

Discussion

In this report, we identified a germline ETV6 missense variant, c.1072A>T (p.I358F), in a family with inherited thrombocytopenia and leukemia predisposition. Several findings support the potential pathogenic relevance of this variant. The variant has been registered as rs1235766887 and it is considered extremely rare in the general population, as it was not recorded in gnomAD, which includes 36,070 East Asian samples, while it was reported in a single case from the European Variation Archive (EVA), and had an unknown frequency in the Japanese Multi Omics Reference Panel (jMorp) at the time of this writing. The variant was located within the functional ETS DNA‐binding domain, where germline variants associated with leukemia predisposition, including missense variants, have been reported to cluster [7, 8, 9]. These findings suggest a possible association between this variant and a predisposition to thrombocytopenia and leukemia.

An additional aspect of this case is that the long‐term follow‐up enabled the recognition of persistent thrombocytopenia and familial clustering across generations, thus prompting reconsideration of an underlying hereditary predisposition. Because germline ETV6‐associated thrombocytopenia has only been recognized relatively recently, historical cases may not have undergone genetic evaluation at the time of diagnosis. The identification of this variant provides an opportunity for anticipatory guidance and education regarding the potential risk of leukemia. The functional validation and accumulation of additional cases are required to clarify the pathogenicity of p.I358F. Nevertheless, careful documentation of such variants in well‐characterized families is essential for future reclassification and an improved understanding of germline predisposition syndromes.

Conclusion

We report an ETV6 variant (p.I358F) that has not been previously reported in association with a predisposition to leukemia and thrombocytopenia. This rare variant may have pathogenic relevance, given its localization within the ETS DNA‐binding domain and the clinical features observed in affected family members. In this case, the long‐term follow‐up provided the clinical context that enabled the recognition of this hereditary predisposition, thus leading to the identification of a possibly causative variant and germline testing for relatives.

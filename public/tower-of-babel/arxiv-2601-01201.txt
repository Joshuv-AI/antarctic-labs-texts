Remark Concerning Cesàro Operator on the Hardy Space H p ​ ( ℂ + ) H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+}) in the Upper Half-Plane
Valentin V. Andreev, Miron B. Bekker, Joseph A. Cima

Abstract
. We consider Cesàro operator on the Hardy space H p ​ ( ℂ + ) H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+}) in the upper half-plane for 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty . In [ 3 ] it was proved that for all 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty the spectrum of the operator V = 2 ​ ( p − 1 ) p ​ C − I V=\frac{2(p-1)}{p}C-I is located on the unit circle and in [ 1 ] the authors of this note showed that for p = 2 p=2 operator V V is unitary. In the present note we show that for 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty , p ≠ 2 p\neq 2 , the norm of the operator V V is strictly greater than one.

The Cesàro operator on the Hardy space
H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
in the upper half-plane was introduced in
[
3
]
. It is defined by the expression

(1)

(Cf)(z)=\frac{1}{z}\int_{0}^{z}f(\zeta)d\zeta,\quad f\in H^{p}(C_{+}).

In
[
3
]
was proved that for
1<p\leq\infty
the operator
C
is bounded on
H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
and for
1<p<\infty
its spectrum
\sigma(C)
is given by

(2)

\sigma(C)=\{w:|w-\frac{p}{2(p-1)}|=\frac{p}{2(p-1)}\}.

Authors of
[
3
]
also proved that the point spectrum of the operator
C
is empty.

Define an operator
V
on
H^{p}(C_{+})
by the formula

(3)

V=\frac{2(p-1)}{p}C-I.

By the Spectral Mapping theorem the spectrum of
V
is the unit circle,

(4)

\sigma(V)=\{w:|w|=1\}.

In
[
1
]
authors of the present note proved that for
p=2
the operator
V
is unitary on
H^{2}(C_{+})
. Further properties of the Cesàro operator on

H^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
are investigated in
[
2
]
.

Since the spectral radius of
V
is equal to one for all
p\in(1,\infty)
, we have
\|V\|_{p}\geq 1
, where
\|V\|_{p}
means the norm of
V
considered as an operator on
H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
.

Theorem 1
.

Let the operator
V
on
H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
(
1<p<\infty
) be defined by formulas (
1
) and (
3
). If
p\neq 2
then
\|V\|_{p}>1
.

Proof
. To prove the theorem we exibit a vector
f
which belongs to
H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
and show that for all such
p

\|Vf\|_{p}\geq\|f\|_{p}
with equality only for
p=2
.

Put

f_{t}(z)=\frac{-it}{(-it-z)^{2}},\quad t>0.

The function
f_{t}
belongs to
H^{p}
and

(5)

\|f_{t}\|^{p}_{p}=\frac{1}{t^{p-1}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(x^{2}+1)^{p}}dx=\frac{1}{t^{p-1}}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(-\dfrac{1}{2}+p)}{\Gamma(p)}.

Then

(Cf_{t})=\frac{1}{-it-z}

and

(Vf_{t})(z)=\frac{2(p-1)}{p}\frac{-i\gamma t-z}{(-it-z)^{2}},

where
\gamma=\gamma(p)=\dfrac{2-p}{2(p-1)}
. Simple calculations give

(6)

\|Vf_{t}\|^{p}_{p}=\frac{1}{t^{p-1}}\left[\frac{2(p-1)}{p}\right]^{p}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(x^{2}+\gamma^{2})^{p/2}}{(x^{2}+1)^{p}}dx.

Since
\gamma(p)=0
only for
p=2
it follows that

\displaystyle\|Vf_{t}\|^{p}_{p}\geq\frac{1}{t^{p-1}}\left[\frac{2(p-1)}{p}\right]^{p}2\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p}}{(x^{2}+1)^{p}}dx=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{p-1}}2\sqrt{\pi}\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p-1}\frac{\Gamma(\dfrac{p}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(\dfrac{p}{2}+1)}

with equality only for
p=2
. Therefore

\|Vf_{t}\|_{p}^{p}/\|f_{t}\|^{p}\geq 2\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p-1}\frac{\Gamma(\dfrac{p}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2})\Gamma(p)}{\Gamma(\dfrac{p}{2}+1)\Gamma(p-\dfrac{1}{2})}

Put

(7)

\Phi(p)=\frac{2(p-1)^{p-1}}{p^{p-1}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{p}{2}\right)\Gamma(p)}{\Gamma\left(\dfrac{p}{2}+1\right)\Gamma\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}+p\right)}.

Then

\displaystyle\Phi(p)=\frac{4(p-1)^{p-1}}{p^{p}}\frac{\Gamma(\dfrac{p}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2})\Gamma(p)}{\Gamma(\dfrac{p}{2})\Gamma(p-\dfrac{1}{2})}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}(p-1)^{p-1}\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)^{p}\frac{\Gamma^{2}(\frac{p}{2}+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(p-\frac{1}{2})}.

Taking logarithmic derivative of both sides one obtains

\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(p)}{\Phi(p)}=\ln{\left(2(1-\frac{1}{p})\right)}+\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(\frac{p}{2}+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{p}{2}+\frac{1}{2})}-\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(p-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(p-\frac{1}{2})}.

Using well known representation of logarithmic derivativa of gamma-function one obtains

(8)

\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(p)}{\Phi(p)}=\ln{\left(2(1-\frac{1}{p})\right)}+(1-\frac{p}{2})\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+p-1/2)(n+p/2+1/2)}.

We see, in particular, that
\Phi^{\prime}(2)=0
and
\Phi^{\prime}(p)\to-\infty
as
p\to 1^{+}
. The series in the right side of (
8
) converges uniformly in
p
for
p>1
, therefore it can be differentiated term-by-term. We have

\displaystyle\frac{\Phi^{\prime\prime}(2)}{\Phi(2)}=\frac{d}{dp}\left(\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(p)}{\Phi(p)}\right)(2)=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+3/2)^{2}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(1-4\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2n+3)^{2}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(5-\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}\right),

where we used the well known formula

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2n-1)^{2}}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}.

Thus,
\Phi^{\prime\prime}(2)>0
and
p=2
is a minimum of the function
\Phi
. From (
7
) it follows that
\Phi(2)=1
. Further analysis indicates that the function
\Phi
monitonically increases for
p>2
.

Subgroups of symmetric groups: enumeration and asymptotic properties
Colva M. Roney-Dougal and Gareth Tracey

Abstract
In this paper, we prove that the symmetric group S n {\mathrm{S}}_{n} has 2 n 2 / 16 + o ⁡ ( n 2 ) 2^{n^{2}/16+o(n^{2})} subgroups, settling a conjecture of Pyber from 1993. We also derive asymptotically sharp upper and lower bounds on the number of subgroups of S n {\mathrm{S}}_{n} of various kinds, including the number of p p -subgroups. In addition, we prove a range of theorems about random subgroups of S n {\mathrm{S}}_{n} . In particular, we prove the surprising result that for infinitely many n n , the probability that a random subgroup of S n {\mathrm{S}}_{n} is nilpotent is bounded away from 1 1 . Introduction

In this paper, we count the subgroups of the symmetric group on
n
points, and determine many group theoretic properties of a random such subgroup. In doing so, we resolve conjectures of Kantor and Pyber from the early 1990s.

Many enumeration problems in algebra and combinatorics can be reduced to the problem of counting the subgroups of some associated group, either absolutely or up to some form of equivalence.
A prime example is Galois theory: by enumerating subgroups (or certain types of subgroups) of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
, one gets precise information on the number of intermediate fields in a field extension of degree
n
.

As a second example, the number of labelled vertex transitive graphs on
n
vertices is at most the product of
2^{n-1}
and the number of minimal transitive subgroups of the symmetric group
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
. This observation led Babai and Sós to the current best known upper bounds on the number of such graphs
[
3
]
.

Motivated by a stunning application to the enumeration problem for isomorphism classes of groups of a given order, Pyber proved in 1993
[
16
]
that for a positive integer
n
, the number
|\mathrm{Sub}({\mathrm{S}}_{n})|
of subgroups of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
is at most
2^{\xi n^{2}+o(n^{2})}
where
\xi=\frac{1}{6}\log{24}
(our logarithms are to the base
2
).
Pyber conjectured, however, that

|\mathrm{Sub}({\mathrm{S}}_{n})|=2^{n^{2}/16+o(n^{2})}
.
In this paper, we prove a strong form of Pyber’s conjecture.

Theorem 1
.

There exist absolute constants
\alpha>0
and
\beta
such that for all integers
n>1

2^{n^{2}/16+\alpha n\log{n}}\leq|\mathrm{Sub}({\mathrm{S}}_{n})|\leq 2^{n^{2}/16+\beta n^{3/2}}.

As a by-product of our methods, we prove considerably tighter bounds on the number
|\mathrm{Sub}_{p}({\mathrm{S}}_{n})|
of
p
-subgroups of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
p
be a prime. There exist constants
\beta_{p}>\alpha_{p}>0
such that for all integers
n\geq p
,

p^{n^{2}/(4p^{2})}2^{\alpha_{p}n\log n}\leq|\mathrm{Sub}_{p}({\mathrm{S}}_{n})|\leq p^{n^{2}/(4p^{2})}2^{\beta_{p}n\log{n}}.

Furthermore, if
p\geq 11
then
\beta_{p}\leq 3/2
.

Notice that since there are most
n!<2^{n\log n}
conjugates in
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
of a given subgroup, both of these theorems immediately transfer to conjugacy classes of subgroups, by making the second coefficients in the lower bounds smaller.

For a property
\mathcal{P}
, we write
\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{P}}(G)
for the set of subgroups of
G
satisfying
\mathcal{P}
. For a prime
p
, let
\mathcal{N}_{p}
be the property of being nilpotent, and of order divisible by no primes less than
p
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
p
be a prime. Then there exists an absolute constant
\gamma(p)
such that

|\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{N}_{p}}({\mathrm{S}}_{n})|\leq p^{n^{2}/(4p^{2})}2^{\gamma(p)n\log n}.

In particular the number of nilpotent subgroups of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
satisfies

|\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathrm{nilp}}({\mathrm{S}}_{n})|\leq 2^{n^{2}/16+\gamma(2)n\log n}.

We remark that it was recently proved by Kassabov, Tyburski and Wilson
[
12
]
that
\mathrm{A}_{n}
(and hence also
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
) has at least
2^{n^{2}/36-o(n^{2})}

isomorphism classes
of subgroups.

One of our motivations for enumerating subgroups of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
is an immediate application to computational group theory. At present, the analysis of algorithms for permutation groups is generally only of worst-case performance, whilst in many other disciplines the study of average case or generic-case performance is widespread. The reason for this deficiency is our lack of understanding up to now of the properties of a random permutation group. The present article is a first step in remedying this problem.

By saying that a
random subgroup of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
has property
\mathcal{P}
, we mean that the probability that a uniform random subgroup of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
satisfies
\mathcal{P}
tends to
1
as
n
tends to

\infty
. For example, Lucchini, Menegazzo and Morigi proved in
[
15
]
that there exists an absolute constant
b
such that the number of transitive subgroup of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
is at most
2^{bn^{2}/\sqrt{\log n}}
, and hence deduced that a random subgroup of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
is intransitive.

Nilpotency is thought to be a prevalent property amongst many natural sequences of finite groups. Indeed, a conjecture attributed to Erdős, amongst others, states that the number of isomorphism classes of groups of order
2^{x}
is greater than or equal to the number of isomorphism classes of groups of order
n
for all
n\leq 2^{x}
. In
[
16
]
, Pyber goes further by conjecturing that a finite group chosen uniformly amongst the groups of order up to
n
is nilpotent, while in
[
11
]
, Kantor states the following permutation group theoretic version:

Conjecture

(Kantor’s conjecture)
.

A random subgroup of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
is nilpotent.

Kantor remarks in
[
11
]
that if this conjecture is true, then the study of average-case complexity for permutation groups is unlikely to prove insightful. In this paper, we disprove Kantor’s conjecture.

Theorem 4
.

Let
n
be congruent to
3
modulo
4
. Then the probability that a random subgroup of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
is nilpotent is bounded away from
1
,
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

As an example of how the tools we develop can be used to study the distribution of subgroups of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
, we also prove two further results about random subgroups.

Theorem 5
.

A random nilpotent subgroup of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
is a
2
-group.

Theorem 6
.

Let
\mu
be a real number in
[0,1/16)
and let
\nu
be a real number in
[0,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4})
. Then a random subgroup of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
has an elementary abelian
2
-section of order at least
2^{\mu n}
, and a Sylow 2-subgroup of order at least
2^{\nu n}
.

Aschbacher and Guralnick prove in
[
1
]
the highly influential result that any finite group can be generated by a soluble subgroup and one other element. This has
been widely applied to reduce
questions concerning all finite groups to questions concerning only finite soluble groups. A natural follow-up question therefore is to ask what other similar reduction results are available. In particular, is it true that a finite group can be generated by a nilpotent subgroup, together with some “small” subset of other elements? This question was addressed in
[
1
]
, where a construction is given of infinite families of finite groups which cannot be generated by two nilpotent subgroups.

Despite this, we prove that one can control the number of additional elements required to generate an arbitrary finite group with a nilpotent subgroup. To state our result, we need two
definitions.
We set
\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}^{0}(G):=1
, and for
i\geq 1
, define
\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}^{i}(G)
so that
\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}^{i}(G)/\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}^{i-1}(G)=\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G/\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}^{i-1}(G))
. The
socle length

\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(G)
of a finite group
G
is the minimal
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}^{i}(G)=G
. Thus, for example,
if
G
is a finite
p
-group then the nilpotency class
\mathrm{cl}(G)\leq\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(G)\leq\log_{p}{|G|}
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we write
\Omega(n)
for the number of prime divisors of
n
, counted with multiplicities.
For a finite group
G
we define

\mathop{\mathrm{ma}}(G)=\max\{\Omega{(|A|)}\text{ : }A\text{ an abelian section of }G\}.

Our result can now be stated as follows.

Theorem 7
.

Let
G
be a finite soluble group. Then
G
can be generated by a nilpotent subgroup together with
4\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(G)\sqrt{\mathop{\mathrm{ma}}(G)}
other elements. Therefore, if
H
is an arbitrary finite group then
H
can be generated by a nilpotent subgroup and
4\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(H)\sqrt{\mathop{\mathrm{ma}}(H)}+1
elements.

We finish these introductory remarks with three questions. Firstly,
does there in fact exist an absolute constant
\gamma
such that
|\mathrm{Sub}({\mathrm{S}}_{n})|\leq 2^{n^{2}/16+\gamma n\log{n}}
? Secondly, is it possible that the probability that a random subgroup of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
is nilpotent tends to
0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
? Thirdly, does there exist an absolute constant
C
such that a random subgroup of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
has no orbits of length greater than
C
? Whilst this third question at first sounds improbable, we present evidence in Section
6
that it may have a positive answer.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we collect notation that will be used throughout the paper
and present various elementary results. Then we begin our detailed study of subdirect products:
in Section
3
we present some new, general results on enumerating subgroups of subdirect products
of finite groups,
in Section
4
we develop new tools for counting homomorphisms between finite
p
groups, and in Section
5
we use a mixture of computational and theoretical tools to bound normal generator numbers for
p
-subgroups of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
.
In Section
6
we
prove an intriguing result that to enumerate various classes of subgroups of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
, it suffices to consider those with all orbits of bounded length.
Then we use this in Section
7
to prove Theorem
2
, with Theorem
3
as an easy corollary. In Section
8
we prove Theorem
7
, and then immediately use it to prove Theorem
1
, and hence Theorem
6
. In our final section we first prove Theorem
5
, then Theorem
4
follows surprisingly easily.

Background: Goursat’s lemma, generation and commutators

In this section we shall present various elementary results, and fix much of the notation that will be used throughout the paper.

2.1
Subdirect products and Goursat’s Lemma

The bulk of our work will be concerned with counting intransitive subgroups of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
,
which will essentially amount to counting certain subgroups of direct products. A subgroup
G
of
D=G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{t}
is
subdirect
if
G\pi_{i}=G_{i}
for all
i
, where
\pi_{i}:G\rightarrow G_{i}
denotes the standard coordinate projection. When dealing with direct products like this, if the context leaves no room for confusion then we will identify
G_{i}
with its natural copy
\{(g_{1},\ldots,g_{t})\text{ : }g_{j}=1\text{ whenever }j\neq i\}
in
D
. Thus, we will often speak of
G_{i}
as a normal subgroup of
D
.

We
start with a standard lemma on the structure of subdirect products. By a
diagonal
subgroup of a direct product
K=K_{1}\times\cdots\times K_{\ell}
of pairwise isomorphic groups
K_{i}
, we mean a subgroup of the form
J=\{(a,a\theta_{2},\ldots,a\theta_{\ell})\text{ : }a\in K_{1}\}
, where
\theta_{i}:K_{1}\rightarrow K_{i}
are isomorphisms.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a subdirect product of
G_{1}\times G_{2}
. Then there exist normal subgroups
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
such that
G\cap G_{i}=N_{i}
. Furthermore,
G_{1}/N_{1}\cong G_{2}/N_{2}
, and
G/(N_{1}\times N_{2})
is isomorphic to a diagonal subgroup of
G_{1}/N_{1}\times G_{2}/N_{2}
.

We now fix some key notation that will be used throughout the paper.

Definition 2.2
.

For group theoretic properties
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
we write
\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}}(G)
for the set of subgroups of
G
satisfying
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
.
Similarly,

\mathrm{Subdir}_{\mathcal{P}}(G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{t})
will denote the set of subdirect products of
G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{t}
that satisfy
\mathcal{P}
. We write
\mathrm{nilp}
and
\mathrm{sol}
for nilpotency and solubility, respectively. For
G\in\mathrm{Subdir}(G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{t})
, we shall write

\pi_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}
for the projection to
G_{i_{1}}\times\cdots\times G_{i_{k}}
.

The primary mechanism for counting subgroups of direct products is Goursat’s lemma, a version of which we now state.
For finite groups
G
and
H
, we write
\mathrm{Hom}(G,H)
to denote the set of homomorphisms from
G
to
H
, and
\mathrm{Epi}(G,H)
for the set of epimorphisms.

Lemma 2.3

(
Goursat’s lemma
)
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be finite groups. Then the following hold.

(i)

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a group theoretic property. Then the number of subgroups
G
of
G_{1}\times G_{2}
in which

G\pi_{2}
has property
\mathcal{P}
is precisely

\sum_{H_{1}\in\mathrm{Sub}(G_{1}),H_{2}\in\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{P}}(G_{2})}|\mathrm{Hom}(H_{2},N_{G_{1}}(H_{1})/H_{1})|.

Therefore, if
\mathcal{P}
is such that whenever a subgroup of
G_{1}\times G_{2}
satisfies
\mathcal{P}
, so does its projection to
G_{2}
, then

|\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{P}}(G_{1}\times G_{2})|
is at most

|\mathrm{Sub}(G_{1})||\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{P}}(G_{2})|\max\{|\mathrm{Hom}(H_{2},N_{G_{1}}(H_{1})/H_{1})|\text{ : }H_{1}\in\mathrm{Sub}(G_{1}),H_{2}\in\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{P}}(G_{2})\}.

(ii)

Let
N_{1}
be a normal subgroup of
G_{1}
. Then the number of subdirect products
G
of
G_{1}\times G_{2}
such that
G\cap G_{1}=N_{1}
is precisely

|\mathrm{Epi}(G_{2},G_{1}/N_{1})|.

For groups
G
and
H
, our first upper bound for
|\mathrm{Hom}(G,H)|
is
|H|^{d(G)}
.

2.2
Basic bounds on subgroup enumeration and generation

Let
G
be a finite group. By a
G
-group
we mean a group on which
G
acts via automorphisms. For such a
G
-group
N
we will write
d_{G}(N)
for the minimal number of elements needed to generate
N
as a
G
-group, and
d(G)=d_{G}(G)
for the generator number of
G
.

If every subgroup of
G
can be generated by
s
elements, then
|\mathrm{Sub}(G)|\leq|G|^{s}
.
The following lemma gives improved bounds when
G
is a
p
-group, together with one related estimate.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
P
be a
p
-group of order
p^{\ell}
with
d(P)=d
. Then

(i)

P
has at most
\zeta_{p}p^{k(\ell-k)}
subgroups of order
p^{k}
, where
\zeta_{p}=\prod_{i\geq 1}(1-p^{i})^{-1}<4
. In particular,
|\mathrm{Sub}(P)|\leq 4\ell p^{\ell^{2}/4}
.

(ii)

If
b\leq d
then
P
has at least
p^{b(d-b)}
subgroups of index
p^{b}
.

(iii)

For all
d\geq 1
we can bound
|\mathrm{GL}_{d}(2)|\geq 2^{d^{2}-d}
.

Proof.

(i). The first statement is
[
18
, Lemma 4.2]
, and the second then follows easily.

(ii). Since
P/\Phi(P)\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}
, we may assume that
P
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space of dimension
d
. Then the number of subspaces of
P
of co-dimension
b
is

\frac{(p^{d}-1)(p^{d}-p)\ldots(p^{d}-p^{b-1})}{(p^{b}-1)(p^{b}-p)\cdots(p^{b}-p^{b-1})}.

A routine exercise shows that this is at least
p^{b(d-b)}
.

(iii) This follows from
|\mathrm{GL}_{d}(2)|=2^{d(d-1)/2}\prod_{i=1}^{d}(2^{i}-1)\geq 2^{d(d-1)/2}\prod_{i=1}^{d}2^{i-1}
.
∎

The following lemma is elementary, but will be used throughout Sections
4
and
5
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group, and let
N
be a normal subgroup of
G
.

(i)

Suppose that
G
acts on a finite
p
-group
H
via automorphisms. The subset
Y
of
H
generates
H
as a G-group if and only if
\langle Y\rangle[G,H]=H
. In particular,
d_{G}(H)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}{(H/H^{p}[G,H])}
and

d_{G}(N)\leq\log_{p}(|N/[G,N]|)
.

(ii)

Suppose that
G
is a transitive subgroup of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
for some
n\neq p
, let
\Sigma
be a minimal block system for
G
, let
\pi
be the homomorphism from
G
to
{\mathrm{Sym}}(\Sigma)
, and let

.

d_{G}(N)\leq d_{S}(N\cap\ker(\pi))+d_{S}(N\pi)
.

2.3
Nilpotent subgroups and abelian sections of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}

For a nilpotent group
G
, we write
G_{p}
for the Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
and
G_{p^{\prime}}
for the Hall
p^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\Sigma
and
\Gamma
be partitions of a set
\Omega
. We say that
\Sigma
and
\Gamma
are
gridded
if each part of
\Sigma
meets each part of
\Gamma
in a unique point. We say that they are
p
-gridded
for some prime
p
if each part of
\Sigma
has size the highest power of
p
dividing
|\Omega|
(which may be
p^{0}=1
).

Notice that if
\Sigma
and
\Gamma
are gridded then all parts of
\Sigma
have size the number of parts of
\Gamma
, and vice versa.
For a partition
\Omega=\Delta_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup\Delta_{k}
, the corresponding
Young subgroup
of
{\mathrm{Sym}}(\Omega)
is the direct product
{\mathrm{Sym}}(\Delta_{1})\times\cdots\times{\mathrm{Sym}}(\Delta_{k})\leq{\mathrm{Sym}}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
G
be a transitive nilpotent subgroup of
{\mathrm{Sym}}(\Omega)
, and write
|\Omega|=n=p^{k}r
with
p
a prime and
p\nmid r
. We assume that
r>1
.
Let
\Gamma_{p}
be the set of
G_{p}
-orbits and
\Gamma_{r}
be the set of
G_{p^{\prime}}
-orbits.
Then

(i)

\Gamma_{p}
and
\Gamma_{r}
are
p
-gridded partitions of
\Omega
, and each part in
\Gamma_{p}
has size
p^{k}
.

(ii)

Let
Y_{p}
and
Y_{r}
be the Young subgroups of
{\mathrm{Sym}}(\Omega)
corresponding to
\Gamma_{p}
and
\Gamma_{r}
. Then
Y_{p}
has a unique diagonal subgroup
D_{p}=D_{p}(\Gamma_{p},\Gamma_{r})\cong{\mathrm{S}}_{p^{k}}
such that
[D_{p},G_{p^{\prime}}]=1
, and similarly
Y_{r}
has a unique diagonal subgroup
D_{r}=D_{r}(\Gamma_{p},\Gamma_{r})\cong{\mathrm{S}}_{r}
such that
[D_{r},G_{p}]=1
. In particular,
G\leq D_{p}\times D_{r}\cong{\mathrm{S}}_{p^{k}}\times{\mathrm{S}}_{r}
, and
D_{p}
and
D_{r}
depend only on
\Gamma_{p}
and
\Gamma_{r}
.

(iii)

There is a unique conjugacy class of maximal transitive nilpotent subgroups of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
. Writing
n=p_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots p_{s}^{k_{s}}
, these subgroups have order the product of the orders of the Sylow
p_{i}
-subgroups of
{\mathrm{S}}_{p_{i}^{k_{i}}}
, which is at most
2^{n-1}
.

Proof.

(i) and (ii). This is a straightforward exercise, since all orbits of a
p
-group have
p
-power order, and if a group
H_{1}\leq{\mathrm{Sym}}(\Omega)
centralises a group
H_{2}\leq{\mathrm{Sym}}(\Omega)
, then
H_{1}
permutes the orbits of
H_{2}
.

(iii). It follows from Part (ii) that the maximum order of a transitive nilpotent subgroup of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
is the product of the orders of the Sylow
p_{i}
-subgroups of
{\mathrm{S}}_{p_{i}^{k_{i}}}
, namely
p_{i}^{(p_{i}^{k_{i}}-1)/(p-1)}
. Now
p^{(m-1)/(p-1)}<2^{m-1}
for

, and
2^{m_{1}-1}\cdot 2^{m_{2}-1}<2^{m_{1}m_{2}-1}
so the maximum is attained when

is a power of
2
.
∎

We shall often write
G^{\prime}
for the derived group
[G,G]
, and

Theorem 2.8

3
Counting subdirect products of finite
p
-groups

Throughout this section, let
D=G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{t}
.
The bulk of our work will come down to counting subdirect products of such groups

.
In this section we introduce two related data structures called
coordinate tableaux
, which are a compact way of representing structural information about subdirect products. After that, we define
efficient
generating sets for subdirect products, and give a lower bound on the number of such sets. We then use these tools to prove the main result of this section, Theorem

.

Definition 3.1
.

A normal series
N_{1}\leq\cdots\leq N_{\ell}=H
of a group
H
is
proper
if
N_{i}<N_{i+1}
for all
i
. For us, the
length
of the series is
\ell
(
not

\ell-1
).
For all
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and all groups
H
, we let
\mathcal{N}(\ell,H)
be the set of proper normal series of
H
of length
\ell
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that
D
is a
p
-group, for some prime
p
.
Let
c\in(0,1]
be a constant, let
n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}
be real numbers such that
|G_{i}|\leq p^{cn_{i}}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
, and let
n=\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}
.
Let
k
be an upper bound on the length of a proper normal series in the groups
G_{i}
,
let
\lambda
be the maximum of
|\mathcal{N}(\ell,G_{i})|
over all

and

,
and let
d=\max\{d_{G_{i}}(N)/n_{i}\text{ : }N\unlhd G_{i},1\leq i\leq t\}
.
Then

|\mathrm{Subdir}(G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{t})|\leq\lambda^{t}t!^{k+2}p^{cdn^{2}/4}.

In particular, for all
C>1
, there exists a constant
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{1}(p,C)\geq 1
such that if all

are less than
C
and the

are quotients of

-subgroups of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n_{i}}
, then

|\mathrm{Subdir}(G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{t})|\leq p^{dn^{2}/(4(p-1))}2^{\kappa_{1}t\log t}.

We now introduce the first of our data structures.

Definition 3.3
.

A

(i)

is a normal subgroup of

Lemma 4.2

For each

Recall that

We now remind the reader of some elementary results about commutators. Commutators will be left-normed, so that

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

be arbitrary, and let

be a subgroup of

Then

(ii)

For all

\gamma_{i}(H)=\langle[x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}]\text{ : }x_{j}\in X\rangle^{H}

Proof.

(i). Here

[y,hg]=[y,g][y,h]^{g}=[y,g][y,h]

By Lemma

In this section we first establish tight bounds on the number of generators of groups with no short orbits, and then prove the surprising result that to count the subgroups of

satisfying a given

The next three results are based on ideas from
[

For brevity, we prove them under the assumption that the group is soluble: it is not difficult to prove

Lemma 6.1

There exists an absolute constant

such that for all

We induct on

is primitive then

for every

of

[

Hence the result follows in this case, and for all

For the inductive step, assume that

By

[

Therefore

Now,

Hence

\displaystyle d_{G}(N)

\displaystyle\leq d_{G}(N\cap K)+d_{G/K}(NK/K)\leq\frac{\kappa\eta s\log r}{\sqrt{\log s}}+\frac{\zeta_{0}s}{\sqrt{\log s}}=\frac{(\kappa\eta\log r+\zeta_{0})s}{\sqrt{\log s}}.

Hence the result follows.
∎

Here is our main result on generating groups with large orbits: the assumption of solubility can be removed.

Proposition 6.2
.

There exists an absolute constant
\zeta
such that the following holds. For all
n\geq 1
and all non-trivial soluble subgroups

of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
, if all non-trivial orbits of

have length at least

. In particular, for any

ensures that each soluble

G\leq{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
with all non-trivial orbit lengths at least

satisfies
d(G)\leq\lambda n
.

Proof.

We shall show that the result holds with
\zeta=\zeta_{0}
, the constant from Lemma
6.1
.

Let
G\leq{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
be a counterexample with
n
minimal, and let
m
be a lower bound on the length of the non-trivial orbits of

6.1
,
G
has more than one non-trivial orbit, so

is a subdirect product of
G_{1}\times G_{2}
for some soluble groups

and
G_{2}\leq{\mathrm{S}}_{n-n_{1}}
, such that
G_{1}
is transitive and

.

Let

, and identify
N
with the corresponding subgroup of

, so
by Lemma

\displaystyle d(G)

\displaystyle\leq d(G/N)+d_{G}(N)=d(G_{2})+d_{G_{1}}(N)\leq d(G_{2})+\zeta n_{1}/\sqrt{\log{n_{1}}}.

Now

G_{2}
has all nontrivial orbits of length at least
m

∎

Let

Here is the main result of this section.

Theorem 6.4
.

Fix a real number

(iii)

-group with all non-trivial orbits of length at least

.

Let

with

By Proposition

-subgroups of

with bounded orbit lengths, i.e. to find a suitable function

Moreover, Conditions (i)–(iii) hold for a number of natural properties

is the property of being a

For a partition

\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}(N_{1},\ldots,N_{t})|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathrm{Subdir}(Q_{1}\times\cdots\times Q_{r}\times Q_{r+1}\times\cdots\times Q_{t}))|\quad\quad\mbox{ by Lemma~\ref{lem:tableaux}}

\displaystyle\leq|\mathrm{Sub}(A)|\cdot|\mathrm{Subdir}(Q_{r+1}\times\cdots\times Q_{t})|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{d_{1}^{2}/16+d_{1}\log{d_{1}}+(n-d_{1})^{2}/16+\kappa_{1}(t-r)\log{(t-r)}+d_{1}(n-d_{1})/8}\leq 2^{n^{2}/16+(1+\kappa_{1})n\log{n}}.

.

-projecting, and

. Let

, and let

We start with three technical lemmas. Recall from the introduction
the definition of the socle length
\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}{G}
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
G
be a finite soluble group, and let
N
be a normal subgroup of
G
. Then

(i)

\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(G/N)\leq\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(G)
; and

(ii)

\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(N)\leq\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(G)
.

Proof.

We will prove both parts together by induction on
|G|
. Clearly the result holds when
|G|\leq 3
, so assume inductively that both claims hold for groups of order less than
|G|
. We may also assume that
|N|>1
.
Then
\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G)N/N
is contained in
E/N:=\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G/N)
, so writing bars to denote reduction modulo
\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G)N
and applying induction gives
\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(\overline{G}/\overline{E})\leq\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(\overline{G})
. Since
\overline{G}/\overline{E}\cong G/E\cong(G/N)/(E/N)=(G/N)/\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G/N)
, it follows that
\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}((G/N)/\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G/N))\leq\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(G/\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G)N)
.
Now

\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(G/N)=\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}((G/N)/\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G/N))+1\leq\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(G/\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G)N)+1\leq\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(G/\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G))+1=\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(G),

where the last inequality follows from the inductive hypothesis for (i) applied to
G/\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G)
. This proves (i).

For (ii), we may assume that
N<G
. Let
J
be a maximal normal subgroup of
G
containing
N
. Then
\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(N)\leq\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(J)
by the inductive hypothesis for (ii) applied to
J
. Thus, we may assume that
N
is a maximal normal subgroup of
G
. Suppose first that
N\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G)=G
. Then
\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G)
is not contained in
N
, so there exists a minimal normal subgroup
M
of
G
which is not contained in
N
. Then
N\cap M=1
, and the maximality of

implies that

G=N\times M
.
Hence
\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G)=\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(N)\times M
, so
G/\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G)\cong N/\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(N)
. Thus

\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(N)=\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(N/\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(N))+1=\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(G/\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G))+1=\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(G),

and the result follows in this case.

Hence we may assume that
N\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G)
is proper in
G
, and hence by maximality
N\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G)=N
.
By Clifford theory and solubility,
N
acts completely reducibly on each minimal normal subgroup of

, and so
\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G)\leq\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(N\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G))=\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(N)
. By applying (i) to

we see that
\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(N/\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(N))\leq\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(N/\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G))
.
Thus inductively applying (ii) to

shows that

\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(N)=1+\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(N/\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(N))\leq 1+\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(N/\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G))\leq 1+\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(G/\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G))=\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(G),

as required.

. Let
N
be a normal subgroup
N
of
G
such that

and

. Then

, so

. Since

is
1
or
M
by minimality of

, we see that

is trivial or
MK_{i}/K_{i}
. Thus

is a minimal normal subgroup of

, and so

. If instead

then trivially

. Thus each minimal normal subgroup of

is contained in

so
\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G)\leq S
.

Conversely, let

be the distinct prime divisors of
|S_{1}\times\cdots\times S_{t}|
. By solubility, each
S_{i}
is abelian, so

, where
S(p_{i})
is the intersection of

with the
p_{i}
-part of
S_{1}\times\cdots\times S_{t}
.
Let
p=p_{j}
for some

, then the
p
-part of
S_{1}\times\cdots\times S_{t}
is the direct product
(S_{1})_{p}\times\cdots\times(S_{t})_{p}
, where
(S_{i})_{p}
is the
p
-part of
S_{i}
. If
(S_{i})_{p}\neq 1
then
(S_{i})_{p}
is a direct product of minimal normal subgroups of
G_{i}
. It follows that
(S_{1})_{p}\times\cdots\times(S_{t})_{p}
is a completely reducible
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
-module, and hence

is also completely reducible as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
-module, and so is
a product of minimal normal subgroups of
G
. Hence,
S(p)\leq\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G)
for all

. We conclude that

S=\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G)
.

If
G_{i}=S_{i}
for all

, then
\mathop{\mathrm{soc}}(G)=S=G
and the result follows. Otherwise, the natural homomorphism from

to
(G_{1}/S_{1})\times\cdots\times(G_{t}/S_{t})

embeds

as
a subdirect product of
(G_{1}/S_{1})\times\cdots\times(G_{t}/S_{t})
. By induction,
\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(G/S)\leq\max\{\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(G_{i}/S_{i})\text{ : }1\leq i\leq t\}
.
Hence,
\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(G)=\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(G/S)+1\leq\max\{\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(G_{i}/S_{i})\text{ : }1\leq i\leq t\}+1=\max\{\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}(G_{i})\text{ : }1\leq i\leq t\}
. This completes the proof.
∎

The proof of Theorem

Proof.

Fix a partition

(iii) to each orbit, we see that

Lemma 9.5
.

Let

,

and

be finite

-groups, with

elementary abelian, and let
d
be a positive integer. Let
\mathcal{S}(P,X,E,d)
be the set of subdirect products of

with the property that the projection to
P\times X
requires at least
d
generators and the projection to
X\times E
is surjective. If

then

|\mathcal{S}(P,X,E,d)|\geq 2^{(d-d(X))d(E)/2}|\mathrm{Subdir}_{\geq d}(P\times X)|.

Proof.

Let
H
be a subdirect product of
P\times X
with
d(H)\geq d
. Choose a subgroup
L
of
H
that is minimal subject to
(H\cap P)L=H
. Writing bars to denote reduction modulo
\Phi(H)

A
of
H
such that the Frattini subgroup
\Phi(H)\leq A
and
\overline{A}
is a complement for
\overline{L}
in the elementary abelian group
\overline{H}
.

Next,
notice that
d(\overline{A})+d(\overline{L})=d(\overline{H})\geq d
, so
d(\overline{A})\geq d-d(\overline{L})
. The minimality of

implies that
H\cap P\cap L\leq\Phi(L)
, so that
P\cap L\leq\Phi(L)
, and hence
d(L)=d(L/P\cap L)=d(X)
. Thus,
d(\overline{L})\leq d(L)=d(X)
, so
d(\overline{A})\geq d-d(\overline{L})\geq d-d(X)

Identifying
\overline{A}
with
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{d(\overline{A})}
and

\mathbb{F}_{2}^{d(E)}

|\mathrm{Epi}(\overline{A},E)|
is at least the product of
|\mathrm{GL}_{d(E)}(2)|
and the number of subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{d(\overline{A})}
of dimension

. We deduce from Lemma

|\mathrm{GL}_{d(E)}(2)|\geq 2^{d(E)^{2}-d(E)}

|\mathrm{Epi}(\overline{A},E)|\geq 2^{d(E)^{2}-d(E)}2^{d(E)((d-d(X))-d(E))}=2^{(d-d(X)-1)d(E)}\geq 2^{(d-d(X))d(E)/2}.

Let
\alpha:\overline{A}\rightarrow E
be one such epimomorphism. We define a homomorphism
\theta_{\alpha}:H\rightarrow E

for

, which is well-defined since

. Notice that

, so
\ker(\theta_{\alpha})
projects onto

, and that
\theta_{\alpha}=\theta_{\beta}
if and only if
\alpha=\beta
. Since
\theta_{\alpha}
is surjective, this defines an injection

,
\alpha\rightarrow\theta_{\alpha}
.

For each

,

G_{\theta}:=\{(p,x,(p,x)\theta)\text{ : }(p,x)\in H\}\leq P\times X\times E
.
It is clear that

if and only if
\theta=\tau
. By definition

projects onto
H
, and since
\ker(\theta)
projects onto
X
the group
G_{\theta}
projects onto

. Hence

so the number of subgroups in
\mathcal{S}(P,X,E,d)
that project onto
H
is at least the number of choices of
\theta
, which is at least
|\mathrm{Im}(f)|=|\mathrm{Epi}(\overline{A},E)|\geq 2^{(d-d(X))d(E)/2}
.

The result now follows from the fact that there are
|\mathrm{Subdir}_{\geq d}(P\times X)|
choices for
H
, and distinct choices of
H
yield distinct subgroups.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
5
.

Proof of Theorem
5
.

Each nilpotent subgroup of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
is either a
2
-group or a
\mathcal{P}
-group. We shall show that for sufficiently large
n
there are exponentially more
2
-subgroups of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
than
\mathcal{P}
-groups, so that the probability that a random nilpotent subgroup of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
is a
2
-group tends to
1
.

Let
\tau>0
be as in Lemma
9.4
.
First we count the
\mathcal{P}
-groups
G
such that
a:=a(G)>n/10^{6}
or
b:=b(G)>9\tau\log{n}
.
Suppose first that
a>n/10^{6}
. Then
|\Xi|\geq 3a
by Lemma
9.2
(ii),
so
e+a\leq(n-3a)+a<n-2n/10^{6}
, which is less than
n-9\tau\log n
for
n
sufficiently large. Similarly, if
b>9\tau\log{n}
, then
e+a\leq n-b<n-9\tau\log{n}
. Thus we may assume in both cases that
e+a<n-9\tau\log{n}
.

Applying Lemma
9.4
(ii) to the set
\Gamma
of all such
(e,a,b)
with
e+a\leq n-9\tau\log{n}
yields a maximum at

e+a=n-9\tau\log{n}
, so for
n
sufficiently large the number of
\mathcal{P}
-groups
G
for which
a>n/(10^{6})
or
b>9\tau\log n
is at most

2^{(n-9\tau\log{n})^{2}/16+(9\tau\log{n})^{2}/36+\tau n\log{n}}\leq 2^{n^{2}/16-(\tau/8)n\log{n}+(117\tau^{2}/16)(\log{n})^{2}},

for
n
sufficiently large this is at most
2^{n^{2}/16-(\tau/9)n\log{n}}
. By Theorem
2
the number of
2
-subgroups of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
is greater than
2^{n^{2}/16}
, so a random nilpotent subgroup of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
is either a
2
-group or a
\mathcal{P}
-group
G
such that
a(G)\leq n/10^{6}
and
b(G)\leq 9\tau\log{n}
.

Next, by Lemma
8.8
, a random nilpotent subgroup of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
has a Sylow
2
-subgroup requiring more than
n/17
generators. Hence such a subgroup is either a
2
-group, or lies in the set

\mathcal{S}=\{G\leq{\mathrm{S}}_{n}\text{ : }G\mbox{ a $\mathcal{P}$-group,}\ d_{2}(G)\geq n/17,\ a(G)\leq n/(10^{6})\text{ and }b(G)\leq 9\tau\log{n}\}

The result will follow if we can show that the ratio
|\mathcal{S}|/|\mathrm{Sub}_{2}({\mathrm{S}}_{n})|
tends to zero as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

For
\Xi\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and integers
a\in\{0,\ldots,\lfloor n/(10^{6})\rfloor\}

and
b\in\{3,\ldots,\lfloor 9\tau\log n\rfloor\}
,
let

\mathcal{S}(\Xi,a,b)=\{G\in\mathcal{S}\text{ : }\Xi(G)=\Xi,\ a(G)=a\text{ and }b(G)=b\},

so that
\mathcal{S}
is the disjoint union of the sets
\mathcal{S}(\Xi,a,b)
.
The main work of the proof will be to bound
|\mathcal{S}(\Xi,a,b)|
.

If
G\in\mathcal{S}(\Xi,a,b)
, then the restriction
Q
of
G
to
\Xi
lies in
\mathrm{Sub}_{\text{mix},a,b}(\Xi)
.
By construction, the Sylow
2
-subgroup
Q_{2}
of
each
Q\in\mathrm{Sub}_{\text{mix},a,b}(\Xi)
embeds in
{\mathrm{S}}_{a}
. Thus, every subgroup of
Q_{2}
requires at most
a/2
generators by Theorem
2.8
, and in particular

d(Q_{2})\leq a/2
.
It follows that each
2
-subgroup
P
of
{\mathrm{Sym}}(\Theta)
such that
P\times Q_{2}
has a subdirect product requiring at least
n/17
generators satisfies
d(P)\geq m:=n/17-a/2
.
Each
G\in\mathcal{S}(\Xi,a,b)
satisfies
d_{2}(G)\geq n/17
, so we may bound

\begin{array}[]{rl}|\mathcal{S}(\Xi,a,b)|&\leq\sum_{P\in\mathrm{Sub}_{2,d\geq m}({\mathrm{Sym}}(\Theta)),Q\in\mathrm{Sub}_{\text{mix},a,b}(\Xi)}|\mathrm{Subdir}_{d_{2}\geq n/17}(P\times Q)|\\
&=\sum_{P\in\mathrm{Sub}_{2,d\geq m}({\mathrm{Sym}}(\Theta)),Q\in\mathrm{Sub}_{\text{mix},a,b}(\Xi)}|\mathrm{Subdir}_{d_{2}\geq n/17}(P\times Q_{2})|\end{array}

where
\mathrm{Subdir}_{d_{2}\geq n/17}(P\times Q)
is the set of subdirect products of
P\times Q
in which the Sylow
2
-subgroup requires at least
n/17
generators.

To bound the size of these sets of subdirect products, first let
T(\Xi,a,b)
be the set of
2
-subgroups
X
of
{\mathrm{Sym}}(\Xi)
that occur as Sylow
2
-subgroups of groups
Q\in\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathrm{mix},a,b}(\Xi)
, and for each
X\in T(\Xi,a,b)
let
\mathcal{Q}(X,a,b)
be the (non-empty) set of groups
Q\in\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathrm{mix},a,b}(\Xi)
with
Q_{2}=X
. Since each such
Q
is a nilpotent group with no orbits of
2
-power length,

\mathcal{Q}(X,a,b)
consists of all groups
X\times L
such that
L

is a fixed-point-free
\mathcal{N}_{3}
-subgroup of
C:=C_{{\mathrm{Sym}}(\Xi)}(X)
.
For some indexing of
a_{1},\ldots,a_{t}
, the centraliser

C
factorises as
W_{1}\times\cdots\times W_{k}\times{\mathrm{S}}_{c}
, where each
W_{i}
is permutation isomorphic to
R_{i}\wr{\mathrm{S}}_{c_{i}}
for some semiregular
2
-group
R_{i}
of degree
a_{i}>1
, and some positive integers
c_{i}\geq 3
and
c\geq 0
with
c+\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}=b
and
c+\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}c_{i}=f=|\Xi|
. (If the Sylow
2
-subgroup of
G
acts equivalently on more than one
G
-orbit then the sum
\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}
will be less than
a
.) Let
U\leq C
be the direct product of the base groups of the
W_{i}
. Then
|U|=|R_{1}^{c_{1}}\times\cdots\times R_{k}^{c_{k}}|\leq\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}^{c_{i}}\leq 2^{\sum c_{i}a_{i}}\leq 2^{f}
. Fix a complement
B
of
U
in
C
so that

B\cong{\mathrm{S}}_{c_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\mathrm{S}}_{c_{k}}\times{\mathrm{S}}_{c}\leq{\mathrm{S}}_{b}
. Since
U
is a
2
-group, by the Schur-Zassenhaus theorem each
\mathcal{N}_{3}
-subgroup of
C=U\mathord{{\!\>{:}\>\!}}B
is
U
-conjugate
to a subgroup of
B
, and so
|\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{N}_{3}}(C)|\leq 2^{f}|\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{N}_{3}}(B)|\leq 2^{f}|\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{N}_{3}}({\mathrm{S}}_{b})|
.
Hence
Theorem
3
yields

\displaystyle|\mathcal{Q}(X,a,b)|\leq|\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{N}_{3}}(C)|\leq 2^{f}|\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{N}_{3}}({\mathrm{S}}_{b})|\leq 2^{b^{2}/36+\gamma(3)b\log{b}+f}.

Since
9\tau\log n\geq b
there exists an absolute constant
\lambda
such that

b^{2}/36+\gamma(3)b\log{b}\leq\lambda(\log{n})^{2}
.
Substituting, we deduce that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}(\Xi,a,b)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{P\in\mathrm{Sub}_{2,d\geq m}({\mathrm{Sym}}(\Theta)),Q\in\mathrm{Sub}_{\text{mix},a,b}(\Xi)}|\mathrm{Subdir}_{d_{2}\geq n/17}(P\times Q_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{P\in\mathrm{Sub}_{2,d\geq m}({\mathrm{Sym}}(\Theta)),X\in T(\Xi,a,b)}|\mathcal{Q}(X,a,b)||\mathrm{Subdir}_{d_{2}\geq n/17}(P\times X)|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\lambda(\log{n})^{2}+f}\sum_{P,X}|\mathrm{Subdir}_{d_{2}\geq n/17}(P\times X)|.

For each
X\in T(\Xi,a,b)
, each
c_{i}
is at least
3
, so the corresponding
B\leq{\mathrm{Sym}}(\Xi)
has at least one elementary abelian
2
-subgroup of order exactly
2^{\lfloor b/3\rfloor}
: fix one and denote it
E_{X}
. Since
E_{X}\leq B\leq C_{{\mathrm{Sym}}(\Xi)}(X)
and
X\cap C_{{\mathrm{Sym}}(\Xi)}(X)\leq U
, the intersection
E_{X}\cap X=1
, so

XE_{X}\cong X\times E_{X}
is a
2
-subgroup of
{\mathrm{Sym}}(\Xi)
. Also,
d(X)\leq a/2\leq n/(2\cdot 10^{6})
so

d(E_{X})=\lfloor b/3\rfloor\leq 3\tau\log n<n/17-a/2\leq n/17-d(X)
for large enough
n
.
Since the group
X
uniquely determines
E_{X}
, for the rest of this proof we shall write
\mathcal{S}(P,X)
instead of
\mathcal{S}(P,X,E_{X},n/17)
for the set of subdirect products of
P\times X\times E_{X}
whose projection to
P\times X
requires at least
n/17
generators and whose projection to
X\times E_{X}
is surjective.
With this notation, for sufficiently large
n
Lemma
9.5
bounds

\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}(\Xi,a,b)|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\lambda(\log{n})^{2}+f}\sum_{P,X}2^{-((n/17)-d(X))d(E_{X})/2}|\mathcal{S}(P,X)|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\lambda(\log{n})^{2}+f+(a/4-n/34)\lfloor b/3\rfloor}\sum_{P,X}|\mathcal{S}(P,X)|.

We remark that
a/4-n/34<-0.029n
.

For distinct
P
and
X
, the sets
\mathcal{S}(P,X)
may not be disjoint. We define an equivalence relation on the sets
\mathcal{S}(P,X)
by
\mathcal{S}(P,X)\sim\mathcal{S}(R,Y)
if and only if
P=R
and
X\times E_{X}=Y\times E_{Y}
.
We now bound the size of the equivalence class
[\mathcal{S}(P,X)]
,
so let
\mathcal{S}(R,Y)\in[\mathcal{S}(P,X)]
. Then

E_{Y}\leq Z(X\times E_{X})=Z(X)\times E_{X}
. Since
X
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
{\mathrm{S}}_{a}
, Theorem
2.8
bounds
|Z(X)|\leq 2^{a/2}
. By assumption
|E_{X}|=|E_{Y}|=2^{\lfloor b/3\rfloor}
, so
|Z(X)\times E_{X}|\leq 2^{a/2+\lfloor b/3\rfloor}
. Hence by Lemma
2.4
(i) there are at most
4\cdot 2^{\lfloor b/3\rfloor(a/2)}
groups
E_{Y}
.
Now let

f(E_{Y})=\{\mathcal{S}(P,Y^{\prime})\in[\mathcal{S}(P,X)]\text{ : }E_{Y^{\prime}}=E_{Y}\}\subseteq[\mathcal{S}(P,X)].

From
d(E_{Y})=d(E_{X})
and the fact that each group in
\mathcal{S}(P,X)
projects to
X\times E_{X}\leq{\mathrm{Sym}}(\Xi)
, we deduce that
d(Y)=d(X)=d(Y^{\prime})\leq a/2
for all
\mathcal{S}(P,Y^{\prime})\in f(E_{Y})
.
Each complement
Y^{\prime}
to
E_{Y}
in
Y\times E_{Y}
has order
|Y|
and projects to
Y
, so has the form
\{(y,y\theta)\text{ : }y\in Y\}
for some homomorphism
\theta:Y\rightarrow E_{Y}
. Hence
|f(E_{Y})|\leq|\mathrm{Hom}(Y,E_{Y})|\leq|E_{Y}|^{d(Y)}\leq 2^{\lfloor b/3\rfloor a/2}
. We have therefore shown that
|[\mathcal{S}(P,X)]|\leq 4\cdot 2^{\lfloor b/3\rfloor a/2}2^{\lfloor b/3\rfloor a/2}=2^{a\lfloor b/3\rfloor+2}
.

An easy exercise shows that for a fixed
\Xi
the intersection
\mathcal{S}(P,X)\cap\mathcal{S}(R,Y)
is non-empty if and only if
\mathcal{S}(P,X)\sim\mathcal{S}(R,Y)
.
To bound
\sum_{P,X}|\mathcal{S}(P,X)|
,
let
\mathcal{C}_{1}:=\mathcal{S}(P_{1},X_{1})
,
\ldots
,
\mathcal{C}_{l}:=\mathcal{S}(P_{\ell},X_{\ell})
be a complete set of
\sim
-class representatives, chosen such that each is of maximal size in its
\sim
-class. Then the sets
\mathcal{C}_{i}
are pairwise disjoint, so

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in\mathrm{Sub}_{2,d\geq m}({\mathrm{Sym}}(\Theta)),X\in T(\Xi,a,b)}|\mathcal{S}(P,X)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|[\mathcal{C}_{i}]||\mathcal{C}_{i}|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}}|[\mathcal{C}_{i}]|\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|\mathcal{C}_{i}|\leq\max_{i}|[\mathcal{C}_{i}]||\bigcup_{P,X}\mathcal{S}(P,X)|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{a\lfloor b/3\rfloor+2}|\bigcup_{P,X}\mathcal{S}(P,X)|\leq 2^{a\lfloor b/3\rfloor+2}|\mathrm{Sub}_{2}({\mathrm{S}}_{n})|.

Since
a\leq n/10^{6}
we can bound
5a/4-n/34\leq-0.029n
. Hence

\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}(\Xi,a,b)|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\lambda(\log{n})^{2}+f+(5a/4-n/34)\lfloor b/3\rfloor+2}|{\mathrm{Sub}}_{2}({\mathrm{S}}_{n})|\leq 2^{\lambda(\log{n})^{2}+f-0.029n\lfloor b/3\rfloor+2}|{\mathrm{Sub}}_{2}({\mathrm{S}}_{n})|.

Let
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
denote the subset of
\mathcal{S}
for which
f>n/1000
.
Now
(a+1)b\geq f
by Lemma
9.2
(ii), so
a\leq n/10^{6}
implies that
b>500
for
n
sufficiently large, and hence

-0.029n\cdot\lfloor b/3\rfloor+2\leq-0.029n\cdot 166+2\leq-4.8n
. Furthermore,
f\leq n
so

\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\sum_{f>n/1000},|\Xi|=f,}a+b\leq f|\mathcal{S}(\Xi,a,b)|\leq\sum_{f>n/1000,|\Xi|=f,a+b\leq f}2^{-3.8n+\lambda(\log{n})^{2}}|\mathrm{Sub}_{2}({\mathrm{S}}_{n})|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}n^{3}2^{-3.8n+\lambda(\log{n})^{2}}|\mathrm{Sub}_{2}({\mathrm{S}}_{n})|.

Now we count the subset of
\mathcal{S}
for which
f\leq n/1000
. It is a standard fact (see for example
[
7
, Lemma 16.19, page 427]
), that the total number of subsets
\Xi
of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
of size at most
n/1000
is at most

\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor n/1000\rfloor}\binom{n}{i}\leq 2^{n(\frac{1}{1000}\log{1000}-\frac{999}{1000}\log{\frac{999}{1000}})}<2^{0.012n}.

From
b\geq 3
we deduce that

-0.029n\cdot\lfloor b/3\rfloor+2\leq-0.028n
for
n
sufficiently large.
Hence

\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{S}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{f\leq n/1000,|\Xi|=f,a+b\leq f}|\mathcal{S}(\Xi,a,b)|\leq\sum_{f\leq n/1000,|\Xi|=f,a+b\leq f}2^{-0.028n+0.001n}2^{\lambda(\log{n})^{2}}|\mathrm{Sub}_{2}({\mathrm{S}}_{n})|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{0.012n}\cdot n^{3}\cdot 2^{-0.027n+\lambda(\log{n})^{2}}|\mathrm{Sub}_{2}({\mathrm{S}}_{n})|.

Thus,
|\mathcal{S}|/|\mathrm{Sub}_{2}({\mathrm{S}}_{n})|
tends to
0
as
n
tends to
\infty
, and the result follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Let
n
be congruent to
3\mod 4
. We shall show that there is a map
f
from the set of
2
-subgroups of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
to the set of non-nilpotent groups of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
, such that
f
maps at most four
2
-subgroups to the same non-nilpotent subgroup. Hence, the number of non-nilpotent subgroups is at least
1/4
of the number of
2
-groups. The result will then follow from Theorem
5
.

Let
G
be a
2
-subgroup of
{\mathrm{S}}_{n}
. Since
n\equiv 3\mod 4
, the group
G
either has at least
3
fixed points, or a single fixed point and at least one orbit of length two. In the first case, let
i,j,k
be the smallest fixed points (with respect to the natural ordering on
[n]
), and in the latter let
i
be the fixed point and
j,k
be the two points in the first orbit of length
2
. We then define
f(G)=\langle G,(i,j,k),(i,j)\rangle
in the first case, and
f(G)=\langle G,(i,j,k)\rangle
in the second case. Then
H:=f(G)
induces
{\mathrm{S}}_{3}
on
\Delta:=\{i,j,k\}
; the pointwise stabiliser
H_{(\Delta)}
is equal to
G_{(\Delta)}
; and the projection of
H
to
\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\Delta
is the same the projection of
G
to this union of orbits.

To see that each group in
\mathrm{Im}(f)
corresponds to at most four groups
G
, let
H
be a group in
\mathrm{Im}(f)
. Then
H
has a unique orbit
\Delta=\{i,j,k\}
on which
H
acts as
{\mathrm{S}}_{3}
. Let
\Gamma=\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\Delta
. If
H
is a direct product of
{\mathrm{Sym}}(\Delta)
with
H|_{\Gamma}
, then
H=f(G)
where
G
is a direct product of
G|_{\Delta}
and
G|_{\Gamma}
. The group
G|_{\Gamma}
is either trivial or
{\mathrm{S}}_{2}
, and there are at most three choices for the group
{\mathrm{S}}_{2}
, so at most four such
G
. Otherwise,
H_{(\Delta)}|_{\Gamma}=G_{(\Delta)}|_{\Gamma}
is an index
2
subgroup of
G|_{\Gamma}
, and
H|_{\Gamma}=G|_{\Gamma}
is also completely determined. There exactly three choices for
G|_{\Delta}
, and so exactly three possible groups
G
.
∎

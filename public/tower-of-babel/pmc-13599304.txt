Population-resourced species-specific InDel markers for identifying putative interspecific hybrids between Populus euphratica and Populus pruinosa
Authors: Ya Li, Shanhe Zhang, Wenli Ma, Xinyue Long, Yafen Chen, Zhongshuai Gai
Published: 2026

Background

Populus euphratica and Populus pruinosa, two endemic tree species belonging to section Turanga of genus Populus, are the dominant constructive species of desert riparian forests in arid northwest China, and undertake irreplaceable ecological functions for stabilizing fragile desert ecosystems. Populus euphratica exhibits strong tolerance to drought, salinity and wind erosion, but its growth rate is slow and trunks are often curved [1]. In contrast, P. pruinosa grows rapidly with straight stems yet possesses weaker abiotic stress resistance, which restricts its natural distribution range [2]. Natural interspecific gene flow occurs between the two species in overlapping distribution zones, and morphologically intermediate individuals integrate complementary growth and stress-resistance traits of both parents, showing potential heterosis for stress-tolerant poplar breeding [3–5].

Accurate identification of authentic hybrid seedlings at the early growth stage is an essential prerequisite for poplar crossbreeding and germplasm purification. However, morphologically intermediate individuals collected from natural contact zones exhibit highly overlapping phenotypic traits with both parental species under field conditions, making morphological discrimination extremely difficult [4]. Traditional identification approaches relying on phenotypic observation and isozyme profiling are heavily affected by environmental conditions, with low detection efficiency and accuracy, which cannot meet the demand of large-scale early hybrid screening [6]. Conventional DNA molecular markers commonly used for tree germplasm identification have inherent technical defects. Dominant markers including RAPD [7] and AFLP [8] suffer poor amplification repeatability and fail to distinguish homozygous and heterozygous genotypes. Co-dominant SSR markers produce complex banding patterns, and few loci exhibit strict species-specific alleles between closely related poplar species, limiting their application in hybrid authentication [9–11]. Insertion/deletion (InDel) markers, characterized by co-dominant inheritance, stable PCR amplification and intuitive fragment length polymorphisms, have become ideal low-cost molecular tools for identifying hybrids of closely related woody plants [12].

At present, a large number of InDel marker systems have been developed for Aigeiros and Tacamahaca poplar sections based on genomic sequencing data [13]. Nevertheless, publicly available, standardized InDel genomic resources specially designed for section Turanga desert poplars are still scarce. Existing poplar pan-genome studies confirmed that Turanga species form an independent ancient evolutionary clade with distinct genetic backgrounds compared with other poplar groups [5]. The lack of population-derived species-diagnostic InDel loci restricts standardized molecular screening of morphologically intermediate putative hybrid candidates in field and laboratory germplasm surveys.

In this study, we utilized large-scale whole-genome resequencing data from 439 P. euphratica and 146 P. pruinosa individuals to systematically screen InDel loci with high intraspecies conservation and significant interspecific fragment differences. After primer design, genome-wide sequence alignment and native polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (native-PAGE) experimental validation, a set of stable, transferable InDel primer pairs was obtained and verified using natural parental populations and artificial hybrid progenies. The primary objective of this work was to establish an open, standardized set of species-specific InDel markers for Populus sect. Turanga, and to construct a repeatable molecular workflow to support the screening of morphologically intermediate putative hybrid seedling candidates, so as to provide shared genomic data and technical support for desert poplar germplasm management and marker-assisted selection (MAS).

Materials and methods

Experimental materials

A total of 184 plant samples were collected for this study, consisting of 50 individuals of P. euphratica, 41 individuals of P. pruinosa, a total of 93 individuals with leaf morphologies intermediate between P. euphratica and P. pruinosa were collected from natural contact zones across different geographical regions. These individuals were regarded as putative natural-hybrid candidates based on morphological criteria described previously [1]. Their intermediate leaf morphological phenotypes were systematically characterized and verified in our previously published morphological survey of Turanga poplars, which serves as independent pre-molecular screening evidence for candidate hybrid individuals. Sampling sites covered multiple public natural desert riparian forest reserves across Xinjiang, China and adjacent Central distribution zones. Official sampling permits were obtained from local Xinjiang forestry and grassland administrative authorities, and all sampling complied with China’s wild plant protection regulations without sampling on private land (Supplementary Table S1). Fresh juvenile leaf tissues were harvested from each individual, immediately desiccated with silica gel in sealed centrifuge tubes, and transported back to the laboratory for subsequent genomic DNA extraction. Voucher leaf specimens corresponding to all sampled individuals were deposited in the Herbarium of Tarim University (TARU, Alar, Xinjiang, China). Voucher serial numbers: Eu-001 ~ Eu-439 (P. euphratica), Pr-001 ~ Pr-146 (P. pruinosa). All voucher specimens were morphologically identified by Dr Zhongshuai Gai and preserved for long-term public access.

Genomic DNA extraction

Genomic DNA was isolated from silica-dried leaf tissues using a modified cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) protocol described by Doyle [14], with critical optimizations adapted for woody desert poplar tissues high in polyphenol and secondary metabolite contents. The optimized lysis buffer contained 2% (w/v) CTAB, 100 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.0), 20 mM EDTA, 1.4 M NaCl, 1.5% polyvinylpyrrolidone-40 (PVP-40) and 0.2% β-mercaptoethanol. PVP-40 and β-mercaptoethanol were included in the lysis buffer to counteract the adverse effects of abundant polyphenols in desert poplar leaves: PVP-40 binds polyphenolic compounds, while β-mercaptoethanol acts as an antioxidant to inhibit polyphenol oxidation during tissue lysis. Leaf samples were incubated in lysis buffer at 65 ℃ for 40 min, followed by two rounds of extraction with chloroform–isoamyl alcohol (24:1, v/v) to remove protein and phenolic impurities. After precipitation with isopropanol and washing with 70% ethanol, air-dried DNA pellets were dissolved in sterile double-distilled water. DNA integrity was preliminarily detected via 1% agarose gel electrophoresis; DNA concentration and A260/A280 purity ratio were quantified using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific, USA). Only DNA samples with A260/A280 values ranging from 1.7 to 2.0 and intact electrophoretic bands were retained, then diluted to a uniform concentration of 10 ng/µL and stored at − 20 ℃ for long-term preservation before PCR amplification.

Screening of species-specific InDel loci

The chromosome-level reference genome assembly of P. euphratica was used as the reference sequence for variant calling [15]. Clean paired-end resequencing reads generated from 439 P. euphratica and 146 P. pruinosa individuals were mapped to the reference genome using the Burrows-Wheeler Aligner (BWA) software [16]. SAMtools v1.15 was applied to convert SAM alignment files to BAM format and remove PCR duplicate reads [17]. Genome-wide single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) and insertion/deletion (InDel) variants were simultaneously detected using GATK [18]. Custom Python scripts were developed to filter qualified species-diagnostic InDel loci with unified screening thresholds as follows: (1) intraspecies conserved allele frequency ≥ 95% within each species; (2) interspecific fragment length difference exceeding 5 bp; (3) InDel loci located in non-repetitive genomic regions. After filtering, 300 bp upstream and downstream flanking sequences of each candidate InDel were extracted for subsequent primer design [19].

Primer design and genome-wide universality evaluation

Primer pairs targeting filtered InDel loci were designed using Primer3 v2.3.7, with unified design criteria: GC content 20% − 46%, optimal annealing temperature 45–55 ℃, predicted target amplification fragment length 85–120 bp [20]. All designed primer sequences were aligned against both P. euphratica and P. pruinosa reference genomes via SeqHunter2 software [21]. Primers with genome matching rate ≥ 95% and consistent single amplification targets in both species were defined as universal candidate primers and synthesized by a commercial DNA synthesis company (Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, China). All 11 finalized primer sequences, corresponding chromosomal positions and predicted fragment sizes are listed in Table 1 of the main manuscript (Supplementary Table S2).

Primer validation and polymorphic screening

PCR amplification was performed in a 15 µL reaction system, containing 7.5 µL 2×PCR Mix, 1.5 µL DNA template (10 ng/µL), 1.0 µL forward primer (2 µmol/L), 1.0 µL reverse primer (2 µmol/L), and ddH2O to make up the final volume. The PCR program was set as follows: initial denaturation at 95 ℃ for 3 min; 35 cycles of 95 ℃ for 30 s, 55 ℃ for 30 s and 72 ℃ for 1 min; final extension at 72 ℃ for 7 min, followed by preservation at 12 ℃.

PCR products were separated by 8% native polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (native-PAGE) at a constant voltage of 180 V for 90 min. The gel was stained with 0.1% silver nitrate solution for 15 min and developed with a mixed solution of 2% NaOH and 1% formaldehyde until clear bands appeared.

Stepwise primer screening and population specificity validation

Two-stage of primer screening were performed sequentially. First-stage preliminary screening: genomic DNA from three randomly selected individuals of P. euphratica and three P. pruinosa individuals were amplified with all 11 candidate primer pairs, and primers with stable single-band amplification and obvious interspecific fragment length variation were retained for large-scale verification. Second-stage population validation: all 50 P. euphratica and 41 P. pruinosa natural individuals were amplified using pre-screened candidate primers to confirm complete intraspecies conservation and fixed interspecific fragment differences. Only two primer pairs (P1599-3 and P1599-4) fully met the standard of species-specific diagnostic markers with zero intraspecific length polymorphism across all tested natural populations [22].

Screening of morphologically intermediate putative hybrid individuals

The two validated diagnostic InDel primer pairs (P1599-3, P1599-4) were applied to amplify genomic DNA extracted from all 93 putative-hybrid individuals. Banding patterns on native-PAGE gels were recorded to judge hybrid authenticity: Individuals simultaneously presenting both species-specific amplification bands matched the heterozygous genotype theoretically expected for interspecific hybrids, whereas samples with only one parental amplification band were assigned to pure parental genotypes.

Results

Genome-wide mining of candidate InDel loci and primer screening

From whole-genome comparative analysis of 439 P. euphratica and 146 P. pruinosa resequencing accessions, a total of 56,864 InDel loci with interspecific fragment length differences greater than 5 bp were preliminarily identified. Among these variants, 3,417 loci satisfied strict screening criteria (intraspecies conserved allele frequency ≥ 95%, fixed fragment divergence between two species), which were selected as high-confidence candidate sites for marker development. Primer 3 was used to design amplification primers for all high-differentiation loci, and 18 primer sets with matched fragment length (85–120 bp) and annealing temperature parameters were initially generated. Subsequent whole-genome alignment screening via SeqHunter2 removed primers with non-specific genome binding sites, and 11 universal InDel primer pairs with stable single-locus amplification were retained for follow-up laboratory validation (Table 1). Detailed chromosomal positions, primer sequences and predicted amplification sizes for all 11 primer combinations are listed in Table 1.

Primary laboratory screening of polymorphic InDel markers

We randomly selected three individual plants of P. euphratica and three individuals of P. pruinosa for preliminary amplification screening of the 11 candidate primer pairs. Electrophoretic band profiles demonstrated that primer P1599-3 and P1599-4 produced clear, single bands in both species, with consistent and significant interspecific fragment length differences, while the remaining nine primer pairs exhibited ambiguous bands or small fragment gaps that could not serve as species-diagnostic markers. These two primer combinations were therefore selected for large-scale population specificity verification. Representative electrophoresis images of preliminary screening are shown in Fig. 1.

Preliminary native-PAGE screening results of 11 candidate InDel primers using three P. euphratica and three P. pruinosa individuals

Species-specific validation in natural parental populations

We performed large-scale native-PAGE genotyping using 50 independent P. euphratica individuals and 41 independent P. pruinosa individuals to test the specificity of P1599-3 and P1599-4. Consistent genotyping results were observed across all tested accessions with zero intraspecific fragment length polymorphism and no non-specific amplification products detected. For primer P1599-3, a stable 90 bp specific fragment was amplified in every P. euphratica sample, whereas a fixed 79 bp band was detected in all P. pruinosa individuals. For primer P1599-4, a 100 bp fragment was specific to P. euphratica, and a 92 bp fragment was exclusive to P. pruinosa. Representative gel electrophoresis results are displayed in Fig. 2. The two primer pairs displayed complete intraspecies conservation and fixed interspecific fragment polymorphism, meeting the standard of co-dominant species-specific molecular markers for desert poplar discrimination.

Identification efficiency of natural interspecific hybrid progenies

Twelve P. euphratica, twelve P. pruinosa, and twelve morphologically intermediate putative hybrid individuals (36 samples in total) were randomly sampled across Xinjiang for preliminary amplification tests of the two diagnostic primer pairs. The results showed that primer P1599-3 only amplified a 90 bp specific band in P. euphratica, only amplified a 79 bp band in P. pruinosa, and simultaneously amplified both the 90 bp and 79 bp characteristic bands in all 12 putative-hybrid individuals (Fig. 3A). Similarly, primer P1599-4 only amplified a 100 bp band in P. euphratica, only amplified a 92 bp band in P. pruinosa, and simultaneously amplified both the 100 bp and 92 bp characteristic bands in all 12 putative-hybrid individuals (Fig. 3B). Further testing of the remaining 81 hybrid progenies showed that all morphologically selected candidates generated dual species-specific diagnostic bands matching heterozygous genotype profiles. Within the 93 putative-hybrid individuals samples validated in the present study, both primer pairs P1599-3 and P1599-4 successfully discriminated the interspecific hybrids of P. euphratica and P. pruinosa (Supplementary Table S3). The combined use of the two primer pairs allows mutual verification, significantly improving the reliability and stability of identification results for our tested materials.

Discussion

Rapid and accurate identification of Populus sect. Turanga interspecific hybrids has long been a technical bottleneck restricting desert poplar germplasm conservation and crossbreeding practice [23]. Morphological ambiguity of hybrid seedlings and the defects of conventional detection systems greatly limit large-scale early screening and germplasm purification [5]. Isozyme markers suffer limited polymorphic information and poor repeatability, making them unsuitable for large-scale germplasm screening [24]. Dominant molecular markers including RAPD and AFLP cannot distinguish homozygous and heterozygous hybrid genotypes [25], and exhibit unstable amplification bands across different batches of PCR reactions [26]. Conventional SSR markers, the most widely used co-dominant markers in forestry research, have obvious defects when applied to P. euphratica and P. pruinosa: abundant shared alleles exist between the two closely related species, resulting in insufficient species-specific loci and ambiguous genotyping results [9, 26]. In contrast, insertion/deletion (InDel) markers rely on fixed fragment length differences between species and feature stable co-dominant amplification [12], simple PAGE detection procedures and low laboratory cost [27], which makes them superior for large-batch hybrid discrimination [20, 28].

Most previously reported poplar InDel markers were developed based on a small number of individual resequencing data or transcriptome sequences, lacking sufficient population-level verification, and many markers showed intra-species polymorphism when extended to natural wild populations [13]. In this study, we screened diagnostic InDel loci using genome variation data from 439 P. euphratica and 146 P. pruinosa wild individuals with strict filtering thresholds (intraspecies allele conservation ≥ 95%). No intraspecific fragment-length variation was detected for markers P1599-3 and P1599-4 among the 91 natural parental samples. All 93 morphologically-selected putative-hybrid individuals amplified dual diagnostic bands from the two InDel markers, consistent with a heterozygous genotype combining alleles inherited from P. euphratica and P. pruinosa. The dual-marker mutual verification system effectively eliminates false-positive genotyping errors, which represents a critical advantage unavailable for single-locus marker assays. The complete set of 11 candidate InDel primers we generated also supplies alternative genomic loci for follow-up population genetic analysis of Populus sect. Turanga, forming a complete open molecular resource panel rather than only two diagnostic markers for simple hybrid identification.

Current poplar molecular marker research mainly concentrates on species from sections Black poplar and green poplar species [24, 29]. A large number of SSR and InDel resources have been published for black poplar, white poplar and their hybrids, while standardized, publicly available species-specific marker systems tailored to the ancient independent clade Turanga remain extremely scarce [5]. Previous phylogenomic studies have confirmed that P. euphratica and P. pruinosa possess unique genetic backgrounds that are highly divergent from other poplar lineages, and the widespread natural hybridization between the two species requires dedicated identification tools for germplasm purification [1, 3, 5]. However, no research has mined population-scale species-diagnostic InDels for these two desert poplars before our work, resulting in a clear shortage of reusable genomic data supporting field germplasm screening.

This study fills this critical resource gap by establishing a standardized whole-genome InDel screening workflow applicable to low-divergence woody species. The analytical pipeline constructed here (large-population resequencing variation filtering → primer design → multi-stage PAGE population validation) provides a repeatable technical reference for developing diagnostic InDel markers in other intercrossable arbor species with weak interspecific genetic differentiation, such as other riparian woody plants with frequent natural hybridization.

Despite the reliable identification performance, this study still has certain limitations. First, the experimental samples were mainly collected from Xinjiang and Central Asia, and broader geographical sampling is required to further verify the universal applicability of these markers [30]. Second, the two core diagnostic InDel markers developed in this study are only mapped to chromosomes 04 and 06, lacking uniform and genome-wide chromosomal coverage. The uneven genomic distribution of the current marker panel may restrict its further large-scale genetic analysis. Subsequent work will mine additional species-diagnostic InDel loci distributed evenly across all chromosomes to enrich this marker panel, facilitating comprehensive population genetic and hybrid origin analysis of Turanga desert poplars.

Conclusion

In this study, two stable species-specific co-dominant InDel markers were developed and validated to screen morphologically intermediate putative interspecific hybrid candidates between P. euphratica and P. pruinosa. All associated genotyping data and marker information have been generated as an open-access genomic resource. These reusable markers provide a reliable genotyping tool for desert poplar hybrid identification and population-genetic investigation. The publicly shared dataset will support future germplasm monitoring and large-scale genetic research on desert poplars.

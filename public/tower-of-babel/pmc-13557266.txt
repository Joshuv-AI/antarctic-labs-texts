Mycosis Fungoides Characterized by Palmar-Plantar Keratosis and Exudation
Authors: Huanhuan Qu, Fengming Chen, Lei Wang
Published: 2026

Case Presentation

A 33-year-old male presented with a one-year history of erythematous plaques with exudation on his hands and feet. Initial lesions manifested as erythema and vesicles, misdiagnosed as eczema and treated with topical corticosteroids, but symptoms worsened, eventually involving the entire palms, soles, and nails (Figure 1A). Histopathological examination revealed marked hyperkeratosis, serous exudation, epidermal hyperplasia (Figure 1B), and multilocular intraepidermal vesicles with abundant lymphocytic infiltration forming Pautrier microabscesses (Figure 1C). Dense infiltration of large, atypical lymphocytes was observed in the dermis (Figure 1D). Immunohistochemistry showed diffuse cluster of differentiation 3 (CD3) positivity in neoplastic cells (Figure 1E), CD4+, CD8−, CD5+, CD7−, and CD30− staining, with a Ki-67 index of 55%. T-cell receptor gene rearrangement confirmed monoclonality, establishing the diagnosis of mycosis fungoides (MF).

Teaching Point

MF involving the palms and soles is rare and presents with atypical features [1]. Without histopathological evaluation, it is frequently misdiagnosed as eczema, palmoplantar pustulosis, or other benign dermatoses. In this case, the clinical presentation aligned with prior reports [2]; however, it exhibited more pronounced keratotic and exudative changes. Histopathology revealed prominent intraepidermal blister formation and marked serous exudation, correlating clinically with intense keratotic and oozing. Immunohistochemistry demonstrated that these microabscesses were composed of CD4+ and CD8− monoclonal T cells, strongly supporting MF diagnosis. Additionally, large, atypical tumor cells were observed, exhibiting morphological features of large cell transformation and a high Ki-67 proliferation index. This case underscores the importance of biopsy in persistent, treatment-resistant lesions.

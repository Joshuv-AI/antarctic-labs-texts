Lyapunov stability of the equilibrium of the non-local continuity equation
Yurii Averboukh, Aleksei Volkov

Abstract
The paper is concerned with the development of Lyapunov methods for the analysis of equilibrium stability in a dynamical system on the space of probability measures driven by a non-local continuity equation. We derive sufficient conditions of stability of an equilibrium distribution relying on an analysis of a non-smooth Lyapunov function. For the linear dynamics we reduce the stability analysis to a study of a quadratic form on a tangent space to the space of probability measures. These results are illustrated by the studies of the stability of the equilibrium measure for gradient flow in the space of probability measures and Gibbs measure for a system of coupled mathematical pendulums. Keywords: non-local continuity equation, second Lyapunov method, non-smooth Lyapunov function, stability, derivatives in the measure space. MSC: 34D20, 35B35, 35F20, 35Q70, 35R06, 82C22.

Introduction

The paper is concerned with the study of qualitative properties of a dynamical system in space probability measures driven by the non-local continuity equation

\partial_{t}m_{t}+\operatorname{div}(f(x,m_{t})m_{t})=0.

This equation describes the dynamics of a system of infinitely many identical particles in the case where the right-hand side depends not only on the position of the particles, but also on their current distribution.
In this case, the function
f
plays the role of a vector field that determines the motion of each particle.

Notice that the continuity equation occurs within modeling of the systems of charged
particles
[
1
]
, behavior of supermassive black holes
[

, behavior of large groups of animals
[
3
]

[

[

Previously, such properties of the non-local continuity equation as stability w.r.t. parameters
[

]
and exponential stability
[
7
]
were studied.
Also in
[
8
]
the issues of stability of the measure carrier and integral stability were studied for the case where the equilibrium distribution under study is a Dirac measure.

In this paper, we examine the stability in the sense of Lyapunov.
This concept was first proposed in the famous works of A. M. Lyapunov
[
9
,
10
]
for systems of ordinary differential equations, after which it received numerous applications.
Since then, the concept of Lyapunov stability attracts a great attention (see, in particular,

The study of stability is relies on the first and second Lyapunov methods
[

10
]

In the first method, a linear approximation of the dynamics is used to characterize the stability of the system.
The second method assumes the existence of a differentiable function with certain properties called a Lyapunov function.
The Lyapunov’s methods have received significant development for controlled systems, especially for stabilization
[
16
,
17
]
.
Note that non-smooth Lyapunov functions are often used in control theory.
In this case, sub-/supergradients are used instead of derivatives.

Recall that the phase space for the non-local continuity equation is the phase space of probability measures that is not linear.
This fact is the main feature of the examined problem.
At the same time, the concept of an intrinsic derivative was introduced for this case in
[
18
]
, as well as a number of analogues of the concepts of non-smooth analysis
[
19
]
.
In particular, among them are strong and weak sub-/superdifferentials.
Notice that the square of the distance in the space of probability measures to a given measure is generally a non-differentiable function.
However, some of the elements of the superdifferentiation can be described explicitly
[
19
]
.

To construct an analogue of the second Lyapunov method, this article uses a non-smooth Lyapunov function.
The proposed construction of the second Lyapunov method relies on the definition of the barycentric sub-/superdifferential introduced in Section
2.3
of this article.
Notice that the barycentric sub-/superdifferential can be built on the basis of the strong Frechet sub-/superdifferential proposed in the work
[
19
]
.
This method allows, in particular, to find sufficient conditions for the stability of particle systems with dynamics determined by a gradient flow in the space of probabilistic measures.

Based on the second Lyapunov method, we study the stability of a stationary solution of the non-local continuity equation in the case of a vector field linear w.r.t. phase variables.
This result is based on the application of the second method with the Lyapunov function equal to half the square of the distance to the equilibrium position.
As an illustration of this method, the stability of the Gibbs measure in a system of coupled mathematical pendulums is considered.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
T>0
, a measure
m_{*}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
, a measure-valued function
m_{\cdot}\in\Gamma_{T}({\mathcal{P}}_{p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}))
is a solution of the initial value problem (
1
), (
2
) on the interval
[0,T]
and for locally Lipschitz continuous function

the following conditions are hold:

\phi
is superdifferentiable at
m_{t}
when
t\in[0,T]
;

2.

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{\gamma\in\partial^{+}_{b}\phi(m_{t})}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}\gamma(x)\cdot f(x,m_{t})\ m_{t}(dx)\leq 0
when

.

Then

\phi(m_{T})-\phi(m_{0})\leq 0.

Proof.

Foremost, we claim the following fact: there exists a constant
C(T,m_{*})
such that, for each
\varepsilon>0
and for every
s\in[0,T)
, one can find
r\in(0,T]
satisfying the inequality

\phi(m_{r})-\phi(m_{s})\leq C(T,m_{*})\cdot\varepsilon\cdot(r-s).

(6)

To prove this fact we fix
\varepsilon>0
and the time
s\in[0,T)
.
From the second condition of the Lemma we can choose a supergradient
\gamma_{s}\in\partial^{+}_{b}(m_{s})
such that the following inequality holds:

\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}\gamma_{s}(x)\cdot f(x,m_{s})\ m_{s}(dx)\leq\varepsilon.

Further, let
r\in(s,T]
and
\tau>0
be such that

\begin{split}\xi_{s}(\tau)&\leq\varepsilon,\\
\tau=(r-s)&\leq\varepsilon.\end{split}

Here
\xi_{s}
is a function, defined in Definition
2.22
that satisfies the inequality

\phi\big((\Id+\tau b_{s})\sharp m_{s}\big)-\phi(m_{s})\leq\tau\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}\gamma_{s}(x)\cdot b_{s}(x)\ m_{s}(dx)+\tau\xi_{s}(\tau).

(7)

Furthermore, let

b_{s}(x)\triangleq f(x,m_{s}).

We consider the difference

\phi(m_{r})-\phi(m_{s})=\Big[\phi(m_{r})-\phi\big((\Id+\tau b_{s})\sharp m_{s}\big)\Big]+\Big[\phi\big((\Id+\tau b_{s})\sharp m_{s}\big)-\phi(m_{s})\Big].

Due to choice of
\tau
we have that

\phi\big((\Id+\tau b_{s})\sharp m_{s}\big)-\phi(m_{s})\leq 2\varepsilon(r-s).

Now, let us evaluate the difference

\phi(m_{r})-\phi\big((\Id+\tau b_{s})\sharp m_{s}\big).

To this end, we put

\beta_{s}(T,m_{*})\triangleq G_{1}(T,\varsigma_{p}(m_{*}))+T\cdot\|f(\cdot,m_{s})\|_{L_{p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}},m_{s};{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}.

Notice that, due to Proposition
2.1
,

\varsigma_{p}((\Id+\tau b_{s})\sharp m_{s})\leq G_{1}(T,\varsigma_{p}(m_{*}))+\tau\|f(\cdot,m_{s})\|_{L_{p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}},m_{s};{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}\leq\beta_{s}(T,m_{*}),

while Proposition
2.15
yields the estimate

\varsigma_{p}(m_{r})\leq G_{1}(T,\varsigma_{p}(m_{*}))\leq\beta_{s}(T,m_{*}).

Now, let us introduce a map

\chi_{1}:z\mapsto\left(z+(r-s)\cdot b_{s}(z),\ z+\int_{s}^{r}f(\mathsf{X}^{{s},{z}}_{m_{\cdot}}(t),m_{t})\ dt\right),

and define a plan by the rule

\pi\triangleq\chi_{1}\sharp m_{s}.

Then, thanks to the local Lipschitz continuity of the function
\phi
, Definition
2.4
and Proposition
2.16
, the following estimation is fulfilled:

\begin{split}\phi(m_{r})&-\phi((\Id+\tau b_{s})\sharp m_{s})\\
&\leq K_{\beta_{s}(T,m_{*})}\cdot W_{p}\big((\Id+\tau b_{s})\sharp m_{s},m_{r}\big)\\
&\leq K_{\beta_{s}(T,m_{*})}\cdot\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left\|x+(r-s)\cdot b_{s}(x)-x-\int_{s}^{r}f(\mathsf{X}^{{s},{x}}_{m_{\cdot}}(t),m_{s})\ dt\right\|^{p}\ m_{s}(dx)\right)^{1/p}\\
&\leq K_{\beta_{s}(T,m_{*})}\cdot G_{2}(T,\varsigma_{p}(m_{*}))\cdot(r-s)^{2}\leq K_{\beta_{s}(T,m_{*})}\cdot G_{2}(T,\varsigma_{p}(m_{*}))\cdot\varepsilon\cdot(r-s).\end{split}

Denoting
\check{K}\triangleq\sup\limits_{s\in[0,T]}K_{\beta_{s}(T,m_{*})}
, we arrive to the inequality

\phi(m_{r})-\phi((\Id+\tau b_{s})\sharp m_{s})\leq\check{K}\cdot G_{2}(T,\varsigma_{p}(m_{*}))\cdot\varepsilon\cdot(r-s).

(8)

Letting
C(T,m_{*})\triangleq 2+\check{K}\cdot G_{2}(T,\varsigma_{p}(m_{*}))
and using inequalities (
7
) and (
8
), we obtain required estimate (
6
).

Next, we consider the set

\Theta\triangleq\{\theta\in[0,T]:\phi(m_{\theta})-\phi(m_{0})\leq C(T,m_{*})\cdot\varepsilon\cdot\theta\}.

This set is non-empty.
Indeed,
0\in\Theta
.
Denote
\check{\theta}\triangleq\sup\Theta
.
The continuity of the functions
m_{\cdot}
and
\phi(\cdot)
imply the inclusion
\check{\theta}\in\Theta
.
Let us show that
\check{\theta}=T
.

By contradiction, assume that
\check{\theta}<T
.
Due to (
6
) one can find
\theta\in(\check{\theta},T]
such that

\phi(m_{\theta})-\phi(m_{\check{\theta}})\leq C(T,m_{*})\cdot\varepsilon\cdot(\theta-\check{\theta}).

By the construction of the constant
\check{\theta}
we have that

\phi(m_{\theta})-\phi(m_{0})=(\phi(m_{\theta})-\phi(m_{\check{\theta}}))-(\phi(m_{\check{\theta}})-\phi(m_{0}))\leq C(T,m_{*})\cdot\varepsilon\cdot\theta.

In other words,
\theta\in\Theta
and
\theta>\check{\theta}=\sup\Theta
.
This contradicts with assumption that
\check{\theta}<T
.

Thus,
\sup\Theta=T\in\Theta
, and therefore

\phi(m_{T})-\phi(m_{0})\leq C(T,m_{*})\cdot\varepsilon\cdot T.

Since we can choose
\varepsilon
to be arbitrary small, the previous inequality implies

\phi(m_{T})-\phi(m_{0})\leq 0.

∎

Let us consider a system of particles driven by the dynamics

The corresponding continuity equation takes the form

\partial_{t}m_{t}-\operatorname{div}\big(\big(\nabla_{m}\phi(m,x)\big)^{\top}m_{t}\big)=0.

(9)

Notice, that (see
[
24

\nabla_{m}\phi(m,x)=\nabla_{x}v(x,m)+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\nabla_{m}v(y,m,x)\ m(dy).

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\varphi(x)\cdot\nabla_{m}\phi(\hat{m},x)^{\top}\ \hat{m}(dx)=\nabla_{x}\varphi(0)\cdot\nabla_{m}\phi(\hat{m},0)^{\top}=0,

for all

The left-hand side of this equality can be rewritten as

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}&\nabla_{x}\varphi(x)\cdot\left(\nabla_{x}v(x,\hat{m})+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\nabla_{m}v(y,\hat{m},x)\ \hat{m}(dy)\right)^{\top}\ \hat{m}(dx)\\
&\hskip 142.26378pt=\nabla_{x}\varphi(0)\cdot\left(\nabla_{x}v(0,\delta_{0})+\nabla_{m}v(0,\delta_{0},0)\right)^{\top}=0.\end{split}

\nabla_{m}\phi(m,\cdot)\in\partial_{b}\phi(m)\subseteq\partial^{+}_{b}\phi(m).

•

•

•

since

&\hskip 42.67912pt\leq\int\limits_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}l\cdot\big(\|x-y\|+W_{p}(\mu,\nu)\big)\ \pi(dxdy)\\

First, let us find the barycentric superdifferential of the function

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\hat{m},m\in{\mathcal{P}}_{2}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
and

Proof.

From

, Theorem 10.2.2]
we have that, for all measures

the following inequality holds true:

\begin{split}\phi(\mu^{3})-\phi(\mu^{1})&\leq\int\limits_{({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})^{3}}(x_{1}-x_{2})^{\top}\cdot(x_{3}-x_{1})\ {\boldsymbol{\mu}}(dx_{1}dx_{2}dx_{3})\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt+\int\limits_{({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})^{3}}\|x_{3}-x_{1}\|^{2}\ {\boldsymbol{\mu}}(dx_{1}dx_{2}dx_{3}).\end{split}

(10)

Further, we choose an arbitrary

and

\mu^{2}&=\hat{m},\\
\mu^{3}&=(\Id+\tau b)\sharp m,\\

Renaming variables:

\begin{split}x&=x_{1},\\

\begin{split}\int\limits_{({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})^{2}}(x-\hat{x})^{\top}\cdot\tau b(x)\ &\pi(d\hat{x}dx)+\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|\tau b(x)\|^{2}\ \pi(d\hat{x}dx)\\

Let us denote

Therefore,

\phi((\Id+\tau b)\sharp m)-\phi(m)\leq\tau\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\hat{\pi}^{1}(x)\cdot b(x)\ m(dx)+\tau\cdot\xi(\tau).

(13)

4.1
Example: A mean field system of pendulums

Let
d=2n
and
x_{1},x_{2}\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
.
Denote

•

\displaystyle x=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{pmatrix}
;

•

\displaystyle H(x)=\frac{x_{1}^{2}}{2}+\frac{x_{2}^{2}}{2}
;

•

\displaystyle H_{1}=H_{x_{1}}
,
H_{2}=H_{x_{2}}
;

•

\displaystyle A=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{O}&\mathbb{I}\\
-\mathbb{I}&\mathbb{O}\end{pmatrix}
,

where
\mathbb{O}
and
\mathbb{I}
are zero and identical matrices with dimension
n\times n
respectively.
Notice that

Ax=\begin{pmatrix}H_{1}(x)\\
-H_{2}(x)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}x_{2}\\
-x_{1}\end{pmatrix}.

If
n>1
, then a vector composed of multidimensional quantities is interpreted as a vertical concatenation, squaring as a scalar square,
H_{1}
(respectively
H_{2}
) denotes the vector of partial derivatives of
H
w.r.t. the components of
x_{1}
(respectively
x_{2}
).

Consider a system

\dot{x}=Ax+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}By\ m(dy)\triangleq f(x,m),

(17)

where
B
is a negative-definite matrix.
Notice that first term in this equation describes the free movement of a pendulum, while the second one shows an influence of all other pendulums on the given one.
Thus, regard dynamics (
17
) as a mean field system of pendulums.
The continuity equation corresponding to this system takes the form:

\partial_{t}m_{t}+\operatorname{div}\left(\left(Ax+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}By\ m_{t}(dy)\right)m_{t}\right)=0.

(18)

Let
\hat{m}
be a Gibbs measure generated by the Hamiltonian
H(x)
.
Recall, that it has a density

g(x)\triangleq\frac{1}{Z(\beta)}e^{-\beta H(x)},

where
Z(\beta)
stands for the normalizing coefficient, and that its first moment is equal to zero.
Now, we show that the measure
\hat{m}
is an equilibrium for equation (
18
), i.e.,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\varphi(x)\cdot\left(Ax+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}By\ \hat{m}(dy)\right)\ \hat{m}(dx)=0.

Due to the described properties of the Gibbs measure, it suffices to prove that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\nabla_{x}(\varphi(x)\cdot Ax)\cdot g(x)\ dx=0.

(19)

Integrating the left-hand side by parts, we arrive to the equality

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(\nabla_{x}\varphi(x)\cdot Ax)\cdot g(x)\ dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\operatorname{div}_{x}\big(Ax\cdot g(x)\big)\varphi(x)\ dx.

The Gibbs measure is invariant w.r.t. the Hamiltonian dynamics.
Therefore, its density satisfies the Liouville equation (see, for example,
[
25
]
):

\operatorname{div}_{x}\big(Ax\cdot g(x)\big)=0.

This implies (
19
).

Let us check the conditions of Theorem
4.4
.
For an arbitrary nonzero integrable function
\xi:{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\to{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
, we have that

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\xi(\hat{x})^{\top}&\cdot A\cdot\xi(\hat{x})\ \hat{m}(d\hat{x})\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\xi(\hat{x})^{\top}\ \hat{m}(d\hat{x})\cdot B\cdot\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\xi(\hat{y})\ \hat{m}(d\hat{y})\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\xi(\hat{x})^{\top}\ \hat{m}(d\hat{x})\cdot B\cdot\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\xi(\hat{y})\ \hat{m}(d\hat{y}).\end{split}

Due to the negative-definiteness of the matrix
B
, this expression is negative.

Hence, the equilibrium
\hat{m}
and the function
f
satisfy the conditions of Theorem
4.4
.
This yields the stability of
\hat{m}
.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we consider an autonomous dynamic system in the space of probabilistic measures defined by a non-local continuity equation.
Sufficient conditions for the local stability based on the second Lyapunov method have been studied.
The above results can be extended to such issues of significant interest as trajectory stability analysis and global stability problems.

Proposition B.4
.

Let

and let a function

Given
i>0
and

\begin{split}\mu_{1}&\triangleq m,\\
\mu_{3}&\triangleq(\Id+\tau b)\sharp m,\\

Appendix C
Boundedness of the barycentric differential

In this section we analyze the uniform boundedness of barycentric superdifferentials in the case of
p=2
.

Proposition C.1
.

Let
m\in{\mathcal{P}}_{2}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
and let
\alpha>0
be such that
\varsigma_{2}(m)\leq\alpha
.
Assume, additionally, that
\phi:{\mathcal{P}}_{2}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
is locally Lipschitz continuous and superdifferentiable at the point
m
.
Then, for all
\gamma\in\partial^{+}_{b}\phi(m)
, the following estimation holds:

\|\gamma\|_{L_{2}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}},m;{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}\leq K_{\alpha},

where
K_{\alpha}
is Lipschitz constant of the
\phi
in the ball of the radius
\alpha
.

Proof.

We let in the definition of the superdifferential
b=-\gamma^{\top}
.
Then, there exists a function
\xi:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that, for all
\tau>0
,

\phi((\Id-\tau\gamma^{\top})\sharp m)-\phi(m)\leq-\tau\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}\gamma^{\top}(x)\cdot\gamma(x)\ m(dx)+\tau\xi(\tau)=-\tau\|\gamma\|_{L_{2}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}},m;{{\mathbb{R}}^{d*}})}^{2}+\tau\xi(\tau).

Using Proposition
2.1
, we have that

\forall\epsilon>0\ \exists\theta>0:\ \forall\tau\in[0,\theta]\ \ \varsigma_{2}((\Id-\tau\gamma)\sharp m)\leq\alpha+\epsilon.

This and the local Lipschitz continuity imply the following estimate:

\begin{split}\tau\|\gamma\|_{L_{2}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}},m;{{\mathbb{R}}^{d*}})}^{2}-\tau\xi(\tau)&\leq-\big(\phi((\Id-\tau\gamma^{\top})\sharp m)-\phi(m)\big)\leq\big|\phi((\Id-\tau\gamma^{\top})\sharp m)-\phi(m)\big|\\
&\leq K_{\alpha+\epsilon}\cdot W_{2}((\Id-\tau\gamma^{\top})\sharp m,m)\leq K_{\alpha+\epsilon}\cdot\tau\left(\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}\|\gamma(x)\|^{2}m(dx)\right)^{1/2}.\end{split}

Further, dividing by
\tau
and letting
\tau\to 0
, we obtain

\|\gamma\|_{L_{2}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}},m;{{\mathbb{R}}^{d*}}}^{2}\leq K_{\alpha+\epsilon}\|\gamma\|_{L_{2}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}},m;{{\mathbb{R}}^{d*}})}.

Finally, letting
\epsilon\to 0
and using the right continuous of the Lipschitz constant, we conclude that

\|\gamma\|_{L_{2}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}},m;{{\mathbb{R}}^{d*}})}^{2}\leq K_{\alpha}\|\gamma\|_{L_{2}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}},m;{{\mathbb{R}}^{d*}})}.

∎

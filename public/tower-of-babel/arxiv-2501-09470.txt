Control and its applications in additive combinatorics
Thomas F. Bloom

Abstract
. We prove new quantitative bounds on the additive structure of sets obeying an L 3 L^{3} ‘control’ assumption, which arises naturally in several questions within additive combinatorics. This has a number of applications – in particular we improve the known bounds for the sum-product problem, the Balog-Szemerédi-Gowers theorem, and the additive growth of convex sets.



1.
Introduction

Everything in this paper, unless specified otherwise, takes place in some fixed (but arbitrary) abelian group
G
. All sets will be assumed to be finite, and all functions assumed to have finite support. We will investigate the following concept, and highlight its relevance to several questions in additive combinatorics.

Definition 1

(Control)
.

Let
\kappa\in(0,1]
. We say that a set
A
has control
\kappa
if
\kappa
is minimal such that, for any finite set
B
,
1
1

1

For basic definitions and conventions about e.g. what the convolution operator
\ast
means see Section
1.4
.

\sum_{x}1_{A}\ast 1_{B}(x)^{3}\leqslant\kappa\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}.

For comparison, the trivial bounds
\left\lVert 1_{A}\ast 1_{B}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\min(\left\lvert A\right\rvert,\left\lvert B\right\rvert)
and
\sum_{x}1_{A}\ast 1_{B}(x)=\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert B\right\rvert
imply that every set has control
\leqslant 1
. We also have the trivial lower bound that, if
A
has control
\kappa
,

\kappa\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1},

which can be seen by taking
B=-A
, for example, and noting that
1_{A}\ast 1_{-A}(0)=\left\lvert A\right\rvert
. This lower bound is achieved (up to logarithmic factors) by, for example, convex sets of real numbers, such as geometric progressions or
\{n^{2}:1\leqslant n\leqslant N\}
(as we will prove in Lemma
4
). It may also be useful to note that if
G
is a finite group and
A\subseteq G
with density
\alpha=\left\lvert A\right\rvert/\left\lvert G\right\rvert
then (taking
B=G
) we have
\kappa\geqslant\alpha
.

This definition may appear strange to those unfamiliar with the area, but this kind of control property arises independently in the study of several central questions within additive combinatorics. The study of such a property was pioneered by Shkredov in a number of papers, particularly
[
20
]
. Since this kind of
L^{3}
control hypothesis is often produced from an application of the Szemerédi-Trotter theorem, Shkredov has referred to sets with such a property as Szemerédi-Trotter sets. We take this opportunity to coin the new term ‘control’ for the relevant
\kappa
, to highlight the importance of this parameter. (Moreover, in at least some applications control is present for reasons unrelated to the Szemerédi-Trotter theorem, so we felt a more neutral term was appropriate.) Of course, what we call control should more properly be called
L^{3}
-control, and one can imagine natural variants for other norms. We hope to return to such generalisations and their applications in future work. (In particular the
L^{4}
analogue would be relevant for sum-product problems in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, arising from the use of the point-line incidence results of Stevens and de Zeeuw
[
23
]
in place of the Szemerédi-Trotter theorem.)

In this paper we will be concerned with, amongst other things, how control relates to more usual notions of additive structure such as the size of the sumset
A+A
and the additive energy, defined by

E(A)=\left\lVert 1_{A}\ast 1_{A}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\sum_{x}1_{A}\ast 1_{A}(x)^{2}.

It is an immediate trivial consequence of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that

E(A)\leqslant\left(\sum 1_{A}\ast 1_{A}(x)^{3}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum 1_{A}\ast 1_{A}(x)\right)^{1/2}\leqslant\kappa^{1/2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}.

Similarly, trivial applications of Hölder’s inequality yield
\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert\geqslant\kappa^{-1/2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
and
\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert\geqslant\kappa^{-1/2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
. These bounds should be thought of as the ‘trivial’ bounds, and they do not make full use of the power of the control assumption. Shkredov was the first to prove bounds which go beyond these thresholds. In this paper we will prove the following non-trivial bounds, which are improvements over those previously available for
E(A)
and
\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert
.

Theorem 1
.

If
A
has control
\kappa
then, for any
\epsilon>0
,

E(A)\ll_{\epsilon}\kappa^{27/50-\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3},

\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert\gg_{\epsilon}\kappa^{-11/19+\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert,

and

\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert\gg_{\epsilon}\kappa^{-2506/4175+\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert.

Note that
27/50=0.54
,
11/19\approx 0.578
, and
2506/4175\approx 0.6002
. The previous best exponents available in the literature (at least implicitly) were
7/13\approx 0.538
,
11/19
, and
3/5=0.6
respectively (due to Shkredov
[
20
]
, Rudnev and Stevens
[
15
]
, and Schoen and Shkredov
[
18
]
). We defer further discussion of the previous literature and such results to Section
2
.

The reader will observe that our quantitative improvements are small, and in the case of
\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert
non-existent. One of our main goals is to show that some improvement is possible, and moreover using purely ‘elementary’ techniques (nothing deeper than the pigeonhole principle and Hölder’s inequality). The original investigations of Shkredov
[
20
]
used a more elaborate spectral method (in particular studying eigenvalues of the convolution operator). (We note however that the energy bound
E(A)\ll_{\epsilon}\kappa^{7/13-\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}
was reproved using elementary methods by Olmezov
[
11
]
, whose approach has many similarities to our own.)

Our second goal is to advertise the concept of control and explain how it is intertwined with several important questions within additive combinatorics, bringing together various scattered threads in the literature. We hope that by demonstrating the utility of the type of quantitative improvements in Theorem
1
to other quantitative questions we can encourage further research.

There are a number of applications of control within additive combinatorics, and we present the highlights (incorporating improvements arising from the new bounds in Theorem
1
) below. This paper has been structured to make the applications easy to export – in particular, the reader should note that improvements to the exponents in Theorem
1
immediately propagate to corresponding improvements on all of the applications listed below. (In particular, and surprisingly, as Shakan
[
19
]
has noted, one can obtain new bounds for the sum-product problem via the purely additive problem of improving the bounds in Theorem
1
.)

1.1.
Application 1: Convex sets

The most direct application of control is to the additive behaviour of convex sets. A convex set is a finite set
A=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
such that
a_{i+1}-a_{i}>a_{i}-a_{i-1}
for
1<i<n
. The relevance of control to convex sets is simple: convex sets have the best possible control of
\kappa\lesssim\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1}
. This can be proved using the Szemerédi-Trotter incidence theorem, as first observed by Schoen and Shkredov
[
18
]
. We include a proof in this paper as Lemma
4
.

Convex sets should exhibit non-trivial growth under addition (first proved qualitatively by Hegyvári
[
6
]
). It has long been conjectured (in particular by Erdős
[
4
]
) that convex sets should exhibit maximal growth, in that
\min(\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert,\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert)\gg_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2-\epsilon}
and
E(A)\ll_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2+\epsilon}
. Theorem
1
, coupled with the fact that convex sets have control
\lesssim\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1}
, immediately implies the following improvements for two out of the three of these measures of additive growth.

Theorem 2
.

If
A\subset\mathbb{R}
is a convex set then, for any
\epsilon>0
,

E(A)\ll_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{123/50+\epsilon},

and

\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert\gg_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{6681/4175-\epsilon}.

The best previous bounds were
E(A)\ll_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{32/13+\epsilon}
(due to Shkredov
[
20
]
) and
\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert\gtrsim\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{8/5}
(due to Schoen and Shkredov
[
18
]
). For comparison note that
32/13\approx 2.461
and
8/5=1.6
, while
123/50=2.46
and
6681/4175\approx 1.6002
.

For
\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert
we have been unable to improve on the bound
\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert\gg_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{30/19-\epsilon}
of Rudnev and Stevens
[
15
]
. Various improvements to these bounds are known if stronger convexity hypotheses are assumed, as shown by Olmezov
[
10
]
. For example, if the convexity assumption is strengthened to a natural fourth-order variant then Olmezov proved
E(A)\ll\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{7/3}
and
\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/3}
. Other improvements under a similar strengthened convexity assumption were proved by Bradshaw, Hanson, and Rudnev
[
3
]
.

One can ask, more generally, about the relationship between the additive growth of
A
and
f(A)
, where
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a convex function (the case of convex
A
is the special case where
A=f(\{1,\ldots,\left\lvert A\right\rvert\})
). This was investigated by Shkredov
[
21
]
and, most recently, by Stevens and Warren
[
24
]
. As a consequence of our methods we deduce the following.

Theorem 3
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}
be a finite set and
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous convex function. For any
\epsilon>0

\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert\left\lvert f(A)+f(A)\right\rvert\gg_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{49/19-\epsilon}

and

\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert\left\lvert f(A)-f(A)\right\rvert\gg_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{10856/4175-\epsilon}.

In particular,

\max(\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert,\left\lvert f(A)+f(A)\right\rvert)\gg_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{49/38-\epsilon}

and

\max(\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert,\left\lvert f(A)-f(A)\right\rvert)\gg_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5428/4175-\epsilon}.

The inequality for sumsets is equal in strength to that proved by Stevens and Warren
[
24
]
. The conclusion for difference sets is slightly stronger than that of Stevens and Warren, who obtained an exponent of
13/10=1.3
, while
5428/4175\approx 1.3001
. (For comparison note that
49/38\approx 1.289
.) In fact Stevens and Warren prove a more general version, allowing for an inequality on
\left\lvert A+B\right\rvert\left\lvert f(A)+g(B)\right\rvert
for possibly different sets
A
and
B
and different (convex)
f
and
g
, which also allows one to bound
\left\lvert A+f(A)\right\rvert
. Our methods can in principle obtain such asymmetric conclusions, but we have chosen to focus on the symmetric case to simplify the exposition.

1.2.
Application 2: The sum-product problem

The sum-product problem is one of the central problems within additive combinatorics: given an arbitrary set of real numbers (say), must either the sum set or the product set grow? In other words, is it true that no set can be simultaneously additively and multiplicatively structured? There are a number of ways to make this question precise. The connection with control is more involved here, and we use the work of a number of authors below, who have found ingenious ways to maximise the amount of control available when studying sum-product problems. In short, the heuristic is that a set with lots of multiplicative structure must have good additive control – and hence via the type of estimates in Theorem
1
must grow under addition.

We present small improvements to a number of sum-product problems that have been considered in the literature. These improvements stem almost entirely from the improved exponents of Theorem
1
, although we take this opportunity to sharpen some of the previous arguments, and consolidate these various applications under the unified notion of control. Once again, the improvements here are marginal, but we want to record some of the consequences of the new bounds of Theorem
1
, and to demonstrate the variety of sum-product problems which are intimately connected with the notion of control.

The usual way to precisely frame the sum-product question is to ask for the largest exponent
c
such that (for any finite
A\subset\mathbb{R}
and
\epsilon>0
)

\max(\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert,\left\lvert AA\right\rvert)\gg_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{c-\epsilon}.

The natural conjecture, first posed by Erdős, is that
c=2
is the truth here. An elegant geometric argument of Solymosi
[
22
]
proved that
c=4/3
is permissible. Konyagin and Shkredov
[
7
]
found a way to combine Solymosi’s argument with the notion of control, which gave an improved exponent of
4/3+\delta
for some small (but explicit)
\delta>0
. This argument in turn saw further refinements, leading to the previous best exponent, due to Rudnev and Stevens
[
15
]
, of

c=\frac{1558}{1167}=\frac{4}{3}+\frac{2}{1167}\approx 1.33504.

We offer a marginal improvement, and also note that a slightly better exponent is available if we also consider the size of the difference set
\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert
.

Theorem 4
.

If
A\subset\mathbb{R}
is any finite set then, for any
\epsilon>0
,

\max(\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert,\left\lvert AA\right\rvert)\gg_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{c_{1}-\epsilon}

and

\max(\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert,\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert,\left\lvert AA\right\rvert)\gg_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{c_{2}-\epsilon}

where

c_{1}=\frac{1270}{951}=\frac{4}{3}+\frac{2}{951}\approx 1.33543.

and

c_{2}=\frac{144511}{108174}\approx 1.33591.

One might instead consider the additive and multiplicative energies, in place of the sizes of the sum set and product set. Balog and Wooley
[
2
]
were the first to consider this variant, and proved that, in any finite
A\subset\mathbb{R}
, there must exist some large subset of
A
with either small additive energy or small multiplicative energy. This was improved by Rudnev, Shkredov, and Stevens
[
14
]
and then further by Shakan
[
19
]
. The best previous estimate in this direction is due to Xue
[
26
]
, who proved that any
A\subset\mathbb{R}
contains
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
of size
\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert
such that, for any
\epsilon>0
,

\min(E_{+}(A),E_{\times}(A))\ll_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{736/271+\epsilon}.

Combining Theorem
1
with the argument of Xue we obtain the following slight improvement.

Theorem 5
.

For any finite
A\subset\mathbb{R}
there exists
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
with
\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert
such that, for any
\epsilon>0
,

\min(E_{+}(A^{\prime}),E_{\times}(A^{\prime}))\ll_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{258/95+\epsilon}.

For comparison, note that
736/271\approx 2.71586
while
258/95\approx 2.71578
. A construction of Balog and Wooley
[
2
]
shows that the best exponent possible here is
7/3
.

An alternative type of sum-product result (sometimes known as expander results) is to prove a non-trivial lower bound for the size of a set under a fixed operation which involves both addition and multiplication, such as
A(A+A)
. Our method produces the following small improvements to previous expander results.

Theorem 6
.

For any finite
A\subset\mathbb{R}
there exists some
a\in A
such that, for any
\epsilon>0
,

\left\lvert A(A+a)\right\rvert\gg_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{229/152-\epsilon},

\left\lvert A(A+A)\right\rvert\gg_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{32/21-\epsilon}

and

\left\lvert A(A-A)\right\rvert\gg_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{7239/4733-\epsilon}.

The previous best exponents were due to Murphy, Roche-Newton, and Shkredov
[
9
]
, and were (respectively)
140/93\approx 1.505
,
184/121\approx 1.521
, and
26/17\approx 1.5294
. For comparison,
229/152\approx 1.507
,
32/21\approx 1.524
and
7239/4733\approx 1.5295
. (The reader may also be interested in a similar expander result of Rudnev and Stevens
[
15
]
, who show that
\left\lvert AA+AA\right\rvert\gg_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{1.5875-\epsilon}
– our methods currently yield no improvement on this exponent.)

Finally, one can ask for a sum-product result which finds (in an arbitrary finite set of real numbers) some set which lacks both additive and multiplicative structure. We say that
A\subset\mathbb{R}
is an additive Sidon set if the only solutions to
a+b=c+d
are the trivial ones
\{a,b\}=\{c,d\}
, and similarly define a multiplicative Sidon set. We say that
A
is a bi-Sidon set if it is simultaenously an additive and multiplicative Sidon set. Ruzsa
[
16
]
observed that every
A\subset\mathbb{R}
contains a bi-Sidon set of size
\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{1/3}
. Pach and Zakharov
[
12
]
have improved this to
\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{11/26}
. The following small improvement is a consequence of our new estimates.

Theorem 7
.

Let
\epsilon>0
. Any finite
A\subset\mathbb{R}
contains a bi-Sidon subset of size

\gg_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{122/285-\epsilon}.

For comparison, note that
11/26\approx 0.423
while
122/285\approx 0.428
. The likely true exponent here is
1/2
– such a bound would follow from Pach and Zakharov’s argument if the upper bound of Theorem
5
could be improved to
\ll_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/2+\epsilon}
.

1.3.
Application 3: Balog-Szemerédi-Gowers theorem

As another application we strengthen the classical Balog-Szemerédi-Gowers theorem. The subject of this theorem is to find some converse to the trivial fact that
\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}/E(A)
– in other words, the fact that if
A
has small additive energy then it must have large difference set. The obvious converse fails, since one could for example have half of
A
being a geometric progression (which creates a near-maximal difference set) and the other half being an arithmetic progression (which creates near-maximal additive energy).

It was first observed by Balog and Szemerédi
[
1
]
that a converse does, however, hold, provided one is willing to pass to a subset of
A
first. This was reproved by Gowers
[
5
]
with polynomial bounds. As a consequence of our investigations into control we are able to improve the quantitative aspect of this polynomial control.

The link between control and the Balog-Szemerédi-Gowers theorem is new to this paper, although our argument has much in common with that of Schoen
[
17
]
.

Theorem 8
.

Let
\epsilon>0
. If
E(A)\geqslant K^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}
then there exists
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
with
\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\gg_{\epsilon}K^{-100/81-\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
such that

\left\lvert A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}\right\rvert\ll_{\epsilon}K^{100/27+\epsilon}\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert.

Note that
100/27\approx 3.703
. The previous best bound, due to Schoen
[
17
]
, was
\left\lvert A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}\right\rvert\ll K^{4}\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert
. Here we are chiefly concerned with the size of
A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}
, rather than the density of
A^{\prime}
. Indeed, Reiher and Schoen
[
13
]
have proved a best possible result on the density, showing that there must exist some
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
with
\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\gg K^{-1/2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
and
\left\lvert A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}\right\rvert\ll K^{4}\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert
.

The best possible exponent of
K
in
\left\lvert A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}\right\rvert
would be
c_{0}=\frac{\log(4)}{\log(27/16)}\approx 2.649
, as shown by Mazur
[
8
]
. This exponent is achieved by the intersection of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with a sphere (as
d\to\infty
). Indeed, such a family shows that, for any
\epsilon>0
and any
K
large enough depending on
\epsilon
, there exist arbitrarily large sets
A
such that
E(A)\geqslant K^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}
and yet any
X\subseteq A
with
\left\lvert X\right\rvert\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{1-\epsilon}
satisfies
\left\lvert X-X\right\rvert\geqslant K^{c_{0}-\epsilon}\left\lvert X\right\rvert
. In particular the exponent on the doubling constant cannot be improved past
c_{0}
even if we weaken the density condition to
\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\geqslant K^{-100}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
, say.

In Section
2
we begin with a general discussion of the notion of control and present some context and history. In Section
3
we prove the various applications of control to other additive combinatorial problems discussed above. Finally, in Sections
4
and
5
we prove the bounds of Theorem
1
.

1.4.
Notation and conventions

As already mentioned, unless specified otherwise all sets are assumed to be finite subsets of some fixed abelian group
G
, and all functions assumed to be real-valued functions with finite support. We define the convolution of
f,g:G\to\mathbb{R}
by

f\ast g(x)=\sum_{y}f(x-y)g(y)

and the difference convolution by

f\circ g(x)=\sum_{y}f(x+y)g(y).

We use the discrete normalisation, so that the inner product is

\langle f,g\rangle=\sum_{x}f(x)g(x).

We note here the useful adjoint property that

\langle f\ast g,h\rangle=\langle f,h\circ g\rangle,

which is an analytic encoding of the triviality that
x+y=z
is equivalent to
x=z-y
. For
1\leqslant p<\infty
the
L^{p}
norm is defined by

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p}=\left(\sum_{x}\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert^{p}\right)^{1/p}

and
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}=\sup_{x}\lvert f(x)\rvert
. We will make frequent use of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Hölder’s inequality, and in particular note the following consequence of Hölder’s inequality.

Lemma 1
.

For any
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
and
1\leqslant p<q<r<\infty

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p}^{p(r-q)}\leqslant\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}^{q(r-p)}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{r}^{r(q-p)}.

In particular,

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{3/2}\leqslant\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{3}^{1/2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{1}^{1/2}.

The Vinogradov
X\ll Y
notation means that
X\leqslant CY
for some absolute constant
C>0
. If the constant
C
depends on some other parameter then we use a subscript, such as
X\ll_{\epsilon}Y
. Similarly,
X\lesssim_{\kappa}Y
denotes inequality up to poly-logarithmic factors. For visual simplicity, we adopt the convention that if the dependendent parameter is
K\geqslant 1
, as in
X\lesssim_{K}Y
, then a loss of
X\leqslant C_{1}(\log K)^{C_{2}}Y
is intended, while if the dependent parameter is
\kappa<1
, as in
X\lesssim_{\kappa}Y
, then a loss of
\leqslant C_{1}\log(1/\kappa)^{C_{2}}
is intended. We omit the subscripts when which parameter is involved in the logarithmic loss is clear from context.

2.
Control

2.1.
Appearance in the literature

What we have called control is a rescaled version of the quantity
d^{+}(A)
considered by Shakan
[
19
, Definition 1.5]
, which in turn is essentially equivalent to the parameter controlling so-called Szemerédi-Trotter sets considered by Shkredov
[
21
, Definition 5]
. For comparison to the previous literature, note that
d^{+}(A)=\kappa\left\lvert A\right\rvert
.

To our knowledge, the first place this quantity was explicitly considered (albeit in a rescaled form) was in the work of Konyagin and Shkredov
[
7
]
improving the bounds for the sum-product exponent.

Shkredov called sets with a non-trivial bound on this
d^{+}(A)
Szemerédi-Trotter sets due to the pivotal role played by the Szemerédi-Trotter theorem in establishing this type of
L^{3}
bound. We will see demonstations of this shortly in the connections to the sum-product problem and convex sets.

The previous bounds known prior to Theorem
1
are summarised below.

Theorem 9
.

If
A
has control
\kappa
then

E(A)\lesssim\kappa^{7/13}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3},

\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert\gtrsim\kappa^{-3/5}\left\lvert A\right\rvert,

and

\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert\gtrsim\kappa^{-11/19}\left\lvert A\right\rvert.

The upper bound on the energy was first proved by Shkredov
[
20
]
, although it is only stated there in the special case when
A
is a convex set (which has control
\lesssim\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1}
). Shkredov’s proof, which uses the eigenvalue method, immediately generalises to the form given here.

The lower bound on
\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert
was essentially first proved by Schoen and Shkredov
[
18
]
, although again it is only stated there for convex sets. Their proof easily generalises to the more general notion of control, however, as observed by Murphy, Roche-Newton, and Shkredov
[
9
, Lemma 16]
.

Similarly, the lower bound on
\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert
was essentially proved by Rudnev and Stevens
[
15
]
, although it is only stated there explicitly for convex sets. Again, nothing more about convex sets is used aside from their control properties, and hence their proof naturally generalises to the form stated here. We give a self-contained variant proof of this sumset lower bound in Section
5
.

Finally, we note that all of the estimates in Theorem
9
in fact hold under a slightly weaker assumption than full control: it suffices to assume that
\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}\right\rVert_{3}\leqslant\kappa^{1/3}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4/3}
and
\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{B}\right\rVert_{2}\leqslant\kappa^{1/4}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3/4}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{3/4}
for any set
B
. This bound follows from the control assumption via Hölder’s inequality, but it appears to be weaker (although we know of no meaningful examples to separate this ‘weak control’ from full control, and these would be valuable). In contrast, when proving the new estimates of Theorem
1
we make full use of the
L^{3}
control assumption.

The following natural conjecture is natural.

Conjecture 1
.

If
A
has control
\kappa
then, for any
\epsilon>0
,

\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert\gg_{\epsilon}\kappa^{-1+\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert

and

\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert\gg_{\epsilon}\kappa^{-1+\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert.

On the other hand, the naive conjecture that
E(A)\ll_{\epsilon}\kappa^{1-\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}
is too much to hope for – the best that one can hope for here is

E(A)\lesssim\kappa^{0.7549\cdots}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}.

This obstacle arises from the connection to the Balog-Szemerédi-Gowers theorem, as we will see in Section
3.3
. It is unclear whether this is the correct exponent, and more constructions of sets with good control and large additive energy would be extremely valuable.

We record here the following convenient lemma, which via a standard ‘layer-cake’ argument shows the control hypothesis yields more a general bound, where
1_{B}
can be replaced by arbitrary functions. (Of course the exponent of
100
in the second summand should not be taken too seriously.)

Lemma 2
.

If
A
has control
\kappa
then, for any
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
,

\left\lVert 1_{A}\ast f\right\rVert_{3}\lesssim_{\kappa}\kappa^{1/3}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2/3}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{3/2}+\kappa^{100}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{1}^{1/3}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2/3}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
f:G\to[0,1]
. Using the trivial inequalities

\left\lVert 1_{A}\ast f\right\rVert_{3}\leqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{3}\leqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{1}^{1/3}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2/3}

the contribution from where
0<f(x)\leqslant\kappa^{200}
(say) can be bounded above by the second summand on the right, and hence we may assume that
f(x)\geqslant\kappa^{200}
on its support.

Let
B_{i}=\{x:f(x)\in(2^{-i-1},2^{-i}]\}
. By the triangle inequality

\left\lVert 1_{A}\ast f\right\rVert_{3}\leqslant\sum_{i}\left\lVert 1_{A}\ast(f1_{B_{i}})\right\rVert_{3}\ll\sum_{i}2^{-i}\left\lVert 1_{A}\ast 1_{B_{i}}\right\rVert_{3}.

Using the control hypothesis we deduce that

\left\lVert 1_{A}\ast f\right\rVert_{3}\ll\kappa^{1/3}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2/3}\sum_{i}2^{-i}\left\lvert B_{i}\right\rvert^{2/3}.

Finally, since the sum here is only over
\ll\log(1/\kappa)
many dyadic scales,

\sum_{i}2^{-i}\left\lvert B_{i}\right\rvert^{2/3}\lesssim_{\kappa}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{3/2}.

∎

We also note the following useful additivity property of control (already observed by Shakan
[
19
, Lemma 2.4]
).

Lemma 3
.

If
A_{1},\ldots,A_{t}
are disjoint sets, each with control
\kappa_{i}
, then
\sqcup A_{i}
has control at most
\sum_{i}\kappa_{i}
.

Proof.

Let
B
be any finite set and
A^{\prime}=\sqcup A_{i}
. By the triangle inequality and Hölder’s inequality

\displaystyle\left\lVert 1_{A^{\prime}}\ast 1_{B}\right\rVert_{3}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i}\left\lVert 1_{A_{i}}\ast 1_{B}\right\rVert_{3}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\sum_{i}\kappa_{i}^{1/3}\left\lvert A_{i}\right\rvert^{2/3}\right)\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2/3}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\sum_{i}\kappa_{i}\right)^{1/3}\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert^{2/3}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2/3}.

∎

3.
Applications of control

3.1.
Application 1: Convex sets

A finite set of reals
A=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{n}\}
is convex if the sequence of consecutive differences
a_{i+1}-a_{i}
is strictly increasing. The classic example is
\{n^{2}:1\leqslant n\leqslant N\}
.

The connection with control is that, for a convex set, an application of the Szemerédi-Trotter theorem shows that
\kappa\lesssim\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1}
. A proof of this fact can be found in a number of places, for example,
[
18
, Lemma 7]
or
[
15
, Lemma 2]
. To keep this paper reasonably self-contained, and to illustrate the connection between the Szemerédi-Trotter incidence theorem and control, we include the proof here. We will use the following incidence bound of Szemerédi and Trotter
[
25
]
.

Theorem 10
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a finite set of points and
L
be a finite set of continuous plane curves, such that any two curves in
L
intersect at most once. The number of incidences between
P
and
L
is

I(P,L)\ll\left\lvert P\right\rvert^{2/3}\left\lvert L\right\rvert^{2/3}+\left\lvert P\right\rvert+\left\lvert L\right\rvert.

This is all that is required to prove strong control for convex sets. We take this opportunity to prove two other bounds on the control of
f(A)
where
A
is arbitrary and
f
is a convex function, which are useful in other applications.

Lemma 4
.

If
A\subset\mathbb{R}
is a finite convex set then
A
has control
\kappa\lesssim\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1}
.

In general, if
X\subset\mathbb{R}
is any finite set and
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a convex function then, for any finite set
Y
,
A=f(X)
has control

\lesssim\frac{\left\lvert X+Y\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert X\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert Y\right\rvert}.

Similarly, if
X
is such that
1_{Y}\ast 1_{Z}(x)\geqslant T
for all
x\in X
then
A=f(X)
has control

\lesssim\frac{1}{T\left\lvert A\right\rvert}\left(\frac{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert Z\right\rvert^{2}}{T^{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert}+\left\lvert Z\right\rvert+\left\lvert Y\right\rvert\right).

Proof.

The first claim follows from the second with
X=Y=\{1,\ldots,N\}
and
f
a suitable continuous convex function such that
A=\{f(n):1\leqslant n\leqslant N\}
.

To prove the second claim, we let
L
be the set of curves of the shape

(x,f(x))+(y,b)

for
y\in Y
and
b\in B
, so that
\left\lvert L\right\rvert=\left\lvert Y\right\rvert\left\lvert B\right\rvert
. The convexity of
f
implies that these have the property that any two curves in
L
intersect at most once. Let
C
be an arbitrary set and consider the point set
P=(X+Y)\times C
. Each solution to
a+b=c
gives at least
\left\lvert Y\right\rvert
distinct incidences between
P
and
L
, since if
a=f(x)
we can take any
y\in Y
and the point

(x+y,f(x)+b)\in P

lies on one of the curves in
L
. It follows by Theorem
10
that

\left\lvert Y\right\rvert\langle 1_{A}\ast 1_{B},1_{C}\rangle\leqslant I(P,L)\ll\left\lvert X+Y\right\rvert^{2/3}\left\lvert Y\right\rvert^{2/3}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2/3}\left\lvert C\right\rvert^{2/3}+\left\lvert Y\right\rvert\left\lvert B\right\rvert+\left\lvert X+Y\right\rvert\left\lvert C\right\rvert,

whence

\langle 1_{A}\ast 1_{B},1_{C}\rangle\ll\frac{\left\lvert X+Y\right\rvert^{2/3}}{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert^{1/3}}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2/3}\left\lvert C\right\rvert^{2/3}+\left\lvert B\right\rvert+\frac{\left\lvert X+Y\right\rvert}{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert}\left\lvert C\right\rvert.

We now let
C_{i}=\{x:1_{A}\ast 1_{B}(x)\in[2^{i},2^{i+1})\}
for
i\geqslant 0
, so that

\displaystyle\sum_{x}1_{A}\ast 1_{B}(x)^{3}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{i\geqslant 0}2^{2i}\langle 1_{A}\ast 1_{B},1_{C_{i}}\rangle

\displaystyle\ll\frac{\left\lvert X+Y\right\rvert^{2/3}}{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert^{1/3}}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2/3}\sum_{i\geqslant 0}2^{2i}\left\lvert C_{i}\right\rvert^{2/3}+\left\lvert B\right\rvert\sum_{i}2^{2i}+\frac{\left\lvert X+Y\right\rvert}{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert}\sum_{i}2^{2i}\left\lvert C_{i}\right\rvert.

Since
1_{A}\ast 1_{B}(x)\leqslant\min(\left\lvert A\right\rvert,\left\lvert B\right\rvert)
for all
x
the sum over
i
is restricted to where
2^{i}\ll\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{1/2}
, and so the second summand is at most
\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}
, and the third summand is at most

\frac{\left\lvert X+Y\right\rvert}{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert}\sum_{x}1_{A}\ast 1_{B}(x)^{2}\leqslant\frac{\left\lvert X+Y\right\rvert}{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}.

Finally, the first summand is at most

\lesssim_{\left\lvert A\right\rvert}\frac{\left\lvert X+Y\right\rvert^{2/3}}{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert^{1/3}}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2/3}\left(\sum_{x}1_{A}\ast 1_{B}(x)^{3}\right)^{2/3}.

Rearranging implies

\sum_{x}1_{A}\ast 1_{B}(x)^{3}\lesssim_{\left\lvert A\right\rvert}\left(\frac{\left\lvert X+Y\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert X\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert Y\right\rvert}+\frac{\left\lvert X+Y\right\rvert}{\left\lvert X\right\rvert\left\lvert Y\right\rvert}\right)\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2},

which yields the required control bound, since trivially
\left\lvert X+Y\right\rvert\geqslant\left\lvert X\right\rvert
.

The second bound is proved with a similar argument, this time with
L
as the set of curves of the shape

y\mapsto(y,f(y+z)+b)

for
z\in Z
and
b\in B
and
P
as the point set
Y\times C
. Every solution to
a+b=c
gives rise to
T
incidences between
P
and
L
, since if
a=f(x)
and
x=y+z
with
y\in Y
and
z\in Z
then the point

(y,f(y+z)+b)\in P

lies on one of the curves in
L
. It follows that

T\langle 1_{A}\ast 1_{B},1_{C}\rangle\ll\left\lvert Y\right\rvert^{2/3}\left\lvert Z\right\rvert^{2/3}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2/3}\left\lvert C\right\rvert^{2/3}+\left\lvert Z\right\rvert\left\lvert B\right\rvert+\left\lvert Y\right\rvert\left\lvert C\right\rvert,

whence, arguing as above,

\sum 1_{A}\ast 1_{B}(x)^{3}\lesssim\left(\frac{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert Z\right\rvert^{2}}{T^{3}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}}+\frac{\left\lvert Z\right\rvert}{T\left\lvert A\right\rvert}+\frac{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert}{T\left\lvert A\right\rvert}\right)\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}.

∎

Theorem
2
follows immediately from Lemma
4
and Theorem
1
. Theorem
3
requires some preparation: we will prove a useful ‘decomposition’ result that states that, given a convex
f
, any finite set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
can be decomposed into large subsets
X
and
Y
such that the control of
f(X)
and the control of
Y
is bounded:

\kappa_{f(X)}\kappa_{Y}\lesssim\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1}.

This is a generalisation of a sum-product decomposition of Shakan
[
19
, Theorem 1.10]
, which corresponds to the special case when
f(x)=\log x
. It is also similar to (although stronger than)
[
24
, Corollary 5]
of Stevens and Warren.

Theorem 11
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}
be a finite set and
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous convex function. There exist
X,Y\subset A
such that
X\cup Y=A
and
\left\lvert X\right\rvert,\left\lvert Y\right\rvert\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert/2
and
f(X)
has control
\kappa_{f(X)}
and
Y
has control
\kappa_{Y}
, where

\kappa_{f(X)}\kappa_{Y}\lesssim_{\left\lvert A\right\rvert}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1}.

Proof.

Following the strategy of Shakan
[
19
]
, we will first establish that, for any finite
T\subset\mathbb{R}
with control
\kappa_{T}
, there exists some
T^{\prime}\subseteq T
such that
\left\lvert T^{\prime}\right\rvert\gtrsim\kappa_{T}\left\lvert T\right\rvert
and
f(T^{\prime})
has control
\kappa_{f(T^{\prime})}
, where

\kappa_{T}\kappa_{f(T^{\prime})}\lesssim\left\lvert T^{\prime}\right\rvert/\left\lvert T\right\rvert^{2}.

Indeed, let
B
be such that

\sum 1_{T}\ast 1_{B}(x)^{3}=\kappa_{T}\left\lvert T\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}.

By dyadic pigeonholing there exists some
\delta\gg\kappa_{T}
and
S
such that
1_{T}\ast 1_{B}(x)\in[\delta,2\delta)\left\lvert T\right\rvert
for all
x\in S
and
\left\lvert S\right\rvert\gtrsim\kappa_{T}\delta^{-3}\left\lvert T\right\rvert^{-1}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}
, so that

\langle 1_{T},1_{S}\circ 1_{B}\rangle=\langle 1_{S},1_{T}\ast 1_{B}\rangle\geqslant\delta\left\lvert T\right\rvert\left\lvert S\right\rvert.

Note that this implies that
\left\lvert B\right\rvert\geqslant\delta\left\lvert T\right\rvert
. By another application of dyadic pigeonholing there exists
T^{\prime}\subseteq T
and
\eta\gg\delta
such that
1_{S}\circ 1_{B}(x)\in[\eta,2\eta)\left\lvert S\right\rvert
for all
x\in T^{\prime}
and
\left\lvert T^{\prime}\right\rvert\gtrsim\eta^{-1}\delta\left\lvert T\right\rvert
. By Lemma
4

f(T^{\prime})
has control

\displaystyle\lesssim(\eta\left\lvert S\right\rvert\left\lvert T^{\prime}\right\rvert)^{-1}\left(\eta^{-2}\left\lvert T^{\prime}\right\rvert^{-1}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert B\right\rvert+\left\lvert S\right\rvert\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\kappa_{T}^{-1}\left\lvert T^{\prime}\right\rvert/\left\lvert T\right\rvert^{2}+\kappa_{T}^{-1}\delta^{2}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{-1}+\delta^{-1}\left\lvert T\right\rvert^{-1}.

Since
\left\lvert B\right\rvert\geqslant\delta\left\lvert T\right\rvert
and
\left\lvert T^{\prime}\right\rvert\gtrsim\delta\left\lvert T\right\rvert
the first term dominates the second. Furthermore, since
\sum_{x}1_{S}\circ 1_{B}(x)^{2}\leqslant\left\lvert S\right\rvert\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}
,

\eta\delta\left\lvert T\right\rvert\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{2}\lesssim\eta^{2}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert T^{\prime}\right\rvert\leqslant\left\lvert S\right\rvert\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}

whence
\kappa_{T}\eta\lesssim\delta^{2}
, and so the first term dominates the third, which proves the claim.

We may now proceed as Shakan does: we define a sequence of sets
X_{i}\subseteq A
by setting
X_{0}=\emptyset
and in general construct
X_{i+1}
, provided
X_{i}\neq A
, by applying the claim with
T=A\backslash X_{i}
and setting
X_{i+1}=X_{i}\cup T^{\prime}
. Note that since
T^{\prime}
is disjoint from
X_{i}
and non-empty the size of the
X_{i}
must grow until
X_{i}=A
. Let
i
be minimal such that
\left\lvert X_{i}\right\rvert\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert/2
, and let
X=X_{i}
and
Y=A\backslash X_{i-1}
. By minimality
\left\lvert Y\right\rvert\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert/2
.

We observe that trivially, by definition of control,
\left\lvert U\right\rvert^{2}\kappa_{U}
is monotonic, and so for any
1\leqslant j<i

\left\lvert Y\right\rvert^{2}\kappa_{Y}\kappa_{f(X_{j+1}\backslash X_{j})}\lesssim\left\lvert A\backslash X_{j}\right\rvert^{2}\kappa_{A\backslash X_{j}}\kappa_{f(X_{j+1}\backslash X_{j})}\lesssim\left\lvert X_{j+1}\right\rvert-\left\lvert X_{j}\right\rvert.

Summing this inequality over
1\leqslant j<i
and using Lemma
3
we deduce that

\left\lvert Y\right\rvert^{2}\kappa_{Y}\kappa_{f(X)}\lesssim\left\lvert X\right\rvert

as required.
∎

With this decomposition in hand Theorem
3
now follows swiftly using the bounds in Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Let
X,Y
be as in the conclusion of Theorem
11
. Let
K_{1}=\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert/\left\lvert A\right\rvert
and
K_{2}=\left\lvert f(A)+f(A)\right\rvert/\left\lvert A\right\rvert
. Since clearly
\left\lvert f(X)+f(X)\right\rvert\ll K_{2}\left\lvert X\right\rvert
and
\left\lvert Y+Y\right\rvert\ll K_{1}\left\lvert Y\right\rvert
, we have, by Theorem
1
,

(K_{1}K_{2})^{19}\gtrsim(\kappa_{f(X)}\kappa_{Y})^{-11}\gtrsim\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{11},

and hence
K_{1}K_{2}\gtrsim\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{11/19}
. The version for difference sets is proved in an identical way.
∎

In addition to the consequences recorded in Theorems
2
and
3
we note the following conditional result. In particular this highlights that, in some sense, a complete answer to the additive growth of convex sets should follow from control alone.

Theorem 12
.

Assume Conjecture
1
. If
A\subset\mathbb{R}
is a convex set then, for any
\epsilon>0
,

\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert\gg_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2-\epsilon},

and similarly for
\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert
. Furthermore, if
A\subset\mathbb{R}
is any finite set and
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous convex function then

\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert\left\lvert f(A)+f(A)\right\rvert\gg_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3-\epsilon}

and similarly for difference sets.

3.2.
Application 2: Sum-product

For this application, we note that the definition of control only involves a single abelian group operation, and hence makes sense for both addition and multiplication. We will therefore write
\kappa^{+}
and
\kappa^{\times}
for the additive and multiplicative control of a set.

The link between the sum-product phenomenon and control was first explored by Konyagin and Shkredov
[
7
]
, and has been used in most subsequent papers on the sum-product problem. One particularly clear demonstration of this link was given by Shakan
[
19
, Theorem 1.10]
. As noted in the previous section, this is a special case of our general decomposition result Theorem
11
.

Theorem 13

(Shakan)
.

For any finite
A\subset\mathbb{R}
there exists some
X,Y\subseteq A
such that
\left\lvert X\right\rvert\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert/2
and
\left\lvert Y\right\rvert\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert/2
and

\kappa^{+}(X)\kappa^{\times}(Y)\lesssim\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1}.

In particular, there exists some
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
with
\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert
and either additive or multiplicative control
\lesssim\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1/2}
.

In particular, Conjecture
1
when coupled with this result of Shakan immediately yields a sum-product exponent of
3/2
.

Corollary 1
.

Assuming Conjecture
1
, for any finite
A\subset\mathbb{R}
,

\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert\left\lvert AA\right\rvert\gtrsim\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}.

In particular

\max(\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert,\left\lvert AA\right\rvert)\gtrsim\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3/2}.

It may be of interest to compare Corollary
1
to the bound of Solymosi
[
22
]
, that

\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert AA\right\rvert\gtrsim\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}.

Perhaps the easiest link between sum-product problems and control arises from the Szemerédi-Trotter theorem, as noted by Konyagin and Shkredov
[
7
]
, which implies that if
A
has small product set then it must have good additive control. While we will not use this particular relationship in this paper, we include it for comparison.

Lemma 5
.

If
A\subset\mathbb{R}
is a finite set with
\left\lvert AA\right\rvert\leqslant K\left\lvert A\right\rvert
(or
\left\lvert A/A\right\rvert\leqslant K\left\lvert A\right\rvert
) then
A
has (additive) control

\ll K^{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1}.

Proof.

This is a single application of the Szemerédi-Trotter theorem, similar to the proof of Lemma
4
, and can be found in a number of papers – for example, one can apply Lemma 2 of
[
15
]
with
\Pi_{1}=A^{-1}
and
\Pi_{2}=AA
and
T=\left\lvert A\right\rvert
.
∎

Rudnev and Stevens
[
15
]
, building on ideas of Konyagin and Shkredov
[
7
]
, gave a more intricate argument, which in most cases is quantitatively superior to Lemma
5
. We give a variant of this argument below.

Lemma 6
.

If
A\subset\mathbb{R}
is a finite set with
\max(\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert,\left\lvert AA\right\rvert)\leqslant K\left\lvert A\right\rvert
then there is a subset
A^{\prime}\subseteq A

such that
A^{\prime}
has (additive) control

\lesssim\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert K^{83/2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-31/2}.

Proof.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have
E^{\times}(A)\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}/K
. Let
r_{A/A}(x)
count the number of representations of
x
as
x=a/b
with
a,b\in A
. By the dyadic pigeonhole principle there exists some
\delta>0
such that
r_{A/A}(x)\in[\delta,2\delta)\left\lvert A\right\rvert
for all
x\in S
, say, and
\left\lvert S\right\rvert\gtrsim\delta^{-2}K^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
. By the argument in the proof of Theorem 1 of
[
15
]
there exists some
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
such that

\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\gg\delta\left\lvert S\right\rvert

such that, for every
x\in A^{\prime}
,

r_{AA/AA}(x)\gtrsim K^{-12}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{-1/2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{6},

where
r_{AA/AA}(x)
counts the number of representations of
x=a/b
with
a,b\in AA
. It follows by Lemma 2 of
[
15
]
that, for any set
B
,

\displaystyle\sum 1_{A^{\prime}}\circ 1_{B}(x)^{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim K^{4}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}\left(K^{-12}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{-1/2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{6}\right)^{-3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\delta^{-3}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{-3/2}K^{40}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-14}\right)\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert^{3}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(K^{83/2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-31/2}\right)\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert^{3}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2},

where in the final inequality we have used the fact that
\left\lvert S\right\rvert\gtrsim\delta^{-2}K^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
.
∎

In
[
15
]
Lemma
6
was (implicitly) invoked, along with the lower bound
\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\gg K^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
. Here we note that, using Lemma
3
, the previous lemma can be improved via iteration, essentially allowing one to assume
\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert
. This observation alone yields our improved sum-product exponent (in particular does not use the improvements of Theorem

).

Lemma 7
.

If
A\subset\mathbb{R}
is a finite set with
\max(\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert,\left\lvert AA\right\rvert)\leqslant K\left\lvert A\right\rvert
then there is a subset
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
with
\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert/2
such that
A^{\prime}
has (additive) control

\lesssim K^{83/2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-29/2}.

Proof.

We repeatedly apply Lemma
6
, removing
\cup A_{i}
from
A
each time, until the union has size at least
\left\lvert A\right\rvert/2
.
∎

With Lemma
7
in hand it is now a simple matter to convert results on control (as in Theorem

) into improved bounds for the sum-product exponent.

Theorem 14
.

If
a>0
is such that any set
A
with control
\kappa
has
\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert\gg\kappa^{-a}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
then, for any finite

,

\max(\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert,\left\lvert AA\right\rvert)\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{1+c}

where

Similarly, if any set
A
with control
\kappa
has
\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert\gg\kappa^{-a}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
then, for any finite
A\subset\mathbb{R}
,

\max\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert,\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert,\left\lvert AA\right\rvert)\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{1+c}.

We note that the former bound for

(which arises from the Shakan decomposition) is superior to the second bound (which arises from the Rudnev-Stevens modifiation of the Konyagin-Shkredov argument) once
a\geqslant 56/83\approx 0.675
.

Theorem
4
follows immediately via Theorem
1
, taking
a=11/19-\epsilon
and
a=2506/4175-\epsilon
respectively.

Proof.

For the bound
c\geqslant a/2
we apply Shakan’s decomposition result Theorem
13
. On the other hand, if
\max(\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert,\left\lvert AA\right\rvert)=K\left\lvert A\right\rvert
then by Lemma

we have

\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert\gtrsim\left(K^{83/2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-29/2}\right)^{-a}\left\lvert A\right\rvert,

and rearranging yields the result. The bound involving
\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert
is proved in an identical fashion.
∎

To prove Theorem

we simply need to repeat the argument of Xue
[

]
with the new bounds of Theorem

; we sketch the details below.

Proof of Theorem
5
.

By
[

, Theorem 1.10]
there exist
W,Z\subseteq A
with
\left\lvert W\right\rvert,\left\lvert Z\right\rvert\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert
such that

\left\lVert 1_{W}\circ 1_{W}\right\rVert_{3}^{12}E_{\times}(Z)^{3}\lesssim\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{22}.

Arguing as in the proof of
[

, Lemma 4.4]
, there exist
X,Y\subseteq W
such that
\left\lvert X\right\rvert,\left\lvert Y\right\rvert\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert
and

has additive control
\kappa
and

has multiplicative control

, where

and

E_{+}(X)^{13/2}\lesssim\kappa^{1/2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{15/2}\left\lVert 1_{W}\circ 1_{W}\right\rVert_{3}^{9},

whence

E_{+}(X)^{13/2}\lesssim\lambda^{-1/2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{7}\left\lVert 1_{W}\circ 1_{W}\right\rVert_{3}^{9}.

Since
E_{\times}(Y)\ll_{\epsilon}\lambda^{27/50-\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}
we have

\lambda^{-1/2}\ll_{\epsilon}E_{\times}(Y)^{-25/27+\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{25/9+\epsilon}

whence

It follows that

E_{+}(X)^{13/2}E_{\times}(Y)^{25/27-\epsilon}E_{\times}(Z)^{9/4}\ll_{\epsilon}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{473/18+\epsilon},

and the claim follows.
∎

Similarly, the improved estimates of Theorem

.

3.3.
Application 3: New bounds for the Balog-Szemerédi-Gowers theorem

The Balog-Szemerédi-Gowers theorem, part of the standard toolkit of additive combinatorics, says that any set with large additive energy must contain a large subset with small sumset. (The converse direction, that a small sumset implies a large additive energy, is an immediate consequence of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.) This was first proved qualitatively by Balog and Szemerédi
[
1
]
, but reproved with polynomial bounds by Gowers
[
5
]
.

There is a strong connection between
\kappa
and the bounds available for the Balog-Szemerédi-Gowers theorem. In particular we have the following.

Theorem 15
.

If there exists
B
such that
\left\lVert 1_{A}\ast 1_{B}\right\rVert_{3}\geqslant\kappa^{1/3}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2/3}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2/3}
then then there exists
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
with
\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\gtrsim_{\kappa}\kappa^{2/3}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
such that

\left\lvert A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}\right\rvert\lesssim_{\kappa}\kappa^{-2}\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert.

Corollary 2
.

If
A
having control
\kappa
implies
E(A)\ll\kappa^{c}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}
then the following holds. If
E(A)\geqslant K^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}
then there exists
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
with
\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\gg K^{-2/3c}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
such that

\left\lvert A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}\right\rvert\ll K^{2/c}\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert.

Theorem
8
follows immediately using Theorem
1
. Note that Corollary
3
implies that, when combined with the sphere construction of Mazur
[
8
]
discussed previously, in any inequality of the shape

E(A)\ll\kappa^{c}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}

(under the assumption that
A
has control
\kappa
) one could not do better than

c=\frac{\log(27/16)}{\log 2}\approx 0.7549.

Note that using the trivial bound
E(A)\leqslant\kappa^{1/2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}
already yields the fact that if
E(A)\geqslant K^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}
then there is
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
with
\left\lvert A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}\right\rvert\ll K^{4}\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert
, which was the previous best exponent known, proved by Schoen
[
17
]
. Indeed, our proof of Theorem
15
is along similar lines to the argument of Schoen, particularly that of
[
17
, Theorem 1.2]
, the novelty being that we allow for an arbitrary set
B
in exploiting a lower bound for
\left\lVert 1_{A}\ast 1_{B}\right\rVert_{3}
, rather than fixing
B=A
.

In the remainder of this section we prove Theorem
15
. We begin with a lemma that is a variant of
[
17
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 8
.

If
A,B,C
are finite sets such that
1_{A}\ast 1_{B}(x)\geqslant\eta\left\lvert A\right\rvert
for all
x\in C
then there exists some
X\subseteq A
such that
\left\lvert X\right\rvert\geqslant\eta\left\lvert A\right\rvert
and, for all but at most
\epsilon\left\lvert X\right\rvert^{2}
many pairs
(a,b)\in X^{2}
,

1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(a-b)>\epsilon\eta^{2}\frac{\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert C\right\rvert}{\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}}\left\lvert A\right\rvert.

Proof.

If
G\subseteq B^{2}
is the set of
(a,b)
such that
1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(a-b)\leqslant\epsilon\eta^{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert C\right\rvert/\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}
then (using the trivial bound
\left\lvert G\right\rvert\leqslant\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}
)

\sum_{(a,b)\in G}\sum_{x\in C}1_{A}(x-a)1_{A}(x-b)\leqslant\epsilon\eta^{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert C\right\rvert.

If we let
X_{x}=B\cap(x-A)
then
\left\lvert X_{x}\right\rvert=1_{A}\ast 1_{B}(x)
and so

\sum_{x\in C}\lvert X_{x}^{2}\cap G\rvert=\sum_{(a,b)\in G}\sum_{x\in C}1_{A}(x-a)1_{A}(x-b)\leqslant\epsilon\eta^{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert C\right\rvert\leqslant\epsilon\sum_{x\in C}\left\lvert X_{x}\right\rvert^{2}.

In particular there exists some
X=X_{x}
such that
\left\lvert X\right\rvert\geqslant\eta\left\lvert A\right\rvert
and at most
\epsilon\left\lvert X\right\rvert^{2}
many pairs
(a,b)\in X^{2}
are in
G
, which is the conclusion (after translating
X
to be a subset of
A
).
∎

Corollary 3
.

If
A,B,C
are such that
1_{A}\ast 1_{B}(x)\geqslant\eta\left\lvert A\right\rvert
for all
x\in C
then there exists some
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
of size
\gg\eta\left\lvert A\right\rvert
such that

\left\lvert A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}\right\rvert\ll\eta^{-6}\frac{\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{4}}{\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert C\right\rvert^{2}}\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert.

Proof.

We let
X
and
G
be the set and graph provided by Lemma
8
with
\epsilon=1/8
, and let
A^{\prime}\subseteq X
be those elements with degree at least
\frac{3}{4}\left\lvert X\right\rvert
in
G
. We know that
G
has at least
\frac{7}{8}\left\lvert X\right\rvert^{2}
many edges, and hence
\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\geqslant 2^{-4}\eta\left\lvert A\right\rvert
. Furthermore, for any
a-b\in A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}
there exist
\gg\left\lvert X\right\rvert
many
c\in X
such that
(a,c)
and
(b,c)
are edges in
G
. This means that both
a-c
and
b-c
have
\gg\eta^{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert C\right\rvert\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{-2}
representations as
x-y
where
x,y\in A
. Using the identity

a-b=(a-c)-(b-c)

we deduce that each element of
A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}
has
\gg\eta^{5}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5}\left\lvert C\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{-4}
representations
x_{1}+x_{2}-x_{3}-x_{4}
where
x_{i}\in A
, and hence

\left\lvert A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}\right\rvert\eta^{5}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5}\left\lvert C\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{-4}\ll\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}

whence

\left\lvert A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}\right\rvert\ll\eta^{-5}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{4}\left\lvert C\right\rvert^{-2}\ll\eta^{-6}\frac{\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{4}}{\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert C\right\rvert^{2}}\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert.

∎

We may now prove Theorem
15
as a consequence of Corollary
3
.

Proof of Theorem
15
.

Let
B
be such that

\sum_{x}1_{A}\ast 1_{B}(x)^{3}=\kappa\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}.

By dyadic pigeonholing there exists some
\eta\gg\kappa
and
C
such that
1_{A}\ast 1_{B}(x)\geqslant\eta\left\lvert A\right\rvert
for all
x\in C
and
\left\lvert C\right\rvert\gtrsim_{\kappa}\kappa\eta^{-3}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}
. On one hand, applying Corollary
3
immediately yields some
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
of size
\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\gg\eta\left\lvert A\right\rvert
such that

\left\lvert A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}\right\rvert\ll\eta^{-6}\frac{\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{4}}{\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert C\right\rvert^{2}}\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\lesssim\kappa^{-2}\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert.

On the other, we know that by definition

\langle 1_{B},1_{C}\circ 1_{A}\rangle=\langle 1_{A}\ast 1_{B},1_{C}\rangle\geqslant\eta\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert C\right\rvert,

and hence by dyadic pigeonholing there exists some
\rho\gg\eta\left\lvert C\right\rvert/\left\lvert B\right\rvert
such that
1_{A}\circ 1_{C}(x)\geqslant\rho\left\lvert A\right\rvert
for all
x\in B^{\prime}
, where
B^{\prime}\subseteq-B
has size
\left\lvert B^{\prime}\right\rvert\gtrsim\eta\rho^{-1}\left\lvert C\right\rvert
.

By another application of Corollary
3
we find some
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
of size

\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\gg\rho\left\lvert A\right\rvert\gg\eta\left\lvert C\right\rvert\left\lvert A\right\rvert/\left\lvert B\right\rvert\gtrsim\kappa\eta^{-2}\left\lvert B\right\rvert

such that

\left\lvert A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}\right\rvert\ll\rho^{-6}\frac{\left\lvert C\right\rvert^{4}}{\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert B^{\prime}\right\rvert^{2}}\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\lesssim\eta^{-6}\frac{\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{4}}{\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert C\right\rvert^{2}}\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\lesssim\kappa^{-2}\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert.

In either case, we have found some
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
such that
\left\lvert A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}\right\rvert\lesssim\kappa^{-2}\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert
and

\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\gtrsim\max\left(\eta\left\lvert A\right\rvert,\kappa\eta^{-2}\left\lvert B\right\rvert\right).

We note that the trivial bound

\sum 1_{A}\ast 1_{B}(x)^{3}\leqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{3}

implies
\left\lvert B\right\rvert\geqslant\kappa\left\lvert A\right\rvert
, and hence the maximum above is at least
\kappa^{2/3}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
as claimed.
∎

This concludes our discussion of the applications of control. The remainder of this paper will focus on proving Theorem
1
, deducing good quantitative bounds on other measures of additive structure from a control hypothesis.

4.
Applications of bounding
\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}

All of the threshold-breaking bounds on
\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert
,
\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert
, and
E(A)
(both in this paper and the previous literature) arise from using the control hypothesis to relate these quantities to
\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}
, where
S
is some ‘symmetry set’; that is, a set of the form
\{x:1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)\geqslant\delta\left\lvert A\right\rvert\}
for some
\delta>0
.

In this section we make these connections explicit. For reference, it may be useful to note that by Hölder’s inequality and the control hypothesis

(1)

\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}\leqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3}^{1/2}\leqslant\kappa^{1/6}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/6}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{5/6}.

The threshold-breaking bounds in the previous literature all arise from using this ‘trivial’ bound combined with (something equivalent to) the arguments in this section. In the following section we will prove improved bounds for
\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}
in certain regimes, which is the source of our improvements for
\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert
and
E(A)
. We have not been able to find a bound which offers an improvement in the range relevant for improving the bounds for
\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert
.

We first present the simplest application, which is to bound the size of
A-A
. This is essentially the argument of Schoen and Shkredov
[
18
]
, and indeed one can check that using (
1
) in conjunction with the following lemma yields the Schoen-Shkredov bound of
\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert\gg\kappa^{-3/5}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
.

Lemma 9
.

If
A
has control
\kappa
and
\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert=K\left\lvert A\right\rvert
then

\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/3}\ll\kappa^{4/3}K^{5/3}\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2},

where

S=\{x:1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)\geqslant(2K)^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\}.

We remark that a similar argument proves the bound

\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}\right\rVert_{5/4}^{5}\lesssim_{K}\kappa^{4/3}K^{2/3}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{22/3}\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2},

which implies the previous inequality (up to logarithmic factors) via Hölder’s inequality. This stronger form does not seem to produce any quantitative improvement, however.

Proof.

Let
S=\{x:1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)\geqslant(2K)^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\}
, so that

\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\ll\langle 1_{A}\circ 1_{A},1_{S}\rangle=\langle 1_{A},1_{A}\ast 1_{S}\rangle.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}\ll\langle 1_{A},(1_{A}\ast 1_{S})^{2}\rangle=\sum_{x,y\in S}\sum_{a\in A}1_{A}(a-x)1_{A}(a-y).

The innermost sum ensures that this sum is restricted to those
x,y\in S
such that
x-y\in A-A
, and hence by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{6}\ll\langle 1_{S}\circ 1_{S},1_{A-A}\rangle\sum_{x,y\in S}\left(\sum_{a\in A}1_{A}(a-x)1_{A}(a-y)\right)^{2}.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{x,y\in S}\left(\sum_{a\in A}1_{A}(a-x)1_{A}(a-y)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a_{1},a_{2}\in A}\left(\sum_{x\in S}1_{A}(a_{1}-x)1_{A}(a_{2}-x)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{z}1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(z)^{3}

\displaystyle\leqslant\kappa\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4},

and hence

\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\ll\kappa\langle 1_{S}\circ 1_{S},1_{A-A}\rangle\ll\kappa K\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1}\langle 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}\circ 1_{S},1_{A-A}\rangle.

By Hölder’s inequality

\langle 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}\circ 1_{S},1_{A-A}\rangle=\langle 1_{A}\ast 1_{A-A},1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\rangle\leqslant\left\lVert 1_{A}\ast 1_{A-A}\right\rVert_{3}\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2},

and the conclusion follows from
\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{A-A}\right\rVert_{3}\leqslant\kappa^{1/3}K^{2/3}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4/3}
.
∎

It is more challenging to bound
\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert
, essentially because the use of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality naturally creates sums with restrictions to the symmetric set
A-A
. To produce
A+A
instead requires some trickery. The following argument is a variant of that of Rudnev and Stevens
[
15
]
. Again, using this lemma in conjunction with (
1
) (and the bounds
\tau\geqslant K^{-1}
and
\left\lvert S\right\rvert\leqslant\delta^{-2}\tau\left\lvert A\right\rvert
) produces the Rudnev-Stevens bound
\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert\gtrsim_{\kappa}\kappa^{-11/19}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
. Unlike the other two applications in this section, we have not found a bound for
\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}
which improves upon (
1
) in the range relevant for this application.

Lemma 10
.

Suppose
A
has control
\kappa
and
E(A)=\tau\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}
. If
\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert=K\left\lvert A\right\rvert
(where
K\leqslant\kappa^{-1}
) then there exists some
K^{-1}\ll\delta\lesssim_{\kappa}\kappa\tau^{-1}
such that

\tau^{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/3}\lesssim_{\kappa}\kappa^{4/3}K^{5/3}\delta^{2}\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}.

where

S=\{x:1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)\geqslant\delta\left\lvert A\right\rvert\}.

Proof.

(All logarithmic losses in this proof are relative to
\kappa
.) We note that
E(A)=\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\left\lVert 1_{A}\ast 1_{A}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}/K
. It follows by dyadic pigeonholing that there exists some
\delta\gg\tau\geqslant K^{-1}
such that if

S=\{x:1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)\geqslant\delta\left\lvert A\right\rvert\}

then
\left\lvert S\right\rvert\gtrsim\delta^{-2}\tau\left\lvert A\right\rvert
. The fact that
\sum 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)^{3}\leqslant\kappa\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}
immediately implies
\delta\lesssim\kappa\tau^{-1}
. By definition

\langle 1_{A},1_{A}\ast 1_{S}\rangle=\langle 1_{S},1_{A}\circ 1_{A}\rangle\geqslant\delta\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert S\right\rvert.

It follows that there exists some
A_{1}\subseteq A
such that
1_{A}\ast 1_{S}(x)\gg\delta\left\lvert S\right\rvert\gtrsim\delta^{-1}\tau\left\lvert A\right\rvert
for all
x\in A^{\prime}
.

If
\left\lvert A_{1}\right\rvert\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert/2
we proceed with the remainder of the proof. Otherwise, we repeat the preceding argument with
A\backslash A_{1}
, noting that we still have
E(A\backslash A_{1})\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}/K
. Repeating this, we produce disjoint
A_{1},\ldots,A_{t}
with associated
\delta_{i}
and
S_{i}
, such that
1_{A}\ast 1_{S_{i}}(x)\gtrsim\delta_{i}^{-1}\tau\left\lvert A\right\rvert
for all
x\in A_{i}
, where
\left\lvert\cup_{i}A_{i}\right\rvert\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert
. By a further dyadic pigeonholing we can find some sub-collection of the
A_{i}
in which the corresponding
\delta_{i}
are all in the same dyadic range, and whose union has size
\gtrsim\left\lvert A\right\rvert
. If we then let
A^{\prime}
be the union of all such
A_{i}
then we have found some
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
such that

(1)

\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\gtrsim\left\lvert A\right\rvert
and

(2)

there exists
\delta\gg\tau
such that if

S=\{x:1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)\geqslant\delta\left\lvert A\right\rvert\}

then for all
x\in A^{\prime}
we have
1_{A}\ast 1_{S}(x)\gtrsim\delta^{-1}\tau\left\lvert A\right\rvert
.

We now let
T=\{x:1_{A^{\prime}}\ast 1_{A}(x)\geqslant(2K)^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\}
, so that

\langle 1_{A^{\prime}},1_{T}\circ 1_{A}\rangle=\langle 1_{A^{\prime}}\ast 1_{A},1_{T}\rangle\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}.

It follows that

\delta^{-1}\tau\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}\lesssim\langle 1_{A^{\prime}},(1_{T}\circ 1_{A})(1_{A}\ast 1_{S})\rangle=\sum_{x\in T}\sum_{y\in S}\sum_{a\in A^{\prime}}1_{A}(x-a)1_{A}(a-y).

We can now proceed as in the proof of Lemma
9
, except that now the sum is restricted to those pairs
x,y
such that
x-y\in A+A
, and hence

\delta^{-2}\tau^{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\lesssim\kappa\langle 1_{T}\circ 1_{S},1_{A+A}\rangle.

We have

\langle 1_{T}\circ 1_{S},1_{A+A}\rangle\ll K\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1}\langle 1_{A}\circ 1_{A+A},1_{S}\circ 1_{A}\rangle\ll\kappa^{1/3}K^{5/3}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{1/3}\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2},

and the claim follows.
∎

Finally, we explain the link between
\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}
and the additive energy
E(A)
. In the work of Shkredov
[
20
]
this is accomplished via a spectral argument, but here we use only elementary methods. We require the following auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 11
.

For any sets
A,B,S

\langle 1_{A}\ast 1_{B},1_{S}\rangle^{8}\leqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{4}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{2}\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}\right\rVert_{3}^{3}\left\lVert 1_{B}\circ 1_{B}\right\rVert_{3}^{2}\left\lVert 1_{S}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3}.

Proof.

We have, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\langle 1_{A}\ast 1_{B},1_{S}\rangle^{2}=\langle 1_{B},1_{S}\circ 1_{A}\rangle^{2}\leqslant\left\lvert B\right\rvert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in A\\
x\in S\end{subarray}}\sum_{y\in S}1_{B}(y-a)1_{A}(x-y+a).

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality once again,

\langle 1_{A}\ast 1_{B},1_{S}\rangle^{4}\leqslant\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert S\right\rvert\sum_{y_{1},y_{2}\in S}\sum_{a\in A}1_{B}(y_{1}-a)1_{A}(y_{2}-a)\sum_{x\in S}1_{A}(x-y_{1}+a)1_{A}(x-y_{2}+a).

The innermost sum is at most
1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(y_{1}-y_{2})
and

\sum_{y_{1},y_{2}\in S}\left(\sum_{a\in A}1_{B}(y_{1}-a)1_{B}(y_{2}-a)\right)^{2}\leqslant\langle 1_{A}\circ 1_{A},(1_{B}\circ 1_{B})^{2}\rangle,

hence

\langle 1_{A}\ast 1_{B},1_{S}\rangle^{8}\leqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{4}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{2}\langle 1_{A}\circ 1_{A},(1_{B}\circ 1_{B})^{2}\rangle\langle 1_{S}\circ 1_{S},(1_{A}\circ 1_{A})^{2}\rangle.

The conclusion now follows from Hölder’s inequality.
∎

Unfortunately, our method for bounding
\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}
in the range relevant for
E(A)
requires several additional technical assumptions. These can be met, which is the content of the following lemma, but it does make the connection harder to parse. On a first reading, the reader should take
A_{1}=A_{2}=A
and
S=S^{\prime}
a
\delta
-level symmetry set of
1_{A}\circ 1_{A}
as before. With this simplification, coupled with (
1
) and the bound
\left\lvert S\right\rvert\leqslant\delta^{-2}\tau\left\lvert A\right\rvert
, the inequality in the following lemma yields the Shkredov
[
20
]
energy bound of
E(A)\lesssim\kappa^{7/13}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}
.

Lemma 12
.

Let
A
have control
\kappa
and
E(A)=\tau\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}
. There exists
\tau\ll\delta\lesssim\kappa\tau^{-1}
and
A_{1},A_{2}\subseteq A
and
S,S^{\prime}
such that, for any
k\geqslant 1

(1)

\delta^{-2}\tau\left\lvert A\right\rvert\lesssim\left\lvert S^{\prime}\right\rvert\lesssim\delta^{-2}\tau\left\lvert A\right\rvert
,

(2)

1_{A_{2}}\circ 1_{A}(x)\gg 2^{-O(k)}\delta\left\lvert A\right\rvert
for all
x\in S
,

(3)

1_{A_{1}}\circ 1_{S^{\prime}}(x)\gg 2^{-O(k)}\delta\left\lvert S\right\rvert
for all
x\in A_{2}
, and

(4)

\left\lVert 1_{A_{1}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}\leqslant(\tau\kappa)^{-O(1/k)}\left\lVert 1_{A_{2}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}
,

and

\tau^{6}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/3}\ll 2^{O(k)}\kappa^{2}\delta^{3}\left\lVert 1_{A_{2}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2},

Proof.

By dyadic pigeonholing there exists some
\delta\gg\tau
such that, if

S^{\prime}=\{x:1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)\geqslant\delta\left\lvert A\right\rvert\},

then
\left\lvert S^{\prime}\right\rvert\gtrsim\delta^{-2}\tau\left\lvert A\right\rvert
(which immediately implies
\delta\lesssim\kappa\tau^{-1}
). Note that
S^{\prime}
is symmetric (so that
f\circ 1_{S^{\prime}}
is the same as
f\ast 1_{S^{\prime}}
).

We claim that there exists a sequence of sets
A_{i}\subseteq A
for all integer
i\geqslant 0
such that

(1)

\langle 1_{S^{\prime}},1_{A_{i}}\circ 1_{A_{i+1}}\rangle\geqslant 2^{-i-1}\delta\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert S^{\prime}\right\rvert
and

(2)

1_{S^{\prime}}\ast 1_{A_{i}}(x)\geqslant 2^{-i-1}\delta\left\lvert S^{\prime}\right\rvert
for all
x\in A_{i+1}
.

The proof is by induction: we begin by noting that

\langle 1_{A},1_{S^{\prime}}\ast 1_{A}\rangle=\langle 1_{S^{\prime}},1_{A}\circ 1_{A}\rangle\geqslant\delta\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert S^{\prime}\right\rvert.

We then set
A_{0}=A
and
A_{1}
to be the set of those
x\in A
such that
1_{S^{\prime}}\ast 1_{A}(x)\geqslant\tfrac{1}{2}\delta\left\lvert S^{\prime}\right\rvert
. In general, with
A_{i-1},A_{i}
defined, so

\langle 1_{A_{i-1}},1_{S^{\prime}}\ast 1_{A_{i}}\rangle\geqslant 2^{-i}\delta\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert S^{\prime}\right\rvert,

we take
A_{i+1}
to be the set of
x\in A_{i-1}
such that
1_{S^{\prime}}\ast 1_{A_{i}}(x)\geqslant 2^{-i-1}\delta\left\lvert S^{\prime}\right\rvert
. This completes the construction; note that this has the property that
A_{i}\subseteq A_{j}
whenever
i\geqslant j
and
i\equiv j\pmod{2}
.

With
k\geqslant 1
as in the assumptions we let
S
be the set of those
x\in S^{\prime}
such that
1_{A_{k}}\circ 1_{A_{k-1}}(x)\geqslant 2^{-k-1}\delta\left\lvert A\right\rvert
, so that

(2)

\langle 1_{S},1_{A}\circ 1_{A}\rangle\geqslant\langle 1_{S},1_{A_{k}}\circ 1_{A_{k-1}}\rangle\gg 2^{-O(k)}\delta\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert S^{\prime}\right\rvert.

Lemma
11
implies

\delta^{8}\left\lvert S^{\prime}\right\rvert^{6}\ll 2^{O(k)}\kappa^{5/3}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{14/3}\left\lVert 1_{S}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3}.

Note that

\left\lVert 1_{A_{i}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}\leqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert S^{\prime}\right\rvert^{2/3}\leqslant(\tau\kappa)^{-O(1)}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/3},

and since (
2
) implies via Hölder’s inequality that
\left\lVert 1_{A_{k}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}\gtrsim 2^{-O(k)}\kappa^{O(1)}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/3}
, the pigeonhole principle implies there exists some
1\leqslant i\leqslant k
such that

\|1_{A_{i-1}}\circ 1_{S}\|_{3/2}\leqslant(\tau\kappa)^{-O(1/k)}\left\lVert 1_{A_{i}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}.

Furthermore, the nested nature of our
A_{i}
ensures that
1_{A_{i}}\circ 1_{A}(x)\geqslant 2^{-O(k)}\delta\left\lvert A\right\rvert
for all
x\in S
, whence

\left\lVert 1_{S}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3}\lesssim 2^{O(k)}\kappa^{1/3}\delta^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1/3}\left\lVert 1_{A_{i}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}.

It follows that

\delta^{8}\left\lvert S^{\prime}\right\rvert^{6}\lesssim 2^{O(k)}\kappa^{2}\delta^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{13/3}\left\lVert 1_{A_{i}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}.

The claim now follows, recalling
\left\lvert S^{\prime}\right\rvert\gtrsim\delta^{-2}\tau\left\lvert A\right\rvert
, setting
A_{2}=A_{i}
and
A_{1}=A_{i-1}
.
∎

5.
Bounding
\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}

In this section we will prove two bounds on
\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}
(where, as usual,
S
is some upper level set of
1_{A}\circ 1_{A}
) which in certain regimes go beyond the ‘trivial’ bound of (
1
).

Many of the manipulations here may appear arbitrary and ad hoc; this is because they are. The particular shape of these arguments was arrived at through a blend of intuition and trial and error, and there seems to be nothing canonical or natural about some of the choices made. If the reader, encountering a particular inequality, thinks “why was the choice made to bound it this way, rather than this other way”, we can only reassure them that we tried every way that occurred to us, but only the path described here led to any improvement at the end.

We believe that the ad hoc nature of the proofs in this section are a reflection of our lack of understanding of control; there should be a more natural way of producing stronger non-trivial bounds for
\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}
, but exactly what form this should take eludes us.

We also cannot rule out the possibility that an elementary proof similar to those below, with only a slightly different path, could produce superior exponents. We have tried to make sure that at least the exponents produced in Theorem
1
are a ‘local maximum’ of these techniques, but the large search space of possible elementary arguments makes this difficult to guarantee.

5.1.
A bound useful for
\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert

The first lemma gives an improvement which is useful when bounding
\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert
(in conjunction with Lemma
9
).

Lemma 13
.

If
A
has control
\kappa
and
\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert\leqslant K\left\lvert A\right\rvert
then, for any
\delta>0
, if

S=\{x:1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)\geqslant\delta\left\lvert A\right\rvert\},

then

\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}\lesssim_{\delta\kappa}\delta^{-3/10}\kappa^{13/60}K^{11/60}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{7/15}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{19/30}\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}\right\rVert_{12/5}^{2/5}.

In particular, if
E(A)=\tau\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}
then

\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}\lesssim_{\delta\kappa}\tau^{1/10}\delta^{-3/10}\kappa^{17/60}K^{11/60}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{31/30}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{19/30}.

Proof.

Let
\eta>0
be arbitrary and
T=\{x:1_{A}\circ 1_{S}(x)\geqslant\eta\left\lvert A\right\rvert\}
. By definition

\eta\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert T\right\rvert\leqslant\langle 1_{A}\circ 1_{S},1_{T}\rangle=\langle 1_{S},1_{A}\circ 1_{T}\rangle

and hence, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\eta^{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert T\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{-1}\leqslant\langle 1_{S},(1_{A}\circ 1_{T})^{2}\rangle=\sum_{y_{1},y_{2}\in T}\sum_{x\in S}1_{A}(x+y_{1})1_{A}(x+y_{2}).

The sum here is restricted to pairs
y_{1},y_{2}
such that
y_{1}-y_{2}\in A-A
. Since

\displaystyle\sum_{y_{1},y_{2}\in T}\left(\sum_{x\in S}1_{A}(x+y_{1})1_{A}(x+y_{2})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{1},x_{2}\in S}\left(\sum_{y\in T}1_{A}(x_{1}+y)1_{A}(x_{2}+y)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\langle 1_{S}\circ 1_{S},(1_{A}\circ 1_{A})^{2}\rangle

another application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields

\eta^{4}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}\left\lvert T\right\rvert^{4}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{-2}\leqslant\langle 1_{T}\circ 1_{T},1_{A-A}\rangle\langle 1_{S}\circ 1_{S},(1_{A}\circ 1_{A})^{2}\rangle.

We first note that, using Hölder’s inequality and the control assumption,

\displaystyle\langle 1_{T}\circ 1_{T},1_{A-A}\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant\eta^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1}\langle 1_{A}\circ 1_{S},1_{T}\ast 1_{A-A}\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant\eta^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1}\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{T}\right\rVert_{3}\left\lVert 1_{S}\ast 1_{A-A}\right\rVert_{3/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\kappa^{1/3}\eta^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1/3}\left\lvert T\right\rvert^{2/3}\left\lVert 1_{S}\ast 1_{A-A}\right\rVert_{3/2}.

By Hölder’s inequality

\displaystyle\left\lVert 1_{S}\ast 1_{A-A}\right\rVert_{3/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant K^{1/2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert 1_{S}\ast 1_{A-A}\right\rVert_{3}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant K^{1/2}\delta^{-1/2}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}\ast 1_{A-A}\right\rVert_{3}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\kappa}\kappa^{1/4}\delta^{-1/2}K^{11/12}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{7/6},

where we have used Lemma
2
and the ‘trivial’ Hölder bound (as in (
1
)) of
\left\lVert 1_{A}\ast 1_{A-A}\right\rVert_{3/2}\leqslant\kappa^{1/6}K^{5/6}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/3}
to bound

\displaystyle\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}\ast 1_{A-A}\right\rVert_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\kappa}\kappa^{1/3}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2/3}\left\lVert 1_{A}\ast 1_{A-A}\right\rVert_{3/2}+\kappa^{100}K^{1/3}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/3}\left\lVert 1_{A}\ast 1_{A-A}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2/3}

\displaystyle\leqslant\kappa^{1/3}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2/3}\left\lVert 1_{A}\ast 1_{A-A}\right\rVert_{3/2}+\kappa^{100}K^{1/3}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{7/3}

\displaystyle\leqslant\kappa^{1/2}K^{5/6}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{7/3}+\kappa^{100}K^{1/3}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{7/3}

\displaystyle\ll\kappa^{1/2}K^{5/6}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{7/3}.

It follows that

\left((\eta\left\lvert A\right\rvert)^{3/2}\left\lvert T\right\rvert\right)^{10/3}\leqslant\kappa^{7/12}\delta^{-1/2}K^{11/12}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{5/2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{11/6}\langle 1_{S}\circ 1_{S},(1_{A}\circ 1_{A})^{2}\rangle.

Summing over a dyadic range of
\eta
(noting that the contribution to the left-hand side from those
\eta\leqslant(\kappa\delta)^{100}
, say, is negligible) we deduce that

\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}^{5}\lesssim\kappa^{7/12}\delta^{-1/2}K^{11/12}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{5/2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{11/6}\langle 1_{S}\circ 1_{S},(1_{A}\circ 1_{A})^{2}\rangle.

We now bound
\langle 1_{S}\circ 1_{S},(1_{A}\circ 1_{A})^{2}\rangle
. Let
\nu>0
and
U=\{x:1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)\in[\nu,2\nu)\left\lvert A\right\rvert\}
. The contribution to the inner product from
U
is

\displaystyle\ll\nu^{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\langle 1_{S},1_{S}\ast 1_{U}\rangle

\displaystyle\ll\delta^{-1}\nu^{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\langle 1_{A}\circ 1_{A},1_{S}\ast 1_{U}\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant\delta^{-1}\nu^{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3}\left\lVert 1_{A}\ast 1_{U}\right\rVert_{3/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\delta^{-1}\nu^{2}\kappa^{1/3}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/3}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{2/3}\left\lVert 1_{A}\ast 1_{U}\right\rVert_{3/2}.

Using once again the ‘trivial’ Hölder bound
\left\lVert 1_{A}\ast 1_{U}\right\rVert_{3/2}\leqslant\kappa^{1/6}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/6}\left\lvert U\right\rvert^{5/6}
this is at most

\delta^{-1}\kappa^{1/2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{2/3}\left(\nu^{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert U\right\rvert^{5/6}\right).

The bracketed expression is at most
\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}\right\rVert_{12/5}^{2}
, and hence summing over a dyadic range of
\nu
we deduce

\langle 1_{S}\circ 1_{S},(1_{A}\circ 1_{A})^{2}\rangle\lesssim\delta^{-1}\kappa^{1/2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{2/3}\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}\right\rVert_{12/5}^{2}.

Rearranging yields the first result. The second follows from Hölder’s inequality, since

\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}\right\rVert_{12/5}\leqslant\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}\right\rVert_{2}^{1/2}\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}\right\rVert_{3}^{1/2}\leqslant\tau^{1/4}\kappa^{1/6}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{17/12}.

∎

5.2.
A bound useful for
E(A)

We now present the second type of improvement, used for improving the bounds on the additive energy
E(A)
– this is more technical, and we require slightly more information. This is not a significant barrier to applications, however, and indeed Lemma
12
was constructed with the hypotheses of Lemma
14
in mind. At first glance the reader should heuristically take
S=S^{\prime}
and
A_{1}=A_{2}=A
in the below, and
\nu=\delta\left\lvert S\right\rvert/\left\lvert A\right\rvert
.

Lemma 14
.

Suppose
A
has control
\kappa
and
A_{1},A_{2}\subseteq A
and
S,S^{\prime}
are such that

(1)

1_{A_{2}}\circ 1_{A}(x)\geqslant\delta\left\lvert A\right\rvert
for all
x\in S
,

(2)

1_{A_{1}}\circ 1_{S^{\prime}}(x)\geqslant\nu\left\lvert A\right\rvert
for all
x\in A_{2}
, and

(3)

\left\lVert 1_{A_{1}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}\leqslant L\left\lVert 1_{A_{2}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}
.

Then

\left\lVert 1_{A_{2}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}\lesssim_{\kappa}L^{1/7}\nu^{-8/7}\kappa^{4/7}\delta^{-2/7}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{8/21}\left\lvert S^{\prime}\right\rvert^{4/7}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{5/7}+\kappa^{99}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4/3}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{1/3}.

Proof.

Let
\eta>0
and

T=\{x:1_{A_{2}}\circ 1_{S}(x)\geqslant\eta\left\lvert A\right\rvert\}.

We have

\eta\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert T\right\rvert\leqslant\langle 1_{T},1_{A_{2}}\circ 1_{S}\rangle=\langle 1_{A_{2}},1_{S}\ast 1_{T}\rangle

and hence

\nu\eta\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert T\right\rvert\leqslant\langle 1_{A_{2}},(1_{A_{1}}\circ 1_{S^{\prime}})(1_{S}\ast 1_{T})\rangle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in S\\
x\in S^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\sum_{y\in T}1_{A_{2}}(z+y)1_{A_{1}}(z+y+x).

Therefore, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\nu^{2}\eta^{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}\left\lvert T\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert S^{\prime}\right\rvert^{-1}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{-1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{y_{1},y_{2}\in T}\sum_{z\in S}1_{A_{2}}(z+y_{1})1_{A_{2}}(z+y_{2})\sum_{x\in S^{\prime}}1_{A_{1}}(z+y_{1}+x)1_{A_{1}}(z+y_{2}+x)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{y_{1},y_{2}\in T}1_{A_{1}}\circ 1_{A_{1}}(y_{1}-y_{2})\sum_{z\in S}1_{A_{2}}(z+y_{1})1_{A_{2}}(z+y_{2}).

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality once again, we deduce that

(3)

\nu^{4}\eta^{4}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{8}\left\lvert T\right\rvert^{4}\left\lvert S^{\prime}\right\rvert^{-2}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{-2}\leqslant\langle 1_{T}\circ 1_{T},(1_{A_{1}}\circ 1_{A_{1}})^{2}\rangle\langle 1_{S}\circ 1_{S},(1_{A_{2}}\circ 1_{A_{2}})^{2}\rangle.

To bound the first factor, we let
\rho>0
and
U=\{x:1_{A_{1}}\circ 1_{A_{1}}(x)\in[\rho\left\lvert A\right\rvert,2\rho\left\lvert A\right\rvert)\}
, so that

\displaystyle\langle 1_{T}\circ 1_{T},(1_{A_{1}}\circ 1_{A_{1}})^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\rho}\rho^{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\langle 1_{T},1_{T}\ast 1_{U}\rangle

\displaystyle\ll\eta^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\sum_{\rho}\rho^{2}\langle 1_{A}\circ 1_{S},1_{T}\ast 1_{U}\rangle

\displaystyle\ll\eta^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\sum_{\rho}\rho^{2}\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{T}\right\rVert_{3}\left\lVert 1_{S}\ast 1_{U}\right\rVert_{3/2}

\displaystyle\ll\kappa^{1/3}\eta^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/3}\left\lvert T\right\rvert^{2/3}\sum_{\rho}\rho^{2}\left\lVert 1_{S}\ast 1_{U}\right\rVert_{3/2},

where the sum over
\rho
is over a dyadic range
\rho=2^{-k}
for
k\geqslant 0
. By Hölder’s inequality

\left\lVert 1_{S}\ast 1_{U}\right\rVert_{3/2}\leqslant\left\lVert 1_{S}\ast 1_{U}\right\rVert_{3}^{1/2}(\left\lvert S\right\rvert\left\lvert{U}\right\rvert)^{1/2}

and by Lemma
2

\displaystyle\left\lVert 1_{S}\ast 1_{U}\right\rVert_{3}

\displaystyle\ll\rho^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1}\left\lVert 1_{A_{1}}\circ 1_{A}\ast 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\kappa}\kappa^{1/3}\rho^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1/3}\left\lVert 1_{A_{1}}\ast 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}+\kappa^{100}\rho^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{1/3}.

It follows that
\langle 1_{T}\circ 1_{T},(1_{A_{1}}\circ 1_{A_{1}})^{2}\rangle
is at most

\kappa^{1/3}\eta^{-1}\left\lvert T\right\rvert^{2/3}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{1/2}\left(\kappa^{1/6}\left\lVert 1_{A_{1}}\ast 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}^{1/2}+\kappa^{50}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2/3}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{1/6}\right)\sum_{\rho}(\rho\left\lvert A\right\rvert)^{3/2}\left\lvert U\right\rvert^{1/2}.

The sum over
\rho
is at most
\lesssim\kappa^{1/2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}
(noting that we can restrict the sum to those
\rho\geqslant\kappa^{100}
, say, since the contribution from very small
\rho
is negligible), whence

\langle 1_{T}\circ 1_{T},(1_{A_{1}}\circ 1_{A_{1}})^{2}\rangle\lesssim\kappa^{5/6}\eta^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert T\right\rvert^{2/3}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{1/2}\left(\kappa^{1/6}\left\lVert 1_{A_{1}}\ast 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}^{1/2}+\kappa^{50}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2/3}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{1/6}\right).

Combining this with (
3
), we deduce

\left(\eta^{3/2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3/2}\left\lvert T\right\rvert\right)^{10/3}\lesssim

\nu^{-4}\kappa^{5/6}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1}\left\lvert S^{\prime}\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{5/2}\left(\kappa^{1/6}\left\lVert 1_{A_{1}}\ast 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}^{1/2}+\kappa^{50}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2/3}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{1/6}\right)\langle 1_{S}\circ 1_{S},(1_{A}\circ 1_{A})^{2}\rangle.

Summing this over
\eta
in a dyadic range we deduce that the same upper bound holds for
\left\lVert 1_{A_{2}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}^{5}
. We then bound the final factor using Hölder’s inequality by

\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}\right\rVert_{3}^{2}\left\lVert 1_{S}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3}\leqslant\kappa^{2/3}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{8/3}\left\lVert 1_{S}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3}.

Finally, by Lemma
2
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert 1_{S}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3}

\displaystyle\leqslant\delta^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1}\left\lVert 1_{A_{2}}\circ 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\kappa}\delta^{-1}\kappa^{1/3}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-1/3}\left\lVert 1_{A_{2}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}+\delta^{-1}\kappa^{100}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{1/3}.

Combining these inequalities we have bounded
\left\lVert 1_{A_{2}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}^{5}
above by (noting that
\left\lVert 1_{A_{1}}\ast 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}=\left\lVert 1_{A_{1}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}\leqslant L\left\lVert 1_{A_{2}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}
)

\lesssim\delta^{-1}\nu^{-4}\kappa^{3/2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4/3}\left\lvert S^{\prime}\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{5/2}\left(L^{1/2}\kappa^{1/6}\left\lVert 1_{A_{2}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}^{1/2}+\kappa^{50}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2/3}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{1/6}\right)\times

\left(\kappa^{1/3}\left\lVert 1_{A_{2}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}+\kappa^{100}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4/3}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{1/3}\right).

In particular, either

\left\lVert 1_{A_{2}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}\lesssim\kappa^{99}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4/3}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{1/3}

or

\left\lVert 1_{A_{2}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}^{5}\lesssim L\nu^{-4}\kappa^{2}\delta^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4/3}\left\lvert S^{\prime}\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{5/2}\left\lVert 1_{A_{1}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}^{1/2}\left\lVert 1_{A_{2}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}.

The claim follows after simplifying this inequality.
∎

5.3.
Completing the proof of Theorem
1

Finally we bring together the various inequalities to prove Theorem
1
. (It is worth noting that the lower bound for
\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert
uses the upper bound for
E(A)
– in particular, an improvement for the latter immediately implies an improvement for the former.)

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We first note that the lower bound

\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert\gtrsim\kappa^{11/19}\left\lvert A\right\rvert

is an immediate consequence of Lemma
10
and (
1
), as previously noted.

To prove the energy bound, we apply Lemma
12
to produce
A_{1},A_{2},S,S^{\prime},\delta
as in the statement with

\tau^{6}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/3}\ll 2^{O(k)}\kappa^{2}\delta^{3}\left\lVert 1_{A_{2}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}.

We may now apply Lemma
14
with
\nu\approx 2^{-O(k)}\delta\left\lvert S^{\prime}\right\rvert/\left\lvert A\right\rvert
and
L\ll\kappa^{-O(1/k)}
(noting that we can assume
\tau\geqslant\kappa
, say, or we are immediately done). This yields

\left\lVert 1_{A_{2}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}\lesssim 2^{O(k)}\kappa^{4/7-O(1/k)}\delta^{-10/7}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{32/21}\left\lvert S^{\prime}\right\rvert^{-4/7}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{5/7}+\kappa^{99}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4/3}\left\lvert S\right\rvert^{1/3}.

Using the bounds
\left\lvert S^{\prime}\right\rvert\gtrsim\delta^{-2}\tau\left\lvert A\right\rvert
and
\left\lvert S\right\rvert\lesssim 2^{O(k)}\delta^{-2}\tau\left\lvert A\right\rvert
this becomes

\left\lVert 1_{A_{2}}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}\lesssim 2^{O(k)}\kappa^{4/7-O(1/k)}\delta^{-12/7}\tau^{1/7}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/3},

and hence

\tau^{6-1/7}\lesssim 2^{O(k)}\kappa^{2+4/7-O(1/k)}\delta^{3-12/7}.

Using
\delta\lesssim\kappa\tau^{-1}
and simplifying this expression, we have

\tau^{50/7}\lesssim 2^{O(k)}\kappa^{27/7-O(1/k)}.

Choosing
k\asymp\epsilon^{-1}
yields
\tau\ll_{\epsilon}\kappa^{27/50-\epsilon}
as required.

Finally, to bound
\left\lvert A-A\right\rvert=K\left\lvert A\right\rvert
, we apply Lemma
9
to yield

\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/3}\ll\kappa^{4/3}K^{5/3}\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2},

where

S=\{x:1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)\geqslant(2K)^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\}.

Lemma
13
(with
\delta=1/2K
and
\left\lvert S\right\rvert\leqslant K\left\lvert A\right\rvert
) yields

\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{S}\right\rVert_{3/2}\lesssim\tau^{1/10}\kappa^{17/60}K^{67/60}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/3},

whence

1\lesssim\tau^{1/10}\kappa^{97/60}K^{167/60}.

Using the bound
\tau\ll_{\epsilon}\kappa^{27/50-\epsilon}
yields

1\ll_{\epsilon}\kappa^{1253/750-\epsilon}K^{167/60},

and rearranging produces

K^{-1}\ll_{\epsilon}\kappa^{2506/4175-\epsilon}

as claimed.
∎

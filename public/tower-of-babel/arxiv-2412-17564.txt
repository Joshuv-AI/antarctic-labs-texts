Abstract
. We prove that the approximately inner automorphism group of a separable strongly stable von Neumann algebra is contractible in the u-topology. Thus the automorphism group of the hyperfinite type III 1 \mathrm{III}_{1} factor is contractible.

Primary 58B05; Secondary 46L10

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we continue Popa and Takesaki’s
study (
[
PT
]
) of contractibility of the unitary group and
the automorphism group of a von Neumann algebra.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra and
\Aut(M)
denote
the group of
*
-automorphisms on
M
,
equipped with the u-topology, i.e., the pointwise
convergence topology on the predual
M_{*}
.
The u-topology makes
\Aut(M)
a topological group,
which is Polish if
M
has separable predual.
We are interested in the closed normal
subgroup
\Int(M)\subset\Aut(M)

of
approximately inner
automorphisms,
which is the closure of the subgroup

\mathrm{Int}(M)
of inner automorphisms.
The von Neumann algebra
M
is said to
be
strongly stable
(or
McDuff
)
if
M\cong M\otimes\mathcal{R}
,
where
\mathcal{R}
denotes the hyperfinite (or AFD) factor
of type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
with separable predual.
The following theorem generalizes Popa
and Takesaki’s theorem (
[
PT
]
).

Theorem A
.

Let
M
be a strongly stable von Neumann algebra
with separable predual.
Then
\Int(M)
is contractible.

The case for the hyperfinite factor
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{III}_{1}}

of type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
with separable predual
has attracted considerable attention because
of applications to geometric topology and
mathematical physics (see e.g.,
[
ST
]
).
The factor
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{III}_{1}}
is strongly stable
(and so are all separable hyperfinite type
\mathrm{II}

and type
\mathrm{III}
von Neumann algebras,
see
[
Ta
]
).
That
\Aut(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{III}_{1}})=\Int(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{III}_{1}})

is proved by Kawahigashi, Sutherland, and Takesaki (
[
KST
]
).

Corollary
.

The automorphism group
\Aut(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{III}_{1}})

of
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{III}_{1}}
is contractible.

We denote by
\mathcal{U}(M)
the unitary group of
M

equipped with the ultrastrong topology.
Let
N\subset M
be an inclusion of von Neumann algebras
(which is always assumed to be unital).
The unitary group
\mathcal{U}(N)
of
N
will be identified
with its image in
\mathcal{U}(M)
, as they are canonically
homeomorphically isomorphic.
The following is asserted in
[
PT
]
,
but the proof contains a gap, as explained in Section
5
.
We bridge the gap on this occasion.

Theorem B
.

Let
N\subset M
be an inclusion of
\sigma
-finite
von Neumann algebras and assume
that
N
is strongly stable.
Then the quotient map from
\mathcal{U}(M)

onto
\mathcal{U}(M)/\mathcal{U}(N)

admits a continuous cross section.
Equivalently, there is a continuous equivariant retraction
from
\mathcal{U}(M)
onto
\mathcal{U}(N)
.

2.
Preliminary for Proof of Theorem
A

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal
state
\omega
.
The corresponding
2
-norm on
M
is given by

\|x\|_{2}\coloneqq\omega(x^{*}x)^{1/2}
.
The topology induced by the
2
-norm coincides
with the ultrastrong topology on bounded subsets of
M
.
It also coincides with the ultraweak topology on
the unitary group
\mathcal{U}(M)
.
For
a\in M
, we define
a\omega\in M_{*}
by

(a\omega)(x)\coloneqq\omega(xa)
.
One has
\|a\omega\|\leq\|a\|_{2}

by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.

Let
\UCP_{\sigma}(M,N)
denote the set of ultraweakly
continuous unital completely positive maps from a von Neumann
algebra
M
into
N
. Note that
\Aut(M)\subset\UCP_{\sigma}(M,M)
.
Let
\mathop{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{n}}}(N)
denote the set of normal states on
N
.
For
\alpha\in\UCP_{\sigma}(M,N)
, we define its pre-conjugate

\alpha^{*}\colon N_{*}\to M_{*}
by

\alpha^{*}(\varphi)=\varphi\circ\alpha
.
Note that
(\alpha\beta)^{*}=\beta^{*}\alpha^{*}
.
We extend the definition of the u-topology on
\Aut(M)
and
define the
u-topology
on
\UCP_{\sigma}(M,N)
to be
the pointwise norm convergence topology of the pre-conjugates.
Namely, the u-topology is induced by the pseudo-metrics

\{\dist_{\varphi}:\varphi\in\mathop{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{n}}}(N)\}
, where

\dist_{\varphi}(\alpha,\beta)\coloneqq\|(\alpha^{*}-\beta^{*})(\varphi)\|=\|\varphi\circ\alpha-\varphi\circ\beta\|.

We also write
\dist_{F}\coloneqq\max_{\varphi\in F}\dist_{\varphi}

for a finite subset
F\subset\mathop{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{n}}}(N)
.
We note that the composition is jointly u-continuous
(because all pre-conjugates are norm contractive)
and that the u-topology is compatible with the tensor product of von Neumann
algebras (because the algebraic tensor product
(M_{1})_{*}\otimes(M_{2})_{*}
is
dense in
(M_{1}\otimes M_{2})_{*}
).
Moreover, the pseudo-metric
\dist_{\varphi}
is right invariant on
\Aut(M)
, i.e.,

\dist_{\varphi}(\alpha,\beta)=\dist_{\varphi}(\mathrm{id},\beta\alpha^{-1})

for every
\alpha,\beta\in\Aut(M)
, and the homomorphism

\mathcal{U}(M)\ni u\mapsto\Ad u\in\Aut(M)
is continuous.
Here
\Ad u
is the inner automorphism
defined by
(\Ad u)(x)=uxu^{*}
.

We consider a type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor
N

and denote by
\End(N)
the set
of unital
*
-endomorphisms on
N
.
The unique tracial state
is denoted by
\tau
, or
\tau_{N}
to emphasize
N
.
We recall that the
p-topology
on
\End(N)
is
the pointwise ultraweak convergence topology.
It coincides with the pointwise 2-norm convergence topology,
where the 2-norm is taken w.r.t. the trace
\tau
.
Every
\psi\in\End(N)
is trace-preserving and thus
isometric w.r.t. the 2-norm.
The p-topology is weaker than the u-topology,
but they coincide on
\Aut(N)
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
N
be a type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor.
Suppose that
\psi_{n}\in\Aut(N)
converge
to
\psi\in\End(N)
in the p-topology,
and denote by
E
the unique trace-preserving conditional
expectation from
N
onto
\psi(N)
.
Then,
E\psi_{n}\to\psi
and
\psi_{n}^{-1}\to\psi^{-1}E
in the u-topology.

Proof.

Let
a\in N
be such that
a\geq 0
and
\tau(a)=1
.
Then since

\|(\psi^{-1}E)(a)-(\psi_{n}^{-1}E)(a)\|_{2}=\|\psi_{n}\psi^{-1}(E(a))-E(a)\|_{2}\to 0,

one has

(E\psi_{n})^{*}(a\tau)=(\psi_{n}^{-1}E)(a)\tau\to(\psi^{-1}E)(a)\tau=(E\psi)^{*}(a\tau)

in norm.
Since the states of the form
a\tau
are norm dense in
\mathop{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{n}}}(N)
,
the first assertion follows. The proof of the second is similar.
∎

We collect a few well-known facts about
the hyperfinite type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor
\mathcal{R}
.
See
[
Ta
]
for general information.
We set
\mathcal{R}_{\infty}\coloneqq\bigotimes_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{R}
and
write
\mathcal{R}_{n}\coloneqq\bigotimes_{m=1}^{n}\mathcal{R}
the first
n
tensor product.
Hence for
\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\mathrm{c}}\coloneqq\bigotimes_{m=n+1}^{\infty}\mathcal{R}
,
one has
\mathcal{R}_{\infty}=\mathcal{R}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\mathrm{c}}
.
Note that
\mathcal{R}_{n}\cong\mathcal{R}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
.
The factor
\mathcal{R}
is

strongly self-absorbing
in the following sense.

Lemma 2
.

Let’s write
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{Q}\otimes\mathcal{S}
,
where
\mathcal{Q}
and
\mathcal{S}
are copies of
\mathcal{R}
.
Then there are
*
-isomorphisms

\rho_{\mathcal{Q}}\colon\mathcal{Q}\to\mathcal{R}
and
\rho_{\mathcal{S}}\colon\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{R}

and a p-continuous map

\psi\colon[0,1]\to\UCP_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathcal{R})

such that
\psi_{0}(x)=x\otimes 1
for
x\in\mathcal{R}
,

\psi_{t}
are surjective
*
-isomorphisms for
t>0
,
and
\psi_{1}(x\otimes y)=\rho_{\mathcal{Q}}(x)\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{S}}(y)

for
x\in\mathcal{Q}
and
y\in\mathcal{S}
.
The map
\psi
satisfies

(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}\times\tau_{\mathcal{R}})\psi_{t}\to\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}

and
\psi_{t}^{-1}\to\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}\times\tau_{\mathcal{R}}

as
t\to 0
in the u-topology,
where
\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}\times\tau_{\mathcal{R}}
is the slice map
given by
(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}\times\tau_{\mathcal{R}})(x\otimes y)=\tau(y)x
.

Proof.

For notational convenience, we replace
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{Q}\otimes\mathcal{S}

with
\mathcal{R}_{\infty}=\mathcal{R}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\mathrm{c}}
.
Moreover we work with

\UCP_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R}_{\infty},\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{\infty})

and swap left with right
and
t\colon 1\mapsto 0
with
s\colon 1\mapsto\infty
.
There is a u-continuous map

\sigma\colon[0,1]\to\Aut(\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathcal{R})

that connects
\sigma_{0}\coloneqq\mathrm{id}
to the flip automorphism

\sigma_{1}
, given by
x\otimes y\mapsto y\otimes x
.
The map
\sigma
can be constructed from that for
the
2
-by-
2
matrix algebra
\mathbb{M}_{2}

via the isomorphism
\mathcal{R}\cong\bigotimes_{\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{M}_{2}
.
We write
\sigma^{(n)}_{t}\in\Aut(\mathcal{R}_{\infty})

the copy of
\sigma_{t}
applied
at the
\{n,n+1\}
-th tensor product component.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in[0,1]
,
we define the
*
-isomorphism

\psi_{n+t}\colon\mathcal{R}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{\infty}
by

\psi_{n+t}=\psi_{n}\sigma^{(n)}_{t}
, where

\psi_{n}(x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\otimes\cdots)\coloneqq x_{n}\otimes(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n-1}\otimes x_{n+1}\otimes\cdots).

One has
\psi_{1}(x\otimes y)=\rho(x)\otimes\rho^{\prime}(y)
for

x\in\mathcal{R}_{1}
and
y\in\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\mathrm{c}}
, where

\rho\colon\mathcal{R}_{1}\to\mathcal{R}
is the canonical identification and

\rho^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\mathrm{c}}\to\mathcal{R}_{\infty}

is the shift isomorphism.
The map
\psi
is u-continuous on
[1,\infty)
, and
since
\bigcup_{n}\mathcal{R}_{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}1_{\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\mathrm{c}}}\subset\mathcal{R}_{\infty}

is ultrastrongly dense, one has

\psi_{s}\to 1\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{R}_{\infty}}
as
s\to\infty

in the p-topology
(NB: but not in the u-topology).
That
\psi
satisfies the last statement
follows from Lemma
1
, as
the conditional expectation
E

onto
\ran\psi_{0}=\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathbb{C}1

is
\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau
and
hence
\psi_{0}^{-1}E=\mathrm{id}\times\tau
.
∎

Lemma 3
.

The trace-preserving conditional expectations

E_{n}\coloneqq\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{R}_{n}}\otimes\tau_{\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\mathrm{c}}}

from
\mathcal{R}_{\infty}
onto
\mathcal{R}_{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}1_{\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\mathrm{c}}}

converge to
\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{R}_{\infty}}
in the u-topology.

Proof.

One has
E_{n}^{*}(a\tau)=E_{n}(a)\tau\to a\tau
for every

a\in\bigcup_{n}\mathcal{R}_{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}1_{\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\mathrm{c}}}

with
a\geq 0
and
\tau(a)=1
.
Since the states of the form
a\tau
are dense
in
\mathop{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{n}}}(\mathcal{R}_{\infty})
, we are done.
∎

A unitary element
u\in\mathcal{U}(M)
is said to be

\omega
-Haar
(or simply
Haar
) if

\omega(u^{n})=0
for all
n\neq 0
.
Let
u=\int_{\mathbb{T}}z\,dE_{u}(z)
be the spectral resolution of
u
,
where
E_{u}
is the spectral measure on
\mathbb{T}\coloneqq\{z:|z|=1\}
.
Then
u
is
\omega
-Haar if and only if the probability measure

\omega\circ E_{u}
coincides with the Haar (Lebesgue) measure
\lambda
on
\mathbb{T}
.
We denote by
\log
the branch of
the logarithm on
\mathbb{T}
that takes values in
[-i\pi,i\pi)
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra with
a faithful normal state
\omega
and

W\subset\mathcal{U}(M)
be a subset consisting entirely
of
\omega
-Haar unitary elements.
Then,

h\colon W\times[0,1]\ni(u,t)\mapsto\exp(t\log u)\in\mathcal{U}(M)

is continuous and satisfies
h(u,0)=1
and
h(u,1)=u
for
u\in W
.
Moreover for every
\varepsilon>0
there is
\delta>0

(independent of
\omega
as long as
u
is
\omega
-Haar)
that satisfies the following property:
If
\dist_{\omega}(\mathrm{id},\Ad u)<\delta
,
then
\dist_{\omega}(\mathrm{id},\Ad h(u,t))<\varepsilon
.

Proof.

For
\kappa>0
, consider the piecewise linear function

g_{\kappa}
from
[-\pi,\pi]
to
[-\pi+\kappa,\pi-\kappa]

that linearly connects
g_{\kappa}(-\pi)=0
,

g_{\kappa}(-\pi+\kappa)=-\pi+\kappa
,

g_{\kappa}(\pi-\kappa)=\pi-\kappa
,
and
g_{\kappa}(\pi)=0
.
Then

f_{\kappa}(\exp(i\theta))\coloneqq\exp(ig_{\kappa}(\theta))
,

\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
,
defines a continuous unitary function
f_{\kappa}
on
\mathbb{T}
.
Since
\log
is continuous on the range of
f_{\kappa}
, the map

h_{\kappa}(u,t)\coloneqq\exp(t\log f_{\kappa}(u))
is continuous
on
W\times[0,1]
.
Since every
u\in W
is
\omega
-Haar, one has

\|h(u,t)-h_{\kappa}(u,t)\|_{2}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\exp(t\log z)-\exp(t\log f_{\kappa}(z))|^{2}\,d\lambda(z)\leq 4\frac{\kappa}{\pi}.

It follows that
h_{\kappa}\to h
uniformly as
\kappa\to 0

and so
h
is continuous.

For the second assertion, let
\varepsilon>0
be given.
For
a\in M
, we define
\Der a
on
M

by
(\Der a)(x)=ax-xa
.
Note that
\Der
is linear,
\|\omega\circ\Der a\|\leq 2\|a\|_{2}

for normal
a
,
and
\|\omega\circ\Der v\|=\dist_{\omega}(\mathrm{id},\Ad v)

for
v\in\mathcal{U}(M)
.
We take
\kappa\coloneqq\varepsilon^{2}/2
and
consider
h_{\kappa}
as above.
Thus
\|h(u,t)-h_{\kappa}(u,t)\|_{2}<\varepsilon
for
u\in W
.
Take a polynomial approximation
p_{\kappa}\in\mathbb{Z}[z,z^{-1},t]

that satisfies

|p_{\kappa}(z,t)-\exp(t\log f_{\kappa}(z))|<\varepsilon

on
\mathbb{T}\times[0,1]
.
Since
\|\omega\circ\Der u^{n}\|\leq|n|\|\omega\circ\Der u\|

for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, there is
\delta>0

such that
\|\omega\circ\Der u\|<\delta

implies
\|\omega\circ\Der p_{\kappa}(u,t)\|<\varepsilon
for all
t\in[0,1]
.
It follows that for
(u,t)\in W\times[0,1]
, that

\|\omega\circ\Der u\|<\delta
implies

\|\omega\circ\Der h\|\leq 2\|h-h_{\kappa}\|_{2}+2\|h_{\kappa}-p_{\kappa}\|_{2}+\|\omega\circ\Der p_{\kappa}\|<5\varepsilon,

where we omitted writing
(u,t)
.
This proves the second assertion.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
A

The strategy of the proof of Theorem
A

is similar to that for Theorem 4
in
[
PT
]
, but the cross section
method is replaced with a plain convexity argument.
Dadarlat and Pennig’s trick provides a room
for the convexity argument to work.

Proposition 5

(cf. Theorem 2.3 in
[
DP
]
)
.

Let
M_{0}
be a strongly stable von Neumann algebra,
and
M\coloneqq M_{0}\otimes\mathcal{R}
.
Then there is a continuous map

H\colon\Aut(M)\times[0,1]\to\Aut(M)

such that
H(\alpha,0)=\alpha
and
H(\alpha,1)\in\Aut(M_{0})\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}
.
Moreover,
H
maps
\Int(M)\times[0,1]
into
\Int(M)
.

Proof.

Let’s write
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{Q}\otimes\mathcal{S}
and
take
\psi_{t}\colon\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathcal{R}

as in Lemma
2
.
In particular,

\psi_{1}(x\otimes y)=\rho_{\mathcal{Q}}(x)\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{S}}(y)

and

(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}\times\tau_{\mathcal{R}})\psi_{t}\to\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}

and
\psi_{t}^{-1}\to\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}\times\tau_{\mathcal{R}}

as
t\to 0
.
We consider
M_{0}\coloneqq M_{00}\otimes\mathcal{Q}
and

M\coloneqq M_{0}\otimes\mathcal{S}=M_{00}\otimes\mathcal{R}
.
We define
H\colon\Aut(M)\times[0,1]\to\Aut(M)
by

H(\alpha,0)=\alpha
and

H(\alpha,t)=(\mathrm{id}_{M_{00}}\otimes\psi_{t}^{-1})(\alpha\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{R}})(\mathrm{id}_{M_{00}}\otimes\psi_{t})

for
t>0
. Then
H
is clearly continuous for
t>0
.
If
\alpha_{n}\to\alpha
and
t_{n}\to 0
, then for every
\varphi\in\mathop{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{n}}}(M)

one has

\displaystyle\lim_{n}H(\alpha_{n},t_{n})^{*}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n}(\mathrm{id}_{M_{00}}\otimes\psi_{t_{n}})^{*}(\alpha_{n}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{R}})^{*}(\mathrm{id}_{M_{00}}\otimes\psi_{t_{n}}^{-1})^{*}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n}(\mathrm{id}_{M_{00}}\otimes\psi_{t_{n}})^{*}(\alpha\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{R}})^{*}(\mathrm{id}_{M}\times\tau_{\mathcal{R}})^{*}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n}(\mathrm{id}_{M_{00}}\otimes\psi_{t_{n}})^{*}(\mathrm{id}_{M}\times\tau_{\mathcal{R}})^{*}\alpha^{*}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{*}(\varphi).

Here we have used

\mathrm{id}_{M_{00}}\otimes(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}\times\tau_{\mathcal{R}})=\mathrm{id}_{M}\times\tau_{\mathcal{R}}
and

(\mathrm{id}_{M}\times\tau_{\mathcal{R}})(\alpha\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{R}})=\alpha(\mathrm{id}_{M}\times\tau_{\mathcal{R}})
.
This proves continuity of
H
.
Finally, observe that

H(\alpha,1)=((\mathrm{id}_{M_{00}}\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{Q}})^{-1}\alpha(\mathrm{id}_{M_{00}}\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{Q}}))\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{S}}\in\Aut(M_{0})\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{S}}.

That
H
keeps the (approximately) inner automorphism
group invariant is obvious.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Let
M=M_{0}\otimes\mathcal{R}
be a strongly stable
von Neumann algebra with separable predual.
Let
F_{0}\subset\mathop{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{n}}}(M_{0})
be a finite subset and
\varepsilon>0
.
Put
F\coloneqq F_{0}\otimes\tau\subset\mathop{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{n}}}(M)
.
Then, there is a continuous map

u\colon\Int(M_{0})\to\mathcal{U}(M)
that satisfies

\dist_{F}(\alpha\otimes\mathrm{id},\Ad u(\alpha))<\varepsilon
for all
\alpha
.

Proof.

We consider the open cover
\{W_{u}:u\in\mathcal{U}(M_{0})\}
for
\Int(M_{0})
given by

W_{u}\coloneqq\{\alpha\in\Int(M_{0}):\dist_{F_{0}}(\alpha,\Ad u)<\varepsilon\}.

Take a partition of unity
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
for
\Int(M_{0})

subordinated by
\{W_{u}\}
.
For each
i
, take
u_{i}\in\mathcal{U}(M_{0})
such that
\supp\psi_{i}\subset W_{u_{i}}
.
We define
p_{i}(\alpha)
to be the orthogonal projection
in
L^{\infty}[0,1]\subset\mathcal{R}
that
corresponds to the interval

[\sum_{j<i}\psi_{j}(\alpha),\sum_{j\leq i}\psi_{j}(\alpha))
.
Note that the defining sum is a locally finite sum and that
the maps
\alpha\mapsto p_{i}(\alpha)
are ultrastrongly continuous.
We define a continuous map
u\colon\Int(M_{0})\to\mathcal{U}(M)
by

u(\alpha)\coloneqq\sum_{i}u_{i}\otimes p_{i}(\alpha)
.
For every
\varphi\in F_{0}
, one has

\displaystyle(\varphi\otimes\tau)\circ\Ad u(\alpha)=\sum_{i}(\varphi\circ\Ad u_{i})\otimes(p_{i}(\alpha)\tau)\approx_{\varepsilon}(\varphi\circ\alpha)\otimes\tau

since
\|\varphi\circ\Ad u_{i}-\varphi\circ\alpha\|<\varepsilon
for every
i
and

\sum_{i}\|p_{i}(\alpha)\tau\|=\sum_{i}\tau(p_{i}(\alpha))=1
.
∎

Lemma 7
.

Let
M=M_{0}\otimes\mathcal{R}
be a strongly stable von Neumann algebra
with separable predual.
Let
F_{0}\subset\mathop{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{n}}}(M_{0})
be a finite subset and
\varepsilon>0
.
Put
F\coloneqq F_{0}\otimes\tau\subset\mathop{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{n}}}(M)
.
Then there are
\delta>0
and a continuous map

h\colon\mathcal{U}(M_{0})\times[0,1]\to\mathcal{U}(M)

such that
h(u,0)=u\otimes 1
and
h(u,1)=1
for
u\in\mathcal{U}(M_{0})

and moreover that

\dist_{F}(\mathrm{id},\Ad h(u,t))<\varepsilon
for all
t\in[0,1]

if
u
satisfies
\dist_{F_{0}}(\mathrm{id},\Ad u)<\delta
.

Proof.

Fix a
\tau
-Haar unitary element
w
in
\mathcal{R}

and define a continuous map

h\colon\mathcal{U}(M_{0})\times[0,1/2]\to\mathcal{U}(M)
by

h(u,t)\coloneqq u\otimes\exp(2t\log w)
.
Note that
h(u,0)=u\otimes 1
,

h(u,1/2)=u\otimes w
, and

\dist_{F}(\mathrm{id},\Ad h(u,t))=\dist_{F_{0}}(\mathrm{id},\Ad u)
for every
u
.
Since all elements in
\mathcal{U}(M_{0})\otimes w

are
\varphi
-Haar for
\varphi\in F
,
Lemma
4
(we may assume that
every
\varphi\in F
is faithful) implies that the map

Extraspecial pairs in the multiply-laced root systems and calculating structure constants
Rafael Stekolshchik

Abstract
. The notions of special and extraspecial pairs of roots were introduced by Carter for calculating structure constants, [ Ca72 ] . Let { r , s } \{r,s\} be a special pair of roots for which the structure constant N ⁡ ( r , s ) N(r,s) is sought, and let { r 1 , s 1 } \{r_{1},s_{1}\} be the extraspecial pair of roots corresponding to { r , s } \{r,s\} . Consider the ordered set { r 1 , r , s , s 1 } \{r_{1},r,s,s_{1}\} , we will call such a set a quartet . By studying the different quartets, we gain additional insight into the internal structure of the root system. It is shown that for the case B n B_{n} we can avoid finding 6 6 squares of lengths in the formula for calculating the structure constants. The calculation formula for B n B_{n} coincides with the formula for the simply-laced case. For the case C n C_{n} , it is possible to avoid the calculation of 4 4 squares of lengths. The calculation formula for C n C_{n} differs from simply-laced case by some parameter, which is fixed for all pairs { r , s } \{r,s\} with given extraspecial pair { r 1 , s 1 } \{r_{1},s_{1}\} .

\begin{split}N(r,&s)=\frac{\lvert r+s\rvert^{2}}{N(r_{1},s_{1})}\bigg(\frac{N(s-r_{1},r_{1})N(s_{1}-r,r)\lvert s_{1}-r\rvert^{2}}{\lvert s\rvert^{2}\lvert s_{1}\rvert^{2}}+\frac{N(r_{1},r-r_{1})N(s_{1}-s,s)\lvert r-r_{1}\rvert^{2}}{\lvert r\rvert^{2}\lvert s_{1}\rvert^{2}}\bigg)\end{split}

(2.9)

Proof.
First, the following relations hold:

\begin{split}&N(s,-r_{1})=N(s-r_{1},r_{1})\frac{\lvert s-r_{1}\rvert^{2}}{\lvert s\rvert^{2}},\qquad N(r,-s_{1})=N(s_{1}-r,r)\frac{\lvert s-r_{1}\rvert^{2}}{\lvert s_{1}\rvert^{2}},\\
&N(-r_{1},r)=N(r_{1},r-r_{1})\frac{\lvert r-r_{1}\rvert^{2}}{\lvert r\rvert^{2}}.\qquad N(s,-s_{1})=N(s_{1}-s,s)\frac{\lvert r-r_{1}\rvert^{2}}{\lvert s_{1}\rvert^{2}}.\end{split}

(2.10)

Let us prove the first relation of (
2.10
). By (
2.7
)
N(s,-r_{1})=N(r_{1},-s)
.
Since the triple of roots
\{r_{1},-s,s-r_{1}\}
forms the Tits triple satisfying eq. (
2.5
),
we have

\frac{N(s,-r_{1})}{\lvert s-r_{1}\rvert^{2}}=\frac{N(r_{1},-s)}{\lvert s-r_{1}\rvert^{2}}=\frac{N(s-r_{1},r_{1})}{\lvert s\rvert^{2}}.

Since
0\prec s-r_{1}
, by Lemma
2.3
we have as follows:
if
s-r_{1}
is a root then
s-r_{1}
is positive.
Otherwise,
N(s-r_{1},r_{1})=0
.

The remaining three relations are proved similarly.
Note that in the second (resp. forth) relation of (
2.10
), we use also the relation

s_{1}-r=s-r_{1}
(resp.
s-s_{1}=r_{1}-r
).
Substituting relations (
2.10
) into (
2.8
) we obtain the
required formula (
2.9
) for
N(r,s)
. ∎

Remark 2.5
.

Consider calculating structure constants for the pairs
\{\alpha,\beta\}
such that
\alpha>0
,
\beta<0
.
If
\alpha+\beta>0
, then by (
2.5
) and (
2.7
), we have

\frac{N(\alpha,\beta)}{\lvert\alpha+\beta\rvert^{2}}\;=\;\frac{N(\beta,-\alpha-\beta)}{\lvert\alpha\rvert^{2}}\;=\;\frac{N(\alpha+\beta,-\beta)}{\lvert\alpha\rvert^{2}}.

(2.11)

In (
2.11

already has two positive roots
as input parameters. If
\alpha+\beta<0

\frac{N(\alpha,\beta)}{\lvert\alpha+\beta\rvert^{2}}\;=\;\frac{N(-\alpha-\beta,\alpha)}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{2}}.

(2.12)

In (

as input parameters.

Lemma 3.1

(H72, Ch.10.2)
.

If
\alpha
is a positive but non-simple root in
\varPhi
, then
\alpha-\beta

is a positive root for some simple root
\beta
. ∎

Corollary 3.2
.

(i)
For any positive non-simple root
\alpha\in\varPhi
, there exist unique simple root
\beta

such that
\{\beta,\alpha-\beta\}
is the extraspecial pair.

(ii)
Each of the root systems
B_{n}
and
C_{n}
has
n^{2}-n
extraspecial pairs.

Proof.
(i) If both
\beta
and
\alpha-\beta
are simple then such a pair is unique for given
\alpha
.
Now, let
\alpha-\beta
be a non-simple root.
The simple root
\beta
precedes to
\alpha-\beta
, since
{\rm ht}(\beta)<{\rm ht}(\alpha-\beta)
.
Let
\{\beta_{1},\alpha-\beta_{1}\}
and
\{\beta_{2},\alpha-\beta_{2}\}
be two special pairs,
where
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
are simple.
If
i
(resp.
j
) is the non-zero coordinate of
\beta_{1}
(resp.
\beta_{2}
) and
i<j

then
\beta_{1}\prec\beta_{2}
. In addition,
i
th coordinate is the first where

(\alpha-\beta_{1})_{i}<(\alpha-\beta_{2})_{i}
, i.e.,
\alpha-\beta_{2}\prec\alpha-\beta_{1}
.
Thus,
\beta_{1}\prec\beta_{2}\prec\alpha-\beta_{2}\prec\alpha-\beta_{1}
.
Here,
\{\beta_{1},\alpha-\beta_{1}\}
is extraspecial. ∎

(ii) There are
n^{2}
positive roots in each of the root systems
B_{n}
and
C_{n}
.
By (i) each of
B_{n}
and
C_{n}
contains
n^{2}-n
extraspecial pairs,
and for each extraspecial pair the first root is simple. ∎

3.2.
How to get extraspecial pairs?

By Corollary
3.2
the number of extraspecial pairs is equal to the number of
non-simple roots. Build a dictionary, where for any extraspecial pair
\{r_{1},s_{1}\}
,
an entry with a key equal to the root
r_{1}+s_{1}
will be made.
The entry corresponding
key=\gamma
is the list of all special pairs
\{r,s\}

such that
r+s=\gamma
.

Assigning structure constants to extraspecial pairs will be made using the Chevalley relation (
2.2
).
If
s_{1}-r_{1}
is not a root, we set
N(s_{1},r_{1}):=1
. Else, if

s_{1}-r_{1}
is a root, but
s_{1}-2r_{1}
is not a root, we set
N(s_{1},r_{1}):=2
.
At last, if
s_{1}-r_{1}
is a root and
s_{1}-2r_{1}
is a root, we assign
N(s_{1},r_{1}):=3
.

See examples of extraspecial pairs and quartets for
B_{6}
,
C_{6}
and
F_{4}

in Tables
A.4
,
A.5
and
A.6
.

3.3.
Extraspecial pairs and quartets in
B_{n}

Let
\{r_{1},s_{1}\}
be an extraspecial pair in
B_{n}
.
For a simple root
r_{1}
there are two possible cases:

\begin{split}(1)&\;\;r_{1}\text{ is one of }\beta_{i,i},\;i=1,\dots,n-1.\text{ Then }\lvert r_{1}\rvert=\sqrt{2}.\\
(2)&\;\;r_{1}\text{ is }\varepsilon_{n}.\text{ Then }\lvert r_{1}\rvert=1.\end{split}

Proposition 3.3

(Case
B_{n}
,
\lvert r_{1}\rvert=\sqrt{2}
)
.

Consider the root system
B_{n}
.

(i)
For any extraspecial pair
\{r_{1},s_{1}\}
with
\lvert r_{1}\rvert=\sqrt{2}
, we have
(r_{1},s_{1})=-1
.

(ii)
Let
q=\{r_{1},r,s,s_{1}\}
be a quartet with
\lvert r_{1}\rvert=\sqrt{2}
.
Then,
q
is a mono-quartet.

Proof.
(i) In the root system
B_{n}
, the inner product of two non-orthogonal roots
can be only
\pm{1}
. For
r_{1}\in\{\beta_{1,1},\dots,\beta_{n-1,n-1}\}
,
the length of
\lvert r_{1}\rvert
is
\sqrt{2}
. Then,

(r_{1},s_{1})=(r_{1},r_{1}+s_{1})-2<0,\text{ i.e. }(r_{1},s_{1})=-1.

(ii) Suppose that both
s-r_{1}
and
r-r_{1}
are roots. Let us show, that in this case

(s,r_{1})=\pm{1}
, and
(r,r_{1})=\pm{1}
.
Really, if
s\perp r_{1}
, then

\lvert s-r_{1}\rvert^{2}=\lvert s\rvert^{2}+\lvert r_{1}\rvert^{2}=\lvert s\rvert^{2}+2,

which contradicts relation
\lvert s-r_{1}\rvert^{2}\leq 2
. So,
(s,r_{1})=\pm{1}
,
and, similarly,
(r,r_{1})=\pm{1}
. The inner product
(r+s,r_{1})
takes the values
0
or
\pm{2}
.
Because
r_{1}+s_{1}=r+s
, we have
(r_{1}+s_{1},r_{1})=2+(r_{1},s_{1})
. Thus,

(r_{1},s_{1})
is
0
or
-2
, which contradicts (i). ∎

Proposition 3.4

(Case
B_{n}
,
r_{1}=\varepsilon_{n}
)
.

In the root system
B_{n}
,
there are two extraspecial pairs
\{r_{1},s_{1}\}

with
r_{1}=\varepsilon_{n}
. In both cases, there is no
other special pair
\{r,s\}
such that
r+s=r_{1}+s_{1}
,
In other words, there is no quartet with
r_{1}=\varepsilon_{n}
.

Proof.
Let us find the roots
s_{1}
that, together with
r_{1}
, can form an extraspecial pair
\{r_{1},s_{1}\}
.
There are only two possible candidates for extraspecial pairs containing
\varepsilon_{n}
:

(1) Let
\{r_{1},s_{1}\}=\{\varepsilon_{n},\varepsilon_{i}\}
, where
i<n
. For
1\leq i\leq n-2
,

\begin{array}[]{l}\begin{matrix}r_{1}+s_{1}=\varepsilon_{n}+\varepsilon_{i}=&\{0&\dots&0&1&\}+\\
&\end{matrix}\begin{matrix}&\{0&\dots&0&1&\dots&1\}=\\
&&&&i\end{matrix}\begin{matrix}&\{0&\dots&0&1&\dots&1&1&2\}.\\
&&&&i\end{matrix}\end{array}

In this case, there is another expansion:

\begin{array}[]{l}\begin{matrix}r_{1}+s_{1}=\beta_{i,i}+\alpha_{i+1,n}=\{0\dots 0&1&0\dots 0\}+\\
&i&\\
\end{matrix}\begin{matrix}\{0\dots&0&1&1\dots 12\}.\\
&i&\\
\end{matrix}\end{array}

(3.1)

So,
\beta_{i,i}\prec\varepsilon_{n}\prec\varepsilon_{i}\prec\alpha_{i+1,n}
.
Therefore, the pair
\{r_{1},s_{1}\}=\{\varepsilon_{n},\varepsilon_{i}\}
is not
extraspecial.

For
i=n-1
, we have the following pair

\{\varepsilon_{n},\varepsilon_{n-1}\}
, which is extraspecial:

\begin{array}[]{l}\begin{matrix}r_{1}+s_{1}=\varepsilon_{n}+\varepsilon_{n-1}=\{0\dots 0&1\}+&\{0&\dots&0&1&1\}.\\
\end{matrix}\end{array}

(3.2)

However, there is no another pair
\{r,s\}
such that
r+s=\varepsilon_{n}+\varepsilon_{n-1}
.

(2) Let
\{r_{1},s_{1}\}=\{\varepsilon_{n},\beta_{i,n-1}\}
, where
i<n
.
By (
3.1
) we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\begin{matrix}r_{1}+s_{1}=\varepsilon_{n}+\beta_{i,n-1}=\{0\dots 0&1\}+&\{0&\dots 0&1&\dots&1&0\}.\\
&&&&i\\
\end{matrix}\end{array}

As above in (
3.1
), for
i=1,\dots,n-2
,
the root
\varepsilon_{n}+\varepsilon_{i}
has another expansion:

\begin{array}[]{l}\begin{matrix}r_{1}+s_{1}=\beta_{i,i}+\varepsilon_{i+1}=\{0\dots 0&1&0\dots 0\}+\{0\dots&0&1&\dots 1\}.\\
&i&&i&\\
\end{matrix}\end{array}

Here,
\beta_{i,i}\prec\varepsilon_{n}\prec\beta_{i,n-1}\prec\varepsilon_{i+1}
.
Again, the pair
\{\varepsilon_{n},\beta_{i,n-1}\}
is not extraspecial.

For
s_{1}=\beta_{n-1,n-1}
, we get an extraspecial pair
\{\beta_{n-1,n-1},\varepsilon_{n}\}

\begin{array}[]{l}\begin{matrix}r_{1}+s_{1}=\beta_{n-1,n-1}+\varepsilon_{n}=\{0\dots 0&1&0\}+&\{0&\dots&0&1\}.\\
\end{matrix}\end{array}

(3.3)

However, there is no another pair
\{r,s\}
such that

.
Thus, there are only two extraspecial pairs containing
\varepsilon_{n}
, see (
3.2
)
and (

).
∎

To summarize, we get the following

Theorem 3.5
.

In the root system
B_{n}
, any quartet is a mono-quartet.
Thus,
B_{n}
is of the Vavilov type.

It turns out that we can say something more precise about the lengths of

Theorem 3.6
.

In the root system
B_{n}
, any quartet
\{r_{1},r,s,s_{1}\}

.

Let

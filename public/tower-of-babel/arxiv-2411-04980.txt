Quantum-limited imaging of a nanomechanical resonator with a spatial mode sorter
M. E. Choi, C. M. Pluchar, W. He, S. Guha, D. J. Wilson

Abstract
We explore the use of a spatial mode sorter to image a nanomechanical resonator, with the goal of studying the quantum limits of active imaging and extending the toolbox for optomechanical force sensing. In our experiment, we reflect a Gaussian laser beam from a vibrating nanoribbon and pass the reflected beam through a commercial spatial mode demultiplexer (Cailabs Proteus). The intensity in each demultiplexed channel depends on the mechanical mode shapes and encodes information about their displacement amplitudes. As a concrete demonstration, we monitor the angular displacement of the ribbon’s fundamental torsion mode by illuminating in the fundamental Hermite-Gauss mode (HG 00 ) and reading out in the HG 10 mode. We show that this technique permits readout of the ribbon’s torsional vibration with a precision near the quantum limit. Our results highlight new opportunities at the interface of quantum imaging and quantum optomechanics.



Spatial mode demultiplexing (SPADE) has emerged as a powerful tool for extreme imaging applications ranging from exoplanet detection
Norris et al. 2020
to superresolution microscopy
Tsang 2017
. Motivations include the advance of low-cross-talk commercial technologies based on multi-plane light conversion (MPLC)
Fontaine et al. 2019
and the identification of protocols for traditional low-light imaging applications (e.g. ground-based telescopy), in which SPADE arises as an optimally efficient receiver
Rouvière et al. 2023
;
Grace and Guha 2022
.

A canonical application of SPADE is tracking small distortions (such as displacement) of a laser beam
Defienne et al. 2024
. As opposed to traditional cameras, which decode information in a pixel basis, SPADE receivers sort in a natural paraxial basis such as the Laguerre- or Hermite-Gauss (HG) modes
Delaubert et al. 2006
. The reduced modal support (typically
\sim
10) of this approach comes with the advantage of higher bandwidth and access to quantum-limited detection of the sorted light, including at the single photon level
Santamaria et al. 2024
. These trade-offs form the basis for recent quantum-enhanced
Grenapin et al. 2023
;
Padilla et al. 2024
or inspired
Lee et al. 2022
;
Tan et al. 2023
SPADE protocols using classical
Lee et al. 2022
;
Tan et al. 2023
and non-classical
Grenapin et al. 2023
;
Padilla et al. 2024
light.

Here we consider SPADE for dynamic imaging of a nanomechanical resonator, a measurement task rooted in practical applications
Rugar and Hansma 1990
;
Sader et al. 2024
and fundamental experiments
Aspelmeyer et al. 2014
, whose implementation has close parallels to laser beam tracking. To our knowledge, MPLC-based tracking of a nanomechanical resonator has not been reported in the literature; however, a recent study applied photonic-integrated SPADE to dynamic imaging of a levitated nanoparticle
Dinter et al. 2024
. Both studies are timely, given the emergence of ultracoherent vacuum-levitated
Gonzalez-Ballestero et al. 2021
and tether nanomechanical resonators
Engelsen et al. 2024
for which the use of “cavity-free" multi-mode imaging techniques is approaching quantum limits
Militaru et al. 2022
;
Hao and Purdy 2024
. In these experiments, the resonator couples different spatial modes of a laser field, and the readout task becomes similar to spatial mode sorting.

The observed high quantum efficiency and absolute sensitivity of our SPADE-based OL is independent of the small mass and high quality factor of the nanoribbon resonator. Their combination signals access to spatio-temporal radiation pressure backaction (Eq.
2

Pluchar et al. 2024a
.

To highlight this potential, a key figure of merit is the ratio of imprecision to the zero-point displacement spectral density of the oscillator, corresponding to the added measurement noise at the Standard Quantum Limit
Teufel et al. 2009
. For the
x=50\;\mu\mathrm{m}
measurement in Fig.
3

S_{\theta}^{\mathrm{ZP}}=\hbar Q_{\mathrm{m}}/(2I\omega_{\mathrm{m}}^{2})=9\times 10^{-20}\;\mathrm{rad}^{2}/\sqrt{\mathrm{Hz}}
, corresponding to a phonon-equivalent imprecision and quantum backaction of
n_{\mathrm{imp}}\equiv S_{\theta}^{\mathrm{imp}}/2S_{\theta}^{\mathrm{ZP}}=0.003
and
n_{\mathrm{BA}}=(16n_{\mathrm{imp}}\eta)^{-1}\approx 150

Looking forward, we envision feedback cooling as one element in a broader program to improve the performance of SPADE-based displacement sensing, explore its radiation pressure quantum limits using nanomechanical resonators, and apply these findings to fundamental experiments and practical applications.

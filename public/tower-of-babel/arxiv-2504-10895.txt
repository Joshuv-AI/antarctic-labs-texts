1.
Introduction

For each
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
, we consider the Laguerre differential operator

\displaystyle L_{\nu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big[-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}+x_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{x_{i}^{2}}\Big(\nu_{i}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\Big)\Big],\quad\quad x\in(0,\infty)^{n}.

The
j
-th partial derivative associated with
L_{\nu}
is given by

\delta_{\nu_{j}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+x_{j}-\frac{1}{x_{j}}\Big(\nu_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\Big).

Then the adjoint of
\delta_{\nu_{j}}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is

\delta_{\nu_{j}}^{*}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+x_{j}-\frac{1}{x_{j}}\Big(\nu_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\Big).

It is straightforward that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\nu_{i}}^{*}\delta_{\nu_{i}}=L_{\nu}{-2}(|\nu|+n).

Let
k=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,\ldots\}
, and
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
be multi-indices. The Laguerre function
\varphi_{k}^{\nu}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
is defined as

\varphi_{k}^{\nu}(x)=\varphi^{\nu_{1}}_{k_{1}}(x_{1})\ldots\varphi^{\nu_{n}}_{k_{n}}(x_{n}),\quad x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

where
\varphi^{\nu_{i}}_{k_{i}}
are the one-dimensional Laguerre functions

\varphi^{\nu_{i}}_{k_{i}}(x_{i})=\Big(\frac{2\Gamma(k_{i}+1)}{\Gamma(k_{i}+\nu_{i}+1)}\Big)^{1/2}L_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}(x_{i}^{2})x_{i}^{\nu_{i}+1/2}e^{-x_{i}^{2}/2},\quad x_{i}>0,\quad i=1,\ldots,n,

given
\nu>-1
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
L_{\nu}^{k}
denotes the Laguerre polynomial of degree
k
and order
\nu
outlined in
[
13
, p.76]
.

Then it is well-known that the system
\{\varphi_{k}^{\nu}:k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},dx)
. Moreover, Each
\varphi_{k}^{\nu}
is an eigenfunction of
L_{\nu}
corresponding to the eigenvalue of
4|k|+2|\nu|+2n
, i.e.,

(1)

L_{\nu}\varphi_{k}^{\nu}=(4|k|+2|\nu|+2n)\varphi_{k}^{\nu},

where
|\nu|=\nu_{1}+\ldots+\nu_{n}
and
|k|=k_{1}+\ldots+k_{n}
. The operator
L_{\nu}
is positive and symmetric in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},dx)
.

If
\nu=\left(-\frac{1}{2},\ldots,-\frac{1}{2}\right)
, then
L_{\nu}
becomes the harmonic oscillator
-\Delta+|x|^{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. Note that the Riesz transforms related to the harmonic oscillator
-\Delta+|x|^{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
were investigated in
[
23
,
24
]
.

The operator

\mathcal{L}_{\nu}f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}(4|k|+2|\nu|+2n)\langle f,\varphi_{k}^{\nu}\rangle\varphi_{k}^{\nu}

defined on the domain
\text{Dom}\,\mathcal{L}_{\nu}=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}(4|k|+2|\nu|+2n)|\langle f,\varphi_{k}^{\nu}\rangle|^{2}<\infty\}
is a self-adjoint extension of
L_{\nu}
(the inclusion
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\subset\text{Dom}\,\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
may be easily verified), has the discrete spectrum
\{4\ell+2|\nu|+2n:\ell\in\mathbb{N}\}
, and admits the spectral decomposition

\mathcal{L}_{\nu}f=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(4\ell+2|\nu|+2n)P_{\nu,\ell}f,

where the spectral projections are

P_{\nu,\ell}f=\sum_{|k|=\ell}\langle f,\varphi_{k}^{\nu}\rangle\varphi_{k}^{\nu}.

Moreover,

(2)

\delta_{\nu_{j}}\varphi_{k}^{\nu}=-2\sqrt{k_{j}}\varphi_{k-e_{j}}^{\nu+e_{j}},\ \ \delta_{\nu_{j}}^{*}\varphi_{k}^{\nu}=-2\sqrt{k_{j}+1}\varphi_{k+e_{j}}^{\nu-e_{j}},

where
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
is the standard basis for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Here and later on we use the convention that
\varphi_{k-e_{j}}^{\nu+e_{j}}=0
if
k_{j}-1<0
and
\varphi_{k+e_{j}}^{\nu-e_{j}}=0
if
\nu_{j}-1<0
. See for example
[
20
]
.

The primary goal of this paper is to establish the boundedness of the higher-order Riesz transform

\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}^{n},

for the full parameter range
\nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
, where

\delta_{\nu}^{k}=\delta_{\nu_{n}}^{k_{n}}\cdots\delta_{\nu_{1}}^{k_{1}}.

The study of Riesz transforms in the setting of orthogonal expansions dates back to the work of Muckenhoupt and Stein
[
17
]
. Since then, substantial progress has been made, particularly in the context of Riesz transforms associated with Laguerre and Hermite operators. For further developments, see
[
4
,
5
,
16
,
20
,
21
,
19
,
15
,
14
,
24
]
and the references therein.

In the case
n=1
, higher-order Riesz transforms were investigated in
[
4
]
for
\nu>-1
. However, the techniques employed there do not work for the higher dimension case. For
n\geq 2
, prior studies have focused only on first-order Riesz transforms of the form

\delta_{\nu_{j}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2},\quad j=1,\ldots,n.

Specifically, it was shown in
[
20
]
that these transforms are Calderón-Zygmund operators under the condition
\nu_{j}\in\{-1/2\}\cup(1/2,\infty)
. This result was later extended by the first author to
\nu_{j}\in[-1/2,\infty)
in
[
6
]
.

The study of higher-order Riesz transforms in dimensions
n\geq 2
is considerably more challenging due to several factors. Firstly, while the
L^{2}
-boundedness of first-order Riesz transforms
\delta_{\nu_{j}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}
follows naturally from spectral theory, establishing the
L^{2}
-boundedness of higher-order Riesz transforms
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
for
|k|\geq 2
is significantly more involved. Secondly, obtaining kernel estimates for these operators requires precise control over the higher-order derivatives of the heat kernel associated with
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
, which remains an underdeveloped area in the literature.

In the one-dimensional case
n=1
, power-weighted estimates for higher-order Riesz transforms were obtained in
[
4
]
for
\nu\in(-1,\infty)
. Their approach relied on a comparison with the corresponding higher-order Riesz transform of the Hermite operator in
\mathbb{R}
. More recently, the first author and X. T. Duong demonstrated that in the higher-dimensional case
n\geq 1
, the higher-order Riesz transforms are Calderón-Zygmund operators for
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
; see
[
8
]
.

Motivated by the above research, we aim to establish that the boundedness of the higher-order Riesz transforms
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
for the full range
\nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
. Our results not only extend those in
[
20
,
6
,
5
,
8
]
but also provide complete the description of the boundedness properties of higher-order Riesz transforms.

In order to state our main results, we introduce some notation. For
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
, we set

(3)

\gamma_{\nu}=\max\{\gamma_{\nu_{j}}:j=1,\ldots,n\}

where

(4)

\gamma_{\nu_{j}}=\begin{cases}-1/2-\nu_{j},\ \ &\ \text{if}\ -1<\nu_{j}<-1/2,\\
0,\ \ &\ \text{if}\ \nu_{j}\geq-1/2\end{cases}

for
j=1,\ldots,n
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
. We define
\sigma(k)=(\sigma(k)_{1},\ldots,\sigma(k)_{n})
, where

\sigma(k)_{j}=\begin{cases}1,\ \ &\text{if $k_{j}$ is odd};\\
0,\ \ \ &\text{if $k_{j}$ is even},\end{cases}

for
j=1,\ldots,n
.

The following theorem is the main result of this paper. For the definitions of the weight classes
A_{p}
and
RH_{q}
, we refer to Section 2.1.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
. Then the Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for all
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}
and
w\in A_{p(1-\gamma_{\nu})}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{p\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}})^{\prime}}
with the convention
\frac{1}{0}=\infty
.

Before giving some comments on Theorem
1.1
, we would like to introduce the following two maximal functions

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f(x)=\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f(x)|

and

\mathcal{M}_{k,\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f(x)=\sup_{t>0}|\delta_{\nu}^{k}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f(x)|,\ \ \ k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}.

By using the kernel estimates in Proposition
3.2
, Theorem
3.10
and Proposition
3.15
, it is straightforward that the maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
, while the maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{k,\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}
. Note that
\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}\leq\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}
.

We turn back to Theorem
1.1
. In the case
n=1
, we have
\sigma(k)=1
as
k
is odd and
\sigma(k)=0
as
k
is even. Hence, Theorem
1.1
reads:

•

if
k
is even, the Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
with
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
and
w\in A_{p(1-\gamma_{\nu})}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{p\gamma_{\nu}})^{\prime}}
;

•

if
k
is odd, then
\gamma_{\nu+1}=0
and hence Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<1
and
w\in A_{p(1-\gamma_{\nu})}
.

Our result is still new even in the case
n=1
, since Theorem
1.1
provides weighted estimates with Muckenhoupt weights instead of power weights as in
[
4
]
. When
k
is even, the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
holds for
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
, which is similar to the boundedness range of the maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
. However, when
k
is odd, the Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
for
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\infty
. Surprisingly, in this case, the range of
p
is larger than the range
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
for the boundedness of the maximal function and the Riesz transforms when
k
is even. However, the boundedness range of the Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
coincides with that of the maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{k,\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
. One possible explanation for this phenomenon is that we obtain better estimates on the higher-order derivatives of the heat kernel when
k
is odd. See Theorem
3.5
.

For the case
n\geq 2
, since
\gamma_{\nu_{j}+1}=0
for
\nu_{j}\in(-1,\infty)
, we have

\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}=\max\{\gamma_{\nu_{j}}:k_{j}\text{ is even}\}.

Hence, we conclude the following:

•

If all entries of
k
are even, then
\sigma(k)=0
, and the Riesz transform is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
for
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
and
w\in A_{p(1-\gamma_{\nu})}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{p\gamma_{\nu}})^{\prime}}
. In this case, the ranges of
p
for the boundedness of the Riesz transform and the maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
are the same.

•

If all entries of
k
are odd, then
\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}=0
. In this case, the Riesz transform is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
for
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\infty
and
w\in A_{p(1-\gamma_{\nu})}
, and the range of
p
is larger than that for the boundedness of the maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
.

•

If at least one entry of
k
is odd, then we have
\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}\leq\gamma_{\nu}
. Theorem
1.1
tells us that in this case, the range of
p
for the boundedness of the Riesz transform is larger than that for the boundedness of the maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
.

Some comments on the techniques used in the paper are in order. Firstly, the approach in
[
4
]
for the case
n=1
relies on the difference between the kernels of the Riesz transform associated with Laguerre expansions and the Riesz transform associated with Hermite operators. However, this approach might not be applicable to the higher-dimensional case
n\geq 2
or for Muckenhoupt weights.

Secondly, although the higher-order Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
in the case
n\geq 2
has been studied in
[
8
]
, the restriction
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
is less challenging since the higher-order Riesz transform is a Calderón-Zygmund operator. Consequently, the boundedness of the Riesz transform does not depend on
k
.

To handle the full range
\nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
, new ideas and techniques are required. In this paper, we employ improved estimates for the derivatives of heat kernels. See, for example, Theorem
3.5
and Proposition
3.6
. Additionally, since the Riesz transform is not a Calderón-Zygmund operator, it is natural to develop and apply techniques from the theory of singular integrals beyond the Calderón-Zygmund setting. For this direction, we refer to
[
10
,
1
,
2
,
3
]
and the references therein.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall the definitions of Muckenhoupt weights, some elementary estimates and a theorem regarding the boundedness criteria of singular integrals beyond the Calderón-Zygmund theory. Section 3 is dedicated to proving kernel estimates related to the heat semigroup
e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
. Section 4 is devoted to the proofs of Theorem
1.1
.

C
and

to denote positive constants that are independent of the main parameters involved but whose values may differ from line to line. We will write
A\lesssim B
if there is a universal constant
C
so that
A\leq CB
and
A\sim B
if
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
. For

, we denote the integer part of
a
by
\lfloor a\rfloor
. For a given ball

to denote the center and

for the radius of the ball.

In the whole paper, we will often use the following inequality without any explanation

for any

and

.

2.
Preliminaries

We start with some notations which will be used frequently. For a measurable subset
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and a measurable function
f
we denote

\fint_{E}f(x)dx=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}f(x)dx.

Given a ball
B
, we denote
S_{j}(B)=2^{j}B\backslash 2^{j-1}B
for
j=1,2,3,\ldots
, and we set
S_{0}(B)=B
.

Let
1\leq q<\infty
. A nonnegative locally integrable function
w
belongs to the Muckenhoupt class
A_{q}
, say
w\in A_{q}
, if there exists a positive constant
C
so that

\Big(\fint_{B}w(x)dx\Big)\Big(\fint_{B}w^{-1/(q-1)}(x)dx\Big)^{q-1}\leq C,\quad\mbox{if}\;1<q<\infty

and

\fint_{B}w(x)dx\leq C\mathop{\mbox{ess-inf}}\limits_{x\in B}w(x),\quad{\rm if}\;q=1,

for all balls
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We define
A_{\infty}=\bigcup_{1\leq p<\infty}A_{p}
.

The reverse Hölder classes are defined in the following way:
w\in RH_{r},1<r<\infty
, if there is a constant
C
such that for
any ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
,

\Big(\fint_{B}w^{r}(x)dx\Big)^{1/r}\leq C\fint_{B}w(x)dx.

The endpoint
r=\infty
is given by the condition:
w\in RH_{\infty}
whenever, there is a constant
C
such that for any ball

B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
,

w(x)\leq C\fint_{B}w(y)dy\ \text{for a.e. $x\in B$}.

For
w\in A_{\infty}
and
0<p<\infty
, the weighted space
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is defined as the space of
w(x)dx
-measurable functions
f
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}:=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\Big)^{1/p}<\infty.

It is well-known that the power weight
w(x)=x^{\alpha}\in A_{p}
if and only if
-n<\alpha<n(p-1)
. Moreover,
w(x)=x^{\alpha}\in RH_{q}
if and only if
\alpha q>-n
.

We sum up some of the properties of Muckenhoupt classes and reverse Hölder classes in the following
results. See
[
9
,
12
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The following properties hold:

(i)

w\in A_{p},1<p<\infty
if and only if
w^{1-p^{\prime}}\in A_{p^{\prime}}
.

(ii)

A_{1}\subset A_{p}\subset A_{q}
for
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
.

(iii)

RH_{q}\subset RH_{p}
for
1<p\leq q<\infty
.

(iv)

If
w\in A_{p},1<p<\infty
, then there exists
1<q<p
such that
w\in A_{q}
.

(v)

If
w\in RH_{q},1<q<\infty
, then there exists
q<p<\infty
such that
w\in RH_{p}
.

(vi)

A_{\infty}=\cup_{1\leq p<\infty}A_{p}=\cup_{1<p<\infty}RH_{p}
.

(vii)

Let
1<p_{0}<p<q_{0}<\infty
. Then we have

w\in A_{\frac{p}{p_{0}}}\cap RH_{(\frac{q_{0}}{p})^{\prime}}\Longleftrightarrow w^{1-p^{\prime}}\in A_{\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}_{0}}}\cap RH_{(\frac{p^{\prime}_{0}}{p^{\prime}})^{\prime}}.

For
r>0
, the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{r}
is defined by

\mathcal{M}_{r}f(x)=\sup_{B\ni x}\Big(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f(y)|^{r}\,dy\Big)^{1/r},\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B
containing
x
. When
r=1
, we write
\mathcal{M}
instead of
\mathcal{M}_{1}
.

We now record the following results concerning the weak type estimates and the weighted estimates of the maximal functions.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
22
]
)
.

Let
0<r<\infty
. Then we have for
p>r
,

\|\mathcal{M}_{r}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

We will end this section by some simple estimates whose proofs will be omitted. See for example
[
7
, Eq. (35)]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For
a\in[0,1/2)
and
c>0
, define

H_{t,a,c}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{a}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{a}

for
t>0
and
x,y>0
.

Then we have

(5)

\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{t,a,c}(x,z)H_{t,a,c}(z,y)dz\lesssim H_{t,a,4c}(x,y)

for
t>0
and
x,y>0
.

We now recall a theorem which is taken from
[
3
, Theorem 6.6]
on a criterion for the singular integrals to be bounded on the weighted Lebesgue spaces.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
1\leq p_{0}<q_{0}<\infty
and let
T
be a linear operator. Assume that
T
can be extended to be bounded on
L^{q_{0}}
. Assume that there exists a family of operators
\{\mathcal{A}_{t}\}_{t>0}
satisfying that for
j\geq 2
and every ball
B

(6)

\Big(\fint_{S_{j}(B)}|T(I-\mathcal{A}_{r_{B}})f|^{q_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}}\leq\alpha(j)\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{q_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}},

and

(7)

\Big(\fint_{S_{j}(B)}|\mathcal{A}_{r_{B}}f|^{q_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}}\leq\alpha(j)\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{p_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/p_{0}},

for all
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
supported in
B
. If
\sum_{j}\alpha(j)2^{jd}<\infty
, then
T
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for all
p\in(p_{0},q_{0})
and
w\in A_{\frac{p}{p_{0}}}\cap RH_{(\frac{q_{0}}{p})^{\prime}}
.

Note that
[
3
, Theorem 6.6]
proves Theorem
2.4
for
q_{0}=2
, but their arguments also work well for any value
q_{0}\in(1,\infty)
.

3.
Some kernel estimates and
L^{p}
-boundedness of integral operators

3.1.
Some kernel estimates

This section is devoted to establishing some kernel estimates related to the heat kernel of
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
. These estimates play an essential role in proving our main results. We begin by providing an explicit formula for the heat kernel of
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
.

Let
\nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
. For each
j=1,\ldots,n
, similarly to
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
, denote by
\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{j}}
the self-adjoint extension of the differential operator

L_{\nu_{j}}:=-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}+x_{j}^{2}+\frac{1}{x_{j}^{2}}(\nu_{j}^{2}-\frac{1}{4})

on
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
as the natural domain. It is easy to see that

\mathcal{L}_{\nu}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{j}}.

Let
p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)
be the kernel of
e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
and let
p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j})
be the kernel of
e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{j}}}
for each
j=1,\ldots,n
. Then we have

(8)

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j}).

For
\nu_{j}\geq-1/2
,
j=1,\ldots,n,
, the kernel of
e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{j}}}
is given by

(9)

p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j})=\frac{2(rx_{j}y_{j})^{1/2}}{1-r}\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1+r}{1-r}(x_{j}^{2}+y_{j}^{2})\Big)I_{\nu_{j}}\Big(\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}x_{j}y_{j}\Big),

where
r=e^{-4t}
and
I_{\alpha}
is the usual Bessel funtions of an imaginary argument defined by

I_{\alpha}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Big(\frac{z}{2}\Big)^{\alpha+2k}}{k!\Gamma(\alpha+k+1)},\ \ \ \ \alpha>-1.

See for example
[
11
,
20
]
.

Note that for each
j=1,\ldots,n
, we can rewrite the kernel
p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j})
as follows

(10)

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j})=\frac{2(rx_{j}y_{j})^{1/2}}{1-r}

\displaystyle\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1+r}{1-r}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{2}\Big)\exp\Big(-\frac{1-r^{1/2}}{1+r^{1/2}}x_{j}y_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\exp\Big(-\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}x_{j}y_{j}\Big)I_{\nu_{j}}\Big(\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}x_{j}y_{j}\Big),

where
r=e^{-4t}
.

The following properties of the Bessel function

with
\alpha>-1
are well-known and are taken from

(11)

I_{\alpha}(z)\sim z^{\alpha},\ \ \ 0<z\leq 1,

(12)

I_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{e^{z}}{\sqrt{2\pi z}}+S_{\alpha}(z),

where

(13)

|S_{\alpha}(z)|\leq Ce^{z}z^{-3/2},\ \ z\geq 1,

(14)

\frac{d}{dz}(z^{-\alpha}I_{\alpha}(z))=z^{-\alpha}I_{\alpha+1}(z)

and

(15)

0<I_{\alpha}(z)-I_{\alpha+2}(z)=\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{z}I_{\alpha+1}(z),\ \ \ z>0.

Let
\nu>-1
, we have

I_{\alpha}(z)-I_{\alpha+1}(z)=[I_{\alpha}(z)-I_{\alpha+2}(z)]-[I_{\alpha+1}(z)-I_{\alpha+2}(z)].

Applying (

I_{\alpha}(z)-I_{\alpha+2}(z)=\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{z}I_{\alpha+1}(z).

Hence,

|I_{\alpha}(z)-I_{\alpha+1}(z)|\leq\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{z}I_{\alpha+1}(z)+|I_{\alpha+1}(z)-I_{\alpha+2}(z)|.

On the other hand, since

0<I_{\alpha+1}(z)-I_{\alpha+2}(z)<2(\alpha+2)\frac{I_{\alpha+2}(z)}{z}<2(\alpha+2)\frac{I_{\alpha+1}(z)}{z},\ \ \ z>0.

See for example
[

Consequently,

(16)

|I_{\alpha}(z)-I_{\alpha+1}(z)|<(4\alpha+6)\frac{I_{\alpha+1}(z)}{z},\ \ \ \alpha>-1,z>0.

Particularly, in the case

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\nu>-1
. Then for any

\frac{1}{x^{k}}|\delta_{\nu+k}p_{t}^{\nu+k}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{(k+2)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

15
) and (

\frac{1}{x^{k}}|\delta_{\nu+k}p_{t}^{\nu+k}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{tx^{k-1}}p_{t}^{\nu+k}(x,y)+\frac{|y-x|}{tx^{k}}p_{t}^{\nu+k+1}(x,y)=:E_{1}+E_{2}.

Using (
19

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{x^{k}}\frac{|x-y|}{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{-(\nu+k+3/2)}

) and (

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Define

T_{\alpha}f(x)=\int_{x/2}^{2x}K_{\alpha}(x,y)f(y)dy,

where

K_{\alpha}(x,y)=\frac{1}{x}\Big(\frac{x}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\alpha}

Then
T_{\alpha}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for all
\frac{1}{1-\alpha}<p<\infty
.

Proof.

Fix
p\in(\frac{1}{1-\alpha},\infty)
and
\frac{1}{1-\alpha}<r<p
. Then we have
\alpha r^{\prime}<1
. This, together with Hölder’s inequality, implies

\displaystyle T_{\alpha}f(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{x/2}^{2x}\frac{1}{x}\Big(\frac{x}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\alpha}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\frac{1}{x}\int_{x/2}^{2x}|f(y)|^{r}dy\Big]^{1/r}\Big[\frac{1}{x}\int_{x/2}^{2x}\Big(\frac{x}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\alpha r^{\prime}}dy\Big]^{1/r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\frac{1}{x}\int_{x/2}^{2x}|f(y)|^{r}dy\Big]^{1/r}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big[\mathcal{M}(|f|^{r})(x)\big]^{1/r}.

Since
\mathcal{M}
is bounded on
L^{q}
for any
q>1
, the above inequality implies that
T_{\alpha}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

This completes our proof.

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in[0,1)
so that
\alpha+\beta<1
. Let the operator
\textbf{S}_{\alpha,\beta}
defined by

\textbf{S}_{\alpha,\beta}f(x):=\sup_{t>0}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}S_{\alpha,\beta}^{t}(x,y)|f(y)|dy\Big|,

where

S^{t}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\alpha}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\beta}.

Then
\textbf{S}_{\alpha,\beta}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for all
\frac{1}{1-\beta}<p<\frac{1}{\alpha}

Proof.

We first claim that

(35)

S^{t}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)+\frac{1}{x}\chi_{\{y/2<x<2y\}}+\frac{1}{x}\Big(\frac{x}{y}\Big)^{\beta}\chi_{\{2y\leq x\}}+\frac{1}{y}\Big(\frac{y}{x}\Big)^{\alpha}\chi_{\{x\leq y/2\}}

for
t>0
and
x,y>0
.

Case 1:
y/2<x<2y

If
x>\sqrt{t}
, then we have

1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\sim 1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\sim 1.

Hence,

S^{t}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big).

If
x\leq\sqrt{t}
, then in this case

1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\sim 1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\sim\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}.

Hence,

\displaystyle S^{t}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\alpha+\beta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\alpha+\beta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{x}.

Consequently,

S^{t}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)\chi_{\{y/2<x<2y\}}\lesssim\Big[\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)+\frac{1}{x}\Big]\chi_{\{y/2<x<2y\}}

Case 2:
x\geq 2y
.
In this case
|x-y|\sim x

If
\sqrt{t}<2y\leq x
, then

S^{t}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}}{ct}\Big)\lesssim\frac{1}{x}.

If
2y\leq\sqrt{t}\leq x
, then

\displaystyle S^{t}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\beta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{x}\Big(\frac{x}{y}\Big)^{\beta}.

If
2y\leq x<\sqrt{t}
, then

\displaystyle S^{t}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\alpha}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\beta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{x}\Big(\frac{x}{y}\Big)^{\beta}.

Consequently,

S^{t}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)\chi_{\{x\geq 2y\}}\lesssim\frac{1}{x}\Big(\frac{x}{y}\Big)^{\beta}\chi_{\{x\geq 2y\}}.

Case 3:
x\leq y/2
.
In this case,
y\geq 2x
and
|x-y|\sim y
.

If
\sqrt{t}<2x\leq y
, then

S^{t}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{y^{2}}{ct}\Big)\lesssim\frac{1}{y}.

If
2x\leq\sqrt{t}\leq y
, then

\displaystyle S^{t}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{y^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{y}\Big(\frac{y}{x}\Big)^{\alpha}.

If
2x\leq y<\sqrt{t}
, then

\displaystyle S^{t}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\alpha}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\beta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{y}\Big(\frac{y}{x}\Big)^{\alpha}.

Consequently,

S^{t}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)\chi_{\{x\leq y/2\}}\lesssim\frac{1}{y}\Big(\frac{y}{x}\Big)^{\alpha}\chi_{\{x\leq y/2\}}.

These estimates confirm (
35
).

We now turn to prove the boundedness of
\textbf{S}_{\alpha,\beta}
. Fix
\frac{1}{1-\beta}<p<\frac{1}{\alpha}
. Due to (
35
),

\displaystyle\textbf{S}_{\alpha,\beta}f(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)|f(y)|dy+\frac{1}{x}\int_{x/2}^{2x}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{x/2}\Big(\frac{x}{y}\Big)^{\beta}|f(y)|dy+\int_{2x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{y}\Big(\frac{y}{x}\Big)^{\alpha}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle=:\textbf{S}^{1}_{\alpha,\beta}f(x)+\textbf{S}^{2}_{\alpha,\beta}f(x)+\textbf{S}^{3}_{\alpha,\beta}f(x)+\textbf{S}^{4}_{\alpha,\beta}f(x).

The standard argument shows that

\textbf{S}^{1}_{\alpha,\beta}f(x)\lesssim\mathcal{M}f(x),

where we recall that
\mathcal{M}
is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Obviously,

\textbf{S}^{2}_{\alpha,\beta}f(x)\lesssim\mathcal{M}f(x).

Fix
p_{1}
such that
\frac{1}{1-\beta}<p_{1}<p<\frac{1}{\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{2x}|f(y)|^{p_{1}}dy\Big)^{1/p_{1}}

.

4.
Weighted estimates for the higher-order Riesz transforms

This section is dedicated to proving Theorem
1.1
. The proof is quite long and complicated and will be done through several steps. We first prove a special case of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
and
a\geq 0
. Suppose that
k=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
such that
k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}\in\{0,1\}
. Then the Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu}+a)^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for all
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}
and
w\in A_{p(1-\gamma_{\nu})}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{p\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}})^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

Note that in this case
k=\sigma(k)
.

It suffices to prove the theorem for
a=0
since the proof for the case
a>0
can be done similarly.

We first prove that the Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. Assume that the first
j
entries of
k
are equal to
1
and other entries are
0
. In this case,
|k|=j
. By (
2
) we have

\displaystyle\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}(4|\alpha|+2|\nu|+2n)^{-k/2}\langle f,\varphi_{\alpha}^{\nu}\rangle\delta_{\nu}^{k}\varphi_{\alpha}^{\nu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\frac{-2^{|k|}\sqrt{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{j}}}{{(4|\alpha|+2|\nu|+2n)^{|k|/2}}}\langle f,\varphi_{\alpha}^{\nu}\rangle\varphi_{\alpha-k}^{\nu+k},

which implies

\|\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}f\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{k}|\langle f,\varphi_{k}^{\nu}\rangle|^{2}=\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

Hence, the Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

We now prove that
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}^{-|k|/2}_{\nu}
is bounded on
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 2
. Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2m-1>n
. In the light of Theorem
2.4
, it suffices to prove that for any
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p_{0}<2
,

(40)

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{S_{j}(B)}|\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}^{-|k|/2}_{\nu}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}f|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}\lesssim 2^{-j(2m-1)}\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}

and

(41)

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{S_{j}(B)}|[I-(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}]f|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}\leq 2^{-j(n+1)}\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{p_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/p_{0}}

for all
j\geq 2
and
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
supported in
B
.

Note that

I-(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}e^{-ir_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}},

where
c_{i}
are constants.

This, along with Theorem
3.5
and Lemma
3.17
, implies (
41
).

It remains to prove (
40
). To do this, recall that

\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}^{-|k|/2}_{\nu}=c\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{|k|/2}\delta_{\nu}^{k}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\frac{dt}{t}

and

(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}=\int_{[0,r_{B}^{2}]^{m}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{m}e^{-(s_{1}+\ldots+s_{m})\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}d\vec{s},

where
d\vec{s}=ds_{1}\ldots ds_{m}
.

Hence, we can write

\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}^{-|k|/2}_{\nu}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}f=c\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{[0,r_{B}^{2}]^{m}}t^{|k|/2}\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{m}e^{-(t+s_{1}+\ldots+s_{m})\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f\,d\vec{s}\,\frac{dt}{t}.

Applying Theorem
3.11
and Lemma
3.17
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}^{-|k|/2}_{\nu}

\displaystyle(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}f\|_{L^{2}(S_{j}(B))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{[0,r_{B}^{2}]^{m}}t^{|k|/2}\big\|\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{m}e^{-(t+s_{1}+\ldots+s_{m})\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f\big\|_{L^{2}(S_{j}(B))}\,d\vec{s}\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(B)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{[0,r_{B}^{2}]^{m}}\frac{t^{|k|/2}}{(t+|\vec{s}|)^{m+|k|/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{(2^{j}r_{B})^{2}}{c(t+|\vec{s}|)}\Big)d\vec{s}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(B)}\int_{0}^{r_{B}^{2}}\ldots\frac{dt}{t}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(B)}\int_{r_{B}^{2}}^{\infty}\ldots\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=:E_{1}+E_{2},

where
|\vec{s}|=s_{1}+\ldots+s_{m}
.

For the first term
E_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(B)}\int_{0}^{r_{B}^{2}}\int_{[0,r_{B}^{2}]^{m}}\frac{t^{|k|/2}}{(t+|\vec{s}|)^{m+|k|/2}}\Big(\frac{t+|\vec{s}|}{(2^{j}r_{B})^{2}}\Big)^{m+|k|/2}d\vec{s}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(B)}\int_{0}^{r_{B}^{2}}\int_{[0,r_{B}^{2}]^{m}}\frac{\sqrt{t}}{(2^{j}r_{B})^{2m+1}}d\vec{s}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\sim\|f\|_{L^{2}(B)}\frac{r_{B}^{2m+1}}{(2^{j}r_{B})^{2m+1}}

\displaystyle\sim 2^{-j(2m+1)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(B)}.

For the second term, note that
t+s_{1}+\ldots+s_{m}\sim t
as
t\geq r_{B}^{2}
and
s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}\in[0,r_{B}^{2}]
. It follows that

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(B)}\int_{r_{B}^{2}}^{\infty}\int_{[0,r_{B}^{2}]^{m}}\frac{\sqrt{t}^{|k|/2}}{t^{m+|k|/2}}\Big(\frac{t}{(2^{j}r_{B})^{2}}\Big)^{m-1/2}d\vec{s}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\sim 2^{-j(2m-1)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(B)}.

Collecting the estimates of
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, we deduce to (
40
).

Next we will prove that
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}^{-|k|/2}_{\nu}
is bounded on
L^{p}
for
2<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}
. By duality, it suffices to prove
(\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}^{-|k|/2}_{\nu})^{*}:=\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}<p_{0}\leq 2
. To do this, fix
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}<p_{0}\leq 2
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2m-1>n
, by Theorem
2.4
, we need to show that

(42)

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{S_{j}(B)}|(\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}^{-|k|/2}_{\nu})^{*}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}+2|k|)})^{m}f|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}\lesssim 2^{-j(2m-1)}\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}

and

(43)

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{S_{j}(B)}|[I-(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}+2|k|)})^{m}]f|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}\leq 2^{-j(n+1)}\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{p_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/p_{0}}

for all
j\geq 2
and
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
supported in
B
.

The estimate (
43
) follows directly from Proposition
3.1
and Lemma
3.17
and the following expansion

I-(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}+2|k|)})^{m}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}c_{k}e^{-kr_{B}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}+2|k|)},

where
c_{k}
are constants.

Hence, we need to prove (
42
). We first note that for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and
\nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
we have, for
t>0
,

\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\frac{e^{-t(4|\ell|+2|\nu|+2n)}}{(4|\ell|+2|\nu|+2n)^{|k|/2}}\langle f,\varphi^{\nu}_{\ell}\rangle\varphi^{\nu}_{\ell}.

Using this and (
2
), by a simple calculation we come up with

\displaystyle e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta^{*})^{k}

\displaystyle(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}+2|k|}))^{m}f

\displaystyle=(\delta^{*})^{k}e^{-t(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}+2|k|)}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}+2|k|)^{-1/2}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}+2|k|}))^{m}f.

At this stage, arguing similarly to the proof of (
40
) we come up with (
42
).

We have just proved that the Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for all
\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}
. To complete the proof, we need to prove the weighted estimate for the Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
. By duality it suffices to show that
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for all
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
and
w\in A_{p(1-\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)})}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{p\gamma_{\nu}})^{\prime}}
. We now fix
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
and
w\in A_{p(1-\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)})}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{p\gamma_{\nu}})^{\prime}}
. By Lemma
2.1
, we can find
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}<p_{0}<p<q_{0}<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
such that
w\in A_{p_{0}(1-\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)})}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{q_{0}\gamma_{\nu}})^{\prime}}
. Similarly to (
42
) and (
43
), by using Theorem
3.10
and Lemma
3.17
we also obtain

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{S_{j}(B)}|\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta^{*})^{k}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}+2|k|)})^{m}f|^{q_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}}\lesssim 2^{-j(2m-1)}\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{q_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}}

and

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{S_{j}(B)}|[I-(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}+2|k|)})^{m}]f|^{{q_{0}}}dx\Big)^{1/{q_{0}}}\leq 2^{-j(n+1)}\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{p_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/p_{0}}

for all
j\geq 2
and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
supported in
B
.

Hence, by Theorem
2.4
,
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta^{*})^{k}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
with
w\in A_{p_{0}(1-\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)})}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{q_{0}\gamma_{\nu}})^{\prime}}
.

This completes our proof.
∎

Secondly, we prove the following result.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
. For
a\geq 0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, the Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu}+a)^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for all
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
and
w\in A_{p(1-\gamma_{\nu})}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{p\gamma_{\nu}})^{\prime}}
.

Note that Theorem
4.2
only gives the sharp estimate as in Theorem
1.1
in the case that all entries of
k
are even. For other case, the estimates in Theorem
4.2
are not sharp. However, if all
\nu_{j}\geq-1/2
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
, the estimates in Theorem
4.2
and Theorem
1.1
are the same since
\gamma_{\nu}=0
in this case. This plays an important role in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.2
:

We first prove that the Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu}+a)^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for all
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
by induction.

\bullet
The statement is true for
|k|=1
due to Theorem

4.1
.

\bullet
Assume that Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu}+a)^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for all
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
for all
|k|\leq\ell
with some
\ell\geq 1
and for all
a\geq 0
. We need to prove the boundedness of
\delta_{\nu}^{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu}+a)^{-|k|/2}
for
|k|=\ell+1
and
a\geq 0
. For the sake of simplicity we might assume
a=0
since the case
a>0
can be done similarly with minor modifications. Indeed, due to Theorem
4.1
, we consider only the case that at lease one entry of
k
is greater than
1
. Without loss of generality, we might assume that
k_{1}\geq 2
. Then, using (
2
) we have

\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}=\delta_{\nu}^{k-e_{1}}\delta_{\nu_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-(|k|-1)/2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}=\delta_{\nu}^{k-e_{1}}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{1}}+2)^{-(|k|-1)/2}\circ\delta_{\nu_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2},

which, together with (
29
), further implies

\displaystyle\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\nu+e_{1}}^{k-e_{1}}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{1}}+2)^{-(|k|-1)/2}\circ\delta_{\nu_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}+\frac{1}{x_{1}}\delta_{\nu+e_{1}}^{k-2e_{1}}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{1}}+2)^{-(|k|-1)/2}\circ\delta_{\nu_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}

By Theorem
4.1
and the facts
\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}\leq\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+e_{1}}}
and
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu+e_{1}}}\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}
(since
\sigma(e_{1})=e_{1}
) and the inductive hypothesis, the Riesz transforms
\delta_{\nu+e_{1}}^{k-e_{1}}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{1}}+2)^{-(|k|-1)/2}
and
\delta_{\nu_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}
are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
. Hence, it suffices to prove that the operator
\displaystyle\frac{1}{x_{1}}\delta_{\nu+e_{1}}^{k-2e_{1}}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{1}}+2)^{-(|k|-1)/2}
for on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
.

Indeed, using the formula

(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{1}}+2)^{-(|k|-1)/2}=c\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{(|k|-1)/2}e^{-2t}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{1}}}\frac{dt}{t},

we have

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{x_{1}}\delta_{\nu_{1}+e_{1}}^{k-2e_{1}}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{1}}+2)^{-(|k|-1)/2}f(x)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x_{1}}t^{(k_{1}-2)/2}|\delta_{\nu_{1}+1}^{k_{1}-2}p_{t}^{\nu_{1}+1}(x_{1},y_{1})|\prod_{i\neq 1}\sup_{t_{i}>0}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|t^{k_{i}/2}\delta_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}p_{t_{i}}^{\nu_{i}}(x_{i},y_{i})||f(y)|d(y_{i})\frac{dt}{t}d{y_{j}}.

From Theorem
3.5
and Propositions
3.1
,
3.15
and
3.16
, we have the following operators

f\mapsto\sup_{t_{i}>0}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|t^{k_{i}/2}\delta_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}p_{t_{i}}^{\nu_{i}}(x_{i},y_{i})||f(y_{i})|d(y_{i}),i\neq 1

and

f\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x_{1}}t^{(k_{1}-2)/2}|\delta_{\nu_{1}+1}^{k_{1}-2}p_{t}^{\nu_{1}+1}(x_{1},y_{1})|f(y_{1})|\frac{dt}{t}dy_{1}

are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
.

It follows that the operator
\displaystyle\frac{1}{x_{1}}\delta_{\nu+e_{1}}^{k-2e_{1}}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{1}}+2)^{-(|k|-1)/2}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
.

Hence, we have proved that the Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu}+a)^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for all
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
.

We now turn to prove the weighted estimates. We need only to prove for the case
a=0
. Fix
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
and
w\in A_{p(1-\gamma_{\nu})}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{p\gamma_{\nu}})^{\prime}}
. Then by Lemma
2.1
, we can find
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p_{0}<p<q_{0}<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
such that
w\in A_{p_{0}(1-\gamma_{\nu})}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{q_{0}\gamma_{\nu}})^{\prime}}
. For
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2m-1>n
, by using the arguments used in the proofs of (
40
) and (
41
), we also obtain that

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{S_{j}(B)}|\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}^{-|k|/2}_{\nu}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}f|^{q_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}}\lesssim 2^{-j(2m-1)}\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{q_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}}

and

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{S_{j}(B)}|[I-(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}]f|^{q_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}}\leq 2^{-j(n+1)}\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{p_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/p_{0}}

for all
j\geq 2
and
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
supported in
B
.

Hence, the conclusion follows directly from these two estimates and Theorem
2.4
.

This completes our proof.
∎

In order to prove the main theorem, we also need the following result.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
such that all odd entries of
k
are equal to
1
, the Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu}+a)^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for all
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}
and
w\in A_{p(1-\gamma_{\nu})}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{p\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}})^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

Assume that the first
j
entries of
k
are equal to
1
and other entries are even. By (
2
),

\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}=\prod_{i=j+1}^{n}\delta_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}^{-(n-j)/1}\circ\prod_{i=1}^{j}\delta_{\nu_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-j/2}.

By Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
, the Riesz transforms
\prod_{i=j+1}^{n}\delta_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}^{-(n-j)/1}
and
\prod_{i=1}^{j}\delta_{\nu_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-j/2}
are bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for all
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}
and
w\in A_{p(1-\gamma_{\nu})}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{p\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}})^{\prime}}
.

This completes the proof.
∎

We are ready to give the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
:

Due to Theorems
4.2
and
4.3
, we need only to prove for the case
k_{\rm odd}\neq 0
and at least one odd index greater than or equal to
3
. Without loss of generality, we might assume that the first
j
entries
k_{1},\ldots,k_{j}
are odd and greater than
1
(and hence, are greater than or equal to
3
), the next
\ell
entries
k_{j+1}=\ldots=k_{j+\ell}=1
for some
j,\ell\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
with
j+\ell\leq n
, and the remaining entries
k_{j+\ell+1},\ldots,k_{n}
are even. In this case,
\sigma(k)=(1,\ldots,1,0,\ldots,0)
, i.e., the first
(j+\ell)
entries are equal to
1
and other are
0
. Then, using (
2
) we have

\delta_{\nu}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}=\delta_{\nu}^{k-\sigma(k)}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}+2(j+\ell))^{-|k-\sigma(k)|/2}\circ\delta^{\sigma(k)}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|\sigma(k)|/2}.

The weighted boundedness of
\delta^{\sigma(k)}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-|\sigma(k)|/2}
follows by Theorem
4.1
.

It suffices to prove the weighted estimates of
\delta_{\nu}^{k-\sigma(k)}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}+2(j+\ell))^{-|k-\sigma(k)|/2}
. To do this, by (
29
), for
i=1,\ldots,j
we have

\delta_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}-1}=\delta_{\nu_{i}+1}^{k_{i}-1}+\frac{1}{x_{i}}\delta_{\nu_{i}+1}^{k_{i}-2}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\delta_{\nu}^{k-\sigma(k)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{j}\Big(\delta_{\nu_{i}+1}^{k_{i}-1}+\frac{1}{x_{i}}\delta_{\nu_{i}+1}^{k_{i}-2}\Big)\prod_{i=j+\ell+1}^{n}\delta_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{j}\delta_{\nu_{i}+1}^{k_{i}-1}\prod_{i=j+\ell+1}^{n}\delta_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}+\sum_{\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{j}\backslash\{\textbf{0}\}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\frac{1}{x_{i}^{\sigma_{i}}}\delta_{\nu_{i}+1}^{k_{i}-1-\sigma_{i}}\prod_{i=j+\ell+1}^{n}\delta_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\delta_{\nu}^{k-\sigma(k)}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}+2(j+\ell))^{-|k-\sigma(k)|/2}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{j}\delta_{\nu_{i}+1}^{k_{i}-1}\prod_{i=j+\ell+1}^{n}\delta_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}+2(j+\ell))^{-|k-\sigma(k)|/2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{j}\backslash\{\textbf{0}\}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\frac{1}{x_{i}^{\sigma_{i}}}\delta_{\nu_{i}+1}^{k_{i}-1-\sigma_{i}}\prod_{i=j+\ell+1}^{n}\delta_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}+2(j+\ell))^{-|k-\sigma(k)|/2}.

By Theorem
4.2
and the fact
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}<\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}
the first operator in the above identity is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for all
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}
and
w\in A_{p(1-\gamma_{\nu})}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{p\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}})^{\prime}}
. For the second operator we have, let
\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{j}\backslash\{\textbf{0}\}
. Without loss of generality, we might assume
\sigma_{1}=1
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\prod_{i=1}^{j}\frac{1}{x_{i}^{\sigma_{i}}}

\displaystyle\delta_{\nu_{i}+1}^{k_{i}-1-\sigma_{i}}\prod_{i=j+\ell+1}^{n}\delta_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}+2(j+\ell))^{-|k-\sigma(k)|/2}f(x)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big|\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x_{1}}t^{(k_{1}-2)/2}\delta^{k_{1}-2}_{\nu_{1}+1}p_{t}^{\nu_{1}+1}(x_{1},y_{1})\Big|

\displaystyle\prod_{i\in\{2,\ldots,j\}\atop\sigma_{i}\neq 0}\sup_{t_{i}>0}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\Big|\frac{\sqrt{t}_{i}}{x_{i}}t_{i}^{(k_{i}-2)/2}\delta^{k_{i}-2}_{\nu_{i}+1}p_{t}^{\nu_{i}+1}(x_{i},y_{i})\Big|\prod_{i\in\{2,\ldots,j\}\atop\sigma_{i}=0}\sup_{t_{i}>0}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|t_{i}^{(k_{i}-1)/2}\delta_{\nu_{i}+1}^{k_{i}-1}p_{t_{i}}^{\nu_{i}+1}(x_{i},y_{i})|

\displaystyle\times\prod_{i=j+\ell+1}^{n}\sup_{t_{i}>0}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|t^{k_{i}/2}\delta_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}p_{t_{i}}^{\nu_{i}}(x_{i},y_{i})||f(y)|d(y_{i})\frac{dt}{t}d{y_{j}}.

Note that since
k_{i}-2
is odd for
i=1,\ldots,j
, due to Proposition
3.6
, for
i\in\{2,\ldots,j\}
with
\sigma_{i}=0
,

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{t}_{i}}{x_{i}}t_{i}^{(k_{i}-2)/2}|\delta^{k_{i}-2}_{\nu_{i}+1}p_{t}^{\nu_{i}+1}(x_{i},y_{i})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{-(\nu_{i}+3/2)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big),

which implies that

\sup_{t_{i}>0}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\Big|\frac{\sqrt{t}_{i}}{x_{i}}t_{i}^{(k_{i}-2)/2}\delta^{k_{i}-2}_{\nu_{i}+1}p_{t}^{\nu_{i}+1}(x_{i},y_{i})\Big||f(y_{i})|dy_{i}\lesssim\mathcal{M}f(x_{i}).

Hence,

f\mapsto\sup_{t_{i}>0}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\Big|\frac{\sqrt{t}_{i}}{x_{i}}t_{i}^{(k_{i}-2)/2}\delta^{k_{i}-2}_{\nu_{i}+1}p_{t}^{\nu_{i}+1}(x_{i},y_{i})\Big||f(y_{i})|dy_{i}

is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for all
1<p<\infty
.

For the same reason, by Proposition
3.1
, for
i\in\{1,\ldots,j\}
with
\sigma_{i}=0
the operator

f\mapsto\sup_{t_{i}>0}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|t_{i}^{(k_{i}-1)/2}\delta_{\nu_{i}+1}^{k_{i}-1}p_{t_{i}}^{\nu_{i}+1}(x_{i},y_{i})||f(y_{i})|dy_{i}

is dominated by (a multiple of) the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function
\mathcal{M}f(x_{i})
and hence, is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for all
1<p<\infty
.

Finally, by Theorem
3.5
and Proposition
3.15
, the operator

f\mapsto\sup_{t_{i}>0}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|t^{k_{i}/2}\delta_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}p_{t_{i}}^{\nu_{i}}(x_{i},y_{i})||f(y_{i})|d(y_{i})

is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for all
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu_{i}}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu_{i}}}
for
i=j+\ell+1,\ldots,n
; and hence is bound
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for all
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}
(since
\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}\geq\gamma_{\nu_{i}}
for
i=j+\ell+1,\ldots,n
).

It follows that the operators

\prod_{i=1}^{j}\frac{1}{x_{i}^{\sigma_{i}}}\delta_{\nu_{i}+1}^{k_{i}-1-\sigma_{i}}\prod_{i=j+\ell+1}^{n}\delta_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}+2(j+\ell))^{-|k-\sigma(k)|/2}

is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}
for all
\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{j}\backslash\{\textbf{0}\}
.

To complete the proof it suffices to prove that for each
\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{j}\backslash\{\textbf{0}\}
, the operator

\prod_{i=1}^{j}\frac{1}{x_{i}^{\sigma_{i}}}\delta_{\nu_{i}+1}^{k_{i}-1-\sigma_{i}}\prod_{i=j+\ell+1}^{n}\delta_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}+2(j+\ell))^{-|k-\sigma(k)|/2}

is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}
and
w\in A_{p(1-\gamma_{\nu})}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{p\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}})^{\prime}}
.

To do this, fix
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}
and
w\in A_{p(1-\gamma_{\nu})}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{p\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}})^{\prime}}
. By Lemma
2.1
, we can find
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p_{0}<p<q_{0}<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}
such that
w\in A_{{p_{0}(1-\gamma_{\nu})}}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{q_{0}\gamma_{\nu+\sigma(k)}})^{\prime}}
. For each
\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{j}\backslash\{\textbf{0}\}
, we set

(44)

\displaystyle T_{\sigma}:

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{j}\frac{1}{x_{i}^{\sigma_{i}}}\delta_{\nu_{i}+1}^{k_{i}-1-\sigma_{i}}\prod_{i=j+\ell+1}^{n}\delta_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}+2(j+\ell))^{-|k-\sigma(k)|/2}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in\{1,\ldots,j\}\atop\sigma_{i}=1}\frac{1}{x_{i}}\delta_{\nu_{i}+1}^{k_{i}-2}\prod_{i\in\{1,\ldots,j\}\atop\sigma_{i}=0}\delta_{\nu_{i}+1}^{k_{i}-1}\prod_{i=j+\ell+1}^{n}\delta_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}+2(j+\ell))^{-|k-\sigma(k)|/2}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in\{1,\ldots,j\}\atop\sigma_{i}=1}\frac{1}{x_{i}}\delta_{\nu_{i}+1}^{k_{i}-2}\prod_{i\in\{1,\ldots,j\}\atop\sigma_{i}=0}\delta_{\nu_{i}+1}^{k_{i}-1}\prod_{i=j+\ell+1}^{n}\delta_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}+2(j+\ell))^{-|k-\sigma(k)|/2}.

Due to Theorem
2.4
, we need only to verify the following two estimates

(45)

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{S_{j}(B)}|T_{\sigma}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}})^{m}f|^{q_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}}\lesssim 2^{-j(2m-1)}\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{q_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}}

and

(46)

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{S_{j}(B)}|[I-(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}})^{m}]f|^{q_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}}\leq 2^{-j(n+1)}\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{p_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/p_{0}}

for
2m-1>n
and all
j\geq 2
and
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
supported in
B
.

Using the identity

I-(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}})^{m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}e^{-ir_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}},

where
c_{i}
are constants, (
21
) in Proposition
3.2
and Lemma
3.17
, we will obtain (
46
).

For the first inequality, from (
44
) and the following identities

(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}+2(j+\ell))^{-|k-\sigma(k)|/2}=c\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{|k-\sigma(k)|/2}e^{-2(j+\ell)t}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}

and

(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}})^{m}=\int_{[0,r_{B}^{2}]^{m}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}^{m}e^{-(s_{1}+\ldots+s_{m})\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}d\vec{s},

where
d\vec{s}=ds_{1}\ldots ds_{m}
, we have

\displaystyle T_{\sigma}

\displaystyle(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}})^{m}f

\displaystyle=c\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{[0,r_{B}^{2}]^{m}}t^{|k-\sigma(k)|/2}e^{-2(j+\ell)t}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{i\in\{1,\ldots,j\}\atop\sigma_{i}=1}\frac{1}{x_{i}}\delta_{\nu_{i}+1}^{k_{i}-2}\prod_{i\in\{1,\ldots,j\}\atop\sigma_{i}=0}\delta_{\nu_{i}+1}^{k_{i}-1}\prod_{i=j+\ell+1}^{n}\delta_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}^{m}e^{-(t+s_{1}+\ldots+s_{m})\mathcal{L}_{\nu+\sigma(k)}}fd\vec{s}\frac{dt}{t}.

Note that in this case all
k_{i}-2
are even for
i\in\{1,\ldots,j\}
with
\sigma_{i}=1
, and both
k_{i},i=j+\ell+1,\ldots,n
and
k_{i}-1,i\in\{1,\ldots,j\}
with
\sigma_{i}=0
are even. Hence, we can arguing similarly to the proof of (
43
) by using Theorem
3.13
and Lemma
3.17
to obtain (
46
).

Hence, this completes our proof.

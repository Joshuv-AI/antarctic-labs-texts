Scattering lengths of the J / ψ ​ π J/\psi\pi and J / ψ ​ K J/\psi K systems Journal: Physics Letters B
Jiang Yan, Xiong-Hui Cao, Meng-Lin Du, Feng-Kun Guo

Abstract
We investigate the low-energy interactions between the charmonium state J / ψ J/\psi and the light pseudoscalar mesons ( π \pi and K K ) within the framework of dispersion relations. We demonstrate that the symmetry-breaking terms in the chiral Lagrangian induce mixing between the bare charmonium fields, necessitating a diagonalization procedure to correctly identify the physical J / ψ J/\psi and ψ ′ \psi^{\prime} states. Following this diagonalization, we relate the threshold scattering amplitudes of J / ψ ​ 𝒫 ​ ( 𝒫 = π , K ) J/\psi{\cal P}~({\cal P}=\pi,K) , which determine the scattering lengths, to the J / ψ ​ J / ψ → 𝒫 ​ 𝒫 ¯ J/\psi J/\psi\to{\cal P}\bar{\cal P} amplitudes via crossing symmetry. The 𝒫 ​ 𝒫 ¯ {\cal P}\bar{\cal P} rescattering effects are incorporated using dispersion relations. We obtain the S S -wave scattering lengths a J / ψ ​ π ≲ − 0.0021 a_{J/\psi\pi}\lesssim-0.0021 fm and a J / ψ ​ K ≲ − 0.028 a_{J/\psi K}\lesssim-0.028 fm, where the negative sign indicates an attractive interaction. Our results show that the J / ψ ​ K J/\psi K interaction is moderately enhanced relative to the pion channel, driven by explicit chiral symmetry breaking. Furthermore, a quantitative comparison of the coupled-channel mechanism, where J / ψ ​ π J/\psi\pi couples to D ​ D ¯ ∗ D\bar{D}^{*} and J / ψ ​ K J/\psi K couples to D ∗ ​ D ¯ s / D ​ D ¯ s ∗ D^{*}\bar{D}_{s}/D\bar{D}_{s}^{*} , reveals that both J / ψ ​ π J/\psi\pi and J / ψ ​ K J/\psi K scatterings are predominantly governed by the soft-gluon exchange mechanism.



1
Introduction

The low-energy interactions between heavy quarkonia and light hadrons, such as pions, kaons, and nucleons, have long attracted considerable theoretical interest
[
42
,
9
]
. These processes provide a unique probe for exploring the gluonic degrees of freedom inside hadrons and for elucidating the underlying mechanisms of inter-hadron interactions. In particular, they offer direct access to nonperturbative aspects of quantum chromodynamics (QCD) that are difficult to explore through reactions involving only light hadrons. Moreover, the interaction between the
J/\psi
and light hadrons plays a pivotal role in the phenomenology of
J/\psi
suppression in relativistic heavy-ion collisions, which is widely regarded as a key signature for the formation of the quark-gluon plasma (QGP)
[
7
]
. Despite decades of intensive study, a quantitative and unified understanding of these interactions remains a challenge.

A distinctive feature of heavy-quarkonium–light-hadron scattering is that the two hadrons share no common valence quark constituents. Consequently, these processes are suppressed by the Okubo–Zweig–Iizuka (OZI) rule
[
40
,
53
,
30
]
, and quark-exchange mechanisms, which dominate many light-hadron interactions, are absent. The interaction is therefore mediated predominantly by gluonic dynamics, making heavy quarkonia particularly clean probes of multi-gluon exchange effects and long-range gluon-induced forces in QCD.

In general, OZI-suppressed quarkonium–hadron interactions can be classified into two distinct mechanisms. The first is the coupled-channel mechanism, in which the quarkonium–hadron system couples to intermediate states containing open-charm mesons, such as
D\bar{D}^{*}
or
D^{*}\bar{D}_{s}/D\bar{D}_{s}^{*}
, and subsequently rescatters back to the original channel. This mechanism may become particularly important when the open-charm thresholds lie close to the quarkonium–hadron threshold and can be further enhanced by the presence of near-threshold exotic states. The second mechanism involves multi-gluon exchange, which can be viewed as a color van der Waals interaction
[
12
,
11
]
between two color-singlet hadrons. In the heavy-quark limit, this interaction is commonly treated within the framework of the QCD multipole expansion
[
26
,
48
,
42
,
9
,
37
,
36
,
45
,
31
,
19
,
8
,
33
]
, in which the quarkonium acts as a compact color dipole interacting with the gluon fields generated by the light hadron.

The relative importance of these two mechanisms depends sensitively on both the quantum numbers of the light hadron and the kinematic regime under consideration. In the case of the pion, additional constraints arise from chiral symmetry. As a pseudo-Nambu–Goldstone (pNG) boson of spontaneous chiral symmetry breaking of QCD, the pion decouples from hadrons in the chiral limit.
Chiral effective theory predicts that the leading-order
S
-wave scattering amplitude between an isoscalar heavy quarkonium state, such as the
J/\psi
, and a pion vanishes, with the first nonzero contribution appearing only at
{\cal O}(m_{\pi}^{2})

[
15
]
(the chiral Lagrangian for the
\psi^{\prime}\to J/\psi\pi\pi
was constructed in Ref.
[
13
]
immediately after the discovery of
J/\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
).
Consequently, the
J/\psi\,\pi
interaction is expected to be extremely weak near threshold. This qualitative expectation has been corroborated by lattice QCD calculations
[
52
,
34
]
, which find that the corresponding scattering length
|a_{0}|
is of order
10^{-2}~{\rm fm}
, consistent with a weakly attractive interaction. Furthermore, an approximate upper bound of
|a_{0}|\lesssim 0.02~{\rm fm}
was estimated in Ref.
[
35
]
.

The situation is somewhat different for the
J/\psi\,K
system. Although the kaon also belongs to the pseudoscalar meson octet, the explicit breaking of
{\rm SU}(3)_{f}
symmetry by the sizable strange quark mass relaxes the chiral constraints that apply to kaons.
Consequently, the chiral suppression governing the
J/\psi\pi
interaction is partially lifted in the kaon channel. One may therefore expect the
J/\psi K
scattering length to be sizeably larger than that of
J/\psi\pi
, while still remaining smaller than typical quarkonium–nucleon interactions. Furthermore, the proximity of open-charm strange thresholds suggests that coupled-channel effects could play a more prominent role in the
J/\psi K
system, potentially leading to nontrivial dynamics that are more strongly suppressed in the pion channel.

Despite its clear theoretical interest, a systematic and quantitative study of low-energy
J/\psi\,K
scattering remains lacking. Most existing investigations have focused either on quarkonium–nucleon interactions or on the pion channel, while the kaon case has received comparatively little attention.
In Ref.
[
51
]
, we found that
J/\psi
–nucleon scattering in the near-threshold region is dominated by multi-gluon exchange rather than the coupled-channel mechanism. Whether the same conclusion holds for
J/\psi\pi
and
J/\psi K
scattering remains to be investigated.

In this work, we present a detailed study of low-energy
S
-wave scattering of
\pi
and
K
off
J/\psi
. Our analysis treats multi-gluon exchange and coupled-channel dynamics separately, enabling us to quantify their relative contributions to the scattering lengths. This paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
2
, we discuss in detail the effects of explicit chiral symmetry-breaking terms in the chiral Lagrangian and demonstrate that diagonalizing the
\psi^{\prime}
and
J/\psi
mass terms does not eliminate their contribution to the
\psi^{\prime}\to J/\psi\pi\pi
contact term, thereby correcting a statement in Ref.
[
16
]
. In Sec.
3
, we present the calculation of the scattering amplitudes and the corresponding scattering lengths using dispersion relations. Finally, Sec.
4
provides a summary.

2
Interaction between charmonia and light hadrons

The interaction between charmonium states and light pseudoscalar mesons can be described by the following chiral effective Lagrangian
[
37
,
16
]
,

\displaystyle{\cal L}=

\displaystyle\,\operatorname{Tr_{s}}[J_{\beta}^{\dagger}J_{\alpha}]\bigg[\frac{c_{1}^{(\alpha\beta)}}{2}\operatorname{Tr_{f}}[u_{\mu}u^{\mu}]+\frac{c_{2}^{(\alpha\beta)}}{2}\operatorname{Tr_{f}}[u_{\mu}u_{\nu}]v^{\mu}v^{\nu}

\displaystyle\,+\frac{c_{m}^{(\alpha\beta)}}{2}\operatorname{Tr_{f}}[\chi_{+}]\bigg]+{\rm H.c.},

(1)

where
\alpha
and
\beta
label the charmonium states, and
\operatorname{Tr_{s}}[...]
and
\operatorname{Tr_{f}}[...]
denote traces over the spinor and flavor spaces, respectively. The composite field
J_{\alpha}=\vec{\psi}_{\alpha}\cdot\vec{\sigma}+\eta_{\alpha}
encodes the charmonium degrees of freedom, with
\vec{\sigma}
representing the Pauli matrices in spinor space. In Eq. (
2
),
v^{\mu}=(1,\vec{0})
is the four-velocity of the charmonia in the rest frame,
\chi_{\pm}=u^{\dagger}\chi u^{\dagger}\pm u\chi^{\dagger}u
, and
\chi=2B_{0}{\cal M}
, where
B_{0}
is a constant related to the quark condensate in the chiral limit and
{\cal M}
denotes the light-quark mass matrix. In the isospin limit, the pion and kaon masses squared are given by
m_{\pi}^{2}=2B_{0}\hat{m}
and
m_{K}^{2}=B_{0}(\hat{m}+m_{s})
, with
\hat{m}
being the average mass of the
u
and
d
quarks. Furthermore, the chiral vielbein is defined as
u_{\mu}=i(u^{\dagger}\partial_{\mu}u-u\partial_{\mu}u^{\dagger})
, where

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{i\Phi}{2F_{\pi}}\right),

(2)

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\pi^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}+\dfrac{\eta}{\sqrt{6}}&\pi^{+}&K^{+}\\
\pi^{-}&-\dfrac{\pi^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}+\dfrac{\eta}{\sqrt{6}}&K^{0}\\
K^{-}&\bar{K}^{0}&-\dfrac{2\eta}{\sqrt{6}}\end{pmatrix},

(3)

with
F_{\pi}
denoting the pion decay constant in the chiral limit. After evaluating the spinor trace
\operatorname{Tr_{s}}[...]
and focusing on the vector charmonium sector, the chiral Lagrangian in Eq. (
2
) can be expanded in powers of the pNG fields. Up to quadratic order, this yields

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\psi}=

\displaystyle\,2\left(\vec{\psi}_{\beta}^{\dagger}\cdot\vec{\psi}_{\alpha}\right)\Bigg[\frac{c_{1}^{(\alpha\beta)}}{F_{\pi}^{2}}\operatorname{Tr_{f}}[\partial_{\mu}\Phi\partial^{\mu}\Phi]

\displaystyle\,+\frac{c_{2}^{(\alpha\beta)}}{F_{\pi}^{2}}\operatorname{Tr_{f}}[\partial_{\mu}\Phi\partial_{\nu}\Phi]v^{\mu}v^{\nu}+2B_{0}c_{m}^{(\alpha\beta)}\tilde{m}

\displaystyle\,-\frac{c_{m}^{(\alpha\beta)}}{F_{\pi}^{2}}2B_{0}\operatorname{Tr_{f}}[{\cal M}\Phi^{2}]\Bigg]+{\rm H.c.},

(4)

where
\tilde{m}=\operatorname{Tr_{f}}[{\cal M}]
. In the isospin limit, one has
B_{0}\tilde{m}=m_{\pi}^{2}/2+m_{K}^{2}
.

The inclusion of the symmetry-breaking
c_{m}
-term has been a source of theoretical ambiguity
[
16
,
18
,
20
,
51
]
. On the one hand, introducing this term induces mixing between charmonium fields with different
\alpha
, giving rise to the physical
J/\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
states; on the other hand, omitting it would eliminate all higher-order
J/\psi\psi^{\prime}\pi\pi
vertices that arise from explicit chiral symmetry-breaking operators in powers of the light quark masses. We demonstrate below that a proper diagonalization procedure resolves this dilemma. To identify the physical states, we introduce a rotation matrix
{\rm U}
,

\displaystyle\Psi^{(\rm phys)}

\displaystyle\equiv\begin{pmatrix}\vec{\psi}_{1}^{(\rm phys.)}\\
\vec{\psi}_{2}^{(\rm phys.)}\end{pmatrix}={\rm U}^{\rm T}\Psi,

\displaystyle{\rm U}

\displaystyle\equiv\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix},\quad\Psi\equiv\begin{pmatrix}\vec{\psi}_{1}\\
\vec{\psi}_{2}\end{pmatrix}

(5)

where
\vec{\psi}_{1}^{(\rm phys.)}
and
\vec{\psi}_{2}^{(\rm phys.)}
represent the physical
J/\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
charmonium fields, respectively, and
\theta
denotes the mixing angle. Collecting the quadratic terms in the charmonium fields, the mass sector of the Lagrangian can be written as
{\cal L}_{m}=\frac{1}{2}\Psi^{\dagger}{\bf M}^{2}\Psi
. The mass-squared matrix
{\bf M}^{2}
, which incorporates the explicit chiral symmetry-breaking
c_{m}^{(\alpha\beta)}
terms, takes the form

\displaystyle{\bf M}^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}m_{1}^{2}+8B_{0}\tilde{m}c_{m}^{(11)}&8B_{0}\tilde{m}c_{m}^{(12)}\\
8B_{0}\tilde{m}c_{m}^{(12)}&m_{2}^{2}+8B_{0}\tilde{m}c_{m}^{(22)}\end{pmatrix},

(6)

where
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
denote the bare masses of the basis fields
\vec{\psi}_{1}
and
\vec{\psi}_{2}
, respectively, in the absence of the symmetry-breaking
c_{m}^{(\alpha\beta)}
terms.

Diagonalization of the mass matrix
{\bf M}^{2}
requires
{\rm U}{\bf M}^{2}{\rm U}^{\rm T}=\operatorname{diag}(M_{J/\psi}^{2},M_{\psi^{\prime}}^{2})
. This condition yields the mixing angle relation

\displaystyle\tan(2\theta)=\frac{16B_{0}\tilde{m}c_{m}^{(12)}}{m_{2}^{2}-m_{1}^{2}+8B_{0}\tilde{m}\left(c_{m}^{(22)}-c_{m}^{(11)}\right)},

(7)

and the diagonalized Lagrangian

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\psi}^{(\rm diag.)}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{2}{F_{\pi}^{2}}\left(\vec{\psi}_{\beta}^{{\rm phys.}\dagger}\cdot\vec{\psi}_{\alpha}^{\rm phys.}\right)\bigg[\tilde{c}_{1}^{(\alpha\beta)}\operatorname{Tr_{f}}[\partial_{\mu}\Phi\partial^{\mu}\Phi]

\displaystyle\,+\tilde{c}_{2}^{(\alpha\beta)}\operatorname{Tr_{f}}[\partial_{\mu}\Phi\partial_{\nu}\Phi]v^{\mu}v^{\nu}

\displaystyle\,-2\tilde{c}_{m}^{(\alpha\beta)}B_{0}\operatorname{Tr_{f}}[{\cal M}\Phi^{2}]\bigg]+{\rm H.c.},

(8)

where the new coefficients
\tilde{c}_{i}^{(\alpha\beta)}\ (i=1,2,m)
are related to the original coefficients
c_{i}^{(\alpha\beta)}\ (i=1,2,m)
via

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{i}^{(11)}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}\left(c_{i}^{(11)}+c_{i}^{(22)}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\cos(2\theta)\left(c_{i}^{(22)}-c_{i}^{(11)}\right)

\displaystyle\,-\sin(2\theta)c_{i}^{(12)},

(9)

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{i}^{(22)}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}\left(c_{i}^{(11)}+c_{i}^{(22)}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\cos(2\theta)\left(c_{i}^{(22)}-c_{i}^{(11)}\right)

\displaystyle\,+\sin(2\theta)c_{i}^{(12)},

(10)

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{i}^{(12)}=

\displaystyle\,\cos(2\theta)c_{i}^{(12)}-\frac{1}{2}\sin(2\theta)\left(c_{i}^{(22)}-c_{i}^{(11)}\right).

(11)

Notably, for the mass-related coupling
\tilde{c}_{m}^{(\alpha\beta)}
, one obtains the explicit expressions

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{m}^{(12)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8B_{0}\tilde{m}}\Delta_{m}\sin(2\theta),

(12)

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{m}^{(11)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8B_{0}\tilde{m}}\bigg[M_{J/\psi}^{2}-\Sigma_{m}+\Delta_{m}\cos(2\theta)\bigg],

(13)

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{m}^{(22)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8B_{0}\tilde{m}}\bigg[M_{\psi^{\prime}}^{2}-\Sigma_{m}-\Delta_{m}\cos(2\theta)\bigg],

(14)

where

\displaystyle\lesssim-0.028~{\rm fm},

(25)

where, in our convention, a negative sign indicates an attractive interaction that is too weak to support a bound state. The strong suppression of the
J/\psi\pi
scattering length reflects the combined effects of chiral and OZI suppressions, whereas the larger magnitude of
a_{J/\psi K}
arises from the larger kaon mass compared to the pion mass, i.e., the more significant explicit chiral symmetry breaking due to the strange quark mass.

3.2
Coupled-channel mechanism

In the coupled-channel mechanism, the
J/\psi\pi
and
J/\psi K
scattering processes proceed through intermediate coupling to open-charm channels. Specifically, the
J/\psi\pi
system couples to the
D\bar{D}^{*}
channel, while the
J/\psi K
system couples to the
D^{*}\bar{D}_{s}/D\bar{D}_{s}^{*}
channels. This mechanism allows the scattering to occur via virtual transitions to these intermediate states, even when direct interactions are highly suppressed. The
S
-wave scattering amplitudes for these coupled-channel systems, encompassing both the
J/\psi{\cal P}
and the corresponding open-charm
D\bar{D}^{*}
(
D^{*}\bar{D}_{s}/D\bar{D}_{s}^{*}
) channels, have been constructed in Ref.
[
23
]
to describe the exotic hadron candidates
Z_{c}(3900)
and
Z_{cs}(3985)
, to which we refer the reader for further details.
Since the direct
J/\psi{\cal P}\to J/\psi{\cal P}
potential is not included in that work, the resulting scattering amplitudes effectively capture only the open-charm coupled-channel contributions, with the gluon-exchange mechanism excluded. Adopting the parameters determined in Ref.
[
23
]
by fitting the BESIII data on
e^{+}e^{-}\to J/\psi\pi^{+}\pi^{-}

[
5
]
,
e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{+}(D^{0}D^{*-})

[
3
]
, and
e^{+}e^{-}\to K^{+}(D^{*0}D_{s}^{-}+D^{0}D_{s}^{*-})

[
6
]
, we obtain the
S
-wave
J/\psi{\cal P}
scattering lengths from the coupled-channel mechanism as

\displaystyle a_{J/\psi\pi}^{\rm CC}

\displaystyle\in[-4.2,6.1]\times 10^{-6}~{\rm fm},

\displaystyle a_{J/\psi K}^{\rm CC}

\displaystyle\in[-0.01,-8.9\times 10^{-6}]~{\rm fm}.

(26)

The sizable uncertainty in
a_{J/\psi K}^{\text{CC}}
originates from significant SU(3) flavor-symmetry breaking in the parameter
C_{12}
within scheme (c) of Sec. IV in Ref.
[
23
]
, where
\delta_{12}\gg C_{12}
. Since
C_{12}
governs the transition potential between the
J/\psi{\cal P}
and open-charm channels, its value strongly influences the resulting scattering length.

Comparing the results in Eqs. (
25
) and (
26
), one finds that the soft-gluon exchange mechanism provides the dominant contribution to
J/\psi{\cal P}
scattering in the near-threshold region, which is analogous to the finding in Ref.
[
51
]
for
J/\psi
-nucleon scattering.

4
Summary

In this work, we have investigated the low-energy scattering of
\pi
and
K
mesons off
J/\psi
. We demonstrate that the symmetry-breaking terms in the charmonium–pNG-boson interaction Lagrangian induce mixing between the bare charmonium fields, rendering them non-diagonal in the mass basis. Upon diagonalization, the physical
J/\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
states are identified, and the symmetry-breaking terms remain nonvanishing for terms with at least two pNG fields, contributing to the transition amplitude of
\psi^{\prime}\to J/\psi\pi\pi
.

Using the resulting effective Lagrangian, we computed the
J/\psi\pi
and
J/\psi K
scattering amplitudes at threshold arising from the soft-gluon exchange mechanism, which is effectively captured by correlated
\pi\pi
and
K\bar{K}
exchanges treated within the Muskhelishvili–Omnès formalism. The corresponding scattering lengths were then extracted via crossing symmetry.
Our results indicate that the
J/\psi\pi
interaction is extremely weak near threshold, consistent with expectations from chiral symmetry and OZI suppression, whereas the
J/\psi K
interaction receives more substantial contributions due to explicit chiral symmetry breaking associated with the strange quark mass.
In contrast, the contributions from the coupled-channel mechanism, where
J/\psi\pi
couples to
D\bar{D}^{*}
and
J/\psi K
couples to
D^{*}\bar{D}_{s}/D\bar{D}_{s}^{*}
, are found to be significantly smaller than those from soft-gluon exchange, indicating that the multi-gluon exchange mechanism dominates the low-energy
J/\psi\pi
and
J/\psi K
scattering.
These predictions can be tested against forthcoming lattice QCD calculations.

Combined with the analogous conclusion reached in Ref.
[
51
]
for
J/\psi
-nucleon scattering, the dominance of the multi-gluon exchange mechanism over the coupled-channel mechanism may be a universal feature for the scattering of light hadrons off charmonia.
Whether this conclusion extends to bottomonia remains an open question, since the chromopolarizabilities of fully-bottom systems are expected to be considerably smaller than those of fully-charm systems
[
21
]
.

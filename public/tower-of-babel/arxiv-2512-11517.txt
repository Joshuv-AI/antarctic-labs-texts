Irreducibility of Quantum Markov Semigroups, uniqueness of invariant states and related properties
Franco Fagnola, Federico Girotti

Abstract
We present different characterizations of the notion of irreducibility for Quantum Markov Semigroups (QMSs) and investigate its relationship with other relevant features of the dynamics, such as primitivity, positivity improvement and relaxation; in particular, we show that irreducibility, primitivity and relaxation towards a faithful invariant density are equivalent when the semigroup admits an invariant density. Moreover, in the case of uniformly continuous QMSs, we present several useful ways of checking irreducibility in terms of the operators appearing in the generator in GKLS form. Our exposition is as much self-contained as possible, we present some well known results with elementary proofs (collecting all the relevant literature) and we derive new ones. We study both finite and infinite dimensional evolutions and we remark that many results only require the QMS to be made of Schwarz maps.



1
Introduction

Quantum Markov semigroups (QMSs) on
\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{h})
, or dynamical semigroups in the Physics terminology,
are a key structure for describing the dynamics of open quantum systems subject to noise because of the interaction
with the surrounding environment.

The structure and their properties are well known in the case where the Hilbert space
\mathsf{h}
is finite dimensional
(see e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
21
,
47
]
and references therein).
However, in the case where
\mathsf{h}
is infinite dimensional, even if the generator is a bounded operator, certain fundamental
properties are not entirely clear or their proof not infrequently depends on deep or complex results of functional analysis.
This is the case of the relationships between irreducibility, uniqueness and faithfulness of invariant densities (primitivity)
and convergence to invariant densities.

In this paper we present elementary proofs (i.e., requiring as a prerequisite only a basic course in functional analysis)
of some results on irreducibility, its relationship with other properties and their consequences for the dynamics.

As in the classical theory of Markov processes, irreducibility implies uniqueness and faithfulness of invariant densities (if they exist),
and, practically, it is the most direct and effective method to show this. In most cases it is the only one. Moreover, for QMSs
with an invariant density, irreducibility is indeed
equivalent
to its uniqueness and faithfulness (Theorem
3.3
).

A consequence of Lévy’s result (see Proposition 1.3 in
[
4
]
) concerning the support of transition probabilities in classical Markov chains is that irreducibility of the semigroup implies that the probability of finding the process in any state at any positive time is strictly positive; the counterpart for QMSs of such a result would be that irreducibility implies that, no matter what is the initial density, the density at any positive time is faithful. This property of the semigroup is often called positivity improvement. The notions of irreducibility and positivity improvement are known to be equivalent for finite dimensional systems (Theorem
4.2
), while the latter is strictly stronger than irreducibility in infinite dimensions, as shown for instance by Example
5.10
. The converse implication can be proved under some extra assumptions on the semigroup (see Theorem
5.9
).

Another feature of quantum dynamics which is relevant for applications is to determine whether the system relaxes towards an invariant density. In finite dimensional systems irreducibility is equivalent to relaxation towards the same faithful invariant density, regardless of the initial density (Theorem
4.2
) and this keeps being true in infinite dimensional systems as well, once one requires the existence of at least an invariant density (otherwise it does not make sense to talk about relaxation in first place), as shown by Theorem
5.8
; to the best of our knowledge, this result in such generality is new in the literature. 2
Subharmonic projections and irreducibility

In this section we recall the basic definitions and notation.

Let
\mathsf{h}
be a complex separable Hilbert space and let
\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{h})
denote the von Neumann algebra
of all bounded linear operators on
\mathsf{h}
. It is well known that
\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{h})
is isomorphic to the dual of the Banach space

\mathfrak{I}_{1}(\mathsf{h})
of trace class operators on
\mathsf{h}
. We denote by
\|\cdot\|_{1}
the trace norm
on
\mathfrak{I}_{1}(\mathsf{h})
and by
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
the operator norm on
\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{h})
. Moreover,
we denote by
\operatorname{tr}(\cdot)
the trace on
\mathfrak{I}_{1}(\mathsf{h})
.

A sequence
(\eta_{n})_{n\geq 0}
converges weakly to
\eta
in
\mathfrak{I}_{1}(\mathsf{h})
if the sequence of complex numbers

({\rm tr}(\eta_{n}\,x))_{n\geq 0}
converges to
{\rm tr}(\eta\,x)
for all

x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{h})
. A sequence
(x_{n})_{n\geq 0}
converges weakly
∗
to
x
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{h})
if the sequence of complex numbers

({\rm tr}(\eta\,x_{n}))_{n\geq 0}
converges to
{\rm tr}(\eta\,x)
for all

\eta\in\mathfrak{I}_{1}(\mathsf{h})
.

An element of
\mathfrak{I}_{1}(\mathsf{h})
is called a
density
if it is positive and
has unit trace. By abuse of terminology, a density is often called state and identified with the
normal state
x\mapsto{\rm tr}(\rho x)
on
\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{h})
. The
support

of a density
\rho
is the closure of the range of
\rho
. The
support projection
is the orthogonal projection
in
\mathsf{h}
onto the range of
\rho
. A density is called
faithful
if its support is the whole
space
\mathsf{h}
, i.e. its support projection is equal to the identity operator
1l
.

A
quantum Markov semigroup
(QMS)
\mathcal{T}=(\mathcal{T}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{h})
is
a weakly
∗
-continuous semigroup of completely positive, identity preserving
normal (i.e. continuous with respect to the weak
∗
topology on
\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{h})
) maps on
\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{h})
.
A QMS is the adjoint semigroup (
[
16
]
5.14 p. 63) of a
c_{0}
semigroup

\mathcal{T}_{*}=(\mathcal{T}_{*t})_{t\geq 0}
of completely positive, trace preserving maps on
\mathfrak{I}_{1}(\mathsf{h})
.
Let us recall that the
c_{0}
semigroup property means that for all trace class operator
\eta
, the map

t\mapsto\mathcal{T}_{*t}(\eta)
is continuous for the trace norm topology,
in an equivalent way for the weak topology on
\mathfrak{I}_{1}(\mathsf{h})
(Theorem 5.8, p. 40
[
16
]
.

We recall that complete positivity of the maps
(\mathcal{T}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
implies that they satisfy the Schwarz property (see Corollary 2.8 in
[
13
]
), that is

\mathcal{T}_{t}(x^{*})\mathcal{T}_{t}(x)\leq\mathcal{T}_{t}(x^{*}x),\quad x\in{\cal B}(\mathsf{h}),\,t\geq 0.

(2.1)

If we consider the class of QMSs which are uniformly continuous, i.e.
t\mapsto\mathcal{T}_{t}
is continuous in operator norm, then the celebrated Gorini-Kossakowski-Surdashan-Lindblad (GKSL) theorem (see
[
31
,
37
]
or Theorem 30.16 in
[
41
]
) provides a detailed description of its infinitesimal generator.

Theorem 2.1
.

If
\mathcal{T}
is uniformly continuous, then its infinitesimal generator is of the form

\mathcal{L}(x)=G^{*}x+xG+\sum_{\ell\in I}L_{\ell}^{*}xL_{\ell},\quad x\in{\cal B}(\mathsf{h})

(2.2)

for some bounded linear operators
G,L_{\ell}\in{\cal B}(\mathsf{h})
,
\ell\in I
with
I
finite or countable
such that
\sum_{\ell\in I}L_{\ell}^{*}L_{\ell}
converges in the strong operator topology and

G^{*}+G+\sum_{\ell\in I}L_{\ell}^{*}L_{\ell}=0.

(2.3)

In order to highlight the difference with unitary evolutions one can denote by
-H
the imaginary part of the operator
G
:
from (
2.3
) it is immediate to see that

G=-\mathrm{i}H-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell\in I}L_{\ell}^{*}L_{\ell}

and that the generator can be written in the following form:

\mathcal{L}(x)=\mathrm{i}[H,x]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell\in I}(L_{\ell}^{*}L_{\ell}x-2L_{\ell}^{*}xL_{\ell}+xL_{\ell}^{*}L_{\ell}),

(2.4)

where one can easily see that the Hamiltonian dynamics is perturbed by the effect of the interaction with the environment, resulting in the additional term with
L_{\ell}
s. We recall that the representation of the generator given in equation (
2.4
) is not unique (Theorem 30.16 in
[
41
]
) and we remark that all the statements made in this work that involve the operators appearing in equation (
2.4
) hold true for every representation.

A density
\rho
is called
invariant
for a QMS
\mathcal{T}
if
\mathcal{T}_{*t}(\rho)=\rho
for all
t\geq 0
or,
in an equivalent way,
{\rm tr}(\rho\mathcal{T}_{t}(x))={\rm tr}(\rho x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{h})
.

The support of an invariant density has the following useful property.

Proposition 2.2
.

The support projection
p
of an invariant density
\rho
satisfies
\mathcal{T}_{t}(p)\geq p
for all
t\geq 0
.

The proof we report is along the lines of the one in Theorem 1 in
[
46
]
, for instance; a different proof for the finite and infinite dimensional case can be found in
[
5
]
(Proposition 9) and in
[
12
]
(Proposition 5.1), respectively.

Proof.

Let
p
be the support projection of an invariant density
\rho
. Clearly
p\rho=\rho p=\rho
. Let us fix
t>0
.
Notice that
p\mathcal{T}_{t}(p)p\leq p\mathcal{T}_{t}(\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl})p=p
and
{\rm tr}(\rho(p-p\mathcal{T}_{t}(p)p))={\rm tr}(\rho(p-\mathcal{T}_{t}(p)))=0
; therefore, since
\rho
is faithful on the subalgebra
p\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{h})p
,

p\mathcal{T}_{t}(p)p=p
.

It follows that the projection
p^{\perp}=\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}-p
satisfies
p\mathcal{T}_{t}(p^{\perp})p=0
and so positivity of
\mathcal{T}_{t}(p^{\perp})

implies
p\mathcal{T}_{t}(p^{\perp})=\mathcal{T}_{t}(p^{\perp})p=0
, i.e.
\mathcal{T}_{t}(p^{\perp})\leq p^{\perp}
(Lemma
A.1
.
Finally,
\mathcal{T}_{t}(p)=\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}-\mathcal{T}_{t}(p^{\perp})\geq p
.
∎

We call
subharmonic
any orthogonal projection
p
that satisfies
\mathcal{T}_{t}(p)\geq p
.
If the semigroup is uniformly continuous we have a characterization of subharmonic projections in terms of the operators appearing in the generator.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let us consider an orthogonal projection
p
; if
\mathcal{T}
is uniformly continuous, the following statements are equivalent:

1.

p
is subharmonic,

2.

\mathcal{L}(p)\geq 0
,

3.

the support of
p
is an invariant subspace for
G
and
L_{\ell}
’s.

We recall that a generalization of the equivalence between
1.
and
3.
was proved in the case of weak*-continuous semigroups admitting an infinitesimal generator in a generalized Lindblad form (Theorem III.1 in
[
19
]
). The proof of the equivalence between
3.
and the alternative characterization of
1.
corresponding to item
3.
in Proposition
2.5
can be found in
[
45
]
(see Corollary 1) and
[
48
]
( Theorem 5.1).

Proof.

Notice that we can rephrase the third item as

p^{\perp}Gp=0,\quad p^{\perp}L_{\ell}p=0\quad\forall\ell\in I.

1.\Rightarrow 2.
It follows from differentiating
\mathcal{T}_{t}(p)\geq p
at
t=0
.

2.\Rightarrow 1.
Since
\mathcal{T}_{t}
is positive,
2.
implies that
\mathcal{T}_{t}(\mathcal{L}(p))\geq 0
and the statement follows integrating.

2.\Rightarrow 3.
Notice that
\mathcal{L}(p)\geq 0
is equivalent to
\mathcal{L}(p^{\perp})\leq 0
; therefore, one has

0\geq p\mathcal{L}(p^{\perp})p=\sum_{\ell\in I}(p^{\perp}L_{\ell}p)^{*}(p^{\perp}L_{\ell}p)\geq 0.

Hence, one obtains that
p^{\perp}L_{\ell}p=0
for all
\ell\in I
and
p\mathcal{L}(p^{\perp})p=0
. The negativity of
\mathcal{L}(p^{\perp})
implies that
\mathcal{L}(p^{\perp})=p^{\perp}\mathcal{L}(p^{\perp})p^{\perp}
(Lemma
A.1
) and from
p\mathcal{L}(p^{\perp})=0
one has that
p^{\perp}Gp=0
.

3.\Rightarrow 2.
Notice that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(p^{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{*}p^{\perp}+p^{\perp}G+\sum_{\ell\in I}L_{\ell}^{*}p^{\perp}L_{\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{\perp}G^{*}p^{\perp}+p^{\perp}Gp^{\perp}+\sum_{\ell\in I}p^{\perp}L_{\ell}^{*}p^{\perp}L_{\ell}p^{\perp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{\perp}\mathcal{L}(p^{\perp})p^{\perp}\leq 0,

where the last inequality follows from the fact that

p^{\perp}\mathcal{T}_{t}(p^{\perp})p^{\perp}\leq p^{\perp}\mathcal{T}_{t}(\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl})p^{\perp}\leq p^{\perp}
.
∎

Definition 2.4
.

A completely positive map
\Phi
is
irreducible
if it does not admit non-trivial subharmonic projections, i.e. if
p
is a projection and
\Phi(p)\geq p
, then
p=0
or
p=\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}
.

A QMS
\mathcal{T}
is
irreducible
if
(\mathcal{T}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
do not admit non-trivial common subharmonic projections, i.e.
if
p
is a projection and
\mathcal{T}_{t}(p)\geq p
for all
t\geq 0
, then
p=0
or
p=\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}
.

Notice that irreducibility of a QMS is an a priori weaker requirement than irreducibility of the single maps belonging to the semigroup. The definition we just gave of irreducibility for QMSs is the natural generalization of the same notion for classical Markov semigroups and was introduced in
[
14
]
. Notice that subharmonic projections allow to
reduce the dynamics
in the sense stated by the following result.

Proposition 2.5
.

The following are equivalent:

1.

p
is a subharmonic projection,

2.

\mathcal{T}
preserves the algebra
p^{\perp}{\cal B}(\mathsf{h})p^{\perp}
, in the sense that

\mathcal{T}_{t}(x)=p^{\perp}\mathcal{T}_{t}(x)p^{\perp},\quad x\in p^{\perp}{\cal B}(\mathsf{h})p^{\perp};

3.

\mathcal{T}_{*}
preserves
p\mathfrak{I}_{1}(\mathsf{h})p
, in the sense that

\mathcal{T}_{*t}(x)=p\mathcal{T}_{*t}(x)p,\quad x\in p\mathfrak{I}_{1}(\mathsf{h})p.

Proof.

First of all, notice that it suffices to prove
2.
and
3.
for positive elements.

1.\Rightarrow 2.
Let us consider
x\geq 0
such that
x=p^{\perp}xp^{\perp}
, then for all
t\geq 0

0\leq\mathcal{T}_{t}(x)\leq\mathcal{T}_{t}(\|x\|p^{\perp})\leq\|x\|p^{\perp}

and we can conclude thanks to the positivity of
\mathcal{T}_{t}(x)
.

2.\Rightarrow 1.
Notice that

\mathcal{T}_{t}(p^{\perp})=p^{\perp}\mathcal{T}_{t}(p^{\perp})p^{\perp}\leq p^{\perp}\mathcal{T}_{t}(\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl})p^{\perp}=p^{\perp}.

2.\Rightarrow 3.
We will prove that
2.
implies
3.
and the opposite implication can be shown in the same way. Let us consider
\eta\geq 0
,
\eta=p\eta p
, then
2.
implies that for every
x\in p^{\perp}{\cal B}(\mathsf{h})p^{\perp}
one has that for all

{\rm tr}(\mathcal{T}_{*t}(\eta)x)={\rm tr}(\eta\mathcal{T}_{t}(x))={\rm tr}(\eta p^{\perp}\mathcal{T}_{t}(x)p^{\perp})=0,

which implies that
\mathcal{T}_{*t}(\eta)=p\mathcal{T}_{*t}(\eta)p
and we are done.

∎

Thanks to Proposition
2.3
, we can rephrase the notion of irreducibility in terms of the operators appearing
in the infinitesimal generator. Before stating the Theorem, let us introduce the following notation: given a set of
bounded linear operators

let us denote by
\mathcal{A}(S)
the smallest algebra closed
in the strong operator topology which contains

{\rm span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{\,A_{1}A_{2}\cdots A_{n-1}A_{n}\,\mid\,\,A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\in S,\,n\geq 1\,\right\}.

Proposition 2.6
.

If
\mathcal{T}
is uniformly continuous, the following are equivalent:

1.

\mathcal{T}
is irreducible,

2.

G
and
L_{\ell}
’s have no common invariant subspaces other than
\{0\}
and

,

3.

For every nonzero

,

\left\{Av\,\mid\,\,A\in\mathcal{A}(\{G,L_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in I})\right\}=\mathsf{h}
.

If

is finite dimensional, the previous statements are also equivalent to

4.

\mathcal{A}(\{G,L_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in I})=\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{h})
.

For references regarding the equivalence between

and

, we refer to

]

The equivalence between

and

and

for some nonzero

, which, in turn, is an invariant subspace for

and

s itself.

Finally, the equivalence between

and

when

is finite dimensional is Burnside theorem
(see
[

]
for a simple short proof).
∎

We remark that an extension of Theorem 2 in
[
44
]
to the infinite dimensional setting is a simple corollary of Proposition
2.6
. It provides a sufficient condition for irreducibility. Before stating the result, we need to introduce some more notation. We say that the set
{\rm span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{L_{\ell}:\ell\in I\}
is self adjoint if for all

, there exist complex numbers

(z_{\ell\,j})_{j\in I}
such that

, the series being strongly convergent; given a set

Corollary 2.7
.

Let us assume that
\mathcal{T}
is uniformly continuous and that the set
{\rm span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{L_{\ell}:\ell\in I\}

{\rm span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{L_{\ell}:\ell\in I\}^{\prime}=\mathbb{C}\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}

Proof.

Let us assume that
G

denote the corresponding orthogonal projection. One has that

for every

as well and

. Therefore, we just showed that

and, consequently,

.
∎

We just mention that another sufficient condition can be found in finite dimensional systems in terms of the so called Kossakowski matrix (see item 3. in Corollary 7.2 in

3
Irreducibility and invariant densities

In this section we focus on QMSs admitting an invariant density and we will mainly investigate the relationship between irreducibility and primitivity.

Definition 3.1
.

A QMS
\mathcal{T}
is said to be
primitive
if it admits a unique faithful invariant density.

This convention is usually adopted in the literature that studies functional inequalities for QMSs in finite dimensional systems (see for instance
[
26
,
36
]
), while, when dealing with discrete time dynamics, it usually refers to, in addition, assuming the absence of eigenvalues of modulus
1
other than
1
itself. In fact, the two notions are equivalent for QMSs in finite dimensions (see Theorem
4.2
).

An old result due to A. Frigerio (
[
23
]
Theorem 1) shows that, if a QMS
\mathcal{T}
has a faithful invariant density,
this is unique (hence the semigroup is primitive) if and only if
\mathcal{T}
is irreducible. The proof in
[
23
]
is not elementary because it depends on
ergodic theorems for
c_{0}
semigroups and the GNS construction which are not covered in standard functional analysis courses.
In this section we present an elementary proof. We start with the following lemma which is notoriously very useful in the analysis
of the asymptotic behavior of semigroups.

Lemma 3.2
.

Any weak
∗
limit of a sequence of Cesàro means
t_{m}^{-1}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\mathcal{T}_{r}(x)\mathrm{d}r
as

t_{m}
tends to infinity is a fixed point for all maps
\mathcal{T}_{t}
.

Proof.

Denote by
y
the weak
∗
limit of the sequence. For all
m,s\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{s}\left(\frac{1}{t_{m}}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\mathcal{T}_{r}(x)\mathrm{d}r\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t_{m}}\int_{s}^{t_{m}+s}\mathcal{T}_{r}(x)\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t_{m}}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\mathcal{T}_{r}(x)\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t_{m}}\int_{t_{m}}^{t_{m}+s}\mathcal{T}_{r}(x)\mathrm{d}r-\frac{1}{t_{m}}\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{T}_{r}(x)\mathrm{d}r

The integrals of the last two terms are bounded because

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{t_{m}}^{t_{m}+s}\mathcal{T}_{r}(x)\mathrm{d}r\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{t_{m}}^{t_{m}+s}\|\mathcal{T}_{r}(x)\|\mathrm{d}r\leq s\|x\|

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{T}_{r}(x)\mathrm{d}r\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\|\mathcal{T}_{r}(x)\|\mathrm{d}r\leq s\|x\|

Therefore, taking weak
∗
limits as
t_{m}
tends to infinity, we find
\mathcal{T}_{s}(y)=y
.
∎

Before stating the next result, let us introduce the set of fixed points of
\mathcal{T}
:

{\cal F}(\mathcal{T}):=\{x\in{\cal B}(\mathsf{h}):\,\text{for all }t\geq 0,\,\mathcal{T}_{t}(x)=x\}.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a QMS with a faithful invariant density
\rho
.

An orthogonal projection
p
is subharmonic if and only if
p\in{\cal F}({\cal T})
.

Moreover, the following are equivalent:

1.

\mathcal{T}
is irreducible,

2.

The set of fixed points is trivial, i.e.
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{T})=\mathbb{C}\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}
,

3.

\rho
is the unique invariant density, i.e.
\mathcal{T}
is primitive.

If
\mathcal{T}
is uniformly continuous, the previous items are equivalent to

4.

\{G,L_{\ell},L_{\ell}^{*}\}^{\prime}=\{H,L_{\ell},L_{\ell}^{*}\}^{\prime}=\mathbb{C}\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}
.

Notice that, since subharmonic projections are in fact harmonic when there exists a faithful invariant density, in this particular case the notion of irreducibility that we are considering (usually referred to as Davies irreducibility, see
[
14
]
) coincides with the one introduced by Evans (
[
17
]
).

Proof.

If an orthogonal projection
p
is in
{\cal F}({\cal T})
, then it is trivially a subharmonic projection as well. Let us show that the converse is also true. Let
p
be a subharmonic projection. By the invariance of
\rho
we have

{\rm tr}(\rho\mathcal{T}_{t}(p))={\rm tr}(\rho p)
, therefore

{\rm tr}\left(\rho(\mathcal{T}_{t}(p)-p)\right)=0
.
Since
\rho
is faithful and
\mathcal{T}_{t}(p)\geq p
, it follows that
\mathcal{T}_{t}(p)=p
for all
t\geq 0
,
namely
p
belongs to
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{T})
.

1.\Rightarrow 2.
The set
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{T})
of fixed points is a von Neumann subalgebra of
\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{h})
.
Indeed, it is a
∗
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{h})
because, if
x,y\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{T})
, then

\mathcal{T}_{t}((x+\theta y)^{*}(x+\theta y))\geq\mathcal{T}_{t}((x+\theta y)^{*})\mathcal{T}_{t}((x+\theta y))=(x+\theta y)^{*}(x+\theta y)

for all
\theta\in\mathbb{C}
,
t\geq 0
. Moreover, by the invariance of
\rho
,

{\rm tr}\left(\rho\mathcal{T}_{t}((x+\theta y)^{*}(x+\theta y))-(x+\theta y)^{*}(x+\theta y)\right)=0

and so, by faithfulness of
\rho
,
\mathcal{T}_{t}((x+\theta y)^{*}(x+\theta y))=(x+\theta y)^{*}(x+\theta y)
.
By the arbitrariness of
\theta
,
\mathcal{T}_{t}(x^{*}y)=x^{*}y
for all
t\geq 0
.
Furthermore,
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{T})
is clearly closed for the weak
∗
topology and so it is a von Neumann algebra which is
generated by its projections.
Any projection
p
in
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{T})
is also a subharmonic projection, and so it is trivial by irreducibility.
Therefore
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{T})=\mathbb{C}\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}
.

2.\Rightarrow 1.
Let
p
be a subharmonic projection. We proved that, under the assumption that there exists a faithful normal invariant state,
p
belongs to
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{T})=\mathbb{C}\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}
. This shows that
p
is either
0
or
1l
,
and
\mathcal{T}
is irreducible.

2.\Rightarrow 3.
Note that, for all
x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{h})
,
the operators
t^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{s}(x)\mathrm{d}s
(
t>0
) are contained in the ball of radius
\|x\|_{\infty}

which is sequentially weakly
∗
compact, therefore we can find a divergent sequence
(t_{m})_{m\geq 0}
weakly
∗
converging
to some limit. This belongs to
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{T})
by Lemma
3.2
, and so it is a multiple
c(x)\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}
of the identity operator.

Note that, for all
t>0
,

{\rm tr}(\rho x)={\rm tr}\left(\rho\,\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{r}(x)\mathrm{d}r\right)

and so

c(x)=\lim_{m\to\infty}{\rm tr}\left(\rho\,\frac{1}{t_{m}}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\mathcal{T}_{r}(x)\mathrm{d}r\right)={\rm tr}(\rho x).

It follows that, for any invariant density
\eta
, we have

{\rm tr}\left(\eta x\right)={\rm tr}\left(\eta\,\left(\operatorname{w}^{*}-\lim_{m\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t_{m}}\!\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\mathcal{T}_{r}(x)\mathrm{d}r\right)\right)=c(x){\rm tr}(\eta)={\rm tr}(\rho x)

and
\eta=\rho
by the arbitrariness of
x
.

3.\Rightarrow 1.
By contradiction, if
\mathcal{T}
is not irreducible, then there exists a non-trivial subharmonic projection
p
. We showed that, since there exists a faithful normal invariant state,
p\in{\cal F}({\cal T})
.
Moreover, in order to prove
1\Rightarrow 2.
, we demostrated that for every
x\in{\cal F}({\cal T})
and for every
t\geq 0
, one has that

{\cal T}_{t}(x^{*}x)={\cal T}_{t}(x^{*}){\cal T}_{t}(x),\quad{\cal T}_{t}(xx^{*})={\cal T}_{t}(x){\cal T}_{t}(x^{*}).

Lemma
B.1
implies that for every
t\geq 0
and
x\in{\cal B}(\mathsf{h})
, the following holds true:

{\cal T}_{t}(qxr)=q{\cal T}_{t}(x)r,\quad q,r\in\{p,p^{\perp}\}.

By duality, the same can be shown to hold for the predual semigroup, i.e. for every
t\geq 0
and
\omega\in\mathfrak{I}_{1}(\mathsf{h})

{\cal T}_{*t}(q\omega r)=q{\cal T}_{*t}(\omega)r,\quad q,r\in\{p,p^{\perp}\}.

Therefore,
p\rho p/{\rm tr}(\rho p)
is another, non-faithful, invariant state
for
\mathcal{T}
contradicting
3.

∎

Leveraging on Theorem
3.3
, one can actually show that the equivalence between
1.
and
3.
keeps holding true even without assuming the faithfulness of
\rho
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a QMS admitting an invariant density
\rho
. The following are equivalent:

1.

\mathcal{T}
is irreducible,

2.

\rho
is faithful and it is the unique invariant density, i.e.
\mathcal{T}
is primitive.

3.

\mathcal{T}_{t_{0}}
is irreducible for all
t_{0}>0
.

4.

\mathcal{T}_{t_{0}}
is irreducible for some
t_{0}>0
.

The content of Proposition
3.4
is shown in
[
47
]
(Proposition 7.5) for finite dimensional systems. The proofs of some of the implications that we present below are simpler and hold in infinite dimensions as well.

Proof.

2.\Rightarrow 1.
If
{\cal T}
admits a unique faithful invariant state, then
{\cal T}
is irreducible thanks to the implication
3.\Rightarrow 1.
in Theorem
3.3
.

1.\Rightarrow 2.
On the other hand, let us now suppose that
{\cal T}
is irreducible. The support projection of
\rho
is subharmonic by Proposition
2.2
and, by irreducibility, it must coincide with the identity operator, therefore
\rho
is faithful. The statement follows now from the implication
1.\Rightarrow 3.
of Theorem
3.3
.

Before proving the equivalence of
1.
and
3.
, we remark that the proof of the equivalence between
1.
and
2.
in Theorem
3.3
and the equivalence between
1.
and
2.
that we just proved hold for discrete time QMSs as well, i.e. powers of a single normal completely positive and unital map. Moreover, one can check that the proof of Theorem
5.8
is independent from the equivalence between
1.
and
3.
that we are about to prove, therefore it does not cause any inconsistency to use it in this proof.

1.\Rightarrow 3.
If
\mathcal{T}
is irreducible, then we just showed that the invariant density
\rho
is unique and strictly positive. Theorem
5.8
implies that for every intial density
\eta
one has

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{T}_{*t}(\eta)=\rho

in trace class norm. By contradiction, let us suppose that there exists a time
t_{0}>0
such that
\mathcal{T}_{t_{0}}
is not irreducible, then, by Theorem
3.3
(applied to discrete time QMSs),
\mathcal{T}_{t_{0}}
admits another invariant density
\rho^{\prime}
other than
\rho
, however we get to the contradiction that

\rho^{\prime}=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{T}_{*nt_{0}}(\rho^{\prime})=\rho.

3.\Rightarrow 4.
is trivial.

4.\Rightarrow 1.
If
\mathcal{T}_{t_{0}}
is irreducible, then Theorem
3.3
(applied to discrete time QMSs) ensures that
\rho>0
and that

{\cal F}((\mathcal{T}_{nt_{0}})_{n\geq 0}):=\{x\in{\cal B}(\mathsf{h}):\mathcal{T}_{nt_{0}}(x)=x,\,n\geq 0\}=\mathbb{C}\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl};

since
{\cal F}(\mathcal{T})\subseteq{\cal F}((\mathcal{T}_{nt_{0}})_{n\geq 0}),
we can conclude.
∎

It is well known that, when the Hilbert space
\mathsf{h}
has finite dimension, one can always find an invariant density.
In fact, given an initial density
\eta
, thanks to the trace-norm compactness of the set of densities, one can find a
divergent sequence
(t_{m})_{m\geq 1}
such that
\Big(t_{m}^{-1}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\mathcal{T}_{*s}(\eta)\mathrm{d}s\Big)_{m\geq 1}

converges in trace norm to a density
\rho
. It is then verified, as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, that this limit is
an invariant density. We can then remove the hypothesis of the existence of an invariant density from Proposition
3.4

and find the following Corollary.

Corollary 3.5
.

If the Hilbert space
\mathsf{h}
is finite dimensional, then the following statements are equivalent:

1.

\mathcal{T}
is irreducible,

2.

\mathcal{T}
is primitive.

3.

\mathcal{T}_{t_{0}}
is irreducible for all
t_{0}>0
.

4.

\mathcal{T}_{t_{0}}
is irreducible for some
t_{0}>0
.

4
Positivity improvement and relaxation (finite dimension)

When
\mathsf{h}
is finite dimensional, irreducibility is equivalent to other properties that are frequently encountered in applications (see for instance
[
44
]
).

Definition 4.1
.

A QMS is called

•

Positivity improving
if
x\geq 0
and
x\not=0
implies
\mathcal{T}_{t}(x)>0

for all
t>0
.

•

Ergodic (relaxing)
if for every density
\eta
, there exists a desnity
\rho
such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{T}_{*t}(\eta)=\rho

in trace class norm.

We remark that
\rho
appearing in the definition of ergodicity is an invariant density, since for every
t\geq 0

{\cal T}_{*t}(\rho)={\cal T}_{*t}\left(\lim_{s\rightarrow+\infty}{\cal T}_{*s}(\rho)\right)=\lim_{s\rightarrow+\infty}{\cal T}_{*(t+s)}(\rho)=\rho.

It is worth noticing that positivity improvement can be defined in an equivalent way for the predual QMS
\mathcal{T}_{*}
.

One can prove the following result (
[
47
]
Proposition 7.5 p.125), which clarifies all the implications between the various notions introduced so far in the finite dimensional case.

Theorem 4.2
.

If the Hilbert space
\mathsf{h}
is finite dimensional, then the following are equivalent

1.

\mathcal{T}
is irreducible,

2.

\mathcal{T}
is primitive,

3.

\mathcal{T}_{t_{0}}
is irreducible for all
t_{0}>0
.

4.

\mathcal{T}_{t_{0}}
is irreducible for some
t_{0}>0
.

5.

\mathcal{T}
is primitive and
\sigma(\mathcal{L})\cap{\rm i}\mathbb{R}=\{0\}
.

6.

\mathcal{T}
is ergodic with limit state
\rho
being faithful.

7.

\mathcal{T}
is positivity improving.

Proof.

The first four items are equivalent by Corollary
3.5
.

3.\Rightarrow 5.
If the map
\mathcal{T}_{t}
for all
t>0
, then, by the Perron-Frobenius theorem
B.2
, the eigenspace of an eigenvalue
\lambda_{t}

with
|\lambda_{t}|=1
is one-dimensional and eigenvectors are multiple of a unitary operator
u
.
Applying
\mathcal{T}_{s}
to the identity
\mathcal{T}_{t}(u)=\lambda_{t}u
, we find

\mathcal{T}_{t}\left(\mathcal{T}_{s}(u)\right)=\mathcal{T}_{s}\left(\mathcal{T}_{t}(u)\right)=\lambda_{t}\mathcal{T}_{s}(u)

which implies that
\mathcal{T}_{s}(u)=\theta_{s}u
for some
\theta_{s}
with
|\theta_{s}|\leq 1
for all
s>0
.
Continuity in
s
, the semigroup property and
\theta_{0}=1,\,\theta_{t}=\lambda_{t}
with
|\lambda_{t}|=1
,
imply
\theta_{s}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta s}
for some
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. But
\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta s}

belongs to a finite subgroup of
\mathbb{T}
for all
s
if and only if
\theta=0
.
Since all the other eigenvalues of
\mathcal{T}_{t}
have modulus strictly smaller than
1
for all
t\geq 0
, it
follows that the generator
\mathcal{L}
has eigenvalue
0
with eigenvector
1l
and all the other
eigenvalues with strictly negative real part.

5.\Rightarrow 6.
The pre-dual generator
\mathcal{L}_{*}
has the same
eigenvalues as
\mathcal{L}
, therefore
\mathcal{T}_{*t}=\mathrm{e}^{t\mathcal{L}_{*}}
has all eigenvalues of the
form
\mathrm{e}^{\lambda t}
with
\operatorname{Re}(\lambda)<0
except
1
. Therefore

\mathcal{T}_{*t}(\eta)
converges to
\rho
as
t
goes to
+\infty
for all initial density
\eta
.

6.\Rightarrow 7.
If we prove the statement for pure states, we are done.
Fix a normalized vector
v\in\mathsf{h}
; since in finite dimensions the set of strictly positive matrices is open and,
by assumption,
\mathcal{T}_{t}(|v\rangle\langle v|)
converges to
\rho>0
for large times, there exists
t_{1}>0
such that
\mathcal{T}_{t}(|v\rangle\langle v|)>0
as well for every
t\geq t_{1}
. Lemma
C.2
shows that the support of
\mathcal{T}_{t}(|v\rangle\langle v|)
for
t>0
is given by
P_{t}\mathcal{S}(v)
, for a certain subspace

\mathcal{S}(v)
of
\mathsf{h}
. Since
P_{t}
is bijective, the dimension of the
support of
\mathcal{T}_{t}(|\psi\rangle\langle v|)
for
t>0
is constant and it is maximum for
t\geq t_{1}
, we are done.

7.
\Rightarrow
1. If
\mathcal{T}
is positivity improving then, for all non-trivial projection
p
,
the kernel of
\mathcal{T}_{t}(p^{\perp})
is
\{0\}
and so the inequality
\mathcal{T}_{t}(p^{\perp})\leq p^{\perp}

cannot hold.
∎

5
Infinite dimensional case

In this section we analyze the relationship between irreducibility and all the notions introduces so far -primitivity, positivity improvement and ergodicity- for a QMS on
\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{h})

with
\mathsf{h}
infinite dimensional.
In this situation two fundamental difficulties emerge:
\mathcal{T}
may not possess any invariant density and
\mathcal{T}
could fail to be uniformly continuous.

5.1
Primitivity

There are many irreducible QMSs without invariant states, as the following simple example shows; therefore we cannot hope of removing the assumption in Proposition
3.4
as in the case of finite dimensional systems.

Example 5.1
.

Let
\mathsf{h}=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N};\mathbb{C})
be the space of square summable complex valued sequences which is naturally
isomorphic to the Fock space
\Gamma(\mathbb{C})
, with canonical
orthonormal basis
(e_{k})_{k\geq 0}
. Let
S
be the right shift defined by
Se_{k}=e_{k+1}
and
let
L_{1}=S
,
L_{2}=S^{*}
. Consider the QMS generated by

\mathcal{L}(x)=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\left(L_{\ell}^{*}L_{\ell}x-2L_{\ell}^{*}xL_{\ell}+xL_{\ell}^{*}L_{\ell}\right)

This is positivity improving because, if we denote
G=-(L_{1}^{*}L_{1}+L_{2}^{*}L_{2})/2
, Lemma
C.2
ensures that the support of any

\mathcal{T}_{*t}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)
is

\mathrm{e}^{tG}\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{\,\psi,\ \delta_{G}^{m_{1}}(L_{\ell_{1}})\delta_{G}^{m_{2}}(L_{\ell_{2}})\cdots\delta_{G}^{m_{n}}(L_{\ell_{n}})\psi\,\right\}

for
n\geq 1
,
\ell_{1}\dots,\ell_{n}\in I
,
m_{1},\dots,m_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
, which is
S,S^{*}
(and so also
G
) invariant.
The only
S,S^{*}
invariant subspaces of
\mathsf{h}
are trivial.
Therefore the QMS generated by
\mathcal{L}
is positivity improving and irreducible.

To check that it has no invariant density we note that the restriction of
\mathcal{L}

to the abelian von Neumann algebra
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{N};\mathbb{C})
generated by rank-one projections
|e_{n}\rangle\langle e_{n}|

determines a classical continuous time Markov chain without invariant density.
Let
\mathcal{E}
be the conditional expectation

\mathcal{E}:\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{h})\mapsto L^{\infty}(\mathbb{N};\mathbb{C})\qquad\mathcal{E}(x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\left\langle e_{n},x\,e_{n}\right\rangle\,|e_{n}\rangle\langle e_{n}|\,.

Indeed, since
Ge_{n}=-e_{n}
for
n>0
,
Ge_{0}=-e_{0}/2
, we have

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{E})(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle e_{0},xe_{0}\rangle\left(|e_{1}\rangle\langle e_{1}|-|e_{0}\rangle\langle e_{0}|\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\langle e_{n},xe_{n}\rangle\left(|e_{n+1}\rangle\langle e_{n+1}|-2|e_{n}\rangle\langle e_{n}|+|e_{n-1}\rangle\langle e_{n-1}|\right)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{E}\circ\mathcal{L})(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\langle e_{n},(G^{*}x+S^{*}xS+SxS^{*}+xG)e_{n}\rangle|e_{n}\rangle\langle e_{n}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\langle e_{1},xe_{1}\rangle-\langle e_{0},xe_{0}\rangle\right)|e_{0}\rangle\langle e_{0}|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\langle e_{n+1},xe_{n+1}\rangle+\langle e_{n-1},xe_{n-1}\rangle-2\langle e_{n},xe_{n}\rangle\right)|e_{n}\rangle\langle e_{n}|

and
(\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{E})(x)-(\mathcal{E}\circ\mathcal{L})(x)=0
. As a consequence

\mathcal{T}\circ\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}\circ\mathcal{T}
for all
t\geq 0
and the restriction of

\mathcal{T}
to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{N};\mathbb{C})
is the Markov semigroup of a classical
time-continuous birth-and-death chain with generator

\displaystyle(Af)(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(f(n+1)-f(n)\right)+\left(f(n-1)-f(n)\right)\quad\text{for}\quad n>0,

\displaystyle(Af)(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(f(1)-f(0)\right).

This has no classical invariant density.

Suppose that
\rho
is an invariant density for
\mathcal{T}
. Note that, for all
x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{h})

and all
t\geq 0
, we have

{\rm tr}(\mathcal{E}_{*}(\rho)\,x)={\rm tr}(\rho\,\mathcal{E}(x))={\rm tr}(\rho\,\mathcal{T}_{t}(\mathcal{E}(x)))={\rm tr}(\rho\,\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{T}_{t}(x)))={\rm tr}(\mathcal{E}_{*}(\rho)\mathcal{T}_{t}(x))

It follows that
\mathcal{E}_{*}(\rho)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\langle e_{n},\rho e_{n}\rangle|e_{n}\rangle\langle e_{n}|
would determine
the classical invariant density
(\langle e_{n},\rho e_{n}\rangle)_{n\geq 1}
of the above birth-and-death chain.
Therefore
\mathcal{T}
has no invariant density.

5.2
Ergodicity

Let us now move to the property of the semigroup of relaxing towards a unique faithful density. When
\mathsf{h}
is finite dimensional, this follows from the Perron-Frobenius
theorem as shown in the proof Theorem
4.2
. First, we will provide an elementary proof for Cesàro means, i.e.

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{*s}(\eta)ds.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a QMS with a faithful invariant density
\rho
. For all initial density
\eta
dominated by
\rho

there exists a divergent sequence
(t_{m})_{m\geq 1}
such that

\left(t_{m}^{-1}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\mathcal{T}_{*r}(\eta)\mathrm{d}r\right)_{m\geq 1}

converges in trace norm to a density
\eta_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Let
b>0
be a constant such that
\eta\leq b\rho
.
Denote
\eta_{t}=t^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{*r}(\eta)\mathrm{d}r
. Let
(e_{j})_{j\geq 0}
be an orthonormal basis
with respect to which
\rho
is diagonal.

For all
n>0
let
p_{n}
be a finite dimensional spectral projection
of
\rho
such that
{\rm tr}(p_{n}^{\perp}\rho)<1/bn
.

Note that, by finite dimensionality of
p_{n}
for all
n>0
,
(p_{n}\eta_{t}p_{n})_{t\geq 0}
are non-negative matrices
with trace norm less or equal than
1
. Therefore we can find a sequence
(t_{n}(m))_{m\geq 0}
such that

(p_{n}\eta_{t_{n}(m)}p_{n})_{m\geq 0}
is trace norm converging.

By the diagonal method we can find a sequence
(t_{n})_{n>0}
such that each
(p_{n}\eta_{t_{n}}p_{n})_{n>0}

trace norm converges as follows. For
n=1
let
(t_{1}(m))_{m\geq 0}
be a strictly increasing
sequence such that
p_{1}\eta_{t_{1}(m)}p_{1}
converges to
\eta^{(1)}=p_{1}\eta^{(1)}p_{1}
.
Proceeding by induction, for all
n>0
, we can extract from the sequence
(t_{n}(m))_{m\geq 0}

such that
p_{n}\eta_{t_{n}(m)}p_{n}
converges to
\eta^{(n)}=p_{n}\eta^{(n)}p_{n}
, another subsequence

(t_{n+1}(m))_{m\geq 0}
such that
p_{n+1}\eta_{t_{n+1}(m)}p_{n+1}
converges to

\eta^{(n+1)}=p_{n+1}\eta^{(n+1)}p_{n+1}
. Choosing
t_{n}:=t_{n}(n)
we are done.

Clearly,
p_{n}\eta^{(n^{\prime})}p_{n}=\eta^{(n)}
for all
n^{\prime}>n
. Therefore, for all
n^{\prime}>n
,

\eta^{(n)}-\eta^{(n^{\prime})}=\left(\eta^{(n)}-\eta^{(n^{\prime})}\right)p_{n}^{\perp}+p_{n}^{\perp}\left(\eta^{(n)}-\eta^{(n^{\prime})}\right)p_{n}

Notice that for a positive operator
a\geq 0
, one has
\|p^{\perp}ap\|_{1}\leq\|ap^{\perp}\|_{1}
(it can be easily seen by duality), therefore it follows that, for
n^{\prime}>n

\left\|\eta^{(n)}-\eta^{(n^{\prime})}\right\|_{1}\leq 2\left\|\eta^{(n)}p_{n}^{\perp}\right\|_{1}+2\left\|\eta^{(n^{\prime})}p_{n}^{\perp}\right\|_{1}.

Note that
\eta\leq b\rho
implies
\eta_{t}\leq b\rho
for all
t>0
and, by Hölder inequality and the domination property, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\eta^{(n)}p_{n}^{\perp}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|(\eta^{(n)})^{1/2}(\eta^{(n)})^{1/2}p_{n}^{\perp}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|(\eta^{(n)})^{1/2}\right\|_{2}\left\|(\eta^{(n)})^{1/2}p_{n}^{\perp}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\rm tr}\left(p_{n}^{\perp}\eta^{(n)}p_{n}^{\perp}\right)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{b}\left({\rm tr}\left(p_{n}^{\perp}\rho p_{n}^{\perp}\right)\right)^{1/2}<1/\sqrt{n}

In the same way we find
\left\|\eta^{(n^{\prime})}p_{n}^{\perp}\right\|_{1}<1/\sqrt{n}
, and we get the inequality

\left\|\eta^{(n)}-\eta^{(n^{\prime})}\right\|_{1}<4/\sqrt{n}.

This implies that
(\eta^{(n)})_{n\geq 1}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathfrak{I}_{1}(\mathsf{h})
. Denoting
\eta_{\infty}
its limit
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{t_{n}}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\mathcal{T}_{*r}(\eta)\mathrm{d}r-\eta_{\infty}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{t_{n}}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\mathcal{T}_{*r}(\eta)\mathrm{d}r-\eta^{(n)}\right\|_{1}+\left\|\eta^{(n)}-\eta_{\infty}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{t_{n}}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\mathcal{T}_{*r}(\eta)\mathrm{d}r-\eta^{(n)}\right\|_{1}+\frac{4}{\sqrt{n}}

which becomes arbitrarily small as
n
tends to infinity.
∎

By another approximation argument we can remove domination.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a QMS with a faithful invariant density
\rho
. For all initial density
\eta

there exists a divergent sequence
(t_{m})_{m\geq 1}
such that

\left(t_{m}^{-1}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\mathcal{T}_{*r}(\eta)\mathrm{d}r\right)_{m\geq 1}

converges in trace norm to a density
\eta_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Since
\rho
is faithful, the set of densities dominated by
\rho
is trace-norm dense in the set of all densities
(truncate with spectral projections of
\rho
and approximate).
For all
\epsilon>0
let
\eta^{(\epsilon)}
be an initial density dominated by
\rho
such that

\|\eta-\eta_{\epsilon}\|_{1}<\epsilon/2
and let
(t_{m})_{m\geq 1}
be a strictly increasing sequence such that

\left(t_{m}^{-1}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\mathcal{T}_{*r}(\eta_{\epsilon})\mathrm{d}r\right)_{m\geq 1}
converges in trace norm
to
\eta^{(\epsilon)}_{\infty}
as
m
tends to infinity. Clearly, by Lemma
5.2
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{m,n\to\infty}\left\|\frac{1}{t_{m}}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\mathcal{T}_{*r}(\eta)\mathrm{d}r-\frac{1}{t_{n}}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\mathcal{T}_{*r}(\eta)\mathrm{d}r\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle<\epsilon+\limsup_{m,n\to\infty}\left\|\frac{1}{t_{m}}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\mathcal{T}_{*r}(\eta_{\epsilon})\mathrm{d}r-\frac{1}{t_{n}}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\mathcal{T}_{*r}(\eta_{\epsilon})\mathrm{d}r\right\|_{1}=\epsilon.

The conclusion follows by the arbitrariness of
\epsilon
.
∎

Finally, we show that convergence of a subsequence implies convergence of the family

\left(t^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{*r}(\eta)\mathrm{d}r\right)_{t>0}
by a semigroup theoretic argument.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\eta
be a density. If there exists a divergent sequence
(t_{n})_{n\geq 1}
such that
\left(t_{n}^{-1}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\mathcal{T}_{*r}(\eta)\mathrm{d}r\right)_{n\geq 1}
trace-norm
converges to a density
\eta_{\infty}
, then
\eta_{\infty}
is an invariant density for
\mathcal{T}
and

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{*r}(\eta)\mathrm{d}r=\eta_{\infty}

in trace norm.

Proof.

\eta_{\infty}
is an invariant density for
\mathcal{T}
by Lemma
3.2
.
Let
\eta_{t}=t^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{*s}(\eta_{0})\mathrm{d}s
.
For all
\epsilon>0
let
n_{\epsilon}>0
such that

\left\|\eta_{t_{n}}-\eta_{\infty}\right\|_{1}=\left\|\frac{1}{t_{n}}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\mathcal{T}_{*s}(\eta_{0})\mathrm{d}s-\eta_{\infty}\right\|_{1}<\epsilon

for all
n\geq n_{\epsilon}
. Now, for all
t>t_{n_{\epsilon}}
,
n\geq n_{\epsilon}
and
0\leq s\leq t_{n_{\epsilon}}
, we have

\mathcal{T}_{*s}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{*r}(\eta_{0})\mathrm{d}r\right)-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{*r}(\eta_{0})\mathrm{d}r=\frac{1}{t}\int_{t}^{s+t}\mathcal{T}_{*r}(\eta_{0})\mathrm{d}r-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{T}_{*r}(\eta_{0})\mathrm{d}r

and so

\left\|\mathcal{T}_{*s}\left(\eta_{t}\right)-\eta_{t}\right\|_{1}\leq\frac{2s}{t}\leq\frac{2t_{n_{\epsilon}}}{t}.

Fix a
t(\epsilon)>0
, depending on
\epsilon
and
n_{\epsilon}
, such that
2t_{n_{\epsilon}}/t\,<\epsilon
.
Integrating for
s\in[0,t_{n_{\epsilon}}]
and dividing by
t_{n_{\epsilon}}
, we find

\left\|\frac{1}{t_{n_{\epsilon}}}\int_{0}^{t_{n_{\epsilon}}}\mathcal{T}_{*s}(\eta_{t})\mathrm{d}r-\eta_{t}\right\|_{1}<\epsilon.

Notice that

\frac{1}{t_{n_{\epsilon}}}\int_{0}^{t_{n_{\epsilon}}}\mathcal{T}_{*s}(\eta_{t})\mathrm{d}r=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{*s}(\eta_{t_{n_{\epsilon}}})\mathrm{d}r.

It follows that, for all
t>t(\epsilon)
,

\displaystyle\left\|\eta_{t}-\eta_{\infty}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{*r}(\eta_{0})\mathrm{d}r-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{*r}(\eta_{\infty})\mathrm{d}r\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{*r}(\eta_{0}-\eta_{t_{n_{\epsilon}}})\mathrm{d}r\right\|_{1}+\left\|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{*r}(\eta_{\infty}-\eta_{t_{n_{\epsilon}}})\mathrm{d}r\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\eta_{t}-\eta_{t_{n_{\epsilon}}}\right\|_{1}+\left\|\eta_{\infty}-\eta_{t_{n_{\epsilon}}}\right\|_{1}<2\epsilon.

This completes the proof.
∎

The results of Lemmas
5.2
,
5.3
,
5.4
are summarized in the following

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a primitive QMS. For every initial density
\eta
,
the family of densities
\left(t^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{*s}(\eta)\mathrm{d}s\right)_{t>0}
is trace norm convergent to
\rho
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
5.5
, one may wonder whether
\mathcal{T}_{*t}(\eta)
converges to
\rho
.
We have no direct elementary proof, so we will rely on some deep results without proving them and refer to the literature. First of all we need to introduce the so called reversible subalgebra
\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})
as the weak* closure of the following linear space:

{\rm span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{\,x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{h})\,\mid\,\text{for all }t\geq 0,\,\mathcal{T}_{t}(x)=\lambda_{t}x\text{ for some }\lambda_{t}\in\mathbb{C},\,|\lambda_{t}|=1\,\right\}.

One can show the following (see Lemma 1 and Theorem 1 in
[
10
]
and Propositions 2.1 and 2.2, noticing that everything extends to the continuous time case).

Theorem 5.6
.

Let us assume that
{\cal T}
admits a faithful invariant density
\rho
, then

\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})
is a von Neumann algebra on which
\mathcal{T}
acts as a semigroup of *-automorphisms. Moreover, if we denote by
B_{1}
and
M_{1}
the unit ball of
{\cal B}(\mathsf{h})
and
\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})
, respectively, the following equality holds true:

M_{1}=\bigcap_{t\geq 0}\mathcal{T}_{t}(B_{1}).

Finally, there exists a unitary operator
U
acting on
\mathsf{h}
such that

U:\mathsf{h}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\alpha\in A}\mathsf{h}_{\alpha}^{L}\otimes\mathsf{h}_{\alpha}^{R}

for some denumerable index set
A
and

\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})=U^{*}\bigoplus_{\alpha\in A}B(\mathsf{h}^{L}_{\alpha})\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\mathsf{h}_{\alpha}^{R}}U.

(5.1)

The reversible subalgebra plays a fundamental role in our investigations due to the following simple observation.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let us consider a QMS
\mathcal{T}
with a faithful invariant density
\rho
. Then, for every
x\in{\cal B}(\mathsf{h})
,
\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})
contains every accumulation point of the net
(\mathcal{T}_{t}(x))_{t\geq 0}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\|x\|\leq 1
. Notice that for every
t\geq s
,
\mathcal{T}_{t}(B_{1})\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{s}(B_{1})
, indeed
\mathcal{T}_{t}(B_{1})=\mathcal{T}_{s}(\mathcal{T}_{t-s}(B_{1}))
and
\mathcal{T}_{t-s}(B_{1})\subseteq B_{1}
; hence,

\mathcal{T}_{t}(B_{1})\subseteq\bigcap_{s\leq t}\mathcal{T}_{s}(B_{1}).

Therefore, if we consider any accumulation point
\overline{x}
of the net
(\mathcal{T}_{t}(x))_{t\geq 0}
, we have that
\overline{x}\in\bigcap_{t\geq 0}\mathcal{T}_{t}(B_{1})=M_{1}
.
∎

The previous Lemma points out that a natural strategy for proving that primitivity implies relaxation towards a unique faithful invariant density is to show that primitivity implies
\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})=\mathbb{C}\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}
and this is, indeed, what we are going to do.

Theorem 5.8
.

If
{\cal T}
is primitive with unique invariant density
\rho>0
, then
\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})=\mathbb{C}\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}
and for every density
\eta

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}{\cal T}_{*t}(\eta)=\rho

in trace class norm.

Notice that
\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})=\mathbb{C}\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}
can be seen as a restatement of item
5.
in Theorem
4.2
(where, in infinite dimensional systems, one only considers the point spectrum).

Proof.

Without loss of generality and in order to simplify notation, we can assume that
U
in Theorem
5.6
is the identity operator.

For every
x\in{\cal B}(\mathsf{h})
, the set
\{{\cal T}_{t}(x)\}_{t\geq 0}
is sequentially relatively compact in the weak* topology and, therefore, in order to show that it converges, it is enough to prove that all accumulation points of the net coincide. We will now show that irreducibility of
{\cal T}
implies that
\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})=\mathbb{C}\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}
.

Step 1.
First of all, we will show that primitivity implies that
A
in Theorem
5.6
is a singleton. Let us denote by
Z
the center of
\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})
, i.e. the set of all the elements in
\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})
that commute with all the other elements in
\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})
; it is easy to see from equation (
5.1
) that the center is the
{\rm W}^{*}
-algebra generated by
p_{\alpha}
s, where
p_{\alpha}:=p_{\mathsf{h}_{\alpha}^{L}\otimes\mathsf{h}_{\alpha}^{R}}
for
\alpha\in A
. Notice that
{\cal T}
maps
Z
into itself. Indeed, for every
z\in\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})^{\prime}
and
y\in\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})

{\cal T}_{t}(z)y={\cal T}_{t}(z){\cal T}_{t}(x)={\cal T}_{t}(zx)={\cal T}_{t}(xz)={\cal T}_{t}(x){\cal T}_{t}(z)=y{\cal T}_{t}(z),

where
x
is the unique element in
\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})
such that
y={\cal T}_{t}(x)
(one can find such
x
because Theorem
5.6
ensures that
\mathcal{T}_{t}
acts as a *-automorphism on
\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})
); we just proved that
\mathcal{T}
maps
\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})^{\prime}
into itself and the claim follows from the fact that
Z=\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})\cap\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})^{\prime}
. Moreover, since
{\cal T}_{t}
acts on
Z
as a
∗
-automorphism,
{\cal T}_{t}(p_{\alpha})=\sum_{\beta\in A}x_{\beta,\alpha}(t)p_{\beta}
is a projection, i.e.
x_{\beta,\alpha}(t)\in\{0,1\}
. By the continuity of
t\mapsto x_{\beta,\alpha}(t)
, one has that
{\cal T}_{t}(p_{\alpha})=p_{\alpha}
for every time
t\geq 0
, i.e.
p_{\alpha}\in{\cal F}({\cal T})
. Since
Z
is generated by
p_{\alpha}
s, then
Z\subseteq{\cal F}({\cal T})=\mathbb{C}\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}
, where the last equality is due to primitivity. Therefore,
A
is a singleton,
\mathsf{h}=\mathsf{h}^{L}\otimes\mathsf{h}^{R}
and
\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})=B(\mathsf{h}^{L})\otimes\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}_{\mathsf{h}^{R}}
.

Step 2.
We will show that
\rho=\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}
for some states
\rho_{1}
,
\rho_{2}
on
B(\mathsf{h}^{L})
and
B(\mathsf{h}^{R})
, respectively. In step
1.
we showed that
\mathcal{T}
maps
\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})^{\prime}=\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}_{\mathsf{h}^{L}}\otimes B(\mathsf{h}^{R})
into itself and, by the definition of the reversible subalgebra, we know that
\mathcal{T}
maps
\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})=B(\mathsf{h}^{L})\otimes\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}_{\mathsf{h}^{R}}
into itself as well; therefore we can define the two QMSs
\mathcal{T}^{1}
and
\mathcal{T}^{2}
acting on
B(\mathsf{h}^{L})
and
B(\mathsf{h}^{R})
, respectively, as

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{1}(x)\otimes\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}_{\mathsf{h}^{R}}:=\mathcal{T}_{t}(x\otimes\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}_{\mathsf{h}^{R}}),\quad x\in B(\mathsf{h}^{L})

\displaystyle\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}_{\mathsf{h}^{L}}\otimes\mathcal{T}_{2}(y):=\mathcal{T}_{t}(\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}_{\mathsf{h}^{L}}\otimes y),\quad y\in B(\mathsf{h}^{R}).

Moreover, notice that for all
x\in B(\mathsf{h}^{L})
,
y\in B(\mathsf{h}^{R})
and
t\geq 0
one has

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}_{t}(x\otimes y)&=\mathcal{T}_{t}((x\otimes\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}_{\mathsf{h}^{R}})(\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}_{\mathsf{h}^{L}}\otimes y))=\mathcal{T}_{t}(x\otimes\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}_{\mathsf{h}^{R}})\mathcal{T}_{t}(\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}_{\mathsf{h}^{L}}\otimes y)\\
&=(\mathcal{T}^{1}_{t}(x)\otimes\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}_{\mathsf{h}^{R}})(\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}_{\mathsf{h}^{L}}\otimes\mathcal{T}^{2}_{t}(y))=\mathcal{T}^{1}_{t}(x)\otimes\mathcal{T}^{2}_{t}(y),\end{split}

that is
\mathcal{T}_{t}=\mathcal{T}^{1}_{t}\otimes\mathcal{T}^{2}_{t}
.

Let us introduce the notation
\rho_{1}={\rm tr}_{2}(\rho)
and
\rho_{2}={\rm tr}_{1}(\rho)
, where
{\rm tr}_{1}
(
{\rm tr}_{2}
) is the partial trace with respect to
\mathsf{h}^{L}
(resp.
\mathsf{h}^{R}
); then, for every
x\in B(\mathsf{h}^{L})
and
t\geq 0
one has

\begin{split}{\rm tr}(\rho_{1}x)&={\rm tr}(\rho x\otimes\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}_{\mathsf{h}^{R}})={\rm tr}(\rho\mathcal{T}_{t}(x\otimes\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}_{\mathsf{h}^{R}}))\\
&={\rm tr}(\rho\mathcal{T}^{1}_{t}(x)\otimes\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}_{\mathsf{h}^{R}})={\rm tr}_{1}(\rho_{1}\mathcal{T}^{1}_{t}(x)),\end{split}

which means that
\rho_{1}
is an invariant density for
\mathcal{T}^{1}
; in the same way, one gets that
\rho_{2}
is an invariant density for
\mathcal{T}^{2}
. Therefore, using that
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{T}^{2}
, one gets that
\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}
is an invariant density for
\mathcal{T}
and, by primitivity,
\rho=\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}
.

Step 3.
For every
t\geq 0
,
{\cal T}^{1}_{t}
is a
∗
-automorphism; by Wigner theorem (Theorem 14.5 in
[
41
]
) there exists a unitary or antiunitary operator
U_{t}
such that
{\cal T}^{1}_{t}(x)=U_{t}^{*}xU_{t}
for every
x\in B(\mathsf{h}^{L})
; if
1<{\rm dim}(\mathsf{h}^{L})<+\infty
, it is easy to see that one can construct more than one invariant state for the semigroup
\mathcal{T}^{1}
and, consequently, for
\mathcal{T}
(attaching
\rho_{2}
). If
{\rm dim}(\mathsf{h}^{L})=+\infty
, let us consider the spectral resolution of
\rho_{1}=\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}p_{i}
(notice that
p_{i}
s are finite dimensional), then one has

\rho_{1}={\cal T}^{1}_{*t}(\rho_{1})=\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}{\cal T}^{1}_{*t}(p_{i})=\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}U_{t}(p_{i})U_{t}^{*}

and
U_{t}(p_{i})U_{t}^{*}
are orthogonal projections summing up (in the strong operator topology) to the identity operator. By uniqueness of the spectral decomposition,
p_{i}=U_{t}(p_{i})U_{t}^{*}={\cal T}_{*t}(p_{i})
for every
i\in I
, therefore
\rho_{i}=p_{i}/{\rm tr}(p_{i})\otimes\rho_{2}
is a collection of invariant densities with orthogonal supports and this contradicts primitivity of the semigroup. Therefore
{\rm dim}(\mathsf{h}^{L})=1
and we showed that
\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})=\mathbb{C}\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}
.

Summing up, for every
x\in{\cal B}(\mathsf{h})
we showed that every accumulation point of the net
\{{\cal T}_{t}(x)\}_{t\geq 0}
is of the form
\alpha\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
(Lemma
5.7
ensures that every accumulation point is in
\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})
and we showed that
\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})
is trivial); however, notice that
\alpha
is completely determined by
x
since for every
t\geq 0
,
{\rm tr}(\rho x)={\rm tr}(\rho{\cal T}_{t}(x))
, which implies that
\alpha=\rho(x)
. Therefore we showed that

{\rm w}^{*}-\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}{\cal T}_{t}(x)={\rm tr}(\rho x)\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl},\quad x\in{\cal B}(\mathsf{h}),

which is equivalent to

{\rm w}-\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}{\cal T}_{*t}(\eta)=\rho

for every density
\eta
. It is easy to see that the convergence in the previous equation is equivalent to convergence in trace class norm: indeed, for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a finite dimensional spectral projection of
\rho
, say
p_{\epsilon}
, such that
{\rm tr}(\rho p_{\epsilon}^{\perp})<\epsilon
. Using the weak convergence, one can find
t_{0}
such that for every
t\geq t_{0}
one has

{\rm tr}({\cal T}_{*t}(\eta)p_{\epsilon}^{\perp})<2\epsilon,\quad\|p_{\epsilon}{\cal T}_{*t}(\eta)p_{\epsilon}-p_{\epsilon}\rho\|_{1}<\epsilon.

Therefore,

\|{\cal T}_{*t}(\eta)-\rho\|_{1}\leq\|p_{\epsilon}{\cal T}_{*t}(\eta)p_{\epsilon}-p_{\epsilon}\rho\|_{1}+2\|({\cal T}_{*t}(\eta)-\rho)p_{\epsilon}^{\perp}\|_{1}\leq 7\epsilon

and we conclude by the arbitrariness of
\epsilon
.

∎

5.3
Positivity improvement

If the Hilbert space
\mathsf{h}
is infinite dimensional positivity improvement is stronger than irreducibility
(Example
5.10
below). However, if
\mathcal{T}
is uniformly continuous and under some extra assumptions, they become equivalent (see
[
35
]
Theorem 4.2). In order to state this result, we need to introduce the decoherence-free subalgebra (
[
2
,
22
,
43
]
):

{\cal N}(\mathcal{T}):=\{x\in{\cal B}(\mathsf{h}):\mathcal{T}_{t}(x^{*}x)=\mathcal{T}_{t}(x^{*})\mathcal{T}_{t}(x),\,\ t\geq 0\};

if the semigroup is uniformly continuous, one can easily see that
{\cal N}(\mathcal{T})
is the biggest von Neumann algebra on which
\mathcal{T}
acts as a semigroup of *-automorphisms (see Theorem 3 in
[
22
]
) and, if the semigroup admits a faithful invariant density, that it coincides with
\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})
(see Theorem 1 in
[
10
]
).

Theorem 5.9
.

Let us consider a uniformly continuous QMS
\mathcal{T}
such that the set
{\rm span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{\,L_{\ell}\,\mid\,\ell\in I\,\right\}
is self-adjoint.

The following are equivalent:

1.

{\cal N}(\mathcal{T})=\mathbb{C}\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}
,

2.

\mathcal{T}
is positivity improving.

If, in addition, we assume that
\mathcal{T}
has an invariant density, then the following are equivalent:

3.

\mathcal{T}
is irreducibile,

4.

\mathcal{T}
is positivity improving.

Proof.

The equivalence between
3.
and
4.
is a corollary of the equivalence of
1.
and
2.
: irreducibility implies that the invariant density is faithful (Proposition
3.4
) and, under the assumption of the existence of a faithful invariant density, irreducibility implies
\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})=\mathbb{C}\hbox{\rm 1\kern-2.8ptl}
(Theorem
5.6
) and
{\cal N}(\mathcal{T})
coincides with
\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})
(Theorem 1 in
[
10
]
).

Let us introduce the notation
\delta_{a}(b)=[a,b]
for every
a,b\in{\cal B}(\mathsf{h})
. Since the support of any pure initial density

|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
is characterized as in Lemma
C.2
, and the commutant of a self-adjoint set of
operators is trivial if and only if every nonzero vector is cyclic for that set (see
[
8
]
Proposition 2.3.8, p. 47)
positivity improvement is equivalent to the commutant of the linear span of the set of operators

\delta^{m_{1}}_{G}(L_{\ell_{1}})\cdots\delta^{m_{n}}_{G}(L_{\ell_{n}}),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}^{*},\ m_{1},..,m_{n}\in\mathbb{N},\ \ell_{1},..,\ell_{n}\in I,

(5.2)

being trivial; indeed, notice that the linear span of the set of operators in equation (
5.2
) is self-adjoint because of our assumption on the linear space generated by noise operators
{\rm span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{\,L_{\ell}\,\mid\,\ell\in I\,\right\}
.

In order to prove the equivalence between
1.
and
2.
it suffices to show that the commutant of the linear span of the operators in equation (
5.2
) coincides with the commutant of the set of operators given by

\{\delta_{H}^{k}(L_{\ell}),\,\delta_{H}^{k}(L_{\ell}^{*}):\,k\geq 0\}.

(5.3)

Indeed, one has that (see Proposition 2.1, 2.2 and 2.3 in
[
15
]
)

{\cal N}(\mathcal{T})=\{\delta_{H}^{k}(L_{\ell}),\,\delta_{H}^{k}(L_{\ell}^{*}):\,k\geq 0\}^{\prime}.

To this end, first note that both sets (
5.3
)
and (
5.2
) contain operators
L_{\ell},L_{k}^{*}
and polynomials in
L_{\ell},L_{k}^{*}
. Then, by the identity

\delta_{G}(L_{\ell})=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}[L_{k}^{*}L_{k},L_{\ell}]-\mathrm{i}\delta_{H}(L_{\ell}),

both sets contain operators
\delta_{G}(L_{\ell}),\delta_{G}(L_{\ell}^{*})
and
\delta_{H}(L_{\ell}),\delta_{H}(L_{\ell}^{*})
.
The conclusion can be extended to iterated commutators of any order by an induction argument as in the proof of
Lemma 4.1 of
[
35
]
and the proof is complete.
∎

To the best of our knowledge, it is yet unclear whether the equivalence between irreducibility and positivity improvement holds for a uniformly continuous irreducible
QMS, without any further assumptions.

Below we show an example of an irreducible QMS which is not positivity improving.
It is not uniformly continuous, neither it has any invariant density, but the unbounded generator
\mathcal{L}
is representable
in a GKSL form as in (
2.4
) allowing an unbounded self-adjoint operator
H
.
The QMS can be constructed from
\mathcal{L}
by the minimal semigroup method or as a bounded perturbation
of the unitary group generated by the unbounded derivation
x\mapsto\mathrm{i}[H,x]
(see Appendix B).

Example 5.10
.

Let
\mathsf{h}=L^{2}(\mathbb{T};\mathbb{C})
be the Hilbert space of periodic functions on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
and consider
the QMS with generator in a (generalized, since
H
is unbounded) GKSL form with

H=\mathrm{i}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\qquad(L_{\ell}f)(x)=2^{-|\ell|-1}\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\pi\ell x}f(x),\quad\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}=\mathbb{Z}-\{\,0\,\}.

Note that
L_{\ell}
are multiples of unitary multiplication operators and

6
Conclusion and Outlook

In this work we presented the state of the art regarding the understanding of irreducibility in open quantum Markovian dynamics and its relationship with several relevant features of the evolution: the existence and uniqueness of invariant densities, positivity improvement and relaxation towards a faithful density; more precisely we showed that, except from positivity improvement, they are all equivalent when the semigroup admits an invariant density. Many proofs have been simplified with respect to the literature and some new results have been added. In finite dimensional systems, everything is well understood and irreducible QMSs share all the relevant properties with their classical counterpart. On the other hand, to the best of our knowledge, the theory in infinite dimensions still has some unclear aspects: while the asymptotic behaviour of Cesàro means is well understood (see Theorem 2.3.23 in
[
29
]
), little is known about the net
\{\mathcal{T}_{*t}(\eta)\}_{t\geq 0}
for QMSs without an invariant density; another natural question is whether irreducibility implies positivity improvement in the case where the semigroup is uniformly continuous or admits an invariant density.

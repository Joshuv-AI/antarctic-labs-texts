Multi-Environment MDPs with Prior and Universal Semantics
Benjamin Bordais, Jean-François Raskin, Université Libre de Bruxelles

Abstract
Multiple-environment Markov decision processes (MEMDPs) equip an MDP with several probabilistic transition functions (one per possible environment) so that the state is observable but the environment is not. Previous work studies two semantics: (i) the universal semantics, where an adversary picks the environment; and (ii) the prior semantics, where the environment is drawn once before execution from a fixed distribution. We clarify the relation between these semantics. For parity objectives, we show that the qualitative questions, i.e. value one, coincide, and we develop a new algorithm for the general value of MEMDP with prior semantics. In particular, we show that the prior value of an MEMDP with a parity objective can be approximated to any precision with a space efficient algorithm; equivalently, the associated gap problem is decidable in PSPACE when probabilities are given in unary (and in EXPSPACE otherwise). We then prove that the universal value equals the infimum of prior values over all beliefs. This yields a new algorithm for the universal gap problem with the same complexity (PSPACE for unary probabilities, EXPSPACE in general), improving on earlier doubly-exponential-space procedures. Finally, we observe that MEMDPs under the prior semantics form an important tractable subclass of POMDPs: our algorithms exploit the fact that belief entropy never increases, and we establish that any POMDP with this property reduces effectively to a prior-MEMDP, showing that prior-MEMDPs capture a broad and practically relevant subclass of POMDPs.



1
Introduction

Multiple-environment Markov decision processes (MEMDPs), first introduced in
[
16
]
, generalize classical MDPs. They model decision-making problems where the system state is perfectly observable, while the stochastic
environment
is unknown but fixed and chosen or drawn before execution from a finite set of candidates.
Formally, an MEMDP consists of a common state and action space together with a probabilistic transition function per possible environment.
A single controller acts without being explicitely revealed which environment is operating. Intuitively, this model lies between fully observable MDPs and POMDPs: it captures a natural class of partial-information problems while avoiding many undecidability barriers of general POMDPs.

Initial work introduced MEMDPs and showed that key synthesis questions that are undecidable for POMDPs become decidable (and sometimes efficiently so) in MEMDPs, thereby justifying the model as a tractable yet expressive
variant
of POMDPs
[
16
]
.
Subsequent work further developed the theory for
\omega
-regular objectives and clarified the algorithmic landscape
[
8
,
19
]
.
Beyond foundational interest, MEMDPs have been advocated as a practical modeling tool: their structure enables cheaper belief updates than in general POMDPs and supports applications such as contextual recommendation and parameter uncertainty in probabilistic models
[
4
]
.

Universal and prior semantics.

In previous works, MEMDPs have been studied under two different semantics:
[
16
,
19
,
8
]
study the model under the
universal
semantics and
[
4
]
studies the model under the
prior
semantics.
In the
universal
(adversarial) semantics, an adversary picks the environment that the fixed controller strategy has to face; the value of a strategy is its probability to satisfy the objective in the worst environment.
In the
prior
semantics, the environment is drawn initially at random from a fixed distribution (the prior); the value is the corresponding expectation.
In both cases, the chosen environment is not revealed to the controller.
We focus on parity objectives, that are a canonical way of expressing
\omega
-regular properties, and establish structural and algorithmic links between these semantics.

Before presenting our main contributions, we note a
qualitative
equivalence between the two semantics. The value 1 notions coincide in both the
almost-sure
and
limit-sure
senses: there exist strategies that achieve value 1 (resp. arbitrarily close to 1) in the universal semantics if and only if there exist strategies whose prior value is 1 (resp. arbitrarily close to 1). This correspondence lets us transfer value 1 results between the two semantics (see Corollary
2
).

Contributions.
Our main contributions can be summarized as follows:

1.

Approximating the prior value.

We give an algorithm that approximates the
prior
value of an MEMDP to arbitrary precision. This is the most technically demanding part of our contribution. More precisely, we show how to solve algorithmically the
\varepsilon
-gap problem for the prior semantics. This problem asks, given an MEMDP
M
, a prior belief
b
about the operating environment, a parity objective
W
, a threshold
0<\alpha<1
, and a precision
\varepsilon>0
, to answer
YES
if the
prior value
is at least
\alpha
,
NO
if this value is at most
\alpha-\epsilon
, with no requirement otherwise. Our algorithm runs in
PSPACE
when probabilities are given in unary, and in
EXPSPACE
otherwise (see Theorem
6
).

2.

From prior to universal.

We also show that the
universal
value equals the infimum, over all prior beliefs, of the corresponding
prior
values (see Theorem
7
).
Using this result together with the 1-Lipschitz continuity of the prior value with respect to the prior belief, we obtain a new algorithm for the universal
\varepsilon
-gap problem with the same complexity as for the prior value (
PSPACE
for unary probabilities,
EXPSPACE
in general), see Theorem
10
, improving on earlier doubly-exponential-space procedures proposed in
[
8
]
.

3.

MEMDPs with prior semantics as a tractable POMDP subclass.

A key reason for MEMDP tractability is that the hidden environment is fixed, which constrains belief dynamics: the (information theory) entropy of the belief about the operating environment is non-increasing.
We show that in fact any POMDP whose belief entropy is non-increasing can be reduced to a prior-MEMDP (at an exponential cost). Thus, our algorithms apply beyond “pure” MEMDPs and capture a significant subclass of POMDPs, while avoiding classical undecidability phenomena for
\omega
-regular objectives (see Theorem
15
).

Related work.

As recalled above, MEMDPs were introduced under the universal semantics by Raskin and Sankur in
[
16
]
as a formal model for decision making in scenarios where the environment is fixed yet unknown, and is chosen from a finite set of candidate models. Within this framework, several qualitative
\omega
-regular synthesis problems become decidable, in contrast to the corresponding situation for partially observable Markov decision processes (POMDPs). Subsequent work has refined the qualitative complexity landscape. For almost-sure satisfaction of Rabin objectives, Suilen et al. establish a PSPACE-complete decision procedure
[
19
]
. For parity objectives, Chatterjee et al. prove that the value-1 problem is PSPACE-complete in general (and solvable in
PTime
when the number of environments is fixed), demonstrate that pure strategies suffice to achieve value 1, and present a double-exponential-space approximation scheme for computing the value
[
8
]
. Under the prior semantics, MEMDPs have further been proposed as a computationally tractable subclass of POMDPs with discounted payoffs, leveraging more efficient belief-state updates and a non-increasing belief-entropy property
[
4
]
.

For context, classical (fully observable) MDP qualitative problems are solvable in polynomial time
[
2
]
, while analogous POMDP questions are much harder as most of them are undecidable
[
14
,
12
]
, and only bounded horizon questions are known to be decidable
[
14
]
.
So, in this context, MEMDPs occupy a particular place: they retain an interesting part of the partial-information expressiveness but avoid key undecidability barriers that arise in POMDPs. The positive results obtained in this paper add and refine those obtained in
[
16
,
4
,
19
,
8
]
.

Objectives.

Consider a non-empty finite set
Q
. An
objective

W\subseteq Q^{\omega}
is a Borel set:

. We focus on parity objectives, i.e. objectives

such that there is a labeling function

such that

W=\{\rho\in Q^{\omega}\mid\max\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\forall i\in\mathbb{N},\;\exists j\geq i:f(\rho_{j})=n\}\text{ is even}\}
.

Consider an MDP
\mathsf{G}=(Q,A,\delta)
. A strategy
\sigma\colon Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}\to\mathcal{D}(A)
induces a probability measure on the set of finite runs, which can be canonically extended to the associated Borel

|\pi|\leq|\rho|

\mathbb{P}_{\rho}^{\sigma}(\mathsf{G},\pi\cdot(a,t)):=\mathbb{P}_{\rho}^{\sigma}(\mathsf{G},\pi)\cdot\sigma(\pi)(a)\cdot\delta(\mathsf{last}(\pi),a)(t).

Then, for all
\pi\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}

\mathbb{P}_{\rho}^{\sigma}[\mathsf{G},\mathsf{cyl}(\pi)]

by
\mathbb{P}_{\rho}^{\sigma}[\mathsf{G},\mathsf{cyl}(\pi)]:=\mathbb{P}_{\rho}^{\sigma}(\mathsf{G},\pi)\in[0,1]

Values in MEMDPs.

Consider an MEMDP and a parity objective whose probability we seek to maximize. When synthesizing a strategy in an MEMDP, we do not know in which environment it executes. Thus, when defining the value of a synthesized strategy, we may require either that it performs well in
all

Specifically, given an MEMDP
\Gamma

, and a parity objective

\mathsf{uni}

\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{uni}}_{q}(\Gamma,W):=\sup_{\sigma\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)}\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{uni}}_{q}(\Gamma,\sigma,W)\text{ with }\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{uni}}_{q}(\Gamma,\sigma,W):=\min_{e\in E}\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\Gamma[e],W]

\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,W):=\sup_{\sigma\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)}\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,\sigma,W)\text{ with }\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,\sigma,W):=\sum_{e\in E}b(e)\cdot\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\Gamma[e],W]

3
Qualitative and quantitative problem

In this section, we focus on deciding the existence of strategies with good enough prior-values. We first study the qualitative problems (value 1) and then turn to the quantitative problem.

3.1
Qualitative problem

For value (arbitrarily close to) 1, the universal and prior settings coincide.

Proposition 1

(Proof
A.1
)
.

Consider an MEMDP
\Gamma
, a state
q\in Q
, and a parity objective
W
. Let
b\in\mathcal{D}(E)
such that
\mathsf{Supp}(b)=E
. Then
\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{uni}}_{q}(\Gamma,W)=1
if and only if
\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,W)=1
and,
for all
\sigma\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)
:
\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{uni}}_{q}(\Gamma,\sigma,W)=1
if and only if
\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,\sigma,W)=1
.

Proof sketch.

If
\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{uni}}_{q}(\Gamma,W)=1
, then there are strategies whose
\mathsf{uni}
-values are arbitrarily close to 1, thus their
\mathsf{pr}
-values are arbitrarily close to 1. Hence,
\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,W)=1
. Furthermore, if a strategy has a
\mathsf{pr}
-value, w.r.t.
b
, of at least
1-d\cdot\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon>0
, with
d:=\min_{e\in E}b(e)>0
, then its smallest value in any environment (since
\mathsf{Supp}(b)=E
) is at least
1-\varepsilon
. Hence, if
\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,W)=1
then
\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{uni}}_{q}(\Gamma,W)=1
. The other equivalence is straightforward.
∎

The complexity of deciding the existence of almost-surely winning strategies (value 1) and of limit-surely winning strategies (values arbitrarily close to 1) is thus the same in MEMDPs with parity objectives in the universal and prior settings.
We deduce the corollary below.

Corollary 2

(of
[
6
, Theorems 4, 13]
)
.

In an MEMDP, given a prior belief and a parity objective, the problem of deciding the existence of strategies of
\mathsf{pr}
-value (arbitrarily close to) 1 is
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-complete. When the number of environments is fixed, it can be solved in polynomial time.

Example.

Consider again the MEMDP from Example
2
. It is easy to see that no strategy can guarantee winning with probability
1
. Indeed, after any finite number of draws, the empirical frequencies may still be atypical and hence suggest the wrong duplicated card.

Nevertheless, for every
\varepsilon>0
, the player can request sufficiently many draws so that the probability of observing such a misleading sample drops below
\varepsilon
. By the law of large numbers, this implies that although almost-sure winning is impossible, the player can make the winning probability arbitrarily close to
1
by choosing an appropriate family of strategies (indexed by
\varepsilon
).

This reasoning applies both under the universal semantics and under the prior semantics whenever the prior distribution assigns positive probability to both environments. In either case there is no surely winning strategy, but there exists a family of strategies witnessing limit-sure winning; in particular, the construction works for any fixed prior over the two environments.

3.2
Quantitative problem

Let us now turn to the quantitative problem.
We design an algorithm that, for any prescribed precision level
\varepsilon>0
, computes an
\varepsilon
-approximation of the
\mathsf{pr}
-value
\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,W)
. This approximation procedure is subsequently employed to solve the associated
gap problem
, defined as follows: given a parameter
\varepsilon>0
, one must output YES if
\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,W)\geq\alpha
, NO if
\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,W)\leq\alpha-\varepsilon
, and an arbitrary answer otherwise. Since there are inputs for which no specific output is required, the gap problem is not a classical decision problem; it is instead a promise problem
[
10
]
.

Our goal is to compute an approximation of the
\mathsf{pr}
-value given a prior environment belief
b\in\mathcal{D}(E)
. When playing in an MEMDP, the environment belief is updated each time distinguishing state-action pairs are encountered: when we visit a transition
(q,a,q^{\prime})\in Q\times A\times Q
such that
\delta_{e}(q,a)(q^{\prime})>\delta_{e^{\prime}}(q,a)(q^{\prime})
for some
e\neq e^{\prime}\in E
, the belief is updated to account for an increased likelihood of
e
and a decreased likelihood in
e^{\prime}
.
We formally define below this belief update.

Definition 3

(Belief update)
.

Consider an MEMDP
\Gamma=(Q,A,E,(\delta_{e})_{e\in E})
. Let
b\in\mathcal{D}(E)
. For all
(q,a)\in Q\times A
, we let
p[b,q,a]\in\mathcal{D}(Q)
be defined by, for all
q^{\prime}\in Q
:
p[b,q,a](q^{\prime}):=\sum_{e\in E}b(e)\cdot\delta_{e}(q,a)(q^{\prime})
. Then, for all
q^{\prime}\in Q
, we let
\lambda[b,q,a,q^{\prime}]\in\mathcal{D}(E)
be an arbitrary distribution if
p[b,q,a](q^{\prime})=0
, and otherwise, for all
e\in E
:
\lambda[b,q,a,q^{\prime}](e):=\frac{b(e)\cdot\delta_{e}(q,a)(q^{\prime})}{p[b,q,a](q^{\prime})}
.

Given any initial state
q\in Q
and prior belief
b\in\mathcal{D}(E)
, we define a (“likelihood”) function
\mathsf{lk}_{b}:Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}\to\mathcal{D}(E)
which iteratively updates the environment belief: for all
q\in Q
,
\mathsf{lk}_{b}(q):=b\in E
and, for all
\rho\cdot(a,q^{\prime})\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{+}
, we let
\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho\cdot(a,q^{\prime})):=\lambda[\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho),\mathsf{last}(\rho),a,q^{\prime}]
.

Example.

Let us illustrate how belief updates work on our running example. Assume that the prior over the two environments is given by the distribution
b=(\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4})
, so the initial belief is skewed towards environment
E_{2}
(i.e., towards card
2
being duplicated). Now suppose that after the first draw we observe card
2
, meaning that we reach state
2
. This observation should increase our confidence that the operating environment is indeed
E_{2}
. Applying the update rule above, the posterior belief becomes
\lambda[b,D,a,C_{2}](E_{2})=\frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{\frac{2}{3}}{\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{4}+\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{3}{4}}=\frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{24}{21}=\frac{6}{7}
, and
\lambda[b,D,a,C_{2}](E_{1})=\frac{1}{7}
. Conversely, if the first draw yields card
1
, then the posterior belief becomes
\lambda[b,D,a,C_{1}](E_{2})=\frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{\frac{1}{3}}{\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{3}{4}}=\frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{12}{15}=\frac{3}{5}
, and
\lambda[b,D,a,C_{1}](E_{1})=\frac{2}{5}
.

Our algorithm computing an approximation of the
\mathsf{pr}
-value works recursively on the number of environments in the support of the belief. When there is a single environment in the support, we are actually in an MDP, and we can compute in polynomial time the values of all states
[
2
]
. Assume now that the support of the prior belief is of size at least 2. If the belief in an environment
e
ever drops to 0, because we have visited a transition
(q,a,q^{\prime})
such that
\delta_{e}(q,a)(q^{\prime})=0
, we can call the algorithm recursively with a belief of smaller support.
Alternatively, the belief in an environment may be close to 0. In that case, it is almost harmless (in terms of value change) to truncate the belief into one with a smaller support, by 1-Lipschitz continuity of the
\mathsf{pr}
-value.

Lemma 4

(Proof
A.2
)
.

Consider an MEMDP
\Gamma
and a parity objective
W
. For all beliefs
b,b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}(E)
, we have
|\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,W)-\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b^{\prime},W)|\leq\mathsf{Diff}(b,b^{\prime})
, with
\mathsf{Diff}(b,b^{\prime}):=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\sum_{e\in E}|b(e)-b^{\prime}(e)|
.
Furthermore, for all beliefs
b\in\mathcal{D}(E)
such that
|\mathsf{Supp}(b)|\geq 2
, there is
\mathsf{Truncate}(b)\in\mathcal{D}(E)
such that
\mathsf{Supp}(\mathsf{Truncate}(b))\subsetneq\mathsf{Supp}(b)
and
\mathsf{Diff}(b,\mathsf{Truncate}(b))\leq\min_{e\in\mathsf{Supp}(b)}b(e)
.

The environment belief is updated each time a distinguishing state-action pairs is visited. However, even if many distinguishing state-action pairs are visited, it could be that there is no environment whose belief drops to 0. Nonetheless, if enough distinguishing state-action pairs are visited, with high probability, there is an environment whose belief drops close to 0.

Theorem 5

(Proof
A.3
)
.

Consider an MEMDP
\Gamma=(Q,A,E,(\delta_{e})_{e\in E})
and a prior environment belief
b\in\mathcal{D}(E)
. Let
k:=|E|
, and
l
denote the maximum number of bits to write any probability occurring in
\Gamma
. For all
\varepsilon>0
, we let:
\mathsf{NeverSmallBelief}(b,\varepsilon):=\{\rho\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}\mid\forall\pi\sqsubseteq\rho,\;\forall e\in E:\;\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\pi)(e)>\varepsilon\}
, with
\pi\sqsubseteq\rho
meaning that
\pi
is a prefix of
\rho
. For all
\rho\in(Q\cdot A)^{\omega}
, we let
\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{dstg}}(\rho)\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
denote the number of times

Proof sketch.

Assume that some

is the operating environment. Consider another environment

and assume, for simplicity, that

for all

\sigma

-distinguishing state-action pairs are such that

is arbitrarily small. We focus on the ratio of the beliefs in the environments

, which initially is equal to
\frac{b(e^{\prime})}{b(e)}

\frac{\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho)(e^{\prime})}{\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho)(e)}=\frac{b(e^{\prime})}{b(e)}\cdot\prod_{\pi\cdot(a,q)\sqsubseteq\rho}\frac{\delta_{e^{\prime}}(\mathsf{last}(\pi),a)(q)}{\delta_{e}(\mathsf{last}(\pi),a)(q)}

\ast

\sigma

(e,e^{\prime})
-distinguishing state-action pairs, the sum

such that, for all
(q,a)\in\mathsf{Dstg}(\Gamma,e,e^{\prime})

\sum_{q^{\prime}\in Q}\delta_{e}(q,a)(q^{\prime})\cdot\log(\frac{\delta_{e^{\prime}}(q,a)(q^{\prime})}{\delta_{e}(q,a)(q^{\prime})})\leq\eta

In order to show (

[

]
(which was also a central tool used in

]
). Informally, this inequality entails that if we consider

. In our case, we could consider the random variables

Overall, if we appropriately choose

(e,e^{\prime})
-distinguishing state-action pairs while having the belief in the environment

Relating the
\mathsf{pr}
- and
\mathsf{uni}
-values

The procedure described in
[
6

that solves the gap problem with
\mathsf{uni}
-values executes in space doubly exponential in
|Q|
(the number of states). On the other hand, we have exhibited an algorithm solving the gap problem with
\mathsf{pr}
-values in polynomial space when the probabilities are given in unary, in exponential space otherwise. Our goal now is to link the
\mathsf{pr}
- and
\mathsf{uni}
-values so that we can use our algorithm to solve more efficiently the gap problem with
\mathsf{uni}
-values.

It is clear that the
\mathsf{uni}
-value is lower than or equal to the
\mathsf{pr}
-value for any prior belief. In fact, the
\mathsf{uni}
-value is actually equal to the infimum
\mathsf{pr}
-value over all possible prior beliefs.

Theorem 7
.

Consider an MEMDP
\Gamma=(Q,A,E,(\delta_{e})_{e\in E})
, a state
q\in Q
, and a parity objective
W\subseteq\mathsf{Borel}(Q)
. We have:
\mathsf{val}_{q}^{\mathsf{uni}}(\Gamma,W)=\inf_{b\in\mathcal{D}(E)}\mathsf{val}_{q}^{\mathsf{pr}}(\Gamma,b,W)
.

Example.

We reconsider our running example and modify the two environments
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
as follows. In both environments, we set
\alpha_{0}=1
, so the player is required to guess the duplicated card immediately after a single draw. If all other aspects of the environments remain unchanged, the
\mathsf{uni}
-value is
\frac{2}{3}
; it is attained by the strategy that guesses the environment corresponding to the single observed card. Under a uniform prior over the two environments, the
\mathsf{pr}
-value coincides with this
\frac{2}{3}
value.
We now introduce asymmetric environments. In
E_{1}
, card 1 is duplicated twice and card 2 is duplicated once (hence
\alpha_{1}=\frac{3}{5}
), while
in
E_{2}
, card 2 is duplicated twice (hence
\alpha_{1}=\frac{1}{4}
). In this setting, the
\mathsf{uni}
-value remains equal to
\frac{2}{3}
; however, the infimum of the
\mathsf{pr}
-values is now achieved under a prior

that is slightly biased towards the less favorable environment, namely
b(E_{1}):=\frac{5}{9}
, rather than under the uniform prior over environments.

To establish Theorem
7
, we introduce the notion of mixed strategies. Given a set of states
Q
and set of actions

(Q,A)
is a probability distribution
\tau\in\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A))
over the strategies (even though the set
\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)
is uncountable, the probability distributions that we consider have a countable support). We naturally define, in an MDP
\mathsf{G}=(Q,A,\delta)

\mathbb{P}_{\rho}^{\tau}[\mathsf{G},\cdot]:\mathsf{Borel}(Q)\to[0,1]
induced by a mixed strategy
\tau\in\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A))
:
\mathbb{P}_{\rho}^{\tau}[\mathsf{G},\cdot]:=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)}\tau(\sigma)\cdot\mathbb{P}_{\rho}^{\sigma}[\mathsf{G},\cdot]
. For all Borel objectives
W\in\mathsf{Borel}(Q)
, we naturally extend to mixed strategies

the
\mathsf{uni}

\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{uni}}_{q}(\Gamma,\tau,W)\in[0,1]
and
\mathsf{pr}

. Then, we can relate the supremum
\mathsf{uni}
-values that mixed strategies can achieve with the infimum

-values over all prior beliefs.

Lemma 8

(Proof
B.1
)
.

Consider an MEMDP
\Gamma

, and a parity objective
W

\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A))}\inf_{b\in\mathcal{D}(E)}\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,\tau,W)=\inf_{b\in\mathcal{D}(E)}\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A))}

\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,\tau,W)

\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A))}\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{uni}}_{q}(\Gamma,\tau,W)=\inf_{b\in\mathcal{D}(E)}\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,W)

Proof sktech.

The first equality is a direct consequence of a standard generalization of von Neuman’s minimax theorem
[
20

, which holds because the set of environments is finite
[
18
]
. Furthermore, for all non-empty sets

\sup_{d\in\mathcal{D}(X)}\sum_{x\in X}d(x)\cdot f(x)=\sup_{x\in X}f(x)

\inf_{b\in\mathcal{D}(E)}\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,\tau,W)=\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{uni}}_{q}(\Gamma,\tau,W)

\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A))}

.

Lemma 9

(Proof

Given any

5
Characterization of MEMDPs with entropy

When playing in an MEMDP, we have only partial information about where we are: we know the current state, but we do not know the operating environment. In fact, MEMDPs are a special kind of Partially Observable MDPs (POMDP for short), i.e. MDPs in which we play on an underlying set of states to which we have only indirect access via an observation that may be identical for different states. Formally, a POMDP is a tuple
\Lambda=(Q,A,\delta,\Omega,O)
where
(Q,A,\delta)
is an MDP,
\Omega
is a non-empty set of observations, and
O\colon Q\to\Omega
. An MEMDP
\Gamma
naturally induces the POMDP
\Lambda(\Gamma)=(Q^{\prime},A,\delta,\Omega,O)
such that
Q^{\prime}:=Q\times E
; for all
(q,e,q^{\prime},a)\in Q\times E\times Q\times A
,
\delta((q,e),a)((q^{\prime},e)):=\delta_{e}(q,a)(q^{\prime})
;
\Omega:=Q
; and for all
(q,e)\in Q\times E
,
O(q,e):=q
. Strategies in POMDPs are functions in
\mathsf{Strat}(\Omega,A)
. Given any strategy
\sigma\in\mathsf{Strat}(\Omega,A)
, for any state
q\in Q
, we naturally define the probability measure
\mathbb{P}^{\sigma}_{q}[\Lambda,\cdot]\colon\mathsf{Borel}(Q)\to[0,1]
. Given
b\in\mathcal{D}(Q)
and
W\in\mathsf{Borel}(Q)
, we let
\mathsf{val}(\Lambda,b,W):=\sup_{\sigma\in\mathsf{Strat}(O,A)}\sum_{q\in Q}b(q)\cdot\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\Lambda,W]
.

The goal of this section is to characterize MEMDPs among POMDPs. To do so, we study the belief in PODMPs. When playing in a POMDP, we start with an initial belief about the current state
2
2

2

In POMDPs, we always have an initial belief about the current state. That is why, although MEMDPs in the “prior” semantics are special kinds POMDPs, it is not the case of MEMDPs in the “universal” semantics.

\lambda[b,a,o](q):=\frac{\sum_{q^{\prime}\in Q}b(q^{\prime})\cdot\delta(q^{\prime},a)(q)}{p(b,a,o)}
otherwise.

When playing in an MEMDP, if we ever know for sure the current environment (i.e. the environment belief is Dirac), then this will never change. POMDPs induced by MEMDPs are said to be
Dirac-preserving
, i.e. for all
(q,a)\in Q\times A
(with
q
seen as a Dirac belief), we have:
\forall o\in\mathsf{Comp}(q,a):\;|\mathsf{Supp}(\lambda[q,a,o])|=1
.

As pointed out in
[
3
]
, there is an alternative way to capture the behavior of POMDPs induced by MEMDPs via the (information theory) notion of entropy
[
17
]
of a belief. Formally, the entropy
H(b)
of a belief
b\in\mathcal{D}(Q)
is defined by
H(b):=-\sum_{q\in Q}b(q)\log(b(q))\geq 0
. The entropy of a belief is a measure of the amount of information carried by that belief: the higher the entropy, the less carried information. A belief is Dirac if and only if its entropy is null; the maximal value of the entropy is
\log(|Q|)
, it is achieved by uniform probability distributions.

In MEMDPs, the amount of information we have about the current environment never shrinks: the more distinguishing state-action pairs we visit, the better we know in which environment we are playing. As established in
[
3
]
, this corresponds to the fact that POMDPs induced by MEMDPs have
non-increasing entropy
i.e. they are such that, for all
b\in\mathcal{D}(Q)
and
a\in A
:
H(b)\geq\sum_{o\in\mathsf{Comp}(b,a)}p(b,a,o)\cdot H(\lambda[q,a,o])
.

Clearly, POMDPs with non-increasing entropy are Dirac-preserving. In fact, the converse is also true: all Dirac-preserving POMDPs have non-increasing entropy. (This is harder to show.)

Proposition 14

(Proof
C.2
)
.

A POMDP has non-increasing entropy iff it is Dirac-preserving.

The benefit of the above proposition is twofold. First, the notion of POMDPs with non-increasing entropy is natural, as this corresponds to POMDPs where the knowledge about the current state is monotonous. However, it is not clear from the definition of POMDPs with non-increasing entropy how to effectively decide if a POMDP satisfies this property. It is now apparent that this can be done in polynomial time, since checking that a POMDP is Dirac-preserving can be done by enumerating all triplets of states, actions and observations.
Second, given any Dirac-preserving POMDP with an observation-compatible parity objective
3
3

3

Observation-compatible parity objectives, or visible objectives, are a common assumption in POMDPs. They define objectives that can be observed by the player: after playing and observing the sequence of observations traversed during the play, the player can determine whether it has won or not; see for instance
[
5
]
.
, that is, a parity objective induced by a labelling function
f:Q\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that
f(q_{1})=f(q_{2})
whenever
O(q_{1})=O(q_{2})
, and given an initial belief, we can construct an exponentially larger MEMDP, together with a parity objective and an prior environment belief, such that the value in the POMDP coincides with the
\mathsf{pr}
-value in the MEMDP. This is formally stated below.

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{s^{2}(b-a)^{2}}{8}\right)\cdot\left[\exp\left(n\frac{s^{2}(b-a)^{2}}{8}\right)+n\exp(s(-M-v(\rho\cdot q^{\prime})+n\cdot c+n\frac{s^{2}(b-a)^{2}}{8}))\right]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left((n+1)\frac{s^{2}(b-a)^{2}}{8}\right)+n\exp\left(s(-M+(n+1)\cdot c)+(n+1)\frac{s^{2}(b-a)^{2}}{8}\right)

This holds for all
q^{\prime}\in Q
, therefore, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathsf{last}(\rho)}^{\sigma_{\rho}}[\mathsf{G},\exp(s(f_{v}^{n+1,M}-(n+1)\cdot\eta))]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left((n+1)\frac{s^{2}(b-a)^{2}}{8}\right)

\displaystyle+n\exp\left(s(-M+(n+1)\cdot c)+(n+1)\frac{s^{2}(b-a)^{2}}{8}\right)

This holds for all
\rho\in\mathsf{NZ}_{v}^{+}
, thus:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{q}^{\sigma}[\mathsf{G},\exp(s(f_{v}^{n+1,M}-(n+1)\cdot\eta))]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left((n+1)\frac{s^{2}(b-a)^{2}}{8}\right)

\displaystyle+n\exp\left(s(-M+(n+1)\cdot c)+(n+1)\frac{s^{2}(b-a)^{2}}{8}\right)

\displaystyle+\exp\left(s(-M+(n+1)\cdot c)+(n+1)\frac{s^{2}(b-a)^{2}}{8}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left((n+1)\frac{s^{2}(b-a)^{2}}{8}\right)

\displaystyle+(n+1)\exp\left(s(-M+(n+1)\cdot c)+(n+1)\frac{s^{2}(b-a)^{2}}{8}\right)

This concludes the induction.
∎

We can proceed to the proof of Lemma
19
.

Proof.

Let
t>0
. For all
M>-n\cdot\eta-t
, we have
\{\mathsf{s}_{v}^{n}\geq n\cdot\eta+t\}\cap\{\mathsf{c}_{v}^{n}=n\}=\{f_{v}^{n,M}\geq n\cdot\eta+t\}
. Furthermore, for all
s>0
, by Markov’s inequality, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\mathsf{G},\{f_{v}^{n,M}\geq n\cdot\eta+t\}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\mathsf{G},\{s(f_{v}^{n,M}-n\cdot\eta)\geq s\cdot t\}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\mathsf{G},\{\exp(s(f_{v}^{n,M}-n\cdot\eta))\geq\exp(s\cdot t)\}]

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-s\cdot t)\cdot\mathbb{E}_{q}^{\sigma}[\mathsf{G},\exp(s(f_{v}^{n,M}-n\cdot\eta))]

Since
n\cdot\exp(-s(M+n\cdot\max(|a|,|b|))+n\frac{s^{2}(b-a)^{2}}{8})\to_{M\to\infty}0
, it follows by Lemma
21
, that:

\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\mathsf{G},\{\mathsf{s}_{v}^{n}\geq n\cdot\eta+t\}\cap\{\mathsf{c}_{v}^{n}\geq n\}]\leq\exp(-s\cdot t)\cdot\exp\left(n\frac{s^{2}(b-a)^{2}}{8}\right)=\exp\left(-st+n\frac{s^{2}(b-a)^{2}}{8}\right)

This holds for all
s>0
, in particular for
s=\frac{4t}{n(b-a)^{2}}
, for which we obtain:

\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\mathsf{G},\{\mathsf{s}_{v}^{n}\geq n\cdot\eta+t\}\cap\{\mathsf{c}_{v}^{n}\geq n\}]\leq\exp\left(-\frac{4t^{2}}{n(b-a)^{2}}+\frac{16t^{2}}{8n(b-a)^{2}}\right)=\exp\left(-\frac{2t^{2}}{n(b-a)^{2}}\right)

∎

A.5
Valuation from beliefs update

As a preliminary result, let us show that when playing in an environment, the belief in that environment cannot drop to 0.

Lemma 22
.

Let
\Gamma=(Q,A,E,(\delta_{e})_{e\in E})
be an MEMDP. Consider a state
q\in Q
and a strategy
\sigma\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)
. For all environments
e\in E
and beliefs
b\in\mathcal{D}(E)
such that
b(e)>0
, for all
\rho\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
such that
\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}(\Gamma[e],\rho)>0
, we have:
\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho)(e)>0
.

Proof.

Let us prove the result by induction on
\rho\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
. This clearly holds if
\rho\in Q
. Now, assume that this holds for some
\rho\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
, and let
(a,t)\in A_{\mathsf{last}(\rho)}\times Q
. If
\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}(\Gamma[e],\rho\cdot(a,t))>0
, then
\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}(\Gamma[e],\rho)\cdot\sigma(\rho)(a)\cdot\delta_{e}(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)(t)>0
, and thus
\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho)(e)>0
. Hence:
\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho\cdot(a,t))(e)=\frac{1}{p[\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho),\mathsf{last}(\rho),a](t)}\cdot\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho)(e)\cdot\delta_{e}(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)(t)>0
.
∎

Our goal is now to apply Lemma
19
to establish Theorem
5
. To do so, we describe bounded valuations in MDPs that can be obtained from belief update in MEMDPs. Before we define this valuation, let us first introduce the notion of revealing state-action pair.

Definition 23
.

Consider an MEMDP
\Gamma=(Q,A,E,(\delta_{e})_{e\in E})
. Let
e\neq e^{\prime}\in E
. We say that a state-action pair
(q,a)\in Q\times A
is
e^{\prime}/e
-revealing if there is some state
q^{\prime}\in Q
such that
\delta_{e^{\prime}}(q,a)(q^{\prime})=0
and
\delta_{e}(q,a)(q^{\prime})>0
.

For all
\rho\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
, we let
\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{rev}}(e^{\prime},e,\rho)\in\mathbb{N}
denote the number of occurrences of
e^{\prime}/e
-revealing state-action pairs in
\rho
.

We can now define the history valuations that we consider.

Definition 24
.

Consider an MEMDP
\Gamma=(Q,A,E,(\delta_{e})_{e\in E})
. Let
q\in Q
and
e\neq e^{\prime}\in E
. We define the history valuation
v[e^{\prime},e]:Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
as follows:
v[e^{\prime},e](q):=0
and for all
\rho=q_{0}\cdot(a_{1},q_{1})\cdots(a_{n},q_{n})\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
:

\displaystyle v[e^{\prime},e](\rho):=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }a_{n}\text{ is }e^{\prime}/e\text{-revealing}\\
0&\text{ if }\delta_{e}(q_{n-1},a_{n})(q_{n})=0\\
\log(\frac{\delta_{e^{\prime}}(q_{n-1},a_{n})(q_{n})}{\delta_{e}(q_{n-1},a_{n})(q_{n})})&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

By definition, the exponential of the sum of the
v[e^{\prime},e]
-values visited on a path
\rho
occurring with positive probability in the MDP
\Gamma[e]
(given any strategy) is closely related to the ratio of the beliefs of the environments
e^{\prime}
and
e
at
\rho
. This is formally stated below.

Lemma 25
.

Let
\Gamma=(Q,A,E,(\delta_{e})_{e\in E})
be an MEMDP. Consider two environments
e\neq e^{\prime}\in E
, and a prior belief
b\in\mathcal{D}(E)
with
b(e)>0
. Consider any strategy
\sigma\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)
and some
\rho\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
such that
\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\Gamma[e],\rho]>0
. Then:

\frac{b(e^{\prime})}{b(e)}\cdot 2^{\left(\sum_{\pi\sqsubseteq\rho}v[e^{\prime},e](\pi)\right)}\cdot\mathsf{ratio}_{\max}(\Gamma)^{\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{rev}}(e^{\prime},e,\rho)}\geq\frac{\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho)(e^{\prime})}{\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho)(e)}

where we write
\pi\sqsubseteq\rho
for runs
\pi\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
that are prefixes of
\rho
.

Proof.

First of all, for all
\rho\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
such that
\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\Gamma[e],\rho]>0
, we have
\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho)(e)>0
, by Lemma
22
.

Then, we show the result by induction on
\rho\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
such that
\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\Gamma[e],\rho]>0
. This straightforwardly holds for
\rho=q
, since
v[e^{\prime},e](q)=0
,
\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{rev}}(e^{\prime},e,q)=0
, and
\mathsf{lk}_{b}(q)(f)=b(f)
for all
f\in\{e,e^{\prime}\}
.

Assume now that this property holds for some
\rho\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
, and let
(a,q)\in A\times Q
such that
\rho\cdot(a,q)\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
and
\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\Gamma[e],\rho\cdot(a,q)]>0
, thus
\delta_{e}(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)(q)>0
. We have:

\frac{\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho\cdot(a,q))(e^{\prime})}{\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho\cdot(a,q))(e)}=\frac{\frac{1}{\lambda[\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho),\mathsf{last}(\rho),a,q]}\cdot\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho)(e^{\prime})\cdot\delta_{e^{\prime}}(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)(q)}{\frac{1}{\lambda[\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho),\mathsf{last}(\rho),a,q]}\cdot\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho)(e)\cdot\delta_{e}(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)(q)}=\frac{\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho)(e^{\prime})}{\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho)(e)}\cdot\frac{\delta_{e^{\prime}}(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)(q)}{\delta_{e}(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)(q)}

Then, there are two cases:

•

If
(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)
is
e^{\prime}/e
-revealing, then
\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{rev}}(e^{\prime},e,\rho\cdot(a,q))=\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{rev}}(e^{\prime},e,\rho)+1
and
v[e^{\prime},e](\rho\cdot(a,q))=0
. Furthermore,
\frac{\delta_{e^{\prime}}(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)(q)}{\delta_{e}(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)(q)}\leq\mathsf{ratio}_{\max}(\Gamma)
. Thus:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho\cdot(a,q))(e^{\prime})}{\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho\cdot(a,q))(e)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho)(e^{\prime})}{\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho)(e)}\cdot\mathsf{ratio}_{\max}(\Gamma)\leq\frac{b(e^{\prime})}{b(e)}\cdot 2^{\left(\sum_{\pi\sqsubseteq\rho}v[e^{\prime},e](\pi)\right)}\cdot\mathsf{ratio}_{\max}(\Gamma)^{\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{rev}}(e^{\prime},e,\rho)+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{b(e^{\prime})}{b(e)}\cdot 2^{\left(\sum_{\pi\sqsubseteq\rho\cdot(a,q)}v[e^{\prime},e](\pi)\right)}\cdot\mathsf{ratio}_{\max}(\Gamma)^{\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{rev}}(e^{\prime},e,\rho\cdot(a,q))}

•

If
a
is not
e^{\prime}/e
-revealing, then
\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{rev}}(e^{\prime},e,\rho\cdot(a,q))=\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{rev}}(e^{\prime},e,\rho)
and
v[e^{\prime},e](\rho\cdot(a,q))=\log(\frac{\delta_{e^{\prime}}(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)(q)}{\delta_{e}(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)(q)})
, thus
2^{v[e^{\prime},e](\rho\cdot(a,q))}=\frac{\delta_{e^{\prime}}(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)(q)}{\delta_{e}(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)(q)}
. Therefore:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho\cdot(a,q))(e^{\prime})}{\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho\cdot(a,q))(e)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho)(e^{\prime})}{\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho)(e)}\cdot 2^{v[e^{\prime},e](\rho\cdot(a,q))}\leq\frac{b(e^{\prime})}{b(e)}\cdot 2^{\left(\sum_{\pi\sqsubseteq\rho\cdot(a,q)}v[e^{\prime},e](\pi)\right)}\cdot\mathsf{ratio}_{\max}(\Gamma)^{\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{rev}}(e^{\prime},e,\rho\cdot(a,q))}

This concludes the induction.
∎

In addition, this valuation
v[e^{\prime},e]
is
\eta(\Gamma)
-bounded.

Lemma 26
.

Consider an MEMDP
\Gamma=(Q,A,E,(\delta_{e})_{e\in E})
, and
e\neq e^{\prime}\in E
. In the MDP
\Gamma[e]
,
v[e^{\prime},e]:Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
[\log(\mathsf{ratio}_{\min}(\Gamma)),\log(\mathsf{ratio}_{\max}(\Gamma))]
-valuation that is
\eta(\Gamma)
-bounded.

The proof of this lemma relies on the lower bound, recalled below, on the difference between the arithmetic and geometric mean.

Lemma 27

(Theorem 1 in
[
1
]
)
.

Let
1\leq k\in\mathbb{N}
. For all
1\leq i\leq k
, consider some
0\leq x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, and assume that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}=1
. Then, we have:

\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\cdot x_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\left(\sqrt{x_{i}}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{k}\sqrt{x_{k}}\right)^{2}

Let us now proceed to the proof of Lemma
26
.

Proof.

For all
(q,a,q^{\prime})\in Q\times A\times Q
, if
\delta_{e}(q,a)(q^{\prime})>0
, we have:

\mathsf{ratio}_{\min}(\Gamma)\leq\frac{\delta_{e^{\prime}}(q,a)(q^{\prime})}{\delta_{e}(q,a)(q^{\prime})}\leq\mathsf{ratio}_{\max}(\Gamma)

It follows that
v[e^{\prime},e]
is a
[\log(\mathsf{ratio}_{\min}(\Gamma)),\log(\mathsf{ratio}_{\max}(\Gamma))]
-valuation.

Consider now some
\rho\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
and
a\in A
such that
(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)\in\mathsf{NZ}_{v[e^{\prime},e]}
. By definition of
v[e^{\prime},e]
, the pair
(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)
is not
e^{\prime}/e
-revealing and we have
\delta_{e}(q,a)\neq\delta_{e^{\prime}}(q,a)
. Let
S:=\mathsf{Supp}(\delta_{e}(q,a))\subseteq Q
. For all
s\in S
, we let
p_{s}:=\delta_{e}(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)(s)>0
and
r_{s}:=\delta_{e^{\prime}}(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)(s)>0
(since
a
is not
e^{\prime}/e
-revealing). By definition, we have
\sum_{q^{\prime}\in Q}\delta_{e}(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)(q^{\prime})\cdot v(\rho\cdot(a,q^{\prime}))=\sum_{s\in S}p_{s}\cdot v(\rho\cdot(a,s))
. Furthermore:

\sum_{s\in S}p_{s}\cdot v(\rho\cdot(a,s))=\sum_{s\in S}p_{s}\cdot\log\left(\frac{r_{s}}{p_{s}}\right)=\log\left(\prod_{s\in S}\left(\frac{r_{s}}{p_{s}}\right)^{p_{s}}\right)

Furthermore, by Lemma
27
, we have:

\prod_{s\in S}\left(\frac{r_{s}}{p_{s}}\right)^{p_{s}}\leq\sum_{s\in S}p_{s}\cdot\frac{r_{s}}{p_{s}}-\sum_{s\in S}p_{s}\left(\sqrt{\frac{r_{s}}{p_{s}}}-\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}p_{s^{\prime}}\sqrt{\frac{r_{s^{\prime}}}{p_{s^{\prime}}}}\right)^{2}

If
\sum_{s\in S}r_{s}<1
, then
\sum_{s\in S}r_{s}\leq 1-p_{\min}(\Gamma)\leq 1-p_{\min}(\Gamma)\cdot\iota(\Gamma)
, thus
\prod_{s\in S}\left(\frac{r_{s}}{p_{s}}\right)^{p_{s}}\leq 1-p_{\min}(\Gamma)\cdot\iota(\Gamma)
.

Assume now that
\sum_{s\in S}r_{s}=1
. Then, since
\delta_{e}(q,a)\neq\delta_{e^{\prime}}(q,a)
, it follows that there some
t\in S
such that
\delta_{e^{\prime}}(q,a)(t)=r_{t}>p_{t}=\delta_{e}(q,a)(t)
, and thus
\frac{r_{t}}{p_{t}}\geq\mathsf{ratio}_{\min}^{>1}(\Gamma)>1
, by definition of
\mathsf{ratio}_{\min}^{>1}(\Gamma)
. Furthermore, by concavity of the square root function, and by Jensen’s inequality, we have:

\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}p_{s^{\prime}}\sqrt{\frac{r_{s^{\prime}}}{p_{s^{\prime}}}}\leq\sqrt{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}p_{s^{\prime}}\frac{r_{s^{\prime}}}{p_{s^{\prime}}}}=\sqrt{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}r_{s^{\prime}}}=1

Therefore:

\displaystyle\sum_{s\in S}p_{s}\left(\sqrt{\frac{r_{s}}{p_{s}}}-\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}p_{s^{\prime}}\sqrt{\frac{r_{s^{\prime}}}{p_{s^{\prime}}}}\right)^{2}\geq p_{t}\left(\sqrt{\frac{r_{t}}{p_{t}}}-\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}p_{s^{\prime}}\sqrt{\frac{r_{s^{\prime}}}{p_{s^{\prime}}}}\right)^{2}\geq p_{\min}(\Gamma)\left(\sqrt{\mathsf{ratio}_{\min}^{>1}(\Gamma)}-1\right)^{2}

Hence:

\prod_{s\in S}\left(\frac{r_{s}}{p_{s}}\right)^{p_{s}}\leq 1-p_{\min}(\Gamma)\left(\sqrt{\mathsf{ratio}_{\min}^{>1}(\Gamma)}-1\right)^{2}\leq 1-p_{\min}(\Gamma)\cdot\iota(\Gamma)

Overall, we obtain:

\sum_{q^{\prime}\in Q}\delta_{e}(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)(q^{\prime})\cdot v(\rho\cdot(a,q^{\prime}))=\sum_{s\in S}p_{s}\cdot v(\rho\cdot(a,s))=\log\left(\prod_{s\in S}\left(\frac{r_{s}}{p_{s}}\right)^{p_{s}}\right)\leq\log(1-p_{\min}(\Gamma)\cdot\iota(\Gamma))=\eta(\Gamma)

Thus,
v[e^{\prime},e]
is
\eta(\Gamma)
-bounded.
∎

Thanks to the above lemma, we will be able to apply the results of Lemma
19
to the valuation
v[e^{\prime},e]
; and in so doing, we will show that if enough distinguishing, and not
e^{\prime}/e
-revealing, actions are visited, then with high probability the ratio of the beliefs in two environments is small enough (this is handled next page). There remains to handle
e^{\prime}/e
-revealing actions. This is done in the lemma below.

Lemma 28
.

Consider an MEMDP
\Gamma=(Q,A,E,(\delta_{e})_{e\in E})
, and
e\neq e^{\prime}\in E
. For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and prior beliefs
b\in\mathcal{D}(E)
, we let:

\displaystyle\mathsf{Reveal}^{k}(\Gamma,b,e^{\prime},e):=

\displaystyle\{\rho\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}\mid\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{rev}}(e^{\prime},e,\rho)\geq k\text{ and }\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho)(e^{\prime})\neq 0\}

Then, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, for all
q\in Q
, for all strategies
\sigma\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)
, we have:

\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}(\Gamma[e],\mathsf{Reveal}^{n}(\Gamma,b,e^{\prime},e))\leq(1-p_{\min}(\Gamma))^{n}

Proof.

We show the result by induction on
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This straightforwardly holds for
n=0
. Assume now that this property holds for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
q\in Q
. We let:

\hskip-22.76228pt\mathsf{FirstRev}:=\{\pi=(q_{0},a_{0})\cdots(q_{n},a_{n})\in(Q\cdot A)^{*}\mid(q_{n},a_{n})\text{ is revealing and }\forall i\leq n-1,\;(q_{i},a_{i})\text{ is not revealing}\}

For all
\rho\in\mathsf{FirstRev}
, we let
\sigma_{\rho}\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)
be defined by, for all
\pi\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
,
\sigma_{\rho}(\pi):=\sigma(\rho\cdot\pi)
(similarly to the proof of Lemma
21
). For all
\rho\in\mathsf{FirstRev}
, we consider some
q_{\rho}\in Q
such that
\delta_{e^{\prime}}(\rho)(q_{\rho})=0
and
\delta_{e}(\rho)(q_{\rho})>0
. Note that
\sum_{q^{\prime}\in Q\setminus\{q_{\rho}\}}\delta_{e}(\rho)(q)=1-\delta_{e}(\rho)(q_{\rho})\leq 1-p_{\min}(\Gamma)
. By definition of
\mathsf{lk}_{b}
, we have that for all prefixes
\pi
of some run
\rho\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
, if
\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\pi)(e^{\prime})=0
, then
\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho)(e^{\prime})=0
. Therefore, letting
x:=\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}(\Gamma[e],\mathsf{Reveal}^{n+1}(\Gamma,b,e^{\prime},e))
, we have:

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho\in\mathsf{FirstRev}}\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}(\Gamma[e],\rho)\cdot\sum_{q^{\prime}\in Q}\delta_{e}(\rho)(q^{\prime})\cdot\mathbb{P}_{q^{\prime}}^{\sigma_{\rho}}(\Gamma[e],\mathsf{Reveal}^{n}(\Gamma,\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho\cdot q^{\prime})),e^{\prime},e)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho\in\mathsf{FirstRev}}\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}(\Gamma[e],\rho)\cdot\sum_{q^{\prime}\in Q\setminus\{q_{\rho}\}}\delta_{e}(\rho)(q^{\prime})\cdot\mathbb{P}_{q^{\prime}}^{\sigma_{\rho}}(\Gamma[e],\mathsf{Reveal}^{n}(\Gamma,\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho\cdot q^{\prime})),e^{\prime},e)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\rho\in\mathsf{FirstRev}}\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}(\Gamma[e],\rho)\cdot\sum_{q^{\prime}\in Q\setminus\{q_{\rho}\}}\delta_{e}(\rho)(q^{\prime})\cdot(1-p_{\min}(\Gamma))^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\rho\in\mathsf{FirstRev}}\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}(\Gamma[e],\rho)\cdot(1-p_{\min}(\Gamma))^{n+1}\leq(1-p_{\min}(\Gamma))^{n+1}

This concludes the induction.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5
.

We now have all the ingredients to establish Theorem
5
.

Proof.

Consider and environment
e\in E
, a prior belief
b\in\mathcal{D}(E)
, and a strategy
\sigma\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)
. If there is some environment
e\in E
such that
b(e)\leq\varepsilon
, then
\mathsf{NeverSmallBelief}(b,\varepsilon)=\emptyset
. Hence, let us assume that this is not the case. Consider some
e^{\prime}\neq e\in E
.

We start by realizing that
\{\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{rev}}(e^{\prime},e)\geq n_{1}(\Gamma,\varepsilon)\}\cap\mathsf{NeverSmallBelief}(b,\varepsilon)\subseteq\mathsf{Reveal}^{n_{1}(\Gamma,\varepsilon)}_{q}(\Gamma,b,e^{\prime},e)
, thus, by Lemma
28
, we have:

\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}(\Gamma[e],\{\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{rev}}(e^{\prime},e)\geq n_{1}(\Gamma,\varepsilon)\}\cap\mathsf{NeverSmallBelief}(b,\varepsilon))\leq(1-p_{\min}(\Gamma))^{n_{1}(\Gamma,\varepsilon)}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3|E|}

Note now that there two cases. Assume first that
\eta(\Gamma)=0
. This is equivalent to
\iota(\Gamma)=0
and
n_{3}(\Gamma,\varepsilon)=0
. This also implies that there are no distinguishing state-action pairs that are not revealing. Therefore:

\left\{\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{dstg}}(e^{\prime},e)\geq\frac{m(\Gamma,\varepsilon)}{|E|}\right\}\subseteq\{\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{rev}}(e^{\prime},e)\geq n_{1}(\Gamma,\varepsilon)\}

Hence, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\left(\Gamma[e],\left\{\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{dstg}}(e^{\prime},e)\geq\frac{m(\Gamma,\varepsilon)}{|E|}\right\}\cap\mathsf{NeverSmallBelief}(b,\varepsilon)\right)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{|E|}

Assume now that
\eta(\Gamma)<0
,
\iota(\Gamma)>0
and
n_{3}(\Gamma,\varepsilon)\geq 1
. Then, by definition, we have:

\displaystyle\left\{\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{dstg}}(e^{\prime},e)\geq\frac{m(\Gamma,\varepsilon)}{|E|}\right\}

\displaystyle\subseteq(\{\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{dstg}}(e^{\prime},e)-\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{rev}}(e^{\prime},e)\geq n_{3}(\Gamma,\varepsilon)\}\cap\{\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{rev}}(e^{\prime},e)<n_{1}(\Gamma,\varepsilon)\})

\displaystyle\bigcup\;\{\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{rev}}(e^{\prime},e)\geq n_{1}(\Gamma,\varepsilon)\}

Consider any
\rho\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
such that
\mathsf{nb}_{\mathsf{rev}}(e^{\prime},e,\rho)\leq n_{1}(\Gamma,\varepsilon)
,
\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}(\Gamma[e],\rho)>0
and
\sum_{\pi\sqsubseteq\rho}v[e^{\prime},e](\pi)\leq-n_{2}(\Gamma,\varepsilon)
. By Lemma
25

\displaystyle\frac{\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho)(e^{\prime})}{\mathsf{lk}_{b}(\rho)(e)}

A.6.3
Complexity of Algorithm

Let us first state a lemma about the number of bits to represent an updated belief.

Lemma 35
.

Consider an MEMDP
\Gamma
and a belief
b\in\mathcal{D}(E)
. Assume that there is
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for all
e\in E
,
b(e)=\frac{x_{e}}{N}
for some

. Assume also that there is

such that, for all
e\in E
, and
(q,a,q^{\prime})\in Q\times A\times Q
, we have
\delta_{e}(q,a)(q^{\prime})=\frac{y_{q,a,q^{\prime},e}}{T}
for some

. Then, for all
(q,a,q^{\prime})\in Q\times A\times Q
such that

, there is
N^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\log(N^{\prime})\leq\log(N)+\log(T)+\log(|E|)
and, for all
e\in E

0\leq z\leq N^{\prime}
such that
\lambda[b,q,a,q^{\prime}](e)=\frac{z}{N^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

Let
(q,a,q^{\prime})\in Q\times A\times Q
such that
p[b,q,a,q^{\prime}]>0
and let
e\in E
. We have:

\displaystyle\lambda[b,q,a,q^{\prime}](e)=\frac{b(e)\cdot\delta_{e}(q,a)(q^{\prime})}{\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E}b(e^{\prime})\cdot\delta_{e^{\prime}}(q,a)(q^{\prime})}=\frac{\frac{x_{e}}{N}\cdot\frac{y_{q,a,q^{\prime},e}}{T}}{\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E}\frac{x_{e^{\prime}}}{N}\cdot\frac{y_{q,a,q^{\prime},e^{\prime}}}{T}}=\frac{x_{e}\cdot y_{q,a,q^{\prime},e}}{\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E}x_{e^{\prime}}\cdot y_{q,a,q^{\prime},e^{\prime}}}

The denominator
\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E}x_{e^{\prime}}\cdot y_{q,a,q^{\prime},e^{\prime}}
does not depend on the environment
e
considered. Furthermore:

\log\left(\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E}x_{e^{\prime}}\cdot y_{q,a,q^{\prime},e^{\prime}}\right)\leq\log\left(\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E}N\cdot T\right)=\log(|E|\cdot N\cdot T)=\log(|E|)+\log(N)+\log(T)

∎

\Gamma

Proof.

Consider any two-non empty sets
A,B
and
f\colon A\times B\to[0,1]
. Let

(with probability distributions with countable support) be such that, for all
(\tau_{A},\tau_{B})\in\mathcal{D}(A)\times\mathcal{D}(B)

. Then, if
A
or
B
is finite:

\sup_{\tau_{a}\in\mathcal{D}(A)}\inf_{\tau_{b}\in\mathcal{D}(B)}g(\tau_{a},\tau_{b})=\inf_{\tau_{b}\in\mathcal{D}(B)}\sup_{\tau_{a}\in\mathcal{D}(A)}g(\tau_{a},\tau_{b})

This result corresponds to von Neuman’s minimax theorem
[
20
]
when
A
and
B
are finite, the case where one of the set is infinite (possibly uncountably) is a standard generalization, see e.g.
[
18
]
. Applied to our case, we obtain:

\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A))}\inf_{b\in\mathcal{D}(E)}\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,\tau,W)=\inf_{b\in\mathcal{D}(E)}\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A))}\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,\tau,W)

e\in E

\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,\tau,W)=\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E}b(e^{\prime})\cdot\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,e^{\prime},\tau,W)\geq\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E}b(e^{\prime})\cdot\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,e,\tau,W)=\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,e,\tau,W)

\inf_{b\in\mathcal{D}(E)}\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,\tau,W)=\min_{e^{\prime}\in E}\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,e^{\prime},\tau,W)=\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{uni}}_{q}(\Gamma,\tau,W)

and
\varepsilon>0

\sigma\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)

\displaystyle\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,\tau,W)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)}\tau(\sigma^{\prime})\cdot\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,\sigma^{\prime},W)\leq\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)}\tau(\sigma^{\prime})\cdot\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,\sigma,W)+\varepsilon

\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A))}\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,\tau,W)=\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,W)

Overall, we have:

\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A))}\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{uni}}_{q}(\Gamma,\tau,W)=\inf_{b\in\mathcal{D}(E)}\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{pr}}_{q}(\Gamma,b,W)

7
, Lemma 19]
.

Proof.

Consider an MEMDP
\Gamma
, a state
q\in Q
, and a mixed strategy
\tau\in\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A))
. For all
\rho\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
,
e\in E
and
\sigma\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)
, we let
p[\sigma,e,\rho]:=\tau(\sigma)\cdot\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\Gamma[e],\rho]
and
p[\tau,e,\rho]:=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)}p[\sigma,e,\rho]=\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\tau}[\Gamma[e],\rho]
. Consider now any
\rho\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
. Let us show that, for all
e,e^{\prime}\in E
such that
p[\tau,e,\rho],p[\tau,e^{\prime},\rho]\neq 0
, we have, for all
\sigma\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)
:

\frac{p[\sigma,e,\rho]}{p[\tau,e,\rho]}=\frac{p[\sigma,e^{\prime},\rho]}{p[\tau,e^{\prime},\rho]}

Indeed, consider any
e\in E
and
\sigma\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)
. Let us write
\rho
as
\rho=q_{0}\cdot(a_{0},q_{1})\cdots(a_{n-1},q_{n})
, with
q_{0}:=q
. For all
0\leq i\leq n
, we let
\rho_{\leq i}:=q_{0}\cdots(a_{i-1},q_{i})
. (In particular,
\rho_{\leq 0}=q_{0}
.) We have:

\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\Gamma[e],\rho]=\Pi_{i=0}^{n-1}\sigma(\rho_{\leq i})(a_{i})\cdot\delta_{e}(q_{i},a_{i})(q_{i+1})

Therefore:

\frac{p[\sigma,e,\rho]}{p[\tau,e,\rho]}=\frac{\tau(\sigma)\cdot\prod\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\sigma(\rho_{\leq i})(a_{i})\cdot\delta_{e}(q_{i},a_{i})(q_{i+1})}{\sum\limits_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)}\tau(\sigma^{\prime})\cdot\prod\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\sigma^{\prime}(\rho_{\leq i})(a_{i})\cdot\delta_{e}(q_{i},a_{i})(q_{i+1})}=\frac{\tau(\sigma)\cdot\prod\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\sigma(\rho_{\leq i})(a_{i})}{\sum\limits_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)}\tau(\sigma^{\prime})\cdot\prod\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\sigma^{\prime}(\rho_{\leq i})(a_{i})}

This does not depend on the environment
e
, thus
\frac{p[\sigma,e,\rho]}{p[\tau,e,\rho]}=\frac{p[\sigma,e^{\prime},\rho]}{p[\tau,e^{\prime},\rho]}
.

Now, let us define a strategy
\sigma\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)
. For all
\rho\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
, we let
\sigma(\rho)\in\mathcal{D}(A)
be such that, for all
a\in A
:

\displaystyle\sigma(\rho)(a):=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)}\sigma^{\prime}(\rho)(a)\cdot\frac{p[\sigma^{\prime},e,\rho]}{p[\tau,e,\rho]}&\text{ for some }e\in E\text{ such that }p[\tau,e,\rho]\neq 0\text{ if one exists}\\
\text{is arbitrary}&\text{ if }p[\tau,e,\rho]=0\text{ for all }e\in E\end{cases}

Consider an environment
e\in E
. Let us show by induction on
\rho\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
that
\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\Gamma[e],\rho]=p[\tau,e,\rho]
. This clearly holds for all
\rho\in Q
. Assume now that this holds for some
\rho\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
and let
(a,q^{\prime})\in Q
. If
p[\tau,e,\rho]=\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\Gamma[e],\rho]=0
, then
p[\tau,e,\rho\cdot(a,q^{\prime})],\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\Gamma[e],\rho\cdot(a,q^{\prime})]=0
. Thus, we assume that
p[\tau,e,\rho]=\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\Gamma[e],\rho]>0
. We have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\Gamma[e],\rho\cdot(a,q^{\prime})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\Gamma[e],\rho]\cdot\sigma(\rho)(a)\cdot\delta_{e}(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)(q^{\prime})

\displaystyle=p[\tau,e,\rho]\cdot\left(\sum\limits_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)}\sigma^{\prime}(\rho)(a)\cdot\frac{p[\sigma^{\prime},e,\rho]}{p[\tau,e,\rho]}\right)\cdot\delta_{e}(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)(q^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)}\sigma^{\prime}(\rho)(a)\cdot p[\sigma^{\prime},e,\rho]\cdot\delta_{e}(\mathsf{last}(\rho),a)(q^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)}p[\sigma^{\prime},e,\rho\cdot(a,q^{\prime})]

\displaystyle=p[\tau,e,\rho\cdot(a,q^{\prime})]

Therefore, the property holds for
\rho\cdot(a,q^{\prime})\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
as well. In fact, it holds for all
\rho\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
. It follows that, for all
\rho\in Q\cdot(A\cdot Q)^{*}
:

\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\Gamma[e],\mathsf{cyl}(\rho)]=\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)}\tau(\sigma^{\prime})\cdot\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma^{\prime}}[\Gamma[e],\mathsf{cyl}(\rho)]=\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\tau}[\Gamma[e],\mathsf{cyl}(\rho)]

By Carathéodory’s extension theorem, a probability measure is entirely defined by the measure of cylinder sets. Since
\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\Gamma[e],\cdot]\colon\mathsf{Borel}(Q)
and
\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A)}\tau(\sigma^{\prime})\cdot\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma^{\prime}}[\Gamma[e],\cdot]\colon\mathsf{Borel}(Q)
are probability measures, we obtain that, for all Borel objectives
W\in\mathsf{Borel}(Q)
:

\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\sigma}[\Gamma[e],W]=\mathbb{P}_{q}^{\tau}[\Gamma[e],W]

This holds for all environment
e\in E
, thus:

\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{uni}}_{q}(\Gamma,\tau,W)=\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{uni}}_{q}(\Gamma,\sigma,W)\leq\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{uni}}_{q}(\Gamma,W)

This holds for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A))
. In addition, we have
\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{uni}}_{q}(\Gamma,W)\leq\sup\limits_{\tau\in\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{Strat}(Q,A))}\mathsf{val}^{\mathsf{uni}}_{q}(\Gamma,\tau,W)
. The lemma follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem

Proof.

As in the proof sketch, let
N:=\lceil\log(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})\rceil
,
k:=|E|
, and
S:=\{b\in\mathcal{D}(E)\mid\forall e\in E,\;b(e)\in\{\frac{x}{k\cdot 2^{N+1}}\mid 0\leq x\leq k\cdot 2^{N+1}\}\}
. We indeed have
|S|=O(2^{k^{2}\cdot N})
, we also have
\varepsilon\geq\frac{1}{2^{N}}
.

Consider any
b\in\mathcal{D}(E)
. Let us define
b^{\prime}\in S
such that
\mathsf{Diff}(b,b^{\prime})\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
. Consider any
e\in E
. For all
e^{\prime}\in E\setminus\{e\}
, we let
b^{\prime}(e^{\prime}):=\frac{x_{e^{\prime}}}{k\cdot 2^{N+1}}
for the largest
0\leq x\leq k\cdot 2^{N+1}
such that
b^{\prime}(e)\leq b(e)
. Therefore, we have
0\leq b(e^{\prime})-b^{\prime}(e^{\prime})\leq\frac{1}{k\cdot 2^{N+1}}
. Furthermore, we have
\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E\setminus\{e\}}x_{e^{\prime}}=k\cdot 2^{N+1}\cdot\left(\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E\setminus\{e\}}b^{\prime}(e^{\prime})\right)\leq k\cdot 2^{N+1}\left(\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E\setminus\{e\}}b(e^{\prime})\right)\leq k\cdot 2^{N+1}
. Thus, we can define
b^{\prime}(e):=\frac{\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E\setminus\{e\}}x_{e^{\prime}}}{k\cdot 2^{N+1}}
and have
b(e)\leq b^{\prime}(e)\in[0,1]
. We have defined
b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}(E)
. As noted in the proof of Lemma
4
, we have:

\mathsf{Diff}(b,b^{\prime})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{e}\in E\\
b(\tilde{e})\geq b^{\prime}(\tilde{e})\end{subarray}}(b(\tilde{e})-b^{\prime}(\tilde{e}))

Therefore:

\mathsf{Diff}(b,b^{\prime})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{e}\in E\\
b(\tilde{e})\geq b^{\prime}(\tilde{e})\end{subarray}}(b(\tilde{e})-b^{\prime}(\tilde{e}))=\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E\setminus\{e\}}(b(e^{\prime})-b^{\prime}(e^{\prime}))\leq\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E\setminus\{e\}}\frac{1}{k\cdot 2^{N+1}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{N+1}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

Therefore, by Theorem
7
and Lemma
4
:

\mathsf{val}_{q}^{\mathsf{uni}}(\Gamma,W)=\inf_{b\in\mathcal{D}(E)}\mathsf{val}_{q}^{\mathsf{pr}}(\Gamma,b,W)\leq\min_{b\in S}\mathsf{val}_{q}^{\mathsf{pr}}(\Gamma,b,W)\leq\inf_{b\in\mathcal{D}(E)}\mathsf{val}_{q}^{\mathsf{pr}}(\Gamma,b,W)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}=\mathsf{val}_{q}^{\mathsf{uni}}(\Gamma,W)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

Thus:

|\min_{b\in S}\mathsf{val}_{q}^{\mathsf{pr}}(\Gamma,b,W)-\mathsf{val}_{q}^{\mathsf{uni}}(\Gamma,W)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

Furthermore, the number of bits to encode any
b\in S
is polynomial in
N
and
k
. Therefore, Algorithm
1
executed on any belief
b\in S
with
\gamma:=\varepsilon/2
and
n:=m(\Gamma,\frac{\varepsilon}{4k})
computes in exponential space (resp. polynomial space if the probabilities are given in unary) a value
v_{b}\in[0,1]
such that
|\mathsf{val}_{q}^{\mathsf{pr}}(\Gamma,b,W)-v_{b}|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
. Letting
b_{m}\in S
be such that
\min_{b\in S}\mathsf{val}_{q}^{\mathsf{pr}}(\Gamma,b,W)=\mathsf{val}_{q}^{\mathsf{pr}}(\Gamma,b_{m},W)
and
b_{s}\in S
be such that
\min_{b\in S}v_{b}=v_{b_{s}}
, we have:

-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\leq\mathsf{val}_{q}^{\mathsf{pr}}(\Gamma,b_{m},W)-v_{b_{m}}\leq\mathsf{val}_{q}^{\mathsf{pr}}(\Gamma,b_{m},W)-v_{b_{s}}\leq\mathsf{val}_{q}^{\mathsf{pr}}(\Gamma,b_{s},W)-v_{b_{s}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

Therefore, we have:

|\min_{b\in S}\mathsf{val}_{q}^{\mathsf{pr}}(\Gamma,b,W)-\min_{b\in S}v_{b}|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

Hence:

|\min_{b\in S}v_{b}-\mathsf{val}_{q}^{\mathsf{uni}}(\Gamma,W)|\leq\varepsilon

The environment beliefs in
S
can be enumerated in polynomial space, thus the value
\min_{b\in S}v_{b}
can be computed in exponential space (resp. polynomial space if the probabilities are given in unary), the theorem follows.
∎

Now, let us show that the function

Lemma 38
.

For all
x<y<z\in[0,1]
, we have:

f(y)\leq\min(f(x),f(z))

Furthermore, consider any
x<z\in[0,1]
. Letting
y:=\frac{z+x}{2}
,
\delta:=z-x
, and
\eta:=\max(|f(y)-f(x)|,|f(z)-f(y)|)

f(y)-\frac{2\eta}{\delta}\leq\inf_{t\in[0,1]}f(t)\leq f(y)

Proof.

Consider first some
x<y<z\in[0,1]
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\sigma\in S
such that
g(\sigma,y)\geq f(y)-\varepsilon
. Let
a_{1}:=g(\sigma,1)
and
a_{2}:=g(\sigma,0)
. There are two cases:

•

If
a_{1}\geq a_{2}\in[0,1]
, then
g(\sigma,z)-g(\sigma,y)=(za_{1}+(1-z)a_{2})-(ya_{1}+(1-y)a_{2})=(z-y)(a_{1}-a_{2})\geq 0
. Therefore:
f(y)-\varepsilon\leq g(\sigma,y)\leq g(\sigma,z)\leq f(z)\leq\min(f(x),f(z))
;

•

If
a_{1}<a_{2}\in[0,1]
, then
g(\sigma,x)-g(\sigma,y)=(xa_{1}+(1-x)a_{2})-(ya_{1}+(1-y)a_{2})=(x-y)(a_{1}-a_{2})>0
. Therefore:
f(y)-\varepsilon\leq g(\sigma,y)\leq g(\sigma,x)\leq f(x)\leq\min(f(x),f(z))
.

Since this holds for all
\varepsilon>0
, we have
f(y)\leq\min(f(x),f(z))
.

Consider now some
x<z\in[0,1]
, and let
y:=\frac{z+x}{2}
,
\delta:=z-x
, and
\eta:=\max(|f(y)-f(x)|,|f(x)-f(z)|)
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and consider some
\sigma\in S
such that
g(\sigma,y)\geq f(y)-\varepsilon
. Let
a_{1}:=g(\sigma,1)
and
a_{2}:=g(\sigma,0)
. We have:
g(\sigma,y)=g(\sigma,z)+(z-y)(a_{2}-a_{1})
. Thus

f(y)-\varepsilon\leq g(\sigma,y)=g(\sigma,z)+(z-y)(a_{2}-a_{1})\leq f(z)+\frac{\delta(a_{2}-a_{1})}{2}

Thus:

a_{1}-a_{2}\leq\frac{2}{\delta}(f(z)-f(y)+\varepsilon)\leq\frac{2}{\delta}(\eta+\varepsilon)

Symmetrically, we have:
g(\sigma,y)=g(\sigma,x)+(y-x)(a_{1}-a_{2})
. Thus

f(y)-\varepsilon\leq g(\sigma,y)=g(\sigma,x)+(y-x)(a_{1}-a_{2})\leq f(x)+\frac{\delta(a_{1}-a_{2})}{2}

Thus:

a_{2}-a_{1}\leq\frac{2}{\delta}(f(x)-f(y)+\varepsilon)\leq\frac{2}{\delta}(\eta+\varepsilon)

As this holds for all
\varepsilon>0
, we obtain:

|a_{1}-a_{2}|\leq\frac{2(\eta+\varepsilon)}{\delta}

Now, let

f(y)-\varepsilon\leq g(\sigma,y)\leq a+\frac{2(\eta+\varepsilon)}{\delta}
. Furthermore, for all

, we have

. Overall, we obtain:

f(y)-\varepsilon-\frac{2(\eta+\varepsilon)}{\delta}\leq a\leq\inf_{t\in[0,1]}f(t)\leq f(y)

As this holds for
\varepsilon>0
, the lemma follows.

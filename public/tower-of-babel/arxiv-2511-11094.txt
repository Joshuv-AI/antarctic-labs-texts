New constraints on equation of state of hot QCD matter
Lu-Meng Liu, Jinhui Chen, Xu-Guang Huang, Jiangyong Jia, Chun Shen, Chunjian Zhang

Abstract
The longitudinal structure of the quark-gluon plasma (QGP) remains a key challenge in heavy-ion physics. In this Letter, we propose a novel observable, event-by-event mean transverse momentum fluctuations Var ⟨ p T ⟩ \mathrm{Var}_{\langle p_{T}\rangle} , which is sensitive to the local pressure gradients and serves as a probe of longitudinal dynamics in the initial state of QGP. We demonstrate that the covariance of averaged transverse momentum at two rapidities Cov ⟨ p T ⟩ ​ ( η 1 , η 2 ) \mathrm{Cov}_{\langle p_{T}\rangle}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2}) and its associated decorrelation measures, R p T ​ ( η 1 , η 2 ) R_{p_{T}}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2}) and r p T ​ ( η , η ref ) r_{p_{T}}(\eta,\eta_{\mathrm{ref}}) , exhibit strong sensitivity to the stiffness of equation of state (EoS) of QGP, while showing negligible dependence on the QGP transport coefficients. This distinctive behavior, revealed through state-of-the-art (3+1)-dimensional hydrodynamic simulations, establishes a powerful approach for constraining the EoS of QCD matter. In the meantime, our results provide new insights into the longitudinal structure of the QGP and its properties under high baryon density.



Introduction.
Determining the equation of state (EoS) in quantum chromodynamics (QCD) matter at finite densities remains a central challenge in nuclear physics, with profound implications spanning from compact stars to heavy-ion collisions
Danielewicz et al. 2002
;
Li et al. 2008
;
Sorensen et al. 2024
. The EoS stiffness manifests distinctly across physical systems: in astrophysics, it governs neutron star structure through the Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff equations
Lattimer 2012
;
Oertel et al. 2017
; while in heavy-ion collisions, the EoS directly controls the quark-gluon plasma (QGP) fireball expansion dynamics, with stiffer equations of state (characterized by larger sound velocity
c_{s}^{2}
) generating larger acceleration from pressure gradients that drive faster hydrodynamic expansion, ultimately manifesting in enhanced anisotropic flow in the final-state particle distributions.

In relativistic heavy-ion collisions, performed at the Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider (RHIC) and the Large Hadron Collider (LHC), the creation of QGP provides a unique opportunity to study the fundamental properties of QCD under extreme conditions
Shuryak 1980
;
Cortese et al. 2004
;
Adams et al. 2005
;
Adcox et al. 2005
;
Gardim et al. 2020a
. Understanding the evolution of QGP from initial state to final-state momentum-space anisotropic flow is a central theme
Ollitrault 1992
;
Sorge 1999
;
Bilandzic et al. 2011
;
Heinz and Snellings 2013
. This evolution is driven by pressure gradient-driven hydrodynamic expansion. For instance, the event-averaged mean transverse momentum
\left\llangle p_{T}\right\rrangle
, driven by radial flow, correlates positively with the inverse transverse size of the initial energy density profile
Bozek and Broniowski 2012
;
Giacalone et al. 2021a
;
Cao et al. 2022
;
Schenke et al. 2020a
;
Jia et al. 2024a
;
Abdulhamid et al. 2024
;
Aboona et al. 2025a
. Studies employing various EoS at RHIC and LHC energies indicate that a larger speed of sound generally leads to increased anisotropic flow and a higher mean transverse momentum at mid-rapidity
Sangaline and Pratt 2016
;
Nijs and van der Schee 2024
;
Gong et al. 2024
. Recently, there have been proposals to measure the speed of sound through the relation of
c_{s}^{2}=dP/de\sim d\,\mathrm{ln}\left\llangle p_{T}\right\rrangle/d\,\mathrm{ln}N_{ch}
with collisions at a fixed volume in ultra-central centrality
Gardim et al. 2020b
;
Hayrapetyan et al. 2024
.
While transverse dynamics have been extensively studied, longitudinal dynamics also play a critical role in shaping QGP’s evolution, particularly in low-energy regimes such as the Beam Energy Scan (BES) program where the Bjorken scaling hypothesis (
\eta_{s}\approx y
) breaks down with
\eta_{s}=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{t+z}{t-z}\right)
and
y=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{E+p_{z}}{E-p_{z}}\right)
representing space-time and flow rapidity, respectively. Furthermore, event-by-event (EbE) fluctuations in the initial state are also expected to induce rapidity-dependent inhomogeneities in entropy deposition, which may decorrelate mean
\left\llangle p_{T}\right\rrangle
at different rapidity bins
Bozek et al. 2011
;
Bozek and Broniowski 2016
;
Jia and Huo 2014
.

Relativistic viscous hydrodynamics simulations successfully describe experimental observables in heavy-ion collisions, with their predictive power fundamentally relying on the EoS that relates local energy density to pressure. At LHC and the top RHIC energies, where the net baryon density and chemical potential
\mu_{B}
approach zero, the EoS can be calculated from the first principles using lattice QCD techniques
Borsanyi et al. 2010
;
Bazavov et al. 2009
;
Bazavov et al. 2014
;
Bazavov et al. 2019
. As collision energies decrease and baryon stopping becomes significant, the finite
\mu_{B}
regime presents both challenges and opportunities
Shen et al. 2025
;
Chen et al. 2025
. This region allows exploration of QGP properties at lower temperatures and higher baryon densities, but lies beyond reliable lattice QCD calculations due to the sign problem
Karsch 2002
;
Muroya et al. 2003
;
Bedaque 2018
;
Ratti 2018
. Current understanding of this crucial region mostly relies on effective QCD models
Fukushima and Sasaki 2013
;
Liu et al. 2021
;
Fu et al. 2020
;
Gao and Pawlowski 2020
, multimessenger astronomy
Tsang et al. 2024
, and Taylor expansion based on higher-order susceptibilities at vanishing net baryon chemical potential
Bazavov et al. 2019
;
Monnai et al. 2019
. Despite these efforts, substantial uncertainties remain in the finite-density EoS, underscoring the importance of heavy-ion collisions for mapping the complete QCD phase diagram under extreme conditions
Fukushima and Hatsuda 2011
;
Luo and Xu 2017
;
Bzdak et al. 2020
;
Chen et al. 2024
.

Prior theoretical and experimental studies have explored longitudinal decorrelations in high-energy nuclear collisions, including the pseudorapidity dependence of anisotropic flow
Petersen et al. 2011
;
Pang et al. 2012
;
Jia et al. 2017
;
Pang et al. 2015

;
Chatterjee and Bozek 2017

;
Jia et al. 2016
;
Jia et al. 2020
. These observations provide crucial first insights into the longitudinal structure of the QGP. Yet, the intrinsic longitudinal dynamics of the QGP and how they are influenced by the equation of state remain largely unexplored. Recent studies indicate that the transverse momentum longitudinal correlation
G_{2}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})
originates from hydrodynamic response to pre-equilibrium correlations
Agakishiev et al. 2011
;
Acharya et al. 2020
;
Savchuk 2025

In this Letter, we employ a full (3+1)D viscous hybrid hydrodynamic framework to simulate
197
Au+
197
Au collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\rm NN}}=
19.6 GeV, the BES program energy where both EoS at such finite baryon density effects and non-Bjorken longitudinal expansion are significant. We propose novel measures of

Observables.
The
p_{T}
decorrelation that origin from the EbE fluctuation of the size and entropy of the QGP initial state along the pseudorapidity direction can be characterized by
Chatterjee and Bozek 2017

\displaystyle R_{p_{T}}\left({\eta_{1}},{\eta_{2}}\right)=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}_{\left\langle p_{T}\right\rangle}\left({\eta_{1}},{\eta_{2}}\right)}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_{\left\langle p_{T}\right\rangle}\left({\eta_{1}}\right)}\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_{\left\langle p_{T}\right\rangle}\left({\eta_{2}}\right)}},

(1)

where

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cov}_{\left\langle p_{T}\right\rangle}\left({\eta_{1}},{\eta_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\left\langle p_{T}\right\rangle_{\eta_{1}}-\left\llangle p_{T}\right\rrangle_{\eta_{1}}\right)\left(\left\langle p_{T}\right\rangle_{\eta_{2}}-\left\llangle p_{T}\right\rrangle_{\eta_{2}}\right)\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}_{\left\langle p_{T}\right\rangle}\left({\eta}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cov}_{\left\langle p_{T}\right\rangle}\left({\eta},{\eta}\right),

(2)

are the covariance and variance of
\left\langle p_{T}\right\rangle_{\eta}
, respectively.

R_{p_{T}}\left({\eta_{1}},{\eta_{2}}\right)
characterizes the correlations of
\left\langle p_{T}\right\rangle
between two pseudorapidity bins
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
, averaged over the ensemble of events, satisfying
R_{p_{T}}\left({\eta},{\eta}\right)=1
.
To suppress nonflow contributions, we employ a three-bin correlator, analogous to the method used in anisotropic flow analysis
Khachatryan et al. 2015
, defined as:

\displaystyle r_{p_{T}}\left({\eta},{\eta_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right)=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}_{\left\langle p_{T}\right\rangle}\left({-\eta},{\eta_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right)}{\operatorname{Cov}_{\left\langle p_{T}\right\rangle}\left({\eta},{\eta_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right)}=\frac{R_{p_{T}}\left({-\eta},{\eta_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right)}{R_{p_{T}}\left({\eta},{\eta_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right)}.

(3)

The second equality is a direct consequence of the variance symmetry
\operatorname{Var}_{\left\langle p_{T}\right\rangle}\left({\eta}\right)=\operatorname{Var}_{\left\langle p_{T}\right\rangle}\left({-\eta}\right)
in symmetric collisions.
In the above, the EbE mean transverse momentum
\left\langle p_{T}\right\rangle\left(\eta\right)
and its event average
\left\llangle p_{T}\right\rrangle\left(\eta\right)
are given by

\displaystyle\left\langle p_{T}\right\rangle_{\eta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{\eta}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\eta}}p_{\mathrm{T},i},~~~~\left\llangle p_{T}\right\rrangle_{\eta}=\frac{1}{N_{ev}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{ev}}\left\langle p_{T}\right\rangle_{\eta,j}

with
N_{\eta}
counts charged particles in the
\eta
bin per event and
N_{ev}
is the total number of events.

Equation of state in QCD matter.
To systematically investigate the effects of the equation of state, we introduce a parametric modification of the pressure
P_{\mathrm{NEOS}}(T,\mu_{B})
provided by lattice-QCD-based NEOS-BQS equation of state at finite baryon densities
Monnai et al. 2019
. This NEOS-BQS equation of state combines the pressure from lattice QCD calculations
Bazavov et al. 2014
with that of the hadron resonance gas model. The finite-density behavior is systematically incorporated via a Taylor expansion approach, utilizing higher-order baryon number susceptibilities calculated at zero net baryon chemical potential from state-of-the-art lattice QCD results
Bazavov et al. 2019
. A free parameter
\alpha(\geq-1)
is introduced to control the stiffness of the EoS via the transformation

P_{\alpha}(T,\mu_{B})=P_{\mathrm{NEOS}}(T,\mu_{B})\left[\frac{P_{\mathrm{NEOS}}(T,\mu_{B})}{T^{4}}\right]^{\alpha}.

(4)

This form maintains thermodynamic consistency while allowing for systematic variation of the EoS stiffness, and simultaneously prevents the speed of sound from exceeding the conformal limit, as shown in Fig.
1
. The corresponding entropy density, conserved charge densities, and energy density are derived as

\displaystyle s_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial P_{\alpha}}{\partial T},~~~~~~~n_{J,\alpha}=\frac{\partial P_{\alpha}}{\partial\mu_{J}},

(5)

\displaystyle e_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-P_{\alpha}+Ts_{\alpha}+\sum_{J}\mu_{J}n_{J,\alpha},

(6)

where
J
denotes the conserved charges. Detailed derivations can be found in the appendix.

Summary.
In summary, we have investigated transverse momentum decorrelation in pseudorapidity for 0-10% central
197
Au+
197
Au collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}
= 19.6 GeV using a (3+1)D hydrodynamic framework. Our results show significant longitudinal decorrelation effect (
R_{p_{\rm T}}
and
r_{p_{\rm T}}
) with approximately 50% deviation from unity across
\Delta\eta\sim 2
, challenging traditional boost-invariance assumptions and highlighting the nature of 3D QGP initial conditions. The decorrelation exhibits pronounced dependence on the stiffness of the EoS, where a stiffer EoS results in weaker decorrelation and a softer EoS leads to stronger decorrelation, while demonstrating negligible sensitivity to viscosity parameters. Our results reveal the particular importance of finite baryon density effects and longitudinal dynamics in low energy. These findings establish
\langle p_{\rm T}\rangle
decorrelations as a powerful probe of QGP initial conditions, providing new constraints on the EoS of QCD matter. Current high-statistical data of BES-II collision energies
Aboona et al. 2025b
;
Collaboration 2025
with larger acceptance using iTPC detector could directly measure such observables. Furthermore, the observables introduced in this paper should also be particularly useful to be performed as energy dependence of EoS based on the high-baryon density FAIR-CBM, NICA, and HIAF facilities.

Implementing more self-consistent equations of state with Bayesian analysis methods
Novak et al. 2014
;
Bernhard et al. 2019
;
Everett et al. 2021
leave as our next work. Recent developments like the Gaussian Process Regression model, which has successfully generated the EoS at zero baryon density
Gong et al. 2024
, could be particularly valuable as it can be naturally extended to finite baryon densities. While our findings based on the 3D-Glauber initial state and the NEOS-BQS baseline are robust, future work employing different initial condition models and a broader class of finite-
\mu_{B}
EoS will also be valuable to further quantify the systematic uncertainties.

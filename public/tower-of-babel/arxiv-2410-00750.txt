Quasi-reversible bullet models
Jérôme Casse

Abstract
We consider a large class of bullet models that contains, in particular, the colliding bullet model with creations and a new loop model. For this large class of bullet models, we give sufficient conditions on their parameter to be rot ​ ( π ) \text{rot}(\pi) -quasi-reversible and to be rot ​ ( π / 2 ) \text{rot}(\pi/2) -quasi-reversible. Moreover, those conditions assure them that one of their stationary measures is described by a Poisson point process. This result applied to the colliding bullet model with creations is a first step to study its property under its non-empty stationary measure.

Introduction

A notorious and difficult bullet problem, and many of its variants, have been studied in the last decade. Send consecutively an infinite number of bullets. If all the bullets have the same speed, they never collide, and there is no problem; but what happens if the speeds of bullets are randomly distributed? This problem is known under at least two names: colliding bullet problem and total annihilation ballistic problem.

It has been studied in different contexts and for different distributions of speeds in many recent papers. Let us mention the works when the speeds are uniformly distributed on an interval
[a,b]

[
BM20
]
, when the speeds are distributed on a set of size two
[
BL21
]
or three
[
HST21
]
, and in many other contexts
[
ABL20
,
ADJ+24
]
. Interested readers on this subject should also refer to references therein.

In
[
BCG+15
]
, a model in an equilibrium context has been studied, called Colliding Bullet Model with Creations (CBMC) here. In the model, there are two types of particles, those of speed
-1
and those of speed
1
. To obtain an equilibrium model, they add the following rule to create particles: particles with speed
-1
(resp.
1
) create particles of speed
1
(resp.
-1
) at rate
1
. On Figure
1
, there is a realisation of the dynamic. For this model, they show that it has only two stationary measures invariant by translation: the empty one and another one with an infinite number of particles. Nevertheless, this second one is hard to describe and does not have a simple form.

In addition, in the same work, they also study bullet models such that, when two particles collide, they are not always both annihilated, but:

1.

with probability
p_{1}
, the particle of speed
1
survives and the one of speed
-1
disappears;

2.

with probability
p_{-1}
, the particle of speed
-1
survives and the one of speed
1
disappears;

3.

with probability
p_{0}
, both particles disappear;

4.

with probability
1-p_{1}-p_{-1}-p_{0}
, they both survive and continue their trajectories.

In some particular cases, e.g. when
p_{1}=p_{-1}=1/2
, they show that one of the stationary measures is two independent Poisson Point Processes of intensity
1
: one for the distribution of particles with speed
-1
and one for the distribution of particles with speed
1
.

2
Definition and presentation of the results

2.1
Bullet models

The bullet models, considered in the article, are parameterised by the
8
following parameters:

•

the
spontaneous creation rate

\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,

•

the
vertical and horizontal split rates

\lambda_{V}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
\lambda_{H}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,

•

the
vertical and horizontal turn rate

\tau_{V}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
\tau_{H}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,

•

the
annihilation probability

p_{0}\in[0,1]
, and

•

the
vertical and horizontal coalescence probability

p_{V}\in[0,1]
and
p_{H}\in[0,1]

such that
p_{0}+p_{V}+p_{H}\leq 1
. The vocabulary comes from the one used in
[
BCES23
]
about Poisson-Kirchhoff Systems.

From this parameter
\mathfrak{p}=(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{V},\lambda_{H},\tau_{V},\tau_{H},p_{V},p_{H},p_{0})
and an initial condition given on the
x
-and
y
-axes, we define a random system of horizontal and vertical lines inside the quarter plane
[0,\infty)^{2}
.

The
initial condition
is a couple
(\mathcal{C}_{X},\mathcal{C}_{Y})
where
\mathcal{C}_{X}
, resp.
\mathcal{C}_{Y}
, is a (finite or enumerable) set of points on the non-negative
x
-axis, resp. on the non-negative
y
-axis. Hence, an element of
\mathcal{C}_{X}
is a point
(x,0)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\{0\}
. Similarly, an element of
\mathcal{C}_{Y}
is a point
(0,y)\in\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Moreover, we assume that both of them are locally finite, i.e. there is no accumulation point.

Finally, take also a PPP
\Xi_{0}
, called ex-nihilo creation points, on
(0,\infty)^{2}
with intensity
\lambda_{0}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y
. From the initial condition
(\mathcal{C}_{X},\mathcal{C}_{Y})
and the parameter
\mathfrak{p}=(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{V},\lambda_{H},\tau_{V},\tau_{H},p_{V},p_{H},p_{0})
, the bullet model is constructed with the following rules:

1_{V}
.

for each point
(x,0)\in\mathcal{C}_{X}
, a vertical line from the point
(x,0)
goes up;

1_{H}
.

for each point
(0,y)\in\mathcal{C}_{Y}
, a horizontal line from the point
(0,y)
goes right;

1_{0}
.

for each point
(x,y)\in\Xi_{0}
, a vertical line going up and a horizontal line going right are started from the point
(x,y)
.

Along a vertical line, two events can occur:

2_{V}(a)
.

at rate
\lambda_{V}
, a horizontal line starts going right and the vertical one continues going up;

2_{V}(b)
.

at rate
\tau_{V}
, the vertical line turns right and becomes then a horizontal line.

Similarly, along a horizontal line:

2_{H}(a)
.

at rate
\lambda_{H}
, a vertical line starts going up and the horizontal one continues going right;

2_{H}(b)
.

at rate
\tau_{H}
, the horizontal line turns up and becomes then a vertical line.

Finally, when two lines (one vertical and one horizontal) intersect, four events can occur:

3(a).

with probability
p_{V}
, the horizontal line stops and the vertical line continues going up;

3(b).

with probability
p_{H}
, the vertical line stops and the horizontal line continues going right;

3(c).

with probability
p_{0}
, both lines stop;

3(d).

with probability
1-(p_{V}+p_{H}+p_{0})
, both lines continue.

Such construction gives then a random set
\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{p},(\mathcal{C}_{X},\mathcal{C}_{Y}))
of segments and lines in the quarter-plane
[0,\infty)^{2}
.

The bullet models are Poisson-Kirchhoff systems defined in
[
BCES23
]
where the set of intensity is reduced to the singleton
\{0\}
, see
[
BCES23
, Section 6.4]
. Hence, the Theorem 2 of
[
BCES23
]
applies and, so, any bullet model is well-defined.

2.3
Initial conditions

Now, we formalise the set of probability laws for the initial condition
(\mathcal{C}_{X},\mathcal{C}_{Y})
.

To consider random initial conditions
(\mathcal{C}_{X},\mathcal{C}_{Y})
, the law of
(\mathcal{C}_{X},\mathcal{C}_{Y})
is represented here as a point process on the line
\mathbb{R}
. Basically, a point process on the line
\mathbb{R}
is a probability measure on the set of countable locally finite sets of points. Hence, a realisation of such a process is a set of points
X=\{x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}:i\in I\}
where
I
is countable and there is no accumulation point in
X
. The law of a point process could be given by the law of its
counting measure

N
, that is, the knowledge of

(\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N([a,b])=i}}\right)=\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\text{card}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(X\cap[a,b]}}\right)}=i}}\right))_{i\in\mathbb{N},a\in\mathbb{R},b\in\mathbb{R},a\leq b},

see
[
DVJ03
, Chapter 3.1]
or
[
Bré20
, Chapter 1]
for some reference books on point processes. The most famous point process is the Poisson Point Process (PPP). The law of the counting measure of a PPP on
\mathbb{R}
with intensity
\lambda\in[0,\infty)
is, for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,
a\leq b
,

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\text{card}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(X\cap[a,b]}}\right)}=i}}\right)=e^{-\lambda(b-a)}\frac{\lambda^{i}(b-a)^{i}}{i!}.

Now, let
X=\{x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}:i\in I\}
be a point process of counting measure
N
. We define the initial condition
(\mathcal{C}_{X},\mathcal{C}_{Y})
by

\mathcal{C}_{X}=\{(x,0):x\in X\text{ and }x\geq 0\}\text{ and }\mathcal{C}_{Y}=\{(0,-x):x\in X\text{ and }x<0\}.

(1)

In the sequel of this article, we do the abuse of language writing that the initial condition
(\mathcal{C}_{X},\mathcal{C}_{Y})
is distributed according to the counting measure
N
.

\mathcal{C}_{X}
and
\mathcal{C}_{Y}

Quasi-reversibility of bullet models

Consider
D_{4}
the dihedral group, the group of the symmetries of the square
[-1,1]^{2}
. The group
D_{4}
has
8

4

(0,0)
and angle

4

is also an automorphism of
\mathbb{R}^{2}

The set of configurations:

We focus, first, on the set of configurations of a bullet model inside any rectangle
[-a,a]\times[-b,b]
.

Let

be two positive real numbers. We define the two sets
\mathcal{S}_{V}
and
\mathcal{S}_{H}
of vertical and horizontal segments inside the rectangle
[-a,a]\times[-b,b]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{V}=\{[(x,y),(x,y+t)]:-a<x<a,-b\leq y<y+t\leq b\},

(12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{H}=\{[(x,y),(x+t,y)]:-a\leq x<x+t\leq a,-b<y<b\}.

(13)

Now, we define the set of configurations
\mathcal{U}
of any realisation of a bullet model inside the rectangle
[-a,a]\times[-b,b]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}

\displaystyle=\{U\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{S}_{V}\cup\mathcal{S}_{H}):|U|<\infty,

\displaystyle\qquad\text{if }S=[(x,y_{0}),(x,y_{1})]\in U\cap\mathcal{S}_{V}\text{, then}\ (y_{0}=-b\text{ or }\exists S^{\prime}\in U\cap\mathcal{S}_{H},\ (x,y_{0})\in S^{\prime})\text{ and}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad(y_{1}=b\text{ or }\exists S^{\prime\prime}\in U\cap\mathcal{S}_{H},\ (x,y_{1})\in S^{\prime\prime}),

\displaystyle\qquad\text{if }S=[(x_{0},y),(x_{1},y)]\in U\cap\mathcal{S}_{H}\text{, then}\ (x_{0}=-a\text{ or }\exists S^{\prime}\in U\cap\mathcal{S}_{V},\ (x_{0},y)\in S^{\prime})\text{ and}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad(x_{1}=a\text{ or }\exists S^{\prime\prime}\in U\cap\mathcal{S}_{V},\ (x_{1},y)\in S^{\prime\prime}),

\displaystyle\qquad\text{if }S=[(x,y_{0}),(x,y_{1})],\ S^{\prime}=[(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}_{0}),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}_{1})]\in U\cap\mathcal{S}_{V}\text{ with }S\neq S^{\prime}\text{, then}\ x\neq x^{\prime},

\displaystyle\qquad\text{if }S=[(x_{0},y),(x_{1},y)],\ S^{\prime}=[(x^{\prime}_{0},y^{\prime}),(x^{\prime}_{1},y^{\prime})]\in U\cap\mathcal{S}_{H}\text{ with }S\neq S^{\prime}\text{, then}\ y\neq y^{\prime}\}.

(14)

In words, this formalism tells that:

•

any realisation is a finite set of vertical and horizontal segments,

•

a vertical segment in the set begins at the bottom edge of the rectangle
[-a,a]\times[-b,b]
or at a horizontal segment in the set and

•

ends at the top edge of the rectangle or at a horizontal segment in the set,

•

similarly for the beginnings and the ends of horizontal segments,

•

the two last conditions are technical: they express the fact that two vertical, resp. horizontal, different segments do not share the same abscissa, resp. ordinate. It permits to assure the uniqueness of vertical, resp. horizontal, segments. Indeed, when a vertical, resp. horizontal, particle creates, or kills, or crosses a horizontal, resp. vertical, one, without these conditions, we could split its representation in many segments. The disadvantage of these conditions is that it forbids two different vertical, resp. horizontal, particles to share the same abscissa, resp. ordinate. Hopefully, in the bullet model considered, this never happens almost surely.

The last point we need to prove now is that if

Proof of Corollary
7
.

Consider a bullet model of parameter
\mathfrak{p}
such that
A,B_{V},B_{H}
, defined in Equations (
4
), (
5
) and (
6
), are all not zero, and such that
B_{V}\lambda_{0}=A\tau_{V}
and
Ap_{0}=B_{H}\tau_{V}
. Then, we take
\nu_{H}=A/B_{H}
,
\nu_{V}=A/B_{V}
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{p}}
defined by:

•

\widetilde{\lambda}_{0}=\frac{A}{B_{V}}\tau_{V}=\lambda_{0}
,
\widetilde{\lambda}_{V}=\frac{A}{B_{V}}p_{H}
and
\widetilde{\lambda}_{H}=\lambda_{V}
,

•

\widetilde{\tau}_{V}=\frac{B_{H}}{B_{V}}\tau_{V}=\frac{A}{B_{V}}p_{0}
and
\widetilde{\tau}_{H}=\tau_{V}=\frac{B_{V}}{A}\lambda_{0}
,

•

\widetilde{p}_{V}=\frac{B_{V}}{A}\lambda_{H}
,
\widetilde{p}_{H}=p_{V}
and
\widetilde{p}_{0}=\frac{B_{V}}{A}\tau_{H}
.

Let us check now that the five conditions of Theorem
6
are satisfied.

1.

\widetilde{\lambda}_{H}+\widetilde{\tau}_{H}=\lambda_{V}+\tau_{V}
is trivial. Now,
\widetilde{\lambda}_{V}+\widetilde{\tau}_{V}=\lambda_{H}+\tau_{H}
?

\displaystyle\widetilde{\lambda}_{V}+\widetilde{\tau}_{V}

\displaystyle=\frac{A}{B_{V}}p_{H}+\frac{B_{H}}{B_{V}}\tau_{V}=\frac{Ap_{H}+B_{H}\tau_{V}}{B_{V}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\lambda_{H}+\tau_{H})(\lambda_{V}+\tau_{V})p_{H}-\tau_{V}\tau_{H}p_{H}+(p_{H}+p_{V})(\lambda_{H}+\tau_{H})\tau_{V}-p_{H}\lambda_{H}\tau_{V}}{B_{V}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\lambda_{H}+\tau_{H})[(\lambda_{V}+\tau_{V})p_{H}+(p_{H}+p_{V})\tau_{V}-\tau_{V}p_{H}]}{B_{V}}=(\lambda_{H}+\tau_{H})\frac{B_{V}}{B_{V}}.

2.

\widetilde{\lambda}_{0}=\lambda_{0}=\frac{A}{B_{V}}\tau_{V}=\nu_{V}\tau_{V}
,
\widetilde{\lambda}_{V}=\frac{A}{B_{V}}p_{H}=\nu_{V}p_{H}
.

3.

\widetilde{\tau}_{V}=\frac{A}{B_{V}}p_{0}=\nu_{V}p_{0}
, and
\nu_{V}\widetilde{\tau}_{H}=\frac{A}{B_{V}}\frac{B_{V}}{A}\lambda_{0}
.

4.

\nu_{V}\widetilde{p}_{V}=\frac{A}{B_{V}}\frac{B_{V}}{A}\lambda_{H}
, and
\nu_{V}\widetilde{p}_{0}=\frac{A}{B_{V}}\frac{B_{V}}{A}\tau_{H}
.

5.

\widetilde{p}_{V}+\widetilde{p}_{H}+\widetilde{p}_{0}=p_{V}+p_{H}+p_{0}
?

\displaystyle\widetilde{p}_{V}+\widetilde{p}_{0}+\widetilde{p}_{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{B_{V}}{A}\lambda_{H}+\frac{B_{V}}{A}\tau_{H}+p_{V}=\frac{B_{V}}{A}(\lambda_{H}+\tau_{H})+p_{V}

\displaystyle=\frac{B_{V}}{A}(\widetilde{\lambda}_{V}+\widetilde{\tau}_{V})+p_{V}=\frac{B_{V}}{A}\widetilde{\lambda}_{V}+\frac{B_{V}}{A}\widetilde{\tau}_{V}+p_{V}

\displaystyle=p_{H}+p_{0}+p_{V}.

Hence, the five conditions of Theorem
6
are satisfied. That’s end the proof of the corollary.

Total disconnectedness and percolation for the supports of super-tree random measures
Edwin Perkins, Delphin Sénizergues

Abstract
Super-tree random measures (STRMs) were introduced by Allouba, Durrett, Hawkes and Perkins as a simple stochastic model which emulates a superprocess at a fixed time. A STRM ν \nu arises as the a.s. limit of a sequence of empirical measures for a discrete time particle system which undergoes independent supercritical branching and independent random displacement (spatial motion) of children from their parents. We study the connectedness properties of the closed support of a STRM ( supp ⁡ ( ν CLOSE \mathrm{supp}(\nu )) for a particular choice of random displacement. Our main results are distinct sufficient conditions for the a.s. total disconnectedness (TD) of supp ⁡ ( ν ) \mathrm{supp}(\nu) , and for percolation on supp ⁡ ( ν ) \mathrm{supp}(\nu) which will imply a.s. existence of a non-trivial connected component in supp ⁡ ( ν ) \mathrm{supp}(\nu) . We illustrate a close connection between a subclass of these STRMs and super-Brownian motion (SBM). For these particular STRMs the above results give a.s. TD of the support in three and higher dimensions and the existence of a non-trivial connected component in two dimensions, with the three-dimensional case being critical. The latter two-dimensional result assumes that p c ​ ( ℤ 2 ) p_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{2}) , the critical probability for site percolation on ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} , is less than 1 − e − 1 1-e^{-1} . (There is strong numerical evidence supporting this condition although the known rigorous bounds fall just short.) This gives evidence that the same connectedness properties should hold for SBM. The latter remains an interesting open problem in dimensions 2 2 and 3 3 ever since it was first posed by Don Dawson over 30 30 years ago.

0
for all times.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
d\geq\frac{4}{\beta}-1
. If
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
f,g\in F_{m}
, then w.p.
1
there is an a.s. finite random variable
N=N(m,f,g)\in\mathbb{N}^{\geq m}
a.s. so that

S^{f}\neq S^{g}\text{ and }\overline{C_{m}(S^{f})}\cap\overline{C_{m}(S^{g})}\neq\emptyset\\
\text{ imply that}\quad\forall n\geq N,\ \forall(f^{\prime},g^{\prime})\in K_{n}^{f}\times K_{n}^{g},\ \overline{C_{n}(S^{f^{\prime}})}\cap\overline{C_{n}(S^{g^{\prime}})}=\emptyset.

We will prove this result below.
The core of the proof, contained in Lemma
5.6
below, relies on a supermartingale convergence argument requiring
d\geq\frac{4}{\beta}-1
. For now we will show how it easily gives Theorem
1.9
.

We may fix
\omega
, outside a null set, so that the above Lemma holds for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and all
f,g\in F_{m}
, the conclusion of Lemma
4.1
holds, and
|K^{0}_{m}|<\infty
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. If
N_{m}(\omega)=\max_{f,g\in K^{0}_{m}}N(m,f,g)\in\mathbb{N}^{\geq m}
, then

\displaystyle\forall m\in\mathbb{N},\ \forall(f,g)\in(K_{m}^{0})^{2},\quad

\displaystyle\left(S^{f}\neq S^{g}\text{ and }\overline{C_{m}(S^{f})}\cap\overline{C_{m}(S^{g})}\neq\emptyset\right)\text{ implies }

\displaystyle\forall n\geq N_{m},\

\displaystyle\forall(f^{\prime},g^{\prime})\in K_{n}^{f}\times K_{n}^{g},\quad\overline{C_{n}(S^{f^{\prime}})}\cap\overline{C_{n}(S^{g^{\prime}})}=\emptyset.

(5.1)

Note also that if
f,g\in K^{0}_{m}
and
\overline{C_{m}(S^{f})}\cap\overline{C_{m}(S^{g})}=\emptyset
, then the conclusion in (
5.1
) holds easily for all
n\geq m
. This follows from (
4.1
) because that result implies for all
f,g\in K^{0}_{m}
,

\cup_{f^{\prime}\in K_{n}^{f}}\overline{C_{n}(S^{f^{\prime}})}\subset\overline{C_{m}(S^{f})}\text{ and }\cup_{g^{\prime}\in K_{n}^{g}}\overline{C_{n}(S^{g^{\prime}})}\subset\overline{C_{m}(S^{g})}.

(5.2)

Therefore we have from (
5.1
) that

\forall m\in\mathbb{N},\ \forall(f,g)\in(K_{m}^{0})^{2},\\
S^{f}\neq S^{g}\quad\text{ implies }\quad\forall n\geq N_{m},\ \forall(f^{\prime},g^{\prime})\in K_{n}^{f}\times K_{n}^{g},\quad\overline{C_{n}(S^{f^{\prime}})}\cap\overline{C_{n}(S^{g^{\prime}})}=\emptyset.

(5.3)

It follows easily from (
5.2
) and the definition of
\widetilde{C}_{m,n}(x)
that for
n\geq m
,

\widetilde{C}_{m,n}(x)\subset\overline{C_{m}(x)},\text{ and so }\mathrm{diam}(\widetilde{C}_{m,n}(x))\leq B^{-m}.

(5.4)

Here the diameter is taken with respect to the
L^{\infty}
norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We also have for
n\geq m
,

\displaystyle\cup_{x\in G_{m}}\widetilde{C}_{m,n}(x)=\cup_{x\in G_{m}}\cup_{f\in K^{0}_{m},\,S^{f}=x}\cup_{f^{\prime}\in K^{f}_{n}}\overline{C_{n}(S^{f^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle=\cup_{f\in K^{0}_{m}}\cup_{f^{\prime}\in K^{f}_{n}}\overline{C_{n}(S^{f^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle=\cup_{f^{\prime}\in K_{n}^{0}}\overline{C_{n}(S^{f^{\prime}})}\supset\mathrm{supp}(\nu).

(5.5)

We have used Lemma
4.1
in the last inclusion, and in the next to last inclusion note that
f^{\prime}\in K^{0}_{n}
iff
f^{\prime}|m\in K^{0}_{m}
and
f^{\prime}\in K_{n}^{f^{\prime}|m}
.

If
x,y
are distinct points in
G_{m}
, then we have for
n\geq N_{m}
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{m,n}(x)\cap\widetilde{C}_{m,n}(y)=\cup_{f\in K_{m}^{0},S^{f}=x}\cup_{g\in K^{0}_{m},S^{g}=y}\cup_{(f^{\prime},g^{\prime})\in K^{f}_{n}\times K^{g}_{n}}\overline{C_{m}(S^{f^{\prime}})}\cap\overline{C_{m}(S^{g^{\prime}})}=\emptyset,

where the final equality holds by (
5.3
) because
S^{f}=x\neq y=S^{g}
. So this and (
5.5
) allows us to write
\mathrm{supp}(\nu)
(for
n>N_{m}
) as a finite disjoint union of closed sets,
\mathrm{supp}(\nu)\cap\widetilde{C}_{m,n}(x)

x\in G_{m}
, each of
L^{\infty}
-diameter at most
B^{-m}
by (
5.4
).
As
m\in\mathbb{N}
is arbitrary, this proves
\mathrm{supp}(\nu)
is a.s. totally disconnected.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let

be in
\mathbb{N}

, and
\ell\in\{0,\dots,d-1\}

(a)

, and so in particular,

.

(b)

If
C_{n_{2}}(S^{f})
and
C_{n_{2}}(S^{g})
are
\ell
-neighbours and
S^{f|n_{1}}\neq S^{g|n_{1}}
, then
C_{n_{1}}(S^{f|n_{1}})
and
C_{n_{1}}(S^{g|n_{1}})
are
\ell^{\prime}
-neighbours for some
\ell^{\prime}\geq\ell
.

\displaystyle|S^{f,k}-S^{g,k}|

\displaystyle\geq|S^{f|n_{1},k}-S^{g|n_{1},k}|-\sum_{m=n_{1}+1}^{n_{2}}B^{-m}|X^{f|m,k}-X^{g|m,k}|

\displaystyle\geq|S^{f|n_{1},k}-S^{g|n_{1},k}|-B^{-n_{1}-1}(B-1)\sum_{m=0}^{n_{2}-n_{1}-1}B^{-m}

\displaystyle=|S^{f|n_{1},k}-S^{g|n_{1},k}|-B^{-n_{1}}+B^{-n_{2}}.

(5.7)

If

-neighbours, and

.

Recalling that

Notation 0.

If

is a
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-valued r.v., then for any

,

\Gamma_{R,n}^{\ell}(f,g):=\{(f^{\prime},g^{\prime})\in K_{n\vee R}^{f}\times K_{n\vee R}^{g}:\,C_{n\vee R}(S^{f^{\prime}})\text{ and }C_{n\vee R}(S^{g^{\prime}})\text{ are }\ell-\text{neighbours}\}.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
R
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-valued random variable.
For all
f,g\in F_{R}
, and
\ell\in\{0,\dots,d-1\}
, if
S^{f}\neq S^{g}
, then for all

, we have

\left(\forall\ell^{\prime}\in\{\ell,\dots,d-1\},\ \Gamma_{R,n}^{\ell^{\prime}}(f,g)=\emptyset\right)\Rightarrow\left(\forall n^{\prime}\geq n,\forall\ell^{\prime}\in\{\ell,\dots,d-1\},\Gamma_{R,n^{\prime}}^{\ell^{\prime}}(f,g)=\emptyset\right).

In particular (
\ell=0,\,n=R
),
if

are not neighbours, then
for all

,

f^{\prime}\in K^{f}_{n^{\prime}}
and
g^{\prime}\in K^{g}_{n^{\prime}}
are not neighbours.

Remark 5.5
.

Note that it is immediate that stating the above result for any
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-valued r.v.

is equivalent to stating it for all possible values in
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.
The current formulation happens to be more convenient for our proofs.
The same will be true for the statement of Lemma

5.6
.

Assume

and

for all

.
Let
\ell^{\prime}\in\{\ell,\dots,d-1\}
,

and, proceeding by contradiction, let

Then
(f^{\prime}|(n\vee R),g^{\prime}|(n\vee R))\in K^{f}_{n\vee R}\times K^{g}_{n\vee R}
and, by Lemma
5.3
(a)
,
S^{f^{\prime}|n\vee R}\neq S^{g^{\prime}|n\vee R}

By Lemma

are

-neighbours, then

Lemma 5.7
.

Assume
\{M_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-valued
(\mathcal{F}_{n})
-supermartingale such that for any

there is a

so that for all

\mathbb{P}(M_{n+1}\neq k\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n})\geq r_{k}\text{ on }\{M_{n}=k\}.

(5.15)

Then

a.s.

By supermartingale convergence we know
M_{n}\to M_{\infty}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
a.s. The above condition easily implies for

and

\mathbb{P}(\forall j\in[N,n+1],\ M_{j}=k)\leq(1-r_{k})\cdot\mathbb{P}(\forall j\in[N,n],\ M_{j}=k),

and therefore by induction, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and

Proof of Proposition

We first restate the proposition.

Proposition B.3
.

Let
\bar{\nu}
be a random measure in

such that
\mathbb{P}(\bar{\nu}(1)=k)>0

k\in\mathbb{N}

\mathbb{R}^{d}

Moreover for all

Fix

and work under
\mathbb{P}(\,\cdot\,|\,\nu(1)=k)
.
Let

\bar{\nu}
, order the (at most
k
) points in
\mathcal{S}
lexicographically, and repeat each

\bar{\nu}(\{x\})

\hat{X}_{1},\dots,\hat{X}_{k}

In words

and one can easily check measurability.

\hat{X}_{2}
is defined as
\hat{X}_{1}
but for

. Proceeding inductively we see these are all r.v.’s.

and set

for

.

.
Let
\tilde{Q}_{k}
be the law of
(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
.
Then
\tilde{Q}_{k}
is symmetric and for
\xi
as above,

\displaystyle\int\prod_{i=1}^{j}\xi(x_{i})\differential\tilde{Q}_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl(\exp\Bigl\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log\xi(X_{i})\Bigr\}\,\Bigl|\,\bar{\nu}(1)=k\Bigr)

For uniqueness define an equivalence relation

on

by

\pi\in S_{k}

for all

(write

).
Now apply Stone-Weierstrass on the locally compact space

(see Ex XI.6.6 on p. 255 of
[
17
]
) to the algebra of linear combinations of

, where
\xi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(0,1]

).
This shows that (
B.8
) uniquely determines
\int\phi\,\differential\tilde{Q}_{k}
for all symmetric functions

shows

Turning to (

∎

Sobolev regularity for the nonlocal ( 1 , p ) (1,p) -Laplace equations in the superquadratic case Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author.
Dingding Li and Chao Zhang ∗

Abstract
. We investigate the interior Sobolev regularity of weak solutions to the nonlocal ( 1 , p ) (1,p) -Laplace equations in the superquadratic case p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 . More precisely, for the fractional diﬀerentiability index s p ∈ ( 0 , p − 1 p ] s_{p}\in\left(0,\frac{p-1}{p}\right] , we establish that the weak solution u u exhibits W loc γ , q W_{\rm loc}^{\gamma,q} -regularity for any γ ∈ ( 0 , s p ​ p p − 1 ) \gamma\in\left(0,\frac{s_{p}p}{p-1}\right) and q ≥ p q\geq p . When s p ∈ ( p − 1 p , 1 ) s_{p}\in\left(\frac{p-1}{p},1\right) , the gradient ∇ u \nabla u of the weak solution is shown to exist and belong to ( L loc q ​ ( Ω ) ) N \left(L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\right)^{N} . As a product, the explicit Hölder continuity estimates of weak solutions are derived. The proof relies on a detailed analysis of the structural characteristics of ( 1 , p ) (1,p) -growth in the nonlocal setting, combined with the finite difference quotient method, tail estimates, refined energy estimates, and a Moser-type iteration scheme.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 2
. In this paper, we consider the following nonlocal
(1,p)
-Laplace equation

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{1})^{s_{1}}u+(-\Delta_{p})^{s_{p}}u=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(1.1)

where
s_{1},s_{p}\in(0,1)
,
p\geq 2
and the fractional operators
(-\Delta_{1})^{s_{1}}
and
(-\Delta_{p})^{s_{p}}
are defined by

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{1})^{s_{1}}u(x):=2\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+s_{1}}}

and

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{p})^{s_{p}}u(x):=2\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+s_{p}p}}\,dy

for
x\in\Omega
. Here
\mathrm{P.V.}
denotes the integral taken in the principal value sense.

The equations of the type considered in (
1.1
) were recently proposed by Górny, Mazón and Toledo
[
38
]

in a general setting of random walk spaces. These equations incorporate two random walk structures so that the associated functionals have diﬀerent growth on each structure, which can be regarded as a special case of
(p,q)
-growth problems. Since the pioneering works
[
44
,
45
]
, this class of problems has been extensively investigated. We refer the readers to
[
56
,
12
,
4
,
18
,
46
]
and the references therein for detailed overviews.

1.1.
Overview of related results.

Let us start with the problems with local
1
-growth. For the inhomogeneous
1
-Laplace Dirichlet problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta_{1}u:=-\mathrm{div}\left(\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\right)=f&\textmd{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\textmd{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.2)

it is well-known that the diffusion singularity of the 1-Laplacian
\Delta_{1}
manifests prominently on the set
\left\{Du=0\right\}
, commonly referred to as a facet. To address this issue, a vector field
Z\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
was introduced by using the Anzellotti-Frid-Chen pairing theory (see
[
3
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
) that serves as a generalized representative of the quotient
\frac{Du}{|Du|}
imprecisely. We refer to a series of works by Mazón and collaborators
[
47
,
40
,
49
,
50
]
for more comprehensive treatments of this approach. Moreover, problem (
1.2
) is usually addressed as the limit version for
p
-Laplace equations as
p
goes to
1^{+}
. The behavior of solutions to (
1.2
) has been rigorously analyzed under various assumptions on the right-hand side term
f
, see
[
16
,
42
,
53
]
. In these works, a smallness condition on
f
is typically imposed to ensure that the solution remains finite almost everywhere in
\Omega
. However, within the
L^{1}
framework, one cannot generally expect that the solutions are finite almost everywhere in
\Omega
. In this context, Mercaldo, Segura de León and Trombetti
[
54
]
established the existence of renormalized solutions.

Regarding the regularity of classical 1-growth problems, as far as we know, since the ellipticity of 1-Laplacian becomes singular over the facet
\left\{Du=0\right\}
, the solutions of
-\Delta_{1}u=f
may fail to be continuously differentiable. Nevertheless, the results on problems with nearly linear growth stand in sharp contrast to those in the 1-growth regime. In particular, De Filippis and Mingione
[
21
]
established the Schauder-type estimates for minimizers of the functional:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(w,\Omega):=\int_{\Omega}\left[c(x)|Dw|\log(1+|Dw|)+a(x)|Dw|^{q}\right]\,dx,\quad q>1.

Under suitable conditions on the coefficients
a(x)
and
c(x)
, these results yield
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity for the corresponding minimizers. We also refer to
[
19
,
23
,
32
,
33
,
30
,
57
]
for more results on the problems with nearly linear growth.

When it comes to the nonlocal counterpart

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{1})^{s}u=0,

there remain relatively few results addressing problems with 1-growth. It is worth noting that Mazón et al. have made a significant contribution to this field. In their seminal works
[
51
,
48
]
,
they not only introduced a rigorous definition of weak solutions for nonlocal evolution problems with 1-growth but also characterized the new qualitative phenomena inherent in these problems. In contrast to the local case, where the expression
\frac{Du}{|Du|}
arises naturally, the nonlocal counterpart takes the form
\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}
. To properly interpret this quantity, one must identify a function
Z
representing the nonlocal quotient. In this direction, Andreu et al.
[
1
,
2
]
demonstrated that for a class of nonlocal diffusion equations, the corresponding quantity satisfies
Z(x,y,t)\in\mathrm{sgn}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))
, where
\mathrm{sgn}(\cdot)
denotes the set-valued sign function with
\mathrm{sgn}(0)=[-1,1]
(see also
[
52
]
).
Bucur et al.
[
10
]
investigated the existence of
(s,1)
-harmonic function and established their equivalence to the minimizers of functional

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{N+s}}\,dxdy.

Assuming the inhomogeneous term
f
belongs to
L^{\frac{N}{s}}(\Omega)
with a smallness assumption, Bucur
[
9
]
studied the minimizer of
\mathcal{F}^{s_{p}}_{p}
given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{s_{p}}_{p}(u):=\frac{1}{2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s_{p}p}}\,dxdy-\int_{\Omega}fu\,dx,

where
p\geq 1
and
s_{p}=N+s-\frac{N}{p}\in[s,1)
. And it is shown that the minimizers correspond to weak solutions of the nonlocal 1-Laplacian problem and a “flatness” result was obtained, which implies that the expression of
Z
towards
\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}
may not precise.

Under certain natural structural conditions, Bucur et al.
[
11
]
proved that any
s
-minimal function
u
lies in
C(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and admits a continuous extension
\overline{u}\in C(\overline{\Omega})
. For further regularity results, we refer to the work of Novaga and Onoue
[
58
]
, who investigated the minimizers of a nonlocal variational problem arising from an image denoising model:

\displaystyle\text{min}\left\{\mathcal{F}_{K,f}(u)\big|u\in BV_{K}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\right\},

where
\mathcal{F}_{K,f}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{K,f}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}K(|x-y|)|u(x)-u(y)|dxdy+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(u(x)-f(x)\right)^{2}dx

and the space
BV_{K}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is given by

\displaystyle BV_{K}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}K(|x-y|)|u(x)-u(y)|dxdy<+\infty\right\}

with
K
being a kernel singular at the origin. In two dimensions, under the assumption that the original image
f
is locally
\beta
-Hölder continuous with
\beta\in(1-s,1]
, the authors showed that the minimizers preserve the same local Hölder regularity.

Finally, we would like to turn to the perturbed 1-Laplace equation of the form

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}\left(\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\right)-\mathrm{div}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)=f\quad\text{in }\Omega.

For the cases
p=2
and
p=3
, this equation arises in fluid mechanics
[
29
]
and materials science
[
59
]
respectively. Such problems have been investigated only in a limited number of specific settings.
The anisotropic case involving the
p
-Laplacian when it has linear growth on some of the coordinates has also been studied, for instance, in
[
37
,
55
]
. Employing De Giorgi-Nash-Moser theory, Tsubouchi
[
60
]
proved the local Lipschitz continuity of weak solutions. Under the additional assumption of convexity for weak solutions, Giga and Tsubouchi
[
35
]
showed that the solutions are actually of
C^{1}
-regular. Subsequently, Tsubouchi
[
61
]
removed the convexity assumption and achieved
C^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
-regularity by employing De Giorgi’s truncation method and the freezing coefficients technique. These methods were further extended to prove regularity results for parabolic
(1,p)
-Laplace system in
[
62
,
63
,
64
]
.

1.2.
Main results.

Inspired by the aforementioned literature, we aim to investigate the regularity of weak solutions to (
1.1
). Although
[
38
]
has formulated a precise notion of weak solutions and systematically analyzed several critical phenomena associated with nonlocal
(1,p)
-growth evolution problems, the fundamental regularity of this model was still unknown. To this end, thanks to the development of the finite difference quotient technique within the fractional framework (see
[
7
,
8
,
5
,
6
,
31
,
26
,
34
]
), we establish the local Sobolev regularity of weak solutions to (
1.1
).

Different from the standard nonlocal
p
-growth case, the tail estimates developed in
[
6
]
fail to yield a suitable energy inequality in our setting, primarily due to the lack of regularity information on the function
Z
. As a result, when dealing with the energy inequality, we have to control the local part of the
p
-growth using the
1
-growth. This leads us to distinguish between two regimes for the parameter
s_{p}
, namely
s_{p}\in\left(0,\frac{p-1}{p}\right]
and
s_{p}\in\left(\frac{p-1}{p},1\right)
, which are consistent with the regularity findings in
[
25
,
7
]
. Additionally, the local boundedness will be established using techniques developed in
[
24
,
41
,
17
,
22
]
.

Before presenting the main results, we give the definition of weak solutions to (
1.1
).

Definition 1.1
.

A function
u\in W^{s_{1},1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap W^{s_{p},p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap L^{p-1}_{s_{p}p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is said to be a weak solution to problem (
1.1
) if

(i)

there exists a function
Z\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying
Z\in\text{sgn}(u(x)-u(y))
;

(ii)

for any
\varphi\in W^{s_{1},1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{s_{p},p}_{0}(\Omega)
, there holds

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Z\frac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{N+s_{1}}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+s_{p}p}}\,dxdy.

(1.3)

Remark 1.2
.

To ensure the existence of weak solutions, a decay assumption is required for the
p
-growth term, i.e.,

\displaystyle u\in L^{p-1}_{s_{p}p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

However, due to the integrability property

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{dy}{|x-x_{0}|^{N+s_{1}}}<+\infty,

(1.4)

no decay assumption is needed for the 1-growth term. The existence of weak solutions to problem (
1.1
) can be established via the direct method used in
[
9
]
.

Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}:=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}+\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},R),

where
\operatorname{Tail}(u;x_{0},R)
denotes the tail term associated with the function
u
at the point
x_{0}
and radius
R
(see Section
2
for details). We are now ready to state the main results of this work.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p\geq 2
,
s_{p}\in\left(0,\frac{p-1}{p}\right]
and let
u
be a locally bounded weak solution to problem (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
1.1
, we have

\displaystyle u\in W^{\gamma,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)

for any
q\geq p
and
\gamma\in\left(0,\frac{s_{p}p}{p-1}\right)
. Moreover, there exist two constants
C
and
\kappa
depending on
N,p,q,s_{1},s_{p},\gamma
such that for any ball
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\subset\Omega
with
R\in(0,1)
and any
r\in(0,R)
,

\displaystyle[u]^{q}_{W^{\gamma,q}(B_{r})}\leq\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+[u]_{W^{s_{p},p}(B_{R})}+1\right)^{q}}{(R-r)^{\kappa}}.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
p\geq 2
,
s_{p}\in\left(\frac{p-1}{p},1\right)
and let
u
be a locally bounded weak solution to problem (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
1.1
, we have

\displaystyle u\in W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)

for any
q\geq p
. Moreover, there exist two constants
C
and
\kappa
depending on
N,p,q,s_{1},s_{p},\gamma
such that for any ball
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\subset\Omega
with
R\in(0,1)
and any
r\in(0,R)
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{r})}\leq\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+[u]_{W^{s_{p},p}(B_{R})}+1\right)^{q}}{(R-r)^{\kappa}}.

By applying the Morrey-type embedding for fractional Sobolev spaces, Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
directly yield the following local Hölder regularity of weak solutions.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
p\geq 2
and
s_{p}\in\left(0,\frac{p-1}{p}\right]
. Then, for any locally bounded weak solution to problem (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
1.1
, we have

\displaystyle u\in C^{0,\gamma}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)

for any
\gamma\in\left(0,\frac{s_{p}p}{p-1}\right)
. Moreover, there exist two constants
C
and
\kappa
depending on
N,p,s_{1},s_{p},\gamma
, such that for any ball
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\subset\Omega
with
R\in(0,1)
and any
r\in(0,R)
,

\displaystyle[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{r})}\leq\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+[u]_{W^{s_{p},p}(B_{R})}+1\right)}{(R-r)^{\kappa}}.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
p\geq 2
and
s_{p}\in\left(\frac{p-1}{p},1\right)
. Then, for any locally bounded weak solution to problem (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
1.1
, we have

\displaystyle u\in C^{0,\gamma}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)

for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
. Moreover, there exist two constants
C
and
\kappa
depending on
N,p,s_{1},s_{p},\gamma
, such that for any ball
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\subset\Omega
with
R\in(0,1)
and any
r\in(0,R)
,

\displaystyle[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{r})}\leq\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+[u]_{W^{s_{p},p}(B_{R})}+1\right)}{(R-r)^{\kappa}}.

Remark 1.7
.

It is worth emphasizing that the appearance of the constant “1” in the inequalities of the main results stems directly from (
1.4
). Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
reveal distinct arguments for the cases
s_{p}\in\left(0,\frac{p-1}{p}\right]
and
s_{p}\in\left(\frac{p-1}{p},1\right)
, with the threshold
\frac{p-1}{p}
characterizing the existence of
\nabla u
. Indeed, we define the following sequence

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}=s_{p},\quad\gamma_{i+1}=\frac{s_{p}p}{q}+\gamma_{i}\frac{q-p+1}{q},

from which it follows that

•

For
s_{p}\in\left(0,\frac{p-1}{p}\right]
,
\gamma_{i}\to\frac{s_{p}p}{p-1}
as
i\to\infty
.

•

For
s_{p}\in\left(\frac{p-1}{p},1\right)
, there exists an index
i_{0}
such that
\gamma_{i}>1
for all
i\geq i_{0}
, thereby establishing the
W^{1,q}
-regularity of weak solutions.

The motivation for introducing the sequence
\{\gamma_{i}\}
is detailed in Lemmas
4.3
and
5.3
, and the iterative process yielding the limit parameter
\min\left\{1,\frac{s_{p}p}{p-1}\right\}
is presented in Lemma
4.5
and Proposition
5.4
. While, for
s_{p}\in\left(\frac{p-1}{p},1\right)
, the fractional Sobolev regularity of
\nabla u
is established, with the precise statement provided in Lemma
5.6
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the notations for fractional Sobolev spaces and state several critical lemmas. Section
3
is dedicated to establishing the local boundedness of weak solutions to problem (
1.1
). Finally, Sections
4
and
5
address the Sobolev regularity of weak solutions in the cases that
s_{p}\in\left(0,\frac{p-1}{p}\right]
and
s_{p}\in\left(\frac{p-1}{p},1\right)
, respectively.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce notations that will be used later. For any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, let
|z|
denote its Euclidean norm. Throughout the paper, we denote by
C
generic constants whose values may vary even within the same inequality. When necessary, we explicitly indicate their dependencies, for instance, if a constant depends on
N,p,s_{1},s_{p}
, it will be written as
C(N,p,s_{1},s_{p})
.

For a function
w:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
we denote by
w\in C^{0,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
that
w\in C^{0,\alpha}\left(B_{R}(x_{0})\right)
for any ball
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\subset\Omega
. The semi-norm of
C^{0,\alpha}
is defined by

\displaystyle[w]_{C^{0,\alpha}\left(B_{R}(x_{0})\right)}:=\sup\limits_{x,y\in B_{R}(x_{0}),x\neq y}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}.

We introduce the (fractional) Sobolev space
W^{1,q}(\Omega)
and
W^{\gamma,q}
, where
q\geq 1
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
. These spaces are respectively defined as

\displaystyle\left\{u\in L^{q}(\Omega)\bigg|\|u\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}:=\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}<+\infty\right\}

and

\displaystyle\left\{u\in L^{q}(\Omega)\bigg|[u]_{W^{\gamma,q}(\Omega)}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N+\gamma q}}\,dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}<+\infty\right\}.

For
q,\gamma>0
, the tail space
L^{q}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
contains all function
w\in L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|w|^{q}}{(1+|x|)^{N+\gamma}}\,dx<+\infty

and we define
\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},R)
for a function
u
belonging to the space
L^{p-1}_{s_{p}p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},R):=\left(R^{s_{p}p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{|u|^{p-1}}{|x-x_{0}|^{N+s_{p}p}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Under the assumption that
u
is locally bounded in
\Omega
, we have the following estimate.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
p>1
and
s_{p}\in(0,1)
. For any
u\in L^{p-1}_{s_{p}p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and any ball
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\subset\Omega
,
r\in(0,R)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},r)^{p-1}\leq C(N)\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{N}\left(\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},R)+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}\right)^{p-1}.

Next, for
\gamma>0
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

\displaystyle J_{\gamma}(a):=|a|^{\gamma-2}a.

We summarize two algebraic inequalities from
[
6
]
, which will be used in Sections
4
and
5
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
\gamma>0
and any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle C_{1}\left(|a|+|b|\right)^{\gamma-1}|b-a|\leq\left|J_{\gamma+1}(b)-J_{\gamma+1}(a)\right|\leq C_{2}\left(|a|+|b|\right)^{\gamma-1}|b-a|

where
C_{1}=\min\left\{\gamma,2^{1-\gamma}\right\}
,
C_{2}=\max\left\{\gamma,2^{1-\gamma}\right\}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
p\geq 2
and
\delta\geq 1
. Then there exists a constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\quad\left(J_{p}(a-b)-J_{p}(c-d)\right)\left(J_{\delta+1}(a-c)e^{2}-J_{\delta+1}(b-d)f^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{C}\left(|a-b|+|c-d|\right)^{p-2}\left(|a-c|+|b-d|\right)^{\delta-1}|(a-c)-(b-d)|^{2}(e^{2}+f^{2})

\displaystyle\quad-C\left(|a-b|+|c-d|\right)^{p-2}\left(|a-c|+|b-d|\right)^{\delta+1}|e-f|^{2}

for any
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R}
and
e,f\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

We now recall a classical iteration lemma that comes from
[
41
, Lemma 4.3]
(see also
[
65
, Lemma 2.1]
) and provide several Sobolev embedding inequalities. Lemmas
2.5
–
2.7
are derived from
[
27
]
and Lemma
2.8
is standard.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\left\{Y_{j}\right\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of positive numbers, satisfying the recursive inequalities

\displaystyle Y_{j+1}\leq Kb^{j}Y_{j}^{1+\delta}

for all
j=0,1,2,\dots
with
K>0,b>1
and
\delta>0
are given numbers. If

\displaystyle Y_{0}\leq\min\left\{1,(2K)^{-\frac{1}{\delta}}b^{-\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}}\right\},

we have
Y_{j}\leq 1
for some
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle Y_{j}\leq\min\left\{1,(2K)^{-\frac{1}{\delta}}b^{-\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}}b^{-\frac{j}{\delta}}\right\}

for any
j\geq j_{0}
, where
j_{0}
is the smallest
j\in\mathbb{N}
that satisfies
Y_{j}\leq 1
. In particular,
Y_{j}
converges to
0
as
j\rightarrow+\infty
.

Lemma 2.5

(
Embedding
W^{\gamma,q}\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{Nq}{N-\gamma q}}
)
.

Let
q\geq 1
,
\gamma\in(0,1)
such that
\gamma q<N
. Then, for any

Lemma 4.5

(4.14)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},\rho_{i-1})^{p-1}\leq C(N)\left(\frac{R}{\rho_{i-1}}\right)^{N}\mathcal{T}^{p-1}\leq C(N)2^{iN}\mathcal{T}^{p-1}.

4.3

4.16

4.14

4.15

\displaystyle[u]^{q}_{W^{\sigma_{i},q}(B_{\rho_{i}})}

\displaystyle[u]^{q}_{W^{\sigma_{i},q}(B_{\rho_{i}})}\leq\frac{(2i+1)C^{i}_{*}2^{(Nq+N+2q+2)\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}\limits^{i}j\right)}\left(\mathcal{T}+[u]_{W^{\sigma,q}(B_{R})}+1\right)^{q}}{(R-r)^{(N+2q+2)i}}

for any

5.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
p\geq 2
,
s_{p}\in\left(\frac{p-1}{p},1\right)
and let

be a locally bounded weak solution to problem (

) in the sense of Definition

with
\sigma\in\left(\max\left\{\mu^{s_{p}}_{p},0\right\},1\right)
. Then, in the case

\displaystyle u\in W^{\alpha,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)

for any

, where

\displaystyle\beta=\frac{\sigma}{p}+s_{p}.

Moreover, there exists a constant

such that for any ball
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\subset\Omega
with

and any

,

\displaystyle[u]^{p}_{W^{\alpha,p}(B_{r})}\leq\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+[u]_{W^{s_{p},p}(B_{R})}+1\right)^{p}}{(R-r)^{N+2p+2}}.

Proof.

We begin our proof with the following inequality that can be obtained in the proof of Lemma

Proposition 5.4

R\in(0,1)
and any
r\in(0,R)
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{r})}\leq\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}+1\right)^{q}}{(R-r)^{\kappa}}.

Proof.

We prove this claim by an iteration argument. First, we define

\displaystyle q_{i}:=\left(\frac{N}{N-\frac{1}{2}\left(s_{p}p-p+1\right)}\right)^{i}p

and

\displaystyle\rho_{i}:=r+\frac{1}{2^{i}}(R-r)

for
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

For a fixed
i\in\mathbb{N}
, suppose that
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q_{i-1}}(B_{\rho_{i-1}})}<+\infty
, we apply Lemma
5.7
with parameter
(r,R,q,\alpha)
being replaced by
(\rho_{i},\rho_{i-1},q_{i-1},\frac{s_{p}p-p+1}{2q_{i-1}})
to have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{\rho_{i}}}|\nabla u|^{q_{i}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q_{i-1}}{q_{i}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{i}\left(\mathcal{T}_{i-1}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q_{i-1}}(B_{\rho_{i-1}})}+1\right)^{q_{i-1}}}{(\rho_{i-1}-\rho_{i})^{N+2q_{i-1}+2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{i}\left(\mathcal{T}_{i-1}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q_{i-1}}(B_{\rho_{i-1}})}+1\right)^{q_{i-1}}}{(R-r)^{N+2q_{i-1}+2}}2^{i(N+2q+2)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{i}2^{i(Nq_{i}+N+2q+2)}\left(\mathcal{T}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q_{i-1}}(B_{\rho_{i-1}})}+1\right)^{q_{i-1}}}{(R-r)^{N+2q_{i-1}+2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{C}_{i}\left(\mathcal{T}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q_{i-1}}(B_{\rho_{i-1}})}+1\right)^{q_{i-1}}}{(R-r)^{N+2q_{i-1}+2}},

where
\tilde{C}_{i}=C_{i}2^{i(Nq_{i}+N+2q+2)}
. Iterating the above inequality, one has

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{q_{i}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{i}}}\leq\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q_{i-1}}(B_{\rho_{i-1}})}+1\right)}{(R-r)^{\kappa}},

where we set
C=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{i}\tilde{C}_{j}
and
\kappa=(N+2)i+2\sum\limits_{j=0}^{i-1}q_{j}
.

Since
q_{i}\rightarrow+\infty
as
i\rightarrow+\infty
, for any
q\geq p
, there exists
i_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
q_{i_{0}}\geq q
. More precisely, by choosing

\displaystyle i_{0}:=\left[\frac{\ln\frac{q}{p}}{\ln\frac{N}{N-\frac{1}{2}(s_{p}p-p+1)}}\right]+1,

and using Hölder’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\left(\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{q_{i_{0}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{i_{0}}}}\leq\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}+1\right)}{(R-r)^{\kappa_{i_{0}}}}

with
C=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{i_{0}}\tilde{C}_{j}
.
∎

Having the previous lemmas in hand, it suffices to prove the almost Lipschitz regularity of weak solutions.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We set

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\frac{R+r}{2}}:=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{R+r}{2}}(x_{0}))}+\mathrm{Tail}\left(u;x_{0},\frac{R+r}{2}\right).

By combining the following inequalities that come from Lemma
5.8
and Proposition
5.4
respectively,

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{r})}\leq\frac{C_{1}\left(\mathcal{T}_{\frac{R+r}{2}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B_{\frac{R+r}{2}})}+1\right)^{q}}{(R-r)^{\kappa_{1}}}

and

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B_{\frac{R+r}{2}})}\leq\frac{C_{2}\left(\mathcal{T}+[u]_{W^{s_{p},p}(B_{R})}+1\right)^{p}}{(R-r)^{\kappa_{2}}},

one can conclude the claim of Theorem
1.4
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{r})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}\left(\mathcal{T}_{\frac{R+r}{2}}+\frac{C_{2}^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\mathcal{T}+[u]_{W^{s_{p},p}(B_{R})}+1\right)}{(R-r)^{\frac{\kappa_{2}}{p}}}+1\right)^{q}}{(R-r)^{\kappa_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(2^{N}q+3^{q})C_{1}C_{2}^{\frac{q}{p}}\left(\mathcal{T}+[u]_{W^{s_{p},p}(B_{R})}+1\right)^{q}}{(R-r)^{\kappa_{1}+\frac{\kappa_{2}q}{p}}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

A direct application of Lemma
2.8
enables us to prove Corollary
1.6
from Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Corollary
1.6
.

From Theorem
1.4
, we know that the locally bounded weak solution
u\in W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
for any
q\geq p
provide that
p\geq 2
and
s_{p}\in\left(\frac{p-1}{p},1\right)
. Thus, by Morrey’s embedding Lemma
2.8
, we have
u\in C^{0,\gamma}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
.

To be specific, for any fixed
\gamma\in(0,1)
, let us apply Lemma
2.8
with parameter
q:=\frac{N}{1-\gamma}
and Theorem
1.4
to conclude the following estimate

\displaystyle[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{r})}=[u]_{C^{0,1-\frac{N}{q}}(B_{r})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(B_{r})}\leq\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+[u]_{W^{s_{p},p}(B_{R})}+1\right)}{(R-r)^{\kappa}}.

∎

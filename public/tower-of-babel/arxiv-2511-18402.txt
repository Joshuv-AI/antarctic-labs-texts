Bi-Hölder invariants in o-minimal structures
An V. Q. Huynh, Minh B. Nguyen, Nhan X. V. Nguyen, Minh Q. Vu

Abstract
. We prove that for any two definable germs in a polynomially bounded o-minimal structure, there exists a critical threshold α 0 ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha_{0}\in(0,1) such that if these germs are bi- α \alpha -Hölder equivalent for some α ≥ α 0 \alpha\geq\alpha_{0} , then they satisfy the following: ∘ \circ The Lipschitz normal embedding (LNE) property is preserved; that is, if one germ is LNE then so is the other; ∘ \circ Their tangent cones have the same dimension; ∘ \circ The links of their tangent cones have isomorphic homotopy groups. As an application, we show that a complex analytic germ that is bi- α \alpha -Hölder homeomorphic to the germ of a Euclidean space for some α \alpha sufficiently close to 1 1 must be smooth. This provides a slightly stronger version of Sampaio’s smoothness theorem, in which the germs are assumed to be bi- α \alpha -Hölder homeomorphic for every α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in(0,1) .

1.
Introduction

A crucial problem in Singularity Theory is to determine which properties of a singular germ are preserved under various notions of metric equivalence. A central object of study is the tangent cone, which serves as a first-order approximation of the germ at the origin. Numerous fundamental invariants associated with tangent cones have been shown to be preserved under bi-Lipschitz homeomorphisms. For instance, Koike and Paunescu
[
9
]
proved that the dimension of the tangent cone of a subanalytic germ is a bi-Lipschitz invariant. This result was later extended to definable sets in o-minimal structures by Koike, Loi, Paunescu, and Shiota
[
8
]
. A major advance was achieved by Sampaio
[
18
]
, who showed that the tangent cone of a subanalytic germ itself is invariant under bi-Lipschitz homeomorphisms (with respect to the outer metric). In a related direction, Fernandes and Sampaio
[
5
]
established that Lipschitz normal embedding (LNE) in an o-minimal structure is inherited by the tangent cone. More recently, the third author
[
15
]
provided a necessary and sufficient condition for two definable germs to have bi-Lipschitz homeomorphic tangent cones.

Nevertheless, the bi-Lipschitz condition (corresponding to
\alpha=1
in Hölder terminology) is often too restrictive for many purposes. A natural relaxation is to consider bi-
\alpha
-Hölder equivalence for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, which provides metric equivalences interpolating between the topological category and the bi-Lipschitz one.

Recent work reveals that this intermediate has both rigid and flexible aspects. For complex analytic plane curves, Fernandes, Sampaio, and Silva
[
4
]
proved that strongly bi-
\alpha
-Hölder equivalence (being bi-
\alpha
-Hölder homeomorphic for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
) determines the characteristic exponents of each branch as well as their pairwise intersection multiplicities. In the real analytic setting, by contrast, Fernandes et al.
[
3
]
showed that analytic function germs admit moduli under bi-
\alpha
-Hölder right equivalence for any fixed
\alpha\in(0,1)
, highlighting the flexibility.

More recently, Sampaio
[
17
]
established a interesting rigidity phenomenon in the complex category: if a complex analytic germ
(X,0)\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is strongly bi-
\alpha
-Hölder equivalent to the germ
(\mathbb{R}^{k},0)
of a Euclidean plane, then
(X,0)
must be smooth.

Motivated by these developments, we consider the following natural questions, ordered by decreasing difficulty, for definable germs
(X,0)
and
(Y,0)
in an o-minimal structure that are strongly bi-
\alpha
-Hölder equivalent:

(1)

Are
(X,0)
and
(Y,0)
bi-Lipschitz equivalent?

(2)

Are their tangent cones
C_{0}(X)
and
C_{0}(Y)
bi-Lipschitz equivalent?

(3)

Are
C_{0}(X)
and
C_{0}(Y)
topologically equivalent?

(4)

Do
C_{0}(X)
and
C_{0}(Y)
have the same dimension?

(5)

If
(X,0)
is Lipschitz normally embedded, must
(Y,0)
also be Lipschitz normally embedded?

In general, the answer to the above questions is negative. Counterexamples are presented in Section
7
; notably, all such examples live in non-polynomially bounded o-minimal structures. This naturally leads to the question of whether positive answers hold in the polynomially bounded case, where the Łojasiewicz inequality imposes strong control on asymptotic distortion.

Our main results provide affirmative answers to Questions (4) and (5), as well as a partial affirmative answer to Question (3), under the assumption that the underlying o-minimal structure is polynomially bounded. More precisely, we prove that given definable germs
(X,0)
and
(Y,0)
in a polynomially bounded o-minimal structure, there exists a threshold
\alpha_{0}\in(0,1)
(depending only on
X
and
Y
) such that if
(X,0)
and
(Y,0)
are bi-
\alpha
-Hölder equivalent for some
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
, then

(i)

if
(X,0)
is Lipschitz normally embedded, then so is
(Y,0)
(Theorem
3.1
);

(ii)

\dim C_{0}(X)=\dim C_{0}(Y)
(Theorem
4.17
);

(iii)

the links of the tangent cones
C_{0}(X)
and
C_{0}(Y)
have isomorphic homotopy groups  (Theorem
5.5
).

Statement (ii) significantly strengthens the classical bi-Lipschitz dimension invariance result of Koike and Paunescu
[
9
]
by replacing bi-Lipschitz equivalence with bi-
\alpha
-Hölder equivalence for
\alpha
sufficiently close to
1
.

As an immediate application of (i) and (iii), we obtain a simple proof showing that if a complex analytic
germ
(X,0)
is bi-
\alpha
-Hölder equivalent to the germ of a
k
-plane for some
\alpha
close
to
1
, then
(X,0)
must be smooth. This yields a slight strengthening of Sampaio’s smoothness theorem
[
17
]
.

Notation

Throughout this paper we adopt the following notation:

•

B(x,r)
and
S(x,r)
denote the open ball and the sphere of radius
r
centered at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, respectively. When the center is the origin and the radius is
1
, we simply write
S^{n-1}:=S(0,1)
.

•

For a definable set
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\overline{X}
denotes its Euclidean closure. A
closed definable germ at
0
means the germ at
0
of a closed definable set. If
X
is a definable germ at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the
link of
X
at
x_{0}
is the set

{\rm Lk}_{r}(X,x_{0}):=X\cap S(x_{0},r).

for
r
sufficiently small.
The link is well-defined up to definable homeomorphism. When the choice of radius is irrelevant, we write
{\rm Lk}(X,x_{0})
.

•

For a definable set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote its homotopy groups by

\pi_{k}(K)
for
k\geq 0
. Note that
\pi_{0}(K)
is not a group, but simply the set of path-connected components of
K
. When no ambiguity arises, we write
\pi_{*}(K)
for the collection of all homotopy groups. We say that two sets have the same homotopy type if their corresponding homotopy groups are isomorphic.

•

{\rm vol}_{k}
(

) denotes the
k
-dimensional Lebesgue (Hausdorff) measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Note that every definable set is Lebesgue measurable.

•

For functions

defined on a set

, we write

f\lesssim g\quad\text{if}\quad\exists\,C>0\quad\text{such that}\quad f(x)\leq C\,g(x)\quad\forall\,x\in U.

The constant
C
is called
a constant for the relation
\lesssim
. We write

if
f\lesssim g
and

.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Bi-
\alpha
-Hölder equivalence

Definition 2.1

(
\alpha
-Hölder map)
.

Let
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
be metric spaces, and let
\alpha\in(0,1]
. A map
f:X\to Y
is called
\alpha
-Hölder
if there exists a constant
L>0
such that

d_{Y}\big(f(x),f(x^{\prime})\big)\leq L\,d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }x,x^{\prime}\in X.

The constant
L
is referred to as the
Hölder constant
of
f
.

In this paper, we work with subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
equipped with the metric induced by the Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition 2.2

(bi-
\alpha
-Hölder equivalence)
.

Let
0<\alpha\leq 1
. Two germs
(X,x_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
(Y,y_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
are said to be
bi-
\alpha
-Hölder equivalent
(or
bi-
\alpha
-Hölder homeomorphic
) if there exists a homeomorphism
\varphi:(X,x_{0})\to(Y,y_{0})
and a constant
L\geq 1
such that

\frac{1}{L}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{1/\alpha}\leq\|\varphi(x_{1})-\varphi(x_{2})\|\leq L\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{\alpha}

for all
x_{1},x_{2}
sufficiently close to
x_{0}
.

Definition 2.3

(embedded bi-
\alpha
-Hölder equivalence)
.

Let
0<\alpha\leq 1
. Two germs
(X,x_{0})
and
(Y,y_{0})
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are said to be
embedded bi-
\alpha
-Hölder equivalent
(or
embedded bi-
\alpha
-Hölder homeomorphic
) if there exist neighborhoods
U
of
x_{0}
and
V
of
y_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a homeomorphism
\varphi:(U,x_{0})\to(V,y_{0})
and a constant
L\geq 1
such that
\varphi(U\cap X)=V\cap Y
and

\frac{1}{L}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{1/\alpha}\leq\|\varphi(x_{1})-\varphi(x_{2})\|\leq L\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{\alpha}

for all
x_{1},x_{2}
in
U
.

Remark 2.4
.

•

When
\alpha=1
, these notions coincide with the standard bi-Lipschitz equivalences.
For
\alpha<1
, they provide a metric notation lying between topological and bi-Lipschitz equivalence.

•

If two germs
(X,x_{0})
and
(Y,y_{0})
are bi-
\alpha
-Hölder equivalent, then they are automatically bi-
\beta
-Hölder equivalent for every
0<\beta\leq\alpha
.

2.2.
O-minimal structures

In this section, we recall the definition of an o-minimal structure on
(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot)

and summarize some basic results that will be used later.
We refer the reader to
[
2
,
22
,
11
]
for detailed expositions on the theory of o-minimal structures.

Definition 2.5
.

A
structure
on
(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot)
is a sequence

\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{D}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that, for each
n\geq 1
, the following properties hold:

(1)

\mathcal{D}_{n}
is a Boolean algebra of subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
;

(2)

If
A\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
then
A\times\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}\times A
are in
\mathcal{D}_{n+1}
;

(3)

For every polynomial
P\in\mathbb{R}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
, the zero set

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:P(x)=0\}
belongs to
\mathcal{D}_{n}
.

(4)

If
A\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
, then the projection

\pi(A)\in\mathcal{D}_{n-1}
, where
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n-1}

is the projection onto the first
(n-1)
coordinates.

Such a structure
\mathcal{D}
is said to be
o-minimal
if, in addition,

(5)

Every set
A\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
is a finite union of points and open intervals.

A set in
\mathcal{D}_{n}
for some
n
is called
definable
set. A map
f:A\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is called definable if its graph is a definable set.

Definition 2.6
.

An o-minimal structure
\mathcal{D}
is said to be
polynomially bounded

if every definable function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies

|f(x)|\leq x^{N}
for some integer
N
and all sufficiently large
x
.

It has been known by Miller’s Dichotomy Theorem
[
14
]
, every o-minimal structure on
(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot)
is either polynomially bounded or exponential (i.e., it contains the graph of
\exp(x)
). The class of semialgebraic sets (
\mathbb{R}_{\rm{alg}}
) and the class of globally subanalytic sets (
\mathbb{R}_{\rm{an}}
) are typical examples of polynomially bounded o-minimal structures. On the other hand, the structure generated by the exponential function (
\mathbb{R}_{\rm exp})
(see
[
23
]
) or the one generated by the exponential function and restricted analytic functions (
\mathbb{R}_{\rm{an,exp}}
) are examples of non-polynomially bounded o-minimal structures (see
[
21
]
)

Definition 2.7
.

Let
p:X\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a continuous definable map, where
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is definable and
n\geq k
.
We say that
p
is
definably trivial over
a definable set
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}

if there exist a definable set
F
and a definable homeomorphism

h:p^{-1}(Y)\longrightarrow F\times Y

such that
p_{1}\circ h=p|_{p^{-1}(Y)}
, where
p_{1}:F\times Y\to Y
is the projection onto the second factor.
If
Z\subset X
is a definable subset, we say that
h
is
compatible with

if there exists a definable subset
G\subset F
such that

h(Z\cap p^{-1}(Y))=G\times Y
. The homeomorphism

is called a

of

over
Y
.

Theorem 2.8

(Hardt’s Triviality Theorem
[
2
]
)
.

Let
p:X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a continuous definable map,

Then there exists a finite definable partition

of

Observation 2.9
.

(1)

For any

, the fibers

and

are definably homeomorphic.
Moreover, one may take the fiber

in the trivialization to be

for any fixed

.

(2)

If
k=1
and

, then for every
\varepsilon_{1}<\varepsilon_{2}
in
(a,b)
,
the trivialization

induces strong deformation retracts from

onto

for every

and from

for every

. To see this, begin with the strong deformation retract

\Phi:[0,1]\times[c,\varepsilon_{2}]\to[c,\varepsilon_{2}]
from

onto

defined by

\Phi_{s}(t)=\Phi(s,t):=\begin{cases}t,&\text{if }t\leq\varepsilon_{1},\\[4.0pt]
(1-s)t+s\varepsilon_{1},&\text{if }t>\varepsilon_{1}.\end{cases}

Then the map

H:[0,1]\times\rho^{-1}([c,\varepsilon_{2}])\to\rho^{-1}([c,\varepsilon_{2}])

defined by

H(s,x,t):=\begin{cases}(x,t),&\text{if }t\leq\varepsilon_{1},\\[4.0pt]
h^{-1}(h(x,t),\,\Phi_{s}(t)),&\text{if }t>\varepsilon_{1},\end{cases}

is a strong deformation retract from

onto

\rho^{-1}([c,\varepsilon_{1}])
.

By similar arguments, one can construct a strong deformation retract from
p^{-1}([\varepsilon_{1},d])
onto
p^{-1}([\varepsilon_{2},d])
for every

.

In the sequel, we assume that the underlying o-minimal structure is polynomially bounded. We will need the following results.

Theorem 2.10

(Łojasiewicz inequality
[
2
]
)
.

Let
f,g:U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous definable functions on a compact definable set
U
. Suppose that

\{x\in U:g(x)=0\}\subset\{x\in U:f(x)=0\}.

Then, there exist constants
C>0
and
\theta>0
such that

|g(x)|\geq C|f(x)|^{\theta}\quad\text{for all }x\in U.

The following result is due to Valette
[
20
]
(see also
[
19
]
).
Although the original statement was proved in the subanalytic setting, the same argument applies to any polynomially bounded o-minimal structure.

Theorem 2.11

(Lipschitz conic structure
[
20
]
)
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a definable set and
x_{0}\in X
.
For
\varepsilon>0
small enough, there exists a Lipschitz definable homeomorphism

H:x_{0}*(S(x_{0},\varepsilon)\cap X)\longrightarrow\overline{B}(x_{0},\varepsilon)\cap X,

satisfying
H|_{S(x_{0},\varepsilon)\cap X}=\mathrm{Id}
, preserving the distance to

,
and having the following metric properties:

(1)

The natural retraction by deformation onto
x_{0}

r:[0,1]\times\overline{B}(x_{0},\varepsilon)\cap X\longrightarrow\overline{B}(x_{0},\varepsilon)\cap X,

defined by

r(s,x):=H\big(sH^{-1}(x)+(1-s)x_{0}\big),

which is Lipschitz. Indeed, there exists a constant
C
such that for every fixed

s\in[0,1]
, the mapping
r_{s}
defined by

is
Cs
-Lipschitz.

(2)

For each
\delta>0
, the restriction of

to

\{x\in X:\delta\leq\|x-x_{0}\|\leq\varepsilon\}

3.
Lipschitz normal embeddings

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a connected definable set.
For points
x,y\in X
, the
outer distance
is defined by

d_{\mathrm{out}}(x,y)=\|x-y\|.

The
inner distance
between
x
and
y
is given by

d_{\mathrm{in}}(x,y)=\inf_{\gamma}{\rm length}(\gamma),

where the infimum is taken over all rectifiable curves
\gamma\subset X
joining
x
and
y
.

We say that
X
is
Lipschitz normally embedded
(or LNE for short) if there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that

d_{\mathrm{in}}(x,y)\leq C\,d_{\mathrm{out}}(x,y)

for all
x,y
in
X
. Intuitively, this means that the intrinsic metric of
X
and the ambient Euclidean metric are equivalent.

Let
x_{0}\in\overline{X}
. The set
X
is said to be LNE at a point
x_{0}\in\overline{X}
(or the germ
(X,x_{0})
is LNE) if there is a small neighborhood
U
of
x_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
U\cap X
is LNE.

It is still unknown whether the inner metric
d_{\mathrm{in}}
is itself definable.
However, Kurdyka and Orro
[
10
]
proved that there exists a definable metric on
X
, denoted by
\tilde{d}
, which is equivalent to
d_{\mathrm{in}}
.
This allows one to verify the LNE condition for
X
using
\tilde{d}
instead of
d_{\mathrm{in}}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(X,0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
(Y,0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be definable set-germs. Suppose
(X,0)
is LNE. There exists
\alpha_{0}\in(0,1)
such that if
(X,0)
and
(Y,0)
are bi-
\alpha
-Hölder equivalent for some
\alpha\in[\alpha_{0},1]
then
(Y,0)
is also LNE.

Proof.

We will prove by contradiction that if
Y
is not LNE then it cannot be bi-
\alpha
-Hölder to
(X,0)
when
\alpha
is sufficiently close to
1
.

Indeed, suppose
(Y,0)
is not LNE,
by the Łojasiewicz inequality (see Theorem
2.10
), there exist constants
C>0
and
0<\beta<1
such that

(1)

\tilde{d}(x,y)\geq C\,\|x-y\|^{\beta}

for all
x,y\in Y
sufficiently close to the origin.

Let
\varphi:(X,0)\to(Y,0)
be an bi-
\alpha
-Hölder homeomorphism.
For points
x,y\in Y
near
0
, set
x^{\prime}:=\varphi^{-1}(x)
and
y^{\prime}:=\varphi^{-1}(y)
. Let
\gamma_{x^{\prime}y^{\prime}}
be a curve in
X
joining
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}
such that

{\rm length}(\gamma_{x^{\prime}y^{\prime}})\sim\tilde{d}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}).

Note that
\varphi(\gamma_{x^{\prime}y^{\prime}})
is a curve in
Y
joining
x
and
y
. Then,

(2)

\tilde{d}(x,y)\lesssim{\rm length}(\varphi(\gamma_{x^{\prime}y^{\prime}}))\lesssim\big({\rm length}(\gamma_{x^{\prime}y^{\prime}})\big)^{\alpha}\sim\tilde{d}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})^{\alpha}.

Since
(X,0)
is LNE,

(3)

\tilde{d}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})^{\alpha}\lesssim\|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\|^{\alpha}\lesssim\|x-y\|^{\alpha^{2}}.

Combining (
2
) and (
3
) gives

(4)

\tilde{d}(x,y)\lesssim\|x-y\|^{\alpha^{2}}.

When
\alpha
is close enough to
1
we have
\beta<\alpha^{2}<1
, inequality (
4
) then contradicts (
1
). The proof is complete.
∎

4.
Dimension of tangent cones

In this section, we prove that the dimension of the tangent cones of given definable germs must be the same if they are bi-
\alpha
-Hölder homeomorphic with when
\alpha
sufficiently close to
1
. The proof is inspired by the arguments of Koike and Paunescu
[
9
]
in the bi-Lipschitz case.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a definable set.

•

The
dimension of
X
is defined by

\dim X=\sup\{\dim S:S\text{ is a }C^{1}\text{ submanifold of }\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{ contained in }X\}.

•

Let
x_{0}\in\overline{X}
. The dimension of the germ
(X,x_{0})
, denoted by
\dim(X,x_{0})
, is the dimension of
X\cap B(x_{0},r)
with
r
sufficiently small.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a set and let
x_{0}\in\overline{X}
. The
tangent cone of
X
at
x_{0}
is defined as

C_{x_{0}}(X):=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\exists(x_{k})\subset X,(x_{k})\to x_{0},\exists(\lambda_{k})\subset\mathbb{R}_{+},\lambda_{k}x_{k}\to v\}.

It is known that if
X
is definable then
C_{x_{0}}X
is also definable, and moreover
\dim C_{x_{0}}(X)\leq\dim(X,x_{0})
.

Definition 4.3

(Cone with vertex at the origin)
.

A subset
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called a
cone with vertex at the origin

(or simply a
cone at
0
) if for every
x\in A
and every
t\geq 0
, we have

tx\in A
.

Definition 4.4

(Sea-tangle neighborhood)
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0\in\overline{X}
, and let
d,C>0
.

•

The
sea-tangle neighborhood

ST_{d}(X,C)
of
X
of degree
d
and width
C
is defined by:

ST_{d}(X,C):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:{\rm dist}(x,X)\leq C|x|^{d}\}.

If

Definition 4.5

(ST equivalence)
.

Let
X,Y
be subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
0\in\overline{X}\cap\overline{Y}
. We say that
X
and
Y
are ST-equivalent, if there are
d_{1},d_{2}>1
,
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that
Y\subset ST_{d_{1}}(X,C_{1})
and
X\subset ST_{d_{2}}(Y,C_{2})
as germs at
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We write
X\sim_{ST}Y
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a germ at
0
. Let
\varphi:(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
be a bi-
\alpha
-Hölder homeomorphism. For
d>1
,
C>0
, there exists
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

ST_{d/\alpha^{2}}(\varphi(X),C_{1})\subset\varphi(ST_{d}(X,C))\subset ST_{d\alpha^{2}}(\varphi(X),C_{2}).

Proof.

Since
\varphi
is a bi-
\alpha
-Hölder homeomorphism, there exists a constant
K>0
such that

\frac{1}{K}\|x\|^{1/\alpha}\leq\|\varphi(x)\|\leq K\|x\|^{\alpha}.

Let
x\in ST_{d}(X,C)
. Then there exists
y\in X
such that

\|x-y\|\leq C\|x\|^{d}.

Applying the homeomorphism
\varphi
, we obtain

{\rm dist}(\varphi(x),\varphi(X))\leq\|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)\|\leq K\|x-y\|^{\alpha}\leq KC^{\alpha}\|x\|^{\alpha d}\leq K^{d\alpha^{2}+1}C^{\alpha}\|\varphi(x)\|^{\alpha^{2}d}.

Set
C_{2}:=K^{d\alpha^{2}+1}C^{\alpha}
. Then

\varphi(x)\in ST_{\alpha^{2}d}(\varphi(X),C_{2}),

that is,

\varphi(ST_{d}(X,C))\subset ST_{\alpha^{2}d}(\varphi(X),C_{2}).

Similarly, one can show that there is
C_{1}>0
such that

ST_{d/\alpha^{2}}(\varphi(X),C_{1})\subset\varphi(ST_{d}(X,C)).

∎

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a definable cone at
0
.
Then, for every
d>1
and
C>0
there exists
r_{0}>0
such that for all
r\in(0,r_{0}]
,

{\rm vol}_{n}\big(ST_{d}(A,C)\cap B(0,r)\big)\sim\ r^{n+(d-1)(n-a)}.

where
a=\dim A
and the constant for ”
\sim
” is independent of
r
.

Proof.

Write points in polar form
x=\rho s
with
\rho=\|x\|\in(0,\infty)
and
s\in S^{n-1}
.
By the polar coarea formula,

{\rm vol}_{n}\big(ST_{d}(A,C)\cap B(0,r)\big)=\int_{0}^{r}\rho^{\,n-1}\ {\rm vol}_{n-1}\big(E_{\rho}\big)\,d\rho,

where

E_{\rho}:=\{\,s\in S^{n-1}:\ \rho s\in ST_{d}(A,C)\}.

.

Let
S_{A}:=A\cap S^{n-1}
. Since
A
is a cone, for all
\rho>0
and
s\in S^{n-1}
,

\operatorname{dist}(\rho s,A)=\rho\,\operatorname{dist}(s,A).

Hence

\rho s\in ST_{d}(A,C)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\operatorname{dist}(s,A)\leq C\,\rho^{\,d-1}.

Thus,

E_{\rho}=\{s\in S^{n-1}:{\rm dist}(s,A)\leq C\rho^{d-1}\}.

Let us denote
d_{S^{n-1}}(s,S_{A})
the spherical distance to
S_{A}
.
On
S^{n-1}
, in a small neighborhood
U
of
S_{A}
, the functions
{\rm dist}(s,A)\sim d_{S^{n-1}}(s,S_{A})
.
Therefore, there exist constants
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}>0
and
\rho_{0}>0
such that, for all
0<\rho\leq\rho_{0}
,

\{\,s\in S^{n-1}:\ d_{S^{n-1}}(s,S_{A})\leq\kappa_{1}\,\rho^{\,d-1}\,\}\ \subset\ E_{\rho}\ \subset\ \{\,s\in S^{n-1}:\ d_{S^{n-1}}(s,S_{A})\leq\kappa_{2}\,\rho^{\,d-1}\,\}.

We now estimate the measure of tubular neighborhoods of
S_{A}
in
S^{n-1}
.
The set
S_{A}
is a definable subset of
S^{n-1}
of dimension
\dim S_{A}=a-1
.
We can stratify
S_{A}
into finitely many
C^{1}
embedded manifolds called strata; the strata of top dimension
a-1
dominate the measure of small neighborhoods, while lower-dimensional strata contribute higher-order terms. Thus, there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2},\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that, for all
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
,

c_{1}\,\varepsilon^{\,n-a}\ \leq\ {\rm vol}_{n-1}\big(\{\,s\in S^{n-1}:\ d_{S^{n-1}}(s,S_{A})\leq\varepsilon\,\}\big)\leq c_{2}\,\varepsilon^{\,n-a}.

Combining
(\ast\ast)
with
(\dagger)
and substituting
\varepsilon=\kappa_{i}\,\rho^{\,d-1}
gives, for
0<\rho\leq\rho_{0}
,

{\rm vol}_{n-1}(E_{\rho})\ \sim\ \rho^{\,(d-1)(n-a)}.

Hence, for
0<r\leq r_{0}:=\rho_{0}
,

{\rm vol}_{n}\big(ST_{d}(A,C)\cap B(0,r)\big)=\int_{0}^{r}\rho^{\,n-1}\,{\rm vol}_{n-1}(E_{\rho})\,d\rho\sim\int_{0}^{r}\rho^{\,n-1+(d-1)(n-a)}\,d\rho\sim\ r^{\,n+(d-1)(n-a)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a definable germ at
0
with
\dim(X,0)=k
. There is
r_{0}>0
such that for all
0<r<\rho\leq r_{0}
, we have

{\rm vol}_{k}\big(X\cap B(0,r)\big)\sim\bigg(\frac{\rho}{r}\bigg)^{k}{\rm vol}_{k}\big(X\cap B(0,r)\big),

where the constant for ”
\sim
” is independent of
r
and
\rho
.

Proof.

Take
r_{0}>0
sufficiently small such that Theorem
2.11
holds for all
\varepsilon<r_{0}
(we may replace
X
by
X\cup\{0\}
if necessary).
Let
\rho=\varepsilon<r_{0}
and
let

r(s,x):=H\big(sH^{-1}(x)+(1-s)x_{0}\big),

be the retraction defined in Theorem
2.11
.
Since
x_{0}=0
, we have

r_{s}(x)=H(s\cdot H^{-1}(x)),

and hence the inverse map of
r_{s}
is

r_{s}^{-1}(y)=H\big((1/s)\,H^{-1}(y)\big)\qquad\text{for }y\in X\cap B(0,s\rho).

For
y_{1},y_{2}\in X\cap B(0,s\rho)
, we have

\displaystyle|r_{s}^{-1}(y_{1})-r_{s}^{-1}(y_{2})|

\displaystyle=\big|H\big((1/s)\,H^{-1}(y_{1})\big)-H\big((1/s)\,H^{-1}(y_{2})\big)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Lip}(H)\cdot\big|(1/s)\,H^{-1}(y_{1})-(1/s)\,H^{-1}(y_{2})\big|

\displaystyle=\frac{\operatorname{Lip}(H)}{s}\,\big|H^{-1}(y_{1})-H^{-1}(y_{2})\big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\operatorname{Lip}(H)\operatorname{Lip}(H^{-1})}{s}\,|y_{1}-y_{2}|=\frac{C}{s}|y_{1}-y_{2}|.

where
C={\rm Lip}(H){\rm Lip}(H^{-1})
which is independent of
s
. Here,
{\rm Lip}(H)
and
{\rm Lip}(H^{-1})
denote the Lipschitz constants of
H
and
H^{-1}
respectively.
This shows that the Lipschitz constant of
r_{s}^{-1}
is
\lesssim\frac{1}{s}
.

With
s=r/\rho
we have
r_{s}\big(X\cap B(0,\rho)\big)=X\cap B(0,r)
. Equivalently,

r_{s}^{-1}\big(X\cap B(0,r)\big)=X\cap B(0,\rho).

Since
r_{s}^{-1}
is Lipschitz map with constant
\lesssim 1/s
, this gives

\displaystyle{\rm vol}_{k}\bigg(r_{s}^{-1}\big(X\cap B(0,r)\big)\bigg)\lesssim\bigg(\frac{1}{s}\bigg)^{k}{\rm vol}_{k}\big(X\cap B(0,r)\big)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle{\rm vol}_{k}\bigg(X\cap B(0,\rho)\bigg)\lesssim\bigg(\frac{\rho}{r}\bigg)^{k}{\rm vol}_{k}\big(X\cap B(0,r)\big).

∎

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a definable germ at
0
with
\dim(X,0)=n
.
Let

h:(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)

be a bi-
\alpha
-Hölder homeomorphism with
\alpha\in(0,1]
.
Then, there exists a constant
r_{0}>0
such that for every
0<r<r_{0}
,

{\rm vol}_{n}\big(h(X)\cap B(0,r)\big)\lesssim r^{(\frac{1}{\alpha}+1)(\alpha-1)n}{\rm vol}_{n}\big(X\cap B(0,r)\big),

where the constant for ”
\lesssim
” is independent of
r
.

Proof.

Let
L
be the Hölder constant of
h
.
For
r>0
small define

\rho=\rho(r):=\sup\{\,|x|:\ |h(x)|\leq r\,\}.

Then,
h^{-1}\big(B(0,r)\big)\subset B(0,\rho)
. Put

E:=X\cap B(0,\rho).

Fix a sufficiently small
r
and put
D:={\rm diam}(E)>0
where
{\rm diam}(E)
denotes the diameter of
E
. Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. By outer regularity of Lebesgue measure and compactness of
\overline{E}
, there exists a finite family of closed axis-parallel cubes
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
of common side length
t
with

0<t\leq D,\qquad E\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}Q_{i},\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\rm vol}_{n}(Q_{i})\leq{\rm vol}_{n}(E)+\varepsilon.

Each cube
Q_{i}
has side
t
and therefore
{\rm diam}(Q_{i})=t\sqrt{n}
. Denote

\omega_{n}:={\rm vol}_{n}\big(B(0,1)\big)

the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By the Hölder bound,

{\rm diam}\big(h(Q_{i})\big)\leq L\big({\rm diam}Q_{i}\big)^{\alpha}=L(\sqrt{n}\,t)^{\alpha}.

By Jung’s theorem,
h(Q_{i})
is contained in a ball of radius
\sqrt{1/2}{\rm diam}(h(Q_{i}))
,

{\rm vol}_{n}\big(h(Q_{i})\big)\leq\omega_{n}\Big(\frac{{\rm diam}(h(Q_{i}))}{2}\Big)^{n}\leq\omega_{n}2^{-n/2}L^{n}n^{\frac{\alpha n}{2}}t^{\alpha n}.

Set the constant

C_{1}:=\omega_{n}2^{-n}L^{n}n^{\frac{\alpha n}{2}}.

Summing over
i
gives

{\rm vol}_{n}\big(h(E)\big)\;\leq\;\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\rm vol}_{n}\!\big(h(Q_{i})\big)\;\leq\;C_{1}\,N\,t^{\alpha n}.

From the measure bound for the cubes we have

Nt^{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\rm vol}_{n}(Q_{i})\leq{\rm vol}_{n}(E)+\varepsilon.

Hence

Nt^{\alpha n}=(Nt^{n})\,t^{(\alpha-1)n}\leq({\rm vol}_{n}(E)+\varepsilon)\,t^{(\alpha-1)n}.

Combining with the previous estimate yields

{\rm vol}_{n}\big(h(E)\big)\leq C_{1}({\rm vol}_{n}(E)+\varepsilon)\,t^{(\alpha-1)n}.

Since
t\leq D
, we have

{\rm vol}_{n}\big(h(E)\big)\leq C_{1}({\rm vol}_{n}(E)+\varepsilon)\,D^{(\alpha-1)n}.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
we obtain

{\rm vol}_{n}\big(h(E)\big)\leq C_{1}\,D^{(\alpha-1)n}\,{\rm vol}_{n}(E).

Since
D\leq 2\rho
, take
C^{\prime}:=2^{(\alpha-1)n}C_{1}
and obtain

(5)

{\rm vol}_{n}\big(h(X)\cap B(0,r)\big)\leq{\rm vol}_{n}(h(E))\leq C^{\prime}\,\rho^{(\alpha-1)n}{\rm vol}_{n}\big(X\cap B(0,\rho)\big).

To finish the proof, we have to compare
{\rm vol}_{n}\big(X\cap B(0,\rho)\big)
and
{\rm vol}_{n}\big(X\cap B(0,r)\big)
. Note from the definition that
(1/L)r^{1/\alpha}\leq\rho\leq Lr^{\alpha}
. Since
\alpha-1<0
, the left-hand-side inequality implies that

(6)

\rho^{(\alpha-1)n}\lesssim r^{\frac{1}{\alpha}(\alpha-1)n}.

and the right-hand-side one gives

(7)

\frac{\rho}{r}\leq Lr^{\alpha-1}

Case 1:

r\geq\rho
. It is clear that
{\rm vol}_{n}\big(X\cap B(0,\rho)\big)\leq{\rm vol}_{n}\big(X\cap B(0,r)\big)
. Hence,

\rho^{(\alpha-1)n}{\rm vol}_{n}\big(X\cap B(0,\rho)\big)\lesssim r^{\frac{1}{\alpha}(\alpha-1)n}{\rm vol}_{n}\big(X\cap B(0,r)\big).

Combining with (
5
) we get

{\rm vol}_{n}\big(h(X)\cap B(0,r)\big)\lesssim r^{\frac{(\alpha-1)n}{\alpha}}{\rm vol}_{n}\big(X\cap B(0,r)\big)\lesssim r^{(\frac{1}{\alpha}+1)(\alpha-1)n}{\rm vol}_{n}\big(X\cap B(0,r)\big).

Case 2:
r<\rho
. By Lemma
4.11
and (
7
),

{\rm vol}_{n}\big(X\cap B(0,\rho)\big)\lesssim\bigg(\frac{\rho}{r}\bigg)^{n}{\rm vol}_{n}\big(X\cap B(0,r)\big)\lesssim r^{(\alpha-1)n}{\rm vol}_{n}\big(X\cap B(0,r)\big).

Combining with (
5
) and (
6
) we get

{\rm vol}_{n}\big(h(X)\cap B(0,r)\big)\lesssim r^{(\frac{1}{\alpha}+1)(\alpha-1)n}{\rm vol}_{n}\big(X\cap B(0,r)\big).

∎

Lemma 4.13
.

be a definable germ at
0
. Then, there is

such that for any bi-
\alpha
-Hölder homeomorphism
\varphi:(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
with

we have

\varphi(X)\sim_{ST}\varphi(C_{0}(X)).

In particular,

C_{0}(\varphi(X))=C_{0}(\varphi(C_{0}(X)).

Since
X
is definable, by Lemma
4.7
,
X
and
C_{0}(X)
is ST-equivalent. That is, there are

and

such that

and

, we get

\varphi(X)\subset\varphi\bigg(ST_{d_{1}}(C_{0}(X),C_{1})\bigg)\subset ST_{d\alpha^{2}}(\varphi(C_{0}(X)),C_{1}^{\prime}),

\varphi(C_{0}(X))\subset\varphi\bigg(ST_{d_{1}}(X,C_{2})\bigg)\subset ST_{d\alpha^{2}}(\varphi(X),C_{2}^{\prime})

.
Choosing
\alpha_{0}
close to

such that

, we have

for all

. It is obvious that

and

4.8
,

. The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a definable cone at
0
. Then, there exists
0<\alpha_{0}<1
such that
if
\varphi:(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
is a bi-
\alpha
-Hölder homeomorphism for some
\alpha_{0}\leq\alpha\leq 1
such that

(i)

F:=\varphi(E)
and
C_{0}(F)
are ST-equivalent,

(ii)

C_{0}(F)
is definable,

then

\dim C_{0}(F)\leq\dim E.

Proof.

Since
C_{0}(F)
and
F
are germs at
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and are ST-equivalent, by Lemma
4.9
, there exist
d_{1}>0
such that

(8)

{\rm vol}_{n}\bigg(ST_{d}(F,C_{1})\cap B(0,r)\bigg)\sim{\rm vol}_{n}\bigg(ST_{d}(C_{0}(F),C_{2})\cap B(0,r)\bigg).

for any
C_{1},C_{2}>0
and any
1<d<d_{1}
and
r
sufficiently small. We now fix
d<d_{1}
. Choose
0<\alpha_{0}<1
such that
d\alpha_{0}^{2}>1
. This implies that for all
\alpha_{0}\leq\alpha\leq 1
we have
d\alpha^{2}>1
.

From Lemma
4.6
, given
C_{3}>0
and
d>1
, there are
C_{4},C_{5}>0
such that

(9)

ST_{d/\alpha^{2}}(F,C_{4})\subset\varphi(ST_{d}(E,C_{3}))\subset ST_{d\alpha^{2}}(F,C_{5}).

By Lemma
4.12
, there exists
r_{0}>0
such that for all
0<r\leq r_{0}
,

(10)

{\rm vol}_{n}\bigg(\varphi(ST_{d\alpha^{2}}(E,C_{5}))\cap B(0,r)\bigg)\lesssim r^{(\frac{1}{\alpha}+1)(\alpha-1)n}{\rm vol}_{n}\bigg(ST_{d\alpha^{2}}(E,C_{5})\cap B(0,r)\bigg).

Again by Lemma
4.6
, there is a constant
C_{6}>0
such that

ST_{d}(F,C_{6})\subset\varphi(ST_{d\alpha^{2}}(E,C_{5})).

It follows that

{\rm vol}_{n}(ST_{d}(F,C_{6})\cap B(0,r))\leq{\rm vol}_{n}(\varphi(ST_{d\alpha^{2}}(E,C_{5}))\cap B(0,r)).

Then, by Lemma
4.10
,

{\rm vol}_{n}(ST_{d\alpha^{2}}(E,C_{5})\cap B(0,r))\sim r^{(\alpha^{2}-1)d(n-a)}{\rm vol}_{n}(ST_{d}(E,C_{5})\cap B(0,r))

where

.
Combining these estimates gives

{\rm vol}_{n}(ST_{d}(F,C_{6})\cap B(0,r))\lesssim r^{K}{\rm vol}_{n}(ST_{d}(E,C_{5})\cap B(0,r)),

K=K(\alpha):=(\frac{1}{\alpha}+1)(\alpha-1)n+(\alpha^{2}-1)d(n-a).

Note that
K<0
and
\lim_{\alpha\to 1}K=0
. Taking
\alpha_{0}
bigger, we may assume that
K+(d-1)>0
for all
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}

Observe that

(11)

1\sim\frac{{\rm vol}_{n}(ST_{d}(F,C_{6})\cap B(0,r))}{{\rm vol}_{n}(ST_{d}(C_{0}(F),C_{6})\cap B(0,r))}\lesssim r^{K}\frac{{\rm vol}_{n}(ST_{d}(E,C_{5})\cap B(0,r))}{{\rm vol}_{n}(ST_{d}(C_{0}(F),C_{6})\cap B(0,r))}.

Now assume on the contradiction that

. By Lemma
4.10
,

\frac{{\rm vol}_{n}(ST_{d}(E,C_{3})\cap B(0,r))}{{\rm vol}_{n}(ST_{d}(C_{0}(F),C_{2})\cap B(0,r))}\sim r^{(d-1)(\dim C_{0}(F)-\dim E)}.

It then follows from (

) that

(12)

1\lesssim r^{K+(d-1)(\dim C_{0}(F)-\dim E)}

Since
\dim C_{0}(F)-\dim E\geq 1
and

,
K+(d-1)(\dim C_{0}(F)-\dim E)>0
. The right hand side of (
12
) tends to

∎

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
X,Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be definable germs at
0
.
Then, there exists
\alpha_{0}\in(0,1)
such that if there is a bi-
\alpha
-Hölder homeomorphism

\varphi:(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
with
\varphi(X)=Y

for some
\alpha\in[\alpha_{0},1]
, then

\dim C_{0}(X)=\dim C_{0}(Y).

Proof.

We first show that

\dim C_{0}(Y)\leq\dim C_{0}(X).

Set
E:=C_{0}(X)
and
F:=\varphi(E)
.
We will verify that the assumptions of Lemma
4.14
are satisfied.
Since
\varphi(X)
is definable, by Lemma
4.13
, there exists
\alpha_{1}
such that if
\alpha\in[\alpha_{1},1]
then

C_{0}(\varphi(X))=C_{0}(\varphi(C_{0}(X)))=C_{0}(F).

Because
X
is definable, it follows from the proof of Lemma
4.13
that

\varphi(X)\sim_{ST}\varphi(C_{0}(Y))=F
.
Moreover, since
\varphi(X)
is definable, Lemma
4.7
yields

\varphi(X)\sim_{ST}C_{0}(\varphi(X))=C_{0}(F)
.
By the transitivity of the ST-equivalence relation, we obtain
F\sim_{ST}C_{0}(F)
.
Applying Lemma
4.14
, we deduce that

\dim C_{0}(Y)=\dim C_{0}(\varphi(X))=\dim C_{0}(F)\leq\dim E=\dim C_{0}(X).

Repeating the same argument for
\varphi^{-1}
, there exists
\alpha_{2}\in(0,1)
such that if
\alpha\in[\alpha_{2},1]
then

\dim C_{0}(Y)\geq\dim C_{0}(X).

Finally, setting
\alpha_{0}:=\max\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}
, we conclude that if
\alpha\in[\alpha_{0},1]
then

\dim C_{0}(X)=\dim C_{0}(Y).

∎

Lemma 4.16
.

Let
X
and
Y
be germ at
0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume there exists a bi-
\alpha
-Hölder homeomorphism
h:(X,0)\to(Y,0)
with
0<\alpha\leq 1
.
Then there exists a bi-
\alpha^{2}
-Hölder homeomorphism

\Phi:(\mathbb{R}^{2n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{2n},0)\quad\text{such that}\quad\Phi\bigl(X\times\{0\}\bigr)=\{0\}\times Y.

Write
h=(h_{1},\dots,h_{n})
and
h^{-1}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})
.
There exist
C,H>0
such that for all
x,x^{\prime}\in X
and
y,y^{\prime}\in Y
,

\|h(x)-h(x^{\prime})\|\leq C\,\|x-x^{\prime}\|^{\alpha},\qquad\|h^{-1}(y)-h^{-1}(y^{\prime})\|\leq H\,\|y-y^{\prime}\|^{\alpha}.

By the McShane–Whitney extension for Hölder functions (see for example
[
12
]
), each coordinate function

h_{i}:X\to\mathbb{R}
admits an
\alpha
-Hölder extension
\widetilde{h}_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
;
and similarly each
k_{j}:Y\to\mathbb{R}
extends to

\widetilde{k}_{j}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
.
Set
\widetilde{h}=(\widetilde{h}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{h}_{n}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

\widetilde{k}=(\widetilde{k}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{k}_{n}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then
\widetilde{h}|_{X}=h
and
\widetilde{k}|_{Y}=h^{-1}
, and there are constants

C^{\prime},H^{\prime}>0
such that

\|\widetilde{h}(u)-\widetilde{h}(u^{\prime})\|\leq C^{\prime}\|u-u^{\prime}\|^{\alpha},\qquad\|\widetilde{k}(v)-\widetilde{k}(v^{\prime})\|\leq H^{\prime}\|v-v^{\prime}\|^{\alpha}\quad(u,u^{\prime},v,v^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Define
\Phi,\Psi:(\mathbb{R}^{2n},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{2n},0)
by

\Phi(u,v):=\bigl(u-\widetilde{k}\bigl(v+\widetilde{h}(u)\bigr),\ v+\widetilde{h}(u)\bigr),\qquad\Psi(z,w):=\bigl(z+\widetilde{k}(w),\ w-\widetilde{h}\bigl(z+\widetilde{k}(w)\bigr)\bigr).

Claim 1:
\Psi=\Phi^{-1}
.

For any
(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
near the orgin, we have

\displaystyle\Psi\bigl(\Phi(u,v)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\Psi\bigl(u-\widetilde{k}(v+\widetilde{h}(u)),\,v+\widetilde{h}(u)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\Bigl(u-\widetilde{k}(v+\widetilde{h}(u))+\widetilde{k}\bigl(v+\widetilde{h}(u)\bigr),\ \ (v+\widetilde{h}(u))-\widetilde{h}\bigl(u-\widetilde{k}(v+\widetilde{h}(u))+\widetilde{k}(v+\widetilde{h}(u))\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=(u,\ v+\widetilde{h}(u)-\widetilde{h}(u))=(u,v).

Similarly, for any
(z,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
near the origin,

\displaystyle\Phi\bigl(\Psi(z,w)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\Phi\bigl(z+\widetilde{k}(w),\,w-\widetilde{h}(z+\widetilde{k}(w))\bigr)

\displaystyle=\Bigl(z+\widetilde{k}(w)-\widetilde{k}\bigl(w-\widetilde{h}(z+\widetilde{k}(w))+\widetilde{h}(z+\widetilde{k}(w))\bigr),\ \ w-\widetilde{h}(z+\widetilde{k}(w))+\widetilde{h}(z+\widetilde{k}(w))\Bigr)

\displaystyle=(z+\widetilde{k}(w)-\widetilde{k}(w),\ w)=(z,w).

Thus
\Psi=\Phi^{-1}
and
\Phi
is a homeomorphism.

Claim 2:

\Phi
and
\Psi
are bi-
\alpha^{2}
-Hölder.

Fix
(u,v),(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
near the origin, write
\Phi=(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Using the
\alpha
-Hölder property of

\widetilde{h}
and
\widetilde{k}
,

\displaystyle\bigl\|\,\Phi_{2}(u,v)-\Phi_{2}(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\,\bigr\|

\displaystyle=\bigl\|\,v+\widetilde{h}(u)-v^{\prime}-\widetilde{h}(u^{\prime})\,\bigr\|

\displaystyle\leq\ \|v-v^{\prime}\|+C^{\prime}\|u-u^{\prime}\|^{\alpha},\lesssim\|(u,v)-(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\|^{\alpha^{2}}\text{ since }\alpha\in(0,1];

and

\displaystyle\bigl\|\,\Phi_{1}(u,v)-\Phi_{1}(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\,\bigr\|

\displaystyle=\bigl\|\,u-u^{\prime}-\widetilde{k}(v+\widetilde{h}(u))+\widetilde{k}(v^{\prime}+\widetilde{h}(u^{\prime}))\,\bigr\|

\displaystyle\leq\|u-u^{\prime}\|+H^{\prime}\,\bigl\|\,(v-v^{\prime})+(\widetilde{h}(u)-\widetilde{h}(u^{\prime}))\,\bigr\|^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\|u-u^{\prime}\|+H^{\prime}\bigl(\|v-v^{\prime}\|+C^{\prime}\|u-u^{\prime}\|^{\alpha}\bigr)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u-u^{\prime}\|+\|v-v^{\prime}\|^{\alpha}+\|u-u^{\prime}\|^{\alpha^{2}}.

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(u,v)-(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\|^{\alpha^{2}}.

This implies that
\Phi
is
\alpha^{2}
-Hölder. The same argument applied to
\Psi
(symmetry of the formulas) shows that
\Psi
is
\alpha^{2}
-Hölder. Therefore,
\Phi
is bi-
\alpha^{2}
-Hölder.

Claim 3:
\Phi(X\times\{0\})=\{0\}\times Y
.

Let
x\in X
. Using the extension properties
\widetilde{h}|_{X}=h
and
\widetilde{k}|_{Y}=h^{-1}
,

\Phi(x,0)=\bigl(x-\widetilde{k}(\widetilde{h}(x)),\ \widetilde{h}(x)\bigr)=\bigl(x-h^{-1}(h(x)),\ h(x)\bigr)=(0,h(x))\in\{0\}\times Y.

Thus
\Phi(X\times\{0\})=\{0\}\times Y
.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.17
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be definable germs at
0
. Then there exists
\alpha_{0}\in(0,1)

(X,0)
and
(Y,0)

\alpha
-Hölder equivalent for some

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
n\geq m
. By embedding
Y
into
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we may consider
Y
as a germ at
0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
h:(X,0)\to(Y,0)
be a bi-
\alpha
-Hölder homeomorphism.
By Lemma
4.16
, there exists a bi-
\alpha^{2}
-Hölder homeomorphism

5.
Homotopy types of the links of the tangent cones

In this section, we prove a bi-
\alpha
-Hölder rigidity result for the homotopy type of links of tangent cones.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed definable cone at
0
.
Then, for any
d>1,C>0
, the inclusion

\iota:(A,0)\hookrightarrow(ST_{d}(A,C),0),

induces isomorphisms between the homotopy groups of
{\rm Lk}(A,0)
and
{\rm Lk}(ST_{d}(A,C),0)
.

Proof.

Let
S_{A}:=A\cap S^{n-1}
and define the definable continuous function

g:S^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\qquad g(u)=\operatorname{dist}(u,A).

Since
A
is a cone,
\operatorname{dist}(ru,A)=r\,\operatorname{dist}(u,A)=r\,g(u)
for all
r>0
,
u\in S^{n-1}
.
Hence, with
\varphi_{r}:S(0,r)\to S^{n-1}
,
\varphi_{r}(x)=x/r
, we have

ST_{d}(A,C)\cap\mathbb{\mathbb{}}S(0,r)=\{ru:u\in S^{n-1},\ g(u)\leq Cr^{d-1}\}=\varphi_{r}^{-1}\!\big(g^{-1}([0,Cr^{d-1}])\big).

By Hardt’s triviality theorem, there exists
\delta>0
such that
g
is a definably trivial over
(0,\delta)
.
Consequently, for
0<\varepsilon_{1}<\varepsilon_{2}<\delta
, there is a strong deformation retract from
g^{-1}([0,\varepsilon_{2}]
onto
g^{-1}([0,\varepsilon_{1}])
(see Observation
2.9
), hence the inclusion

g^{-1}([0,\varepsilon_{1}])\hookrightarrow g^{-1}([0,\varepsilon_{2}])
induces isomorphisms on all homotopy groups.

Since
S_{A}
is a compact definable set, it is triangulable; thus there exist arbitrarily small open neighborhood
U
admitting a definable deformation retraction onto
S_{A}
.
By continuity of
g
and compactness of
S_{A}
, we may choose two such neighborhoods
U_{1}\subset U_{2}
and

0<\varepsilon_{1}<\varepsilon_{2}<\delta
such that

g^{-1}([0,\varepsilon_{1}])\subset U_{1}\subset g^{-1}([0,\varepsilon_{2}])\subset U_{2}.

It is clear that the inclusion maps from
\iota_{1}:g^{-1}([0,\varepsilon_{1}])\to g^{-1}([0,\varepsilon_{2}])
and
\iota_{2}:U_{1}\to U_{2}
induce isomorphisms between corresponding homotopy groups.

Consider the following commutative diagram induced from inclusion maps:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{*}(g^{-1}([0,\varepsilon_{1}])}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{*}(g^{-1}([0,\varepsilon_{2}])}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{*}(S_{A})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{*}(U_{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{*}(U_{2})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{1*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{0*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{2*}}

Since
\iota_{i*}
,
i=0,1,2
are isomorphisms, so all morphisms in the diagram are isomorphic. This implies that the homotopy groups of
S_{A}
and
g^{-1}([0,\varepsilon])

are isomorphic for all
\varepsilon<\delta
.
Conjugating by the homeomorphism

\varphi_{r}:S(0,r)\to S^{n-1}
shows that

A\cap\mathbb{\mathbb{}}S(0,r)=\varphi_{r}^{-1}(S_{A})\ \hookrightarrow\ ST_{d}(A,C)\cap\mathbb{\mathbb{}}S(0,r)=\varphi_{r}^{-1}\big(g^{-1}([0,Cr^{d-1}])\big)

induces isomorphisms on all homotopy groups for sufficiently small
r>0
provided
Cr^{d-1}<\delta
.
This completes the proof.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(X,0)
and
(Y,0)
be closed definable germs in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For every
C>0
there exists
d_{0}>1
and
\alpha_{0}\in(0,1)
such that
if
(X,0)
and
(Y,0)
are embedded bi-
\alpha
-Hölder equivalent for some
\alpha\in[\alpha_{0},1]
,
then for every
d\in(1,d_{0})
the links
{\rm Lk}(ST_{d}(X,C),0)
and
{\rm Lk}(ST_{d}(Y,C),0)

have the same homotopy type.

Proof.

Fix
C>0
. For a definable germ
Z\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(with
Z=X
or
Z=Y
), set

\Omega_{Z}:=\{x\in B(0,1):{\rm dist}(x,Z)\leq C\|x\|^{d}\text{ for some }d\in(1,2]\},

and define

\rho_{Z}(x):=\sup\{\,d\in(1,2]:{\rm dist}(x,Z)\leq C\|x\|^{d}\,\},\qquad x\in\Omega_{Z}.

For
x\in\Omega_{Z}\setminus Z
, the function

q_{Z}(x):=\frac{\log({\rm dist}(x,Z)/C)}{\log\|x\|}

is continuous.
Since

\rho_{Z}(x)=\min\{2,q_{Z}(x)\},

\rho_{Z}
is continuous on
\Omega_{Z}\setminus Z
.

Although
\Omega_{Z}
need not be definable in the original structure, it is definable in an exponential o-minimal expansion (see
[
13
]
). In particular,
\rho_{Z}
is definable on the same structure as
\Omega_{Z}
. Applying Hardt’s triviality theorem to
\rho_{Z}|_{\Omega_{Z}\setminus Z}
, we obtain
\delta_{Z}>1
such that
\rho_{Z}
is definably trivial over
(1,\delta_{Z})
.
By Observation
2.9
, for any

1<d<d^{\prime}<\delta_{Z}
, there exist strong deformation retract from
\rho_{Z}^{-1}([d,\delta_{Z}])=ST_{d}(Z,C)\setminus X
onto
\rho_{Z}^{-1}([d^{\prime},\delta_{Z}])=ST_{d^{\prime}}(Z,C)\setminus Z
. Since
Z\subset ST_{d}^{\prime}(Z,C)
, we may extend this deformation by defining it to be the identity on
Z
. This yields a strong deformation retract from
ST_{d}(Z,C)
onto
ST_{d^{\prime}}(Z,C)
. This deformation retract fixes
Z
, hence fixes
0
as well. Consequently, it induces a strong deformation retract from
ST_{d}(Z,C)\setminus\{0\}
onto
ST_{d^{\prime}}(Z,C)\setminus\{0\}
.

Let
\varphi:(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
be a bi-
\alpha
-Hölder homeomorphism such that
\varphi(X)=Y
. Set
d_{0}:=\min\{\delta_{X},\delta_{Y}\}
and fix an
d\in(1,d_{0})
. It follows from Lemma
4.6
that there are
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

(13)

ST_{d/\alpha^{2}}(Y,C_{1})\subset\varphi(ST_{d}(X,C))\subset ST_{d\alpha^{2}}(Y,C_{2})

Set
d_{1}=d_{1}(\alpha):=d/\alpha^{2}+(1-\alpha)>d/\alpha^{2}
,
d_{2}=d_{2}(\alpha):=d\alpha^{2}-(1-\alpha)<d\alpha^{2}
. There is
\alpha_{0}<1
close to
1
such that for all
\alpha_{0}\leq\alpha<1
, both
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
are greater than
1
. Since
ST_{d_{1}}(Y,C)\subset ST_{d/\alpha^{2}}(Y,C_{1})
and
ST_{d\alpha^{2}}(Y,C_{2})\subset ST_{d_{2}}(Y,C)
, we have

(14)

ST_{d_{1}}(Y,C)\subset\varphi(ST_{d}(X,C))\subset ST_{d_{2}}(Y,C).

Again by Lemma
4.6
there is
C_{3}>0
such that

\varphi(ST_{d_{1}/\alpha^{2}}(X,C_{3}))\subset ST_{d_{1}}(Y,C).

Choosing
d_{3}=d_{3}(\alpha):=d_{1}/\alpha^{2}+(1-\alpha)>d_{1}/\alpha^{2}
, we have

ST_{d_{3}}(X,C)\subset ST_{d_{1}/\alpha^{2}}(X,C_{3}).

Thus,

(15)

\varphi(ST_{d_{3}}(X,C))\subset ST_{d_{1}}(Y,C)

Combining (
14
) and (
15
) gives

\varphi(ST_{d_{3}}(X,C))\subset ST_{d_{1}}(Y,C)\subset\varphi(ST_{d}(X,C))\subset ST_{d_{2}}(Y,C).

Note that
d_{3}>d_{1}>d_{2}
all tend to
d
as
\alpha
tends to
1
. Take
\alpha_{0}
close enough to
1
such that for all
\alpha\in[\alpha_{0},1]
,
d_{i}\in(1,d_{0})
for
i=1,2,3
. Consider the following commutative diagram induced by inclusion maps:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{*}\bigg(\varphi(ST_{d_{3}}(X,C)\setminus\{0\})\bigg)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{*}\bigg(\varphi(ST_{d}(X,C)\setminus\{0\})\bigg)}

Since

are isomorphic, so are the others in the diagram. As

and

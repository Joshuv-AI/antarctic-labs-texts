Panorbital residues and elliptic summability
Carlos E. Arreche, Matthew W. Babbitt

Abstract
For τ \tau the translation automorphism defined by a non-torsion point in an elliptic curve, we consider the elliptic summability problem of deciding whether a given elliptic function f f is of the form f = τ ⁡ ( g ) − g f=\tau(g)-g for some elliptic function g g . We introduce two new panorbital residues and show that they, together with the orbital residues introduced in 2018 by Dreyfus, Hardouin, Roques, and Singer, comprise a complete obstruction to the elliptic summability problem. The underlying elliptic curve can be described in any of the usual ways: as a complex torus, as a Tate curve, or as a one-dimensional abelian variety. We develop the necessary results from scratch intrinsically within each setting; in the last two of them, we also work in arbitrary characteristic. We include several basic concrete examples of computation of orbital and panorbital residues for some summable and non-summable functions in each setting. We conclude by applying the technology of orbital and panorbital residues to obtain several new results of independent interest.

, the
panorbital residues
of
f(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}
of orders
0
and
1
relative to
\mathcal{R}
are respectively

\begin{gathered}\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,0):=c_{0}^{\mathcal{R}}(f);\vphantom{\sum_{A}}\qquad\text{and}\\
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,1):=\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n\cdot c_{1}(f,\alpha_{\omega}+ns).\end{gathered}

(2.5)

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{R}=\{\alpha_{\omega}\ |\ \omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}/\tau}\}
be any
\tau
-pinning of
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}
, and let
f(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}
. Then
f
is elliptically summable if and only if
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,0)=0
,
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,1)=0
, and
\ores(f,\omega,k)=0
for every
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}/\tau}
and
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

First suppose
f(z)=g(z+s)-g(z)
for some
g(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}
. From the fact that
c_{k}(\tau(g),\alpha)=c_{k}(g,\alpha+s)
for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
and
k\geq 1
follows the relation
\ores(\tau(g),\omega,k)=\ores(g,\omega,k)
for every
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}/\tau}
and
k\geq 1
, and therefore every
\ores(f,\omega,k)=0
. Moreover, applying
\tau
to both sides of the zeta expansion (
2.4
) for
g(z)
, we find that similarly
c^{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(\tau(g))=c^{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(g)
, and therefore
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,0)=0
. Finally, we see that

\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(\tau(g),1)=\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n\cdot c_{1}\bigl(g,\alpha_{\omega}+(n+1)s\bigr)\\
=\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(g,1)-\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{1}(g,\alpha_{\omega}+ns)=\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(g,1),

by Cauchy’s Residue Theorem applied to the differential form
g(z)dz
over an appropriate fundamental parallelogram in
\mathbb{C}
. Thus
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,1)=0
.

To prove the converse, let us now suppose that every orbital residue and both panorbital residues of
f(z)
are zero. To simplify notation, let us define

\varphi_{\omega,n,k}^{\mathcal{R}}(z):=\begin{cases}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\zeta_{\Lambda}^{(k-1)}(z-\alpha_{\omega}-ns)\in\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}&\text{for}\ k\geq 2;\\
\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-\alpha_{\omega}-ns)-\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-ns)\in\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}&\text{for}\ k=1.\end{cases}

(2.6)

for
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}/\tau}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let us consider

\tilde{f}(z):=f(z)+\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}(f,\alpha_{\omega}+ns)\cdot\bigl(\varphi_{\omega,n,k}^{\mathcal{R}}(z+ns)-\varphi_{\omega,n,k}^{\mathcal{R}}(z)\bigr).

(2.7)

Since
\varphi(z+ns)-\varphi(z)
is summable for any
\varphi(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
f(z)
is summable if and only if
\tilde{f}(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}
is summable. Moreover, since each
\varphi_{\omega,n,k}^{\mathcal{R}}(z+ns)=\varphi_{\omega,0,k}^{\mathcal{R}}(z)
, writing
f(z)
as in (
2.4
) yields

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(z)

\displaystyle=0+\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}/\tau}}\bigg(\sum_{k\geq 1}\ores(f,\omega,k)\cdot\varphi_{\omega,0,k}^{\mathcal{R}}(z)+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{1}(f,\alpha_{\omega}+ns)\cdot\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-ns)\bigg)

\displaystyle=0+\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{1}(f,\alpha_{\omega}+ns)\cdot\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-ns),

(2.8)

where the leftmost
0
’s in (
2.8
) arise from our assumptions that
c_{0}^{\mathcal{R}}(f)
and the
\ores(f,\omega,k)
all vanish. Next let us again simplify notation by defining

\displaystyle\tilde{c}(n):=c_{1}\bigl(\tilde{f},ns\bigr)=\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}/\tau}}c_{1}(f,\alpha_{\omega}+ns)\in\mathbb{C},

so that
\tilde{f}(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{c}(n)\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-ns)
in (
2.8
) and
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n\tilde{c}(n)=\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,1)
. Let us also define the auxiliary
\Lambda
-periodic functions

\psi_{j}(z):=\begin{cases}\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-js)-\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-s)\in\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}&\text{for}\ j\geq 2;\\
\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-js)-\zeta_{\Lambda}(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}&\text{for}\ j\leq-1.\end{cases}

(2.9)

We now consider the further reduction of
f(z)
by summable elements of
\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}
:

\bar{f}(z):=\tilde{f}(z)+\sum_{n\leq-1}\sum_{j=n}^{-1}\tilde{c}(n)\cdot\bigl(\psi_{j}(z-s)-\psi_{j}(z)\bigr)+\sum_{n\geq 2}\sum_{j=2}^{n}\tilde{c}(n)\cdot\bigl(\psi_{j}(z+s)-\psi_{j}(z)\bigr).

(2.10)

Once again we see that
f(z)
is summable if and only if
\bar{f}(z)
is summable. Moreover, it follows from the definitions (
2.9
) that, for
n\geq 2
,

\sum_{j=2}^{n}\bigl(\psi_{j}(z+s)-\psi_{j}(z)\bigr)=-\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-ns)+n\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-s)-(n-1)\zeta_{\Lambda}(z);

(2.11)

and similarly for
n\leq-1
that

\sum\limits_{j=n}^{-1}(\psi_{j}(z-s)-\psi_{j}(z))=-\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-ns)+n\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-s)-(n-1)\zeta_{\Lambda}(z).

(2.12)

Thus, writing the
\tilde{f}(z)
as in (
2.8
) in (
2.10
), the computations (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) yield that

\bar{f}(z)=\tilde{c}(0)\zeta_{\Lambda}(z)+{\overbrace{\tilde{c}(1)\zeta_{\Lambda}(z)-\tilde{c}(1)\zeta_{\Lambda}(z)}^{0}}+\tilde{c}(1)\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-s)\\
+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}-\{0,1\}}\Bigl(n\tilde{c}(n)\bigl(\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-s)-\zeta_{\Lambda}(z)\bigr)+\tilde{c}(n)\zeta_{\Lambda}(z)\Bigr)\\
=\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,1)\cdot\bigl(\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-s)-\zeta_{\Lambda}(z)\bigr)+\left(\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}/\tau}}\ores(f,\omega,1)\right)\cdot\zeta_{\Lambda}(z)=0.

This concludes the proof that
f(z)
is summable.∎

Remark 2.7
.

Whether or not any orbital or panorbital residues of
f(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}
vanish, defining
\tilde{f}(z)
in terms of
f(z)
as in (
2.7
), and subsequently
\bar{f}(z)
in terms of
\tilde{f}(z)
as in (
2.10
), results in the
reduced form
for
f(z)
(cf.
[
5
, Lem. B.14]
):

\bar{f}(z)=\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,0)+\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,1)\cdot\bigl(\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-s)-\zeta_{\Lambda}(z)\bigr)\\
+\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}/\tau}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\ores(f,\omega,k)\cdot\varphi^{\mathcal{R}}_{\omega,0,k},

relative to a choice of
\tau
-pinning
\mathcal{R}
as in Definition
2.3
and with the
\varphi^{\mathcal{R}}_{\omega,0,k}\in\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}
defined as in (
2.6
), and such that
\bar{f}(z)-f(z)
is summable.

Remark 2.8
.

For any other choice
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}=\{\alpha_{\omega}^{\prime}\ |\ \omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}/\tau}\}
of
\tau
-pinning, there exist unique
\lambda_{\omega}\in\Lambda
and
n_{\omega}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\alpha_{\omega}^{\prime}=\alpha_{\omega}+\lambda_{\omega}+n_{\omega}s
for each
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}/\tau}
. We see immediately from the definition (
2.5
) that

\pano^{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}(f,1)=\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,1)-\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}/\tau}}n_{\omega}\ores(f,\omega,1).

(2.13)

Similarly, comparing the rightmost sums in the
\zeta
-expansions (
2.4
) relative to
\mathcal{R}
and to
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
we find that

\pano^{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}(f,0)=\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,0)-\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}/\tau}}\eta_{\Lambda}(\lambda_{\omega})\ores(f,\omega,1).

(2.14)

Thus in particular the panorbital residues of
f(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}
are independent of the choice of
\tau
-pinning of
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}
whenever all the (first-order) orbital residues of
f(z)
vanish.

2.4
Basic examples

To conclude this section, we illustrate the above general results with some basic examples. We choose once and for all an arbitrary
\tau
-pinning
\mathcal{R}=\{\alpha_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}/\tau}}
of
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}
as in Definition
2.3
.

Example 2.9
.

Consider first
\wp_{\Lambda}(z),\wp_{\Lambda}^{\prime}(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}
as in (
2.1
). It is easy to see directly that neither
\wp_{\Lambda}(z)
nor
\wp_{\Lambda}^{\prime}(z)
is summable. We see that both
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(\wp_{\Lambda},1)=0
and
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(\wp_{\Lambda}^{\prime},1)=0
, since
c_{1}(\wp_{\Lambda},\alpha)=0
and
c_{1}(\wp_{\Lambda}^{\prime},\alpha)=0
for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
. The expansions (
2.4
) in this case are given by
\wp_{\Lambda}(z)=0+\zeta_{\Lambda}^{\prime}(z-\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}\check{s}}-ns)
and
\wp_{\Lambda}^{\prime}(z)=0+\zeta_{\Lambda}^{\prime\prime}(z-\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}\check{s}}-ns)
, relative to whichever
\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}\check{s}}\in\Lambda+\mathbb{Z}s
is chosen in
\mathcal{R}
so that
0=\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}\check{s}}+\lambda+ns
. Thus
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(\wp_{\Lambda},0)=0
and
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(\wp_{\Lambda}^{\prime},0)=0
. Finally, we see that every other orbital residue of these functions vanishes except for
\ores(\wp_{\Lambda},\mathbb{Z}\check{s},2)=1
and
\ores(\wp_{\Lambda}^{\prime},\mathbb{Z}\check{s},3)=-2
, confirming the conclusion of Theorem
2.6
. A similar computation shows more generally that for
k\geq 2
each panorbital residue of
\zeta_{\Lambda}^{(k-1)}(z)
also vanishes, as does each of its orbital residues except for
\ores\bigl(\zeta_{\Lambda}^{(k-1)},\mathbb{Z}\check{s},k)=(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!
.

Example 2.10
.

Consider
f_{t}(z):=\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-t)-\zeta_{\Lambda}(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}
for
t\in\mathbb{C}
(see Lemma
2.1
). It is not difficult to see directly that
f_{t}(z)
cannot be summable unless it is
0
. Let us further simplify notation by writing
\alpha_{t},\alpha_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
for the representatives chosen in
\mathcal{R}
for the orbits
\omega_{t}:=\check{t}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\check{s}
and
\omega_{0}:=\check{0}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\check{s}
in
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}
. Then there exist unique
\lambda_{t},\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda
and
n_{t},n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
t=\alpha_{t}+\lambda_{t}+n_{t}s
and
0=\alpha_{0}+\lambda_{0}+n_{0}s
. We compute directly that
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f_{t},1)=n_{t}-n_{0}
. The expansion (
2.4
) in this case is

f_{t}(z)=\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f_{t},0)+\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-\alpha_{t}-n_{t}s)-\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-\alpha_{0}-n_{0}s),

and it follows that
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f_{t},0)=-\eta_{\Lambda}(\lambda_{t})+\eta_{\Lambda}(\lambda_{0})=\eta_{\Lambda}(\lambda_{0}-\lambda_{t})
. Moreover
\ores(f_{t},\omega_{0},1)=0
if and only if
t\in\Lambda+\mathbb{Z}s
(i.e., if
\check{t}\in\mathbb{Z}\check{s}
); otherwise,
\ores(f_{t},\omega_{t},1)=1=-\ores(f_{t},\omega_{0},1)
. But if
t\in\Lambda+\mathbb{Z}s
and
n_{t}\neq n_{0}
then
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f_{t},1)\neq 0
. Thus the only way to have all orbital residues and the first-order panorbital residue of
f_{t}(z)
vanish is to have
\check{t}=\check{0}\in\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}
. But in this case
f_{t}(z)=\eta_{\Lambda}(\lambda_{0}-\lambda_{t})\in\mathbb{C}
is summable if and only if it is
0
. This again confirms the conclusion of Theorem
2.6
.

Example 2.11
.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, consider the elements

\Psi_{n}(z):=-\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-ns)+n\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-s)-(n-1)\zeta_{\Lambda}(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}.

Then
\ores(\Psi_{n},\mathbb{Z}\check{s},1)=-1+n-(n-1)=0
, and every other orbital residue of
\Psi_{n}(z)
also vanishes, since
\Psi_{n}(z)
is holomorphic away from
\Lambda\cup(s+\Lambda)\cup(ns+\Lambda)
and only has at worst first-order poles at these places. For
\alpha_{0}=-\lambda_{0}-n_{0}s\in\mathcal{R}
as in Example
2.10
, so that
js=\alpha_{0}+\lambda_{0}+(j+n_{0})s
, we have

\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(\Psi_{n},1)=(n+n_{0})\cdot(-1)+(1+n_{0})\cdot n+n_{0}\cdot(1-n)=0.

The expansion (
2.4
) in this case is

\Psi_{n}(z)=c^{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(\Psi_{n})+(-1)\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-ns+\lambda_{0})+n\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-s+\lambda_{0})+(1-n)\zeta_{\Lambda}(z+\lambda_{0}),

whence

\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(\Psi_{n},0)=c_{0}^{\mathcal{R}}(\Psi_{n})=\eta_{\Lambda}(\lambda_{0})-n\eta_{\Lambda}(\lambda_{0})+(n-1)\eta_{\Lambda}(\lambda_{0})=0.

Therefore by Theorem
2.6
each
\Psi_{n}(z)
is summable. This agrees with the computations (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) in case
n\neq 0,1
, and
\Psi_{0}(z)=\Psi_{1}(z)=0
.

3
Tate setting

3.1
Reminders and notation

In this section we denote by
\mathcal{C}
an algebraically closed field complete with respect to a non-Archimedean absolute value
|\ \ |
. Everything we do here is also valid in the Archimedean case
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{C}
. Given
q\in\mathcal{C}
such that
0<|q|<1
, we denote by
\mathcal{K}_{q}
the field of (multiplicatively)
q
-periodic meromorphic functions
f(z)
on
\mathcal{C}^{\times}
, i.e., such that
f(qz)=f(z)
, which is identified with the field of meromorphic functions on the elliptic curve
\mathcal{E}_{q}:=\mathcal{C}/q^{\mathbb{Z}}
. We again denote the projection map
\mathcal{C}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{q}:t\mapsto\check{t}:=t\cdot q^{\mathbb{Z}}
. We consider as in
[
16
, §2]
and
[
7
, §5.1]
(cf.
[
17
, Prop. V.3.2]
) the
basic theta function

\theta_{q}(z)=\prod_{m\geq 0}(1-q^{m}z^{-1})\cdot\prod_{m\geq 1}(1-q^{m}z),

(3.1)

which is holomorphic on
\mathcal{C}^{\times}
and satisfies
\theta_{q}(qz)=-(qz)^{-1}\theta_{q}(z)
. Denoting by
\delta=z\frac{d}{dz}
the Euler derivation, we consider similarly as in
[
16
, §3]
the “zeta function”

\zeta_{q}(z):=-\frac{\delta\theta_{q}(z)}{\theta_{q}(z)}=\frac{1}{1-z}+\sum_{m\geq 1}\left(\frac{q^{m}z}{1-q^{m}z}-\frac{q^{m}z^{-1}}{1-q^{m}z^{-1}}\right)

(3.2)

which is meromorphic on
\mathcal{C}^{\times}
and satisfies
\zeta_{q}(qz)=\zeta_{q}(z)+1
. This immediately implies the following analogue of Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For each
t\in\mathcal{C}^{\times}
, the difference
\zeta_{q}(t^{-1}z)-\zeta_{q}(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{q}
is
q
-periodic
. Moreover, if
t\notin q^{\mathbb{Z}}
, then
\zeta_{q}(t^{-1}z)-\zeta_{q}(z)
has simple poles at
\check{t}
and at
\check{1}
on
\mathcal{E}_{q}
, and is holomorphic elsewhere in
\mathcal{C}^{\times}
.

It is known (see e.g.
[
16
, §3]
,
[
13
, §15.1]
, or
[
7
, Thm. 5.1.10]
) that
\mathcal{K}_{q}=\mathcal{C}(\wp_{q},\wp_{q}^{\prime})
is a separable quadratic extension of its subfield
\mathcal{C}(\wp_{q})
, where

\displaystyle\wp_{q}(z)

\displaystyle=\delta\zeta_{q}(z)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{q^{m}z}{(1-q^{m}z)^{2}};\qquad\text{and}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\wp_{q}^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\delta\wp_{q}(z)-\wp_{q}(z)\bigr)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{q^{2m}z^{2}}{(1-q^{m}z)^{3}}.

The normalizations
\tilde{\wp}_{q}(z)=\wp_{q}(z)+-2s_{1,q}
and
\tilde{\wp}_{q}^{\prime}(z)=\wp_{q}^{\prime}(z)+s_{1,q}
, where the constant
s_{1,q}=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{q^{m}}{(1-q^{m})}\in\mathcal{C}
, yield the following analogue of the Weierstrass equation (
2.2
), which is valid in arbitrary characteristic:

\tilde{\wp}_{q}^{\prime}(z)^{2}+\tilde{\wp}_{q}(z)\tilde{\wp}_{q}^{\prime}(z)=\tilde{\wp}_{q}(z)^{3}+b_{q}\wp_{q}(z)+c_{q}.

(3.4)

The constants
b_{q},c_{q}\in\mathcal{C}
depend only on
q
and satisfy the non-singularity condition
b_{q}^{2}-c_{q}-64b_{q}^{3}+72b_{q}c_{q}-432c_{q}^{2}\neq 0
.

Given any meromorphic function
f(z)
on
\mathcal{C}^{\times}
, we write
c_{k}(f,\alpha)\in\mathcal{C}
for the coefficients occurring in the principal part of the Laurent series expansion of
f(z)
at
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{\times}
:

f(z)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{c_{k}(f,\alpha)}{\bigl(1-\alpha^{-1}z\bigr)^{k}}+h(z),\qquad\text{with}\quad h(z)\in\mathcal{C}\bigl[\bigl[1-\alpha^{-1}z\bigr]\bigr].

(3.5)

We again define the
c_{k}(f,\alpha)
in this way for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and every
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{\times}
, even though all but finitely many of them are zero for each given
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{\times}
. We again note that if
f(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{q}
then the
c_{k}(f,\alpha)
depend only on the point
\check{\alpha}\in\mathcal{E}_{q}
, which we occasionally emphasize by writing
c_{k}(f,\check{\alpha})
in this case.

As in the introduction, we denote by
\tau
the automorphism of
\mathcal{K}_{q}
given by
\tau(f)(z)=f(sz)
for a fixed
s\in\mathcal{C}^{\times}
such that
s
and
q
are multiplicatively independent, or equivalently, such that
\check{s}
is a non-torsion point of
\mathcal{E}_{q}
. The set of
\tau
-
orbits
in
\mathcal{E}_{q}
is

{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}:=\mathcal{E}_{q}/\mathbb{Z}\check{s}.

The following definition is a rigid analytic reinterpretation of
[
5
, Def. B.7]
.

Definition 3.2

(Orbital residues - Tate version)
.

The
orbital residue
of
f(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{q}
at the orbit
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}
of order
k\in\mathbb{N}
is

\ores(f,\omega,k):=\sum_{\check{\alpha}\in\omega}c_{k}(f,\check{\alpha}).

We again emphasize that the finite sum above is being taken over the points
\check{\alpha}\in\omega\subset\mathcal{E}_{q}
, rather than meaninglessly over the set of pre-images of these
points in
\mathcal{C}^{\times}
.

3.2
Pinnings and their resulting zeta-expansions

The following definition is the rigid analytic version of Definition
2.3
.

Definition 3.3

(
\tau
-pinnings - Tate version)
.

A
\tau
-
pinning
of
\mathcal{E}_{q}
is a choice
\mathcal{R}=\{\alpha_{\omega}\in\mathcal{C}^{\times}\ |\ \omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}\}
of representatives with
\check{\alpha}_{\omega}\in\omega
for each
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}
.

The following special functions
\zeta_{q}^{({k})}(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{q}
will serve as a sort of replacement for the higher derivatives
\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\frac{d^{k}}{dz^{k}}\zeta_{\Lambda}(z)
that were utilized in the Weierstrass setting. We recall that
\delta=z\frac{d}{dz}
denotes the Euler derivation.

Definition 3.4
.

We set
\zeta_{q}^{({0})}(z):=\zeta_{q}(z)
as in (
3.2
), and, for
k\geq 1
,

\zeta_{q}^{({k})}(z):=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{q^{km}z^{k}}{\bigl(1-q^{m}z\bigr)^{k+1}}=\frac{1}{k}\Bigl(\delta\zeta_{q}^{({k-1})}(z)-(k-1)\zeta_{q}^{({k-1})}(z)\Bigr).

Of course,
\zeta_{q}^{({1})}(z)=\wp_{q}(z)
and
\zeta_{q}^{({2})}(z)=\wp_{q}^{\prime}(z)
as in (
3.3
), and evidently every
\zeta_{q}^{({k})}(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{q}
for
k\geq 1
. Presumably the following rigid analytic version of Proposition
2.4
will not be surprising to the experts, but we are not aware of a similar result being mentioned explicitly in the literature.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{R}=\{\alpha_{\omega}\ |\ \omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}\}
be any
\tau
-pinning of
\mathcal{E}_{q}
, and let
f(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{q}
. There exists a unique constant
c_{0}^{\mathcal{R}}(f)\in\mathcal{C}
such that

f(z)=c_{0}^{\mathcal{R}}(f)+\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}(f,s^{n}\alpha_{\omega})\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{k-1}{i-1}\zeta_{q}^{({i-1})}\bigl(s^{-n}\alpha_{\omega}^{-1}z\bigr)

(3.6)

as meromorphic functions on
\mathcal{C}^{\times}
.

Proof.

First we prove the preliminary claim that, for
\ell,k\in\mathbb{N}
,

c_{\ell}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{k-1}{i-1}\zeta_{q}^{({i-1})},1\right)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\ \ell=k;\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(3.7)

The claim is obvious in case
\ell>k
, since each
\zeta_{q}^{({i-1})}(z)
has a pole of order
i
at
1
for
i\geq 1
. It follows from the elementary computation

\frac{z^{i-1}}{(1-z)^{i}}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{i}(-1)^{i-\ell}\binom{i-1}{\ell-1}\frac{1}{(1-z)^{\ell}}

that
c_{\ell}\bigl(\zeta_{q}^{({i-1})},1\bigr)=(-1)^{i-\ell}\binom{i-1}{\ell-1}
, where as usual
\binom{i-1}{\ell-1}=0
if
\ell>i
. Thus

c_{\ell}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{k-1}{i-1}\zeta_{q}^{({i-1})},1\right)=\sum_{i=\ell}^{k}(-1)^{i-\ell}\binom{k-1}{i-1}\binom{i-1}{\ell-1},

(3.8)

concluding the proof of the preliminary claim (cf.
[
14
, Lem. 4.27]
).

By construction, the set
\{s^{n}\alpha_{\omega}\ |\ \omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau},\ n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
contains precisely one coset representative in
\mathcal{C}^{\times}
of each point in
\mathcal{E}_{q}
. Each summand in the right-hand side of (
3.6
) with
i\geq 2
is
q
-periodic by (
3.3
) and Definition
3.4
. Comparing local expansions, one could immediately reduce to the case where
f(z)
has only simple poles. The remaining portion of the sum
\sum_{\omega,n,k}c_{k}\bigl(f,s^{n}\alpha_{\omega}\bigr)\zeta_{q}\bigl(s^{-n}\alpha_{\omega}^{-1}z\bigr)
is seen to be
q
-periodic by the transformation law
\zeta_{q}(qz)=\zeta_{q}(z)+1
, together with the fact that
\sum_{\omega,n,k}c_{k}(f,s^{n}\alpha_{\omega})=0
by the Residue Theorem
[
7
, Thm. 2.3.3]
applied to the differential form
f(z)\frac{dz}{z}
over any fundamental domain
\bigl\{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{\times}\ \big|\ r|q|\leq|\alpha|\leq r\bigr\}
with
r
in the valuation group
|\mathcal{C}^{\times}|
such that
f(z)
has no poles of absolute value
r
. It follows that the difference
c_{0}^{\mathcal{R}}(f)
between
f(z)
and the rightmost sum in (
3.6
) is
q
-periodic and holomorphic on
\mathcal{C}^{\times}
, and therefore it must be constant.
∎

3.3
Panorbital residues and summability

Definition 3.6

(Panorbital residues - Tate version)
.

For a given
\tau
-pinning
\mathcal{R}=\{\alpha_{\omega}\ |\ \omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}\}
of
\mathcal{E}_{q}
, the
panorbital residues
of
f(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{q}
of orders
0
and
1
relative to
\mathcal{R}
are respectively

\begin{gathered}\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,0):=c_{0}^{\mathcal{R}}(f);\vphantom{\sum_{A}}\qquad\text{and}\\
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,1):=\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\geq 1}n\cdot c_{k}\bigl(f,s^{n}\alpha_{\omega}\bigr).\end{gathered}

(3.9)

Remark 3.7
.

The analogous “zeta-expansions,” given in (
2.4
) in the Weierstrass setting and in (
3.6
) in the present Tate setting, provide a direct analogy between the order
0
panorbital residues given in Definition
2.5
and Definition
3.6
. The difference between the corresponding order
1
panorbital residues, however, may perhaps seem more stark due to the appearance of the higher-order
k\geq 2
terms in the Tate setting, which are absent in the Weierstrass setting. However, we see that in the Weierstrass setting we could have defined equivalently

\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,1)=\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n\cdot\mathrm{res}(f(z)dz,\alpha_{\omega}+ns),

and that similarly in the Tate setting we have

\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,1)=\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n\cdot\mathrm{res}\biggl(f(z)\frac{dz}{z},s^{n}\alpha_{\omega}\biggr),

where in both cases
\mathrm{res}(f(z)\varpi,\alpha)
refers to the residues of the corresponding meromorphic differential forms
f(z)\varpi
at the corresponding points
\alpha
. We note that
\varpi=dz
in the Weierstrass setting and
\varpi=dz/z
in the Tate setting both descend to a nowhere-vanishing global invariant differential on the corresponding elliptic curve (cf. Proposition
4.20
).

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{R}=\{\alpha_{\omega}\ |\ \omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}\}
be any
\tau
-pinning of
\mathcal{E}_{q}
, and let
f(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{q}
. Then
f
is elliptically summable if and only if
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,0)=0
,
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,1)=0
, and
\ores(f,\omega,k)=0
for every
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}
and
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

First suppose
f(z)=g(sz)-g(z)
, for some
g(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{q}
. From the fact that
c_{k}(\tau(g),\alpha)=c_{k}(g,s\alpha)
for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{\times}
and
k\geq 1
follows the relation
\ores(\tau(g),\omega,k)=\ores(g,\omega,k)
for every
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}
and
k\geq 1
, and therefore every
\ores(f,\omega,k)=0
. Moreover, applying
\tau
to both sides of the “zeta expansion” (
3.6
) for
g(z)
, we find that similarly
c^{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(\tau(g))=c^{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(g)
, and therefore
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,0)=0
. Finally, we see that

\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(\tau(g),1)=\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\geq 1}n\cdot c_{k}\bigl(g,s^{n+1}\alpha_{\omega}\bigr)\\
=\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(g,1)-\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}(g,s^{n}\alpha_{\omega})=\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(g,1),

by the Residue Theorem
[
7
, Thm. 2.3.3]
applied to the differential form
g(z)\frac{dz}{z}
over an appropriate fundamental domain in
\mathcal{C}^{\times}
. Thus
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,1)=0
.

To prove the converse, let us now suppose that every orbital residue and both panorbital residues of
f(z)
are zero. To simplify notation, let us define

\varphi^{\mathcal{R}}_{\omega,n,k}(z):=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{k-1}{i-1}\zeta_{q}^{({i-1})}\bigl(s^{-n}\alpha_{\omega}^{-1}z\bigr)-\zeta_{q}\bigl(s^{-n}z\bigr)\in\mathcal{K}_{q}

(3.10)

for
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
k\geq 1
. Let us consider

\tilde{f}(z):=f(z)+\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}(f,s^{n}\alpha_{\omega})\cdot\bigl(\varphi^{\mathcal{R}}_{\omega,n,k}(s^{n}z)-\varphi^{\mathcal{R}}_{\omega,n,k}(z)\bigr).

(3.11)

Since
\varphi\bigl(s^{n}z\bigr)-\varphi(z)
is summable for any
\varphi(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{q}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
f(z)
is summable if and only if
\tilde{f}(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{q}
is summable. Moreover, since each
\varphi_{\omega,n,k}^{\mathcal{R}}\bigl(s^{n}z\bigr)=\varphi_{\omega,0,k}^{\mathcal{R}}(z)
, writing
f(z)
as in (
3.6
) yields

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(z)

\displaystyle=0+\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\bigg(\ores(f,\omega,k)\cdot\varphi_{\omega,0,k}^{\mathcal{R}}(z)+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{k}\bigl(f,s^{n}\alpha_{\omega}\bigr)\cdot\zeta_{q}\bigl(s^{-n}z\bigr)\bigg)

\displaystyle=0+\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}\bigl(f,s^{n}\alpha_{\omega}\bigr)\cdot\zeta_{q}\bigl(s^{-n}z\big),

(3.12)

where the leftmost
0
’s in (
3.12
) arise from our assumptions that
c_{0}^{\mathcal{R}}(f)
and the
\ores(f,\omega,k)=0
all vanish.

Next let us again simplify notation by defining

\displaystyle\tilde{c}(n):=c_{1}\bigl(\tilde{f},s^{n}\bigr)=\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}}\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}\bigl(f,s^{n}\alpha_{\omega}\bigr)\in\mathcal{C},

so that
\tilde{f}(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{c}(n)\zeta_{q}\bigl(s^{-n}z\bigr)
in (
3.12
) and
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n\tilde{c}(n)=\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,1)
. Let us also define the auxiliary
q
-periodic functions

\psi_{j}(z):=\begin{cases}\zeta_{q}\bigl(s^{-j}z\bigr)-\zeta_{q}\bigl(s^{-1}z\bigr)\in\mathcal{K}_{q}&\text{for}\ j\geq 2;\\
\zeta_{q}\bigl(s^{-j}z\bigr)-\zeta_{q}\bigl(z\bigr)\in\mathcal{K}_{q}&\text{for}\ j\leq-1.\end{cases}

(3.13)

We now consider the further reduction of
f(z)
by summable elements of
\mathcal{K}_{q}
:

\bar{f}(z):=\tilde{f}(z)+\sum_{n\leq-1}\sum_{j=n}^{-1}\tilde{c}(n)\cdot\bigl(\psi_{j}(s^{-1}z)-\psi_{j}(z)\bigr)+\sum_{n\geq 2}\sum_{j=2}^{n}\tilde{c}(n)\cdot\bigl(\psi_{j}(sz)-\psi_{j}(z)\bigr).

(3.14)

Once again we see that
f(z)
is summable if and only if
\bar{f}(z)
is summable, and like before we find that

\sum_{j=2}^{n}\bigl(\psi_{j}(sz)-\psi_{j}(z)\bigr)=-\zeta_{q}\bigl(s^{-n}z\bigr)+n\zeta_{q}\bigl(s^{-1}z\bigr)-(n-1)\zeta_{q}\bigl(z\bigr)

(3.15)

for each
n\geq 2
, and again similarly that

\sum_{j=n}^{-1}\bigl(\psi_{j}(s^{-1}z)-\psi_{j}(z)\bigr)=-\zeta_{q}\bigl(s^{-n}z\bigr)+n\zeta_{q}\bigl(s^{-1}z\bigr)-(n-1)\zeta_{q}\bigl(z\bigr)

(3.16)

for each
n\leq-1
. It follows that
\bar{f}(z)
is holomorphic on
\mathcal{C}^{\times}
away from
q^{\mathbb{Z}}
and
sq^{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Thus, writing the
\tilde{f}(z)
as in (
3.12
) in (
3.14
), the computations (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) yield that

\bar{f}(z)=\tilde{c}(0)\zeta_{q}(z)+{\overbrace{\tilde{c}(1)\zeta_{q}(z)-\tilde{c}(1)\zeta_{q}(z)}^{0}}+\tilde{c}(1)\zeta_{q}\bigl(s^{-1}z\bigr)\\
+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}-\{0,1\}}\biggl(n\tilde{c}(n)\Bigl(\zeta_{q}\bigl(s^{-1}z\bigr)-\zeta_{q}(z)\Bigr)+\tilde{c}(n)\zeta_{q}(z)\biggr)\\
=\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,1)\cdot\Bigl(\zeta_{q}\bigl(s^{-1}z\bigr)-\zeta_{q}(z)\Bigr)+\left(\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\ores(f,\omega,k)\right)\cdot\zeta_{q}(z)=0.

This concludes the proof that
f(z)
is summable.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

Whether or not any orbital or panorbital residues of
f(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{q}
vanish, defining
\tilde{f}(z)
in terms of
f(z)
as in (
3.11
), and subsequently
\bar{f}(z)
in terms of
\tilde{f}(z)
as in (
3.14
), results in the
reduced form
for
f(z)
(cf.
[
5
, Lem. B.14]
):

\bar{f}(z)=\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,0)+\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,1)\cdot\bigl(\zeta_{q}(z-s)-\zeta_{q}(z)\bigr)\\
+\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\ores(f,\omega,k)\cdot\varphi^{\mathcal{R}}_{\omega,0,k},

relative to a choice of
\tau
-pinning
\mathcal{R}
as in Definition
3.3
and with the
\varphi^{\mathcal{R}}_{\omega,0,k}\in\mathcal{K}_{q}
defined as in (
3.10
) (cf. Remark
2.7
), and such that
\bar{f}(z)-f(z)
is summable.

Remark 3.10
.

The definitions of
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,1)
and
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,0)
are direct analogues of those in Definition
2.5
in the Weierstrass case. For any other choice
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}=\{\alpha_{\omega}^{\prime}\ |\ \omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}\}
of
\tau
-pinning, there exist unique
m_{\omega},n_{\omega}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\alpha_{\omega}^{\prime}=q^{m_{\omega}}s^{n_{\omega}}\alpha_{\omega}
for each
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}
. We see immediately from the definition (
3.9
) that

\pano^{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}(f,1)=\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,1)-\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}}\sum_{k\geq 1}n_{\omega}\ores(f,\omega,k).

(3.17)

It similarly follows from the definition (
3.9
) that

\pano^{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}(f,0)=\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,0)-\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}}\sum_{k\geq 1}m_{\omega}\ores(f,\omega,k).

(3.18)

Thus in particular the panorbital residues of
f(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{q}
are independent of the choice of
\tau
-pinning of
\mathcal{E}_{q}
whenever all the orbital residues of
f(z)
vanish.

3.4
Basic examples

To conclude this section, we illustrate the above general results with some basic examples. We choose once and for all an arbitrary
\tau
-pinning
\mathcal{R}=\{\alpha_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}}
of
\mathcal{E}_{q}
as in Definition
3.3
.

Example 3.11
.

Consider first the
\zeta_{q}^{({k-1})}(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{q}
for
k\geq 2
as in Definition
3.4
. It is easy to see directly that none of these elements is summable. We saw in the proof of Proposition
3.5
that

c_{\ell}\bigl(\zeta_{q}^{({k-1})},1\bigr)=(-1)^{k-\ell}\binom{k-1}{\ell-1}=\ores\bigl(\zeta_{q}^{({k-1})},\mathbb{Z}\check{s},\ell\bigr)\neq 0

for
1\leq\ell\leq k
, already confirming the conclusion of Theorem
3.8
. In particular, since
\wp_{q}(z)=\zeta_{q}^{({1})}(z)
and
\wp_{q}^{\prime}(z)=\zeta_{q}^{({2})}(z)
as in (
3.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\ores(\wp_{q},\mathbb{Z}\check{s},1)=-1;\qquad\ores(\wp_{q},\mathbb{Z}\check{s},2)=1;

\displaystyle\ores(\wp_{q}^{\prime},\mathbb{Z}\check{s},1)=1;\qquad\ores(\wp_{q}^{\prime},\mathbb{Z}\check{s},2)=-2;\qquad\ores(\wp_{q}^{\prime},\mathbb{Z}\check{s},3)=1;

(cf. Example
2.9
). Moreover,

\pano^{\mathcal{R}}\bigl(\zeta_{q}^{({k-1})},1)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}n(-1)^{k-\ell}\binom{k-1}{\ell-1}=0

for every
k\geq 2
, regardless of the value of
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\check{\alpha}_{\mathbb{Z}\check{s}}=\ominus n\check{s}\in\mathcal{E}_{q}
. The expansions (
3.6
) in this case collapse:

\zeta_{q}^{({k-1})}(z)=c^{\mathcal{R}}_{0}\bigl(\zeta_{q}^{({k-1})}\bigr)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}(-1)^{k-\ell}\binom{k-1}{\ell-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{\ell-1}{i-1}\zeta_{q}^{({i-1})}\bigl(s^{-n}\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}\check{s}}^{-1}z\bigr)\\
=\pano^{\mathcal{R}}\bigl(\zeta_{q}^{({k-1})},0\bigr)+\zeta_{q}^{({k-1})}\bigl(s^{-n}\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}\check{s}}^{-1}z\bigr)

(cf. the proof of Proposition
3.5
), and therefore
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}\bigl(\zeta_{q}^{({k-1})},0)=0
, since
s^{n}\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}\check{s}}\in q^{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\zeta_{q}^{({k-1})}(z)
is
q
-periodic for
k\geq 2
. In particular and analogously as in Example
2.9
, we find that the non-summability of
\wp_{q}(z)
and
\wp_{q}^{\prime}(z)
is witnessed by their orbital residues, but not by their panorbital residues.

Example 3.12
.

Consider
f_{t}(z):=\zeta_{q}(t^{-1}z)-\zeta_{q}(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{q}
for
t\in\mathcal{C}^{\times}
(see Lemma
3.1
). It is not difficult to see directly that
f_{t}(z)
cannot be summable unless it is
0
. Let us further simplify notation by writing
\alpha_{t},\alpha_{1}\in\mathcal{C}
for the representatives chosen in
\mathcal{R}
for the orbits
\omega_{t}:=\check{t}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\check{s}
and
\omega_{1}:=\check{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\check{s}
in
\mathcal{E}_{q}
. Then there exist unique
m_{t},m_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
n_{t},n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
t=\alpha_{t}q^{m_{t}}s^{n_{t}}
and
1=\alpha_{1}q^{m_{1}}s^{n_{1}}
. We compute directly that
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f_{t},1)=n_{t}-n_{1}
. The expansion (
2.4
) in this case is

f_{t}(z)=\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f_{t},0)+\zeta_{q}\bigl(q^{m_{t}}s^{-n_{t}}\alpha_{t}^{-1}z\bigr)-\zeta_{q}\bigl(a^{m_{1}}s^{-n_{1}}\alpha_{1}^{-1}z\bigr),

and it follows that
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f_{t},0)=m_{1}-m_{t}
. Moreover
\ores(f_{t},\omega_{1},1)=0
if and only if
t\in q^{\mathbb{Z}}\cdot s^{\mathbb{Z}}
(i.e., if
\check{t}\in\mathbb{Z}\check{s}
); otherwise,
\ores(f_{t},\omega_{t},1)=1=-\ores(f_{t},\omega_{1},1)
. But if we have
t\in q^{\mathbb{Z}}\cdot s^{\mathbb{Z}}
and
n_{t}\neq n_{0}
, then we have
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f_{t},1)\neq 0
. Thus the only way to have all orbital residues and the first-order panorbital residue of
f_{t}(z)
vanish is to have
\check{t}=\check{1}\in\mathcal{E}_{q}
. But in this case
f_{t}(z)=m_{1}-m_{t}\in\mathcal{C}
is summable if and only if it is
0
. This again confirms the conclusion of Theorem
3.8
. Analogously as in Example
2.10
, the non-summability of
\zeta_{q}(t^{-1}z)-\zeta_{q}(z)
, in the most interesting case where
\check{t}\in\mathbb{Z}\check{s}
, is witnessed by its panorbital residues, but not by its orbital residues.

Example 3.13
.

For
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
k\geq 2
, consider the elements

\varphi^{\mathcal{R}}_{\omega,n,k}(z)=\sum_{i=2}^{k}\binom{k-1}{i-1}\zeta_{q}^{({i-1})}(s^{-n}\alpha_{\omega}^{-1}z)+\bigl(\zeta_{q}(s^{-n}\alpha_{\omega}^{-1}z)-\zeta_{q}(s^{-n}z)\bigr)\in\mathcal{K}_{q}

as in (
3.10
) (the case
k=1
corresponds to
f_{t}(s^{-n}z)
as in Example
3.12
with
t=\alpha_{\omega}
). Since
\varphi^{\mathcal{R}}_{\omega,n,k}(z)=\varphi^{\mathcal{R}}_{\omega,0,k}(s^{-n}z)
and the orbital and panorbital residues are
\tau
-invariant, let us treat only the case
n=0
, for simplicity. Since the orbital residues are
\mathcal{C}
-linear, it follows from Example
3.11
, (
3.8
), and Example (
3.12
) (with
t=\alpha_{\omega}
), that
c_{1}\bigl(\varphi^{\mathcal{R}}_{\omega,0,k},1\bigr)=\ores\bigl(\varphi^{\mathcal{R}}_{\omega,0,k},\mathbb{Z}\check{s},1\bigr)=-1
;

c_{\ell}\bigl(\varphi^{\mathcal{R}}_{\omega,0,k},\alpha_{\omega}\bigr)=\ores\bigl(\varphi^{\mathcal{R}}_{\omega,n,k},\omega,\ell)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\ \ell=k;\\
-1&\text{if}\ \ell=1\ \text{and}\ \omega=\mathbb{Z}\check{s};\\
0&\text{otherwise};\end{cases}

and every other orbital residue of
\varphi^{\mathcal{R}}_{\omega,0,k}(z)
vanishes. Moreover, since the panorbital residues are also
\mathcal{C}
-linear, both panorbital residues of
\varphi^{\mathcal{R}}_{\omega,0,k}(z)
agree with those of
f_{t}(z)
as computed in Example
3.12
with
t=\alpha_{\omega}
.

Example 3.14
.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, consider the elements

\Psi_{n}(z):=-\zeta_{q}(s^{-n}z)+n\zeta_{q}(s^{-1}z)-(n-1)\zeta_{q}(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{q}.

Then
\ores(\Psi_{n},\mathbb{Z}\check{s},1)=-1+n-(n-1)=0
, and every other orbital residue of
\Psi_{n}(z)
also vanishes, since
\Psi_{n}(z)
is holomorphic away from
q^{\mathbb{Z}}\cup sq^{\mathbb{Z}}\cup s^{n}q^{\mathbb{Z}}
and only has at worst first-order poles at these places. For
\alpha_{1}=q^{-m_{1}}s^{-n_{1}}\in\mathcal{R}
as in Example
3.12
, so that
s^{j}=\alpha_{1}q^{m_{1}}s^{j+n_{1}}
, we have

\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(\Psi_{n},1)=(n+n_{1})\cdot(-1)+(1+n_{1})\cdot n+n_{1}\cdot(1-n)=0.

The expansion (
2.4
) in this case is

\Psi_{n}(z)=c^{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(\Psi_{n})+(-1)\zeta_{\Lambda}(q^{m_{1}}s^{-n}z)+n\zeta_{\Lambda}(q^{m_{1}}s^{-1}z)+(1-n)\zeta_{\Lambda}(q^{m_{1}}z),

whence

\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(\Psi_{n},0)=c_{0}^{\mathcal{R}}(\Psi_{n})=m_{1}-nm_{1}+(n-1)m_{1}=0.

Therefore by Theorem
3.8
each
\Psi_{n}(z)
is summable. This agrees with the computations (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) in case
n\neq 0,1
, and
\Psi_{0}(z)=\Psi_{1}(z)=0
.

4
Algebraic setting

4.1
Reminders and notation

In this section we denote by
\mathcal{C}
an algebraically closed field, and by
\mathcal{E}
an elliptic curve over
\mathcal{C}
, i.e., a connected smooth projective algebraic curve of genus
1
over
\mathcal{C}
with a distinguished
\mathcal{C}
-point
{o}\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{C})
, which is the identity element for an abelian group law on
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{C})
that we denote by
\oplus
. The field of rational functions on
\mathcal{E}
is denoted by
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{E})
. The group of divisors
\mathrm{Div}(\mathcal{E})
is the free abelian group on the set of
\mathcal{C}
-points of
\mathcal{E}
:

\mathrm{Div}(\mathcal{E})=\left\{\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{C})}m_{\alpha}[\alpha]\ \middle|\ \begin{gathered}m_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}\ \text{for every}\ \alpha\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{C})\\
\text{and almost every}\ m_{\alpha}=0\end{gathered}\right\}.

(4.1)

For a divisor
D=\sum_{\alpha}m_{\alpha}[\alpha]\in\mathrm{Div}(\mathcal{E})
, its degree is
\mathrm{deg}(D)=\sum_{\alpha}m_{\alpha}
, its support is
\mathrm{supp}(D)=\{\alpha\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{C})\ |\ m_{\alpha}\neq 0\}
,
and we say that
D
is effective if
m_{\alpha}\geq 0
for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{C})
, in which case we write
D\geq 0
. For a rational function
f\in\mathcal{K}
, its divisor
\mathrm{div}(f)\in\mathrm{Div}(\mathcal{E})
is given by

\mathrm{div}(f)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{C})}\nu_{\alpha}(f)[\alpha],

where
\nu_{\alpha}(f)
is the valuation of
f
at
\alpha\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{C})
. For a divisor
D\in\mathrm{Div}(\mathcal{E})
, its Riemann-Roch space is the
\mathcal{C}
-vector space

\mathcal{L}(D)=\{f\in\mathcal{K}\ |\ \mathrm{div}(f)-D\geq 0\}.

More explicitly, if
D=\sum_{\alpha}m_{\alpha}[\alpha]
then
\mathcal{L}(D)
consists of those
f\in\mathcal{K}
such that
f
has a zero at
\alpha
of order at least
m_{\alpha}
whenever
m_{\alpha}>0
and a pole at
\alpha
of order at most
-m_{\alpha}
whenever
m_{\alpha}<0
. We shall make repeated use of the following consequence of the Riemann-Roch Theorem
[
18
, Cor. II.5.5(c)]
.

Theorem 4.1

(Riemann-Roch)
.

If
D\in\mathrm{Div}(\mathcal{E})
has
\mathrm{deg}(D)\geq 1
then

Definition-Lemma 4.6
.

For
\mathcal{U}
a
\tau
-compatible set of local uniformizers as in Definition

2.

3.

Proof.

By the Riemann-Roch Theorem
4.1
, any

can be uniquely scaled to satisfy condition (1), and then uniquely modified by an additive constant to satisfy condition (3). The fact that this unique

already satisfies condition (2) follows from
[
5
, Lem. B.15]

[
5
, Appendix B]
that the ground field (
\mathcal{C}

on
\mathcal{K}
such that the differentials

for every

commutes with

(cf.
[
18
, Prop. III.5.1]

and any

whatsoever, the residue of the differential form

at

are
\mathrm{res}(\zeta\varpi,s)=1/\delta(u_{s})(s)
,

Lemma 4.18

Let

(4.9)

The function

, say at

(cf. Remark

).
∎

Taking into account the fact that the structural constants

for every

by Corollary

Corollary 4.19

For any
\tau
-compatible set of local uniformizers

\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\ores^{\mathcal{U}}(f,\omega,k)\cdot d^{\mathcal{U}}_{\omega,k}=0.

of

, the constant function

.
∎

Proposition 4.20
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{U})
be a
\tau
-pinning of
\mathcal{E}
with
\mathcal{U}=\{u_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{C})}
as in Definition
4.2
. For the unique invariant differential
0\neq\varpi\in H^{0}(\mathcal{E},\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{C}})
such that the valuation
\nu_{o}(du_{o}-\varpi)\geq 1
, we have

\pano^{\mathbf{P}}(f,1)=\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n\cdot\mathrm{res}(f\varpi,\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns).

Proof.

For any initial choice of
0\neq\tilde{\varpi}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{E},\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{C}})
, we have a local formal expansion
\tilde{\varpi}=\left(\sum_{i\geq 0}\tilde{h}_{i}u_{o}^{i}\right)du_{o}
with
\tilde{h}_{0}\neq 0
, and after scaling we obtain the unique
\varpi:=\tilde{h}_{0}^{-1}\tilde{\varpi}
with
\nu_{o}(du_{o}-\varpi)\geq 1
. By Lemma
4.18
, for each
\mathbb{Z}s\neq\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

\mathrm{res}(f\varpi,\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns)=\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}^{\mathcal{U}}(f,\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns)\cdot\mathrm{res}\bigl(\varphi^{\mathbf{P}}_{\omega,n,k}\varpi,\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns\bigr).

By the Residue Theorem
[
8
, Thm. III.7.14.2]
,

\mathrm{res}\bigl(\varphi^{\mathbf{P}}_{\omega,n,k}\varpi,\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns\bigr)=-\mathrm{res}\bigl(\varphi^{\mathbf{P}}_{\omega,n,k}\varpi,ns\bigr)=-c_{1}^{\mathcal{U}}\bigl(\varphi^{\mathbf{P}}_{\omega,n,k},ns\bigr)=d^{\mathcal{U}}_{\omega,k}.

It remains to show that, for each
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\mathrm{res}(f\varpi,ms)=\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}^{\mathcal{U}}(f,ms)\cdot d^{\mathcal{U}}_{\mathbb{Z}s,k}.

A priori we only know that

\mathrm{res}(f\varpi,ms)=\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}^{\mathcal{U}}(f,ms)\cdot\rho_{k-1},

where
\rho_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
are the coefficients occurring in the formal local expansion
\varpi=\left(1+\sum_{i\geq 1}\rho_{i}u_{ms}^{i}\right)du_{ms}
at
ms
. These coefficients
\rho_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
are independent of
m\in\mathbb{Z}
due to the
\tau
-compatibility of
\mathcal{U}
and the translation invariance of
\varpi

[
18
, Prop. III.5.1]
. It suffices to show that
\rho_{k-1}=d^{\mathcal{U}}_{\mathbb{Z}s,k}
from Definition
4.15
. By Lemma
4.12
, this is already proved in case
k=1
, and for the remaining
k\geq 2
we have

0=\mathrm{res}(\hat{\varphi}^{\mathcal{U}}_{\mathbb{Z}s,n,k}\varpi,ns)=\rho_{k-1}\cdot c_{k}^{\mathcal{U}}(\hat{\varphi}^{\mathcal{U}}_{\mathbb{Z}s,n,k},ns)+\rho_{0}\cdot c_{1}^{\mathcal{U}}(\hat{\varphi}^{\mathcal{U}}_{\mathbb{Z}s,n,k},ns)=\rho_{k-1}-d^{\mathcal{U}}_{\mathbb{Z}s,k},

again by the Residue Theorem
[
8
, Thm. III.7.14.2]
.
∎

Remark 4.21
.

The proof of Proposition
4.20
above gives an alternative way to compute the structural constants
d^{\mathcal{U}}_{\mathbb{Z}s,k}
: since
\varpi=\sum_{k\geq 1}d^{\mathcal{U}}_{\mathbb{Z}s,k}u_{o}^{k-1}du_{o}
and
du_{o}=\delta(u_{o})\varpi
, it follows that

\sum_{k\geq 1}d^{\mathcal{U}}_{\mathbb{Z}s,k}u_{o}^{k}=\frac{u_{o}}{\delta(u_{o})}\in u_{o}\mathcal{C}[[u_{o}]].

If
\mathcal{U}
happens to be supercompatible (see Remark
4.14
), then this gives a recipe to compute every
d^{\mathcal{U}}_{\omega,k}\in\mathcal{C}
at once without having to compute every local expansion prescribed by Definition
4.15
.

Theorem 4.22
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{U})
be any
\tau
-pinning of
\mathcal{E}
, and let
f\in{\mathcal{K}}
. Then
f
is elliptically summable if and only if
\pano^{\mathbf{P}}(f,0)=0
,
\pano^{\mathbf{P}}(f,1)=0
, and
\ores^{\mathcal{U}}(f,\omega,k)=0
for every
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}
and
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

First suppose
f=\tau(g)-g
for some
g\in\mathcal{K}
. From the fact that
c_{k}^{\mathcal{U}}(\tau(g),\alpha)=c_{k}^{\mathcal{U}}(g,\alpha\oplus s)
for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{C})
and
k\geq 1
follows the relation
\ores^{\mathcal{U}}(\tau(g),\omega,k)=\ores^{\mathcal{U}}(g,\omega,k)
for every
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}
and
k\geq 1
, and therefore every
\ores(f,\omega,k)=0
. Moreover, since each
\tau\bigl(\varphi^{\mathbf{P}}_{\omega,n,k}\bigr)=\varphi^{\mathbf{P}}_{\omega,n-1,k}
(see Definition
4.10
and Remark
4.11
) and each
\tau\bigl(\zeta^{\mathcal{U}}_{({n},{0})}\bigr)=\zeta^{\mathcal{U}}_{({n-1},{0})}+\zeta^{\mathcal{U}}_{({0},{-1})}
(see Definition
4.7
and Remark
4.8
), applying
\tau
to both sides of the expansion (
4.9
) for
g
, we find that similarly

\displaystyle\pano^{\mathbf{P}}(g,0)

\displaystyle=\pano^{\mathbf{P}}(\tau(g),0)-\zeta^{\mathcal{U}}_{({0},{-1})}\cdot\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\ores^{\mathcal{U}}(g,\omega,k)\cdot d^{\mathcal{U}}_{\omega,k}

\displaystyle=\pano^{\mathbf{P}}(\tau(g),0)

by Corollary
4.19
, and therefore
\pano^{\mathbf{P}}(f,0)=0
. Finally, for the unique invariant differential
\varpi\in H^{0}(\mathcal{E},\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{C}})
such that
\nu_{o}(du_{o}-\varpi)\geq 1
we similarly have that
\mathrm{res}(\tau(g)\varpi,\alpha)=\mathrm{res}(g\varpi,\alpha\oplus s)
, and thus by Proposition
4.20

\pano^{\mathbf{P}}(\tau(g),1)=\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n\cdot\mathrm{res}\bigl(g\varpi,\alpha_{\omega}\oplus(n+1)s\bigr)\\
=\pano^{\mathbf{P}}(g,1)-\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{res}\bigl(g\varpi,\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns\bigr)=\pano^{\mathbf{P}}(g,1),

by the Residue Theorem
[
8
, Thm. III.7.14.2]
. Thus
\pano^{\mathcal{R}}(f,1)=0
.

To prove the converse, let us suppose that every orbital residue and both panorbital residues of
f
are zero. For every
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
k\geq 1
, let
\varphi^{\mathbf{P}}_{\omega,n,k}\in\mathcal{K}
be as in Definition
4.10
, and let us consider

\tilde{f}:=f+\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}^{\mathcal{U}}(f,\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns)\cdot\Bigl(\tau^{n}\bigl(\varphi^{\mathbf{P}}_{\omega,n,k}\bigr)-\varphi^{\mathbf{P}}_{\omega,n,k}\Bigr).

(4.10)

Since
\tau^{n}(\varphi)-\varphi
is summable for any
\varphi\in{\mathcal{K}}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
f
is summable if and only if
\tilde{f}\in{\mathcal{K}}
is summable. Moreover, since each
\tau^{n}\bigl(\varphi^{\mathbf{P}}_{\omega,n,k}\bigr)=\varphi^{\mathbf{P}}_{\omega,0,k}
(see Remark
4.11
), writing
f
as in (
4.9
) yields

\displaystyle\tilde{f}

\displaystyle=0+\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\left(\ores^{\mathcal{U}}(f,\omega,k)\cdot\varphi_{\omega,0,k}^{\mathbf{P}}+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c^{\mathcal{U}}_{k}(f,\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns)\cdot d^{\mathcal{U}}_{\omega,k}\cdot\zeta^{\mathcal{U}}_{({n},{0})}\right)

\displaystyle=0+\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\geq 1}c^{\mathcal{U}}_{k}(f,\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns)\cdot d^{\mathcal{U}}_{\omega,k}\cdot\zeta^{\mathcal{U}}_{({n},{0})},

(4.11)

where the leftmost
0

4.11
) arise from our assumptions that
\pano^{\mathbf{P}}(f,0)
and the
\ores^{\mathcal{U}}(f,\omega,k)=0
all vanish. Next let us again simplify notation by defining

\displaystyle\tilde{c}(n)

\displaystyle:=c_{1}^{\mathcal{U}}(\tilde{f},ns)=\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}^{\mathcal{U}}(f,\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns)\cdot d_{\omega,k}^{\mathcal{U}}\in\mathcal{C}\qquad\text{for}\ n\neq 0;\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle:=c_{1}^{\mathcal{U}}(\tilde{f},{o})=\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\ores^{\mathcal{U}}(f,\omega,k)\cdot d^{\mathcal{U}}_{\omega,k}=0\qquad\text{(cf.~Corollary~\ref{cor:zetaExp-a}),}

so that

in (

) and

. Let us also define the auxiliary functions as in Definition

Remark 4.27
.

The delicate computations in the proof of Lemma
4.25
all rest on a kind of expansion-contraction principle: the basic change-of-variables expansion of the
c^{\mathcal{U}}_{k}(f,\alpha)
in (
4.5
) gets absorbed by the ancillary functions
\varphi^{\mathbf{P}}_{\omega,n,k},\zeta^{\mathcal{U}}_{({n},{0})}\in\mathcal{K}
and constants

engendered by the choice of
\mathbf{P}
, according to (
4.23
), (
4.22
), and (
4.24
), respectively. These special elements privilege the special orbit
\mathbb{Z}s
among all others, and the special basepoint
{o}
in particular, in a way that their Weierstrass and Tate counterparts do not (cf. Remark
3.7
). This helps explain the emergence of the additional correction terms in (

) whenever it happens that
\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}s}={o}
. One way to avoid this this would be to disallow
\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}s}={o}
in the Definition
4.9
– an artificial restriction that we prefer not to make. One could also try to use whatever
\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}s}
is in place of
{o}
in the Definition
4.10
of the
\varphi^{\mathbf{P}}_{\omega,n,k}
, and subsequently in the corresponding analogues of the
\zeta^{\mathcal{U}}_{({\ell},{m})}
in Definition
4.7
, which is probably workable but would have the perverse effect of obscuring the latter elements’ independence from the choice of
\mathcal{R}
. These undesirable consequences seem too high a price to pay for the sake of having the conclusion of Lemma

be independent of the choice of
\mathcal{R}
.

5
Applications

5.1
Reminders and notation

In this section we continue using the notation and assumptions set out at the beginning of Section
4
:
\mathcal{K}
is the field of rational functions on an elliptic curve
(\mathcal{E},{o})
over the algebraically closed field
\mathcal{C}
, which is still allowed to be of arbitrary characteristic, and
\tau
is the automorphism of
\mathcal{K}
induced by the translation on
\mathcal{E}
with respect to a non-torsion
\mathcal{C}
-rational point
s\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{C})
. We neither require nor forbid the existence of a Weierstrass uniformization
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}
of
\mathcal{E}
identifying
\mathcal{K}
with
\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}
as in Section
2
, nor of a Tate uniformization
\mathcal{E}_{q}
of
\mathcal{E}
identifying
\mathcal{K}
with
\mathcal{K}_{q}
as in Section
3
. But we shall work in the general algebraic setting, and explain at appropriate intervals how the general results of this section can be made simpler and more concrete if one has access to the complex/rigid analytic tools that are available in those special situations.

Let us choose once and for all an invariant differential
0\neq\varpi\in H^{0}(\mathcal{E},\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{C}})
, and denote by
\delta
the unique dual derivation on
\mathcal{K}
such that the differential
df=\delta(f)\varpi
for each
f\in\mathcal{K}
. In the Weierstrass setting we choose
\varpi=dz
, so that
\delta=\frac{d}{dz}
, whereas in the Tate setting we choose
\varpi=\frac{dz}{z}
, so that
\delta=z\frac{d}{dz}
.

5.2
An analogue of the Riemann-Roch Theorem

Our goal in this subsection is to prove an analogue of the Riemann-Roch Theorem
4.1
for summable elliptic functions in Theorem
5.3
. We begin by defining some useful variants of usual notions, adapted to our context.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
D:=\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{C})}m_{\alpha}[\alpha]\in\mathrm{Div}(\mathcal{E})
as in (
4.1
).

1.

The
summable Riemann-Roch space
of
D
is

\mathcal{S}(D):=\bigl\{f\in\mathcal{L}(D)\ \big|\ f\ \text{is summable}\bigr\}.

2.

The
\tau
-support
of
D
is

\mathrm{supp}_{\tau}(D):=\{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}\ |\ m_{\alpha}\neq 0\ \text{for some}\ \alpha\in\omega\}.

3.

For
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}
, the
\omega
-component
of
D
is
D_{\omega}:=\sum_{\alpha\in\omega}m_{\alpha}[\alpha]
.

We begin with the following basic preliminary result.

Lemma 5.2
.

If
f\in\mathcal{K}
is summable, then for each
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}
the set of poles of
f
in
\omega
is either empty or else contains at least two elements.

Proof.

The following basic argument also dates back to at least
[
1
]
. A version adapted to the elliptic context is given in
[
5
, p. 196]
, which we follow here. Let
\mathcal{R}=\{\alpha_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}
be a choice of
\tau
-orbit representatives. For
g\in\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{C}
, there exist
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}
;
m,M\in\mathbb{Z}
with
m\leq M
; and

Next is our analogue of the Riemann-Roch Theorem

Theorem 5.3
.

Let

be an effective divisor. For

1.

If

Proof.

Item (1) follows immediately from Lemma

. Supposing

and

for each

as in item (2), let us choose a
\tau
-pinning

of

as in Definition

A general

These parameters are not free, but rather are subject to a single non-trivial linear relation arising from the the Residue Theorem

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(D):=\bigl\{f\in\mathcal{L}(D)\ \big|\ \ores^{\mathcal{U}}(f,\omega,k)=0\ \text{for every}\ \omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}\ \text{and}\ k\geq 1\bigr\}

5.3
Differential integrability and gauge equivalence to constants

A linear difference system of order
N\geq 1
over
(\mathcal{K},\tau)
is one of the form

\tau(Y)=AY\qquad\text{with}\quad A\in\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathcal{K}),

(5.2)

where
Y
denotes a column vector of formal unknowns and
\tau
is applied componentwise. Selecting a cyclic vector in the
(\mathcal{K},\tau)
-module associated with (
5.2
) (see
[
10
, Appendix B]
), one obtains an equivalent homogeneous scalar difference equation of order
N
:

\tau^{N}(y)+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}a_{i}\tau^{i}(y)=0\qquad\text{with}\quad a_{0},\dots,a_{N-1}\in\mathcal{K}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad a_{0}\neq 0.

(5.3)

On the other hand, given a scalar difference equation (
5.3
) one writes its companion system of the form (
5.2
) in the usual way (see
[
22
, p. 5]
).

In case
\mathrm{char}(\mathcal{C})=0
, the differential Galois theory of
[
11
]
associates with (
5.2
) a differential Galois group that encodes in its algebraic structure the differential equations over
\mathcal{K}
that are satisfied by the solutions to (
5.2
), with respect to the
\tau
-invariant derivation
\delta
on
\mathcal{K}
. A fundamental question in this theory is whether the system (
5.2
) can be extended to a compatible difference-differential system

\begin{cases}\tau(Y)=AY&A\in\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathcal{K});\\
\delta(Y)=BY&B\in\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(\mathcal{K});\end{cases}\qquad\text{such that}\quad\delta(A)A^{-1}=\sigma(B)-B.

(5.4)

The system (
5.2
) is called
\delta
-
integrable
if there exists a
B\in\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(\mathcal{K})
as in (
5.4
). Similarly, we say that the scalar equation (
5.3
) is
\delta
-integrable if its associated companion system as in (
5.2
) is
\delta
-integrable, which we see is equivalent to the existence of
b_{1},\dots,b_{N}\in\mathcal{K}
such that

a_{i}\cdot\frac{\delta(a_{0})}{a_{0}}-\delta(a_{i})=\tau(b_{i})-b_{i}\qquad\text{for}\quad i=1,\dots,N,

(5.5)

where we write
a_{N}:=1
.

Whether testing for the summability of each entry of
\delta(A)A^{-1}
in (
5.4
) or of each
a_{i}\frac{\delta(a_{0})}{a_{0}}-\delta(a_{i})
in (
5.5
), we see that Theorem
4.22
mediates an immediate equivalence between computing orbital and panorbital residues and deciding
\delta
-integrability of linear difference equations over
\mathcal{E}
. Our results below go beyond this immediate application of Theorem
4.22
, to show that some of the (pan)orbital obstructions to
\delta
-integrability are redundant already for the simplest first-order (multiplicative) systems and second-order unipotent (additive) systems. This is in concordance with some upcoming interesting results of Charlotte Hardouin and Julien Roques, which they have kindly shared with us in
[
9
]
before their formal dissemination, concerning the precise nature of the meromorphic solutions over
\mathbb{C}
to compatible difference-differential systems (
5.4
) of arbitrary order over complex elliptic curves
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}
.

Although the differential Galois theory for difference equations of
[
11
]
is restricted to characteristic zero, we nonetheless choose to address the integrability of difference equations as discussed above in arbitrary characteristic, even though the “correct” version of differential integrability of a difference system (
5.2
) in arbitrary characteristic
p\geq 0
should presumably be made relative to a system of (iterative) Hasse-Schmidt derivations
\{\delta^{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
(cf.
[
15
]
) that is suitably compatible with
\tau
. Even though we do not pursue this path here explicitly, we consider our results below as initial steps in that direction.

5.3.1
Additive systems

We begin by addressing the simplest case of a difference system as in (
5.2
) of the special form

\tau(Y)=\begin{pmatrix}1&f\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}Y,

(5.6)

for some
0\neq f\in\mathcal{K}
, which is equivalent to the companion system for the homogenization of the inhomogeneous first-order difference equation

\tau(y)-y=f.

(5.7)

For
A:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&f\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
the coefficient matrix in (
5.6
), we compute
\delta(A)A^{-1}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\delta(f)\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. Hence the
\delta
-integrability of (
5.6
) is equivalent to the summability of
\delta(f)
. By Theorem
4.22
, the system (
5.6
) is
\delta
-integrable if and only if the orbital and panorbital residues of
\delta(f)
all vanish. Our goal in this subsection is to prove a new characterization of the
\delta
-integrability of (

) in Theorem
5.4
below.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\mathrm{char}(\mathcal{C})=:p\geq 0
. Let

, let
\mathcal{U}
be a
\tau
-compatible set of local uniformizers as in Definition

1.

\ores^{\mathcal{U}}(f,\omega,k)=0
for every

and

such that

; and

2.

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\bigl(d^{\mathcal{U}}_{\omega,k}\bigr)^{p}\cdot\ores^{\mathcal{U}}(f,\omega,pk)=0

In particular, if
\ores^{\mathcal{U}}(f,\omega,k)=0

The proof of this result will rely on some earlier results and ideas developed in
[
5

, which was the first systematic study of the differential properties of solutions to difference equations over elliptic curves, but is restricted to characteristic zero. Below we give a compact summary of these results and methods, which of them do or do not extend to arbitrary characteristic, and how they compare with our results and methods.

Theorem 5.5

(
[
5
, Prop. B.5 & Prop. B.8]

Suppose
\mathrm{char}(\mathcal{C})=0

Remark 5.6

The implications (3)

Lemma 5.7
.

For any
\alpha\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{C})
and for any

is summable.

Proof.

It suffices to prove this for any one single

the automorphism of
\mathcal{K}
induced by the translation by
\alpha

is summable then so is the arbitrary

, since the automorphism
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}
commutes with

and with

The special case results from an elementary computation, which we abridge below for the reader’s benefit. For
\wp\in\mathcal{L}(2[{o}])-\mathcal{C}

for

Proof.

This follows from the uniqueness properties of

∎

In case

5.3.2
Multiplicative systems

Next we address the case of a first-order difference system as in (
5.2
):

\tau(y)=ay

(5.16)

for some
0\neq a\in\mathcal{K}
. Although seemingly simpler than the higher-order additive systems of the form (
5.6
), but it turns out that these first-order systems present some new challenges. We are able to provide a satisfactory characterization of the
\delta
-integrability of (
5.16
) only in case
\mathrm{char}(\mathcal{C})=0
(see Theorem
5.13
below). In case
\mathrm{char}(\mathcal{C})
our results are less definitive.

The
\delta
-integrability condition (
5.5
) for (

) is equivalent to the summability of
f:=\delta(a)/a
, which by Theorem
4.22
is in turn equivalent to the vanishing of the orbital and panorbital residues of this

. Since
f
depends only on the divisor
\mathrm{div}(a):=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{C})}\nu_{\alpha}(a)
, one would like to have a more meaningful characterization of the
\delta
-integrability of (
5.16
) in terms of
\mathrm{div}(a)
itself. The following result represents partial progress in this direction. We adopt the usual convention that
m\equiv n\pmod{0}
if and only if
m=n
.

Proposition 5.12
.

Let
\mathrm{char}(\mathcal{C})=:p\geq 0
. Let
\mathbf{P}=(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{U})
be a
\tau
-pinning of
\mathcal{E}
as in Definition
4.9
, with
\mathcal{R}=\{\alpha_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}
, and let
a\in\mathcal{K}^{\times}
. Then
\delta(a)/a
is summable if and only if the following conditions hold.

1.

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\omega}\nu_{\alpha}(a)\equiv 0\pmod{p}
for each
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}
;

2.

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n\cdot\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns}(a)\equiv 0\pmod{p}
; and

3.

\pano^{\mathbf{P}}\left(\dfrac{\delta(a)}{a},0\right)=0
.

Proof.

Let us write
f:=\frac{\delta(a)}{a}
. We first prove this in the special case where
\mathcal{U}=\{u_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{C})}
is supercompatible, in the sense of Remark
4.14
, which we can choose such that
\nu_{\alpha}(du_{\alpha}-\varpi)\geq 1
for
every

\alpha\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{C})
, rather than just
\alpha={o}
as in Proposition
4.20
. Then

c^{\mathcal{U}}_{1}(f,\alpha)=\mathrm{res}\left(\frac{da}{a},\alpha\right)\equiv\nu_{\alpha}(a)\pmod{p}

for each
\alpha\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{C})
. Since
f
has at worst first-order poles, condition (1) is equivalent to the vanishing of every
\ores^{\mathcal{U}}(f,\omega,k)
. Similarly, it follows from Proposition
4.20
that condition (2) is equivalent to the vanishing of
\pano^{\mathbf{P}}(f,1)
. Thus by Theorem
4.22
, this concludes the proof of the Proposition
5.12
in the special case, and also proves that the summability of
f
implies all the conditions in the general case.

It follows from the triangularity of the linear system (
4.6
) that the property that all the orbital residues of
f
vanish is independent of the choice of
\mathcal{U}
. Thus if (1) holds then every
\ores^{\mathcal{U}}(f,\omega,k)
vanishes with respect to any
\mathcal{U}
, since they would vanish for the special choice. Similarly, by Lemma
4.25
, the vanishing of
\pano^{\mathbf{P}}(f,1)
is independent of the choice of
\mathcal{U}
. So once again, if condition (2) holds then
\pano^{\mathbf{P}}(f,1)=0
regardless of the choice of
\mathcal{U}
, since it would vanish for the special choice. Finally, although the vanishing of
\pano^{\mathbf{P}}(f,0)
is
not
independent of the choice of
\mathcal{U}
in general, this vanishing is independent of the choice of
\mathcal{U}
for those
f\in\mathcal{K}
such that its orbital residues as well as its first-order panorbital residue all vanish, as we see from Lemma
4.25
and Corollary
4.26
. Thus if conditions (1), (2), and (3) hold for our special choice of
\mathcal{U}
if and only if they all hold for any other choice of
\mathcal{U}
, thus reducing the general case to the special case already proved.
∎

The statement of Proposition
5.12
above is somewhat unsatisfactory, due to the occurrence of the condition (3) that
\pano^{\mathbf{P}}(\delta(a)/a,0)=0
. One expects that this condition should be replaced with another simpler equivalent condition whose dependence on the data in
\mathrm{div}(a)
is less mysterious. In fact, in characteristic zero the condition is entirely superfluous.

Theorem 5.13
.

Suppose
\mathrm{char}(\mathcal{C})=0
. Let
\mathcal{R}=\{\alpha_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}
be a set of
\tau
-orbit
representatives in
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{C})
, as in Definition
4.9
, and let
a\in\mathcal{K}^{\times}
. Then
\delta(a)/a
is summable if and only if the following conditions hold.

1.

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\omega}\nu_{\alpha}(a)=0
for each
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}
; and

2.

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n\cdot\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns}(a)=0
.

Proof.

That conditions (1) and (2) hold if
\delta(a)/a
is summable is already proved in Proposition
5.12
. We prove the opposite implication first in the special case where
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{C}
. In this case we may choose a Tate parameterization
\mathcal{E}_{q}
of
\mathcal{E}
(see
[
17
, §V.1]
). Choose a lift
\hat{s}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that
\check{\hat{s}}=s\in\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{C})
. Let us also upgrade the
\mathcal{R}
in the statement to a
\tau
-pinning
\hat{\mathcal{R}}=\{\hat{\alpha}_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}}\subset\mathbb{C}^{\times}
of
\mathcal{E}_{q}
in the sense of Definition
3.3
, such that
\check{\hat{\alpha}}_{\omega}=\alpha_{\omega}\in\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{C})
for each orbit
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}={\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}
. It is well-known (see e.g.
[
16
, p. 14]
) that we may then express

\begin{gathered}a=\gamma z^{-m}\prod_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}}\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\theta_{q}(\hat{s}^{-n}\hat{\alpha}_{\omega}^{-1}z)^{\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns}(a)};\qquad\text{where}\\
m\in\mathbb{Z}\qquad\text{such that}\quad\prod_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}}\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(\hat{s}^{n}\hat{\alpha}_{\omega})^{\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns}(a)}=q^{m};\end{gathered}

(5.17)

\gamma\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is some irrelevant constant; and the theta function
\theta_{q}(z)
in (
3.1
) satisfies the functional equation
\theta_{q}(qz)=-z^{-1}\theta_{q}(z)
. Then for
\delta=z\frac{d}{dz}
the Euler derivation, we have that

\frac{\delta(a)}{a}=m-\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}_{q}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns}(a)\cdot\zeta_{q}(\hat{s}^{-n}\hat{\alpha}_{\omega}^{-1}z).

We again confirm in this setting that conditions (1) and (2) respectively correspond to the vanishing of all the (Tate) orbital residues and the (Tate) first-order panorbital residue. But now we also obtain that
\pano^{\hat{\mathcal{R}}}\bigl(\frac{\delta(a)}{a},0\bigr)=m
. It remains to show that conditions (1) and (2) imply that
m=0
already. To see this, let us write
f:=\frac{\delta(a)}{a}
as before, and simply note that

q^{m}=\left(\prod_{\omega,n}\hat{s}^{n\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns}(a)}\right)\cdot\left(\prod_{\omega,n}\hat{\alpha}_{\omega}^{\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns}(a)}\right)=\hat{s}^{\pano^{\hat{\mathcal{R}}}(f,1)}\cdot\prod_{\omega}\hat{\alpha}_{\omega}^{\ores(f,\omega,1)}=1.

The general statement for our arbitrary algebraically closed field
\mathcal{C}
of characteristic zero follows from the usual “Lefschetz principle”-type argument: one can replace
\mathcal{C}
with a countable algebraically closed subfield containing all the necessary data (the coordinates of
s
, the coefficients of the Weierstrass equation (
4.2
), the coefficients of
a
as a rational function in
\mathcal{C}(\wp,\wp^{\prime})
), and embed this field in
\mathbb{C}
. The same equation (
4.2
) defines now an algebraic elliptic curve
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}
over
\mathbb{C}
, which one can analytify to obtain the Riemann surface
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathrm{an}}
, which admits a Tate parameterization as above. We can write
f=\frac{\delta(a)}{a}
uniquely as

f=F_{1}(\wp)+F_{2}(\wp)\cdot\wp^{\prime},

where
F_{1}(X),F_{2}(X)\in\mathcal{C}(X)
are rational functions. The analytic proof of the existence of
g(z)\in\mathbb{C}(\wp_{q}(z),\wp_{q}^{\prime}(z))=\mathbb{C}(\wp,\wp^{\prime})
such that
\tau(g)-g=f
implies that there exist rational functions
G_{1}(X),G_{2}(X)\in\mathbb{C}(X)
such that

\bigl(F_{1}(X),F_{2}(X)\bigr)=T\bigl(G_{1}(X),G_{2}(X)\bigr)-\bigl(G_{1}(X),G_{2}(X)\bigr),

(5.18)

where
T:\mathcal{C}(X)\times\mathcal{C}(X)
is the
\mathcal{C}
-rational transformation formally deduced from the algebraic addition-by-
s
formulas on
\mathcal{E}

[
18
, §III.2]
(see also (
5.9
)). After replacing the
\mathbb{C}
-coefficients in the rational expressions for
g=G_{1}(\wp)+G_{2}(\wp)\wp^{\prime}
with formal unknowns, the relation (
5.18
) defines an affine (not necessarily irreducible) algebraic variety over
\mathcal{C}
. Having proved that this variety has a
\mathbb{C}
-point, it must also have a
\mathcal{C}
-point, since
\mathcal{C}
is algebraically closed. Therefore if
f
is summable in
\mathbb{C}(\wp,\wp^{\prime})
, then
f
is also summable in
\mathcal{C}(\wp,\wp^{\prime})=\mathcal{K}
.
∎

In fact, the same argument above also works in arbitrary characteristic, under some supplementary assumptions on
\mathcal{C}
and on
\mathcal{E}
, to show a related one-directional result.

Corollary 5.14
.

Let
\mathcal{R}=\{\alpha_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}
be a set of
\tau
-orbit
representatives in
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{C})
, as in Definition
4.9
, and let
a\in\mathcal{K}^{\times}
. Suppose moreover that
\mathcal{C}
admits a non-Archimedean absolute value
|\ \ |
with respect to which the
j
-invariant
|j(\mathcal{E})|>1
, and that the following conditions hold:

1.

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\omega}\nu_{\alpha}(a)=0
for each
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}
; and

2.

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n\cdot\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns}(a)=0
.

Then
\delta(a)/a
is summable.

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
5.13
, we first prove the result in the special case where
\mathcal{C}
is already complete with respect to
|\ \ |
. The non-integrality condition on the
j
-invariant of
\mathcal{E}
is precisely what is needed in order for
\mathcal{E}
to admit a (unique!) Tate parameterization as
\mathcal{E}_{q}

[
16

. After replacing every instance of
\mathbb{C}
with
\mathcal{C}
, the same argument in the first part of the proof of Theorem
5.13
proves our result in this special case. The proof of the general case follows from the special case by the same argument as in the second part of the proof of Theorem
5.13
, after replacing every instance of
\mathbb{C}
with the completion
\bm{\mathcal{C}}
of
\mathcal{C}
with respect to
|\ \ |
.
∎

Two first-order systems
\tau(y)=ay
and
\tau(y)=a^{\prime}y
as in (
5.16
) are said to be
gauge equivalent
is there exists
r\in\mathcal{K}^{\times}
such that
a=a^{\prime}\frac{\tau(r)}{r}
. The following concluding result, which is again valid in arbitrary characteristic, completely characterizes when the first-order system (
5.16
) is gauge equivalent to a constant system
\tau(y)=cy
with
c\in\mathcal{C}^{\times}
, which is a strictly stronger property than that of being only
\delta
-integrable (see Remark
5.16
below).

Theorem 5.15
.

Let
\mathcal{R}=\{\alpha_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}
be a set of
\tau
-orbit representatives in
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{C})
, as in Definition
4.9
, and let
a\in\mathcal{K}^{\times}
. There exist
r\in\mathcal{K}^{\times}
and
c\in\mathcal{C}^{\times}
such that
a=c\cdot\tau(r)/r
if and only if the following conditions hold:

1.

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\omega}\nu_{\alpha}(a)=0
for each
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}
;

2.

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n\cdot\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns}(a)=0
; and

3.

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n\cdot\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns}(a)\cdot\alpha_{\omega}\oplus\displaystyle\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}\cdot\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns}(a)\cdot s={o}
.

Proof.

Let us denote by
\tau^{*}
the additive endomorphism of
\mathrm{Div}(\mathcal{E})
defined by
\tau^{*}([\alpha])=[\alpha\ominus s]
, so that
\tau^{*}(\mathrm{div}(a))=\mathrm{div}(\tau(a))
. Consider the divisor

D:=\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns}(a)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl[\alpha_{\omega}\oplus is\bigr]-\sum_{n\leq-1}\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns}(a)\sum_{i=n+1}^{0}\bigl[\alpha_{\omega}\oplus is\bigr]\right)

(5.19)

We claim that
D-\tau^{*}(D)=\mathrm{div}(a)
if and only if condition
(1)
holds. It suffices to prove that
D_{\omega}-\tau^{*}(D_{\omega})=\mathrm{div}_{\omega}(a)
, where the
\omega
-component
D_{\omega}
of
D
is as in Definition
5.1
(3), and
\mathrm{div}_{\omega}(a)
similarly denotes the
\omega
-component of
\mathrm{div}(a)
. Indeed, since we can also write

\displaystyle D_{\omega}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i\geq 1}[\alpha_{\omega}\oplus is]\sum\limits_{n\geq i}\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns}(a)-\sum\limits_{i\leq 0}[\alpha_{\omega}\oplus is]\sum\limits_{n\leq i-1}\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns}(a);

\displaystyle\tau^{*}(D_{\omega})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}[\alpha_{\omega}\oplus is]\sum\limits_{n\geq i+1}\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns}(a)-\sum\limits_{i\leq-1}[\alpha_{\omega}\oplus is]\sum\limits_{n\leq i}\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns}(a);

we find that the difference

D_{\omega}-\tau^{*}(D_{\omega})=[\alpha_{\omega}]\sum\limits_{i\neq 0}(-\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus is}(a))+\sum\limits_{i\neq 0}[\alpha_{\omega}\oplus is]\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus is}(a).

This is precisely the divisor of
a
if and only if
\sum\limits_{i\neq 0}(-\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus is}(a))=\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}}(a)
for each orbit
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}
, which is equivalent to condition
(1)
. We also claim that
\deg(D)=0
if and only if condition (2) holds. Indeed, in any case we have

\mathrm{deg}(D)=\sum\limits_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n\cdot\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns}(a).

Finally, we see that condition (3) holds if and only if the evaluation
\mathrm{ev}(D)={o}
. Indeed, in any case we have

\mathrm{ev}(D)=\displaystyle\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n\cdot\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns}(a)\cdot\alpha_{\omega}\oplus\displaystyle\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}\cdot\nu_{\alpha_{\omega}\oplus ns}(a)\cdot s.

Thus by the Abel-Jacobi Theorem
[
18
, Cor. III.3.5]
, if conditions (2) and (3) hold then there exists
r\in\mathcal{K}^{\times}
such that
\mathrm{div}(r)=-D
as in (
5.19
), and if we further have condition (1) then
\mathrm{div}\bigl(a\cdot\frac{r}{\tau(r)}\bigr)=\mathrm{div}(r)-D+\tau^{*}(D)=0
, i.e.,
c:=a\cdot\frac{r}{\tau(r)}\in\mathcal{C}^{\times}
.

Suppose on the other hand that there exist
r\in\mathcal{K}
and
c\in\mathcal{C}^{\times}
such that
a=c\cdot\frac{\tau(r)}{r}
. Then for each
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}/\tau}
, we must have

\sum_{\alpha\in\omega}\nu_{\alpha}(a)=\sum_{\alpha\in\omega}(\nu_{\alpha}(\tau(r))-\nu_{\alpha}(r))=\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{div}_{\omega}(r))-\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{div}_{\omega}(r))=0.

Hence condition (1) is satisfied, and therefore the divisor
D
in (
5.19
) satisfies

D-\tau^{*}(D)=\mathrm{div}(a)=\mathrm{div}(\tau(r))-\mathrm{div}(r)=(-\mathrm{div}(r))-\tau^{*}(-\mathrm{div}(r)).

It follows that
D+\mathrm{div}(r)
is fixed by
\tau^{*}
. But the only fixed point of
\tau^{*}
on
\mathrm{Div}(\mathcal{E})
is the trivial divisor
0
, and therefore
D=-\mathrm{div}(r)
. Then
\mathrm{deg}(D)=-\mathrm{deg}(r)=0
and
\mathrm{ev}(D)=\ominus\mathrm{ev}(\mathrm{div}(r))={o}
, again by the Abel-Jacobi Theorem
[
18
, Cor. III.3.5]
, which as we saw above implies that conditions (2) and (3) are also satisfied.
∎

Remark 5.16
.

It is easy to see that if (
5.16
) is equivalent to a constant system then it is
\delta
-integrable. Indeed, if
a=c\cdot\frac{\tau(r)}{r}
as in Theorem
5.15
then
b=\frac{\delta(r)}{r}
witnesses the
\delta
-integrability condition (
5.5
) for
a_{0}=a
.

For difference systems as in (
5.2
) but over the projective line over
\mathbb{C}
, with
\mathcal{K}
replaced with
\mathbb{C}(x)
, and with
\tau
replaced with one of the “standard” difference operators
x\mapsto x+1
(shift); or
x\mapsto qx
for some
q\in\mathcal{C}^{\times}
of infinite multiplicative order; or
x\mapsto x^{m}
for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
(Mahler); it is proved in the landmark result of
[
20
]
that a suitable notion of differential integrability of the difference system is equivalent to its gauge equivalence to a constant system. Based on this, one might have expected a similar equivalence to hold also for difference equations over elliptic curves.

But as we first learned from Hardouin and Roques
[
9
]
, for a difference system (
5.2
) over an elliptic curve
\mathcal{E}
such as we consider here, its differential integrability no longer implies that it is gauge equivalent to a constant system. In
[
9
]
they showed us the fundamental counterexample to this: the additive system of the form (
5.6
) with
f
the elliptic function in
\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}
described in Lemma
2.1
:

\tau(Y)=\begin{pmatrix}1&\zeta_{\Lambda}(z-s)-\zeta_{\Lambda}(z)\\

MEC-enabled load balancing framework for DFA-IRS aided wearable healthcare networks
Authors: Jarallah Alqahtani, Ashu Taneja, Nayef Alqahtani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe underlying challenges in the wearable electronic market are the limited power and processing capability often resulting in failure to handle complex computations. In order to balance the computational demands with resource constraints, the potential of intelligent reflecting surfaces (IRSs) are exploited in this paper. The paper presents a wearable electronics network in which the wearable nodes communicate with the assistance of double faced active (DFA) IRSs. By simultaneously controlling reflection and transmission links with active amplification, DFA-IRS enables reliable mobile edge computing (MEC)-based task offloading from wearable devices to nearby processing nodes. A resource utilization (RU) algorithm is proposed that associates the devices with DFA-IRSs. The optimal phase shifts of DFA-IRSs are obtained. Further, the impact of transmit power , number of DFA-IRSs , number of DFA-IRS elements N, per element amplification , power budget on the average sum rate of the system is evaluated. It is observed that the DFA-IRS aided system offers average sum rate of 8.2bps/Hz with N =120 and of 20dBm with optimal phase shifts and in the reflection and transmission space respectively. Also, there is an improvement of 5.80% in average sum rate over random phase shifts. The comparison with conventional IRS, single faced active (SFA)-IRS and simultaneously transmitting and reflecting (STAR) IRS is also presented. In the end, the use case of proposed network for personalized healthcare is discussed.Subject terms: Engineering, Health care, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Motivation

Consumer electronics market involve a wide range of electronics devices used by the consumers for communication, entertainment, health and everyday use1. Wearable electronics devices are an important segment of consumer electronics that are gaining popularity owing to its portability, functionality and easy adoption. Smart watches, fitness trackers, smart glasses, AR/VR headsets and wearable cameras offer consumer convenience, personalised experience and lifestyle improvement. These are integrated into number of applications involving health monitoring, fitness tracking, gaming, movies, education2. The advancement of technologies like artificial intelligence (AI), augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR) further boost the capabilities of these consumer electronics. AI powered smart home assistants and voice assistants like Alexa and Siri enhance the user experience by predicting the user behaviors and preferences. These distributed Internet-of-things (IoT) devices, sensors, wearables exchange massive amount of real-time data3. The massive machine type communication (mMTC) suffer from the limited computing capability of energy constrained consumer devices4. Moreover, for real-time data processing and analytics, reliable communication support is another challenge5. To support the uninterrupted data transmission involving massive energy limited consumer electronics devices, there is an urgent need to integrate energy aware solutions in future communication systems6,7.

IRS for 6G enabled consumer electronics

The large number of consumer devices, sensors, IoT devices, wearables require constant data transfer to the cloud servers8. This real-time data transfer is subject to poor connectivity, unfavorable propagation environments, signal degradation owing to interference and bandwidth constraints. This requires dependence on next generation communication networks with advanced capabilities that include high data rate, seamless connectivity and high bandwidth9.

The challenge of poor connectivity and unfavourable propagation environment in 6G can be avoided with the use of promising technology, namely reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RISs). These are intelligent reflecting surfaces (IRSs) that can control the propagation environment by controlling the phase shifts enabling desired reflection10. With its variants, namely, passive IRSs11 and active IRSs12, these enhance the received signal strength at user through controlled reflection ad amplification respectively. The double fading effect in passive IRSs have advanced the research on active IRSs. The literature includes number of works that analyse the performance of active IRS aided communication networks13–15. The capacity is shown to improve by ten times with active IRSs over passive IRSs13. The spectral efficiency and energy efficiency analysis are carried out in14 and15 respectively. Multiple active and passive IRSs aided networks are also investigated in the literature for maximum received signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)16. A hybrid IRS architecture involving passive and active elements is investigated in17 in order to achieve the advantages of both. The half-space coverage in active IRSs is solved with another novel IRS variant, simultanoeusly transmitting and reflecting (STAR) IRS18,19. It offers full space coverage by serving the users in the transmission space and reflection space20. The active STAR IRS is investigated for outage probability in19 while20 integrates STAR IRS in a UAV system in which the trajectory and beamforming are optimised for maximum system performance. The challenge of limited coverage in single faced active IRSs is overcome with double faced active (DFA) IRS. This is a novel variant of IRS which uses element pairs on both sides of a plate to cover the users in backward space also21. It consists of reflection amplifier, power splitting circuit and phase shifters that reflects ad refracts the incident signals after amplification22. The spectral efficiency analysis of DFA-IRS system is carried out in23 which takes into account the power constraint of each amplifier. The authors in24 evaluates the secrecy rate in a DFA-IRS assisted system using the transmit beamforming optimal configuration. Since the research on DFA-IRS is still in its early stages, the researchers are exploring its potential for different application domains.

In this paper, the potential of DFA-IRS is exploited for offering reliable communication support in wearable electronics network. Better the connectivity, faster the data exchange and lesser is the time the device remains active, thereby resulting in longer device lifespan. The paper also proposes resource utilization approach that enables efficient offloading to balance the computational demands in resource constraint wearable device network.

Contributions and outcomes

Wearable devices such as smartwatches, smart glasses, health monitors and fitness trackers suffer from the challenge of limited power and processing capability. The communication bandwidth and storage capability are few other challenges25. These devices fail to handle the complex computations owing to low-power microprocessors or microcontrollers involved in computational tasks that include real-time patient monitoring or AI-driven personalized health analysis. To balance the computational efficiency with resource constraints, the computational tasks like signal processing, real-time analytics need to be offloaded to nearby mobile edge computing (MEC) nodes. In order to enhance the computational offloading in wearable electronics network, this paper presents the use of DFA-IRS that improves the wireless communication efficiency and optimize resource allocation in edge computing environments. Use case scenario of proposed framework in personalized healthcare

This section presents the use case scenario of IRS assisted wearable network for personalized healthcare. Consider a patient suffering from diabetes and hypertension. With the use of wearable devices such as smart watch, fitness tracker, glucose monitor, smart patch, medical sensors, the vital health parameters like heart rate, glucose level, physical activity, blood pressure are continuously collected. This real-time health data collection and processing require reliable and seamless communication support27. However, the wearable electronics network is vulnerable to signal blockages, interference and limited bandwidth. To support the wireless transmission from the wearable devices, the DFA-IRSs are deployed in the wearable network. These optimize the wireless signals and enhance the connectivity. The real-time health data of the patient is analyzed with the help of sophisticated AI models to predict the health risks. The high risks of hypertension or hyperglycemia are predicted. Limitations and practical considerations

While the proposed DFA-IRS-assisted MEC framework shows significant improvements in system performance, several practical limitations exist that are discussed below:

The proposed approach assumes quasi-static channel conditions, however in high user mobility scenarios, the channel coherence time reduces significantly. Frequent reconfiguration of DFA-IRS elements in such dynamic environments can introduce additional signaling overhead and latency. To address this, mobility-aware and predictive phase optimization schemes, can be developed by leveraging machine learning or channel prediction techniques to adapt DFA-IRS configurations in real time.

The proposed approach assumes quasi-static channel conditions, however in high user mobility scenarios, the channel coherence time reduces significantly. Frequent reconfiguration of DFA-IRS elements in such dynamic environments can introduce additional signaling overhead and latency. To address this, mobility-aware and predictive phase optimization schemes, can be developed by leveraging machine learning or channel prediction techniques to adapt DFA-IRS configurations in real time.

The framework relies on accurate channel state information (CSI), which is difficult to obtain in practice due to estimation errors and feedback delays. Imperfect CSI may result in suboptimal phase alignment and reduced gains. To overcome this, robust CSI-based algorithms or data-driven approaches can be designed.

Although MEC-based offloading alleviates the computational burden on wearable devices, the end-to-end latency also depends on edge server processing and queueing delays. Under heavy traffic conditions, these delays may offset the benefits of offloading. To address this, joint communication–computation optimization, including task scheduling, load balancing across multiple edge servers, and latency-aware offloading strategies can be implemented.

Conclusion

Owing to limited power and processing capability, the wearable devices fail to handle the complex computations. Thus, balancing the computational demands with resource constraints is very important in wearable electronics network. This paper presents a wearable electronics network that leverages DFA-IRS technology to offer enhanced connectivity and reliable task offloading. The optimized phase shifts of DFA-IRS are obtained such that the wearable nodes in the reflection and transmission spaces are served. Further, a resource utilization algorithm is proposed that associates nodes with DFA-IRSs for efficient resource utilization. It is observed that the DFA-IRS with proposed RU algorithm offers 5.80% gain in average sum rate with optimal phase control over random phase control. For a given power budget , the sum rate of the system decreases with increase in the distance between AP and DFA-IRS. At transmit power of 20 dBm, the average sum rate is found to be 13.5 bps/Hz. Increasing the number of IRSs increases the sum rate. This is owing to more degrees of freedom offered with more . There is a gain of 12.5% in average sum rate with = 4 over = 2. The average power usage to achieve a particular target rate is also evaluated for different IRS aided systems. The comparison of DFA-IRS, SFA-IRS and STAR-IRS system models with varying suggests that the DFA-IRS aided system outperforms the SFA-IRS system and STAR-IRS system. It achieves average sum rate of 22.5 bps/Hz at of 20 dB. While the SFA-IRS offers a gain of 16.6% in sum rate over STAR IRS.

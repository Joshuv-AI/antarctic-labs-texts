Dietary AFETOXIN-BD supplementation mitigates multi-mycotoxin effects on performance, egg quality, and health status of late-laying hens
Authors: Luke Liu, Yongqiang Wang, Shuangping Wang, Alison Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Mycotoxins in poultry feed remain a major challenge to laying hen productivity, mainly in the late laying period when hens are at a higher risk of oxidative stress, compromised gut integrity and reduced reproductive performance. The efficacy of a commercial mycotoxin degrading preparation (AFETOXIN-BD) in reducing the negative impact of multi-mycotoxin contaminated feeds was evaluated. Two hundred 54-week-old Hy-Line Brown laying hens were randomly allotted to four dietary treatments (5 replicates of 10 hens) and fed for 35 days: a control diet (CON), a mycotoxin-contaminated diet (MT), CON plus 1,000 g/ton AFETOXIN-BD (BD), or MT plus 1,000 g/ton AFETOXIN-BD (MT+BD). The MT diet significantly (P < 0.05) reduced egg production rate, daily egg mass, eggshell strength, Haugh unit, antioxidant enzyme activities (T-SOD, GSH-Px, T-AOC), beneficial lactic acid bacteria populations and general health status of the hens, while increasing (P < 0.05) the feed-to-egg ratio, malondialdehyde (MDA) content, pathogenic bacteria counts (E. coli and Salmonella spp.), and the presence of mycotoxin residues in the eggs. Adverse effects were effectively mitigated (P < 0.05) in the MT+BD diet, while the BD diet exhibited better performance than the CON diet. Hens fed with BD diet showed (P < 0.05) superior production, egg quality, antioxidant activity, and intestinal microbial balance compared to all dietary treatments. From these results, it can be concluded that 0.1% AFETOXIN-BD supplementation is an effective nutritional intervention to mitigate the toxicity of multi-mycotoxin including AFB1, ZEN, FB1, OTA, and DON toxicity and improve productive efficiency, egg quality, and food safety in late-phase laying hens under commercial conditions, while also prolonging the productive lifespan.

Introduction

Mycotoxins are secondary toxic metabolites formed by fungi and are common in poultry diets around the world (Gurikar et al., 2023). In poultry diet, the most common and harmful mycotoxins are AFB1, deoxynivalenol (DON), zearalenone (ZEN), ochratoxin A (OTA) and fumonisin B1 (FB1). These mycotoxins can cause noteworthy poultry health, productivity, egg quality and food safety issues (Fakhri et al., 2025). Worldwide, around 25% of food crops are adulterated with mycotoxins each year, causing noteworthy economic losses (Eskola et al., 2020).

AFB1 is regarded a highly toxic aflatoxins (Yunus et al., 2011). The most detected mycotoxins are DON and its acetylated metabolites that can harm both human and bird health, causing symptoms such as abdominal pain, fever, nausea, headache, diarrhea and vomiting (Sobrova et al., 2010). Furthermore, ZEN has a similar structure to some natural estrogen. This property of ZEN is responsible for its binding to oestrogen receptors and causes anabolic and estrogenic effects in animals (Rogowska et al., 2019). Furthermore, AFB1, DON and ZEN have been reported to impair antioxidant capacity in poultry.

The late laying period (usually >50 weeks of age) is physiologically challenging for laying hens, involving a reduction in egg production persistency, egg quality, and immunity (Lee et al., 2025). During this period, mycotoxin exposure is further intensified by disrupting the reliability and purpose of the intestinal barrier, affecting nutrient uptake, oxidative stress, and the balance of reproductive hormones (Ma et al., 2025; Yuan et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2021). Consequently, decreases in egg production, eggshell strength, Haugh unit, and flock health are often reported (Arulnathan et al., 2024).

Mycotoxin-degrading preparations have emerged as a practical and effective strategy to mitigate these adverse effects (Kihal et al., 2022). Some of these additives bind mycotoxins within the gastrointestinal tract, thus decreasing the bioavailability and the systemically toxic effects of mycotoxins, and some might also help maintain gut microbiota balance and antioxidant status (De Mil et al., 2017).

Given the heightened susceptibility of late-laying hens to mycotoxins, effective mitigation strategies are critically needed. This research aimed to compare the alleviating impact of a commercial mycotoxin degrading preparation in 54-week-old Hy-Line Brown laying hens fed a normal diet and a mouldy (mycotoxin contaminated) diet. Production performance, egg quality, antioxidant capacity, intestinal microbiota, egg contamination with mycotoxins, and health status were the important parameters that were evaluated. This research provides valuable insights for the poultry industry, demonstrating how targeted supplementation can extend productive life, improve egg quality and safety, and enhance economic outcomes in late-laying flocks.

Therefore, the current research hypothesized that the addition of 0.1% AFETOXIN-BD (a commercial mycotoxin degrading preparation) would effectively mitigate the adverse effects of multi-mycotoxin contaminated feed on overall performance, including production, egg quality, antioxidant status, intestinal microbiota balance, mycotoxin residues in eggs, and overall health in late-phase laying hens.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics statement

The experimental protocol was approved by the Animal Welfare Committee of Tangshan Finely Animal Care Co., Ltd., Tangshan, Hebei, China (reference no. TFAC-LH-2026-01). Birds were managed in accordance with the Guide for the Care and Use of Agricultural Animals in Research and Teaching (4th ed., 2020).

Experimental design.

Birds were kept in 3 tier battery cages. Each individual cage measures 40 cm in length and 45 cm in depth. Its front height is 45 cm, rear height 38 cm, with the base slope ranging from 6° to 8°. Feed and water were given freely. Feeding was done twice daily (at 08:00 and 15:00 h) and eggs were picked every day at 16:00 h. The three-day pre-experimental adaptation period was followed by 35-day experimental period.

Experimental diets

All experimental diets were formulated to meet the nutritional standards of laying hens in the late laying period (National Research Council, 1994). Four dietary treatments were used: a control diet (CON), a mycotoxin-contaminated diet (MT), CON supplemented with 1,000 g/ton AFETOXIN-BD (BD), and MT supplemented with 1,000 g/ton AFETOXIN-BD (MT+BD). The commercial mycotoxin-degrading preparation AFETOXIN-BD was manufactured by Tangshan Finely Animal Care Co., Ltd., China, and is composed of carefully selected probiotics and mycotoxin-degrading enzymes. Production performance

The production performance parameters included daily egg collection, egg weight (EW), feed consumption, cracked eggs, and mortality were recorded. The residual feed was calculated on a weekly basis. Furthermore, egg production, average daily feed intake and egg weight, feed-to-egg ratio, cracked egg rate, and mortality rate were calculated using the following formulas (Ma et al., 2025).

Egg quality

At the end of each week, three eggs from each replicate were arbitrarily selected to assess egg quality. An egg analyser (EMT-7300, Robotmation Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) was used to measure EW, albumen height (AH), Haugh unit (HU) and yolk color (YC). The strength of eggshells (ESS) was measured with a digital eggshell strength tester (AC220, Orka Company, Shanghai, China). The eggshell thickness without the inner membrane was taken at three points (blunt end, equator and sharp end) by an eggshell thickness gauge (Guilin Measuring Tool Cutting Tool Co., Ltd., Guilin, China) and then the mean value was recorded. Egg shape index, eggshell ratio (%) and yolk ratio (%) were measured as decribed by (Iqbal et al., 2025).

Antioxidant parameters

On the 35th day of the trial, five birds per treatment were randomly selected for antioxidant parameters. Approximately 5 mL blood samples were collected in anti-coagulant tubes from the wing vein, then centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 10 min, and the plasma was frozen at −20°C until it was analysed. The concentrations of total superoxide dismutase (T-SOD), glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px), catalase (CAT), malondialdehyde (MDA), and total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC) were measured in the plasma by using Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China commercial kits (Hussain et al., 2025).

Intestinal microbiota composition

On d 35, one hen per replicate was randomly selected and euthanized by cervical dislocation, and the ceca were removed under sterile conditions. The digesta contents were diluted and plated on selective media to determine the microbial populations of Escherichia coli, Salmonella and lactic acid bacteria according to the protocol (Sedaghat and Torshizi, 2017).

Mycotoxin residues

To determine the presence of residues of mycotoxins in eggs, two eggs per replicate were arbitrarily collected at week 2. On the 35th day of the trial, the eggs were boiled for 15 minutes at 100°C. After cooling, the yolk and the egg white were separated and pooled for the determination of AFB1, DON, ZEA, OTA, and FB1 residues using LC-MS as performed by (Omar et al., 2023)

Health status

The general health status (consistency of faeces, condition of feathers, color of comb and hen behaviour) was noted and recorded daily.

Statistical analysis

Experimental data were analysed using one way ANOVA, using SPSS software (version 25.0, SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Tukey’s post-hoc test was used for multiple comparisons. Data are given as mean ± SE (standard error). All differences were deemed to be statistically significant at a P < 0.05.

Health status

Observations of health status made daily throughout the experimental period showed that the hens receiving the MT diet were in the worst condition regarding their health status, which included loose stools, passage of feed, pale combs, and poor feathering. The healthy overall condition of the birds in the BD group was indicated by normal faecal consistency, feed consumption, ruddy combs, and glossy feathers.

Discussion

The present study illustrates that multi-mycotoxin contamination (AFB1, ZEN, FB1, OTA, and DON) of late-phase laying hen diets causes systemic toxicity, as manifested by reduced production performance, poor egg quality, oxidative stress, gut dysbiosis, egg contamination with mycotoxins, and health deterioration. The adverse effects were mitigated through dietary supplementation with a clay mineral plus yeast cell wall extract (AFETOXIN-BD). Interestingly, the binder-supplemented diet without mycotoxin challenge was superior in several parameters, which indicates that there may be direct prebiotic and antioxidant effects in addition to the adsorption of toxins.

Mycotoxin challenge caused a significant reduction in egg production rate and EM, and a significant increase in feed: egg ratio (Zhao et al., 2021). These effects are mechanistically related to hepatorenal toxicity, impaired protein synthesis in the liver, impaired energy metabolism, and endocrine interference, which involves the effect of ZEN as a xenoestrogen on the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis. Susceptibility is increased by age-related ovarian decline in late-phase hens (Siloto et al., 2011; Yuan et al., 2022). AFETOXIN-BD restored performance parameters to levels comparable to or higher than the control, in large part due to its adsorptive properties: aluminosilicates bind polar toxins through cation exchange, while yeast β-glucans bind non-polar mycotoxins (ZEN and DON), which lowers their bioavailability. This is coherent with the results of Zhao et al. (2021) and a recent meta-analysis indicating that egg production increased by 4.2% and EW by 1.37 g when hens were fed the yeast cell wall extracts under mycotoxin challenge (Weaver et al., 2024). The better digestibility and gut health in the BD group suggests that yeast-derived mannan-oligosaccharides improve nutrient digestibility and gut health, whether they bind toxins (Baek et al., 2024).

Mycotoxins negatively affected eggshell strength and Haugh units, possibly because of disturbed calcium and phosphorus metabolism, inflammation of the uterine shell glands, and decreased albumen protein synthesis (Wei et al., 2024). AFETOXIN-BD completely recovered these parameters, and the BD group had better values than the control group. This improvement could be due to decreased intestinal inflammation, improved calcium absorption, and decreased oxidative damage to the intestinal epithelium (Zhao et al., 2021). These improvements are beneficial for both internal egg production and marketability.

The activities of T-SOD, GSH-Px, and T-AOC were significantly reduced, and MDA was significantly increased because of mycotoxin challenge, suggesting severe oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction. These modifications align with previously reported alterations in lipid peroxidation and glutathione redox systems in laying hens following Fusarium mycotoxins (Kulcsár et al., 2021, 2024). AFETOXIN-BD has successfully countered this oxidative imbalance by controlling the absorption of toxins. The antioxidant status of the unchallenged BD group is also improved, underscoring the AFETOXIN-BD's intrinsic ability to enhance endogenous antioxidant mechanisms.

The consumption of the mycotoxin-contaminated diet caused an increase in the quantity of pathogenic E. coli and Salmonella spp. and a decrease in that of lactic acid bacteria. AFETOXIN-BD was used to restore the microbiota by adsorbing toxins (preserving tight junction proteins like ZO-1 and claudin-1) and by prebiotic effects on the growth of Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium (Yuan et al., 2022; Zhai et al., 2022).

The detectable residues of AFB1, DON, ZEN,and OTA detectable residues in eggs of mycotoxin challenged group supports systemic absorption and transfer to developing oocytes (Antonissen, 2025). AFETOXIN-BD significantly lowered the residue levels for all toxins, with the lowest residues being found in the BD group. This decrease is important in terms of food security and public health because it enables the possibility of more efficient use of water and alleviates scarcity (Fakhri et al., 2021; Heuel et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2023).

The MT group of health status hens had loose stools, undigested feed passed, pale combs and poor feathering, all of which were clinical signs of maldigestion, anaemia, oxidative stress and chronic inflammation. The results showed notable differences in faecal consistency, comb colour, and feather condition AFETOXIN-BD led to healthier, normal looking faeces, ruddy combs, and shiny feathers which highlighting significant enhancements in gut integrity, nutrient utilization and overall welfare.

Conclusion

To conclude, the supplementation of 0.1% AFETOXIN-BD during the late phase of egg-laying was effective in mitigating the adverse effects of multi-mycotoxin contamination in laying hens by enhancing production performance, egg quality, antioxidant status, gut microbiota balance, and decreasing egg mycotoxin residues. The binder was also effective in the unchallenged poultry, suggesting that it also has prebiotic and antioxidant properties. The results support the use of AFETOXIN-BD as a useful tool to optimize productivity, egg safety and hen health in the late phase of laying.

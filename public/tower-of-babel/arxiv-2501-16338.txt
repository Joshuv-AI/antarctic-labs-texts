A converse theorem for quasi-split even special orthogonal groups over finite fields
Alexander Hazeltine

Abstract
. We prove a converse theorem for the case of quasi-split non-split even special orthogonal groups over finite fields. There are two main difficulties which arise from the outer automorphism and non-split part of the torus. The outer automorphism is handled similarly to the split case, while new ideas are developed to overcome the non-split part of the torus.

Introduction

Let

F

G:=\mathrm{G}(F).
Converse theorems aim to classify certain representations of

\gamma

For the moment, let

\pi

\gamma

\gamma(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi)
where

, using Rankin–Selberg convolutions
[

[

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
be irreducible generic representations
of
\mathrm{GL}_{l}(F)
with the same central character.
If

\gamma(s,\pi_{1}\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(s,\pi_{2}\times\tau,\psi),

The remaining case for classical groups over finite fields is that of quasi-split non-split

Hereinafter, by “quasi-split,” we mean quasi-split non-split as the analogous results for split case are contained in

In this paper, we develop new ideas in order to overcome these obstacles and prove the converse theorem. That is, we define the twisted gamma factors

(see Proposition

Theorem 1.2

(The Converse Theorem for quasi-split
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
, Theorem

)
.

Let
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
be irreducible cuspidal
\psi
-generic representations of the quasi-split group
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}(F)
with the same central character. If

\gamma(\pi\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(\pi^{\prime}\times\tau,\psi),

for all irreducible generic representations
\tau
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
with
n\leq l,

then
\pi\cong\pi^{\prime}
or
\pi\cong\pi^{\prime}{}^{c},

where
c
is the outer automorphism.

Theorem
1.2
and Corollaries
6.4
and
7.4
imply that twisted gamma factors are unable to distinguish irreducible generic cuspidal representations
\pi
and
\pi^{c}
of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}(F)
, which is a unique phenomenon for
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
among all the classical groups. This was also viewed in the split case in
[
11
]
. Over non-Archimedean fields, this is consistent with Arthur’s work on the local Langlands correspondence and the local Langlands functoriality, and the work of Jiang and Soudry on local descent for even special orthogonal groups (see
[
2
]
and
[

). It remains an open problem to find a set of invariants which can distinguish between
\pi
and
\pi^{c}
. For split
\mathrm{SO}_{4}
over a
p

[
32
]
using twisted exterior square local
\gamma
-factors, but remains open in higher rank (see the discussion at the end of
[
32

or
[
24

Now, we introduce the key ideas needed to prove Theorem
1.2
. Recall that there are two major difficulties in our case, the outer automorphism, which we will denote by
c

(see §
2
for a definition), and the quasi-split torus. The outer automorphism is handled in a similar manner to the split case considered in
[
11
]
. Namely, instead of considering solely the normalized Bessel function
\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}
of
\pi
(see §
4
), we also involve the normalized Bessel function
\mathcal{B}_{\pi^{c},\psi}
of
\pi^{c}
(specifically, we do this when we twist by
\mathrm{GL}_{l}(F)
) to establish the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
8.1
)
.

Let
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
be irreducible cuspidal
\psi
-generic representations of the quasi-split group
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}(F)
with the same central character. If

\gamma(\pi\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(\pi^{\prime}\times\tau,\psi),

for all irreducible generic representations
\tau
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
with
1\leq n\leq l,

then we have that

({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}+{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{c},\psi})(g)=({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime},\psi}+{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime}{}^{c},\psi})(g)

for any
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}(F)
.

While the result is similar to the split case, the proofs have two main differences. The first one is that in the split case, the outer conjugation acts nontrivially on some of the simple roots and Weyl elements, while for the non-split case, the outer conjugation acts trivially on all the simple roots and Weyl elements. Consequently, the outer conjugation only nontrivially affects the torus and its effect is to partition the Bruhat cells corresponding to certain Weyl elements into two parts: those elements which are fixed by the outer conjugation and those which do not. More specifically, we see that the twists up to
\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}(F)
determines the elements which are fixed by the outer conjugation (Theorem
6.2
) while the twists by
\mathrm{GL}_{l}(F)
determines the elements which are not fixed by the outer conjugation (Theorem
7.3
).

The second main difference is presented by the non-split part of the maximal torus. Indeed, in the split case, normalized Bessel functions
\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}
are nonzero on the part of the torus which is precisely the center (
[
11
, Lemma 4.3]
). The proof of this relies on the fact that, for split groups, the center is precisely the intersection of all of the kernels of the simple roots. This fact is not true for quasi-split groups. In the quasi-split case, we still have that normalized Bessel functions
\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}
are nonzero on the part of the torus which is precisely the center (Lemma
4.3
); however, the proof requires a more careful analysis of the effect of conjugating certain unipotent elements by elements of the torus. This careful analysis must also be carried out in other arguments where properties of the Bessel functions are examined in conjunction with elements of root subgroups. Note that, unlike split groups, the root subgroups of quasi-split groups are not necessarily 1-dimensional or even commutative in general.

2
, the relevant groups, representations, and notions are introduced. In §
3
, we establish multiplicity one results (Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
) and define the zeta integrals and
\gamma
-factors. In §
4
, the Bessel functions are defined and relevant properties are collected. In §
5
, §
6
, and §
7
we study the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
twists,
1\leq n\leq l
, and determine the relation between the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
twists and the Bessel functions (Theorems
5.4
,
6.2
,
7.3
). In §
8
, we prove Theorem
1.3
and the converse theorem (Theorem
1.2
).

2.
The groups and representations

Let
n,l\in\mathbb{N}
and
q=p^{r}
for some odd prime number
p
. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field of
q
elements and fix a nontrivial additive character
\psi
on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Let
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
to be the group
n\times n
of matrices with entries in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and non-zero determinant. Let
I_{n}
be the identity element,

J_{n}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&0&\cdots&0&1\\
0&0&\cdots&1&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\iddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&1&\cdots&0&0\\
1&0&\cdots&0&0\\
\end{matrix}\right),

and, for
\rho\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,

J_{2l,\rho}=\mathrm{diag}(I_{l-1},\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
-\rho&0\end{matrix}\right),I_{l-1})\cdot J_{2l}.

We set
\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2n+1}\,|\,\mathrm{det}(g)=1,{}^{t}gJ_{2n+1}g=J_{2n+1}\}
and
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}(J_{2l,\rho})=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2l}\,|\,\mathrm{det}(g)=1,{}^{t}gJ_{2l,\rho}g=J_{2l,\rho}\}
. We often simply write this as
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}.

\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
is split if
\rho\in(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{2}
and is quasi-split, but not split, otherwise (in this case
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
splits over the quadratic extension
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\sqrt{\rho})
). Hereinafter, we fix
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
to be the quasi-split (non-split) group
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}(J_{2l,\rho})
for a fixed
\rho\notin(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{2}.

Let
U_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}
and
U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
be the subgroups of upper triangular matrices in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
and
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
, respectively. Fix
B_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}=T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
to be the standard Borel subgroup of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
where
T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
consists of all elements of the form

t=\mathrm{diag}(t_{1},\dots,t_{l-1},\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\rho\\
b&a\end{matrix}\right),t_{l-1}^{-1},\dots,t_{1}^{-1}),

where
t_{1},\dots,t_{l-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
a^{2}-b^{2}\rho=1.
Note that
T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
is a maximal torus in
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}.

Set

c=\mathrm{diag}(I_{l-1},1,-1,I_{l-1}).

Note that
c\notin\mathrm{SO}_{2l};
however,
c\mathrm{SO}_{2l}c^{-1}=c\mathrm{SO}_{2l}c=\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
. Given a representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l},
we define a new representation
\pi^{c}
of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
by
\pi^{c}(g)=\pi(cgc).

We now discuss the embeddings needed to define the zeta integrals later. These are the analogues for finite fields of the local cases defined by Kaplan in
[
20
,
21
]
. If
n<l
we embed
\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}
into
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
via

\left(\begin{matrix}A&B&C\\
D&E&K\\
L&P&Q\end{matrix}\right)\mapsto\mathrm{diag}(I_{l-n-1},M^{-1}\left(\begin{matrix}A&&B&C\\
&1&&\\
D&&E&K\\
L&&P&Q\end{matrix}\right)M,I_{l-n-1})

where
A
and
Q
are
n\times n
matrices and

M=\mathrm{diag}(I_{n},\left(\begin{matrix}0&2\\
1&0\end{matrix}\right),I_{n})

This embeds
\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}
into the standard Levi subgroup
\mathrm{GL}_{l-n-1}\times\mathrm{SO}_{2n+2}
of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
.

Set
\gamma=\frac{\rho}{2}.
If
l=n
, we embed
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
into
\mathrm{SO}_{2l+1}
via

\left(\begin{matrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{matrix}\right)\mapsto M^{-1}\left(\begin{matrix}A&&B\\
&1&\\
C&&D\end{matrix}\right)M

where
A,B,C,
and
D
are
l\times l
matrices and

M=\mathrm{diag}(I_{l-1},\left(\begin{matrix}0&1&0\\
\frac{1}{2}&0&\frac{1}{2\gamma}\\
\frac{1}{2}&0&\frac{-1}{2\gamma}\end{matrix}\right),I_{l-1})

Note that the embedding takes the torus
T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
to a torus in
\mathrm{SO}_{2l+1}
, but not the standard torus. Indeed, the embedding takes
t
(as above) to

\mathrm{diag}(s,\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{2}(1+a)&b&\frac{1}{2\gamma}(1-a)\\
\gamma b&a&-b\\
\frac{\gamma}{2}(1-a)&-\gamma b&\frac{1}{2}(1+a)\end{matrix}\right),s^{*})

where
s=\mathrm{diag}(t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{l-1})
.

Next, we define when a representation is be generic. Recall that
U_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}
and
U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
are the subgroups of upper triangular matrices in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
and
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
respectively and that we fixed an additive nontrivial character
\psi
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We abuse notation and define a generic character, which we will also call
\psi
, on
U_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}
and
U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
. For
u=(u_{i,j})_{i,j=1}^{n}\in U_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}
, we set
\psi(u)=\psi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}u_{i,i+1}\right).
For
u=(u_{i,j})_{i,j=1}^{2l}\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}},
we set

\psi(u)=\psi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l-2}u_{i,i+1}+\frac{1}{2}u_{l-1,l+1}\right).

We say an irreducible representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
is
\psi
-generic if

\mathrm{Hom}_{U_{SO_{2l}}}(\pi,\psi)\neq 0.

Similarly, we say an irreducible representation
\tau
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
is
\psi
-generic if

\mathrm{Hom}_{U_{GL_{n}}}(\tau,\psi)\neq 0.

A nonzero intertwining operator in these spaces is called a Whittaker functional and it is well known that Whittaker functionals are unique up to scalars (by uniqueness of Whittaker models). Fix
\Gamma\in\mathrm{Hom}_{U_{SO_{2l}}}(\pi,\psi)
to be a nonzero Whittaker functional. For
v\in\pi
, let
W_{v}(g)=\Gamma(\pi(g)v)
for any
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
and set
\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)=\{W_{v}\,|\,v\in\pi\}.
The space
\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
is called the
\psi
-Whittaker model of
\pi.
By Frobenius reciprocity,
\mathrm{Hom}_{U_{SO_{2l}}}(\pi,\psi)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{SO_{2l}}(\pi,\mathrm{Ind}_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}}^{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}(\psi)).
Thus,
\pi
can be realized as a subrepresentation of
\mathrm{Ind}_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}}^{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}(\psi)
via the map
\pi\rightarrow\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
given by
v\mapsto W_{v}.
Moreover, by uniqueness of Whittaker models, this subrepresentation occurs with multiplicity one inside
\mathrm{Ind}_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}}^{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}(\psi)
. We also note that the analogous results hold for
\psi
-generic representations
\tau
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}.

Let
Q_{n}=L_{n}V_{n}
be the standard Siegel parabolic subgroup of
\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}
with Levi subgroup
L_{n}\cong\mathrm{GL}_{n}.
For
a\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}
we let
l_{n}(a)=\mathrm{diag}(a,1,a^{*})\in L_{n}
where
a^{*}=J_{n}{}^{t}a^{-1}J_{n}.
Let
\tau
be an irreducible
\psi^{-1}
-generic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
and set
I(\tau)=\mathrm{Ind}_{Q_{n}}^{\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}}\tau.
An element
\xi\in I(\tau)
is a function
\xi:\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}\rightarrow\tau
satisfying

\xi(l_{n}(a)ug)=\tau(a)\xi(g),\forall a\in\mathrm{GL}_{n},u\in V_{n},g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}.

Let
\Lambda_{\tau}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{U_{GL_{n}}}(\tau,\psi^{-1})
be a fixed nonzero homomorphism. For
\xi\in I(\tau)
, let
f_{\xi}:\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be the function given by

f_{\xi}(g,a)=\Lambda_{\tau}(\tau(a)\xi(g)).

Let
I(\tau,\psi^{-1})
be the space of functions generated by
f_{\xi},\xi\in I(\tau).
Note that for
f\in I(\tau,\psi^{-1}),
we have

f(g,ua)=\psi^{-1}(u)f(g,a),\forall g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1},u\in U_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}},a\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}.

We will also let
\tau^{*}
be the contragradient representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
defined by
\tau^{*}(a)=\tau(a^{*}).

3.
Multiplicity one theorems and the gamma factor

The goal of this section is to show that Bessel models for even quasi-split special orthogonal groups over finite fields are unique. The analogous results in the split case are
[
11
, Propositions 3.1 and 3.2]
. The quasi-split case follows from similar arguments. We illustrate the proofs here for convenience. Our primary reference for the setup is
[
21
]
. Note that the notation of this section matches with the notation in the setup of
[
7
]
with
H=N^{l-n}\times\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}
and
\nu=\psi_{\gamma}
in the case
l>n
and
H=N_{0}\times\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
and
\nu=\psi_{\gamma}
in the case
l=n
(in both cases
\psi_{\gamma}
is extended to be trivial on the special orthogonal group). Moreover, in the case
l=n,

N_{0}
and
\nu
are both trivial.

3.1.
The case
l=n

Assume
l=n.

In this case, the unipotent piece
N_{0}
is trivial and

is
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
embedded inside of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l+1}
as in the previous section.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
Q=LV
be a parabolic subgroup of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l+1}
and
\sigma
be an irreducible representation of
L.
Let
\pi
be an irreducible cuspidal representation of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
and
\tau=\mathrm{Ind}_{Q}^{\mathrm{SO}_{2l+1}}\sigma

\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}(\pi,\tau)\leq 1.

Proof.

Note that Proposition 5.1 of
[
6
]
holds in our case. Indeed, we can follow their proof closely, except instead of using the multiplicity one result of
[
1
]
, we use an analogous result for special orthogonal groups from

]

Now suppose that
\sigma
is not cuspidal. Then there exists a parabolic subgroup

of

\sigma^{\prime}
of

such that

By transitivity of parabolic induction,
\tau\subseteq\mathrm{Ind}_{L^{\prime}V^{\prime}V}^{\mathrm{SO}_{2l+1}}\sigma^{\prime}\otimes 1_{V}
and hence the claim follows from the case where

∎

The case

.
∎

3.3.
The Integrals

Now, let
\pi
be an irreducible
\psi
-generic cuspidal representation of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
and
\tau
be an irreducible
\psi^{-1}
-generic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}.
Let
W\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
and
f\in I(\tau,\psi^{-1})
. Next, we define “integrals”
\Psi(W,f)
analogous to the local integrals of
[
21
]
. Over local fields, the corresponding objects are actually integrals; however, over finite fields, these integrals are just sums. Nevertheless, we refer to them as integrals or zeta integrals. Note that
[
21
]
defines integrals for any
n
and
l
; however, we only need the case of
n\leq l
for the converse theorem and so we do not consider the case
n>l
.

First, suppose that
l=n.
Then we define

\Psi(W,f)=\sum_{g\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}\setminus\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}W(g)f(w_{l,l}g,I_{l})

where

w_{l,l}=\left(\begin{matrix}\gamma I_{l}&&\\
&1&\\
&&\gamma^{-1}I_{l}\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l+1}.

The integral satisfies the property
\Psi(g\cdot W,g\cdot f)=\Psi(W,f)
for any
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}.

Next, suppose that
n<l.
Then we define

\Psi(W,f)=\sum_{g\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}}\setminus\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}}\left(\sum_{r\in R^{l,n}}W(rw^{l,n}g(w^{l,n})^{-1})\right)f(g,I_{l})

where

w^{l,n}=\left(\begin{matrix}&I_{n}&&&\\
I_{l-n-1}&&&&\\
&&I_{2}&&\\
&&&&I_{l-n-1}\\
&&&I_{n}&\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}

and

R^{l,n}=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}I_{n}&&&&\\
x&I_{l-n-1}&&&\\
&&I_{2}&&\\
&&&I_{l-n-1}&\\
&&&x^{\prime}&I_{n}\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}\right\}.

The integral satisfies the property
\Psi((gn)\cdot W,g\cdot f)=\psi^{-1}_{\gamma}(n)\Psi(W,f)
for any
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}
and
n\in N^{l-n}.
Note that in the case
n<l
, our integral differs from
[
21
]
slightly. The difference is a right translation of the Whittaker functional by
(w^{l,n})^{-1}.
This is not a significant theoretical issue since right translation preserves
\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi);
however, it simplifies the calculations of the integrals later.

Next, we define an intertwining operator
M(\tau,\psi^{-1}):I(\tau,\psi^{-1})\rightarrow I(\tau^{*},\psi^{-1})
by

M(\tau,\psi^{-1})f(h,a)=\sum_{u\in V_{n}}f(w_{n}uh,d_{n}a^{*})

where
w_{n}=\left(\begin{matrix}&&I_{n}\\
&(-1)^{n}&\\
I_{n}&&\end{matrix}\right)
,
d_{n}=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,-1,\dots,(-1)^{n})\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, and
V_{n}
is the unipotent radical of the standard parabolic subgroup
Q_{n}=L_{n}V_{n}
of
\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}
where
L_{n}\cong\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, and
a^{*}=J_{n}{}^{t}a^{-1}J_{n}.

We now define the
\gamma
-factor in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible
\psi
-generic cuspidal representation of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
and
\tau
be a
\psi^{-1}
-generic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}.
Let
W\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
and
f\in I(\tau,\psi^{-1})
. Then there exists a constant
\gamma(\pi\times\tau,\psi)\in\mathbb{C}
, called the
\gamma
-factor, such that

\gamma(\pi\times\tau,\psi)\Psi(W,f)=\Psi(W,M(\tau,\psi^{-1})f).

Proof.

This is immediate from Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
.
∎

4.
Bessel Functions

In this section, we introduce and study various properties of the Bessel functions. Many of these ideas have local analogues that can be found in
[
3
]
. However, over local fields, the situation is more complicated as one has to work with partial Bessel functions as opposed to Bessel functions.

Let
\pi(U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}},\psi)
be the subspace of
\pi
generated by

\{\pi(u)v-\psi(u)v\,|\,u\in U_{SO_{2l}},\,v\in\pi\}

and let
\pi_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}},\psi}=\pi/\pi(U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}},\psi)
be the twisted Jacquet module. Since
\pi
is an irreducible
\psi
-generic representation, we have
\mathrm{dim}\pi_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}},\psi}=1.
Let
v\in\pi
such that
v\notin\pi(U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}},\psi)
and set

v_{0}=\frac{1}{|U_{SO_{2l}}|}\sum_{u\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}}\psi^{-1}(u)\pi(u)v.

By the Jacquet-Langlands lemma (see
[
4
, Lemma 2.33]
),
v_{0}\neq 0.
Recall that we fixed
\Gamma\in\mathrm{Hom}_{U_{SO_{2l}}}(\pi,\psi)
. We have
\Gamma(v_{0})\neq 0
and by construction it follows that
\pi(u)v_{0}=\psi(u)v_{0}
for any
u\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}.
Any such vector is called a Whittaker vector of
\pi.

For
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
, set

\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}(g)=\frac{\Gamma(\pi(g)v_{0})}{\Gamma(v_{0})}.

\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}
is called the normalized Bessel function for
\pi
(normalized such that the value at the identity is
1
) and it is immediate that
\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi).

Proposition 4.1
.

We have

\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}(I_{2l})=1
and
\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}(u_{1}gu_{2})=\psi(u_{1}u_{2})\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}(g)
for any
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
and any
u_{1},u_{2}\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}.

Proof.

This is immediate from the definitions.
∎

We fix the split part, denoted
S_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
, of the torus
T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
to be the subset consisting of elements of the form
t=\mathrm{diag}(t_{1},\dots,t_{l-1},I_{2},t_{l-1}^{-1},\dots,t_{1}^{-1}).
Let
W(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})
be the Weyl group of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
and let
\Delta(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})
be the set of simple roots with respect to
S_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
. We can choose the simple roots to be given by
\alpha_{i}(t)=t_{i}t_{i+1}^{-1}
for
i=1,\dots,l-2
and
\alpha_{l-1}(t)=t_{l-1}.
In the case of non-split groups, the root subgroups are not guaranteed to be isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and so we define elements of
U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
that we need here. Let
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}.
For
i=1,\dots,l-2
we define
\mathrm{\mathbb{x}}_{\alpha_{i}}(x)=(u_{i,j})_{i,j=1}^{2l}\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
where
u_{j,j}=1,
for any
j=1,\dots,2l,

u_{i,i+1}=x,u_{2l-i,2l-(i-1)}=-x,
and
u_{i,j}=0
for any other pair
(i,j).
We also define

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbb{x}}_{\alpha_{l-1}}(x)=\left(\begin{matrix}I_{l-1}&&\\
&\left(\begin{matrix}1&0&x&\frac{\rho^{-1}x^{2}}{2}\\
0&1&0&0\\
0&0&1&\rho^{-1}x\\
0&0&0&1\end{matrix}\right)&\\
&&I_{l-1}\end{matrix}\right).

It is checked directly that
\mathrm{\mathbb{x}}_{\alpha}(x)\in U_{\alpha},
where
U_{\alpha}
denotes the root subgroup of
\alpha,
for any
\alpha\in\Delta(\mathrm{SO}_{2l}).

We say that a Weyl element
w\in W(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})
supports Bessel functions if for any
\alpha\in\Delta(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})
,
w\alpha
is either negative or simple. We let B(
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
) denote the set of Weyl elements which support Bessel functions. Recall
B_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}=T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
is a fixed Borel subgroup of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible
\psi
-generic representation of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
with Bessel function
\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}
. Then, for any
w\in W(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})\setminus\mathrm{B}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l}),
we have
\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}(g)=0,
for any
g\in B_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}wB_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}.

Proof.

Since
B_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}=T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}=U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
as a set,

\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}(g)=\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}(t_{1}u_{1}wt_{2}u_{2})=\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}(u_{1}^{\prime}t_{1}^{\prime}wt_{2}u_{2})

for some
u_{1},u_{1}^{\prime},u_{2}\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
and
t_{1},t_{1}^{\prime},t_{2}\in T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}.
By Proposition
4.1
, we find
\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}(u_{1}^{\prime}t_{1}^{\prime}wt_{2}u_{2})=\psi(u_{1}^{\prime}u_{2})\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}(t_{1}^{\prime}wt_{2})
and hence it is enough to show that
\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}(t_{1}wt_{2})=0
for any
t_{1},t_{2}\in T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}.
Since
W(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})=N_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}(T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}})/T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
,
wt_{2}=t_{2}^{\prime}w
for some
t_{2}^{\prime}\in T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}.
Hence,
\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}(t_{1}wt_{2})=\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}(t_{1}t_{2}^{\prime}w)
and so it is enough to show that
\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}(tw)=0
for any
t\in T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}.

Now, since
w\notin\mathrm{B}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l}),
there exists
\alpha\in\Delta(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})
such that
w\alpha
is positive but not simple. Let
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
tw\mathrm{\mathbb{x}}_{\alpha}(x)=u^{\prime}tw
for some
u^{\prime}
in the root subgroup
U_{w\alpha}
. Now,
\psi(\mathrm{\mathbb{x}}_{\alpha}(x))
is a nonzero constant multiple of
\psi(x)
and this constant is independent of
x
. Since
w\alpha
is positive but not simple,
\psi(u^{\prime})=\psi(0)=1.
By Proposition
4.1
, there exists a nonzero constant
c\in\mathbb{C}
such that
c\psi(x)\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}(tw)=\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}(tw)
for any
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Since
\psi
is nontrivial, we must have
\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}(tw)=0.

∎

The statement of the following lemma is similar to the split case (see
[
11
, Lemma 4.3]
); however, the proof requires more effort here. In the split case, the proof relied on the fact that the intersection of the kernels of the simple roots is precisely the center. This is true in general for split groups, but is not guaranteed for quasi-split groups. To overcome this obstacle, we examine the action of the non-split torus on
\mathrm{\mathbb{x}}_{\alpha}(x)
.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t)\neq 0
for some
t\in T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}},
then
t
is in the center of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}.

Proof.

Let
t\in T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
such that
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t)\neq 0
and let
\delta\in\Delta(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})\setminus\{\alpha_{l-1}\}.
For
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, let
\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x)
be in the root subgroup of
\delta
. Then
t\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x)=\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(\delta(t)x)t.
Hence, by Proposition
4.1
,

\psi(\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x)){\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t)=\psi(\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(\delta(t)x)){\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t).

Thus,
\psi(\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x))=\psi(\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(\delta(t)x))
for any
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Since
\psi
is nontrivial and
x
is arbitrary, we must have
\delta(t)=1
for all
\delta\in\Delta(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})\setminus\{\alpha_{l-1}\}.
Write

t=\mathrm{diag}(t_{1},\dots,t_{l-1},\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\rho\\
b&a\end{matrix}\right),t_{l-1}^{-1},\dots,t_{1}^{-1})

where
a^{2}-b^{2}\rho=1.
Since
\delta(t)=1
for all
\delta\in\Delta(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})\setminus\{\alpha_{l-1}\},
we must have
t_{1}=\cdots=t_{l-1}.

Next, we examine the root subgroup for
\delta=\alpha_{l-1}.
We verify directly that

\displaystyle\psi(\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x)){\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t)={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x))={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x)t^{-1}t)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t)\psi(t\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x)t^{-1})

\displaystyle=\psi(\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(at_{l-1}x)){\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t).

Similar to the previous case, we deduce that
a=t_{l-1}^{-1}.

On the other hand, we verify directly

\displaystyle\psi(\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x)){\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t)={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x)t)={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(tt^{-1}\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x)t)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t)\psi(t^{-1}\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x)t)

\displaystyle=\psi(\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(at_{l-1}^{-1}x)){\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t).

Hence
a=t_{l-1}.
Since
a=t_{l-1}=t_{l-1}^{-1}
, we have
t_{1}=t_{2}=\cdots=t_{l-1}=a=\pm 1
and
b=0
which proves the lemma.
∎

4.1.
Conjugate Bessel Function

We introduce the Bessel function for
\pi^{c}
here.
We have
cU_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}c=U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}.
Hence we define another character of
U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
by
\psi_{c}(u):=\psi(cuc).
That is,

\psi_{c}(u)=\psi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l-2}u_{i,i+1}-\frac{1}{2}u_{l-1,l+1}\right),

for any
u=(u_{i,j})_{i,j=1}^{2l}\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}.
Fix
\tilde{t}:=\mathrm{diag}(I_{l-1},-1,-1,I_{l-1})\in T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}.
It is checked directly that
\psi_{c}(\tilde{t}^{-1}u\tilde{t})=\psi(u).
Recall that we fixed a nonzero Whittaker functional
\Gamma\in\mathrm{Hom}_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}}(\pi,\psi).
Then
\Gamma\in\mathrm{Hom}_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}}(\pi^{c},\psi_{c})
and

\mathcal{B}_{\pi^{c},\psi_{c}}(g):=\frac{\Gamma(\pi^{c}(g)v_{0})}{\Gamma(v_{0})}=\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}(cgc)

defines the normalized Bessel function for
\pi^{c}
in
\mathrm{Ind}_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}}^{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}\psi_{c}
. On the other hand,
\pi^{c}
is also
\psi
-generic, since
\psi_{c}(\tilde{t}^{-1}u\tilde{t})=\psi(u)
for any
u\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}.

Let

\mathcal{B}_{\pi^{c},\psi}(g):=\mathcal{B}_{\pi^{c},\psi_{c}}(\tilde{t}^{-1}g\tilde{t})=\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}(c\tilde{t}^{-1}g\tilde{t}c).

The notation is justified by the following proposition.

Proposition 4.4
.

\mathcal{B}_{\pi^{c},\psi}
is the normalized Bessel function for
\pi^{c}
in
\mathrm{Ind}_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}}^{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}\psi.

Proof.

The proof is similar to
[
11
, Proposition 4.4]
.
∎

4.2.
Partition of the Bessel Support

The maximal standard parabolic subgroups of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
are
P_{n}=M_{n}N_{n}
for
n\leq l-1
where
M_{n}
is the standard Levi subgroup that is isomorphic to
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{SO}_{2(l-n)}
. Let
w_{long}
be the long Weyl element of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
and
w_{M_{n}}
be the long Weyl element in
M_{n}.
Define
\tilde{w}_{n}=w_{long}^{-1}w_{M_{n}}.
The analogues of these elements played an essential role in the arguments for the other classical groups
[
11
,
22
]
and they continue to do so in our case. To be explicit, we have

w_{long}=\left(\begin{matrix}&&J_{l-1}\\
&\left(\begin{matrix}-1&0\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)^{l-1}&\\
J_{l-1}&&\end{matrix}\right),w_{M_{n}}=\left(\begin{matrix}J_{n}&&\\
&w_{long,2(l-n)}&\\
&&J_{n}\end{matrix}\right),

where
w_{long,2(l-n)}
is the long Weyl element of
\mathrm{SO}_{2(l-n)}.

Hence,

\tilde{w}_{n}=\left(\begin{matrix}&&&&I_{n}\\
&I_{l-n-1}&&&\\
&&\left(\begin{matrix}-1&0\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)^{n}&&&\\
&&&I_{l-n-1}&\\
I_{n}&&&&\end{matrix}\right).

Recall that the definition of the intertwining operator
M(\tau,\psi^{-1})
involved the Weyl element
w_{n}
of
\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}.
The image of
w_{n}
under the embedding of
\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}
into
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
and conjugated by
w^{l,n}
is
\tilde{w}_{n}.
That is,
w^{l,n}w_{n}(w^{l,n})^{-1}=\tilde{w}_{n}.

Let
t
be in the split part of the torus and write

t=\mathrm{diag}(t_{1},\dots,t_{l-1},I_{2},t_{l-1}^{-1},\dots,t_{1}^{-1}).

So

\tilde{w}_{n}^{-1}t\tilde{w}_{n}=\mathrm{diag}(t_{n}^{-1},\dots,t_{1}^{-1},t_{n+1},\dots,t_{l-1},I_{2},t_{l-1}^{-1},\dots,t_{n+1}^{-1},t_{1},\dots,t_{n}).

Thus, we have

\tilde{w}_{n}\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{n-i}\,\,\mathrm{for}\,\,1\leq i\leq n-1,

\tilde{w}_{n}\alpha_{n}(t)=t_{1}^{-1}t_{n+1}^{-1},

\tilde{w}_{n}\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i}\,\,\mathrm{for}\,\,n+1\leq i\leq l-1.

Let
\theta_{w}=\{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})\,|\,w\alpha\in\Phi^{+}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})\}.
The assignment
w\mapsto\theta_{w}
gives a bijection from
\mathrm{B}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})
to the power set of
\Delta(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})
, which we denote by
\mathcal{P}(\Delta(\mathrm{SO}_{2l}))
. Then, for
n\leq l-1,

\theta_{\tilde{w}_{n}}=\Delta(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})\setminus\{\alpha_{n}\}
.

For
n\leq l-1,
let
\mathrm{B}_{n}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})
be the set of
w\in\mathrm{B}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})
such that there exists
w^{\prime}\in W(\mathrm{GL}_{n})
such that
w=t_{n}(w^{\prime})\tilde{w}_{n}.
We let
\mathrm{B}_{0}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})
be the identity. Also, let

P_{n}=\{\theta\subseteq\Delta(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})\,|\,w_{\theta}\in\mathrm{B}_{n}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})\}.

Proposition 4.5
.

P_{n}=\{\theta\subseteq\Delta(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})\,|\,\{\alpha_{n+1},\dots,\alpha_{l-1}\}\subseteq\theta\subseteq\Delta(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})\setminus\{\alpha_{n}\}\}.

Proof.

This is implicitly proved in
[
22
, §5, 7]
. Indeed, we do not need to refer to the matrices explicitly. If
w=t_{n}(w^{\prime})\tilde{w}_{n}
for some
w^{\prime}\in W(\mathrm{GL}_{n}),
then
t_{n}(w^{\prime})\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i}
for
i\geq n+1
. Also,
t_{n}(w^{\prime})
acts on the first
n
coordinates of
\tilde{w}_{n}^{-1}t\tilde{w}_{n}
by permuting the
t_{i}^{-1}
’s for
i\leq n.
Hence
w\alpha_{n}(t)=t_{i}^{-1}t_{n+1}^{-1}
for some
i\leq n
and is hence negative. Thus,

P_{n}\subseteq\{\theta\subseteq\Delta(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})\,|\,\{\alpha_{n+1},\dots,\alpha_{l-1}\}\subseteq\theta\subseteq\Delta(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})\setminus\{\alpha_{n}\}\}.

The reverse inclusion is straightforward to check.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

The sets
\mathrm{B}_{n}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})
for
n\leq l-1
form a partition of the Bessel support
\mathrm{B}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l}).

Proof.

Again, this is implicit in
[
22
, §5, 7]
. The map
w\mapsto\theta_{w}
gives an bijection from
\mathrm{B}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})
to
\mathcal{P}(\Delta(\mathrm{SO}_{2l}))
. By Proposition
4.5
, the sets
P_{n}
such that
n\leq l-1
form a partition of
\mathcal{P}(\Delta(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})).
From the bijection
w\mapsto\theta_{w}
, we have that the sets
\mathrm{B}_{n}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})
for
n\leq l-1
form a partition of the Bessel support
\mathrm{B}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l}).

∎

The following proposition is used later to show that the zeta integrals are nonzero.

Proposition 4.7
.

If
w\in W(\mathrm{GL}_{l-1})
and
w\neq I_{l-1}
, then
t_{l-1}(w)\notin\mathrm{B}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l}).
In particular, if
a\in\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}
and
a
is not upper triangular, then
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t_{l-1}(a))=0.

Proof.

We use the partition of the Bessel support in Corollary
4.6
.
First, suppose that
t_{l-1}(w)=t_{n}(w^{\prime})\tilde{w}_{n}
for some
n
with
1\leq n\leq{l-1}
and
w^{\prime}\in W(\mathrm{GL}_{n}).
Then,
\tilde{w}_{n}\in W(\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}).
However, this is not the case for any
n
. Thus we have
t_{l-1}(w)\notin\mathrm{B}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l}).

For the second part of the claim, suppose
a\in\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}
is not upper triangular, Then, by the Bruhat decomposition of
\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}
, there exists
w\in W(\mathrm{GL}_{l-1})
such that
w\neq I_{l-1}
and
a=u_{1}twu_{2}
where
t\in T_{\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}}
and
u_{1},u_{2}\in U_{\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}}.
We obtain
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t_{l-1}(a))={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t_{l-1}(t)t_{l-1}(w))\psi(t_{l-1}(u_{1}))\psi(t_{l-1}(u_{2})).
However,
t_{l-1}(w)\notin\mathrm{B}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})
and hence
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t_{l-1}(t)t_{l-1}(w))=0
by Lemma
4.2
.
∎

The next proposition concerns the support of the Bessel functions on Bruhat cells associated to
w\in\mathrm{B}_{n}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l}).
The argument is similar to that of Lemma
4.3
.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
t\in T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
and
w\in\mathrm{B}_{n}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})
for
n<l-1.
Suppose that
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(tw)\neq 0.
If we write

t=\mathrm{diag}(t_{1},\dots,t_{l-1},\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\rho\\
b&a\end{matrix}\right),t_{l-1}^{-1},\dots,t_{1}^{-1})

where
a^{2}-b^{2}\rho=1,
then we must have
a=t_{l-1}=\cdots=t_{n+1}=\pm 1
and
b=0.

Proof.

By definition, there exists
w^{\prime}\in W(\mathrm{GL}_{n})
such that
w=t_{n}(w^{\prime})\tilde{w}_{n}.
We verify directly that
w\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i}
for
i=n+1,\dots,l-1.
Let
\delta\in\{\alpha_{n+1},\dots,\alpha_{l-2}\}.
For
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, let
\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x)
be in the root subgroup of
\delta
. Then
tw\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x)=\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(\delta(t)x)tw.
Hence, by Proposition
4.1
,

\psi(\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x)){\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(tw)=\psi(\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(\delta(t)x)){\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(tw).

Thus,
\psi(\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x))=\psi(\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(\delta(t)x))
for any
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Since
\psi
is nontrivial and
x
is arbitrary, we must have
t_{n+1}=\cdots=t_{l-2}.

Next, we examine the root subgroup for
\delta=\alpha_{l-1}.
We verify directly that

\displaystyle\psi(\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x)){\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(tw)={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(tw\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x))={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x)t^{-1}tw)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(tw)\psi(t\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x)t^{-1})

\displaystyle=\psi(\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(at_{l-1}x)){\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(tw).

Similar to the previous case, we deduce that
a=t_{l-1}^{-1}.

On the other hand, we verify directly

\displaystyle\psi(\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x)){\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(tw)={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x)tw)={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(twt^{-1}\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x)t)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(tw)\psi(t^{-1}\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(x)t)

\displaystyle=\psi(\mathbb{x}_{\delta}(at_{l-1}^{-1}x)){\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(tw).

Hence
a=t_{l-1}.
Since
a=t_{l-1}=t_{l-1}^{-1}
,
t_{n+1}=\cdots=t_{l-1}=a=\pm 1
and
b=0
which proves the lemma.
∎

5.
Twists by
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
for
n\leq l-2

We begin by collecting some results which will be used in determining the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-twists. The first result is a mild strengthening of
[
27
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 5.1

(
[
11
, Lemma 5.1]
)
.

Let
H
be function on
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
such that
H(ug)=\psi(u)H(g)
for any
u\in U_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}
and
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}.

Let
X
be a subset of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
and suppose that

\sum_{x\in X}H(x)W_{v}(x)=0

for any
v\in\tau
and with
\tau
running through all
\psi^{-1}-
generic irreducible representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}.
Then,
H(x)=0
for any
x\in X.

Now we start the calculations of the zeta integrals for twists up to
\mathrm{GL}_{l-2}.

Let
\tau
be an irreducible
\psi^{-1}
-generic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
,
v\in\tau
be a fixed vector, and define
\xi_{v}\in I(\tau)
by supp
(\xi_{v})=Q_{n}
and

\xi_{v}(l_{n}(a)u)=\tau(a)v,\forall a\in\mathrm{GL}_{n},u\in V_{n},

where
Q_{n}=L_{n}V_{n}
is the standard Siegel parabolic of
\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}.

We define the function
f_{v}=f_{\xi_{v}}\in I(\tau,\psi^{-1}).
That is,
f_{v}(g,a)=\Lambda_{\tau}(\tau(a)\xi_{v}(g))
where we have
\Lambda_{\tau}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{U_{GL_{n}}}(\tau,\psi^{-1}),

a\in\mathrm{GL}_{n},
and
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}.
We also fix the Whittaker function
W_{v}(a)=\Lambda_{\tau}(\tau(a)v).

Proposition 5.2
.

Suppose that
n\leq l-2
and
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}
is the normalized Bessel function of
\pi
, an irreducible cuspidal
\psi
-generic representation of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
. Then
\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},f_{v})=W_{v}(I_{n})
. Moreover, we may choose
v\in\tau
such that
\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},f_{v})\neq 0.

Proof.

By definition,

\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},f_{v})=\sum_{g\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}}\setminus\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}}\left(\sum_{r\in R^{l,n}}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(rw^{l,n}g(w^{l,n})^{-1})\right)f_{v}(g,I_{l}).

The support of
f_{v}(g,I_{n})
is contained in
V_{n}L_{n}
. Since
V_{n}\subseteq U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}}
, we have

\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},f_{v})=\sum_{a\in U_{GL_{n}}\setminus\mathrm{GL}_{n}}\left(\sum_{r\in R^{l,n}}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(rw^{l,n}l_{n}(a)(w^{l,n})^{-1})\right)W_{v}(a).

Now, the embedding of
\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}
into
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
takes
l_{n}(a)
to
q_{n}(a)=\mathrm{diag}(I_{l-n-1},a,I_{2},a^{*},I_{l-n-1}).
Thus,
w^{l,n}q_{n}(a)(w^{l,n})^{-1}=\mathrm{diag}(a,I_{2l-2n},a^{*}).
Let

r_{x}=\left(\begin{matrix}I_{n}&&&&\\
x&I_{l-n-1}&&&\\
&&I_{2}&&\\
&&&I_{l-n-1}&\\
&&&x^{\prime}&I_{n}\end{matrix}\right).

Then,

r_{x}w^{l,n}q_{n}(a)(w^{l,n})^{-1}=\left(\begin{matrix}a&&&&\\
xa&I_{l-n-1}&&&\\
&&I_{2}&&\\
&&&I_{l-n-1}&\\
&&&x^{\prime}a^{*}&a^{*}\end{matrix}\right).

By Proposition
4.7
,
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(r_{x}w^{l,n}q_{n}(a)(w^{l,n})^{-1})=0
unless
a
is upper triangular and
x=0.
Hence,

\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},f_{v})=\sum_{a\in T_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t_{n}(a))W_{v}(a).

Finally, by Lemma
4.3
,
\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},f_{v})=W_{v}(I_{n}).
Finally, if we choose
v
to be a Whittaker vector for
\tau
, then
W_{v}(I_{n})\neq 0.

∎

Let
\tilde{f}_{v}=M(t,\psi^{-1})f_{v}
and
W_{v}^{*}(a)=\Lambda_{\tau}(\tau(d_{n}a^{*})v).
The following lemma lists several properties of
\tilde{f}_{v}
that will be used in computing the zeta integrals.

Lemma 5.3

(
[
11
, Lemma 6.2]
)
.

For any
n\leq l
, we have the following:

(1)

If
\tilde{f}_{v}(g,I_{n})\neq 0,
then
g\in Q_{n}w_{n}Q_{n}=Q_{n}w_{n}V_{n}.

(2)

If
x\in V_{n}
, then
\tilde{f}_{v}(w_{n}x,I_{n})=W^{*}_{v}(I_{n}).

(3)

For
a\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}
and
x\in V_{n},

\tilde{f}_{v}(l_{n}(a)w_{n}x,I_{n})=W_{v}^{*}(a).

The following theorem is the main result for twists up to
\mathrm{GL}_{l-2}.

Theorem 5.4
.

Suppose that
n\leq l-2.
Let
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
be irreducible cuspidal
\psi
-generic representations of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
which share the same central character. If
\gamma(\pi\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(\pi^{\prime}\times\tau,\psi)
for all irreducible
\psi^{-1}
-generic representations
\tau
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, then

{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t_{n}(a)\tilde{w_{n}})={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime},\psi}(t_{n}(a)\tilde{w_{n}})

for any
a\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.2
,
\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},f_{v})=\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime},\psi},f_{v})=W_{v}(I_{n}).
By assumption,
\gamma(\pi\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(\pi^{\prime}\times\tau,\psi)
for all irreducible
\psi^{-1}
-generic representations
\tau
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, and hence it follows that
\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},\tilde{f_{v}})=\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime},\psi},\tilde{f_{v}}).
By definition,

\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},\tilde{f}_{v})=\sum_{g\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}}\setminus\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}}\left(\sum_{r\in R^{l,n}}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(rw^{l,n}g(w^{l,n})^{-1})\right)\tilde{f}_{v}(g,I_{n}).

By Lemma
5.3
,

\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},\tilde{f}_{v})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in U_{GL_{n}}\setminus\mathrm{GL}_{n}\\
x\in V_{n}\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{r\in R^{l,n}}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(rw^{l,n}l_{n}(a)w_{n}x(w^{l,n})^{-1})\right)\tilde{f}_{v}(l_{n}(a)w_{n}x,I_{n}).

Now, the embedding of
\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}
into
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
takes
l_{n}(a)
to
q_{n}(a)=\mathrm{diag}(I_{l-n-1},a,I_{2},a^{*},I_{l-n-1}).

The embedding also takes the unipotent element
x=\left(\begin{matrix}I_{n}&*&*\\
&1&*\\
&&I_{n}\end{matrix}\right)
to
\tilde{x}=\mathrm{diag}(I_{l-n-1},\left(\begin{matrix}I_{n}&*&*\\
&I_{2}&*\\
&&I_{n}\end{matrix}\right),I_{l-n-1}).
Now,

\tilde{x}(w^{l,n})^{-1}=(w^{l,n})^{-1}\left(\begin{matrix}I_{n}&0&*&0&*\\
&I_{l-n-1}&0&0&0\\
&&I_{2}&0&*\\
&&&I_{l-n-1}&0\\
&&&&I_{n}\end{matrix}\right).

The character is trivial on the last unipotent matrix and hence, by Lemma
5.3
,

\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},\tilde{f}_{v})=|V_{n}|\sum_{a\in U_{GL_{n}}\setminus\mathrm{GL}_{n}}\left(\sum_{r\in R^{l,n}}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(rw^{l,n}q_{n}(a)w_{n}(w^{l,n})^{-1})\right)W_{v}^{*}(a).

Next,
w^{l,n}q_{n}(a)=t_{n}(a)w^{l,n}
where
t_{n}(a)=\mathrm{diag}(a,I_{2l-2n},a^{*}).
Let

r_{x}=\left(\begin{matrix}I_{n}&&&&\\
x&I_{l-n-1}&&&\\
&&I_{2}&&\\
&&&I_{l-n-1}&\\
&&&x^{\prime}&I_{n}\end{matrix}\right).

Then,
r_{x}t_{n}(a)=t_{n}(a)r_{ax}.
Recall that
\tilde{w}_{n}=w^{l,n}w_{n}(w^{l,n})^{-1}
where
w_{n}\in\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}
is realized under the embedding into
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}.
Then,

\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},\tilde{f}_{v})=|V_{n}|\sum_{a\in U_{GL_{n}}\setminus\mathrm{GL}_{n}}\left(\sum_{r_{x}\in R^{l,n}}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t_{n}(a)r_{ax}\tilde{w}_{n})\right)W_{v}^{*}(a).

Now,

r_{ax}\tilde{w}_{n}=\tilde{w}_{n}\left(\begin{matrix}I_{n}&&&x^{\prime}(a^{*})&\\
&I_{l-n-1}&&&xa\\
&&I_{2}&&\\
&&&I_{l-n-1}&\\
&&&&I_{n}\end{matrix}\right).

The character is trivial on the last unipotent element and hence

\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},\tilde{f}_{v})=|V_{n}||R^{l,n}|\sum_{a\in U_{GL_{n}}\setminus\mathrm{GL}_{n}}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t_{n}(a)\tilde{w}_{n})W_{v}^{*}(a).

Since
\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},\tilde{f_{v}})=\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime},\psi},\tilde{f_{v}}),
it follows that

0=\sum_{a\in U_{GL_{n}}\setminus\mathrm{GL}_{n}}({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}-{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime},\psi})(t_{n}(a)\tilde{w}_{n})W_{v}^{*}(a).

Now, let
f
be the function on
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
defined by
f(a)=({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}-{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime},\psi})(t_{n}(a)\tilde{w}_{n}).
Then,
f(ua)=\psi(u)f(a)
and

0=\sum_{a\in U_{GL_{n}}\setminus\mathrm{GL}_{n}}f(a)W_{v}^{*}(a).

By Lemma
5.1
,
f(a)=0
for any
a\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}
which gives the claim.
∎

6.
Twists by
\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}

In this section, we consider the twists by
\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}
. The arguments in this section are similar to the previous section, except that
R^{l,n}
and
w^{l,n}
are trivial which leads to some minor differences.

Let
\tau
be an irreducible
\psi^{-1}
-generic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}
,
v\in\tau
be a fixed vector, and define
\xi_{v}\in I(\tau)
by supp
(\xi_{v})=L_{l-1}V_{l-1}=Q_{l-1}
and

\xi_{v}(l_{l-1}(a)u)=\tau(a)v,\forall a\in\mathrm{GL}_{l-1},u\in V_{l-1}.

Let
f_{v}=f_{\xi_{v}}\in I(\tau,\psi^{-1}).
That is, for
\Lambda_{\tau}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{U_{GL_{l-1}}}(\tau,\psi^{-1}),a\in\mathrm{GL}_{l-1},
and
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l-1}
, we have
f_{v}(g,a)=\Lambda_{\tau}(\tau(a)\xi_{v}(g))
. We also let
W_{v}(a)=\Lambda_{\tau}(\tau(a)v).
The zeta integrals in this case are nonzero.

Proposition 6.1
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}
be the normalized Bessel function of
\pi
, an irreducible cuspidal
\psi
-generic representation of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
. Then, we have

\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},f_{v})=W_{v}(I_{l-1})
. In particular, we may choose
v\in\tau
such that
\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},f_{v})\neq 0.

Proof.

By definition,

\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},f_{v})=\sum_{g\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l-1}}\setminus\mathrm{SO}_{2l-1}}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(g)f_{v}(g,I_{l-1}).

The support of
f_{v}(\cdot,I_{l-1})
is
Q_{l-1}.
Thus,

\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},f_{v})=\sum_{a\in U_{\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}}\setminus\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(l_{l-1}(a))f_{v}(l_{l-1}(a),I_{l-1}).

Under the embedding of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l-1}
into
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
,
l_{l-1}(a)
maps to
t_{l-1}(a).
Hence,

\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},f_{v})=\sum_{a\in U_{\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}}\setminus\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t_{l-1}(a))W_{v}(a).

By Proposition
4.7
,
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t_{l-1}(a))=0
unless
a
is upper triangular. Thus, by Lemma
4.3
,

\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},f_{v})=W_{v}(I_{l-1}).

If we choose
v
to be a Whittaker vector for
\tau
, then
W_{v}(I_{l-1})\neq 0.
This proves the proposition.
∎

The next theorem shows that the twists by
\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}
determine that the Bessel functions are equal on a subset of the Bruhat cells in
\mathrm{B}_{l-1}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l}).
More specifically, the Bessel functions are determined on the parts of the cell corresponding to the split part of the torus.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
be irreducible cuspidal
\psi
-generic representations of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
which share the same central character. If
\gamma(\pi\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(\pi^{\prime}\times\tau,\psi)
for all irreducible generic representations
\tau
of
\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}
, then we have

{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t_{l-1}(a)\tilde{w}_{l-1})={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime},\psi}(t_{l-1}(a)\tilde{w}_{l-1})

for any
a\in\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.1
,
\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},f_{v})=\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime},\psi},f_{v})=W_{v}(I_{l-1}).
From our assumption,
\gamma(\pi\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(\pi^{\prime}\times\tau,\psi)
for all irreducible generic representations
\tau
of
\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}
, and hence it follows that
\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},\tilde{f_{v}})=\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime},\psi},\tilde{f_{v}}).
By definition,

\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},\tilde{f}_{v})=\sum_{g\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l-1}}\setminus\mathrm{SO}_{2l-1}}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(g)\tilde{f}_{v}(g,I_{l-1}).

From Lemma
5.3
, we have

\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},\tilde{f}_{v})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in U_{\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}}\setminus\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}\\
u\in V_{l-1}\end{subarray}}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(l_{l-1}(a)w_{l-1}u)W_{v}^{*}(a).

The embedding of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l-1}
into
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
takes
l_{l-1}(a)
to
t_{l-1}(a)
and
w_{l-1}
to
\tilde{w}_{l-1}.
Let

u=\left(\begin{matrix}I_{l-1}&x&y\\
&1&x^{\prime}\\
&&I_{l-1}\end{matrix}\right).

Then the embedding takes
u
to

\tilde{u}=\left(\begin{matrix}I_{l-1}&x&&y\\
&1&&x^{\prime}\\
&&1&\\
&&&I_{l-1}\end{matrix}\right).

Since
\psi(\tilde{u})=1,
we have

\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},\tilde{f}_{v})=|V_{l-1}|\sum_{a\in U_{\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}}\setminus\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t_{l-1}(a)\tilde{w}_{l-1})W_{v}^{*}(a).

From
\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},\tilde{f_{v}})=\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime},\psi},\tilde{f_{v}}),
it follows that

0=\sum_{a\in U_{GL_{l-1}}\setminus\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}}({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}-{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime},\psi})(t_{l-1}(a)\tilde{w}_{l-1})W_{v}^{*}(a).

Let
f
be the function on
\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}
defined by
f(a)=({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}-{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime},\psi})(t_{l-1}(a)\tilde{w}_{l-1}).
Then,
f(ua)=\psi(u)f(a)
and

0=\sum_{a\in\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}}f(a)W_{v}^{*}(a).

By Lemma
5.1
,
f(a)=0
for any
a\in\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}
which gives the theorem.
∎

The following corollary shows that Theorems
5.4
and
6.2
hold for the Bessel functions of
\pi^{c}
and
\pi^{\prime}{}^{c}
as well.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
be irreducible cuspidal
\psi
-generic representations of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
which share the same central character. If
\gamma(\pi\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(\pi^{\prime}\times\tau,\psi)
for all irreducible generic representations
\tau
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
for any
n\leq l-1
, then

{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{c},\psi}(t_{n}(a)\tilde{w}_{n})={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime}{}^{c},\psi}(t_{n}(a)\tilde{w}_{n})
for any
a\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}.

Proof.

By Theorems
5.4
and
6.2
, we have

{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t_{n}(a)\tilde{w}_{n})={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime},\psi}(t_{n}(a)\tilde{w}_{n})
for any
a\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}
and
n\leq l-1.
Also,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t_{n}(a)\tilde{w}_{n})={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(c\tilde{t}^{-1}\tilde{t}ct_{n}(a)\tilde{w}_{n}c\tilde{t}^{-1}\tilde{t}c)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{c},\psi}(\tilde{t}ct_{n}(a)\tilde{w}_{n}c\tilde{t}^{-1})

\displaystyle={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{c},\psi}(t_{n}(a)\tilde{w}_{n}).

This proves the corollary.
∎

The following corollary shows the equivalence of
\gamma
-factors of conjugate representations for
n\leq l-1
. This is not necessary to show the converse theorem, but when later paired with its analogue for
n=l
, Corollary
7.4
, it shows that the
\gamma
-factor cannot distinguish between a representation and its conjugate. In the setting of local fields, this is already known (
[
2
,
15
]
).

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible cuspidal
\psi
-generic representation of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
. Then we have
\gamma(\pi\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(\pi^{c}\times\tau,\psi)
for all irreducible generic representations
\tau
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
for any
n\leq l-1
.

Proof.

We abuse notation and consider the zeta integrals simultaneously for each
n\leq l-1.
By Propositions
5.2
and
6.1
we can choose
v\in\tau
such that
\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},f_{v})=\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{c},\psi},f_{v})\neq 0.
By the proofs of Theorems
5.4
and
6.2
,

\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},\tilde{f}_{v})=|V_{n}||R^{l,n}|\sum_{a\in U_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}\setminus\mathrm{GL}_{n}}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t_{n}(a)\tilde{w}_{n})W_{v}^{*}(a).

Since for any
n\leq l-1,
we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(t_{n}(a)\tilde{w}_{n})

\displaystyle={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(c\tilde{t}^{-1}\tilde{t}ct_{n}(a)\tilde{w}_{n}c\tilde{t}^{-1}\tilde{t}c)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{c},\psi}(\tilde{t}ct_{n}(a)\tilde{w}_{n}c\tilde{t}^{-1})

\displaystyle={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{c},\psi}(t_{n}(a)\tilde{w}_{n}),

it follows that
\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},\tilde{f}_{v})=\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{c},\psi},\tilde{f}_{v}).
Finally, since
\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},f_{v})=\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{c},\psi},f_{v})
is nonzero, we have
\gamma(\pi\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(\pi^{c}\times\tau,\psi)
for all irreducible generic representations
\tau
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
for any
n\leq l-1
. This concludes the proof of the corollary.
∎

7.
Twists by
\mathrm{GL}_{l}

In this section, we consider the twists by
\mathrm{GL}_{l}.
These twists are significantly different from the previous twists. The embedding of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
into
\mathrm{SO}_{2l+1}
plays a crucial role in these arguments. For reference, we explicate the embedding here. Recall we embed
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
into
\mathrm{SO}_{2l+1}
via

g:=\left(\begin{matrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{matrix}\right)\mapsto M^{-1}\left(\begin{matrix}A&&B\\
&1&\\
C&&D\end{matrix}\right)M

where
A,B,C,
and
D
are
l\times l
matrices and

M=\mathrm{diag}(I_{l-1},\left(\begin{matrix}0&1&0\\
\frac{1}{2}&0&\frac{1}{2\gamma}\\
\frac{1}{2}&0&\frac{-1}{2\gamma}\end{matrix}\right),I_{l-1}).

For brevity, we set

\tilde{f}_{v}(w_{l,l}twu,I_{l})
. Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{t\in T_{a},w\in\mathrm{B}_{l-1}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l}),u\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}}({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}-{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{c},\psi})(twu)\tilde{f}_{v}(w_{l,l}twu,I_{l})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t\in T_{a},w\in\mathrm{B}_{l-1}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l}),u\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}}({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{c},\psi}-{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{c},\psi})(twu)\tilde{f}_{v}(w_{l,l}twu,I_{l})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

By Proposition
7.1
, we may choose a nonzero
v\in\tau
such that
\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi},f_{v})=\Psi({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{c},\psi},f_{v})=W_{v}(w_{l,l})\neq 0.
Thus, we have
\gamma(\pi\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(\pi^{c}\times\tau,\psi)
. This proves the corollary.
∎

8.
The converse theorem

In this section, we prove the converse theorem. First, we combine the results of the previous sections to obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
be irreducible cuspidal
\psi
-generic representations of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
with the same central character. If

\gamma(\pi\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(\pi^{\prime}\times\tau,\psi),

for all irreducible generic representations
\tau
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
with
n\leq l,

then we have that

({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}+{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{c},\psi})(g)=({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime},\psi}+{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime}{}^{c},\psi})(g)

for any
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

Proof.

By the Bruhat decomposition, we may assume
g=u_{1}twu_{2}\in B_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}wB_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
for some
w\in W(\mathrm{SO}_{2l}).
By Proposition
4.1
and the definition of the Bessel support, it is enough to show that

(8.1)

({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}+{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{c},\psi})(tw)=({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime},\psi}+{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime}{}^{c},\psi})(tw)

for any
t\in T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
and
w\in\mathrm{B}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l}).
By Proposition
4.6
, we only need to show Equation (
8.1
) for
w\in\mathrm{B}_{n}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})
for
n\leq l-1.

Since
\mathrm{B}_{0}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})=\{I_{2l}\},
we have
({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}+{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{c},\psi})(t)=({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime},\psi}+{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime}{}^{c},\psi})(t)
for any
t\in T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
by Lemma
4.3
and equality of the central characters. Equation (
8.1
) for
1\leq n\leq l-2
follows from the equality of the central characters, Proposition
4.8
, Theorem
5.4
, and Corollary
6.3
. Equation (
8.1
) for
n=l-1
follows from the equality of the central characters, Theorems
6.2
and
7.3
, and Corollary
6.3
.
∎

We now prove the converse theorem. The proof is essentially the same as in the split case since we have established Theorem
8.1
. However, we include it for completeness.

Theorem 8.2

(The Converse Theorem for
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
)
.

Let
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
be irreducible cuspidal
\psi
-generic representations of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
with the same central character. If

\gamma(\pi\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(\pi^{\prime}\times\tau,\psi),

for all irreducible generic representations
\tau
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
with
n\leq l,

then
\pi\cong\pi^{\prime}
or
\pi\cong\pi^{\prime}{}^{c}.

Proof.

By Theorem
8.1
,
({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}+{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{c},\psi})=({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime},\psi}+{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime}{}^{c},\psi})
on all of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}.
We let
W_{\pi},W_{\pi^{c}},W_{\pi^{\prime}},
and
W_{\pi^{\prime}{}^{c}}
be the Whittaker models of
\pi,\pi^{c},\pi^{\prime}
, and
\pi^{\prime}{}^{c}
respectively.

First, suppose that
\pi
is isomorphic to
\pi^{c}
. By uniqueness of Whittaker models, we have
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}+{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{c},\psi}=2{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}\in W_{\pi}
. Then,
2{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime},\psi}+{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime}{}^{c},\psi}
and hence
W_{\pi}\cap(W_{\pi^{\prime}}\oplus W_{\pi^{\prime}{}^{c}})\neq 0.
Since
W_{\pi}
is isomorphic to
\pi
and is hence irreducible, we have that
W_{\pi}\cap(W_{\pi^{\prime}}\oplus W_{\pi^{\prime}{}^{c}})=W_{\pi}
and therefore, by uniqueness of Whittaker models,
W_{\pi}
must be isomorphic to one of
W_{\pi^{\prime}}
or
W_{\pi^{\prime}{}^{c}}
.

Second, suppose
\pi
is not isomorphic to
\pi^{c}
. We have that
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}\in W_{\pi}
. We also have that
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime},\psi}+{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{\prime}{}^{c},\psi}-{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi^{c},\psi}\in W_{\pi^{\prime}}\oplus W_{\pi^{\prime}{}^{c}}\oplus W_{\pi^{c}}
. Thus,
W_{\pi}\cap(W_{\pi^{\prime}}\oplus W_{\pi^{\prime}{}^{c}}\oplus W_{\pi^{c}})\neq 0
and hence the intersection is a nonzero subrepresentation of
W_{\pi}
. Since
W_{\pi}
is isomorphic to
\pi
and is hence irreducible, we have that
W_{\pi}\cap(W_{\pi^{\prime}}\oplus W_{\pi^{\prime}{}^{c}}\oplus W_{\pi^{c}})=W_{\pi}
and therefore, by uniqueness of Whittaker models,
W_{\pi}
must be isomorphic to one of
W_{\pi^{c}},W_{\pi^{\prime}},
or
W_{\pi^{\prime}{}^{c}}
. By assumption,
W_{\pi}
is not isomorphic to
W_{\pi^{c}}
and hence must be isomorphic to
W_{\pi^{\prime}}
or
W_{\pi^{\prime}{}^{c}}
.

Therefore, in either case, we have shown the converse theorem.
∎

Assouad and quasi-Assouad dimensions of Moran sets
Jun-Jie Miao, Ming-Hui Xu

Abstract
. Moran sets are a non-autonomous generalization of self-similar sets. In this paper, we study the quasi-Assouad and Assouad dimensions of Moran sets in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . First we provide quasi-Assouad dimension formulae for Moran sets satisfying c ∗ > 0 c_{*}>0 . Then, we provide the upper and lower bounds for quasi-Assouad dimension formulae for Moran sets without assuming c ∗ > 0 c_{*}>0 . To obtain the exact dimension formulae in this case, we define quasi-normal and normal Moran sets, and provide quasi-Assouad dimension formulae for these sets.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Assouad and quasi-Assouad dimensions

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. For
A\subset X
and
r>0
, denote by
N_{r}(A)
the minimal number of balls of radius
r
required to cover
A
. The
Assouad dimension
of a non-empty subset
F
of
X
is defined by

\displaystyle\quad\dima F=\inf\Big\{\alpha:\text{there exist \ }C>0\text{\ and\ }0<\rho<1\text{\ such that for all\ }x\in F\textit{ and }

\displaystyle 0<r<R<\rho,\vskip 5.0pt\ N_{r}(B(x,R)\cap F)\leqslant C\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{\alpha}\Big\}.

We refer readers to
[
12
,
14
]
for background. The Assouad dimension was originally introduced in the study of bi-Lipschitz embeddings of metric spaces
[
1
]
. It has since become a fundamental notion in metric geometry and fractal analysis, capturing the most extreme local scaling behavior of sets through the maximal growth rate of covering numbers at small scales.
This property makes the Assouad dimension an important tool in areas such as embedding theory, geometric measure theory, and the analysis of dynamics on fractals, see
[
14
,
19
,
29
]
.

Recently, Wang and Zahl
[
31
]
proved that in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, every Kakeya set has Assouad dimension
3
and every Ahlfors-David regular Kakeya
set has Hausdorff dimension
3
. In
[
13
]
, Fraser systematically investigated the basic properties of Assouad-type dimensions and computed the Assouad dimensions for various classes of self-affine and quasi-self-similar sets. In
[
2
]
, Banaji and Fraser studied the Assouad type dimensions of limit sets of iterated function systems consisting of a countably infinite number of conformal contractions, and they obtained the Assouad dimension formula for such fractals under some separation conditions. We refer readers to
[
3
,
16
,
18
]
for various studies on this topic.

However, the Assouad dimension is highly sensitive to local irregularities, which often renders it less suitable for capturing the typical geometric structure of many fractal sets. To overcome this limitation, Lü and Xi
[
25
]
introduced the quasi-Assouad dimension, a more refined variant of the Assouad dimension. For
\eta\in(0,1)
, define

\displaystyle h_{F}(\eta)\!=\inf\Big\{\alpha:\text{there exist \ }C>0\text{\ and\ }0<\rho<1\text{\ such that}\text{ for all\ }x\in F\text{\ and\ }

\displaystyle\quad 0<r<R^{1+\eta}<R<\rho,\ N_{r}(B(x,R)\cap F)\leqslant C\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{\alpha}\Big\},

and the
quasi-Assouad dimension
of
F
is defined as

\dimqa F=\lim_{\eta\to 0}h_{F}(\eta).

It is worth noting that
h_{F}(\eta)
corresponds to the upper Assouad spectrum
{\overline{\dim}}^{\theta}_{\mathrm{A}}F
in
[
16
]
, where
\theta=\frac{1}{\eta+1}
.
The quasi-Assouad dimension provides a finer description of the geometric structure at intermediate scales while preserving key metric properties. For a compact subset
E
, the following chain of inequalities holds:

(1.1)

\dimh E\leqslant\odimb E\leqslant\dimqa E\leqslant\dima E,

where
\dimh
and
\odimb
denote the Hausdorff and upper box dimensions, respectively. We refer readers to
[
14
]
for further reading. In
[
17
]
, Fraser proved that all dimensions in (
1.1
) are equal for self-similar sets satisfying the weak separation condition. The inequalities in (
1.1
) can strictly hold; see Example
2.1
.

However, the study of the quasi-Assouad dimension in this setting remains relatively underdeveloped. Although some results for quasi-Assouad dimension have been established in
[
25
,
15
]
, explicit formulas and sharp bounds for Moran sets—especially in the general (possibly non-homogeneous) case—are still largely unexplored.

1.2.
Fractal dimensions of Moran sets

As a generalization of self-similar and Cantor-like constructions, Moran structures are distinguished by their ability to accommodate significant inhomogeneity. This inherent flexibility provides a rich framework, establishing them as a fundamental and widely applicable class of fractals. In
[
30
]
, Shmerkin studied Bernoulli convolutions defined on a class of homogeneous Moran sets and obtained their
L^{q}
dimensions under certain conditions. As an application, he settled Furstenberg’s long-standing conjecture on the dimension of intersections of
\times p
- and
\times q
-invariant sets. We refer readers to
[
4
,
20
,
5
,
26
,
10
,
11
]
for further studies on Moran structures.

Let
\{n_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 1}
be a sequence of integers with
n_{k}\geqslant 2.
For
k_{1}\leqslant k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, we write

\displaystyle D_{k_{1},k_{2}}=\{\mathbf{u}=u_{k_{1}}u_{k_{1}+1}\cdots u_{k_{2}}:1\leqslant u_{j}\leqslant n_{j}\ ,k_{1}\leqslant j\leqslant k_{2}\}.

For simplicity, we write
D_{k}=D_{1,k}
with
D_{0}=\{\emptyset\}
containing only the empty word
\emptyset
, and write

D^{*}=\bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty}D_{k}

for the set of all finite words. For
\mathbf{u}\in D_{k_{1},k_{2}}
, we denote by
\mathbf{u}^{-}
the word obtained from
\mathbf{u}
by removing its last symbol. For
\mathbf{i}=i_{1}\ldots i_{k}\in D_{k}
and
\mathbf{j}=j_{1}\ldots j_{l}\in D_{k+1,k+l}
, we denote by
\mathbf{i}*\mathbf{j}=i_{1}\ldots i_{k}j_{1}\ldots j_{l}\in D_{k+l}
the concatenation of
\mathbf{i}
and
\mathbf{j}
.

Let
J\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set with
\mbox{int}(J)\neq\varnothing
, where int(
\cdot)
denotes the interior of a set. Let
\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 1}
be a sequence of positive real vectors where
\mathbf{c}_{k}=(c_{k,1},c_{k,2},\cdots,c_{k,n_{k}})
and
\sum_{j=1}^{n_{k}}c_{k,j}^{\,d}\leqslant 1
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We say the
collection
\mathcal{F}=\{J_{\mathbf{u}}:\mathbf{u}\in D^{*}\}
of closed
subsets of
J
fulfills the
Moran structure
if it satisfies the
following Moran structure conditions (MSC):

(1)

For each
\mathbf{u}\in D^{*}
,
J_{\mathbf{u}}
is geometrically similar to

J
, i.e., there exists a similarity
\Psi_{\mathbf{u}}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
J_{\mathbf{u}}=\Psi_{\mathbf{u}}(J)
. We write
J_{\emptyset}=J
for empty word
\emptyset
.

(2)

For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{u}\in D_{k-1}
, the elements
J_{\mathbf{u}*1},J_{\mathbf{u}*2},\cdots,J_{\mathbf{u}*n_{k}}
of
\mathcal{F}

are the subsets of
J_{\mathbf{u}}
with
\mbox{int}(J_{\mathbf{u}*i})\cap\mbox{int}(J_{\mathbf{u}*i^{\prime}})=\varnothing

for
i\neq i^{\prime}
and

\frac{|J_{\mathbf{u}*i}|}{|J_{\mathbf{u}}|}=c_{k,i},

for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant n_{k}
, where
|\cdot|
denotes the diameter.

The non-empty compact set

(1.2)

E=E(\mathcal{F})=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\,\bigcup_{\mathbf{u}\in D_{k}}J_{\mathbf{u}}

is called a
Moran set
determined by
\mathcal{F}
or
(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
. We write
\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
for the collection of all sets determined by
\mathcal{F}
. For all
\mathbf{u}\in D_{k}
, the elements
J_{\mathbf{u}}

are called

k
th-level basic sets
of
E
. We say that
\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
or
E
is
homogeneous
if for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a number
c_{k}
such that
c_{k,j}=c_{k},1\leqslant j\leqslant n_{k}
. We refer readers to
[
32
]
for the background reading.

In the article, we always assume
J=[0,1]^{d}
. For
p,q\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
with
1\leqslant p<q
and
\mathbf{u}=u_{p}\ldots u_{q}\in D_{p,q}
, we denote
c_{\mathbf{u}}=c_{p,u_{p}}c_{p+1,u_{p+1}}\cdots c_{q,u_{q}}
. We also write

\begin{split}\overline{M}_{p,q}=\max_{\mathbf{u}\in D_{p,q}}c_{\mathbf{u}},\quad&\quad\underline{M}_{p,q}=\min_{\mathbf{u}\in D_{p,q}}c_{\mathbf{u}}\\
\overline{c}_{k}=\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant n_{k}}c_{k,j},\quad&\quad\underline{c}_{k}=\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant n_{k}}c_{k,j},\end{split}

and
\overline{M}_{k}=\overline{M}_{1,k}=\max\limits_{\mathbf{u}\in D_{k}}c_{\mathbf{u}}
,
\underline{M}_{k}=\underline{M}_{1,k}=\min\limits_{\mathbf{u}\in D_{k}}c_{\mathbf{u}}
for simplicity. For
k\in\mathbb{N}^{+},l\in\mathbb{N}
, let
s_{k,k+l}
be the unique real solution of the equation

(1.3)

\prod_{i=k}^{k+l}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}c_{i,j}^{s}\Big)=1.

and write
s_{k}=s_{k,k}
for simplicity.

The fractal dimensions of Moran sets are usually determined by the double index sequence
\{s_{k,k+l}\}
. If
\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
satisfies
c_{*}=\inf_{k,j}c_{k,j}>0
, then, for any
E\in\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
,

(1.4)

\dimh E=\liminf_{l\to\infty}s_{1,l},\quad\odimb E=\limsup_{l\to\infty}s_{1,l},\quad\dima E=\limsup_{l\to\infty}\sup_{k\geqslant 1}s_{k+1,k+l}.

See
[
24
]
for details.
In
[
20
]
, Hua, Rao, wen and Wu proved that the Hausdorff and upper box dimension formulae in (
1.4
) still hold if
c_{*}>0
is relaxed to the following condition:

(1.5)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\underline{c}_{k}}{\log\overline{M}_{k}}=0.

Conditions
c_{*}>0
and (
1.5
) are most frequently used in the study of Moran-type sets.

Given
\mathbf{i}\in D^{*}
and
\delta>0
, we define

D_{\mathbf{i}}(\delta)=\{\mathbf{i}*\mathbf{j}\in D^{*}:c_{\mathbf{j}}\leqslant\delta<c_{\mathbf{j}^{-}}\}.

In particular, when
\mathbf{i}=\emptyset
, we write
D(\delta)
instead of
D_{\emptyset}(\delta)
; that is,

D(\delta)=\{\mathbf{j}\in D^{*}:c_{\mathbf{j}}\leqslant\delta<c_{\mathbf{j}^{-}}\}.

Recently, Käenmäki and Rutar
[
22
]
showed that the Assouad dimension in (
1.4
) remains valid if
c_{*}>0
is replaced by the bounded neighbourhood condition which bears a strong relation to the finite clustering property studied in
[
21
,
23
,
28
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Given
E\in\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
, we say that
E
satisfies the
bounded neighbourhood condition
(BNC) if

\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\sup_{x\in E}\#\left\{\mathbf{u}\in D(\delta):E\cap J_{\mathbf{u}}\cap B(x,\delta)\neq\emptyset\right\}<\infty.

In Lemma
5.1
, we show that (
1.5
) implies a weak version of BNC.
In
[
22
]
, Käenmäki and Rutar also introduced the following.

Definition 1.2
.

Given
\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
, we say that
\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
satisfied the
bounded branching condition
(BBC) if

\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\sup_{\mathbf{u}\in D(\delta)}\#\left\{\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\in D(\delta):\mathbf{u}^{\prime}=\mathbf{u}^{-}*\mathbf{i}\text{\ for some }\mathbf{i}\,\right\}<\infty.

We prove that BBC is sufficient for the BNC (Proposition
5.4
), which partially extends
[
22
, Theorem B]
. An equivalent characterization of BBC is provided in Proposition
3.1
. Observe that the implication
c_{*}>0\Rightarrow(\text{\eqref{club}}\text{ and }\text{BBC})
is straightforward (Corollary
3.5
).

Note that conditions (
1.5
) and BBC are mutually independent. They control different aspects of the Moran structure. Specifically, (
1.5
) bounds the relative scaling between the “thinnest” and “fattest” basic sets across levels, and the BBC
[
22
]
restricts the number of “geometric siblings” in
D(\delta)
. The essential difference between the BNC and the two aforementioned conditions lies in that the BNC depends strongly on the locations of the sets in
\mathcal{F}=\{J_{\mathbf{u}}:\mathbf{u}\in D^{*}\}
, whereas (
1.5
) and BBC do not.
Examples illustrating these cases are presented below, with proofs given in Section
6
.

Example 1.1
.

(1)
The Moran structure
\mathcal{M}\big(J,\{n_{k}\equiv 2\},\big\{(2^{-2^{k}},2^{-2^{k}})\big\}_{k\geqslant 1}\big)
satisfies BBC but fails to satisfy (
1.5
).

(2) The Moran structure
\mathcal{M}\big(J,\{k+1\}_{k\geqslant 1},\big\{\big(\frac{1}{2k},\ldots,\frac{1}{2k}\big)\big\}_{k\geqslant 1}\big)
satisfies (
1.5
) but fails to satisfy BBC.

The following example was suggested by Alex Rutar in a private conversation, illustrating that the BNC does not necessarily imply BBC.

Example 1.2
.

Let
E\in\mathcal{M}([0,1],\{n_{k}\equiv 2\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
, where

c_{k,1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{1}{1+2^{n}},&k=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\\
\frac{1}{2},&\text{otherwise}\\
\end{array},\right.\ c_{k,2}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{2^{n}}{1+2^{n}},&k=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\\
\frac{1}{2},&\text{otherwise}\\
\end{array},\right.\ n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}.

Assume that for each
\mathbf{i}\in D^{*}
, the left and right endpoints of

J_{\mathbf{i}}
coincide respectively with the left endpoint of

J_{\mathbf{i}*1}
and the right endpoint of
J_{\mathbf{i}*2}
. Then
E=[0,1]
satisfies the BNC, but BBC does not hold.

In
[
27
,
33
]
, the authors provide a different Assouad dimension formula for homogeneous Moran sets
E\in\mathcal{J}(I_{0},\{n_{k}\},\{c_{k}\},\{\eta_{k,j}\})
with
\sup_{k}n_{k}<\infty
,

\dima E=\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{k\geqslant m,l\geqslant m}s_{k+1,k+l}.

Consequently, it is natural to ask under what conditions the two formulae are equivalent, that is,

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{k\geqslant m,l\geqslant m}s_{k+1,k+l}=\limsup_{l\to\infty}\sup_{k\geqslant 1}s_{k+1,k+l}.

A sufficient condition for this equality is derived as a direct consequence of our principal conclusions; see Propostion
2.2
and Section
6
for details.

In the paper, we study the Assouad and quasi-Assouad dimensions of Moran sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and establish our main findings in the subsequent section.

2.
Notation and main results

Given
\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
, we write

(2.1)

\begin{split}&t^{*}=\lim_{\eta\to 0}\limsup_{l\to\infty}\sup_{k\in\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{l+1,\eta}}s_{k+1,k+l},\qquad t=\lim_{\eta\to 0}\limsup_{l\to\infty}\sup_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{l,\eta}}s_{k+1,k+l},\\
&t_{*}=\lim_{\eta\to 0}\limsup_{l\to\infty}\sup_{k\in\underline{\mathcal{K}}_{l,\eta}}s_{k+1,k+l},\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{l,\eta}=\Big\{k\geqslant 2:\frac{\log\underline{M}_{k+1,k+l}}{\log\overline{M}_{k-1}}>\eta\Big\},\qquad\mathcal{K}_{l,\eta}=\Big\{k:\frac{\log\underline{M}_{k+1,k+l}}{\log\overline{M}_{k}}>\eta\Big\},

\displaystyle\underline{\mathcal{K}}_{l,\eta}=\Big\{k:\frac{\log\overline{M}_{k+1,k+l}}{\log\overline{M}_{k}}>\eta\Big\}.

Then, for
0<\eta<\frac{\log\overline{c}_{2}}{\log\overline{c}_{1}}
,
1\in\underline{\mathcal{K}}_{l,\eta}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{l,\eta}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{l,\eta}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{l+1,\eta}
for all
l\geqslant 1
and thus

\displaystyle\limsup_{l\to\infty}s_{1,l}\leqslant t_{*}\leqslant t\leqslant t^{*}\leqslant\limsup_{l\to\infty}\sup_{k\geqslant 1}s_{k+1,k+l}.

Note that for each
\eta>0
, it is clear that
\bigcup_{l\geqslant 1}\,\mathcal{K}_{l,\eta}=\bigcup_{l\geqslant 1}\,\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{l,\eta}=\mathbb{N}^{+},
and

\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{l,\eta}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{l+1,\eta},\qquad\mathcal{K}_{l,\eta}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{l+1,\eta},\qquad\underline{\mathcal{K}}_{l,\eta}\subseteq\underline{\mathcal{K}}_{l+1,\eta}.

Moreover, if
\lim_{k\to\infty}\overline{M}_{k}=0
, then

\bigcup_{l\geqslant 1}\,\underline{\mathcal{K}}_{l,\eta}=\mathbb{N}^{+}.

For
c_{*}>0
, we obtain the quasi-Assouad dimension of Moran sets.

Theorem 2.1
.

If
\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
has
c_{*}>0
, then every
E\in\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
satisfies

\dimqa E=t_{*}=t=t^{*}.

To derive dimension formulae without
c_{*}>0
, we first provide two special results.

Proposition 2.2
.

If
\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
has
\lim_{k\to\infty}\overline{M}_{k}=0
, then for all
K\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
,

\limsup_{l\to\infty}\sup_{k\geqslant K}s_{k,k+l}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{k\geqslant m,l\geqslant m}s_{k,k+l}.

Proposition
2.2
serves to unify the Assouad dimension formulas presented in
[
27
,
33
]
and
[
24
]
. The assumption
\lim_{k\to\infty}\overline{M}_{k}=0
in Proposition
2.2
is crucial, as the proposition fails when the limit is positive; see Example
2.2
(i).

Theorem 2.3
.

If
\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
satisfies
\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\underline{c}_{k}}{\log\overline{M}_{k}}>0
and BBC, then every
E\in\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
satisfies

\dimqa E=\dima E=\limsup_{l\to\infty}\sup_{k\geqslant 1}s_{k+1,k+l}.

Theorem
2.3
in fact identifies a class of Moran sets that are quasi-Lipschitz Assouad-minimal (i.e., compact sets whose Assouad dimension does not decrease under any quasi-Lipschitz map). The detailed arguments can be found in
[
25
]
.

To investigate the quasi-Assouad dimensions of Moran sets without assuming
c_{*}>0
, we introduce the following definitions.

Definition 2.4
.

The Moran structure
\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
is said to be
quasi-normal
if there exists a strictly increasing function
\varphi:(0,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
such that

\limsup_{l\to\infty}\sup_{k\geqslant 1}\,\varphi\left(\frac{\log\underline{M}_{k+1,k+l}}{\log\overline{M}_{k}}\right)\bigg/\frac{\log\overline{M}_{k+1,k+l}}{\log\overline{M}_{k}}<\infty.

It is said to be
normal
if there also exists a strictly increasing function
\varphi:(0,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
such that

\limsup_{l\to\infty}\sup_{k\geqslant 2}\,\varphi\left(\frac{\log\underline{M}_{k+1,k+l+1}}{\log\overline{M}_{k-1}}\right)\bigg/\frac{\log\overline{M}_{k+1,k+l}}{\log\overline{M}_{k}}<\infty.

The generality of this definition is evidenced by two key cases: first, any
\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
satisfying
c_{*}>0
is normal and consequently quasi-normal (Proposition
3.7
); second, all homogeneous Moran structures are quasi-normal, dispensing with the
c_{*}>0
requirement altogether. As direct verification of these properties is often challenging, Proposition
3.7
offers a set of simpler and more verifiable criteria.

Next, we state our main results on quasi-Assouad dimensions of quasi-normal and normal Moran sets.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
be quasi-normal and satisfy (
1.5
). Then every
E\in\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
satisfies

\dimqa E=t_{*}=t.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
be quasi-normal and satisfy

(2.2)

\lim_{l\to\infty}\sup_{k\geqslant 1}\frac{\log\underline{c}_{k}}{\log\overline{M}_{k+1,k+l}}=\lim_{l\to\infty}\sup_{k\geqslant 1}\frac{\log\underline{c}_{k+l+1}}{\log\overline{M}_{k+1,k+l}}=0.

Then every
E\in\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
satisfies

\dimqa E=t_{*}=t=t^{*}.

The next conclusion is a direct consequence of Theorem
2.5
which was first proved by Lü and Xi in
[
25
]
,

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
be homogeneous with (
1.5
). Then every
E\in\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
satisfies

\dimqa E=t_{*}=t.

The final result concerns the quasi-Assouad dimensions of normal Moran sets.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
be normal and satisfy BBC. Then every
E\in\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
satisfies

\dimqa E=t_{*}=t^{*}.

Finally, we conclude by presenting two examples. The first example shows that the dimension inequality (
1.1
) is typically strict for Moran fractals. The second example demonstrates that the conclusions of Theorems
2.5
and
2.8
may fail when either (
1.5
) or BBC does not hold, and that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\overline{M}_{k}>0
does not necessarily imply that the sequence
\{s_{k,k+l}\}
exhibits “trivial” behavior.

Example 2.1
.

Let
{a_{n}}
be a sequence of positive integers with
a_{1}=1
, satisfying
a_{n+1}>2a_{n}+n
for all
n
, and assume that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n}{a_{n}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n-1}}{a_{n}}=0.

Let
E\in\mathcal{M}([0,1],\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
, where

n_{k}\equiv 2,\quad c_{k,1}=\frac{1}{2},\quad c_{k,2}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{4},&a_{n}\leqslant k<2a_{n}\\
\frac{1}{2},&2a_{n}\leqslant k<2a_{n}+n\\
\frac{1}{8},&2a_{n}+n\leqslant k<a_{n+1}\end{array}\right.,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}.

Then the structure is normal, and the dimensions of
E
satisfy

\dimh E=-\frac{\log p}{\log 2}<\odimb E=-\frac{\log q}{\log 2}<\dimqa E=-\frac{\log r}{\log 2}<\dima E=1,

where
p,q,r
satisfy
p+p^{3}=(q+q^{2})(q+q^{3})=r+r^{2}=1.

Example 2.2
.

Given
0<\alpha<\beta
, let
E\in\mathcal{M}([0,1],\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
where

n_{k}\equiv 2,\quad c_{k,1}=1-(k+1)^{-\alpha},\quad c_{k,2}=(k+1)^{-\beta}.

Then the structure is not quasi-normal, and

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{k\geqslant m,l\geqslant m}s_{k,k+l}=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}.

Assume that for any
\mathbf{i}\in D^{*}

3.
Quasi-normal sets

The properties of BBC, quasi-normality, and normality for a Moran structure are inherently difficult to verify directly from their definitions. To remedy this, we establish several alternative characterizations that are substantially easier to check in this section.

Proposition 3.1
.

For the Moran structure
\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
, define

\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})

\sup_{k\geqslant 1}b_{k}<\infty\text{\ and }\sup_{k\geqslant 1}n_{k}<\infty.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that for each

,

Denote by
\mathbf{u}_{k}=11\ldots 1\in D_{k}
.

Suppose that BBC holds, and let

for

. Since
\underline{c}_{k}^{d}\leqslant 1-\overline{c}_{k}^{d}
for every

. We obtain that

\{\mathbf{u}\in D(\delta_{k}):\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}_{m}*i*\mathbf{j}\text{\ for some\ }\mathbf{j}\,\}\neq\emptyset

for

. Note that the case

may occur.

Therefore, it follows that

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\sup_{\mathbf{u}\in D(\delta_{k})}\#\{\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\in D(\delta_{k}):\mathbf{u}^{\prime}=\mathbf{u}^{-}*\mathbf{i}\text{\ for\ some\ }\mathbf{i}\}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\#\{\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\in D(\delta_{k}):\mathbf{u}^{\prime}=\mathbf{u}_{k-1}*\mathbf{i}\text{\ for\ some\ }\mathbf{i}\}

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{b_{k}}n_{k+i}-b_{k}\Big).

Moreover, the preceding argument implies that

Conversely, suppose that

and
\sup_{k}n_{k}<N

and it implies that

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbf{u}\in D(\delta)}\#\{\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\in D(\delta):\mathbf{u}^{\prime}=\mathbf{u}^{-}*\mathbf{i}\text{ for some }\mathbf{i}\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{\mathbf{u}\in D(\delta)}\#D_{|\mathbf{u}|,|\mathbf{u}|+L-1}\leqslant N^{L}.

Theorem 5.3

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
satisfy the BBC. Then every
E\in\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
satisfies the BNC.

Proof.

Given
\delta>0
, let
\mathcal{S}(\delta)=\big\{\mathbf{u}^{-}:\mathbf{u}\in D(\delta)\big\}
. For
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{S}(\delta)
, remove
\mathbf{v}
from
\mathcal{S}(\delta)
if
\mathbf{v}*\mathbf{i}\in\mathcal{S}(\delta)
for some
\mathbf{i}
, and we denote the resulting set by
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\delta)
. Note that
|J_{\mathbf{u}}|=c_{\mathbf{u}}>\delta
for any
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\delta)
and the elements of
\{\mbox{int}(J_{\mathbf{u}}):\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\delta)\}
are pairwise disjoint.

Since in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, a ball of radius
\delta
can intersect at most finitely many pairwise disjoint balls whose radii exceed
\frac{\delta}{2}
, there exists a constant
N_{d}
depending only on the ambient dimension
d
such that

\sup_{\delta>0,x\in E}\#\big\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\delta):E\cap J_{\mathbf{u}}\cap B(x,\delta)\neq\emptyset\big\}<N_{d}.

It then follows that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in E}\#\big\{\mathbf{u}\in D(\delta):E\cap J_{\mathbf{u}}\,\cap

\displaystyle\,B(x,\delta)\neq\emptyset\big\}

\displaystyle<N_{d}\sup_{\mathbf{u}\in D(\delta)}\#\big\{\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\in D(\delta):\mathbf{u}^{\prime}=\mathbf{u}^{-}*\mathbf{i}\text{\ for some }\mathbf{i}\big\}.

Since
\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
satisfies the BBC, the conclusion holds by taking
\delta\to 0
.
∎

Theorem 5.5
.

\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})

\mathbf{i}\in D^{*}

\mathbf{v}

\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{i}}(\delta)

\mathbf{j}

N

4.1

1.1
) and (
2.1

\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})

Lemma 5.6
.

For the Moran structure
\mathcal{M}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
, the following statements hold.

(i)

\lim_{l\to\infty}\sup_{k\geqslant 1}\frac{\log\underline{c}_{k}}{\log\overline{M}_{k+1,k+l}}=0

\eta>0

\limsup_{l\to\infty}\sup_{k\in\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{l,\eta}}s_{k+1,k+l}=\limsup_{l\to\infty}\sup_{k\in\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{l,\eta}}s_{k,k+l}.

(ii)

If
\lim_{l\to\infty}\sup_{k\geqslant 1}\frac{\log\underline{c}_{k+l+1}}{\log\overline{M}_{k+1,k+l}}=0

\eta>0

\limsup_{l\to\infty}\sup_{k\in\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{l+1,\eta}}s_{k+1,k+l}=\limsup_{l\to\infty}\sup_{k\in\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{l+1,\eta}}s_{k+1,k+l+1}=\limsup_{l\to\infty}\sup_{k\in\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{l,\eta}}s_{k+1,k+l}.

s_{k,k}

5.12
) holds with equality.

Case B. If
s_{k}>s_{k+1,k+l}

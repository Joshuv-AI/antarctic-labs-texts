Dataset on composite efficiency indices for ASEAN foreign exchange markets
Authors: Tran Trong Huynh, Nguyen Thi Kieu An
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis data article presents a reproducible dataset designed to measure time-varying efficiency in selected ASEAN foreign exchange (FX) markets. The dataset covers six ASEAN currencies against the U.S. dollar—Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, and Vietnam—over the period January 2000 to August 2025. Daily exchange rate observations (open, high, low, and close) are obtained from publicly accessible financial data providers and official sources. These raw observations are not redistributed; instead, they serve as inputs to a fully documented transformation pipeline.Using rolling-window procedures applied to daily data, ten efficiency sub-indices are constructed to capture distinct dimensions of deviations from random-walk behavior, including serial dependence, volatility dynamics, long-memory characteristics, distributional asymmetry, and tail risk. All sub-indices are standardized and aggregated through principal component analysis to form a Composite Efficiency Index (CEI), yielding a harmonized monthly measure that is comparable across markets and over time.The repository provides the processed monthly efficiency sub-indices, the Composite Efficiency Index, and complete R scripts that reproduce the entire data-generation workflow. By separating data acquisition from transformation and ensuring full computational reproducibility, the dataset supports transparent empirical research on FX market efficiency, cross-country comparisons, forecasting applications, and methodological extensions in financial econometrics.Keywords: Foreign exchange markets, Market efficiency, Composite efficiency index, ASEAN, Financial econometrics

Data Description

The repository contains the processed monthly efficiency sub-indices (emh_subindices_fx_10idx_Hlocal.csv), the Composite Efficiency Index in CSV format (emh_composite_fx_10idx_Hlocal.csv), a fully documented R script (CEI.R) that reproduces the complete data-generation workflow, and a license file specifying usage terms.

The R script is designed to transform publicly accessible daily foreign exchange data—daily open, high, low, and close exchange rates for six ASEAN currencies against the U.S. dollar—into a structured set of monthly efficiency indicators. In accordance with third-party data policies, the repository does not redistribute the original daily exchange rate files. Users are required to independently obtain the raw daily series from the respective public sources and execute the provided script to reproduce all derived measures.

Using rolling 12-month windows applied to daily observations, ten efficiency sub-indices are constructed to capture distinct dimensions of deviations from random-walk behavior. These include measures based on return autocorrelation, variance ratios, volatility clustering, range-based volatility predictability, long-memory characteristics, distributional asymmetry, excess kurtosis, return decomposition (overnight versus intraday), tail-event frequency, and extreme-movement intensity. The resulting statistics are aggregated and reported at the monthly frequency for each market.

The standardized sub-indices are subsequently combined using principal component analysis to produce the CEI, which serves as a harmonized monthly measure of market inefficiency across ASEAN foreign exchange markets. Experimental Design, Materials, and Methods

Data collection and preprocessing

Daily foreign exchange rates for six ASEAN currencies against the U.S. dollar (IDR, MYR, PHP, SGD, THB, and VND) are collected from publicly available financial data providers. For each market, the raw data include open, high, low, and close prices. All series are aligned using market-specific trading calendars. Non-trading days due to local holidays are retained as missing observations and are not forward-filled. This approach ensures that all subsequent calculations rely solely on information that would have been available to market participants at the time.

Daily log-returns are computed from closing prices and serve as the primary input for return-based measures. Overnight and intraday returns are additionally constructed from open and close prices to support return decomposition indicators.

Rolling-window construction

Efficiency measures are computed using rolling windows applied to daily data. For each market and each calendar month, a fixed-length rolling window covering the preceding 12 months of daily observations is extracted. All statistics within a given window are calculated using only information available up to the end of the corresponding month. The resulting values are assigned to that month, ensuring that the dataset is free from look-ahead bias and suitable for time-series and panel applications.

The rolling-window design allows the sub-indices to capture gradual changes in market efficiency over time while maintaining sufficient observations for stable estimation. Rolling-window estimation is commonly used to capture time variation in market efficiency [1].

Construction of efficiency sub-indices

Within each rolling window, ten efficiency sub-indices are constructed to capture distinct deviations from random-walk behavior in foreign exchange markets. These include indicators based on return autocorrelation, variance ratios, volatility clustering, volatility predictability from price ranges, long-memory behavior, distributional asymmetry, excess kurtosis, return decomposition between overnight and intraday periods, tail-event intensity, and extreme price movements. Each sub-index is designed such that larger values correspond to stronger deviations from the random-walk benchmark. All indicators are computed from daily observations and aggregated to the monthly level. Several sub-indices are based on autocorrelation and variance-ratio diagnostics commonly applied in efficiency testing [2]. Range-based volatility measures are constructed following established estimators in the literature [3,4].

The construction of the efficiency sub-indices builds on established empirical methodologies widely used in the market efficiency literature, including autocorrelation diagnostics, variance-ratio testing, range-based volatility estimation, and long-memory analysis [1,[5], [6], [7], [8]]. These approaches are integrated into a unified and reproducible measurement framework tailored to ASEAN foreign exchange markets.

The full implementation details, including parameter choices and estimation procedures, are documented in the accompanying R script.

Standardization

Because the raw sub-indices differ in scale and dispersion, direct aggregation is not appropriate. Each efficiency sub-index is therefore standardized using a pooled panel normalization approach. Specifically, for each sub-index j, values are transformed into z-scores, zi,t,j=xi,t,j−μjσj, where μj and σj denote the mean and standard deviation computed across all markets and months in the sample. This pooled standardization ensures cross-market comparability while preserving relative time variation within each market.

To mitigate the influence of extreme observations, standardized values are truncated to the interval [−3, 3]. This clipping procedure reduces the impact of outliers without altering the overall distributional structure of the indicators.

Construction of the composite efficiency index

The standardized sub-indices are aggregated using principal component analysis (PCA). PCA is applied to the pooled matrix of standardized sub-indices across all markets and time periods. Because normalization is performed prior to aggregation, the PCA is estimated without additional centering or scaling. The first principal component (PC1) is retained as the Composite Efficiency Index. In the pooled panel, PC1 explains 34.4% of the total variance of the standardized sub-indices, indicating that a single latent factor captures a substantial share of the common variation across efficiency measures.

To ensure interpretability, the sign of PC1 is oriented such that higher CEI values consistently correspond to stronger deviations from the random-walk benchmark and thus lower levels of market efficiency. Monthly CEI values are reported for each market and constitute the final output of the data-generation process.

Implementation and reproducibility

All data processing and index construction steps are implemented in R and fully documented in the script provided in the repository. The script reproduces the complete workflow, from raw daily exchange rates to the final monthly Composite Efficiency Index. Users can modify key design choices, such as window length, standardization rules, or aggregation methods, and regenerate the dataset without altering the underlying raw data. This design facilitates replication, validation, and methodological extension.

Data Validation and Descriptive Features

Data consistency and integrity

Data integrity is ensured through a transparent and fully reproducible transformation pipeline linking the underlying raw exchange rate data, the processed efficiency sub-indices, and the Composite Efficiency Index. All derived variables are generated exclusively from the raw daily data using a single, documented R workflow. Time indices, country identifiers, and variable names are harmonized across all files, enabling users to trace each output variable back to its original data source. To verify internal consistency, the PCA step was independently re-executed using the processed sub-index file, reproducing identical CEI scores. All computations were performed in R version 4.5.0 (2025–04–11).

Missing observations arise only from market-specific non-trading days and from the rolling-window design at the beginning of the sample period. No interpolation or forward-filling is applied at any stage of the data construction process.

Limitations

The dataset relies on publicly available daily exchange rate data and focuses on bilateral rates against the U.S. dollar. The rolling-window design reduces early-sample coverage, and intraday microstructure features are not captured.

Ethics Statement

This study does not involve human participants or confidential data. All data are obtained from public sources.

2.Variance ratio–based inefficiency index: inef_vr=∑k∈{2,5,10}|VR(k)−1|, where VR(k)=Var(∑i=1krt+1)kVar(rt).

Variance ratio–based inefficiency index: inef_vr=∑k∈{2,5,10}|VR(k)−1|, where VR(k)=Var(∑i=1krt+1)kVar(rt).

Volatility clustering–based inefficiency index: inef_vc=∑k=1p[|ρk(|rt|)|+|ρk(rt2)|]

4.Range-based volatility predictability index: inef_rvp=∑k=1p||ρk(σtrange)||

Range-based volatility predictability index: inef_rvp=∑k=1p||ρk(σtrange)||

5.Hurst exponent deviation index: inef_hurst=|H−0.5|,where H is the rolling-window Hurst exponent.

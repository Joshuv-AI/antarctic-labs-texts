Efficacy and mechanism of transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation for angina severity in patients with chronic coronary syndromes: study protocol for a multicentre randomised controlled trial
Authors: Mengqi Li, Ziyang Zhang, Xiangmu Gai, Mengyuan Li, Tie Li, Mengmeng Sun, Min He, Xiaobo Jiang, Chenfeng Zhang, Yanxin Wang, Hongfeng Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionStimulating acupoints is beneficial for improving heart health but the clinical efficacy of transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS) as a complementary therapy for chronic coronary syndromes (CCSs) remains unclear. This study aims to evaluate whether TEAS can alleviate angina severity in patients with CCS and to explore the potential mechanisms underlying TEAS.Methods and analysisThis study, conducted across two clinical centres, involved 90 participants distributed equally into three groups via simple randomisation (1:1:1 ratio). The research cycle was 28 weeks including a 4-week baseline, 12-week treatment and 12-week follow-up period. All groups will receive basic treatment with the TEAS group additionally receiving 36 sessions of TEAS stimulation over the 12 weeks. The two control groups will either undergo sham TEAS or no additional intervention alongside their basic treatment. The primary outcome is the 6-minute walk test; eight other indicators will serve as secondary outcomes.Ethics and disseminationApproval for this study was granted by the Medical Research Ethics Committee of the Third Clinical Affiliated Hospital of Changchun University of Chinese Medicine in May 2023. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications.Trial registration numberChiCTR2400079383.Keywords: Protocols & guidelines, Cardiovascular disease, Electric stimulation therapy

Introduction

According to a global epidemiological survey in 2020, cardiovascular diseases accounted for 31% of global deaths with the leading cause of death being coronary artery disease (CAD).1 Despite receiving conventional comprehensive treatment such as revascularisation, 20% of patients with CAD still experience cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events 5 years later.2 Therefore, the importance of daily cardiac rehabilitation in chronic coronary syndrome (CCS) is evident.

Based on Chinese traditional medical experience, stimulation of specific acupoints can be beneficial for heart health such as Neiguan (PC6) and Zusanli (ST36).3 4 A multicentre randomised controlled trial5 in 2019 demonstrated that acupuncture on related meridians can relieve the symptoms of angina pectoris but due to the complexity of acupuncture and technology, this method of acupoint stimulation cannot be used for home rehabilitation. Transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS) has emerged as a new technology combining traditional acupoint and transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS) principles. During the past three decades, TEAS technology has rapidly developed. It has widened its fields of application from controlling pain to regulating the autonomic nervous system.6 7 Thus, TEAS is used to control postoperative pain and reduce myocardial injury.8 9 Against this background, it is feasible to use TEAS to control pain symptoms and regulate the cardiac autonomic nervous system in patients with CCS.

The pathogenesis of CCS is closely linked to lipid infiltration.10 Lipid mediators, essential chemicals in living organisms, can be converted into highly active compounds through processes such as fatty acid oxidation. Among these, the oxidative products generated by lipid molecules via endogenous peroxidase and reactive oxygen species pathways are known as oxylipins.11 In recent years, oxylipins have emerged as circulating biomarkers of the cardiovascular system and hold promise as new therapeutic targets for improving cardiovascular health.12 Whether TEAS has a regulatory effect on lipids, especially oxylipins, deserves further exploration.

Therefore, we designed this trial to mainly study the effectiveness of TEAS in the treatment of CCS and to explore the physiological mechanism of oxylipins metabolism underlying this treatment method.

Methods and analysis

Design

This study is a two-centre, single-blind, randomised controlled, exploratory trial in China. 90 participants will be involved adhering to simple randomisation principles. Participants will be recruited from the Affiliated Hospital of Changchun University of Chinese Medicine and the third Affiliated Hospital of Changchun University of Chinese Medicine; both hospitals are tertiary A hospitals. Trial flow chart.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public will not be involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination of this research.

Sample size

This trial is an exploratory study with no prior relevant clinical trials available. According to Julious’s theory, the accuracy of parameters plays a critical role in determining the sample size, sample size estimation can be approached from two perspectives: Mean and variance. If the expected variation in the population is minimal, a sample size of 12 cases per group may be sufficient. However, if variation is expected to be high, a sample size of at least 30 cases per group is recommended to maximise parameter accuracy.13 Given the potential diversity in demographic characteristics and baseline disease levels within the population of this trial and to ensure the feasibility of the trial, each group will consist of 30 cases.

Randomisation, blinding and concealment

Participants will be randomly assigned to one of three groups using SPSS V.28.0 statistical software for random number generation. The distribution ratio between the three groups is 1:1:1. The randomly grouped information will be hidden in opaque envelopes and assigned to two hospitals by dedicated personnel. After eligible participants complete the first examination, the envelopes are opened and clinical operators will know specific treatment measures but the subjects, evaluators and statisticians will not be aware of the grouping situation (the group of subjects who only receive basic treatment cannot be blinded but the other two groups can be blinded due to the placebo setting).

Participants

Study population

The population recruited for this study is patients with CCS and the specific conditions are as follows.

Inclusion criteria

Participants will be included if they: (1) Have been diagnosed with CCS based on the 2019 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines;14 (2) exhibit stable angina; (3) fall within angina pectoris severity classifications of Grade I, II or III as per the Canadian Cardiovascular Society; (4) are aged between 30 and 80 years regardless of gender; (5) have experienced angina pectoris for more than 3 months with episodes occurring more than two times per week; (6) have signed informed consent and voluntarily agreed to participate in the trial.

Exclusion criteria

Patients with any of the following conditions will be excluded: (1) Poorly controlled blood pressure and glucose; (2) serious cardiovascular diseases such as severe cardiopulmonary insufficiency, severe cardiac arrhythmia, severe arterial stenosis or aortic dissection; (3) severe cerebrovascular, liver, kidney, blood system disease or malignant tumours; (4) have bleeding tendencies or allergic constitution; (5) installed a cardiac pacemaker; (6) pregnant or lactating women or women who have fertility requirements in the past 6 months; (7) mental illness or unwillingness to cooperate; (8) participation in other clinical trials within the past 3 months.

Termination criteria

If the following situations exist, the patient needs to terminate this trial: (1) Severe adverse reactions occur during the study making further participation inadvisable; (2) the subjects experienced severe complications or worsening of the condition requiring emergency measures; (3) the subject requested to withdraw from the clinical study midway; (4) patients who do not cooperate and do not comply with treatment are ineffective after repeated explanations by clinical doctors.

Eliminate and dropout criteria

Eliminate and dropout criteria include following circumstances: (1) Cases mistakenly included; (2) failure to receive treatment according to regulations or incomplete information affecting the evaluation of efficacy and safety; (3) subjects who have poor compliance and voluntarily withdraw from the treatment course; (4) those who combine the prohibited treatment methods in this plan or change treatment methods midway on their own; (5) those who have experienced severe adverse events or complications and are not suitable for further treatment and have been discontinued from the trial.

Recruitment procedures

To recruit qualified patients, we have developed the following plan: (1) Electronic recruitment information will be displayed on hospital information screens and WeChat official accounts of the two centre hospitals; (2) propaganda posters will be put in outpatient clinical departments; 3 (3) participants in the outpatient department and inpatient department will be told the details about this trial by clinicians. After the recruitment, researchers will screen patients based on inclusion/exclusion criteria and only eligible patients will be included in the trial.

Interventions

Considering participants’ rights and security, participants in each group will accept the basic treatment as recommended by the 2019 ESC guideline; also, based on the domestic guideline and clinical practice, nitro-glycerine and Suxiao Jiuxin pills will be used for participants who are experiencing an acute angina attack.

The final intervention for this trial has been revised based on clinical acupuncture specialists’ suggestions. TEAS group will be applied to the experimental group while participants in one control group will receive sham-TEAS and in the other group will only receive basic treatment. The detailed procedures for each group are shown in table 1. The whole research period will last for 28 weeks including a 4-week baseline, 12-week treatment and 12-week follow-up period. During the treatment period, participants in the TEAS group will receive TEAS treatment for 36 sessions over 12 weeks. Clinical doctors will provide health guidance to ensure medical adherence and participants will be required to record angina diaries.

Basic treatment

Basic treatment includes health education and conventional drug therapy, ensuring all patients in this trial maintain a healthy lifestyle. Drug treatment adheres to a combination of antiplatelet drugs (aspirin, clopidogrel, ticagrelor), beta-blockers, renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system inhibitors (angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitors) and statins as per the guidelines.14 Basic treatment runs through the entire study cycle.

Anti-angina treatment

In case of angina attacks during the study, participants will receive anti-angina therapies based on their medical history and specific contraindications. In general, we recommend nitro-glycerine as the first choice and given the medical situation and guidelines in China, we also provide Suxiao Jiuxin pills to help with remission.

Researchers will provide these two types of drugs for free: Nitro-glycerine (made by Shandong Xinyi Pharmaceutical, Shandong, China, SFDA: H37021445) sublingual dose of 0.5 mg. Suxiao Jiuxin pills (made by Zhongxin Pharma Tianjin No. 6 Traditional Chinese Medicine Factory, Tianjin, China, SFDA: Z12020025) sublingual dose of 5–10 pills. Other anti-angina drugs are prohibited in this experiment for which the patient would be eliminated.

TEAS group

Neiguan Point (PC6) and Zusanli (ST36) will be used as treatment points, for they are the commonly used sensitised acupoints in CAD15 and can stimulate autonomic nervous, improving myocardial ischaemia.16,18

All therapists involved have at least 5 years of experience and hold a Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) license. During the operation, 75% alcohol is needed to apply to the treated area to enhance electrical conductivity; then, the TEAS instrument (made by Beurer GmbH, Germany) is activated, electrical piece (30×30 mm) will be put in treatment points to induce stimulation for 20 min with 2/10 HZ, dilatational wave, current stimulation intensity ranges from 0.1 mA to 10 mA to take local muscles jump and make participants feel comfortable. Following the session, any unusual symptoms experienced by participants will be recorded.

Sham-TEAS group

To evaluate the impact of the placebo effect of TEAS, we used a control method of sham-TEAS. All procedures mimic those of the TEAS group except the current stimulation intensity is set to 0 mA ensuring no therapeutic effect.

Basic treatment group

Patients in this group will only receive health education and drug treatment.

Follow-up

This trial includes a 12-week follow-up period, medical examinations will occur in the 4th and 12th weeks after the end of treatment. We will remind the patients to participate in the follow-up regularly via phone. Patient schedules of the whole study are shown in table 2.

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions and follow-up

The protocol’s compliance rate is expected to be high due to (1) the safety of the TEAS treatment scheme; (2) the supervision of treatment by professional doctors; (3) the provision of professional guidance and health education to patients.

To encourage continued participation and minimise dropout rates, each patient will receive a TEAS instrument and a transportation subsidy on completing the trial.

Outcome indicators

The primary outcome indicator is the 6-minute walk test while the secondary outcome indicators are the frequency of angina attacks, the Seattle Angina Scale Score, ECG changes, 24-hour dynamic ECG (including heart rate variability), Visual Analogue Scale score of angina attacks and dosage of nitro-glycerine or Suxiao Jiuxin pills, serum hypersensitive C-reactive protein, lipid profile.

The above outcome indicators will be evaluated on the baseline period, the last day of the 4th, 8th and 12th week of the treatment period and the last day of the 4th and 8th week of the follow-up period, respectively.

Adverse indicators include blood routine, urine routine, stool routine, liver function and kidney function. In addition, we will also record adverse events and major adverse cardiovascular events to evaluate the safety of TEAS. Additional indicators include serum oxylipidomics.

Adverse indicators and additional indicators will be evaluated on the baseline period and the last day of the whole treatment period.

Quality control

All researchers and relevant personnel participating in this study must undergo training courses to ensure all procedures are standardised and consistent.

Safety and adverse events

In the event of adverse reactions, emergency and cardiovascular department doctors will intervene with appropriate preventive measures and emergency medical plans. Adverse events include discomfort from TEAS such as pain or fainting; adverse medication reactions like headaches, nausea and vomiting; and serious adverse events including death, life-threatening incidents and hospitalisation. Adverse events should be recorded in detail whether they relate to TEAS. At the same time, researchers should take necessary measures according to the specific situation until participants get stabilised.

Variables evaluation

Evaluation of subject compliance

Subject compliance is assessed using the treatment frequency counting method. The compliance rate is calculated as follows:

A subject is considered compliant if they have received 80% or more of the prescribed treatments.

Evaluation of adverse events

Researchers involved in the treatment must record and report both adverse events and serious adverse events regardless of their relation to the treatment method under study. Detailed records should include: (1) Descriptions of adverse and serious adverse events including their onset time, termination time and duration; (2) the severity and frequency of these events; (3) information on the treatment method used including the amount, duration and rationale as well as the treatment outcomes; (4) an analysis of the causal relationship between the adverse events and the experimental treatment; (5) ongoing tracking of adverse and serious adverse events; (6) documentation of all related clinical data such as laboratory reports and ECGs in the original files.

In the event of a serious adverse event, the treatment method used should be immediately identified and the patient should be withdrawn from the trial and classified as a dropped case. The clinical trial monitor must be informed of the results.

Other variables evaluation

Additional variables such as age, gender and history condition must be recorded in detail before the experiment. If there are significant differences in these variables between the two groups or if certain factors significantly affect efficacy, these factors should be considered as covariates requiring analysis via covariance or logistic regression. A detailed list of concomitant medications should also be provided to document the situation thoroughly.

Statistical analysis

This experiment will use the modified safety data set, full analysis set and compliant protocol set for statistical analyses. Frequency (percentage) will be used to describe categorical data. Based on whether the variable is normally distributed, the mean (SD) or median (quartile range) will be used to describe quantitative data. Using Per-protocol (PP) and Intention-to-treat Analysis (ITT) analysis for major and global indicators. The significance level (p value) will be set to 0.05 if there are no special instructions. The Nemenyi test or Bonferroni method will be used to adjust the significance level of multiple comparisons of the main results. The statistical results of the primary outcome indicators will lead to the final experimental conclusion and the analysis of all secondary outcomes will support the results of the primary analysis. SAS V.9.4 software (SAS Institute, Cary, North Carolina, USA) is planned for statistical analysis of data results.

Three options will be used to process any missing data from the main results to test the robustness of the conclusions. First, any data lost at 12 weeks will be calculated using the last observation carryover method. Second, we will directly delete any missing data. Third, any missing data for 12 weeks will be imputed using multiple imputations.

Data collection and management

Data will be collected using case report forms (CRFs) conducted by researchers. The CRFs for recording experimental data in different centres are the same and the standards used for relevant tests and checks are unified. After the trial, the CRFs will be stored in a unified folder; only the researcher team can open the data folder. The data management centre and our project team are jointly responsible for data management. The researchers propose data management and statistical requirements and the data management centre formulates relevant plans according to the requirements proposed by the project.

Data monitoring

A data monitoring committee (DMC) comprising cardiovascular and acupuncture experts and ethics committee members will oversee safety, effectiveness, operation quality and offer recommendations. Especially after the DMC conducts mid-term analysis, it is necessary for the DMC to determine whether the validity results meet the conditions for early termination of clinical trials. The main role of DMC is to provide advice but whether its recommendations are accepted is determined by the sponsor.

Audit of trial conduct

Two audits will be conducted by inspectors, one after 50% of the subjects and the other at the completion of the project. The inspection will be conducted through a simple random sampling method.

Plans for communicating important protocol amendments

Significant changes in the experimental process will be communicated to all relevant personnel including researchers, research assistants, auditors, supervisors, trial subjects and trial registration institutions as these personnel need to participate in the modification of the research protocol together.

Discussion

The 2019 ESC Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Management of Chronic Coronary Syndrome introduced the concept of CCS which refers to the manifestations of all coronary atherosclerotic plaque diseases except acute coronary syndrome. It also includes different stages of CAD including asymptomatic myocardial ischaemia, vasospasm and microcirculatory lesions. While timely coronary interventions and surgical treatments have effectively reduced clinical mortality, the management of post-treatment complications and potential cardiovascular events remains crucial in the comprehensive treatment plans for CCS. Up to now, the treatment pattern of patients with CCS has been consistent with the CAD secondary prevention plan. However, 20% of patients still experience cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events 5 years post-treatment.

Therefore, seeking a practical and effective adjunctive home cardiac rehabilitation therapy for controlling risk factors and reducing the incidence of cardiovascular events in CCS is currently one of the important goals in CCS treatment.

TEAS is a combination of acupuncture and TENS. TEAS has been validated in many randomised controlled trials to have therapeutic effects on some cardiovascular diseases such as hypertension and heart rate abnormalities.19,21 TEAS can effectively regulate heart rate variability, improve the ultrastructure of myocardial tissue and exert cardioprotective effects.9 21 22 Similarly, In a long-term study on 23 patients, the favourable effects of TENS in chronic refractory angina pectoris were confirmed.23 The treatment group reduced ST segment depression, improved the ability to perform cycling tests, reduced the frequency of angina attacks and reduced nitro-glycerine consumption. However, due to the unspecific placebo effect, small sample size and the difference of treatment area, we are unable to know the effect of TEAS in patients with CCS.

As for the mechanism of chronic CAD, it is well-known that lipids play an essential role in the progress of atherosclerosis. Recent studies have identified that oxidised phospholipids can be considered biomarkers and therapeutic targets of CAD,24 25 meanwhile, metabolomics studies have also found that lipid peroxidation may be a significant feature in the pathogenesis of CAD.26 The polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) are esterified into phospholipids and cholesterol within low-density lipoprotein particles making them the primary targets of lipid peroxidation induced by free radicals.27 28 Glycerophospholipid metabolism containing PUFA is also a link between oxidative stress, inflammation, platelet activity, etc, which are the driving factors of coronary disease.29 Therefore, there is a reasonable possibility to consider that oxidative lipid metabolites may be a breakthrough point in revealing the mechanism of CCS and the operating mechanism of TEAS.

Informed consent

This trial adheres to the Declaration of Helsinki (Version Edinburgh 2000). The patient’s informed consent form is shown in online supplemental file 1. All participants volunteer and can drop out of this trial anytime. The researchers have no power to intervene in the participants’ choices. Before the trials, researchers will inform the details, benefits and risks of this trial. Importantly, the allocation to the three groups is equal and neither participants nor researchers will know the allocation in advance. If the participants experience adverse events during the trial period and are determined to be related to treatment, they will receive a certain amount of economic compensation.

Additional consent provisions for the collection and use of participant data and biological specimens

In this study, biological indicators will be presented in the form of additional clauses and we will inform them of the use and risks of biological specimens to obtain informed consent from the participants. Additional clauses are shown in online supplemental file 2.

Confidentiality

Participants’ data will be stored using the research team’s identifier during recording and the detailed information of the subjects will not be disclosed when the research data is used for publication.

Trial status

The trial is currently recruiting patients. The recruitment of this trial began on 31 January 2024 and will be ongoing until 31 January 2025. Data analysis has not yet started.

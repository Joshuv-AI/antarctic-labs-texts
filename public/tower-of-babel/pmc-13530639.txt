Poster 170. Achilles Tendinitis and Subsequent Lower Extremity Injuries in Young Athletes
Authors: Yazin Hindosh, Tawanda Zvavamwe, Jonathan Sayegh, Diego Garcia, Bryce Michael, Audrey Yang, Lincoln Koyejo, Jacob Bershatski, Seth Catchpole, Nicholas Bonazza
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:The objective of our study is to assess if ankle tendonitis puts athletes at an increased risk for more severe lower extremity injuries.Methods:We conducted a retrospective cohort study using the TriNetX Research Network, a global database of de-identified electronic health records. Two independent analyses were performed to assess laterality-specific outcomes. For the left-leg cohort, patients aged 13-25 years with a documented sports physical were identified. Individuals who subsequently developed left Achilles tendinitis (n=724) were compared with those without tendinitis (n=329,314). For the right-leg cohort, the same criteria were applied, yielding 693 patients with right Achilles tendinitis and 329,366 without. Each exposure group was matched 1:1 with controls using propensity scores for age, sex, race, ethnicity, obesity, diabetes, nicotine dependence, chronic kidney disease, hyperlipidemia, hypertension, and rheumatoid arthritis. Post-matching, the left-leg cohorts had 722 patients per group and the right-leg cohorts had 690 patients per group. Primary outcomes were ipsilateral and contralateral Achilles rupture, anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) tear, and ankle sprain at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months. Risk ratios (RR) with p<0.05 were considered statistically significant. TriNetX does not generate RRs when outcomes are absent in one cohort.Results:Patients with Achilles tendonitis in their left leg were at a significantly increased 1-month risk of ipsilateral Achilles rupture (p=0.002), ipsilateral ACL tear (p<0.001), contralateral ACL tear (p=0.002), and ipsilateral ankle sprain (p<0.001). At 3 months, they continued to be at an increased risk of ipsilateral achilles rupture (p=0.002), ipsilateral ACL tear (RR=3.500; p<0.001), and contralateral ACL tear (p=0.002). Additionally, they were at a significantly increased 6-month risk of ipsilateral achilles rupture (p=0.002), contralateral achilles rupture (p=0.002), contralateral ACL tear (p=0.002), ipsilateral ACL tear (RR=3.500; p<0.001), and ipsilateral ankle sprain (RR:2.100; p=0.046). Finally, these patients were at an increased 1-year risk of ipsilateral achilles rupture (p=0.002), contralateral achilles rupture (p=0.002), ipsilateral ACL tear (RR=3.600; p<0.001), and ipsilateral ankle sprain (RR=1.900; p=0.015).Patients with Achilles tendonitis in their right leg were at a significantly increased 1-month risk of ipsilateral Achilles rupture (p=0.002), ipsilateral ACL tear (p<0.001), contralateral ACL tear (p=0.002), ipsilateral ankle sprain (p=0.002), and contralateral ankle sprain (p=0.002). These patients were also at an increased 3-month risk of ipsilateral Achilles rupture (p=0.002), contralateral achilles rupture (p=0.002), ipsilateral ACL tear (p<0.001), and ipsilateral ankle sprain (p<0.001). At 6 months, these patients were at an increased risk of ipsilateral Achilles rupture (p=0.002), contralateral achilles rupture (p=0.002), and ipsilateral ACL tear (p<0.001). Finally, at 1 year, they were at increased risk for ipsilateral Achilles rupture (p=0.002), contralateral achilles rupture (p=0.002), ipsilateral ACL tear (p<0.001), and ipsilateral ankle sprain (RR=2.200; p=0.032).Conclusions:Ankle tendonitis may be associated with an increased risk of contralateral and ipsilateral achilles rupture, ACL tear, and ankle sprain. Though several factors could contribute to this increased risk, surgeons may use this information to better advise patients regarding their return to sport.

Optimal L 2 {L^{2}} error estimates of fully discrete finite element methods for the 2D/3D diffuse interface two-phase MHD flows Note: This work is partly supported by the NSF of China (No. 12471392) support.
Ke Zhang, Haiyan Su

Abstract
In this paper, we perform an optimal 𝑳 2 \bm{L}^{2} -norm error analysis of a fully discrete convex-splitting finite element method (FEM) for the two-phase diffuse interface magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) system. The method use the semi-implicit backward Euler scheme in time and use the standard inf–sup stable Taylor–Hood/Mini elements to discretize the velocity and pressure. The previous works provided the optimal 𝑯 1 \bm{H}^{1} -norm error estimates for all components, but not the optimal 𝑳 2 \bm{L}^{2} -norm estimates, which are caused by the nonlinear coupled terms. The optimal 𝑳 2 \bm{L}^{2} -norm error analysis is achieved through the novel Ritz and Stokes quasi-projections. In addition, the mass conservation and unconditional energy stability of the finite element convex-splitting scheme are ensured. Numerical examples are presented to validate the theoretical analysis.

Introduction

The two-phase MHD focuses on the dynamic behavior of two incompressible and immiscible conducting fluids under an external electromagnetic field. The governing model consists of the Cahn-Hilliard equations (describing the free interface), the Navier-Stokes equations (describing the hydrodynamics), and the Maxwell equations (describing the magnetic field), which are coupled through convection, stresses, and Lorentz forces. It has extensive application prospects in the fields of nuclear fusion, metallurgy, liquid metal magnetic pumps, aluminum electrolysis, as well as in addressing problems encountered in other fields
[
7
,
11
,
20
]
.

In this paper, we mainly consider the following two-phase diffuse interface MHD model
[
31
,
21
]
:

\displaystyle\phi_{t}+\nabla\phi\cdot\bm{u}=\gamma\nabla\cdot(M\nabla\omega),

(1.1)

\displaystyle\omega=-\gamma\Delta\phi+\gamma^{-1}f(\phi),

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{t}-\nabla\cdot(\nu\nabla\bm{u})+(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla)\bm{u}+\frac{1}{\mu}\bm{B}\times\nabla\times\bm{B}+\nabla p=\lambda\omega\nabla\phi+\bm{f},

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{u}=0,

\displaystyle\bm{B}_{t}+\frac{1}{\mu}\nabla\times(\frac{1}{\sigma}\nabla\times\bm{B})-\nabla\times(\bm{u}\times\bm{B})=\bm{0},

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{B}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\bm{n}}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\,\frac{\partial w}{\partial\bm{n}}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\,\bm{u}|_{\partial\Omega}=\bm{0},\,\bm{B}\times\bm{n}|_{\partial\Omega}=\bm{0},

\displaystyle\phi|_{t=0}=\phi_{0},\,\bm{u}|_{t=0}=\bm{u}_{0},\,\bm{B}|_{t=0}=\bm{B}_{0},

for
(\bm{x},t)\in\Omega\times(0,T]
,
\Omega
is a bounded smooth polyhedral domain in

\phi(x,t)=\left\{\begin{aligned} -1,\qquad\mathrm{fluid\ 1},\\
1,\qquad\mathrm{fluid\ 2}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

The function

is the derivative of the Ginzburg-Landau double-well potential function

with respect to
\phi

[
4
]

\omega

[

Moreover, the second-order schemes

Additionally, theoretical investigations into two-phase MHD system remain an active area. Recently, the first-order semi-implicit stabilization scheme was developed in
[
6
]

\|\phi^{k+1}-\phi_{h}^{k+1}\|+\|\nabla(\phi^{k+1}-\phi_{h}^{k+1})\|+\|\nabla(\bm{u}^{k+1}-\bm{u}_{h}^{k+1})\|+\|\nabla(\bm{B}^{k+1}-\bm{B}_{h}^{k+1})\|\leq C_{0}(\Delta t+h).

(1.3)

The first-order Euler semi-implicit discretization based on a convex-splitting scheme was given in
[
21
]
. This discretization employs the standard inf-sup stable Taylor-Hood finite elements pair
(\bm{u}_{h}^{k+1},p_{h}^{k+1})\in\bm{X}_{h}^{r+1}\times\mathring{S}_{h}^{r}
, with
\bm{B}_{h}^{k+1}\in\bm{Y}_{h}^{r+1}

\phi_{h}^{k+1},\omega_{h}^{k+1}\in S_{h}^{r+1}

[
21
]

\|\nabla(\phi^{k+1}-\phi_{h}^{k+1})\|+\|\bm{u}^{k+1}-\bm{u}_{h}^{k+1}\|+\|\bm{B}^{k+1}-\bm{B}_{h}^{k+1}\|\leq C_{0}(\Delta t+h^{r+1}).

(1.4)

The second-order modified Crank-Nicolson-type fully discrete scheme was designed for solving the two-phase MHD model
[
27
]
. Specifically, it achieved the same spatial convergence order as that presented in (
1.4
). The

-norm error estimate of the phase field

-norm is not. The primary reason for this is the artificial pollution resulting from the approximation of the phase field variable

5

-norm error estimates for the velocity field and magnetic field (

In this paper, our goal is to derive the optimal
L^{2}
-norm error estimates for the phase field, velocity field and magnetic field by employing the elliptic Ritz, Stokes quasi-projections and Maxwell projection. For the uniform MINI type finite elements
(\phi_{h}^{k+1}
,
\omega_{h}^{k+1}
,
\bm{u}_{h}^{k+1}
,
p_{h}^{k+1},\bm{B}_{h}^{k+1})\in S_{h}^{1}\times S_{h}^{1}\times\bm{X}_{h}^{1b}\times\mathring{S}_{h}^{1}\times\bm{Y}_{h}^{1}

\|\phi^{k+1}-\phi_{h}^{k+1}\|+\|\bm{u}^{k+1}-\bm{u}_{h}^{k+1}\|+\|\bm{B}^{k+1}-\bm{B}_{h}^{k+1}\|\leq C_{0}(\Delta t+h^{2}).

(1.5)

For the inf-sup stable Taylor-Hood type finite elements pair
(\phi_{h}^{k+1},\omega_{h}^{k+1},\bm{u}_{h}^{k+1},p_{h}^{k+1},\bm{B}_{h}^{k+1})\in S_{h}^{r}\times S_{h}^{r}\times\bm{X}_{h}^{r+1}\times\mathring{S}_{h}^{r}\times\bm{Y}_{h}^{r+1}

\left\{\begin{aligned} \|\phi^{k+1}-\phi_{h}^{k+1}\|&\leq C_{0}(\Delta t+h^{r+1}),\\
\|\bm{u}^{k+1}-\bm{u}_{h}^{k+1}\|&\leq C_{0}(\Delta t+\beta_{h}),\\
\|\bm{B}^{k+1}-\bm{B}_{h}^{k+1}\|&\leq C_{0}(\Delta t+\beta_{h}),\end{aligned}\right.\quad\text{where}\quad\beta_{h}=\left\{\begin{aligned} h^{r+2},\quad&r\geq 2,\\
h^{r+1},\quad&r=1.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.6)

Obviously, the error estimate for the phase field is optimal. And the error estimates for the velocity field and magnetic field are one order lower than the interpolation error when
r
=1, while our numerical results confirm that the estimates are also optimal. For
r\geq
2, the results in conclusion (
1.6
) indicate that the error estimates for the phase field, velocity field, and magnetic field are optimal. The more details can be seen in Theorem
2.1

\displaystyle C_{\varepsilon}\left(\|e_{\bm{u}}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\|e_{\bm{u}}^{k}\|^{2}+\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{k}\|^{2}+\beta_{h}^{2}+\Delta t^{2}+\|R_{3}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\|e_{\bm{B}}^{k}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\|e_{\omega}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\left(\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\times\bm{B}_{h}^{k},e_{\bm{u}}^{k+1}\right).

By summing up the above estimate from time step
t_{0}
to
t_{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|e_{\bm{u}}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{k}\|\nabla e_{\bm{u}}^{m+1}\|^{2}\leq&C_{\varepsilon}\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{k}\left(\|e_{\bm{u}}^{m+1}\|^{2}+\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{m+1}\|^{2}+\|e_{\bm{B}}^{m+1}\|^{2}\right)+C_{\varepsilon}\left(\beta_{h}^{2}+\Delta t^{2}\right)\\
&+\varepsilon\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{k}\|e_{\omega}^{m+1}\|^{2}+\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{k}\left(\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{m+1}\times\bm{B}_{h}^{m},e_{\bm{u}}^{m+1}\right),\end{aligned}

(4.11)

noting that
\|e_{\bm{u}}^{0}\|^{2}\leq C_{0}\beta_{h}
and the estimate of the truncation error
R_{3}^{k+1}
is given in (
4.2
).

4.2
Estimates for
e_{\textbf{B}}^{k+1}
.

Taking
\bm{\zeta}_{h}=e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}
in equation (
4.1e
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2}d_{t}\|e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\Delta t}\|e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}-e_{\bm{B}}^{k}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\|^{2}&+\|\nabla\cdot e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\|^{2}=\left(d_{t}(\bm{\Pi}_{h}\bm{B}^{k+1}-\bm{B}^{k+1}),e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\right)+\left(R_{4}^{k+1},e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\right)\\
&-\left((\bm{u}_{h}^{k+1}\times\bm{B}_{h}^{k},\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1})-(\bm{u}^{k+1}\times\bm{B}^{k},\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1})\right):=\sum_{i=1}^{3}Q_{i}.\end{aligned}

(4.12)

According to the regularity assumption (
2.1
), the Taylor expansion, and the estimates of the Maxwell projection, we obtain

\displaystyle Q_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(d_{t}(\bm{\Pi}_{h}\bm{B}^{k+1}-\bm{B}^{k+1}),e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\right)

(4.13)

\displaystyle\leq\|d_{t}(\bm{\Pi}_{h}\bm{B}^{k+1}-\bm{B}^{k+1})\|\|e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\left((h^{r+2})^{2}+\|e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\|^{2}\right),

\displaystyle Q_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(R_{4}^{k+1},e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\right)\leq C_{0}\left(\Delta t^{2}+\|e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\|^{2}\right).

Thus, combining estimates in (
4.13
), we derive

\displaystyle Q_{1}+Q_{2}\leq C_{0}\left((h^{r+2})^{2}+\Delta t^{2}+\|e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\|^{2}\right).

(4.14)

By further expanding
Q_{3}
, we have

\displaystyle Q_{3}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\bm{u}^{k+1}\times(\bm{B}^{k}-\bm{\Pi}_{h}\bm{B}^{k}),\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\right)+\left(\bm{u}^{k+1}\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k},\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle(\text{term}\quad Q_{3,1})

\displaystyle+\left((\bm{u}^{k+1}-\bm{P}_{h}\bm{u}^{k+1})\times\bm{B}_{h}^{k},\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle(\text{term}\quad Q_{3,2})

\displaystyle+\left(e_{\bm{u}}^{k+1}\times\bm{B}_{h}^{k},\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\right).

With the help of the regularity assumption (
2.1
) and Remark
3.1
, we consider the following estimate

\displaystyle Q_{3,1}=

\displaystyle\left(\bm{u}^{k+1}\times(\bm{B}^{k}-\bm{\Pi}_{h}\bm{B}^{k}),\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\right)+\left(\bm{u}^{k+1}\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k},\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{u}^{k+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\bm{B}^{k}-\bm{\Pi}_{h}\bm{B}^{k}\|\|\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\|+\|\bm{u}^{k+1}|\|_{L^{\infty}}\|e_{\bm{B}}^{k}\|\|\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}\left(\|e_{\bm{B}}^{k}\|^{2}+\beta_{h}^{2}\right)+\varepsilon\|\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\|^{2},

\displaystyle Q_{3,2}=

\displaystyle-\left((\bm{u}^{k+1}-\bm{P}_{h}\bm{u}^{k+1})\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k},\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\right)+\left((\bm{u}^{k+1}-\bm{P}_{h}\bm{u}^{k+1})\times(\bm{\Pi}_{h}\bm{B}^{k}-\bm{B}^{k}),\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle+\left((\bm{u}^{k+1}-\bm{P}_{h}\bm{u}^{k+1})\times\bm{B}^{k},\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}^{k+1}-\bm{P}_{h}\bm{u}^{k+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\|e_{\bm{B}}^{k}\|+\|\bm{\Pi}_{h}\bm{B}^{k}-\bm{B}^{k}\|\right)\|\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\|+\|\bm{u}^{k+1}-\bm{P}_{h}\bm{u}^{k+1}\|\,\|\bm{B}^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\varepsilon}\left(\|e_{\bm{B}}^{k}\|^{2}+\beta_{h}^{2}\right)+\varepsilon\|\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\|^{2}.

And obviously, we estimate
Q_{3}
as

\displaystyle Q_{3}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\left(\|e_{\bm{B}}^{k}\|^{2}+\beta_{h}^{2}\right)+\varepsilon\|\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\|^{2}-\left(\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\times\bm{B}_{h}^{k},e_{\bm{u}}^{k+1}\right).

(4.15)

For a sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, by combining the inequalities (
4.14
) and (
4.15
), the equation (
4.12
) reduces to

\displaystyle d_{t}\|e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\|^{2}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\left(\|e_{\bm{B}}^{k}\|^{2}+\|e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\beta_{h}^{2}+\Delta t^{2}\right)-\left(\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\times\bm{B}_{h}^{k},e_{\bm{u}}^{k+1}\right).

Then, by summing the result from time step
t_{0}
to
t_{k}
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{k}\left(\|\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{m+1}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot e_{\bm{B}}^{m+1}\|^{2}\right)\\
\leq&C_{\varepsilon}\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{k}\|e_{\bm{B}}^{m+1}\|^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\left(\beta_{h}^{2}+\Delta t^{2}\right)-\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{k}\left(\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{m+1}\times\bm{B}_{h}^{m},e_{\bm{u}}^{m+1}\right).\end{aligned}

(4.16)

By virtue of (ii)-(iii) in Lemma
4.2
, by adding inequalities (
4.2
), (
4.11
) and the above inequality, we have the following estimate

\displaystyle\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\|e_{\bm{u}}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\|e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{k}\left(\|\nabla e_{\omega}^{m+1}\|^{2}+\|\nabla e_{\bm{u}}^{m+1}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{m+1}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot e_{\bm{B}}^{m+1}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\varepsilon}\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{k}\left(\|e_{\bm{u}}^{m+1}\|^{2}+\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{m+1}\|^{2}+\|e_{\bm{B}}^{m+1}\|^{2}\right)+C_{\varepsilon}\left(\beta_{h}^{2}+\Delta t^{2}\right)+C_{0}\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{k}\|d_{t}Z^{m+1}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|e_{\phi}^{m}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}.

By using the discrete Gronwall’s inequality Lemma
4.1
, there exists a positive constant
\Delta t_{2}
such that, if
\Delta t\leq\Delta t_{1}
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\|e_{\bm{u}}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\|e_{\bm{B}}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{k}\left(\|\nabla e_{\omega}^{m+1}\|^{2}+\|\nabla e_{\bm{u}}^{m+1}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\times e_{\bm{B}}^{m+1}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot e_{\bm{B}}^{m+1}\|^{2}\right)\leq C_{\varepsilon}\left(\beta_{h}^{2}+\Delta t^{2}\right).

(4.17)

By employing the above inequality, (ii)-(iii) in Lemma
4.2
and the estimates of projection operators in Section
3
, we give the error estimates in Theorem
2.1

\bm{L}^{2}
-norm error estimates can be obtained:

\displaystyle\max\limits_{0\leq k\leq K-1}\|\phi^{k+1}-\phi_{h}^{k+1}\|+\left(\Delta t\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\|\omega^{k+1}-\omega_{h}^{k+1}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{0}(\Delta t+h^{r+1}),

\displaystyle\max\limits_{0\leq k\leq K-1}\|\nabla(\phi^{k+1}-\phi_{h}^{k+1})\|\leq C_{0}(\Delta t+h^{r}),

\displaystyle\max\limits_{0\leq k\leq K-1}\left(\|\bm{u}^{k+1}-\bm{u}_{h}^{k+1}\|+\|\bm{B}^{k+1}-\bm{B}_{h}^{k+1}\|\right)\leq C_{0}(\Delta t+\beta_{h}),

\displaystyle\left(\Delta t\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\left(\|\nabla(\bm{u}^{k+1}-\bm{u}_{h}^{k+1})\|^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot(\bm{B}^{k+1}-\bm{B}_{h}^{k+1})\|^{2}+\|\nabla\times(\bm{B}^{k+1}-\bm{B}_{h}^{k+1})\|^{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{0}(\Delta t+\beta_{h}^{\star}).

However, the current work focuses only on the matched elements in velocity field and magnetic field. The development of lower-order approximations for the magnetic field will be addressed in our future research.

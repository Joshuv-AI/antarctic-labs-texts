High Signal-to-Noise Ratio Asymptotics of Entropy-Constrained Gaussian Channel Capacity
Adway Girish, Shlomo Shamai (Shitz), Emre Telatar

Abstract
We study the input-entropy-constrained Gaussian channel capacity problem in the asymptotic high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) regime. We show that the capacity-achieving distribution as SNR goes to infinity is given by a discrete Gaussian distribution supported on a scaled integer lattice. Further, we show that the gap between the input entropy and the capacity decreases to zero exponentially in SNR, and characterize this exponent.



1
Introduction

Consider an additive Gaussian noise channel with an input-output relationship given by
Y=X+Z
, where
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1/\mathsf{snr})
is a Gaussian random variable, independent of
X
, and
\mathsf{snr}>0
is the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of the channel. The capacity of this channel with an average-power constraint on the input is given by

C(\mathsf{snr})=\sup_{\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]\leq 1}I(X;Y)=\tfrac{1}{2}\log(1+\mathsf{snr}),

with the supremum taken over all distributions of
X
over
\mathbb{R}
, achieved uniquely by taking
X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
.

In modern cloud-based communication systems, the channel input is not generated directly at the transmitter but is instead produced or selected by a remote agent and conveyed to the transmitter over a rate-limited fronthaul link
[
17
]
. Motivated by this, in earlier work
[
10
]
, we introduced an entropy constraint in addition to the power constraint, to model the rate-limited link.
In such a setting, the quantity of interest is the entropy-constrained capacity, defined as

C_{H}(h,\mathsf{snr})=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]\leq 1,\\
H(X)\leq h\end{subarray}}I(X;Y)=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]=1,\,\mathbb{E}[X]=0,\\
H(X)=h\end{subarray}}I(X;Y),

(1)

for
h>0
. The second equality, i.e., existence of a capacity-achieving distribution satisfying the constraints on entropy, mean and variance with equality, can be justified easily and was shown earlier
[
10
, Proposition 1]
.

For any finite
h>0
, we necessarily have that
X
is discrete, and hence,
C_{H}(h,\mathsf{snr})<C(\mathsf{snr})
. As the mutual information is upper bounded by the entropy, we also trivially have
C_{H}(h,\mathsf{snr})<h
, with the inequality strict for all finite
\mathsf{snr}
. Though these upper bounds are strict, the gaps
C(\mathsf{snr})-C_{H}(h,\mathsf{snr})
and
h-C_{H}(h,\mathsf{snr})
decay to 0 as
\mathsf{snr}
goes to 0 and
\infty
respectively. We showed earlier
[
10
]
that
C(\mathsf{snr})-C_{H}(h,\mathsf{snr})=\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{snr}^{4})
as
\mathsf{snr}\to 0
for any
h>0
, implying that the entropy constraint imposes a negligible penalty on the capacity at low SNR, as
C(\mathsf{snr})\approx\frac{\mathsf{snr}}{2}\gg\mathsf{snr}^{4}
. Equivalently, at low SNR, it is possible to closely approximate the Gaussian capacity by using an arbitrarily low entropy. The optimal distribution turns out to be the one that matches as many initial
moments as possible with
W\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
while satisfying the entropy constraint.

In this paper, we consider the high SNR asymptotics of
C_{H}
. Note that
\lim_{\mathsf{snr}\to\infty}C_{H}(h,\mathsf{snr})=h
, and this limit of
h
is attained by any distribution with entropy equal to
h
.
We are interested in finding the distribution which “achieves capacity as
\mathsf{snr}\to\infty
”, defined to be the one for which the gap
h-I(X;Y)=H(X\mid Y)
decays the fastest in
\mathsf{snr}
. It is a priori not obvious that any distribution achieves the fastest decay or if it is unique. We show that this is indeed the case, and that this asymptotic optimal distribution is the one that has the largest minimum distance between its discrete atoms, which is in turn given by a discrete Gaussian distribution
[
5
,
19
]
supported on a scaled integer lattice.
We also show that the gap
h-C_{H}(h,\mathsf{snr})
goes to zero at least exponentially fast in
\mathsf{snr}
, with an exponent that is decreasing in
h
. We observe that the exponent behaves differently at “small” and “large” entropies, and give an explicit approximation for each case.

1.1
Prior work

Several variations of Shannon’s classical Gaussian channel capacity
[
15
]
have been proposed to study the effect of practical limitations on the capacity
[
1
,
12
,
9
,
13
]
. A common theme is the appearance of discrete distributions as optimal distributions, even when there is no explicit requirement of discreteness, such as with a peak-amplitude constraint
[

,
16
,
8
,

.
Discreteness may also be imposed explicitly, such as the cardinality constraint considered by

[

]
.

The high-SNR regime that we consider was previously studied by Alvarado et al.
[
2

, who showed that the conditional entropy decays exponentially in SNR and characterized the exponent for discrete, finite constellations in terms of the minimum distance between consecutive atoms. We extend this to arbitrary discrete distributions, and use it to conclude that the optimal distribution as
\mathsf{snr}\to\infty
is the discrete Gaussian distribution
[
5
,
19
]
.
Being the discrete analogue of the Gaussian distribution, it arises naturally in computer science applications
[
11
]
, lattice-based cryptography
[
7
,
14
]
, probabilistic shaping of codes
[
4
]
, differential privacy
[
5
]
, to name a few.

Uppercase letters (e.g.
X
,
Y
, …) denote random variables. We use
\mathbb{E}[X]
to denote the expectation of
X
.
We refer to a random variable and its probability distribution interchangeably.

\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^{2})
denotes the Gaussian distribution with mean
\mu
and variance
\sigma^{2}
, and
\mathcal{N}_{\beta\mathbb{Z}}(\lambda)
denotes the discrete Gaussian distribution (see Section
2
for its definition).
The mutual information between
X
and
Y
is denoted by
I(X;Y)
. The entropy of
X
is denoted by
H(X)
, and the conditional entropy is given by
H(X\mid Y)=H(X)-I(X;Y)
.

\log
and
\exp
are inverses of each other and are taken to base
e
, but we write all numerical values in bits, e.g.,
h=\log e=1
as
\log_{2}e\approx 1.44
bits, simply to make them more interpretable.
We write
f(x)=\mathsf{O}(g(x))
if
\lim_{x}f(x)/g(x)<\infty
(the limiting value of
x
will be clear from context) and
f(x)=\mathsf{o}(g(x))
if the limit is
0
.

2
Main results

We say that a distribution
X^{*}
is “
capacity achieving
or
optimal as
\mathsf{snr}\to\infty
” if for any distribution
X
, there exists some
\mathsf{snr}_{0}>0
such that
I(X^{*};X^{*}+Z)\geq I(X;X+Z)
for all
\mathsf{snr}>\mathsf{snr}_{0}
, where
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1/\mathsf{snr})
is independent of
X,X^{*}
. This
X^{*}
can be used to obtain a lower bound on
C_{H}(h,\mathsf{snr})
that is asymptotically tight as
\mathsf{snr}\to\infty
.
For any discrete random variable
X
with atoms
x_{i}
, let
\mathsf{d}_{\min}(X)\coloneqq\inf_{i\neq j}|x_{i}-x_{j}|
, where
x_{i}
’s are the atoms of
X
, be the
minimum distance
of
X
. It is worth noting that this infimum is necessarily attained if
X
has a finite support, but may not be attained if
X
has an infinite support, in which case we may also have
\mathsf{d}_{\min}(X)=0
.

We define the
discrete Gaussian distribution
with parameters
\beta,\lambda
as follows: we write
X\sim\mathcal{N}_{\beta\mathbb{Z}}(\lambda)
to mean the discrete distribution supported on the lattice
\beta\mathbb{Z}=\{\beta n:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
with probability mass

\mathsf{P}_{X}(x_{i})=\exp(-\lambda i^{2})/\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp(-\lambda j^{2})=\exp(-L(\lambda)-\lambda i^{2})

at
x_{i}=\beta i
, where
L(\lambda)=\log\big(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp(-\lambda i^{2})\big)
. Note that the atoms of
X\sim\mathcal{N}_{\beta\mathbb{Z}}(\lambda)
are equally spaced, with a distance
\beta
between any consecutive atoms. In the following theorem, we show that the capacity-achieving distribution as
\mathsf{snr}\to\infty
is given by the discrete Gaussian with
\beta,\lambda
chosen to satisfy the power and entropy constraints with equality.

Theorem 1

(High-SNR asymptotics of
C_{H}
)
.

For finite
h>0
, the distribution achieving capacity as
\mathsf{snr}\to\infty
is
\mathcal{N}_{\mathsf{d}_{h}\mathbb{Z}}(\lambda_{h})
, where
\lambda_{h}
is the
value of
\lambda
that solves
h=L(\lambda)-\lambda L^{\prime}(\lambda)
and
\mathsf{d}_{h}=1/\sqrt{-L^{\prime}(\lambda_{h})}
.
Further, the capacity is given by the asymptotic expression

C_{H}(h,\mathsf{snr})=h-\mathsf{O}\left(\exp\left(-\mathsf{snr}\tfrac{\mathsf{d}_{h}^{2}}{8}\right)\right).

(2)

Proof.

See Section
3
.
∎

The quantity
\mathsf{d}_{h}
in the above theorem is the largest minimum distance among all distributions satisfying the power and entropy constraints with equality, i.e.,
\mathsf{d}_{h}=\sup\{\mathsf{d}_{\min}(X):\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]=1,H(X)=h\}
, which (as we show in Section
3.2
), is achieved by the discrete Gaussian.
We conjecture that this “capacity-achieving as
\mathsf{snr}\to\infty
” property can be strengthened to also say that the sequence of capacity-achieving distributions at finite
\mathsf{snr}
’s converges weakly (i.e., in distribution) to
\mathcal{N}_{\mathsf{d}_{h}\mathbb{Z}}(\lambda_{h})
, as Wu and Verdú
[
21
]
show for a cardinality constraint: the optimal distribution as
\mathsf{snr}\to\infty
is shown to be an equally spaced uniform distribution and this is the weak limit of the sequence of true capacity-achieving distributions at finite SNRs.

The quantity
L(\lambda)
admits tight approximate characterizations in two regimes:
\lambda\gg\pi
and
\lambda\ll\pi

[
5
]
.
In Section
3.3
, we use these approximate expressions for
\mathsf{d}_{h}
at
h\ll L(\pi)-\pi L^{\prime}(\pi)
and
h\gg L(\pi)-\pi L^{\prime}(\pi)
respectively, together with Theorem
2
, to obtain the following explicit approximation for
C_{H}(h,\mathsf{snr})
for small and large entropy (still in the high SNR asymptotic regime). The threshold
L(\pi)-\pi L^{\prime}(\pi)
is approximately 0.48 bits.

Corollary 1

(Low- and high-entropy approximation of
C_{H}
at asymptotic high SNR)
.

As
\mathsf{snr}\to\infty
, we approximate the gap
h-C_{H}(h,\mathsf{snr})
as

\begin{cases}\mathsf{O}\left(\exp\big(-\mathsf{snr}\frac{1}{8h}\log\frac{2}{h}\big)\right)&h\ll 0.48\text{ bits},\\
\mathsf{O}\left(\exp\big(-\mathsf{snr}\frac{\pi e}{4}e^{-2h}\big)\right)&h\gg 0.48\text{ bits}.\end{cases}

(3)

Further, for
h\ll 0.48\text{ bits}
, as
\mathsf{snr}\to\infty
, the capacity-achieving distribution is approximately the symmetric three-point distribution with a large mass at the origin. Meanwhile, as
h\to\infty
, we have
\mathcal{N}_{\mathsf{d}_{h}\mathbb{Z}}(\lambda_{h})
converges in distribution to
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
.

Proof.

See Section
3.3
.
∎

3
Technical details

To prove Theorem
2
, we first show that as
\mathsf{snr}\to\infty
, the conditional entropy
H(X\mid Y)=H(X)-I(X;Y)
is maximized by the distribution with the largest minimum distance between atoms. (Section
3.1
, Lemma
1
). Hence, for a given
h
, the distribution that achieves
C_{H}(h,\mathsf{snr})
as
\mathsf{snr}\to\infty
is the one with the largest minimum distance among all distributions with variance 1 and entropy
h
. We show that the distribution that achieves this largest minimum distance is the discrete Gaussian (Section
3.2
). Section
3.3
then considers the low- and high-entropy regimes and obtains an explicit approximation to
\mathsf{d}_{h}
in each case, which gives us Corollary
1
.

3.1
Reduction to minimum distance between atoms

Let
X
be an arbitrary discrete random variable having atoms
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}

with probability
p_{i}>0
, where
i
goes over some contiguous subset of
\mathbb{Z}
.
As
\mathsf{snr}
goes to
\infty
, we have that
H(X\mid Y)
goes to zero for any such fixed
X
. The following lemma shows that this decay is exponential in
\mathsf{snr}
and quantifies the rate as
H(X\mid Y)=\exp\left(-\mathsf{snr}\frac{\mathsf{d}_{\min}(X)^{2}}{8}+\mathsf{o}(\mathsf{snr})\right)
. This was shown earlier
[
2
]
for finite support distributions that have
\mathsf{d}_{\min}(X)>0
, we extend it to arbitrary support discrete distributions with
\mathsf{d}_{\min}(X)\geq 0
.

Lemma 1

(Exponent of conditional entropy)
.

For any discrete
X
with a finite entropy, and
Y=X+Z
with
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1/\mathsf{snr})
independent of
X
, we have

\lim_{\mathsf{snr}\to\infty}-\frac{1}{\mathsf{snr}}\log H(X\mid Y)=\frac{\mathsf{d}_{\min}(X)^{2}}{8}.

Proof.

Suppose
X
is discrete and has entropy
H(X)<\infty
. We will show the above result by deriving upper and lower bounds to
H(X\mid Y)
that have the same exponential rate in
\mathsf{snr}\eqqcolon\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}
.
We also use the following notation: let
\varphi_{\sigma}(x)
denote the density of a Gaussian random variable with mean zero and variance
\sigma^{2}
, i.e.,
\varphi_{\sigma}(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}\exp(-\frac{x^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}})
, and let
\varphi\coloneqq\varphi_{1}
denote the standard Gaussian density.

(a) Lower bound to
H(X\mid Y)
.

Fix
\epsilon>0
.
Let
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
be such that
x_{0}<x_{1}
and
\Delta=x_{1}-x_{0}<\mathsf{d}_{\min}+\epsilon
. Note that
x_{0},x_{1},\Delta
depend on
\epsilon
but we do not write this dependence explicitly. Since
H(X\mid Y)
is the average of
\log\frac{1}{p_{X|Y}}(x|y)
(which is always non-negative) over the joint distribution
(X,Y)
, we can lower bound
H(X\mid Y)
as

\displaystyle H(X\mid Y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\log\frac{\sum_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}}p_{x^{\prime}}\varphi_{\sigma}(y-x^{\prime})}{p_{x}\varphi_{\sigma}(y-x)}\ p_{x}\varphi_{\sigma}(y-x)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\log\left(1+\frac{p_{x_{1}}\varphi_{\sigma}(y-x_{1})}{p_{x_{0}}\varphi_{\sigma}(y-x_{0})}\right)p_{x_{0}}\varphi_{\sigma}(y-x_{0})\,\mathrm{d}y,

by considering only the
x_{0}
term in the outer sum and only the
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
terms in the sum inside
\log
.
We now further lower bound this integral by picking an arbitrary
\delta>0
and restricting the domain of integration to the interval
(\frac{x_{0}+x_{1}}{2},\frac{x_{0}+x_{1}}{2}+\delta)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle H(X\mid Y)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\frac{x_{0}+x_{1}}{2}}^{\frac{x_{0}+x_{1}}{2}+\delta}\log\left(1+\frac{p_{x_{1}}\varphi_{\sigma}(y-x_{1})}{p_{x_{0}}\varphi_{\sigma}(y-x_{0})}\right)p_{x_{0}}\varphi_{\sigma}(y-x_{0})\,\mathrm{d}y.

Consider the expression

\frac{p_{x_{1}}\varphi_{\sigma}(y-x_{1})}{p_{x_{0}}\varphi_{\sigma}(y-x_{0})}=\frac{p_{x_{1}}\exp(-\frac{(y-x_{1})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}})}{p_{x_{0}}\exp(-\frac{(y-x_{0})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}})}=\frac{p_{x_{1}}}{p_{x_{0}}}\exp\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}(x_{1}-x_{0})(y-\tfrac{x_{0}+x_{1}}{2})\right).

This is increasing in
y
as
x_{1}>x_{0}
, so we can obtain a lower bound to the above expression by substituting
y=\frac{x_{0}+x_{1}}{2}
, which makes the argument of the exponent vanish, giving

\displaystyle H(X\mid Y)

\displaystyle\geq p_{x_{0}}\log\left(1+\tfrac{p_{x_{1}}}{p_{x_{0}}}\right)\int_{\frac{x_{0}+x_{1}}{2}}^{\frac{x_{0}+x_{1}}{2}+\delta}\varphi_{\sigma}(y-x_{0})\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=p_{x_{0}}\log\left(1+\tfrac{p_{x_{1}}}{p_{x_{0}}}\right)\int_{\frac{\Delta}{2\sigma}}^{\frac{\Delta}{2\sigma}+\frac{\delta}{\sigma}}\varphi(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,

with the second line obtained by making the substitution
\frac{y-x_{0}}{\sigma}\mapsto y
.
The integrand
\varphi(y)
is decreasing for
y
in the domain of integration (positive
y
), so we can lower bound the integrand by substituting
y=\frac{\Delta}{2\sigma}+\frac{\delta}{\sigma}
, i.e.

\displaystyle H(X\mid Y)

\displaystyle\geq p_{x_{0}}\log\left(1+\tfrac{p_{x_{1}}}{p_{x_{0}}}\right)\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}\exp\left(-\tfrac{(\Delta+2\delta)^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}\right)\cdot\tfrac{\delta}{\sigma},

for every
\delta,\epsilon>0
. Hence, the logarithm of the conditional entropy is lower bounded as

\displaystyle\log H(X\mid Y)\geq\log\left[\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}\tfrac{\delta}{\sigma}p_{x_{0}}\log\left(1+\tfrac{p_{x_{1}}}{p_{x_{0}}}\right)\right]-\tfrac{(\Delta+2\delta)^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}},

which implies that the exponential rate as
\sigma\to 0
(i.e.,
\mathsf{snr}\to\infty
) is

\displaystyle\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\sigma^{2}\log H(X\mid Y)

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\sigma^{2}\log\left[\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}\tfrac{\delta}{\sigma}p_{x_{0}}\log\left(1+\tfrac{p_{x_{1}}}{p_{x_{0}}}\right)\right]-\tfrac{(\Delta+2\delta)^{2}}{8}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{(\Delta+2\delta)^{2}}{8},

for every
\delta,\epsilon>0
. Letting
\delta\to 0
, we have

\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\sigma^{2}\log H(X\mid Y)\geq-\tfrac{\Delta^{2}}{8}>-\tfrac{(\mathsf{d}_{\min}+\epsilon)^{2}}{8},

as
\Delta<\mathsf{d}_{\min}+\epsilon
, by our choice of
x_{0},x_{1}
. Now letting
\epsilon\to 0
, we have the desired result, i.e.,
\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\sigma^{2}\log H(X\mid Y)\geq-\tfrac{\mathsf{d}_{\min}^{2}}{8}
. (Note that we do not require
\mathsf{d}_{\min}>0
anywhere, the proof also works for
\mathsf{d}_{\min}=0
.)

(b) Upper bound to
H(X\mid Y)
.

If
\mathsf{d}_{\min}=0
, then we use the trivial upper bound
H(X\mid Y)\leq H(X)
, as this gives
\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\sigma^{2}\log H(X\mid Y)\leq\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\sigma^{2}\log H(X)=0=-\mathsf{d}_{\min}^{2}/8
.
For the remainder of this upper bound, assume that
\mathsf{d}_{\min}>0
.
Hence, the support is either finite, or if it is infinite, there are no finite accumulation points.

In either case, let
\mathcal{X}=(x_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
be the set of atoms of
X
, with
\mathcal{I}=\{1,\dots,n\}
if
X
is finite and
\mathcal{I}=\mathbb{Z}
if
X
is infinite. We may, without loss of generality, assume that
x_{i}<x_{i+1}
for all
i
, as there are no accumulation points. (This may not be possible if the atoms of
X
have an accumulation point, consider the example
\mathcal{X}=\{0\}\cup\{-1-1/n:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}\cup\{1+1/n:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.)

By definition, we have
h(Y)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{Y}(y)\log f_{Y}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y
, with
f_{Y}(y)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p_{x}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}p_{i}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{(y-x_{i})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)
being the density of
Y
. This can be simplified as

\displaystyle h(Y)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{Y}(y)\log\left[\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}}p_{j}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{(y-x_{j})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\right]\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{Y}(y)\log\left[\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}}p_{j}\exp\left(-\frac{(y-x_{j})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\right]\,\mathrm{d}y-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{Y}(y)\log\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi\sigma^{2})-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}p_{i}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{(y-x_{i})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\log\left[\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}}p_{j}\exp\left(-\frac{(y-x_{j})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\right]\,\mathrm{d}y,

as
f_{Y}
integrates to 1. For each
y
, let
i^{*}(y)\in\mathcal{I}
be the index of the atom of
X
that is closest to
y
, i.e.,
i^{*}(y)=\argmin_{i}|y-x_{i}|
(in case of ties, choose one arbitrarily — there will only be countably many such
y
’s; a minimizing
i^{*}
is also guaranteed to exist for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
as
\mathsf{d}_{\min}>0
). Also let
d^{*}(y)=y-x_{i^{*}(y)}
and
d_{i}(y)=y-x_{i}
. We write simply
i^{*}
,
d^{*}
and
d_{i}
when
y
is clear from context.

As
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}}p_{j}=1
, we have that
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}}p_{j}\exp\left(-\tfrac{d_{j}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\leq 1
, and the integrand above is negative.
Then, we can lower bound
h(Y)
by considering only the term corresponding to
i=i^{*}(y)
in the summation over
i
, to obtain

\displaystyle h(Y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi\sigma^{2})-\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{i^{*}}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}\exp\left(-\tfrac{{d^{*}}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\log\left[\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}}p_{j}\exp\left(-\tfrac{d_{j}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\right]\,\mathrm{d}y.

Note that
H(X\mid Y)=H(X)-I(X;Y)=H(X)-h(Y)+h(Y\mid X)=H(X)+\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e\sigma^{2})-h(Y)
, as
h(Y\mid X)=h(Z)
, which gives

\displaystyle H(X\mid Y)

L\coloneqq L_{0}
.
∎

Lemma 2

(Tangents with same
y
-intercept to convex functions)
.

Let
f,g:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be two convex, decreasing functions
such that
f(x)>g(x)
for all
x>0
. Let
\ell_{f}
and
\ell_{g}
be
the tangents to
f
and
g
respectively that intersect the
y
-axis at
the same point. Then, the slope of
\ell_{f}
is strictly smaller in
magnitude (less negative) than that of
\ell_{g}
.

Proof.

Suppose that the slope of
\ell_{f}
is strictly larger in magnitude,
i.e., more negative, than that of
\ell_{g}
. Since
\ell_{f}
and
\ell_{g}

intersect each other on the
y
-axis, we have
\ell_{f}(x)<\ell_{g}(x)

for all
x>0
. There exists a point
x_{f}>0
where the tangent

\ell_{f}
touches
f
, i.e.,
\ell_{f}(x_{f})=f(x_{f})
.
Further, since
g
is convex, it always lies above its tangent

\ell_{g}
. Hence, we have the chain of inequalities

g(x_{f})\geq\ell_{g}(x_{f})>\ell_{f}(x_{f})=f(x_{f}),

which contradicts the hypothesis that
f(x)>g(x)
for all
x>0
.
Thus, the slope of
\ell_{f}
must be strictly smaller in magnitude than
that of
\ell_{g}
.
∎

3.3
Low- and high-entropy regimes

From Section
3.2
, we have a parametric expression for
\mathsf{d}_{h}
given by
1/\sqrt{-L^{\prime}(\lambda)}
at
h=L(\lambda)-\lambda L^{\prime}(\lambda)
. Fig.
3
shows the tradeoff between
\mathsf{d}_{h}
and
h
as
\lambda
varies from
0
to
\infty
; in particular, we have
h\to 0
and
\mathsf{d}_{h}\to\infty
as
\lambda\to\infty
, and
h\to\infty
and
\mathsf{d}_{h}\to 0
as
\lambda\to 0
.

Consider the function
L(\lambda)
. It is known that
L(\lambda)\approx\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\pi}{\lambda}
for
\lambda\ll\pi
and
L(\lambda)\approx\log(1+2e^{-\lambda})
for
\lambda\gg\pi
, and that these approximations are surprisingly tight as long as
\lambda
is not too close to
\pi

[
5
]
.
Hence,
\mathsf{d}_{h}
behaves differently depending on whether
h
is smaller than or larger than
L(\pi)-\pi L^{\prime}(\pi)\approx 0.48
bits, as characterized in the lemma below and shown in Fig.
5
.

(a)

\mathsf{d}_{h}
versus
h
for small
h
. Both axes in log-scale.

(b)

\mathsf{d}_{h}
versus
h
for large
h
.
y
-axis in log-scale.

Figure 5
:
Comparison of
\mathsf{d}_{h}
and its approximations in the small and large entropy regimes: (a)
\mathsf{d}_{h}\approx\sqrt{\frac{1}{h}\log\frac{2}{h}}
for small
h\ll 0.48
bits, and (b)
\mathsf{d}_{h}\approx\sqrt{2\pi e}\exp(-h)
for large
h\gg 0.48
bits.

Lemma 3

(Approximation for
\mathsf{d}_{h}
at low and high entropy)
.

We have the following approximations for
\mathsf{d}_{h}
at small and large
h
:

\mathsf{d}_{h}\approx\begin{cases}\sqrt{\frac{1}{h}\log\frac{2}{h}}&h\ll L(\pi)-\pi L^{\prime}(\pi)\approx 0.48\text{ bits},\\
\sqrt{2\pi e}\,\exp(-h)&h\gg L(\pi)-\pi L^{\prime}(\pi).\end{cases}

Proof.

We have the following pairs of upper and lower bounds on
L(\lambda)
that are true for all
\lambda>0
but are tight for small and large
\lambda
respectively
[
19
,
5
]
.
For small
\lambda\ll\pi
, we use

\displaystyle\sqrt{\tfrac{\pi}{\lambda}}\left(1+2e^{-\frac{\pi^{2}}{\lambda}}\right)\leq e^{L(\lambda)}\leq\sqrt{\tfrac{\pi}{\lambda}}\left(1+2e^{-\frac{\pi^{2}}{\lambda}}\right)+e^{-\frac{\pi^{2}}{\lambda}},

giving us the approximation
L(\lambda)\approx\log\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\lambda}}
,
and for large
\lambda\gg\pi
, we use

\displaystyle 1+2e^{-\lambda}\kern 5.0pt\leq\kern 5.0pte^{L(\lambda)}\kern 5.0pt\leq\kern 5.0pt1+2e^{-\lambda}+\sqrt{\tfrac{\pi}{\lambda}}e^{-\lambda},

which gives us
L(\lambda)\approx\log\big(1+2e^{-\lambda}\big)\approx 2e^{-\lambda}
.
We now prove the claim by using these to approximate
\lambda_{h}
and write
\mathsf{d}_{h}=1/\sqrt{-L^{\prime}(\lambda_{h})}
in terms of
h=L(\lambda_{h})-\lambda_{h}L^{\prime}(\lambda_{h})
in each regime. The arguments can be made rigorous by replacing the approximations with the appropriate lower and upper bounds at each stage.

For small
\lambda
, we have
L(\lambda)\approx\log\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\lambda}}
. Hence,
L^{\prime}(\lambda)\approx-1/(2\lambda)
, which implies that
\mathsf{d}_{h}\approx\sqrt{2\lambda_{h}}
. The entropy is
h=L(\lambda)-\lambda L^{\prime}(\lambda)\approx\log\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\lambda}}+\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\pi e}{\lambda}
, or equivalently,
\lambda_{h}\approx\pi e\exp(-2h)
. Putting the two together, we have
\mathsf{d}_{h}\approx\sqrt{2\pi e}\exp(-h)
.

On the other hand, for large
\lambda
, we have
L(\lambda)\approx\log\big(1+2e^{-\lambda}\big)\approx 2e^{-\lambda}
. Hence,
L^{\prime}(\lambda)\approx-2e^{-\lambda}
. The entropy is
h=L(\lambda)-\lambda L^{\prime}(\lambda)\approx 2e^{-\lambda}(1+\lambda)\approx 2\lambda e^{-\lambda}
. This is related to the secondary branch of the Lambert-
W
function
W_{-1}

[
6
]
, where
W_{-1}(y)
, for small negative
y
, is the large negative
x
that solves
y=xe^{x}
.
W_{-1}(x)
can be approximated as
\log(-x)-\log(-\log(-x))
. Here, we have
-\lambda_{h}\approx W_{-1}(-\frac{h}{2})
, and hence,
-\lambda_{h}\approx\log\frac{h}{2}-\log\log\frac{2}{h}
, or equivalently,
\exp(\lambda_{h})\approx\frac{2}{h}\log\frac{2}{h}
. Hence,
\mathsf{d}_{h}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-L^{\prime}(\lambda_{h})}}\approx\sqrt{\frac{\exp(\lambda_{h})}{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{h}\log\frac{2}{h}}
.
∎

With the above characterization of
\mathsf{d}_{h}
, we can now complete the proof of Corollary
1
.

Proof of Corollary
1
.

We get (
2
) by directly plugging into (
1
) the approximations from Lemma
3
.

To obtain the expression for
h\ll 0.48
bits, we used the approximation
L(\lambda)\approx\log(1+2e^{-\lambda})
for large
\lambda
, which is exactly what we obtain if we restrict the summation in
\log\big(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp(-\lambda i^{2})\big)
to include only
i=0,\pm 1
. This gives the symmetric three-point distribution with a large mass at the origin.

Finally, to check that
\mathcal{N}_{\mathsf{d}_{h}\mathbb{Z}}(\lambda_{h})
converges in distribution to
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
as
h\to\infty
, note that
\mathsf{d}_{h}\approx\sqrt{2\lambda_{h}}
. Hence, it is enough to check that
\mathcal{N}_{a\mathbb{Z}}(a^{2}/2)
converges to
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
as
a
goes to 0, which follows from
[
5
, Proposition 27]
.
∎

4
Discussion and conclusion

We studied the problem of computing the capacity of a power-constrained Gaussian channel with an additional entropy constraint in the asymptotic high-SNR regime. We observed that the asymptotic optimal distribution is the one that has the largest minimum distance between consecutive atoms, showed that the discrete Gaussian uniquely achieves this largest minimum distance, and characterized the exponential rate at which the entropy-constrained capacity approaches
h
as
\mathsf{snr}\to\infty
.

One claim that remains unresolved is the convergence of the sequence
(X^{*}_{\mathsf{snr}})_{\mathsf{snr}}
of true capacity-achieving distributions at finite SNRs to the discrete Gaussian as
\mathsf{snr}\to\infty
. The main difficulty is that Lemma
1
no longer holds in general if
X
is allowed to change with
\mathsf{snr}
, and indeed, it is possible to find counterexamples by allowing the probability masses at the atoms achieving
\mathsf{d}_{\min}
to decay exponentially fast in
\mathsf{snr}
. Nonetheless, we believe that
X^{*}_{\mathsf{snr}}
should still satisfy Lemma
1
, i.e.,
\lim_{\mathsf{snr}\to\infty}-\frac{1}{\mathsf{snr}}\log H(X^{*}_{\mathsf{snr}}\mid X^{*}_{\mathsf{snr}}+Z)=\lim_{\mathsf{snr}\to\infty}\mathsf{d}_{\min}(X^{*}_{\mathsf{snr}})^{2}/8
, where
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1/\mathsf{snr})
.
This is exactly the setup for which the tools of
\Gamma
-convergence
[
3
]
have been developed, but it remains unexplored by the information theory community as yet.

Since such a result clearly relies on
X^{*}_{\mathsf{snr}}
being the optimal distribution at finite SNR, the necessary KKT conditions satisfied by the optimal
X
with atoms
x_{i}
of probability
p_{i}
might be of use. They are given by the following self-consistent equations:

p_{i}\propto\exp\Big(\frac{U(x_{i})+\lambda{x_{i}}^{2}}{\mu}\Big)\quad\text{and}\quad U^{\prime}(x_{i})=-2\lambda x_{i}

for all
i
, where
U(x)=\mathbb{E}[\log f_{Y}(x+Z)]
and
f_{Y}
is the density of
Y=X+Z
, and
\lambda,\mu>0
are parameters to be chosen to make
\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]=1
and
H(X)=h
.

In the absence of an entropy constraint, we would only have had the second condition, and it can be checked that it is satisfied at all
x\in\mathbb{R}
for
X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
. With the entropy constraint, however, the parameter
\mu
acts as a “temperature” controlling the spread of the distribution. Another interpretation is the following: the quantity
U(x)+\lambda x^{2}
represents an “energy”, the atoms are the stationary points of this energy, and the probabilities are given by a softmax of the energies (but
U
also depends on the probability distribution). It would be interesting to see if such a line of thinking can yield bounds on the cardinality of the optimal distribution at finite SNR.

Rootstock selection and grafting systems affect pepino (Solanum muricatum) fruit nutritional quality and plant performance
Authors: Shanhua Bao, Cheng Si, Zhu Sun, Yujiang Wu, Xiaotong Wang, Qiwen Zhong, Shipeng Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPepino (Solanum muricatum) is a nutritionally valuable Solanaceae crop, but optimizing its grafting practices to improve survival, growth, and fruit quality remains a critical challenge for commercial production. In this study, we systematically evaluated four pepino scions grafted onto nine Solanaceae rootstocks—including tomato (Solanum lycopersicum), pepper (Capsicum annuum), and eggplant (Solanum melongena)—and optimized grafting parameters (cotyledon removal, grafting height, glucose pretreatment, and shading duration). We measured grafting survival, transplant success, vegetative growth, photosynthetic performance, metabolic activity, and antioxidant enzyme levels throughout early development. The optimal combination, ‘Qingcanxiang’ scion grafted onto eggplant rootstock ‘Moshi’, achieved survival rates above 90%, promoted shoot and root biomass accumulation, enhanced net Pn, and maintained elevated soluble protein and sugar contents. Multivariate analysis confirmed that genus-level taxonomic compatibility is a key determinant of graft success and physiological performance. Furthermore, heterografts improved fruit vitamin C content and sucrose accumulation while maintaining comparable yield, fruit morphology, and sensory quality. These findings provide a comprehensive and reproducible protocol for pepino grafting and practical guidance for improving growth performance, fruit quality, and stress resilience in commercial production.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-025-07848-1.Keywords: Pepino (Solanum muricatum), Grafting, Rootstock screening, Compatibility, Grafting procedure optimization, Fruit quality

Introduction

Pepino (Solanum muricatum) is a nutritionally valuable Solanaceae crop [1, 2], and optimizing its grafting practices is critical for enhancing survival, growth, and fruit quality under commercial cultivation. These nutritional and health attributes have contributed to growing commercial interest [3], but cultivation remains constrained by limited germplasm resources, narrow genetic base, and environmental stresses. These challenges are especially pronounced under Qinghai’s cool, large-diurnal-range climate and continuous-cropping pressures.

Grafting is a time-honored but powerful method of asexual propagation [4] that can substantially enhance crop resilience to biotic and abiotic stresses [5], overcome continuous‐cropping obstacles, and optimize yield and quality through judicious rootstock selection [6]. In Solanaceous vegetables, including tomato, pepper, eggplant, and pepino, grafting has been adopted commercially to improve tolerance to soil-borne diseases and environmental stresses while influencing scion physiology and fruit traits via rootstock-mediated effects, revealing potential benefits for plant growth and fruit quality [7–9]. Although pepino has been subject to some grafting studies, research on its grafting techniques and performance remains limited. Existing studies often focus on single experimental conditions, short-term physiological responses, or isolated combinations [10]. These limitations leave critical gaps in understanding graft compatibility, physiological integration, and stress resilience, which hinder the development of reproducible grafting protocols for Pepino under challenging environmental conditions. Addressing these knowledge gaps is essential for establishing reproducible and high-efficiency grafting protocols for pepino cultivation, particularly in challenging environments such as the Qinghai Plateau.

To address these gaps, this study evaluates graft compatibility using an integrated framework that considers morphological, physiological-biochemical, and anatomical traits [11, 12]. Graft compatibility is influenced by genetic proximity [13], technical practices [10], and environmental conditions [14–16]. Proper genetic matching [17], correct grafting techniques [18], and favorable environmental conditions [19] facilitate callus formation and vascular reconnection, while rootstock vigor and stress-responsive biochemical pathways contribute to scion performance [20, 21]. By focusing on these key factors, we aim to identify the main determinants of grafting success and provide practical guidance for high-efficiency Pepino production under challenging conditions such as the Qinghai Plateau.

Materials and methods

Plant materials

Four pepino scion cultivars developed by the Qinghai Academy of Agricultural and Forestry Sciences were used: elliptical fruit ‘Qingcanxiang’ (LOF), heart-shaped fruit ‘Qingningxiang’ (GYF), green heart-shaped fruit ‘Qingyuxiang’ (GGF), and round small fruit ‘Qingtianxiang’ (SRF).Nine Solanaceae rootstock varieties with different characteristics were selected (Fig. 1; Table 1).

Grafting methods

Rootstocks and scions were sown on 20 and 22 July 2022, respectively. Cleft grafting was performed 38 days after sowing (38 DAS) when rootstocks had 5–7 true leaves. Scion segments of approximately 5 cm were taken from the middle stem with one leaf and one growing point. Both scion and rootstock were cut at a 60° parallel angle using sterilized blades. The two parts were aligned for full cambial contact and secured with transparent silicone clips (1.5–2.0 mm diameter).

Post-grafting management: Grafted seedlings were placed in a greenhouse, watered thoroughly, and shaded with a 60% shade net for seven days. Environmental conditions were maintained at 28–30 °C (day) and 18–20 °C (night) with relative humidity above 80%. After healing, seedlings were gradually exposed to light over 3 days and subsequently managed under controlled greenhouse conditions with the following specific practices: irrigation to maintain soil moisture at 50–60%, temperature control at 28–30 °C (day) and 18–20 °C (night) through natural ventilation, and routine visual inspection for pest and disease symptoms with manual removal when detected.

Screening of optimal scion–rootstock combinations

A total of 36 scion–rootstock combinations were established using all four scion cultivars with nine rootstock varieties (Table 2). Both grafted and self-rooted seedlings were transplanted to greenhouse tunnels on 15 January 2023 in a double-row system (35 × 50 cm spacing, three replicates, six plants per replicate). Performance parameters including survival rate, growth vigor, and yield potential were evaluated to identify optimal combinations.

Optimization of grafting techniques

Based on the comprehensive screening evaluation of 36 scion-rootstock combinations, the optimal combination showing superior performance was selected for grafting technique optimization experiments conducted from May to December 2023. Six independent single-factor experiments were arranged in a completely randomized design with identical environmental conditions (25–30 °C, relative humidity > 80%, 60% shading for 7 days). Six sequential single-factor experiments were arranged using a step-wise optimization approach. Each experiment tested one factor while maintaining all other conditions constant. Each treatment included 30 grafted plants with three replicates.

The factors and levels tested were: Grafting method: approach grafting vs. cleft grafting; Scion position: middle stem segment vs. apical shoot; Rootstock cotyledon retention: with vs. without cotyledons; Grafting height: 5 cm vs. 8 cm above soil surface; Scion pretreatment: soaking in 0%, 0.1%, or 0.5% glucose solution for 2 h; Shading duration: 3, 5, or 7 days post-grafting.

Low-temperature stress treatment

Low-temperature stress experiments were conducted using grafted plants of the optimal combination (same as Optimization of Grafting Techniques section) and self-rooted plants (cutting-propagated seedlings of the corresponding scion cultivar) as controls. This design ensured valid comparison using identical scion genotypes to assess rootstock effects on cold tolerance. Both plant types were at the same developmental stage (6–8 true leaves, approximately 45 days post-grafting for grafted plants) with uniform morphology and vigor at treatment initiation. Each treatment consisted of 30 plants with three replications.

Temperature treatments

Plants were subjected to either control conditions (25 °C/18°C day/night) or chilling stress (10 °C/3°C day/night). Plants were maintained in controlled growth chambers under a 14 h light/10 h dark photoperiod with light intensity of 200 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ and relative humidity maintained at 75–80%. Air circulation was provided through gentle ventilation (0.5 m/s) to prevent condensation and ensure uniform environmental conditions throughout the 7-day treatment period.

Measurement methods

Sampling

Six grafted plants were randomly selected from each treatment. At 14, 28, and 42 days after grafting, three to four fully expanded leaves from different canopy positions were collected, sealed in plastic bags, kept on ice, and transported to the laboratory for physiological and biochemical analysis.

Survival and morphological traits

Graft survival was recorded at 14 days after grafting (14 DAG). Plants were considered successfully grafted if they maintained green coloration of the scion, showed no wilting or browning at the graft junction, and displayed visible new leaf emergence or active apical growth. The graft survival rate (%) was calculated as:

Graft survival rate(%)=(Number of survived plants/Total grafted plants)*100%.

Graft union rate (%) was evaluated 14 days after grafting as the proportion of survived plants exhibiting complete callus formation and vascular continuity at the graft interface. Plants showing firm adhesion between scion and rootstock tissues without visible cracking, necrosis, or separation were classified as “healed unions,” whereas those displaying partial adhesion or interface separation were regarded as “unhealed unions.” The graft union rate was calculated using the following formula:

Graft union rate(%)=(Number of healed grafted plants/Number of survived plants) *100%.

Transplant survival was assessed two weeks after transplanting (42 DAG), with success criteria including normal growth maintenance, absence of permanent wilting or yellowing, and stable establishment in the greenhouse. Plant height was measured from 1 cm above the graft union to the shoot tip. The number of new shoots was counted. Scion and rootstock diameters were measured 1 cm above and below the union using a Vernier caliper.

Photosynthetic pigments and gas exchange

For pigment analysis, 0.2 g of fresh leaf tissue (without midrib) was extracted with 5 mL of 95% ethanol for 24 h in darkness. Absorbance was measured at 665, 649, and 470 nm using a UV-2600 spectrophotometer. Calculations were: Chl a = 13.95A665 − 6.88A649; Chl b = 24.96A649 − 7.32A665; Car = (1000A470 − 2.05Chla − 114Chlb)/245. Gas-exchange parameters—net photosynthetic rate (Pn), transpiration rate (Tr), stomatal conductance (Gs), and intercellular CO₂ concentration (Ci)—were determined using a LI-688 portable photosynthesis system (LI-COR Inc., USA) at 14, 28, and 42 DAG on three plants per treatment.

Soluble compounds and antioxidant enzyme activities

Soluble sugar content (SS) and soluble protein content (SP) were determined by the anthrone and Coomassie brilliant blue methods, respectively. Antioxidant enzyme activities, including superoxide dismutase (SOD), peroxidase (POD), and catalase (CAT), were measured using commercial assay kits (Solarbio, China; Cat. Nos. BC00095, BC0205, BC5156).

Histological observation of the graft union

At 14, 28, and 42 DAG, graft-union samples were fixed in FAA solution for at least 4 h, dehydrated, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned at 4–10 μm thickness. Sections were stained with 1% safranin for 1–2 h and 0.5% fast green for 2 min, dehydrated, mounted, and observed under a light microscope to evaluate vascular reconnection and callus formation.

Fruit yield and quality measurements

At the maturity of the first fruit cluster, fruit number, single fruit weight, and yield per plant were recorded. Soluble solids were determined using a refractometer (Atago PAL-1, Japan). Vitamin C content was measured by 2,6-dichlorophenol-indophenol titration. Sugar composition was analyzed using HPLC equipped with a C18 column, and flavor profiles were evaluated using an electronic tongue (TS-5000Z, Insent Inc., Japan). Fruit shape and color traits were analyzed using the PhenoAI (Zhinong Yunxin Big Data Technology Co., Ltd., Nanjing, China) automated analysis system.

HPLC analysis conditions (Sugar composition analysis)

Agilent 1260 HPLC system with C18 column (250 mm × 4.6 mm, 5 μm). Mobile phase: acetonitrile: water (80:20, v/v), flow rate 1.0 mL/min, column temperature 30 °C, injection volume 10 µL.

Electronic tongue analysis flavor profile evaluation

TS-5000Z electronic tongue (Insent Inc., Japan) with seven sensors (umami, sweetness, sourness, bitterness, astringency, aftertaste-B, aftertaste-A). Sample preparation: fruit juice filtered through 0.45 μm membrane, measurement time 120 s per sample.

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics 20.0 (IBM Corp., Chicago, USA). For multiple group comparisons, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed, and when significant differences were detected, Duncan’s multiple range test was used for post-hoc analysis (P < 0.05). For two-group comparisons, independent samples t-tests were conducted (P < 0.05). Different letters above the bars in figures indicate statistically significant differences among treatments according to ANOVA and Duncan’s test, while asterisks (*) denote significant differences from t-tests. Microsoft Excel 2019 was used for data organization and table preparation, and GraphPad Prism 8.0 (San Diego, USA) was used for figure preparation and statistical annotations.

Results

Taxonomic compatibility determines graft union success rates

Rootstock genus fundamentally determined graft compatibility in pepino, with taxonomic boundaries overriding cultivar-specific effects (Table 3). Systematic evaluation of 36 rootstock-scion combinations revealed a striking taxonomic hierarchy: most elite Solanum rootstock combinations achieved 94.66% to 95.33% grafting survival with variance ranges of ± 1.04% to ± 2.51%, representing significantly lower variability compared to Capsicum rootstocks (± 1.02% to ± 8.54%), while the majority of Capsicum rootstocks showed significantly reduced survival rates ranging from 13.01% to 76.66% (though notable exceptions like GGF/P2 achieved 76.66% survival), with most combinations, exhibiting 4.7 fold lower success rates than optimal Solanum combinations. This general survival differential demonstrates that intrageneric compatibility typically exceeds intergeneric barriers, with top-performing Solanum-Solanum grafts maintaining 97.33% transplant survival while most Capsicum combinations declined to as low as 3.66%. Performance consistency proved equally discriminatory: optimal combinations GGF/T3 and LOF/E3 exhibited relatively low variance of ± 2.51% and ± 1.04% respectively, whereas most pepper rootstocks showed erratic failure patterns of ± 4.58% to ± 8.54%, indicating potential biological incompatibility rather than technical variability in most cases. Critically, transplant survival amplified taxonomic effects: while initial grafting showed 4.7 fold differences in optimal combinations, post-transplant performance diverged up to 24.25 fold, with the best Solanum combinations achieving 97% survival compared to as low as 4% for certain Capsicum combinations, revealing that many intergeneric combinations suffer progressive developmental failure. These quantitative patterns establish genus-level compatibility as a major biological constraint, providing empirical thresholds of greater than 90% for optimal Solanum combinations compared to highly variable results (3.3% to 63.3%) for Capsicum for rational rootstock selection in commercial pepino propagation systems.

Genus-specific rootstocks orchestrate scion morphology in pepino grafted seedlings

Morphological analysis 14 days post-grafting revealed that rootstock genus exerts profound control over pepino scion development through coordinated regulation of growth parameters (Fig. 2). Solanum rootstocks induced superior architectural traits compared to Capsicum alternatives, with tomato T3 and eggplant E2 rootstocks conferring the greatest developmental advantages. Vertical growth responses demonstrated clear taxonomic specificity: scion height on elite Solanum combinations GGF/T3 reached 12.1 cm while LOF/E2 achieved 12.7 cm, exceeding Capsicum rootstocks by 1.9 fold with LOF/P1 at 6.3 cm and ungrafted controls by 1.7 fold with CK1 at 7.1 cm (Fig. 2C). Lateral development followed similar patterns, with optimal tomato rootstocks GYF/T2 and GYF/T3 stimulating 5.0 new shoots per plant, representing a 2.5 fold enhancement over Capsicum combinations at 2.0 shoots (Fig. 2D). Vascular development reflected rootstock-mediated resource allocation efficiency. Scion stem diameter consistently exceeded 0.40 mm across all Solanum rootstocks, reaching 0.48 mm in LOF/T2, while Capsicum rootstocks failed to surpass 0.30 mm (Fig. 2E). Rootstock stem thickening patterns corroborated this trend, with tomato rootstocks achieving 0.55 to 0.60 mm diameter versus less than 0.35 mm in Capsicum treatments (Fig. 2F). Quantitative ranking established a performance hierarchy: T3 ≈ E2 > E3 > T1/T2 ≫ P1-P3, indicating that genus-level compatibility governs resource partitioning between vertical extension, lateral branching, and vascular development.

Rootstock genus differentially modulates pepino scion photosynthetic pigment accumulation and gas exchange

Photosynthetic parameters were monitored in nine representative graft combinations throughout at 14, 28, and 42 days after grafting to elucidate the physiological underpinnings of their morphological differences(Fig. 3). Rootstock genus exerted temporally dynamic effects on scion photosynthetic capacity, with Solanum rootstocks demonstrating superior integration compared to Capsicum alternatives. Photosynthetic pigment accumulation exhibited rootstock-specific temporal patterns. While initial differences were modest at 14 days after grafting (14 DAG), eggplant rootstocks induced dramatic pigment enhancement by 28 DAG, with G/E1 reaching 195 mg g⁻¹ FW, exceeding tomato combinations by 30% before stabilizing at equivalent levels by 42 DAG(Fig. 3A). This temporal divergence suggests rootstock-mediated regulation of chloroplast biogenesis during critical developmental phases. Gas exchange parameters revealed systematic taxonomic effects on photosynthetic machinery. Net Pn showed maximal divergence at 28 DAG, with eggplant combination L/E2 achieving 14.5 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ and tomato combination L/T2 reaching 10.4 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ both exceeding Capsicum rootstocks by greater than 2 fold (Fig. 3D). Tr paralleled this hierarchy, with elite Solanum combinations maintaining 3.8 mmol m⁻² s⁻¹ versus less than 2 mmol m⁻² s⁻¹ in Capsicum grafts at 42 DAG(Fig. 3C). Mechanistic analysis through intercellular Ci measurements revealed that performance differences stemmed from stomatal and biochemical limitations rather than CO₂ availability, as Ci remained stable at 250 to 370 µmol mol⁻¹ across treatments (Fig. 3E). Gs correlated directly with photosynthetic capacity, confirming that rootstock genus determines hydraulic-photosynthetic coupling efficiency (Fig. 3F). These temporal patterns establish that Solanum rootstocks enhance scion photosynthetic performance through coordinated regulation of pigment biosynthesis, stomatal function, and hydraulic conductivity, with eggplant rootstocks conferring optimal physiological integration during critical developmental windows.

Rootstock genus influences pepino scion biochemical metabolism and antioxidant defense systems

Biochemical profiling revealed that rootstock genus determines scion metabolic capacity through coordinated regulation of nutrient metabolism and antioxidant defense systems, with histological evidence confirming the mechanistic basis of compatibility differences (Fig. 4). Elite Solanum combinations demonstrated superior metabolic coordination compared to Capsicum alternatives. SP analysis revealed sustained high level synthesis in tomato combinations with L/T2 reaching 13.11 mg/g and G/T3 achieving 13.01 mg/g, while carbohydrate transport efficiency peaked in eggplant combination L/E2 at 17.53 mg/g SS, representing 1.6 fold enhancement over suboptimal combinations(Fig. 4A-B). These patterns indicate rootstock-mediated optimization of nitrogen and carbon partitioning pathways. Antioxidant enzyme systems exhibited sophisticated temporal coordination that distinguished taxonomic compatibility classes(Fig. 4C). SOD activity in elite combination LOF/E2 achieved early peak performance at 20,874 U·g⁻¹·FW at 14 DAG, maintaining 1.4 fold superiority over controls before stabilizing at enhanced levels of 24,941 U·g⁻¹·FW at 42 DAG. Capsicum combinations remained consistently suppressed below 16,000 U·g⁻¹·FW throughout development. CAT activity patterns reinforced this hierarchy, with optimal Solanum combinations GGF/T3 reaching 321 U·g⁻¹·FW and LOF/E2 achieving 298 U·g⁻¹·FW at 42 DAG, demonstrating 2.3 to 2.7 fold superiority over Capsicum rootstocks at 105 to 141 U·g⁻¹·FW. POD activity revealed rootstock-specific programming: while initial activities were equivalent across treatments, elite Solanum combinations maintained elevated performance at 14.5 to 16.0 U·g⁻¹·FW, whereas Capsicum rootstocks declined systematically to 7.0 to 9.0 U·g⁻¹·FW by 42 DAG. Anatomical analysis provided mechanistic validation for biochemical differences (Fig. 4D). Pepino-eggplant grafts achieved complete vascular reconnection with extensive parenchyma proliferation and minimal necrotic tissue by 42 DAG, while pepino-pepper combinations exhibited persistent anatomical discontinuity with incomplete vascular integration and sustained necrotic barriers.

Multivariate analysis establishes integrated compatibility framework

Principal component analysis (PCA) of 28 morpho-physiological traits was conducted to quantitatively validate the taxonomic compatibility hierarchy, extracting seven components that together accounted for 95.94% of the total variance (Fig. 5). The first two components captured 53.8% of variation, with PC1 (33.4%) predominantly loading on photosynthetic parameters including chlorophyll content, carbohydrate metabolism, and net Pn, while PC2 (20.4%) represented survival and hydraulic traits including survival rate, Gs, and Tr (Fig. 5C). Composite scoring definitively established L/E2 as the optimal grafting combination at 2.06 points, substantially outperforming other elite combinations G/T3 at 1.42 points and L/T2 at 1.05 points, while exceeding ungrafted controls CK-L at 1.03 points (Fig. 5D). L/E2’s statistical supremacy reflected its exceptional performance across multiple dimensions: 97% transplant survival, peak photosynthetic capacity, and superior carbohydrate accumulation patterns. Capsicum rootstock combinations uniformly clustered in negative scoring regions from negative 2.57 to negative 1.04 points, demonstrating systematic failure across physiological, morphological, and biochemical dimensions. The high variance explanation of 95.94% demonstrates that graft compatibility operates through coordinated multi-trait regulation rather than single factor determination, with PC1’s emphasis on metabolic traits and PC2’s focus on hydraulic parameters indicating that successful grafting requires simultaneous optimization of photosynthetic machinery and water transport systems, precisely the combination achieved by genus-compatible Solanum rootstocks.

Effects of technical parameters on grafting efficiency and biomass accumulation of pepino

Optimal technical protocols emerged from systematic evaluation of grafting parameters across diverse rootstock-scion combinations, with approach grafting in pepino-eggplant heterografts achieving survival rates above 80%, roughly 78% higher than those obtained by cleft grafting (Fig. 6A). Graft union rates followed similar patterns, exceeding 90% in approach grafted L/E combinations (Fig. 6B). Biomass analysis confirmed approach grafted L/E combinations achieved higher aboveground fresh weight at 1.45 g and dry weight at 0.26 g than cleft grafting (Table S1, Figure S1). Stem internode scions significantly outperformed shoot apex scions, with L/E combinations achieving 80% survival rates, representing 45.5% higher than apex scions, and 90% union rates (Fig. 6C-D). Despite producing shorter plants, stem internode scions showed superior biomass accumulation, with L/E combinations demonstrating higher aboveground fresh weight at 1.44 g versus 1.14 g and dry weight at 0.26 g versus 0.16 g, as well as enhanced root development (Table S2, Figure S2). Rootstock cotyledon excision substantially enhanced survival rates across all combinations, with L/E combinations showing 2.5 fold increases compared to cotyledon retention (Fig. 6E). The most dramatic improvements appeared in underground development, with cotyledon excision L/E combinations achieving 5.2 fold higher underground dry weight at 0.62 g versus 0.12 g (Table S3, Figure S3). The 3 cm grafting height demonstrated superior performance compared to 5 cm height, with L/E combinations achieving 83.5% survival rates, representing 22.8% higher. While aboveground biomass was comparable, underground dry weight at 3 cm was 3.5 fold higher at 0.38 g versus 0.11 g, indicating better root establishment (Table S4, Figure S4). Glucose pretreatment at 0.5% significantly enhanced graft success, with L/E combinations achieving nearly 90% survival rates, representing 25% higher than water controls. This treatment increased aboveground fresh weight by 12.1% at 1.67 g versus 1.49 g and underground dry weight by 11.5% (Table S5, Figure S5). Extended shading duration of 7 D significantly improved both survival rates to greater than 90%, representing 23.3% higher than 3 D treatments, and biomass accumulation. Seven day treatments for L/E combinations showed 45.2% higher aboveground fresh weight and 135.7% higher dry weight compared to 3 D treatments (Table S6, Figure S6).

This integrated system consistently achieved greater than 90% success rates in pepino/eggplant heterografts while significantly enhancing biomass accumulation through optimized resource allocation, providing reliable support for commercial production.

Effects of grafting on cold tolerance and fruit quality of pepino

Key physiological indicators of self-grafted pepino (LOF) and pepino-eggplant heterograft (LOF/E2) were measured under normal conditions at 25 °C/18°C and low temperature conditions at 10 °C/3°C to assess the effects of temperature across rootstock-scion combinations (Fig. 7). Results showed distinct physiological response patterns despite no significant differences in most measured indicators. Photosynthetic pigment analysis revealed significant differences in chlorophyll a content between grafting methods under normal temperature conditions (Fig. 7A). LOF maintained higher stability, decreasing from 19.97 mg g⁻¹ to 5.54 mg g⁻¹ representing 72.3% reduction over 7 D, while LOF/E2 exhibited a more dramatic decline from 16.22 mg g⁻¹ to 2.43 mg g⁻¹ representing 85.0% reduction. Carotenoid content showed even more pronounced differences (Fig. 7C), with LOF plants maintaining significantly higher stability, decreasing only 29.2% from 5.06 mg g⁻¹ to 3.58 mg g⁻¹, while LOF/E2 showed a dramatic 87.7% reduction from 4.97 mg g⁻¹ to 0.61 mg g⁻¹. Antioxidant enzyme activity under low temperature stress revealed important response differences. SOD activity decreased substantially in both combinations, with LOF showing a 60.0% reduction from 194.04 mg g⁻¹ to 77.57 mg g⁻¹ and LOF/E2 exhibiting a slightly lower 55.3% reduction (Fig. 7D). POD activity showed similar patterns, with LOF decreasing 69.9% from 59,192 mg g⁻¹ to 17,798 mg g⁻¹, while LOF/E2 demonstrated better enzyme retention with only a 55.1% decrease from 54,577 mg g⁻¹ to 24,478 mg g⁻¹(Fig. 7E). CAT activity showed no significant differences between rootstock-scion combinations (Fig. 7F).

Effects of grafting on fruit yield and quality

Growth parameters and fruit quality characteristics were assessed to evaluate the comprehensive impact of heterografting on pepino plants (Fig. 8). Results revealed distinct impacts of eggplant rootstock on pepino plant development and fruit composition.

Note: A: Effects of grafting on pepino plant growth and yield indicators, including plant height, stem diameter, number of mature fruits, individual fruit weight, and yield per plant; B: Effects of grafting on sugar content and soluble solids content in pepino fruits, including sucrose, fructose, glucose, total soluble solids, and vitamin C content; C: Effects of grafting on pepino fruit roundness; D: Radar chart analysis of effects of grafting on pepino fruit flavor characteristics, including sweetness, acidity, saltiness, bitterness, umami, and aftertaste.

Plant growth analysis demonstrated that pepino/eggplant heterografts LOF/E2 exhibited significantly reduced plant height at 88.2 cm compared to self-grafted pepino plants LOF at 102.7 cm, representing a 14.1% reduction (p = 0.001) (Fig. 8A). However, other growth parameters including stem diameter at 8.70 versus 8.97 cm, number of mature fruits at 8.7 versus 9.0, average fruit weight at 0.097 versus 0.104 kg, and overall yield at 0.78 versus 0.89 kg showed no significant differences between rootstock-scion combinations. Fruit internal quality analysis revealed striking differences in nutritional composition (Fig. 8B). Total soluble solids content in LOF/E2 fruits at 6.51% was significantly lower than in LOF fruits at 9.51%, representing a 31.5% reduction (p = 0.019). In contrast, vitamin C content in LOF/E2 fruits at 6.31 µg g⁻¹ was substantially higher than in LOF fruits at 2.13 µg g⁻¹, demonstrating a nearly 3 fold increase(p = 0.015). Individual sugar analysis showed that sucrose content in heterografted fruits at 114.23 mg g⁻¹ was 56.8% higher than in self-grafted fruits at 72.86 mg g⁻¹, though this difference approached but did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.090). Fructose at 253.63 versus 270.83 mg g⁻¹ and glucose at 122.62 versus 153.90 mg g⁻¹ contents showed no significant differences between treatments. Fruit morphological characteristics showed that heterografting did not significantly alter fruit roundness at 1.10 versus 1.11, with both treatments producing fruits with similar shape parameters (Fig. 8C). Sensory evaluation indicated comparable flavor profiles between LOF/E2 and LOF fruits across multiple taste attributes (Fig. 8D).

These results demonstrate that while eggplant rootstock grafting significantly enhances vitamin C accumulation in pepino fruits, it reduces plant height and total soluble solids content without significantly affecting other growth parameters, fruit morphology, or sensory qualities. This suggests that heterografting provides selective benefits for vitamin C enrichment at the expense of modest reductions in plant height and sugar content.

Discussion

The present study demonstrated pronounced differences in graft compatibility, survival, and subsequent physiological performance of pepino when grafted onto different Solanum and Capsicum rootstocks. Pepino grafted onto eggplant exhibited markedly higher survival and union formation rates, accompanied by more continuous vascular connections and faster recovery of photosynthetic activity, whereas combinations with pepper rootstocks showed incomplete vascular reconnection and delayed physiological recovery. These results indicate that graft success in pepino is largely governed by phylogenetic proximity [22]. Within the Solanum lineage, conserved developmental and signaling frameworks likely promote coordinated cambial activation and vascular differentiation, while more distant taxa display impaired tissue adhesion and delayed vascular reconnection [23].

Anatomical observations further confirmed these findings. The pepino–eggplant unions rapidly formed dense parenchymatous bridges and continuous xylem–phloem networks within several days after grafting, whereas pepper rootstocks often exhibited disorganized callus and misaligned vascular strands [24]. This structural integrity corresponds to the higher survival observed in compatible combinations, implying more efficient activation of cambial cells and cell-wall–modifying enzymes [25]. Such coordinated reactivation may be regulated by shared auxin–cytokinin gradients within the Solanum genus [26]. Therefore, graft compatibility in pepino represents not only mechanical cohesion but also anatomical and molecular synchronization between partners of close genetic affinity.

Beyond interspecific compatibility, several technical parameters significantly affected graft performance. Approach grafting achieved the highest survival and union rates, likely because the retained root systems preserved water balance and expanded the cambial contact surface during the critical healing phase [27, 28]. Stem-internode scions performed better than shoot-apex scions, probably owing to greater carbohydrate reserves and a higher density of meristematic cells that accelerate callus proliferation [29]. Cotyledon removal significantly improved survival by reducing transpiration demand and reallocating assimilates and signaling molecules toward the graft interface [30]. Lower grafting height (3 cm) enhanced success through thicker, well-aligned cambial tissues near the base, while glucose pretreatment promoted cell-wall synthesis and mitotic activation as both a metabolic substrate and a signal molecule [29]. Moreover, extended post-grafting shading reduced evaporative stress and maintained favorable humidity, thereby further improving survival and vascular continuity [31, 32]. Together, these quantified improvements underscore the importance of precise physical alignment, carbohydrate supply, and microclimatic control in ensuring rapid and stable graft union formation in pepino.

In addition to enhancing survival and stress tolerance, grafting altered fruit quality traits. Fruits from pepino grafted onto eggplant accumulated significantly higher levels of ascorbic acid but exhibited a notable reduction in soluble solids. The increase in vitamin C likely results from rootstock-induced stimulation of NADPH-dependent ascorbate biosynthesis, whereas the decline in soluble solids may stem from vigorous vegetative growth causing dilution effects, or from restricted assimilate accumulation during prolonged shading in the healing stage [33, 34]. Such trade-offs are common in heterografted systems, reflecting altered hydraulic conductance and carbon partitioning between partners. Moderating shading duration or adjusting harvest timing may help maintain both high nutritional quality and desirable sweetness.

Under low-temperature stress, grafted seedlings showed a more complex response pattern. Although activities of SOD and POD decreased under chilling in both grafted and self-rooted plants, their reduction magnitudes were similar, indicating that the rootstock did not substantially enhance early ROS-scavenging capacity [35, 36]. Meanwhile, grafted seedlings suffered more pronounced chlorophyll loss. This suggests that the eggplant rootstock may prioritize metabolic recovery and growth-related processes over chloroplast maintenance during acute chilling, or that altered hormonal signaling (auxin–cytokinin–ABA crosstalk) transiently disrupts redox homeostasis [21]. Despite this short-term disadvantage, grafted plants eventually recovered growth after temperature normalization, implying that the structural compatibility advantages of the eggplant rootstock may contribute to long-term resilience once acclimation is established.

Collectively, these findings highlight that successful grafting in pepino depends on two complementary aspects: biological compatibility, determined by phylogenetic and developmental congruence, and technical optimization, achieved through refined operational parameters. The integrated combination of approach grafting, cotyledon removal, low graft height, glucose pretreatment, and prolonged shading maximized survival and union formation, while introducing physiological shifts that require balanced management. The concurrent increase in vitamin C and decrease in soluble solids, together with the nuanced antioxidant responses under chilling, reveal the complexity of source–sink regulation and signaling interactions across the graft interface.

Conclusion

This study systematically established an efficient grafting system for pepino by integrating rootstock selection and technical optimization. Pepino grafted onto Solanum melongena rootstocks exhibited significantly higher graft survival, faster vascular reconnection, and better growth recovery than those grafted onto Capsicum species, confirming that phylogenetic proximity is a key determinant of compatibility. Optimization of grafting method, scion position, cotyledon removal, grafting height, glucose pretreatment, and post-grafting shading further improved healing efficiency by enhancing cambial alignment, carbohydrate supply, and microclimatic stability at the union. Although eggplant rootstocks increased ascorbic acid accumulation, they also reduced soluble solids in fruit, revealing a physiological trade-off that can be managed through shading and harvest adjustments. Under low-temperature stress, grafted seedlings did not show stronger antioxidant enzyme activities but maintained structural integrity and recovery potential.

Overall, this work provides a comprehensive framework linking anatomical, physiological, and technical factors governing graft success in pepino. The results not only clarify the biological basis of Solanum compatibility but also offer practical guidance for improving grafting efficiency, fruit quality, and stress resilience in commercial cultivation systems.

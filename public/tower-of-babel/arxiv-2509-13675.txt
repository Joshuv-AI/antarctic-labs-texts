A Framework for Connecting G-Expectations and Rough Paths via Parameterized Control
Guangqian Zhao

Abstract
This work proposes a framework for exploring the profound connection between G-expectation and Rough Paths Theory. We initiate this program by constructing a parameterized control representation and establishing an injective homomorphism between the space of G-random variables and families of classical random variables. Building on this, we outline a promising path-space formulation inspired by Rough Paths Theory, suggesting that G-expectations can ultimately be represented as deterministic functionals. This synthesis provides a new perspective and a clear roadmap for future research in model uncertainty.



Key Words:

G-expectation, Rough Paths, Parameterized Control Representation, Stochastic Control, Nonlinear Expectations, Path-determinacy, Quadratic Variation, Model Uncertainty

1
Introduction

The theory of nonlinear expectations, pioneered by Peng with the framework of
G
-expectation[1], provides a mathematically robust foundation for decision-making under
Knightian uncertainty
—where the probability model itself is unknown or ambiguous. This is particularly relevant in finance, where volatility is notoriously difficult to estimate. Unlike classical stochastic analysis, which operates under a single fixed probability measure, the
G
-framework necessitates reasoning over an entire family of measures, capturing the uncertainty in the underlying model’s volatility.

While powerful, this approach presents a profound methodological challenge: how to reconcile the pathwise behavior of stochastic processes across an uncountable family of mutually singular probability measures. The canonical construction of the
G
-expectation space, though elegant, is inherently abstract. This abstraction can obscure the intrinsic geometry of fundamental objects like
G
-Brownian motion and the solutions to
G
-stochastic differential equations (
G
-SDEs).

This work proposes a new paradigm to address this challenge. We introduce a parameterized control representation that seeks to bridge the
G
-expectation and Rough Paths theory. Our core thesis is that the essence of a
G
-expectation can be captured by a supremum over classical expectations associated with controlled stochastic differential equations:

\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\cdot]=\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}}\mathbb{E}_{P}[\cdot^{\theta}],

where each control process
\theta_{t}\in\Theta
dictates a specific volatility scenario. In this viewpoint, a
G
-Brownian motion is not a single path but is represented by a family
\{B_{t}^{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}}
, where
B_{t}^{\theta}=\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{s}dW_{s}
.

We construct a canonical mapping
\Phi
from the space of
G
-random variables to such families of classical random variables. We demonstrate that
\Phi
preserves the essential algebraic and analytic structure, providing a transformative methodology: a problem under
G
-expectation can be approached by solving a family of parameterized classical SDEs.

The central and profound challenge that emerges from this construction—and the primary focus of our ongoing research—is the problem of gluing. The mapping
\Phi
is inherently injective, translating a single
G
-random variable into a coherent family of classical random variables. The converse direction, however, is the crux of the matter: Given a family of solutions
\{X^{\theta}\}
to controlled SDEs, what are the necessary and sufficient conditions for this family to cohere or glue together to form a single, well-defined stochastic process
X
under the
G
-expectation? This is not merely a question of surjectivity but a deep
analytic and geometric
problem concerning the quasi-sure structure of path spaces. It requires understanding how pathwise properties defined under a spectrum of singular measures can be unified.

Building upon this framework, we outline a path-space formulation inspired by Rough Paths theory.
We conjecture that the fundamental objects of the
G
-framework can be understood as deterministic functionals of the driving path and its quadratic variation. This perspective suggests that the initial, probabilistic step of generating the path and its volatility can be separated from the subsequent, pathwise analysis of the solution. The gluing problem thus translates into identifying the correct geometric and analytic constraints on the lifted path that ensure a deterministic solution exists.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows: Section 2 reviews the fundamental concepts of
G
-expectation. Section 3 details the construction of the parameterized control representation and the mapping
\Phi
. Section 4 demonstrates its application for solving
G
-SDEs. Finally, Section 5,6 explores the connections to Rough Paths theory and formalizes the gluing problem as the key to a complete pathwise interpretation of model uncertainty.

Although the connection between
G
-expectation and stochastic control is well-established (see,[1]]), prior research has not systematically constructed a specific homomorphism that preserves the underlying algebraic and analytic structures. The core contributions of this work are threefold:

(a)

We rigorously construct, for the first time, a canonical homomorphism
\Phi
between the
G
-expectation space and a space of parameterized classical random variables, and prove that it preserves key operations including addition, multiplication, stochastic integration, and even the
G
-Itô formula.

(b)

Using this homomorphism as a foundation, we reframe the problem of solving
G
-SDEs entirely into the problem of analyzing the consistency of a family of parameterized classical SDEs.

(c)

Most significantly, we delineate the ultimate destination of this framework—a fully deterministic, rough path-based perspective via path functionals—and identify the “gluing problem” as the central challenge to be overcome for its completion.

2
Fundamental Concepts and Parametric Representation

2.1
G-Expectation as a Value Function

The fundamental insight is that G-expectation can be represented as the
value function
of a stochastic control problem. For a G-Brownian motion
B_{t}
and terminal payoff
\varphi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have:

\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(B_{T})]=\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}_{0,T}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\varphi\left(\int_{0}^{T}\theta_{s}dW_{s}\right)\right]

(2.1)

where:

•

\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}_{0,T}
denotes admissible controls taking values in
\Theta

•

W_{t}
is a classical Brownian motion under reference measure
\mathbb{P}

•

\Theta=[\underline{\sigma}^{2}I_{d},\overline{\sigma}^{2}I_{d}]\subset S^{d}_{+}
models volatility uncertainty

This equivalence reveals that:

1.

Volatility ambiguity
is characterized by the control set
\Theta

2.

G-Brownian paths
emerge as controlled processes:
B_{t}^{\theta}=\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{s}dW_{s}

2.2
Dynamic Programming and Nonlinear PDEs

The control-theoretic perspective leads to a
Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation
. Define the value function:

v(t,x)=\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}_{t,T}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\varphi\left(x+\int_{t}^{T}\theta_{s}dW_{s}\right)\right]

(2.2)

It satisfies the nonlinear parabolic PDE:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v+G(D_{x}^{2}v)=0,&(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
v(T,x)=\varphi(x)\end{cases}

(2.3)

where the nonlinearity
G
encodes the uncertainty set:

G(A)=\frac{1}{2}\sup_{\gamma\in\Theta}\text{tr}(A\gamma\gamma^{\top}),\quad A\in S^{d}

(2.4)

This PDE is the
infinitesimal manifestation
of the
Dynamic Programming Principle (DPP)
:

v(t,x)=\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}_{t,t+h}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[v\left(t+h,x+\int_{t}^{t+h}\theta_{s}dW_{s}\right)\right]

(2.5)

The central insight of this work is establishing a
natural homomorphism
between elements in the G-expectation space and parameterized families of controlled processes. Specifically:

1.

G-Brownian motion as controlled paths
:
For each volatility control process
\theta\in\Theta
, we define:

B_{t}^{\theta}=\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{s}dW_{s}

with quadratic variation correctly given by:

d\langle B^{\theta}\rangle_{t}^{ij}=(\theta_{t}\theta_{t}^{\top})^{ij}dt

2.

G-SDE solutions as parameterized families
:
For a general G-SDE:

dX_{t}=b(X_{t})dt+h_{ij}(X_{t})d\langle B\rangle_{t}^{ij}+\sigma_{j}(X_{t})dB_{t}^{j}

the solution is
intrinsically represented
by the parameterized family:

X_{t}^{\theta}=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(X_{s}^{\theta})ds+\int_{0}^{t}h_{ij}(X_{s}^{\theta})(\theta_{s}\theta_{s}^{\top})^{ij}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{j}(X_{s}^{\theta})\theta_{s}^{j}dW_{s}^{j}

3.

The homomorphism
:
There exists a injective correspondence:

\begin{array}[]{c}\text{G-expectation space}\\
\Updownarrow\\
\left\{X^{\theta}:\theta\in\Theta\right\}\end{array}

where the parameterized family
\{X^{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\Theta}

is
the G-SDE solution

This homomorphism reveals that:

•

G-processes are
intrinsically controlled trajectories
parameterized by volatility uncertainty

•

The G-expectation framework provides a
geometric encoding
of model uncertainty

•

Solutions to G-SDEs emerge as
universal families
of classical SDE solutions

2.3
Classical G-Expectation and G-Brownian Motion

2.3.1
G-Normal Distribution

The cornerstone of the theory is the
G-normal distribution
. Let
\xi
be a random variable. It is said to follow a G-normal distribution, denoted
\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,[\underline{\sigma}^{2},\overline{\sigma}^{2}])
, if for any
\phi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R})
, its sublinear expectation is defined by the following nonlinear heat equation:

\mathbb{E}[\phi(\xi)]=u(1,0),

(2.6)

where
u(t,x)
is the unique viscosity solution to the following nonlinear partial differential equation (PDE):

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=G\left(\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}\right),\quad(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R},

(2.7)

\displaystyle u(0,x)

\displaystyle=\phi(x),

(2.8)

with the nonlinear generator
G:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
given by:

G(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}\sup_{\sigma\in[\underline{\sigma},\overline{\sigma}]}(\sigma^{2}\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\overline{\sigma}^{2}\alpha^{+}-\underline{\sigma}^{2}\alpha^{-}\right).

(2.9)

Here,
\alpha^{+}=\max(\alpha,0)
and
\alpha^{-}=-\min(\alpha,0)
. This distribution is
normal
in the sense that it is centered and stable under scaling and addition of independent random variables, yet it is
not linear
due to the sublinearity of
G
.

A particularly revealing special case arises when the uncertainty set collapses to a single point, i.e.,
\underline{\sigma}=\overline{\sigma}=\sigma_{0}>0
. In this case, the generator becomes linear,
G(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{0}^{2}\alpha
, and the G-normal distribution reduces to the classical normal distribution
\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{0}^{2})
.

2.3.2
G-Brownian Motion

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\hat{E})
be a sublinear expectation space. A
d
-dimensional stochastic process
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is called a
G-Brownian motion
if the following properties hold:

(i)

B_{0}=0
.

(ii)

For any
t,s\geq 0
, the increment
B_{t+s}-B_{t}
is identically distributed to
B_{s}
, and
B_{t+s}-B_{t}
is independent of
(B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\dots,B_{t_{n}})
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq t_{1}\leq\dots\leq t_{n}\leq t
.

(iii)

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\hat{E}[|B_{t}|^{3}]t^{-1}=0
.

Moreover, if
\hat{E}[B_{t}]=\hat{E}[-B_{t}]=0
for all
t
, then
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is called a
symmetric G-Brownian motion
.

In the context of G-expectation, the increments of G-Brownian motion follow a G-normal distribution. Specifically, for any
t,s\geq 0
,
B_{t+s}-B_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,[\underline{\sigma}^{2}s,\overline{\sigma}^{2}s])
, where
\mathcal{N}(0,[\underline{\sigma}^{2},\overline{\sigma}^{2}])
denotes the G-normal distribution with uncertainty interval
[\underline{\sigma}^{2},\overline{\sigma}^{2}]
.

The quadratic variation process
\langle B\rangle_{t}
of G-Brownian motion is a continuous, non-decreasing process such that ,

\underline{\sigma}^{2}t\leq\langle B\rangle_{t}\leq\overline{\sigma}^{2}t.

This process captures the accumulated uncertainty over time.

The corresponding infinitesimal generator for a function
\phi
is given by the same nonlinear operator
G
:

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\phi(x+B_{t})]-\phi(x)}{t}=G(\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)).

(2.10)

2.4
Parameterized Control Representation

Now we draw upon Peng’s framework to construct the corresponding parameterized control representation.For details of the construction, see [1].

Definition 2.1

(Construction via Supremum over Linear Expectations)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}_{t},P)
be a filtered probability space sufficient to support a standard Brownian motion
\{W_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. For a given compact, convex parameter set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and set
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, we define two fundamental random objects:

Max-Distribution
: For a parameter
\mu\in\Lambda
, define the random variable
\eta(\mu)=\mu
. The associated nonlinear expectation
\mathbb{E}[\eta]
is defined for a test function
\phi
as:

\mathbb{E}[\phi(\eta)]:=\sup_{\mu\in\Lambda}E_{P}[\phi(\eta(\mu))].

(2.11)

This object captures the pure parameter uncertainty in its most extreme form.

G-Brownian Motion
:
Let
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be compact convex. Define:

B_{t}^{\theta}=\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{s}dW_{s},\quad\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}

(2.12)

with quadratic variation:

d\langle B^{\theta}\rangle_{t}=(\theta_{t}\theta_{t}^{\top})dt

(2.13)

The genuine G-Brownian motion is then characterized by:

{\mathbb{E}}[\phi(B_{t})]=\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}}E_{P}[\phi(B_{t}^{\theta})]

(2.14)

The space of admissible control processes is defined as:

\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}=\left\{\theta:[0,\infty)\times\Omega\to\Theta\ \middle|\ \theta\text{ is }\mathcal{F}_{t}\text{-adapted and }\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|\theta_{t}|^{2}dt\right]<\infty\ \forall T>0\right\}

where
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is a compact convex set representing the volatility uncertainty.

For the G-Brownian motion
B_{t}
, the conditional expectation is:

{\mathbb{E}}_{t}[\varphi(B_{T})]=\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}_{t,T}^{\Theta}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\varphi\left(\int_{t}^{T}\theta_{s}dW_{s}\right)\middle|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

This definition provides the mathematical foundation for conditional expectations in the G-framework, capturing the essence of model uncertainty through the supremum over volatility controls.

Remark 2.1
.

The control set
\Theta
in the definition of the G-expectation can be restricted to a countable dense subset
\Theta_{d}\subset\Theta
without changing the value of the supremum:

{\mathbb{E}}[\phi(B_{T}]=\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}}E_{P}\left[\varphi\left(\int_{0}^{T}\theta_{s}dW_{s}\right)\right]=\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\Theta_{d}}}E_{P}\left[\varphi\left(\int_{0}^{T}\theta_{s}dW_{s}\right)\right].

This follows from a fundamental principle in stochastic control theory: for a continuous value function defined on a compact control set, the supremum over the entire set equals the supremum over any dense subset. This justifies using countable approximations while maintaining the original theoretical framework.Throughout the subsequent analysis, the control set will be considered countable where needed.

Definition 2.2

(Conditional G-Expectation Operator)
.

For a G-Brownian motion
\{B_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
and a test function
\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the conditional G-expectation operator
\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{s}[\cdot]
is defined as:

{\mathbb{E}}_{s}[\varphi(B_{t})]=\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}_{s,t}^{\Theta}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\varphi\left(B_{s}+\int_{s}^{t}\theta_{u}dW_{u}\right)\middle|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]

where:

•

\mathcal{A}_{s,t}^{\Theta}
denotes admissible control processes on
[s,t]

•

\theta_{u}\in\Theta=[\underline{\sigma},\overline{\sigma}]
represents volatility controls

•

W_{u}
is a standard Brownian motion under
\mathbb{P}

Definition 2.3

(Conditional G-Expectation for Cylinder Functions)
.

For any cylinder function of the form:

\xi=\varphi(B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}}-B_{t_{1}},\cdots,B_{t_{n}}-B_{t_{n-1}}),\quad\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{d\times n})

with
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{n}\leq T
, the conditional G-expectation is defined as:

{\mathbb{E}}_{s}[\xi]=\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}_{s,T}^{\Theta}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\varphi\left(B_{t_{1}}^{\theta},B_{t_{2}}^{\theta}-B_{t_{1}}^{\theta},\cdots,B_{t_{n}}^{\theta}-B_{t_{n-1}}^{\theta}\right)\middle|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]

where
B_{t}^{\theta}=\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{u}dW_{u}
is the controlled process.

This definition coincides with the classical G-expectation definition when
s=0
:

{\mathbb{E}}[\xi]=\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}_{0,T}^{\Theta}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\varphi\left(B_{t_{1}}^{\theta},B_{t_{2}}^{\theta}-B_{t_{1}}^{\theta},\cdots,B_{t_{n}}^{\theta}-B_{t_{n-1}}^{\theta}\right)\right]

Theorem 2.4

(Conditional Expectation Simplification)
.

For any cylinder function
\xi=\varphi(B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}}-B_{t_{1}},\cdots,B_{t_{n}}-B_{t_{n-1}})
and
s\leq T
, the conditional G-expectation simplifies to:

{\mathbb{E}}_{s}[\xi]=\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}_{s,T}^{\Theta}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\varphi\left(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{k-1},B_{t_{k}}^{\theta}-B_{t_{k-1}}^{\theta},\cdots,B_{t_{n}}^{\theta}-B_{t_{n-1}}^{\theta}\right)\right]\bigg|_{x_{i}=B_{t_{i}}}

where for
t_{k-1}\leq s
,
t_{k}>s
, we treat
B_{t_{i}}
as constants
x_{i}
in the expectation.

Note that our definition of conditional expectation here is completely consistent with the classical definition of G-conditional expectation under the definition of cylindrical Brownian motion, so we can similarly extend it to its completion space.

Theorem 2.5

(Fundamental Properties)
.

The conditional G-expectation operator satisfies:

1.

Sublinearity
:
{\mathbb{E}}_{s}[X+Y]\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{s}[X]+{\mathbb{E}}_{s}[Y]

2.

Positive Homogeneity
:
{\mathbb{E}}_{s}[\lambda X]=\lambda{\mathbb{E}}_{s}[X]
for
\lambda\geq 0

3.

Preservation of Constants
:
{\mathbb{E}}_{s}[c]=c
for any constant
c

4.

Monotonicity
: If
X\leq Y
, then
{\mathbb{E}}_{s}[X]\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{s}[Y]

Theorem 2.6

(Tower Property)
.

For
s\leq t\leq u
, the conditional expectation satisfies:

{\mathbb{E}}_{s}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{t}[X]\right]={\mathbb{E}}_{s}[X]

This ensures the time consistency of the G-expectation framework.

3
The homomorphism

This chapter is devoted to the rigorous construction of the fundamental homomorphism
\Phi
between the space of random variables under the G-expectation and the space of parameterized families of classical random variables. The central goal is to demonstrate that this mapping not only exists but also preserves the essential algebraic structure of the spaces involved. The construction proceeds in a canonical manner: we first define
\Phi
explicitly on a dense subset of cylinder functions, establish its key properties there, and then extend it continuously to the entire space. The preservation of addition, scalar multiplication, and multiplication under this extension is then proven.

3.1
Notation and Preliminaries

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
be a complete probability space supporting a standard Brownian motion
\{W_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, and let
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}
be its natural filtration, augmented to satisfy the usual conditions. Let
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}
be a compact convex set representing the volatility uncertainty. The set of admissible controls is defined as:

\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}:=\left\{\theta:[0,\infty)\times\Omega\to\Theta\mid\theta\text{ is }\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}\text{-progressively measurable and }E_{P}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|\theta_{s}|^{2}ds\right]<\infty\ \forall T>0\right\}.

For any
\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}
, the controlled process is defined by:

B_{t}^{\theta}=\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{s}dW_{s}.

The G-expectation space
L_{G}^{p}(\Omega_{T})
is the completion of the space of cylinder functions under the norm
\|X\|_{L_{G}^{p}}=\hat{E}[|X|^{p}]^{1/p}
, where
\hat{E}
denotes the G-expectation operator.

A
cylinder function
is a random variable of the form:

\xi=\varphi\left(B_{t_{1}\wedge T},B_{t_{2}\wedge T},\cdots,B_{t_{n}\wedge T}\right),\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\in[0,\infty),\varphi\in C_{l.Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

where
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{n}\leq T
and
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a local Lipschitz continuous function. The collection of all such cylinder functions is denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{T}
.

Lemma 3.1

(Density of Cylinder Functions)
.

The set
\mathcal{C}_{T}
is dense in
L_{G}^{p}(\Omega_{T})
for any
p\geq 1
.

Proof.

This is a foundational result in the theory of G-expectation. See, for example, [1]
∎

3.2
Construction of the homomorphism
\Phi

We now construct the mapping
\Phi:L_{G}^{p}(\Omega_{T})\to{P}
, where
{P}
denotes the space of families of random variables
\{X^{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}}
indexed by the control, with appropriate integrability conditions.

Definition 3.2

(Definition on Cylinder Functions)
.

For any cylinder function
\xi=\varphi(B_{t_{1}\wedge T},\ldots,B_{t_{n}\wedge T})\in\mathcal{C}_{T}
, we define:

\Phi(\xi):=\left\{\xi^{\theta}\right\}_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}},\quad\text{where}\quad\xi^{\theta}:=\varphi(B_{t_{1}\wedge T}^{\theta},\ldots,B_{t_{n}\wedge T}^{\theta}).

A crucial property of this definition is the preservation of the G-norm, which follows from the definition of G-expectation as a supremum over controls.

Lemma 3.3

(Isometry on Cylinder Functions)
.

For any cylinder function
\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{T}
, we have:

\hat{E}[|\xi|^{p}]=\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}}E_{P}[|\xi^{\theta}|^{p}].

Consequently,
\|\xi\|_{L_{G}^{p}}=\sup_{\theta}\|\xi^{\theta}\|_{L^{p}(P)}
.

Proof.

This follows directly from the representation of the G-expectation for cylinder functions. See[1].
∎

This isometry property allows us to extend
\Phi
to the entire space
L_{G}^{p}(\Omega_{T})
by continuity.

Definition 3.4

(Extension by Continuity)
.

For any
X\in L_{G}^{p}(\Omega_{T})
, let
\{\xi_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{C}_{T}
be a sequence such that
\|X-\xi_{n}\|_{L_{G}^{p}}\to 0
. We define:

\Phi(X):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Phi(\xi_{n}),

where the limit is taken in the sense that:

\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}}\|X^{\theta}-\xi_{n}^{\theta}\|_{L^{p}(P)}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

3.3
Preservation of Algebraic Operations

We now prove that the homomorphism
\Phi
preserves the algebraic operations of addition, scalar multiplication, and multiplication.

Theorem 3.5

(Preservation of Addition)
.

For any
X,Y\in L_{G}^{p}(\Omega_{T})
, we have:

\Phi(X+Y)=\Phi(X)+\Phi(Y).

Proof.

Let
\{\xi_{n}\},\{\eta_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{C}_{T}
be sequences approximating
X
and
Y
, respectively. Then
\{\xi_{n}+\eta_{n}\}
is a sequence of cylinder functions approximating
X+Y
.

By the definition on cylinder functions, for each
n
:

\Phi(\xi_{n}+\eta_{n})=\{(\xi_{n}+\eta_{n})^{\theta}\}=\{\xi_{n}^{\theta}+\eta_{n}^{\theta}\}=\Phi(\xi_{n})+\Phi(\eta_{n}).

Taking the limit as
n\to\infty
on both sides, and using the continuity of
\Phi
(which follows from its isometry property), we obtain:

\Phi(X+Y)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Phi(\xi_{n}+\eta_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}[\Phi(\xi_{n})+\Phi(\eta_{n})]=\Phi(X)+\Phi(Y).

Note that the last equality holds because the limit of a sum is the sum of the limits in the topology of uniform convergence in
L^{p}(P)
over
\theta
.
∎

Theorem 3.6

(Preservation of Scalar Multiplication)
.

For any
X\in L_{G}^{p}(\Omega_{T})
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we have:

\Phi(\lambda X)=\lambda\Phi(X).

Proof.

The proof is analogous. Let
\{\xi_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{C}_{T}
approximate
X
. Then
\{\lambda\xi_{n}\}
approximates
\lambda X
. For each
n
:

\Phi(\lambda\xi_{n})=\{(\lambda\xi_{n})^{\theta}\}=\{\lambda\xi_{n}^{\theta}\}=\lambda\Phi(\xi_{n}).

Taking limits yields the result.
∎

Theorem 3.7

(Preservation of Multiplication)
.

For any
X,Y\in L_{G}^{p}(\Omega_{T})
, we have:

\Phi(X\cdot Y)=\Phi(X)\cdot\Phi(Y).

Proof.

Let
\{\xi_{n}\},\{\eta_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{C}_{T}
approximate
X
and
Y
, respectively. Note that the product
\xi_{n}\eta_{n}
is also a cylinder function (by considering a function that multiplies the corresponding components).

By the definition on cylinder functions:

\Phi(\xi_{n}\eta_{n})=\{(\xi_{n}\eta_{n})^{\theta}\}=\{\xi_{n}^{\theta}\eta_{n}^{\theta}\}=\Phi(\xi_{n})\cdot\Phi(\eta_{n}).

We now claim that
\{\xi_{n}\eta_{n}\}
approximates
X\cdot Y
in
L_{G}^{p/2}
(if
X,Y\in L_{G}^{p}
, then
X\cdot Y\in L_{G}^{p/2}
by the Hölder inequality under G-expectation). To see this, note:

|\xi_{n}\eta_{n}-XY|\leq|\xi_{n}||\eta_{n}-Y|+|Y||\xi_{n}-X|.

.
we obtain:

∎

Note that the last equality holds because the limit of a sum is the sum of the limits in the topology of uniform convergence in

over
\theta

The following definition makes precise the notion that a sequence of parameterized random variables converges in probability, and that the rate of convergence is

(Uniform Convergence in

\Theta

The power of the

(

5.1

The classical theory of stochastic processes is built upon an abstract probability space

-expectation framework, and our parameterized representation in particular, compellingly argues for a shift in this perspective. The canonical sample space for a
G
-Brownian motion is the
path space
itself:

\Omega^{*}:=C_{0}([0,T])=\{\omega:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}\mid\omega(0)=0,\ \omega\text{ is continuous}\}.

A sample point
\tilde{\omega}\in\Omega^{*}
is now a
full continuous path
. The Brownian motion is simply the canonical process defined by the evaluation maps:

B_{t}(\tilde{\omega}):=\tilde{\omega}(t),\quad\forall\tilde{\omega}\in\Omega^{*}.

This shift from an abstract
\omega
to a concrete path
\tilde{\omega}
is not merely philosophical; it is the key to a geometric understanding of model uncertainty.

Under this lens, the control set
\Theta
and the family of controls
\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}
take on a new meaning. Each control process
\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}
acts as a
path generator
. Through the integral construction

B_{t}^{\theta}=\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{s}dW_{s},

each control
\theta
generates a specific, continuous path
B_{.}^{\theta}\in\Omega^{*}
. Here, we consider the continuous modification of the path generator.

5.2
The homomorphism in the Path-Space View

The homomorphism and the framework established in the preceding sections can now be restated in more intrinsic terms. At its core, it constitutes an homomorphism between two fundamental spaces:

1.

The space of
G
-random variables,
L_{G}^{p}(\Omega_{T})
, whose elements are equivalence classes of path functionals under the quasi-sure equality relation.

2.

A space of parameterized families
\{X^{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}}
constructed on a fixed probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
.

The bridge connecting these two mathematical worlds is built upon a fundamental result from classical stochastic analysis: the existence of a
canonical path-continuous representative
for any solution of a stochastic differential equation (SDE).

For each process
B^{\theta}
and
X^{\theta}
(which are formally
\mathbb{P}
-equivalence classes), we consider their unique continuous modifications, denoted
\widetilde{B}^{\theta}
and
\widetilde{X}^{\theta}
respectively. These modifications are now well-defined functions from the sample space
\Omega
to the path space
C_{0}([0,T])
, mapping each outcome
\omega\in\Omega
to a concrete, continuous path.

Remark 5.1
.

The foundational idea, as clearly indicated in the accompanying figure, is that the reference probability measure
\mathbb{P}
induces a functional relationship that allows for the pathwise construction of stochastic objects. Specifically, for a process like
B_{t}^{\theta}=\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{s}dW_{s}
, we do not treat it merely as an equivalence class in
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
. Instead, we consider its
continuous modification

\tilde{B}^{\theta}
, which is a well-defined, continuous path for
every

\omega\in\Omega
. This process of continuous modification transforms the abstract equivalence class into a concrete, pointwise-defined random variable taking values in the path space
C_{0}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

Consequently,
\tilde{B}^{\theta}(\omega)
is no longer just a ’random outcome’; it is a bona fide
sample path
that can be directly fed into other functionals and operators. This is the crucial step that bridges the abstract theory with concrete computation: the sample path
\tilde{B}^{\theta}(\omega)
becomes a valid input, a deterministic element of
C_{0}([0,T])
, which can be ’plugged in’ or ’substituted into’ subsequent calculations. This methodology, moving from abstract equivalence classes to concrete pathwise objects, forms the rigorous backbone of our parameterized framework.

Remark 5.2

(Null-Set Issues and the Deterministic Nature of Solutions)
.

A fundamental challenge in the classical probabilistic framework arises when switching between probability measures (or “changing paths”): null sets associated with one measure may become significant under another, complicating the analysis of path-wise properties. However, as developed in this chapter through the rough path perspective, we have established that for each control parameter
\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}
, the essential geometric data—the path
B^{\theta}_{[0,T]}
and its quadratic variation
\langle B^{\theta}\rangle_{[0,T]}
—are well-defined and deterministic once generated.

Crucially, once the quadratic variation is constructed for a given path, the solution to the G-SDE becomes a deterministic functional of this data, independent of the underlying probability measure. This effectively eliminates the interference of null sets, as the analysis now resides entirely in the deterministic realm of path spaces. The probabilistic aspects are confined to the initial generation of the paths and their quadratic variations, after which the evolution proceeds deterministically.

This key insight—that the solution process is fully determined by the path and its quadratic variation—will be further elaborated and applied in the following sections, where we explore the implications for the stability and regularity of solutions under parameter variations.

6
Discussion and Future Directions: A Rough Path Perspective on the Path-Space Formulation

The core insight of Chapter 6 is the reformulation of the G-expectation framework on the path space
C_{0}([0,T])
, moving from an abstract probability space to a concrete geometric setting where sample points are paths. This supplement aims to refine and elevate this perspective by employing the language and theory of
Rough Paths
. This framework provides the most natural and profound mathematical foundation for the path-space view, rigorously explaining why, “once the quadratic variation is constructed, everything else follows naturally,” effectively sidestepping the measure-theoretic complexities of null sets.

6.1
From Parameterized Control to Rough Paths

Sections 6.1 and 6.2 argue for the path space
\Omega^{*}=C_{0}([0,T])
as the canonical sample space for G-Brownian motion. The parameterized representation theorem establishes that a G-random variable
X\in L_{G}^{p}(\Omega_{T})
is isomorphic to a family
\{X^{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}}
of classical random variables.

A deeper question arises: what is the fundamental
geometric object
that drives the entire G-framework? The Rough Paths theory provides a definitive answer: the
signature
of the driving path – namely, the path itself and its
quadratic variation
(or more generally, its
Lévy area
) – is the essential core object that encapsulates all necessary information.

For a G-Brownian motion
B_{t}
, its
rough path lift
is a pair
\mathbf{B}=(B,\mathbb{B})
, where:

•

The
path component
is
B:t\mapsto B_{t}
.

•

The
area component
is
\mathbb{B}_{s,t}=\int_{s}^{t}(B_{u}-B_{s})\otimes dB_{u}
, encoding the path’s volatility.

For continuous semimartingales, the Lévy area is intrinsically linked to the quadratic variation process
\langle B\rangle_{t}
via the identity:

\mathbb{B}_{s,t}+\mathbb{B}_{s,t}^{\top}=(B_{t}-B_{s})\otimes(B_{t}-B_{s})-\langle B\rangle_{s,t}.

(6.1)

In one dimension, this relation simplifies, confirming that
\langle B\rangle
carries the crucial volatility information. Therefore, under the G-expectation framework (where uncertainty stems from volatility), the
essential information
of the driving process is completely captured by the path
B_{[0,t]}
and its quadratic variation path
\langle B\rangle_{[0,t]}
.

6.2
Rough Path representation and the Resolution of Null-Set Issues

We can now construct a more fundamental
Rough Path homomorphism
that refines the parameterized representation theorem.

Definition 6.1

(Rough Path homomorphism)
.

There exists an homomorphism
\Psi
, mapping a G-random variable
X
to a
deterministic functional

F_{X}
on the space of rough paths, such that:

\Psi:L_{G}^{p}(\Omega_{T})\to\mathcal{F}(RP([0,T]),\mathbb{R}),\quad X\mapsto\Psi(X)=F_{X}

(6.2)

where
RP([0,T])
denotes the space of rough paths over
[0,T]
, and

X=F_{X}(\mathbf{B}_{[0,T]})=F_{X}(B_{[0,T]},\langle B\rangle_{[0,T]}).

(6.3)

Profound Implications of this representation

1.

Determinization:
It transforms a random object
X
into a deterministic functional
F_{X}
. The realization of
X
in a particular “world” (or under a specific probability measure
P^{\theta}
) is obtained simply by evaluating
F_{X}
on the corresponding path
(B^{\theta},\langle B^{\theta}\rangle)
:
X^{\theta}(\omega)=F_{X}(B^{\theta}_{[0,T]}(\omega),\langle B^{\theta}\rangle_{[0,T]}(\omega))
.

2.

Natural Resolution of Null-Set Issues:
In classical probability, phrases like “almost surely” necessitate careful handling of null sets. This complexity is magnified when considering a family of measures
\{P^{\theta}\}
, as a
P^{\theta_{1}}
-null set might be significant under
P^{\theta_{2}}
.

The rough path perspective
inherently bypasses this problem
. We no longer reason about “
P^{\theta}
-almost every
\omega
” but rather about “
for all paths

(B^{\theta},\langle B^{\theta}\rangle)
”. Since the solution
X
is defined as a functional of the path, its properties (continuity, boundedness) are determined by the functional
F_{X}
and the input path, independent of any specific probability measure. The role of probability measures is reduced to a
generating mechanism
for these path pairs. Once the path is generated, all subsequent analysis is deterministic.

3.

The Natural Flow of Solutions:
As you keenly observed, the solution process becomes natural and straightforward. Consider a G-SDE:

dX_{t}=b(X_{t})dt+\sigma(X_{t})dB_{t}.

(6.4)

Solving it within the rough path framework means finding a path functional
F_{X}
such that for any given driving rough path
\mathbf{B}=(B,\langle B\rangle)
, the process
X_{t}=F_{X}(\mathbf{B}_{[0,t]})
satisfies the equation. The solution is uniquely determined by
\mathbf{B}
, devoid of any probabilistic “almost sure” qualifications or null set concerns.

6.3
Conclusion: Unification Through the Rough Path View

In conclusion, the core idea of Chapter 6 – founding the G-expectation framework on the path space – finds its most natural and complete expression within Rough Path Theory.

•

The essence of a
G-Brownian motion
is its
lifted rough path

\mathbf{B}=(B,\langle B\rangle)
.

•

The essence of a
G-random variable
is a
deterministic functional
on the space of these rough paths.

•

The solution to a
G-SDE
is a
new path functional
constructed via rough integration theory.

The role of probability theory is simplified to: using parameterized controls
\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}
to
generate
all possible rough paths
\{(B^{\theta},\langle B^{\theta}\rangle)\}
. Once this family of rough paths is generated, the probabilistic machinery has fulfilled its primary role. Subsequent operations, such as calculating the G-expectation
\hat{E}[X]=\sup_{\theta}E_{P}[X^{\theta}]
, transform into problems of finding extrema over a
determined family of paths
.

Therefore, Rough Path Theory not only provides a rigorous mathematical foundation for Chapter 6 but also dramatically simplifies its conceptual formulation. It shifts the analytical focus from the cumbersome intricacies of families of probability measures and null sets to the more geometrically intuitive study of
path spaces and deterministic functionals
. This perfectly validates the insightful thesis: “
we only need to generate the quadratic variation, and then everything follows naturally!
”

Remark 6.1
.

A profound implication of the path-space formulation is the decoupling of probabilistic generation from deterministic evolution. The family of probability measures
\{P^{\theta}\}
serves the sole purpose of generating the paths and, crucially, their quadratic variation
\langle B\rangle_{[0,T]}
in a quasi-sure sense. Once this essential geometric data—the path and its volatility—is constructed, the role of the probability measures is complete. Subsequent analysis, including solving G-SDEs, can proceed in a pathwise, deterministic manner by treating
(B,\langle B\rangle)
as a deterministic input. This effectively resolves the analytical challenges associated with the uncountable family of singular measures, shifting the focus to the study of deterministic functionals on the space of paths.

7
Conclusion and Future Work

This work has laid the foundation for a new, constructive framework that bridges the theory of
G
-expectation and Rough Paths through a parameterized control representation. We have demonstrated that the fundamental objects of the
G
-framework—
G
-Brownian motion,
G
-martingales, and solutions to
G
-SDEs—can be naturally generated by families of classical stochastic processes, each corresponding to a specific volatility scenario
\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}
.

Our central contribution is the construction of a canonical mapping
\Phi
that translates the algebraic and analytic operations of the
G
-expectation space into operations on these parameterized families. This provides a powerful computational methodology: problems under model uncertainty can be approached by solving a family of controlled classical SDEs and then aggregating the results.

The primary insight that emerges from this work is that the deepest challenge in this program is an
analytic and geometric problem of ”gluing” or ”fusion”
. The fundamental open question is: Under what precise conditions does a family of solutions
\{X^{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}}
, each defined pathwise under its own measure
P^{\theta}
,
cohere
to form a single, well-defined stochastic process
X
under the
G
-expectation? This
gluing problem
is the key to completing the homomorphism and lies at the heart of understanding pathwise properties under model uncertainty.

The challenge of gluing is intrinsically linked to the quasi-sure analysis of path spaces. It requires identifying the precise consistency conditions that allow a property holding
P^{\theta}
-almost surely for every
\theta
to be lifted to a property that holds
quasi-surely
—that is, on a path set that is simultaneously non-null for all measures in the family.

Looking forward, the most pressing research directions emanating from this work are:

1.

Establishing a Complete Gluing Theory.
: The foremost task is to establish a complete theory of gluing for solutions of parameterized SDEs. This will involve deriving necessary and sufficient analytic conditions on the family
\{X^{\theta}\}
that guarantee the existence of a unique
G
-random variable
X
such that
\Phi(X)=\{X^{\theta}\}
.

2.

Developing the Geometry of Quasi-Sure Path Space.
: A rigorous path-space formulation demands a thorough understanding of the quasi-sure structure of the
G
-expectation. Future work must focus on characterizing the path properties that are quasi-sure, thereby enabling a deterministic, pathwise analysis after the initial generation of the quadratic variation.

3.

Bridging Theory to Practice.
: The control representation suggests practical computational algorithms. By sampling over the control set
\Theta
and solving the resulting classical SDEs, one can approximate the
G
-expectation. A key challenge is to develop efficient methods to ”glue” these sampled paths to approximate the true
G
-solution.

In conclusion, this paper has reframed the problem of model uncertainty in terms of the gluing of parameterized paths. By identifying and isolating this core challenge, we provide a clear and promising pathway for future research. We are optimistic that a synthesis of techniques from rough paths, quasi-sure analysis, and stochastic control will lead to a complete geometric theory of
G
-expectation and its path-space properties.

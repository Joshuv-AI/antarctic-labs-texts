Probabilistic degenerate logarithm and heterogeneous Stirling numbers
Dae San Kim, Taekyun Kim

Abstract
. Let Y Y be a random variable whose moment-generating function exists in some neighborhood of the origin. While probabilistic Stirling numbers of the first and second kind have been introduced, early definitions often failed to satisfy fundamental orthogonality and inverse relations or lacked consistency with classical forms in the case when Y = 1 Y=1 . This paper addresses these limitations by utilizing redefined probabilistic Stirling numbers, S 1 Y ​ ( n , k ) S_{1}^{Y}(n,k) and S 2 Y ​ ( n , k ) S_{2}^{Y}(n,k) , alongside their degenerate counterparts. Our primary objective is twofold: first, to introduce the probabilistic (degenerate) logarithm associated with Y Y , providing explicit expressions for various random variables and defining new probabilistic degenerate Daehee and Cauchy numbers; and second, to investigate probabilistic heterogeneous Stirling numbers and establish a probabilistic degenerate version of the Schlömilch formula, demonstrating that these new frameworks maintain the essential algebraic properties of their classical counterparts.

1.
Preliminaries

The falling and the rising factorial sequences are respectively given by

\displaystyle(x)_{0}=1,\quad(x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1),\quad(n\geq 1),

\displaystyle\langle x\rangle_{0}=1,\quad\langle x\rangle_{n}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1),\quad(n\geq 1).

Let
\lambda
be any nonzero real number. Then
the degenerate falling and rising factorial sequences are defined by

\displaystyle(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\quad(x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)\cdots(x-(n-1)\lambda),\quad(n\geq 1),

\displaystyle\langle x\rangle_{0,\lambda}=1,\quad\langle x\rangle_{n,\lambda}=x(x+\lambda)\cdots(x+(n-1)\lambda),\quad(n\geq 1).

The degenerate exponentials are introduced as

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=(1+\lambda t)^{\frac{x}{\lambda}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t)=(1+\lambda t)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(1)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

The degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind are equivalently defined as (see [12])

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0),

\displaystyle(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),

\displaystyle S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}(j)_{n,\lambda},\quad(n\geq k).

The degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind are variously specified as (see [11])

(2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0),

\displaystyle(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0),

where
\log_{\lambda}t=\frac{1}{\lambda}(t^{\lambda}-1)
is called the degenerate logarithm and
\log_{\lambda}(1+t)
is the compositional inverse of
e_{\lambda}(t)-1
.

Taking the limits
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, we find that

\displaystyle(x)_{n,\lambda}\rightarrow x^{n},\quad\langle x\rangle_{n,\lambda}\rightarrow x^{n},\quad e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)\rightarrow e^{xt},\quad e_{\lambda}(t)\rightarrow e^{t},

\displaystyle\log_{\lambda}t\rightarrow\log t,\quad S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\rightarrow S_{1}(n,k),\quad S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\rightarrow S_{2}(n,k),

where
S_{1}(n,k)
and
S_{2}(n,k)
are respectively the Stirling numbers of the first kind and those of the second kind characterized by

(3)

\frac{1}{k!}(e^{t}-1)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log(1+t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

Furthermore, taking the limits
\lambda\rightarrow 1
yields

(x)_{n,\lambda}\rightarrow(x)_{n},\quad\langle x\rangle_{n,\lambda}\rightarrow\langle x\rangle_{n}.

Recall that a power series
f(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}
is a delta series if
a_{0}=0
and
a_{1}\neq 0
.
We recall three of the Lagrange inversion formula and its variants: for any formal power series
g(t)
and any delta series
f(t)
, they are stated as (see [6,24]):

\displaystyle(A)\,\,\,[t^{n}]\,g(\bar{f}(t))=\frac{1}{n}[t^{n-1}]\,g^{\prime}(t)\Big(\frac{t}{f(t)}\Big)^{n},

\displaystyle(B)\,\,\,[t^{n}]\,\big(\bar{f}(t)\big)^{k}=\frac{k}{n}[t^{n-k}]\,\Big(\frac{t}{f(t)}\Big)^{n},

\displaystyle(C)\,\,\,[t^{n}]\,\bar{f}(t)=\frac{1}{n}[t^{n-1}]\,\Big(\frac{t}{f(t)}\Big)^{n},

where
\bar{f}(t)
is the compositional inverse of
f(t)
,
n
is any nonnegative integer and
k
is any positive integer.

Applying the formula (C) to
f(t)=e_{\lambda}(t)-1
, we arrive at

(4)

[t^{n}]\,\log_{\lambda}(1+t)=\frac{1}{n}[t^{n-1}]\,\Big(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\Big)^{n}=\frac{1}{n!}\beta_{n-1,\lambda}^{(n)},

where the degenerate Bernoulli polynomials and numbers of order
\gamma
are respectively given by

(5)

\Big(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\Big)^{\gamma}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(\gamma)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(\gamma)}=\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(\gamma)}(0),

for any complex number
\gamma
.
We see from (
4
) that

(6)

\log_{\lambda}(1+t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n-1,\lambda}^{(n)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

In addition, applying the formula (B) to
f(t)=e_{\lambda}(t)-1
gives

(7)

[t^{n}]\,\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k}=\frac{k}{n}\beta_{n-k,\lambda}^{(n)}\frac{1}{(n-k)!}.

Thus it follows from (
7
) that

(8)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\binom{n-1}{k-1}\beta_{n-k,\lambda}^{(n)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

Now, one observes from (
2
) and (
8
) that

(9)

S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\beta_{n-k,\lambda}^{(n)},\quad(n\geq k).

Taking the limits
\lambda\rightarrow 0
in (
6
) and (
9
) yields

B_{n-1}^{(n)}=(-1)^{n-1}(n-1)!,\quad S_{1}(n,k)=\binom{n-1}{k-1}B_{n-k}^{(n)},

where the Bernoulli polynomials and numbers of order
\gamma
are respectively defined by

(10)

\Big(\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}\Big)^{\gamma}e^{xt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(\gamma)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad B_{n}^{(\gamma)}=B_{n}^{(\gamma)}(0).

The Lah numbers are equivalently specified as

(11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\frac{t}{1-t}\Big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}L(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0),\quad L(n,k)=\frac{n!}{k!}\binom{n-1}{k-1},\quad(n\geq k),

\displaystyle\langle x\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}L(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

The Lah-Bell polynomials are variously characterized by

(12)

\displaystyle LB_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}L(n,k)x^{k},\quad e^{x\big(\frac{1}{1-t}-1\big)}=e^{x\frac{t}{1-t}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}LB_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

\displaystyle LB_{n}(x)=e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\langle k\rangle_{n}}{k!}x^{k}.

The heterogeneous Stirling numbers of the second kind are equivalently introduced as (see [17])

(13)

\displaystyle\langle x\rangle_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{-\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},,\quad(k\geq 0),

\displaystyle H_{\lambda}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}\langle j\rangle_{n,\lambda},\quad(n\geq k),

which show

H_{\lambda}(n,k)=S_{2,-\lambda}(n,k)=(-1)^{n}S_{2,\lambda}^{-1}(n,k),\quad(n\geq k),

with
\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}^{x}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}
.

Here we see from (
3
), (
11
) and (
13
) that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}H_{\lambda}(n,k)=S_{2}(n,k),\quad\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 1}H_{\lambda}(n,k)=L(n,k).

The heterogeneous Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined, by inverting the relations in (
13
), as (see [17])

(14)

\displaystyle(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}G_{\lambda}(n,k)\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\Big(-\log_{\lambda}\Big(\frac{1}{1+t}\Big)\Big)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\log_{-\lambda}\big(1+t\big)\Big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}G_{\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

From (
14
) and the formula (B), we find that

G_{\lambda}(n,k)=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\beta_{n-k,-\lambda}^{(n)},\quad(n\geq k).

Applying the formula (C) to
f(t)=\log_{-\lambda}(1+t)
gives

[t^{n}]\,\big(e_{-\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\frac{k}{n}[t^{n-k}]\,\Big(\frac{t}{\log_{-\lambda}(1+t)}\Big)^{n}=\frac{k}{n}C_{n-k,-\lambda}^{(n)}\frac{1}{(n-k)!},

which shows

(15)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{-\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\binom{n-1}{k-1}C_{n-k,-\lambda}^{(n)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

Thus, from (
1
), (
2
), (
13
) and (
15
), we arrive at

H_{\lambda}(n,k)=\binom{n-1}{k-1}C_{n-k,-\lambda}^{(n)},\quad S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)=\binom{n-1}{k-1}C_{n-k,\lambda}^{(n)},\quad(n\geq k).

Here the degenerate Cauchy numbers of order
\gamma
are given by

(16)

\Big(\frac{t}{\log_{\lambda}(1+t)}\Big)^{\gamma}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{n,\lambda}^{(\gamma)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Heterogeneous Bell polynomials and numbers are respectively defined by

(17)

H_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{\lambda}(n,k)x^{k},\quad H_{n,\lambda}=H_{n,\lambda}(1).

This entails that

(18)

\displaystyle e^{x\big(e_{-\lambda}(t)-1\big)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

\displaystyle H_{n,\lambda}(x)=e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\langle k\rangle_{n,\lambda}}{k!}x^{k}.

Moreover, one shows that

\displaystyle H_{\lambda}(n,k)=\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{n-l}S_{2}(l,k)S_{1}(n,l)\lambda^{n-l},

\displaystyle L(n,k)=\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{n-l}S_{1,\lambda}(n,l)H_{\lambda}(l,k),\quad(n\geq k).

The partial Bell polynomials are characterized by

B_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n-k+1})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+j_{2}+\cdots=k\\
j_{1}+2j_{2}+\dots=n\end{subarray}}\frac{n!}{j_{1}!j_{2}!\cdots}\Big(\frac{x_{1}}{1!}\Big)^{j_{1}}\Big(\frac{x_{2}}{2!}\Big)^{j_{2}}\cdots,

where
j_{1},j_{2},\dots
are nonnegative integers. The generating function of the partial Bell polynomials is specified as

(19)

\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}x_{m}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n-k+1})\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

General references for this paper include [6,23,24,25].

2.
Introduction

Throughout this paper, we assume that
Y
is a random variable such that the moment-generating function of
Y

(20)

E[e^{tY}]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E[Y^{n}]\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(|t|<r)

exists for some
r>0
, where
E
is the mathematical expectation. We assume further that
E[Y]\neq 0
. Let
(Y_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) copies of
Y
, and let
S_{k}
denote their partial sums:

(21)

S_{k}=Y_{1}+\cdots+Y_{k},\quad(k\geq 1),\quad S_{0}=0.

The probabilisitc degenerate Bernoulli polynomials and numbers of order
\gamma
associated with
Y
are respectively given by

(22)

\bigg(\frac{t}{E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1}\bigg)^{\gamma}\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]\big)^{x}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(\gamma,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(\gamma,Y)}=\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(\gamma,Y)}(0).

The probabilistic degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
are equivalently defined as

(23)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0),

\displaystyle S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E[(S_{j})_{n,\lambda}],\quad(n\geq k),

\displaystyle S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=B_{n,k}\big(E[(Y)_{1,\lambda}],E[(Y)_{2,\lambda}],\dots,E[(Y)_{n-k+1,\lambda}]\big),\quad(n\geq k).

Let
e_{Y,\lambda}(t)=E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1
. Then the probabilistic degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with
Y
are introduced as

(24)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0),

where
\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)
is the compositional inverse of
e_{Y,\lambda}(t)
.
Among other things, from (
23
) and (
24
), we note that
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
and
S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
satisfy the orthogonality and inverse relations.

Proposition 2.1
.

The following orthogonality and inverse relations are valid for
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
and
S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
.

\displaystyle(a)\,\,\,\sum_{k=l}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(k,l)=\delta_{n,l},\quad\sum_{k=l}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(k,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle(b)\,\,a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)b_{k}\,\,\iff\,\,b_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle(c)\,\,a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(k,n)b_{k}\,\,\iff\,\,b_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(k,n)a_{k}.

By using the formulas (B) and (C), (
22
) and (
24
), we show that

(25)

\displaystyle\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n-1,\lambda}^{(n,Y)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\binom{n-1}{k-1}\beta_{n-k,\lambda}^{(n,Y)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

Taking the limits
\lambda\rightarrow 0
in (
25
) and for
e_{Y}(t)=E[e^{Yt}]-1
, we see that

(26)

\displaystyle\bar{e}_{Y}(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{n-1}^{(n,Y)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(\bar{e}_{Y}(t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\binom{n-1}{k-1}B_{n-k}^{(n,Y)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

Here the probabilisitc Bernoulli polynomials and numbers of order
\gamma
associated with
Y
are respectively characterized by

(27)

\bigg(\frac{t}{E[e^{Yt}]-1}\bigg)^{\gamma}\big(E[e^{Yt}]\big)^{x}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(\gamma,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad B_{n}^{(\gamma,Y)}=B_{n}^{(\gamma,Y)}(0).

The following theorem follows from (
19
) and (
25
).

Theorem 2.2
.

For any integers
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\beta_{n-k,\lambda}^{(n,Y)}=B_{n,k}\Big(\beta_{0,\lambda}^{(1,Y)},\beta_{1,\lambda}^{(2,Y)},\dots\beta_{n-k,\lambda}^{(n-k+1,Y)}\Big).

In recent years, various degenerate versions of many special numbers and polynomials have been studied, yielding significant identities and combinatorial properties (see [9,11,12,17,19] and the references therein).
For example, we found the degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind and the second kind which turned out to be very important in studying degenerate versions of special polynomials and numbers. This exploration of degenerate versions originated with Carlitz’s work on degenerate Bernoulli and Euler polynomials (see [5]). Notably, this area of study has expanded beyond polynomials and numbers to encompass transcendental functions (see [13,14]) and umbral calculus (see [8]).

Concurrently, probabilistic extensions of special polynomials and numbers have emerged as a vibrant intersection of combinatorics, probability theory, and mathematical physics (see [4,7,15,16,18,20,21,26]). Driven by the need to model complex stochastic systems, mathematicians have increasingly focused on “probabilistic” versions of classical sequences, such as the Bernoulli, Euler, Bell, and Stirling polynomials, by associating them with specific random variables
Y
through their moment-generating functions.

Recently, Adell and Lekuona introduced the probabilistic Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with a random variable
Y
, denoted by
S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)
(see [1,3,20]). Subsequently, Adell and Bényi defined the probabilistic Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with
Y
,
s_{Y}(n,k)
, by employing the cumulant generating function (see [2]). However, these versions of
S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)
and
s_{Y}(n,k)
fail to satisfy the standard orthogonality, limiting their utility in inversion formulas. Furthermore,
s_{Y}(n,k)
does not reduce to the classical Stirling numbers of the first kind,
S_{1}(n,k)
, in the case where
Y=1
. To address these shortcomings, a redefined version of the probabilistic Stirling numbers of the first kind, denoted
S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)
, was introduced in [7,26]. These redefined numbers, together with
S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)
, satisfy the expected orthogonality and inverse relations, and
S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)
correctly reduces to
S_{1}(n,k)
when
Y=1
. Similarly, their degenerate counterparts–the probabilistic degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind
S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
and of the first kind
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
–were considered in [7]. These also satisfy orthogonality and inverse relations (see Proposition 1.1), with
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
reducing to the degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind
S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)
when
Y=1
. We note that an alternative definition for
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
was proposed in [16] using the degenerate cumulant generating function; however, that version does not satisfy the orthogonality and inverse relations when paired with
S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
.

The aim of this paper is twofold. First, we define the probabilistic logarithm associated with
Y
,
\log^{Y}(1+t)
, and the probabilistic degenerate logarithm associated with
Y
,
\log_{\lambda}^{Y}(1+t)
. These are defined such that for any integer
k\geq 0
, the following generating functions hold:

\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\log_{\lambda}^{Y}(1+t)\Big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\,\,\,\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\log^{Y}(1+t)\Big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\,\,(k\geq 0).

Specifically, for
k=1
, our definitions for the probabilistic logarithms (see (
42
)) are:

\log_{\lambda}^{Y}(1+t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,1)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad\log^{Y}(1+t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}S_{1}^{Y}(n,1)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

In Section 5, we provide explicit expressions for these logarithms for various discrete and continuous random variables. Furthermore, using our definition of the probabilistic degenerate logarithm, we introduce the probabilistic degenerate Daehee numbers of order
\gamma
associated with
Y
(see (
43
), (
44
)),
D_{n,\lambda}^{(\gamma,Y)}
, and the probabilistic degenerate Cauchy numbers of order
\gamma
associated with
Y
(see (
45
), (
46
)),
C_{n,\lambda}^{(\gamma,Y)}
. For the case
\gamma=1
, the probabilistic degenerate Daehee numbers associated with
Y
are defined by:

\frac{\log_{\lambda}^{Y}(1+t)}{t}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{n,\lambda}^{Y}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

We note that previous definitions for probabilistic degenerate Daehee polynomials
D_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)
and
d_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)
(see [22,27]) exist; however, they possess a significant limitation: for
x=0
, both
D_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(0)
and
d_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(0)
become independent of the random variable
Y
, thereby losing their probabilistic character.

Second, we investigate the probabilistic heterogeneous Stirling numbers of the second kind,
H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
, and of the first kind,
G_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
. These satisfy orthogonality and inverse relations, and reduce to
H_{\lambda}(n,k)
and
G_{\lambda}(n,k)
when
Y=1
. While a probabilistic version of the Schlömilch formula–expressing
S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)
in terms of
S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)
–was shown in [26], we demonstrate that a probabilistic degenerate version also holds. Specifically, we show that the Schlömilch identity remains valid when
S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)
and
S_{2}^{Y}(n-k+j,j)
are replaced by
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
and
S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n-k+j,j)
, or by
G_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
and
H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n-k+j,j)
. This result further yields an expression for
\log_{\lambda}^{Y}(1+t)
in terms of the probabilistic degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind. While probabilistic heterogeneous Stirling numbers of the second kind are discussed in [18], those of the first kind have not yet been addressed.

3.
Probabilistic heterogeneous Stirling numbers

The probabilistic heterogeneous Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
are defined by

(28)

\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E[e_{-\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1\Big)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)]-1\Big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\,\,(k\geq 0),

which implies that

(29)

H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=(-1)^{n}S_{2,\lambda}^{-Y}(n,k)=S_{2,-\lambda}^{Y}(n,k),\quad(n\geq k).

We observe that

(30)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E[e_{-\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1\Big)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E[e_{-\lambda}^{S_{j}}(t)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E[(S_{j})_{n,-\lambda}]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E[\langle S_{j}\rangle_{n,\lambda}]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E[\langle S_{j}\rangle_{n,\lambda}]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Moreover, from (
19
), we note

(31)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}E[\langle Y\rangle_{m,\lambda}]\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}(E[\langle Y\rangle_{1,\lambda}],E[\langle Y\rangle_{2,\lambda}],\dots,E[\langle Y\rangle_{n-k+1,\lambda}])\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

), (

) and (

Theorem 3.1
.

For any integers
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}S_{2,\lambda}^{-Y}(n,k)=S_{2,-\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)

\displaystyle=B_{n,k}(E[\langle Y\rangle_{1,\lambda}],E[\langle Y\rangle_{2,\lambda}],\dots,E[\langle Y\rangle_{n-k+1,\lambda}]).

We let

\mathcal{E}_{Y,\lambda}(t)=E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)]-1=E[e_{-\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1.

The
probabilistic heterogeneous Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with

(33)

\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{Y,\lambda}(t)\Big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}G_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

By using the formula (C), (

) and the first expression in (

(34)

\displaystyle\,\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{Y,\lambda}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}(-1)^{n}[t^{n-1}]\,\bigg(\frac{-t}{E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)]-1}\bigg)^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}(-1)^{n}[t^{n-1}]\,\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\beta_{m,\lambda}^{(n,-Y)}(-1)^{m}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}=-\beta_{n-1,\lambda}^{(n,-Y)}\frac{1}{n!}.

Also, by using the formula (C), (
22
) and the second expression in (

(35)

\displaystyle\,\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{Y,\lambda}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}[t^{n-1}]\,\bigg(\frac{t}{E[e_{-\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1}\bigg)^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}[t^{n-1}]\,\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\beta_{m,-\lambda}^{(n,Y)}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}=\beta_{n-1,-\lambda}^{(n,Y)}\frac{1}{n!}.

Thus, from (

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\beta_{m-1,-\lambda}^{(m,Y)}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\bigg)^{k},\quad(k\geq 0).

In addition, using the formula (B) and the first and second expressions in (

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\binom{n-1}{k-1}(-1)^{k}\beta_{n-k,\lambda}^{(n,-Y)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\binom{n-1}{k-1}\beta_{n-k,-\lambda}^{(n,Y)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

), (

), (

), (

) and Theorem 2.2, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

For any integers
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle G_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)

\displaystyle=B_{n,k}\big(-\beta_{0,\lambda}^{(1,-Y)},-\beta_{1,\lambda}^{(2,-Y)},\dots,-\beta_{n-k,\lambda}^{(n-k+1,-Y)}\big)

\displaystyle=B_{n,k}\big(\beta_{0,-\lambda}^{(1,Y)},\beta_{1,-\lambda}^{(2,Y)},\dots,\beta_{n-k,-\lambda}^{(n-k+1,Y)}\big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\binom{n-1}{k-1}\beta_{n-k,\lambda}^{(n,-Y)}=(-1)^{k}S_{1,\lambda}^{-Y}(n,k)

\displaystyle=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\beta_{n-k,-\lambda}^{(n,Y)}=S_{1,-\lambda}^{Y}(n,k).

Lemma 3.3
.

For any integers
n\geq k\geq 0

\displaystyle B_{n,k}

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{E[(Y)_{2,\lambda}]}{2},\frac{E[(Y)_{3,\lambda}]}{3},\dots,\frac{E[(Y)_{n-k+2,\lambda}]}{n-k+2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{n+k}{k-j}\frac{n!}{(n+k)!}\big(-E[Y]\big)^{k-j}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n+j,j).

Lemma 3.4
.

For any integer

, the following identity holds true.

\displaystyle\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(\gamma,Y)}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-\gamma)_{k}E[Y]^{-\gamma-k}B_{n,k}\Big(\frac{E[(Y)_{2,\lambda}]}{2},\frac{E[(Y)_{3,\lambda}]}{3},\dots,\frac{E[(Y)_{n-k+2,\lambda}]}{n-k+2}\Big).

Combining the above two lemmas, we arrive at the next result.

Theorem 3.5
.

For any integer
n\geq 0
, the following identity holds true.

\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(\gamma,Y)}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{\gamma+k-1}{k}\binom{k}{j}\binom{n+j}{j}^{-1}(-1)^{j}E[Y]^{-\gamma-j}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n+j,j).

Again, the following probabilistic degenerate version of the classical Schlömilch formula for the probabilisitc Stirling numbers of both kinds is obtained by proceeding just as in the proof of Theorem 3.1 of [26]. However, for the sake of completeness, we give the short proof. For this, we need the following identities:

(39)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=j}^{n-k}\binom{n+i-1}{i}\binom{i}{j}=\frac{n}{n+j}\binom{2n-k}{n}\binom{n-k}{j},

\displaystyle\binom{n-1}{k-1}\binom{2n-k}{n}\frac{n}{n+j}\binom{n-k}{j}\binom{n-k+j}{j}^{-1}=\binom{n+j-1}{n+j-k}\binom{2n-k}{n-k-j}.

The second identity is straightforward. The first identity follows by using
\sum_{i=j}^{m}\binom{i}{j}=\binom{m+1}{j+1}
and noting that

\sum_{i=j}^{n-k}\frac{n+j}{n}\binom{n+i-1}{i}\binom{i}{j}=\binom{n+j}{j}\sum_{i=n+j-1}^{2n-k-1}\binom{i}{n+j-1}.

Theorem 3.6
.

For any integers
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\binom{n+j-1}{n+j-k}\binom{2n-k}{n-k-j}(-1)^{j}E[Y]^{-n-j}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n-k+j,j).

Proof.

From Theorem 3.5, we see that

(40)

\displaystyle\beta_{n-k,\lambda}^{(n,Y)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n-k}\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{n+i-1}{i}\binom{i}{j}\binom{n-k+j}{j}^{-1}(-1)^{j}E[Y]^{-n-j}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n-k+j,j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\binom{n-k+j}{j}^{-1}(-1)^{j}E[Y]^{-n-j}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n-k+j,j)\sum_{i=j}^{n-k}\binom{n+i-1}{i}\binom{i}{j}.

From Theorem 2.2, (
39
) and (
40
), we note

\displaystyle S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\binom{n-1}{k-1}\binom{2n-k}{n}\frac{n}{n+j}\binom{n-k}{j}\binom{n-k+j}{j}^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times(-1)^{j}E[Y]^{-n-j}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n-k+j,j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\binom{n+j-1}{n+j-k}\binom{2n-k}{n-k-j}(-1)^{j}E[Y]^{-n-j}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n-k+j,j).

∎

From Theorems 3.1 and 3.2, we recall that

(41)

\displaystyle H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=(-1)^{n}S_{2,\lambda}^{-Y}(n,k)=S_{2,-\lambda}^{Y}(n,k),

\displaystyle G_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=(-1)^{k}S_{1,\lambda}^{-Y}(n,k)=S_{1,-\lambda}^{Y}(n,k).

By Theorem 3.6 and (

Corollary 3.7
.

For any integers
n\geq k\geq 0

G_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\binom{n+j-1}{n+j-k}\binom{2n-k}{n-k-j}(-1)^{j}E[Y]^{-n-j}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n-k+j,j).

4.
Probabilistic degenerate logarithm

The Stirling numbers of the second kind and those of the first kind are respectively given by

\frac{1}{k!}(e^{t}-1)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log(1+t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

The degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind and those of the first kind are respectively defined by

\frac{1}{k!}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\,\,\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\,\,(k\geq 0).

The probabilistic Stirling numbers of the second kind are specified as

\frac{1}{k!}(E[e^{Yt}]-1)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

The probabilistic degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind are introduced as

\frac{1}{k!}(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

Note that
e^{t}-1
and
\log(1+t)
are compositional inverses, as are
e_{\lambda}(t)-1
and
\log_{\lambda}(1+t)
.
Furthermore, we observe that

\log(1+t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,1)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad\log_{\lambda}(1+t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,1)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Let

e_{Y}(t)=E[e^{Yt}]-1,\quad e_{Y,\lambda}(t)=E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1.

Then the probabilistic Stirling numbers of the first kind and the probabilisitc degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind are respectively defined by

\frac{1}{k!}\big(\bar{e}_{Y}(t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad\frac{1}{k!}\big(\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0),

where
\bar{e}_{Y}(t)
and
\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)
are respectively the compositional inverses of
e_{Y}(t)
and
e_{Y,\lambda}(t)
.

Taking into account these considerations, it is natural to define the
probabilistic logarithm associated with
Y
,
\log^{Y}(1+t)
, and the
probabilisitc degenerate logarithm associated with
Y
,
\log_{\lambda}^{Y}(1+t)
, respectively as

(42)

\displaystyle\log^{Y}(1+t)=\bar{e}_{Y}(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}S_{1}^{Y}(n,1)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{n-1}^{(n,Y)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

\displaystyle\log_{\lambda}^{Y}(1+t)=\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,1)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n-1,\lambda}^{(n,Y)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
B_{n}^{(\gamma,Y)}
and
\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(\gamma,Y)}
are respectively as in (
27
) and (

Now, we have

\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}^{Y}(1+t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0),

and

\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log^{Y}(1+t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

Recalling that
S_{1}(n,1)=(-1)^{n-1}(n-1)!
and

, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,1)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-1}(n-1)!\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\log(1+t),

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,1)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(\lambda)_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\big((1+t)^{\lambda}-1\big)=\log_{\lambda}(1+t),

as they should be.

(Y\sim G(p))
, with
0<p<1
:

\displaystyle E[Y]=\frac{1}{p},\quad E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]=\frac{pe_{\lambda}(t)}{1-(1-p)e_{\lambda}(t)},

\displaystyle S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\frac{1}{p-1}\Big)^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}h_{n,\lambda}^{(j)}\Big(\frac{1}{1-p}\Big),

\displaystyle S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}L(n,j)p^{j}(p-1)^{n-j}S_{1,\lambda}(j,k),

\displaystyle\log_{\lambda}^{Y}(1+t)=\log_{\lambda}\Big(\frac{1+t}{1+(1-p)t}\Big),

where
h_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(u)
are the degenerate Frobenius-Euler numbers of order
r
, given by

\Big(\frac{1-u}{e_{\lambda}(t)-u}\Big)^{r}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}h_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(u)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(u\neq 1).

(g) Normal random variable with parameters
(\mu,\sigma^{2})

(Y\sim N(\mu,\sigma^{2})
, with
\mu\neq 0,\sigma>0
:

\displaystyle E[Y]=\mu,\quad E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]=e^{\mu\log e_{\lambda}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\big(\log e_{\lambda}(t)\big)^{2}},

\displaystyle S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{m=k}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{m}\frac{m!}{j!}\binom{j}{m-j}\mu^{2j-m}\Big(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Big)^{m-j}\lambda^{n-m}S_{2}(j,k)S_{1}(n,m),

\displaystyle S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{k}}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{j}(-1)^{j+l}\frac{1}{j!}\Big(\frac{\lambda\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big)^{j}\Big(\frac{l}{2}\Big)_{m}2^{m}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{\mu}\Big)^{2m}\binom{j}{l}S_{2}(j,k)S_{1}(n,m),

\displaystyle\log_{\lambda}^{Y}(1+t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{j}(-1)^{j+l}\frac{1}{j!}\binom{j}{l}\Big(\frac{\lambda\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big)^{j}\Big(\frac{l}{2}\Big)_{m}2^{m}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{\mu}\Big)^{2m}S_{1}(n,m)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

(h) Negative binomial random variable
(Y\sim NB(r,p))
, with
0<p<1
,
r
a positive integer. This is not treated in [7]. The expression for
E[Y]
is well known and those for
E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]
and
\log_{\lambda}^{Y}(1+t)
are easy to see. We show here only the expression for
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
, leaving the proof for
S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
to the reader.

\displaystyle E[Y]=\frac{r(1-p)}{p},\quad E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]=\bigg(\frac{p}{1-(1-p)e_{\lambda}(t)}\bigg)^{r},

\displaystyle S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{j}\sum_{l=0}^{m}\frac{1}{(k-l)!}(p-1)^{j}(p^{r}-1)^{k-l}p^{rl}(-r)^{m}\frac{(j)_{n,\lambda}}{j!}S_{1}(j,m)S_{2}(m,l),

\displaystyle S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\sum_{m=l}^{\infty}\frac{(-p)^{m}}{(k-l)!}\frac{\big(-\frac{m}{r}\big)_{n}}{m!}\Big(\frac{1}{1-p}\Big)^{\lambda l}\Big(\log_{\lambda}\Big(\frac{1}{1-p}\Big)\Big)^{k-l}S_{1,\lambda}(m,l),

\displaystyle\log_{\lambda}^{Y}(1+t)=\log_{\lambda}\Big(\frac{1}{1-p}\big(1-p(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{r}}\big)\Big).

To derive the expression for
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
, we first observe that

\log_{\lambda}(AB)=A^{\lambda}\log_{\lambda}(B)+\log_{\lambda}(A).

Here, for
e_{Y,\lambda}(t)=E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1=\big(\frac{p}{1-(1-p)e_{\lambda}(t)}\big)^{r}-1
, it is immediate to see that

\displaystyle\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\log_{\lambda}^{Y}(1+t)=\log_{\lambda}\Big(\frac{1}{1-p}\big(1-p(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{r}}\big)\Big).

Now, we proceed as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}

\displaystyle\big(\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)\big)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\log_{\lambda}\Big(\frac{1}{1-p}\Big)+\Big(\frac{1}{1-p}\Big)^{\lambda}\log_{\lambda}(1-p(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{r}}\big)\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\Big(\log_{\lambda}\Big(\frac{1}{1-p}\Big)\Big)^{k-l}\Big(\frac{1}{1-p}\Big)^{\lambda l}l!\frac{1}{l!}\Big(\log_{\lambda}\big(1-p(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{r}}\big)\Big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\Big(\log_{\lambda}\Big(\frac{1}{1-p}\Big)\Big)^{k-l}\Big(\frac{1}{1-p}\Big)^{\lambda l}l!

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\sum_{m=l}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(m,l)\frac{1}{m!}(-p)^{m}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(-\frac{1}{r}\Big)^{n}\langle m\rangle_{n,r}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\sum_{m=l}^{\infty}\frac{(-p)^{m}}{(k-l)!}\frac{\big(-\frac{m}{r}\big)_{n}}{m!}\Big(\frac{1}{1-p}\Big)^{\lambda l}\Big(\log_{\lambda}\Big(\frac{1}{1-p}\Big)\Big)^{k-l}S_{1,\lambda}(m,l)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

(i) Uniform random variable
(Y\sim U[0,1])
:

\displaystyle E[Y]=\frac{1}{2},\quad E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]=\frac{\lambda}{\log(1+\lambda t)}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)=\frac{1}{\log e_{\lambda}(t)}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big),

\displaystyle S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\binom{m+j}{j}^{-1}(-1)^{k-j}\lambda^{n-m}S_{2}(m+j,j)S_{1}(n,m).

The above quantities are known from [7]. Here we find an explicit expression for
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
by using Lagrange inversion formulas, from which an expression for
\log_{\lambda}^{Y}(1+t)
follows as well.
For this, we first show the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

For
Y\sim U[0,1]
and
n\geq k+1
, we have

[t^{n}]\frac{1}{k!}\big(\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)\big)^{k}=\frac{1}{(n-k)k!}[t^{n-k-1}]\bigg(\frac{t}{\frac{e^{t}-1}{t}-1}\bigg)^{n-k}\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\frac{\log_{\lambda}(e^{t})}{\frac{e^{t}-1}{t}-1}\bigg)^{k},

where
e_{Y,\lambda}(t)=E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1=\frac{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}{\log e_{\lambda}(t)}-1=\frac{e^{\log e_{\lambda}(t)}-1}{\log e_{\lambda}(t)}-1
.

Proof.

By the formula (B), we have

(50)

[t^{n}]\big(\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)\big)^{k}=\frac{k}{n}[t^{n-k}]\Big(\frac{t}{e_{Y,\lambda}(t)}\Big)^{n},

where
\big(\frac{t}{e_{Y,\lambda}(t)}\big)^{n}=g(\bar{f}(t))
,

with

g(t)=\bigg(\frac{\log_{\lambda}(e^{t})}{\frac{e^{t}-1}{t}-1}\bigg)^{n}=\bigg(\frac{t^{2}}{e^{t}-1-t}\frac{e^{\lambda t}-1}{\lambda t}\bigg)^{n},\quad\bar{f}(t)=\log e_{\lambda}(t).

Note here that
f(t)=\log_{\lambda}e^{t}
. Using the formula (A), we see that

(51)

\displaystyle g(\bar{f}(t))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}[t^{m-1}]g^{\prime}(t)\Big(\frac{t}{f(t)}\Big)^{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}[t^{m-1}]n\bigg(\frac{\log_{\lambda}(e^{t})}{\frac{e^{t}-1}{t}-1}\bigg)^{n-m-1}\bigg(\frac{\log_{\lambda}(e^{t})}{\frac{e^{t}-1}{t}-1}\bigg)^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{t}{\frac{e^{t}-1}{t}-1}\bigg)^{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{m(n-m)}[t^{m-1}]\bigg(\bigg(\frac{\log_{\lambda}(e^{t})}{\frac{e^{t}-1}{t}-1}\bigg)^{n-m}\bigg)^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{t}{\frac{e^{t}-1}{t}-1}\bigg)^{m}

Applying (
51
) with
m=n-k
to (
50
), we get the desired result.
∎

Before proceeding further, we recall special cases of Bernoulli-Padé numbers
A_{k,n}
studied by Howard (see [10]), which are defined by:

\frac{\frac{1}{k!}t^{k}}{e^{t}-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{k,n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(A_{k,0}=1).

Then it is immediate to see that

(52)

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{t^{2}}{e^{t}-1-t}\bigg)^{n}=2^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{n}=m}\binom{m}{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{n}}A_{2,k_{1}}A_{k_{2}}\cdots A_{k_{n}}\frac{t^{m}}{m!},

(53)

\displaystyle\bigg(\bigg(\frac{t^{2}}{e^{t}-1-t}\bigg)^{n}\bigg)^{\prime}=2^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{n}=m+1}\binom{m+1}{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{n}}A_{2,k_{1}}A_{k_{2}}\cdots A_{k_{n}}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}.

We also need

(54)

\frac{e^{\lambda t}-1}{\lambda t}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{n}}{n+1}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad\bigg(\frac{e^{\lambda t}-1}{\lambda t}\bigg)^{\prime}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{n+1}}{n+2}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Theorem 5.2
.

For
Y\sim U[0,1]
, we have

(55)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}

\displaystyle\big(\log_{\lambda}^{Y}(1+t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\frac{2^{n}}{n}\binom{n}{k}\sum_{m=0}^{n-k}(n-m)\binom{n-k}{m}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}+\cdots+j_{n}=m}\binom{m}{j_{1},j_{2},\dots,j_{n}}A_{2,j_{1}}A_{2,j_{2}}\cdots A_{2,j_{n}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}+\cdots+l_{k}=n-k-m}\binom{n-k-m}{l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{k}}\frac{\lambda^{n-k-m}}{(l_{1}+1)(l_{2}+1)\cdots(l_{k}+1)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where the number
A_{2,n}
’s are given by

\frac{\frac{1}{2!}t^{2}}{e^{t}-1-t}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{2,n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Proof.

Here we only give the proof for
k=1
. Applying the above lemma with
k=1
and using (
52
)-(
54
), for
n\geq 2
, we have

(56)

\displaystyle[t^{n}]\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\frac{1}{n-1}[t^{n-2}]\bigg(\frac{t}{\frac{e^{t}-1}{t}-1}\bigg)^{n-1}\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\frac{\log_{\lambda}(e^{t})}{\frac{e^{t}-1}{t}-1}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n-1}[t^{n-2}]\bigg(\frac{t^{2}}{e^{t}-1-t}\bigg)^{n-1}\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\frac{t^{2}}{e^{t}-1-t}\frac{e^{\lambda t}-1}{\lambda t}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n-1}[t^{n-2}]\bigg(\frac{t^{2}}{e^{t}-1-t}\bigg)^{n-1}

\displaystyle\times\bigg\{\bigg(\frac{t^{2}}{e^{t}-1-t}\bigg)^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{e^{\lambda t}-1}{\lambda t}\bigg)+\bigg(\frac{t^{2}}{e^{t}-1-t}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{e^{\lambda t}-1}{\lambda t}\bigg)^{\prime}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n-1}[t^{n-2}]\bigg\{\frac{1}{n}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{t^{2}}{e^{t}-1-t}\bigg)^{n}\bigg)^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{e^{\lambda t}-1}{\lambda t}\bigg)+\bigg(\frac{t^{2}}{e^{t}-1-t}\bigg)^{n}\bigg(\frac{e^{\lambda t}-1}{\lambda t}\bigg)^{\prime}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n}}{n!}\sum_{m=0}^{n-2}\binom{n-2}{m}\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}+\cdots+j_{n}=m+1}\binom{m+1}{j_{1},j_{2},\dots,j_{n}}A_{2,j_{1}}A_{2,j_{2}}\cdots A_{2,j_{n}}\frac{\lambda^{n-m-2}}{n-m-1}

\displaystyle+\frac{2^{n}}{(n-1)!}\sum_{m=0}^{n-2}\binom{n-2}{m}\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}+\cdots+j_{n}=m}\binom{m}{j_{1},j_{2},\dots,j_{n}}A_{2,j_{1}}A_{2,j_{2}}\cdots A_{2,j_{n}}\frac{\lambda^{n-m-1}}{n-m}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n}}{n!}\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-2}{m-1}\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}+\cdots+j_{n}=m}\binom{m}{j_{1},j_{2},\dots,j_{n}}A_{2,j_{1}}A_{2,j_{2}}\cdots A_{2,j_{n}}\frac{\lambda^{n-m-1}}{n-m}

\displaystyle+\frac{2^{n}}{(n-1)!}\sum_{m=0}^{n-2}\binom{n-2}{m}\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}+\cdots+j_{n}=m}\binom{m}{j_{1},j_{2},\dots,j_{n}}A_{2,j_{1}}A_{2,j_{2}}\cdots A_{2,j_{n}}\frac{\lambda^{n-m-1}}{n-m}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n}}{n!}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{m}\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}+\cdots+j_{n}=m}\binom{m}{j_{1},j_{2},\dots,j_{n}}A_{2,j_{1}}A_{2,j_{2}}\cdots A_{2,j_{n}}\lambda^{n-m-1}.

Now, the result follows from (

) by observing that
[t]\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)=[t^{0}]\big(\frac{t}{e_{Y,\lambda}(t)}\big)=2

∎

For

Corollary 5.4

For

\displaystyle\log_{\lambda}^{Y}(1+t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,1)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{m}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}+\cdots+j_{n}=m}\binom{m}{j_{1},j_{2},\dots,j_{n}}A_{2,j_{1}}A_{2,j_{2}}\cdots A_{2,j_{n}}\lambda^{n-m-1}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

6.
Conclusion

The study of special polynomials and numbers has recently shifted toward two significant frontiers: degenerate versions, originating from the work of Carlitz, and probabilistic extensions, which associate classical sequences with random variables
Y
. In this paper, we have successfully bridged gaps in the existing literature regarding these sequences. By defining the probabilistic (discrete) logarithms
\log^{Y}(1+t)
and
\log_{\lambda}^{Y}(1+t)
, we provided a consistent framework for generating
S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)
and
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
that preserves orthogonality and inverse relations. Furthermore, our introduction of probabilistic degenerate Daehee and Cauchy numbers offers a more robust model than previous versions, as they maintain a dependency on the random variable
Y
even at
x=0
. Finally, the derivation of the probabilistic degenerate Schlömilch formula and the exploration of heterogeneous Stirling numbers of the first kind complete a comprehensive structural symmetry between classical and probabilistic combinatorics. These results offer promising tools for further application in modeling stochastic systems and exploring new identities within umbral calculus.

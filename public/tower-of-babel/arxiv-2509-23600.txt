Geodesic Deviation to All Orders via a Tangent Bundle Formalism Preprint: CALT-TH 2025-004
Joon-Hwi Kim

Abstract
We establish an in-in formalism for geodesic deviation as an alternative to Synge calculus, based on a covariant calculus of differential forms in tangent bundle. This derives the exact Lagrangian and equations governing the finite geodesic deviation between a free-falling test particle and an arbitrary observer, in terms of infinite sums whose coefficients are products of binomial coefficients. Explicit expressions are provided up to tenth order, finding agreements with the previous fourth-order result.

Geodesic Deviation in Tangent Bundle

Let
({\mathcal{M}},g)
be
a
d
-dimensional real-analytic manifold
with local coordinates
x^{\mu}

and metric
g_{\mu\nu}
,
which we call spacetime.
Its tangent bundle,
T{\mathcal{M}}
,
can be viewed as a
2d
-dimensional manifold
with local coordinates
(x^{\mu},y^{\mu})
,
based on
its local trivialization
by the coordinate vector fields.

Coordinate transformations of
T{\mathcal{M}}

are restricted in the form

(x^{\mu},y^{\mu})\mapsto(f^{\mu}(x),f^{\mu}{}_{,\nu}(x)\hskip 1.00006pty^{\nu})
,
where
f^{\mu}(x)
describes a set of real-analytic functions in a local patch of
{\mathcal{M}}
.
Importantly,
the notion of
covariance in
T{\mathcal{M}}
is defined with respect to
these coordinate transformations.

In particular,
the following vector field
in
T{\mathcal{M}}

is
invariant
under
such coordinate transformations:

\displaystyle N\,=\,y^{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}-\Gamma^{\mu}{}_{\rho\sigma}(x)\hskip 1.00006pty^{\rho}\hskip 1.00006pty^{\sigma}\hskip 1.00006pt\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\mu}}\,,

(2)

where
\Gamma^{\mu}{}_{\rho\sigma}(x)
denote the Christoffel symbols.
This can be easily seen by considering its interior products with
a one-form basis in
T{\mathcal{M}}
that transform covariantly:

\displaystyle{\iota}_{N}\hskip 0.50003ptdx^{\mu}=y^{\mu}\,,\quad{\iota}_{N}Dy^{\mu}=0\,.

(3)

Here,
D
denotes the covariant exterior derivative:

Dy^{\mu}=dy^{\mu}+\Gamma^{\mu}{}_{\nu\rho}(x)\hskip 1.00006pty^{\nu}\hskip 1.00006ptdx^{\rho}
.
In light of its invariance,

N
defines a structure
characteristic of the tangent bundle.
In the mathematical jargon,
it describes a horizontal vector field
due to the Ehresmann notion of a connection

ehresmann1948connexions
;
Mason:2013sva
.

A geodesic segment joins two spacetime points

(observer) and

The tangent vectors are respectively denoted as
y^{\mu}
and
y^{\mu{\mathrlap{\smash{{}^{\prime}}}{\hskip 0.40974pt}}}
,
which are normalized such that

x

are related by unit-time geodesic flows.

Crucially,

N

holds the very significance as
the
generator of geodesic deviation
.
To see this, consider
the first-order formulation of the geodesic equation:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{d\eta}\hskip 1.00006ptX^{\mu}(\eta)&\hskip 1.00006pt=\hskip 1.00006ptY^{\mu}(\eta)\,,\\
\frac{d}{d\eta}\hskip 1.00006ptY^{\mu}(\eta)&\hskip 1.00006pt=\hskip 1.00006pt-\Gamma^{\mu}{}_{\rho\sigma}(X(\eta))\,Y^{\rho}(\eta)\,Y^{\sigma}(\eta)\,.\end{split}

(4)

Evidently,
the explicit components of
N
in Eq. (
2
)
encode Eq. (
4
).
This implies that
the power series solution of Eq. (
4
)
is given by

X^{\mu}(\eta)={\scalebox{1.05}[1.01]{$\mathrm{e}$}}^{\eta N}x^{\mu}

and

Y^{\mu}(\eta)={\scalebox{1.05}[1.01]{$\mathrm{e}$}}^{\eta N}y^{\mu}

for initial conditions

X^{\mu}(0)=x^{\mu}
and
Y^{\mu}(0)=y^{\mu}
,
where

N
is understood as a differential operator.
Namely,
the time-
\eta
flow of
N
solves the geodesic equation.

In particular, consider the unit-time flow:

\displaystyle X^{\mu}(1)\hskip 1.00006pt=\hskip 1.00006pt{\scalebox{1.05}[1.01]{$\mathrm{e}$}}^{N}x^{\mu}\,,\quad Y^{\mu}(1)\hskip 1.00006pt=\hskip 1.00006pt{\scalebox{1.05}[1.01]{$\mathrm{e}$}}^{N}y^{\mu}\,.

(5)

Borrowing the notation in the Synge bitensor formalism

;
Synge:1931zz
;
Synge:1960ueh
;
Poisson:2011nh
,
we rephrase Eq. (
5
) as the following:

\displaystyle z^{\mu{\mathrlap{\smash{{}^{\prime}}}{\hskip 0.40974pt}}}\hskip 1.00006pt=\hskip 1.00006pt\delta^{\mu{\mathrlap{\smash{{}^{\prime}}}{\hskip 0.40974pt}}}{}_{\mu}\hskip 1.00006pt({\scalebox{1.05}[1.01]{$\mathrm{e}$}}^{N}x^{\mu})\,,\quad y^{\mu{\mathrlap{\smash{{}^{\prime}}}{\hskip 0.40974pt}}}\hskip 1.00006pt=\hskip 1.00006pt\delta^{\mu{\mathrlap{\smash{{}^{\prime}}}{\hskip 0.40974pt}}}{}_{\mu}\hskip 1.00006pt({\scalebox{1.05}[1.01]{$\mathrm{e}$}}^{N}y^{\mu})\,,

(6)

where
\delta^{\mu{\mathrlap{\smash{{}^{\prime}}}{\hskip 0.40974pt}}}{}_{\mu}
is Kronecker delta.
Namely, we assign unprimed and primed indices
to objects at the original (
x
) and deviated (
z
) points,
respectively.
The rationale might be that indices represent the behavior under coordinate transformations,
and
tensors at
x
and

transform with different Jacobian factors.

The unit-time flow of
N

can be regarded as a diffeomorphism in
T{\mathcal{M}}
.
By the very definition of the Lie derivative,
the pullback of
a tensor

\alpha
in

is given by
{\scalebox{1.05}[1.01]{$\mathrm{e}$}}^{\pounds_{N}}\alpha

Practically, this pullback

with

with
{\scalebox{1.05}[1.01]{$\mathrm{e}$}}^{N}y

For instance, the following identity holds:

\displaystyle dz^{\mu{\mathrlap{\smash{{}^{\prime}}}{\hskip 0.40974pt}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 1.00006pt=\hskip 1.00006pt\delta^{\mu{\mathrlap{\smash{{}^{\prime}}}{\hskip 0.40974pt}}}{}_{\mu}\hskip 1.00006pt({\scalebox{1.05}[1.01]{$\mathrm{e}$}}^{\pounds_{N}}dx^{\mu})\,.

(7)

Notably,
for differential forms,
Lie derivatives are neatly computed
by using the Cartan magic formula:

\displaystyle\pounds_{N}\hskip 1.00006pt=\hskip 1.00006ptd\hskip 1.00006pt{\iota}_{N}+{\iota}_{N}d\,.

(8)

We leave it
as an exercise to

)
at each order in

by using Eq. (

Geodesic Deviation in A Direct Sum Bundle

For pedagogical reasons, let us also consider
a slight generalization of the above construction.
Suppose the direct sum of
the tangent and cotangent bundles:

{\mathcal{P}}=T{\mathcal{M}}\oplus T^{*}\hskip-1.00006pt{\mathcal{M}}
,
where local coordinates are
(x^{\mu},y^{\mu},p_{\mu})
.
Eventually, this
3d
-dimensional space
will serve as our geometrical arena
for a first-order formulation of GDE.
In this larger bundle,
the vector field
N
is uniquely defined by

{\iota}_{N}\hskip 0.50003ptdx^{\mu}=y^{\mu}
,

{\iota}_{N}Dy^{\mu}=0
,
and

{\iota}_{N}Dp_{\mu}=0

It is not difficult to see that this

is the generator of geodesic deviation and parallel transport.
In particular,
consider the analog of the first-order differential equations in Eq. (
4
)
to find that

p_{\mu{\mathrlap{\smash{{}^{\prime}}}{\hskip 0.40974pt}}}=({\scalebox{1.05}[1.01]{$\mathrm{e}$}}^{N}p_{\mu})\hskip 1.00006pt\delta^{\mu}{}_{\mu^{\prime}}

is the covector

parallel-transported to the point

along the geodesic;
see Fig.
1

This finding is succinctly stated as

\displaystyle p_{\mu{\mathrlap{\smash{{}^{\prime}}}{\hskip 0.40974pt}}}\,=\,p_{\mu}\hskip 1.00006ptW^{\mu}{}_{\mu^{\prime}}\,,

(9)

where
W^{\mu}{}_{\mu{\mathrlap{\smash{{}^{\prime}}}{\hskip 0.40974pt}}}
denotes the
parallel propagator

the Wilson line of the Levi-Civita connection
computed about the geodesic between

and

Namely,

describes the isomorphism between the cotangent spaces

x
and

Again,

(x,y,p)\mapsto{\scalebox{1.05}[1.01]{$\mathrm{e}$}}^{\eta N}(x,y,p)

describes the time-
\eta

{\mathcal{P}}
generated by the vector field
N
.
Due to
this geometrical interpretation,
the unit-time geodesic deviation and parallel transport
is given by the
exponentiated Lie derivative
{\scalebox{1.05}[1.01]{$\mathrm{e}$}}^{\pounds_{N}}
.

For a concrete example,
consider a one-form
p_{\mu}\hskip 0.50003ptdx^{\mu}
,
i.e., the trivial extension of the cotangent bundle’s tautological one-form
to
{\mathcal{P}}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle p_{\mu^{\prime}}\hskip 0.50003ptdz^{\mu{\mathrlap{\smash{{}^{\prime}}}{\hskip 0.40974pt}}}\hskip 1.00006pt=\hskip 1.00006pt{\scalebox{1.05}[1.01]{$\mathrm{e}$}}^{\pounds_{N}}(p_{\mu}\hskip 1.00006ptdx^{\mu})\,,

(10)

the right-hand side of
which can be computed order-by-order
by series-expanding
{\scalebox{1.05}[1.01]{$\mathrm{e}$}}^{\pounds_{N}}
.
We leave it as an exercise to carry out this computation
and check consistency between the left-hand and right-hand sides.

Covariant Lie Derivative and Dressing Identity

The computations utilizing
\pounds_{N}
,
however,
are not very efficient.
One encounters unoccupied connection coefficients
arising from the components of
N

so covariance at the original point
x

is not manifested
in intermediate steps.

In this light, we introduce a shorthand notation,

\displaystyle\pounds_{N}^{D}\hskip 1.00006pt=\hskip 1.00006ptD\hskip 1.00006pt{\iota}_{N}+{\iota}_{N}D\,,

(11)

defined on any tensor-valued differential form.
This describes a covariantized analog of the Lie derivative;
more information might be found in
Refs.
Jackiw:2001jb
;

;
de2015covariant

Crucially,
since

is a scalar-valued one-form carrying no free indices,
using
D
instead of

makes no difference.
Hence we can equivalently use
\exp({\pounds_{N}^{D}})

in Eq. (
10

It is a privilege of exponentiated operators
that they are distributable as

\displaystyle{\scalebox{1.05}[1.01]{$\mathrm{e}$}}^{\pounds_{N}^{D}}(p_{\mu}\hskip 0.50003ptdx^{\mu})\hskip 0.50003pt=\hskip 0.50003pt({\scalebox{1.05}[1.01]{$\mathrm{e}$}}^{\pounds_{N}^{D}}p_{\mu})\hskip 0.50003pt({\scalebox{1.05}[1.01]{$\mathrm{e}$}}^{\pounds_{N}^{D}}dx^{\mu})\hskip 0.50003pt=\hskip 0.50003ptp_{\mu}\hskip 0.50003pt({\scalebox{1.05}[1.01]{$\mathrm{e}$}}^{\pounds_{N}^{D}}dx^{\mu})\,,

(12)

where
the last equality follows from

\pounds_{N}^{D}p_{\mu}={\iota}_{N}Dp_{\mu}=0
.
Therefore, from
Eqs. (
9
),
(
10
),
and (
12
),
it follows that

\displaystyle W^{\mu}{}_{\mu^{\prime}}\hskip 1.00006ptdz^{\mu{\mathrlap{\smash{{}^{\prime}}}{\hskip 0.40974pt}}}\hskip 1.00006pt=\hskip 1.00006pt{\scalebox{1.05}[1.01]{$\mathrm{e}$}}^{\pounds_{N}^{D}}dx^{\mu}\,.

(13)

In the same way,
it can be shown that
the
\exp(\pounds_{N}^{D})

of a tensor-valued differential form
computes its value at the deviated point,
followed by the
parallel-transportation back to the original point
via dressing by the Wilson lines.
This fact will be referred to as
the
dressing identity
.

The evaluation of the differential terms,

The issue in particular is the evaluation of the

When

-derivative hits a
Q
-tensor,
it inserts

in all possible positions in the string of

Such terms can be gathered and simplified
by commuting covariant derivatives
and using Bianchi identities of the Riemann tensor
in various ways.

For the part linear in both
u

v

this issue is simply avoided
by virtue ot
the zero-torsion identity in Eq. (
33b

Hence it suffices to massage
the part quadratic in
v
,
which is the third term in the right-hand side of Eq. (
31

As the result of this simplification,
we obtain the final expression
as a sum of terms
that strictly conform to the following particular tensor structures:

\displaystyle\text{Derivative}:\quad

\displaystyle(Q_{{\gamma}_{1}}\cdots Q_{{\gamma}_{r}})^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\,(Q_{\ell})^{\nu}{}_{\kappa|\rho}\,(Q_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots Q_{\alpha_{p}})^{\kappa}{}_{\sigma}\,,

\displaystyle(Q_{{\gamma}_{1}}\cdots Q_{{\gamma}_{r}})^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\,(\acute{Q}_{\ell})^{\nu}{}_{\lambda\kappa}\,(Q_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots Q_{\alpha_{p}})^{\kappa}{}_{\sigma}\,(Q_{\beta_{1}}\cdots Q_{\beta_{q}})^{\lambda}{}_{\rho}\,.

(76b)

\displaystyle(Q_{\ell})^{\mu}{}_{\sigma|\rho}\,:=\,y^{\kappa_{1}}{\cdots}y^{\kappa_{\ell-1}}\hskip 1.00006ptR^{\mu}{}_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\sigma;\kappa_{3};\cdots;\kappa_{\ell-1};\rho}(x)\quad\text{for}\quad\ell\geq 3\,.

the free index
\rho

or

is made to
describe the last (outmost) covariant derivative
acting on the Riemann tensor.
This is always possible by commuting covariant derivatives,
while
employing the Bianchi identities
facilitates
rewriting the remaining terms
strictly in terms of the
Q
- and
\acute{Q}
-tensors.

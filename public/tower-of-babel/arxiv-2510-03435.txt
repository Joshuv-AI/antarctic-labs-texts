Holomorphic, rational Lax pairs of a q q -discrete Painlevé VI equation
Robert Conte 1. Université Paris-Saclay, ENS Paris-Saclay, CNRSCentre Borelli, F-91190 Gif-sur-Yvette, France2. Department of mathematics, The University of Hong Kong,Pokfulam, Hong Kong3. Abstract
We build several matrix Lax pairs of q − P VI {\rm q-P_{\rm VI}} valid even when the two eigenvalues of the residue of the monodromy matrix at infinity are equal. Their elements are rational functions of the dependent variables.



Keywords and phrases

Discrete Painlevé equations, Bonnet surfaces, discrete Lax pairs

2
{\rm P_{\rm VI}}
. The prescriptions of Schlesinger for a matrix Lax pair

In the matrix case, the simplest nontrivial monodromy is defined
by a second order matrix whose Fuchsian singularities
are four nonapparent ones and zero apparent
[
14
]
.
This is an advantage over the scalar case,
in which the simplest nontrivial monodromy arises from
a second order linear ordinary differential equation
containing four nonapparent and one apparent Fuchsian singularities
[
12
, p 219]
.

Neither Poincaré nor Schlesinger performed the associated computations.
As is well known, this was achieved for the first time
in the scalar case by Richard Fuchs
[
7
]
(the brother-in-law of Schlesinger),
and in the matrix case by Jimbo and Miwa
[
9
]
.

However, this matrix Lax pair displays the same two unpleasant features
(meromorphic dependence on one parameter, nonrational elements) than the one of
{\rm q-P_{\rm VI}}
,
and this is for the same reason, so let us first point out this reason,
then explain how to restore a holomorphic dependence on the monodromy exponents
and a rational dependence for the matrix elements.

The Lax pair of Schlesinger has the structure (
x
denotes the independent variable,
t
the spectral parameter),

\displaystyle{\hskip-42.67912pt}\hbox{d}\Psi=L\Psi\hbox{d}x+M\Psi\hbox{d}t,\

\displaystyle{\hskip-42.67912pt}M=\frac{M_{0}}{t}+\frac{M_{1}}{t-1}+\frac{M_{x}}{t-x},\ L=-\frac{M_{x}}{t-x}+L_{\infty},\ M_{\infty}=-M_{0}-M_{1}-M_{x},

(9)

in which the four residues
M_{j}
and the regular term
L_{\infty}
only depend on
x

and can be chosen traceless.

In particular,
L_{\infty}
is not necessarily zero,
and
M_{\infty}
is not necessarily constant.
However, Schlesinger points out
[
14
, p 105]
a simplification,
namely the existence,

on the condition
that the residue at infinity
M_{\infty}
be invertible,
of a transition matrix able to, at the same time, make
M_{\infty}
diagonal and constant,
and make
L_{\infty}
zero.

Implementing this suggested simplification,
Jimbo and Miwa assumed

\displaystyle({\rm P_{\rm VI}}):\ M_{\infty}=\hbox{constant}=\mathop{\rm diag}\nolimits(a_{\infty}/2,-a_{\infty}/2),L_{\infty}=0,

(10)

and the condition
a_{\infty}\not=0

(otherwise the monodromy matrix would admit less than four Fuchsian singularities)
led them to the restriction
\theta_{\infty}^{2}\not=1
,
where
\theta_{\infty}
is one of the four monodromy exponents, defined
in terms of the usual four parameters
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta
of
{\rm P_{\rm VI}}

by the relations

\displaystyle(\theta_{\infty}^{2},\theta_{0}^{2},\theta_{1}^{2},\theta_{x}^{2})=(2\alpha,-2\beta,2\gamma,1-2\delta).

(11)

Similarly,
the assumption of Jimbo and Sakai, in their notation
[
10
, Eq (10)]
,

\displaystyle({\rm q-P_{\rm VI}}):\ A_{2}=\hbox{constant}=\mathop{\rm diag}\nolimits(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}),

(12)

led them to the restriction that the denominator
\kappa_{1}-\kappa_{2}
should not vanish.

3
{\rm P_{\rm VI}}
. The various realizations of a holomorphic matrix Lax pair

At least two other realizations of the prescriptions of Schlesinger have been made,
which succeed to remove the meromorphic dependence on one monodromy exponent
and to make the off-diagonal elements rational.

1.

Assuming a nonconstant never diagonal matrix for the residue at infinity,
a ternary symmetry for the three other residues,

\displaystyle{\hskip-25.60747pt}M_{\infty}=\hbox{nondiagonal}=\pmatrix{a_{\infty}/2&\not=\hbox{constant}\cr 0&-a_{\infty}/2\cr},L_{\infty}=\pmatrix{f_{11}(x)&f_{12}(x)\cr f_{21}(x)&-f_{11}(x)\cr},

\displaystyle{\hskip-25.60747pt}M_{j}=\pmatrix{a_{j}&b_{j}\cr(\theta_{j}^{2}/4-a_{j}^{2})/b_{j}&-a_{j}\cr},j=0,1,x\hbox{ (invariance 3!)},

\displaystyle{\hskip-25.60747pt}(b_{0},b_{1},b_{x})=\left(-\frac{u}{x},\frac{u-1}{x-1},\frac{u-x}{x(1-x)}\right)\hbox{ so that }M_{12}=\frac{t-u}{t(t-1)(t-x)},

4.1
Monodromy matrix
A

By discretizing the Lax pair of Jimbo and Miwa,

\displaystyle t_{1}t_{2}=\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}a_{4}.

Conclusion

It would be interesting to examine

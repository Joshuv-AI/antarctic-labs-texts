Introduction

A topological dynamical system is a pair
(X,T)
, where
X
is a compact metric space and
T
is a group acting on
X
. In this paper, we mainly focus on abelian group actions. Minimal topological systems possess a rich and vast theory. This theory provides a classification based on fundamental system types and extensions, allowing one to construct any given minimal system using towers of extensions and factors. For a comprehensive exposition of this theory, we refer the reader to
[
3
,
12
]
.
Key concepts of this theory are the equicontinuous systems and equicontinuous/proximal extensions. Minimal abelian equicontinuous systems, that is, where the group forms an abelian equicontinuous family of transformations, can be characterized through the triviality of a closed, invariant, equivalence relation, called the equicontinuous regionally proximal relation, and usually denoted by
\mathbb{RP}
. That is, minimal equicontinuous systems can be characterized as
\mathbb{RP}=\Delta
, where
\Delta
is the diagonal
\{(x,x):x\in X\}
. Moreover, any minimal topological dynamical system admits a maximal equicontinuous factor, which is, in fact, its quotient by the
\mathbb{RP}
relation (see
[
3
, Chapter 9]
for a more in-depth discussion).

In recent years, there has been much interest in the study of nilsystems and their inverse limits, since they relate to many dynamical properties such as being characteristic and have important applications in number theory and additive combinatorics. (see for example
[
34
, Chapter 1]
and the references therein). This class, a subclass of distal systems, strictly includes equicontinuous systems while keeping many of the important and useful properties of equicontinuous systems (see for instance
[
34
, Chapter 11]
).

In their pioneering work, Host, Kra and Maass in
[
35
]
introduced the higher order regionally proximal relations
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
for
\mathbb{Z}
-actions, extending available theory for equicontinuous systems, which allows us to precisely characterize nilsystems and their inverse limits (see
Section

2.2
for the precise definitions). They showed, among other things, that in a distal minimal system,
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
is an equivalence relation and that the quotient by it is the maximal system of order
d
(that is, an inverse limit of
d
-step nilsystems).

Soon after, Shao and Ye in
[
40
]
extended these results by proving that
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
is an equivalence relation in any minimal
\mathbb{Z}
-system (and even for a general abelian group action), and that the quotient by this relation is the maximal system of order
d
.

It is classical that for abelian group actions, equicontinuity can be characterized through algebraic properties of its enveloping semigroup: indeed, they are characterized by having an abelian enveloping semigroup. It is natural to ask if there is any algebraic property, or even characterization, of nilsystems and their inverse limits. This is indeed the case: it was proven in
[
14

39

that the property of having an enveloping semigroup that is a
d

d
for
\mathbb{Z}
-actions.

All the aforementioned results belong to the class of distal systems, where it is known that the enveloping semigroup is a group. In the structural theory of minimal systems, proximal extensions play a central role and from many aspects, a proximal extension of a system behaves similarly to the factor system. However, a proximal extension of a distal system is never distal (unless the extension is an isomorphism). So, even though the system is dynamically similar to the factor, all the nice algebraic structure of the enveloping semigroup might be lost under a proximal extension. In particular, the enveloping semigroup is no longer a group.

A key aspect of the structural theory of minimal systems is the development of the so-called algebraic theory, or Galois theory, of minimal systems, proposed by Ellis and developed by several authors during several decades
[
21
,
18
,
19
,
4

1

[
12
, Chapters V and VI]
for an exhaustive exposition).

In this paper, we are interested in understanding proximal extensions of nilsystems from an algebraic point of view. To do so, we study cubic structures, Host-Kra groups and the Ellis group of a pointed minimal system and study which properties of them are related to nilsystems and their inverse limits.

Characteristic factors along cubes

2.
Background

2.1.
Topological dynamical systems

A topological dynamical system (or just a system) is a pair
(X,T)
, where
X
is a compact Hausdorff space and
T
is a discrete group acting as a group of homeomorphisms of the space
X
. Unless otherwise stated, we will assume in this paper that
T
is abelian and
X
is a compact metric space. For convenience, we write
(X,S)
instead of
(X,T)
whenever the group
T
is generated by a single homeomorphism. If
(X_{i},T)_{i\in I}
is a family of systems, the action of
T
on the product space
\prod_{i\in I}X_{i}
is defined coordinatewise:
t(x_{i})_{i\in I}=(tx_{i})_{i\in I}
for each
t\in T
and
(x_{i})_{i\in I}\in\prod_{i\in I}X_{i}
.

The system is
transitive
if there is a point
x\in X
such that its orbit
Tx:=\{tx:t\in T\}
is dense in
X
. The system is
minimal
if the orbit of any point is dense in
X
. A pair of points
(x,y)
in
X\times X
is
proximal
if there is a net
(t_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
in
T
and a point
z\in X
such that
\lim t_{\lambda}x=\lim t_{\lambda}y=z
and
(x,y)
is a
distal
pair if it is not proximal. A point
x\in X
is a
distal point
if it is proximal only to itself. The set of proximal pairs is denoted by
\mathbb{P}(X)
, and called the
proximal relation
.

An onto continuous map
\pi\colon(X,T)\to(Y,T)
between the topological dynamical systems is an
extension
(or a
factor
) if
t\pi(x)=\pi(tx)
for every
x\in X
and
t\in T
. We say that the extension is proximal (distal) if, for every
y\in Y
, every pair of points in
\pi^{-1}(y)
is proximal (distal). We say that
\pi
is
almost one-to-one
if there exists a dense
G_{\delta}
set
X_{0}\subseteq X
such that
\pi^{-1}(\pi(x))=\{x\}
for any
x\in X_{0}
. If an extension
\pi\colon(X,T)\to(Y,T)
between minimal systems is an almost one-to-one extension, then it is also a proximal extension (
[
15
, Lemma 2.1]
).

An important class of topological dynamical systems is the class of equicontinuous systems. A system
(X,T)
is
equicontinuous
if the collection of maps defined by the group
T
is an equicontinuous family. These systems can be characterized by the
regional proximal relation
. A pair
(x,y)\in X\times X
is said to be regionally proximal if there are nets
(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda},(y_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
in
X
and
(t_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
in
T
with
x_{\lambda}\to x
,
y_{\lambda}\to y
and
(t_{\lambda}x_{\lambda},t_{\lambda}y_{\lambda})\to(z,z)
, for some
z\in X
. The set of regionally proximal pairs is denoted by
\mathbb{RP}(X)
and is the
regionally proximal relation
.

One can relativize the notion of equicontinuity. We say that an extension
\pi\colon(X,T)\to(Y,T)
is equicontinuous if for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that if
(x,y)\in R_{\pi}
and
d(x,y)<\delta
then
d(tx,ty)<\varepsilon
for all
t\in T
. Here,
R_{\pi}=\{(x,y)\in X\times X:\pi(x)=\pi(y)\}
.

2.2.
Nilpotent groups, nilmanifolds and nilsystems

Let
L
be a group. For
g,h\in L
we write
[g,h]=g^{-1}h^{-1}gh
for the commutator of
g
and
h
and for
A,B\subseteq L
we write
[A,B]
for the subgroup spanned by
\{[a,b]:a\in A,b\in B\}
. The commutator subgroups
L_{j}
,
j\geq 1
, are defined inductively by setting
L_{1}=L
and
L_{j+1}=[L_{j},L]
. Let
d\geq 1
be an integer. We say that
L
is
d
-step nilpotent
if
L_{d+1}
is the trivial subgroup.

Since we will work with groups that are also topological spaces, we can also consider a topological definition of nilpotency, which will be more suitable for our purposes. Let
L
be a topological space that has a group structure. (Here, we do not assume the group to be a topological group; in fact, in many cases of interest, it will not be.) For
A,B\subseteq L
, we define
[A,B]_{\mathrm{top}}
as the closed subgroup spanned by
\{[a,b]:a\in A,b\in B\}
. Note that
[A,B]\subseteq[A,B]_{\mathrm{top}}
for all
A,B\subseteq L
. The topological commutator subgroups
L^{\mathrm{top}}_{j}
,
j\geq 1
, are similarly defined as
L^{\mathrm{top}}_{1}=L
and
L^{\mathrm{top}}_{j+1}=[L^{\mathrm{top}}_{j},L]_{\mathrm{top}}
. For
d\geq 1
an integer, we say that
L
is
d
-step topologically nilpotent
if
L^{\mathrm{top}}_{d+1}
is the trivial subgroup. Since
L_{j}\subseteq L^{\mathrm{top}}_{j}
for every
j\geq 1
, we have that if
L
is
d
-step topologically nilpotent, then
L
is also
d
-step nilpotent.

Let
L
be a
d
-step nilpotent Lie group and
\Gamma
a discrete cocompact subgroup of
L
. The compact manifold
X=L/\Gamma
is called a
d
-step nilmanifold
. We recall here that since
L
is a nilpotent Lie group, the commutator subgroups are closed, and then, in this case, the notions of
d
-step nilpotent and
d
-step topologically nilpotent coincide.

Observe that
L
acts naturally on
X
by left translation. If
T
is a topological group and
\phi:T\to L
is a continuous homomorphism, the induced action
(X,T)
is called a
nilsystem of order
d
.

2.3.
Host-Kra cube groups associated with a group

Let
d\geq 1
be an integer, and write
[d]=\{1,2,\dots,d\}
. We view an element of
\{0,1\}^{d}
, the Euclidean cube, either as a sequence
\epsilon=(\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{d})
of 0’s and 1’s; or as a subset of
[d]
. A subset
\epsilon
corresponds to the sequence
(\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{d})\in\{0,1\}^{d}
such that
i\in\epsilon
if and only if
\epsilon_{i}=1
for
i\in[d]
. For example,
\overrightarrow{0}=(0,\dots,0)\in\{0,1\}^{d}
is the same as
\emptyset\subset[d]
and
\overrightarrow{1}=(1,\dots,1)
is the same as
[d]
.

If
X
is a set, we denote
X^{2^{d}}
by
X^{[d]}
and we write a point
{\mathbb{x}}\in X^{[d]}
as
{\mathbb{x}}=(x_{\epsilon}:\epsilon\subseteq[d])
. For example, for
d=2
we have
{\mathbb{x}}=(x_{\emptyset},x_{\{1\}},x_{\{2\}},x_{\{1,2\}})
. Sometimes, it is convenient to view an element of
X^{[d]}
as a map from
\{0,1\}^{d}
to
X
. For a point
x\in X
we let
x^{[d]}\in X^{[d]}
be the diagonal point all of whose coordinates are
x
.

Let
0\leq\ell\leq d
be an integer. An
\ell
-dimensional face
of
\{0,1\}^{d}
, or equivalently a
face of codimension
d-\ell
of
\{0,1\}^{d}
, is a subset of
\{0,1\}^{d}
obtained by fixing the values of
d-\ell
coordinates. We write dim(
\alpha
) and codim(
\alpha
) for the dimension and codimension of a face
\alpha
. A
facet
of
\{0,1\}^{d}
is defined to be a face of codimension
1
. A face
\alpha
is an
upper face
if
\overrightarrow{1}\in\alpha
.

Let
L
be a group and
d\geq 1
be an integer. If
\alpha
is a face of
\{0,1\}^{d}
, for
g\in L
we define
g^{(\alpha)}\in L^{[d]}
by

\displaystyle g^{(\alpha)}(\epsilon)=\left\{\begin{matrix}g&\text{if }\epsilon\in\alpha\\
e&\text{otherwise}.\end{matrix}\right.

where
e
is the identity element of
L
.

We call the subgroup of
L^{[d]}
generated by all
g^{(\alpha)}
, where
g\in L
and
\alpha
is a facet of
\{0,1\}^{d}
, the
Host-Kra cube group
(of order
d
) associated with
L
and denote it by
\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(L)
. We call the subgroup of
L^{[d]}
generated by all
g^{(\alpha)}
, where
g\in L
and
\alpha
is an upper facet of
\{0,1\}^{d}
, the
face cube group
and denote it by
\mathcal{F}^{[d]}(L)
.

The Host-Kra and face cube groups originally appeared in
[
33
, Section 5]
and coincide with the parallelepiped groups and face groups respectively of
[
35
, Definition 3.1]
introduced for abelian actions. See also
[
31
, Appendix E]
for treatment of Host-Kra cube groups in nilpotent Lie groups.

The following proposition illustrates the connection between these two groups.

Proposition 2.1

(
[

)
.

Let

be a group and

be an integer. Then,
\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(L)=\mathcal{F}^{[d]}(L)\Delta^{[d]}(L)
, where

.

The following proposition gives a more precise description of the groups

by introducing a particular parametrization.

[

)

Let

2.5.

Let
(X,T)
be a topological dynamical system and
d\geq 1
be an integer. A pair
(x,y)\in X\times X
is said to be

(\mathbb{f}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}

\displaystyle(\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}x_{\lambda}^{[d]},\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}y_{\lambda}^{[d]})\to(x,\mathbb{a}_{*},y,\mathbb{a}_{*})

The set of regionally proximal pairs of order

is denoted by
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
and is called the

(X,T)
is a

The relation

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathbb{RP}^{[d+1]}(X)\subseteq\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathbb{RP}^{[1]}(X).

be an integer. Then,

(1)

(x,y)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
if and only if
(x,y,\dots,y)\in\bm{Q}^{[d+1]}(X)
if and only if
(x,y,\dots,y)\in\overline{\mathcal{F}^{[d+1]}(T)x^{[d+1]}}
.

(2)

\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
is an equivalence relation.

(3)

\pi\times\pi(\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X))=\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(Y)
.

(4)

\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)\subseteq R_{\pi}
if and only if
(Y,T)
is a system of order
d
.

Furthermore the quotient of
X
under
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
is the maximal system of order
d
. It follows that every factor of a system of order
d
is a system of order
d
.

2.6.
Enveloping semigroups

The
enveloping semigroup
(or
Ellis semigroup
)
E(X,T)
of a system
(X,T)
is defined as the closure of
T
in
X^{X}
endowed with the product topology. For an enveloping semigroup
E(X,T)
, the applications
E(X,T)\to E(X,T)
,
p\mapsto pq
and
p\mapsto tp
are continuous for all
q\in E(X,T)
and
t\in T
.

Ellis introduced this notion, and it has proved to be a useful tool in studying dynamical systems. The algebraic properties of
E(X,T)
can be converted into the dynamical properties of
(X,T)
and the reverse is also true. To exemplify this, we recall the following theorem.

Theorem 2.5

(see
[
3
, Chapters 3, 4 and 5]
)
.

Let
(X,T)
be a topological dynamical system. Then,

(1)

(X,T)
is distal if and only if
E(X,T)
is a group.

(2)

(X,T)
is equicontinuous if and only if
E(X,T)
is an abelian group if and only if
E(X,T)
is a group of continuous transformations.

In particular, a system of order
1
can be characterized by its enveloping semigroup. It is a natural question to ask whether it is possible to characterize the systems of order
d
by their enveloping semigroup. The following theorem answers this question in the case of
\mathbb{Z}
-actions.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
,
[
39
, Theorem 1.3]
)
.

Let
(X,S)
be a minimal topological dynamical system. Then,
(X,S)
is a system of order
d
if and only if
E(X,S)
is a
d
-step topologically nilpotent group.

(Here we recall that we use the notation
(X,S)
to refer to a
\mathbb{Z}
-action).

For a distal system, we let
(E_{d}^{\mathrm{top}}(X,T))_{d\in\mathbb{N}}
denote the sequence of topological commutators of
E(X,T)
.

Theorem 2.7

(
[
39
, Theorem 1.4]
)
.

Let
(X,S)
be a minimal system of order
d
. Then, for
k=1,\dots,d
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{RP}^{[k]}(X)=\{(x,px):x\in X,p\in E_{k+1}^{\mathrm{top}}(X,T)\}.

2.7.
The universal system

We refer to
[
12
, Sections IV, V and VI]
for the material discussed in this section. It is known that the semigroup
\beta T
, the Stone–Čech compactification of the discrete group
T
, is the universal point transitive system. That is, for every transitive system
(X,T)
and a point
x\in X
with dense orbit, there exists an extension of systems
(\beta T,T)\to(X,T)
which sends
e
, identity element of
T
, onto
x
. Therefore, by universality, there exists a unique extension
\Phi_{X}:(\beta T,T)\to(E(X,T),T)
, which is also a semigroup homomorphism, and we can interpret the
\beta T
action on
X
via this homomorphism. We say that two elements
p,q\in\beta T

coincide
on
X
if
px=qx
for all
x\in X
.

The semigroup
\beta T
admits many minimal left ideals, which coincide with the minimal subsystems. All these ideals are isomorphic to each other both as compact right topological semigroups and as minimal systems. We will fix a minimal left ideal
M
of
\beta T
. The universality of
\beta T
implies that
(M,T)
is the universal minimal system. For a semigroup, the element
v
with
v^{2}=v
is called an
idempotent
. By the Ellis Namakura theorem, the set
J(M)
of idempotents in
M
is nonempty. Moreover,
vM
is a group with identity
v
, where
v\in J(M)
. We define an equivalence relation in
J(M)
by
u\sim v
if
uv=v
and
vu=u
. We fix one such idempotent
u\in J(M)
.

If
(X,T)
is minimal, then
X=Mx
for every
x\in X
. A necessary and sufficient condition for
x
to be minimal is that
ux=x
for some
u\in J(M)
. A minimal system
(X,T)
is distal if and only if
X=vX
for every
v\in J(M)
(see
[
3
, Chapter 6]
). So, a minimal system
(X,T)
is distal iff
\Phi_{X}(M)=\Phi_{X}(vM)=E(X,T)
for every
v\in J(M)
. Also, we can characterize the proximal points: a pair
(x,y)\in X\times X
is proximal if and only if there exists a minimal left ideal
I
of
\beta T
such that
y=vx
for some
v\in J(I)
.

Let
2^{X}
be the collection of nonempty closed subsets of
X
endowed with the Hausdorff topology. The action of
T
on
2^{X}
is given by

\displaystyle tA=\{ta:a\in A\}

for each
t\in T
and
A\in 2^{X}
. This action induces another action of
\beta T
on
2^{X}
, and we denote this action by the circle operation:
p\circ A
, where
p\in\beta T
and
A\subset X
, to distinguish it from the subset
pA
. For
p\in\beta T
and
A\subseteq X
, we define
p\circ A=p\circ\overline{A}
and
p\circ\emptyset=\emptyset
, where
\overline{A}
denotes the closure of
A
in the usual topology of
X
. It holds that

\displaystyle p\circ A=\{x\in X:\exists(t_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subseteq T,\exists(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subseteq A\text{ with }t_{\lambda}\to p,t_{\lambda}x_{\lambda}\to x\}.

for all
p\in\beta T
and
A\in 2^{X}
, where
t_{\lambda}\to p
denotes the convergence of
(t_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
to
p
in the usual topology of
\beta T
. We use this notation for convergence in the usual topology throughout the paper.

The set
G=uM
is a subgroup of
M
with identity
u
. The set
\{vM:v\in J(M)\}
is a partition of
M
and each
p\in M
has a unique representation
p=vg
, where
v\in J(M)
and
g\in G
. We sometimes write
p^{-1}
for
vg^{-1}
. The group
G
plays a central role in the algebraic theory of minimal systems. It carries a
T_{1}
compact topology, called by Ellis the
\uptau
-topology
, which is weaker than the relative topology induced on
G
as a subset of
M
. This topology was first introduced by Furstenberg in
[
22
]
, and developed by Ellis, Glasner and Shapiro in
[
21
]
. For any subset
A\subseteq G
, the
\uptau
-topology is determined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{cl}_{\uptau}(A)=u(u\circ A)=(u\circ A)\cap G.

Let
(g_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a net in
G
and
g\in G
. The convergence of
(g_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
to
g
with respect to the
\uptau
-topology is denoted by
g_{\lambda}\xrightarrow{\uptau}g
. The
\uptau
-topology has many useful properties, some of which we state below for later purposes.

Proposition 2.8

(see
[
12
, Chapter V]
)
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal topological dynamical system,
(g_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a net in
G
and
p,r\in M
.

(1)

Both left and right multiplication by a fixed element of
G
are homeomorphisms with respect to the
\uptau
-topology.

(2)

Consider
(g_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
a net in
G
such that
g_{\lambda}\to p
. Then
(g_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
has a subnet
(g_{\lambda_{i}})_{i\in I}
such that
g_{\lambda_{i}}\xrightarrow{\uptau}up
.

(3)

Let
(p_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
and
(g_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be nets in
\beta T
and suppose that
p_{\lambda}g_{\lambda}\to r
and
p_{\lambda}\to p
. Then,
(g_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
has a subnet
(g_{\lambda_{i}})_{i\in I}
such that
g_{\lambda_{i}}\xrightarrow{\uptau}up^{-1}r
.

From now on, to lighten the notation, whenever we need to pass to a subnet, we will keep the notation of the original net, and the notation
g_{\lambda}\xrightarrow{\uptau}g
will implicitly mean that we may pass to a subnet to obtain the limit.

For a
\uptau
-closed subgroup
F
of
G
the
derived group

H(F)
is given by:

\displaystyle H(F)=\bigcap\{\mathrm{cl}_{\uptau}(V):V\text{ a }\uptau\text{-open neighborhood of }u\text{ in }F\}.

The group
H(F)
is a
\uptau
-closed normal subgroup and is the smallest
\uptau
-closed subgroup
H
of
F
such that
F/H
is a compact Hausdorff topological group (for the quotient topology induced by the
\uptau
-topology).

For a point
x_{0}\in uX
, the
Ellis group
of the pointed system
(X,x_{0})
is the
\uptau
-closed subgroup

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{G}}(X,x_{0})=\{g\in G:gx_{0}=x_{0}\}.

When there is no ambiguity, we omit the fixed point
x_{0}
and denote the Ellis group of
X
by
{\mathfrak{G}}(X)
. Whenever
x_{0}
is used, we will refer to the element with respect to which the group
{\mathfrak{G}}(X)
is defined. For
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,T)
an extension of minimal systems we have
{\mathfrak{G}}(X)\subseteq{\mathfrak{G}}(Y)
, where the group
{\mathfrak{G}}(Y)
is defined by the point
\pi(x_{0})
. The Ellis group is very useful when studying extensions. Below, there are a few applications of this concept.

Theorem 2.9

(see
[
3
, Chapters 10 and 14]
)
.

Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,T)
be an extension of minimal systems. Then,

(1)

\pi
is proximal if and only if
{\mathfrak{G}}(X)={\mathfrak{G}}(Y)
.

(2)

\pi
is distal if and only if
\pi^{-1}(py_{0})=p{\mathfrak{G}}(Y)x_{0}
, for all
p\in M
.

(3)

\pi
is equicontinuous if and only if
\pi
is distal and
H({\mathfrak{G}}(Y))\subseteq{\mathfrak{G}}(X)
.

(4)

If
\pi
is open, then
p\circ\pi^{-1}(y)=\pi^{-1}(py)
for every
p\in M
and
y\in Y
.

Proposition 2.10

(see
[
12
, Chapter V]
)
.

Let
\pi\colon(X,T)\to(Y,T)
an extension of minimal systems, and set
\pi_{u}=\pi|_{uX}
. Then,

(1)

\pi_{u}:uX\to uY
is
\uptau
-continuous and
\uptau
-closed.

(2)

\pi_{u}:uX\to uY
is a
\uptau
-homeomorphism if and only if
\pi
is a proximal extension.

(3)

\pi_{u}:uX\to uY
is
\uptau
-open.

Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,T)
be an extension of minimal systems. We say that
\pi
is
RIC (relatively incontractible)
if for every
p\in M

\displaystyle\pi^{-1}(py_{0})=p\circ u\pi^{-1}(y_{0})=p\circ Fx_{0},

where
F={\mathfrak{G}}(Y)
and
y_{0}=\pi(x_{0})
. We will sometimes use the following characterization of a RIC extension proved in
[
2
, Theorem A.2]
: an extension
\pi
is RIC if and only if it is open and for every
n\geq 1
the minimal points are dense in the relation

\displaystyle R_{\pi}^{n}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in X^{n}:\pi(x_{i})=\pi(x_{j}),\forall 1\leq i\leq j\leq n\}.

Note that every distal extension of minimal systems is RIC, and every distal extension of minimal systems is open. The following are relative versions of weakly mixing.

We say that
\pi
is

(1)

weakly mixing
if
(R_{\pi},T)
is transitive.

(2)

totally weakly mixing
if
(R_{\pi}^{n},T)
is transitive for every
n\geq 2
.

Note that, for RIC extensions, weak mixing is equivalent to total weak mixing.

3.
Algebraic characterization of dynamical cubes

This section is devoted to studying the Host-Kra cube groups associated with subgroups of the universal minimal system. We start with generalities and show, among other things, the characterization of proximal extensions of systems of order
d
announced in the introduction.

Recall that throughout this paper,
u
denotes the idempotent of
M
fixed in
Section

2.7
to define the group
G
.

The proof of the next lemma is similar to that of
[
26
, Proposition 1.55]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal topological dynamical system,
d\geq 1
be an integer and
\epsilon\subseteq[d]
. Let
u^{[d]}=(u,\dots,u)\in\bm{Q}^{[d]}(M)
. Then
u^{[d]}
coincides with a minimal idempotent of
\beta\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)
on
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
.

Proof.

Consider
\pi_{\epsilon}:\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)\to X_{\epsilon}=X
the projection of
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
on the
\epsilon
coordinate. We consider the action of the group
\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)
on the
\epsilon
coordinate via the projection
\pi_{\epsilon}
. Note that
\beta T
coincides with
\beta\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)
on
X_{\epsilon}
, that is, for any
p\in\beta T
there is a
\mathbb{q}\in\beta\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)
such that
px=\mathbb{q}x
for all
x\in X_{\epsilon}
.

It follows from the fact that
(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(M),\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T))
is minimal that there is a minimal idempotent
\mathbb{v}
in
\beta\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)
such that
\mathbb{v}u^{[d]}=u^{[d]}
. Consider
\mathbb{v}_{\epsilon}\in\beta T
such that
\mathbb{v}_{\epsilon}\pi_{\epsilon}(\mathbb{p})=\pi_{\epsilon}(\mathbb{vp})
for all
\mathbb{p}\in\bm{Q}^{[d]}(M)
. Observe that
\mathbb{v}_{\epsilon}
is a minimal idempotent on
M
. Let
u^{\prime}\in J(\beta Tv_{\epsilon})
be a minimal idempotent equivalent to
u
, then

\displaystyle u^{\prime}=uu^{\prime}=\mathbb{v}_{\epsilon}uu^{\prime}=\mathbb{v}_{\epsilon}u^{\prime}=\mathbb{v}_{\epsilon},

and it follows that
u^{[d]}\mathbb{v}=\mathbb{v}
on
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(M)
. Thus, we have
u^{[d]}\in\beta\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)\mathbb{v}
on
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(M)
. Since
\beta\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)\mathbb{v}
is a minimal left ideal, we conclude that
u^{[d]}
coincide with a minimal idempotent of
\beta\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)
on
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(M)
. Therefore,
u^{[d]}
coincides with a minimal idempotent on
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

One can alternatively prove
Lemma

3.1
using the proof of
[
40
, Proposition 4.8]
and the fact that minimal idempotents in
E(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(M))
can be lifted to minimal idempotents in
\beta\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)
.

Now, we define
M(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T))=\beta\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)u^{[d]}
as the minimal left ideal of
\beta\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)
that contains
u^{[d]}
, and
G(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T))=u^{[d]}\beta\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)u^{[d]}
as the group with identity
u^{[d]}
. Observe that
G(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T))
coincides with
G
on
X_{\epsilon}
for any
\epsilon\subseteq[d]
and
(X,T)
minimal system.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal topological dynamical system and
d\geq 1
be an integer. Then,
\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(G)\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)\subseteq u^{[d]}\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
. Moreover, for each
{\mathbb{x}}\in\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{cl}_{\uptau}(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(G)){\mathbb{x}}=u^{[d]}\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X),

where the closure is with respect to the
\uptau
-topology in
G(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T))
.

Proof.

Let
g\in G
,
{\mathbb{x}}\in\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
and
\alpha
be a facet of
\{0,1\}^{d}
. Let
(t_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subseteq T
be a net such that
t_{\lambda}\to g
. Then, we have
t_{\lambda}^{(\alpha)}\to g^{(\alpha)}
. Let
({\mathbb{s}}_{i})_{i\in I}\subseteq\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)
be such that
{\mathbb{s}}_{i}x_{0}^{[d]}\to{\mathbb{x}}
. Since
\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)
is a group we get that
(t_{\lambda}^{(\alpha)}{\mathbb{s}}_{i})_{i\in I}\subseteq\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)
, and then we have
(t_{\lambda}^{(\alpha)}{\mathbb{x}})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subseteq\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
. Therefore, we deduce that
g^{(\alpha)}{\mathbb{x}}\in u^{[d]}\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
. The set
\{\bm{g}\in\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(G):\bm{g}\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)\subseteq u^{[d]}\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)\}
is clearly a subgroup of
\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(G)
and it contains the facets. Hence, it is all of
\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(G)
. We deduce
\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(G)\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)\subseteq u^{[d]}\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
.

Let
{\mathbb{x}}\in\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
. Then, there is
({\mathbb{t}}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)
a net such that
{\mathbb{t}}_{\lambda}x_{0}^{[d]}
converge to
{\mathbb{x}}
(recall whenever
x_{0}
is used, we will refer to the element with respect to which the group
{\mathfrak{G}}(X)
is defined). By
Proposition

2.2
, there is
\mathbb{h}\in T^{[d]}
such that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{t}}_{\lambda}=\prod_{i=1}^{2^{d}}\mathbb{h}_{i}^{(\alpha_{i})},

where
\alpha_{i}
are the faces of the
Proposition

2.2
. Therefore, it follows that

\displaystyle u^{[d]}{\mathbb{t}}_{\lambda}=\prod_{i}^{2^{d}}(u\mathbb{h}_{i})^{(\alpha_{i})}.

Note that,

\displaystyle u^{[d]}{\mathbb{t}}_{\lambda}x_{0}^{[d]}={\mathbb{t}}_{\lambda}u^{[d]}x_{0}^{[d]}\to{\mathbb{x}}.

Thus, we get that
u^{[d]}{\mathbb{t}}_{\lambda}x_{0}^{[d]}\xrightarrow{\uptau}u^{[d]}{\mathbb{x}}
. Since
\prod_{i}^{2^{d}}(u\mathbb{h}_{i})^{(\alpha_{i})}
is an element of
\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(G)
, we conclude that
u^{[d]}{\mathbb{x}}\in\mathrm{cl}_{\uptau}(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(G))x_{0}^{[d]}
.
∎

Note that this proof of the theorem also allows us to extend it to general group actions, which are not necessarily abelian, for proximal extensions of distal systems. Also, when
X=M
and
x_{0}=u
, it can be shown that
\mathrm{cl}_{\uptau}(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(G))=G(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T))
. Moreover, we have the following corollary:

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
d\geq 1
be an integer. Then,
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(M)=M(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T))
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(M)\subseteq M(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T))
. Conversely, we have

\displaystyle M(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T))=\bigcup_{\mathbb{v}\in J(M(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)))}\mathbb{v}G(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)).

Note that
G(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T))\subseteq\mathrm{cl}_{\uptau}(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(G))
. Indeed, let
\mathbf{g}\in G(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T))
. Then there exists a net
({\mathbb{t}}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)
such that
{\mathbb{t}}_{\lambda}\to\mathbf{g}
. Thus, it follows that
{\mathbb{t}}_{\lambda}u^{[d]}\xrightarrow{\uptau}\mathbf{g}
, and hence
\mathbf{g}\in\mathrm{cl}_{\uptau}(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(G))
.

Therefore, by
Theorem

3.3
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\bigcup_{\mathbb{v}\in J(M(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)))}\mathbb{v}\mathrm{cl}_{\uptau}(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(G))=\bigcup_{\mathbb{v}\in J(M(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)))}\mathbb{v}\bm{Q}^{[d]}(M)=\bm{Q}^{[d]}(M).

Hence, we conclude that
M(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T))\subseteq\bm{Q}^{[d]}(M)
.
∎

, (using that

Using the algebraic characterization of the dynamical cubes, we can show an algebraic characterization of proximal extensions of equicontinuous systems.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal topological dynamical system. Then,
(X,T)
is a proximal extension of its maximal equicontinuous factor if and only if
H(G)
is trivial on
X
.

Proof.

Suppose
(X,T)
is an equicontinuous system. By
Theorem

2.9
, we get that
H(G)\subseteq{\mathfrak{G}}(X)
. Since every element
g\in G
is continuous in
X
, we conclude that
H(G)
is trivial on
X
. By
Proposition

2.10
, we deduce that
H(G)
is trivial on proximal extensions of an equicontinuous system.

Conversely, suppose that
H(G)
is trivial on
X
. Let
(x,y)\in\mathbb{RP}(X)
. By
Theorem

3.3
, there exist
(g_{0}^{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda},(g_{1}^{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda},(g_{2}^{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subseteq G
nets such that

\displaystyle g_{0}^{\lambda}x

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\uptau}ux

\displaystyle g_{1}^{\lambda}g_{0}^{\lambda}x

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\uptau}ux

\displaystyle g_{2}^{\lambda}g_{0}^{\lambda}x

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\uptau}ux

\displaystyle g_{2}^{\lambda}g_{1}^{\lambda}g_{0}^{\lambda}x

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\uptau}uy.

Let
p_{0},p_{1},p_{2}\in M
be such that
g_{i}^{\lambda}\to p_{i}
for each
i=0,1,2
. By
Proposition

2.8
, we have
g_{i}^{\lambda}\xrightarrow{\uptau}up_{i}
for each
i=0,1,2
. Then, as
G/H(G)
is a compact Hausdorff topological group for the quotient topology induced by the
\uptau
-topology, we obtain

Theorem 3.7

Definition 3.8
.

Let

Lemma 3.9
.

For each integer

is a normal subgroup of

Proof.

We proceed by induction on

Let

(g_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda},(h_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
in

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\uptau}g

\displaystyle h_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\uptau}h.

4.
Regionally proximal relation for distal systems and their proximal extensions

In this section, we provide an algebraic characterization of the regionally proximal relation for distal systems and their proximal extensions. Next, we extend this characterization to any minimal system. We begin by outlining Qiu and Zhao’s proof of the algebraic characterization of
\mathbb{RP}^{[j]}
for systems of order
d
, where
d
and
j
are integers with
d,j\geq 1
, extending their proof to proximal extensions of distal systems using the Galois theory of minimal systems.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal topological dynamical system and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. We define

\displaystyle R_{d}^{\uptau}(X)=\{(x,vgx):x\in X,M^{\prime}\subseteq\beta T\text{ minimal left ideal},v,u^{\prime}\in J(M^{\prime})\text{ s.t }u\sim u^{\prime},g\in G(u^{\prime},M^{\prime})_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}\}.

where for a left minimal ideal
M^{\prime}
and
v\in J(M^{\prime})
,
G(v,M^{\prime})\coloneqq vM^{\prime}
.

We will see later that this relation generalizes the definition given by Qiu and Zhao for systems of order
d
in
[
39
]
. Note that if
G
is a group
d
-step
\uptau
-topologically nilpotent on a minimal system
(X,T)
, we have
R_{d}^{\uptau}(X)=\mathbb{P}(X)
. Consequently, if
R_{d}^{\uptau}(X)=\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
, then
Question

3.13
has a positive answer. It is therefore useful to identify sufficient conditions for which
R_{d}^{\uptau}(X)=\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
. Later, we will show that a sufficient condition is that
X
is a proximal extension of a distal system.

Observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq R_{d+1}^{\uptau}(X)\subseteq R_{d}^{\uptau}(X)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq R_{1}^{\uptau}(X).

Note that
R^{*}_{d}(X)
, the smallest closed equivalence relation which contains
R_{d}^{\uptau}(X)
, for a minimal system
(X,T)
and
d\geq 1
an integer satisfies the following property:
X/R^{*}_{d}(X)
is the maximal distal factor of
X
such that
G_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}
is trivial on it. Moreover, the relation
R_{d}^{*}
coincides with the regionally proximal relation of order
d
, as can be seen below.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(X,S)
be a minimal topological dynamical system and
d\geq 1
be an integer. Then,
R_{d}^{\uptau}(X)\subseteq\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
. Moreover,
R_{d}^{*}(X)=\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
.

Proof.

Consider
\pi:X\to X/\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
the quotient map. Let
(x,y)\in R_{d}^{\uptau}(X)
, note that
\pi\times\pi(x,y)\in R_{d}^{\uptau}(X/\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X))
. It follows from
Theorem

3.12
that
R_{d}^{\uptau}(X/\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X))=\Delta
. Therefore, we have
\pi(x)=\pi(y)
, that is,
(x,y)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
.

Since
R_{d}^{\uptau}(X)\subseteq\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
, we get that
X/R_{d}^{*}(X)
is an extension of
X/\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
. Note that
X/R_{d}^{*}(X)
is a distal system such that its enveloping semigroup is a
d
-step topologically nilpotent, so we have that
X/R_{d}^{*}(X)
is a system of order
d
. Therefore, by
Theorem

2.6
, we conclude that
R_{d}^{*}(X)=\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
.
∎

By the following results, we can show that
R_{d}^{\uptau}(X)
is a closed relation for proximal extensions of distal systems.

Theorem 4.3

(see
[
3
, Chapter 4]
)
.

Let
T
be a locally compact Hausdorff space with a group structure for which group multiplication is separately continuous, and suppose
T
acts on the compact Hausdorff space
X
in a separately continuous manner. Then, the action of
T
on
X
is jointly continuous.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
F\leq G
be a
\uptau
-closed subgroup and

such that
H(F)\leq K
. Then,
\mathrm{cl}_{\uptau}(K)\unlhd F
.

Proof.

Let
g,h\in\mathrm{cl}_{\uptau}(K)
. Let
(g_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
and
(h_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be nets in
K
such that
g_{\lambda}\xrightarrow{\uptau}g
and
h_{\lambda}\xrightarrow{\uptau}h
.

Since
F/H(F)
is a Hausdorff topological group, therefore there is a

such that

\displaystyle g_{\lambda}h_{\lambda}\xrightarrow{\uptau}ghr.

Note that
(g_{\lambda}h_{\lambda}r^{-1})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
converges to

with respect to
\uptau

It follows from the facts that

.

Let

be such that
qx=wgrpx
. Then,

\displaystyle(x,y)=(x,wgrpx)=(x,wgrx)\in R_{d}^{\uptau}(X).

Hence, we conclude that
R_{d}^{\uptau}(X)
is closed.
∎

By
Proposition

4.2
and
Theorem

4.6
, we can conclude the following result.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
(X,S)
be a topological dynamical system such that it is a proximal extension of a distal system and
d\geq 1
be an integer. Then,
R_{d}^{\uptau}(X)=\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
.

Remark 4.8
.

If we did not know a priori that
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
is an equivalence relation for all systems, we may still be able to deduce
Corollary

4.7
if
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
is known to be an equivalence relation for distal systems.

By
Corollary

4.7
, we have the following natural question:

Question 4.9
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal topological dynamical system. Is it true that
R_{d}^{\uptau}(X)=\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
?

Now, we will show that
R_{d}^{\tau}
coincide with the relation defined by Qiu and Zhao in
[
39
]
for systems of order
j
, where
d,j\geq 1
are integers.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
(X,S)
be a minimal system of order
d
. Then,
G_{k}^{\mathrm{top}}x=G_{k}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}x
for each
x\in X
and
k=1,\dots,d+1
.

Proof.

We show it by induction on
d
. Note that the statement is true for
d=1
.

Let
d>1
be an integer and
x\in X
; suppose the statement is true for each
j\leq d
. By
Theorem

3.12
, we have
G_{d+1}^{\mathrm{top}}x=G_{d+1}^{\uptau-\mathrm{top}}x=\{x\}
, so it suffices to prove the statement for
k=1,\dots,d
. Let
g\in G_{k}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}
,
k\in\{1,\dots,d\}
and
\pi:X\to X/\mathbb{RP}^{[d-1]}(X)
be the quotient map. Since
X/\mathbb{RP}^{[d-1]}(X)
is a system of order
d-1
, then, by the induction hypothesis, there is a
g^{\prime}\in G_{k}^{\mathrm{top}}
such that

\displaystyle\pi(gx)=g\pi(x)=g^{\prime}\pi(x)=\pi(g^{\prime}x)

Therefore, we get that
(gx,g^{\prime}x)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d-1]}(X)
. It follows from
Theorem

2.7
that there is a
r\in G_{d}^{\mathrm{top}}
such that
gx=g^{\prime}rx
. Thus, we have
G_{k}^{\mathrm{top}}x=G_{k}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}x
. By
Theorem

3.12
, we conclude that the statement is true for each
k=1,\dots,d+1
.
∎

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
(X,S)
be a minimal topological dynamical system, such that it is a proximal extension of a system of order
d
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{RP}^{[j]}(X)=R_{j}^{\uptau}(X)=\{(x,vgx):v\in J(M),g\in G_{j+1}^{\mathrm{top}}\}.

for each
j=1,\dots,d+1
.

Proof.

Let
j=1,\dots,d+1
. By
Corollary

4.7
, we have
\mathbb{RP}^{[j]}(X)=R_{j}^{\uptau}(X)
. Furthermore, it follows from
Lemma

4.10
that

\displaystyle R_{j}^{\uptau}(X)=\{(x,vgx):v\in J(M),g\in G_{j+1}^{\mathrm{top}}\},

which completes the proof.
∎

We can extend the results of systems of order
d
to systems of order
\infty
. Let
(X,T)
be a topological dynamical system. A pair
(x,y)\in X\times X
is said to be
regionally proximal of order
\infty
if it is regionally proximal of order
d
for all
d\geq 1
. The set of regionally proximal pairs of order
\infty
is denoted by
\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(X)
and is called the
regionally proximal relation of order
\infty
. We say that
(X,T)
is a
system of order
\infty
if
\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(X)
coincides with the diagonal relation.

Dong, Donoso, Maass, Shao and Ye studied this relation
[
13
]
and proved that a system of order
\infty
is an inverse limit of nilsystems for
\mathbb{Z}
-actions. It is easy to prove that a system of order
\infty
is an inverse limit of its maximal factor systems of order
d
, for
d\geq 1
.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
(X,S)
be a minimal system of order
\infty
. Then,
G_{j}^{\mathrm{top}}x=G_{j}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}x
for each
x\in X
and
j\geq 1
.

Proof.

Since
(X,S)
is system of order
\infty
, then, without loss of generality,
X=\underset{\leftarrow}{\lim}X/\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)\penalty\
, that is, there are
\pi_{d}:X/\mathbb{RP}^{[d+1]}(X)\to X/\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
extensions, such that

\displaystyle X=\left\{(x_{d})_{d\geq 1}\in\prod_{d\geq 1}X/\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X):\pi_{d}(x_{d+1})=x_{d}\right\}.

Let
j\geq 1
be an integer and let
g\in G_{j}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}
. Note that for
(x_{d})_{d\geq 1}\in X
we have
g(x_{d})_{d}=(gx_{d})_{d\geq 1}
. By
Theorem

3.12
, we get that
G_{j}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}x_{d}=x_{d}
for each
d>j
. Moreover, by
Lemma

4.10
, there is a
g^{\prime}\in G_{j}^{\mathrm{top}}
such that
gx_{j}=g^{\prime}x_{j}
. Then,

\displaystyle gx_{j-1}=\pi_{j-1}(gx_{j})=\pi_{j-1}(g^{\prime}x_{j})=g^{\prime}x_{j-1}.

Inductively, we can conclude that
g(x_{d})_{d\geq 1}=g^{\prime}(x_{d})_{d\geq 1}
.∎

Therefore, by
Proposition

2.10
,
Lemma

4.12
and
Corollary

4.7
, we can conclude the following result.

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
(X,S)
be a minimal topological dynamical system such that it is a proximal extension of a system of order
\infty
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)=\{(x,vgx):v\in J(M),g\in G_{d+1}^{\mathrm{top}}\}.

for every
d\geq 1
.

Using the enveloping semigroup, we show an algebraic characterization of systems of order
\infty
.

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
(X,S)
be a distal minimal topological dynamical system. Then,
(X,S)
is a system of order
\infty
if and only if
\bigcap_{d\in\mathbb{N}}E_{d}^{\mathrm{top}}(X,S)
is trivial.

Proof.

Suppose that
(X,S)
is a system of order
\infty
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(X)=\bigcap_{d\geq 1}\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)=\bigcap_{d\geq 1}R_{d}^{\uptau}(X)=\Delta.

Let
p\in\bigcap_{d\geq 1}E_{d}^{\mathrm{top}}(X,S)
. By
Lemma

4.12
and distality of
(X,S)
, we get that
(x,px)\in R_{d}^{\uptau}(X)
for each
x\in X
and
d\geq 1
. Thus, we have
px=x
for all
x\in X
. Therefore, we deduce that
\bigcap_{d\geq 1}E_{d}^{\mathrm{top}}(X,S)
is trivial.

Suppose that
\bigcap_{d\geq 1}E_{d}^{\mathrm{top}}(X,S)
is trivial. Let
x\in X
and let
(x,px)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(X)
, where
p\in E(X,S)
. In particular, we have
(x,px)\in R_{d}^{\uptau}(X)
for each
d\geq 1
. Therefore, there is
g_{d}\in G_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}
such that
px=g_{d}x
.

Note that
G_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}=\bigcap_{j=1}^{d+1}G_{j}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}
and
G_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}
is a closed set. Then, there is a
y\in\bigcap_{j\geq 1}G_{j}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}x
such that
g_{d}x\to x
. Since
\bigcap_{d\geq 1}E_{d}^{\mathrm{top}}(X,S)
is trivial, we get that
y=x
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle px=g_{d}x\to y=x.

Hence, it follows that
px=x
, and we conclude that
\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(X)=\Delta
.
∎

5.
Applications

5.1.
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
is an equivalence relation

In this subsection, we give a novel proof that
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
is an equivalence relation for any minimal system establishing an algebraic characterization of regionally proximal relations for arbitrary minimal systems, not necessarily proximal extensions of distal systems. To prove this, we need to describe the Ellis group of the maximal factor of order
d
. It is also worth noting that throughout this section, we will only assume that
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
is an equivalence relation on distal systems.

For a topological group
T
, let
M_{dis}
be the associated universal minimal distal system and let
D={\mathfrak{G}}(M_{dis})
. The group
D
is a
\uptau
-closed normal subgroup of
G
and is generated by
\{g\in G:(p,gp)\in\overline{\mathbb{P}(M)}\text{ for each }p\in M\}
. This group has the property that a minimal topological dynamical system
(X,T)
is a proximal extension of a distal system if
D\subseteq{\mathfrak{G}}(X)
. (For more details on this group, see
[
6
]
). By the following results, we can explicitly describe the Ellis group of the maximal factor of order
d
for a minimal system.

Lemma 5.1

(see
[
3
, Chapter 14]
)
.

Let
A,F
be
\uptau
-closed subgroups of
G
with
F
normal in
G
. Then,

(1)

AF
is a
\uptau
-closed subgroup of
G
.

(2)

If
g\in G
,
g\circ A=u\circ A
if and only if
g\in A
.

Theorem 5.2

(see
[
3
, Chapter 10]
)
.

Let
(X,T)
and
(Y,T)
be minimal systems, with
Y
distal. Then
{\mathfrak{G}}(X)\subseteq{\mathfrak{G}}(Y)
if and only if there is an extension
\pi:X\to Y
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
(X,S)
be a minimal distal topological dynamical system with
A={\mathfrak{G}}(X)
and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. Then,
{\mathfrak{G}}(X/\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X))=AG_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}
.

Proof.

Since
G_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}
is trivial on
X/\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
, we get that
G_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{G}}(X/\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X))
. Since
X/\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
is a factor of
X
, then we have
A\subseteq{\mathfrak{G}}(X/\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X))
.

It follows from
Lemma

5.1
that
AG_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}
is a
\uptau
-closed subgroup of
G
. Consider
\Pi(AG_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}})=\{p\circ AG_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}:p\in M\}
. Note that
(\Pi(AG_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}),S)
has a closed proximal relation, so its maximal distal factor
(Y,S)
satisfies that
{\mathfrak{G}}(Y)=AG_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}
. Moreover, by
Lemma

5.1
, for each
p\in M
, we have

\displaystyle gp\circ AG_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}=upg^{\prime}\circ AG_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}=up(g^{\prime}\circ AG_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}})=up\circ AG_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}},

where
g^{\prime}\in G_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}
satisfies that
gp=upg^{\prime}
. Then, we get that
G_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}
is trivial on this system.

It follows from
Theorem

5.2
that
Y
is an extension of
X/\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
and is a factor of
X
. By
Proposition

4.2
, we have that
X/\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
is the maximal distal factor of
X
with
G_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}
trivial on it. Hence, we conclude that
Y=X/\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
.
∎

Note that by
Proposition

3.6
, using a similar proof to that of
Theorem

5.3
, we can derive
Proposition

5.4
and
Proposition

5.5
below. Note that
Proposition

5.4
appears as an ingredient in the proof of the structure theorem for minimal systems (see
[
3
, Chapter 14, page 213]
, while
Proposition

5.5
can be deduced combining
[
6
, Theorem 4.7, Lemma 5.2 and Theorem 5.3]
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal topological dynamical system with
A={\mathfrak{G}}(X)
. Then,
{\mathfrak{G}}(X/\mathbb{RP}(X))=AH(G)
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal topological dynamical system with
A={\mathfrak{G}}(X)
. Then,
{\mathfrak{G}}(X_{dis})=AD
, where
X_{dis}
is the maximal distal factor of
X
.

Since
X
has the same maximal factor of order
d
as its maximal distal factor, we get the following result.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
(X,S)
be a minimal topological dynamical system with
A={\mathfrak{G}}(X)
. Then,
{\mathfrak{G}}(X_{d})=ADG_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}
, where
X_{d}
is the maximal factor of order
d
.

Note that
Corollary

5.6
can be extended to any group for which having a
d
-step topologically nilpotent enveloping semigroup is equivalent to being a system of order
d
. In particular, for
d=1
, this characterization is already known in the case of abelian actions on Hausdorff compact spaces (which are not necessarily metric), implying that
Corollary

5.6
also holds in this context. In particular, considering the case where
X=M
, we obtain that
G_{2}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}\subseteq H(G)
. We do not know whether the reverse inclusion holds. If this inclusion were true, then by
Proposition

3.6
we would obtain a positive answer to
Question

3.13
in the case
d=1
.

Since
X/\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(X)
is the inverse limit of
(X/\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X))_{d\geq 1}
for a minimal system
(X,T)
, we can deduce the following results using
Theorem

5.3
.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
(X,S)
be a minimal topological dynamical system with
{\mathfrak{G}}(X)=A
. Then,
{\mathfrak{G}}(X/\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(X))=AD\bigcap_{d\geq 1}G_{d}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}
.

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
(X,S)
be a distal minimal topological dynamical system with
{\mathfrak{G}}(X)=A
. Then,
{\mathfrak{G}}(X/\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(X))=A\bigcap_{d\geq 1}G_{d}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}
.

Note that the group
D
allows us to characterize the maximal factor of order
d
(modulo proximal extensions), so it is expected to have a connection to the regionally proximal relation of order
d
. We can establish a characterization of this relation in terms of the group
D
. To prove this characterization, we will not use the strong property that the face transformations acting on the diagonal points are the unique minimal subsystem of the dynamical cube (
Theorem

2.3
, item
(3)
). We believe that our proof strategy could be applied in different contexts, in which the aforementioned condition
(3)
may not hold. For example, in the case of directional dynamical cubes
[
17
,
8
]
. By using the following result, we can give an algebraic characterization of
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
.

Lemma 5.9

(
[
35
, Lemma 3.3]
)
.

Let
(X,S)
be a minimal topological dynamical system and
d\geq 1
be an integer. Then
(x,y)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
if and only if there exists
a_{*}\in X^{2^{d}-1}
such that
(x,a_{*},y,a_{*})\in\bm{Q}^{[d+1]}(X)
.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
(X,S)
be a minimal topological dynamical system and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. Then, the relation
R_{d}^{\mathrm{dis}}(X)
defined as

\displaystyle R_{d}^{\mathrm{dis}}(X):=\{(x,vghx):v\in J(M),g\in G_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}},h\in D,x\in X\},

equals
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
. Moreover,
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
is an equivalence relation.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
and let
g\in G
such that
(p,gp)\in\overline{\mathbb{P}(M)}
for every
p\in M
. In particular, we have
(x,gx)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
. It follows from
Lemma

5.9
that there exists
\mathbb{a}_{*}\in X^{2^{d}-1}
such that
(x,\mathbb{a}_{*},gx,\mathbb{a}_{*})\in\bm{Q}^{[d+1]}(X)
. Therefore, by
Theorem

3.3
, we have
(ux,\mathbb{u}\mathbb{a}_{*},ugx,\mathbb{u}\mathbb{a}_{*})\in\mathrm{cl}_{\uptau}(\mathcal{HK}^{[d+1]}(G))x_{0}^{[d+1]}
, where
\mathbb{u}=(u,\dots,u)\in G^{2^{d}-1}
. Since
D
is generated by
\{g\in G:(p,gp)\in\overline{\mathbb{P}(M)}\text{ for every }p\in M\}
, we get that
(ux,\mathbb{u}\mathbb{a}_{*},hx,\mathbb{u}\mathbb{a}_{*})\in\mathrm{cl}_{\uptau}(\mathcal{HK}^{[d+1]}(G))x_{0}^{[d+1]}
for each
h\in D
, so
(ux,hx)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
for every
h\in D
.

Let
g\in G_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}
and let
(x,y)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
. By
Lemma

5.9
, there exists
\mathbb{a}_{*}\in X^{2^{d}-1}
such that
(x,\mathbb{a}_{*},y,\mathbb{a}_{*})\in\bm{Q}^{[d+1]}(X)
. Since
g\in G_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}
, by
Lemma

3.10
and
Theorem

3.3
, it follows that
(ux,\mathbb{g}\mathbb{a}_{*},gy,\mathbb{g}\mathbb{a}_{*})\in\bm{Q}^{[d+1]}(X)
, where
\mathbb{g}=(g,\dots,g)\in G^{2^{d}-1}
. Therefore,
Lemma

5.9
implies that
(ux,gy)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
. Thus, by a preceding argument, we deduce that
(ux,ghx)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
for each
x\in X
,
h\in D
and
g\in G_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}
.

Now, let
(x,y)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
and
v,w\in J(M)
. By
Lemma

5.9
, there exists
\mathbb{a}_{*}\in X^{2^{d}-1}
such that
(x,\mathbb{a}_{*},y,\mathbb{a}_{*})\in\bm{Q}^{[d+1]}(X)
. It follows from
Lemma

3.5
that
(vx,\mathbb{w}\mathbb{a}_{*},wy,\mathbb{w}\mathbb{a}_{*})\in\bm{Q}^{[d+1]}(X)
, where
\mathbb{w}=(w,\dots,w)\in M^{2^{d}-1}
. Therefore,
Lemma

5.9
implies that
(vx,wy)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
. Thus, we obtain that
(x,vghx)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
for each
x\in X
,
v\in J(M)
,
h\in D
and
g\in G_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}
, and hence we conclude that
R_{d}^{dis}(X)\subseteq\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
.

Let
(x,y)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
and let
\pi:X\to X_{d}
the factor map to the maximal factor of order
d
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\pi\times\pi(x,y)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X_{d})=R_{d}^{\uptau}(X_{d})=\Delta.

Let
p,q\in M
be such that
y=px_{0}
and
x=qx_{0}
. Since
\pi(y)=\pi(x)
, we obtain that
up^{-1}q\pi(x_{0})=\pi(x_{0})
. It follows from
Theorem

5.3
that
up^{-1}q\in{\mathfrak{G}}(X_{d})=G_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}DA
. In particular, we have
up^{-1}qx_{0}\in G_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}Dx_{0}
. Thus, there exists
g\in D
and
h\in G_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}
such that
upx_{0}=hgqx_{0}
. Let
v\in J(M)
be such that
vy=y
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle y=vupx_{0}=vhgqx_{0}=vhgx,

completing the proof.

To conclude that
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
is an equivalence relation, it suffices to show that
R_{d}^{dis}
is an equivalence relation. The proof of this is identical to that in
Theorem

4.6
, but we include it here for completeness. Let
(x,vgx),(x,whx)\in R_{d}^{dis}(X)
, where
v,w\in J(M)
and
g,h\in G_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}D
. Note that
hg^{-1}\in G_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}D
. Then, we have

\displaystyle(vgx,whx)=(vgx,w(hg^{-1})vgx)\in R_{d}^{dis}(X).

Therefore,
R_{d}^{dis}(X)
is an equivalence relation.
∎

Let us note that, for the characterization of the above theorem, we only use the regionally proximal relations in distal systems and then extend them to an arbitrary minimal system using the group
D
. Observe that
Theorem

5.10
can be extended to any group such that in which having a
d
-step topologically nilpotent enveloping semigroup is equivalent to being a system of order
d
.

Using the algebraic characterization of the regionally proximal relation, we can give a new and short proof of the lifting property of
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
.

Corollary 5.11
.

Let
\pi\colon(X,S)\to(Y,S)
be an extension of minimal systems and
d\geq 1
be an integer. Then
\pi\times\pi(\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X))=\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(Y)
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\pi\times\pi(\mathbb{RP}^{[d]})(X)\subseteq\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(Y)
, so we only need to prove the reverse inclusion. Let
(y,y^{\prime})\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(Y)
. By
Theorem

5.10
, there exists
v\in J(M)
,
d\in D
and
g\in G_{d+1}^{\uptau\mathrm{-top}}
such that
y^{\prime}=vdgy
. Consider
x\in X
such that
y=\pi(x)
. Then, by
Theorem

5.10
we have
(x,vdgx)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
, and
\pi(vdgx)=y^{\prime}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

5.2.
Topological characteristic factor along cubes

The counterpart of characteristic factors in topological dynamics was first studied by Glasner in
[
24
]
. In the recent work by Cai and Shao in
[
10
]
, they introduced the concept of the topological cubic characteristic factor of order
d
.

Definition 5.12
.

Let
(X,T)
be a topological dynamical system and let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,T)
be an extension. A subset
L
of
X
is called
\pi
-saturated
if
L=\pi^{-1}(\pi(L))
.

The definition of the topological cubic characteristic factor of order
d
is as follows:

Definition 5.13
.

Let
(X,T)
be a topological dynamical system and let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,T)
be an extension. The system
(Y,T)
is said to be a
topological cubic characteristic factor of order
d
if there exists a dense
G_{\delta}
set
X_{0}
of
X
such that for each
x\in X_{0}
the set
\rho_{*}(\overline{\mathcal{F}^{[d]}(T)x^{[d]}})
is
\pi^{2^{d}-1}
-saturated, where
\rho_{*}:X^{[d]}\to X^{2^{d}-1}
is the projection on the latter
2^{d}-1
coordinates.

Cai and Shao
[
10
]
established that, up to proximal extensions, the maximal factor of order

5.3.
Structure theorem of dynamical cubes

In this subsection, we will show that for abelian actions, a minimal system
(X,T)
and its associated system of cubes
(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X),\mathcal{HK}(T))
share a similar structural description, which we will clarify further below.

A minimal system
(X,T)
is called a
strictly PI system
if there exists an ordinal
\eta
(which is countable when
X
is metrizable) and a family of systems
\{(W_{\iota},T)\}_{\iota\leq\eta}
satisfying the following conditions: (i)
W_{0}
is the trivial system, (ii) for every
\iota<\eta

\phi_{\iota}:W_{\iota+1}\to W_{\iota}

\nu\leq\eta

W_{\nu}
is the inverse limit of the systems
\{W_{\iota}\}_{\iota<\nu}
, and (iv)
W_{\eta}=X
. A minimal system
(X,T)
is called a
PI-system
if there exists a strictly PI system
X_{\infty}
and a proximal extension
\theta:X_{\infty}\to X
.

If, in the definition of PI systems, the extensions
\phi_{\iota}:W_{\iota+1}\to W_{\iota}
are either almost one-to-one or equicontinuous, we obtain the notion of
AI systems.
Note that any AI system is a PI system. If we further ask that all the
\phi_{\iota}
are equicontinuous (meaning that no nontrivial proximal extensions are allowed), we obtain the notion of
I systems
. In this terminology, the structure theorem for distal systems can be stated as follows:

Theorem 5.22

(
[
22
, Theorems 2.3 and 2.4]
)
.

A metric minimal system is distal if and only if it is an
I
-system.

A minimal system is called
point distal
if there exists a point that is proximal only to itself. The Veech’s structure theorem for point distal systems then states:

Theorem 5.23

(
[
41
, Theorem 7.2]
)
.

A metric minimal system is point distal if and only if it is an
AI
-system.

be a RIC weakly mixing extension of minimal systems. Then, for each
y\in Y
and
d\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle(\pi^{-1}(y))^{[d]}=(\pi^{-1}(y))^{2^{d}}\subseteq\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X).

Proposition 5.32
.

Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,T)
be a RIC extension of minimal systems. Then,
\pi^{[d]}:(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X),\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T))\to(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(Y),\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T))
is a RIC extension.

Proof.

Since
\pi
is RIC, we have

\displaystyle(\pi^{[d]})^{-1}(y_{0}^{[d]})=(u\circ Fx_{0})^{[d]}\cap\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)=u^{[d]}\circ F^{[d]}x_{0}^{[d]}\cap\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X),

where
y_{0}=\pi(x_{0})
and

. Since
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
is an
\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)
-invariant closed set, we have
u^{[d]}\circ\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)=\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
. It follows from
Theorem

3.3
that
u^{[d]}\circ\mathrm{cl}_{\uptau}(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(G))
is contained in
u^{[d]}\circ\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
. Then,

\displaystyle u^{[d]}\circ(F^{[d]}\cap\mathrm{cl}_{\uptau}(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(G)))x_{0}^{[d]}\subseteq u^{[d]}\circ F^{[d]}x_{0}^{[d]}\cap\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)

Let
{\mathbb{x}}\in u^{[d]}\circ F^{[d]}x_{0}^{[d]}\cap\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
. Consider
{\mathbb{p}}\in u^{[d]}\circ F^{[d]}
,
{\mathbb{v}}\in J(M(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)))
and
{\mathbb{g}}\in\mathrm{cl}_{\uptau}(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(G))
such that
x={\mathbb{p}}x_{0}^{[d]}={\mathbb{v}}{\mathbb{g}}x_{0}^{[d]}
. Then, we get that
{\mathbb{g}}(\epsilon){\mathbb{p}}(\epsilon)^{-1}\in A\subseteq F
for each
\epsilon\subseteq[d]
.

Since
{\mathbb{p}}\in u^{[d]}\circ F^{[d]}
, there are
({\mathbb{t}}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
and
(\mathbb{f}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
nets in
\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)
and
F^{[d]}
respectively such that
{\mathbb{t}}_{\lambda}\to u^{[d]}
and
{\mathbb{t}}_{\lambda}\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}\to{\mathbb{p}}
. It follows from
Proposition

that

\displaystyle\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\epsilon)\xrightarrow{\uptau}uu^{-1}{\mathbb{p}}(\epsilon)=u{\mathbb{p}}(\epsilon)

for each
\epsilon\subseteq[d]
. Therefore, we get that
u^{[d]}{\mathbb{p}}\in F^{[d]}
. Since
{\mathbb{g}}(\epsilon){\mathbb{p}}(\epsilon)^{-1}\in F
, we have
{\mathbb{g}}\in\mathrm{cl}_{\uptau}(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(G))\cap F^{[d]}
. Thus,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{x}}\in v(\mathrm{cl}_{\uptau}(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(G))\cap F^{[d]})x_{0}^{[d]}\subseteq u^{[d]}\circ v(\mathrm{cl}_{\uptau}(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(G))\cap F^{[d]})x_{0}^{[d]}=u^{[d]}\circ(\mathrm{cl}_{\uptau}(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(G))\cap F^{[d]})x_{0}^{[d]}.

, we get that

\displaystyle(\pi^{[d]})^{-1}(y_{0}^{[d]})=u^{[d]}\circ(\mathrm{cl}_{\uptau}(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(G))\cap F^{[d]})x_{0}^{[d]}=u^{[d]}\circ{\mathfrak{G}}(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(Y))x_{0}^{[d]}.

Since
\pi
is RIC, we have that
\pi^{[d]}
is an open extension. Hence, by
Lemma

5.29
, we conclude that
\pi^{[d]}:\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)\to\bm{Q}^{[d]}(Y)
is RIC.
∎

Proposition 5.33
.

Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,T)
be a RIC weakly mixing extension of minimal systems. Then,
\pi^{[d]}:(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X),\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T))\to(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(Y),\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T))
is a RIC weakly mixing extension.

Proof.

Let
y_{0}\in Y
and
(x_{1},\dots,x_{2^{d+1}})\in R_{\pi}^{2^{d+1}}
, with
x_{1},\dots,x_{2^{d+1}}\in\pi^{-1}(y_{0})
, be a transitive point. By
Lemma

5.31
, we have that
(x_{1},\dots,x_{2^{d}}),(x_{2^{d}+1},\dots,x_{2^{d+1}})\in\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
.
Let
({\mathbb{x}},{\mathbb{x}}^{\prime})\in R_{\pi^{[d]}}
. Therefore, we have
\pi^{[d]}({\mathbb{x}})=\pi^{[d]}({\mathbb{x}}^{\prime})={\mathbb{y}}
for some
{\mathbb{y}}\in\bm{Q}^{[d]}(Y)
. Then, there exist a net
({\mathbb{t}}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)
such that
{\mathbb{t}}_{\lambda}y_{0}^{[d]}\to{\mathbb{y}}
. Since
\pi
is RIC,
\pi^{[d]}
is open and
(\pi^{[d]})^{-1}
is continuous. Thus, we have that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{t}}_{\lambda}(\pi^{[d]})^{-1}(y_{0}^{[d]})\to(\pi^{[d]})^{-1}({\mathbb{y}}).

Therefore, there exist
(\mathbb{a}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda},(\mathbb{b}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
nets in
(\pi^{[d]})^{-1}(y_{0}^{[d]})
such that
{\mathbb{t}}_{\lambda}\mathbb{a}_{\lambda}\to{\mathbb{x}}
and
{\mathbb{t}}_{\lambda}\mathbb{b}_{\lambda}\to{\mathbb{x}}^{\prime}
. It follows from
(\mathbb{a}_{\lambda,1},\dots,\mathbb{a}_{\lambda,2^{d}},\mathbb{b}_{\lambda,1},\dots,\mathbb{b}_{\lambda,2^{d}})\in R_{\pi}^{2^{d+1}}
, where
\mathbb{a}_{\lambda}=(\mathbb{a}_{\lambda,1},\dots,\mathbb{a}_{\lambda,2^{d}})
and
\mathbb{b}_{\lambda}=(\mathbb{b}_{\lambda,1},\dots,\mathbb{b}_{\lambda,2^{d}})
, that
\mathbb{a}_{\lambda}\in\overline{T(x_{1},\dots,x_{2^{d}})}
and
\mathbb{b}_{\lambda}\in\overline{T(x_{2^{d}+1},\dots,x_{2^{d+1}})}
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle({\mathbb{x}},{\mathbb{x}}^{\prime})\in\overline{\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)((x_{1},\dots,x_{2^{d}})(x_{2^{d}+1},\dots,x_{2^{d+1}}))}.

Hence, we get that
R_{\pi^{[d]}}
is transitive, completing the proof.
∎

By definition, the same results are obtained for distal and equicontinuous extensions.

5.4.
Maximal distal factor of dynamical cubes

In this subsection, we will show that the maximal distal factor and maximal factor of order
\infty
of
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
is
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X_{dis})
, where
X_{dis}
is the maximal distal factor of
X
, and
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X/\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(X))
, respectively, for any minimal system
(X,T)
. This provides an alternative proof to the one given by Qiu and Zhao in
[
38
]
for the maximal factor of order
\infty
of dynamical cubes.

We will first prove the theorem for the maximal distal factor of dynamical cubes. The proof of this is analogous to the proof of
[
43
, Theorem 1.2]
for the system
N_{d}(X)
(see
[
43
]
for the definition of
N_{d}(X)
), using the saturatedness of dynamical cubes proved in
Proposition

5.16
. For completeness, we include the proof. The proof makes use of the capturing operation, a kind of reverse orbit closure, which was introduced in
[
6
]
to characterize the distal and equicontinuous structure relations in minimal systems.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal topological dynamical system and
K\subseteq X
. The capturing set of
K
is
C(K)=\{x\in X:\overline{Tx}\cap K\neq\emptyset\}
. Let
R
be a symmetric and reflexive relation, and let
\mathcal{E}(R)
be the equivalence relation generated by
R
. Thus,
\mathcal{E}(R)=\bigcup\{R^{n}:n=1,2,\dots\}
, where

\displaystyle R^{n}=\{(x,z):\exists y_{1},\dots,y_{n-1}\text{ s.t }(x,y_{1}),(y_{1},y_{2}),\dots,(y_{n-1},z)\in R\}.

Every system
(X,T)
has a maximal distal factor. Moreover, there exists a closed, invariant equivalence relation
S_{dis}(X)
such that the quotient
X/S_{dis}(X)
is the maximal distal factor of
X
,
S_{dis}(X)
is called the
distal structure relation
of
X
. The following results show some properties of the distal structure relation.

Theorem 5.34

(
[
6
, Theorem 4.5]
)
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal topological dynamical system. Then,
S_{dis}(X)=C(\overline{\mathcal{E}(\overline{\mathbb{P}(X)}}))
.

Theorem 5.35

(
[
6
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,T)
be an extension of minimal systems. Then,
\pi\times\pi(S_{dis}(X))=S_{dis}(Y)
.

Before we can prove the theorem, we need some preliminary results.

Lemma 5.36

(
[
43
, Lemma 4.6]
)
.

Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,T)
be an extension of minimal systems. If
R_{\pi}\subseteq S_{dis}(X)
, then
X_{dis}=Y_{dis}
. In particular, this holds for proximal extensions.

Lemma 5.37

(
[
43
, Lemma 4.7]
)
.

Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,T)
be an extension of minimal systems and
Z
be a distal factor of
Y
. If the maximal distal factor of
X
is
Z
, then
Z
is also the maximal distal factor of
Y
.

Lemma 5.38
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal topological dynamical system with proximal relation closed, and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}((\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X))=\{({\mathbb{x}},{\mathbb{y}})\in\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)\times\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X):({\mathbb{x}}_{\epsilon},{\mathbb{y}}_{\epsilon})\in\mathbb{P}(X),\epsilon\subseteq[d]\}.

Proof.

Clearly, we have the following inclusion

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}((\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X))\subseteq\{({\mathbb{x}},{\mathbb{y}})\in\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)\times\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X):({\mathbb{x}}_{\epsilon},{\mathbb{y}}_{\epsilon})\in\mathbb{P}(X),\epsilon\subseteq[d]\}.

Let
({\mathbb{x}},{\mathbb{y}})\in\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)\times\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
be such that
({\mathbb{x}}_{\epsilon},{\mathbb{y}}_{\epsilon})\in\mathbb{P}(X)
. Consider
\pi:X\to X_{dis}
, where
X_{dis}
is the maximal distal factor. Since
\pi
is a proximal extension, by
Proposition

5.28
, we have
\pi^{[d]}:\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)\to\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X_{dis})
is a proximal extension. It follows from
\pi^{[d]}({\mathbb{x}})=\pi^{[d]}({\mathbb{y}})
that
({\mathbb{x}},{\mathbb{y}})\in\mathbb{P}(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X))
, completing the proof.
∎

Theorem 5.39
.

Let
(X,S)
be a minimal topological dynamical system and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. Then,
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X_{dis})
is the maximal distal factor of
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
, where
X_{dis}
is the maximal distal factor of
X
.

Proof.

Let
\pi:X\to X_{dis}
be the factor map. By
Theorem

5.17
, there is a commutative diagram of extensions of minimal systems

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X_{dis}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X_{dis}^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta}

is an open extension and
\theta

\theta^{\prime}
are almost one-to-one extensions. Thus, we have the following diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X^{\prime})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X_{dis})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X_{dis}^{\prime})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{[d]}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\pi^{\prime}}^{[d]}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta^{[d]}}

Let
({\mathbb{x}},{\mathbb{y}})\in R_{\phi^{[d]}}
, and let
{\mathbb{x}}^{\prime}={\pi^{\prime}}^{[d]}({\mathbb{x}})
and
{\mathbb{y}}^{\prime}={\pi^{\prime}}^{[d]}({\mathbb{y}})
. Observe that
({\mathbb{x}}^{\prime},{\mathbb{y}}^{\prime})\in R_{\theta^{[d]}}
. Since

is distal, we have that
\mathbb{P}(X_{dis}^{\prime})
is closed. Therefore, by
Lemma

, we get that
({\mathbb{x}}^{\prime},{\mathbb{y}}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{P}(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X_{dis}^{\prime}))
. Then, there exists
\mathbb{v}\in J(M(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(S)))
such that

. Let

and
\mathbb{b}=\mathbb{v}{\mathbb{y}}
. Note that

.

It follows from that

is a proximal extension and
Lemma

5.36
that
X_{dis}
is the maximal distal factor of
X^{\prime}
. Since
X/\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(X)
is a factor of

and

, we get that

is

and

, it follows from the saturated property of

that

\displaystyle(a_{1},\dots,a_{i},b_{i+1},\dots,b_{2^{d}})\in\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X^{\prime})

for each

. Note that

, we have

\displaystyle\overline{S(a_{1},b_{1})}\cap\overline{\mathcal{E}(\overline{\mathbb{P}(X^{\prime})})}\neq\emptyset.

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(S)(\mathbb{a},(b_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{2^{d}}))}\cap\overline{\mathcal{E}(\overline{\mathbb{P}(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X^{\prime})})}\neq\emptyset.

\displaystyle((b_{1},a_{2},a_{3},\dots,a_{2^{d}}),(b_{1},b_{2},a_{3},\dots,a_{2^{d}}))\in S_{dis}(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X^{\prime})).

\displaystyle((b_{1},\dots,b_{i-1},a_{i},\dots,a_{2^{d}}),(b_{1},\dots,b_{i-1},b_{i},a_{i+1},\dots,a_{2^{d}}))\in S_{dis}(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X^{\prime}))

such that
\mathbb{w_{1}}\mathbb{x}=\mathbb{x}
and
\mathbb{w_{2}}\mathbb{y}=\mathbb{y}
, we have that

and

. We deduce that

. It follows from

that the maximal distal factor of
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X^{\prime})
is
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X_{dis})
. Hence, by
Lemma

5.36
and

, we can conclude that
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X_{dis})
is the maximal distal factor of
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
.

Using the last theorem, we have the following relation between
D(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T))
and
D
, where
D(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T))
is the Ellis group of the universal distal minimal system for the action of
\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)
.

Corollary 5.40
.

Let
(X,S)
be a minimal topological dynamical system and
d\geq 1
be an integer. Then,
D(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(S)){\mathbb{x}}=D^{[d]}{\mathbb{x}}
for each
{\mathbb{x}}\in\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
.

Proof.

Note that
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(M_{dis})
is a distal system, so
D(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(S))\subseteq{\mathfrak{G}}(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(M_{dis}))
. By
Lemma

5.14
and
Lemma

5.27
, we have
{\mathfrak{G}}(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(M_{dis}))=D^{[d]}
. Therefore,
D(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(S))\subseteq D^{[d]}
.

Let
A={\mathfrak{G}}(X)
. It follows from
Theorem

5.39
,
Lemma

5.27
and
Proposition

5.5
that

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{G}}(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X_{dis}))={\mathfrak{G}}((\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X))_{dis})=(\mathrm{cl}_{\uptau}(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(G))\cap A^{[d]})D(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(S)).

By
Lemma

5.27
,
Proposition

5.5
and
Lemma

5.14
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{G}}(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X_{dis}))=\mathrm{cl}_{\uptau}(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(G))\cap A^{[d]}D^{[d]}.

Let
{\mathbb{g}}\in D^{[d]}
,
{\mathbb{x}}\in\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
and
{\mathbb{p}}\in M(\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(S))
be such that
{\mathbb{x}}={\mathbb{p}}x_{0}^{[d]}
. Since

is a normal subgroup, there is a
{\mathbb{g}}^{\prime}\in D^{[d]}
such that

. It follows from
{\mathfrak{G}}(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X_{dis}))={\mathfrak{G}}((\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X))_{dis})
that there are

and

such that

. Thus,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{g}}{\mathbb{x}}={\mathbb{g}}{\mathbb{p}}x_{0}^{[d]}=u^{[d]}{\mathbb{p}}{\mathbb{g}}^{\prime}x_{0}^{[d]}=u^{[d]}{\mathbb{p}}{\mathbb{h}}\mathbb{a}x_{0}^{[d]}=u^{[d]}{\mathbb{p}}{\mathbb{h}}x_{0}^{[d]}.

is a normal subgroup, there is a

such that

. Therefore, we get that

and hence

.
∎

In
[
39
]
, Qiu and Zhao showed that for a minimal distal system
(X,T)
the maximal factor of order
\infty
of
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
is
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X/\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(X))
. Soon after, they extended this result for any minimal system in
[
38
]
. In this paper, we provide an alternative proof of this result using
Theorem

5.39
.

Theorem 5.41

(
[
39
, Theorem 3.3]
)
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal distal topological dynamical system and
d\geq 1
be an integer. Then, the maximal factor of order
\infty
of
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
is
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X/\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(X))
.

Observe that we have the following lemma.

Lemma 5.42
.

Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,T)
be an extension of minimal systems. If
R_{\pi}\subseteq\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(X)
, then
X/\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(X)=Y/\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(Y)
.

Theorem 5.43
.

Let
(X,S)
be a minimal topological dynamical system and
d\geq 1
be an integer. Then, the maximal factor of order
\infty
of
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
is
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X/\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(X))
.

Proof.

Since
S_{dis}(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X))
is the smallest closed invariant equivalence relation containing
P(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X))
, we deduce that
S_{dis}(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X))\subseteq\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X))
. It follows from
Lemma

5.42
that the maximal distal factor of
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
has the same maximal factor of order
\infty
as
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
. Similarly, we get
X/\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(X)=X_{dis}/\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(X_{dis})
. By
Theorem

5.39
, we have
(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X))_{dis}=\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X_{dis})
. Thus, by
Theorem

5.41
, we conclude that the maximal factor of order
\infty
of
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X_{dis})
is
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X/\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(X))
.
∎

It is worth mentioning that in
[
38
]
the authors also showed that the maximal factor of order
j
of
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
is
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X_{j})
, where
X_{j}
is the maximal factor of order
j
of

. Currently, we are not able to give a proof for this fact using our methods. It would be interesting to provide a proof using the algebraic methods developed in this article. Moreover, to provide an algebraic proof, it would suffice to show that
{\mathfrak{G}}(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)/\mathbb{RP}^{[j]}(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)))={\mathfrak{G}}(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X_{j}))
for any minimal system
(X,T)
and any integers
d,j\geq 1
. Furthermore, it would also be of interest to provide an algebraic proof of
Theorem

5.41
and
Theorem

5.43
. As before, to give an algebraic proof, it is sufficient to prove that
{\mathfrak{G}}(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)/\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)))={\mathfrak{G}}(\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X/\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(X)))
for any minimal system
(X,T)
and any integer
d\geq 1
. The difficulty in both problems lies in using the results of this paper to prove these algebraic equalities.

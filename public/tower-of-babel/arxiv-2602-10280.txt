Wider systems for linear logic with fixed points: proof theory and complexity
Anupam Das, Tikhon Pshenitsyn

Abstract
We investigate infinitary wellfounded systems for linear logic with fixed points, with transfinite branching rules indexed by some closure ordinal α \alpha for fixed points. Our main result is that provability in the system for some computable ordinal α \alpha is complete for the ω α ω \omega^{\alpha^{\omega}} level of the hyperarithmetical hierarchy. To this end we first develop proof theoretic foundations, namely cut elimination and focussing results, to control both the upper and lower bound analysis. Our arguments employ a carefully calibrated notion of formula rank, calculating a tight bound on the height of the (cut-free) proof search space.

Introduction

Fixed points
abound in logic and computation, providing a robust way to formalise induction and recursion.
The last 15-20 years has seen renewed interest in fixed points from the point of view of
structural proof theory
, building a finer picture of their logical behaviour, e.g. see
[
7
,
8
,
6
,
5
,
14
,
13
,
21
,
12
]
in just the setting of linear logic.
These investigations cover several proposals for reasoning about fixed points, including systems with explicit (co)induction rules, cyclic or non-wellfounded proofs, and systems with infinitely branching rules.
The setting of linear logic allows us to compare these methodologies in a robust way, largely free from artefacts of, say, classical or intuitionistic logic, or first-order theories, such as arithmetic.
In particular recent works, such as
[
10
,
12
,
11
]
, have improved our understanding of how various methodologies compare in terms of the theorems they admit, and the complexity of reasoning therein.

In this paper we extend this line of work to systems whose branching is indexed by an arbirtary (computable) ordinal.
Previous work such as
[
12
,
11
]
only considered
\omega
-branching which are suitable for only continuous models, where definable operators reach their fixed points in
\leq\omega
many steps.
General models, e.g. of the arithmetical/first-order or modal
\mu
-calculus, may admit arbitrary closure ordinals (see, e.g.,
[
20
,
27
,
1
]
).
For instance
\mu x\,(1+\nu y\,(x\times y))
represents the class of all (countable) well-founded trees, when interpreted as a subset of Cantor space.
Apart from the expanded scope of our investigations, this work serves as a further testbed for the tools developed in structural proof theory for handling fixed points in the last couple decades.

1.1
Contribution

We investigate the proof theory and complexity of the systems
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}
for (multiplicative additive) linear logic with fixed points, first proposed by De in
[
15
]
, parametrised by an arbitrary closure ordinal
\alpha
for fixed points.
We prove
cut-elimination
and
focussing
results, by exploiting an ordinal measure of formulas,
rank
, and adapting existing methods.
Our definition of rank is carefully calibrated in order to tightly bound the height of the cut-free proof search space, unlike previous approaches, e.g.
[
2
,
15
]
.
The main application of these foundational developments is a classification of the complexity of system
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}
for computable
\alpha

\omega^{\alpha^{\omega}}
level of the hyperarithmetical hierarchy (under Turing reductions).
The upper bound follows cleanly from the aforementioned property of ranks, under a natural encoding of cut-free proof search. For the lower bound we encode the problems of
true computable infinitary sentences
complete for each level of the hyperarithmetical hierarchy, exploiting our focussing result to show adequacy.

2.3
Statement of main result

The main question we ask in this work is about the algorithmic complexity of theoremhood in
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}
: is a
\mu\mathsf{MALL}
sequent
\Gamma
provable in
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}
?
We restrict this problem to only
\mu\mathsf{MALL}
input sequents for two reasons: (i) so that the theorems of all logics are over the same language; and, (ii) to avoid representation issues of the input sequent when ordinal annotations appear (recall that
\mu
is identified with
\mu^{\alpha}
in
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}
).
Our main result comprises a tight answer to this question for appropriate finitely representable ordinals
\alpha
:

Theorem 2.8
.

\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}
is
\Sigma^{0}_{\omega^{\alpha^{\omega}}}
-complete, for computable ordinals
\alpha\geq\omega
.

The complexity class
\Sigma^{0}_{\omega^{\alpha^{\omega}}}
is a level of the
hyperarithmetical hierarchy
—a classification of
\Delta^{1}_{1}
-sets extending the arithmetical hierarchy.
One can obtain a complete set at the
n
th
level of the arithmetical hierarchy by
n
-fold application of the Turing jump to
\varnothing
.
Likewise, a complete set at the
\beta
th
level of the hyperarithmetical hierarchy is obtained by iterating the Turing jump
\beta
times, taking
effective unions
at limit stages.
The hyperarithmetical hierarchy is construed under
Turing
reductions, not many-one reductions. This is explained further in the sequel.

The remainder of this paper is devoted to proving
Theorem

2.8
, along with some necessary proof theoretic foundations.
Before that, let us recall sufficient effective descriptive set theory for the reader to make sense of the theorem statement above, not least to make precise our notion of
computable reduction
therein.

3.1

As we mentioned before we shall ultimately need a notion of rank that tightly bounds the height of proof search for our main complexity results.
To this end we will define in

Corollary 3.3

Cut-free proof search is terminating.

4
Bounds on formula ranks

In this section we shall make explicit the rank function
\mathrm{rk}
mentioned earlier and duly prove
Proposition

3.2
.
Our intention is to define ranks in a
tight

This will also induce a more fine-grained notion of rank of a sequent than the multiset measure of
Corollary

3.3

For this reason we shall also carry out a number of calculations for bounding formula ranks.

Henceforth we shall write
\mathsf{Ord}
for the class of all ordinals.

4.1
Natural operations on ordinals

Due to commutativity of the binary connectives, our definition of rank will rather work with commutative variants of arithmetic operations on
\mathsf{Ord}
, known as the
natural
operations.
In this subsection we shall recall some of their properties.
We assume the reader’s familiarity with basic ordinal arithmetic, namely, with ordinal addition, multiplication and exponentiation. We denote a sum of the form
\alpha_{0}+\ldots+\alpha_{k-1}
by
\sum\limits_{i<k}\alpha_{i}
.

Definition 4.1

(Degrees)
.

The
degree

\deg\alpha
of an ordinal
\alpha>0
is the unique ordinal
\beta
such that
\alpha=\omega^{\beta}\cdot n+\gamma
for some
0<n<\omega
and some
\gamma<\omega^{\beta}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

If
\alpha\geq\omega
, then
\alpha^{\omega}=\omega^{(\deg\alpha)\omega}
.

Proof.

(\omega^{\deg\alpha})^{\omega}\leq\alpha^{\omega}\leq\left(\omega^{\deg\alpha+1}\right)^{\omega}=\omega^{(\deg\alpha+1)\omega}=\omega^{(\deg\alpha)\omega}
. The last inequality holds since
\deg\alpha>0
.
∎

Definition 4.3

(Natural sum and product)
.

Write

\alpha=\sum\limits_{i<k}\omega^{\gamma_{i}}a_{i}
and

\beta=\sum\limits_{i<k}\omega^{\gamma_{i}}b_{i}

where
\gamma_{0}>\ldots>\gamma_{k-1}
and
a_{i},b_{i}<\omega
. The
natural sum

\alpha\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxplus$}}}}\beta
is defined as:

\alpha\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxplus$}}}}\beta:=\sum\limits_{i<k}\omega^{\gamma_{i}}(a_{i}+b_{i})

and the
natural product

\alpha\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxtimes$}}}}\beta
is defined as:

\alpha\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxtimes$}}}}\beta:=\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}_{i,j<k}{(\omega^{\gamma_{i}\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxplus$}}}}\gamma_{j}}a_{i}b_{j})}.

Here
\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}
is

if
I=\varnothing
, else
\alpha_{j}\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxplus$}}}}\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}_{i\in I\setminus\{j\}}\alpha_{i}
for some
j\in I
(the index set

is finite). This is a well-defined operation independent of the choice of

, thanks to the following proposition.

Proposition 4.4

(
[
16
, Lemma 3.3]
)
.

1.

\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxplus$}}}}
and

are commutative, associative and monotone operations.

2.

Definition 4.5

Let us define

\displaystyle\alpha^{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxtimes$}}}}0}

\displaystyle:=1;

\displaystyle\alpha^{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxtimes$}}}}\beta}

(

We do not call the above operation natural exponentiation in order not to confuse with the notion of natural exponentiation from

4.2
Definition of formula ranks

We are now finally ready to present our definition of formula rank, cf.
Proposition

3.2
.

Definition 4.7

(Partial valuations)
.

A

is a partial function
s:\mathsf{Var}\rightharpoonup\mathsf{Ord}
.

•

If

where

, then we denote

.

•

We write

such that

for each

.

•

We write
s\geq s^{\prime}
for two partial valuations if
s(x)\geq s^{\prime}(x)
for each
x\in\mathop{\mathrm{dom}}s\cap\mathop{\mathrm{dom}}s^{\prime}
.

•

If
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
are two partial valuations, then ‘
s_{1},s_{2}
’ denotes the following partial valuation

s(x):=\begin{cases}s_{2}(x),&x\in\mathop{\mathrm{dom}}s_{2}\\
s_{1}(x),&x\in\mathop{\mathrm{dom}}s_{1}\setminus\mathop{\mathrm{dom}}s_{2}\\
\text{undefined},&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

•

If
s
is a partial valuation and
\gamma
is an ordinal, then
\gamma\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxtimes$}}}}s
is a partial valuation

defined as follows:

s^{\prime}(x):=\begin{cases}\gamma\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxtimes$}}}}s(x),&x\in\mathop{\mathrm{dom}}s\\
\text{undefined},&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Definition 4.8

(Ranks)
.

Let
s:\mathsf{Var}\to\mathsf{Ord}
be a total valuation function mapping variables to ordinals.
We define
|A|^{s}
as follows:

•

|c|^{s}:=1
if
c
has form
p,p^{\perp},0,\top,1,\bot
;

•

|x|^{s}:=s(x)
for
x\in\mathsf{Var}
;

•

|A_{1}\circ A_{2}|^{s}:=1\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxplus$}}}}|A_{1}|^{s}\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxplus$}}}}|A_{2}|^{s}
for
\circ\in\{\mathbin{\bindnasrepma},\mathbin{\varoplus},\mathbin{\varotimes},\mathbin{\binampersand}\}
;

•

|\eta^{\beta}x.B|^{s}:=\sup\limits_{\gamma<\beta}\left\{|B|^{s,x\mapsto|\eta^{\gamma}x.B|^{s}}+1\right\}
for
\eta\in\{\mu,\nu\}
.

Define
\mathrm{rk}(A):=|A|^{1}
. If
\Gamma=A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
is a multiset of formulae, then
\mathrm{rk}(\Gamma):=\mathrm{rk}(A_{1})\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxplus$}}}}\ldots\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxplus$}}}}\mathrm{rk}(A_{n})
.

Note that, unlike the earlier proof of
Corollary

3.3
, we here calculate the rank of a sequent directly as an ordinal by taking natural sums of its formulas.
The reason for this, again, is so that we may assign an explicit ordinal measure to each sequent, towards proving our main result,
Theorem

2.8
.

A similar rank is introduced for modal
\mu
-formulae in
[
2
]
in order to justify induction on such formulae. However, there is one significant difference between the rank introduced above and that from
[
2
]
: the latter’s definition for the binary operation case involves maximum instead of natural sum, i.e.
|A_{1}\circ A_{2}|^{s}
is defined as
\max\{|A_{1}|^{s},|A_{2}|^{s}\}+1
in
[
2
]
. The reason why

4.8
involves natural sum instead is because we want each inference step of
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}^{\mathit{cf}}
to strictly decrease the rank of a sequent, bottom-up.
Note that, if ranks of formulae and sequents were defined using maximum, this result would not hold. Indeed, in this case, the rule for multiplicative disjunction ‘
\mathbin{\bindnasrepma}
’ would have a premise and a conclusion of the same rank.

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{rk}(C)+1)^{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxtimes$}}}}\beta}

Finally,
Item

5
is proved by induction on the size of
A
. The cases where
A
is a variable, a literal, or a logical constant are trivial since
1<\omega^{\alpha^{\omega}}
. The case
A=A_{1}\circ A_{2}
follows from
Item

3
of
Proposition

4.4
.
Let us consider the case
A=\eta^{\beta}z.C
. By the induction hypothesis,
\mathrm{rk}(C)<\omega^{\alpha^{\omega}}=\omega^{\omega^{(\deg\alpha)\omega}}
, hence
\mathrm{rk}(C)<\omega^{\omega^{(\deg\alpha)m}}
for some
m<\omega
. Now, the reasoning proceeds as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{rk}(\eta^{\beta}z.C)

\displaystyle\leq(\mathrm{rk}(C)+1)^{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxtimes$}}}}\beta}

\displaystyle\leq(\mathrm{rk}(C)+1)^{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxtimes$}}}}\alpha}

\displaystyle\beta\leq\alpha

\displaystyle\leq\left(\omega^{\omega^{(\deg\alpha)m}}\right)^{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxtimes$}}}}\alpha}

\displaystyle=\omega^{\omega^{(\deg\alpha)m}\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\omega^{\omega^{(\deg\alpha)m}\omega^{\deg\alpha+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\omega^{\omega^{(\deg\alpha)(m+2)}}<\omega^{\omega^{(\deg\alpha)\omega}}=\omega^{\alpha^{\omega}}\qed

4.4
Lower bound on ranks

Now, let us establish lower bounds on ranks. It turns out that the bound from
Lemma

4.9
is tight. To show this, let us fix a sequence of variables
(x_{i})_{i<\omega}
and define formulae
R_{n}
as follows.

Definition 4.11
.

Define the formulas
R_{n}
, for
n<\omega
inductively by:

\begin{array}[]{r@{\ \df\ }l}R_{0}\ :=&x_{0}\\
R_{n+1}\ :=&\mu^{\alpha}x_{n}.\left(x_{n+1}\mathbin{\bindnasrepma}R_{n}\right)\end{array}

Lemma 4.12
.

|R_{n}|^{x_{n}\mapsto\gamma}\geq\gamma\cdot f(n)
where
f(0)=1
and
f(n+1)=\omega^{\omega^{(\deg\alpha)n}}
.

Proof.

By induction on
n
. The case
n=0
is trivial, so let us prove the induction step right away. Observe that,

|R_{n+1}|^{x_{n+1}\mapsto\gamma}=|\mu^{\alpha}x_{n}.\left(x_{n+1}\mathbin{\bindnasrepma}R_{n}\right)|^{x_{n+1}\mapsto\gamma}=\sup\limits_{\beta<\alpha}(o_{\beta}+1)

where,

\displaystyle o_{\beta}

\displaystyle=|x_{n+1}\mathbin{\bindnasrepma}R_{n}|^{x_{n+1}\mapsto\gamma,x_{n}\mapsto p_{\beta}}

\displaystyle p_{\beta}

\displaystyle=|\mu^{\beta}x_{n}.\left(x_{n+1}\mathbin{\bindnasrepma}R_{n}\right)|^{x_{n+1}\mapsto\gamma}

Let us establish lower bounds on
o_{\beta}
. First,
o_{1}\geq|x_{n+1}|^{x_{n+1}\mapsto\gamma}=\gamma
. Secondly,

\displaystyle o_{\beta+1}

\displaystyle=|x_{n+1}\mathbin{\bindnasrepma}R_{n}|^{x_{n+1}\mapsto\gamma,x_{n}\mapsto p_{\beta+1}}

\displaystyle=1\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxplus$}}}}\gamma\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxplus$}}}}|R_{n}|^{x_{n}\mapsto p_{\beta+1}}

\displaystyle\geq\gamma\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxplus$}}}}(p_{\beta+1}\cdot f(n));

I.H.

\displaystyle p_{\beta+1}

\displaystyle=|\mu^{\beta+1}x_{n}.\left(x_{n+1}\mathbin{\bindnasrepma}R_{n}\right)|^{x_{n+1}\mapsto\gamma}

\displaystyle\geq o_{\beta}+1.

Combining the inequalities, we obtain that
o_{\beta+1}\geq\gamma\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxplus$}}}}(o_{\beta}\cdot f(n))
.
Finally, notice that
o_{\beta}\geq\sup\limits_{\beta^{\prime}<\beta}o_{\beta^{\prime}}
for limit
\beta
. Using all this, we deduce the following.

•

If
n=0
, then
o_{\beta+1}\geq\gamma\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxplus$}}}}o_{\beta}\geq o_{\beta}+\gamma
, hence
o_{\beta}\geq\gamma\cdot\beta
and

|R_{n+1}|^{x_{n+1}\mapsto\gamma}=\sup\limits_{\beta<\alpha}(o_{\beta}+1)\geq\sup\limits_{\beta<\alpha}\gamma\cdot\beta=\gamma\cdot\alpha\geq\gamma\cdot\omega=\gamma\cdot f(1).

•

If
n>0
, then
o_{\beta+1}\geq\gamma\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{355207}{$\boxplus$}}}}(o_{\beta}\cdot f(n))\geq o_{\beta}\cdot f(n)
, which implies that
o_{\beta}\geq\gamma\cdot f(n)^{\beta-1}
for
1\leq\beta<\omega
and
o_{\beta}\geq\gamma\cdot f(n)^{\beta}
for
\beta\geq\omega
. Therefore,

\displaystyle|R_{n+1}|^{x_{n+1}\mapsto\gamma}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{\beta<\alpha}(o_{\beta}+1)

\displaystyle\geq\sup\limits_{\omega\leq\beta<\alpha}\gamma\cdot f(n)^{\beta}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{\omega\leq\beta<\alpha}\gamma\cdot\omega^{\omega^{(\deg\alpha)(n-1)}\beta}

\displaystyle\geq\sup\limits_{\omega\leq\beta<\omega^{\deg\alpha}}\gamma\cdot\omega^{\omega^{(\deg\alpha)(n-1)}\beta}

\displaystyle=\gamma\cdot\omega^{\omega^{(\deg\alpha)n}}=\gamma\cdot f(n+1)

\displaystyle\text{$\omega^{\deg\alpha}$ is limit}\qed

Corollary 4.13
.

\sup\limits_{n<\omega}\mathrm{rk}(R_{n})=\omega^{\alpha^{\omega}}
.

Proof.

\sup\limits_{n<\omega}\mathrm{rk}(R_{n})\geq\sup\limits_{n<\omega}f(n)=\sup\limits_{n<\omega}\omega^{\omega^{(\deg\alpha)(n-1)}}=\omega^{\omega^{(\deg\alpha)\omega}}=\omega^{\alpha^{\omega}}
. The other inequality follows trivially from
Lemma

4.9
.
∎

5
Upper bounds on provability in
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}

The proof of the upper bound for Theorem
2.8
relies on a general fact about inductive definitions proved e.g. in
[
23
, Theorem 6.2]
, which we present below.

Definition 5.1

(Positive formulas)
.

Let
\Phi(u,U)
be an arithmetical formula with a free variable
u
and a fresh unary predicate
U
.

\Phi(u,U)
is
positive
if
U
does not occur under the scope of a negation.

We write
\mathbb{N}\vDash\varphi
if a formula
\varphi
is true in the standard model of arithmetic. For
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}
define:

[\Phi](S)\ :=\ \{n\in\omega\mid\mathbb{N}\vDash\Phi(n,S)\}\qquad[\Phi]^{\beta}\ :=\ \bigcup\limits_{\gamma<\beta}[\Phi]([\Phi]^{\gamma})

Theorem 5.2

(
[
23
]
)
.

Let
b\in\mathcal{O}
be a notation of a limit ordinal
\beta=|b|_{\mathcal{O}}
. Then,
[\Phi]^{\beta}
is computably enumerable in
H(b)
.

At the level of informal intuition,
[\Phi]^{\beta}
can be thought of as a set defined by the informal predicate:

\underbrace{\Phi(\cdot,\{n\mid\Phi(n,\{n\mid\Phi(n,\ldots\{n\mid\Phi(n,\varnothing)\}\ldots)\})\})}_{\text{``$\beta$ times''}}

If
\Phi
in prenex normal form has
k
quantifier alternations, then the above informal predicate has “
k\cdot\beta
quantifier alternations,” and since
\beta
is limit,
k\cdot\beta=\beta
.
Again informally, this gives us a set at the
\beta
-th level of hyperarithmetical hierarchy. This is of course nothing but a high-level metaphor behind the proof of
Theorem

5.2
, which is duly formalized in
[
23
]
.

Fix some Gödel numbering of
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}
sequents, mapping a sequent
\Gamma
to its Gödel number
\sharp\Gamma\in\omega
. We need to assign Gödel numbers, in particular, to all formulae of the form
\mu^{\beta}xC
for
\beta<\alpha
. This is done by fixing
a\in\mathcal{O}
such that
|a|_{\mathcal{O}}=\alpha
and identifying
\beta
with the unique
b<_{\mathcal{O}}a
such that
|b|_{\mathcal{O}}=\beta
.

Let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(u,U)
be the predicate ‘
u=\sharp\Gamma
for some
\Gamma
and
\Gamma
is the conclusion of a
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}^{\mathit{cf}}
step such that, for each its premisses
\Delta
, already
\sharp\Delta\in U
.’
It is routine to show that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(u,U)
can be expressed by a positive formula. For example, given the rule

\{\Gamma,B[\nu^{\gamma}x.B/x]\}_{\gamma<\beta}

\scriptstyle\nu^{\beta}\;

\Gamma,\nu^{\beta}x.B

consider the sets

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle:=\{(b,\sharp(\Gamma,\nu^{\beta}x.B))\mid|b|_{\mathcal{O}}=\beta,\ b\leq_{\mathcal{O}}a\}

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle:=\{(b,c,\sharp(\Gamma,\nu^{\beta}x.B),\sharp(\Gamma,B[\nu^{\gamma}x.B/x]))\mid|b|_{\mathcal{O}}=\beta,\ |c|_{\mathcal{O}}=\gamma,\ c<_{\mathcal{O}}b\leq_{\mathcal{O}}a\}.

They both are recursively enumerable, hence definable by
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-formulae. Define:

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\nu}(u,U):=\exists b\ (b,u)\in P\mathrel{\&}\forall c(c<_{\mathcal{O}}b)\to\exists y\big((b,c,u,y)\in P\mathrel{\&}y\in U\big)

This formula is positive. It specifies that
u=\sharp(\Gamma,\nu^{\beta}x.B)
and, for each
\gamma<\beta
,
\sharp(\Gamma,B[\nu^{\gamma}x.B/x])\in U
. Introducing similar formulae for other rules and taking their disjunction yields a desired positive formula representing
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(u,U)
.

Define
\mathcal{D}(u,U):=(u\in U)\vee\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(u,U)
.
By induction on
\mathrm{rk}(\Gamma)
, it is easy to prove that
\Gamma
is provable in
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}^{\mathit{cf}}
iff
\sharp\Gamma\in[\mathcal{D}]^{\mathrm{rk}(\Gamma)}
. This follows directly from Proposition
4.10
.
Therefore, if
b
is a notation of
\beta=\omega^{\alpha^{\omega}}
, then
[\mathcal{D}]^{\beta}=\{\sharp\Gamma\mid\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}^{\mathit{cf}}\vdash\Gamma\}
.
This is exactly the set of notations of theorems of
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}
, by
Theorem

3.5
; so let us denote it by
\mathrm{Th}_{\alpha}=\{\sharp\Gamma\mid\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}\vdash\Gamma\}
.
Note that
\mathrm{Th}_{\alpha}=[\mathcal{D}^{\prime}](\mathrm{Th}_{\alpha})
because each theorem is obtained by some rule application from other theorems.

By
Theorem

5.2
,
\mathrm{Th}_{\alpha}
is computably enumerable w.r.t.
H(b)
.
What we need is to show that
\mathrm{Th}_{\alpha}
is computable in
H(b)
, which is equivalent to
\mathrm{Th}_{\alpha}\leq_{\mathrm{T}}H(b)
. It remains to show that the complement of
\mathrm{Th}_{\alpha}
is computably enumerable in
H(b)
. Define the predicate
\overline{\mathcal{D}}(u,U):=(u\in U)\vee\lnot\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(u,\omega\setminus U)
, which is also expressible by an positive formula.

Proposition 5.3
.

We have the following:

1.

For each
\gamma\in\mathsf{Ord}
,
[\overline{\mathcal{D}}]^{\gamma}\cap\mathrm{Th}_{\alpha}=\varnothing
.

2.

If
u\neq\sharp\Gamma
for any
\Gamma
, then
u\in[\overline{\mathcal{D}}]
.

3.

If
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}\not\vdash\Gamma
, then
\sharp\Gamma\in[\overline{\mathcal{D}}]^{\mathrm{rk}(\Gamma)+1}
.

4.

\omega\setminus\mathrm{Th}_{\alpha}=[\overline{\mathcal{D}}]^{\beta}
, when
\beta\geq\omega^{\alpha^{\omega}}
.

Proof.

Item

1
is proved by induction on
\gamma
. The case
\gamma=0
or
\gamma
being limit is trivial. Let
\gamma=\delta+1
and
u\in[\overline{\mathcal{D}}]^{\gamma}=[\overline{\mathcal{D}}]([\overline{\mathcal{D}}]^{\delta})
. This means that
\mathbb{N}\vDash(u\in[\overline{\mathcal{D}}]^{\delta})\vee\lnot\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(u,\omega\setminus[\overline{\mathcal{D}}]^{\delta})
. By the induction hypothesis, if
u\in[\overline{\mathcal{D}}]^{\delta}
, then
u\notin\mathrm{Th}_{\alpha}
.
Consider the case
\mathbb{N}\vDash\lnot\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(u,\omega\setminus[\overline{\mathcal{D}}]^{\delta})
. Assume, by way of contradiction, that
u\in\mathrm{Th}_{\alpha}=[\mathcal{D}^{\prime}](\mathrm{Th}_{\alpha})
. This implies that
\mathbb{N}\vDash\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(u,\mathrm{Th}_{\alpha})
. By the induction hypothesis,
\mathrm{Th}_{\alpha}\subseteq\omega\setminus[\overline{\mathcal{D}}]^{\delta}
, therefore,
\mathbb{N}\vDash\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(u,\omega\setminus[\overline{\mathcal{D}}]^{\delta})
. This is a contradiction.

To prove
Item

2
, note that, for any

,
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(u,U)
is true only if
u
is the notation of a sequent; therefore, if
u
is not a notation, then
\lnot\mathcal{D}(u,\omega)
holds, meaning that
u\in[\overline{\mathcal{D}}]
.

Item

3
is proved by induction on
\mathrm{rk}(\Gamma)
. The case
\mathrm{rk}(\Gamma)=0
is trivial since the rank of any sequent is at least

. Let
\mathrm{rk}(\Gamma)>0
. Assume, by way of contradiction, that
\sharp\Gamma\notin[\overline{\mathcal{D}}]^{\mathrm{rk}(\Gamma)+1}
, or equivalently, that:

\mathbb{N}\vDash(u\notin[\overline{\mathcal{D}}]^{\mathrm{rk}(\Gamma)})\mathrel{\&}\mathcal{D}(\sharp\Gamma,\omega\setminus[\overline{\mathcal{D}}]^{\mathrm{rk}(\Gamma)}).

This implies that
\Gamma
is a conclusion of a rule application of the form,

(\Delta_{i})_{i<\zeta}

\Gamma

for some
\zeta
such that
\sharp\Delta_{i}\notin[\overline{\mathcal{D}}]^{\mathrm{rk}(\Gamma)}
for each
i<\zeta
. Thanks to
Proposition

4.10
,
\mathrm{rk}(\Delta_{i})<\mathrm{rk}(\Gamma)
, hence
\sharp\Delta_{i}\notin[\overline{\mathcal{D}}]^{\mathrm{rk}(\Delta_{i})+1}
. By the induction hypothesis,
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}\vdash\Delta_{i}
, and therefore
\Gamma
is derivable in
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}
as well using the above rule application. This is a contradiction.

Finally
Item

4
follows from
Items

2
and

since ranks of all sequents are less than
\beta=\omega^{\alpha^{\omega}}
.
∎

Propositions

and

imply that

(as well as
\mathrm{Th}_{\alpha}
) is computably enumerable in

when

is a notation for
\omega^{\alpha^{\omega}}
.
Thus we have shown the membership direction of our main result,

2.8
:

Theorem 5.4

(Membership)
.

Provability in
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}
is in
\Sigma^{0}_{\omega^{\alpha^{\omega}}}
, for computable ordinals
\alpha
.

6
Lower bound on provability in
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}

Let us now turn towards proving the lower bound for
Theorem

2.8
, which is much more involved.
The main result of this section is:

Theorem 6.1

(Hardness)
.

Provability in
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}
is
\Sigma^{0}_{\omega^{\alpha^{\omega}}}
-hard, for computable ordinals
\alpha\geq\omega
.

To this end, for the remainder of this section, let us fix a computable ordinal
\alpha\geq\omega
, and thereby the system
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}
.

For the proof, we need a handy tool of hyperarithmetical theory: computable infinitary formulae which are built from the truth
\top
and falsity
\bot
constants by means of computable infinitary conjunctions and disjunctions.
We shall follow mostly the exposition of
[
4
]
.

Definition 6.2

(Computable infinitary propositional sentences)
.

For
a\in\mathcal{O}
, define:

S^{\Sigma}_{a}\ :=\ {\left\{\langle 0,a,e\rangle\mid e\in\omega\right\}}\quad\text{and}\quad S^{\Pi}_{a}\ :=\ {\left\{\langle 1,a,e\rangle\mid e\in\omega\right\}}.

Elements of these sets are called
indices
. Indices are mapped to
infinitary propositional sentences
by means of a function
\mathrm{ips}{(\cdot)}
defined by:

•

For
\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}
, let
\mathrm{ips}{(\langle\varepsilon,1,e\rangle)}=\begin{cases}\bot&e=0;\\
\top&e>0.\\
\end{cases}

•

For
a\neq 1
, let
\mathrm{ips}{(\langle 0,a,e\rangle)}:=

\bigvee\left\{\mathrm{ips}{(k)}\mid{\mathtt{U}\left(e,k\right)}\ \text{converges and}\ {\left(\exists b<_{\mathcal{O}}a\right)}\,{\left(\exists e^{\prime}\right)}\,{\left(k=\langle 1,b,e^{\prime}\rangle\right)}\right\}.

•

Symmetrically, for
a\neq 1
, let
\mathrm{ips}{(\langle 1,a,e\rangle)}:=

\bigwedge\left\{\mathrm{ips}{(k)}\mid{\mathtt{U}\left(e,k\right)}\ \text{converges and}\ {\left(\exists b<_{\mathcal{O}}a\right)}\,{\left(\exists e^{\prime}\right)}\,{\left(k=\langle 0,b,e^{\prime}\rangle\right)}\right\}.

The definition of truth of an infinitary propositional sentence is classical:
\top
is true,
\bot
is not true, a disjunction is true iff one of the disjuncts is true, a conjunction is true iff all the conjuncts is true.
Let
\mathrm{Tr}{(a)}:=\{\langle\varepsilon,b,e\rangle\mid b<_{\mathcal{O}}a,\ \mathrm{ips}{(\langle\varepsilon,b,e\rangle)}~\text{is true}\}
be the set of indices of true infinitary propositional sentences with ordinal notations below
a
.

The main fact relating computable infinitary sentences to hyperarithmetical hierarchy is presented below.

Fact 6.3
.

Let
b\in\mathcal{O}
represent an infinite limit ordinal
|b|_{\mathcal{O}}
. Then
\mathrm{Tr}{(b)}
is many-one equivalent to
H(b)
.

See e.g.
[
4
]
for a proof. In particular, the fact that
H(b)
is many-one reducible to
\mathrm{Tr}{(b)}
, proved in
[
4
, Proposition 7.8(a)]
, shows that
\mathrm{Tr}{(b)}
is
\Sigma^{0}_{|b|_{\mathcal{O}}}
-hard.

Our main goal in this section is to provide a Turing reduction from
\mathrm{Tr}{(c)}
to the
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}
provability predicate for some
c
a notation for
\omega^{\alpha^{\omega}}
.

6.1
Encoding computable functions in
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}

First, in order to reduce
\mathrm{Tr}{(c)}
to provability in
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}
, we want to simulate computable functions within
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}
. This is done in the spirit of the constructions from
[
11
,
24
]
: we take a Minsky machine that implements a computable function and encode its instructions in
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}
.

Definition 6.4
.

A
Minsky machine
is a tuple
\mathtt{M}=(Q,s,t,I)
where
Q
is a set of states,
s,t\in Q
are the start state and the accepting state resp., and
I
is a finite set of instructions. An instruction is either of the form
\mathtt{INC}(p,i,q)
or of the form
\mathtt{JZDEC}(p,i,q,r)
where
p,q,r\in Q
and
i\in\omega
.

A Minsky machine’s
configuration
is of the form
p(k_{0},k_{1},\ldots,k_{n-1})
where
p\in Q
and
c_{i}\in\omega
.
Operational semantics, mapping a configuration to the next one, is given by:

•

The semantics of the
\mathtt{INC}(p,i,q)
is: from the state
p
, increase
k_{i}
, i.e. the value of the counter No.
i
, by
1
and switch to the state
q
.

•

The semantics of
\mathtt{JZDEC}(p,i,q,r)
is: from the state
p
, if
k_{i}
is greater than
0
, decrease it by
1
and switch to the state
q
; otherwise, do not change counter values and switch to the state
r
.

The Minsky machine
\mathtt{M}

computes
a partial function
f:\underbrace{\omega\times\ldots\times\omega}_{k~\text{times}}\rightharpoonup\underbrace{\omega\times\ldots\times\omega}_{l~\text{times}}

if the following two statements are equivalent:

1.

t(m_{0},m_{1},\ldots)
is reachable from
s(n_{0},\ldots,n_{k-1},0,0,\ldots)
;

2.

f(n_{0},\ldots,n_{k-1})
is defined and equals
(m_{0},\ldots,m_{l-1})
, while
m_{i}=0
for
i\geq l
.

Fact 6.5

(
[
26
]
)
.

Any computable function is computed by some Minsky machine.

Now let us set up some notation for formulas in
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}
.

•

Fix three propositional symbols, denoted by
\mathit{acc}
,
\mathit{key}_{1}
and
\mathit{key}_{2}
, and also countably many symbols, denoted by
(r_{i})_{i\in\omega}
.

•

Let
\mathrm{tup}(n_{0},\ldots,n_{k})=c_{0}^{n_{0}},\ldots,c_{k}^{n_{k}}
.
(The symbol
c_{i}
repeated
k
times corresponds to the
i
th
counter of a Minsky machine being equal to
k
.)

•

Let
\mathit{Pass}:=\mathit{acc}^{\perp}\mathbin{\varotimes}\mathit{acc}
.

•

Let
\mathit{Acc}:=\mathit{acc}^{\perp}\mathbin{\bindnasrepma}\mathit{acc}
(note that
\mathit{Acc}
is the negation of
\mathit{Pass}
).

Definition 6.6
.

Let
\Xi\subseteq\mathsf{Prop}
. A formula is
\Xi
-locked
if it has the form,

(p^{\perp}_{1}\mathbin{\varotimes}A_{1})\mathbin{\varoplus}\ldots\mathbin{\varoplus}(p^{\perp}_{m}\mathbin{\varotimes}A_{m})\mathbin{\varoplus}\mathit{Pass}

where each
p_{i}\in\Xi
.
A sequent
\Gamma
is
\Xi
-locked
if all its formulae are
\Xi
-locked.

Informally, elements of
\Xi
are ‘keys’, and
p_{i}
is required to ‘unlock’ the
i
th
disjunct of the above formula (while
\mathit{acc}
‘unlocks’ the last disjunct).

Lemma 6.7
.

For any
l
partial computable functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{l}
, there exists a formula
\mathit{Comp}
and
s_{1},\ldots,s_{l},t\in\mathsf{Prop}
such that, for any
\{t,\mathit{key}_{1}\}
-locked multiset
\Gamma
and any
d\in\{1,\ldots,l\}
, the sequent,

\Gamma,\mathrm{tup}(\vec{n}),s_{d},\mathit{Comp}

is derivable if and only if
f_{d}(\vec{n})
is defined and the sequent,

\Gamma,\mathrm{tup}(f_{d}(\vec{n})),t

is derivable.

Proof.

For each
j=1,\ldots,l
, fix a Minsky machine
\mathtt{M}_{j}=(Q_{j},s_{j},t_{j},I_{j})
that computes
f_{j}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that the sets
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{l}
are disjoint. There is some
N\in\omega
such that the counters No.
(N+1)
, No.
(N+2)
etc. are used by neither of
\mathtt{M}_{i}
’s (i.e. the instructions of
\mathtt{M}_{i}
’s involve only the first
(N+1)
counters).

Fix a variable
u
. We encode a Minsky machine instruction
\mathit{ins}
by a formula
[\mathit{ins}]
defined by,

•

[\mathtt{INC}(p,i,q)]:=p^{\perp}\mathbin{\varotimes}(c_{i}\mathbin{\bindnasrepma}q\mathbin{\bindnasrepma}x)

•

[\mathtt{JZDEC}(p,i,q,r)]:=p^{\perp}\mathbin{\varotimes}[(c^{\perp}_{i}\mathbin{\varotimes}(q\mathbin{\bindnasrepma}x))\mathbin{\varoplus}(Z_{i}\mathbin{\binampersand}(r\mathbin{\bindnasrepma}x))]

8
Conclusions

In this work we investigated systems
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}
for linear logic with least and greatest fixed points parametrised by a closure ordinal
\alpha
.
We developed its proof theoretic foundations, namely proving cut-elimination and focussing results, and applied them to classify the complexity of these logics for computable
\alpha
:
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}
is complete for the
\omega^{\alpha^{\omega}}
level of the hyperarithmetical hierarchy (under Turing reductions).

For
\alpha=\omega
, the system
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\omega,\omega}
was previously studied in
[
21
]
where a sound and complete phase semantics was given.
In this vein an interesting special case of our results is:

Corollary 8.1
.

If
\omega\leq\alpha<\omega^{\omega}
, then provability in
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\alpha,\alpha}
is
\Sigma^{0}_{\omega^{\omega^{\omega}}}
-complete. In particular, the provability problems for all such
\alpha
are Turing-equivalent.

Proof.

1\leq\deg\alpha<\omega
for such
\alpha
, therefore,
\omega^{\alpha^{\omega}}=\omega^{\omega^{(\deg\alpha)\omega}}=\omega^{\omega^{\omega}}
. The result follows from Theorem
2.8
.
∎

In a related direction,
[
23
,
29
]
investigate the complexity of
infinitary action logic with multiplexing
.
This logic differs from
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\omega,\omega}
in several respects.
It is based on an intuitionistic non-commutative fragment of
\mathsf{MALL}
with certain fixed points in the form of a multiplexing subexponential and Kleene
*
.
This logic turns out to be
\Sigma^{0}_{\omega^{\omega}}
-complete, a whole
\omega
-tower lower than for
\mu\mathsf{MALL}_{\omega,\omega}
.
It would be interesting to understand more generally how combinations of logical features relate to hyperarithmetical complexity.

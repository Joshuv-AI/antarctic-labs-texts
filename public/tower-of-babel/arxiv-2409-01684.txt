Optimal control of quantum stochastic systems in fermion fields: The Pontryagin-type maximum principle (II)
Penghui Wang, Shan Wang

Abstract




Abstract

In the present paper, by using the relaxed transposition method
[
29
]
, we solve the second-order adjoint equations, corresponding to the optimal control of quantum stochastic systems in fermion fields, which plays the fundamental roles in the study of the Pontryagin-type maximum principle in quantum stochastic optimal control. The second-order adjoint equation is a backward operator-valued quantum stochastic differential equation, which has no definition in the algebra of bounded operators, and the solution derived from the relaxed transposition method makes sense in
W^{*}
-topology.

2020 AMS Subject Classification:
47C15, 81S25, 81Q93, 81V74.

Keywords.
The second-order adjoint equations; the relaxed transposition solution; quantum stochastic control; noncommutative probability spaces.

Quantum stochastic control systems arise through the interaction of quantum system with an external field, which can be expressed in the form of quantum stochastic differential equations (QSDEs, for short)

The observed process is fed into a controller, which can control the motion of the atoms through an actuator, e.g., a time-varying magnetic field.

Let

The anti-symmetric Fock space over
\mathscr{H}

\Lambda(\mathscr{H}):=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\Lambda_{n}(\mathscr{H}),

where

\Lambda_{0}(\mathscr{H}):=\mathbb{C}

For any

The annihilation operator

is the adjoint of

A(z)=C(z)^{*}

The fermion field
\Psi(z)
is defined on

by

\Psi(z):=C(z)+A(Jz),

(1.1)

where the map

\{\Psi(z),\Psi(z^{\prime})\}\equiv\Psi(z)\Psi(z^{\prime})+\Psi(z^{\prime})\Psi(z)=2\langle Jz^{\prime},z\rangle I,\quad z,z^{\prime}\in\mathscr{H}.

(1.2)

Let
\mathscr{C}
be von Neumann algebra generated by

By

is a quantum (noncommutative) probability space. For

For any von Neumann subalgebra

In this paper, let

[

By CAR (

Inspired by the classical control theory, we have investigated the optimal control problem of the following quantum stochastic control system in fermion fields:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dx(t)=&\widehat{D}(t,x(t),u(t))dt+\widehat{F}(t,x(t),u(t))dW(t)+dW(t)\widehat{G}(t,x(t),u(t)),\textrm{ in }(t_{0},T],\\

\mathcal{P}_{t}:\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C}))\to\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{t}))

\mathfrak{S}:=\left\{\sum_{k}a_{k}\chi_{[t_{k},t_{k+1})};a_{k}\in\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C}))\ \textrm{and}\ \{t_{k}\}_{k=0}^{n}\textrm{ is a partition of }[0,T]\right\},

which is dense in
L^{p}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))
. Denote
\mathbb{E}:\mathfrak{S}\to L_{\mathbb{A}}^{p}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))
by

(\mathbb{E}h)(t)=\sum_{k}(\mathcal{P}_{t_{k}}a_{k})\chi_{[t_{k},t_{k+1})}(t),\ t\in[0,T],

then
\|\mathbb{E}\|\leq 1
. Therefore,
\mathbb{E}
can be extended to

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}:L^{p}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))\to L_{\mathbb{A}}^{p}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))

with
\left\|\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\right\|\leq 1
. For any
h\in L^{p}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))
, there exists a sequence
\{h_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}\subseteq\mathfrak{S}
such that

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|h_{n}-h\|_{L^{p}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2})}=0.

Then there exists a subsequence
\{h_{n_{k}}\}_{n_{k}\geq 0}\subseteq\{h_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
such that
h_{n_{k}}\to h
, a.e.
Obviously,
\mathbb{E}h_{n_{k}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{E}}h\in L_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))
.
Besides,

\left(\mathbb{E}h_{n_{k}}\right)(t)=\mathcal{P}_{t}h_{n_{k}}(t)\to\mathcal{P}_{t}h(t),\ \textrm{a.e.}\ t\in[0,T].

Therefore,

(\widehat{\mathbb{E}}h)(t)=\mathcal{P}_{t}h(t),\ \textrm{a.e.}\ t\in[0,T].

For any
\ell\in\left(L^{p}_{\mathbb{A}}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))\right)^{*}
, by Hahn-Banach Theorem, there exists
\widehat{\ell}\in\left(L^{p}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))\right)^{*}
such that

\ell=\widehat{\ell}|_{L^{p}_{\mathbb{A}}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))},\quad\|\ell\|_{\left(L^{p}_{\mathbb{A}}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))\right)^{*}}=\left\|\widehat{\ell}\right\|_{\left(L^{p}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))\right)^{*}}.

In view of

\left(L^{p}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))\right)^{*}=L^{q}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C}))),

thus, there exists
g\in L^{q}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))
such that

\widehat{\ell}(f)=\int_{0}^{T}\langle g(t),f(t)\rangle_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}dt,\quad f\in L^{p}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C}))),

and

\left\|\widehat{\ell}\right\|_{(L^{p}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C}))))^{*}}=\|g\|_{L^{q}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2})}.

Then for any
f\in L^{p}_{\mathbb{A}}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))
,

\ell(f)=\widehat{\ell}(f)=\int_{0}^{T}\langle g(t),f(t)\rangle_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}dt=\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\left(\widehat{\mathbb{E}}g\right)(t),f(t)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}dt.

(2.3)

For
g\in L^{q}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))\subseteq L^{1}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))
,
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}g\in L^{1}_{\mathbb{A}}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))
is measurable. Further,

\left\|\widehat{\mathbb{E}}g\right\|_{L^{q}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2})}\leq\|g\|_{L^{q}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2})}
and
\left(\widehat{\mathbb{E}}g\right)(t)\in\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{t}))
a.e.
t\in[0,T]
, thus,

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}g\in L^{q}_{\mathbb{A}}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))
.

By (
2.3
) and Hölder inequality, it is obvious that

\|\ell\|_{\left(L^{p}_{\mathbb{A}}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))\right)^{*}}\leq\left\|\widehat{\mathbb{E}}g\right\|_{L^{q}_{\mathbb{A}}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2})}.

On the other hand,

\|\ell\|_{\left(L^{p}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))\right)^{*}}=\left\|\widehat{\ell}\right\|_{\left(L^{p}_{\mathbb{A}}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))\right)^{*}}=\|g\|_{L^{q}_{\mathbb{A}}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2})}\geq\left\|\widehat{\mathbb{E}}g\right\|_{L^{q}_{\mathbb{A}}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2})}.

Then,
\|\ell\|_{\left(L^{p}_{\mathbb{A}}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))\right)^{*}}=\left\|\widehat{\mathbb{E}}g\right\|_{L^{q}_{\mathbb{A}}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2})}
. Hence, (
2.2
) holds.
∎

(2.4)

Then for any

(2) If

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of
[

\displaystyle\|\{U(r,t)-U(r,t_{0})\}\xi_{1}\|_{2}

(2.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{t}^{r}D(s)\{U(s,t)-U(s,t_{0})\}\xi_{1}ds\right\|_{2}+\left\|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}D(s)U(s,t_{0})\xi_{1}ds\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle+\left\|\int_{t}^{r}F(s)\{U(s,t)-U(s,t_{0})\}\xi_{1}dW(s)\right\|_{2}+\left\|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}F(s)U(s,t_{0})\xi_{1}dW(s)\right\|_{2}.

\displaystyle\left(\int_{t}^{r}\left\|D(s)\{U(s,t)-U(s,t_{0})\}\xi_{1}\right\|_{2}ds\right)^{2}

3
The Solution to BQSDEs

In this section, we prove that the second-order adjoint equation (
1.4
) admits a unique relaxed transposition solution. To make it, we introduce the transposition solution with
P_{T}\in\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{T}))
,
\mathbb{H}\in L_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))
.

Definition 3.1
.

The pair

(P(\cdot),Q(\cdot))\in C_{\mathbb{A}}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))\times L_{\mathbb{A}}^{2}\left([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C}))\right)

is called to be a transposition solution to (
1.4
) if for any
t_{0}\in[0,T]
,
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{t_{0}})
,

\displaystyle\langle P_{T}x_{2}(T),x_{1}(T)\rangle-\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\langle\mathbb{H}(t)x_{2}(t),x_{1}(t)\rangle dt

(3.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle P(t_{0})\xi_{2},\xi_{1}\rangle+\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\langle P(t)u_{2}(t),x_{1}(t)\rangle dt+\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\langle P(t)x_{2}(t),u_{1}(t)\rangle dt

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\langle P(t)F(t)x_{2}(t),v_{1}(t)\rangle dt+\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\langle P(t)v_{2}(t),F(t)x_{1}(t)+v_{1}(t)\rangle dt

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\langle Q(t)\Upsilon v_{2}(t),x_{1}(t)\rangle dt+\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\langle Q(t)\Upsilon x_{2}(t),v_{1}(t)\rangle dt,

where

and

solve (

3.1
The transposition solution to BQSDEs

This subsection is dedicated to the transposition solution to the second-order adjoint equation (

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that

\mathbb{H}\in L_{\mathbb{A}}^{\infty}([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C})))

(P(\cdot),Q(\cdot))

(3.2)

Proof.

Because the proof is too long, we divide the proof into several steps.

Step 1.
We shall prove that the following linear BQSDE admits a unique transposition solution

\left\{\begin{aligned} &dP=g(t)dt+Q(t)dW(t),\quad\textrm{in}\ [0,T),\\

\left\{\begin{aligned} &dX=z(t)dt+w(t)dW(t),\quad\textrm{in}\ (t_{0},T],\\
&X(t_{0})=X_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.4)

where the initial condition

,

2.1
) and Picard iteration, similar to

(3.5)

Quantiles and Quantile Regression on Riemannian Manifolds a measure-transportation-based approach
Marc Hallin, Hang Liu

Abstract
Increased attention has been given recently to the statistical analysis of variables with values on nonlinear manifolds. A natural but nontrivial problem in that context is the definition of quantile concepts. We are proposing a solution for compact Riemannian manifolds without boundaries; typical examples are polyspheres, hyperspheres, and toroïdal manifolds equipped with their Riemannian metrics. Our concept of quantile function comes along with a concept of distribution function and, in the empirical case, ranks and signs. The absence of a canonical ordering is offset by resorting to the data-driven ordering induced by optimal transports. Theoretical properties, such as the uniform convergence of the empirical distribution and conditional (and unconditional) quantile functions and distribution-freeness of ranks and signs, are established. Statistical inference applications, from goodness-of-fit to distribution-free rank-based testing, are without number. Of particular importance is the case of quantile regression with directional or toroïdal multiple output, which is given special attention in this paper. Extensive simulations are carried out to illustrate these novel concepts.

3.2
Riemannian empirical quantile regions and contours, ranks, and signs

Unlike the real line, Riemannian manifolds are not canonically ordered, which makes the definition of ranks and signs problematic. The definition of ranks, actually, is directly related to the definition of empirical quantile contours: the rank
R^{(n)}_{i}
of the observation
\mathbf{Y}^{(n)}_{i}

R^{(n)}_{j}
of the observation
\mathbf{Y}^{(n)}_{j}

\mathbf{Y}^{(n)}_{i}

\mathbf{Y}^{(n)}_{j}

In
{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
(

A
Some basic concepts of Riemannian geometry

This section collects some basic concepts of Riemannian geometry that are used in the main part of the paper. For more details, we refer to the monographs by
Petersen (2006)
,
Jost (2008)
, and
Lee (2012)
.

To begin with, recall that a
Riemannian manifold
is a pair
({\cal M},g)
, where
{\cal M}
is a
smooth manifold
and
g
is a
Riemannian metric
on
{\cal M}
. Before defining what is meant with a
smooth
manifold, the following notions are needed.

Definition A.1
.

A topological space
{\cal M}
is a
topological
m
-manifold
if (i) it is a
Hausdorff space
, (ii) it is
second-countable
, and (iii) it is
locally
m
-dimensional Euclidean
, that is, each point in
{\cal M}
has a neighborhood that is homeomorphic to an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Definition A.2
.

A
chart
on a topological
m
-manifold
{\cal M}
is a pair
(U,\varphi)
, where
U
is an open subset of
{\cal M}
and
\varphi:U\rightarrow V
is a homeomorphism from
U
to an open subset
V=\varphi(U)\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
. An
atlas

{\cal A}
for
{\cal M}
is a collection of charts whose domains cover
{\cal M}
.

Definition A.3
.

Two charts
(U,\varphi)
and
(W,\psi)
on a topological
m
-manifold M are
smoothly compatible
if either
U\cap W=\emptyset
or the
transition map

\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}:\varphi(U\cap W)\rightarrow\psi(U\cap W)

is a diffeomorphism. An atlas
{\cal A}
is called a
smooth atlas
if any two charts on
{\cal A}
are smoothly compatible. A smooth atlas
{\cal A}
on
{\cal M}
is
maximal
if it is not contained in any strictly larger smooth atlas.

Now we are ready to provide the definition of a
smooth manifold
.

Definition A.4
.

A
smooth manifold
is a pair
({\cal M},{\cal A})
, where
{\cal M}
is a topological manifold and
{\cal A}
is a maximal smooth atlas on
{\cal M}
.

As in Euclidean spaces, a Riemannian metric on a smooth manifold
{\cal M}
is defined via an inner product on its tangent spaces (see, e.g.,
Lee (2012, Chapter 3)
).

Definition A.5
.

A
Riemannian metric

g
on a smooth manifold
{\cal M}
assigns an inner product

g_{\mathbf{y}}(\cdot,\cdot)=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbf{y}}

on the tangent space
T_{\mathbf{y}}{\cal M}
to each point
\mathbf{y}\in{\cal M}
.

Note that, using a local coordinate chart
(U,\varphi)
(with
U\ni\mathbf{y}
), the Riemannianmetric
g
can be characterized by a positive definite symmetric matrix
(g_{ij}(\mathbf{y}))_{i,j=1,\ldots,p}
, where
g_{ij}(\mathbf{y})
is such that

\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\rangle_{\mathbf{y}}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{p}g_{ij}(\mathbf{y})u_{i}v_{j}

for any pair
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{p})^{\top}
,
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p})^{\top}
of tangent vectors in
T_{\mathbf{y}}{\cal M}
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle{\rm P}\left[{d}_{\rm BL}\big({\rm P}^{(n)}_{\mathbf{w}^{(n)}(\mathbf{X})},{\rm P}_{\mathbf{Y}|\mathbf{X}}\big)>\epsilon\right]=

\displaystyle\,{\rm P}\left[\sup_{f\in{\cal F}_{{\rm BL}_{1}}^{\cal M}}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}^{(n)}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X}^{(n)})f(\mathbf{Y}_{j}^{(n)})-{\rm E}(f(\mathbf{Y})|\mathbf{X}))\right|>\epsilon\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,{\rm P}\left[\sup_{k=1,\ldots,m_{\epsilon}}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}^{(n)}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X}^{(n)})f_{k}(\mathbf{Y}_{j}^{(n)})-{\rm E}(f_{k}(\mathbf{Y})|\mathbf{X}))\right|>\epsilon/2\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\sum_{k=1}^{m_{\epsilon}}{\rm P}\left[\left|\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}^{(n)}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X}^{(n)})f_{k}(\mathbf{Y}_{j}^{(n)})-{\rm E}(f_{k}(\mathbf{Y})|\mathbf{X}))\right|>\epsilon/2\right].

Since
\mathbf{w}^{(n)}
is a consistent weight function, the RHS of the above inequality is bounded by a positive number
\delta
that can be arbitrarily small. The result follows.
\square

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Define

G_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})\coloneqq{\rm E}_{{\rm P}_{\mathbf{Y}|\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{x}}}[c(\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{y})],\,G_{\mathbf{x}}^{(n)}(\mathbf{y})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}^{(n)}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{X}^{(n)})c(\mathbf{Y}_{j},\mathbf{y})

and let
a\coloneqq\min\{G_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y}):\mathbf{y}\in{\cal M}\}
and
\delta(\epsilon)>0
be such that

a+\delta(\epsilon)=\min\{G_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y}):\mathbf{y}\in{\cal M}_{1}\}

where
{\cal M}_{1}\coloneqq{\cal M}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{\rm Fr}^{\epsilon}(\mathbf{x})
for some
\epsilon>0
. It is sufficient to prove that

\max_{\mathbf{y}\in{\cal M}}|G_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})-G_{\mathbf{x}}^{(n)}(\mathbf{y})|=o_{\rm P}(1)

(B.2)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. To see this, note that, if (
B.2
) holds, then

\min\{G_{\mathbf{x}}^{(n)}(\mathbf{y}):\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm Fr}^{\epsilon}(\mathbf{x})\}\leq a+\frac{\delta(\epsilon)}{3}+o_{\rm P}(1)

and

\min\{G_{\mathbf{x}}^{(n)}(\mathbf{y}):\mathbf{y}\in{\cal M}_{1}\}\geq a+\frac{\delta(\epsilon)}{2}+o_{\rm P}(1),

from which the desired result in Proposition
4.2
follows.

Now, for any
\mathbf{y},\tilde{\mathbf{y}}\in{\cal M}
, by definition of
G_{\mathbf{x}}(\cdot)
, we have

\displaystyle|G_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})-G_{\mathbf{x}}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})|

\displaystyle\leq\max\{|c(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{y})-c(\mathbf{z},\tilde{\mathbf{y}})|:\mathbf{z}\in{\cal M}\}

\displaystyle=\max\{|d^{2}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{y})-d^{2}(\mathbf{z},\tilde{\mathbf{y}})|/2:\mathbf{z}\in{\cal M}\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{|u^{2}-\tilde{u}^{2}|/2:|u-\tilde{u}|\leq d(\mathbf{y},\tilde{\mathbf{y}}),\quad u,\tilde{u}\in[0,{\rm diam}({\cal M})]\}

where
{\rm diam}({\cal M})
denotes the diameter of
{\cal M}
(the last inequality above is due to the fact that
|d(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{y})-d(\mathbf{z},\tilde{\mathbf{y}})|\leq d(\mathbf{y},\tilde{\mathbf{y}})
). In the same manner, we have

|G^{(n)}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})-G^{(n)}_{\mathbf{x}}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})|\leq\max\{|u^{2}-\tilde{u}^{2}|/2:|u-\tilde{u}|\leq d(\mathbf{y},\tilde{\mathbf{y}}),\quad u,\tilde{u}\in[0,{\rm diam}({\cal M})]\}.

Therefore,
G_{\mathbf{x}}(\cdot)
and
G^{(n)}_{\mathbf{x}}(\cdot)
are uniformly continuous on
{\cal M}
, and hence there exists
\delta(\tilde{\epsilon})>0
such that, for
d(\mathbf{y},\tilde{\mathbf{y}})<\delta(\tilde{\epsilon})
,

|G_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})-G_{\mathbf{x}}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})|\leq\frac{\tilde{\epsilon}}{4}\ \ \text{and}\ \ |G^{(n)}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})-G^{(n)}_{\mathbf{x}}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})|\leq{\tilde{\epsilon}}/{4}.

Let
\{\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{k}\}
be a
\delta(\tilde{\epsilon})
-net of
{\cal M}
—namely, be such that, for all
\mathbf{y}\in{\cal M}
, there exists a
\tilde{\mathbf{y}}\in\{\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{k}\}
such that
d(\mathbf{y},\tilde{\mathbf{y}})<\delta(\tilde{\epsilon})
. It follows from (
4.2
) that, for any
\tilde{\epsilon}>0
,

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}{\rm P}[|G_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y}_{\ell})-G^{(n)}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y}_{\ell})|>\tilde{\epsilon}/4]=0,\ \forall\ell=1,\ldots,k.

Then, for all
\mathbf{y}\in{\cal M}
,

\displaystyle|G_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})-G_{\mathbf{x}}^{(n)}(\mathbf{y})|

\displaystyle\leq|G_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})-G_{\mathbf{x}}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})|+|G_{\mathbf{x}}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})-G_{\mathbf{x}}^{(n)}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})|+|G^{(n)}_{\mathbf{x}}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})-G_{\mathbf{x}}^{(n)}(\mathbf{y})|\leq\frac{3\tilde{\epsilon}}{4}

with probability tending to one as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore, (
B.2
) holds and the desired result follows.
\square

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.
Denote by
\mathbf{Y}_{j}
,
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
the sample point closest to
\hat{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}^{(n)}(\mathbf{x})
in the
d(\cdot,\cdot)
metric. By Assumption
8
,
\mathbf{Y}_{j}
is a consistent estimator of
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{x})
. For any fixed
\mathbf{x}\in{\cal M}_{\mathbf{X}}
, by the definition of
{\scriptstyle\mathfrak{G}}^{(N)}_{*}(\mathbf{x})
, there exists a
c
-convex function
\psi^{(N,n)}_{\mathbf{x}}
such that

{\scriptstyle\mathfrak{G}}^{(N)}_{*}(\mathbf{x})=\exp_{\mathbf{Y}_{j}}(\nabla\psi_{\mathbf{x}}^{(N,n)}(\mathbf{Y}_{j})).

Repeating the same arguments as in the proof of Proposition
3.1
, we obtain that
\exp_{\mathbf{Y}_{j}}(\nabla\psi_{\mathbf{x}}^{(N,n)}(\mathbf{Y}_{j}))

\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{Y}_{j}|\mathbf{x})
. Therefore, due to the continuity of
\mathbf{F}

\mathbf{Y}_{j}

{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{x})
,
{\scriptstyle\mathfrak{G}}^{(N)}_{*}(\mathbf{x})

Proof of Proposition
4.3
.
Part (i). The a.s. uniqueness of
\mathbf{F}
and Proposition
2.2
imply that, for each
\mathbf{x}\in{\cal M}_{0}
, there exists a
c
-convex function
\phi_{\mathbf{x}}
such that

\mathbf{Q}({\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{G}}}^{(N)}_{1i}(\mathbf{x})|\mathbf{x})=\exp_{{\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{G}}}^{(N)}_{1i}}(\nabla\phi_{\mathbf{x}}({\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{G}}}^{(N)}_{1i}(\mathbf{x})))=\partial_{c}\phi_{\mathbf{x}}({\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{G}}}^{(N)}_{1i}(\mathbf{x}))

is
\rm P
-a.s. a singleton. Also, there exists a
c
-convex function
\phi_{\mathbf{x}}^{(N,n)}
such that

\mathbf{Q}_{\mathbf{w}}^{(N,n)}({\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{G}}}^{(N)}_{1i}(\mathbf{x})|\mathbf{x})=\exp_{{\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{G}}}^{(N)}_{1i}}(\nabla\phi_{\mathbf{x}}^{(N,n)}({\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{G}}}^{(N)}_{1i}(\mathbf{x}))).

Due to Proposition
4.1
, it follows from the same arguments as in the proof of Lemma 12 in
Hallin, Liu and Verdebout (2024)
that
\partial_{c}\phi_{\mathbf{x}}^{(N,n)}\to_{\rm PK}\partial_{c}\phi_{\mathbf{x}}
as
N,n\rightarrow\infty
. To conclude the proof of part (i), assume that there exists a sequence
(\mathbf{y}_{n},\mathbf{z}_{n})\in{\cal M}\times\partial_{c}\phi_{\mathbf{x}}^{(N,n)}(\mathbf{y}_{n})
such that, for all
n
and some
\delta>0
,

c(\mathbf{z}_{n},\partial_{c}\phi_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y}_{n}))>\delta>0.

(B.3)

Since
{\cal M}
is compact, there exists a subsequence of
\mathbf{y}_{n}
that converges to some
\mathbf{y}\in{\cal M}
. The continuity of
\partial_{c}\phi_{\mathbf{x}}
together with the fact that
\partial_{c}\phi_{\mathbf{x}}^{(N,n)}\to_{\rm PK}\partial_{c}\phi_{\mathbf{x}}
imply that
\mathbf{z}_{n}
converges to
\partial_{c}\phi_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})=\mathbf{Q}(\mathbf{y}|\mathbf{x})
, which contradicts (
B.3
). Part (i) follows.

Part (ii). Let

\mathfrak{H}_{r}^{(N)}(\mathbf{x})\coloneqq\begin{cases}\mathring{\mathfrak{G}}^{(N_{0})}_{\widehat{\cal M}^{(N)}_{0}(\mathbf{x})}&\text{if}\ \ r=0\\
{\mathfrak{G}}^{(N_{S})}_{\widehat{\cal M}^{(N)}_{0}(\mathbf{x})}(1)\bigcup\cdots\bigcup{\mathfrak{G}}^{(N_{S})}_{\widehat{\cal M}^{(N)}_{0}(\mathbf{x})}(r)&\text{if}\ \ r\geq 1\\
\end{cases}

denote the subset of
\mathfrak{G}^{(N)}_{\widehat{\cal M}^{(N)}_{0}(\mathbf{x})}
that is used in the construction of the conditional (on
\mathbf{X}=~\!\mathbf{x}
) quantile regions.
We first show that, for all
r\in{\cal R}_{N}
, the Hausdorff distance between
\mathfrak{H}_{r}^{(N)}(\mathbf{x})
and
\mathbb{C}_{{\cal M}_{0}(\mathbf{x})}^{\rm U}(\tau_{r}|\mathbf{x})
(
\tau_{r}=r/(N_{R}+1)
) converges to
0
.

The proof of Lemma
4.1
implies that, with probability one,
{\scriptstyle\mathfrak{G}}^{(N)}_{*}(\mathbf{x})
belongs to the
\epsilon_{2}
-fattening of
\{\mathbf{F}({\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{x})|\mathbf{x})\}
for any
\epsilon_{2}>0
. Indeed, it implies that, as
n,N\rightarrow\infty
,

\max_{r\in{\cal R}_{N}}d_{\rm H}\left(\mathfrak{H}_{r}^{(N)}(\mathbf{x}),\tilde{\mathfrak{H}}_{r}^{(N)}(\mathbf{x})\right)=o_{\rm P}(1),

where
\tilde{\mathfrak{H}}_{r}^{(N)}(\mathbf{x})
denotes the set of regular grids with center
{\scriptstyle\mathfrak{G}}^{(N)}_{*}(\mathbf{x})
replaced by
\mathbf{F}({\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{x})|\mathbf{x})
in the definition of
\mathfrak{H}_{r}^{(N)}(\mathbf{x})
. By this construction, we have

\max_{r\in{\cal R}_{N}}d_{\rm H}\left(\tilde{\mathfrak{H}}_{r}^{(N)}(\mathbf{x}),\mathbb{C}_{{\cal M}_{0}(\mathbf{x})}^{\rm U}(\tau_{r}|\mathbf{x})\right)=o_{\rm P}(1)\quad\text{as}\ \ N\rightarrow\infty.

Therefore, as
n
and
N\rightarrow\infty
,

\max_{r\in{\cal R}_{N}}d_{\rm H}\left({\mathfrak{H}}_{r}^{(N)}(\mathbf{x}),\mathbb{C}_{{\cal M}_{0}(\mathbf{x})}^{\rm U}(\tau_{r}|\mathbf{x})\right)=o_{\rm P}(1).

Now, the continuity of
\mathbf{Q}
entails that

\max_{r\in{\cal R}_{N}}d_{\rm H}\left(\{\mathbf{Q}(\mathbf{y}_{1}|\mathbf{x}):\mathbf{y}_{1}\in\mathfrak{H}_{r}^{(N)}(\mathbf{x})\},\{\mathbf{Q}(\mathbf{y}_{2}|\mathbf{x}):\mathbf{y}_{2}\in\mathbb{C}_{{\cal M}_{0}(\mathbf{x})}^{\rm U}(\tau_{r}|\mathbf{x})\right)=o_{\rm P}(1)

as

. The desired uniform convergence of

\mathbb{C}_{{\cal M}_{0}(\mathbf{x})}^{\rm P}(\cdot|\mathbf{x})
then follows from the uniform convergence of

to
\mathbf{Q}(\cdot|\mathbf{x})
in part (i). The same arguments yield the desired uniform convergence of

to
\mathcal{C}_{{\cal M}_{0}(\mathbf{x})}^{\rm P}(\cdot|\mathbf{x})
.
\square

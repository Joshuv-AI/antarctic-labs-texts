Re-evaluation of oxygenation and oxygen exposure in patients with ARDS and veno-venous extracorporeal membrane oxygenation
Authors: Victoria Bünger, Martin Ruß, Luigi La Via, Oliver Hunsicker, Mario Menk, Wolfgang M. Kuebler, Steffen Weber-Carstens, Jan A. Graw
Published: 2026

Correspondence

Optimal oxygen target levels for critically ill patients have been discussed for decades and very low but also high PaO2-levels were both associated with higher mortality [1, 2]. In patients with ARDS and refractory hypoxemic respiratory failure, venovenous Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation (VV-ECMO) can be implemented as a rescue therapy and blood flow of the VV-ECMO offers some control over the PaO2-levels. While severe hyperoxemia in patients treated with venoarterial ECMO was found associated with increased mortality and adverse events, data on patients with ARDS and VV-ECMO treatment are scarce [3].

In n = 443 patients with ARDS (all non-COVID19) admitted to a tertiary ARDS referral center and treated with VV-ECMO, 88.328 arterial blood gas measurements taken during therapy with VV-ECMO were available and time-weighted averages of PaO2 and FiO2 over VV-ECMO therapy time were calculated [4]. Mean PaO2 differed significantly between survivors and non-survivors (95.70 [87.46,109.20] vs. 84.27 [75.36, 96.99], p < 0.001) as did mean FiO2 (0.54 [0.45–0.62] vs. 0.66 [0.54–0.81], p < 0.001). Simple logistic regression analysis was performed for mortality and mean PaO2 and showed a significant association for mean PaO2 and ICU mortality (per mmHg OR 0.99 [95%CI 0.98-1.00], p = 0.002). Considering a possible non-linear relation between mortality and PaO2, a restricted cubic spline (RCS) for mean PaO2 was utilized for a simple logistic regression model.

RCS analysis showed a U-shaped association of mortality and mean PaO2 (per mmHg PaO2: PaO2 69.70-90.24mmHg: OR 0.92 [95%CI 0.89–0.94], p < 0.001, PaO2 90.24-123.40mmHg: OR 1.09 [1.06–1.13], p < 0.001, Fig. 1A) with both, low and high PaO2-levels associated with an increased risk of death. The U-shaped relationship between mean PaO2 and mortality was similarly evident when only the initial period of ECMO therapy (up to 72 h) was analyzed to address the potential reverse causality of PaO2-levels (Additional file 1).

A bivariate logistic regression model was calculated for mean PaO2 and mean FiO2. While PaO₂ demonstrated no significant association with mortality (OR 1.00, 95% CI 0.99–1.01, p = 0.413), FiO₂ emerged as an independent predictor of mortality (OR 82.59, 95% CI 21.19-348.68, p < 0.001; Fig. 1B) and collinearity assessment confirmed minimal interdependence between these variables (VIF < 1.11). However, a model including PaO2 as restricted cubic spline variable and FiO2 showed significant associations of mortality with both variables (PaO2 69.70-90.24mmHg: OR 0.94 [0.91–0.97], p < 0.001; PaO2 90.24-123.40mmHg: OR 1.07 [1.04–1.11], p < 0.001; FiO2: OR 35.98 [8.67–158.60], p < 0.001, VIF < 1.14). A visualization of the predictions of this multivariable model is shown in Fig. 1C. The associations were confirmed in a multivariable logistic regression model of mortality with mean PaO2 (RCS) and mean FiO2 adjusted for ECMO blood and sweep gas flow, pulmonary compliance, driving pressure, initial, pre-ECMO PaO2/FiO2 ratio, and ECMO therapy time (Additional file 1).

Patients with ARDS differ from the general ICU population in their reduced potential to achieve extreme hyperoxemia. In 202 patients with ARDS, Boyle and colleagues demonstrated a U-shaped relationship between PaO2-levels during the first 7 days of ARDS and mortality with the lowest ICU-mortality risk at an average time-weighted PaO2 between 93.8 and 105 mmHg [5]. Increased tissue oxygenation facilitated by VV-ECMO with concurrent avoidance of pulmonary hyperoxia might thus be associated with reduced mortality in patients with ARDS and VV-ECMO exposed to higher PaO2-levels. In contrast to the work by Boyle and colleagues, the current analysis considers a cumulative dose-response model of oxygen toxicity, assuming that biological harm is associated with total oxygen exposure over time, rather than with threshold effects from individual peak values.

The current analysis is limited by the retrospective and single-center data acquisition potentially limiting generalizability to different patient populations or management approaches. The observational nature precludes causal inferences regarding the relationship between PaO₂-levels and mortality. Sicker patients might have received higher FiO2 due to operator bias and surviving patients might accumulate higher PaO₂-levels over the course of ECMO-therapy compared to non-survivors. The adjustments for ECMO settings, ventilation parameters and pre-ECMO pulmonary gas exchange reduced, yet without being able to fully eliminate, confounding by ARDS severity. Importantly, FdO2 is fixed at 1.0 over the entire course of therapy with VV-ECMO as a standard of care at our ARDS center, ruling out potential confounding effects of varying FdO2 settings. Despite our rigorous analytical approach, unknown and/or unmeasured confounding variables may influence the observed associations. In addition, the potential interaction between PaO₂-levels and other physiological parameters such as arterial carbon dioxide tension, pH, hemoglobin concentration, or cardiac output, which collectively determine oxygen delivery to tissues as well as the ratio of ECMO flow to cardiac output were not analyzed and may have impacted the results and conclusions of the present study. The ECMO-flow-to-cardiac-output ratio was not available in our dataset. Due to the very nature of retrospective data analyses, a certain degree of reverse causality for the PaO2-levels cannot be fully ruled out despite adjustment for ECMO duration and pre-ECMO severity of ARDS. Finally, very short periods of relevant hypoxemia may have a tremendous impact on mortality which is neglected analyzing time-weighted averages of the PaO₂- and FiO2-levels in the assumption of a cumulative dose-response to oxygen toxicity.

Taken together the presented data demonstrate that PaO₂-levels in patients with ARDS and treatment with VV-ECMO have a U-shaped association with mortality. Whether increased tissue oxygenation with concurrent avoidance of pulmonary hypoxia is beneficial should be subject of future research.

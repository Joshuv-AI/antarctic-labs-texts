Random Permutations in Computational Complexity A preliminary version of this paper appeared the Proceedings of the 50th International Symposium on Mathematical Foundations of Computer Science (MFCS 2025) [ 16 ] .
John M. Hitchcock, Adewale Sekoni, Hadi Shafei

Abstract
Classical results of Bennett and Gill (1981) show that with probability 1, 𝖯 A ≠ 𝖭𝖯 A \mathsf{P}^{A}\neq\mathsf{NP}^{A} relative to a random oracle A A , and with probability 1, 𝖯 π ≠ 𝖭𝖯 π ∩ 𝖼𝗈𝖭𝖯 π \mathsf{P}^{\pi}\neq\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{\pi} relative to a random permutation π \pi . Whether 𝖯 A = 𝖭𝖯 A ∩ 𝖼𝗈𝖭𝖯 A \mathsf{P}^{A}=\mathsf{NP}^{A}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{A} holds relative to a random oracle A A remains open. While the random oracle separation has been extended to specific individually random oracles–such as Martin-Löf random or resource-bounded random oracles–no analogous result is known for individually random permutations. We introduce a new resource-bounded measure framework for analyzing individually random permutations. We define permutation martingales and permutation betting games that characterize measure-zero sets in the space of permutations, enabling formal definitions of polynomial-time random permutations, polynomial-time betting-game random permutations, and polynomial-space random permutations. Our main result shows that 𝖯 π ≠ 𝖭𝖯 π ∩ 𝖼𝗈𝖭𝖯 π \mathsf{P}^{\pi}\neq\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{\pi} for every polynomial-time betting-game random permutation π \pi . This is the first separation result relative to individually random permutations, rather than an almost-everywhere separation. We also strengthen a quantum separation of Bennett, Bernstein, Brassard, and Vazirani (1997) by showing that 𝖭𝖯 π ∩ 𝖼𝗈𝖭𝖯 π ⊈ 𝖡𝖰𝖯 π \mathsf{NP}^{\pi}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}\not\subseteq\mathsf{BQP}^{\pi} for every polynomial-space random permutation π \pi . We investigate the relationship between random permutations and random oracles. We prove that random oracles are polynomial-time reducible from random permutations. The converse–whether every random permutation is reducible from a random oracle–remains open. We show that if 𝖭𝖯 ∩ 𝖼𝗈𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP} is not a measurable subset of 𝖤𝖷𝖯 \mathsf{EXP} , then 𝖯 A ≠ 𝖭𝖯 A ∩ 𝖼𝗈𝖭𝖯 A \mathsf{P}^{A}\neq\mathsf{NP}^{A}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{A} holds with probability 1 relative to a random oracle A A . Conversely, establishing this random oracle separation with time-bounded measure would imply 𝖡𝖯𝖯 \mathsf{BPP} is a measure 0 subset of 𝖤𝖷𝖯 \mathsf{EXP} . Our framework builds a foundation for studying permutation-based complexity using resource-bounded measure, in direct analogy to classical work on random oracles. It raises natural questions about the power and limitations of random permutations, their relationship to random oracles, and whether individual randomness can yield new class separations.



1
Introduction

The seminal work of Bennett and Gill
[
5
]
established two foundational separations in computational complexity theory:

1.

\mathsf{P}^{A}\neq\mathsf{NP}^{A}
relative to a random oracle
A
with probability 1.

2.

\mathsf{P}^{\pi}\neq\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}
relative to a random permutation
\pi
with probability 1.

Subsequent research extended the first separation to hold for specific, individually random oracles, including algorithmically (Martin-Löf) random oracles
[
6
]
, polynomial-space-bounded random oracles
[
22
]
, and polynomial-time betting-game random oracles
[
15
]
. However, the second separation has not yet been strengthened in an analogous way.
Whether
\mathsf{P}^{A}\neq\mathsf{NP}^{A}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{A}

holds relative to a random oracle
A
remains an open question.

In this paper, we develop a novel framework for resource-bounded permutation measure and randomness, introducing
permutation martingales
and
permutation betting
games. These concepts generalize classical martingales and betting games to the space
\mathsf{\Pi}
of all length-preserving permutations
\pi:\{0,1\}^{*}\to\{0,1\}^{*}
where
|\pi(x)|=|x|
for all
x\in\{0,1\}^{*}
.

1.1
Background

Bennett and Gill
[
5
]
initiated the study of random oracles in computational complexity, proving that
\mathsf{P}^{A}\neq\mathsf{NP}^{A}
for a random oracle
A
with probability 1. Subsequent work extended this to individual random oracles. Book, Lutz, and Wagner
[
6
]
showed that
\mathsf{P}^{A}\neq\mathsf{NP}^{A}
for every oracle
A
that is algorithmically random in the sense of Martin-Löf
[
23
]
. Lutz and Schmidt
[
22
]
improved this further to show
\mathsf{P}^{A}\neq\mathsf{NP}^{A}
for every oracle
A
that is pspace-random in the sense of resource-bounded measure
[
20
]
. Hitchcock, Sekoni, and Shafei
[
15
]
extended this result to polynomial-time betting-game random oracles
[
7
]
.

The complexity class
\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}
is particularly significant because it comprises problems that have both efficiently verifiable proofs of membership and non-membership. This class includes important problems such as integer factorization and discrete logarithm, which are widely believed to be outside
\mathsf{P}
but are not known to be
\mathsf{NP}
-complete. These problems play a central role in cryptography, as the security of widely-used cryptosystems relies on their presumed intractability
[
28
,
8
]
.
Furthermore, under derandomization hypotheses,
\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}
has been shown to contain problems such as graph isomorphism
[
19
]
, further underscoring its importance in complexity theory. Thus, understanding the relationship between
\mathsf{P}
and
\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}
relative to different notions of randomness could shed light on the structure of these classes and the limits of efficient computation.

1.2
Our Approach: Permutation Martingales and Permutation Measure

In this work, we develop a novel framework for resource-bounded permutation measure and randomness. We introduce permutation martingales and permutation betting games, extending classical notions of random permutations. Our theory captures essential properties of random permutations while enabling complexity separations. We prove that random oracles can be computed in polynomial time from a random permutation; however, the converse remains unresolved.

First, we recall the basics of resource-bounded measure.
A martingale in Cantor space may be viewed as betting on the membership of strings in a language. The standard enumeration of
\{0,1\}^{*}
is
s_{0}=\lambda,s_{1}=0,s_{2}=1,s_{3}=00,s_{4}=01,\ldots
. In the
{i^{\mathrm{th}}}
stage of the game, the martingale has seen the membership of the first
i
strings and bets on the membership of
s_{i}
in the language. The martingale’s value is updated based on the outcome of the bet.
Formally, a classical martingale is a function
d:\{0,1\}^{*}\to[0,\infty)
satisfying the fairness condition

d(w)=\frac{d(w0)+d(w1)}{2}

for all strings
w
. Intuitively,
d(w)
represents the capital that a gambler has after betting on the sequence of bits in
w
according to a particular strategy. The fairness condition ensures that the expected capital after the next bit is equal to the current capital. A martingale succeeds on a language
A\subseteq\{0,1\}^{*}
if

\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}d(A\negthinspace\upharpoonright\negthinspace n)=\infty,

where
A\negthinspace\upharpoonright\negthinspace n
is the length-
n
prefix of
A
’s characteristic sequence. The
success set
of
d
is
S^{\infty}[d]
, the set of all sequences that
d
succeeds on. Ville
[
31
]
proved that a set
X
has Lebesgue measure zero if and only if there is a martingale that succeeds on all elements of
X
. Lutz
[
20
]
defined resource-bounded measure by imposing computability and complexity constraints on the martingales in Ville’s theorem.

We take a similar approach in developing resource-bounded permutation measure. Unlike a classical martingale betting on the bits of a language’s characteristic sequence, a permutation martingale bets on the function values of a permutation
\pi
.
Instead of seeing the characteristic string of a language, a permutation martingale sees a list of permutation function values. More precisely, after
i\geq 0
rounds of betting, a permutation martingale has seen a
prefix partial permutation

g=[g(s_{0}),\ldots,g(s_{i-1})]

where
|g(s_{i})|=|s_{i}|
for all
i
. The permutation martingale will bet on the next function value
g(s_{i})
. The current
betting length
is
l(g)=|s_{i}|
, the length of the next string
s_{i}
in the standard enumeration.
The set of
free strings
available for the next function value is

\mathsf{free}(g)=\left\{x\in\{0,1\}^{l(g)}\left|x\text{ is not listed in }g\right.\right\}.

For any prefix partial permutation
g
,
a permutation martingale
d
outputs a value
d(g,x)\geq 0
for each
x\in\mathsf{free}(g)
. The values satisfy the averaging condition

d(g)=\frac{1}{|\text{free}(g)|}\sum_{x\in\text{free}(g)}{d(g,x)}.

Here
g,x
denotes appending the string
x
as the next function value in prefix partial permutation
g
. See Figure
1.1
for an example of a permutation martingale betting on strings of length 2.

Prefix partial permutations may be used as cylinders to define a measure in
\mathsf{\Pi}
that is equivalent to the natural product probability measure. We detail this in
Section

3
. Briefly, a class
X\subseteq\mathsf{\Pi}
has measure 0 if for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a sequence of cylinders
\{\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\}
that has total measure at most
\epsilon
and covers
X
.
This is difficult to work with computationally as the covers may be large and require exponential time to enumerate.

We prove an analogue of Ville’s theorem
[
31
]
,
showing that permutation martingales characterize measure 0 sets in the permutation space
\mathsf{\Pi}
: a class
X\subseteq\mathsf{\Pi}
has measure 0 if and only if there a permutation martingale
d
with
X\subseteq S^{\infty}[d]
. This permutation martingale characterization allows us to impose computability and complexity constraints in the same way Lutz did for resource-bounded measure in Cantor space
[
20
]
. In the following, let
\Delta
be a resource bound such as
{\mathsf{p}}
,
{{\mathsf{p}}_{\thinspace\negthinspace{}_{2}}}
,
{\mathsf{pspace}}
, or
{\mathsf{p}_{\thinspace\negthinspace{}_{2}}\mathsf{space}}
(see
Section

3.5
for more details).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\Delta
be a resource bound.
A class of permutations
X\subseteq\mathsf{\Pi}
has
\Delta
-measure 0 if there is a
\Delta
-computable permutation martingale that succeeds on
X
.

Betting games
[
7
,
24
]
are a generalization of martingales that are allowed to bet on strings in an adaptive order rather than the standard order.
We analogously introduce permutation betting games as a generalization of both permutation martingales and classical betting games by allowing the betting strategy to adaptively choose the order in which it bets on the permutation’s values. We use these betting games to define resource-bounded permutation betting-game measure.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\Delta
be a resource bound.
A class of permutations
X\subseteq\mathsf{\Pi}
has
\Delta
-betting game measure 0 if there is a
\Delta
-computable permutation betting game that succeeds on
X
.

We also define
individually
random permutations.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\pi\in\mathsf{\Pi}
be a permutation and let
\Delta
be a resource bound.

1.

\pi
is
\Delta
-random if no
\Delta
-permutation martingale succeeds on
\pi
.

2.

\pi
is
\Delta
-betting game random if no
\Delta
-permutation betting game succeeds on
\pi
.

1.3
Our Results

Our main result strengthens the Bennett–Gill permutation separation by proving that
\mathsf{P}\neq\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}
relative to every polynomial-time betting-game random permutation
\pi
. Formally,
Theorem

5.1
establishes that

\mathsf{P}^{\pi}\neq\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}

for every
{\mathsf{p}}
-betting-game random permutation
\pi
. In fact, we obtain even stronger separations in terms of bi-immunity
[
9
,
3
]
, a notion formalizing the absence of infinite, easily-decidable subsets (see
Section

5
for more details). We show that for a
{\mathsf{p}}
-betting-game random permutation
\pi
, the class
\mathsf{NLIN}^{\pi}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NLIN}^{\pi}
contains languages that are bi-immune to
\mathsf{DTIME}^{\pi}(2^{kn})
for all
k\geq 1
, where
\mathsf{NLIN}
denotes nondeterministic linear time. Moreover, relative to a
{{\mathsf{p}}_{\thinspace\negthinspace{}_{2}}}
-betting-game random permutation, we derive that
\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}
contains languages that are bi-immune to
\mathsf{DTIME}^{\pi}(2^{n^{k}})
for every
k\geq 1
.

Bennett et al.
[
4
]
showed
that
\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}\not\subseteq\mathsf{BQTIME}^{\pi}(o(2^{n/3}))
relative to a random permutation
\pi
with probability 1. We apply our resource-bounded permutation measure framework to improve this to individual space-bounded random oracles.
Specifically, we show that relative to a
{\mathsf{p}_{\thinspace\negthinspace{}_{2}}\mathsf{space}}
-random permutation
\pi
,

\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}\not\subseteq\mathsf{BQP}^{\pi}.

This illustrates the power of our framework for analyzing the interplay between randomness, classical complexity, and quantum complexity.

1.4
Random Oracles and Measure 0-1 Laws in
\mathsf{EXP}

Tardos
[
29
]
proved that if
\mathsf{AM}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{AM}\neq\mathsf{BPP}
, then
\mathsf{P}^{A}\neq\mathsf{NP}^{A}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{A}
with probability 1 for a random oracle
A
.

This is proved using
\mathsf{ALMOST}
complexity classes.
For a relativizable complexity class
{\cal C}
, its
\mathsf{ALMOST}\mbox{-}{\cal C}
class consists of all languages that are in the class with probability 1 relative to a random oracle:

\mathsf{ALMOST}\mbox{-}{\cal C}=\{L\mid\mathsf{Pr}[L\in{\cal C}^{A}]=1\}.

We have
\mathsf{ALMOST}\mbox{-}\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{BPP}

[
5
]
and
\mathsf{ALMOST}\mbox{-}\mathsf{NP}=\mathsf{AM}

[
26
]
. The condition
\mathsf{AM}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{AM}\neq\mathsf{BPP}
implies that there exist problems in
\mathsf{ALMOST}\mbox{-}\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{ALMOST}\mbox{-}\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}
that are not in
\mathsf{ALMOST}\mbox{-}\mathsf{P}
. Since the intersection of measure 1 classes is measure 1, this implies
\mathsf{NP}^{A}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{A}\neq\mathsf{P}^{A}
relative to a random oracle
A
with probability 1.
Recent work of Ghosal et al.
[
10
]
shows that if
\mathsf{UP}\not\subseteq\mathsf{RP}
, then
\mathsf{P}^{A}\neq\mathsf{NP}^{A}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{A}
with probability 1 for a random oracle
A
.
In
Section

7
we pivot from permutation randomness to classical random oracles and show that resolving the long‑standing question “does
\mathsf{P}^{R}=\mathsf{NP}^{R}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{R}
with probability 1?” is tightly linked to quantitative structure inside
\mathsf{EXP}
. Leveraging the conditional oracle separations of Tardos
[
29
]
and of Ghosal et al.
[
10
]
, we prove that if
\mathsf{P}^{R}=\mathsf{NP}^{R}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{R}
holds almost surely, then several familiar subclasses of
\mathsf{EXP}
obey strong 0-1 laws: specifically, either
\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}
,
\mathsf{UP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{UP}
, (and, in a weaker form,
\mathsf{UP}
vs.
\mathsf{FewP}
) each has
{\mathsf{p}}
-measure 0 or else fills all of
\mathsf{EXP}
. Consequently, non‑measurability of any one of these classes immediately forces
\mathsf{P}^{R}\neq\mathsf{NP}^{R}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{R}
with probability 1. We further show that placing the same oracle separation in
{{\mathsf{p}}_{\thinspace\negthinspace{}_{2}}}
measure would collapse BPP below EXP, thereby framing the random-oracle problem in terms of concrete measure-theoretic thresholds inside exponential time.

Preliminaries

The binary alphabet is
\Sigma=\{0,1\}
,
the set of all binary strings is
\Sigma^{*}
, the set of all binary strings of
length
n
is
\Sigma^{n}
, and the set of all infinite binary sequences is

\Sigma^{\infty}
. The empty string is denoted by
\lambda
. We use
the standard enumeration of strings,

s_{0}=\lambda,s_{1}=0,s_{2}=1,s_{3}=00,s_{4}=01,\ldots
.
The characteristic sequence of a language
A
is the sequence

\chi_{A}\in\Sigma^{\infty}
, where

\chi_{A}[n]=1\iff s_{n}\in A
. We refer to
\chi_{A}[s_{n}]=\chi_{A}[n]
as the
characteristic bit of
s_{n}
in
A
. A language
A
can alternatively be seen
as a subset of
\Sigma^{*}
, or as an element of
\Sigma^{\infty}
via
identification with its characteristic sequence
\chi_{A}
. Given strings

x,y

we denote by
[x,y]
the set of all strings
z
such that
x\leq z\leq y
.
For any string
s_{n}
and natural number
k
,
s_{n}+k
is the string
s_{n+k}
; e.g.

\lambda+4=01
. Similarly we denote by
A[x,y]
the substring of the
characteristic sequence
\chi_{A}
that corresponds to the characteristic bits
of the strings in
[x,y]
. We use parentheses for intervals that do not
include the endpoints. We write
A\negthinspace\upharpoonright\negthinspace n
for the length
n
prefix of
A
. A statement
\mathcal{S}_{n}
holds infinitely
often (written i.o.) if it holds for infinitely many
n
, and it holds almost
everywhere (written a.e.) if it holds for all but finitely many
n
.

3
Permutation Martingales and Permutation Measure

3.1
Permutation Measure Space

Resource-bounded measure is typically defined in the Cantor Space

\mathsf{C}=\{0,1\}^{\infty}=2^{\mathbb{N}}
of all infinite binary sequences.
For measure in
\mathsf{C}
, we use the open balls or cylinders
\mathsf{C}_{w}=w\cdot\mathsf{C}
that
have measure
\mu(\mathsf{C}_{w})=2^{-|w|}
for each
w\in\Sigma^{*}
. Let

{\cal C}
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by

\{\mathsf{C}_{w}\mid w\in\{0,1\}^{*}\}
. Resource-bounded measure and algorithmic randomness typically work in the probability space
(\mathsf{C},{\cal C},\mu)
.

We only consider permutations in
\mathsf{\Pi}
, the set of permutations on

\{0,1\}^{*}
that preserve string lengths. Given a permutation

\pi\in\mathsf{\Pi}
, we denote by
\pi_{n}
the permutation
\pi
restricted to

\{0,1\}^{n}
i.e.,
\pi_{n}
is a permutation on
\{0,1\}^{n}
. Similarly,
\mathsf{\Pi}_{n}

denotes the set of permutations in
\mathsf{\Pi}
restricted to
\{0,1\}^{n}
. Bennett and Gill
[
5
]
considered random permutations by placing the uniform measure on each
\mathsf{\Pi}_{n}
and taking the product measure to get a measure on
\mathsf{\Pi}
. We now define this measure space more formally so we may place martingales on it.

Standard resource-bounded measure identifies a language
A\subseteq\{0,1\}^{*}
with its infinite binary characteristic sequence
\chi_{A}\in\mathsf{C}
.
For permutations, we analogously use the value sequence consisting of all function values.

Definition 3.1
.

The
value sequence
of a permutation
f\in\mathsf{\Pi}
is the sequence

\nu_{f}=[f(s_{0}),f(s_{1}),f(s_{2}),\ldots]

of function values where
s_{0},s_{1},s_{2},\ldots
is the standard enumeration of
\{0,1\}^{*}
.

We identify a permutation
f\in\mathsf{\Pi}
with its value sequence
\nu_{f}
. Initial segments of permutations are called prefix partial permutations.

Definition 3.2
.

A
prefix partial permutation
is a list

g=[g(s_{0}),\ldots,g(s_{N-1})]

of function values for some
N\geq 0
where no value is repeated and
|g(s_{i})|=|s_{i}|
for all
0\leq i<N
.
We let
\mathsf{PP}\mathsf{\Pi}
denote the class of all
prefix partial permutations.

We write each
g\in\mathsf{PP}\mathsf{\Pi}
as a list
g=[g(s_{0}),\ldots,g(s_{N-1})]
. The
length
of
g
is
N
, the number of function values assigned, and is denoted
|g|
. We use
[\hskip 1.42262pt]
to denote the
empty list
, the list of length 0. We write
f\negthinspace\upharpoonright\negthinspace N
for the length
N
prefix partial permutation of
f\in\mathsf{\Pi}
.

Definition 3.3
.

For each
g=[g(s_{0}),\ldots,g(s_{N-1})]\in\mathsf{PP}\mathsf{\Pi}
, the
cylinder
of all permutations in
\mathsf{\Pi}
that extend
g
is

\llbracket g\rrbracket=\{h\in\mathsf{\Pi}\mid h(s_{0})=g(s_{0}),\ldots,h(s_{N-1})=g(s_{N-1})\}.

For measure in
{\mathsf{\Pi}}
, we are taking the uniform distribution on
the set of all
\mathsf{\Pi}_{n}
of length-preserving permutations for all
n
. Our basic open sets are
\{\llbracket g\rrbracket\mid g\in\mathsf{PP}\mathsf{\Pi}\}
.
Suppose
g\in\mathsf{PP}\mathsf{\Pi}
has
|g|=2^{n}-1
for some
n\geq 0
. Then, following Bennett and Gill
[
5
]
, the measure

\mu(\llbracket g\rrbracket)=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{(2^{k})!}

is easy to define because the distribution is uniform over the
(2^{k})!
permutations at each length.
If
2^{n}-1\leq|g|<2^{n+1}-1
, let
m=|g|-2^{n}+1
and then

\mu(\llbracket g\rrbracket)=\left(\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{(2^{k})!}\right)\frac{(2^{n}-m)!}{(2^{n})!}=\left(\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{(2^{k})!}\right)\frac{1}{P(2^{n},m)},

where
P(n,k)=\frac{n!}{(n-k)!}
denotes the number of
k
-permutations on
n
elements. For convenience, we commonly write
\mu(g)=\mu(\llbracket g\rrbracket).

Let

{\cal F}_{\mathsf{\Pi}}=\sigma(\mathsf{PP}\mathsf{\Pi})
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the collection of all
\llbracket g\rrbracket
where
g\in\mathsf{PP}\mathsf{\Pi}.

By Carathéodory’s extension theorem,
\mu
extends
uniquely to
{\cal F}_{\mathsf{\Pi}}
, yielding the probability space

(\mathsf{\Pi},{\cal F}_{\mathsf{\Pi}},\mu).

We will work in this probability space.
Because
\mu
is outer regular, we have the typical open cover characterization of measure zero:

Theorem 3.4
.

A class
X\subseteq\mathsf{\Pi}
has measure 0 if and only if for every
\epsilon>0
, there is an open covering
G=\{g_{0},g_{1},\ldots,\}\subseteq\mathsf{PP}\mathsf{\Pi}
such that

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}\mu(g_{i})<\epsilon\quad\text{and}\quad X\subseteq\bigcup\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket.

3.2
Permutation Martingales

In resource-bounded measure in Cantor Space, a
martingale
is a function
d:\Sigma^{*}\to[0,\infty)
such that for all
w\in\Sigma^{*}
, we have the
following averaging condition:

d(w)=\frac{d(w0)+d(w1)}{2}.

A martingale in Cantor space may be viewed as betting on the membership of strings in a language. The standard enumeration of
\{0,1\}^{*}
is
s_{0}=\lambda,s_{1}=0,s_{2}=1,s_{3}=00,s_{4}=01,\ldots
. In the
{i^{\mathrm{th}}}
stage of the game, the martingale has seen the membership of the first
i
strings and bets on the membership of
s_{i}
in the language. The martingale’s value is updated based on the outcome of the bet. For further background on resource-bounded measure,
we refer to
[
20
,
21
,
1
,
7
,
11
]
.

A permutation martingale operates similarly, but instead of betting on the membership of a string in a language it bets on the next function value of the permutation. Instead of seeing the characteristic string of a language, a permutation martingale sees a
prefix partial permutation
, which is a list of permutation function values
g=[g(s_{0}),\ldots,g(s_{i-1})]
satisfying
|g(s_{i})|=|s_{i}|
for all
i
.
The permutation martingale will bet on the next function value
g(s_{i})
. The current
betting length
is the length of the next string
s_{|g|}
in the standard enumeration:

l(g)=|s_{|g|}|.

The set of
free strings
available for the next function value is

\mathsf{free}(g)=\{x\in\{0,1\}^{l(g)}\mid x\text{ is not in }g\}.

For example,

\mathsf{free}([\lambda])=\{0,1\}
,
\mathsf{free}([\lambda,1,0,11])=\{00,01,10\}
,
and
\mathsf{free}([\lambda,1,0,11,00,01])=\{10\}
.

We now introduce our main conceptual contribution, permutation martingales.

Definition 3.5
.

A
permutation martingale
is a function

d:\mathsf{PP}\mathsf{\Pi}\to[0,\infty)

such that for every prefix partial permutation
g\in\mathsf{PP}\mathsf{\Pi}
,

d(g)=\frac{1}{|\mathsf{free}(g)|}\sum_{x\in\mathsf{free}(g)}d(g,x),

where
(g,x)
is the result of appending
x
to
g
.

Success is defined for permutation martingales analogously to success for classical martingales.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
d
be a permutation martingale.
We say
d

succeeds on

f\in\mathsf{\Pi}
if

\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}d(f\negthinspace\upharpoonright\negthinspace N)=\infty.

The
success set
of
d
is

S^{\infty}[d]=\{f\in\mathsf{\Pi}\mid d\textrm{ succeeds on }f\}

and the
unitary success set
of
d
is the set

S^{1}[d]=\{f\in\mathsf{\Pi}\mid(\exists n)\ d(f\negthinspace\upharpoonright\negthinspace n)\geq 1\}.

In the remainder of this section, we establish the analogue of Ville’s theorem
[
31
]
for measure in
\mathsf{\Pi}
and permutation martingales.

The following statements are equivalent for every

3.3
A Permutation Martingale Example

We construct a permutation martingale
d
that succeeds on any length-preserving permutation whose restriction to length
n
is a cycle permutation for all but finitely many
n
.
We partition the initial capital into infinitely many shares
a_{i}=1/i^{2}
.
For each
i
, the share
a_{i}
is used to bet on the event that, for all
n\geq i
, the length-
n
restriction of the permutation is a cycle permutation.

The betting strategy is simple: when moving from length
n-1
to
n
, the martingale wagers all relevant capital on the image of the
n
-bit string
1^{n-1}0
. In the final step of forming a cycle of length
2^{n}
, there are exactly two choices for the image of
1^{n-1}0
.
One choice yields a cycle of length
2^{n}
; the other does not.
Since it is a binary choice, the martingale places its entire stake
a_{i}
(for all
i\leq n
) on the cycle outcome, thereby doubling its capital whenever the cycle is formed.

Hence, on any permutation whose restriction to length
n
is a cycle permutation for all but finitely many
n
, infinitely many of these bets succeed.
Consequently, each of those corresponding shares
a_{i}
grows without bound, and so the overall martingale
d
succeeds on all such permutations.

3.4
Permutation Martingales as Random Variables

Hitchcock and Lutz
[
13
]
showed how the martingales used in computational complexity are a special case of martingales used in probability theory. We explain how this extends to permutation martingales. Given a martingale
d:\{0,1\}^{*}\to[0,\infty)
, Hitchcock and Lutz define the random variable
\xi_{d,n}:\mathsf{C}\to[0,\infty)
by

\xi_{d,n}(S)=d(S\negthinspace\upharpoonright\negthinspace n)

for each
n\geq 0
.
Let
{\cal M}_{n}=\sigma(\{\mathsf{C}_{w}\mid w\in\{0,1\}^{n}\})
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the cylinders of length
n
.
Then the sequence
(\xi_{d,n}\mid n\geq 0)
is a martingale in the probability theory sense with respect to the filtration
({\cal M}_{n}\mid n\geq 0)
: for all
n\geq 0
,

E[\xi_{d,n+1}\mid{\cal M}_{n}]=\xi_{d,n}.

Similarly, given a permutation martingale
d:\mathsf{PP}\mathsf{\Pi}\to[0,\infty)
, for each
N
we can define the random variable

X_{d,N}:\mathsf{\Pi}\to[0,\infty)
by

X_{d,N}(f)=d(f\negthinspace\upharpoonright\negthinspace N)
for each
N\geq 0
.
Let

{\cal G}_{N}=\sigma(\{\llbracket g\rrbracket\mid g\in\mathsf{PP}\mathsf{\Pi}\textrm{ and }|g|=N\})

be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the cylinders in
\mathsf{PP}\mathsf{\Pi}
of length
N
.
Then
(X_{d,N}\mid N\geq 0)
is a martingale in the probability theory sense with respect to the filtration
({\cal G}_{N}\mid N\geq 0)
: for all
N\geq 0
,

E[X_{d,N+1}\mid{\cal G}_{N}]=X_{d,N}.

3.6
Permutation Betting Games

Originated in the field of algorithmic information theory, betting games are a generalization of martingales
[
25
,
24
]
, which were introduced to computational complexity by Buhrman et al.
[
7
]
. Similar to martingales, betting games can be thought of as strategies for betting on a binary sequence, except that with betting games we have the additional capability of selecting which position in a sequence to bet on next. In other words, a betting
game is permitted to select strings in a nonmonotone order, with the important restriction that it may not bet on
the same string more than once (see Buhrman et al.
[
7
]
for more details).

A permutation betting game is a
generalization of a permutation martingale, implemented by an oracle Turing machine, where it is allowed to select strings
in nonmonotone order. Prefixes of permutation betting games can be represented as
ordered partial permutations
defined below.

Definition 3.15
.

An
ordered partial permutation
is a list

g=[(x_{1},y_{1}),\ldots,(x_{n},y_{n})]

of pairs of strings for some
n\geq 0
where for all
1\leq i<j\leq n
,
x_{i}\neq x_{j}
and
y_{i}\neq y_{j}
, and
|x_{i}|=|y_{i}|
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.
We let
\mathsf{OP}\mathsf{\Pi}
denote the class of all
ordered partial permutations.

For a permutation betting game, the averaging
condition takes into consideration the length of the next string to be queried as follows.
Let
w\in\mathsf{OP}\mathsf{\Pi}
be the list of queried strings
paired with their images, and
a\in\{0,1\}^{n}
be the next string the betting game will query. Define free
(w,n)
to be the set of length-
n
strings that are available for the next function value, i.e., length-
n
strings that are not the function value of any of the queried strings.
Then the following averaging condition over free strings of length
n
must hold for the permutation betting game
d:\mathsf{OP}\mathsf{\Pi}\to[0,\infty)

d(w)=\sum_{b\in\mathsf{free}(w,n)}\frac{d(w[a,b])}{|\mathsf{free}(w,n)|}

where
w[a,b]
is the list
w
appended with the pair
(a,b)
.

Definition 3.16
.

A betting game is a
t(n)
-time betting game
if for all
n
, all strings of length
n
have been queried by time
t(2^{n})
.

We define betting game measure 0 and betting game randomness analogously.

Definition 3.17
.

Let
\Delta\in\{{\mathsf{p}},{{\mathsf{p}}_{\thinspace\negthinspace{}_{2}}},{\mathsf{pspace}},{\mathsf{p}_{\thinspace\negthinspace{}_{2}}\mathsf{space}}\}
.

1.

A class
X\subseteq\mathsf{\Pi}
has
\Delta
-betting-game measure 0
if there is a
\Delta
-computable permutation betting game
d
with

.

A permutation
\pi\in\mathsf{\Pi}
is
\Delta

3.7

Lutz’s Measure Conservation Theorem implies that resource-bounded measure gives nontrivial notions of measure within exponential-time complexity classes:
\mu_{\mathsf{p}}(\mathsf{E})\neq 0
and
\mu_{{\mathsf{p}}_{\thinspace\negthinspace{}_{2}}}(\mathsf{EXP})\neq 0

Let

For any

Let

y

D
outputs the string assigned to
x

Clearly,

D

cannot succeed
on this permutation. The runtime of

4
Elementary Properties of Random Permutations

In this section, we explore fundamental properties of random permutations that provide insights into how permutation martingales and betting games operate. Understanding these properties is crucial for applying permutation randomness in computational complexity.
We show that random permutations are computationally difficult to compute and to invert. We then investigate the relationship between random permutations and random oracles, showing how random permutations can generate random oracles.

4.1
Intractability of Random Permutations

Definition 4.1

The proof uses a simple averaging argument: we partition the set of length-

Given a permutation

Random Permutations for
\mathsf{NP}

\cap

\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}

Bennett and Gill
[
5
]
studied the power of random oracles in separating complexity classes. In particular, they showed that
\mathsf{P}^{A}\neq\mathsf{NP}^{A}
relative to a random oracle with probability 1. However, they were not able to separate
\mathsf{P}
from
\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}
relative to a random oracle. They also made the observation that if
\mathsf{P}^{A}=\mathsf{NP}^{A}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{A}
for a random oracle
A
, then
\mathsf{P}^{A}
must include seemingly computationally hard problems such as factorization. They also proved that any non-oracle-dependent language that belongs to
\mathsf{P}^{A}
with probability 1, also belongs to
\mathsf{BPP}
. As a result, if
\mathsf{P}^{A}=\mathsf{NP}^{A}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{A}
for a random oracle
A
with probability 1, then these difficult problems in
\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}
would be solvable in
\mathsf{BPP}
. To achieve a separation between
\mathsf{P}^{A}
and
\mathsf{NP}^{A}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{A}

\{0,1\}^{*}
and showed that

\mathsf{P}^{\pi}\neq\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}
for every random permutation
\pi
.

Using resource-bounded permutation betting games on the set of all
length preserving permutations of
\{0,1\}^{\star}
, we strengthen the Bennett-Gill permutation separation, proving that

\mathsf{P}^{\pi}\neq\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}
for any
{\mathsf{p}}
-betting-game random
permutations
\pi
. More generally, we show that the set of permutations

\pi
such that,
\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}
is not
\mathsf{DTIME}^{\pi}(2^{kn})
-bi-immune has

{\mathsf{p}}
-permutation-betting-game measure 0. Recall that a language

is bi-immune to a complexity class

if no infinite subset of
L
or its complement is decidable in
C

[
9
,
3
]
.

The following is our main theorem where its first part states that relative to a
{\mathsf{p}}
-betting-game random permutation
\pi
, there is a language
L
in
\mathsf{NLIN}^{\pi}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NLIN}^{\pi}
such that no infinite subset of
L
or its complement is
\mathsf{DTIME}^{\pi}(2^{kn})
-decidable.

Theorem 5.1
.

1.

If
\pi
is a
{\mathsf{p}}

.

2.

If
\pi

Our headline result is a corollary of

.

Corollary 5.2
.

If
\pi
is a
{\mathsf{p}}
-betting-game random permutation, then
\mathsf{P}^{\pi}\neq\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}

To prove

\displaystyle\mathsf{HRNG}_{k}^{\pi}

\displaystyle=\{x\ |\ \exists\ y\in\{0,1\}^{k|x|-1},\ \pi(0y)=x^{k}\}

\displaystyle=\{x\ |\ \forall\ y\in\{0,1\}^{k|x|-1},\ \pi(1y)\neq x^{k}\},

\displaystyle\mathsf{POLYHRNG}_{k}^{\pi}

\displaystyle=\{x\ |\ \exists\ y\in\{0,1\}^{|x|^{k}-1},\ \pi(0y)=x^{|x|^{k-1}}\}

\displaystyle=\{x\ |\ \forall\ y\in\{0,1\}^{|x|^{k}-1},\ \pi(1y)\neq x^{|x|^{k-1}}\}.

A string

belongs to

if the preimage of

(
k

) in
\{0,1\}^{kn}

does not belong to
\mathsf{HRNG}_{k}^{\pi}
, then the preimage of

begins with 1. In either case, the preimage serves as a witness for

. The language
\mathsf{POLYHRNG}_{k}^{\pi}
is similar, but we are looking for a preimage in

of

(

It follows that

\mathsf{HRNG}_{k}^{\pi}\in\mathsf{NLIN}^{\pi}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NLIN}^{\pi}

and

\mathsf{HRNG}_{k}^{\pi}\in\mathsf{NTIME}^{\pi}(n^{k})\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NTIME}^{\pi}(n^{k})

for all

.

The following lemma implies
Theorem

5.1
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
k\geq 0
.

1.

The set

X=\{\pi\in\mathsf{\Pi}\mid\mathsf{HRNG}_{k+3}^{\pi}\mathrm{\ is\ not\ }\mathsf{DTIME}(2^{kn})^{\pi}\mathrm{-immune}\}

has
O(n)
-honest
{\mathsf{p}}
-permutation-betting-game measure 0.

2.

The set

X=\{\pi\in\mathsf{\Pi}\mid\mathsf{POLYHRNG}_{k+1}^{\pi}\mathrm{\ is\ not\ }\mathsf{DTIME}(2^{n^{k}})^{\pi}\mathrm{-immune}\}

has
O(n^{k})
-honest
{{\mathsf{p}}_{\thinspace\negthinspace{}_{2}}}
-permutation-betting-game measure 0.

Proof.

We design a betting game that can succeed on two classes of permutations.
The argument breaks down into two main cases.

The betting game succeeds on permutations
\pi
that do
not
map
strings of the form
1y
to
x^{k+3}
.
These permutations are relatively straightforward, because one can easily
compute the probability that, for all
y
,
\pi(1y)\neq 0^{(k+3)n}
, i.e.
0^{n}\not\in\mathsf{HRNG}_{k+3}^{\pi}
.
This calculation merely requires examining the status of all length-
(k+3)n

strings that have been queried.
In particular, the order in which the betting game queries strings

does not
affect its ability to wager on this event, making the
betting strategy simpler in this case.

We then consider the complementary class of permutations that
do
map
some string
1y
to a string of the form
x^{k+3}
. Here,
y
is a

potential witness string
. Unlike the previous case, we exploit
the betting game’s ability to query strings
out of order
.
In particular, we know there exists some permutation TM capable of predicting
events of the form
\pi(1y)=x^{k+3}
. Accordingly, our betting game attempts

all
TMs by simulating them on all length-
n
strings.
It is precisely this universal simulation that triggers queries
on (potential) witness strings in arbitrary orders.
Whenever the simulation would query a witness string, the betting game
places a bet on that string
first
.

We show that if, for infinitely many
n
, a witness string is indeed
queried during the simulation, then the betting game wins unbounded capital.
On the other hand, if none of the potential witness strings are queried,
then there is a TM that makes infinitely many
correct
predictions
of the form
\pi(0y)=x^{k+3}
, thereby allowing another betting strategy
to succeed.

We now formalize the above outline and construct the required betting game
in detail.
Frist, partition
X
into the two sets

X_{1}\;=\;\{\>\pi\in X\mid\mathsf{HRNG}_{k+3}^{\pi}\text{ is finite}\}\quad\text{and}\quad X_{2}\;=\;\{\>\pi\in X\mid\mathsf{HRNG}_{k+3}^{\pi}\text{ is infinite}\}.

We now design an honest
{\mathsf{p}}
-permutation-betting game,
G
,
that succeeds on all permutations in
X_{1}
as well as in
X_{2}
.
To do this, we split
G
’s initial capital into infinitely many sub-shares
according to

a_{i}=b_{i}=c_{i}=\frac{1}{i^{2}},i=1,2,\dots.

The sub-shares
\{a_{i}\}
are used to bet on permutations in
X_{1}
,
while the sub-shares
\{b_{i},c_{i}\}
are reserved for betting on those
in
X_{2}
.

Succeeding on
X_{1}
.

For a randomly selected permutation
\pi
, consider the event
\mathcal{A}_{n}
, that
0^{n}\not\in\mathsf{HRNG}_{k+3}^{\pi}
i.e.,
\pi(1y)=0^{n(k+3)}
, for some string
y
. The probability of
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is 1/2 and it only depends on images of length-
(k+3)n
strings. To keep track of which length-
(k+3)n
strings have been queried, we let

\omega\in(\,\{\{0,1\}^{(k+3)n}\}\;\cup\;\{\star\})^{2^{(k+3)n}}

encode the current
status
of each strings. Specifically, if the
i
th length-
(k+3)n
string has already
been queried and mapped to some string
z
, then we set

\omega[i]=z\in\{0,1\}^{(k+3)n}
; otherwise, if it has not
been queried yet, we set
\omega[i]=\star
.

Recall that
G
is our overall betting game, which updates its
capital shares
a_{i}
through a “subgame”
G_{a_{i}}
whenever
i\geq n
.
We define

G_{a_{i}}(\omega)\;=\;\frac{a_{i}}{\mathsf{Pr}(\mathcal{A}_{n})}\;\mathsf{Pr}\bigl(\mathcal{A}_{n}\,\big|\,\omega\bigr).

Let
\omega^{\,i\to b}
denote the string
\omega
with its
i
th component
replaced by
b\in\{\{0,1\}^{(k+3)n}\}\cup\{\star\}
. We define

\mathsf{free}(\omega)
to be the set of all length-
(k+3)n
strings available as an image. It is routine to verify that:

G_{a_{i}}(\omega^{\,i\to\star})\;=\;\frac{1}{\bigl|\mathsf{free}(\omega^{\,i\to\star})\bigr|}\;\sum_{\,b\,\in\,\mathsf{free}(\omega^{\,i\to\star})}\;G_{a_{i}}\bigl(\omega^{\,i\to b}\bigr).

Hence, the summation of all
G_{a_{i}}
forms a betting game.

Since
\mathsf{Pr}(\mathcal{A}_{n})=\tfrac{1}{2}
, it is easy to verify that whenever

\omega
represents a configuration in which
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is true, we have

G_{a_{i}}(\omega)\;=\;2\,a_{i}.

Thus every time
\mathcal{A}_{n}
occurs, the subgame
G_{a_{i}}
doubles its capital
from
a_{i}
to
2\,a_{i}
. For every permutation
\pi\in X_{1}
,
\mathcal{A}_{n}

holds for all but finitely many
n
, so infinitely many
a_{i}
’s grow
unboundedly. Therefore,
G
succeeds on all
\pi\in X_{1}
.

The “subgame”

can be computed in
O\bigl(2^{(k+3)n}\bigr)
time
by examining the mapping of every string of length
(k+3)n
. Moreover,
the order in which
G
queries length-
2n
strings has no impact on its
success for
\pi\in X_{1}
. As we shall see next, this changes when dealing
with permutations in
X_{2}
.

Succeeding on
X_{2}
.

Given
\pi\in X_{2}
, we know
\mathsf{HRNG}_{k+3}^{\pi}
is not

\mathsf{DTIME}(2^{kn})^{\pi}
-immune. Thus there is some
\mathsf{DTIME}(2^{kn})^{\pi}

oracle TM that recognizes an infinite subset of
\mathsf{HRNG}_{k+3}^{\pi}
.
A string
y\in\{0,1\}^{k(n+1)-1}
is a
witness
for the membership of
a length-
n
string

in
\mathsf{HRNG}_{k+3}^{\pi}
if

\pi(0y)=x^{(k+1)}
. We simulate the first
n
such
\mathsf{DTIME}(2^{kn})^{\pi}

machines on all length-
n
strings. Two cases arise:

(1) We query witnesses of some

for
infinitely many
n
during the simulation phase. Let

Q_{n}\subseteq\{0,1\}^{(k+3)n-1}
be the set of strings that might
witness membership of any length-
n
string
x
queried in the simulation.
We use a portion of

to bet on the event that

is indeed
a witness to

. For large

,

|Q_{n}|<n^{2}\,2^{(k+1)n}
because each of the

n

n

We split

evenly among the

2^{(k+3)n}-n^{2}\,2^{(k+2)n}
(because the TMs running so far could have

(\frac{b_{n}}{n\,2^{(k+2)n}})(2^{(k+3)n}-n^{2}\,2^{(k+2)n})=\omega\bigl(2^{0.9n}\bigr),

and since one of these events arises infinitely often, the betting game

(2) Witness strings are queried only finitely many times. Eventually, none
of the TMs ever queries a witness string. At that stage, after simulating
the TMs on length-
n
strings, the betting game employs

to wager on
any
\mathsf{DTIME}(2^{kn})^{\pi}

that has not made a mistake and accepts

. Let

be the event

, i.e. there exists

\pi(0y)=x^{(k+1)}
. For a random permutation,
\mathsf{Pr}(B_{x})=\tfrac{1}{2}
.
After simulating all TMs on length-
n
strings
without
querying any

, the conditional probability of

remains at least

\frac{2^{(k+3)n}-n^{2}\,2^{(k+2)n}}{2^{(k+3)n}}\approx\tfrac{1}{2}.

Hence the betting game approximately doubles

each time

is
accepted. Because we are guaranteed that some

correctly decides an
infinite subset of
\mathsf{HRNG}_{k+3}^{\pi}
, this betting also
succeeds.

Clearly, this construction is implementable in
\mathsf{DTIME}(2^{O(n)})
.
Notice moreover that while querying strings of length

, the betting
game places wagers only on strings of length

, making it an

(k+3)n
-honest
{\mathsf{p}}
-permutation betting game.

Sketch for part (2).

The second statement uses
\mathsf{POLYHRNG}_{k+1}^{\pi}

{{\mathsf{p}}_{\thinspace\negthinspace{}_{2}}}
-permutation-betting-game measure 0.

∎

\mathsf{NP}

\cap

\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}
versus Quantum Computation

Bennett, Bernstein, Brassard, and Vazirani
[
4
]
showed
that
\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}\not\subseteq\mathsf{BQTIME}^{\pi}(o(2^{n/3}))
relative to a random permutation
\pi
with probability 1. In this section we investigate how much of their result holds relative to individual random oracles at the space-bounded level.

We begin with a general lemma about test languages and
\mathsf{QTM}
s. We write
\mathsf{PP}\mathsf{\Pi}_{\leq n}=\{g\in\mathsf{PP}\mathsf{\Pi}\mid|g|\leq 2^{n+1}-1\}
for all prefix partial permutations defined on strings in
\{0,1\}^{\leq n}
. For a string
s_{i}
in the standard enumeration, we write
g\negthinspace\upharpoonright\negthinspace s_{i}
for the length
i
prefix of
g
. In other words,
g\negthinspace\upharpoonright\negthinspace s_{i}=[g(s_{0}),\ldots,g(s_{i-1})]
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\pi
be a permutation with an associated test language
L_{\pi}
and let
p(n)
be a linear function (polynomial function, respectively). If for some oracle
\mathsf{QTM}

M
the following conditions hold, then
\pi
is not a
{\mathsf{pspace}}
-random (
{\mathsf{p}_{\thinspace\negthinspace{}_{2}}\mathsf{space}}
-random, respectively) permutation.

1.

The membership of
0^{n}
in
L_{\pi}
depends on the membership of the strings
of length at most
p(n)
.

2.

M^{\pi}
decides
L_{\pi}
with error probability
\delta
, for some constant
0<\delta<1
, and queries only strings of length at most
p(n)
.

3.

For any partial prefix permutation
\rho\in\mathsf{PP}\mathsf{\Pi}_{\leq p(n)}
, the conditional
probability

\underset{|\psi|=p(n)}{\mathsf{Pr}}\left[M^{\psi}(0^{n})=L_{\psi}(0^{n})\bigm|\rho\sqsubseteq\psi\right]

is computable in

O(2^{O(n)})

space
(
O(2^{n^{O(1)}})
space, respectively).

4.

For some constant
1>\epsilon>\delta
and for all but finitely many
n
,

\underset{|\psi|=l(n)}{\mathsf{Pr}}\left[M^{\psi}(0^{n})=L_{\psi}(0^{n})\bigm|\pi\negthinspace\upharpoonright\negthinspace 0^{n}\sqsubseteq\psi\right]<1-\epsilon.

Proof.

Let
n_{0}<n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots
be a sequence of numbers such that
p(n_{i})<n_{i+1}
for all
i\geq 0
. Based on the statement of the lemma,
M^{\psi}(0^{n_{j-1}})
cannot query any string of length
n_{j}
and the membership of
0^{n_{j-1}}
does not depend on the membership of any string of length greater than
n_{j}
. Let
n_{k}
be the first number in the sequence above such that for any
n>n_{k}
we have
\underset{|\psi|=l(n)}{\mathsf{Pr}}\left[M^{\psi}(0^{n})=L_{\psi}(0^{n})\mid\pi\negthinspace\upharpoonright\negthinspace 0^{n}\sqsubseteq\psi\right]<1-\epsilon
and define the following martingale:

d(\rho)=\begin{cases}1&\textrm{if }|\rho|\leq 2^{n_{k}+1}-1\\
\frac{d(\rho\;\negthinspace\upharpoonright\negthinspace\;0^{n_{j-1}})}{\mathrm{Pr}(\psi\;|\;\rho\;\negthinspace\upharpoonright\negthinspace\;0^{n_{j-1}})}\mathrm{Pr}(\psi\;|\;\rho)&\textrm{if }2^{n_{j-1}}<|\rho|\leq 2^{n_{j}+1}-1\textrm{, for some }j>k\end{cases}

where
\mathrm{Pr}(\psi|\nu)
is the probability that
M^{\psi}(0^{n})=L_{\psi}(0^{n})
given that
\nu\sqsubseteq\psi
. The third condition in the statement of the lemma implies that this martingale is
{\mathsf{pspace}}
computable (
{\mathsf{p}_{\thinspace\negthinspace{}_{2}}\mathsf{space}}
computable, respectively). Now consider a permutation
\pi
that satisfies the conditions of the lemma. Then for
j>k
we have:

d(\pi\negthinspace\upharpoonright\negthinspace 0^{n_{j}})=\frac{d(\pi\negthinspace\upharpoonright\negthinspace 0^{n_{j-1}})}{\mathrm{Pr}(\psi\;|\;\pi\;\negthinspace\upharpoonright\negthinspace\;0^{n_{j-1}})}\mathrm{Pr}(\psi\;|\;\pi\negthinspace\upharpoonright\negthinspace 0^{n_{j}})\geq\frac{d(\pi\negthinspace\upharpoonright\negthinspace 0^{n_{j-1}})}{1-\epsilon}(1-\delta)

The last inequality holds because it follows from the first two conditions of the lemma that
\mathrm{Pr}(\psi\;|\;\pi\negthinspace\upharpoonright\negthinspace 0^{n_{j}})\geq 1-\delta
. By repeating this process, we can see that
d
succeeds on
\pi
.

∎

In the following theorem, we use
Section

6
to extend the result by Bennett, Bernstein, Brassard, and Vazirani
[
4
]
to
{\mathsf{p}_{\thinspace\negthinspace{}_{2}}\mathsf{space}}
-random permutations.

Theorem 6.2
.

If
\pi
is a
{\mathsf{p}_{\thinspace\negthinspace{}_{2}}\mathsf{space}}
-random permutation,
then
\mathsf{NLIN}^{\pi}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NLIN}^{\pi}

Proof.

Let

From Bennett et al.

We can compute

[

∎

In the following theorem, we have three hypotheses where a complexity class

Theorem 7.4
.

Suppose that
\mathsf{P}^{R}=\mathsf{NP}^{R}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{R}
for a random oracle
R
with probability 1. Then all of the following hold:

1.

\mathsf{NP}\neq\mathsf{EXP}\Rightarrow\mu_{\mathsf{p}}(\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP})=0.

2.

\mathsf{UP}\neq\mathsf{EXP}\Rightarrow\mu_{\mathsf{p}}(\mathsf{UP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{UP})=0.

3.

\mathsf{FewP}\neq\mathsf{EXP}\Rightarrow\mu_{\mathsf{p}}(\mathsf{UP})=0.

Proof.

1.

Suppose
\mu_{\mathsf{p}}(\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP})\neq 0
and
\mathsf{NP}\neq\mathsf{EXP}
. From
Section

7
we have
\mathsf{BPP}\subseteq\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}=\mathsf{AM}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{AM}
. Therefore
\mathsf{BPP}\subseteq\mathsf{NP}\neq\mathsf{EXP}
, so
\mu_{\mathsf{p}}(\mathsf{BPP})=0
by the zero-one law for
\mathsf{BPP}

[
30
]
. Since
\mathsf{AM}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{AM}
and
\mathsf{BPP}
have different
{\mathsf{p}}
-measures, the classes are not equal. The result follows from
Theorem

7.1
.

2.

Suppose
\mu_{\mathsf{p}}(\mathsf{UP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{UP})\neq 0
and
\mathsf{UP}\neq\mathsf{EXP}
. From
Section

7
we have
\mathsf{BPP}\subseteq\mathsf{UP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{UP}
.
Therefore
\mathsf{BPP}\subseteq\mathsf{UP}\neq\mathsf{EXP}
, so
\mu_{\mathsf{p}}(\mathsf{BPP})=0
by the zero-one law. Therefore
\mathsf{UP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{UP}\not\subseteq\mathsf{BPP}
, so
\mathsf{UP}\not\subseteq\mathsf{RP}
. The result follows from
Theorem

7.2
.

3.

Suppose
\mu_{\mathsf{p}}(\mathsf{UP})\neq 0

\mathsf{FewP}\neq\mathsf{EXP}

7
implies
\mathsf{BPP}\subseteq\mathsf{FewP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{FewP}

\mathsf{BPP}\subseteq\mathsf{FewP}\neq\mathsf{EXP}

\mu_{\mathsf{p}}(\mathsf{BPP})=0
by the zero-one law. Therefore
\mathsf{UP}\not\subseteq\mathsf{BPP}
, so
\mathsf{UP}\not\subseteq\mathsf{RP}

Theorem

7.2
.

Corollary 7.5
.

Suppose that
\mathsf{P}^{R}=\mathsf{NP}^{R}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{R}
for a random oracle
R
with probability 1. Then all of the following hold:

1.

\mu(\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}\mid\mathsf{EXP})\in\{0,1\}.

2.

\mu(\mathsf{UP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{UP}\mid\mathsf{EXP})\in\{0,1\}.

3.

\mu(\mathsf{UP}\mid\mathsf{EXP})=0

or
\mu(\mathsf{FewP}\mid\mathsf{EXP})=1.

Proof.

The two key facts we need in this proof are that for any class
X\subseteq\mathsf{EXP}
:

(a)

If
X
is closed under finite union and intersection, then
X=\mathsf{EXP}
if and only if
\mu(X\mid\mathsf{EXP})=1

[

,
21
]
.

(b)

If
\mu_{\mathsf{p}}(X)=0
, then
\mu(X\mid\mathsf{EXP})=0

[
20
]
.

We write each implication in
Theorem

7.4
as a disjunction and apply the above facts:

1.

We have
\mathsf{NP}=\mathsf{EXP}\Leftrightarrow\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}=\mathsf{EXP}\Leftrightarrow\mu(\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}\mid\mathsf{EXP})=1

\mu_{\mathsf{p}}(\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP})=0\Rightarrow\mu(\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}\mid\mathsf{EXP})=0

2.

We have
\mathsf{UP}=\mathsf{EXP}\Leftrightarrow\mathsf{UP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{UP}=\mathsf{EXP}\Leftrightarrow\mu(\mathsf{UP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{UP}\mid\mathsf{EXP})=1
or
\mu_{\mathsf{p}}(\mathsf{UP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{UP})=0\Rightarrow\mu(\mathsf{UP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{UP}\mid\mathsf{EXP})=0.

3.

We have
\mathsf{FewP}=\mathsf{EXP}\Leftrightarrow\mu(\mathsf{FewP}\mid\mathsf{EXP})=1

\mu_{\mathsf{p}}(\mathsf{UP})=0\Rightarrow\mu(\mathsf{UP}\mid\mathsf{EXP})=0
.

In the third case of

8
Conclusion

We have introduced resource-bounded random permutations and shown that

\mathsf{P}^{\pi}\neq\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}
for all
{\mathsf{p}}
-betting-game random permutations. We remark that all of the results in
Sections

5
and
6
about
\mathsf{NLIN}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NLIN}
and
\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}
hold for their unambiguous versions
\mathsf{ULIN}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{ULIN}
and
\mathsf{UP}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{UP}
, respectively.
An interesting open problem is whether our main theorem can be improved from betting-game random permutations to random permutations.

\mathsf{P}^{\pi}\neq\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}\cap\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}^{\pi}

{\mathsf{p}}
-random permutation
\pi

More generally, the relative power of permutation martingales versus betting games should be investigated.

Are polynomial-time permutation martingales and permutation betting games equivalent?

We proved two restricted versions of the Bennett et al.
[

]

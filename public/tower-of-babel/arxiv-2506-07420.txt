p p –adic congruences in iterated derivatives of ℘ \wp
Kiran Luecke, Eric Peterson

Abstract
We use homotopy theoretic methods to prove congruence relations of number theoretic interest. Specifically, we use the theory of 𝔼 ∞ \mathbb{E}_{\infty} complex orientations to establish p p –adic Kümmer congruences among iterated derivatives of the Weierstrass elliptic function. The machinery of [ AHR ] was developed by Ando, Hopkins, and Rezk with the intended application of taking congruence relations as input and producing 𝔼 ∞ \mathbb{E}_{\infty} –orientations as output. We run their machine in reverse, using as input the recent results of [ CL25 ] on the existence of 𝔼 ∞ \mathbb{E}_{\infty} –orientations of Tate fixed-point objects.

Definition 1.1

(

)
.

For
A
a torsion-free
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebra, an
A
–valued
p
–adic moment sequence
is a sequence
M_{n}\in A
such that for any rational polynomial
f(r)=\sum_{n}a_{n}r^{n}\in\mathbb{Q}[r]
with
f(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times})\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, the sum
\sum_{n}a_{n}M_{n}\in A\otimes\mathbb{Q}
is an
A
–integer.

Moment sequences arise out of
p
–adic integrals via the correspondence

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}dM\,\sum_{n=0}^{N}a_{n}r^{n}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n=0}^{N}a_{n}M_{n},

\displaystyle M_{n}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}dM\,r^{n}.

The integrality condition can be interpreted as a family of
Kümmer congruences
on the sequence elements.
For example, the polynomial
p^{-1}(r^{p}-r)
is
p
–adically integer-valued, hence
p^{-1}(M_{p}-M_{1})
must be an
A
–integer, or equivalently
M_{p}\equiv M_{1}\pmod{p}
.
Other choices of test polynomial yield other congruences.

We prove the following:

Main Theorem A
.

Pick any

\displaystyle M_{1}^{\mathrm{Wit}^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle=(1-c)\cdot\frac{\log(1-t)}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\cdot(G_{2}(q)-p\cdot G_{2}(q^{p}))

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1-c}{2\pi i}\cdot\left(\wp_{q}^{(-1)}\left(\frac{\log(1-t)}{-2\pi i}\right)-\wp_{q^{p}}^{(-1)}\left(\frac{p\log(1-t)}{-2\pi i}\right)\right),

\displaystyle M_{2}^{\mathrm{Wit}^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle=(1-c^{2})\cdot\frac{G_{2}(q)-p\cdot G_{2}(q^{p})}{-2(2\pi i)^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+(1-c^{2})\cdot\left(\wp_{q}\left(\frac{\log(1-t)}{-2\pi i}\right)-\wp_{q^{p}}\left(\frac{p\log(1-t)}{-2\pi i}\right)\right),

\displaystyle M_{n\geq 3}^{\mathrm{Wit}^{\sharp}}

2.1
Résumé on elliptic functions

It will be convenient to have formulas on hand for a variety of standard functions appearing in the theory of complex elliptic curves.
To fix terms, let us consider (twisted) functions on the compactified moduli of elliptic curves, referred to as
modular forms
.
Such functions are typically presented in three ways
[
Sil94
, Section I.1, Remark 3.3]
:

1.

A modular form
f
of weight
k
is a function on choices of lattice
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{C}
with the property
f(c\cdot\Lambda)=c^{-k}f(\Lambda)
and which extends to a meromorphic function at
\operatorname{span}\{+i\cdot\infty,1\}
.

2.

Selecting a basis
\{\tau,1\}
for the (possibly positively rescaled) lattice
\Lambda
,
f
can be understood as a function of
\tau\in\mathfrak{h}
satisfying
f(M\cdot\tau)=(c\tau+d)^{-k}f(\tau)
and extending to a meromorphic function at
\tau=+i\cdot\infty
.

3.

Using periodicity against
\mathrm{SL}_{2}\mathbb{Z}
and meromorphicity at
\tau=+i\cdot\infty
, we may write the Fourier series of
f
as a bounded-below sum
\sum_{n=-m}^{\infty}a_{n}q^{n}
, where
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
, called the
q
–expansion
.

2.2
Résumé on elliptic cohomology

In this subsection we recall some basic elements of complex orientation theory.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
R
be a homotopy ring spectrum.
The following two pieces of data are equivalent:

1.

A
complex orientation
of
R
is a homotopy ring map
\mathrm{MU}\to R
from the complex bordism spectrum
[
Ada74
, Lemma I.4.6]
.

2.

A
coordinate
is a class
\xi\in R^{2}\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
which restricts to
\Sigma^{2}1\in R^{2}S^{2}
along
S^{2}\simeq\mathbb{CP}^{1}\to\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}

[
Ada74
, I.2.1]
.

A coordinate induces an isomorphism
R_{*}\llbracket{\xi}\rrbracket\to R^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
.
The Künneth formula then gives an isomorphism
R^{*}(\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}\times\mathbb{CP}^{\infty})\simeq R_{*}\llbracket{\xi_{l},\xi_{r}}\rrbracket
where
\xi_{l}
and
\xi_{r}
are the pullbacks of
\xi
along the two projections.
Let
\mu\colon\thinspace\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}\times\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}\to\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
be the multiplication map classifying the tensor product of line bundles.
The
formal group law

F(\xi_{l},\xi_{r})
is defined to be the power series associated to the element
\mu^{*}\xi

[
Ada74
, Lemma I.2.7]
.
We often denote it by
\xi_{l}+_{F}\xi_{r}
.

Example 2.4
.

Note that
\mathrm{MU}
is a connective ring spectrum with
\pi_{0}\mathrm{MU}=\mathbb{Z}

\mathrm{MU}\to\mathbb{Z}
.
Thus, if
R
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra, there is a canonical orientation
\mathrm{MU}\to\mathbb{Z}\to R
.
We will refer to this as the

.

Example 2.5
.

Write
\pi_{*}\mathrm{KU}=\mathbb{Z}[\beta^{\pm}]

\pi_{*}\mathrm{KU}_{\mathrm{Tate}}=\mathbb{Z}[\beta^{\pm}]\llbracket{q}\rrbracket

•

2.3
Résumé on
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
–rings

Whereas in classical algebra commutativity is a
property
of a ring and maps of rings are automatically compatible with any commutativity present, a ring spectrum in homotopy theory requires

\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
–ring, and such a map an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
–ring map.
The complex bordism spectrum
\mathrm{MU}
is naturally an

–ring (
[

, Chapter III]

is an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
–ring one can ask when a complex orientation
\mathrm{MU}\to R
admits an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
–structure.
This question has received much interest and has seen recent progress (
[
AHR
]
,
[
HL18
]
,
[
HY20
]
,
[
Bal23
]
,
[
BSY24
]
, …), but it is usually quite difficult—the number of known
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
–orientations is small, the crown jewel of which is the
string orientation
of
\mathrm{tmf}
.

Our interest comes from a result of the first author:

Theorem 2.13

(
[
CL25
, Theorem 5.14]
)
.

The sharp construction sends
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
–orientations to
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
–orientations.
∎

Therefore the sharped string orientation (or any of the handful of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
–orientations it begets, cf.

2.20
) automatically admits this extra
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
–structure, and we would like to understand it in detail.
Ultimately, we will conclude that it entails the congruences advertised in the introduction.

Let us describe others’ pursuit of the program to express this
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
–structure in a manageable form for palatable target rings.
There is a distinguished class of spectra, called
K(1)
–local

A key insight of Ando, Hopkins, and Rezk is that if

is

\mathbb{E}_{\infty}

\omega\colon\thinspace\mathrm{MU}\to R
is well captured by its interaction with
\psi^{p}

Our rings of interest,
\mathrm{KU}^{t\mathbb{T}}
and
\mathrm{KU}_{\mathrm{Tate}}^{t\mathbb{T}}

Example 2.14

Upon

\displaystyle\psi^{p}(q)

Lemma 3.1

The series expansion of the right-hand side follows from:

Proof of

and hence that second summand can be written as

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{\exp_{\mathrm{Wit}}(x+y)}{\exp_{\mathrm{Wit}}(y)}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{G_{2}(q)}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\log(1-t)\right)\cdot x+\frac{-G_{2}(q)}{2(2\pi i)^{2}}\cdot x^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\cdot\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\cdot\wp_{q}^{(n-2)}\left(\frac{\log(1-t)}{-2\pi i}\right).

The moments are then calculated to be exactly the expressions appearing in the Theorem statement, so
Theorem

2.18
finishes the proof.
∎

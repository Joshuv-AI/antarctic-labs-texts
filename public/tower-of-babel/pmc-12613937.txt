Protocol for a two-arm parallel randomized controlled trial to evaluate patient and researcher co-presentation of research results: the Scleroderma Patient-centered Intervention Network—Patients Alongside Investigators in Research-Sharing (SPIN-PAIRS) trial
Authors: Elsa-Lynn Nassar, Amanda Wurz, Marie-Eve Carrier, Meira Golberg, Claire Adams, Linda Kwakkenbos, Sabrina Hoa, Andrea Benedetti, Brett D. Thombs
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundResearchers are mandated ethically to share study results with participants, and funding agencies emphasize dissemination to others with relevant lived experience. Many researchers, however, find it difficult to communicate study purposes, methods, results, and significance to patients. Our Scleroderma Patient-centered Intervention Network (SPIN) piloted “co-presentation,” which involves researchers and patient partners jointly presenting results to other patients. The SPIN Patients Alongside Investigators in Research-Sharing (SPIN-PAIRS) Trial will be conducted as part of a 90-min virtual research event for people with systemic sclerosis (SSc, scleroderma) and will compare patient and researcher partner co-presentation versus researcher-alone presentation. Primary outcomes will be event attendee ratings of presentations on (1) information completeness, (2) understandability, (3) relevance to patients, and (4) trust in findings. Secondary objectives are to (1) conduct subgroup analyses of primary outcomes by participant characteristics (gender, age, race or ethnicity, country, education level, health literacy) and (2) use qualitative interviews to better understand outcome ratings and inform co-presentation.MethodsThis will be a mixed-method study with (1) a two-arm parallel superiority randomized controlled trial embedded in a patient-oriented research event and (2) interviews with patient and researcher co-presenters, separately, and with trial participants. Pre-event, researchers will be selected to present via an open call for abstracts and a patient-led selection committee. To avoid presentation-related biases, pre-event, researchers will record researcher-alone presentations. They will then receive co-presentation training and develop co-presentations with patient partners, which will also be recorded. Eligible trial participants will be adults aged 18 or older who indicate they have been diagnosed with SSc by a physician. We will recruit participants via social media and email lists from our multinational SPIN Cohort and from patient organization partners to attend the event and rate presentations. Participants will be recruited to register for the event beginning in August 2025. Registered participants will be invited to confirm their registration and enroll in the trial on the day of the event. We will require at least 116 participants for ≥ 80% power but will not restrict the number of enrollees. We will randomly assign event attendees 1:1 to virtual rooms with (1) four pre-recorded patient-researcher co-presentations or (2) four pre-recorded researcher-alone presentations. The same studies will be presented in each arm with live questions and answers after each presentation in each virtual room. Attendees will rate each presentation immediately following the presentation. Participants will not be informed that they are part of a randomized trial, or that two conditions are being compared and will be blind to study comparisons and hypotheses. Presenters will not be blinded during the event. We will compare outcomes, all measured via 0–10 numerical rating scales, using linear mixed models with four observations per participant for each outcome (one observation for each presentation). Interviews will be conducted < 2 weeks post-event, and verbatim transcripts will be analyzed using an inductive-deductive thematic approach.DiscussionFindings will contribute to the evidence base on effective strategies for sharing results with study participants and others with relevant lived experience.Trial registrationISRCTN12805381 (https://www.isrctn.com/ISRCTN12805381)Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13063-025-09256-0.

Introduction

Sharing health research results with study participants is mandated ethically [1–3], and communicating results to others with relevant lived experience and members of the public is encouraged by research funding agencies [4–7]. Effective communication of research results can help build trust in research, encourage research participation, and support people to become informed partners in their health care [8]. Most researchers, however, do not share results with study participants or disseminate them to others with relevant lived experience [9–15].

Ethics guidelines and research funders emphasize or require patient engagement in research [1, 16–20]. Engaging patients in research, including dissemination, presents an opportunity to improve communication of results; however, when patients are engaged in research, they are usually not involved in disseminating findings, based on reviews of mental health, rheumatology, and cancer research [21–23]. Our Scleroderma Patient-centered Intervention Network (SPIN) [24] piloted “co-presentation” at the 2022 Scleroderma Canada National Patient Conference. Co-presentation is a strategy in which researchers and patients who partnered in research jointly present results to other patients [25]. Post-conference interviews with researchers and patient partners who co-presented identified potential benefits of co-presentation and provided suggestions for strengthening the approach [26]. Co-presentation requires training and multiple meetings between researchers and patient partners to establish expectations and roles, design the presentation format, refine content, and practice delivery. Whether co-presentation is more effective than researcher-alone presentation is not known.

In rare diseases, patient organizations often hold conferences where researchers present results and discuss implications with patient audiences [27]. Researcher presentations at these events, however, are sometimes not patient-friendly [11]. The SPIN Patients Alongside Investigators in Research-Sharing (SPIN-PAIRS) Trial will be conducted as part of a 90-min virtual research event for people living with systemic sclerosis (SSc, scleroderma) and family members or others close to them and will compare patient and researcher partner co-presentation versus researcher-alone presentation. Primary outcomes will be event attendee ratings of presentation (1) information completeness, (2) perceived understandability, (3) relevance to patients, and (4) trust in the research findings. In secondary analyses, we will conduct subgroup analyses of each primary outcome by participant characteristics (gender, age, race or ethnicity, country, education level, health literacy). Post-trial, we will conduct interviews with patient and researcher presenters, separately, and with patient trial attendees, to better understand and explain trial results and obtain information to improve and optimize co-presentation implementation.

Methods

Study design

The proposed study will be a pragmatic mixed-methods study [28–30] that includes: (1) A two-arm parallel randomized controlled trial (RCT) embedded within SPIN’s planned virtual event, What’s New in Scleroderma Research: An Online Event for Patients & Families, which SPIN will co-sponsor with 15 organizational partners (see Additional File 1) and (2) interviews to better understand results and gain insights to optimize implementation of the co-presentation method. We will use an explanatory sequential design in which RCT results are collected and then qualitative methods are used to generate explanations and insights to better understand outcomes [28–30]. In the RCT, participants will be randomized 1:1 to separate virtual event rooms where they will receive either four patient-researcher co-presentations or four researcher-alone presentations. To avoid confounding due to differences in researcher presenters and studies, the same research studies will be presented in each virtual room by the same researchers.

The SPIN-PAIRS trial has been registered (ISRCTN12805381). The trial protocol follows Standard Protocol Items Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) 2025 statement reporting recommendations [31]. All items from the World Health Organization trial registration data set are available as Additional File 2, and the SPIRIT checklist is available as Additional File 3. The participant consent form is provided in Additional File 4.

Trial setting and involvement of people with lived experience

SPIN [32–34] is a collaboration of researchers, clinicians, and people with SSc. SSc is a rare, chronic, autoimmune connective tissue disease that can affect multiple organ systems and cause immune dysfunction and vascular injury [35, 36]. Onset typically occurs between ages 30 and 50 years, and approximately 85% of people with SSc are female. There is no cure, and a primary care goal is to prevent or reduce disability and sustain health-related quality of life [35, 36]. SPIN maintains a cohort with > 1400 active participants from 54 centers in 7 countries (Canada, France, USA, UK, Australia, Spain, Mexico) who complete patient-reported outcomes every 3 months via the internet and participate in additional sub-studies, including trials [32–34, 37–45].

People with SSc are involved in SPIN as leaders, collaborators, and consultants. Eleven people with SSc have oversight and decision-making roles as SPIN Steering Committee members, more than 30 people with SSc contribute to project-specific advisory teams, and many others have helped identify research priorities via focus groups and surveys [46]. SPIN’s Steering Committee prioritized research to strengthen our patient engagement and more effectively disseminate results. We recruited a 13-member Patient Engagement Advisory Team, and members participated in developing this project, including determining research questions, selecting an approach, and choosing and refining outcomes. Members of the Patient Engagement Advisory Team will contribute additionally by partnering to develop and deliver our co-presentation training, select research studies to include in our planned event, interpret results, co-author manuscripts, and co-present findings of our planned trial to other patients. In addition, we have partnered with 15 partner organizations, including 13 regional and national patient organizations from Canada, the USA, and Australia, a not-for-profit SSc research funder, and a professional organization that provides SSc education. Partner organization representatives contributed to the project’s development and event planning and will promote the event by sharing announcements with their communities to recruit researcher presenters and event attendees.

Eligible participants

Eligible participants will be adults aged 18 or older who indicate via self-report that they have been diagnosed with SSc by a physician. Not restricting eligibility further reflects the pragmatic nature of our trial [47, 48] and matches how similar research events are delivered by our organizati\onal partners. Consistent with what occurs in typical patient-oriented research events held by our organizational partners, other people who wish to attend the event (e.g., family members, health professionals) will be permitted to register and will be assigned to one of the event rooms, but they will not be enrolled in the trial. Family members will be assigned to the same room as their identified eligible trial participant.

Trial interventions

Our primary outcomes (information completeness, understandability, relevance to patients, trust in findings) could be influenced by researcher presentation skills and the nature and complexity of studies being presented. To avoid confounding of these variables with our trial comparison (patient-researcher co-presentation versus researcher-alone presentation), we will include the same studies and researchers in both trial arms. To achieve this and align our events as much as possible with real-world conditions, we will pre-record each research presentation via Zoom and then play the recordings during the Zoom event. On Zoom, recorded and live presentations are similar. To ensure researchers’ experiences partnering with a patient co-presenter do not influence their researcher-alone presentations, we will record researcher-alone presentations first, followed by co-presentation training and partnered presentation development, and then recording of co-presentations. Question-and-answer periods will be live during the event because doing otherwise would negatively influence the experience of attendees and diverge from how real-world patient-oriented research events are conducted. We will schedule the order of presentations in co-presentation and researcher-alone event rooms so that researchers can be present for the question-and-answer periods in both event rooms, even though they are occurring simultaneously.

Selection of research studies to disseminate

SPIN managed the selection processes for research presentations at the 2022 and 2024 patient-oriented Canadian National Scleroderma Conferences [25, 26, 49, 50], and we will follow a similar procedure. Between 5 and 6 months prior to our planned event, we will disseminate, multiple times, a call for “patient-oriented abstracts” via SPIN and organizational partner social media networks and email lists. The call will emphasize that abstracts must focus on topics relevant to people with SSc, be ≤ 400 words, and use the United States Patient-Centered Outcomes Research Institute plain-language summary headings (“What was the research about?”, “What were the results?”, “Who was in the study?”, “What did the research team do?”, “What were the limits of the study?,” “How can people use the results?”) [51]. Abstracts will be due 5 months pre-event and submitted via Qualtrics [52]. Researchers will be asked to indicate if they engaged patients in their study and confirm their willingness to co-present with a patient partner, either one they worked with in their study or one assigned by the SPIN Team. Only researchers not involved in the trial will be eligible to present. Researchers who conducted their studies with patient partners will co-present with those partners. Other researchers will be paired with a patient volunteer from the SPIN Patient Engagement Advisory Team or from one of our partner organizations.

As we have done previously, a research selection committee composed of people with SSc will review submissions. A patient partner who led this process previously will chair the committee, and a SPIN clinician-researcher will be available to the committee to consult and answer questions about submitted abstracts. Prior to meeting, committee members will independently review abstracts and rate them on a 1 to 5 scale based on how interested they believe event attendees would be in the topic. The committee will then meet virtually to discuss ratings and rank abstracts as a group. SPIN Team members will review top-ranked abstracts and select the final 4 with a focus on diversity in presenter characteristics (e.g., gender, age, race or ethnicity, geographic location). Priority will also be given to early-career researchers (e.g., trainees, early-career investigators). This decision was made based on organizational partner input that early-career researchers are more likely to do research presentations in their real-world conference settings. The final selections will be shared with committee members for confirmation. Selected researchers will be notified by SPIN approximately 4 months pre-event.

Researcher-alone trial arm

Approximately 4 months pre-event, we will provide selected researchers with written guidance on communicating research to patient audiences that has been developed by the United States National Institutes of Health [4]. This reflects real-world practice, where researchers may receive written guidance but do not receive formal training before presenting to patients. Approximately 3 months pre-event, the SPIN Team will meet with researchers to record their presentations via Zoom in a single sitting, as if live, as will be done in the co-presentation arm.

Co-presentation trial arm

Approximately 2 to 3 months prior to our planned virtual event, and after researchers have recorded their researcher-alone presentations, we will hold a one-hour co-presentation training workshop for patient-researcher dyads. The workshop will be developed and presented by SPIN researchers and SPIN Patient Engagement Advisory Team members who have experience co-presenting. At the start of the training session, attendees will rate their confidence to co-present using a single-item question: “How confident do you feel in your ability to co-present?” on a 0 to 10 scale (0 = not at all confident, 10 = extremely confident). The training will cover strategies identified in our qualitative interview study on how to support effective co-presentation [26], including establishing clear roles and expectations, engaging patients early and consistently, maintaining flexibility in planning, and addressing potential technology challenges [26]. Following the training, from approximately 12 weeks to 3 weeks pre-event, patient and researcher co-presenter dyads will meet three times to clarify roles and discuss the study to be co-presented (session no. 1); review, revise, and refine researcher-generated PowerPoint slides developed based on discussions and agreements reached in session no. 1 (session no. 2); and practice co-presenting (session no. 3). Co-presenter dyads may also interact between workshop sessions. We will use structured session guides and post-session reporting to ensure that all tasks occur as planned. The SPIN Team will facilitate and record final presentations via Zoom in a single sitting, as if they were being done live, approximately 3 weeks before the event. Following this, we will obtain feedback on the workshop training from patient and researcher presenters via the same confidence rating used pre-training (“How confident do you feel in your ability to co-present?”; 0 = not at all confident to 10 = extremely confident) and a satisfaction item rated on a 0 to 10 scale (“How satisfied are you with the co-presentation training workshop and preparation sessions overall?”; 0 = not at all satisfied, 10 = extremely satisfied) plus two open-ended questions (“What aspects of the training and preparation process were most helpful for you?” and “What aspects of the training and preparation process could be improved?”).

Trial outcomes and measures

We will include four primary outcomes: (1) information completeness (“This presentation provided everything I wanted to know about the study”), (2) understandability (“The information in this presentation was easy to understand”), (3) relevance (“The information in this presentation was relevant to me”), and (4) trustworthiness (“I trust that this presentation’s information is accurate and unbiased”). Response options for all items will be on a 0 to 10 numerical rating scale (0 = strongly disagree to 10 = strongly agree) [53]. Similar outcomes were used in a previous trial from the UK [53], and we modified wording minimally based on Patient Engagement Advisory Team input. These outcomes are also being used in a series of SPIN trials that are comparing results dissemination to study participants and others with lived experience via different tools (e.g., plain language summary, infographic, podcast) [54]. Single-item outcomes have been shown to perform equivalently to multi-item outcome measures with reduced burden to participants when the constructs being assessed are unidimensional, clearly defined, and narrow in scope [55–58], as is the case with our outcomes. We found our outcomes to have excellent face validity based on review by patient advisors. We analyzed data from similar items used in the previous trial [53] and found that correlations between items were between 0.40 and 0.61, suggesting reasonable convergence but measurement of distinct constructs.

Participants will provide their sociodemographic information (sex, gender, age, race or ethnicity, country, education, relationship status, occupation), SSc information (diffuse or limited subtype, years since diagnosis), and complete a 12-item health literacy measure (Health Literacy Survey Questionnaire 12; HLS19-Q12), which will be scored as a continuous variable at registration [59]. Outcome ratings will be collected during the virtual event using the Zoom polling feature immediately after each presentation. Ratings will be linked to participants’ registration information based on the email associated with their personal Zoom link.

To better understand and explain trial outcomes and obtain information to improve and optimize co-presentation implementation, we will conduct semi-structured interviews separately with patient and researcher co-presenters and with a purposefully recruited subset of conference attendees. Interview guides, developed via an iterative process involving Patient Advisory Team consultation, discussion amongst research team members, and pilot testing, will be used. Our team has developed interview guides using this process in previous SPIN studies that have incorporated qualitative data collection [26, 60–62]. Interviews will take place via Zoom and will be performed by research team members with specialized training, experience conducting interviews, and who have worked with people with SSc, which will enable them to build rapport with patients, researchers, and conference attendees – a requisite of strong qualitative inquiry [63, 64]. See Additional File 5 for the interview guides.

Sample size

We are interested in comparing co-presentation and researcher-alone presentation on four criteria rather than testing a single universal null hypothesis of no difference. Thus, we have powered the trial per comparison between arms without adjusting for multiple primary outcomes [65–68]. For each outcome, with four presentations rated per participant, an assumed effect size between co-presentation and researcher-alone presentation of standardized mean difference (SMD) = 0.50, an assumed intra-class correlation (ICC) of 0.80 for ratings within each participant, and a 2-tailed test with α = 0.05 and N = 116 (58 participants per arm) would be needed for ≥ 80% power [69]. We assumed an effect size of SMD = 0.50 because there are no established minimal important differences for our outcome variables, and an SMD = 0.50 has been found to estimate minimal important differences reasonably well in many studies [70, 71]. We could not identify other examples of laypersons rating research, but we identified several studies [72–78] that reported ICCs for other types of judging scenarios (e.g., gymnastics, music performance). ICCs varied widely across scenarios, but most were between 0.30 and 0.80. Higher ICCs result in less power, so we conservatively assumed ICC = 0.80. Our sample size estimate does not consider increases in power from adjustments for prognostic covariates [79–81].

Based on our previous experience, we expect to recruit far more participants than the 116 required to power our primary outcome analyses. SPIN has held patient webinars on a range of topics in partnership with Scleroderma Canada, and attendance was between 100 and 200 people (videos of individual webinars have been viewed over 3000 times) [82]. These events were advertised for short periods (e.g., 2 weeks) compared to the planned 3 to 4 months with recurrent teasers for our planned event. The current numbers of people reached by SPIN and Scleroderma Canada via social media (approximately 11,000) and email (approximately 9000) are far smaller than the combined numbers of our partner organization coalition involved in the SPIN-PAIRS Trial (social media > 60,000; email > 40,000).

We will interview all patient and researcher co-presenters and a subset of event attendees. We will recruit among attendees purposefully to maximize diversity, based on participant characteristics reported at enrollment, using the PROGRESS-Plus [83, 84] framework and responses to post-conference outcome measures. We will continue interviewing conference attendees until no new information is shared, which fits within a pragmatic paradigm of qualitative research [85, 86]. In past SPIN studies, we have interviewed approximately 20 participants [61, 62].

Recruitment

SPIN and its organizational partners will publicize the virtual event through social media and email lists, including emails to SPIN Cohort participants and to members of partner organizations. We will begin event announcements and recruitment 3 to 4 months pre-event. Announcements will include a link to a Qualtrics [52] page where people can register, provide informed consent or contact investigators to ask questions, and provide sociodemographic and medical information and complete a health literacy measure. We will send periodic “teasers” with information about the event (e.g., sponsors, researcher presenter profiles) to people who are registered and send new recruitment messages to our broader networks.

On the day of the event, consented participants will be sent a Qualtrics link to confirm registration and formally enroll. Those who click on the link will be enrolled in the trial and randomly assigned to the co-presentation or researcher-alone presentation rooms via randomization in Qualtrics. Based on randomization, participants will be emailed a Zoom link for their assigned room. The co-presentation and researcher-alone presentation events will occur simultaneously and last 90 min (four presentations, which will each include 10 min for the presentation, 8 min for live questions and answers, and 2 min to complete outcome measures; 5 min each for welcoming and closing). Participants will not be aware that they are being randomized or that there are separate events with different presentation formats occurring simultaneously.

Randomization

Participants in the SPIN-PAIRS Trial who have registered, consented to participate, and completed pre-trial measures will be provided a link to Qualtrics to log in on the day of the event. On the day of the event, those who log in to attend will be automatically randomized using the Qualtrics built-in randomization feature using simple randomization with 1:1 allocation to receive by email: (1) a Zoom link to the co-presentation virtual event or (2) a Zoom link to the researcher-alone virtual event.

Blinding and protecting against sources of bias

Randomization and allocation will occur immediately and automatically upon event login in Qualtrics [52]. This will occur without researchers knowing who enrolls or in what sequence; thus, we will have complete allocation concealment. Trial participants will consent to participate in a virtual research event and rate presentations. They will not be informed that they are part of a randomized trial or that 2 conditions are being compared. Thus, participants will be blind to study comparisons and hypotheses. Presenters will not be blinded during the event.

We will use several methods to control for potential presentation-related biases. First, we will include the same research studies and researcher presenters in both trial arms to standardize presentation content and protect against confounding that could occur if there were differences in presenter skill, study complexity, or interest level. The two trial arm events will occur simultaneously, and researchers will switch event rooms midway through the event without participants being aware. Second, to avoid contamination between co-presentation and researcher-alone conditions, researchers will first record their individual presentations and then be paired with a patient partner for training, presentation development, and to record the co-presentation. Pre-recorded presentations will not alter the Zoom-based participant experience substantively and, visually, will be similar to live presentations. Third, we will provide presenters with instructions to ensure that they do not disclose information about the trial design or hypotheses during the live question-and-answer period.

Attendees will have 2 min after each presentation to complete the four single-item outcome measures, which will reduce recall bias. We expect minimal missing data but will use intent-to-treat analyses with multiple imputation to address missing data and will control for key participant characteristics (gender, age, health literacy) to account for any imbalances between trial arms.

Data collection and management

Primary outcome ratings will be collected immediately after each presentation using Zoom’s poll feature. Post-event, we will conduct qualitative interviews with patient and researcher co-presenters and with a subset of attendees. These will occur within 2 weeks of our planned virtual event.

All information obtained from participants during the trial will be treated confidentially within the limits of the law. To protect the privacy of participants, a unique participant identification number will be automatically assigned to each participant. During the trial, access to the trial database will be limited to study investigators. Once the trial ends and results are reported, anonymized data will be made available upon reasonable request. No biological specimens will be collected.

All posttrial interviews will be audio-recorded and transcribed using Otter.ai [87]. A research team member will review transcriptions to verify accuracy, correct any transcription errors, and remove any information that could potentially compromise participants’ anonymity [88]. To ensure confidentiality, a unique identification number will be assigned to interview participants, and an encrypted database will be created.

Data analysis

We will compare participants by trial arm on sociodemographic and SSc characteristics via descriptive statistics. For each outcome measure (information completeness, perceived understandability, relevance, trust in research findings), each participant will provide four responses (one for each presentation). We will also record the number of questions asked and the length of the question-and-answer period for each presentation.

The primary analysis will use a linear mixed-effects model with multiple observations per participant [89] (lmer function from the lme4 package in R [90, 91]). For all models, to account for participant-level variability, we will fit a random intercept by participant and evaluate whether we should also include a random slope by comparing fit and residual errors between random intercept and random slope models. In all models, we will adjust for pre-specified covariates based on the PROGRESS-Plus framework [83, 84], including gender, age (continuous), health literacy as measured with the HLS19-Q12 [59] (continuous), and presentation. We will use multiple imputation by chained equations (mice package in R, 20 imputed datasets, 15 cycles per dataset) to account for missing data [92], which we expect to be minimal. Pooled standard errors and 95% confidence intervals will be estimated using Rubin’s rules [93]. Based on PROGRESS-Plus [83, 84], we will perform subgroup analyses by gender (women, men, other if sufficient number of participants), age (18 to 44 years, 45 to 64 years, 65 years or older), race or ethnicity (White, other), country (Canada, France, USA, other), education level (≤ 12 years, > 12 years), and health literacy (HLS19-Q12 ≥ 66.67 = sufficient or excellent, HLS19-Q12 < 66.67 = inadequate or problematic) [59]. We will also conduct subgroup analyses by presentation to assess whether there were differences in outcome ratings across presentations. We will use the Instrument to assess the Credibility of Effect Modification Analyses criteria to evaluate subgroup effect credibility [94].

The statistical analysis plan is available in Additional File 6.

Interview data will be analyzed using elements of interpretive description [95] and thematic analysis [96], via a hybrid inductive–deductive approach. First, coding will progress inductively; deductive reasoning will then be incorporated to align interpretations with previous literature and theory when appropriate and consistent with the data. This combined approach will extend existing theory and empirical knowledge while enabling novel themes to be explored. Analyses will be conducted in sequential and iterative steps, and strategies recommended in the literature will be used to enhance transparency and rigor [63, 64, 97].

Trial coordination and data monitoring

The project will be coordinated by the SPIN Team in Montréal, Québec, Canada. The SPIN Steering Committee and principal investigators, Drs. Thombs, Wurz, and Benedetti, will oversee the project. The SPIN director (Dr. Thombs) and investigators will be responsible for monitoring data quality and RCT protocol execution. The SPIN Steering Committee will be updated on and oversee the progress of the project.

Risks and potential benefits

We do not anticipate any serious risks or safety concerns associated with participating in the SPIN-PAIRS Trial. The only possible harm we identified is that being informed of research results may lead to disappointment if the results are not as hoped [11]. Nonetheless, any adverse event that is reported will be recorded, and, when necessary, the event will be discussed with clinical members of the team. Any serious adverse events that occur will also be reported to the Research Ethics Board of the Centre intégré universitaire de santé et de services sociaux du Centre-Ouest-de-l'Île-de-Montréal. Possible benefits from participation in the trial include learning about new SSc research in a format designed for people with lived SSc experience and being able to contribute to research. There will be no financial compensation for participants in the trial.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethics approval was obtained from the Research Ethics Board of the Centre intégré universitaire de santé et de services sociaux du Centre-Ouest-de-l'Île-de-Montréal (no. 2025-4165). Any modifications to the protocol that may impact the conduct of the study, including changes of study objectives, study design, eligible participants, sample sizes, study procedures, or significant administrative aspects, will undergo a formal amendment to the protocol. Any such amendment will be submitted to the research ethics committee for approval and documented in the trial’s registration.

The trial has been registered on the International Standard Randomised Controlled Trial Number Registry (ISRCTN12805381) and will be reported per the Consolidated Standard of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) statement [98, 99], relevant CONSORT extensions [47, 100, 101], Template for Intervention Description and Replication intervention reporting guidance [102], and reporting guidance for mixed-methods studies [103].

There is limited evidence on methods for engaging patients in research dissemination. Our findings will inform researchers, patient organizations, and other stakeholders on whether patient-researcher co-presentation enhances information completeness, perceived understandability, relevance, and trust in research findings compared to traditional researcher-alone presentations. Funding agencies and research ethics committees that emphasize patient engagement may also consider these findings when evaluating knowledge translation strategies. Additionally, qualitative insights from co-presenters and attendees will guide refinements to the co-presentation method. Our partner organizations plan to implement trial findings into their dissemination events. We will make our co-presentation training workshop publicly available and offer it on an ongoing basis to SSc organizations and other groups interested in incorporating this approach.

Knowledge mobilization

Our knowledge mobilization plan, which is published on the Open Science Framework [104], describes (1) how we incorporated integrated knowledge translation into our research plan, (2) our target audiences and how tools for end-of-grant dissemination will be tailored, and (3) what we hope to achieve and how we will monitor success. We actively engaged patients and stakeholder partners in our project planning and will engage them in conduct and dissemination. Our strategy includes multiple dissemination tools: scientific publications, patient organization news articles, patient-oriented tools, toolkits, conferences, and co-presentation workshops. Our patient organization partners intend to work with SPIN to make our patient-oriented research event into an annual event. If co-presentation is found to be effective, co-presentation will be the required format in future years. A key aspect of our knowledge mobilization plan is the development and dissemination of virtual and in-person training to support others to implement co-presentation. To monitor the effectiveness of our knowledge mobilization, we will track publication citations, website engagement, and participation in our training initiatives.

Discussion

Ethics policies and funding agencies emphasize or require patient engagement throughout the research process [1, 16–20], yet patients are rarely engaged in dissemination [21–23]. Researchers are mandated ethically to share study results with participants [1–3], and funding agencies emphasize broader dissemination to other patients [4–7]. However, many researchers find it difficult to simplify complex findings, and many patients struggle to understand study purposes, results, or significance. Our SPIN-PAIRS Trial will evaluate one method of engaging patients in dissemination by evaluating a novel approach in which patient partners co-present research findings alongside investigators during a live online event for people with SSc [25, 26]. Findings from SPIN-PAIRS will contribute to the evidence base on effective strategies for sharing results with study participants and others with relevant lived experience.

There are limitations to consider related to our proposed trial. This will be the first trial to test co-presentation. No single trial can produce results that are fully generalizable to all study types or populations. Our findings will be directly applicable to the SSc patient organization community, and they will also provide insights relevant to other research and patient support contexts. To avoid contamination between patient–researcher co-presentation and researcher-alone presentation, we will pre-record presentations, which differs from real-world patient-oriented events. We will, however, record presentations as if they were live via Zoom (e.g., one shot with no “redos”), and we will have a live question-and-answer period. We believe that this jointly maximizes the participant experience and scientific validity. We will carefully outline limitations and any constraints on the generalizability of our findings in publications.

Trial status

This is protocol version no. 1, finalized on August 5, 2025. Participant recruitment and trial enrollment have not begun. We anticipate initiating recruitment for the trial in August 2025.

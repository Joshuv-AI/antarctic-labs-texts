A Superalgebra Within: representations of lightest standard model particles form a ℤ 2 5 \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{5} -graded algebra
N. Furey

Abstract
It is demonstrated how a set of particle representations, familiar from the Standard Model, collectively form a superalgebra. Those representations mirroring the internal behaviour of the Standard Model’s gauge bosons, and three generations of fermions, are each included in this algebra, with exception only to those irreps involving the top quark. This superalgebra is isomorphic to the Euclidean Jordan algebra of 16 × 16 16\times 16 hermitian matrices, ℋ 16 ​ ( ℂ ) , \mathcal{H}_{16}(\mathbb{C}), and is generated by division algebras. The division algebraic substructure enables a natural factorization between internal and spacetime symmetries. It also allows for the definition of a ℤ 2 5 \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{5} grading on the algebra. Those internal symmetries respecting this substructure are found to be 𝔰 ​ 𝔲 ​ ( 3 ) C ⊕ 𝔰 ​ 𝔲 ​ ( 2 ) L ⊕ 𝔲 ​ ( 1 ) Y , \mathfrak{su}(3)_{C}\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_{L}\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)_{Y}, in addition to four iterations of 𝔲 ⁡ ( 1 ) \mathfrak{u}(1) . For spatial symmetries, one finds multiple copies of 𝔰 ​ 𝔬 ​ ( 3 ) \mathfrak{so}(3) . Given its Jordan algebraic foundation, and its apparent non-relativistic character, the model may supply a bridge between particle physics and quantum computing. We close by describing current research directions. These include (1) detailing how this construction fits into the larger picture of Bott Periodic Particle Physics, first introduced in [ 1 ] , [ 2 ] , [ 3 ] , (2) investigating how the origin of this Peirce decomposition might be grounded in the unsung algebra 𝕆 ⊕ ℍ ⊕ ℂ ⊕ ℝ \mathbb{O}\oplus\mathbb{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{R} , and (3) proposing how these two directions may merge by reframing the model in terms of the 16 ℝ \hskip 1.42262pt\mathbb{R} dimensional sedenion algebra, 𝕊 \mathbb{S} .

As with SUSY models, the model proposed in this article is based on the structure of a superalgebra. However, we use this superalgebra in a different way, and as a result, find a description of elementary particles that is not supersymmetric. Notably, this description does not entail a doubling of the Standard Model’s particles.

With this said, our ultimate goal is to encode not only a single generation, but rather, the Standard Model’s three generations, together with its gauge and Higgs bosons. How might one leverage

Inspired by nature’s genetic code,

of

Recall that
\mathbb{C}l(8)
is the 256-dimensional unital associative algebra over
\mathbb{C}
generated by

satisfying the relations

\{\gamma_{j},\gamma_{k}\}=2\delta_{jk}

(20)

for all
j,k

For later use, we may define
\mathbb{A}
’s right multiplication algebra,

to be the subalgebra of

generated by

\mathbb{A}

of

In this article, we will be most interested in the hermitian subset of

The algebra in which this

Readers can confirm that

and

One can construct a number of proposals to explain the origin of this quaternionic substructure. For example, one may adapt the model by considering

instead of

VIII.1
Peirce decomposition

Given a set
\{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\}
of mutually orthogonal idempotents in
\mathcal{H}_{16}(\mathbb{C})
satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}=1
, one may form the
Peirce decomposition
of
\mathcal{H}_{16}(\mathbb{C})
as

\mathcal{H}_{16}(\mathbb{C})=\underset{1\leq j\leq k\leq n}{\bigoplus}\mathcal{H}_{jk}

(33)

with respect to
\{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\}
. The subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{jk}
are defined as follows: we have

\mathcal{H}_{jj}=s_{j}\mathcal{H}_{16}(\mathbb{C})s_{j}

(34)

for all
j
. When
j\neq k
, we have

\mathcal{H}_{jk}=\{s_{j}xs_{k}+s_{k}xs_{j}\mid x\in\mathcal{H}_{16}(\mathbb{C})\}.

(35)

In the decomposition of
L_{\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{O}}
into
\mathfrak{su}(3)
representations of Figure
1
, it is clear that the set of idempotents

\mathcal{S}_{4}:=\{\hskip 1.42262ptsS,\hskip 1.42262ptsS^{*},\hskip 1.42262pts^{*}S,\hskip 1.42262pts^{*}S^{*}\}

(36)

induced a Peirce decomposition of
L_{\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{O}}.

Defining now

\begin{array}[]{l}S_{a}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+L_{e_{2}}L_{e_{6}}L_{e_{7}}\right),\hskip 8.53581ptS_{\bar{a}}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-L_{e_{2}}L_{e_{6}}L_{e_{7}}\right),\end{array}

(37)

we will consider the Peirce decomposition of
\mathcal{H}_{16}(\mathbb{C})
with respect to the set

\mathcal{S}_{5}:=\{\hskip 1.42262ptsS,\hskip 1.42262ptsS^{*},\hskip 1.42262pts^{*}S\hskip 1.42262ptS_{a},\hskip 1.42262pts^{*}S\hskip 1.42262ptS_{\bar{a}},\hskip 1.42262pts^{*}S^{*}S_{\bar{a}}\hskip 1.42262pt\}.

(38)

We label these idempotents as
s_{1}:=sS,

s_{2}:=sS^{*},

s_{3}:=s^{*}S\hskip 1.42262ptS_{a},

s_{4}:=s^{*}S\hskip 1.42262ptS_{\bar{a}},

s_{5}:=s^{*}S^{*}S_{\bar{a}}=s^{*}S^{*}.
It can be confirmed that this is a set of mutually orthogonal idempotents that sum to
1
.

\mathcal{S}_{4}
to
\mathcal{S}_{5}
indeed induces the electroweak decomposition of expression (
32
). This change in Peirce sets induces a further partitioning of the Peirce blocks in Figure (

and

idempotents help encode a certain
i\hskip 1.42262pt\mathfrak{su}(5)
algebra within the

blocks of Figure

[

A natural question to ask at this point is: What motivates these particular

VIII.2
Symmetries

With a division algebraic Peirce decomposition fixed, we would now like to determine: which symmetries respect it? As mentioned earlier, the set
L_{\mathbb{A}}\simeq\mathbb{C}l(8)
may be viewed as

\mathbb{C}l(8)=\mathcal{H}_{16}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\mathfrak{u}(16),

(39)

whereby
\mathfrak{u}(16)
acts on
\mathcal{H}_{16}(\mathbb{C})
via a commutator, per equations (
17
) and reference
[
24
]
.

The internal symmetries of our system will be represented by those elements
r_{a}u_{a}\in\mathfrak{u}(16)
obeying

ir_{a}u_{a}\hskip 2.84526pt\in\hskip 2.84526pts_{j}\hskip 1.42262pt\mathcal{H}_{L_{\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{O}}}\hskip 1.42262pts_{j}

(40)

for idempotent
s_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{5},
and
r_{a}\in\mathbb{R}.
On the other hand, spatial symmetries of our system obey

ir_{a}u_{a}\hskip 2.84526pt\in\hskip 2.84526pts_{j}\hskip 1.42262pt\mathcal{H}_{L_{\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{H}}}\hskip 1.42262pts_{j}.

(41)

This natural octonion-quaternion splitting allows for easy compliance with the Coleman-Mandula theorem.

Clearly, these symmetries commute with all idempotents in
\mathcal{S}_{5}.
Readers may verify that octonionic (internal) symmetries then take the form

is_{5}\hskip 1.42262pt\mathcal{H}_{L_{\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{O}}}\hskip 1.42262pts_{5}&\simeq&\mathfrak{u}(1).\end{array}

(42)

We identify the Standard Model’s
\mathfrak{su}(3)_{C}
within

with generators as in equation (

within

Anomaly cancellation is to be considered once a dynamical model is established.

VIII.3
Weyl Super operators

The utility of the Peirce decomposition above is that it molds
\mathcal{H}_{16}(\mathbb{C})
into a space of
Weyl super operators.
By ‘Weyl super operators’, we mean elements of a superalgebra that generalize the familiar operator
p_{\mu}\sigma^{\mu}
from the momentum space Weyl equation.

Explicitly, the degrees of freedom of Weyl operators
p_{\mu}\sigma^{\mu}
and gauge bosons reside in
diagonal

\mathcal{H}_{jj}
blocks. Together they form a momentum-space representation of the covariant derivative.

On the other hand, the complex degrees of freedom of three generations are represented in the
off-diagonal
subspaces,
\mathcal{H}_{jk}
for
j\neq k
. For fermions, particle-anti-particle partners are mapped one to the other via hermitian conjugation.

The algebraic feature that distinguishes gauge bosons from three generations of fermions is idempotent structure. That is, gauge bosons are distinguished by symmetric idempotents, as in equation (
34
), whereas three generations have non-symmetric idempotents, as in equation (
35
). Given that this model is intended to be pre-geometric, with no spacetime a priori, the usual spin statistics will need to be reconstructed carefully. This task is outside of the scope of this article.

For another research programme wherein a related superconnection is central, please see Alvarez, Delage, Valenzuela, and Zanelli,
[
46
]
,
[
47
]
.

In the material to follow, the Standard Model particle representations are mapped to specific Peirce subspaces of
\mathcal{H}_{L_{\mathbb{A}}}\simeq\mathcal{H}_{16}(\mathbb{C}).
There, the objects
\alpha_{i}^{\dagger}\alpha_{j}^{\dagger}\alpha_{k}^{\dagger}\alpha_{l}^{\dagger}\hskip 1.42262ptv\hskip 1.42262pt\alpha_{m}\alpha_{n}\alpha_{p}\alpha_{q}
may be viewed as basis vectors, while the
\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}e_{L}^{\uparrow},\nu_{eL}^{\uparrow},\dots
written in grey, are coefficients providing particle identification.

Readers should notice that this model has not yet been gauged. Of course, gauge bosons are known to transform infinitesimally as

sequences

VIII.3.4
A Jordan algebraic mosaic

In summary, within
\mathcal{H}_{16}(\mathbb{C})
we find irreducible representations corresponding to a (cursory) momentum-space covariant derivative, including gluons and electroweak bosons on the diagonal. We find two full generations of fermions within the outer off-diagonals blocks. Finally, within the inner-off-diagonal blocks, we find the fermion representations for a third generation, with only the heaviest particles evading a simple description. We identify a possible inclusion of a third sterile neutrino and the Higgs. Please see Figure

It is worth noting a couple of interesting ratios that arise in counting the Standard Model’s states. In this model, we have found a 3:1 ratio of the fermioinic sector relative to the boson sector. This counting was made possible in part by the inclusion of separate Weyl operators
p_{\mu}\sigma^{\mu}
for the various particle representations. Recently, it was pointed out by Jorge Zanelli that when the Higgs and Weyl operators are excluded, the fermionic ratio becomes 4:1.

In contrast to the Standard Model’s usual counting, we find that the Weyl operators
p_{\mu}\sigma^{\mu}
appear as distinct degrees of freedom. That is before any constraints are applied to the
\mathcal{H}_{16}(\mathbb{C})
algebra. It is worth investigating whether these additional Weyl operators may in fact be
dark matter candidates.

2. The model exhibits a non-trivial relationship between generations (mass) and spin.

XIII.1
Bott Periodic Particle Physics

When constructing a physical theory, perhaps the most difficult step is in deciding which fundamental mathematical object to choose. On one hand, we might opt for a mathematical object that is somehow the
most special.
Take for example, the unique exceptional Jordan algebra,
H_{3}(\mathbb{O}),
or the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{e}_{8},
maximal within the exceptional simple Lie algebras.

Alternatively, we might opt for a mathematical object that is somehow the
most common.
At least within the realm of non-degenerate Clifford algebras,
Cl(0,8)
and
\mathbb{C}l(2)
can be viewed as being the most common. That is, Bott Periodicity tells us that the vast majority of non-degenerate real Clifford algebras
Cl(p,q)
can be decomposed as matrix algebras in the form:

\begin{array}[]{ll}Cl(p,q)&=Cl(p_{0},q_{0})\otimes Cl(0,8)\otimes Cl(0,8)\otimes Cl(0,8)\dots\\
&=Cl(p_{0},q_{0})\otimes Cl(0,8)^{\otimes m}\end{array}

(58)

for some large m. Here,
p_{0}=p\textup{ mod }8
and
q_{0}=q\textup{ mod }8.
That is, internally, these Clifford algebras are
overwhelmingly dominated
by factors of
Cl(0,8)
.

Similarly, the vast majority of non-degenerate complex Clifford algebras
\mathbb{C}l(n)
can be decomposed as matrix algebras in the form:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{C}l(n)&=\mathbb{C}l(n_{0})\otimes\mathbb{C}l(2)\otimes\mathbb{C}l(2)\otimes\mathbb{C}l(2)\dots\\
&=\mathbb{C}l(n_{0})\otimes\mathbb{C}l(2)^{\otimes m}\end{array}

(59)

for some large m. Here,
n_{0}=n\textup{ mod }2.
Internally, these Clifford algebras are
overwhelmingly dominated
by factors of
\mathbb{C}l(2)
. Readers may appreciate that these
\mathbb{C}l(2)
factors may be interpreted as qubit algebras.

The Tenfold Way,
[
72
]
,
[
73
]
, is tied to these repeating structures, and, we speculate, may very well underlie elementary particle physics,
[
3
]
.

Now, one motivation for studying the algebra
\mathbb{R}\otimes\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{O}
came from the finding that
\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{H}\simeq\mathbb{R}\otimes\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{H}\simeq\mathbb{C}l(2)
concisely describes each of the known Lorentz representations of the Standard Model,
[
20
]
,
[
3
]
. Inclusion of the final normed division algebra,
\mathbb{O},
provides a viable medium with which to generate internal symmetries.

However,
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{R}\otimes\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{O}
might also be useful for other purposes.
Namely, it generates

Consider the full multiplication algebra of

XIII.2
Incorporating gravity

Beyond this,

\begin{array}[]{rccc}M_{\mathbb{A}}\simeq&\mathbb{C}l(6)&\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}&\left(\mathbb{C}l(2)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}l(2)\right).\\

Under Lie-Jordan splitting, we have

In this article, we have described something close to the Standard Model’s particle spectrum within the first two factors of this product,

\mathcal{H}_{8}(\mathbb{C})

One may interpret
\mathcal{H}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
as a set of
4\hskip 1.42262pt\mathbb{R}
dimensional objects of the form
\sim p_{\mu}\sigma^{\mu},
that is, with coefficients of a single spacetime index. Extending this, one may identify the

\tilde{\otimes}
taken into account. We propose identifying an element
e^{\mu}_{\nu}\in\mathcal{H}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\tilde{\otimes}\mathcal{H}_{2}(\mathbb{C})

From these beginnings, we aim to produce an emergent theory of gravity, free from an
a priori
notion of spacetime,
[

]

XIV
Outlook:
\mathbb{O}\oplus\mathbb{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{R}

One long-standing challenge in this construction has been to find the origin of the quaternionic idempotents of our Peirce decomposition. See Section
VII.3
.

Here we propose a solution by reframing this model in terms of the 15
\hskip 1.42262pt\mathbb{R}
dimensional algebra

\mathbb{O}\oplus\mathbb{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{R}.

Specifically, one may consider the subalgebras of those multiplication algebras (endomorphisms) of
\mathbb{R},

\mathbb{C},

\mathbb{H},

\mathbb{O}
commuting with a complex structure specified by the volume element of their corresponding Clifford algebras. Specifically,

\begin{array}[]{lll}\textup{\bf Multiplication algebra}&&\textup{\bf Subalgebra}\\
End_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{O})\simeq Cl(0,6)\simeq Cl(4,2)&\hskip 2.84526pt\mapsto&\mathbb{C}\oplus M_{3}(\mathbb{C})\\
End_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{H})\simeq Cl(3,1)&\hskip 2.84526pt\mapsto&M_{2}(\mathbb{C})\\
End_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{C})\simeq Cl(2,0)&\hskip 2.84526pt\mapsto&\mathbb{C}\\
End_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{R})\simeq Cl(0,0)&\hskip 2.84526pt\mapsto&\mathbb{R},\end{array}

(65)

where we have taken care to account for both left- and right-multiplication algebras. Doing so produces a set of diagonal blocks as
\mathbb{C}\oplus M_{3}(\mathbb{C})\oplus M_{2}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{R},
in close connection with those of Figure

It is important to note that the quaternionic case above differs significantly from the others, since it comprises the unique division algebra whose left- and right-multiplication algebras lead to distinct sets of endomorphisms. As such, one may view its endomorphisms as
End_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{H})\simeq Cl(0,2)\otimes Cl(0,2).
Reducing each factor to its even subalgebra gives

\begin{array}[]{lll}End_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{H})\simeq Cl(0,2)\otimes Cl(0,2)&\hskip 2.84526pt\mapsto&\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}.\\
\end{array}

(66)

One may anticipate this fact to be the source of electroweak symmetry breaking.

We propose to study these structures in the context of Connes’ non-commutative geometry,

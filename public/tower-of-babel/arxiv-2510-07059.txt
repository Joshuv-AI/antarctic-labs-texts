The isotropy group of a derivation on a Danielewski-type algebra
Abdessamad Ahouita, Rene Baltazar, M’hammed El Kahoui, Sergey Gaifullin

Abstract
Given an algebraically closed field K K of characteristic zero, we consider in this paper K K -algebras of the form A c , q = K ⁡ [ x , y , z ] / ( c ⁡ ( x ) ​ z − q ⁡ ( x , y ) ) , A_{c,q}=K[x,y,z]/\big(c(x)z-q(x,y)\big), where c ⁡ ( x ) ∈ K ⁡ [ x ] c(x)\in K[x] is a polynomial of degree at least two and q ⁡ ( x , y ) ∈ K ⁡ [ x , y ] q(x,y)\in K[x,y] is a quasi-monic polynomial of degree at least two with respect to y y . We give a complete description of the K K -automorphism group of A c , q A_{c,q} as an abstract group. Moreover, for every non-locally nilpotent K K -derivation δ \delta of A c , q A_{c,q} we prove that the isotropy group of δ \delta is a linear algebraic group of dimension at most three.



1
Introduction

Throughout this paper
K
denotes an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. For a
K
-algebra
A
and a positive integer
n
we write
A=K^{[n]}
to mean that
A
is isomorphic to the
K
-algebra of polynomials in
n
variables.

Let
A
be an affine
K
-algebra, and let
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A)
and
{\rm Der}_{K}(A)
be respectively the
K
-automorphism group and the
A
-module of
K
-derivations of
A
. The group
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A)
naturally acts on
{\rm Der}_{K}(A)
by conjugation, and a fundamental question is to classify the
K
-derivations of
A
up to conjugation. A first step towards this task is to describe the
isotropy group
of every
K
-derivation
\delta
of
A
, i.e., the subgroup of
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A)
, denoted by
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A,\delta)
, consisting of automorphisms that commute with
\delta
.

Recall that a
K
-derivation
\delta
of
A
is said to be
locally nilpotent
if for every
a\in A
we have
\delta^{k}(a)=0
for some
k\geq 1
. A classical result due to Rentschler
[
18
]
completely classifies, up to conjugation, locally nilpotent derivations of the polynomial algebra
A=K^{[2]}
, and allows to easily describe the isotropy group of every such a derivation. In the case the ground field is replaced by a principal ideal domain
R
containing
\mathbb{Q}
, a description of the group
{\rm Aut}_{R}(A,\delta)
was recently obtained in
[
7
]
for every
irreducible
locally nilpotent
R
-derivation of
A=R^{[2]}
. In dimension three, the isotropy group of a locally nilpotent
K
-derivation
\delta
of
A=K^{[3]}
was first described in
[
10
]
under the assumption that
\delta
is
triangularizable
, and then in
[
20
]
for the general case. When
A
is the coordinate ring of a
Danielewski surface

[
5
]
the isotropy group of a locally nilpotent
K
-derivation of
A
was described in
[
2
,
6
]
.

In
[
1
]
the authors studied the isotropy group of an arbitrary
K
-derivation
\delta
of
A=K^{[2]}
and obtained, among other things, criteria for
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A,\delta)
to be an algebraic group. Based on this work it is proved in
[
16
]
that
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A,\delta)
is an algebraic group if and only if
\delta
is not locally nilpotent. It is also proved that such a result no longer holds in higher dimensions. Therefore, a natural question is to ask whether the main result in
[
16
]
holds for other types of two-dimensional affine
K
-algebras. In this paper we address this question for the coordinate rings of a class of Danielewski-type surfaces embedded in the affine
3
-space
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{3}
. More precisely, we consider
K
-algebras of the form

A_{c,q}=K[x,y,z]/\big(c(x)z-q(x,y)\big),

where
c(x)\in K[x]
has degree
n\geq 2
and
q(x,y)\in K[x,y]
is
quasi-monic
of degree
d\geq 2
with respect to
y
, i.e., its leading coefficient with respect to

is a constant in
K^{\star}
. This class of affine algebras includes the ones defined and studied in

[
14
,
4
]
. We first give in Theorem

a complete description of
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
as an abstract group. We then obtain in Theorem

that for every non-locally nilpotent

-derivation
\delta
of

the group
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
is algebraic of dimension at most three. We also give a typical example where
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
is three-dimensional.

2
Preliminaries

In this section we recall some basic facts on locally nilpotent derivations theory, and we refer to the books
[
15
,
9
,
11
]
for more details. Throughout this paper,
K
stands for an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

Let
R
be a ring containing
\mathbb{Q}
and
A
be an
R
-algebra. Given an
R
-derivation
\xi
of
A
, the kernel of
\xi
is an
R
-subalgebra of
A
. The
R
-derivation
\xi
is said to be locally nilpotent if every
a\in A
satisfies
\xi^{k}(a)=0
for some
k\geq 1
. It is said to be
irreducible
if its image
\xi(A)
is not contained in any proper principal ideal of
A
. We denote by
{\rm LND}_{R}(A)
the set of locally nilpotent
R
-derivations of
A
.

A locally nilpotent
R
-derivation
\xi
of
A
induces an
R
-algebra homomorphism
\exp(t\xi):A\longrightarrow A[t]=A^{[1]}
defined for every
a\in A
by

\exp(t\xi)(a)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{k!}\xi^{k}(a)t^{k}.

If we let
B=\ker(\xi)
, then the extension of
\exp(t\xi)
to
A[t]
, as an
R
-algebra homomorphism, by setting
\exp(t\xi)(t)=t
yields a
B[t]
-automorphism of
A[t]
whose inverse is
\exp(-t\xi)
. In particular, for every
a\in B
the map
\exp(a\xi)
is a
B
-automorphism of
A
, called an
exponential automorphism
of
A
. The following result
[
9
, Proposition 2.1.3]
characterizes the exponential automorphisms of
A
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
A
be a ring containing
\mathbb{Q}
and
\sigma
be an endomorphism of
A
. Then
\sigma=\exp(\xi)
for some locally nilpotent derivation
\xi
of
A
if and only if the additive map
\eta=\sigma-{\rm id}_{A}
is locally nilpotent. In this case we have

\xi=\sum_{k\geq 1}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{\eta^{k}}{k}.

The subset of
B=\ker(\xi)
defined by

\mathfrak{pl}(\xi)=\big\{\xi(s)\mid\xi^{2}(s)=0\big\}

is an ideal of
B
called the
plinth ideal
of
\xi
. For every nonzero
a\in A
we define the
degree
of
a
with respect to
\xi
, denoted by
\deg_{\xi}(a)
, to be the degree of the polynomial
\exp(t\xi)(a)
with respect to
t
. When
A
is an integral domain
\deg_{\xi}
is a degree function, i.e., for every nonzero
a,b\in A
we have

\deg_{\xi}(ab)=\deg_{\xi}(a)+\deg_{\xi}(b).

In particular, we obtain that
B
is
factorially closed
in
A
. That is for every
a,b\in A
such that
ab\in B\setminus\{0\}
we have
a,b\in B
.

Throughout this paper we will consider
K
-algebras of the form

A_{c,q}=K[x,y,z]/\big(c(x)z-q(x,y)\big)=K[\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{z}],

where
c(x)\in K[x]
has degree
n\geq 2
and
q(x,y)\in K[x,y]
is quasi-monic of degree
d\geq 2
with respect to
y
. Such an algebra is the coordinate ring of the Danielewski surface, embedded in the affine space
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{3}
, defined by the equation
c(x)z-q(x,y)=0
and will be called a
Danielewski algebra
. The
K
-algebra
A_{c,q}
is an integral domain and can naturally be endowed with a locally nilpotent
K
-derivation denoted
\xi_{c,q}
and defined by

\xi(\bar{x})=0,\quad\xi_{c,q}(\bar{y})=c(\bar{x}),\quad\xi_{c,q}(\bar{z})=\partial_{y}q(\bar{x},\bar{y}).

The derivation
\xi_{c,q}
will be called the
standard derivation
of
A_{c,q}
. The following result
[
4
, Theorem 5]
gathers some fundamental properties of Danielewski algebras.

Theorem 2
.

Let
c(x)\in K[x]
be a non-constant polynomial and
q(x,y)\in K[x,y]
be a quasi-monic polynomial of degree
d\geq 2
with respect to
y
. Then
\ker(\xi_{c,q})=K[\bar{x}]
and
{\rm LND}_{K[\bar{x}]}(A_{c,q})=K[\bar{x}]\xi_{c,q}
. In particular,
\xi_{c,q}
is irreducible.

Let
A
be an integral domain containing
\mathbb{Q}
endowed with a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation
\xi
and let
B=\ker(\xi)
. Following the terminology introduced in
[
12
]
, we will say that
(A,\xi)
has the

freeness property
if
A
is a free
B
-module and has a basis
(s_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\deg_{\xi}(s_{k})=k
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Such a basis is called a
\xi
-basis of
A
over
B
. The following is a particular case of
[
elkahoui_2023
, Theorem 3.3]
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
c\in K[x]
be a non-constant polynomial and
q(x,y)\in K[x,y]
be a quasi-monic polynomial of degree
d\geq 2
with respect to
y
. Then

\big\{\bar{y}^{i}\bar{z}^{j}\mid 0\leq i<d,\;j\in\mathbb{N}\}

is a
\xi_{c,q}
-basis of

over

Let
R
be a ring and
p(y)\in R[y]
be a quasi-monic polynomial of degree

. We will say that

is

if its coefficient of degree

is zero. Now assume that

and let

be non-constant and

with respect to

. Borrowing the terminology of
[
17
, Definition 4]
, the Danielewski surface defined by

is said to be in

if

is centered,

is centered as a polynomial in

and
\deg_{x}(q)<\deg(c)
. In this case, we will also say that the Danielewski algebra

is in reduced form. The following lemma
[
8
, Lemma 3.1]
is an adaptation of
[
17
, Lemma 2]
to our situation.

Proposition 4
.

Let

be non-constant and

with respect to

. Then there exists a triangular

-automorphism
\sigma
of

such that

\sigma\big(c(x)z-q(x,y)\big)=\tilde{c}(x)z-\tilde{q}(x,y),

a.

\tilde{c}(x)

\deg(\tilde{c})=\deg(c)
,

\tilde{q}(x,y)

Therefore, the

(A_{c,q},\xi_{c,q})
onto
(A_{\tilde{c},\tilde{q}},\xi_{\tilde{c},\tilde{q}})
.

3
Structure of the automorphism group of a Danielewski algebra

Recall that the
Makar-Limanov invariant
of a
K
-algebra
A
, denoted by
{\rm ML}(A)
, is the intersection of the kernels of all locally nilpotent
K
-derivations of
A
. When
\deg(c)\geq 2
the Makar-Limanov invariant of
A_{c,q}
is
{\rm ML}(A_{c,q})=K[\bar{x}]
. This property is proved in
[
14
,
17
]
for the case
c(x)=x^{n}
and then for the general case in
[
4
]
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
c(x)\in K[x]
be a polynomial of degree
n\geq 2
and
q(x,y)\in K[x,y]
be a quasi-monic polynomial of degree
d\geq 2
with respect to
y
. Then we have the following properties.

1.

Every
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
satisfies

\sigma\big(K[\bar{x}]\big)=K[\bar{x}],\quad\sigma\big(\mathfrak{pl}(\xi_{c,q})\big)=\mathfrak{pl}(\xi_{c,q}).

2.

For every
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
there exists a unique
(u_{\sigma},h_{\sigma})\in K^{\star}\times K[x]
such that
\sigma(\bar{y})=u_{\sigma}\bar{y}+h_{\sigma}(\bar{x})
.

Proof.

1.
The fact that
{\rm ML}(A_{c,q})=K[\bar{x}]
yields
{\rm LND}_{K}(A_{c,q})={\rm LND}_{K[\bar{x}]}(A_{c,q})
. Thus, for every
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
the locally nilpotent derivation
\sigma\xi_{c,q}\sigma^{-1}
is irreducible, and hence
\sigma\xi_{c,q}\sigma^{-1}=e\xi_{c,q}
for some
e\in K^{\star}
. The two claimed properties immediately follow from the fact that
\ker(\sigma\xi_{c,q}\sigma^{-1})=\sigma\big(\ker(\xi_{c,q})\big)
and
\mathfrak{pl}(\sigma\xi_{c,q}\sigma^{-1})=\sigma\big(\mathfrak{pl}(\xi_{c,q})\big)
.

2.
Let
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
and write
\sigma\xi_{c,q}\sigma^{-1}=e\xi_{c,q}
for some
e\in K^{\star}
. A direct computation shows that
\xi_{c,q}\big(\sigma(\bar{y})\big)=e^{-1}\sigma\big(c(\bar{x})\big)
. The fact that
\mathfrak{pl}(\sigma\xi_{c,q}\sigma^{-1})=\sigma\big(\mathfrak{pl}(\xi_{c,q})\big)
and that
c(\bar{x})
generates
\mathfrak{pl}(\xi_{c,q})
then imply that
e^{-1}\sigma\big(c(\bar{x})\big)=u_{\sigma}c(\bar{x})
for some uniquely determined
u_{\sigma}\in K^{\star}
. Thus, we have
\xi_{c,q}\big(\sigma(\bar{y})-u_{\sigma}\bar{y}\big)=0
, and hence there exists a unique
h_{\sigma}(x)\in K[x]
such that
\sigma(\bar{y})=u_{\sigma}\bar{y}+h_{\sigma}(\bar{x})
.
∎

By Lemma
5
, every automorphism
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
leaves
K[\bar{x}]
and
\mathfrak{pl}(\xi_{c,q})
invariant and so there exists a unique pair
(e_{\sigma},a_{\sigma})\in K^{\star}\times K
such that
\sigma(\bar{x})=e_{\sigma}\bar{x}+a_{\sigma}
. Lemma
5
also implies that there exists a unique pair
(u_{\sigma},h_{\sigma})\in K^{\star}\times K[x]
such that
\sigma(\bar{y})=u_{\sigma}\bar{y}+h_{\sigma}(\bar{x})
. This yields a map

\psi:{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})\longrightarrow K^{\star}\times K^{\star}

defined for every
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
by
\psi(\sigma)=(e_{\sigma},u_{\sigma})
. As we will see in the following lemma,
\psi
is a group homomorphism. We will call it the
canonical homomorphism
from
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
to
K^{\star}\times K^{\star}
. The
unipotent

K
-automorphism group of
A_{c,q}
, denoted by
{\rm\mathbb{U}}_{K}(A_{c,q})
, is the subgroup of
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
generated by
\exp(\delta)
, where
\delta
ranges over
{\rm LND}_{K}(A_{c,q})
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
c(x)\in K[x]
be a polynomial of degree
n\geq 2
and
q(x,y)\in K[x,y]
be a quasi-monic polynomial of degree

with respect to
y
. Then the map

\psi:\sigma\in{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})\longrightarrow(e_{\sigma},u_{\sigma})\in K^{\star}\times K^{\star}

is a group homomorphism and its kernel is
{\rm\mathbb{U}}_{K}(A_{c,q})
. In other words, we have the following short exact sequence

Proof.

Let

and let us write
\sigma_{i}(\bar{x})=e_{i}\bar{x}+a_{i}
and
\sigma_{i}(\bar{y})=u_{i}\bar{y}+h_{i}
, where
(e_{i},a_{i})\in K^{\star}\times K

(u_{i},h_{i})\in K^{\star}\times K[\bar{x}]

\begin{array}[]{ll}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}(\bar{x})=e_{1}e_{2}\bar{x}+e_{2}a_{1}+a_{2},\\

\eta=\sigma-{\rm id}_{A_{c,q}}

\eta^{m+1}\big(p(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big)=0
, where
m=\deg_{y}(p)

. This gives
a(\bar{x})\eta^{m+1}(g)=0
, where
m=\deg_{y}(p)
, and then
\eta^{m+1}(g)=0
.
∎

is split exact. Let

For every positive integer

\mathbb{T}_{r}=\mathbb{G}_{m}^{r}

Clearly, we have

for some
a\in K
and
h\in K[x]
. Since
\sigma\big(c(\bar{x})\big)=e^{n}c(\bar{x})
and
c(x)
is centered it follows immediately that
a=0
.

Let us now show that
c(x)
divides
h(x)
. Let
f(x,y)\in K[x,y]
be the polynomial defined by

f(x,y)=q\big(ex,uy+h(x)\big)-u^{d}q(x,y)

and notice that, since
q(x,y)
is quasi-monic with respect to
y
, we have
\deg_{y}(f)<d
. Since moreover
q(x,y)
, viewed in
K[x][y]
, is centered the coefficient of degree
d-1
with respect to
y
of
f(x,y)
is
du^{d-1}h
. On the other hand, from the equality
c(\bar{x})\bar{z}=q(\bar{x},\bar{y})
in
A_{c,q}
it follows that

c(e\bar{x})\sigma(\bar{z})=q\big(e\bar{x},u\bar{y}+h(\bar{x})\big)=u^{d}c(\bar{x})\bar{z}+f(\bar{x},\bar{y}),

and since
c(e\bar{x})=e^{n}c(\bar{x})
it follows that
c(\bar{x})
divides
f(\bar{x},\bar{y})
in
A_{c,q}
. By Proposition
3
, there exists a unique polynomial
g\in K[x,y,z]
such that
\deg_{y}(g)<d
and
f(\bar{x},\bar{y})=cg(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{z})
in
A_{c,q}
. The fact that
\deg_{y}(f)<d
,
\deg_{y}(g)<d
and
f\in K[x,y]
then imply that
g\in K[x,y]
and
f(x,y)=cg(x,y)
. In particular,
du^{d-1}h(\bar{x})=c(\bar{x})g_{d-1}(\bar{x})
, where
g_{d-1}
is the coefficient of degree
d-1
of
g
with respect to
y
. Since
d,u\in K^{\star}
it follows that
c(\bar{x})
divides
h(\bar{x})
in
K[\bar{x}]
. Thus, we have
q\big(ex,uy+h(x)\big)-u^{d}q(x,y)=cg(x,y)
and
c(x)
divides
h(x)
, and this clearly implies that

q(ex,uy)=u^{d}q(x,y)\mod c(x).

Since on the other hand
\deg_{x}(q)<\deg(c)
we actually have
q(ex,uy)=u^{d}q(x,y)
and hence
(e,u)\in\mathbb{G}_{c,q}
. We have thus proved that
{\rm Im}(\psi)=\mathbb{G}_{c,q}
, and since moreover
\psi\varphi={\rm id}_{\mathbb{G}_{c,q}}
the exact sequence

\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\rm\mathbb{U}}_{K}(A_{c,q})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi}
\textstyle{{\rm Im}(\psi)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{1}

is split. Therefore,
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
is the inner semidirect product of
{\rm\mathbb{U}}_{K}(A_{c,q})
and
{\rm Im}(\varphi)=\varphi\big(\mathbb{G}_{c,q}\big)
.
∎

4
Isotropy group of a derivation of
A_{c,q}

Throughout this section we let
c(x)\in K[x]
be a polynomial of degree
n\geq 2
and
q(x,y)\in K[x,y]
be a quasi-monic polynomial of degree
d\geq 2
with respect to
y
, and assume that the
K
-algebra
A_{c,q}
is in reduced form. Given a
K
-derivation
\delta
of
A_{c,q}
we let
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
be the subgroup of
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
consisting of automorphisms that commute with
\delta
. In this section we study the structure of
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
. Our main result is the following Theorem.

Theorem 8
.

Let
\delta
be a non-locally nilpotent derivation of
A_{c,q}
. Then
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
is a closed algebraic subgroup of
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
of dimension at most
3
. Moreover, it falls into one of the following two cases.

1.

{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
is isomorphic to a closed subgroup of
\mathbb{G}_{c,q}
.

2.

{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
is the semi-direct product of a closed algebraic subgroup of
{\rm\mathbb{U}}_{K}(A_{c,q})
isomorphic to the additive group
\mathbb{G}_{a}
of the field
K
and a closed algebraic subgroup of
\mathbb{G}_{c,q}
.

In order to prove Theorem
8
we first give a digest on the structure of
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
as an
ind-group
introduced by Shafarevich
[
19
]
, and we refer to
[
21
,
13
]
for a comprehensive treatment.

Throughout, all affine varieties are viewed over
K
and are equipped with the Zariski topology. An
affine ind-variety
is a set
\mathcal{V}
together with a sequence

\mathcal{V}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{2}\subseteq\cdots\mathcal{V}_{k}\subseteq\cdots

of affine varieties such that

\mathcal{V}=\displaystyle{\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{V}_{k}}

and for every positive integer
k
the inclusion map
\mathcal{V}_{k}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V}_{k+1}
is a closed embedding of affine varieties. An affine ind-variety
\mathcal{V}
carries a natural topology, namely the
Zariski topology
, where a subset
\mathcal{U}
of
\mathcal{V}
is closed if and only if for every positive integer
k
the intersection
\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{V}_{k}
is a closed subset of
\mathcal{V}_{k}
. In this case,
\mathcal{U}
can be endowed with an affine ind-variety structure through the sequence

\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{V}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{V}_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{V}_{k}\subseteq\cdots.

We will say that
\mathcal{U}
is a closed
affine ind-subvariety
of
\mathcal{V}
. A
filtration
on an affine ind-variety
\mathcal{V}
is a sequence

\mathcal{W}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{k}\subseteq\cdots

of closed subsets of
\mathcal{V}
such that
\mathcal{V}=\bigcup\mathcal{W}_{k}
. The filtration is said to be
admissible
if it defines an equivalent ind-variety structure on
\mathcal{V}
, i.e., for very
k\geq 1
there exist
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\geq 1
such that
\mathcal{V}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{\ell_{1}}
and
\mathcal{W}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{\ell_{2}}
.

Given two affine ind-varieties
\mathcal{V}=\bigcup\mathcal{V}_{k}
and
\mathcal{W}=\bigcup\mathcal{W}_{k}
, a map
\varphi:\mathcal{V}\longrightarrow\mathcal{W}
is an
ind-morphism
if for every
k\geq 1
there exists
\ell\geq 1
such that
\varphi(\mathcal{V}_{k})\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{\ell}
and the induced map
\mathcal{V}_{k}\longrightarrow\mathcal{W}_{\ell}
is a morphism of affine varieties. The product
\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{W}
is naturally endowed with an affine ind-variety structure through the filtration

\mathcal{V}_{1}\times\mathcal{W}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{2}\times\mathcal{W}_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{k}\times\mathcal{W}_{k}\subseteq\cdots.

An
affine ind-group

\mathcal{G}
is an affine ind-variety endowed with a group structure such that the map
(g,h)\in\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{G}\longrightarrow gh^{-1}\in\mathcal{G}
is an ind-morphism of affine ind-varieties. A closed subgroup
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{G}
endowed with its ind-subvariety structure is an ind-group that will be called a closed
ind-subgroup
of
\mathcal{G}
. Given two affine ind-groups
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
, a group homomorphism
\varphi:\mathcal{G}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
which is also an ind-morphism is called an
ind-group morphism
.

Let
A
be a finitely generated
K
-algebra, and let us sketch how to endow the abstract group
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A)
with a natural affine ind-group structure. We refer to
[
21
, Proposition 2.1]
and
[
13
, Section 5]
for more details.

Let
A=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]/\mathfrak{a}
, where
\mathfrak{a}
is an ideal of
K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
, be a presentation of
A
and

\pi:K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]\longrightarrow A

be the canonical epimorphism. For every
m\geq 1
let

\mathcal{W}_{m}=\big\{\pi(p)\mid p\in K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]\;{\rm and}\;\deg(p)\leq m\big\},

and

\mathcal{V}_{m}=\big\{\sigma\in{\rm Aut}_{K}(A)\mid\sigma(\bar{x}_{i}),\sigma^{-1}(\bar{x}_{i})\in\mathcal{W}_{m}\;{\rm for}\;i=1,\ldots,r\big\}.

Then the sequence
\mathcal{V}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{m}\subseteq\cdots
endows
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A)
with an affine ind-group structure which actually does not depend on the chosen presentation of
A
.

Let
\psi:{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})\longrightarrow\mathbb{T}_{2}
and
\varphi:\mathbb{G}_{c,q}\longrightarrow{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
be the canonical homomorphisms. By Theorem
7
, for every
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
there exist unique
(e_{\sigma},u_{\sigma})\in\mathbb{G}_{c,q}
and
\ell_{\sigma}\in K[x]
such that
\sigma(\bar{x})=e_{\sigma}\bar{x}
and
\sigma(\bar{y})=u_{\sigma}\bar{y}+c(\bar{x})\ell_{\sigma}(\bar{x})
. For every
m\geq 1
let

\mathcal{G}_{m}=\Big\{\sigma\in{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})\mid\deg(\ell_{\sigma})\leq m\Big\}.

Clearly, every
\mathcal{G}_{m}
is an affine variety isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{c,q}\times\mathbb{A}_{K}^{m+1}
, and also a subgroup of
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
. Moreover,
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
is an increasing union of the
\mathcal{G}_{m}
’s.

Lemma 9
.

The
\mathcal{G}_{m}
’s form an admissible filtration of
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
. Moreover,
\psi
and
\varphi
are morphisms of ind-groups.

Proof.

Let
\pi:K[x,y,z]\longrightarrow A_{c,q}
be the canonical epimorphism, and for every
m\geq 1
let

\mathcal{W}_{m}=\big\{\pi(p)\mid\deg(p)\leq m\big\}

and

\mathcal{V}_{m}=\Big\{\sigma\in{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})\mid\sigma(v),\sigma^{-1}(v)\in\mathcal{W}_{m}\;{\rm for}\;v=\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{z}\Big\}.

Then the sequence
\mathcal{V}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{m}\subseteq\cdots
is an admissible filtration of the ind-group
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
. On the other hand, for every
m\geq 1
and every
\sigma\in\mathcal{G}_{m}
we have
\sigma(\bar{z})\in\mathcal{W}_{(m+n)d}
and hence
\mathcal{V}_{m}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{m}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{(m+n)d}
. Moreover, we have

\mathcal{G}_{m}=\Big\{\sigma\in\mathcal{V}_{(m+n)d}\mid\sigma(\bar{y})\in\mathcal{W}_{m+n}\Big\}

which shows that
\mathcal{G}_{m}
is a closed subset of
\mathcal{V}_{(m+n)d}
. Therefore, the
\mathcal{G}_{m}
’s form an admissible filtration of
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
.

It remains to prove that
\varphi
and
\psi
are ind-group morphisms. Notice first that
\varphi(\mathbb{G}_{c,q})\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{1}
and the map
(e,u)\in\mathbb{G}_{c,q}\longrightarrow\varphi_{e,u}\in\mathcal{G}_{1}
is a morphism of affine algebraic groups. Therefore,
\varphi
is an ind-group morphism and
{\rm Im}(\varphi)
is a closed algebraic subgroup of
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
. On the other hand, since for every
m\geq 1
the affine variety
\mathcal{G}_{m}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{c,q}\times\mathbb{A}_{K}^{m+1}
it follows that the restriction of
\psi
to
\mathcal{G}_{m}
is a morphism of affine varieties. This proves that
\psi
is an ind-group morphism.
∎

Lemma 10
.

Let
\delta
be a
K
-derivation of
A_{c,q}
. Then
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
is a closed ind-subgroup of
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
. If moreover
\delta
is not locally nilpotent then
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
is an algebraic subgroup of
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
.

Proof.

In order to prove that
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
is a closed subgroup of
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
we need to show that
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)\cap\mathcal{G}_{m}
is closed in
\mathcal{G}_{m}
for every
m\geq 0
, where

\mathcal{G}_{m}=\big\{\sigma\in{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})\mid\deg(\ell)\leq m\big\},

and
\ell(x)\in K[x]
is such that
\sigma(\bar{y})=u\bar{y}+c(\bar{x})\ell(\bar{x})
.

Let
k\geq 0
be such that

c^{k}(\bar{x})\delta(\bar{x})=f_{1}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\quad\text{and}\quad c^{k}(\bar{x})\delta(\bar{y})=f_{2}(\bar{x},\bar{y}),

(1)

where
f_{i}\in K[x,y]=K^{[2]}
depends only on
\delta
. Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{G}_{m}
and write
\sigma(\bar{x})=e\bar{x}
and
\sigma(\bar{y})=u\bar{y}+h(\bar{x})
, where
(e,u)\in\mathbb{G}_{c,q}
and
h(x)=c(x)\ell(x)
and
\deg(\ell)\leq m
. Then by applying
\sigma
to the equalities in (
1
), and taking into account
c(ex)=e^{n}c(x)
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ccc}f_{1}(e\bar{x},u\bar{y}+h(\bar{x}))&=&e^{kn}c^{k}(\bar{x})\sigma\delta(\bar{x}),\\
f_{2}(e\bar{x},u\bar{y}+h(\bar{x}))&=&e^{kn}c^{k}(\bar{x})\sigma\delta(\bar{y}).\end{array}

On the other hand, we have

\begin{array}[]{ccc}e^{kn}c^{k}(\bar{x})\delta\sigma(\bar{x})&=&e^{kn+1}f_{1}(\bar{x},\bar{y}),\\
e^{kn}c^{k}(\bar{x})\delta\sigma(\bar{y})&=&e^{kn}f_{2}(\bar{x},\bar{y})+e^{kn}h^{\prime}(\bar{x})f_{1}(\bar{x},\bar{y}).\end{array}

Since
A_{c,q}
is an integral domain and
c(\bar{x})\bar{z}=q(\bar{x},\bar{y})
it follows that
\sigma\delta=\delta\sigma
if and only if

f_{1}(e\bar{x},u\bar{y}+h(\bar{x}))-e^{kn+1}f_{1}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=0

(2)

and

f_{2}(e\bar{x},u\bar{y}+h(\bar{x}))-e^{kn}uf_{2}(\bar{x},\bar{y})-e^{kn}h^{\prime}(\bar{x})f_{1}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=0.

(3)

Since moreover
K[\bar{x},\bar{y}]=K^{[2]}
the equations (
2
) and (
3
) yield a system of polynomial equations in terms of
e,u
and the coefficients of
\ell(x)
, where
h(x)=c(x)\ell(x)
. This proves that
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)\cap\mathcal{G}_{m}
is closed in
\mathcal{G}_{m}
, and since this holds for every
m\geq 0
it follows that
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
is a closed ind-subgroup of
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
.

Assume now that
\delta
is not locally nilpotent. In order to prove that
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
is an algebraic subgroup of
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
we only need to show that
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{m}
for some positive integer
m
. That is, for every
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
, with
\sigma(\bar{y})=u\bar{y}+h(\bar{x})
, we have
\deg(h)\leq m+n
where
n=\deg(c)
.

Let
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
, and let
(e,u)\in\mathbb{G}_{c,q}
and
h(x)\in c(x)K[x]
be such that

\sigma(\bar{x})=e\bar{x},\quad\sigma(\bar{y})=u\bar{y}+h(\bar{x}).

Then we have several possibilities, depending on the nature of
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
.

– The case
f_{1}(x,y)\in K[x,y]\setminus K[x]
. From the equation (
2
) we have

f_{1}(e\bar{x},u\bar{y}+h(\bar{x}))=e^{kn+1}f_{1}(\bar{x},\bar{y})

and since
K[\bar{x},\bar{y}]=K^{[2]}
we actually have

f_{1}(ex,uy+h(x))=e^{kn+1}f_{1}(x,y).

(4)

Let
s=\deg_{y}(f_{1})\geq 1
and let us write

f_{1}(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{s}f_{1,i}(x)y^{i},

where
f_{1,i}(x)\in K[x]
.
By looking at the coefficient of degree
s-1
with respect to
y
in both sides of (
4
)
we obtain

u^{s-1}f_{1,s-1}(ex)+su^{s-1}f_{1,s}(ex)h(x)=e^{kn+1}f_{1,s-1}(x).

Since
f_{1,s}(x)\neq 0
we have
\deg(h)\leq\deg_{x}(f_{1,s-1})\leq\deg_{x}(f_{1})
.

– The case
f_{1}(x,y)=f_{1}(x)\in K[x]
and
s=\deg_{y}(f_{2})\geq 2
. From the equation (
3
) we have

f_{2}(e\bar{x},u\bar{y}+h(\bar{x}))=e^{kn}uf_{2}(\bar{x},\bar{y})+e^{kn}h^{\prime}(\bar{x})f_{1}(\bar{x})

and since
K[\bar{x},\bar{y}]=K^{[2]}
we actually have

f_{2}(ex,uy+h(x))=e^{kn}uf_{2}(x,y)+e^{kn}h^{\prime}(x)f_{1}(x).

(5)

Let us write

f_{2}(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{s}f_{2,i}(x)y^{i},

where
f_{2,i}(x)\in K[x]
. By looking at the coefficient of degree
s-1\geq 1
with respect to
y
in both sides of (
5
)
we obtain

u^{s-1}f_{2,s-1}(ex)+su^{s-1}f_{2,s}(ex)h(x)=e^{kn}uf_{2,s-1}(x).

Since
f_{2,s}(x)\neq 0
, we have
\deg(h)\leq\deg_{x}(f_{2,s-1})\leq\deg_{x}(f_{2})
.

– The case
f_{1}(x,y)=f_{1}(x)\in K[x]
and
s=\deg_{y}(f_{2})=1
. To simplify, we let
\xi
stand for
\xi_{c,q}
and recall that
\deg_{\xi}
denotes the degree function associated to
\xi
. The assumption that
\deg_{y}(f_{2})=1
implies that
\deg_{\xi}(\delta(\bar{y}))=1
and from Proposition
3
it follows that

\delta(\bar{y})=a(\bar{x})\bar{y}+b(\bar{x})

(6)

where
a(x),b(x)\in K[x]
and
a(x)\neq 0
. On the other hand, since
c(\bar{x})^{k}\delta(\bar{x})=f_{1}(\bar{x})\in K[\bar{x}]
and
c(\bar{x})\neq 0
and
K[\bar{x}]
is factorially closed in
A_{c,q}
it follows that
\delta(\bar{x})=g(\bar{x})\in K[\bar{x}]
.

By applying
\sigma
to both sides of (
6
), and taking into account
\sigma\delta=\delta\sigma
and Proposition
3
and
K[\bar{x},\bar{y}]=K^{[2]}
we obtain
a(ex)=a(x)
and

a(ex)h(x)-g(x)h^{\prime}(x)=ub(x)-b(ex).

(7)

If we have

\deg\big(a(ex)h(x)-g(x)h^{\prime}(x)\big)=\max\big(\deg(a(ex)h(x)),\deg(g(x)h^{\prime}(x))\big)

then from (
7
) it follows in particular that
\deg(h)\leq\deg(b)
and we are done. Otherwise,
g(x)
is nonzero and the leading coefficients of
a(ex)h(x)
and
g(x)h^{\prime}(x)
cancel out. Thus, if we let
{\rm lc}(a)
and
{\rm lc}(g)
be respectively the leading coefficients of
a(x)
and
g(x)
then
\deg(h)={\rm lc}(a){\rm lc}(g)^{-1}
, which proves that
\deg(h)
depends only on
\delta
and not on the automorphism
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
.

– The case
f_{1}(x,y)=f_{1}(x)\in K[x]
and
f_{2}(x,y)=f_{2}(x)\in K[x]
. From the assumption that
\delta
is not locally nilpotent we deduce that
f_{1}(x)\neq 0
. On the other hand, using similar arguments as in the first three cases we obtain

f_{2}(ex)=e^{kn}uf_{2}(x)+h^{\prime}(x)f_{1}(x)

and hence
\deg(h)\leq\deg(f_{2})+1
.
∎

Lemma 11
.

Let
\delta
be a
K
-derivation of
A_{c,q}
such that
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)\cap{\rm\mathbb{U}}_{K}(A_{c,q})
is nontrivial. Then

\delta(\bar{x})=g(\bar{x})\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\delta(\bar{y})=a(\bar{x})\bar{y}+b(\bar{x})

for some
g(x),a(x),b(x)\in K[x]
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)\cap{\rm\mathbb{U}}_{K}(A_{c,q})
be a nontrivial automorphism and let us write
\sigma=\exp(\eta)
where, by Theorem
2
,
\eta=\ell(\bar{x})\xi_{c,q}
for some nonzero
\ell(x)\in K[x]
. The assumption that
\sigma
and
\delta
commute implies that
\eta\delta=\delta\eta
, and hence
\ker(\eta)=K[\bar{x}]
is stable under
\delta
. This proves that
\delta(\bar{x})=g(\bar{x})
for some
g(x)\in K[x]
. On the other hand, since
c(\bar{x})=\xi_{c,q}(\bar{y})
generates the plinth ideal
\mathfrak{pl}(\xi_{c,q})
it follows that
\mathfrak{pl}(\eta)
is generated by
\ell(\bar{x})c(\bar{x})=\eta(\bar{y})
. Since moreover
\eta\delta(\bar{y})=\delta(\eta(\bar{y}))\in K[\bar{x}]
it follows that
\delta(\bar{y})=a(\bar{x})\bar{y}+b(\bar{x})
for some
a(x),b(x)\in K[x]
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
8
.

By Lemma
10
,
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
is a closed algebraic subgroup of the ind-group
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
. Let
\psi:{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})\longrightarrow\mathbb{T}_{2}
be the canonical homomorphism, and recall that, by Theorem
7
,
{\rm Im}(\psi)=\mathbb{G}_{c,q}
and, by Lemma
9
,
\psi
is an ind-group homomorphism. Therefore the restriction of
\psi
to
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
, say
\vartheta
, is an algebraic group homomorphism. Moreover,
{\rm Im}(\vartheta)
is a closed algebraic subgroup of
\mathbb{G}_{c,q}
and
\ker(\vartheta)={\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)\cap{\rm\mathbb{U}}_{K}(A_{c,q})
is a closed algebraic subgroup of
{\rm\mathbb{U}}_{K}(A_{c,q})
. Thus, we have one of the following two cases.

– The homomorphism
\vartheta
is injective. In this case,
\vartheta
induces an algebraic group isomorphism from
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
onto a closed algebraic subgroup of
\mathbb{G}_{c,q}
. Since the dimension of
\mathbb{G}_{c,q}
is a most two it follows that
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
is a most two-dimensional.

– The morphism
\vartheta
is not injective. We will show in this case that the exact sequence

\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ker(\vartheta)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\vartheta}
\textstyle{{\rm Im}(\vartheta)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{1}

is split and
\ker(\vartheta)
, as a closed algebraic subgroup of
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
, is isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{a}=(K,+)
.

From Lemma
11
it follows that there exist
g(x),a(x),b(x)\in K[x]
such that

\delta(\bar{x})=g(\bar{x})\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\delta(\bar{y})=a(\bar{x})\bar{y}+b(\bar{x}).

Since
\delta
is not locally nilpotent it follows that either
a(x)\neq 0
or
g(x)\neq 0
. Now let

L=\big\{\ell(x)\in K[x]\mid\exp(\ell(\bar{x})\xi_{c,q})\in\ker(\vartheta)\big\}.

Then
L
is a nonzero
K
-vector subspace of
K[x]
. On the other hand, for every
\ell(x)\in K[x]
the automorphism
\sigma=\exp(\ell(\bar{x})\xi_{c,q})
commutes with
\delta
if and only if
\sigma\delta(\bar{y})=\delta\sigma(\bar{y})
. Taking into account the fact that
K[\bar{x},\bar{y}]=K^{[2]}
, this is equivalent to

a(x)h(x)-g(x)h^{\prime}(x)=0,

(8)

where
h(x)=c(x)\ell(x)
. Therefore,
\ell(x)\in L
if and only if
h(x)=c(x)\ell(x)
satisfies the equation (
8
). The fact that
L
is nonzero, and
a(x)
and
g(x)
are not both zero then implies that
g(x)\neq 0
. Let
\ell_{1}(x),\ell_{2}(x)\in L
be nonzero and let
h_{i}(x)=c(x)\ell_{i}(x)
. Then we have

\frac{h_{1}^{\prime}(x)}{h_{1}(x)}=\frac{h_{2}^{\prime}(x)}{h_{2}(x)}=\frac{a(x)}{g(x)},

and then

\left(\frac{h_{1}(x)}{h_{2}(x)}\right)^{\prime}=0.

This proves that
h_{2}(x)=\mu h_{1}(x)
for some
\mu\in K
, and hence the
K
-vector space
L
is one-dimensional. Let us fix a nonzero polynomial
\ell_{0}(x)\in L
and write
h_{0}(x)=c(x)\ell_{0}(x)
, and let
v\in K
be such that
h_{0}(v)\neq 0
. The map

\ell(x)\in L\longmapsto c(v)\ell(v)\in K

is then an isomorphism of
K
-vector spaces, and hence the map

\sigma=\exp(\ell(\bar{x})\xi_{c,q})\in\ker(\vartheta)\longmapsto c(v)\ell(v)\in K

is an algebraic group isomorphism from the closed algebraic subgroup
\ker(\vartheta)
of
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
onto
\mathbb{G}_{a}
.

Let
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
, and write
\sigma(\bar{x})=e\bar{x}
and
\sigma(\bar{y})=u\bar{y}+h(\bar{x})
. Then
\sigma\delta=\delta\sigma
if and only if
\sigma\delta(\bar{x})=\delta\sigma(\bar{x})
and
\sigma\delta(\bar{y})=\delta\sigma(\bar{y})
. A direct computation shows that this is equivalent to
g(ex)=eg(x)
,
a(ex)=a(x)
and

a(x)h(x)-g(x)h^{\prime}(x)=ub(x)-b(ex).

(9)

The solutions of the equation (
9
) have the form
h(x)=h_{1}(x)+\mu h_{0}(x)
, where
h_{1}(x)
is a particular solution of (
9
) and
\mu\in K
.

Let
m
be the multiplicity of
0
as a root of the polynomial
h_{0}(x)
. Then there exists a unique
\ell_{e,u}(x)\in K[x]
such that
h_{e,u}(x)=c(x)\ell_{e,u}(x)
satisfies the equation (
9
) and
h_{e,u}^{(m)}(0)=0
, where
h_{e,u}^{(m)}(x)
is the
m
-th derivative of
h_{e,u}(x)
. Let
\sigma_{e,u}\in{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
be the
K
-automorphism defined by
\sigma_{e,u}(\bar{x})=e\bar{x}
and
\sigma_{e,u}(\bar{y})=u\bar{y}+h_{e,u}(\bar{x})
, and let us show that the map

\varrho:(e,u)\in{\rm Im}(\vartheta)\longmapsto\sigma_{e,u}\in{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)

is a group homomorphism.

Let
(e_{1},u_{1}),(e_{2},u_{2})\in{\rm Im}(\vartheta)
and to simplify let us write
\varrho(e_{i},u_{i})=\sigma_{i}
and
\sigma_{i}(\bar{y})=u_{i}\bar{y}+h_{i}(\bar{x})
. A direct computation shows that
\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}(\bar{x})=e_{1}e_{2}\bar{x}
and

\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}(\bar{y})=u_{1}u_{2}\bar{y}+u_{2}h_{1}(\bar{x})+h_{2}(e_{1}\bar{x}).

Since
\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\in{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
it follows that
u_{2}h_{1}(\bar{x})+h_{2}(e_{1}\bar{x})
satisfies the equation (
9
) corresponding to
(e,u)=(e_{1}e_{2},u_{1}u_{2})
. On the other hand, we have

\big(u_{2}h_{1}(x)+h_{2}(e_{1}x)\big)^{(m)}=u_{2}h_{2}^{(m)}(x)+e_{1}^{m}h_{1}^{(m)}(e_{1}x),

which clearly shows that

\big(u_{2}h_{1}(x)+h_{2}(e_{1}x)\big)^{(m)}(0)=0.

Therefore,
\varrho(e_{1}e_{2},u_{1}u_{2})=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}=\varrho(e_{1},u_{1})\varrho(e_{2},u_{2})
. This finally proves that
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
is the semi-direct product of
\ker(\vartheta)\cong\mathbb{G}_{a}
and
{\rm Im}(\vartheta)
which is a closed algebraic subgroup of
\mathbb{G}_{c,q}
. As a consequence, the dimension of
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
is at most three.
∎

The following is a typical example where
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
has dimension three.

Example 12
.

Let
c(x)=x^{n}
and
q(x,y)=y^{d}
, where
n,d\geq 2
, and consider

A_{c,q}=K[x,y,z]/(x^{n}z-y^{d}).

Clearly, we have
\mathbb{G}_{c,q}=\mathbb{T}_{2}
the two-dimensional algebraic torus over
K
.

Let
a>n
be an integer and
b\in K
, and consider the
K
-derivation
\Delta
of
K[x,y,z]
defined by

\Delta(x)=x,\quad\Delta(y)=ay+bx^{n},\quad\Delta(z)=(ad-n)z+dby^{d-1}.

A direct computation shows that
\Delta(x^{n}z-y^{d})=ad(x^{n}z-y^{d})
, and hence
\Delta
induces a
K
-derivation
\delta
of
A_{c,q}
. The equation (
8
) writes in this case as

ah(x)-xh^{\prime}(x)=0,

and its general solution is
\mu x^{a}
where
\mu
ranges over
K
. Since
a>n
it follows that
c(x)=x^{n}
divides
\mu x^{a}
, and hence
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)\cap{\rm\mathbb{U}}_{K}(A_{c,q})
consists of the automorphisms
\exp(\mu\bar{x}^{a-n}\xi_{c,q})
. On the other hand, for every
(e,u)\in\mathbb{G}_{c,q}
the equation (
9
) writes as

ah(x)-xh^{\prime}(x)=(u-e^{n})bx^{n},

and a particular solution of this equation is
h_{e,u}(x)=\frac{u-e^{n}}{a-n}bx^{n}
. Therefore, the general solution of (
9
) is
h_{e,u}(x)+\mu x^{a}
, where
\mu\in K
. Since the multiplicity of
0
as a root of
x^{a}
is
a
it follows that the unique solution of (
9
) that satisfies
h^{(a)}(0)=0
is
h_{e,u}(x)
. Thus, if we let
\sigma_{e,u}
be the
K
-automorphism of
A_{c,q}
given by
\sigma(\bar{x})=e\bar{x}
and
\sigma(\bar{y})=u\bar{y}+\bar{x}^{n}h_{e,u}(\bar{x})
then
\sigma_{e,u}
commutes with
\delta
and the map

(e,u)\in\mathbb{G}_{c,q}\longmapsto\sigma_{e,u}\in{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)

is a morphism of algebraic groups. By Theorem
8
,
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
is the semi-direct product of a subgroup isomorphic to
\mathbb{T}_{2}
and a subgroup isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{a}
. This proves in particular that
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
is three-dimensional. It is also worth mentioning that if
b\neq 0
then
h_{e,u}(x)
is in general nonzero, and hence the group action underlying the semi-direct product structure of
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q},\delta)
is not induced by the group action underlying the semi-direct product structure of
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A_{c,q})
given in Theorem
7
.

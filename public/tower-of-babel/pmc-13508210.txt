Global research landscape of natural medicines for lower urinary tract symptoms: bibliometric and clinical trial analysis
Authors: Qi Xiong, Lin Chen, Jin Yang, Qiuxia Ding, Yafei Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNatural medicines have demonstrated significant therapeutic potential in the management and attenuation of lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS). According to the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC), a total of 497 related articles have been published during the last 25 years. China was the most productive country, followed by India and the United States. However, the United States showed substantial influence in earlier years and contributed prominently to clinical trial activity. This study employs a systematic, visualized synthesis of bibliometric and clinical trial data to effectively delineate the research frontier and inform future directions.MethodsTo map the research landscape, a bibliometric examination was performed using a structured search query to gather relevant papers on natural medicines and urinary symptoms published over a 25-year span. The information extracted for analysis included titles, journals, authors, affiliations, citations, keywords, and a set of keywords associated with natural medicines for urinary symptoms. Furthermore, the ClinicalTrials.gov registry was utilized to evaluate the profile of trials testing natural agents against LUTS.ResultsThe bibliometric analysis included 497 publications from 78 countries and identified a total of 22,160 cited references. China produced the highest number of publications. Review of the clinical trial data indicated that most trials were conducted in phases II–IV. Research activity in this field demonstrated phased growth, accompanied by continued institutional funding.ConclusionThis analysis has traced research outcomes and transformation models regarding natural medicines in LUTS management. Clinical investigation confirms the field's evolution toward practical implementation. A central goal of present-day investigation involves delineating the pathological mechanisms of LUTS progression, defining particular common signaling routes, and producing effective clinical medications.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s41182-026-01064-9.

Introduction

The presenting features of lower urinary tract symptoms often have core components like frequent urination, urgent voiding, and difficulty in passing urine [1]. Lower urinary tract symptoms affect approximately 2.3 billion individuals worldwide, with a substantial burden among older men. US statistics indicate 13% benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) frequency in 40–49-year-olds and 28% LUTS incidence in men over 70. These disorders potentially affect 40% of males beyond 50 years. Nearly 63% of men aged 65+ received BPH medication within 5 years following diagnosis. This symptomatic complex is particularly widespread among elderly males [2], frequently accompanying BPH [3, 4]. The detrimental impact of LUTS on daily life and healthcare costs is well-established [1, 5]. While α1-adrenoceptor antagonists and 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors form the cornerstone of pharmacological management [6, 7], their utility is constrained by untoward effects like erectile dysfunction and blood pressure changes [8], hindering sustained use [9–13]. Therefore, identifying novel and well-tolerated interventions is a pressing clinical priority [14]. Scientific evidence supports the LUTS-alleviating effects of botanical products including saw palmetto, nettle root extracts, and pumpkin seed oil. Traditional Chinese medicinal formulas like Pule’an Tablets and Qianliekang have achieved satisfactory clinical efficacy. Mechanistically, the ability of natural medicines to interact with a network of targets, along with their reduced toxicity, presents an appealing strategy for treating LUTS [15–17]. While the scientific literature concerning natural medicines and LUTS has seen a marked increase in recent years, no study has yet systematically synthesized and visualized the intellectual landscape, including its evolutionary pathways and collaborative structures. Bibliometric analysis provides quantitative evaluation of scholarly works, identifying research formations, evolutionary courses, and emerging fronts in particular domains. The objective of this work is to uncover the knowledge structure and dynamics within the domain of natural medicines for LUTS. By analyzing a corpus of literature from 2000 to 2025 with CiteSpace, we will profile contributions across various levels and visualize networks to highlight future directions for the field [18]. Although laboratory studies show encouraging results, clinical evidence is needed to confirm therapeutic value in patients [19, 20]. Studies of natural medicinal compounds are developing through ongoing scientific investigation and initial clinical testing, while complete bibliometric assessment and comprehensive overviews of current knowledge have not yet been published. Our work establishes the first comprehensive profile of global research output on natural medicines for LUTS. We systematically chart the research territory, identify frontier areas, and trace evolutionary patterns. Clinical trial trends for natural medicines in LUTS were further examined by integrating and visually analyzing data from ClinicalTrials.gov.

Materials and methods

Data source and literature search strategy

Data for our bibliometric analysis were obtained from the WoSCC. A static bibliometric analysis was performed using publications concerning natural medicines for LUTS retrieved from WoSCC. After implementing a systematic literature retrieval strategy, we assembled a dataset spanning the previous quarter-century. The workflow diagram is shown in Fig. 1. The investigative team unanimously adopted the systematic search approach utilizing TS=("Nature medicine" OR "nature drug" OR "Traditional Chinese medicine" OR "Chinese medicine" OR "Chinese medicinal formula*" OR "Prescription of traditional Chinese medicine" OR "Herb" OR "Medicinal plant" OR "Herbal medicine" OR "Plant extract" OR "Herb extract*" OR "Herbal compound*" OR "Plant compound*" OR "Phytochemical*") AND ("Chronic prostatitis" OR "Interstitial cystitis" OR "Lower urinary tract symptoms" OR "Lower urinary tract dysfunction" OR "Overactive bladder" OR "Incontinence" OR "Urolithiasis" OR "urinary disorder" OR "Benign prostatic hyperplasia" OR "Benign prostatic enlargement"). Two investigators independently screened the retrieved records according to the prespecified eligibility criteria and independently verified the bibliographic information of the eligible studies. Disagreements concerning study eligibility or data extraction were resolved through discussion and consensus, with consultation of a third investigator when necessary. After completion of the screening and verification procedures, the final WoSCC records were downloaded in a single session on July 23, 2025, to minimize potential inconsistencies resulting from database updates over time. A total of 497 scholarly articles and reviews published from 2000 to July 23, 2025, were selected for analysis, with only formal English publications eligible. Comprehensive bibliographic metadata were preserved to prevent inconsistencies during multiple retrieval sessions. The complete WoSCC dataset was exported in plain text format with tab separators. Data analysis and graph acquisition

Our methodology applied CiteSpace combined with VOSviewer for bibliometric investigation. The visualization of literature networks employed contemporary R and RStudio versions with integration of bibliometrix (v4.1.4). To delineate research fronts and analyze thematic concentrations, we compiled PubMed data on natural medicinal applications for LUTS. Cross-national cooperation frequencies were visualized utilizing ArcMap (v10.8). We utilized Microsoft Excel 2019 and GraphPad Prism 9.3.1 for data compilation and graphical presentation. Network charts were produced using VOSviewer (version 1.6.20) after data importation. Citation data served as the foundation for network development, enabling exploration of co-authorship patterns, keyword co-occurrences, and citation relationships. A full counting methodology was applied, where each publication was counted once regardless of author count. The VOSviewer application determined publication and citation metrics along with keyword frequencies. Visualization and construction of key term co-occurrence networks were achieved using the software's integrated clustering functionality. Our research emphasizes co-occurrence examination as its fundamental analytical strategy, representing the study's main contribution. This methodology was applied to evaluate collaborative interactions across geographical, institutional, and author levels. The software identifies strongly cited publications and terms showing notable citation acceleration over time.

Analysis of clinical trials data

Clinical trial registration information was sourced from ClinicalTrials.gov to enable a detailed analysis of natural medicines research progress for LUTS. These registry data were analyzed separately as a complementary description of registered clinical studies and were not merged with the WoSCC bibliometric dataset. We implemented a search using the terms "natural medicines", "herbal medicines", "lower urinary tract symptoms", "benign prostatic hyperplasia", and "overactive bladder", which included all publications through July 2025. Extracted details consisted of experimental phase, launch date, investigation design, funding classification, and national dispersion. After arrangement with Microsoft Excel 2019, descriptive analysis and trend commentary were carried out in conjunction with bibliometric insights, addressing gaps in traditional literature appraisal regarding clinical implementation.

Analysis of co-cited references

From the 497 retrieved publications, a total of 22,160 cited references were extracted for the co-citation analysis. Co-cited references constitute an important component of the intellectual foundation of a research field. Investigation of citation networks establishes the intellectual background for nature drugs and LUTS research. As shown in Fig. 4B, CiteSpace identified 11 major co-citation clusters using the log-likelihood ratio algorithm, with cluster labels generated from the keywords of the citing articles. The identified clusters comprised "symptom relief" (cluster #0, size=61), "integrative therapy" (#1, 56), "natural drug" (#2, 39), "plant-based therapy" (#3, 37), "chronic pelvic pain syndrome" (#4, 32), "prostatic hyperplasia" (#5, 25), "botanical medicine" (#6, 24), "uvaria rufa blume" (#7, 23), "benign" (#8, 23), "herb-drug interaction" (#9, 22), and "urolithiasis mitigation" (#10, 18). Clusters #5, #8, #4, and #7 address multiple etiologies underlying LUTS. Clusters #9 and #2 represent the varieties and mechanisms of natural medicines cited in LUTS research. Clusters #0, #1, and #3 demonstrate the clinical value and therapeutic potential of natural medicines.

Clinical evidence from clinicalTrials.gov studies

Based on ClinicalTrials.gov documentation, this study evaluated clinical investigation features of natural medicinal interventions for LUTS. Analysis showed incremental development with heightened activity between 2006 and 2020, then contraction from 2021 onward potentially affected by international health emergencies. Research efforts predominantly target phases II–IV rather than phase I, underscoring increasing concern for enduring treatment benefits and safety documentation in clinical development (Fig. 7A). Financial support primarily comes from corporate players (excluding large drug firms) and academic establishments, with cooperative networks exercising important influence. Italian, Canadian, and other developed nation participation signals accommodating regulatory policies for botanical preparations (Fig. 7C). The most frequent interventions comprise plant-based extracts, nutritional supplements, and redox/anti-inflammatory agents, consistent with traditional remedies' widespread employment in urinary symptom management (Fig. 7D). Discussion

General information

This study evaluated 497 WoSCC records related to natural medicines in LUTS management. Employing bibliometric R software, VOSviewer, and CiteSpace, we executed detailed examinations of country contributions, organizational partnerships, periodical sources, collaborative networks, and keyword occurrences. Our research also assessed the discipline's conceptual architecture, active research areas, and developmental patterns. The outcomes offer essential groundwork for advancing natural medicine applications in LUTS therapy. Development of medical research and clinical trials necessitates enhanced understanding of natural medicines' contributions to LUTS management [9]. Our investigation represents the first bibliometric examination of natural medicines in LUTS therapy patterns [22–24] across 2000–2025. Growing interest in their therapeutic advantages and clinical outcomes [11, 19, 25] has significantly influenced the research field's expansion. Natural medicines' documented capacity to counter oxidative stress and control inflammatory [26] processes rationalizes the increasing publication volume.

Analysis of leading countries and institutions

The evolution of natural medicine and LUTS investigations unfolded in two phases. Initial slow progression characterized the pre-2015 period, despite 22 studies appearing in 2014. Following this, the field entered a rapid growth phase where yearly publication counts surpassed 20 from 2016 onward (except 2017). By 2024, annual output peaked at 53 articles, marking substantial advancement in this research area. China and India produced the most substantial publication numbers in natural medicine and LUTS literature. Parallel to country-level patterns, six leading Chinese institutions each generated more than ten articles. The top-ten institutional list featured five Chinese universities. The predominant collaborative format involved international alliances with Chinese participation. These findings recognize China's important role and advanced position in natural medicine research, underpinned by economic resources and scientific investments. Widespread international collaboration would improve overall research quality. China, Pakistan, and Saudi Arabia reveal substantially growing publication outputs, potentially resulting from expanding geriatric communities.

Hotspots and trends

Initial scholarly work predominantly focused on treatment efficacy and symptom management through natural preparations including saw palmetto and nettle root extracts for urinary symptoms. Modern scientific approaches have evolved toward mechanism exploration, specifically examining oxidative stress regulation, anti-inflammatory mechanisms, and proliferation modulation such as prostate cell hyperplasia suppression. Increasing prevalence of terms like "oxidative stress", "inflammation", and "mechanism" mirrors scientific progression toward molecular-level understanding of natural medicines' modes of action. The global scientific community has shown expanding interest in Chinese compound formulas and traditional preparations from India and Africa. Research efforts verify clinical benefits while applying network pharmacology and compositional studies to expound multi-target therapeutic approaches, bridging traditional experiences with modern medical evidence. Early vocabulary such as "in-vivo", "rats", and "calcium oxalate" demonstrates original emphasis on animal-based research and experimental models. Current research methodologies reveal expanded application of randomized controlled trials, meta-analyses, and real-world evidence, yielding improved study designs and evidentiary hierarchy. The increasing emphasis on terms including "double blind", "clinical trial", and "management" indicates advancement from basic investigation toward clinical translation. With population aging and rising cancer prevalence, natural medicines gain prominence for managing prostate cancer-associated voiding symptoms, overactive bladder, and calculus prevention. Moreover, medicinal-edible botanicals such as parsley and pumpkin seeds demonstrate emerging utilization in health-functional foods and dietary supplements, providing integrated prophylactic and therapeutic actions. Research leadership is maintained by China, the US, and India, while Egypt and Saudi Arabia demonstrate substantial growth in international alliances, reflecting the domain's global expansion. Multidisciplinary cooperation is particularly visible, merging fundamental medical research, drug discovery, nutrition studies, and computational methods to drive natural medicine exploration toward integrated and precision-oriented approaches.

Mechanism of natural medicines in LUTS

Progress in molecular biological techniques and multi-omics platforms has enabled sophisticated mechanism studies. In benign prostatic hyperplasia, natural medicines including nettle root [41, 42] and pumpkin seed [43, 44] extracts mediate Nrf2/HO-1 and NF-κB pathway modulation, effectively countering oxidative stress and inflammatory processes independent of classical hormonal mechanisms [45–51]. For neurogenic bladder, capsaicin analogs desensitize TRPV1 to normalize sensation [52, 53]. Fibrosis is countered by astragalus polysaccharide and tetramethylpyrazine through TGF-β1/Smad downregulation [54, 55]. Resveratrol and curcumin promote Nrf2 activity, mitigating oxidative damage to neural and muscular structures [56, 57]. Clinical studies have also evaluated traditional Chinese herbal interventions for refractory CP/CPPS [58]. Systems pharmacology investigations further reveal the multi-target actions of herbal formulations on cytokine networks, including TNF-α and IL-6 [59–61]. Research on overactive bladder increasingly concentrates on TRP channel regulation and sensory nerve sensitization [62–67]. Polyphenolic compounds and terpenoids [68] improve overactive bladder symptoms via anti-inflammatory properties, C-fiber afferent regulation, and acetylcholine release suppression [68–74].

Recent scientific exploration has extended to natural medicines' neuromodulatory and muscular mechanisms in LUTS pathology. Berbamine administration counteracted c-Fos upregulation from retinoic acid exposure while decreasing TRPV1 expression in urinary epithelium [75]. Alpinia oxyphylla extract mediated PKA pathway activation to promote detrusor relaxation, consequently augmenting bladder capacity [76]. Berbamine therapy significantly reduced NGF and BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) levels in urine, neurotrophins connected to bladder hypersensitivity and involuntary contractions [75].

Specific natural medicines including rosmarinic acid and ellagic acid demonstrate calcium [77–80] oxalate crystallization inhibition and urinary pH adjustment properties, supporting their adjunct administration for urolithiasis. Innovative approaches such as network pharmacology and metabolomics are promoting an evolution from single-agent single-target research toward multi-component multi-pathway systematic investigations.

Natural medicines in the treatment of LUTS: AI and multi-omics research

Advancing studies of natural medicines for LUTS have highlighted the significant roles of artificial intelligence combined with multi-omics strategies. Early multi-omics development periods featured research that mainly applied genomic and proteomic methodologies for basic target recognition of botanical medicines [81, 82]. Proteomic studies reveal that nettle root preparations mediate anti-inflammatory responses through proteins including COX-2 and iNOS [41, 42, 83, 84]. With progressive accumulation of multi-omics data from transcriptional, metabolic, and protein analyses, network pharmacology has become a significant research emphasis. Through component–target–pathway network modeling, research has thoroughly explained TCM formulas’ multi-target modulation of signaling pathways associated with inflammation, oxidative damage, and apoptosis [15, 16, 33, 39, 58]. Multi-platform omics enable prediction of natural medicine component interactions with LUTS targets. Artificial intelligence screening detects plant-derived compounds with 5α-reductase inhibitory activity [85]. Deep learning integration with multi-omics [86] represents a growing research focus in LUTS. Metabolomic and transcriptomic data fusion with Mendelian randomization reveals BPH pathological pathways and therapeutic targets. CP/CPPS treatment personalization utilizes predictive frameworks combining omics information with clinical metrics [87].

Limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the bibliometric analysis was based exclusively on English-language articles and reviews indexed in the Web of Science Core Collection. Relevant studies indexed only in Scopus, Embase, PubMed, regional databases, or non-English-language sources may therefore have been missed. This database and language restriction may have influenced publication and citation metrics, collaboration networks, and the rankings of countries, institutions, authors, and journals. Accordingly, the findings should be interpreted as a mapping of the WoSCC-indexed literature rather than an exhaustive representation of global research on natural medicines for LUTS.

Second, the clinical trial analysis relied solely on ClinicalTrials.gov and may not have captured studies registered exclusively in other national or regional registries. In addition, citation-based indicators may favor older publications, whereas recently published studies may be underestimated. Variations in terminology and author or institutional names may also have affected network construction despite data standardization. Finally, publications and trial registrations added after July 23, 2025, were not included. Future studies should integrate multiple bibliographic databases and international trial registries to improve coverage and reproducibility.

Conclusions

This investigation delineates the global research trajectory of natural medicines for LUTS between 2000 and 2025, employing bibliometric and clinical registry analyses. The scholarly emphasis has transitioned from preliminary compound isolation to systematic inquiry into multi-target mechanisms, enabled by advances in network pharmacology and omics technologies. Nevertheless, progress is hindered by a paucity of high-caliber randomized trials with extended follow-up and inadequate standardization in quality assessment. Subsequent research ought to establish uniform assessment frameworks for botanical products and implement extensive multicenter studies. These steps will facilitate the transition of natural remedies from supportive care toward precision therapeutic interventions.

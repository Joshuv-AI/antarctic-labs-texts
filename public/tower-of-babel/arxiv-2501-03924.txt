Schur’s orthogonality relations for the free group Thanks: Thanks to Ch. Pittet, A. Boyer and A. Pinochet Lobos for their great help and generosity
Guillaume Delord

Abstract
. We prove an explicit convergence of suitably normalized integrals on balls where the integrand is the product of coefficients of the quasi-regular representation of the finitely generated free group. This result follows from the fact that the quasi-regular representation of the free group is c-tempered in the sens of Kazhdan and Yom Din [ KYD22 , def 2.1] . The convergence can be summed up by the formula: lim n → ∞ ​ 1 n 3 ​ ∫ B n ⟨ π ⁡ ( g ) ​ ψ 1 , ψ 2 ⟩ ​ ⟨ π ⁡ ( g ) ​ ψ 3 , ψ 4 ⟩ ¯ ​ 𝑑 μ ​ ( g ) = 3 ​ q ​ ( q + 1 ) ( q − 1 ) 2 ​ ⟨ ψ 1 , ψ 3 ⟩ ​ ⟨ ψ 2 , ψ 4 ⟩ ¯ \underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\frac{1}{n^{3}}\int_{B_{n}}\langle\pi(g)\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\rangle\overline{\langle\pi(g)\psi_{3},\psi_{4}\rangle}d\mu(g)=\frac{3q(q+1)}{(q-1)^{2}}\langle\psi_{1},\psi_{3}\rangle\overline{\langle\psi_{2},\psi_{4}\rangle} where q + 1 q+1 is twice the rank of the free group, B n B_{n} is the ball of radius n n with respect to the canonical word metric and ψ 1 , ψ 2 , ψ 3 , ψ 4 \psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3},\psi_{4} are square summable functions on the boundary of the free group.



1.
Introduction.

Given an irreducible unitary representation of a compact group
\rho:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{V})
, we know (see for instance
[
BBP23
]
) that the Hilbert space
\mathcal{V}
is finite dimensional (say
d:=dim_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{V})
) and, for all
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}
and
v_{4}
in
\mathcal{V}
, we have:

\int_{G}\langle\rho(g)v_{1},v_{2}\rangle\overline{\langle\rho(g)v_{3},v_{4}\rangle}dg=\frac{1}{d}\langle v_{1},v_{3}\rangle\overline{\langle v_{2},v_{4}\rangle}

where
dg
denotes the normalised Haar measure on
G
.

This formula (Schur’s orthogonality relations) can be seen as a generalisation of the fact that characters of finite groups are unitary in
l^{2}(G,\mathbf{C})
but does not make sense when
G
is not compact and the coefficients not square summable.

In this paper, we compute an equivalent asymptotic formula for the boundary representation of the free group.

In
[
BG16
, First theorem]
, one can find similar results for Gromov hyperbolic groups when the metric is non arithmetic .

1.1.
Settings and notations.

Let
G
be the free group with
N
generators,
X
the Cayley graph associated to right multiplication in
G
and
x_{0}
a base point in
X
. Then
X
is an homogeneous tree of degree
2N=:q+1
equipped with the unique distance
d
which gives the value 1 to any pair of adjacent vertices (see figure
1
for an example where
N=2
and
x_{0}=e
the neutral element of
G
). We denote by
[x,y]
the unique geodesic joining
x
to
y
in
X
,
S_{k}:=S(x_{0},k):=\{x\in X||x|:=d(x_{0},x)=k\}
the sphere centered at
x_{0}
with radius
k\in\mathbf{N}
(for an element
g
of
G
, we write also
g\in S_{k}
whenever
gx_{0}\in S(x_{0},k)
as an element of
X
). We also denote by
B_{n}:=B(x_{0},n)
the ball with radius
n\in\mathbf{N}
.

Figure 1.
Example for
N=2
and
x_{0}=e

A point
\omega
of the boundary
\Omega
can be seen as a direction to infinity or, more precisely, as an infinite geodesics
[x_{0},\omega)
starting at
x_{0}
. We equip
\Omega
with a topological structure declaring its basis of open sets to be all the shadows
\Omega_{x}=\{\omega\in\Omega|[x_{0},x]\subset[x_{0},\omega)\}
, where
x
is in
X
, which makes
\Omega
a compact topological space (for more details, see the introduction of
[
KS92
]
where Kuhn embeds
X\bigsqcup\Omega
in a cartesian product of compact spaces).

We also equip
\Omega
with a Borel probability measure
\nu
which satisfy:

\forall x\in X,\nu(\Omega_{x})=\frac{1}{|S_{|x|}|}=\frac{1}{(q+1)q^{|x|-1}}.

The isometric left action of
G
on
X
clearly extends to a left action on
\Omega
and one can show that
\nu
is quasi-invariant under this action (see
[
BOU95
, Corollary 2.6.3]
where Bourdon shows that
G
acts by conformal maps on
\Omega
).

In particular, for all
g
in
G
,
g_{*}\nu<<\nu
. One can show that
\frac{dg_{*}\nu}{d\nu}(\omega)=P(g^{-1},\omega):=q^{\beta_{\omega}(x_{0},gx_{0})}
where
\beta
is the Busemann function (see also
[
BOU95
]
).

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\Omega,\nu,\mathbf{C})
. We define the unitary representation
\pi:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
by:

\forall g\in G,\psi\in\mathcal{H},\omega\in\Omega,\pi(g)\psi(\omega):=P(g^{-1},\omega)^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi(g^{-1}\omega).

It is well known
\pi
is irreducible (see for example
[
BL17
, thm 1.2]
). In particular, by Schur’s lemma:

(1)

Hom_{G}(\pi,\pi)=\{T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\text{ such that }T\circ\pi(g)=\pi(g)\circ T,\forall g\in G\}=\mathbf{C}Id_{\mathcal{H}}.

2.2.
Computation: The Harish-Chandra function is spherical.

Here we define
\Xi
and show that it is constant on
S_{n}
by computing its value.

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Xi:&G&\to&\mathbf{R}^{+*}\\
&g&\mapsto&\langle\pi(g)1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}\rangle\end{array}

Note that
\Xi(0):=\Xi(e)=1

Now, let
n\geq 1
, and
g
such that
g\in S_{n}
and compute

\displaystyle\Xi(g)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\pi(g)1_{\Omega}\overline{1_{\Omega}}d\nu=\int_{\Omega}P(g^{-1},\omega)^{\frac{1}{2}}d\nu(\omega)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{E_{k}(gx_{0})}q^{\frac{\beta_{\omega}(x_{0},gx_{0})}{2}}d\nu(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}q^{\frac{2k-n}{2}}\nu(E_{k}(gx_{0}))\qquad(\text{by Lemma }\ref{partition of finite geodesic})

\displaystyle=q^{-\frac{n}{2}}\frac{q}{q+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}q^{\frac{2k-n}{2}}\frac{q-1}{(q+1)q^{k}}+q^{\frac{n}{2}}\frac{1}{(q+1)q^{n-1}}\quad(\text{from Equation }\ref{E_k(x) measure})

\displaystyle=\bigg[1+\big(\frac{q-1}{q+1}\big)n\bigg]q^{-\frac{n}{2}}.

So,
\Xi
is spherical and we can define:

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\Xi:&\mathbf{N}&\to&\mathbf{R}^{+*}&\\
&n&\mapsto&\Xi(g)&(\text{where }g\text{ is any element of }S_{n})\end{array}

3.
\pi
is c-tempered.

Here we will prove that
\pi
is c-tempered, in the sense of Kazhdan and Yom Din in section 2 «Notion of c-temperedness»
[
KYD22
, def 2.1]
.

For all subset
L\subset G
and all
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in\mathcal{H}
, one can define the quantity:

M_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(L):=\int_{L}|\langle\pi(g)\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\rangle|^{2}d\mu(g)

In particular, we have

M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(L)=\int_{L}\Xi(g)^{2}d\mu(g)

Using the spherical property of
\Xi
, one computes:

(3)

M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(S_{k})=\frac{q+1}{q}\left(1+\frac{q-1}{q+1}k\right)^{2}\quad(k>0).

and

(4)

\displaystyle M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(B_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(S_{k})

\displaystyle=1+\frac{q+1}{q}\bigg[\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}\left(\frac{q-1}{q+1}\right)^{2}+n(n+1)\frac{q-1}{q+1}+n\bigg]

\displaystyle\underset{n\to\infty}{\sim}\frac{n^{3}}{K}\text{ where }K=\frac{3q(q+1)}{(q-1)^{2}}.

Considering the sequence
\{B_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
of balls in
G
and our unit vector
1_{\Omega}\in\mathcal{H}
. Then if the two following conditions are satisfied:

(5)

\forall\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in\mathcal{H},\quad\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\frac{M_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(B_{n})}{M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(B_{n})}<\infty;

(6)

\forall g,h\in G\text{ and }\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in\mathcal{H},\quad\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\frac{M_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(B_{n}\Delta h^{-1}B_{n}g)}{M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(B_{n})}=0.

One says that
\pi
is c-tempered with Følner sequence
\{B_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
(see
[
KYD22
, def 2.1]
).

Remark 3.1
.

The condition (
6
) is equivalent to the second condition in the definition
[
KYD22
, def 2.1]
because
G
is discret, therefore its compacts are the finite sets.

Lemma 3.2
.

\pi
satisfies the first condition (
5
).

Proof.

By
[
Kuh94
]
, we know that
\pi
is weakly contained in the regular representation
\pi_{reg}
.
In particular, the extensions on
l^{1}(G)
of these representations:

\rho^{ext}(\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}\delta_{g})=\int_{G}a_{g}\rho(g)dg=\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}\rho(g)

(where
\rho\in\{\pi,\pi_{reg}\}
) satisfy:

\forall f\in l^{1}(G),\quad\|\pi^{ext}(f)\|_{op}\leq\|\pi_{reg}^{ext}(f)\|_{op}.

Moreover, by
[
Haa78
]
, we have:

\|\pi_{reg}^{ext}(f)\|_{op}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+1)\|f1_{S_{k}}\|_{2}.

Consider the sequence of
l^{2}(G)
functions
\{f_{k}\}_{k}
defined as

f_{k}(g):=1_{S_{k}}(g)\overline{\langle\pi(g)\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\rangle}\text{ where }\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in\mathcal{H}.

One has, fixing arbitrary unitary
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in\mathcal{H}
:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<M_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(S_{k})=\langle\pi^{ext}(f_{k})\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|\pi^{ext}(f_{k})\|_{op}\|\psi_{1}\|_{2}\|\psi_{2}\|_{2}\leq\|\pi_{reg}^{ext}(f_{k})\|_{op}

\displaystyle\leq(k+1)\|f_{k}\|_{2}.

and since
\|f_{k}\|_{2}=M_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(S_{k})^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we obtain

M_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(S_{k})\leq(k+1)^{2}.

So, by equation (
3
),

\frac{M_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(S_{k})}{M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(S_{k})}\leq\frac{q}{q+1}u_{k}^{2}\quad\left(\text{ for }u_{k}:=\frac{1+k}{1+\frac{q-1}{q+1}k}\right).

But one can easily check that
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbf{N}}
is bounded. So there is a
C\in\mathbf{R}^{+}
(which does not depend on
k
) such that:

(7)

\forall\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in\mathcal{H},\quad M_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(S_{k})\leq CM_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(S_{k}).

Hence,

M_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(B_{n})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}M_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(S_{k})\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n}CM_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(S_{k})=CM_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(B_{n}).

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

\pi
satisfies the second condition (
6
).

Proof.

Let
k:=|g|+|h|

One can easily show that:

B_{n}\Delta h^{-1}B_{n}g\subset B_{n+k}-B_{n-k}

and

\displaystyle M_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(B_{n}\Delta h^{-1}B_{n}g)\leq M_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(B_{n+k}-B_{n-k})=\sum_{j=n-k+1}^{n+k}M_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(S^{j})

\displaystyle\overset{\text{ (by \ref{bound coeff sphere})}}{\leq}C\sum_{j=n-k+1}^{n+k}M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(S^{j})=C\big(M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(B_{n+k})-M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(B_{n-k})\big)

Recalling the computation done in (
4
) which gives:

M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(B_{m})\underset{m\to\infty}{\sim}\frac{m^{3}}{K}

So

\displaystyle\frac{M_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(B_{n}\Delta h^{-1}B_{n}g)}{M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(B_{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(B_{n+k})-M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(B_{n-k})}{M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(B_{n})}\underset{n\to\infty}{\sim}C\frac{(n+k)^{3}-(n-k)^{3}}{n^{3}}.

The left hand side converging to zero since the degree 3 coefficient of the numerator vanishes.
∎

4.
Asymptotic Schur’s orthogonality relations for
\pi
.

Now that we have the conditions (
5
) and (
6
) for our representation, we can detail the proof of proposition 2.3 found in
[
KYD22
]
. Namely, in our case, for all
\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3}
and
\psi_{4}\in\mathcal{H}
:

(8)

\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\frac{\int_{B_{n}}\langle\pi(g)\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\rangle\overline{\langle\pi(g)\psi_{3},\psi_{4}\rangle}d\mu(g)}{M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(B_{n})}=\langle\psi_{1},\psi_{3}\rangle\overline{\langle\psi_{2},\psi_{4}\rangle}.

We denote by
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
the conjugate of our vector space
\mathcal{H}
.
This allows us to see any sesquilinear form of
\mathcal{H}
(like
\langle\pi(g)\cdot,\cdot\rangle,\forall g\in G
) to be a bilinear one on
\mathcal{H}\times\overline{\mathcal{H}}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
B:\mathcal{H}\times\overline{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathbf{C}
be a bounded bilinear form such that

B(\pi(g)\psi_{1},\pi(g)\psi_{3})=B(\psi_{1},\psi_{3})\quad\forall g\in G
and
\psi_{1},\psi_{3}\in\mathcal{H}
.
Then

B\in\mathbf{C}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle.

In other words, there is a constant
\lambda
in
\mathbf{C}
such that
B=\lambda\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.

Proof.

For all
\psi_{1}
in
\mathcal{H}
, Riesz lemma gives us an element
T(\psi_{1})
such that
B(\psi_{1},\cdot)=\langle T(\psi_{1}),\cdot\rangle
.

This defines a map
T:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbf{C}
which is linear and bounded. Indeed:

\displaystyle\|\underbrace{T(\lambda\psi_{1}+\Psi_{1})-\lambda T(\psi_{1})-T(\Psi_{1})}_{=:\psi}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=B(\lambda\psi_{1}+\Psi_{1},\psi)-\lambda B(\psi_{1},\psi)-B(\Psi_{1},\psi)=0.

and,

\|T(\psi_{1})\|\leq\|B\|\|\psi_{1}\|,

Moreover,
T
is an intertwining operator since:

\|\underbrace{(T\circ\pi(g)-\pi(g)\circ T)\psi_{1}}_{=:\psi}\|^{2}=B(\pi(g)\psi_{1},\psi)-B(\psi_{1},\pi(g^{-1})\psi)=0.

Hence, by irreducibility of
\pi
and the application of Schur’s Lemma mentioned in (
1
) implies that
T\in\mathbf{C}Id_{\mathcal{H}}
and
B=\langle T(\cdot),\cdot\rangle\in\mathbf{C}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

If
D:\overline{\mathcal{H}}\times\mathcal{H}\to\mathbf{C}
is a bounded bilinear form such that

\forall g\in G
and
\psi_{2},\psi_{4}\in\mathcal{H},\quad D(\pi(g)\psi_{2},\pi(g)\psi_{4})=B(\psi_{2},\psi_{4})
.

Then, composing it with the flip operator
F
which swap the coordinates,
we obtain that
B:=D\circ F
satisfies the conditions of the previous lemma (
4.1
). So

D\circ F\in\mathbf{C}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, that is to say
D\in\mathbf{C}\overline{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}
.

One last simple lemma ( about convergence in
\mathbf{C}
) before proving the equation (
8
) mentioned at the beginning of the section:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbf{N}}\in\mathbf{C}^{\mathbf{N}}
and
l\in\mathbf{C}
. Then

\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}u_{n}=l\text{ is equivalent to the following condition:}

\displaystyle\text{For all subsequences }(u_{\alpha(n)})_{n\in\mathbf{N}},\text{ there is a further subsequence }(u_{\alpha\circ\gamma(n)})_{n\in\mathbf{N}}

\displaystyle\text{ such that }\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}u_{\alpha\circ\gamma(n)}=l.

Theorem 4.4
.

Given any
\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3}
and
\psi_{4}\in\mathcal{H}
:

\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\frac{\int_{B_{n}}\langle\pi(g)\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\rangle\overline{\langle\pi(g)\psi_{3},\psi_{4}\rangle}d\mu(g)}{M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(B_{n})}=\langle\psi_{1},\psi_{3}\rangle\overline{\langle\psi_{2},\psi_{4}\rangle}.

Proof.

Define
\forall n\in\mathbf{N}
:

S_{n}:\mathcal{H}\times\overline{\mathcal{H}}\to L^{2}(G,\mu),\text{ by}

S_{n}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})(g):=1_{B_{n}}\frac{\langle\pi(g)\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\rangle}{M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(B_{n})^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Then
S_{n}
is clearly bilinear and, since
\pi
is c-tempered, we have by the first condition of c-temperedness (
5
),

\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\int_{\omega}|S_{n}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})(g)|^{2}d\mu(g)=\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\frac{M_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(B_{n})}{M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(B_{n})}<\infty.

This shows that
(S_{n}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2}))_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
bounded in
L^{2}(G)
and,
by the Banach-Steinhaus theorem,

(9)

\exists C\in\mathbf{R}^{+}\text{ such that }\|S_{n}\|^{2}\leq C.

Now,
\forall n\in\mathbf{N}
, one can define the quadrilinear form

\displaystyle Q_{n}:\mathcal{H}\times\overline{\mathcal{H}}\times\overline{\mathcal{H}}\times\mathcal{H}\to\mathbf{C}\text{ as:}

\displaystyle Q_{n}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3},\psi_{4}):=\langle S_{n}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2}),S_{n}(\psi_{3},\psi_{4})\rangle.

(Q_{n})_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
is also uniformly bounded, since, using Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and equation (
9
), we see, for unitary
\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3}
and
\psi_{4}
:

\|Q_{n}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3},\psi_{4})\|\leq\|S_{n}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})\|\|S_{n}(\psi_{3},\psi_{4})\|\leq C.

Next we will show that,
\forall g,h\in G
, we have:

(10)

\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\bigg(Q_{n}\big(\pi(g)\psi_{1},\pi(h)\psi_{2},\pi(g)\psi_{3},\pi(h)\psi_{4}\big)-Q_{n}\big(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3},\psi_{4}\big)\bigg)=0.

Indeed, one can show that:

\displaystyle\left|Q_{n}\big(\pi(g)\psi_{1},\pi(h)\psi_{2},\pi(g)\psi_{3},\pi(h)\psi_{4}\big)-Q_{n}\big(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3},\psi_{4}\big)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\frac{M_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(B_{n}\Delta h^{-1}B_{n}g)}{M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(B_{n})}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\frac{M_{\psi_{3},\psi_{4}}(B_{n}\Delta h^{-1}B_{n}g)}{M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(B_{n})}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

which tends to
0
when
n
goes to infinity since
\pi
is c-tempered (see condition 2 of c-temperedness (
6
)).

Now, One can consider any subsequence
(Q_{\alpha(n)})_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
and an application of the Banach-Alaoglu theorem in
\overline{B_{\|\|}(0,C)}
gives us a further subsequence
(Q_{\alpha\circ\gamma(n)})_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
which converges point-wise to some
Q
in
\overline{B_{\|\|}(0,C)}
:

(11)

\forall\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3},\psi_{4},\quad Q\big(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3},\psi_{4}\big)=\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}Q_{\alpha\circ\gamma(n)}\big(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3},\psi_{4}\big)\text{ in }\mathbf{C}.

Now, by (
10
), we have:

\displaystyle Q\big(\pi(g)\psi_{1},\pi(h)\psi_{2},\pi(g)\psi_{3},\pi(h)\psi_{4}\big)=Q\big(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3},\psi_{4}\big)

\displaystyle\text{and }Q\text{ is also quadrilinear.}

In particular, if we fix
(\psi_{2},\psi_{4})\in\overline{\mathcal{H}}\times\mathcal{H}
and consider the map

Q(\cdot,\psi_{2},\cdot,\psi_{4})\text{ (which satisfies the conditions of lemma \ref{bilinear are multiple})}.

We know that,
\exists\lambda_{\psi_{2},\psi_{4}}\in\mathbf{C}
such that:

Q(\cdot,\psi_{2},\cdot,\psi_{4})=\lambda_{\psi_{2},\psi_{4}}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle.

Similarly, fixing
(\psi_{1},\psi_{3})\in\mathcal{H}\times\overline{\mathcal{H}}
and by the remark
4.2
following the mentioned lemma, we have
\exists\lambda^{\psi_{1},\psi_{3}}\in\mathbf{C}
such that:

Q(\psi_{1},\cdot,\psi_{3},\cdot)=\lambda^{\psi_{1},\psi_{3}}\overline{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}.

Next, using the definition of these coefficient one calculate
\lambda_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}=1
,
\lambda^{\psi_{1},\psi_{3}}=\langle\psi_{1},\psi_{3}\rangle
and

Q(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3},\psi_{4})=\lambda^{\psi_{1},\psi_{3}}\overline{\langle\psi_{2},\psi_{4}\rangle}=\langle\psi_{1},\psi_{3}\rangle\overline{\langle\psi_{2},\psi_{4}\rangle}
.

To finish the proof, Let
\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3},\psi_{4}\in\mathcal{H}
and define:

u_{n}:=\frac{\int_{B_{n}}\langle\pi(g)\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\rangle\overline{\langle\pi(g)\psi_{3},\psi_{4}\rangle}d\mu(g)}{M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(B_{n})}

and consider any subsequence
(u_{\alpha(n)})_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
.

Since
u_{\alpha(n)}=Q_{\alpha(n)}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3},\psi_{4})
, has further subsequence converging to
\langle\psi_{1},\psi_{3}\rangle\overline{\langle\psi_{2},\psi_{4}\rangle}
, we have by the lemma (
4.3
),
\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}u_{n}=\langle\psi_{1},\psi_{3}\rangle\overline{\langle\psi_{2},\psi_{4}\rangle}
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

A unitary representation which satisfies these orthogonality relations has to be irreducible.

So, using the equation result (
8
) of our last theorem, and the computation of
M_{1_{\Omega},1_{\Omega}}(B_{n})
we did in (
4
), we obtain the concrete result mentioned at the beginning of this paper:

\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\frac{1}{n^{3}}\int_{B_{n}}\langle\pi(g)\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\rangle\overline{\langle\pi(g)\psi_{3},\psi_{4}\rangle}d\mu(g)=\frac{3q(q+1)}{(q-1)^{2}}\langle\psi_{1},\psi_{3}\rangle\overline{\langle\psi_{2},\psi_{4}\rangle}.

Abstract
This paper revisits the slope consistency of QMLE for binary choice models. Ruud (1983, Econometrica ) introduced a set of conditions under which QMLE may yield a constant multiple of the slope coefficient of binary choice models asymptotically. However, he did not fully establish slope consistency of QMLE, which requires the existence of a positive multiple of slope coefficient identified as an interior maximizer of the population QMLE likelihood function over an appropriately restricted parameter space. We fill this gap by providing a formal proof for slope consistency under the same set of conditions for any binary choice model identified as in Horowitz (1992, Econometrica ). Our result implies that the logistic regression, which is used extensively in machine learning to analyze binary outcomes associated with a large number of covariates, yields a consistent estimate for the slope coefficient of binary choice models under suitable conditions.

Introduction

Ruud 1983
provided conditions under which the quasi-maximum likelihood estimator (QMLE) for binary choice models may asymptotically yield a slope coefficient that is a constant multiple of the true slope coefficient. He, however, did not fully establish the slope consistency of QMLE, which requires the existence of a positive multiple of slope coefficient identified as an interior maximizer of the population QMLE likelihood function over an appropriately restricted parameter space. He showed neither that a solution to the first order conditions for the maximizer of the likelihood function exists within the restricted parameter space, nor that the multiplicative constant is strictly positive. There may be no interior solution over the restricted parameter set. The multiplicative constant may also be zero or even negative, which could lead to incorrect conclusions of no effect or a reverse effect, respectively. In this paper, we fill this gap by providing a formal proof of slope consistency under the same set of conditions for any binary choice model identified as in
Horowitz 1992
or
Manski 1975
;
Manski 1985
.

We consider a binary outcome
Y
given by the sign of
Y^{\ast}=V-U=\alpha_{0}+X^{\prime}\beta_{0}-U
, where
X
is an
m
-dimensional covariate, and
V
and
U
are the scalar-valued systematic component and error term, respectively. For this binary choice model, we assume the
index dependence
given by
\mathcal{L}(U|X)=\mathcal{L}(U|V)
with
\mathcal{L}
denoting distribution and the
linearity in expectation
given by
\mathbb{E}(X|V)=aV+b
with
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Ruud 1983
assumed the linearity in expectation, as well as the independence of
X
and
U
, in which case the index dependence is also trivially satisfied. We establish slope consistency of QMLE with no other restrictive assumptions, except for necessary identification conditions and high level technical conditions such as concavity and differentiability of the likelihood function which are commonly imposed to establish consistency of the maximum likelihood estimator (MLE). The linearity in expectation is certainly restrictive, but it holds, for instance, if
X
has an elliptical distribution. Moreover, we may weight observations on
X
appropriately so that the weighted observations can be regarded as a distribution satisfying the condition. See
Ruud 1986
and
Newey and Ruud 1994
.

For binary outcomes generated by a binary choice model, QMLE is thus a viable option to use. In fact, the logistic regression is used extensively in machine learning to analyze binary outcomes associated with a large number of covariates.
This paper shows that it actually yields a consistent slope coefficient of the underlying binary choice model from which the binary outcomes are generated, provided that it satisfies the required index dependence and linearity-in-expectation conditions, in addition to other standard technical conditions.
Needless to say, for a large dimensional binary choice model, it is crucial to estimate the slope coefficient consistently. Once the slope coefficient is estimated consistently, it is relatively simple and straightforward to consistently estimate the intercept, which is one dimensional. For the rest of the paper, we will present the model and background theory, our main results on slope consistency, and their mathematical proofs.

3
Slope Consistency

Let

V=\alpha_{0}+X^{\prime}\beta_{0}

in what follows. We introduce two important assumptions that are crucial for the slope consistency of QMLE.

Assumption 3.1
.

\mathcal{L}(U|X)=\mathcal{L}(U|V)
.

Assumption
3.1
requires that the error distribution depends on
X
only through the index
V
. Such an

condition for the error distribution is often used in the literature. It’s used in, e.g.,
Klein and Spady 1993

Ichimura 1993
for a class of single index models, among many others. This condition is satisfied trivially if
U
is independent of
X

Assumption 3.2
.

\mathbb{E}(X|V)=aV+b
for some
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Assumption
3.2
requires that the conditional mean of
X
given
V
is a linear function of
V
, which will be referred to simply as the

condition. This condition is certainly restrictive, but it holds, for instance, if

has an elliptical distribution.
We may also weight observations on

appropriately so that the distribution of the weighted observations can be regarded as satisfying this condition.
See, e.g.,
Ruud 1986
and
Newey and Ruud 1994

, and

is a local kernel density estimator of

. Note that the weighted observations

may be regarded approximately as the observations drawn from

having density

. This was proposed by
Ruud 1986
for QMLE, and subsequently used by
Newey and Ruud 1994
for weighted least squares.

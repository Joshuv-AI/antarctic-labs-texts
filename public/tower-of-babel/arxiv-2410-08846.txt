The velocity jump Langevin process and its splitting scheme: long time convergence and numerical accuracy
Nicolaï Gouraud, Lucas Journel, Pierre Monmarché

Abstract
The Langevin dynamics is a diffusion process extensively used, in particular in molecular dynamics simulations, to sample Gibbs measures. Some alternatives based on (piecewise deterministic) kinetic velocity jump processes have gained interest over the last decade. One interest of the latter is the possibility to split forces (at the continuous-time level), reducing the numerical cost for sampling the trajectory. Motivated by this, a numerical scheme based on hybrid dynamics combining velocity jumps and Langevin diffusion, numerically more efficient than their classical Langevin counterparts, has been introduced for computational chemistry in [ 43 ] . The present work is devoted to the numerical analysis of this scheme. Our main results are, first, the exponential ergodicity of the continuous-time velocity jump Langevin process, second, a Talay-Tubaro expansion of the invariant measure of the numerical scheme on the torus, showing in particular that the scheme is of weak order 2 2 in the step-size and, third, a bound on the quadratic risk of the corresponding practical MCMC estimator (possibly with Richardson extrapolation). With respect to previous works on the Langevin diffusion, new difficulties arise from the jump operator, which is non-local.



1
Motivations and overview

Molecular dynamics (MD) is a popular numerical tool to infer macroscopic properties of matter from simulations at the microscopic level. In the framework of statistical physics, thermodynamical quantities are seen as average values of certain functions called observables with respect to a probability measure. The primary objective of MD is to efficiently sample these measures, typically by computing long trajectories of stochastic processes. Here, we focus on the so-called canonical ensemble, in which the number of particles, the volume of the system
and its temperature are fixed.

Let us consider a system of
N
interacting particles. Denote by
x\in\mathcal{X}
their positions and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3N}
their velocities, with
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}^{3N}
or
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{T}^{3N}=\mathbb{R}^{3N}/\mathbb{Z}^{3N}
if periodic boundary conditions are enforced. Let
M=\text{diag}(m_{1}I_{3},\dots,m_{N}I_{3})
be the mass matrix of the particles,
\beta=1/(k_{B}T)
the inverse temperature of the system (where
k_{B}
is the Boltzmann constant),
U:\mathbb{R}^{3N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
the potential energy function encoding the interactions between particles and finally
H(x,v)=U(x)+\frac{1}{2}v^{T}Mv
the Hamiltonian of the system, corresponding to its total energy.
In the canonical ensemble, the statistical distribution of microscopic configurations is described by the Boltzmann-Gibbs measure, defined by

\text{d}\mu(x,v)=\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}^{-1}\exp(-\beta H(x,v))\text{d}x\text{d}v\,,

(1)

where
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6N}}\exp(-\beta H(x,v))\text{d}x\text{d}v
. In most cases, computing macroscopic quantities of the form
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\varphi]
analytically or with deterministic numerical quadrature is out of reach, as they are high-dimensional integrals involving an unknown constant
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
. Instead, they can be approximated by simulating a Markov process
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
that is ergodic with respect to
\mu
, which means that for any
\varphi
(in a certain class of functions), almost surely:

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(X_{s},V_{s})\text{d}s\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6N}}\varphi(x,v)\text{d}\mu(x,v)=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\varphi].

A popular process that has this property (under mild conditions on the potential
U
) is the kinetic Langevin diffusion, defined as the solution of the following SDE:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\text{d}X_{t}&=&V_{t}\text{d}t\\
\text{d}V_{t}&=&-M^{-1}\nabla U(X_{t})\text{d}t-\gamma M^{-1}V_{t}\text{d}t-M^{-1}\sqrt{2\gamma\beta^{-1}}\text{d}B_{t}\,,\end{array}\right.

(2)

where
(B_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is a standard Brownian motion in
\mathbb{R}^{3N}
and
\gamma>0
is a friction parameter. In practice, the Langevin diffusion cannot be simulated exactly, leading to discretization splitting schemes such as BAOAB
[
9
,
28
,
29
,
30
]
where the parts of the dynamics corresponding to the free transport, the forces and the friction/dissipation are simulated separately.

When simulating the dynamics, the most expensive part is the computation of the forces
\nabla U
. A common approach to reduce the cost is to substitute this gradient with a stochastic version, as in the stochastic gradient descent in optimization (ubiquitous in machine learning
[
8
]
, see also examples in MD in
[
23
,
24
,
46
]
), or to treat various parts of the potentials with different time steps (multi-time-step methods), see
[
48
]
. We are interested here in an alternative approach that involves replacing (
2
) with a hybrid model combining a classical Langevin diffusion and a piecewise deterministic Markov process. This hybrid model still samples from
\mu
but can be simulated using a numerical splitting scheme that requires fewer gradient computations per time step.

The idea involves decomposing
\nabla U
as a sum of vector fields
(F_{i})_{0\leqslant i\leqslant K}
for some
K\in\mathbb{N}
, where
F_{0}
denotes the computationally inexpensive component (typically in MD the short-range forces exhibiting fast variation), while the
F_{i}
terms, for
i\geqslant 1
, represent long-range forces, which are computationally more demanding than
F_{0}
(as each atom interacts with all others through these forces, unlike the short-range ones). Then, we define the velocity jump Langevin process
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
as the Markov process which follows the Langevin diffusion process associated with the force
F_{0}
, with the velocity
V_{t}
undergoing additional random jumps at rate
\lambda_{i}(x,v)
following a jump kernel
q_{i}(x,v,\text{d}v^{\prime})
(both defined below in (
5
)), that depend on
F_{i}
in a way that ensures that the equilibrium measure of the process is indeed the Gibbs measure
\mu
.

This process can be simulated with a splitting scheme similar to BAOAB, but with an additional part arising from the velocity jumps. The simulation of the jump times is based on the thinning method, see
[
31
,
32
]
. Suppose that
\lambda_{i}(x,v)\leqslant\overline{\lambda_{i}}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant K
, and denote
\overline{\lambda}=\sum_{i}\overline{\lambda_{i}}
. A direct computation on the generator shows that the jumps can be exactly simulated this way: starting from
(x,v)
,

1.

Draw
\mathcal{E}
a standard exponential random variable, and let
T=\mathcal{E}/\overline{\lambda}
be the next jump time proposal.

2.

Draw
I
in
\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket
such that
\mathbb{P}(I=i)=\overline{\lambda_{i}}/\overline{\lambda}
. Propose a jump of type
I
at time
T
.

3.

Accept the jump with probability
\lambda_{I}(x,v)/\overline{\lambda_{I}}
, in which case the velocity is resampled at time
T
according to
q_{I}(x,v,\text{d}v^{\prime})
, otherwise the velocity at time
T
is simply
v
(there is no jump).

Unlike a traditional numerical scheme for the Langevin diffusion (like BAOAB), where the gradient
\nabla U
is computed at every time step, here we only need to evaluate
F_{i}
when a jump of type
i
is proposed (at step 3 above), which does not occur at every time step for every
i
. However, similarly to BAOAB and contrary to multi-time-step splitting methods, there is still a unique step-size in the discretization and no additional parameters to tune: the frequency at which
F_{i}
is evaluated is random and is adapted to each force through the bound
\overline{\lambda_{i}}
. By contrast, when using piecewise deterministic Markov processes such as the Bouncy Particle (BPS)
[
10
,
36
,
45
]
or Zig-Zag
[
5
]
samplers (which can be seen as particular cases of the velocity jump Langevin diffusion where
F_{0}=0
and
\gamma=0
, so that, between velocity jumps, the deterministic motion is simply the free transport
\dot{x}=v
), thinning is usually employed to sample exactly the full continuous-time process, without any time-discretization. However, when
F_{0}\neq 0
, this is only possible when it is a linear function (as in the so-called boomerang sampler
[
6
]
), which is not the case we are interested in. Besides, note that the use of discretization schemes even for piecewise deterministic Markov processes have recently gained interest
[
3
,
4
,
12
,
44
]
.

The idea of the hybrid velocity jump Langevin approach was first introduced in
[
43
]
, in the particular case where the jump mechanism is the one of the BPS, with a decomposition of the forces based on long-range pairwise interactions. Later, in
[
21
]
, a generalized version was introduced in which the jump mechanism, originally defined in
[
42
]
, can be seen as an interpolation between the BPS and the Hamiltonian dynamics, which is shown to be numerically more stable, while preserving dynamical properties, also of interest in MD.

While
[
21
]
presents an implementation of the Langevin velocity jump approach on a molecular dynamics code and numerical experiments, the present work is devoted to its mathematical study. We prove a second order weak error expansion
à la Talay-Tubaro

[
47
]
of the discretization bias on the invariant measure, see Theorem
2
. The proof is based on classical weak backward error analysis arguments
[
27
,
30
,
34
,
47
]
and, in particular, crucially relies on the ergodicity properties of the continuous-time process, which is of interest by itself and is in our case the topic of Theorem
1
.

By comparison with previous works, the main new difficulties arise from the combination of, first, the degenerate diffusion feature of the kinetic Langevin diffusion, second, the non-local jump operator and, third, the necessity to get pointwise bounds on derivatives of the semi-group. Indeed, the exponential
L^{2}
convergence estimates obtained for kinetic piecewise deterministic Markov processes in recent works
[
1
,
42
]
with the Dolbeault-Mouhot-Schmeiser modified norm
[
15
]
are not sufficient to conduct the rest of the argument, and neither are the results in
V
-norm based on Harris theorem
[
7
,
11
,
13
,
17
,
36
]
or in Wasserstein distances using coupling
[
14
]
. We thus have to work with the
H^{1}
modified norm of Villani
[
49
]
, or rather with its extension to higher order Sobolev spaces
[
25
,
26
,
51
]
. Thus, one of our main contributions is that we successfully implemented this method in a case with a non-local operator (i.e. a non-diffusive Markov process), leading to Theorem
5
(which is an important step in the proof of Theorem
1
). Although the
H^{1}
modified norm method has mostly been used for diffusion processes, it has already been successfully applied to non-diffusive dynamics in
[
14
,
20
,
35
,
38
]
; however, let us emphasize that, contrary to our case, in all these works the dynamics is a contraction of the Wasserstein 2 distance, from which the exponential decay of the
H^{1}
norm is clear, and in fact the computations to prove the decay of the Wasserstein distance and of the gradient part of the
H^{1}
norm are essentially the same (as discussed in
[
35
,
39
,
41
]
). In particular, in these cases, to get the decay of the
H^{1}
norm, the dissipation of the
L^{2}
part of the norm is not exploited, which in the general case (i.e. without a Wasserstein 2 contraction) is a crucial feature of Villani’s method (as in Lemma
11
below). Note that this is possible in our case because, although the process undergoes non-local jumps, it also has a diffusive part, from which the
L^{2}
dissipation is bounded below by a first-order term which would be the dissipation of the usual Langevin diffusion (cf. the
carré du champ
operator (
18
)).

A second important difficulty is the design of a suitable Lyapunov function for the velocity jump Langevin diffusion, given its combination of diffusive, non-local and kinetic features (see Propositions
6
for the continuous-time process and Proposition
22
for the numerical scheme). The last important ingredient for establishing our main result is a finite-time numerical analysis of the discretization bias, Theorem
26
, whose proof is close to the proof of Theorem 2.6 of the recent
[
4
]
(which is a similar result for piecewise deterministic samplers).

This work is organized as follows. In Section
2
, the process and its splitting scheme are defined, our main results are stated, and some comments on the method from the applicable angle are given. Section
3
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1
(the long-time convergence of the continuous-time process). The weak error result, Theorem
2
, is proven in Section
4
.

Setting and main results

2.1
Notations

In all the following,
|\cdot|
denotes the Euclidean norm and
\cdot
the standard dot product. In the case of multi-indices
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, we also denote
|\alpha|=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}
. The set of smooth functions with compact support from
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
to
\mathbb{R}
is denoted
\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. For any operators
A
and
B
, we denote
[A,B]=AB-BA
their commutator. In the remainder of this work, we will denote by
C
various constants that may change from line to line.

2.2
Definitions and assumptions

Fix some smooth potential
U\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
where either
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
or
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{T}^{d}
for some
d\geqslant 1
. In the case of particle systems,
d=3N
. For simplicity, in the remainder of this work, we fix
\beta=1
,
M=I_{d}
and consider the following decomposition of the forces. Assuming that
U=U_{0}+U_{1}
with
U_{0},U_{1}
smooth, let

F_{0}=\nabla U_{0},\quad F_{i}=\partial_{i}U_{1}e_{i},\,i\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket\,,

(3)

where
(e_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant d}
stands for the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
In other words, we decompose
\nabla U_{1}
in a similar fashion as in the Zig-Zag process:

\nabla U_{1}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{i}U_{1}(x)e_{i}\,.

Fix a friction parameter
\gamma>0
, a jump parameter
\rho\in[-1,1)
and an activation function
\Psi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}_{+})
. The velocity jump Langevin process is defined as the Markov process on
\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
with infinitesimal generator given by

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{H}+\mathcal{L}_{D}+\mathcal{L}_{J}\,,

(4)

where for any function
h\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}\left(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
:

\begin{array}[]{rcll}\mathcal{L}_{H}h(x,v)&=&v\cdot\nabla_{x}h(x,v)-\nabla U_{0}(x)\cdot\nabla_{v}h(x,v)&\text{ (Hamiltonian dynamics)}\\
&&\\
\mathcal{L}_{D}h(x,v)&=&-\gamma v\cdot\nabla_{v}h(x,v)+\gamma\Delta_{v}h(x,v)&\text{ (friction/dissipation)}\\
&&\\
\mathcal{L}_{J}h(x,v)&=&\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathcal{L}_{J}^{i}h(x,v)&\text{ (velocity jumps)}\end{array}

with

\mathcal{L}_{J}^{i}h(x,v)=\lambda_{i}(x,v)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(h(x,v^{\prime})-h(x,v)\right)q_{i}(x,v,\text{d}v^{\prime}),

where
\lambda_{i},q_{i}
satisfy

\lambda_{i}(x,v)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(x,v^{\prime})q_{i}(x,v,\text{d}v^{\prime})=\frac{2}{1-\rho}\mathbb{E}\left[h\left(x,V^{i}\right)\Psi\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}(x)}{2}\left(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i}\right)\right)\right],

(5)

and where

V_{i}^{i}=\rho v_{i}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\xi\,,\qquad V^{i}_{j}=v_{j}\quad\forall j\neq i,

and
\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
is a one-dimensional standard Gaussian variable. In the definition above,
q_{i}(x,v,\cdot)
is a probability measure for all
(x,v)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, in other words
\lambda_{i}
, for all
i\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket
, is obtained by taking
h=1
in the formula above.

We will work under the following set of assumptions on
U
and
\Psi
.

Assumption 1
.

1.

the Gibbs measure is well defined, i.e.
\int_{\mathcal{X}}e^{-U(x)}\text{d}x<\infty
.

2.

The derivatives of
U_{1}
of all order are bounded.

3.

\nabla^{2}U_{0}
as well as its derivatives of all order are bounded, and there exist
\kappa>0
and a compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{X}
such that for all
x\notin\mathcal{K}

-x\cdot\nabla U_{0}(x)\leqslant-\kappa|x|^{2}.

4.

\Psi
has all its derivatives bounded, and for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

\Psi(s)-\Psi(-s)=s.

(6)

Under Assumption
1
, it is not difficult to see that the process is well-defined, see
[
42
]
. Let
(X_{t},V_{t})
be a Markov process with generator
\mathcal{L}
, and let
(P_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
be the associated semi-group,

P_{t}f(x,v)=\mathbb{E}_{(x,v)}\left[f(X_{t},V_{t})\right],

defined for suitable
f:\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
. Define as well the set of admissible functions:

\mathcal{A}:=\left\{f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})|\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{2d},\,\exists\,C>0,\,0<c<1,\,|\partial^{\alpha}f|\leqslant Ce^{cH}\right\}.

Let us now introduce a splitting scheme for the process. Starting from (
4
), we further split the Hamiltonian part as
\mathcal{L}_{H}=\mathcal{L}_{A}+\mathcal{L}_{B}
where
\mathcal{L}_{A}=v\cdot\nabla_{x}
stands for the free transport and
\mathcal{L}_{B}=-\nabla U_{0}\cdot\nabla_{v}
for the acceleration. Motivated by the Trotter/Strang formula

e^{t\mathcal{L}}=e^{\frac{t}{2}\mathcal{L}_{B}}e^{\frac{t}{2}\mathcal{L}_{J}}e^{\frac{t}{2}\mathcal{L}_{A}}e^{t\mathcal{L}_{O}}e^{\frac{t}{2}\mathcal{L}_{A}}e^{\frac{t}{2}\mathcal{L}_{J}}e^{\frac{t}{2}\mathcal{L}_{B}}+\underset{t\rightarrow 0}{\mathcal{O}}(t^{3})\,,

(7)

given a step-size
\delta>0
, we call BJAOAJB the Markov chain
(\overline{X}_{n},\overline{V}_{n})
on
\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
with transition kernel

Q=e^{\frac{\delta}{2}\mathcal{L}_{B}}e^{\frac{\delta}{2}\mathcal{L}_{J}}e^{\frac{\delta}{2}\mathcal{L}_{A}}e^{\delta\mathcal{L}_{O}}e^{\frac{\delta}{2}\mathcal{L}_{A}}e^{\frac{\delta}{2}\mathcal{L}_{J}}e^{\frac{\delta}{2}\mathcal{L}_{B}}\,.

(8)

From the definition of
\mathcal{L}_{A}
,
\mathcal{L}_{B}
,
\mathcal{L}_{J}
and
\mathcal{L}_{O}
, we have, for
t>0
,

\displaystyle e^{t\mathcal{L}_{B}}f\left(x,v\right)

\displaystyle=f\left(x,v-t\nabla U_{0}(x)\right),

\displaystyle e^{t\mathcal{L}_{A}}f\left(x,v\right)

\displaystyle=f\left(x+tv,v\right),

\displaystyle e^{t\mathcal{L}_{O}}f\left(x,v\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[f(x,e^{-\gamma t}v+\sqrt{1-e^{-2\gamma t}}\xi)\right]\qquad\text{with }\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d}),

\displaystyle e^{t\mathcal{L}_{J}}f\left(x,v\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[f(x,W_{t})\right],

where
(W_{s})_{s\geqslant 0}
is a continuous-time Markov chain on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
initialized at
W_{0}=v
and with jump rate
\lambda(x,\cdot)
and jump kernel
q(x,\cdot,\cdot)
given by

\lambda(x,w)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}(x,w)\,,\qquad\lambda(x,w)q(x,w,\text{d}w^{\prime})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}(x,w)q_{i}\left(x,w,\text{d}w^{\prime}\right)\,.

Hence, one transition of the chain
(\overline{X}_{n},\overline{V}_{n})
is given by the succession of steps BJAOAJB, where:

\displaystyle(B)

\displaystyle:v\leftarrow v-\frac{\delta}{2}\nabla U_{0}(x)

\displaystyle(J)

\displaystyle:v\leftarrow W_{\delta/2}

\displaystyle(A)

\displaystyle:x\leftarrow x+\frac{\delta}{2}v

\displaystyle(O)

\displaystyle:v\leftarrow e^{-\gamma\delta}v+\sqrt{1-e^{-2\gamma\delta}}\xi\,.

2.3
Main results

Our first main result, established in Section
3
, gives point-wise estimates for the exponential long-time convergence of the semi-group, together with all its derivatives.

Theorem 1
.

Under Assumption
1
, for all
f\in\mathcal{A}
and all multi-index
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{2d}
, there exist
C,q>0
,
b\in(0,1)
, such that for all
t>0
,
(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
,

|\partial^{\alpha}(P_{t}f-\mu(f))(x,v)|\leqslant Ce^{-qt}e^{bH(x,v)}.

(9)

Note that although
e^{bH(x,v)}
grows fast as
x
and
v
go to infinity, this term is an optimal prefactor depending on the initial conditions of the process. When
t=0
, the inequality reads
|\partial^{\alpha}f(x,v)-\mu(f)|\leqslant Ce^{bH(x,v)}
, which corresponds to the assumption
f\in\mathcal{A}
, i.e. that
f
and its derivatives grow at most exponentially. In particular, the case
\alpha=0
in (
9
) corresponds to the weak convergence of the process for all test functions in
\mathcal{A}
. In addition, since
b<1
, a crucial property of the right-hand side of (
9
) is that it is
\mu
-integrable, which will be used to control the remainder terms in the expansion of the weak error of the numerical scheme. In addition, ergodicity of the continuous-time process is necessary to deduce the expansion of the invariant measure of the numerical scheme from the finite-time error estimates, by letting
t
go to infinity.

Formally, Equation (
7
) yields that this palindromic form gives rise to a second-order scheme. Our second main result is that, indeed, the discretization bias on the invariant measure is of order
\delta^{2}
. Here the analysis is restricted to the compact torus, since this is the main case of interest in practice in MD and otherwise the construction of a Lyapunov function is more intricate for the numerical scheme than the continuous-time process (as in other cases such as
[
26
]
).

Theorem 2
.

In the case
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{T}^{d}
, under Assumption
1
, there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that for all
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
, the BJAOAJB chain with transition kernel
Q
admits a unique invariant measure
\mu_{\delta}
. Moreover, for all
f\in\mathcal{A}
, there exists
c_{f}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\mu_{\delta}(f)=\mu(f)+c_{f}\delta^{2}+\underset{\delta\rightarrow 0}{\mathcal{O}}(\delta^{3})\,.

The proof of this theorem is the topic of Section
4
.
Along the proof of Theorem
2
, we have to prove that the Markov chain with transition
Q
is ergodic. In fact we prove a more precise statement, namely that the BJAOAJB chain converges to its equilibrium in
V
-norm exponentially fast with a rate which, as should be expected, is linear in the step-size, as we state now. Given
V:\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow[1,\infty)
, for
f:\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and a signed measure
\mu
over
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
we write
\|f\|_{V}=\|f/V\|_{\infty}
and
\|\mu\|_{V}=\sup\{\mu(f),\ \|f\|_{V}\leqslant 1\}
. The next statement is established in Section
4.3
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
b\in(0,1/2)
and
V(x,v)=e^{b|v|^{2}}
for
(x,v)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In the case
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{T}^{d}
, under Assumption
1
, there exist
\delta_{0},\lambda,C>0
such that for all
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}],(x,v)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\|\delta_{(x,v)}Q^{n}-\mu_{\delta}\|_{V}\leqslant Ce^{-\lambda n\delta}V(x,v)\,.

Combining Theorems
2
and
3
, classical arguments then allow to bound the quadratic risk of an MCMC estimator based on the BJAOAJB chain, possibly with a Richardson extrapolation, as detailed in Section
4.5
:

Corollary 4
.

Let
b\in(0,1/4)
and
V(x,v)=e^{b|v|^{2}}
for
(x,v)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In the case
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{T}^{d}
, under Assumption
1
, there exist
\delta_{0}>0
such that for all
f\in\mathcal{A}
with
\|f\|_{V}<\infty
, there exists
C>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and all
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
, given
(X_{n},V_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a BJAOAJB chain,

\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(X_{k})-\mu(f)\right|^{2}\right)\leqslant C\left(\delta^{4}+\frac{1}{(n\delta)^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left(e^{2b|V_{0}|^{2}}\right)\right)\,.

Moreover, if
(\widetilde{X}_{n},\widetilde{V}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is another independent BJAOAJB chain with step-size
\delta/2
,

\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\frac{4}{3n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(\widetilde{X}_{k})-\frac{1}{3n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(X_{k})-\mu(f)\right|^{2}\right)\leqslant C\left(\delta^{6}+\frac{1}{n\delta}\mathbb{E}\left(e^{2b|V_{0}|^{2}}+e^{2b|\widetilde{V}_{0}|^{2}}\right)\right)\,.

Here we state a simple bound but it is easily checked in the proof that in fact the dependency in the initial condition disappears exponentially fast with
n\delta
, i.e. in the bounds above,
\mathbb{E}(e^{2b|V_{0}|^{2}})
can be replaced by
1+e^{-cn\delta}\mathbb{E}(e^{2b|V_{0}|^{2}})
for some
c>0
, and similarly with
\widetilde{V}_{0}
.

2.4
Discussion and applications

Decomposition of the forces.

Following the construction of the jump mechanism presented in
[
42
]
, the velocity jump Langevin process could be defined for any decomposition of
\nabla U_{1}
into arbitrary vector fields
F_{i}
. In the present work, the choice of decomposing
\nabla U_{1}
along the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
was made to avoid singular terms of the form
\nabla U_{1}/|\nabla U_{1}|
in the hypocoercivity computations. However, each of the
F_{i}
in (
3
) could be further decomposed along the vector
e_{i}
, and all the results presented in the previous sections would still hold, as the proofs would lead to exactly the same computations.

To give an example, consider for instance the following classical functional form

U=U_{0}+\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant d}g(|x_{i}-x_{j}|)\,,

where
U_{0}
gathers short-range, computationally inexpensive terms, to be treated by the Langevin part of the process, and
U_{1}=U-U_{0}
is a sum of pairwise long-range terms (for instance, in molecular dynamics, long-range electrostatic and van der Waals interactions), with
g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Here, the term “long-range” refers to the fact that we suppose that there exists a cutoff radius
r_{c}>0
such that
g(r)=0
for all
r<r_{c}
, which implies that there are no singularities when
x_{i}=x_{j}
for any
i,j
. Following the decomposition (
3
), for all
i\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket
,
F_{i}(x)=\sum_{j\neq i}\partial_{i}g(|x_{i}-x_{j}|)e_{i}
. We can then define
F_{ij}=\partial_{i}g(|x_{i}-x_{j}|)e_{i}
(namely the force of the particle
j
acting on the particle
i
), and associate a jump mechanism to each
F_{ij}
.

This type of decomposition was used in the implementations of the method for MD applications presented in
[
21
,
43
]
, except that the splitting was done at the level of the three-dimensional atoms instead of individual coordinates.

Conditions on
U
.

In general, when using piecewise deterministic samplers such as BPS or Zig-Zag, it is not necessary to assume that
\nabla U
is bounded: jump times can be sampled with thinning using a time-inhomogeneous bound (for instance, if
\nabla^{2}U
is bounded, we can bound
|\nabla U(x+tv)|\leqslant|\nabla U(x)|+t\|\nabla^{2}U\|_{\infty}|v|
). We could do the same here but this is less convenient because, between jumps, the process follows a random Langevin diffusion and thus we cannot have an almost sure bound on
|\nabla U_{1}(x_{t})|
. Regardless, this is not our cases of interest, which are the practical settings of
[
43
,
21
]
in MD simulations. In this situation,
U
has some singular parts (short-range forces) and thus, even if it is possible in principle to use thinning, it is not efficient. The point made in
[
43
,
21
]
is precisely that thinning is efficient for bounded forces, so that in these works
\nabla U_{1}
only gathers long-range interaction forces, which vanish at infinity. This is why, in Assumption
1
, we assume
\nabla U_{1}
to be bounded. In our theoretical study, this condition is used in the proof of Lemma
13
.

Note however that when
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{T}^{d}
, the condition on
U
in Assumption
1
is simply that
U_{0}
and
U_{1}
are smooth (taking
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{T}^{d}
).

Choice of
\Psi
.

The condition (
6
) on
\Psi
is here to ensure the invariance of
\mu
. The usual choice, used in the Zig-Zag or BPS samplers, and in the practical implementations in
[
21
,
43
]
is
\Psi(s)=\max(0,s)
, which indeed satisfies (
6
). However, the computations in Sobolev spaces in our proofs require that the generator has smooth coefficients, which prevents such a
\Psi
. An admissible example is given by
\Psi(s)=a\log(\exp(s/a)+1)
for any
a>0
.

Role of
\rho
.

In the jump mechanism, the case
\rho=-1
corresponds to the Bouncy Particle Sampler, namely
\lambda_{i}(x,v)=\Psi(\partial_{i}U_{1}(x)v_{i})
, with deterministic jumps:
q_{i}(x,v,\text{d}v^{\prime})=\delta_{(x,-v_{i})}
. This version, introduced and implemented in
[
43
]
is the most natural one and simplest to implement. Moreover it yields the least amount of jumps (since
\lambda_{i}
grows with
\rho
), and therefore corresponds to the best computational speedup.

However, as explored in
[
21
]
, choosing the BPS as a jump mechanism has also several issues. First, numerical instabilities may arise in MD applications. Then, although the process samples the Gibbs measure (
1
), dynamical properties such as diffusion constants are not well preserved. Indeed, a specificity of MD applications, and contrary to other fields such as Bayesian statistics, is that sampling (
1
) or its position marginal (with density proportional to
e^{-\beta U}
) is not the only objective, and dynamical properties such as transition times or transport coefficients are also quantities of interest. In MD, the Langevin diffusion is in fact a model for the time evolution of molecular systems, and it is usually not possible to replace (
2
) by some other Markov sampler of the Gibbs measure, like the overdamped Langevin diffusion, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo or random walk Metropolis. Additionally, in that case, in (
2
), the friction parameter
\gamma
is fixed instead of chosen by the user.

That being said, the generalized jump mechanism (
5
), first introduced in
[
42
]
as a general class of kinetic jump processes sampling the Gibbs measure (of which the BPS and Zig-Zag process are particular cases), was integrated to the velocity jump Langevin approach in
[
21
]
. An interesting property, proved in
[
42
, Theorem 3.6]
, is that this process can be tuned to be arbitrarily close to the Langevin diffusion in terms of stochastic trajectories: when
\rho\rightarrow 1
, the distribution of the velocity jump Langevin process converges to the Langevin diffusion in the space of
càdlàg
trajectories endowed with the Skorokhod topology. Therefore, the general expression (
5
) can be seen as an interpolation between the BPS and Hamiltonian dynamics associated to
U_{1}
. As shown numerically in
[
21
]
, a choice of
\rho
not too far from
1
makes the velocity jump Langevin process more suitable to estimate the dynamical statistics, and numerically more stable, with, of course, a trade-off between the accuracy of these dynamical properties and the numerical cost of the simulation, as would be the case with any numerical approximation of (
2
). Indeed, as
\rho
grows, there are more jumps, therefore more pairwise interactions to compute.

In addition, note that in the present work, we take
\rho\in[-1,1)
as a constant for simplicity, but it could actually be taken as a function of
x
or depend on
i\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket
.

Simulation of the jumps.

As stated in the introduction, when simulating the jump process with generator
\mathcal{L}_{J}
, jump times can be sampled by using the thinning procedure, provided that a bound on the jump rates
\lambda_{i}
is available. In our case, since
\Psi^{\prime}
and
\nabla U_{1}
are bounded, for each
i\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(x,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{1-\rho}\mathbb{E}\left[\Psi\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}(x))}{2}(v_{i}-V_{i}^{i})\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{1-\rho}(2\Psi(0)+||\Psi^{\prime}||_{\infty}||\partial_{i}U_{1}||_{\infty}\mathbb{E}[|v_{i}-V_{i}^{i}|])

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{1-\rho}\left(2\Psi(0)+||\Psi^{\prime}||_{\infty}||\partial_{i}U_{1}||_{\infty}\left((1-\rho)|v_{i}|+\sqrt{\frac{2(1-\rho^{2})}{\pi}}\right)\right)=:\overline{\lambda_{i}}(|v_{i}|).

Note that since this bound depends on
|v_{i}|
, the value of
\overline{\lambda_{i}}
changes after each jump, except if
\rho=-1
, where
v_{i}
becomes
-v_{i}
after a jump of type
i
. In this case, using the properties of exponential distributions, the simulation of
\mathcal{L}_{J}
during a time
\delta
using thinning is simpler: starting from
(x,v)
,

1.

Draw
M\sim\text{Poisson}(\delta\sum_{i}\overline{\lambda_{i}}(|v_{i}|))
, the total number of jump proposals.

2.

For each
m\in\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket
, draw
I_{m}\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket
such that
\mathbb{P}(I_{m}=i)=\overline{\lambda_{i}}/\sum_{j=1}^{d}\overline{\lambda_{j}}
and with probability
\lambda_{I_{m}}/\overline{\lambda I_{m}}
, change the sign of
v_{I_{m}}
.

If
\rho>-1
, when a jump of type
i
occurs,
v_{i}
becomes

\rho v_{i}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\tilde{\xi}\,,

where
\tilde{\xi}
is a random variable with density proportional to

f_{(x,v)}(y)=\Psi\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}(x)}{2}\left((1-\rho)v_{i}-\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}y)\right)\right)e^{-y^{2}/2}\,.

It is shown in
[
42
, Section 5.3]
, and implemented in
[
21
]
in the context of the velocity jump Langevin method, that this random variable can be simulated exactly with rejection sampling, by using either Gamma, exponential, shifted Rayleigh or Gaussian distributions as proposals, depending on the values of
(x,v)
.

Complexity of the algorithm.

Let us discuss the efficiency of the procedure. In a classical discretization scheme of the Langevin diffusion, all the
d
derivatives of
U_{1}
are computed at each time step, whereas in the BJAOAJB scheme, there are as many computations of terms
\partial_{i}U_{1}
as jump proposals. As we saw, when
\rho=-1
, there are in average
\delta\sum_{i=1}^{d}\overline{\lambda_{i}}(|v_{i}|)
such proposals per time step. At equilibrium, the velocity
v
is distributed according to a standard Gaussian, which implies that on average,

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\overline{\lambda_{i}}]=\Psi(0)+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}||\Psi^{\prime}||_{\infty}||\partial_{i}U_{1}||_{\infty}\,.

Therefore, for
\rho=-1
, we expect the simulation of the velocity jump Langevin using thinning to yield a computational advantage if

\delta\left(\Psi(0)+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}||\Psi^{\prime}||_{\infty}||\nabla U_{1}||_{\infty}\right)<1\,.

(10)

When
\rho
increases, the average number number of jumps increases, and goes to infinity when
\rho\rightarrow 1
, therefore computational advantage is only to be expected if
\rho
is not too close to
1
. We provide some numerical values in the next paragraph in the case of MD simulations.

Application to molecular dynamics.

In MD simulations, the inequality (
10
) is easily satisfied since the commonly used time step is extremely small (
\delta\approx 10^{-15}
seconds), a choice imposed by the fastest vibrational modes of molecular systems. In
[
21
,
43
]
, the part of the interaction potential to be treated by the jump process is constituted of the long-range electrostatic and van der Waals forces, namely

U_{1}(x)=\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant N}\chi(r_{ij})\left(\varepsilon\left[\left(\frac{\sigma}{r_{ij}}\right)^{12}-\left(\frac{\sigma}{r_{ij}}\right)^{6}\right]+\frac{q_{i}q_{j}}{r_{ij}}\right)\,,

where
N
is the number of the atoms in the system,
r_{ij}
is the distance between atoms
i
and
j
,
q_{i}
is the electric charge of the atom
i
,
\sigma
and
\varepsilon
are parameters of the Lennard-Jones potential, and
\chi
is a smooth switching function such that
\chi(r)=0
if
r<r_{c}
, with
r_{c}
a given cutoff radius.

In a box of water molecules with the classical SPC/Fw model, by choosing
r_{c}=7
angstroms as a cutoff, the bound on the derivatives of
U_{1}
is of the order of
10^{-2}
kcal.mol
-1
.Å
-1
, and if we consider
\Psi(s)=\log(\exp(s)+1)
,
\Psi(0)=\log(2)
,
\Psi^{\prime}<1
, which implies that (
10
) is largely satisfied, with
\delta=10^{-3}
ps in standard MD units, the left hand side of (
10
) being of order
10^{-3}
. We refer to
[
43
, Section 4.C]
for further theoretical discussion on the speedup provided by the procedure in the Bouncy particle sampler case, when simulating boxes of water molecules.

When
\rho>-1
, the average number of jumps increases. However, as shown extensively in
[
21
]
on molecular dynamics simulations, a significant speedup is provided, as long as
\rho
is not too close to
1
, for several reasons. First, thanks to the extremely small time step, the average number of jump proposals per time step remains significantly smaller than the total number of pairwise interactions. Second, the jump procedure is easily parallelizable over all atoms, which make it suitable for parallel architectures such as GPUs. In particular, jumps on pairwise interactions scale better than certain many-body interactions such as the Ewald sum appearing the reciprocal electrostatic force (classicaly used to compute long range electrostatic interactions, see for instance
[
19
]
), and replacing a part of this sum by jumps on pairwise direct electrostatic forces yields a better speedup. Finally, by combining the jump procedure with classical multi-time-step methods, the intrinsically random nature of jumps allows to mitigate some well-known resonance issues of multi-time-stepping, which allows to choose a slightly larger time step.
In
[
21
]
, the choice
\rho=0
seems to provide a good compromise between speedup and preservation of dynamical properties, leading for instance to a total acceleration of
19.3\%
in simulation speed compared to a state-of-art multi-time-step discretization of Langevin dynamics for a system of 96000 water molecules.

3
Geometric ergodicity

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1
, which relies on hypocoercive computations
à la Villani
in Sobolev spaces. We only consider the case
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the proof being easily adapted and simpler in the compact periodic case.

3.1
Proof of Theorem
1

In this section, we give the key steps of the proof, postponing the proofs of the main intermediary results to the rest of Section
3
.

Step 1. Sobolev hypocoercivity.
Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
and define the Sobolev space of order
k
by

H^{k}(\mu)=\left\{f:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\to\mathbb{R}\,\text{ measurable},\|f\|_{H^{k}}^{2}:=\sum_{i+j\leqslant k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{j}f|^{2}\text{d}\mu<\infty\right\},

where we denoted

|\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{j}f|^{2}=\sum_{|\alpha_{1}|=i;|\alpha_{2}|=j}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}f|^{2}\,.

We will first establish the exponential convergence of
P_{t}
to
\mu
in
H^{k}
. The velocity jump Langevin process is hypocoercive, in the sense that the generator of this process does not satisfy

\left<f,\mathcal{L}f\right>_{H^{k}}\leqslant-\rho\|f\|_{H^{k}}^{2},\,\forall f\in H^{k},

for any
\rho>0
, but such that there exists a norm equivalent to
\|\cdot\|_{H^{k}}
such that the previous inequality holds. To prove exponential convergence to equilibrium, following
[
25
,
26
,
51
]
, for
f\in H^{k}(\mu)
, we introduce

N_{k}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}f^{2}\text{d}\mu+\sum_{p=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\omega_{i,p}|\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{p-i}f|^{2}+\omega_{p,p}|(\nabla_{x}^{p}-\nabla_{x}^{p-1}\nabla_{v})f|^{2}\right)\text{d}\mu\,,

(11)

where
(\omega_{i,p})_{p\in\mathbb{N},1\leqslant i\leqslant p}
are positive numbers, which is equivalent to
\|f\|_{H^{k}}^{2}
. We refer to
N_{k}
as the modified Sobolev norm (although strictly speaking it is the square of a norm). With computations similar to
[
49
]
(although rather presented in terms of Gamma calculus as in
[
37
]
), and proceeding by induction in
k
, our goal is to design
(\omega_{i,p})_{p\in\mathbb{N},1\leqslant i\leqslant p}
in such a way that, at least informally,

\partial_{t}N_{k}\left(P_{t}f-\mu(f)\right)\leqslant-\rho_{k}N_{k}\left(P_{t}f-\mu(f)\right)\,,

for all
f\in H^{k}
for some
\rho_{k}>0
. To make rigorous this argument, instead of justifying the time differentiation, we will rely on Lumer-Philips theorem
[
50
, Chapter IX, p.250]
, see in Section
3.5
. This leads to the following:

Theorem 5
.

There exist positive
(\omega_{i,p})_{p\in\mathbb{N},1\leqslant i\leqslant p}
and
(\rho_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
such that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
f\in H^{k}(\mu)
and
t\geqslant 0
,

N_{k}(P_{t}f-\mu(f))\leqslant e^{-\rho_{k}t}N_{k}(f-\mu(f))\,.

This is proven below (from Section
3.2
to Section
3.5
).

Thanks to Sobolev embeddings, this already gives a result similar to Theorem
1
except that in the right-hand-side of (
9
),
b=1
. This would not be enough for establishing Theorem
2
since
e^{H}
is not integrable with respect to
\mu
, from which it cannot be a Lyapunov function for
P_{t}
, and then neither for the BJAOAJB chain. Hence, we wouldn’t be able to control the expectation of the discretization bias in the proof of Theorem
2
.

Step 2. Lyapunov function and re-weighted Sobolev norms.
To solve this issue, we will work with a particular weighted Sobolev norms. The weight
V_{b}:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined, for any
a,b>0
, by

V_{b}(x,v)=\exp\left(b\left(U_{0}(x)+\frac{|v^{2}|}{2}-a\frac{x\cdot v}{\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}}\right)\right).

(12)

We show in Section
3.6
that it is a Lyapunov function for
\mathcal{L}^{*}
, the adjoint operator of
\mathcal{L}
in
L^{2}(\mu)
(which is computed in Proposition
9
):

Proposition 6
.

There exist
a>0,b_{0}\in(0,1)
such that for all
b\in(0,b_{0})
, there exist
\eta>0,C>0
such that

\mathcal{L}^{*}V_{b}\leqslant-\eta V_{b}+C\,.

Combining Theorem
5
and Proposition
6
, following computations as in the proof of Theorem
5
with a modified norm similar to (
11
) but with the additional weight
V_{b}
, we end up with the following:

Proposition 7
.

For all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{2d}
and all
f\in\mathcal{A}
, there exist
b\in(0,1),\eta>0,C>0
such that for all
t\geqslant 0
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\partial^{\alpha}(P_{t}f-\mu(f))|^{2}V_{b}\text{d}\mu\leqslant Ce^{-\eta t}\,.

The proof is postponed to Section
3.6
.

Step 3. Conclusion.
Using Step 1 and 2, we prove Theorem
1
:

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{2d}
. Without loss of generality we assume that
\mu(f)=0
. Let
b^{\prime}\in(0,1)
such that the result of Proposition
7
holds for
V_{b^{\prime}}
, i.e.

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\partial^{\nu}P_{t}f|^{2}V_{b^{\prime}}\text{d}\mu\leqslant Ce^{-\eta t}\,,

for all
\nu\leqslant\alpha
, and let
b\in(0,1)
and
\varepsilon>0
such that
b^{\prime}>1-(1-\varepsilon)b
. We then have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\partial^{\alpha}(P_{t}fe^{-bH})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sum_{\nu\leqslant\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\partial^{\nu}P_{t}f|^{2}|\partial^{\alpha-\nu}e^{-bH}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sum_{\nu\leqslant\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\partial^{\nu}P_{t}f|^{2}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)bH}

\displaystyle=C\sum_{\nu\leqslant\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\partial^{\nu}P_{t}f|^{2}e^{(1-(1-\varepsilon)b)H}\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sum_{\nu\leqslant\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\partial^{\nu}P_{t}f|^{2}V_{b^{\prime}}\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leqslant Ce^{-\rho t}\,,

for some
\rho>0
. We conclude with Sobolev embedding.

3.2
Preliminary: Gamma calculus

The proof of Theorem
5

. Define the generalized

(14)

where, for

\Gamma_{i,p}(h)=\Gamma_{L,|\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{p-i}\cdot|^{2}}(h)\quad\text{and}\quad\Gamma_{p,p}(h)=\Gamma_{L,|(\nabla_{x}^{p}-\nabla_{x}^{p-1}\nabla_{v})\cdot|^{2}}(h)\,.

If we could show that for all

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\Gamma(h)\text{d}\mu+\sum_{p=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\omega_{i,p}\Gamma_{i,p}(h)+\omega_{p,p}\Gamma_{p,p}(h)\right)\text{d}\mu\geqslant\rho_{k}N_{k}(h-\mu(h))\,,

for some

[
37

the proposition that allows for the computations of this generalized

(the proof is straightforward).

Proposition 8
.

If there exists
A=(A_{1},\cdots,A_{p}):\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\to(\mathcal{C}^{\infty})^{p}
a linear operator such that
\phi(h)=|Ah|^{2}
, then

\Gamma_{L,\phi}(h)=\Gamma_{L}(Ah)+Ah\cdot[L,A]h,

where
\Gamma_{L}(Ah)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\Gamma_{L}(A_{i}h)

and
[L,A]=\left([L,A_{1}],\cdots,[L,A_{p}]\right)
.

In the remainder of this work, we will denote
\Gamma
for

.
For all

such that

, the scalar product here has to be understood as

\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{j}h\cdot\nabla_{x}^{i^{\prime}}\nabla_{v}^{j^{\prime}}h:=\sum_{|\alpha_{1}|=i}\sum_{|\alpha_{2}|=j^{\prime}}\sum_{|\nu|=i^{\prime}-i}(\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}+\nu}h)(\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}+\nu}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}h)\,.

In order to justify the discussion above, we will start by proving that the Gibbs measure is indeed invariant for the velocity jump Langevin process in Section

will treat the case

which is the initialization of the induction argument. Section

will present the proof of convergence in

3.3
Invariance of
\mu

The use of the velocity jump process for the sampling of
\mu
, as well as the use of hypocoercivity method rely on the fact that
\mu
is indeed an invariant measure of velocity jump Langevin process. Let us prove this property by computing the adjoint of
\mathcal{L}
in
L^{2}(\mu)
, denoted as
\mathcal{L}^{*}
.

Proposition 9
.

Under Assumption
1
, for all
h\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
:

\mathcal{L}^{*}h=-v\cdot\nabla_{x}h+\nabla U_{0}\cdot\nabla_{v}h-\gamma v\cdot\nabla_{v}h+\gamma\Delta_{v}h+\frac{2}{1-\rho}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}\left[(h(\cdot,V^{i})-h)\Psi\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(V_{i}^{i}-v_{i})\right)\right]\,.

Proof.

Let
g,h\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. By integrating by parts, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}g\mathcal{L}_{H}h\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}g(v\cdot\nabla_{x}h-\nabla U_{0}\cdot\nabla_{v}h)e^{-U(x)}e^{-|v|^{2}/2}\text{d}x\text{d}v

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}hv\cdot\nabla_{x}(ge^{-U(x)})e^{-|v|^{2}/2}\text{d}x\text{d}v+\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}h\nabla_{v}\cdot(ge^{-|v|^{2}/2}\nabla U_{0}(x))e^{-U(x)}\text{d}x\text{d}v

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}hv\cdot\nabla_{x}g\text{d}\mu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}ghv\cdot\nabla U(x)\text{d}\mu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}h\nabla U_{0}(x)\cdot\nabla_{v}g\text{d}\mu-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}ghv\cdot\nabla U_{0}(x)\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}h(-v\cdot\nabla_{x}g+\nabla U_{0}\cdot\nabla_{v}g)\text{d}\mu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}ghv\cdot\nabla U_{1}\text{d}\mu\,.

We also have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}g\mathcal{L}_{D}h\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}g(-\gamma v\cdot\nabla_{v}h+\gamma\Delta_{v}h)e^{-U(x)}e^{-|v|^{2}/2}\text{d}x\text{d}v

\displaystyle=\gamma\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}^{-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}g\nabla_{v}(e^{-|v|^{2}/2})\cdot\nabla_{v}he^{-U(x)}\text{d}x\text{d}v+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}h\Delta_{v}(ge^{-|v|^{2}/2})e^{-U(x)}\text{d}x\text{d}v\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}^{-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nabla_{v}(ge^{-|v|^{2}/2})\cdot\nabla_{v}he^{-U(x)}\text{d}x\text{d}v-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nabla_{v}g\cdot\nabla_{v}he^{-U(x)}e^{-|v|^{2}/2}\text{d}x\text{d}y\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\gamma\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}h\Delta_{v}(ge^{-|v|^{2}/2})e^{-U(x)}\text{d}x\text{d}v

\displaystyle=-\gamma\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nabla_{v}g\cdot\nabla_{v}h\text{d}\mu=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}h\mathcal{L}_{D}g\text{d}\mu\,.

For the jump part, denote
k_{0}(x,v,\cdot)
the law of the random variable
V^{i}
. Then
k_{0}
is reversible with respect to the standard Gaussian measure, in the sense that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

k_{0}(x,v,\text{d}v^{\prime})e^{-|v|^{2}/2}\text{d}v=k_{0}(x,v^{\prime},\text{d}v)e^{-|v^{\prime}|^{2}/2}\text{d}v^{\prime}.

(15)

Indeed, since
V_{j}^{i}=v_{j}
for
j\neq i
and

V_{i}^{i}=\rho v_{i}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\xi\,,

the coordinates of
V^{i}
are independent and each transition
v_{j}\mapsto V_{j}^{i}
is reversible for the standard one-dimensional Gaussian.
Using this reversibility, and the fact that
\Psi(s)-\Psi(-s)=s
, we get that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}g\mathcal{L}_{J}h\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{1-\rho}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}g\mathbb{E}\left[(h(x,V^{i})-h(x,v))\Psi\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}(x)}{2}(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})\right)\right]\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{1-\rho}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}h\mathbb{E}\left[(g(x,V^{i})-g)\Psi\left(-\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}(x)}{2}(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{2}{1-\rho}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}gh\mathbb{E}\left[\Psi\left(-\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}(x)}{2}(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})\right)-\Psi\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}(x)}{2}(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})\right)\right]\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{1-\rho}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}h\mathbb{E}\left[(g(x,V^{i})-g)\Psi\left(-\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{1-\rho}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}gh\partial_{i}U_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})\right]\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{1-\rho}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}h\mathbb{E}\left[(g(x,V^{i})-g)\Psi\left(-\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})\right)\right]-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}ghv\cdot\nabla U_{1}\text{d}\mu.

Therefore, by summing the three terms, we get the result.
∎

3.4
Exponential decay in

In this section, we prove convergence of the process in

, which is the initialization step of the induction argument to prove Theorem

. The modified Sobolev norm here reads

N_{1}(h)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}h^{2}\text{d}\mu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\omega_{0,1}|\nabla_{v}h|^{2}+\omega_{1,1}|(\nabla_{x}-\nabla_{v})h|^{2}\text{d}\mu,

and Equation (
14

\partial_{t}N_{1}(P_{t}f-\mu(f))=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\Gamma(P_{t}f)\text{d}\mu-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\omega_{0,1}\Gamma_{0,1}(P_{t}f)+\omega_{1,1}\Gamma_{1,1}(P_{t}f)\right)\text{d}\mu\,.

The goal of this section is to prove the following which, in view of (
14

H^{1}
as it bounds the norm
N_{1}
in terms of its dissipation along the semi-group.

Lemma 11
.

Under Assumption
1
, there exist
\omega_{0,1},\omega_{1,1},\rho_{1}
such that for all
h\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
satisfying
\mu(h)=0
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\Gamma(h)\text{d}\mu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\omega_{0,1}\Gamma_{0,1}(h)+\omega_{1,1}\Gamma_{1,1}(h)\right)\text{d}\mu\geqslant\rho_{1}\left(N_{1}(h)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\nabla_{x}\nabla_{v}h|^{2}+|\nabla_{v}^{2}h|^{2}\text{d}\mu\right)\,.

The terms of order
2
in the right hand side are present to compensate future terms in the induction, but are not required for the the convergence in
H^{1}(\mu)
. This convergence relies on the so-called Poincare inequality.

Proposition 12
.

Under Assumption
1
, the Gibbs measure (
1
) satisfies a Poincaré inequality: there exists
C_{P}>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(h-\mu(h)\right)^{2}\text{d}\mu\leqslant C_{P}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\nabla h|^{2}\text{d}\mu\,.

(16)

for all
h\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.

Proof.

Proof of such inequality can be found in
[
2
]
.
∎

In order to apply Proposition
8
, we need to express the commutators. For the Hamiltonian part we get:

[\mathcal{L}_{H},\nabla_{x}]=\nabla^{2}U_{0}\nabla_{v},\qquad[\mathcal{L}_{H},\nabla_{v}]=-\nabla_{x}.

For the diffusion part we get:

[\mathcal{L}_{D},\nabla_{x}]=0,\qquad[\mathcal{L}_{D},\nabla_{v}]=\gamma\nabla_{v}.

Regarding the jump part, we have the following.

Lemma 13
.

Under Assumption

To prove Lemma
13
, we need two intermediate results:

Lemma 14
.

For any function
h\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and any

,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v|^{2}h^{2}(x,v)e^{-|v|^{2}/2}\text{d}v\leqslant 2d\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h^{2}(x,v)e^{-|v|^{2}/2}\text{d}v+4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{v}h(x,v)|^{2}e^{-|v|^{2}/2}\text{d}v\,.

Proof.

This is a particular case of the Lemma A.24 of
[
49
]
. Notice that
ve^{-|v|^{2}/2}=-\nabla e^{-|v|^{2}/2}
. Hence, an integration by parts and Young’s inequality yield, for any

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v|^{2}h^{2}(x,v)e^{-|v|^{2}/2}\text{d}v&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h^{2}(x,v)v\cdot\nabla e^{-|v|^{2}/2}\text{d}v\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\cdot(h^{2}(x,v)v)e^{-|v|^{2}/2}\text{d}v\\
&=d\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h^{2}(x,v)e^{-|v|^{2}/2}\text{d}v+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}2h(x,v)\nabla h(x,v)\cdot ve^{-|v|^{2}/2}\text{d}v\\
&\leqslant d\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h^{2}(x,v)e^{-|v|^{2}/2}\text{d}v+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}2|\nabla h(x,v)|^{2}e^{-|v|^{2}/2}\text{d}v+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v|^{2}h^{2}(x,v)e^{-|v|^{2}/2}\text{d}v\,,\end{split}

and thus the result.

Proposition 15

(Poincaré for Gaussians)
.

For any function

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(h(x)-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(y)e^{-|y|^{2}/2}\text{d}y\right)^{2}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\text{d}x\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla h(x)|^{2}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\text{d}x\,.

Proof of Lemma

.

We have

[\nabla_{x},\mathcal{L}_{J}]h=\sum_{i=1}^{d}[\nabla_{x},\mathcal{L}_{J}^{i}]h=\frac{1}{1-\rho}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}\left[(v_{i}-V_{i}^{i})(h(x,V^{i})-h)\Psi^{\prime}\left(\frac{\partial U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})\right)\right]\nabla\partial_{i}U_{1}\,,

and

[\nabla_{v},\mathcal{L}_{J}]h=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}\left[\partial_{i}U_{1}(h(x,V^{i})-h)\Psi^{\prime}\left(\frac{\partial U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})\right)-2\partial_{v_{i}}h(x,V^{i})\Psi\left(\frac{\partial U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})\right)\right]e_{i}\,.

Using the fact that
\Psi^{\prime}
,
\nabla U_{1}
and
\nabla^{2}U_{1}
are bounded, there exists a constant

such that

|[\nabla_{x},\mathcal{L}_{J}]h|^{2}\leqslant C\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}[(v_{i}-V_{i}^{i})^{2}(h(x,V^{i})-h)^{2}]\,,

and

|[\nabla_{v},\mathcal{L}_{J}]h|^{2}\leqslant C\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}[(h(x,V^{i})-h)^{2}+(\partial_{v_{i}}h(x,V^{i}))^{2}(1+(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})^{2})]\,.

Recall from (

is reversible with respect to the standard Gaussian measure. Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\mathbb{E}\left[(1+(v_{i}-V_{i}^{i})^{2})\left(\partial_{v_{i}}h(x,V^{i})\right)^{2}\right]\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}(\partial_{v_{i}}h)^{2}\left(1+\mathbb{E}\left[(v_{i}-V_{i}^{i})^{2}\right]\right)\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leqslant 4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left((\partial_{v_{i}}h)^{2}+(\partial_{v_{i}}^{2}h)^{2}\right)\text{d}\mu\,,

where we used Lemma

with

in the last inequality.
By denoting

\Pi_{v_{i}}h(x,v)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(x,v_{1},\dots,v_{i-1},w_{i},v_{i+1},\dots,v_{d})e^{-{w_{i}}^{2}/2}\text{d}w_{i}\,,

we also have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\mathbb{E}\left[(1+(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})^{2})(h(x,V^{i})-h)^{2}\right]\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\mathbb{E}\left[(1+(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})^{2})((h(x,V^{i})-\Pi_{v_{i}}h)^{2}+(h-\Pi_{v_{i}}h)^{2})\right]\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leqslant 4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(1+\mathbb{E}\left[(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})^{2}\right]\right)(h-\Pi_{v_{i}}h)^{2}\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leqslant 4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}(2+v_{i}^{2})(h-\Pi_{v_{i}}h)^{2}\text{d}\mu

where we used again Lemma

with

) can be written

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}(h-\Pi_{v_{i}}h)^{2}\text{d}\mu\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\partial_{v_{i}}h|^{2}\text{d}\mu\,,

and thus

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\mathbb{E}\left[(1+(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})^{2})(h(x,V^{i})-h)^{2}\right]\text{d}\mu\leqslant 32\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}(\partial_{v_{i}}h)^{2}\text{d}\mu\,.

Therefore, there exists
C>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|[\nabla,\mathcal{L}_{J}]h|^{2}\text{d}\mu\leqslant C\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}((\partial_{v_{i}}h)^{2}+|\nabla_{v}\partial_{v_{i}}h|^{2})\text{d}\mu=C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\nabla_{v}h|^{2}\text{d}\mu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\nabla_{v}^{2}h|^{2}\text{d}\mu\right)\,,

which concludes the proof.

3.5
Exponential decay in

Recall that we defined the modified Sobolev norm as

N_{k}(h):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}h^{2}\text{d}\mu+\sum_{p=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\omega_{i,p}|\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{p-i}h|^{2}+\omega_{p,p}|(\nabla_{x}^{p}-\nabla_{x}^{p-1}\nabla_{v})h|^{2}\right)\text{d}\mu\,.

In order to control its derivative (
14
), we prove by induction on

the following lemma, generalizing Lemma
11
.

Proposition 16
.

Under Assumption
1
, there exist
(\omega_{i,p})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant p}
such that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\rho_{k}>0
such that for all
h\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},\mathbb{R})
with

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\Gamma(h)\text{d}\mu+\sum_{p=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\omega_{i,p}\Gamma_{i,p}(h)+\omega_{p,p}\Gamma_{p,p}(h)\right)\text{d}\mu\geqslant\rho_{k}\left(N_{k}(h)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\sum_{i=0}^{k}|\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+1-i}h|^{2}\text{d}\mu\right).

(19)

To prove this lemma, we will need formulas for partial derivatives of products, provided by the Leibniz formula

Proposition 17
.

For all
g,h\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}
and
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,

\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}(gh)=\sum_{\nu_{1}\leq\alpha_{1},\nu_{2}\leq\alpha_{2}}\binom{\alpha_{1}}{\nu_{1}}\binom{\alpha_{2}}{\nu_{2}}(\partial_{x}^{\nu_{1}}\partial_{v}^{\nu_{2}}g)(\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}-\nu_{1}}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}-\nu_{2}}h)

where for
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d})
and
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{d})
,

\binom{\alpha}{\nu}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\binom{\alpha_{i}}{\nu_{i}}\,,

and

\nu\leq\alpha\iff\forall i\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket,\nu_{i}\leq\alpha_{i}.

For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
we will also denote
|\alpha|=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}
.
Let us look at the commutators. As in the
H^{1}
case, we will look separately the parts associated to the Hamiltonian dynamic
\mathcal{L}_{H}
, the diffusion
\mathcal{L}_{D}
and the jump process
\mathcal{L}_{J}
.

Lemma 18

(Hamiltonian commutators)
.

Let
i\in\llbracket 0,k\rrbracket
. There exists
C>0
such that the following holds. If
i<k
, we have

\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+1-i}h\cdot[\mathcal{L}_{H},\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+1-i}]h\geqslant-|\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+1-i}h|^{2}-C\left(|\nabla_{x}^{i+1}\nabla_{v}^{k-i}h|^{2}+\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}|\nabla_{x}^{j}\nabla_{v}^{k+2-i}h|^{2}\right)\,.

In the case
i=k
, for all
\eta>0
, we have

\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}h\cdot[\mathcal{L}_{H},\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}]h\geqslant-\frac{\eta}{8}|(\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v})h|^{2}-C\left(1+\frac{1}{\eta}\right)|\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}h|^{2}-C\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}|\nabla_{x}^{j}\nabla_{v}^{2}h|^{2}\,.

Finally, we have

(\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v})h\cdot[\mathcal{L}_{H},\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}]h\geqslant\frac{1}{2}|(\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v})h|^{2}-C\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k}|\nabla_{x}^{j}\nabla_{v}h|^{2}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}|\nabla_{x}^{j}\nabla_{v}^{2}h|^{2}\right)\,.

Proof.

For all
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
with
|\alpha_{1}|=i
and
|\alpha_{2}|=k+1-i
, where
\alpha_{2}=(\alpha_{2,1},\dots,\alpha_{2,d})
, write

\alpha_{2}^{j}=(\alpha_{2,1},\dots,\alpha_{2,j-1},\alpha_{2,j}-1,\alpha_{2,j+1},\dots,\alpha_{2,d}),

and we have

\begin{split}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}\mathcal{L}_{H}h&=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}\left(v_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}h-\partial_{j}U_{0}\partial_{v_{j}}h\right)\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}(v_{j}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{x_{j}}h)-\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}(\partial_{j}U_{0}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}\partial_{v_{j}}h)\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\sum_{\nu_{2}\leqslant\alpha_{2}}\binom{\alpha_{2}}{\nu_{2}}\partial_{v}^{\nu_{2}}(v_{j})\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}-\nu_{2}}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{x_{j}}h-\sum_{\nu_{1}\leqslant\alpha_{1}}\binom{\alpha_{1}}{\nu_{1}}\partial_{x}^{\nu_{1}}\partial_{j}U_{0}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}-\nu_{1}}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}\partial_{v_{j}}h\right)\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\alpha_{2,j}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}^{j}}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{x_{j}}h+v_{j}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{x_{j}}h-\sum_{\nu_{1}\leqslant\alpha_{1}}\binom{\alpha_{1}}{\nu_{1}}\partial_{x}^{\nu_{1}}\partial_{j}U_{0}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}-\nu_{1}}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}\partial_{v_{j}}h\right)\,.\end{split}

Therefore,

[\mathcal{L}_{H},\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}]h=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\sum_{\nu_{1}\leqslant\alpha_{1};\nu_{1}\neq 0}\binom{\alpha_{1}}{\nu_{1}}\partial_{x}^{\nu_{1}}\partial_{j}U_{0}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}-\nu_{1}}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}\partial_{v_{j}}h-\alpha_{2,j}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}^{j}}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{x_{j}}h\right)\,.

Using the bound on the derivatives of
U_{0}
, this yields that there exists a
C>0
such that

\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+1-i}h\cdot[\mathcal{L}_{H}\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+1-i}]h\geqslant-|\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+1-i}h|^{2}-C\left(|\nabla_{x}^{i+1}\nabla_{v}^{k-i}h|^{2}+\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}|\nabla_{x}^{j}\nabla_{v}^{k+2-i}h|^{2}\right).

If
i=k
, then for any
\eta>0
, using the Young inequality

\begin{split}\sum_{\underset{j\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket}{|\alpha_{1}|=k;|\alpha_{2}|=1}}(\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}h)(\alpha_{2,j}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}^{j}}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{x_{j}}h)&=\sum_{|\alpha_{1}|=k;j\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket}(\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{v_{j}}h)(\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{x_{j}}h)\\
&=\sum_{|\alpha_{1}|=k;j\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket}(\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{v_{j}}h)(\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{x_{j}}-\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{v_{j}}+\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{v_{j}})h\\
&\leqslant\left(1+\frac{2}{\eta}\right)|\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}h|^{2}+\frac{\eta}{8}|(\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v})h|^{2}\,.\end{split}

which again implies that

\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}h\cdot[\mathcal{L}_{H},\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}]h\geqslant-C\left(1+\frac{1}{\eta}\right)|\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}h|^{2}-\frac{\eta}{8}|(\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v})h|^{2}-C\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}|\nabla_{x}^{j}\nabla_{v}^{2}h|^{2}\,.

Similarly, for
\beta
a multi-index such that
|\beta|=k
and
i\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket
,

[\mathcal{L}_{H},\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{x_{i}}-\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{v_{i}}]h=\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{x_{i}}h+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{\nu\leqslant\beta}\binom{\beta}{\nu}\partial^{\nu}\partial_{i,j}U_{0}\partial_{x}^{\beta-\nu}\partial_{v_{j}}h\\
+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{\nu\leqslant\beta;\nu\neq 0}\binom{\beta}{\nu}\partial^{\nu}\partial_{j}U_{0}\partial_{x}^{\beta-\nu}\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{v_{j}}h-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{\nu\leqslant\beta;\nu\neq 0}\binom{\beta}{\nu}(\partial^{\nu}\partial_{j}U_{0})(\partial_{x}^{\beta-\nu}\partial_{v_{i}}\partial_{v_{j}}h)\,,

and thus, by applying Young inequality,

\begin{split}(\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v})h\cdot[\mathcal{L}_{H},\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}]h\geqslant\frac{1}{2}|(\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v})h|^{2}-C\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k}|\nabla_{x}^{j}\nabla_{v}h|^{2}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}|\nabla_{x}^{j}\nabla_{v}^{2}h|^{2}\right)\,,\end{split}

which concludes the proof.
∎

Regarding the diffusion part, we have

Lemma 19
.

For
i\in\llbracket 0,k\rrbracket
,

\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+1-i}h\cdot[\mathcal{L}_{D},\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+1-i}]h=\gamma(k+1-i)|\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+1-i}h|^{2}\,,

and there exists
C>0
such that

(\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v})h\cdot[\mathcal{L}_{D},\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}]h\geqslant-\frac{1}{8}|(\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v})h|^{2}-C|\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}h|^{2}\,.

Proof.

We have, for all
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,

\begin{split}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}\mathcal{L}_{D}h(x,v)&=-\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}(v_{j}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{v_{j}}h)+\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}(\partial_{v_{j}^{2}}^{2}h)\\
&=-\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{d}v_{j}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{v_{j}}h-\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{2,j}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}^{j}}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{v_{j}}h+\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}(\partial_{v_{j}^{2}}^{2}h).\end{split}

Therefore, if
|\alpha_{1}|=i
and
|\alpha_{2}|=k+1-i
, we have

[\mathcal{L}_{D},\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}]h(x,v)=\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{2,j}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}h=\gamma(k+1-i)\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}h\,,

and

\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+1-i}h\cdot[\mathcal{L}_{D},\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+1-i}]h=\gamma(k+1-i)|\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+1-i}h|^{2}.

Similarly, for all
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
and
i\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket
,

\begin{split}(\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{x_{i}}-\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{v_{i}})\mathcal{L}_{D}h(x,v)&=-\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{d}v_{i}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{v_{j}}h+\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{v_{i}}(v_{j}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{v_{j}}h)+\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{v_{j}^{2}}^{2}h-\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{v_{i}}\partial_{v_{j}^{2}}^{2}h\\
&=-\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{d}v_{j}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{v_{j}}h+\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{d}v_{j}\partial_{v_{i}}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{v_{j}}h+\gamma\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{v_{i}}h\\
&\qquad+\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{v_{j}^{2}}^{2}h-\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{v_{i}}\partial_{v_{j}^{2}}^{2}h\,.\end{split}

Therefore,

[\mathcal{L}_{D},\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{x_{i}}-\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{v_{i}}]h(x,v)=\gamma\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{v_{i}}h\,,

and finally, by applying Young inequality, we get

(\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v})h\cdot[\mathcal{L}_{D},\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}]h=\gamma\sum_{|\beta|=k;j\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket}(\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{x_{j}}-\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{v_{j}})h\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{v_{j}}h\\
\geqslant-\frac{1}{8}|(\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v})h|^{2}-C|\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}h|^{2}\,,

for some
C>0
.
∎

Finally, let us look at the jump part.

Lemma 20
.

For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
C>0
such that for
i\in\llbracket 0,k\rrbracket
and
\varepsilon>0
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+1-i}h\cdot[\mathcal{L}_{J},\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+1-i}]h\text{d}\mu\geqslant-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+1-i}h|^{2}\text{d}\mu\\
-C\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\sum_{\underset{l\leqslant k+1-j}{j\leqslant i}}|\nabla_{x}^{j}\nabla_{v}^{l}h|^{2}+|\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+2-i}h|^{2}\right)\text{d}\mu\,.

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}(\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{k}^{k}\nabla_{v}h)\cdot[\mathcal{L}_{J},\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}]h\text{d}\mu\geqslant-\frac{1}{8}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\left(\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}\right)h|^{2}\text{d}\mu\\
-C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\sum_{\underset{j\leqslant k+1-i}{i\leqslant k}}|\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{j}h|^{2}\text{d}\mu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}^{2}h|^{2}\text{d}\mu\right)\,.

To prove this Lemma, we need a generalization of Proposition
14
:

Lemma 21
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. There exists
C>0
such that for any
h\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}v^{2n}h^{2}(v)e^{-v^{2}/2}\text{d}v\leqslant C\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(h^{(k)}(v))^{2}e^{-v^{2}/2}\text{d}v\,.

Proof.

We prove the inequality by induction on
n
. The case
n=1
corresponds to Lemma
14
.
Suppose that the inequality holds for some
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}v^{2n+2}h^{2}(v)e^{-v^{2}/2}\text{d}v

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}v^{2n}(vh(v))^{2}e^{-v^{2}/2}\text{d}v

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}((vh(v))^{(k)})^{2}e^{-v^{2}/2}\text{d}v

\displaystyle=C\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(kh^{(k-1)}+vh^{(k)})^{2}e^{-v^{2}/2}\text{d}v

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sum_{k=0}^{n}2\int_{\mathbb{R}}(k^{2}(h^{(k-1)})^{2}+v^{2}(h^{(k)})^{2})e^{-v^{2}/2}\text{d}v

\displaystyle\leqslant 2Cn^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(h^{(k)}(v))^{2}e^{-v^{2}/2}\text{d}v+4C\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(h^{(k)}(v))^{2}e^{-v^{2}/2}\text{d}v

\displaystyle\qquad+8C\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(h^{(k)})^{2}e^{-v^{2}/2}\text{d}v

\displaystyle\leqslant\widetilde{C}\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(h^{(k)})^{2}e^{-v^{2}/2}\text{d}v\,,

and hence it holds for
n+1
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
20
.

For all
i,j\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket
,

\partial_{v_{j}}(x,V^{i})=(0,e_{j}+(\rho-1)e_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{j=i}),\quad\partial_{x_{j}}(x,V^{i})=(e_{j},0),\quad\partial^{\nu}_{x}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(x,V^{i})=0\quad\forall\,|\nu|+|\alpha|\geqslant 2\,.

As a consequence, for any
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, we have

\partial_{x}^{\nu_{1}}\partial_{v}^{\nu_{2}}(h(x,V^{i}))=\rho^{\nu_{2,i}}(\partial_{x}^{\nu_{1}}\partial_{v}^{\nu_{2}}h)(x,V^{i})\,.

For all
i\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket
,

\partial_{x}^{\nu}\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V_{i}^{i})=\partial^{\nu}\partial_{i}U_{1}\frac{v_{i}-V_{i}^{i}}{2},\quad\partial_{x}^{\nu}\partial_{v_{i}}\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V_{i}^{i})=\frac{1-\rho}{2}\partial^{\nu}\partial_{i}U_{1}\,.

This yields that the only non-vanishing derivatives of
\Psi(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V_{i}^{i}))
are of the type
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v_{i}}^{k}\Psi(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V_{i}^{i}))
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, we have:

\partial_{v_{i}}^{k}\Psi\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V_{i}^{i})\right)=\Psi^{(k)}\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V_{i}^{i})\right)\left(\frac{1-\rho}{2}\right)^{k}(\partial_{i}U_{1})^{k}\,,

and

\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v_{i}}^{k}\Psi\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V_{i}^{i})\right)=\left(\frac{1-\rho}{2}\right)^{k}\sum_{\nu\leqslant\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\nu}\partial_{x}^{\nu}\Psi^{(k)}\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V_{i}^{i})\right)\partial_{x}^{\alpha-\nu}(\partial_{i}U_{1})^{k}.

Therefore, using that
\Psi(s)\leqslant c+|s|
for some
c>0
, and that
\Psi^{\prime}
and
\partial_{i}U_{1}
and their derivatives of all order are bounded, we get that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
there exists
C>0
such that:

\left|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v_{i}^{k}}\Psi\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V_{i}^{i})\right)\right|\leqslant C\left(1+|v_{i}-V_{i}^{i}|^{|\alpha|+k}\right)\,.

Thus, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{2d}
, we have

\displaystyle[\partial^{\alpha},\mathcal{L}_{J}^{i}]h

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{1-\rho}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\partial^{\alpha}\left[(h(x,V^{i})-h)\Psi\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V_{i}^{i})\right)\right]\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[(\partial^{\alpha}h(x,V^{i})-\partial^{\alpha}h)\Psi\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V_{i}^{i})\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{1-\rho}\sum_{\nu<\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\nu}\mathbb{E}[(\partial^{\nu}(h(x,V^{i}))-\partial^{\nu}h)\partial^{\alpha-\nu}\left(\Psi\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V_{i}^{i})\right)\right)]

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{2}{1-\rho}\mathbb{E}\left[(\partial^{\alpha}(h(x,V^{i}))-\partial^{\alpha}h(x,V^{i}))\Psi\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V_{i}^{i})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{1-\rho}\sum_{\nu<\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\nu}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\rho^{\nu_{i+d}}\partial^{\nu}h(x,V^{i})-\partial^{\nu}h\right)\partial^{\alpha-\nu}\left(\Psi\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V_{i}^{i})\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{2}{1-\rho}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\rho^{\alpha_{i+d}}-1\right)\partial^{\alpha}h(x,V^{i})\Psi\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V_{i}^{i})\right)\right]\,.

As a consequence, there exists a
C>0
such that

|[\partial^{\alpha},\mathcal{L}_{J}^{i}]h|^{2}\leqslant C\left(\mathbb{E}[(\partial^{\alpha}h(x,V^{i}))^{2}(1+|v_{i}-V^{i}_{i}|^{2})]+\sum_{\nu<\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[(\partial^{\nu}h(x,V^{i})-\partial^{\nu}h)^{2}(1+|v_{i}-V^{i}_{i}|^{2|\alpha-\nu|})\right]\right)\,.

As in the
H^{1}
case, the integrals with respect to
\mu
of the previous expectations can be bounded using the reversibility of the law of
V^{i}
with respect to the Gaussian measure, as well as Lemma
21
.
For any
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,

\mathbb{E}[|v_{i}-V_{i}^{i}|^{2n}]=\mathbb{E}[|(1-\rho)v_{i}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\xi|^{2n}]\leqslant C(1+|v_{i}|^{2n})\,.

Therefore, using (
15
) and Lemma
21
, for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{2d}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\mathbb{E}[|v_{i}-V^{i}_{i}|^{2n}\partial^{\nu}h(x,V^{i})|^{2}]\text{d}\mu=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}(\partial^{\nu}h)^{2}\mathbb{E}[|v_{i}-V^{i}_{i}|^{2n}]\\
\leqslant C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(1+v_{i}^{2n}\right)|\partial^{\nu}h|^{2}\text{d}\mu\leqslant C\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\partial_{v_{i}}^{k}\partial^{\nu}h|^{2}\text{d}\mu\,.

Putting everything together, we get that for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{2d}
, there is
C>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|[\partial^{\alpha},\mathcal{L}_{J}^{i}]h|^{2}\text{d}\mu\leqslant C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}(\partial_{v_{i}}\partial^{\alpha}h)^{2}\text{d}\mu+\sum_{\nu\leqslant\alpha}\sum_{k=0}^{|\alpha-\nu|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}(\partial_{v_{i}}^{k}\partial^{\nu}h)^{2}\text{d}\mu\right)\,.

For any
i\in\llbracket 0,k\rrbracket
and
\varepsilon>0
, Young inequality yields

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+1-i}h\cdot[\mathcal{L}_{J},\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+1-i}]h\text{d}\mu\geqslant-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+1-i}h|^{2}\text{d}\mu\\
-C\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\sum_{\underset{l\leqslant k+1-j}{j\leqslant i}}|\nabla_{x}^{j}\nabla_{v}^{l}h|^{2}+|\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+2-i}h|^{2}\right)\text{d}\mu\,.

To treat the term with derivative
\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{k}^{k}\nabla_{v}
, write for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}

\begin{split}[\partial_{x}^{\alpha},\mathcal{L}_{J}^{i}]h&=\frac{2}{1-\rho}\sum_{\nu<\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\nu}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\partial_{x}^{\nu}h(x,V^{i})-\partial_{x}^{\nu}h\right)\partial_{x}^{\alpha-\nu}\left(\Psi\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V_{i}^{i})\right)\right)\right]\,,\end{split}

so that no derivatives of order
k+2
appear, nor derivatives of the form
\nabla_{x}^{k+1}
, and we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|[\partial_{x}^{\alpha},\mathcal{L}_{J}^{i}]h|^{2}\text{d}\mu\leqslant C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\sum_{\nu<\alpha}\sum_{k=0}^{|\alpha-\nu|}(\partial_{v_{i}}^{k}\partial_{x}^{\nu}h)^{2}\text{d}\mu.

Hence, the only derivatives of order
k+2
that appear in
[\mathcal{L}_{J},\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}]h
come from the term
[\mathcal{L}_{J},\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}]h
that we treated above. Using Young inequality, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}(\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}h)\cdot[\mathcal{L}_{J},\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}]h\text{d}\mu\\
\geqslant-\frac{1}{8}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\nabla_{x}^{k+1}-\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}h|^{2}\text{d}\mu-C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\sum_{\underset{j\leqslant k+1-i}{i\leqslant k}}|\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{j}h|^{2}\text{d}\mu-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}^{2}h|^{2}\text{d}\mu\right)\,,

which concludes the proof.
∎

Combining all the commutator terms, we get that for
i\in\llbracket 0,k-1\rrbracket
there exists
C>0
such that for all
\varepsilon>0
small enough,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+1-i}h\cdot[\mathcal{L},\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+1-i}]h\text{d}\mu\\
\geqslant-\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+1-i}h|^{2}\text{d}\mu-C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\nabla_{x}^{i+1}\nabla_{v}^{k-i}h|^{2}\text{d}\mu-C\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\nabla_{x}^{j}\nabla_{v}^{k+2-i}h|^{2}\text{d}\mu\\
-C\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\sum_{\underset{l\leqslant k+1-j}{j\leqslant i}}|\nabla_{x}^{j}\nabla_{v}^{l}h|^{2}+|\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{k+2-i}h|^{2}\right)\text{d}\mu\,,

as well as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}h\cdot[\mathcal{L},\nabla_{x}^{k}\nabla_{v}]h\text{d}\mu\geqslant\\

Proof of Theorem
5
.

Our goal is to apply Lumer-Phillips theorem to the operator
\mathcal{L}+\rho_{k}/2I
, where
I
denotes the identity operator of
H^{k}
. This theorem can be stated as follows: an operator
A
on a Hilbert space generates a contraction semi-group if and only if it is maximally dissipative, see
[
50
, Chapter IX, p.250]
. Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The scalar product

\left\langle f,g\right\rangle_{k}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}fg\text{d}\mu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\sum_{p=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\omega_{i,p}\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{p-i}f\cdot\nabla_{x}^{i}\nabla_{v}^{p-i}g+\omega_{p,p}\left(\nabla_{x}^{p}-\nabla_{x}^{p-1}\nabla_{v}\right)f\cdot\left(\nabla_{x}^{p}-\nabla_{x}^{p-1}\nabla_{v}\right)g\right)\text{d}\mu

generates a norm equivalent to the usual norm of
H^{k}
.
Proposition
16
and a density argument yield that the operator
\mathcal{L}+\rho_{k}/2I
, is dissipative:

\forall h\in D(\mathcal{L}),\,\left\langle(\mathcal{L}+\rho_{k}/2I)h,h\right\rangle_{k}\leqslant 0,

where
D(\mathcal{L})
denote the domain of
\mathcal{L}
in
H^{k}
defined by:

f\in D(\mathcal{L}),\,g=\mathcal{L}f\Leftrightarrow f\in H^{k},\,\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\left\|\frac{P_{t}f-f}{t}-g\right\|_{H^{k}}=0.

We are left to show that
\mathcal{L}+\rho I
, for some
\rho<\rho_{k}/2
, is surjective. Fix such a
\rho<\rho_{k}/2
. Thanks to Proposition
16
, we have that

\Lambda:(f,g)\mapsto\left\langle-(\mathcal{L}+\rho I)f,g\right\rangle_{k}

is coercive and continuous from
\left(H^{k}\right)^{2}
to
\mathbb{R}
for
k\geqslant 1
. Hence, we may apply Lax-Milgram theorem to get that for all
g\in H^{k}
, there exists
f\in H^{k}
such that for all
h\in H^{k}
, we have:

\left\langle-(\mathcal{L}+\rho I)f,h\right\rangle_{H^{k}}=\left\langle-g,h\right\rangle_{H^{k}},

which implies that
f
is a solution to the equation

(\mathcal{L}+\rho I)f=g,

and
\mathcal{L}+\rho_{k}/2I
is maximally dissipative. Lumer-Phillips Theorem then yields that the semi-group generated by
\mathcal{L}+\rho_{k}/2
is a contraction on
H^{k}
: for all
f\in H^{k}

N_{k}\left(e^{\rho_{k}t/2}(P_{t}f-\mu(f))\right)\leqslant N_{k}\left(f-\mu(f)\right),

which concludes the proof.
∎

with respect to the standard Gaussian measure,

\displaystyle\int|\mathcal{L}_{J}h|^{2}V_{b}\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int\mathbb{E}\left[(h(x,V^{i})-h)^{2}\Psi^{2}\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})\right)\right]V_{b}\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int\mathbb{E}\left[(h(x,V^{i})^{2}+h^{2})\Psi^{2}\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})\right)\right]V_{b}\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int h^{2}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[V_{b}(x,V^{i})\Psi^{2}\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(V^{i}_{i}-v_{i})\right)\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\Psi^{2}\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})\right)\right]V_{b}\right)\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int h^{2}(\mathbb{E}[V_{b}(x,V^{i})(1+|v_{i}-V_{i}^{i}|^{2})]+(1+|v_{i}|^{2})V_{b})\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leqslant C\int h^{2}V_{b^{\prime}}\text{d}\mu\,.

Finally, notice that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int f_{t}^{2}V_{b}\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle=2\int f_{t}\mathcal{L}f_{t}V_{b}\text{d}\mu=-2\int\Gamma(f_{t})V_{b}\text{d}\mu+\int\mathcal{L}(f_{t}^{2})V_{b}\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leqslant\int f_{t}^{2}\mathcal{L}^{*}V_{b}\text{d}\mu\leqslant-\eta\int f_{t}^{2}V_{b}\text{d}\mu+C\int f_{t}^{2}\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leqslant-\eta\int f_{t}^{2}V_{b}\text{d}\mu+Ce^{-\rho t}\,.

Therefore,

\int f_{t}^{2}V_{b}\text{d}\mu\leqslant Ce^{-\rho t}\,.

Using the Theorem
5
, we then have that for
b
small enough, there exist
C>0
and
\rho>0
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\partial^{\alpha}f_{t}\partial^{\alpha}\mathcal{L}_{J}(f_{t})V_{b}\text{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}N_{2|\alpha|}(f_{t})\text{d}\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}f_{t}^{2}V_{5b}\text{d}\mu}

\displaystyle\leqslant Ce^{-\rho t}\,,

which concludes the proof.
∎

4
Weak error of the numerical scheme

In this section, devoted to the proof of Theorem
2
,
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{T}^{d}
. The structure of the proof is the following. First, in Section
4.1
, by a Lyapunov argument, we establish uniform-in-time Gaussian moment bounds for the BJAOAJB chain (this is Proposition
22
). This is then used in Section
4.2
to provide an upper bound for the weak error of the scheme in a finite-time horizon, leading to Theorem
26
, which is of interest for itself (the proof essentially relies on Taylor expansions of the semi-groups, the expectation of the local errors being bounded thanks to the Gaussian moments established in Proposition
22
). Geometric ergodicity for the BJAOAJB chain is established in Section
4.3
(since a Lyapunov function is already available, in order to apply Harris’ theorem, it only remains to prove a minorization condition). It is then possible to let time go to infinity in Theorem
26
and, following the classical proof of Talay and Tubaro, to conclude the proof of Theorem
2
, as detailed in Section
4.4
. For completeness, the classical proof of Corollary
4
is provided in Section
4.5
.

4.1
Lyapunov function for the numerical scheme

For all
z=(x,v)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
b>0
, we define the function
V_{b}
by

V_{b}(z)=e^{b|v|^{2}}\,.

In the following, we will denote by
(\overline{Z}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
the discrete-time Markov chain with transition kernel
Q
defined in (
8
), corresponding to the BJAOAJB splitting scheme with time step
\delta
, and by
A,B,J
and
O
the kernels of the different steps of the scheme, namely
B=e^{\delta/2\mathcal{L}_{B}},A=e^{\delta/2\mathcal{L}_{A}},J=e^{\delta/2\mathcal{L}_{J}},O=e^{\delta\mathcal{L}_{O}}
.
The goal of this section is to show the following result.

Proposition 22
.

Let
b<\frac{1}{2}
. There exist
\delta_{0},C,C^{\prime}>0
such that for all
\delta\leqslant\delta_{0}
,

QV_{b}(z)\leqslant(1-C\delta)V_{b}(z)+C^{\prime}\delta\,.

As a consequence, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbb{E}_{z}[V_{b}(\overline{Z}_{n})]=Q^{n}V_{b}(z)\leqslant e^{-C\delta n}V_{b}(z)+\frac{C^{\prime}}{C}\,.

In order to prove this proposition, we will need to bound the terms
BV_{b},AV_{b},OV_{b}
and
JV_{b}
. First, since
A
only acts on positions,
AV_{b}=V_{b}
.

Lemma 23
.

For all
\delta_{0},\varepsilon>0,b\in(0,1/2)
, there exists
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that for all
0<\delta\leqslant\delta_{0}
,

BV_{b}\leqslant(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)e^{b(1+\varepsilon\delta)|v|^{2}}\,.

Proof.

For all
z=(x,v)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, using the Young inequality,

BV_{b}(z)=e^{b|v-\frac{\delta}{2}\nabla U_{0}(x)|^{2}}=e^{b\left(|v|^{2}-\delta\nabla U_{0}(x)\cdot v+\frac{\delta^{2}}{4}|\nabla U_{0}(x|^{2}\right)}\leqslant e^{b\left((1+\varepsilon\delta)|v|^{2}+\delta\left(\frac{\delta}{4}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}\right)||\nabla U_{0}||_{\infty}^{2}\right)}\leqslant(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)e^{b(1+\varepsilon\delta)|v|^{2}}\,,

the last inequality being satisfied if
\delta
is small enough.
∎

Lemma 24
.

There exists
\delta_{0}>0
(independent from
b
) such that for all
b\in(0,1/2)
, there exist
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that for
0<\delta\leqslant\delta_{0}
,

\displaystyle OV_{b}(z)\leqslant(1+C_{2}\delta)e^{b(1-C_{1}\delta)|v|^{2}}\,.

Proof.

We start by noticing that for all
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, the random variable
\exp(a_{1}\xi^{2}+a_{2}\xi)
is integrable if and only if
a_{1}<1/2
. In that case, by recognizing the density of
\mathcal{N}(a_{2}/(1-2a_{2}),1/(1-2a_{1}))
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\exp(a_{1}\xi^{2}+a_{2}\xi)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp(a_{1}x^{2}+a_{2}x-x^{2}/2)\text{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(\frac{-(1-2a_{1})}{2}\left(x-\frac{a_{2}}{1-2a_{1}}\right)^{2}+\frac{a_{2}^{2}}{2(1-2a_{1})}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2a_{1}}}\exp\left(\frac{a_{2}^{2}}{2(1-2a_{1})}\right)\,.

Using this remark,

\displaystyle OV_{b}(z)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(b|e^{-\gamma\delta}v+\sqrt{1-e^{-2\gamma\delta}}\xi|^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\exp\left(be^{-2\gamma\delta}|v|^{2}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(2be^{-\gamma\delta}\sqrt{1-e^{-2\gamma\delta}}v\cdot\xi+b(1-e^{-2\gamma\delta})|\xi|^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left(1-2b(1-e^{-2\gamma\delta})\right)^{d/2}}\exp\left(be^{-2\gamma\delta}|v|^{2}+\frac{2b^{2}e^{-2\gamma\delta}(1-e^{-2\gamma\delta})|v|^{2}}{1-2b(1-e^{-2\gamma\delta})}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left(1-2b(1-e^{-2\gamma\delta})\right)^{d/2}}\exp\left(b|v|^{2}\frac{e^{-2\gamma\delta}}{1-2b(1-e^{-2\gamma\delta})}\right)\,.

Then, there exist
\delta_{0}>0
and constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that for all
0<\delta\leqslant\delta_{0}
,

\frac{e^{-2\gamma\delta}}{1-2b(1-e^{-2\gamma\delta})}\leqslant 1-C_{1}\delta\,,

and

\frac{1}{\left(1-2b(1-e^{-2\gamma\delta})\right)^{d/2}}\leqslant 1+C_{2}\delta\,.

Hence

OV_{b}(z)\leqslant(1+C_{2}\delta)e^{b(1-C_{1}\delta)|v|^{2}}\,.

Notice that
\delta_{0}
can in fact be chosen independently from
b
.
On one side,

\frac{1}{\left(1-2b(1-e^{-2\gamma\delta})\right)^{d/2}}\leqslant\frac{1}{(1-2\gamma\delta)^{d/2}}\leqslant 1+d\gamma\delta+O(\delta^{2}),

and on the other side, the Taylor-Lagrange formula shows that there exists
0<c<2\gamma\delta
such that

\frac{e^{-2\gamma\delta}}{1-2b(1-e^{-2\gamma\delta})}=1-2\gamma(1-2b)\delta+\left(4b^{2}-3b+\frac{1}{2}\right)c^{2}.

If
1/4<b<1/2
,
4b^{2}-3b+\frac{1}{2}<0
so we directly have

\frac{e^{-2\gamma\delta}}{1-2b(1-e^{-2\gamma\delta})}\leqslant 1-2\gamma(1-2b)\delta\,,

without any condition on
\delta
. Now, if
0<b<1/4
,
4b^{2}-3b+\frac{1}{2}<1/2
, and

\frac{e^{-2\gamma\delta}}{1-2b(1-e^{-2\gamma\delta})}\leqslant 1-\gamma\delta+2\gamma^{2}\delta^{2},

and
\delta_{0}
can be chosen independently from
b
.
∎

Lemma 25
.

For all
0<b_{1}<b_{2}<1/2
, there exist
\delta_{0}>0
and
K>0
such that for all
\delta\leqslant\delta_{0}
and all
b\in[b_{1},b_{2}]
,

JV_{b}(z)\leqslant V_{b}(z)(1+\delta K)+\delta K\,.

Proof.

Let
0<b_{1}<b_{2}<1/2
and
b\in[b_{1},b_{2}]
. In the proof of this lemma, constants
C
can depend on
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
, but not on
b
. We will denote
Z_{k}=(X_{k},V_{k})
the jump process after
k
jumps, and
Z_{\delta}
the jump process after a time
\delta
. We start by expanding
JV_{b}(z)
according to the number of jumps:

\mathbb{E}_{z}[V_{b}(Z_{\delta})]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{z}[V_{b}(Z_{\delta})\mathbbm{1}_{\{k\text{ jumps}\}}]\,.

First,

\mathbb{E}_{z}[V_{b}(Z_{\delta})\mathbbm{1}_{\{0\text{ jumps}\}}]=V_{b}(z)\mathbb{P}(0\text{ jumps})\leqslant V_{b}(z)\,.

Then, let us show by induction on
k
that there exists a constant
C
such that for all
k\geqslant 1
,

\mathbb{E}_{z}[V_{b}(Z_{\delta})\mathbbm{1}_{\{k\text{ jumps}\}}]\leqslant C^{k}\delta^{k}(1+V_{b}(z))\,.

(21)

In order to prove this inequality, we will use some useful bounds. First, recall that there exist a constant
C
such that for all
z\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\lambda(z)\leqslant C(1+|v|)\,.

(22)

As we saw in the proof of Lemma
24
, if
a_{1}<1/2
, then the random variable
\exp(a_{1}\xi^{2}+a_{2}\xi)
is integrable and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\exp(a_{1}\xi^{2}+a_{2}\xi)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2a_{1}}}\exp\left(\frac{a_{2}^{2}}{2(1-2a_{1})}\right)\,.

More generally, by recognizing the density of a Gaussian random variable with mean
a_{2}/(1-2a_{1})
and variance
1/(1-2a_{1})
,

\mathbb{E}[|\xi|^{n}\exp(a_{1}\xi^{2}+a_{2}\xi)]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2a_{1}}}\exp\left(\frac{a_{2}^{2}}{2(1-2a_{1})}\right)\mathbb{E}[|X|^{n}]\,,

where
X\sim\mathcal{N}\left(a_{2}/(1-2a_{1}),1/(1-2a_{1})\right)
. As a consequence, the random variable
V_{b}(Z_{1})
is integrable if and only if
b(1-\rho^{2})<1/2
, which is always true since
b\in(0,1/2)
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{z}[V_{b}(Z_{1})]

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}_{z}\left[\exp(b|\rho v_{i}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\xi|^{2})\Psi\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}\left((1-\rho)v_{i}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\xi\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}_{z}\left[\exp(b|\rho v_{i}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\xi|^{2})(1+|(1-\rho)v_{i}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\xi|)\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sum_{i=1}^{d}\exp(b\rho^{2}|v_{i}|^{2})\mathbb{E}[\exp(b(1-\rho^{2})\xi^{2}+2b\rho\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}v_{i}\xi)(1+2|v_{i}|+|\xi|)]\,,

so that

\mathbb{E}_{z}[V_{b}(Z_{1})]\leqslant C(1+|v|^{2})e^{b\alpha|v|^{2}}\,,

(23)

where we denoted
\alpha=\frac{\rho^{2}}{1-2b(1-\rho^{2})}
. Notice that

\displaystyle\alpha<1\iff\rho^{2}<1-2b(1-\rho^{2})\iff b<1/2\,,

and that
b\alpha=b\rho^{2}/(1-2b(1-\rho^{2}))
, seen as a function of
b
, is continuous and strictly positive on
(0,1/2)
, so is bounded and reaches its extrema on
[b_{1},b_{2}]
. Since
|v|^{2}e^{b\alpha|v|^{2}}=o(V_{b})
and
|v|=o(V_{b})
when
v
goes to infinity, let

K=\max(R^{2},R)\sup_{b\in[b_{1},b_{2}]}e^{b\alpha R^{2}}\,,

where
R>0
satisfies

|v|\geqslant R\implies\max(|v|,|v|^{2})\leqslant\inf_{b\in[b_{1},b_{2}]}e^{b(1-\alpha)|v|^{2}}\,,

so that

|v|^{2}e^{b\alpha|v|^{2}}\leqslant V_{b}(z)+K\,,

(24)

and

|v|\leqslant V_{b}(z)+K\,.

(25)

Furthermore, we also have that

\int_{0}^{\delta}(\delta-t)\lambda(z)e^{-\lambda(z)t}\text{d}t=\frac{\lambda(z)\delta+e^{-\lambda(z)\delta}-1}{\lambda(z)}\leqslant\frac{\lambda(z)\delta^{2}}{2}\leqslant C\delta^{2}(1+|v|)\,.

(26)

We start by showing (
21
) in the case
k=1
. We suppose that
\lambda(z)>0
(otherwise there is no jump and the result is straightforward). By conditioning with respect to the only jump time,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{z}[V_{b}(Z_{\delta})\mathbbm{1}_{\{1\text{ jump}\}}]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\delta}\lambda(z)e^{-\lambda(z)t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}q(z,\text{d}\tilde{z})V_{b}(\tilde{z})e^{-(\delta-t)\lambda(\tilde{z})}\text{d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{\delta}\lambda(z)e^{-\lambda(z)t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}q(z,\text{d}\tilde{z})V_{b}(\tilde{z})\text{d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{z}[V_{b}(Z_{1})]\lambda(z)\delta\,.

Then, using (
23
), (
24
) and (
25
), we have

\mathbb{E}_{z}[V_{b}(Z_{\delta})\mathbbm{1}_{\{1\text{ jump}\}}]\leqslant C\delta e^{b\alpha|v|^{2}}(1+|v|^{2})\leqslant C\delta\left(V_{b}+K\right)\leqslant C\delta(1+V_{b})\,,

which proves the inequality for
k=1
. Now let
k\geqslant 1
and assume that (
21
) holds for
k
. By conditioning with respect to the first jump time,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{z}[V_{b}(Z_{\delta})\mathbbm{1}_{\{k+1\text{ jumps}\}}]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\delta}\lambda(z)e^{-\lambda(z)t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}q(z,\text{d}\tilde{z})\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{z}}[V_{b}(Z_{\delta-t})\mathbbm{1}_{\{k\text{ jumps}\}}]\text{d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant C^{k}\int_{0}^{\delta}(\delta-t)^{k}\lambda(z)e^{-\lambda(z)t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}q(z,\text{d}\tilde{z})(V_{b}(\tilde{z})+1)\text{d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant\delta^{k-1}C^{k}\left(\mathbb{E}_{z}[V_{b}(Z_{1})]+1\right)\int_{0}^{\delta}(\delta-t)\lambda(z)e^{-\lambda(z)t}\text{d}t\,.

Then, using (
26
),

\mathbb{E}_{z}[V_{b}(Z_{\delta})\mathbbm{1}_{\{k+1\text{ jumps}\}}]\leqslant\delta^{k+1}C^{k+1}\left(e^{b\alpha|v|^{2}}+1\right)(1+|v|^{2})\leqslant\delta^{k+1}C^{k+1}\left(1+V_{b}\right)\,,

which proves the inequality for
k+1
and ends the induction. By choosing
\delta<1/C
, we can sum the inequalities (
21
):

JV_{b}(z)=\mathbb{E}_{z}[V_{b}(Z_{\delta})]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{z}[V_{b}(Z_{\delta})\mathbbm{1}_{\{k\text{ jumps}\}}]\leqslant V_{b}(z)+(1+V_{b}(z))\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(C\delta)^{k}\\
\leqslant V_{b}(z)+\delta\frac{C}{1-C\delta}(1+V_{b}(z))\,,

so there exist
\delta_{0}
and
K>0
such that for all
\delta<\delta_{0}
,

JV_{b}\leqslant V_{b}(1+\delta K)+\delta K.

∎

We are now able to prove Proposition
22
.

Proof of Proposition
22
.

Let
b\in(0,1/2)
and
\varepsilon>0
, whose value will be specified later.
Thanks to Lemma
23
, for all
\delta_{1}>0
, there exist
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that for all
\delta<\delta_{1}
,

BV_{b}\leqslant(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)e^{b(1+\varepsilon\delta)|v|^{2}}\,.

Now let
\delta_{2}>0
such that
b_{2}=b(1+\varepsilon\delta_{2})<1/2
. Notice that for all
0<\delta\leqslant\delta_{2}
,
b<b^{\prime}:=b(1+\varepsilon\delta)<b_{2}
. Thanks to Lemma

25
, there exist
\delta_{3}>0
and
K>0
(whose value is uniform over
[b,b_{2}]
) such that for all
\delta<\delta_{3}
,

JV_{b^{\prime}}\leqslant V_{b^{\prime}}(1+\delta K)+\delta K\,.

Therefore, for all
\delta<\min(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3})
,

JBV_{b}(z)\leqslant(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)JV_{b^{\prime}}(z)\leqslant(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)V_{b^{\prime}}(z)(1+\delta K)+\delta(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)K\,,

and

AJBV_{b}(z)\leqslant(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)AV_{b^{\prime}}(z)(1+\delta K)+\delta(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)K=(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)V_{b^{\prime}}(z)(1+\delta K)+\delta(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)K\,.

Lemma
24
gives a
\delta_{4}>0
(whose value is independent from
b
or
b^{\prime}
) such that for
\delta<\delta_{4}
,

\displaystyle OV_{b^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leqslant(1+C_{2}\delta)\exp\left(b^{\prime}(1-C_{1}\delta)|v|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant(1+C_{2}\delta)\exp\left(b(1+\varepsilon\delta)(1-C_{1}\delta))|v|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant(1+C_{2}\delta)\exp\left(b(1-(C_{1}-\varepsilon)\delta)|v|^{2}\right)\,.

We would like to have
\varepsilon<C_{1}
(In fact, because of the two applications of the kernel
B
, we will need to have
\varepsilon<C_{1}/2
). When looking at the proof of the Lemma
24
, the value of
C_{1}
can depend on
b^{\prime}
(and therefore on
\varepsilon
) if
1/4<b^{\prime}<1/2
, in which case
C_{1}=2\gamma(1-2b^{\prime})=2\gamma(1-2b(1+\varepsilon\delta))
. The constant
\varepsilon
can always be chosen smaller than half of this value if
\varepsilon<\frac{\gamma(1-2b)}{1+4\gamma b\delta_{1}}
. In the other cases,
C_{1}
does not depend on
b^{\prime}
, so we can also choose
\varepsilon<C_{1}/2
. We now denote
a=C_{1}-\varepsilon
. For all
\delta<\min(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3},\delta_{4})
,

OAJBV_{b}(z)\leqslant(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)OV_{b^{\prime}}(z)(1+\delta K)+\delta(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)K\leqslant(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)(1+C_{2}\delta)\exp(b(1-a\delta)|v|^{2})(1+\delta K)+\delta(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)K\,,

and

AOAJBV_{b}(z)\leqslant(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)(1+C_{2}\delta)\exp(b(1-a\delta)|v|^{2})(1+\delta K)+\delta(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)K\,.

From there, in order to re-apply the bounds on the jump kernel, we can let
\delta_{5}
such that
b_{3}=b(1-a\delta_{5})>0
, which gives a
\delta_{6}>0
such that for
0<\delta\leqslant\min(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3},\delta_{4},\delta_{5},\delta_{6})
, corresponding to a
b_{3}<b(1-a\delta)<b
,

J\left(e^{b(1-a\delta)|v|^{2}}\right)\leqslant e^{b(1-a\delta)|v|^{2}}(1+\delta K^{\prime})+\delta K^{\prime}\,,

which yields

JAOAJBV_{b}(z)\leqslant(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)(1+C_{2}\delta)(1+\delta K)((1+\delta K^{\prime})\exp(b(1-a\delta)|v|^{2})+\delta K^{\prime})+\delta(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)K\,.

Finally, for the last application of the kernel

, we need to have

(1+\varepsilon\delta)(1-a\delta)=1-(a-\varepsilon)\delta-a\varepsilon\delta^{2}<1\,,

which is possible because

and that
\varepsilon<C_{1}/2
. We then denote

. Finally, let
\delta_{0}=\min\left(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3},\delta_{4},\delta_{5},\delta_{6}\right)

such that for all

\displaystyle BJAOAJBV_{b}(z)

\displaystyle\leqslant(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)(1+C_{2}\delta)(1+\delta K)((1+\delta K^{\prime})B\exp(b(1-a\delta)|v|^{2})+\delta K^{\prime})+\delta(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)K

\displaystyle\leqslant(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)(1+C_{2}\delta)(1+\delta K)((1+\delta K^{\prime})(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)\exp(b(1-c\delta)|v|^{2})+\delta K^{\prime})+\delta(1+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)K

\displaystyle\leqslant V_{b}(z)(1+C^{\prime}\delta)^{5}\exp(-bc\delta|v|^{2})+\delta C^{\prime}\,,

and hence

BJAOAJBV_{b}(z)\leqslant V_{b}(z)\exp(5C^{\prime}\delta-bc\delta|v|^{2})+\delta C^{\prime}\,.

(27)

Now, writing
R_{0}^{2}=10C^{\prime}/(bc)
, distinguishing cases, we see that

e^{5C^{\prime}\delta-bc\delta|v|^{2}}\leqslant e^{-5C^{\prime}\delta}\mathbbm{1}_{|v|\geqslant R_{0}}+e^{5C^{\prime}\delta}\mathbbm{1}_{|v|<R_{0}}\leqslant\left(1-5C^{\prime}e^{-5C^{\prime}\delta_{0}}\delta\right)\mathbbm{1}_{|v|\geqslant R_{0}}+\left(1+5C^{\prime}e^{5C^{\prime}\delta_{0}}\delta\right)\mathbbm{1}_{|v|<R_{0}}\\
\leqslant 1-c^{\prime}\delta+C^{\prime\prime}\delta\mathbbm{1}_{|v|<R_{0}}\,,

for some

independent from
\delta
.
Plugging this in (

) we obtain that there exist

independent from
\delta
such that for all

,

QV_{b}(z)\leqslant(1-K\delta)V_{b}(z)+K^{\prime}\delta\,,

which by induction yields

\displaystyle Q^{n}V_{b}(z)

\displaystyle\leqslant(1-K\delta)^{n}V_{b}(z)+K^{\prime}\delta\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-K\delta)^{k}

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{-K\delta n}V_{b}(z)+\frac{K^{\prime}}{K}\,,

concluding the proof.
∎

4.2
Finite-time error expansion of the numerical scheme

In this section,
Z_{t}=(X_{t},V_{t})
denotes the continuous-time process with generator
\mathcal{L}
and with Markov semi-group

Proposition 28

(Higher derivatives of composite functions)
.

Let
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{l}
and
f:\mathbb{R}^{l}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be smooth functions, and
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
be a multi-index.
\alpha
is said to be decomposed into

(pairwise distinct) parts
p_{1},...,p_{s}
in
\mathbb{N}^{n}
with multiplicities
m_{1},\dots,m_{s}
in
\mathbb{N}^{l}
(the
p
’s and the
m
’s are multi-indexes) if

\alpha=|m_{1}|p_{1}+\dots|m_{s}|p_{s}

holds and all parts are different. We define the total multiplicity by

m=m_{1}+\dots+m_{s}

and
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}
as the set of all such decompositions of
\alpha
. Then,

\partial^{\alpha}(f\circ g)(x)=\alpha!\sum_{(s,p,m)\in\mathcal{D}}(\partial^{m}f)(g(x))\prod_{k=1}^{s}\frac{1}{m_{k}!}\left[\frac{1}{p_{k}!}\partial^{p_{k}}g(x)\right]^{m_{k}}

where
\alpha!=\prod_{j}\alpha_{j}!
and
x^{\alpha}=\prod_{j}x_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}
. The term
\left[\frac{1}{p_{k}!}\partial^{p_{k}}g(x)\right]^{m_{k}}
is thus the short form for

\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left[\frac{1}{p_{k,1}!\dots p_{k,n}!}\partial^{p_{k}}g_{i}(x)\right]^{m_{k,i}}

where
g=(g_{1},\dots,g_{l})
.

Proof of Lemma

.

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,

such that

small enough.

First, for all

thus

||\mathcal{L}_{B}f||_{b,k-1}\leqslant C||f|_{b,k}\,.

Similarly, for
|\alpha|\leqslant k-1

which yields

||\mathcal{L}_{A}f||_{b^{\prime},k-1}\leqslant C||f|_{b,k}\,.

If
|\alpha|\leqslant k-2
,

|\partial^{\alpha}\mathcal{L}_{O}f|=|-\gamma\partial^{\alpha}(v\cdot\nabla_{v}f)+\gamma\partial^{\alpha}\Delta_{v}f|\leqslant CV_{b^{\prime}}(z)||f||_{b,k}\,,

and thus

||\mathcal{L}_{O}f||_{b^{\prime},k-2}\leqslant C||f|_{b,k}\,.

Regarding the jump generator, we saw in the proof of Lemma
20
that for all
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,

\partial_{x}^{\nu_{1}}\partial_{v}^{\nu_{2}}(f(x,V^{i}))=\rho^{\nu_{2,i}}(\partial_{x}^{\nu_{1}}\partial_{v}^{\nu_{2}}f)(x,V^{i})\,,

and that the only non-vanishing derivatives of
\Psi\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})\right)
are of the type
\partial_{x}^{\nu}\partial_{v_{i}}^{n}\Psi\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})\right)
with

\left|\partial_{x}^{\nu}\partial_{v_{i}^{n}}\Psi\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})\right)\right|\leqslant C\left(1+|v_{i}-V_{i}^{i}|^{|\nu|+n}\right)\,.

Therefore, if
|\alpha|\leqslant k
,

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}\mathcal{L}_{J}f(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{1-\rho}\sum_{i}\partial^{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[(f(x,V^{i})-f(x,v))\Psi\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{1-\rho}\sum_{i}\sum_{\nu\leqslant\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[\partial^{\nu}(f(x,V^{i})-f(x,v))\partial^{\alpha-\nu}\Psi\left(\frac{\partial_{i}U_{1}}{2}(v_{i}-V^{i}_{i})\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant C\frac{2}{1-\rho}\sum_{i}\sum_{\nu\leqslant\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\rho^{\nu_{d+i}}\partial^{\nu}f(x,V^{i})-\partial^{\nu}f(x,v)\right)(1+|v_{i}-V_{i}^{i}|^{|\alpha-\nu|})\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant C||f||_{b,k}\sum_{i}\sum_{\nu\leqslant\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(V_{b}(x,V^{i})+V_{b}(z)\right)(1+|v_{i}-V_{i}^{i}|^{|\alpha-\nu|})\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant C||f||_{b,k}\left(V_{b^{\prime}}(z)+\mathbb{E}[(1+|v|^{|\alpha|}+|\xi|^{|\alpha|})V_{b}(x,V^{i})]\right)\,.

In the proof of Lemma
25
, we saw that

and that if

where

which yields

being bounded (recall that the position space is a torus in this section), we have for all

Similarly, for

Finally, by denoting
(Z_{t})

\partial^{n}_{t}\overline{P}_{t}=\sum_{k_{1},...k_{7}}\frac{B^{k_{1}}}{2^{k_{1}}}e^{\frac{t}{2}B}\frac{J^{k_{2}}}{2^{k_{2}}}e^{\frac{t}{2}J}\frac{A^{k_{3}}}{2^{k_{3}}}e^{\frac{t}{2}A}O^{k_{4}}e^{tO}\frac{A^{k_{5}}}{2^{k_{5}}}e^{\frac{t}{2}A}\frac{J^{k_{6}}}{2^{k_{6}}}e^{\frac{t}{2}J}\frac{B^{k_{7}}}{2^{k_{7}}}e^{\frac{t}{2}B}\,,

where the sum is over all nonnegative integers
k_{1}\dots k_{7}
such that
k_{1}+\dots+k_{7}=n
. In fact, thanks to the particular palindromic form of
\overline{P}_{t}
, those operators can be computed algebraically using the symmetric Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff (BCH) formula (see for instance
[
18
]
, Section III.4.2), that states that for any
A,B
elements of a Lie algebra of a Lie group,

\exp\left(\frac{t}{2}A\right)\exp\left(\frac{t}{2}B\right)\exp\left(\frac{t}{2}A\right)=\exp(tS_{1}+t^{3}S_{3}+t^{5}S_{5}+...)

where

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=A+B

\displaystyle S_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{24}(2[B,[B,A]]-[A,[A,B]])\,.

Therefore, in our case,

e^{\frac{t}{2}A}e^{tO}e^{\frac{t}{2}A}=e^{t\mathcal{B}_{1}}\,,

with
\mathcal{B}_{1}=\hat{S_{1}}+t^{2}\hat{S_{3}}+t^{4}\hat{S_{5}}+...
with
\hat{S_{1}}=A+O
and

\hat{S_{3}}=\frac{1}{24}(2[O,[O,A]]-[A,[A,O]])\,.

A second application of the formula gives

e^{\frac{t}{2}J}e^{t\mathcal{B}_{1}}e^{\frac{t}{2}J}=e^{t\mathcal{B}_{2}}\,,

where
\mathcal{B}_{2}=\tilde{S_{1}}+t^{2}\tilde{S_{3}}+...
with
\tilde{S_{1}}=\hat{S_{1}}+J=A+O+J
and

\displaystyle\tilde{S_{3}}

\displaystyle=\hat{S_{3}}+\frac{1}{24}(2[\hat{S_{1}},[\hat{S_{1}},J]]-[J,[J,\hat{S_{1}}]])

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{24}(2[O,[O,A]]-[A,[A,O]]+2[A+O,[A+O,J]]-[J,[J,A+O]])\,.

A third and final application gives

\overline{P}_{t}=e^{\frac{t}{2}B}e^{t\mathcal{B}_{2}}e^{\frac{t}{2}B}=e^{t\mathcal{B}_{3}}\,,

with
\mathcal{B}_{3}=\overline{S_{1}}+t^{2}\overline{S_{3}}+t^{4}\overline{S_{5}}+...
where
\overline{S_{1}}=\tilde{S_{1}}+B=A+B+J+O=L
and

\displaystyle\overline{S_{3}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{S_{3}}+\frac{1}{24}(2[\tilde{S_{1}},[\tilde{S_{1}},B]]-[B,[B,\tilde{S_{1}}]])

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{24}(2[O,[O,A]]-[A,[A,O]]+2[A+O,[A+O,J]]-[J,[J,A+O]]

\displaystyle\qquad+2[A+J+O,[A+J+O,B]]-[B,[B,A+J+O]])\,.

What we just showed is that

\partial_{t}\overline{P}_{t}f(z)|_{t=0}=Lf(z),\qquad\partial_{t}^{2}\overline{P}_{t}f(z)|_{t=0}=L^{2}f(z),\qquad\partial_{t}^{3}\overline{P}_{t}f(z)|_{t=0}=(L^{3}+7\overline{S_{3}})f(z)\,.

Finally, set
S_{3}=\frac{7}{6}\overline{S_{3}}
, which yields

\overline{P}_{t}f(z)-P_{t}f(z)=t^{3}S_{3}f(z)+\frac{1}{6}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{3}(\partial^{4}_{s}\overline{P}_{s}-L^{4}P_{s})f(z)\text{d}s\,,

with

\frac{1}{6}\left|\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{3}(\partial^{4}_{s}\overline{P}_{s}-L^{4}P_{s})f(z)\text{d}s\right|\leqslant C(z)t^{4}\,,

which concludes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
26
.

We write the Talay-Tubaro expansion of the weak error.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{z}[f(Z_{n\delta})]-\mathbb{E}_{z}[f(\overline{Z}_{n})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[P_{n\delta}f(z)-P_{0}f(\overline{Z}_{n})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}_{z}[P_{(n-k)\delta}f(\overline{Z}_{k})-P_{(n-k-1)\delta}f(\overline{Z}_{k+1})]\,.

By conditioning each term by
\overline{Z}_{k}
, we have, since
(\overline{Z}_{n})
is a Markov chain,

\mathbb{E}_{z}[f(Z_{n\delta})]-\mathbb{E}_{z}[f(\overline{Z}_{n})]=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}[h_{k}(\overline{Z}_{k})]\,,

where, by using the semi-group property of
(P_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
,

\displaystyle h_{k}(z)

\displaystyle=P_{(n-k)\delta}f(z)-\mathbb{E}[P_{(n-k-1)\delta}f(\overline{Z}_{1})]

\displaystyle=P_{\delta}P_{(n-k-1)\delta}f(z)-\overline{P}_{\delta}P_{(n-k-1)\delta}f(z)\,.

We are now interested in the difference
P_{\delta}\varphi-\overline{P}_{\delta}\varphi
for
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}
. As we saw in Lemma
30
,

\overline{P}_{t}\varphi(z)-P_{t}\varphi(z)=t^{3}S_{3}\varphi(z)+\frac{1}{6}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{3}(\partial^{4}_{s}\overline{P}_{s}-L^{4}P_{s})\varphi(z)\text{d}s\,.

Now, Lemma
29
implies that there exist
b<1/2
and
C>0
such that

|h_{k}(z)|=|P_{\delta}P_{(n-k-1)\delta}f(z)-\overline{P}_{\delta}P_{(n-k-1)\delta}f(z)|\leqslant C\delta^{3}e^{-q(n-k-1)\delta}V_{b}(z)\,,

and then, by using Proposition
22
,

|\mathbb{E}_{z}[h_{k}(\overline{Z}_{k})]|\leqslant C\delta^{3}e^{-q(n-k-1)\delta}\mathbb{E}_{z}[V_{b}(\overline{Z}_{k})]\leqslant C\delta^{3}e^{-q(n-k-1)\delta}\left(e^{-C^{\prime}\delta k}V_{b}(z)+1\right)\,.

By summing those inequalities on
k
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}_{z}[f(Z_{t})]-\mathbb{E}_{z}[f(\overline{Z_{n}})]|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}[|h_{k}(\overline{Z_{k}})|]

\displaystyle\leqslant C\delta^{3}\left(V_{b}(z)e^{-q(n-1)\delta}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}e^{(q-C^{\prime})k\delta}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}e^{-qk\delta}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\delta^{3}V_{b}(z)\frac{e^{-qn\delta}-e^{-C^{\prime}n\delta}}{e^{-q\delta}-e^{-C^{\prime}\delta}}+C^{\prime\prime}\delta^{2}\leqslant C\delta^{2}(V_{b}(z)e^{-qn\delta}+1)\,,

which shows the result, with
C(n\delta,z)=C(V_{b}(z)e^{-qn\delta}+1)
. More explicitly,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{z}[f(Z_{t})]-\mathbb{E}_{z}[f(\overline{Z_{n}})]

\displaystyle=\delta^{3}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}[S_{3}P_{(n-k-1)\delta}f(\overline{Z_{k}})]\right)+\frac{1}{6}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\delta}(\delta-s)^{3}\mathbb{E}_{z}[(\partial^{4}_{s}\overline{P}_{s}-L^{4}P_{s})g_{k}(\overline{Z_{k}})]\text{d}s\,,

and the same computations lead to

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}[S_{3}P_{(n-k-1)\delta}f(\overline{Z_{k}})]\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant C\left(V_{b}(z)\frac{e^{-qn\delta}-e^{-C^{\prime}n\delta}}{e^{-q\delta}-e^{-C^{\prime}\delta}}+\frac{1}{1-e^{-q\delta}}\right)\leqslant\frac{C}{\delta}(V_{b}(z)e^{-qn\delta}+1)\,,

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\delta}(\delta-s)^{3}\mathbb{E}_{z}[(\partial^{4}_{s}\overline{P}_{s}-L^{4}P_{s})g_{k}(\overline{Z_{k}})]\text{d}s\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}e^{-q(n-k-1)\delta}\mathbb{E}_{z}[V_{b}(\overline{Z_{k}})]\int_{0}^{\delta}(\delta-s)^{3}e^{-qs}\text{d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant C\delta^{4}\left(V_{b}(z)\frac{e^{-qn\delta}-e^{-C^{\prime}n\delta}}{e^{-q\delta}-e^{-C^{\prime}\delta}}+\frac{1}{1-e^{-q\delta}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\delta^{3}(V_{b}(z)e^{-qn\delta}+1)\,.

∎

4.3
Ergodicity of the numerical scheme

Proof of Theorem
3
.

The result follows from Harris Theorem (see for instance
[
22
, Theorem 1.2]
; the constants are made explicit in
[
17
, Theorem 24]
). More precisely, we will prove that, for
\delta
small enough,
Q^{\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor}
satisfy the conditions of Harris theorem with constants which are independent from
\delta
. Namely, we need to check, first, a Lyapunov condition

Q^{\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor}V_{b}\leqslant\gamma V_{b}+C\,,

(28)

where
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
C>0
are independent from
\delta
, and, second, a local coupling condition: for any compact set
K
of
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists
\alpha>0
(again, independent from
\delta
) such that for all
(x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in K
,

\|\delta_{(x,v)}Q^{\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor}-\delta_{(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})}Q^{\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor}\|_{TV}\leqslant 2(1-\alpha)\,.

(29)

When we will have proven these two conditions, we will get by
[
22
, Theorem 1.2]
that there exists
C^{\prime\prime},\lambda>0
(which are independent from
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
since they can be expressed in terms of
b,\gamma,C
and
\alpha
where
\alpha
is given by the coupling condition on the compact set
K=\{V_{b}\leqslant 4C/(1-\gamma)\}
) such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and all probability measures
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}
over
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\|\nu_{1}Q^{n\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor}-\nu_{2}Q^{n\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor}\|_{V}\leqslant C^{\prime\prime}e^{-\lambda n}\|\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}\|_{V}\,.

Besides,
[
22
, Theorem 1.2]
also gives the existence and uniqueness of an invariant measure
\mu_{\delta}
for
Q^{\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor}
(which by standard semi-group argument is thus the unique invariant measure for
Q
). Before proceeding with the proofs of (
28
) and (
29
), let us first explain the conclusion of the proof of Theorem
3
from this. Thanks to Proposition
22
, for all
k\geqslant 1
,

Q^{k}V_{b}\leqslant(1-C\delta)^{k}V_{b}+C^{\prime}\frac{1-(1-C\delta)^{k}}{C}\leqslant\left(1+\frac{C^{\prime}}{C}\right)V_{b}\,,

since
V_{b}\geqslant 1
. As noticed in
[
17
, Equation (86)]
, this implies that for all
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}
,

\|\nu_{1}Q^{k}-\nu_{2}Q^{k}\|_{V}\leqslant\left(1+\frac{C^{\prime}}{C}\right)\|\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}\|_{V}\,.

Decomposing any
n\geqslant 1
as
n=m\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor+k
with
k\in\llbracket 0,\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor-1\rrbracket
, using the invariance of
\mu_{\delta}
by
Q
,

\displaystyle\|\delta_{(x,v)}Q^{n}-\mu_{\delta}\|_{V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\delta_{(x,v)}Q^{n}-\mu_{\delta}Q^{n}\|_{V}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{C^{\prime}}{C}\right)\|\delta_{(x,v)}Q^{m\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor}-\mu_{\delta}Q^{m\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor}\|_{V}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C^{\prime\prime}e^{-\lambda m}\left(1+\frac{C^{\prime}}{C}\right)\|\delta_{(x,v)}-\mu_{\delta}\|_{V}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C^{\prime\prime}e^{-\lambda(n-1)\delta}\left(1+\frac{C^{\prime}}{C}\right)\left(V_{b}(x,v)+\mu_{\delta}(V_{b})\right)\,.

Thanks to Proposition
22
and the invariance of
\mu_{\delta}
,

\mu_{\delta}(V_{b})\leqslant(1-C\delta)\mu_{\delta}(V_{b})+C^{\prime}\delta\implies\mu_{\delta}(V_{b})\leqslant\frac{C^{\prime}}{C}\,.

Using again that
V_{b}\geqslant 1
, we end up with

\|\delta_{(x,v)}Q^{n}-\mu_{\delta}\|_{V}\leqslant C^{\prime\prime}e^{-\lambda(n\delta-\delta_{0})}\left(1+\frac{C^{\prime}}{C}\right)^{2}V_{b}(x,v)\,,

with
C,\lambda,C^{\prime},C^{\prime\prime}
which are independent from
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
. This will conclude the proof of Theorem
3
.

It remains to check (
28
) and (
29
). The Lyapunov condition (
28
) straightforwardly follows from Proposition
22
, using that
(1-C\delta)^{\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor}
goes to
e^{-C}<1
as
\delta\rightarrow 0
. The rest of the proof is dedicated to establishing the coupling condition (
29
). This result has been established for the BAOAB chain in
[
16
]
. More precisely, given a compact set
K
of
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
[
16
, Theorem 3]
shows that there exists
\delta_{0}^{\prime},\varepsilon>0
such that for all
\delta\in(0,\delta^{\prime}_{0})
and
(x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in K
,

\|\delta_{(x,v)}\widetilde{Q}^{\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor}-\delta_{(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})}\widetilde{Q}^{\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor}\|_{TV}\leqslant 2(1-\varepsilon)\,,

with
\widetilde{Q}
the transition operator of the BAOAB chain with potential
U_{0}
(i.e. the BJAOAJB chain with
U_{1}=0
). By the coupling characterisation of the total variation norm, it means that, for any
(x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in K
, it is possible to define two BAOAB chains
(X_{n},V_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(X_{n}^{\prime},V_{n}^{\prime})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
initialized respectively at these points which are equal at time
\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor
with probability lager than
\varepsilon>0
(to see that we can define such a trajectory, see e.g. the proof of
[
40
, Lemma 3.2]
). Denote
\mathcal{A}_{1}
the event where these two BAOAB chains have merged before
\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor
transitions. In particular, for all
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}^{\prime})
,
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}_{1})\geqslant\varepsilon
.

Next, let us prove that there exists a compact set
K^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, independent from
\delta
small enough and from
(x,v)\in K
, such that the probability that the BAOAB chain
(X_{n},V_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
initialized at
(x,v)
exits
K^{\prime}
before
\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor
transitions is smaller than
\varepsilon/4
. This classically follows from the fact
V_{b}
is a Lyapunov function for the BAOAB chain. Indeed, notice that Assumption
1
implies that the same assumptions are satisfied when replacing
U_{1}
by
0
, which means that Proposition
22
applies to BAOAB. Using that
V_{b}\geqslant 1
, this implies that
\widetilde{Q}V_{b}\leqslant(1+C^{\prime}\delta)V_{b}\leqslant e^{C^{\prime}\delta}V_{b}
, i.e.
e^{-C^{\prime}\delta n}e^{b|V_{n}|^{2}}
is a supermartingale. For
R>0
, consider the stopping time
\tau=\inf\{n,|V_{n}|\geqslant R\}
. Then, letting
n\rightarrow\infty
in
\mathbb{E}(e^{-C^{\prime}\delta(n\wedge\tau)}e^{b|V_{n\wedge\tau}|^{2}})\leqslant e^{b|v|^{2}}
, we get
\mathbb{E}(e^{-C^{\prime}\delta\tau})\leqslant De^{-bR^{2}}
with
D=\sup\{e^{b|v_{0}|^{2}},(x_{0},v_{0})\in K\}
. In particular,
\mathbb{P}(\tau\leqslant\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor)\leqslant e^{C^{\prime}}De^{-bR^{2}}
. Taking
R
large enough (depending on
K,b,C^{\prime}
and
\varepsilon
, but not on
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
), conclusion follows with
K^{\prime}=\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,R]
.

Considering the two coupled BAOAB chains
(X_{n},V_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(X_{n}^{\prime},V_{n}^{\prime})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as before, denote by
\mathcal{A}_{2}
the event where both chains remain in
K^{\prime}
up to time
\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor
. The choice of
K^{\prime}
ensures that
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{A}_{2})\geqslant 1-\varepsilon/2
, from which
\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{A}_{1}\cap\mathcal{A}_{2}\right)\geqslant\varepsilon/2
.

Now, we define two BJAOAJB chains by following the two previous BAOAB chains and adding random jumps. More precisely, let
E
be a standard exponential variable independent from the BAOAB chains and let
S=\inf\{k\geqslant 0,E\leqslant\frac{\delta}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\lambda(\widetilde{X}_{j},\widetilde{V}_{j})\}
, where
(\widetilde{X}_{j},\widetilde{V}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
are the intermediary of the chain
(X_{n},V_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
(after the first B step of transition, and then after the BAOA steps; which are the two places where the jumps are performed in BJAOAJB, hence the jump rate
\lambda
is evaluated). We define a BJAOAJB chain
(Y_{n},W_{n})
which follows
(X_{n},V_{n})
up to half-step
S
(i.e. up to the intermediary half-step J where a jump occurs), at which point a velocity jump is performed, and after that
(Y_{n},W_{n})
is evolved independently from the BAOAB chain. Similarly we define a BJAOAJB chain
(Y_{n}^{\prime},W_{n}^{\prime})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
initialized at
(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})
from the BAOAB trajectory
(X_{n}^{\prime},V_{n}^{\prime})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
(with the same variable
E
to define the first jump time). Let
\lambda_{*}=\sup_{K^{\prime}}\lambda
and
\mathcal{A}_{3}=\{E>\lambda_{*}\}
, which is independent from the two BAOAB chains and in particular from
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
. Under
\mathcal{A}_{1}\cap\mathcal{A}_{2}\cap\mathcal{A}_{3}
, the jump rate is bounded by
\lambda_{*}>0
along the two coupled BAOAB trajectories and thus
S>2\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor
, which means there is no jump up to time
\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor
. Hence, under this event, the two BJAOAJB trajectories coincide with the BAOAB ones and thus have merged at time
\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor
. As a conclusion,

\|\delta_{(x,v)}Q^{\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor}-\delta_{(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})}Q^{\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor}\|_{TV}\leqslant 2\left(1-\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{A}_{1}\cap\mathcal{A}_{2}\cap\mathcal{A}_{3}\right)\right)\leqslant 2(1-e^{-\lambda_{*}}\varepsilon/2)\,.

This concludes the proof of (
29
), hence of Theorem
3
.
∎

4.4
Expansion of the invariant measure of the numerical scheme

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{A},\delta_{0}>0
such that the result of Theorem
3
holds and
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
. The ergodicity of the continuous time process
(Z_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
(Theorem
1
) and the BJAOAJB chain
(\overline{Z}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
(Theorem
3
) imply, since
\|f\|_{V_{b}}<\infty
for some
b\in(0,1/2)
, that

\mu f=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{z}[f(Z_{k\delta})]\,,\qquad\mu_{\delta}f=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{z}[f(\overline{Z_{k}})]\,.

Thanks to Theorem
26
, for any
n\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{z}[f(Z_{k\delta})-f(\overline{Z_{k}})]\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C\delta^{2}}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(V_{b}(z)e^{-nq\delta}+1)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\delta^{2}\left(\frac{V_{b}(z)}{n(1-e^{-q\delta})}+1\right)\,.

Thus, by taking the limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
in the previous inequality, we get that

|\mu f-\mu_{\delta}f|\leqslant C\delta^{2}\,.

(30)

Let us now look at the error term of order
\delta^{2}
. Again, we saw in Theorem
26
that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,

\left|\mathbb{E}_{z}[f(Z_{k\delta})-f(\overline{Z_{k}})]-\delta^{3}\sum_{p=0}^{k-1}\mathbb{E}_{z}[S_{3}P_{(k-p-1)\delta}f(\overline{Z_{p}})]\right|\leqslant C\delta^{3}(V_{b}(z)e^{-qn\delta}+1)\,,

thus

\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\mathbb{E}_{z}[f(Z_{k\delta})-f(\overline{Z_{k}})]-\delta^{3}\sum_{p=0}^{k-1}\mathbb{E}_{z}[S_{3}P_{(k-p-1)\delta}f(\overline{Z_{p}})]\right)\right|\leqslant C\delta^{3}\left(\frac{V_{b}(z)}{n(1-e^{-q\delta})}+1\right)\,.

(31)

Then,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{p=0}^{k-1}\mathbb{E}_{z}[S_{3}P_{(k-p-1)\delta}f(\overline{Z_{p}})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{p=0}^{k-1}\mathbb{E}_{z}[S_{3}P_{p\delta}f(\overline{Z}_{k-p-1})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}\sum_{k=p+1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{z}[S_{3}P_{p\delta}f(\overline{Z}_{k-p-1})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-p-1}\mathbb{E}_{z}[S_{3}P_{p\delta}f(\overline{Z}_{k})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{z}[S_{3}P_{p\delta}f(\overline{Z}_{k})]-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}\sum_{k=n-p}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{z}[S_{3}P_{p\delta}f(\overline{Z}_{k})]\,.

By ergodicity of the numerical scheme, for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
,

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{z}[S_{3}P_{p\delta}f(\overline{Z}_{k})]\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\mu_{\delta}S_{3}P_{p\delta}f\,,

and thanks to Lemma
29
and Proposition
22
, there exist
b\in(0,1/2),q>0
and
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{z}[S_{3}P_{p\delta}f(\overline{Z}_{k})]\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C}{n}e^{-pq\delta}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{z}[V_{b}(\overline{Z}_{k})]

\displaystyle\leqslant Ce^{-pq\delta}\left(\frac{V_{b}(z)}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}e^{-C^{\prime}\delta k}+1\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant Ce^{-qp\delta}\left(\frac{V_{b}(z)}{n(1-e^{-C^{\prime}\delta})}+1\right)\,.

Since the right term of the last inequality is summable in
p
, Lebesgue convergence theorem implies that

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{z}[S_{3}P_{p\delta}f(\overline{Z}_{k})]\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\mu_{\delta}S_{3}P_{p\delta}f\,.

Similarly, there exist
b\in(0,1/2),q>0
and
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}\sum_{k=n-p}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{z}[|S_{3}P_{p\delta}f(\overline{Z}_{k})|]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C}{n}\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}e^{-qp\delta}\left(V_{b}(z)\sum_{k=n-p}^{n}e^{-C^{\prime}\delta k}+p\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C}{n}\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}e^{-qp\delta}\left(\frac{V_{b}(z)}{1-e^{-C^{\prime}\delta}}+p\right)\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0\,,

which shows that

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{p=0}^{k-1}\mathbb{E}_{z}[S_{3}P_{(k-p-1)\delta}f(\overline{Z_{p}})]\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\mu_{\delta}S_{3}P_{p\delta}f\,.

We may then let
n\rightarrow\infty
in (
31
) to obtain

\left|\mu f-\mu_{\delta}f-\delta^{3}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\mu_{\delta}S_{3}P_{p\delta}f\right|\leqslant C\delta^{3}\,.

We have shown that

|\mu f-\mu_{\delta}f-\delta^{2}\mu_{\delta}g_{\delta}|\leqslant C\delta^{3}\quad\text{ with }\quad g_{\delta}=\delta\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}S_{3}P_{p\delta}f\,.

Thanks to Theorem
1
and Lemma
29
, for all
t\geqslant 0
,

|\partial_{t}S_{3}P_{t}f(z)|=|S_{3}LP_{t}f(z)|\leqslant C^{-qt}V_{b}(z)\,,

for a certain
b<1/2
. Therefore, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{n\delta}S_{3}P_{s}f\text{d}s-\delta\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}S_{3}P_{p\delta}f\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}\int_{p\delta}^{(p+1)\delta}|S_{3}P_{s}f-S_{3}P_{p\delta}f|\text{d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}\sup_{p\delta\leqslant t\leqslant(p+1)\delta}|\partial_{t}S_{3}P_{t}f|\int_{p\delta}^{(p+1)\delta}(s-p\delta)\text{d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{CV_{b}(z)\delta^{2}}{2}\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}e^{-pq\delta}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{CV_{b}(z)\delta}{2}\,,

which implies, by taking the limit
n\rightarrow\infty
, that

\|g_{\delta}-g\|_{V_{b}}\leqslant C\delta\quad\text{ with }\quad g=\int_{0}^{\infty}S_{3}P_{s}f\text{d}s\,,

and thus

|\mu_{\delta}g_{\delta}-\mu_{\delta}g|\leqslant C\delta\,.

Again,
g\in\mathcal{A}
. Indeed, if
f\in\mathcal{A}
, thanks to Lemma
27
, we know that all spatial derivatives
of
S_{3}P_{t}f
are bounded by a term of the form
Ce^{-qt}V_{b}(z)
for a certain
b<1/2
, which is integrable in
t
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Therefore, the theorem of differentiation under the integral sign yields that for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,

|\partial^{\alpha}g(z)|\leqslant CV_{b}(z)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-qs}\text{d}s\leqslant CV_{b}(z)\,.

This allows to apply (
30
) to the function
g
, that is

|\mu_{\delta}g-\mu g|\leqslant C\delta^{2}\,.

Finally,

\displaystyle|\mu f-\mu_{\delta}f-\delta^{2}\mu g|

\displaystyle\leqslant|\mu f-\mu_{\delta}f-\delta^{2}\mu_{\delta}g_{\delta}|+\delta^{2}|\mu_{\delta}g_{\delta}-\mu_{\delta}g|+\delta^{2}|\mu_{\delta}g-\mu g|

\displaystyle\leqslant C\delta^{3}\,.

We have therefore explicited the first term in the expansion of the invariant measure:

\mu_{\delta}f=\mu f+\delta^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu S_{3}P_{s}f\text{d}s+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{3})\,,

which concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

4.5
Quadratic risk

Proof of Corollary
4
.

Denoting
g=f-\mu(f)
and
\nu_{0}
the distribution of
Z_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(Z_{k})-\mu(f)\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(g(Z_{k})g(Z_{j})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\nu_{0}Q^{j}(g^{2})+\frac{2}{n^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=j+1}^{n}\nu_{0}Q^{j}\left(gQ^{k-j}g\right)\,.

Bounding
g^{2}\leqslant 4\|f\|_{V_{b}}^{2}V_{b}^{2}
and using Proposition
22
(applied to
V_{b}^{2}=V_{2b}
since
b<1/4
), we treat the first term as

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\nu_{0}Q^{j}(g^{2})\leqslant\frac{4\|f\|_{V_{b}}^{2}}{n^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\nu_{0}Q^{j}(V_{b}^{2})\leqslant\frac{4\|f\|_{V_{b}}^{2}}{n^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left[(1-C\delta)^{j}\nu_{0}(V_{b}^{2})+\frac{C^{\prime}}{\delta C}\right]\,.

For the second term, thanks to Theorem
3
, we bound
\|Q^{k}g\|_{V_{b}}\leqslant 2Ce^{-\lambda k\delta}\|f\|_{V_{b}}
, hence
|gQ^{k}g|\leqslant 4Ce^{-\lambda k\delta}\|f\|_{V_{b}}^{2}V_{b}^{2}
, and then

\frac{2}{n^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=j+1}^{n}\nu_{0}Q^{j}\left(gQ^{k-j}g\right)\leqslant\frac{8C\|f\|_{V_{b}}^{2}}{n^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=j+1}^{n}e^{-\lambda(k-j)\delta}\nu_{0}Q^{j}(V_{b}^{2})\\
\leqslant\frac{8C\|f\|_{V_{b}}^{2}}{n^{2}(1-e^{-\lambda\delta})}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\nu_{0}Q^{j}(V_{b}^{2})\,,

and we bound
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\nu_{0}Q^{j}(V_{b}^{2})
as before.

For the Richardson extrapolation, we simply bound

\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\frac{4}{3n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(\tilde{Z}_{k})-\frac{1}{3n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(Z_{k})-\mu(f)\right|^{2}\right)\leqslant 3\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\frac{4}{3n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(\tilde{Z}_{k})-\frac{4}{3}\mu_{\delta/2}(f)\right|^{2}\right)\\
+3\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\frac{1}{3n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(Z_{k})-\frac{1}{3}\mu_{\delta}(f)\right|^{2}\right)+3\left(\frac{4}{3}\mu_{\delta/2}(f)-\frac{1}{3}\mu_{\delta}(f)-\mu(f)\right)^{2}\,.

The two first terms are bounded by
\frac{C}{n\delta}
for some
C>0
as before, and conclusion follows from Theorem
2
(the leading terms of order
\delta^{2}
in the asymptotic bias cancelling out).
∎

Representations of Skew Left Braces of order p ​ q pq
Nishant Rathee, Ayush Udeep ∗

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the irreducible representations of skew braces of order p ​ q pq , which is equivalent to studying the representation theory of groups of order p 2 ​ q 2 p^{2}q^{2} arising from skew left braces, where p > q p>q are primes. To achieve this, we classify all semidirect product groups Λ A \Lambda_{A} associated with skew left braces A A of order p ​ q pq , up to isomorphism.

1.
Introduction

Rump introduced the concept of left braces
[
10
]
, providing an algebraic framework for constructing and classifying involutive, non-degenerate set-theoretic solutions of the Yang–Baxter equation. Guarnieri and Vendramin
[
5
]
later extended this notion to skew left braces, a non-abelian generalization that yields a broader class of bijective, non-degenerate solutions. The study of skew left braces has since expanded in various directions, with significant focus on their algebraic properties, including nilpotency, extension theory, and their deep connections to Hopf-Galois theory
[
2
,
4
,
13
]
. These developments establish skew left braces as a central object of study, bridging multiple areas of algebra and mathematical physics.

In recent years, the structural properties of skew left braces have been extensively studied (see
[
8
,
9
]
).
A skew left brace is a set
A
equipped with two group structures,
(A,\cdot)
and
(A,\circ)
, related by the identity

a\circ(b\cdot c)=(a\circ b)\cdot a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ c),

for all
a,b,c\in A
, where
a^{-1}
denotes the inverse in the group
(A,\cdot)
. This structure induces a natural action

\lambda:(A,\circ)\to\operatorname{Aut}(A,\cdot),\quad\lambda_{a}(b)=a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ b),

which is known as the lambda map associated with skew left brace
A
. This action allows one to construct the associated semidirect product group

\Lambda_{A}=(A,\cdot)\rtimes_{\lambda}(A,\circ).

Letourmy and Vendramin
[
9
]
defined a representation of a skew brace as a pair of representations on the same vector space, one for the additive group and the other for the multiplicative group, satisfying a certain compatibility condition (also see
[
7
]
).
It was observed in
[
11
]
that there exists a one-to-one correspondence between representations of a skew brace
A
and those of
\Lambda_{A}
, marking a crucial breakthrough in the study of skew brace representations.
The group
\Lambda_{A}
has been studied and referred to as the triply factorized group in
[
3
]
, where various structural properties, including left and right nilpotency, substructures, and Fitting-like ideals, were explored. These properties play a fundamental role in understanding the structure of skew left braces and their associated group representations.

Acri and Bonatto
[
1
]
classified all skew left braces of size
pq
, where
p>q
are prime numbers (see Theorem
3.1
). In the present paper, we classify the irreducible representations of all skew left braces of size
pq
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A
be a skew left brace of size
pq
. If
p\not\equiv 1\bmod q
, then there are exactly
p^{2}q^{2}
many 1-dimensional representations. If
p\equiv 1\bmod q
, then

(1)

A=B
has exactly
p^{2}q^{2}
many 1-dimensional representations.

(2)

A=C
has exactly
q^{2}
many 1-dimensional representations and
p^{2}-1
many
q
-dimensional irreducible representations of
A
.

(3)

if
A=D
, or
F_{\gamma}
for
1<\gamma<q
, or
G_{\mu}
for
1<\mu\leq q
, then
A
has exactly
q^{2}
many 1-dimensional representations,
2(p-1)
many
q
-dimensional irreducible representations and
(p-1)^{2}/q^{2}
many
q^{2}
-dimensional irreducible representations.

(4)

if
A=E
, or
F_{q}
, then
A
has exactly
pq^{2}
many 1-dimensional representations and
p^{2}-p
many
q
-dimensional irreducible representations.

Here
B,C,D,E,F_{\gamma}
and
G_{\mu}
are the same as in Theorem
3.1
.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the irreducible representations of the associated group
\Lambda_{A}
of order
p^{2}q^{2}
and utilize the isomorphism of such groups. We also classify the groups
\Lambda_{A}
associated with the skew left braces
A
of size
pq
, up to isomorphism, in Theorem
3.10
.

To the best of our knowledge, representations of groups of order
p^{2}q^{2}
have not been computed yet, owing to the fact that the representation theory of arbitrary groups of order
p^{2}q^{2}
is generally intractable.
Our results demonstrate how skew brace theory provides an effective framework for addressing problems in representation theory that are otherwise difficult to approach purely from a group-theoretic perspective.
A key observation in our approach is that the commutator subgroup of
\Lambda_{A}
can be computed in terms of the commutator of the corresponding skew brace, a relation that was established in
[
11
]
.

We mention the preliminaries required in our computations in Section
2
. In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.1
and
3.10
. We conclude the article with examples in which we compute the irreducible representations of a skew brace and its associated group (see Example
3.11
and
3.12
).

2.
Preliminaries

Let
(A,\cdot,\circ)
be a skew left brace. The groups
(A,\cdot)
and
(A,\circ)
are called the
additive
and the
multiplicative
groups of
(A,\cdot,\circ)
, respectively. The structure
(A,\cdot^{\operatorname{op}},\circ)
is also a skew left brace, called the
opposite
skew left brace of
A
, where
a\cdot^{\operatorname{op}}b:=b\cdot a
for all
a,b\in A
.
Note that the lambda map
\lambda^{\operatorname{op}}
of
(A,\cdot^{\operatorname{op}},\circ)
is a group homomorphism given by

\lambda^{\operatorname{op}}:(A,\circ)\to\operatorname{Aut}(A,\cdot),\lambda^{\operatorname{op}}_{a}(b)=(a\circ b)\cdot a^{-1}=a\cdot\lambda_{a}(b)\cdot a^{-1}.

A subset
I
of
A
is called an
ideal
of
(A,\cdot,\circ)
if it is a normal subgroup of both
(A,\cdot)
and
(A,\circ)
, and
\lambda_{a}(I)\subseteq I
for all
a\in A
. The
commutator

A^{\prime}
of
(A,\cdot,\circ)
is a subgroup of
(A,\cdot)
generated by elements of the form
a\cdot b\cdot a^{-1}\cdot b^{-1}
and
a\cdot\lambda_{b}(a^{-1})
for all
a,b\in A
. The commutator
A^{\prime}
turns out to be an ideal of
(A,\cdot,\circ)
.

Remark 2.1
.

The commutator of a skew brace is the smallest ideal that makes the quotient a trivial brace.

Notation 2.1
.

Before proceeding further, we set some notations:

•

For groups
G
and
H
and a homomorphism
\psi:G\to\operatorname{Aut}(H)
, the group operation in the semi-direct product
H\rtimes_{\psi}G
is given by

(h_{1},g_{1})(h_{2},g_{2})=(h_{1}\psi_{g_{1}}(h_{2}),g_{1}g_{2})

for all
h_{1},h_{2}\in H
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
.

•

For a skew left brace
A:=(A,\cdot,\circ)
,

\Lambda_{A}=(A,\cdot)\rtimes_{\lambda}(A,\circ)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\Lambda_{A^{\operatorname{op}}}=(A,\cdot)\rtimes_{\lambda^{\operatorname{op}}}(A,\circ)

are the semi-direct products induced by
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\operatorname{op}}
, respectively.

•

For a group
G
and an element
g
of
G
,
G^{\prime}
denotes the commutator subgroup of
G
,
\operatorname{Cl}^{G}(g)
denotes the conjugacy class of
g
in
G
and
k(G)
denotes the number of conjugacy classes of
G
.

Next, we state a fundamental result concerning the isomorphism of semidirect product groups.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
G
and
H
be groups, and let
\psi:G\to\operatorname{Aut}(H)
be a homomorphism. For any
f\in\operatorname{Aut}(H)
, the semi-direct product groups
H\rtimes_{\psi f}G
and
H\rtimes_{\psi}G
are isomorphic under the map

\varphi:H\rtimes_{\psi f}G\to H\rtimes_{\psi}G,\quad(g,h)\mapsto(g,f(h)),

for all
g\in G
and
h\in H
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
A
be a skew left brace. Then

(i)

(
[
11
, Proposition 3.3]
)

\Lambda_{A}\cong\Lambda_{A^{\operatorname{op}}}
, under the map
(x,y)\mapsto(xy,y)
.

(ii)

(
[
11
, Theorem 3.7]
)

\Lambda_{A}^{\prime}=A^{\prime}\rtimes_{\lambda}(A,\circ)^{\prime}
.

In
[
8
]
, Letourmy and Vendramin defined a representation of a skew left brace
A
as follows.

Definition 2.4
.

A representation of a skew left brace
A
is a triple
(V,\beta,\rho)
, where

(1)

V
is a vector space over some field;

(2)

\beta:(A,\cdot)\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}(V)
is a representation of
(A,\cdot)
;

(3)

\rho:(A,\circ)\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}(V)
is a representation of
(A,\circ)
;

such that the relation

(2.1)

\beta(\lambda^{\operatorname{op}}_{a}(b))=\rho(a)\beta(b)\rho(a)^{-1}

holds for all
a,b\in A

Lemma 2.5
.

[
11
, Corollary 4.7]

There is a one-to-one correspondence between the set of equivalence classes of irreducible representations of a skew left brace
A
and the set of equivalence classes of irreducible representations of the group
\Lambda_{A^{\operatorname{op}}}
.

Remark 2.6
.

By Lemma
2.3
and
2.5
, it is enough to classify the irreducible representations of
\Lambda_{A}
to classify the irreducible representations of a skew left brace
A
.

Next, we establish an important result concerning the commutator of a skew brace of order
pq
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A
be a skew left brace of order
pq
. Then,
|A^{\prime}|=p
unless
A
is a trivial brace.

Proof.

Since
p>q
, the additive group of
A
has a unique subgroup of order
p
, which we denote by
P
. It is straightforward to verify that
P
is characteristic and thus invariant under the lambda map, making it a normal subgroup of the multiplicative group and hence an ideal. Moreover, since
A/P
has order
q
, it must be a trivial brace. Now, suppose that
A
is not a trivial brace. Since we know that
|A^{\prime}|>1
and that
A^{\prime}\subset P
, it follows that
A^{\prime}=P
.
∎

Skew left brace

Skew left brace

(3.1)

\lambda_{(n,m)}(s,t)=(-n,-m)+((n,m)\circ(s,t))=(g^{m}\cdot s,t),

\text{ for all }n,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\text{ and }m,t\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}

\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\{0\}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\{0\}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}

\displaystyle((0,x),(0,y))\ast((a,0),(b,0))\ast((0,x),(0,y))^{-1}

Since

\lambda_{(n,m)}(s,t)=(s,t),\text{ for all }n,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\text{ and }m,t\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}.

3.2.
Irreducible representations of skew left braces of order

Throughout this section, all the representations are over the field of complex numbers. We denote the set of all inequivalent irreducible representations of
G
by
\operatorname{Irr}(G)
, and the set of the dimensions of irreducible representations of
G
by
\operatorname{cd}(G)
.

In this section, we classify the irreducible representations of skew left braces of order
pq
given in Theorem
3.1
. From Remark
2.6
, we achieve our goal by classifying the irreducible representations of the groups of order
p^{2}q^{2}
associated with the skew left braces of size
pq
.
Before we prove our results, we mention a few fundamental results in representation theory of finite groups that we use in our computations.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
G
be a group. Then

(i)

(
[
6
, Corollary 2.6]
)
every irreducible representation is 1-dimensional if and only if
G
is abelian.

(ii)

(
[
6
, Corollary 2.7]
)
The number of irreducible representations of
G
equals the number of conjugacy classes of
G
, and

|G|=\sum_{\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)}\left(\dim(\rho)\right)^{2}.

(iii)

(
[
6
, Corollary 2.23]
)
the number of 1-dimensional representations is equal to the index of the commutator subgroup in
G
.

(iv)

(
[
6
, Theorem 3.11]
)

\dim(\rho)
divides the order of
G
, for every
\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
.

The following result provides a necessary and sufficient condition for a group to have
\{1,p\}
as its set of the irreducible representation dimensions.

Lemma 3.7
.

[
6
, Theorem 12.11]

Let
G
be a non-abelian group. Then
\operatorname{cd}(G)=\{1,p\}
if and only if one of the following holds.

(i)

There exists an abelian normal subgroup
A
of
G
of index
p
.

(ii)

|G:Z(G)|=p^{3}
.

For solvable groups, we have the following result.

Lemma 3.8
.

[
6
, Corollary 12.34]

Let
G
be a solvable group. Then
G
has a normal abelian Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
if and only if every element of
\operatorname{cd}(G)
is relatively prime to
p
.

Observe that if
G
is a group of order
p^{2}q^{2}
(
p>q
), then Sylow
p
-subgroup is unique in
G
. Then, by Lemma
3.8
, every element of
\operatorname{cd}(G)
is relatively prime to
p
. Hence, by Lemma
3.6
, the possibility for the dimension of an irreducible representation of a group of order
p^{2}q^{2}
is 1,
q
or
q^{2}
. We shall be using this information to prove our results.

In Lemma
3.9
, we compute the irreducible representations for a class of groups of order
p^{2}q^{2}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
G
be a non-abelian group of order
p^{2}q^{2}
. Then
\operatorname{cd}(G)=\{1,q,q^{2}\}
if and only if the action of the Sylow
q
-subgroup of
G
on the Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
is faithful.
Moreover, if the action is not faithful, then there are exactly
p^{2-i}q^{2}
one-dimensional representations and
(p^{2}-p^{2-i})
irreducible representations of dimension
q
, where
|G^{\prime}|=p^{i}
for some
i
with
1\leq i\leq 2
.

Proof.

Let
G=P\rtimes_{\psi}Q
such that
\ker(\psi)
is nontrivial. Then
P\times\ker(\psi)
is a subgroup of
G
. Since
\ker(\psi)\leq Q
, we must have
|\ker(\psi)|=q
or
q^{2}
. However, if
|\ker(\psi)|=p^{2}
, then
G
would contain an abelian subgroup of order
p^{2}q^{2}
, contradicting the non-abelian nature of
G
. Hence, we conclude that
|\ker(\psi)|=q
, and consequently,
P\times\ker(\psi)
is an abelian subgroup of order
p^{2}q
in
G
.

Moreover,
P\times\ker(\psi)
is normal in
G
since its index in
G
is
q
and
p>q
. By Lemma
3.7
, we have
\operatorname{cd}(G)=\{1,q\}
. Let
|G^{\prime}|=p^{i}
for some
i
with
1\leq i\leq 2
. From Lemma
3.6
, the number of 1-dimensional representations of
G
is given by
|G/G^{\prime}|
, and we have the relation

|G|=p^{2}q^{2}=|G/G^{\prime}|+r\cdot q^{2},

where
r
denotes the number of irreducible representations of
G
of dimension
q
. Therefore, there are exactly
p^{2-i}q^{2}
one-dimensional representations and
(p^{2}-p^{2-i})
irreducible representations of dimension
q
.

∎

Now, we prove our main result.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

If
p\not\equiv 1\bmod q
, then there is only the trivial skew brace, say
A
. In this case,
\Lambda_{A}
is an abelian group of order
p^{2}q^{2}
. By Lemma
3.6
,
\Lambda_{A}
does not have any nonlinear irreducible representations and there are
p^{2}q^{2}
many representations of dimension 1.

Now onwards,
p\equiv 1\bmod q
unless stated otherwise. We deal with each skew left brace separately.

Representations of
B
:
Clearly,
\Lambda_{B}
is an abelian group of order
p^{2}q^{2}
. Hence,
\Lambda_{B}
does not have any nonlinear irreducible representations and there are
p^{2}q^{2}
many representations of dimension 1.

Representations of
C
:
By Equation (
3.1
), we have

\lambda_{(n,m)}(s,t)=(g^{m}\cdot s,t),\text{ for all }n,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\text{ and }m,t\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}.

Direct calculation shows that the subgroup
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\{0\}
is contained in the kernel of the lambda map of
C
. Hence, the subgroup
(C,\cdot)\rtimes_{\lambda}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\{0\})
is an abelian subgroup of order
p^{2}q
.
By Lemma
3.9
,
\Lambda_{C}
has
q^{2}
many representations of dimension 1 and
p^{2}-1
many irreducible representations of dimension
q
.

Representations of
D
:
In this case,
\Lambda_{D}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{q}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{q}
. Let us denote the group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{q}
by
H
so that
|H^{\prime}|=p
and
\Lambda_{D}=H\times H
. Since
H
has an abelian normal subgroup of index
q
, by Lemma
3.7
, we get
\operatorname{cd}(H)=\{1,q\}
. Hence, from Lemma
3.6
, there are exactly
q
many 1-dimensional representations of
H
and
(p-1)/q
many
q
-dimensional irreducible representations of
H
. By
[
6
, Theorem 4.21]
, for each
\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(H\times H)
, we have

\dim(\rho)=\dim(\rho_{1})\dim(\rho_{2}),\text{ for some }\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in\operatorname{Irr}(H).

Therefore,

\operatorname{cd}(\Lambda_{D})=\{1,q,q^{2}\}
and we have
q^{2}
many 1-dimensional representations,
2(p-1)
many
q
-dimensional irreducible representations and
(p-1)^{2}/q^{2}
many
q^{2}
-dimensional irreducible representations of
\Lambda_{D}
.

Representations of
E
:
In this case, the subgroup
(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p})\rtimes_{\xi}(\{0\}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q})
is abelian and has index
q
in

\Lambda_{E}
, and
\{0\}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q}
is contained in the kernel of
\xi
(see Equation (
3.4
) for the definition of
\xi
).
By Lemma
3.9
,
\Lambda_{E}
has
pq^{2}
many representations of dimension 1 and
p(p-1)
many irreducible representations of dimension
q
.

Representations of
F_{\gamma}
:
We have the following two cases:

(i):

By Lemma
3.4
, when
1<\gamma<q
,
\Lambda_{F_{\gamma}}
has
q^{2}
many 1-dimensional representations,
2(p-1)
many
q
-dimensional irreducible representations and
(p-1)^{2}/q^{2}
many
q^{2}
-dimensional irreducible representations.

(ii):

By Lemma
3.5
,
\Lambda_{F_{q}}
has
pq^{2}
many representations of dimension 1 and
p(p-1)
many irreducible representations of dimension
q
.

Representations of
G_{\mu}
:
By Lemma
3.4
, when
1<\gamma\leq q
,
\Lambda_{G_{\mu}}
has
q^{2}
many 1-dimensional representations,
2(p-1)
many
q
-dimensional irreducible representations and
(p-1)^{2}/q^{2}
many
q^{2}
-dimensional irreducible representations. ∎

With the help of Theorem
1.1
, we classify up to isomorphism the groups associated with the skew left braces of size
pq
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
A
be a skew left brace of size
pq
. Then
\Lambda_{A}
is isomorphic to one of the following groups

(1)

(\mathbb{Z}_{pq}\times\mathbb{Z}_{pq})
,

(2)

(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p})\rtimes_{\theta}(\mathbb{Z}_{q}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q})
,

(3)

(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p})\rtimes_{\psi}(\mathbb{Z}_{q}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q})
,

(4)

(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p})\rtimes_{\eta}(\mathbb{Z}_{q}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q})
,

where
\theta
,
\psi
and
\xi
are given in Equation (
3.2
), (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), respectively.

Proof.

The proof of part (i) follows from Lemma
3.4
, and that of part (ii) follows from
3.5
. Further, from Theorem
1.1
, the number of irreducible representations of
A,B,C
and
D
are different, which completes the proof.
∎

We conclude this article with the following examples in which we obtain a
q
-dimensional irreducible representation of
C
, and hence of
\Lambda_{C}
.

Example 3.11
.

Let us consider the skew left brace
C
, where
p=3
and
q=2
. Then
(C,+)
is the cyclic group of order 6 and
(C,\circ)
is the dihedral group of order 6, denoted by
D_{6}
. Note that
D_{6}=\langle(1,0),(0,1)\rangle
.
Let
\beta
be a 2-dimensional representation of
(C,+)=\mathbb{Z}_{6}
defined by

\beta(1,1)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\omega_{3}&0\\
0&\omega_{3}^{2}\end{array}\right),

and
\rho
be the 2-dimensional irreducible representation of
(C,\circ)=D_{6}
given by

\rho(1,0)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\omega_{3}&0\\
0&\omega_{3}^{2}\end{array}\right)\text{ and }\rho(0,1)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right).

Here,
\omega_{3}
is a primitive cube root of unity and
\lambda_{(n,m)}^{\operatorname{op}}(s,t)=(2^{m}\cdot s,t)
. Through routine computations, it is easy to verify that Equation (
2.1
) holds. Therefore,
(V,\beta,\rho)
is a 2-dimensional representation of
C
. Since
\rho
is irreducible,
(V,\beta,\rho)
is irreducible as well. By
[
7
, Remark 2.2]
,

\phi_{(\beta,\rho)}:\Lambda_{C}\to\operatorname{GL}(V),\quad\phi_{(\beta,\rho)}(a,b)=\beta(a)\rho(b)

is a representation of
\Lambda_{C}
given by

\displaystyle((s,t),(n,0))

\displaystyle\mapsto\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\omega_{3}^{l+n}&0\\
0&\omega_{3}^{2(l+n)}\end{array}\right),\quad((s,t),(0,1))\mapsto\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\omega_{3}^{l}\\
\omega_{3}^{2l}&0\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle((s,t),(1,1))

\displaystyle\mapsto\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\omega_{3}^{l+1}\\
\omega_{3}^{2(l+1)}&0\end{array}\right),\quad((s,t),(2,1))\mapsto\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\omega_{3}^{2+l}\\
\omega_{3}^{2l+1}&0\end{array}\right),

where
s,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
,
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
l\in\mathbb{N}
such that
l(1,1)=(s,t)
. It is easy to check that
\phi_{(\beta,\rho)}
is an irreducible 2-dimensional representation of
\Lambda_{C}
.

There is another way, through group-theoretic arguments, to obtain a
q
-dimensional irreducible representation of
\Lambda_{C}
. In this, we use
[
12
, Proposition 25]
.

Example 3.12
.

From Equation (
3.2
),
\Lambda_{C}\cong P\rtimes_{\theta}Q
, where
P=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\{0\}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\{0\}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
Q=\{0\}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q}\times\{0\}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{q}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q}
.
Let
\chi
be a 1-dimensional representation of
P
such that
\chi=\chi_{1}\chi_{2}
, where
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
given by
\chi_{1}(a,0)=1
and
\chi_{2}(b,0)=\omega_{p}
, where
\omega_{p}
is a
p^{th}
root of unity and
(a,b)
is a generator of
P
. Let
Q_{1}
be the subgroup of
Q
consisting of those elements
h=(x,y):=((0,x),(0,y))
such that
\chi(hkh^{-1})=\chi(k)
for all
k\in P
. Since the action of
Q
on
P
is given by

\theta_{(x,y)}(a,b)=(g^{y}\cdot a,g^{y}\cdot b),

we obtain

\chi(((0,x),(0,y))((a,0),(b,0))((0,x),(0,y))^{-1})=\chi((g^{y}\cdot a,g^{y}\cdot b))=\omega_{p}^{g^{y}}.

Now,
\omega_{p}^{g^{y}}=\chi(a,b)=\chi_{1}(a,0)\chi_{2}(b,0)=\omega_{p}
implies that
g^{y}\equiv 1\bmod p
and
y\equiv 0\bmod q
. Thus,
(x,y)\notin Q_{1}
if
y\neq 0
, and hence,
|Q_{1}|=q
.

Take
G_{1}=PQ_{1}
so that
|G_{1}|=p^{2}q
. Let
\rho
be an irreducible representation of
Q_{1}
and
\pi
be the canonical projection of
G_{1}
on
Q_{1}
. Then
\tilde{\rho}:\rho\circ\pi
is an irreducible representation of
G_{1}
. The tensor product
\chi\otimes\tilde{\rho}
is again an irreducible representation of
G_{1}
, where

\chi\otimes\tilde{\rho}(t)=\chi(t)\tilde{\rho}(t),\quad\chi(t),\tilde{\rho}(t)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}.

Let
\theta_{\chi,\rho}
be the corresponding induced representation of
G
. Then

\dim\left(\theta_{\chi,\rho}\right)=|\Lambda_{A_{\gamma}}:G_{1}|\dim\left(\chi\otimes\tilde{\rho}\right)=q\cdot 1=q.

From
[
12
, Proposition 25]
,
\theta_{\chi,\rho}
is an irreducible
q
-dimensional representation of
\Lambda_{C}
.

In fact, as is stated in
[
12
, Proposition 25]
, all the irreducible representations of the group can be obtained following the process given in the example above.

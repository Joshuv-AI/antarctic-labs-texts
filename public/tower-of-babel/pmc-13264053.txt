Comment on “Localized Tenosynovial Giant Cell Tumors and Indicators of Surgical Recurrence”
Authors: Neeraj Singh, Monika Srivastav
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with interest the study by Hurst et al1 evaluating recurrence indicators after excision of localized tenosynovial giant cell tumors of the hand. The long follow-up, histological confirmation, and consistent treatment by fellowship-trained hand surgeons make the recurrence estimate clinically useful, especially for counseling patients with bone or neurovascular bundle involvement.

The interpretation of bone involvement would benefit from closer separation of preoperative risk prediction and intraoperative extent. Bone involvement was the strongest recurrence-associated variable, yet it appears to combine radiographic, magnetic resonance imaging, and operative impressions. For surgeons planning treatment before incision, a distinction between cortical contact, pressure erosion, medullary invasion, and curetted bony bed would improve how this finding is applied.2 A patient with shallow cortical scalloping may not carry the same recurrence risk as one with tumor extending into bone, even if both are grouped as “on or in bone.”3

The neurovascular bundle finding is clinically important because it reflects the exact situation where complete marginal excision conflicts with preservation of digital nerve and artery function.4 Rather than treating bundle involvement only as a recurrence marker, future analyses could examine whether recurrence clustered around cases requiring pseudocapsule opening, nerve-sparing peel-off, or residual adherence to the bundle. This would convert the finding from a descriptive risk factor into a practical surgical decision point.

We commend the authors for strengthening recurrence counseling in localized tenosynovial giant cell tumors and for emphasizing meticulous excision. Future studies should standardize magnetic resonance imaging definitions of bone and neurovascular involvement and link them to operative maneuvers, recurrence location, and functional outcomes.

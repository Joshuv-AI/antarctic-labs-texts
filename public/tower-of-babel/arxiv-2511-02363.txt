Electromagnetic Quantum Memory Printed by Gravity
Jie Sheng, Tsutomu T. Yanagida, Bo Gao, Hong Ding

Abstract
The electromagnetic memory is a theoretically predicted effect of great conceptual importance. In this Letter, we show that gravitation acceleration can serve as a source to print memory phases in superconducting states, through the electric field and vector potential it induces inside a conductor. This physical picture offers a novel perspective on the control of quantum phases and the test of gravitational effects in conductors.

According to Ginzburg–Landau theory

Gravity can serve as a generator of an intrinsic and stable electric field inside a conductor region. Schiff and Barnhill

IV
Acceleration-Driven Control of Quantum Memory

In the classical EM memory effect, the EM field must be generated by accelerating charges. Although the electric field discussed here has an unusual origin, the gravity, the variation of gravitational acceleration can effectively control the electric field.
The vector potential thus experiences an irreversible change caused by an electric-field pulse, representing a localized memory effect.
We propose using a free-fall elevator to imprint the EM quantum memory onto the superconducting phases and subsequently measure it.

According to the equivalence principle, gravity and acceleration are locally indistinguishable.
The switching and even magnitude of gravity can be finely controlled through free fall and acceleration.
During free fall, the field is effectively nullified.

Exploration of the potential mechanism of cyclolinopeptides-mediated antiosteoporosis effects via network pharmacology analysis
Authors: Yuan Xiao, Ranjing Wang, Yingxin Long, Ji Xie, Zizhe Cai, Ziwei Lai, Jianhao Zhao, Yong Wang, Hong Nie
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOsteoporosis poses a significant global health burden. Cyclolinopeptides (CLPs), cyclic hydrophobic peptides derived from flaxseed oil, exhibit antiosteoporotic, antioxidant, and immunomodulatory properties, but their therapeutic potential for osteoporosis remains underexplored. This study was aimed to investigate the potential protective effects of CLP-A, CLP-E, and CLP-P on the treatment of osteoporosis in vitro.MethodsNetwork pharmacology identified overlapping targets of CLP-A/E/P and osteoporosis (sourced from GeneCards, DrugBank, DisGeNET, PharmMapper, and BindingDB). Core targets were prioritized via PPI network analysis (Cytoscape). In vitro assays were used to assess CLP cytotoxicity (CCK-8 on MC3T3-E1 and RAW264.7 cells), osteogenic differentiation (alizarin red S staining), and alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity.ResultsNetwork pharmacology analysis revealed that the compounds CLP-A/ E/P were involved in the regulation of diverse biological functions associated with osteoporosis, including signal transduction and positive regulation of transcription from the RNA polymerase II promoter. These signaling pathways are involved primarily in the pathways related to cancer, the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway and the Ras signaling pathway. The experimental results demonstrated that CLP-A/ E/P had no direct toxic effects on MC3T3-E1 Cells or RAW264.7 Cells for 24–48 h, respectively. CLP-A/E/P were shown to enhance MC3T3-E1 Cell osteogenesis and ALP activity.ConclusionsThis study demonstrated that CLP-A, CLP-E, and CLP-P enhance osteogenesis and ALP activity in MC3T3-E1 cells without causing cytotoxicity, suggesting their potential as therapeutic agents for osteoporosis. Network pharmacology further highlights their involvement in key signaling pathways, such as the PI3K-Akt and Ras signaling pathways, which are critical in bone metabolism and osteoporosis, however, further investigation is needed to elucidate the underlying mechanism involved.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12906-025-05146-9.Keywords: Cyclolinopeptides, Osteoporosis, Network pharmacology analysis, Signaling pathway

Introduction

Osteoporosis is a prevalent systemic bone disorder, that is one of the diseases with the highest morbidity and mortality worldwide, affecting approximately 50% of women and one-third of men [1]. Osteoporosis is characterized by abnormalities in bone structure and an increased susceptibility to fractures. Following hip and spine fractures, morbidity and mortality rates significantly increase [2, 3]. It is estimated that 25% of people over the age of 50 who experience osteoporotic hip fractures are at risk of death within a year [4]. The etiology of osteoporosis is invariably associated with the age, gender, and lifestyle of the patient [2, 3, 5]. Refractures associated with osteoporosis impose a significant burden on both individuals and society, profoundly compromising their quality of life [6].

Bone remodeling, which is essential for maintaining bone health, involves bone resorption by osteoclasts and bone formation by osteoblasts. Osteoporosis occurs as a result of disruption of the balance between bone resorption and bone formation. Osteoblasts are responsible for the process of bone formation and maintenance of bone mass, whereas osteoclasts play a crucial role in facilitating bone destruction [7, 8]. RAW264.7 cells can be stimulated to generate multinucleated cells with the anticipated markers of fully differentiated osteoclasts, thereby inducing osteoclast differentiation in vitro [9, 10]. Estrogen levels have a direct impact on bone cells, and a decrease in estrogen can disrupt the balance between bone formation and bone resorption, promoting excessive bone resorption, particularly in postmenopausal osteoporosis patients [5]. Increasing estrogen levels is a crucial therapeutic approach for the treatment of osteoporosis. Current strategies, such as bisphosphonates, denosumab and estrogen administration, aim to inhibit osteoclast activity and decelerate bone resorption. However, long-term suppression of bone resorption seems ineffective in promoting bone remodeling [11–13]. The exploration of novel therapeutic approaches to augment osteoblast activity and bone formation, thereby addressing the treatment of osteoporosis.

Studies have demonstrated that traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) has unique advantages in treating various diseases. Houttuynia cordata Thunb., a commonly used herbal medicine in TCM, has gained global recognition in ethnomedical practices. Its primary active component, Houttuynia cordata Thunb. polysaccharides (HCP), demonstrates significant regulatory effects on the intestinal microbiota [14]. As a key component of TCM, Lonicera japonica Thunb. has been utilized in China for millennia. Historical records indicate its traditional applications in treating febrile diseases, dysentery, and inflammatory conditions, whereas modern clinical practice extends its use to diabetes management, obesity intervention, and the treatment of various infectious diseases including cutaneous infections, appendicitis, and sepsis. Notably, its therapeutic efficacy has been documented in respiratory disorders such as pneumonia and suppurative tonsillitis [15]. Chinese herbal medicine, both as primary anticancer agents and adjuvant treatments, has distinctive advantages in oncological therapy. R. rubescens, a traditional antitumor herb with documented use dating back to the Tang Dynasty, was initially employed for pharyngalgia relief [16]. Furthermore, TCM exhibits remarkable therapeutic potential in osteoporosis management [17]. Flaxseed, derived from seeds of the flax plant Linum usitatissimum L., is a significant oil crop and plays a vital role in TCM [18]. CLPs are a class of naturally occurring cyclic hydrophobic peptides derived from flaxseed oil that possess diverse properties, including antiosteoporotic, antioxidative and immunomodulatory effects [19–22]. In a previous study, 12 samples of cyclic peptides were verified via offline MS/MS with a purity of more than 95.5%, which consists of 8 or 9 amino acid residues and has a molecular weight of approximately 1 kDa [23]. Owing to their unique pharmacological properties, CLPs hold significant potential as therapeutic agents [24]. Recent studies have revealed the potential anti-osteoporotic activity of flaxseed, as both flaxseed and flax oil exhibit preventive effects against osteoporosis caused by estrogen deficiency through a reduction in osteoclast production [25, 26]. Additionally, flax extract directly contributes to the protective effect on bone mass and strength loss in rat models of postmenopausal osteoporosis [27]. However, there is still limited research on the therapeutic treatment of osteoporosis with CLPs, which are important active ingredients in flaxseed. Therefore, we aimed to investigate the antiosteoporotic effect of CLPs in vitro.

In the present study, network pharmacology analysis was employed to predict the potential protective functions of CLP-A, CLP-E, and CLP-P against osteoporosis. The antiosteoporosis effects of CLP-A, CLP-E and CLP-P were subsequently validated in vitro.

Materials and methods

Network pharmacology

Screening of cyclolinopeptide targets

The chemical instructions for CLP- A/E/P were obtained from the SciFinder database (https://scifinder-n.cas.org/). The canonical SMILEs were downloaded and subsequently converted into 3D structures via Chem3D 20.0 software. After MM2 energy minimization, the structures were saved in mol2 format. The targets of CLP-A/E/P were identified by submitting the structures of CLPs to the PharmMapper database (http://www.lilab-ecust.cn/pharmmapper/) and Binding DB databases (http://www.bindingdb.org/bind/index.jsp), while inputting the canonical SMILES of CLPs to the SuperPred database (https://prediction.charite.de/). To improve data quality and reliability, the “Similarity” of the BindingDB database was set to the minimum threshold of 0.85. Finally, the obtained targets were standardized via the UniProt database (https://www.uniprot.org).

Screening for osteoporosis targets

The targets of “osteoporosis” were retrieved from the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org), the Drugbank database (https://go.drugbank.com) and the DisGeNET database (https://www.disgenet.org). The obtained targets were standardized via the UniProt database, and merged, and the duplicate targets were eliminated.

Construction of a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network and the screening of the core target

To elucidate the interactions between CLPs-related targets and osteoporosis-related targets, the screened CLPs targets and osteoporosis-related targets were uploaded to the Venny 2.1.0 online tool (https://bioinfogp.cnb.csic.es/tools/venny/) for analysis. Venn diagrams were then generated to visualize the common targets of “CLPs-osteoporosis” common target.

The common targets of “CLPs-osteoporosis” were subsequently imported into the STRING database (https://cn.string-db.org/), with species limited to “Homo sapiens”. The minimum required interaction score was set at 0.7, nodes with no correlation were hidden, and default settings were used for other parameters. Finally, a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network of the common targets was obtained and exported as a TSV-formatted file. The PPI network was characterized via Cytoscape software and analyzed via Network Analyzer and CytoNCA functions within Cytoscape software. Key targets in the PPI network were identified by selecting those with a degree greater than twice the median value.

Analysis of gene ontology functional and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes pathway enrichment

The common targets of CLPs and osteoporosis were imported to DAVID Bioinformatics Resources (https://david.ncifcrf.gov/).and GO enrichment analysis and KEGG signaling pathway analysis were performed. Tthe species were limited to “Homo sapiens”, and P < 0.05, and the 20 P values corresponding to the smallest entries were visualized via a bioinformatics website (http://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/).

Pharmacodynamic experiments in vitro

Cell culture

RAW264.7 cells (Procell CL-0190, Wuhan Procell Life Technology Co., Ltd.) and MC3T3-E1 cells (Procell CL-0378, Wuhan Procell Life Technology Co., Ltd) were cultured in MEM-ALPHA culture medium (containing 10% FBS, 1% penicillin, and 1% streptomycin) at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 incubator.

Cell proliferation and toxicity experiments

In accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol, cell proliferation and toxicity were evaluated via a Cell Counting Kit 8 assay kit (DOJINDO, Japan). MC3T3-E1 cells and RAW264.7 cells at a density of 1 × 104 cells per well were seeded into a 96-well plate and incubated in an incubator at 37 °C and 5% CO2 overnight. After the cells reached 70–80% confluence, the MC3T3-E1 cells were divided into several groups, including the control group, estrogen group (E2 group, positive control group), CLP-A group, CLP-E group, and CLP-P group. CLP-A, CLP-E, and CLP-P were obtained from Professor Yong Wang at Guangdong Saskatchewan Oilseed Joint Laboratory, Department of Food Science and Engineering, Jinan University, Guangzhou 510,632, China.

The day after MC3T3-E1 cells were seeded, the MC3T3-E1 cells were treated with osteogenic differentiation medium (the final concentration was 1 µg/mL) or medicine (the final concentration was 1 µg/mL) above, and the medium was changed every 2–3 days until day 8. MC3T3-E1 cells were induced with complete medium supplemented with ascorbic acid + sodium β-glycerophosphate (ascorbic acid 50 µg/ml + sodium β-glycerophosphate 10 mM) for osteogenic differentiation, and the control group was treated with FBS-free MEM alpha media. The estrogen group (E2 group), CLP-A group, CLP-E group, CLP-P group, high-cyclosporin A (CsA) concentration group (1 µg/ml) and low-CsA concentration group (0.1 µg/ml) were treated with differentiation medium.

After the above treatment, cell viability was determined via a Cell Counting Kit 8 (CCK-8) assay. On day 9, 10 µL of CCK-8 solution was added to each well for 2 h in the dark. The absorbance at 450 nm was measured with a SYNERGY H1 microplate reader (Bio-Tek, USA).

Imaging and quantitative analysis of osteogenic differentiation and Alizarin red S staining in MC3T3-E1 cells

MC3T3-E1 cells were divided into the following groups: the negative group (without osteogenic differentiation medium), the control group (osteogenic differentiation medium was added only), the vehicle group (1% absolute ethanol was added), the 10 nM estrogen group (E2 group), the CLP-A group, the CLP-E group, the CLP-P group, and the Secoisolariciresinol diglucoside (SDG) group.

The osteogenic differentiation of MC3T3-E1 cells was assessed via Alizarin Red S staining (Alizarin Red S Staining Kit for Osteogenesis, Beyotime) following the manufacturer’s instructions. MC3T3-E1 cells were seeded at a density of 1 × 105/well in 12-well plates. Mineralized calcium nodules were induced by replacing the osteogenic differentiation medium, except for those in negative control group. The control group contained only osteogenic differentiation medium, whereas the vehicle group contained with 1% absolute ethanol. The E2 group was exposed to 10 nM estrogen, the CLP-A group was treated with 0.1 µg/mL CLP-A, the CLP-E group was treated with 0.1 µg/mL CLP-E, and the CLP-P group was treated with 0.1 µg/mL CLP-P. Subsequently, MC3T3-E1 cells were cultivated in osteogenic differentiation medium for 30 days, and the media was changed every 2–3 days until evidence of osteogenesis.

After the aforementioned treatment, the medium was aspirated and rinsed twice with PBS, followed by fixation in a 4% paraformaldehyde solution for 15 min. Subsequently, Alizarin red staining solution was added, and the mixture was incubated at room temperature (25℃) for 10 min. Following double distilled water washing, MC3T3-E1 cells were imaged visualized via an upright microscope (OLYMPUS, Japan).

A solution of 0.5 M HCl and 5% SDS solution was added to the stained well and agitated for 30 min, after which 200 µL of the suspension solution was transferred to each well of a 96-well plate. The absorbance at 415 nm was measured via a SYNERGY H1 microplate reader (Bio-Tek, USA).

Staining of alkaline phosphatase kinase

MC3T3-E1 cells were induced with osteogenic differentiation medium consisting of α-MEM (1% P/S and 10% FBS) supplemented with mineralized inducers (10 mM β-sodium glycerophosphate and 50 µg/mL ascorbic acid) for a duration of 7 days. The induction medium was changed every 2 days. On the eighth day, the medium was discarded, followed by washing each well with PBS twice.

The level of ALP was measured by using alkaline phosphatase kinase (Sainto-bio, Shanghai, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. After treatment, the medium was removed, and the cells were washed with PBS and then fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 10 min. Subsequently, the ALP solution was added and the MCTC-E1 cells were incubated for 20 min under lightless conditions and stained with methyl green staining solution for 3–5 min. Finally, the MC3T3-E1 cells were imaged via an upright microscope (OLYMPUS, Japan), and the deep color indicated the ALP activity. The quantification of ALP was uperformed via ImageJ software.

Statistical analysis

The experimental data in this study were obtained from at least three independent experiments. Statistical analysis was performed, and the data were graphed via GraphPad Prism software (GraphPad Software, Inc., USA).

Results

Network pharmacology analysis

On the basis of the PharmMapper database, the BindingDB database and the SuperPred database, 420 targets of CLP-A, 399 targets of CLP-E, and 410 targets of CLP-P were obtained. A total of 487 potential targets of CLPs were obtained after removing the repetitive values.

Compounds and target screening

The target genes related to osteoporosis were collected from disease-related databases, among which 4959, 205 and 1098 targets were obtained from the GeneCards, DrugBank and DisGeNET databases, respectively, and 5315 targets were obtained by deleting duplicate targets. The Venn diagram of these intersecting targets revealed that the targets act on osteoporosis from CLP, as shown in Fig. 1.

GO enrichment analysis and KEGG enrichment analysis

After the 76 core targets were imported, GO enrichment and KEGG enrichment analyses were performed via the DAVID database. The GO enrichment analysis included biological process (BP) (Fig. 4) and molecular function (MF) (Fig. 5) terms. The color displayed became darker as the P value decreased. KEGG enrichment analysis was employed to investigate the molecular mechanism involved in the anti-osteoporosis effect of the CLPs. The mechanisms involved include the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, the Ras signaling pathway, lipid and atherosclerosis, chemical carcinogenesis-receptor activation, hepatitis B, the thyroid hormone signaling pathway, endocrine resistance, the estrogen signaling pathway, and EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor resistance (Fig. 6). As Fig. 7 showed, the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway and estrogen signaling pathway are involved in the regulation of a variety of biological processes including cell apoptosis, survival signal growth and proliferation, and signal transduction, all of which are closely associated with osteoporosis progression. The reference of Fig. 7 about the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway and estrogen signaling is provided by the DAVID Bioinformatics Resources and the figures were created by using PowerPoint 2016.

Pharmacodynamic experiment

Effects of CLPs on MC3T3-E1 cell proliferation and toxicity

When MC3T3-E1 cells were exposed to CLP-A for 24 or 48 h, the cell viability significantly decreased with increasing concentrations of CLP-A exceeding 199.05 × 10−1 µg/mL (Fig. 8A). The IC50 for 24 h of exposure was determined to be 401.4 × 10−1 µg/mL. After 24 h and 48 h of exposure to CLP-E, the IC50 values of MC3T3-E1 cells were reduced to 19.24 × 10−1 µg/mL and 10.91 × 10−1 µg/mL, respectively (Fig. 8B). Following a treatment duration of 48 h with CLP-P and SDG in MC3T3-E1 cells, the IC50 values were found to be at levels of 425.1 × 10−1 µg/mL and 179.6 × 10−1 µg/mL respectively (Fig. 8C), whereas an IC50 value of 417.4 × 10−1 µg/mL was observed after a treatment duration of 24 h with CLP-P (Fig. 8D).

MC3T3-E1 cell proliferation and toxicity

Effects of CLPs on RAW267.4 cell proliferation and toxicity

A CCK-8 assay demonstrated that when RAW 264.7 cells were exposed to CLP-A for 24 or 48 h, the IC50 values were 264.9 × 10−1 µg/mL and 261.5 × 10−1 µg/mL, respectively. As shown in Fig. 9, CLP-E at a concentration of less than 1 µg/mL had no significant toxicity toward RAW264.7 cells. However, the cell viability obviously decreased at CLP-E concentrations higher than 1 µg/mL. The IC50 values after treatment with CLP-E for 24 h and 48 h were 34.71 × 10−1 µg/mL and 34.29 × 10−1 µg/mL, respectively. The viability of RAW264.7 cells treated with CLP-P and SDG at concentrations lower than 100 µg/mL for 24 h and 48 h did not significantly decrease.

Discussion

Osteoporosis still threatens the lives of people worldwide. A previous study revealed that osteoporosis is responsible for causing more than 1.6 million fractures [28]. The presence of bone pain significantly impacts individuals’ quality of life, with osteoporosis emerging as the primary cause of skeletal-related events. Over the past few years, the research has demonstrated that osteoporosis is associated with abnormal bone metabolism, leading to accelerated bone loss [28]. The process of bone remodeling occurs when osteoblast activity is counteracted by the uptake of osteoclasts, resulting in the dynamic maintenance of bone mass in a steady state. However, the support for osteoclastic bone resorption gradually diminishes as osteoblast activity decreases, leading to osteopenia of various etiologies and ultimately resulting in osteoporosis [29]. The development of osteoporosis necessitates the utilization of osteoblasts.

The process of osteogenesis is characterized by the proliferation of preosteoblast, their subsequent differentiation into osteoblasts, and the formation of a collagen extracellular matrix (ECM). It commences with active proliferation of undifferentiated cells, followed by growth arrest and subsequent collagen ECM formation. The initiation of matrix synthesis triggers the activation of early osteoblast differentiation marker genes such as ALP, leading to the commencement of collagen ECM mineralization and the deposition of calcium and phosphate [30]. The MC3T3-E1 cell line is a representative cell line with a preosteoblast phenotype, and CLPs are cyclic peptides that exhibit inhibitory effects on osteoclast differentiation [31]. The CLP-F compound effectively suppresses the formation of osteoclasts induced by RANKL through downregulating of RANK expression [32]. In the present study, CLP-A, CLP-E and CLP-P were found to increase ALP activity and the increase mineralization rate in preosteoblastic MC3T3-E1 cells induced with osteogenic differentiation medium. Furthermore, CLP-A, CLP-E and CLP-P augment osteogenic activity.

The network pharmacology analysis conducted in this study predicted that CLP-A, CLP-E and CLP-P are primarily targeted in osteoporosis through the modulation of SRC, STAT3, HSP90AA1, MAPK1, PIK3R1, EGFR, HRAS, GRB2, ESR1, PTPN11, HDAC1, IGF1, JAK2, LCK and MAPK8. The GO analysis revealed that the primary biological processes of CLP-A, CLP-E and CLP-P involved in the treatment of osteoporosis include the regulation of signal transduction, positive regulation of transcription from the RNA polymerase II promoter, regulation of transcription, protein phosphorylation, and positive regulation of cell proliferation, etc. Numerous studies have shown that treatment of osteoporosis requires the regulation of signal transduction. This includes mediating the TGF-beta/Smad signaling pathway [28]and the SIRT1 and PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathways [33] to increase osteoblast viability. The induction of differentiation through increased phosphorylation of the AMP-activated protein kinase α subunit (AMPKα) and activation of the AMPK/Smad1 signaling pathway can effectively promote bone growth in vivo [11]. The promotion of osteoblast proliferation and the inhibition of apoptosis may contribute to the prevention of osteoporosis [34].

In addition, enriched molecular functions of the targets included protein binding, ATP binding, identical protein binding, enzyme binding, zinc ion binding, protein kinase binding and transmembrane receptor tyrosine kinase activity. Osteoblasts secrete the blood growth factor M-CSF and bind to their homologous receptor C-FMS on the surface of osteoclasts and monocytes/macrophages, which is essential for osteoclast formation [35]. ATP-binding cassette g1 plays an important role in regulating osteogenesis [36]. The targets are mainly enriched in cellular components, including the nucleus, cytoplasm, cytosol, nucleoplasm, plasma membrane and macromolecular complex. Oxidative stress-induced DNA damage in the nucleus, apoptosis, and cellular senescence contribute to tissue dysfunction and imbalances in bone homeostasis in the cytoplasm [37]. In addition, the results of the KEGG enrichment analysis suggested that the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, the Ras signaling pathway, lipid and atherosclerosis, chemical carcinogenesis-receptor activation, hepatitis B, the thyroid hormone signaling pathway, endocrine resistance, the estrogen signaling pathway, and EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor resistance are the main potential KEGG pathways. Estrogen deficiency affects cell differentiation and apoptosis, increases bone turnover, and enhances bone resorption, leading to osteoporosis. Estrogen can also upregulate BMP signaling, promoting the differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells from former osteoblasts to osteoblasts rather than fat cells [38]. Inhibition of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway exacerbates osteoblast and osteoclast differentiation and induces osteoporosis [39]. However, PI3K/Akt signaling and erythropoiesis in the bone marrow can prevent osteoporotic bone loss by promoting angiogenesis and H-type angiogenesis [40]. In this study, pharmacological analysis revealed that the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway and the estrogen signaling pathway are involved in the mechanism of action of CLP-A, CLP-E and CLP-P in the treatment of osteoporosis. In vitro, CLP-A, CLP-E and CLP-P promoted osteogenesis and increased the ALP activity.

In conclusion, this study demonstrated that cyclolinopeptides (CLP-A, CLP-E, and CLP-P), derived from flaxseed oil, exhibit promising potential for protection against osteoporosis in vitro. These findings reveal that these compounds enhance osteogenesis and ALP activity in MC3T3-E1 cells without causing cytotoxicity, suggesting their potential as therapeutic agents for osteoporosis. Network pharmacology analysis revealed that CLP-A, CLP-E and CLP-P might exert protective effects on osteoporosis through the estrogen signaling pathway and the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, potentially by targeting SRC, STAT3, HSP90AA1, MAPK1, PIK3R1, EGFR, etc. Our study confirms that CLP-A, CLP-E and CLP-P might be potent agents for treating osteoporosis. Therefore, the mechanism by which CLP-A, CLP-E and CLP-P exert antiosteoporosis effects should be further verified in our research group. While promising, the study’s limitations include in vitro nature, necessitating in vivo validation. Future research should explore the molecular mechanisms, pharmacokinetics, and toxicity in preclinical models. Collaborative efforts are essential to advance CLP-based therapies for osteoporosis, emphasizing the need for comprehensive preclinical and clinical studies to harness their therapeutic potential.

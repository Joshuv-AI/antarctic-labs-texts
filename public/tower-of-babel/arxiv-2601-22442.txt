AsyncMesh: Fully Asynchronous Optimization for Data and Pipeline Parallelism
Thalaiyasingam Ajanthan, Sameera Ramasinghe, Gil Avraham, Hadi Mohaghegh Dolatabadi, Chamin P Hewa Koneputugodage, Violetta Shevchenko, Yan Zuo, and Alexander Long Pluralis Research

Abstract
Data and pipeline parallelism are key strategies for scaling neural network training across distributed devices, but their high communication cost necessitates co-located computing clusters with fast interconnects, limiting their scalability. We address this communication bottleneck by introducing asynchronous updates across both parallelism axes , relaxing the co-location requirement at the expense of introducing staleness between pipeline stages and data parallel replicas. To mitigate staleness, for pipeline parallelism, we adopt a weight look-ahead approach, and for data parallelism, we introduce an asynchronous sparse averaging method equipped with an exponential moving average based correction mechanism. We provide convergence guarantees for both sparse averaging and asynchronous updates. Experiments on large-scale language models (up to 1B parameters ) demonstrate that our approach matches the performance of the fully synchronous baseline, while significantly reducing communication overhead.

Recall that our aim is to optimize the consensus objective Eq. (
2

and

Conclusion

In this paper, we studied a new AsyncMesh setup where both
DP
and
PP
are asynchronous, and introduced a fully asynchronous approach that can match the performance of the fully synchronized method. We theoretically showed that our method converges to a fixed point of the consensus objective on expectation, despite sparse averaging and asynchronous communication. By alleviating communication overhead without any performance penalty, our approach takes a step towards realizing large-scale collaborative training over the internet.

Appendix A
Theoretical Insights

We restate the problem setup, introduce some notations, and then turn to the proofs.

A.1
Setup and Notations

We consider the
DP
setup without
PP
for simplified theoretical analysis. In this, the consensus objective takes the following form:

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{w}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f({\mathbf{w}};{\mathcal{D}})\coloneqq

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{w}}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,\sum_{i=1}^{m}f({\mathbf{w}}_{i};{\mathcal{D}}_{i})\ ,

(14)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\quad{\mathbf{w}}_{i}={\mathbf{w}}\ ,\qquad\forall\,i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\ .

Here,
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
denotes the workers,
{\mathbf{w}}_{i},{\mathcal{D}}_{i}
denote the model weights and data chunk for worker
i
, and
f
is the loss function. Typically,
{\mathcal{D}}_{i}
is an i.i.d.
subset of
{\mathcal{D}}
.

Let us first define some notations:

\displaystyle\bar{{\mathbf{w}}}^{t}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{t}\ ,\qquad

\displaystyle\mbox{averaged weights}\ ,

(15)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{t};{\mathcal{B}}_{i}^{t})\ ,\qquad

\displaystyle\mbox{minbatch gradient}\ ,

\displaystyle\bar{{\mathbf{g}}}^{t}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}\ ,\qquad

\displaystyle\mbox{average of gradients}\ ,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\lVert{\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{t}-\bar{{\mathbf{w}}}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\ ,\qquad

\displaystyle\mbox{consensus error}\ .

Here,
\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}
denotes the post-averaging consensus error at iteration

\left\lVert\hat{\bm{\Delta}}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}
. Analogously,
\bm{\Delta}^{t}_{i}\coloneqq{\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{t}-\bar{{\mathbf{w}}}^{t}
.

We are now ready to prove that sparse averaging ensures consensus across the weights of all workers on expectation.

A.2
Sparse Averaging Ensures Consensus

Lemma 1
.

Let
p
be the probability of an element
\mu\in{\mathcal{S}}^{t}
independent of others, then sparse averaging shrinks the consensus error by a factor of
(1\!-\!p)
on expectation,
i.e
.
,
{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]=(1-p)\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert\hat{\bm{\Delta}}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]}
.

Proof.

By definition of sparse averaging,
w_{i:\mu}^{t+1}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i}\hat{w}_{i:\mu}^{t}
for all
\mu\in{\mathcal{S}}^{t}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{\mu=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left(\bm{\Delta}_{i:\mu}^{t}\right)^{2}\right]\ ,\qquad

\displaystyle\mbox{linearity of expectation}\ ,

(16)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{\mu=1}^{d}\left(\hat{\bm{\Delta}}_{i:\mu}^{t}\right)^{2}\,\mathbb{E}\!\,\!\left\llbracket\mu\notin{\mathcal{S}}^{t}\right\rrbracket\ ,\qquad

\displaystyle\mbox{$\!\left\llbracket\cdot\right\rrbracket$ is the indicator}\ ,

\displaystyle=(1-p)\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert\hat{\bm{\Delta}}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\ .\qquad

\displaystyle\mbox{$P\!\left\llbracket\mu\notin{\mathcal{S}}^{t}\right\rrbracket=1-p$}\ .

∎

Theorem 3
.

Let
f
be a
L
-smooth function, the stochastic gradient
{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}
be an unbiased estimate of
\nabla f
and have bounded variance
\sigma^{2}
, and
p>0
be the averaging probability for an element
\mu
, then, for an appropriate choice of learning rate
\eta_{t}>0
the consensus error diminishes on expectation,
i.e
.
,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]=0
.

Proof.

Let us expand
\hat{\bm{\Delta}}^{t+1}_{i}
:

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{\Delta}}^{t+1}_{i}

\displaystyle=\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}_{i}^{t+1}-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i}\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}_{i}^{t+1}\ ,

(17)

\displaystyle={\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{t}-\eta_{t}\,{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i}\left({\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{t}-\eta_{t}\,{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}\right)\ ,\qquad

\displaystyle\mbox{local update}\ ,

\displaystyle=\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t}-\eta_{t}\left({\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}-\bar{{\mathbf{g}}}^{t}\right)\ .

Now, the pre-averaging consensus error can be written as:

\displaystyle\left\lVert\hat{\bm{\Delta}}^{t+1}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t}-\eta_{t}\left({\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}-\bar{{\mathbf{g}}}^{t}\right)\right\rVert^{2}\ ,

(18)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\left(\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}-2\,\eta_{t}\,\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t}\cdot\left({\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}-\bar{{\mathbf{g}}}^{t}\right)+\eta_{t}^{2}\left\lVert{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}-\bar{{\mathbf{g}}}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right)\ ,\qquad

\displaystyle\mbox{$\cdot$ is the dot product}\ ,

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\left(\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}-2\,\eta_{t}\,\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t}\cdot{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}+\eta_{t}^{2}\left\lVert{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}-\bar{{\mathbf{g}}}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right)\ ,\qquad

\displaystyle\mbox{$\sum_{i}\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t}=0$}\ .

We now bound each of the terms following techniques similar to that of
Yu et al. 2019
.
Consider
\left\lVert{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}-\bar{{\mathbf{g}}}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}
:

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}-\bar{{\mathbf{g}}}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}-\nabla f({\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{t})+\nabla f({\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{t})-\bar{\nabla}f({\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{t})+\bar{\nabla}f({\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{t})-\bar{{\mathbf{g}}}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\ ,

(19)

\displaystyle\leq 3\left\lVert{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}-\nabla f({\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{t})\right\rVert^{2}+3\left\lVert\nabla f({\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{t})-\bar{\nabla}f({\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{t})\right\rVert^{2}+3\left\lVert\bar{\nabla}f({\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{t})-\bar{{\mathbf{g}}}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\ ,

where

(a+b+c)^{2}\leq 3(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2})

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\left\lVert{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}-\nabla f({\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{t})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq m\,\sigma^{2}\ .

(20)

2) using

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\left\lVert\nabla f({\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{t})-\bar{\nabla}f({\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{t})\right\rVert^{2}

(21)

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\left\lVert\bar{\nabla}f({\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{t})-\bar{{\mathbf{g}}}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}

(22)

Altogether for some constant

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{i}\left\lVert{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}-\bar{{\mathbf{g}}}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\leq C\left(m\,\sigma^{2}+L^{2}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\right)\ .

(23)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{i}\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t}\cdot{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}\right]

Putting everything together,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\hat{\bm{\Delta}}^{t+1}\right\rVert^{2}

(25)

From Lemma

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}^{t+1}\right\rVert^{2}\right]

A.3

We consider a

where all workers are initialized to the same point, the data chunks are i.i.d.

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}_{i}^{t}-\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}_{i}^{t-\tau}\right]

{\mathbf{d}}_{i}^{t}
between different workers diminishes, ensuring consensus. In this section, with a slight abuse of notation, we define

Lemma 2
.

In a homogeneous setup as defined above, the expected value of the weight drift is independent of the worker,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}_{i}^{t}-\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}_{i}^{t-\tau}\right]={\mathbf{D}}^{t}

Proof.

Considering a single local update:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}_{i}^{k+1}-{\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{k}\right]

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}\!\left[\eta_{k}\,{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{k}\right]=-\eta_{k}\nabla f({\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{k})\ ,\qquad

\displaystyle\mbox{${\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{k}$ is unbiased}\ .

(27)

This is independent and identical for each worker if
{\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{k}={\mathbf{w}}^{k}
for all
i
. This argument can be extended to multiple steps for a homogeneous setup due to the same initialization, i.i.d.
data samples, and identical optimizer hyperparameters. Intuitively, one can see that a particular weight trajectory is equally probable for all workers in a homogeneous setup.

Additionally, the
EMA
update parameters (
{\mathbf{d}}_{i}^{0}
and
\lambda_{t}
) are also identical across workers.
Therefore, the average weight drift
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}_{i}^{t}-\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}_{i}^{t-\tau}\right]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}_{i}^{t}-{\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{t-\tau}\right]+\mathbb{E}\!\left[{\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{t-\tau}-\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}_{i}^{t-\tau}\right]
is independent of the worker
i
.
∎

Theorem 4
.

Consider a homogeneous setup where the average staleness
{\mathbf{D}}^{t}
is bounded and its drift
\bm{\alpha}_{t}\coloneqq{\mathbf{D}}^{t}-{\mathbf{D}}^{t-1}
is diminishing,
i.e
.
,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\left\lVert\bm{\alpha}_{t}\right\rVert=0
. Then, the
EMA
based delay correction with
\lambda_{t}
satisfying

\sum_{t}\lambda_{t}=\infty
,
\sum_{t}\lambda_{t}^{2}<\infty
, and
\sum_{t}\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\alpha}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}}{\lambda_{t}}<\infty
ensures consensus,
i.e
.
,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]=0
.

Proof.

Now consider
\bm{\Delta}^{t}_{i}
:

\displaystyle\bm{\Delta}^{t}_{i}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{w}}_{i}^{t}-\bar{{\mathbf{w}}}_{i}^{t}\ ,

(28)

\displaystyle=\bar{{\mathbf{w}}}^{t-\tau}+{\mathbf{d}}_{i}^{t}-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i}\left(\bar{{\mathbf{w}}}^{t-\tau}+{\mathbf{d}}_{i}^{t}\right)\ ,\qquad

\displaystyle\mbox{\lx@glossaries@gls@link{acronym}{EMA}{{{}}EMA} }\ ,

\displaystyle={\mathbf{d}}_{i}^{t}-\bar{{\mathbf{d}}}_{i}^{t}\ .

Note that,
\bar{{\mathbf{d}}}_{i}^{t}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i}{\mathbf{d}}_{i}^{t}
is the empirical estimate of
\mathbb{E}\!\left[{\mathbf{d}}_{i}^{t}\right]
. Following the arguments from Lemma
2
, we can see that
\mathbb{E}\!\left[{\mathbf{d}}_{i}^{t}\right]={\mathbf{D}}^{t}
for all workers. If
{\mathbf{D}}^{t}
is a constant, then the standard stochastic approximation theory
Robbins and Monro 1951
yields the desired result. However, since
{\mathbf{D}}^{t}
varies with time, we need an additional assumption on the interplay between the drift and the
EMA
coefficient, that
\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\alpha}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}}{\lambda_{t}}\to 0
and carefully bound the consensus error.

Substituting
\bar{{\mathbf{d}}}_{i}^{t}={\mathbf{D}}^{t}
, together with the
EMA
update:

\displaystyle\bm{\Delta}^{t}_{i}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{d}}_{i}^{t}-{\mathbf{D}}^{t}\ ,

(29)

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda_{t})\,{\mathbf{d}}_{i}^{t-1}+\lambda_{t}\left(\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}_{i}^{t}-\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}_{i}^{t-\tau}\right)-{\mathbf{D}}^{t}\ ,

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda_{t})\left({\mathbf{d}}_{i}^{t-1}-{\mathbf{D}}^{t-1}+{\mathbf{D}}^{t-1}-{\mathbf{D}}^{t}\right)+\lambda_{t}\left(\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}_{i}^{t}-\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}_{i}^{t-\tau}-{\mathbf{D}}^{t}\right)\ ,

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda_{t})\,\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t-1}-(1-\lambda_{t})\,\bm{\alpha}_{t}+\lambda_{t}\,\bm{\xi}_{i}^{t}\ ,\qquad\qquad\mbox{$\bm{\xi}_{i}^{t}=\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}_{i}^{t}-\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}_{i}^{t-\tau}-{\mathbf{D}}^{t}$}\ .

Now square both sides:

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}=\

\displaystyle(1-\lambda_{t})^{2}\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t-1}\right\rVert^{2}+(1-\lambda_{t})^{2}\left\lVert\bm{\alpha}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}+\lambda_{t}^{2}\left\lVert\bm{\xi}_{i}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}

(30)

\displaystyle+2(1-\lambda_{t})\lambda_{t}\,\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t-1}\cdot\bm{\xi}_{i}^{t}-2(1-\lambda_{t})^{2}\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t-1}\cdot\bm{\alpha}_{t}-2(1-\lambda_{t})\lambda_{t}\,\bm{\alpha}_{t}\cdot\bm{\xi}_{i}^{t}\ ,

where
\cdot
is the dot-product.Consider the first cross term:

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t-1}\cdot\bm{\xi}_{i}^{t}\right]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t-1}\cdot\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bm{\xi}_{i}^{t}\mid\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t-1}\right]\right]=0\ ,\qquad\mbox{due to Lemma\penalty\ \ref{lem:ema1}}\ .

(31)

Using Young’s inequality for the other cross terms,
i.e
.
,
2ab\leq\epsilon a^{2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}b^{2}
for any
\epsilon>0
, we can write:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\leq\

\displaystyle(1-\lambda_{t})^{2}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t-1}\right\rVert^{2}\right]+(1-\lambda_{t})^{2}\left\lVert\bm{\alpha}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}+\lambda_{t}^{2}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert\bm{\xi}_{i}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]

(32)

\displaystyle+(1-\lambda_{t})^{2}\left(\epsilon\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t-1}\right\rVert^{2}\right]+\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\alpha}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}}{\epsilon}\right)+(1-\lambda_{t})\lambda_{t}\left(\epsilon\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert\bm{\xi}_{i}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]+\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\alpha}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}}{\epsilon}\right)\ .

The term
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert\bm{\xi}_{i}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]
can be bounded due to bounded delay
\tau
. By setting
\epsilon=\lambda_{t}
, the above can be simplified as:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda_{t})^{2}(1+\lambda_{t})\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t-1}\right\rVert^{2}\right]+\frac{A}{\lambda_{t}}\left\lVert\bm{\alpha}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}

(33)

\displaystyle\quad+B\left(\left\lVert\bm{\alpha}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}+\lambda_{t}^{2}\right)\ ,\qquad\mbox{for some constants $A,B>0$}\ ,

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda_{t})\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t-1}\right\rVert^{2}\right]+\frac{A}{\lambda_{t}}\left\lVert\bm{\alpha}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}+B\left(\left\lVert\bm{\alpha}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}+\lambda_{t}^{2}\right)\ ,\qquad\mbox{$0<\lambda_{t}<1$}\ .

Since
\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\alpha}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}}{\lambda_{t}}
,
\left\lVert\bm{\alpha}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}
, and
\lambda_{t}^{2}
are diminishing, the above can be shown to be a contraction and therefore,
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\to 0
following the classical stochastic approximation theory
Robbins and Monro 1951
;
Robbins and Siegmund 1971
. Consequently, the consensus error vanishes:

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{i}\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]=\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}_{i}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\to 0\ .

(34)

∎

Note that the
EMA
update and the arguments in the theorem are elementwise, hence, they naturally extend to the
PP
with sparse averaging setup. This, together with Theorem
3
and the convergence proof of
SGD
, provides a theoretical justification for convergence for our method in the
PP
setup with sparse averaging on expectation, despite a fixed delay.

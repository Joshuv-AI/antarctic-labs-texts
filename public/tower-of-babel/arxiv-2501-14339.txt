Which coprime graphs are divisor graphs?
Xuanlong Ma, Liangliang Zhai, Nan Gao School of Science, Xi’an Shiyou University, Xi’an 710065, P.R. China

Abstract
For a finite group G G , the coprime graph Γ ⁡ ( G ) \Gamma(G) of G G is a graph with vertex set G G , in which two distinct vertices a a and b b are adjacent if the order of a a and the order of b b are coprime. In this paper, we first give a characterization for which generalized lexicographic products are divisor graphs. As applications, we show that every of power graph, reduced power graph and order graph is a divisor graph, which also implies the main result in [N. Takshak, A. Sehgal, A. Malik, Power graph of a finite group is always divisor graph, Asian-Eur. J. Math. 16 (2023), ID: 2250236]. Then, we prove that the coprime graph of a group is a generalized lexicographic product, and give two characterizations for which coprime graphs are divisor graphs. We also describe the groups G G with | π ⁡ ( G ) | ≤ 4 |\pi(G)|\leq 4 , whose coprime graph is a divisor graph. Finally, we classify the finite groups G G so that Γ ⁡ ( G ) \Gamma(G) is a divisor graph if G G is a nilpotent group, a dihedral group, a generalized quaternion group, a symmetric group, an alternating group, a direct product of two non-trivial groups, and a sporadic simple group. Key words: Coprime graph; Divisor graph; Finite group 2020 MSC: 05C25

Generalized Lexicographic Products

In this section, we give a characterization for which generalized lexicographic products are divisor graphs. As applications, we show that every of power graph, reduced power graph and order graph is a divisor graph.

We first give the definition of a generalized lexicographic
product, which was first introduced by Sabidussi
[
26
]

Given a graph
\mathcal{H}
and a family of graphs
\mathbb{F}
indexed by

\mathbb{F}=\{\mathcal{F}_{v}:v\in V(\mathcal{H})\},

the

of

\mathcal{H}[\mathbb{F}]

\{(v,w):v\in V(\mathcal{H}),w\in V(\mathcal{F}_{v})\}
and edge set

\{\{(v_{1},w_{1}),(v_{2},w_{2})\}:\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\in E(\mathcal{H}),\text{ or $v_{1}=v_{2}$ and }\{w_{1},w_{2}\}\in E(\mathcal{F}_{v_{1}})\}.

Theorem 3.1

\mathcal{H}[\mathbb{F}]
is a divisor graph if and only if
\mathcal{H}
is a divisor graph and for every
v\in V(\mathcal{H})
,
\mathcal{F}_{v}
is also a divisor graph.

Proof.
Suppose first that
\mathcal{H}
is a divisor graph and
\mathcal{F}_{v}
is also a divisor graph for every

.
By Theorem
2.6
, let

be a transitive orientation of
\mathcal{H}
, and let
\mathcal{D}_{v}
be a transitive orientation of
\mathcal{F}_{v}
for every
v\in V(\mathcal{H})
.
For
\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\in E(\mathcal{H})
,
w_{1}\in V(\mathcal{F}_{v_{1}})
and
w_{2}\in V(\mathcal{F}_{v_{2}})
, let the direction of

\{(v_{1},w_{1}),(v_{2},w_{2})\}
be the direction of
\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
. Also, for
\{w_{1},w_{2}\}\in E(\mathcal{F}_{v_{1}})
,
let the direction of

\{(v_{1},w_{1}),(v_{1},w_{2})\}
be the direction of
\{w_{1},w_{2}\}
in

. Then we obtain a orientation of

is transitive, and so

For the converse, let

be a divisor graph and let
\mathcal{D}
be its a transitive orientation. Then it is easy to see that the subgraph of

\mathcal{F}_{v}

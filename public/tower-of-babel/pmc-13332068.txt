Carbon nanotube reinforced soybean oil for sustainable machining of Monel 400: experimental investigation and FIS-based optimization
Authors: Mustafa Abdullah, Pardeep Singh Bains, Kumel K. Nagori, T. Ramachandran, Shahbaz Juneja, Hrushikesh Sarangi, Jasgurpreet Singh Chohan, Pradeep Kumar Singh, A Bhowmik, Yalew Tamene
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The present study investigates the effectiveness of carbon nanotube (CNT)-reinforced soybean oil as a nano-lubricant under minimum quantity lubrication (MQL) for machining Monel 400 alloy. CNTs were dispersed in soybean oil at varying concentrations (0–0.8 vol%), and their thermo-physical properties were evaluated through wettability (contact angle) and rheological (viscosity) analyses. The results identified 0.5 vol% CNT as the optimal concentration, exhibiting improved wettability and enhanced viscosity, which are favorable for stable lubricating film formation. Machining experiments were conducted under different lubrication environments, namely dry, compressed air, pure soybean oil, and CNT-enriched soybean oil. The results demonstrated that the CNT-based nano-lubricant significantly improved machining performance. Relative to dry machining conditions, substantial improvements were observed across all performance metrics. Surface roughness decreased by 73.7%, while cutting force and cutting temperature were reduced by 56.1% and 59.0%, respectively. In addition, tool wear exhibited a significant decline of 82.1%, indicating enhanced machining efficiency and tool longevity. These improvements are attributed to the superior tribological characteristics of CNTs, including tribo-film formation, enhanced thermal conductivity, reduced interfacial shear resistance, and improved load-bearing capacity. Furthermore, a Taguchi L16 experimental design integrated with a Fuzzy Mamdani inference system was employed to determine the optimal machining conditions. The best-performing combination of machining conditions—cutting speed of 70 m/min, feed rate of 0.05 mm/tooth, and depth of cut of 0.15 mm under CNT-MQL lubrication—resulted in the maximum multi-performance characteristics index (MPCI), achieving a value of 0.78. Overall, CNT-reinforced soybean oil demonstrates strong potential as an effective nano-lubricant for improving machining performance of nickel-based alloys under MQL conditions. The findings of this research demonstrate that nano-lubrication incorporating carbon nanotubes (CNTs) serves as a promising strategy to significantly improve machining performance, prolong tool durability, and achieve superior surface finish when processing hard-to-machine materials.

Introduction

Nickel-based alloys are extensively utilized in high-performance engineering applications because they maintain their strength and structural integrity even when exposed to severe conditions, including elevated temperatures, high pressures, and aggressive corrosive environments. Among these, Monel 400, a nickel–copper alloy, has gained significant industrial importance in marine, aerospace, and chemical processing industries owing to its excellent corrosion resistance, high strength, and superior toughness1. Although Monel 400 offers several beneficial properties, it is widely recognized as a challenging material to machine. This is primarily attributed to its strong tendency to work harden, coupled with high ductility and poor thermal conductivity. As a result, machining processes often encounter accelerated tool degradation, increased heat generation, and compromised surface quality. Therefore, enhancing machinability while ensuring sustainability has become a major concern for modern manufacturing industries.

In recent decades, numerous approaches have been explored to enhance the machining performance of nickel-based alloys. One of the most commonly employed methods is the use of cutting fluids in flood lubrication mode, which helps in minimizing friction while effectively carrying away heat from the cutting interface2. The widespread reliance on traditional cutting fluids has become increasingly problematic, as their hazardous composition poses risks to both human health and the environment, while also creating difficulties in disposal and adding to overall operational expenses. With the increasing emphasis on green and sustainable manufacturing, industries are shifting toward lubrication techniques that comply with ISO 14,000 environmental standards3. In this context, minimum quantity lubrication (MQL) has gained considerable attention as an efficient substitute, as it drastically minimizes the use of cutting fluids while still ensuring adequate lubrication performance. Moreover, the adoption of MQL aligns well with the core dimensions of sustainability by enhancing cost efficiency, reducing environmental impact, and promoting safer working conditions4.

Vegetable-based oils are increasingly being considered as viable alternatives to traditional mineral cutting fluids, primarily because they are environmentally friendly, derived from renewable sources, and offer enhanced lubrication performance owing to their long-chain fatty acid composition5. Among various vegetable oils, soybean oil has attracted attention because of its favorable thermo-physical properties, such as high viscosity index, good lubricity, and relatively higher oxidation stability. These properties make soybean oil a suitable candidate for MQL applications. Vegetable oils tend to lose their stability when exposed to high temperatures, as they are susceptible to oxidation and thermal breakdown. This can adversely affect their effectiveness as lubricants, particularly in high-speed machining conditions6. Therefore, enhancing the thermo-physical properties of vegetable oils is essential to fully exploit their potential as sustainable lubricants.

Recent progress in nanotechnology has created promising opportunities to improve the effectiveness of vegetable oil–based lubricants by integrating nanoscale particles into their formulation7. The addition of nanoparticles significantly enhances both the frictional behavior and thermal characteristics of conventional fluids, ultimately resulting in improved machining efficiency and performance. Zhang et al.8 investigated MoS₂ nanoparticle-enriched vegetable oils and reported significant enhancement in lubrication characteristics. Similarly, Jia et al.9 observed that nano-enhanced vegetable oils resulted in reduced surface roughness and lower grinding forces. Singh et al.10 reported that graphene-infused nanofluids promote the development of a stable tribological layer between the cutting tool and the workpiece, which significantly enhances overall machining efficiency. A couple of years ago, Khatai et al.11 reported that hard turning is a viable alternative to grinding for machining hardened steels, offering reduced time, cost, and environmental impact, though it remains challenging due to high tool wear and temperatures. This study evaluates a dual-nozzle MQL system using ZnO nano-cutting fluid in hard turning of AISI 52,100 steel, focusing on flank wear, surface roughness, temperature, power, and noise. Results show low flank wear (0.027–0.095 mm). Statistical analyses (main effects, interaction plots, ANOVA) and WASPAS optimization identified optimal conditions: 0.3 mm depth of cut, 0.05 mm/rev feed, 210 m/min speed, and 50 mL/hr flow rate.

In recent times, nanomaterials derived from carbon, particularly graphene and carbon nanotubes (CNTs), have gained significant prominence due to their outstanding mechanical robustness, excellent heat transfer capability, and enhanced tribological performance12,13. CNTs, in particular, exhibit a unique tubular morphology that enables a hybrid lubrication mechanism involving sliding, limited rolling, and load-bearing effects at the tool–work interface. This behavior contributes to reduced frictional resistance and improved machining efficiency. Moreover, the layered graphitic nature of CNTs facilitates the development of a protective tribological film on the machined surface. This film acts as a barrier that reduces direct contact between metallic interfaces, thereby improving the overall surface quality and integrity. Moreover, CNTs facilitate efficient heat dissipation because of their elevated thermal conductivity, thereby reducing cutting temperature. Previous investigations by Şirin et al.14 demonstrated that nano-MQL environments significantly improve machining performance as compared to dry and conventional MQL conditions. Similarly, Öndin et al.15 reported that MWCNT-based nanofluids effectively enhance machinability by advancing lubrication and thermal dissipation characteristics. The application of nano-MQL systems has increased global attention because of their ability to improve machining efficiency while minimizing environmental impact. However, the performance of nano-lubricants strongly depends on their thermo-physical properties, such as wettability and viscosity. Soybean oil, when reinforced with CNTs, is expected to exhibit enhanced heat transfer capability, improved lubricity, and superior stability compared to conventional lubricants. The high surface energy and aspect ratio of CNTs enable better dispersion and interaction with the base fluid, leading to improved tribological behavior.

Although several studies have explored nanoparticle-enhanced lubricants, limited research has been conducted on CNT-reinforced soybean oil for machining Monel 400, particularly in the context of sustainability assessment. Moreover, the integration of advanced decision-making techniques such as fuzzy logic for evaluating multi-performance characteristics remains relatively unexplored. Existing CNT-based nano-lubrication studies largely focus on performance enhancement under specific conditions, with limited emphasis on systematic thermo-physical characterization and integrated multi-response optimization. In contrast, the present study establishes a comprehensive framework that bridges nano-lubricant development, property characterization, machining evaluation, and intelligent decision-making. Therefore, a comprehensive investigation is required to assess the combined effects of CNT-based nano-lubricants on machining performance, economic feasibility, and environmental impact. In the present study, CNT-enriched soybean oil has been developed and its lubricating performance has been evaluated during the machining of Monel 400 under MQL conditions. Various machining responses, including surface roughness, cutting force, cutting temperature, and tool wear, have been analyzed. Finally, a Fuzzy-Mamdani model has been employed to determine the most suitable machining conditions based on a multi-performance characteristics index. A critical comparison with recent literature has also been incorporated to highlight the advantages of CNT-based bio-lubricants over conventional and other nanofluid systems in terms of enhanced wettability, thermal conductivity, and tribological performance. The major contributions of the present study can be summarized as follows: (a) Development and characterization of CNT-reinforced soybean oil nano-lubricant with analysis of wettability and viscosity. (b) Experimental investigation of machining performance during MQL-assisted milling of Monel 400. (c) Application of a Fuzzy-Mamdani model for multi-criteria decision-making and identification of optimal machining conditions. (d) Establishment of an integrated and sustainable machining framework combining material design, process optimization, and intelligent modeling, which distinguishes this work from existing CNT-based lubrication studies.

Materials and methods

Preparation of nano-lubricants

Multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs) were employed as nanoparticle additives for the preparation of the nano-lubricant in the present investigation. The CNTs were procured from a commercial supplier and possessed an average outer diameter of 20–40 nm, length ranging from 5 to 15 μm, and purity greater than 95%. These unique structural features make CNTs highly effective in enhancing both tribological and thermal properties of lubricants. The development of CNT-enriched soybean oil was carried out by varying the nanoparticle concentration from 0 to 0.8 vol%, with increments of 0.2 vol%. This concentration range and step size were selected based on standard nanofluid practices to systematically evaluate performance while avoiding instability at higher loadings, such as agglomeration and excessive viscosity. A sequential two-stage approach was employed to synthesize the nanofluid. In the first stage, a precise amount of carbon nanotubes (CNTs) was measured and introduced into soybean oil, followed by mechanical agitation using a magnetic stirrer at 800 rpm for 40 min to ensure uniform initial dispersion. Strong intermolecular Van der Waals interactions between carbon nanotubes promote their clustering, leading to agglomeration that compromises the stability of their dispersion.

To address the issue of uneven particle distribution, a probe-based ultrasonication system (Cromtech 200) was employed to achieve uniform dispersion. The treatment was carried out at an operating frequency of 20 kHz using an intermittent pulse mode of 3 s active sonication followed by 2 s of rest, continued over a total period of 2 h. This process effectively disintegrated CNT agglomerates and facilitated uniform distribution of nanoparticles within the base fluid. The complete synthesis procedure of the CNT-based nano-lubricant is illustrated in Fig. 1, highlighting the sequential steps involved in mixing, stirring, and ultrasonication. Nanofluid stability is primarily determined by the balance between intermolecular attractive forces, such as Van der Waals interactions, and repulsive electrostatic forces that prevent particle aggregation16. In the case of CNTs, their high aspect ratio and tendency to form entangled networks significantly influence dispersion behavior and viscosity characteristics. The attractive forces promote agglomeration and sedimentation of nanoparticles, whereas repulsive forces help maintain dispersion stability. In addition, gravitational effects can further contribute to nanoparticle settling over time.

Although surfactants are commonly used to improve the stability of nanofluids, they were deliberately excluded in the present study to maintain environmental compatibility. Many surfactants are derived from petrochemical sources and may introduce ecological concerns, including toxicity and persistence in the environment. Furthermore, their production often involves energy-intensive processes, increasing the overall carbon footprint. Therefore, the preparation of CNT-enriched soybean oil without surfactants ensures a more sustainable and eco-friendly lubrication system suitable for green machining applications.

Wettability characterization of CNT-based nano-lubricants

Wettability describes how effectively a lubricant can disperse and form a continuous film over a solid surface, enabling efficient lubrication and improved heat dissipation at the interface between the cutting tool and the workpiece. It is typically assessed by measuring the angle created at the interface where a liquid droplet meets a solid surface. The equilibrium contact angle is determined by the interplay between the internal cohesive interactions of the liquid molecules and the adhesive interactions occurring at the interface between the liquid and the solid surface. A smaller contact angle reflects superior wetting ability, allowing the fluid to spread more uniformly across the surface, thereby improving its lubrication effectiveness. The wetting behavior of soybean oil infused with CNTs was evaluated using a drop shape analysis system (KRUSS DSA25). The contact angle was determined using the sessile drop technique by placing a liquid droplet onto a thoroughly cleaned glass surface. Before conducting the tests, the glass surface was meticulously cleaned with acetone to eliminate any impurities or residues. Under normal laboratory conditions, a precisely measured 2 µL lubricant droplet was carefully applied to the substrate using a micro-syringe.

The variation of contact angle with CNT concentration is presented in Fig. 2. Which clearly demonstrates the influence of CNT concentration on the wettability behavior of soybean oil, where a lower contact angle corresponds to improved spreading and lubrication capability. It was observed that the contact angle decreased significantly with increasing CNT concentration up to 0.5 vol%, indicating improved wettability. Specifically, the contact angle reduced from 41.2° for pure soybean oil to 24.6° at 0.5 vol% CNT concentration. This enhancement is primarily due to stronger interfacial bonding along with the structural disjoining pressure effects arising from the uniform dispersion of CNTs. However, beyond the optimal concentration, the contact angle exhibited a slight increase. At 0.8 vol% CNT concentration, the contact angle increased to 27.8°, which can be attributed to increased viscosity and nanoparticle agglomeration. At higher concentrations, CNT entanglement restricts fluid mobility, thereby limiting spreading behavior. Furthermore, CNTs influence wettability through their high surface energy and ability to modify interfacial interactions. Their elongated tubular structure allows partial alignment at the interface, enhancing adhesion with the substrate. However, excessive CNT content leads to clustering, negatively affecting dispersion and spreading. Thus, the trend observed in Fig. 2 confirms that 0.5 vol% CNT provides optimal wettability, ensuring effective lubricant spreading and improved machining performance.

Rheological behavior and flow dynamics

The flow characteristics of nano-lubricants play a crucial role in determining their effectiveness in machining applications, as they influence lubricant transport, film formation, and stability at the tool–work interface. In this study, the rheological properties of CNT-enriched soybean oil were evaluated using an Anton Paar rheometer (MCR-102 model). A constant sample volume of 7 mL was used for each test, and measurements were conducted at 30 °C under a steady shear rate of 100 s⁻¹. The variation in viscosity with CNT concentration is presented in Fig. 3. The results indicate that viscosity increases with the addition of CNTs, reaching a maximum at 0.5 vol%. The viscosity increased by approximately 41.38% compared to pure soybean oil, which can be attributed to the formation of interconnected CNT networks within the base fluid. These networks enhance intermolecular interactions and increase resistance to flow, thereby improving load-bearing capacity. Beyond this optimal concentration, a slight reduction in viscosity was observed. At higher CNT concentrations (0.8 vol%), the viscosity decreased due to nanoparticle agglomeration and non-uniform dispersion, which disrupt the effective interaction between CNTs and the base fluid. The high aspect ratio of CNTs promotes entanglement at moderate concentrations, enhancing rheological properties; however, excessive clustering at higher loadings adversely affects flow behavior and stability. Furthermore, the nano-lubricant exhibited near-Newtonian behavior under the applied shear rate, indicating stable flow characteristics suitable for MQL applications. Overall, the 0.5 vol% CNT concentration provides an optimal balance between enhanced viscosity and adequate flowability, ensuring effective lubricant delivery and stable film formation during machining.

Machining framework and experimental design

The experimental machining trials were performed on a computer numerically controlled (CNC) three-axis vertical machining center, specifically the HARDINGE VMC 600 II model. All milling experiments were carried out using a PVD TiAlN-coated carbide insert (VP15TF grade) manufactured by Mitsubishi. The cutting tool geometry consisted of a rake angle of 10°, clearance angle of 7°, and a nose radius of 0.8 mm, as per manufacturer specifications, ensuring stable cutting performance and effective chip evacuation. Monel 400, a nickel–copper alloy recognized for its high mechanical strength and superior resistance to corrosion, was chosen as the work material for this investigation. The test samples were fabricated as rectangular blocks measuring 60 mm in length, 50 mm in width, and 10 mm in thickness. The corresponding thermo-mechanical characteristics of Monel 400 are summarized in Table 1. All experiments were conducted under controlled laboratory conditions (room temperature ~ 25 ± 2 °C and relative humidity ~ 50–60%) to minimize environmental variability.

The machining trials were performed in a two-stage sequence. Initially, a comparative assessment was carried out to examine the performance of various lubrication conditions, including dry machining, compressed air cooling, pure soybean oil, and CNT-reinforced soybean oil. The cutting conditions were maintained at fixed values, with a cutting velocity of 70 m/min, a feed per tooth of 0.07 mm, and a depth of cut set to 0.3 mm. A specially designed single-nozzle MQL setup was utilized to precisely supply the lubricant to the machining interface. Throughout the experimental trials, the MQL system was operated under fixed conditions, with its parameters consistently held unchanged, as detailed in Table 2. The parameters of the MQL system were selected based on manufacturer guidelines, established literature, and preliminary trial experiments. Initial settings were adopted from previous studies to ensure effective atomization and delivery of the nano-lubricant, and were subsequently fine-tuned to achieve stable spray characteristics and efficient penetration into the tool–chip interface. Thus, the selected parameters represent optimal and practically feasible conditions for consistent lubrication performance. To ensure repeatability and reliability of the results, each experiment was conducted three times, and the average values were reported. Statistical validation was performed using standard deviation analysis, and error bars were incorporated in the corresponding figures to represent data variability.

In the subsequent stage, a Taguchi design approach employing an L16 orthogonal array was adopted to identify optimal machining parameters that ensure sustainability. The study was structured around four key process variables—cutting speed, feed rate, depth of cut, and lubrication conditions—with each parameter evaluated across four distinct levels. The comprehensive experimental design matrix is outlined in Table 3. The selection of process parameters and their respective levels was based on relevant literature, tool manufacturer recommendations, and machine capability constraints to ensure practical feasibility. The Taguchi orthogonal array was employed to achieve a balanced experimental design, enabling systematic evaluation of multiple factors with a reduced number of experiments while maintaining statistical reliability. It should be emphasized that the nano-lubricant incorporated an optimal CNT concentration of 0.5 vol%, which was determined from the characterization analysis detailed in Sect. 2.2. This concentration corresponds to the lowest contact angle and highest viscosity, ensuring enhanced wettability and stable lubricating film formation. A higher viscosity promotes the formation of a stronger lubricating layer, while a lower contact angle ensures improved spreading of the lubricant over the tool–work interface17. The effectiveness of the machining process was assessed using a comprehensive set of performance indicators, encompassing surface finish, cutting forces, thermal conditions, and tool degradation characteristics.

Multi-scale measurement of machining responses

The evaluation of machining performance was carried out through a comprehensive multi-scale measurement approach, incorporating advanced instrumentation to ensure high accuracy and repeatability of results. This study focuses on evaluating key machining performance indicators such as surface finish quality, machining forces, thermal effects in the cutting zone, and progression of tool degradation. These responses collectively provide a holistic understanding of the machining behavior under different lubrication environments. The integrity and finish of the machined surface were characterized using a high-precision three-dimensional optical profilometer (KLA Tencor MicroXAM-100). To enhance the accuracy and consistency of the measurements, surface roughness (Ra) was evaluated at five distinct points across the machined surface, and the mean of these readings was used for subsequent analysis. This approach minimizes localized measurement errors and provides a representative evaluation of surface integrity. The forces produced along different directions during the milling operation were quantified using a KISTLER 9170 A piezoelectric dynamometer. The dynamometer system consists of a force sensor, multi-channel charge amplifier (5238B), and DynoWare software for real-time data acquisition. During machining, forces acting along the three Cartesian directions (Fx, Fy, and Fz) were recorded, and the resultant cutting force was computed using the following relation:

This resultant force provides a comprehensive measure of the mechanical load experienced during cutting.

The cutting temperature was recorded using a MICRO-EPSILON TIM 8 infrared thermal imaging system, which enables non-contact measurement and can monitor temperature fields spanning from − 20 °C to 900 °C. The emissivity value was set to 0.5 in accordance with established literature for nickel-based alloys19. Thermal images were recorded during machining, and the maximum temperature in the cutting zone was extracted for analysis. The extent of tool wear was assessed through microscopic examination using a ZEISS SteREO Discovery.V20 optical microscope. Because the wear developed irregularly during the machining of Monel 400, the maximum flank wear (VBmax) was selected as the key indicator for tool wear evaluation. This provides a more accurate representation of tool degradation compared to average wear measurements. A HITACHI TM3000 scanning electron microscope was utilized to perform an in-depth examination of wear characteristics and chip morphology. SEM analysis enabled the identification of dominant wear mechanisms such as adhesion, abrasion, and coating delamination. Additionally, the morphology of chips generated under different lubrication conditions was examined to understand the influence of lubrication on chip formation behavior. This integrated framework ensures a systematic evaluation of machining performance and optimization routine.

Results and discussion

Influence of lubrication strategies on machining performance

Surface roughness analysis

Surface finish serves as a key measure of machining effectiveness, as it significantly affects the operational performance, durability under cyclic loading, and friction–wear characteristics of manufactured parts. It is strongly influenced by machining conditions, cutting tool design, and the applied lubrication strategy19,20. This study systematically examines how various lubrication approaches—namely dry machining, compressed air cooling, pure soybean oil, and CNT-reinforced soybean oil—affect the resulting surface finish of Monel 400. To specifically evaluate the influence of lubrication, all other machining variables were maintained unchanged throughout the experiments. The resulting surface roughness obtained under various lubrication environments is presented in Fig. 5. The results clearly indicate that the CNT-based nano-lubricant significantly improves surface finish compared to other lubrication environments. Under dry machining conditions, the highest surface roughness value of approximately 1.18 μm was recorded, which can be attributed to severe friction, elevated temperature, and material adhesion at the tool–work interface.

Fig. 5Comparative surface roughness analysis under different lubrication environments.

Comparative surface roughness analysis under different lubrication environments.

The application of compressed air resulted in a moderate reduction in surface roughness to 0.62 μm, primarily due to limited cooling effects. However, the absence of effective lubrication still led to considerable friction and surface irregularities. The use of pure soybean oil under MQL conditions further reduced the surface roughness to 0.34 μm, owing to its inherent lubricity and ability to form a thin oil film that minimizes direct contact between the tool and workpiece. The use of CNT-infused soybean oil led to a remarkable enhancement in surface quality, achieving a surface roughness as low as 0.31 μm. This represents a substantial decrease of 73.7% relative to dry machining, 50.0% compared to compressed air cooling, and 8.8% when benchmarked against conventional soybean oil lubrication. This enhanced performance can be attributed to the synergistic effects of CNTs within the lubricant.

The enhanced surface quality observed with CNT-assisted lubrication arises from several underlying mechanisms. Primarily, carbon nanotubes promote the development of a consistent and adherent tribological film at the interface between the cutting tool and the workpiece. This film serves as a protective layer, minimizing direct interaction between metallic surfaces and thereby reducing friction and wear. Secondly, the graphitic structure of CNTs provides low shear resistance, enabling smoother sliding between interacting surfaces. Additionally, the high thermal conductivity of CNTs enhances heat dissipation, thereby reducing thermal softening and adhesion of work material on the tool surface. Furthermore, CNTs contribute to a micro-polishing or mending effect, wherein nanoparticles fill surface asperities and smoothen the machined surface. Although CNTs may exhibit limited rolling behavior due to their tubular morphology, their dominant contribution arises from sliding, load-bearing, and film-forming mechanisms. These combined effects result in reduced cutting forces and vibration, ultimately leading to improved surface integrity. The marginal difference in surface roughness between soybean oil and CNT-enriched soybean oil suggests that while vegetable oil itself provides effective lubrication, the addition of CNTs further enhances performance under demanding machining conditions. These findings are consistent with previous studies21,22, which reported the formation of protective tribo-films in nano-lubrication environments leading to improved surface quality.

Cutting force analysis

Cutting force is a key indicator in machining that characterizes the mechanical engagement between the tool and the work material. It plays a crucial role in determining energy requirements, tool degradation, surface finish, and the overall effectiveness of the machining process21. Therefore, analyzing the variation of cutting force under different lubrication environments is essential for understanding the effectiveness of the developed nano-lubricant. In this investigation, the overall cutting force was determined by combining the force components recorded along the three mutually perpendicular axes, as described in Sect. 2.4. Figure 6 presents the changes in cutting force across various lubrication environments. The results clearly indicate that dry machining produced the maximum force, approximately 365.4 N, primarily due to intensified frictional interaction and material adhesion at the tool–workpiece interface when machining Monel 400.

Fig. 6Variation of resultant cutting force under different lubrication environments.

Variation of resultant cutting force under different lubrication environments.

The application of compressed air slightly reduced the cutting force to 318.6 N, primarily due to limited cooling effects. However, the absence of an effective lubricating film resulted in continued high friction at the tool–chip interface. When soybean oil was applied under MQL conditions, the cutting force decreased significantly to 182.7 N, indicating the beneficial role of vegetable oil in reducing friction through boundary lubrication. A further substantial reduction in cutting force was achieved using CNT-enriched soybean oil, where the cutting force decreased to 160.5 N. This corresponds to reductions of 56.1%, 49.6%, and 12.1% compared to dry, compressed air, and soybean oil conditions, respectively. The superior performance of CNT-based nano-lubricant can be attributed to its enhanced tribological characteristics.

The reduction in cutting force under CNT-assisted lubrication can be explained by multiple interacting mechanisms. Firstly, CNTs contribute to the formation of a robust tribological film at the tool–chip interface, which minimizes direct contact and reduces adhesion. Secondly, the inherent low shear strength of the graphitic structure of CNTs facilitates smoother sliding between the tool and workpiece, thereby lowering frictional resistance. Additionally, CNTs exhibit excellent load-bearing capacity due to their high mechanical strength, enabling them to withstand high contact pressures and distribute the applied load more uniformly across the interface. This reduces localized stress concentrations and suppresses plastic deformation of the work material. Furthermore, the high thermal conductivity of CNTs promotes efficient heat dissipation, thereby reducing thermal softening and adhesion of the workpiece material.

Although CNTs may exhibit a limited rolling effect due to their tubular structure, their dominant contribution lies in sliding, load-bearing, and tribo-film formation mechanisms. The combined effect of these mechanisms results in a significant reduction in cutting forces during machining. Moreover, it is important to note that excessive nanoparticle concentration may adversely affect lubrication performance due to agglomeration and increased viscosity, which can hinder effective penetration of the lubricant into the cutting zone23,24. Therefore, the selection of an optimal CNT concentration (0.5 vol%) is critical to achieving maximum reduction in cutting force.

Cutting zone temperature analysis

The cutting temperature generated during machining plays a crucial role in determining tool life, surface integrity, dimensional accuracy, and overall machining efficiency. Excessive temperature at the tool–chip interface accelerates tool wear, promotes adhesion, and degrades surface quality25,26. Therefore, effective control of cutting temperature is essential for sustainable machining, particularly in the case of difficult-to-machine materials such as Monel 400. In the present study, the maximum cutting temperature under different lubrication environments was evaluated using a thermal imaging camera, as described in Sect. 2.4. The thermal profiles corresponding to different lubrication conditions are presented in Fig. 7, where high-temperature zones are indicated by yellow and red regions, while lower temperatures are represented by blue and magenta regions.

Fig. 7Maximum cutting temperature under different lubrication environments.

Maximum cutting temperature under different lubrication environments.

The results indicate that dry machining produced the highest cutting temperature, reaching approximately 305 °C, due to intense friction and absence of any cooling or lubrication mechanism. The use of compressed air resulted in a moderate reduction in temperature to 268 °C, primarily due to convective cooling effects. However, the lack of lubrication continued to contribute to significant heat generation. When soybean oil was applied under MQL conditions, the cutting temperature decreased significantly to 142 °C, demonstrating the effectiveness of vegetable oil in reducing friction and dissipating heat. A further reduction was observed with CNT-enriched soybean oil, where the cutting temperature decreased to 125 °C, corresponding to reductions of 59.0%, 53.4%, and 12.0% compared to dry, compressed air, and soybean oil conditions, respectively.

The superior thermal performance of CNT-based nano-lubricant can be attributed to several key mechanisms. Firstly, CNTs possess exceptionally high thermal conductivity, which facilitates rapid heat transfer away from the cutting zone. This enhanced thermal transport capability reduces localized heat accumulation at the tool–chip interface. Secondly, the formation of a stable tribo-film by CNTs reduces frictional heat generation by minimizing direct contact between the tool and workpiece. Additionally, the improved wettability of CNT-enriched soybean oil ensures better penetration of the lubricant into the cutting zone, thereby enhancing cooling efficiency. Furthermore, CNTs contribute to improved heat dissipation through the formation of conductive pathways within the lubricant. These interconnected CNT networks act as thermal bridges, enabling efficient heat flow from the cutting zone to the surrounding environment. This mechanism is particularly effective under MQL conditions, where the quantity of lubricant is limited. The reduction in cutting temperature also leads to decreased thermal softening of the work material and reduced adhesion at the tool surface, which in turn contributes to lower tool wear and improved surface finish. These findings are consistent with previous studies27, which highlighted the role of nanoparticles in enhancing heat transfer and reducing cutting temperature during machining.

Tool degradation analysis

Tool wear is a dominant factor influencing machining performance, dimensional accuracy, and production cost28. In machining of nickel-based alloys such as Monel 400, tool degradation is accelerated due to severe adhesion, work hardening, and elevated cutting temperatures. Therefore, a detailed investigation of tool wear behavior under different lubrication environments is essential for evaluating the effectiveness of CNT-based nano-lubrication. In the present study, the maximum flank wear (VBmax) was considered as the primary indicator of tool wear due to the non-uniform wear patterns observed during machining. The variation of flank wear under different lubrication conditions is presented in Fig. 8. Each machining trial was conducted for a constant duration of 25 min to ensure consistency in wear evaluation. A substantial reduction in tool wear was achieved with soybean oil under MQL conditions, where the VBmax decreased to 0.26 mm. This improvement is attributed to the formation of a lubricating oil film that reduces direct contact between the tool and workpiece. The most significant reduction in tool wear was observed with CNT-enriched soybean oil, where the VBmax decreased to 0.14 mm, corresponding to reductions of 82.1%, 76.3%, and 46.2% compared to dry, compressed air, and soybean oil conditions, respectively. The enhanced wear resistance under CNT-based nano-lubrication can be explained through several interacting mechanisms. Firstly, CNTs facilitate the formation of a stable tribological film at the tool–work interface, which acts as a protective barrier and minimizes direct metal-to-metal contact. This tribo-film reduces adhesive wear and prevents material transfer from the workpiece to the tool. Secondly, the high load-bearing capacity of CNTs enables them to withstand elevated contact pressures, thereby reducing stress concentration and mechanical damage on the tool surface. Additionally, the graphitic structure of CNTs provides low shear resistance, promoting smoother sliding and reducing abrasion. Furthermore, the high thermal conductivity of CNTs contributes to effective heat dissipation, thereby minimizing thermal wear mechanisms such as diffusion and oxidation. Reduced cutting temperature also suppresses thermal cracking and coating delamination on the tool surface. It is important to note that excessive nanoparticle concentration may negatively impact tool life due to agglomeration and increased viscosity, which hinder effective lubrication29,30. Therefore, the selection of an optimal CNT concentration (0.5 vol%) plays a crucial role in achieving superior tool performance.

The SEM micrographs (Fig. 9) provide a clear comparative insight into the dominant wear mechanisms under different lubrication environments—namely dry, compressed air, soybean oil, and CNT-enriched soybean oil conditions. A systematic interpretation of these surfaces reveals the progressive transition from severe to mild wear regimes with the improvement in lubrication quality. Under dry machining conditions, the tool surface exhibits the most severe damage. Pronounced peeling and delamination at the cutting edge indicate intense adhesive wear coupled with plastic deformation. The presence of extensive abrasive grooves suggests the action of hard asperities or detached particles sliding across the tool surface. Additionally, the localized material adhesion (built-up layer) highlights strong interfacial bonding between the tool and workpiece, which subsequently detaches, leading to surface tearing. The absence of lubrication intensifies frictional heating, promoting diffusion and adhesion-driven wear.

Furthermore, the combined effect of high temperature and severe plastic deformation accelerates coating degradation and edge chipping, which significantly shortens tool life under dry conditions. In the case of compressed air cooling, a slight improvement is observed, but the wear mechanisms remain largely similar. The adhesion marks are still evident, although somewhat reduced in severity due to minor cooling effects. The abrasive wear tracks persist, indicating that compressed air lacks sufficient lubricity to prevent direct tool–chip contact. While the cooling effect marginally reduces thermal softening, it does not effectively suppress adhesion or abrasion, resulting in moderate wear. The absence of a lubricating film means that frictional interaction remains dominant, and therefore, only partial mitigation of wear is achieved.

With the introduction of soybean oil as a lubricant, a significant reduction in wear severity is observed. The lubricating film formed by the oil reduces direct asperity contact, thereby minimizing friction. Consequently, adhesion is considerably reduced, appearing only as small, localized patches. The peeling at the cutting edge is less severe compared to dry and compressed air conditions, indicating improved surface protection. However, some degree of wear persists due to limitations in load-carrying capacity and thermal stability of the base oil under high cutting temperatures. This indicates that although vegetable oil provides effective boundary lubrication, it is insufficient to fully withstand extreme cutting conditions without reinforcement. The most favorable wear characteristics are observed under CNT-enriched soybean oil lubrication. The addition of carbon nanotubes enhances both the thermal conductivity and load-bearing capacity of the lubricant. The SEM image shows predominantly mild abrasive marks with a notable absence of severe adhesion or delamination. The CNTs likely act as nano-scale rolling elements or form a protective tribo-film at the interface, reducing shear stress and preventing metal-to-metal contact. The peeling is minimal and more uniform, suggesting stable cutting conditions and reduced stress concentration at the tool edge. Furthermore, improved heat dissipation mitigates thermal softening, thereby enhancing tool life. Overall, the comparative SEM analysis clearly confirms that CNT-based nano-lubrication effectively suppresses dominant wear mechanisms such as adhesion and abrasion, leading to a significant improvement in tool durability and machining performance.

Fuzzy logic-based sustainability optimization framework

Development of fuzzy-mamdani inference system

In modern manufacturing, sustainability assessment requires the simultaneous consideration of multiple conflicting performance indicators. To address this complexity, a Fuzzy-Mamdani inference system was employed in the present study to evaluate and optimize machining performance under different lubrication environments. This approach enables the integration of technical responses into a single comprehensive sustainability index. Sustainability in machining is broadly defined as the ability to achieve optimal performance while minimizing resource consumption and environmental impact, ensuring long-term viability of manufacturing processes31. The Fuzzy-Mamdani model is particularly suitable for such multi-criteria decision-making problems, as it incorporates human reasoning and expert knowledge into the evaluation framework32. The implementation of the fuzzy model involves mapping multiple input variables to a single output through a set of linguistic rules. In the present study, four key machining responses—surface roughness, cutting force, cutting temperature, and tool wear—were selected as input variables. These parameters represent the technical performance of the machining process. Each input parameter was classified into three linguistic categories: Low (L), Medium (M), and High (H). The output variable, termed as the Multi-Performance Characteristics Index (MPCI), was categorized into five linguistic levels: Very Low (VL), Low (L), Medium (M), High (H), and Very High (VH) (Fig. 10). Triangular membership functions were employed to define the fuzzy sets for both input and output variables due to their simplicity and computational efficiency. Additionally, these functions are widely used in machining optimization problems as they provide a good balance between accuracy and ease of implementation. The ranges and shapes of the membership functions were defined based on the distribution of experimental data and engineering judgment, ensuring an appropriate representation of input–output relationships. The fuzzy rule base was constructed using expert knowledge, where a set of IF–THEN rules was formulated to establish relationships between input parameters and the output index. where (p) is the number of linguistic terms and (j) is the number of input variables. In this case, a total of 81 rules were generated to cover all possible combinations of input conditions. A typical fuzzy rule can be expressed as:

The developed nano-lubrication strategy not only enhances machining performance but also contributes to the advancement of environmentally conscious manufacturing practices. From an industrial perspective, the findings of this work provide a viable pathway for reducing tool wear, improving surface quality, and achieving stable machining of difficult-to-cut materials such as Monel 400 under MQL conditions. Additionally, the incorporation of real-time monitoring and AI-driven optimization techniques could further enhance the efficiency and adaptability of sustainable manufacturing processes.

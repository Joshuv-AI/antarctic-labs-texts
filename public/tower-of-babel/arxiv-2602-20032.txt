Quantum metrics from length functions on étale groupoids
Are Austad

Abstract
. We show how to construct a compact quantum metric space from a proper continuous length function on an étale groupoid with compact unit space, where the unit space additionally has the structure of a compact metric space. Using compactly supported Fourier multipliers on the reduced groupoid C ∗ C^{*} -algebra we provide a sufficient condition for verifying when we obtain a compact quantum metric space in this manner. The condition is sometimes also necessary, and is new even in the case of length functions on discrete groups. Lastly, we employ the condition to show that any AF groupoid with compact unit space can be equipped with a length function from which we obtain a compact quantum metric space, thereby providing a groupoid approach to understanding the quantum metric geometry of unital AF algebras.

1.
Introduction

The theory of compact quantum metric spaces as pioneered by Rieffel
[
36
,
38
,
39
,
40
]
extends the classical theory of compact metric spaces to the quantum (or non-commutative) setting.
Since its inception, the literature on compact quantum metric spaces has grown vast, seeing contributions from many people.
Formally, we view a compact quantum metric space as a pair
(\mathcal{X},L)
consisting of a (not necessarily closed) operator system
\mathcal{X}
and a
*
-invariant seminorm
L\colon\mathcal{X}\to[0,\infty)
with
\mathbb{C}\cdot 1_{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\ker L
and for which the associated Monge-Kantorovič metric

\displaystyle\mathrm{mk}_{L}(\phi,\psi)=\sup\{|\phi(x)-\psi(x)|\mid x\in\mathcal{X}\text{ and }L(x)\leq 1\},\quad\phi,\psi\in S(\mathcal{X})

metrizes the weak
∗
topology on
S(\mathcal{X})
, the state space of
\mathcal{X}
. That these generalize compact metric spaces is discussed in
[
38
]
.

A rich source of examples of candidates for compact quantum metric spaces come from unital spectral triples. As such, the study of compact quantum metric spaces is strongly linked to Connes’ noncommutative geometry
[
20
,
21
]
. Indeed, suppose
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},D)
is a unital spectral triple, where
\mathcal{A}
is a dense unital
*
-subalgebra of a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
A
,
\mathcal{H}
is a separable Hilbert space such that
A\subseteq B(\mathcal{H})
, and
D
is a (typically unbounded) self-adjoint operator on
\mathcal{H}
. We could use the bounded commutator condition for spectral triples to define a seminorm

(1.1)

\displaystyle L(a):=\|[D,a]\|\quad\text{for $a\in\mathcal{A}$},

and ask if
(\mathcal{A},L)
is a compact quantum metric space.
Of particular interest to us is the fact that proper
length functions on a discrete group
G
give rise to Dirac operators on
\ell^{2}(G)
. These in turn yield spectral triples in a natural way, and there are deep results regarding when this construction yields compact quantum metric spaces as above
[
37
,
9
,
32
,
18
]
.
Similar questions have been considered for compact quantum groups using generalizations of length functions
[
15
,
12
]
, or other spectral triples
[
1
,
27
]
.
Moreover, recent work has also lent insight into how we might construct compact quantum metric spaces from crossed products
[
14
,
24
,
29
,
11
]
.
Notably absent from the literature are results on quantum metric structures arising from étale groupoids.
The main focus of the present article is to remedy this. In particular, we show how an étale groupoid
\mathcal{G}
with compact unit space
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
together with

•

a proper continuous length function on
\mathcal{G}
, and

•

a metric
d
on the unit space
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
for which
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},d)
is a compact metric space

give rise to a candidate
(\mathcal{X},L)
for a compact quantum metric space. More precisely, we find an operator system
\mathcal{X}
in the reduced groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
along with a seminorm
L\colon\mathcal{X}\to[0,\infty)
from which we may attempt to obtain a compact quantum metric space.
The construction jointly generalizes the analogous constructions known for length functions on countable discrete groups, and those for compact metric spaces.

Then, having defined the correct technical notions, we are able to provide a condition which guarantees that the pair
(\mathcal{X},L)
arising from the groupoid, length function, and metric on the unit space actually is a compact quantum metric space in terms of existence of Fourier multipliers with additional properties on the reduced groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra. In some instances, this condition is also necessary, see
Theorem

3.16
. The resulting characterization is new even in the discrete group case, see
Corollary

3.19
.

Using this condition we are able to provide the first examples of compact quantum metric spaces arising from groupoids (which do not merely reduce to groups, spaces, or transformation groupoids), namely AF groupoids with compact unit spaces, see
Theorem

4.10
. Such groupoids provide models for unital AF algebras. In particular through the work of Aguilar a lot of effort has gone into understanding quantum metric aspects of unital AF algebras
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
. Using the fact that any unital AF algebra admits a groupoid model, we therefore provide a groupoid approach to understanding the quantum metric geometry of unital AF algebras.

The construction of compact quantum metric spaces arising from groupoids with length functions and metrics on the unit space should generalize the known constructions from groups with length functions, while simultaneously capturing compact metric spaces as quantum metric structures.
However, beyond this it is not obvious how to even specify an operator system
\mathcal{X}\subseteq C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
nor a seminorm, which combined is sufficiently refined to yield a compact quantum metric space.

The notion of a length function on a discrete group generalizes straightforwardly to étale groupoids, see
Definition

2.2
. Indeed, a proper continuous length function
\ell
on an étale groupoid
\mathcal{G}
(with compact unit space) gives rise to an unbounded operator
D_{\ell}
on the Hilbert
C^{*}
-module
\mathcal{E}
naturally associated with the left regular representation
\Lambda
of
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
. We could then further mimic the construction from the discrete group case by defining the seminorm

\displaystyle L_{\ell}(f):=\|[D_{\ell},\Lambda(f)]\|

where the norm is taken in the
C^{*}
-algebra of adjointable operators on
\mathcal{E}
. We quickly realize however, that the pair
(C_{c}(\mathcal{G}),L_{\ell})
will not be a compact quantum metric space if
\mathcal{G}
is not a discrete group. The issue is that if
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is not just a point, then the kernel of
L_{\ell}
is too big, see
Remark

2.12
. Specifically, the issue is that there are functions
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
for which
L_{\ell}(f)=0
but
f
need not be a scalar multiple of the identity function
1_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}
. Furthermore,
(C_{c}(\mathcal{G}),L_{\ell})
will not be a compact quantum metric space if
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
and
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},d)
is just a compact metric space, as
L_{\ell}
will then be identically zero.

To fix this, we incorporate the fact that
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},d)
is a compact metric space into the seminorm. As such, we immediately have access to another seminorm which assigns to a function
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
the Lipschitz constant of
f_{|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}}
. However, from the literature on crossed products
[
14
,
24
,
29
,
11
]
we know that we should impose some version of global Lipschitzness on the functions used to define the operator system
\mathcal{X}
and associated seminorm
L
. It is natural to think that the same should at least be true for transformation groupoids.

The first technical novelty is the introduction of metric stratifications in
Definition

3.2
which will allow us to specify functions in
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
that are globally Lipschitz in a manner suitable for our purposes.
More specifically, a metric stratification is a decomposition
\mathcal{K}=(K_{i})_{i\in I}
of
\mathcal{G}
into countably many disjoint subsets, or strata, for some countable index set
I
, subject to a series of technical assumptions, see
Definition

3.2
.
In particular, every stratum
K_{i}
is required to be a totally bounded metric space when inducing a metric on it through the range and source maps on the groupoid, see (
3.3
).
Thus, a metric stratification
\mathcal{K}
is a way of transferring the metric structure present on the unit space to the whole groupoid, therefore allowing us to consider an operator subsystem
C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G})
of
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
consisting of functions which are “globally Lipschitz” with respect to the metric stratification
\mathcal{K}
, see (
3.6
). We then have an associated seminorm
L^{\mathcal{K}}_{\rm Lip}\colon C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G})\to[0,\infty)
which assigns to a function its “global Lipschitz constant” with respect to the stratification
\mathcal{K}
, see (
3.5
).
We remark that metric stratifications are in general not unique, therefore allowing for choices which lead to different operator systems and seminorms, and in turn different candidates for compact quantum metric spaces.

Combining the above seminorms we obtain a new seminorm, namely

\displaystyle L(f)=\max\{L_{\ell}(f),L^{\mathcal{K}}_{\rm Lip}(f)\}

for
f\in C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G})
, see
Definition

3.8
, and we may ask

Is
(C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G}),L)
a compact quantum metric space?

More generally we could ask the same question, but allowing for iterated commutators being used in the definition of
L_{\ell}
, see (
3.2
).

Having now specified a candidate for a compact quantum metric space arising from an étale groupoid equipped with a length function and a metric on its compact unit space, we provide a verifiable condition for when the above question has a positive answer in
Theorem

3.16
. This is done by relating the question to the study of Fourier multipliers, building on state-of-the-art results about Fourier multipliers on groupoid crossed products from
[
17
]
, which allow us to lift the Fourier multipliers to groupoid Roe algebras. As such, we are able to provide a sufficient condition for verifying that
(C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G}),L)
is a compact quantum metric space in terms of existence of compactly supported Fourier multipliers on
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
providing sufficiently good norm approximations on the
L
-unit ball, see (
3.10
).
Using
Theorem

3.16
we moreover show that under the assumption of rapid decay as well as mild assumptions on the length function and the metric stratification, there will always exist
n
large enough so that the above question has a positive answer if
L_{\ell}
is defined using
n
iterated commutators. This is the content of
Proposition

3.20
and extends the famous result from
[
9
]
.

Lastly in the article, we use
Theorem

3.16
to show that AF groupoids with compact unit spaces give rise to compact quantum metric spaces. We will consider AF groupoids where the unit space can be identified with a Cantor space, so there are natural candidates for metrics on the unit space making it a compact metric space. We then show that any AF groupoid can be equipped with a proper continuous length function arising naturally from any Bratteli diagram for the AF groupoid. This appears to be the first time this length function has been considered in the literature. Using
Theorem

3.16
we show that the combined data yield a compact quantum metric space.
In fact, any AF groupoid comes equipped with a family of compact subgroupoids, and we show convergence of the associated subsystems in the quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance, see
Theorem

4.10
.

The article is structured as follows. In
Section

2
we collect results about groupoids, Fourier multipliers on groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras, and fundamental notions from the study of compact quantum metric spaces.
In
Section

3
we introduce metric stratifications of groupoids. Then we show how these can be used to construct a compact quantum metric space from a groupoid with a compact metric space as unit space, and equipped with a proper continuous length function. In this section we also prove the first main theorem of the article, namely
Theorem

3.16
. Lastly, in
Section

4
we demonstrate the first examples of compact quantum metric spaces coming from groupoids by showing that they arise from AF groupoids in a natural way.

Lastly in this section, we remind the reader about the notions of polynomial growth and rapid decay for groupoids. To do so we must define length functions.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an étale groupoid. By a
continuous length function on
\mathcal{G}
we will mean a continuous map
\ell\colon\mathcal{G}\to[0,\infty)
satisfying

(1)

\ell(\gamma)=0
if and only if
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
,

(2)

\ell(\gamma^{-1})=\ell(\gamma)
for all
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}
,

(3)

\ell(\gamma\mu)\leq\ell(\gamma)+\ell(\mu)
for all
(\gamma,\mu)\in\mathcal{G}^{(2)}
.

The length function is said to be
proper
if the set
\ell^{-1}([0,r])
is compact for every
r\geq 0
.

For
t\geq 0
we define the closed
\ell
-ball of radius
t
by
B_{\ell}(t):=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}\mid\ell(\gamma)\leq t\}
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an étale groupoid equipped with a proper continuous length function
\ell
. We will call the pair
(\mathcal{G},\ell)
a
metric groupoid
. We introduce the following norm on
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{2,s}:

\displaystyle=\max\{\sup_{u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{u}}|f(\gamma)|^{2}(1+\ell(\gamma))^{2s}\right)^{1/2},\sup_{u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}^{u}}|f(\gamma)|^{2}(1+\ell(\gamma))^{2s}\right)^{1/2}\}

\displaystyle=\max\{\sup_{u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{u}}|f(\gamma)|^{2}(1+\ell(\gamma))^{2s}\right)^{1/2},\sup_{u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{u}}|f^{*}(\gamma)|^{2}(1+\ell(\gamma))^{2s}\right)^{1/2}\}.

We then say that
(\mathcal{G},\ell)
has
rapid decay
if there exist constants
C,s\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\|\Lambda(f)\|\leq C\|f\|_{2,s}

for all
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
.

Examples of metric groupoids with rapid decay are those with polynomial growth
[
26
, Proposition 3.5]
. We say
(\mathcal{G},\ell)
has
polynomial growth
if there is a polynomial
p\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}|\{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{u}\mid\ell(\gamma)\leq t\}|=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}|B_{\ell}(t)\cap\mathcal{G}_{u}|\leq p(t)

for all
t\geq 0
.

Assumption 2.3
.

We will assume that any étale groupoid and proper continuous length function considered in this article satisfy that for every
t\geq 0
there is
F_{t}\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}|B_{\ell}(t)\cap\mathcal{G}_{u}|\leq F_{t}.

This can be seen to hold true if
(\mathcal{G},\ell)
has polynomial growth. Moreover, for discrete groups it is implied by properness of the length function
\ell
. Thus it will also be true for transformation groupoids when using the proper continuous length function from (
3.4
).

2.2.
Multipliers of reduced groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras

In our characterization of metric groupoids yielding compact quantum metric spaces (in a manner which will be explained in
Section

3
) we will use Fourier multipliers on reduced groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras. Crucial material is developed and covered in the article
[
17

. However, there it is stated in the very general setting of multipliers for twisted groupoid actions and the associated reduced section
C^{*}
-algebras. For the reader’s convenience,
we rephrase the results we will need in the setting of reduced groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras, that is, without the added complexity of twisted actions.
We first introduce the notion of a left Hilbert
\mathcal{G}
-bundle for an étale groupoid
\mathcal{G}
.
Fix an étale groupoid
\mathcal{G}
, and suppose moreover that
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is compact. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a continuous Hilbert bundle over
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, that is, a continuous field of Hilbert spaces over
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
. Denote by
\mathcal{H}_{u}
the fiber over
u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
and by
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{u}}
the associated norm.
By
\Gamma(\mathcal{H})
we will mean the continuous bounded sections of
\mathcal{H}
. Then
\Gamma(\mathcal{H})
is a Banach space with the norm

\|\xi\|_{\Gamma(\mathcal{H})}=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\|\xi(u)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{u}}
.
We say
(\mathcal{H},L)
is a
\mathcal{G}
-Hilbert bundle if for each
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}
there is a linear invertible isometry
L_{\gamma}\colon\mathcal{H}_{s(\gamma)}\to\mathcal{H}_{r(\gamma)}
, such that for all
\xi,\zeta\in\Gamma(\mathcal{H})
the map
u\mapsto\langle L_{\gamma}\xi(s(\gamma)),\zeta(r(\gamma))\rangle
is continuous, and such that
L\colon\gamma\mapsto L_{\gamma}
is a groupoid homomorphism from
\mathcal{G}
to the isomorphism groupoid of
\coprod_{u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\mathcal{H}_{u}
.

With

\mathcal{G}
as above, for each
\mathcal{G}
-Hilbert bundle
(\mathcal{H},L)
and sections
\xi,\zeta\in\Gamma(\mathcal{H})
, we obtain a function

\displaystyle C_{\mathcal{H},L,\xi,\zeta}\colon\mathcal{G}\ni\gamma\mapsto\langle L_{\gamma}\xi(s(\gamma)),\zeta(r(\gamma))\rangle\in\mathbb{C}.

We denote by
\mathrm{FS}(\mathcal{G})
the collection of all
C_{\mathcal{H},L,\xi,\zeta}
as we vary over all
\mathcal{G}
-Hilbert bundles
(\mathcal{H},L)
and sections
\xi,\zeta\in\Gamma(\mathcal{H})
.
\mathrm{FS}(\mathcal{G})
are the
Fourier-Stieltjes coefficients
of
\mathcal{G}
. We may also equip
\mathrm{FS}(\mathcal{G})
with a norm

\displaystyle\|C\|_{\mathrm{FS}(\mathcal{G})}:=\inf\{\|\xi\|_{\Gamma(\mathcal{H})}\|\zeta\|_{\Gamma(\mathcal{H})}\mid C=C_{\mathcal{H},L,\xi,\zeta}\text{ for some $(\mathcal{H},L)$ and $\xi,\zeta\in\Gamma(\mathcal{H})$}\}.

With this norm,
\mathrm{FS}(\mathcal{G})
becomes a Banach space
[
17
, Proposition 7.10]
.
The following result is
[
17
, Theorem 7.13]
specialized to our setting.

Proposition 2.4
.

Any
C\in\mathrm{FS}(\mathcal{G})
gives rise to a completely bounded map
m_{C}\colon C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})\to C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
given by
m_{C}(f)=C\cdot f
, where
(C\cdot f)(\gamma)=C(\gamma)f(\gamma)
for
f\in C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
. Moreover, the
completely bounded multiplier norm

satisfies

is completely positive and
\|m_{C}\|_{\mathrm{cb}}=\|C\|_{\mathrm{FS}(\mathcal{G})}=\|\xi\|_{\Gamma(\mathcal{H})}^{2}
.

Remark 2.5
.

Some results in

Proposition 2.6

Let

Proof.

The proof of

∎

2.3.
Compact quantum metric spaces

In recent years, a number of techniques in the study of compact quantum metric spaces have been developed using the formalism of operator systems.
This is the approach to compact quantum metric spaces we will take in this article.
Let us first recall that
an
operator system

\mathcal{X}
is a unital and
*
-invariant subspace of a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
A
. The operator system
\mathcal{X}
is said to be complete if it is closed in the
C^{*}
-norm of the ambient
C^{*}
-algebra
A
. We will often identify the scalars
\mathbb{C}
with the subspace
\mathbb{C}\cdot 1\subseteq\mathcal{X}
spanned by the unit in
\mathcal{X}
.
An element
x\in\mathcal{X}
is positive if it is positive in the ambient
C^{*}
-algebra, and we define the state space
S(\mathcal{X})
to be the positive, unital functionals on
\mathcal{X}
. Letting
X:=\overline{\mathcal{X}}^{\|\cdot\|_{A}}
be the closure, we note that
S(\mathcal{X})
and
S(X)
are homeomorphic through the restriction map.

The following terminology can be found in
[
40
]
.

Definition 2.10
.

Given an operator system
\mathcal{X}
, a
slip-norm
on
\mathcal{X}
is a seminorm
L\colon\mathcal{X}\to[0,\infty)
satisfying

(1)

L(x^{*})=L(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

(2)

\mathbb{C}\subseteq\ker L:=\{x\in\mathcal{X}\mid L(x)=0\}
.

Given a slip-norm
L
on
\mathcal{X}
, we can consider the associated
Monge-Kantorovič metric

\mathrm{mk}_{L}
on the state space
S(\mathcal{X})
, which is given by

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathrm{mk}_{L}(\phi,\psi):=\sup\{|\phi(x)-\psi(x)|\mid L(x)\leq 1\}\quad\text{for all $\phi,\psi\in S(\mathcal{X})$.}

Despite calling
\mathrm{mk}_{L}
a metric, it is a priori only an extended metric, that is,
\mathrm{mk}_{L}
may assign infinite distances between states.

Definition 2.11
.

Suppose
\mathcal{X}
is an operator system equipped with a slip-norm
L
. If the (extended) metric
\mathrm{mk}_{L}
of (
2.8
) metrizes the weak
∗
topology on
S(\mathcal{X})
, we say that
(\mathcal{X},L)
is a
compact quantum metric space
.

Remark 2.12
.

Suppose
L
is a slip-norm on an operator system
\mathcal{X}
. If
(\mathcal{X},L)
is a compact quantum metric space, then it must in fact be true that
\ker(L)=\mathbb{C}
. To see this, note that if there were
x\in\ker(L)\setminus\mathbb{C}
, then we could find states
\phi,\psi\in S(\mathcal{X})
with
\phi(x)\neq\psi(x)
and then

\displaystyle\sup\{|\phi(y)-\psi(y)|\mid L(y)\leq 1\}=\infty

since
tx\in\ker(L)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. But
S(\mathcal{X})
is compact and connected in the weak
∗
topology, and so it can not be metrized by an extended metric attaining the value
\infty
.

The statement in
Definition

2.11
is difficult to verify in practice.
We record the following very useful reformulation from
[
32
]
, which we will make extensive use of in the sequel. Note that in
[
32
]
the result is stated for dense
*
-subalgebras of unital
C^{*}
-algebras. However, the result and proof is based on
[
35
, Theorem 1.8]
, wherein only an operator system structure is required. We therefore state the result for operator systems.

Proposition 2.13

(Proposition 1.3 in
[
32
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an operator system in an ambient unital
C^{*}
-algebra
A
, and let
L\colon\mathcal{X}\to[0,\infty)
be a slip-norm. Suppose
\sigma
is a state on
\mathcal{X}
.
Then

(\mathcal{X},L)
is a compact quantum metric space if and only if

\displaystyle E=E_{L}=\{x\in\mathcal{A}\mid L(x)\leq 1\text{ and }\sigma(x)=0\}

is a norm-totally bounded subset of
A
.

The following two examples show how we may attempt to construct compact quantum metric spaces from discrete groups equipped with length functions and from classical compact metric spaces. Indeed, as étale groupoids may be viewed as joint generalizations of discrete groups and locally compact Hausdorff spaces, these examples will provide the inspiration for how to construct compact quantum metric spaces from étale groupoids with compact unit spaces.

Example 2.14
.

Let
\Gamma
denote a countable discrete group with unit
e
. Suppose
\ell\colon\Gamma\to[0,\infty)
is a length function, by which we mean
\ell
satisfies
\ell(x)=0
if and only if
x=e
,
\ell(x^{-1})=\ell(x)
for all
x\in\Gamma
, and
\ell(xy)\leq\ell(x)+\ell(y)
for all
x,y\in\Gamma
. Moreover, suppose
\ell
is proper, that is,
\ell^{-1}([0,r])
is finite for all
r\geq 0
.
The proper length function
\ell
gives rise to a self-adjoint unbounded operator given by the self-adjoint closure of the operator

\displaystyle D_{\ell}\colon C_{c}(\Gamma)\to\ell^{2}(\Gamma),\quad\delta_{x}\mapsto\ell(x)\delta_{x}

where
(\delta_{x})_{x\in\Gamma}
is the canonical basis for
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
. We also denote the closure of the above operator by
D_{\ell}
for simplicity. The triple
(C_{c}(\Gamma),\ell^{2}(\Gamma),D_{\ell})
then defines a spectral triple for the
C^{*}
-algebra
C_{r}^{*}(\Gamma)

[
20
]
. We obtain a derivation

\displaystyle\delta\colon C_{c}(\Gamma)\to B(\ell^{2}(\Gamma)),\quad f\mapsto\overline{[D_{\ell},\Lambda(f)]},

where
\Lambda\colon C_{r}^{*}(\Gamma)\to B(\ell^{2}(\Gamma))
is the left regular representation. By
\overline{[D_{\ell},\Lambda(f)]}
we mean the closure of the operator
[D_{\ell},\Lambda(f)]
. In
[
39
]
Rieffel uses the derivation
\delta
to construct a slip-norm on
C_{c}(\Gamma)
. Note that here
C_{c}(\Gamma)
is realized as an operator system inside the
C^{*}
-algebra
C_{r}^{*}(\Gamma)
. Moreover,
C_{c}(\Gamma)
is dense in
C_{r}^{*}(\Gamma)
, so
S(C_{r}^{*}(\Gamma))\cong S(C_{c}(\Gamma))
through the restriction map. Specifically, for
f\in C_{c}(\Gamma)
, he sets

(2.9)

\displaystyle L_{\ell}(f):=\|\delta(f)\|_{B(\ell^{2}(\Gamma))}

and asks whether the pair
(C_{c}(\Gamma),L_{\ell})
gives rise to a compact quantum metric space structure on
C_{r}^{*}(\Gamma)
. This has been shown in the positive for word-hyperbolic groups
[
32
]
and for metric groups
(\Gamma,\ell)
with bounded doubling (in particular finitely generated groups of polynomial growth)
[
18
]
, and there are currently no known counterexamples using word-length functions. In
[
9
]
, they consider the analogous question but allow for iterated application of the derivation
\delta
. That is, considering the slip-norm
L^{n}_{\ell}(f):=\|\delta^{n}(f)\|_{B(\ell^{2}(\Gamma))}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, they ask when the pair
(C_{c}(\Gamma),L^{n}_{\ell})
yields a compact quantum metric space structure for
C_{r}^{*}(\Gamma)
. It is shown that whenever the metric group
(\Gamma,\ell)
has rapid decay, there is
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(C_{c}(\Gamma),L^{n}_{\ell})
is a compact quantum metric space for all
n\geq k
. Moreover,
k
can be determined by the rate of rapid decay for
(\Gamma,\ell)
, that is, by the quantity

\displaystyle\inf\{s\geq 0\mid\text{There is $C>0$ such that $\|f\|_{C_{r}^{*}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|f\|_{2,s}$ for all $f\in C_{c}(\Gamma)$}\}.

Example 2.15
.

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space, and denote by
\mathrm{Lip}(X)
the dense
*
-subalgebra of
C(X)
consisting of Lipschitz continuous functions on
(X,d)
. Let
L\colon\mathrm{Lip}(X)\to\mathbb{R}
be the assignment of a function to its Lipschitz constant, that is

\displaystyle L(f)=\sup\{\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{d(x,y)}\mid x\neq y\}

for
f\in\mathrm{Lip}(X)
. It is easily verified that
L
is a slip-norm, and in fact
(\mathrm{Lip}(X),L)
becomes a compact quantum metric space, because

\displaystyle\mathrm{mk}_{L}(\phi,\psi)=\sup\{|\phi(f)-\psi(f)|\mid f\in\mathrm{Lip}(X)\text{ with }L(f)\leq 1\}

recovers the weak
∗
-topology on
S(C(X))
, see
[
36
]
.

In

First,

\mathcal{K}=\{K_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, where
I
is a countable index set, for which the following holds

(1)

\mathcal{G}=\bigcup_{i\in I}K_{i}
.

(2)

Each
K_{i}
is precompact
and open.

(3)

We have
K_{i_{1}}\cap K_{i_{2}}=\emptyset
for
i_{1}\neq i_{2}
.

(4)

For every
K_{i}\in\mathcal{K}
, we also have
K_{i}^{-1}\in\mathcal{K}
.

(5)

There is a distinguished element
K_{e}\in\mathcal{K}
for which
K_{e}=\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
.

(6)

For each
i\in I
, the map

(3.3)

\displaystyle d^{(i)}(\gamma,\mu)=\max\{d(s(\gamma),s(\mu)),d(r(\gamma),r(\mu))\},\quad\gamma,\mu\in K_{i},

must induce on
K_{i}
the structure of a totally bounded metric space, that is, for any
\varepsilon>0
, it can be covered by finitely many
\varepsilon
-balls.

If the metric
d
is implied, we simply say that
\mathcal{K}
is a
metric stratification of
\mathcal{G}
.

Remark 3.3
.

(1)

Note that
Definition

3.2
does not rule out the possibility that
K_{i}=\emptyset
for some
i\in I
.

(2)

To verify condition (6) of
Definition

3.2
it suffices to show that for each
i\in I
, we have
d^{(i)}(\gamma,\mu)=0
if and only if
\gamma=\mu
. Indeed, if this holds, then (
3.3
) tells us that
(K_{i},d^{(i)})
can be realized as a subspace of
(\mathcal{G}^{0}\times\mathcal{G}^{0},\tilde{d})
through the map
s\times r
, where

\displaystyle\tilde{d}((x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2}))=\max\{d(x_{1},x_{2}),d(y_{1},y_{2})\}\quad\text{for $(x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2})\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\times\mathcal{G}^{(0)}$}.

Since
(\mathcal{G}^{0}\times\mathcal{G}^{0},\tilde{d})
is a compact metric space, we deduce that
d^{(i)}
induces on
K_{i}
the structure of a totally bounded metric space.

The conditions in
Definition

3.2
deserve an explanation. We illustrate first with the case of a transformation groupoid

\mathcal{G}=\Gamma\ltimes X

where
\Gamma
is a countable group, and
(X,d)
is a compact metric space. Let
\ell_{\Gamma}
be a proper length function on
\Gamma
, and induce on

\Gamma\ltimes X

the proper continuous length function
\ell
given by

(3.4)

\displaystyle\ell(g,x)=\ell_{\Gamma}(g)\quad\text{ for $(g,x)\in\Gamma\ltimes X$.}

We would then immediately get a very natural looking decomposition satisfying all the above conditions by setting
\mathcal{K}=(K_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}
where

K_{g}=\{g\}\times X
.
Notably, in this case we would have that each
K_{g}
is compact and open, but we have weakened the statement to precompact to allow the setup to cover partial action groupoids. However, suppose that
\ell_{\Gamma}
is integer-valued. Then another decomposition which would satisfy conditions (1)-(5) is given by setting
\mathcal{K}=(K_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}
where
K_{n}=\ell^{-1}(\{n\})
. However, it is entirely possible there could be
g_{1},g_{2}\in\Gamma
with
\ell_{\Gamma}(g_{1})=\ell_{\Gamma}(g_{2})
, and
x\in X
for which
g_{1}x=g_{2}x
. Then

\displaystyle r(g_{1},x)=r(g_{2},x)\quad\text{and}\quad s(g_{1},x)=s(g_{2},x)

and the map in condition (6) would not be a metric. The absence of condition (6) will make analysis difficult, especially in the proof of the crucial
Lemma

3.15
. Indeed, we may think of condition (6) as a substitute for requiring that the decomposition consists of bisections, that is, subsets of
\mathcal{G}
for which the range and source maps are local homeomorphisms. Were we to require
\mathcal{K}
to consist of bisections however, we would find few examples where condition (3) is simultaneously satisfied, and this condition likewise plays an important role in the proof of
Lemma

3.15
, as does conditions (2) and (5). Lastly, condition (4) is necessary to get
*
-invariance of the seminorm defined in (
3.5
) below, and therefore to obtain a slip-norm in (
3.7
).

Remark 3.4
.

(1)

Suppose
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is just a compact Hausdorff space, and
d
is a metric on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
for which
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},d)
is a compact metric space. Considering condition (5) of
Definition

3.2
, we see that the only metric stratification of
\mathcal{G}
available is
\mathcal{K}=\{K_{e}\}
with
K_{e}=\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
.

(2)

Suppose
\mathcal{G}=\Gamma
is a countable discrete group, and let
\ell\colon\Gamma\to[0,\infty)
be a proper length function. Then
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}=\{e\}
, where
e\in\Gamma
is the unit, and there is of course a unique metric
d
making
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},d)
a compact metric space. Considering condition (6) of
Definition

3.2
we see that the only metric stratification available is
\mathcal{K}=(K_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}
with
K_{g}=\{g\}\times\mathcal{G}^{(0)}=\{g\}\times\{e\}
.

In general, the choice of
\mathcal{K}
will depend on the situation. We note however, that under certain assumptions a particular choice of
\mathcal{K}
always exists.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a principal étale groupoid, and suppose
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},d)
is a compact metric space. Suppose moreover that
\ell\colon\mathcal{G}\to[0,\infty)
is a proper continuous length function whose image
\mathrm{im}(\ell)\subseteq[0,\infty)
is countable. Then
\mathcal{K}=(K_{s})_{s\in\mathrm{im}(\ell)}
given by
K_{s}=\ell^{-1}(\{s\})
is a metric stratification for
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

By inspection we see that conditions (1)-(5) hold for
K_{s}=\ell^{-1}(\{s\})
whenever
\ell
is a proper continuous length function. To verify (6), note that by
Remark

3.3
it suffices to show that given any
s\in\mathrm{im}(\ell)
the statement
d^{(s)}(\gamma,\mu)=0
implies
\gamma=\mu
for
\gamma,\mu\in K_{s}
. But since
d
is a metric on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, we see that
d^{(s)}(\gamma,\mu)=0
implies
s(\gamma)=s(\mu)
and
r(\gamma)=r(\mu)
, from which
\gamma=\mu
follows by principality.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

A transformation groupoid
\mathcal{G}=\Gamma\ltimes X
arising from a free action is principal. We have already seen that for transformation groupoids we have a natural choice
\mathcal{K}=(K_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}
with
K_{g}=\{g\}\times X
, which definitely differs from the metric stratification
(K_{s})_{s\in\mathrm{im}(\ell)}
from
Lemma

3.5
. As can be gleaned from (
3.5
) and (
3.7
) below, the choice
(K_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}
will lead to a smaller seminorm than by using
(K_{s})_{s\in\mathrm{im}(\ell)}
.

Given a metric
d
on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
for which
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},d)
is a compact metric space, we now fix a metric stratification
\mathcal{K}=(K_{i})_{i\in I}
of
\mathcal{G}
with respect to
d
, and define for every
i\in I
and
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{Lip}}^{K_{i}}(f):=\sup\{\frac{|f(\gamma)-f(\mu)|}{d^{(i)}(\gamma,\mu)}\mid\gamma,\mu\in K_{i}\}

where
d^{(i)}
is the metric from (
3.3
).
L_{\mathrm{Lip}}^{K_{i}}
therefore measures the Lipschitz constant of
f
restricted to
K_{i}
for the metric induced by the range and source maps through
d^{(i)}
, and is therefore
sensitive to our particular choice of metric stratification
\mathcal{K}
.
We then further define

(3.5)

\displaystyle L^{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{Lip}}(f):=\sup_{i\in I}L^{K_{i}}_{\mathrm{Lip}}(f)

for
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
. Without further assumptions, it is entirely possible that
L^{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{Lip}}(f)=\infty
for
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
, and indeed we will soon restrict
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
to a sup-operator system in order to construct compact quantum metric spaces. We first prove the following easy observation.

Lemma 3.7
.

For every
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
, we have
L^{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{Lip}}(f)=L^{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{Lip}}(f^{*})
.

Proof.

Fix
i\in I
, and denote by
d^{(i^{-1})}
the metric on
K_{i}^{-1}
from (
3.3
). Importantly, note that for
\gamma,\mu\in K_{i}
, we have
d^{(i)}(\gamma,\mu)=d^{(i^{-1})}(\gamma^{-1},\mu^{-1})
. We calculate

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{Lip}}^{K_{i}}(f^{*})

\displaystyle=\sup\{\frac{|f^{*}(\gamma)-f^{*}(\mu)|}{d^{(i)}(\gamma,\mu)}\mid\gamma,\mu\in K_{i}\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\frac{|\overline{f(\gamma^{-1})}-\overline{f(\mu^{-1})}|}{d^{(i)}(\gamma,\mu)}\mid\gamma,\mu\in K_{i}\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\frac{|f(\gamma^{-1})-f(\mu^{-1})|}{d^{(i)}(\gamma,\mu)}\mid\gamma,\mu\in K_{i}\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\frac{|f(\gamma^{-1})-f(\mu^{-1})|}{d^{(i^{-1})}(\gamma^{-1},\mu^{-1})}\mid\gamma,\mu\in K_{i}\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\frac{|f(\tilde{\gamma})-f(\tilde{\gamma})|}{d^{(i^{-1})}(\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\mu})}\mid\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\mu}\in K_{i}^{-1}\}

\displaystyle=L^{K_{i}^{-1}}_{\mathrm{Lip}}(f).

Since
L^{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{Lip}}
is defined in terms of the supremum over all
i\in I
, and
K_{i}\in\mathcal{K}
implies
K_{i}^{-1}\in\mathcal{K}
,
the result follows.
∎

In alignment with the setup of
Section

2.3
, we proceed to specify a sub-operator system
\mathcal{X}
of
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
for which
L^{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{Lip}}(f)<\infty
for all
f\in\mathcal{X}
. We define

(3.6)

\displaystyle C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G}):=\{f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})\mid L^{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{Lip}}(f)<\infty\},

where we have made explicit the fact that this operator system depends on the metric stratification
\mathcal{K}
of
\mathcal{G}
. Note that since
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}=K_{e}\in\mathcal{K}
, the characteristic function
1_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}
is in
C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G})
. Combined with
Lemma

3.7
we deduce that
C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G})
is in fact a sub-operator system of
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an étale groupoid with compact unit space
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, suppose
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},d)
is a compact metric space, and fix a metric stratification
\mathcal{K}=(K_{i})_{i\in I}
for
\mathcal{G}
with respect to
d
.
Suppose moreover that
\ell\colon\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\to[0,\infty)
is a proper continuous length function. For every
n\geq 1
we define a

given by

(3.7)

\displaystyle L^{\mathcal{K},n}(f):=\max\{L^{n}_{\ell}(f),L_{\mathrm{Lip}}^{\mathcal{K}}(f)\},

for
f\in C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G})
(cf. (
3.6
)), where

\displaystyle L^{n}_{\ell}(f)=\|\delta^{n}(f)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})}(\mathcal{E})}

as in (
3.2
) and

\displaystyle L^{\mathcal{K}}_{\rm Lip}(f)=\sup_{i\in I}L^{K_{i}}_{\mathrm{Lip}}(f)=\sup_{i\in I}\sup\{\frac{|f(\gamma)-f(\mu)|}{d^{(i)}(\gamma,\mu)}\mid\gamma,\mu\in K_{i}\}

as in (
3.5
).

Note that by
Proposition

3.1
and (
3.6
), we have
L^{\mathcal{K},n}(f)<\infty
for all
f\in C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G})
. We therefore wish to study the following question.

Question 3.9
.

Is
(C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G}),L^{\mathcal{K},n})
a compact quantum metric space?

Remark 3.10
.

Note that for a transformation groupoid
\mathcal{G}=\Gamma\ltimes X
, the seminorm in (
3.7
) (in the case
n=1
) bears a close resemblance to the seminorm defined in
[
11
]
for crossed products using discrete groups of polynomial growth. Moreover, the operator system defined in (
3.6
) also has a counterpart in
[
11
]
, where the operator subsystem of the crossed product consist only of those elements which are sufficiently smooth with respect to the action, and these elements generally only form a dense sub-operator system of the algebraic crossed product.

Remark 3.11
.

Instead of the seminorm defined in (
3.7
), another natural choice would be

\displaystyle\tilde{L}^{\mathcal{K},n}(f)=L^{n}_{\ell}(f)+L_{\mathrm{Lip}}^{\mathcal{K}}(f).

Note however that
\tilde{L}^{\mathcal{K},n}(f)
and
L^{\mathcal{K},n}(f)
are equivalent seminorms with
L^{\mathcal{K},n}(f)\leq\tilde{L}^{\mathcal{K},n}(f)\leq 2\cdot L^{\mathcal{K},n}(f)
for all
f\in C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G})
, and so they induce equivalent metrics and topologies on

S(C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G}))

through the Monge-Kantorovič metric construction from (
2.8
).

3.2.
A characterization of metric groupoids yielding compact quantum metric spaces

In this section we prove
Theorem

3.16
which provides a sufficient condition for
3.9
to have a positive answer. It does so in terms existence of Fourier multipliers which are compatible with the metric stratification
\mathcal{K}
in a sense we will make precise in
Definition

3.12
. The condition will be in terms of a norm inequality being satisfied for all
f\in E_{L}
, where
E_{L}
is the set from
Proposition

2.13
, which when adapted to our setting takes the form found in (
3.8
). Loosely speaking,
3.9
has a positive answer whenever
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
admits compactly supported Fourier multipliers which provide uniform norm approximations for
f\in E_{L}
.

The first step towards
Theorem

3.16
is to show that Fourier-Stieltjes coefficients extend to the groupoid Roe algebra exactly in the manner in which we would like.
We will in the sequel
need that the Fourier multipliers are sufficiently well-behaved with respect to a chosen metric stratification
\mathcal{K}
of
\mathcal{G}
and the resulting seminorm
L_{\mathrm{Lip}}^{\mathcal{K}}
from (
3.5
). We define the following notion.

Definition 3.12
.

Suppose
\mathcal{G}
is an étale groupoid with compact unit space
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
. Suppose moreover that
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},d)
is a compact metric space, and let
\mathcal{K}
be a metric stratification of
\mathcal{G}
. Let
\phi\in\mathrm{FS}(\mathcal{G})
.
If there exists a real number
C_{\phi}\geq 0
for which

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{Lip}}^{\mathcal{K}}(m_{\phi}(f))\leq C_{\phi}L_{\mathrm{Lip}}^{\mathcal{K}}(f)

for all
f\in C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G})
, we say that
\phi
is
\mathcal{K}
-continuous with coefficient
C_{\phi}
. If the coefficient is not important, we just say that
\phi
is
\mathcal{K}
-continuous
.

We may now establish the following result, which tells us that if a Fourier multiplier is
\mathcal{K}
-continuous, then
m_{\phi}
is in fact continuous with respect to the total seminorm
L^{\mathcal{K},n}
for any
n\geq 1
. The analogous statement in the case of discrete groups was first noted in
[
10
, Lemma 2.12]
.

Lemma 3.13
.

Suppose
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
and
\phi\in\mathrm{FS}(\mathcal{G})
.
Let
T^{\phi}
be
the extension of
m_{\phi}
to
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}\ltimes\beta\mathcal{G})
given by (
2.7
).
Then for all
n\geq 1

\displaystyle\delta^{n}(m_{\phi}(f))=T^{\phi}(\delta^{n}(f)).

Denote by
\|T^{\phi}\|
the multiplier norm of
T^{\phi}
.
If
\phi
is
\mathcal{K}
-continuous with coefficient
C_{\phi}\geq 0

we immediately get

\displaystyle L^{\mathcal{K},n}(m_{\phi}(f))\leq\max\{\|T^{\phi}\|,C_{\phi}\}\cdot L^{\mathcal{K},n}(f)

for all
f\in C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G})
.

Proof.

Note first that

\delta^{n}(f)\in C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}\ltimes\beta\mathcal{G})

by
Proposition

3.1
, The multiplier
T^{\phi}
exists by
Remark

2.9
and acts as

\displaystyle(T^{\phi}F)(\gamma,\mu)=\phi(\gamma)F(\gamma,\mu)

for
(\gamma,\mu)\in\mathcal{G}\mathchoice{\hphantom{{}^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[3.77432pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[3.77432pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.29686pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.64063pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}_{{{s}}}}*^{{\kern-13.37927pt\mathchoice{\makebox[3.77432pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[3.77432pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.29686pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.64063pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}\kern To give our characterization below, we will make use of

2.13
. It is therefore useful to specify what the set

takes the following form

\displaystyle E_{\mathcal{K},n}:=\{f\in C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G})\mid\phi_{\mu}(f)=0\text{ and }L^{\mathcal{K},n}(f)\leq 1\}.

In preparation for the main theorem, we record the following observation. Recall that
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})\subseteq\mathrm{FS}(\mathcal{G})
by
Proposition

2.6
.

Lemma 3.14
.

Suppose
(\mathcal{G},\ell)
is a metric groupoid where

\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is compact and that
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},d)

\mathcal{K}
of
\mathcal{G}
with respect to

.
Assume further that
\mathcal{G}
admits a sequence

such that

for which
\sup_{i}\|T^{\phi_{i}}\|\leq K^{\prime}
, where

such that
\phi_{i}

\mathcal{K}
-continuous with coefficient

,

\displaystyle\sup_{i}\sup_{f\in E_{\mathcal{K},n}}L^{\mathcal{K},n}(m_{\phi_{i}}(f))\leq K

.

\mathcal{K}
-continuity for all

, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{i}\sup_{f\in E_{\mathcal{K},n}}L_{\mathrm{Lip}}^{\mathcal{K}}(m_{\phi_{i}}(f))\leq C.

In the sequel, we will want to cut

. We set

(3.9)

\begin{split}E_{\mathcal{K},n}[t]:&=\{f\in C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G})\mid\phi_{\mu}(f)=0,L^{\mathcal{K},n}(f)\leq 1\text{ and $f(\gamma)=0$ for $\ell(\gamma)>t$}\}\\
&=\{f\in E_{\mathcal{K},n}\mid f(\gamma)=0\text{ for $\ell(\gamma)>t$}\}.\end{split}

We record the following lemma, which will be of importance in the proof of
Theorem

3.16
, and which could also be of independent interest.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\ell)
be a metric groupoid with
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
compact, and where
\ell\colon\mathcal{G}\to[0,\infty)
is a proper continuous length function.
Suppose moreover that
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},d)
is a compact metric space, and fix
a metric stratification
\mathcal{K}
of
\mathcal{G}
.
Let
\mathcal{X}\subseteq C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G})
be a sub-operator system such that there is
t\geq 0
for which
\mathrm{supp}f\subseteq B_{\ell}(t)
for all
f\in\mathcal{X}
.
Then
(\mathcal{X},(L^{\mathcal{K},n})_{|_{\mathcal{X}}})
is a compact quantum metric space for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular, if
\mathcal{G}
is compact,
then
(C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G}),L^{\mathcal{K},n})
is a compact quantum metric space for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Fix a state
\phi_{\mu}\in S(C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}))
. This restricts to a state on
\mathcal{X}
which we also denote by
\phi_{\mu}
.
To show the first statement it suffices by
Proposition

2.13
to show that the set

\displaystyle\{f\in\mathcal{X}\mid\phi_{\mu}(f)=0\text{ and }L^{\mathcal{K},n}(f)\leq 1\}

is totally bounded. Since this set is contained in
E_{\mathcal{K},n}[t]
by assumption on
\mathcal{X}
, we prove that
E_{\mathcal{K},n}[t]
is totally bounded, from which the first statement will follow.
Let therefore
\varepsilon>0
be given. We wish to show that
E_{\mathcal{K},n}[t]
can be covered by finitely many
\varepsilon
-balls in the
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
-norm.

Note first that
B_{\ell}(t)
is compact since
\ell
is proper.
Find a cover of
B_{\ell}(t)
by sets from
\mathcal{K}
, which by compactness of
B_{\ell}(t)
has a finite subcover
B_{\ell}(t)\subseteq\coprod_{i=1}^{m}K_{i}
, after reindexing if necessary. Importantly, since the sets in
\mathcal{K}
are disjoint and
B_{\ell}(t)^{-1}=B_{\ell}(t)
, we have that if
K_{i}
is in the subcover, so is
K_{i}^{-1}
.
Let
d^{(i)}
be the metric induced on
K_{i}
through (
3.3
) so that
(K_{i},d^{(i)})
is a totally bounded metric space for every
i
.
Then, for every
i=1,\ldots,m
we may find finitely many points
\gamma_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,\gamma_{h_{i}}^{(i)}
for which
K_{i}\subseteq\cup_{j=1}^{h_{i}}B_{d^{(i)}}(\gamma_{j}^{(i)},\varepsilon)
, where
B_{d^{(i)}}(\gamma_{j}^{(i)},\varepsilon)
is the open
\varepsilon
-ball around
\gamma_{j}^{(i)}
in the metric
d^{(i)}
.

Now, for each
i=1,\ldots,m
, find a partition of unity subordinate to
(B_{d_{i}}(\gamma_{j}^{(i)},\varepsilon))_{j=1}^{h_{i}}
, say
(\rho_{j}^{(i)})_{i=1}^{h_{i}}
. We may arrange that all the
\rho_{j}^{(i)}
are Lipschitz with respect to the metrics
d_{i}

[
19
, Theorem 2.6.5]
.
Note moreover that as
\rho_{j}^{(i)}
is a Lipschitz continuous function on
K_{i}
for each
i,j
, and subordinate to open subsets of
K_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
,
the extension by zero of
\rho_{j}^{(i)}
can be viewed as an element of
C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G})
for each
i=1,\ldots,m
and
j=1,\ldots,h_{i}
.

Now, for
f\in E_{\mathcal{K},n}[t]
, write
f=\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{|_{K_{i}}}
, which we may do uniquely as the
K_{i}
are disjoint, and set

\displaystyle\Phi_{\varepsilon}^{(i)}(f_{|_{K_{i}}}):=\sum_{j=1}^{h_{i}}f_{|_{K_{i}}}(\gamma_{j}^{(i)})\rho_{j}^{(i)}=\sum_{j=1}^{h_{i}}f(\gamma_{j}^{(i)})\rho_{j}^{(i)}.

We then have a finite-dimensional approximation of
f
through

\displaystyle f\approx\Phi_{\varepsilon}(f):=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Phi_{\varepsilon}^{(i)}(f_{|_{K_{i}}})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{h_{i}}f(\gamma_{j}^{(i)})\rho_{j}^{(i)}.

Recall the definition of the
I
-norm from (
2.1
) and that it dominates the reduced
C^{*}
-algebra norm.
Noting that
K_{e}
is in the subcover, we then calculate

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle f-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{h_{i}}f(\gamma_{j}^{(i)})\rho_{j}^{(i)}\|=\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{|_{K_{i}}}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{h_{i}}f(\gamma_{j}^{(i)})\rho_{j}^{(i)}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{|_{K_{i}}}-\sum_{j=1}^{h_{i}}f_{|_{K_{i}}}(\gamma_{j}^{(i)})\rho_{j}^{(i)}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{|_{K_{i}}}-\sum_{j=1}^{h_{i}}f_{|_{K_{i}}}(\gamma_{j}^{(i)})\rho_{j}^{(i)}\|_{I}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\max\{\sup_{u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\sum_{\mu\in G_{u}}\big|f_{|_{K_{i}}}(\mu)-\sum_{j=1}^{h_{i}}f_{|_{K_{i}}}(\gamma_{j}^{(i)})\rho_{j}^{(i)}(\mu)\big|,\sup_{u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\sum_{\mu\in G^{u}}\big|f_{|_{K_{i}}}(\mu)-\sum_{j=1}^{h_{i}}f_{|_{K_{i}}}(\gamma_{j}^{(i)})\rho_{j}^{(i)}(\mu)\big|\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\max\{\sup_{u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\sum_{\mu\in G_{u}}\sum_{j=1}^{h_{i}}\big|f_{|_{K_{i}}}(\mu)-f_{|_{K_{i}}}(\gamma_{j}^{(i)})\big|\rho_{j}^{(i)}(\mu),\sup_{u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\sum_{\mu\in G^{u}}\sum_{j=1}^{h_{i}}\big|f_{|_{K_{i}}}(\mu)-f_{|_{K_{i}}}(\gamma_{j}^{(i)})\big|\rho_{j}^{(i)}(\mu)\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\max\{\sup_{u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\sum_{\mu\in G_{u}\cap K_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{h_{i}}d^{(i)}(\mu,\gamma_{j}^{(i)})L^{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{Lip}}(f_{|_{K_{i}}})\rho_{j}^{(i)}(\mu),\sup_{u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\sum_{\mu\in G^{u}\cap K_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{h_{i}}d^{(i)}(\mu,\gamma_{j}^{(i)})L^{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{Lip}}(f_{|_{K_{i}}})\rho_{j}^{(i)}(\mu)\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\max\{\sup_{u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\sum_{\mu\in G_{u}\cap K_{i}}\varepsilon\cdot L^{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{Lip}}(f_{|_{K_{i}}}),\sup_{u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\sum_{\mu\in G^{u}\cap K_{i}}\varepsilon\cdot L^{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{Lip}}(f_{|_{K_{i}}})\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\max\{F\varepsilon\cdot L^{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{Lip}}(f_{|_{K_{i}}}),F\varepsilon\cdot L^{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{Lip}}(f_{|_{K_{i}}})\}

\displaystyle=mF\varepsilon\cdot L^{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{Lip}}(f_{|_{K_{i}}}),

\displaystyle\leq mF\varepsilon L^{\mathcal{K},n}(f),

where
F>0
is a uniform upper bound on the quantities
|\{x\in G_{u}\cap K_{i}\}|
and
|\{x\in G^{u}\cap K_{i}\}|
for
i=1,\ldots m
. This must exist by
2.3
since we are considering finitely many
K_{i}
, and

\max_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}\sup_{\gamma\in K_{i}}\ell(\gamma)<\infty
.
Thus we may arrange that
E_{\mathcal{K},n}[t]
is less than
\varepsilon
away in
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
-norm from its image under
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
in the finite-dimensional subspace of
C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G})
spanned by
((\rho_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{h_{i}})_{i=1}^{m}
. We proceed to show that
\mathrm{im}(\Phi_{\varepsilon})
is bounded in operator norm.

For this purpose, let
f\in E_{\mathcal{K},n}[t]
, and

\displaystyle\Phi_{\varepsilon}(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{h_{i}}f(\gamma_{j}^{(i)})\rho_{j}^{(i)}.

We will show there is a uniform upper bound on the reduced
C^{*}
-algebra norm on such elements. First, we obtain the upper bound

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(f)\|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{h_{i}}|f(\gamma_{j}^{(i)})|\|\rho_{j}^{(i)}\|\leq\max_{i,j}\{\|\rho_{j}^{(i)}\|\}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{h_{i}}|f(\gamma_{j}^{(i)})|,

where
\max_{i,j}\{\|\rho_{j}^{(i)}\|\}
is certainly finite as there are only finitely many
\rho_{j}^{(i)}
. The value is independent of
f
. We can obtain a further bound by noting that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{h_{i}}|f(\gamma_{j}^{(i)})|\leq m\cdot\max\{h_{i}\mid i=1,\ldots,m\}\cdot\max\{|f(\gamma_{j}^{(i)})\},

where only the last factor depends on
f
. As such, it will suffice to show that
|f(\gamma_{j}^{(i)})|
is uniformly bounded above for all
f\in E_{\mathcal{K},n}[t]
and
i=1,\ldots,m
,
j=1,\ldots,h_{i}
.

We first consider the case
i\neq e
. Let
A_{n}>0
be a real number which bounds the
\infty
-norm by the
\ell^{n}
-weighted
2
-norm for functions with compact support away from the unit space (recall that
\ell
may take values in
(0,1)
). That is,
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq A_{n}\cdot\sup_{u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\|f\cdot\ell^{n}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{u})}
for all
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
with
\mathrm{supp}(f)\subseteq\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
.
We then calculate

\displaystyle|f(\gamma_{j}^{(i)})|

\displaystyle\leq\|f|_{K_{i}}\|_{\infty}\leq A_{n}\cdot\sup_{u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\|f|_{K_{i}}\cdot\ell^{n}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{u})}

\displaystyle\leq A_{n}\cdot\sup_{u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\|f\cdot\ell^{n}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{u})}=A_{n}\cdot\|\delta^{n}(f)1_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\|_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle\leq A_{n}\cdot\|\delta^{n}(f)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})}(\mathcal{E})}\leq A_{n}\cdot L^{\mathcal{K},n}(f).

Since
L^{\mathcal{K},n}(f)
is uniformly bounded for
f\in E_{\mathcal{K},n}[t]
, this covers the case
i\neq e
.

If
i=e
, we take a different approach. Since
f\in E_{\mathcal{K},n}[t]
is subject to

\phi_{\mu}=\overline{\mu}\circ E(f)=\overline{\mu}(f_{|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}})=0

and
f|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\in C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})
, we know that
f|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}(u)=0
for some
u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
. We may therefore calculate an upper bound on the supremum norm of
f|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}=f_{|_{K_{e}}}
as follows

\displaystyle\|f|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\|_{\infty}\leq\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},d)\cdot L^{K_{e}}_{\mathrm{Lip}}(f|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}})\leq\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},d)\cdot L^{\mathcal{K},n}(f),

where
\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},d)
is the diameter of the compact metric space
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},d)
.
We therefore have a uniform upper bound on the values
|f(\gamma_{j}^{(i)})|
, from which we deduce that
\mathrm{im}(\Phi_{\varepsilon})
is a bounded set in operator norm. Since it spans a finite-dimensional subspace of
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
, we deduce that
\mathrm{im}(\Phi_{\varepsilon})
is totally bounded. The first part of the lemma then follows.

The statement that for
\mathcal{G}
compact,
(C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G}),L^{\mathcal{K},n})
is a compact quantum metric space for every
n\geq 1
follows easily by the first part.
Since
\ell
is bounded on
\mathcal{G}
, we observe that
E_{\mathcal{K},n}\subseteq E_{\mathcal{K},n}[t]
for
t
large enough, for any
n\geq 1
. The conclusion now follows by the first part of the lemma.
∎

We are now in a position to prove the following result, which
constitutes the first main theorem of the article. In the proof, we will use Fourier multipliers to send elements from
E_{\mathcal{K},n}
to elements of
D^{\prime}\cdot E_{\mathcal{K},n}[t]
for some
t\geq 0
and for some sufficiently large
D^{\prime}>0
. If
f\in E_{\mathcal{K},n}
, then in particular
\phi_{\mu}(f)=0
. To guarantee that
m_{\psi}(f)
satisfies the same condition we will additionally assume that
\psi_{|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}}\in\mathbb{C}\cdot 1_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}
, that is,
\psi_{|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}}
is a constant function on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
.

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\ell)
be a metric groupoid where
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is compact, and
\ell\colon\mathcal{G}\to[0,\infty)
is a proper continuous length function.
Suppose
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},d)
is a compact metric space, and fix a metric stratification
\mathcal{K}
of
\mathcal{G}
.
Consider the statements

(1)

For every
\varepsilon>0
there is

\psi\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})\subseteq\mathrm{FS}(\mathcal{G})
with
\psi_{|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}}\in\mathbb{C}\cdot 1_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}
which is
\mathcal{K}
-continuous and for which

(3.10)

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in E_{\mathcal{K},n}}\|f-m_{\psi}(f)\|_{C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})}<\varepsilon.

(2)

(C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G}),L^{\mathcal{K},n})
is a compact quantum metric space.

Then (1) implies (2).

Furthermore suppose
\mathcal{G}

admits a sequence
(\phi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\phi_{j}\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})\subseteq\mathrm{FS}(\mathcal{G})
such that

(i)

(\phi_{j})_{|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}}\in\mathbb{C}\cdot 1_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}
for all
j
, and

(ii)

\phi_{j}\to 1
uniformly on compact subsets, and

(iii)

\sup_{j}\|m_{\phi_{j}}\|<\infty
, and

(iv)

there is
D<\infty
such that
\phi_{j}
is
\mathcal{K}
-continuous with coefficient
D
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

Then (2) implies (1).

Proof.

Suppose (1) holds, and let
\varepsilon>0
.
Choose
\psi\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})\subseteq\mathrm{FS}(\mathcal{G})
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in E_{\mathcal{K},n}}\|f-m_{\psi}(f)\|<\varepsilon.

Combining the assumption that
\psi
is
\mathcal{K}
-continuous with
Lemma

3.13
, it follows that there exists
D^{\prime}>0
such that
L^{\mathcal{K},n}(m_{\psi}f)\leq D^{\prime}\cdot L^{\mathcal{K},n}(f)
for all
f\in C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G})
.
As
\psi\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
, there is
t\geq 0
large enough so that
\mathrm{supp}(\psi)\subseteq B_{\ell}(t)
, and therefore in turn
\mathrm{supp}(m_{\psi}(f))\subseteq B_{\ell}(t)
for all
f\in E_{\mathcal{K},n}
.
Since
\psi_{|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}}\in\mathbb{C}\cdot 1_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}
, these observations together combine
to guarantee
m_{\psi}(f)\in D^{\prime}\cdot E_{\mathcal{K},n}[t]
for any
f\in E_{\mathcal{K},n}
.
The set
E_{\mathcal{K},n}[t]
is totally bounded by
Lemma

3.15
,
and therefore so is
D^{\prime}\cdot E_{\mathcal{K},n}[t]
. Thus the assumption
\sup_{f\in E_{\mathcal{K},n}}\|f-m_{\psi}(f)\|<\varepsilon
tells us that
E_{\mathcal{K},n}
is
\varepsilon
away in norm from a norm-totally bounded set, and is therefore itself totally bounded in norm. It follows that (2) holds.

Conversely, suppose (2) holds and that we have a sequence
(\phi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
as in the statement of the theorem.
Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary, and let
D^{\prime}\geq 1
be such that
\max\{D,\sup_{j}\|T^{\phi_{j}}\|,\sup_{j}\|m_{\phi_{j}}\|\}\leq D^{\prime}
. By
Proposition

2.13
, we may fix
f_{1},\ldots f_{l}\in E_{\mathcal{K},n}
such that for all
f\in E_{\mathcal{K},n}
there exists
k\leq l
such that
\|f-f_{k}\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{4D^{\prime}}
. Since all the
f_{k}
are compactly supported, there exists
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
j\geq M
and for all
k=1,\ldots,l
we have

\displaystyle\|f_{k}-m_{\phi_{j}}(f_{k})\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

Since
\|m_{\phi_{j}}\|\leq D^{\prime}
for all
j
, we also have

\displaystyle\|m_{\phi_{j}}(f_{k})-m_{\phi_{j}}(f)\|\leq\|m_{\phi_{j}}\|\cdot\|f-f_{k}\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

Now, given
f\in E_{\mathcal{K},n}
we find
k\leq l
such that
\|f-f_{k}\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{4D^{\prime}}
. Then, for all
m\geq M
we have

\displaystyle\|f-m_{\phi_{j}}(f)\|\leq\|f-f_{k}\|+\|f_{k}-m_{\phi_{j}}(f_{k})\|+\|m_{\phi_{j}}(f_{k})-m_{\phi_{j}}(f)\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{4D^{\prime}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}<\varepsilon.

We conclude that condition (1) holds.
∎

Remark 3.17
.

Note that in condition (iii) in

\|\cdot\|_{\rm cb}
, as

Remark 3.18
.

By
[
6
, Proposition 2.2.13 and Proposition 2.2.7]
a second-counable étale groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is amenable if and only if it admits a sequence
(h_{j})_{j}
of continuous positive definite functions with compact support such that

(a)

(h_{j})_{|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}}=1
, and

(b)

\lim_{j}h_{j}=1
uniformly on every compact subset of
\mathcal{G}
.

Then the associated multipliers
m_{h_{j}}
are unital completely positive maps by
Proposition

2.7
. Thus if
(h_{j})_{j}
are
\mathcal{K}
-continuous for some coefficient
D\geq 0
for all
j
, we see that (2) implies (1) in
Theorem

3.16
, and we may in fact use
(h_{j})_{j}
to verify (
3.10
). In fact, this will be implicit when we verify that AF groupoids give rise to compact quantum metric spaces in
Theorem

4.10
.

The characterization of compact quantum metric spaces arising from groupoids in
Theorem

3.16
is new even in the case of discrete groups. Recall that for a discrete group
\Gamma
there is a unique choice of metric stratification
\mathcal{K}
by
Remark

3.3
, and we see that
\mathcal{K}
-continuity of
\phi\in\mathrm{FS}(\Gamma)
is automatic.
A countable discrete group
\Gamma
is said to be
weakly amenable
if it admits a sequence
(\phi_{j})_{j}
such that
\phi_{j}\to 1
uniformly on compact subsets and

\sup_{j}\|m_{\phi_{j}}\|_{\rm cb}<\infty
.
Given a proper length function
\ell\colon\Gamma\to[0,\infty)
, we therefore see that
Theorem

3.16
provides a sufficient and necessary condition for
(\Gamma,\ell)
to yield a compact quantum metric space as in
Example

2.14
whenever
\Gamma
is weakly amenable. Notably, groups with polynomial growth are weakly amenable as they are amenable, and word-hyperbolic groups are weakly amenable by
[
33
]
. We summarize these observations in the following result.

Corollary 3.19
.

Suppose
\Gamma
is a weakly amenable countable discrete group and
(\phi_{j})_{j}
is a sequence as above. Let
L_{\ell}
be the seminorm from (
2.9
). Then
(C_{c}(\Gamma),L_{\ell})
is a compact quantum metric space if and only if for any
\varepsilon>0
there is
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
j\geq M
we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in E}\|f-m_{\phi_{j}}(f)\|_{C_{r}^{*}(\Gamma)}<\varepsilon,

where
E=\{f\in C_{c}(\Gamma)\mid f(e)=0\text{ and }L_{\ell}(f)\leq 1\}
. In particular, (
3.11
) holds for finitely generated groups of polynomial growth and word-hyperbolic groups with their associated sequences
(\phi_{j})_{j}
.

It is natural to compare
Corollary

3.19
to to Kaad’s characterization of compact quantum metric spaces from
[
28
, Theorem 3.1]
. In it, he characterizes compact quantum metric spaces as pairs
(\mathcal{X},L)
consisting of operator systems
\mathcal{X}
and slip-norms
L\colon\mathcal{X}\to[0,\infty)
which admit positive finite-dimensional approximations in the following sense: For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist an operator system
\mathcal{Y}
, a unital isometry
\iota\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
and a unital positive map
\Phi\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
with finite-dimensional range, such that

\displaystyle\|\iota(x)-\Phi(x)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq\varepsilon\cdot L(x)

for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
. By comparison,
Corollary

3.19
tells us that if the countable discrete group
\Gamma
is weakly amenable with associated sequence
(\phi_{j})_{j}
, then we obtain finite-dimensional approximations with
\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{X}=C_{c}(\Gamma)
,
\iota
the identity map on
C_{c}(\Gamma)
, and
\Phi=m_{\phi_{j}}
for
j
sufficiently large. Note however that to have the codomain
\mathcal{Y}
equal
\mathcal{X}=C_{c}(\Gamma)
, we have sacrificed positivity of
\Phi
in exhange for complete boundedness. If
(\Gamma,\ell)
has polynomial growth, then
\Gamma
is amenable and the sequence
(\phi_{j})_{j}
can be chosen so that
\phi_{j}
is positive definite and hence
m_{\phi_{j}}
is completely positive for all
j
.

Lastly in this section we prove a groupoid analogue of the result from
[
9
]
, namely that under the assumption of rapid decay, we may always find
n
large enough so that when employing the seminorm from (

) using

iterated commutators will obtain a compact quantum metric space. Note however that in the result below we still have to impose some compatibility between the metric stratification
\mathcal{K}
and the length function in order to guarantee that the Fourier multiplier is
\mathcal{K}
-continuous.
We will also impose the very weak condition on the length function that for any

there is some
\delta_{t}>0
such that
[t,t+\delta_{t})\cap\ell(\mathcal{G})=\{t\}
. This condition is of course satisfied in the case of integer-valued length functions. Note also that for discrete groups, this condition is implied by properness of the length function.

Proposition 3.20
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\ell)
be a metric groupoid where
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is compact, and
\ell\colon\mathcal{G}\to[0,\infty)
is a proper continuous length function. Suppose further that for any
t\in\ell(\mathcal{G})\subseteq\mathbb{R}
there is some
\delta_{t}>0
with
[t,t+\delta_{t})\cap\ell(\mathcal{G})=\{t\}
. Furthermore, assume that
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},d)
is a compact metric space. Fix a metric stratification
\mathcal{K}=(K_{i})_{i\in I}
, and assume moreover that for each
i\in I
there is
r_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
K_{i}\subseteq\ell^{-1}(\{r_{i}\})
.
If
(G,\ell)
has rapid decay with constants
C,s>0
, then
for any
n\geq\lfloor s\rfloor+1
the pair
(C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G}),L^{\mathcal{K},n})
is a compact quantum metric space.

Remark 3.21
.

Note that in the statement of the proposition we are not requiring that for
i\neq j\in I
we have
r_{i}\neq r_{j}
. So we could have several

be subsets of the same
\ell^{-1}(\{r\})
,
r\in\mathbb{R}
. This is for example very natural when considering a transformation groupoid
\Gamma\ltimes X
, where
K_{i}=\{\gamma_{i}\}\times X
and
K_{j}=\{\gamma_{j}\}\times X
would be subsets of the same
\ell^{-1}(\{r\})
if
\ell_{\Gamma}(\gamma_{j})=\ell_{\Gamma}(\gamma_{j})
.

Proof.

By
Theorem

3.16
it suffices to show that under the given assumptions, for any
\varepsilon>0
there is
\phi\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
with
\phi_{|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}}\in\mathbb{C}\cdot 1_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}
such that
\phi
is
\mathcal{K}
-continuous and

(3.12)

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in E_{\mathcal{K},n}}\|f-m_{\phi}(f)\|_{C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})}<\varepsilon.

Let therefore
\varepsilon>0
be given. Like in the proof of
[

, Theorem 2.6]
, observe that if
1\leq t\leq\ell(\gamma)
, then for any
n>s
, in particular for
n\geq\lfloor s\rfloor+1
, we have

\displaystyle(1+\ell(\gamma))^{2s}\leq 2^{2s}\ell(\gamma)^{2s}=2^{2s}\ell(\gamma)^{2n}\ell(\gamma)^{2(s-n)}\leq 2^{2s}\ell(\gamma)^{2n}t^{2(s-n)}.

Since
2s-2n<0
, we may choose a

such that
2^{2s}t^{2s-2n}<\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{C^{2}}
. Fix such a
t
.

By assumption on the image of
\ell
there is
\delta_{t}
such that
(t,t+\delta_{t})\cap\ell(\mathcal{G})=\emptyset
.
Using Tietze’s extension theorem, we construct a continuous function
\phi
taking values in
[0,1]
such that
\phi_{|_{B_{\ell}(t)}}=1
and
\phi_{|_{B_{\ell}(t+\delta_{t})^{c}}}=0
.
By
Proposition

2.6
, we have that
\phi\in\mathrm{FS}(\mathcal{G})
, and so we get a completely bounded multiplier
m_{\phi}\colon C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})\to C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
.
By construction of
\phi
and assumptions on
K_{i}
,
i\in I
, we either have
\phi_{|_{K_{i}}}=1
or
\phi_{|_{K_{i}}}=0
for every
i\in I
. As such,
m_{\phi}
is
\mathcal{K}
-continuous. Note in particular that
\phi_{|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}}=1
. Thus it suffices to verify that (

) holds for this

.

To this end fix an arbitrary
f\in E_{\mathcal{K},n}
. Using the rapid decay condition, we calculate

\displaystyle\|f-m_{\phi}(f)\|^{2}\leq C^{2}\|f-m_{\phi}(f)\|_{2,s}^{2}

\displaystyle=C^{2}\max\{\sup_{u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{u}}|f(\gamma)-\phi(\gamma)f(\gamma)|^{2}(1+\ell(\gamma))^{2s},\sup_{u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{u}}|f(\gamma^{-1})-\phi(\gamma^{-1})f(\gamma^{-1})|^{2}(1+\ell(\gamma))^{2s}\}

To continue the calculation, we fix
u\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
and show that independently of

we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{u}}|f(\gamma)-\phi(\gamma)f(\gamma)|^{2}(1+\ell(\gamma))^{2s}<\varepsilon^{2}/C^{2}.

The analogous estimate may be done for
\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{u}}|f(\gamma^{-1})-\phi(\gamma^{-1})f(\gamma^{-1})|^{2}(1+\ell(\gamma))^{2s}
.

Since

we in particular have

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{u}}|f(\gamma)|^{2}\ell(\gamma)^{2n}\leq\|\delta^{n}(f)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})}(\mathcal{E})}^{2}\leq L^{\mathcal{K},n}(f)^{2}\leq 1.

By the construction of
\phi
and since

, we may calculate

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{u}}|f(\gamma)

\displaystyle-\phi(\gamma)f(\gamma)|^{2}(1+\ell(\gamma))^{2s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{u}\cap B_{\ell}(t)}|f(\gamma)-\phi(\gamma)f(\gamma)|^{2}(1+\ell(\gamma))^{2s}+\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{u}\setminus B_{\ell}(t)}|f(\gamma)-\phi(\gamma)f(\gamma)|^{2}(1+\ell(\gamma))^{2s}

\displaystyle=0+\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{u}\setminus B_{\ell}(t)}|f(\gamma)|^{2}(1+\ell(\gamma))^{2s}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2s}t^{2s-2n}\cdot\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{u}\setminus B_{\ell}(t)}|f(\gamma)|^{2}\ell(\gamma)^{2n}

\displaystyle<\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{C^{2}}\cdot\|\delta^{n}(f)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})}(\mathcal{E})}^{2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{C^{2}}.

Since all estimates are independent of

∎

4.
AF groupoids

We begin this section by reminding the reader about AF groupoids, in particular those with compact unit spaces.
Suppose
\mathcal{G}
is a second-countable ample étale groupoid, where
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is a
compact Cantor space. We then say that
\mathcal{G}
is an
AF-groupoid
if there exists an increasing sequence
\mathcal{G}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{2}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq\mathcal{G}
consisting of clopen subgroupoids for which

•

\mathcal{G}_{n}
is principal for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
;

•

\mathcal{G}_{n}^{(0)}=\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
;

•

\mathcal{G}_{n}

is compact for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
;

•

\mathcal{G}=\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}_{n}
.

Note that such a
\mathcal{G}
must be principal. As shown in
[
23
, Theorem 3.9]
, any AF groupoid may be realized as the groupoid arising from a Bratteli diagram. Since we will be interested in constructing length functions from Bratteli diagrams, we proceed to explain how the groupoid and the Bratteli diagram are related.

A
Bratteli diagram

B=(V,E)
is a directed graph whose vertex set
V
and edge set
E
may be written as countable disjoint unions of non-empty finite sets, i.e.

\displaystyle V=\coprod_{n=0}^{\infty}V_{n}\quad\text{ and }\quad E=\coprod_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n},

along with maps
i\colon E_{n}\to V_{n-1}
and
t\colon E_{n}\to V_{n}

subject to the additional relations
i(E_{n})=V_{n-1}
and
t(E_{n})\subseteq V_{n}
for
n\geq 1
. The map
i
is known as the
source map
, and
t
is known as the
range map
. We will refer to the vertices in
V_{n}
as being in
level
n
for simplicity. Denote by
S(B)
the set of all sources in
B
, that is, the set of vertices
v\in V
for which are no edges with
v
as target, that is, there is no
e\in E
with
t(e)=v
.
While a Bratteli diagram could have infinitely many sources,
we will only be interested in groupoids with compact unit spaces, which will correspond to the Bratteli diagram only having finitely many sources.

To associate a groupoid to a Bratteli diagram
B=(V,E)
, we first construct its infinite path space. Given a source
v\in S(B)\cap V_{n}
, the set of infinite paths starting at
v
is the set

\displaystyle X_{v}=\{e_{n+1}e_{n+2}\ldots\mid e_{i}\in E_{i},i(e_{n+1})=v,\text{ and }i(e_{n+k+1})=t(e_{n+k})\text{ for all $k\geq 1$}\}.

As a piece of suggestive notation, for
x\in X_{v}
we will write
x=x_{n+1}x_{n+2}\ldots=x_{[n+1,\infty)}
where
x_{i}\in E_{i}
for all
i\geq n+1
. The infinite path space associated to
B
is then, as a set, given by

\displaystyle X_{B}=\coprod_{v\in S(B)}X_{v}

We endow
X_{B}
with the topology which has a basis of compact open cylinder sets defined by the finite paths. Specifically, given a finite path
\mu
with with
i(\mu)\in S(B)\cap V_{n}
and length denoted by
|\mu|
, the cylinder set associated with
\mu
is

\displaystyle Z(\mu)=\{e_{n+1}e_{n+2}\ldots\in X_{i(\mu)}\mid e_{n+1}\ldots e_{n+|\mu|}=\mu\}.

From the infinite path space, we may now construct a groupoid.
For every
N\geq 1
we define

\displaystyle P_{N}=\{(x,y)\in X_{B}^{2}\mid i(x)\in V_{m}\cap S(B),i(y)\in V_{n}\cap S(B),m,n\leq N,x_{k}=y_{k}\forall k>N\},

which we may view as the set of pairs of paths which eventually agree. Equipped with the relative topology,
P_{N}
is a compact principal ample Hausdorff groupoid with
((x,y),(z,w))\in P_{N}^{(2)}
if and only if
y=z
, and so

(4.1)

\displaystyle(x,y)(y,z)=(x,z)\quad\text{and}\quad(x,y)^{-1}=(y,x).

The unit space may be identified with

\displaystyle\coprod_{n=0}^{N}\coprod_{v\in S(B)\cap V_{n}}Z(v).

We then define the groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{B}
associated with the Bratteli diagram
B=(V,E)
as the increasing union

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{B}:=\bigcup_{N\geq 1}P_{N}

equipped with the inductive limit topology. The groupoid multiplication and inversion are given by the natural extensions of (
4.1
). Of importance to us is that the topology has a basis defined by pairs of finite paths
\mu
and
\lambda
with
i(\mu)\in S(B)\cap V_{m}
,
i(\lambda)\in S(B)\cap V_{n}
for
m,n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, and with
t(\mu)=t(\lambda)
. The cylinder set is given by

(4.2)

\displaystyle Z(\mu,\lambda)=\{(x,y)\in Z(\mu)\times Z(\lambda)\mid x_{[m+|\mu|+1,\infty)}=y_{[n+|\lambda|+1,\infty)}\}.

The unit space of
\mathcal{G}_{B}
may be identified with
X_{B}
, and it is compact if and only if
B
has finitely many sources. .
Setting

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{n}:=P_{n}\cup\mathcal{G}^{(0)},

the groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{B}
is realized as an increasing union of principal clopen compact subgroupoids as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{B}:=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}_{n}.

We proceed to introduce a length function on
\mathcal{G}_{B}
constructed from the Bratteli diagram
B=(V,E)
, which in turn will be used in the construction of compact quantum spaces below. For this we introduce the following notation.
For
v,w\in V
we will by
P(v,w)
denote the set

(4.4)

\displaystyle P(v,w):=\{\mu\mid\mu\text{ is a finite path with }i(\mu)=v\text{ and }t(\mu)=w\}.

If
W\subset V
is a subset of the vertices, define

(4.5)

\displaystyle P(v,W):=\{\mu\mid\mu\text{ is a finite path with }i(\mu)=v\text{ and }t(\mu)\in W\}.

We may now define the length function we will consider in this section.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an AF groupoid with compact unit space, and suppose we are given a Bratteli diagram
B=(V,E)
for
\mathcal{G}
. For
(x,y)\in\mathcal{G}
, denote by

(4.6)

\displaystyle k(x,y)=\min\{m\mid x_{[m+1,\infty)}=y_{[m+1,\infty)}\}.

Moreover, we define the function

(4.7)

\displaystyle\ell(x,y)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $x=y$}\\
\sum_{v\in S(B)}|P(v,V_{k(x,y)})|&\text{ if $x\neq y$}.\end{cases}

Remark 4.2
.

Note that different Bratteli diagrams could be associated with the same AF groupoid, therefore leading to (potentially) different length functions.

We collect a basic observation about
k
and
\ell
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose
\mathcal{G}
is an AF groupoid with compact unit space, and suppose
B
is a Bratteli diagram for
\mathcal{G}
. Let
k
and
\ell
be as in
Definition

4.1
.
Suppose
(x,y),(z,w)\in\mathcal{G}
with
x\neq y
and
z\neq w
. If
\ell(x,y)>\ell(z,w)
, then
k(x,y)>k(z,w)
.

Proof.

Note that
x\neq y
and
z\neq w
implies that
(x,y),(z,w)\not\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, and hence

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S(B)}|P(v,V_{k(x,y)})|=\ell(x,y)>\ell(w,z)=\sum_{v\in S(B)}|P(v,V_{k(w,z)})|,

from which we see that
k(x,y)>k(w,z)
.
∎

We proceed to verify that
\ell
is indeed a continuous length function.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an AF groupoid with compact unit space, and suppose we are given a Bratteli diagram
B=(V,E)
for
\mathcal{G}
. The function
\ell
from
Definition

4.1

is a continuous length function on
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

We first verify that
\ell
is a length function. Recall that for
(x,y)\in\mathcal{G}_{B}
, we set
k(x,y)=\max\{m\mid x_{m+1}\neq y_{m+1}\}
as in (
4.6
).
First, note that
\ell(x,x)=0
by definition. Moreover, if
x\neq y
, then
k(x,y)\geq 1
and so
P(v,V_{k(x,y)})\neq\emptyset
for at least one
v\in S(B)
. We conclude that
\ell(x,y)=0
if and only if
x=y
.

Since
(x,y)^{-1}=(y,x)
in
\mathcal{G}_{B}
, we see that
\ell((x,y)^{-1})=\ell(x,y)
, since
k(x,y)=k(y,x)
.

Now, let
(x,y),(y,z),(x,z)\in\mathcal{G}_{B}
. We wish to show that

\displaystyle\ell(x,z)\leq\ell(x,y)+\ell(y,z).

Suppose for the sake of contradiction that
\ell(x,z)>\max\{\ell(x,y),\ell(y,z)\}
. By
Lemma

4.3
we then have
k(x,z)>\max\{k(x,y),k(y,z)\}
. But then
x_{[l_{1},\infty)}=y_{[l_{1},\infty)}
for some
l_{1}<k(x,z)
, and
y_{[l_{2},\infty)}=z_{[l_{2},\infty)}
for some
l_{2}<k(x,z)
. We conclude that
x_{[l,\infty)}=z_{[l,\infty)}
for
l<k(x,z)
contradicting minimality of
k(x,z)
. We deduce a stronger version of the triangle inequality, namely
\ell(x,z)\leq\max\{\ell(x,y),\ell(y,z)\}
.

To see that
\ell
is continuous, suppose
(x^{(j)},y^{(j)})\to(x,y)
in
\mathcal{G}_{B}
. There are finite paths
\mu,\lambda
with
t(\mu)=t(\lambda)
such that
(x,y)\in Z(\mu,\lambda)
, cf. (
4.2
). We then have
x_{[m+|\mu|+1,\infty)}=y_{[n+|\lambda|+1,\infty)}
, and suppose that
m+|\mu|
is minimal with this property, i.e., that
x
and
y
do not start agreeing at an earlier level. Since
Z(\mu,\lambda)
is open, there is
N
such that for all
j\geq N
,
(x^{(j)},y^{(j)})\in Z(\mu,\lambda)
. We claim that
N
can also be chosen so that
x^{(j)}
and
y^{(j)}
do not agree at a level earlier than
m+|\mu|=n+|\lambda|
. Indeed, if no such
N
exists, that is, if for arbitrarily large
j
, there is
l_{j}<m+|\mu|+1
such that
x^{(j)}_{[l_{j},\infty)}=y^{(j)}_{[l_{j},\infty)}
, we would have

\displaystyle s(x^{(j)},y^{(j)})\not\to s(x,y)=y\quad\text{or}\quad r(x^{(j)},y^{(j)})\not\to r(x,y)=x,

thus implying that
s
or
r
is not continuous, a contradiction. Thus
x^{(j)}
and
y^{(j)}
eventually agree from level
m+|\mu|
, and
\ell(x^{(j)},y^{(j)})=\ell(x,y)
. Thus
\ell
is continuous.
∎

To employ the machinery from
Section

3
, it will also be important that the length function
\ell
is proper, which we proceed to verify.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an AF groupoid with compact unit space, and suppose we are given a Bratteli diagram
B=(V,E)
for
\mathcal{G}
. Let
\ell
be the length function from
Definition

4.1
. For any
M\geq 0
there is
N(M)
such that
B_{\ell}(M)\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{N(M)}
. In particular,
\ell
is a proper length function.

Proof.

Note first that if
\ell(\mathcal{G})\subseteq[0,R]
for some
R<\infty
, then the Bratteli diagram must stabilize, that is, eventually there are no new paths from
V_{k}
to
V_{k+1}
. Then
\mathcal{G}
is itself a compact groupoid, and
B_{\ell}(M)\subseteq\mathcal{G}
is compact for any
M\geq 0
as
\ell
is continuous and
B_{\ell}(M)
is therefore a closed subset of a compact groupoid.
Thus
\ell
is proper.

Suppose therefore that
\ell
is an unbounded function, so that
the Bratteli diagram does not stabilize.
Let
M\geq 0
. Since
\ell
counts the number of paths from the sources to a specific level, there must be
N(M)
such that
B_{\ell}(M)\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{N(M)}
. As before, we deduce that
B_{\ell}(M)
is a closed subset of a compact groupoid, and therefore compact itself. The statement follows.

∎

Remark 4.6
.

We will later want to relate the subgroupoids
\mathcal{G}_{n}
to the balls
B_{\ell}(R)
,
R\geq 0
.
It could happen that the number of paths from the sources to
V_{n}
is the same as the number of paths from the sources to
V_{n+1}
, while
\mathcal{G}_{n+1}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{n}\neq\emptyset
. This happens for example if there is only one edge from each vertex in
V_{n}
to vertices in
V_{n+1}
, but one of the vertices in
V_{n+1}
receives (at least) two edges. It is therefore not necessarily the case that every
\mathcal{G}_{n}
is a ball for

. However, for any

there is

such that
\mathcal{G}_{m}
equals

for some

. If
\mathcal{G}
is compact, this holds as

is then bounded and
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}_{m}
for sufficiently large

.
\mathcal{G}
then equals the
\ell
-ball of any sufficiently large radius. If
\mathcal{G}
is not compact, then
\ell
must be unbounded since it is proper by

. As

counts the number of paths up to a level, we see that such an

and accompanying

must exist.

For the sake of completeness, we verify that the metric groupoid

has linear growth, and therefore has rapid decay, almost by construction.

.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an AF groupoid with compact unit space, and suppose we are given a Bratteli diagram

for
\mathcal{G}
. Then the groupoid

has linear growth with respect to the length function

from (

.

If
\mathcal{G}
is compact we are done since

\mathcal{G}

is unbounded by

.
Let

. Let

, then

Recall that an AF groupoid
\mathcal{G}
with compact unit space, as well as all the subgroupoids
\mathcal{G}_{n}

are principal. Moreover,
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is a Cantor space. Pick any metric

we then have a metric stratification of

Note that many of the

We record the following result, guaranteeing that natural sub-systems determined by the clopen compact subgroupoids

, yield compact quantum metric spaces.

.

Suppose
\mathcal{G}
is an AF groupoid with compact unit space, and that

to be the a metric stratification of

as in (

).

be a Bratteli diagram for
\mathcal{G}
with associated length function

given by (

).

as in

.

, let

Remark 4.9
.

If we venture far enough out in the diagram we may relate our length function to the number of paths in the augmented diagram:

We may now prove the main theorem of this section, showing that AF groupoids induce compact quantum metric space structures through data naturally associated with it, namely a Bratteli diagram and a metric on the unit space. Note that despite the linear growth result from

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x,y)\in\mathcal{G}_{y}\\
\ell(x,y)>M\end{subarray}}\ell(x,y)^{-2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=M+1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x,y)\in\mathcal{G}_{y}\\
\ell(x,y)=k\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell(x,y)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=M+1}^{\infty}\frac{|\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{G}_{y}\mid\ell(x,y)=k\}|}{k^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{h=l(M+1)}^{\infty}\frac{|\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{G}_{y}\mid\ell(x,y)=|P(\overline{v},V_{h})|\}|}{|P(\overline{v},V_{h})|^{2}}.

We are therefore interested in the number of paths from

We may express the number of paths as follows

\displaystyle|P(\overline{v},t(y_{h}))|=|P(\overline{v},t(y_{h-1}))|\cdot(|P(t(y_{h-1}),t(y_{h}))|-1))+A_{h}

•

which are not

.

Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{h=l(M+1)}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{|\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{G}_{y}\mid\ell(x,y)=|P(\overline{v},V_{h})|\}|}{|P(\overline{v},V_{h})|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{h=l(M+1)}^{\infty}\frac{|P(\overline{v},t(y_{h-1}))|(|P(t(y_{h-1}),t(y_{h}))|-1)+A_{h}}{|P(\overline{v},V_{h})|^{2}}

\displaystyle I_{1}:=\sum_{h=l(M+1)}^{\infty}\frac{|P(\overline{v},t(y_{h-1}))|(|P(t(y_{h-1}),t(y_{h}))|-1)}{|P(\overline{v},V_{h})|^{2}}<\varepsilon/2,

and

by choosing

large enough. Noting that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{h=l(M+1)}^{\infty}\frac{|P(\overline{v},t(y_{h-1}))|(|P(t(y_{h-1}),t(y_{h}))|-1)}{|P(\overline{v},V_{h})|^{2}}

|P(t(v_{h-1}),t(v_{h}))|\geq 2\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|P(\overline{v},V_{h})|}.

The sum is only over levels where we get new contributions, so we reindex such that each term is non-zero. We end up with a new sequence

where we have received

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}B_{p}\leq\frac{1}{|P(\overline{v},V_{l(M+1)})|}+\sum_{p=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|P(\overline{v},V_{l(M+1)})|+2^{p-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|P(\overline{v},V_{l(M+1)})|}+\frac{1}{|P(\overline{v},V_{l(M+1)})|^{1/2}}\cdot\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2^{p})^{1/2}}

.

For the second sum, we get a bound by pretending that the only increases to

\displaystyle I_{2}^{\prime}:=\sum_{h=l(M+1)}^{\infty}\frac{A_{h}}{(\sum_{j=l(M+1)}^{h}A_{j})^{3/2}}=\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\frac{B_{t}}{(\sum_{s=1}^{t}B_{s})^{3/2}}=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\sum_{r=2^{t}}^{2^{t+1}-1}\frac{B_{r}}{(\sum_{s=1}^{r}B_{s})^{3/2}}.

. We may therefore bound the sum as follows

Since

It follows that there is

Example 4.11

(The CAR algebra)
.

The CAR algebra, see e.g.
[
22
, Example III.5.4]
, is a well-studied
C^{*}
-algebra with a groupoid model which admits a particularly simple Bratteli diagram and resulting length function. Note that one approach to quantum metric geometry of the CAR algebra has previously been noted by Aguilar in for example
[
5
, Example 2.4]
. We provide a groupoid alternative to this using
Theorem

4.10
. Indeed, a Bratteli diagram
B=(V,E)
for the CAR algebra is given by one vertex at each level, with two edges connecting level
k
to
k+1
for
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
as follows

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bullet}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bullet}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bullet}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bullet}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}

Denote by
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}_{B}
the resulting groupoid, and
\ell
the length function as in (
4.7
).
The length function takes a particularly simple form, namely

\displaystyle\ell(x,y)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $x=y$}\\
2^{m}&\text{ if $m=k(x,y)\geq 1$}\end{cases}

for
(x,y)\in\mathcal{G}_{B}
, where
k(x,y)
is as in
Definition

4.1
. The unit space
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
for
\mathcal{G}
is the Cantor space, and we may pick any metric
d
on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
for which
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},d)
is a compact metric space. Choosing the metric stratification as in (
4.8
), we conclude that
(C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G}),L^{\mathcal{K},n})
is a compact quantum metric space for any
n\geq 1
. Moreover, we obtain compact quantum metric spaces
(C_{c}^{\mathcal{K}_{m},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G}_{m}),(L^{\mathcal{K},n})_{|_{C_{c}^{\mathcal{K}_{m},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G}_{m})}})
from the subgroupoids
\mathcal{G}_{m}
. We see that
\mathcal{G}_{m}=B_{\ell}(2^{m})
for
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and therefore by
Theorem

4.10
we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}_{Q}((C_{c}^{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G}),L^{\mathcal{K},n})

\displaystyle,(C_{c}^{\mathcal{K}_{m},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G}_{m}),(L^{\mathcal{K},n})_{|_{C_{c}^{\mathcal{K}_{m},\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{G}_{m})}})\leq\left(\sup_{y\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x,y)\in\mathcal{G}_{y}\\
\ell(x,y)>2^{n}\end{subarray}}\ell(x,y)^{-2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2\cdot\sum_{k\geq n+1}\frac{1}{2^{2k}}\right)^{1/2}=\frac{2^{1-2n}}{3},

where the second inequality comes from using the fact that for each level there are two edges along which an infinite path might follow.

Pseudo-Differential Operators and Generalized Random Fields over Tori
Nicolas Escobar

Abstract
Matérn covariance functions are ubiquitous in spatial statistics, valued for their interpretable parameters and well-understood sample path properties in Euclidean settings. This paper examines whether these desirable properties transfer to manifold domains through rigorous analysis of Matérn processes on tori using pseudo-differential operator theory. We establish that processes on d d -dimensional tori require smoothness parameter ν > 3 ​ d / 2 \nu>3d/2 to achieve regularity C loc ( ν − 3 ​ d / 2 ) − C^{(\nu-3d/2)^{-}}_{\text{loc}} , revealing a dimension-dependent threshold that contrasts with the Euclidean requirement of merely ν > 0 \nu>0 . Our proof employs the Cardona-Martínez theory of pseudo-differential operators, providing new analytical tools to the study of random fields over manifolds. We also introduce the canonical-Matérn process, a parameter family that achieves regularity C loc ( ν − 3 ​ d / 2 + 2 ) − C^{(\nu-3d/2+2)^{-}}_{\text{loc}} , gaining two orders of smoothness over standard Matérn processes.

Introduction

The analysis of spatial data on closed manifolds has become increasingly relevant across diverse scientific disciplines,
from climate modeling on spherical domains
[
1
]
to neuroimaging on cortical surfaces
[
2
,
3
]
.
A predominant approach in this field involves modeling spatial phenomena as Gaussian random fields (GRFs),
with the Matérn covariance function serving as the de facto standard for characterizing spatial dependence
[
6
]
.
This methodological consensus is reinforced by the computational accessibility of tools such as INLA (Integrated Nested Laplace Approximations)
[
4
,
5
]

and the SPDE approach
[
6
]
, which has seen extensive development over the past decade
[
7
]
.

This paper challenges the automaticity of this approach by examining a fundamental but often overlooked question:
to what extent do the desirable properties of the Matérn covariance function in Euclidean space transfer to manifold settings?
While the mathematical formulation of the Matérn covariance can be extended to general Riemannian manifolds
through the spectral decomposition of the Laplace-Beltrami operator, such formal extensions
do not guarantee that the resulting stochastic processes retain the interpretability
and regularity properties that make the Euclidean Matérn so appealing.

The Euclidean Matérn covariance function possesses exceptional properties.
Its parameters, smoothness and range, translate directly to sample path properties,
providing practitioners with intuitive control over the random field’s behavior.
However, this remarkable correspondence between parameters and path properties is,
in fact, exceptional rather than generic.
For most covariance functions, even in Euclidean settings,
deducing sample path regularity from the covariance structure can be challenging.
When extending to manifold domains, these challenges are compounded by geometric considerations.
The curvature and topological properties of the underlying manifold
can fundamentally alter the relationship between covariance parameters and sample path behavior.
In principle, what appears as a minor mathematical generalization (replacing
the Euclidean Laplacian with the Laplace-Beltrami operator) could result in
stochastic processes with markedly different characteristics than their Euclidean counterparts.

To address these fundamental questions, we adopt the framework of pseudo-differential operators on tori,
which provides both theoretical insight and computational tractability.
The torus serves as an ideal testing ground: it is geometrically non-trivial
(possessing periodic boundary conditions and non-trivial topology) yet mathematically tractable,
allowing for explicit calculations that would be intractable on more general manifolds.
Moreover, tori appear naturally in many applications, from atmospheric modeling
(where longitude is naturally periodic) to crystallography and signal processing on periodic domains.

Our analysis reveals surprising dimension-dependent phenomena that have profound implications for practitioners.
Most strikingly, we establish that Matérn processes on tori require fundamentally different smoothness conditions
than their Euclidean counterparts to achieve comparable sample path regularity.
Specifically, while Euclidean Matérn processes with smoothness parameter
\nu>0
yield well-defined sample paths,
processes on
d
-dimensional tori require
\nu>3d/2
to achieve analogous regularity.

Beyond the standard Matérn family, we introduce and analyze the canonical-Matérn process on tori,
a novel construction that unifies geometric and stochastic aspects of spatial modeling.
This process incorporates two additional degrees of freedom beyond the classical Matérn parameters,
allowing practitioners to independently control the contribution of geometric structure (through the canonical field component)
and fine-scale variability (through the white noise component).
The resulting flexibility enables models that can capture both large-scale spatial trends
dictated by the domain geometry and local stochastic fluctuations,
providing a richer modeling framework than either pure Matérn or purely geometric approaches alone.

This paper makes several contributions to the theory and practice of spatial modeling on manifolds.
First, in Section 2, we establish the mathematical preliminaries, introducing the framework
of pseudo-differential operators on tori and generalized random fields (GeRFs).
This framework provides the necessary tools
to rigorously analyze the relationship between spectral properties and sample path behavior.
Section 3 presents our main theoretical results on the regularity of Matérn processes on tori,
providing both necessary and sufficient conditions for sample path continuity and differentiability.
The proof techniques developed here, based on the Cardona-Martínez theory of pseudo-differential operators,
offer a new perspective that may extend to more general manifold settings.

Section 4 introduces the canonical-Matérn process and establishes its fundamental properties.
We derive the complete characterization of this three-parameter family,
demonstrate how the two additional degrees of freedom control the balance between geometric and stochastic components,
and establish regularity results that extend those of the classical Matérn family.
This construction provides practitioners with a flexible modeling framework that can be tuned to specific applications.
The appendix contains technical extensions, including a generalization of the Cardona-Martínez theorem
to negative integer orders, which may be of independent mathematical interest.

The implications of our results extend beyond theoretical considerations.
For practitioners working with spatial data on periodic domains, our findings suggest that
standard software implementations of Matérn models may produce misleading results when naively applied to manifold settings.
The dimension-dependent smoothness threshold we identify implies that models that work well in low dimensions
may require significant parameter adjustments in higher dimensions to maintain desired properties.
Furthermore, our canonical-Matérn construction offers an enhanced modeling framework
that naturally incorporates the geometric structure of the domain, potentially leading to more
parsimonious and interpretable models for certain applications.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Matérn Operator

The Matérn operator is defined as
\mathcal{L}^{\nu/2}
where
\mathcal{L}=\kappa^{2}-\Delta
is a differential operator with
\kappa>0
(the range parameter) and
\Delta
the Laplacian.
The parameter
\nu>0
is called the smoothness parameter.

On the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, the Matérn operator acts via its symbol in Fourier space.
For a function
f
, the result
g=\mathcal{L}^{\nu/2}f
has Fourier coefficients

\hat{g}(k)=\sigma(k)\hat{f}(k),

where
\sigma(k)=(\kappa^{2}+|k|^{2})^{\nu/2}
is the symbol of the Matérn operator.

Equivalently, this can be expressed as an integral operator:

g(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}k(x,y)f(y)dy,

where the kernel
k(x,y)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\kappa^{2}+|k|^{2})^{\nu/2}e^{2\pi ik\cdot(x-y)}

may have support equal to the entire torus.

2.2
Function Spaces

Definition 2.1

(Test Function Space)
.

The space of rapidly decreasing Fourier coefficients on the torus is:

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{T}^{d})=\left\{u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}):\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(1+|k|^{2})^{N}|\hat{u}(k)|<\infty\text{ for all }N\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

where
\hat{u}(k)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u(x)e^{-2\pi ik\cdot x}dx
denotes the
k
-th Fourier coefficient.

This is the natural analog of the Schwartz space for tori, where compactness eliminates the need for spatial decay conditions.

Proposition 2.2

(Dual Space)
.

The dual space
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
consists of continuous linear functionals on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, characterized as sequences
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
with polynomial growth:

|\mu_{k}|\leq C(1+|k|^{2})^{N}\quad\text{for some }C>0,N\in\mathbb{N}

2.3
Pseudo-Differential Operators

Definition 2.3

(Pseudo-differential Operators)
.

Let
g:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
be polynomially bounded with
\check{g}=\bar{g}
. The operator
\mathcal{L}_{g}
acts on

D(\mathcal{L}_{g})=\{\mu\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d}):\mathscr{F}\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{SG}^{+}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\}

via:

\mathcal{L}_{g}[\mu]=\mathscr{F}^{-1}[g\cdot\mathscr{F}[\mu]]

where multiplication is pointwise:
\{g(k)\mu_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
.

The Fourier transform
\mathscr{F}
and its inverse
\mathscr{F}^{-1}
are defined in the standard way for functions on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, with dual extension to distributions.

2.4
Generalized Random Fields

Definition 2.4

(Generalized Random Field)
.

A real Generalized Random Field (GeRF)
Z
on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
is a continuous linear map from
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
to
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
.

For a mean-zero GeRF
Z
, there exists a covariance distribution
C_{Z}
such that

\mathbb{C}\mathrm{ov}(\langle Z,\varphi\rangle,\langle Z,\phi\rangle)=\langle C_{Z},\varphi\otimes\bar{\phi}\rangle.

We adopt the standard notation
\check{f}(x)=f(-x)
for the reflection of a function about the origin.

Definition 2.5

(Stationarity and Spectral Measure)
.

A GeRF
Z
is
stationary
if there exists a distribution
\rho_{Z}
such that

\langle C_{Z},\varphi\otimes\bar{\phi}\rangle=\langle\rho_{Z},\varphi\ast\check{\bar{\phi}}\rangle.

The
spectral measure

\mu_{Z}
is a positive discrete measure on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
\rho_{Z}=\mathscr{F}^{-1}(\mu_{Z})
.

This framework allows us to study how pseudo-differential operators act on random fields:
if
U
is stationary with spectral measure
\mu_{U}
, then
\mathcal{L}_{g}U
is stationary with spectral measure
|g|^{2}\mu_{U}
.

The Matérn covariance function arises as the covariance of a stationary solution to the stochastic equation
\mathcal{L}^{\nu/2}u=W
, where
W
is white noise.

3
Regularity of Matérn Processes on Tori

Sample path regularity is best measured by determining whether paths belong to Hölder spaces.

Definition 3.1

(Local Hölder and almost-Hölder spaces)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\gamma\in[0,1]
. Let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{T}^{d}
be an open set.

1.

The local Hölder space
C^{n,\gamma}_{loc}(\mathcal{O})
is the space of functions
f
on
\mathcal{O}
for which
\partial^{\bm{\alpha}}f
exists for all multi-indices
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
|\bm{\alpha}|:=\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{d}\leq n
, and such that the highest order partial derivatives satisfy a Hölder condition of the form: for all compact subsets
K\subset\mathcal{O}
there is a constant
C_{K}>0
such that

|\partial^{\bm{\alpha}}f(\bm{x})-\partial^{\bm{\alpha}}f(\bm{y})|\leq C_{K}\|\bm{x}-\bm{y}\|^{\gamma}

for all
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in K
and
|\bm{\alpha}|=n
.

2.

The local almost-Hölder space
C^{(n+\gamma)^{-}}_{loc}(\mathcal{O})
is defined as
\bigcap_{n^{\prime}+\gamma^{\prime}<n+\gamma}C^{n^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}}_{loc}(\mathcal{O})
.

For Euclidean Matérn processes,
[
10
]
establishes the following benchmark result.

Proposition 3.2
.

A centered Matérn GP with smoothness parameter
\nu>0
has samples in
C^{\nu}_{loc}(\mathcal{O})
and no more. In particular, if
\nu=n+1/2
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the GP has samples in
C^{(n+1/2)-}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and no more.

The natural question is whether this result extends to processes over tori.
For torus Matérn processes, Borovitskiy and collaborators showed that the covariance function is

k_{\nu}(x,x^{\prime})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{\sigma^{2}2^{1-\nu}}{C_{\nu}^{\prime}\Gamma(\nu)}\left(\sqrt{2\nu}\frac{\|x-x^{\prime}+n\|}{\kappa}\right)^{\nu}K_{\nu}\left(\sqrt{2\nu}\frac{\|x-x^{\prime}+n\|}{\kappa}\right)

(1)

This infinite series form makes the direct argument from the Euclidean case inapplicable.
Nevertheless, the analogous result holds, and we provide an alternative proof using pseudo-differential operator theory.

Proposition 3.3

(Path regularity of Torus Matérn Processes)
.

A GP over
\mathbb{T}^{n}
with covariance function (
1
) and smoothness parameter
\nu>3n/2
has samples in
C^{(\nu-3n/2)^{-}}_{loc}(\mathcal{O})
and no more.

Proof.

Write
\rho_{Z}(x-x^{\prime})=k_{\nu}(x,x^{\prime})
. Then
\int k(x,x^{\prime})\varphi(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}=\rho\ast\varphi(x)
.
Thus, the stationary covariance (
1
) defines an operator on
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
by

L_{\rho_{Z}}f=\rho_{Z}\ast f

It is easy to see that this is in fact a pseudo-differential operator with symbol
\sigma(k)=(\kappa^{2}+|k|^{2})^{-\nu}
.
Clearly

|\sigma(k)|\leq C|k|^{-2\nu}

In our application, we use the extended version of Theorem 3.1 from
[
9
]
(see Theorem
A.15
in Appendix
A.7
). With
\rho=1
,
\beta=0
and
|\alpha|=2n
, for
N>-2\nu+3n
,

\sup_{x\neq y}|x-y|^{N}|\partial^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}k(x,y)|=C_{\alpha\beta\gamma}<\infty

Hence,

|\partial^{\alpha}k(h)-\partial^{\alpha}k(0)|=\mathcal{O}(\norm{h}^{2\epsilon})

for all
\epsilon<\nu-3n/2
. The result now follows from the stationary case of Theorem 7 in
[
10
]
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

While Theorem 3.1 in
[
9
]
is stated only for non-negative
N
, Theorem
A.15
extends this result to negative
N
using oscillatory integral methods. For
N<0
, the kernel exhibits Hölder continuity of order
|N|
rather than a singularity bound, which is precisely what we require for the regularity analysis when
\nu<3n/2
.

This result shows that torus Matérn processes with
\nu>3n/2
achieve regularity
C^{(\nu-3n/2)^{-}}_{loc}
. While the smoothness requirement is more restrictive than in the Euclidean case, the underlying theory extends to the manifold setting.

4
Canonical Field and Canonical-Matérn Process

4.1
The Canonical Field

An interesting construction emerges when we consider the relationship between graph Laplacians and manifold operators.
On a graph, the canonical Gaussian field has covariance structure equal to the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse of the graph Laplacian,
L^{+}
.
Since the symbol of the negative Laplacian on the torus is
\sigma_{L}(k)=k^{2}
, this motivates extending the concept to continuous spaces.

Definition 4.1

(Canonical Field)
.

The canonical field
G
over
\mathbb{T}^{d}
is the GeRF with spectral measure

\mu_{B}(k)=\begin{cases}|k|^{-2}&k\neq 0\\
0&k=0\end{cases}

(2)

The covariance function of this field has an explicit form that reveals dimension-dependent behavior.

Proposition 4.2

(Explicit Covariance Function of the Canonical Field)
.

The covariance function of the canonical field can be computed explicitly using the spectral measure.
For
h\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we have:

\rho_{G}(h)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{|k|^{2}}e^{ik\cdot h}

This series has dimension-dependent behavior:

•

In dimension
d=1
: The covariance function is given by

\rho_{G}(h)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}-\pi|h|+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\quad\text{for }h\in[-\pi,\pi]

•

In dimension
d=2
: The covariance exhibits a logarithmic singularity at the origin:

\rho_{G}(h)\sim-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|h|\quad\text{as }h\to 0

•

In dimensions
d\geq 3
: The covariance has a power-law singularity:

\rho_{G}(h)\sim\frac{c_{d}}{|h|^{d-2}}\quad\text{as }h\to 0

4.1.1
Discretization and Numerical Implementation

The canonical field admits a natural discretization on regular grids that converges to the continuous field.

Proposition 4.3

(Discrete Canonical Field Convergence)
.

Consider the discrete canonical field on a regular
n\times n\times\cdots\times n
grid over
\mathbb{T}^{d}
.
It has discrete spectral measure

\mu_{n}(k)=\lambda_{k}^{-1}

where
\lambda_{k}=\frac{4}{h^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi k_{j}/n)
are the eigenvalues of the discrete Laplacian
with grid spacing
h=2\pi/n
.

As
n\to\infty
, we have:

1.

The discrete spectral measure converges:
\mu_{n}(k)\to(4\pi^{2}|k|^{2})^{-1}
for each
k\neq 0

(which matches the continuous canonical field up to normalization constants).

2.

For Gaussian fields, this spectral measure convergence implies weak convergence
of the finite-dimensional distributions of the discrete field to those of the continuous canonical field.

The proof uses standard spectral convergence results for discrete Laplacians and the continuous mapping theorem for Gaussian fields.

4.2
Canonical-Matérn Process

The canonical field provides a practical route to increased regularity without repeatedly solving large linear systems. From the literature of Lindgren’s approach that obtaining solutions with higher smoothness parameters
\nu
requires recursive application of the solver, increasing computational cost.

Definition 4.4

(Canonical-Matern GeRF)
.

Let
u_{C}
be the solution to
\mathcal{L}^{\nu/2}u_{C}=G
, where
G
is the canonical field from Definition
4.1
.

It is easy to see that the canonical-Matérn GeRF has spectral density

\mu_{CM}=|k|^{-2}\cdot(\kappa^{2}+|k|^{2})^{-\nu/2}

This construction inherits regularity properties from both the canonical field and the Matérn operator.

Proposition 4.5

(Regularity of the canonical-Matern process)
.

The canonical-Matérn process
has samples in
C^{(\nu-3d/2+2)^{-}}_{loc}(\mathcal{O})
and no more.

Proof.

The proof follows the same structure as the proof of Proposition
3.3
. The spectral density
\mu_{CM}
has symbol
\sigma_{CM}
such that

|\sigma_{CM}(k)|\leq C|k|^{-\nu-2}

The additional decay from the
|k|^{-2}
factor from the canonical field provides the extra two orders of regularity.
∎

The canonical-Matérn process thus achieves regularity
C^{(\nu-3d/2+2)^{-}}_{loc}
, gaining two additional orders compared to the standard torus Matérn process with the same smoothness parameter
\nu
.

4.2.1
Convolution Structure and Computational Methods

While Proposition
4.5
establishes the regularity properties of the canonical-Matérn process, the convolution structure provides insight into its computational implementation.

To make the convolution structure explicit, we introduce the following notation:

•

C_{B}
denotes the covariance function of the canonical field
G
from Definition
4.1
, corresponding to spectral measure
\mu_{B}(k)=|k|^{-2}
for
k\neq 0

•

C_{M}
denotes the periodized Matérn covariance on the torus, given by
C_{M}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}k_{\nu}(\|x+2\pi n\|)

•

C_{CM}
denotes the canonical-Matérn covariance function, which we show equals the convolution
C_{B}\star C_{M}

The following theorem reveals that the canonical-Matérn covariance can be expressed as an absolutely convergent series with terms that decay exponentially fast.

Theorem 4.6

(Convolution Decomposition on Tori)
.

Let
\mathbb{T}^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{d}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{d}
be the d-dimensional torus,
C_{B}
be the covariance function of the canonical field from Definition
4.1
, and
k_{\nu}
be the Matérn kernel with smoothness parameter
\nu>0
and range parameter
\rho>0
. Consider the canonical-Matérn covariance function defined as the convolution:

C_{CM}(x)=(C_{B}\star C_{M})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}C_{B}(y)C_{M}(x-y)\,dy

where
C_{M}
is the periodized Matérn covariance:

C_{M}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}k_{\nu}(\|x+2\pi n\|)

(a)

Decomposition.
The canonical-Matérn covariance admits the representation:

C_{CM}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}I_{n}(x)

where each term is defined as:

I_{n}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}C_{B}(y)\,k_{\nu}(\|x-y+2\pi n\|)\,dy

(b)

Exponential Convergence.
The series converges absolutely and uniformly on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, with exponential decay:

|I_{n}(x)|\leq K\cdot\|n\|^{\nu-1/2}\cdot e^{-2\pi\sqrt{2\nu}\|n\|/\rho}

where
K
is a constant depending on
\nu
,
\rho
,
\sigma^{2}
, and
\|C_{B}\|_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Proof of (a).
By the periodization formula for Matérn covariance on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
:

C_{M}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}k_{\nu}(\|x+2\pi n\|)

To justify interchanging convolution with the infinite sum, we verify uniform convergence. The Matérn kernel satisfies the asymptotic bound (see Lemma
A.14
in Appendix):

k_{\nu}(r)\leq C(\nu,\rho,\sigma)\cdot r^{\nu-1/2}\cdot e^{-r\sqrt{2\nu}/\rho}

for
r
sufficiently large.

For the partial sums
S_{N}(x)=\sum_{\|n\|_{\infty}\leq N}k_{\nu}(\|x+2\pi n\|)
, since
\|x+2\pi n\|\geq 2\pi\|n\|-2\pi\sqrt{d}
for
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we have for large
\|n\|
:

k_{\nu}(\|x+2\pi n\|)\leq C^{\prime}\cdot e^{-2\pi\sqrt{2\nu}\|n\|/\rho}

where
C^{\prime}
absorbs the polynomial factors and constant shifts.

Therefore:

|C_{M}(x)-S_{N}(x)|=\left|\sum_{\|n\|_{\infty}>N}k_{\nu}(\|x+2\pi n\|)\right|\leq\sum_{\|n\|_{\infty}>N}C^{\prime}e^{-2\pi\sqrt{2\nu}\|n\|/\rho}

This geometric series converges uniformly in
x
since the bound is independent of
x
. For the convolution:

\displaystyle\left|(C_{B}\star C_{M})(x)-(C_{B}\star S_{N})(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|C_{B}\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|C_{M}(x-y)-S_{N}(x-y)|dy

(3)

\displaystyle\leq\|C_{B}\|_{\infty}\cdot(2\pi)^{d}\cdot\sum_{\|n\|_{\infty}>N}C^{\prime}e^{-2\pi\sqrt{2\nu}\|n\|/\rho}\to 0

(4)

uniformly in
x
as
N\to\infty
. This justifies the interchange:

C_{CM}(x)=C_{B}\star C_{M}=C_{B}\star\left(\lim_{N\to\infty}S_{N}\right)=\lim_{N\to\infty}(C_{B}\star S_{N})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}I_{n}(x)

Proof of (b).
For
\|n\|_{\infty}\geq 1
, the distance
\|x-y+2\pi n\|\geq 2\pi(\|n\|-\sqrt{d})
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

By the asymptotic bound on
k_{\nu}
, for large
\|n\|
:

k_{\nu}(\|x-y+2\pi n\|)\leq C\cdot\|x-y+2\pi n\|^{\nu-1/2}\cdot e^{-\sqrt{2\nu}\|x-y+2\pi n\|/\rho}

Since
\|x-y+2\pi n\|\geq 2\pi(\|n\|-\sqrt{d})
:

\displaystyle k_{\nu}(\|x-y+2\pi n\|)

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\cdot(2\pi\|n\|)^{\nu-1/2}\cdot e^{-\sqrt{2\nu}\cdot 2\pi(\|n\|-\sqrt{d})/\rho}

(5)

\displaystyle=C^{\prime\prime}\cdot\|n\|^{\nu-1/2}\cdot e^{-2\pi\sqrt{2\nu}\|n\|/\rho}

(6)

where
C^{\prime\prime}=C^{\prime}(2\pi)^{\nu-1/2}e^{2\pi\sqrt{2\nu d}/\rho}
. Therefore:

|I_{n}(x)|\leq\|C_{B}\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}k_{\nu}(\|x-y+2\pi n\|)dy\leq K\cdot\|n\|^{\nu-1/2}\cdot e^{-2\pi\sqrt{2\nu}\|n\|/\rho}

The exponential factor dominates the polynomial growth, ensuring absolute and uniform convergence.
∎

Corollary 4.7

(Truncation Error)
.

For practical computation with truncation at
\|n\|_{\infty}\leq N
:

\left|C_{CM}(x)-\sum_{\|n\|_{\infty}\leq N}I_{n}(x)\right|\leq\frac{K^{\prime}}{(1-e^{-2\pi\sqrt{2\nu}/\rho})^{d}}\cdot e^{-2\pi\sqrt{2\nu}(N+1)/\rho}

where
K^{\prime}
depends on the dimension
d
, kernel parameters, and
\|C_{B}\|_{\infty}
.

Proof.

The tail sum can be bounded by:

\sum_{\|n\|_{\infty}>N}|I_{n}(x)|\leq K\sum_{\|n\|_{\infty}>N}\|n\|^{\nu-1/2}e^{-2\pi\sqrt{2\nu}\|n\|/\rho}

For large
N
, the sum is dominated by terms with
\|n\|_{\infty}=N+1
. The number of such lattice points is at most
(2N+3)^{d}-(2N+1)^{d}=O(N^{d-1})
. Each contributes at most
K(N+1)^{\nu-1/2}e^{-2\pi\sqrt{2\nu}(N+1)/\rho}
.

For dimensions
d\geq 2
, we can bound the entire tail by geometric series considerations, giving the stated bound.
∎

5
Conclusion

This paper has examined the fundamental question of how the desirable properties of Matérn covariance functions in Euclidean space transfer to manifold settings, focusing on the tractable case of tori. Through the framework of pseudo-differential operators on tori, we have established both theoretical results and practical insights that challenge common assumptions in spatial modeling on closed manifolds.

Our main theoretical contribution is the complete characterization of sample path regularity for Matérn processes on tori. We established that processes on
d
-dimensional tori require smoothness parameter
\nu>3d/2
to achieve regularity
C^{(\nu-3d/2)^{-}}_{loc}
, in contrast to the Euclidean requirement of merely
\nu>0
. This dimension-dependent threshold reveals that the exceptional correspondence between covariance parameters and path properties observed in Euclidean settings does not transfer automatically to manifold domains. The proof technique, based on the Cardona-Martínez theory of pseudo-differential operators, provides a new analytical perspective that extends the classical Kolmogorov-Chentsov approach and may prove valuable for studying regularity on more general compact manifolds.

Beyond these regularity results, we introduced the canonical-Matérn process, a novel three-parameter family that extends the classical two-parameter Matérn family. This construction incorporates two additional degrees of freedom that allow practitioners to independently control geometric structure (through the canonical field component) and fine-scale variability (through the white noise component). The canonical-Matérn process achieves regularity
C^{(\nu-3d/2+2)^{-}}_{loc}
, gaining two orders of smoothness compared to standard Matérn processes with the same smoothness parameter. We established the convolution structure of this process and proved exponential convergence rates for practical computation via series truncation.

The practical implications of our findings are significant. Standard software implementations of Matérn models that work well in Euclidean settings may produce processes with markedly different regularity properties when applied to manifold domains. The dimension-dependent smoothness threshold we identify implies that parameter choices suitable for low-dimensional problems require adjustment in higher dimensions. For practitioners working with spatial data on periodic domains, our results provide both a cautionary note about naive parameter transfer and a constructive framework through the canonical-Matérn family for developing geometrically appropriate models.

Several directions for future research emerge naturally from this work. First, the pseudo-differential operator framework employed here extends to general compact manifolds with known spectral decompositions, suggesting that analogous regularity results might be obtained for spheres, projective spaces, and other geometries relevant to applications in climate science and neuroimaging. Second, our results raise questions about optimal inference procedures under the modified regularity conditions we establish—existing estimation methods for Matérn parameters may require adaptation when the smoothness constraint
\nu>3d/2
binds. Third, the computational efficiency of the canonical-Matérn approach relative to iterated SPDE solutions deserves systematic investigation, particularly in high-dimensional settings where our exponential convergence results suggest practical advantages.

Finally, this work demonstrates the value of rigorous mathematical analysis in spatial statistics. While computational tools like INLA and the SPDE approach have greatly expanded the practical applicability of Matérn models, understanding the theoretical properties of these models on non-Euclidean domains remains essential for their appropriate use. We hope this paper provides both theoretical foundations for future work on spatial modeling on manifolds and practical guidance for applied researchers working with spatial data on periodic and compact domains.

A.6
Technical Lemmas

The following technical lemma provides asymptotic bounds for the Matérn kernel that are essential for establishing convergence rates in the convolution decomposition.

Lemma A.14

(Exponential Decay of Matérn Kernel)
.

The Matérn kernel with smoothness parameter
\nu>0
and range parameter
\rho>0
is given by:

k_{\nu}(r)=\sigma^{2}\frac{2^{1-\nu}}{\Gamma(\nu)}\left(\frac{r\sqrt{2\nu}}{\rho}\right)^{\nu}K_{\nu}\left(\frac{r\sqrt{2\nu}}{\rho}\right)

where
K_{\nu}
is the modified Bessel function of the second kind. For large
r
, this satisfies:

k_{\nu}(r)\leq C(\nu,\rho,\sigma)\cdot r^{\nu-1/2}\cdot e^{-r\sqrt{2\nu}/\rho}

Proof.

The modified Bessel function
K_{\nu}(z)
has the asymptotic expansion for large
z
:

K_{\nu}(z)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2z}}e^{-z}\left(1+O(1/z)\right)\quad\text{as }z\to\infty

Setting
z=r\sqrt{2\nu}/\rho
and substituting into the kernel expression:

k_{\nu}(r)\sim\sigma^{2}\frac{2^{1-\nu}}{\Gamma(\nu)}\left(\frac{r\sqrt{2\nu}}{\rho}\right)^{\nu}\sqrt{\frac{\pi\rho}{2r\sqrt{2\nu}}}e^{-r\sqrt{2\nu}/\rho}

Simplifying:

k_{\nu}(r)\sim\sigma^{2}\frac{2^{1-\nu}}{\Gamma(\nu)}\sqrt{\frac{\pi\rho}{2\sqrt{2\nu}}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{2\nu}}{\rho}\right)^{\nu}r^{\nu-1/2}e^{-r\sqrt{2\nu}/\rho}

Therefore, for sufficiently large
r
, the bound holds with:

C(\nu,\rho,\sigma)=\sigma^{2}\frac{2^{1-\nu}}{\Gamma(\nu)}\sqrt{\frac{\pi\rho}{2\sqrt{2\nu}}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{2\nu}}{\rho}\right)^{\nu}\cdot(1+\delta)

for any small
\delta>0
to account for the
O(1/z)
correction terms.
∎

A.7
Extension of Cardona-Martínez Theorem to Negative Orders

The following theorem extends the kernel estimates of Cardona-Martínez to negative orders, providing rigorous justification for the Hölder continuity properties used in our regularity analysis.

Theorem A.15

(Extended Cardona-Martínez Kernel Estimate)
.

Let
T\in\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})
be a pseudodifferential operator with symbol of order
m
, where
0<\rho\leq 1
,
0\leq\delta<\rho
. Let
k(x,y)
denote the kernel of
T
. For any integer
N\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying

N>\frac{m+n+|\alpha+\beta|}{\rho}

and multi-indices
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}
, the following holds:

(a)

If
N\geq 0
:

\sup_{x\neq y}|x-y|^{N}|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{y}k(x,y)|<\infty

(b)

If
N<0
:

|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{y}k(x,y)|\leq C|x-y|^{|N|}

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
with
x\neq y
, where
C=C_{m,n,N,\alpha,\beta}>0
is a constant.

Proof.

We provide the proof for case (b), as case (a) is established in
[
9
]
. Let
N<0
and set
s=|N|=-N>0
. We need to establish that

|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{y}k(x,y)|\leq C|x-y|^{s}

We begin by establishing the kernel representation. The kernel
k(x,y)
has the representation

k(x,y)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}e^{i2\pi(x-y)\cdot\xi}\tilde{p}(x,\xi)

where
\tilde{p}(x,\xi)
is the symbol of
T
satisfying the standard estimates: for all multi-indices
\gamma
,

|\partial^{\gamma}_{\xi}\tilde{p}(x,\xi)|\leq C_{\gamma}\langle\xi\rangle^{m-\rho|\gamma|+\delta|x|}

where
\langle\xi\rangle=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{1/2}
.

After applying derivatives
\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{y}
, we obtain multiple terms via the Leibniz formula. When
\partial^{\alpha}_{x}
acts on the product
e^{i2\pi x\cdot\xi}\tilde{p}(x,\xi)
, we get:

\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\left[e^{i2\pi x\cdot\xi}\tilde{p}(x,\xi)\right]=\sum_{\gamma\leq\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\gamma}(i2\pi)^{|\gamma|}\xi^{\gamma}e^{i2\pi x\cdot\xi}\cdot\partial^{\alpha-\gamma}_{x}\tilde{p}(x,\xi)

For the regularity analysis, we focus on the dominant term where all derivatives act on the exponential (i.e.,
\gamma=\alpha
), yielding:

\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{y}k(x,y)=(-1)^{|\alpha|}(2\pi)^{|\alpha+\beta|}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}e^{i2\pi(x-y)\cdot\xi}\xi^{\alpha+\beta}\tilde{p}(x,\xi)+\text{lower order terms}

where we have absorbed the symbol derivatives into the lower order terms. In what follows, we work with this principal contribution.

Next, we decompose the sum into low-frequency and high-frequency parts. Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a smooth cutoff function with
\chi(\xi)=1
for
|\xi|\leq 1
and
\chi(\xi)=0
for
|\xi|\geq 2
. Working with the dominant term, define

\displaystyle k_{\text{low}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|\alpha|}(2\pi)^{|\alpha+\beta|}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}e^{i2\pi(x-y)\cdot\xi}\chi\left(\frac{\xi}{R}\right)\xi^{\alpha+\beta}\tilde{p}(x,\xi)

(10)

\displaystyle k_{\text{high}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|\alpha|}(2\pi)^{|\alpha+\beta|}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}e^{i2\pi(x-y)\cdot\xi}\left(1-\chi\left(\frac{\xi}{R}\right)\right)\xi^{\alpha+\beta}\tilde{p}(x,\xi)

(11)

where
R=|x-y|^{-1}
.

For the low-frequency estimate, consider the low-frequency part where
|\xi|\preceq R=|x-y|^{-1}
. Using the symbol estimate for the dominant term:

|\xi^{\alpha+\beta}\tilde{p}(x,\xi)|\leq C|\xi|^{|\alpha+\beta|}\langle\xi\rangle^{m}

Since
|\xi|\preceq R

|k_{\text{low}}(x,y)|\leq C\sum_{|\xi|\preceq R}R^{|\alpha+\beta|}R^{m}=CR^{m+|\alpha+\beta|}\cdot R^{n}=C|x-y|^{-(m+n+|\alpha+\beta|)}

Since
N>\frac{m+n+|\alpha+\beta|}{\rho}\geq m+n+|\alpha+\beta|
, we have
-(m+n+|\alpha+\beta|)>-N=s
. Thus:

|k_{\text{low}}(x,y)|\leq C|x-y|^{s}

For the high-frequency estimate, we employ oscillatory integral methods. For the high-frequency part where
|\xi|\succeq R=|x-y|^{-1}
, we use the method of non-stationary phase. Define the differential operator:

L=\frac{1}{i2\pi|x-y|^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(x_{j}-y_{j})\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{j}}

This operator satisfies
L[e^{i2\pi(x-y)\cdot\xi}]=e^{i2\pi(x-y)\cdot\xi}
. Indeed, since
\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{j}}e^{i2\pi(x-y)\cdot\xi}=i2\pi(x_{j}-y_{j})e^{i2\pi(x-y)\cdot\xi}

L[e^{i2\pi(x-y)\cdot\xi}]=\frac{1}{i2\pi|x-y|^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(x_{j}-y_{j})\cdot i2\pi(x_{j}-y_{j})e^{i2\pi(x-y)\cdot\xi}=e^{i2\pi(x-y)\cdot\xi}

For any positive integer
M

k_{\text{high}}(x,y)=(-1)^{|\alpha|}(2\pi)^{|\alpha+\beta|}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}e^{i2\pi(x-y)\cdot\xi}(L^{*})^{M}\left[\left(1-\chi\left(\frac{\xi}{R}\right)\right)\xi^{\alpha+\beta}\tilde{p}(x,\xi)\right]

where
L^{*}

L

. Thus:

k_{\text{high}}(x,y)=(-1)^{|\alpha|+M}(2\pi)^{|\alpha+\beta|}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}e^{i2\pi(x-y)\cdot\xi}L^{M}\left[\left(1-\chi\left(\frac{\xi}{R}\right)\right)\xi^{\alpha+\beta}\tilde{p}(x,\xi)\right]

Here we use the fact that
L[e^{i2\pi(x-y)\cdot\xi}]=e^{i2\pi(x-y)\cdot\xi}
to transfer the operator from the oscillatory factor to the amplitude through the adjoint.

To estimate the symbol decay, observe that the operator
L

L=\frac{(x-y)\cdot\nabla_{\xi}}{i2\pi|x-y|^{2}}

introduces a factor of

\partial^{\gamma}_{\xi}

For
|\gamma|\leq M

\left|\partial^{\gamma}_{\xi}\left[\left(1-\chi\left(\frac{\xi}{R}\right)\right)\xi^{\alpha+\beta}\tilde{p}(x,\xi)\right]\right|\leq C_{\gamma}\langle\xi\rangle^{m+|\alpha+\beta|-\rho|\gamma|}

We now verify summability. After applying

|k_{\text{high}}(x,y)|\leq C|x-y|^{-M}\sum_{|\xi|\succeq R}\langle\xi\rangle^{m+|\alpha+\beta|-\rho M}

Since
N>\frac{m+n+|\alpha+\beta|}{\rho}

With this choice of

|k_{\text{high}}(x,y)|\leq C|x-y|^{-M}\leq C|x-y|^{s}

|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{y}k(x,y)|\leq|k_{\text{low}}(x,y)|+|k_{\text{high}}(x,y)|\leq C|x-y|^{s}

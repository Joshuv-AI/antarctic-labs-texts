Anisotropic approximation on space-time domains
Pedro Morin, Cornelia Schneider, Nick Schneider

Abstract
We investigate anisotropic (piecewise) polynomial approximation of functions in Lebesgue spaces as well as anisotropic Besov spaces. For this purpose we study temporal and spacial moduli of smoothness and their properties. In particular, we prove Jackson- and Whitney-type inequalities on Lipschitz cylinders, i.e., space-time domains I × D I\times D with a finite interval I I and a bounded Lipschitz domain D ⊂ ℝ d D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} , d ∈ ℕ d\in\mathbb{N} . As an application, we prove a direct estimate result for adaptive space-time finite element approximation in the discontinuous setting. Key Words: Jackson inequality; Whitney inequality; anisotropic Besov spaces; anisotropic polynomials; direct estimates . MSC2020 Math Subject Classifications: Primary 41A10, 41A17, 41A25, 41A63; Secondary 65M50, 65M60 .

As a main tool for our studies we now rely on the so-called spacial and temporal moduli of smoothness and their properties. For a long time moduli of smoothness have been known to be a very useful concept. Already in 1927 a version of the inequality for moduli of smoothness of different orders, the so-called Marchaud’s inequality, was proven by Marchaud
[
Mar27
]
. In the literature most results for the moduli are established in the Banach setting when

In particular, the Jackson and Whitney inequalities, two fundamental results in approximation theory often used in numerical analysis, can be stated and proven with the help of such moduli of smoothness.

Jackson’s estimate allows to measure the error of approximation of an arbitrary
L_{p}
-function by its modulus of smoothness. Additionally, if the function is smooth enough, e.g. has some Besov regularity, one can even estimate the (global) local error of approximation with respect to (piecewise) polynomials by the Besov-seminorm of the function leading to Whitney’s estimate (the proof makes use of the Jackson’s estimate). In this sense Jackson’s inequality is somehow preferable to Whitney’s since it applies to any
f

for
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. In particular, let
r_{1},r_{2},k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
k_{i}\leq r_{i}
,
i=1,2
,
h\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\bm{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, the algebraic identities together with the
\mu(p)
-subadditivity of the
||\cdot||_{L_{p}}
-
(quasi-)
norm imply the estimates
\|\Delta_{h,t}^{r_{1}}f\|_{L_{p}(I_{r_{1},h}\times D)}^{\mu(p)}\leq 2^{r_{1}-k_{1}}\|\Delta_{h,t}^{k_{1}}f\|_{L_{p}(I_{k_{1},h}\times D)}^{\mu(p)}
and
\|\Delta_{\bm{h},\bm{x}}^{r_{2}}f\|_{L_{p}(I\times D_{r_{2},\bm{h}})}^{\mu(p)}\leq 2^{r_{2}-k_{2}}\|\Delta_{\bm{h},\bm{x}}^{k_{2}}f\|_{L_{p}(I\times D_{r_{2},\bm{h})}}^{\mu(p)}
for any
f\in L_{p}(I\times D)
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty]
,
f\in L_{p}(I\times D)
,
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
\delta\in(0,\infty)
. The
temporal and spacial moduli of smoothness
in the supremum form are defined as

\displaystyle\omega_{r_{1},t}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}:=\sup\limits_{|h|\leq\delta}\|\Delta_{h,t}^{r_{1}}f\|_{L_{p}\left(I_{r_{1},h}\times D\right)},\quad\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}:=\sup\limits_{|\bm{h}|\leq\delta}\|\Delta_{\bm{h},\bm{x}}^{r_{2}}f\|_{L_{p}\left(I\times D_{r_{2},\bm{h}}\right)}

as well as in the averaged form, for
0<p<\infty
:

\displaystyle w_{r_{1},t}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}:=\left(\frac{1}{2\delta}\int\limits_{|h|\leq\delta}\|\Delta_{h,t}^{r_{1}}f\|^{p}_{L_{p}\left(I_{r_{1},h}\times D\right)}\,dh\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

\displaystyle w_{r_{2},\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}:=\left(\frac{1}{(2\delta)^{d}}\int\limits_{|h|\leq\delta}\|\Delta_{\bm{h},\bm{x}}^{r_{2}}f\|^{p}_{L_{p}\left(I_{r_{1},h}\times D\right)}\,d\bm{h}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Further,
w_{r_{1},t}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{\infty}:=\omega_{r_{1},t}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{\infty}
,
w_{r_{2},\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{\infty}:=\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{\infty}
, and
\omega_{r_{1},t}(f,I\times D,0)_{p}:=w_{r_{1},t}(f,I\times D,0)_{p}:=\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,0)_{p}:=w_{r_{2},\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,0)_{p}:=0
, if
r_{1},r_{2}\geq 1
.

Remark 2.4
.

Let
f\in L_{p}(I\times D)
,
(r_{i},dir)\in\{(r_{1},t),(r_{2},\bm{x})\}
,
k_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
k_{i}\leq r_{i}
,
\delta\in[0,\infty)
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and
l\in[0,\infty)
. Then, as in the isotropic case,

(i)

\omega_{r_{i},dir}(f,I\times D,\cdot)_{p}
is monotonically increasing,

(ii)

\omega_{r_{i},dir}(\cdot,I\times D,\delta)^{\mu(p)}_{p}
is subadditive,

(iii)

\omega_{r_{i},dir}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}^{\mu(p)}\leq 2^{r_{i}-k_{i}}\omega_{k_{i},dir}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}^{\mu(p)}
, and

(iv)

admits the two similar scaling properties

\displaystyle\omega_{r_{i},dir}(f,I\times D,m\delta)_{p}^{\mu(p)}

\displaystyle\leq m^{r_{i}}\omega_{r_{i},dir}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}^{\mu(p)},

\displaystyle\omega_{r_{i},dir}(f,I\times D,l\delta)_{p}^{\mu(p)}

\displaystyle\leq(l+1)^{r_{i}}\omega_{r_{i},dir}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}^{\mu(p)}.

Furthermore, property
(iii)
still holds true in the case
r_{i}=0
when
\omega_{0,dir}(f,I\times D,\cdot)_{p}:=\|f\|_{L_{p}(I\times D)}
is used. Moreover, the third, and fourth property hold true for the averaged moduli. These results can be proven as in
[
DL93
, Ch. 2.7 and Ch. 12.5]
.

We will also make use of the following lemma that characterizes the behaviour of the moduli of smoothness under affine linear transformations.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty]
,
f\in L_{p}(I\times D)
,
\delta\in[0,\infty)
, and
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Further, let
I^{\prime}
be a bounded interval,
D^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
, a bounded Lipschitz domain, and
\varphi:I^{\prime}\times D^{\prime}\rightarrow I\times D
an affine linear and bijective mapping with
\varphi(\tau,\bm{z})=(a\tau+b,c\bm{z}+d)
for
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\begin{split}\omega_{r_{1},t}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}&=\left(\frac{|I\times D|}{|I^{\prime}\times D^{\prime}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\omega_{r_{1},t}\left(f\circ\varphi,I^{\prime}\times D^{\prime},\delta\,\frac{|I^{\prime}|}{|I|}\right)_{p}\\
\text{and}\qquad\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}&=\left(\frac{|I\times D|}{|I^{\prime}\times D^{\prime}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}\left(f\circ\varphi,I^{\prime}\times D^{\prime},\delta\,\frac{|D^{\prime}|^{\frac{1}{d}}}{|D|^{\frac{1}{d}}}\right)_{p}.\end{split}

(7)

Such mappings will be called
scalings
in the sequel. More generally, for
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
with
\varphi_{1}:I^{\prime}\rightarrow I
and
\varphi_{2}:D^{\prime}\rightarrow D
affine linear and bijective, it holds,

\begin{split}\omega_{r_{1},t}(f,I\times D,|I|)_{p}&=|\textup{det }D\varphi|^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\omega_{r_{1},t}\left(f\circ\varphi,I^{\prime}\times D^{\prime},|I^{\prime}|\right)_{p}\\
\text{and}\qquad\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\textup{diam}(D))_{p}&=|\textup{det }D\varphi|^{\frac{1}{p}}\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}\left(f\circ\varphi,I^{\prime}\times D^{\prime},\textup{diam}(D^{\prime})\right)_{p}.\end{split}

(8)

The corresponding results hold for the averaged moduli as well.

Proof.

The first two results (
7
) are an immediate consequence of the Jacobi transformation theorem. The second two in (
8
) follow by the latter together with the fact that

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}\big(\big\{h^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid h^{\prime}=t^{\prime}-\tau^{\prime},\text{ for }t^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}\in\overline{I^{\prime}}\big\}\big)=\big\{h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid h=t-\tau,\text{ for }t,\tau\in\overline{I}\big\}.

and

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}\big(\big\{\bm{h^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\bm{h^{\prime}}=\bm{y^{\prime}}-\bm{z^{\prime}},\text{ for }\bm{y^{\prime}},\bm{z^{\prime}}\in\overline{D^{\prime}}\big\}\big)=\big\{\bm{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\bm{h}=\bm{y}-\bm{z},\text{ for }\bm{y},\bm{z}\in\overline{D}\big\}.

∎

Remark 2.6
.

The corresponding result to (
8
) for the isotropic case has (for example) been used in
[
DL04
, proof of Thm. 1.4]
.

Furthermore, both versions of the moduli are equivalent for small enough
\delta
:

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty]
,
f\in L_{p}(I\times D)
, and
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

w_{r,t}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}\leq\omega_{r,t}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}\lesssim_{\,p,r}w_{r,t}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}

for all
\delta\in[0,\delta_{0}]
and
0<\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(r,|I|))=\frac{|I|}{4r}
.

Proof.

Analogous to Lemma 5.1 of
[
DL93
, Ch. 6.5]
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty]
,
f\in L_{p}(I\times D)
, and
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

w_{r,\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}\leq\omega_{r,\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}\lesssim_{\,d,p,r,\textup{LipProp}(D)}w_{r,\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}

for all
\delta\in[0,\delta_{0}]
and

.

Proof.

Qualitatively, this can be shown as in

, Cor. 4.2]
. Since we state an explicit range for admissible

, we give a proof in
Appendix

A
for the readers convenience.

2.2
Marchaud’s inequality

In order to be able to prove the Jackson-type inequality in our setting, we first need a Marchaud-type inequality for our newly defined moduli of smoothness. For the temporal one, this works as in the isotropic case.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k<r
,
f\in L_{p}(I\times D)
, and
\delta\in[0,\infty)
. Then it holds

\omega_{k,t}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}^{\mu(p)}\lesssim_{\,p,k,r}\delta^{k\mu(p)}\left(\|f\|_{L_{p}(I\times D)}^{\mu(p)}+\int\limits_{\delta}^{\infty}\frac{\omega_{r,t}(f,I\times D,s)_{p}^{\mu(p)}}{s^{k\mu(p)}}\frac{ds}{s}\right).

Proof.

Analogous to
[
DL93
, Ch. 2.8]
. In particular, scaling from
I\times D
to
[0,1]\times D
and again rescaling shows that the constant in the estimate can be chosen independently of
I
, due to the first part of
2.5
.
∎

In the spacial direction, it is not possible to proceed as in the proof of the corresponding isotropic result for higher-dimensional domains as in
[
Dit88
, Thm. 2]
, where
p\geq 1
was chosen, since this proof relies on extension operators that we do not have available in our setting. Also, we will not use the technical approach from
[
Dek22
, Thm. 1.21 and Cor. 1.22]
for the case
p\in(0,1)
. Instead, we now state the isotropic Marchaud’s inequality in the setting/formulation from
[
GM14
, Rem. 4.8]
and then derive a Marchaud’s inequality for our spacial modulus of smoothness using the results from
Section

2.1
.

Lemma 2.10

(
[
GM14
, Rem. 4.8]
)
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty]
,
r\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k<r
,
f\in L_{p}(D)
, and
\delta\in[0,\infty)
. Then

\displaystyle\omega_{k}(f,D,\delta)_{p}^{\mu(p)}\lesssim_{\,p,k,r,\textup{LipProp}(D)}\delta^{k\mu(p)}\left(\|f\|_{L_{p}(D)}^{\mu(p)}+\int\limits_{\delta}^{\infty}\frac{\omega_{r}(f,D,s)_{p}^{\mu(p)}}{s^{k\mu(p)}}\frac{ds}{s}\right).

Remark 2.11
.

The modulus
\omega_{r}(\cdot,D,\cdot)_{p}
above is defined like our spacial modulus of smoothness from
Section

2.1
except for the missing time dependency. In the same manner, we will also use the averaged version
w_{r}(\cdot,D,\cdot)_{p}
, which is defined accordingly. Also, for these moduli,
2.8
holds correspondingly.

Now the Marchaud inequality for the spacial modulus of smoothness can be stated as follows.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty]
,
r\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k<r
,
f\in L_{p}(I\times D)
, and
\delta\in[0,\infty)
. Then

\displaystyle\omega_{k,\bm{x}}(f,

\displaystyle I\times D,\delta)_{p}^{\mu(p)}\lesssim_{\,d,p,k,r,\textup{LipProp}(D)}\delta^{k\mu(p)}\left(\|f\|_{L_{p}(I\times D)}^{\mu(p)}+\int\limits_{\delta}^{\infty}\frac{\omega_{r,\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,s)_{p}^{\mu(p)}}{s^{k\mu(p)}}\frac{ds}{s}\right).

Proof.

First, we consider the case
p<\infty
. Since the estimate which we want to prove remains invariant under scalings, due to the first part of
2.5
, which, in particular, does not change the Lipschitz properties of
D
, we can assume
\sigma(D)=1
without loss of generality. For
\tau\in I
, we define
f_{\tau}:=f(\tau,\cdot)
. Then
f_{\tau}\in L_{p}(D)
almost everywhere on
I
such that we can calculate

\displaystyle\omega_{k,\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}^{\mu(p)}

\displaystyle=\left(\sup\limits_{|\bm{h}|\leq\delta}\int\limits_{I}\|\Delta^{k}_{\bm{h}}f_{\tau}\|^{p}_{L_{p}(D_{r,\bm{h}})}\,d\tau\right)^{\frac{\mu(p)}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int\limits_{I}\sup\limits_{|\bm{h}|\leq\delta}\|\Delta^{k}_{\bm{h}}f_{\tau}\|^{p}_{L_{p}(D_{r,\bm{h}})}\,d\tau\right)^{\frac{\mu(p)}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int\limits_{I}\left(\omega_{k}(f_{\tau},D,\delta)_{p}^{\mu(p)}\right)^{\frac{p}{\mu(p)}}\,d\tau\right)^{\frac{\mu(p)}{p}}.

Next, we can use this together with
2.10
to derive

\begin{split}\omega_{k,\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}^{\mu(p)}&\lesssim_{\,p,k,r,\textup{LipProp}(D)}\delta^{k\mu(p)}\left(\int\limits_{I}\left(\|f_{\tau}\|_{L_{p}(D)}^{\mu(p)}+\int\limits_{\delta}^{\infty}\frac{\omega_{r}(f_{\tau},D,s)_{p}^{\mu(p)}}{s^{k\mu(p)}}\frac{ds}{s}\right)^{\frac{p}{\mu(p)}}\,d\tau\right)^{\frac{\mu(p)}{p}}\\
&\lesssim_{\,p}\delta^{k\mu(p)}\left(\int\limits_{I}\|f_{\tau}\|_{L_{p}(D)}^{p}+\left(\int\limits_{\delta}^{\infty}\frac{\omega_{r}(f_{\tau},D,s)_{p}^{\mu(p)}}{s^{k\mu(p)}}\frac{ds}{s}\right)^{\frac{p}{\mu(p)}}\,d\tau\right)^{\frac{\mu(p)}{p}}\\
&=\delta^{k\mu(p)}\left(\|f\|_{L_{p}(I\times D)}^{p}+\int\limits_{I}\left(\int\limits_{\delta}^{\infty}\frac{\omega_{r}(f_{\tau},D,s)_{p}^{\mu(p)}}{s^{k\mu(p)}}\frac{ds}{s}\right)^{\frac{p}{\mu(p)}}\,d\tau\right)^{\frac{\mu(p)}{p}}\\
&\lesssim_{\,p}\delta^{k\mu(p)}\left(\|f\|_{L_{p}(I\times D)}^{\mu(p)}+\left(\int\limits_{I}\left(\int\limits_{\delta}^{\infty}\frac{\omega_{r}(f_{\tau},D,s)_{p}^{\mu(p)}}{s^{k\mu(p)}}\frac{ds}{s}\right)^{\frac{p}{\mu(p)}}\,d\tau\right)^{\frac{\mu(p)}{p}}\right).\end{split}

(9)

Since
\frac{p}{\mu(p)}\geq 1
, we can use Minkowski’s inequality for integrals to get

\begin{split}\Bigg(\int\limits_{I}\bigg(\int\limits_{\delta}^{\infty}&\frac{\omega_{r}(f_{\tau},D,s)_{p}^{\mu(p)}}{s^{k\mu(p)}}\frac{ds}{s}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{\mu(p)}}\,d\tau\Bigg)^{\frac{\mu(p)}{p}}\leq\int\limits_{\delta}^{\infty}\Bigg(\int\limits_{I}\frac{\omega_{r}(f_{\tau},D,s)_{p}^{p}}{s^{kp}}\,d\tau\Bigg)^{\frac{\mu(p)}{p}}\frac{ds}{s}\\
&\leq\int\limits_{\min(\delta,\delta_{0})}^{\delta_{0}}\Bigg(\int\limits_{I}\omega_{r}(f_{\tau},D,s)_{p}^{p}\,d\tau\Bigg)^{\frac{\mu(p)}{p}}\frac{ds}{s^{k\mu(p)+1}}+\int\limits^{\infty}_{\delta_{0}}\left(\int\limits_{I}\omega_{r}(f_{\tau},D,s)_{p}^{p}\,d\tau\right)^{\frac{\mu(p)}{p}}\frac{ds}{s^{k\mu(p)+1}}.\end{split}

(10)

For
s\leq\delta_{0}(r):=\frac{1}{4r}
,
2.8
gives

\displaystyle\left(\int\limits_{I}\omega_{r}(f_{\tau},D,s)_{p}^{p}\,d\tau\right)^{\frac{\mu(p)}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\,d,p,r,\textup{LipProp}(D)}\left(\int\limits_{I}w_{r}(f_{\tau},D,s)_{p}^{p}\,d\tau\right)^{\frac{\mu(p)}{p}}=w_{r,\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,s)_{p}^{\mu(p)}.

(11)

In the last step, we have used the following identity

\displaystyle\int\limits_{I}w_{r}(f_{\tau},D,s)_{p}^{p}\,dt=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\delta)^{d}}\int\limits_{\{|\bm{h}|\leq s\}}\int\limits_{I}\|\Delta^{r}_{\bm{h}}f_{\tau}\|_{L_{p}(D)}^{p}\,d\tau\,d\bm{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\delta)^{d}}\int\limits_{\{|\bm{h}|\leq s\}}\int\limits_{I}\int\limits_{D}|\Delta^{r}_{\bm{h}}f_{\tau}(\bm{z})|^{p}\,d\bm{z}\,d\tau\,d\bm{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\delta)^{d}}\int\limits_{\{|\bm{h}|\leq s\}}\int\limits_{I\times D}|\Delta^{r}_{\bm{h},\bm{x}}f(\tau,\bm{z})|^{p}\,d(\tau,\bm{z})\,d\bm{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\delta)^{d}}\int\limits_{\{|\bm{h}|\leq s\}}\|\Delta^{r}_{\bm{h},\bm{x}}f\|_{L_{p}(I\times D)}^{p}\,d\bm{h}=w_{r,\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,s)_{p}^{p},

where we have applied Fubini’s theorem in the first and third step, and the identity
\Delta^{r}_{\bm{h}}f_{\tau}(\bm{z})=\Delta^{r}_{\bm{h},\bm{x}}f(\tau,\bm{z})
in the third step as well. Furthermore, with the estimate
\omega_{r}(f_{\tau},D,s)_{p}\lesssim_{\,p,r}\|f_{\tau}\|_{L_{p}(D)}
we derive

\displaystyle\int\limits^{\infty}_{\delta_{0}}\left(\int\limits_{I}\omega_{r}(f_{\tau},D,s)_{p}^{p}\,d\tau\right)^{\frac{\mu(p)}{p}}\frac{ds}{s^{k\mu(p)+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\,p,r}\|f\|_{L_{p}(I\times D)}^{\mu(p)}\int\limits_{\delta_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{s^{k\mu(p)+1}}\lesssim_{\,p,k,r}\|f\|_{L_{p}(I\times D)}^{\mu(p)}.

(12)

Combining (
9
), (
10
), (
11
), and (
12
) we arrive at the assertion for
p<\infty
. The case
p=\infty
works similarly with the usual changes; mainly exchanging some integrals with essential suprema and setting
\frac{p}{\mu(p)}
to
1
.
∎

2.3
Jackson-type estimate

In this subsection, we proceed similar to
[
DL04
, Ch. 2]
. The crucial part is to extend the construction of the (spacial) step function from
[
DL04
, Lem. 2.8]
on convex domains to arbitrary Lipschitz domains using the cone property from
1.7
(ii)
while simultaneously adding a time-dependency. The idea of this application of the cone property (in space) has been roughly sketched in
[
GM14
, Lem. 4.4]
with respect to a certain subclass of Lipschitz domains, there called
“reference stars”
. It will allow us to establish
1.1
, i.e., provide a time-dependent Jackson’s estimate for arbitrary Lipschitz domains, not only convex ones. In particular, our construction allows us to avoid the domain convergence technique from
[
DL04
, Def. 2.11 and Lem. 2.12]
, which was applied in
[
DL04
, Thm. 1.4]
for proving the case
0<p<1
.

We start studying approximation with constants, i.e., polynomials of order
(1,1)
.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty]
and
f\in L_{p}(I\times D)
. Then there is a constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\|f-c|

\displaystyle|_{L_{p}(I\times D)}\lesssim_{\,p}\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|I|}\int\limits_{\{|h|\leq|I|\}}\|\Delta_{h,t}^{1}\|_{L_{p}(I_{1,h}\times D)}^{p}\,dh+\frac{1}{|D|}\displaystyle\int\limits_{\{|\bm{h}|\leq\textup{diam}(D)\}}\|\Delta_{\bm{h},\bm{x}}^{1}\|_{L_{p}(I\times D_{1,\bm{h}})}^{p}\,d\bm{h}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for
p\in(0,\infty)
and

\displaystyle\|f-c\|_{L_{\infty}(I\times D)}\leq\omega_{1,t}(f,I\times D,|I|)_{\infty}+\omega_{1,\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\textup{diam}(D))_{\infty}

for
p=\infty
.

Proof.

We first consider the case
p<\infty
and define

\varphi:I\times D\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\,\,\varphi(s,\bm{y}):=\int\limits_{I\times D}|f(\tau,\bm{z})-f(s,\bm{y})|^{p}\,d(\tau,\bm{z}).

Then it exists
(s,\bm{y})\in I\times D
, such that

\varphi(s,\bm{y})\leq\frac{1}{|I\times D|}\int\limits_{I\times D}\varphi(\tau,\bm{z})\,d(\tau,\bm{z}).

We set
c:=f(s,\bm{y})
and derive

\displaystyle\|f-c\|^{p}_{L_{p}(I\times D)}=\varphi(s,\bm{y})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|I\times D|}\int\limits_{I\times D}\varphi(\tau,\bm{z})\,d(\tau,\bm{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|I\times D|}\int\limits_{I\times D}\int\limits_{I\times D}|f(\tilde{\tau},\tilde{\bm{z}})-f(\tau,\bm{z})|^{p}\,d(\tau,\bm{z})\,d(\tilde{\tau},\tilde{\bm{z}})

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\,p}\frac{1}{|I\times D|}\int\limits_{I\times D}\int\limits_{I\times D}|f(\tilde{\tau},\tilde{\bm{z}})-f(\tau,\tilde{\bm{z}})|^{p}\,d(\tau,\bm{z})\,d(\tilde{\tau},\tilde{\bm{z}})

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\>\frac{1}{|I\times D|}\int\limits_{I\times D}\int\limits_{I\times D}|f(\tau,\tilde{\bm{z}})-f(\tau,\bm{z})|^{p}\,\,d(\tau,\bm{z})\,d(\tilde{\tau},\tilde{\bm{z}})

\displaystyle:=(I)+(II).

The integrand of
(I)
is independent of
\bm{z}
, thus with Fubini’s theorem we can conclude

\displaystyle(I)=\frac{|D|}{|I\times D|}\int\limits_{I\times D}\int\limits_{I}|\Delta^{1}_{\tilde{\tau}-\tau,t}f(\tau,\tilde{\bm{z}})|^{p}\,d\tau\,d(\tilde{\tau},\tilde{\bm{z}})\,=\frac{1}{|I|}\int\limits_{\{|h|\leq|I|\}}\|\Delta_{h,t}^{1}f\|^{p}_{L_{p}(I_{1,h}\times D)}\,d\bm{h},

since
|I\times D|=|I\|D|
. Analogously, the integrand of
(II)
is independent from
\tilde{\tau}
, which shows the assertion for
p<\infty
. Moreover, for
p=\infty
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
with
\essinf\limits_{(s,\bm{y})\in I\times D}f(s,\bm{y})\leq c\leq\esssup\limits_{(s,\bm{y})\in I\times D}f(s,\bm{y})
, we can estimate similarly when we replace the integrals by (essential) suprema.
∎

Lemma 2.14
.

1.1
holds true for
(r_{1},r_{2})=(1,1)
. In particular, for this choice of parameters there is no dependency on
\mathcal{N}(D)
.

Proof.

For
p=\infty
,
2.13
directly states this result. For
p<\infty
, first consider

\frac{1}{|I|}\int\limits_{\{|h|\leq|I|\}}\|\Delta_{h,t}^{1}f\|^{p}_{L_{p}(I_{1,h}\times D)}\,dh\leq\frac{2\,|I|}{|I|}\omega_{1,t}(f,I\times D,|I|)_{p}^{p}=2\,\omega_{1,t}(f,I\times D,|I|)_{p}^{p}.

Further, be aware of the fact that we can again scale to
\textup{diam}(D)=1
without changing
\textup{LipProp}(D)
and that the interior cone condition from
1.7
(ii)
implies especially that there is some
\bm{x}\in D
and
r=r(d,\mathcal{L}(D),\sigma(D))
, such that
B_{r}(\bm{x})\subset D
. Then,

\displaystyle||P-\psi||^{\mu(p)}_{L_{p}(I\times D)}

3
Approximation of anisotropic Besov functions

Now we will define function spaces as subspaces of
L_{p}(I\times D)
-functions with some (local) regularity constraint measured by the (decay of) the temporal and spacial moduli of smoothness. Since this approach is very similar to the one for (isotropic) Besov spaces but allows for the possibility of different smoothness in time and space, we call these spaces
anisotropic Besov spaces
. As described in
Section

1
, there are similar approaches in the literature but they were not suited for our purposes.

3.1
Anisotropic Besov spaces

Definition 3.1
.

Let
p,q\in(0,\infty]
,
s_{1},s_{2}\in(0,\infty)
,
r_{i}:=\lfloor s_{i}\rfloor+1
,
i=1,2
, and put

\displaystyle|f|_{B_{p,q}^{s_{1},s_{2}}(I\times D)}:=\begin{cases}\left(\displaystyle{\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}}\delta^{-s_{1}q}\omega_{r_{1},t}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}^{q}\frac{d\delta}{\delta}+\displaystyle{\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}}\delta^{-s_{2}q}\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}^{q}\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},&q<\infty,\\
\sup\limits_{\delta\in[0,\infty)}\delta^{-s_{1}}\omega_{r_{1},t}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}+\sup\limits_{\delta\in[0,\infty)}\delta^{-s_{2}}\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p},&q=\infty.\end{cases}

Now we can define the corresponding anisotropic Besov space as

\displaystyle B^{s_{1},s_{2}}_{p,q}(I\times D):=\left\{f\in L_{p}(I\times D)\>\big|\>||f||_{B_{p,q}^{s_{1},s_{2}}}(I\times D):=||f||_{L_{p}(I\times D)}+|f|_{B_{p,q}^{s_{1},s_{2}}(I\times D)}<\infty\right\}.

Remark 3.2
.

As in the isotropic case,
|f|_{B_{p,q}^{s_{1},s_{2}}(I\times D)}
is a
(quasi-)
seminorm and
\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{s_{1},s_{2}}(I\times D)}
is a
(quasi-)
norm. Further, as in the isotropic case, one can show that the equivalences

\displaystyle\left(\displaystyle{\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}}\delta^{-s_{1}q}\omega_{r_{1},t}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}^{q}\frac{d\delta}{\delta}+\displaystyle{\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}}\delta^{-s_{2}q}\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}^{q}\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\qquad\sim_{\,s_{1},s_{2},p,q,|I|,\textup{diam}(D)}\left(\displaystyle{\int\limits_{0}^{1}}\delta^{-s_{1}q}\omega_{r_{1},t}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}^{q}\frac{d\delta}{\delta}+\displaystyle{\int\limits_{0}^{1}}\delta^{-s_{2}q}\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{p}^{q}\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\qquad\sim_{\,s_{1},s_{2},p,q,a,b}\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a^{ns_{1}q}\omega_{r_{1},t}\left(f,I\times D,a^{-n}\right)_{p}^{q}+\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}b^{ns_{2}q}\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}\left(f,I\times D,b^{-n}\right)_{p}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

hold true for
q\in(0,\infty)
and arbitrary
a,b\in(1,\infty)
. The case
q=\infty
can be treated similarly with the usual modifications. Also the following embeddings hold true:

(i)

B^{s_{1},s_{2}}_{p,q_{1}}(I\times D)\hookrightarrow B^{s_{1},s_{2}}_{p,q_{2}}(I\times D),\text{ if }q_{1}\leq q_{2}
,

(ii)

B^{s_{1},s_{2}}_{p_{1},q}(I\times D)\hookrightarrow B^{s_{1},s_{2}}_{p_{2},q}(I\times D),\text{ if }p_{1}\geq p_{2}\ \text{ and }\ |I\times D|<\infty
,

(iii)

B^{s_{1},s_{2}}_{p,q_{1}}(I\times D)\hookrightarrow B^{\tilde{s_{1}},\tilde{s_{2}}}_{p,q_{2}}(I\times D),\text{ if }s_{i}>\tilde{s_{i}},i=1,2
.

All of these results can be proven following the procedure in
[
DL93
, Ch. 2.10]
.

If we choose
p=q
, we also obtain an equivalent
(quasi-)
norm if we use the averaged moduli of smoothness instead of the supremum versions.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
q\in(0,\infty]
,
s_{1},s_{2}\in(0,\infty)
, and
r_{i}:=\lfloor s_{i}\rfloor+1
,
i=1,2
. Then

\displaystyle|f|^{\bullet}_{B_{q,q}^{s_{1},s_{2}}(I\times D)}:=\begin{cases}\left(\displaystyle{\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}}\delta^{-s_{1}q}\mathrm{w}_{r_{1},t}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{q}^{q}\frac{d\delta}{\delta}+\displaystyle{\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}}\delta^{-s_{2}q}\mathrm{w}_{r_{2},\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{q}^{q}\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},&q<\infty,\\
\sup\limits_{\delta\in[0,\infty)}\delta^{-s_{1}}\mathrm{w}_{r_{1},t}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{q}+\sup\limits_{\delta\in[0,\infty)}\delta^{-s_{2}}\mathrm{w}_{r_{2},\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{q},&q=\infty\end{cases}

is a
(quasi-)
seminorm equivalent to
|f|_{B_{q,q}^{s_{1},s_{2}}(I\times D)}
with equivalence constants only depending on

, and
\textup{LipProp}(D)
.

Remark 3.4
.

The corresponding results for isotropic moduli are mentioned in
[
BDDP02
, p. 403]
and
[
GM14
, p. 2144]
, but no explicit proofs have been given there.

Proof.

For

for

, the corresponding

, and

. Therefore, let

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\delta^{-s_{2}q}\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{q}^{q}\frac{d\delta}{\delta}=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\frac{\sigma(D)}{4r_{2}}}\delta^{-s_{2}q}\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{q}^{q}\frac{d\delta}{\delta}+\int\limits_{\frac{\sigma(D)}{4r_{2}}}^{1}\delta^{-s_{2}q}\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\delta)_{q}^{q}\frac{d\delta}{\delta}

(28)

\displaystyle B_{\omega}

\displaystyle\geq\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}\left(f,I\times D,\frac{\sigma(D)}{4r_{2}}\right)_{q}^{q}\int\limits_{\frac{\sigma(D)}{4r_{2}}}^{1}\delta^{-s_{2}q}\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\gtrsim_{\,q,s_{2},\sigma(D)}\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}\left(f,I\times D,\frac{\sigma(D)}{4r_{2}}\right)_{q}^{q}.

\displaystyle C_{\omega}=\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}\left(f,I\times D,1\right)_{q}^{q}\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}\delta^{-s_{2}q}\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\lesssim_{\,q,s_{2}}\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}\left(f,I\times D,\frac{\sigma(D)}{4r_{2}}\right)_{q}^{q}\lesssim_{\,q,s_{2},\sigma(D)}B_{\omega},

from above using the scaling properties of the modulus of smoothness and linear substitution

\displaystyle B_{\omega}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\frac{\sigma(D)}{4r_{2}}}^{1}\delta^{-s_{2}q}\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}\left(f,I\times D,\frac{4r_{2}}{\sigma(D)}\frac{\delta\sigma(D)}{4r_{2}}\right)_{q}^{q}\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\lesssim_{\,q,s_{2},\sigma(D)}\int\limits_{\frac{\sigma(D)}{4r_{2}}}^{1}\delta^{-s_{2}q}\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}\left(f,I\times D,\frac{\delta\sigma(D)}{4r_{2}}\right)_{q}^{q}\frac{d\delta}{\delta}

(32)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\,q,s_{2},\sigma(D)}\int\limits_{\left(\frac{\sigma(D)}{4r_{2}}\right)^{2}}^{\frac{\sigma(D)}{4r_{2}}}\tilde{\delta}^{-s_{2}q}\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}\left(f,I\times D,\tilde{\delta}\right)_{q}^{q}\frac{d\tilde{\delta}}{\tilde{\delta}}\leq A_{\omega}.

Now, (

), (

.
In a similar manner, one can treat the temporal part, which lastly shows the assertion.

3.2
Whitney-type estimate

First, we will need a result for polynomial scaling whose isotropic form is well-known and can be found, for example in
[
GM14
, Lem. 3.3]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
J
be a finite interval and
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
, a
d
-dimensional simplex. Additionally, assume

\displaystyle\|P\|_{L_{p}(J\times S)}\sim_{\,d,p,q,s_{1},s_{2}}|J\times S|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\|P\|_{L_{q}(J\times S)}\quad\text{for all}\quad P\in\Pi^{r_{1},r_{2}}_{t,\bm{x}}(J\times S).

Proof.

For
J=[0,1]
and
S=S_{{\textrm{standard}}}
the
d
-dimensional reference simplex, i.e.,

S_{{\textrm{standard}}}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid x_{i}\geq 0,\quad i=1,\dots,d,\quad\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}\leq 1\right\},

this follows directly by
|[0,1]\times S_{{\textrm{standard}}}|=|S_{{\textrm{standard}}}|\sim_{\,d}1
and the
(quasi-)

\Pi^{r_{1},r_{2}}_{t,\bm{x}}([0,1]\times S_{{\textrm{standard}}})

Let

Proof.

such that, in particular
C\geq C_{1}^{\frac{1}{s_{1}}+\frac{d}{s_{2}}}
. Now we have to distinguish two cases. If
\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{s_{1}}+\frac{d}{s_{2}}}>C
, then (
5
) implies that
\#\mathcal{P}-\#\mathcal{P}_{0}<1
, i.e.,
\#\mathcal{P}=\#\mathcal{P}_{0}
, and therefore
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{0}
. Now the choice of
C
,
C_{1}
, and (
3
) yield the existence of some
P\in\mathbb{V}^{r_{1},r_{2}}_{\mathcal{P}_{0},\textup{DC}}=\mathbb{V}^{r_{1},r_{2}}_{\mathcal{P},\textup{DC}}
with

\displaystyle\|f-P\|_{L_{p}(I\times D)}\leq C_{1}|f|_{B^{s_{1},s_{2}}_{q,q}(I\times D)}\leq C^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{s_{1}}+\frac{d}{s_{2}}}}|f|_{B^{s_{1},s_{2}}_{q,q}(I\times D)}<\varepsilon|f|_{B^{s_{1},s_{2}}_{q,q}(I\times D)}.

Now consider
\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{s_{1}}+\frac{d}{s_{2}}}\leq C
and let
P_{J\times S}\in\Pi^{r_{1},r_{2}}_{t,\bm{x}}(J\times S)
be a best approximation of
f
with respect to
||\cdot||_{L_{p}(J\times S)}
on
J\times S\in\mathcal{P}
. Such
P_{J\times S}
always exists (not necessarily unique), since
\Pi^{r_{1},r_{2}}_{t,\bm{x}}(J\times S)
is finite dimensional. Further, define
P:=\sum\limits_{J\times S\in\mathcal{P}}\mathds{1}_{J\times S}P_{J\times S}\in\mathbb{V}^{r_{1},r_{2}}_{\mathcal{P},\textup{DC}}(I\times D)
and estimate

\displaystyle||f-P||_{L_{p}(I\times D)}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{J\times S\in\mathcal{P}}||f-P_{J\times S}||_{L_{p}(J\times S)}^{p}\leq\sum\limits_{J\times S\in\mathcal{P}}\delta^{p}=\#\mathcal{P}\,\delta^{p}\leq\left(C\varepsilon^{-\left(\frac{1}{s_{1}}+\frac{d}{s_{2}}\right)}+\#\mathcal{P}_{0}\right)\delta^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\#\mathcal{P}_{0})\varepsilon^{-\left(\frac{1}{s_{1}}+\frac{d}{s_{2}}\right)}\delta^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\,\#\mathcal{P}_{0}}C\varepsilon^{-\left(\frac{1}{s_{1}}+\frac{d}{s_{2}}\right)}\varepsilon^{\left(1+\frac{1}{s_{1}p}+\frac{d}{s_{2}p}\right)p}|f|^{p}_{B^{s_{1},s_{2}}_{q,q}(I\times D)}=C\varepsilon^{p}|f|^{p}_{B^{s_{1},s_{2}}_{q,q}(I\times D)},

with the use of (
5
), for
p<\infty
. The case
p=\infty
works in the same way by replacing the sums by the corresponding suprema and observing that in this case
\delta=\varepsilon|f|_{B^{s_{1},s_{2}}_{q,q}(I\times D)}
. In any case, this directly implies assertion (
6
).
∎

Proofs of auxiliary lemmata

Proof of
1.7
(ii)
.

For any
j\in\{1,\dots,\mathcal{N}(D)\}
,
D_{j}=\Gamma_{j}(G_{j})
for a rotation
\Gamma_{j}
and a Lipschitz graph domain
G_{j}
with corresponding Lipschitz constant
\mathcal{L}(D)
. Therefore, an infinite cone
\mathcal{C}_{inf}=\mathcal{C}_{inf}(\mathcal{L}(D))
exists, such that
G_{j}+\mathcal{C}_{inf}\subset G_{j}
, and thus,
D_{j}+\mathcal{C}_{inf,j}\subset D_{j}
with
\mathcal{C}_{inf,j}:=\Gamma_{j}(\mathcal{C}_{inf})
, for any
j\in\{1,\dots,\mathcal{N}(D)\}
. Now we cut
\mathcal{C}_{inf,j}
off appropriately such that we get a bounded cone
\mathcal{C}_{j}
with
\textup{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{j})\leq\frac{\sigma(D)}{4}
for any such
j
. In particular, these cones can be chosen such that
(\mathcal{C}_{j})_{j=1,\dots,\mathcal{N}(D)}
is a set of congruent cones. Now let
\bm{x}\in D
. If
d(\bm{x},\partial D)\geq\frac{\sigma(D)}{2}
, then
d(\bm{x}+\mathcal{C}_{1},\partial D)\geq\frac{\sigma(D)}{4}
, therefore
\bm{x}+\mathcal{C}_{1}\subset D
. If otherwise
d(\bm{x},\partial D)<\frac{\sigma(D)}{2}
, then, according to
1.6
(iii)c
), there must be
j\in\{1,\dots,\mathcal{N}(D)\}
such that
\bm{x}\in U_{j}
and
d(\bm{x},\partial U_{j})>\sigma(D)
. Therefore,
\bm{x}+\mathcal{C}_{j}\subset U_{j}
, due to
d(\bm{x}+\mathcal{C}_{j},\partial U_{j})>\frac{3}{4}\sigma(D)
. Further, the choice of
\mathcal{C}_{j}\subset\mathcal{C}_{inf,j}
implies that
\bm{x}+\mathcal{C}_{j}\subset D_{j}
, since
\bm{x}\in D\cap U_{j}=D_{j}\cap U_{j}
due to
1.6
(iii)b
). The latter now also shows
\bm{x}+\mathcal{C}_{j}\subset D
, since
\bm{x}+\mathcal{C}_{j}\subset D_{j}\cap U_{j}=D\cap U_{j}\subset D
.
∎

Proof of
1.7
4
.

Assume
\sigma(D)>\textup{diam}(D)
was true. Then

\displaystyle\max\{d(\bm{x},\partial D),d(\bm{y},\partial D),|\bm{x}-\bm{y}|\leq\textup{diam}(D)<\sigma(D)

would be true for arbitrary
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in D
. Therefore,
1.6
(iii)c
) implies that there is
j\in\{1,\dots,\mathcal{N}(D)\}
such that
D\subset U_{j}
. However,
d(D,U_{j})>0
, i.e.
D\subsetneq U_{j}
since
\sigma(D)
is strictly larger than
\textup{diam}(D)
. Hence, since
D_{j}
unbounded,
D\cap D_{j}
must be a true subset of
U_{j}\cap D_{j}
. But also
D\cap D_{j}=(U_{j}\cap D_{j})\cap D_{j}=U_{j}\cap D_{j}
. This now clearly is a contradiction.
∎

Proof of

.

The first inequality is trivial. The second one is, too, if

D
. Therefore, we can assume
\textup{diam}(D)=1
without loss of generality.

Now we set
\rho:=\frac{\sigma(D)}{4}
,
t\in\Big(0,\frac{\rho}{r^{2}}\Big]
, and
0\leq|\bm{h}|\leq t
. Further, by
(D_{j})_{j=1,\dots,\mathcal{N}(D)}
and
(\mathcal{C}_{j})_{j=1,\dots,\mathcal{N}(D)}
we denote the set of special Lipschitz domains from
1.6
and corresponding cones from
1.7
(ii)
, chosen as in the corresponding proof above, with
\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}:=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\frac{\bm{x}}{t}\in\mathcal{C}_{j}\}
for
j=1,\dots,\mathcal{N}(D)
. Next, define
D^{j}:=\left(D\cap U_{j}\right)_{r,\bm{h}}=\left(D_{j}\cap U_{j}\right)_{r,\bm{h}}
, due to
1.6
(iii)b
) (see Definition
2.1
regarding the notation
(D\cap U_{j})_{r,\bm{h}}
etc.),
j=1,\dots,\mathcal{N}(D)
, and
D_{\mathcal{N}(D)+1}:=D\setminus D_{\frac{\sigma(D)}{2}}
with

\displaystyle D_{\frac{\sigma(D)}{2}}:=\left\{\bm{z}\in D\mid d(\bm{z},\partial D)<\frac{\sigma(D)}{2}\right\},

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}(D)+1}:=\mathcal{C}_{1}
, and
\mathcal{C}^{t}_{\mathcal{N}(D)+1}
consistent to the above cases.

We will first show that
D_{r,\bm{h}}\subset\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\mathcal{N}(D)+1}D^{j}
. Since,
D^{\mathcal{N}(D)+1}=D\setminus D_{\frac{\sigma(D)}{2}}\subset D_{r,\bm{h}}
, due to
|i\bm{h}|\leq rt\leq\frac{\sigma(D)}{4}<\frac{\sigma(D)}{2}
for any
i=1,\dots,r
, it is sufficient to show
D_{r,\bm{h}}\cap D_{\frac{\sigma(D)}{2}}\subset\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\mathcal{N}(D)}D^{j}
.

This can be seen as follows. Let
\bm{x}\in D_{r,\bm{h}}\cap D_{\frac{\sigma(D)}{2}}
, then
d(\bm{x},\partial D)<\frac{\sigma(D)}{2}<\sigma(D)
and
\bm{x}+r\bm{h}\in D
. As above, for any
i=1,\dots,r
,
|i\bm{h}|<\frac{\sigma(D)}{2}
and therefore by the triangle inequality
d(\bm{x}+i\bm{h},\partial D)<\sigma(D)
. Now
1.6
(iii)c
) implies the existence of
j\in\{1,\dots,\mathcal{N}(D)\}
with
\bm{x}
,
\bm{x}+i\bm{h}\in U_{j}
. Thus,
\bm{x}\in D^{j}\subset\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\mathcal{N}(D)+1}D^{j}
.

Further, we proceed to show that
D^{j}\subset D_{r,\bm{h}+r\bm{s}}
, if
\bm{s}\in\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}
for any

. First, consider
j\leq\mathcal{N}(D)
. Then
\bm{x}+i\bm{h}\in D^{j}
for any

, and therefore
\bm{x}+i\bm{h}+\mathcal{C}_{j}\subset D
due to our choice of the cone
\mathcal{C}_{j}
. Since,
\bm{s}\in\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}
,
\frac{\bm{s}}{t}\in\mathcal{C}_{j}
, and
\lambda\,\frac{\bm{s}}{t}\in\mathcal{C}_{j}
for any

\lambda:=tm

, which means

. Together, this shows that

\displaystyle\Delta_{\bm{h},\bm{x}}^{r}f(\tau,\bm{z})=\sum\limits_{l=1}^{r}(-1)^{l}\binom{r}{l}\left(\Delta^{r}_{l\bm{s},\bm{x}}f(\tau,\bm{z}+l\bm{h})-\Delta^{r}_{\bm{h}+l\bm{s},\bm{x}}f(\tau,\bm{z})\right),

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{\bm{h},\bm{x}}^{r}f\|^{p}_{L_{p}(I\times D^{j})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\,p,r}\sum\limits^{r}_{l=1}\binom{r}{l}\left(\|\Delta^{r}_{l\bm{s},\bm{x}}f(\cdot,\cdot+l\bm{h})\|^{p}_{L_{p}(I\times D_{r,\bm{h}+l\bm{s}})}+\|\Delta^{r}_{\bm{h}+l\bm{s},\bm{x}}f\|^{p}_{L_{p}(I\times D_{r,\bm{h}+l\bm{s}})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits^{r}_{l=1}\binom{r}{l}\left(\|\Delta^{r}_{l\bm{s},\bm{x}}f\|^{p}_{L_{p}(I\times D_{r,l\bm{s}})}+\|\Delta^{r}_{\bm{h}+l\bm{s},\bm{x}}f\|^{p}_{L_{p}(I\times D_{r,\bm{h}+l\bm{s}})}\right).

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{\bm{h},\bm{x}}^{r}f\|^{p}_{L_{p}(I\times D^{j})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\,p,r}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|}\sum\limits^{r}_{l=1}\binom{r}{l}\left(\int\limits_{\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}}\|\Delta^{r}_{l\bm{s},\bm{x}}f\|^{p}_{L_{p}(I\times D_{r,l\bm{s}})}\,d\bm{s}+\int\limits_{\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}}\|\Delta^{r}_{\bm{h}+l\bm{s},\bm{x}}f\|^{p}_{L_{p}(I\times D_{r,\bm{h}+l\bm{s}})}\,d\bm{s}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\,\mathcal{L}(D),\sigma(D)}\frac{1}{t^{d}}\sum\limits^{r}_{l=1}\binom{r}{l}\left(\int\limits_{\mathcal{C}_{j}^{lt}}\|\Delta^{r}_{\bm{u},\bm{x}}f\|^{p}_{L_{p}(I\times D_{r,\bm{u}})}\,d\bm{u}+\int\limits_{\mathcal{C}_{j}^{lt}+\bm{h}}\|\Delta^{r}_{\bm{v},\bm{x}}f\|^{p}_{L_{p}(I\times D_{r,\bm{v}})}\,d\bm{v}\right),

where we have used
|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|=|\mathcal{C}_{j}|\,t^{d}\sim_{\,\mathcal{L}(D),\sigma(D)}t^{d}
as well as the substitutions
\bm{u}:=l\bm{s}
and
\bm{v}:=\bm{h}+l\bm{s}
. Since
\textup{diam}(\mathcal{C})\leq\textup{diam}(D)=1
, we have
\mathcal{C}_{j}\subset[-1,1]^{d}
. This shows
\mathcal{C}_{j}^{lt}\subset[-lt,lt]^{d}\subset[-rt,rt]^{d}
and, due to
|\bm{h}|\leq t
,
\mathcal{C}_{j}^{lt}+\bm{h}\subset[-(r+1)t,(r+1)t]^{d}
. This allows us to further estimate

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{\bm{h},\bm{x}}^{r}f\|^{p}_{L_{p}(I\times D^{j})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\,p,r,\mathcal{L}(D),\sigma(D)}\frac{1}{t^{d}}\int\limits_{[-(r+1)t,(r+1)t]^{d}}\|\Delta^{r}_{\bm{u},\bm{x}}f\|^{p}_{L_{p}(I\times D_{r,\bm{u}})}d\bm{u}\lesssim_{\,d,r}w_{r}(f,I\times D,(r+1)t)_{p}^{p}.

, going over to the supremum over all

\delta=0

Proof of
2.16
.

First, we will show assertion
5
. The scaling estimates for the moduli of smoothness from
2.4
and the monotonicity immediately imply
\omega_{r_{1},t}(f,I\times D,|I|)_{p}=0=\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\textup{diam}(D))_{p}
. For
\tau\in I
, set
f_{\tau}:D\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
via

\displaystyle\infty>\|f\|_{L_{p}(I\times D)}^{p}=\int\limits_{I}\|f_{\tau}\|_{L_{p}(D)}^{p}\,d\tau

for
p<\infty
. Therefore,
f_{\tau}\in L_{p}(D)
for almost every
\tau\in I
. Similarly,
f_{\tau}\in L_{\infty}(D)
almost surely if

. Further,
\omega_{r_{2},\bm{x}}(f,I\times D,\textup{diam}(D))_{p}=0

\displaystyle 0=\Delta_{\bm{h},\bm{x}}^{r_{2}}f(\tau,\bm{z})=f(\tau,\bm{z}+\bm{h})-f(\tau,\bm{z})=f_{\tau}(\bm{z}+\bm{h})-f_{\tau}(\bm{z})=\Delta_{\bm{h}}^{r_{2}}f_{\tau}(\bm{z}).

for almost every
(\tau,\bm{z})\in I\times D_{1,\bm{h}}
and every
\bm{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Thus,
\omega_{r_{2}}(f_{\tau},D,\textup{diam}(D))_{p}=0
for almost every
\tau\in I
. According to

DL04
, Lem. 2.13]
, this means
f_{\tau}\in\Pi_{\bm{x}}^{r_{2}}(D)

(56)

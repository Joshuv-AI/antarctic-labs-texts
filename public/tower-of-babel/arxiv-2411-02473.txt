Schmidt Decomposition of Multipartite States
Mithilesh Kumar

Abstract
Quantum states can be written in infinitely many ways depending on the choices of basis. The Schmidt decomposition of a quantum state has many useful properties in the study of entanglement. All bipartite states admit Schmidt decomposition, but this does not extend to multipartite systems. We obtain necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of Schmidt decompositions of multipartite states. Moreover, we provide an efficient algorithm to obtain the decomposition for a Schmidt decomposable multipartite state.



I
Introduction

Consider Alice and Bob who share a system in some state
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
. Alice and Bob may choose orthonormal bases
\{|i_{A}\rangle\}
and
\{|j_{B}\rangle\}
independently and express the state as

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}|i_{A}\rangle|j_{B}\rangle

(1)

such that
a_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\sum_{ij}|a_{ij}|^{2}=1
. In general, such an expansion may have at most
n_{A}\times n_{B}
terms. The actual number of terms depends on the chosen bases. Bases that would require minimum number of terms can be obtained by the so-called Schmidt decomposition. Schmidt decomposition provides orthonormal set of vectors (which can be extended to bases)
\{|k_{A}\rangle\}
and
\{|k_{B}\rangle\}
such that

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}|k_{A}\rangle|k_{B}\rangle

(2)

where
\lambda_{k}\geq 0
are real and
k\leq\min(n_{A},n_{B})
.

Schmidt decomposition of bipartite states is an immediate consequence of singular value decomposition (SVD) introduced by Schmidt
[
11
]
: the matrix
A
of coefficients
a_{ij}
has SVD as
A=UDV
. The unitary matrix
U
transforms states
|i_{A}\rangle
to
|k_{A}\rangle
and the unitary matrix
V
transforms states
|j_{B}\rangle
to
|k_{B}\rangle
. For an introduction to Schmidt decomposition of bipartite states and its properties, readers are referred to Nielsen and Chuang
[
8
]
. Some of these properties are listed below:

1.

Minimality
: Schmidt vectors ensure minimum number of terms in the expansion.

2.

Bijection
: There is a bijection between the sets
\{|k_{A}\rangle\}
and
\{|k_{B}\rangle\}
.

3.

Schmidt number
: If the number of terms (known as Schmidt number) is more than one, then the system is entangled.

4.

Identical spectrum
: The reduced density matrices
\rho_{A}
and
\rho_{B}
have the same spectrum given by
\{\lambda_{k}^{2}\}
.

5.

Local unitary independence
: Schmidt coefficients
\lambda_{k}
are independent of local unitary transformations.

Given Schmidt form of a state, computing entropy or deciding state transformation through majorization or approximation (through keeping the largest
k
terms) becomes easy. Naturally, one would like to generalize such a decomposition to multipartite states. Although Schmidt decomposition exists for every bipartite state, this does not extend to multipartite states. There are tri-partite states that do not admit such a decomposition. Asher Peres
[
10
]
studied tripartite Schmidt decomposition by obtaining conditions on intermediate matrices after first considering the Schmidt decomposition of a bipartition of the state. Thapliyal
[
13
]
obtained a condition for Schmidt decomposability of a state and connected it to the separability of the state. Pati
[
9
]
obtained another criterion for tripartite case using partial inner product. Acín et al.
[
1
]
extended the Schmidt decomposition to three-qubit states.

Carteret et al.
[
2
]
worked on a specific generalization of Schmidt decomposition of multipartite states. In their work, it is shown that a multipartite state can be expressed in orthonormal sets of vectors such that all terms have only real coefficients and local unitary independence is ensured. But rest of properties listed above are not guaranteed.

In this paper, we want to work with Schmidt decompositions that satisfy all the properties 1-5 listed above. A state of a multipartite system with subsystems labeled by
A_{1},A_{2},...,A_{n}
can be written as

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{n}}a_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{n}}|i_{1}^{A_{1}}\rangle|i_{2}^{A_{2}}\rangle\cdots|i_{n}^{A_{n}}\rangle

(3)

A natural generalization of
Schmidt form
of the state is

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell_{A_{1}}\rangle|\ell_{A_{2}}\rangle\cdots|\ell_{A_{n}}\rangle

(4)

where
\lambda_{\ell}\geq 0
are real, states
|\ell_{A_{1}}\rangle,|\ell_{A_{2}}\rangle,\cdots,|\ell_{A_{n}}\rangle
are orthonormal in their respective Hilbert spaces. For example, a state of a tripartite system with subsystems
A,B
and
C
can be written as

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum_{ijk}a_{ijk}|i_{A}\rangle|j_{B}\rangle|k_{C}\rangle

(5)

with Schmidt form expressed as

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell_{A}\rangle|\ell_{B}\rangle|\ell_{C}\rangle

(6)

where
\lambda_{\ell}\geq 0
are real, states
|\ell_{A}\rangle,|\ell_{B}\rangle
and
|\ell_{C}\rangle
are orthonormal in their respective Hilbert spaces. Similarly, a state of a quadripartite system with subsystems
A,B,C
and
D
can be written as

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum_{lmno}a_{lmno}|l_{A}\rangle|m_{B}\rangle|n_{C}\rangle|o_{D}\rangle

(7)

with
Schmidt form
of the state as

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}|k_{A}\rangle|k_{B}\rangle|k_{C}\rangle|k_{D}\rangle

(8)

where
\lambda_{k}\geq 0
are real, states
|k_{A}\rangle,|k_{B}\rangle,|k_{C}\rangle
and
|k_{D}\rangle
are orthonormal in their respective Hilbert spaces.

I.1
Contributions

The main contribution of this paper is a necessary and sufficient condition for Schmidt decomposition (Equation
4
) of multipartite states. In addition, separate such conditions are provided in the special case of tripartite (Equation
6
) and quadripartite states (Equation
8
). Using these conditions, we have provided algorithms to check whether a multipartite state is Schmidt decomposable and provide such a decomposition if it is. Finally, we prove that SCHMIDT-PARTITION problem is NP-complete.

II
Schmidt decomposition of tripartite states

We start with obtaining necessary and sufficient condition for Schmidt decomposition of Equation
6
.
For a given matrix
A
, its adjoint
A^{\dagger}
is a matrix obtained by first taking transpose of
A
and then complex conjugating its elements, i.e

\displaystyle A^{\dagger}_{ij}=A^{*}_{ji}

(9)

Definition 1
.

Two matrices
A
and
B
are said to
commute
if
AB=BA
.

Definition 2
.

A matrix
A
is called
normal
if it commutes with its adjoint, i.e.
AA^{\dagger}=A^{\dagger}A
.

Normal operators satisfy the following well-known spectral decomposition theorem.

Theorem 1
.

A matrix is normal if and only if it is diagonalizable, i.e. there exists a unitary matrix
U
such that

\displaystyle A=U^{\dagger}DU

(10)

where
D
is a diagonal matrix. We say that
U
diagonalizes
A
.

Definition 3
.

A matrix is called positive semi-definite if all of its eigenvalues are non-negative.

Positive semi-definite matrices are normal and hence spectral decomposition applies to them. The following is a well-known result that

Theorem 2
.

For any matrix
A
, the matrices
A^{\dagger}A
and
AA^{\dagger}
are positive semi-definite and have the same eigenvalue spectrum, i.e. there exist unitary matrices
P
and
Q
such that

\displaystyle A^{\dagger}A

\displaystyle=Q^{\dagger}DQ

(11)

\displaystyle AA^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=PDP^{\dagger}

(12)

where
D
is diagonal matrix of eigenvalues.

Singular value decomposition can be stated as follows

Theorem 3
.

[
8
]

For any matrix
A
, let
P
diagonalizes
AA^{\dagger}
and
Q
diagonalizes
A^{\dagger}A
, then
P^{\dagger}AQ^{\dagger}
is diagonal.

Usually, the spectral decomposition of two matrices
A
and
B
require different unitary matrices
U
and
V
. When same unitary matrix diagonalizes matrices
A
and
B
, we say
A
and
B
are
simultaneously
diagonalizable.

Theorem 4
.

Two normal matrices are simultaneously diagonalizable if and only if they commute.

We can extend the notion of commutation to a set of matrices.

Definition 4
.

A set of matrices
\mathcal{A}
is said to commute if every pair of its elements commute.

Definition 5
.

A set
\mathcal{A}
of
m\times n
matrices are said to
positively commute
if for each
A_{i}\in\mathcal{A}

1.

A^{\dagger}_{i}A_{i}
commute with each other

2.

A_{i}A^{\dagger}_{i}
commute with each other

The following lemma applies diagonalization to positively commuting set
\mathcal{A}
of matrices.

Lemma 1
.

If
\mathcal{A}
commutes positively, then there exists unitary matrices
P
and
Q
such that for each
A_{i}\in\mathcal{A}

1.

A_{i}^{\dagger}A_{i}=Q^{\dagger}D_{i}Q
and

2.

A_{i}A_{i}^{\dagger}=PD_{i}P^{\dagger}

where
D_{i}
are positive semi-definite and diagonal.

Proof.

Since
A_{i}^{\dagger}A_{i}
is positive semi-definite, it admits spectral decomposition given by

\displaystyle A_{i}^{\dagger}A_{i}=Q^{\dagger}D_{i}Q

where
D_{i}
is a diagonal matrix of non-negative eigenvalues.
The same unitary matrices work for every
A_{j}^{\dagger}A_{j}
as they commute and we can apply Theorem
4
. The above arguments hold for
AA^{\dagger}
analogously.
∎

Definition 6
.

For a positively commuting
\mathcal{A}
, we call the pair of unitary matrices
(P,Q)
in the above lemma as diagonalizing pair.

Definition 7
.

A matrix
S
is called a
scaled unitary
if
S
can be decomposed as
S=\Lambda U
where

1.

U
is unitary,

2.

\Lambda
is positive semi-definite, diagonal and
\mathsf{Tr}(\Lambda^{2})=1

From the definition, the following lemma follows immediately.

Lemma 2
.

If
S
is scaled unitary, then
SS^{\dagger}
is diagonal.

Definition 8
.

Given a tripartite state
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{ijk}a_{ijk}|i_{A}\rangle|j_{B}\rangle|k_{C}\rangle
, we define
matrix set

\mathcal{A}
of
|\psi\rangle
as the collection of matrices
A_{i}=(a_{i})_{jk}
, i.e. we fix index
i
and vary indices
j,k
to obtain the elements of
A_{i}
.

Definition 9
.

For any square matrix
A
,
diag(A)
represents a column with entries from the diagonals of
A
, i.e.
diag(A)_{i}=A_{ii}
.

Theorem 5
.

A tripartite state
|\psi\rangle
is Schmidt decomposable if and only if

1.

the matrix set
\mathcal{A}
commutes positively, and

2.

the matrix
S=[diag(P^{\dagger}A_{i}Q^{\dagger})]
is scaled unitary.

Proof.

Suppose
|\psi\rangle
is Schmidt decomposable. We’ll start with the Schmidt decomposition and show that the matrix set
\mathcal{A}
commutes positively and
S
is scaled unitary. We’ll use the unitary relations
V,P,Q
between Schmidt bases
|\ell\rangle
and starting basis
|i\rangle
, where
\Lambda
is diagonal matrix of coefficients
\lambda
.

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell_{A}\rangle|\ell_{B}\rangle|\ell_{C}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\left(\Lambda V|i^{\ell}_{A}\rangle\right)|\ell_{B}\rangle|\ell_{C}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\left(\sum_{i}d^{i}_{\ell\ell}|i_{A}\rangle\right)|\ell_{B}\rangle|\ell_{C}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}|i_{A}\rangle\sum_{\ell}d^{i}_{\ell\ell}\left(\sum_{j}P_{j\ell}|j_{B}\rangle\right)\left(\sum_{k}Q_{k\ell}|k_{C}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{ijk}\left(\sum_{\ell}P_{j\ell}d^{i}_{\ell\ell}Q_{\ell k}\right)|i_{A}\rangle|j_{B}\rangle|k_{C}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{ijk}a_{ijk}|i_{A}\rangle|j_{B}\rangle|k_{C}\rangle

The matrices
A_{i}
in
\mathcal{A}
are defined as

\displaystyle A_{i}=PD_{i}Q

(13)

which gives

\displaystyle A^{\dagger}_{i}A_{i}

\displaystyle=Q^{\dagger}|D_{i}|^{2}Q

(14)

\displaystyle A_{i}A^{\dagger}_{i}

\displaystyle=P|D_{i}|^{2}P^{\dagger}

(15)

This implies that each of
A_{i}^{\dagger}A_{i}
are diagonalized by
Q
and each of
A_{i}A_{i}^{\dagger}
are diagonalized by
P
. By Theorem
4
, they must commute with each other. Hence,
\mathcal{A}
commutes positively. In addition, we define

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\Lambda V

(16)

\displaystyle=[diag(P^{\dagger}A_{i}Q^{\dagger})]

(17)

which is scaled unitary. Notice that the normalization condition implies that
\mathsf{Tr}(\Lambda^{2})=1
.

For the other direction with
\mathcal{A}
commuting positively and
S
being a scaled unitary, we’ll construct the Schmidt decomposition of
|\psi\rangle
.

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{ijk}a_{ijk}|i_{A}\rangle|j_{B}\rangle|k_{C}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}|i_{A}\rangle\sum_{jk}a_{ijk}|j_{B}\rangle|k_{C}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}|i_{A}\rangle\sum_{jk}\sum_{\ell}P_{j\ell}d^{i}_{\ell\ell}Q_{k\ell}|j_{B}\rangle|k_{C}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}|i_{A}\rangle\sum_{\ell}d^{i}_{\ell\ell}\left(\sum_{j}P_{j\ell}|j_{B}\rangle\right)\left(\sum_{k}Q_{k\ell}|k_{C}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\left(\sum_{i}d^{i}_{\ell\ell}|i_{A}\rangle\right)|\ell_{B}\rangle|\ell_{C}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell_{A}\rangle|\ell_{B}\rangle|\ell_{C}\rangle

Notice that the elements
d^{i}_{\ell\ell}
form the diagonal of decomposition of
A_{i}=PD_{i}Q
. Therefore, the matrix columns of matrix
S
formed by elements
d^{i}_{\ell\ell}
are formed by taking diagonal of
P^{\dagger}A_{i}Q^{\dagger}
as vector. Since
S
is given to be scaled unitary, it has decomposition of the form
\Lambda V
where
\Lambda
is diagonal and
V
is unitary. This provides us the required Schmidt decomposition.
∎

II.1
Algorithm

Theorem
5
can be used to obtain Schmidt decomposition of a decomposable state efficiently. As the matrix set is diagonalized by matrices
(P,Q)
, we only need to look for an appropriate
A_{i}
to obtain them. Using
P,Q
, we obtain
S
. Here are the steps:

1.

Consider matrices
A_{i}
, and compute
L_{i}=A_{i}A_{i}^{\dagger}
and
M_{i}=A_{i}^{\dagger}A_{i}
.

2.

Choose random real numbers
r_{i}
and compute
L=\sum_{i}r_{i}L_{i}
and
M=\sum_{i}r_{i}M_{i}
. This random linear combination is to remove degeneracies.

3.

Compute spectral decompositions of
L
and
M
to obtain diagonalizing pairs of unitary matrices
P,Q
.

4.

Compute the diagonal matrices
D_{i}=P^{\dagger}A_{i}Q^{\dagger}
. If
D_{i}
are not diagonal, terminate.

5.

Compute the matrix
S=[diag(D_{i})]
.

6.

Compute
SS^{\dagger}
, the square root of the diagonal elements gives
\lambda_{\ell}
.

7.

Divide the rows of
S
by the non-zero
\lambda_{\ell}
which gives the unitary matrix
V
. If
V
is not unitary, terminate.

The correctness of the above algorithm follows from the correctness of Theorem
5
. Since the running time of each step in the algorithm is polynomial in the size of the input, the above algorithm terminates in polynomial time.

III
Schmidt decomposition of quadripartite states

The above scheme for tripartite states can be extended to quadripartite states with minor changes as will be shown in this section. To start with, the matrix set
\mathcal{A}
needs to account for an extra index.

Definition 10
.

A set
\mathcal{D}
of square matrices is called
unit decomposable
if each matrix
D_{k}\in\mathcal{D}
satisfies following conditions

1.

rank of
D_{k}
is 1,

2.

D_{k}
can be decomposed as
D_{k}=\lambda_{k}u_{k}v_{k}^{T}
with column vectors
u_{k},v_{k}
such that
\lambda_{k}\geq 0
,
U=[u_{k}]
and
V=[v_{k}]
are unitary.

We extend the notion of matrix set defined above to quadripartite states as follows.

Definition 11
.

Given a quadripartite state
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{lmno}a_{lmno}|l_{A}\rangle|m_{B}\rangle|n_{C}\rangle|o_{D}\rangle
, we define
matrix set

\mathcal{A}
of
|\psi\rangle
as the collection of matrices
A^{lm}=(a^{lm})_{no}
, i.e. fix two indices
l,m
and vary other indices
n,o
.

Theorem 6
.

A quadripartite state
|\psi\rangle
is Schmidt decomposable if and only if

1.

the matrix set
\mathcal{A}
commutes positively, and

2.

the matrix set
\mathcal{D}
with matrices
D_{k}=[diag(P^{\dagger}A^{lm}Q^{\dagger})_{k}]
is unit decomposable, where
D_{k}
is a matrix formed by taking
k
th element of each of vectors
diag(P^{\dagger}A^{lm}Q^{\dagger})
.

Proof.

Suppose
|\psi\rangle
is Schmidt decomposable.

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}|k_{A}\rangle|k_{B}\rangle|k_{C}\rangle|k_{D}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}\left(\sum_{l}u^{A}_{lk}|l_{A}\rangle\right)\left(\sum_{m}u^{B}_{mk}|m_{B}\rangle\right)|k_{C}\rangle|k_{D}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\left(\sum_{l,m}\lambda_{k}u^{A}_{lk}u^{B}_{mk}|l_{A}\rangle|m_{B}\rangle\right)|k_{C}\rangle|k_{D}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\left(\sum_{l,m}d^{lm}_{kk}|l_{A}\rangle|m_{B}\rangle\right)|k_{C}\rangle|k_{D}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m}|l_{A}\rangle|m_{B}\rangle\sum_{k}d^{lm}_{kk}|k_{C}\rangle|k_{D}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m}|l_{A}\rangle|m_{B}\rangle\sum_{k}\sum_{n,o}P_{nk}d^{lm}_{kk}Q_{ko}|n_{C}\rangle|o_{D}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m}|l_{A}\rangle|m_{B}\rangle\sum_{n,o}a_{lmno}|n_{C}\rangle|o_{D}\rangle

Hence, we have (1) the matrix set
\mathcal{A}
commutes positively, and (2) the matrix set
\mathcal{D}
with matrices
D_{k}=[diag(P^{\dagger}A^{lm}Q^{\dagger})_{k}]
is unit decomposable, where
D_{k}
is a matrix formed by taking
k
th element of each of vectors
diag(P^{\dagger}A^{lm}Q^{\dagger})
.

For the other direction we assume that
\mathcal{A}
commutes positively and
D_{k}
are unit decomposable. We construct the Schmidt decomposition of the state
|\psi\rangle
. The proof effectively runs backwards as above.

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m,n,o}a_{lmno}|l_{A}\rangle|m_{B}\rangle|n_{C}\rangle|o_{D}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m}|l_{A}\rangle|m_{B}\rangle\sum_{n,o}a_{lmno}|n_{C}\rangle|o_{D}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m}|l_{A}\rangle|m_{B}\rangle\sum_{k}\sum_{n,o}P_{nk}d^{lm}_{kk}Q_{ko}|n_{C}\rangle|o_{D}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m}|l_{A}\rangle|m_{B}\rangle\sum_{k}d^{lm}_{kk}|k_{C}\rangle|k_{D}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\left(\sum_{l,m}d^{lm}_{kk}|l_{A}\rangle|m_{B}\rangle\right)|k_{C}\rangle|k_{D}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\left(\sum_{l,m}\lambda_{k}u^{A}_{lk}u^{B}_{mk}|l_{A}\rangle|m_{B}\rangle\right)|k_{C}\rangle|k_{D}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}\left(\sum_{l}u^{A}_{lk}|l_{A}\rangle\right)\left(\sum_{m}u^{B}_{mk}|m_{B}\rangle\right)|k_{C}\rangle|k_{D}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}|k_{A}\rangle|k_{B}\rangle|k_{C}\rangle|k_{D}\rangle

Decomposition of
a_{lmno}
has been according to
\mathcal{A}
. The matrices defined by
d^{lm}_{kk}
considering the diagonals of
P^{\dagger}A^{lm}Q^{\dagger}
as vectors and used as columns, i.e
D_{k}=[diag(P^{\dagger}A^{lm}Q^{\dagger})_{k}]
.
∎

III.1
Algorithm

Theorem
6
can be used to obtain Schmidt decomposition of a decomposable state efficiently. As the matrix set is diagonalized by matrices
(P,Q)
, we only need to look for an appropriate
A^{lm}
to obtain them. Using
P,Q
, we obtain matrices
D_{k}
. Here are the steps:

1.

Consider any matrix
A^{lm}
, possibly with least number of zeroes and compute
L^{lm}=A^{lm}A^{lm\dagger}
and
M^{lm}=A^{lm\dagger}A^{lm}
.

2.

Compute spectral decompositions of random linear combinations of
L^{lm}
and
M^{lm}
to obtain diagonalizing pairs of unitary matrices
P,Q
.

3.

Compute the diagonal matrices
S^{lm}=P^{\dagger}A^{lm}Q^{\dagger}
. If any of them is not diagonal, terminate.

4.

Compute the matrix
D_{k}=[diag(S^{lm})_{k}]
.

5.

Compute rank 1 decompositions of
D_{k}=\lambda_{k}u^{A}_{k}u^{BT}_{k}
. If any one of
D_{k}
are not rank 1, terminate.

6.

Output
\lambda_{k},U^{A}=[u^{A}_{k}],U^{B}=[u^{B}_{k}],P
and
Q
, that can be used to construct the Schmidt decomposition.

The correctness of the above algorithm follows from the correctness of Theorem
6
. Since the running time of each step in the algorithm is polynomial in the size of the input, the above algorithm terminates in polynomial time.

IV
Schmidt decomposition of multipartite states

In this section, we generalize the schemes for tripartite and quadripartite states to general multipartite states.

Definition 12
.

A family
\mathcal{M}
of
N
positively commuting sets
\mathcal{A}_{i}
of matrices is called
central
if the diagonalizing pairs
(P_{i},Q_{i})
for each set
\mathcal{A}_{i}
is of the form
(P_{i},Q_{N})
.

Definition 13
.

For given set of indices
\{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{n}\}
, define
S_{i_{j}i_{k}}
as set of indices
\{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{n}\}-\{i_{j},i_{k}\}
.
Given a set of numbers
a_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{n}}
, define a matrix
A^{S_{i_{j}i_{k}}}
by fixing the indices
S_{i_{j}i_{k}}
and only varying
i_{j}
and
i_{k}
.

Definition 14
.

Given a multipartite state
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{n}}a_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{n}}|i_{1}^{A_{1}}\rangle|i_{2}^{A_{2}}\rangle\cdots|i_{n}^{A_{n}}\rangle
, we define
matrix set
of
|\psi\rangle
as the family
\mathcal{M}
of collection
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
of matrices
A^{S_{i_{\ell},i_{n}}}
.

Theorem 7
.

A multipartite state
|\psi\rangle
is Schmidt decomposable if and only if its matrix set
\mathcal{M}
is central.

Proof.

Suppose
|\psi\rangle
is Schmidt decomposable.

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell_{A_{1}}\rangle|\ell_{A_{2}}\rangle\cdots|\ell_{A_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\left(U_{A_{1}}|i^{A_{1}}_{\ell}\rangle\right)\left(U_{A_{2}}|i^{A_{2}}_{\ell}\rangle\right)\cdots\left(U_{A_{n}}|i^{A_{n}}_{\ell}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\left(\sum_{i_{1}}u^{A_{1}}_{i_{1}\ell}|i^{A_{1}}_{1}\rangle\right)\left(\sum_{i_{2}}u^{A_{2}}_{i_{2}\ell}|i^{A_{2}}_{2}\rangle\right)\cdots

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i_{n}}u^{A_{n}}_{i_{n}\ell}|i^{A_{n}}_{n}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{n}}\left(\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}u^{A_{1}}_{i_{1}\ell}u^{A_{2}}_{i_{2}\ell}\cdots u^{A_{n}}_{i_{n}\ell}\right)|i_{1}^{A_{1}}\rangle|i_{2}^{A_{2}}\rangle\cdots|i_{n}^{A_{n}}\rangle

This implies that

\displaystyle a_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{n}}=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}u^{A_{1}}_{i_{1}\ell}u^{A_{2}}_{i_{2}\ell}\cdots u^{A_{n}}_{i_{n}\ell}

(18)

From this decompositions of
a_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{n}}
, we can define any matrix
A^{S_{i_{k}i_{n}}}
with elements

\displaystyle a^{S_{i_{k}i_{n}}}_{i_{k}i_{n}}=\sum_{\ell}u^{A_{k}}_{i_{k}\ell}d^{S_{i_{k}i_{n}}}_{\ell\ell}u^{A_{n}}_{\ell i_{n}}

(19)

This shows that we can decompose

\displaystyle A^{S_{i_{k}i_{n}}}=U^{A_{k}}D^{S_{i_{k}i_{n}}}U^{A_{n}}

(20)

Hence, the sets
\mathcal{A}_{k}
positively commute and diagonalizing pairs are of the form
(U^{A_{k}},U^{A_{n}})
. This implies that the matrix set
\mathcal{M}
is central.

For the other direction, we assume that the matrix set
\mathcal{M}
is central.

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{n}}a_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{n}}|i_{1}^{A_{1}}\rangle|i_{2}^{A_{2}}\rangle\cdots|i_{n}^{A_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{S_{i_{k},i_{n}}}|S_{i_{k},i_{n}}\rangle\sum_{i_{k},i_{n}}a_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{n}}|i_{k}\rangle|i_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{S_{i_{k},i_{n}}}|S_{i_{k},i_{n}}\rangle\sum_{i_{k},i_{n}}\left(\sum_{\ell}u^{A_{k}}_{i_{k}\ell}d^{S_{i_{k},i_{n}}}_{\ell\ell}u^{A_{n}}_{\ell i_{n}}\right)|i_{k}\rangle|i_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{S_{i_{k},i_{n}}}|S_{i_{k},i_{n}}\rangle\sum_{i_{k}}\left(\sum_{\ell}u^{A_{k}}_{i_{k}\ell}d^{S_{i_{k},i_{n}}}_{\ell\ell}\right)|i_{k}\rangle\left(\sum_{i_{n}}u^{A_{n}}_{\ell i_{n}}|i^{A_{n}}_{n}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\left(\sum_{S_{i_{n}}}u^{A_{k}}_{i_{k}\ell}d^{S_{i_{k},i_{n}}}_{\ell\ell}|S_{i_{n}}\rangle\right)|\ell_{A_{n}}\rangle

The above decomposition can be obtained for any
1\leq k\leq n-1
. This implies that

\displaystyle u^{A_{1}}_{i_{1}\ell}d^{S_{i_{1},i_{n}}}_{\ell\ell}=u^{A_{2}}_{i_{1}\ell}d^{S_{i_{2},i_{n}}}_{\ell\ell}=\cdots=u^{A_{n-1}}_{i_{1}\ell}d^{S_{i_{n-1},i_{n}}}_{\ell\ell}=z^{S_{i_{n}}}_{\ell}

(21)

This implies that each of the indices in
z^{S_{i_{n}}}_{\ell}
can be factored. This must be applicable for each
d^{S_{i_{k},i_{n}}}_{\ell\ell}
. Therefore, we can write

\displaystyle z^{S_{i_{n}}}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\ell}u^{A_{1}}_{i_{1}\ell}u^{A_{2}}_{i_{2}\ell}\cdots u^{A_{n-1}}_{i_{n-1}\ell}

(22)

The above factors imply Equation
18
and hence provide the Schmidt decomposition of
|\psi\rangle
. This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

IV.1
Algorithm

Theorem
7
can be used to obtain Schmidt decomposition of a decomposable state efficiently. The matrix set is diagonalized by matrix pairs
(P_{i},Q_{n})
. The set of unitary matrices
\{P_{1},P_{2},...,P_{n-1},Q_{n}\}
obtained by considering matrices
A^{S_{i_{k},i_{n}}}
form the transformation
P_{1}\otimes P_{2}\otimes\cdots Q_{n}
of
|\psi\rangle
that gives the Schmidt basis. The coefficients
\lambda_{\ell}
can be obtained by solving a system of
\ell
linear equations using Equation
18
.

The correctness of the above algorithm follows from the correctness of Theorem
7
. Since the running time of each step in the algorithm is polynomial in the size of the input, the above algorithm terminates in polynomial time.

Classification based on Schmidt bases

This section appeared in arXiv pre-print
[
7
]
. As is noted in
[
8
]
, for a bipartite state, if
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}|i_{A}\rangle|i_{B}\rangle
is the Schmidt decomposition, then
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}(U|i_{A}\rangle)(V|i_{B}\rangle)
are Schmidt decomposition of
(U\otimes V)|\psi\rangle
. This observation extends to multipartite systems as well. Moreover, if two states
|\psi\rangle
and
|\phi\rangle
have the same Schmidt coefficients, then they must be unitary transforms of each other, i.e.
|\psi\rangle=(U\otimes V)|\phi\rangle
.

Theorem 8
.

Suppose
|\psi\rangle
and
|\phi\rangle
are two Schmidt decomposable multipartite states on the same Hilbert space. Then,
|\psi\rangle=(U_{1}\otimes U_{2}\otimes\cdots U_{n})|\phi\rangle
if and only if the Schmidt decompositions of
|\psi\rangle
and
|\phi\rangle
have the same Schmidt coefficients.

Proof.

If
|\psi\rangle=(U_{1}\otimes U_{2}\otimes\cdots U_{n})|\phi\rangle
, we can use the Schmidt decomposition of
|\phi\rangle
as

\displaystyle|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell_{A_{1}}\rangle|\ell_{A_{2}}\rangle\cdots|\ell_{A_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}(U_{A_{1}}|\ell_{A_{1}}\rangle)(U_{A_{2}}|\ell_{A_{2}}\rangle)\cdots(U_{A_{n}}|\ell_{A_{n}}\rangle)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell^{\psi}_{A_{1}}\rangle|\ell^{\psi}_{A_{2}}\rangle\cdots|\ell^{\psi}_{A_{n}}\rangle

As states
|\ell^{\psi}_{A_{k}}\rangle
are obtained via unitary transform of orthonormal states
|\ell_{A_{k}}\rangle
, they remain orthonormal and hence provide a valid Schmidt decomposition of
|\psi\rangle
. Therefore, both
\psi
and
\phi
have the same Schmidt coefficients.

For converse, assume
\psi
and
\phi
have the same Schmidt coefficients, i.e.

\displaystyle|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell_{A_{1}}\rangle|\ell_{A_{2}}\rangle\cdots|\ell_{A_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell^{\psi}_{A_{1}}\rangle|\ell^{\psi}_{A_{2}}\rangle\cdots|\ell^{\psi}_{A_{n}}\rangle

Since states
\{|\ell_{A_{k}}\rangle\}
and
\{|\ell^{\psi}_{A_{k}}\rangle\}
are orthonormal vectors, there exists unitary matrix
U_{A_{k}}
such that
|\ell^{\psi}_{A_{k}}\rangle=U_{A_{k}}|\ell_{A_{k}}\rangle
. This implies that

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell^{\psi}_{A_{1}}\rangle|\ell^{\psi}_{A_{2}}\rangle\cdots|\ell^{\psi}_{A_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}(U_{A_{1}}|\ell_{A_{1}}\rangle)(U_{A_{2}}|\ell_{A_{2}}\rangle)\cdots(U_{A_{n}}|\ell_{A_{n}}\rangle)

\displaystyle=(U_{1}\otimes U_{2}\otimes\cdots U_{n})\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell_{A_{1}}\rangle|\ell_{A_{2}}\rangle\cdots|\ell_{A_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle=(U_{1}\otimes U_{2}\otimes\cdots U_{n})|\phi\rangle

∎

Theorem
8
implies that if we define Schmidt decomposable states
|\psi\rangle
and
|\phi\rangle

equivalent
if
|\psi\rangle=(U_{1}\otimes U_{2}\otimes\cdots U_{n})|\phi\rangle
, then the Schmidt number is decided by the number of separable orthonormal basis states. We can consider Schmidt decomposition as establishing a bijection between orthonormal set of vectors of each component space.

Figure 1:
Schmidt decomposition establishes a bijection between orthonormal set of vectors of each subsystem. Equivalent states having the same Schmidt coefficients are generated using unitary operations on individual subsystems.

Observation 1
.

All two qubit Schmidt bases with Schmidt number
1
are equivalent to
|00\rangle
and Schmidt number
2
are equivalent to
\{|00\rangle,|11\rangle\}
.

In case of three qubit system, we can partition the system in two ways, (a) one subsystem with
1
qubit and another with
2
qubits, and (b) three subsystems with
1
qubit each. In both the cases, Schmidt rank
1
bases are
|000\rangle
. Schmidt rank
2
bases are
\{|000\rangle,|111\rangle\}
.

Observation 2
.

There are no Schmidt rank
3
states on a three qubit system (irrespective of partition).

Observation 3
.

The Schmidt number of an
n
qubit system is bounded by
2^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}
irrespective of partition. In fact, the highest Schmidt number is achieved for equal bipartition of the system.

Let us define the problem SCHMIDT-PARTITION as follows:

Input:

n
systems of with
d_{1},d_{2},...,d_{n}
qubits

Task:
partition the system into two parts such that there exists a state of Schmidt number
K

Theorem 9
.

SCHMIDT-PARTITION is
NP
-complete.

Proof.

To have a state of the Schmidt number
K
, the dimension of the smallest subsystem must be at least
K
. The dimension of combined system of two subsystems of dimensions
2^{d_{1}}
and
2^{d_{2}}
is
2^{d_{1}+d_{2}}
. Since all dimensions are positive numbers, we can take
\log
of the dimensions and ask whether there exists a bipartition of the system such that the sum of
d_{i}
for both parts is at least
\log(K)
each. This is equivalent to the
NP
-complete PARTITION problem in which the input is a set of positive numbers and the task is to partition the set into two subsets whose sum is at least
K
. It is important to note that not all instances of PARTITION are
NP
-complete. As the dimensions
2^{d_{i}}
can be arbitrarily large, even
d_{i}
can be large making the corresponding instance hard.
∎

The Theorem
9
immediately implies the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

Given
n
systems of
d_{1},d_{2},...,d_{n}
qubits, it is
NP
-complete to find a Schmidt decomposable state of highest Schmidt number.

Theorem 10
.

If
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{ij}a_{ij}|i_{A}\rangle|j_{B}\rangle
is a bipartite state where
|i_{A}\rangle
and
|j_{B}\rangle
for orthonormal bases of their respective spaces, then the reduced density matrix of any subsystem is given by

\displaystyle\rho^{A}

\displaystyle=AA^{\dagger}

(23)

\displaystyle\rho^{B}

\displaystyle=BB^{\dagger}=(A^{\dagger}A)^{T}

(24)

where
A
is the matrix of coefficients
a_{ij}
and
B=A^{T}
.

Proof.

The density matrix of the system is

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|

\displaystyle=\sum_{ijkl}a_{ij}a^{*}_{kl}|i_{A}j_{B}\rangle\langle k_{A}l_{B}|

Tracing out B gives

\displaystyle\rho_{A}

\displaystyle=\sum_{ijk}a_{ij}a^{*}_{kj}|i_{A}\rangle\langle k_{A}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{ik}\left(\sum_{j}a_{ij}a^{*}_{kj}\right)|i_{A}\rangle\langle k_{A}|

\displaystyle=AA^{\dagger}

Tracing out A gives

\displaystyle\rho_{B}

\displaystyle=\sum_{ijl}a_{ij}a^{*}_{il}|j_{B}\rangle\langle l_{B}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{jl}\left(\sum_{i}a^{*}_{il}a_{ij}\right)|j_{B}\rangle\langle l_{B}|

\displaystyle=A^{T}(A^{T})^{\dagger}=BB^{\dagger}

∎

Theorem
10
can be extended to multipartite states as well. We show this in case of tripartite states. Similar pattern can be used for higher partite states.

Theorem 11
.

If
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{ijk}a_{ijk}|i_{A}\rangle|j_{B}\rangle|k_{C}\rangle
is a tripartite state where
|i_{A}\rangle,|j_{B}\rangle
and
|k_{C}\rangle
for orthonormal bases of their respective spaces, then the reduced density matrices of one-level and two-level subsystems are given by

\displaystyle\rho^{A}

\displaystyle=AA^{\dagger}

(25)

\displaystyle\rho^{AB}

\displaystyle=BB^{\dagger}

(26)

where
B
is the matrix of coefficients
a_{ij,k}
of dimension
n_{A}n_{B}\times n_{C}
and
A
is the matrix of coefficients
a_{i,jk}
of dimension
n_{A}\times n_{B}n_{C}
.

Proof.

Let us calculate
\rho_{A}
first by tracing over
B
and
C
in the matrix

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|=\sum_{ijk,lmn}a_{ijk}a^{*}_{lmn}|i_{A}j_{B}k_{C}\rangle\langle l_{A}m_{B}n_{C}|

(27)

Tracing out subsystems
B
and
C
gives

\displaystyle\rho_{A}

\displaystyle=\sum_{ijkl}a_{ijk}a^{*}{ljk}|i_{A}\rangle\langle l_{A}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{il}\left(\sum_{jk}a_{ijk}a^{*}_{ljk}\right)|i_{A}\rangle\langle l_{A}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{il}\left(\sum_{jk}a_{i,jk}a^{\dagger}_{jk,l}\right)|i_{A}\rangle\langle l_{A}|

In the inner sum, we treat the indices
jk
as one giving us a matrix
A
of dimension
n_{A}\times n_{B}n_{C}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\rho_{A}=AA^{\dagger}

Next, tracing out
C
only gives

\displaystyle\rho_{AB}

\displaystyle=\sum_{ijk,lm}a_{ijk}a^{*}_{lmk}|i_{A}j_{B}\rangle\langle l_{A}m_{B}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij,lm}\left(\sum_{k}a_{ij,k}a^{*}_{lm,k}\right)|i_{A}j_{B}\rangle\langle l_{A}m_{B}|

In the inner sum, we define matrix
B
of elements
a_{ij,k}
of dimension
n_{A}n_{B}\times n_{C}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\rho_{AB}=BB^{\dagger}

Reduced density matrices of other subsystems can be obtained by relabeling the indices appropriately.
∎

It is easy to see that the Schmidt number of a bipartite state is equal to the rank of the reduced density matrix of one of the subsystems by using
rank(A)=rank(AA^{\dagger})=rank(A^{\dagger}A)=rank((A^{\dagger}A)^{T})
. This result holds for multipartite states as well.

Theorem 12
.

If
|\psi\rangle
is a Schmidt decomposable multipartite state, then the Schmidt number is equal to the rank of the reduced density matrix of one of the subsystems.

Proof.

We show here for tripartite states.
Consider the Schmidt decomposition of
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell_{A}\rangle|\ell_{B}\rangle|\ell_{C}\rangle
.

\displaystyle\rho^{ABC}

\displaystyle=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell,m}\lambda_{\ell}\lambda_{m}|\ell_{A}\rangle\langle m_{A}|\otimes|\ell_{B}\rangle\langle m_{b}|\otimes|\ell_{C}\rangle\langle m_{C}|

\displaystyle\rho^{AB}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}^{d}\lambda_{\ell}^{2}|\ell_{A}\ell_{B}\rangle\langle\ell_{A}\ell_{B}|

Consider the spectral decomposition of
\rho^{AB}
given as

\displaystyle\rho^{AB}=\sum_{i}^{r}p_{i}|i_{AB}\rangle\langle i_{AB}|

Since the states
\{|\ell_{A}\ell_{B}\rangle\}
and
\{|i_{AB}\rangle\}
are orthonormal sets, they can each be extended to orthonormal bases using Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization process. This implies that there is unitary matrix
U
that takes set of vectors
\{|\ell_{A}\ell_{B}\rangle\}
to
\{|i_{AB}\rangle\}
.

\displaystyle U\rho^{AB}U^{\dagger}=\sum_{i}^{r}p_{i}|\ell_{A}\ell_{B}\rangle\langle\ell_{A}\ell_{B}|

Since
\rho^{AB}
and
U\rho^{AB}U^{\dagger}
are similar matrices, they must have the same rank, i.e.
d=r
.
∎

In the book
[
8
]
(attributed to Thapliyal), for a bipartite state
|\psi\rangle=\alpha|\phi\rangle+\beta|\gamma\rangle
, the Schmidt number satisfy the inequality
Sch(\psi)\geq|Sch(\phi)-Sch(\gamma)|
. Tt is a simple consequence of Theorem
10
. We show that it is true for multipartite Schmidt decomposable states.

Theorem 13
.

Given three multipartite Schmidt decomposable states
|\psi\rangle,|\phi\rangle
and
|\gamma\rangle
such that
|\psi\rangle=\alpha|\phi\rangle+\beta|\gamma\rangle
, then

\displaystyle Sch(\psi)\geq|Sch(\phi)-Sch(\gamma)|

(28)

Proof.

We show it for tripartite state for readability.

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\alpha|\phi\rangle+\beta|\gamma\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{ijk}(\alpha a^{\phi}_{ijk}+\beta a^{\gamma}_{ijk})|i_{A}\rangle|j_{B}\rangle|k_{C}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{ijk}a_{ijk}|i_{A}\rangle|j_{B}\rangle|k_{C}\rangle

Using Theorem
11
, we get

\displaystyle\rho_{A}

\displaystyle=AA^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=(\alpha A_{\phi}+\beta A_{\gamma})(\alpha A_{\phi}+\beta A_{\gamma})^{\dagger}

This implies that

\displaystyle Sch(\psi)

\displaystyle=rank(\rho_{A})

\displaystyle=rank((\alpha A_{\phi}+\beta A_{\gamma})(\alpha A_{\phi}+\beta A_{\gamma})^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=rank(\alpha A_{\phi}+\beta A_{\gamma})

\displaystyle\geq|rank(\alpha A_{\phi})-rank(\beta A_{\gamma})|

\displaystyle=|rank(A_{\phi})-rank(A_{\gamma})|

\displaystyle=|Sch(\phi)-Sch(\gamma)|

∎

Observation 4
.

Linear combination of Schmidt decomposable multipartite states is not necessarily Schmidt decomposable. An an example, we can work with
|W\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(|001\rangle)+|010\rangle+|100\rangle
which is not Schmidt decomposable, but each of the terms are Schmidt decomposable.

In light of the above observation, we prove the following result for tensor product of Schmidt decomposable states.

Definition 15
.

An
n
-partite Schmidt decomposable state of Schmidt rank
k
is said to have Schmidt dimension
(n,k)
.

Theorem 14
.

Given two states
|\psi\rangle
and
|\phi\rangle
of Schmidt dimensions
(m,k_{\psi})
and
(n,k_{\phi})
such that
m\geq n
, then
|\psi\rangle|\phi\rangle
has dimension
(n,k_{\psi}k_{\phi})
and there are
{m-1\choose n-1}
ways to construct the
n
-partite systems.

Proof.

Starting with the Schmidt decompositions of
|\psi\rangle
and
|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}^{k_{\psi}}\lambda_{i}^{\psi}|i_{A_{1}}...i_{A_{m}}\rangle

\displaystyle|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}^{k_{\phi}}\lambda_{j}^{\phi}|j_{B_{1}}...i_{B_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}^{k_{\psi},k_{\phi}}\lambda_{i}^{\psi}\lambda_{j}^{\phi}|i_{A_{1}}...i_{A_{m}}j_{B_{1}}...j_{B_{n}}\rangle

Notice that the above expression for
|\psi\rangle|\phi\rangle
is not a Schmidt decomposition of
(m+n)
-partite system as we lose the bijection between states of each part. We partition
m
-parts of
|\psi\rangle
into
n
groups such that each group has at least 1 part. Let
x_{1},...,x_{n}
be the sizes of each groups. For each group, we link one part from
|\phi\rangle
as shown below:

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}^{k_{\psi},k_{\phi}}\lambda_{i}^{\psi}\lambda_{j}^{\phi}|i_{A_{1}}...i_{A_{x_{1}}}j_{B_{1}}\rangle|i_{A_{x_{1}+1}}...A_{x_{1}+x_{2}}j_{B_{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle...|i_{m-(x_{n})+1}...i_{m}j_{B_{n}}\rangle

It is easy to verify that states of each group are orthonormal and they don’t repeat in the sum, giving a valid Schmidt decomposition. Hence,
|\psi\rangle|\phi\rangle
can be partitioned into
n
parts such that the
n
-partite system has Schmidt decomposition of rank
k_{\psi}k_{\phi}
.

The number of such groups for fixed permutation of parts in
|\psi\rangle
and
|\phi\rangle
is equal to the number of solutions of

\displaystyle x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}

\displaystyle=m

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle\geq 1

which is given by
{m-1\choose n-1}
.
∎

VI
Purification

Given a density matrix
\rho
of a system
A
, the task of purification is to attach another subsystem
R
such that the combine system is in pure state
|AR\rangle
and the tracing out
R
gives back
\rho
. The standard trick
[
12
,
5
,
6
,
4
,
3
]
is to keep the dimension of
R
to be at least the rank of
\rho
and an embed orthonormal basis into
\rho
as follows starting with the spectral decomposition of
\rho
.

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}|i_{A}\rangle\langle i_{A}|

\displaystyle|AR\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|i_{A}\rangle|i_{R}\rangle

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\mathsf{Tr}_{R}(|Ar\rangle\langle Ar|)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}|i_{A}\rangle\langle i_{A}|

Essentially, we have used the Schmidt decomposition of
|AR\rangle
.

Regarding purification, one can ask some interesting questions like given a density matrix of a composite system, can it be purified to a Schmidt decomposable state? If a multipartite state is not Schmidt decomposable, can an additional system make it Schmidt decomposable?

Lemma 3
.

[
8
]

Two purifications
|AR_{1}\rangle
and
|AR_{2}\rangle
of a state
\rho
are unitarily linked as

\displaystyle|AR_{1}\rangle=(I\otimes U_{R})|AR_{2}\rangle

Proof.

The key idea is that two orthonormal sets are unitarily connected. Using the spectral decomposition of
\rho
, we have
\rho=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}|i_{A}\rangle\langle i_{A}|
.

\displaystyle|AR_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|i_{A}\rangle|i^{R}_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle|AR_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|i_{A}\rangle|i^{R}_{2}\rangle

Since the orthonormal sets
\{|i^{R}_{1}\rangle\}
and
{|i^{R}_{2}\rangle}
can be connected by unitary matrix
U
such that
|i^{R}_{1}\rangle=U|i^{R}_{2}\rangle
, we have

\displaystyle|AR_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|i_{A}\rangle|i^{R}_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|i_{A}\rangle U|i^{R}_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=(I\otimes U)\sum_{i}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|i_{A}\rangle|i^{R}_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=(I\otimes U)|AR_{2}\rangle

∎

Theorem 15
.

Every purification of a multipartite system
\rho
is either Schmidt decomposable or not Schmidt decomposable.

Proof.

The proof follows by combining Lemma
3
with Theorem
8
.
∎

VII
Conclusion

In this paper, we obtained a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of Schmidt decomposition of multipartite states. This condition is constructive and is used to obtain an efficient algorithm for obtaining the decomposition of a Schmidt decomposable state.

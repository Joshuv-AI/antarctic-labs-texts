1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

The classical Bohr theorem
[
17
]
was examined a century ago and has since generated a substantial volume of research activity, which is collectively referred to as the Bohr phenomenon. Let
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
denote the Banach algebra of all bounded linear operators acting on complex Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
with the norm

\displaystyle\|A\|=\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|Ah\|}{\|h\|}=\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H},\|h\|=1}\|Ah\|,\;\text{where}\;A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}).

Let
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},X)
denote the space of bounded analytic functions from the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
into a complex Banach space
X
with

\|f\|_{H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},X)}:=\sup_{|z|<1}\|f(z)\|
. Let
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D},X)
be the class of functions
f
in
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\|f\|_{H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},X)}\leq 1
. The Bohr radius
R(X)

[
15
]
for the class
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D},X)
is defined by

\displaystyle R(X):=\sup\left\{r\in(0,1):\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|a_{n}\|r^{n}\leq 1\;\text{for all}\;f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D},X),z\in\mathbb{D}\right\}.

H. Bohr
[
17
]
showed in a remarkable result (improved form) that if
X=\mathbb{C}
, then
R(X)=1/3
, where the norm of
X
is the usual modulus of complex numbers.
Dixon
[
21
]
brought back interest in Bohr’s theorem after utilizing it to answer a long-standing query concerning the characterization of Banach algebras satisfying the non-unital
von Neumann inequality. A substantial body of research has been conducted over the past two decades on a range of topics within complex analysis, including the extensions of
analytic functions of several complex variables, planar harmonic mappings, polynomials, solutions of elliptic partial differential equations, and other abstract settings. In 1997, Boas
and Khavinson
[
16
]
established the upper and lower bounds of the
n
-dimensional Bohr radius
K_{n}
for the Hardy space of bound analytic functions on the unit polydisk.
Further, the authors show that the Bohr radius
K_{n}
approaches zero as the domain’s dimension
n
increases to
\infty
.
In 2006, Defant and Frerick
[
19
]
derived an improved lower estimate for
K_{n}
. Another estimation of
K_{n}
was obtained by Defant
et al.

[
20
]
by employing the
hypercontractivity of the polynomial Bohnenblust-Hille inequality. In 2014, Bayart
et al.

[
12
]
derived the exact asymptotic behavior of
K_{n}
.
In 2019, Popescu
[
30
]
expanded the significance of the Bohr inequality for free holomorphic functions to polyballs.

2.
Bohr theorem for operator valued analytic function

The primary aim of this paper is to investigate Bohr’s inequalities for operator valued analytic functions defined on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, specifically for functions in
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
. Before we continue, there are some basic notations that need to be fixed. For
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
\|T\|
denotes the operator norm of
T
and the adjoint
operator
T^{*}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
of
T
is defined by
\langle Tx,y\rangle=\langle x,T^{*}y\rangle
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
. The operator
T
is said to be normal if
T^{*}T=TT^{*}
, self-adjoint if
T^{*}=T
and positive if
\langle Tx,x\rangle\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
. The absolute value of
T
is defined by
|T|:=(T^{*}T)^{1/2}
, while
S^{1/2}

denotes the unique positive square root of a positive operator
S
. Let
I
be the identity operator on
\mathcal{H}
. Let
f\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))

be a bounded analytic function with the expansion

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n}\;\;\text{for}\;z\in\mathbb{D},\text{where}\;A_{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\;\text{for all}\;n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

(2.1)

For operator valued bounded analytic functions in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, Popescu
[
30
]
proved the following notable result in 2019. This result is analogous to the classical Bohr theorem.

Theorem A
.

[
30
]

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
be an operator valued bounded analytic function with the expansion (
2.1
) such that
A_{0}=a_{0}I
,
a_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\|A_{n}\|r^{n}\leq\|f(z)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))}\;\text{for}\;|z|=r\leq 1/3

(2.2)

and
1/3
is the best possible constant. Moreover, the inequality is strict unless
f
is a constant.

If every function in
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
satisfies the inequality (
2.2
) for
r\leq 1/3
, then

\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
satisfies the Bohr phenomenon. It is important to note that the constant
1/3
is independent of the coefficient of the function.

In recent years, a prominent area of research in the field of one and several complex variables has been the examination of several variations of Bohr’s inequality in the context of
bounded analytic functions. Several improved and refined versions of Bohr’s inequality have been established by different authors (see
[
1
,
9
,
23
,
25
,
26
,
28
]
). We recall some of them here.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C})
be of the form

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\;\;\text{for}\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

(2.3)

In 2020, Ponnusamy
et al.

[
31
]
obtained the following refined Bohr inequality.

Theorem B
.

[
31
]

Let
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C})
of the form (
2.3
). Then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|a_{k}|r^{k}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{2}r^{2k}\leq 1\;\;\text{for}\;\;r\leq 1/(2+|a_{0}|).

The numbers
1/(2+|a_{0}|)
and
1/(1+|a_{0}|)
cannot be improved.

Note that
1/3\leq 1/(2+|a_{0}|)\leq 1/2
. It is evident that the sharp Bohr radius cannot be obtained by directly substituting
a_{0}
. The improved versions of Theorem B obtained by Ponnusamy
et al.

[
31
]
are presented below.

Theorem C
.

[
31
]

Let
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C})
of the form (
2.3
). Then

(a)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|r^{k}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{1}|}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{2}r^{2k-1}\leq 1
for
r\leq 3/5
. The number
3/5
is sharp.

(b)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|r^{k}+\left(\frac{r^{-1}}{1+|a_{1}|}+\frac{1}{1-r}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{2}r^{2k}\leq 1
for
r\leq(5-\sqrt{17})/2
. The number
(5-\sqrt{17})/2
is sharp.

3.
Operator valued analogues of multidimensional Bohr inequality

Unlike the study of the Bohr inequality for certain classes of analytic functions of one complex variable, several researchers investigate this topic with respect to
functions of n-complex variables. Before we proceed further, it is necessary to introduce some key concepts. Let
\alpha
be an
n
-tuple

(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
of non-negative integers,
|\alpha|
is the sum
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}
of its components,
\alpha!
is the product

\alpha_{1}!\alpha_{2}!\cdots\alpha_{n}!
,
z
denotes an
n
-tuple
(z_{1},z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})
of complex numbers and
z^{\alpha}
denotes the product
z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}z_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots z_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
.
Using the standard multi-index notation, we write an operator valued
n
-variable power series

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{\alpha}A_{\alpha}z^{\alpha},\;\;A_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}).

(3.1)

Let
\mathbb{D}^{n}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}),|z_{j}|<1,j=1,2,\ldots,n\}
be the open unit polydisk. Let
\mathcal{K}_{n}(\mathcal{H})
be the largest
non-negative number such that the
n
-variables power series (
3.1
) converges in
\mathbb{D}^{n}
and
\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))}\leq 1
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}\|A_{\alpha}\|\left|z^{\alpha}\right|\leq 1\;\text{for all}\;z\in\mathcal{K}_{n}(\mathcal{H})\cdot\mathbb{D}^{n}.

(3.2)

Definition 3.1
.

[
2
]
A domain
\mathcal{R}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is said to be Reinhardt domain centered at
0\in\mathcal{R}
if for any

z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathcal{R}
, we have that
(z_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}},z_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}},\ldots,z_{n}e^{i\theta_{n}})\in\mathcal{R}
for each
\theta_{k}\in[0,2\pi]

(1\leq k\leq n)
. We say that
\mathcal{R}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a complete Reinhardt domain if for each
z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathcal{R}
and for each

|u_{k}|\leq 1

(1\leq k\leq n)
, we have that
u.z=(u_{1}z_{1},u_{2}z_{2},\ldots,u_{n}z_{n})\in\mathcal{R}
.

Definition 3.2
.

[
2
]
A domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is said to be a circular domain centered at
0\in\Omega
if for any
z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\in\Omega
and for each
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
, we have that
e^{i\theta}z=(z_{1}e^{i\theta},z_{2}e^{i\theta},\ldots,z_{n}e^{i\theta})\in\Omega
. We say that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a complete
circular domain at
0\in\Omega
if for any
z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\in\Omega
and for each
|u|\leq 1
, we have that
u.z=(u_{1}z_{1},u_{2}z_{2},\ldots,u_{n}z_{n})\in\Omega
.

If
\Omega
is a complete circular domain centered at
0\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, then every holomorphic function
f
in
\Omega
can be expanded into homogeneous polynomials given by

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{k}(z)\;\text{for}\;z\in\Omega,

(3.3)

where
P_{k}(z)
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k
and
P_{0}(z)=f(0)
.

The following questions are expected in such a context.

Question 3.1
.

Is it possible to establish the analogous results of Theorem B for operator valued functions in
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
?
If so, what is the multidimensional analogue of Theorem B for functions in the class
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
?

Question 3.2
.

Is it possible to establish a refined version of Theorem B by employing the operator norm of
f
, i.e.,
\|f\|
for
f\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)
?

The primary purpose of this paper is to provide the positive answers to Questions
3.1
and
3.2
.

4.
key lemmas

The following are key lemmas of this paper and are used to prove the main results. We introduce some notations for
j=1,2
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{f}(r):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|A_{n}\|r^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+\|A_{1}\|}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\|A_{n}\|^{2}r^{2n-1},

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{j,f}(r):=\|f(z)\|^{j}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|A_{n}\|r^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+\|A_{1}\|}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\|A_{n}\|^{2}r^{2n-1},

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{f}(r):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|A_{n}\|r^{n}+\left(\frac{r^{-1}}{1+\|A_{1}\|}+\frac{1}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|A_{n}\|^{2}r^{2n}\;\;\text{and}

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{j,f}(r):=\|f(z)\|^{j}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|A_{n}\|r^{n}+\left(\frac{r^{-1}}{1+\|A_{1}\|}+\frac{1}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|A_{n}\|^{2}r^{2n}

(4.4)

for the function

Lemma 4.1
.

[
10
, counterpart of Schwarz-Pick inequality]
Let
B(z)
be an analytic function with values in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and satisfying
\|B(z)\|\leq 1
on
\mathbb{D}
. Then

\displaystyle(1-|a|)^{n-1}\left\|\frac{B^{(n)(a)}}{n!}\right\|\leq\frac{\|I-B(a)^{*}B(a)\|^{1/2}\|I-B(a)B(a)^{*}\|^{1/2}}{1-|a|^{2}}

for each
a\in\mathbb{D}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Note that for
f\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)
of the form
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n}
with
A_{0}=a_{0}I
,
|a_{0}|<1
. Without loss of generality, assume that
\|f\|_{H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))}\leq 1
. Then, by virtue of the Lemma
4.1
with
a=0
, we get

\displaystyle\|A_{n}\|\leq\|I-|A_{0}|^{2}\|=1-|a_{0}|^{2}\quad\text{for}\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.5)

Lemma 4.2
.

[
8
]
Let
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be an operator valued bounded holomorphic function with the expansion
f(z)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n}
such that
A_{k}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
A_{0}=a_{0}I
,
|a_{0}|<1
. If
\|f(z)\|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
, then for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we have

\displaystyle\|f(z)\|\leq\frac{\|f(0)\|+|z|}{1+\|f(0)\||z|}.

Lemma 4.3
.

[
18
]
[
31
, Lemma B]
Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C})
and
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
. Then the following inequalities hold.

|a_{2k+1}|\leq 1-|a_{0}|^{2}-\cdots-|a_{k}|^{2}
,
k=0,1,\ldots

|a_{2k}|\leq 1-|a_{0}|^{2}-\cdots-|a_{k-1}|^{2}-\frac{|a_{k}|^{2}}{1+|a_{0}|}
,
k=1,2,\ldots
.

To prove Lemma
4.4
, we need the following result.

Remark 4.1
.

We know that, if
T
is a bounded linear operator on a Hilbert space
X
, then the following conditions are equivalent:

T^{*}T=I
;

\langle Tx,Ty\rangle=\langle x,y\rangle\quad\text{for all}\quad x\quad\text{and}\quad y
;

\|Tx\|=\|x\|\quad\text{for all}\quad x
.

In order to prove our main results, we need to establish the analogous result of Lemma
4.3
for functions
f
belonging to the class
\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)
with the expansion
f(z)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
with
A_{0}=a_{0}I
,
|a_{0}|<1
such
that
A_{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Then the following inequalities hold.

\|A_{2k+1}\|\leq 1-\|A_{0}\|^{2}-\cdots-\|A_{k}\|^{2}
,
k=0,1,\ldots

\|A_{2k}\|\leq 1-\|A_{0}\|^{2}-\cdots-\|A_{k-1}\|^{2}-\frac{\|A_{k}\|^{2}}{1+\|A_{0}\|}
,
k=1,2,\ldots

Proof.

Since
f\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)
of the form
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n}
with
A_{0}=a_{0}I
,
|a_{0}|<1
. From (
4.5
), we have
\|A_{n}\|\leq 1-|a_{0}|^{2}<1
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Without loss of generality, let
A_{n}=a_{n}I
, where
a_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
with
|a_{n}|<1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Let us define a function
g(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|f(z)\|=\left\|I\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\right)\right\|=\left|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\right|=|g(z)|.

Since

, we have
|g(z)|\leq 1
and hence
g\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C})
. In view of Lemma
4.3
,
we get the desired inequalities.
∎

The following result provides an operator valued analogue of Theorem C (a).

Lemma 4.5

Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)

Proof.

\displaystyle\|B_{n}\|\leq\left\|I-|B_{0}|^{2}\right\|\quad\text{for }\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.6)

Let

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|A_{n}\|r^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|B_{n}\|r^{n+1}=r\left(\|B_{0}\|+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|B_{2n}\|r^{2n}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|B_{2n+1}\|r^{2n+1}\right).

4.4

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|B_{2n}\|r^{2n}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|B_{2n+1}\|r^{2n+1}

(4.8)

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle=\|B_{1}\|^{2}\left(\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}r^{k}\right)+\|B_{2}\|^{2}\left(\sum_{k=5}^{\infty}r^{k}\right)+\|B_{3}\|^{2}\left(\sum_{k=7}^{\infty}r^{k}\right)+\cdots

\displaystyle=\frac{\|B_{1}\|^{2}r^{3}}{1-r}+\frac{\|B_{2}\|^{2}r^{5}}{1-r}+\frac{\|B_{3}\|^{2}r^{7}}{1-r}+\cdots=\frac{r}{1-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|B_{j}\|^{2}r^{2j}.

(4.9)

From (
4.7
), (
4.8
) and (
4.9
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|A_{n}\|r^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle ra+(1-a^{2})\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}-\left(\frac{1}{1+a}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|B_{n}\|^{2}r^{2n+1}

(4.10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ra+(1-a^{2})\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}-\left(\frac{1}{1+a}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\|A_{n}\|^{2}r^{2n-1}.

As

, from (

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{f}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|A_{n}\|r^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+\|A_{1}\|}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\|A_{n}\|^{2}r^{2n-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle ar+\frac{r^{2}(1-a^{2})}{1-r}=G_{1}(a,r),

We claim that

\mathcal{H}_{2,f}(r)\leq 1
for
|z|=r\leq r_{2}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{2,f}(r)
is given in (
4.4
) and
r_{2}(\approx 0.393401)
is the unique positive root of the equation
2r^{3}-r^{2}-5r+2=0
in
(0,1)
. The radius
r_{2}
is the best possible.

Proof.

By applying analogous arguments to those presented in Lemma
4.9
and from (
4.16
), we can deduce that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{1,f}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f(z)\|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|A_{n}\|r^{n}+\left(\frac{r^{-1}}{1+\|A_{1}\|}+\frac{1}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|A_{n}\|^{2}r^{2n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{r(a+r)}{(1+ar)}\right)^{2}+ar+\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}+\frac{ra^{2}}{1+a}=\Phi_{4}(a,r).

Differentiating partially
\Phi_{4}(a,r)
with respect to
a
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\Phi_{4}(a,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r-\frac{a^{2}r}{(1+a)^{2}}+\frac{2ar}{1+a}-\frac{2r^{3}(a+r)^{2}}{(1+ar)^{3}}+\frac{2r^{2}(a+r)}{(1+ar)^{2}}

(4.19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r+\frac{ar}{(1+a)^{2}}(2+a)+\frac{2r^{2}(a+r)}{(1+ar)^{3}}\left(1-r^{2}\right)\geq 0.

This implies that
\Phi_{4}(a,r)
is an increasing function of
a
and this leads to the conclusion that

\displaystyle\Phi_{4}(a,r)\leq\Phi_{4}(1,r)=\frac{-2r^{3}+r^{2}+3r}{2(1-r)}\leq 1\quad\text{for}\quad r\leq r_{1},

where
r_{2}(\approx 0.393401)
is the unique positive root of the equation
2r^{3}-r^{2}-5r+2=0
between
0
and
1
.
In order to show that the constant
r_{2}
is sharp, we consider the function
\Psi_{a}(z)
given in (
4.12
).
By utilizing the function
\Psi_{a}(z)
, a straightforward computation demonstrates that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{2,\Psi_{a}}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\Psi_{a}(-r)\|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|A_{n}\|r^{n}+\left(\frac{r^{-1}}{1+\|A_{1}\|}+\frac{1}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|A_{n}\|^{2}r^{2n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{r(a+r)}{(1+ar)}\right)^{2}+ar+(1-a^{2})\frac{r^{2}}{1-ar}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{r^{-1}}{1+a}+\frac{1}{1-r}\right)\left(a^{2}r^{2}+\frac{(1-a^{2})^{2}r^{4}}{1-a^{2}r^{2}}\right)=\Phi_{5}(a,r).

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\lim_{a\to 1^{-}}\Phi_{5}(a,r)=\frac{-2r^{3}+r^{2}+3r}{2(1-r)}>1\;\text{for}\;r>r_{2}.

This demonstrates that the radius
r_{2}
represents the best possible value. This completes the proof.∎

Proof of
Theorem
5.1
.

In order to derive inequality (
5.1
), we first convert the multidimensional power series (
3.3
) into a power series of one complex variable and then apply Lemma
4.5
. In each section of the domain
\Omega
by a complex line

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}:=\{z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}):z_{j}=\alpha_{j}t,j=1,2,\ldots,n,\;t\in\mathbb{C}\},

(6.1)

the series (
3.3
) with
f(0)=0
turns into the power series in the complex variable
t
:

\displaystyle f(\alpha t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}P_{k}(\alpha)t^{k}.

(6.2)

Since
\|f(\alpha t)\|<1
for all
t\in\mathbb{D}
, so in view of Lemma
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|P_{k}(\alpha)t^{k}\right\|+\left(\frac{r^{-1}}{1+\|P_{1}(\alpha)t\|}+\frac{1}{1-r}\right)\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\left\|P_{k}(\alpha)t^{k}\right\|^{2}\leq 1

(6.3)

for
z
in the section
\mathcal{L}\cap\left(\frac{3}{5}\Omega\right)
and for
r\leq 3/5
. It should be noted that
\mathcal{L}
is an arbitrary complex line passing through the
origin. Thus, the inequality (
6.3
) is nothing but the desired inequality
\mathcal{T}_{f}(r)\leq 1
.

In order to demonstrate the sharpness of the constant
3/5
, let us consider the domain
\Omega
to be convex. Then
\Omega
is an intersection of half spaces

\displaystyle\Omega=\bigcap_{\alpha\in\mathcal{J}}\left\{z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}):\text{Re}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}z_{j}\right)<1\right\}\;\;\text{for some}\;\mathcal{J}.

Since
\Omega
is a circular domain, so we have

\displaystyle\Omega=\bigcap_{\alpha\in\mathcal{J}}\left\{z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}):\left|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}z_{j}\right|<1\right\}\;\;\text{for some}\;\mathcal{J}.

Consequently, in order to demonstrate that the constant
3/5
represents the best possible value, it is sufficient to illustrate that no improvement can be made to
3/5
in each domain
\Omega_{\alpha}=\left\{z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}):\left|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}z_{j}\right|<1\right\}

, there exists an analytic function

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(\eta)=\frac{\eta(3/5-\eta)}{1-3\eta/5}I=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}A_{k}\eta^{k}\;\;\text{for}\;\eta\in\mathbb{D},

where

and

for

such that

in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
with

fails to be hold in the disk

. We now consider the function

∎

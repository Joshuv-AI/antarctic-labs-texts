Fast energy decay for damped wave equations with a potential and rotational inertia terms Thanks: ikehatar@hiroshima-u.ac.jp
Ruy Coimbra Charão

Abstract
We consider damped wave equations with a potential and rotational inertia terms. We study the Cauchy problem for this model in the one dimensional Euclidean space 𝐑 {\bf R} and we obtain fast energy decay and L 2 L^{2} -decay of the solution itself as t → ∞ t\to\infty . Since we are considering this problem in the one dimensional space, we have no useful tools such as the Hardy and/or Poincaré inequalities. This causes significant difficulties to derive the decay property of the solution and the energy. A potential term will play a role for compensating these weak points.

Before starting our main result, we shall mention the assumption on the potential
V(x)
, which can be admitted in our argument.

On the potential
V(x)
, we assume that

(V.1)

V\in{\rm BC}^{2}({\bf R})
,
V(x)>0
and
|V^{\prime}(x)|\leq\alpha V(x)
(
\forall x\in{\bf R}
) with some constant
\alpha>0
.

Example.
One can show a typical example satisfying
(V.1)
:
V(x):=V_{0}(1+x^{2})^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}
(
\alpha>0
and
V_{0}>0
). However, one can not choose an exponential type such as
V(x)=e^{-x^{2}}
. It imposes some limits on the degree of decay in the spatial distance.

Our purpose is to find a fast decay rate of the total energy. Several experiences in analysing plate-type equations with rotational inertia terms is documented in
[
3
,
11
]
, but careful consideration is needed here to compensate for the critical weakness of not being able to use Hardy-type inequalities. Our result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1

Assume
(V.1)
and
\alpha^{2}\|V\|_{\infty}<1
. Let
u_{0}\in H^{3}({\bf R})
and
u_{1}\in H^{3}({\bf R})
satisfy

\frac{u_{0}+u_{1}-\Delta u_{1}}{\sqrt{V(\cdot)}}\in L^{2}({\bf R}).

Then, the strong solution
u(t,x)
to problem (
1.1
)-(
2.1
) satisfies

E_{u}(t)\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\left\|\dfrac{u_{0}+u_{1}-\Delta u_{1}}{\sqrt{V(\cdot)}}\right\|^{2})(1+t)^{-2},\quad(t\geq 0),

\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\left\|\dfrac{u_{0}+u_{1}-\Delta u_{1}}{\sqrt{V(\cdot)}}\right\|^{2})(1+t)^{-1},\quad(t\geq 0),

where
C=C(\alpha,\|V\|_{\infty},\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty},\|V^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty})>0
is a constant depending only on the quantities
\alpha
,
\|V\|_{\infty}
,
\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
and
\|V^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}
.

The proof of this theorem will be done in the next sections.

Remark 1.1

It should be pointed out that even when the initial value is smooth enough, e.g.,
(u_{0},u_{1})\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\bf R})\times C_{0}^{\infty}({\bf R})
, the result of the theorem is new enough. In this case the assumption
\frac{u_{0}+u_{1}-\Delta u_{1}}{\sqrt{V(\cdot)}}\in L^{2}({\bf R})
automatically holds.

Remark 1.2

Comparing the results of Theorem
1.1
above and
[
23
, Theorem 4.1]
, both the energy and the
L^{2}
norm of the solution derive faster decay rates in Theorem
1.1
. This is still an effect of the addition of the potential term
V(x)u
. Here we are limited to one spatial dimension, but a slightly different method would be needed to generalize this to a more general dimension.

Example.
For the concrete example
V(x):=V_{0}(1+x^{2})^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}
, since
\|V\|_{\infty}=V_{0}
, the condition
\alpha^{2}\|V\|_{\infty}<1
implies
\alpha^{2}V_{0}<1
. That is, if we choose large
\alpha\gg 1
, then we must choose small
V_{0}
, and vice versa. Incidentally, the large
\alpha>0
corresponds to the so-called short range potential.

Remark 1.3

To get faster decay like
O(t^{-2})
of the total energy one must assume further regularity on the initial data and the potential. This may reflect an essential effect from the rotational inertia term
-\Delta u_{tt}
. This can be seen by comparing it to the case of the damped wave equation with potential (see
[
7
, Theorem 1.3]
). In
[
7
, Theorem 1.3]
, the same decay order as in Theorem
1.1
above can be derived under weaker regularity assumption
(u_{0},u_{1})\in H^{1}\times L^{2}
to the one dimensional equation:

u_{tt}-\Delta u+V(x)u+u_{t}=0.

Remark 1.4

Although Theorem
1.1
definitely yields a fast decay rate, at this stage it is a future issue to be addressed as to the best decay rate. An interesting recent paper
[
15
]
may be of interest in this regard.

Main Estimates

We first prepare important lemma to proceed our argument in this paper. A main idea comes from
[
7
, Lemma 3.4]
.

Lemma 2.1

Let
V\in{\rm BC}^{1}({\bf R})
satisfies
V(x)>0
for
x\in{\bf R}
and
[u_{0},u_{1}]\in H^{2}({\bf R})\times H^{2}({\bf R})
satisfying

\frac{u_{0}+u_{1}-\Delta u_{1}}{\sqrt{V(\cdot)}}\in L^{2}({\bf R}).

Then the strong solution
u\in C^{2}([0,\infty);H^{2}({\bf R}))
to problem (
1.1
)-(
2.1
)
satisfies

\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds\leq J_{0}^{2}\quad(t\geq 0),

where

J^{2}_{0}:=\dfrac{1}{2}\big(\|u_{0}\|^{2}+\|\nabla u_{0}\|^{2}\big)+\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|\dfrac{u_{0}+u_{1}-\Delta u_{1}}{\sqrt{V(\cdot)}}\right\|^{2}.

Proof:

Let us define
\displaystyle w(t,x):=\int_{0}^{t}u(s,x)\,ds
. Then
w_{t}(t,x)=u(t,x)
and
w(0)=0
,
w_{t}(0)=u_{0}
and the function
w(t,x)
satisfies

\displaystyle w_{tt}-\Delta w_{tt}-\Delta w+V(x)w+w_{t}=u_{0}+u_{1}-\Delta u_{1},\ \ \ (t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\bf R},

(2.1)

\displaystyle w(0,x)=0,\quad w_{t}(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\ \ \ x\in{\bf R}.

Multiplying both sides of the equation in (
2.1
) by
w_{t}
, and integrating over
[0,t]\times{\bf R}
one has

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\|w_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\|\nabla w_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\|\nabla w(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\|\sqrt{V(x)}w(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\right)+\int_{0}^{t}\|w_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\|u_{0}\|^{2}+\|\nabla u_{0}\|^{2}\right)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\bf{R}}\left[u_{0}+u_{1}-\Delta u_{1}\right]w_{s}(s,x)\,dx\,ds

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\|u_{0}\|^{2}+\|\nabla u_{0}\|^{2}\right)+\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{d}{ds}\left(u_{0}+u_{1}-\Delta u_{1},\;w(s,\cdot)\right)_{L^{2}}ds

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\|u_{0}\|^{2}+\|\nabla u_{0}\|^{2}\right)+\left(u_{0}+u_{1}-\Delta u_{1},\;w(t,\cdot)\right)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\|u_{0}\|^{2}+\|\nabla u_{0}\|^{2}\right)+\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|\dfrac{u_{0}+u_{1}-\Delta u_{1}}{\sqrt{V(x)}}\right\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{V(x)}w(t,\cdot)\|^{2}.

The above identity implies the following inequality

\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\|w_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\|w_{xt}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\|w_{x}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\right)+\int_{0}^{t}\|w_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\,ds

\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\|u_{0}\|^{2}+\|\nabla u_{0}\|^{2}\right)+\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|\dfrac{u_{0}+u_{1}-\Delta u_{1}}{\sqrt{V(x)}}\right\|^{2}=J^{2}_{0},

which proves Lemma
2.1
because of
w_{t}=u
.

\square

Next we prove the following intermediate result concerning the decay of the total energy. Conditions
|V^{\prime}(x)|\leq\alpha V(x)
and
\alpha^{2}\|V\|_{\infty}<1
, as in Theorem
1.1
, are unnecessary here just to obtain slower decay
E_{u}(t)=O(t^{-1})
(
t\to\infty

Proposition 2.1

Assume that
V\in{\rm BC}^{1}({\bf R})
satisfies
V(x)>0
for
x\in{\bf R}
, and let
[u_{0},u_{1}]\in H^{2}({\bf R})\times H^{2}({\bf R})
satisfying

\frac{u_{0}+u_{1}-\Delta u_{1}}{\sqrt{V(\cdot)}}\in L^{2}({\bf R}).

Then the strong solution
u\in C^{2}([0,\infty);H^{2}({\bf R}))
to problem (
1.1
)-(
2.1
)
satisfies the following asymptotic behavior

E_{u}(t)\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\dfrac{u_{0}+u_{1}-\Delta u_{1}}{\sqrt{V(\cdot)}}\right\|^{2})(1+t)^{-1},\quad(t\geq 0),

where
C=C(\|V\|_{\infty},\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty})>0
is a constant depending only on the quantities
\|V\|_{\infty}
and
\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
.

Proof:
To prove the proposition, we first note that it is standard to see the following basic estimate:

(1+t)E_{u}(t)\leq E_{u}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds

+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds

\leq E_{u}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds

+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\|V\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds\quad(t\geq 0).

(2.2)

Next, by multiplying both sides of (
1.1
) by
u(t,x)
and integrating over
{\bf R}
it follows that

\frac{d}{dt}(u_{t}(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot))+\|\nabla u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\frac{d}{dt}(\nabla u_{t}(t,\cdot),\nabla u(t,\cdot))+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}

=\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\|\nabla u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}.

By integrating it on
[0,t]
, one has

(u_{t}(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot))+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+(\nabla u_{t}(t,\cdot),\nabla u(t,\cdot))+\frac{1}{2}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}

=I_{0}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds,

(2.3)

where

I_{0}:=\left((u_{0},u_{1})+(\nabla u_{0},\nabla u_{1})+\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

While, multiplying both sides of (
1.1
) by
-\Delta u_{t}
and integrating over
{\bf R}\times[0,t]
it follows that

\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}

+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)u(s,x)(-\Delta u_{s}(s,x))dxds=I_{1}^{2},

(2.4)

where

I_{1}:=\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta u_{1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta u_{0}\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(2.5)

Note that

\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)u(s,x)(-\Delta u_{s}(s,x))dx=\int_{{\bf R}}u(s,x)\nabla u_{t}(s,x)\cdot\nabla V(x)dx+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{ds}\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|\nabla u(s,x)|^{2}dx.

Then integration over
[0,\;t]
implies that

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)u(s,x)(-\Delta u_{s}(s,x))dxds

=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\bf R}}u(s,x)\nabla u_{s}(s,x)\cdot\nabla V(x)dxds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}dx.

By substituting this equality into (
2.4
), one can get the identity

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds

(2.6)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\bf R}}u(s,x)\nabla u_{s}(s,x)\cdot\nabla V(x)dxds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}dx=I_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}dx.

Here, we need to use the estimate

\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\bf R}}u(s,x)\nabla u_{s}(s,x)\cdot\nabla V(x)dxds\right|\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\bf R}}|u(s,x)||\nabla u_{s}(s,x)||V^{\prime}(x)|dxds

\leq\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\bf R}}|u(s,x)||\nabla u_{s}(s,x)|dxds

\leq\varepsilon\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+C_{\varepsilon}\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds

(2.7)

with some constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
which depends only on
\varepsilon>0
. Thus, from (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) it follows that

\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+(1-\varepsilon\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty})\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds

+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}dx\leq I_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}dx+C_{\varepsilon}\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds.

(2.8)

On the other hand, it follows from (
2.3
) that

\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\frac{1}{2}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}

\leq I_{0}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\frac{1}{2}\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u(t,\cdot)\|^{2},

which implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds

(2.9)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle I_{0}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\frac{1}{2}\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}.

Thus, for small
\varepsilon>0
, it follows from Lemma
2.1
, (
2.8
), (
2.9
) and (
1.3
) that

(1-\varepsilon\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty})\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds

\leq I_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}dx+C_{\varepsilon}\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}J_{0}^{2}=:K_{0}^{2},

(2.10)

and so

\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds\leq I_{0}^{2}+E_{u}(0)+\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}}\left(I_{1}^{2}+\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}\nabla u_{0}\|^{2}\right)+\frac{C_{\varepsilon}\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}}{1-\varepsilon\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}}J_{0}^{2}=:K_{1}^{2}.

(2.11)

Finally, based on the estimates just obtained in Lemma
2.1
, (
1.3
), (
2.2
), (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) the statement of Proposition
2.1
can be easily obtained. Indeed, one can get

(1+t)E_{u}(t)\leq 2E_{u}(0)+\|V\|_{\infty}J_{0}^{2}+\frac{K_{0}^{2}}{1-\varepsilon\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}}K_{1}^{2}=:C_{1}^{2}.

(2.12)

Remark 2.1

The case of
\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}=0
(that is
V(x)=V_{0}>0
for all
x\in{\bf R}
) is more easy to be treated, so it suffices to assume
\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\neq 0
when checking the proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Remark 2.2

From (
2.10
) in the proof of Proposition
2.1
the following estimate holds,

\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds\leq\frac{K_{0}^{2}}{(1-\varepsilon\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty})}=:K_{2}^{2}.

(2.13)

3
Fast Decay Estimates

The idea to prove Theorem
1.1
comes from the identity

\frac{d}{dt}\big[(1+t)^{2}E(t)\big]=2(1+t)E(t)+(1+t)^{2}\frac{d}{dt}E(t),\;\;t>0,

which implies that

\displaystyle(1+t)^{2}E(t)\leq E(0)+2\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)E(s)ds,\;\;t>0,

(3.1)

because
E(t)
is a non-increasing function of
t
.

Thus, to get the proof of faster decay of the total energy in Theorem
1.1
it is necessary to estimate the integral in the right hand side of (
3.1
). That is,
from the definition of
E(t)
one has to deal with the following inequality,

(1+t)^{2}E_{u}(t)\leq E_{u}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds

+\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds.

(3.2)

The first estimate one has to prove is the following.

Lemma 3.1

The strong solution satisfies the inequality

\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds\leq\;K_{3}^{2}

(3.3)

where for some
0<\epsilon<1
determined in (
2.12
):

K_{3}=\Big(2E(0)+\|V\|_{\infty}J_{0}^{2}+\frac{K_{0}^{2}}{1-\varepsilon\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}}K_{1}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

Proof:

By multiplying both sides of the equation (
1.1
) by
u_{t}
it results that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\Big(||u_{t}(t,\cdot)||^{2}+||\nabla u_{t}(t,\cdot)||^{2}+||\nabla u(t,\cdot)||^{2}+||\sqrt{V(x)}u(t,\cdot)||^{2}\Big)+||u_{t}(t,\cdot)||^{2}=0,\;\;t>0.

Now, multiplication this identity by
(1+t)
implies that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\Big[(1+t)\big(||u_{t}(t,\cdot)||^{2}+||\nabla u_{t}(t,\cdot)||^{2}+||u_{x}(t,\cdot)||^{2}+||\sqrt{V(x)}u(t,\cdot)||^{2}\big)\Big]+(1+t)||u_{t}(t,\cdot)||^{2}

{\bf=}\frac{1}{2}\Big(||u_{t}(t,\cdot)||^{2}+||\nabla u_{t}(t,\cdot)||^{2}+||u_{x}(t,\cdot)||^{2}+||\sqrt{V(x)}u(t,\cdot)||^{2}\Big),\;\;t>0.

Integration the above identity over
[0,\;t]
one gets for
t>0

\frac{1}{2}\Big[(1+t)\big(||u_{t}(t,\cdot)||^{2}+||\nabla u_{t}(t,\cdot)||^{2}+||\nabla u(t,\cdot)||^{2}+||\sqrt{V(x)}u(t,\cdot)||^{2}\big)\Big]+\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)||u_{s}(s,\cdot)||^{2}ds

=E(0)+\int_{0}^{t}E(s)ds.

At this point, by re-examining the proof of Proposition
2.1
we can see that it was already proven that

\int_{0}^{t}E(s)ds\leq E(0)+\|V\|_{\infty}J_{0}^{2}+\frac{K_{0}^{2}}{1-\varepsilon\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}}K_{1}^{2},\;\;t>0.

This estimate implies the proof of Lemma
3.1
.

\square

To obtain the next estimate, the following identity can be derived by multiplying both sides of the equation (
1.1
) by
(1+t)u
, integrating over
[0,t]\times{\bf R}
, and by the integration by parts, similarly to the proof of Proposition
2.1
:

\displaystyle(1+t)(u_{t}(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot))-(u_{1},u_{0})=\frac{1}{2}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds-\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds-(1+t)(\nabla u_{t}(t,\cdot),\nabla u(t,\cdot))

\displaystyle+(\nabla u_{1},\nabla u_{0})+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{0}\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(1+t)\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds.

(3.4)

By clean up this identity one has

\frac{1}{2}(1+t)\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds

=K_{0}-(1+t)(u_{t}(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot))+\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds-(1+t)(\nabla u_{t}(t,\cdot),\nabla u(t,\cdot))

+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\frac{1}{2}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2},

(3.5)

where

K_{0}:=(\nabla u_{1},\nabla u_{0})-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{0}\|^{2}+(u_{1},u_{0}).

Since

-(1+t)(u_{t}(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot))\leq(1+t)\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\frac{1+t}{4}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2},

and

-(1+t)(\nabla u_{t}(t,\cdot),\nabla u(t,\cdot))\leq\frac{1+t}{2}\|\nabla u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\frac{1+t}{2}\|\nabla u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}

\leq(1+t)E_{u}(t)\leq C_{1}^{2}\quad(t\geq 0)

with some constant
C_{1}>0
(see (
2.12
)), it follows from (
3.5
) that

\frac{1}{4}(1+t)\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds

\leq K_{0}+3C_{1}^{2}+K(t)+\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds,

(3.6)

where

K(t):=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds.

(3.7)

Here we denote the second energy by

2E^{*}(t):=\|\nabla u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\|\Delta u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\|\Delta u(t,\cdot)\|^{2},\;\;t\geq 0.

Then, we have

\frac{d}{dt}E^{*}(t)+\big(V(\cdot)u(t,\cdot),-\Delta u_{t}(t,\cdot)\big)+\|\nabla u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}=0.

Here, by integration by parts one has

(V(\cdot)u(t,\cdot),-\Delta u_{t}(t,\cdot))=\int_{{\bf R}}u(t,x)\nabla V(x)\cdot\nabla u_{t}(t,x)dx+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}dx,

so that we get

\frac{d}{dt}E^{*}(t)+\int_{{\bf R}}u(t,x)\nabla V(x)\cdot\nabla u_{t}(t,x)dx+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}dx+\|\nabla u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}=0.

Thus, it follows that

(1+t)E^{*}(t)+\frac{1+t}{2}\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds

=E^{*}(0)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{t}E^{*}(s)ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|\nabla u(s,x)|^{2}dxds-F(t),

(3.8)

where

F(t):=\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\int_{{\bf R}}u(s,x)(\nabla u_{s}(s,x)\cdot\nabla V(x))dxds.

Let us estimate the function
F(t)
by using the assumption on
V(x)
such that

|V^{\prime}(x)|\leq\alpha V(x)

with some
\alpha>0
. Indeed,

|F(t)|\leq\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\int_{{\bf R}}|u(s,x)|\;|\nabla u_{s}(s,x)|\;|V^{\prime}(x)|dxds

\leq\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\int_{{\bf R}}|u(s,x)||\nabla u_{s}(s,x)|\alpha V(x)dxds

\leq\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)^{2}|u(s,x)|^{2}dxds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\int_{{\bf R}}|\nabla u_{s}(s,x)|^{2}dxds

\leq\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\|V\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|u(s,x)|^{2}dxds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\int_{{\bf R}}|\nabla u_{s}(s,x)|^{2}dxds,

which implies from (
3.8
) that

(1+t)E^{*}(t)+\frac{1+t}{2}\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds

\leq E^{*}(0)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\bf R}V(x)|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{t}E^{*}(s)ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|\nabla u(s,x)|^{2}dxds

+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\|V\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|u(s,x)|^{2}dxds.

(3.9)

From (
3.9
) one has

\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds\leq 2E^{*}(0)+\int_{\bf R}V(x)|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}dx+2\int_{0}^{t}E^{*}(s)ds

+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|\nabla u(s,x)|^{2}dxds+\alpha^{2}\|V\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|u(s,x)|^{2}dxds.

(3.10)

By combining (
3.6
) and (
3.10
) one can arrive at the meaningful bound:

\frac{1}{4}(1+t)\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+(1-\alpha^{2}\|V\|_{\infty})\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds

\leq K_{0}+3C_{1}^{2}+K(t)+2E^{*}(0)+\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}dx+2\int_{0}^{t}E^{*}(s)ds

+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|\nabla u(s,x)|^{2}dxds+\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds.

(3.11)

Thus, applying the estimates (
2.11
) and (
3.3
) one has

\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+(1-\alpha^{2}\|V\|_{\infty})\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds

\leq K_{0}+3C_{1}^{2}+K(t)+2E^{*}(0)+\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}dx+2\int_{0}^{t}E^{*}(s)ds

+\,\,||V||_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\bf R}}|\nabla u(s,x)|^{2}dxds+\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds

\leq K_{0}+3C_{1}^{2}+K(t)+2E^{*}(0)+\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}dx+2\int_{0}^{t}E^{*}(s)ds+||V||_{\infty}K_{1}^{2}+K_{3}^{2}.

(3.12)

Because of Lemma
2.1
, the definition of
K(t)
in (
3.7
) implies that

K(t)\leq E(0)+\frac{1}{2}J_{0}^{2}=:J_{1}^{2}.

Moreover, it is standard that
E^{*}(0)
is bounded in terms of the norms of
u_{0}
and
u_{1}
in
H^{2}(\mbox{\boldmath$R$})
.

Considering these two above estimates in (
3.12
) one has arrived at the following results, which will help one to estimate the integrals on the right hand side of (
3.2
).

Lemma 3.2

Under the assumption
\alpha^{2}\|V\|_{\infty}<1
, it holds that

\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds\leq C^{*}\Big(E_{0}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}E^{*}(s)ds\Big),

where

Moreover,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds\leq E_{0}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}E^{*}(s)ds.

The next steps to complete the estimates in the above two lemmas is to obtain an upper bound to the integral

(3.13)

Note that the estimate to the first integral on the hight hand side of (

Lemma 3.3

The

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\leq I_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}dx+C_{\varepsilon}\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}J_{0}^{2}=:J_{2}^{2},

(3.14)

is defined in (

).

). In fact, from that inequality one has for some constant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\leq

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle I_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}dx+C_{\varepsilon}\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}J_{0}^{2},

(3.16)

by using the estimate in Lemma

.

In the next lemma one needs the assumption on the

Lemma 3.4

Let
u(t,x)
be the regular solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
2.1
). Then,

\dfrac{1}{2}\|\Delta u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\,ds\leq L^{2}_{0},\quad t>0,

where

L_{0}^{2}=:E_{2}(0)+C_{\delta}\|V^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\,J^{2}_{0}+2C_{\delta}\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\,E(0)+\|V\|_{\infty}\,J_{2}^{2}=L_{0}^{2},

and

2E_{2}(0):=\Big[\|\Delta u_{1}\|^{2}+\|\Delta(u_{1})_{x}\|^{2}+\|\Delta(u_{0})_{x}\|^{2}\Big].

Proof:

Using the non-standard multiplier
\Delta^{2}u_{t}
for the wave equation, one has

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\left[\dfrac{d}{dt}\|\Delta u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\dfrac{d}{dt}\|\Delta u_{tx}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\dfrac{d}{dt}\|\Delta u_{x}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\right]+\|\Delta u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\Bigl(\Delta\bigr(V(x)u(t,\cdot)\bigr),\;\Delta u_{t}(t,\cdot)\Bigr)_{L^{2}},\;\;t>0.

Integration over
[0,\;t]
,
t>0
, implies that for some
\delta>0

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\Big[\|\Delta u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\|\Delta u_{tx}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\|\Delta u_{x}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\Big]+\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\quad=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\Big[\|\Delta u_{1}\|^{2}+\|\Delta(u_{1})_{x}\|^{2}+\|\Delta(u_{0})_{x}\|^{2}\Big]-\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl(\Delta\big(V(x)u(s,\cdot)\big),\;\Delta u_{s}(s,\cdot)\Bigl)_{L^{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle E_{2}(0)-\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl(V^{\prime\prime}(x)u(s,\cdot)+2V^{\prime}(x)\nabla u(s,\cdot)+V(x)\Delta u(s,\cdot),\;\Delta u_{s}(s,\cdot)\Bigr)_{L^{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle\quad=

\displaystyle E_{2}(0)-\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl(V^{\prime\prime}(x)u(s,\cdot),\;\Delta u_{s}(s,\cdot)\Bigr)_{L^{2}}\,ds-\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl(2V^{\prime}(x)\nabla u(s,\cdot),\;\Delta u_{s}(s,\cdot)\Bigr)_{L^{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl(V(x)\Delta u(s,\cdot),\;\Delta u_{s}(s,\cdot)\Bigr)_{L^{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle E_{2}(0)+C_{\delta}\|V^{\prime\prime}\|^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\,ds+\delta\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\,ds+2C_{\delta}\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+

\displaystyle\delta\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\,ds-\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl(\sqrt{V(x)}\Delta u(s,\cdot),\;\sqrt{V(x)}\Delta u_{s}(s,\cdot)\Bigr)\,ds,

is a constant depending only on

\|u(t)\|
and
\|u\|
, etc., will be used in place of
\|u(t,\cdot)\|
in the following. Then, taking in the last estimate

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\Big[\|\Delta u_{t}\|^{2}+\|\Delta u_{tx}\|^{2}+\|\Delta u_{x}\|^{2}\Big]+(1-2\delta)\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta u_{s}\|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq E_{2}(0)+C_{\delta}\|V^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\|u\|^{2}\,ds+2C_{\delta}\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u\|^{2}\,ds-\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{d}{ds}\Big(\left\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}\Delta u(s)\right\|\Big)\,ds

\displaystyle=E_{2}(0)+C_{\delta}\|V^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\|u\|^{2}\,ds+2C_{\delta}\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u\|^{2}\,ds-\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}\Delta u\right\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}\Delta u_{0}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq E_{2}(0)+C_{\delta}\|V^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\,J^{2}_{0}+2C_{\delta}\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\,K_{1}^{2}-\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}\Delta u\right\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}\Delta u_{0}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq E_{2}(0)+C_{\delta}\|V^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\,J^{2}_{0}+2C_{\delta}\|V^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\,K_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}\Delta u_{0}\|^{2}=:L_{0}^{2},

due to Lemma
2.1
and estimate (
2.11

Finally one has to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5

The regular solution
u(t,x)
of the problem (

)–(
2.1

\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\,ds\leq L^{2}_{1}

are defined in this and previous sections,

Multiplying both sides of (

) by

\displaystyle\left(\nabla u_{t},\;\nabla u\right)+\left(\Delta u_{t},\;\Delta u\right)-\int_{0}^{t}\Big[\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}+\|\Delta u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\Big]\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta u\|^{2}\,ds+\dfrac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{0}\|^{2}+\left(\nabla u_{1},\;\nabla u_{0}\right)+\left(\Delta u_{1},\;\Delta u_{0}\right)+\int_{0}^{t}\left(V(x)u,\;\Delta u\right)\,ds.

Then, rearranging the terms of above identity and using (

), one has

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta u\|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle=\big(\nabla u_{1},\;\nabla u_{0}\big)+\big(\Delta u_{1},\;\Delta u_{0}\big)+\dfrac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{0}\|^{2}-\left(\nabla u_{t},\;\nabla u\right)-\left(\Delta u_{t},\;\Delta u\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\Big[\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}+\|\Delta u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\Big]\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\big(V(x)u,\Delta u\big)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{t}\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|\Delta u_{t}\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|\Delta u\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\,ds+\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|V(x)u\|^{2}\,ds+\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta u\|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}+E(0)+\dfrac{1}{2}\|\Delta u_{t}\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|\Delta u\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\,ds+\dfrac{1}{2}\|V||_{\infty}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|u\|^{2}\,ds+\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta u\|^{2}\,ds.

This inequality implies the following estimate for
t>0

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta u\|^{2}\,ds\leq C_{0}+E(0)+\dfrac{1}{2}\|\Delta u_{t}\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|\Delta u\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\,ds+\dfrac{1}{2}\|V||_{\infty}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|u\|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}+E(0)+L_{0}^{2}+K_{2}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|V||_{\infty}^{2}J_{0}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|\Delta u\|^{2}.

(3.17)

by Lemma
3.4
, estimate (
2.13
) and Lemma
2.1
.

To get the upper bound estimate to
\|\Delta u(t)\|^{2}
we use the inequality in (
2.4
). In fact, from that inequality we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\leq I_{1}^{2}-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\bf R}}V(x)u(s,x)(-\Delta u_{s}(s,x))dxds

\displaystyle\leq I_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|V||_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|^{2}\;ds+\frac{1}{2}\|V\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\;\|\Delta u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq I_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|V\|_{\infty}\big(J_{0}^{2}+2L_{0}^{2}\big),

(3.18)

by Lemmas
2.1
and
3.4
, where the constant
I_{1}
depending only on the initial data is again given by

I_{1}:=\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta u_{1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta u_{0}\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Now, combining estimates (
3.17
) and (
3.18
) the proof of Lemma
3.5
follows
with

\displaystyle L_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=C_{0}+E(0)+L_{0}^{2}+K_{2}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|V||_{\infty}^{2}J_{0}^{2}+I_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|V\|_{\infty}\big(J_{0}^{2}+2L_{0}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=C_{0}+E(0)+K_{2}^{2}+I_{1}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|V||_{\infty}^{2}\big(2J_{0}^{2}+2L_{0}^{2}\big)+L_{0}^{2}.

.

\square

Let us finalize the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
completed.

Now, using the estimates in Remark 2.2 and Lemmas
3.4
and
3.5
we get the important estimate

\int_{0}^{t}E^{*}(s)ds\leq K_{2}^{2}+L_{0}^{2}+L_{1}^{2}=:C_{2}^{2},

(3.19)

and from Lemma

the following estimates

\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds\leq C^{*}\Big(E_{0}^{2}+2C_{2}^{2}\Big)

(3.20)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds\leq E_{0}^{2}+2C_{2}^{2}.

(3.21)

can be obtained under the assumption that

.

To get the fast decay for the energy, using the estimate (

) in Lemma

) and (

(3.22)

4
Appendix

In this appendix we discuss the existence of regular solution and its regularity issue to problem (
1.1
)-(
2.1
). We apply the Lumer-Phillips theorem via semi-group theory. For generalization, we treat the problem (
1.1
)-(
2.1
) in the
n
-dimensional space
{\bf R}^{n}
.

Define the operator
{\cal A}:D({\cal A})={\cal H}\to{\cal H}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\
-J(I+A)&0\end{pmatrix},~~~

where
A:=-\Delta
with
D(A)=H^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
with the identity operator
I
in
L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
, and we set
J:=(I+A)^{-1}
,
{\cal H}:=H^{2}({\bf R}^{n})\times H^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
. Note that
J(I+A)u=u
for
u\in D(A)
.

Setting

\displaystyle U(t)=\begin{pmatrix}u(t)\\
v(t)\end{pmatrix},~~~U_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{0}\\
u_{1}\end{pmatrix},~~~

the problem (
1.1
)-(
2.1
) can be converted into the following Cauchy one in
{\cal H}
:

U_{t}(t)={\cal A}U(t)+L_{V}U(t)+FU(t),t>0,

(4.1)

and

U(0)=U_{0}.

(4.2)

Here, we have just defined two operators
L_{V}:{\cal H}\to{\cal H}
and
F:{\cal H}\to{\cal H}
as follows:

L_{V}\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}:=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
JV(\cdot)u\end{pmatrix},\quad F\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}:=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
-J(v-u)\end{pmatrix}

for each
\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}\in{\cal H}
. For this expression it should be noticed the following equivalent expression to the equation (
1.1
):

u_{tt}+J(I+A)u+JV(x)u+J(u_{t}-u)=0.

Note that the mapping
L_{V}
is well-defined because
V(\cdot)u\in L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
for
u\in H^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
by the assumption
V\in L^{\infty}({\bf R}^{n})
.

Let us check the dissipativity of the operator
{\cal A}
. In fact, one can see that for each
\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}\in{\cal H}
,

<{\cal A}U,U>_{{\cal H}}=<\begin{pmatrix}v\\
-u\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}>_{{\cal H}}=(v,u)_{H^{2}}-(u,v)_{H^{2}}=0,

where
<U,V>_{{\cal H}}
and
(u,v)_{H^{2}}
mean the standard inner product in
{\cal H}
and
H^{2}
spaces respectively.

Next, we can check the surjectivity of the mapping
{\cal I}-{\cal A}:D({\cal A})\to{\cal H}
, where
{\cal I}
is the identity on
{\cal H}
, that is, we should find a vector
U:=\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}\in D({\cal A})
for each
G:=\begin{pmatrix}f\\
g\end{pmatrix}\in{\cal H}
such that

U-{\cal A}U=G.

But, this is quite easy because of the property
J(I+A)u=u
for
u\in D(A)
. We shall omit the details. Therefore, the operator
{\cal A}:D({\cal A})={\cal H}\to{\cal H}
is m-dissipative. By the Lumer-Phillips Theorem it is a generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup of contractions on
{\cal H}
(see
[
12
, Section 1.4, Theorem 4.3]
). Furthermore, by a perturbation theory of the
C_{0}
-semigroup (see
[
12
, Section 3.1, Theorem 1.1]
) the operator
{\cal A}+L_{V}+F
is a generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup. Note that the operator
L_{V}+F
is bounded in
{\cal H}
, and this fact can be easily checked as follows. Indeed, we first note that the
H^{2}
-norm is equivalent to the graph norm of the Laplacian
A
, that is,
\|u\|_{H^{2}}\sim\|u\|+\|Au\|
for each
u\in H^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
. Furthermore, we use two facts about the Yosida approximation such that

AJw=w-Jw,\quad\|Jw\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|w\|_{L^{2}}

for
w\in L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
. Then, for
U:=\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}\in{\cal H}
, we see that

\|L_{V}U\|_{{\cal H}}=\|JV(\cdot)u\|_{H^{2}}\sim\|JV(\cdot)u\|_{L^{2}}+\|AJV(\cdot)u\|_{L^{2}}

=\|JV(\cdot)u\|_{L^{2}}+\|V(\cdot)u-JV(\cdot)u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left(\|JV(\cdot)u\|_{L^{2}}+\|V(\cdot)u\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\leq C^{\prime}\|V(\cdot)u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C^{\prime}\|V\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C^{\prime}\|V\|_{L^{\infty}}\|U\|_{{\cal H}},

which implies the boundedness of the operator
L_{V}
in
{\cal H}
. Similarly, one can easily check the boundedness of the operator
F
in
{\cal H}
. Thus, for each initial data
U_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{0}\\
u_{1}\end{pmatrix}\in{\cal H}=H^{2}({\bf R}^{n})\times H^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
,
there exists a unique solution
U(t)=\begin{pmatrix}u(t)\\
v(t)\end{pmatrix}\in C([0,\infty);D({\cal A}))\cap C^{1}([0,\infty);{\cal H})=C^{1}([0,\infty);{\cal H})
to problem (
4.1
)-(
4.2
). This implies
v=u_{t}
and
u\in C^{2}([0,\infty);H^{2}({\bf R}^{n}))
for the solution
u(t,x)
of the problem (
1.1
)-(
2.1
).

Remark 4.1

By a similar consideration to above discussion, if we rechoose
{\cal H}:=H^{4}({\bf R}^{4})\times H^{4}({\bf R}^{4})=D({\cal A})
, one can obtain
u\in C^{2}([0,\infty);H^{4}({\bf R}^{n}))
for the solution
u(t,x)
of the problem (
1.1
)-(
2.1
), which is sufficiently useful in our discussion.

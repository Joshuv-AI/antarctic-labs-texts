Spinorial Superspaces and Super Yang-Mills Theories
Johannes Moerland

Abstract
In physics literature about supersymmetry, many authors refer to super Minkowski spaces . These spaces are affine supermanifolds with certain distinguished spin structures. In these notes, we make the notion of such spin structures precise and generalise the setup to curved supermanifolds. This leads to the more general notion of spinorial superspaces . By working in a suitable geometric and coordinate-free setting, many explicit coordinate computations appearing in physics literature can be replaced by more conceptual methods. As an application of the rather general framework of spinorial superspaces, we formulate 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 super Yang-Mills theories on curved superspaces of spacetime dimensions d = 3 d=3 and d = 4 d=4 and show how to reduce the theory to field theories defined on an underlying ordinary spacetime manifold.



1
Introduction

Modern differential geometry allows for an inherently coordinate-free approach to physical field theories. The theory of smooth manifolds and principal bundles is a suitable framework to define gauge theories such as Yang-Mills theories. In this language, symmetries such as gauge invariance are often manifest in the theories of interest. The upshot of this approach is that lengthy and cumbersome coordinate computations can often be replaced by more conceptual algebraic computations, commutative diagrams and exact sequences. This often allows for a rather pleasant geometric picture.

To extend this geometric approach to super field theories, a generalisation of the category of smooth manifolds is desirable. In sight of the anticommutativity properties of fermionic particles, the ‘even’ (ordinary) directions should be complemented by ‘odd’ (nilpotent) directions which are described by anti-commuting (Grassmannian) coordinate functions. By making this notion precise in an algebro-geometric picture, one arrives at the category of
supermanifolds
. In this work, we make use of the language initially suggested by Leites
[
Lei80
]
as well as functorial geometric extension as introduced by Kessler
[
Keß18
]
. Further comprehensive and introductory texts are presented in
[
Del+99
,
Var04
]
.

While the theory of supermanifolds is well-established, it remains a highly non-trivial task to cast
super field theories in this language. There are several reasons for this: Firstly, when physicists
speak about superspaces, more structure than the mere notion of a supermanifold is usually implied.
In particular, a certain spin structure plays an important role. Secondly, the computational steps
giving validity to super field theories (for example, coordinate independence) are often carried out in
long computations involving a plethora of indices, and are hence difficult to keep track of. Thirdly,
we would like to mention that many constructions on Minkowski space do not generalise canonically,
if at all) to curved supermanifolds.

In this document, we make the notion of the aforementioned extra structure on superspaces precise, leading us to the notion of spinorial superspaces. We discuss emergent operators and maps such as a canonical connection and partial pseudo-Riemannian metrics. We show how to reduce field theories defined on superspaces to ones defined on ordinary spacetimes. Furthermore, we explicitly construct spinorial superspaces in terms of ordinary smooth manifolds. Finally, we showcase super Yang-Mills theories in
d=3
and
d=4
spacetime dimensions as theories defined on spinorial superspaces.

This document is structured as follows: in section
2
, we give a geometric and mathematically precise definition of superspaces.

In section
3
, we define certain distinguished spin structures which globalise the notion of the super translation algebra, leading us to the definition of spinorial superspaces. We analyse the implications of such spin structures by providing a description of the space of connections and listing invariant morphisms on the tangent bundle.

Depending on the spacetime dimension and signature, the spinorial part of the complexified tangent sheaf of the superspace decomposes into chiral and antichiral components. This is discussed in section
4
.

In section
5
, we show that any embedded purely even manifold, thought of as ordinary spacetime, is canonically equipped with a plethora of structures such as a pseudo-Riemannian metric and a spinor bundle.

We then construct spinorial superspaces by means of ordinary Riemannian spin manifolds in section
6
.

As an application of the theory, we formulate super Yang-Mills theories in spacetime dimensions
3
and
4
in Lorentzian signature in section
7
.

2
Superspaces

Physical field theories are often expressed explicitly in terms of coordinates. While this approach allows for simple comparison of theoretical and experimental data, it lacks a certain intuition. What gives the theories geometric significance is that they are invariant under change of coordinates and/or gauge transformations.

Differential geometry and the theory of principal
G
-bundles admit an inherently invariant description of field theories on manifolds. This bears two major advantages: Firstly, upon phrasing the theories accordingly in this framework, one dodges the infamous ‘battle of indices’ which is rather prone to miscalculations and sign errors. Secondly, the invariant formulation in terms of manifolds and principal bundles generalises physical field theories – which are often defined explicitly in terms of coordinates on Minkowski space – straight-forwardly to topologically non-trivial spaces.

In sight of the required anticommutativity properties of fermionic particles, it is thus sensible to extend the theory of manifolds to that of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded manifolds, better known as
supermanifolds
. In what follows (up to principle
1
) we summarise the essential notions in the theory of super differential geometry (or in short,
supergeometry
) following Kessler
[
Keß18
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
supermanifold of dimension
m|n
is a locally ringed space
M=(\underline{M},\mathcal{O}_{M})
, where each
x\in\underline{M}
has an open neighbourhood
U
such that the restriction of the sheaf of sections
\mathcal{O}_{M}
to
U
is isomorphic to

\mathcal{O}_{M}(U)\cong C^{\infty}(\hat{U})\otimes\wedge(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\vee}

(1)

as local
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded ring. Here,
\hat{U}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
is an open subset.

Note that this definition characterises the supermanifold
M
by its sheaf of sections over the topological space
\underline{M}
. Roughly speaking, this is necessary as the odd coordinate functions are nilpotent, and therefore, they cannot correspond to directions supported on some topological space.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
M,N
be supermanifolds. A
morphism of supermanifolds

f:M\to N
is a tuple
f=(\underline{f},f^{*})
, where
\underline{f}:\underline{M}\to\underline{N}
is a continuous map, and the map of sheaves

preserves parity and the maximal ideals of the stalks. Together with this notion of morphisms, supermanifolds constitute a category, denoted
\mathbf{sMfd}
.

Note that the definition implies that the morphisms are purely even. To allow for a mixture of odd and even directions on the supermanifold, we use the language of Ref.
[
Keß18
]
, where the author introduces families of supermanifolds:

Definition 2.3
.

A
family of supermanifolds
is a surjective submersion of supermanifolds
M\to A
. We say that
M
has
relative dimension

\dim(M)-\dim(A)
.

Note that since we defined supermanifolds as locally ringed spaces, they technically do not possess isolated geometrical points: In fact, we can only look at the stalks, which capture infinitesimal neighbourhoods of points. However, upon considering families of supermanifolds
M\to A
, we can consider
A
-points
of
M
, which are sections
A\to M
of the submersion. By viewing all supermanifolds over
A
in this way, we can use the odd directions of
A
to mix even and odd components of
M
and
N
in morphisms
f:M\to N
. In other words, we use the auxiliary base space
A
to parametrise the supermanifold
M
.

Definition 2.4
.

We shall denote by
\mathbf{sMfd}_{A}
the category of supermanifolds over
A
. The homset is given by morphisms of supermanifolds
M\to N
that preserve the base
A
.

We can replace the base by pulling back the submersion
\sigma:M\to A
along a surjective submersion of supermanifolds
\varphi:A\to\tilde{A}
, giving rise to a covariant functor

\mathbf{sMfd}_{A}\to\mathbf{sMfd}_{\tilde{A}},\quad(M,\sigma,A)\mapsto(M,\sigma\circ\varphi,\tilde{A}).

(2)

Principle 1

Henceforth, we shall implicitly view each supermanifold over a sufficiently large base

Let us now elaborate on the second point of this definition. To that end, recall that once we specify a distribution

3
Spin Structures

In what follows, we develop the notion of
spinorial superspaces
. The latter intertwine the spinor algebra with the properties of the distribution
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{T}_{M}
, thus allowing for a pleasant geometric interpretation of fermionic particles.

Throughout, we shall denote by
(V,q)
the quadratic vector space that infinitesimally models the ordinary spacetime manifold, and by

a real spinorial representation of
\Spin(V,q)
.
Any irreducible representation
S_{0}
has a
type

\mathbb{F}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H}\}

K^{\mathbb{F}}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}:|x|^{2}=1\}
is (isomorphic to) the
\Spin(V,q)
-invariant automorphisms of
S_{0}

(V,q)
is Minkowskian. The following theorems provide existence and uniqueness results of the
\Gamma

Definition 3.1

Let

Lemma 2
.

The bosonic distribution is integrable along any purely even submanifold
\iota:\check{M}\to M
whenever
\underline{\check{M}}=\underline{M}
and
\underline{\iota}=\id_{\underline{M}}
.

Proof.

Note first that
\mathrm{d}\iota:T\check{M}\to\iota^{*}TM
corestricts to an isomorphism

T\check{M}\cong\iota^{*}B\cong B_{\iota(\check{M})}.

(31)

For integrability, it suffices to show that the Frobenius obstruction
f_{B}\coloneqq\pr_{F}([-,-]|_{\mathcal{B}\wedge\mathcal{B}})
vanishes. To that end, note that the obstruction presented in this way is a graded skew
\mathcal{O}_{M}
-bilinear map
\mathcal{B}\wedge\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{F}

corresponds fibrewise to a bilinear

-equivariant map

. Then,
-1\in\Spin(V,q)
is in the kernel of the action on

, whereas it changes the sign on
S
– a contradiction. Hence, the Frobenius obstruction vanishes.
∎

The above lemma will be crucial when we identify ordinary spacetime manifolds within superspaces.

The Chirality Decomposition

We shall now consider the scenario where under complexification, the spinorial distribution decomposes into chiral and antichiral components, i.e.,

\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathcal{F}^{1,0}\oplus\mathcal{F}^{0,1},

(52)

where the distributions
\mathcal{F}^{1,0}
and
\mathcal{F}^{0,1}
correspond to the two holomorphic structures on the complexified spin representation
S^{\mathbb{C}}=S^{1,0}\oplus S^{0,1}
. We make use of the language presented in appendix
A
.

By using the approach via the bundle of frames, the nature of this decomposition can be expressed algebraically and occurs if and only if the underlying spin representation is of complex type. In this case, the structure group of
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{C}}
is
\Spin^{\mathbb{C}}(V,q)
and embeds canonically into
\GL_{\mathbb{C}}(S^{\mathbb{C}})
. Since the complex nature of the CS manifold
(M,\mathcal{O}_{M}\otimes\mathbb{C})
only enters along the odd directions (and is therefore to be regarded as a completely algebraic property of the structure sheaf), the almost complex structure can be viewed as a complex structure along
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{C}}
. This means that
\mathcal{F}^{1,0}
and
\mathcal{F}^{0,1}
can be interpreted as holomorphic and antiholomorphic distributions, respectively.

We have the Dolbeault-like multigrading decomposition of the de Rham complex

\Omega^{p|q,r}(M)=\wedge^{p}(\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}})^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{M}}\wedge^{q}(\mathcal{F}^{1,0})^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{M}}\wedge^{r}(\mathcal{F}^{0,1})^{\vee}.

(53)

It follows that the differential on functions decomposes as

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}:\mathcal{O}_{M}\otimes\mathbb{C}\to\Omega^{1}(M,\mathbb{C})=(\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}})^{\vee}\oplus(\mathcal{F}^{1,0})^{\vee}\oplus(\mathcal{F}^{0,1})^{\vee},

(54)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}=\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}}\oplus\partial\oplus\bar{\partial}.

(55)

Due to the non-integrability conditions on
\mathcal{F}
, we do not try to further decompose the exterior differential on forms into holomorphic and antiholomorphic parts. Rather, we consider a
spinorial connection
\varphi^{s}\in\mathcal{A}_{\hat{Q}}
. Since
\varphi^{s}
is a
\Spin^{\mathbb{C}}(V,q)
connection, and
S^{\mathbb{C}}=S^{1,0}\oplus S^{0,1}
as
\Spin^{\mathbb{C}}(V,q)
representations,
such a spinorial connection preserves the bosonic resp. spinorial distributions. The corresponding covariant exterior derivative thus decomposes globally as

\mathrm{d}^{\varphi^{s}}=\mathrm{d}^{\varphi^{s}}_{B}\oplus\partial^{\varphi^{s}}\oplus\bar{\partial}^{\varphi^{s}}

(56)

on fibre bundles over
M
arising from the decomposition
\mathcal{T}_{M}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}\oplus\mathcal{F}^{0,1}\oplus\mathcal{F}^{0,1}
. By this notation, we mean that

\partial^{\varphi^{s}}:\Omega^{\bullet}(M,F^{1,0})\to\Omega^{\bullet+1}(M,F^{1,0}),

(57)

and likewise for the other components.
The suggestive notation is chosen due to the analogy with the Dolbeault operators on complex manifolds.

Definition 4.1
.

We define the sheaf of
chiral functions

\mathcal{O}^{\omega}_{M}\coloneqq\{f\in\mathcal{O}_{M}\otimes\mathbb{C}:Xf=0\textrm{ for all }X\in\Omega^{0}(M,\mathcal{F}^{0,1})\},

(58)

and the sheaf of

\mathcal{O}^{\bar{\omega}}_{M}\coloneqq\{f\in\mathcal{O}_{M}\otimes\mathbb{C}:Xf=0\textrm{ for all }X\in\Omega^{0}(M,\mathcal{F}^{1,0})\}.

(59)

Lemma 7
.

Upon complexification of the structure sheaf,
M
gives rise to the two CS submanifolds
M^{1|0}=(\underline{M},\mathcal{O}_{M}^{\omega})
and
M^{0|1}=(\underline{M},\mathcal{O}_{M}^{\bar{\omega}})

Proof.

It is clear that both
M^{1|0}
and

constitute locally ringed spaces. We prove now that
M^{1|0}
is a CS manifold (the case of
M^{0|1}
is analogous). By lemma
17
, it suffices to check that the distribution
\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}\oplus\mathcal{F}^{1,0}
is integrable. By Frobenius’ theorem, this amounts to verifying that the Frobenius pairing vanishes, that is, the
\mathcal{F}^{0,1}
components of the commutator are zero. As in the discussion of Equation (

•

Suppose

is non-zero for some

•

A similar reasoning holds for the contribution from

•

By theorem

Thus, the Frobenius obstruction vanishes, such that lemma

Upon pulling back the soldering form component
\theta^{\mathcal{F}}
by
i

\mathcal{F}|_{\check{M}}
with
\Omega^{0}(\check{M},S\check{M})
is given explicitly by

\Omega^{0}(\check{M},S\check{M})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Omega^{0}(i^{*}Q,\Pi S)^{\Spin(V,q)}\xrightarrow{\langle i^{*}\theta^{\mathcal{F}},\cdot\rangle}i^{*}\mathcal{F}.

(65)

∎

Finally, we have the following relations between the spinorial connection on
M
and the spin connection on
i^{*}Q^{F}\to\check{M}

Lemma 11
.

Let
\varphi^{s}\in\mathcal{A}^{s}_{Q}
be a spinorial connection. The pullback connection
i^{*}\varphi^{s}\in\mathcal{A}_{i^{*}Q}
is a
\Spin^{\mathbb{F}}(V,q)
-connection on
i^{*}Q=\check{Q}
(and thus in particular on the spinor bundle
S\check{M}
); and the pullback
i^{*}\left((\varphi^{s})^{\mathcal{B}}\right)\in\mathcal{A}_{i^{*}Q^{B}}
of the
Q^{B}
-part of
\varphi^{s}
is the Levi-Civita connection on
(\check{M},g|_{\check{M}})
.

Proof.

It is clear that the pullback of the connection is a connection on the pullback bundle. Note that on spinorial connections, the
2|0
-component of the
V

\Theta^{\varphi}
vanishes. Upon pulling back to
\check{M}
, this is the only part that remains – hence,
i^{*}\left((\varphi^{s})^{\mathcal{B}}\right)
is torsion free and metric, and thus, the Levi-Civita connection.
∎

In this section, we first construct a class of spinorial superspaces that arise from spinor bundles over ordinary Riemannian spin manifolds, and then give relevant formulas for computing integrals on these superspaces.

Definition 6.2
.

The

on a vector space
S
is the image
\mathcal{E}
of the identity map
\id_{S}
under the canonical identification of

\{s^{i}\}
, such that

\mathcal{E}_{S}=\sum_{i}s^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial s^{i}}.

(83)

\GL(S)

\mathcal{E}_{S}
is invariant under linear transformations. We can thus define such vector fields on fibres of associated vector bundles as follows:

Definition 6.3
.

The Euler vector field
\mathcal{E}

\Pi S\check{M}

\mathcal{E}_{S}

\delta_{\mathcal{F}}:\tau^{*}\Pi S\check{M}\to T\Pi S\check{M},\quad X\mapsto X+\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{\varphi}_{\Gamma(\mathcal{E},X)},

where
\Gamma:\tau^{*}\Pi S\check{M}\otimes\tau^{*}\Pi S\check{M}\to\tau^{*}T\check{M}

\mathcal{F}\coloneqq\Omega^{0}\left(\Pi S\check{M},\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\tau^{*}\Pi S\check{M})\right)\to\mathcal{T}_{\Pi S\check{M}}.

(86)

.

\mathcal{F}
is a spinorial distribution on
M=\Pi S\check{M}
, and turns
M
into a spinorial superspace.

Proof.

We need to show that upon restricting to the body, the commutator coincides with the
\Gamma
map of representations. To that end, we compute

\left.[\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(X),\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(Y)]\right|_{\check{M}}=\left.[X+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\Gamma(\mathcal{E},X)}^{\varphi},Y+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\Gamma(\mathcal{E},Y)}^{\varphi}]\right|_{\check{M}}

(87)

for
X,Y\in\Omega^{0}(\Pi S\check{M},\tau^{*}\Pi S\check{M})
.
We lift the vertical vector fields
X,Y
to
\Spin(V,q)
-invariant vector fields on
Q=\check{Q}\times\Pi S
according to the diagram in equation (

).
We may equip
S
with a basis
\{f_{\mu}\}
, inducing coordinate functions
\{s^{\mu}\}
, and it follows that

f_{\mu}=\frac{\partial}{\partial s^{\mu}}\eqqcolon\partial_{\mu}

(88)

is a coordinate frame.
Note that
\Spin(V,q)
acts linearly on
\Pi S
, such that coordinate frames are taken to coordinate frames under the
\Spin(V,q)
action.
We first evaluate equation (
87
) on constituents of the coordinate frame.
The expression for the commutator simplifies as coordinate vector fields commute; hence,

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\Gamma(\mathcal{E},\partial_{\mu})}^{\varphi},\partial_{\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\Gamma(\mathcal{E},\partial_{\nu})}^{\varphi}\right]=\frac{1}{2}[\partial_{\mu},\nabla_{\Gamma(\mathcal{E},\partial_{\nu})}^{\varphi}]+\frac{1}{2}[\nabla_{\Gamma(\mathcal{E},\partial_{\mu})}^{\varphi},\partial_{\nu}]+\frac{1}{4}[\nabla_{\Gamma(\mathcal{E},\partial_{\mu})}^{\varphi},\nabla_{\Gamma(\mathcal{E},\partial_{\nu})}^{\varphi}].

(89)

Recall that using coordinate frames on
\Pi S
, the Euler vector field can be written as

\mathcal{E}=\sum_{\lambda}s^{\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}.

(90)

Now, we can evaluate the individual terms of equation (
89
). Note that upon restricting to
\check{M}
, we are only interested in terms that are non-vanishing along the inclusion. In particular, the coordinates
s^{\mu}
vanish, such that

\displaystyle[\partial_{\mu},\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\Gamma(\mathcal{E},\partial_{\nu})}^{\varphi}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\lambda}[\partial_{\mu},s^{\lambda}\nabla_{\Gamma(\partial_{\lambda},\partial_{\nu})}^{\varphi}]

(91)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\Gamma(\partial_{\mu},\partial_{\nu})}\mod\Pi S.

(92)

Upon restricting to the body, it follows

[\partial_{\mu},\nabla_{\Gamma(\mathcal{E},\partial_{\nu})}^{\varphi}]|_{\check{M}}=(\psi_{S}^{*}\Gamma)(\partial_{\mu}|_{\check{M}},\partial_{\nu}|_{\check{M}})

(93)

as after restriction, the structure sheaf is reduced to ordinary functions on
\check{M}
, where the covariant derivative reduces to the ordinary differential.
The term involving
[\nabla_{\Gamma(\mathcal{E},\partial_{\mu})}^{\varphi},\nabla_{\Gamma(\mathcal{E},\partial_{\nu})}^{\varphi}]
vanishes along the body as it has coefficients that are linear in
\Pi S
.
Putting everything together, we find

\left.[\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{\mu}),\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{\nu})]\right|_{\check{M}}=(\psi_{S}^{*}\Gamma)(\partial_{\mu}|_{\check{M}},\partial_{\nu}|_{\check{M}}),

(94)

proving the claim.

Remark
.

Due to Batchelor’s theorem
[
Bat84
]
, any
trivial
family of supermanifolds, i.e., a supermanifold over a base
A
with the submersion given by
M\times A\xrightarrow{\pr_{A}}A
, is non-canonically isomorphic to a split one. It can be shown that in this case, all spinorial superspaces are non-canonically isomorphic to split ones.

An analogous statement might hold true in the category of supermanifolds that only contain
submersed
bases
A
by replacing the topological space
\underline{M}
with an underlying ordinary spacetime manifold as discussed in the preceding section. However, such a ‘relative version’ of Batchelor’s theorem remains to be worked out.

As a concrete example, let us apply the preceding notions to an affine space.

Example 3

(Affine Superspace)
.

Let
(\check{M},\check{g})
be the affine space modelled by
(V,q)
. In this case, the preceding construction becomes particularly simple: Note that we have a canonical trivialisation of the tangent bundle

T\check{M}\cong\check{M}\times V\xrightarrow{\pr}\check{M},

(96)

inducing the trivialisation of the bundle of orthogonal frames

\Fr^{\SO(V,q)}(T\check{M})\cong\check{M}\times\SO(V,q)\xrightarrow{\pr}M.

(97)

The lift
\Spin(V,q)\to\SO(V,q)
induces the corresponding spin structure
\check{Q}\to\check{M}
. We choose a spinorial representation
S
of
\Spin(V,q)
, and obtain the supermanifold

M=\check{M}\times\Pi S.

(98)

The functions on
M
are all of the form

f=\sum_{I}f_{I}\xi^{I}\in\mathcal{O}_{M},\quad f_{I}\in C^{\infty}(\check{M}),\quad\xi^{I}\in\wedge^{\bullet}(S^{\vee}),

(99)

where
I
is a multiindex.
The tangent bundle decomposes as

TM\cong M\times(V\oplus\Pi S)\to M.

(100)

Let us fix (global) generators
\{\partial_{i}\}
corresponding to an orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}
of
V
, and
\{\partial_{\mu}\}
corresponding to a basis
\{f_{\mu}\}
of
\Pi S
. Let
{\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{i}}e_{i}=\Gamma(f_{\mu},f_{\nu})
. In these coordinates, the structure distribution is given by

\mathcal{F}=\vspan_{\mathcal{O}_{M}}\big\{D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\xi^{\nu}{\Gamma_{\mu\nu}}^{i}\partial_{i}:\mu=1,\dots,\dim(S)\big\},

(101)

such that
[D_{\mu},D_{\nu}]={\Gamma_{\mu\nu}}^{i}\partial_{i}
. This affine superspace constitutes a local model for general spinorial superspaces.

The case where the signature of
q
is
(1,m-1)
is referred to as
super Minkowski space
, and is the running example in physics literature, see, for example,
[
CdF91
]
.

6.2
Computing Integrals

In local spin frames on the total space
\Pi S\check{M}
, that is, sections of
Q=\check{Q}\times\Pi S\to\Pi S\check{M}
, we have

q=(\tau^{*}(e_{1},\dots,e_{m}),\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{1})^{\vee},\dots,\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{n})^{\vee}),

(102)

where the
(e_{i})
are a local frame on
\check{M}
, i.e., a local section of
\check{Q}\to\check{M}
. In what follows, we identify frames and coframes by dualising the bases of the respective distributions.
Note that there exists a
\GL(m|n)
transformation of unit Berezinian relating
q
with a frame that has the
\partial_{\mu}
as odd generators (rather than
\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{\mu}
), given explicitly by

\Psi:(\tau^{*}(e_{1},\dots,e_{n}),\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{n})\mapsto(e_{1},\dots,e_{n},\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{1}),\dots,\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{n})),

(103)

or, as a matrix,

\Psi=\left(\begin{array}[]{ c | c }\id&\Gamma(\mathcal{E},\cdot)\\
\hline\cr 0&\id\end{array}\right).

(104)

In particular, it holds:

Corollary 3
.

The Berezinian section associated to a spin frame
q
is the same as the one associated to
q.\Psi^{-1}
upon embedding
Q
into the bundle of
\GL(m|n)
frames.

Since
i^{*}\tau^{*}(e_{1},\dots,e_{m})=(\tau\circ i)^{*}(e_{1},\dots,e_{m})=(e_{1},\dots,e_{m})
, the usual formula

\int_{M}f[q]=\int_{\check{M}}i^{*}(\partial_{1}\dots\partial_{n}f)e^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge e^{m}

(105)

remains valid. Furthermore, note that under the pullback by
i
, we may – after antisymmetrising – replace the coordinate derivations by the deformed ones again if the odd dimension of the supermanifold is equal to two:

Lemma 15
.

Let
n=2
be the odd dimension of the superspace. It holds

i^{*}\left(\partial_{1}\partial_{2}f\right)=\frac{1}{2}i^{*}\left((\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{1})\circ\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{2})-\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{2})\circ\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{1}))f\right).

(106)

Proof.

It holds

i^{*}(\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{1})\circ\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{2})f)=i^{*}(\partial_{1}\circ(\partial_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{\varphi}_{\Gamma(\mathcal{E},\partial_{2})}f)=i^{*}\left(\left(\partial_{1}\circ\partial_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{\varphi}_{\Gamma(\partial_{1},\partial_{2})}\right)f\right).

(107)

Upon antisymmetrising, the second term vanishes due to the symmetry properties of
\Gamma
. Finally, recall that for coordinate frames,
\partial_{1}\partial_{2}=-\partial_{2}\partial_{1}
, concluding the proof.
∎

To summarise, we have the following theorem:

Theorem 4
.

Let
n=2
be the odd dimension of the superspace, and let us denote by
q=(\tau^{*}(e^{1},\dots,e^{m}),\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{1}),\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{2}))
a spin frame. Let
\mathrm{vol}=[q]
be the canonical superspace volume form, and let
\check{\mathrm{vol}}=e^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge e^{m}
be the volume form on the ordinary spacetime. It holds

\int_{M}f\mathrm{vol}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\check{M}}i^{*}\left(\left(\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{1})\circ\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{2})-\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{1})\circ\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{2})\right)f\right)\check{\mathrm{vol}}.

(108)

If
n\geq 4
, correction terms involving the Riemannian curvature of
(\check{M},\check{g})
will appear. However, in our treatment of super Yang-Mills theories, the odd dimensions of the superspaces we consider will always be equal to two.

7
Super Yang-Mills Theories in Dimensions 3 and 4

We finally have all the required tools to invariantly define pure super Yang-Mills (sYM) theory on spinorial superspaces. We first give a general overview of the expected component fields, and then explicitly treat the
\mathcal{N}=1
case in
d=3
and
d=4
spacetime dimensions in Minkowski signature. We reduce the superspace Lagrangians to underlying ordinary spacetimes
i:\check{M}\to M
in each of the cases and discuss the component fields that appear due to the reduction. In both cases, the reduced Lagrangian is of the form

\check{\mathcal{L}}(a,\lambda)=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\mu\|F^{a}\|^{2}-\langle\lambda,D\!\!\!\!/\,^{a}\lambda\rangle\right)\check{\mathrm{vol}},

(109)

where
\mu=1/2
in
d=3
and
\mu=1
in
d=4
.
Here,
\check{\mathrm{vol}}
is the Riemannian volume form on
\check{M}
,
\lambda
denotes a real
\ad(i^{*}P)
-valued spinor field, and
a=i^{*}\mathsf{a}
is a connection on the principal bundle
\check{P}=i^{*}P\to\check{M}
restricted to the ordinary spacetime. Thus, the reduced Lagrangian is a minimally coupled system of Dirac and Yang-Mills Lagrangians.

The upshot of the superspace formulations is that the Lagrangians we consider are manifestly gauge invariant. Furthermore, both the Yang-Mills and the Dirac component fields emerge from a single component of the superspace curvature, emphasising the duality between the spinor field and the
field strength tensor
(the curvature of the reduced connection).

We will first define the component fields of the theory. These correspond to certain components of the gauge connection and its curvature. Then, we will give superspace formulations of the super Yang-Mills theories in
d=3
and
d=4
and show how to reduce them to an underlying ordinary spacetime manifold. We will restrict the procedure to the case where the family of supermanifolds is trivial, i.e., of the form
M\times A\to A
.

7.1
The Component Fields

To define super Yang-Mills theories, we consider some fixed principal
G
-bundle
P\to M
over the superspace. Here,
G
is an ordinary Lie group. As the form of the superspace Lagrangian will depend on the dimension and signature of the infinitesimal model
(V,q)
of the ordinary spacetime, we shall first discuss the fields that arise upon restricting the super Yang-Mills fields to the ordinary spacetime via the embedding
i:\check{M}\to M
. The space of relevant fields will be given by the gauge constrained connections

\mathfrak{F}^{sYM}=\mathfrak{F}_{P}=\{\mathsf{a}\in\mathcal{A}_{P}:\langle\mathcal{F}\wedge\mathcal{F},F^{\mathsf{a}}\rangle=0\},

(110)

that is, connections that are flat along the spinorial distribution
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{T}_{M}
. Here,

F^{\mathsf{a}}=\mathrm{d}\mathsf{a}+\frac{1}{2}[\mathsf{a}\wedge\mathsf{a}]\in\Omega^{2}(P,\mathfrak{g}))\cong\Omega^{2}(M,\ad(P))

(111)

is the curvature of the connection
\mathsf{a}
. Due to the gauge constraint, it decomposes according to

F^{\mathsf{a}}=f^{\mathsf{a}}\oplus\phi^{\mathsf{a}}\in\Omega^{2|0}(P,\mathfrak{g})^{G}_{\hor}\oplus\Omega^{1|1}(P,\mathfrak{g})^{G}_{\hor}

(112)

as the
0|2
-component vanishes. We can dualise the
\tilde{\Gamma}
map to give rise to a morphism
\tilde{\gamma}:\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{B}^{\vee}\to\mathcal{F}
, allowing us to produce an
\ad(P)
-valued section of the spinorial distribution
\mathcal{F}
by means of the duality pairing

\psi^{\mathsf{a}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\langle\phi,\tilde{\gamma}\rangle\in\Omega^{0}(M,\ad(P))\otimes\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\cong\Omega^{0}(Q\times_{M}P,\Pi S\otimes\mathfrak{g})^{\Spin(V,q)\times G}_{\hor}.

(113)

This superspace spinor will play a central role in the
d=3
and
d=4
super Yang-Mills theories.
We now restrict the theory to the ordinary spacetime via the pullback
i^{*}P\eqqcolon\check{P}\to\check{M}
and obtain the following component fields of the theory:

Definition 7.1
.

The
component fields
of the sYM theory are given by

•

an ordinary connection
a=i^{*}\mathsf{a}\in\mathcal{A}_{\check{P}}
,

•

an
\ad(\check{P})
-valued spinor field
\lambda=(\psi_{S}^{-1}\otimes\id)(\psi^{\mathsf{a}}|_{\check{M}})
,

where
\psi_{S}:\Omega^{0}(\check{M},S\check{M})\to i^{*}\mathcal{F}
is the isomorphism from lemma
10
.

These component fields will constitute the relevant fields upon reducing the theory to ordinary spacetime manifolds, and are the only fields appearing in the
d=3
and
d=4
sYM theory. In higher dimensions and different signatures, there may occur further component fields.

7.2
Lorentzian
d=3
sYM

The irreducible real spinorial representation
S
in
d=3
with Minkowskian signature is two-dimensional and of real type. The invariant bilinear form extends
g_{\mathcal{B}}
to a super-Riemannian metric
g=g_{\mathcal{B}}\oplus g_{\mathcal{F}}
on
TM
. We give the superspace Lagrangian, suitably adapted from
[
Del+99
]
:

Definition 7.2
.

The
d=3
Lorentzian superspace Lagrangian is defined by

\mathcal{L}:\mathfrak{F}^{\textup{sYM}}\to{\mathcal{B}}er_{M},\quad\mathsf{a}\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\|\psi^{\mathsf{a}}\|^{2}_{\kappa\otimes\epsilon}\mathrm{vol}_{g},

(114)

where
\kappa
denotes the Killing form on
\mathfrak{g}
, and
\epsilon=g_{\mathcal{F}}
the restriction of the super-Lorentzian metric to the spinorial distribution
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{T}_{M}
.

Note that this Lagrangian is defined directly on the superspace. Gauge-invariance is immediately clear since
F^{\mathsf{a}}
is
G
-equivariant, and hence, so is
\psi^{\mathsf{a}}
, and the Killing form is
G
-invariant.

We now want to reduce the field theory to the underlying ordinary spacetime manifold:

Theorem 5
.

Upon restricting the theory to the embedded ordinary spacetime manifold, we obtain the Yang-Mills-Dirac Lagrangian

\check{\mathcal{L}}(a,\lambda)=\left(-\frac{1}{4}\|F^{a}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\langle\lambda,D\!\!\!\!/\,^{a}\lambda\rangle\right)\check{\mathrm{vol}},

(115)

where the component fields are given by

\displaystyle a=i^{*}\mathsf{a}\in\mathcal{A}_{\check{P}},\quad\lambda=(\psi_{S}^{-1}\otimes\id)(\psi|_{\check{M}})\in\Omega^{0}(\check{M},S\check{M}\otimes\ad(\check{P})).

(116)

Proof.

Recall that the reduced Lagrangian in terms of local coordinates is obtained by taking derivatives in the odd directions and suitably adapting the volume form, see, e.g.,
[
Keß18
]
.
We would like to circumvent the use of coordinates on superspace and instead solely rely on coordinates on the relevant spinor module as well as
\Spin(V,q)
frames on
M
. This bears the advantage that we can exploit the representation theory of the
\Spin(V,q)
group and require calculus only on the vector space
S
.

We first sketch the general situation: Any Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}
on superspace can be reduced to a Lagrangian
\check{\mathcal{L}}
on the embedded ordinary manifold
\check{M}
by ‘integrating out the odd directions’, as discussed in references
[
Del+99
,
Keß18
]
.
At this point, recall that integration along the odd directions is purely algebraic. It is thus sensible to invoke Batchelor’s theorem, together with the remark in section
6
, which tells us that the superspace
M
is diffeomorphic to a split one of the kind
\Pi S\check{M}\to\check{M}
. As the integral is invariant under diffeomorphisms, it thus suffices to compute the claim on such a split superspace. Using the frame bundle, we can write

\Pi S\check{M}=\check{Q}\times_{\Spin(V,q)}\Pi S,

(117)

arising as an associated bundle using the real irreducible representation
S
. Here, we identify
i^{*}Q=\check{Q}\to\check{M}
as the spin structure on the body.
Following the discussion in section
6
, we can use lemma
4
to compute the reduced Lagrangian, and find

\check{\mathcal{L}}=\frac{1}{2}i^{*}\left(\sum_{X,Y}\tilde{\epsilon}(X^{\vee},Y^{\vee})X\circ Y\big((\epsilon\otimes\kappa)(\psi^{\mathsf{a}},\psi^{\mathsf{a}})\big)\right)\check{\mathrm{vol}}

(118)

where
X=\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{1})
and
Y=\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{2})
for brevity.
Let us pick a spinorial superspace connection
\varphi^{s}\in\mathcal{A}_{Q}^{s}
and consider the pullback bundle
P\times_{M}Q\to M
. Note that
\mathsf{a}\oplus\varphi\eqqcolon A
defines a connection that is compatible with both
G
and the spinorial structure. In what follows, we denote by
\nabla^{A}\eqqcolon\nabla
the corresponding covariant derivative. Then

\frac{1}{2}X\circ Y\big((\epsilon\otimes\kappa)(\psi,\psi)\big)=X\big((\epsilon\otimes\kappa)(\nabla_{Y}\psi,\psi)\big)=(\epsilon\otimes\kappa)(\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}\psi,\psi)-(\epsilon\otimes\kappa)(\nabla_{Y}\psi,\nabla_{X}\psi).

(119)

Upon pulling this function back to
\check{M}
, we obtain

\frac{1}{4}i^{*}\left(\sum_{X,Y}\tilde{\epsilon}(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})\big((\epsilon\otimes\kappa)(\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}\psi,\psi)-(\epsilon\otimes\kappa)(\nabla_{Y}\psi,\nabla_{X}\psi)\big)\right)=\frac{1}{2}\langle\lambda,D\!\!\!\!/\,^{a}\lambda\rangle-\frac{1}{4}\|F^{a}\|^{2},

(120)

where we made use of lemmas
20
and
22
(see appendix).
∎

We thus found that upon reducing the theory to the body, the superspace formulation of super Yang-Mills theory reproduces the well-established Dirac-Yang-Mills-Lagrangian.

7.3
Lorentzian
d=4
sYM

The irreducible spinorial representation
S
of
d=4
Minkowski space is four-dimensional and of complex type. Upon complexification, it turns into two half-spin representations
S^{\mathbb{C}}=S^{+}\oplus S^{-}
. Alternatively,
S^{\mathbb{C}}
can be decomposed into chiral and antichiral subspaces

S^{\mathbb{C}}=S^{1,0}\oplus S^{0,1},

(121)

obtained by the projections
\pr_{1,0}=\id-\mathrm{i}\otimes J
and
\pr_{0,1}=\id+\mathrm{i}\otimes J
as discussed in section
4
.
The
\mathbb{C}
-module structure of
S^{1,0}\oplus S^{0,1}
is thus given by
z.(s,t)=(zs,\bar{z}t)
, such that the invariant pairing
S^{1,0}\otimes S^{0,1}\to\mathbb{C}
is invariant under
\operatorname{U}(1)
, and the non-degenerate bilinear form respects the decomposition
S^{1,0}\oplus S^{0,1}
.

Passing to the spinorial distribution via the construction of associated bundles, the complexified spinorial distribution
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{C}}
decomposes into chiral and antichiral components
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathcal{F}^{1,0}\oplus\mathcal{F}^{0,1}
, as discussed in section
4
.

Lemma 16
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{M}^{\omega}\subset\mathcal{O}_{M}\otimes\mathbb{C}
be the sheaf of functions that are annihilated by
\mathcal{F}^{0,1}
, and let
\mathcal{O}_{M}^{\bar{\omega}}\subset\mathcal{O}_{M}\otimes\mathbb{C}
be the sheaf of functions that are annihilated by
\mathcal{F}^{0,1}
. The locally ringed spaces
M^{1,0}=(\underline{M},\mathcal{O}^{\omega}_{M})
and
M^{0,1}=(\underline{M},\mathcal{O}^{\bar{\omega}}_{M})
are CS manifolds of dimensions
4|2
.

Proof.

This follows directly from lemma
7
.
∎

In the same way, one verifies that the sheaf of sections annihilated by
\mathcal{F}^{1,0}
defines a complementary CS manifold of dimension
4|2
.

Moving on, we shall work in the complexified description and take real parts. Since the complexified spinor
(\psi^{\mathsf{a}})^{\mathbb{C}}
arises from a real representation of
\Spin(V,q)
, we obtain a reality condition, which reads

(\psi^{\mathsf{a}})^{\mathbb{C}}=\chi^{\mathsf{a}}\oplus\overline{\chi^{\mathsf{a}}}\in\Omega^{0}\big(M,\ad(P)^{\mathbb{C}}\big)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{M}}\big((\mathcal{F}^{1,0})^{\vee}\oplus(\mathcal{F}^{0,1})^{\vee}\big).

(122)

In this complexified picture, we also complexified the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of the structure group
G
, and the tensor products are taken over
\mathbb{C}
. Note that the real form inside
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
is imaginary / skew-Hermitean for compact matrix Lie groups. We can now define the
d=4
sYM Lagrangian:

Definition 7.3
.

The
d=4
Lorentzian superspace sYM Lagrangian is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}:\mathfrak{F}^{\textup{sYM}}\to{\mathcal{B}}er_{M}^{1,0}\oplus{\mathcal{B}}er_{M}^{0,1},

(123)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\mathsf{a})=\frac{1}{4}\big(\|\chi^{\mathsf{a}}\|^{2}_{\epsilon\otimes\kappa}\mathrm{vol}^{1,0}+\|\overline{\chi^{\mathsf{a}}}\|^{2}_{\overline{\epsilon\otimes\kappa}}\mathrm{vol}^{0,1}\big)=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Re}\big(\|\chi^{\mathsf{a}}\|^{2}_{\epsilon\otimes\kappa}\mathrm{vol}^{1,0}\big)

(124)

where the chiral resp. antichiral sections of the respective Berezinian bundles are interpreted as in section
4
.

As in the
d=3
case, the Lagrangian is manifestly gauge invariant. We also have similar reduction results upon restricting the theory to an ordinary spacetime manifold:

Theorem 6
.

The
d=4
Lorentzian sYM Lagrangian reproduces the component Lagrangian

\check{\mathcal{L}}(a,\psi,E)=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\|F^{a}\|^{2}-\langle\lambda,D\!\!\!\!/\,^{a}\lambda\rangle\right)\check{\mathrm{vol}},

(125)

with the component fields

\displaystyle a=i^{*}\mathsf{a}\in\mathcal{A}_{\check{P}},\quad\lambda=(\psi_{S}^{-1}\otimes\id)(\psi|_{\check{M}})\in\Omega^{0}(\check{M},S\check{M}\otimes\ad(\check{P})).

(126)

Proof.

We continue working in the complexified picture.
The
1|1
-component
\phi^{\mathsf{a}}
of the curvature decomposes into
(\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}})^{\vee}\wedge(\mathcal{F}^{+})^{\vee}
- and
(\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}})^{\vee}\wedge(\mathcal{F}^{-})^{\vee}
-components, which we shall denote by
\phi^{\mathsf{a}}=\zeta\oplus\bar{\zeta}
. Bianchi’s identity now takes the form

\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}f+\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\zeta+\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\bar{\zeta}=0.

(127)

On the complexified spinor modules, the
\Gamma
map is of the type
S^{+}\otimes S^{-}\to V
– thus,

\chi=\langle\bar{\zeta},\tilde{\gamma}\rangle,\quad\bar{\chi}=\langle\zeta,\tilde{\gamma}\rangle.

(128)

We shall compute the fibrewise integration of the chiral component
\epsilon(\chi,\chi)\mathrm{vol}
along the odd directions – the antichiral component will be its complex conjugate. As in the preceding chapter, we compute the integral fibre-wise on the split superspace. It holds

\check{\mathcal{L}}=\frac{1}{4}i^{*}\left(\epsilon^{XY}X\circ Y(\epsilon\otimes\kappa)(\chi,\chi)\right)\check{\mathrm{vol}}+c.c.,

(129)

where
c.c.
denotes the complex conjugate with respect to the canonical real form arising from the complexification, and
X,Y
is a frame of the chiral distribution
\mathcal{F}^{+}
. Summation over
X
and
Y
is implied. As in the
d=3
case, we pick a spinorial connection on
\hat{Q}\to M
, and lift
X
and
Y
to horizontal vector fields on the pullback bundle
P\times_{M}\hat{Q}\to M
. We shall denote by
A=\mathsf{a}\oplus\varphi^{s}
the connection on the latter bundle, and by
\nabla^{A}\eqqcolon\nabla
the induced covariant derivative on the bundle
\ad(P)^{\mathbb{C}}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{+}
.
It holds

\left(\epsilon^{XY}X\circ Y\right)(\epsilon\otimes\kappa)(\chi,\chi)=-2\epsilon^{XY}\big((\epsilon\otimes\kappa)(\nabla_{X}\chi,\nabla_{Y}\chi)-(\epsilon\otimes\kappa)(\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}\chi,\chi)\big).

(130)

The first of the two terms on the right hand side is computed in lemma
23
, and its restriction to
\check{M}
equates to
\|F^{a}\|^{2}
. The restriction to
\check{M}
of the second term evaluates to

\epsilon(\chi|_{\check{M}},D\!\!\!\!/\,^{a}(\chi_{\check{M}}))).

(131)

Recall that the Hermitean form intertwines
\mathcal{F}^{1,0}
and
\mathcal{F}^{0,1}
, such that we may identify the preceding equation as the duality pairing

\langle\overline{\chi}|_{\check{M}},D\!\!\!\!/\,^{a}(\chi_{\check{M}}))\rangle.

(132)

By adding the complex conjugate, we obtain

\check{\mathcal{L}}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\|F^{a}\|^{2}-\langle\lambda,D\!\!\!\!/\,^{a}\lambda\rangle\right)\check{\mathrm{vol}}

(133)

as the Hermitean pairing takes
\mathcal{F}^{1,0}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}^{0,1}\to\mathcal{O}_{M}
.
∎

Note that in physics literature, one often finds an additional
\ad(\check{P})\otimes\mathfrak{u}(1)
-valued component field
E
. This is due to the fact that in the corresponding computations, a musical isomorphism is used in-between to turn
\lambda
into a one-form on superspace.

8
Conclusion and Outlook

In this document, we established a mathematically precise and concise notion of spinorial superspaces
by means of
\Spin(V,q)
-structures on supermanifolds. We constructed a rather general framework
in which Lagrangian field theories on these superspaces can be reduced to the underlying ordinary
spacetime manifold, giving rise to component fields that are well-known in the physics literature.
Finally, we applied the results to
\mathcal{N}=1
super Yang-Mills theories in
d=3
and
d=4
spacetime
dimensions. Our treatment has the upshot of being coordinate-free and manifestly gauge invariant.
Together with the spinorial structure of the superspaces we consider, this allows for a rather geometric interpretation of the theories at hand. The coordinate-free description also allows for the
formulation of these theories on curved superspaces, generalising the established physics treatment
which is usually restricted to the case where the ordinary spacetime is chosen to be Minkowski
space, or a torus.
Further work has to be carried out to characterise the general supersymmetry properties of the
considered models when considered over arbitrary superspaces. In particular, a component field
characterisation of the supersymmetries arising from our framework of spinorial superspaces is
desirable.
It would also be of interest to construct similar super Yang-Mills theories in higher dimensions and
in different signatures. The Euclidean case is of particular interest in the mathematical community
in hopes of assigning invariants to the manifold by characterising the solution space of the theory.
To that end, it would be of interest to explore the structure of spinorial superspaces when the
underlying spinorial representation is of quaternionic type. In this way, a
d=4
Euclidean sYM
theory could be formulated on superspaces.
As our framework of spinorial superspaces is rather general, it is also feasible to consider other field
theories on them. In particular, the supersymmetric sigma model can likely be cast as a theory on
spinorial superspaces. On the other hand, one could construct more general field theories directly
on superspace, and explore the arising component fields as well as the reduction of the theory to
the underlying ordinary spacetime manifold.

Appendix A
Complex Supermanifolds

In what follows, we discuss the notion of
complex supermanifolds
:

Definition A.1
.

Let
M=(\underline{M},\mathcal{O}_{M})
be a supermanifold. The associated
complex supermanifold
(or
CS manifold
for short) is the locally graded ringed space
M=(\underline{M},\mathcal{O}_{M}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C})
constructed by complexifying the structure sheaf.

A few remarks are in order.

•

The complex nature of this kind of manifold only enters the picture along the odd directions; and the underlying real supermanifold can be obtained by restricting the structure sheaf to its real part.

•

By construction, we only requested
smoothness
of the functions instead of real or complex analycity. This is due to the fact that the complex behaviour of functions is merely algebraic and has no underlying topological implications.

•

There exists the more restrictive notion of
complex analytic supermanifolds
, which is discussed in detail in reference
[
Keß18
]
. In this setting, one indeed requires the sheaf of functions to be locally isomorphic to the sheaf of holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
. We shall not require this notion.

We can restrict the structure sheaf of a CS manifold to obtain a CS submanifold as follows:

Lemma 17
.

Let
M
be a CS manifold of dimension
m|n_{1}+n_{2}
that admits a decomposition
TM=D_{1}\oplus D_{2}
into regular distributions of rank
m|n_{1}
and
0|n_{2}
, respectively. If
D_{1}
is an integrable distribution, we obtain a CS manifold
M_{1}
of dimension
m|n_{1}
, where
M_{1}
is given by

M_{1}=(\underline{M},\mathcal{O}_{M_{1}}),\quad\mathcal{O}_{M_{1}}=\{s\in\mathcal{O}_{M}\otimes\mathbb{C}:Xs=0\textrm{ for all }X\in\Omega^{0}(M,D_{2})\}.

(134)

In other words, the structure sheaf arises by requiring that the functions are annihilated by
D_{2}
.

Proof.

By Leibnitz’ rule, it is clear that the restriction of the sheaf is a sheaf of locally graded subrings: Suppose that
X\in\Omega^{0}(M,D_{1})
, and
s,t\in\mathcal{O}_{M_{1}}
. It follows

X(st)=(Xs)t+(-1)^{p(X)p(s)}s(Xt)=0

(135)

as each of the summands is zero. Therefore,
st\in\mathcal{O}_{M_{1}}
. Grading and locality follow from the corresponding properties of
\mathcal{O}_{M}
, and integrability of
D_{1}
ensures the existence of the CS submanifold in question.
∎

Thus whenever a function on
M
is actually contained in a suitable subsheaf
\mathcal{O}_{M_{1}}
, we can view it as a function defined on the CS submanifold
M_{1}
. We will exploit this fact to define the superspace Lagrangian of
d=4
super Yang-Mills theory.

Appendix B
Frame Computations

General Remarks

The proofs of the following lemmas intertwine the superspace structure with Cartan’s and Bianchi’s identities. To economically account for these different geometric concepts, we shall resort to computations in local spin frames. We apologise in advance for the ‘battle of indices’ that is about to take place.

We now fix the notation surrounding the musical isomorphisms in local frames. To that end, note that whenenver
X
and
Y
are odd vector fields, it holds
g(X,Y)=-g(Y,X)
by the sign rule. Let
X,Y
and
Z
be sections of the spinorial distribution, that is,
X,Y,Z\in\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{T}_{M}
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is purely odd, it suffices to establish the notation for odd local generators (for example, one could pick them such that they arise from spin frames). The general case follows by extending in a graded
\mathcal{O}_{M}
-multilinear fashion.

Let us write
\epsilon=g_{\mathcal{F}}
to emphasize the signs occurring due to the grading. We shall use the second index of
\epsilon
to raise indices

A^{\flat}=\epsilon(\cdot,A)\Leftrightarrow\epsilon^{XY}A_{Y}=A^{X}.

(136)

Since by definition
(A^{\flat})^{\sharp}=A
, or equivalently
\epsilon^{XY}\epsilon_{YZ}=\delta^{X}_{Z}
, it follows

A_{Z}=\delta^{X}_{Z}A_{X}=\epsilon^{XY}\epsilon_{YZ}A_{X}=-\epsilon_{YZ}A^{Y}

(137)

such that
\epsilon_{ZY}A^{Y}=A_{Z}
. Thus, we consistently use the second index of
\epsilon
to both raise and lower indices. Since frame computations, especially these involving raising or lowering an even amount of indices, are rather prone to sign errors, we try avoiding the use of local frames and instead, resort to more conceptual methods whenever possible.

Lemma 18
.

Let
X,Y
be horizontal lifts of
\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}\in\mathcal{F}
. It holds

\iota_{X}\iota_{Y}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\phi=-\iota_{[X,Y]}f

(138)

Proof.

The proof is a little exercise involving Cartan’s magic formula:

\displaystyle\iota_{X}\iota_{Y}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\phi

\displaystyle=-\iota_{X}\iota_{Y}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}f

(139)

\displaystyle=-\iota_{X}L_{Y}f

(140)

\displaystyle=-\iota_{[X,Y]}f,

(141)

where we used that
f
vanishes on the spinorial vectors; and
\iota_{X}\mathsf{a}=\iota_{Y}\mathsf{a}=0
as
X
and
Y
are horizontal.
∎

In the following,
n
shall denote the odd dimension of
M
(or, equivalently, the rank of the spinorial distribution
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{T}_{M})
.

Lemma 19
.

Let
\psi
be the sYM spinor associated to a connection
\mathsf{a}\in\mathcal{A}_{P}
, let
\varphi^{s}\in\mathcal{A}_{\hat{Q}}^{s}
be a spinorial connection, and let
X
be the horizontal lift of a section of the spinorial distribution
\mathcal{F}
to
\mathcal{T}_{P}
. Set
\nabla^{\mathsf{a}}\eqqcolon\nabla
and let
i:\check{M}\to M
be the embedding of the ordinary spacetime manifold.
It holds

(\nabla_{X}\psi)\big|_{\check{M}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\langle f,\tilde{\gamma}\circ\gamma(X)\rangle\big|_{\check{M}}.

(142)

Proof.

We start off by plugging in the definition of the sYM spinor, such that

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\nabla_{X}\psi

\displaystyle=\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\langle\phi,\tilde{\gamma}\rangle=\langle\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\phi,\tilde{\gamma}\rangle,

(143)

where we used that
\tilde{\gamma}
is covariantly constant in the final step. We now expand
\tilde{\gamma}
in a local frame

\tilde{\gamma}=\sum_{Y}\tilde{\gamma}(Y^{\vee})\otimes Y

(144)

and make use of lemma
18
, such that

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\nabla_{X}\psi

\displaystyle=\left\langle\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\phi,\sum_{Y}\tilde{\gamma}(Y^{\vee})\otimes Y\right\rangle

(145)

\displaystyle=\sum_{Y}\langle\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\phi,\tilde{\gamma}(Y^{\vee})\rangle

(146)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{Y}\langle\iota_{[X,Y]}f,\tilde{\gamma}(Y^{\vee})\rangle.

(147)

Upon restricting to the underlying even manifold, we have
[X,Y]|_{\check{M}}=(\psi_{S}^{*}\Gamma)(X|_{\check{M}},Y_{\check{M}}))
, such that

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\cdot(\psi_{S}^{-1}\otimes\id)\big((\nabla_{X}\psi)\big|_{\check{M}}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{Y}\left\langle f,\tilde{\gamma}(Y^{\vee})\otimes\Gamma(X,Y)\right\rangle\Big|_{\check{M}}

(148)

\displaystyle=\langle f,\tilde{\gamma}\circ\gamma(X)\rangle\big|_{\check{M}},

(149)

concluding the proof.
∎

In the remainder of this appendix, we shall work out algebraic properties of component fields associated to a fixed connection
\mathsf{a}
of the principal bundle
P\to M
on the superspace. Therefore, we shall frequently write
F=F^{\mathsf{a}}
for the curvature, as well as
f^{\mathsf{a}}=f
,
\psi^{\mathsf{a}}=\psi
and
\phi^{\mathsf{a}}=\phi
for the component fields.

d=3
Frame Computations

We now collect a few lemmas that contain the key computations to deduce key properties of the sYM superspace Lagrangian in
d=3
. For notational convenience, we shall frequently identify the restricted spinorial distribution
i^{*}\mathcal{F}
with a spinor bundle
S\check{M}\to\check{M}
over the ordinary spacetime manifold.

Lemma 20
.

Let
\psi
be the sYM spinor, let
i:\check{M}\to M
be an embedding of an underlying even manifold, and let
i^{*}\mathsf{a}=a\in\mathcal{A}_{i^{*}P}
be the pullback connection. It holds

i^{*}\big(\epsilon^{XY}(\epsilon\otimes\kappa)(\nabla_{X}\psi,\nabla_{Y}\psi)\big)=\|F^{a}\|^{2},

(150)

where
\kappa
denotes the Killing form on
\mathfrak{g}
, and the norm of the curvature is taken with respect to
\kappa
and the induced Riemannian metric
\check{g}
on
\check{M}
.

Proof.

Recall first the Clifford identities

\displaystyle\tr(\gamma\circ\tilde{\gamma})=n\cdot g,\quad Q\coloneqq\tr(\tilde{\gamma}\circ\gamma\circ\tilde{\gamma}\circ\gamma)|_{\Sym^{2}(\Omega^{2|0}(M))}=-n\|\cdot\|^{2}_{g},

(151)

where
n
is the odd dimension of
M
, and compositions of the kind
\gamma\circ\tilde{\gamma}
are interpreted as duality pairings of
S
with
S^{\vee}
. With help of the preceding lemma, we find

\displaystyle n\cdot i^{*}\big(\epsilon^{XY}(\epsilon\otimes\kappa)(\nabla_{X}\psi,\nabla_{Y}\psi)\big)

\displaystyle=i^{*}\big(\epsilon^{XY}(\epsilon\otimes\kappa)(\langle f,\tilde{\gamma}\circ\gamma(X)\rangle,\langle f,\tilde{\gamma}\circ\gamma(Y)\rangle)\big)

(152)

\displaystyle=i^{*}\big(\epsilon^{XY}\epsilon_{WZ}\kappa(\langle f,\tilde{\gamma}(W^{\vee})\circ\gamma(X)\rangle,\langle f,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\circ\gamma(Y)\rangle)\big),

(153)

where summation over
W,X,Y
and
Z
is implied.
We make use of the identity
\epsilon^{XY}\epsilon_{WZ}=\delta^{X}_{W}\delta^{Y}_{Z}-\delta^{X}_{Z}\delta^{Y}_{W}

as well as
\sum_{X}\gamma(X)\circ\tilde{\gamma}(X^{\vee})=\tr(\gamma\circ\tilde{\gamma})
, such that

i^{*}\big(\delta^{X}_{W}\delta^{Y}_{Z}\kappa(\langle f,\tilde{\gamma}(W^{\vee})\circ\gamma(X)\rangle,\langle f,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\circ\gamma(Y)\rangle)\big)=i^{*}\big(\kappa(\langle f,g\rangle,\langle f,g\rangle)\big)=0

(154)

as
g
is symmetric and
i^{*}f
is a two-form (and hence skew). Next, keeping in mind the symmetry properties of forms and the traces of
\gamma
(resp.
\tilde{\gamma}
) maps,

\displaystyle i^{*}\big(\delta^{X}_{Z}\delta^{Y}_{W}\kappa(\langle f,\tilde{\gamma}(W^{\vee})\circ\gamma(X)\rangle,\langle f,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\circ\gamma(Y)\rangle)\big)

\displaystyle=i^{*}\big(\langle\kappa(f\wedge f),\tr(\tilde{\gamma}\circ\gamma\circ\tilde{\gamma}\circ\gamma)\rangle\big)

(155)

\displaystyle=n\|i^{*}f\|^{2}=n\|F^{a}\|^{2},

(156)

as claimed.
∎

Let us express the Dirac operator in terms of the language presented above:

Lemma 21
.

Let
\psi
be the sYM spinor, and let
(\psi_{S}^{-1}\otimes\id)(\psi|_{\check{M}})=\lambda
be the
associated component field.
Denote by
a=i^{*}\mathsf{a}
the pullback connection on the ordinary spacetime manifold. It holds

D\!\!\!\!/\,^{a}\lambda=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\langle\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\phi,\tilde{\gamma}\circ\gamma\circ\tilde{\epsilon}\rangle\big|_{\check{M}},

(157)

where we identified
i^{*}\mathcal{F}
with the spinor bundle
S\check{M}\to\check{M}
.

Proof.

The Dirac operator, applied to a spinor, is defined as

D\!\!\!\!/\,^{a}\check{\psi}=\langle\mathrm{d}^{a}\check{\psi},\gamma\circ\tilde{\epsilon}\rangle\quad\text{(duality pairing along }T\check{M}\otimes S\check{M}\text{)},

(158)

where
\tilde{\epsilon}
acts as the musical isomorphism.
Hence,
D\!\!\!\!/\,^{a}\check{\psi}
is again a section of
S\check{M}\otimes\ad(\check{P})\to\check{M}
. We plug in the definition of
\check{\psi}
such that

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\cdot D\!\!\!\!/\,^{a}\check{\psi}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathrm{d}^{a}i^{*}\langle\psi,\tilde{\gamma}\rangle,\gamma\circ\tilde{\epsilon}\rangle

(159)

\displaystyle=i^{*}\langle\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\phi,\tilde{\gamma}\circ\gamma\circ\tilde{\epsilon}\rangle,

(160)

where we used that
\gamma
and
\tilde{\epsilon}
are covariantly constant.
∎

This allows us to compute the second appearing term arising in the sYM setup:

Lemma 22
.

Let
\Delta_{\epsilon}^{\mathsf{a}}=\epsilon^{XY}\nabla_{X}\circ\nabla_{Y}
, let
a=i^{*}\mathsf{a}
, and let
\check{\psi}=i^{*}\psi
. It holds

\big(\Delta^{\mathsf{a}}_{\epsilon}\psi\big)\big|_{\check{M}}=2D\!\!\!\!/\,^{a}\lambda.

(161)

Proof.

As before, we expand
\tilde{\gamma}
in a local frame, such that

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\cdot\Delta^{\mathsf{a}}_{\epsilon}\psi

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{XY}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\iota_{Y}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\langle\phi,\tilde{\gamma}\rangle

(162)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{Z}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\iota_{Y}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\phi,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\rangle

(163)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{X}\iota_{Z}L_{Y}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\phi,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee}),\rangle

(164)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{X}(\iota_{[Z,Y]}-\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\iota_{Y}\iota_{Z})\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\phi,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\rangle

(165)

\displaystyle=-\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{[Z,Y]}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\phi,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\rangle-\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\iota_{Y}\iota_{Z}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\phi,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\rangle

(166)

Here, we used that
\epsilon^{XY}\iota_{X}\iota_{Y}=\epsilon^{XY}\iota_{[X,Y]}=0
, eliminating many of the otherwise appearing terms.
Upon restricting to
\check{M}
, the first summand equates to

\displaystyle\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{[Z,Y]}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\phi,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\rangle\big|_{\check{M}}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\phi,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\otimes\Gamma(Z,Y)\rangle\big|_{\check{M}}

(167)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\phi,\tilde{\gamma}\circ\gamma(Y)\rangle\big|_{\check{M}}

(168)

\displaystyle=-\langle\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\phi,\tilde{\gamma}\circ\gamma\circ\tilde{\epsilon}\rangle\big|_{\check{M}}

(169)

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{n}\cdot D\!\!\!\!/\,^{a}\lambda

(170)

by the preceding lemma. For the second summand, note that

\displaystyle-\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\iota_{Y}\iota_{Z}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\phi,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\rangle\big|_{\check{M}}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\iota_{[Y,Z]}f,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\rangle\big|_{\check{M}}

(171)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{XY}\langle L_{X}\iota_{[Y,Z]}f,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\rangle\big|_{\check{M}}

(172)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{[X,[Y,Z]]}f+\iota_{[Y,Z]}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\phi,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\rangle\big|_{\check{M}}

(173)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{[X,[Y,Z]]}f,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\rangle\big|_{\check{M}}+\sqrt{n}\cdot D\!\!\!\!/\,^{a}\lambda

(174)

due to lemma 3, the Cartan relations and the fact that
\iota_{X}f=\iota_{Z}f=0
as
X,Z
are spinorial sections. Note that upon restricting to the ordinary spacetime,
[X,[Y,Z]]
is purely odd – however,
i^{*}f
is a purely even
2|0
-form, hence, the first term vanishes. Adding up the remaining contributions, we obtain

\Delta^{\mathsf{a}}_{\epsilon}\psi\big|_{\check{M}}=2D\!\!\!\!/\,^{a}\lambda,

(175)

as claimed.
∎

Lemma 23
.

Let
\psi=\chi+\bar{\chi}
be the decomposition of the sYM spinor into chiral and antichiral components. Let
\varphi^{s}\in\mathcal{A}^{s}_{\hat{Q}}
be a spinorial connection, and let
A=\varphi^{s}\oplus\mathsf{a}
be the product connection on the bundle
\hat{Q}\times_{M}P\to M
. Denote by
\nabla\coloneqq\nabla^{A}
the covariant derivative on
\ad(P)\otimes F^{1,0}
, where
F^{1,0}\to M
is the chiral distribution, corresponding to
\mathcal{F}^{1,0}\subset\mathcal{T}_{M}
. It holds

\sum_{XY}\epsilon^{XY}(\epsilon\otimes\kappa)(\nabla_{X}\chi,\nabla_{Y}\chi)|_{\check{M}}=\|F^{a}\|^{2},

(176)

where
a=i^{*}\mathsf{a}
is the restricted gauge connection.

Proof.

We begin by computing the covariant derivatives of the spinor fields. Note that in the chiral picture,
F^{\mathsf{a}}
decomposes as

F^{\mathsf{a}}=f^{\mathsf{a}}\oplus\zeta^{\mathsf{a}}\oplus\bar{\zeta}^{\mathsf{a}},

(177)

where
f^{\mathsf{a}}
is a
2|0,0
-form,
\zeta^{\mathsf{a}}
is a
1|1,0
-form, and
\bar{\zeta}^{\mathsf{a}}
is a
1|0,1
-form. The
\Gamma
maps restrict to
\wedge^{2}\mathcal{F}^{1,0}\to\mathcal{B}
such that

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\zeta}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bar{\zeta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\chi}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bar{\chi}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\langle\cdot,\tilde{\gamma}\rangle}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\langle\cdot,\tilde{\gamma}\rangle}

(178)

In what follows, we shall consider a fixed connection
\mathsf{a}
and drop it from the notation in the component fields. It holds

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\nabla_{X}\chi

\displaystyle=\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\langle\zeta,\tilde{\gamma}\rangle

(179)

\displaystyle=\sum_{Y}\langle\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\zeta,\tilde{\gamma}(Y^{\vee})\rangle

(180)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{Y}\langle\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}(f+\bar{\zeta}),\tilde{\gamma}(Y^{\vee})\rangle

(181)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{Y}\langle\iota_{[Y,X]}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}(f+\bar{\zeta}),\tilde{\gamma}(Y^{\vee})\rangle

(182)

as
\iota_{X}\bar{\zeta}=\iota_{X}f=0
.
Upon restricting to
\check{M}
, the commutator on
\mathcal{F}^{1,0}
turns into the
\gamma

\sqrt{n}\nabla_{X}\chi=\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\langle\zeta,\tilde{\gamma}\rangle|_{\check{M}}=-\langle f+\bar{\zeta},\tilde{\gamma}\circ\gamma(X)\rangle|_{\check{M}}=-\langle f,\tilde{\gamma}\circ\gamma(X)\rangle|_{\check{M}}

(183)

as the duality pairing is along
\wedge^{2}\mathcal{B}|_{\check{M}}
, and
\bar{\zeta}
is a
1|0,1
-form.

It follows that

\epsilon^{XY}(\epsilon\otimes\kappa)(\nabla_{X}\chi,\nabla_{Y}\chi)|_{\check{M}}=\epsilon^{XY}(\epsilon\otimes\kappa)\big(\langle f,\tilde{\gamma}\circ\gamma(X)\rangle,\langle f,\tilde{\gamma}\circ\gamma(Y)\rangle\big)\big|_{\check{M}}=\|F^{a}\|^{2},

(184)

where summation over
X,Y
is implied, and the last equality can be verified analogously to the
d=3
case.
∎

Finally, the computation giving rise to the Dirac operator goes as follows.

Lemma 24
.

Let the notation be as in the preceding lemma. It holds

\big(\Delta^{\mathsf{a}}_{\epsilon}\chi\big)\big|_{\check{M}}\coloneqq\Big(\epsilon^{XY}\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}\chi\Big)\Big|_{\check{M}}=-D\!\!\!\!/\,^{a}\big(\chi|_{\check{M}}\big).

The proof is completely analogous to the

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\cdot\Delta^{\mathsf{a}}_{\epsilon}\chi

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{XY}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\iota_{Y}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\langle\zeta,\tilde{\gamma}\rangle

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{Z}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\iota_{Y}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\zeta,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\rangle

(187)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{X}\iota_{Z}L_{Y}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\zeta,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee}),\rangle

(188)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{X}(\iota_{[Z,Y]}-\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\iota_{Y}\iota_{Z})\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\zeta,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\rangle

(189)

\displaystyle=-\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{[Z,Y]}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\zeta,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\rangle-\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\iota_{Y}\iota_{Z}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\zeta,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\rangle

\epsilon^{XY}\iota_{X}\iota_{Y}=\epsilon^{XY}\iota_{[X,Y]}=0
, eliminating many of the otherwise appearing terms.
Upon restricting to
\check{M}
, the first summand equates to

\displaystyle\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{[Z,Y]}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\zeta,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\rangle\big|_{\check{M}}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\zeta,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\otimes\Gamma(Z,Y)\rangle\big|_{\check{M}}

(191)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\zeta,\tilde{\gamma}\circ\gamma(Y)\rangle\big|_{\check{M}}

(192)

\displaystyle=-\langle\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\zeta,\tilde{\gamma}\circ\gamma\circ\tilde{\epsilon}\rangle\big|_{\check{M}}

(193)

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{n}\cdot D\!\!\!\!/\,^{a}(\chi|_{\check{M}})

(194)

by the preceding lemma. For the second summand, note that

\displaystyle-\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\iota_{Y}\iota_{Z}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\zeta,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\rangle\big|_{\check{M}}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\iota_{[Y,Z]}(f+\bar{\zeta}),\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\rangle\big|_{\check{M}}

(195)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{XY}\langle L_{X}\iota_{[Y,Z]}(f+\bar{\zeta}),\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\rangle\big|_{\check{M}}

(196)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{[X,[Y,Z]]}(f+\bar{\zeta})+\iota_{[Y,Z]}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{a}}\zeta,\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\rangle\big|_{\check{M}}

(197)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{XY}\langle\iota_{[X,[Y,Z]]}(f+\bar{\zeta}),\tilde{\gamma}(Z^{\vee})\rangle\big|_{\check{M}}+\sqrt{n}\cdot D\!\!\!\!/\,^{a}(\chi|_{\check{M}})

(198)

due to lemma 3, the Cartan relations and the fact that
\iota_{X}f=\iota_{X}\bar{\zeta}=0
as
\bar{\zeta}
is a
1|0,1
form, and upon restricting to the ordinary spacetime,
[X,[Y,Z]]
is supported on
\mathcal{F}^{1,0}|_{\check{M}}
. Adding up the remaining contributions, we obtain

\Delta^{\mathsf{a}}_{\epsilon}\chi\big|_{\check{M}}=2D\!\!\!\!/\,^{a}(\chi|_{\check{M}}),

(199)

as claimed.

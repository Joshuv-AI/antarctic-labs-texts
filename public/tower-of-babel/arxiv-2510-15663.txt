Gurevič pressure and equidistribution for amenable extensions of countable state Markov shifts Thanks: ©2025. This work is licensed by a CC BY license.
Richard Sharp

Abstract
. We obtain a weighted equidistribution theorem for amenable skew product extensions of countable state Markov shifts satisfying the BIP property. We also show, without requiring the BIP property, that Gurevič pressure for an amenable skew product agrees with the Gurevič pressure for the abelianized system. This had been proved by Dougall and Sharp in the case where the base is a subshift of finite type. The equality of Gurevič pressures is a key part of the proof of the equidistribution result.



1.
Introduction

It is well-known that, for mixing subshifts of finite type

\sigma:\Sigma\to\Sigma
(and hyperbolic systems more generally), weighted averages of periodic points
become equidistributed with respect to the Gibbs measure for the weighting potential, and this continues to hold
in the countable state case provided the so-called
big images and pre-images
(BIP) property
is satisfied.
Precisely, if
\delta_{x}
is the Dirac measure at
x
and

\tau_{x,n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\delta_{\sigma^{j}x}
then, for suitably regular
\varphi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
,
and writing

\varphi^{n}:=\varphi+\varphi\circ\sigma+\cdots\varphi\circ\sigma^{n-1},

the sequence of probability measures

\left(\sum_{\sigma^{n}x=x}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}\right)^{-1}\sum_{\sigma^{n}x=x}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}\tau_{x,n}

converges weakly to
\mu_{\varphi}
, the Gibbs measure for
\varphi
, as
n\to\infty
.
(For subshifts of finite type, see Bowen
[
2
]
when
\varphi=0
and Parry
[
25
]
,
Parry and Pollicott
[
26
]
for general Hölder continuous
\varphi
. The result
in
[
25
]
and
[
26
]
is given for flows
but the shift case follows directly by considering a constant suspension.
For the countable state case with BIP, the equidistribution result can be obtained from the stronger large deviation results in
Takahasi
[
35
]
.)
However, more interesting phenomena occur if we consider extensions of
\Sigma
by a (finitely generated, infinite)
countable group
G
and only consider those periodic points for
\sigma:\Sigma\to\Sigma

which correspond to periodic points in the extension.
More precisely, given
\psi:\Sigma\to G
, define the skew product extension

T_{\psi}:\Sigma\times G\to\Sigma\times G
by

T_{\psi}(x,g)=(\sigma x,g\psi(x)).

Then we consider points
x\in\Sigma
such that
T_{\psi}^{n}(x,e)=(x,e)
, i.e.
\sigma^{n}x=x
and

\psi_{n}(x)=e
, where
e
is the identity in
G

and

\psi_{n}(x):=\psi(x)\psi(\sigma x)\cdots\psi(\sigma^{n-1}x).

(Note
that
T_{\psi}^{n}(x,e)=(x,e)
if and only if
T_{\psi}^{n}(x,g)=(x,g)
for all
g\in G
.)
Given a (sufficiently nice)
function
\varphi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
, we might seek to understand the limiting behaviour of the measures

\mathfrak{M}_{n}:=\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{n}x=x\\
\psi_{n}(x)=e\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}\right)^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{n}x=x\\
\psi_{n}(x)=e\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}\tau_{x,n}

This problem was studied when
\Sigma
is a subshift of finite type and
G=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, in which case,
subject to a mixing condition on the extension,
the weak limit exists and is equal to the Gibbs measure for
\varphi+\langle\xi,\psi\rangle
, for some
(unique)
\xi=\xi(\varphi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the standard inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
).
This value
\xi
is characterised by minimizing the pressure function
P(\varphi+\langle w,\psi\rangle,\sigma)

over
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, or by the variational principle

h(\mu_{\varphi+\langle\xi,\psi\rangle})=\sup\left\{h(\nu)+\int\varphi\,d\nu\hbox{ : }\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\Sigma),\ \int\psi\,d\nu=0\right\},

where
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\Sigma)
is the set of
\sigma
-invariant Borel probability measures on
\Sigma

and
h(\nu)
is the measure-theoretic entropy of
\nu
with respect to
\sigma
.
In the technically more involved situation of Anosov flows and homology, the analogous
convergence statement for
\varphi=0
was proved by Sharp
[
32
]
(see also Lalley
[
20
]

and Babillot and Ledrappier
[
1
]
)
and for general Hölder continuous

\varphi
by Coles and Sharp
[
7
]
, and it is easy to recover the shift case from the results in these
papers.
It is now apparent that a version of this result should hold whenever
G
is amenable:
again a result for Anosov flows was proved by Dougall and Sharp
[
13
]

and one can adapt this to subshifts of finite type (indeed, the analysis in
[
13
]

proceeds via subshifts of finite type and symbolic dynamics to obtain the flow result).
In the amenable case, the limit measure is exactly the same as one would obtain if one abelianized the group
G
. More precisely, if
\overline{G}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is the torsion-free part of the abelianization of
G

and
\pi:G\to\overline{G}
is the projection homomorphism, then
the limiting measure is
\mu_{\varphi+\langle\xi,\bar{\psi}\rangle}
, where

\bar{\psi}:\Sigma\to\overline{G}
is defined by
\bar{\psi}=\pi\circ\psi
and

\xi=\xi(\varphi)
is as above. (If
d=0
then
\xi=0
and one simply obtains
\mu_{\varphi}
in the limit.)

Our first main result is to extend this equidistribution for amenable skew products to mixing countable state Markov shifts
satisfying BIP. Omitting some more technical hypotheses, we show that if

•

\sigma:\Sigma\to\Sigma
satisfies BIP;

•

G
is amenable;

•

T_{\psi}:\Sigma\times G\to\Sigma\times G
is topologically mixing; and

•

\varphi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
is locally Hölder continuous

then there exists
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\mathfrak{M}_{n}
converges to

\mu_{\varphi+\langle\xi,\bar{\psi}\rangle}
, as
n\to\infty
,
with respect to the weak
∗
topology on
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\Sigma)
.
The result is stated precisely as Theorem
6.1
.
The key technical assumption is that, writing
|\cdot|
for the
2
-norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(1.1)

\sup_{x\in\Sigma}\sum_{\sigma^{n}y=x}e^{\varphi^{n}(y)+r|\bar{\psi}^{n}(y)|}

is finite for some
r>0
– this is somewhat analogous to Cramér’s condition for random walks
(existence of an exponential moment) or to the entropy gap assumption
that appears in
[
3
]
and
[
17
]
, for example.
Of course, for a subshift of finite type, (
1.1
) is automatically finite for all
r>0
.

The equidistribution result is proved via large deviations, and to prove the latter, we need to show that,
when
G
is amenable, the Gurevič pressure
of
\varphi
with respect to the skew product
T_{\psi}
is equal to the Gurevič pressure
of
\varphi
with respect to the abelianized skew product
T_{\bar{\psi}}
(which, in turn, is equal to
the Gurevič pressure of
\varphi+\langle\xi,\bar{\psi}\rangle
with respect to
\sigma
).
The largest part of the paper is devoted to proving the first equality.
To motivate this, we briefly outline
some older results for random walks, spectral geometry and group theory, before returning to
dynamical questions.

A classical result of Kesten for symmetric random walks on countable groups is that the exponential rate of
return to the identity is equal to
1
if the group is amenable and is less than
1
otherwise
[
19
]
.
Variants of this amenability dichotomy were subsequently obtained in the settings of
the spectrum of the Laplacian for coverings of Riemannian manifolds

[
4
]
,
[
5
]
, group theory and graph theory

[
6
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
24
]

and critical exponents for discrete groups
[
8
]
,

[
9
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
28
]
,
[
33
]
.
The latter results also have a dynamical interpretation in terms of geodesic flows
(over manifolds with negative sectional curvatures) and, more generally, Anosov flows with a time-reversing
involution. In this setting, with time-reversal symmetry, one compares the growth rate of the number of periodic orbits
of the flow (which is equal to the topological entropy) with the corresponding quantity for the lift of
the flow to a regular cover: the growth rates agree if and only if the covering group is amenable.
For general Anosov flows, without this additional symmetry, the amenability dichotomy fails (even for abelian covers).
However, in
[
13
]
, Dougall and the author showed that a modified result holds, where the comparison is between growth rates for the given cover and the maximal abelian subcover:
these two quantities agree if and only if the covering group is amenable.
The argument in
[
13
]
(with some errors rectified in the associated correction)
used symbolic dynamics, where the Anosov flow is
coded by a suspension flow over a subshift of finite type. In particular, it boils down showing equality of Gurevič
pressure for a skew product extension associated to the covering group with the corresponding quantity for its abelianization.
Here we give a new proof, modelled on the argument given for random walks
in
[
14
]
, which works in the countable state case and which gives a cleaner proof.
(Both proofs have their roots in ideas of Roblin
[
28
]
and Stadlbauer
[
34
]
.)
We stress that, for the direction “amenability implies equality”
stated in Theorem
1.1
below, the shift
\sigma:\Sigma\to\Sigma
does not need to satisfy BIP.
(The recent paper
[
15
]
obtains the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
when the base is a full branch Gibbs–Markov map satisfying BIP.)

We now state this result in detail (although some definitions will be deferred until
section
2
).
Given a locally Hölder continuous function
\varphi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
, we have induced functions

\tilde{\varphi}:\Sigma\times G\to\mathbb{R}
and
\bar{\varphi}:\Sigma\times\overline{G}\to\mathbb{R}
, defined by

\tilde{\varphi}(x,\cdot)=\varphi(x)
and
\bar{\varphi}(x,\cdot)=\varphi(x)
, respectively.
To avoid over-cluttering formulae, we will slightly abuse notation by denoting all three functions

\varphi
,
\bar{\varphi}
and
\tilde{\varphi}
by
\varphi
.
We have the following inequalities for the (Gurevič) pressure of these functions:

(1.2)

P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\psi})\leq P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\bar{\psi}})\leq P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi+\langle w,\bar{\psi}\rangle,\sigma),

where the second inequality holds for all
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. (This will be justified in section
3
.)

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that

•

\sigma:\Sigma\to\Sigma
is a (one-sided)
countable state Markov shift,

•

G
is a countable group,

•

T_{\psi}:\Sigma\times G\to\Sigma\times G
be a topologically transitive skew-product extension,
where
\psi((x_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty})=\psi(x_{1})
, and

•

\varphi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
is a locally Hölder continuous function.

If
G
is amenable then

P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\psi})=P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\bar{\psi}})
.

Remarks 1.2
.

(i) Since
T_{\psi}:\Sigma\times G\to\Sigma\times G
is topologically transitive,
\sigma:\Sigma\to\Sigma
is also topologically transitive, so we do not need to include this as a separate assumption on the base.

(ii) If
\Sigma
satisfies BIP
and
\varphi
and
\psi
satisfy the same conditions needed for Theorem
6.1
,
then the converse of statement also holds:
if
P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\psi})=P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\bar{\psi}})
then
G
is amenable. This includes the case of subshifts of finite type (where the moment condition is automatic). The proof, which relies heavily on results
of Stadlbauer
[
33
]
is given in section
7
.

We will now outline the contents of the rest of the paper.
Section
2
and
section
3
provide background on countable state Markov shifts, Gurevič pressure and skew product extensions.
Section
4
contains the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Section
5
introduces the BIP property and makes precise the assumptions we shall impose in our
equidistribution results and section
6
contains the statement and proof of these results.
Finally, section
7
establishes the converse to Theorem
1.1
subject to the assumption of BIP.

2.
Countable state Markov shifts

Let
S
be a countable set and let
A
be a matrix indexed by
S\times S
with
entries in
\{0,1\}
. We call
S
the set of states and
A
the transition matrix.
We write
A(s,s^{\prime})
for the
(s,s^{\prime})
entry of
A
.
We then let

\Sigma=\{x=(x_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}\hbox{ : }x_{n}\in S\mbox{ and }A(x_{n},x_{n+1})=1\ \forall n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\}.

Let
\sigma:\Sigma\to\Sigma
be the shift map defined by

(\sigma x)_{n}=x_{n+1}
. Then we call
(\Sigma,\sigma)

a (one-sided) countable state Markov shift. If
S
is finite, then
(\Sigma,\sigma)
is called a (one-sided) subshift of finite type.

A word
w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})\in S^{n}
is called
allowed
if
A(w_{i},w_{i+1})=1
for all
i=1,\ldots,n-1

and we call
[w]=\{x\in\Sigma\hbox{ : }x_{i}=w_{i},\ \forall i=1,\ldots,n\}
the cylinder defined by
w
.
Let

\mathcal{W}_{n}\subset S^{n}
denote the set of all allowed words of length
n
and let

\mathcal{W}:=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{W}_{n}
.
For
a\in S
, we write

\mathcal{W}_{n}(a):=\{w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})\in\mathcal{W}_{n}\hbox{ : }w_{1}=a\mbox{ and }A(w_{n},a)=1\}.

If
w
is a finite work and
x
is either a finite word or element of
\Sigma
the we let
wx
denote
the concatenation.

The topology on
\Sigma
is the topology generated by cylinder sets. The shift map is continuous and
we havre that
\Sigma
is compact if and only if
S
is finite.
We say that
\sigma:\Sigma\to\Sigma
is
topologically transitive
if there is a
point with a dense orbit.
We say that

\sigma:\Sigma\to\Sigma
is
topologically mixing

if for all
a,b\in S
there exists
N(a,b)

such that for all
n\geq N(a,b)
there exists
w\in\mathcal{W}_{n}
with
awb\in\mathcal{W}
.
(This is equivalent to the usual definition of topological mixing in topological dynamical systems.)

We say that
\varphi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
is
locally Hölder continuous
if

\sup_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{n}}\sup_{x,y\in[w]}|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|\leq C\theta^{n},

for some
C>0
and
0<\theta<1
, for all
n\geq 1
.
If we wishe to specify
\theta
then we say that
\varphi
is

\theta
-locally Hölder continuous.
We write

\varphi^{n}(x):=\varphi(x)+\varphi(\sigma x)+\cdots+\varphi(\sigma^{n-1}x).

A locally Hölder function satisfies the following estimates.

Lemma 2.1

(Lemma 1 of
[
29
]
)
.

Let
\varphi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
be locally Hölder continuous.
Then, for all
a\in S
and all
o,o^{\prime}\in[a]
there exists
B>0
such that

|\varphi^{n}(wo)-\varphi^{n}(wo^{\prime})|\leq B

for all
w\in\mathcal{W}_{n}
such that
wa\in\mathcal{W}_{n+1}
.

Assume that
\sigma
is topologically transitive and that

\varphi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
is locally Hölder continuous. Then we can define the
Gurevič pressure

P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,\sigma)\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
by

P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,\sigma)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{\sigma^{n}x=x}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]}(x),

for any choice of
a\in S

[
29
]
.
If
\sigma
is topologically mixing, then the limsup may be replaced by a limit.

The transfer operator
L_{\varphi}
acts on real-valued function on
\Sigma
by the formula

(L_{\varphi}v)(x)=\sum_{\sigma y=x}e^{\varphi(y)}v(y)

(which may be infinite).
We say that
\varphi
is
summable

if
\|L_{\varphi}1\|_{\infty}<\infty
.
For later use, we note that, using the local Hölder continuity of
\varphi
, this is equivalent to the condition

\sum_{a\in S}\exp\left(\sup\{\varphi(y)\hbox{ : }y\in[a]\}\right)<\infty

used in
[
18
]
,
[
21
]
and
[
22
]
.
If
\varphi
is summable then
P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,\sigma)<\infty
.

We may also characterize the Gurevič pressure directly in terms of the transfer operator.

Lemma 2.2

(Theorem 4.4 of
[
31
]
)
.

If
P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,\sigma)<\infty
and
v:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous, non-negative, not identically zero
and with support contained inside a finite union of cylinders then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log(L_{\varphi}^{n}v)(x)=P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,\sigma)

for all
x\in\Sigma
.
If the shift is topologically mixing then the limsup may be replaced by a limit.

3.
Skew product extension

We now consider skew product extensions.
Let
G
be a finitely generated countable group (with the discrete topology), with identity
e
.
Suppose we are given a function
\psi:\Sigma\to G
with
\psi((x_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty})=\psi(x_{1})
.
(Any continuous function from
\Sigma
to
G
is locally constant and can be reduced to this form
by recoding.)
Then we can define the skew production extension

T_{\psi}:\Sigma\times G\to\Sigma\times G

by

T_{\psi}(x,g)=(\sigma x,g\psi(x)).

Note that

T_{\psi}^{n}(x,g)=(\sigma^{n}x,g\psi_{n}(x)),

where

\psi_{n}(x)=\psi(x)\psi(\sigma x)\cdots\psi(\sigma^{n-1}x).

We will always assume that
T_{\psi}
is topologically transitive.

The system

T_{\psi}:\Sigma\times G\to\Sigma\times G
is also a countable state Markov shift with set of states

S\times G
and a transition matrix
\mathbb{A}
defined by

\mathbb{A}((i,g),(j,h))=1\quad\iff\quad A(i,j)=1\mbox{ and }g\psi(i,j)=h.

Thus, Gurevič pressure is also defined for the extension.

Given an extension with group
G
, we will also need to consider the abelianized system
as follows.
Let
G^{\mathrm{ab}}
denote the abelianization of
G
and let
\overline{G}
denote the torsion-free part of
G^{\mathrm{ab}}
, i.e.
\overline{G}
is the quotient of
G^{\mathrm{ab}}
by its torsion subgroup.
Then
\overline{G}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, for some
d\geq 0
.
We use additive notation and let

0
to denote the identity.
Let
\pi:G\to\overline{G}
be the projection homomorphism.
Define
\bar{\psi}:\Sigma\to\overline{G}
by
\bar{\psi}=\pi\circ\psi
;
then we have the skew product
T_{\bar{\psi}}:\Sigma\times\overline{G}\to\Sigma\times\overline{G}
.
(If
d=0
then we can regard
T_{\bar{\psi}}
as
\sigma
.)

Given
\varphi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
, define
\tilde{\varphi}:\Sigma\times G\to\mathbb{R}
by

\tilde{\varphi}(x,g)=\varphi(x)
and

\bar{\varphi}:\Sigma\times\overline{G}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\bar{\varphi}(x,k)=\varphi(x)
. Following the convention in the introduction,
we write all of these functions as
\varphi
.
It is immediate from the definition of Gurevič pressure that

P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\psi})\leq P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\bar{\psi}})\leq P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,\sigma),

Furthermore, if
T_{\bar{\psi}}^{n}(x,m)=(x,m)
then
\sigma^{n}x=x
and
\bar{\psi}^{n}(x)=0
, so we see that

P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\bar{\psi}})=P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi+\langle w,\bar{\psi}\rangle,T_{\bar{\psi}}),

for all
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Together, these imply the inequalities (
1.2
).

Each of the skew products has its own transfer operator (associated to
\varphi
),
which we distinguish by using different fonts, as follows:

(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}v)(x,g):=\sum_{T_{\psi}(y,h)=(x,g)}e^{\varphi(y)}v(y,h)=\sum_{\sigma y=x}e^{\varphi(y)}v(y,g\psi(y)^{-1})

and

(\mathfrak{L}_{\varphi}v)(x,m):=\sum_{T_{\bar{\psi}}(y,k)=(x,m)}e^{\varphi(y)}v(y,k)=\sum_{\sigma y=x}e^{\varphi(y)}v(y,m-\bar{\psi}(y))

The formulae for iterates are

(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n}v)(x,g)=\sum_{T_{\psi}^{n}(y,h)=(x,g)}e^{\varphi^{n}(y)}v(y,h)=\sum_{\sigma^{n}y=x}e^{\varphi^{n}(y)}v(y,g\psi_{n}(y)^{-1})

and

(\mathfrak{L}_{\varphi}^{n}v)(x,m)=\sum_{T_{\bar{\psi}}^{n}(z,k)=(x,m)}e^{\varphi(y)}v(z,k)=\sum_{\sigma y=x}e^{\varphi(y)}v(y,m-\bar{\psi}_{n}(y)).

4.
Amenability implies equality

In the section we prove Theorem
1.1
. Since we know from (
1.2
) that

P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\psi})\leq P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\bar{\psi}})
, we only
need to prove that
P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\bar{\psi}})\leq P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\psi})
.

Fix
a\in S
; for
o\in[a]
and
g\in G
, we will work with the quantity

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{n}x=o\\
x_{1}=a\\
\psi_{n}(x)=g\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{n}y=x\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(y)}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}}(x,g\psi_{n}(x)^{-1})=(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(o,g).

Since
T_{\psi}
is topologically transitive, it follows from Lemma

2.2
that

(4.1)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(o,g)=P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\psi}).

To simplify the notation, write
\rho=e^{P_{\mathrm{G}}(\tilde{\varphi},T_{\psi})}
. Define

\eta_{o,g}(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}t^{-n}(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(o,g).

This converges for
t>\rho
but may either diverge or converge at
t=\rho
. In the latter case, we need to modify the series so that it diverges at
t=\rho
. We use the following standard lemma.

Lemma 4.1

(Lemma 3.2 of
[
10
]
)
.

Let
a_{n}
be a sequence of positive real numbers such that
\limsup_{n\to\infty}a_{n}^{1/n}=\rho
.
Then there is a sequence
b_{n}
of positive real numbers such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}b_{n+1}/b_{n}=1
,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}b_{n}t^{-n}
converges for all
t>\rho
, but

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}b_{n}\rho^{-n}
diverges.

Let
b_{n}
be the sequence given by the lemma corresponding
to

a_{n}=(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(o,e)

and write

c_{n}=1/b_{n}
. Let

\zeta_{o,g}(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}t^{-n}\frac{1}{c_{n}}(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(o,g).

If
o^{\prime}\in[a]
then the is a natural bijection between the pre-images
of
o
and
o^{\prime}
, so we can compare
\zeta_{o,g}(t)
and
\zeta_{o^{\prime},g}(t)
term-by-term.
Using Lemma
2.1
, for all
g\in G
and
t>\rho
,

(4.2)

e^{-B}\zeta_{o,g}(t)\leq\zeta_{o^{\prime},g}(t)\leq e^{B}\zeta_{o,g}(t)

and so the same sequence

c_{n}
works for all
o\in[a]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\rho^{\prime}>\rho
.
For each
a\in S
and each
g\in G
,

0<\inf_{o\in[a]}\inf_{\rho<t\leq\rho^{\prime}}\frac{\zeta_{o,g}(t)}{\zeta_{o,e}(t)}\leq\sup_{o\in[a]}\sup_{\rho<t\leq\rho^{\prime}}\frac{\zeta_{o,g}(t)}{\zeta_{o,e}(t)}<\infty.

Proof.

We begin by observing that, for every
g,h\in G
, we have

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n+k}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(o,g)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{k}z=o\\
z_{1}=a\\
\psi_{k}(z)=h^{-1}g\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{k}(z)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{n}x=z\\
x_{1}=a\\
\psi_{n}(x)=h\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{k}z=o\\
z_{1}=a\\
\psi_{k}(z)=h^{-1}g\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{k}(z)}[(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(z,h)]

and (due to the transitivity of
T_{\psi}
) we can find a
k\geq 1
so that the right
hand side is positive. Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\zeta_{o,g}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{k}\frac{t^{-n}}{c_{n}}(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{m}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(o,g)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{-(n+k)}}{c_{n+k}}(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n+k}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(o,g)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{m=1}^{k}\frac{t^{-n}}{c_{n}}(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{m}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(o,g)

\displaystyle+t^{-k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{k}z=o\\
z_{1}=a\\
\psi_{k}(z)=h^{-1}g\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{k}(z)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{-n}}{c_{n}}\frac{c_{n}}{c_{n+k}}[(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(z,h)].

Since the numbers
c_{n}
are positive and, for each fixed
k
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}c_{n}/c_{n+k}=1
, we have

\inf_{n\geq 1}c_{n}/c_{n+k}>0
.
Furthermore,

\inf_{o\in[a]}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{k}z=o\\
z_{1}=a\\
\psi_{k}(z)=h^{-1}g\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{k}(z)}>0.

Hence, for
t\in(\rho,\rho^{\prime}]
, we have

\zeta_{o,g}(t)\geq C_{1}(g,k,a)+\frac{C_{2}(g,h,k,a)}{C_{3}(k)}\zeta_{o,h}(t),

for positive
C_{1},C_{2}
and
C_{3}
.
We conclude that

\inf_{\rho<t\leq\rho^{\prime}}\frac{\zeta_{o,g}(t)}{\zeta_{o,h}(t)}>0.

Since
g
and
h
are arbitrary, we have

0<\inf_{\rho<t\leq\rho^{\prime}}\frac{\zeta_{o,g}(t)}{\zeta_{o,e}(t)}\leq\sup_{\rho<t\leq\rho^{\prime}}\frac{\zeta_{o,g}(t)}{\zeta_{o,e}(t)}<\infty.

Using (
4.2
), the bounds can be made uniform over
o\in[a]
.
∎

Define

\mathcal{Z}=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\{z\in\Sigma\hbox{ : }\sigma^{n}z=o\}

the set of pre-images of
o
;
this is a
countable subset of
\Sigma
.
By a standard diagonal argument, we can find a sequence
t_{n}\downarrow\rho
such that
the following limit exists and lies in
(0,\infty)
for all
(z,g)\in\mathcal{Z}\times G
:

\Phi(z,g):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\zeta_{z,g}(t_{n})}{\zeta_{z,e}(t_{n})}.

Furthermore, for all
g\in G
,

(4.3)

0<\inf_{z\in\mathcal{Z}\cap[a]}\Phi(z,g)\leq\sup_{z\in\mathcal{Z}\cap[a]}\Phi(z,g)<\infty.

Lemma 4.3
.

For all
z\in\mathcal{Z}\cap[a]
and all
n\geq 1
,

(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n}\Phi)(z,g)=\rho^{n}\Phi(z,g).

Proof.

To simplify notation, we shall write the proof for the case
z=o
and note that, using
(
4.2
), the same argument holds for general
z\in\mathcal{Z}\cap[a]
.
Fix
k\geq 1
and let
\epsilon>0
. Since

\lim_{n\to\infty}c_{n-k}/c_{n}=1
, we can choose
n_{0}
such that

1-\epsilon\leq c_{n-k}/c_{h}\leq 1+\epsilon
, for all

n\geq n_{0}
.
We will also use that

(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(o,g)=\sum_{\sigma^{k}z=o}e^{\varphi^{k}(z)}\,(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n-k}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(z,g\psi_{k}(z)^{-1}).

Then, for
n\geq n_{0}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1-\epsilon}{c_{n-k}}\sum_{\sigma^{k}z=o}e^{\varphi^{k}(z)}\,(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n-k}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(z,g\psi_{k}(z)^{-1})\leq\frac{1}{c_{n}}(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(o,g)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\epsilon}{c_{n-k}}\sum_{\sigma^{k}z=o}e^{\varphi^{k}(z)}\,(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n-k}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(z,g\psi_{k}(z)^{-1}).

Setting

C_{1}(g,t,n_{0})=\sum_{n=1}^{n_{0}}\frac{t^{-n}}{c_{n}}(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(o,g),

we have

\displaystyle t^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{-n}}{c_{n}}(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(o,g)\leq C_{1}(g,t,n_{0})

\displaystyle+t^{k}(1+\epsilon)\sum_{\sigma^{k}z=o}e^{\varphi^{k}(z)}\,\sum_{n=n_{0}+1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{-n}}{c_{n-k}}(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n-k}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(z,g\psi_{k}(z)^{-1})

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(g,t,n_{0})+(1+\epsilon)\sum_{\sigma^{k}z=o}e^{\varphi^{k}(z)}\Phi(z,g\psi_{k}(z)^{-1})\zeta_{o,e}(t).

This gives

\displaystyle\rho^{k}\Phi(o,g)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{C_{1}(g,t_{m},n_{0})}{\zeta_{e}(t_{m})}+(1+\epsilon)\sum_{\sigma^{k}z=o}e^{\varphi^{k}(z)}\Phi(z,g\psi_{k}(z)^{-1})

\displaystyle=(1+\epsilon)\sum_{\sigma^{k}z=o}e^{\varphi^{k}(z)}\Phi(z,g\psi_{k}(z)^{-1})

and, since
\epsilon
is arbitrary,

\displaystyle\rho^{k}\Phi(o,g)\leq\sum_{\sigma^{k}z=o}e^{\varphi^{k}(z)}\Phi(z,g\psi_{k}(z)^{-1})

For the lower bound, we have

\displaystyle t^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{-n}}{c_{n}}(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(o,g)

\displaystyle\geq(1-\epsilon)\sum_{\sigma^{k}z=o}e^{\varphi^{k}(z)}\sum_{n=n_{0}+1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{-(n-k)}}{c_{n-k}}(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n-k}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(z,g\psi_{k}(z)^{-1})

\displaystyle=(1-\epsilon)\sum_{\sigma^{k}z=o}e^{\varphi^{k}(z)}\sum_{n=n_{0}+k+1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{-n}}{c_{n}}(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(z,g\psi_{k}(z)^{-1}),

which gives

\rho^{k}\Phi(o,g)\geq(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{k}\Phi)(o,g).

∎

Corollary 4.4
.

For all
(z,\gamma)\in\mathcal{Z}\times G
, we have

0<\inf_{g\in G}\frac{\Phi(z,g\gamma^{-1})}{\Phi(o,g)}\leq\sup_{g\in G}\frac{\Phi(z,g\gamma^{-1})}{\Phi(o,g)}<\infty.

Proof.

Here we again use the topological transitivity of
T_{\psi}
.
Given
(z,\gamma)\in\mathcal{Z}\times G
, we can find
k\geq 1

such that
\sigma^{k}z=o^{\prime}\in[a]
and
\psi_{k}(z)=\gamma
. This gives us

\displaystyle e^{\varphi^{k}(z)}\Phi(z,g\gamma^{-1})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\sigma^{k}y=o^{\prime}}e^{\varphi^{k}(y)}\Phi(y,g\psi_{k}(y)^{-1})

\displaystyle=\rho^{k}\Phi(o^{\prime},g)\leq e^{2B}\rho^{k}\Phi(o,g).

Hence,

\sup_{g\in G}\Phi(z,g\gamma^{-1})/\Phi(o,g)
is finite.

Next we find
x\in[a]
and
k\geq 1
such that
\sigma^{k}x=z
and
\psi_{k}(x)=\gamma^{-1}
.
Then

\displaystyle e^{-2B}\epsilon^{\varphi^{k}(x)}\Phi(o,g)

\displaystyle\leq e^{\varphi^{k}(x)}\Phi(x,g)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\sigma^{k}y=o^{\prime}}e^{\varphi^{k}(y)}\Phi(y,g\gamma^{-1}\psi_{k}(y)^{-1})=\rho^{k}\Phi(z,g\gamma^{-1}).

Hence,

\inf_{g\in G}\Phi(z,g\gamma^{-1})/\Phi(o,g)
is positive.
∎

We are now able to use the amenability of
G
to replace
\Phi
with a function
\phi:\mathcal{Z}\times G\to\mathbb{R}^{>0}
which, after normalization, is a homomorphism in the second factor. This will allow us to descend to
\overline{G}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

There is a function
\phi:\mathcal{Z}\times G\to\mathbb{R}^{>0}
such that

(i)

for all
g\in G
and
n\geq 1
,

(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n}\phi)(o,g)\leq\rho^{n}\phi(o,g),

(ii)

for all
z\in\mathcal{Z}
, the function

\gamma\mapsto\frac{\phi(z,\gamma)}{\phi(z,e)}

is a homomorphism from
G
to
\mathbb{R}^{>0}
.

Furthermore, for all
a\in S
and all
\gamma\in G
, we have

\inf_{z\in\mathcal{Z}\cap[a]}\phi(z,\gamma)>0.

Proof.

The construction of the function
\phi
uses the amenability of
G
.
Let
M
be a right-invariant Banach mean on
\ell^{\infty}(G)
. By Jensen’s inequality, if
\alpha
is convex
and
f\in\ell^{\infty}(G)

then

M(\alpha(f))\geq\alpha(M(f)).

We apply this to the functions

f_{z,\gamma}(g)=\log\frac{\Phi(z,g\gamma^{-1})}{\Phi(o,g)},

where
(z,\gamma)\in\mathcal{Z}\times G
.
It follows from Corollary
4.4
that
f_{z,\gamma}\in\ell^{\infty}(G)
, for all

(z,\gamma)\in\mathcal{Z}\times G
.
Thus, we have

M\left(g\mapsto\frac{\Phi(z,g\gamma^{-1})}{\Phi(o,g)}\right)=M\left(g\mapsto\exp\log\frac{\Phi(z,g\gamma^{-1})}{\Phi(o,g)}\right)\geq\exp M(f_{z,\gamma}).

Now define
\phi:\mathcal{Z}\times G\to\mathbb{R}^{>0}
by

\phi(z,\gamma)=\exp M(f_{z,\gamma}).

We will first prove that the inequality in part (i) holds. We need a little care as
M
is only finitely additive. Thus, we work with finite subsets of
G
that exhaust
G
.
Let
\{g_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be an enumeration of
G
and, for any
N\geq 1
, let

G_{N}=\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{N}\}
. Then Lemma
4.3
gives us

\displaystyle\rho^{n}

\displaystyle=M\left(g\mapsto\frac{(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n}\Phi)(o,g)}{\Phi(o,g)}\right)=M\left(g\mapsto\frac{1}{\Phi(o,g)}\sum_{\sigma^{n}z=o}e^{\varphi^{n}(z)}\Phi(z,g\psi_{n}(z)^{-1})\right)

\displaystyle\geq M\left(g\mapsto\frac{1}{\Phi(o,g)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{n}z=o\\
\psi_{n}(z)\in G_{N}\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(z)}\Phi(z,g\psi_{n}(z)^{-1})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{n}z=o\\
\psi_{n}(z)\in G_{N}\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(z)}M\left(g\mapsto\frac{\Phi(z,g\psi_{n}(z)^{-1})}{\Phi(o,g)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{n}z=o\\
\psi_{n}(z)\in G_{N}\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(z)}\exp M\left(g\mapsto\log\frac{\Phi(z,g\psi_{n}(z)^{-1})}{\Phi(o,g)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{n}z=o\\
\psi_{n}(z)\in G_{N}\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(z)}\phi(z,g\psi_{n}(z)^{-1}).

Taking the supremum over
N
gives the inequality in (i).

Next we show that part (ii) holds.
First note that we have

\displaystyle\log\phi(o,\gamma\delta)

\displaystyle=M\left(g\mapsto\log\frac{\Phi(o,g\delta^{-1}\gamma^{-1})}{\Phi(o,g)}\right)

\displaystyle=M\left(g\mapsto\log\frac{\Phi(o,g\delta^{-1}\gamma^{-1}}{\Phi(o,g\delta^{-1})}\right)+M\left(g\mapsto\log\frac{\Phi(o,g\delta^{-1})}{\Phi(o,g)}\right)

\displaystyle=\phi(o,\gamma)+\phi(o,\delta)

and

\displaystyle\log\phi(o,\gamma^{-1})

\displaystyle=M\left(g\mapsto\log\frac{\Phi(o,g\gamma)}{\Phi(o,g)}\right)=M\left(g\mapsto\log\frac{\Phi(o,g)}{\Phi(o,g\gamma^{-1})}\right)

\displaystyle=M\left(g\mapsto-\log\frac{\Phi(o,g\gamma^{-1})}{\Phi(o,g)}\right)=-\log\phi(o,\gamma).

Hence
\gamma\mapsto\phi(o,\gamma)
is a homomorphism.
Next, we observe that

\displaystyle\log\phi(z,\gamma)

\displaystyle=M\left(g\mapsto\log\frac{\Phi(z,g\gamma)}{\Phi(o,g)}\right)

\displaystyle=M\left(g\mapsto\log\frac{\Phi(o,g\gamma)}{\Phi(o,g)}\right)+M\left(g\mapsto\log\frac{\Phi(z,g\gamma)}{\Phi(o,g\gamma)}\right)

\displaystyle=M\left(g\mapsto\log\frac{\Phi(o,g\gamma)}{\Phi(o,g)}\right)+M\left(g\mapsto\log\frac{\Phi(z,g)}{\Phi(o,g)}\right)

\displaystyle=\log\phi(o,\gamma)+\log\phi(z,e).

Hence

\gamma\mapsto\frac{\phi(z,\gamma)}{\phi(z,e)}=\phi(o,\gamma)

is a homomorphism.

To finish, we note that, by Corollary
4.4
,
\phi(z,\gamma)>0
and
one easily sees that this positive lower bound can be taken uniformly in
z\in\mathcal{Z}\cap[a]
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

There is a function
\bar{\phi}:\mathcal{Z}\times\overline{G}\to\mathbb{R}^{>0}

satisfying

(\mathfrak{L}_{\varphi}^{n}\bar{\phi})(o,g)\leq\rho^{n}\bar{\phi}(o,g),

for all
n\geq 1
, and,
for all
a\in S
and all
m\in\overline{G}
, we have

\inf_{z\in\mathcal{Z}\cap[a]}\bar{\phi}(z,m)>0.

Proof.

By part (ii) of Lemma
4.5
, for each
z\in\mathcal{Z}
,
there is a homomorphism

\Upsilon_{z}:\overline{G}\to\mathbb{R}^{>0}
such that

(\Upsilon_{z}\circ\pi)(\gamma)=\frac{\phi(z,\gamma)}{\phi(z,e)}.

We deduce that if
\pi(\gamma)=\pi(\delta)
then
\phi(z,\gamma)=\phi(z,\delta)
,
and we define
\bar{\phi}:\mathcal{Z}\times\overline{G}\to\mathbb{R}^{>0}
by

\bar{\phi}(z,m)=\phi(z,\gamma)

for any
\gamma\in G
satisfying
\pi(\gamma)=m
.

We then have

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{L}_{\varphi}^{n}\bar{\phi})(o,0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma^{n}z=o}e^{\varphi^{n}(z)}\bar{\phi}(z,\bar{\psi}_{n}(z)^{-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma^{n}z=o}e^{\varphi^{n}(z)}\phi(z,\psi_{n}(z)^{-1})

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n}\phi)(o,e)\leq\rho^{n}\phi(o,e)=\rho^{n}\bar{\phi}(o,0).

The final statement follows from Lemma
4.5
and the definition
of
\bar{\phi}
.
∎

We now complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Let

c=\inf_{z\in\mathcal{Z}\cap[a]}\bar{\phi}(z,0)>0.

Using Lemma
2.2
, we have that

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\bar{\psi}})

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log(\mathfrak{L}_{\varphi}^{n}(c\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{0\}}))(o,0)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log(\mathfrak{L}_{\varphi}^{n}\bar{\phi})(o,0)\leq\rho,

as required.

Remark 4.7
.

We could reformulate Theorem
1.1
and the above proof in terms of the skew product

T_{\psi^{\mathrm{ab}}}:\Sigma\times G^{\mathrm{ab}}\to\Sigma\times G^{\mathrm{ab}}
without significantly changing the argument.
We observe that
\langle\xi,\cdot\rangle
represents a choice of homomorphism from
\overline{G}
to
\mathbb{R}
and
there is a natural isomorphism between
\mathrm{Hom}(\overline{G},\mathbb{R})
and
\mathrm{Hom}(G^{\mathrm{ab}},\mathbb{R})
.
Then
\langle\xi,\bar{\psi}\rangle
is replaced by
\chi\circ\psi^{\mathrm{ab}}
, for an appropriate
choice of
\chi\in\mathrm{Hom}(G^{\mathrm{ab}},\mathbb{R})
.

5.
The BIP property and skew product extensions

We now introduce the stronger condition on
\Sigma
required for the equidistibution result we described
in the introduction.
Let
\sigma:\Sigma\to\Sigma
be a countable state Markov shift.
We say that
\sigma:\Sigma\to\Sigma
satisfies the
big images and pre-images
(BIP) property if
there exist
b_{1},\ldots,b_{N}\in S
such that, for all
a\in S
, there exist

i,j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
with
A(b_{i},a)=1
and
A(a,b_{j})=1
.
Note that topologically transitive subshifts of finite type trivially satisfy this condition and, indeed,
BIP shifts share many thermodynamic properties with subshifts of finite type, in particular the existence of Gibbs measures.
(The terminology BIP appears in, for example, the work of Sarig.
If
\sigma:\Sigma\to\Sigma
is topologically mixing then BIP
is equivalent to the finite primitivity condition
used by Mauldin and Urbański
[
21
]
,
[
22
]
. Thus, we can cite results from both sets of authors.)

Let
\varphi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
be a locally Hölder continuous function.
We say that a
\sigma
-invariant probability measure
\mu
on
\Sigma
is a

Gibbs measure
for
\varphi
if there are constants
A,B
and
P

(depending only on
\varphi
) such that

A\leq\frac{\mu([w])}{e^{\varphi^{n}(x)-nP}}\leq B

for all
w\in\mathcal{W}_{n}
, for all
n\geq 1
and all
x\in[w]
.
If
\varphi
has a Gibbs measure then
P=P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,\sigma)
.

From now on, we assume that
\sigma:\Sigma\to\Sigma
is topologically mixing and satisfies BIP.
Let
\varphi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
be locally Hölder continuous and summable, so that

P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,\sigma)
is finite. Then
\varphi
has a unique Gibbs measure, which we denote by
\mu_{\varphi}
, and
P=P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi)
(Theorem 1
of
[
30
]
) .
We also have a characterization of Gurevič pressure in terms of periodic points:

(5.1)

P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,\sigma)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{\sigma^{n}x=x}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}

(Corollary 1 of
[
30
]
). (Note that the equality (
5.1
) may fail
in the absence of the BIP property.)

In the presence of BIP, we also have stronger results for our transfer operators, acting
on an appropriate Banach space.
Let
\mathcal{F}_{\theta}^{b}
denote the space of bounded
\theta
-locally Hölder continuous functions

v:\Sigma\to\mathbb{C}
with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\theta}=\|\cdot\|_{\infty}+|\cdot|_{\theta}
,
where

|v|_{\theta}=\sup_{n\geq 1}\sup_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{n}}\sup_{x,y\in[w]}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|}{\theta^{n}}.

If
\varphi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
is
summable
then

L_{\varphi}:\mathcal{F}_{\theta}^{b}\to\mathcal{F}_{\theta}^{b}
is a well-defined bounded
linear operator (Lemma 2.4.1 of
[
22
]
).

We have the following analyticity result.

Lemma 5.1

(Theorem 2.6.13 and Proposition 2.6.13 of
[
22
]
)
.

Let
\sigma:\Sigma\to\Sigma
be topologically mixing and satisfy BIP and let
\varphi,\theta:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
be weakly Hölder continuous. Suppose there exists
\epsilon>0
such
that
\varphi+t\theta
is summable for
|t|<\epsilon
. Then
t\mapsto P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi+t\theta,\sigma)
is real analytic on
(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
and

\frac{dP_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi+t\theta,\sigma)}{dt}\Bigg|_{t=0}=\int\theta\,d\mu_{\varphi}.

We need an analogue of equation (
5.1
) for
T_{\psi}
.
If

T_{\psi}:\Sigma\times G\to\Sigma\times G
is mixing then
we have the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\sigma:\Sigma\to\Sigma
be topologically mixing and satisfy BIP and, furthermore,
let
T_{\psi}:\Sigma\times G\to\Sigma\times G
be topologically mixing.
Then we have

P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\psi})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{n}x=x\\
\psi_{n}(x)=e\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}.

Proof.

It is immediate from the definition of Gurevič pressure that

P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\psi})\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{n}x=x\\
\psi_{n}(x)=e\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}.

For the other direction, we adapt the proof of Theorem 1 in
[
30
]
.
Given
w\in\mathcal{W}_{n}
, let

\varphi^{n}_{+}(w)=\sup\{\varphi^{n}(y)\hbox{ : }y\in[w]\}
.
Now fix
a\in S
. By the mixing of
T_{\psi}
,
there exists
n_{0}\geq 1
and, for
i=1,\ldots,N
,
w_{a,b_{i}},w_{b_{i},a}\in\mathcal{W}_{n_{0}}

such that

aw_{a,b_{i}}b_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{n_{0}+2}
,
b_{i}w_{b_{i},a}a\in\mathcal{W}_{n_{0}+2}
,

\psi_{n_{0}}(w_{a,b_{i}})=e
and
\psi_{n_{0}}(w_{b_{i},a})=e
.
By the BIP property, given
w\in\mathcal{W}_{n}
, there exists
i,j\in\{i,\ldots,N\}
such that

b_{i}wb_{j}\in\mathcal{W}_{n+2}
.
Hence

aw_{a,b_{i}}b_{i}wb_{j}w_{b_{j},a}a\in\mathcal{W}_{n+M+1},

where
M=2n_{0}+3
, and concatenating
aw_{a,b_{i}}b_{i}wb_{j}w_{b_{j},a}
gives a periodic point
of period
n+M
.
Furthermore, if
\psi_{n}(w)=e
then

|\psi_{n+M}(aw_{a,b_{i}}b_{i}wb_{j}w_{b_{j},a})|

is bounded independently of
n
.
We conclude that there exists
n_{1}\geq 1
and
C>0
so that

(5.2)

\sum_{|g|\leq n_{1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{n+M}x=x\\
\psi_{n+M}(x)=g\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n+M}(x)}\geq C\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\mathcal{W}_{n}\\
\psi_{n}(w)=e\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}_{+}(w)}\geq C(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]\times\{e\}})(o,g),

for any choice of
o\in[a]
.

Now, the mixing of
T_{\psi}
implies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{n}x=x\\
\psi_{n}(x)=g\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}

is independent of
g\in G
. Combining this with (
5.2
), we see that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{n}x=x\\
\psi_{n}(x)=e\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}\geq P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\psi}),

as required.
∎

We now wish to impose more conditions on
\psi
and
\varphi
.
As above, let
\sigma:\Sigma\to\Sigma
be mixing and satisfy BIP and let
\varphi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
be a locally
Hölder function which is summable.
Let
G
be a finitely generated group and let
\psi:\Sigma\to G
satisfy

\psi(x)=\psi(x_{1})
.
To simplify notation, we identify
\bar{\psi}:\Sigma\to\overline{G}
with a function

f:\Sigma\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Let

\delta(\varphi,f):=\sup\{r\geq 0\hbox{ : }L_{\varphi+r|f|}\mbox{ is summable}\},

where
|\cdot|
denotes the
2
-norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We impose two assumptions on
\varphi

and
\psi
.

Assumption (I)
:

T_{\psi}:\Sigma\times G\to\Sigma\times G
is topologically mixing.

Assumption (II)
:

\delta(\varphi,f)>0
.

Assumption (I) immediately implies that
T_{f}:\Sigma\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\Sigma\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is also topologically mixing. Furthermore, Assumption (I) implies that
f
is
aperiodic
:
if
e^{2\pi i\langle t,f(x)\rangle}=zu(\sigma x)/u(x)
for all
x\in\Sigma
, where
t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

z\in\mathrm{U}(1)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\hbox{ : }|z|=1\}
and
u\in C(\Sigma,\mathrm{U}(1))
, then

t=0\in\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
z=1
.
If Assumption (II) holds then we easily see that
\varphi+\langle w,f\rangle
is summable,

L_{\varphi+\langle w,f\rangle}:\mathcal{F}_{\theta}^{b}\to\mathcal{F}_{\theta}^{b}
is a well-defined bounded linear operator and

\mathfrak{p}(w):=P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi+\langle w,f\rangle,\sigma)

is finite for
|w|<\delta(\varphi,f)
.

We also need to consider the transfer operator with complex potentials. Write
\delta=\delta(\varphi,f)

and let

B(\delta)=\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\hbox{ : }|w|<\delta\}

and

B_{\mathbb{C}}(\delta)=\{s\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\hbox{ : }|\mathrm{Re}(s)|<\delta\}.

One can easily check that
s\mapsto L_{\varphi+\langle s,f\rangle}
is analytic on

B_{\mathbb{C}}(\delta)
(cf. Corollary 2.6.10 in
[
22
]
).
Furthermore, for
s
in a neighbourhood of
B(\delta)
in
B_{\mathbb{C}}(\delta)
,

L_{\varphi+\langle s,f\rangle}
has a simple isolated maximal eigenvalue
\lambda(s)
,
such that

\lambda(w)=e^{\mathfrak{p}(w)},

for
w\in B(\delta)
(Proposition 2.8 in
[
18
]
). As a consequence,
using spectral perturbation theory, we have that

s\mapsto e^{\mathfrak{p}(s)}
is analytic for
s
is a neighbourhood of
B(\delta)
in
B_{\mathbb{C}}(\delta)
.

We may also calculate the derivatives of
\mathfrak{p}
.
From Lemma
5.1
, we then have

\nabla\mathfrak{p}(w)=\int f\,d\mu^{w},

where

\mu^{w}:=\mu_{\varphi+\langle w.f\rangle}.

Furthermore, the function
\mathfrak{p}
is strictly convex. (Strict convexity fails only if unless there is
w\neq 0
such that

\langle w,f\rangle
is cohomologous to a constant, which violates the transitivity of
T_{f}
.)

We make a further assumption on
\varphi
and
\psi
.

Assumption (III)
.
The function
w\mapsto\mathfrak{p}(w)
has a unique minimum at
\xi\in\mathrm{int}(B(\delta))
.

Under Assumption (III), we have

\nabla\mathfrak{p}(\xi)=\int f\,d\mu^{\xi}=0.

Remark 5.3
.

Assumptions (II) and (III) are similar in spirit to the “entropy gap” conditions that appear in the recent
papers

[
3
]
and
[
17
]
.

The value
\xi
is important and allows us to relate the Gurevič pressure
P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{f})

to a Gurevič pressure with respect to
\sigma
. First, we give a technical lemma.
We observe that since
f
is valued in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
L_{\varphi+\langle\xi+2\pi it,f\rangle}
is well-defined
for
t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

For
t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,

L_{\varphi+\langle\xi+2\pi it,f\rangle}

has spectral radius strictly less than
e^{\mathfrak{p}(\xi)}
.

Proof.

As noted above Assumption (I) implies that
f
is aperiodic. It then follows from Theorem 2.14 of

[
18
]
that
L_{\varphi+\langle\xi+2\pi it,f\rangle}

has spectral radius strictly less than
e^{\mathfrak{p}(\xi)}
.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

We have

P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{f})=P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi+\langle\xi,f\rangle,\sigma).

Proof.

By the definition of Gurevič pressure (and recalling our convention that
\varphi:\Sigma\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}

is defined by
\varphi(x,\cdot)=\varphi(x)
), we have

P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{f})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{n}x=x\\
f^{n}(x)=0\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]}(x).

On the other hand, we may make the following calculation. Choose
o\in[a]
and set

\mathcal{Z}(n,a,o)=\{y\in[a]\hbox{ : }\sigma^{n}y=o\}.

There is a natural bijection
q:\mathrm{Fix}_{n}(\sigma)\cap[a]\to\mathcal{Z}(n.a,o)
given by

q(x)=x_{1}\ldots x_{n}o
.
We have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{n}x=x\\
f^{n}(x)=0x\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{n}x=x\\
f^{n}(x)=0\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)+\langle\xi,f^{n}(x)\rangle}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathrm{Fix}_{n}(\sigma)\cap[a]}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)+\langle\xi+2\pi it,f^{n}(x)\rangle}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Z}(n,a,z)}e^{\varphi^{n}(y)+\langle\xi+2\pi it,f^{n}(y)\rangle}\,dt+O(\theta^{n}e^{n\mathfrak{p}(\xi)})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(L^{n}_{\varphi+\langle\xi+2\pi it,f\rangle}\mathbbm{1}_{[a]})(z)+O(\theta^{n}e^{n\mathfrak{p}(\xi)}),

for some
0<\theta<1
. Using Lemma
5.4
and the analysis in
[
27
]
, we can show that
the right hand side behaves like
e^{n\mathfrak{p}(\xi)}n^{-d/2}
, so the result follows.
∎

6.
Equidistribution for skew product extensions

In this section, we obtain a weighted equidistribution result for periodic points and pre-images with respect to
skew product extensions of countable state Markov shifts with the BIP property satisfying Assumptions (I), (II) and (III).

For
x\in\Sigma
, let
\delta_{x}
denote the Dirac measure at
x
and write

\tau_{x,n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\delta_{\sigma^{j}x}.

For a sequence of countable sets
\Lambda_{n}\subset\Sigma
, we write

\Pi_{\varphi}(\Lambda_{n})=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}

and introduce a sequence of probability measures on
\Sigma
,

\mathfrak{M}_{\varphi}(\Lambda_{n})=\frac{1}{\Pi_{\varphi}(\Lambda_{n})}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}\tau_{x,n}.

We consider the following choices for
\Lambda_{n}
:

(1)

\Lambda^{\psi}(n)=\{x\in\Sigma\hbox{ : }\sigma^{n}x=x,\ \psi_{n}(x)=0\};

(2)

for
a\in S
,

\Lambda_{a}^{\psi}(n)=\{x\in[a]\hbox{ : }\sigma^{n}x=x,\ \psi_{n}(x)=0\};

(3)

for
o\in\Sigma
,

\Lambda^{\psi}(n,o)=\{x\in\Sigma\hbox{ : }\sigma^{n}x=o,\ \psi_{n}(x)=e\};

(4)

for
a\in S
and
o\in[a]
,

\Lambda_{a}^{\psi}(n,o)=\{x\in[a]\hbox{ : }\sigma^{n}x=o,\ \psi_{n}(x)=e\}.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\sigma:\Sigma\to\Sigma
be a countable state Markov shift which satisfies BIP
and let
G
be a finitely generated group. Let
\varphi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
be locally Hölder continuous
and let
\psi:\Sigma\to G

satisfy
\psi(x)=\psi(x_{1})
, such that
\varphi
and
\psi
satisfy Assumptions (I), (II) and (III).
If
G
is amenable then

\mathfrak{M}_{\varphi}(\Lambda^{\psi}(n))
,

\mathfrak{M}_{\varphi}(\Lambda_{a}^{\psi}(n))
,

\mathfrak{M}_{\varphi}(\Lambda^{\psi}(n,o))
and

\mathfrak{M}_{\varphi}(\Lambda_{a}^{\psi}(n,o))

all converge to
\mu^{\xi}
, as
n\to\infty
,
with respect to the weak
∗
topology on
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)
.

We give the proof for the sequence
\mathfrak{M}_{\varphi}(\Lambda^{\psi}(n))
,
the other cases being similar. The proof is based on the following lemma.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
g:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded locally Hölder continuous function.
Given
\epsilon>0
, there exists
C>0
and
\eta>0
such that

\frac{1}{\Pi_{\varphi}(\Lambda^{\psi}(n))}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Lambda^{\psi}(n)\\
|\int g\,d\tau_{x,n}-\int g\,d\mu_{\xi}|>\epsilon\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}\leq Ce^{-\eta n}.

Proof.

We consider
x\in\Lambda^{\psi}(n)
such that

\int g\,d\tau_{x,n}>\int g\,d\mu_{\xi}+\epsilon
and

\int g\,d\tau_{x,n}<\int g\,d\mu_{\xi}-\epsilon
separately.
For
s>0
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Lambda^{\psi}(n)\\
\int g\,d\tau_{x,n}>\int g\,d\mu^{\xi}+\epsilon\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}\leq\sum_{x\in\Lambda^{\psi}(n)}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)+sg^{n}(x)-ns\int g\,d\mu^{\xi}-ns\epsilon},

so that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Lambda^{\psi}(n)\\
\int g\,d\tau_{x,n}>\int g\,d\mu^{x}i+\epsilon\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}\leq P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi+\langle\xi,f\rangle+sg,\sigma)-s\int g\,d\mu^{\xi}-s\epsilon.

Similarly, for
s<0
, we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Lambda^{\psi}(n)\\
\int g\,d\tau_{x,n}<\int g\,d\mu^{\xi}-\epsilon\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}\leq P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi+\langle\xi,f\rangle+sg,\sigma)-s\int g\,d\mu^{\xi}+s\epsilon.

If we write
\mathfrak{p}_{g}(s):=P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi+\langle\xi,f\rangle+sg)
then,
applying Lemma
5.1
, we have a bound of the form

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Lambda^{\psi}(n)\\
|\int g\,d\tau_{x,n}-\int g\,d\mu_{\xi}|>\epsilon\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}\leq\mathfrak{p}_{g}(s)-s\mathfrak{p}_{g}^{\prime}(0)-\epsilon|s|,

which is strictly smaller than
P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi+\langle\xi,f\rangle)
for sufficiently small values of
s
.
The proof is completed by noting that, by
Theorem
1.1
and
Proposition
5.5
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\Pi_{\varphi}(\Lambda^{\psi}(n))=P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi+\langle\xi,f\rangle,\sigma).

∎

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Write
\mathfrak{M}_{n}=\mathfrak{M}_{\varphi}(\Lambda^{\psi}(n))
.
We need to show that for every bounded continuous function

g:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int g\,d\mathfrak{M}_{n}=\int g\,d\mu^{\xi}.

It is enough to prove this when
g
is a bounded locally Hölder continuous function.
We have

\displaystyle\int g\,d\mathfrak{M}_{n}-\int g\,d\mu^{\xi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Pi_{\varphi}(\Lambda^{\psi}(n))}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Lambda^{\psi}(n)\\
\left|\int\chi\,d\tau_{x,n}-\int\chi d\mu^{\xi}\right|>\epsilon\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}\int g\,d\tau_{x,n}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\Pi_{\varphi}(\Lambda^{\psi}(n))}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Lambda^{\psi}(n)\\
\left|\int\chi\,d\tau_{x,n}-\int gd\mu^{\xi}\right|\leq\epsilon\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}\int g\,d\tau_{x,n}-\int\chi\,d\mu^{\xi}.

By Lemma
6.2
, the first term on the right hand side tends to zero
exponentially fast. Also,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\Pi_{\varphi}(\Lambda^{\psi}(n))}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Lambda^{\psi}(n)\\
\left|\int g\,d\tau_{x,n}-\int gd\mu^{\xi}\right|\leq\epsilon\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}\int g\,d\tau_{x,n}-\int g\,d\mu^{\xi}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon+\frac{\left|\int g\,d\mu^{\xi}\right|}{\Pi_{\varphi}(\Lambda^{\psi}(n))}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Lambda^{\psi}(n)\\
\left|\int g\,d\tau_{x,n}-\int gd\mu^{\xi}\right|>\epsilon\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi^{n}(x)}\leq\epsilon+Ce^{-\eta(\epsilon)n},

which, since
\epsilon
is arbitrary, gives the result.

∎

7.
Equality implies amenability

In this section we show that, under Assumptions (I), (II) and (III), the converse of Theorem

1.1

holds, i.e.
if
P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\psi})=P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\bar{\psi}})
then
G
is
amenable. As above, we write
f=\bar{\psi}
.

To do this, we need to specify a function space for
\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}
to act on.
Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the set of continuous functions

v:\Sigma\times G\to\mathbb{C}
such that
g\mapsto\|v(\cdot,g)\|_{\infty}
is in
\ell^{2}(G)
, with norm

\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\left(\sum_{g\in G}\|v(\cdot,g)\|_{\infty}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

and let
\mathrm{spr}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi})
denote the spectral radius of
\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}
on

\mathcal{H}
.
We always have

P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\psi})\leq\log\mathrm{spr}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi})\leq P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,\sigma).

We state a result due to Stadlbauer.

Proposition 7.1

(Stadlbauer
[
33
]
, Theorem 5.4)
.

Suppose that
\sigma:\Sigma\to\Sigma
satisfies BIP
and that
T_{\psi}:\Sigma\times G\to\Sigma\times G
is topologically transitive.
If
G
is not amenable then
\log\mathrm{spr}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi})<P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,\sigma)
.

We now have the desired result.

Proposition 7.2
.

If
P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\psi})=P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\bar{\psi}})
then

G
is amenable.

Proof.

Let
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be as in the previous section and let
\varphi_{\xi}=\varphi+\langle\xi,f\rangle
.
Suppose that
G
is not amenable. Then

P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\psi})=P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi_{\xi},T_{\psi})\leq\log\mathrm{spr}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi_{\xi}})<P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi_{\xi},\sigma)=P_{\mathrm{G}}(\varphi,T_{\bar{\psi}}),

where the strict inequality uses Proposition
7.1
.
∎

Generalized coherent states for the harmonic oscillator by the J J -matrix method with an extension to the Morse potential
Hashim A. Yamani ∗, Zouhaïr Mouayn ∗ Dar Al-Jewar, Knowledge Economic City, Medina, Saudi Arabia e-mail: hashim.haydara@gmail.com ⋄ Department of Mathematics, Faculty of Sciences and Technics (M’Ghila), Sultan Moulay Slimane University, BP. 523, Béni Mellal 23000, Morocco

Abstract
While dealing with the J J -Matrix method for the harmonic oscillator to write down its tridiagonal matrix representation in an orthonormal basis of L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) , L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right), we rederive a set of generalized coherent states (GCS) of Perelomov type labeled by points z z of the complex plane ℂ \mathbb{C} and depending on an integer number m ∈ ℤ + m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+} . The number states expansion of these GCS gives rise to coefficients that are complex Hermite polynomials whose linear superpositions provide eigenfunctions for the two-dimensional magnetic Laplacian associated with the m m th Landau level. We extend this procedure to the Morse oscillator by constructing a new set of GCS of Glauber type.



1
Introduction

Coherent states (CS) are mathematical tools which provide a close connection
between classical and quantum formalisms so as to play a central role in the
semiclassical analysis
\left[1\right]
. In general, CS are an
overcomplete family of normalized
ket
vectors
\left|\zeta\right\rangle
which are labeled by points
\zeta
of a phase-space domain

X
, belonging to a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
that corresponds to a
specific quantum model and provides
\mathcal{H}
with a resolution of its
identity operator with respect to a suitable integration measure on
X.

In this paper, we will be dealing with the
J
-matrix method for the
harmonic oscillator Hamiltonian
H_{har}
to write down its tridiagonal
matrix representation in an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H\equiv}L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right).
We rederive a set of generalized coherent
states (GCS), say
\phi_{m}^{\omega,z},
of Perelomov type labeled by
points
z
of the phase space
X\equiv\mathbb{C}
and depending on an
integer number
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, obtained as orbits of the operators of
the Schrödinger representation
T_{\omega}
,
\omega>0,
of the
Heisenberg group on a
m
th Hermite function
h_{m}
. The resolution of the
identity operator on
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
is ensured by the
square integrability of
T_{\omega}
modulo the center of the group.
Beside, the number states expansion in the basis of eigenstates of
H_{har}

gives rise to coefficients that are complex Hermite polynomials whose linear
superpositions provide eigenfunctions for the two-dimensional magnetic
Laplacian associated with the
m
th Landau level. This gives an alternative
answer to the problem rised by the authors
\left[2\right]
who,
essentialy have used the cross-Wigner transform in order to intertwine
unitarly the two operators. Next we extend this procedure to the Morse
oscillator by constructing a new set of coherent states of Glauber type.

The paper is organized as follows. In section 2 we review the classical
coherent states of the harmonic and some of their generalization depending
on the discret or continuous dynamics. In section 3, we review some
properties of a Hamiltonian having a tridiagonal representation in an
orthonormal basis of some Hilbert space. Section 4, deal with the
tridiagonalization of the harmonic oscillator which enables to recover a set
of generalized coherent states for this Hamiltonian. As application the
planar Landau problem and complex Hermite polynomials are also discussed. In
section 5 we give an extension of our procedure to the Hamiltonian with the
Morse potential.

2
Coherent states

2.1
Canonical coherent states

Coherent states were first introduced by E. Schrödinger
\left[3\right]
in order to obtain quantum states in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
that follow the
classical flow associated to the harmonic oscillator Hamiltonian

H_{har}=-\frac{\hbar}{2m_{\ast}}\frac{d^{2}}{d\xi^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\omega^{2}\xi^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\omega.

(2.1)

Here, we consider the unit system where the Planck parameter
\hslash
, the
mass
m_{\ast}
and
\omega
equal one. These CS are ket vectors denoted
by
\left|z\right\rangle\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and labeled by elements
of
\,z\in

\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{R}^{2}
which is the phase space of a
particle moving on
\mathbb{R}
, given by

\langle\xi\left|z\right\rangle=\pi^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left(e^{zz^{\ast}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}z^{\ast 2}+\sqrt{2}z^{\ast}\xi-\frac{1}{2}\xi^{2}\right),\text{ \ \ }\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.2)

Their most important property is the resolution of the identity operator

\mathbf{1}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int\limits_{\mathbb{C}}d\nu(z)\left|z\right\rangle\left\langle z\right|,

(2.3)

d\nu(z)
being the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{R}^{2}.
The
property
\left(2.3\right)
bridges between classical and quantum
mechanics in the sense that every operator acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
or
any vector lying there may be decomposed over the phase space
\mathbb{C}
.
Moreover, the Bargmann transform
B_{0\text{ }}
defined by

B_{0\text{ }}\left[\varphi\right]\left(z\right):=\left(e^{zz^{\ast}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\varphi\left|z\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\pi^{-\frac{1}{4}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}z^{\ast 2}+\sqrt{2}z^{\ast}\xi-\frac{1}{2}\xi^{2}\right)\varphi\left(\xi\right)d\xi,\text{ \ \ }\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

(2.4)

maps isometrically
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
onto the well known
\left[4\right]

Fock-Bargmann space
\mathcal{F}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
of analytic
Gaussian square integrable functions on
\mathbb{C}
, whose reproducing
kernel is
K_{0}\left(z,w\right)

\propto e^{zw^{\ast}}
for
z,w\in\mathbb{C}
. The CS in
\left(2.2\right)
may also be expanded over the
Fock basis of eigenfunctions
\left|\psi_{n}\right\rangle
of the
operator
H_{har}
as

\left|z\right\rangle=\left(e^{zz^{\ast}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{z^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}\right)^{\ast}\left|\psi_{n}\right\rangle.

(2.5)

where the analytic coefficients
\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!}}z^{n}\right)
in
this expansion form an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{F}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
. This also means that the normalization prefactor in
\left(2.5\right)
encodes information on the diagonal of the reproducing kernel
function
K_{0}\left(z,z\right).

2.2
Generalized coherent states

For dynamics with discrete spectrum, one of the generalization of
\left(2.5\right)
to the finite dimensional case could described as follows. Let
(X,\sigma)
be a measure space and let
\mathcal{A}^{2}\subset L^{2}(X,\sigma)
be a closed subspace of finite dimension
N
. Let
\left\{D_{n}\right\}_{n=0}^{N-1}
be an orthonormal system of functions in

\mathcal{A}^{2}
. For arbitrary
\zeta\in X,
denote

\omega_{d}\left(\zeta\right):=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left|D_{n}\left(\zeta\right)\right|^{2}.

(2.6)

Let
\mathfrak{H}_{d}
be a Hilbert space with dimension
N
and
\left\{\phi_{n}\right\}_{n=0}^{N-1}
be a complete orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{H}_{d},
which may be considered as eigenfunctions of some
Hamiltonian, associated with a finite spectrum
E_{0}<E_{1}<...<E_{N-1}
.
That is,

1_{\mathfrak{H}_{d}}=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left|\phi_{n}\right\rangle\left\langle\phi_{n}\right|.

(2.7)

The CS labeled by points
\zeta\in X
are defined as the ket-vectors
\left|\zeta\right\rangle\in\mathfrak{H}_{d}:

\left|\zeta\right\rangle_{d}:=\left(\omega_{d}\left(\zeta\right)\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}D_{n}\left(\zeta\right)\left|\phi_{n}\right\rangle.\quad\quad

(2.8)

Now, by definition
\left(2.8\right)
, it is straightforward to show that
\langle u\left|u\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}_{d}}=1
and for
\phi,\psi\in\mathfrak{H}_{d}
, we have

\langle\phi\left|\psi\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}_{d}}=\int\limits_{X}d\sigma\left(\zeta\right)\omega_{d}\left(\zeta\right)\langle\phi\left|\zeta\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}\langle\zeta\left|\psi\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}.

(2.9)

Thereby, we have a resolution of the identity operator of
\mathfrak{H}_{d}\mathfrak{,}
which can be expressed as

\mathbf{1}_{\mathfrak{H}_{d}}=\int\limits_{X}d\sigma\left(\zeta\right)\omega_{d}\left(\zeta\right)\left|\zeta\right\rangle\left\langle\zeta\right|\quad\quad

(2.10)

where
\omega_{d}\left(\zeta\right)
appears as a weight function.

For the dynamics with continum spectra, we denote by
\left|\lambda\right\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
be an eigenfunction of the Hamiltonian
H\left|\lambda\right\rangle=E\left(\lambda\right)\left|\lambda\right\rangle
and for the continuous spectrum
\lambda
may be
thought of some continuous variable such that the spectrum
E\left(\lambda\right)
is monotonically covered by variation of
\lambda
from
\lambda_{0}
to
\infty.
We assume that the continuous spectrum states are
normalized by the condition
\left\langle\lambda\right|\beta\rangle\varpropto\delta\left(\lambda-\beta\right).
Consider a set of
functions
X\backepsilon\zeta\rightarrow C_{\lambda}\left(\zeta\right)

indexed by
\lambda
, such that
\left\{C_{\lambda}\right\}
are
orthonormal in
L^{2}\left(X,d\mu\right)

\int\limits_{X}C_{\lambda}\left(\zeta\right)\overline{C_{\beta}\left(\zeta\right)}d\mu\left(\zeta\right)=\delta\left(\lambda-\beta\right).

(2.11)

Assume that

\omega_{c}\left(\zeta\right):=\int\limits_{\lambda_{0}}^{\infty}\left|C_{\lambda}\left(\zeta\right)\right|^{2}d\lambda<\infty

(2.12)

The CS labeled by points
\zeta\in X
are defined as the ket-vectors
\left|\zeta\right\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
by

\left|\zeta\right\rangle_{c}:=\left(\omega_{c}\left(\zeta\right)\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int\limits_{\lambda_{0}}^{\infty}C_{\lambda}\left(\zeta\right)\left|\lambda\right\rangle d\lambda.\quad\quad

(2.13)

We assume that there exists a measure
d\nu
on
X
such that

\int\limits_{X}d\nu\left(\zeta\right)\omega_{c}\left(\zeta\right)\left|\zeta\right\rangle\left\langle\zeta\right|==\int\limits_{\lambda_{0}}^{\infty}\left|\lambda\right\rangle\left\langle\lambda\right|d\lambda=1_{\mathcal{H}_{c}}

(2.14)

One can define the generalized CS for both discrete and continuous spectra
via the combination

\left|\zeta\right\rangle_{d,c}:=\left(\omega_{d}\left(\zeta\right)\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}D_{n}\left(\zeta\right)\left|\phi_{n}\right\rangle+\left(\omega_{c}\left(\zeta\right)\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int\limits_{\lambda_{0}}^{\infty}C_{\lambda}\left(\zeta\right)\left|\lambda\right\rangle d\lambda.\quad

(2.15)

Of course we still have to precise the form of the suitable measure on
X

that ensures the resolution of the identity operator which would be of the
form
1_{\mathcal{H}_{d}}+1_{\mathcal{H}_{c}}
, see
\left[5\right].

Actually, various generalizations of CS have been proposed. For an overview
of all aspects of the theory of CS and their genesis, we refer to the survey

\left[6\right]
and
\left[1\right],\left[7\right].

3
The
J
-matrix method

We assume that we are given a positive semi-definite Hamiltonian
H
(with
zero as the value of the lowest energy in its spectrum) acting on a Hilbert
space
\mathcal{H}
, that has a tridiagonal matrix representation in the
orthonormal basis
\{{\left|\phi_{n}\right\rangle}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}

of
\mathcal{H}
with known coefficients
\{a_{n},b_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}

H_{n,m}={\left\langle\phi_{n}\right|}H{\left|\phi_{m}\right\rangle}=b_{n-1}\,\delta_{n,m+1}+\,a_{n}\,\delta_{n,m}\,+\,b_{n}\,\delta_{n,m-1}.

(3.1)

We solve the energy eigenvalue equation
H{\left|E\right\rangle}=E{\left|E\right\rangle}
by expanding the eigenvector
{\left|E\right\rangle}
in the basis
{\left|\phi_{n}\right\rangle}
as
{\left|E\right\rangle}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}(E){\left|\phi_{n}\right\rangle}
. Making use of the tridiagonality of
H
, we readily
obtain the following recurrence relations for the expansion coefficients:

\begin{array}[]{l}{Ef_{0}(E)=a_{0}f_{0}(E)+b_{0}f_{1}(E)\quad\quad}\\
{Ef_{n}(E)=b_{n-1}f_{n-1}(E)+a_{n}f_{n}(E)+b_{n}f_{n+1}(E),\quad n=1,2,...,.\quad}\end{array}

(3.2)

The operator
H
may admit a continuous spectrum
\sigma_{c}
and a
discrete part
\{E_{\mu}\}_{\mu}
, both of which lead to the following
form of the resolution of the identity operator on
\mathcal{H}
:

\sum_{\mu}{\left|E_{\mu}\right\rangle}{\left\langle E_{\mu}\right|}+\int_{\sigma_{c}}{\left|E\right\rangle}{\left\langle E\right|}\,dE\;=1_{\mathcal{H}}.\quad\quad

(3.3)

This translates into the following orthogonality relation for the expansion
coefficients:

\sum_{\mu}f_{n}(E_{\mu})\left(f_{m}(E_{\mu})\right)^{\ast}+\int_{\sigma_{c}}f_{n}(E)\left(f_{m}(E)\right)^{\ast}dE\,=\delta_{n,m}.

(3.4)

If we now define
p_{n}(E)=\frac{f_{n}(E)}{f_{0}(E)}
, then
\{p_{n}(E)\}

is a set of polynomials that satisfy the three-term recursion relation

Ep_{n}(E)=b_{n-1}p_{n-1}(E)+a_{n}p_{n}(E)+b_{n}p_{n+1}(E),\quad n=1,2,...,

(3.5)

with the initial conditions
p_{0}(E)=1
and
p_{1}(E)=(E-a_{0})b_{0}^{-1}
.
If we further define
\Omega(E)=\left|f_{0}(E)\right|^{2}
and
\Omega_{\mu}=\left|f_{0}(E_{\mu})\right|^{2}
, then the
relation
\left(2.4\right)
now translates into the following
orthogonality relation for the polynomial
p_{n}
:

\sum_{\mu}\Omega_{\mu}p_{n}(E_{\mu})\left(p_{m}(E_{\mu})\right)^{\ast}+\int\limits_{\sigma_{c}}\Omega(E)p_{n}(E)\left(p_{m}\left(E\right)\right)^{\ast}dE\,=\delta_{n,m}.

(3.6)

We have shown
\left[8\right]
that we can write the Hamiltonian
H
in
the form
H=A^{{\dagger}}A
, where the forward-shift operator
A
is defined by
its action on the basis vector as

A{\left|\phi_{n}\right\rangle}=c_{n}\,{\left|\phi_{n}\right\rangle}+\,d_{n}\,{\left|\phi_{n-1}\right\rangle}.

(3.7)

Furtheremore, we require from the adjoint operator
A^{{\dagger}}
to act on the
ket vectors
{\left|\phi_{n}\right\rangle}
in the following way

A^{\dagger}{\left|\phi_{n}\right\rangle}=c_{n}^{\ast}\,{\left|\phi_{n}\right\rangle}+\,d_{n+1}^{\ast}\,{\left|\phi_{n+1}\right\rangle.}

(3.8)

Here, the coefficients
\{c_{n},d_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
are related to the
coefficients
\{a_{n},b_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and the polynomials
\{p_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}

{d_{0}=0,\,\;c_{n}}c_{n}^{\ast}{=-b_{n}\frac{p_{n+1}(0)}{p_{n}(0)},\quad d_{n+1}d_{n+1}^{\ast}=-b_{n}\frac{p_{n}(0)}{p_{n+1}(0)},\quad}{a_{n}=c}_{n}c_{n}^{\ast}+{d_{n}d_{n}^{\ast},\quad b_{n}=c_{n}d_{n+1}^{\ast}}.

(3.9)

4
Tridiagonalization of the harmonic oscillator

4.1
Construction of the basis
\left(\Phi_{m}^{z,\omega}\right)_{m\geq 0}

We recall the following regarding the one-dimensional harmonic oscillator

H_{har}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d^{2}}{d\xi^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\omega^{2}\xi^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\omega,

(4.1)

\omega>0
being a fixed parameter. It decomposes as

H_{har}=A^{\dagger}A

(4.2)

where

A=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{d}{d\xi}+W\left(\xi\right),\text{ \ }A^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{d}{d\xi}+W\left(\xi\right),\text{ \ }W\left(\xi\right)=-\frac{\omega}{\sqrt{2}}\xi.

(4.3)

The spectrum of
H_{har}
in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R},d\xi\right)
consists
on a set of discrete eigenvalues
\varepsilon_{n}=n\omega,

n=0,1,2,...,

with the corresponding eigenfunctions given by

\psi_{n}\left(\xi\right)=\left(-1\right)^{n}\left(\frac{\omega}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(2^{n}n!\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\omega\xi^{2}}H_{n}\left(\sqrt{\omega}\xi\right),\text{ \ \ }\xi\in\mathbb{R},

(4.4)

in terms of the Hermite polynomial
\left(\left[9\right]\text{, p.100}\right):

H_{n}\left(u\right)=n!\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2}n\right\rfloor}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{\ell}\left(2u\right)^{n-2\ell}}{\ell!\left(n-2\ell\right)!},\text{ \ \ }u\in\mathbb{R}.

(4.5)

But and since the Hamiltonian is shape invariant, we know
\left[8\right]

that we can generate the infinite set of coefficients
\left\{c_{n},d_{n}\right\}
from the three values,
(d_{0},d_{1},c_{0})
together
with the energy spectrum of the system, as follows

c_{n}^{2}=c_{0}^{2}+\left[nd_{1}^{2}-\varepsilon_{n}\right],\quad d_{n}^{2}=nd_{1}^{2}+\left[n\varepsilon_{1}-\varepsilon_{n}\right].

(4.6)

Starting now with
d_{0}=0,

d_{1}=\sqrt{\omega}
and
c_{0}=-z^{\ast},

z\in\mathbb{C}
. It is easy to deduce that
\left(4.6\right)
gives the
simple results
c_{n}^{2}=z^{\ast 2}
and
d_{n}^{2}=n\omega
. Therefore,
the coefficients of the Hamiltonian tridiagonal matrix are of the form

a_{n}\equiv a_{n}\left(z,\omega\right)=\left|c_{n}\right|^{2}+\left|d_{n}\right|^{2}=zz^{\ast}+n\omega,

(4.7)

b_{n}\equiv b_{n}\left(z,\omega\right)=c_{n}d_{n+1}^{\ast}=-z^{\ast}\sqrt{(n+1)\omega}.

(4.8)

This is a situation where we know the coefficients
\left\{a_{n},b_{n}\right\}
explicitly, but we do not know the elements of the
tridiagonal basis, say
\left\{\Phi_{n}^{z,\omega}\right\}_{n\geq 0}
,
except for the first two elements. Indeed, we start with

\Phi_{0}^{z,\omega}\left(\xi\right)=\left(\frac{\omega}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(e^{\frac{1}{\omega}zz^{\ast}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\omega}z^{\ast 2}+\sqrt{2}z^{\ast}\xi-\frac{1}{2}\omega\xi^{2}\right)

which is the canonical CS given in
\left(2.2\right).
From the relations
in
\left(4.3\right),
we have
A+

A^{\dagger}=2W
. Hence,
A^{\dagger}=W-A.
Since
A\Phi_{0}^{z,\omega}=z\Phi_{0}^{z,\omega}
and
A^{\dagger}\Phi_{0}^{z,\omega}=z^{\ast}\Phi_{0}^{z,\omega}+d_{1}\Phi_{1}^{z,\omega},
we deduce that
d_{1}\Phi_{1}^{z,\omega}=2\left(W-\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}z\right)\Phi_{1}^{z,\omega}.
Thus,

d_{1}d_{1}^{\ast}=4\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\left(W\left(\xi\right)-\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}z\right)^{2}\left|\Phi_{0}^{z,\omega}\left(\xi\right)\right|^{2}d\xi=\omega.

Now, with this explicit value of
d_{1},
we obtain
\Phi_{1}^{z,\omega}

explicitly as

\Phi_{1}^{z,\omega}=\left(\frac{\omega}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\frac{-1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(e^{\frac{1}{\omega}zz^{\ast}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\omega}z^{\ast 2}+\sqrt{2}z^{\ast}\xi-\frac{1}{2}\omega\xi^{2}\right)H_{1}\left(\sqrt{\omega}\xi-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\omega}}\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}z\right).

To determine the full basis elements, we first proceed by fixing an
eigenfunction
\psi_{s}
in
\left(4.4\right)
and expanding it over
elements of the basis
\left\{\Phi_{n}^{z,\omega}\right\}_{n\geq 0}
as

\psi_{s}(\xi)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[\varrho\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right)p_{n}\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right)\right]\Phi_{n}^{z,\omega}\left(\xi\right),\text{ \ }\xi\in\mathbb{R}

(4.9)

with
p_{n}
being an orthogonal polynomial satisfying the conditions

\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\varrho\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right)p_{n}\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right)\left(\varrho\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right)p_{m}\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right)\right)^{\ast}=\delta_{n,m},\text{ \ }p_{0}\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right)=1.

(4.10)

where
\varrho\left(\bullet\right)
stands for the square root of an
orthonormalization density function. From the tridiagonality condition, we
know that
p_{n}
satisfy the following three-term recurrence relation

\varepsilon_{s}p_{n}\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right)=b_{n-1}p_{n-1}\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right)+a_{n}p_{n}\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right)+b_{n}^{\ast}p_{n+1}\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right).

(4.11)

By
\left(4.7\right)
-
\left(4.8\right)
, Eq.
\left(4.11\right)
takes
the form

\varepsilon_{s}p_{n}\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right)=-z^{\ast}\sqrt{n\omega}p_{n-1}\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right)+\left(\left|z\right|^{2}+n\omega\right)p_{n}\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right)-z\sqrt{\left(n+1\right)\omega}p_{n+1}\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right).

(4.12)

We introduce the follwing change of polynomial by setting

p_{n}\left(u\right)=\omega^{-\frac{1}{2}n}\frac{z^{\ast n}}{\sqrt{n!}}Q_{n}\left(u;\left|z\right|^{2}\right),\text{ \ }u\equiv\varepsilon_{s}

(4.13)

Inroducing the function

(4.15)

then, eq.

From the latter one we recognize the three-term recurrence relation of the

(4.18)

Thus,

g_{n}^{\left(\omega,\left|z\right|^{2}\right)}\left(s\right)=\left(-1\right)^{n}n!\left(\frac{\omega}{\left|z\right|^{2}}\right)^{n}L_{n}^{\left(s-n\right)}\left(\frac{\left|z\right|^{2}}{\omega}\right)

(B10)

which gives

c_{k+1}=\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2}}\left(k+1+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\beta}z\right)

(B11)

Appendix C

We start from the decomposition of eigenfunctions of
H
over the basis
\left\{\phi_{m}^{z,\beta,D}\right\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
as follows

\psi\left(\varepsilon,x\right)=\sqrt{\Omega\left(\varepsilon\right)}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}p_{m}\left(\varepsilon\right)\phi_{m}^{z,\beta,D}\left(x\right)

(C1)

where the coefficients
p_{m}\left(\varepsilon\right)
satisfy the three
terms recursion relation

\varepsilon p_{m}\left(\varepsilon\right)=b_{m-1}p_{m-1}\left(\varepsilon\right)+a_{m}p_{m}\left(\varepsilon\right)+b_{m}^{\ast}p_{m+1}\left(\varepsilon\right)

(C2)

with
p_{0}\left(\varepsilon\right)=1
and

\sum\limits_{m=0}^{N_{b}-1}\left|\Omega\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right)\right|p_{m}\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right)p_{m}^{\ast}\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right)+\int\limits_{E_{\min}}^{+\infty}\left|\Omega\left(\varepsilon\right)\right|p_{m}\left(\varepsilon\right)p_{m}^{\ast}\left(\varepsilon\right)d\varepsilon=\delta_{n,m}.

(C3)

Here
N_{b}=\left\lfloor D\right\rfloor+1
is the number of bound states of
the Morse oscillator. Also,
E_{\min}=\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}D^{2}
is the
minimum energy of the continous part of the energy spectrum. We may, then,
rescale the energy
\varepsilon
in terms of the variable
\varsigma
such
that
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}\left(D^{2}+\varsigma^{2}\right).

By setting
p_{m}\left(\varepsilon\right)=p_{m}\left(0\right)q_{m}\left(\varepsilon\right),

q_{m}\left(0\right)=q_{0}\left(\varepsilon\right)=1,
Eq.
\left(C2\right)
takes the form

\varepsilon p_{m}\left(\varepsilon\right)=b_{m-1}\left(\frac{p_{m-1}\left(0\right)}{p_{m}\left(0\right)}\right)q_{m-1}\left(\varepsilon\right)+a_{m}q_{m}\left(\varepsilon\right)+b_{m}^{\ast}\left(\frac{p_{m+1}\left(0\right)}{p_{m}\left(0\right)}\right)q_{m+1}\left(\varepsilon\right).

(C4)

We know that the coefficients
\left\{c_{n},d_{n}\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}

are related to the polynomials
\left\{p_{n}\left(0\right)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
by the relation

c_{m}c_{m}^{\ast}=-b_{m}^{\ast}\frac{p_{m+1}\left(0\right)}{p_{m}\left(0\right)},\text{ \ }d_{m}d_{m}^{\ast}=-b_{m}\frac{p_{m-1}\left(0\right)}{p_{m}\left(0\right)}.

(C5)

Then
\left(C4\right)
becomes

\varepsilon p_{m}\left(\varepsilon\right)=-\left|d_{m}\right|^{2}q_{m-1}\left(\varepsilon\right)+\left(\left|c_{m}\right|^{2}+\left|d_{m}\right|^{2}\right)q_{m}\left(\varepsilon\right)-\left|c_{m}\right|^{2}q_{m+1}\left(\varepsilon\right)

(C6)

In terms of the variable
\varsigma

-\left(D^{2}+\varsigma^{2}\right)q_{m}\left(\varsigma\right)=-m\left(m+2\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\xi_{z}\right)q_{m-1}\left(\varsigma\right)

(C7)

+\left[\left(m+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\beta}z\right)\left(m+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\beta}z^{\ast}\right)+m\left(m+2\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\xi_{z}\right)\right]q_{m}\left(\varsigma\right)-\left(m+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\beta}z\right)\left(m+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\beta}z^{\ast}\right)q_{m+1}\left(\varsigma\right)

This is the recursion equation of the continous dual Hahn polynomial
\left[10\right].
The solution is given by

q_{m}\left(\varsigma\right)=_{3}\digamma_{2}\left(-m,a+i\varsigma,a-i\varsigma;a+b,a+c;1\right)

(C8)

where
a=-D,b=D+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\beta}z,

c=D+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\beta}z^{\ast}
and

\frac{p_{m+1}\left(0\right)}{p_{m}\left(0\right)}=-\frac{\left(m+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\beta}z\right)}{\sqrt{\left(m+1\right)\left(m+\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\xi_{z}+1\right)}}

(C9)

with

p_{m}\left(0\right)=\left(-1\right)^{m}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m!}}\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma\left(\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\xi_{z}+1\right)}{\Gamma\left(m+\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\xi_{z}+1\right)}}\frac{\Gamma\left(m+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\beta}z\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\beta}z\right)}

(C10)

Therefore,

p_{m}\left(\varsigma\right)=\frac{\left(-1\right)^{m}}{\sqrt{m!}}\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma\left(\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\xi_{z}+1\right)}{\Gamma\left(m+\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\xi_{z}+1\right)}}\frac{\Gamma\left(m+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\beta}z\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\beta}z\right)}._{3}\digamma_{2}\left(-m,-D+i\varsigma,-D-i\varsigma,\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\beta}z,\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\beta}z^{\ast};1\right).

(C11)

The density
\Omega\left(\varepsilon\right)
associated with continuous
part of the energy spectrum and
\Omega\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right)

associated with the bound states
\left\{\varepsilon_{s}\right\}
are
found explicitly in
\left[21\right]
as

\Omega\left(\varepsilon\right)=\left|\frac{\Gamma\left(-D+i\varsigma\left(\varepsilon\right)\right)\Gamma^{2}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\xi_{z}+\frac{1}{2}+i\varsigma\left(\varepsilon\right)\right)}{2\pi\Gamma\left(2i\varsigma\left(\varepsilon\right)\right)}\right|\frac{1}{\beta^{2}\varsigma\left(\varepsilon\right)\Gamma^{2}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\xi_{z}+\frac{1}{2}-D\right)\Gamma\left(2\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\xi_{z}+1\right)}

(C12)

and

\Omega\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right)=\frac{\left(-1\right)^{s}}{s!}\frac{\Gamma^{2}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\xi_{z}+\frac{1}{2}+D\right)\left(-2D\right)_{s}\left[\left(-D+\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\xi_{z}+\frac{1}{2}\right)_{s}\right]^{2}}{\Gamma\left(2D\right)\Gamma\left(2\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\xi_{z}+1\right)\left(-D\right)_{s}\left[\left(-D-\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\xi_{z}+\frac{1}{2}\right)_{s}\right]^{2}}.

(C13)

Therefore, we have may expand any eigenfunction
\psi_{s}
of
H_{mor}

over the generalized CS basis
\phi_{m}^{z,\beta,D}\left(x\right)
as

\psi_{s}\left(x\right)=\sqrt{\Omega\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right)}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}p_{m}\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right)\phi_{m}^{z,\beta,D}\left(x\right)

(C14)

for bound states, and

\psi\left(\varepsilon,x\right)=\sqrt{\Omega\left(\varepsilon\right)}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}p_{m}\left(\varepsilon\right)\phi_{m}^{z,\beta,D}\left(x\right)

(C15)

for scattering states. Thus, we may expand
\phi_{m}^{z,\beta,D}\left(x\right)
over all these eigenstates as

\phi_{m}^{z,\beta,D}\left(x\right)=\sum\limits_{s=0}^{N_{b}-1}\sqrt{\Omega\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right)}p_{m}^{\ast}\left(\varepsilon_{s}\right)\psi_{s}\left(x\right)+\int\limits_{E_{\min}}^{\infty}\Omega\left(\varepsilon\right)p_{m}^{\ast}\left(\varepsilon\right)\psi\left(\varepsilon,x\right)d\varepsilon.

(C16)

Appendix D

We recall the following regarding the Morse Hamiltonian. The
potential is given by

V\left(x\right)=V_{0}\left(e^{-2\beta x}-2e^{-\beta x}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}D^{2}

(D1)

where

D=\frac{\sqrt{2V_{0}}}{\beta}-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(K-1\right),\text{ \ }y=\frac{\sqrt{8V_{0}}}{\beta}e^{-\beta x}=Ke^{-\beta x}.

(D2)

We use Popov’s
\left[24\right]
definition of the harmonic limit of the
Morse system by adopting the following limiting values of the physical
parameters

\lim_{HO}\equiv\left|\begin{array}[]{c}V_{0}\rightarrow\infty\\
K\rightarrow\infty\\
\beta\rightarrow 0\\
\frac{V_{0}}{K}\rightarrow\frac{1}{4}\omega\\
V_{0}\beta^{2}\rightarrow\frac{1}{2}\omega^{2}\\
K\beta^{2}\rightarrow 2\omega\end{array}\right..

(D3)

Recalling Eq.
\left(6.1\right),
we see that of the variable
\xi_{z}

satisfies
2\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\xi_{z}=K+\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\beta}\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}z-2.
By
introducing the variable

Y=\frac{y-2\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\xi}{\sqrt{2\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\xi}},

(D4)

then

Y\equiv\frac{\left[-K\beta^{2}x+\frac{1}{2}K\beta^{3}x^{2}-...\right]\left[2\sqrt{2}\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}z-2\beta\right]}{\sqrt{K\beta^{2}+2\beta\sqrt{2}\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}z-2\beta^{2}}}

(D5)

One may check that

\lim_{HO}Y\equiv-\sqrt{2\omega}\left(x+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\omega}\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}z\right)

By another hand, we may apply the following limit for the Laguerre

\left(\left[28\right]\right):

\left(-1\right)^{n}n!2^{\frac{1}{2}n}\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow\infty}\left[\alpha^{-\frac{1}{2}n}L_{n}^{\left(\alpha\right)}\left(\alpha+u\sqrt{2\alpha}\right)\right]=H_{n}\left(u\right)

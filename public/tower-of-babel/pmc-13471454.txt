Objective

The oral cavity offers diverse microhabitats for streptococci, with subgingival biofilm serving as a unique ecological niche defined by anaerobic conditions and intimate host–microbe interactions [1–5]. Understanding how bacteria adapt to such site-specific environments is crucial for elucidating their ecological roles within the oral microbiome [1, 6]. However, progress in this area is often constrained by the availability of genomic information for individual resident species [3, 7].

Streptococcus lingualis is a recently described member of the genus Streptococcus that has been isolated from human saliva [8]. Although its taxonomic characterization has been established, genomic information for this species remains extremely limited, with only a single complete genome sequence currently available for the type strain S5ᵀ [9]. Consequently, the genomic basis underlying the ecological distribution and potential site-specific adaptation of S. lingualis within the oral cavity remains poorly understood.

To address this knowledge gap, we performed complete genome sequencing of a S. lingualis strain isolated from subgingival biofilm of a healthy adult. The primary objective of this study was to generate a high-quality, complete genome sequence that expands the currently limited genomic resources available for S. lingualis. This genome serves as a reference for comparative genomic analyses with the type strain S5ᵀ and other phylogenetically related oral streptococci. By providing genomic data derived from a defined oral niche, this dataset supports analyses of strain-level genomic diversity, population structure, and site-specific adaptations of S. lingualis within the human oral microbiome.

Data description

The S. lingualis strain KHUD_012 was isolated from subgingival biofilm of a healthy Korean male adult and grown on Fastidious Anaerobe Agar (MB cell, Republic of Korea) supplemented with 0.25% (w/v) porcine gastric mucin and defibrinated sheep blood under anaerobic conditions at 37 °C. Genomic DNA was extracted using the Wizard HMW DNA Extraction Kit (Promega, USA). Species identity was confirmed by sequencing the nearly full-length 16 S rRNA gene (~ 1.5 kb), amplified using universal bacterial primers (forward: 5′-AGAGTTTGATCCTGGCTCAG-3′; reverse: 5′-GGTTACCTTGTTACGACTT-3′) [10], and comparing the resulting sequence to entries in the GenBank database using BLAST.

Whole-genome sequencing of the S. lingualis strain KHUD_012 was performed on the PacBio Sequel IIe platform. A SMRTbell library was prepared according to the manufacturer’s protocols (Pacific Biosciences). Sequencing of a single SMRT cell yielded 144,821 HiFi reads, with a mean read length of 9,187 bp and an N50 of 10,172 bp (Data file 1). The genome was assembled de novo using the IPA HiFi genome assembler v2.0 (Pacific Biosciences) under default parameters. Genome annotation was conducted using the NCBI Prokaryotic Genome Annotation Pipeline (PGAP).

The complete genome of S. lingualis KHUD_012 consists of a single circular chromosome of 2,115,610 bp with a G + C content of 42.0% (Data files 2 and 3). Genome annotation identified 1,956 protein-coding sequences, four rRNA operons, and 62 tRNA genes. BUSCO [11] analysis indicated 99.5% completeness (Data file 4). Pairwise whole-genome comparisons with the type strains S. lingualis S5ᵀ, S. gingivalis S2ᵀ, and S. dentalis S1ᵀ showed that KHUD_012 was most closely related to S. lingualis S5ᵀ, with an OrthoANI value of 96.44% (Data file 5). Although the dDDH value was 69.6%, its 95% confidence interval (66.6–72.4%) included the 70% species threshold. A core-genome SNP-based phylogenetic tree generated using Parsnp further supported the placement of KHUD_012 relative to the compared type strains (Data file 6).

Comparative genomic analysis against the only publicly available S. lingualis genome, the type strain S5ᵀ (assembly ASM3413480v1, isolated from saliva) identified 1,717 core gene clusters shared between the two strains and 384 strain-specific clusters (Data file 7). Strain KHUD_012 harbored 198 unique gene clusters containing 201 genes (Data file 8). Functional annotation of these unique gene clusters revealed that a large proportion was assigned to COG categories “–”, indicating a substantial fraction of uncharacterized genes. Among annotated categories, genes related to transcription (K), replication, recombination, and repair (L), defense mechanisms (V), and cell wall/membrane biogenesis (M) were most abundant, suggesting potential roles in genomic stability and niche adaptation within the subgingival environment.

The complete genome sequence of S. lingualis strain KHUD_012 adds a high-quality genomic resource derived from a subgingival biofilm isolate and expands the currently limited genomic representation of this species. This genome enables direct comparative analyses with the type strain S5ᵀ and provides strain-level genomic information relevant to understanding genetic diversity within S. lingualis. Limitation

This study is based on the genomic characterization of a single Streptococcus lingualis isolate (KHUD_012) obtained from a healthy Korean adult. Therefore, the genomic features described here may not fully represent the pangenomic diversity or global population structure of the species. Additional genomes from diverse hosts, geographic regions, and oral niches will be required to comprehensively elucidate intraspecies variation and ecological adaptation.

Data availability

Data files 1–6 described in this Data note can be freely and openly accessible on Figshare (https://figshare.com/) 12–19 . Data sets 1–2 are available on the NCBI database. The raw sequencing reads have been deposited in the NCBI Sequence Read Archive (SRA) under accession number SRX31836078 (Data Set 1) [18]. The genome assembly of the *S. lingualis* KHUD_012 was submitted to NCBI GenBank and is available under the accession number GCA_052588255.1 (Data set 2) [19].

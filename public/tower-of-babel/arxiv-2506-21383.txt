Introduction

Let
\mathbb{N}
denote the set of positive integers, and

\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Throughout this paper all abelian
groups will be written additively, and for
n,r\in\mathbb{N}
, we let

C_{n}
denote the cyclic group of
n
elements.
Let
G
be an additive finite abelian group and
G^{*}=G\setminus\{0\}
.
It is well known that
|G|=1
or
G=C_{n_{1}}\oplus C_{n_{2}}\oplus\cdots\oplus C_{n_{r}}
, where
1<n_{1}|n_{2}|\cdots|n_{r}
.
Then
r(G)=r
is the rank of
G
and

\exp(G)=n_{r}
is the exponent of
G
. Let

S:=g_{1}\cdots g_{l}

be a sequence with elements in
G
.
We call
S
a zero-sum sequence if
g_{1}+\cdots+g_{l}=0
holds in
G
.
Let
\mathsf{D}(G)
denote the
Davenport constant
of
G
, which is defined
as the smallest positive integer
t
such that every sequence
S
over

G
of length
|S|\geq t
has a nonempty zero-sum subsequence. Set

\mathsf{D}^{*}(G):=1+\sum_{i=1}^{r}(n_{i}-1).

Obviously, we have
\mathsf{D}(G)\geq\mathsf{D}^{*}(G)
.
In most of the cases, a direct zero-sum problem asks for the
the smallest integer
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
such that every sequence
S
over
G

with length
|S|\geq\ell
has a zero-sum subsequence with prescribed length.

Let

be a nonempty subset and let
\mathsf{s}_{L}(G)
be the
smallest
\ell\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
such that every sequence
S
over
G

has a zero-sum subsequence
T
with length
|T|\in L
. Thus for the classical
zero-sum invariants, we clearly have
\mathsf{D}(G)=\mathsf{s}_{\mathbb{N}}(G)
,

\eta(G)=\mathsf{s}_{[1,\exp(G)]}(G)
,
\mathsf{s}(G)=\mathsf{s}_{\{\exp(G)\}}(G)
, and
\mathsf{s}_{k\exp(G)}(G)=\mathsf{s}_{\{k\exp(G)\}}(G)
,
where
\mathsf{s}(G)
is called
Erdös-Ginzburg-Ziv constant
of
G
and
\mathsf{s}_{k\exp(G)}(G)
is called
Generalized Erdös-Ginzburg-Ziv constant
of

.

Definition 1.1
.

Denote by
\mathsf{s}_{\leq k}(G)=\mathsf{s}_{[1,k]}(G)
the smallest positive integer

Throughout the paper, for real numbers

.

We consider the sequences as elements of the
free abelian monoid
\mathscr{F}(G)
over
G
and our notations and
terminologies coincide with

,
18
,
20
]

Let

G
, where
\mathsf{v}_{g}(S)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

of

is the

of
S
.
The

of elements in
S
is denoted by
\sigma(S)=\sum^{\ell}_{i=1}g_{i}=\sum_{g\in G}v_{g}(S)\cdot g\in G
, and the maximal repetition of a

.
A sequence

is called a

\sum^{\ell}_{i=1}g_{i}=0\in G
.
We say

\mathsf{v}_{g}(S)\geq\mathsf{v}_{g}(T)

and denote it by

of

, then we call

of

If

is

. We say that

is a zero-sum sequence
and each proper subsequence is zero-free, then

is called a

.
By
\mathsf{supp}(S)
we denote the subset of

which

. We write

as a subgroup of

. If

the sequence obtained from

by deleting

. If

, we denote by

the sequence obtained from

. We also write

In

We will use the following lemmas repeatly.

Lemma 4.1

(Lucas’ Theorem,
[

Let

For any integer

(

c_{1},u_{1}\in[1,p-1]

.
It follows that

Note that if

,

.
Since

, we have that

, which implies that

(4.7)

\displaystyle p-1\geq u_{1}\geq c_{1}\geq 1.

Lemma 4.7
.

Suppose that
2k-\mathsf{D}(G)\geq p+d-v
.
If

{{u_{1}\choose c_{1}-1}}+(-1)^{p+d-v}\times{{u_{1}+1\choose c_{1}}}\not\equiv 0\mod p,

Proof.

By the definition of

, it suffices to prove that

Since

4.3
) of

, we have that

\displaystyle a_{p+d-v}

\displaystyle={{up+v\choose(c-1)p+v}}+(-1)^{p+d-v}\times{{(u+1)p+d-1\choose cp+d-1}}

\displaystyle\equiv{{u\choose c-1}}{{v\choose v}}+(-1)^{p+d-v}\times{{u+1\choose c}}{{d-1\choose d-1}}

\displaystyle={{u_{1}p^{t}+u_{2}\choose c_{1}p^{t}-1}}+(-1)^{p+d-v}\times{{u_{1}p^{t}+u_{2}+1\choose c_{1}p^{t}}}\mod p.

If

Now suppose that

\displaystyle a_{p+d-v}

\displaystyle\equiv{{u_{1}p^{t}+u_{2}\choose c_{1}p^{t}-1}}+(-1)^{p+d-v}\times{{u_{1}p^{t}+u_{2}+1\choose c_{1}p^{t}}}

\displaystyle\equiv{{u_{1}\choose c_{1}-1}}{{u_{2}\choose p^{t}-1}}+(-1)^{p+d-v}\times{{u_{1}\choose c_{1}}}{{u_{2}+1\choose 0}}

If

, then it follows from the above that

\displaystyle a_{p+d-v}

\displaystyle\equiv{{u_{1}p^{t}+u_{2}\choose c_{1}p^{t}-1}}+(-1)^{p+d-v}\times{{u_{1}p^{t}+u_{2}+1\choose c_{1}p^{t}}}

\displaystyle={{(u_{1}+1)p^{t}-1\choose c_{1}p^{t}-1}}+(-1)^{p+d-v}\times{{(u_{1}+1)p^{t}\choose c_{1}p^{t}}}

\displaystyle\equiv{{u_{1}\choose c_{1}-1}}{{p^{t}-1\choose p^{t}-1}}+(-1)^{p+d-v}\times{{u_{1}+1\choose c_{1}}}{{0\choose 0}}

\displaystyle={{u_{1}\choose c_{1}-1}}+(-1)^{p+d-v}\times{{u_{1}+1\choose c_{1}}}\not\equiv 0\mod p.

The last inequality follows from the lemma’s assumed condition.
We complete the proof.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian
p
-group and
k\in[\exp(G)+1,\mathsf{D}(G)]
be a positive integer.
Let
S
be a sequence over
G
of length
2\mathsf{D}(G)-k+1
.
Suppose that
\mathsf{N}^{i}(S)=0
for
i\in[\mathsf{D}(G)+1,|S|]
.
If
\binom{\mathsf{D}(G)}{k-1}\not\equiv 0\mod p
, then
S
has a nonempty zero-sum subsequence
T
with
|T|\leq k-1
.

Proof.

Suppose that each zero-sum subsequence of
S
has the length
\geq k
.
Since
\mathsf{N}^{i}(S)=0
for
i\in[\mathsf{D}(G)+1,|S|]
,
by Lemma
4.2
we have that the following equation holds for any
T|S
with
|T|\in[\mathsf{D}(G),|S|]

1+(-1)^{k}\mathsf{N}^{k}(T)+\ldots+(-1)^{\mathsf{D}(G)}\mathsf{N}^{\mathsf{D}(G)}(T)\equiv 0\mod p.

It follows that for
0\leq t\leq|S|-\mathsf{D}(G)

\sum\limits_{T\mid S,\ |T|=|S|-t}\big(1+(-1)^{k}\mathsf{N}^{k}(T)+\ldots+(-1)^{\mathsf{D}(G)}\mathsf{N}^{\mathsf{D}(G)}(T)\big)\equiv 0\mod p.

Analysing the number of times each subsequence is counted, one obtains

(5.1)

\displaystyle\binom{|S|}{|T|}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{k}\binom{|S|-k}{|T|-k}\mathsf{N}^{k}(S)

\displaystyle+\ldots+(-1)^{\mathsf{D}(G)}\binom{|S|-\mathsf{D}(G)}{|T|-\mathsf{D}(G)}\mathsf{N}^{\mathsf{D}(G)}(S)

\displaystyle=\binom{|S|}{t}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{k}\binom{|S|-k}{t}\mathsf{N}^{k}(S)

\displaystyle+\ldots+(-1)^{\mathsf{D}(G)}\binom{|S|-\mathsf{D}(G)}{t}\mathsf{N}^{\mathsf{D}(G)}(S)\equiv 0\mod p.

Set
X=(1,(-1)^{k}\mathsf{N}^{k}(S),\ldots,(-1)^{\mathsf{D}(G)}\mathsf{N}^{\mathsf{D}(G)}(S))^{T}
and

A:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}{{|S|\choose 0}}&{{|S|-k\choose 0}}&\cdots&{{|S|-\mathsf{D}(G)\choose 0}}\\
{{|S|\choose 1}}&{{|S|-k\choose 1}}&\cdots&{{|S|-\mathsf{D}(G)\choose 1}}\\
{\vdots}&{\vdots}&{\vdots}&{\vdots}\\
{{|S|\choose|S|-\mathsf{D}(G)}}&{{|S|-k\choose|S|-\mathsf{D}(G)}}&\cdots&{{|S|-\mathsf{D}(G)\choose|S|-\mathsf{D}(G)}}\\
\end{array}\right)_{(|S|-\mathsf{D}(G)+1)\times(\mathsf{D}(G)-k+2)}.

On the one hand, it can be deduced from (
5.1
) that

AX\equiv 0\mod p.

It follows from Lemma
4.3
that by some row transformations,

can be brought to the following form

A^{\prime}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}{{\mathsf{D}(G)\choose 0}}&{{\mathsf{D}(G)-k\choose 0}}&\cdots&{{0\choose 0}}\\
{{\mathsf{D}(G)\choose 1}}&{{\mathsf{D}(G)-k\choose 1}}&\cdots&{{0\choose 1}}\\
{\vdots}&{\vdots}&{\vdots}&{\vdots}\\
{{\mathsf{D}(G)\choose|S|-\mathsf{D}(G)}}&{{\mathsf{D}(G)-k\choose|S|-\mathsf{D}(G)}}&\cdots&{{0\choose|S|-\mathsf{D}(G)}}\\
\end{array}\right)_{(|S|-\mathsf{D}(G)+1)\times(\mathsf{D}(G)-k+2)}

and

AX\equiv A^{\prime}X\equiv 0\mod p.

Since
|S|=2\mathsf{D}(G)-k+1
, we have that
|S|-\mathsf{D}(G)+1=\mathsf{D}(G)-k+2
. It follows that
A^{\prime}
is a square matrix and
|S|-\mathsf{D}(G)>\mathsf{D}(G)-k
.
Note that
\binom{a}{b}=0
if
0\leq a<b
. We have that

{{\mathsf{D}(G)-k\choose|S|-\mathsf{D}(G)}}=\cdots={{0\choose|S|-\mathsf{D}(G)}}=0.

Thus

\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}{{\mathsf{D}(G)\choose|S|-\mathsf{D}(G)}}&{{\mathsf{D}(G)-k\choose|S|-\mathsf{D}(G)}}&\cdots&{{0\choose|S|-\mathsf{D}(G)}}\\
\end{array}\right)X={{\mathsf{D}(G)\choose|S|-\mathsf{D}(G)}}=\binom{\mathsf{D}(G)}{k-1}\equiv 0\ \text{mod}\ p.

This is a contradiction to
\binom{\mathsf{D}(G)}{k-1}\not\equiv 0\mod p
.
We complete the proof.
∎

Strategy formulation of agricultural machinery development for sustainable potato farming in upland of Banjarnegara Regency, Central Java, Indonesia
Authors: Dewi Sahara, Cahyati Setiani, Dewa Ketut Sadra Swastika, Robet Asnawi, Sri Bananiek Sugiman, Andi Yulyani Fadwiwati, Tota Suhendrata, Amiruddin Syam, Agus Supriyo, Atman, Triyani Dewi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the agricultural sector, labor reduction is a significant obstacle to increasing potato production and the use of machinery is considered an effective solution. Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the potential and profitability of implementing agricultural machinery and develop a specific strategy in Banjarnegara Regency, Central Java, Indonesia. Data collection was carried out from September 2021 to April 2022, and the demonstration farming (demfarm) method was used to test three technology package, with and without agricultural machinery and with existing technology as a control. The study also surveyed farmer characteristics, use the demfarm method, and conducted focus group discussion on internal and external indicators influencing agricultural machinery development. Data analysis included the benefit-cost ratio (BCR), marginal BCR, and strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats (SWOT). The results showed that the development of agricultural machinery for potato cultivation was highly profitable, with a BCR of 1.28, suggesting that the development of agricultural machinery is worthwhile. The agricultural machinery technology demonstrated the best performance among the three packages studied. Based on the results, the policy strategy to support the sustainability of potato farming in Central Java involves the application of efficient agricultural technology by optimizing land availability, involving farmer institutions, and the use of agricultural machinery.Keywords: Agricultural machinery, Technological change, Potato farming, Development strategy

Introduction

Potato (Solanum tuberosum L.) is a plant that originated from the Andes in South America and has been developed in Europe, Asia, and other parts of the world [1], including 160 countries. The plant is a horticultural crop potentially used as a source of carbohydrates to replace rice and wheat. According to various studies, potato is considered the main food globally similar to rice, wheat, and corn [[2], [3], [4]]. As one of the best alternatives for providing the same nutrition as rice, the demand for potato has increased in recent years [5]. Therefore, it is necessary to increase production by expanding planting areas or by implementing modern agricultural technology innovations [6].

Cultivation should be concentrated in suitable areas to increase yields and economic benefits, improve ecological stability, and support agricultural sustainability [7]. A highly suitable land (S1) has a slope of <3 %, a moderately suitable land (S2) has a slope of 3%–8%, and marginally suitable land (S3) has a slope of 8%–15 % [8]. High potato yields are achieved on loamy and sandy loam soils rich in organic matter, good drainage and aeration are the main requirements [9].

Intensification and extensification programs have been implemented to increase potato production nationally. These programs increase planting intensity (PI) and area while reducing yield losses. Their requirements include agricultural machinery, an analysis of the suitability and needs for labor input, planting area, PI, and agroecosystem suitability. The availability and suitability of these inputs produces the desired output, including increased production targets, low production costs per product unit, improved farmer income, and improved labor efficiency. Furthermore, government policies, education, and adopted technology must support analyses of the needs and suitability of agricultural machinery [10].

Central Java Province is the second largest potato production center after East Java in Indonesia, producing 277,725 tons of potatoes and contributing 20.40 % of the national potato production. One of the production centers in the province is Banjarnegara Regency, which drives the economic growth of farmers in the northern uplands. Potato productivity in 2020 was 19.84 tons/ha, which was lower than the value reported in a previous study (25.87 tons/ha) [11]. This yield gap is caused by conventional cultivation systems that use self-harvested seeds, chemical pest and diseases control, and unbalanced fertilizer use. Increasing production is difficult to achieve when conventional agricultural systems employ intensive labor [12], especially for land preparation, planting, and harvesting [13]. Moreover, the workforce in the agricultural sector is relatively old, and the numbers are decreasing [14], underscoring the need for strategies and alternatives to attract youth to increase land and labor productivity, as well as to overcome labor scarcity. These targets can be achieved through the development of modern agriculture using machinery. In Banjarnegara Regency, factors such as land area, fertilizers, and pesticides affect potato production, which remains profitable with an R/C ratio of 1.77 [15]. Potato yields increased by 2999 kg/ha (or 9.92 %) with dip fertilization compared with broadcast fertilization. According to a previous study, the shift from broadcast to dip fertilization technology yielded significant benefits, with a marginal benefit-cost ratio (MBCR) of 8.11 [16]. Production inputs for potato farming vary among farmers and often differ from recommended practices. In particular, seeds, manure, and labor are the inputs that influence production [17]. Potatoes farming in Indonesia from 2008 to 2017 was highly profitable, as indicated by an increasing R/C ratio [18].

The development of agricultural machinery in Indonesia generally requires good mapping and institutions to increase effectiveness. Using agricultural machinery can reduce farming costs and provide farmers with profit, thus allowing effective production [10]. It also has good prospects when preceded by mapping the needs and potential institutional environment, leading to decreased farming costs, and improved farming efficiency.

The application of agricultural machinery has been carried out to overcome labor shortages and working hours and to increase worker productivity and agricultural output [[19], [20], [21], [22]]. In addition to being a rural revitalization strategy, the use of agricultural machinery has a positive effect on production costs and output values, increasing farmers’ income, and minimizes negative climate conditions [23]. It also reduces the yield losses of rice, wheat [24,25], and sesame [26]. In developing agricultural machinery, technical factors must be considered as well as the social aspects of farmers, including limited land ownership, capital, and their limited education level [27].

Technological innovation in using agricultural machinery for grow potatoes cultivation is a new idea in Central Java, especially in Banjarnegara Regency. Agricultural machine technology that supports potato consists of land-processing machines (four-wheel tractors), rotaries, mound makers, planting machines, and harvesting machines.

The use of agricultural machinery plays a vital role in (a) providing additional mechanical power as a complement to the shortage of human labor, (b) increasing labor productivity and the added value of agricultural products and by-products, (c) reducing yield losses and maintaining product quality, (d) supporting the provision of facilities (input), (e) reducing labor intensity in agricultural production activities, and (f) transforming traditional agriculture into more efficient modern agriculture.

The novelty of this study is that agricultural machinery used for potato farming in Central Java has never been used and profitability and development strategies have yet to be determined. Therefore, this study aimed to analyze the characteristics of farmers, the performance of agricultural machinery, and the profitability of implementation, as well as to formulate specific strategies for the development of potato farming in Central Java.

Methods

Conceptual framework

Labor shortages are a serious issue affecting the productivity and efficiency of agricultural businesses [20,28]. Factors causing labor shortages include the low interest of the younger generation in the agricultural sector, labor migration to other sectors that offer higher wages and better working conditions, and demographic changes, such as the aging of rural populations [29,30]. Labor shortages lead to increased production costs, decreased productivity, and reduced cultivation land area. Meanwhile, existing potato farming carried out by farmers uses production inputs without considering plant needs and land conditions, leading to low production of X tons/ha with a profit of IDR Y/season. Existing farming requires more labor than machines, especially for soil cultivation, ridge creation, planting, and harvesting. As stated by Ref. [19], using agricultural machinery is one way to increase practical farming but requires land suitability and business scale. This condition increases production costs, which in turn reduces profits [31,32]. New technology with more appropriate production inputs in terms of type and quantity without the use of agricultural machinery increased productivity from X to (X + a) tons/ha with a profit of IDR (Y + i)/season. Using agricultural machinery technology, specifically ridge, planting, and harvesting machines, alongside proper production inputs further enhanced yields to (X + a + b) tons/ha, with profits of IDR (Y + i + j)/season.

[20] reported that using agricultural machinery that replaces a part of the workforce makes farming more productive and efficient. According to Ref. [33], planting machines can increase the efficiency of manual labor. The transition of existing farming businesses to agricultural machinery is an effective solution to reducing the dependence on labor and increasing efficiency, productivity, and profits. It can be developed in areas with expansive characteristics and access to farm roads [30].

New technologies using agricultural machinery have produced the best and most feasible demonstration farming (demfarm) results. Technology development requires a survey to determine farmers’ characteristics, forum group discussion (FGD), and internal and external factors. In particular, internal and external factors were identified through in-depth interviews and discussions with key informants and other participants, including farmers, and policymakers. The FGD results were analyzed using SWOT to developed alternative strategies for developing agricultural machinery. The framework of this study is summarized in Fig. 1.

Study location

The study was conducted in Sumberejo Village, Batur District, Banjarnegara Regency (Fig. 2). It has the largest potato production center in Central Java, with a harvested area of 16,386 ha, accounting for 22.85 % of the national harvested area in Indonesia, according to BPS-Statistic of Indonesia for 2022. Sumberejo Village is located in the mountainous area of the Dieng Plateau, with an altitude of more than 1700 m above sea level and a temperature of 9–20oC. The study was conducted from September 2021 to April 2022.

Implementation methods

a.Survey research

Survey research

The study was conducted using the survey, demfarm, and FGD methods. The survey method was used to determine the characteristics of farmers engaged in potato farming. Respondents were selected randomly, with 87 people accounting for 13.57 % of the 641 farmers who met the criteria to be included in the survey [34].

b.Demfarm research

Demfarm research

The demfarm method was used to test three technology packages: one with agricultural machinery, one without agricultural machinery, and one with existing technology. Farming technology with and without agricultural machinery was controlled; hence, the use of inputs was the same in quantity and type, except for the use of human labor and agricultural machinery. The application of the three technology packages was for 1.5 ha of land (three farmers) as cooperative farmers in the demfarm, whereas existing technology was treated as a comparison (control).

c.Focus group discussions

Focus group discussions

In addition to observations on the demfarm, in-depth interviews were conducted with key informants/experts and policymakers in the study area during FGDs. FGD is a method for collecting qualitative data by conducting group interviews to understand the main problems. The participants included 15 key informants who understood potato farming both manually and using agricultural machinery namely, the head of the farmer group, agricultural extension workers, agricultural machinery service providers (AMSPs), policymakers at the village, subdistrict and district levels, and individuals from the Center for Agricultural Mechanization. FGDs related to internal and external factors for developing agricultural machinery for potato farming were led by a moderator. The results are presented as data or information derived from internal and external factors that can be used as recommendations for agricultural machinery development policies in potato farming.

Data collection

Primary data were collected through the following: (1) surveys covering farmer characteristics; (2) demonstration farms, which involved observations of agricultural machinery performance, the amount and cost of using production inputs (seeds, fertilizers, pesticides, labor, machine rental), and the amount and price of production output from the three technologies packages; and (3) FGDs which covered indicators of internal factors (strengths and weaknesses) and external factors (opportunities and threats) of agricultural machinery development in potato farming in Central Java.

Data analysis

Descriptive statistical analysis was used to analyze the characteristics of farmers and agricultural machinery used in potato cultivation, presented has minimum, maximum, mean, and standard deviation values and operational capacity/capabilities.

A specific strategy for developing agricultural machinery was developed by analyzing strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats (SWOTs). In general, SWOT analysis is a method for strategic planning that evaluates each business development's strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats [39,40]. The analysis was initiated by identifying internal strengths (S) and weaknesses (W) in agricultural machinery development, as well as opportunities (O) and threats (T) from the external environment. This approach was used in Ref. [41] to develop a strategy for developing sorghum in Indonesia. Internal strategy factor analysis Summary (IFAS) and external strategy factor analysis summary (EFAS) were also calculated. Before calculating the matrices, the weighted values, ratings, and scores of each strength, weakness, opportunity, and threat factor were initially determined. Specifically, the weight was determined by comparing the value of each horizontal factor with that of the vertical factor. In the assessment calculation, there are four scales: 1 (has no effect), 2 (quite influential), 3 (influential), and 4 (very influential). The score value was obtained based on the weighted value calculation results multiplied by the rating value [42]. Subsequently, all the weighted values, ratings, and scores obtained from each factor of S, W, O, and T were tabulated into an IFAS–EFAS matrix [43]. Using the SWOT strategy formulation, operational policy strategies were developing using four main strategies in four SWOT quadrants: (1) quadrant I: expansion (S–O), strategy using S to achieve O; (2) quadrant II: diversification (S–T), the strategy used S to minimize T; (3) quadrant III: stability or rationalization (W–O), the strategy to overcome W by using O; and (4) quadrant IV: defensive (W–T), the strategy to improve W by minimizing T [41].

Results

Characteristics of farmers and agricultural machinery

The majority of farmers were in the age range of 21–60 years (95.56 %), whereas the remaining 4.44 % were either younger than 21 years or older than 60 years. The highest level of education was university, with a period of 17 years, and the lowest was 6 years with an education level equivalent to elementary school. Most farmers completed high school (44.44 %), followed by middle school (26.67 %), university (15.56 %), and elementary school (13.33 %). Family sizes ranged from two to six members, with an average of four. Most farmers had four family members (46.67 %), followed by five (20.00 %), three (13.33 %), two (11.11 %), and six (8.89 %).

On average, farmers had planted potatoes for 15.73 years, ranging from 1 to 31 years in an average area of 2.93 ha, with land ownership ranging from 1 to 17 ha. A significant proportion were also classified as large-scale farmers with land ownership of >1 ha (Table 1).

The combination of internal factors, namely strengths (S) and weaknesses (W), produced a total IFAS score of 5.80 for the development of agricultural machinery in potato farming. This comprised a strength value of 3.58 with the highest score being 1.26 (scarcity/shortage of labor), and a weakness score of 2.22, with the highest score being 0.67 (limited capital in farming and land slope). A resultant score of 1.36 was obtained from these values, suggesting that the strength factor was more dominant than the weakness factor. Therefore, actively developing agricultural machinery for potato farming is a strong position for further advancement.

The results also demonstrate that the combination of the opportunity (O) and threat (T) factors produced a total EFAS score of 6.70. This comprised an opportunity score of 3.93, with the highest being 1.60 (the support of government policy). In addition, the threat score was 2.77, with the highest score being 1.15 (low access to fuel) (Fig. 3). A resultant score of 1.16 was obtained from these values, suggesting that the opportunity factor was more dominant than the threat factor. Therefore, there is an opportunity to develop agricultural machinery for potato farming.

Diagram of the SWOT quadrant analysis for developing potato farming machinery in Central Java, Indonesia.

The total score of internal factors (5.80) was lower than that of external factors (6.70), indicating that external factors largely determine the development of agricultural machinery for potato farming. Using SWOT quadrant analysis, the coordinate points of strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats were assessed to determine priority strategies and alternative approaches for developing agricultural machinery in potato cultivation. This process aggregated scores obtained from the IFAS and EFAS analysis. The coordinate points of the SWOT quadrant were as follows: X = 1.36 and Y = 1.16. This implied that the influence of strengths was more dominant than the influence of weaknesses, and the impact of opportunities was more prevalent than the impact of threats. This coordinate was in quadrant I, suggesting an expansion strategy (S–O), that is, using strengths (S) to achieve existing opportunities (O). Based on these results, the application of agricultural machinery for potato farming has a strong position and presents an opportunity for development.

Discussion

Characteristics of farmers and agricultural machinery

The majority of farmers (95.56 %) under study were of productive age, ranging from 21 to 60 years old, and engaged in potato farming in Banjarnegara Regency (Table 1). The productive age makes it easier for farmers to accept new technological innovations, including the implementation of agricultural machinery. Older farmers (over 60 years) rarely accept new technological innovations, preferring to keep up with traditional practices and only using innovations when given incentives [46]. According to a previous study, age influences the understanding of agricultural technology and innovation [47]. Observations have also revealed a positive correlation between age and technology adoption [48]. Young farmers are more open to modern technology and receptive to new methods that improving farming productivity and efficiency [49,50].

Regarding education, most farmers graduated from high school, and some even completed university. This educational background has a positive impact on the application and development of agricultural machinery. As stated in a previous study, formal education is critical for shaping farmer behavior [51]. Higher education can improve farmers' knowledge of modern cultivation techniques, efficient land management methods, and sustainable agricultural practices [52,53]. Furthermore, higher education may serve as a motivator and agent of change in technology use. Highly educated farmers tend to be more efficient in production [48] and relatively quick in understanding the benefits of new technology [54]. This is because higher education influences farmers’ attitudes and thinking, making them more open, rational, and able to analyze the benefits of new technology when applied to crop cultivation.

On average, Banjarnegara farmers have planted potatoes for 15.73 years, ranging from 1 to 31 years. Extended experience enhances potato cultivation performance and plant productivity [55]. Moreover, experienced farmers tend to be more selective and precise in choosing innovation types. These farmers are often more careful in decision-making process [54], and advise others to adopt the use of agricultural machinery [12].

Based on the results, the average number of members in a farming family was four, with approximately 71.11 % having two to four members and the remaining 28.89 % having more than four members. Farmer family members may serve as human resources in the production process [56]. However, small family members require assistance [57], in the form of additional labor to run the farming business optimally. Several family members actively engaged in farming can positively impact agricultural performance, income, and socioeconomic welfare.

Soil is a primary resource for agricultural production [58,59] and based on these results, most farmers (78.05 %) own land between 0.5 and 3 ha. Farmers with large land areas are relatively flexible in terms of adopting to technological innovations [50].

Agricultural machinery for potato cultivation in Banjarnegara Regency is a new technological innovation for developing modern agriculture. It consists of four units: a tractor, ridge tool, planting machine, and harvester machine. The mounds formed are adjusted to machine specifications including a width of 90 cm and a capacity of 8 h/ha while planter and harvester machines also operate with a capacity of 8 h/ha. Furthermore, soil tillage, planting, and harvesting are the three main activities of potato production. Among these activities, planting is one of the most important factors affecting production quality and potato yield [13].

Several processes are involved in preparing land for potato cultivation, including soil tillage and mound preparation. Soil tillage is performed using tractors and rotaries, whereas the mounds are prepared using rolling equipment. A rotary tool is used for tilling soil, and it consists of knives on a rotating shaft driven by a motor. A four-wheel tractor with a minimum power of 40 HP drives the rotary plow forward. Furthermore, the mounding equipment capacity is 4 h/ha, and a tractor pulls the tool forward. The mounds were shaped with as bunches with a bottom width of 120 cm, top width of 90 cm, and height of 30 cm, according to the spacing between and within rows. Soil tillage and mound construction can be completed using machines operated at 8 h/ha and IDR 3,600,000. In contrast, when performed manually, individuals complete a piecework basis at IDR 7,000,000/ha in 7 days. Therefore, the machine has a cost efficiency of IDR 3,400,000/ha (48.57 %) and a time efficiency of 85.71 %.

The potato seed planter equipped with a fertilizer spreader is towed by a four-wheel tractor with a minimum power of 40 HP. This tool has two rows of planting lines with a planting distance between rows of 60 cm and spacing within rows of 30 cm. The planting distance between rows can be adjusted, for example, 40 cm. With a working speed of 3 km/h, this tool plants seeds within 8 h/ha. The cost of planting potato seeds using a planting machine is IDR 1,500,000/ha, whereas the manual method takes 30 man-days and costs IDR 2,400,000/ha. The cost difference results in IDR 900,000 or 37.50 % cost efficiency. Mechanical potato planting enhances cost efficiency and reduces labor costs [60,61].

Farmers generally harvest potatoes manually using hand hoes, spading forks, or other simple tools. These methods require a long time and are labor intensive [62]; therefore, using a machine makes harvesting time more efficient. Potato harvester machines can reduce yield losses due to physical damage by approximately 20%–25 % when harvesting tubers using a hand hoe [62,63]. The machine operation at the study location was 8 h/ha, costing IDR 1,500,000/ha. In contrast, manual harvesting is a piece-labor (by contract) system costing IDR 3,600,000/ha. This implies that using a harvester machine resulted in a cost efficiency of IDR 2,100,000/ha or 58.33 %. The high time and cost efficiency of agricultural machinery effectively overcomes labor shortage problems and increases labor costs [64,65].

Profitability of potato farming

The highest cost of potato farming with and without machines was from purchasing seeds, accounting for 54 % of the total cost, except for existing farming technology (49.36 %). This was followed by labor costs which accounted for 19.51 %, 23.72 %, and 28.05 % of the total costs of farming technology using machines, manual methods, and existing technology, respectively. The variations in the number of seeds can be attributed to different tuber sizes. Furthermore, the use of agricultural machinery can reduce labor costs. Based on the results, manual methods accounted for the highest total cost of potato farming at IDR 122,235,000/ha, whereas the lowest cost was found for existing technology, at IDR 89,140,000/ha. Farmers who plant potatoes using planter machines can reduce farming costs because rental services are cheaper than manual labor and can shorten work time [66]. Planting machines can reduce production costs [45,64,67]. The highest potato production was recorded in farming with and without agricultural machinery at 32,205 and 30,200 tons/ha, respectively, whereas the lowest was found in the existing technology group at 22,155 kg/ha. Agricultural technology and devices increase production by enabling land leveling, preparation, deep turning, and scarification, all of which enhance land capacity compared with manual and traditional farming practices. In addition, planting potato seeds using a planter machine allows the plant population to grow uniformly and promotes better growth [68]. Farming technology using agricultural machinery effectively increases yields [61,68].

Replacing the production method with agricultural machinery resulted in additional profits of IDR 55,715,000 and IDR 22,841,000/ha, for existing farmers and manual new technology, respectively. These results are in line with the previous studies [69,70], which found that using agricultural machinery can reduce production costs and increase harvest yields in terms of quality and quantity. In other words, the profits obtained are higher. Similarly, this study is consistent with the results of [71], which stated that using agricultural machinery leads to cost savings and increased production. An increase in labor costs decreases profits [52], which are generally higher than the cost of renting agricultural machinery services in general [67,72]. For instance, using potato diggers in Ethiopia increased net income by 500 ETB/ha compared with profits from traditional plowing [73].

The benefits of potato farming using agricultural machinery, as indicated by the BCR, are more significant than those of new manual technology, (1.28 compared with 1.03 and 1.04). A BCR of 1.28 for agricultural machinery technology indicates that farmers earn a profit of IDR 1280 for every IDR 1000 expenditure. Swastika et al. [74] found similar results in West and Central Java, obtaining benefits ranging from 52 % to 139 % of the total cost. Another example is potato farming in Nepal, which has yielded a benefit value of 173 % of the total costs. Based on these results, potato farming, especially using agricultural machinery, can be profitable for farmers and can be prospective for long-term development [75].

The MBCR analysis results for the transition from manual machinery to agricultural machinery showed a value of −2.57. This negative value was due to changes in the opposite direction, specifically a decrease in production costs (negative change) of IDR 6,400,000 and an increase in revenues (positive change) of IDR 16,441,000. This suggests double gains and an increase in income of IDR 22,841,000 from two sources of change. Specifically, changing manual methods to agricultural machinery obtained an MBCR of 3.09, suggesting that an additional cost of IDR 1000 due to agricultural machinery resulted in an additional revenue of IDR 3090. These results imply that technological change from manual and existing technology to agricultural machinery is feasible for development in the study area and holds significant potential for expansion into other Indonesian regions.

It produced four alternative strategies, namely S–O, S–T, W–O, and W–T (Table 6), offering insights for policymakers and stakeholders on the development of agricultural machinery for potato farming in Central Java (Table 7).

The SWOT analysis results were applied by creating operational policy strategies explained in the action plan (Table 7). Ideally, policymakers and stakeholders can adopt this operational policy to guide the development of strategies for advancing agricultural machinery in potato farming in Central Java.

1.Strategy S–O

Strategy S–O

The alternative S–O strategy involves is applying efficient potato farming technology by optimizing land availability and farmer institutions and using facilities and infrastructure provided by the government. A shortage of agricultural workers is among the obstacles to increasing potato production. However, it is one of the strengths and determining factors in the success of agricultural machinery development, which must be carried out in areas with labor shortages to avoid conflicts [76]. Agricultural machinery is an option that must be considered to attract the interest of the younger generation because of the decreasing availability of labor (young age) in agricultural business activities [77]. Based on data from the BPS-Statistic of Indonesia in February 2023, 40.69 million people were working in the agricultural sector, but this has decreased annually. The number of young people working in the agricultural sector has declined over the last decade. In 2011, 29.18 % of young people worked in the agricultural sector, but this percentage decreased to 19.18 % in 2021. Sustainable application of agricultural machinery improves agricultural businesses' efficiency [23,48]. The development of mechanized potato farming in Central Java requires government and private support, including (1) strengthening capital through people's business credit (PBC), and corporate social responsibility (CSR); (2) assistance or grants; (3) training in efficient and sustainable potato cultivation provided by farmer groups, and (4) training in the use and maintenance of agricultural machinery. Assistance increasing agricultural machinery use is usually provided by service providers rather than individual farmers [76].

2.Strategy W–O

Strategy W–O

The W–O strategy facilitates access to financing and improves the quality of farmer resources. Small farmers have limited capital for agricultural machinery and skills. Potato farming requires relatively large business capital and adequate skills and knowledge of agricultural machinery. The challenge for small farmers accessing mechanization is the inability to purchase equipment and secure capital support through subsidies or limited financing schemes, although rental services can be adopted [76]. To address this challenge, government and private sector support is needed in the form of (1) policies with easy procedures (without collateral) to access capital from financial institutions; (2) microfinance management training for farmer groups, farmers’ associations, and AMSPs; and (3) agricultural machinery workshop training for AMSPs (Table 7). Training to improve skills and facilitate the transition of workers from manual to machine operations must be provided by the government and private sector [78].

3.Strategy S–T

Strategy S–T

The S–T strategy establishes cooperation between farmer institutions and Pertamina/public fuel filling stations around the location. The use of agricultural machinery requires continuous supply of fuel oil at a stable price. However, potato farming is found in the highlands, where access to gas stations is relatively far. At the farmer level, scarcity or increase in fuel prices directly affect the performance of agricultural machinery service businesses. Therefore, a partnership between the local government and the private sector is needed to provide quality fuel services to farmers by building farmer fuel filling stations (FFFSs) in locations that are easily accessible to all users, such as fishermen's fuel filling stations (FFFSs) in fish landing areas. The objective is to ensure that fuel subsidies, in the form of diesel, are appropriately distributed to farmers and a steady supply is maintained without competing with other users. In addition, it is necessary to establish pioneer cooperation between farmer groups/farmer group associations/AMSPs that manage agricultural machinery with gas stations around potato farming areas (Table 7).

4.Strategy W–T

Strategy W–T

The W–T strategy facilitates effective cooperation between farmers and microfinance institutions in rural areas, gas stations, the private sector, and workshops. The problem of limited access to fuel needs to be overcome by establishing and conducting practical cooperation between farmer institutions (farmer groups, farmer groups association, and AMSPs) with microfinance institutions, gas stations, and motor vehicle workshops in the location, agricultural machinery dealers, or workshops managed by AMSPs.

Conclusion

Potato farming using agricultural machinery is profitable for farmers and has great potential in the long term both Banjarnegara Regency and other locations with similar agroecosystems in Indonesia. This was indicated by a BCR >1.00 and technological changes from manual to agricultural machinery, which resulted in an MBCR value of −2.57, as well as changes from existing to new technology, resulting an MBCR value of 3.09. The strategy for developing agricultural machinery includes the following: (1) implementing efficient technology by optimizing land availability, farmer institutions, and using government-assisted agricultural machinery; (2) establishing cooperation between farmer institutions and local gas stations; (3) facilitating access to finance and improving the quality of resources; and 4) establishing cooperation between farmers and financial institutions in rural areas, gas stations, the private sector and workshops around the location more effective.

The practical implication of this study is the development of agricultural machinery through increased cooperation between farmers, the government and the private sector. Specifically, the private sector, with its resources and expertise, can play a significant role in the study, development, and distribution of agricultural machinery. The theoretical implications of using agricultural machinery include overcoming labor shortages, and increasing productivity and profits, and improving the efficiency of farming businesses. Further studies are needed to clarify the impact of agricultural machinery on the socio-culture of rural communities.

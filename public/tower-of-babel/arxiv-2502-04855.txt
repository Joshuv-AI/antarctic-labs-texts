Open system dynamics in linear-time beyond the wide-band limit
Y. Pavlyukh, R. Tuovinen

Abstract
Nonequilibrium heat transport in quantum systems coupled to wide-band embeddings provides a striking example of the limitations of the generalized Kadanoff-Baym ansatz (GKBA), while solving the full two-time Kadanoff-Baym equations remains computationally prohibitive. To address this challenge, we propose an iterated solution to the reconstruction problem, resulting in a time-linear evolution scheme involving 14 correlators for systems with narrow-band embeddings. This approach eliminates GKBA-related artifacts and resolves convergence issues associated with the wide-band limit. Furthermore, it enables the calculation of energy- and time-resolved currents, facilitating the modeling of heat flows in quantum systems and energy- and time-resolved photoemission experiments, all at significantly reduced computational cost.

Density and currents.—

We focus here on the electronic transport mechanism and therefore start from the Kadanoff-Baym equation (KBE) for the electronic Green’s function (GF) on the Keldysh contour
\mathcal{C}
,

\left[i\frac{d}{dz}-h(z)\right]G(z,z^{\prime})=\delta(z,z^{\prime})\\
+\int_{\mathcal{C}}d\bar{z}\left[\Sigma_{c}(z,\bar{z})+\Sigma_{\mathrm{em}}(z,\bar{z})\right]G(\bar{z},z^{\prime}),

(2)

where

is the one-electron mean-field (MF) Hamiltonian,
\Sigma_{c}
is the correlation self-energy, and
\Sigma_{\mathrm{em}}=\sum_{\alpha}\Sigma_{\alpha}
is the embedding self-energy (SE) due to leads—they are matrices in a suitably chosen basis. For simplicity of presentation, we include only the latter part into consideration noting that it is possible to incorporate electronic and electron-boson correlations as demonstrated in Ref.
[
13

different physical observables including currents

\displaystyle J_{\alpha}^{\nu}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\left\langle\textstyle\sum_{k}E_{k\alpha}^{\nu}\hat{c}_{k\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{k\alpha}\right\rangle=2\re\tr[I_{\alpha}^{\nu}(t)].

(3)

Here,
\hat{c}_{k\alpha}

\nu=0

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}^{\nu}(t)=\int dt^{\prime}\big[\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\nu,<}(t,t^{\prime})G^{\mathrm{A}}(t^{\prime},t)+\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\nu,\mathrm{R}}(t,t^{\prime})G^{<}(t^{\prime},t)\big].

(4)

The charge part was derived by Jauho, Meir and Wingreen
[
1
,
19
]

[
20
]

\Sigma_{\alpha}^{0}=\Sigma_{\alpha}
, and
\Sigma_{\alpha}^{1}
entering the expression for the energy current will be defined below. In Eq. (

Dealing with the lesser GF component in Eq. (

\displaystyle F^{<}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\theta(t-t^{\prime})F^{<}_{\mathrm{R}}(t,t^{\prime})-\theta(t^{\prime}-t)F^{<}_{\mathrm{A}}(t,t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle G^{<}_{\mathrm{A}}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\rho(t)G^{\mathrm{A}}(t,t^{\prime})-\int\mathrm{d}{y}\mathrm{d}{x}\big\{G^{\mathrm{R}}(t,y)\Sigma^{<}(y,x)

\displaystyle+G_{\mathrm{R}}^{<}(t,y)\Sigma^{\mathrm{A}}(y,x)\big\}\theta(x-t)G^{\mathrm{A}}(x,t^{\prime}),

(6)

with
x
,

denoting intermediate times. Over years, only the first part of this equation — what is conventionally

[
22
,
23
]
,

[
24
,
25
]
, and open
[
13
,
26
]
systems. The
key to this success was the ability to reformulate the complicated integro-differential equation (
2
)
into a system of ordinary differential equations (ODEs), which can conveniently be propagated with linear scaling in physical time. However, some limitations of the time-linear scheme have been identified such as unphysical electron
densities in electronic

In a series of works

, Kalvová, Špička, Velický, and Lipavský demonstrated dramatic improvements in the treatment of electronic transport, by approximately including correction terms in Eq. (

We demonstrate here that it is possible to deal with the reconstruction equation in a pure ODE way (akin of Ref.

In the considered scenario, in addition to time-dependent MF
h(t)
, the dynamics is driven by voltages
V_{\alpha}(t)
applied to the leads, and by the ramp function
s_{\alpha}(t)
associated with tunneling matrix elements, i.e.,
T_{ik\alpha}\rightarrow T_{ik\alpha}s_{\alpha}(t)
. This allows us to build a correlated initial state by the adiabatic switching procedure, and for the lead
\alpha
, results in the time-prefactor

\displaystyle s_{\alpha}(t)e^{-i\phi_{\alpha}(t,t^{\prime})}s_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})=s_{\alpha}(t)\sigma_{\alpha}(t,t^{\prime})=s_{\alpha}(t)u_{\alpha}(t,t^{\prime})s_{\alpha}(t^{\prime}),

(7)

where
\phi_{\alpha}(t,t^{\prime})\equiv\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}\mathrm{d}{x}V_{\alpha}(x)
is the accumulated phase due to the applied voltage. The SE components read
[
10
]
:

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\nu,\mathrm{R}}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-is_{\alpha}(t)\sigma_{\alpha}(t,t^{\prime})\mathcal{F}\left[\omega^{\nu}\Gamma_{\alpha}(\omega)\right](t-t^{\prime})\theta(t-t^{\prime}),

(8a)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\nu,<}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=is_{\alpha}(t)\sigma_{\alpha}(t,t^{\prime})\mathcal{F}\left[\omega^{\nu}f_{\alpha}(\omega)\Gamma_{\alpha}(\omega)\right](t-t^{\prime}),

(8b)

where
\mathcal{F}[a](\tau)=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\omega}{2\pi}{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}\omega\tau}a(\omega)
is the Fourier transform and
f_{\alpha}(\omega)
is the Fermi-Dirac (FD) distribution function (for inverse temperature
\beta_{\alpha}
and chemical potential
\mu_{\alpha}
), which we write using a suitable pole expansion
[
33
]

f_{\alpha}(\omega)=\frac{1}{e^{\beta_{\alpha}(\omega-\mu_{\alpha})}+1}=\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\eta_{\ell}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\beta_{\alpha}(\omega-\mu_{\alpha})+i\zeta_{\ell}}\\
+\frac{1}{\beta_{\alpha}(\omega-\mu_{\alpha})-i\zeta_{\ell}}\Bigr],\quad\text{with\; $\re\zeta_{\ell}>0$.}

(9)

Performing the Fourier transforms (
8
) by closing the integration contour in the complex lower, upper half-plane we obtain for
\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{R}}(t,x)=s_{\alpha}(t)\bar{\Sigma}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{R}}(t,x)
,
\Sigma_{\beta}^{\mathrm{A}}(x,t)=\bar{\Sigma}_{\beta}^{\mathrm{A}}(x,t)s_{\beta}(t)
, respectively:

\displaystyle\bar{\Sigma}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{R}}(t,x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2}\Omega_{\alpha}\gamma_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}(t,x)s_{\alpha}(x)e^{-i\bar{\epsilon}_{\alpha}(t-x)}\theta(t-x),

(10a)

\displaystyle\bar{\Sigma}_{\beta}^{\mathrm{A}}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\Omega_{\beta}\gamma_{\beta}e^{i\bar{\epsilon}_{\beta}^{*}(t-x)}s_{\beta}(x)u_{\beta}(x,t)\theta(t-x).

(10b)

where we introduced
\bar{\epsilon}_{\alpha}=\epsilon_{\alpha}-i\Omega_{\alpha}
and used
x
to denote intermediate times in convolution integrals, and Greek subscripts to enumerate leads (
1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq N_{\text{leads}}

For the lesser component of embedding SE [
\Sigma_{\mathrm{R},\alpha}^{<}(t,x)=s_{\alpha}(t)\bar{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{R},\alpha}^{<}(t,x)

\Sigma_{\mathrm{A},\beta}^{<}(x,t)=\bar{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{A},\beta}^{<}(x,t)s_{\beta}(t)

\displaystyle\bar{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{R},\alpha}^{<}(t,x)

\displaystyle=iu_{\alpha}(t,x)\Bigg\{\gamma_{\alpha}\frac{\Omega_{\alpha}}{2}f_{\alpha}(\epsilon_{\alpha}-i\Omega_{\alpha})e^{-i\bar{\epsilon}_{\alpha}(t-x)}+i\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{\eta_{\ell}}{\beta_{\alpha}}\Gamma_{\alpha}\left(\mu_{\alpha}-i\frac{\zeta_{\ell}}{\beta_{\alpha}}\right)e^{-i\left(\mu_{\alpha}-i\frac{\zeta_{\ell}}{\beta_{\alpha}}\right)(t-x)}\Bigg\}s_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=u_{\alpha}(t,x)s_{\alpha}(x)\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\bar{\eta}_{\alpha\ell}e^{-i\bar{\mu}_{\alpha\ell}(t-x)}=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\bar{\eta}_{\alpha\ell}\bar{\bar{\Sigma}}_{\mathrm{R},\alpha\ell}^{<}(t,x).

(11)

where the expansion coefficients
\bar{\eta}_{\alpha\ell}
and the exponents
\bar{\mu}_{\alpha\ell}
are given by

\displaystyle\bar{\eta}_{\alpha\ell}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}i\frac{\gamma_{\alpha}}{2}\Omega_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}(\epsilon_{\alpha}-i\Omega_{\alpha})&\ell=0,\\
-\frac{\eta_{\ell}}{\beta_{\alpha}}\Gamma_{\alpha}\left(\mu_{\alpha}-i\frac{\zeta_{\ell}}{\beta_{\alpha}}\right)&\ell\geq 1;\end{cases}

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}_{\alpha\ell}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\epsilon_{\alpha}-i\Omega_{\alpha}&\ell=0,\\
\mu_{\alpha}-i\frac{\zeta_{\ell}}{\beta_{\alpha}}&\ell\geq 1.\end{cases}

(12)

Analogously, for the lesser SE of advanced type (remember the incorporated minus sign, cf. Eq.
5
) we obtain:

\displaystyle\bar{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{A},\beta}^{<}(x,t)

\displaystyle=u_{\beta}(x,t)s_{\beta}(x)\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\bar{\eta}^{*}_{\beta\ell}e^{i\bar{\mu}^{*}_{\beta\ell}(t-x)}=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\bar{\eta}^{*}_{\beta\ell}\bar{\bar{\Sigma}}_{\mathrm{A},\beta\ell}^{<}(x,t).

(13)

Inserting the embedding SEs (
10a
) and (
11
) into Eq. (
4
) yields four contributions to the collision integral. Defining

\displaystyle[af\cdot b](t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\smallint\!\mathrm{d}{x}\,a(t,x)f(x)b(x,t^{\prime}),

(14a)

\displaystyle[af\cdot b]_{\mathrm{A}}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\theta(t^{\prime}-t)[af\cdot b](t,t^{\prime})

(14b)

to simplify notations, the first term in the collision integral (

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\alpha\ell}^{c}(t)

\displaystyle=\bigl[\bar{\bar{\Sigma}}_{\mathrm{R},\alpha\ell}^{<}\cdot G^{\mathrm{A}}\bigr](t,t).

Second collision term in combination with the first term of the reconstruction equation leads to

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}^{d}(t)

\displaystyle=\left[\bar{\Sigma}^{\mathrm{R}}_{\alpha}\rho\cdot G^{\mathrm{A}}\right](t,t).

(16)

Finally, the correction terms in Eq. (

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\alpha\beta}^{a}(t)

\displaystyle=\left[\bar{\Sigma}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{R}}\cdot\big[G^{\mathrm{R}}\cdot\Sigma^{<}_{\beta}\big]_{\mathrm{A}}\cdot G^{\mathrm{A}}\right](t,t),

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\alpha\beta}^{b}(t)

Numerical demonstration.—

A stringent test for open system calculations with narrow-band leads involves a scenario where a central system is connected to two leads with aligned chemical potentials and identical temperatures. From the Landauer–Büttiker (LB) formula
[
10

J_{\alpha}^{(0)}=\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\sum_{\beta}\left(f_{\alpha}(\omega-V_{\alpha})-f_{\beta}(\omega-V_{\beta})\right)\\
\times\tr\left[\Gamma_{\alpha}(\omega-V_{\alpha})G^{\mathrm{R}}(\omega)\Gamma_{\beta}(\omega-V_{\beta})G^{\mathrm{A}}(\omega)\right]

(21)

such a system should exhibit no steady-state currents, even when the spectral densities
\Gamma_{\alpha}
and
\Gamma_{\beta}
are centered at different energies
[

]
. In our first setup, a central site with energy
\varepsilon_{0}=-3
is connected to two leads with

, inverse temperatures
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=30
, and level widths
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}=0.5
. The initial state is prepared by adiabatically turning on the hopping to the leads on the time interval
[t_{i},0]
,

using the ramp functions

. For

, the system evolves without any applied voltages. In Fig.

,

,

,

This framework paves the way for exploring higher-order iterative reconstructions and potential extensions to correlated electronic and bosonic systems. We anticipate applications in studying nonequilibrium heat flows
